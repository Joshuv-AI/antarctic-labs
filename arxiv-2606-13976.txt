l p − L q l^{p}-L^{q} boundedness of sequence-to-function Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type operators
Jianjun Jin

Abstract
. In this paper we deal with the boundedness of certain generalized sequence-to-function Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type operators. We completely characterize the l p − L q l^{p}-L^{q} boundedness of these operators for all ( p , q ) ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ] × [ 1 , ∞ ] (p,q)\in[1,\infty]\times[1,\infty] . These results complement some previous results in the literature. Our method relies on the ideas of generalized Schur’s tests developed by Okikiolu, Sinnamon, Tao and Zhao.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
p\geq 1
, we use
p^{\prime}
to denote the conjugate of
p
, i.e.,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. In particular,
p^{\prime}=\infty
when
p=1
. Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,+\infty)
and let
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the class of all real-valued measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}.
Let
L^{p}:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the usual Lebesgue space on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, i.e.,

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\{f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\|f\|_{p}=(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}dx)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\}.

When
p>1
. If
f\in L^{p},g\in L^{p^{\prime}}
, then we have the famous Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya (
{\mathcal{HLP}}
) inequality as

(1.1)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{f(x)g(y)}{\max\{x,y\}}dxdy\Big|\leq(p+p^{\prime})\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}},

where the constant
p+p^{\prime}
in (
1.1
) is the best possible, see
[
3
]
. (
1.1
) has the following equivalent form

(1.2)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{f(y)}{\max\{x,y\}}dy\right|^{p}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq(p+p^{\prime})\|f\|_{p}.

(
1.2
) is also called
{\mathcal{HLP}}
inequality and the constant
p+p^{\prime}
in (
1.2
) is still the best possible.

We can restate the
{\mathcal{HLP}}
inequality (
1.2
) in the language of operator theory. We define the
{\mathcal{HLP}}
integral operator
H
induced by
{\mathcal{HLP}}
kernel
\frac{1}{\max\{x,y\}}
as

Hf(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{f(x)}{\max\{x,y\}}dx,\>f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\>y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Then we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>1
. Let
H
be as above. Then
H
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and the norm
\|H\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}
of
H
is
pp^{\prime}
. Here

\|H\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}=\sup_{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\frac{\|Hf\|_{p}}{\|f\|_{p}}.

There is also a discrete version of Theorem
1.1
. For a sequence
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, we define
{\mathcal{HLP}}
operator
\mathcal{H}
as

{\mathcal{H}}a(n):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}}{\max\{m,n\}},\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

We use
l^{p}
to denote the space of sequences of real numbers, i.e.,

l^{p}:=\{a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:\|a\|_{p}=(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\}.

Then we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>1
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be as above. Then
\mathcal{H}
is bounded on
l^{p}
and the norm
\|\mathcal{H}\|_{l^{p}\rightarrow l^{p}}
of
\mathcal{H}
is
pp^{\prime}
. Here

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{l^{p}\rightarrow l^{p}}=\sup_{a\in l^{p}}\frac{\|\mathcal{H}a\|_{p}}{\|a\|_{p}}.

The Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator is related to some important topics in analysis and there have been many results about this operator and its analogues and generalizations. The classical results of this operator can be found in the well-known monograph
[
3
]
. In the past three decades, the so-called Hilbert-type operators, including
{\mathcal{HLP}}
-type operators, have been extensively studied by Yang and his coauthors, see the survey
[
10
]
and Yang’s book
[
11
]
. For more recent results see for example
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
. In the work
[
1
]
, Brevig established some norm estimates for certain
{\mathcal{HLP}}
-type operators in terms of Riemann zeta function. Some further results can be found in
[
2
]
.

Recently, much attention has been paid to the study of the boundedness and estimate for the norm of the following operator
H_{K}
, which is defined as

H_{K}a(x):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}K(m,x)a_{m},\,\>a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty},x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

where
K(y,x)
is a non-negative measurable function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. For more detailed introductions to this topic, see the book
[
9
]
of Yang and Lokenath, and the references cited therein. In particular, take
K(y,x)=[\max\{y,x\}]^{-1}
, we get the following operator

{\bf H}a(x):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}}{\max\{m,x\}},\,\>a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty},x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

In this paper, we continue the study of this topic. Let
\lambda,\alpha,\beta
be real numbers. For
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, we define the operator
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
as

(1.3)

{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a(x):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\alpha}x^{\beta}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}a_{m},\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

We call
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}

sequence-to-function
{\mathcal{HLP}}
-type operator
. We will investigate the conditions of the boundedness of
H_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
. Note that previous studies have primarily focused on the
l^{p}-L^{q}
boundedness of sequence-to-function
{\mathcal{HLP}}
-type operators for
p=q>1
. In the present paper, we shall completely characterize the
l^{p}-L^{q}
boundedness of these operators for all
(p,q)\in[1,\infty]\times[1,\infty]
. Our method relies on the ideas of generalized Schur’s tests, see
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
13
]
. The method used in this paper can be applied to other non-negative measurable kernel
K(y,x)
defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

To state our results, we introduce some notation. For
1\leq p<\infty,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the weighted Lebesgue space

L_{\theta}^{p}:=L_{\theta}^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
as

L_{\theta}^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\{f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):||f||_{p,\theta}=(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}x^{\theta}dx)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\}.

We write
L^{p}
and
\|f\|_{p}
instead of
L_{\theta}^{p}
and

\theta=0
.
We define the class

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\{f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\|f\|_{\infty}:=\mathop{\text{ess sup}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(x)|<\infty\}.

Meanwhile, we define the weighted space
l_{\theta}^{p}
of sequences of real numbers as

l_{\theta}^{p}=\{a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:||a||_{p,\theta}=(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\theta}|a_{n}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\}.

We use
l^{p}
and
\|a\|_{p}
to denote
l_{\theta}^{p}
and
\|a\|_{p,\theta}

\theta=0
. The class
l^{\infty}

l^{\infty}=\{a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:\|a\|_{\infty}=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|a_{n}|<\infty\}.

We will prove the following theorems for the boundedness of
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
for the cases
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty

.

Let
1\leq p\leq q<\infty

\lambda,\alpha,\beta,\varphi,\psi

\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
be as in (

\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from

l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{q}
if and only if

\begin{cases}\lambda\geq\alpha+\beta+1+\frac{\psi+1}{q}-\frac{\varphi+1}{p},\\
-q\beta<\psi+1<q(\lambda-\beta).\end{cases}

.

Let
\lambda,\alpha,\beta
be real numbers and
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}

). Then
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from

l_{\varphi}^{1}
to
L^{\infty}
if and only if

\begin{cases}\lambda\geq\beta\geq 0,\\
\lambda\geq\alpha+\beta-\varphi.\end{cases}

Let

be as in (

). Then
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}

to

if and only if

\begin{cases}\lambda>\beta\geq 0,\\

or

\begin{cases}\lambda\geq\beta\geq 0,\\
\lambda>\alpha+\beta+1-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}.\end{cases}

2.
Lemmas

In this section, we list some known lemmas and establish some new ones. We first recall the following two lemmas, see
[
7
, Problem 5.5]
for more general versions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
K(y,x)
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
0\leq K(y,x)<\infty
for all
(y,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. For
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, let
T
be the operator with kernel
K
defined as

Ta(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}K(m,x)a_{m},\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Then
T
is bounded from
l^{p}
to
L^{1}
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}K(m,x)dx\in l^{p^{\prime}}.

Moreover, when
T
is bounded from
l^{p}
to
L^{1}
, the norm of
T
is given by

\|T\|_{l^{p}\rightarrow L^{1}}=\|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}K(m,x)dx\|_{p^{\prime}}.

The following is a dual version of Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
K(y,x)
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
0\leq K(y,x)<\infty
for all
(y,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. For
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, let
T
be the operator with kernel
K
defined as

Ta(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}K(m,x)a_{m},\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Then
T
is bounded from
l^{\infty}
to
L^{p}
if and only if

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}K(m,x)\in L^{p}.

Moreover, when
T
is bounded from
l^{\infty}
to
L^{p}
, the norm of
T
is given by

\|T\|_{l^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{p}}=\|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}K(m,x)\|_{p}.

Remark 2.3
.

In particular, when
p=\infty
,
T
is bounded from
l^{\infty}
to
L^{\infty}
if and only if

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}K(m,x)<\infty.

We also need the following generalized Schur’s test, see
[
6
]
for its general form.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1<q\leq p<\infty
. Let
K(y,x)
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
0\leq K(y,x)<\infty
for all
(y,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. For
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, let
T
be the operator with kernel
K
defined as

Ta(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}K(m,x)a_{m},\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

If there exist two constants
C_{1}>0,C_{2}>0
and a sequence
u=\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
|u_{n}|\leq C_{1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and satisfying
I_{K}u(n)\leq C_{2}u(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
T
is bounded from
l^{p}
to
L^{q}.
Here

I_{K}u(n)=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}K(n,x)\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}K(m,x)u_{m}\Big)^{q-1}dx\Big)^{p^{\prime}-1},\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

We will use the following lemmas in our later arguments.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\tau,\lambda
be two real numbers and let
t>0
be fixed. Then the integral

I(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{x^{\tau}}{[\max\{t,x\}]^{\lambda}}dx,

converges if and only if
\tau>-1
and
\lambda-\tau-1>0
. When the integral converges, we have

I(t)=t^{\tau+1-\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{\tau+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda-\tau-1}\Big).

Proof.

For fixed
t>0
, note that

(2.1)

I(t)=t^{-\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}x^{\tau}dx+\int_{t}^{\infty}x^{\tau-\lambda}dx.

Then we conclude that
I(t)<\infty
if and only if
\tau+1>0
and
\lambda-\tau-1>0
. On the other hand, when
\tau+1>0
and
\lambda-\tau-1>0
, it follows from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle I(t)=t^{\tau+1-\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{\tau+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda-\tau-1}\Big).

The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\tau,\lambda
be real numbers and
x>0
be fixed. Then

S(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}<\infty

if and only if
\lambda-\tau-1>0
. Moreover,
(1)
when
\lambda-\tau-1>0
and
-1<\tau\leq 0
, we have

S(x)\leq\Big(\frac{1}{\tau+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda-\tau-1}\Big)x^{\tau+1-\lambda},\,\,\,\,x>0,

(2)

when
\lambda-\tau-1>0
and
\tau>0
, we have

S(x)\leq Cx^{\tau+1-\lambda},\,\,\,x>0,

Here,
C
is a positive constant which is independent of
x
.

Proof.

For fixed
x>0
, we note that

\frac{1}{m^{\tau-\lambda}}\cdot\frac{m^{\tau}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}\rightarrow 1,\,\,{\text{as}}\,\,m\rightarrow\infty.

Then we see that
S(x)<\infty
if and only if
\lambda-\tau>1
. Moreover,

Case 1. When
\lambda-\tau-1>0
and
-1<\tau\leq 0
, we obtain from Lemma
2.5
that

\displaystyle S(x)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{t^{\tau}}{[\max\{{t,x}\}]^{\lambda}}dt=t^{\tau+1-\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{\tau+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda-\tau-1}\Big).

Case 2. When
\lambda-\tau-1>0
and
\tau>0
, we have, for
x\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{m^{\lambda}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{\lambda-\tau}}\leq C_{1}x^{\tau+1-\lambda}.

Here we can take
C_{1}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{\lambda-\tau}}
since
x^{\tau+1-\lambda}\geq 1
.

When
\lambda-\tau-1>0
and
\tau>0
, we have, for
x>1
,

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil x\rceil}\frac{m^{\tau}}{x^{\lambda}}+\sum_{m=\lceil x\rceil+1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{m^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lceil x\rceil\frac{\lceil x\rceil^{\tau}}{x^{\lambda}}+\int_{\lceil x\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{\lambda-\tau}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x^{\tau+1-\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda-\tau-1}\lceil x\rceil^{\tau+1-\lambda}.

Here,
\lceil x\rceil
is the ceiling function. Note that
\lceil x\rceil^{\tau+1-\lambda}=1
for each
x\in(1,2)
so that

\lceil x\rceil^{\tau+1-\lambda}\leq 2^{\lambda-\tau-1}x^{\tau+1-\lambda},

and for each
x\geq 2
, we have
\lceil x\rceil\geq\frac{1}{2}x
so that

\lceil x\rceil^{\tau+1-\lambda}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\tau+1-\lambda}}x^{\tau+1-\lambda}.

Consequently, we obtain that, for
x>1
,

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}x^{\tau+1-\lambda}.

Here we can take

C_{2}=2^{\lambda-\tau-1}+\frac{2^{\lambda-\tau-1}}{\lambda-\tau-1}.

Hence, when
\lambda-\tau-1>0
and
\tau>0
, it holds that, for any
x>0
,

S(x)\leq(C_{1}+C_{2})x^{\tau+1-\lambda}=Cx^{\tau+1-\lambda}.

Here,
C>0
is independent of
x
. This proves Lemma
2.6
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

When
\lambda-\tau-1>0
, for fixed
x>0
, we note that
\frac{m^{\tau}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}
is decreasing with respect to
m
for
m\geq\lceil x\rceil+1
.
Then it is not hard to see that, for fixed
x>0
,

S(x)\geq\int_{\lceil x\rceil+1}^{\infty}x^{\tau-\lambda}dx\geq\frac{(\lceil x\rceil+1)^{\tau+1-\lambda}}{\lambda-\tau-1}\geq\frac{(x+1)^{\tau+1-\lambda}}{\lambda-\tau-1}.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\beta,\tau,\lambda
be real numbers.
If
\beta<\lambda
,
\tau=-1
,
\lambda>0
, or
\beta\leq\lambda
,
\tau<-1
,
\lambda-\tau-1>0
, then

A:=\sup_{x>1}x^{\beta}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}<\infty.

Proof.

We still let

S(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}.

When
\lambda-\tau-1>0
and
\tau\leq-1
, we have, for
x>1
,

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil x\rceil}\frac{m^{\tau}}{x^{\lambda}}+\sum_{m=\lceil x\rceil+1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\tau}}{m^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x^{-\lambda}(1+\int_{1}^{\lceil x\rceil}t^{\tau}dt)+\int_{\lceil x\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{\lambda-\tau}}dt.

We will consider the following two cases.

Case 1. When
\tau=-1
, we easily see that there is a constant
C_{1}>0
, which is independent of
x
and such that

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x^{-\lambda}(1+\ln\lceil x\rceil)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\lceil x\rceil^{-\lambda}\leq C_{1}x^{-\lambda+\epsilon},

for any
\epsilon>0
since
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\ln t}{t^{\epsilon}}=0
for any
\epsilon>0
. Consequently, by
\beta-\lambda<0
, we obtain that

A\leq C_{1}\sup_{x>1}x^{\beta}{x^{-\lambda+\epsilon}}=C_{1}\sup_{x>1}{x^{\beta-\lambda+\epsilon}}\leq C_{1},

when
\varepsilon<\lambda-\beta
.

Case 2. When
\tau<-1
so that
x^{\tau+1}\leq 1
for
x>1
, then we get that

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x^{-\lambda}[1+\frac{1}{\tau+1}(\lceil x\rceil^{\tau+1}-1)]+\frac{1}{\lambda-\tau-1}\lceil x\rceil^{\tau+1-\lambda}\leq C_{2}x^{-\lambda}.

Here,
C_{2}>0
is also independent of
x
. It follows from again
\beta-\lambda\leq 0
that

A\leq C_{2}\sup_{x>1}x^{\beta}{x^{-\lambda}}=C_{1}\sup_{x>1}{x^{\beta-\lambda}}\leq C_{2}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
2.8
.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\lambda,\tau,\omega
be real numbers. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{m^{\tau}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}\leq Cx^{\omega},

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, if
\lambda\geq\tau-\omega
,
\lambda\geq\tau
,
\lambda\geq-\omega
, and
\omega\leq 0.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that, if
\lambda\geq\tau-\omega
,
\lambda\geq\tau
,
\lambda\geq-\omega
, and
\omega\leq 0
, then

\mathcal{S}:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N},x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{m^{\tau}x^{-\omega}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}<\infty.

First, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N},x\in(0,1]}\frac{m^{\tau}x^{-\omega}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N},x\in(0,1]}\frac{x^{-\omega}}{m^{\lambda-\tau}}\leq 1<\infty.

When
x>1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}:=\sup_{m<x,m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{m^{\tau}x^{-\omega}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}=\sup_{m<x,m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{m^{\tau}}{x^{\lambda+\omega}}.

Case I. If
\tau>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}\leq\sup_{m<x,m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{x^{\lambda+\omega-\tau}}\leq 1<\infty.

Case II. If
\tau\leq 0
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}\leq\sup_{m<x,m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{x^{\lambda+\omega}}\leq 1<\infty.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{3}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{m\geq x,m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{m^{\tau}x^{-\omega}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{m\geq x,m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{x^{-\omega}}{m^{\lambda-\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{m>x,m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{m^{\lambda+\omega-\tau}}<\infty.

The lemma follows from
\mathcal{S}=\max\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{3}\}
. The proof of Lemma
2.9
is complete.

The following lemmas provide the necessary conditions for the boundedness of
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
. Let
\lambda,\alpha,\beta,\varphi,\psi
be real numbers and
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
be as in (
1.3
). We have

(1)
If
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from

l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{q}
, then

\begin{cases}\lambda\geq\alpha+\beta+1+\frac{\psi+1}{q}-\frac{\varphi+1}{p},\\
-q\beta<\psi+1<q(\lambda-\beta).\end{cases}

(2)
If
1\leq q<p<\infty
and
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from

l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{q}
, then

\begin{cases}\lambda>\alpha+\beta+1+\frac{\psi+1}{q}-\frac{\varphi+1}{p},\\
-q\beta<\psi+1<q(\lambda-\beta).\end{cases}

Proof.

For
1\leq p,q<\infty
, we suppose that
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from
l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{q}
. Take
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
with
a_{1}=1
and
a_{m}=0
for
m\geq 2.
Then we see that
\|a\|_{p,\varphi}=1,
and

\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a(x)=\frac{x^{\beta}}{[\max\{1,x\}]^{\lambda}},

so that

\|\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a\|_{q,\psi}^{q}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{x^{q\beta+\psi}}{[\max\{1,x\}]^{q\lambda}}dx<\infty.

This implies that
q\lambda-q\beta-\psi>1
and
q\beta+\psi+1>0
. That is

-q\beta<\psi+1<q(\lambda-\beta).

Now, we let

\rho:=\alpha+\beta+1+\frac{\psi+1}{q}-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\lambda.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we take
a_{\varepsilon}:=\{a_{m}^{\varepsilon}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
with

(2.2)

a_{m}^{\varepsilon}=m^{-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}},m\in\mathbb{N}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}=\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1-\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{p,\varphi}^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 1+\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1-\varepsilon}dx=1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

It follows from the boundedness of
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
that, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\alpha}x^{\beta}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}m^{-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}<\infty.

Consequently, from Lemma
2.6
, we know that

\displaystyle\lambda-\alpha+\frac{\varphi+1}{p}+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}>1,

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Hence, in view of Remark
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{q,\psi}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\psi}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}x^{\beta}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}\right]^{q}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-q}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{\psi}\left[x^{\beta}(x+1)^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}+1-\lambda}\right]^{q}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-q}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{\psi}\left[x^{\beta}(2x)^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}+1-\lambda}\right]^{q}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-q}C_{2}^{q}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{\psi+q(\alpha+\beta+1-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}-\lambda)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-q}C_{2}^{q}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1+q\rho-\frac{q\varepsilon}{p}}dx.

Here

(2.3)

C_{1}=\lambda-\alpha+\frac{\varphi+1}{p}+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}-1,\,\,\,C_{2}=2^{-C_{1}}.

Thus, if
\rho>0
, when
\varepsilon<q\rho
, we obtain that

\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1+q\rho-\frac{q\varepsilon}{p}}dx=\infty,

so that
\|\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{q,\psi}^{q}=\infty
, which contradicts the boundedness of
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
. This implies that
\rho\leq 0
. That is

\lambda\geq\alpha+\beta+1+\frac{\psi+1}{q}-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}.

To finish the proof, we next only need to show that, when
1\leq q<p<\infty
, if

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\lambda=\alpha+\beta+1+\frac{\psi+1}{q}-\frac{\varphi+1}{p},\\
-q\beta<\psi+1<q(\lambda-\beta),\\
\end{cases}

then
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is not bounded from
l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{q}
. We suppose that
1\leq q<p<\infty
and (
2.4
) holds. For
\varepsilon>0
, we take
a_{\varepsilon}=\{a_{m}^{\varepsilon}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
as above, then
\|a\|_{p,\varphi}^{p}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}[1+o(1)]
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and by repeating some above arguments, we have

\displaystyle\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{q,\psi}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-q}C_{2}^{q}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{\psi+q(\alpha+\beta+1-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}-\lambda)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-q}C_{2}^{q}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1-\frac{q\varepsilon}{p}}dx=C_{1}^{-q}C_{2}^{q}\frac{p}{q\varepsilon}.

Here
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are the same as in (
2.3
). It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{q,\psi}}{\|a\|_{p,\varphi}}\geq C_{1}^{-1}C_{2}(pq^{-1})^{\frac{1}{q}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\rightarrow\infty,

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
since
p>q
. This means that
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is not bounded from
l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{q}
. The proof of Lemma
2.10
is finished.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\lambda,\alpha,\beta,\varphi,\psi
be real numbers and
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
be as in (
1.3
).

(1)
If
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from

to

(2)

\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}

l_{\varphi}^{p}
to

, then

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\lambda\geq\beta\geq 0,\\
\lambda>\alpha+\beta+1-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}.\end{cases}

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we suppose that
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from
l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L^{\infty}
. Take
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
with
a_{1}=1

a_{m}=0

\varepsilon>0

of Lemma

3.1.
Proof of Theorem

Note that the necessity of the boundedness of

. We only need to prove that, for

{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}

to

3.7

3.8

(L_{1},R_{1})\cup(L_{2},R_{2})

{\bf C}_{3}>0
such that

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{m^{\frac{\lambda-\beta}{s}+D}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\frac{\lambda}{s}}}\leq{\bf C}_{3}x^{D-\frac{\beta}{s}},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. It follows that, for
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\in l_{\varphi}^{1}
with
a_{m}\geq 0
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}[k(m,x)]^{\frac{1}{s}}[k(m,x)]^{\frac{1}{t}}a_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\frac{\lambda-\beta}{s}+D}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\frac{\lambda}{s}}}m^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-\lambda}{s}-D}x^{\frac{\beta}{s}}[k(m,x)]^{\frac{1}{t}}a_{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}x^{D}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-\lambda}{s}-D}[k(m,x)]^{\frac{1}{t}}a_{m}.

Consequently, we have

\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a\|_{q,\psi}\leq{\bf C}_{3}\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-\lambda}{s}-D}[k(m,x)]^{\frac{1}{t}}a_{m}\Big)^{q}x^{qD+\psi}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

By using Minkowski’s inequality again, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a\|_{q,\psi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}[k(m,x)]^{\frac{q}{t}}x^{qD+\psi}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}m^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-\lambda}{s}-D}a_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\frac{\alpha}{t}q}x^{\frac{\beta}{t}q+qD+\psi}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\frac{\lambda}{t}q}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}m^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-\lambda}{s}-D}a_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{{\bf I}_{2}}^{\frac{1}{q}}m^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-\lambda}{s}-D}a_{m}.

From (
3.19
) and Lemma
2.6
, we see that there is a constant
{\bf C}_{4}>0
such that

\displaystyle{{\bf I}_{2}}={\bf C}_{4}m^{\frac{\alpha}{t}q+\frac{\beta}{t}q+qD+\psi+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}q}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a\|_{q,\psi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}{\bf C}_{4}^{\frac{1}{q}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{\frac{1}{q}[\frac{\alpha}{t}q+\frac{\beta}{t}q+qD+\psi+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}q]+\frac{\alpha+\beta-\lambda}{s}-D}a_{m}.

Meanwhile, we note from
\lambda\geq\alpha+\beta+\frac{\psi+1}{q}-\varphi
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}[\frac{\alpha}{t}q+\frac{\beta}{t}q+qD+\psi+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}q]+\frac{\alpha+\beta-\lambda}{s}-D

\displaystyle=\alpha+\beta-\lambda+\frac{\psi+1}{q}\leq\varphi.

This means that

\displaystyle\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a\|_{q,\psi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}{\bf C}_{4}^{\frac{1}{q}}\|a\|_{1,\varphi},

so that
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from
l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{q}
for

. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

, let
\tau:=\lambda-\beta-\frac{\psi+1}{q}>0
, then we see from the proof of Lemma
2.8
that there is a constant
\epsilon\in(0,\tau)
such that

\displaystyle{\bf I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{q\beta+\psi}x^{-q\lambda+q\epsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{4}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{q(\beta+\frac{\psi+1}{q}-\lambda)-1+q\epsilon}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1-q(\tau-\epsilon)}dx<\infty.

Combining (1) and (2), we obtain that
\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{I}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{2}<\infty
when (
6.4
) is satisfied. Now, the if part of Theorem
1.9
is proved so that we finish the proof of Theorem
1.9
.

7.
Sharp norm estimates of
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
for some special cases

In this section, we will establish sharp norm estimates of
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
for certain special cases. We shall prove that

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Let
\lambda,\alpha,\beta,\varphi,\psi
be real numbers with

(7.1)

\lambda=\alpha+\beta+1+\frac{\psi-\varphi}{p},

and
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
be as in (
1.3
). Then
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from
l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{p}
if and only if

(7.2)

-p\beta<\psi+1<p(\lambda-\beta),

or equivalently,

(7.3)

p(\alpha+1-\lambda)<\varphi+1<p(\alpha+1).

Moreover, if the conditions (
7.1
), (
7.2
)(or (
7.3
)), and

(7.4)

\varphi+1\geq p\alpha,

are all satisfied, then
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from
l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{p}
, and the norm of
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is given by

\|\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\|_{l_{\varphi}^{p}\rightarrow L_{\psi}^{p}}=\frac{1}{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{p}(\varphi+1)}+\frac{1}{\beta+\frac{1}{p}(\psi+1)}.

Here,

\|\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\|_{l_{\varphi}^{p}\rightarrow L_{\psi}^{p}}=\sup_{a\in l_{\varphi}^{p}}\frac{\|\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a\|_{p,\psi}}{\|a\|_{p,\varphi}}.

Proof.

Note that if (
7.1
) holds, then it is easy to see that (
7.2
) is equivalent to (
7.3
). By Lemma
2.5
and repeating the arguments in the proof of
Case
{\bf I}
of Theorem
1.3
, we can prove that
{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from
l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{p}
if and only if (
7.2
)(or (
7.3
)) holds. Next, we will prove that if (
7.1
), (
7.2
) and (
7.4
) are all satisfied, then

\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\|_{l_{\varphi}^{p}\rightarrow l_{\psi}^{p}}=\frac{1}{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{p}(\varphi+1)}+\frac{1}{\beta+\frac{1}{p}(\psi+1)}.

We shall borrow some arguments in the proof of
Case
{\bf I}
of Theorem
1.3
. Actually, when
p=q
, in the proof of
Case
{\bf I}
of Theorem
1.3
, we can take
s=p^{\prime},t=p
and
A=-\frac{\varphi+1}{pp^{\prime}}
. Then, when (
7.4
) holds so that (
3.13
) is satisfied, we have

{\bf C}_{1}={\bf C}_{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{p}(\varphi+1)}+\frac{1}{\beta+\frac{1}{p}(\psi+1)}.

Consequently, we have

\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\|_{l_{\varphi}^{p}\rightarrow L_{\psi}^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{p}(\varphi+1)}+\frac{1}{\beta+\frac{1}{p}(\psi+1)}.

Finally, we prove that

\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\|_{l_{\varphi}^{p}\rightarrow L_{\psi}^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{p}(\varphi+1)}+\frac{1}{\beta+\frac{1}{p}(\psi+1)}.

For
0<\varepsilon<p(\alpha+1)-(\varphi+1)
, we take the same
a_{\varepsilon}
as in (
2.2
). Then

(7.5)

\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{p,\alpha}^{p}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}[1+o(1)],\,\,{\text{as}}\,\,\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

and from
\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}<0
we have

\displaystyle\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{p,\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\psi}\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\alpha}x^{\beta}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}m^{-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}\Big)^{p}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\psi}\Big(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{y^{\alpha}x^{\beta}}{[\max\{y,x\}]^{\lambda}}y^{-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}dy\Big)^{p}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\psi+p(\alpha+\beta+1-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}-\lambda)}\Big(\int_{\frac{1}{x}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}-\lambda}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}dt\Big)^{p}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\varepsilon-1}\Big(\int_{\frac{1}{x}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}dt\Big)^{p}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}

Now, we take
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{z}
with
z>[p(\alpha+1)-(\varphi+1)]^{-1}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{p,\psi}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{z}^{\infty}x^{-\varepsilon-1}\Big(\int_{\frac{1}{z}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}dt\Big)^{p}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon^{\varepsilon-1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\int_{\frac{1}{z}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}dt.

It follows that

(7.6)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\|_{l_{\varphi}^{p}\rightarrow L_{\psi}^{p}}\geq\frac{\|{\bf H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{p,\psi}}{\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{p,\alpha}}\geq{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}[1+o(1)]^{-\frac{1}{p}}\int_{\frac{1}{z}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}dt.

Here
o(1)
is the same as in (
7.5
). We let

\mathcal{S}_{{z}}=\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:t\geq\frac{1}{{z}}\}.

Then

(7.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{z}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{1}{{z}p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{\mathcal{S}_{z}}(t)t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{1}{{z}p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}dt.

Note that, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(7.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{\mathcal{S}_{z}}(t)t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{1}{{z}p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}\rightarrow\frac{t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}},\,\,{\text{a}s}\,\,{z}\rightarrow\infty,

and
{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}[1+o(1)]^{-\frac{1}{p}}\rightarrow 1
as
{z}\rightarrow\infty.

Consequently, from (
7.6
), (
7.7
), (
7.8
) and by using Fatou’s lemma, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\|_{l_{\varphi}^{p}\rightarrow L_{\psi}^{p}}\geq\liminf_{z\to\infty}{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}[1+o(1)]^{-\frac{1}{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{\mathcal{S}_{z}}(t)t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{1}{{z}p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}}}{[\max\{t,1\}]^{\lambda}}\,dt=\frac{1}{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{p}(\varphi+1)}+\frac{1}{\beta+\frac{1}{p}(\psi+1)}.

This proves Theorem
7.1
.
∎

8.
Final remarks

Remark 8.1
.

We first point out that the condition
\alpha+1\geq 0
in Theorem
1.9
is necessary. As a counterexample, we consider the case
\alpha<-1
,
\lambda=0
and
q,\psi
satisfying that

0<\beta+\frac{\psi+1}{q}<-\alpha-1
.
Then

\displaystyle{\bf I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\alpha}x^{\beta+\frac{\psi}{q}}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{\lambda}}\Big]^{q}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}x^{q\beta+\psi}\Big[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{\alpha}\Big]^{q}dx=C(\alpha,q)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}x^{q\beta+\psi}dx.

But we always have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}x^{q\beta+\psi}dx=+\infty,\,{\text{ for any}}\,q,\beta,\psi.

This means that the operator is not bounded in this case and therefore the condition in Theorem
1.9
can not be removed.

Remark 8.2
.

We finally show by another counterexample that the condition

\varphi+1<p(\alpha+1)
in Theorem
1.8
is necessary and can not be removed.

We take
p=3,q=2,\alpha=-1/2,\beta=0,\varphi=2,\psi=0,\lambda=1/2
. Then

\varphi+1=3>p(\alpha+1)=3/2
, and

0=-q\beta<\psi+1=1,

and

\frac{1}{2}=\lambda>\alpha+\beta+1+\frac{\psi+1}{q}-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}=0.

Now, for
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the sequence
a_{\varepsilon}=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
with

a_{m}=m^{-\frac{\varphi+1}{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}=m^{-1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}},\,m\in\mathbb{N}.

It is easy to see that

\|a\|_{p,\varphi}^{p}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-1-\varepsilon}<\infty,

so that
a_{\varepsilon}\in l_{\varphi}^{p}
. Note that for
x>1
,

\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{-3/2-\varepsilon/2}}{[\max\{m,x\}]^{1/2}}.

It follows that for any
x>1
,

\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil x\rceil}\frac{m^{-3/2-\varepsilon/2}}{x^{1/2}}\geq x^{-1/2}\sum_{m=1}^{\lceil x\rceil}m^{-3/2-\varepsilon/2}\geq x^{-1/2},

Consequently, we obtain that

\|\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{q,\psi}^{q}=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\psi}|\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}a_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{q}dx\geq\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1}dx=+\infty.

Hence
\mathbf{H}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
is not bounded from
l_{\varphi}^{p}
to
L_{\psi}^{q}
, although all the conditions of Theorem
1.8
are satisfied except the one
\phi+1<p(\alpha+1)
. This means that
the condition
\varphi+1<p(\alpha+1)
in Theorem
1.8
can not be removed.
