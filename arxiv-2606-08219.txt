Equivalence between time and norm optimal control problems of stochastic differential equations
Yanqing Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we establish the equivalence between minimal time and minimal norm control problems for controllable stochastic differential equations (SDEs). The core of this equivalence lies in proving the continuity of the minimal norm function. Unlike the deterministic setting, where the time translation technique is effective, this approach is not applicable due to the adapted nature of the admissible controls in SDEs. To address this challenge, we construct the minimal norm control explicitly, thereby achieving the desired continuity. This constructive approach not only proves theoretical equivalence but also facilitates the design of efficient numerical schemes for minimal norm and time controls.



Keywords:
equivalence, minimal norm control, minimal time control, stochastic differential equation

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
be a complete probability space on which a
standard one-dimensional Brownian motion
W=\{W(t)\,|\,t\geq 0\}
is defined,
with
\mathbb{F}=\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
being its natural filtration augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
{\cal F}
.
For fixed
T\in(0,\infty)
and a given Hilbert space
(\mathbb{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{H}})
, we denote by
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})
for
t\in[0,T]
the Hilbert space consisting of all
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable,
\mathbb{H}
-valued random variables
\xi

such that
\mathbb{E}\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}<\infty
; by
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{H})
the Hilbert space consisting of all
\mathbb{H}
-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-adapted stochastic processes
\varphi(\cdot)
such that
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|\varphi(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
; by
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{H}))
the Banach space consisting of continuous processes
\varphi(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{H})

such that
\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\varphi(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\big]<\infty
. In a similar way, one can define
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{H})
.

This paper is devoted to study the equivalence of two optimal control problems — minimal
time
and
norm
control problems — subject to the following linear controlled stochastic differential equation (SDE, for short)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathrm{d}}x(t)=\big[Ax(t)+Bu(t)\big]\,\mathrm{d}t+\big[Cx(t)+Du(t)\big]\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\qquad t\geq 0\,,

(1.1)

where
A\,,C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
B\,,D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
for
n,m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and
u(\cdot)
is a
control variable taken from

L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
For a given initial condition
x(0)=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will denote by
x\big(\cdot;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)

the solution to SDEs (
1.1
).

Intuitively, the minimal time control problem focuses on reaching a desired target in the shortest possible time, while the minimal norm control problem aims to achieve the target with the least amount of energy or control effort.
The solvability of these two problems for systems governed by SDEs, and
the exact controllability of linear controlled SDEs, along with
the validity of
observability inequalities for backward SDEs (BSDEs, for short) are four notable problems in stochastic control theory. In
[
16
]
, the equivalence of the solvability of minimal norm problems, controllability of SDEs and the validity of observability inequalities for BSDEs was established.
Additionally, there exist references addressing one or more of these topics, such as
[
12
,
8
,
4
,
18
,
3
,
17
,
11
]
.
In the deterministic setting, some works have explored the equivalence between minimal time and norm control problems; see
[
15
,
14
,
13
]
and the references therein.
However,
to the best of the author’s knowledge, there are no published works
on the equivalence of the two problems in the context of SDEs.
Therefore, this work aims to fill this gap.

If
C=0
,
D=0
and
u(\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, (
1.1
) is
reduced to a linear controlled ordinary differential equation (ODE, for short), which will be denoted by
[A,B]
.
The controllability of
[A,B]
is well-studied. However,
differing from the equivalence of exact, approximate and null controllability for the ODE system
[A,B]
, the controllability of SDEs is more complicated and not yet completely understood. For studies on exact controllability,
see e.g.,
[
12
,
8
,
10
,
16
]
; for approximate and null controllability,
see e.g.,
[
2
,
5
]
.
Throughout this work, we adopt the following assumption:

(A)
System (
1.1
) is exactly controllable,
i.e.
, for any
T>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a control

u(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
x\big(T;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)=\xi
.

In the below, we specify minimal
time
and
norm
control problems governed by SDE (
1.1
).
Suppose that
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is any given initial state and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is any fixed target with

T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
It is obvious that if
T_{0}=0
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a deterministic vector.
Now the minimal time control problem
subject to (
1.1
) can be stated as Problem
(TP)^{N}_{x_{0},\xi}
:
for any
N\geq 0
,

T(N,x_{0},\xi)\triangleq\inf_{u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{N}}\big\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\,\big|\,x\big(t;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)=\xi\big\}\,,

(1.2)

where the feasible control set
{\cal U}_{N}
is defined by

{\cal U}_{N}\triangleq\big\{u(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R}^{m})\,\big|\,\|u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq N\big\}\,.

(1.3)

If
\big\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\,|\,x\big(t;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)=\xi\,,\mbox{for any }u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{N}\big\}=\emptyset
, set

T(N,x_{0},\xi)=\inf\emptyset=\infty
.

Definition 1.1
.

T(N,x_{0},\xi)
given in (
1.2
) is called the minimal time of Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
. If
u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{N}
and there exists some

t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

such that
x\big(t;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)=\xi
, then
u(\cdot)
is called an admissible control. An admissible control
u(\cdot)
is called
a minimal time control if
T(N,x_{0},\xi)<\infty
and
x\big(T(N,x_{0},\xi),x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)=\xi
.
If the restrictions of all minimal time controls over
\big(0,T(N,x_{0},\xi)\big)
are the same, then the minimal time control is
said to be unique.

By the adaptedness property of solutions to SDEs, we know that for any
t<T_{0}\,,N\geq 0
and
u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{N}
,
it holds that
x\big(t;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\subsetneq L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Thus, if
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the “smallest” space where
\xi
belongs to,
i.e.
,

\xi\notin L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T_{0})\,,

(1.4)

then
it is impossible to find a control
u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{N}
such that
x(t;x_{0},u(\cdot))=\xi
for any
t<T_{0}
.
Equivalently, this implies

\displaystyle T(N,x_{0},\xi)\geq T_{0}\qquad\forall\,N\geq 0\,.

For example, if the target
\xi=\varepsilon W(T_{0})
with
\varepsilon>0
, no matter how small
\varepsilon
is or how great the control capability is,
\xi
cannot be reached before time
T_{0}
.

Similar to Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
, we also can define
the minimal norm control problem
stated as Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
: for any
T>0
,

N(T,x_{0},\xi)\triangleq\inf\big\{\|u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\,\big|\,x\big(T;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)=\xi\big\}\,.

(1.5)

Definition 1.2
.

In Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
,
N(T,x_{0},\xi)
defined in (
1.5
) is called the minimal norm.
If
x\big(T;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)=\xi
, then the
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
-valued
u(\cdot)
is called an admissible control.
An admissible control
u(\cdot)
is called a
minimal norm control if
\|u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N(T,x_{0},\xi)
.

Definition 1.3
.

Problems
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
and
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
are said to be mutually equivalent if the following conditions hold:

(i)

Problems
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
and
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
have minimal time and norm controls, respectively;

(ii)

When
u_{*}(\cdot)
is a minimal time control of Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
, then
u_{*}|_{(0,T)}(\cdot)
, the restriction of
u_{*}(\cdot)
to the interval
(0,T)
, is a minimal norm control of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
;

(iii)

When
u^{*}(\cdot)
is a minimal norm control of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
, then
\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)
, defined on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
by extending
u^{*}(\cdot)
to zero outside of
(0,T)
, is a minimal time control of Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
.

Compared to deterministic minimal time and norm control problems, the stochastic counterparts enjoy their own characteristics:

(a)
Construction of the minimal norm control.
In the deterministic minimal norm control problem for a system presented by
[A,B]
, for any initial state
x_{0}
and target
\xi
, the minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
can be presented by

\displaystyle u^{*}(t)=-B^{\top}e^{A^{\top}(T-t)}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}e^{As}BB^{\top}e^{A^{\top}s}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]^{-1}\big[e^{AT}x_{0}-\xi\big]\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,.

In the stochastic setting, however, the above method cannot be directly used to construct admissible controls.
Driving the presentation of the minimal norm control in the stochastic context is a non-trivial task, even for specific cases; see e.g.,
[
1
]
.
In Section
2
, we will adopt the method of Lagrange multipliers to derive the representation of the minimal norm control; see Proposition
2.7
for details.

(b)
Adaptedness property of
x(\cdot)
and
u(\cdot)
.
Intuitively, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
g(t)
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable, where
g(\cdot)
represents either
x(\cdot)
or
u(\cdot)
. Hence, after applying a time translation
t\to t+t_{0}
for some
t_{0}>0
,

g_{0}(t)\triangleq g(t+t_{0})
becomes
\mathcal{F}_{t+t_{0}}
-measurable, but may no longer be
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.
To study the equivalence of Problems
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
and
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
, one needs continuity of the minimal norm function
N(\cdot,x_{0},\xi)
. In the deterministic
setting, time translation is a common technique; see e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 2.6]
. But in the stochastic setting, this technique fails due to the loss of adaptedness of
g_{0}(\cdot)
.
In this work, we apply the derived representation of the minimal norm control in Proposition
2.7
to derive the continuity of the minimal norm function; see Theorem
2.9
.

(c)
Forward vs. backward equations.
In the deterministic ODE setting, by the time translation
s=T-t
, one can derive a backward ODE. Since this translation is invertible, methods to study optimal control problems for ODEs and backward ODEs are essentially the same. But in the stochastic setting, the solution to a BSDE is a stochastic pair with two components;
for example,
\big(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot)\big)
is the solution to BSDE (
1.6
). The second component of the solution to
the concerned system (from the backward perspective) may play different roles in the same problem — that could be a component of the control variable from the forward
perspective, or be a component of the state variable from the backward perspective.
For example, in Problem
(\widetilde{NP})_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
in Section
2
, from the backward perspective
z_{0}(\cdot)
in (
2.12
) is a
part of the state
\big(x(\cdot),z_{0}(\cdot)\big)
, while by viewing (
2.12
) as a SDE
z_{0}(\cdot)
is a part of the control
\big(z_{0}(\cdot),v_{0}(\cdot)\big)
.
For further references, see e.g.,
[
19
,
7
,
17
]
.

To deal with the equivalence of Problems
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
and
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
, we introduce another optimal control problem.
For any
T>0
, consider the following BSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\,\mathrm{d}y(t)=-\big[A^{\top}y(t)+C^{\top}Y(t)\big]\,\mathrm{d}t+Y(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\\
&y(T)=\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

and define a cost functional

\displaystyle J^{T}(\eta;x_{0},\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\big\|B^{\top}y(\cdot)+D^{\top}Y(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}+\langle x_{0},y(0;\eta,T)\rangle-\mathbb{E}\langle\xi,\eta\rangle\,.

We now state the optimal control problem associated with BSDE (
1.6
) and
J^{T}(\cdot)
, which we denote as Problem
(OP)^{T}_{x_{0},\xi}
:

\displaystyle V(T,x_{0},\xi)\triangleq\inf_{\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}J^{T}(\eta;x_{0},\xi)\,.

The following result, from
[
16
, Theorems 4.1, 4.5, 5.1]
, is on the solvability and equivalence of Problems
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
and
(OP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
.

Lemma 1.4
.

For any
T>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
T_{0}\in[0,T]
, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

System (
1.1
) is exactly controllable on
[0,T]
;

(ii)

The following observability inequality holds

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\eta\|^{2}\big]\leq{\cal C}(T)\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\big\|B^{\top}y(t)+D^{\top}Y(t)\big\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\forall\,\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,

(1.7)

where
\big(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot)\big)
solves BSDE (
1.6
), and
{\cal C}(T)
is a positive constant depending on
T
;

(iii)

Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
has a unique minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
;

(iv)

Problem
(OP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
admits a unique solution
\eta^{*}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Moreover, the minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)
to Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
has a representation

u^{*}(\cdot)=B^{\top}y^{*}(\cdot)+D^{\top}Y^{*}(\cdot)\,,

(1.8)

where
\big(y^{*}(\cdot),Y^{*}(\cdot)\big)
solves BSDE (
1.6
) with
y^{*}(T)=\eta^{*}
, and

\displaystyle V(T,x_{0},\xi)=-\frac{1}{2}N^{2}(T,x_{0},\xi)\,.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we construct
the optimal tuple for an auxiliary optimal control problem
(\widetilde{NP})_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
, whose cost functional achieves a minimum equal to the minimal norm of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
. By establishing the continuity of this minimum with respect to the time variable
T
, we demonstrate the equivalence of Problems
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
and
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
. In Section
3
, we focus on the minimal norm function
N(T,x_{0},\xi)
for the stable target
\xi=0
and derive its strict monotonicity, which provides a basis for calculating the minimal time control.

2
Equivalence of minimal time and norm control problems with a general target

In this section, we primarily address the equivalence between minimal time and norm control problems with a general target. In the deterministic setting, previous works such as
[
14
,
13
]
focused on optimal control problems with the stable target
\xi=0
, and utilized an existence-proof method. A key technique in those studies was the time translation method, used to establish the continuity of the minimal norm function to the time variable
T
. However, this technique is not applicable to our problems involving a general target. To address this challenge, we explicitly construct the minimal norm control, which enables us to establish the desired continuity of the minimal norm function. This continuity is essential for deriving the equivalence between minimal time and norm control problems.

For any given
T>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the state to system (
1.1
) can be transferred to
x(T;x_{0},0)
at time
T
. Hence if
\xi=x(T;x_{0},0)
, it is evident that the unique minimal norm of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
is
u^{*}(\cdot)=0
. The following result formalizes this observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under the assumption
(A)
, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\Psi(T)x_{0}\neq\xi
, where
\Psi(\cdot)
solves the following
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
-valued SDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}\Psi(t)=A\Psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+C\Psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\\
&\Psi(0)=I_{n}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

(ii)

u^{*}(\cdot)\neq 0
, where
u^{*}(\cdot)
is the minimal norm control of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
.

(iii)

\eta^{*}\neq 0
, where
\eta^{*}
is the minimizer of Problem
(OP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
.
By contradiction, we suppose that the minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)=0
. Then
x^{*}\big(\cdot;x_{0},u^{*}(\cdot)\big)
, the corresponding optimal state, satisfies
x^{*}\big(t;x_{0},u^{*}(\cdot)\big)=\Psi(t)x_{0}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Note that
\Psi(\cdot)
is invertible; see
[
19
, Theorem 6.14, Chapter 1]
.
Therefore the condition that
\Psi(T)x_{0}\neq\xi
leads to

\displaystyle x^{*}\big(T,x_{0},u^{*}(\cdot)\big)=\Psi(T)x_{0}\neq\xi\,,

which contradicts the fact that
u^{*}(\cdot)
can be used to transfer the state to
\xi
at
T
.
That settles the assertion (ii).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
.
By contradiction, we suppose that
\eta^{*}=0
. Then the unique solution of BSDE (
1.6
)
\big(y^{*}(\cdot),Y^{*}(\cdot)\big)=0
,
and by Lemma
1.4
,
we know that the minimal norm control of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}

\displaystyle u^{*}(\cdot)=B^{\top}y^{*}(\cdot)+D^{\top}Y^{*}(\cdot)=0\,,

that contradicts the statement (ii). Hence, (iii) holds.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
.
Firstly, observability inequality (
1.7
) and the representation of the minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)
in (
1.8
) lead to

0<\mathbb{E}\big[\|\eta^{*}\|^{2}\big]\leq{\cal C}(T)\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\big\|B^{\top}y^{*}(t)+D^{\top}Y^{*}(t)\big\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t={\cal C}(T)\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{*}(t)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(2.2)

which means that the minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)\neq 0
.
By contradiction, suppose that
\Psi(T)x_{0}=\xi
. Then it is obvious that the minimal norm control of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
is

u^{*}(\cdot)=0
, which contradicts (
2.2
).
That completes the proof.

For any given
T>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying (
1.4
),
define

{\cal J}_{N}\triangleq\big\{t\in[T_{0},\infty)\,\big|\,N(t,x_{0},\xi)\leq N\big\}\qquad\forall\,N\geq 0\,.

(2.3)

The following property shows the relationship between
{\cal J}_{\cdot}
and the minimal time control
T(\cdot,x_{0},\xi)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
N\geq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle T(N,x_{0},\xi)=\inf{\cal J}_{N}\,.

Proof.

The proof draws on ideas from
[
13
, Theorem 2.4]
, and we present it by considering two separate cases.

Case I.

{\cal J}_{N}=\emptyset

In this case,
\inf{\cal J}_{N}=\infty
. We claim that

x\big(t;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)\neq\xi\qquad\forall\,t\in[T_{0},\infty)\,,u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{N}\,,

(2.4)

where
{\cal U}_{N}
is given in (
1.3
).

If not, then there would exists
t\geq T_{0}
and
u(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that

x\big(t;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)=\xi\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\|u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq N\,.

(2.5)

By (
2.5
), we know that
u|_{(0,t)}(\cdot)
is an admissible control of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{t}
, and

N(t,x_{0},\xi)\leq\|u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,t;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq N
, which means that
t\in{\cal J}_{N}
. That leads to a contradiction since in this case

{\cal J}_{N}=\emptyset
. Subsequently, the claim (
2.4
) holds, and

\displaystyle T(N,x_{0},\xi)=\infty=\inf{\cal J}_{N}\,.

Case II.

{\cal J}_{N}\neq\emptyset

For any
t\in{\cal J}_{N}
, it follows that
N(t,x_{0},\xi)\leq N
. By the unique solvability of
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{t}
,
we extend the minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)
to
\mathbb{R}^{+}
by setting it to zero outside the interval
(0,t)
,
obtaining
\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)
.
Then we can easily check that

x\big(t;x_{0},\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)\big)=\xi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}=\|u^{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,t;\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N(t,x_{0},\xi)\leq N\,.

(2.6)

By the fact that
t
is arbitrary in
{\cal J}_{N}
, (
2.6
) and the definition of
T(\cdot,x_{0},\xi)
, we find that

T(N,x_{0},\xi)\leq\inf{\cal J}_{N}\,.

(2.7)

To prove the reversion of (
2.7
), define

{\cal T}_{N,x_{0},\xi}\triangleq\big\{t\geq T_{0}\,\big|\,\mbox{there exists some }u(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{N}\mbox{ such that }x\big(t;x_{0},u(\cdot)\big)=\xi\big\}\,.

(2.8)

By (
2.6
), if follows that
{\cal T}_{N,x_{0},\xi}\neq\emptyset
. For any
\widetilde{t}\in{\cal T}_{N,x_{0},\xi}
, there exists a control
\widetilde{u}(\cdot)
such that

x\big(\widetilde{t};x_{0},\widetilde{u}(\cdot)\big)=\xi\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\|\widetilde{u}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq N\,,

(2.9)

by which we can deduce that
\widetilde{u}|_{(0,\widetilde{t}\,)}(\cdot)
is an admissible control of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{\widetilde{t}}
, and

\displaystyle N(\widetilde{t},x_{0},\xi)\leq\big\|\widetilde{u}|_{(0,\widetilde{t}\,)}(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,\widetilde{t};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq N\,.

\big(x(\cdot),z_{0}(\cdot)\big)

Proposition 2.4

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathrm{d}}x^{*}(t)=\big[A_{0}x^{*}(t)+K_{0}z_{0}^{*}(t)+L_{0}v_{0}^{*}(t)\big]\,\mathrm{d}t+z_{0}^{*}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\\

&\qquad\qquad+\big[-K_{0}^{\top}y^{*}(t)-R_{0}Cx^{*}(t)+R_{0}z_{0}^{*}(t)\big]\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\\
&x(T)=\xi\,,y^{*}(0)=\lambda\,,\end{aligned}\right.

with the optimality condition

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\big|\|z_{2}(\cdot)-Cx_{2}(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{2};\mathbb{R}^{n})}-\|z_{1}(\cdot)-Cx_{1}(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{1};\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big|\\

The following result builds a relationship between

and

Proposition 2.10
.

Under the assumption
(A)
and (
1.4
),
for each
N\geq 0
with

, it holds that

N\big(T(N,x_{0},\xi),x_{0},\xi\big)=N\,.

(2.46)

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, it follows that
{\cal J}_{N}\neq\emptyset
. Then by the definition of
{\cal J}_{\cdot}
and
T(\cdot,x_{0},\xi)
,
there exists a sequence
\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset[T_{0},\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}=T(N,x_{0},\xi)\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad N(t_{k},x_{0},\xi)\leq N\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\,.

On the other hand, continuity of
N(\cdot,x_{0},\xi)
derived in Theorem
2.9
implies that

N\big(T(N,x_{0},\xi),x_{0},\xi\big)\leq N\,.

(2.47)

We proceed to establish the reverse of (
2.47
). By contradiction, we suppose that it was not true. Then, by (
2.47
), we would
have
N\big(T(N,x_{0},\xi),x_{0},\xi\big)<N
.
Given the continuity of
N(\cdot,x_{0},\xi)
at
T(N,x_{0},\xi)
,
there exists a constant
\delta_{0}\in\big(0,T(N,x_{0},\xi)-T_{0}\big)
such that
N\big(T(N,x_{0},\xi)-\delta_{0},x_{0},\xi\big)<N
.
By the definition of
{\cal J}_{N}
, this implies that

, which contradicts Lemma
2.2
. This completes the proof of assertion (

).

By applying Proposition
2.10
, we can settle the equivalence of concerned Problems
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
and
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let the assumption
(A)
and (
1.4
) hold, and
suppose that

(N,T)\in\big\{\big(N,T(N,x_{0},\xi)\big)\,\big|\,T(N,x_{0},\xi)\in(T_{0},\infty)\big\}\,.

Then Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}

is equivalent to Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps.

(1)
We claim that: Problems
(NP)^{T}_{x_{0},\xi}
and
(TP)^{N}_{x_{0},\xi}
are solvable.

By Lemma
1.4
, there exists a unique minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
to Problem

(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
and

\displaystyle\|u^{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N(T,x_{0},\xi)\,,\qquad x\big(T;x_{0},u^{*}(\cdot)\big)=\xi\,.

These two statements lead to the solvability of Problem
(NP)^{T}_{x_{0},\xi}
.
Extend
u^{*}(\cdot)
to
[0,\infty)
by setting it to be zero on
(T,\infty)
, and denote the extension by
\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)
.
It is obvious that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}=\|u^{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N(T,x_{0},\xi)

\displaystyle\mbox{and}

\displaystyle x\big(T(N,x_{0},\xi);x_{0},\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)\big)=x\big(T;x_{0},\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)\big)=\xi\,.

Proposition
2.10
then is used to deduce that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N(T,x_{0},\xi)=N(T(N,x_{0},\xi),x_{0},\xi)=N\,.

Thus, Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
has a minimal time control.

(2)
By using
T=T(N,x_{0},\xi)
,
for an arbitrarily fixed minimal time control
u_{*}(\cdot)
of Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
, we know that
u_{*}|_{(0,T)}(\cdot)
, the restriction of
u_{*}(\cdot)
to the interval
(0,T)
, is an admissible control of Problem

(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
,
i.e.
,

x\big(T,x_{0},u_{*}|_{(0,T)}(\cdot)\big)=\xi\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|u_{*}|_{(0,T)}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq\|u_{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq N\,.

(2.48)

Then

N\big(T(N,x_{0},\xi),x_{0},\xi\big)=N(T,x_{0},\xi)\leq\|u_{*}|_{(0,T)}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq N\,,

(2.49)

which, together with (
2.46
) in Proposition
2.10
, yields

N(T,x_{0},\xi)=\|u_{*}|_{(0,T)}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N\,.

(2.50)

Hence,
u_{*}|_{(0,T)}(\cdot)
is a minimal norm contro of Problem
(NP)^{T}_{x_{0},\xi}
.

(3)

For the minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)
of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
, denote by
\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)
the zero extension of
u^{*}(\cdot)

on
[0,\infty)
. Then
\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)
is an admissible control of Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N(T,x_{0},\xi)}
,
i.e.
,

(2.51)

By the second conclusion of (
2.51
) and (
2.46
), we know that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N(T,x_{0},\xi)=N(T(N,x_{0},\xi),x_{0},\xi)=N\,,

which yields that
\widetilde{u}^{*}(\cdot)
is a minimal time control of Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
.
That completes the proof.

3
Equivalence of minimal time and norm control problems with zero target

In this section, we consider simplified versions of Problems
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
and
(NP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{T}
with target
\xi=0
.
To shorten notations, we will respectively write
(TP)_{x_{0}}^{N}
,
(NP)_{x_{0}}^{T}
,

T(N,x_{0})
and
N(T,x_{0})
, instead of
(TP)_{x_{0},0}^{N}
,
(NP)_{x_{0},0}^{T}
,

T(N,x_{0},0)
and
N(T,x_{0},0)
.

When
x_{0}=0
, Lemma
2.1
implies
that for any
T\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the corresponding minimal norm control
u^{*}(\cdot)=0
, and
consequently
N(T,0)=0
.
Hence, we focus only on the nontrivial case where
x_{0}\neq 0
.
The following result concerns the regularity of
N(\cdot,x_{0})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that the assumption
(A)
holds, and
x_{0}\neq 0
. Then

(i)

N(\cdot,x_{0}):\,\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is decreasing;

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to 0}N(T,x_{0})=+\infty\,.

Proof.

(1) Verification of (i).

By Lemma
2.1
, we know that for any
T>0
, the minimal norm
N(T,x_{0})=\|u^{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}>0
.

Suppose that
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
T_{1}<T_{2}
and
u_{1}(\cdot)
resp.
u_{2}(\cdot)
are minimal norm controls of Problems

(NP)_{x_{0}}^{T_{1}}
resp.
(NP)_{x_{0}}^{T_{2}}
.
By setting

\displaystyle\bar{u}(t)=\left\{\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{1}(t)&t\in[0,T_{1}]\,,\\
0&t\in(T_{1},T_{2}]\,,\end{array}\right.

we know that
\bar{u}(\cdot)
is an admissible control to Problem
(NP)_{x_{0}}^{T_{2}}
, and

\displaystyle N(T_{2},x_{0})\leq\|\bar{u}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{2};\mathbb{R}^{m})}=\|u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N(T_{1},x_{0})\,.

That proves the assertion (i).

(2) Verification of (ii).
By contradiction, suppose that there exists a sequence
\{T_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset(0,1)
,
T_{k}\downarrow 0
, and

\displaystyle\lim_{T_{k}\to 0}N(T_{k},x_{0})=\widehat{N}\in(0,+\infty)\,.

For any
k
,
suppose that
u_{k}(\cdot)
is the minimal norm control of Problem
(NP)_{x_{0}}^{T_{k}}
.
By the monotonicity of
N(\cdot,x_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{k}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{k};\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N(T_{k},x_{0})\leq\widehat{N}\,.

For any
\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{1}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by applying Itô’s formula to
\big\langle x\big(t;x_{0},u_{k}(\cdot)\big),y(t;\eta,T_{1})\big\rangle
and setting

\Phi(\cdot;\eta,T_{1})=B^{\top}y(\cdot;\eta,T_{1})+D^{\top}Y(\cdot;\eta,T_{1})\,.

(3.1)

we arrive at

\langle x_{0},y(0;\eta,T_{1})\rangle+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{k}}\langle u_{k}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t=0\,.

(3.2)

Utilizing Hölder’s inequality, the fact that
y(\cdot;\eta,T_{1}),Y(\cdot;\eta,T_{1})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{1};\mathbb{R}^{n})

(which implies that
\Phi(\cdot;\eta,T_{1})
defined in (
3.1
) belongs to
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})
) and

T_{k}\downarrow 0
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{k}}\langle u_{k}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\|u_{k}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{k};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\|\Phi(\cdot;\eta,T_{1})\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{k};\mathbb{R}^{m})}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{N}\|\Phi(\cdot;\eta,T_{1})\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{k};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\to 0\,.

Hence, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) imply that

\displaystyle\langle x_{0},y(0;\eta,T_{1})\rangle=0\qquad\forall\,\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{1}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,

which leads to
x_{0}=0
contradicting the assumption
x_{0}\neq 0
, and then settles the assertion (ii).

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

Under the assumption
(A)
, for any
x_{0}\neq 0
, by Lemma
3.1

(i)
and the nonnegativity of
N(\cdot,x_{0})
, we can define

\displaystyle\widehat{N}(x_{0})=\lim_{T\to\infty}N(T,x_{0})\,.

Furthermore, Theorem
2.9
and Lemma
3.1
imply that

N(\cdot,x_{0}):\,\mathbb{R}^{+}\to[\widehat{N}(x_{0}),\infty)
is continuous.

(2)
When
\xi\neq 0
, the decreasing property of
N(\cdot;x_{0},\xi)
cannot be established, as demonstrated by the following example.
For the system (
1.1
) with

\displaystyle A=1,\,B=(0,1),\,C=0,\,D=(1,0)\,,x_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\,,\xi=-1\,,

we can follow the procedure outlined in Remark
2.8
to obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma(t)=\frac{1-\exp\{-2(T-t)\}}{2}\,,\,\,\big(\varphi(t),\beta(t)\big)=\big(\exp\{-(T-t)\},0\big)\,,\,\,\Pi(t)=\exp\{-t\}\,,\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,

and subsequently,

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}=\frac{2}{\exp\{-2T\}-1}\big(\exp\{-T\}-\frac{1}{2}\big)\,,\,\,y(t)=\exp\{-t\}\lambda^{*}\,,\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,.

The optimal norm control of Problem
(NP)^{T}_{x_{0},\xi}
can then be derived by (
2.31
) as

\displaystyle u^{*}(t)=\big(z^{*}(t),v^{*}(t)\big)=\big(0,\exp\{-t\}\lambda^{*}\big)\,,\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,.

Thus, the minimal norm function is given by

\displaystyle N(T,x_{0},\xi)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-\exp\{-2T\}}}\Big|\exp\{-T\}-\frac{1}{2}\Big|\,,

from which we observe that
N(\cdot,x_{0},\xi)
is decreasing on
(0,\ln 2)
and then incresing on
(\ln 2,\infty)
.

Under the setting
\xi=0
,
the following result improves the result in Proposition
2.10
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let the assumption
(A)
hold and
x_{0}\neq 0
, and suppose that
N>\widehat{N}(x_{0})
and
0<T<\infty
.
Then, the following two conditions are equivalent

(i)

N=N(T,x_{0})\,;

(ii)

T=T(N,x_{0})\,.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
.
Suppose that
u^{*}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the minimal norm control to Problem
(NP)_{x_{0}}^{T}
such
that

\displaystyle\|u^{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}=N(T,x_{0})=N\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad x\big(T;x_{0},u^{*}(\cdot)\big)=0\,,

which means that

T(N,x_{0})\leq T<\infty\,,

(3.4)

and hence, Problem
(TP)_{x_{0}}^{N}
admits admissible controls.

Set
T_{0}=T(N,x_{0})
.
By the definition of
T(N,x_{0})
in (
1.2
),
there exists
a decreasing sequence
\{T_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset(T_{0},T_{0}+1)
and a sequence

\{u_{k}(\cdot)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset{\cal U}_{N}
such that
x\big(T_{k};x_{0},u_{k}(\cdot)\big)=0
, which yields that there exists a subsequence of
\{u_{k}(\cdot)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
denoted in the same way satisfying

\displaystyle u_{k}(\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{w}}}{{\longrightarrow}}u_{*}(\cdot)\qquad\mbox{in }L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})\,,

and

\|u_{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq\mathop{\underline{\rm lim}}_{k\to\infty}\|u_{k}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq N\,.

(3.5)

This shows that
u_{*}(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{N}
.

For any
\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{k}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, applying Itô’s formula to
\big\langle x\big(t;x_{0},u_{k}(\cdot)\big),y(t;\eta,T_{k})\big\rangle
yields

0=\langle x_{0},y(0;\eta,T_{k})\rangle+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{k}}\langle u_{k}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{k})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(3.6)

where
\big(y(\cdot;\eta,T_{k}),Y(\cdot;\eta,T_{k})\big)
solves BSDE (
1.6
) with
T=T_{k}
, and

\Phi(\cdot;\eta,T_{k})=B^{\top}y(\cdot;\eta,T_{k})+D^{\top}Y(\cdot;\eta,T_{k})
.
To establish the convergence of (
3.6
), we estimate each term on the right-hand side. Specifically, we decompose the integral term
into three components:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{k}}\langle u_{k}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{k})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\langle u_{k}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{k})-\Phi(t;\eta,T_{0})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\langle u_{k}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{0})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{k}}\langle u_{k}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{k})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{3}I_{i}\,.

By stability and linearity of BSDEs, we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\cdot;\eta,T_{k})-\Phi(\cdot;\eta,T_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{0};\mathbb{R}^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}\|y(T_{0};\eta,T_{k})-y(T_{0};\eta,T_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(3.7)

\displaystyle={\cal C}\,\mathbb{E}\big[\|y(T_{0};\eta,T_{k})-\eta\|^{2}\big]\,.

Based on BSDE (
1.6
), we proceed to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|y(T_{0};\eta,T_{k})-\eta\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|-\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{k}}\big[A^{\top}y(t;\eta,T_{k})+C^{\top}Y(t;\eta,T_{k})\big]\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{k}}Y(t;\eta,T_{k})\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\Big\|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}(T_{k}-T_{0})\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{k}}\big[\|y(t;\eta,T_{k})\|^{2}+\|Y(t;\eta,T_{k})\|^{2}\big]\,\mathrm{d}t+{\cal C}\,\mathbb{E}\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{k}}\|Y(t;\eta,T_{k})\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

where
{\cal C}
is independent of
k
. This estimate, together with (
3.7
) and the following

\|y(\cdot;\eta,T_{k})\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T_{k}];\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|Y(\cdot;\eta,T_{k})\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{k};\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq{\cal C}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

(3.8)

where
{\cal C}
is independent of
k
,
implies that

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\cdot;\eta,T_{k})-\Phi(\cdot;\eta,T_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T_{0};\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq{\cal C}\,\mathbb{E}\big[\|y(T_{0};\eta,T_{k})-\eta\|^{2}\big]\to 0\,.

Thus,
I_{1}\to 0
. Similarly, we conclude by (
3.8
) that
I_{3}\to 0
. The weak convergence of
\{u_{k}(\cdot)\}
yields

I_{2}\to\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\langle u_{*}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{0})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t
.
In a similar vein, we can settle
\langle x_{0},y(0;\eta,T_{k})\rangle\to\langle x_{0},y(0;\eta,T_{0})\rangle
. Combining (
3.6
) and the above convergence results, we
have

0=\langle x_{0},y(0;\eta,T_{0})\rangle+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\langle u_{*}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{0})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

(3.9)

Additionally, by applying Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle x\big(T_{0};x_{0},u_{*}(\cdot)\big),\eta\big\rangle=\langle x_{0},y(0;\eta,T_{0})\rangle+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\langle u_{*}(t),\Phi(t;\eta,T_{0})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

which, together with (
3.9
) and the fact that
\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is arbitrary, leads to

x\big(T_{0};x_{0},u_{*}(\cdot)\big)=x\big(T(N,x_{0});x_{0},u_{*}(\cdot)\big)=0\,.

(3.10)

Hence, a combination of (
3.5
) and (
3.10
) yields that
u_{*}(\cdot)
is a minimal time control of
Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
.
Then, the optimality of
u_{*}(\cdot)
to Problem
(TP)_{x_{0},\xi}^{N}
implies

\displaystyle N\big(T(N,x_{0}),x_{0}\big)\leq\|u_{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T(N,x_{0}));\mathbb{R}^{m}}\leq N=N(T,x_{0})\,.

Since
N(\cdot,x_{0})
is decreasing, we have

T(N,x_{0})\geq T\,.

(3.11)

Hence, the condition (ii) can be derived by combining with (
3.4
) and (
3.11
).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
.
Directly derived by Proposition
2.10
.
That completes the proof.

Corollary 3.4
.

Under the assumption
(A)
,
for any
x_{0}\neq 0
,
N(\cdot,x_{0})
is strictly decreasing on
(0,T_{sup})
, where

\displaystyle T_{sup}=\inf\big\{T>0\,\big|\,N(T,x_{0})=\widehat{N}(x_{0})\big\}\,.

Moreover, the minimal time function
T(\cdot,x_{0})
is strictly decreasing and continuous on
(\widehat{N}(x_{0}),\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
0<T_{1}<T_{2}<T_{sup}
, and we know by Lemma
3.1
that

\displaystyle N(T_{1},x_{0})\geq N(T_{2},x_{0})\,.

We make use of contradiction to derive the assertion. Suppose that
N(T_{1},x_{0})=N(T_{2},x_{0})
,
and set

\displaystyle N_{0}\triangleq N(T_{1},x_{0})=N(T_{2},x_{0})>\widehat{N}(x_{0})\,.

By Proposition
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle T_{1}=T(N_{0},x_{0})=T_{2}\,,

which contradicts our assumption
T_{1}<T_{2}
.

The strict monotonicity of
T(\cdot,x_{0})
can then be established by using the strict monotonicity of
N(\cdot,x_{0})
and Proposition

3.3
.
That completes the proof.
