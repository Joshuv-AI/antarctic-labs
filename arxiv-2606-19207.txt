Extrema of microscopically slowed-down Gaussian fields
Zuodi Xie

Abstract
. We introduce a family of Gaussian fields whose covariance structure exhibits an inhomogeneous, microscopic slowdown and it interpolates between a log \log profile (for a certain interpolation parameter α = 0 \alpha=0 ) and a log ⁡ log \log\log profile (when the interpolation parameter is α = 1 / 2 \alpha=1/2 ). We consider both one dimensional such objects (which we call Branching Brownian Motions in a cooling environment ) as well as higher dimensional, spatial fields. We identify the correct centering of the maximum at time T T and prove tightness of the recentered maximum. While the exponent in the first-order growth varies linearly with α \alpha , giving a leading order of T 1 − α T^{1-\alpha} , the second-order correction exhibits a phase transition at α = 1 / 3 \alpha=1/3 .

1.
Introduction

The study of extrema of log-correlated random fields has advanced rapidly in the past decade; see, for example, the notes
[
6
,
11
,
39
]
. This direction is important for several reasons. First, in one dimension, branching Brownian motion (BBM) and its connection with the Fisher–KPP equation provide a fundamental setting in which extremal behavior plays a central role; see
[
17
]
. Second, the high points of log-correlated Gaussian fields (for example, Gaussian free field) are closely tied to Gaussian multiplicative chaos (GMC), which is a fundamental and universal model in random geometry; see the reviews
[
38
]
and
[
9
]
. For example, thick points and their Hausdorff dimension are intimately related to subcritical GMC; see
[
30
]
. In addition, the law of the recentered maximum and the limiting extremal process are themselves closely connected to GMC-type objects. For example, in
[
33
]
, a critical GMC-type object, namely the derivative martingale, governs the random shift of the maximum, while in
[
10
]
, the intermediate high points for discrete Gaussian free fields converge, after rescaling, to a GMC-like measure.

Substantial progress has also been made in the study of extrema of log-correlated models with time-inhomogeneous variance profiles. In BBM, most of the existing literature concerns the “
macroscopic
” regime, where the variance profile varies on the time scale
T
, typically through a form such as
\sigma(t)=\widetilde{\sigma}(t/T)
with
\widetilde{\sigma}
bounded on
[0,1]
. These models remain log-correlated and preserve many of the qualitative features of the homogeneous case. For classical BBM, Bramson
[
14
]
proved convergence in law of the recentered maximum, and the extremal process was later identified in
[
4
]
and
[
3
]
. For macroscopic time-inhomogeneous BBM, analogous results have been obtained for two-speed profiles and for more general classes of variance functions; see, for example,
[
12
,
13
]
. In the decreasing-variance case, a macroscopic slowdown with the characteristic Airy-type
T^{1/3}
correction was discovered in
[
26
]
and further refined in
[
34
]
. In particular,
[
34
]
developed Airy PDE estimates for probabilities associated with slowed-down Brownian motion, leading to the precise centering of the maximum and to a randomly shifted Gumbel limit. One of our objectives is to adapt this Airy-estimate method to variance profiles that vary on “
microscopic
” time scales.

Parallel developments have been established for branching random walks (BRW) with time-inhomogeneous variances; see, for instance,
[
25
,
32
,
37
,
35
]
. Natural higher-dimensional analogues of these BBM and BRW models arise in the form of log-correlated Gaussian fields, such as the scale-inhomogeneous Gaussian free field; see, for example,
[
5
,
36
,
27
]
. More broadly, time-inhomogeneous Gaussian models such as those in
[
26
,
34
]
can also provide useful intuition for more complicated non-Gaussian systems, see
[
8
,
7
,
29
,
24
]
.

For log-correlated Gaussian fields on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a useful approach to the tightness of the recentered maximum was developed in
[
1
]
, where two auxiliary Gaussian comparison fields were introduced: the mollified branching Brownian motion and the Brownian sheet. This comparison strategy was inspired by earlier arguments for the discrete Gaussian free field, notably
[
16
,
15
]
.

The present paper, however, concerns a different regime. Rather than a
macroscopic
time-inhomogeneity, we study a
microscopically
time-inhomogeneous setting, in which the variance profile varies on “
short time scales
”. This leads to a covariance scale substantially smaller than that in the usual log-correlated setting, and the resulting extremal behavior falls outside the standard macroscopic universality classes. Specifically, we introduce a class of Gaussian fields whose variance structure interpolates between log and
\log\log
correlation. For these weak-covariance models, the leading orders of their maxima differ from those obtained for i.i.d. models, and the second orders exhibit a phase transition in the
(\log)^{1/3}
-covariance case.

Motivation.

The present work is primarily motivated by the desire to study high peak values of the critical two-dimensional stochastic heat flow (SHF), which was constructed in
[
18
]
. See
[
19
,
20
]
for reviews. The Critical SHF is a positive random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(hence not Gaussian), that exhibits a log-correlations. Even though it is
shown not to be an exponential of a Gaussian field (i.e. not a
GMC)
[
21
]
, it appears to share some GMC features. For example its moments on small balls blow up at the same rate as those of a GMC associated with a Gaussian field with
\log\log
covariance structure
[
31
]
and there is a conditional GMC structure
[
23
]
. Furthermore,
approximate
multiplicative structures, have been observed at criticality
[
28
]
, quasi-criticality
[
21
]
and sub-criticality
[
24
]
.
Therefore, we hope that extremal behaviour of the weakly correlated Gaussian models studied here, especially that of the
\log\log
-Gaussian fields, can provide useful insights into the extremal landscape of the SHF. Conversely, the results obtained here may help clarify the distinctions between the SHF and
\log\log
-GMCs.

We mention that a similar comparison has led to important progress in the study of extremal of the two-dimensional
subcritical
DPRE. In the recent work
[
24
]
, the leading-order asymptotics of the maximum of the rescaled logarithmic partition function for
subcritical
two-dimensional DPRE, viewed as a random field, were determined and the leading term in that asymptotic was shown to admit a form reminiscent to the leading order of the maximum of the macroscopically slowed-down BBM obtained in
[
26
,
34
]
.

1.1.
Main Results

We first construct a family of Gaussian fields on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\geq 2
, whose covariance structure interpolates between the
\log\log
-correlated and log-correlated regimes. Specifically, let
W(\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}t)
be space-time white noise on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
. For
t>0
, set

q_{t}(x):=\frac{g_{1/(4t)}(x)}{\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{1/(4t)}(x)^{2}\text{d}x}}=\left(\frac{4t}{\pi}\right)^{d/4}\exp\{-2t|x|_{2}^{2}\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
g_{1/(4t)}(x):=(2t/\pi)^{d/2}e^{-2t|x|_{2}^{2}}
is the heat kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
|x|_{2}:=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i})^{2}}
is the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For any
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, set

(1.1)

I_{\alpha}(t):=\int_{0}^{\log(1+t)}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

and define the field
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
by the stochastic integral

(1.2)

X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z):=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)}\,q_{t}(z-u)\,W(\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}t),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

with
W
the
d+1
white noise and

I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t){:=}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}I_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{1}{1+t}\bigl(1+\log(1+t)\bigr)^{-2\alpha}.

By the Itô isometry for white-noise integrals, we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle:=\text{Cov}\bigl(X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{1}),X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{2})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{-2}}I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q_{t}(z_{1}-u)q_{t}(z_{2}-u)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{-2}}e^{-t\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

By Proposition
A.2
, the covariance structure of
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\cdot)
has asymptotics, for all
z_{1},\ z_{2}\in[0,1]^{d}
with
\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}
sufficiently small,

k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})\asymp\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}}\right)^{1-2\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in(0,1/2),

and

k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})\asymp\log\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}}\right),\qquad\alpha=1/2,

where
f\asymp g
means that there exist positive constants
c
and
C
such that
cg\leq f\leq Cg
. Thus
\{X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\cdot)\}
indeed achieves the interpolation between log correlation with
\alpha=0
, and
\log\log
correlation, with
\alpha=1/2
.

By Proposition
A.3
, we have

(1.4)

\mathbb{E}\bigl[(X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{1})-X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{2}))^{2}\bigr]\leq C\,\vartheta_{\alpha}(\varepsilon)\,\frac{\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}}{\varepsilon},

where

\vartheta_{\alpha}(\varepsilon):=(1+\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon})^{-2\alpha}.

Remark 1.1.

By the
L^{2}
distance estimate (
1.4
) and the moments for Gaussian random variables, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigl[(X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{1})-X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{2}))^{2h}\bigr]\leq C\left(\vartheta_{\alpha}(\varepsilon)\,\frac{\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{h},\quad\forall h\geq 2.

Thus the Kolmogorov continuity theorem implies that, for each fixed
\varepsilon>0
, the Gaussian field
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
is a.s. continuous. As a result, we have a.s.

\sup_{x\in[0,1]^{d}}X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x)=\sup_{x\in[0,1)^{d}}X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x)

Before introducing our main results, we define the functions needed to specify the centerings for the maxima of our models. For any
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, define

(1.5)

\sigma_{\alpha}(t):=\sqrt{2}\,(1+2t)^{-\alpha},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

which appears in the covariance estimates (
A.8
) for
\{X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\cdot)\}
. Then define

(1.6)

\eta_{\alpha}(t):=\alpha_{1}\,2^{-1/3}(\sqrt{2d})^{2/3}\,|\sigma_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)|^{2/3}\sigma_{\alpha}(t)^{1/3},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

where
-\alpha_{1}=-2.33811\ldots
denotes the principal eigenvalue of the Airy operator
Lu=u^{\prime\prime}-xu
on
[0,\infty)
; see (
B.8
) for details. A direct computation yields

(1.7)

\eta_{\alpha}(t)=c_{\eta}(1+2t)^{-\alpha-2/3},\qquad c_{\eta}:=\alpha_{1}\alpha^{2/3}(\sqrt{2d})^{2/3}2^{5/6}.

We then set

(1.8)

A_{\alpha}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{2d}\,\sigma_{\alpha}(s)-\frac{\eta_{\alpha}(s)}{\sqrt{2d}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

In our main results, the centering of the maximum of
\{X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x):x\in[0,1]{{}^{d}}\}
is given by
A_{\alpha}(T)
, where
T:=\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
. For large
T
, direct computation gives

(1.9)

A_{\alpha}(T)=c_{1}(\alpha,d)\,T^{1-\alpha}-\begin{cases}\displaystyle c_{2}(\alpha,d)T^{1/3-\alpha}+{O(1)},&\text{if }\alpha<1/3,\\
c_{2}(\alpha,d)\log T+{O(1)},&\text{if }\alpha=1/3,\\
{O(1)},&\text{if }1/3<\alpha\leq 1/2,\end{cases}

with

(1.10)

c_{1}(\alpha,d):=\frac{2^{1-\alpha}\sqrt{d}}{1-\alpha},

and

(1.11)

c_{2}(\alpha,d):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{2^{1/3-\alpha}c_{\eta}}{2\sqrt{2d}(1/3-\alpha)}&\text{ if }\alpha<\frac{1}{3},\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{c_{\eta}}{2\sqrt{2d}}&\text{ if }\alpha=\frac{1}{3}.\end{cases}

Here is our main result for the maximum of
\{X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x):x\in[0,1]^{d}\}
.

Theorem 1.2 (Tightness of recentered maximum).

Let
T:=\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
and define
\{X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x):x\in[0,1]^{d}\}
by (
1.2
). For any
d\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, there exist constants
C>0
,
c>0
,
T_{*}>1
, and
\lambda_{*}>1
such that for all
T>T_{*}
and
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
,

P\left(\left|\max_{x\in[0,1]^{d}}X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x)-A_{\alpha}(T)\right|\geq\lambda\right)\leq Ce^{-c\lambda},

where
A_{\alpha}(T)
is defined in (
1.8
).

Remark 1.3.

From the expansion (
1.9
) of
A_{\alpha}(T)
, we find a continuous phase transition at
\alpha=1/3
, which is due to the integral expression (
1.8
). Specifically, the second-order term is of order
T^{1/3-\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1/3)
, of order
\log T
when
\alpha=1/3
, and is absorbed into the
O(1)
term when
\alpha>1/3
. Note also that the coefficients
c_{2}(\alpha,d)
in (
1.11
) blow up when
\alpha\uparrow\frac{1}{3}
, while
c_{2}(1/3,d)
remains finite.

As an analogue of the Gaussian field
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\geq 2
, we introduce a
microscopically slowed-down BBM: At time
t=0
, there is a single particle whose position evolves according to

Y_{t}^{\alpha}:=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)\text{d}B_{s}=\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+2t)^{-\alpha}\text{d}B_{s},\qquad t\geq 0,

where
\sigma_{\alpha}
is the same as (
1.5
). The particle carries an independent exponential clock with parameter
1
. When the clock rings, the particle splits into two offsprings, and each offspring subsequently evolves as an independent copy of the same process, with its own independent
\mathrm{Exp}(1)
clock. This branching mechanism is iterated until the terminal time
T
. Note that the variance profile
\sigma(\cdot)
decreases drastically as time goes. Especially, when
t
is close to the terminal time
T
, it is only of scale
O(T^{-\alpha})
. We will call this branching explorers in a cooling medium. See Figure
1
for an illustration. Unlike macroscopically slowed-down BBM studied in
[
26
]
and
[
34
]
, whose variance profile decreases effectively only on the macroscopic time scale
O(T)
(
\sigma(\cdot)
is a function of
t/T
there), the microscopic model here exhibits a variance profile that changes on a much finer scale.

.

Let us introduce some notations for clarity. We write
\mathcal{N}_{t}
for the set of particles alive at time
t\in[0,T]
, and
N_{t}:=|\mathcal{N}_{t}|
for the number of such particles. For any
v\in\mathcal{N}_{t}
, we let
\{Y_{s}^{\alpha,v}:\ s\in[0,t]\}
denote the trajectory of particle
v
before time
t
. It is well known that the number of BBM particles satisfies

(1.12)

\displaystyle E[N_{t}]

\displaystyle=e^{t},

\displaystyle P(N_{t}=n)

\displaystyle=e^{-t}(1-e^{-t})^{n-1},\qquad n\geq 1,

\displaystyle P\bigl(N_{t}\leq e^{ct}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-(1-c)t},\qquad\forall\,c<1.

See, for example, the proof of Lemma 2.2 in
[
22
]
. The following theorem shows that the rightmost positions of our microscopically slowed-down BBM exhibit the same centering as the higher-dimensional Gaussian fields introduced above, with dimension
d=1
.

Theorem 1.4.

Fix any
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. There exist constants
C>0
,
c>0
,
T_{*}>1
, and
\lambda_{*}>1
such that for all
T>T_{*}
and
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
,

(1.13)

P\left(\left|\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}-A_{\alpha}(T)\right|\geq\lambda\right)\leq Ce^{-c\lambda},

where
A_{\alpha}(\cdot)
is given in (
1.8
) with
d=1
.

To end this subsection, we compare the leading-order behavior of our microscopically slowed-down BBM with that of the corresponding i.i.d. model. Consider
e^{T}
i.i.d. centered Gaussian random variables whose common variance matches that of a single BBM particle at time
T
, i.e.,

\operatorname{Var}(Y_{T})=\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(1+2T)^{1-2\alpha}-1}{1-2\alpha},&\alpha\in(0,1/2),\\[6.0pt]
\log(1+2T),&\alpha=1/2.\end{cases}

By adapting the proof of Proposition 4.1.1 in
[
6
]
, one obtains that the maximum of the i.i.d. model has leading order
\sqrt{2T\,\operatorname{Var}(Y_{T})}
. In particular, for
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, it is

2^{1-\alpha}(1-2\alpha)^{-1/2}T^{1-\alpha},

whereas for
\alpha=1/2
it is of order
\sqrt{T\log T}
. From (
1.9
), the leading order of the maximum of our BBM models is given by

\frac{2^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}T^{1-\alpha}.

Thus, for
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, the maximum of our BBM remains on the same scale as that of the i.i.d. model, but with a strictly smaller leading constant. By contrast, in the critical case
\alpha=1/2
, corresponding to the
\log\log
-correlated regime, the BBM maximum is only of order
T^{1/2}
, which is substantially smaller than
(T\log T)^{1/2}
in the i.i.d. setting. A similar discussion also applies to the higher-dimensional models
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
.

1.2.
Main ideas for the proofs

In this subsection, we explain the main ideas behind the analysis of the maxima of the micro-models introduced above.

Scale decomposition: From micro-BBM to macro-BBM.
We first give a heuristic explanation of why
A_{\alpha}(T)
in (
1.8
) is the natural centering for the maximum of our
microscopically
slowed-down BBM. We also explain the mechanism behind the phase transition in the second-order term in (
1.9
). The key observation is a scale-similarity property of the model, which allows us to compare the dynamics on successive time blocks with the
macroscopically
slowed-down BBM studied in
[
26
,
34
]
.

Consider a macroscopically slowed-down BBM with variance profile

(1.14)

\sigma(t)=\widetilde{\sigma}(t/\mathbf{T}),\qquad t\in[0,\mathbf{T}],

where
\mathbf{T}
is the terminal time and

\widetilde{\sigma}\in C^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{R}_{+})
is nonincreasing. Let
M_{\mathbf{T}}
denote the rightmost position of this BBM at time
\mathbf{T}
. By
[
34
]
,

(1.15)

M_{\mathbf{T}}=c_{1}\mathbf{T}-c_{2}\mathbf{T}^{1/3}-c_{3}\log\mathbf{T}+O_{\mathbf{T}}(1),

where the constants
\{c_{i}:i=1,2,3\}
are determined in
[
34
]
, and
O_{\mathbf{T}}(1)
is tight as
\mathbf{T}\to\infty
.

We now turn to our microscopically slowed-down model, whose variance profile is

\sigma_{\alpha}(t)=\sqrt{2}(1+2t)^{-\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in(0,1/2].

We decompose the trajectory of a single
Y^{\alpha}
-particle according to a geometrically increasing time mesh. This decomposition reveals the connection between the motion on each time block and a macroscopically slowed-down Brownian motion.

Fix
H>0
large, and assume for simplicity that
T=(2^{M}H-1)/2
for some large
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Define

T_{m}:=(2^{m}H-1)/2,\qquad m=0,\ldots,M,

so that
T_{M}=T
. A
Y^{\alpha}
-particle evolves as

Y_{t}^{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}B_{s}=\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{2}(1+2s)^{-\alpha}\,\text{d}B_{s}.

For every
m\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
, we have

\{Y_{T_{m}+t}^{\alpha}-Y_{T_{m}}^{\alpha}:t\in[0,T_{m+1}-T_{m}]\}\overset{d}{=}\{(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}Z_{t}^{(m)}:t\in[0,2^{m-1}H]\},

where

Z_{t}^{(m)}\overset{d}{=}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{\sigma}_{m}(s)\,\text{d}B_{s},\qquad t\in[0,2^{m-1}H],

with

(1.16)

\hat{\sigma}_{m}(t):=\sqrt{2}\left(1+\frac{t}{2^{m-1}H}\right)^{-\alpha},\qquad t\in[0,2^{m-1}H].

The profile
\hat{\sigma}_{m}
in (
1.16
) is exactly of the form (
1.14
), with the terminal time
\mathbf{T}
replaced by
2^{m-1}H
. Thus, after extracting the spatial factor
(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}
, the motion on the
m
-th dyadic block falls into the macroscopically slowed-down regime of
[
34
]
.

Using the macroscopic centering (
1.15
), the leading contribution from the
m
-th block is of order

(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}(2^{m-1}H),

and summing these terms over
m=0,\ldots,M-1
gives a contribution of order
T^{1-\alpha}
. The Airy correction from the
m
-th block is of order

(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}(2^{m-1}H)^{1/3}\asymp(2^{m}H)^{1/3-\alpha}.

Therefore,

\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(2^{m}H)^{1/3-\alpha}\asymp\begin{cases}T^{1/3-\alpha},&\alpha<1/3,\\
\log T,&\alpha=1/3,\\
1,&\alpha>1/3.\end{cases}

This explains the phase transition at
\alpha=1/3
in (
1.9
). The logarithmic correction in each macroscopic block is summable after multiplication by
(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}
, and hence contributes only to the
O(1)
error when
\alpha>0
.

Carrying out this decomposition rigorously leads precisely to the form
A_{\alpha}(T)
in (
1.8
), up to additional
O(1)
terms. In the proof of the lower bound in Theorem
1.4
, specifically in Subsection
2.2
, we make this block-decomposition argument rigorous. The same scale-decomposition idea can also be used for more general micro-models.

Barrier estimates: From macro-Airy PDE to micro-Airy PDE.
To prove the upper-tail bound for the recentered maximum of the micro-BBM, Proposition
2.1
, and the lower-tail bound for the recentered maximum of the Gaussian fields
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
, Proposition
3.5
, we use a barrier argument, a standard tool in the study of maxima of branching processes. Roughly speaking, a barrier is a deterministic curve in time that tracks the typical location of extremal particles. It is chosen so that particles contributing to the maximum are expected to remain below the barrier until close to the terminal time. A central step in the analysis is therefore to estimate the probability that a particle trajectory crosses a prescribed barrier.

As in
[
34
]
, a natural choice of barrier in our setting is

b(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{2d}\ \sigma_{\alpha}(s)-\frac{\eta_{\alpha}(s)+r(s)}{\sqrt{2d}}\right)\text{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0,

where the main term

\sqrt{2d}\ \sigma_{\alpha}(s)-\frac{\eta_{\alpha}(s)}{\sqrt{2d}}

is the infinitesimal contribution to the centering in (
1.9
), and
r
is a rectifying function introduced to control the barrier-crossing probability. For example, in the study of upper tail for micro-BBM in Subsection
2.1
, we take
r
in (
2.7
) which is negative and thus slightly rises the barrier. The decreasing variance profile (
1.5
) then leads, through Brownian crossing-probability estimates, to an Airy-type PDE; see Subsection
B.1
.

There is, however, an important difference between the Airy PDE used in
[
34
]
and the PDE used here. In
[
34
, (A.4)]
, the coefficients depend on the macroscopic variable
t/T
, and therefore their derivatives are of order
O(1/T)
. By contrast, in our equation (
B.6
), the coefficients are functions of the microscopic time variable itself, rather than of
t/T
. Their derivatives must therefore be estimated directly.

This distinction is important for the rectifying function
r
appeared in the Airy PDE
B.13
. In the setting of
[
34
]
, the corresponding rectifying function does not significantly affect the Airy estimate, because the derivatives of the coefficients are on the
T^{-1}
scale. In our setting, the effect of
r
is not automatically negligible. We must therefore verify explicitly which conditions on
r
are sufficient for the Airy estimate to remain valid; see (
B.16
). We follow the eigenfunction decomposition method used in Appendix A of
[
34
]
, while treating the contribution of the rectifying function more carefully.

We also emphasize that, in our setting, the variance profile (
1.5
) can be very small when
t
is close to the terminal time
T
. As a result, the probability of crossing a barrier is highly sensitive to the terminal segment of the path; see, for example, Lemma
B.2
. This sensitivity is discussed further in Remark
3.4
.

Gaussian comparison: From BBM to Gaussian fields.
To study the maxima of the Gaussian fields
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
defined in (
1.2
), we use the Gaussian comparison strategy based on Slepian’s lemma, as in
[
1
]
. Specifically, in Subsection
3.1
, we construct a Gaussian field on the grid

V_{\varepsilon}:=[0,1)^{d}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d},

which we call the mollified BBM. This field captures the main contribution to the centering of the maximum. We then construct another Gaussian field, given by a Brownian sheet on each box
v+[0,\varepsilon)^{d}
,
v\in V_{\varepsilon}
, which controls the local fluctuations of
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
inside each
\varepsilon
-box.

This reduces the study of the maximum of
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
over
[0,1)^{d}
to an extreme-value problem for a BBM decorated by an independent Gaussian field with smaller-order fluctuations. We can therefore use the estimates for micro-BBM obtained in Section
2
.

1.3.
Outline of the paper

In Section
2
, we prove Theorem
1.4
, establishing the tightness of the recentered maximum for microscopically slowed-down BBM. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.2
, the corresponding tightness result for higher-dimensional Gaussian fields with weak correlations.

The appendices contain several auxiliary estimates used in the proofs. In Appendix
A
, we derive variance and
L^{2}
-distance estimates for our Gaussian fields. In Appendix
B.2
, we establish the Airy-type PDE estimates needed to control the slowdown-Brownian-motion expectations appearing in the main text. In Appendix
C
, we prove a result on the maximum of BBM with fixed splitting times.

2.
Proof for theorem
1.4

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.4
. We first recall the basic parameters of the branching Brownian motion considered there: each particle branches at rate
1
and is replaced by two offspring. The terminal time is
T>0
, which will always be assumed large. For any fixed
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, the motion of each particle is given by

(2.1)

Y_{t}^{\alpha}:=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}B_{s},\qquad t\in[0,T],

where
B
is a standard Brownian motion and

(2.2)

\sigma_{\alpha}(t):=\sqrt{2}\,(1+2t)^{-\alpha}.

We also recall some notation: for
t\in[0,T]
, we denote the set of particles alive at time
t
by
\mathcal{N}_{t}
, and its cardinality by
N_{t}:=|\mathcal{N}_{t}|
. We shall repeatedly use (
1.12
) for estimates on
N_{t}
. For any fixed
s\in[0,t]
and
v\in\mathcal{N}_{s}
, we write
\mathcal{N}_{t}^{s,v}
for the set of descendants of
v
that are alive at time
t
. For each particle at the terminal time, say
v\in\mathcal{N}_{T}
, we denote by
\{Y_{t}^{\alpha,v}:t\in[0,T]\}
its ancestral trajectory up to time
T
.

2.1.
Upper tail

In this subsection we prove the upper bound for Theorem
1.4
:

Proposition 2.1.

Fix any
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. There exists
c>0
and
\lambda_{*}>1
,
T_{*}>1
large such that,

P\left(\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{\alpha}(T)+\lambda\right)\leq e^{-c\lambda},\quad\forall\lambda\geq\lambda_{*},\ T\geq T_{*}

where
A_{\alpha}(T)
is defined in (
1.8
) with
d:=1
.

In the proof, we will decompose the time interval
[0,T]
into
[0,T_{0}]
and
[T_{0},T]
, where
T_{0}
is large but fixed independently of
T
. The reason for doing so is that the displacement of each particle after
T_{0}
has the law

(2.3)

Y_{t+T_{0}}^{\alpha}-Y_{T_{0}}^{\alpha}\overset{d}{=}\int_{T_{0}}^{t+T_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}B_{s}=\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\sigma}(s)\,\text{d}B_{s},\qquad\forall t\in[0,T-T_{0}]

with

(2.4)

\bar{\sigma}(t):=\sigma_{\alpha}(t+T_{0})=\sqrt{2}(1+2T_{0}+2t)^{-\alpha},\quad\forall t\in[0,T-T_{0}].

To apply the Airy estimate in Proposition
B.3
, we only need to choose
T_{0}
so that
1+2T_{0}\geq s_{c}
, where
s_{c}
is the constant in Proposition
B.3
.

In the proof, we will see that the displacement accumulated during the long time interval
[T_{0},T]
is the main contribution to the centering of the maximum. By the branching property, conditional on the configuration at time
T_{0}
, the descendant systems branching from different particles in
\mathcal{N}_{T_{0}}
evolve independently and each has the law of the auxiliary BBM

(2.5)

\bigl\{\bar{Y}_{T_{0},t}^{\alpha,w}:t\in[0,T-T_{0}],\ w\in\mathcal{M}_{t}\bigr\},

where
\mathcal{M}_{t}
is the set of all particles at time
t\in[0,T-T_{0}]
, and the variance profile is
\bar{\sigma}(\cdot)
given in (
2.4
).

We now derive a useful estimate for the maximal displacement accumulated over the interval
[T_{0},T]
. By the branching property and a union bound,

(2.6)

\displaystyle P\Bigl(\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{0}}}\max_{w\in\mathcal{N}_{T}^{T_{0},v}}\bigl(Y_{T}^{\alpha,w}-Y_{T_{0}}^{\alpha,v}\bigr)\geq L\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E\left[N_{T_{0}}P\Bigl(\max_{w\in\mathcal{M}_{T-T_{0}}}\bar{Y}_{T_{0},T-T_{0}}^{\alpha,w}\geq L\Bigr)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E[N_{T_{0}}]P\Bigl(\max_{w\in\mathcal{M}_{T-T_{0}}}\bar{Y}_{T_{0},T-T_{0}}^{\alpha,w}\geq L\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{T_{0}}P\Bigl(\max_{w\in\mathcal{M}_{T-T_{0}}}\bar{Y}_{T_{0},T-T_{0}}^{\alpha,w}\geq L\Bigr),

where in the first equality we use the fact that particles at time
T_{0}
, i.e.
\mathcal{N}_{T_{0}}
, generate
N_{T_{0}}
number of independent BBMs with the same law as
\{\bar{Y}_{T_{0},T-T_{0}}^{\alpha,w}\}
, and in the last equality we use
E[N_{T_{0}}]=e^{T_{0}}
.

As another preparation, we set a suitable moving barrier for the auxiliary BBM introduced in (
2.5
). Roughly speaking, a barrier is a positive function of time such that the probability that the whole BBM system crosses it is small. Due to the slowdown property of our BBM model, it is natural to take the integral of the variance profile minus the Airy correction, as in
[
34
]
. Here this is the function
A_{\alpha}(\cdot)
defined in (
1.8
). However, to control the exceeding probability, we use a small rectification which raises the barrier by a bounded amount only. This is achieved by the rectifying function
\bar{r}(\cdot)
defined in (
2.7
).

Specifically, we set

\bar{\eta}(t):=\eta(t+T_{0}),

for any
t\in[0,T-T_{0}]
, with
\eta(\cdot)
defined in (
1.7
) with
d=1
. Set the auxiliary function

(2.7)

\bar{r}(t):=-(1+2T_{0}+2t)^{-1-\beta},\quad\forall t\in[0,T-T_{0}],

for a fixed
\beta\in(0,\alpha)
. This choice satisfies the conditions (
B.16
) of Proposition
B.3
. Define

(2.8)

\bar{A}_{r}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\bar{k}_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s,\qquad t\in[0,T-T_{0}],

with

\bar{k}_{r}(s):=\sqrt{2}\,\bar{\sigma}(s)-\frac{\bar{\eta}(s)+\bar{r}(s)}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad s\in[0,T-T_{0}].

Note that, when
T_{0}
is large enough,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{k}_{r}(0)}{\bar{\sigma}(0)^{2}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{\sigma}(0)^{2}}\left(\sqrt{2}\,\bar{\sigma}(0)-\frac{\bar{\eta}(0)+\bar{r}(0)}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c(1+2T_{0})^{\alpha}>0

for some constant
c>0
.

Recall the definition (
1.8
) of
A_{\alpha}(\cdot)
. We have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\big|A_{\alpha}(T)-A_{\alpha}(T_{0})-\bar{A}_{r}(T-T_{0})\big|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{T_{0}}^{T}k_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}s-\int_{0}^{T-T_{0}}\bar{k}_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|\int_{0}^{T-T_{0}}\bar{r}(s)\,\text{d}s\right|\leq C,

since
\bar{r}(\cdot)
defined in (
2.7
) is integrable.

With all the preparations above, we give the proof of Proposition
2.1
. The argument follows the proof of Lemma 2.1 in
[
34
]
, with the Airy estimate replaced by Proposition
B.3
.

Fix any
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. Choose
T_{0}
so large that
1+2T_{0}\geq s_{c}
, where
s_{c}
is the constant in Proposition
B.3
. By (
2.10
), it is enough to prove that there exist
T_{*}>1
,
\lambda_{*}>1
and
c>0
such that

(2.11)

P\left(\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{\alpha}(T_{0})+\bar{A}_{r}(T-T_{0})+\lambda\right)\leq e^{-c\lambda},\quad\forall\lambda\geq\lambda_{*},\ T\geq T_{*}.

By the branching property at time
T_{0}
and by (
2.6
), we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle P\bigg(\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{\alpha}(T_{0})+\bar{A}_{r}(T-T_{0})+\lambda\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P\bigg(\exists v\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{0}}:Y_{T_{0}}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{\alpha}(T_{0})+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle+e^{T_{0}}P\bigg(\max_{w\in\mathcal{M}_{T-T_{0}}}\bar{Y}_{T_{0},T-T_{0}}^{\alpha,w}\geq\bar{A}_{r}(T-T_{0})+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg).

The first probability is estimated by a union bound and a Gaussian tail:

\displaystyle P\bigg(\exists v\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{0}}:Y_{T_{0}}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{\alpha}(T_{0})+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq e^{T_{0}}P\left(\mathcal{N}\left(0,\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}(s)\,\text{d}s\right)\geq A_{\alpha}(T_{0})+\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)\leq e^{-c\lambda},

for all
\lambda
large, since
T_{0}
is fixed. Hence it remains to show that

(2.13)

P\bigg(\max_{w\in\mathcal{M}_{T-T_{0}}}\bar{Y}_{T_{0},T-T_{0}}^{\alpha,w}\geq\bar{A}_{r}(T-T_{0})+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg)\leq Ce^{-c\lambda}.

To simplify notation, write

\bar{Y}_{t}:=\bar{Y}_{T_{0},t}^{\alpha},\qquad t\in[0,T-T_{0}].

Since the event in (
2.13
) is contained in the event that the moving barrier is crossed at some time, it is enough to bound

P\bigg(\exists t\in[0,T-T_{0}],\ \exists w\in\mathcal{M}_{t}:\bar{Y}_{t}^{w}\geq\bar{A}_{r}(t)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg).

Let
\tau_{0}(\bar{Y})
be the first hitting time of zero for the diffusion
\bar{Y}
under
E^{(\lambda/2)}
, namely for

\bar{Y}_{t}=\frac{\lambda}{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\sigma}(s)\,\text{d}B_{s}.

By the many-to-one lemma, followed by Lemma
B.1
with
c_{A}=\sqrt{2}
, the branching factor
e^{t}
is cancelled by the term
e^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}t}=e^{-t}
in the Girsanov representation. Therefore,

(2.14)

\displaystyle P\bigg(\exists t\in[0,T-T_{0}],\ \exists w\in\mathcal{M}_{t}:\bar{Y}_{t}^{w}\geq\bar{A}_{r}(t)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\exp\left\{-\frac{\bar{k}_{r}(0)}{\bar{\sigma}^{2}(0)}\frac{\lambda}{2}\right\}E^{(\lambda/2)}\left[\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(\bar{Y})}\left(-\left(\frac{\bar{k}_{r}}{\bar{\sigma}^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(s)\bar{Y}_{s}+\frac{\bar{\eta}(s)+\bar{r}(s)}{\bar{\sigma}(s)}\right)\,\text{d}s\right\}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(\bar{Y})\leq T-T_{0}\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-c\lambda}E^{(\lambda/2)}\left[\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(\bar{Y})}\left(-\left(\frac{\bar{k}_{r}}{\bar{\sigma}^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(s)\bar{Y}_{s}+\frac{\bar{\eta}(s)+\bar{r}(s)}{\bar{\sigma}(s)}\right)\,\text{d}s\right\}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(\bar{Y})\leq T-T_{0}\}}\right],

where the last inequality follows from (
2.9
). By the Feynman–Kac representation (
B.5
), the expectation in the last display is

(2.15)

\int_{0}^{T-T_{0}}\frac{1}{2}\bar{\sigma}^{2}(t)\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G_{r}(\lambda/2,y;0,t)\right|_{y=0}\,\text{d}t,

where
G_{r}
is the fundamental solution of

(2.16)

\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}\bar{\sigma}(t)^{2}\partial_{xx}u-\left(\frac{\bar{k}_{r}}{\bar{\sigma}^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(t)xu+\frac{\bar{\eta}(t)+\bar{r}(t)}{\bar{\sigma}(t)}u

on
[0,T-T_{0}]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with Dirichlet boundary condition
u(t,0)=0
.

For this equation, Proposition
B.3
applies with
c_{A}=\sqrt{2}
,
c_{0}=2
, and
s_{0}=1+2T_{0}
. Thus

(2.17)

V(t):=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\bar{\sigma}^{2}(t)}\left[\frac{1}{\bar{\sigma}(t)}\right]^{\prime}=2\alpha(1+2T_{0}+2t)^{3\alpha-1}.

For
t\geq(1+2T_{0})^{2/3}
, (
B.18
) gives

(2.18)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G_{r}(\lambda/2,y;0,t)\right|_{y=0}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda\sqrt{V(0)}\sqrt{V(t)}\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\bar{r}(s)}{\bar{\sigma}(s)}\,\text{d}s\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda(1+2T_{0}+2t)^{(3\alpha-1)/2}\exp\{-ct^{\alpha-\beta}\},

after increasing
C
and decreasing
c
, since
T_{0}
is fixed and
\beta<\alpha
. For
t\leq(1+2T_{0})^{2/3}
, the killed heat-kernel estimate used in (
B.54
) gives

(2.19)

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G_{r}(\lambda/2,y;0,t)\right|_{y=0}\leq C\lambda.

Combining (
2.15
),
(
2.18
), and
(
2.19
), we obtain

\int_{0}^{T-T_{0}}\frac{1}{2}\bar{\sigma}^{2}(t)\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G_{r}(\lambda/2,y;0,t)\right|_{y=0}\,\text{d}t\leq C\lambda.

Plugging this into (
2.14
) yields

P\bigg(\exists t\in[0,T-T_{0}],\ \exists w\in\mathcal{M}_{t}:\bar{Y}_{t}^{w}\geq\bar{A}_{r}(t)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg)\leq C\lambda e^{-c\lambda}\leq e^{-c^{\prime}\lambda}

for all
\lambda
large enough. This proves (
2.13
), and hence the proposition.
∎

2.2.
Lower tail

In this subsection we prove the lower tail in Theorem
1.4
:

Proposition 2.2.

Fix any
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. There exist
c>0
and
\lambda_{*}>1
,
T_{*}>1
such that, for any
\lambda\geq\lambda_{*}
and
T\geq T_{*}
,

P\left(\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}\leq A_{\alpha}(T)-\lambda\right)\leq e^{-c\lambda},

where
A_{\alpha}(T)
is defined in (
1.8
) with
d:=1
.

Instead of turning to a barrier and second moment argument, as in
[
34
]
, we use the self-similarity of the microscopically slowdown BBM. Let
H>1
and define the geometrically increasing time mesh

(2.20)

T_{m}:=\frac{2^{m}H-1}{2},\qquad m=0,\ldots,M.

Then
1+2T_{m}=2^{m}H
and
T_{m+1}-T_{m}=2^{m-1}H
.

For a
Y^{\alpha}
-particle,

Y_{t}^{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{2}(1+2s)^{-\alpha}\,\text{d}B_{s},

we have, for
t\in[0,T_{m+1}-T_{m}]
,

\displaystyle Y_{t+T_{m}}^{\alpha}-Y_{T_{m}}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{m}}^{t+T_{m}}\sqrt{2}(1+2s)^{-\alpha}\,\text{d}B_{s}

\displaystyle\overset{d}{=}(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{2}\left(1+\frac{s}{2^{m-1}H}\right)^{-\alpha}\,\text{d}B_{s}.

Thus

(2.21)

\{Y_{t+T_{m}}^{\alpha}-Y_{T_{m}}^{\alpha}:0\leq t\leq 2^{m-1}H\}\overset{d}{=}\{(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}Z_{t}^{(m)}:0\leq t\leq 2^{m-1}H\},

where

(2.22)

Z_{t}^{(m)}:=\int_{0}^{t}\hat{\sigma}_{m}(s)\,\text{d}B_{s},\qquad\hat{\sigma}_{m}(s):=\sqrt{2}\left(1+\frac{s}{2^{m-1}H}\right)^{-\alpha}.

The process
Z^{(m)}
is in the macroscopic slowdown regime of
[
26
,
34
]
. For each
m
, let

\{Z_{t}^{(m),w}:0\leq t\leq 2^{m-1}H,\ w\in\mathcal{N}_{t}^{Z}\}

be a BBM with branching rate
1
, binary splitting, and variance profile
\hat{\sigma}_{m}
.

By Lemma
2.3
, applied with terminal time
2^{m-1}H
, for any
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{3}\wedge\alpha)
there exists
c>0
such that, uniformly in
m
and
H
large,

(2.23)

P\left(\max_{w\in\mathcal{N}^{Z}_{2^{m-1}H}}Z_{2^{m-1}H}^{(m),w}\geq\int_{0}^{2^{m-1}H}k_{m}(s)\,\text{d}s-(2^{m-1}H)^{\beta}\right)\geq 1-e^{-c(2^{m-1}H)^{\beta}},

where

k_{m}(s):=\sqrt{2}\,\hat{\sigma}_{m}(s)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\alpha_{1}|\hat{\sigma}_{m}^{\prime}(s)|^{2/3}\hat{\sigma}_{m}(s)^{1/3}.

A direct scaling computation gives

(2.24)

(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{2^{m-1}H}k_{m}(s)\,\text{d}s=\int_{T_{m}}^{T_{m+1}}k_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}s,

where

k_{\alpha}(s):=\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{\alpha}(s)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\alpha_{1}|\sigma_{\alpha}^{\prime}(s)|^{2/3}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{1/3}.

Consequently,

(2.25)

P\left(\max_{w\in\mathcal{N}^{Z}_{2^{m-1}H}}(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}Z_{2^{m-1}H}^{(m),w}\geq\int_{T_{m}}^{T_{m+1}}k_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}s-(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}(2^{m-1}H)^{\beta}\right)\geq 1-e^{-c(2^{m}H)^{\beta}}.

We now use a greedy construction. Starting from a particle
v_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{0}}
, choose
v_{m+1}\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{m+1}}^{T_{m},v_{m}}
to maximize the displacement during
[T_{m},T_{m+1}]
among the descendants of
v_{m}
. See Figure
2
. By the branching property and (
2.25
),

(2.26)

\displaystyle P\left(\max_{v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{T}^{T_{0},v_{0}}}\bigl(Y_{T}^{\alpha,v^{\prime}}-Y_{T_{0}}^{\alpha,v_{0}}\bigr)\geq\int_{T_{0}}^{T}k_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}s-\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}(2^{m-1}H)^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{m=0}^{M-1}\left(1-e^{-c(2^{m}H)^{\beta}}\right)\geq 1-\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}e^{-c(2^{m}H)^{\beta}}\geq 1-e^{-c^{\prime}H^{\beta}}.

Since
\beta<\alpha
,

\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}(2^{m}H)^{-\alpha}(2^{m-1}H)^{\beta}\leq CH^{\beta-\alpha}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-m(\alpha-\beta)}\leq C.

Therefore, for every
v_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{0}}
,

(2.27)

P\left(\max_{v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{T}^{T_{0},v_{0}}}\bigl(Y_{T}^{\alpha,v^{\prime}}-Y_{T_{0}}^{\alpha,v_{0}}\bigr)\geq\int_{T_{0}}^{T}k_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}s-C\right)\geq 1-e^{-cH^{\beta}}.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. First consider the case
\lambda>10T
. Since
A_{\alpha}(T)\leq CT
and
\lambda>10T
, after increasing
\lambda_{*}
if necessary we have

A_{\alpha}(T)-\lambda\leq-\frac{\lambda}{2}.

Following a fixed genealogical line gives a centered Gaussian with variance

\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}(s)\,\text{d}s\leq 2T.

Thus

P\left(\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}\leq A_{\alpha}(T)-\lambda\right)\leq P\left(\mathcal{N}\left(0,\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}(s)\,\text{d}s\right)\leq-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)\leq e^{-c\lambda}.

It remains to consider
\lambda\leq 10T
. Set

H_{0}:=1+\frac{\lambda}{50}.

Choose
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

H:=2^{-M}(1+2T)\in[H_{0},2H_{0}).

Then
T_{M}=T
for the mesh (
2.20
), and

T_{0}=\frac{H-1}{2}\qquad\text{satisfies}\qquad c_{1}\lambda\leq T_{0}\leq c_{2}\lambda

for deterministic constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and all large
\lambda
.

For
v\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{0}}
, define

D_{v}:=\max_{w\in\mathcal{N}_{T}^{T_{0},v}}\bigl(Y_{T}^{\alpha,w}-Y_{T_{0}}^{\alpha,v}\bigr).

By (
2.27
), conditional on
\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}
,

P\left(D_{v}\geq\int_{T_{0}}^{T}k_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}s-C\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right)\geq 1-e^{-cH^{\beta}},

and these events are conditionally independent over different

.

Let

B:=\left\{\exists v\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{0}}:Y_{T_{0}}^{\alpha,v}\leq\int_{0}^{T_{0}}k_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}s-\lambda+C\right\}.

On
B^{c}
, every particle at time

is high enough. Hence, if at least one of the descendant systems realizes the good event above, then

\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}>\int_{0}^{T}k_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}s-\lambda=A_{\alpha}(T)-\lambda.

Therefore,

(2.28)

\displaystyle P\left(\max_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}\leq A_{\alpha}(T)-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P(B)+E\left[\left(e^{-cH^{\beta}}\right)^{N_{T_{0}}}\right].

Since
k_{\alpha}(s)\leq C
and
T_{0}\leq c_{2}\lambda
, with the constants above chosen as in the definition of
H_{0}
, we have for all large
\lambda

\int_{0}^{T_{0}}k_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}s+C\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}.

Thus, by a union bound and a Gaussian tail,

\displaystyle P(B)

\displaystyle\leq e^{T_{0}}P\left(\mathcal{N}\left(0,\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}(s)\,\text{d}s\right)\leq-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-c\lambda},

because
T_{0}\asymp\lambda
and
\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}(s)\,\text{d}s\leq 2T_{0}
.

For the second term, (
1.12
) gives

P\bigl(N_{T_{0}}\leq e^{T_{0}/2}\bigr)\leq e^{-cT_{0}}.

Hence

E\left[\left(e^{-cH^{\beta}}\right)^{N_{T_{0}}}\right]\leq e^{-cT_{0}}+\exp\{-cH^{\beta}e^{T_{0}/2}\}\leq e^{-c^{\prime}\lambda},

because
H\asymp\lambda
and
T_{0}\asymp\lambda
. Combining the last estimates proves the proposition.

The following lemma is for the macroscopically slowdown BBM, which is used to prove (
2.25
).

Lemma 2.3.

Consider a BBM

For any fixed dimension

Fix any
\alpha\in(0,1/2]

\Lambda_{1}(x):={x+[-1/2,1/2]^{d}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and

denotes the space-time white noise on
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

s_{0}
is to slightly enlarge the covariance of the field

\{\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z}:z\in V_{\varepsilon}\}
and a BBM in the proof of Proposition

For distinct

Since the two boxes
\Lambda_{1}(e^{s-s_{0}}z_{1})

are disjoint for all

and

. In particular, for every

,

(3.2)

\text{Cov}\Bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{1}}(t)-\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{1}}(s),\,\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{2}}(t)-\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{2}}(s)\Bigr)=0.

The following lemma gives the covariance estimate for the mollified BBM constructed above.

Lemma 3.1.

Let
\{\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z}(t):t\geq 0,\ z\in V_{\varepsilon}\}
be the MBBM defined in (
3.1
). Then, for all
s_{0}>0
sufficiently large, all distinct
z_{1},z_{2}\in V_{\varepsilon}
, and all
t\geq t_{z_{1},z_{2}}+s_{0}
, we have

(3.3)

\int_{0}^{t_{z_{1},z_{2}}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s\;\leq\;\text{Cov}\bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{1}}(t),\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{2}}(t)\bigr)\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{t_{z_{1},z_{2}}+s_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s.

By (
3.1
), we have

(3.4)

\text{Cov}\bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{1}}(t),\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{2}}(t)\bigr)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\bigl|\Lambda_{1}(e^{s-s_{0}}z_{1})\cap\Lambda_{1}(e^{s-s_{0}}z_{2})\bigr|\,\text{d}s.

Moreover, for every
s\geq 0
,

\bigl|\Lambda_{1}(e^{s-s_{0}}z_{1})\cap\Lambda_{1}(e^{s-s_{0}}z_{2})\bigr|=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(1-e^{s-s_{0}}|z_{1}^{i}-z_{2}^{i}|\bigr)_{+},

where
a_{+}:=\max\{a,0\}
. Since the intersection vanishes whenever
s>t_{z_{1},z_{2}}+s_{0}
, for all
t\geq t_{z_{1},z_{2}}+s_{0}
we get

\text{Cov}\bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{1}}(t),\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{2}}(t)\bigr)=\int_{0}^{t_{z_{1},z_{2}}+s_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(1-e^{s-s_{0}}|z_{1}^{i}-z_{2}^{i}|\bigr)_{+}\,\text{d}s.

The upper bound in (
3.3
) follows immediately from

\prod_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(1-e^{s-s_{0}}|z_{1}^{i}-z_{2}^{i}|\bigr)_{+}\leq 1.

We now turn to the lower bound. Put
t_{12}:=t_{z_{1},z_{2}}
and
L:=t_{12}+s_{0}
. For
0\leq s\leq L
, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(1-e^{s-s_{0}}|z_{1}^{i}-z_{2}^{i}|\bigr)_{+}\geq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{s-s_{0}}|z_{1}^{i}-z_{2}^{i}|\geq 1-de^{s-L}.

Hence

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{1}}(t),\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{2}}(t)\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{L}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s-d\int_{0}^{L}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}e^{s-L}\,\text{d}s.

Moreover,

{\int_{0}^{L}(1+2s)^{-2\alpha}e^{s-L}\,\text{d}s\leq C_{\alpha}(1+2L)^{-2\alpha},}

for some constant
C_{\alpha}>0
independent of
z_{1},z_{2}
and
L
; this follows by estimating the integral over
[0,L/2]
and
[L/2,L]
separately. On the other hand,

\int_{t_{12}}^{L}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s\geq 2s_{0}(1+2L)^{-2\alpha}.

Choosing
s_{0}
sufficiently large so that the last display dominates

d\int_{0}^{L}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}e^{s-L}\,\text{d}s
, we obtain

\text{Cov}\bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{1}}(t),\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{2}}(t)\bigr)\geq\int_{0}^{t_{12}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s,

which proves the lower bound.

.

We compare
\{X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta x):x\in[0,1)^{d}\}
with

(3.17)

Y_{x}:=a(x)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,[x]_{\varepsilon}}(T+T_{r})+\sqrt{\vartheta(\varepsilon)}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{x},\qquad x\in[0,1)^{d},

where
a:[0,1)^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
will be defined in (
3.20
). Choose
\delta>0
small and then choose
T_{r}>0
so that

(3.18)

\displaystyle T_{r}\geq

\displaystyle\max\left\{s_{0},\,C_{K}+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right\},

\displaystyle T_{r}=

\displaystyle N_{r}\log 2\text{ for some }N_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

\displaystyle-C_{X}+c_{0}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\geq

\displaystyle\log C_{d}+s_{0}+10,

where
s_{0}
is the constant in Lemma
3.1
, and
C_{d}
is a fixed dimensional constant such that

(3.19)

\|[x]_{\varepsilon}-[y]_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\geq C_{d}^{-1}\|x-y\|_{2}\qquad\text{whenever }[x]_{\varepsilon}\neq[y]_{\varepsilon}.

For instance, one may take
C_{d}=2\sqrt{d}
.

We now check the hypotheses of Slepian’s lemma.

(1)

Equal variances.
Define

(3.20)

a(x)^{2}:=\frac{\mathbb{E}\bigl[X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta x)^{2}\bigr]-\vartheta(\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\psi_{\varepsilon}^{x})^{2}\bigr]}{\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,[x]_{\varepsilon}}(T+T_{r}))^{2}\bigr]}.

For
T\geq T_{*}
, after increasing
T_{*}
if necessary, the numerator is non-negative. Hence
a(x)
is well-defined and

\operatorname{Var}\bigl(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta x)\bigr)=\operatorname{Var}(Y_{x}).

Moreover, by (
3.1
), (
3.11
), and (
A.8
),

(3.21)

a(x)^{2}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{C_{K}+\log\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{r}+\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s}\leq 1,

where the last inequality follows from the choice of
T_{r}
in (
3.18
). Thus
0\leq a(x)\leq 1
.

(2)

Comparison of local
L^{2}
distances.
Suppose
[x]_{\varepsilon}=[y]_{\varepsilon}
. Then
\delta x
and
\delta y
lie in the same
(\delta\varepsilon)
-box. Hence, using (
A.10
) and the monotonicity
\vartheta(\delta\varepsilon)\leq\vartheta(\varepsilon)
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta x)-X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta y)\right)^{2}\right]\leq\vartheta(\delta\varepsilon)\frac{\|x-y\|_{2}}{\varepsilon}\leq\vartheta(\varepsilon)\frac{\|x-y\|_{2}}{\varepsilon}.

On the other hand, by (
3.12
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(Y_{x}-Y_{y})^{2}]

\displaystyle\geq\vartheta(\varepsilon)\,\mathbb{E}\left[(\psi_{\varepsilon}^{x}-\psi_{\varepsilon}^{y})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\vartheta(\varepsilon)\frac{\|x-y\|_{1}}{\varepsilon}\geq\vartheta(\varepsilon)\frac{\|x-y\|_{2}}{\varepsilon}.

Thus

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta x)-X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta y)\right)^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[(Y_{x}-Y_{y})^{2}].

(3)

Covariance comparison between distinct boxes.
Suppose
[x]_{\varepsilon}\neq[y]_{\varepsilon}
, and write

z_{x}:=[x]_{\varepsilon}
,
z_{y}:=[y]_{\varepsilon}
. Since the Brownian-sheet fields are independent across different
\varepsilon
-boxes, and since
a(\cdot)\leq 1
,

\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{x},Y_{y})=a(x)a(y)\operatorname{Cov}\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{x}}(T+T_{r}),\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{y}}(T+T_{r})\right)\leq\operatorname{Cov}\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{x}}(T+T_{r}),\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{y}}(T+T_{r})\right).

By (
3.3
), the last covariance is at most

\int_{0}^{\log\frac{1}{\|z_{x}-z_{y}\|_{\infty}}+s_{0}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s.

On the other hand, (
3.19
) and (
3.18
) imply

\log\frac{1}{\|z_{x}-z_{y}\|_{\infty}}+s_{0}\leq-C_{X}+c_{0}\log\frac{1}{\delta\|x-y\|_{2}},

and therefore the covariance lower bound in (
A.8
) gives

\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{x},Y_{y})\leq\operatorname{Cov}\left(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta x),X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta y)\right).

Equivalently, using the already established equality of variances,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta x)-X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta y)\right)^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[(Y_{x}-Y_{y})^{2}].

The variance identity and the two
L^{2}
comparisons imply, by Slepian’s lemma, that

(3.22)

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in[0,1)^{d}}X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(\delta x)\geq A_{\alpha}(T)+\lambda\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in[0,1)^{d}}Y_{x}\geq A_{\alpha}(T)+\lambda\right).

For
z\in V_{\varepsilon}
, define

\psi_{\varepsilon}^{*,z}:=\sup_{x\in\Box_{\varepsilon}^{z}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{x}.

For
n\geq 1
, let

(3.23)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}

\displaystyle:=\left\{z\in V_{\varepsilon}:\sqrt{\vartheta(\varepsilon)}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{*,z}\leq 0\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{z\in V_{\varepsilon}:\sqrt{\vartheta(\varepsilon)}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{*,z}\in[n-1,n)\right\},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

From (
3.13
), for all
n\geq\lambda/2
and
\lambda
sufficiently large,

(3.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\Gamma_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in V_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{P}\left(\sqrt{\vartheta(\varepsilon)}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{*,z}\geq n-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}\exp\left(-c\frac{n^{2}}{\vartheta(\varepsilon)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\left(dT-cT^{2\alpha}n^{2}\right),

where we used (
3.16
) in the last line.

For
n\geq 1
, on the event
z\in\Gamma_{n}
, we have

\sqrt{\vartheta(\varepsilon)}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{*,z}<n,

while for
z\in\Gamma_{0}
we have

\sqrt{\vartheta(\varepsilon)}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{*,z}\leq 0.

Hence

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in[0,1)^{d}}Y_{x}\geq A_{\alpha}(T)+\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq n\leq\lfloor\lambda/2\rfloor}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{n}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z}(t)\geq m_{\alpha}(T+T_{r})+\lambda-n-C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\lambda/2<n\leq A_{\alpha}(T+T_{r})+\lambda+C_{0}}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{n}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z}(t)\geq m_{\alpha}(T+T_{r})+\lambda-n-C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n>A_{\alpha}(T+T_{r})+\lambda+C_{0}}\mathbb{P}\left(\Gamma_{n}\neq\varnothing\right).

Indeed, in the first two sums the threshold

A_{\alpha}(T)+\lambda-n
is positive, after increasing
T_{*}
and

\lambda_{*}
if necessary. Therefore, since
0\leq a(x)\leq 1
,

a(x)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,[x]_{\varepsilon}}(t)\geq A_{\alpha}(T)+\lambda-n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,[x]_{\varepsilon}}(t)\geq A_{\alpha}(T)+\lambda-n.

For the first sum in (
3.25
), condition on the Brownian-sheet field. Since
\Gamma_{n}
is then deterministic and independent of the MBBM field, (
3.6
) in Proposition
3.2
gives

\sum_{0\leq n\leq\lfloor\lambda/2\rfloor}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{n}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z}(T+T_{r})\geq m_{\alpha}(T+T_{r})+\lambda-n-C_{0}\right)\leq C\lambda e^{-c\lambda}\leq Ce^{-c\lambda}.

We next treat the intermediate range

\lambda/2<n\leq A_{\alpha}(T+T_{r})+\lambda+C_{0}:=n^{\prime}.

Using (
3.6
) in Proposition
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda/2<n\leq n^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{n}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z}(T+T_{r})\geq m_{\alpha}(T+T_{r})+\lambda-n-C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda/2<n\leq n^{\prime}}\left(Ce^{-c\lambda-cn}+C\,\mathbb{E}|\Gamma_{n}|\,e^{-dT-c\lambda+cT^{\alpha}n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda/2<n\leq n^{\prime}}\left(Ce^{-c\lambda-cn}+C\exp\{-c\lambda+cT^{\alpha}n-c^{\prime}T^{2\alpha}n^{2}\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-c\lambda}.

Finally, we discuss the large-
n
range. By
Markov’s inequality and (
3.24
),

\displaystyle\sum_{n>A_{\alpha}(t)+\lambda+C_{0}}\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{n}\neq\varnothing)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>A_{\alpha}(T+T_{r})+\lambda+C_{0}}\mathbb{E}|\Gamma_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n>A_{\alpha}(t)+\lambda+C_{0}}\exp\left\{dT-cT^{2\alpha}n^{2}\right\}.

Since
A_{\alpha}(T+T_{r})\asymp T^{1-\alpha}
, we have

T^{2\alpha}A_{\alpha}(t)^{2}\asymp T^{2}.

Thus, after increasing
T_{*}
and
\lambda_{*}
if necessary,

\sum_{n>A_{\alpha}(t)+\lambda+C_{0}}\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{n}\neq\varnothing)\leq Ce^{-cT^{2}-c\lambda}.

Plugging these three bounds into (
3.25
), togather with (
3.22
), proves (
3.15
).

3.3.
Lower tail

The goal of this subsection is to prove the following lower-tail estimate for the
maximum of the Gaussian field
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
. The essential tool we use here is the well-known Paley-Zygmund inequality and second moment estimate.

Remark 3.4.

We emphasize that, different from the macroscopically slowdown case like
[
34
]
where the variance profile is always of order
O(1)
, due the fast decaying of the variance profile
\sigma_{\alpha}(t)
in (
1.5
), we must be especially careful to take a proper barrier (see (
3.46
)). Specifically, when
t
is very close to
T
,
\sigma_{\alpha}(t)
is of order
T^{-\alpha}
which means all ’particles’ moves rather slowly, any slight change in the barrier (see the rectified function
r
defined in (
3.36
) and the tatget set
I
defined in (
3.50
) at terminal time) may have an overwhelming influence to the exceeding probability. Moreover, some technical estimate like Lemma
3.6
is required.

Proposition 3.5.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
and
d\geq 2
, and set

T:=\log(1/\varepsilon)
. There exist constants
C,c>0
and

T_{*},\lambda_{*}>0
such that

(3.26)

P\left(\sup_{x\in[0,1)^{d}}X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x)\leq A_{\alpha}(T)-\lambda\right)\leq Ce^{-c\lambda},\qquad\forall\,T\geq T_{*},\ \lambda\geq\lambda_{*},

where
A_{\alpha}(T)
is defined in (
1.8
).

We will later compare
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
with the auxiliary mollified BBM field introduced in (
3.1
), with
s_{0}=0
. More precisely, define

(3.27)

\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z}(t):=\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)\,\mathds{1}_{\Lambda_{1}(e^{s}z)}(y)\,W(\text{d}s,\text{d}y),\qquad z\in V_{\varepsilon},\quad t\geq 0,

where

V_{\varepsilon}:=[0,1)^{d}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d},\qquad\Lambda_{1}(x):=x+[-1/2,1/2]^{d}.

As in the derivation of (
3.3
), there exists
C_{\xi}>0

such that, for any
t\geq 0
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in V_{\varepsilon}
,

(3.28)

\text{Cov}\!\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{1}}(t),\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,z_{2}}(t)\right)\geq\int_{0}^{{\,t\wedge(t_{z_{1},z_{2}}-C_{\xi})_{+}\,}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s,

where

t_{z_{1},z_{2}}:=\log\frac{1}{\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{\infty}},\qquad{t_{z,z}:=+\infty.}

Fix
\delta,\varrho\in(0,1)
such that

(3.29)

-C_{K}+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\geq 1,\qquad C_{K}+\log\varrho\leq-C_{\xi},

where
C_{K}
and
C_{\xi}
are the constants in
(
A.8
) and
(
3.28
), respectively.

We first reduce the proof to a lower-tail estimate for the auxiliary field.
We claim that there exist constants
C,c>0
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, and

\lambda_{*}>0
such that

(3.30)

P\left(\max_{x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varrho}\right)\leq A_{\alpha}\!\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon}\right)-\lambda\right)\leq Ce^{-c\lambda}

for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}/\delta
and
\lambda\geq\lambda_{*}
.
Indeed, once this is proved, setting
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\delta\varepsilon
gives

\left\{\frac{x}{\varrho}:x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}\right\}\subset[0,1)^{d},

and therefore

\displaystyle P\left(\sup_{x\in[0,1)^{d}}X_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\alpha}(x)\leq A_{\alpha}\!\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\right)-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\max_{x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varrho}\right)\leq A_{\alpha}\!\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon}\right)-\lambda\right)\leq Ce^{-c\lambda}.

This yields (
3.26
).

We first study the range
\lambda\geq(10+d)T
directly for
the field
X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
. Fix

x_{0}\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}
. By the covariance estimates for

X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
and since
\delta,\varrho
are fixed,

\var\!\left(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x_{0}/\varrho)\right)\leq CT.

Moreover,

A_{\alpha}(T-\log\delta)\leq CT^{1-\alpha}.

Hence, for all sufficiently large
T
and all

\lambda\geq(10+d)T
,

A_{\alpha}(T-\log\delta)-\lambda\leq-\frac{\lambda}{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle P\left(\max_{x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varrho}\right)\leq A_{\alpha}(T-\log\delta)-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x_{0}/\varrho)\leq-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)\leq\exp\left(-c\frac{\lambda^{2}}{T}\right)\leq e^{-c\lambda}.

Thus, for the comparison argument below, it is enough to assume

{\lambda\leq(10+d)T.}

We will compare the Gaussian field

\left\{X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varrho}\right):x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}\right\}

with

\left\{b(x)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,x}(T):x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}\right\},

where

(3.31)

b(x)^{2}:=\frac{\var\!\left(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x/\varrho)\right)}{\var\!\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,x}(T)\right)}.

By (
A.8
) and
(
3.27
), the choice of
\delta

implies

(3.32)

b(x)^{2}\geq\frac{\int_{0}^{-C_{K}+\log\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s}{\int_{0}^{\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s}\geq 1.

Moreover, since
\delta
and
\varrho
are fixed, the same covariance
estimates imply

(3.33)

1\leq b(x)\leq 1+\frac{C}{T},\qquad x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d},

for all sufficiently large
T
.

For any distinct
x,y\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}
, the covariance upper
bound for
X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
gives

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varrho}\right),X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{y}{\varrho}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{{\left(C_{K}+\log\frac{\varrho}{\|x-y\|_{\infty}}\right)_{+}}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s.

On the other hand, by (
3.28
)
and
b(x),b(y)\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(b(x)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,x}(T),b(y)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,y}(T)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\,\left(\log\frac{1}{\|x-y\|_{\infty}}-C_{\xi}\right)_{+}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s.

The choice of
\varrho
in (
3.29
) therefore
implies

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(b(x)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,x}(T),b(y)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,y}(T)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\text{Cov}\left(X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varrho}\right),X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{y}{\varrho}\right)\right).

For
x=y
, the two fields have the same variance by definition of
b(x)
.
Hence Slepian’s comparison theorem gives

\displaystyle P\left(\max_{x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}X_{\delta\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varrho}\right)\leq A_{\alpha}(T-\log\delta)-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\max_{x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}b(x)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,x}(T)\leq A_{\alpha}(T-\log\delta)-\lambda\right).

Using (
3.33
), the expansion
(
1.9
), and the restriction

{\lambda\leq(10+d)T}
, we have uniformly in

x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}
,

\frac{A_{\alpha}(T-\log\delta)-\lambda}{b(x)}\leq A_{\alpha}(T)-\lambda+C,

where
C
is independent of
T
and
\lambda
. Hence

\displaystyle P\left(\max_{x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}b(x)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,x}(T)\leq A_{\alpha}(T-\log\delta)-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\max_{x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,x}(T)\leq A_{\alpha}(T)-\lambda+C\right).

Thus, it suffices to prove

(3.34)

P\left(\max_{x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,x}(T)\leq A_{\alpha}(T)-\lambda\right)\leq Ce^{-c\lambda}.

For notational simplicity, we write
\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}
for

\xi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha,x}
in the rest of the proof.

Step 1: Decomposition at time
t_{\lambda}
.

Set

(3.35)

t_{\lambda}:=\log\lambda,\qquad K_{\lambda}:=t_{\lambda}^{-\alpha}.

Recall that

A_{\alpha}(T)=\int_{0}^{T}k(s)\,\text{d}s.

Let
\beta\in(3/4,1)
. We choose a nonnegative
C^{2}
function

r:[t_{\lambda},T]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
r=0
on

[t_{\lambda},T-T^{\beta}]
and, for some constants
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}>0
,

(3.36)

\displaystyle|r(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{0}c_{r}\sigma_{\alpha}(T)\log T}{T^{\beta}},\qquad

\displaystyle t\in[t_{\lambda},T],

\displaystyle|r^{\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{1}c_{r}\sigma_{\alpha}(T)\log T}{T^{2\beta}},\qquad

\displaystyle t\in[t_{\lambda},T],

\displaystyle|r^{\prime\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{a_{2}}c_{r}\sigma_{\alpha}(T)\log T}{T^{3\beta}},\qquad

\displaystyle t\in[t_{\lambda},T],

\displaystyle a_{2}^{-1}c_{r}\log T

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{\lambda}}^{T}\frac{r(t)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(t)}\,\text{d}t\leq a_{2}c_{r}\log T,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{\lambda}}^{T}r(t)\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle\leq a_{2}c_{r}(\log T)\sigma_{\alpha}(T).

Such a function is obtained by smoothing a piecewise quadratic-linear profile supported on
[T-T^{\beta},T]
. We mention that, our auxiliary function here is in the similar form as that in Section 3.2 of
[
34
]
. However, the support of the auxiliary function therein is
[T-T^{2/3},T]
.
In our case, the condition
\beta>3/4
guarantees
that
r
satisfies the regularity assumptions required in
Proposition
B.3
.

Define

(3.37)

k_{r}(t):=k(t)-\frac{r(t)}{\sqrt{2d}},\qquad t\in[t_{\lambda},T].

By (
3.36
), definitions (
1.5
) and (
1.7
) for
\sigma_{\alpha}
and
\eta_{\alpha}
respectively, we can check that

(3.38)

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\eta(s)+r(s)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(s)}\right)^{2}\,\text{d}s\leq C.

and

(3.39)

A_{\alpha}(T)=\int_{0}^{t_{\lambda}}k(s)\,\text{d}s+\int_{t_{\lambda}}^{T}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s+o_{T}(1).

Consequently, by (
3.39
), it is enough to prove the (
3.34
) with

A_{\alpha}(T)
replaced by

\int_{0}^{t_{\lambda}}k(s)\,\text{d}s+\int_{t_{\lambda}}^{T}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s.

Choose

z_{*}\in\arg\max_{z\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}\bigl\{\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(T)-\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t_{\lambda})\bigr\}.

Then

\displaystyle P\left(\max_{x\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(T)\leq\int_{0}^{t_{\lambda}}k(s)\,\text{d}s+\int_{t_{\lambda}}^{T}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z_{*}}(t_{\lambda})+\bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z_{*}}(T)-\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z_{*}}(t_{\lambda})\bigr)\leq\int_{0}^{t_{\lambda}}k(s)\,\text{d}s+\int_{t_{\lambda}}^{T}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z_{*}}(t_{\lambda})\leq\int_{0}^{t_{\lambda}}k(s)\,\text{d}s-\lambda-K_{\lambda}\right)

(3.40)

\displaystyle\quad+P\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z_{*}}(T)-\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z_{*}}(t_{\lambda})\leq\int_{t_{\lambda}}^{T}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s+K_{\lambda}\right).

The random point
z_{*}
is measurable with respect to the noise after time

t_{\lambda}
, and is therefore independent of

\{\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t_{\lambda}):z\in V_{\varepsilon}\}
. Hence

\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z_{*}}(t_{\lambda})
has the same one-dimensional law as

\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t_{\lambda})
for any fixed
z
. Since

\var\bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z_{*}}(t_{\lambda})\bigr)=\int_{0}^{t_{\lambda}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\text{d}s\leq Ct_{\lambda}=C\log\lambda

and

\int_{0}^{t_{\lambda}}k(s)\,\text{d}s-\lambda-K_{\lambda}\leq-\lambda/2

for all sufficiently large
\lambda
, the Gaussian tail estimate gives

(3.41)

P\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z_{*}}(t_{\lambda})\leq\int_{0}^{t_{\lambda}}k(s)\,\text{d}s-\lambda-K_{\lambda}\right)\leq C\exp\left(-c\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\log\lambda}\right)\leq Ce^{-c\lambda}.

It remains to estimate

P\left(\max_{z\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}\bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(T)-\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t_{\lambda})\bigr)\leq\int_{t_{\lambda}}^{T}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s+K_{\lambda}\right).

To exploit independence, choose disjoint
{\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
-lattice
boxes

{E_{1},\dots,E_{N_{\lambda}}\subset V_{\varepsilon}\cap[0,\varrho]^{d}}

which are translates of one another, have side length comparable to

1/\lambda
, and are separated from each other by distance at least

1/\lambda=e^{-t_{\lambda}}
. Then

{N_{\lambda}\geq c\lambda^{d},\qquad|E_{i}|\asymp(\lambda\varepsilon)^{-d}=e^{d(T-t_{\lambda})}.}

For definiteness, we may take

{E_{1}=V_{\varepsilon}\cap[0,1/\lambda]^{d}}.

Since the sets
\{E_{i}\}
are separated by distance at least
e^{-t_{\lambda}}
, by the definition (
3.27
),
the families

{\{\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(T)-\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t_{\lambda}):z\in E_{i}\},\qquad i=1,\dots,N_{\lambda},}

are independent and identically distributed. Hence

\displaystyle P\left(\max_{z\in V_{\varepsilon}\cap\varrho[0,1)^{d}}\bigl(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(T)-\xi_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t_{\lambda})\bigr)\leq\int_{t_{\lambda}}^{T}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s+K_{\lambda}\right)

p_{u}^{D}(y,z)\leq C\frac{yz}{u^{3/2}}e^{-c\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{u}},

we have

p_{u}^{D}(y,z)\leq C\frac{yz}{u^{3/2}}e^{-c^{\prime}\frac{y^{2}}{u}},\ \forall y\geq 0,\ z\in[0,\sqrt{C_{0}u}]

for some
c^{\prime}>0
. Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}yF_{u}(y)^{2}\text{d}y=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\sqrt{C_{0}u}}yF_{u}(y)^{2}\text{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{\sqrt{C_{0}u}}y\left(\frac{y}{u^{3/2}}e^{-c^{\prime}\frac{y^{2}}{u}}\int_{0}^{\infty}zw(z)\text{d}z\right)^{2}\text{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{M_{1}(w)^{2}}{u}

where in the last line we use

\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{3}e^{-c^{\prime}\frac{y^{2}}{u}}\text{d}y\leq Cu^{2}

which finishes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Estimates on the covariance kernels

Recall from (
1.1
) and
(
1.3
) that, for any

\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, the centered Gaussian field
X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}

defined in (
1.2
) has
covariance kernel

(A.1)

\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{1}),X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{2})\bigr)=:k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{-2}}e^{-t\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

(A.2)

I_{\alpha}(t):=\int_{0}^{\log(1+t)}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Equivalently,

(A.3)

I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{(1+\log(1+t))^{-2\alpha}}{1+t}.

We first record a few elementary integral estimates.

Lemma A.1.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. For every
A>0
, there exists
C_{A}>0

such that, for every
t\geq 1
,

(A.4)

I_{\alpha}(t)+A(1+\log(1+t))^{-2\alpha}\leq\int_{0}^{2\log(1+\sqrt{t})+C_{A}}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Moreover, for every
A>0
, there exist constants
C_{A}>0
and

t_{A}\geq 1
such that, for every
t\geq t_{A}
,

(A.5)

I_{\alpha}(t)-A(1+\log(1+t))^{-2\alpha}\geq\int_{0}^{2\log(1+\sqrt{t})-C_{A}}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Finally, there exists
C>0
such that

(A.6)

\int_{0}^{L}t\,I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\,L\,(1+\log(1+L))^{-2\alpha},\qquad L\geq 1.

For
t\geq 1
,

(A.7)

0\leq 2\log(1+\sqrt{t})-\log(1+t)\leq\log 2.

We first prove the upper estimate. Choose
C_{A}>0
so large that

\inf_{a\geq\log 2}(1+a)^{2\alpha}\int_{a}^{a+C_{A}}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s\geq A.

This is possible because the left-hand side tends to infinity as

C_{A}\to\infty
. Applying this with
a=\log(1+t)
and using
(
A.7
), we obtain

\int_{\log(1+t)}^{2\log(1+\sqrt{t})+C_{A}}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s\geq A(1+\log(1+t))^{-2\alpha}.

This proves (
A.4
).

We next prove the lower estimate. Choose
C_{A}>A+\log 2+1
, and then
choose
t_{A}\geq 1
so large that

2\log(1+\sqrt{t})-C_{A}\geq 0
for all
t\geq t_{A}
. By
(
A.7
), for every
t\geq t_{A}
,

2\log(1+\sqrt{t})-C_{A}\leq\log(1+t)-(C_{A}-\log 2).

Therefore

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(t)-\int_{0}^{2\log(1+\sqrt{t})-C_{A}}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\log(1+t)-(C_{A}-\log 2)}^{\log(1+t)}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq(C_{A}-\log 2)(1+\log(1+t))^{-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq A(1+\log(1+t))^{-2\alpha}.

This proves (
A.5
).

Finally, by (
A.3
),

\int_{0}^{L}t\,I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{L}(1+\log(1+t))^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t.

For
L\geq 4
, split the last integral at
\sqrt{L}
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}(1+\log(1+t))^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{L}+L(1+\log(1+\sqrt{L}))^{-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C\,L\,(1+\log(1+L))^{-2\alpha}.

The case
1\leq L<4
is absorbed by increasing
C
. This proves
(
A.6
).
∎

Proposition A.2.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. The kernel
k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}
is positive
definite. Moreover, there exists
C_{K}>0
such that, for all

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and all
z_{1},z_{2}\in[0,1]^{d}
,

(A.8)

\int_{0}^{(t_{z_{1},z_{2}}-C_{K})_{+}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})\leq\int_{0}^{t_{z_{1},z_{2}}+C_{K}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

where

\sigma_{\alpha}(s):=\sqrt{2}\,(1+2s)^{-\alpha},\qquad t_{z_{1},z_{2}}:=\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{\infty}}.

The positive definiteness follows since
e^{-t\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}
is a
positive definite kernel for each
t\geq 0
, and
I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
.

We prove the covariance estimate. We use the identity

(A.9)

\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{2T}(1+u)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}u,\qquad T\geq 0.

We also use, without further comment, the equivalence of

\|\cdot\|_{2}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

First we prove the lower bound. Since

(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-2}\leq\varepsilon^{-2}
,

\displaystyle k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-2}}e^{-t(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{2}}I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=I_{\alpha}\bigl((\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-2}\bigr)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-2}}\left(1-e^{-t(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{2}}\right)I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using
1-e^{-x}\leq x
and
(
A.6
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-2}}\left(1-e^{-t(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{2}}\right)I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\log\left(1+(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-2}\right)\right)^{-2\alpha}.

Therefore

\displaystyle k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})\geq I_{\alpha}\bigl((\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-2}\bigr)-C\left(1+\log\left(1+(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-2}\right)\right)^{-2\alpha}.

If
\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}
is bounded away from zero, then

t_{z_{1},z_{2}}
is bounded from above, and the lower bound follows by
choosing
C_{K}
large enough. Otherwise, applying
(
A.5
) with

t=(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-2}
gives

k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})\geq\int_{0}^{2\log\left(1+(\varepsilon\vee\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2})^{-1}\right)-C}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Using the equivalence of the two norms and
(
A.9
), this implies

k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})\geq\int_{0}^{(t_{z_{1},z_{2}}-C_{K})_{+}}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

after increasing

.

We now prove the upper bound. If
\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
, then

k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})\leq I_{\alpha}(\varepsilon^{-2})\leq\int_{0}^{2\log(1+\varepsilon^{-1})}(1+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
\log(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\leq\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log 2
, and since

t_{z_{1},z_{2}}=\log(1/\varepsilon)+O(1)

from (
A.9
) after increasing

.

It remains to consider the case
\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}>\varepsilon

\displaystyle k_{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=I_{\alpha}(\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{-2})+\int_{\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{-2}}^{\infty}e^{-t\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using the monotonicity of
(1+\log(1+t))^{-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\int_{\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{-2}}^{\infty}e^{-t\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}I_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\log(1+\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{-2})\right)^{-2\alpha}\int_{\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{-2}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}}{1+t}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\log(1+\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{-2})\right)^{-2\alpha}.

Therefore

If
\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}

.
Otherwise, applying
(

) with

t=\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}^{-2}

Proposition A.3.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. There exists
C>0
such that, for every

\mathbb{E}\bigl[(X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{1})-X_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(z_{2}))^{2}\bigr]\leq C\,\vartheta_{\alpha}(\varepsilon)\,\frac{\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{2}}{\varepsilon},

Appendix B
Airy-like PDE estimate

In this section, we introduce two tools used in the analysis of slowdown BBM
and its higher-dimensional analogues. The first is a pair of
Girsanov-transform-based representations for exceedance probabilities and
Brownian bridge-like barrier probabilities. The second consists of
Feynman–Kac estimates for the parabolic PDEs associated with these
representations.

B.1.
Results from the Girsanov transform

For any continuous stochastic process
X=\{X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
, we write

\tau_{0}(X):=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}=0\}

for its first hitting time of zero.

Lemma B.1 (Hitting probability under a moving barrier).

Fix a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
carrying a Brownian motion
B
.
Let
\sigma\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}_{+})
and define

Y_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)\,\text{d}B_{s},\qquad t\geq 0.

Let
c_{A}>0
and let
\eta,r\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R})
be deterministic.
Set

(B.1)

k_{r}(t):=c_{A}\sigma(t)-\frac{\eta(t)+r(t)}{c_{A}},\qquad t\geq 0,

and

(B.2)

A_{r}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s.

Fix
T>0
. Then, for any
\lambda>0
and any interval
I\subset[0,T]
,

(B.3)

\displaystyle P\left(\tau_{0}(A_{r}+\lambda-Y)\in I\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{k_{r}(0)}{\sigma(0)^{2}}\lambda\right\}E^{(\lambda)}\left[\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}\tau_{0}(Y)+\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(Y)}\left(-\left(\frac{k_{r}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(s)Y_{s}+\frac{\eta(s)+r(s)}{\sigma(s)}\right)\,\text{d}s\right\}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(Y)\in I\}}\right],

where
E^{(\lambda)}
denotes expectation for the diffusion

Y_{t}=\lambda+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)\,\text{d}B_{s}.

Write

\overline{Y}_{t}:=Y_{t}-A_{r}(t)=Y_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s.

For
t\leq T
, define a new probability measure
Q
by

\frac{\text{d}Q}{\text{d}P}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}=\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{k_{r}(s)}{\sigma(s)^{2}}\,\text{d}Y_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{k_{r}(s)^{2}}{\sigma(s)^{2}}\,\text{d}s\right\}.

Since all coefficients are deterministic and smooth on
[0,T]
, Novikov’s
condition holds. By Girsanov’s theorem, under
Q
the process

\overline{Y}
has the same law as
Y
under
P
.

Let

f(t):=\frac{k_{r}(t)}{\sigma(t)^{2}}.

By integration by parts,

\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\text{d}\overline{Y}_{s}=f(t)\overline{Y}_{t}-f(0)\overline{Y}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(s)\overline{Y}_{s}\,\text{d}s.

Since
\overline{Y}_{0}=0
, this gives

\frac{\text{d}Q}{\text{d}P}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}=\exp\left\{f(t)\overline{Y}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(s)\overline{Y}_{s}\,\text{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{k_{r}(s)^{2}}{\sigma(s)^{2}}\,\text{d}s\right\}.

Set
\tau:=\tau_{0}(\lambda-\overline{Y})
and
\tau_{T}:=\tau\wedge T
.
Applying the preceding identity at the bounded stopping time
\tau_{T}
and then
restricting to the event
\{\tau\in I\}
gives

\displaystyle P\left(\tau_{0}(A_{r}+\lambda-Y)\in I\right)

\displaystyle=E_{Q}\left[\exp\left\{-f(\tau)\overline{Y}_{\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{\prime}(s)\overline{Y}_{s}\,\text{d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{k_{r}(s)^{2}}{\sigma(s)^{2}}\,\text{d}s\right\}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(\lambda-\overline{Y})\in I\}}\right]

(B.4)

\displaystyle=E_{P}\left[\exp\left\{-f(\tau_{0})Y_{\tau_{0}}+\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}f^{\prime}(s)Y_{s}\,\text{d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}\frac{k_{r}(s)^{2}}{\sigma(s)^{2}}\,\text{d}s\right\}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(\lambda-Y)\in I\}}\right],

where, in the last line,
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(\lambda-Y)
.

By the symmetry
Y\overset{d}{=}-Y
, the last display equals

E_{P}\left[\exp\left\{-f(\tau_{0})\lambda-\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}f^{\prime}(s)Y_{s}\,\text{d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}\frac{k_{r}(s)^{2}}{\sigma(s)^{2}}\,\text{d}s\right\}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(\lambda+Y)\in I\}}\right].

Equivalently, if under
E^{(\lambda)}
we write

Y_{t}=\lambda+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)\,\text{d}B_{s},

then the preceding expression becomes

E^{(\lambda)}\left[\exp\left\{-f(0)\lambda-\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(Y)}f^{\prime}(s)Y_{s}\,\text{d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(Y)}\frac{k_{r}(s)^{2}}{\sigma(s)^{2}}\,\text{d}s\right\}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(Y)\in I\}}\right].

Finally, using

-\frac{1}{2}\frac{k_{r}(s)^{2}}{\sigma(s)^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}+\frac{\eta(s)+r(s)}{\sigma(s)}-\frac{(\eta(s)+r(s))^{2}}{2c_{A}^{2}\sigma(s)^{2}}\leq-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}+\frac{\eta(s)+r(s)}{\sigma(s)},

we obtain (
B.3
).
∎

In the main text, we need to estimate an expectation slightly different from
(
B.3
), namely

E^{(\lambda)}\left[\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(Y)}\left(-\left(\frac{k_{r}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(s)Y_{s}+\frac{\eta(s)+r(s)}{\sigma(s)}\right)\,\text{d}s\right\}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(Y)\in I\}}\right].

This expectation can be represented as

(B.5)

\int_{I}\frac{1}{2}\sigma(t)^{2}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G_{r}(\lambda,y;0,t)\right|_{y=0}\,\text{d}t,

where
G_{r}
is the fundamental solution of the parabolic equation

(B.6)

\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}\sigma(t)^{2}\partial_{xx}u-\left(\frac{k_{r}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(t)xu+\frac{\eta(t)+r(t)}{\sigma(t)}u,

on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with Dirichlet boundary condition

u(t,0)=0
. For references on this representation, see the discussion
following formula
(2.5)
in
[
34
]
.

The following lemma gives a PDE representation of the bridge–barrier
probability.

Lemma B.2 (Bridge-like probability).

Under the same assumptions as Lemma

B.1
, for any
x,y>0
and

0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle P\left(Y_{t}-Y_{t_{1}}\leq x+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s\ \forall t\in[t_{1},t_{2}],\ x+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s-(Y_{t_{2}}-Y_{t_{1}})\in\text{d}y\right)

(B.7)

\displaystyle\quad=\exp\left\{-\frac{k_{r}(t_{1})}{\sigma(t_{1})^{2}}x+\frac{k_{r}(t_{2})}{\sigma(t_{2})^{2}}y-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}(t_{2}-t_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{(\eta(s)+r(s))^{2}}{c_{A}^{2}\sigma(s)^{2}}\,\text{d}s\right\}G_{r}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})\,\text{d}y,

where
G_{r}
is the fundamental solution of
(
B.6
).

Let

Z_{t}:=x+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s-(Y_{t}-Y_{t_{1}}),\qquad t\in[t_{1},t_{2}].

Then the event in the statement is

\{Z_{t}\geq 0\ \forall t\in[t_{1},t_{2}],\ Z_{t_{2}}\in\text{d}y\}.

Applying the same Girsanov calculation as in
(
B.4
), now on the interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
, gives

\displaystyle{P\left(Y_{t}-Y_{t_{1}}\leq x+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s\ \forall t\in[t_{1},t_{2}],\ Z_{t_{2}}\in\text{d}y\right)}

\displaystyle{=E_{P}^{(t_{1},x)}\left[\exp\left\{-\frac{k_{r}(t_{1})}{\sigma(t_{1})^{2}}x+\frac{k_{r}(t_{2})}{\sigma(t_{2})^{2}}y+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(-\left(\frac{k_{r}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(s)Y_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{k_{r}(s)^{2}}{\sigma(s)^{2}}\right)\,\text{d}s\right\}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(Y)>t_{2}\}}\mathds{1}_{\{Y_{t_{2}}\in\text{d}y\}}\right].}

Here
E_{P}^{(t_{1},x)}
denotes expectation for the diffusion started from

x
at time
t_{1}
. Using

{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{k_{r}(s)^{2}}{\sigma(s)^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}+\frac{\eta(s)+r(s)}{\sigma(s)}-\frac{(\eta(s)+r(s))^{2}}{2c_{A}^{2}\sigma(s)^{2}},}

and applying the Feynman–Kac formula for the killed diffusion on

\mathbb{R}_{+}
yields the claimed representation.
∎

B.2.
Airy PDE estimate

In the remainder of this section, we estimate the fundamental solution of the Airy-type PDE (
B.6
). The difference betwen the setting here and that of
[
34
]
can be seen in Subsection
1.2
. The argument here extends the estimates in Appendix A of
[
34
]
and the proof is adapted from the eigenvector-decomposition method therein.

We begin by recalling some basic facts about the Airy operator. The Airy function of the first kind is defined by

\mathrm{Ai}(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos\left(\frac{t^{3}}{3}+xt\right)\,dt,

and satisfies the ODE

\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime\prime}(x)-x\mathrm{Ai}(x)=0.

Its zeros are located at
-\alpha_{n}
, where

(B.8)

0<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots,\qquad\alpha_{1}=2.33811\ldots

For each
q>0
, consider the operator

L_{q}u:=\partial_{xx}u-qxu

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with Dirichlet boundary condition at
x=0
. Its eigenfunctions are given by

\psi_{n}^{q}(x):=q^{1/6}\psi_{n}(q^{1/3}x),\qquad n\geq 1,

with corresponding eigenvalues
-\alpha_{n}q^{2/3}
, where

\psi_{n}(x):=\frac{\mathrm{Ai}(x-\alpha_{n})}{\|\mathrm{Ai}(\cdot-\alpha_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}}.

Moreover,
\{\psi_{n}^{q}\}_{n\geq 1}
forms an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

We shall use the following facts from Lemma A.1 of
[
34
]
.

(1)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\psi_{n}(x)|\leq Cx,\qquad\forall n\geq 1,\ x\geq 0.

Therefore, by scaling,

(B.9)

|\psi_{n}^{q}(x)|\leq C\sqrt{q}\,x,\qquad\forall n\geq 1,\ q>0,\ x\geq 0.

(2)

As
n\to\infty
,

\alpha_{n}n^{-2/3}\to\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)^{2/3}.

Consequently, for every
c>0
,

(B.10)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-c\alpha_{n}}<\infty.

From Cauchy inequality and (
B.9
), we have

(B.11)

{\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(\vartheta_{2})\psi_{n}^{q(\vartheta_{2})}(y)\right|\leq C\|a(\vartheta_{2})\|_{2}\sqrt{q(\vartheta_{2})}\,y,\qquad y\geq 0}

for any real vector
a=(a_{1},...)
.

In the proof of Corollary A.4 of
[
34
]
, it is also shown that, for every
a>0
, there exists
C(a)\in(0,\infty)
such that

\psi_{1}(x)\geq\frac{1}{C(a)}x,\qquad\forall x\in[0,a].

By scaling, it follows that, for every
a>0
and
q>0
,

(B.12)

\psi_{1}^{q}(x)\geq\frac{1}{C(a)}\sqrt{q}\,x,\qquad\forall x\in\bigl(0,a\,q^{-1/3}\bigr).

After these preliminaries, we specify the coefficient functions
(\sigma,\eta,r)
in the PDE

(B.13)

\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(t)\partial_{xx}u-\left(\frac{k}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(t)xu+\frac{\eta+r}{\sigma}(t)u,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

with Dirichlet boundary condition
u(t,0)=0
, where

{k(t):=c_{A}\sigma(t)-\frac{\eta(t)+r(t)}{c_{A}}}

for the same constant
c_{A}>0
as above. Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, and define

(B.14)

\sigma(t):=\sqrt{c_{0}}(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{-\alpha},\qquad t\geq 0,

for some constants
c_{0}>0
and
s_{0}>0
. To simplify notation, we suppress the dependence on
\alpha
. Next set

(B.15)

\eta(t):=\alpha_{1}\,2^{-1/3}\,c_{A}^{2/3}\,|\sigma^{\prime}(t)|^{2/3}|\sigma(t)|^{1/3}.

Then

\eta(t)=c_{\eta}(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{-\alpha-2/3},

where

c_{\eta}=\alpha_{1}\,2^{-1/3}c_{A}^{2/3}c_{0}^{7/6}\alpha^{2/3}.

Here
\alpha_{1}
denotes the first positive zero of the Airy function, equivalently the absolute value of the principal eigenvalue of
L_{1}=\partial_{xx}-x
.

Finally, let
r\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R})
be a deterministic function satisfying

(B.16)

|r(t)|\leq(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{-\beta_{1}},\qquad|r^{\prime}(t)|\leq(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{-\beta_{2}},\qquad|r^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{-\beta_{3}},

for some exponents satisfying

\beta_{1}>\alpha+\frac{1}{2},\qquad\beta_{2}>\alpha+\frac{3}{2},\qquad\beta_{3}>2+\alpha.

Proposition B.3 (Airy PDE estimate).

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
.
Consider the PDE (
B.13
), with coefficient functions
\sigma
and
\eta
given by (
B.14
) and (
B.15
), and with
r
satisfying (
B.16
). Let its fundamental solution be denoted by
G_{u}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})
. Set

(B.17)

V(t):=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\left[\frac{c_{A}}{\sigma}\right]^{\prime}(t)=2c_{A}(c_{0})^{-1/2}\alpha(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{3\alpha-1}.

Then there exist constants
s_{c}>0
,
T_{0}>0
sufficiently large, and
C>1
such that, for any
s_{0}\geq s_{c}
and
T\geq T_{0}
,

(B.18)

G_{u}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})\leq Cxy\sqrt{V(t_{1})}\sqrt{V(t_{2})}e^{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{r}{\sigma}(s)\text{d}s},\quad\forall t_{2}-t_{1}\geq(s_{0}+t_{1})^{2/3},\ \forall x,y\geq 0.

Moreover,

(B.19)

\displaystyle G_{u}(x,y;0,T)\geq

\displaystyle C^{-1}xy\sqrt{V(0)}\sqrt{V(T)}e^{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{r}{\sigma}(s)\text{d}s},

\displaystyle\forall T\geq T_{0},\ x\in[0,V(0)^{-1/3}],\ y\in[0,V(T)^{-1/3}].

Without loss of generality, it suffices to consider the case
c_{0}=1
. We denote by
C
numerical constants that may vary from line to line. We split the proof into several steps.

Step 1: Change of variables.
Set

\vartheta(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(s)\text{d}s.

Define
w(\vartheta,x)
by

u(t,x)=w(\vartheta(t),x)e^{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\eta+r}{\sigma}(s)\text{d}s}.

Then
w
satisfies

(B.20)

\partial_{\vartheta}w=\partial_{xx}w-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\left[\frac{k}{\sigma^{2}}\right]^{\prime}(t(\vartheta))xw=:\partial_{xx}w-q(\vartheta)xw

with potential
q(\vartheta)=Q(t(\vartheta))
, where

Q(t):=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\left[\frac{k}{\sigma^{2}}\right]^{\prime}(t).

By this change of variables, the fundamental solution of (
B.20
) is given by

(B.21)

G_{w}(x,y;\vartheta(t_{1}),\vartheta(t_{2}))=e^{-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\eta+r}{\sigma}(s)\text{d}s}G_{u}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2}).

Using the expression (
B.15
) for
\eta
, a direct calculation gives

\displaystyle Q(t)=

\displaystyle 2c_{A}\alpha(s_{0}+t)^{3\alpha-1}-\frac{2c_{\eta}}{c_{A}}(\alpha-2/3)(s_{0}+t)^{3\alpha-5/3}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-\frac{2}{c_{A}}(s_{0}+t)^{2\alpha}\left[\frac{r}{(s_{0}+t)^{-2\alpha}}\right]^{\prime}(t)}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle V(t)+R(t),

where

(B.22)

V(t):=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\left[\frac{c_{A}}{\sigma}\right]^{\prime}(t)=2c_{A}\alpha(s_{0}+t)^{3\alpha-1}

and the residual term is

{R(t):=-\frac{2c_{\eta}}{c_{A}}(\alpha-2/3)(s_{0}+t)^{3\alpha-5/3}-\frac{2}{c_{A}}(s_{0}+t)^{2\alpha}\left[\frac{r}{(s_{0}+t)^{-2\alpha}}\right]^{\prime}(t).}

We will also need the expression of
V(\cdot)
in the
\vartheta
-coordinate. Set
v(\vartheta):=V(t(\vartheta))
. From
\vartheta(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(s)\text{d}s
and the expression (
B.14
) for
\sigma(\cdot)
, a direct calculation gives

(B.23)

{v(\vartheta)=\begin{cases}2c_{A}\alpha\left(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+2(1-2\alpha)\vartheta\right)^{\frac{3\alpha-1}{1-2\alpha}},&\alpha\in(0,1/2),\\[3.99994pt]
2c_{A}\alpha\sqrt{s_{0}}e^{\vartheta},&\alpha=1/2.\end{cases}}

Step 2: Check that
Q(\cdot)
is well approximated by
V(\cdot)
.
By our assumptions (
B.16
) on
r(\cdot)
, we have

|R(t)|\leq C(s_{0}+t)^{-\gamma_{1}},\qquad\left|\frac{dR}{dt}(t)\right|\leq C(s_{0}+t)^{-\gamma_{2}}

for some
\gamma_{1}>\frac{3}{2}-3\alpha
and
\gamma_{2}>2-3\alpha
.
Consequently, for
s_{0}
sufficiently large,

(B.24)

C^{-1}V(t)\leq Q(t)\leq CV(t),\qquad|Q^{\prime}(t)-V^{\prime}(t)|\leq C(s_{0}+t)^{-\gamma_{2}}.

Equivalently, if
v(\vartheta)=V(t(\vartheta))
, then

(B.25)

C^{-1}v(\vartheta)\leq q(\vartheta)\leq Cv(\vartheta),\qquad|(\log q)^{\prime}(\vartheta)|\leq\begin{cases}C\left(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta\right)^{-1},&\alpha<1/2,\\
C,&\alpha=1/2.\end{cases}

We also record the following estimate, which shows that
V(\cdot)
is a good approximation for
Q(\cdot)
and will be used later:

\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta(t_{1})}^{\vartheta(t_{2})}Q(t(\vartheta))^{2/3}\text{d}\vartheta=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(V(t)+R(t))^{2/3}\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(V(t)^{2/3}-|R(t)|^{2/3})\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}V(t)^{2/3}\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}\text{d}t-C\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(s_{0}+t)^{-2\gamma_{1}/3-2\alpha}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}V(t)^{2/3}\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}\text{d}t-C,

where, in the first equality, we use
\vartheta(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(s)\text{d}s
, and, in the last inequality, we use
-2\gamma_{1}/3-2\alpha<-1
. By a similar calculation, we also have

\int_{\vartheta(t_{1})}^{\vartheta(t_{2})}Q(t(\vartheta))^{2/3}\text{d}\vartheta\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}V(t)^{2/3}\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}\text{d}t+C.

Thus,

(B.26)

\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(Q(t)^{2/3}-V(t)^{2/3}\right)\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}\text{d}t\right|\leq C.

Step 3: Freeze the potential on an initial short interval and use parabolic comparison.
With the preparations above, we now estimate the fundamental solution
G_{w}
of the Airy PDE (
B.20
) using a sandwich-type argument. Specifically, we construct two potentials
q_{*}(\cdot)
and
q^{*}(\cdot)
close to
q(\cdot)
, satisfying
q_{*}\leq q\leq q^{*}
, and both constant on a small initial interval.

Set

(B.27)

\displaystyle\Delta\vartheta:=

\displaystyle\vartheta(t_{1}+(s_{0}+t_{1})^{2/3})-\vartheta(t_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{1}+(s_{0}+t_{1})^{2/3}}(s_{0}+s)^{-2\alpha}\text{d}s.

Here the second equality follows from the definition of
\vartheta
and from

\sigma(s)=(s_{0}+s)^{-\alpha}
after setting
c_{0}=1
. When
s_{0}
is large, we have

(B.28)

\Delta\vartheta\asymp(s_{0}+t_{1})^{-2\alpha+2/3}.

Define
q_{*}(\cdot)
and
q^{*}(\cdot)
by

(B.29)

q_{*}(\vartheta):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\min_{\vartheta^{\prime}\in[\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta]}q(\vartheta^{\prime})&\text{ if }\vartheta\in[\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta],\\[3.99994pt]
q(\vartheta)&\text{ if }\vartheta\in[\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta,\vartheta_{2}],\end{cases}

and

(B.30)

q^{*}(\vartheta):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\max_{\vartheta^{\prime}\in[\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta]}q(\vartheta^{\prime})&\text{ if }\vartheta\in[\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta],\\[3.99994pt]
q(\vartheta)&\text{ if }\vartheta\in[\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta,\vartheta_{2}].\end{cases}

These satisfy
q_{*}(\vartheta)\leq q(\vartheta)\leq q^{*}(\vartheta)
. Denote the fundamental solutions of (
B.20
) with potentials
q_{*}
and
q^{*}
by
G_{*}
and
G^{*}
, respectively. By the parabolic comparison principle,

(B.31)

G_{*}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})\geq G_{w}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})\geq G^{*}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}).

We now check that replacing
q(\cdot)
with
q_{*}(\cdot)
or
q^{*}(\cdot)
does not significantly affect the final estimate. Since

\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta(t_{1})}^{\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta}q^{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\max_{\vartheta\in[\vartheta(t_{1}),\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta]}q(\vartheta)^{2/3}\Delta\vartheta

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[t_{1},t_{1}+(s_{0}+t_{1})^{2/3}]}Q(t)^{2/3}(s_{0}+t_{1})^{-2\alpha+2/3}

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[t_{1},t_{1}+(s_{0}+t_{1})^{2/3}]}V(t)^{2/3}(s_{0}+t_{1})^{-2\alpha+2/3},

where, in the second line, we use
\Delta\vartheta\asymp(s_{0}+t_{1})^{-2\alpha+2/3}
, and, in the last line, we use
Q\asymp V
. By the expression (
B.22
) for
V
, we have

\max_{t\in[t_{1},t_{1}+(s_{0}+t_{1})^{2/3}]}V(t)^{2/3}\asymp(s_{0}+t_{1})^{2\alpha-2/3}.

Thus,

(B.32)

\int_{\vartheta(t_{1})}^{\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta}q^{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}=q^{*}(\vartheta(t_{1}))^{2/3}\Delta\vartheta\asymp 1.

Similarly, we can prove that

(B.33)

\int_{\vartheta(t_{1})}^{\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta}q_{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}=q_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1}))^{2/3}\Delta\vartheta\asymp 1.

As a result,

(B.34)

\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta(t_{1})}^{\vartheta(t_{2})}\left|q^{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}-q(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\right|\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C,

\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta(t_{1})}^{\vartheta(t_{2})}\left|q_{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}-q(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\right|\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C,

where we have used the fact that
q_{*}=q=q^{*}
on
[\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta,\vartheta(t_{2})]
and

\max\left\{\left|q^{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}-q(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\right|,\left|q_{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}-q(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\right|\right\}\leq q^{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}+q_{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3},

by our construction (
B.29
) and the positivity of
q_{*}
,
q
, and
q^{*}
.

Step 4: Upper bound for
G_{w}
.
We now prove the upper bound for the fundamental solution
G_{w}
of (
B.20
). Set, for
\vartheta\in[\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}]
,

(B.35)

E_{*}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta):=\exp\left\{\alpha_{1}\int_{\vartheta_{1}}^{\vartheta}q_{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\,\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}\right\}G_{*}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta).

Once we bound
E_{*}
, we immediately obtain the bound for
G_{*}
and hence for
G_{w}
from (
B.31
).

The idea is to decompose
E_{*}
with respect to the orthonormal basis
\{\psi^{q_{*}(\vartheta)}_{n}:n\geq 1\}
of Airy eigenvectors and then study the ODE satisfied by the coefficients

c_{*,n}(\vartheta):=\left\langle E_{*}(x,\cdot;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta),\psi_{n}^{q_{*}(\vartheta)}\right\rangle_{L^{2}},\qquad n\geq 1.

In particular, when
\vartheta=\vartheta_{1}
, since
G_{*}(x,\cdot;\vartheta(t_{1}),\vartheta(t_{1}))=\delta_{x}(\cdot)
, we have

c_{*,n}(\vartheta(t_{1}))=\psi^{q_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1}))}_{n}(x),\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

Denote
\vec{c}_{*}:=(c_{*,1},c_{*,2},\ldots)^{T}
and
\bar{c}_{*}:=(c_{*,2},c_{*,3},\ldots)^{T}
. Then, from (
B.20
) and the Airy eigenvalues defined in (
B.8
), we obtain the ODE; see formula (A.2) in
[
34
]
:

(B.36)

\frac{d}{d\vartheta}\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta)=[D(\vartheta)+A(\vartheta)]\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta),

with

D(\vartheta)=-q_{*}(\vartheta)^{2/3}\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{1})_{i\geq 1}

and

(B.37)

A(\vartheta)=(\log q_{*})^{\prime}(\vartheta)A,

where

A=\frac{1}{6}\left(\left\langle y(\psi^{1}_{i})^{\prime},\psi^{1}_{j}\right\rangle-\left\langle y(\psi^{1}_{j})^{\prime},\psi^{1}_{i}\right\rangle\right)_{i,j\geq 1}.

The matrix
A
is antisymmetric and satisfies

(B.38)

\sum_{j\geq 2}|A_{1,j}|^{2}<C<\infty,

as proved in the proof of Lemma A.3 in
[
34
]
.

For
\vartheta\in[\vartheta(t_{1}),\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta]
,
q_{*}(\vartheta)
is constant by definition (
B.29
). Thus
A(\vartheta)
in the ODE (
B.36
) vanishes, and we have

(B.39)

\displaystyle c_{*,1}(\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta)=

\displaystyle c_{*,1}(\vartheta(t_{1}))=\psi_{1}^{q_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1}))}(x),

\displaystyle c_{*,n}(\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta)=

\displaystyle e^{-q_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1}))^{2/3}(\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{1})\Delta\vartheta}\psi^{q_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1}))}_{n}(x),

for all
n\geq 2
. We next bound
\|\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}
. First, note that

(B.40)

\displaystyle\|\bar{c}_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\geq 2}|c_{*,n}(\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\geq 2}Ce^{-c(\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{1})}{q_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1}))}x^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sqrt{q(\vartheta(t_{1}))}x,

where the first inequality follows from (
B.33
) and (
B.9
), while the last inequality follows from (
B.10
). Then, by (
B.9
) again,

|c_{*,1}(\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta)|\leq C\sqrt{q(\vartheta(t_{1}))}x.

Thus,

(B.41)

\|\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}\leq C\sqrt{q(\vartheta(t_{1}))}x.

For
\vartheta\in[\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta,\vartheta(t_{2})]
, the ODE (
B.36
) gives

\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}\vartheta}\|\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}^{2}=2\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta)^{T}D(\vartheta)\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta)\leq 0,

because
A(\vartheta)
is antisymmetric. Therefore,

(B.42)

\|\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta(t_{2}))\|_{2}\leq\|\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}\leq C\sqrt{q(\vartheta(t_{1}))}x.

Furthermore, when

(B.43)

\vartheta(t_{2})\geq\vartheta(t_{1})+\Delta\vartheta,

we have, by the reconstruction estimate (
B.11
),

(B.44)

\displaystyle E_{*}(x,y;\vartheta(t_{1}),\vartheta(t_{2}))\leq

\displaystyle C\|\vec{c}_{*}(\vartheta(t_{2}))\|_{2}\sqrt{q(\vartheta(t_{2}))}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sqrt{q(\vartheta(t_{1}))}\sqrt{q(\vartheta(t_{2}))}xy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sqrt{V(t_{1})}\sqrt{V(t_{2})}xy,

where, in the last inequality, we use
q(\vartheta(t_{i}))=Q(t_{i})\asymp V(t_{i})
for
i=1,2
. Recalling the definition of
\Delta\vartheta
in (
B.27
), the condition (
B.43
) is equivalent to

t_{2}\geq t_{1}+(s_{0}+t_{1})^{2/3}.

On the other hand,

(B.45)

\displaystyle E_{*}(x,y;\vartheta(t_{1}),\vartheta(t_{2}))=

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\alpha_{1}\int_{\vartheta(t_{1})}^{\vartheta(t_{2})}q_{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}\right\}G_{*}(x,y;\vartheta(t_{1}),\vartheta(t_{2}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\exp\left\{\alpha_{1}\int_{\vartheta(t_{1})}^{\vartheta(t_{2})}q(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}\right\}G_{w}(x,y;\vartheta(t_{1}),\vartheta(t_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\exp\left\{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\alpha_{1}Q(t)^{2/3}\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}-\frac{\eta+r}{\sigma}(t)\right)\text{d}t\right\}G_{u}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\exp\left\{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\alpha_{1}V(t)^{2/3}\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}-\frac{\eta+r}{\sigma}(t)\right)\text{d}t\right\}G_{u}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2}).

Here the first inequality follows from (
B.34
) and (
B.31
), the second equality follows from the relationship (
B.21
) between
G_{w}
and
G_{u}
, and the final inequality follows from (
B.26
). By our choice (
B.15
) of
\eta(\cdot)
and (
B.22
) for
V(\cdot)
, we have

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\alpha_{1}V(t)^{2/3}\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}-\frac{\eta(t)}{\sigma(t)}\right)\text{d}t=0.

Thus, combining (
B.44
) and (
B.45
), we obtain

G_{u}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})\leq Ce^{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{r}{\sigma}(s)\text{d}s}\sqrt{V(t_{1})}\sqrt{V(t_{2})}xy

for any
t_{2}\geq t_{1}+(s_{0}+t_{1})^{2/3}
.

Step 5: Lower bound for
G_{w}
.

Recall the definition of
q^{*}(\cdot)
in (
B.30
), and let
G^{*}
denote the fundamental solution of the Airy PDE (
B.20
) with
q
replaced by
q^{*}
. From now on, we fix
t_{1}=0
and write

\vartheta_{1}:=\vartheta(t_{1})=\vartheta(0),\qquad\vartheta_{2}:=\vartheta(t_{2})=\vartheta(T).

As in Step 4 above, define

E^{*}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}):=\exp\left\{\alpha_{1}\int_{\vartheta_{1}}^{\vartheta_{2}}q^{*}(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\,\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}\right\}G^{*}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}),

and, for each
n\geq 1
, let

c_{n}^{*}(\vartheta):=\left\langle E^{*}(x,\cdot;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta),\,\psi_{n}^{q^{*}(\vartheta)}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Then
\vec{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta):=(c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta),c_{2}^{*}(\vartheta),\dots)^{T}
satisfies the same ODE as in (
B.36
), with
q_{*}
replaced by
q^{*}
. In particular, for
\bar{c}^{*}(\vartheta):=(c^{*}_{2}(\vartheta),\dots)^{T}
, we have

\frac{d}{d\vartheta}\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}^{2}=2\,\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)^{T}D(\vartheta)\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)-2c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta)\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}A_{1,j}(\vartheta)c_{j}^{*}(\vartheta).

Using Cauchy’s inequality and
\sum_{j\geq 2}A_{1,j}(\vartheta)^{2}\leq C|(\log q)^{\prime}(\vartheta)|^{2}
, it follows that

\frac{d}{d\vartheta}\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}^{2}\leq-2q^{*}(\vartheta)^{2/3}(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}^{2}+C|c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta)|\,\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}\,|(\log q)^{\prime}(\vartheta)|.

Therefore,

(B.46)

\frac{d}{d\vartheta}\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}\leq-q^{*}(\vartheta)^{2/3}(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}+C|c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta)|\,|(\log q)^{\prime}(\vartheta)|.

As in (
B.40
), we have

(B.47)

\|\bar{c}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}\leq C\sqrt{q^{*}(\vartheta_{1})}x.

In what follows, we estimate
\|\bar{c}^{*}(\vartheta_{2})\|_{2}
using (
B.46
) and (
B.47
). We discuss only the case
\alpha<1/2
, since the case
\alpha=1/2
is analogous. For
\vartheta\geq\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta
, recall that
q^{*}(\vartheta)=q(\vartheta)
by (
B.30
). Also, from (
B.25
) and (
B.23
), we have

(B.48)

\displaystyle q(\vartheta)

\displaystyle\asymp\left(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta\right)^{\frac{3\alpha-1}{1-2\alpha}},

\displaystyle|(\log q)^{\prime}(\vartheta)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta}.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality to (
B.46
), we obtain

(B.49)

\|\bar{c}^{*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}\leq\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II},\qquad\forall\vartheta\geq\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta,

where

\mathrm{I}:=\exp\left\{-\int_{\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta}^{\vartheta}(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})q(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\,\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}\right\}\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}

and

\mathrm{II}:=C\int_{\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta}^{\vartheta}|c_{1}^{*}(\widetilde{\vartheta})|\,|(\log q)^{\prime}(\widetilde{\vartheta})|\exp\left\{-\int_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}^{\vartheta}(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})q(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\,\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}\right\}\text{d}\widetilde{\vartheta}.

By (
B.48
), we have

\int_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}^{\vartheta}q(\vartheta^{\prime})^{2/3}\,\text{d}\vartheta^{\prime}\geq c\Bigl((s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}-(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\widetilde{\vartheta})^{\gamma}\Bigr),\qquad\gamma:=\frac{1}{3(1-2\alpha)}.

Hence,

\mathrm{I}\leq Ce^{-c\left((s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}-(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)^{\gamma}\right)}|c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)|.

For
\mathrm{II}
, using

|c_{1}^{*}(\widetilde{\vartheta})|\leq\|\vec{c}^{\,*}(\widetilde{\vartheta})\|_{2}\leq\|\vec{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2},

where the second inequality can be obtained in the same way as (
B.42
), we get, together with (
B.48
),

\mathrm{II}\leq C\|\vec{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}\int_{\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta}^{\vartheta}\frac{1}{s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\widetilde{\vartheta}}e^{-c\left((s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}-(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\widetilde{\vartheta})^{\gamma}\right)}\,\text{d}\widetilde{\vartheta}.

Splitting the integral into
[\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta,\vartheta/2]
and
[\vartheta/2,\vartheta]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta}^{\vartheta}\frac{1}{s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\widetilde{\vartheta}}e^{-c\left((s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}-(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\widetilde{\vartheta})^{\gamma}\right)}\,\text{d}\widetilde{\vartheta}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\vartheta}{s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}}e^{-c\left((s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}-(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta/2)^{\gamma}\right)}+\frac{1}{(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}}\right).

Thus,

\mathrm{II}\leq C\|\vec{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}\left[\frac{\vartheta}{s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}}e^{-c\left((s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}-(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta/2)^{\gamma}\right)}+\frac{1}{(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}}\right].

Plugging the estimates for
\mathrm{I}
and
\mathrm{II}
into (
B.49
), and using the comparison between
c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)
and
\|\vec{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}
proved below, we conclude that

(B.50)

\displaystyle\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle C|c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)|\bigg[e^{-c\left((s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}-(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)^{\gamma}\right)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\vartheta}{s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}}e^{-c\left((s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}-(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta/2)^{\gamma}\right)}+\frac{1}{(s_{0}^{1-2\alpha}+\vartheta)^{\gamma}}\bigg].

Since

\vartheta(T)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}(s_{0}+s)^{-2\alpha}\,\text{d}s\to\infty,\qquad\text{as }T\to\infty,

it follows from (
B.50
) that

(B.51)

\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta(T))\|_{2}\leq o_{T}(1)\|\vec{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2},

where
o_{T}(1)\to 0
as
T\to\infty
.

Next, we estimate
c_{1}^{*}(\cdot)
. For the same reason as in (
B.39
), we have

c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)=\psi_{1}^{q^{*}(\vartheta_{1})}(x).

By (
B.12
), we have

c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\geq C^{-1}\sqrt{q^{*}(\vartheta_{1})}x,\quad\forall x\leq q^{*}(\vartheta_{1})^{-1/3}.

As in (
B.41
), we have

\|\vec{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}\leq C\sqrt{q^{*}(\vartheta_{1})}x.

Thus,

(B.52)

c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\geq C^{-1}\|\vec{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\|_{2}.

From the ODE (
B.36
), we have

(B.53)

\displaystyle|c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta(T))-c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta}^{\vartheta(T)}\left|\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}A_{1,j}(\vartheta)c_{j}^{*}(\vartheta)\right|\text{d}\vartheta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta}^{\vartheta_{2}}|(\log q)^{\prime}(\vartheta)|\,\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta)\|_{2}\,\text{d}\vartheta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{s_{0}^{(1-2\alpha)\gamma}}\,|c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)|.

Here the second inequality follows from (
B.37
) and (
B.38
), and the final inequality follows from (
B.48
) and (
B.50
).

Next, we estimate
E^{*}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})
. By definition,

E^{*}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})=c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{2})\psi_{1}^{q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})}(y)+\sum_{n\geq 2}c_{n}^{*}(\vartheta_{2})\psi_{n}^{q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})}(y).

From (
B.9
), (
B.12
), and (
B.11
), for any
y\leq q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})^{-1/3}
we have

\psi_{1}^{q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})}(y)\geq C^{-1}\sqrt{q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})}\,y,

and

\left|\sum_{n\geq 2}c_{n}^{*}(\vartheta_{2})\psi_{n}^{q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})}(y)\right|\leq C\|\bar{c}^{\,*}(\vartheta_{2})\|_{2}\sqrt{q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})}\,y.

As a result, using (
B.51
), (
B.52
), and (
B.53
),

\displaystyle E^{*}(x,y;\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{C}{s_{0}^{(1-2\alpha)\gamma}}-Co_{T}(1)\right)c_{1}^{*}(\vartheta_{1}+\Delta\vartheta)\sqrt{q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})}\,y

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{-1}\sqrt{q^{*}(\vartheta_{1})}\sqrt{q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})}\,xy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{-1}\sqrt{q(\vartheta_{1})}\sqrt{q(\vartheta_{2})}\,xy.

Here
s_{0}
and then
T
are chosen sufficiently large so that the factor in parentheses is positive.

Finally, by the same reasoning as in (
B.45
), and using
q(\vartheta(t))=Q(t)\asymp V(t)
, we obtain

G_{u}(x,y;0,T)\geq C^{-1}e^{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{r}{\sigma}(s)\,\text{d}s}\sqrt{Q(0)}\sqrt{Q(T)}\,xy

for all sufficiently large
T
, provided that

x\leq q^{*}(\vartheta_{1})^{-1/3},\qquad y\leq q^{*}(\vartheta_{2})^{-1/3}.

Recall that
Q\asymp V
, with
V
defined in (
B.22
). After adjusting the constant in the lower eigenfunction bound (
B.12
), these conditions are implied by

x\leq V(0)^{-1/3},\qquad y\leq V(T)^{-1/3}.

This completes the proof of (
B.19
).
∎

Remark B.4.

In Proposition
B.3
, the time interval is assumed to be sufficiently long so that the Airy spectral behavior dominates. We will also need a short-time upper bound on the fundamental solution
G_{u}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})
of (
B.6
). This follows from comparison with Brownian motion killed at the origin.

Indeed, under our assumptions, the linear potential term in (
B.6
) is nonpositive on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Hence, by the comparison principle,

G_{u}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})\leq G_{\widetilde{u}}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2}),

where
G_{\widetilde{u}}
denotes the fundamental solution of

\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(t)\partial_{xx}\widetilde{u}+\frac{\eta+r}{\sigma}(t)\widetilde{u}

on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with Dirichlet boundary condition
\widetilde{u}(t,0)=0
. By the Feynman–Kac formula and the standard heat-kernel bound for one-dimensional Brownian motion killed at the origin, there exists
C>0
such that

G_{\widetilde{u}}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})\leq C\frac{xy}{\bigl(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\sigma^{2}(s)\,ds\bigr)^{3/2}}\exp\!\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\eta+r}{\sigma}(s)\,ds\right).

Therefore,

(B.54)

G_{u}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})\leq C\frac{xy}{\bigl(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\sigma^{2}(s)\,ds\bigr)^{3/2}}\exp\!\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\eta+r}{\sigma}(s)\,ds\right),\qquad x,y\geq 0.

Appendix C
Results for microscopically slowed-down BBM with fixed splitting times

The proof in this section follows the same strategy as in Subsection

2.1
, where the branching times are random
and distributed as Exp
(1)
.

We first introduce the model. Fix a branching period
\Delta t>0
and an
integer offspring number
m\geq 2
. Let
T=N\Delta t
for some integer
N\geq 1
. At each deterministic branching time
k\Delta t
,
k=1,\ldots,N
, every particle is replaced by exactly
m
offspring. As usual, we write
\mathcal{N}_{t}
for the set of particles alive at time

t\in[0,T]
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. Assume the movement of each particle follows the law

(C.1)

Y_{t}^{\alpha}:=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)\,\text{d}B_{s},\qquad t\in[0,T],

where
B
is a standard Brownian motion and

(C.2)

\sigma_{\alpha}(t):=\sqrt{c_{0}}\,(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{-\alpha},

for some constants
c_{0}>0
and
s_{0}\geq 1
.

Set

c_{A}:=\sqrt{\frac{2\log m}{\Delta t}}.

Define

(C.3)

\eta(t):=\alpha_{1}2^{-1/3}c_{A}^{2/3}|\sigma_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)|^{2/3}|\sigma_{\alpha}(t)|^{1/3}=c_{\eta}(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{-\alpha-2/3},

where

c_{\eta}:=\alpha_{1}2^{-1/3}c_{A}^{2/3}\alpha^{2/3}c_{0}^{7/6}.

Let

(C.4)

r(t):=-(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{-1-\beta},\qquad\beta\in(0,\alpha),

It is straightforward to check that
r
satisfies the assumptions in
(
B.16
). We then define

(C.5)

k_{r}(t):=c_{A}\sigma_{\alpha}(t)-\frac{\eta(t)+r(t)}{c_{A}},\qquad A_{r}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}k_{r}(s)\,\text{d}s,

and

(C.6)

A(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\left(c_{A}\sigma_{\alpha}(s)-\frac{\eta(s)}{c_{A}}\right)\text{d}s.

Since
r
is integrable on
[0,\infty)
, there exists a constant
C>0
such
that

(C.7)

|A(T)-A_{r}(T)|\leq C,\qquad T\geq 0.

Moreover, for all sufficiently large
t
,

(C.8)

\frac{k_{r}(t)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(t)^{2}}\asymp\frac{c_{A}}{\sigma_{\alpha}(t)}\asymp(s_{0}+c_{0}t)^{\alpha}.

For later use in Proposition
B.2
, we also
record the following bound. Since

\frac{\eta(s)+r(s)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(s)}=\frac{c_{\eta}}{\sqrt{c_{0}}}(s_{0}+c_{0}s)^{-2/3}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{0}}}(s_{0}+c_{0}s)^{-(1+\beta-\alpha)},

and since both exponents are strictly larger than
1/2
, we have

(C.9)

{0\leq\int_{0}^{T}\frac{(\eta(s)+r(s))^{2}}{\sigma_{\alpha}(s)^{2}}\,\text{d}s\leq C}

for some
0<C<\infty
, uniformly for all sufficiently large
T
.

Proposition C.1.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. Consider the BBM with deterministic branching
period
\Delta t>0
, offspring number
m\geq 2
, and spatial motion given
by (
C.1
). Then there exist constants

c,C>0
and
K_{*},T_{*}>0
such that, for all integers
N\geq 1
with

T=N\Delta t\geq T_{*}
and all
K\geq K_{*}
,

(C.10)

P\Bigl(\exists\,t\in[0,T],\ \exists\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{t}:Y_{t}^{\alpha,v}\geq A(t)+K\Bigr)\leq Ce^{-cK}.

By (
C.7
), it suffices to prove
(
C.10
) with
A(t)
replaced
by
A_{r}(t)
, after adjusting the constants.

We first assume that
s_{0}
is large enough for Proposition

B.3
to apply. This restriction is only
technical. For general
s_{0}\geq 1
, one decomposes the process into an
initial time interval
[0,N_{0}\Delta t]
, with
N_{0}
fixed but large,
followed by the descendant BBMs on
[N_{0}\Delta t,T]
. The contribution
from the initial interval is controlled by a standard Gaussian tail bound
and a union bound over finitely many particles. On the remaining interval,
each descendant BBM has shifted variance profile

\sigma_{\alpha}(N_{0}\Delta t+\cdot)
, so the assumptions of the Airy
estimate hold once
N_{0}
is chosen large enough. This changes only the
constants.

For any continuous process
X
, let

\tau_{0}(X):=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}=0\}

denote its first hitting time of
0
. By a union bound over the branching
intervals, we have

(C.11)

\displaystyle P\Bigl(\exists\,t\in[0,T],\ \exists\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{t}:Y_{t}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{r}(t)+K\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}P\Bigl(\exists\,t\in[j\Delta t,(j+1)\Delta t],\ \exists\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{t}:Y_{t}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{r}(t)+K\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}m^{j+1}P\Bigl(\tau_{0}\bigl(Y^{\alpha}_{\cdot}-A_{r}(\cdot)-K\bigr)\in[j\Delta t,(j+1)\Delta t]\Bigr).

Here
m^{j+1}
is a crude upper bound on the number of particles that may
contribute on the interval
[j\Delta t,(j+1)\Delta t]
.

By the definition of
A_{r}
and Proposition

B.1
,

\displaystyle P\Bigl(\tau_{0}\bigl(Y^{\alpha}_{\cdot}-A_{r}(\cdot)-K\bigr)\in[j\Delta t,(j+1)\Delta t]\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}j\Delta t}e^{-\frac{k_{r}(0)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(0)^{2}}K}E^{(K)}\!\left[\exp\!\left\{\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(Y)}\left(-\left(\frac{k_{r}}{\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(s)Y_{s}+\frac{\eta(s)+r(s)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(s)}\right)\text{d}s\right\}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(Y)\in[j\Delta t,(j+1)\Delta t]\}}\right],

where
E^{(K)}
denotes expectation for the process
Y
started from

Y_{0}=K
.

Since
c_{A}^{2}=2\log m/\Delta t
, we have

e^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}j\Delta t}=m^{-j}.

Therefore (
C.11
) gives

(C.12)

\displaystyle P\Bigl(\exists\,t\in[0,T],\ \exists\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{t}:Y_{t}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{r}(t)+K\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq me^{-\frac{k_{r}(0)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(0)^{2}}K}E^{(K)}\!\left[\exp\!\left\{\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}(Y)}\left(-\left(\frac{k_{r}}{\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(s)Y_{s}+\frac{\eta(s)+r(s)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(s)}\right)\text{d}s\right\}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{0}(Y)\in[0,T]\}}\right].

By the Feynman–Kac representation, see for instance the discussion after
equation
(2.5)
in
[
34
]
, the expectation on the
right-hand side can be written as

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\alpha}(t)^{2}\left.\partial_{y}G_{r}(K,y;0,t)\right|_{y=0}\,\text{d}t,

where
G_{r}
is the fundamental solution of the Airy-type equation

(C.13)

\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\alpha}(t)^{2}\partial_{xx}u-\left(\frac{k_{r}}{\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}}\right)^{\prime}(t)xu+\frac{\eta(t)+r(t)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(t)}u,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

with Dirichlet boundary condition
u(t,0)=0
.

The same argument leading to
(
2.18
) and
(
2.19
),
combined with the Feynman–Kac representation above, gives

(C.14)

{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\alpha}(t)^{2}\left.\partial_{y}G_{r}(K,y;0,t)\right|_{y=0}\,\text{d}t\leq C(1+K),}

uniformly in
T
and
K
.

Since, under the present assumption that
s_{0}
is sufficiently large,

q_{0}:=\frac{k_{r}(0)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(0)^{2}}>0,

we obtain from (
C.12
) and
(
C.14
) that

\displaystyle P\Bigl(\exists\,t\in[0,T],\ \exists\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{t}:Y_{t}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{r}(t)+K\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq Cm(1+K)e^{-q_{0}K}\leq Ce^{-cK},

after adjusting the constants and taking
K
large enough. Thus

(C.15)

P\Bigl(\exists\,t\in[0,T],\ \exists\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{t}:Y_{t}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{r}(t)+K\Bigr)\leq Ce^{-cK}.

This proves the proposition.
∎

The next estimate gives a bridge-type upper bound for the
fixed-splitting BBM.

Proposition C.2.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
and consider the same BBM as in Proposition

C.1
. Recall that
\mathcal{N}_{T}
is the index set of all particles at the terminal time
T
. There exist constants
C,c,C^{\prime},c^{\prime}>0
and thresholds
K_{*},T_{*}>0
such that, for all integers
N\geq 1
with
T=N\Delta t\geq T_{*}
, all
K_{0}\geq K_{*}
, all

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and every deterministic subset
\Gamma\subset\mathcal{N}_{T}
,

(C.16)

P\Bigl(\exists\,v\in\Gamma:Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}\geq A(T)+K_{0}-n\Bigr)\leq Ce^{-c(K_{0}+n)}+C^{\prime}|\Gamma|e^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}T-c^{\prime}K_{0}+c^{\prime}T^{\alpha}(n+1)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1},}

where
c_{A}:=\sqrt{2\log m/\Delta t}
.

As in the proof of Proposition
C.1
,
we may assume that
s_{0}
is large enough for Proposition

B.3
to apply. The general case follows by
decomposing off a fixed initial time interval and adjusting the constants.

Replacing
K
by
K_{0}+n
in (
C.15
), we obtain

P\Bigl(\exists\,t\in[0,T],\ \exists\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{t}:Y_{t}^{\alpha,v}\geq A_{r}(t)+K_{0}+n\Bigr)\leq Ce^{-c(K_{0}+n)}.

Therefore,

(C.17)

\displaystyle P\Bigl(\exists\,v\in\Gamma:Y_{T}^{\alpha,v}\geq A(T)+K_{0}-n\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-c(K_{0}+n)}+|\Gamma|\,P\Bigl(Y_{T}^{\alpha}\geq A(T)+K_{0}-n,\ Y_{t}^{\alpha}\leq A_{r}(t)+K_{0}+n\ \text{for all }t\in[0,T]\Bigr).

By (
C.7
), there exists a constant

C_{0}>0
such that

A(T)\geq A_{r}(T)-C_{0},\qquad T\geq 0.

Hence the last probability in
(
C.17
) is bounded
above by

P\Bigl(Y_{T}^{\alpha}\geq A_{r}(T)+K_{0}-n-C_{0},\ Y_{t}^{\alpha}\leq A_{r}(t)+K_{0}+n\ \text{for all }t\in[0,T]\Bigr).

By Proposition
B.2
and
(
C.9
), this probability is bounded by

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}T-\frac{k_{r}(0)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(0)^{2}}(K_{0}+n)}\int_{0}^{2n+C_{0}}e^{\frac{k_{r}(T)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(T)^{2}}z}G_{r}(K_{0}+n,z;0,T)\,\text{d}z,

where
G_{r}
is the fundamental solution of (
C.13
).

By Proposition
B.3
,

\displaystyle G_{r}(K_{0}+n,z;0,T)

\displaystyle\leq C(K_{0}+n)z\sqrt{s_{0}^{3\alpha-1}}\sqrt{(s_{0}+c_{0}T)^{3\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{r(s)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(s)}\,\text{d}s\right\}.

Since

{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{r(s)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(s)}\,\text{d}s=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{0}}}\int_{0}^{T}(s_{0}+c_{0}s)^{\alpha-1-\beta}\,\text{d}s\leq C-cT^{\alpha-\beta},}

for some constants
C,c>0
, where
\beta<\alpha
, we have

G_{r}(K_{0}+n,z;0,T)\leq C(K_{0}+n)z(s_{0}+c_{0}T)^{(3\alpha-1)/2}e^{-cT^{\alpha-\beta}}.

Consequently,

(C.18)

\displaystyle P\Bigl(Y_{T}^{\alpha}\geq A(T)+K_{0}-n,\ Y_{t}^{\alpha}\leq A_{r}(t)+K_{0}+n\ \text{for all }t\in[0,T]\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}T-\frac{k_{r}(0)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(0)^{2}}(K_{0}+n)}(K_{0}+n)(s_{0}+c_{0}T)^{(3\alpha-1)/2}e^{-cT^{\alpha-\beta}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{2n+C_{0}}z\,e^{\frac{k_{r}(T)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(T)^{2}}z}\,\text{d}z.

Using (
C.8
), we have

\frac{k_{r}(T)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(T)^{2}}\leq CT^{\alpha}

for all sufficiently large
T
. Therefore,

\int_{0}^{2n+C_{0}}z\,e^{\frac{k_{r}(T)}{\sigma_{\alpha}(T)^{2}}z}\,\text{d}z\leq C(n+1)^{2}e^{CT^{\alpha}(n+1)}.

Since
T\geq T_{*}
and
K_{0}\geq K_{*}
which can be large, the factors

(K_{0}+n)(n+1)^{2}
and
(s_{0}+c_{0}T)^{(3\alpha-1)/2}
can be absorbed by decreasing the coefficient of
K_{0}
and increasing the coefficient of

T^{\alpha}(n+1)
. Thus, after adjusting constants,

P\Bigl(Y_{T}^{\alpha}\geq A(T)+K_{0}-n,\ Y_{t}^{\alpha}\leq A_{r}(t)+K_{0}+n\ \text{for all }t\in[0,T]\Bigr)\leq Ce^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{A}^{2}T-c^{\prime}K_{0}+c^{\prime}T^{\alpha}(n+1)}.

Plugging this estimate into
(
C.17
) completes the proof.
∎
