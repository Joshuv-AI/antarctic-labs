 E2C Technical Assistance Opportunities

In-Depth Partnerships ~3 years
Multiyear partnership made up of teams (local governments, community-based organizations, and electric utilities) that work
alongside national laboratory staff to apply robust modeling and analysis tools and simulate hardware in conditions that match
communities' unique contexts to evaluate potential scenarios and strategies before full technology deployment.
Supports nine communities currently.

Peer-Learning Cohorts ~6 months
Six-months of regular convenings where cohorts of up to 15 participants exchange strategies and best practices, learn in a
collaborative environment, and workshop plans and strategies to overcome challenges relating to a similar energy
topic. Eligibility varies by cohort topic.
Supports about 100 communities per year.

Expert Match ~3 months
Short-term, no-cost technical assistance for communities seeking to answer near-term energy questions. Eligible entities include
local governments, utilities, community-based organizations, schools, and nonprofits.
Supports about 200 communities per year. Applications are accepted all year on a rolling basis.

 Budget Justification Example
• For an example of how one city justified expanding fleet telematics
  integration, you may refer to the City of San Francisco Board of Supervisors
  August 2020 Policy Analysis Report: Vehicle Telematics Update. This analysis
  organizes quantifiable cost savings data across several telematics categories:
  speeding, aggressive driving behavior, underutilized vehicles, emissions and
  maintenance, take-home vehicle and misuse, and rental vehicle use. It is
  important to note that the City’s Budget and Legislative Analyst Office noted
  that simply installing the telematics devices did not lead to the highest
  potential cost saving, as robust reporting mechanisms for City departments
  and effective management and analysis of the data is required for the devices
  to be maximally effective.

                                                                 National Laboratory of the Rockies | 14

 Budget Justification Example (continued)
• For overarching cost savings, you may refer to the “Executive Summary” beginning on PDF page 1. Specifically,
  on PDF page 4:
 ◦ “More could be done to curb dangerous driving behavior, reduce vehicle emissions and lower spending and potential
 waste related to vehicle use. Combined, we estimate that these factors cost the City $10.5 million annually. These
 costs could be reduced if Central Shops reported and followed up on more detailed and effective metrics from the
 telematics system… To achieve this, we recommend that the Board of Supervisors consider requesting a minimum set
 of specific and detailed measures for Central Shops to track and report… Central Shops assume these duties with the
 addition of two staff positions costing an estimated $340,348 in annual salary and benefits. We expect that if our
 recommendations are implemented in full, that the cost of these added positions would pay for themselves by
 reducing annual savings related to vehicles use and liability claims across the City.”
• For an example of cost savings from a particular telematics component, you may refer to the “Underutilized
  Vehicles” section on PDF page 22, which discusses how reducing underutilized vehicles can save vehicle, fuel,
  and maintenance costs. Specifically:
 ◦ “Currently, Central Shops uses the telematics system to report on the use, frequency, and characteristics of vehicles.
 In their monthly reports to departments they classify vehicles according to their utilization rates of days used, trips
 taken, and mileage. Central Shops said that ’33 vehicles have been identified as underutilized using telematics data
 and confirmed to have been taken out of their fleet during this process.’ Aside from the value of their vehicle and
 fuel savings, these reductions saved an average in annual maintenance costs of roughly $2,750 per turned-in
 vehicle, which results in an estimated maintenance cost savings of $90,750 per year.”
