Unintended Pregnancy Among Tanzanian Women: Prevalence and Risk Factors From the 2022 Demographic and Health Survey
Authors: Mwajuma Bakari Mdoe, Elihuruma Eliufoo Stephano, Tegemea Patrick Mwalingo, Salma Karim Mohamed, Abeid Issa Ikwasa, Victoria Godfrey Majengo, Mtoro J. Mtoro
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsUnintended pregnancies pose a significant public and reproductive health challenge, with substantial adverse effects on mothers, children, and the wider population. Despite these negative consequences, unintended pregnancies remain prevalent in Tanzania. This study aimed to determine the prevalence and associated factors of unintended pregnancy in Tanzania.MethodsThis study employed an analytical cross‐sectional study that was conducted using secondary data from the 2022 Tanzania Demographic and Health Survey. We included 1182 women selected through a two‐stage sampling strategy. Statistical analyses were conducted using STATA version 18, employing descriptive statistics and a weighted binary logistic regression model to identify factors associated with unintended pregnancy. Odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated to assess the strength and magnitude of the associations.ResultsThe prevalence of unintended pregnancy among women of reproductive age in Tanzania was 27.7% (95% CI: 24.6–31.0). In the adjusted analysis, women aged 25–34 (AOR = 0.49, 95% CI: 0.30–0.80), those aged ≥ 35 years (AOR = 0.50, 95% CI: 0.26–0.96), married (AOR = 0.14, 95% CI: 0.07–0.28), cohabiting (AOR = 0.13, 95% CI: 0.06–0.27), and previously married women (AOR = 0.23, 95% CI: 0.10–0.56) had significantly lower odds of unintended pregnancy. Women from a household with at least six members had higher odds of unintended pregnancy compared to their counterparts (AOR = 1.79, 95% CI: 1.25–2.57). Regarding geographical zones, women in eastern (AOR = 2.06, 95% CI: 1.03–4.14) and Zanzibar (AOR = 2.79, 95% CI: 1.44–5.38) had a higher likelihood of unintended pregnancy than those in the western zone of mainland Tanzania.ConclusionNearly three in ten women of reproductive age experienced an unintended pregnancy in Tanzania. This study recommends the use of multifaceted strategies that emphasize youth‐friendly reproductive health services, reduction of social barriers for unmarried women, tailored family planning support for large households, and strengthening healthcare access in geographically vulnerable regions to reduce the rate of unintended pregnancies.

Background

An unplanned pregnancy is a pregnancy that occurs when a woman desires no children or no more children (unwanted pregnancy) [1]. It is a major public health concern due to its social, economic, and health consequences. Worldwide, 44% of all pregnancies are unintended pregnancies [2]. In Sub‐Saharan Africa (SSA), unintended pregnancies account for 33.9% of all pregnancies [2]. About 6 in every 10 pregnancies end in abortion, which has significant health consequences if they are done unsafely [3].

The health burden of unintended pregnancy is well documented. It is associated with unfavorable birth outcomes, including preterm birth and low birthweight. They may result in neonatal mortality, lack of preconception care that exposes the fetus to illicit drug use, alcohol, and smoking, as well as poor nutrition, with long‐lasting impact on health and development of the fetus [2, 4]. Furthermore, unintended pregnancy is linked to late antenatal care booking and may result in a lack of essential obstetric care. Additionally, unintended pregnancies have been associated with gestational diabetes, more vaginal bleeding during childbirth, low self‐esteem, and depression in parents [4, 5].

Multiple socio‐demographic and behavioral factors have been identified as predictors of unintended pregnancy. These are young maternal age, migration background, lower educational level, poverty, being single, drug use before pregnancy, multiple sexual partners in the year before the pregnancy, younger age of first sexual initiation, and a history of abortion [4]. Also, limited access to health care services, higher parity, preference of the partner for children, and domestic violence have been reported as determinants of unplanned pregnancy [5].

One of the most effective strategies to reduce unintended pregnancies is improving access to comprehensive family planning services [6]. This helps women to plan and space their pregnancies according to their preferences. Despite the evidence shown by family planning to prevent approximately 44% maternal deaths annually [7], over 160 million women and adolescents lack access to contraceptive methods of their choice worldwide [8].

In Tanzania, efforts to reduce unintended pregnancy include reducing the unmet need for family planning, which declined from 28% in 1992 to 21% in 2022 [9]. Youth‐friendly services and postpartum family planning have also been integrated into the national reproductive health strategy [10, 11]. Most of the existing estimates of intended pregnancy are outdated and may not accurately reflect the current context. Given that high rates of unintended pregnancies weaken progress toward national development objectives and Sustainable Development Goal 3, particularly in reducing maternal and child mortality, up‐to‐date and contextualized evidence is crucial. This study aimed to fill this gap by using the latest Tanzania Demographic and Health Survey (TDHS 2022) data to estimate the prevalence of unintended pregnancies and examine their associated factors among women of reproductive age.

Methods

Data Source and Setting, Study Design, and Sampling

A detailed description of the TDHS methodology, including sampling and data collection procedures, has been explained elsewhere [9]. In summary, the TDHS employed a two‐stage sampling design. In the first stage, 629 clusters based on census clusters were selected and listed. In the second stage, 26 households were systematically sampled from each cluster, resulting in a total of 16,354 households. This study employed analytical cross‐sectional design using secondary data from 2022 TDHS. This study utilized individual file records with 15,254 women. However, our analysis was restricted to 1182 women who self‐reported being pregnant, after excluding women with missing information on pregnancy intention.

Study Variables

Dependent Variable

The dependent variable for the study was “pregnancy intentions,” which was derived from the question: “Did you want your current pregnancy?”. It had three responses: “then”, “later”, and “not at all”. We recoded these three responses: then = 0 “intended”; “later and not at all” = 1 “unintended” as used by previous studies in SSA [12, 13].

Independent Variables

Independent variables were selected based on 2022 TDHS data and relevant literature [1, 12, 13, 14, 15]. Women's age categories in years (15–24, 25–34, or 35–49), education level (no formal education, primary, secondary, or higher), and place of residence (urban or rural). Marital status was recoded from the DHS categories into four groups: never married, married, cohabiting, and separated/divorced/widowed for analytical and comparability purposes. Household economic status was assessed using a wealth index constructed from indicators of asset ownership and living conditions. Dichotomous variables representing household assets were reduced through principal component analysis (PCA), with item weights applied to generate a composite wealth score [16]. This score was divided into five quintiles: poorest, poorer, middle, richer, and richest. For this analysis, the quintiles were grouped into three categories: “poor” (poorest and poorer), “middle” (middle), and “rich” (richer and richest). Media exposure (yes: listening to the radio, reading newspapers, or watching television at least once a week, and no if otherwise). Number of household members (< 6 or ≥ 6), employment (working or not working), parity (none, 1–3 or ≥ 4), visited health facility in the past 12 months (yes or no), visited by community health worker in the past 12 months (yes or no), ever experienced sexual violence (yes or no), sex of household head (male or female), ever experienced pregnancy termination (yes or no) and geographical zones (western, northern, central, southern, eastern or Zanzibar).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using STATA 18 (STATA Corp, College Station, TX). Individual sampling weights (v005/1,000,000) were used to account for the TDHS‐MIS complex sampling structure. Stratification (v023) and PSUs (clusters; v021) were also taken into consideration to reduce sampling biases and generalizability of findings. Descriptive analysis was conducted by summarizing means, standard deviations (SD), median (interquartile range [IQR]) for continuous variables, and frequencies with percentages for categorical variables. The “svyset” command in Stata was used in declaring the survey data for the appropriate estimation of the results.

Bivariate analysis using the Pearson χ 2 test of independence was used to compare the differences in the proportion of unintended pregnancies across participants' characteristics [17]. This approach provides considerable information about how each of the groups performed in the analysis. A weighted binary logistic regression model was used to determine factors associated with unintended pregnancy, with results presented using odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). Backward stepwise regression with a p value of < 0.2 was used to identify the covariates to be included in the multivariable analyses. This higher p value was chosen to allow a more flexible selection process, ensuring that potential confounding factors were effectively considered and ruled out [18]. Multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF). The mean VIF of 3.81 confirmed the absence of significant multicollinearity. The multivariable binary logistic regression model was fitted, controlling for potential confounders, including women's age. Despite the high prevalence of the unintended pregnancy, this study used a logistic regression model as it was suggested by the lowest Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), suggesting a better model fit [19]. The proportion of missing data was minimal (< 2% for most variables). Therefore, a complete case analysis was applied, as there was no indication of systematic bias. Statistical significance was set at a p value of < 0.05.

Results

Characteristics of Study Participants and Distribution of Unintended Pregnancy

A total of 1182 pregnant women were analyzed. The mean age was 27.3 years (standard deviation = 6.9), with nearly 40% of participants aged 15–24. Over half (57.5%) had completed primary education, and 60.4% were married. Regarding socioeconomic status, 42.3% were in the poorest quintile, while 40.3% were in the wealthiest quintile. About 16.0% had ever terminated a pregnancy, and 5.5% had experienced sexual violence. Regarding access to healthcare, 4.4% had been visited by community healthcare workers in the past year, and 64.8% had visited a health facility during the same period.

The distribution of unintended pregnancies varied significantly by age group, marital status, parity, and household size (p < 0.05). The prevalence of unintended pregnancy was higher among women aged 35–49 (36.3%) compared to women aged 15–24 (27.5%). Never‐married women reported a higher proportion of unintended pregnancy (55.3%) than cohabiting women (22.1%). Similarly, women with higher parity revealed a higher proportion of unintended pregnancy (41.7%) than nulliparous women (22.1%). Women living in a household of more than six members had a higher proportion of unintended pregnancy (37.0%) than their counterparts (21.0%) (Table 1).

Prevalence of Unintended Pregnancy

The prevalence of unintended pregnancy among women of reproductive age in Tanzania was 27.7% (95%CI: 24.6–31.0). Overall, the prevalence of unintended pregnancy was low in Zanzibar (4.1%) and highest in the Lake Zone (30.1%) of mainland Tanzania (Figure 1).

Factors Associated With Unintended Pregnancy

Table 2 presents a weighted binary logistic regression analysis for factors associated with unintended pregnancy. In the adjusted analysis, women aged 25–34 years (AOR = 0.49, 95% CI: 0.30–0.80) and those aged 35 years and above (AOR = 0.50, 95% CI: 0.26–0.96) had lower odds of unintended pregnancy compared to those aged 15–24 years. Women who were married (AOR = 0.14, 95% CI: 0.07–0.28), cohabiting (AOR = 0.13, 95% CI: 0.06–0.27), and previously married (AOR = 0.23, 95% CI: 0.10–0.56) had significantly lower odds of unintended pregnancy compared to those who were never married. Women from a household with at least six members had higher odds of unintended pregnancy compared to their counterparts (AOR = 1.79, 95% CI: 1.25–2.57). Women in eastern (AOR = 2.06, 95% CI: 1.03–4.14) and in Zanzibar (AOR = 2.79, 95% CI: 1.44–5.38) were more likely to have an unintended pregnancy compared to those in the western zone of mainland Tanzania (Table 2).

Discussion

This study aimed to assess the prevalence and associated factors of unintended pregnancies among women of reproductive age in Tanzania using the secondary analysis of the 2022 DHS. The study reports a prevalence of unintended pregnancy at 27.7% (95% CI: 24.6–31.0) among the studied population. The finding highlights that more than one in four women in Tanzania experience pregnancies that are either mistimed or unwanted, emphasizing a significant public health challenge in the country. When compared with other studies in SSA, this prevalence is consistent with the regional trend of unintended pregnancies. Pooled data from multiple countries in SSA indicate an unintended pregnancy prevalence of approximately 30.01% [1, 20]. Similarly, studies have shown variations across the continent, with Nigeria reporting a prevalence of approximately 28% and Ghana a higher rate of around 33.4% [21]. At the same time, Namibia and the Southern African regions have recorded even higher figures, up to 54.5% in some instances [20]. A previous study conducted in a single area in Tanzania showed a prevalence lower than that of the current study [14], similar to another study involving several Asian countries [15]. These variations can be attributed to differences in socio‐economic factors, contraceptive knowledge and usage, educational levels, cultural influences, and accessibility to reproductive health services across countries. Overall, the Tanzanian prevalence highlights the urgent need for interventions to improve family planning awareness, enhance contraceptive access, and address socio‐cultural barriers to empower women in reproductive decision‐making.

The logistic regression analysis reveals important demographic and contextual factors associated with unintended pregnancy among women of reproductive age in Tanzania.

Finding indicates that an increase in age was associated with significantly lower odds of unintended pregnancy. This age‐related pattern is supported by prior research showing that younger women, particularly adolescents and young adults, face a disproportionately higher risk of unintended pregnancies due to factors such as limited knowledge of contraception, inconsistent use, and social vulnerability [15, 22, 23]. Given this, adolescent‐ and youth‐friendly reproductive health services should be strengthened, incorporating comprehensive sexual education, counseling, and access to contraceptive commodities to empower younger women to make informed reproductive choices [23, 24]. There is a need for enhanced community outreach programs and peer education to improve contraceptive uptake and reduce unintended pregnancies in this vulnerable age group.

Regarding marital status, the adjusted analysis shows that married and previously married women have a reduced rate of unintended pregnancy relative to never‐married women. This finding aligns with studies from both Tanzania and other African countries, demonstrating that unmarried women often encounter higher risks due to stigma, social isolation, and limited access to reproductive health services [20, 22]. To address this disparity, policies should aim to reduce social barriers and discrimination against unmarried women seeking contraception [15]. Interventions could include the provision of confidential youth‐friendly services, empowerment programs promoting autonomy in sexual and reproductive health decisions [25], and involving men and community leaders to foster supportive environments [1, 23]. Additionally, integrating reproductive health education with social support mechanisms can reduce the unmet need among unmarried women.

The finding that women with higher parity showed an increased odds of unintended pregnancy highlights a critical area warranting further investigation into the underlying factors. One possible explanation is contraceptive fatigue, where repeated use and potential side effects of contraceptive methods can lead to decreased adherence or discontinuation, negatively impacting effective contraception among high‐parity women [26]. Furthermore, constricted access to family planning services, including limited method choice, stock‐outs, provider biases, and financial or logistical barriers, can disproportionately affect high‐parity women who may require tailored interventions yet encounter systemic obstacles [27]. These intersecting factors suggest that addressing the elevated unmet family planning needs that require culturally sensitive programming, improved healthcare system responsiveness, and psychosocial support to mitigate contraceptive fatigue and empower women to exercise informed reproductive choices effectively.

Household size also emerges as a salient factor, with women from households of six or more members showing higher odds of unintended pregnancy compared to those in smaller households. Previous studies reported similar associations, suggesting that larger households might face resource constraints, reduced privacy, and challenges accessing health information and contraceptives [1, 23]. Another study conducted in the Oromia region, Ethiopia, and Sierra Leone also showed that having large household sizes was associated with an increased rate of unintended pregnancy [28, 29]. Programs targeting family planning services should consider household composition, potentially deploying community health workers to deliver tailored counseling and contraceptives in densely populated households. Moreover, improving mass media campaigns and community engagement in larger households might enhance knowledge and encourage contraceptive uptake, reducing unintended pregnancy risks.

Geographically, women residing in the eastern zone and Zanzibar have higher odds of unintended pregnancy compared to those in the western zone. While crude estimates show significant regional disparities, these differences are attenuated after adjusting for confounding factors such as socioeconomic status, education, and access to health services, indicating that variations in these determinants partly explain the geographic differences. Nonetheless, persistent disparities suggest that regional differences in contraceptive prevalence, cultural norms, and health service accessibility continue to influence unintended pregnancy rates [30]. To effectively mitigate these regional inequities, interventions should target local barriers by enhancing the availability and consistent supply of diverse contraceptive methods, improving the quality of family planning counseling, and integrating reproductive health services within existing community structures. Addressing both individual‐level factors and systemic regional challenges will be crucial to reducing unintended pregnancies across Tanzanian regions [31].

The nonsignificant predictive value of media exposure and place of residence in family planning utilization may be attributed to factors such as message saturation, where repeated exposure to family planning messages leads to diminished impact and decreased variability in behavioral responses across populations [32]. Additionally, the quality of the messages disseminated through mass media may influence their effectiveness; low‐quality or poorly tailored content might fail to motivate changes in contraceptive use despite widespread reach [33]. Furthermore, the similarity in access to family planning services across urban and rural areas could reduce residence‐based disparities, as comparable availability and outreach efforts minimize differences in contraceptive uptake attributable to location [34]. Together, these findings suggest that simply increasing media exposure or physical access is insufficient. Therefore, enhancing the potency and relevance of family planning communications, as well as addressing nuanced socio‐cultural barriers, are essential for improving utilization rates across different demographic groups.

Strengths and Limitations

This study's key strength lies in its use of the nationally representative 2022 TDHS data, which provides a large and diverse sample of women of reproductive age, enabling robust and generalizable knowledge of unintended pregnancy prevalence and associated factors nationwide. Employing rigorous statistical methods enables the careful adjustment of confounding variables and the identification of independent predictors with high reliability. Additionally, using standardized DHS survey instruments enhances the credibility and comparability of findings within and beyond Tanzania. However, limitations include the cross‐sectional nature of the survey data, which precludes establishing causal relationships between identified factors and unintended pregnancies, limiting interpretations to associations only. Self‐reported pregnancy intention may be subject to recall bias or social desirability bias, potentially resulting in underreporting or misclassification of unintended pregnancies. Furthermore, some relevant contextual or behavioral factors influencing pregnancy intention may not have been captured or measured in the survey, constraining the comprehensiveness of the explored determinants. The exclusion of women who were not currently pregnant but sexually active could restrict the generalizability of the findings to all women at risk of unintended pregnancy. Lastly, regional or cultural heterogeneity in reporting might affect response uniformity, possibly affecting the accuracy of prevalence estimates in certain subpopulations.

Conclusion and Recommendation

This study highlights a significant prevalence of unintended pregnancies among women of reproductive age in Tanzania, underscoring the persistent reproductive health challenges faced by this population. Identifying key associated factors, including younger age, marital status, household size, and geographic location, provides valuable direction for targeted public health interventions. This study recommends the use of multi‐dimensional strategies that emphasize youth‐friendly reproductive health services, reduction of social barriers for unmarried women, tailored family planning support for large households, and strengthening healthcare access in geographically vulnerable regions to reduce the rate of unintended pregnancy. Interventions like mobile clinics, which have proven to be effective, as well as the use of community health workers (CHW). These findings underscore the need for policymakers, healthcare providers, and community stakeholders to collaborate in developing culturally sensitive, accessible, and equitable reproductive health programs. Ultimately, reducing unintended pregnancies will contribute to improved maternal and child health outcomes and foster women's empowerment in Tanzania's broader social and development agenda. Further longitudinal analyses and qualitative research are required so as to fully examine the decision‐making process for adopting these suggested interventions and hence formulate targeted and sustained policies to reduce unintended pregnancies.
