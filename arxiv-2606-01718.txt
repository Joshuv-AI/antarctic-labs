Asymptotic Recovery in Fourier Spectral Methods for the Schrödinger Equation with Point Singularities
Yanjie Li, Sihong Shao

Abstract
This paper studies the Fourier spectral method (FSM) for the Schrödinger equation with singular potentials V ∈ H s V\in H^{s} , where s > max ⁡ { d / 2 − 2 , − 1 } s>\max\{d/2-2,-1\} and d d denotes the spatial dimension. This setting includes a broad class of singular potentials, such as the 3D Coulomb potential and the 1D Dirac-delta potential. First, we combine the Feshbach-Schur map with a refined perturbation argument to derive sharp convergence orders for FSM, yielding order 2 ​ s + 2 2s+2 for eigenvalues and order s + 1 s+1 for eigenfunctions in the H 1 H^{1} norm. More importantly, the H 1 H^{1} error with respect to the projected eigenfunction converges with a higher order s + 1 + b s+1+b , where b = min ⁡ { s + 2 − d / 2 − ε , s + 1 , 2 } > 0 b=\min\{s+2-d/2-\varepsilon,\;s+1,\;2\}>0 for arbitrarily small ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , revealing a super-convergence phenomenon. Second, in the presence of potentials with isolated point singularities, we develop an asymptotic-recovery (AR) technique to post-process the FSM solutions. The resulting method, dubbed AR-FSM, fully exploits the super-convergence property and achieves convergence orders 2 ​ s + 2 + 2 ​ b 2s+2+2b for eigenvalues and s + 1 + b s+1+b for eigenfunctions in the H 1 H^{1} norm, while the AR post-processing requires only a computational cost that is linear in the number of FSM degrees of freedom. The analysis introduces a rigorous definition of point singularities and develops a foundational framework for their study. It further establishes an asymptotic expansion of eigenfunctions consisting of a regular component in H s + 4 H^{s+4} together with d + 1 d+1 asymptotic functions associated with each singular point. Numerical experiments confirm the sharpness of these theoretical bounds.

Introduction

Accurate calculation of eigenpairs of the Schrödinger operator is of broad scientific and engineering importance, motivating the development of numerous numerical schemes. In practice, however, physically relevant potentials often contain point singularities, such as the Coulomb potential in electronic systems or Dirac‑delta interactions that model point defects and impurities
dean2021delta
;
holmer2007delta
. The presence of such singularities can cause standard numerical methods to break down or suffer from severely degraded performance. This limitation creates a strong demand for robust, high‑order accurate schemes capable of handling singular potentials efficiently.

Several approaches have been proposed to address the difficulty caused by singular potentials.
Pseudo-potential methods
troullier1991PP
;
kresse1994PP
;
ramer1999PP
;
hamann2013PP
seek to replace the singular potential with an effective one, thus lifting the regularity of the solution.
Other strategies include the use of linear combinations of atomic orbitals
bachmayr2014LCAO
;
scholz2017LCAO
;
li2017invpow
, as well as explicit correlation methods
shiozaki2009EC
;
ten2012EC
;
kong2012EC
;
li2012EC
;
hattig2012EC
, which incorporate electron–electron correlations directly into the approximation space.

From the perspective of Fourier spectral methods (FSM), also known as plane-wave methods, the global Fourier basis offers several attractive features, including natural periodicity, exponential convergence for smooth problems, and efficient implementation via the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT).
However, the presence of point singularities in the potential severely degrades the performance of FSM.
The underlying difficulty stems from the cusp-like local behavior of the eigenfunction near each singular point.
These cusps lead to slow decay of the Fourier coefficients, which limits the overall convergence order. To address this issue within the plane-wave framework, several hybrid approaches have been developed.
Among them, the linearized augmented plane-wave method
madsen2001LAPW
;
blugel2006LAPW
and the projector augmented-wave method
blochl1994PAW
;
kresse1999PAW
are among the most widely used techniques in electronic structure calculations.

From a theoretical standpoint, it has been established in
cances2010THM
that FSM achieves convergence of order
2s+2
for eigenvalues and order
s+1
for eigenfunctions in the
H^{1}
norm when the potential satisfies
V\in H^{s}
with
s>d/2
, where
d
denotes the spatial dimension and
H^{s}
is the periodic Sobolev space. The same convergence orders were shown for piecewise smooth potentials
V\in H^{1/2-\varepsilon}
in two dimensions
norton2010FSM-Theorem
. For singular potentials with
s\leq d/2
, however, rigorous convergence analysis of FSM remains limited. To the best of our knowledge,
dusson2023FSM-Theorem
provides one of the few available results in this direction, showing that FSM achieves convergence of order
2r
for eigenvalues and eigenfunctions in the
L^{2}
norm, provided that
V
acts as a bounded operator from
H^{1-r}
to
H^{-1+r}
. However, these orders appear not to be sharp in general.

In this paper, we impose the following assumption on the potential. This assumption includes a wide range of singular potential including the 1D Dirac delta potential (
s=-0.5^{\textbf{-}}
) as well as the 3D Coulomb potential (
s=0.5^{\textbf{-}}
), where the notation
a^{\textbf{-}}
denotes
a-\varepsilon
for arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
.

Assumption 1
.

The potential satisfies
V\in H^{s}
with
s>\max\{d/2-2,\,-1\}
.

Under this assumption, we adapt the Feshbach-Schur map developed in
dusson2023FSM-Theorem
to obtain sharp convergence orders for FSM. The main improvement in the analysis is a perturbation argument performed directly on the first
n
eigenvalues, which allows full exploitation of the regularity of the corresponding eigenfunctions. As a result, FSM achieves convergence orders
2s+2
for eigenvalues and
s+1
for eigenfunctions in the
H^{1}
norm. Moreover, the
H^{1}
error with respect to the projected eigenfunction converges with order
s+1+b
, where
b=\min\{(s+2-d/2)^{\textbf{-}},s+1,2\}
, revealing a super-convergence phenomenon. This result extends the analysis for regular potentials in
cances2010THM
;
cances2018THM
to the singular setting, constituting the first contribution of this paper.

Motivated by the super-convergence property, we develop an asymptotic-recovery (AR) technique for potentials with point singularities, providing a post-processing procedure to recover the coefficients in the asymptotic expansion of eigenfunctions and thereby reconstruct the high-frequency content of the FSM solution. The resulting method, termed AR-FSM, fully exploits the super-convergence property and achieves convergence orders
2s+2+2b
for eigenvalues and
s+1+b
for eigenfunctions in the
H^{1}
norm.
The analysis introduces a rigorous definition of point singularities and develops a foundational framework for their study. Within this framework, we establish an asymptotic expansion of eigenfunctions consisting of a regular component in
H^{s+4}
together with
d+1
asymptotic functions associated with each singular point. These results provide a precise description of the high-frequency behavior of the Fourier coefficients and are of independent interest beyond the AR-FSM framework. This constitutes the second contribution of this paper.

1.1
Problem set up

Consider the
d
-dimensional Schrödinger operator

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{V}=-\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}}+V(\bm{x}),

(1.1)

with a periodic potential
V
on
\Omega=[0,1]^{d}
, acting on the Hilbert space

L_{\text{per}}^{2}(\Omega):=\{\psi(\bm{x})\in L_{\text{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})|\text{$\psi(\bm{x})$ is periodic on $\Omega$}\},

equipped with the inner product
\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}\overline{f(\bm{x})}{g(\bm{x})}\,dx
.
Under Assumption
1
, the operator
\mathcal{H}
admits a non-decreasing sequence of eigenpairs
(\lambda_{n},\psi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}\times H^{1}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, with
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq\cdots
, satisfying

\langle\phi,\mathcal{H}\psi_{n}\rangle=\lambda_{n}\langle\phi,\psi_{n}\rangle\quad\forall\,\phi\in H^{1}.

(1.2)

Here,
H^{s}
denotes the periodic Sobolev space of order
s\in\mathbb{R}
with the norm

\|\psi\|_{H^{s}}:=\left(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(4\pi^{2}|\bm{k}|^{2}+1)^{s}|\hat{\psi}_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(1.3)

where
\hat{\psi}_{\bm{k}}=\langle e_{\bm{k}},\psi\rangle
is the Fourier coefficients of
\psi
. and
e_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})=e^{2\pi i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}

is the Fourier basis. The goal is to develop numerical methods for computing the eigenpairs
(\lambda_{n},\psi_{n})
defined by (
1.2
).

Throughout this paper, we write
A\lesssim B
to denote that there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
and
\psi
such that
A\leq CB
.
For simplicity, we also omit the domain
\Omega
when referring to periodic function spaces (for instance,
H^{s}
and
W^{t,\infty}
in Lemma
5.5
), whereas the domain is written explicitly for non-periodic function spaces (for instance,
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
H^{s}(B_{R})
in Lemma
6.3
).

1.2
Fourier spectral method

FSM solves (
1.2
) by expanding the eigenfunction
\psi
in a finite Fourier basis.
For truncation parameter
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, define the truncated index set and the corresponding finite-dimensional function space by

\mathscr{K}_{N}:=\left\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\ |\ \|\bm{k}\|_{\infty}\leq N\right\},\quad\mathscr{X}_{N}:=\text{span}\{e_{\bm{k}}|\ \bm{k}\in\mathscr{K}_{N}\}.

Applying the Galerkin method to (
1.2
) over
\mathscr{X}_{N}

leads to the algebraic eigenvalue problem: Find

(\lambda_{n}^{N},\psi_{n}^{N})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathscr{X}_{N}
such that

\langle\phi,\mathcal{H}\psi_{n}^{N}\rangle=\lambda_{n}^{N}\langle\phi,\psi_{n}^{N}\rangle,\qquad\forall\,\phi\in\mathscr{X}_{N}.

(1.4)

Let
\mathcal{P}_{N}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
\mathscr{X}_{N}
, defined by

\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi:=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathscr{K}_{N}}\langle e_{\bm{k}},\psi\rangle e_{\bm{k}}.

Then (
1.4
) can equivalently be written as

\mathcal{P}_{N}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi_{n}^{N}=\lambda_{n}^{N}\psi_{n}^{N},

Our convergence analysis for FSM follows from the key lemma below.

Lemma 1.1
.

Under Assumption
1
, let

b=\min\{(s+2-d/2)^{\textbf{-}},\,s+1,\,2\}.

Then for fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and sufficiently large
N
, we have

\|\psi_{n}^{N}-\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim N^{-(s+1+b)}\|\psi_{n}^{N}\|_{L^{2}},

(1.5)

where
\psi\in\operatorname{ker}(\lambda_{n}-\mathcal{H})
is an exact eigenfunction associated with
\lambda_{n}
.

Based on this lemma, we prove the optimal convergence order of FSM.

Theorem 1

(
Convergence order of FSM
)
.

Under Assumption
1
, for fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and sufficiently large
N
, we have

\|\psi_{n}^{N}-\psi\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim N^{-(s+1)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}},\qquad|\lambda_{n}^{N}-\lambda_{n}|\lesssim N^{-(2s+2)}.

(1.6)

The proofs of Lemma
1.1
and Theorem
1
are deferred to Section
5
.

The estimate in Lemma
1.1
implies a super-convergence of order
b
for the eigenfunctions in the
H^{1}
norm.
This observation naturally suggests the use of post-processing techniques to exploit this super-convergence.
In particular, two-grid methods
cances2016post
;
cances2021post
;
dusson2021post2
have been proposed for this purpose.
These approaches solve the problem on a coarse grid and then lift the solution to a finer grid, thereby reducing the computational cost of the eigensolver. However, the overall computational complexity includes the cost of FFT performed on the finer Fourier grid.
Consequently, while these methods improve efficiency, they do not fundamentally enhance the convergence order.

The proposed AR post-processing is based on decomposing the eigenfunction into the low and high-frequency components, in the spirit of two-grid methods. However, instead of representing the high-frequency part on a finer Fourier grid, we approximate it using the intrinsic asymptotic behavior of the eigenfunctions. To illustrate this idea, we provide a simple yet representative example. Consider the one‑dimensional Schrödinger operator with Dirac-delta potential
V(x)=\gamma\delta(x)
. The weak form of the eigenvalue problem (
1.2
) then reads

\langle\phi,\mathcal{T}\psi\rangle+\gamma\overline{\phi(0)}\psi(0)=\lambda\langle\phi,\psi\rangle,\quad\forall\,\phi\in H^{1}.

(1.7)

Substituting the test function
\phi(x)=e_{k}(x)
into the weak formulation (
1.7
) gives

\hat{\psi}_{k}=\frac{-\gamma\psi(0)}{4\pi^{2}k^{2}-\lambda}.

(1.8)

This relation reveals the leading asymptotic decay
\hat{\psi}_{k}\sim-\gamma\psi(0)/4\pi^{2}k^{2}
of the eigenfunction
\psi(x)
. To match this decay, we introduce the asymptotic function

\Phi(x)=1+\gamma\frac{\sin(\pi|x|)}{2\pi}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\hat{\Phi}_{k}=\delta_{0k}+\frac{-\gamma}{4\pi^{2}k^{2}-\pi^{2}},

(1.9)

whose Fourier coefficients reproduce the same
k^{-2}
asymptotic decay.
Let
\psi^{N}\in\mathscr{X}_{N}
be a numerical solution.
We define the AR operator
\mathcal{A}_{N}
by introducing a high-frequency correction

\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi^{N}:=\psi^{N}+\beta\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}\Phi=\underbrace{\psi^{N}-\beta\mathcal{P}_{N}\Phi}_{\in\mathscr{X}_{N}}+\beta\Phi,

(1.10)

where
\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}=1-\mathcal{P}_{N}
is the projection operator onto high-frequency space. The coefficient
\beta
is then determined by substituting
\psi=\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi^{N}
into (
1.8
) and matching coefficient of the
k^{-2}
decay,

\beta=[\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi](0)=\left[\psi^{N}-\beta\mathcal{P}_{N}\Phi\right](0)+\beta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left[\psi^{N}-\beta\mathcal{P}_{N}\Phi\right](0)=0.

(1.11)

This forms a linear equation for
\beta
, where the coefficients can be evaluated within the complexity
\mathcal{O}(N)
. As presented in Figure

2
Expansion of eigenfunctions in point-singular potential

This section is devoted to deriving an expansion of eigenfunction
\psi
of the form

\psi=\underbrace{\psi^{(0)}}_{\in H^{s+2}}+\underbrace{\psi^{(1)}}_{\in H^{s+3}}+\underbrace{\psi^{\geq 2}}_{\in H^{s+4}}.

(2.1)

We retain only the first two orders of the expansion, as this is sufficient for the post-processing of FSM, for which the super-convergence gain
b\leq 2
according to Lemma
1.1
.

We begin by introducing a formal definition of point singularities, followed by the asymptotic expansion of eigenfunction.

2.1
Potential with point singularities

Let
V
be a potential with a point singularity at
\bm{x}=\bm{0}
. Intuitively, the asymptotic decay in Fourier coefficients of
\mathcal{{V}}\psi
is governed by the local behavior
of
\psi
near
\bm{x}=\bm{0}
. More specifically, for a function
\psi
with sufficient regularity to admits a Taylor expansion, the action of
V
on monomials determines the high-frequency asymptotic decay of
V\psi
.
We therefore adopt the following definition.

Definition 1

(Periodic point‑singular Sobolev space)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
a function
V
is said to belong to
PSH^{s,r}
, if and only if, there exists a compactly supported function
V_{\mathrm{lc}}
such that

V(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}V_{\mathrm{lc}}(\bm{x}+\bm{n}),

(2.2)

and for every multi-index
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r
,

\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}V_{\mathrm{lc}}(\bm{x})\in H^{s+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(2.3)

where
\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
and
|\bm{\alpha}|:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}
.

Remark 1
.

The requirement that
V_{\mathrm{lc}}
be compactly supported is sufficient to ensure the convergence of the periodic sum in (
2.2
). Long-range potentials, such as the logarithmic potentials
\log|\bm{x}|
and Coulomb potentials
1/|\bm{x}|
, also fall within this framework in the sense that their short-range components in the Ewald decomposition
toukmaji1996ewald
satisfy this compact-support condition.

Remark 2
.

In Fourier representation, the multiplication by
\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}
translates into the differential operator
(2\pi i)^{-|\bm{\alpha}|}\partial_{\bm{k}}^{\bm{\alpha}}
. Equivalently, (
2.3
) implies that the Fourier coefficients of
V_{\mathrm{lc}}
exhibit accelerated decay after applying the partial derivative in
\bm{k}
-space. Consequently, the space
PSH^{s,r}
includes functions in
H^{s}
whose Fourier coefficients behave like
|\bm{k}|^{-\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Typical singular potentials, such as the Coulomb potential, the Dirac‑delta potential, and the logarithmic potential, all belong to this class.

In general, we allow the potential to possess isolated point singularities, which are specified in the following assumption.

Assumption 2
.

Let
s>\max\left\{d/2-2,-1\right\}
. The potential
V(x)
admits a decomposition of the form

V(\bm{x})=V_{0}(\bm{x})+\sum_{p=1}^{n_{p}}V_{p}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{p}),

(2.4)

where
V_{0}\in H^{s+2}
,
V_{p}(\cdot-\bm{x}_{p})\in PSH^{s,2}
,
and
r_{m}=\min_{p\neq q}|\bm{x}_{p}-\bm{x}_{q}|>0
.
Accordingly, we decompose potential operator as
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{0}+\sum_{p=1}^{n_{p}}\mathcal{V}_{p}.

2.2
Asymptotic expansion

Assumption
2
enables an expansion of
\mathcal{V}\psi
based on the Taylor expansion of
\psi
around each singular point
\bm{x}_{p}
. However, the eigenfunction
\psi
may not possess sufficient regularity to admit such expansion. Therefore, we assume that the
r
-th order term of the expansion admits

\psi^{(r)}:=\sum_{p=1}^{n_{p}}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=r}\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}},\quad r\in\{0,1\},

(2.5)

where the functions
\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
are periodic, low-regularity analogues of
(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{p})^{\bm{\alpha}}
associated with
V_{p}
, and
\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
are the expansion coefficients. We require each
\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
approximately solves single potential problem and satisfies

\bigl(\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{V}_{p}\bigr)\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}:=R_{p,\bm{\alpha}}\in H^{s+2},\quad\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}_{\bm{x}_{p}}[\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}]=\delta_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},\bm{\alpha}},\quad\forall\,|\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}|\leq|\bm{\alpha}|\leq 1,

(2.6)

where
\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}_{\bm{x}_{p}}
denote the linear functional that extracts the derivative of order
\bm{\alpha}
at
\bm{x}_{p}
.

To further determine the coefficients
\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
, we substitute the expansion (
2.1
) and (
2.5
) into the eigenvalue equation
(\mathcal{H}-\lambda)\psi=0
, which yields

\mathcal{T}\psi^{\geq 2}+\sum_{p=1}^{n_{p}}\mathcal{V}_{p}\bigl(\psi-\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq 1}\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}\bigr)+R=0,

(2.7)

where the total residual is given by

R=\sum_{p=1}^{n_{p}}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq 1}\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}R_{p,\bm{\alpha}}+\bigl(\mathcal{V}_{0}-\lambda\bigr)\psi\in H^{s+2}.

(2.8)

The
H^{s}
and
H^{s+1}
components in left-hand-side of (
2.7
) vanish. In view of condition (
2.3
), each singular potential
V_{p}(\bm{x})
, and hence the operator
\mathcal{V}_{p}
, acts on a function whose value and first-order derivatives vanish at
\bm{x}_{p}
, which implies

\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}_{\bm{x}_{p}}\Bigl(\psi-\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq 1}\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}\Bigr)=0,\quad\forall\,|\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}|\leq 1.

(2.9)

By (
2.6
), (
2.9
) is equivalent to

\beta_{p,\bm{0}}=\mathcal{D}^{\bm{0}}_{\bm{x}_{p}}[\psi],\qquad\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}=\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}_{\bm{x}_{p}}\bigl[\psi-\beta_{p,\bm{0}}\Phi_{p,\bm{0}}\big],\quad\forall\,|\bm{\alpha}|=1.

(2.10)

This implies that the coefficients are invariant with respect to the expansion order.

The proceeding argument is formalized in Lemma
2.1
, whose proof is deferred to Section
6.3
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under Assumption
2
, the eigenfunction
\psi
of
\mathcal{H}
admits an expansion of the form (
2.1
). Specifically, there exists
\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
satisfying (
2.6
), and each expansion term admits the representation (
2.5
), where the coefficients
\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
are determined by (
2.10
). Moreover,

\|\psi^{(0)}\|_{H^{s+2}}+\|\psi^{(1)}\|_{H^{s+3}}+\|\psi^{\geq 2}\|_{H^{s+4}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.11)

Remark 3
.

We refer to (
2.1
) as an asymptotic expansion in the sense that the Fourier coefficients
\hat{\psi}_{\bm{k}}^{(r)}
exhibit faster decay as
|\bm{k}|\to\infty
for larger values of
r
.

Remark 4
.

One can generalize (
2.1
) to higher order term
\psi^{(r)}
with
r\geq 2
, while determining the coefficients
\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
by matching the
H^{s+|\bm{\alpha}|}
components in (
2.7
).
In this case, the residual
R
contains a
H^{s+2}
contribution from the term
\lambda\psi
, and thus the higher-order coefficients
\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
(for
|\bm{\alpha}|\geq 2
) will depend on
\lambda
.

3
Asymptotic recovery

Let
\psi^{N}\in\mathscr{X}_{N}
be an approximation to
\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi
, for instance, obtained by FSM. The AR operator recovers the coefficients
\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
in
\psi^{(0)}
and
\psi^{(1)}

from
\psi^{N}
, and then adds these terms in high-frequency coefficients

\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi^{N}:=\psi^{N}+\mathcal{P}^{\perp}_{N}\sum_{p=1}^{n_{p}}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq 1}\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}.

(3.1)

To determine the coefficients
\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
, substituting
\psi=\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi^{N}
into equation (
2.9
) yields, for each
|\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}|\leq 1,\ 1\leq p\leq n_{p}
, the linear system for

{D}^{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}_{\bm{x}_{p}}\Bigl(\psi^{N}-\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq 1}\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}\mathcal{P}_{N}\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}+\sum_{q\neq p}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq 1}\beta_{q,\bm{\alpha}}\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}\Phi_{q,\bm{\alpha}}\Bigr)=0.

(3.2)

A detailed analysis presented in Section
6.4
shows that this linear system is uniformly well conditioned for all sufficiently large
N
; therefore, it uniquely determines
\beta_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
. Furthermore, constructing the coefficients of the linear system requires only
O(N^{d})
operations. Indeed, evaluating the derivatives of
\psi^{N}
and
\mathcal{P}_{N}\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
at a given point requires only
O(N^{d})
operations using their Fourier coefficients, while the
\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
term can be evaluated in the same way using the identity
\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}=\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}-\mathcal{P}_{N}\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
.

For the rest of this section, we establish the convergence order of AR and AR-FSM, and then derive explicit expressions for the asymptotic functions
\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}
for the 1D Dirac-delta potential and the 3D Coulomb potential.

3.1
Convergence order of AR and AR-FSM

By the triangle inequality, the error of AR post-processing can be decomposed as

\|\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi^{N}-\psi\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|\mathcal{A}_{N}(\psi^{N}-\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\mathcal{A}_{N}\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi-\psi\|_{H^{1}}.

(3.3)

Bounding the first term requires a stability estimate for
\mathcal{A}_{N}
, while the second term quantifies its approximation accuracy. A detailed analysis of these properties is presented in Section
6.4
, leading to the following theorem.

Theorem 3

(
Convergence order of AR).

Under Assumption
2
, let
\Phi_{p,\bm{\alpha}}
satisfy (
2.6
),
\psi
be an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{H}
and
\psi^{N}\in\mathscr{X}_{N}
.
Then the estimate holds for AR operator
\mathcal{A}_{N}
given by (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), for all sufficiently large
N

\|\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi^{N}-\psi\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|\psi^{N}-\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi\|_{H^{1}}+N^{-(s+3)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.4)

By this lemma, the overall convergence order of eigenfunctions is governed by the minimum of
s+3
and the order of projected error
\|\psi^{N}-\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi\|_{H^{1}}
. According to Lemma
1.1
, FSM produces an approximation
\psi^{N}\in\mathscr{X}_{N}
such that the projected error converges at order
s+1+b
. Provided Lemma
1.1
and Theorem
3
, we can establish the convergence order of AR-FSM in Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Combining Lemma
1.1
and Theorem
3
together,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi_{n}^{N}-\psi\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{n}^{N}-\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi\|_{H^{1}}+N^{-(s+3)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-(s+1+b)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}},

where
\psi\in\operatorname{ker}(\lambda_{n}-\mathcal{H})
is an exact eigenfunction associated with
\lambda_{n}
. This proves the estimate for eigenfunction since
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N}=\mathcal{A}_{N}\psi_{n}^{N}
. The estimate for eigenvalue is obtained using the Rayleigh quotient and Lemma
5.3
:

\displaystyle|\tilde{\lambda}_{n}^{N}-\lambda_{n}|\|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N},(\mathcal{H}-\lambda_{n})\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N}\rangle\right|

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N}-\psi,(\mathcal{H}-\lambda_{n})(\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N}-\psi)\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N}-\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+(\lambda_{n}+M)\|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N}-\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-(2s+2+2b)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-(2s+2+2b)}\|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where the last two inequalities are obtained using (
3.5
). Thus, dividing both sides
\|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
yields the desired estimate for eigenvalue.
∎

(3.7)

for the 1D Dirac delta potential and the 3D Coulomb potential.

3D Coulomb potential

The potential
V_{\mathrm{lc}}(\bm{x})\sim\gamma|\bm{x}|^{-1}
where

s=0.5^{\textbf{-}}

(3.13)

Let
s_{\bm{\alpha}}={(2\pi)}^{-1}\sin(2\pi\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}})

\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x}):=-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi B}\Bigl((\Psi^{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x})-s_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x})\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}_{\bm{0}}[\Psi^{\bm{\alpha}}]\Bigr)+s_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x}),\qquad|\bm{\alpha}|=1.

The asymptotic functions are explicitly constructed in (

Since the super-convergence order of FSM satisfies

\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\frac{1}{|\bm{k}|^{2}}-\frac{\gamma}{4\pi B}\frac{4\pi^{2}|\bm{k}|^{2}B}{\pi^{2}(|\bm{k}|^{2}+B^{2})^{2}}=\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{|\bm{k}|^{2}}-\frac{|\bm{k}|^{2}}{(|\bm{k}|^{2}+B^{2})^{2}}\right),

On the other hand, the function

5
Analysis of FSM

This section is devoted to proving Lemma
1.1
and Theorem
1
.
The proof proceeds in three main steps. First, in Section
5.1
, we establish preliminary results, including the regularity properties of eigenfunctions and related estimates.
Next, in Section
5.2
, we connect the FSM solution to the exact eigenpair through the Feshbach-Schur map.
Finally, in Section
5.3
, we derive a perturbation bound that controls the error in the subspace spanned by the first
n
eigenfunctions. Combining these steps leads to the main convergence results of FSM.

Throughout Sections
5
and
6
, the parameters
s,b
and
l
are fixed and retain the same meaning. In particular,
s>\max\{d/2-2,-1\}
denotes the regularity index of the potential
V
, and we set
b=\min\{(s+2-d/2)^{\textbf{-}},s+1,2\}>0
and
l=2-b=\max\{-(s-d/2)^{\textbf{-}},1-s,0\}
. For any
-1\leq t\leq s
, the following index conditions are satisfied

t\leq\min\{t+l,s\},\qquad t+d/2<t+l+s,\qquad t+l+s\geq 0.

(5.1)

5.1
Preliminary results

We first provide the boundedness property of potential
V
satisfying Assumption
1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

(Theorem A.1 in
behzadan2021multiplication
)
Let
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
belong to the index set

I:=\bigl\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|\ z\leq\min\{x,y\},\ z+d/2<x+y,\ x+y\geq 0\bigr\}.

Then the embedding
H^{\alpha}(\cdot)\times H^{\beta}(\cdot)\hookrightarrow H^{\gamma}(\cdot)
holds, where
H^{s}(\cdot)
denotes
H^{s}
or
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. That is, for all
u\in H^{\alpha}(\cdot)
,
v\in H^{\beta}(\cdot)
,

\|uv\|_{H^{\gamma}(\cdot)}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{\alpha}(\cdot)}\|v\|_{H^{\beta}(\cdot)}.

(5.2)

Proof.

The only case not covered by Theorem A.1 in
behzadan2021multiplication
occurs when
\gamma<0
and
\min\{\alpha,\beta\}\geq 0
, where we require
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
rather than
\alpha+\beta>0
. In this remaining case
\alpha=\beta=0
, we have

H^{0}(\cdot)\times H^{0}(\cdot)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\cdot)\hookrightarrow\bigl(L^{\infty}(\cdot)\bigr)^{\star}\hookrightarrow\bigr(H^{-\gamma}(\cdot)\bigl)^{\star}=H^{\gamma}(\cdot),

where the last embedding follows from
H(\cdot)^{-\gamma}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\cdot)
using
-\gamma>d/2
.

∎

Combining this lemma with (
5.1
), we obtain the following corollary.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V\in H^{s}
. Then for any
-1\leq t\leq s
and
\phi\in H^{t+l}
,

\|\mathcal{V}\phi\|_{H^{t}}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{t+l}}.

(5.3)

Next, we establish boundedness and regularity results for the operator

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{V}
. We note that Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
hold under the sole assumption that
\mathcal{V}
satisfies (
5.3
) (without requiring
\mathcal{V}
to be a multiplication operator).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{V}
with
\mathcal{V}
satisfying (
5.3
). Then there exists a constant
M>0
such that

\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\langle\phi,(\mathcal{H}+M)\phi\rangle\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2},\qquad\forall\,\phi\in H^{1}.

(5.4)

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, there exists a constant
C_{V}
such that

\langle\phi,\mathcal{V}\phi\rangle\leq\|\phi\|_{H^{l/2}}\|\mathcal{V}\phi\|_{H^{-l/2}}\leq C_{V}\|\phi\|_{H^{l/2}}^{2}.

(5.5)

By the Sobolev interpolation inequality and Young’s inequality, for any
\delta>0
,

\|\phi\|_{H^{l/2}}^{2}\leq\delta\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
C_{\delta}=\delta^{-l/(2-l)}
.
Choosing
\delta=(2C_{V})^{-1}
, we deduce

\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-(1+C_{V}C_{\delta})\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\langle\phi,\mathcal{H}\phi\rangle\leq\tfrac{3}{2}\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+(-1+C_{V}C_{\delta})\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This implies (
5.4
) by taking

.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{V}
with
\mathcal{V}
satisfying (

), and let
(\lambda,\psi)
be an eigenpair of
\mathcal{H}
with

. Then

\|\psi\|_{H^{s+2}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}.

(5.6)

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
, there exists

such that

\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq 2\langle\psi,(\mathcal{H}+M)\psi\rangle=2(\lambda+M)\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which implies
\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}
. To obtain higher regularity, we employ a bootstrap argument. For

, we rewrite the eigenvalue equation as

(\mathcal{T}+1)\psi=(\lambda+1-\mathcal{V})\psi.

Taking the

\|\psi\|_{H^{t+2}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{H^{t}}+\|\mathcal{V}\psi\|_{H^{t}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{H^{t}}+\|\psi\|_{H^{t+l}}.

(5.7)

Since

, the estimate implies the bootstrap implication

\psi\in H^{t+l}\;\Longrightarrow\;\psi\in H^{t+2}.

Starting from
\psi\in H^{1}\hookrightarrow H^{-1+l}
, a finite iteration of this argument yields (
5.6
).

5.2
The Feshbach-Schur map

This section introduces the Feshbach-Schur map, which maps the original problem (
1.2
) in
H^{1}
to discrete problem in
\mathscr{X}_{N}
.
Decompose
\psi\in H^{1}
as
\psi=\psi^{N}+\psi^{\perp}
, where
\psi^{N}=\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi
and
\psi^{\perp}=\mathcal{P}^{\perp}_{N}\psi
. Note that the operator
\mathcal{T}
commutes with
\mathcal{P}_{N}
and
\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}
, the eigenvalue equation
\mathcal{H}\psi=\lambda\psi
can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{N},(\mathcal{H}-\lambda)\psi^{N}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\phi^{N},\mathcal{V}\psi^{\perp}\rangle,

\displaystyle\forall\phi^{N}\in\mathscr{X}_{N}

(5.11a)

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{\perp},(\mathcal{H}-\lambda)\psi^{\perp}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\phi^{\perp},\mathcal{V}\psi^{N}\rangle,

\displaystyle\forall\,\phi^{\perp}\in\mathscr{X}^{\perp}_{N}.

(5.11b)

Here
\mathscr{X}_{N}^{\perp}
denotes the orthonormal complement of
\mathscr{X}_{N}
in
H^{1}
.
By Lemma
5.6
(i) , (
5.11b
) is uni-solvent for
\psi^{\perp}
, obtaining
\psi^{\perp}=-\mathcal{G}_{N}(\lambda)\mathcal{V}\psi^{N}
, where

\mathcal{G}_{N}(\lambda):=\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}(\mathcal{H}-\lambda)^{-1}_{\mathscr{X}_{N}^{\perp}}\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}

is the solution operator.
Substituting
\psi^{\perp}
into (
5.11a
) yields a reduced nonlinear eigenvalue problem in finite-dimensional space
\mathscr{X}_{N}
,

\langle\phi^{N},\left(\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{V}\mathcal{G}_{N}(\lambda)\mathcal{V}\right)\psi^{N}\rangle=\lambda\langle\phi^{N},\psi^{N}\rangle,\quad\forall\,\phi^{N}\in\mathscr{X}_{N}.

(5.12)

Define the self-adjoint operator

\mathcal{F}_{N}(\lambda^{\star})=\mathcal{V}\mathcal{G}_{N}(\lambda^{\star})\mathcal{V}.

The mapping
\mathcal{H}\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{N}\bigl(\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F}_{N}(\lambda)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{N}
is referred to as the Feshbach-Schur map. The above construction establishes a one-to-one correspondence between the eigenspaces,

\mathcal{H}\psi=\lambda\psi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal{P}_{N}\bigl(\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F}_{N}(\lambda)\bigr)\mathcal{P}_{N}\psi^{N}=\lambda\psi^{N}.

Now fix an value
\lambda^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}
on the left-hand side of (
5.12
), denote the algebraic eigenpairs of
\mathcal{P}_{N}\bigl[\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F}_{N}(\lambda^{\star})\bigr]\mathcal{P}_{N}
in
\mathscr{X}_{N}
by

\big(\sigma_{n}^{N}(\lambda^{\star}),\psi^{N}_{n}(\lambda^{\star})\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathscr{X}_{N}
, which satisfy

\langle\phi^{N},(\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F}_{N}(\lambda^{\star}))\psi^{N}_{n}(\lambda^{\star})\rangle=\sigma_{n}^{N}(\lambda^{\star})\langle\phi^{N},\psi^{N}_{n}(\lambda^{\star})\rangle,\quad\forall\,\phi^{N}\in\mathscr{X}_{N}.

(5.13)

The following lemma establishes a connection between the eigenpairs of
\mathcal{H}
and those of
\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F}_{N}(\lambda^{\star})
,
implying that (
5.13
) reproduces the exact
n
-th eigenpair when
\lambda^{\star}=\lambda_{n}
. In this sense, the operator
\mathcal{F}_{N}(\lambda_{n})
can be interpreted as a perturbation operator that connects the FSM solution with the exact solution.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let

\{(\lambda_{n},\psi_{n})\}_{n\geq 1}

and
\{(\sigma_{n}^{N}(\lambda^{\star}),\psi^{N}_{n}(\lambda^{\star})\}_{n\geq 1}
denote the solutions of (

\mathrm{(i)}

For

) is uni-solvent for

\mathrm{(ii)}

For

\sigma_{n}^{N}(\lambda^{\star})
is continuous and non-increasing in
\lambda^{\star}
;

\mathrm{(iii)}

If

The proof is deferred to Appendix

.

Next, we show that
\mathcal{F}_{N}(\lambda^{\star})
also enjoys the boundedness properties in (
5.3
). Consequently, we can apply Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
to the operator
\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F}_{N}(\lambda^{\star})

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
V\in H^{s}
. There exists
N_{0}
such that for all
N\geq N_{0}

\lambda^{\star}<\pi^{2}N^{2}
and

(5.14)

Proof.

Let

solves

\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}\bigl(\mathcal{H}-\lambda^{\star}\bigr)g^{\perp}=\mathcal{P}_{N}^{\perp}f

and

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}_{N}(\mathcal{H}-\lambda^{\star})g^{\perp}\|_{H^{t}}

5.3
Perturbation bound of the first
n
eigenpairs

The perturbation theory for eigenvalues of self-adjoint operators (e.g.,
kato2013perturbation
) provides uniform estimates that valid for all eigenvalues. To obtain sharper bounds for the first
n
eigenvalues, we present Proposition
4
, which controls the eigenvalue error using the subspace spanned by the first
n
eigenvectors.

To state the perturbation result, we take the following notations.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a self‑adjoint operator with eigenpairs
\{(\lambda_{n},u_{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
. The space spanned by the first
n
eigenvectors of
\mathcal{S}
is denoted by

W_{n}(\mathcal{S}):=\operatorname{span}\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n}\}.

The orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace
\ker\big(\mathcal{S}-\lambda_{n}\big)
is written as
\mathcal{P}_{n,\mathcal{S}}
.
The
n
-th spectral gap of
\mathcal{S}
is

\gamma_{n,\mathcal{S}}:=\min_{\lambda_{j}\neq\lambda_{n}}|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{n}|.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
be two self-adjoint operators in
\mathscr{X}_{N}
, with respective eigenpairs denoted by
\{(\lambda_{1,n},u_{1,n})\}_{n\geq 1},\ \{(\lambda_{2,n},u_{2,n})\}_{n\geq 1}
listed in non-decreasing order.

Then the following estimate for eigenvalues holds,

|\lambda_{1,n}-\lambda_{2,n}|\leq\sup_{u\in W_{n}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\cup W_{n}(\mathcal{S}_{2})}\left|\frac{\langle u,(\mathcal{S}_{2}-\mathcal{S}_{1})u\rangle}{\langle u,u\rangle}\right|.

(5.18)

Let
M>0
be a constant such that
\mathcal{S}_{1}+M
is positive definite, and define the
\mathcal{S}_{1}
norm as

\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{2}:=\langle\psi,(\mathcal{S}_{1}+M)\psi\rangle.

If
|\lambda_{2,n}-\lambda_{1,n}|\leq\frac{\gamma_{n,\mathcal{S}_{1}}}{3}
,
then the following estimate for the eigenvectors holds:

\|u_{2,n}-\mathcal{P}_{n,\mathcal{S}_{1}}u_{2,n}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq 6(1+\frac{\lambda_{1,n}+M}{\gamma_{n,\mathcal{S}_{1}}})\sup_{v\in\mathscr{X}_{N}\setminus\{0\}}\left|\frac{\langle v,(\mathcal{S}_{2}-\mathcal{S}_{1})u_{2,n}\rangle}{\|v\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}}\right|.

(5.19)

The proof of this proposition follows from standard techniques in the perturbation theory of eigenvalues and is deferred to Appendix
10
.

6.1
Properties of function space

Our previous definition Definition

, but is less suitable for theoretical analysis since the choice of

is not unique. We therefore first establish Lemma
6.1
, which provides some equivalent characterizations of
PSH^{t,r}
better suited for analysis.
To state this lemma, let
h_{c}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
be a
C^{\infty}
cutoff function such that

\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}h_{c}(\bm{x}+\bm{n})=1,\quad\forall\,\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad h_{c}(\bm{x})=0,\quad\forall\,\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}},

(6.1)

where
\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}}:=[-3/4,3/4]^{d}
is chosen so as to exclude all periodic images of the singular point
\bm{x}=\bm{0}
.
One can choose
h_{c}
as the tensor product of one-dimensional cutoff functions. For each multi-index
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, define the periodic analogue of
\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}
by

m_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(e^{2\pi ix_{j}}-1\bigr)^{\alpha_{j}}.

(6.2)

The function
m_{\bm{\alpha}}
is periodic in
[0,1]^{d}
and
C^{\infty}
smooth, and satisfies

m_{\bm{\alpha}}/\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}/m_{\bm{\alpha}}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}}).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. The following are equivalent:
(\mathrm{i})

f\in PSH^{t,r}
;
(\mathrm{ii})

\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}hf\in H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\forall\,|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r
;
(\mathrm{iii})

m_{\bm{\alpha}}f\in H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}
,
\forall\,|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r
.

Proof.

We prove the equivalence by showing

\mathrm{(i)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(iii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(i)}
.

\mathrm{(i)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(iii)}
. Let
f\in PSH^{t,r}
. By Definition
1
, there exists a compactly supported
f_{\mathrm{lc}}
such that

f=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{\mathrm{lc}}(\cdot+\bm{n})

with

\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}f_{\mathrm{lc}}\in H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
for every
|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r
. Owing to the compact support of
f_{\mathrm{lc}}
, only finitely many
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
contribute to

f(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{n}}f_{\mathrm{lc}}(\bm{x}+\bm{n})
for
\bm{x}\in[0,1]^{d}
. This allows us to bound

\|m_{\bm{\alpha}}f\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}}
by
\|m_{\bm{\alpha}}f_{\mathrm{lc}}\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Therefore, for every
|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r

\|m_{\bm{\alpha}}f\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}}\lesssim\|m_{\bm{\alpha}}f_{\mathrm{lc}}\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}f_{\mathrm{lc}}\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<+\infty,

where the second follows from
m_{\bm{\alpha}}/\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

\mathrm{(iii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(ii)}
.
Since
h_{c}
is compactly supported in
\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}}
and

\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}h_{c}/m_{\bm{\alpha}}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}})
, we conclude
\mathrm{(ii)}
by

\|\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}h_{c}f\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}h_{c}f\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}})}\lesssim\|m_{\bm{\alpha}}f\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}})}\lesssim\|m_{\bm{\alpha}}f\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}}.

\mathrm{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(i)}
.
Let
f_{\mathrm{lc}}(\bm{x}):=h_{c}(\bm{x})f(\bm{x})
for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
\mathrm{(ii)}
is exactly
\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}f_{\mathrm{lc}}\in H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \forall\,|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r
. Therefore we conclude
f\in PSH^{t,r}
by

\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{\mathrm{lc}}(\bm{x}+\bm{n})=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}h_{c}(\bm{x}+\bm{n})f(\bm{x})=f(\bm{x}).

∎

The function space
PSH^{t,r}
is generated by two operators
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{D}
denotes the first-order derivative operator and
\mathcal{M}
denotes the multiplication operator by
\bm{x}
(or
m_{\bm{\alpha}}
in the periodic setting).
In accordance with Lemma

(ii), we define the
PSH^{t,r}
norm by

\|f\|_{PSH^{t,r}}:=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r}\bigl\|\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}h_{c}f\bigr\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\alpha}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\asymp\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|\leq|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r}\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}\mathcal{M}^{\bm{\alpha}}[h_{c}f]\bigr\|_{H^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(6.3)

This definition directly implies the embedding

PSH^{t,r+1}\hookrightarrow PSH^{t,r}\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow PSH^{t,0}=H^{t},

(6.4)

and the induced commutative diagram structure

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PSH^{t,r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PSH^{t+1,r-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PSH^{t,0}=H^{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PSH^{t-1,r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PSH^{t,r-1},}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PSH^{t-1,0}=H^{t-1}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}}

(6.5)

As a consequence, applying this diagram iteratively, we obtain

\bigl\|\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[h_{c}f]\bigr\|_{H^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{PSH^{t+|\bm{\beta}|-|\bm{\alpha}|,|\bm{\alpha}|}}.

(6.6)

Next, we study the boundedness property of
V\in PSH^{s,r}
as a multiplication operator. Lemma

shows that, for
t\leq s
, multiplication by
V
defines a bounded map from
H^{t+r+l}
to
H^{t+r}
, extending the admissible index range by

compared with Lemma

.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
V\in PSH^{s,r}
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\phi\in H^{t+r+l}
satisfies
\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}_{\bm{0}}[\phi]=0
for every
\bm{|}\bm{\alpha}|<r
, then for
-1\leq t+r\leq s+r
, we have

\|V\phi\|_{H^{t+r}}\lesssim\|V\|_{PSH^{s,r}}\|\phi\|_{H^{t+r+l}}.

(6.7)

The proof of this lemma relies on an auxiliary lemma stated in Lemma
6.3
, whose proof is deferred to Appendix
11
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
m>\max\{0,d/2-1\}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\phi\in H^{m+r}
satisfies
{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}_{\bm{0}}[\phi]=0
for every
|\bm{\alpha}|<r
, then
\phi
admits the decomposition

\phi(\bm{x})=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=r}\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}\phi_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x}),\quad\|\phi_{\bm{\alpha}}\|_{H^{m}(B_{R})}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{m+r}},

(6.8)

where
B_{R}
denotes the ball centered at
\bm{x}=\bm{0}
with radius
R>0
.

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

We prove by induction on
r
. The case
r=0
follows from Lemma
5.2
. Assume that, for some
r\geq 1
, the statement holds for every
0\leq r^{\prime}<r
. We show that it also holds for
r
.
The proof is divided into three cases.

Case 1.
If
t\leq s-1
, set
r^{\prime}=r-1,t^{\prime}=t+1
. Then
-1\leq t^{\prime}+r^{\prime}\leq s+r^{\prime}
, the induction hypothesis with
r^{\prime}
applied to
t^{\prime}
and
\phi
implies

\|V\phi\|_{H^{t^{\prime}+r^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|V\|_{PSH^{s,r^{\prime}}}\|\phi\|_{H^{t^{\prime}+r^{\prime}}}\leq\|V\|_{PSH^{s,r}}\|\phi\|_{H^{t^{\prime}+r^{\prime}}},

(6.9)

which is the desired (
6.7
) since
t^{\prime}+r^{\prime}=t+r
.

Case 2.
If
s-1<t\leq s
and
s\geq 0
, then
t\geq-1
. We claim that for all
0\leq|\bm{\beta}|\leq r
, if a function
f
satisfies
\mathcal{D}_{\bm{0}}^{\bm{\alpha}}f=0
for every
|\bm{\alpha}|<|\bm{\beta}|
, then

\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[V]f\|_{H^{t}}\lesssim\|V\|_{PSH^{s,|\bm{\beta}|}}\|f\|_{H^{t+l+|\bm{\beta}|}}.

(6.10)

To prove this claim, choose
R>0
such that
\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}}\subset B_{R}
. Since
t+l>s+l-1\geq\max\{0,d/2-1\}
, we decompose
f=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=|\bm{\beta}|}\bm{x}^{\bm{\beta}}f_{\bm{\alpha}}
via Lemma
6.3
, with estimate
\|f_{\bm{\alpha}}\|_{H^{t+l}(B_{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{t+l+|\bm{\beta}|}}
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be the extension operator from
B_{R}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies
\mathcal{I}f_{\bm{\alpha}}|_{B_{R}}=f_{\bm{\alpha}}
and
\|\mathcal{I}f_{\bm{\alpha}}\|_{H^{t+l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{t+l}(B_{R})}
.
Then we prove the claim (
6.10
) by

\displaystyle\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[V]f\bigr\|_{H^{t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[h_{c}V]f\bigr\|_{H^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=|\bm{\beta}|}\bigl\|\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[h_{c}V]\mathcal{I}f_{\bm{\alpha}}\bigr\|_{H^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=|\bm{\beta}|}\bigl\|\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[h_{c}V]\bigr\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\mathcal{I}f_{\bm{\alpha}}\|_{H^{t+l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V\|_{PSH^{s,|\bm{\beta}|}}\|f\|_{H^{t+r+l}}.

Here the third inequality follows from the embedding
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times H^{t+l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow H^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
implied by Lemma
5.1
, and the last inequality follows from (
6.6
). Applying the claim (
6.10
) to
f=\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}\phi
for every
|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r-|\bm{\beta}|
, we obtain the desired (
6.7
) by

\displaystyle\|V\phi\|_{H^{t+r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|\leq r}\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[V\phi]\bigr\|_{H^{t}}

(6.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|\leq r}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r-|\bm{\beta}|}\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[V]\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}[\phi]\bigr\|_{H^{t}}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|\leq r}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r-|\bm{\beta}|}\|V\|_{PSH^{s,|\bm{\beta}|}}\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}\phi\bigr\|_{H^{t+|\bm{\beta}|+l}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V\|_{PSH^{s,r}}\|\phi\|_{H^{t+l+|\bm{\beta}|}}.

Case 3.
If
s-1<t\leq s
and
-1<s<0
, then
l\geq 1-s>1
.
We modify (
6.10
) into the following claim. For
0\leq|\bm{\beta}|\leq r-1
, if a function
f
satisfies
\mathcal{D}_{\bm{0}}^{\bm{\alpha}}f=0
for every
|\bm{\alpha}|<|\bm{\beta}|+1
, then

\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[V]f\|_{H^{t+1}}\lesssim\|V\|_{PSH^{s,|\bm{\beta}|+1}}\|f\|_{H^{t+l+|\bm{\beta}|+1}}.

(6.13)

The proof of this claim follows from the same argument in (
6.11
)

\displaystyle\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[V]f\bigr\|_{H^{t+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=|\bm{\beta}|+1}\bigl\|\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[h_{c}V]\mathcal{I}f_{\bm{\alpha}}\bigr\|_{H^{t+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(6.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=|\bm{\beta}|+1}\bigl\|\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[h_{c}V]\bigr\|_{H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\mathcal{I}f_{\bm{\alpha}}\|_{H^{t+l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V\|_{PSH^{s,|\bm{\beta}|+1}}\|f\|_{H^{t+l+|\bm{\beta}|+1}}.

The second inequality follows from the embedding
H^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times H^{t^{\prime}+l^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow H^{t^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
implied by Lemma
5.1
with
t^{\prime}=t+1,\ s^{\prime}=s+1
and
l^{\prime}=\min\{0,l-1\}=l-1
, and the last inequality follows from (
6.6
). Applying the claim (
6.13
) to
f=\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}\phi
for every
|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r-1-|\bm{\beta}|
, we obtain the desired (
6.7
) by

\displaystyle\|V\phi\|_{H^{t+r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|\leq r-1}\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[V\phi]\bigr\|_{H^{t+1}}

(6.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|\leq r-1}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r-1-|\bm{\beta}|}\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[V]\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}[\phi]\bigr\|_{H^{t+1}}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|\leq r-1}\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r-1-|\bm{\beta}|}\|V\|_{PSH^{s,|\bm{\beta}|+1}}\bigl\|\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}\phi\bigr\|_{H^{t+l+|\bm{\beta}|+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V\|_{PSH^{s,r}}\|\phi\|_{H^{t+r+l}}.

∎

At the end of this section, we establish Lemma
6.4
, showing that
f\in PSH^{t,r}
has
H^{t+r}
regularity away from the singular point
\bm{x}=\bm{0}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
f\in PSH^{t,r}
with
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
\delta>0
,
f
admits a decomposition
f=f^{\mathrm{L}}+f^{\mathrm{G}}
such that
f^{\mathrm{L}}=0
on
[0,1]^{d}\setminus B_{\mathrm{per}}(\delta)
and

\|f^{\mathrm{L}}\|_{PSH^{t,r}}+\|f^{\mathrm{G}}\|_{H^{t+r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{PSH^{t,r}},

(6.16)

where the
B_{\mathrm{per}}(\delta)
denotes the periodic ball of radius
\delta

B_{\mathrm{per}}(\delta):=\left\{\bm{x}\in[0,1]^{d}:|\bm{x}+\bm{n}|<\delta\text{ for some }\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}.

Proof.

Define
f_{\mathrm{lc}}=h_{c}f
. Let
h^{\prime}_{c}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a
C^{\infty}
smooth cutoff function that is
1
inside the ball of radius
\delta/2
,
0
outside the ball of radius
\delta
. Define

f^{\mathrm{L}}(\bm{x}):=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}h_{c}^{\prime}\bigl(\bm{x}+\bm{n}\bigr)f_{\mathrm{lc}}(\bm{x}+\bm{n}),

which is 0 on
[0,1]^{d}\setminus B_{\mathrm{per}}(\delta)
. Since
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have
\|f^{\mathrm{L}}\|_{PSH^{t,r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{PSH^{t,r}}
. Note that
f^{\mathrm{G}}:=f-f^{\mathrm{L}}
is the periodic sum of
(1-h_{c}^{\prime})f_{\mathrm{lc}}
, where

(1-h_{c}^{\prime}(\bm{x}))f_{\mathrm{lc}}(\bm{x})=\frac{1-h_{c}^{\prime}(\bm{x})}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{2r}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{r}\bigl(x_{j}^{r}f_{\mathrm{lc}}(\bm{x})\bigr).

Since
(1-h_{c}^{\prime}(\bm{x}))x_{j}^{r}/(\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{2r})\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}})
, we obtain

\|f^{\mathrm{G}}\|_{H^{t+r}}\lesssim\|(1-h_{c}^{\prime})f_{\mathrm{lc}}\|_{H^{t+r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl\|x_{j}^{r}f_{\mathrm{lc}}\bigr\|_{H^{t+r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{PSH^{t,r}}.

∎

6.2
Existence and regularity of asymptotic function

This section studies the asymptotic functions

\{\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}:|\bm{\alpha}|\leq 1\}
, which satisfy

(\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{V})\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}\in H^{s+2},\qquad\mathcal{D}_{\bm{0}}^{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}[\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}]=\delta_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}},\quad\forall\,|\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}|\leq|\bm{\alpha}|,

(6.17)

for potentials
V\in PSH^{s,2}
. Lemma
6.5
guarantees the existence of
\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}
. Its proof, based on an iterative regularity-bootstrapping procedure for the residual, is deferred to Appendix
12
.
Provided that
\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}
satisfies (
6.17
), Lemma
6.6
implies

\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}\in PSH^{s+2+|\bm{\alpha}|,\,2-|\bm{\alpha}|}.

For convenience in the proof, the statements of Lemmas
6.5
and
6.6
are formulated for the operator
\mathcal{T}+1+\mathcal{V}
.
Since
V^{\prime}=V-1\in PSH^{s,2}
, applying Lemmas
6.5
and
6.6
to
V^{\prime}
recovers (
6.17
).

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
V\in PSH^{s,2}
.
Then for every multi-index
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\bm{\alpha}|\leq 1
, there exists a function
\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}\in H^{1}
satisfying

(\mathcal{T}+1+\mathcal{V})\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}=R_{\bm{\alpha}}\in H^{s+2},\qquad\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}_{\bm{0}}[\Phi_{\bm{\alpha}}]=\delta_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},\bm{\alpha}},\quad\forall\,|\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}|\leq|\bm{\alpha}|.

(6.18)

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
V\in PSH^{s,2}
and
r\in\{0,1,2\}
.
Suppose
\Phi\in H^{1}
satisfies

(\mathcal{T}+1+\mathcal{V})\Phi=R\in H^{s+2},\qquad\mathcal{D}_{\bm{0}}^{\bm{\alpha}}[\Phi]=0,\quad\forall\,|\bm{\alpha}|<r.

(6.19)

Then
\Phi\in PSH^{s+2+r,\,2-r}
.

Proof of Lemma
6.6
.

Define
\mathcal{T}_{\star}=\mathcal{T}+1
. The proof proceeds in two steps.

Step 1.

\Phi\in H^{s+2+r}
.
We argue by a bootstrap argument. By Lemma
6.2
, for
-1\leq t+r\leq s+r
, taking the
H^{t+r}
norm on equation

\mathcal{T}_{\star}\Phi=R-\mathcal{V}\Phi

gives

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{H^{t+r+2}}\lesssim\|R\|_{H^{t+r}}+\|V\Phi\|_{H^{t+r}}\lesssim\|R\|_{H^{t+r}}+\|\Phi\|_{H^{t+r+l}}.

(6.20)

This estimate implies the bootstrapping implication

\Phi\in H^{t+r+l}\Longrightarrow\Phi\in H^{t+r+2}
.
Starting from
\psi\in H^{1}
, a finite iteration of this argument lifts the regularity to
\Phi\in H^{s+2+r}
.

Step 2.

\Phi\in PSH^{s+2+r,2-r}
. We first claim that for every
r^{\prime}\leq 2-r

\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{\star}+\mathcal{V}\bigr)(m_{\bm{\alpha}}\Phi)\in H^{s+r+|\bm{\alpha}|},\ \forall\,|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r^{\prime}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Phi\in PSH^{s+2+r,r^{\prime}}.

(6.21)

To prove this claim, we observe from the diagram (
6.5
) that
m_{\bm{\alpha}}V\in PSH^{s+|\bm{\alpha}|,\,2-|\bm{\alpha}|}
. Then, by taking
s^{\prime}=s+|\bm{\alpha}|
,
t^{\prime}=s^{\prime}
and
l^{\prime}=\max\{0,l-|\bm{\alpha}|\}
in Lemma
6.2
, we have

\|m_{\bm{\alpha}}V\Phi\|_{H^{s+r+|\bm{\alpha}|}}\lesssim\|m_{\bm{\alpha}}V\|_{PSH^{s+|\bm{\alpha}|,\,2-|\bm{\alpha}|}}\|\Phi\|_{H^{s+r+|\bm{\alpha}|+l^{\prime}}}.

(6.22)

Since

s+r+|\bm{\alpha}|+l^{\prime}=s+r+\max\{|\bm{\alpha}|,l\}\leq s+r+2,

and
\Phi\in H^{s+r+2}
, we conclude from (
6.22
) that
m_{\bm{\alpha}}V\Phi\in H^{s+r+|\bm{\alpha}|}
.
Then, by the left-hand side of (
6.21
), we obtain

m_{\bm{\alpha}}\Phi\in H^{s+r+|\bm{\alpha}|+2}
for every
|\bm{\alpha}|\leq r^{\prime}
, which means
\Phi\in PSH^{s+2+r,r^{\prime}}
by Lemma
6.1
(iii).

Now we turn to estimate the left-hand side of (

). A direct calculation by expanding
\Delta(m_{\bm{\alpha}}\Phi)
yields

\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{\star}+\mathcal{V}\bigr)(m_{\bm{\alpha}}\Phi)=m_{\bm{\alpha}}R+\Phi\mathcal{T}m_{\bm{\alpha}}-2\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|=1}\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}}]\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[\Phi].

(6.23)

Since
m_{\bm{\alpha}}
is
C^{\infty}
smooth and
\Phi\in H^{s+2+r}
, we have

m_{\bm{\alpha}}R\in H^{s+2},\quad\Phi\mathcal{T}_{\star}m_{\bm{\alpha}}\in H^{s+2+r},\quad\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}}]\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[\Phi]\in H^{s+1+r}.

(6.24)

Combining (
6.23
) and (
6.24
), we establish the left-hand side of (
6.21
) for
r,r^{\prime}
satisfying

s+r+r^{\prime}\leq\min\{s+2,\;s+2+r,\;s+1+r\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r^{\prime}\leq\min\{2-r,\,1\}.

This implies that
\Phi\in PSH^{s+2+r,r^{\prime}}
for
r^{\prime}\leq\min\{2-r,1\}
. The only remaining case for proving
\Phi\in PSH^{s+2+r,2-r}
is
r=0
.
In this case, it suffices to show the cross term

\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}}]\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[\Phi]\in H^{s+2}.

Write
\bm{\alpha}=\bm{\alpha}_{1}+\bm{\alpha}_{2}
with
|\bm{\alpha}_{1}|=|\bm{\alpha}_{2}|=1
. Then

\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}}]\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[\Phi]=\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}_{2}}]\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}_{1}}\Phi]+\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}_{1}}]\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}_{2}}\Phi]-2\Phi\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}_{2}}]\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\beta}}[m_{\bm{\alpha}_{1}}].

Each term on the right‑hand side lies in
H^{s+2}
owing to
m_{\bm{\alpha}_{i}}\Phi\in H^{s+3}
(by the previously established
\Phi\in PSH^{s+2,1}
in the case
r=0,r^{\prime}=1
). This completes the proof of Lemma
6.6
.
∎

2.1

This section is devoted to the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
We denote
\mathcal{D}_{p}^{\bm{\alpha}}:=\mathcal{D}^{\bm{\alpha}}_{\bm{x}_{p}}
for the

\bm{x}_{p}
in Assumption

. By Lemmas
6.5

We define
\psi^{\geq 1}:=\psi^{(1)}+\psi^{\geq 2}.

For

(6.27)

By Lemma
5.4
, we have

.
The proof proceeds in two steps of bootstrapping via (

).

Step 1.

r=1\Rightarrow\psi^{\geq 1}\in H^{s+3}
. By the Sobolev embedding

, the coefficients

Since

7
Conclusions and discussions

In this paper we studied FSM for Schrödinger eigenvalue problems with potentials containing isolated point singularities. We derived an asymptotic expansions of eigenfunctions that separate a regular component from a finite number of explicitly characterized asymptotic functions associated with each singular point.
Based on this expansion, we proposed an asymptotic-recovery (AR) post-processing technique that recover the high-frequency components from the truncated Fourier coefficients. The resulting AR-FSM significantly improves the convergence behavior of the standard Fourier spectral method.

Rigorous error estimates are established for FSM and AR-FSM for singular potentials
V\in H^{s}
with
s>\max\{d/2-2,-1\}
, where
d
denotes the spatial dimension. We first prove the optimal convergence orders of FSM, showing that it achieves order
2s+2
for eigenvalues, order
s+1
for eigenfunctions in the
H^{1}
norm together with a super-convergence gain of order
b=\min\{(s+2-d/2)^{\textbf{-}},s+1,2\}
. Building on this result, we show that the AR-FSM fully exploits this super-convergence, yielding convergence rates of order
2s+2+2b
for eigenvalues and
s+1+b
for eigenfunctions. Numerical experiments confirm the theoretical predictions and demonstrate that the proposed methods substantially improve the convergence rates of FSM while retaining its computational efficiency.

In the analysis, we introduce a rigorous definition of point singularities and develops a foundational framework for their study. It further establishes an asymptotic expansion of eigenfunctions consisting of a
regular component in
H^{s+4}
together with
d+1
asymptotic functions associated with each singular point. These contributions are of independent interest beyond the AR-FSM framework.

We do not further investigate the dependence of the error estimates on the eigenvalue index
n
. Intuitively, one should replace the regularity results used throughout the analysis by estimates of the form

\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{t}}\lesssim(\lambda_{n}+M)^{t/2}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}
.
Such estimates are of interest for two reasons. First, they provide quantitative criteria for determining how many computed eigenpairs are reliable at a given truncation parameter
N
. Second, they constitute a necessary step toward the analysis of time-dependent Schrödinger equation, where the solution typically involves contributions from infinitely many eigenpairs.

An interesting observation from the convergence results is that the lower bound of convergence order improves as
d
increases. Indeed, the eigenvalue convergence order of FSM is
2s+2\geq d-2
, since
s\geq d/2-2
. On the other hand, the computational complexity of FSM scales as
M=N^{d}\log N
. Consequently, the eigenvalue error measured with respect to the computational complexity scales like
M^{-1+2/d}
,
which approaches the rate
M^{-1}
as
d\to\infty
.
This observation raises two questions related to the curse of dimensionality: What is the minimum computational complexity
M
required to observe the asymptotic
M^{-1}
convergence regime, and how can the tensor-product grid be avoided in order to reduce minimum computational cost.

One direction of advance is to incorporate the asymptotic expansions Lemma
2.1
directly into the discretization, leading to an enhanced Fourier spectral methods that further improve the convergence order.
Such developments may provide a systematic framework for constructing high-accuracy spectral algorithms for Schrödinger equation with point-singular potential.

Another direction is to extend the analysis of point singularities to broader classes of partial differential equations. Such extensions could provide a unified framework for treating singularities in a wide range of applications
