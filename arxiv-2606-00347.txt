1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Marcinkiewicz discretization

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be a probability space, where
\mathcal{F}
is a
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of
\Omega
and
\mu
is a probability measure on
\mathcal{F}
. For
1\leqslant p<\infty
, denote by
L_{p}(\mu)\equiv L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
the usual Lebesgue space of measurable functions
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

Let
B(\Omega)
denote the space of all bounded functions
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
equipped with the uniform norm

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|.

Let
X_{N}\subset B(\Omega)
be an
N
-dimensional linear space of bounded functions. We assume that any function
f\in X_{N}
that vanishes
\mu
-a.e.​​ on
\Omega
is identically zero (equivalently,
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}
defines a norm on
X_{N}
).

Our goal is to discretize the
L_{p}
norm on
X_{N}
by Marcinkiewicz-type inequalities. Specifically, given
1\leqslant p<\infty
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we seek sampling points
\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
(with
m\geqslant N
)
such that

(1.1)

(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\leqslant\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p},\quad\forall\,f\in X_{N}.

The main problem is to determine the asymptotically optimal sample size
m
for which (
1.1
) holds on an arbitrary
N
-dimensional subspace
X_{N}
under natural structural assumptions.

1.2.
Background and motivation

In the classical setting,
X_{N}
typically consists of trigonometric or algebraic polynomials of bounded degree on a compact domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, equipped with a normalized (possibly weighted) Lebesgue measure. In this case, inequalities of the form (
1.1
) are known as Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequalities.
For these polynomial spaces, such inequalities with an asymptotically optimal sample size
m\asymp N
have been established on various regular compact domains, such as tori, intervals, spheres, cubes, and
C^{2}
-smooth domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; see, for instance,
[
32
,
33
,
25
,
34
,
24
,
11
,
13
,
57
]
. These inequalities play a fundamental role in the convergence of Fourier series, Lagrange interpolation, and polynomial approximation.
However, classical techniques rely heavily on geometric properties of
\Omega
and often suffer from the curse of dimensionality as the ambient dimension
d
increases. These limitations motivate a more general high-dimensional theory in which
X_{N}
is an arbitrary
N
-dimensional subspace of
B(\Omega)
.
In this general setting, Marcinkiewicz discretization inequalities (
1.1
)
are governed by the norms of the embeddings
(X_{N},\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}})\hookrightarrow B(\Omega)
,
which are conveniently expressed via Nikolskii-type inequalities.

1.3.
Nikolskii-type inequality

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
, an
N
-dimensional subspace
X_{N}\subset B(\Omega)

is said to satisfy the
(p,\infty)
–Nikolskii-type inequality with constant
H\geqslant 1
if

\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant H^{1/p}\,\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)},\quad\forall\,f\in X_{N}.

In this case, we write
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(H)
.

It is readily seen that for any
p\geqslant 2
,

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(H)\implies X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(H).

In particular, for
p\geqslant 2
, the condition
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(H)
implies that
H\geqslant N
.
Therefore, in this case we may write
H=KN
for some constant
K\geqslant 1
.

1.4.
Prior work

In recent years, the Marcinkiewicz discretization problem (
1.1
)
has been extensively studied under the
(2,\infty)
–Nikolskii-type inequality,
namely, in the case when

(1.2)

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(KN),

where
K\geqslant 1
is fixed
(see, for instance,
[
3
,
12
,
26
,
27
,
31
,
15
,
14
,
50
,
29
]
).
The techniques employed in these works are largely drawn from the theory of embeddings of finite-dimensional subspaces (see
[
5
,
23
,
41
,
42
,
44
,
46
,
47
]
).
Although a constant
K
in (
1.2
) always exists by finite-dimensional norm equivalence, its size is crucial.

We now recall the main known results under the assumption
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(KN)
.

(i) The case
p=2
.

By
[
31
,
26
]
, building on the groundbreaking work
[
35
]
, the bound

m\geqslant C\varepsilon^{-2}KN

ensures an
L_{2}
-discretization inequality of the form (
1.1
), up to an additional factor
K
on the right-hand side (see also
[
41
]
).

(ii) The case
1\leqslant p<2
.

It was shown in
[
12
]
, using a refined form of Talagrand’s estimate
[
44
]
*Theorem 16.8.2, that (
1.1
) holds whenever

m\geqslant C(p,\varepsilon)\begin{cases}(KN)\log(KN),&p=1,\\[2.84526pt]
(KN)\log(KN)(\log\log(KN))^{2},&1<p<2.\end{cases}

(iii) The case
p>2
.

This regime is different. If the
L_{p}
and
L_{2}
norms are equivalent on a subspace
X_{N}
, that is,

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}\leqslant M\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mu)},\quad\forall\,f\in X_{N},

then the Marcinkiewicz discretization inequality necessarily requires at least

(1.3)

m\geqslant c(p)M^{-p}N^{p/2}

sampling points (see
[
4
]
and
[
24
]
*
D.20
). Under (
1.2
), it was shown in
[
27
]
that

(1.4)

m\geqslant C(p,\varepsilon)(KN)^{p/2}\log(KN)

suffices for (
1.1
).
To obtain nearly linear bounds in
N
for
p>2
,

[
27
]
replaced (
1.2
) by the stronger condition

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(KN),

under which it was shown that (
1.1
) holds whenever

(1.5)

m\geqslant C(p,\varepsilon)KN(\log(KN))^{p}.

This was later improved in
[
16
]
to

(1.6)

m\geqslant C(p,\varepsilon)KN(\log(KN))^{\min\{3,p\}}.

It is known (see Example
2.1
below) that for certain subspaces

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(N)
one cannot achieve (
1.1
)
with fewer than
N\log N

random
samples.
Thus, for
p>2
, the main unresolved issue in the random-sampling setting was whether one could bridge the gap between the lower bound of order
N\log N
and the previously known upper bounds of order
N(\log N)^{\min\{p,3\}}
.

1.5.
The main result on Marcinkiewicz discretization

In this paper, we establish the following probabilistic version of the

L_{p}
Marcinkiewicz discretization inequality for
p>2
.

Given a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
, we consider independent
random points
\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
in
\Omega
, not necessarily identically
distributed, such that the average of their distributions is
\mu
, that is,

(1.7)

\mu(E)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}\{\xi^{j}\in E\},\quad E\in\mathcal{F}.

In particular, this condition is satisfied whenever the points
\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{m}
are i.i.d.​​ with distribution
\mu
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be a probability space, and let

\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
be independent random points in
\Omega
satisfying
(
1.7
). Let
2<p<\infty
, and let
X_{N}\subset B(\Omega)
be a
finite-dimensional linear subspace of bounded functions satisfying

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(H)
with
H\geqslant 16
. Then there exists a
constant
c=c(p)>0
such that, whenever
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda\geqslant e
and

\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
satisfy

m\geqslant\bigl(\lambda\varepsilon^{-1}\log\varepsilon^{-1}\bigr)^{p}H\log H\,(\log\log H)^{p-1},

the Marcinkiewicz discretization inequality (
1.1
) holds with probability
at least

1-2e^{-c\lambda}
.

In particular, for
X_{N}\in\mathrm{NI}_{p,\infty}(KN)
, this improves the previously known bounds (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), which involved polynomial losses in
\log(KN)
, to

m\asymp KN\log(KN)(\log\log(KN))^{p-1},

which is optimal up to the factor
(\log\log(KN))^{p-1}
in the
random-sampling
setting.

We also note that, up to the factor
(\log\log(KN))^{p-1}
,
Theorem
1.1
recovers the bound (
1.4
) for spaces

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(KN)
. Indeed, in this case

\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant(KN)^{1/2}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mu)}\leqslant\bigl((KN)^{p/2}\bigr)^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)},

so that
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}\bigl((KN)^{p/2}\bigr)
.

1.6.
Restricted isometry property

Another sampling discretization problem considered in this paper is related to the construction of matrices with the
restricted isometry property
(RIP), a central notion in compressed sensing introduced by Candès and Tao
[
8
]
(see also
[
9
]
). Recall that an
m\times N
matrix
A
is said to satisfy the RIP of order
s
with constant
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
if

(1.8)

(1-\varepsilon)\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell_{2}^{N}}^{2}\leqslant\|A{\bf a}\|_{\ell_{2}^{m}}^{2}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell_{2}^{N}}^{2},\quad\forall\,{\bf a}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\text{ with }\|{\bf a}\|_{0}\leqslant s,

where
\|{\bf a}\|_{0}:=|\operatorname{supp}{\bf a}|
denotes the number of nonzero coordinates of the vector
{\bf a}
.

A common approach (see
[
19
]
*Chapter 12) to construct such matrices is to sample from a bounded orthonormal system

\mathcal{D}_{N}=\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{N}\}\subset B(\Omega)
in
L_{2}(\mu)
satisfying

(1.9)

\|\varphi_{j}\|_{\infty}\leqslant K_{0},\quad j=1,\dots,N.

More precisely, the central problem is to determine how many random samples

\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
are required so that the sampled matrix

(1.10)

A=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}(\xi^{1})&\cdots&\varphi_{N}(\xi^{1})\\
\varphi_{1}(\xi^{2})&\cdots&\varphi_{N}(\xi^{2})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varphi_{1}(\xi^{m})&\cdots&\varphi_{N}(\xi^{m})\end{pmatrix}

satisfies (
1.8
) with high probability.

Rudelson and Vershynin
[
40
]
proved that this holds whenever

m\geqslant C(K_{0},\varepsilon)\,s\log N\,(\log s)^{2}\,\log(s\log N),

and Haviv and Regev
[
22
]
(see also
[
7
]
) later improved this to

(1.11)

m\geqslant C(K_{0},\varepsilon)\,s\log N\,(\log s)^{2}.

Some intermediate results can be found in
[
6
,
10
]
.

For an orthonormal dictionary
\mathcal{D}_{N}
, the RIP for a matrix of the form (
1.10
) is equivalent to sampling discretization of the
L_{2}
norm on the class of
s
-sparse functions. Indeed, if
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and

f=\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varphi_{j},

then
A{\bf a}
is the vector of sampled values
(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))
up to the factor
m^{-1/2}
, and (
1.8
) becomes

(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mu)}^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mu)}^{2},\quad\forall\,f\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),

where

\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}):=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,2,\ldots,N\}\\
|J|=s\end{subarray}}\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_{j}\colon j\in J\}.

This identifies RIP as a special case of universal sampling discretization.

1.7.
Universal sampling discretization

The RIP formulation above suggests a natural extension in which the
L_{2}
norm is replaced by the
L_{p}
norm. This leads to a universal version of the Marcinkiewicz discretization problem: instead of discretizing the norm on a fixed finite-dimensional space, one seeks a single random sample that simultaneously discretizes the
L_{p}
norm over a family of finite-dimensional spaces generated by sparse subsets of a fixed finite dictionary. Problems of this type have recently been studied in
[
17
,
18
,
28
]
.

To move beyond the orthogonal setting, we replace orthogonality by a one-sided
s
-sparse Riesz-type condition.
Namely, we say that a dictionary
\mathcal{D}_{N}=\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{N}\}\subset B(\Omega)

is a uniformly bounded one-sided
s
-sparse Riesz system with constant
K\geqslant 1
if

\|\varphi_{j}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1,\quad j=1,\dots,N,

and

(1.12)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|a_{j}|^{2}\leqslant K\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\Bigr\|_{L_{2}(\mu)}^{2},\quad\forall\,{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\ \text{with }\|{\bf a}\|_{0}\leqslant s.

In particular, if
\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}\}
is an orthonormal system satisfying (
1.9
), then the rescaled system

\mathcal{D}_{N}:=\{\tfrac{1}{K_{0}}\varphi_{1},\ldots,\tfrac{1}{K_{0}}\varphi_{N}\}

forms a uniformly bounded
s
-sparse Riesz system with constant
K=K_{0}^{2}
.
We note that this condition can be viewed as a variant of the Nikolskii-type inequality
\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{Ks})
, since it implies that

\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant\sqrt{Ks}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mu)}\quad\forall\,f\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}).

The universal sampling discretization problem asks for conditions on
m
under which, for i.i.d.​​ random points
\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{m}\in\Omega
, one has

(1.13)

(1-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\leqslant\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p},\quad\forall\,f\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),

with high probability.
The non-Euclidean
L_{p}
case with
p\neq 2
is, as usual, significantly more challenging than the
L_{2}
case. The best previously known result in this direction was obtained in
[
18
]
, where the Rudelson–Vershynin theorem was extended from
p=2
to the full range
p\in[1,2]
. More precisely, it was shown there that when

(1.14)

m\geqslant C(p)Ks\log N\,(\log(Ks))^{2}\,\log(Ks\log N),

then
m
random samples suffice to guarantee (
1.13
) for a uniformly bounded
s
-sparse Riesz system with constant
K\geqslant 1
. Our next result improves this bound.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be a probability space, and let

\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}
be independent random points in
\Omega
satisfying
(
1.7
).
Then, for any
p\in[1,2]
,
there exists a constant
c=c(p)>0

such that for
all integers
N\geqslant 4
and
s\in[4,N]
, every uniformly bounded
4s
-sparse Riesz system

\mathcal{D}_{N}
in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)

with constant
K\geqslant 16
, and all parameters
\lambda\geqslant e
and

\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
, the universal Marcinkiewicz discretization inequality (
1.13
) holds with probability
at least

1-2e^{-c\lambda(\log\lambda)^{1/2}}

provided that the sample size
m
satisfies

m\geqslant\bigl(\lambda\,\varepsilon^{-1}\bigr)^{2}(\log\varepsilon^{-1})\,Ks\,\log N\,\log s\,\log(Ks)\,\log\log(Ks).

Compared with (
1.14
), Theorem
1.2
reduces the logarithmic loss from

(\log(Ks))^{2}\,\log(Ks\log N)

to

\log s\,\log(Ks)\,\log\log(Ks),

thus
improving the overall dependence both on
s
and
N
. Moreover, our bound differs from the best known
L_{2}
sample complexity (
1.11
) only by an additional factor of
\log\log s
.

2.
Probabilistic setting and connection with empirical moments

2.1.
Probabilistic framework

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}:=(\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m})
be a sequence of independent random
points in
\Omega
satisfying condition (
1.7
), that is,

\mu(E)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\mathbb{P}}[\xi^{j}\in E],\quad E\in\mathcal{F}.

Given a subclass
F\subset B(\Omega)
, we define the discretization error by

\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\sup_{f\in F}\biggl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}-\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\biggr|.

Thus, for

X_{N}^{p}:=\{f\in X_{N}\colon\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}\leqslant 1\},

the Marcinkiewicz discretization inequality (
1.1
) holds if and only if

\Er_{p}(X_{N}^{p},\boldsymbol{\xi})\leqslant\varepsilon.

Our goal is to estimate the minimal sample size
m
for which
\Er_{p}(X_{N}^{p},\boldsymbol{\xi})
is small, either in expectation or with high probability. More precisely, given
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)
, one seeks conditions ensuring that

\mathbb{E}\,\Er_{p}(X_{N}^{p},\boldsymbol{\xi})\leqslant\varepsilon\quad\text{or}\quad{\mathbb{P}}\bigl[\Er_{p}(X_{N}^{p},\boldsymbol{\xi})>\varepsilon\bigr]\leqslant\delta.

2.2.
Random sampling and empirical moments

The probabilistic Marcinkiewicz discretization problem is closely related to the problem of approximating moments of a random vector by empirical moments. Let
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an
N
-dimensional random vector, and let
\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{m}
be independent copies of
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
. For a set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, consider

V_{p}(B):=\sup_{y\in B}\biggl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{j},y\rangle|^{p}-\mathbb{E}|\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi},y\rangle|^{p}\biggr|.

The problem of controlling
V_{p}(B)
with high probability has been extensively studied in high-dimensional probability. The most classical case corresponds to
p=2
and
B
being the Euclidean unit ball, which is equivalent to approximating the covariance matrix by the empirical covariance matrix in operator norm (see, for instance,
[
36
,
37
,
52
,
43
,
54
]
). More general values of
p
and more general index sets
B
have been studied to a much lesser extent, with only a few works addressing this level of generality, typically under some additional assumptions (see, for instance,
[
53
,
1
,
20
,
21
,
2
]
).

The connection with probabilistic Marcinkiewicz discretization becomes immediate after choosing a basis of
X_{N}
. For simplicity, we assume that
X_{N}
consists solely of real-valued functions. Let
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{N}
be a basis of
X_{N}
, and define the random vector

\boldsymbol{\varphi}:=(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{N})\colon(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}.

If
\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{m}
are i.i.d. random points in
\Omega
with distribution
\mu
, then

\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{j}:=\bigl(\varphi_{1}(\xi^{j}),\dots,\varphi_{N}(\xi^{j})\bigr),\quad j=1,\dots,m,

are independent copies of
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
. Moreover,

\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})=V_{p}(B),

where

B:=\Bigl\{y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon\sum_{k=1}^{N}y_{k}\varphi_{k}\in F\Bigr\}.

Thus, probabilistic Marcinkiewicz discretization is equivalent to the empirical
moment problem for random vectors associated with
X_{N}
, and results obtained
in either language can be readily translated into the other.

However, the standard assumptions used in the empirical moment literature are not well suited to the regime
p>2
considered in this paper. Typically, one assumes an almost sure bound

(2.1)

\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{\ell_{2}^{N}}\leqslant\sqrt{KN}

together with a moment-comparison estimate of the form

(2.2)

\bigl(\mathbb{E}|\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi},y\rangle|^{q}\bigr)^{1/q}\leqslant M\bigl(\mathbb{E}|\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi},y\rangle|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2},\qquad\forall\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

for some
q\geqslant p
. When the basis
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
is orthonormal in
L_{2}(\mu)
, condition (
2.1
) corresponds to the usual
(2,\infty)
-Nikolskii inequality
X_{N}\in\mathrm{NI}_{2,\infty}(KN)
. By contrast, condition (
2.2
) translates into the norm-equivalence assumption

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}\leqslant\|f\|_{L_{q}(\mu)}\leqslant M\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mu)},\quad\forall\,f\in X_{N},

which is too restrictive for the discretization problem when
p>2
: it fails in many classical spaces, such as spaces of trigonometric or algebraic polynomials, and, more importantly, it is precisely this condition that forces the required number of sampling points to grow polynomially in the dimension (see (
1.3
)).

For this reason, we do not impose any moment-comparison assumptions. Instead, we work under the more flexible
(p,\infty)
-Nikolskii condition
X_{N}\in\mathrm{NI}_{p,\infty}(H)
and seek high-probability discretization bounds in terms of the Nikolskii constant
H
. It is worth mentioning that this framework still contains the classical
(2,\infty)
setting, since

\mathrm{NI}_{2,\infty}(KN)\implies\mathrm{NI}_{p,\infty}\bigl((KN)^{p/2}\bigr).

2.3.
Limitations of the
(p,\infty)
-Nikolskii assumption

The generality of the
(p,\infty)
-Nikolskii assumption comes with an intrinsic limitation: in this setting, one cannot in general obtain probabilistic Marcinkiewicz discretization with fewer than
\mathcal{O}(N\log N)
random samples. This already appears in the model case of the uniform measure on an
N
-point set.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\Omega:=\{1,\ldots,N\}
, let
X_{N}:=\{f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and let
\mu(\{j\})=\frac{1}{N}
for all
j\in\Omega
. Then

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}=\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|f(j)|^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant N^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)},

so that
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(N)
for every
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Let
\xi^{1},\xi^{2},\ldots
be independent random variables distributed according to
\mu
, and define
T
to be the smallest positive integer
m
such that

\{1,\ldots,N\}\subset\{\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m}\}
.
Clearly, for every fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Er_{p}(X_{N}^{p},\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\sup_{\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}\leqslant 1}\biggl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}-\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\biggr|\geqslant\mathbf{1}_{\{T>m\}}.

Hence, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

{\mathbb{P}}\bigl[\Er_{p}(X_{N}^{p},\boldsymbol{\xi})\leqslant\varepsilon\bigr]\leqslant{\mathbb{P}}(T\leqslant m).

This implies that for every
\delta\in(0,1)
and every
m\leqslant\delta N\log N
,

{\mathbb{P}}\bigl[\Er_{p}(X_{N}^{p},\boldsymbol{\xi})\leqslant\varepsilon\bigr]\leqslant{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(|T-\mathbb{E}(T)|\geqslant\mathbb{E}(T)-m\bigr)\leqslant\tfrac{\operatorname{Var}(T)}{(\mathbb{E}(T)-\delta N\log N)^{2}}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0,

where we used the classical coupon collector estimates

\mathbb{E}(T)=N\log N+O(N)\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Var}(T)=O(N^{2}).

Thus, under the sole assumption
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(N)
, one cannot in general expect probabilistic Marcinkiewicz discretization with fewer than
\mathcal{O}(N\log N)
random samples.

3.
The main abstract results

3.1.
A general estimate in terms of the chaining functional

Our main abstract discretization theorem bounds the expected uniform discretization error
\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})
of the
L_{p}
norm over an arbitrary function set
F
containing
0
, in terms of Talagrand’s chaining functional (see Definition
4.4
)

\gamma_{p,p_{0}}\Bigl(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}\Bigr):=m^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\gamma_{p,p_{0}}\Bigl(F(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\|\cdot\|_{p_{1}}\Bigr),

where
p\geqslant 2
,
p_{0}\in(1,p]
,
p_{1}:=\frac{pp_{0}}{p-p_{0}}
, and
F(\boldsymbol{\xi})
is defined in (
1.16
).

To formulate the theorem, let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be a probability space,
and let
\boldsymbol{\xi}:=(\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m})
be a collection of
m
independent
random elements taking values in
\Omega
and satisfying
condition (
1.7
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
2\leqslant p<\infty
, and let
F
be a class of bounded functions on
\Omega

which contains
0
and satisfies the following two conditions with parameters

p_{0}\in(1,p]
,
H>0
,
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cap[1,m]
, and
1\leqslant q<\infty
:

(3.1)

\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\,\Bigl(\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p-p_{0}}\cdot\sup_{g\in F}\|g\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p_{0}}\Bigr)^{q}\Bigr)^{1/q}\leqslant H^{p_{0}/p},

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}\,\Bigl[\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p-p_{0}}\cdot\Bigl(\gamma_{p,p_{0}}\Bigl(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}\Bigr)\Bigr)^{p_{0}}\Bigr]\leqslant(Hm_{0})^{p_{0}/p},

where
p_{1}:=\frac{pp_{0}}{p-p_{0}}
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(p)>0
such that, whenever

(3.3)

m\geqslant\max\Bigl\{16m_{0},\ e\bigl(e\log\log\tfrac{m}{m_{0}}\bigr)^{p/p_{0}^{\prime}}Hm_{0}\Bigr\},

one has

\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\,\bigl|\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})\bigr|^{q}\Bigr)^{1/q}\leqslant\frac{Cq}{1+\log q}\,\Theta,

where

\Theta:=\Bigl(\frac{Hm_{0}}{m}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigl(\log\log\frac{m}{m_{0}}+\log\frac{m}{Hm_{0}}\Bigr)^{1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\Bigl(1+\sup\limits_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\Bigr).

Moreover, if (
3.1
) holds for all
1\leqslant q<\infty
, then one has

(3.4)

\mathbb{P}\bigl[\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})>C\,\Theta\,t\bigr]\leqslant\exp\Bigl(-\frac{t\log t}{e}\Bigr),\quad\forall\,t\geqslant e.

The results announced in Section
1
will be derived from this theorem.

Our approach relies on Talagrand’s generic chaining
[
44
]
, combining ideas from
[
47
]
with van Handel’s more recent approach
[
56
,
55
]
, which utilizes interpolation functionals and the contraction principle.
This framework is particularly convenient in our setting, since it allows one to compare a given chaining functional with more tractable auxiliary ones.

Let
F\subset B(\Omega)
be a bounded set. A standard symmetrization argument, combined with Talagrand’s majorizing measure theorem, implies (see (
5.5
)) that

\mathbb{E}\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})\leqslant\frac{C(p)}{m}\,\mathbb{E}\,\Bigl[\gamma_{p,1}\bigl(T_{p}(F(\boldsymbol{\xi})),\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}\bigr)\Bigr],

thereby reducing the problem to the estimation of

\gamma_{p,1}\bigl(T_{p}(G),\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}\bigr)

for the vector set

G:=F(\boldsymbol{\xi})\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}.

The main difficulty is that the geometry of
T_{p}(G)
is usually much more complicated than that of the original set
G
, making it hard to obtain direct, sharp estimates of

\gamma_{p,1}\bigl(T_{p}(G),\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}\bigr)

in concrete situations. The key step in our argument is therefore to bound this functional in terms of more tractable auxiliary chaining quantities on
G
. This is formalized in the following abstract comparison theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
2\leqslant p<\infty
,
p_{0}\in(1,p]
, and
p_{1}:=\frac{pp_{0}}{p-p_{0}}
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(p)>0

such that for every nonempty set
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}

and any choice of parameters
b>0
,
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
,
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cap[1,m)
, one has

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{p,1}(T_{p}(G),\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Biggl[\Bigl(bn_{0}+b^{2}\cdot(\alpha_{0})^{1/p}+\frac{\log\frac{m}{m_{0}}}{(2^{n_{0}}\alpha_{0})^{1/p}}\Bigr)\sup_{x\in G}\|x\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle+\sup_{x\in G}\|x\|_{\infty}^{p-p_{0}}\cdot\frac{m_{0}^{p_{0}/p}\bigl[\diam(G,\|\cdot\|_{p_{1}})\bigr]^{p_{0}}+\bigl[{\gamma}_{p,p_{0}}(G,\|\cdot\|_{p_{1}})\bigr]^{p_{0}}}{b^{p_{0}-1}}\Biggr].

Remark 3.3
.

In the case of
p_{0}:=p
, Theorem
3.2
improves upon the naive estimate

{\gamma}_{p,1}(T_{p}(G),\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant 2p\Bigl(\sup_{x\in G}\|x\|_{p}^{p/p^{\prime}}\Bigr){\gamma}_{p,1}(G,\|\cdot\|_{\infty}),

since
{\gamma}_{p,p}(G,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
typically grows at least logarithmically more slowly in
m
than
{\gamma}_{p,1}(G,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
(see, for example, Lemma
4.11
when
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
p
-convex). By utilizing these superior estimates for
{\gamma}_{p,p}(G,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
, and optimizing the parameters
b,{\alpha}_{0}
and
n_{0}
in Theorem
3.2
, one typically obtains notably sharper bounds for
{\gamma}_{p,1}(T_{p}(G),\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})
.

Remark 3.4
.

It suffices to prove Theorem
3.2
in the real case, that is, for sets
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
. To see this, let
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
and consider the set

T_{1}(G):=\{(|z_{1}|,\ldots,|z_{m}|)\colon(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in G\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Then, for every
q\geqslant 1
,

\diam(T_{1}(G),\|\cdot\|_{q})\leqslant\diam(G,\|\cdot\|_{q}),

and (see (
4.3
)), for any
\alpha>0
,
\beta\geqslant 1
,

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(T_{1}(G),\|\cdot\|_{q})\leqslant C(\alpha)\,\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(G,\|\cdot\|_{q}),

while

{\gamma}_{p,1}(T_{p}(G),\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})={\gamma}_{p,1}\bigl(T_{p}\bigl(T_{1}(G)\bigr),\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}\bigr).

Applying Theorem
3.2
to the real set
T_{1}(G)
yields (
3.5
) for
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
.

3.2.
Uniformly convex classes

Theorem
3.1
is especially useful when the indexing sets
F
are
\theta
-convex for some
\theta\geqslant 2
(see Definition
4.8
).
It is well-known that Euclidean balls are
2
-convex. More generally, for any
1<p<\infty
, and any finite-dimensional subspace
X_{N}
of
L_{p}(\mu)
,
the unit ball

X_{N}^{p}:=\{f\in X_{N}\colon\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}\leqslant 1\}

is
\max\{p,2\}
-convex with a constant
\eta=\eta(p)
depending only on
p
.

Guédon and Rudelson
[
21
]
established the following estimate for every

p\geqslant\theta
and every
\theta
-convex subset
F
of a Euclidean ball

D
, with
\theta
-convexity constant
\eta
:

(3.6)

\mathbb{E}\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})\leqslant C(p,\eta)\Bigl(A+A^{1/2}\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p/2}\Bigr),

where

A=\frac{(\log m)^{2(1-\frac{1}{\theta})}}{m}\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sup_{f\in D}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{2}\sup_{g\in F}\|g\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p-2}\Bigr).

The specific case where
F=D
is the Euclidean ball and
p=2
was treated earlier by Rudelson
[
39
]
. The estimate (
3.6
) was further improved in
[
27
]
*Corollary 4.4, where it was shown to hold with

A=\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sup_{f\in D}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{2}\sup_{g\in F}\|g\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p-2}\Bigr)+\frac{\log m}{m}\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\sup_{g\in F}\|g\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p}\Bigr).

Taking
\theta=p\geqslant 2
,
F=X_{N}^{p}
, and
D=X_{N}^{2}
, one obtains the Marcinkiewicz discretization
inequality (
1.1
) with

m=C(p,\varepsilon)H^{p/2}\log H
sampling points
for each subspace
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{2,\infty}(H)
.

To cover the case
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(H)
with
p>2
, the following counterpart of the estimate (
3.6
) was proved in
[
27
]
*Corollary 4.7 for any
\theta
-convex subset
F
of
B(\Omega)
:

\mathbb{E}\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})\leqslant C(p,\eta)\Bigl(A+A^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p(1-\frac{1}{\theta})}\Bigr),

where

A=\frac{(\log m)^{\theta}}{m}\mathbb{E}\sup_{g\in F}\|g\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p}.

Taking here
F=X_{N}^{p}
and
\theta=p
, one obtains the Marcinkiewicz discretization inequality (
1.1
) with

m=C(p,\varepsilon)\,H(\log H)^{p}

sampling points for every subspace
X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(H)
(see
[
16
]
*Theorem 2.2 for further refinements).

In this paper, we deduce from Theorem
3.1
the following general result on the discretization error over
\theta
-convex sets
F\subset B(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, where
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
denotes an arbitrary probability space, and

B(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of all bounded real-valued functions on
\Omega
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}:=(\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{m})
be a collection of
m
independent
random elements taking values in
\Omega
and satisfying
condition (
1.7
).
Let
p\geqslant 2
and let
F\subset B(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
be a
p
-convex subset
with constant
\eta>0
such that

\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}\leqslant 1.

Assume that
for some constants
H\geqslant 16
and
1\leqslant q<\infty
,

(3.7)

\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{pq}\Bigr)^{1/q}\leqslant H.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(p,\eta)>0
such that, whenever

m\geqslant(8ep)^{2p}\,H\,(\log H)\,(\log\log H)^{p-1},

one has

\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\,|\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{q}\Bigr)^{1/q}\leqslant\frac{Cq}{1+\log q}\Bigl(\frac{H\log m}{m}\Bigr)^{1/p}\Bigl(\log\frac{m}{H}\Bigr)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Furthermore, if condition (
3.7
) is satisfied for every
q\geqslant 1
, and the sample size
m
satisfies

m\geqslant(\lambda\,\varepsilon^{-1}\log\varepsilon^{-1})^{p}H\,(\log H)\,(\log\log H)^{p-1}

for some constants
\lambda\geqslant e
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
, then

\mathbb{P}\bigl[\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})>\varepsilon\bigr]\leqslant 2e^{-c\lambda}

for some constant
c=c(p,\eta)>0
.

Taking
F=X_{N}^{p}
, one deduces Theorem
1.1
for a subspace

X_{N}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(H)
in the real case.

Remark 3.6
.

Using the equivalence between the two formulations described in
Subsection
2.2
, the preceding theorem gives a counterpart
for uniform approximation of moments of a random vector
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
by
empirical moments based on its i.i.d.​​ copies

\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{m}
, uniformly over
p
-convex sets

B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Under this equivalence, assumption (
3.7
) translates into the following
control of the dual norm:

\bigl(\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{j}\|_{B^{\circ}}^{pq}\bigr)^{1/q}\leqslant H,

where

\|\mathbf{x}\|_{B^{\circ}}:=\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in B}|\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}{\rangle}|,\ \ \ \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

A quantity of this type already appears in the result of
Guédon and Rudelson
[
21
]
.

4.
Preliminaries: entropy, entropy numbers and chaining functionals

In this section, we recall several basic facts concerning entropy numbers and Talagrand’s generic chaining theory
[
44
]
. We begin by fixing some notation.

Let
(\mathbf{T},\varrho)
be a metric space. For
x\in\mathbf{T}
and
r>0
, we write

B_{\varrho}(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbf{T}\colon\varrho(x,y)\leqslant r\}

for the closed ball of radius
r
centered at
x
. For a set
A\subset\mathbf{T}
, we define

\diam(A,\varrho):=\sup_{s,t\in A}\varrho(s,t)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \varrho(s,A):=\inf_{t\in A}\varrho(s,t)\ \ \text{for $s\in\mathbf{T}$}.

If the metric
\varrho
is induced by a norm
\|\cdot\|
, we write
\diam(A,\|\cdot\|)
instead of
\diam(A,\varrho)
.

For a finite set
\Lambda
, we denote its cardinality by
|\Lambda|
. For
x\geqslant 0
, we write
\lceil x\rceil
for the smallest integer greater than or equal to
x
. Finally, we set

N_{0}:=1,\quad N_{n}:=2^{2^{n}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

4.1.
Entropy and entropy numbers

Definition 4.1
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, the covering number
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(A,\varrho)
of a bounded set
A\subset\mathbf{T}
is defined as the least positive integer
n
for which there exist points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in A
such that

A\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}B_{\varrho}(x_{j},\varepsilon).

The
\varepsilon
-entropy of
A
with respect to
\varrho
is defined by

\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(A,\varrho):=\log_{2}\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(A,\varrho).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
A\subset\mathbf{T}
be bounded. For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the
n
-th entropy number of
A
with respect to
\varrho
is defined by

e_{n}(A,\varrho):=\inf_{x_{1},\dots,x_{N_{n}}\in A}\sup_{x\in A}\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N_{n}}\varrho(x,x_{j}),

or equivalently,

e_{n}(A,\varrho)=\inf\Bigl\{\varepsilon>0\colon A\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{n}}B_{\varrho}(x_{j},\varepsilon)\text{ for some }x_{1},\dots,x_{N_{n}}\in A\Bigr\}.

Clearly,

e_{n}(A,\varrho)=\inf\bigl\{\varepsilon>0\colon\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(A,\varrho)\leqslant 2^{n}\bigr\}.

One may also define entropy numbers without requiring the centers of the covering balls to belong to
A
, namely

\tilde{e}_{n}(A,\varrho):=\inf\Bigl\{\varepsilon>0\colon A\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{n}}B_{\varrho}(x_{j},\varepsilon)\text{ for some }x_{1},\dots,x_{N_{n}}\in\mathbf{T}\Bigr\}.

Then

(4.1)

\tilde{e}_{n}(A,\varrho)\leqslant e_{n}(A,\varrho)\leqslant 2\,\tilde{e}_{n}(A,\varrho).

If the metric
\varrho
is induced by a norm
\|\cdot\|
, we also write
e_{n}(A,\|\cdot\|)
instead of
e_{n}(A,\varrho)
.

We shall use the following standard properties of entropy numbers in finite-dimensional spaces, which follow from
[
48
]
*Corollary 7.2.2 and
[
48
]
*Estimate (7.1.6).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(X_{N},\|\cdot\|)
be an
N
-dimensional real normed space, and let

B:=\{x\in X_{N}\colon\|x\|\leqslant 1\}

be its unit ball. Then

e_{n}(B,\|\cdot\|)\leqslant 4N_{n}^{-1/N}=4\cdot 2^{-2^{n}/N},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Moreover, for every bounded set
A\subset X_{N}
, the sequence

\{N_{n}^{1/N}e_{n}(A,\|\cdot\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

is almost decreasing, in the sense that for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
k\leqslant n
,

(4.2)

N_{n}^{1/N}e_{n}(A,\|\cdot\|)\leqslant 3\,N_{k}^{1/N}e_{k}(A,\|\cdot\|).

For further properties of entropy numbers, we refer to
[
48
]
*Chapter 7.

4.2.
The Talagrand chaining functional

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(\mathbf{T},\varrho)
be a metric space. A sequence of partitions

\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of
\mathbf{T}
is called
increasing
if, for every

n\geqslant 0
and every
I\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
,
J\in\mathcal{A}_{n+1}
, one has either

J\subset I
or
J\cap I=\emptyset
.
It is called an
admissible sequence of partitions
if it is increasing and
satisfies
|\mathcal{A}_{n}|\leqslant N_{n}
for all
n\geqslant 0
.

For
x\in\mathbf{T}
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}_{n}(x)
the unique element of

\mathcal{A}_{n}
containing
x
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
1\leqslant\beta<\infty
. The
chaining functional
of

(\mathbf{T},\varrho)
is defined by

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\varrho):=\biggl(\inf_{\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl[2^{n/\alpha}\diam(\mathcal{A}_{n}(x),\varrho)\bigr]^{\beta}\biggr)^{1/\beta},

where the infimum is taken over all admissible sequences of partitions

\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of
\mathbf{T}
. If the metric
\varrho
is induced by a norm
\|\cdot\|
, we write

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)
instead of

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\varrho)
.

By definition,

\diam(\mathbf{T},\varrho)=\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\diam(\mathcal{A}_{0}(x),\varrho)\leqslant\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\varrho).

Thus, without loss of generality, we may always assume that

\diam(\mathbf{T},\varrho)<\infty
.

There is an equivalent description of the chaining functional in terms of approximating sets.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
1\leqslant\beta<\infty
, and let
(\mathbf{T},\varrho)
be a metric space. Define

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}(\mathbf{T},\varrho):=\biggl(\inf_{\{T_{n}\}}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl[2^{n/\alpha}\varrho(x,T_{n})\bigr]^{\beta}\biggr)^{1/\beta},

where the infimum is taken over all sequences of subsets
T_{n}\subset\mathbf{T}

satisfying
|T_{n}|\leqslant N_{n}
.

It is known (see
[
44
]
*Theorem 2.3.1 and
[
56
]
*Lemma 4.2) that:

(4.3)

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}(\mathbf{T},\varrho)\leqslant\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\varrho)\leqslant C(\alpha)\,\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}(\mathbf{T},\varrho).

The chaining functional is also linked to entropy numbers through the estimate

(4.4)

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\varrho)\leqslant C(\alpha)\biggl(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl[2^{n/\alpha}e_{n}(\mathbf{T},\varrho)\bigr]^{\beta}\biggr)^{1/\beta}.

The following theorem of Talagrand, which connects the supremum of a random process with the associated chaining functional, is a basic tool in what follows.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\{W_{x}\colon x\in\mathbf{T}\}
be a random process indexed by a metric space

(\mathbf{T},\varrho)
. Assume that there exists
\alpha>0
such that

(4.5)

{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(|W_{x}-W_{y}|\geqslant u\,\varrho(x,y)\bigr)\leqslant 2e^{-u^{\alpha}},\quad\forall\,u>0,\ \forall\,x,y\in\mathbf{T}.

Then there exists a constant
c=c(\alpha)>0
such that for every
x_{0}\in\mathbf{T}
,

{\mathbb{P}}\bigl[\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}|W_{x}-W_{x_{0}}|\geqslant\gamma_{\alpha,1}(\mathbf{T},\varrho)\,u\bigr]\leqslant 2e^{-cu^{\alpha}},\quad\forall\,u>0.

In particular,

\mathbb{E}\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}|W_{x}-W_{x_{0}}|\leqslant C(\alpha)\,\gamma_{\alpha,1}(\mathbf{T},\varrho).

4.3.
Entropy and chaining functionals of
\theta
-convex sets

Uniform convexity, and in particular
\theta
-convexity, plays an important role in the study of entropy numbers and Talagrand’s chaining functionals in normed spaces. In this section, we collect several known estimates for
\theta
-convex sets that will be used later to derive a convenient upper bound for the chaining functional appearing in our main arguments.

We begin by recalling the definition of a
\theta
-convex set.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
X
be a real linear space and let
\theta\geqslant 2
.
A centrally symmetric convex set
F\subset X
is called

\theta
-convex with constant
\eta>0

if there exists a norm
\|\cdot\|_{F}
on a linear subspace
Y
of
X
such that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{F})
is a Banach space,

F=\{x\in Y\colon\|x\|_{F}\leqslant 1\}
and

\biggl\|\frac{f+g}{2}\biggr\|_{F}\leqslant\max\{\|f\|_{F},\|g\|_{F}\}-\eta\,\|f-g\|_{F}^{\theta},\quad\forall\,f,g\in F.

In this case, we also call
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{F})
a
\theta
-convex Banach space

with constant
\eta>0
.

Classical examples of
\theta
-convex Banach spaces are the spaces
L_{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, and their closed linear subspaces, with
\theta=\max\{p,2\}
.

Remark 4.9
.

It is known (see, for instance,
[
38
]
*Proposition 2.4) that the above property is equivalent to the inequality

\left\|\frac{f+g}{2}\right\|_{F}^{\theta}+\lambda\left\|\frac{f-g}{2}\right\|_{F}^{\theta}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\|f\|_{F}^{\theta}+\|g\|_{F}^{\theta}\bigr),\quad\forall\,f,g\in Y,

where
\lambda>0
depends only on
\eta
and
\theta
.

Remark 4.10
.

The property of
\theta
-convexity is invariant under linear mappings. More precisely, assume that
F
is a
\theta
-convex subset of a real linear space
X
with constant
\eta>0
and

F:=\{x\in X\colon\|x\|_{F}\leqslant 1\}.

If
T:X\to Y
is a linear mapping onto another real linear space
Y
such that
\ker(T)
is closed with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{F}
, then the image
T(F):=\{Tx\colon x\in F\}
is a
\theta
-convex subset of
Y
with the same constant
\eta
and the associated norm on
Y
given by

\|y\|_{T(F)}:=\min\Bigl\{\|x\|_{F}\colon x\in X,\ T(x)=y\Bigr\},\quad y\in Y.

This invariance can be established using the fact that every uniformly convex
Banach space is reflexive.

The entropy bound (
4.4
) provides a general estimate for chaining functionals in terms of entropy numbers. While this estimate is sufficiently strong for many purposes, it is often not optimal and may lose logarithmic factors. A substantial improvement is possible when the underlying set is uniformly convex (see, for instance,
[
44
,
56
,
55
]
). In particular, for
\theta
-convex sets one has the following refinement of (
4.4
).

Lemma 4.11

(
[
44
]
*Theorem 4.1.4,
[
56
]
*Theorem 5.8)
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a real Banach space, and let
\theta\geqslant 2
. If
F\subset X
is a
\theta
-convex set with constant
\eta>0
, then for every
\alpha\geqslant 1
,

\gamma_{\alpha,\theta}(F,\|\cdot\|)\leqslant C(\alpha,\theta)\,\eta^{-1/\theta}\sup_{n\geqslant 0}2^{n/\alpha}e_{n}(F,\|\cdot\|).

For
\theta
-convex sets one also has the following estimate for entropy numbers in the sampled
\ell_{\infty}
norm (see
[
44
]
*Lemma 16.5.4,
[
27
]
*Corollary 4.2,
[
51
]
).

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
X
be a linear space of real-valued functions on a set
\Omega
, and let
\theta\geqslant 2
. Suppose that
F\subset X
is a
\theta
-convex set with constant
\eta>0

Step 1: Symmetrization

Proof.

\displaystyle m\,\mathbb{E}\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})

\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})=\sup_{f\in F}\Bigl|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\,-\,\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\Bigr|=\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}\Bigr\|_{V},

where
\varphi_{j}\colon F\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\varphi_{j}(f):=\frac{1}{m}\Bigl(|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}-\mathbb{E}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\Bigr),\quad f\in F.

Clearly,
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m}
are independent
V
-valued random variables with mean zero such that

\Bigl\|\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{V}\Bigr\|_{L_{q}}=\frac{1}{m}\bigg\|\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\sup_{f\in F}\Bigl||f(\xi^{j})|^{p}-\mathbb{E}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\Bigr|\bigg\|_{L_{q}}\leqslant\frac{2H_{0}}{m}.

Thus, using Lemma
5.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_{L_{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}\Bigr\|_{V}\bigg\|_{L_{q}}\leqslant\frac{Cq}{1+\log q}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\,\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}\Bigr\|_{V}+\Bigl\|\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{V}\Bigr\|_{L_{q}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{Cq}{1+\log q}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})+\frac{H_{0}}{m}\bigg)\leqslant\frac{Cq}{1+\log q}\max\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi}),\ \frac{H_{0}}{m}\Bigr\},

which proves the claim.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the claim of Theorem
3.1
for all
q>1
. First, note that

\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\sup_{f\in F}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leqslant m^{1-{\frac{p_{0}}{p}}}\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p-p_{0}}\cdot\sup_{g\in F}\|g\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p_{0}}.

Thus, (
3.1
) implies (
5.13
)
with

H_{0}=H^{p_{0}/p}m^{1-\frac{p_{0}}{p}},

and hence, by Lemma
5.3
, we obtain

(5.14)

\left(\mathbb{E}|\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant\frac{Cq}{1+\log q}\max\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi}),\ \Bigl(\frac{H}{m}\Bigr)^{p_{0}/p}\Bigr\}.

Next, by Hölder’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\,\Bigl(\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p-p_{0}}\sup_{g\in F}\|g\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}^{p_{0}}\Bigr)\leqslant H^{p_{0}/p},

so we may apply the results from Step 2 (Subsection
5.2
).
For all
m
satisfying (
3.3
), we then obtain

(5.15)

\mathbb{E}\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})\leqslant C_{9}(p)\Bigl(\frac{Hm_{0}}{m}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigl(\log\log\frac{m}{m_{0}}+\log\frac{m}{Hm_{0}}\Bigr)^{1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\Bigl(1+\sup\limits_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\Bigr).

On the other hand, (
3.3
) clearly implies that

\Bigl(\frac{H}{m}\Bigr)^{p_{0}/p}\leqslant\Bigl(\frac{Hm_{0}}{m}\Bigr)^{1/p}\leqslant\text{RHS of \eqref{9-2a}}.

This combined with (
5.14
) and (
5.15

\left(\mathbb{E}|\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant\frac{C_{10}(p)q}{1+\log q}\Bigl(\frac{Hm_{0}}{m}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigl(\log\log\frac{m}{m_{0}}+\log\frac{m}{Hm_{0}}\Bigr)^{1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\Bigl(1+\sup\limits_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\Bigr),

which proves Theorem
3.1
for any
q>1
.

5.4.
Step 4: Deriving the probability estimate (
3.4
)

We will apply the following simple lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
Z
be a real valued random variable satisfying

(\mathbb{E}|Z|^{q})^{1/q}\leqslant\frac{q}{1+\log q},\quad\forall\,q\geqslant 1.

Then we have

(5.16)

{\mathbb{P}}(|Z|\geqslant t)\leqslant\exp\biggl(-\frac{t\log t}{e}\biggr),\quad\forall\,t\geqslant e.

Proof.

Given any
t\geqslant e
, choose
q=q_{t}\geqslant 1
such that
t=\frac{eq}{1+\log q}
. By Chebyshev’s inequality, we obtain

(5.17)

{\mathbb{P}}(|Z|\geqslant t)\leqslant\frac{\mathbb{E}|Z|^{q}}{t^{q}}\leqslant\Bigl(\frac{q}{t(1+\log q)}\Bigr)^{q}=e^{-q}.

Since

\log t=1+\log q-\log(1+\log q)\leqslant\frac{eq}{t},

it follows that

q\geqslant\frac{t\log t}{e}.

Substituting into (
5.17
), we prove the desired estimate (
5.16
).
∎

Now, if (
3.1
) holds for every
q>1
, then, by the results of Subsection
5.3
, we have

\left(\mathbb{E}|\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant\frac{C(p)q}{1+\log q}\,\Theta,\quad\forall\,q\geqslant 1,

where

\Theta:=\Bigl({\frac{Hm_{0}}{m}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigl(\log\log\frac{m}{m_{0}}+\log\frac{m}{Hm_{0}}\Bigr)^{1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\Bigl(1+\sup\limits_{f\in F}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\Bigr).

Applying Lemma
5.5
to the random variable

Z=\frac{\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})}{C(p)\Theta},

we obtain the estimate

\mathbb{P}\bigl[\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})>C(p)\Theta\,t\bigr]\leqslant\exp\Bigl(-\frac{t\log t}{e}\Bigr),\quad\forall\,t\geqslant e.

, we deduce

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{p,p}(Y_{N}^{p},\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}})})

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p}\sup_{n\geqslant 0}2^{\frac{n}{p}}e_{n}(Y_{N}^{p},\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}})})\leqslant C_{p}\Bigl(\sup_{\widetilde{f}\in Y_{N}^{p}}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L_{\infty}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}})}\Bigr)(\log m)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p}\,(\log m)^{1/p}\,H^{1/p}.

This combined with (
6.15
) confirms the estimate (
6.12
). Thus, applying Lemma
6.1
completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
in the complex case.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Let
X_{N}\subset B(\Omega)
be an
N
-dimensional linear space of bounded functions.
For
1\leqslant p<\infty
and
x\in\Omega
, define the
L_{p}
Christoffel function
associated with
X_{N}
by

\lambda_{p}(x)\equiv\lambda(X_{N},p;x):=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{p}\,\mu(dz)\colon f\in X_{N},\ |f(x)|=1\Bigr\}.

Then
\lambda_{p}(x)>0
for every
x\in\Omega
.
Define

(Uf)(x):=\lambda_{p}(x)^{1/p}f(x),\quad f\in X_{N},\ x\in\Omega,

and set

H:=\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{p}(x)^{-1}\,\mu(dx).

Assume that
H<\infty
, and define a probability measure
\nu
on
\Omega
by

\nu(dx)=\frac{1}{H\lambda_{p}(x)}\,\mu(dx).

By the definition of
\lambda_{p}(x)
, for every
f\in X_{N}
and every
x\in\Omega
one has

|(Uf)(x)|^{p}=\lambda_{p}(x)|f(x)|^{p}\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{p}\,\mu(dz)=H\int_{\Omega}|Uf(z)|^{p}\,\nu(dz).

Hence,

\|Uf\|_{\infty}\leqslant H^{1/p}\,\|Uf\|_{L_{p}(\nu)},\quad\forall\,f\in X_{N};

namely,

Y_{N}:=\{Uf\colon f\in X_{N}\}\in\textnormal{NI}_{p,\infty}(H).

Applying Theorem
1.1
to
Y_{N}
and
\nu
, we conclude that for every
2<p<\infty
there exists a constant
C(p)>0
such that, whenever

m\geqslant C(p)\,H\,\log H\,(\log\log H)^{p-1},

there exist points
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in\Omega
satisfying

\frac{1}{2}\,\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{H\lambda_{p}(x_{j})}{m}\,|f(x_{j})|^{p}\leqslant\frac{3}{2}\,\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p},\quad\forall\,f\in X_{N}.

We note that
L_{p}
Christoffel functions have been extensively studied in the classical setting when
X_{N}
is a space of algebraic or trigonometric polynomials. In many such cases, sharp pointwise estimates for
\lambda_{p}(x)
imply that

H=KN
with a uniformly bounded constant
K
.
See, for instance,
[
34
]
*Section 4.3.

7.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we deduce Theorem
1.2
from Lemma
6.1
. Before delving into the technical details, we outline the primary strategy of the proof. We define

F:=\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N}):=\bigl\{f\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N})\colon\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}\leqslant 1\bigr\}.

Then, observing that
\Er_{p}(F,\boldsymbol{\xi})=\Er_{2}(T_{p/2}(F),\boldsymbol{\xi})
for any
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m})\in\Omega^{m}
, we apply Lemma
6.1
with the parameter
p=2
to the modified function class
T_{p/2}(F)
rather than directly to
F
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
proceeds in two stages.

First, in Subsection
7.1
, we establish two technical lemmas. Lemma
7.1
will imply that for any
1\leqslant p_{0}<2
, we have

\displaystyle\ {\gamma}_{2,p_{0}}(T_{p/2}(F),\|\cdot\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})})\leqslant C(p)\Bigl[{\gamma}_{p,\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\boldsymbol{\xi})})\Bigr]^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
p_{1}:=\frac{2p_{0}}{2-p_{0}}
and
q:=\frac{pp_{0}}{2-p_{0}}
. Lemma
7.2
then provides bounds for the chaining functionals
{\gamma}_{p,\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\boldsymbol{\xi})})
for any
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m})\in\Omega^{m}
. This bound constitutes the core technical component of the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Second, in Subsection
7.2
, we
use the estimates from Lemmas
7.1
and
7.2
to verify the conditions of Lemma
6.1
for the function class
T_{p/2}(F)
and the parameter
p=2
. Applying Lemma
6.1
then allows us to estimate the probability
{\mathbb{P}}[\Er_{2}(T_{p/2}(F),\boldsymbol{\xi})>\varepsilon]
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Throughout the remainder of this section,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
denotes our base probability space. Any supplementary probability measure
\nu
on
\Omega
is assumed to be defined on an extended
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{1}
containing
\mathcal{F}
.
Recall that we define

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|\quad\text{for all }f\in B(\Omega).

In our applications,
\nu
will be a uniform probability measure supported on a finite sequence of points in
\Omega
.

7.1.
Technical lemmas

We start with the following simple observation for the
\gamma
-functionals.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume that
\lambda\in(0,1]
,
\alpha>0
, and
\beta\geqslant 1/\lambda
.
Then there exists a constant
C({\alpha})>0
such that for any
p_{1}\in[\lambda^{-1},\infty]
, any
F\subset B(\Omega)
, and any probability measure
\nu
on
\Omega
,

(7.1)

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}\bigl(T_{\lambda}(F),\|\cdot\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\nu)}\bigr)\leqslant C(\alpha)\bigl[\gamma_{\lambda\alpha,\lambda\beta}\bigl(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{\lambda p_{1}}(\nu)}\bigr)\bigr]^{\lambda}.

Proof.

To begin, we recall the elementary inequality for any
0<\lambda\leqslant 1
and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
:

||a|^{\lambda}-|b|^{\lambda}|\leqslant|a-b|^{\lambda}.

Applying this inequality pointwise to functions
f_{1},f_{2}\in B(\Omega)
yields

\Bigl\||f_{1}|^{\lambda}-|f_{2}|^{\lambda}\Bigr\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\nu)}\leqslant\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{L_{\lambda p_{1}}(\nu)}^{\lambda}.

It follows that for every
f\in F
and every subset
A\subset F
,

\varrho_{L_{p_{1}}(\nu)}\big(|f|^{\lambda},T_{\lambda}(A)\big)\leqslant\varrho_{L_{\lambda p_{1}}(\nu)}\big(f,A\big)^{\lambda},

where

\varrho_{L_{r}(\nu)}(f,A)=\inf_{g\in A}\|f-g\|_{L_{r}(\nu)},\ \ 1\leqslant r\leqslant\infty.

Thus, for any sequence
\{A_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of finite subsets of
F
with
|A_{n}|\leqslant N_{n}
for all
n\geqslant 0
, we have

\sup_{f\in F}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl[2^{n/\alpha}\varrho_{L_{p_{1}}(\nu)}\big(|f|^{\lambda},T_{\lambda}(A_{n})\big)\Bigr]^{\beta}\leqslant\sup_{f\in F}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl[2^{n/(\lambda\alpha)}\varrho_{L_{\lambda p_{1}}(\nu)}\big(f,A_{n}\big)\Bigr]^{\lambda\beta}.

Taking the infimum over all such sequences of subsets
\{A_{n}\}
, and applying the equivalence (
4.3
) between the
\gamma
and
\gamma^{*}
functionals, we arrive at the desired estimate (
7.1
).
∎

The main technical component of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is contained in the following lemma.

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume that
s,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
4\leqslant s\leqslant N
. Let

\mathcal{D}_{N}:=\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{N}\}\subset B(\Omega)

be a uniformly bounded
4s
-sparse Riesz system in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)

with constant
K\geqslant 16
.
Then, given any
p\in[1,2]
, there exist a constant
C:=C(p)>0
and a number

p_{0}:=p_{0}(s,K)\in[\frac{3}{2},2)
such that for any probability measure
\nu
on
\Omega
, and for
q:=\frac{pp_{0}}{2-p_{0}}\geqslant 3
, we have

(7.2)

\sup_{f\in\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\ \cdot\ \Bigl[\gamma_{p,\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\bigl(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\bigr)\Bigr]^{\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\leqslant C\bigl(Ks\,\log s\,\log(Ks)\,\log N\bigr)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}

and

(7.3)

\sup_{f\in\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\cdot\sup_{g\in\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})}\|g\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}^{\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\leqslant C(Ks)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}},

where

\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N}):=\bigl\{f\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N})\colon\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}\leqslant 1\bigr\}.

Proof.

For clarity, we divide the proof of this lemma into several steps.

Step 1 (Reduction).
By adapting the argument from the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we show that it suffices to establish the lemma for real-valued functions and uniformly bounded
2s
-sparse (rather than
4s
-sparse) real Riesz systems.

To this end, we consider the product space
\widetilde{\Omega}:=\{0,1\}\times\Omega
equipped with the probability measure

\widetilde{\mu}(A):=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\mu\bigl(\{x\in\Omega\colon(0,x)\in A\}\bigr)+\mu\bigl(\{x\in\Omega\colon(1,x)\in A\}\bigr)\Bigr),\quad A\subset\widetilde{\Omega}.

For any
f\in B(\Omega)
, define its real-valued counterpart
\widetilde{f}\in B(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R})
by

\widetilde{f}(0,x):={\rm Re}\,f(x),\quad\widetilde{f}(1,x):={\rm Im}\,f(x),\ \ x\in\Omega.

We write
f^{\sim}
for
\widetilde{f}
whenever it is more convenient. By definition, for any
f\in B(\Omega)
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

(7.4)

\bigl((a+ib)f\bigr)^{\sim}=a\widetilde{f}+b(if)^{\sim}.

Furthermore, since for any
r\geqslant 2
,

|{\rm Re}\,f|^{r}+|{\rm Im}\,f|^{r}\leqslant|f|^{r}\leqslant 2^{\frac{r}{2}-1}\Bigl(|{\rm Re}\,f|^{r}+|{\rm Im}\,f|^{r}\Bigr),

it follows that

(7.5)

2^{1/r}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L_{r}(\widetilde{\mu})}\leqslant\|f\|_{L_{r}(\mu)}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L_{r}(\widetilde{\mu})},\ \ \forall\,f\in B(\Omega).

Next, we define the system of real-valued functions

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{2N}:=\bigl\{\widetilde{\varphi_{1}},\ldots,\widetilde{\varphi_{N}},(i\varphi_{1})^{\sim},\ldots,(i\varphi_{N})^{\sim}\bigr\}\subset B(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R}).

Note that
\|\widetilde{\varphi_{j}}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
and
\|(i\varphi_{j})^{\sim}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
for all
j
.
Furthermore, given a
4s
-sparse real vector
(a_{1},\dots,a_{N},b_{1},\dots,b_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}
, the vector
(a_{1}+ib_{1},\ldots,a_{N}+ib_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
is
4s
-sparse in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, thus, using (
1.12
), (
7.4
) and (
7.5
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}(|a_{j}|^{2}+|b_{j}|^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant K\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}(a_{j}+ib_{j})\varphi_{j}\Bigr\|_{L_{2}(\mu)}^{2}\leqslant 2K\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big((a_{j}+ib_{j})\varphi_{j}\big)^{\sim}\Bigr\|_{L_{2}(\widetilde{\mu})}^{2}

\displaystyle=2K\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\widetilde{\varphi_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}(i\varphi_{j})^{\sim}\Bigr\|_{L_{2}(\widetilde{\mu})}^{2}.

This means that
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{2N}
is a uniformly bounded real system on
\widetilde{\Omega}
satisfying a one-sided
4s
-sparse Riesz inequality (
1.12
) with constant
2K
.

Third, for another probability measure
\nu
on
\Omega
, we also define

\widetilde{\nu}(A):=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\nu\bigl(\{x\in\Omega\colon(0,x)\in A\}\bigr)+\nu\bigl(\{x\in\Omega\colon(1,x)\in A\}\bigr)\Bigr),\quad A\subset\widetilde{\Omega}.

Then
\widetilde{\nu}
is a probability measure on
\widetilde{\Omega}
satisfying

(7.6)

\|f\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\nu})},\ \ \forall\,f\in B(\Omega).

This implies that for
{\alpha}:=p
and
\beta:=\frac{pp_{0}}{2}
,

(7.7)

\gamma_{{\alpha},\beta}\bigl({\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})},\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\bigr)\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\gamma_{{\alpha},\beta}\Bigl(\big(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})\big)^{\sim},\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\nu})}\Bigr),

where

\bigl(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})\bigr)^{\sim}:=\Bigl\{\widetilde{f}\colon f\in\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})\Bigr\}\subset B(\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R}).

However,
if
f=\sum\limits_{j\in J}(a_{j}+ib_{j})\varphi_{j}\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N})
with
|J|\leqslant s
, then using (
7.4
), we have

\widetilde{f}=\sum_{j\in J}a_{j}\widetilde{\varphi_{j}}+\sum_{j\in J}b_{j}(i\varphi_{j})^{\sim}\in\Sigma_{2s}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{2N}),

which combined with (
7.5
) implies that

(7.8)

\bigl(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})\bigr)^{\sim}\subset\Sigma_{2s}^{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{2N}).

Thus, using (
7.7
), we obtain

(7.9)

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}\bigl(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\bigr)\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}\bigl(\Sigma_{2s}^{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{2N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\nu})}\bigr).

Using (
7.6
) and (
7.8
), we also have

(7.10)

\sup_{f\in\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})}\|f\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\sup_{g\in\Sigma_{2s}^{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{2N})}\|g\|_{L_{q}(\widetilde{\nu})}.

Now combining the above observations, using (
7.9
) and (
7.10
), we conclude that the complex case of Lemma
7.2
follows by applying the real-valued result to the uniformly bounded
4s
-sparse real Riesz system
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{2N}
and its corresponding class
\Sigma_{2s}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{2N})
. This completes the reduction.

For the remainder of the proof, we assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{D}_{N}
is a uniformly bounded, real-valued
2s
-sparse (rather than
4s
-sparse) Riesz system, and that

\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N})
is restricted to real coefficients.

Step 2.
In this step we prove that for any
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
, there exists a constant
C=C(p)>0
such that for any
3\leqslant q<\infty
,

(7.11)

e_{n}(\Sigma^{p}_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)})\leqslant C\Bigl(\frac{Ks\cdot q\log N}{2^{n}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2\theta}},\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
\theta:=\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}.

First, we prove that there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that for any
2<q<\infty
,

(7.12)

e_{n}(\Sigma^{2}_{2s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)})\leqslant C\Bigl(\frac{Ks\cdot q\log N}{2^{n}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Note that this bound is slightly stronger than (
7.11
) for
p=2
.

The proof of (
7.12
) relies on the following known result, which is a direct consequence of Theorems 7.4.3 and 8.6.6, and Remark 8.6.10 (see also Theorem 9.2.1) in
[
48
]
; see also
[
49
]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

There exists a universal constant
C>0
such that for any
2\leqslant q<\infty
, any measure
\nu
on
\Omega
, and any system of functions

\mathcal{D}_{N}=\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}\}\subset B(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\leqslant 1,

we have

e_{n}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\bigr)\leqslant C\sqrt{\frac{q\log N}{2^{n}}},\qquad\forall\,0\leqslant n\leqslant\log_{2}N,

where
\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathcal{D}_{N})
denotes the absolute convex hull of
\mathcal{D}_{N}
, defined as

\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathcal{D}_{N}):=\biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\colon a_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\ \sum_{j=1}^{N}|a_{j}|\leqslant 1\biggr\}.

By (
1.12
), we get
\Sigma^{2}_{2s}(\mathcal{D}_{N})\subset\sqrt{2Ks}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathcal{D}_{N})
. Furthermore,

\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\leqslant\sup_{x\in\Omega}|\varphi_{j}(x)|\leqslant 1,\ \ \forall\,1\leqslant j\leqslant N.

Thus, using Lemma
7.3
, we obtain (
7.12
)
for
0\leqslant n\leqslant k_{0}:=\lceil\log_{2}N\rceil
. For
n>k_{0}
, (
7.12
) can be deduced by applying (
4.2
) and (
7.12
) for the already proven case
n=k_{0}
:

\displaystyle e_{n}\bigl(\Sigma^{2}_{2s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{3\cdot 2^{2^{k_{0}}/N}}{2^{2^{n}/N}}e_{k_{0}}\bigl(\Sigma^{2}_{2s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\bigr)\leqslant C\sqrt{\frac{qKs\log N}{N}}2^{-2^{n}/N}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sqrt{\frac{qKs\log N}{2^{n}}}\sup_{t\geqslant 1/2}2^{-t}\sqrt{t}\leqslant C\sqrt{\frac{qKs\log N}{2^{n}}}.

Next, combining estimate (
7.12
) with Lemma
7.4
below, and taking into account that (
7.12
) holds for an arbitrary probability measure
\nu
on
\Omega
, we deduce estimate (
7.11
) for
1\leqslant p<2
and
3\leqslant q<\infty
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{N}=\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}\}\subset B(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
be a system of bounded, real-valued functions on
\Omega
.
Assume that for some
q\in[3,\infty)
and integer
s\in[0,N]
, there exists a constant
B>0
such that

(7.13)

e_{n}\bigl(\Sigma^{2}_{2s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\mu)}\bigr)+e_{n}\bigl(\Sigma^{2}_{2s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\bigr)\leqslant B2^{-n/2},\quad\forall\,n\geqslant 0.

Then for any
p\in[1,2)
, there exists a constant
C(p)>0
such that

(7.14)

e_{n}\bigl(\Sigma^{p}_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}\bigr)\leqslant C(p)(B2^{-n/2})^{1/\theta},\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},\ \ \text{where}\ \ \theta:=\tfrac{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Lemma
7.4
for the case
\mu=\nu
follows directly from Lemma 3.1 in
[
17
]
. In the general case, we use the following inequality:

(7.15)

e_{n+1}\bigl(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),L_{q}(\nu)\bigr)\leqslant 2e_{n}\bigl(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),L_{2}(\mu)\bigr)\cdot e_{n}\bigl(\Sigma_{2s}^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),L_{q}(\nu)\bigr).

Using (3.3) of
[
17
]
and the estimate (
7.13
) for
e_{n}(\Sigma_{2s}^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\mu)})
, we obtain

e_{n}\bigl(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\mu)}\bigr)\leqslant C_{1}(p)(B2^{-n/2})^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}},

which, combined with (
7.15
) and (
7.13
), yields the desired estimate (
7.14
).

Step 3.
In this step we prove that for any
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
and
n\geqslant\log_{2}\bigl(s\log_{2}\tfrac{eN}{s}\bigr)
, we have

(7.16)

e_{n+1}\bigl(\Sigma^{p}_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\bigr)\leqslant 4(Ks)^{1/p}2^{-2^{n}/s},

where we recall that
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|
for each
f\in B(\Omega)
.

For each
f\in\Sigma_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N})
, we have

\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant\sqrt{Ks}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mu)}\leqslant\sqrt{Ks}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{\frac{p}{2}}\|f\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{p}{2}},

implying

(7.17)

\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant K_{0}:=(Ks)^{1/p},\ \ \forall\,f\in\Sigma^{p}_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N}).

For each
J\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
with
|J|=s
, define
V_{J}:={\rm span}\{\varphi_{j}\colon j\in J\}
,

V_{J,\mu}^{p}:=\{f\in V_{J}\colon\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}\leqslant 1\}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{J}^{\infty}:=\{f\in V_{J}\colon\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1\}.

Then (
7.17
) implies that

V_{J,\mu}^{p}\subset K_{0}\cdot V_{J}^{\infty}
.
It follows that (see
[
48
]
*Theorem 7.2.1 and Corollary 7.2.2) for any
u>0
,

\mathcal{N}_{u}(V_{J,\mu}^{p},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leqslant\mathcal{N}_{u}\bigl(K_{0}\cdot V_{J}^{\infty},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\bigr)\leqslant\Bigl(1+\tfrac{2K_{0}}{u}\Bigr)^{s}.

Since

\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N})=\bigcup_{|J|=s}V_{J,\mu}^{p},

we deduce that for any
u>0
,

\mathcal{N}_{u}(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leqslant\tfrac{N!}{s!(N-s)!}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{2K_{0}}{u}\Bigr)^{s}\leqslant\bigr(\tfrac{eN}{s}\bigl)^{s}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{2K_{0}}{u}\Bigr)^{s}.

Setting
u=u_{n}:=4K_{0}2^{-2^{n}/s}
and assuming that
n\geqslant\log_{2}(s\log_{2}\frac{eN}{s})\geqslant\log_{2}s
,
we obtain

\mathcal{N}_{u_{n}}(\Sigma_{s}^{p}(\mathcal{D}_{N}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leqslant 2^{s\log_{2}(\frac{eN}{s})}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{2^{\frac{2^{n}}{s}}}{2}\Bigr)^{s}\leqslant 2^{2^{n+1}},

which implies (
7.16
).

Step 4 (final step).
In this step we prove estimates (
7.2
) and (
7.3
).

For simplicity, set
F:=\Sigma^{p}_{s}(\mathcal{D}_{N})
.
Let
p_{0}=\frac{2}{1+\delta}\in[3/2,2)
for some constant
\delta\in(0,1/3)
to be specified later. Then
q:=\frac{pp_{0}}{2-p_{0}}=\frac{p}{{\delta}}\geqslant 3
, and

\theta:=\tfrac{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}=\frac{\frac{p}{2}-\delta}{1-\delta}\in\bigl[\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{p}{2}\bigr].

By (
4.4
), we have

(7.18)

\displaystyle\Bigl[\gamma_{p,\frac{p_{0}p}{2}}(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)})\Bigr]^{\frac{p_{0}p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}(p)\cdot S,

where

S:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(2^{n/p}e_{n}(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)})\Bigr)^{\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}.

We split the sum
S
into three parts:

S=\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}+\sum_{n=n_{0}+1}^{n_{1}+{3}}+\sum_{n=n_{1}+{4}}^{\infty}:=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3},

where

n_{0}:=\bigl\lceil\log_{2}(2\log_{2}(eN))\bigr\rceil+2\text{ and }n_{1}:=\bigl\lceil\log_{2}(2s\log_{2}(eN))\bigr\rceil+2.

For the first sum, we apply (
7.17
) to obtain

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}\Bigl(2^{n/p}e_{n}(F,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\Bigr)^{\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\leqslant(Ks)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}2^{\frac{np_{0}}{2}}\leqslant C_{2}(p)(Ks\ \log N)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}.

For the second sum, we apply (
7.11
) to obtain

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{3}(p)\big(Ks{\delta}^{-1}\big)^{\frac{pp_{0}}{4\theta}}\sum_{n=n_{0}+1}^{n_{1}+{3}}2^{\frac{np_{0}}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\log N}{2^{n}}\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{2\theta}\cdot\frac{p_{0}}{2}}.

Since
2^{n}\geqslant\log N
for
n>n_{0}
and since
2\theta\leqslant p
, it follows that

\displaystyle S_{2}\leqslant C_{3}(p)\big(Ks{\delta}^{-1}\big)^{\frac{pp_{0}}{4\theta}}\sum_{n=n_{0}+1}^{n_{1}+{3}}2^{\frac{np_{0}}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\log N}{2^{n}}\Bigr)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}\leqslant C_{4}(p)\big(Ks{\delta}^{-1}\big)^{\frac{pp_{0}}{4\theta}}(\log N)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}\log s.

For the sum
S_{3}
, we apply (
7.16
) to obtain

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n=n_{1}+{4}}^{\infty}\Bigl(2^{\frac{n}{p}}e_{n}(F,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\Bigr)^{\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\leqslant C_{5}(p)(Ks)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}\sum_{n=n_{1}+3}^{\infty}2^{-(\frac{2^{n}}{s}-\frac{n}{p})\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}.

For
n\geqslant n_{1}+3
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{2^{n}}{s}-\frac{n}{p}\Bigr)-\Bigl(\frac{2^{n_{1}}}{s}-\frac{n_{1}}{p}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n_{1}}}{s}(2^{n-n_{1}}-1)-\frac{n-n_{1}}{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant(2^{n-n_{1}}-1)-\frac{n-n_{1}}{p}\geqslant n-n_{1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{5}(p)(Ks)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}2^{-(\frac{2^{n_{1}}}{s}-\frac{n_{1}}{p})\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}2^{-k/2}\leqslant C_{6}(p)(Ks)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}},

where the last step uses the fact that
\frac{2^{n_{1}}}{s}\geqslant n_{1}
.
Putting the above estimates together, and recalling
\frac{p}{2\theta}\geqslant 1
, we obtain

S\leqslant C_{7}(p)(Ks{\delta}^{-1})^{\frac{pp_{0}}{4\theta}}(\log N)^{p_{0}/2}\log s.

Substituting into (
7.18
), we then obtain

\Bigl[\gamma_{p,\frac{p_{0}p}{2}}(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)})\Bigr]^{\frac{p_{0}p}{2}}\leqslant C_{8}(p)(Ks{\delta}^{-1})^{\frac{pp_{0}}{4\theta}}(\log N)^{p_{0}/2}\log s.

This combined with (
7.17
) implies

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\ \cdot\ \Bigl[\gamma_{p,\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)})\Bigr]^{\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\leqslant C_{8}(p)(Ks)^{\frac{p}{2\theta}}{\delta}^{-\frac{pp_{0}}{4\theta}}(\log N)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}\log s

\displaystyle=C_{8}(p)\Bigl((Ks)^{1+\delta\cdot\frac{2-p\delta}{p-2\delta}}(\log s)^{1+\delta}\cdot\delta^{-1-\delta\cdot\frac{2-p}{p-2\delta}}\log N\Bigr)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{9}(p)\Bigl((Ks)^{1+6\delta}(\log s)^{1+\delta}\cdot\delta^{-1}\log N\Bigr)^{p_{0}/2}.

Finally, setting
\delta:=\frac{1}{3\log Ks}
, and recalling
p_{0}=\frac{2}{1+{\delta}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\ \cdot\ \Bigl[\gamma_{p,\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\nu)})\Bigr]^{\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\leqslant C_{10}(p)\Bigl(Ks\ \log s\ \log(Ks)\ \log N\Bigr)^{p_{0}/2},

and

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\cdot\sup_{g\in F}\|g\|_{L_{q}(\nu)}^{\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\leqslant\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p}\leqslant Ks\leqslant C(Ks)^{p_{0}/2}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

7.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

For simplicity, we again set

.
Fix the random points

with

(7.19)

\sup_{f\in F}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\ \cdot\ \Bigl[{\gamma}_{p,\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}(F,\|\cdot\|_{L_{q}(\boldsymbol{\xi})})\Bigr]^{\frac{pp_{0}}{2}}\leqslant C(p)\bigl(Ks\ \log s\ \log(Ks)\ \log N\bigr)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}},

6.1
to the function class

\leqslant C_{2}(p)\bigl(Ks\ \log s\ \log(Ks)\ \log N\bigr)^{\frac{p_{0}}{2}}.

8.
van Handel’s approach: the contraction principle and
K
-functionals

Talagrand’s generic chaining provides sharp estimates for the expected supremum of random processes in terms of chaining functionals (see, for instance, Theorem
4.7
). In concrete applications, however, these functionals are often difficult to estimate directly. Van Handel
[
56
,
55
]
developed a powerful approach to bounding them by combining a contraction principle with interpolation techniques based on
K
-functionals. In this section, we recall the main ingredients of this method and derive a finite-dimensional version of the contraction principle that is particularly convenient for our later applications.

We begin with van Handel’s contraction principle, which bounds chaining functionals in terms of suitable local entropy estimates. Throughout this section, unless stated otherwise,
\mathbf{T}=(\mathbf{T},\varrho)
denotes a metric space.

Lemma 8.1

(
[
56
]
*Theorem 3.1)
.

Assume that there exist a constant
a\geqslant 0
and a sequence of nonnegative functions

s_{n}:\mathbf{T}\to[0,\infty),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

such that for every set
A\subset\mathbf{T}
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

(8.1)

e_{n}(A,\varrho)\leqslant a\cdot\diam(A,\varrho)+\sup_{x\in A}s_{n}(x).

Then, for any
{\alpha}>0
and
1\leqslant\beta<\infty
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\varrho)\leqslant C({\alpha})\left[a\cdot\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\varrho)+\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\Bigl(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(2^{n/\alpha}s_{n}(x)\right)^{\beta}\Bigr)^{1/\beta}\right].

In particular, if
0<a\leqslant\frac{1}{2C({\alpha})}
, then

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\varrho)\leqslant 2C({\alpha})\ \sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\Bigl(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(2^{n/\alpha}s_{n}(x)\right)^{\beta}\Bigr)^{1/\beta}.

We will mostly work in a finite-dimensional setting, where
\mathbf{T}
is a subset of a finite-dimensional normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
and
\varrho
is the metric induced by
\|\cdot\|
. Combining Lemma
8.1
with Lemma
4.3
, we obtain the following finite-dimensional version of Lemma
8.1
, involving only finitely many functionals
s_{n}\colon\mathbf{T}\to[0,\infty)
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\mathbf{T}
be a subset of an
m
-dimensional real normed space
X=(X,\|\cdot\|)
. Assume that there exist a constant
a\geqslant 0
and a finite sequence of nonnegative functions

s_{n}\colon\mathbf{T}\to[0,\infty),\ \ n=0,1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil,

such that for every subset
A\subset\mathbf{T}
and every integer
n\in[0,\log_{2}m]
,

(8.2)

e_{n}(A,\|\cdot\|)\leqslant a\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|)+\sup_{x\in A}s_{n}(x).

Then, for any
{\alpha}>0
and
1\leqslant\beta<\infty
,

\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)\leqslant C(\alpha)\left[a\cdot\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)+\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\Bigl(\sum_{n=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}\big(2^{n/\alpha}s_{n}(x)\big)^{\beta}\Bigr)^{1/\beta}\right].

In particular, if
0<a\leqslant\frac{1}{2C({\alpha})}
, then

{\gamma}_{{\alpha},\beta}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)\leqslant 2C({\alpha})\ \sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\Bigl(\sum_{n=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}\big(2^{n/\alpha}s_{n}(x)\big)^{\beta}\Bigr)^{1/\beta}.

Proof.

Let
n_{*}=\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil
. Using (
4.2
), we obtain that for any
A\subset\mathbf{T}
, and any integer
n>n_{*}
,

\displaystyle e_{n}(A,\|\cdot\|)

\displaystyle\leqslant 3\cdot 2^{-2^{n}/m}2^{2^{n_{*}}/m}e_{n_{*}}(A,\|\cdot\|)\leqslant 12\cdot 2^{-2^{n}/m}e_{n_{*}}(A,\|\cdot\|).

Applying (
8.2
) with
n_{*}
in place of
n
then yields

(8.3)

e_{n}(A,\|\cdot\|)\leqslant 12\cdot a\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|)+12\cdot 2^{-2^{n}/m}\sup_{x\in A}s_{n_{\ast}}(x),\ \ \forall\,n>n_{\ast}.

Next, define

s_{n}(x):=12\cdot 2^{-2^{n}/m}s_{n_{*}}(x),\ \ n=n_{*}+1,n_{*}+2,\ldots.

Combining (
8.3
) with (
8.2
), we get

e_{n}(A,\|\cdot\|)\leqslant 12a\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|)+\sup_{x\in A}s_{n}(x),\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

It then follows by Lemma
8.1
that

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{{\alpha},\beta}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)

\displaystyle\leqslant C({\alpha})\left[12a\cdot\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)+\Bigl(\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\sum_{n=0}^{n_{*}}\big(2^{n/\alpha}s_{n}(x)\big)^{\beta}\Bigr)^{1/\beta}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\ \hskip 56.9055pt+\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}s_{n_{*}}(x)\cdot\Bigl(\sum_{n=n_{*}+1}^{\infty}\big(2^{n/\alpha}\cdot 2^{-2^{n}/m}\big)^{\beta}\Bigr)^{1/\beta}\right].

However, it is readily seen (see, for instance,
[
27
]
*Lemma 2.11) that

\Bigl(\sum_{n=n_{*}+1}^{\infty}\bigl(2^{n/\alpha}\cdot 2^{-2^{n}/m}\bigr)^{\beta}\Bigr)^{1/\beta}\leqslant C_{1}(\alpha)\,2^{n_{*}/\alpha}.

Thus,

{\gamma}_{{\alpha},\beta}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)\leqslant C_{2}({\alpha})\left[a\cdot\gamma_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)+\Bigl(\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\sum_{n=0}^{n_{*}}\big(2^{n/\alpha}s_{n}(x)\big)^{\beta}\Bigr)^{1/\beta}\right],

which completes the proof.
∎

In applications of Lemma
8.1
, the main difficulty is to construct, for a sufficiently small constant
a>0
, a sequence of functionals
s_{n}\colon\mathbf{T}\to[0,\infty)
such that the entropy estimate (
8.1
) holds for every subset
A\subset\mathbf{T}
, while the quantity

\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\bigl(2^{n/\alpha}s_{n}(x)\bigr)^{\beta}

remains under control. Van Handel
[
56
]
proposed an elegant way to do this by means of interpolation
K
-functionals. We briefly recall this approach below, in a slightly generalized form. Since only the case
\beta=1
will be used later, we restrict ourselves to this case throughout the rest of the section.

Definition 8.3
.

Fix
\alpha>0
. Let

u_{n}\colon\mathbf{T}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

be a sequence of nonnegative functionals such that for some constant
u^{\ast}>0
,

0\leqslant u_{n}(x,r)\leqslant u_{n+1}(x,r)\leqslant u^{\ast},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbf{T},\ \forall\,r\geqslant 0,\ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define the associated
K
-functional by

K_{n}(x,t):=\inf_{r\geqslant 0}\bigl[tr+u_{n}(x,r)\bigr],\quad x\in\mathbf{T},\ t\geqslant 0.

Given a constant
a>0
, let
r_{n}^{a}\colon\mathbf{T}\to[0,\infty)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, be any sequence of functions satisfying

t_{n}r_{n}^{a}(x)+u_{n}(x,r_{n}^{a}(x))\leqslant K_{n}(x,t_{n})+2^{-n}u^{\ast},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbf{T},\ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where

t_{n}:=a\,2^{n/\alpha}
.

By definition, it is clear that

(8.4)

0\leqslant K_{n}(x,t)\leqslant K_{n+1}(x,t)\leqslant u_{n+1}(x,0)\leqslant u^{\ast},\ \ \forall\,x\in\mathbf{T},\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We will adhere to the notation and assumptions introduced in Definition
8.3
throughout the remainder of this section.

The next two lemmas record the basic properties of the quantities introduced in Definition
8.3
. Since we will use them repeatedly, we include their proofs for completeness.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
a>0
,
x\in\mathbf{T}
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Define

(8.5)

\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x):=K_{n+n_{0}}(x,t_{n+n_{0}})-K_{n}(x,t_{n})+\frac{u^{\ast}}{2^{n+n_{0}}},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
t_{n}=a2^{n/{\alpha}}
.
Then

(8.6)

\ \Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)\geqslant(1-2^{-\frac{1}{{\alpha}}})a2^{(n+n_{0})/{\alpha}}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)\geqslant 0,\ \ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

and

(8.7)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)\leqslant(n_{0}+1)u^{\ast}.

In particular,

(8.8)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{n/{\alpha}}\cdot r_{n}^{a}(x)\leqslant\frac{C({\alpha})u^{\ast}}{a}.

Proof.

For brevity, write
r_{k}^{a}:=r_{k}^{a}(x)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. By definition, for each integer
n\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant t_{n+n_{0}}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}+u_{n+n_{0}}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a})-\inf_{r\geqslant 0}\Bigl[t_{n}r+u_{n}(x,r)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geqslant(t_{n+n_{0}}-t_{n})r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}+u_{n+n_{0}}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a})-u_{n}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a})

\displaystyle\geqslant(t_{n+n_{0}}-t_{n})r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}\geqslant(1-2^{-\frac{1}{{\alpha}}})t_{n+n_{0}}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a},

which proves (
8.6
).
To establish (
8.7
), we use (
8.4
) to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)\leqslant u^{\ast}+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}}K_{n+j}(x,t_{n+j})\leqslant(n_{0}+1)u^{\ast}.

Finally, applying (
8.6
) and (
8.7
) with
n_{0}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle a\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{n/{\alpha}}r_{n}^{a}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t_{n+1}r_{n+1}^{a}(x)\leqslant C({\alpha})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{n,1}(x)\leqslant C({\alpha})u^{\ast},

which proves (
8.8
).
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Under the notation of Lemma
8.4
, for every
r\geqslant 0
, one has

(8.9)

u_{n+n_{0}}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x))-u_{n}(x,r)\leqslant\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)+a2^{n/{\alpha}}r.

Proof.

By Definition
8.3
, for any
r\geqslant 0
,
we have

\displaystyle u_{n+n_{0}}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x))

\displaystyle\leqslant t_{n+n_{0}}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)+u_{n+n_{0}}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x))

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{n+n_{0}}(x,t_{n+n_{0}})+\frac{u^{\ast}}{2^{n+n_{0}}}=K_{n}(x,t_{n})+\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)+t_{n}r+u_{n}(x,r).

Rearranging terms yields the desired inequality (
8.9
).
∎

We now combine Lemma
8.2
with Lemmas
8.4
and
8.5
to derive an estimate for the chaining functional
\gamma_{\alpha,1}
in terms of the growth of the associated
K
-functionals in a finite-dimensional setting. This estimate will be the main tool in the next section.

Theorem 8.6
.

Let
\mathbf{T}
be a subset of an
m
-dimensional real normed space
X=(X,\|\cdot\|)
, and let
{\alpha}>0
,
b_{1},b_{2}>0
, and
n_{0},n_{1},\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
n_{1}\leqslant\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil
be given parameters.
Set

a:=\frac{1}{4b_{2}C(\alpha)},

where
C(\alpha)
is the constant from Lemma
8.2
. Assume that there exists a finite sequence of nonnegative functions

\widetilde{s}_{k}\colon\mathbf{T}\to[0,\infty),\quad k=0,1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil,

such that for every subset
A\subset\mathbf{T}
and every integer
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
satisfying
n+\ell\leqslant\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil
, one has

(8.10)

\displaystyle e_{n+\ell}(A,\|\cdot\|)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{4C({\alpha})}\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|)+\sup_{x\in A}\widetilde{s}_{n}(x)+b_{1}\cdot 2^{n_{0}/{\alpha}}\cdot\sup_{x\in A}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{b_{2}}{2^{n/{\alpha}}}\cdot\sup_{x\in A}\Bigl[u_{n+n_{0}}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x))-u_{n}(x,r_{A})\Bigr],

where

r_{A}=2^{n_{0}/{\alpha}}\cdot\sup\limits_{x\in A}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)+\diam(A,\|\cdot\|).

Then

(8.11)

{\gamma}_{{\alpha},1}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)\leqslant C({\alpha},\ell)\Bigg[(b_{2}n_{0}+b_{1}b_{2})u^{\ast}+2^{n_{1}/\alpha}\diam(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)+\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\sum_{n=n_{1}}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}2^{n/{\alpha}}\widetilde{s}_{n}(x)\Bigg].

In applications of Theorem
8.6
, the parameters
b_{1},b_{2},n_{0}
, and
n_{1}
must typically be optimized. By contrast, the parameter
\ell
is inessential, and the implicit constants are allowed to depend on it.

Proof.

We may assume that
n_{1}+\ell\leqslant\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil
, since otherwise, combining (
4.4
) with (
4.2
), one readily obtains

\gamma_{\alpha,1}(T,\|\cdot\|)\leqslant C(\alpha)\,2^{(n_{1}+\ell)/\alpha}\diam(T,\|\cdot\|).

We apply Lemma
8.2
.
For
0\leqslant k\leqslant n_{1}+\ell
, define
s_{k}(x):=\diam(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)
. For
n_{1}+\ell<k\leqslant\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil+\ell
, define

(8.12)

\displaystyle s_{k}(x)

\displaystyle:=b_{2}\cdot 2^{-k/{\alpha}}\Delta_{k-\ell,n_{0}}(x)+\widetilde{s}_{k-\ell}(x)+b_{1}\cdot 2^{n_{0}/{\alpha}}\cdot r_{k+n_{0}-\ell}^{a}(x),

where
\Delta_{k-\ell,n_{0}}(x)
is defined in (
8.5
).
We claim that for every subset
A\subset\mathbf{T}
and every integer
0\leqslant k\leqslant\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil
, the following estimate holds:

(8.13)

e_{k}(A,\|\cdot\|)\leqslant\frac{1}{2C({\alpha})}\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|)+C({\alpha},\ell)\sup_{x\in A}s_{k}(x).

By the definition of
s_{k}
, the estimate (
8.13
) holds trivially if
0\leqslant k\leqslant n_{1}+\ell
. Now suppose
k=n+\ell
for some integer
n>n_{1}
. Invoking Lemma
8.5
with
r=r_{A}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in A}\Bigl[u_{n+n_{0}}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x))-u_{n}(x,r_{A})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sup_{x\in A}\Bigl[\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)+a2^{(n+n_{0})/{\alpha}}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)\Bigr]+a2^{n/{\alpha}}\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}({\alpha})\sup_{x\in A}\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)+a2^{n/{\alpha}}\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|),

where the last inequality follows from (
8.6
). Substituting this into the assumption (
8.10
), we find

\displaystyle e_{k}(A,\|\cdot\|)=e_{n+\ell}(A,\|\cdot\|)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Bigl(\frac{1}{4C({\alpha})}+ab_{2}\Bigr)\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|)+C_{1}({\alpha})\cdot\sup_{x\in A}\left[\widetilde{s}_{n}(x)+b_{1}2^{n_{0}/{\alpha}}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)+\frac{b_{2}\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)}{2^{n/{\alpha}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2C({\alpha})}\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|)+C_{2}({\alpha},\ell)\cdot\sup_{x\in A}s_{k}(x).

This proves the claim (
8.13
).

Finally, applying Lemma
8.2
yields

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{{\alpha},1}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{3}({\alpha},\ell)\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}2^{k/{\alpha}}s_{k}(x)

(8.14)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{4}({\alpha},\ell)\ 2^{n_{1}/\alpha}\diam(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|)+C_{3}({\alpha},\ell)\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}}\sum_{k=n_{1}+\ell}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}2^{k/{\alpha}}s_{k}(x).

Using (
8.12
) and Lemma
8.4
, for any
x\in\mathbf{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n_{1}+\ell}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}2^{k/{\alpha}}s_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{5}({\alpha},\ell)\sum_{n=n_{1}}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}\Bigl[b_{2}\Delta_{n,n_{0}}(x)+2^{n/{\alpha}}\widetilde{s}_{n}(x)+b_{1}2^{(n+n_{0})/{\alpha}}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{6}({\alpha},\ell)\Bigl[b_{2}n_{0}u^{\ast}+b_{1}b_{2}u^{\ast}\Bigr]+C_{5}({\alpha},\ell)\sum_{n=n_{1}}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}2^{n/{\alpha}}\widetilde{s}_{n}(x).

Substituting this last estimate into (
8.14
) yields the desired estimate (
8.11
).
∎

9.
Proof of Theorem
3.2

In this section, we prove Theorem
3.2
, which is restated below for convenience. Throughout the paper,
\|\cdot\|_{q}
denotes the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{q}^{m}}
for any
1\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
.

Theorem
3.2
.

Let
2\leqslant p<\infty
,
p_{0}\in(1,p]
, and
p_{1}:=\frac{pp_{0}}{p-p_{0}}
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(p)>0

such that for every nonempty set
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}

and any choice of parameters
b>0
,
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
,
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cap[1,m)
, one has

(
3.5
)

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{p,1}(T_{p}(G),\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Biggl[\Bigl(bn_{0}+b^{2}\cdot(\alpha_{0})^{1/p}+\frac{\log\frac{m}{m_{0}}}{(2^{n_{0}}\alpha_{0})^{1/p}}\Bigr)\sup_{x\in G}\|x\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle+\sup_{x\in G}\|x\|_{\infty}^{p-p_{0}}\cdot\frac{m_{0}^{p_{0}/p}\bigl[\diam(G,\|\cdot\|_{p_{1}})\bigr]^{p_{0}}+\bigl[{\gamma}_{p,p_{0}}(G,\|\cdot\|_{p_{1}})\bigr]^{p_{0}}}{b^{p_{0}-1}}\Biggr].

By Remark
3.4
, we only need to prove Theorem
3.2
for the real case
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The proof is based on Theorem
8.6
, applied to the set

\mathbf{T}:=T_{p}(G)=\{|g|^{p}\colon g\in G\},

together with a suitable sequence of functionals
\widetilde{s}_{k}:\mathbf{T}\to[0,\infty)
constructed from an admissible sequence of partitions of
G
. A central step is to verify a local entropy estimate of the form (
8.10
) for an appropriate increasing sequence of functionals

u_{n}\colon\mathbf{T}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

This requires two technical lemmas, Lemmas
9.2
and
9.3
, presented in Subsection
9.1
. We prove Lemma
9.2
in that subsection, but the proof of the more involved Lemma
9.3
is deferred to Subsection
9.3
. Finally, the proof of Theorem
3.2
is completed in Subsection
9.2
using Lemma
9.3
.

Throughout this section, we fix
2\leqslant p<\infty

and assume
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a nonempty set satisfying

(9.1)

\sup_{g\in G}\|g\|_{p}^{p}=:u^{\ast}<\infty.

Let
\mathbf{T}:=T_{p}(G)
. For brevity, we also write
q
in place of
p_{0}
.

For any index set
I\subset\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, let

\mathbb{R}^{I}:=\operatorname{span}\{e_{j}\colon j\in I\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},

and denote by

R_{I}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}

the orthogonal projection onto
\mathbb{R}^{I}
, given by

R_{I}x=\sum_{j\in I}x_{j}e_{j},\quad x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Denoting the complement of
I
by

I^{c}:=\{1,2,\dots,m\}\setminus I,

we define the threshold set
for any
\tau\geqslant 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
as

I^{c}(x;\tau):=\{j\in I^{c}\colon x(j)\geqslant\tau\}.

9.1.
Key lemmas

We start with the following elementary inequality.

Lemma 9.1
.

For any
f,g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
I\subset\{1,2,\dots,m\}
,
r\in[1,\infty)
, and
\tau\geqslant 0
, we have

(9.2)

\displaystyle\Bigl\||f|^{p}-|g|^{p}\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Bigl\||R_{I}f|^{p}-|R_{I}g|^{p}\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}+\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigl(\|R_{I^{c}}f\|_{p}^{p-1}+\|R_{I^{c}}g\|_{p}^{p-1}\Bigr)+

\displaystyle+2p\bigl(S^{r}(\tau)\bigr)^{\frac{1}{rp^{\prime}}}\|R_{I^{c}}(f-g)\|_{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}},

where

S^{r}(\tau)=\max\Biggl\{\ \sum_{j\in I^{c}(|f|^{p};\tau)}\ |f(j)|^{pr},\ \sum_{j\in I^{c}(|g|^{p};\tau)}\ |g(j)|^{pr}\Biggr\}.

Proof.

First, we show that for any
a,b\geqslant 0
and
\eta\geqslant 0
,

(9.3)

|a^{p}-b^{p}|^{p^{\prime}}\leqslant p^{p^{\prime}}(a_{\eta}^{p}+b_{\eta}^{p})|b-a|^{p^{\prime}}+\eta^{p^{\prime}}(a^{p}+b^{p}),

where
u_{\eta}=u\cdot\mathbf{1}_{[\eta,\infty)}(u)
for
u\geqslant 0
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a\geqslant b
.
If
a<\eta
, then

|a^{p}-b^{p}|^{p^{\prime}}\leqslant(a^{p})^{p^{\prime}}=(a^{p})^{p^{\prime}-1}a^{p}\leqslant(\eta^{p})^{p^{\prime}-1}(a^{p}+b^{p})=\eta^{p^{\prime}}(a^{p}+b^{p}).

If
a\geqslant\eta
, then
a=a_{\eta}
and

\displaystyle|a^{p}-b^{p}|^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(pa^{p-1}|b-a|)^{p^{\prime}}=p^{p^{\prime}}a^{p}|b-a|^{p^{\prime}}\leqslant p^{p^{\prime}}(a_{\eta}^{p}+b_{\eta}^{p})|b-a|^{p^{\prime}}.

Thus, in either case, we prove (
9.3
).

Next, we prove (
9.2
). Let
f,g\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
I\subset\{1,2,\dots,m\}
. Then

(9.4)

\displaystyle\Bigl\||f|^{p}-|g|^{p}\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Bigl\||R_{I}f|^{p}-|R_{I}g|^{p}\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}+\Bigl\||R_{I^{c}}f|^{p}-|R_{I^{c}}g|^{p}\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}.

Using (
9.3
) with
\eta=\tau^{1/p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigl\|

\displaystyle|R_{I^{c}}f|^{p}-|R_{I^{c}}g|^{p}\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}=\sum_{j\in I^{c}}\Bigl||f(j)|^{p}-|g(j)|^{p}\Bigr|^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant p^{p^{\prime}}\sum_{j\in I^{c}}|f(j)-g(j)|^{p^{\prime}}\Bigl(|f(j)|^{p}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{|f(j)|\geqslant\eta\}}(j)+|g(j)|^{p}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{|g(j)|\geqslant\eta\}}(j)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\eta^{p^{\prime}}\sum_{j\in I^{c}}\Bigl(|f(j)|^{p}+|g(j)|^{p}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leqslant\tau^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(\|R_{I^{c}}f\|_{p}^{p}+\|R_{I^{c}}g\|_{p}^{p})+2p^{p^{\prime}}\bigl(S^{r}(\tau)\bigr)^{1/r}\|R_{I^{c}}(f-g)\|_{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}.

This combined with (
9.4
) yields (
9.2
).
∎

We now apply Lemma
9.1
to establish a key local entropy estimate, which provides control of the form (
8.2
).

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\ell\geqslant 1
be a fixed, inessential integer, and let
(\mathcal{A}_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
be an arbitrary admissible sequence of partitions of
G
. Then,
for any
q\in(1,p]
, any integer
n\geqslant 0
, any parameters
\beta>0
and
\tau>0
(possibly depending on
n
), and every subset
A\subset\mathbf{T}:=T_{p}(G)
, the following estimate holds with
p_{1}:=\frac{pq}{p-q}
:

(9.5)

\displaystyle e_{n+\ell+1}(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant 16\cdot 2^{-2^{\ell}}\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})+8\tau^{1/p}(u^{\ast})^{1/p^{\prime}}+

\displaystyle\ \ +8p\beta\cdot\min_{|I|\leqslant 2^{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau)+8p\beta^{-(q-1)}\cdot\sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\cdot\sup_{x\in A}\ \left[\diam\,(\mathcal{A}_{n+\ell}(g_{x}),\|\cdot\|_{p_{1}})\right]^{q},

where
I\subset\{1,2,\dots,m\}
and

S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau):=\sup_{x\in A}\ \sum_{j\in I^{c}(x;\tau)}\ x(j).

Here, for each
x\in A
, we denote by
g_{x}\in G
a vector such that
x=|g_{x}|^{p}
, and by

\mathcal{A}_{n+\ell}(g_{x})
the unique cell of the partition
\mathcal{A}_{n+\ell}
that contains
g_{x}
.

Proof.

Let
G_{A}=T_{p}^{-1}(A):=\{g\in G\colon|g|^{p}\in A\}
, so that
A=\{|g|^{p}\colon g\in G_{A}\}
. For convenience, define
D:=\diam(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

(9.6)

e_{n+\ell+1}(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})>16\cdot 2^{-2^{\ell}}D,

as otherwise (
9.5
) holds trivially. Fix an index set
I\subset\{1,2,\dots,m\}
such that
|I|\leqslant 2^{n}
.
Note that for each
x=|g_{x}|^{p}\in A
, we may decompose

x=R_{I}x+R_{I^{c}}x=|R_{I}g_{x}+R_{I^{c}}g_{x}|^{p}=|R_{I}g_{x}|^{p}+|R_{I^{c}}g_{x}|^{p}.

Let
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset A
be arbitrary subsets with
|\Lambda_{1}|,|\Lambda_{2}|\leqslant N_{n+\ell}
, and define

\Lambda:=\Bigl\{R_{I}y+R_{I^{c}}z\colon y\in\Lambda_{1},z\in\Lambda_{2}\Bigr\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Then
|\Lambda|\leqslant|\Lambda_{1}||\Lambda_{2}|\leqslant N_{n+\ell+1}
, and hence,

(9.7)

e_{n+\ell+1}(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant 2\sup_{x\in A}\min_{u\in\Lambda}\|x-u\|_{p^{\prime}}.

The factor
2
appears because of (
4.1
), since in general
\Lambda\not\subset A
.

Using Lemma
9.1
with
r=\frac{q^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}\geqslant 1
, for any
x\in A
and
u=R_{I}y+R_{I^{c}}z\in\Lambda
with
y\in\Lambda_{1}
and
z\in\Lambda_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|x-u\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\bigl\||g_{x}|^{p}-|R_{I}g_{y}+R_{I^{c}}g_{z}|^{p}\bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|R_{I}x-R_{I}y\|_{p^{\prime}}+2\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}(u^{\ast})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}+2p\bigl(S^{r}_{x,z}(\tau)\bigr)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|g_{x}-g_{z}\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}},

where we have

\displaystyle S^{r}_{x,z}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\max\Biggl\{\sum_{j\in I^{c}(|g_{x}|^{p};\tau)}|g_{x}(j)|^{pr},\ \ \sum_{j\in I^{c}(|g_{z}|^{p};\tau)}|g_{z}(j)|^{pr}\Biggr\},

\displaystyle=\max\Biggl\{\sum_{j\in I^{c}(x;\tau)}|x(j)|^{r},\ \ \sum_{j\in I^{c}(z;\tau)}|z(j)|^{r}\Biggr\}\leqslant\sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{\frac{q^{\prime}(p-q)}{q}}\cdot S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau),

with the inequality following from
r-1=\frac{q^{\prime}}{qp}(p-q)
. Substituting into (
9.7
) yields

\displaystyle e_{n+\ell+1}(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\sup_{x\in A}\min_{y\in\Lambda_{1}}\bigl\|R_{I}x-R_{I}y\bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}+4\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}(u^{\ast})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}+4p\bigl(S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau)\bigr)^{1/q^{\prime}}\sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{\frac{p-q}{q}}\sup_{x\in A}\min_{z\in\Lambda_{2}}\|g_{x}-g_{z}\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\sup_{x\in A}\min_{y\in\Lambda_{1}}\bigl\|R_{I}x-R_{I}y\bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}+4\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}(u^{\ast})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}+4p\beta S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau)+

\displaystyle+4p\beta^{-(q-1)}\sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\sup_{x\in A}\min_{z\in\Lambda_{2}}\|g_{x}-g_{z}\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}^{q},

where we used Young’s inequality in the last step.
Taking infimum over all such subsets
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset A
, we deduce

(9.8)

\displaystyle e_{n+\ell+1}(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e_{n+\ell}(R_{I}A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})+4\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}(u^{\ast})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}+4p\beta S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau)+

\displaystyle+4p\beta^{-(q-1)}\ \sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset G_{A}\\
|S|\leqslant N_{n+\ell}\end{subarray}}\ \sup_{x\in A}\ \min_{g\in S}\|g_{x}-g\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}^{q}.

Since

R_{I}A\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{I}\colon\|x\|_{p^{\prime}}\leqslant D\},

and
|I|\leqslant 2^{n}
, we apply Lemma
4.3
to obtain

\displaystyle e_{n+\ell}(R_{I}A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leqslant D\cdot e_{n+\ell}(B_{p^{\prime}}^{2^{n}},\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant 4\cdot 2^{-2^{\ell}}\cdot D<\frac{1}{4}e_{n+\ell+1}(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}),

where
B_{p^{\prime}}^{k}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\colon\|x\|_{p^{\prime}}\leqslant 1\}
, and we used (
9.6
) in the last step.
Substituting into (
9.8
), we then deduce

(9.9)

\displaystyle e_{n+\ell+1}(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leqslant 8\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}(u^{\ast})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}+8p\beta S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau)

\displaystyle+8p\beta^{-(q-1)}\ \sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset G_{A}\\
|S|\leqslant N_{n+\ell}\end{subarray}}\ \sup_{x\in A}\ \min_{g\in S}\|g_{x}-g\|_{p_{1}}^{q},

where
p_{1}:=\frac{pq}{p-q}
.

To estimate the last term, we use the given admissible sequence of partitions of
G
.
Choose a set
S_{n}\subset G_{A}
such that for each cell
F
from the partition
\mathcal{A}_{n+\ell}
with
F\cap G_{A}\neq\emptyset
,
F\cap S_{n}
contains exactly one point from
F\cap G_{A}
. Then
|S_{n}|\leqslant N_{n+\ell}
, and moreover,

(9.10)

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset G_{A}\\
|S|\leqslant N_{n+\ell}\end{subarray}}\ \sup_{x\in A}\ \min_{g\in S}\|g_{x}-g\|_{p_{1}}^{q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\ \min_{g\in S_{n}}\|g_{x}-g\|_{p_{1}}^{q}\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\min_{g\in S_{n}\cap\mathcal{A}_{n+\ell}(g_{x})}\|g_{x}-g\|_{p_{1}}^{q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\left[\diam(\mathcal{A}_{n+\ell}(g_{x}),\|\cdot\|_{p_{1}})\right]^{q}.

Substituting (
9.10
) into (
9.9
) completes the proof of (
9.5
).
∎

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we will apply Theorem
8.6
with the parameter
{\alpha}=p
. The crucial part is to establish a local entropy number estimate of the form (
8.10
). Lemma
9.2
provides the entropy estimate (
9.5
), which can be rewritten as

(9.11)

\displaystyle e_{n+\ell+1}(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant 16\cdot 2^{-2^{\ell}}\diam(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})+\sup_{x\in A}\widetilde{s}_{n}(x)+8p\beta\cdot\min_{|I|\leqslant 2^{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau),

where
\widetilde{s}_{n}(x)=\widetilde{s}_{n,1}(x)+\widetilde{s}_{n,2}(x)
,
\widetilde{s}_{n,1}(x):=8\tau^{1/p}(u^{\ast})^{1/p^{\prime}}
and

(9.12)

\displaystyle\widetilde{s}_{n,2}(x):=8p\beta^{-(q-1)}\cdot\sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\cdot\left[\diam\,(\mathcal{A}_{n+\ell}(g_{x}),\|\cdot\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}})\right]^{q}.

For the term
\widetilde{s}_{n,1}
, choosing
\tau:=({\alpha}_{0}2^{n+n_{0}-1})^{-1}u^{\ast}
for some parameters
{\alpha}_{0}\in(0,1)
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

(9.13)

\sup_{x\in A}\sum_{n=n_{1}}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}2^{\frac{n}{p}}\widetilde{s}_{n,1}(x)=\frac{8u^{\ast}}{{\alpha}_{0}^{1/p}}\sum_{n=n_{1}}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}2^{-\frac{n_{0}-1}{p}}\leqslant\frac{C\cdot(\log_{2}m-n_{1})\cdot u^{\ast}}{(2^{n_{0}}{\alpha}_{0})^{1/p}},

for any
n_{1}\in[1,\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil]\cap\mathbb{N}
. For the term
\widetilde{s}_{n,2}
, we may choose
\beta=b2^{-\frac{n}{p}}
for some parameter
b>0
, and optimize the admissible sequence so that

(9.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in A}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{\frac{n}{p}}\widetilde{s}_{n,2}(x)

\displaystyle=8pb^{-(q-1)}\cdot\sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\sup_{x\in A}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl[2^{\frac{n}{p}}\diam\,(\mathcal{A}_{n+\ell}(g_{x}),\|\cdot\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}})\Bigr]^{q}

\displaystyle\leqslant 16pb^{-(q-1)}\sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\Bigl[{\gamma}_{p,q}(G,\|\cdot\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}})\Bigr]^{q}.

The main difficulty comes from the term involving
\min\limits_{|I|\leqslant 2^{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau)
.
To establish a local entropy number bound of the form (
8.10
), it is necessary to construct an increasing sequence of bounded nonnegative functions
u_{n}\colon{\bf T}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
satisfying the estimate (
9.15
) below. The existence of such a sequence is ensured by the following lemma.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
.
Let
{\alpha}_{0}\in(0,1)
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cap[2,\infty)
be given parameters. Define, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

{\alpha}_{n}:={\alpha}_{0}2^{n}\ \ \text{ and}\ \ \tau:=\tau_{n}=({\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1})^{-1}u^{\ast},

where
u^{\ast}>0
is the constant given in (
9.1
).
Then there exists an increasing sequence of bounded, nonnegative functions

u_{n}\colon{\bf T}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),\ \ n=0,1,2,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil,

such that

0\leqslant u_{n}(x,r)\leqslant u_{n+1}(x,r)\leqslant u^{\ast},\ \ \forall\,x\in{\bf T},\ \ \forall\,r\geqslant 0,\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

and the following growth condition holds:

for every subset
A\subset{\bf T}
, every constant
a>0
and every integer
1\leqslant n\leqslant\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil
, one has

(9.15)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in A}

\displaystyle\Bigl[u_{n+n_{0}}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x))-u_{n}(x,r_{A})\Bigr]+({\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\sup_{x\in A}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\min_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}\sup_{x\in A}\ \sum_{j\in I^{c}(x,\tau)}|x(j)|=\min_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau){\geqslant\min_{|I|\leqslant 2^{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A;\tau)},

where
r_{n}^{a}(x)\geqslant 0
is as defined in Definition
8.3
, and

r_{A}=2^{n_{0}/p}\sup_{x\in A}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)+\diam(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}).

The proof of Lemma
9.3
will be deferred to Subsection
9.3
.
For now, we accept it as given and proceed to the proof of Theorem
3.2
in the next subsection.

9.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.2
(under the assumption of Lemma
9.3
)

Let
q:=p_{0}\in(1,p]
,
b>0
,
{\alpha}_{0}\in(0,1)
and
n_{0},m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be the parameters given in Theorem
3.2
. Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer depending only on
p
, to be specified later.

First, as we discussed above, we may apply Lemma
9.2
with the choices of parameters

\alpha=p,\ \beta=2^{-n/p}b,\ \ \text{and}\ \tau=(\alpha_{0}2^{n+n_{0}-1})^{-1}u^{\ast}.

This yields the entropy estimate (
9.11
) for every subset
A\subset{\bf T}
.

Next, we invoke Lemma
9.3
to estimate the term
\min\limits_{|I|\leqslant 2^{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau)
in (
9.11
). Specifically, substituting the bound from (
9.15
) into
(

), we obtain, for any

,

\displaystyle e_{n+\ell+1}(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant 16\cdot 2^{-2^{\ell}}\cdot\diam(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})+\sup_{x\in A}\widetilde{s}_{n}(x)+

\displaystyle+8pb\cdot(2^{n_{0}}{\alpha}_{0})^{1/p}\cdot\sup_{x\in A}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)+\frac{8pb}{2^{n/p}}\cdot\sup_{x\in A}\Bigl[u_{n+n_{0}}(x,r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x))-u_{n}(x,r_{A})\Bigr],

where

\widetilde{s}_{n}=\widetilde{s}_{n,1}+\widetilde{s}_{n,2}
, as defined in (
9.12
), and

r_{A}=2^{n_{0}/p}\sup_{x\in A}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)+\diam(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}).

We now specify the constants
\ell
and
a
. Let
C(p)>1
denote the constant
C({\alpha})
from Theorem
8.6
, evaluated at
{\alpha}=p
. Define
a:=\frac{1}{32pC(p)b}
, and choose
\ell=\ell(p)
to be the smallest positive integer such that

16\cdot 2^{-2^{\ell}}\leqslant\frac{1}{4C(p)}.

Invoking Theorem
8.6
with the parameters

{\alpha}=p,\ \ b_{1}=8pb\cdot({\alpha}_{0})^{1/p},\ \ b_{2}=8pb,\ \ \text{and}\ \ n_{1}=\lceil\log_{2}m_{0}\rceil,

we obtain

{\gamma}_{p,1}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant C_{1}(p)\Bigg[\big(bn_{0}+b^{2}({\alpha}_{0})^{1/p}\big)u^{\ast}+2^{n_{1}/p}\diam({\bf T},\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})+\sup_{x\in{\bf T}}\sum_{n=n_{1}}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}2^{n/p}\widetilde{s}_{n}(x)\Bigg].

To estimate the last term, we take the infimum over all admissible sequences
(\mathcal{A}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of partitions of
G
. Using the estimates (
9.13
) and (
9.14
),
and taking
n_{1}=\lceil\log_{2}m_{0}\rceil

we then obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in T}\sum_{n=n_{1}}^{\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}2^{n/p}\widetilde{s}_{n}(x)\leqslant C_{2}(p)\Bigg[\frac{(\log_{2}\frac{m}{m_{0}})\cdot u^{\ast}}{(2^{n_{0}}{\alpha}_{0})^{1/p}}+\sup\limits_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\cdot\frac{\Bigl[{\gamma}_{p,q}(G,\|\cdot\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}})\Bigr]^{q}}{b^{q-1}}\Bigg].

We now estimate the diameter
\diam({\bf T},\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})
of
{\bf T}=T_{p}(G)
in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}
. For any
f,g\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\||f|^{p}-|g|^{p}\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant p\Bigl\|(|f|^{p-1}+|g|^{p-1})|f-g|\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2p\|f-g\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}\cdot\sup_{h\in G}\|h\|_{\infty}^{\frac{p-q}{q}}\cdot(u^{\ast})^{1/q^{\prime}}.

Applying Young’s inequality, we then obtain, for all
f,g\in G
,

\Bigl\||f|^{p}-|g|^{p}\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}\leqslant 2pbm_{0}^{-1/p}u^{\ast}+2p(bm_{0}^{-1/p})^{-(q-1)}\cdot\sup_{h\in G}\|h\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\cdot\|f-g\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}}^{q}.

Taking the supremum over all
f,g\in G
, we conclude

m_{0}^{1/p}\diam({\bf T},\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant 2pbu^{\ast}+2pb^{-(q-1)}m_{0}^{q/p}\,\sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\cdot\bigl[\diam(G,\|\cdot\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}})\bigr]^{q}.

Finally, combining all of the above estimates, we obtain the desired bound:

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{p,1}(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{3}(p)\Biggl[\left(bn_{0}+b^{2}\cdot({\alpha}_{0})^{1/p}+\frac{\log_{2}\frac{m}{m_{0}}}{(2^{n_{0}}{\alpha}_{0})^{1/p}}\right)u^{\ast}

\displaystyle+\sup_{f\in G}\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-q}\cdot\frac{m_{0}^{q/p}\bigl[\diam(G,\|\cdot\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}})\bigr]^{q}+\bigl[{\gamma}_{p,q}(G,\|\cdot\|_{\frac{pq}{p-q}})\bigr]^{q}}{b^{q-1}}\Biggr].

Theorem
3.2
is now proved up to Lemma
9.3
. ∎

9.3.
Proof of Lemma
9.3

We consider the metric space
{\bf T}=(\mathbf{T},\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})
, where

{\bf T}=T_{p}(G)=\{|f|^{p}\colon f\in G\}.

Recall that

u^{\ast}=\sup_{g\in G}\|g\|_{p}^{p}=\sup_{x\in{\bf T}}\|x\|_{1}<\infty.

For
x\in{\bf T}
and
r\geqslant 0
, let
B_{\bf T}(x,r):=\{y\in{\bf T}\colon\|x-y\|_{p^{\prime}}\leqslant r\}
.
We need to estimate the quantity

{\min_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau),}

where

S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau)=\sup_{x\in A}\sum_{j\in I^{c}(x;\tau)}\ x(j).

Throughout the proof, the letters
I,J
always denote subsets of
\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.

We temporarily fix an index set
I\subset\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
with
|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}
.
For any
x\in\mathbf{T}
, we have

|I^{c}(x;\tau)|\leqslant\frac{\|x\|_{1}}{\tau}\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in A}\|R_{I^{c}(x;\tau)}x\|_{1}\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}J:\ I\cap J=\emptyset\\
|J|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1}\end{subarray}}\|R_{J}x\|_{1}.

Let
s\colon{\bf T}\to[0,\infty)
be any nonnegative function.
If
x\in{\bf T}
,
y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))
and
|J|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1}
, then

\displaystyle\Bigl|\|R_{J}x\|_{1}-\|R_{J}y\|_{1}\Bigr|\leqslant\bigl\|R_{J}(x-y)\bigr\|_{1}\leqslant|J|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|x-y\|_{p^{\prime}}\leqslant s(x)\cdot({\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1})^{\frac{1}{p}},

which implies

\|R_{J}x\|_{1}\leqslant({\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot s(x)+\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{J}y\|_{1}.

Hence,

(9.16)

S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau)\leqslant S_{1}(I;A,\tau)+({\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1})^{1/p}\sup_{x\in A}s(x),

where

S_{1}(I;A,\tau):=\sup_{x\in A}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}J:\ I\cap J=\emptyset\\
|J|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1}\end{subarray}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{J}y\|_{1}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in A}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}J:\ I\cap J=\emptyset\\
|J|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1}\end{subarray}}\Bigg[\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{J}y\|_{1}+\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}J:\ I\cap J=\emptyset\\
|J|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1}\end{subarray}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{I\cup J}y\|_{1}\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\max_{|J|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{J}y\|_{1},

where the last step uses the inequality
{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1}+{\alpha}_{n}\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}}
.
For any
x,z\in A
, we have

B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))\subset B_{\bf T}(z,s_{A}),\ \ \text{where}\ \ s_{A}:=\sup_{z\in A}s(z)+\diam(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}),

implying

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}\geqslant\sup_{z\in A}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(z,s_{A})}\|R_{I}y\|_{1},\ \ \forall\,x\in A.

It follows that

\displaystyle S_{1}(I;A,\tau)+{\sup_{z\in A}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(z,s_{A})}}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\max_{|J|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{J}y\|_{1}.

This combined with (
9.16
) yields

\displaystyle S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau)+\sup_{x\in A}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s_{A})}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\max_{|J|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{J}y\|_{1}+({\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1})^{1/p}\sup_{x\in A}s(x).

Taking the maximum over all
I\subset\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
with
|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\min_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau)+\sup_{x\in A}\max_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s_{A})}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}\Bigg[S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau)+\sup_{x\in A}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s_{A})}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\max_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}+({\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1})^{1/p}\sup_{x\in A}s(x).

Thus, it follows that

(9.17)

\displaystyle\min_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau)-({\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1})^{1/p}\sup_{x\in A}s(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Bigg[\sup_{x\in A}\max_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}-\sup_{x\in A}\max_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s_{A})}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\Bigg[\max_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n+n_{0}}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s(x))}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}-\max_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,s_{A})}\|R_{I}y\|_{1}\Bigg].

Now we define
a sequence of functionals
u_{n}\colon{\bf T}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
as follows:

u_{n}(x,r)=\max_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}\inf_{y\in B_{\bf T}(x,r)}\|R_{I}y\|_{1},\quad x\in\mathbf{T},\ \ r\geqslant 0.

By definition, the sequence
(u_{n}(x,r))_{n\geqslant 0}
is decreasing in
r
and satisfies

0\leqslant u_{n}(x,r)\leqslant u_{n+1}(x,r)\leqslant u^{\ast}\ \ \text{for all $x\in\mathbf{T}$ and $r\geqslant 0$.}

Furthermore, we can rewrite (
9.17
) equivalently in the form:

\min_{|I|\leqslant{\alpha}_{n}}S_{I^{c}}(A,\tau)\leqslant\sup_{x\in A}\Bigl[u_{n+n_{0}}(x,s(x))-u_{n}(x,s_{A})\Bigr]+({\alpha}_{n+n_{0}-1})^{1/p}\sup_{x\in A}s(x).

To complete the proof, we set

s(x):=r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)
, and observe that

s_{A}:=\sup_{z\in A}s(z)+\diam(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})\leqslant r_{A}:=2^{n_{0}/p}\sup_{x\in A}r_{n+n_{0}}^{a}(x)+\diam(A,\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}}).

Since the function
u_{n}(x,\cdot)
is decreasing, we prove the growth condition (
9.15
) for all
a>0
.
∎
