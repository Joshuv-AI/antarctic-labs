Introduction

The fixed points of the complex exponential are classical:
\exp(z)=z
holds exactly at
z=-W_{k}(-1)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
W_{k}
is the
k

function
[
1
]
. The principal one, lying in the strip

, is the constant we write
\varrho

\varrho
but to gather, with proofs, the analytic and algebraic structure it carries, and to mark plainly where that structure is proved and where it is only computed.

The picture has three parts. The first is complex-analytic. At
\varrho
the rectangular and log-polar descriptions of a complex number coincide, so the defining equation collapses into the self-consistent identities
\operatorname{Re}\varrho=\log|\varrho|
and
\arg\varrho=\operatorname{Im}\varrho
(
Theorem

3
), and a single real transcendental equation in

pins the constant down (
Corollary

2
). As a dynamical point
\varrho
is repelling for
\exp
and attracting for
\log
, with a Koenigs coordinate linearizing both at once (
Theorems

6
and
7
); it also satisfies a transpose identity

which, among principal-branch bases, holds only at
\varrho
and its conjugate (
Theorem

4

The second part is arithmetic and dynamical: an aperiodic log-polar lattice generated by
\varrho
and
\bar{\varrho}
(
Theorem

8
), the near-equality
\operatorname{Re}(\varrho)\,\pi=0.99944\ldots
whose small defect we show to be forced (
Theorem

9
), and a discrete family of solutions

to
\exp(z)-\log(z)=2\pi in

Theorem

10
).

The third part is, to our knowledge, new. Solving
\exp(z)=z
in the octonions rather than

by a round six-sphere of fixed points,

Definition and Existence

Definition 1
.

Let
\varrho
be the unique solution of

\exp(\varrho)=\varrho

in the strip

\varrho=-W_{-1}(-1)

Theorem 1
.

The equation
\exp(z)=z
has exactly one solution in the strip

\varrho=0.31813150520473746\ldots+1.3372357014306598\ldots\,i

|\varrho|=1.374557010743673\ldots

\arg(\varrho)=1.3372357014306598\ldots=\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)

Multiplying

[

\displaystyle=a,

(1)

\displaystyle e^{a}\sin b

\displaystyle=b.

Dividing (

Newton’s method converges to

∎

Remark
.

The equation
\exp(z)=z

3
Nonexistence on the Real Line

Theorem 2
.

The equation
\exp(x)=x
has no solution
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Define
f(x)=e^{x}-x
. Then
f^{\prime}(x)=e^{x}-1
and
f^{\prime\prime}(x)=e^{x}>0
for all
x
. The unique critical point is at
x=0
, where
f(0)=1
and
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=1>0
, so
x=0
is a global minimum. Hence
e^{x}-x\geq 1>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, with equality only at
x=0
. No real
x
satisfies
e^{x}=x
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

\varrho\notin\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

2
,
e^{x}-x\geq 1>0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, so no real number is a fixed point of
\exp
.
∎

Remark
.

The obstruction is order-theoretic. The inequality
e^{x}\geq 1+x>x
is a first-order property of the real exponential field
[
5
]
, so it persists in every model of its theory, in particular every real-closed exponential field: wherever the power series respects an order,
\exp
overshoots the identity and fixes nothing. The complex numbers escape on both counts. They carry no compatible order, and
\exp
is
2\pi i
-periodic, so in the imaginary direction its graph wraps around and meets the diagonal at
\varrho
, a turn the monotone real exponential cannot make.

The following identities hold for

\displaystyle=\log|\varrho|,

\displaystyle\arg(\varrho)

\displaystyle=b,

\displaystyle|\varrho|

The imaginary part

5
The
\varrho
-Transpose

Definition 2
.

For a fixed
z\in\mathbb{C}
, write
\mathbb{C}^{+}:=\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
for the slit plane on which the principal complex power is single-valued, and define the map
T_{z}\colon\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
by

T_{z}(w)=w^{z},

where the principal branch of the complex power is used.

Theorem 4
.

For
\varrho
satisfying
\exp(\varrho)=\varrho
and any
w\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
,

w^{\varrho}=\varrho^{\log w},

where
\log
denotes the principal branch. Conversely, if
w^{z}=z^{\log w}
holds for all
w\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
, then
z=\log z
, equivalently
\exp(z)=z
; on the principal branch the only such
z
are
\varrho
and its conjugate
\bar{\varrho}
, the two fixed points of
\exp
in the strip
|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle w^{\varrho}

\displaystyle=\exp(\varrho\log w),

\displaystyle\varrho^{\log w}

\displaystyle=\exp((\log w)(\log\varrho))=\exp((\log w)\varrho),

since
\log\varrho=\varrho
(from
\exp(\varrho)=\varrho
, taking the principal logarithm). The two expressions are equal. Conversely,
w^{z}=z^{\log w}
for all
w\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
forces
z=\log z
(since
\log w
ranges over a set with an accumulation point), which is equivalent to
\exp(z)=z
; on the principal branch the only solutions are
\varrho
and
\bar{\varrho}
by
Theorem

1
.
∎

Properties
.

Whenever
\arg a+\arg b\in(-\pi,\pi]
, so that
\log(ab)=\log a+\log b
and no branch cut is crossed, the
\varrho
-transpose is multiplicative,

\varrho^{\log(ab)}=(ab)^{\varrho}=a^{\varrho}b^{\varrho}=\varrho^{\log a}\,\varrho^{\log b},

verified to
10^{-16}
. It is not an involution: composing principal-branch powers gives

only up to a branch factor

, and

in general. Its fixed points satisfy

, namely

(with

).

The exponents

trace a logarithmic spiral: each step multiplies by
\varrho
, scaling the modulus by
|\varrho|
and rotating by
\arg(\varrho)=76.618^{\circ}
, so the iterated powers

z,\;z^{\varrho},\;z^{\varrho^{2}},\;z^{\varrho^{3}},\;\ldots,\qquad z^{\varrho^{k}}=\exp(\varrho^{k}\log z),

wind outward around the origin (
Fig.

,

The Hyperoperation Ladder

Definition 3
.

The hyperoperation sequence
\{H_{n}\}

[
4
]
is defined recursively:

•

H_{1}(a,b)=a+b
(addition),

•

H_{2}(a,b)=a\times b
(multiplication),

•

H_{3}(a,b)=a^{b}
(exponentiation),

•

H_{n+1}(a,b)=H_{n}(a,H_{n+1}(a,b-1))

, with

A

is a value

H_{n}(x,x)=x
.

Theorem 5
.

The diagonal rest states
H_{n}(x,x)=x
are trivial at every level of the ladder:
x=0
for addition,
x\in\{0,1\}
for multiplication, and
x=1
for exponentiation. The constant
\varrho
is

such a rest state; it enters one level higher, as the fixed point of base-

Proof.

For addition

; for multiplication
x^{2}=x\implies x\in\{0,1\}
; for exponentiation
x^{x}=x
gives, on

,
(x-1)\log x=0
, hence
x=1
. (Directly,
\varrho
fails
x^{x}=x
:
\varrho^{\varrho-1}=e^{\varrho^{2}-\varrho}\neq 1
.) For the infinite power tower

{}^{\infty}x=x^{x^{x^{\cdot^{\cdot^{\cdot}}}}},

its limit

satisfies

, i.e.

; at

this is
\log y=y
, equivalently

, whose solution is
\varrho
. Since
\varrho
is a

fixed point of
\exp
(
Theorem

6
,
|\varrho|>1
), the real

\varrho
is the value its limiting equation singles out, approached only under the inverse iteration

.
∎

Remark
.

The trivial rest states

and

belong to the additive and multiplicative levels; the first transcendental self-referential value,

, appears only once iterated exponentiation enters. The jump from

\varrho

The Exponential–Logarithm Duality

Theorem 6
.

\varrho
is simultaneously an unstable fixed point of
\exp
and a stable fixed point of
\log
:

\displaystyle|\exp^{\prime}(\varrho)|

\displaystyle=|\varrho|=1.374557\ldots>1,

\displaystyle|\log^{\prime}(\varrho)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\varrho|}=0.727507\ldots<1.

Thus perturbations of
\varrho
under iteration of
\exp
spiral outward, while perturbations under iteration of
\log
spiral inward.

Proof.

\exp^{\prime}(z)=\exp(z)
, so
\exp^{\prime}(\varrho)=\varrho
with modulus
|\varrho|>1
.
\log^{\prime}(z)=1/z
, so
\log^{\prime}(\varrho)=1/\varrho
with modulus
1/|\varrho|<1
.
∎

Corollary 3
.

Since
\varrho
is attracting for
\log
, the iterated logarithm satisfies
\log^{n}(z)\to\varrho
for every
z
in a neighborhood of
\varrho
; numerically the basin extends across most of the upper half-plane. The iterated exponential
\exp^{n}(z)
diverges from
\varrho
unless
z=\varrho
.

Theorem 7

(Koenigs Linearization)
.

In a neighborhood of
\varrho
,
\exp
is analytically conjugate to the linear map
w\mapsto\varrho w
. Explicitly, there exists a Koenigs coordinate

\varphi(\exp(z))=\varrho\cdot\varphi(z)

\varrho

\varphi^{\prime}(\varrho)=1

\varphi^{-1}

\exp(z)=\varphi^{-1}(\varrho\cdot\varphi(z)).

Proof.

This is a standard application of the Koenigs theorem
[
2

6
]
for analytic functions with a repelling fixed point:
\exp^{\prime}(\varrho)=\varrho

∎

Corollary 4
.

In Koenigs coordinates,
\log
is conjugate to

.

The product

The Quantized Spectrum

Theorem 10
.

For each integer

∎

Remark
.

The quantization unit is

\mathbb{C}
:

\exp_{\mathbb{O}}(X)=e^{s}\left(\cos r+\frac{\sin r}{r}v\right).

Setting
\exp_{\mathbb{O}}(X)=X
and equating scalar and imaginary parts gives

\displaystyle e^{s}\cos r

\displaystyle=s,

\displaystyle e^{s}\frac{\sin r}{r}v

\displaystyle=v.

For
v\neq 0
the imaginary equation forces
e^{s}\sin r=r
, which is (
2
), and the scalar equation
e^{s}\cos r=s
is (
1
). By
Theorem

1
the unique solution with
r\in(0,\pi)
is
s=\operatorname{Re}(\varrho)
,
r=\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)
, while
U=v/r
is free on
S^{6}
. The fixed points of radius
r\in(0,\pi)
are therefore exactly
\operatorname{Re}(\varrho)\cdot 1+\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)\,U
.
∎

Corollary 5
.

Every point of this sphere has modulus
|\varrho|
, a value invariant under the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
automorphism group of the octonions.

Corollary 6
.

For fixed points
\varrho_{U}=\operatorname{Re}(\varrho)\cdot 1+\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)\,U
with
U\in S^{6}
, the associator obeys

[\varrho_{U},\varrho_{V},\varrho_{W}]=\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)^{3}\,[U,V,W].

In particular
|[\varrho_{U},\varrho_{V},\varrho_{W}]|=\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)^{3}\,|[U,V,W]|
, which equals
2\,\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)^{3}
when
U,V,W
are pairwise orthogonal and generic.

Proof.

The associator
[X,Y,Z]=(XY)Z-X(YZ)
is trilinear, and the unit
1
associates with every pair, so
[1,Y,Z]=[X,1,Z]=[X,Y,1]=0
. Expanding each
\varrho_{U}=\operatorname{Re}(\varrho)\cdot 1+\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)\,U
by trilinearity, every term carrying a factor of
1
drops out, leaving
[\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)\,U,\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)\,V,\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)\,W]=\operatorname{Im}(\varrho)^{3}\,[U,V,W]
. For pairwise orthogonal generic pure-imaginary units the associator attains its maximal magnitude
|[U,V,W]|=2
.
∎

12
Spherical Trigonometry on the Fixed-Point Sphere

The sphere
S^{6}
of
Theorem

11
carries geometry of its own. A triple of unit imaginary octonions (three points of
S^{6}
) holds more invariant structure than a spherical triangle on
S^{2}
, and the surplus is governed by an exact identity.

Definition 5
.

For unit imaginary octonions
a,b,c
, let
I_{1}=\langle b,c\rangle
,
I_{2}=\langle a,c\rangle
,
I_{3}=\langle a,b\rangle
be the pairwise inner products (cosines of the geodesic side lengths), let
I_{4}=\langle a\times b,\,c\rangle
be the scalar
3
-form, and let
I_{5}=|[a,b,c]|
be the associator magnitude. Here
a\times b=\operatorname{Im}(ab)
is the seven-dimensional cross product, and
\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}:=\operatorname{span}\{a,b,a\times b\}
is the imaginary part of the quaternion subalgebra generated by
a
and
b
.

Lemma 1
.

On
\mathbb{O}
the associator
[x,y,z]
is alternating: it changes sign under any transposition of its arguments, and so vanishes whenever two of them coincide or all three lie in a common quaternion subalgebra.

Proof.

The octonions are alternative
[
3
,
7
]
, so
[x,x,y]=[x,y,y]=0
; a trilinear form vanishing on equal arguments is alternating. By Artin’s theorem any two elements generate an associative subalgebra, and a quaternion subalgebra is associative, so the associator vanishes when its three arguments lie in one.
∎

Lemma 2

(The order-gate identity)
.

For imaginary octonions
a,b
, the linear map
A_{a,b}\colon\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{O}\to\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{O}
given by
A_{a,b}(x)=[a,b,x]
vanishes on
\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}
and satisfies

A_{a,b}^{2}=-4\,|a\times b|^{2}\,P_{a,b},

where
P_{a,b}
is the orthogonal projection onto the four-dimensional complement
\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}^{\perp}
. In particular
|[a,b,x]|=2\,|a\times b|\,|x|
for every
x\perp\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

1
,
A_{a,b}
vanishes on
\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}
. For its action on the complement we may take
a,b
orthonormal: writing
b=\langle b,\hat{a}\rangle\,\hat{a}+b^{\prime}
with
\hat{a}=a/|a|
and
b^{\prime}\perp a
,
Lemma

1
gives
A_{a,b}=A_{a,b^{\prime}}
, and bilinearity gives
A_{a,b}=|a|\,|b^{\prime}|\,A_{\hat{a},\hat{b}^{\prime}}=|a\times b|\,A_{\hat{a},\hat{b}^{\prime}}
with
\hat{a},\hat{b}^{\prime}
orthonormal and
\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}=\mathcal{Q}_{\hat{a},\hat{b}^{\prime}}
. It therefore suffices to prove
A_{u,v}^{2}=-4P_{u,v}
for an orthonormal pair
u,v
of imaginary units. The automorphism group
G_{2}=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{O})
acts transitively on such ordered pairs
[
3
]
and preserves the product, the inner product, the cross product, and hence
\mathcal{Q}
and
P
; so we may take
u=e_{1}
,
v=e_{2}
in the standard basis. There
e_{1}\times e_{2}=e_{4}
, whence
\mathcal{Q}_{e_{1},e_{2}}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{4}\}
, and on the four complementary units the multiplication table gives

A(e_{3})=-2e_{6},\quad A(e_{6})=2e_{3},\qquad A(e_{5})=2e_{7},\quad A(e_{7})=-2e_{5}.

So
A
preserves the orthogonal planes
\operatorname{span}\{e_{3},e_{6}\}
and
\operatorname{span}\{e_{5},e_{7}\}
, acting on each as
2J
for an orthogonal
J
with
J^{2}=-I
. Hence
A^{2}=-4I
on
\mathcal{Q}_{e_{1},e_{2}}^{\perp}
and
A^{2}=0
on
\mathcal{Q}_{e_{1},e_{2}}
, that is
A^{2}=-4P
; and since each
J
preserves length,
|[a,b,x]|=2|a\times b|\,|x|
for every
x\perp\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}
.
∎

Lemma 3

(Associator as escaping volume)
.

I_{5}=2\,|a\times b|\,\operatorname{dist}\!\big(c,\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}\big)
. The associator magnitude is twice the volume by which
c
escapes the associative world of
a
and
b
, and it vanishes exactly when
a,b,c
share a quaternion subalgebra.

Proof.

Split
c=c_{\parallel}+c_{\perp}
with
c_{\parallel}\in\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}
and
c_{\perp}\perp\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}
. By
Lemma

2
,
[a,b,c]=A_{a,b}(c)=A_{a,b}(c_{\perp})
, so
I_{5}=|A_{a,b}(c_{\perp})|=2|a\times b|\,|c_{\perp}|=2|a\times b|\,\operatorname{dist}(c,\mathcal{Q}_{a,b})
, which vanishes exactly when
c\in\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}
.
∎

Theorem 12

(Oct-Pythagorean identity)
.

For unit imaginary octonions
a,b,c
,

I_{4}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}I_{5}^{2}=\operatorname{Gram}(a,b,c)=1-I_{1}^{2}-I_{2}^{2}-I_{3}^{2}+2I_{1}I_{2}I_{3}.

Proof.

The Gram determinant equals the squared volume of the parallelepiped spanned by
a,b,c
, so
\operatorname{Gram}=|a\times b|^{2}\,\operatorname{dist}(c,\operatorname{span}\{a,b\})^{2}
. The component of
c
orthogonal to
\operatorname{span}\{a,b\}
splits, by Pythagoras, into its part along the unit vector
a\times b/|a\times b|
, of length
I_{4}/|a\times b|
, and its part escaping the three-space
\mathcal{Q}_{a,b}
, of length
\operatorname{dist}(c,\mathcal{Q}_{a,b})
:

\operatorname{dist}(c,\operatorname{span}\{a,b\})^{2}=\Big(\frac{I_{4}}{|a\times b|}\Big)^{2}+\operatorname{dist}(c,\mathcal{Q}_{a,b})^{2}.

Multiplying through by
|a\times b|^{2}
and applying
Lemma

3
,

\operatorname{Gram}=I_{4}^{2}+|a\times b|^{2}\,\operatorname{dist}(c,\mathcal{Q}_{a,b})^{2}=I_{4}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}I_{5}^{2}.

The closed form of the Gram determinant for unit vectors is standard.
∎

Corollary 7

(The twist angle)
.

Setting
I_{4}=\sqrt{\operatorname{Gram}}\,\cos\psi
and
I_{5}=2\sqrt{\operatorname{Gram}}\,\sin\psi
defines a single angle
\psi\in[0,\pi]
. By
Theorem

12
the five invariants satisfy exactly this one relation, and the Jacobian of
(a,b,c)\mapsto(I_{1},\dots,I_{5})
has rank
4
at a generic triple, so exactly four of them are functionally independent: the three side lengths and the twist
\psi
. These four are complete: up to a
G_{2}
automorphism, a triple is determined by
(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},\psi)
, the third point being recovered from its projections onto
a
,
b
,
a\times b
and an escaping direction of length
\tfrac{1}{2}I_{5}
. A spherical triangle on
S^{6}
thus carries exactly one invariant beyond its
S^{2}
counterpart, the twist
\psi
, with no analogue in ordinary spherical trigonometry; its extremes
\psi=0
and
\psi=\pi/2
are the associative and maximally non-associative configurations.

Corollary 8

(Concentric fixed-point shells)
.

Theorem

11
applies on every branch of the logarithm: the octonionic fixed-point set of
\exp_{\mathbb{O}}
is a disjoint union of concentric spheres
\operatorname{Re}(\varrho_{n})+\operatorname{Im}(\varrho_{n})\,S^{6}
, one for each solution
\varrho_{n}
of
\exp(z)=z
on branch
n
. Their imaginary radii grow as
\operatorname{Im}(\varrho_{n})\sim 2\pi n
, spacing the shells by the exponential period
2\pi
, the same scale that fixes the quantized spectrum of
Theorem

10
.

Remark
.

The order-gate identity (
Lemma

2
) and the oct-Pythagorean identity (
Theorem

12
) admit exact verification. The action of
A_{e_{1},e_{2}}
above is an exact integer computation in the multiplication table; the homogeneous form
4I_{4}^{2}+I_{5}^{2}=4\operatorname{Gram}
holds with no error across
2\times 10^{5}
random integer-valued imaginary triples, certifying it as a polynomial identity; and the rank of the invariant Jacobian was measured to be
4
, as the twist-angle count requires.

Open Questions

The following questions arise from the properties above and are left for future investigation:

1.

.
