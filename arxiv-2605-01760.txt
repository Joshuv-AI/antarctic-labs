Two-place Laplacian matching root integral variations are impossible Thanks: Corresponding author. Yi Wang Thanks: Funding. Supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (No. 12331012, 12571360, 12471320)
Sebastian M. Cioabă, LeLe Liu, Yi Wang

Abstract
. Wang, Cui, and Cioabă introduced the Laplacian matching root integral variation of a graph and proved that it cannot occur in one place. They also showed that the two-place variation is impossible for connected graphs satisfying g ⁡ ( G ) / c ⁡ ( G ) > 7 / 6 g(G)/c(G)>7/6 , where g ⁡ ( G ) g(G) is the girth and c ⁡ ( G ) c(G) is the dimension of the cycle space, and conjectured that no connected graph admits such a two-place variation. In this paper, we confirm this conjecture. The proof combines a structural relation obtained in their paper with two new power-sum identities for Laplacian matching roots.

1.
Introduction

There are many graph polynomials whose roots encode structural information of the underlying graph such as the characteristic polynomial, the chromatic polynomial, the matching polynomial, and the Tutte polynomial (see
[
1
]
).

In this paper, we continue the study of a root integral variation problem for the Laplacian matching polynomial.

Throughout the paper, graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. Let
G
be a graph with
vertex set
V(G)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
and edge set
E(G)
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
,
we denote by
N_{G}(v)
the set of neighbors of
v
, and by
d_{G}(v)
its degree.
If
e
is a non-edge of
G
, then
G+e
denotes the graph obtained from
G
by adding
e
.

For a graph
G
of order
n
, the matching polynomial of
G
is defined by

(1.1)

\mathscr{M}_{G}(x)=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}(G)}(-1)^{|M|}x^{|V(G)\setminus V(M)|}=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\phi_{j}(G)x^{n-2j},

where
\mathcal{M}(G)
is the set of matchings of
G
and
\phi_{j}(G)
is the number of

j
-matchings. The matching polynomial was introduced by Heilmann and Lieb
[
4
]
.
We refer to Godsil
[
2
]
for further background on matching polynomials.

Motivated by the relation between the adjacency characteristic polynomial and the
matching polynomial established by Godsil and Gutman
[
3
]
,
Mohammadian
[
6
]
introduced the
Laplacian matching polynomial

(1.2)

\mathscr{LM}_{G}(x)=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}(G)}(-1)^{|M|}\prod_{v\in V(G)\setminus V(M)}(x-d_{G}(v)).

Independently, Zhang and Chen
[
10
]
studied the same polynomial under the
name
average Laplacian polynomial
. Mohammadian
[
6
]
proved
that all roots of
\mathscr{LM}_{G}(x)
are real and nonnegative. We write them in non-increasing
order as

\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(G)\geq 0.

Wan, Wang, and Mohammadian
[
9
]
proved that these roots satisfy an interlacing
relation under edge addition: if
e
is a non-edge of
G
, then

(1.3)

\lambda_{1}(G+e)\geq\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{2}(G+e)\geq\lambda_{2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(G+e)\geq\lambda_{n}(G).

Moreover, by Vieta’s formulas applied to (
1.2
),

(1.4)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(G)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_{G}(v)=2|E(G)|.

Hence, whenever a non-edge is added, the sum of Laplacian matching roots increases by exactly
2
.

Following Wang, Cui, and Cioabă
[
8
]
, one says that
Laplacian matching root
integral variation
(LMRIV) occurs in one place if exactly one root increases by
2
, and in
two places if exactly two roots increase by
1
while all the remaining roots stay unchanged.
In
[
8
]
, it was proved that one-place LMRIV is impossible for connected graphs,
and that two-place LMRIV is impossible whenever
g(G)/c(G)>7/6
, where
g(G)
is the girth
and
c(G)
is the dimension of the cycle space. The paper concludes with the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
8
, Conjecture 3.11]
)
.

If
G
is a connected graph and
e
is a non-edge of
G
, then two-place LMRIV does not occur when
e
is added.

The purpose of the present paper is to settle this conjecture affirmatively.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and let
e
be a non-edge of
G
. Then two-place LMRIV cannot
occur when
e
is added. Equivalently, if
G^{\prime}=G+e
, then it is impossible that exactly two
Laplacian matching roots of
G
increase by
1
and all the others remain unchanged.

Our proof is short. Besides one key structural lemma from
[
8
]
, it uses two power-sum
identities for Laplacian matching roots that seem to be of independent interest.

The motivation for studying root integral variation comes in part from analogous questions for Laplacian eigenvalues, see So
[
7
]
and Kirkland
[
5
]
for example.

2.
Two power-sum identities

This section aims to establish two power-sum identities, which will play a key role in the sequel. For a graph
G
and
r\geq 1
, define

p_{r}(G):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(G)^{r}
. We also write

A_{r}(G):=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_{G}(v)^{r},\qquad B(G):=\sum_{xy\in E(G)}d_{G}(x)d_{G}(y),

C(G):=\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\bigl(d_{G}(x)^{2}d_{G}(y)+d_{G}(x)d_{G}(y)^{2}\bigr).

Henceforth, whenever there is no risk of confusion, we will suppress the argument
G
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then

p_{4}(G)=A_{4}+4A_{3}+2A_{2}+4B-2|E(G)|,

and

p_{5}(G)=A_{5}+5A_{4}+5A_{3}-5A_{2}+5C+10B.

Proof.

Write
\mathscr{LM}_{G}(x)=x^{n}-b_{1}x^{n-1}+b_{2}x^{n-2}-b_{3}x^{n-3}+b_{4}x^{n-4}-b_{5}x^{n-5}+\cdots+(-1)^{n}b_{n}
.

Denote by
E_{r}
the
r
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in the degree sequence

\{d(v):v\in V(G)\}
. By expanding (
1.2
), one sees that only matchings of
size at most
2
contribute to
b_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq 5
. A direct coefficient computation yields

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=E_{1},

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=E_{2}-|E(G)|,

\displaystyle b_{3}

\displaystyle=E_{3}-\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\bigl(E_{1}-d(x)-d(y)\bigr),

\displaystyle b_{4}

\displaystyle=E_{4}-\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\bigl(E_{2}-E_{1}(d(x)+d(y))+d(x)^{2}+d(y)^{2}+d(x)d(y)\bigr)+\phi_{2}(G),

\displaystyle b_{5}

\displaystyle=E_{5}-\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\!\bigl(E_{3}-E_{2}(d(x)+d(y))+E_{1}\bigl(d(x)^{2}+d(x)d(y)+d(y)^{2}\bigr)\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\!\bigl(d(x)^{3}+d(x)^{2}d(y)+d(x)d(y)^{2}+d(y)^{3}\bigr)+\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{2}(G)}\!\!\bigg(E_{1}-\sum_{z\in V(M)}d(z)\bigg),

where
\mathcal{M}_{2}(G)
is the set of
2
-matchings of
G
.

Now, by the definition of
A_{r}
, we have

A_{2}=\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\!\bigl(d(x)+d(y)\bigr),~~~A_{3}=\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\!\bigl(d(x)^{2}+d(y)^{2}\bigr),~~~A_{4}=\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\!\bigl(d(x)^{3}+d(y)^{3}\bigr),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{2}(G)}\sum_{z\in V(M)}d(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\bigl(d(x)+d(y)\bigr)\bigl(|E(G)|-d(x)-d(y)+1\bigr)

\displaystyle=(|E(G)|+1)A_{2}-A_{3}-2B.

Set
m:=|E(G)|
. Using the identities above, together with

\phi_{2}(G)=\binom{m}{2}-\sum_{v\in V(G)}\binom{d(v)}{2}=\frac{m^{2}+m-A_{2}}{2},

the coefficient formulas become

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=E_{1},

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=E_{2}-m,

\displaystyle b_{3}

\displaystyle=E_{3}-mE_{1}+A_{2},

\displaystyle b_{4}

\displaystyle=E_{4}-mE_{2}+E_{1}A_{2}-A_{3}-B+\phi_{2}(G),

\displaystyle b_{5}

\displaystyle=E_{5}-mE_{3}+E_{2}A_{2}-E_{1}(A_{3}+B)+A_{4}+C

\displaystyle\quad+E_{1}\phi_{2}(G)-(m+1)A_{2}+A_{3}+2B.

Now let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
be the roots of
\mathscr{LM}_{G}(x)
.
Newton’s identities imply that

\displaystyle p_{1}

\displaystyle=b_{1},

\displaystyle p_{2}

\displaystyle=b_{1}^{2}-2b_{2},

\displaystyle p_{3}

\displaystyle=b_{1}^{3}-3b_{1}b_{2}+3b_{3},

\displaystyle p_{4}

\displaystyle=b_{1}^{4}-4b_{1}^{2}b_{2}+2b_{2}^{2}+4b_{1}b_{3}-4b_{4},

\displaystyle p_{5}

\displaystyle=b_{1}^{5}-5b_{1}^{3}b_{2}+5b_{1}b_{2}^{2}+5b_{1}^{2}b_{3}-5b_{2}b_{3}-5b_{1}b_{4}+5b_{5}.

Substituting the expressions for
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4}
into the formula for
p_{4}
, we get that

\displaystyle p_{4}

\displaystyle=E_{1}^{4}-4E_{1}^{2}(E_{2}-m)+2(E_{2}-m)^{2}+4E_{1}(E_{3}-mE_{1}+A_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-4\bigl(E_{4}-mE_{2}+E_{1}A_{2}-A_{3}-B+\phi_{2}(G)\bigr)

\displaystyle=E_{1}^{4}-4E_{1}^{2}E_{2}+2E_{2}^{2}+4E_{1}E_{3}-4E_{4}+4A_{3}+4B+2m^{2}-4\phi_{2}(G).

We now apply Newton’s identities to the degree sequence

d(v_{1}),\dots,d(v_{n})
, whose elementary symmetric polynomials are precisely

E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{n}
, and we get that

A_{4}=E_{1}^{4}-4E_{1}^{2}E_{2}+2E_{2}^{2}+4E_{1}E_{3}-4E_{4}

and

p_{4}=A_{4}+4A_{3}+4B+2m^{2}-4\phi_{2}(G)=A_{4}+4A_{3}+2A_{2}+4B-2m.

Similarly, substituting the expressions for
b_{1},\dots,b_{5}
into the formula for
p_{5}
gives

\displaystyle p_{5}

\displaystyle=E_{1}^{5}-5E_{1}^{3}(E_{2}-m)+5E_{1}(E_{2}-m)^{2}+5E_{1}^{2}(E_{3}-mE_{1}+A_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-5(E_{2}-m)(E_{3}-mE_{1}+A_{2})-5E_{1}\bigl(E_{4}-mE_{2}+E_{1}A_{2}-A_{3}-B+\phi_{2}(G)\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+5\bigl(E_{5}-mE_{3}+E_{2}A_{2}-E_{1}(A_{3}+B)+A_{4}+C

\displaystyle\quad+E_{1}\phi_{2}(G)-(m+1)A_{2}+A_{3}+2B\bigr)

\displaystyle=E_{1}^{5}-5E_{1}^{3}E_{2}+5E_{1}E_{2}^{2}+5E_{1}^{2}E_{3}-5E_{2}E_{3}-5E_{1}E_{4}+5E_{5}

\displaystyle\quad+5A_{4}+5A_{3}-5A_{2}+10B+5C.

Applying Newton’s identities to the degree sequence again, we have

A_{5}=E_{1}^{5}-5E_{1}^{3}E_{2}+5E_{1}E_{2}^{2}+5E_{1}^{2}E_{3}-5E_{2}E_{3}-5E_{1}E_{4}+5E_{5}.

Therefore,
p_{5}=A_{5}+5A_{4}+5A_{3}-5A_{2}+10B+5C
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The next lemma is the key input from
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Assume that two-place LMRIV occurs to a connected graph
G
by adding a non-edge
uv
. If
a=d_{G}(u),b=d_{G}(v)
,
and the changed Laplacian matching roots of
G
are
\lambda_{1}(G)
and
\lambda_{k}(G)
, then

(2.1)

\lambda_{1}(G)+\lambda_{k}(G)=a+b+1,\text{ and }\lambda_{1}(G)\lambda_{k}(G)=ab.

3.
Proof of the conjecture

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume, to the contrary, that two-place LMRIV occurs to a connected graph
G
when the non-edge
uv
is added. Set
G^{\prime}:=G+uv
,
a:=d_{G}(u)
, and
b:=d_{G}(v)
.
Since
G
is connected and
uv\notin E(G)
, we have
a,b\geq 1
. Let the changed roots of
G
be
\lambda_{1}:=\lambda_{1}(G)
,
\lambda_{k}:=\lambda_{k}(G)
, so that

\lambda_{1}(G^{\prime})=\lambda_{1}+1
,
\lambda_{k}(G^{\prime})=\lambda_{k}+1
,
while all the other Laplacian matching roots remain unchanged.

For a vertex
w\in V(G)
, define

S_{w}:=\sum_{x\in N_{G}(w)}d_{G}(x),\quad T_{w}:=\sum_{x\in N_{G}(w)}d_{G}(x)^{2}.

We need the following two claims.

Claim 1
.

S_{u}+S_{v}=2ab+a+b
.

Proof.

For convenience, for
r\geq 1
, write

\Delta A_{r}:=A_{r}(G^{\prime})-A_{r}(G),\qquad\Delta B:=B(G^{\prime})-B(G),\qquad\Delta C:=C(G^{\prime})-C(G).

Since only the degrees of
u
and
v
change when the edge
uv
is added, we see

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Delta A_{r}

\displaystyle=(a+1)^{r}-a^{r}+(b+1)^{r}-b^{r}\quad(r\geq 1)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Delta B

\displaystyle=S_{u}+S_{v}+(a+1)(b+1).

Using Proposition
2.1
, we obtain

p_{4}(G^{\prime})-p_{4}(G)=\Delta A_{4}+4\Delta A_{3}+2\Delta A_{2}+4\Delta B-2.

Substituting (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) into the above equation, and then expanding, gives

\displaystyle p_{4}(G^{\prime})-p_{4}(G)

\displaystyle=4(S_{u}+S_{v})+4a^{3}+18a^{2}+4ab+24a

(3.3)

\displaystyle\quad+4b^{3}+18b^{2}+24b+16.

On the other hand, only two roots change, so

(3.4)

p_{4}(G^{\prime})-p_{4}(G)=(\lambda_{1}+1)^{4}-\lambda_{1}^{4}+(\lambda_{k}+1)^{4}-\lambda_{k}^{4}=4(\lambda_{1}^{3}+\lambda_{k}^{3})+6(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{2})+4(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k})+2.

By Lemma
2.2
, we deduce that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=a+b+1,\qquad\lambda_{1}\lambda_{k}=ab,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k})^{2}-2\lambda_{1}\lambda_{k}=a^{2}+b^{2}+2a+2b+1,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{3}+\lambda_{k}^{3}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k})^{3}-3\lambda_{1}\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle=a^{3}+b^{3}+3a^{2}+3b^{2}+3ab+3a+3b+1.

Substituting (
3.5
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
) into (
3.4
), we get

p_{4}(G^{\prime})-p_{4}(G)=4a^{3}+18a^{2}+12ab+28a+4b^{3}+18b^{2}+28b+16.

Comparing with (
3.3
), we obtain
S_{u}+S_{v}=2ab+a+b
.
∎

Claim 2
.

(2a{+}3)S_{u}{+}(2b{+}3)S_{v}{+}T_{u}{+}T_{v}=3a^{2}b{+}3ab^{2}{+}8ab{+}2a^{2}{+}2b^{2}{+}4a{+}4b
.

Proof.

For
\Delta C
, note that for each edge
ux
with
x\in N_{G}(u)
, the quantity

d_{G}(u)^{2}d_{G}(x)+d_{G}(u)d_{G}(x)^{2}
increases by

(a+1)^{2}d_{G}(x)+(a+1)d_{G}(x)^{2}-a^{2}d_{G}(x)-ad_{G}(x)^{2}=(2a+1)d_{G}(x)+d_{G}(x)^{2}.

Summing over all
x\in N_{G}(u)
gives the contribution
(2a+1)S_{u}+T_{u}
.
Similarly, the edges incident with
v
contribute
(2b+1)S_{v}+T_{v}
.
Finally, the new edge
uv
contributes

(a+1)^{2}(b+1)+(a+1)(b+1)^{2}=(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2).

Hence,

(3.8)

\Delta C=(2a+1)S_{u}+T_{u}+(2b+1)S_{v}+T_{v}+(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2).

Using Proposition
2.1
again, we have

p_{5}(G^{\prime})-p_{5}(G)=\Delta A_{5}+5\Delta A_{4}+5\Delta A_{3}-5\Delta A_{2}+5\Delta C+10\Delta B.

Substituting (
3.1
), (
3.2
), and (
3.8
)
into the above equation and simplifying, we obtain

(3.9)

\begin{split}p_{5}(G^{\prime})-p_{5}(G)&=10aS_{u}+15S_{u}+10bS_{v}+15S_{v}+5T_{u}+5T_{v}\\
&\quad+5a^{4}+30a^{3}+5a^{2}b+60a^{2}+5ab^{2}+30ab+55a\\
&\quad+5b^{4}+30b^{3}+60b^{2}+55b+32.\end{split}

Since only
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{k}
change,

\displaystyle p_{5}(G^{\prime})-p_{5}(G)

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}+1)^{5}-\lambda_{1}^{5}+(\lambda_{k}+1)^{5}-\lambda_{k}^{5}

\displaystyle=5(\lambda_{1}^{4}+\lambda_{k}^{4})+10(\lambda_{1}^{3}+\lambda_{k}^{3})+10(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{2})+5(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{k})+2.

Using (
3.5
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
) once more, together with

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{4}+\lambda_{k}^{4}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{2}\bigr)^{2}-2(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle=a^{4}+b^{4}+4a^{3}+4b^{3}+4a^{2}b+4ab^{2}+6a^{2}+6b^{2}+8ab+4a+4b+1,

we obtain

\displaystyle p_{5}(G^{\prime})-p_{5}(G)

\displaystyle=5a^{4}+30a^{3}+20a^{2}b+70a^{2}+20ab^{2}+70ab+75a

(3.10)

\displaystyle\quad+5b^{4}+30b^{3}+70b^{2}+75b+32.

Comparing (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we get the desired result.
∎

Finally, we will get a contradiction via Claim
1
and Claim
2
. To this end, by Cauchy’s inequality, we have

T_{u}\geq S_{u}^{2}/a
and
T_{v}\geq S_{v}^{2}/b
.
Set for short,

R:=3a^{2}b+3ab^{2}+8ab+2a^{2}+2b^{2}+4a+4b.

By Claim
1
, we may write
S_{u}=x
,
S_{v}=2ab+a+b-x

for some real number
x
. Then Claim
2
implies

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\geq\frac{x^{2}}{a}+\frac{(2ab+a+b-x)^{2}}{b}+(2a+3)x+(2b+3)(2ab+a+b-x)

\displaystyle=R+2ab+\frac{a+b}{ab}\bigl(x-a(b+1)\bigr)^{2}.

Since
a,b\geq 1
, the right-hand side is strictly larger than
R
, a contradiction.
This contradiction shows that two-place LMRIV cannot occur. The proof is complete.
∎

Let
F\subseteq E(G^{c})
be nonempty, where
G^{c}
is the complement of
G
. Set
H=G+F
.
We say that
multi-edge Laplacian matching root integral variation
for
G
occurs when there exist integers

t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\qquad t_{1}+\cdots+t_{n}=2|F|,

such that the multiset of Laplacian matching roots of
H
is obtained from that of
G
by adding these integers, that is,

\{\lambda_{1}(H),\dots,\lambda_{n}(H)\}=\{\lambda_{1}(G)+t_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}(G)+t_{n}\}

as multisets.

When
|F|=1
, the only possible integral patterns are
(2,0)
and
(1,1)
, corresponding
to the classical one-place and two-place LMRIV considered in the paper.

We finish the paper with the conjecture below which has been verified by computer for
n\leq 9
.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then multi-edge Laplacian matching root integral variation does not occur for
G
.
