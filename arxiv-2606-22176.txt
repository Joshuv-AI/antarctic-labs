Completely contractive covariant representations of product system over ℕ 0 2 \mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
Dimple Saini

Abstract
. A pure completely contractive covariant representations of a C ∗ C^{*} -correspondence dilate to a pure isometric covariant representations due to Muhly-Solel. More specifically, we are curious about the following question for pairs: Does a pure completely contractive covariant representations of a product system dilate to a pure isometric covariant representations of a product system? The goal of this study is to find a pure completely contractive covariant representations of a product system that provide an affirmative answer for the previous question.

1.
Introduction

According to Wold’s fundamental theorem (cf.
[
28
]
), every isometric operator
V
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is unitarily equivalent to
M_{z}\oplus U,
where
U
is unitary on
\mathcal{H}_{u}:=\cap_{n\geq 0}V^{n}\mathcal{H}
, and
M_{z}
is the shift on
\mathcal{D}
-valued Hardy space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{D})
over unit disc
\mathbb{D},
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{D}.
A contraction
T
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is said to be pure if
T^{*m}\to 0
in strong operator topology (SOT), (i.e.,
\|T^{*m}h\|\rightarrow 0
as
m\rightarrow\infty
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
). Sz.-Nagy and Foias theorem
[
14
]
proved that every pure contraction
T
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is unitarily equivalent to an operator of the form
P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z}|_{\mathcal{Q}},
where
\mathcal{Q}
is a closed
M_{z}^{*}
-invariant subspace of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{D})
and
P_{\mathcal{Q}}
is an orthogonal projection of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{D})
onto
\mathcal{Q}.

Berger, Coburn, and Lebow
[
4
]
discussed the idea of a pure pair of commuting isometries, which is a significant advancement in the study of representation and Fredholm theory for
C^{*}
-algebras produced by commuting isometries. Berger, Coburn, and Lebow
[
4
]
studied the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
(V_{1},V_{2})
to be a pair of commuting isometries on
\mathcal{H},
and
V=V_{1}V_{2}
to be pure. Then there exist a unitary
U
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
and an orthogonal projection
P
on
\mathcal{E}
such that

{\Psi}(z)=U(P^{\perp}+zP)\quad and\quad{\Phi}(z)=(P+zP^{\perp})U^{*}

are inner functions in
H^{\infty}_{B(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{D})
and the triple
(M_{\Phi},M_{\Psi},M_{z})
is unitarily equivalent to
(V_{1},V_{2},V).

Das, Sarkar and Sarkar
[
6
]
obtained a similar result for pure pairs of commuting contractions in the following way:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T
be a pure contraction and
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a pair of commuting
contractions on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.
Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the Sz.-Nagy and Foias representation of
T.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

T=T_{1}T_{2};

(2)

There exist a triple
(\mathcal{E},U,P)
and a joint
(M_{z}^{*},M_{\Phi}^{*},M_{\Psi}^{*})
-invariant subspace
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
of
H_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}(\mathbb{D})

such that

T_{1}\cong P_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}}M_{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}},T_{2}\cong P_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}}M_{\Psi}|_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}},T\cong P_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}}M_{z}|_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}}\quad and\quad M_{\Phi}M_{\Psi}=M_{\Psi}M_{\Phi}=M_{z}.

(3)

There exist
B(\mathcal{D})
-valued polynomials
\phi
and
\psi
of degree
\leq 1
such that
\mathcal{Q}
is a joint

(M_{\phi}^{*},M_{\psi}^{*})
-invariant subspace,

P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{\phi\psi}|_{\mathcal{Q}}=P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{\psi\phi}|_{\mathcal{Q}}=P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z}|_{\mathcal{Q}}\quad and\quad(T_{1},T_{2})\cong(P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{\phi}|_{\mathcal{Q}},P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{\psi}|_{\mathcal{Q}}).

Covariant representations of product system are one of the most advanced theories in operator theory and operator algebras that offer a cohesive method for examining both commuting and non-commuting tuples of operators on Hilbert spaces.

For
C^{*}
-correspondences, Muhly and Solel
[
12
]
proved Wold decomposition for the isometric covariant representations. Muhly and Solel
[
11
]
introduced isometric dilation for completely contractive covariant representations of
C^{*}
-correspondences. The goal of this study is to find an isometric dilation of pure completely contractive covariant representations of product system. Our work is based on an explicit version of the isometric dilation for pure pairs of commuting contractions obtained in
[
6
,
22
]
.

Preliminaries and Notations

First, we’ll review some basic concepts from
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. Assume
\mathcal{B}
to be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
E
to be a
C^{*}
-correspondence
over
\mathcal{B}

and

Throughout this article, we will use the following notations:
E
denotes a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space,
B(\mathcal{H})
represents the collection of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H},
each

Definition 1.3
.

Assume

to be a linear map and

(\sigma,T)

is completely contractive and

T(axb)=\sigma(a)T(x)\sigma(b)\quad\quad(a,b\in\mathcal{B}~\mbox{and}~x\in E).

Due to Muhly and Solel
[
11
]
, the following result is helpful to classify the completely contractive covariant representations of a
C^{*}
-correspondence.

Lemma 1.4
.

The function
(\sigma,T)\mapsto\widetilde{T}
gives a bijection between the set of all c.c.c. representations
(\sigma,T)

and the set of all contractive linear maps
\widetilde{T}:E\otimes_{\sigma}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
defined by

\widetilde{T}(x\otimes h)=T(x)h\quad\quad(h\in\mathcal{H},x\in E),

and satisfies
\widetilde{T}(\phi(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=\sigma(a)\widetilde{T}
for all
a\in\mathcal{B}
. Further,
\widetilde{T}
is
isometry
(resp.
co-isometry, partial isometry
) if and only if
(\sigma,T)
is isometric (resp.
co-isometric, partial isometric
).

Assume
E
to be a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{B}.
Then,

E^{\otimes m}:=E\otimes_{\phi}\cdots\otimes_{\phi}E
(
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
times) is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{B}
with
E^{\otimes 0}=\mathcal{B}
and left action of
\mathcal{B}
on
E^{\otimes m}
is defined by

\phi^{m}(a)(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{m}):=ax_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{m}\quad\quad\quad(x_{i}\in E,a\in\mathcal{B}).

For
m\in\mathbb{N},
define
\widetilde{T}_{m}:E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
by

\widetilde{T}_{m}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{m}\otimes h)=T(x_{1})\cdots T(x_{m})h\quad\quad\quad(x_{i}\in E,h\in\mathcal{H}).

The Fock module,
\mathcal{F}(E):=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}E^{\otimes m},
is a
C^{*}
correspondence over
\mathcal{B},
where the left module action of
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{F}(E)
is given by

\phi_{\infty}(a)(\oplus_{m\geq 0}x_{m}):=\oplus_{m\geq 0}a\cdot x_{m},\>\>x_{m}\in E^{\otimes m}.

Let
\xi\in E,
define the
creation operator

T_{\xi}
on
\mathcal{F}(E)
by

T_{\xi}(x):=\xi\otimes x,\>\>x\in E^{\otimes m}.

Let
\pi
be a representation of
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{H}
. An isometric covariant representation
(\rho,S)
of
E
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{H}
given by

\displaystyle\rho(a):=\phi_{\infty}(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}\>\>,a\in\mathcal{B}\quad and\quad S(\xi):=T_{\xi}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}},\>\>\xi\in E.

We say that
(\rho,S)
is an induced representation of
E
(
[
17
]
) induced by
\pi
. Note that any isometric representation which is isomorphic to an induced representation is also induced.

Definition 1.5
.

A closed subspace
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is said to be
invariant
under the covariant representation
(\sigma,T)

(resp.(\sigma,T)
-
reducing
) if
\mathcal{W}
is
\sigma{(\mathcal{B})}
-invariant and (resp. both
\mathcal{W}^{\perp},\mathcal{W}

T(x)
for all
x\in E.
The restriction
(\sigma,T)|_{\mathcal{W}}
gives a new representation of
E
on
\mathcal{W}.
We say that
\mathcal{W}
is
co-invariant
if
\widetilde{T}^{*}{(\mathcal{W})}\subseteq E\otimes\mathcal{W}.

Assume
(\sigma,T)
to be a c.c.c. representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H},
that is,
I_{\mathcal{H}}-\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{*}\geq 0.
We say that
(\sigma,T)
is
pure
if
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}\to 0
in SOT. Now we define a positive operator
\Delta(T):=(I-\widetilde{T}{\widetilde{T}}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and the corresponding defect space
\mathcal{D}_{T}:=\overline{Im\Delta(T)}
. Since

\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{*}\sigma(a)=\widetilde{T}(\phi(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})\widetilde{T}^{*}=\sigma(a)\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{*}

for
a\in\mathcal{B},
we get
\Delta(T)\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})^{\prime},
and hence
\mathcal{D}_{T}
is
\sigma(\mathcal{B})
-reducing. Define
(\sigma_{0},T_{0}):=(\sigma,T)|_{\mathcal{D}_{T}},
for simplicity we write
(\sigma,T)
instead of
(\sigma_{0},T_{0})
. The Poisson kernel of
(\sigma,T)

[
11
]
is the operator
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes_{\sigma}\mathcal{D}_{T}
given by

\Pi u:=\sum_{m\geq 0}(I_{E^{\otimes m}}\otimes\Delta(T))\widetilde{T}_{m}^{*}u,\>\>\>u\in\mathcal{H}.

Now, we present an isometric dilation of c.c.c. representations of a
C^{*}
-correspondence due to Muhly and Solel
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume
(\sigma,T)
to be a c.c.c. representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H},
then the poisson kernel
\Pi
is a contraction and

(1.1)

\displaystyle(I_{E}\otimes\Pi)\widetilde{T}^{*}=\widetilde{S}^{*}\Pi\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\Pi\sigma(a)=\rho(a)\Pi,\>\>\>a\in\mathcal{B}.

Moreover,
\Pi
is an isometry if and only if
(\sigma,T)
is pure. The induced representation
(\rho,S)
of
E
induced by
\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}_{T}}
is called

of

2.
Completely contractive covariant representations over product system

A
product system

\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
consists of a collection of
C^{*}
-correspondences
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
(see
[
7
,
23
,
25
,
26
]
) along with unitary isomorphisms
t_{j,i}:E_{j}\otimes E_{i}\to E_{i}\otimes E_{j}
(
j>i
). Using this, we identify each correspondence
\mathbb{E}(m)
for
m=(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
with
E_{1}^{\otimes^{m_{1}}}\otimes E_{2}^{\otimes^{m_{2}}}.
We also set
t_{j,j}={\textrm{id}}_{E_{j}\otimes E_{j}}
and
t_{j,i}=t_{i,j}^{-1}
when
j<i
.

Definition 2.1
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}
to be a product system over
\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0},

\sigma:\mathcal{B}\to B(\mathcal{H})
to be a representation and
T^{(i)}:E_{i}\to B(\mathcal{H})
,
i\in I_{2}:=\{1,2\}
to be the linear maps. Then the tuple
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
is said to be
completely contractive covariant representation
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
if each pair
(\sigma,T^{(i)})
is a c.c.c. representation of
E_{i}
on
\mathcal{H}
and satisfies

(2.1)

\widetilde{T}^{(j)}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{(i)})=\widetilde{T}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{(j)})(t_{j,i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}),\quad\quad j,i\in I_{2}.

We say that two such representations
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
and
(\psi,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
of
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0},

respectively on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
, are
isomorphic
if there exists a unitary
U:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
such that
U\sigma(a)=\psi(a)U
and
V^{(i)}(x_{i})U=UT^{(i)}(x_{i})
for all
a\in\mathcal{B}
and
x_{i}\in E_{i},i\in I_{2}.

Assume
\pi
to be a representation of a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathbb{E}
to be a product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}.
Suppose that
\Theta:E_{2}\to B(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K})
is a completely contractive bi-module map, that is,
\Theta
is completely contractive and
\Theta(a\eta b)=\rho(a)\Theta(\eta)\rho(b)
for all
\eta\in E_{2},a,b\in\mathcal{B}.
Define a contractive linear map
\widetilde{\Theta}:E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}
by

\widetilde{\Theta}(\eta\otimes h)=\Theta(\eta)h~~\>\>\>\mbox{for}~~~~~~\>\>\>\eta\in E_{2},h\in\mathcal{K},

and it satisfy the relation
\widetilde{\Theta}(\phi_{2}(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})=\rho(a)\widetilde{\Theta},a\in\mathcal{B},
where
\phi_{2}
is a module left action of
\mathcal{B}
on
E_{2}.
A completely contractive map
M_{\Theta}:E_{2}\to B(\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K})

[
19
]
is defined by

\displaystyle M_{\Theta}(\eta)(S_{n}(x_{n})h)=\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{\Theta})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(\eta\otimes x_{n}\otimes h),

where
\eta\in E_{2},x_{n}\in E_{1}^{\otimes n},h\in\mathcal{K},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}:E_{2}\otimes E_{1}^{\otimes n}\rightarrow E_{1}^{\otimes n}\otimes E_{2}
is a composition of the isomorphisms
\{t_{j,i}\>:\>1\leq j,i\leq 2\}
and
(\rho,S)
is the induced representation of
E_{1}
induced by
\pi.
Clearly,
(\rho,{M}_{\Theta})
is a c.c.c. representation of
E_{2}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K},

M_{\Theta}(\eta)|_{\mathcal{K}}=\Theta(\eta)
for each
\eta\in E_{2}
and satisfy the relation

\displaystyle M_{\Theta}(\eta)\left(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}x_{n}\otimes h_{n}\right)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{\Theta})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(\eta\otimes x_{n}\otimes h_{n})

for all
\eta\in E_{2},x_{n}\in E_{1}^{\otimes n},h_{n}\in\mathcal{K}.
It is easy to verify that

\widetilde{M}_{\Theta}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{S})=\widetilde{S}(I_{{E}_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{M}_{\Theta})(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}}).
Hence
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta})
is a c.c.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}.
Note that
\Theta
is an isometry if and only if
(\rho,M_{\Theta})
is an isometric covariant representation. In this paper, we focus on
\Theta
such that
(\rho,M_{\Theta})
is a c.c.c. representation.

The concept of pure isometric representation of product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
introduced in
[
19
]
is an important development in the study of representation and Fredholm theory for
C^{*}
-algebras generated by commuting isometries.
They showed that: Assume
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
to be a pure isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
on
\mathcal{H},
then the BCL- representation
(\rho,M_{{\Theta_{1}}},M_{{\Theta_{2}}})

[
19
]
of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is defined by

\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}=(P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}^{\perp}+\widetilde{S}(I_{E_{1}\otimes E_{2}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}))U^{\prime}

and

\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}=(P_{\mathcal{W}_{2}}^{\perp}+\widetilde{S}(t_{2,1}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{2}}))U,

where

U^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}}&0\\
0&(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)*}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})})\end{bmatrix}:E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}\oplus E_{1}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})\to\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})\oplus E\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}
and

U=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}|_{E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}}&0\\
0&(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})})\end{bmatrix}:E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus E_{2}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})\to\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})\oplus E_{2}\otimes E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}
are unitary isomorphisms.

Since

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{H}}-\widetilde{T}^{(1)}\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}+\widetilde{T}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\widetilde{T}^{(2)}\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}=I_{\mathcal{H}}-{\widetilde{T}}{\widetilde{T}^{*}}

Theorem 2.2
.

Using the above notations, let

(2.5)

U=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&0\end{bmatrix}:\mathcal{K}\oplus(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}})\to(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{K})\oplus\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}},

be a unitary module map such that

for all

Then there exists a contractive linear map
\widetilde{\Phi}:E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}
such that

(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{V})\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}=\widetilde{M}_{\Phi}^{*}\Pi_{V},

where

3.
Dilating to pure isometric covariant representations

We now demonstrate that a pure contractive covariant representation can be dilated to a pure isometric covariant representation. First, we explicitly outline the construction of these dilations in the context of finite-dimensional defect spaces.

2.2

onto

Theorem 3.2
.

\mathcal{K}
onto

\mathcal{K}

onto

Theorem 4.1
.

\mathcal{H}
and let
\widetilde{T}\cong P_{\mathcal{Q}}\widetilde{S}|_{E\otimes\mathcal{Q}}
be the isometric dilation for
(\sigma,T)

(
see Theorem
1.6
)
. If
\widetilde{T}=\widetilde{T}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{(2)})
for some c.c.c. representation
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H},
then there exist completely bounded maps
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
such that
\mathcal{Q}
is co-invariant for
(\rho,M_{\phi_{1}},M_{\phi_{2}})
of
\mathbb{E}
on a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T}
and

(\sigma,\widetilde{T}^{(1)},\widetilde{T}^{(2)})\cong(\rho,P_{\mathcal{Q}}\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{1}}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{Q}},P_{\mathcal{Q}}\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{2}}|_{E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{Q}}).

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
(\sigma,T)
be a pure c.c.c. representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
and let
\widetilde{T}\cong P_{\mathcal{Q}}\widetilde{S}|_{E\otimes\mathcal{Q}}
be the isometric dilation for
(\sigma,T)

(
see Theorem
1.6
)
. Then
\widetilde{T}=\widetilde{T}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{(2)})
for some c.c.c. representation
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
if and only if there exist completely bounded linear maps
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
such that
\mathcal{Q}
is co-invariant for
(\rho,M_{\phi_{1}},M_{\phi_{2}})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T},

P_{\mathcal{Q}}\widetilde{S}|_{E\otimes\mathcal{Q}}=P_{\mathcal{Q}}\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{1}}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{2}})|_{E\otimes\mathcal{Q}}=P_{\mathcal{Q}}\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{2}}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{1}})(t_{1,2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T}})|_{E\otimes\mathcal{Q}}.

and

(\sigma,\widetilde{T}^{(1)},\widetilde{T}^{(2)})\cong(\rho,P_{\mathcal{Q}}\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{1}}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{Q}},P_{\mathcal{Q}}\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{2}}|_{E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{Q}}).

5.
Commuting pure contractions

In this section, we study completely contractive covariant representations
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
of
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
that admit a canonical BCL triple associated with them.

Let
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
be a completely contractive covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
From Equation (
2.2
), we define an isometric module map

U:\{(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h\oplus\Delta(T^{(2)})h:h\in\mathcal{H}\}\to\{\Delta(T^{(1)})h\oplus(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}h:h\in\mathcal{H}\}

by

(5.1)

\displaystyle U((I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h,\Delta(T^{(2)})h)=(\Delta(T^{(1)})h,(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}h)\quad h\in\mathcal{H}.

Let us now introduce some subspaces

\mathcal{M}_{U}:=\{(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h\oplus\Delta(T^{(2)})h:h\in\mathcal{H}\}\subseteq E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}},

\mathcal{N}_{U}:=\{\Delta(T^{(1)})h\oplus(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}h:h\in\mathcal{H}\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}\oplus E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}},

Assume
P
to be an orthogonal
projection of
\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}\oplus(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}})
onto
\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}
, i.e.,

P(h_{1},h_{2})=(h_{1},0)\quad\quad\quad(h_{1},h_{2})\in\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}\oplus(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}).

If we now assume that
\mbox{dim~}((\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}\oplus(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}))\ominus\mathcal{N}_{U})=\mbox{dim~}(((E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}})\oplus\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}})\ominus\mathcal{M}_{U}),
then
U
extends to a unitary (again denoted by
U
) from
E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}
to
\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}\oplus E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}.
This discussion allows us to identify a canonical BCL triple associated to
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)}).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
be a c.c.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Then a BCL triple
(\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}\oplus(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}),U,P)

is said to be complete for
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
if
\mbox{dim~}((\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}\oplus(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}))\ominus\mathcal{N}_{U})=\mbox{dim~}(((E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}})\oplus\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}})\ominus\mathcal{M}_{U}).

We have established in our discussion that the assumption:
(\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}\oplus E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}},U,P)
is a complete BCL triple for
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
is equivalent to showing that there exists a unitary,

(5.2)

U=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{A}&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}:(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}})\oplus\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}\to\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}\oplus(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}),

where
(\sigma_{1},A)
is a completely contractive covariant representation of
E_{2}
on
\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}
define by
A(\xi)h=\widetilde{A}(\xi\otimes h)
for
\xi\in E_{2},h\in\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}},\sigma_{1}=\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}},

B:\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}\to\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}},C:E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}\to E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}
and
D:\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}\to E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}
(see
[
18
]
). For every
m\in\mathbb{N},
define

\displaystyle D^{(m)}=(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes m-1}}\otimes D)\dots(I_{E_{1}}\otimes D)D:\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}\to E_{1}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}.

Observe that
D^{(m+n)}=(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes m}}\otimes D^{(n)})D^{(m)}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}.
The following identities based on Equation
5.1
are satisfied for all
h\in\mathcal{H},

(5.3)

\Delta(T^{(1)})h=\widetilde{A}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h+B\Delta(T^{(2)})h,

and

(5.4)

(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}h=C(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h+D\Delta(T^{(2)})h.

Note that with respect to the orthogonal projections
P,Q
defined as above in Theorem
3.1
, we have

(5.5)

U=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{A}&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}QUP^{\perp}|_{ran{P^{\perp}}}&QUP|_{ran{P}}\\
Q^{\perp}UP^{\perp}|_{ran{P^{\perp}}}&Q^{\perp}UP|_{ran{P}}\end{bmatrix},

Since
U^{*}U=I=UU^{*},
we get

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{A}^{*}\widetilde{A}+C^{*}C&\widetilde{A}^{*}B+C^{*}D\\
B^{*}\widetilde{A}+D^{*}C&B^{*}B+D^{*}D\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{A}\widetilde{A}^{*}+BB^{*}&\widetilde{A}C^{*}+BD^{*}\\
C\widetilde{A}^{*}+DB^{*}&CC^{*}+DD^{*}\end{bmatrix}.

If we act by
\widetilde{A}^{*}
on the left of Equation (
5.3
), we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)})h

\displaystyle=\widetilde{A}^{*}\widetilde{A}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h+\widetilde{A}^{*}B\Delta(T^{(2)})h

\displaystyle=(I-C^{*}C)(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h-C^{*}D\Delta(T^{(2)})h

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h-C^{*}(C(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h+D\Delta(T^{(2)})h)

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h-C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}h.

Similarly, if we act by
D^{*}
on the left of Equation (
5.4
), we get

\displaystyle D^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}h

\displaystyle=D^{*}C(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h+D^{*}D\Delta(T^{(2)})h

\displaystyle=-B^{*}\widetilde{A}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h+(I-B^{*}B)\Delta(T^{(2)})h

\displaystyle=\Delta(T^{(2)})h-B^{*}(\widetilde{A}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h+B\Delta(T^{(2)})h)

\displaystyle=\Delta(T^{(2)})h-B^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)})h.

Therefore, we obtain

(5.6)

\Delta(T^{(2)})h=D^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}h+B^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)})h

and

(5.7)

(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}h=\widetilde{A}^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)})h+C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}h.

We now derive a recurrence relation connecting the entries of
U
and
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)}).

Lemma 5.2
.

For all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
h\in\mathcal{H},
we have

(1)

\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)})h=(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{m}^{(2)^{*}}h-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{A}_{k}^{*})C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\\
\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{m-1-k}^{(2)^{*}}h;

(2)

D^{(m)}\Delta(T^{(2)})h=(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes m}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}_{m}^{(1)^{*}}h-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes m-1-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{1}}\otimes D^{(k)})C(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{m-1-k}^{(1)^{*}}h.

Proof.

We shall prove by the mathematical induction. It is clear from Equation (
5.7
) that
(1)
is satisfied for
m=1
and from Equation (
5.7
), we get

(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1}}\otimes\widetilde{A}^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{m-1}^{(2)^{*}}h=(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{m}^{(2)^{*}}h-(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1}}\otimes C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))T^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{m-1}^{(2)^{*}}h.

Now, let us assume that
(1)
is satisfied for
m=n-1
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
that is,

\widetilde{A}_{n-1}^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)})h=(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes{n-1}}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{n-1}^{(2)^{*}}h-\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n-2-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{A}_{k}^{*})C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{n-2-k}^{(2)^{*}}h.

Then, for
m=n
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{n}^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)})h=(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n-1}}\otimes\widetilde{A}^{*})\widetilde{A}_{n-1}^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)})h=(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n-1}}\otimes\widetilde{A}^{*})(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes{n-1}}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{n-1}^{(2)^{*}}h

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n-1}}\otimes\widetilde{A}^{*})(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n-2-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{A}_{k}^{*})C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{n-2-k}^{(2)^{*}}h

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes{n-1}}}\otimes\widetilde{A}^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{n-1}^{(2)^{*}}h-\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n-2-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{A}_{k+1}^{*})C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{n-2-k}^{(2)^{*}}h

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{n}^{(2)^{*}}h-(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n-1}}\otimes C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{n-1}^{(2)^{*}}h

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n-2-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{A}_{k+1}^{*})C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{n-2-k}^{(2)^{*}}h

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{n}^{(2)^{*}}h-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes n-1-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{A}_{k}^{*})C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{n-1-k}^{(2)^{*}}h.

Hence
(1)
holds for
m\in\mathbb{N}.
Statement
(2)
follows in a similar manner by using Equation (
5.4
).
∎

Let
(\sigma,T)
be a c.c.c. representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
, we define a subspace

H_{i}(\widetilde{T}):=\{h\in\mathcal{H}:\|\widetilde{T}_{n}(\xi_{n}\otimes h)\|=\|\xi_{n}\otimes h\|~~\mbox{for}~~n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\xi_{n}\in E^{\otimes n}\}.

In particular,
H_{i}(\widetilde{A}^{*})=\{h\in\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}:\|\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h\|=\|h\|~~\mbox{for}~~n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
and
H_{i}(D)=\{h\in\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}:\|D^{(n)}h\|=\|h\|~~\mbox{for}~~n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\},
where
\widetilde{A}
and
D
defined as above in Eqquation (
5.2
).

We are now ready to obtain our main result on the interplay between pairs of commuting pure contractions and their corresponding BCL triples.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
to be a c.c.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
(\mathcal{D}_{T^{(2)}}\oplus(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}),U,P)
to be a complete BCL triple for
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)}).
Then
H_{i}(\widetilde{A}^{*})=H_{i}(D)=\{0\}.

Proof.

Let us begin by observing that
U
is a unitary operator implies that
\widetilde{A}^{*}=P^{\perp}U^{*}Q|_{ran{Q}}
is a contraction. Moreover,
\|\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h\|^{2}=\|\widetilde{A}_{m-1}^{*}h\|^{2}-\|(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1}}\otimes B^{*})\widetilde{A}_{m-1}^{*}h\|^{2}
for all
h\in\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
h\in H_{i}(\widetilde{A}^{*}),
then
\|\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h\|=\|h\|
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
and hence
\|(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1}}\otimes B^{*})\widetilde{A}_{m-1}^{*}h\|=0.

More precisely,
H_{i}(\widetilde{A}^{*})
is a
\widetilde{A}^{*}
-invariant subspace of
ker(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1}}\otimes B^{*}).

For
h\in H_{i}(\widetilde{A}^{*}),\Delta(T^{(1)})h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}
and
m\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\displaystyle\langle h,\Delta(T^{(1)})h^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\widetilde{A}_{m}\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h,\Delta(T^{(1)})h^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h,\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}\Delta(T^{(1)})h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h,(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{m}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle-

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h,(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{A}_{k}^{*})C^{*}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{m-1-k}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h,(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{m}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle-

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1-k}}\otimes C(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{A}_{k}))\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h,(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{m-1-k}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h,(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{m}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle-

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1-k}}\otimes C\widetilde{A}^{*})\widetilde{A}_{m-k-1}^{*}h,(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{m-1-k}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h,(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{m}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\langle(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1-k}}\otimes DB^{*})\widetilde{A}_{m-k-1}^{*}h,(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m-1-k}}\otimes(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(2)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{T}_{m-1-k}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{A}_{m}^{*}h,(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes m}}\otimes\Delta(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}_{m}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle.

This implies that
|\langle h,\Delta(T^{(1)})h^{\prime}\rangle|\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\|h\|\|\widetilde{T}_{m}^{(2)^{*}}h^{\prime}\|=0,
and hence
H_{i}(\widetilde{A}^{*})\perp\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}}.
This is a contradiction to the fact that
H_{i}(\widetilde{A}^{*})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{T^{(1)}},

unless
H_{i}(\widetilde{A}^{*})=0.
Similarly, from statement
(2)
of Lemma
5.2
we get
H_{i}(D)=\{0\}.
This completes the proof.
∎
