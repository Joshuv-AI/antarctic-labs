First of all, the letter received from Dr. Dilip Kumar Kakru is highly appreciated, as it represents transparency of information and collaboration between researchers from all over the world, generating more knowledge and learning derived from other researchers with more experience.

Regarding the comments on the use of the Bootstrap method for the calculation of differences between medians in the size of the shoots and their respective duration, the error was indeed detected and the method used was verified. On this, the necessary figures are modified in Table 1 (attached at the end of the letter). The difference in the median shoot size between period B-A is indeed 3 [−3.3–9.3] (p-value: 0.352); and between the period C-A is 0 [−6.1–6.1) (p-value: 1.0). The difference in the median duration of outbreaks between period B-A is −28.5 [−86.8–29.8] (p-value: 0.338) and between period C-A is −65.5 [−121.2–9.8] (p-value: 0.021).

With regard to the comment on values of the confidence intervals of the calculated proportions that are difficult to achieve or interpret, it is reported that the calculation method was revised. The “Prtest” or “Prtesti” command was used, both representing a score-based method to compare independent proportions. A discrepancy was detected in the reported P-values, which are corrected in Table 1 attached at the end of the letter, providing confidence in the interpretation. For the calculation of the difference between the proportions of attributable deaths between period B-A, the corrected value of P-value is 0.1; for period C-A it is <0.001. Regarding associated deaths, the P-value is corrected for the difference between period B-A to 0.08. In fact, as mentioned in the letter received, the calculation of simple confidence intervals can directly cross logical limits, so it would be a weakness of the statistics used, given the nature of the data at the population level.

Regarding the calculation of attack rates only considering cases of infections and not considering cases of colonization, it can be commented that attack rates were calculated considering only cases that developed CPE infection, in line with widely accepted national and international definitions. Since routine screening for CPE has not been uniformly implemented in Chilean hospitals, we recognize that some cases may have arisen from the progression of a previously unrecognized colonization rather than documented contact with another case.

It is hoped that the comments have been resolved to clarify the results of the valuable study carried out to the reviewers and the global scientific community.

Cordial greetings.
