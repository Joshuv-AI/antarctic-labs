Large Deviation Functions for Open Quantum Systems with a Strong Symmetry
Fei Liu 1, Jiayin Gu 2, Hailong Wang 1, Shanhe Su 3

Abstract
In open quantum systems with strong symmetries, the global scaled cumulant generating function (SCGF) is generally nonanalytic, so the Gärtner-Ellis theorem cannot directly yield the genuine large-deviation rate function. To address this issue, we propose that the theorem remains valid within blocks of the systems’ operator space: we first obtain local rate functions for each block via the theorem and then recover the global one by minimization. This approach is justified by the dissipative freezing phenomenon in such systems. We demonstrate the scheme in an analytical model and a three-spin model with XX interaction. In the latter, we find that the vanishing of a nonanalytic point in the global SCGF under dephasing appears as an avoided “level” crossing, and we quantify this behavior using a degenerate perturbation theory.

Open quantum system with a strong symmetry

We start with an open quantum system whose density matrix
\rho(t)

,

,

]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho(t)=-\text{i}[H,\rho(t)]+\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\mathscr{D}[A_{j}]\rho(t)\equiv{\mathscr{L}}\rho(t),

(1)

where the Planck constant
\hbar
is set to 1,
H
represents the system’s Hamiltonian, and
r_{j}
for

are the positive rates. The dissipation term in Eq. (
1
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}[A_{j}]\rho(t)=A_{j}\rho(t){A_{j}}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{{A_{j}}^{\dagger}A_{j},\rho(t)\right\},

(2)

where
A_{j}
for
j=1,\cdots,M
represent the jump operators. We assume that the system has a strong symmetry. Its operator representation is
S
, which commutes with the Hamiltonian
H
and all the jump operators
A_{j}
and
A_{j}^{\dagger}

[
2
]
. Let eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the operator
S
be
\nu_{\alpha}
and
|\alpha,k\rangle
:

\displaystyle S|\alpha,\beta\rangle=\nu_{\alpha}|\alpha,\beta\rangle

(3)

with
\alpha=1,\cdots,N_{S}
. The index
\beta
in Eq. (
3
) indicates possible degeneracy and ranges from
1
to
d_{\alpha}
. Since
[H,S]=0
, the Hilbert space
\cal H
of the system is a direct sum of subspaces
{\cal H}_{\alpha}
. Here, each subspace is spanned by all the eigenvectors
|\alpha,k\rangle
with a fixed quantum number
\alpha
. Moreover, the symmetry also induces a decomposition of the systems’ operator space
{\cal B}({\cal H})
into a direct sum of blocks
{\cal B}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
. These blocks are spanned by the operators
|\alpha,k\rangle\langle\alpha^{\prime},k^{\prime}|
, where

=
1,\cdots,d_{\alpha}
and
k^{\prime}
=
1,\cdots,d_{\alpha^{\prime}}
. Previous studies have shown that, under the strong symmetry

, the generator
\mathscr{L}
of the quantum master equation (

) cannot
map operators within one block to another block
[
2
]
. Thus, in the operator space,
\mathscr{L}
is block-diagonal:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}=\oplus_{\alpha=1}^{N_{s}}\oplus_{\alpha^{\prime}=1}^{N_{s}}{\mathscr{L}}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}.

(4)

Significantly, this causes the open quantum system to have at least
N_{S}
steady states,
\rho^{(0)}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha=1,\cdots,N_{s}
, and each residing in the diagonal blocks
{\cal B}_{\alpha\alpha}

[
2
]
. Formally, this is expressed as

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{\alpha\alpha}[\rho^{(0)}_{\alpha}]=0.

(5)

Equation (
5
) clearly shows that if the system initially starts in one of the diagonal blocks, it will remain in the block and reach the steady state of the same block after a long time.

III
Large deviation function in presence of strong symmetry

III.1
Large deviation principle

Equation (
1
) can be unraveled into quantum jump trajectories
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
. These trajectories are composed of alternating deterministic pieces and random jumps of the wave functions. Consider a quantum jump trajectory with
n
jumps. Let the instant and type of each jump be
t_{k}
and
a_{k}
, respectively, for
k=1,\cdots,n
. We can denote this trajectory as

\displaystyle{X}_{n}(t)=({a_{1}},{a_{2}},\cdots,a_{n}),

(6)

where
t
represents the duration of the process. The jump type
a_{k}
is specified by the jump operators in Eq. (
1
) corresponding to each jump event. Therefore, it can take one of the values from
1
to
M
. We define a counting variable for Eq. (
6
) as follows:

\displaystyle C[{X}_{n}(t)]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{a_{k}},

(7)

where the coefficient
w_{a_{k}}
represents a weight assigned according to the jump type
a_{k}
at the jump instant
t_{k}
. In this paper, we focus on the case where the weights are simply equal to
1
. Thus, the counting variable (
7
) represents the total activity
[
11
]
.

Since the quantum jump trajectories are random in nature. Let value of the counting variable. (
7
) be
K
. The large-deviation principle states that in the large time limit, the distribution
p(j,t)
of the counting rate
j=K/t
approximates an exponential function,

\displaystyle p(j,t)\approx e^{-tI(j)},

(8)

where the exponent
I(j)
is the rate function
[
23
]
. Obtaining this function in open quantum systems with strong symmetries is the core of the following discussions.

III.2
Gärtner-Ellis theorem in blocks

As mentioned at the beginning, strong symmetries give rise a dissipative freezing phenomenon
[
8
,
10
]
: in the large time limit, even if a quantum system is initially in a quantum superpostion of different symmetry subspaces, the wave function of the trajectory will have a nonzero coefficient in only one of them. The “freezing” probability of a particular subspace is determined by the norm square of the initial wave function within that subspace. An implication of this phenomenon is that, after a finite period, two quantum jump trajectories, one with an initial wave function in a quantum superposition of different subspaces, and the other with an initial wave function confined to a single subspace, will become identical. Based on the quantum-to-classcial transition, we can approximate the distribution of the counting rate to a conditional probability form:

\displaystyle p(j,t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}(j,t)p_{\alpha}(0),

(9)

where
p_{\alpha}(j,t)
is the distribution of the counting rate
j
if the quantum system starts with a wave function in the subspace
{\cal H}_{\alpha}
, and
p_{\alpha}(0)
is
nonzero
probability (the square of coefficient’s norm) of the system initially being in the same subspace. We assume that the large-deviation principle holds in each diagonal block as well:

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}(j,t)\approx e^{-tI_{\alpha}(j)},

(10)

where
I_{\alpha}(j)
is the local rate function in the block
{\cal B}_{\alpha\alpha}
. Then, substituting Eq. (
10
) into Eq. (
9
) and taking the large time limit, we derive the global rate function

\displaystyle I(j)=\min_{\alpha}\{I_{\alpha}(j)\}.

(11)

Here, the minimization is carried out over the diagonal blocks in which the initial wave function has nonzero coefficients. In the large time limit, the exact value of
p_{\alpha}(0)
no longer matters. Equation (
11
) clearly shows that the rate function in open quantum systems with strong symmetries is generally nonconvex.

Before applying Eq. (
11
), we still need to obtain the local rate functions in these diagonal blocks. Except for a few cases, such as the first model in Sec. (
IV
), it is very difficult, if not impossible, to solve them directly. However, the previous assumption that the large principle holds in each block suggests that we may firstly obtain the local SCGFs
\varphi_{\alpha}(\lambda)
in each blocks, where
\lambda
represents the counting field
[
19
,
11
,
22
]
. These functions are equal to the largest real parts of the eigenvalues of the tilted generator
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}
in the block
{\cal B}_{\alpha\alpha}
, i.e.,
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\alpha\alpha}
. If the SCGFs are analytic functions of the counting field, which is always assumed to be the case in this paper, according to the Gärtner-Ellis theorem, we can apply the Lengendre transform to them to obtain the local rate functions, i.e.,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(j)=\sup_{\lambda}\{\lambda j-\varphi_{\alpha}(\lambda)\}.

(12)

Except for being carried out in the blocks, this procedure is exactly the same as the conventional one used in open quantum systems with a unique steady state
[
19
,
11
,
22
]
. Therefore, we remain the technical details in Appendix A.

IV.1
An analytical model

In this model, the system’s Hamiltonian and the only jump operator are assumed to be proportional to a symmetry operator

Here,

is obtained by taking the minimum of

from Eq. (

\displaystyle I(j)=\min_{m=1,3,5}\{I_{m}(j)\}.

An advantage of the simple model is that its rate function can be solved exactly:

\displaystyle p(n,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\frac{1}{n!}\left(rm^{2}t\right)^{n}e^{-rm^{2}t}c_{m}

(16)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{m}e^{-tI_{m}(j)}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle e^{-t{\min}_{m}\{I_{m}(j)\}}

The first equation was given in Ref.
[
8
]
, see Eq. (23) therein. The second and last equations are the results of taking large time limit. In this case, the precise values of

IV.2.1
Effects of dephasing

It is interesting to observe how the nonanalyticity of the global SCGF vanishes when strong symmetries are broken. This issue is physically relevant, as such symmetries are subject to stringent conditions that are rarely satisfied in reality. A typical case is the presence of local dephasing
[
31
,
7
]
. Formally, this amounts to adding a superoperator

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{d}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\gamma\mathscr{D}[\sigma_{i}^{\dagger}\sigma_{i}^{-}],

(21)

to the generator
\mathscr{L}
of the original quantum master equation, where
\gamma
represents the dephasing strength. Because the dephasing breaks the strongy symmetry (the permutation of the spins 2 and 3), the system reverts to having a unique steady state. Then, the degeneracy of the local SCGFs is lifted, and a nonzero Liouvillian gap
\Delta
emerges at
\lambda=0

[
4
]
. To illustrate these results, in Fig.
2
, we depict the first two largest real parts of the eigenvalues
\mu_{\pm}(\lambda)
of the tilted generator for two different values of
\gamma
. In addition to the nonzero gap, we observe that, when the dephasing is weaker, the upper of the two curves, which is also the SCGF of the three-spin system without strong symmetry, is formed by smoothly connecting the two local SCGFs of the same system with the strong symmetry. This implies that there is a phase crossover in fluctuations and the intermittent phenomenon occurs in the quantum jump trajectories
[
16
,
11
]
. This finding is consistent with the previous result
[
7
]
. In particular, the precise meaning of the phases are clarified now.

When comparing the eigenvalue curves in Fig.
1
(a) and Fig.
2
(b), we note that their relationship is analogous to a transition from the level crossing to the avoided level crossing in the closed quantum systems
[
32
]
. Additionally, at
\lambda=0
, the steeper of them is not shifted in the opposite direction. This is a requirement for the unique steady state. These observations inspire us to conduct a perturbation analysis
[
32
,
33
,
34
]
. To this end, we consider the tilted generator with dephasing as a perturbation to the original generator
{\mathscr{L}}
, and the perturbed terms include the dephasing superoperator (
21
) and the superoperator with the counting field,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}()=r_{-}(e^{\lambda}-1)\sigma_{1}^{-}()\sigma_{1}^{+}+r_{+}(e^{\lambda}-1)\sigma_{1}^{+}()\sigma_{1}^{-}.

(22)

Because the original generator has two degenerate eigenmatrices with eigenvalue
0

\mu_{\pm}
of the tilted generator with dephasing are given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}L\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(\operatorname{tr}L)^{2}-4\det L}.

(23)

Here, the trace and determinant are calculated for a
2\times 2
matrix
L
, whose elements are

\displaystyle L_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\langle\alpha|\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}|\beta\rangle\rangle+\langle\langle\alpha|\mathscr{L}_{d}|\beta\rangle\rangle,

(24)

where
\alpha,\beta=\pm
, and
\langle\langle\alpha|\mathscr{L}_{i}|\beta\rangle\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\omega_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\mathscr{L}_{i}\varrho_{\beta}\right]
for
i=d,\lambda
. Here,
\omega_{\alpha}
and
\varrho_{\alpha}
are the left and right eigenmatrix of the generator
\mathscr{L}
in the
(\alpha,\alpha)
-block with eigenvalue
0
, respectively. That is,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\alpha\alpha}\varrho_{\alpha}=0,\hskip 11.38092pt\mathscr{L}_{\alpha\alpha}^{\dagger}\omega_{\alpha}=0.

(25)

Equation (
23
) makes several predictions. Firstly, in the absence of the counting field perturbation, i.e.,
L_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\langle\alpha|\mathscr{L}_{d}|\beta\rangle\rangle
, the uniqueness of the steady state in the open quantum system with dephasing implies
\det L=0
, so the Liouvillian gap is given by

\displaystyle\Delta=\left|\langle\langle+|\mathscr{L}_{d}|+\rangle\rangle+\langle\langle-|\mathscr{L}_{d}|-\rangle\rangle\right|.

(26)

Second, in the absence of the dephasing perturbation, i.e.,
L_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\langle\alpha|\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}|\beta\rangle\rangle

L_{\pm\mp}
must be zero. Thus, the two local SCGFs around
\lambda=0

\langle\langle\pm|\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}|\pm\rangle\rangle

L_{\pm\mp}
are no longer zero in this case, the value of Eq. (
23

\mathscr{L}_{d}
and
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}
. These three results indicate that in the three-spin system, if the strong symmetry is slightly broken, the system’s relaxation dynamics, which is mainly determined by the Liouvillian gap, and fluctuations around the steady state can be fully characterized by the perturbed superoperators in the diagonal blocks. V
Conclusion

In this paper, we demonstrate that the Gärtner-Ellis theorem can be utilized to obtain the nonconvex rate function of an open quantum system with a strong symmetry. Instead of applying the theorem in the global operator space, where the SCGF is nonanalytic due to the symmetry, we apply it in the diagonal blocks of the space. After obtaining the local rate functions by taking Legendre transforms on the analytic SCGFs in these blocks, the global rate function is equal to their minimum value. The method is justified by the dissipative freezing phenomenon. In the three-spin model, by means of a degenerate perturbation theory, we also quantify how the symmetry-breaking dephasing causes the nonanalyticity in the global SCGF to vanish. We find that, under a weak dephasing, the properties of the diagonal blocks mainly determine the behaviors of the open system lacking such a strong symmetry. This paper focuses on presenting the basic idea and method. We do not explore general theoretical scenarios, such as the presence of multiple strong symmetries or nonAbelian group, open quantum many-body systems, current statistics, etc. These issues merit investigation in the near future.

Appendix A
Evaluating local SCGFs

It is beneficial to outline the conventional procedure for obtaining rate function in an open quantum system with a unique steady state. We focus on the SCGF rather than the function itself:

\displaystyle\varphi(\lambda)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln M(\lambda,t),

(27)

where
M(\lambda,t)
is the moment generating function of the counting distribution
p(n,t)
, that is, the Laplace transform of the distribution with a real parameter
\lambda
(the counting field). If the large deviation is satisfied, the SCGF is equal to the largest real part of the eigenvalues
{\mu}_{i}(\lambda)
of the tilted generator
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}

[
35
,
19
,
11
,
22
]
:

\displaystyle{\tilde{\mathscr{L}}}[\varrho_{i}]\equiv-\text{i}[H,\varrho_{i}]+\sum_{j=1}^{M}r_{j}\tilde{\mathscr{D}}[A_{j}]\varrho_{i}={\mu}_{i}(\lambda){\varrho}_{i},

(28)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathscr{D}}[A_{j}]{\varrho}_{i}=e^{\lambda\omega_{j}}A_{j}{\varrho}_{i}A^{\dagger}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{A^{\dagger}_{j}A_{j},{\varrho}_{i}\right\}.

(29)

If the SCGF is analytic with respect to
\lambda
, according to the Gärtner-Ellis theorem, the rate function
I(j)
is the Legendre transform of
\varphi(\lambda)
with respect to
\lambda
(analogous to Eq. (
12
))
[
23
]
.

In the presence of strong symmetries, the Hamiltonian and jump operators commute with the symmetry operators. Hence, following an analogous discussion to that in Sec.
II
, in the operator space, the tilted generator
\mathscr{L}
is also block-diagonal:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathscr{L}}=\oplus_{\alpha=1}^{N_{s}}\oplus_{\alpha^{\prime}=1}^{N_{s}}\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}.

(30)

The above mentioned conventional procedure can be extended. The local SCGF
\varphi_{\alpha}(\lambda)
in the diagonal block
\cal B_{\alpha\alpha}
is equal to the largest real part of the eigenvalues
{\mu}^{(\alpha)}_{i}(\lambda)
of the tilted block
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\alpha\alpha}
, and the local rate function
I_{\alpha}(j)

\alpha=1,\cdots,N_{S}

To conclude this appendix, we explain why the global SCGF in the presence of strong symmetries is nonanalytic, using the notations of this paper
[
7
]
. The moment generating function of the counting distribution is given by

\displaystyle M(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[\exp\left(t\tilde{\mathscr{L}}\right)\rho(0)\right]

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[\oplus_{\alpha=1}^{N_{s}}\oplus_{\alpha^{\prime}=1}^{N_{s}}\exp\left(t\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\right)\rho_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(0)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_{s}}{\rm Tr}\left[\exp\left(t\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\alpha\alpha}\right)\rho_{\alpha\alpha}(0)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_{s}}M_{\alpha}(\lambda,t).

The third equation holds due to the orthogonality between the symmetry subspaces. In the last equation,
M_{\alpha}(\lambda,t)
is precisely the moment generating function in the diagonal block
{\cal B}_{\alpha\alpha}
.
The large-deviation principle is assumed to hold in each block, i.e.,

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}(\lambda,t)\approx e^{t\varphi_{\alpha}(\lambda)}.

(32)

Substituting this result into Eq. (
31
) and taking the large time limit, we obtain the global SCGF as

\displaystyle\varphi(\lambda)=\max_{\alpha}\{\varphi_{\alpha}(\lambda)\}.

(33)

In general,
\varphi_{\alpha}(\lambda)
differs for different
\alpha
. Thus,
\varphi(\lambda)
is nonanalytic at
\lambda=0
.

33
