Key Terms and Definitions
  • NMAC: Nuclear Material Accounting and Control
  • TID (Tamper-Indicating Device): A security device that visibly shows if it has been
 tampered with.
  • VTR: Vault type room
  • Adhesive Mylar TID: An adhesive tamper-evident material that changes its
 appearance when disturbed.
  • Tampering: Unauthorized or illegal interference with secure devices or materials.
  • Regulatory Compliance: Adhering to legal and industry standards regarding security
 devices and practices.

 Item Weighing Demonstration
 Item Weighing Demonstration Video

This training video provides a demonstration of utilizing balances/scales for item weight
measurements, with a specific focus on a common electronic balance. Scales are a critical
part of nuclear security, used to determine differing weight measurements of facility items,
in various forms and functions. The scale techniques shown in this video help in material’s
weight verification against the declared weight and ensure that discrepancies are
identified and resolved. By following a proceduralized set up, this video will also visualize
proper material weighing practices to ensure that all materials being tracked in a physical
inventory are accurately weighed and logged.
The full script is available in Item Weighing Demonstration Video- Full Script, included in
compilation LANL document INS Nuclear Material Accounting and Control Instructional
Video Material- FY 25 Full Scripts.
Video Reference: SAND2025-04917V; NMAC Snip Its Material Weighing Procedure
Youtube: https://youtu.be/KaD6fop_DDM

 • Demonstrate and provide proceduralized steps for proper material weighing to
 ensure that all materials being tracked in a physical inventory are accurately
 weighed and logged.
 • Verify the weight of materials against the declared weight and ensure that
 discrepancies are identified and resolved.
 • Emphasizes accuracy, safety, and proper documentation, with a strict adherence to
 the two-person rule to ensure the integrity of the inventory data.

 1. Preparation
 a. Verification of scale calibration.
 b. Ensuring the scale is functioning correctly before use.
 2. Weighing Process
 a. Weighing individual cans and ensuring that the weight is consistent with the
 declared weight.
 b. Recording the weight in the Inventory Weighing Form.
 c. Verifying each reading with the second team member.
 3. Final Review
 a. Summing the total weight of all materials.
 b. Performing a final calibration check.
 c. Submitting the completed inventory records for approval.

 Item Weighing Demonstration

This training video provides a demonstration of utilizing balances/scales for item weight
measurements, with a specific focus on a common electronic balance. Scales are a critical
part of nuclear security, used to determine differing weight measurements of facility items,
in various forms and functions. The scale techniques shown in this video help in material’s
weight verification against the declared weight and ensure that discrepancies are
identified and resolved. By following a proceduralized set up, this video will also visualize
proper material weighing practices to ensure that all materials being tracked in a physical
inventory are accurately weighed and logged.

 1. Demonstrate and provide proceduralized steps for proper material weighing to
 ensure that all materials being tracked in a physical inventory are accurately
 weighed and logged.
 2. Verify the weight of materials against the declared weight and ensure that
 discrepancies are identified and resolved.
 3. Emphasizes accuracy, safety, and proper documentation, with a strict adherence to
 the two-person rule to ensure the integrity of the inventory data.

 1. Preparation
 a. Verification of scale calibration.
 b. Ensuring the scale is functioning correctly before use.
 2. Weighing Process
 a. Weighing individual cans and ensuring that the weight is consistent with the
 declared weight.
 b. Recording the weight in the Inventory Weighing Form.
 c. Verifying each reading with the second team member.
 3. Final Review
 a. Summing the total weight of all materials.
 b. Performing a final calibration check.
 c. Submitting the completed inventory records for approval.
