 Abstract

 This note describes the solution of the free-space Poisson equation in the interior of a d-
 dimensional homogeneous ellipsoid, and the associated space charge fields. An explicit formula
 (42) is provided that relates the d × d matrix describing the space charge (quadratic) potential
 to the d × d covariance matrix of the ellipsoid. For the cases d = 2 and d = 3, this result is
 used to determine the linear map corresponding to a space charge kick, that may be used to
 push the beam 6 × 6 covariance matrix during envelope tracking. The treatment of upright
 ellipsoids for d = 2 and d = 3 is well-represented in the literature. However, the approach
 taken here emphasizes a general ellipsoid with arbitrary correlations in any dimension. The
 Appendix provides a general solution of the free-space Poisson equation in dimension d for a
 source distribution with ellipsoidal symmetry.

1 Definition of the Homogeneous Ellipsoid

Consider an arbitrary ellipsoid in the space Rd , described by coordinates (r1 , r2 , . . . , rd ). Such an
ellipsoid is defined as the loci of points satisfying an equation of the form:

                                            Q(r1 , . . . , rd ) = 1,                                     (1)

where Q is a homogeneous polynomial of degree 2 (a quadratic form). Any such quadratic form
can be expressed in terms of a d × d symmetric matrix M in the form:

                                Q(r) = rT M r,         r = (r1 , r2 , . . . , rd )T .                    (2)

For Q to define an ellipsoid (as opposed to, e.g., a hyperboloid), it is necessary and sufficient that
the matrix M be positive definite. Any positive definite symmetric matrix M can be diagonalized
by an orthogonal matrix A ∈ SO(d), so that:
