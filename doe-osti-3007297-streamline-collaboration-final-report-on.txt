1 Abstract
The DOE-funded STREAMLINE1 collaboration pushed the frontiers of theoretical and computa-
tional research on the nuclear many-body problem using machine learning (ML). By leveraging
ML techniques, STREAMLINE advanced large-scale nuclear-physics computations, substantially
enhancing predictive power and improving our understanding of nuclear structure and dynamics,
bulk nucleonic matter, and emergent many-body phenomena.
 This final report focuses on the achievements made by the supported early-career researchers
at Ohio University, advised by the PI Christian Drischler, and their STREAMLINE collaborators.
Their efforts, which lie at the cutting edge of ML applications in nuclear physics, have enabled
significant progress in the principled quantification of uncertainties in nuclear theory predictions.
 First, we employed Bayesian ML to combine predictions from models that are mutually ex-
clusive with high confidence. This means that not all models can be both accurate and precise
simultaneously. While our developed hierarchical Bayesian framework is broadly applicable, we
specifically used it to derive rigorous constraints on the empirical nuclear saturation point inferred
from phenomenological density functional theory (DFT), which is calibrated to nuclear observables
such as nuclear binding energies. The nuclear saturation point serves as an important in-medium
benchmark for nuclear forces derived from chiral effective field theory (EFT), which is the dominant
approach for deriving microscopic nuclear interactions consistent with the low-energy symmetries
of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the theory of strong interactions. Our uncertainty-quantified
constraints laid the groundwork for improving the saturation properties of next-generation chi-
ral interactions and for guiding the development of improved energy-density functionals. These
benchmarks are critical for accurately predicting nuclear structure and nuclear-matter observables,
including the neutron star structure.
 Second, we addressed the computational bottleneck in two- and three-body scattering calcula-
tions by introducing reduced-order emulators, or surrogate models, equipped with active learning
for training and error estimation. These projection-based emulators significantly reduced the cost
of solving scattering equations in the parameter space of nuclear interactions, while maintaining
accuracy and providing reliable error estimates. The developed approach applies to both two-body
and three-body scattering and supports Bayesian parameter estimation, allowing for uncertainty
quantification in complex nuclear scattering and reaction processes. This development is critical in
light of recent efforts in nuclear experiment throughout the United States.
 Led by PI Dean Lee at Michigan State University (MSU), STREAMLINE brought together
leading researchers in theoretical nuclear physics and ML. The co-PIs included Christian Drischler
from Ohio University, Kévin Fossez from Florida State University (FSU), Richard Furnstahl from
Ohio State University (OSU), Gaute Hagen from Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), Morten
Hjorth-Jensen from Oslo/MSU, Sebastian König from North Carolina State University (NCSU),
Alessandro Lovato from Argonne National Laboratory (ANL), Witold Nazarewicz from MSU,
Noemi Rocco from Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (FNAL), Jorge Piekarewicz from FSU,
and Thomas Papenbrock from the University of Tennessee (UT), Knoxville.

2.1 Constraining the nuclear saturation in symmetric nuclear matter
Nuclear matter, a strongly interacting system made of protons and neutrons, is pervasive through-
out the visible universe. While the strong interaction binds nucleons into atomic nuclei, it provides
the pressure required to support neutron stars against gravitational collapse, driving nuclear mat-
ter in their cores to densities far beyond those probed in atomic nuclei. Understanding how the
properties of nuclear matter emerge and evolve across such a wide range of densities has been a
critical subject of study [7].
 The nuclear equation of state (EOS) encodes how the energy per particle E(nb , δ, T )/A changes
as a function of the nucleon (number) density nb , isospin asymmetry δ, and temperature T . Among
the hallmarks of the nuclear dynamics is the saturation property of isospin symmetric nuclear matter
(SNM) at zero temperature (T = 0), characterized by equal neutron and proton densities (δ = 0).
In that limit, the EOS exhibits a minimum at the density n0 ≈ 0.16 fm−3 , which is related to the
typical central density of heavy nuclei, while the corresponding ground-state energy per particle
E(n0 )/A ≡ E0 ≈ −16 MeV is closely related to the volume term of the semi-empirical mass formula.
Together, (n0 , E0 ) are referred to as the nuclear saturation point.
 Although not directly measurable, the nuclear saturation point (n0 , E0 ) can be extrapolated by
density functional theory (DFT), providing tight constraints for microscopic interactions derived
from chiral effective field theory (EFT). However, when considering several DFT predictions for
(n0 , E0 ) from Skyrme and Relativistic Mean Field (RMF) models together, a discrepancy between
these model classes emerges at high confidence levels that the uncertainties of individual model
predictions cannot explain. Given that DFT predictions typically report saturation points that,
when considered together, are clearly inconsistent with each other, one must conclude that not all
DFT predictions can be both precise and accurate simultaneously.
 In the first project [1], we addressed the critical question of how one can leverage these DFT
constraints to benchmark nuclear saturation properties of chiral interactions rigorously. To this
end, we developed a hierarchical Bayesian mixture model that combines multiple DFT predictions
for (n0 , E0 ) using an efficient conjugate prior approach. The inferred posterior distribution for
the saturation point’s mean and covariance matrix belonged to the Normal-inverse-Wishart class,
yielding posterior predictives in the form of correlated bivariate t-distributions. The DFT uncer-
tainty reports were then used to mix these posteriors using an ordinary Monte Carlo approach. At

2.2 Active learning emulators for nuclear scattering
Nuclear scattering experiments yield invaluable data to test, validate, and improve theoretical
models such as chiral EFT, which has been the method of choice for deriving microscopic nu-
clear interactions at low energies [8]. However, there are competing formulations of chiral EFT,
with open questions regarding EFT power counting, sensitivity to regulator artifacts, and differ-
ing predictions for medium-mass atomic nuclei. Low-energy nuclear scattering data, combined
with statistical methods such as Bayesian parameter estimation, model comparison, and sensitivity

 5

Figure 3: Flowchart depicting the process of constructing an emulator basis using the developed
greedy algorithm. See also the illustration of the greedy algorithm in Fig. 2. The greedy algorithm
iteratively refines an initial emulator basis during the offline (or training) stage. Once the greedy
algorithm terminates, the emulator can be used in the online (or evaluation) stage, analogous to
the POD, although with a different emulator basis. The acronym FOM stands for full-order model,
and θ denotes the vector of free parameters. The figure was taken from Ref. [3].

analysis, applied to chiral EFT predictions, can provide essential insights to address these issues.
However, to fully exploit the available data with such statistical methods, fast and accurate pre-
dictions across a wide range of model parameters are required. While, in principle, the scattering
equations can be solved accurately in few-body systems, doing so is prohibitively slow for statisti-
cal analyses of three- and higher-body scattering, and even for two-body scattering, more efficient
alternatives are appealing.
 In the second project [2, 3], we developed, implemented, and tested two model-driven emulators
based on Galerkin and Petrov-Galerkin projection using the prototypical test case of two-body
scattering with the Minnesota potential and a more realistic local chiral potential. The high-
fidelity scattering equations were solved with the matrix Numerov method, a reformulation of the
popular Numerov recurrence relation for solving special second-order differential equations as a
linear system of coupled equations. These reduced-basis method (RBM) emulators allow one to
approximate scattering solutions in the parameter space of the chiral interactions θ, characterized
by the NN contact interactions. This project is part of the PI’s longer-term effort to construct fast
and accurate emulators for nuclear scattering [8–11].
 A novel error estimator based on reduced-space residuals was applied to an active-learning ap-
proach (a so-called greedy algorithm) to select training samples (“snapshots”) for the emulator,
and was contrasted with the standard, although naı̈ve, proper orthogonal decomposition (POD)
approach. Figure 2 shows an illustrative comparison between the POD and greedy approaches.
During emulator training, the greedy algorithm iteratively updates the emulator basis, placing
snapshots in the interaction’s parameter space where the emulator’s error is estimated to be max-
imal. Figure 3 provides a flowchart of this greedy iteration process. Both approaches enable
computationally efficient offline-online decompositions, but the greedy approach required substan-
tially fewer snapshot calculations, thereby enabling emulator training in applications that would be
otherwise computationally too expensive, such as three-body scattering. Furthermore, the greedy
algorithm provided emulator error estimates for emulated wave functions and associated phase
shifts, which was a critical development, as studying emulator errors was in its infancy in nuclear
physics, and has wide-ranging applications for emulating solutions to large linear systems.
 This project was led by STREAMLINE-supported graduate student Joshua Maldonado (Ohio
University), working with Drischler and STREAMLINE member R. J. Furnstahl (OSU). Maldonado
presented this research at the “2024 Ohio University Student Research and Creative Activity Expo,”

for which he received a poster award. Soon after, he completed and defended his master’s thesis on
this project [2] and graduated from Ohio University in the fall of 2024. The research project was

 Maldonado was succeeded by Abhinav Giri, the second graduate student supported by STREAM-

approach in the following essential ways:

 • scattering in momentum space in both coupled and uncoupled partial-wave channels by solv-
 ing the Lippmann-Schwinger (LS) integral equation for the scattering t-matrix, giving access
 to a wide range of modern chiral interactions;

 • proof-of-principle demonstration of the efficacy of these active learning emulators with error

 • high-performance computing (HPC) implementation of these emulators and assessment of
 their speed-ups.

An advanced draft of a manuscript is currently a work in progress and is expected to be submitted in
a few weeks. Giri successfully presented his work at the 2025 Ohio University Student Research and
Creative Activity Expo. He plans to present his Ph.D. prospectus in the spring of 2026, with ongoing
support from the STREAMLINE successor, the DOE-funded STREAMLINE2 collaboration. In
September 2025, Kim transitioned to ANL to begin her second postdoctoral position, where she
has continued to work with STREAMLINE2.
 Together with the STREAMLINE members Xilin Zhang (FRIB/MSU) and Furnstahl, Drischler
has been collaborating with Alex Gnech (Old Dominion University) and the Pisa group to develop
active learning emulators for nucleon-deuteron (Nd) scattering above the deuteron-breakup thresh-
old. The Pisa group has unique expertise in few-body scattering calculations based on the Kohn
variational principle (KVP) and hyperspherical harmonics, which provide high-fidelity calculations
for training scattering emulators. The efficient emulator training is facilitated by the active learning
approach developed in Refs. [2, 3]. In a proof-of-principle calculation [12, 13], we demonstrated
that these three-body emulators enable the accurate and efficient emulation of the R-matrix for
proton-deuteron scattering in the parameter space of the two 3N low-energy couplings at N2 LO,
cD and cE . The STREAMLINE collaboration significantly contributed to the development of these
three-body emulators, and they were subsequently submitted to the preprint server arXiv upon
STREAMLINE’s conclusion. The continued development will be supported by the STREAM-
LINE2 collaboration, which includes an Ohio University portion led by Drischler.
 These developments laid the groundwork for emulating scattering observables using chiral NN
and 3N interactions, as well as optical models in the FRIB era, where computational speedups are
necessary for Bayesian uncertainty quantification of scattering processes. The source code for the
two-body scattering emulators was made publicly available on GitHub, enabling practitioners to
use and extend our emulators in their research.
