Note a, Thermo-nuclear Concept

The Commerce member said he felt strongly that whatever happens when
next there is a major multilateral meeting, one way or another we are going
to be faced with the thermo-nuclear concept and, regardless of the techniques
that may be devised, he felt we should start preparing ourselves to handle
this problem.

The Defense member replied that the JCS had stated its willingness to
discuss this matter with their British counterparts. He felt that this subject
should be limited to discussions by the military and not discussed in C0C0M
since they do not have the competence. It should be taken out of the C0C0M
frame of reference until the military elements have been discussed.

1. Work Program for Paragraph 9 of Economic Defense Polity

The Chairman of the Ad Hoc Subcommittee reported that the first meeting of
that group would be held Wednesday, October 2, 1957.

The Executive Committee Chairman said he hoped all Executive Committee
members would assist Mr. George in obtaining prompt help if he needs it and
also to help in getting priorities established. He hoped everyone recognized
this as a priority activity.

3. List II Quotas for 1958
Decisions

The Executive Committee members would discuss the State Department draft
instruction to the USDEL re List II quotas for 1958 with their EDAC members
and notify the State Executive Committee member if there were any objections
to it being sent.

The Chairman of the Executive Committee should also be notified of the
agencies' decisions in the event an EDAC meeting would have to be called for
Thursday, October 3, to review alternatives presented by agencies*

Discussion

The State member distributed to the members a draft instruction to the USDEL
on the subject of List II quotas for 1958= He stated that this draft is the
result of serious review within the Department of State and it represents the
views of EDAC level thinking in State. He requested the members to discuss it
with their EDAC members and to inform him if any agency would object to its trans-
mission. He felt that if there was disagreement there would have to be a higher-
level review*

The Commerce member asked if State had determined where the physical and
institutional obstacles now hindering transshipments (point (2) in draft under
review) would apply to List II items.

The State member replied that they have not received the results of a review
by the H area. There has not been full agreement in State and, in the absence
of a finding by the intelligence area in State, this message leaves the issue
open as to items which might be affected by this arrangement.

The members did not agree with the statement in (1) that "...it cannot be
assumed that strategic industrial materials available to one ^SSR and Communist
China/ automatically become available to other.” The State member said this was
the big argument in terms of economic impact of the differential but State's view
was and remains that there is not a complete freedom of movement between Communist
China and the remainder of the Bloc. After further discussion of this point the
members felt that the statement would be more accurate if the word "items" were
substituted for "materials". SECRET
