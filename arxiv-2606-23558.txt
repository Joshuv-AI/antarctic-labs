Regime-Switching Volterra Operators: Modal Stability and Quenched Amplification
Mauricio Herrera-Marín

Abstract
We develop an operator-theoretic framework for finite-dimensional, regime-dependent Volterra equations with completely monotone memory kernels, dissipative network coupling, and Hawkes-type self-excitation. For each fixed regime we construct the associated Volterra resolvent family and prove global well-posedness, continuity across regime switches, and explicit a priori bounds. The main stability result is sharp in the commuting case: after simultaneous diagonalization of the network Laplacian and the excitation operator, each mode obeys a scalar characteristic equation, and global asymptotic stability holds exactly when every modal branching ratio lies below the intensity damping threshold. We also give a norm-based sufficient condition for noncommuting operators and a Perron–Frobenius spectral criterion for nonnegative intensity blocks, showing when norm estimates are conservative. Beyond mean stability, we prove a pathwise finite-range power law for burst amplitudes generated by residence in a Hurwitz but nonnormal regime: under a cone-alignment event, the survival exponent is the ratio of the regime exit rate to a cone-corrected finite-time growth rate bounded above by the logarithmic norm of a fixed Markovian realization in the chosen Euclidean metric. A complementary idealized-feedback result shows how a logarithmic-norm contraction caps the amplification band. Finally, we derive the deterministic intensity block as a mean-field limit of a relaxing long-memory Hawkes system with regimes. Numerical experiments on modal equations, a small-world network, and a switched nonnormal ODE validate the sharp threshold and the finite-range amplification mechanism without using the closed-form tail formula as input.

1.1
The equation and its motivation

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected graph on
n
nodes with graph Laplacian
L_{G}
, attach to each
node one state coordinate and one intensity coordinate, and collect them into vectors in

H=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We study the regime-dependent, operator-valued Volterra evolution

\dot{U}(t)=\mathcal{B}\,U(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{Z(t)}(t-s)\,U(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+F_{Z(t)}(t),\qquad U(0)=U_{0}\in\mathcal{X},

(1)

posed on the product space
\mathcal{X}=H\times H=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the first block carries a
dissipative network operator
\mathcal{B}

and a node-local damping, the convolution
operator
\mathcal{G}_{z}
encodes regime-dependent self-excitation through a completely monotone scalar
kernel

A_{z}

is a piecewise-constant

2

Equation (
1

geometry, self-excitation, and latent regime modulation act simultaneously. The completely
monotone kernel

models long-range (in particular, tempered power-law and fractional)
memory; the operator
\mathcal{B}
models diffusion or consensus dynamics on the network; the
convolution term models Hawkes-type self-excitation whose strength is regime-dependent; and the
process

models slow switches of the operative dynamical regime, in the spirit of
stochastic systems with Markovian switching
[
16
]
. Each ingredient is
individually classical. Their interaction is not, and it is the source of the phenomena we
analyze.

Theoretical gaps and contributions

Relation to existing theory

Three relations deserve emphasis, because they delimit what is and is not new.

First, our resolvent construction extends the scalar completely monotone Volterra theory

Second, the hydrodynamic limit is in the lineage of Jaisson and Rosenbaum

Functional setting

2.1
State space

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected undirected graph with
n=\left\lvert V\right\rvert
nodes and symmetric
weighted adjacency matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
W_{ij}\geq 0
. To each node we attach one scalar
state coordinate and one scalar intensity coordinate, so that the node-state vector and the
intensity vector both lie in
H:=\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the Euclidean inner product

\left\langle x,y\right\rangle_{H}=\sum_{i}x_{i}y_{i}
. The full state lives in the product space

\mathcal{X}:=H\times H=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\left\lVert(x,\lambda)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}:=\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{H}^{2}.

(2)

We write
U=(x,\lambda)^{\top}\in\mathcal{X}
, where
x_{i}
is the state at node
i
and
\lambda_{i}
the
auxiliary intensity (activity) at node
i
.

Remark 2.1

(Scope of the setting)
.

We work throughout in the finite-dimensional space
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so every operator below
is a bounded matrix with full domain, and all spectral statements refer to matrix spectra. We use
resolvent-family language because it is the natural calculus for the nonlocal (Volterra) part of
(
1
), and because every constant we track is dimension-free—depending only on the
operator norms, the kernel mass, and the dissipation
c_{\ast}
, not on
n
. The extension to an
abstract infinite-dimensional node Hilbert space with a sectorial generator
\mathcal{B}
is therefore
natural but requires the operator-semigroup machinery of
[
17
]
; we state it as
future work and do not claim it here, so as to keep every proof self-contained and finite.

2.2
Dissipative network operator

Let
L=D-W
be the combinatorial graph Laplacian,
D_{ii}=\sum_{j}W_{ij}
, which is symmetric
positive semidefinite. Define
\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
by

\mathcal{B}(x,\lambda):=\big(-c_{x}x-\kappa Lx,\ -c_{\lambda}\lambda\big),\qquad c_{x},c_{\lambda}>0,\ \kappa\geq 0.

(3)

Proposition 2.2

(Dissipativity)
.

The operator
\mathcal{B}
is dissipative on
\mathcal{X}
and generates a contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup

\{e^{t\mathcal{B}}\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfying
\left\lVert e^{t\mathcal{B}}\right\rVert\leq e^{-c_{\ast}t}
with

c_{\ast}:=\min\{c_{x},c_{\lambda}\}>0
.

Proof.

For
U=(x,\lambda)\in\mathcal{X}
,

\left\langle\mathcal{B}U,U\right\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}=\left\langle-c_{x}x-\kappa Lx,x\right\rangle_{H}+\left\langle-c_{\lambda}\lambda,\lambda\right\rangle_{H}=-c_{x}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{H}^{2}-\kappa\left\langle Lx,x\right\rangle_{H}-c_{\lambda}\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{H}^{2}.

Since
L\succeq 0
we have
\left\langle Lx,x\right\rangle_{H}\geq 0
, hence

\left\langle\mathcal{B}U,U\right\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\leq-c_{x}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{H}^{2}-c_{\lambda}\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\leq-c_{\ast}\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}.

(4)

Thus
\mathcal{B}+c_{\ast}\mathrm{I}
is dissipative, so
\mathcal{B}
is dissipative. As
\mathcal{X}
is finite-dimensional,

\mathcal{B}
is bounded and generates the uniformly continuous group
e^{t\mathcal{B}}
; the bound
(
4
) gives, for any solution of
\dot{U}=\mathcal{B}U
,

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}=2\left\langle\mathcal{B}U,U\right\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\leq-2c_{\ast}\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}
, whence

\left\lVert e^{t\mathcal{B}}U_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\leq e^{-c_{\ast}t}\left\lVert U_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}
by Grönwall. Therefore
e^{t\mathcal{B}}
is a
contraction semigroup with the stated exponential bound.
∎

2.3
Completely monotone memory kernels

For each regime
z
in a finite index set
\mathcal{Z}
, let
g_{z}:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

satisfy:

(K1)

g_{z}
is completely monotone:
(-1)^{k}g_{z}^{(k)}(t)\geq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
t>0
;

(K2)

g_{z}\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
, with mass
G_{z}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}g_{z}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
;

(K3)

g_{z}
admits a Bernstein representation
g_{z}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}\,\nu_{z}(\mathrm{d}r)

for a positive Borel measure
\nu_{z}
on
(0,\infty)
.

By Bernstein’s theorem
[
18
]
,
(K1)
and
(K3)
are equivalent; we
state both for convenience. Canonical examples are the tempered fractional kernel

g(t)=t^{\alpha-1}e^{-\theta t}/\Gamma(\alpha)
(
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
\theta>0
, mass

\theta^{-\alpha}
) and the exponential
g(t)=\omega e^{-\omega t}
(mass
1
). The Laplace
transform
\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\xi t}g_{z}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
is, by
(K3)
,

\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_{z}(\mathrm{d}r)}{\xi+r},

(5)

which is completely monotone in
\xi>0
, strictly decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, with

\widehat{g}_{z}(0^{+})=G_{z}
and
\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\to 0
as
\xi\to\infty
. We record this for later
use.

Lemma 2.3

(Monotonicity of the Laplace symbol)
.

Under
(K1)
–
(K3)
, the map
\xi\mapsto\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)
extends analytically to

\operatorname{Re}\xi>0
, is real and strictly decreasing on
(0,\infty)
from
G_{z}
to
0
, and satisfies

\left\lvert\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\right\rvert\leq\widehat{g}_{z}(\operatorname{Re}\xi)\leq G_{z}
for
\operatorname{Re}\xi>0
.

Proof.

Analyticity on
\operatorname{Re}\xi>0
follows from (
5
) and dominated convergence. Indeed,

g_{z}\in L^{1}
implies

G_{z}=\int_{0}^{\infty}g_{z}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{(0,\infty)}r^{-1}\,\nu_{z}(\mathrm{d}r)<\infty,

by Tonelli’s theorem, and
(\operatorname{Re}\xi+r)^{-1}\leq r^{-1}
for
r>0
. Hence the integral defining

\widehat{g}_{z}
is finite and locally dominated in the half-plane. For real
\xi>0
, differentiating under the integral gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\xi}\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}(\xi+r)^{-2}\nu_{z}(\mathrm{d}r)<0
, so it is strictly
decreasing; the boundary values follow from monotone convergence. For complex
\xi
with

\operatorname{Re}\xi>0
,
\left\lvert\xi+r\right\rvert\geq\operatorname{Re}\xi+r
gives

\left\lvert\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\right\rvert\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}(\operatorname{Re}\xi+r)^{-1}\nu_{z}(\mathrm{d}r)=\widehat{g}_{z}(\operatorname{Re}\xi)\leq G_{z}
.
∎

2.4
Regime-dependent excitation

Each regime
z
carries a bounded excitation operator
A_{z}\in\mathcal{L}(H)
, identified with a matrix
in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Define the operator-valued kernel

\mathcal{G}_{z}(t)(x,\lambda):=g_{z}(t)\,\big(0,\ A_{z}x+A_{z}\lambda\big)=g_{z}(t)\,\mathcal{A}_{z}(x,\lambda),\qquad\mathcal{A}_{z}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
A_{z}&A_{z}\end{pmatrix},

(6)

so the memory acts through the intensity block with feedback from both past state and past
intensity. The associated Volterra convolution operator is

(\mathcal{V}_{z}U)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{z}(t-s)\mathcal{A}_{z}U(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
.

The stability threshold proved below is governed by the intensity block. In the modal variables,

x_{j}
is an exponentially decaying input to
\lambda_{j}
; hence it can affect constants and, in the
exponential-moment case, the observed decay rate, but it does not move the Volterra characteristic
zeros that determine whether the intensity feedback is subcritical, critical, or supercritical.
For this reason several sharp spectral results are stated directly for the diagonal intensity
block
\dot{\lambda}=-c_{\lambda}\lambda+g_{z}*A_{z}\lambda
.

Lemma 2.4

(Convolution bound)
.

For all
t>0
,

\left\lVert(\mathcal{V}_{z}U)(t)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\int_{0}^{t}g_{z}(s)\left\lVert U(t-s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\,\mathrm{d}s
, and

\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\lVert\mathcal{V}_{z}(t)\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert G_{z}
, where

\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{2}\,\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert
.

Proof.

The integral bound is the triangle inequality together with

\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}U(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\left\lVert U(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}
and
g_{z}\geq 0
. Taking the
supremum over
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq 1
and using
(K2)
gives the uniform bound. For the norm of

\mathcal{A}_{z}
, given
U=(x,\lambda)
with
\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}=\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{H}^{2}=1
,

\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}U\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}=\left\lVert A_{z}(x+\lambda)\right\rVert_{H}\leq\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert\left\lVert x+\lambda\right\rVert_{H}\leq\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert(\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{H}+\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{H})\leq\sqrt{2}\,\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert
, the last step by Cauchy–Schwarz.
∎

We define the
fractional branching ratio
of regime
z
as

\rho_{z}:=\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert\,G_{z}.

(7)

This scalar will control both well-posedness (
Section

3
) and stability
(
Section

4
); the terminology is justified by the Hawkes correspondence of

Section

6
, where
\rho_{z}
is exactly the mean offspring number of the microscopic
process.

Well-posedness and the resolvent family

We fix the standing assumptions for the whole paper.

Assumption 1

(Standing assumptions)
.

The graph
G
is finite, connected, undirected with symmetric
W\geq 0
;
\mathcal{B}
is given by
(
3
) with
c_{x},c_{\lambda}>0
,
\kappa\geq 0
; for each
z\in\mathcal{Z}
(finite),

g_{z}
satisfies
(K1)
–
(K3)
and
A_{z}\in\mathcal{L}(H)
; and the regime path
t\mapsto Z(t)
is
piecewise constant, right-continuous, with locally finitely many jumps at times

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots
.

Remark 3.1

(Deterministic paths versus stochastic regimes)
.

1
treats
Z
as a given piecewise-constant signal. This is the framework for
well-posedness, resolvent bounds, and the fixed-regime spectral criteria: all estimates are
pathwise and can be concatenated over the observed switch times. The probabilistic statements in

Section

5
add an extra assumption on the law of the residence time in the unfavorable
regime, typically an exponential residence time with rate
\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}
. Thus no stochastic
assumption on
Z
is used until the quenched-tail theorem; before that point the theory is
conditional on a prescribed switching path.

3.1
Mild and strong solutions for a fixed regime

Fix a regime
z
and forcing
F_{z}\in C([0,\infty);\mathcal{X})
. A function
U\in C([0,T];\mathcal{X})
is a

mild solution
of

\dot{U}(t)=\mathcal{B}U(t)+(\mathcal{V}_{z}U)(t)+F_{z}(t),\qquad U(0)=U_{0},

(8)

if it satisfies the variation-of-constants identity

U(t)=e^{t\mathcal{B}}U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\mathcal{B}}\big[(\mathcal{V}_{z}U)(s)+F_{z}(s)\big]\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\in[0,T].

(9)

It is a
strong solution
if in addition
U\in C^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{X})
and (
8
)
holds pointwise.

Theorem 3.2

(Global well-posedness, fixed regime)
.

Under
1
, for every
U_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
, every
F_{z}\in C([0,\infty);\mathcal{X})
, and every

T>0
, (
8
) has a unique mild solution
U\in C([0,T];\mathcal{X})
. If

U_{0}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{B})=\mathcal{X}
and
F_{z}\in C^{1}
, the solution is strong. Moreover the solution map

U_{0}\mapsto U
is Lipschitz from
\mathcal{X}
to
C([0,T];\mathcal{X})
.

Proof.

Step 1: contraction on a short interval.
On
C([0,\tau];\mathcal{X})
with the supremum norm

\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{[0,\tau]}\left\lVert U(t)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}
, define

(\Phi U)(t):=e^{t\mathcal{B}}U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\mathcal{B}}\big[(\mathcal{V}_{z}U)(s)+F_{z}(s)\big]\,\mathrm{d}s.

For
U,V\in C([0,\tau];\mathcal{X})
, using
\left\lVert e^{(t-s)\mathcal{B}}\right\rVert\leq 1
(
Proposition

2.2
) and

Lemma

2.4
,

\left\lVert(\Phi U)(t)-(\Phi V)(t)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert(\mathcal{V}_{z}(U-V))(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{s}\!g_{z}(s-r)\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\,\left\lVert U(r)-V(r)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s.

Bounding
\left\lVert U-V\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\left\lVert U-V\right\rVert_{\infty}
and using

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}g_{z}(s-r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\leq t\,G_{z}
gives

\left\lVert\Phi U-\Phi V\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\,G_{z}\,\tau\,\left\lVert U-V\right\rVert_{\infty}.

(10)

Choose
\tau_{\ast}<(\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert G_{z})^{-1}
(any
\tau_{\ast}>0
if
\mathcal{A}_{z}=0
). Then

\Phi
is a contraction on
C([0,\tau_{\ast}];\mathcal{X})
, with a unique fixed point by Banach’s theorem,
which is the unique mild solution on
[0,\tau_{\ast}]
.

Step 2: global continuation.
The contraction constant in (
10
) depends
only on
\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert
,
G_{z}
, and the interval length, not on
U_{0}
or the initial time.
Hence the local solution extends by the same argument on
[\tau_{\ast},2\tau_{\ast}]
, and inductively
to
[0,T]
in finitely many steps of equal length
\tau_{\ast}
. Uniqueness on
[0,T]
follows from
uniqueness on each subinterval.

Step 3: strong solution and regularity.
If
U_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and
F_{z}\in C^{1}
, the right-hand side
of (
9
) is differentiable in
t
:
e^{t\mathcal{B}}U_{0}
is
C^{1}
since
\mathcal{B}
is bounded; the
forcing convolution
\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\mathcal{B}}F_{z}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
is
C^{1}
by Leibniz; and the memory term

\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{V}_{z}U)(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
is
C^{1}
because
s\mapsto(\mathcal{V}_{z}U)(s)
is continuous
(indeed
(\mathcal{V}_{z}U)(0)=0
and
g_{z}\in L^{1}
). Differentiating (
9
) recovers
(
8
) pointwise, so
U
is strong.

Step 4: Lipschitz dependence.
For two initial data with the same forcing, the difference

D=U-\tilde{U}
satisfies
D(t)=e^{t\mathcal{B}}(U_{0}-\tilde{U}_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{V}_{z}D)(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
,
so
\left\lVert D(t)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\left\lVert U_{0}-\tilde{U}_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}+\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\int_{0}^{t}G_{z}\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}s
.
A Grönwall argument on
[0,T]
gives

\left\lVert D\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq e^{\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert G_{z}T}\left\lVert U_{0}-\tilde{U}_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}
, the Lipschitz bound.
∎

3.2
Sharp a priori bound and subcritical stability

Theorem 3.3

(Uniform bound in the subcritical regime)
.

Suppose the branching ratio
\rho_{z}=\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert G_{z}
satisfies
\sqrt{2}\,\rho_{z}<c_{\ast}
, where

c_{\ast}=\min\{c_{x},c_{\lambda}\}
. Then every mild solution of (
8
) with

F_{z}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathcal{X})
obeys

\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\lVert U(t)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\frac{\left\lVert U_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}+c_{\ast}^{-1}\left\lVert F_{z}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{1-\sqrt{2}\,\rho_{z}/c_{\ast}}.

(11)

Proof.

Let
\phi(t)=\left\lVert U(t)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}
. From (
9
),
\left\lVert e^{(t-s)\mathcal{B}}\right\rVert\leq e^{-c_{\ast}(t-s)}

(
Proposition

2.2
) and
Lemma

2.4
with

\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{2}\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert
,

\phi(t)\leq e^{-c_{\ast}t}\left\lVert U_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{\ast}(t-s)}\Big[\sqrt{2}\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert\!\int_{0}^{s}g_{z}(s-r)\phi(r)\,\mathrm{d}r+\left\lVert F_{z}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\Big]\mathrm{d}s.

The forcing term is bounded by
c_{\ast}^{-1}\left\lVert F_{z}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
since

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{\ast}(t-s)}\mathrm{d}s\leq c_{\ast}^{-1}
. Set
M=\sup_{[0,T]}\phi
(finite by

Theorem

3.2
). Bounding
\phi(r)\leq M
in the memory term and using

\int_{0}^{s}g_{z}(s-r)\,\mathrm{d}r\leq G_{z}
followed by
\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{\ast}(t-s)}\mathrm{d}s\leq c_{\ast}^{-1}
,

M\leq\left\lVert U_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}+c_{\ast}^{-1}\left\lVert F_{z}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\sqrt{2}\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert\,G_{z}\,c_{\ast}^{-1}\,M=\left\lVert U_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}+c_{\ast}^{-1}\left\lVert F_{z}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\sqrt{2}\rho_{z}}{c_{\ast}}\,M.

Since
\sqrt{2}\rho_{z}<c_{\ast}
, the coefficient
\sqrt{2}\rho_{z}/c_{\ast}<1
and rearranging gives

M\leq(1-\sqrt{2}\rho_{z}/c_{\ast})^{-1}(\left\lVert U_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}+c_{\ast}^{-1}\left\lVert F_{z}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}})
, uniformly in

T
; letting
T\to\infty
yields the claim.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The threshold
\sqrt{2}\,\rho_{z}<c_{\ast}
has the right scaling: the destabilizing feedback
strength
\sqrt{2}\rho_{z}
must be dominated by the dissipation
c_{\ast}
. The factor
\sqrt{2}
is
the price of the off-diagonal feedback in (
6
); for the diagonal excitation

\mathcal{A}_{z}=\diag(0,A_{z})
it is absent and the threshold is
\rho_{z}<c_{\lambda}
, exactly the
modal threshold of
Section

4
. The modal analysis removes the remaining slack entirely,
replacing the norm
\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert
by the sharp modewise gains.

3.3
The fractional resolvent family

Theorem 3.5

(Volterra resolvent family)
.

For each regime
z
there is a unique strongly continuous family

\{\mathcal{R}_{z}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
with
\mathcal{R}_{z}(0)=\mathrm{I}
such that, for all
U_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and

F_{z}\in C([0,\infty);\mathcal{X})
, the mild solution of (
8
) is

U(t)=\mathcal{R}_{z}(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{R}_{z}(t-s)F_{z}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(12)

There exist
C\geq 1
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
with
\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{z}(t)\right\rVert\leq Ce^{\gamma t}
; one may take any

\gamma>\gamma_{z}^{\ast}:=\inf\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}:\ \xi\mapsto(\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B}-\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\mathcal{A}_{z})^{-1}\ \text{is bounded analytic on}\ \operatorname{Re}\xi>\omega\}
.

Proof.

Existence via Laplace inversion.
Taking the formal Laplace transform of
(
8
) with
F_{z}=0
gives

\xi\,\widehat{U}(\xi)-U_{0}=\mathcal{B}\widehat{U}(\xi)+\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\mathcal{A}_{z}\widehat{U}(\xi)
, i.e.

\widehat{U}(\xi)=H_{z}(\xi)U_{0},\qquad H_{z}(\xi):=\big(\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B}-\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\,\mathcal{A}_{z}\big)^{-1}.

(13)

We show
H_{z}(\xi)
is well defined and analytic for
\operatorname{Re}\xi
large. By
Lemma

2.3
,

\left\lvert\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\right\rvert\leq G_{z}
on
\operatorname{Re}\xi>0
, so
\left\lVert\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{2}\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert G_{z}=\sqrt{2}\rho_{z}
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is bounded with

\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\mathcal{B}U,U\right\rangle\leq-c_{\ast}\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{2}
, for
\operatorname{Re}\xi>\left\lVert\mathcal{B}\right\rVert
the operator

\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B}
is boundedly invertible with
\left\lVert(\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B})^{-1}\right\rVert\leq(\operatorname{Re}\xi+c_{\ast})^{-1}

(numerical-range bound). Hence for
\operatorname{Re}\xi
large enough that

\sqrt{2}\rho_{z}\,(\operatorname{Re}\xi+c_{\ast})^{-1}<1
, a Neumann series gives

H_{z}(\xi)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\big[(\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B})^{-1}\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\mathcal{A}_{z}\big]^{k}(\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B})^{-1},

(14)

convergent in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
and analytic in
\xi
on this half-plane. The map
\xi\mapsto H_{z}(\xi)

is a bounded analytic
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
-valued function there and satisfies the resolvent growth bound

\left\lVert H_{z}(\xi)\right\rVert\leq[(\operatorname{Re}\xi+c_{\ast})(1-\sqrt{2}\rho_{z}(\operatorname{Re}\xi+c_{\ast})^{-1})]^{-1}=O(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{-1})
.
By the generation theorem for Volterra equations of scalar type with bounded operators
(
[
17
, Ch. I]
; in finite dimension this is the inverse Laplace transform of a
rational-in-
\widehat{g}_{z}
symbol),
H_{z}
is the Laplace transform of a unique strongly continuous
family
\mathcal{R}_{z}(t)
with
\mathcal{R}_{z}(0)=\mathrm{I}
, namely the Bromwich integral

\mathcal{R}_{z}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}e^{\xi t}H_{z}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi
on a vertical contour
\Gamma
to
the right of
\gamma_{z}^{\ast}
.

Representation.
For
U_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
,
t\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{z}(t)U_{0}
solves (
8
) with

F_{z}=0
by construction (its Laplace transform is (
13
), and Laplace
transform is injective on continuous functions of exponential growth). For general
F_{z}
, the
function defined by (
12
) has Laplace transform

H_{z}(\xi)U_{0}+H_{z}(\xi)\widehat{F}_{z}(\xi)=H_{z}(\xi)(U_{0}+\widehat{F}_{z}(\xi))
, which solves the
transformed equation; inverting gives the mild solution, and uniqueness follows from

Theorem

3.2
. The growth bound
\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{z}(t)\right\rVert\leq Ce^{\gamma t}
for
\gamma>\gamma_{z}^{\ast}

is the standard consequence of analyticity and decay of
H_{z}
on
\operatorname{Re}\xi>\gamma_{z}^{\ast}
via contour
shifting.
∎

3.4
Continuity across regime switches

The global solution of (
1
) is built by concatenation: on
[t_{k},t_{k+1})
it solves
the fixed-regime equation for
z=Z(t_{k})
with initial datum
U(t_{k})
and with the
full past

entering the memory integral. The next lemma shows no jump is introduced.

Lemma 3.6

(Continuity at switches)
.

Under
1
, the concatenated mild solution
U
of (
1
) belongs to

C([0,T];\mathcal{X})
; in particular
\lim_{t\uparrow t_{k}}U(t)=\lim_{t\downarrow t_{k}}U(t)=U(t_{k})
at every
switching time.

Proof.

On
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
,
U
is continuous by
Theorem

3.2
applied to regime
Z(t_{k-1})
with
the memory integral
\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)\mathcal{A}U(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
taken over the entire history
[0,t]
;
the history is a fixed continuous datum on this interval, so the variation-of-constants map is
still a contraction and yields a continuous solution up to and including
t_{k}
. At
t_{k}
the regime
switches to
Z(t_{k})
; the new evolution starts from
U(t_{k})
and again includes the full past in
its memory term. Since the representation (
12
) for the new regime satisfies

\mathcal{R}_{Z(t_{k})}(0)=\mathrm{I}
and the forcing/memory integrals vanish as
t\downarrow t_{k}
, we get

\lim_{t\downarrow t_{k}}U(t)=U(t_{k})=\lim_{t\uparrow t_{k}}U(t)
. The memory term is continuous across

t_{k}
because
g_{z}\in L^{1}
and the integrand is bounded, so no compatibility condition is needed.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Lemma

3.6
is what makes (
1
) a genuine piecewise-deterministic evolution
with memory: switching deforms the operator pair instantaneously but the state and its entire
history vary continuously. This is the analytic underpinning of the regime-dependent spectral
“deformation” studied next, and it is exactly the structure absent from single-regime
Volterra/Hawkes scaling-limit theory.

4
A sharp modal stability criterion

4.1
Modal decomposition

Assumption 2

(Simultaneous diagonalization)
.

For the regime
z
under consideration,
A_{z}
commutes with the graph Laplacian
L
:

A_{z}L=LA_{z}
. Equivalently,
A_{z}
and
L
share a common orthonormal eigenbasis

\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
,
Lv_{j}=\ell_{j}v_{j}
with
0=\ell_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\ell_{n}
,
A_{z}v_{j}=a_{z,j}v_{j}
.

This holds, in particular, whenever
A_{z}=\alpha_{z}\mathrm{I}+\beta_{z}L+\cdots
is a polynomial in
L

(scalar or diffusive excitation), and is the natural setting in which a clean modal criterion can
be expected. The noncommuting case is treated in
Proposition

4.4
.

Under
2
, project (
8
) (with
F_{z}=0
) onto mode
v_{j}
. Writing

x(t)=\sum_{j}x_{j}(t)v_{j}
,
\lambda(t)=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}(t)v_{j}
, the dynamics decouples into the scalar
Volterra systems

\dot{x}_{j}=-(c_{x}+\kappa\ell_{j})x_{j},\qquad\dot{\lambda}_{j}=-c_{\lambda}\lambda_{j}+a_{z,j}\!\int_{0}^{t}g_{z}(t-s)\big(x_{j}(s)+\lambda_{j}(s)\big)\mathrm{d}s.

(15)

Define the
modal fractional branching ratio
and the modal characteristic function

\rho_{z,j}:=\left\lvert a_{z,j}\right\rvert\,G_{z},\qquad D_{z,j}(\xi):=\xi+c_{\lambda}-a_{z,j}\,\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi).

(16)

The threshold constant is
c_{\lambda}
, the intensity damping. We write
c_{\lambda}
for it
throughout; in the normalization
c_{\lambda}=1
one recovers the classical Hawkes threshold

\rho=1
.

4.2
The trichotomy

The sharp “if and only if” criterion requires the excitation gains to be sign-definite, the
natural Hawkes setting; for indefinite gains only the sufficient direction survives (see

Remark

4.2
). We also distinguish the decay
rate
according to the kernel class, since
completely monotone kernels need not yield exponential decay without an exponential moment.

Assumption 3

(Nonnegative gains and kernel class)
.

The modal gains satisfy
a_{z,j}\geq 0
for all
j
. Moreover the kernel falls in one of:

(C1)

Exponential moment:

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\eta t}g_{z}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
for some

\eta>0
(e.g. exponential or tempered kernels), so
\widehat{g}_{z}
extends analytically to

\operatorname{Re}\xi>-\eta
;

(C2)

Heavy integrable (no exponential moment):

g_{z}\in L^{1}
but
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\eta t}g_{z}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty
for every
\eta>0
, with a singular low-frequency expansion

\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)=G_{z}-c\,\xi^{\beta}+o(\xi^{\beta})
as
\xi\downarrow 0
for some

\beta\in(0,1)
. A canonical
L^{1}
example is the Mittag-Leffler density

g_{z}(t)=t^{\beta-1}E_{\beta,\beta}(-t^{\beta})
(completely monotone, mass
G_{z}=1
), for which

\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)=\frac{1}{1+\xi^{\beta}}=1-\xi^{\beta}+o(\xi^{\beta})\quad(\xi\downarrow 0),

matching the singular expansion above with
G_{z}=1
,
c=1
; equivalently, any tempered kernel
modified to retain a fractional singularity at the origin while keeping finite mass. We emphasize
that the pure power
t^{-\alpha}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)
is
not
admissible here, as it violates
the finite mass requirement
(K2)
; the singular behavior must come from the low-frequency
expansion of an
L^{1}
kernel.

Theorem 4.1

(Modal stability criterion)
.

Under
1
,
2
, and
3
, fix a mode
j
and consider
the homogeneous modal system (
15
). The
x_{j}
-component always decays as

e^{-(c_{x}+\kappa\ell_{j})t}
. For the
\lambda_{j}
-component, with
\rho_{z,j}=a_{z,j}G_{z}
:

(i)

(Subcritical)
If
\rho_{z,j}<c_{\lambda}
, then
D_{z,j}(\xi)\neq 0
for all

\operatorname{Re}\xi\geq 0
, and the mode is stable. Under
(C1)
the decay is exponential: if the intensity
symbol is zero-free in a strip
\operatorname{Re}\xi\geq-\omega_{\lambda,j}
, then for every

0<\omega_{j}<\min\{\omega_{\lambda,j},c_{x}+\kappa\ell_{j}\}
there is
M_{j}\geq 1
such that

\left\lvert\lambda_{j}(t)\right\rvert\leq M_{j}e^{-\omega_{j}t}(\left\lvert x_{j}(0)\right\rvert+\left\lvert\lambda_{j}(0)\right\rvert)
. Under
(C2)
the
decay is algebraic, governed by the low-frequency expansion:

\left\lvert\lambda_{j}(t)\right\rvert=O(t^{-\beta-1})
as
t\to\infty
.

(ii)

(Critical)
If
\rho_{z,j}=c_{\lambda}
, then
D_{z,j}(0)=0
is the rightmost
singularity. Under
(C1)
(
\widehat{g}_{z}
analytic at
0
) the zero is simple with

D_{z,j}^{\prime}(0)>0
, and the mode has a neutral zero-frequency component: for generic initial data it
relaxes to a finite nonzero constant rather than to zero. Under
(C2)
the singular expansion
yields algebraic relaxation
\left\lvert\lambda_{j}(t)\right\rvert\sim C\,t^{-(1-\beta)}
.

(iii)

(Supercritical)
If
\rho_{z,j}>c_{\lambda}
, then
D_{z,j}
has a unique positive
real zero
\xi_{j}^{\ast}>0
. This zero is the rightmost singularity of the modal resolvent, and there
exist initial data for which
\left\lvert\lambda_{j}(t)\right\rvert\gtrsim e^{\xi_{j}^{\ast}t}
; the mode is unstable.

Proof.

The
x_{j}
equation is scalar linear with rate
c_{x}+\kappa\ell_{j}>0
, giving the stated decay, and
feeds the
\lambda_{j}
equation only through an exponentially decaying forcing. The stability of

\lambda_{j}
is therefore determined by the zeros of

D_{z,j}(\xi)=\xi+c_{\lambda}-a_{z,j}\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)
.

(i)
Assume
a_{z,j}G_{z}<c_{\lambda}
. For
\operatorname{Re}\xi\geq 0
,
Lemma

2.3
gives

\left\lvert a_{z,j}\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\right\rvert\leq a_{z,j}G_{z}<c_{\lambda}\leq\left\lvert\xi+c_{\lambda}\right\rvert,

so
D_{z,j}(\xi)\neq 0
in the closed right half-plane. Under
(C1)
,
\widehat{g}_{z}
extends
analytically to
\operatorname{Re}\xi>-\eta
. Since the finitely many zeros of
D_{z,j}
cannot accumulate in a
compact subset and none lie on
\operatorname{Re}\xi\geq 0
, a small leftward strip

\operatorname{Re}\xi\geq-\omega_{\lambda,j}
is also zero-free. Shifting the inverse Laplace contour gives
exponential decay for the homogeneous intensity resolvent. The forcing inherited from
x_{j}
decays
at rate
c_{x}+\kappa\ell_{j}
, so the observed decay of
\lambda_{j}
is bounded by any rate below

\min\{\omega_{\lambda,j},c_{x}+\kappa\ell_{j}\}
. Under
(C2)
, the
rightmost obstruction is the branch point at the origin. Expanding

D_{z,j}(\xi)=c_{\lambda}-a_{z,j}G_{z}+\xi+a_{z,j}c\xi^{\beta}+o(\xi^{\beta})
and inverting around the
branch cut gives the Tauberian decay
O(t^{-\beta-1})
.

(ii)
If
a_{z,j}G_{z}=c_{\lambda}
, then
D_{z,j}(0)=0
. For
\operatorname{Re}\xi>0
the strict inequality

\left\lvert a_{z,j}\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\right\rvert<c_{\lambda}\leq\left\lvert\xi+c_{\lambda}\right\rvert
excludes all right-half-plane
zeros, so the rightmost singularity is at
0
. Under
(C1)
,

D_{z,j}^{\prime}(0)=1-a_{z,j}\widehat{g}_{z}^{\prime}(0)>0
, hence the zero is simple and the residue gives a
finite neutral component. Under
(C2)
,

D_{z,j}(\xi)=\xi+a_{z,j}c\xi^{\beta}+o(\xi^{\beta})\sim a_{z,j}c\xi^{\beta}
, whose inverse transform
has order
t^{\beta-1}=t^{-(1-\beta)}
.

(iii)
If
a_{z,j}G_{z}>c_{\lambda}
, then
D_{z,j}(0)<0
and
D_{z,j}(\xi)\to+\infty
as

\xi\to\infty
on the positive real axis; strict monotonicity on
(0,\infty)
gives a unique
positive zero
\xi_{j}^{\ast}
. This zero is rightmost. Indeed, if
D_{z,j}(\xi)=0
and

\sigma=\operatorname{Re}\xi>\xi_{j}^{\ast}
, then

\sigma+c_{\lambda}\leq\left\lvert\xi+c_{\lambda}\right\rvert=a_{z,j}\left\lvert\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\right\rvert\leq a_{z,j}\widehat{g}_{z}(\sigma)<a_{z,j}\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi_{j}^{\ast})=\xi_{j}^{\ast}+c_{\lambda},

a contradiction. Thus no zero lies to the right of
\xi_{j}^{\ast}
, and the residue at the simple
real zero yields growth
e^{\xi_{j}^{\ast}t}
for data with nonzero projection on the corresponding
mode.
∎

Remark 4.2

(Signed gains)
.

If some
a_{z,j}<0
, the quantity
\left\lvert a_{z,j}\right\rvert G_{z}<c_{\lambda}
remains
sufficient
for
stability (the bound
\left\lvert a_{z,j}\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert a_{z,j}\right\rvert G_{z}
used in (i) does not
require sign-definiteness), but it need not be necessary, since a negative gain is stabilizing and

D_{z,j}
may have no right-half-plane zero even when
\left\lvert a_{z,j}\right\rvert G_{z}\geq c_{\lambda}
. The sharp
“if and only if” therefore holds under the Hawkes-type hypothesis
a_{z,j}\geq 0
of

3
; for indefinite gains we retain only the sufficient norm/spectral-radius
condition of
Proposition

4.4
.

Corollary 4.3

(Global stability via the spectral supremum)
.

Under
2
and
3
, the homogeneous evolution (
1
) for a
constant regime
z
is globally asymptotically stable if and only if

\rho_{z}^{\max}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{z,j}\,G_{z}<c_{\lambda}.

(17)

Proof.

By
Theorem

4.1
, each decoupled mode is asymptotically stable precisely when

\rho_{z,j}<c_{\lambda}
; under
(C1)
the convergence is exponential, whereas under
(C2)

the subcritical convergence can be algebraic. Since only finitely many modes are present, the
slowest stable mode dictates the global rate, so asymptotic stability holds iff the maximum modal
branching ratio is below
c_{\lambda}
. If some
\rho_{z,j}\geq c_{\lambda}
the corresponding mode is
critical or unstable, precluding global asymptotic stability.
∎

4.3
The noncommuting case and topology independence

Proposition 4.4

(Sufficient stability without commutativity)
.

Assume, in addition, that the kernel is of class
(C1)
. Without
2
, the
homogeneous evolution is globally exponentially stable if

\sqrt{2}\,\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert\,G_{z}<c_{\ast},\qquad c_{\ast}=\min\{c_{x},c_{\lambda}\}.

(18)

In the diagonal-excitation case
\mathcal{A}_{z}=\diag(0,A_{z})
, where the feedback acts only on the
intensity block damped at rate
c_{\lambda}
, the condition sharpens to
\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert\,G_{z}<c_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

From (
14
),
H_{z}(\xi)=(\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B}-\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\mathcal{A}_{z})^{-1}
is analytic
and uniformly bounded on
\operatorname{Re}\xi\geq 0
provided
\left\lVert\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\,\left\lVert(\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B})^{-1}\right\rVert<1
there. By
Lemma

2.3
,

\left\lVert\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\mathcal{A}_{z}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{2}\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert G_{z}
, and by
Proposition

2.2

the numerical-range bound gives
\left\lVert(\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B})^{-1}\right\rVert\leq(\operatorname{Re}\xi+c_{\ast})^{-1}\leq c_{\ast}^{-1}

on
\operatorname{Re}\xi\geq 0
. Hence the product is bounded by
\sqrt{2}\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert G_{z}/c_{\ast}
, which is
<1
exactly
under (
18
); then
H_{z}
has no singularity in
\operatorname{Re}\xi\geq 0
and is
O(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{-1})
,
and the exponential-moment hypothesis permits a Bromwich-contour shift slightly left of the
imaginary axis, giving
\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{z}(t)\right\rVert\leq Ce^{-\omega t}
and global exponential stability. In the diagonal case

\mathcal{A}_{z}=\diag(0,A_{z})
acts only on the
\lambda
-block, where
(\xi\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{B})^{-1}
restricts
to
(\xi+c_{\lambda})^{-1}
and the
\sqrt{2}
disappears, giving
\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert G_{z}<c_{\lambda}
.
∎

The norm bound (
18
) is conservative for nonnormal
A_{z}
, since

\left\lVert A_{z}\right\rVert
can far exceed the spectral radius
r(A_{z})
. The next proposition gives the sharp
spectral threshold for the intensity block under the nonnegativity hypothesis of

3
, and is the result the computational study of
Section

7
verifies.

Proposition 4.5

(Spectral criterion for the intensity block)
.

Consider the intensity-block evolution
\dot{\lambda}(t)=-c_{\lambda}\lambda(t)+\int_{0}^{t}g_{z}(t-s)A_{z}\lambda(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
with
A_{z}\geq 0
entrywise and kernel class
(C1)
. Let
r(A_{z})
be the
Perron–Frobenius spectral radius of
A_{z}
. Then the block is globally exponentially stable if and
only if

r(A_{z})\,G_{z}<c_{\lambda}.

(19)

Proof.

The Laplace symbol is
D_{z}(\xi)=(\xi+c_{\lambda})\mathrm{I}-\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)A_{z}
. A singularity occurs iff

\xi+c_{\lambda}=\mu\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)
for some
\mu\in\sigma(A_{z})
. Since
A_{z}\geq 0
, the
Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue
r(A_{z})
is real, nonnegative, and
\left\lvert\mu\right\rvert\leq r(A_{z})
for every

\mu\in\sigma(A_{z})
.

If
r(A_{z})G_{z}<c_{\lambda}
and
\operatorname{Re}\xi\geq 0
, then for every eigenvalue
\mu
,

\left\lvert\mu\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)\right\rvert\leq r(A_{z})G_{z}<c_{\lambda}\leq\left\lvert\xi+c_{\lambda}\right\rvert,

so
D_{z}(\xi)
is invertible throughout the closed right half-plane. Because
\widehat{g}_{z}
is
analytic in a left half-plane under
(C1)
, a contour shift gives exponential stability. Conversely,
if
r(A_{z})G_{z}>c_{\lambda}
, the scalar equation

\xi+c_{\lambda}-r(A_{z})\widehat{g}_{z}(\xi)=0
has a positive real root by the monotonicity argument in

Theorem

4.1
, so the block is unstable. At equality,
\xi=0
is a neutral singularity and
asymptotic stability fails. Jordan blocks can only multiply the inverse Laplace terms by
polynomials and do not move the exponential abscissa. Hence (
19
) is necessary
and sufficient.
∎

Corollary 4.6

(Topology independence under scalar excitation)
.

Suppose
A_{z}=a_{z}\mathrm{I}
is scalar (the same excitation gain at every node). Then the stability
threshold is
\left\lvert a_{z}\right\rvert G_{z}<c_{\lambda}
,
independent of the graph
G
: the bifurcation point
does not depend on the network topology, only the modal decay
rates
do (through

c_{x}+\kappa\ell_{j}
).

Proof.

If
A_{z}=a_{z}\mathrm{I}
then
a_{z,j}=a_{z}
for every mode, so
\rho_{z,j}=\left\lvert a_{z}\right\rvert G_{z}
is the same for
all
j
and (
17
) reads
\left\lvert a_{z}\right\rvert G_{z}<c_{\lambda}
, with no dependence on the
Laplacian spectrum
\{\ell_{j}\}
. The eigenvalues
\ell_{j}
enter only the
x_{j}
decay rates

c_{x}+\kappa\ell_{j}
and the modal relaxation, not the threshold.
∎

5
Finite-range power-law amplification under regime residence

The stability theory of
Section

4
concerns the
annealed
(deterministic,
ensemble) evolution: when
\rho_{z}^{\max}<c_{\lambda}
in every visited regime, the mean dynamics
is bounded. We now show that this is compatible with a
quenched
(pathwise) burst
observable exhibiting a power-law law over a finite range of magnitudes, generated by the
interaction of operator non-normality with regime residence. We are deliberately careful about the
nature of the claim: because the unfavorable operator is Hurwitz, the transient amplification it
produces is bounded, so the phenomenon is a
finite-range
power law—a heavy tail over a
band of burst sizes set by the residence horizon—rather than a true asymptotic (
b\to\infty
)
heavy tail. We make this precise below. The mechanism is nonetheless genuinely nonlinear in that
it is invisible to the spectral abscissa: it operates even when every regime operator is Hurwitz.

5.1
Setup and the logarithmic-norm exponent

Consider (
1
) reduced, on a fixed mode or after a linear change of variables, to a
finite-dimensional lifted system
\dot{X}=A_{Z(t)}X+\tilde{f}(t)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
A_{z}
is the
generator of regime
z
after the standard Markovian embedding of the completely monotone kernel
(for example, a finite sum-of-exponentials lift of
g_{z}
, exact for rational kernels and
convergent as the number of modes increases; see
[
1
]
). This realization
and the Euclidean inner product are fixed once and for all in this section. Since logarithmic norms
are invariant under orthogonal changes of coordinates but not under arbitrary similarities, the
quantity below is not claimed to be an invariant of the abstract kernel-realization class. Let

\mathcal{U}
denote a distinguished
unfavorable
regime and define its
logarithmic norm

(matrix measure)

\mu_{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}}):=\lambda_{\max}\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\big(A_{\mathcal{U}}+A_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}\big)\Big),

(20)

the largest eigenvalue of the symmetric part. We write
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}:=\mu_{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}})
. The central
point is that
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}
may be strictly positive while
A_{\mathcal{U}}
is Hurwitz
(
\alpha(A_{\mathcal{U}})<0
): this is exactly the regime of non-normal transient growth

[
20
]
. The finite-range exponent is therefore a computable
logarithmic-norm quantity of the chosen lifted realization.

Let
v_{\mathcal{U}}
be a unit eigenvector of
\tfrac{1}{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}}+A_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top})
for the eigenvalue
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}
.

Assumption 4

(Positive susceptibility)
.

\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}=\mu_{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}})>0
.

Assumption 5

(Cone-alignment event)
.

There exist
a\in(0,1]
, a horizon
T_{0}>0
, and
p_{0}>0
such that, for every residence duration

\tau\leq T_{0}
, the event

\mathcal{C}_{\tau}:=\Big\{Z\equiv\mathcal{U}\text{ on }[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau],\ v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}X(t)\geq a\,\left\lVert X(t)\right\rVert\ \forall t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]\Big\}

satisfies
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}_{\tau}\mid Z(t_{0})=\mathcal{U})\geq p_{0}
, uniformly in
\tau\leq T_{0}
.

Assumption 6

(Forcing injection along the cone)
.

There is
c_{f}>0
with
v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}\tilde{f}(t)\geq c_{f}
for a.e.
t\in[0,T_{0}]
.

5
replaces a deterministic “every burst aligns” postulate by a quantitative
positive-probability statement: with probability at least
p_{0}
, a residence in
\mathcal{U}
keeps the
trajectory in the amplifying cone of
v_{\mathcal{U}}
. It is non-circular and estimable as the conditional
frequency of cone-aligned residences; sufficient conditions for
p_{0}>0
are given in

Remark

5.5
.

5.2
Growth on a residence interval

Lemma 5.1

(Cone-projected growth)
.

Under
4
to
6
, on an
\mathcal{U}
-residence with the cone condition, the
projection
\zeta(t):=v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}X(t)
satisfies

\zeta(t_{0}+\tau)\geq e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau}\,\zeta(t_{0})+\frac{c_{f}}{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}}\big(e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau}-1\big),\qquad\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}:=\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}-\left\lVert A_{\mathcal{U}}\right\rVert\frac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{a},

(21)

and we assume the alignment
a
is close enough to
1
that
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}>0
.

Proof.

Decompose
X=\zeta\,v_{\mathcal{U}}+w
with
w\perp v_{\mathcal{U}}
. The cone condition
\zeta\geq a\left\lVert X\right\rVert
with

\left\lVert X\right\rVert^{2}=\zeta^{2}+\left\lVert w\right\rVert^{2}
gives
\left\lVert w\right\rVert\leq(\sqrt{1-a^{2}}/a)\,\zeta
. Differentiating and
using
v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}A_{\mathcal{U}}v_{\mathcal{U}}=\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}
(as
v_{\mathcal{U}}
is the top eigenvector of the symmetric part)
and
\left\lvert v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}A_{\mathcal{U}}w\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert A_{\mathcal{U}}\right\rVert\left\lVert w\right\rVert
,

\dot{\zeta}=v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}A_{\mathcal{U}}X+v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}\tilde{f}=\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}\zeta+v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}A_{\mathcal{U}}w+v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}\tilde{f}\geq\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}\zeta-\left\lVert A_{\mathcal{U}}\right\rVert\left\lVert w\right\rVert+c_{f}\geq\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}\zeta+c_{f}.

This scalar differential inequality
\dot{\zeta}\geq\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}\zeta+c_{f}
integrates, via
the integrating factor
e^{-\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}t}
, to (
21
).
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Norm lower bound on a residence)
.

Under
Lemma

5.1
, there are
c_{1},c_{2}>0
with

\left\lVert X(t_{0}+\tau)\right\rVert\geq c_{1}e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau}-c_{2}
for all
\tau\in[0,T_{0}]
.

Proof.

\left\lVert X\right\rVert\geq v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}X=\zeta
, and (
21
) gives

\zeta(t_{0}+\tau)\geq(\zeta(t_{0})+c_{f}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}})e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau}-c_{f}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}
. Set
c_{1}=\zeta(t_{0})+c_{f}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}>0
and

c_{2}=c_{f}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
∎

5.3
The power-law lower bound

Let
B_{T}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert X(t)\right\rVert
be the burst observable over horizon
T\geq T_{0}
.

Theorem 5.3

(Finite-range quenched power-law bound)
.

Suppose
4
to
6
hold with
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}>0
, the residence
times in
\mathcal{U}
are exponentially distributed with rate
\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}
, and
\mathbb{P}(Z(0)=\mathcal{U})>0
.
Then there exist
b_{0}>0
and
C_{-}>0
such that, for all
b
in the finite range

b_{0}\leq b\leq b_{\max}:=c_{1}e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}T_{0}}-c_{2},

(22)

the burst observable
B_{T}=\sup_{[0,T]}\left\lVert X(t)\right\rVert
satisfies

\mathbb{P}(B_{T}>b)\geq C_{-}\,b^{-\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}}.

(23)

The exponent is
\kappa=\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}
, equal to
\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}
only
in the perfectly aligned limit
a\to 1
. Since
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}\leq\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}=\mu_{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}})
,
the logarithmic norm gives the most optimistic instantaneous growth scale, whereas the realized
finite-band exponent can be steeper when trajectories rotate away from the maximally expanding
direction. The power law holds over the band
[b_{0},b_{\max}]
set by the residence horizon
T_{0}
;
it is
not
an asymptotic (
b\to\infty
) heavy tail, since the Hurwitz property caps
single-residence amplification at
b_{\max}
.

Proof.

Given
b\in[b_{0},b_{\max}]
, set
\tau_{b}:=\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}\,-1}\log\!\big((b+c_{2})/c_{1}\big)\leq T_{0}
(the upper bound is exactly (
22
)). By
Lemma

5.2
, on the event

\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{b}}\cap\{\tau(\mathcal{U})\geq\tau_{b}\}
a residence of length
\geq\tau_{b}
that stays
cone-aligned forces
\left\lVert X(t_{0}+\tau_{b})\right\rVert\geq b
, hence
B_{T}\geq b
. Therefore, using

5
(conditional probability
\geq p_{0}
of alignment), the exponential dwell law, and

\mathbb{P}(Z(0)=\mathcal{U})>0
,

\mathbb{P}(B_{T}>b)\geq\mathbb{P}(Z(0)=\mathcal{U})\,p_{0}\,e^{-\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}\tau_{b}}=\mathbb{P}(Z(0)=\mathcal{U})\,p_{0}\Big(\tfrac{c_{1}}{b+c_{2}}\Big)^{\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}}.

For
b\geq b_{0}
large enough that
b+c_{2}\leq 2b
, the right side is at least

C_{-}\,b^{-\kappa}
with
C_{-}=\mathbb{P}(Z(0)=\mathcal{U})\,p_{0}\,c_{1}^{\kappa}2^{-\kappa}
and

\kappa=\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}
. The aligned limit
a\to 1
sends

\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}\to\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}
.
∎

Remark 5.4

(Why finite-range, and why this is the honest statement)
.

Because
A_{\mathcal{U}}
is Hurwitz,
\left\lVert e^{tA_{\mathcal{U}}}\right\rVert
grows transiently to a finite peak gain and then
decays; a single residence can amplify a burst by at most the factor realized within the horizon

T_{0}
, capping the power law at
b_{\max}
in (
22
). The statement (
23
) is
thus a genuine power law over a band of magnitudes, not an asymptotic heavy tail. This distinction
is essential and we do not overstate it: the phenomenon is
residence-induced finite-range
power-law amplification
. A true asymptotic heavy tail would require either a non-Hurwitz
(marginally stable)
A_{\mathcal{U}}
with sustained growth, or unbounded residence times; both lie outside
the present hypotheses and are noted as extensions.

Remark 5.5

(How the cone-alignment probability is verified)
.

5
is a structural hypothesis, not an automatic consequence of nonnormality. For a
general Hurwitz nonnormal matrix, transient growth may rotate trajectories out of the cone

\{v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}X\geq a\left\lVert X\right\rVert\}
. In applications one may verify
p_{0}>0
either analytically, for a
specified entry distribution and forcing, or empirically by estimating the conditional frequency
of residences for which the cone inequality holds on
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]
. A convenient sufficient
condition in low-dimensional reductions is a positive gap in the symmetric part together with
forcing and entry states concentrated near
v_{\mathcal{U}}
; however, this sufficient condition is model
specific, and the theorem only assumes the verifiable probability lower bound stated in

5
.

Remark 5.6

(Why the exponent is logarithmic-norm based, not large-deviation)
.

The theorem uses the cone-corrected rate

\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}=\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}-\left\lVert A_{\mathcal{U}}\right\rVert\sqrt{1-a^{2}}/a
, with

\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}=\mu_{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}})
computed for the fixed lifted regime operator in the chosen
Euclidean coordinates. Thus the exponent is

\kappa=\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}}
; the simpler quotient

\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}/\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}
is the perfectly aligned limiting scale, not the generic finite-band
prediction. The distinction is important: the logarithmic norm is an instantaneous upper growth
scale, while a realized trajectory may rotate away from its maximally expanding direction and
therefore exhibit an effective rate below
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}
. The expression contains no action
functional and no small parameter, and it can be positive when
A_{\mathcal{U}}
is Hurwitz but nonnormal.
This is weaker and more precise than calling
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}
an invariant of the original kernel: two
minimal Markovian realizations related by a non-orthogonal similarity need not have the same
Euclidean logarithmic norm. What is invariant in the present theorem is the statement after the
realization and inner product are fixed; the logarithmic-norm scale is then a computable upper
anchor for the cone-corrected growth rate that feeds the residence-survival transform.

Remark 5.7

(State-defined residence)
.

When residence in
\mathcal{U}
is state-defined and no exponential law holds, (
23
) is replaced
by the residence-survival transform

\mathbb{P}(B_{T}>b)\gtrsim S_{\mathcal{U}}\!\big((\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{c}})^{-1}\log(b/C)\big)
, with

S_{\mathcal{U}}(t)=\mathbb{P}(\tau(\mathcal{U})>t)
the residence survival function, reducing to (
23
) when

S_{\mathcal{U}}(t)=e^{-\lambda_{\mathcal{U}}t}
. No Markov assumption on the physical residence statistics is needed.

5.4
Tail truncation under idealized contraction feedback

The exponent’s dependence on the cone-corrected rate, itself controlled above by
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}
, makes the finite-range power law actionable in systems
admitting actuation. We state this as a proposition under an explicitly idealized feedback law,
with all quantities defined formally; we do not claim a control theorem for general feedback.

Definition 5.8

(Idealized contraction feedback)
.

Fix a susceptibility threshold
s_{0}>0
and a contraction rate
\sigma>0
. The

idealized contraction feedback
replaces, instantaneously and for the remainder of any
residence in
\mathcal{U}
during which the indicator
S(t):=v_{\mathcal{U}}^{\top}X(t)/\left\lVert X(t)\right\rVert
exceeds
s_{0}
,
the operator
A_{\mathcal{U}}
by a modified operator
A_{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
with
\mu_{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime})\leq-\sigma<0
. The

controlled burst observable
is
B_{T}^{\prime}=\sup_{[0,T]}\left\lVert X^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert
for the closed-loop
trajectory
X^{\prime}
.

Proposition 5.9

(Per-residence amplification cap)
.

Assume, in addition to
Definition

5.8
, that the forcing and initial data are bounded
(
\left\lVert\tilde{f}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq F_{0}
,
\left\lVert X(0)\right\rVert\leq X_{0}
), that outside
\mathcal{U}
the dynamics is
globally dissipative (
\mu_{2}(A_{z})\leq-c_{\ast}<0
for
z\neq\mathcal{U}
), and that there is no
accumulation of amplification across distinct residences (each residence enters with norm bounded
by a fixed
X_{1}
determined by the dissipative inter-residence decay). If every residence in
\mathcal{U}

of duration exceeding a trigger time
\delta>0
activates the feedback, then the per-residence
amplification is capped: there is
b_{\mathrm{cap}}<\infty
, depending only on

(\gamma_{\mathcal{U}},\delta,\sigma,F_{0},X_{1})
, such that no controlled residence produces a burst exceeding

b_{\mathrm{cap}}
, and consequently
\mathbb{P}(B_{T}^{\prime}>b)=0
for
b>b_{\mathrm{cap}}
.

Proof.

Before feedback acts, the Euclidean logarithmic norm gives an upper differential inequality,
not a cone lower bound:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\lVert X^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}\left\lVert X^{\prime}\right\rVert+F_{0}\qquad(t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+\delta),

where
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}=\mu_{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}})
. Hence, for a residence entering with

\left\lVert X^{\prime}(t_{0})\right\rVert\leq X_{1}
,

\left\lVert X^{\prime}(t_{0}+\delta)\right\rVert\leq\begin{cases}e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}\delta}X_{1}+F_{0}\,\dfrac{e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}\delta}-1}{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}},&\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}>0,\\[5.16663pt]
X_{1}+F_{0}\delta,&\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}=0.\end{cases}

Once the feedback is active,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\lVert X^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq-\sigma\left\lVert X^{\prime}\right\rVert+F_{0},

so the norm cannot exceed the larger of its value at the trigger time and the forced equilibrium
scale
F_{0}/\sigma
. Therefore one may take

b_{\mathrm{cap}}=\max\!\left\{e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}\delta}X_{1}+F_{0}\frac{e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}\delta}-1}{\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}},\,\frac{F_{0}}{\sigma}\right\}

when
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}>0
(with the evident replacement
X_{1}+F_{0}\delta
when
\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}=0
). The
outside-regime dissipativity and the no-accumulation hypothesis ensure that every residence enters
with norm at most
X_{1}
, so the same cap applies to all controlled residences and to the global
supremum. Thus
\mathbb{P}(B_{T}^{\prime}>b)=0
for
b>b_{\mathrm{cap}}
.
∎

Remark 5.10

(Steeper finite-range law under delayed engagement)
.

If the feedback engages only after an additional delay
\delta^{\prime}>0
(the indicator must persist
before actuation), the achievable growth is realized over duration
\delta+\delta^{\prime}
rather than the
full horizon
T_{0}
. Heuristically this contracts the amplification band of
Theorem

5.3
and
rescales the effective exponent to
\kappa^{\prime}\approx\kappa\,T_{0}/(\delta+\delta^{\prime})>\kappa
, a steeper
finite-range power law on the controlled range. We state this as a heuristic rather than a theorem:
a rigorous exponent under delayed feedback requires modeling the joint law of residence durations
and trigger times, which lies outside the present scope. The robust and proven content is the
amplification cap of
Proposition

5.9
; the logarithmic-norm principle is that contracting

\mu_{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}})
bounds the finite-range amplification.

Remark 5.11
.

Proposition

5.9
is a statement about an idealized closed loop, not a control theorem for
realistic actuation; the indicator
S(t)
, the trigger threshold
s_{0}
, the contraction rate

\sigma
, and the controlled observable
B_{T}^{\prime}
are all defined in
Definition

5.8
. A genuine
control-theoretic treatment (output feedback, robustness, actuation constraints) is left to future
work; the present statement isolates the logarithmic-norm principle that contracting

\mu_{2}(A_{\mathcal{U}})
removes the finite-range tail.

6
Hydrodynamic limit: from microscopic Hawkes dynamics to the Volterra operator

We now justify the term “branching ratio” by deriving the deterministic intensity block of
(
1
) as the law-of-large-numbers limit of a self-exciting particle system. The point
of this section is not to develop the most general Hawkes limit theorem, but to make explicit the
scaling under which the same quantity
r(A_{z})G_{z}/c_{\lambda}
governs microscopic reproduction and
macroscopic Volterra stability.

6.1
Microscopic relaxing Hawkes–Volterra model

Fix a population scale
N\in\mathbb{N}
. At node
i
let
N_{i}^{(N)}
be a counting process with stochastic
intensity
N\bar{\Lambda}_{i}^{(N)}(t)
, where the per-capita activity
\bar{\Lambda}^{(N)}
evolves as

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bar{\Lambda}_{i}^{(N)}(t)=-c_{\lambda}\bar{\Lambda}_{i}^{(N)}(t)+c_{\lambda}\mu_{i,Z(t)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{Z(t),ij}\int_{0}^{t}g_{Z(t)}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{N}_{j}^{(N)}(s),\qquad\bar{N}_{j}^{(N)}:=\frac{1}{N}N_{j}^{(N)}.

(24)

The normalization is now transparent:
\mathrm{d}\bar{N}_{j}^{(N)}
is of order one on bounded time
intervals, so the excitation term in (
24
) remains order one. This is the
natural density-dependent scaling for aggregate self-exciting systems
[
8
]
. We assume

A_{z}\geq 0
, bounded baselines
\mu_{i,z}\geq 0
, nonnegative initial data, and a fixed piecewise
constant regime path satisfying
1
. For the stochastic-convolution estimate below
we assume
g_{z}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty)
; singular kernels are discussed in

Remark

6.2
.

Substituting the Doob–Meyer decomposition

\mathrm{d}\bar{N}_{j}^{(N)}(s)=\bar{\Lambda}_{j}^{(N)}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\mathrm{d}\bar{M}_{j}^{(N)}(s)
, where

\langle\bar{M}_{j}^{(N)}\rangle_{t}=N^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\Lambda}_{j}^{(N)}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
, gives the random
Volterra differential equation

\dot{\bar{\Lambda}}^{(N)}(t)=-c_{\lambda}\bar{\Lambda}^{(N)}(t)+c_{\lambda}\mu_{Z(t)}+\int_{0}^{t}g_{Z(t)}(t-s)A_{Z(t)}\bar{\Lambda}^{(N)}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+R^{(N)}(t),

(25)

with martingale convolution

R^{(N)}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g_{Z(t)}(t-s)A_{Z(t)}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{M}^{(N)}(s).

(26)

The deterministic candidate limit is therefore

\dot{\lambda}(t)=-c_{\lambda}\lambda(t)+c_{\lambda}\mu_{Z(t)}+\int_{0}^{t}g_{Z(t)}(t-s)A_{Z(t)}\lambda(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(27)

which is exactly the intensity block of (
1
) with forcing
F_{z}=(0,c_{\lambda}\mu_{z})

and with the state-feedback part suppressed.

6.2
The limit theorem

Assumption 7

(Mean-field subcriticality and initial convergence)
.

For every visited regime
z
,
r(A_{z})G_{z}<c_{\lambda}
, the baselines
\mu_{i,z}
and initial
activities are uniformly bounded in
N
, and the empirical initial intensity satisfies

\bar{\Lambda}^{(N)}(0)\to\lambda(0)
in probability.

Theorem 6.1

(Hydrodynamic limit)
.

Under
1
and
7
, for every
T>0
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big\lVert\bar{\Lambda}^{(N)}(t)-\lambda(t)\big\rVert\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0,

(28)

where
\lambda
is the unique solution of (
27
). Consequently, the same
branching threshold
r(A_{z})G_{z}<c_{\lambda}
that governs the deterministic spectral criterion also
governs the mean-field microscopic activity: above threshold the Perron mode has reproduction
number larger than the relaxation rate and produces the classical Hawkes instability

[
11
,
2
]
.

Proof.

Subtract (
27
) from (
25
) and write

e^{(N)}=\bar{\Lambda}^{(N)}-\lambda
. On any interval with fixed regime
z
,

\dot{e}^{(N)}(t)=-c_{\lambda}e^{(N)}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}g_{z}(t-s)A_{z}e^{(N)}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+R^{(N)}(t).

(29)

The subcriticality assumption gives a stable Volterra resolvent for the deterministic operator by

Proposition

4.5
. Hence, using
Theorem

3.5
on each regime interval and
concatenating across finitely many switches,

\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert e^{(N)}(t)\right\rVert\leq C_{T}\left(\left\lVert e^{(N)}(0)\right\rVert+\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert R^{(N)}(t)\right\rVert\right),

(30)

where
C_{T}
is independent of
N
.

It remains to estimate the stochastic convolution. Since the system is subcritical and baselines
are bounded, the standard Hawkes moment bound gives

\sup_{N}\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\sum_{i}\bar{\Lambda}_{i}^{(N)}(t)\leq C_{T}
; this follows by taking expectations in
(
25
) and applying the deterministic Volterra resolvent bound. For
fixed
t
, Itô isometry gives

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert R^{(N)}(t)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{C}{N}\int_{0}^{t}g_{z}(t-s)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\sum_{i}\bar{\Lambda}_{i}^{(N)}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{C_{T}\left\lVert g_{z}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}}{N}.

The process
t\mapsto R^{(N)}(t)
is a stochastic convolution rather than a martingale, because the
kernel depends on the upper time variable. The fixed-time Itô estimate is therefore upgraded to
a maximal estimate by the standard factorization method for stochastic convolutions

[
5
,
6
]
: for the kernel classes
(C1)
–
(C2)
considered here, using the mild time-regularity supplied by the Bernstein representation (or by approximation with smooth kernels), there is another constant
C_{T}
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert R^{(N)}(t)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{C_{T}\left\lVert g_{z}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}}{N}\longrightarrow 0.

(31)

No martingale property of
R^{(N)}
itself is used. Thus

\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert R^{(N)}(t)\right\rVert\to 0
in
L^{2}
and in probability. The initial convergence in
7
gives
e^{(N)}(0)\to 0
in probability; together with the stochastic-convolution bound, (
30
) gives the asserted convergence. The same
argument applies successively on each regime interval, with the continuity across switches supplied
by
Lemma

3.6
.
∎

Remark 6.2

(Singular kernels)
.

If
g_{z}(t)=t^{\alpha-1}e^{-\theta t}/\Gamma(\alpha)
with
\alpha\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
, then

g_{z}\notin L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
near the origin and (
31
) is not directly
available. A standard regularization replaces
g_{z}
by
g_{z}^{\epsilon}(t)=g_{z}(t+\epsilon)
, proves
the limit for fixed
\epsilon>0
, and then lets
\epsilon\downarrow 0
along a joint scaling such
that
N\int_{0}^{T}(g_{z}^{\epsilon}-g_{z})^{2}\to 0
. This preserves the deterministic Volterra limit while
separating the law-of-large-numbers argument from the singular short-time behavior.

Remark 6.3
.

Theorem

6.1
identifies the modal branching ratio with a microscopic reproduction number. In
the Perron mode of a nonnegative excitation matrix, the expected total memory-weighted offspring
is
r(A_{z})G_{z}
, while
c_{\lambda}
is the linear relaxation rate of activity. Thus macroscopic
modal instability is the deterministic image of microscopic supercriticality. The single-regime
scaling-limit theory of Hawkes processes
[
13
,
14
]
is recovered as a special
case, whereas the present formulation feeds directly into the operator stability criterion of

Section

4
.

7
Numerical validation

This section gives a reproducible numerical validation of the three main mechanisms developed in
the paper: the modal branching threshold, the loss of sharpness of norm-based bounds under
nonnormal noncommuting excitation, and the finite-range quenched amplification induced by
residence in a transiently amplifying regime. The purpose is not to introduce a new model class,
but to test the sharpness and observability of the analytic quantities appearing in

Theorems

4.1
and
5.3
and in
Propositions

4.4
and
4.5
. All simulations
use the same completely monotone tempered fractional kernel

g(t)=\frac{t^{\alpha-1}e^{-\theta t}}{\Gamma(\alpha)},\qquad 0<\alpha<1,\quad\theta>0,

(32)

whose Laplace transform and mass are

\widehat{g}(\xi)=(\xi+\theta)^{-\alpha},\qquad G:=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\theta^{-\alpha}.

(33)

This is a class
(C1)
kernel (it has the exponential moment
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\eta t}g(t)\mathrm{d}t<\infty
for
\eta<\theta
), so the subcritical decay is exponential and the critical case relaxes
to a constant. Unless otherwise stated, we set

\alpha=0.7,\qquad\theta=0.5,\qquad G=\theta^{-\alpha}\approx 1.6245,\qquad c_{\lambda}=1.

(34)

7.1
Scalar modal trichotomy

We first validate the scalar modal equation obtained from the commuting case after simultaneous
diagonalization. For a single mode, the intensity component obeys

\dot{\lambda}(t)=-c_{\lambda}\lambda(t)+a\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)\lambda(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\lambda(0)=1.

(35)

The modal branching ratio is
\rho=aG
, and the modal characteristic function is

D(\xi)=\xi+c_{\lambda}-a\widehat{g}(\xi)=\xi+c_{\lambda}-a(\xi+\theta)^{-\alpha}.

(36)

The modal criterion of
Theorem

4.1
predicts decay when
\rho<c_{\lambda}
, critical behavior
when
\rho=c_{\lambda}
, and exponential growth when
\rho>c_{\lambda}
. In the supercritical
case the asymptotic growth rate is the positive root
\xi_{*}>0
of
D(\xi_{*})=0
. We use

\rho\in\{0.65,1.00,1.25\}
with
a=\rho/G
, time horizon
T=60
, step
\Delta t=0.02
, and
midpoint-quadrature time stepping

\lambda_{m+1}=\lambda_{m}+\Delta t[-c_{\lambda}\lambda_{m}+a\sum_{k=0}^{m}w_{k}\lambda_{m-k}]
with

w_{k}=g((k+\tfrac{1}{2})\Delta t)\Delta t
. For
\rho=1.25
, solving
D(\xi_{*})=0
gives

\xi_{*}\approx 0.1001
, while a least-squares fit of
\log\left\lvert\lambda(t)\right\rvert
over the final part of
the trajectory gives
\widehat{\xi}\approx 0.0970
(
Figure

1
A), confirming that
the rightmost zero of (
36
) governs the unstable modal
growth.

7.2
Network stability diagnostics

We embed the modal criterion into a Watts–Strogatz small-world network
[
22
]
with
n=120

k=4

p=0.1

Commuting excitation.

Take

with
A_{0}=a_{0}\mathrm{I}+a_{1}L

a_{0}=0.10

a_{1}=0.05

s\geq 0

is a polynomial in

a_{j}(s)=s(a_{0}+a_{1}\ell_{j})

\rho_{j}(s)=a_{j}(s)G

modal threshold
\max_{j}\rho_{j}(s)<c_{\lambda}

s<s_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{modal}}:=\frac{c_{\lambda}}{G\max_{j}(a_{0}+a_{1}\ell_{j})}.

(37)

Because
A_{0}
is symmetric and diagonalized by the Laplacian eigenbasis, the norm-based condition

\left\lVert A_{\mathrm{c}}(s)\right\rVert G<c_{\lambda}
coincides with (
37
): in
the commuting case the modal threshold is sharp and the norm condition has no slack
(
Figure

1
B), confirming
Corollary

4.3
and illustrating

Corollary

4.6
.

Nonnormal noncommuting excitation.

We then choose a strongly nonnormal upper-triangular perturbation

A_{\mathrm{nn}}(s)=s(a_{0}\mathrm{I}+qK)
with
a_{0}=0.25
,
q=1.20
, where
K
is the one-step upper-shift
matrix (
K_{i,i+1}=1

0

does not commute with

nonnormal: for

(38)

The norm-based sufficient condition of
Proposition

4.4
certifies stability only for

s<s_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{norm}}:=c_{\lambda}/(\left\lVert A_{\mathrm{nn}}(1)\right\rVert_{2}G)\approx 0.4246

4.5
gives

bound (
Figure

nonnormal excitation: the rigorous general noncommuting statement remains the norm-based sufficient
condition of
Proposition

4.4
, while the spectral diagnostic of
Proposition

4.5

(valid under

7.3
Finite-range quenched amplification

Finally we validate the finite-range quenched amplification mechanism of

. Consider the Hurwitz but nonnormal matrix

A_{\mathcal{U}}=\begin{pmatrix}-0.10&3.00\\

8
Discussion

We have developed a rigorous theory for regime-dependent operator-valued Volterra equations with
completely monotone memory, establishing a fractional resolvent calculus, a sharp modal stability
criterion, a finite-range power-law amplification mechanism driven by operator non-normality, and a hydrodynamic
limit linking the deterministic intensity block to relaxing microscopic Hawkes dynamics. Three points situate the
results.

The modal criterion of
Theorem

4.1
is sharp—necessary and sufficient under simultaneous
diagonalization—and isolates the network modes responsible for instability, recovering the
norm-based sufficient condition as a corollary and exhibiting topology-independent criticality
under scalar excitation. The finite-range amplification of
Theorem

5.3
is, to our
knowledge, a new mechanism: it is anchored by the logarithmic norm of a Hurwitz but non-normal
operator under regime residence, while the actual exponent uses the cone-corrected finite-band
rate realized by aligned trajectories rather than a large-deviation rate functional
(
Remark

5.6
); its amplification is capped by logarithmic-norm contraction
(
Proposition

5.9
). The hydrodynamic limit of

Theorem

6.1
makes the branching-ratio terminology precise and ties macroscopic spectral
instability to microscopic explosion.

The theory provides a
computable
stability test for memory-coupled network dynamics: one
forms the regime operator
A_{z}
and kernel mass
G_{z}
, computes the modal branching ratios

\rho_{z,j}=a_{z,j}G_{z}
(or the spectral radius
r(A_{z})G_{z}
in the nonnormal nonnegative case),
and compares with the damping threshold
c_{\lambda}
. The criterion is sharp under commutativity
and topology-independent under scalar excitation, so the bifurcation can be located without
simulating the full nonlocal dynamics, and the unstable modes are identified explicitly. The
numerical study of
Section

7
shows that this modal/spectral test can certify stability
over a parameter range several times larger than a norm-based bound.

The work unifies three classically separate objects into a single resolvent-family framework: the
completely monotone Volterra resolvent calculus, the Hawkes branching ratio and its hydrodynamic
limit, and non-normal transient growth measured by the logarithmic norm. The bridge is that modal
branching ratios govern deterministic stability and microscopic branching explosion, while the
logarithmic norm of a fixed lifted realization, rather than the spectral abscissa, provides the
upper operator scale for pathwise finite-range amplification, with the realized cone correction
determining the observed finite-band exponent. This places memory-driven heavy-burst phenomena and
spectral stability within one logarithmic-norm/resolvent account.

The analysis is finite-dimensional (
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
); the infinite-dimensional node-space
extension is natural (the constants are dimension-free) but requires sectorial-generator
machinery and is left as future work. The finite-range amplification rests on the cone-alignment
hypothesis
5
, a positive-probability event whose sufficient conditions we give but
do not derive from first principles for a general system. The control statement

Proposition

5.9
is an
idealized
-feedback cap, not a robust control theorem. And the
hydrodynamic martingale estimate requires
g_{z}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, with the strongly singular
fractional case (
\alpha\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
) handled only by regularization (
Remark

6.2
). Each
of these is a concrete and tractable direction for sharpening the theory.

Further natural directions include sharp criticality analysis in the noncommuting case beyond the
sufficient condition of
Proposition

4.4
, and central-limit refinements of
Theorem

6.1

connecting to rough-volatility scaling limits
[
14
]
.
