Stability of the smooth Casselman-Jacquet functor
Kei Yuen Chan, Kaidi Wu, Jun Yu, Hongfeng Zhang

Abstract
. We establish and prove several results for the smooth Casselman-Jacquet submodule and quotient functors for real reductive groups. Other than exactness, surjectivity and globalization results, we establish a stability on the intersection of Jacquet subspaces. As an application, we establish a full version of the real Bernstein-Zelevinsky filtrations for smooth Fréchet representations of moderate growth rate.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The Casselman-Jacquet functor is developed in earlier time in
[
Ca78
]
, following the Harish-Chandra suggestive parallels in the
p
-adic and real worlds, or the so-called Lefschetz principle. The
p
-adic world is considerably simpler in both statements and proofs. One possible reason is on the topological difference:
p
-adic reductive groups are totally disconnected while real reductive groups are not.

The classical study of the Casselman-Jacquet functor mainly focuses on the Harish-Chandra modules. See, for example, some computations on principal series representations in
[
Ab08
]
, and also an application in the Casselman embedding theorem
[
CM82
]
. Nevertheless, there is also study of Jacquet modules from the work
[
AGS15a
,
AGS15b
]
some time ago.

The key inputs in this article are to overcome several topological difficulties to achieve some first properties of the Casselman-Jacquet functors. Our approach involves more extensive use of semi-norms, a fundamental nature of moderate growth rate representations (see
[
Ca89
,
BK14
]
).

This article is a part of the project
[
Ch23
,
CW26
]
towards a comprehensive mathematical proof on branching laws of real general linear groups, following the blueprint of
p
-adic general linear groups
[
Ch21
,
CS21
,
Ch23+
,
CP25+
]
. A mathematical proof becomes more important when we are stepping in the era of mathematical formalism in the twenty-first century. The main problem dealt with in this article is to obtain a comprehensive theory for the real Bernstein-Zelevinsky filtration. Our results complement
[
AGS15a
,
AGS15b
]
some time ago and in particular are for representations of moderate growth rate. The arguments such as showing the Hausdorffness is also quite different from
[
AGS15a
,
AGS15b
]
e.g. an application of the imprimitivity theorem from
[

. One may also see
[

1.2.
Main results

For a reductive Lie group
G
, let
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
be the category of smooth Fréchet representations of
G
with moderate growth rate.

Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
with the uniptoent radical
N
. Let
\mathfrak{n}=\mathrm{Lie}(N)
. Define two functors:

\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}:\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(P),\quad\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}(\pi):=\varprojlim\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi},

and

\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}:\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(P),\quad\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}(\pi):=\bigcap_{k\geq 0}\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}.

We emphasize that our Casselman-Jacquet functor is defined with closure. In other words, the quotient is automatically Hausdorff. The definition suffices for the representation-theoretic applications as long as it is computable.

The first main result of this article is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
.

(1)

The map from
\pi
to
\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}(\pi)
is surjective, and hence we have the following short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}(\pi)\rightarrow\pi\rightarrow\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}(\pi)\rightarrow 0.

(2)

For any epi morphism
f:\pi_{1}\rightarrow\pi_{2}
, the map
\mathbf{CJ}_{s,P}^{\infty}(f)
is also epi.

(3)

\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\mathbf{CJ}_{s,P}^{\infty}(\pi)}=\mathbf{CJ}_{s,P}^{\infty}(\pi)
.

Remark 1.2
.

The map from
\pi
to
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}(\pi)
behaves quite differently between the Harish-Chandra modules and modules in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
. In the Harish-Chandra module case,
\tau
is injective
[
CM82
, Corollary 8.19]
, rather than surjective. This difference arises some analytic behaviors from completeness.

Remark 1.3
.

We shall refer Theorem
1.1
(3) for the stability of the Casselman-Jacquet module.

The key part in (1) is the surjectivity, and the key of our proof is to construct a semi-norm on
\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}(\pi)
. The condition of moderate growth rate allows one to define a continuous action of the Schwartz algebra
\mathcal{S}(N)
associated to
N
on
\pi
. This defines a continuous map

\mathcal{S}(N)\widehat{\otimes}\pi\rightarrow\pi.

The classical Dixmier-Millivian theorem forces that the action is surjective. Now, by the open mapping theorem, one obtains the topology on
\pi
from the quotient topology and the tensor product topology.

Theorem 1.4
.

If we restrict the functors
\mathbf{CJ}_{s,P}^{\infty}
and
\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}
to the category of Casselman-Wallach representations of
G
, then both
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}
and
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}
are exact.

We remark that the exactness of
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}
does not directly come from the exactness of the Casselman-Jacquet functor
[
Ca78
]
because there is no immediate equivalence of categories for non-admissible representations.

A key aspect in the proof of the Casselman-Jacquet functor is a version of Artin-Rees lemma. The key statement can be stated as: Let
\sigma
be a subrepresentation of a Casselman-Wallach representation
\pi
of
G
. Let
N
be the unipotent subgroup of a parabolic subgroup of
G
, and let
\mathfrak{n}=\mathrm{Lie}(N)
. Then, there exists a sufficiently large integer
k
such that, for any
n>k
,

\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{n}.\pi}\cap\sigma\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{n-k}.\sigma}.

1.3.
Application on real BZ filtrations

As an application, we obtain a complete version of Bernstein-Zelevinsky filtrations for real general linear groups. To state precisely the results, let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
and we consider:

G_{n}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{K}),\quad V_{n-1}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&v\\
&1\end{pmatrix}:v\in\mathbb{K}^{n-1}\right\},\quad\mathfrak{v}_{n-1}:=\mathrm{Lie}(V_{n-1}).

According to Theorem
1.1
(1), considering
G=G_{n}
and
N=V_{n}
, one has a short exact sequence: for
\pi
in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(V_{n})
,

0\rightarrow\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi)\rightarrow\pi\rightarrow\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}(\pi)\rightarrow 0.

A key point in the Bernstein-Zelevinsky filtration is to provide an alternate description of
\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)
.

We have to define more notations for this. Let
M_{n}
be the mirabolic subgroup of
G_{n}
and let
\phi
be defined as in Section
17.1
. Define

\Phi^{-}:\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(M_{n})\rightarrow\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(M_{n-1})

given by

\Phi^{-}(\pi):=\delta^{-1}\cdot\pi/\mathfrak{v}_{n-1}.(\pi\otimes\phi),

where
\delta
is a modular character of
M_{n}
. We shall denote the left adjoint functor of
\Phi^{-}
by
\Phi^{+}
, see Section
18.1
for the details.

The moderate growth rate condition allows one to define an action of the Schwartz algebra
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{v}_{n-1})\simeq\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1})
on
\pi
i.e. there exists a continuous map:

a:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1})\widehat{\otimes}\pi\rightarrow\pi.

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1}\setminus\left\{0\right\})
be the subspace of all functions in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1})
with all vanishing derivatives at
0
. Under the Fourier transform in Section
3.1
, it takes
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1}\setminus\left\{0\right\})
to
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{v}_{n-1}^{k}\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1})
. We write
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi
to be the image of
(\mathfrak{v}_{n-1}^{k}\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1}))\widehat{\otimes}\pi
under the map
a
above. The role of Fourier transform will become clearer when one has to describe the space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi
in terms of the Schwartz induction, see the details in Section
16.1
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(M_{n})
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

\Phi^{-}(\pi)
is Hausdorff.

(2)

\overline{\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi}=\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)
.

(3)

There is a continuous bijective map from

to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{n-1}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi

One repeatedly applies Proposition
1.5
to obtain a full version of Bernstein-Zelevinsky filtration in Theorem

(c.f.
[
BZ76

]
for
p

[

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
General notions

Let
G
be a Lie group. A representation
\pi
of
G
is said to be
continuous
if
\pi
is a topological vector space over
\mathbb{C}
such that the map

G\times\pi\rightarrow\pi

is continuous.

Let
\pi
be a continuous representation of
G
. A vector
v\in\pi
is said to be
smooth
if the map
G\to\pi
given by
g\mapsto g.v
is a smooth map. A continuous representation of
G
is said to be
Fréchet
if the underlying topology is Fréchet i.e. the topology is defined by a metric and the space is Hausdorff and complete under the topology.

For a reductive Lie group
G
(see
[
BW00
, Chapter 0, Sec. 3.1]
), we fix an algebraic scale
||.||_{G}
on
G
as in
[
BK14
]
. A smooth representation
\pi
is of
moderate growth rate
if for any semi-norm
p_{1}
of
\pi
, there exists an integer
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and a semi-norm
p_{2}
of
\pi
such that, for all
v\in\pi
and all
g\in G
,

p_{1}(\pi(g)v)\leq||g||^{r}_{G}\cdot p_{2}(v).

Let
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
be the category of smooth Fréchet representations of
G
of moderate growth rate with morphisms to be continuous maps. A representation
\pi
in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
is said to be (topologically) irreducible if there is no proper non-zero closed
G
-invariant subspace of
\pi
. We remark that the category
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
is not abelian, but it is quasi-abelian, see e.g.
[
Ka93
, Section 2]
for more discussions.

For a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
, write
N_{P}
to be the unipotent subgroup of
P
and
M_{P}
to be the Levi subgroup of
P
.

2.2.
Quotient topology

Let
\pi
be a Fréchet space. Let
\left\{p_{i}\right\}
be a countable family of semi-norms defining the topology on a vector space
\pi
. One has a metric on
\pi
defining the topology of
\pi
given by:

d(v,v^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\frac{p_{i}(v-v^{\prime})}{1+p_{i}(v-v^{\prime})}.

The Hausdorffness for
\pi
is equivalent to:

p_{n}(v)=0\mbox{ for all $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}$}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v=0.

By summing the semi-norms if necessary, we may always assume the semi-norms are increasing i.e.

p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq p_{3}\leq\ldots

Now let
\kappa
be a closed subspace of
\pi
, equipped with the subspace topology. Define a metric on
\pi/\kappa
by

\widetilde{d}([v],[v^{\prime}]):=\mathrm{inf}\left\{d(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{v}^{\prime}):\widetilde{v}\in[v],\widetilde{v}^{\prime}\in[v^{\prime}]\right\}.

The topology defined by the metric
\widetilde{d}
agrees with the quotient topology and so the projection from
\pi
to
\pi/\kappa
is continuous.

Alternatively, one can define a family of semi-norms as:

\widetilde{p}_{n}([v]):=\mathrm{inf}\left\{p_{n}(\widetilde{v}):\widetilde{v}\in[v]\right\}.

This family
\left\{\widetilde{p}_{n}\right\}
of semi-norms defines the topology on
\pi/\kappa
. This defines another metric

\overline{d}([v],[v^{\prime}]):=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\frac{\widetilde{p}_{i}([v]-[v^{\prime}])}{1+\widetilde{p}_{i}([v]-[v^{\prime}])}.

The topology defined by
\widetilde{d}
is in priori weaker than the topology defined by
\overline{d}
. By the open mapping theorem, the two topologies agree.

2.3.
Projective tensor products

Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be two Fréchet spaces. Let
\left\{p_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\left\{q_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be families of semi-norms defining the topology of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
respectively. Define:

(p_{i}\otimes q_{j})(v)=\inf\left\{\sum_{k}p_{i}(v_{1}^{k})\cdot q_{j}(v_{2}^{k})\right\},

where
v_{1}^{k}
and
v_{2}^{k}
run for all the finite sums such that

v=\sum_{k}v_{1}^{k}\otimes v_{2}^{k}.

TThe family
\left\{p_{i}\otimes q_{j}\right\}_{i,j}
of seminorms defines a metric on the (algebraic) tensor product
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}
. According to
[
Tr06

,
\pi_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\pi_{2}
is defined to be the completion of
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}
.

3.1.
Fourier transform on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi)

Let
\pi
be a Fréchet space (over
\mathbb{C}
) with the associated metric
d
. We shall write the variable vector

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
. Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi)
be the space of
\pi
-valued Schwartz functions of

i.e. any smooth function

satisfying that for any
k,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\mathrm{sup}_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{(1+|x_{1}|^{2}+\ldots+|x_{n}|^{2})^{k}\cdot d(\partial_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots\partial_{n}^{k_{n}}f((x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})))\right\}<\infty.

Define a family of seminorms on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi)
: for
k,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

p_{k,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}(f)=\mathrm{sup}_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{(1+|x_{1}|^{2}+\ldots+|x_{n}|^{2})^{k}\cdot d(\partial_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots\partial_{n}^{k_{n}}f((x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})))\right\}.

We shall equip
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi)
with the topology induced by the family of seminorms
\left\{p_{k,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}\right\}
.

We fix a non-degenerate pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi)
and let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Fourier transform sends the space of Schwartz functions to the space of Schwartz functions:

\widehat{f}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-2\sqrt{-1}\pi\langle x,y\rangle}f(x)\penalty\ dx,

with the standard Euclidean measure
dx
and so

\widehat{\partial_{i}f}(y)=2\sqrt{-1}\pi x_{i}\widehat{f}(x).

The Fourier transform defines a (continuous) homeomorphism between
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi)
. Now suppose we have a smooth group action of a Lie group
G
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We shall assume we can fix a
G
-equivariant bilinear form on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The group action
G
on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi)
is given by
(g.f)(x)=f(g^{-1}x)
. Under the Fourier transform, for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi)
and
g\in G
,

\widehat{g.f}(y)=\delta(g)\cdot(\widehat{f}(g^{T}y)),

where
\delta(g)
appears from changing variables from
x
to
g^{T}x
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi)
and let
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Now, one defines:

(h\star f)(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)f(y-x)\penalty\ dx.

Then

\widehat{h\star f}(y)=\widehat{h}(y)\widehat{f}(y).

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\},\pi)
be the space of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Then the Fourier transform defines a homeomorphism between two spaces
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\},\pi)
and
\bigcap_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\partial_{n}^{m_{n}}\ldots\partial_{1}^{m_{1}}f:f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\ \text{s.t.}\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}=i\right\}
.

We need the following simple lemma in
[
dCl91
]
:

Lemma 3.1
.

The space
\overline{\left\{f_{1}f_{2}:f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\})\right\}}
is equal to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\})
.

Proof.

Let

. Then there exists a sequence of compactly supported functions

in the support of

. This implies the lemma.

-action. We shall only prove for

and the general case follows from case

Thus it suffices to check the

-action for the elements of the form:

respectively. For example,

X_{1}f=f_{X_{1}}^{\prime}\otimes f_{X_{2}}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{X_{n}}.

Now, we also have, for
i_{1}+1\leq i\leq i_{2}
,

X_{i}f=f_{X_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{X_{i-1}}\otimes f_{X_{i}}^{\prime}\otimes f_{X_{i+1}}\otimes\ldots f_{X_{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{i_{1}}X_{j}f_{X_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{X_{j-1}}\otimes f_{X_{j}}^{\prime}\otimes f_{X_{j+1}}\otimes\ldots f_{X_{n}}

for some
X_{i}
in the sum is regarded as a multiplication on the term
f_{X_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{X_{j-1}}\otimes f_{X_{j}}^{\prime}\otimes f_{X_{j+1}}\otimes\ldots f_{X_{n}}
. Inductively, we see that the terms, for
j=1,\ldots,i_{1}
,

p_{j}f_{X_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{X_{j-1}}\otimes f_{X_{j}}^{\prime}\otimes f_{X_{j+1}}\otimes\ldots f_{X_{n}}

are in
\mathcal{C}
. Now one proceeds inductively and similarly to show other derivatives.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f\in\bigcap_{k\geq 0}\mathfrak{n}^{k}\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n})
. Let
\pi
be a trivial smooth Fréchet representation of
N
. Then
f
acts identically zero on
\pi
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{n}
acts trivially on
\pi
, this reduces to integration on real-valued functions by (
3.2
). Now, one uses the property that a Schwartz function vanishes at infinities to conclude the lemma.
∎

4.
Semi-norms on Casselman-Jacquet modules

4.1.
Two functors

We shall consider
G
to be a reductive Lie group in the reminaing of Part
I
. Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
. Let
\mathfrak{n}=\mathrm{Lie}(N_{P})
and simply write
N
for
N_{P}
. Note that
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(P)
is not abelian because for a given morphism
f:\pi_{1}\rightarrow\pi_{2}
, the space
\pi_{1}/f^{-1}(0)
is not necessarily homeomorphic to
f(\pi_{1})
. Note that any monic is injective while any epi
f:\pi_{1}\rightarrow\pi_{2}
has dense set-theoretic image in the codomain i.e.
\overline{f(\pi_{1})}=\pi_{2}
.

Define the
submodule intersection functor
from
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(P)
to
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(P)
:

\mathbf{CJ}_{s,P}^{\infty}(\pi)=\mathbf{CJ}_{s,N}^{\infty}(\pi):=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}.

Note that the space is invariant under the action of
P
and so is well-defined. We sometimes omit the subscript
N
if the unipotent subgroup is understood. Suppose we have a continuous morphism
f
from
\pi_{1}
to
\pi_{2}
. Then
f(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{1}})\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{2}}
. Thus
f(\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi_{1}))\subset\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi_{2})
, and so induces the corresponding morphism for the functor
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}
.

Define the
Casselman-Jacquet (quotient) functor
from
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(P)
to
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(P)
:

\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}(\pi)=\mathbf{CJ}_{N}^{\infty}(\pi):=\varprojlim_{k}\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}.

We shall regard
\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}(\pi)
as a
P
-representation.

We sometimes omit the subscript
N
or
P
in above notion. In principle, we can consider other Lie groups that contain
N
as a normal subgroup or even
N
itself. The above functor is still well-defined and we shall sometimes use such variations.

4.2.
Semi-norms on the Casselman-Jacquet functor

Let
N
be the unipotent subgroup of a parabolic subgroup of a reductive Lie group
G
and let
\mathfrak{n}=\mathrm{Lie}(N)
. We shall now specialize discussions in Section
3.1
to the setting of the Casselman-Jacquet functor. Let
\mathfrak{n}^{*}
be the linear dual of
\mathfrak{n}
. Then the Fourier transform sends from
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n})
to
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
. Then it takes the intersection
\bigcap_{k\geq 0}\mathfrak{n}^{k}\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n})
in Lemma
3.4
to the space
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\})
. In the action map (
3.1
), we shall write
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi=a(\mathcal{\bigcap}_{k\geq 0}\mathfrak{n}^{k}\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n})\widehat{\otimes}\pi)
. This notion is more convenient if one later compares with the
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]
-action on the Casselman-Jacquet quotients.

It follows from the Fourier transform and Lemma
3.1
that:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. Then the action of
\bigcap_{k\geq 0}\mathfrak{n}^{k}\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n})
on
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi
is non-degenerate.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. Then
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi\subset\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi)
.

Proof.

This follows from:

S(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi=\left(\bigcap\mathfrak{n}^{k}S(\mathfrak{n})\right).\pi\subset\bigcap(\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi).

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. Then there exists a non-degenerate continuous action of
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]
on
\pi/\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda=\pi/\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi)
. Then the action map:

\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n})\widehat{\otimes}\lambda\rightarrow\lambda

is continuous. We shall consider the action after applying the Fourier transform discussed in Section
3.1
, and so we have a continuous map from
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})\widehat{\otimes}\lambda
to
\lambda
.

Now
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\lambda
is zero by Lemma
4.2
. Then one has a well-defined map from
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]\widehat{\otimes}\pi
to
\pi
given by:

f\otimes x\mapsto\pi(\widetilde{f})x,

where
\widetilde{f}
is a lift of
f
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*})
. It follows from Lemma
4.2
that the action is well-defined i.e. independent of a chocie of
\widetilde{f}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. There exists a countable family
\left\{q_{j}\right\}
of semi-norms defining the topology on
\pi/\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)
such that the for each fixed
q_{j}
, there exists a sufficiently large integer
k_{j}>0
such that the semi-norm
q_{j}
identically vanishes on
\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}/\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)
for all
k>k_{j}
. In particular, for any
r>k>k_{j}
, the induced norm
\widetilde{q}_{k}
vanishes on
\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{r}.\pi}
.

Proof.

Since
\pi
is non-degenerate and continuous, the action map is surjective:

\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]\widehat{\otimes}\pi\rightarrow\pi.

Then, by the open mapping theorem,
\pi
can be realized as a Hausdorff quotient of
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]\widehat{\otimes}\pi
. We now have semi-norms
\left\{p_{i}\right\}
defining the Fréchet topology of
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]
:

p_{i}(\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}c_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}X_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots X_{r}^{i_{r}}):=\mathrm{max}_{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{r}\leq i}\left\{|c_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}|\right\}.

Thus the projective norm
p_{i}\otimes q_{j}
defines the topology on
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]\widehat{\otimes}\pi
.

Recall that the quotient semi-norms
\widetilde{p_{i}\otimes q_{j}}
define the topology of
\pi
in Section
2.2
. For fixed
i
(and
j
), we choose a sufficiently large integer
k
such that
\mathfrak{n}^{k}\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]
vanishes on
p_{i}
. Note that
\mathfrak{n}^{k}(\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]\widehat{\otimes}\pi)
surjects onto
\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi
. Then, for
x\in\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi
, we can choose a representative
\widetilde{x}
such that
(p_{i}\otimes q_{i})(\widetilde{x})=0
and so
\widetilde{p_{i}\otimes q_{j}}(\widetilde{x})=0
. Now, taking the continuity on the closure, we have that
\widetilde{p_{i}\otimes q_{j}}
vanishes on
\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. Let
x_{k}
be a sequence in
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}(\pi)
such that
x_{k}\in\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}/\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi)
. Then the sequence
(x_{k})
converges to
0
.

Proof.

Let
\left\{q_{i}\right\}
be a family of semi-norms defining the topology of
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}(\pi)
in Proposition
4.4
. It follows from Section
2.2
that the topology of
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}(\pi)
is also defined from the metric:

d(v,v^{\prime})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\frac{q_{j}(v-v^{\prime})}{1+q_{j}(v-v^{\prime})}.

Now, from the previous proposition, we have that
d(x_{k},0)\rightarrow 0
and so
x_{k}\rightarrow 0
as desired.
∎

4.3.
\mathfrak{n}
-stable property

Let
N
be a unipotent subgroup of a reductive Lie group, and let
\mathfrak{n}=\mathrm{Lie}(N)
. For
\pi
in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
,
\pi
is said to satisfy the
\mathfrak{n}
-stable property
if
\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\pi}=\pi
.

We sketch the intuitative idea and then provide a formal proof on the stability result. A main idea is to “glue” non-trivially
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}(\pi)
with a module
\omega
with the trivial
\mathfrak{n}
-action. The way to glue modules to form a module is in the sense that
\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi^{\prime})
is
\omega
. A module
\omega
has to be the submodule of
\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi^{\prime}}
. Now one picks an element
v
in
\omega
and constructs a sequence converging to
v
, and finally shows from Proposition
4.4
that such the sequence also has to converge to
0
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. Then
\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)
is
\mathfrak{n}
-stable.

Proof.

Let
\lambda=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}=\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)
. Let

\pi^{\prime}=\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\lambda}.

Define
\mathrm{pr}:\pi\rightarrow\pi^{\prime}
be the projection map. Let
v
be an arbitrary element in
\mathrm{pr}(\lambda)
. We proceed to show that
v=0
.

Claim:
We fix a basis
\left\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\right\}
for
\mathfrak{n}
. There exists a sequence of sets
\left\{x^{k}_{1},\ldots,x^{k}_{n}\right\}
of vectors
\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})
such that

X_{1}x^{k}_{1}+\ldots+X_{n}x^{k}_{n}\longrightarrow v.

Proof of Claim:

We fix a non-negative integer
k
. By the definition of
\lambda
, we have:

\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\lambda}\subset\lambda\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}.\pi}.

It is clear that

\mathfrak{n}.\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})\subset\overline{\mathrm{pr}(\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}.\pi)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})}\subset\overline{\mathrm{pr}(\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}.\pi)}.

We also have

\mathrm{pr}(\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}.\pi)\subset\mathfrak{n}.\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}.\pi})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\overline{\mathrm{pr}(\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}.\pi)}\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})}.

Combining with
\overline{\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}.\pi})}=\overline{\mathrm{pr}(\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}.\pi)}
, this implies that
\overline{\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}.\pi})}=\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})}
and so

\mathrm{pr}(\lambda)\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})}.

This then implies that, for each
k
, there exists a sequence of sets
\left\{x_{1}^{k},\ldots,x_{n}^{k}\right\}
in
\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})
such that
d(X_{1}x^{k}_{1}+\ldots+X_{n}x^{k}_{n},v)<\frac{1}{2^{k}}
. This provides the desired sequence.

Claim 1 above provides a sequence of vectors in
\lambda
converging to
v
. However, we need to make some good choices, which we shall need Claim 2 below.

Claim 2:
Let
x_{1}^{k},\ldots,x_{n}^{k}
be a sequence of the vectors in the previous claim. Suppose
y^{k}_{1},\ldots,y^{k}_{n}
form another sequence of vectors such that, for each
k
and
i
,
x_{i}^{k}
and
y_{i}^{k}
have the same image in the quotient
\pi/\lambda
. Then

X_{1}y_{1}^{k}+\ldots+X_{n}y_{n}^{k}\longrightarrow v

Proof of Claim 2:
Note that

(X_{1}x_{1}^{k}+\ldots+X_{n}x_{n}^{k})-(X_{1}y_{1}^{k}+\ldots+X_{n}y_{n}^{k})=X_{1}(x_{1}^{k}-y_{1}^{k})+\ldots+X_{n}(x_{n}^{k}-y_{n}^{k})

is zero because
x_{1}^{k}-y_{1}^{k},\ldots,x_{n}^{k}-y_{n}^{k}
are in
\lambda
and so the above expression is in
\mathfrak{n}\lambda
.

Now back to the proof, from Claim 1, we can find a sequence of
x_{1}^{k},\ldots,x_{n}^{k}\in\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})
such that
X_{1}z_{1}^{k}+\ldots+X_{n}z_{n}^{k}
converges to
v
.

On the other hand, let
\mathrm{pr}^{\prime}:\pi^{\prime}\rightarrow\pi/\lambda
be the projection. We now consider the projections of
x_{1}^{k},\ldots,x_{n}^{k}

\mathrm{pr}^{\prime}(x_{1}^{k}),\ldots,\mathrm{pr}^{\prime}(x_{n}^{k})

to
\pi/\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)
. By Corollary
4.5
,

\mathrm{pr}^{\prime}(x_{1}^{k}),\ldots,\mathrm{pr}^{\prime}(x_{n}^{k})

converge to
0
. Hence, from the quotient topology, we can find representatives
y_{1}^{k},\ldots,y_{n}^{k}
in
\pi^{\prime}
such that each sequence
(y_{i}^{k})_{k}
converges to
0
. In particular, we also have:

X_{1}y_{1}^{k}+\ldots+X_{n}y_{n}^{k}\rightarrow 0

Now by Claim 2, such the sequence also converges to
v
. Hence,
v=0
.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
. Then
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P,s}\circ\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P,s}(\pi)=\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P,s}(\pi)
.

Proof.

Corollary
4.6
implies that
\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)}=\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi)
, and this implies the corollary.
∎

5.
Surjectivity of the two Casselman-Jacquet functors

5.1.
Surjectivity of Casselman-Jacquet functors

The key idea of proving the surjectivity is that for the given element
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
in
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi)
, we choose suitable representatives
\widetilde{x}_{i}
in
\pi
with the property that the projection of
\widetilde{x}_{i}
onto
\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{i}.\pi}
is
x_{i}
. The good choices are determined by the seminorms in Proposition
4.4
and allow one to show that the sequence converges.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
N
be the unipotent subgroup of a parabolic subgroup of
G
and let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. The natural map from
\pi
to
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}(\pi)
is surjective, and hence

\pi/\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)\cong\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}(\pi).

Proof.

Note that the second assertion follows from the first one and the open mapping theorem.

We now prove the first assertion. Let
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
be in
\varprojlim_{k}\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}
. We shall construct a convergent sequence
(y_{n})
in
\pi
such that the limit of
y_{n}
is projected to
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
.

Let
\left\{p_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
be a countable family of semi-norms defining the topology of
\pi
satisfying:

p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\ldots

By Corollary
4.5
, there exists an integer
k_{i}
such that the semi-norm induced from
p_{i}
vanishes on
\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{i}}.\pi}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{r}.\pi}
for any
r>k_{i}
. Such integers exist by Proposition
4.4
. We may and shall further assume that
k_{1}<k_{2}<k_{3}<\ldots
.

Let
\iota_{k_{2}}:\pi\rightarrow\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{2}}.\pi}
. Let
y_{1}
be an element in
\pi
such that the projection of
y_{1}
from
\pi
to
\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{1}}.\pi}
is
x_{k_{1}}
. Then the element
x_{k_{2}}-\iota_{k_{2}}(y_{1})
is in
\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{1}}.\pi}
. Since the induced semi-norm of
p_{1}
vanishes on
\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{1}}.\pi}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{2}}.\pi}
, we can find an element
y_{2}
in
\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{2}}.\pi}
with the following holds:
p_{1}(y_{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}
and; the projection of
y_{2}
from
\pi
to
\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{2}}.\pi}
is
x-y_{1}
.

We similarly and inductively construct elements
y_{s}
such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

p_{s}(y_{s+1})\leq\frac{1}{2^{s}}

(2)

the projection of
y_{s+1}
from
\pi
to
\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{s}+1}.\pi}
is
x-y_{1}-\ldots-y_{s}
.

In

d(y_{s+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\cdot\frac{p_{i}(y_{s+1})}{1+p_{i}(y_{s+1})}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\cdot\frac{p_{i}(y_{s+1})}{1+p_{i}(y_{s+1})}

the first summation is bounded by
\frac{1}{2^{s}}
and the second summation is also bounded by
\frac{1}{2^{s}}
. Thus,
\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}\}
forms a Cauchy sequence and so converges in
\pi
. It remains to observe that the element
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}y_{i}
under the map from
\pi
to
\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}(\pi)
is
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
.
∎

6.
Epiness of Casselman-Jacquet submodule functor

Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
. Note that a map
f:\pi_{1}\rightarrow\pi_{2}
is epi if and only if the image of
f
is dense in
\pi_{2}
; and
f
is monic if and only if
f
is injective.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
N
be the unipotent subgroup of a parabolic subgroup of a reductive group
G
. Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
.

(1)

If
f:\pi_{1}\rightarrow\pi_{2}
is monic (and so injective), then
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(f)
is also monic (and so injective).

(2)

If
f:\pi_{1}\rightarrow\pi_{2}
is epi, then
\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(f)
is also epi.

Proof.

(1) is clear as the induced map is simply restricted to
\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi_{1})
and so is still injective.

We now consider (2). Let
f:\pi_{1}\rightarrow\pi_{2}
be a surjection in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. Let

\lambda_{1}=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{1}},\quad\lambda_{2}=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{2}}.

Note that

f(\lambda_{1})\subset\lambda_{2}.

In order to show that
f
is epi, it suffices to show that
\overline{f(\lambda_{1})}=\lambda_{2}
. Suppose there exists a non-zero element
x
in
\lambda_{2}/\overline{f(\lambda_{1})}
. Let
x^{\prime}
be a lift of
x
in
\lambda_{2}
.

Claim:
For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, there exists
\widetilde{x}_{k}\in\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{2}
such that
d(\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{x}_{k}),x)<\frac{1}{2^{k}}
, where
d
is the function defining the metric on
\pi_{2}/\overline{f(\lambda_{1})}
.

Proof of Claim:
Let
\mathrm{pr}:\pi_{2}\rightarrow\pi_{2}/\overline{f(\lambda_{1})}
be the projection map. By the
\mathfrak{n}
-stable property on
\lambda_{2}
, for any
k\geq 0
,

x^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\lambda_{2}}.

We have that:

x\in\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\lambda_{2}})\subset\overline{\mathrm{pr}(\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\lambda_{2})}

for each
k
. Thus, we can find
x_{k}
in
\mathrm{pr}(\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\lambda_{2})
such that

d(x_{k},x)<\frac{1}{2^{k}}.

Then, one finds a lift
\widetilde{x}_{k}
of
x_{k}
in
\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{2}
. This shows the claim.

Now, it follows from Proposition
4.4
that the sequence
(\widetilde{x}_{k})
converges to
0
, and so
\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{x}_{k})
converges to
0
. Thus
x=0
as desired.
∎

7.
Alternate descriptions of Casselman-Jacquet submodule functor

We shall use the notations in Section
4.2
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
N
be a unipotent subgroup of a parabolic subgroup of
G
. Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. Suppose the following two conditions hold:

(1)

\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi)=\pi
; and

(2)

\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi=0
.

Then
\pi=0
.

Proof.

By the same argument in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, the condition (2) implies that there exists a continuous surjective map:

\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]\widehat{\otimes}\pi\rightarrow\pi.

Let
\left\{q_{j}\right\}
be a countable family of semi-norms defining the topology of
\pi
. On the other hand, the topology on
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]
is determined by the semi-norms:

p_{i}(\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\geq 0}c_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}X_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots X_{r}^{i_{r}}):=\mathrm{max}_{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{r}\leq i}\left\{|c_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}|\right\}.

Recall that the topology on
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]\widehat{\otimes}\pi
is defined in Section
2.3
. Let
x\in\pi
. It suffices to show that
p_{i}\otimes q_{j}(x)=0
for all
i
and
j
. We now choose
k>i
. By the given assumption, we can find a sequence
(x_{n})
in
\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi
converging to
x
. Now, it suffices to show that
p_{i}\otimes q_{j}(x_{n})=0
for each
n
. To this end, we write

x_{n}=P_{1}.y_{1}+\ldots+P_{r}.y_{r}

with each
P_{k}
to be a monomial in
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]
with degree
k
and each
y_{i}
to be in
\pi
. Note that

(p_{i}\otimes q_{j})(\sum_{k=1}^{r}P_{k}\otimes y_{k})=0.

Recall that, by the open mapping theorem, we can identify
\pi
with the quotient

(\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]\widehat{\otimes}\pi)/K,

where
K
is the kernel of the quotient map from
\mathbb{C}[[\mathfrak{n}^{*}]]\widehat{\otimes}\pi
to
\pi
. Since
P_{1}\otimes y_{1}+\ldots+P_{r}\otimes y_{r}
projects to
x_{n}
by the above action map, the quotient norm
\widetilde{p_{i}\otimes q_{j}}
on
x_{n}
is zero. Since our choices of
i
and
j
are arbitrary, this implies that
x_{n}
vanishes for all the semi-norms defining the topology on
\pi
. Thus,
x_{n}=0
and so
x=0
.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(N)
. Then

\mathbf{CJ}_{s}^{\infty}(\pi)=\overline{\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi}=\overline{\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}.

Proof.

The inclusion
\overline{\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi}\subset\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi)
follows from Lemma
4.2
. Now, one considers
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s}(\pi)/\overline{\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi}
and one has Proposition
7.1
(1) from Corollary
4.6
. Now one obtains the first equality by Proposition
7.1
.

We now prove the second equation. Let
\lambda=\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}).\pi
. Note that
\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\})=\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{n}^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\})
and so
\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\lambda=\lambda
. Thus

\lambda\subset\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi\subset\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi}.

Now one takes the closure, and the first equality forces all inclusions to be equal.
∎

8.
Transitivity of Casselman-Jacquet functors

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
. Let
Q\subset P\subset G
be parabolic subgroups of
G
. Let
\mathfrak{n}_{P}=\mathrm{Lie}(N_{P})
and let
\mathfrak{n}_{Q}=\mathrm{Lie}(N_{Q})
. Let
\lambda=\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}(\pi)
. Let
\mathrm{pr}:\pi\rightarrow\lambda
be the projection map. Then, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\pi})=\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\lambda},\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\pi/(\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\pi})\cong\lambda/(\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\lambda}).

Proof.

We first show the former equation. Since
\mathrm{pr}
is surjective, we also have:

\mathrm{pr}(\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi)=\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\lambda

for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Let
x\in\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\lambda}
. Then there exists a sequence
(x_{n})
in
\mathfrak{n}^{k}_{Q}.\lambda
such that

x_{n}\rightarrow x.

It follows from
\lambda=\pi/\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}(\pi)
that

\mathfrak{n}^{k}_{Q}.\lambda=(\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\pi)/\mathbf{CJ}_{s,P}^{\infty}(\pi).

Let
\widetilde{x}
be a lift of
x
in
\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\pi
. As
\mathbf{CJ}_{s,P}^{\infty}(\pi)\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\pi}
, we first find a lift
\widetilde{x}_{n}
of
x_{n}
in
\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\pi
for each
n
, such that

\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{x}_{n},\widetilde{x})=d(x_{n},x)+\frac{1}{2^{n}},

where
\widetilde{d}
is the metric on
\pi
while
d
is the metric on
\lambda
. Then
(\widetilde{x}_{n})
forms a sequence converging to
\widetilde{x}
. This implies that
\mathrm{pr}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})=\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\lambda}
.

We now show the latter isomorphism. Since
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}(\pi)\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{P}^{k}.\pi}\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\pi}
,

\frac{\pi}{\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\pi}}\cong\frac{\pi/\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}(\pi)}{\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{k}.\pi}/\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}(\pi)}.

The latter isomorphism follows from
\lambda=\pi/\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}(\pi)
and the first assertion.
∎

Proposition 8.2
.

(Transtivity of CJ functor)
Let
\pi
be in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(G)
. Let
Q\subset P\subset G
be parabolic subgroups of
G
. Let
\mathfrak{n}_{P}=\mathrm{Lie}(N_{P})
and let
\mathfrak{n}_{Q}=\mathrm{Lie}(N_{Q})
. Then

\mathbf{CJ}_{Q}^{\infty}\circ\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}(\pi)\cong\mathbf{CJ}_{Q}^{\infty}(\pi).

Proof.

This follows from the second assertion in Lemma
8.1
.
∎

Let

9.2.
Categories for parabolic subgroups

Let

\mathfrak{p}
-modules

\mathfrak{n}^{k}

\pi

The morphisms are

and

10.2.
Lifting Lie algebra action

We use the notations in Lemma
10.1
. We now exponentiate the action of
\mathfrak{n}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{n}
is defined over
\mathbb{R}
and let
n_{v}=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(\pi_{v})
. Then we have a map:

\phi_{v}:\mathfrak{n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\pi(v))\cong\mathfrak{gl}(n_{v},\mathbb{C}).

Since

and
\mathrm{GL}(n_{v},\mathbb{C})
are simply-connected, the classical Lie theorem provides a map:

\Phi_{v}:N\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(\pi(v)).

The map is well-defined i.e. for all
n\in N
,
\Phi_{v_{1}}(n)|_{\pi(v_{1})\cap\pi(v_{2})}=\Phi_{v_{2}}(n)|_{\pi(v_{1})\cap\pi(v_{2})}
for any

, by the uniqueness of lifting.

Lemma 10.2
.

The map
\phi:N\times\pi\rightarrow\pi
given by
(n,v)\mapsto n.v
defines a continuous map.

Proof.

Fix
v_{0}\in\pi
. The map

(10.3)

\displaystyle X\mapsto\mathrm{exp}(X).v=(\mathrm{Id}+X+\frac{X^{2}}{2}+\ldots+\frac{X^{k}}{k!}).v_{0}

is continuous since each

is continuous by Lemma
10.1
. Since
N
and
\mathfrak{n}
are diffeomorphic (in paticular homeomorphic), we then have the map
n\in N\mapsto n.v_{0}
is continuous. Thus, by the Banach-Steinhaus Theorem, the set of operators

\left\{\Phi_{v_{0}}(g):g\in\Lambda\right\},

for any compact subset
\Lambda
in
N
, is equicontinuous, and so
\phi
is continuous by
[
Wa72
, Page 219]
.
∎

Corollary 10.3
.

We use the notations in Lemma

(2).

\displaystyle f(pg)=\delta(p)^{1/2}p.(f(g))\quad\mbox{for $p\in P$ and $g\in G$ },

The Zuckerman functor

-module category. Explicitly, for a

We now also discuss the parabolic induction for Harish-Chandra modules. Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of

. Let
\mathfrak{m}=\mathrm{Lie}(M_{P})

.

There is a natural isomorphism

11.4.

Definition 11.3

Proposition 11.5
.

(Frobenius reciprocity)
Let
\pi
be in
\mathcal{CW}_{G}
and let
\sigma
be in
\mathcal{CW}_{P,k}
. Then there is a natural isomorphism:

\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(\pi,\mathrm{Ind}^{G,\infty}_{P}\sigma)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{P}(\pi/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi},\sigma).

Proof.

Frobenius reciprocity implies that

\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(\pi,\mathrm{Ind}^{G,\infty}_{P}\sigma)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{P}(\pi,\sigma).

The maps in
\mathrm{Hom}_{P}(\pi,\sigma)
factor through the projection
\pi\rightarrow\pi/(\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi)

∎

Generalized Jacquet modules

We also need to compare the globalization for the parabolic subgroup
P

Definition 11.6
.

Let

We first have the following Frobenius reciprocity:

Proposition 11.7
.

For any
\sigma\in\mathcal{HC}_{P,k}
and

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g},K}(\pi,\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{p},K_{P}}^{\mathfrak{g},K}\sigma)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{p},K_{P}}(\mathbf{CJ}^{HC}_{P,k}(\pi),\sigma).

A topological consequence

Lemma 11.8
.

The functor
\mathbf{CJ}_{P,k}^{\infty}

Corollary 11.9
.

Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be Casselman-Wallach representations of
G
with a surjective map

from
\pi
to
\pi^{\prime}
. Let

be a parabolic subgroup of

. Then, for any

Proof.

It is clear that
f(\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi})\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi^{\prime}}
. We consider the short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\omega\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\rightarrow}}\pi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}\pi^{\prime}\rightarrow 0,

where

12.
Artin-Rees lemma for Casselman-Wallach representations

12.1.
Closure for the Casselman-Jacquet submodule functor

Lemma 12.1
.

Let
\pi
be a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module. Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
and let
\mathfrak{n}=\mathrm{Lie}(N_{P})
. Then
\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\pi}=\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\pi^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

Fix a basis
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
for
\mathfrak{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}_{i}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\left\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{i}\right\}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and
\mathfrak{n}_{0}=0
.

We inductively have that
\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{i-1}.\pi}=\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{i-1}.\pi^{\infty}}
. On the other hand, since
\pi
is dense in
\pi^{\infty}
and the action of
X_{i}
is continuous,
\overline{X_{i}.\pi}=\overline{X_{i}.\pi^{\infty}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{i-1}.\pi^{\infty}\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{i}.\pi}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad X_{i}.\pi^{\infty}\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{i}.\pi}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\pi^{\infty}+X_{i}.\pi^{\infty}\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{i}.\pi}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{i}.\pi^{\infty}}\subset\overline{\mathfrak{n}_{i}.\pi}

Another inclusion is quite straightforward and so completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We first recall the Artin-Rees lemma for

Let

since

. ∎

12.3.
Exactness of the Casselman-Jacquet functor

Recall that
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}
is defined in Section
4.1
. We highlight that the key inputs in the following proof are the topological ingredients in Corollary
11.9
and Lemma
12.3
. The proof is inspired from the exactness of the localization functor in commutative algebra.

Theorem 12.4
.

Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
. Then the functor

\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}:\mathrm{CW}_{G}\rightarrow\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(M_{P})

is exact.

Proof.

Let
0\rightarrow\pi_{1}\rightarrow\pi_{2}\rightarrow\pi_{3}\rightarrow 0
be an exact sequence of Casselman-Wallach representations of
G
.

We first show that the induced map from
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}(\pi_{2})
to
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}(\pi_{3})
is surjective. Let

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots)\in\varprojlim\pi_{3}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{3}}.

Pick
y_{1}\in\pi_{2}
such that the image of
y_{1}
under the projection
\pi_{2}\rightarrow\pi_{3}\rightarrow\pi_{3}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\pi_{3}}
is
x_{1}
. Let
y_{1}^{\prime}
be the image of
y_{1}
under the projection from
\pi_{2}
to
\pi_{2}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\pi_{2}}
, and let
x_{1}^{\prime}
be the image of
y_{1}
under the projection from
\pi_{2}\rightarrow\pi_{3}\rightarrow\pi_{3}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{2}.\pi_{3}}
. Note that
y_{1}^{\prime}-x_{1}^{\prime}
is in
\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\pi_{3}}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{2}.\pi_{3}}
and so by Corollary
11.9
, one can find an element
w_{2}
in
\overline{\mathfrak{n}.\pi_{2}}
such that the projection
\pi_{2}\rightarrow\pi_{3}\rightarrow\pi_{3}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{2}.\pi_{3}}
is
x_{2}-x_{1}^{\prime}
. We now set
y_{2}=w_{2}+y_{1}
. We can construct elements
y_{3},y_{4},\ldots
in a similar fashion and then check that
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\ldots)
is in
\varprojlim_{k}\pi_{2}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{2}}
, and the image of
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\ldots)
under the natural map from
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}(\pi_{2})
to
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}(\pi_{3})
is
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots)
.

We now prove the middle exactness and the injectivity. With the above surjectivity, we have the following short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\varprojlim\penalty\ \pi_{1}/(\pi_{1}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{2}})\rightarrow\varprojlim\penalty\ \pi_{2}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{2}}\rightarrow\varprojlim\penalty\ \pi_{3}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{3}}\rightarrow 0.

Note that the natural map from
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}(\pi_{1})
to
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P}(\pi_{2})
factors through the natural projection
\mathrm{pr}
from
\varprojlim\penalty\ \pi_{1}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{1}}
to
\varprojlim\penalty\ \pi_{1}/(\pi_{1}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{2}})
. Thus, it suffices to show that
\mathrm{pr}
is an isomorphism. This follows from Lemma
12.3
that one can find an increasing sequence
k_{1}<k_{2}<k_{3}<\ldots
to define a natural map

\varprojlim_{i}\pi_{1}/(\pi_{1}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{i}}.\pi_{2}})\rightarrow\varprojlim_{k}\pi_{1}/\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{1}}.

With the fact that
\varprojlim_{i}\pi_{1}/(\pi_{1}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k_{i}}.\pi_{2}})\cong\varprojlim_{k}\pi_{1}/(\pi_{1}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{n}^{k}.\pi_{2}})
, one defines the inverse of
\mathrm{pr}
and shows that
\mathrm{pr}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Corollary 12.5
.

The Casselman-Jacquet submodule functor
\mathbf{CJ}_{s,P}^{\infty}
restricted to the Casselman-Wallach category

Recall that we have a short exact sequence: for

The exactness of
\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{s,P}(\pi)
follows from the exactness of
\mathbf{CJ}_{P}^{\infty}(\pi)
and tracing commutative diagrams.
∎

Theorem 13.1
.

Let

is isomorphic to the Casselman-Wallach globalization of

By Proposition

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(\pi,\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}\sigma)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{P}(\mathbf{CJ}^{\infty}_{P,k}(\pi),\sigma).

14.1.
Mirabolic subgroups

Define the mirabolic subgroup
M_{n}
of

to be the subgroup containing all matrices of the form:

\begin{pmatrix}g&v\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

with
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
and
v\in\mathbb{K}^{n-1}

V_{n-1}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&v\\

We shall regard
M_{n}
as a subgroup of
M_{n+1}
via the embedding
m\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}m&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}
. We also write
\langle.,.\rangle
for the standard inner product on
\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

14.2.
Compact realizations

We begin with notations that will be used throughout the remaining sections for real general linear groups. Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\mathbb{K}^{\times}=\mathbb{R}^{>0}
or
\mathbb{C}^{\times}(=\mathbb{C}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
) respectively.

Define

K_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}O(n)&\mbox{ if $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}$}\\
U(n)&\mbox{ if $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}$ }\end{array}\right..

Define
\mathrm{Ind}_{K_{n-1}}^{K_{n}}\sigma
to be the space of functions from
K_{n}
to
\sigma
such that

f(kg)=k.f(g)

for all
k\in K_{n}
. When
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{\times})
is defined to be the subspace of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
consisting of all objects whose all derivatives vanish at the values less than or equal to
0
, equipped with the subspace topology. When
K=\mathbb{C}
,
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{\times})
is defined to be the subspace of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
consisting of all objects whose all derivatives vanish at

, again equipped with the subspace topology.

Define
\mathrm{SInd}_{K_{n-1}}^{K_{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{\times}}\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{triv}}
to be the space

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{\times})\widehat{\otimes}\left(\mathrm{Ind}_{K_{n-1}}^{K_{n}}\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{triv}}\right)\cong\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{\times},\mathrm{Ind}_{K_{n-1}}^{K_{n}}\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{triv}})\cong\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{K}^{\times}\times(K_{n-1}\setminus K_{n})).

14.3.
Mirabolic inductions

We now define the functor:

\mathrm{SInd}_{M_{n}V_{n}}^{M_{n+1}}:\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(M_{n}V_{n})\rightarrow\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty,F}(M_{n+1}),

such that the underlying space of
\mathrm{SInd}_{M_{n}V_{n}}^{M_{n+1}}\pi
is

\mathrm{SInd}_{K_{n-1}}^{K_{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{\times}}(\pi|_{K_{n-1}}).

Let
\phi:\mathbb{K}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by
\phi(v)=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\langle(0,\ldots,0,1),v\rangle}
. Let
U_{n}
be the subgroup of unipotent upper triangular matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. We have the following decomposition:

(14.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{K})=K_{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{\times}\times(B\cap M_{n}),

via the map

K_{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{\times}\times(B\cap M_{n})\ni(k,a,b)\mapsto k\cdot\mathrm{diag}(a,\ldots,a,1)\cdot b.

For each
f\in\mathrm{SInd}_{K_{n-1}}^{K_{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{\times}}\pi
, we denote
\widetilde{f}:M_{n+1}\rightarrow\pi

C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\times})
. We equip
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\times})
with the
M_{2}
-action given by:

\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.f\right)(x)=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}xa}f(x),\quad\left(\begin{pmatrix}t&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}.f\right)(x)=f(tx).

Let
N=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:x\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
and let
\mathfrak{n}=\mathrm{Lie}(N)
. Then

\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{n}^{k}.C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\times})

is the space of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
(as vectors spaces). However, the topology on this subspace is weaker than the usual Schwartz topology, and does not form a complete subspace. We consider

f_{n}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}xe^{-(x^{\frac{1}{n}}(1-x))^{-1}}&\mbox{ if $0<x<1$}\\
0&\mbox{if $x\geq 1$ or $x\leq 0$ }\end{array}\right.

Note that
f_{n}
converges to
xe^{-(1-x)^{-1}}
under above seminorms. Each
f_{n}
is Schwartz, but the limit is not. Hence, the space of Schwartz functions is not complete in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and so has weaker topology.

Example 19.5
.

Let
\pi=\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
equipped with
M_{2}
-action given by:

(\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.f)(x)=f(x+a),\quad(\begin{pmatrix}t&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}.f)(x)=f(t^{-1}x).

Then
\mathbf{CJ}(\pi)\cong\mathbb{C}[[x]]
with the action given in Example
19.1
. We also have
\Phi^{+}\circ\Phi^{-}(\pi)\cong\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{\times})
.
