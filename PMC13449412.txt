3′UTR shortening of ZCRB1 promotes malignant progression of hepatocellular carcinoma through GOLM1
Authors: Changyu Chen, Zhi Wang, Siyu Li, Xu Wang, Xiaoting Yin, Yong Zhu, Zhiguo Cao, Sheng Tan, Keshuo Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•ZCRB1 was highly expressed in primary HCC and recurrent HCC tissues; High level of ZCRB1 was associated with poor prognosis in HCC patients.•In HCC, percentage of the ZCRB1 mRNA isoform with short 3′UTR increased, resulting in the enhanced expression level of ZCRB1 and tumor malignant behaviors promotion.•GOLM1 was identified to be regulated by ZCRB1 through a post-transcriptional 3′UTR binding manner, partly mediated the promoting role of ZCRB1 in HCC.

Introduction

Liver cancer is a common malignant tumor with the sixth-highest incidence rate and the third-highest mortality rate worldwide, posing a serious threat to human health [1]. Among liver cancers, approximately 75%−85% are hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), 10%−15% are intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, and the remainder are rare special pathological subtypes [2]. Hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection is the primary cause of HCC globally, while genetic alterations in TERT, TP53 and CTNNB1 are associated with the development of HCC [3]. Surgical resection remains the first-line treatment recommended by clinical guidelines for patients with resectable tumors [[2], [3], [4], [5]]. Despite rapid advancements in targeted therapy and immunotherapy for malignant tumors in recent years, their therapeutic efficacy against HCC remains unsatisfactory [6]. Compared with other malignancies, HCC exhibits stronger invasiveness and metastatic potential, with relatively low resection rates, high postoperative recurrence rates, and poor drug sensitivity. These clinical characteristics lead to the higher mortality of HCC relative to other solid tumors [7,8]. As reported, the average postoperative recurrence rate of HCC is 60%−70%; even following the most radical surgical resection, the recurrence rate exceeds 50%. The 5-year overall survival rate of HCC patients is <30%. Restricted by multiple tumor lesions and residual hepatic dysfunction, secondary surgeries for recurrent HCC are often deemed unacceptable [2,7]. Exploring reliable predictive biomarkers for HCC recurrence and developing effective targeted therapeutic strategies remain urgent clinical priorities.

ZCRB1 (Zinc Finger CCHC-type and RNA-Binding Motif 1, also known as MADP-1 or RBM36) is a specific protein that possesses both RNA-binding motifs and CCHC zinc finger motifs [9]. As reported, ZCRB1 plays an important role in the synthesis of coronavirus RNA [10]. Several studies have revealed the role of ZCRB1 in human cancers. Guo et al. used bioinformatics methods to identify that ZCRB1 is involved in m6A RNA methylation in lung adenocarcinoma, and the three gene features composed of ZCRB1, ADH1C and YTHDC2 are reliable prognostic indicators for lung adenocarcinoma patients; ZCRB1 can be used as an independent risk factor for lung adenocarcinoma [11]. ZCRB1 is expressed at low levels in glioblastoma multiforme; ZCRB1/circHEATR5B/HEATR5B-881aa/JMJD5/PKM2 pathway inhibits aerobic glycolysis and proliferation of glioblastoma cells [12]. In head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, Chen et al. constructed a prognostic prediction model composed of six genes: ZCRB1, HNRNPC, RBM12B, SF3A2, SF3B3, and SRSF11. Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma tumors with high ZCRB1 expression have a higher risk score, and ZCRB1 knockout leads to abnormal splicing events in the DNA damage response [13]. Ma et al. and Fan Zhang et al. provided evidence that ZCRB1 has a pro-cancer effect in HCC [14,15]. The roles and mechanisms of ZCRB1 in human HCC are complex, and more in-depth research is needed.

In mammals, mRNAs commonly exhibit 3′-terminal polyadenylation (poly (A)) characteristics; approximately 70% of mammalian genes contain alternative polyadenylation (APA) sites, which may encode mRNA isoforms with different 3′UTRs [16,17]. The action of 3′UTR APA has a significant impact on the cellular localization, stability, and translation of mRNAs, and plays an important role in various physiological and pathological processes in the human body; activated or rapidly proliferating cells tend to utilize the 3′UTR proximal polyadenylation site (PAS) to produce mRNAs with shortened 3′UTR during the APA process; cells that are differentiated or in a quiescent non-proliferative state tend to utilize the 3′UTR distal PAS, resulting in the production of mRNAs with longer 3′UTR during APA processes [16,18]. RNA binding proteins, microRNAs (miRNAs), long non coding RNAs (LncRNAs) and circular RNAs (circRNAs) lose their binding sites on 3′UTR shortened mRNAs through miRNA regulation, mediating abnormal expression of regulatory genes [19,20]. Therefore, 3′UTR APA is an important mechanism associated with gene expression.

Methods

Clinical samples

In the present study, 5 groups of paraffin-embedded tissues including normal liver tissues, primary HCC tissues and recurrent HCC tissues (each group of tissues was from the same patient) were collected from the Department of Pathology in the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (Hefei, Anhui, China). (Inclusion criteria: Tissues from patients who underwent original surgical resection and secondary surgical resection between 2019 and 2024; sufficient paraffin-embedded tissues available for the study. Exclusion criteria: Patients with other diseases or patients who underwent special therapies before surgery were excluded.) Another 100 pairs of human HCC paraffin tissues and adjacent non-tumor normal liver paraffin tissues were also collected from the Department of Pathology in the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. The clinicopathological parameters of the 100 HCC patients were collected for subsequent analysis. (Inclusion criteria: Tissues from patients who underwent surgical resection between 2022 and 2024; sufficient paraffin-embedded tissues available for the study; patients with full clinicopathological parameter data. Exclusion criteria: Patients with other diseases or patients who underwent special therapies before surgery were excluded.) Moreover, 20 pairs of fresh human HCC tissues and adjacent non-tumor normal liver tissues were collected from the Department of General Surgery in the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. (Inclusion criteria: Tissues from patients who underwent surgical resection between 2023 and 2024; sufficient fresh tissues available for the study. Exclusion criteria: Patients with other diseases or patients who underwent special therapies before surgery were excluded.) This study plan was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (2025,238) and we performed this study in accordance with The Code of Ethics of the World Medical Association (Declaration of Helsinki). Written informed consent was obtained from all enrolled patients.

Cell culture

Human HCC cell lines Huh-7 and Hep3B were got from the Shanghai Cell Bank (Shanghai, China) and cultured in an incubator containing 5% CO₂ at 37 °C as recommended. Both cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM, Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Sangon, China).

Plasmid construction and stable cell line generation

In this study, we used lentiviral vector pLKO.1-Puro backbone for shRNA plasmid construction. ZCRB1 CDS with long or short 3′UTR sequences and GOLM1 CDS sequences were cloned into the lentiviral vectors pSIN—Neo for overexpression plasmid construction. Respective lentiviruses were produced using the 293T cells. Huh-7 and Hep3B cells were infected by the 293T supernatant containing respective lentivirus, and were selected using puromycin (2µg/ml) or G418 (600 µg/ml or 1.2 mg/ml) respectively. The shRNA sequences were as follows: ZCRB1 shRNA-1 (shZCRB1– 1) 5′-CCGGCAATGACTTGTACCGGATATTCTCGAGAATATCCGGTACAAGTCATTGTTTT TTG-3′; ZCRB1 shRNA-2 (shZCRB1– 2) 5′-CCGGGCATTGCTATTGACAATGGAACTCGAGT TCCATTGTCAATAGCAATGCTTTTTTG-3′; GOLM1 shRNA-1 (shGOLM1– 1) 5′-CCGGCGAATAGAAGAGGTCACCZCRB1CTCGAGTTTGGTGACCTCTTCTATTCGTTTTTTG-3′; GOLM1 shRNA-2 (shGOLM1– 2) 5′-CCGGGTTGAGZCRB1GAGGZCRB1C CAATCTCGAGATTGGTTTCCTCTTTCTCAACTTTTTTG-3′.

RNA-Seq analyse

The mRNA sequencing (RNA-Seq) assays, one for screening differentially expressed genes across five groups of paraffin-embedded tissues including normal liver tissues, primary HCC tissues and recurrent HCC tissues, and another for identifying differentially expressed genes between Huh-7 shZCRB1 cells and Huh-7 shNC cells, as well as between Hep3B shZCRB1 cells and Hep3B shNC cells, were performed at Kangcheng Biotechnology Company (Shanghai, China).

RT-quantitative PCR (qPCR) analysis

RT-qPCR analysis was performed to examine the mRNA levels of total ZCRB1, ZCRB1 transcripts containing different 3′UTR regions, and GOLM1 in patient tissues and HCC cells, respectively, essentially as described in our previous study using PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Perfect Real Time) (Takara, Japan) and TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Tli RNaseH Plus) (Takara) [21,22]. The primers used were as follows: ZCRB1 (Total), forward 5′-CTCTGCACZCRB1ACTGTACCAGG-3′ and reverse 5′-CTTCGGATGAACTCAGCTGCTC-3′; ZCRB1 (Region-1), forward 5′-GAGGAAGTAGAAGZCRB1GTGAAG-3′ and reverse 5′-CACTAAGTTCTTCCTCATCACTG-3′; ZCRB1 (Region-2), forward 5′-CATGCCTGATTT GTTTACATGAG-3′ and reverse 5′-CTCATGTZCRB1CZCRB1TCAGGCATG-3′; ZCRB1 (Region-3), forward 5′-CTGAGCCAAGGAATATGCTATG-3′ and reverse 5-CTCTAATC CCATTACCATATACC-3′; GOLM1, forward 5′-CAAGGGAAGGGZCRB1CGTGCTTG-3′ and reverse 5′-CTTCCACCTACAGGTCTGTCTTC-3′; GAPDH, forward 5′-TGCACCACCAACT GCTTAGC −3′ and reverse 5′- GGCATGGACTGTGGTCATGAG-3′.

RNA 3′RACE analysis

We performed RNA 3′RACE analysis to detect different isoforms of ZCRB1 with long and short 3′UTR essentially as described in our previous study [23]. The primers used were: 3′RACE-RT GCGAGCACAGAATTAATACGACTCACTATAGGTTTTTTTTTTTTT, ZCRB1, forward 5′- ATCAGATGATTCAAGACGCCCAAG-3′, and reverse 5′-GCGAGCACAGAATTAATACGAC T-3′.

Western blots

Western blots were carried out to examine protein levels of ZCRB1 and GOLM1 in tissues and cells respectively as described in our previous study [24]. Rabbit polyclonal antibody against ZCRB1 (25,629-1-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech Group, Inc., Chicago, USA), rabbit polyclonal antibody against GOLM1 (15,126-1-AP, 1:4000, Proteintech Group, Inc., Chicago, USA), and mouse monoclonal antibody against β-actin (66,009-1-Ig, 1:5000, Proteintech Group, Inc., Chicago, USA) were used.

Survival rate and gene level analysis based on the TCGA database

Based on the TCGA-Clinical Data Resource (CDR), we collected information about ZCRB1 expression levels and overall survival rates in 261 HCC patients, ZCRB1 expression levels and disease-free survival rates in 243 HCC patients, and expression levels of ZCRB1 in 61 HCC patients without early recurrence and 35 HCC patients with early recurrence. The “survminer” and “survival” packages in R were used for cut point selection of ZCRB1 expression and Kaplan-Meier curve analysis. Based on TCGA database, correlation analysis of expression levels of ZCRB1 and GOLM1 in HCC patients was carried out.

Cell proliferation and metastasis assays

In this study, we performed cell proliferation assays including total cell number assay, CCK-8 assay, cell colony formation assay and cell metastasis assays including cell migration assay, cell invasion assay to evaluate cell growth, proliferation, and metastasis, respectively, essentially as described in our previous studies [24,25]. For the total cell number assay, 1 × 105 corresponding cells (per well) were seeded into 6-well plates and subsequently counted once a day during a period of 5 days using cell counting boards. For the CCK-8 assay, 3000 corresponding cells (per well) were seeded into 96-well plates. After 72 h, 10 μl/well CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. The absorbance was detected at a 450 nm wavelength. For the cell colony formation assay, 1000 corresponding cells (per well) were seeded into 6-well plates. After 15 days, cell colonies were stained with crystal violet and quantified. For cell migration and invasion assays, Transwell chambers were utilized. In the migration assay, cells migrated directly from the upper chambers to the lower chambers; in the invasion assay, Matrigel was pre-coated in the upper chambers, and cells invaded through the Matrigel before migrating into the lower chambers. Cell migration ability of Huh-7 and Hep3B-derived cells was detected after 24 h of incubation, while cell invasion ability was assessed after 48 h of incubation.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Immunohistochemical analysis was performed to examine the protein levels of ZCRB1, GOLM1 and Ki-67 levels in human tissues and tumor tissues from mice. We carried out immunohistochemistry as described in our previous studies using the Ultra-Sensitive-SP kit (Maixin-Bio, Fuzhou, China) [22,25]. Rabbit polyclonal antibody against ZCRB1 (25,629-1-AP, 1:100, Proteintech Group, Inc., Chicago, USA), rabbit polyclonal antibody against GOLM1 (15,126-1-AP, 1:300, Proteintech Group, Inc., Chicago, USA), and mouse monoclonal antibody against Ki-67 (1:1, Zhongshan Goldenbridge Biotechnology Co, Beijing, China) were used. For ZCRB1/GOLM1 analysis in human HCC and/or adjacent non-tumor normal liver tissues, the IRS scoring system was employed. Scores 0–3 was designated to be Weak; Scores 4–8 was designated to be Moderate; Scores 9–12 was designated to be Strong. Weak and Moderate staining were designated to be Low expression; Strong staining was designated to be High expression. For ZCRB1, GOLM1 and Ki-67 analysis in mouse tumors, positively stained cells were calculated.

Mouse experiments

This study protocol for mouse experiments was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Anhui Medical University (LLSC20250195). For subcutaneous tumorigenicity assay, Hep3B-derived cells (5 × 106 cells/125 µl/site) were injected into the flank of 4-week-old BALB/c-nu/nu mice. Six injection sites were set for each cell line, and palpable tumors formed after 5–7 days. Tumor volume was calculated every 5 days: Volume (mm3) = L×W2 ×Π/6, (L, length; W, width). Mice were sacrificed after 30 days, and tumors were harvested. Tumor weight was calculated and then the tumors in each group were prepared for histologic examination. For tumor metastasis assay, Hep3B-derived cells (5 × 106 cells in 250 µl of PBS) were injected into the lateral tail vein of 4-week-old BALB/c-nu/nu mice. Each group contained 10 mice. Mice were sacrificed after 40 days, and their lungs were harvested. The number of pulmonary micro-metastases per mouse was quantified by histological assessment using 5 randomly selected hematoxylin and eosin-stained sections.

Luciferase reporter assay

We performed luciferase reporter assay using the Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega Corp.).

mRNA decay assay

In Huh-7-shNC, Huh-7-shZCRB1– 1, Huh-7-shZCRB1– 2, Hep3B-shNC, Hep3B-shZCRB1– 1, Hep3B-shZCRB1– 2 cells, actinomycin D (10 μg/ml) was added and cells were harvested at 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8 h respectively. The RNA levels of GOLM1 were examined by RT-qPCR.

Ribonucleoprotein (RNP) immunoprecipitation (IP) (RNP-IP) assay

RNP-IP assay was performed as described in our previous study [26]. The ZCRB1 protein-GOLM1 mRNA complex was captured by the anti-ZCRB1 antibody, and the mRNA levels of GOLM1 were examined by RT-qPCR. IgG served as the negative control.

Biotin pulldown assay

Essentially as described previously, biotin pulldown assay was carried out using the MAXIScript T7 kit (Invitrogen) and Streptavidin-coupled Dynabeads (Invitrogen) [26]. Western blot analysis was performed to examine the protein-mRNA complex.

Statistical analyses

Experiments involved in this study were all performed for at least three times, and all figures represent the average results. Unpaired two-tailed t- test was used to analyze results of RT-qPCR, total cell number assay, CCK-8 assay, cell colony formation assay, cell migration and invasion assays, mouse tumor volume/weight/metastasis analyses, IHC calculation, and luciferase reporter assay. Kaplan-Meier curve analyses were performed with the log-rank test. Correlation analyses were performed using Pearson’s chi-square test. P < 0.05 represented statistically significant.

Results

ZCRB1 was highly expressed in HCC and recurrent HCC tissues

To screen out potential important genes in human HCC, especially in recurrent HCC, RNA sequencing was performed on samples from 5 groups, including normal liver tissues, primary HCC tissues and recurrent HCC tissues (each tissue set was obtained from the same patient). As shown in Fig. 1A, several candidate genes including NUDT17, CGREF1, ELL3, ZCRB1, CCDC170 and others were highly expressed in HCC tissues relative to normal liver tissues. The expression level of ZCRB1 was higher in primary HCC tissues than in normal liver tissues, and further elevated in recurrent HCC tissues compared with primary HCC tissues; ZCRB1 exhibited the most prominent differential expression trend across normal liver, primary HCC and recurrent HCC tissues. The expression changes of ZCRB1 among these 5 groups were further validated via RT-qPCR, and the results were consistent with the RNA sequencing data (Fig. 1B). We collected 100 pairs of paraffin-embedded human HCC tissues and matched adjacent non-tumor liver tissues, and detected the protein expression of ZCRB1 by immunohistochemistry. As shown in Fig. 1C, immunoreactive ZCRB1 protein was mainly located in the nucleus of hepatocytes and HCC cells. In normal liver tissues, ZCRB1 protein expression was strong in 9/100 cases, moderate in 33/100 cases and weak in 58/100 cases; by contrast, in HCC tissues, strong expression was observed in 51/100 cases, moderate expression in 38/100 cases and weak expression in 11/100 cases. In addition, 20 pairs of fresh HCC tissues and matched adjacent non-tumor liver tissues were subjected to western blot analysis to detect ZCRB1 protein levels. As shown in Fig. 1D, ZCRB1 was significantly upregulated in 19 out of 20 fresh HCC tissue samples compared with paired normal tissues. Collectively, ZCRB1 protein expression was markedly higher in human HCC tissues than those in adjacent normal liver tissues.

Association of ZCRB1 expression with clinicopathological features in HCC patients.

Furthermore, based on the TCGA database, we collected information of ZCRB1 expression levels and survival rates in HCC patients, and performed association analysis by Kaplan-Meier method. As shown in Fig. 1E, both overall survival (OS) rate (P < 0.0001) and disease-free survival (DFS) rate were significantly lower in patients with high ZCRB1 expression compared with patients with low ZCRB1 expression. In monolayer cultures, ZCRB1-KD (knockdown) significantly reduced total cell numbers over a period of 5 days in both Huh-7 and Hep3B cells (Fig. 2B). Concordantly, ZCRB1-KD-mediated ZCRB1 depletion markedly suppressed cell viability (verified via CCK-8 assay) and cell colony formation in Huh-7 and Hep3B cells (Fig. 2C, D). Moreover, Transwell assay results revealed that cell migration and invasion abilities were significantly impaired in Huh-7 and Hep3B cells transfected with shZCRB1– 1 or shZCRB1– 2 relative to shNC controls (Fig. 2E-G).

To further explore the effects of ZCRB1 on tumor growth and metastasis in vivo, Hep3B-shNC/Hep3B-shZCRB1– 1 + Hep3B-shZCRB1– 2 (designated as Hep3B-shZCRB1) cells were subcutaneously injected into the dorsal flanks and intravenously administered into nude mice, respectively. As shown in Fig. 2H, the tumor growth curves indicated that ZCRB1 depletion robustly inhibited subcutaneous tumor growth in mice over 30 days of observation. At the experimental endpoint, the weight of tumors derived from Hep3B-shZCRB1 cells was significantly lower than that of Hep3B-shNC-derived tumors (Fig. 2I). Immunohistochemical staining further confirmed that the proportions of ZCRB1-positive and Ki-67-positive cells were markedly decreased in Hep3B-shZCRB1 xenograft tumors compared with control tumors (P < 0.001) (Fig. 2J). Consistently, in the mouse tail vein injection metastasis model, lung metastases were observed in 6 out of 10 mice in the Hep3B-shNC group, whereas only 2 out of 10 mice developed lung metastases in the Hep3B-shZCRB1 group. In addition, the total number of lung micro-metastatic foci was significantly reduced in mice injected with Hep3B-shZCRB1 cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2K).

Taken together, these results demonstrate that ZCRB1 promotes the proliferation and metastasis of HCC cells both in vitro and in vivo.

GOLM1 was regulated by ZCRB1 in HCC cells

To identify the downstream mechanism of ZCRB1 in HCC cells, mRNA sequencing was carried out in Huh-7-shNC/Huh-7-shZCRB1– 1 + Huh-7-shZCRB1– 2 (designated as Huh-7-shZCRB1) and Hep3B-shNC/Hep3B-shZCRB1– 1 + Hep3B-shZCRB1– 2 (designated as Hep3B-shZCRB1) cells respectively. As shown in Fig. 3A, a total of 327 genes, including PYGL, XKR8, RRM1, GOLM1, EMX1, etc. decreased significantly in Huh-7-shZCRB1 cells compared with Huh-7-shNC cells. Meanwhile, 421 genes including SHOX1, GNAZ, GOLM1, LUZP1, WDR19 etc. exhibited markedly reduced expression in Hep3B-shZCRB1 cells compared with Hep3B-shNC cells. Twenty-four genes displayed a consistent downward expression trend in both Huh-7 and Hep3B cells following ZCRB1 knockdown. Among these 24 overlapping genes, GOLM1 exhibited prominent downregulation upon ZCRB1 depletion in both cell lines. Combined with KEGG and GO enrichment analyses (Fig.S1), GOLM1 was selected as the target gene for subsequent investigation. To validate the mRNA-sequencing results, RT-qPCR and western blot assays were performed to measure mRNA and protein levels of GOLM1 in Huh-7 and Hep3B cells with ZCRB1 knockdown. Consistently, both the mRNA and protein abundance of GOLM1 decreased significantly in Huh-7 and Hep3B cells with ZCRB1-KD compared with control groups (Fig. 3B, C). Analysis based on the TCGA database revealed a positive correlation between ZCRB1 and GOLM1 expression in HCC patient samples (R = 0.129, P = 0.013) (Fig. 3D). Immunohistochemical staining of 100 clinical paraffin-embedded human HCC tissue specimens further confirmed a positive correlation between ZCRB1 and GOLM1 protein expression (Fig. 3E). GOLM1 was regulated by ZCRB1 through a post-transcriptional 3′UTR binding manner

Using mRNA decay assay, we found that ZCRB1-KD dramatically increased the mRNA decay rate of GOLM1 in both Huh-7 and Hep3B cells (Fig. 4A). To further study the mechanisms of the regulation of GOLM1 by ZCRB1, we constructed a psiCHECK-2-GOLM1– 3′UTR plasmid (Fig. 4B) and performed luciferase reporter assay. As shown in Fig. 4C, the levels of GOLM1 3′UTR luciferase reporter activity decreased significantly in ZCRB1-KD cells compared with control cells. In ribonucleoprotein (RNP) immunoprecipitation (IP) assay, anti-ZCRB1 antibody dramatically enriched the GOLM1 mRNA compared with control IgG group in both Huh-7 and Hep3B cells (Fig. 4D). Moreover, as determined by biotin pulldown assay, ZCRB1 directly bound to the 3′UTR region of GOLM1 but did not bind to the 5′UTR or CR regions of GOLM1 mRNA; ZCRB1 did not bind to the mutant 3′UTR region of GOLM1 mRNA (Fig. 4E). By biotin pulldown assay using mutant 3′UTR region (CGGAACCG) of GOLM1, we identified that the ZCRB1 binding sequence of GOLM1 3′UTR was TACTTAAT. GOLM1 promoted proliferation and metastasis of HCC cells

Next, the biological function of GOLM1 in HCC progression was further investigated. GOLM1 protein expression in both Huh-7 and Hep3B cells was significantly decreased following GOLM1 knockdown (GOLM1-KD) (Fig. 5A). During a 5-day continuous culture period, Huh-7 shGOLM1– 1, Huh-7 shGOLM1– 2, Hep3B shGOLM1– 1, and Hep3B shGOLM1– 2 cells exhibited markedly reduced cell numbers compared with their corresponding shNC control cells, respectively (Fig. 5B, C). Consistent with this finding, GOLM1 knockdown significantly impaired cell viability in the CCK-8 assay and suppressed colony formation capacity in both Huh-7 and Hep3B cell lines (Fig. 5D, E). In addition, depletion of GOLM1 also remarkably inhibited the migration and invasion abilities of HCC cells (Fig. 5F, G). GOLM1 mediated cell proliferation and metastasis promoting role of ZCRB1 in HCC cells

To examine whether the promotive effects of ZCRB1 on the proliferation and metastasis of HCC cells were mediated by GOLM1, cell functional experiments were performed in Huh-7 and Hep3B cells with ZCRB1 knockdown (KD) via shRNA (a combination of shZCRB1– 1 and shZCRB1– 2) and GOLM1 overexpression via plasmid transfection. As shown in Fig. 6A, GOLM1 protein levels were significantly decreased in ZCRB1-KD cells, and GOLM1 overexpression restored the downregulated GOLM1 protein levels in ZCRB1-KD cells. Consistent with previous results, total cell number, cell viability (CCK-8 assay), colony formation, migration, and invasion abilities were markedly reduced in ZCRB1-depleted Huh-7 and Hep3B cells. Notably, forced GOLM1 expression effectively reversed the impaired oncogenic phenotypes induced by ZCRB1 depletion (Fig. 6B-F, Fig. S2A-C). Moreover, in vivo subcutaneous tumor formation and tail vein injection metastasis assays were performed in Hep3B cells co-transfected with shNC/shZCRB1 and empty vector/GOLM1 overexpression (GOLM1-OE) plasmids. Consistent with the in vitro findings, ZCRB1 knockdown significantly suppressed subcutaneous tumor growth, as evidenced by reduced tumor growth curves and tumor weight. Additionally, ZCRB1 depletion decreased the proportion of Ki-67-positive proliferating cells and attenuated lung metastasis in the tail vein injection model. Importantly, all these inhibitory phenotypes caused by ZCRB1-KD were successfully rescued by GOLM1 overexpression (Fig. 6G-J, Fig. S2D). To explore the biological functions of distinct ZCRB1 isoforms bearing short or long 3′UTR in HCC cells, we constructed plasmids containing the ZCRB1 CR/short 3′UTR regions and the CR/long 3′UTR regions which were designated as ZCRB1– 3US and ZCRB1– 3UL respectively. As shown by the luciferase reporter assay, luciferase reporter activities were markedly higher in both Huh-7 and Hep3B cells transfected with ZCRB1– 3US than in those transfected with ZCRB1– 3UL (Fig. 7C). As determined by western blot, both ZCRB1– 3US and ZCRB1– 3UL increased the protein levels of ZCRB1 and GOLM1 in Huh-7 and Hep3B cells; cells transfected with ZCRB1– 3US exhibited significantly higher ZCRB1 and GOLM1 protein expression than those transfected with ZCRB1– 3UL (Fig. 7D). Consistent with the above results, since transfection with ZCRB1– 3US or ZCRB1– 3UL increased ZCRB1 expression, both constructs significantly enhanced the proliferation, migration and invasion capacities of Huh-7 and Hep3B cells, as verified by cell counting, CCK-8, colony formation and Transwell assays, respectively. Notably, Huh-7 and Hep3B cells transfected with ZCRB1– 3US displayed markedly stronger proliferative, migratory and invasive abilities than ZCRB1– 3UL-transfected cells (Fig. 7E-J). Collectively, the short 3′UTR ZCRB1 isoform upregulates ZCRB1 expression and facilitates the proliferation and metastasis of HCC cells.

Discussion

In this study, we found the expression level of ZCRB1 was critically enhanced in human HCC tissues compared with normal liver tissues and in recurrent HCC tissues compared with primary HCC tissues. High expression level of ZCRB1 was correlated with worse clinicopathological parameters including lymph node metastasis, TNM stage, as well as lower OS and DFS rates in HCC patients. In HCC cells, knockdown of ZCRB1 dramatically decreased total cell number, cell viability, colony formation, cell migration, invasion and tumor growth/metastasis in mice. As reported previously, the expression levels of ZCRB1 are different in different normal human tissues, with the highest expression levels in the heart and testes and the lowest expression level in liver tissue. However, among several randomly detected malignant tumor cells (including gastric cancer, B-cell lymphoma, T-cell lymphoma, cervical cancer, and liver cancer), ZCRB1 showed the highest expression level in liver cancer cells [9]. Ma et al. constructed a five-gene (CDKN2A, DLAT, KLF9, GEMIN2, ZCRB1) HCC scoring system related to copper homeostasis and copper toxicity; the high-risk score indicated poor clinical prognosis, increased tumor malignancy, and immune suppression in the tumor microenvironment; depletion of ZCRB1 with siRNA could decrease cell proliferation and metastasis in HCC cells [14]. Fan Zhang et al. demonstrated that inhibition of ZCRB1 mediates retention of the USP21 minor intron to suppress malignant progression in hepatocellular carcinoma [15]. These findings were concordant with our present results. Therefore, ZCRB1 was an important biomarker involved in human HCC malignant behaviors, but also tumor recurrency.

3′UTR APA was involved in the regulation of gene expression and contributed to many physiological and pathological processes. Previous studies have shown that APA causes extensive shortening of the 3′UTR in tumors, and overall shortening of the 3′UTR in tumor cells is significantly associated with poor patient prognosis and high proliferation, metastasis, and invasion capabilities of tumor cells [27,28]. In tumors, shortening of the 3′UTR of some oncogenes can mediate an increase in their expression levels; during gene expression, the 3′end of the gene terminates abnormally in the intron region, producing mutated proteins with small molecular weight that promote the occurrence and development of tumors; shortening of the 3′UTR in tumors leads to the loss of function of competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) [29,30]. In the present study, we found that the isoforms of ZCRB1 with shortened 3′UTR increased, mediating the enhanced expression level of ZCRB1 in HCC cells. Meanwhile, ZCRB1 CR-short 3′UTR stably transfected HCC cells showed increased oncogenic behaviors including proliferation, migration and invasion. Therefore, we uncovered that the high level of ZCRB1 in human (recurrent) HCC was mediated by alternative polyadenylation and 3′UTR shortening. Core CPA (cleavage and polyadenylation) factors, splicing, transcription dynamics, chromatin structure and modifications, RNA modifications, and RNA-binding proteins contribute to 3′UTR APA [31]. However, the exact alternative splicing regulatory factors involved in ZCRB1 3′UTR shortening were not demonstrated in the present study, which needs to be further studied in our future work. High-throughput sequencing helps detect 3′UTR shortening, and targeting APA associated RBPs (RNA binding proteins) or CPA factors involved in ZCRB1 regulation were potential therapy strategy for human HCC [32].

For downstream mechanism, we demonstrated GOLM1 was positively regulated by ZCRB1 through a direct 3′UTR binding manner. Knockdown of GOLM1 retarded the malignant behaviors of HCC cells and rescue experiments showed the promoting role of ZCRB1 in the proliferation and metastasis of HCC cells was partly mediated by GOLM1. As reported, GOLM1 was a biomarker for early diagnosis and prognosis in HCC; GOLM1 was involved in immune regulation, regulation of intracellular signaling factors and promotion of epithelial-mesenchymal transition; GOLM1 promoted HCC progression and metastasis [33]. Lan Zhen et al. demonstrated that GOLM1-ACLY pathway regulated macrophage-secreted EFEMP1 via H3K27ac modifications to drive tumor progression in HCC [34]. Through augmented GSK-3β/MMPs, mTOR/miR-145-regulated exosomal GOLM1 performed a tumor promoting role in HCC [35]. EGFR/RTK cell-surface recycling was modulated by GOLM1, promoting HCC metastasis [36]. Jingliang He et al. revealed that targeted degradation of GOLM1 by CC-885 via CRL4-CRBN E3 ligase inhibited HCC progression [37]. GOLM1-CSN5 positive feedback loop mediated acquired Lenvatinib resistance in HCC upon EGFR signaling activation [38]. These findings were consistent. We have expanded the mechanism of GOLM1 in the oncogenic role on HCC in this study. Moreover, GOLM1 was directly regulated by miR-27b in prostate cancer cells, contributing to malignant behaviors of prostate cancer cells [39]; miR-200a repressed GOLM1 expression by directly targeting its 3′UTR, and GOLM1 promoted lung adenocarcinoma cells proliferation [40]. In this study, we demonstrated that in HCC cells, binding of ZCRB1 in GOLM1 3′UTR antagonized the effect of miR-27b and miR-200a in GOLM1 3′UTR, thereby promoting the expression of GOLM1.

This study has several limitations. First, the initial RNA sequencing analysis was performed on only 5 groups of paired normal liver tissues, primary HCC tissues, and recurrent HCC tissues (all specimens were collected from matched individual patients). Second, all clinical tissue samples enrolled in this study were obtained from a single clinical center. Third, only two HCC cell lines (Huh-7 and Hep3B) were applied for in vitro functional validation. Fourth, the in vivo experiments were conducted solely in immunodeficient mice; immune-competent mouse models and orthotopic HCC models were not included in this study. Finally, the upstream regulatory mechanisms driving 3′UTR shortening of ZCRB1 remain unidentified.

Conclusion

In summary, our findings demonstrate the oncogenic role of the ZCRB1/GOLM1 regulatory axis in HCC progression. As illustrated in Fig. 8, under normal physiological conditions, ZCRB1 is predominantly expressed as the long 3′UTR isoform with a relatively low expression level. ZCRB1 binds to the 3′UTR region of GOLM1 and positively modulates GOLM1 expression. In (recurrent) HCC cells, the proportion of the ZCRB1 short 3′UTR isoform is significantly elevated, which consequently increases the overall expression of ZCRB1. As a downstream target gene positively regulated by ZCRB1, GOLM1 expression is also upregulated. The aberrantly activated ZCRB1/GOLM1 axis exerts oncogenic effects to promote malignant phenotypes of HCC cells.
