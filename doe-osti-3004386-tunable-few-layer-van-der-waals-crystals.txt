Final Technical Report
 EPSCoR-State/National Laboratory Partnership
 Tunable Few-Layer van der Waals Crystals and Heterostructures as Emerging
 Energy and Quantum Materials
                                     Project No. DE-SC0023437
 PIs: P. Sutter, E. Sutter, University of Nebraska-Lincoln

1. Goals of the Project
2D and layered (van der Waals) semiconductors offer extraordinary opportunities for manipulating
optically excited charge carriers, many-body excitations, and non-charge based quantum numbers.
To date, research has focused on a limited group of materials, mostly transition metal
dichalcogenides in the monolayer limit. Other van der Waals semiconductors, and especially few-
layer to multilayer crystals and their heterostructures, carry large potential for the discovery of
phenomena of interest for future energy and information technologies. But they remain largely
unexplored, often due to a lack of access to high-quality materials and approaches for measuring
their properties at the relevant scales. The goal of this project was to develop an EPSCoR-
State/National Laboratory Partnership that addresses the challenges of preparing high-quality van
der Waals semiconductors and of probing their structure, composition, and especially their
optoelectronic and photonic properties, near the atomic scale using electron microscopy techniques.
A central component of the project was the development of advanced methods for electron
microscopy and electron-excited spectroscopy, taking advantage of unique samples as well as
leading capabilities and expertise at the partner institutions. Efforts to advance leading-edge
techniques was supported by ancillary developments, such as precision sample preparation for
electron microscopy/spectroscopy, coordinated chemical imaging, and analytical electron
microscopy. Experiments in materials synthesis and technique development were closely linked to
theory and computation.
The results obtained under this project yielded multifaceted benefits to the involved partners and
their institutions, DOE-BES, the wider scientific community, and society at large, particularly in
the State of Nebraska through dividends from knowledge and human capital generated under the
project.

2. Major Accomplishments
a) Multilayer van der Waals heterostructures with atomically connected lateral interfaces
[ACS Nano (2023), DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.3c02411]
Compared to traditional heterostructure architectures based on atomically thin 2D crystals, lateral
heterostructures in the few-layer and multilayer regime stand out due to their enhanced optical
thickness combined with emergent interfacial effects on charge carriers, many-body excitations, or
photonic modes. We performed correlated synthesis, electron microscopy, STEM-
cathodoluminescence spectroscopy, and complementary measurements to investigate the structure
and properties of multilayer lateral heterostructures of the van der Waals semiconductors SnS and
GeSe, which are of broad interest for their optoelectronic, valleytronic, ferroelectric, and
thermoelectric properties.
A two-step synthesis process resulted in a purely lateral integration of the components with sharp
interfaces. High-resolution STEM imaging on cross-sectional samples, obtained in partnership with
Brookhaven National Laboratory, demonstrated a stunning level of atomic connectivity where
individual SnS and GeSe atomic layers are covalently stitched to form correlated interfaces across

hundreds of layers. Atomic force microscopy and Kelvin probe force microscopy demonstrated
height alignment of the components to within a few atomic layers and a sharp transition in the
electrostatic potential across the lateral interface.
Cathodoluminescence spectroscopy combined with density-functional theory was used to probe
bandgaps, band alignments, and interfacial carrier flows. Luminescence far from interfaces showed
the characteristics of the individual semiconducting components. Near the interface, strain effects
(in GeSe) and luminescence quenching due to carrier separation (in SnS) were detected. In addition
to these optoelectronic properties, the lateral heterostructures promise reduced thermal conductance
combined with high electrical conductivity, i.e., they will be of interest as thermoelectrics,
especially if lateral superlattices with many interfaces can be formed.
b) Development of monochromated STEM-EELS for probing electronic excitations and phonons
Through partnership with Oak Ridge National Laboratory, experiments using a Nion Hermes
monochromated and aberration-corrected (MAC) STEM instrument were conducted at highest
energy resolution to probe electronic excitations and local vibrational properties in van der Waals
semiconductors and heterostructures.
Series of experiments were carried out both on plan-view and focused ion beam milled cross-
sectional samples comprising SnS-GeSe multilayer lateral heterostructures. Valence EELS showed
for the first time absorption edges due to both major conduction band valleys in SnS at 1.28 eV and
1.45 eV, respectively, as well as signatures due to near-interface band bending consistent with those
seen in STEM-CL. Off-axis EELS, used to probe phonons, showed distinct vibrational signatures
in SnS and GeSe on either side of the interface.
c) Whispering gallery modes as probes of chirality effects in single nanostructures
[Materials Horizons (2023), DOI: 10.1039/D3MH00693J]
Chirality plays an important role in diverse scientific and technological contexts. Chiral optical
phenomena are typically explored by spatially averaging measurements on ensembles of
nanostructures, which is problematic for two reasons: (i) advanced synthesis is required to avoid
racemic mixtures of structures with opposite chirality; and (ii) scenarios requiring individual chiral
nanostructures cannot be addressed.
We overcame these limitations by developing STEM-cathodoluminescence for probing whispering
gallery modes (WGMs) in single nanostructures, using nanowires of the layered semiconductor
GeS. By joining a chiral segment (a growth spiral around an axial screw dislocation) to an achiral
(non-dislocated) segment, such wires allowed the direct comparison of chiral and achiral photonics
in the same measurement. Cathodoluminescence linescans showed characteristic dispersive fringes
resulting from electron beam excitation of WGMs and their damping by light emission. Comparing
such data with simulations, we identified a systematic blue shift as a telltale characteristic of chiral
WGMs. The results represent the first experimental detection of chiral photonics in single
nanostructures, paving the way for the broader exploration of chirality effects for integrated
photonic information processing systems.
d) Multimaterials heterostructures of multilayer van der Waals crystals
[Small (2024), DOI: 10.1002/smll.202307372]
Building on progress in the synthesis and probing of lateral heterostructures between pairs of
layered crystals, we carried out pioneering work on joining multiple layered materials with sharp,
atomically connected lateral interfaces. Specifically, a multilayer SnS seed crystal was laterally
connected to a sequence of several other layered crystals.
Raman spectroscopy and cross-sectional transmission electron microscopy documented the tight
integration of the components with lateral interfaces across all layers. In terms of emerging

functionality, we explored the possibility of using joined crystals with different bandgaps to filter
photonic modes. Uniform van der Waals crystals carry two principal modes, lateral waveguide
modes and out-of-plane modes. Reflected by specular side- or top/bottom facets, both modes
undergo self-interference in the material, producing complex light emission patterns. Interference
between lateral modes can be suppressed by damping via absorption by a narrow-gap material.
Here, an active GeS1-xSex alloy was laterally sandwiched between SnS and GeSe, eliminating lateral
waveguide mode interference and promoting pure vertical (Fabry-Perot) interference. STEM-
cathodoluminescence was used to demonstrate this photonic filtering. CL linescans showed unusual
luminescence within the active GeS1-xSex layers comprising several sharp, discrete emission peaks,
explained as Fabry-Perot interference of electron-beam excited vertical waveguide modes.
Building on our EPSCoR State/National Laboratory partnership, this research addressed challenges
in materials synthesis and nanometer-scale spectroscopy. The results demonstrated the possibility
of designing heterostructures to filter competing photonic modes and thus realize light emission at
within narrow bands at tunable photon energy.
e) Complex van der Waals heterostructures
[Materials Today Chemistry (2024), DOI: 10.1016/j.mtchem.2024.102064]
The layered semiconductor SnSe has been of long-standing interest for its promising thermoelectric
and ferroelectric properties. Integration of SnSe with other layered crystals can modify charge- and
thermal transport, polarization, and other properties such as chemical stability or optoelectronics.
We demonstrated the synthesis of SnSe single-crystals that are spontaneously encapsulated in a
thin, layered SnSe2-xSx shell. Subsequent exposure to GeSe vapor caused the partial reduction of
the wrap-around SnSe2-xSx shell to open up edge sites, which allowed the lateral stitching to GeSe
while preserving the dichalcogenide covering the basal facets. This complex architecture was
confirmed by multiple techniques including analytical electron microscopy and diffraction as well
as cathodoluminescence spectroscopy. The results expanded the capabilities for materials
integration by forming heterostructures with both vertical (van der Waals) and lateral (covalent)
interfaces between layered semiconductors.
f) Development of electron microscopy methods for the atomistic analysis of domains in large
few-layer van der Waals ferroics
[ACS Nano (2024), DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.4c11341; Journal of the American Chemical Society
(2024), DOI: 10.1021/jacs.4c11558]
The PIs’ recent discovery of methods for growing few-layer van der Waals crystals to
unprecedented sizes presented special opportunities for developing electron microscopy techniques
for the analysis of ferroic domains and domain walls, both in homogeneous crystals and in
heterostructures. To support such efforts, protocols were conceived for transferring ultrathin
monochalcogenide crystals and heterostructures from their growth substrate (mica) to ultrathin
electron transparent membranes suitable for imaging and diffraction analysis across large areas.
Combined electron microscopy and nanobeam electron diffraction were applied to analyze stripe
domains in large few-layer crystals of the van der Waals ferroelectrics SnS and SnSe, as well as
lateral SnS-SnSe heterostructures. Imaging showed atomically sharp domain walls aligned along
{110} in-plane directions. Nanobeam electron diffraction detected single-crystalline material
within the domains and identified the domain walls as a twin boundaries. In some cases, twinning
was not observed but unusual, purely rotational domain walls were found between stripe domains.
In heterostructrures, the transfer of domains across lateral interfaces was investigated. In-situ
imaging and diffraction at variable temperatures, finally, provided the first measurements of Curie
temperatures in this new class of layered ferroelectrics.

The development of advanced sample preparation, microscopy, and diffraction techniques provided
unique insight into the crystallography of ferroic domains in layered van der Waals crystals, thus
contributing to the development of these materials for applications such as nanoelectronics,
neuromorphic computing, and shift-current photovoltaics.
g) Novel van der Waals semiconductors with exceptional promise for electronics
[Advanced Electronic Materials (2025), DOI: 10.1002/aelm.202400691]
Van der Waals semiconductors combine several attractive characteristics for applications in
electronics, including chemical stability, compatibility with arbitrary substrates, widely tunable
carrier concentration by gating, and – in some cases – high carrier mobility. However, most van
der Waals semiconductors show low carrier concentrations, limiting the achievable current
densities in devices.
Few-layer SnSe2 crystals, investigated in this project, can provide a solution to this issue.
Exfoliation from high-quality bulk crystals was used to obtain field-effect transistors (FETs) with
few-layer SnSe2 channels. Measurements on FETs gated by a de-ionized water solution gate
showed exceptionally high room-temperature carrier mobilities (exceeding 800 cm2V-1s-1) along
with high on-off current ratios (>105) and subthreshold swing below the thermodynamic limit for
Si FETs. A unique combination of high carrier mobility and large sheet carrier density was
confirmed by Hall-effect measurements. Probing light-matter interactions, finally, the SnSe2 FETs
showed a gate voltage controlled switching between negative and positive photoconductance,
promising for advanced photodetectors.
The combined results demonstrated a combination of exceptional electronic properties, qualifying
van der Waals semiconductors as serious competitors to established materials in applications such
as high-performance computing, displays, and sensing.
h) Band structure engineering in anisotropic van der Waals semiconductors
[Small (2025), DOI: 10.1002/smll.202508578]
Alloying is one of the main tools of bandgap engineering, allowing the tuning of crystal structure,
lattice parameters, and electronic structure of 3D and 2D/layered semiconductors. Among the latter,
it can play a key role in tailoring the properties of tin monochalcogenides, a class of van der Waals
semiconductors of interest for optoelectronics, thermoelectrics, ferroelectrics, and valleytronics.
In collaboration with National Laboratory partners and an international team of experts, we
investigated the synthesis and properties of large flakes of the anion substitution alloys SnSe1-xSx.
Alloy flakes across a wide range of compositions were obtained systematically by repeated growth
from the same mixed (SnS, SnSe) powder precursor. Combined experiment and theory showed full
miscibility for all compositions, along with tunable lattice constants, bandgaps, and vibrational
modes. Atomic resolution electron microscopy identified a surprising composition profile,
characterized by the accumulation of S and Se in alternating atomic layers of the SnSe1-xSx unit cell.
Polarized Raman spectroscopy confirmed anisotropic vibrational modes, while both the calculated
and measured band structure – accessed experimentally via advanced angle-resolved photoelectron
spectroscopy (nano-ARPES) – showed systematic changes in the band edge energies and
anisotropic electronic structure due to the anisotropic in-plane lattice of the monochalcogenides.
Optoelectronic analysis by cathodoluminescence spectroscopy, finally, concluded that a unique
configuration of two non-degenerate, direct-bandgap valleys along orthogonal k-space directions
persists all the way from SnS to SnSe, making SnSe1-xSx alloys interesting for valleytronics.
The combined results provided a basis for tuning a class of anisotropic van der Waals
semiconductors by alloying, so as to enhance their properties for applications in energy conversion,
energy efficient computing, and information storage.

3. Opportunities for Training and Professional Development Provided by the Project
This EPSCoR-State/National Laboratory partnership presented outstanding opportunities for
training and professional development to all participants. In addition to senior researchers at the
University of Nebraska-Lincoln, Oak Ridge National Laboratory, and Brookhaven National
Laboratory, this included 4 graduate students as well as 2 postdoctoral research associates who
were provided with advanced training in different experimental and computational techniques
developed and applied in this project.
The project fostered additional domestic and international collaborations, e.g., on theory and
computation (USA, Finland), charge transport in van der Waals semiconductors (USA, China), and
advanced synchrotron measurements of electronic structure at the nanometer scale (Italy).

6. Project Impact
This EPSCoR-State/National Laboratory partnership has created impact at multiple levels.
Leveraging unique capabilities and expertise at the partner institutions, the fundamental research
pursued under the project fostered advanced technique development, especially in the areas electron
microscopy, diffraction, and electron-stimulated optoelectronic spectroscopy at atomic to
nanometer scales. Advances in technique development were enabled by breakthroughs in the
synthesis of van der Waals semiconductors, for example the creation of multilayer heterostructures
with layer-by-layer connected lateral interfaces between different layered materials; nanostructures
combining chiral and achiral segments; and few-layer ferroics of narrow-bandgap semiconductors
with unprecedented crystal size. Use of the resulting unique samples made possible important
scientific advances, such as the first identification of chiral light-matter interactions in single
nanostructures and the atomic-scale characterization of stripe domains and domain walls in van der
Waals ferroics. Impact beyond the primary discipline of the project was achieved through advances
in understanding gated charge transport in few-layer van der Waals semiconductors, and by
providing insight into alloying and interface formation that can translate into enhanced performance
in computing and energy conversion applications.
In addition to these scientific and technological impacts, the EPSCoR State/National Laboratory
partnership had impact on human resource development and workforce training by creating
extensive opportunities for training and career development to all participants, including students,
postdocs, and senior researchers. Finally, the project contributed to improving domestic and
international scientific cooperation through targeted collaborations on topics of interest to this
partnership.
