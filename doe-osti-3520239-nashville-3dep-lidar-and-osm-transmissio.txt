Nashville 3DEP LiDAR and OSM Transmission-Corridor Workflow
Technical white paper | Finalized production workflow | August 2026
Chad Melton
ORNL/SPR-2026/4793

Executive summary
This document describes a finalized workflow that converts USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP) LiDAR into a reusable 1 m digital
terrain model (DTM), digital surface model (DSM), and canopy height model (CHM) for the Nashville service area. Public USGS
Entwine Point Tile (EPT) resources were queried first so PDAL streams only point-cloud nodes intersecting each buffered 5 km tile.
The completed regional surface was retained as lossless compressed GeoTIFF tiles and lightweight VRT mosaics.
OpenStreetMap (OSM) transmission centerlines were then filtered to explicit voltages of 115 kV and above, projected to
EPSG:26916, and buffered by voltage class. Those corridor polygons were used as raster cutlines against the 1 m DTM, DSM, and
CHM TIFF/VRT surfaces, and produced a corridor-specific GeoTIFFs for terrain, surface, vegetation-height, and profile analysis.

1. Regional 3DEP raster production
The Nashville Electric service-area KML was dissolved to a single geometry and transformed to NAD83 / UTM zone 16N
(EPSG:26916). The USGS National Map inventory was queried for LiDAR point-cloud products intersecting the AOI. Source selection
uses a newest-non-overlapping rule so newer acquisitions are used first and older collections are retained only where they add
otherwise-uncovered area. Selected collections were matched to public USGS EPT project resources by project identity and spatial
overlap.
A regular 5 km grid was intersected with the AOI. Each tile was processed with a 25 m surrounding buffer so neighboring points can
support rasterization near tile edges before cropping to the exact output footprint. PDAL reads bounded EPT data, reprojects
horizontally to EPSG:26916, and writes aligned 1 m surfaces. DTM uses classified ground returns with inverse-distance weighting;
DSM uses maximum valid Z after excluding standard noise classes. Small NoData gaps were filled only within 10 m of valid data. CHM
was calculated as DSM minus DTM, and the production output retains the hole-filled DTM, DSM, and CHM GeoTIFF tiles plus
regional VRT mosaics.

2. OSM transmission-line corridor preparation
The transmission network was derived from OSM power-line geometry. The production layer retains power=line features with an
explicit operating voltage of at least 115,000 V. Semicolon-separated voltage values were parsed individually, with the highest
applicable level controlling inclusion and buffer width. Available source attributes such as voltage, circuits, operator, name, and
reference were preserved. Centerlines were projected to EPSG:26916 before buffering.

The attribute-preserving buffer layer was retained for line-specific analysis. When needed for raster masking, overlapping corridor
polygons can be unioned into a single cutline while the original per-line vector attributes remain intact.

3. Clipping the buffered area to the TIFF products
The buffered transmission polygons were intersected with the regional raster footprint and used as cutlines against the 1 m DTM,
DSM, and CHM TIFF/VRT surfaces. The operation was polygon-based: pixels inside the voltage-specific corridor were retained and
pixels outside were written as NoData. The clipped rasters preserve EPSG:26916, the 1 m grid, and the regional source values,
allowing direct comparison of terrain elevation, surface elevation, and relative canopy/object height.
The corridor-specific GeoTIFF collection, buff_filtered_tiles, was paired with a lightweight corridor VRT for QGIS. These smaller
rasters support elevation profiles, cross-corridor sampling, vegetation-height screening, and related transmission analyses without
repeatedly reading the full metropolitan mosaic.

4. Quality assurance and deliverables
Acceptance was based on spatial completeness, consistent raster geometry, and traceable data lineage. The Nashville production
run contains 132 expected 5 km tiles for each regional raster product. DTM, DSM, and CHM outputs were checked for readable data,
expected CRS and 1 m resolution, and consistent NoData behavior.
 • AOI, tile grid, rasters, OSM centerlines, and voltage buffers use EPSG:26916 for spatial operations.
 • DTM uses ground returns; DSM excludes noise classes; CHM was produced only where DTM and DSM were valid.
 • OSM lines were filtered by explicit voltage values and buffered after projection; source attributes were retained.
 • Corridor rasters use the buffered polygon as the mask and preserve the regional 1 m raster alignment.

Data lineage and limitations
The regional surface can contain acquisitions from different years because the source rule prioritizes newer coverage while retaining
older data where needed for completeness. No new vertical-datum transformation was introduced, so absolute vertical reference
follows the 3DEP source metadata. OSM is a collaboratively maintained infrastructure dataset rather than an operator-certified
utility inventory; corridor products reflect the geometry and attributes present in the selected OSM extract and should retain
OpenStreetMap attribution under the ODbL.

Conclusion
The finalized method separates regional LiDAR production from transmission-corridor extraction. USGS 3DEP EPT data provide the 1
m DTM, DSM, and CHM foundation; OSM transmission lines define voltage-specific buffers; and those polygons were used directly
to clip the regional TIFF/VRT surfaces. The result is a reusable regional elevation framework and compact corridor-specific raster
products for QGIS visualization, profiles, and vegetation-height analysis.
