Schur States, Average Mixing, and Counting Trees on Line Graphs’ CTQW
Musung Kang

Abstract
We introduce a family of complex-valued edge weights on a finite simple graph Γ \Gamma arising from a continuous-time quantum walk on the line graph ℓ ​ Γ \ell\Gamma , packaged as the Schur state : an n × n n\times n Hermitian matrix encoding the amplitudes of an edge-state walk. The entrywise modulus square induces a real-weighted adjacency matrix A ⁡ ( e ) A(e) and Laplacian L ⁡ ( e ) L(e) , and time-averaging yields a weighted graph whose spanning-tree count we relate to that of Γ \Gamma . Our main result is t ​ n ​ ( Γ , 1 m ) = 1 m n − 1 ​ t ​ n ​ ( Γ ) , tn\!\left(\Gamma,\tfrac{1}{m}\right)=\frac{1}{m^{n-1}}\,tn(\Gamma), valid whenever the initial edge state is uniform commutative , where n = | V ​ Γ | n=|V\Gamma| , m = | E ​ Γ | m=|E\Gamma| , and t ​ n ​ ( Γ , w ) tn(\Gamma,w) denotes the weighted spanning-tree count. We further identify a structural mechanism — the − 2 -2 eigenspace of ℓ ​ Γ \ell\Gamma — providing uniform commutative states beyond the regular case, in particular for line graphs of Eulerian graphs with an even number of edges. As a side result, we establish that commutative states are precisely the states whose von Neumann entropy is preserved under average mixing.

Motivation: average mixing on edges

Continuous-time quantum walks on graphs were introduced as a quantum analogue of classical random walks. Given an adjacency matrix
A

\ell\Gamma

[
4

and revisited from a graph-theoretic perspective by Coutinho et al.
[
5
]
, who raised questions about how

2.3
Schur states and the Schur space

The next definition is central. It packages the amplitudes
\langle{e_{v,w}}|{U(t)e_{j}}\rangle
into a Hermitian
n\times n
matrix supported on
E\Gamma

Definition 4

(Schur state)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a simple graph with
|V\Gamma|=n
. The
Schur state

S^{j}(t)
on
\Gamma
at time
t
, starting from a pure edge state
e_{j}
, is the
n\times n
matrix

S^{j}_{v,w}(t)=\begin{cases}\langle e_{v,w}|U(t)|e_{j}\rangle&\text{if }(v,w)\text{ is an arc,}\\
\overline{\langle e_{v,w}|U(t)|e_{j}\rangle}&\text{if }(w,v)\text{ is an arc,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For a superposition
|e\rangle=\sum_{i}a_{i}|e_{i}\rangle
, we set
S^{e}:=\sum_{i}a_{i}S^{i}
.

Remark 5
.

By construction,
S^{j}(t)
is Hermitian:
S^{j}_{w,v}(t)=\overline{S^{j}_{v,w}(t)}
whenever
(v,w)
or
(w,v)
is an arc, and both entries vanish otherwise. The diagonal is identically zero.

Definition 6

(Schur space)
.

The
Schur space
of
\Gamma
is

\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}:=\Bigl\{M\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n,n}(\mathbb{C}):M_{w,v}=\overline{M_{v,w}},\,M_{v,w}=0\text{ if }\{v,w\}\notin E\Gamma\Bigr\},

equipped with the inner product

\langle M,N\rangle:=\langle J|\!\left(\overline{M}\circ N\right)\!|J\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(M^{\dagger}N),

where
|J\rangle
is the all-ones
n
-vector and
\circ
is the entrywise (Schur) product.

Proposition 7
.

Every Schur state from a pure edge state has squared norm
2
:

\langle S^{j}(t),S^{j}(t)\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(S^{j}(t)^{\dagger}S^{j}(t)\right)=2.

Proof.

By Hermiticity of

, each undirected edge

contributes
|S^{j}_{v,w}|^{2}+|S^{j}_{w,v}|^{2}=2|S^{j}_{v,w}|^{2}
to the squared Frobenius norm. Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(S^{j}(t)^{\dagger}S^{j}(t))

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E\Gamma}\left|\langle{e_{v,w}}|{U(t)e_{j}}\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\,\langle e_{j}|U(-t)\!\left(\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E\Gamma}|e_{v,w}\rangle\!\langle e_{v,w}|\right)\!U(t)|e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\,\langle e_{j}|U(-t)U(t)|e_{j}\rangle=2\,\langle e_{j}|e_{j}\rangle=2,

since
\sum_{e\in E\Gamma}|e\rangle\langle e|=I
on
\mathbb{C}^{E\Gamma}
and
U(-t)U(t)=I
.
∎

The same calculation for a normalized superposition

3
Induced Graphs and Entropy

A Schur state is a complex-weighted graph. From it we extract a real-weighted graph (whose weights are physical probabilities) via the entrywise conjugate-square.

Definition 8

(Adjacency and Laplacian of a Schur state)
.

Let
S^{e}
be a Schur state. Its
adjacency matrix
and
Laplacian
are

A(e):=\overline{S^{e}}\circ S^{e},\qquad L(e):=D(e)-A(e),

where
D(e)
is the diagonal matrix chosen so that every row sum (equivalently, every column sum) of
L(e)
vanishes; explicitly,
D(e)_{vv}=\sum_{w}A(e)_{v,w}
.

Remark 9
.

The total weight
\sum_{v,w}A(e)_{v,w}=\|S^{e}\|^{2}=2
. Equivalently,
A(e)
assigns probability-like weights to each oriented arc, summing to
1
on the undirected edge set.

3.1
Tensor products

Proposition 10

(Schur–tensor compatibility)
.

For matrices
A,B,C,D
of compatible sizes,

\displaystyle(A\otimes B)\circ(C\otimes D)

\displaystyle=(A\circ C)\otimes(B\circ D),

(1)

\displaystyle(A\circ B)(C\circ D)

\displaystyle=(AC)\circ(BD).

(2)

Consequently, for vectors
|e\rangle,|f\rangle
and matrices
S,T
,

(\langle e|\otimes\langle f|)(\overline{S\otimes T}\circ S\otimes T)(|e\rangle\otimes|f\rangle)=\langle e|(\overline{S}\circ S)|e\rangle\,\langle f|(\overline{T}\circ T)|f\rangle.

Proof.

Both (
1
) and (
2
) are proved by checking entry-by-entry. For (
1
), the entry at position
(pr+v,\,qs+w)
on each side equals
A_{r,s}C_{r,s}B_{v,w}D_{v,w}
. For (
2
), the
(i,j)
entry of either side is
\sum_{k}A_{ik}B_{ik}C_{kj}D_{kj}
. The corollary follows by writing the bilinear form as a trace and applying the identities to the matrix-product structure.
∎

Definition 11

(Tensor product of graphs)
.

For simple graphs
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
, the (categorical)
tensor product

\Gamma_{1}\otimes\Gamma_{2}
has vertex set
V\Gamma_{1}\times V\Gamma_{2}
, with
(u_{1},u_{2})\sim(v_{1},v_{2})
iff
\{u_{1},v_{1}\}\in E\Gamma_{1}
and
\{u_{2},v_{2}\}\in E\Gamma_{2}
.

Definition 12

(Tensor product of Schur states)
.

Let
S^{e}
on
\Gamma_{1}
and
T^{f}
on
\Gamma_{2}
be Schur states. Their tensor product is the
|V\Gamma_{1}||V\Gamma_{2}|
-square matrix
S^{e}\otimes T^{f}
(Kronecker product), whose configuration graph is the tensor product
\Gamma_{1}\otimes\Gamma_{2}
.

3.2
Entropy

Two notions of entropy will be used.

Definition 13

(Von Neumann entropy)
.

For a density matrix
\rho
on
\mathbb{C}^{|E\Gamma|}
,

E(\rho):=-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log\rho).

For an edge state
|e\rangle
, write
E(e):=E(|e\rangle\langle e|)
.

Definition 14

(Vertex spectral entropy)
.

Let
S^{e}
be a Schur state with Laplacian
L(e)
. Its
normalized Laplacian
is
\mathcal{L}(e):=L(e)/\mathrm{Tr}(L(e))
, with eigenvalues
\{\mu_{i}\}
. The
vertex entropy
is

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L}(e)):=-\sum_{i}\mu_{i}\log_{2}\mu_{i}=-\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{L}(e)\log_{2}\mathcal{L}(e)),

with the convention
0\log_{2}0=0
.

The two entropies measure different things:
E(\rho)
measures the spread of
\rho
over basis edge states, while
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L}(e))
measures the spectral spread of the induced (real-weighted) Laplacian.

Proposition 15
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected graph with
|V\Gamma|=n
. Then:

(a)

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L}(e))\geqslant 0
, with maximum value
\log_{2}(n-1)
achieved when
\Gamma=K_{n}
.

(b)

If
|e\rangle
is a pure edge state, then
E(|e\rangle\langle e|)=0
and
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L}(e))|_{t=0}=0
.

(c)

(Tensor product) If
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are density matrices on
\mathbb{C}^{E\Gamma_{1}},\mathbb{C}^{E\Gamma_{2}}
respectively, then
E(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})=E(\rho_{1})+E(\rho_{2})
.

(d)

(Disjoint union) If
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
are vertex-disjoint and
0\leqslant p\leqslant 1
, the convex combination
\rho_{1\sqcup 2}:=p\rho_{1}\oplus(1-p)\rho_{2}
on
\mathbb{C}^{E(\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2})}
has entropy

E(\rho_{1\sqcup 2})=h(p)+pE(\rho_{1})+(1-p)E(\rho_{2}),

where
h(p)=-p\log_{2}p-(1-p)\log_{2}(1-p)
.

3.3
Average mixing and the dephasing limit

Definition 16

(Average mixing matrix)
.

Let
A(\ell\Gamma)=\sum_{r}\theta_{r}E_{r}
be the spectral decomposition (where the sum runs over distinct eigenvalues and
E_{r}
is the orthogonal projector onto the
\theta_{r}
-eigenspace). The
average mixing matrix
is

\widehat{M}:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}M(t)\,dt=\sum_{r}E_{r}\circ E_{r}.

The following lemma — a standard fact in ergodic theory — is used repeatedly.

Lemma 17

(Dephasing limit)
.

Let
A=\sum_{r}\theta_{r}E_{r}
be the spectral decomposition of a Hermitian matrix
A
(sum over distinct eigenvalues). For any matrix
X
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{itA}Xe^{-itA}\,dt=\sum_{r}E_{r}XE_{r}.

Proof.

Expand
X=\sum_{r,s}E_{r}XE_{s}
using
\sum_{r}E_{r}=I
. Then

e^{itA}Xe^{-itA}=\sum_{r,s}e^{it(\theta_{r}-\theta_{s})}E_{r}XE_{s}.

For
r=s
,
e^{it(\theta_{r}-\theta_{s})}=1
contributes
E_{r}XE_{r}
to every
t
. For
r\neq s
, the eigenvalues are distinct (
\theta_{r}\neq\theta_{s}
), so

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{it(\theta_{r}-\theta_{s})}\,dt=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{e^{iT(\theta_{r}-\theta_{s})}-1}{iT(\theta_{r}-\theta_{s})}=0.

Summing the contributions yields the claim.
∎

Definition 18

(Average mixing of a Schur graph)
.

For a Schur state
S^{e}
with adjacency
A(e)
and Laplacian
L(e)
, define

\widehat{N}(e):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}N(e)\,dt

for
N\in\{A,L\}
. The
time-averaged density matrix
of
|e\rangle
is, by Lemma
17
,

\widehat{\rho}_{e}:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}U(t)|e\rangle\langle e|U(t)^{\dagger}\,dt=\sum_{r}E_{r}|e\rangle\langle e|E_{r}.

Proposition 19

(Entropy under average mixing)
.

For any edge state
|e\rangle
,

E(\widehat{\rho}_{e})\geqslant E(|e\rangle\langle e|),

with equality if and only if
|e\rangle\langle e|\in\mathrm{Comm}(A(\ell\Gamma))
.

Proof.

Unitary evolution preserves entropy:
E(U(t)\rho U(t)^{\dagger})=E(\rho)
. Since the von Neumann entropy is concave on the set of density matrices
[
10
]
, applying Jensen’s inequality to the integral defining
\widehat{\rho}_{e}
gives

E(\widehat{\rho}_{e})=E\!\left(\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}U(t)|e\rangle\langle e|U(t)^{\dagger}\,dt\right)\geqslant\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}E(U(t)|e\rangle\langle e|U(t)^{\dagger})\,dt=E(|e\rangle\langle e|).

Equality in Jensen’s inequality (for strictly concave
E
on the relevant simplex) holds iff the integrand
U(t)|e\rangle\langle e|U(t)^{\dagger}
is constant in
t
, which holds iff
[U(t),|e\rangle\langle e|]=0
for all
t
, equivalently
[A(\ell\Gamma),|e\rangle\langle e|]=0
.
∎

This motivates the following terminology.

Definition 20

(Commutative state)
.

An edge state
|e\rangle
is
commutative
if
|e\rangle\langle e|\in\mathrm{Comm}(A(\ell\Gamma))
.

Lemma 21
.

A pure state
|e\rangle\langle e|
commutes with
A(\ell\Gamma)
if and only if
|e\rangle
is an eigenvector of
A(\ell\Gamma)
.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
If
A|e\rangle=\lambda|e\rangle
, then
A|e\rangle\langle e|=\lambda|e\rangle\langle e|=|e\rangle\langle e|A
.

(\Rightarrow)
Suppose
A|e\rangle\langle e|=|e\rangle\langle e|A
. Apply both sides to any vector
|f\rangle
:

A|e\rangle\langle{e}|{f}\rangle=|e\rangle\langle e|A|f\rangle=|e\rangle\overline{\langle f|A|e\rangle}.

Choosing
|f\rangle=|e\rangle
gives
A|e\rangle=|e\rangle\cdot\overline{\langle e|A|e\rangle}
, so
A|e\rangle
is a scalar multiple of
|e\rangle
, i.e.,
|e\rangle
is an eigenvector.
∎

4
Main Theorem: Tree Counting under Average Mixing

4.1
Uniform commutative states

Definition 22

(Uniform commutative state)
.

An edge state
|e\rangle=a_{1}|e_{1}\rangle+\cdots+a_{m}|e_{m}\rangle
on
\ell\Gamma
is
uniform commutative
if

(i)

|a_{1}|=\cdots=|a_{m}|
(uniform amplitudes),

(ii)

|e\rangle\langle e|\in\mathrm{Comm}(A(\ell\Gamma))
(commutativity).

If
|e\rangle
satisfies only (ii), it is called
commutative
.

Remark 23
.

On a connected
k
-regular graph
\Gamma
, the line graph
\ell\Gamma
has the all-ones vector
\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}(1,\ldots,1)^{T}
as a Perron eigenvector with eigenvalue
2k-2
. This is a uniform commutative state.

4.2
Spanning-tree counts and the matrix-tree theorem

Definition 24

(Weighted tree count)
.

For
\Gamma
with weight function
w:E\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
,

tn(\Gamma,w):=\sum_{T\text{ spanning tree}}\prod_{e\in E(T)}w(e).

If
\Gamma
is disconnected,
tn(\Gamma,w)=0
.

Lemma 25

(Matrix–tree theorem
[
3
, § 13.2]
,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
L(\Gamma,w)
be the weighted Laplacian, with eigenvalues
0=\lambda_{1}^{w}\leqslant\lambda_{2}^{w}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\lambda_{n}^{w}
. Then

tn(\Gamma,w)=\frac{1}{n}\prod_{i\geqslant 2}\lambda_{i}^{w}=\det\!\bigl(L(\Gamma,w)_{[i,i]}\bigr)\quad\text{for any }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
L(\Gamma,w)_{[i,i]}
denotes the principal
(n-1)\times(n-1)
submatrix obtained by deleting row
i
and column
i
.

4.3
The main theorem

Theorem 26
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges, and let
|e\rangle
be a uniform commutative state on
\ell\Gamma
with full support on
E\Gamma
. Then the time-averaged Schur graph satisfies

tn\!\left(\Gamma,\tfrac{1}{m}\right)=\frac{1}{m^{n-1}}\,tn(\Gamma).

Equivalently, the time-averaged Schur graph carries the same combinatorial complexity (up to the normalization
m^{-(n-1)}
) as
\Gamma
.

Proof.

The proof has three steps: compute the time-averaged adjacency entry-by-entry, deduce the time-averaged Laplacian, then apply the matrix–tree theorem.

Step 1: Time-averaged adjacency.
Write
|e\rangle=\sum_{p}a_{p}|e_{p}\rangle
with
|a_{p}|=1/\sqrt{m}
for all
p
(by condition (i) and normalization
\sum_{p}|a_{p}|^{2}=1
). For an arc
(v,w)
with corresponding edge
e_{vw}\in E\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\widehat{A(e)}_{v,w}

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}|\langle e_{vw}|U(t)|e\rangle|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\langle e_{vw}|U(t)|e\rangle\langle e|U(t)^{\dagger}|e_{vw}\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle=\langle e_{vw}|\widehat{\rho}_{e}|e_{vw}\rangle\qquad\quad\text{(Lemma~\ref{lem:dephase})}

\displaystyle=\langle e_{vw}|\!\left(\sum_{r}E_{r}|e\rangle\langle e|E_{r}\right)\!|e_{vw}\rangle.

By condition (ii) and Lemma
21
,
|e\rangle
is an eigenvector of
A(\ell\Gamma)
, so all but one of the projections
E_{r}|e\rangle
vanish, and
\sum_{r}E_{r}|e\rangle\langle e|E_{r}=|e\rangle\langle e|
. Therefore

\widehat{A(e)}_{v,w}=|\langle{e_{vw}}|{e}\rangle|^{2}=|a_{vw}|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{m}.

That is,
\widehat{A(e)}=\tfrac{1}{m}A(\Gamma)
as matrices on
V\Gamma
(with entries
1/m
on edges of
\Gamma
and
0
elsewhere).

Step 2: Time-averaged Laplacian.
The diagonal matrix
D(e)
in the definition of
L(e)
satisfies
D(e)_{vv}=\sum_{w}A(e)_{v,w}
. Time-averaging (which commutes with finite sums),

\widehat{D(e)}_{vv}=\sum_{w}\widehat{A(e)}_{v,w}=\sum_{w:\{v,w\}\in E\Gamma}\tfrac{1}{m}=\tfrac{\deg_{\Gamma}(v)}{m},

so
\widehat{D(e)}=\tfrac{1}{m}D(\Gamma)
. Hence

\widehat{L(e)}=\widehat{D(e)}-\widehat{A(e)}=\tfrac{1}{m}\bigl(D(\Gamma)-A(\Gamma)\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{m}\,L(\Gamma).

Step 3: Matrix–tree theorem.
By Lemma
25
, the spanning-tree count of the weighted graph
(\Gamma,1/m)
equals the determinant of any principal
(n-1)\times(n-1)
submatrix of
\widehat{L(e)}=\tfrac{1}{m}L(\Gamma)
. Scaling an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix by
1/m
scales its determinant by
1/m^{n-1}
, so

tn\!\left(\Gamma,\tfrac{1}{m}\right)=\det\!\left(\tfrac{1}{m}L(\Gamma)_{[i,i]}\right)=\tfrac{1}{m^{n-1}}\det\!\bigl(L(\Gamma)_{[i,i]}\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{m^{n-1}}\,tn(\Gamma).\qed

(3)

4.4
Optimality

Proposition 27
.

The weighted tree count
tn(\Gamma,w)
is log-concave as a function of the weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{E\Gamma}
(Huh, via Lorentzian polynomials).

Corollary 28
.

Among all weights
w
on
E\Gamma
with
\sum_{e}w(e)=1
,

tn(\Gamma,w)\leqslant tn\!\left(\Gamma,\tfrac{1}{m}\right)=\frac{1}{m^{n-1}}\,tn(\Gamma),

with equality iff
w
is the uniform weight.

The uniform commutative state is therefore
optimal
in the sense of maximizing the spanning-tree count among all commutative states with constrained total weight.

5
Beyond Regularity: The
-2
Eigenspace

A natural question: when does
\ell\Gamma
admit a uniform commutative state? Regularity of
\Gamma
is sufficient (the Perron eigenvector is constant). It is, however,
not
necessary, thanks to a structural feature of line-graph spectra known from algebraic graph theory. In condensed-matter physics, line graphs of certain graphs are studied for their highly degenerate
-2
eigenspace, termed a
flat band

[
7
,
9
]
.

5.1
The incidence-matrix identity

For any simple graph
H
with unsigned vertex–edge incidence matrix
B\in\{0,1\}^{|VH|\times|EH|}
, the following identities hold:

BB^{T}=D(H)+A(H),\qquad B^{T}B=2I+A(\ell H).

(4)

The second identity gives the spectral relationship

A(\ell H)=B^{T}B-2I.

(5)

5.2
Flat-band states and uniformity

Lemma 29
.

Let
\psi\in\ker(B)
, viewed as a function
\psi:EH\to\mathbb{C}
. Then
\psi
is an eigenvector of
A(\ell H)
with eigenvalue
-2
.

Proof.

B\psi=0\Rightarrow B^{T}B\psi=0\Rightarrow A(\ell H)\psi=(B^{T}B-2I)\psi=-2\psi
.
∎

Theorem 30
.

Let
H
be a connected graph in which (i) every vertex has even degree, and (ii) the number of edges is even. Then
\ell H
admits a uniform commutative state with eigenvalue
-2
, even when
\ell H
is non-regular.

Proof.

We construct a
\pm 1
edge labeling
\psi:EH\to\{\pm 1\}
satisfying
B\psi=0
, i.e.,
\sum_{e\ni v}\psi(e)=0
at every vertex
v
. By Lemma
29
, such
\psi
is an eigenvector of
A(\ell H)
with eigenvalue
-2
, and the normalization
\psi/\sqrt{|EH|}
yields a state with
|\psi(e)|^{2}=1/|EH|
on every edge — a uniform commutative state in the
-2
eigenspace.

By condition (i),
H
is connected and Eulerian, so by Euler’s theorem
[
11
, Theorem 3.5]
it admits a closed Eulerian trail
T=(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{|EH|})
traversing every edge exactly once and returning to its starting vertex.

Define

\psi(e_{i}):=(-1)^{i-1},\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,|EH|.

We verify
\sum_{e\ni v}\psi(e)=0
at every vertex
v
. Each visit of the trail to
v
uses one incoming and one outgoing edge; these are consecutive in the trail, so they receive opposite signs
(-1)^{j-1}
and
(-1)^{j}
. Hence each visit contributes
(-1)^{j-1}+(-1)^{j}=0
to the local sum, except possibly at the
closing visit
at the start vertex
v_{0}
, where the trail’s last edge
e_{|EH|}
pairs with its first edge
e_{1}
. The closing contribution is

\psi(e_{|EH|})+\psi(e_{1})=(-1)^{|EH|-1}+1.

By condition (ii),
|EH|
is even, so
(-1)^{|EH|-1}=-1
and the closing contribution is
0
.

Therefore
\sum_{e\ni v}\psi(e)=0
at every vertex
v
, so
B\psi=0
and
\psi
is the desired eigenvector. The state
\psi/\sqrt{|EH|}
has uniform amplitudes
|\psi(e)|/\sqrt{|EH|}=1/\sqrt{|EH|}
on every edge, completing the proof.
∎

6
Pure States and Bridges

What is the tree count for
|e\rangle\notin\mathrm{Comm}(A(\ell\Gamma))
? We restrict to the simplest non-commutative case: a pure phase-shifted edge state
|e\rangle=e^{i\alpha}|e_{q}\rangle
on a single edge
e_{q}
.

Lemma 35
.

For
|e\rangle=e^{i\alpha}|e_{q}\rangle
and
e_{p}=\{v,w\}\in E\Gamma
,

\widehat{A(e)}_{v,w}=\widehat{M}_{p,q},

where
\widehat{M}
is the average mixing matrix of
\ell\Gamma
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\widehat{A(e)}_{v,w}

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}|\langle e_{p}|U(t)|e\rangle|^{2}\,dt=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}|e^{i\alpha}\langle e_{p}|U(t)|e_{q}\rangle|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}|\langle e_{p}|U(t)|e_{q}\rangle|^{2}\,dt=\widehat{M}_{p,q}.\qed

The weighted tree count is then

tn(\Gamma,w_{e_{q}})=\sum_{T\text{ tree}}\prod_{e_{p}\in E(T)}\widehat{M}_{p,q},

where the global phase
e^{i\alpha}
does not contribute.

6.1
Bridges

Let
e_{q}
be a bridge:
\Gamma\setminus\{e_{q}\}
has two connected components
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
of sizes
n_{1},n_{2}
. Every spanning tree of
\Gamma
uses
e_{q}
, so

tn(\Gamma,w_{e_{q}})=\widehat{M}_{q,q}\cdot tn(\Gamma_{1},w_{e_{q}}|_{\Gamma_{1}})\cdot tn(\Gamma_{2},w_{e_{q}}|_{\Gamma_{2}}).

Caution: the effects of the bridge
e_{q}
on
w_{e_{q}}|_{\Gamma_{1}}
and
w_{e_{q}}|_{\Gamma_{2}}
remain unknown in general.

6.2
Path graph example

Corollary 36
.

Let
\Gamma=P_{n}
and
e_{q}\in EP_{n}
. Then

tn(\Gamma,w_{e_{q}})<\frac{1}{(n-1)^{n}}.

If
n
is even and
c
is the central edge, then

tn(\Gamma,w_{c})<tn(\Gamma,w_{e})<\frac{1}{(n-1)^{n}}.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n-1}
be the spectral idempotents of
\ell(P_{n})=P_{n-1}
, and let
T
be the permutation matrix with
T|u\rangle=|n-u\rangle
. Godsil
[
4
]
computed

\widehat{M}=\sum_{r}E_{r}\circ E_{r}=\frac{1}{2n}(2J+I+T),

where
J
is the all-ones matrix. Reading off diagonal entries:
\widehat{M}_{q,q}=3/(2n)
if
e_{q}
is not the central edge, and
\widehat{M}_{c,c}=2/n
at the center (when
n
is even).

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the sum
tn(\Gamma,w_{e_{q}})=\sum_{T}\prod_{e\in T}\widehat{M}_{p,q}
and comparing with the optimal value
\frac{1}{(n-1)^{n}}
from Corollary
28
(with
m=n-1
for
P_{n}
) yields the strict inequalities. The inequality
tn(\Gamma,w_{c})<tn(\Gamma,w_{e})
for
e\neq c
follows from the strict log-concavity (Proposition
27
) and the fact that
w_{c}
has a single maximum entry while
w_{e}
for off-center
e
has a more spread distribution.
∎

7
Concluding Remarks

We have introduced the
Schur state framework
: a packaging of edge-walk amplitudes as a complex weighted graph living in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
canonically attached to
\Gamma
. The principal results:

(1)

Schur states induce real-weighted adjacency
A(e)
and Laplacian
L(e)
, with
\sum A(e)=2
encoding probability conservation.

(2)

Commutative states are precisely the dynamical fixed points of the dephasing channel, i.e., the states whose von Neumann entropy is preserved by average mixing (Proposition
19
).

(3)

For uniform commutative states on a connected
\Gamma
with full support,

tn\!\left(\Gamma,\tfrac{1}{m}\right)=\frac{1}{m^{n-1}}\,tn(\Gamma),

relating quantum-walk dynamics to classical spanning-tree counts (Theorem
26
).

(4)

Beyond the regular case, the
-2
eigenspace of
\ell\Gamma
(a flat band, in physics terminology) provides uniform commutative states for non-regular line graphs of Eulerian root graphs with even edge counts (Theorem
30
).
