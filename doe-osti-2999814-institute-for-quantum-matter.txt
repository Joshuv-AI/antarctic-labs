Abstract
The mission of the Institute for Quantum Matter (IQM) Energy Frontier Research Center was to
discover and understand emergent properties in material systems with the potential for
transformative impacts on energy and information technologies.

In the 2022-2024 funding period IQM focused on selected high impact collaborative projects
involving multiple PIs and combining a full range of synthesis, spectroscopic, and theoretical
methods to achieve conclusive answers to key questions in quantum materials science. The
research was organized in three interlocking thrusts.

Quantum Entangled Magnetism: While conventional magnetic materials contain ordered static
structures of atomic scale magnetic moments, these continue to fluctuate in quantum entangled
magnets and give rise to unique collective materials properties.

Engineering Correlated Superconductivity: We explored superconductivity built from correlated,
topological, and quantum entangled matter. We completed ongoing collaborative IQM projects
exploring superconductivity in metals with light electrons resulting from topologically protected
band crossings.

Magnetic Topological Materials and Phenomena: IQM developed new materials functionality
based on electronic topology in conducting and insulating single crystals and thin films that
incorporate magnetism. A signature focus was the Mn3X family of compounds where the rich
interplay between antiferromagnetic domains and electrical transport properties were explored
including electrical domain switching monitored through the anomalous Hall effect.

IQM discoveries as an Energy Frontier Research Center from 2018-2024 are documented in 193
scientific publications that have received 5,961 Web of Science citations through October 2025.
More than 52 of these papers were published after the start of the final 2 year funding period.
This body of research forms a significant part of our continuously developing understanding of
quantum materials and their expanding application potential. In the course of the scientific work,
the IQM EFRC attracted and educated outstanding young quantum material scientists. A number
of these individuals are now award-winning tenure track faculty or scientists at US national
laboratories. IQM alumni are also working in the high tech sector where the unique skills gained
from participating in collaborative multidisciplinary research at the edge of knowledge bring
value.

Scientific Accomplishments
For a complete technical account of IQM EFRC research accomplishments, we refer to the
scientific publications listed at the end of the report. These are available to the public through
DOE’s Public Access Gateway for Energy and Science http://www.osti.gov/pages/ and through
the IQM google scholar web page. Here we provide a categorized synopsis of select high impact
results with reference to the chronological full list of IQM EFRC papers at the end of the report.

Frustrated and Quantum Entangled Magnetism
 • Tyrel McQueen’s group at IQM/JHU and Stephen Wilson’s group of UCSB discovered the
 kagome prototype materials KV3Sb5,RbV3Sb5, and CsV3Sb5 [173].
 • IQM published influential review articles on quantum spin liquids, their materials
 realizations, and future applications [116, 123,124, 168].
 • IQM introduced and studied a number of important triangular lattice antiferromagnets
       including Na2BaCo(PO4)2 [179], BaCo2(AsO4)2 [52,57,134], and
 NaBaYb(BO3)2 [172,174], which illustrate new aspects of this canonical model of
 frustrated quantum magnetism.
 • IQM has made important theoretical progress in the understanding of long wavelength
 magnetic excitations in spin systems based on triangular units such as the triangular
       lattice [2,13,137,143].
 • IQM discovered a strongly tunable transition to a singlet ground state in the 2D van der
 Waals layered trimerized kagome magnet Nb3Cl8 [177].
 • IQM developed a new understanding of the anisotropic spin-1/2 chain compound
 CoNb2O6 in terms of a twisted Kitaev model [119].
 • IQM provided new insight through experimental work on important model systems in
 frustrated quantum magnetism NiGa2S4 [15,135], RuCl3 [128] and Yb2Ti2O7
       [58,135,154,194].

Engineering Correlated Superconductivity
 • IQM provided evidence of bulk superconductivity in LaCuSb2 and showed that as a
 consequence of the massless Dirac electrons and the associated strongly anisotropic
 mass the material has both a Type I and a Type II phase depending on the orientation of
 the magnetic field [6].
 • IQM provided evidence of quasi-two-dimensional antiferromagnetic spin fluctuations in
 the spin-triplet superconductor candidate CeRh2As2 [11].
 • IQM has proposed specific phase sensitive probes of monopole superconductivity [14].

Magnetic Topological Materials and Phenomena
  • IQM characterized and explored the potential for applications of the topological time
 reversal symmetry breaking Weyl antiferromagnet Mn3Sn in bulk single crystalline and
     thin film device like formats [62,70,85,86,125,193,196].
  • Detailed characterization of helical magnetic phases in the Weyl material NdAlSi [103]
 and SmAlSi [47]

 • IQM developed a method to probe topological aspects of an electronic band structure
 through the associated spectrum of the electronic Raman scattering (1,25]
 • IQM obtained general theoretical results concerning the magnetism and electronic
 properties of topological quantum materials [9,140].

Methodological developments
  • IQM Developed a method to synthesize kinetically stable perovskite single crystalline
 -CsPbI3 with an optical bandgap that is 90 meV less than for thin film samples and
 has excitonic resonance [148,178].
  • IQM proposed a method to extract single particle components from two particle
 continuum scattering. The method is based on THz 2D Coherent Spectroscopy [182].
  • IQM developed a method to probe time dependent phenomena in magnetic materials
 on time scales from 0.1 ms to hours using event mode neutron scattering at steady or
 pulsed neutron sources. This technique was used to detect two distinct magnetic
 relaxation phenomena in the spin ice material Ho2Ti2O7 [111].

Educating Quantum Materials Scientists
Beyond the direct scientific output of IQM, the Institute attracts some of the brightest young,
condensed matter scientists as graduate students and postdocs. The inter-disciplinary
collaborative environment is an outstanding place for these individuals to develop the
knowledge and independence needed to be able to make their own original contributions to
solid state physics in the future.
