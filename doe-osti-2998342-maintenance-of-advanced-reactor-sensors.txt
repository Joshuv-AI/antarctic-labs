Public Executive Summary
High-temperature advanced reactors (ARs) under development are expected to offer lower
levelized cost of energy (LCOE) compared to existing light water reactors (LWRs) because of
longer time between refueling due to efficient fuel burnup, and higher efficiency of the energy
conversion thermal cycle of AR. Operation and maintenance (O&M) costs are projected to
constitute the single largest item of AR total operating costs. This project aims to develop
advanced sensing and automation technologies to minimize O&M of ARs through increase in
operation efficiency. The focus of the technology development is on reducing O&M cost of the
Kairos Power Fluoride Salt-Cooled, High Temperature Reactor (KP-FHR). Because of high
temperature and radiation fields and chemical hazard in the primary heat transport system, KP-
FHR is envisioned to be sealed and autonomously operated. Furthermore, compared to LWRs,
which are typically shut down for refueling every 18 to 24 months, the KP-FHR utilizing pebble
bed TRISO fuel is designed for on-line refueling. Building advanced sensing and automation into
the design of KP-FHR can result in longer uninterrupted reactor operation to capitalize on the
online refueling advantage. The main challenge is that few sensors are compatible with the high
temperature and high radiation environment of AR. In this project, fluid thermophysical
properties were measured with fiber-optics sensors which provide distributed information (e.g.,
temperature field) and multi-modal information (measure several parameters simultaneously).
Fluid chemistry was measured with multi-electrode arrays and optical impurity probe, which
detect impurities and monitor salt elemental composition. Initial development and testing of the
sensors was performed using liquid salt and liquid sodium facilities at Argonne National
Laboratory and the University of Wisconsin Madison. Following proof-of-concept tests,
performance of sensors was demonstrated at Kairos Power molten salt facilities.
Technoeconomic analysis indicates that O&M expense related to conventional sensor
maintenance could be more than $11M incurred every 18 months. These O&M costs result from
revenue lost due to reactor shutdown, and labor costs involved in sensors recalibration and
replacement by technicians working in the high radiation and high temperature environment.
The total cost of sensor maintenance O&M cost savings of $11M/18months are expected from
the deployment of resilient sensors that will require minimal maintenance, deployment of
multifunctional distributed sensors that require minimal physical penetrations through piping
and vessel pressure boundaries, as well as developing a strategy for on-line replacement of
distributed temperature and strain sensors without shutting down the reactor.

Project Activities

This project aims were to develop advanced sensing and automation technologies to minimize
operation and maintenance (O&M) of advanced reactors (ARs) through increase in operation
efficiency. While ARs are in the development stage, it is important to integrate instrumentation
and control (I&C) features into their design to minimize O&M costs. The specific objectives are
Data Generation (DG), Sensor Development (SD), and Techno-Economic Analysis (TEA). The focus
of the technology development is on reducing O&M cost of the Kairos Power Fluoride Salt-
Cooled, High Temperature Reactor (KP-FHR). Fluid thermophysical properties were measured
with fiber-optics sensors which provide distributed information (e.g., temperature field) and
multi-modal information (measure several parameters simultaneously). Fluid chemistry was
measured with multi-electrode arrays and optical impurity probe, which detect impurities and
monitor salt elemental composition. Techno-economic analysis examined the findings of data
generation and sensor development objectives in terms of O&M cost reduction and guide further
development towards automation of KP-FHR sensing. Initial development and testing of the
sensors was performed using liquid salt and liquid sodium facilities at Argonne National
Laboratory and the University of Wisconsin Madison. Following proof-of-concept tests,
performance of sensors was demonstrated at Kairos Power Instrumentation Test Unit (ITU) and
STL-03 molten salt facilities. O&M cost savings of more than $11M every 18 months are expected
from the deployment of resilient sensors that will require minimal maintenance, deployment of

multifunctional distributed sensors that require minimal number of physical penetrations
through pressure boundaries, as well as developing a strategy for on-line replacement of
distributed temperature and strain sensors without shutting down the reactor.
