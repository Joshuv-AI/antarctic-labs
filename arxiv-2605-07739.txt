Beyond the conventional Emery model: crucial role of long-range hopping for cuprate superconductivity Preprint: arXiv
Eric Jacob, M. O. Malcolms, Viktor Christiansson, Leonard M. Verhoff, Paul Worm, Liang Si, Philipp Hansmann, Thomas Schäfer, Karsten Held

Abstract
The Emery model is the quintessential model for cuprate superconductors. In his eponymous paper, Emery only considered the next-nearest-neighbor oxygen-copper hopping. Later, also the relevance of nearest- and next-nearest oxygen-oxygen hoppings has been pointed out. Using dynamical vertex approximation, we find a superconducting dome consistent with cuprates. However, long-range hoppings beyond the three conventional hopping parameters are necessary for the quantitatively correct phase diagram and for a proper d d -wave order parameter.



Introduction.

Cuprates are charge-transfer insulators according to
the scheme of Zaanen, Sawatzky and Allen
62
. That is, doped holes primarily
go into the oxygen orbitals, as experimentally confirmed
16
;
23
;
8
. This makes the Emery model
13
with the copper
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
and two in-plane oxygen orbitals (see inset of Fig.
1
)
the
quintessential model for superconducting cuprates
6
.

Despite this multi-orbital nature, oxygen and copper orbitals lead to a single
Fermi surface
9
of mixed copper-oxygen character. This motivates the use of the
one-band Hubbard model instead of the more involved three-band Emery model. Indeed, most theoretical studies of cuprate superconductivity are based on this Hubbard model
19
;
46
;
2
. On a qualitative level, such a simplified calculation with a Hubbard model is reasonable;
however, quantitatively reliable calculations
with a single set of parameters across dopings require the more fundamental Emery model
53
.

The Emery model has been studied with various methods, including – most closely to our study –
dynamical mean-field theory

;

;
21

;
53
;
55
,
its cluster
57
;
14
;
29
;
33
;
34
;
50
;
48
;
30
and diagrammatic extensions
36
, as well as the two-particle self-consistent (TPSC) approach
18
.

In contrast, diagrammatic extensions of DMFT

These diagrammatic extensions and, in particular, the dynamical vertex approximation (D

The approximation underlying the diagrammatic extensions relies on the momentum-independence of the

In this Letter, we employ D

We find that the hopping terms conventionally used in virtually all Emery calculations so far (blue box in Fig.

insufficient

beyond 15% hole doping

Long-range hopping parameters (orange box in Fig.

correct

in the Hubbard model, which is known to be important for superconductivity
39
;
44

Without these long-range hopping parameters we also obtain a strongly modulated
d
-wave superconducting order
parameter against conventional wisdom for cuprates.

Model and Method.

We study various two-dimensional
d
–
p
models defined by the Hamiltonian

H=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}\psi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}\,\bar{h}_{0}(\mathbf{k})\,\psi_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}+U\sum_{i}n^{d}_{i,\uparrow}n^{d}_{i,\downarrow},

(1)

where

\psi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}=(d^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\sigma},p^{\dagger}_{x,\mathbf{k},\sigma},p^{\dagger}_{y,\mathbf{k},\sigma})

contains the electronic creation operators for the copper

d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
orbital and the oxygen
p_{x}
and
p_{y}
orbitals
with spin
\sigma
and momentum
\mathbf{k}=(k_{x},k_{y})
;
\psi_{\mathbf{k},\sigma}
is the corresponding annihilation operator.
The momentum-dependent
3\times 3
matrix
\bar{h}_{0}(\mathbf{k})

defines the noninteracting band dispersion, and
U
denotes the local
on-site Coulomb repulsion on the copper
d
orbital;
n^{d}_{i,\sigma}=d^{\dagger}_{i,\sigma}d_{i,\sigma}
is the occupation number operator for spin
\sigma
on site
i
.

To obtain
\bar{h}_{0}(\mathbf{k})
we first
perform
density-functional theory (DFT) calculations for
the arguably simplest cuprate superconductor, CaCuO
2
,

with
P4/mmm
space group (No. 123) and lattice parameters
a=b=3.85
Å,
and

In a second step, the DFT band structure is projected onto maximally localized Wannier functions

The DFT-Wannier

d
-

The DFT-derived Wannier Hamiltonian
\bar{h}_{0}(\mathbf{k})

is Fourier transformed to obtain the hopping elements between two lattice sites
\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}

\bar{h}_{r}(\mathbf{R-R}^{\prime})

In the conventional (conv.) Emery model only the three elements
t_{pd}

t_{pp}
and
t^{\prime}_{pp}

sketched in Fig.
1

While two of these,
t_{pd}
and
t_{pp}

In particular, they become relevant when considering longer-range hopping paths beyond nearest neighbors, which in the conv. Emery model requires combining several
t_{pd}
,
t_{pp}
, and
t^{\prime}_{pp}
hoppings.

In Fig.

dp

\Delta I=\frac{1}{n_{\mathbf{k}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}|\varepsilon({\mathbf{k}})-\mathbf{\hat{\varepsilon}(k)}|

\varepsilon({\mathbf{k}})

(for the
highest

\hat{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{k}})
where the respective hopping is explicitly excluded. The 1-norm is normalized to the number of
\mathbf{k}
points
n_{\mathbf{k}}

The size of the orange circles in Fig.

Conclusions.

Superconductivity in the conventional Emery model has hitherto only been studied for

small
clusters that are known to largely overestimate
T_{c}
—at least for the Hubbard model. We here calculate superconductivity of the full
d
-
p
model
using D
\Gamma
A, and find a phase diagram in
good agreement with cuprates
. F
or an accurate quantitative description,
hopping matrix elements beyond the
t_{pd}
,
t_{pp}
and
t^{\prime}_{pp}
of the conventional Emery model are absolutely necessary.
Otherwise, the superconducting dome is found at too small hole doping and there is also a strong
modulation of the
d
-wave order parameter.
Our work opens the path to a more material realistic description of cuprate superconductivity which certainly necessitates the inclusion of oxygen orbitals.
