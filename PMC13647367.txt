ZEB1-mediated suppression of circDennd1b promotes the migration and invasion of lung cancer cells

Seongran Cho, Jin-A Choi, Jihye Park, Keunsoo Kang, Young-Ho Ahn

Abstract

Zinc finger E-box binding homeobox 1 (ZEB1) is a key transcription factor that induces the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and promotes lung cancer progression. While circular RNAs (circRNAs) are also involved in EMT, their connection with ZEB1 remains unclear. Here, using RNA sequencing coupled with CIRI-full analysis, we identified 25 circRNAs differentially expressed in ZEB1-overexpressing lung cancer cells. Among them, circDennd1b was significantly downregulated. circDennd1b knockdown promoted cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, and induced EMT. Conversely, circDennd1b overexpression inhibited cancer cell migration and invasion. We also found that circDennd1b inhibits cell migration by sponging the miR-181 family. These data indicate that the ZEB1/circDennd1b/miR-181 axis promotes lung cancer progression and could offer a new approach for treatment.

Introduction

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) have emerged as critical regulators of cancer progression, particularly through their interactions with epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) signaling pathways (Shang et al. ). EMT is a dynamic process where epithelial cells acquire mesenchymal traits, enhancing their invasive and metastatic potential (Kalluri and Weinberg ). Key signaling pathways, including TGFβ, Wnt/β-catenin, and Notch, drive this phenotypic shift while also influencing circRNA expression and activity, suggesting a complex bidirectional relationship. TGFβ, a potent EMT inducer, promotes the expression of circRNA_0006508 in hepatocellular carcinoma. This circRNA functions as a molecular sponge for miR-125a/miR-125b, effectively reducing their inhibition of EMT-related transcription factors such as SNAIL1. Consequently, this interaction enhances cancer cell migration and invasion (Deshmukh et al. ; Papatsirou et al. ). Similarly, Wnt/β-catenin signaling enhances circFoxo3 expression, which regulates the cell cycle and apoptosis to support EMT and increase cancer invasiveness (Xu et al. ; Papatsirou et al. ). In breast cancer, Notch signaling upregulates circRNA_0001785, which sponges miR-326 to promote EMT by modulating Notch pathway genes, driving cancer cell proliferation and migration (Deshmukh et al. ; Papatsirou et al. ). These examples underscore the interplay between EMT pathways and circRNA networks in shaping aggressive cancer phenotypes.

ZEB1, a key regulator of EMT, controls circRNA biogenesis through transcriptional and post-transcriptional mechanisms. In bladder cancer, ZEB1 promotes circNIPBL formation by binding to intronic regions of the NIPBL gene, establishing a positive feedback loop via the circNIPBL/miR-16-3p/Wnt5a/ZEB1 axis to sustain metastasis (Li et al. ). In breast cancer, ZEB1 binds to the promoter of WWC3, which increases the expression of circWWC3. This circular RNA serves as a molecular sponge for miR-26b-3p and miR-660-3p to activate the Ras signaling pathway, such as EGFR and MAPK1 (Meng et al. ). In contrast, ZEB1 recruits the RNA-binding protein EIF4A3 to reduce circDOCK5 formation in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. This disrupts the miR-627-3p/TGF-β signaling and promotes metastasis (Meng et al. ). Also, the ZEB1/miR-200 axis controls the biogenesis of EMT-associated circRNAs by regulating the RNA-binding protein QKI (Conn et al. ; Kim et al. ). These studies show that ZEB1 is a key regulator of circRNAs during cancer progression.

Despite these findings, the role of ZEB1-regulated circRNAs in lung cancer is still unclear. In this study, we identified circDennd1b as a circular RNA suppressed by ZEB1 in lung cancer cells. Previous studies showed circDennd1b promotes cholesterol efflux by sponging miR-17-5p in atherosclerosis (Xu et al. ) and inhibits tumorigenicity by sponging miR-122-5p in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (Chen et al. ). However, its connection to EMT in lung cancer has not been investigated. Here, we show that circDennd1b suppresses lung cancer cell migration and invasion by sponging miR-181 family. This ZEB1/circDennd1b axis contributes to lung cancer progression and could be considered for future therapies.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

393P, 344SQ, and ZEB1-overexpressing 393P (393P-ZEB1) cell lines were cultured in RPMI 1640 (Welgene; Gyeongsan, Korea) supplemented with 10% FBS (Corning; Corning, NY, USA) at 37°C with 5% CO2. To induce EMT, 393P cells were treated with 5 ng/mL TGFβ for 7 days. For cell growth assays, cells were seeded in 96-well plates (2 × 103 cells/well), and cell viability was measured daily for 3-4 days using the WST-8 reagent (Biomax; Guri, Korea).

RNA sequencing

Total RNA was isolated in triplicate from 393P-vec and 393P-ZEB1 cell lines utilizing the XENOPURE Total RNA Purification Kit (Xenohelix; Incheon, Korea). Following extraction, samples were transferred to Macrogen (Seoul, Korea) for transcriptome analysis. RNA quality and concentration were verified before library preparation using the TruSeq Stranded Total RNA Library Prep Gold Kit (Illumina; San Diego, CA, USA). Sequencing was executed on an Illumina NovaSeq platform, generating paired-end reads. For circRNA identification, circRNA-seq reads were mapped to the GRCm38/mm10 mouse genome with BWA-MEM v0.7.17 (Li ). We used CIRI-full v2.0 (Zheng et al. ) to detect circRNAs, as it integrates CIRI2 for back-splicing junction (BSJ) detection with CIRI-AS for isoform reconstruction. A minimum of 10 BSJ-spanning reads was required per circRNA candidate. BSJ structures were checked with CIRI-vis. Expression of circRNAs was quantified by CIRIquant (Zhang, Chen, et al. ). Genomic annotations were added using HOMER v4.11 (Heinz et al. ), and differential expression between groups was tested from BSJ count matrices using Python 2.7. The RNA-seq raw data and processed files have been deposited in NCBI’s Gene Expression Omnibus (GSE319378).

Knockdown and overexpression of circDennd1b and miR-181c/d

To knockdown circDennd1b, a custom siRNA targeting the back-splice junction (5′-GAGCAAAUUAAGCACUGAA-3′) was synthesized by Bioneer (Daejeon, Korea) and transiently transfected into 393P cells using the TransIT-X2 Dynamic Delivery System (Mirus Bio; Madison, WI, USA). For stable knockdown, an shRNA oligomer featuring the same target sequence was cloned into the pLKO.1-Puro lentiviral vector (Addgene #10878). miR-181c/d inhibitors were also obtained from Bioneer and transiently transfected using the TransIT-X2 system. To generate the circDennd1b expression construct, exons 4–9 of murine Dennd1b were first PCR-cloned into the pcDNA3.1(+) ZKSCAN1-MCS Exon Vector (Addgene #69901). The resulting cassette, including the flanking intronic repeats required for efficient circularization, was subcloned into the pLVX-Puro lentiviral vector (Clontech; Mountain View, CA, USA). To overexpress miR-181c/d, a genomic region (846 bp) containing the pre-miR-181c/d cluster was PCR-amplified from 344SQ murine lung cancer cells and cloned into the pLVX-Puro vector. Lentiviral particles were then produced in 293T cells via co-transfection of the transfer vectors with packaging plasmids pMD2.G (Addgene #12259) and psPAX2 (Addgene #12260) using Lipofector-EXT (AptaBio; Yongin, Korea) (Kim et al. ). Target cells were infected with the viral supernatant and stably selected using puromycin for at least two weeks before downstream assays.

Reverse transcription-PCR (RT–PCR)

Total RNA was isolated using the WelPrep Total RNA Isolation Reagent (Welgene), following the manufacturer’s protocol. For cDNA synthesis, reverse transcription was performed using either TOPscript RT DryMIX (Enzynomics; Daejeon, Korea) or SuperScript IV VILO Master Mix (Invitrogen; Waltham, MA, USA). To check circRNA expression, PCR was performed using Lamp Taq DNA Polymerase (Biofact; Daejeon, Korea) with divergent primers targeting the back-splice junction (Supplementary Table 1). PCR products were run on agarose gels and band densities were quantified using ImageJ. Quantitative RT–PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed using Real-Time PCR Master Mix with SFCgreen I (Biofact), and mRNA levels were normalized to Rpl32. All primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 1. Also, TaqMan probes (Applied Biosystems; Waltham, MA, USA) were used to measure circDennd1b levels. To quantify miRNAs, the HB miR Multi Assay Kit (Heimbiotek; Seongnam, Korea) was used, and data were normalized to RNU6B snoRNA.

Transwell migration assay

Cells (1 × 105 per insert) in serum-free medium were seeded into the upper chambers, and complete medium with 10% FBS was added to the lower chambers of Transwell inserts (Labselect; Hefei, China). After 24 h, the migrated cells stained with crystal violet were photographed and manually counted using ImageJ.

Three-dimensional (3-D) spheroid invasion assay

Multicellular cancer cell spheroids were generated using agarose micromolds prepared with the 3D Petri Dish micro-mold kit (MicroTissues; Providence, RI, USA). Cells (2.56 × 105 cells per mold) were seeded into 2% agarose micromolds and allowed spheroids to form for 2 days. The harvested spheroids were then embedded in collagen domes (comprising spheroids in 0.5× PBS, 0.01 N NaOH, and 3 mg/mL collagen) at the center of each well in a 12-well plate. After 1-2 days, invasion ratios were quantified as the total invaded area divided by the initial central spheroid area using ImageJ.

AGO2-RNA immunoprecipitation

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) was performed using the EpiNext CUT&LUNCH RIP Kit (EpigenTek; New York, NY, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Control and miR-181c/d-overexpressing 393P cells were permeabilized and incubated with an AGO2 antibody (EpigenTek). After enzymatic RNA cleavage, AGO2-bound RNA complexes were captured with affinity beads. The RNA was then purified and analyzed for circDennd1b levels by qRT-PCR.

Luciferase assay

Exons 4 through 9 of circDennd1b were inserted into the psiCHECK-2 vector (Promega; Madison, WI, USA), and the miR-181-binding site was mutated using PCR-based site-directed mutagenesis. Control or miR-181c/d-overexpressing 393P cells (1 × 105 cells/well) were seeded in 24-well plates. On the next day, the cells were transfected with the reporter constructs using Lipofector-EXT. After 48 h, luciferase activity was measured with the Luc-Pair Duo-Luciferase Assay Kit (GeneCopoeia; Rockville, MD, USA) on an INNO-M microplate luminometer (LTek; Seongnam, Korea).

Statistics

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (La Jolla, CA, USA). Significant differences between groups were determined using Student’s t-test or two-way ANOVA. Experiments were repeated 3-4 times unless noted otherwise. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05, indicated as *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, and ***P < 0.001.

Results

ZEB1 overexpression downregulates circDennd1b in lung cancer cells

ZEB1 overexpression in murine epithelial-like 393P lung cancer cells (393P-ZEB1) induced a mesenchymal-like spindle shape (A). ZEB1 overexpression upregulated mesenchymal markers (Zeb1, Twist1, and Cdh2) and downregulated epithelial markers (Cdh1, Crb3, and Cldn3), as previously reported (Kim et al. ; Lee et al. ). To identify circRNAs regulated by ZEB1, we performed RNA sequencing on 393P-ZEB1 and control cells (393P-vec). CIRI-full analysis (Zheng et al. ) revealed 25 circRNAs significantly regulated by ZEB1 (11 upregulated and 14 downregulated; fold-change ≥ 2, P ≤ 0.05) (B; Supplementary Figure 1A). We then validated the expression of eight candidates by RT–PCR using divergent primers targeting the predicted back-splicing junctions (C and D; Supplementary Figure 1B). Among them, circDennd1b, which is formed by exons 4 through 9 of Dennd1b mRNA, was the most significantly downregulated (D and E). Sequencing of the RT–PCR product confirmed the back-splicing junction between Exon 4 and Exon 9 (F). The linear Dennd1b mRNA level remained unchanged (G), suggesting that ZEB1 indirectly regulates circDennd1b back-splicing rather than host gene transcription. circDennd1b expression was also lower in murine mesenchymal-like 344SQ cells compared with epithelial-like 393P cells (H). TGFβ-induced EMT (Kim et al. ) similarly reduced circDennd1b levels (I), suggesting that its downregulation is related to the EMT process.

circDennd1b knockdown promotes the migration and invasion of lung cancer cells

To study its functional role, we stably knocked down circDennd1b in 393P cells using a junction-specific shRNA (A), which did not affect Dennd1b mRNA levels (B). circDennd1b knockdown slightly but significantly increased cell growth at day 4 (C). It also increased cell migration at 24 h (D) and 3-D spheroid invasion at 48 h (E). qRT-PCR of EMT markers confirmed that circDennd1b knockdown induced EMT (F). siRNA-mediated transient knockdown showed similar effects on cellular migration and invasion (G–I). Together, these results indicate that circDennd1b knockdown enhances lung cancer cell migration and invasion, although this effect might partly result from increased growth.

circDennd1b overexpression suppresses the migration of lung cancer cells

We next overexpressed circDennd1b in 393P-ZEB1 cells using a lentiviral vector modified from the ZKSCAN1-MCS system, which contains intronic repeats to ensure efficient circRNA production (A) (Kramer et al. ). This viral transduction successfully increased circDennd1b levels without affecting the expression of its linear mRNA counterpart, Dennd1b (B). Overexpression of circDennd1b significantly suppressed the growth and migration of 393P-ZEB1 cells (C and D). Furthermore, circDennd1b overexpression suppressed the expression of mesenchymal markers (E). These results indicate that circDennd1b suppresses EMT and lung cancer cell migration.

circDennd1b functions as a sponge of miR-181 family

CircRNAs often act as miRNA sponges to regulate gene expression (Choi et al. ; Hwang et al. ; Yoon et al. ). To find miRNAs sponged by circDennd1b, we used the circBank database (Liu et al. ) and our previous miRNA profiling data (NCBI-GEO GSE38386) (Ahn et al. ). Among the predicted targets, the miR-181 family (miR-181a, miR-181c, and miR-181d) was significantly upregulated in 393P-ZEB1 cells (A). miR-181 levels increased after circDennd1b knockdown and decreased after its overexpression (B and C). This suggests that circDennd1b may act as a partial sponge for the miR-181 family in our experimental conditions. Ectopic miR-181c/d expression increased 393P cell growth (D and E) and motility (F). In contrast, miR-181c/d inhibition reduced migration in 393P-ZEB1 or circDennd1b knockdown cells (G and H; Supplementary Figure 2). Finally, AGO2-RIP and luciferase reporter assays confirmed that circDennd1b interacts with miR-181 (I and J). Overall, circDennd1b may attenuate cell migration, partly by sponging the pro-migratory miR-181 family.

Discussion

In this study, we identified circDennd1b as a circular RNA suppressed by the EMT transcription factor ZEB1 in lung cancer cells. ZEB1 controls the expression of various circRNAs, including circDennd1b. As a tumor suppressor, circDennd1b inhibits EMT-associated migration and invasion, partly by sponging the miR-181 family.

ZEB1 regulates various non-coding RNAs to facilitate cancer progression (Ahn et al. ; Kim et al. ), in addition to the transcriptional repression of epithelial genes such as CDH1 and miR-200 family (Kalluri and Weinberg ). Previously, we found that lnc-Nr2f1, a ZEB1-regulated long non-coding RNA, promotes the migration and invasion of lung adenocarcinoma cells (Kim et al. ). In this study, we report the ZEB1/circDennd1b axis. This shows that ZEB1 drives EMT through the regulation of miRNA, long non-coding RNAs, and circRNAs.

Here, we showed that ZEB1 downregulates circFbln2, circOxr1, circHipk3, circTlk1, and circSlc8a1, but upregulates circTut4. circHipk3 generally promotes tumor growth, but it can act as a tumor suppressor depending on the contexts (Zhang et al. ). circFbln2 inhibits myoblast proliferation by sponging miR-22-5p (Liu et al. ); however, in our data, its downregulation by ZEB1 may contribute to the mesenchymal phenotype in lung cancer. This suggests that ZEB1 broadly changes circRNA expression during cancer progression.

ZEB1 overexpression significantly reduced circDennd1b levels without affecting its host gene Dennd1b. DENND1B is a guanine nucleotide exchange factor involved in endocytic trafficking and asthma-related immune responses (Yang et al. ). While the role of Dennd1b in cancer is unclear, circDennd1b was recently reported as a tumor suppressor in clear cell renal cell carcinoma by sponging miR-122-5p (Chen et al. ). Here, we show that circDennd1b inhibits EMT in lung cancer and is directly regulated by ZEB1.

We found that miR-181 family is the main target of circDennd1b. These oncogenic miRNAs promote tumor development by inhibiting various tumor suppressors. For example, miR-181a targets SMAD7 to induce EMT in ovarian cancer (Parikh et al. ), and targets PTEN to change metabolism in colon cancer (Wei et al. ). Also, exosomal miR-181d from cancer-associated fibroblasts increases EMT and tumor growth in breast cancer (Wang et al. ). Here, we suggest that the decrease of circDennd1b during ZEB1-induced EMT allow miR-181 to promote lung cancer cell invasion. To find downstream targets, we compared our RNA-seq data with TargetScan prediction (Supplementary Figure 3A). Known miR-181 targets like SMAD7 and PTEN did not change in our model, showing that this regulation is context-dependent. Among the predicted targets, the lipid phosphatase MTMR9 was a candidate downstream target (Supplementary Figure 3B-D). MTMR9 knockdown has been reported to increase AKT activation, similar to PTEN loss (Guo et al. ). We will investigate the detailed functions of MTMR9 and other candidates in future studies.

It is unclear how ZEB1 selectively inhibits circDennd1b circularization without affecting its linear transcript. As a transcription factor, ZEB1 might act indirectly through RNA-binding proteins (RBPs). Using RBPmap (https://rbpmap.technion.ac.il/), we identified potential RBP binding sites in the flanking introns of circDennd1b. Among them, three candidate RBPs (ESRP1, YBX2, and EIF4A3) were downregulated by ZEB1 overexpression (Supplementary Figure 4). ESRP1 is a known ZEB1 target that regulates circRNA biogenesis (Liu et al. ), and EIF4A3 is involved in ZEB1-dependent circDOCK5 formation (Meng et al. ). Thus, ZEB1 likely controls circDennd1b levels by repressing these RBPs. Alternatively, ZEB1 may directly bind to the flanking introns to regulate circDennd1b, similar to its effect on circNIPBL (Li et al. ). Using in silico prediction, we found potential ZEB1-binding motifs in these introns of circDennd1b (112- and 197-nt upstream of Exon 4 and 11- and 323-nt downstream of Exon 9). We will test these possibilities in future studies. Finally, although we focused on the miR-181 family, circDennd1b may also interact with other miRNAs, such as miR-122 (Supplementary Figure 5) (Chen et al. ), or specific proteins depending on the cellular context.

Our findings suggest that the ZEB1/circDennd1b/miR-181 axis regulates lung cancer progression. Thus, restoring circDennd1b expression or inhibiting the miR-181 family could be considered for treating lung adenocarcinoma.
