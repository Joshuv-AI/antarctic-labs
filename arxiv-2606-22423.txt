When Is a Columnar Scan Bandwidth-Bound? A Decode-Throughput Law and Its Cross-Hardware Validation
Madhulatha Mandarapu, Sandeep Kunkunuru

Abstract
A columnar scan that decompresses, filters, and aggregates should be limited only by memory bandwidth—the roofline floor T ≥ BytesRead / β T\geq\text{BytesRead}/\beta —yet real kernels are often compute-bound and leave bandwidth idle. We give a predictive answer to when a scan is bandwidth-bound. Across encodings, predicate selectivities, and two different machines, we find that a decoder’s value throughput T dec T_{\mathrm{dec}} (values decoded per second) is essentially independent of the bit-width b b : it is set by the decode layout/strategy , not by how many bits each value occupies. Consequently the achieved bandwidth fraction obeys a one-parameter law, f = min ⁡ ( 1 , T dec ​ b / ( 8 ​ β ) ) f=\min\!\big(1,\ T_{\mathrm{dec}}\,b/(8\beta)\big) , with the compute-to-bandwidth ridge at b ⋆ = 8 ​ β / T dec b^{\star}=8\beta/T_{\mathrm{dec}} . Fitting one T dec T_{\mathrm{dec}} per strategy reproduces measured bandwidth fractions with median error 0.027 0.027 on x86/AVX2 and 0.003 0.003 on a held-out Apple M4/NEON machine, and the ridge b ⋆ b^{\star} shifts correctly with each machine’s bandwidth. Inserting FastLanes’ reported decode throughput into the law reproduces its “decode is free at three bits” headline as the large- T dec T_{\mathrm{dec}} limit, unifying our decoder and hand-tuned production code in one curve. We add two crossovers, validated on both machines: branch-free predicate evaluation beats branchy in a mid-selectivity band (the σ ⁡ ( 1 − σ ) \sigma(1-\sigma) misprediction parabola), and zone-map skipping is clustering -gated rather than selectivity-gated. We release the micro-benchmark, the correctness oracle, and a one-command reproduction. This is a baseline and model, not a faster kernel: our portable C decoders reach ∼ 2 \sim 2 values/cycle, far below hand-tuned SOTA; the law holds because it is parameterized by the measured T dec T_{\mathrm{dec}} .

\pi/\beta

given my encoding, my selectivity,
and my hardware, what fraction of memory bandwidth will this scan achieve, and at what bit-width does

Problem and Model

A scan reads a compressed column, decodes each value, evaluates a predicate, and aggregates. Let

\beta
be sustainable memory bandwidth (bytes/s) and
b
the bits per (encoded) value. Reading a
column of
N
values costs
\text{BytesRead}=Nb/8
, so the roofline floor is
T\geq Nb/(8\beta)
and
the achieved
bandwidth fraction
is

f\;=\;\frac{\text{BytesRead}/T}{\beta}\;=\;\frac{\text{achieved read GB/s}}{\beta}\in(0,1].

(1)

Write
T_{\mathrm{dec}}
for the decoder’s value throughput (values/s) in the compute-bound regime, i.e. the
rate at which the kernel can produce decoded values when bandwidth is not the bottleneck. Then the
achieved read bandwidth is
T_{\mathrm{dec}}\cdot b/8
bytes/s until it saturates
\beta
:

\boxed{\,f\;=\;\min\!\Big(1,\ \frac{T_{\mathrm{dec}}\,b}{8\beta}\Big)\,},\hskip 20.00003ptb^{\star}\;=\;\frac{8\beta}{T_{\mathrm{dec}}}\ \ \text{(the compute$\to$bandwidth ridge).}

(2)

Equation (
2
) is the roofline written in scan-native units. Its predictive content is the
empirical claim, validated in §
5
, that
T_{\mathrm{dec}}
is independent of
b
: a
decoder processes a near-constant number of values per second regardless of how many bits each value
takes, so the
bytes
it pulls—and thus
f
—grow linearly in
b
until the ridge
b^{\star}
.

3
Method: encodings, kernels, harness

We implement three decode strategies over 32-bit integer columns:
(i)
scalar row-major
bit-unpacking (a serial variable-shift extractor; the compute-bound
baseline); (ii) a
FastLanes-style transposed
layout
(
Afroozeh and Boncz 2023
)
that stores
1024-value blocks across 32 lanes so that all lanes share one shift+mask and the inner loop
auto-vectorizes; and (iii)
byte-aligned
widths (
b\in\{8,16,32\}
) that coincide with plain
integer arrays. Predicates are equality counts and a materializing select; we also implement RLE and
per-block zone maps (min/max skipping). The fused kernels decode, filter, and aggregate in one pass.

\beta
is measured per machine as STREAM-Triad
(
McCalpin 1995
)
and an empirical
sequential-read maximum (buffers
\gg
LLC); we report
f
against the empirical read maximum (tighter
than theoretical peak). A correctness oracle checks encode/decode round-trips for all widths
1\!-\!32

in both layouts, scan counts against brute force, and three negative controls (byte-aligned
=

generic decode; random data
\Rightarrow
no zone skipping; sub-LLC throughput
>\beta
). All code,
seeds, and a one-command reproduction are released.

8
Limitations and Honest Negatives

(1) Absolute throughput.
Our portable-C decoders reach
\sim\!2
values/cycle,
\sim\!5\%
of
FastLanes’ hand-tuned
\sim\!40
; we did not reimplement the full 1024-bit interleaving and
instruction scheduling. The law is parameterized by the measured
T_{\mathrm{dec}}
, so this does not affect the
fit, but the absolute ridge
b^{\star}
we report is specific to our decoders (and the law predicts how

b^{\star}
moves for a faster one).
(2) Single-thread.
We model per-core bandwidth; the
many-core shared-controller (aggregate) wall is out of scope.
(3) Counting vs materializing.

The branch crossover requires a data-dependent kernel; for count aggregates the compiler removes the
branch.
(4) Integer columns.
Strings/nested types (where SOTA decode is hardest) are future
work; we expect the law to hold with their
T_{\mathrm{dec}}
.
(5) Measurement.
Apple-silicon lacks
turbo/pinning control, so M4 is a generalization point, not a precision gate; we report medians.

9
Conclusion

A columnar scan’s bandwidth fraction is captured by one number per decode strategy: its
bit-width-independent value throughput
T_{\mathrm{dec}}
, via
f=\min(1,T_{\mathrm{dec}}b/8\beta)
with ridge

b^{\star}=8\beta/T_{\mathrm{dec}}
. The law fits across two ISAs, predicts where decode stops being free, and
reproduces hand-tuned SOTA as a limit. With the branch and clustering crossovers and an open harness,
it gives practitioners a direct answer to “will this scan be bandwidth-bound, and at what bit-width?”
