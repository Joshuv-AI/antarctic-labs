1 Accomplishments
The original proposed project timeline was 3 years, while the awarded grant was shortened
to a 7 month funding period. Below we will focus on those aspects of the project that were
either accomplished, or on which significant progress was made, during the funding period.

1.1 What are the major goals of the project?
Quantum field theory (QFT) is a unifying language that pervades many areas of modern
physics. The project revolves around the study of strongly-coupled QFTs that are not
amenable to conventional perturbative techniques – with a particular focus on strongly-
coupled gauge theories, such as quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the gauge theory of the
strong nuclear interaction. Two major topics of interest were:
 1

  • The properties of confining strings in theories such as QCD with adjoint (rather than
 fundamental) quarks, where stable confining strings exist.

  • Phases, and phase transitions, in strongly-coupled gauge theories.

1.2 What was accomplished under these goals?
  • In [1] the PI analyzed a version of QED (the QFT describing photons and electrons)
 in 2+1 spacetime dimensions. Unlike its familiar cousin in 3+1 spacetime dimensions,
 which is a quintessential example of a weakly-coupled QFT, the analogous theory in
 2+1 dimensions is strongly coupled. It thus acts in many ways like a foil for the
 strong-coupling dynamics of QCD. In [1] the PI and his collaborators argued – using
 a wide array of techniques, including anomaly matching and the Vafa-Witten theorem
 – that the theory spontaneously breaks its chiral symmetries via the condensation of
 a monopole operator, which creates magnetic flux.

  • In [2] the PI, together with his collaborators, completed the detailed analysis of non-
 supersymmetric SU (Nc ) gauge theory with Nf = 2 adjoint quarks in 3+1 dimensions,
 for all values of Nc , by leveraging its relationship to the N = 2 supersymmetric pure
 gauge theory. The upshot of a very long and detailed analysis is that the deformed
 supersymmetric theory provides strong evidence that Nf = 2 adjoint QCD confines
 and spontaneously breaks its chiral symmetries via quark-bilinear condensation for
 all values of Nc .

  • As stated above, a major objective of this project is to study the properties of confin-
 ing strings in theories such as QCD with adjoint quarks (either N = 1 supersymmet-
 ric or non-supersymmetric) in which these strings exist and are stable. The PI has
 worked on this in collaboration with his PhD students, Amey Gaikwad and Yanyan
 Li, with several papers to appear soon. So far, results have been obtained in three
 directions:

 (i) The construction of non-supersymmetric string (or vortex) solutions has been
 studied in detail using a combination of numerical and analytic methods. In
 particular it was found that these solutions simplify considerably in the limit of
 large vorticity, in which the strings expand and become very thick.
 (ii) These methods have been generalized to study strings in supersymmetric field
 theories that are not themselves BPS, i.e. not protected by supersymmetry.
 An important class of N = 1 supersymmetric theories in which such non-
 supersymmetric strings occur are deformed Seiberg-Witten theories – to date
 the best understood setting for confinement via monopole superconductivity.
 (iii) The spectrum of fluctuations around the simplest non-supersymmetric strings
 has been studied in detail, and in particular the massive bound state excitations
 on the strings have been identified. It was found that these excitations do not
 include the light “worldsheet axion” particle that was recently observed in lattice
 simulations. The generalization of this analysis to deformed Seiberg-Witten
 2

 theory – where such an axion is expected to eventually occur – is currently
 under investigation.

3 Impact
3.1 What is the impact on the development of the principal dis-
 cipline(s) of the project?
In the context of quantum field theory (broadly defined to also include particle physics
and string theory), the new results described above constitute concrete progress in our
understanding of strongly-coupled theories that are either of direct physical interest, or are
relevant by analogy with such systems.

3.2 What is the impact on other disciplines?
The results about 2+1 dimensional quantum electrodynamics described above are of direct
interest to condensed matter physics, where they describe the long-distance behavior of
certain strongly-coupled spin systems.

3.3 What is the impact on the development of human resources?
The training of PhD students Gaikwad and Li, as well as postdoctoral scholars Niro and
Sun.
