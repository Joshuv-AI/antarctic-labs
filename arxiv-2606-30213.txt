Solitary waves for a higher order Boussinesq system: Stability and numerical experiments Thanks: *Corresponding author: roberto.capistranofilho@ufpe.br
Roberto de A. Capistrano–Filho*, Juan Carlos Muñoz, José Raul Quintero

Abstract
. In this work, we study the nonlinear orbital stability of solitary-wave solutions for a class of higher-order Boussinesq systems with Hamiltonian structure. Using variational methods and the asymptotic connection with generalized fifth-order KdV equations, we establish orbital stability results for a broad family of homogeneous and nonhomogeneous nonlinearities satisfying suitable scaling assumptions. We also perform numerical simulations to investigate the stability criterion associated with the solitary waves. The numerical results suggest that the range of wave velocities leading to orbital stability may be larger than that predicted by the theoretical analysis.

1.
Introduction

In the present work, we study the nonlinear orbital stability of solitary-wave solutions (travelling waves of finite energy) of the one-dimensional higher-order Boussinesq evolution system

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\left(I-d\partial^{2}_{x}+d_{2}\partial_{x}^{4}\right)u_{t}+\eta_{x}+c\partial_{x}^{3}\eta+c_{2}\partial_{x}^{5}\eta&=\partial_{x}\left(G_{1}(\eta,\eta_{x},\eta_{xx},u,u_{x},u_{xx})\right),\\
\\
\left(I-b\partial_{x}^{2}+b_{2}\partial_{x}^{4}\right)\eta_{t}+\partial_{x}u+a\partial_{x}^{3}u+a_{2}\partial_{x}^{5}u&=\partial_{x}\left(G_{2}(\eta,\eta_{x},\eta_{xx},u,u_{x},u_{xx})\right),\end{array}\right.

where
\eta=\eta(x,t)
and
u=u(x,t)
are real functions, and the nonlinearity
G=(G_{1},G_{2})^{T}
has the variational structure

\begin{split}G_{1}(q,r,z,s,t,w)=&F_{q}(q,r,s,t)-rF_{qr}(q,r,s,t)-zF_{rr}(q,r,s,t)-tF_{sr}(q,r,s,t)\\
&-wF_{tr}(q,r,s,t),\end{split}

\begin{split}G_{2}(q,r,z,s,t,w)=&F_{s}(q,r,s,t)-rF_{qt}(q,r,s,t)-zF_{rt}(q,r,s,t)-tF_{st}(q,r,s,t)\\
&-wF_{tt}(q,r,s,t),\end{split}

where
F
is a function with some properties (like homogeneity).

We want to point out that some classical Boussinesq systems were derived by Bona, Chen, and Saut for the first- and second-order approximations to the full two-dimensional Euler equations, to describe the motion of short waves of small amplitude on the surface of an ideal fluid under gravity force (see
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
). In the first approximation, the authors obtained a four-parameter family of Boussinesq systems from the two-dimensional Euler equations, known as the
abcd
Boussinesq system,

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rl}\left(I-b\partial^{2}_{x}\right)\partial_{t}\eta+\partial_{x}w+a\partial_{x}^{3}w&=-\partial_{x}\left(\eta w\right),\\
\left(I-d\partial_{x}^{2}\right)\partial_{t}w+\partial_{x}\eta+c\partial_{x}^{3}\eta&=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\left(w^{2}\right),\end{array}\end{cases}

with
a+b+c+d=\frac{1}{3}-\sigma
(
\sigma\geq 0
is the surface tension),
and for the second approximation, an eight-parameter family of Boussinesq systems

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rl}\left(I-d\partial^{2}_{x}+d_{2}\partial_{x}^{4}\right)\partial_{t}w+\partial_{x}\eta+c\partial_{x}^{3}\eta+c_{2}\partial_{x}^{5}\eta=\partial_{x}\left(H_{1}(\eta,\partial_{x}\eta,\partial^{2}_{x}\eta,w,\partial_{x}w,\partial^{2}_{x}w)\right),\\
\\
\left(I-b\partial_{x}^{2}+b_{2}\partial_{x}^{4}\right)\partial_{t}\eta+\partial_{x}u+a\partial_{x}^{3}u+a_{2}\partial_{x}^{5}u=\partial_{x}\left(H_{2}(\eta,\partial_{x}\eta,\partial^{2}_{x}\eta,w,\partial_{x}w,\partial^{2}_{x}w)\right),\end{array}\end{cases}

where
H_{i}
for
i=1,2
are given by

\displaystyle H_{1}(\eta,\partial_{x}\eta,\partial^{2}_{x}\eta,w,\partial_{x}w,\partial^{2}_{x}w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}w^{2}-c\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}w)-\eta\partial^{2}_{x}\eta-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(w^{2})+(c+d)w\partial^{2}_{x}w,

and

\displaystyle H_{2}(\eta,\partial_{x}\eta,\partial^{2}_{x}\eta,w,\partial_{x}w,\partial^{2}_{x}w)

\displaystyle=-\eta w+b\partial^{2}_{x}(\eta w)-\left(a+b-\frac{1}{3}\right)\eta\partial^{2}_{x}w,

with the constants
a
,
a_{2}
,
b
,
b_{2}
,
c_{2}
,
d
, and
d_{2}
satisfying the following condition

a+b+c+d=\frac{1}{3}-\sigma,

and also that

a_{2}-b_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}(\theta^{2}-\frac{1}{3})b+\frac{5}{24}(\theta^{2}-\frac{1}{5})^{2},\ \ c_{2}-d_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}(1-\theta^{2})c+\frac{5}{24}(1-\theta^{2})(\theta^{2}-\frac{1}{5}),

for
\theta\in[0,1]
. In the second approximation case for
\sigma=\frac{1}{3}
, the Boussinesq system still captures the dispersive nature of the water wave problem, which does not hold for the
abcd
-Boussinesq system corresponding to the first approximation. This fact will be crucial to achieve the stability result in our work.

1.1.
Background

It is important to highlight that, as happens for the
abcd
-Boussinesq system and the KdV equation at the traveling wave level (see, for example,

[
8
]
), the higher-order Boussinesq system (
1.1
) at the traveling wave level, depending on the nonlinearity, is related to traveling wave solutions of the fifth-order KdV equation:

(1.2)

\partial_{t}u+\alpha\partial^{3}_{x}u+\beta\partial^{5}_{x}u=\partial_{x}(f(u,\partial_{x}u,\partial^{2}_{x}u)),

where the nonlinearity takes the variational form

f(q,r,s)=F_{q}(q,r)-rF{qr}(q,r)-sF_{rr}(q,r),

for some
C^{2}
function
F
, which is not necessarily homogeneous, as discussed by Esfahani and Levandosky in
[
14
]
.

The fifth-order KdV equation (
1.2
) arises in several physical contexts. For the choice
F(u,u_{x})=-u^{3}
, the equation models the evolution of gravity–capillary waves in shallow water, as studied by J. Hunter and J. Scheurle
[
18
]
and J. Zufiria
[
33
]
. It also appears in the description of chains of coupled nonlinear oscillators and magneto-acoustic wave propagation in plasmas
[
19
]
.

On the other hand, P. Olver
[
25
]
derived equation (
1.2
) as a second-order approximation for unidirectional wave propagation in the setting of irrotational motion of an inviscid and incompressible fluid under gravity, describing the interaction of small-amplitude long waves over a shallow horizontal bottom (see also
[
11
]
). In this framework, several nonlinear structures arise naturally, including terms of the form
F(u,u_{x})=uu_{x}^{2}-u^{3},

as well as related nonlinearities such as
G(u,u_{x})=-uu_{x}^{2}
,
G(u,u_{x})=-u^{3}
, and
G(u,u_{x})=u^{4}+uu_{x}^{2},
which are associated with perturbations of the Hamiltonian structure of the full water-wave problem. Related models and asymptotic derivations were also investigated by D. Benney
[
1
]
, W. Craig and M. Groves
[
11
]
, and several other authors. For further developments on higher-order water-wave models, we refer the reader to
[

,

,

,

,

.

As is well known, the approach developed by Grillakis
et al.
in
[
16
,
17
]
for the analysis of orbital stability and instability relies on the existence of a Hamiltonian structure, through which travelling waves are characterized as critical points of an associated action functional. For the higher-order Boussinesq system (
1.1
), the Hamiltonian functional is given formally by

\mathcal{H}\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
\Phi\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\eta^{2}-c(\eta_{x})^{2}+c_{2}(\eta_{xx})^{2}+u^{2}-a(u_{x})^{2}+a_{2}(u_{xx})^{2}+F(\eta,\eta_{x},u,u_{x})\right)\,dx.

Moreover, the system can be written in the Hamiltonian form

\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{t}\\
\Phi_{t}\end{pmatrix}=-\mathcal{J}_{bdb_{2}d_{2}}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
\Phi\end{pmatrix},

where

\mathcal{J}_{bdb_{2}d_{2}}=\partial_{x}\begin{pmatrix}0&\left(I-b\partial_{x}^{2}+b_{2}\partial_{x}^{4}\right)^{-1}\\
\left(I-d\partial_{x}^{2}+d_{2}\partial_{x}^{4}\right)^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.

In the particular case
b=d
and
b_{2}=d_{2}
, the system possesses a genuine Hamiltonian structure, since the operator
\partial_{x}M^{-1}
, with

M^{-1}=\mathcal{J}_{bbb_{2}b_{2}},

is skew-symmetric. Furthermore, if the initial data
\eta(0)
and
u(0)
have zero mean value on
\mathbb{R}
, then this property is preserved by the flow for as long as the solution exists. In this case, the system also admits the conserved quantity

\mathcal{Q}
, usually referred to as the
charge
in the sense of Noether’s theorem. Formally, it is defined by

\mathcal{Q}\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
u\end{pmatrix}=-\frac{1}{2}\left<\mathcal{J}_{bb_{2}}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
u\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
u\end{pmatrix}\right>=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I-b\partial_{x}^{2}+b_{2}\partial_{x}^{4})\eta u\,dx.

We emphasize that both functionals
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are well defined for

(\eta,u)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

which therefore constitutes the natural energy space for the well-posedness theory and for the construction of travelling-wave solutions.

Under this Hamiltonian framework, travelling waves with speed
\omega
correspond to stationary solutions of the modulated system

\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{t}\\
\Phi_{t}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{J}_{bb_{2}}J_{\omega}^{\prime}\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
\Phi\end{pmatrix},

where the action functional is given by

J_{\omega}(Y)=\mathcal{H}(Y)+\omega\mathcal{Q}(Y).

Equivalently, travelling-wave solutions are characterized as critical points of
J_{\omega}
, that is, solutions of

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(Y)+\omega\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}(Y)=0.

We recall that the general theory developed in
[
16
,
17
]
provides a powerful framework for proving orbital stability of solitary waves in abstract Hamiltonian systems. In this setting, least-energy solitary waves
Y_{\omega}
are characterized as minimizers of the action functional
J_{\omega}
. The corresponding stability analysis relies essentially on the positivity properties of the linearized operator

J_{\omega}^{\prime\prime}(Y_{\omega}),

up to at most two distinguished directions, together with the strict convexity of the scalar function,

S_{1}(\omega)=\inf\left\{J_{\omega}(Y):Y\in\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\right\},

where
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
denotes an appropriate constraint manifold.

For the higher-order Boussinesq system considered here, the spectral characterization of
J_{\omega}^{\prime\prime}(Y_{\omega})
within the Grillakis–Shatah–Strauss framework is highly challenging due to the complexity of the associated differential operator and the failure of the range of
\partial_{x}
to satisfy the abstract assumptions of the theory (see
[
7
,
8
,
12
,
24
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
). Therefore, we adopt a variational approach based on the minimization properties of
S_{1}
, following ideas developed in
[
32
,
30
,
31
,
12
,
8
]
.

From the numerical perspective, the stability and instability of solitary waves in dispersive equations have been extensively investigated in
[
6
,
13
,
15
,
5
]
. These studies combine finite-difference, spectral, and high-order time discretization methods to analyze stability thresholds, nonlinear interactions, and long-time dynamics, providing valuable support to the analytical theory.

1.2.
Main results and heuristic

In this paper, we study the orbital stability of solitary waves for the higher-order Boussinesq system (
1.1
) in the regime
0<\omega<1
. A key ingredient is the KdV scaling limit as
\omega\to 1^{-}
, which links the Boussinesq system to a generalized fifth-order KdV equation and allows us to establish the positivity of
S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)
near the limiting velocity. Combined with variational methods, concentration–compactness arguments, and the Grillakis–Shatah–Strauss theory, this yields orbital stability for a broad class of nonlinearities. We also develop a Fourier-based iterative scheme to compute solitary-wave profiles and the stability functional. The numerical results agree with the theory and suggest that the stability region may extend beyond the range covered by the analytical results.

Before we go further, by a solitary-wave solution, we mean a solution
(\eta,u)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for the Boussinesq system (
1.1
) of the form
\eta(t,x)=\psi\left({x-\omega t}\right)
and
u(x,t)=v\left({x-\omega t}\right)
. In this case, the travelling wave profile
(\psi,v)
must satisfy the system

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\omega\left(v-dv^{\prime\prime}+d_{2}v^{(iv)}\right)+\psi+c\psi^{\prime\prime}+c_{2}\psi^{(iv)}-G_{1}(\psi,\psi_{x},\psi_{xx},v,v_{x},v_{xx})&=0,\\
\\
-\omega\left(\psi-b\psi^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}\psi^{(iv}\right)+v+av^{\prime\prime}+a_{2}v^{(iv)}-G_{2}(\psi,\psi_{x},\psi_{xx},v,v_{x},v_{xx})&=0.\end{array}\right.

Owing to the Hamiltonian structure of the system, when

b=d,\qquad b_{2}=d_{2},\qquad a,c<0,\qquad a_{2},c_{2}>0,

travelling-wave solutions correspond to critical points of the action functional
J_{\omega}
. In particular, ground-state solitary waves are characterized as minimizers of
J_{\omega}
under an appropriate constraint. In this case, the existence of solitary-wave solutions for this higher-order Boussinesq system (
1.1
) follows from the variational approach that applies a minimax-type result, since solutions
(\psi,v)
of the system (
1.3
) are critical points of the functional
J_{\omega}
given by

J_{\omega}(\psi,v)=\frac{1}{2}I_{\omega}(\psi,v)-K(\psi,v),

where the functionals
I_{\omega}
and
K
are defined on the space
X=H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by

I_{\omega}(\psi,v)=I_{1}(\psi,v)+I_{2,{\omega}}(\psi,v),

and

K(\psi,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(\psi,\psi_{x},v,v_{x})\,dx,

with

\displaystyle I_{1}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\psi^{2}-c(\psi^{\prime})^{2}+c_{2}(\psi^{\prime\prime})^{2}+v^{2}-a(v^{\prime})^{2}+a_{2}(v^{\prime\prime})^{2}\right]dx,

\displaystyle I_{2}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\psi-b\psi^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}\psi^{(iv)}\right)v\,dx=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\psi v+b\psi^{\prime}v^{\prime}+b_{2}v^{\prime\prime}\psi^{\prime\prime}\right)\,dx,

where
I_{2,\omega}=\omega I_{2}
. Before we go further, we observe that

K^{\prime}(\psi,v)=(G_{1}(\psi,\psi_{x},\psi_{xx},v,v_{x},v_{xx}),G_{2}(\psi,\psi_{x},\psi_{xx},v,v_{x},v_{xx}))^{t}.

Now, we set the operators

\displaystyle P_{\omega}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=\left<J^{\prime}_{\omega}(\psi,v),(\psi,v)\right>=I_{\omega}(\psi,v)-N(\psi,v),

\displaystyle N(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=\left<K^{\prime}(\psi,v),(\psi,v)\right>,

and consider the manifold
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}=\{(\psi,v)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\ P_{\omega}(\psi,v)=0\},

and define the number

S(\omega)=\inf\{J_{\omega}(\psi,v):(\psi,v)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega}\}.

With all this notation and definitions in hand, we state the main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
a+c+2b=0
.

a)

If
0<p<8
and
0<p<q
, there exist
\omega
arbitrarily close to
1^{-}
such that
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
is stable.

b)

Suppose that
F(q,r,s,t)
is a homogeneous function of order
p+2
. If
0<p<8
. Then, there is
\omega*\in(0,1)
such that
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
is stable for
\omega\in(\omega^{*},1)
.

c)

Suppose that
F(q,r,s,t)
is a homogeneous function of order
p+2
and
F(\cdot,r,\cdot,t)
is a homogeneous function of order
\beta
. If
p+2\beta<8
, then there exists
\omega_{0}>0
such that
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
is stable for
\omega\in(\omega_{0},1)
.

Here the set of the ground state solutions
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
is given by

\mathcal{G}_{\omega}=\left\{(\psi,v)\in X\setminus\{0\}:J_{\omega}(\psi,v)=S(\omega),\ P_{\omega}(\psi,v)=0\right\}.

The proof of orbital stability is based on a variational characterization of the solitary waves as minimizers of the action functional
J_{\omega}(\psi,v)
under the natural constraint
P_{\omega}(\psi,v)=0.
The first step consists of showing that the set of ground states is nonempty and that every minimizing sequence is relatively compact in the energy space
X=H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, modulo translations. This compactness property is obtained through concentration–compactness arguments, where the KdV scaling regime plays a crucial role. More precisely, when the wave velocity satisfies
|\omega|\to 1^{-}
, the higher-order Boussinesq system asymptotically approaches a generalized fifth-order KdV equation, at the travelling wave level. This asymptotic reduction allows us to identify the limiting variational structure and to exclude vanishing and dichotomy scenarios for minimizing sequences. As a consequence, minimizing sequences converge strongly, up to translations, towards elements of the ground-state manifold
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
. The key fact to prove that the ground-state solution set
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
is stable under Grillakis’
et al.
variational approach is based on the compactness property of
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
and that the limit equation for a special renormalized sequence in
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
is precisely a general fifth-order KdV equation, as
\omega\to 1^{-}
. Our stability results are exactly those obtained for S. Levandoski in the case of a fifth-order KdV equation with a
(p+2)
-homogeneous nonlinearity and for Esfahani-Levandosky for a fifth-order KdV equation with a nonlinearity not necessarily homogeneous, but with special scaling properties (see
[
21
]
,
[
14
]
).

1.3.
Novelties and structure

The main contribution of this work is the study of orbital stability for a broad class of higher-order Boussinesq systems by combining variational methods, KdV-type asymptotic analysis, and Hamiltonian stability theory. A key observation is that, as
|\omega|\to 1^{-}
, Boussinesq solitary waves converge, after a suitable rescaling, to solitary-wave solutions of a generalized fifth-order KdV equation. This asymptotic connection provides essential information for the stability analysis.
Another novelty is the use of the Grillakis–Shatah–Strauss framework without requiring a detailed spectral analysis of the linearized operator. Instead, the proof relies on variational methods, concentration–compactness arguments, and asymptotic estimates. As a result, we obtain orbital stability for a wider class of nonlinear interactions than those previously considered. Numerical simulations also suggest that the stability region may be larger than that predicted by the analytical theory.

We conclude this introduction by outlining the organization of the paper. In Section
2
, we present several preliminary results and review the existence theory for solitary-wave solutions of system (
1.3
), including the existence of ground-state solitary waves obtained through a variational approach. Section
3
is devoted to the analysis of the long-wave KdV scaling limit and the convergence of minimizers toward solitary-wave solutions of a limiting generalized fifth-order KdV equation. In Section
4
, we establish the positivity of the second derivative of the action functional in the small-amplitude regime and use this property to prove the orbital stability of ground-state solitary waves, thereby establishing the main result of the paper, Theorem
1.1
. Finally, section
5
presents numerical simulations that illustrate and complement the theoretical results.

Remark 2.3

For

3.
The fifth-order KdV scaling for the higher-order Boussinesq system

In this section, we present some auxiliary lemmas that will be used to prove the main result of this article. We show that a suitably renormalized family of solitary-wave solutions of the generalized Boussinesq system converges to a nontrivial solitary-wave solution of a generalized fifth-order KdV equation as the wave speed
|\omega|
approaches
1^{-}
.

To this end, for
\epsilon>0
, we choose the wave speed according to

\omega^{2}=1-\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}.

Now, given a pair
(\psi,v)\in X
, we introduce the KdV scaling

(3.1)

\psi(x)=\alpha z(y),\qquad v(x)=\alpha w(y),\qquad y=\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}x,

where
\alpha>0
will be chosen appropriately. We see directly that

J_{\omega}(\psi,v)=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}J^{\epsilon}(z,w),\ \ \ J^{\epsilon}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}I^{\epsilon}(z,w)-K^{\epsilon}(z,w),

where
I^{\epsilon}(z,w)
and
K^{\epsilon}(z,w)
are given by

\displaystyle I^{\epsilon}(z,w)

\displaystyle=I^{1,\epsilon}(z,w)+I^{2,\epsilon}(z,w),

\displaystyle I^{1,\epsilon}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{p+1}}z^{2}-c\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{p+1}}(z^{\prime})^{2}+c_{2}(z^{\prime\prime})^{2}+\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{p+1}}w^{2}-a\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{p+1}}(w^{\prime})^{2}+a_{2}(w^{\prime\prime})^{2}\right)\,dy,

\displaystyle I^{2,\epsilon}(z,w)

\displaystyle=-2\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{p+1}}zw+b\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{p+1}}z^{\prime}w^{\prime}+b_{2}z^{\prime\prime}w^{\prime\prime})\,dy,

\displaystyle K^{\epsilon}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F\left(\alpha(z,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}z^{\prime},w,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}w^{\prime})\right)\,dy.

We also define the corresponding functional
N^{\epsilon}
as

N^{\epsilon}(z,w)=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(z,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}z^{\prime},w,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}w^{\prime})\nabla F\left(\alpha(z,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}z^{\prime},w,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}w^{\prime})\right)\,dy.

Remark 3.1
.

We compute the functionals in some specific cases that correspond to appropriate generalizations of the nonlinearities in Esfahani-Lewandosky’s paper
[
14
]
, mainly,

F_{1}(v)=\frac{|v|^{q+2}}{q+2}+\frac{|v|^{p+2}}{p+2},\ \ F_{2}(u,v)=u^{p+1}v,\ \ F_{3}(v)=|v|^{p+2}+v_{x}^{2r}v^{s},

under the assumption for the third case

p(2-r)=2(2r+s-2).

With
F_{4}(v,v_{x})
, we denote a
(p+2)
-homogeneous function
F_{4}(v,v_{x})
and with
F_{5}(v,v_{x})
, we denote a
(p+2)
-homogeneous function such that
F_{{5}}(q,\cdot)
is
\sigma
-homogeneous and
F_{{5}}(\cdot,r)
is
\beta
-homogeneous with
\sigma+\beta=p+2
.

For cases
F_{1}
,
F_{3}
,
F_{4}
and
F_{5}
, we consider
\widetilde{F}_{j}(q,r.s,t)=F_{j}(q,r)+F_{j}(s,t)
. For
\alpha=\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p(p+1)}}
, we have

\displaystyle K_{1}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{\epsilon^{\frac{4(q-p)}{p(p+1)}}(|z|^{q+2}+|w|^{q+2})}{q+2}+\frac{(|z|^{p+2}+|w|^{p+2})}{p+2}\right)dy:=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}K_{1}^{\epsilon}(z,w),

\displaystyle K_{2}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{p+1}wdy:=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}K_{2}(z,w),

\displaystyle K_{3}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|z|^{p+2}+|w|^{p+2}+z_{y}^{2r}z^{s}+w_{y}^{2r}w^{s}\right)dy:=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}K_{3}(z,w),

\displaystyle K_{4}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{F}_{4}(z,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}z^{\prime},w,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}w^{\prime})\,dy:=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}K^{\epsilon}_{4}(z,w),

\displaystyle K_{5}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\epsilon^{\frac{\beta}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{F}_{5}(z,z^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})\,dy:=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\epsilon^{\frac{\beta}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}K_{5}(z,w).

For these
K_{j}
, we have that

\displaystyle N_{1}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\epsilon^{\frac{4(q-p)}{p(p+1)}}(|z|^{q+2}+|w|^{q+2})+|z|^{p+2}+|w|^{p+2}\right)dy

\displaystyle:=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+2}}\alpha^{2}N_{1}^{\epsilon}(u,z),

\displaystyle N_{2}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=(p+2)\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}K_{2}(z,w).

\displaystyle N_{3}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((p+2)(|z|^{p+2}+|w|^{p+2})+(2r+s)((z^{\prime})^{2r}z^{s}+(w^{\prime})^{2r}w^{s})\right)dy

\displaystyle:=\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}N_{3}^{\epsilon}(z,w),

\displaystyle N_{4}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=(p+2)\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}K_{4}^{\epsilon}(z,w)

\displaystyle N_{5}(\psi,v)

\displaystyle=(p+2)\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p+1}}\epsilon^{\frac{\beta}{p+1}}\alpha^{2}K_{5}(z,w).

From the properties in Lemma
2.1
(generalization of Esfahani-Lewandosky’s paper
[
14
]
), we see that the following limits exist:

(3.2)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}K^{\epsilon}(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon}),\ \ \lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}N^{\epsilon}(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon}).

Now, for
0<|\omega|<\min\left\{1,\frac{-a}{b},\frac{-c}{b},\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}},\frac{c_{2}}{b_{2}}\right\}
, we know that there is a family
\left((\psi_{\omega},v_{\omega})\right)_{\omega}\,
such that

J_{\omega}(\psi_{\omega},v_{\omega})=S(\omega),\ \ P_{\omega}(\psi_{\omega},v_{\omega})=0.\ \

Then, for
\alpha=\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p(p+1)}}
, if we denote

S^{\epsilon}:=\inf\left\{J^{\epsilon}(z,w)\ :\ (z,w)\in X\,\,\,\,\text{with}\ \ \ P^{\epsilon}(z,w)=0\right\},\ \ P^{\epsilon}=I^{\epsilon}-N^{\epsilon},

there is a correspondent family
((z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon}))_{\epsilon}
such that

(3.3)

S^{\epsilon}=J^{\epsilon}(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon}),\ \ \ P^{\epsilon}(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon})=0,\ \ \ S(\omega)=\epsilon^{\frac{3p+8}{p(p+1)}}S^{\epsilon}\,.

We also have that in the distributional sense, the family
((z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon}))_{\epsilon}
is a solution of the system

(3.4)

\begin{cases}-\omega(\epsilon)\left(w-b\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}w^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}w^{(iv)}\right)+z+c\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}z^{\prime\prime}+c_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}z^{(iv)}-\alpha^{-1}G_{j,\epsilon,\alpha}(z,w)&=0,\\
-\omega(\epsilon)\left(z-b\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}z^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}z^{(iv)}\right)+w+a\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}w^{\prime\prime}+a_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}w^{(iv)}-\alpha^{-1}G_{j,\epsilon,\alpha}(z,w)&=0,\end{cases}

where
G_{j,\epsilon,\alpha}
for
j=1,2
is given by

G_{j,\epsilon,\alpha}(z,w)=G_{j}\left(\alpha\left(z,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}z^{\prime},\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}z^{\prime\prime},w,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}w^{\prime},\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}w^{\prime\prime}\right)\right).

In the case where
F(q,r,s,t)
is a
(p+2)
-homogeneous function, we see that

(3.5)

G_{j,\epsilon,\alpha}(z,w)=\alpha^{p+1}G_{j}\left(z,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}z^{\prime},\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}z^{\prime\prime},w,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p+1}}w^{\prime},\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}w^{\prime\prime}\right).

Moreover, if
F(q,r,s,t)
is a
(p+2)
-homogeneous function and homogeneous of degree
\beta
with respect to the variable
(r,t)
, then we see that

(3.6)

G_{j,\epsilon,\alpha}(z,w)=\alpha^{p+1}\epsilon^{\frac{\beta}{p+1}}G_{j}\left(z,z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime},w,w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}\right).

We observe that in the case
a+c+2b=0
, or equivalently
\sigma=\frac{1}{3}
, the family
((z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon}))_{\epsilon}
is related to solitary-wave solutions for a generalized fifth-order KdV equation as

\epsilon\to 0\qquad(|\omega|\to 1^{-}).

To see this, let us define on
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
the functionals

(3.7)

\Gamma_{\epsilon}(w)=J^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)w,w),

and

(3.8)

\wp_{\epsilon}(w)=P^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)w,w).

We also define

(3.9)

\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}=\inf_{w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left\{\Gamma_{\epsilon}(w):\wp_{\epsilon}(w)=0\right\}.

Now, if
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\wp_{\epsilon}(v)=0,
then
P^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)v,v)=0,
and therefore

\Gamma_{\epsilon}(v)=J^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)v,v)\geq S^{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-\frac{3p+8}{p(p+1)}}S(\omega(\epsilon)).

Consequently,

\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\geq S^{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-\frac{3p+8}{p(p+1)}}S(\omega(\epsilon))=\frac{p}{2(p+2)}\epsilon^{-\frac{3p+8}{p(p+1)}}I_{\omega(\epsilon)}(\psi_{\omega(\epsilon)},v_{\omega(\epsilon)}).

On the other hand, under the condition
a+c+2b=0
, we define the limiting functionals

\begin{split}I_{0}(w)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(w^{2}+(c_{2}-2b_{2}+a_{2})(w^{\prime\prime})^{2}\right)dx,\\
K_{0}(w)&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}K^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)w,w),\\
N_{0}(w)&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}N^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)w,w),\\
\wp_{0}(w)&=I_{0}(w)-N_{0}(w),\\
J_{0}(w)&=\frac{1}{2}I_{0}(w)-K_{0}(w),\end{split}

whose existence follows from (
3.2
). We then consider the limiting minimization problem

\mathcal{J}_{0}=\inf_{w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left\{J_{0}(w):\wp_{0}(w)=0\right\}.

Using the condition
a+c+2b=0
, we conclude that for every
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\Gamma_{\epsilon}(v)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(v^{2}+((-c-2b)\omega(\epsilon)^{2}-a)(v^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+((c_{2}-2b_{2})\omega(\epsilon)^{2}+a_{2})(v^{\prime\prime})^{2}\Big)\,dx-K^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)v,v)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(v^{2}+(c_{2}-2b_{2}+a_{2})(v^{\prime\prime})^{2}\right)\,dx-K_{0}(v)

\displaystyle=J_{0}(v).

Now, let
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
be such that
\wp_{0}(v)=0
. Then necessarily
N_{0}(v)>0
. Hence, for
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small,

N^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)v,v)>0.
Moreover, for
\beta>0
,

\wp_{\epsilon}(\beta v)=P^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)\beta v,\beta v)=\beta^{2}\left(I^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)v,v)-N^{\epsilon}(\beta\omega(\epsilon)v,\beta v)\right).

Using the properties of
N
in Lemma
2.1
, the function
\lambda(\beta)=\wp_{\epsilon}(\beta v)
is positive for
\beta<1
, but small and negative for
\beta>1
, but large. Then, there is
\beta(\epsilon)>0
such that

\wp_{\epsilon}(\beta(\epsilon)v)=0.

Furthermore,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\beta^{2}I^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)v,v)}{N^{\epsilon}(\beta\omega(\epsilon)v,\beta v)}=\frac{\beta_{0}^{2}I_{0}(v)}{N_{0}(\beta_{0}v)}=1,

where
\beta_{0}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\beta(\epsilon)
. Using the properties of
N_{0}
inherited from
N
in Lemma
2.1
, we see that
\beta_{0}=1
. For
\beta_{0}>1
, we get

1<\frac{I_{0}(v)}{\beta^{p}_{0}N_{0}(v)}=\frac{1}{\beta_{o}^{p}}

since
\wp_{0}(v)=0
, which is a contradiction. The same argument shows that
\beta_{0}<1
is not possible. So
\beta_{0}=1
. Therefore,

\Gamma_{\epsilon}(\beta(\epsilon)v)=J^{\epsilon}(\beta(\epsilon)\omega(\epsilon)v,\beta(\epsilon)v)\geq S^{\epsilon}.

Passing to the limit,

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}S^{\epsilon}\leq J_{0}(v).

Taking the infimum over all admissible
v
, we conclude that

(3.10)

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}S^{\epsilon}\leq\mathcal{J}_{0}.

Finally, for
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small,

(3.11)

\frac{p\epsilon^{-\frac{3p+8}{p(p+1)}}}{2(p+2)}I_{\omega(\epsilon)}(\psi_{\omega(\epsilon)},v_{\omega(\epsilon)})\leq S^{\epsilon}\leq 2\mathcal{J}_{0}.

Now, we are ready to establish the behavior as
\epsilon\to 0
of the functional (
3.8
) and the number (
3.9
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
a+c+2b=0.
Let
(\epsilon_{j})_{j}
be a sequence such that
\epsilon_{j}\to 0^{+}
, and let
\left((z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of minimizers of
J^{\epsilon_{j}}
under the constraint

P^{\epsilon_{j}}(z,w)=0.

Then there exists a subsequence (still denoted the same), a sequence
(x_{j})_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}
, and
(z_{0},w_{0})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

such that the translated sequence

(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}}):=(z^{\epsilon_{j}}(\cdot+x_{j}),w^{\epsilon_{j}}(\cdot+x_{j})),

converges strongly in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
(z_{0},w_{0})
. Moreover,
z_{0}=w_{0}
.

Proof.

From the previous discussion, we know that

S^{\epsilon_{j}}=J^{\epsilon_{j}}(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}}),\qquad P^{\epsilon_{j}}(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})=0,

and

\limsup_{j\to\infty}S^{\epsilon_{j}}\leq 2\mathcal{J}_{0}.

Moreover, from (
3.11
),

\frac{p}{2(p+2)}I^{\epsilon_{j}}(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})\leq S^{\epsilon_{j}}\leq 2\mathcal{J}_{0}.

Hence,
\left(I^{\epsilon_{j}}(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})\right)_{j}

is uniformly bounded.

We now apply the Lions’ concentration–compactness principle to the sequence
\left((z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})\right)_{j}.

Using the positivity of
I^{\epsilon_{j}}
(see Remark
2.3
), we define the positive measures

d\nu_{j}=\rho_{j}(x)\,dx,

where

\begin{split}\rho_{j}(z,w)=&\epsilon_{j}^{-\frac{4}{p+1}}\left(z-\omega({\epsilon_{j}})w\right)^{2}+w^{2}+\epsilon_{j}^{-\frac{2}{p+1}}|c|\left(z^{\prime}-\frac{b\omega({\epsilon_{j}})}{|c|}w^{\prime}\right)^{2}\\
&+c_{2}\left(z^{\prime\prime}-\frac{b\omega({\epsilon_{j}})}{c_{2}}w^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}+\epsilon_{j}^{-\frac{2}{p+1}}\left(\frac{ac-b^{2}\omega^{2}({\epsilon_{j}})}{|c|}\right)(w^{\prime})^{2}+\left(\frac{a_{2}c_{2}-b_{2}^{2}\omega^{2}({\epsilon_{j}})}{c_{2}}\right)\left(w^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}\,,\end{split}

which is the integrand of
I^{\epsilon_{j}}\left(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}}\right)
.

We note that, arguing exactly as in
[
9
, Lemmas 2.5 and 2.6]
, the Vanishing and Dichotomy alternatives can be excluded. Therefore, the Compactness alternative holds. Consequently, there exists a subsequence (still denoted the same) and a sequence
(x_{j})_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that for every
\gamma>0
, there exists
R>0
satisfying

\int_{B_{R}(x_{j})}d\nu_{j}\geq L_{0}-\gamma,\qquad j=1,2,\dots,

where

L_{0}:=\limsup_{j\to\infty}I^{\epsilon_{j}}(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}}).

Define

\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}}(x)=z^{\epsilon_{j}}(x+x_{j}),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}}(x)=w^{\epsilon_{j}}(x+x_{j}).

Then

(3.12)

\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{R}(0)}\tilde{\rho}_{j}(x)\,dx\leq 2\gamma,

where

\tilde{\rho}_{j}(x)=\rho_{j}(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}}(x),\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}}(x)).

Since
\left((\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}})\right)_{j}

is bounded in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
the Sobolev embedding theorem ensures that there exist
(z_{0},w_{0})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and a subsequence, still denoted by the same index, such that, as
j\to\infty
,

\displaystyle(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}})

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup(z_{0},w_{0})\quad\text{weakly in }H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}})

\displaystyle\to(z_{0},w_{0})\quad\text{strongly in }H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}})

\displaystyle\to(z_{0},w_{0})\quad\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Using (
3.12
) and arguing as usual in concentration–compactness theory, we conclude that

(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}})\to(z_{0},w_{0})\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Moreover, since

1-\omega^{2}(\epsilon_{j})=\epsilon_{j}^{\frac{4}{p+1}},

and

I^{\epsilon_{j}}(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})

remains uniformly bounded, we deduce that

\|\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}}-\omega(\epsilon_{j})\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to 0.

Since

\omega(\epsilon_{j})\to 1,

it follows that

\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}}-\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}}\to 0\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

and therefore

z_{0}=w_{0}.

Now, by weak lower semicontinuity,

I_{0}(z_{0})\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}I^{\epsilon_{j}}(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}})=L_{0}.

On the other hand, by weak convergence in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle I^{\epsilon_{j}}(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}}-z_{0},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}}-w_{0})

\displaystyle=I^{\epsilon_{j}}(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}})-I^{\epsilon_{j}}(z_{0},w_{0})+o(1).

Passing to the limit and using the previous inequality, we obtain

\limsup_{j\to\infty}I^{\epsilon_{j}}(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}}-z_{0},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}}-w_{0})\leq 0.

Since the quadratic form is nonnegative,

\lim_{j\to\infty}I^{\epsilon_{j}}(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}}-z_{0},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}}-w_{0})=0.

Finally, using the coercivity of
I^{\epsilon_{j}}
together with the already established
L^{2}
convergence, we conclude that

(\tilde{z}^{\epsilon_{j}},\tilde{w}^{\epsilon_{j}})\to(z_{0},w_{0})\quad\text{strongly in }H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

This completes the proof.
∎

The next result establishes the convergence properties of the functionals
defined in (
3.7
)–(
3.9
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
c+a+2b=0
. Let
\{(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon})\}_{\epsilon}
be a family of minimizers associated with

S^{\epsilon}
, namely,

J^{\epsilon}(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon})=S^{\epsilon},\qquad P^{\epsilon}(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon})=0.

Then, up to translations and subsequences, there exists

w_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
such that

z^{\epsilon}-w^{\epsilon}\to 0\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

and

w^{\epsilon}\to w_{0}\quad\text{strongly in }H^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{0}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}S^{\epsilon}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\Gamma_{\epsilon}(w^{\epsilon}),

and

\wp_{0}(w_{0})=0.

Proof.

From (
3.10
), we already know that

\mathcal{J}_{0}\geq\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\geq\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}S^{\epsilon}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.2
, after extraction of a subsequence
and suitable translations, there exists

w_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
such that

(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})\to(w_{0},w_{0})\quad\text{strongly in }H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Since

P^{\epsilon_{j}}(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})=0,

passing to the limit and using the convergence properties of

I^{\epsilon_{j}}
and
N^{\epsilon_{j}}
, we obtain

\wp_{0}(w_{0})=0.

Therefore, by the definition of
\mathcal{J}_{0}
,

\mathcal{J}_{0}\leq J_{0}(w_{0}).

Now, using the strong convergence together with the definitions of

\Gamma_{\epsilon}
,
K_{0}
, and
J_{0}
, we infer that

\displaystyle J_{0}(w_{0})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\Gamma_{\epsilon_{j}}(w^{\epsilon_{j}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}J^{\epsilon_{j}}(\omega(\epsilon_{j})w^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}}).

Since

z^{\epsilon_{j}}-w^{\epsilon_{j}}\to 0\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

and
\omega(\epsilon_{j})\to 1
, we also obtain

J^{\epsilon_{j}}(\omega(\epsilon_{j})w^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})-J^{\epsilon_{j}}(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})\to 0.

Hence,

J_{0}(w_{0})=\lim_{j\to\infty}J^{\epsilon_{j}}(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})=\lim_{j\to\infty}S^{\epsilon_{j}}.

Combining the previous inequalities yields

\mathcal{J}_{0}\leq\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}S^{\epsilon}\leq\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}S^{\epsilon}\leq\mathcal{J}_{0},

which proves that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}S^{\epsilon}=\mathcal{J}_{0}.

Finally, since

\Gamma_{\epsilon}(w^{\epsilon})=J^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)w^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon}),

and

J^{\epsilon}(\omega(\epsilon)w^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon})-J^{\epsilon}(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon})\to 0,

we conclude thus that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\Gamma_{\epsilon}(w^{\epsilon})=\mathcal{J}_{0},

and the proof is completed.
∎

Now, we are in a position to see that a translated subsequence of the renormalized sequence
(w^{\epsilon})
converges weakly to a

that satisfies the system (
1.3
). i.e.,

is a weak solution of a general fifth KdV-type equation (

) with

and

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
c+a+2b=0.
Let
\{\epsilon_{j}\}_{j}
be any sequence such that
\epsilon_{j}\to 0^{+}
. Then, up to translations and extraction of a subsequence, there exists a nontrivial function

such that

(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})\to(w_{0},w_{0})\qquad\text{strongly in }H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Moreover,

satisfies, in the distributional sense,

w_{0}+\left(c_{2}+a_{2}-2b_{2}\right)\partial_{x}^{4}w_{0}=f(w_{0},\partial_{x}w_{0},\partial_{x}^{2}w_{0}),

where

f(q,r,z)=H_{q}(q,r)-rH_{rq}(q,)-zH_{rr}(q,r),

and

K_{0}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}H(u,u_{x})\,dx,

for some functional
H
satisfying Esfahani-Lewandosky conditions in
[
14
]
. Consequently,
w_{0}
is a solitary-wave solution of the limiting generalized fifth-order KdV equation.

Proof.

The convergence statement follows directly from Lemma
3.2
.
In particular,

(z^{\epsilon_{j}},w^{\epsilon_{j}})\to(w_{0},w_{0})\quad\text{strongly in }H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

with
w_{0}\neq 0
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.3
,

\wp_{0}(w_{0})=0,\qquad J_{0}(w_{0})=\mathcal{J}_{0}.

Therefore,
w_{0}
is a minimizer of
J_{0}
constrained to the manifold

\mathcal{N}_{0}=\{w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}:\wp_{0}(w)=0\}.

By the Lagrange multiplier principle, there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

such that

J_{0}^{\prime}(w_{0})=\lambda\,\wp_{0}^{\prime}(w_{0}).

Now, using the homogeneity assumptions on the nonlinearity, we obtain

\langle N_{0}^{\prime}(w_{0}),w_{0}\rangle\geq(p+2)N_{0}(w_{0}).

Since

\wp_{0}(w_{0})=I_{0}(w_{0})-N_{0}(w_{0})=0,

it follows that

\displaystyle\langle\wp_{0}^{\prime}(w_{0}),w_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=2I_{0}(w_{0})-\langle N_{0}^{\prime}(w_{0}),w_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2I_{0}(w_{0})-(p+2)N_{0}(w_{0})

\displaystyle=-p\,I_{0}(w_{0})<0.

Hence,
\lambda=0
, and therefore
J_{0}^{\prime}(w_{0})=0
.

Finally, computing the Euler–Lagrange equation associated with

, we conclude that
w_{0}
satisfies

w_{0}+\left(c_{2}+a_{2}-2b_{2}\right)\partial_{x}^{4}w_{0}=f(w_{0},\partial_{x}w_{0},\partial_{x}^{2}w_{0}),

in the distributional sense, giving the proof.
∎

We end this section with a technical result that will be clever in the stability analysis in the homogeneous case.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
a+c+2b=0
and that
F(q,r,s,t)
is a homogeneous function of degree
(p+2)
. Let
(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the family of solutions to the scaled travelling-wave system (
3.4
) that strongly converges to
(z_{0},w_{0})
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
. Define the
\epsilon
-dependent energy norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2,\epsilon}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by

\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2.\epsilon}}^{2}=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\left(\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}\left(\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Then, there exists a constant
M>0
, independent of
\epsilon
, such that, for all
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small,

\epsilon^{\frac{p-3}{p+1}}\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2,\epsilon}}\leq M.

Proof.

We begin by introducing the shorthand notation
\delta=\frac{2}{p+1}
for the exponents, so that
\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}=\epsilon^{\delta}
and
\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}=\epsilon^{2\delta}
. Differentiating the relation
\omega^{2}(\epsilon)=1-\epsilon^{2\delta}
with respect to
\epsilon
yields

2\omega(\epsilon)\omega^{\prime}(\epsilon)=-2\delta\epsilon^{2\delta-1}\implies\omega^{\prime}(\epsilon)=-\frac{\delta\epsilon^{2\delta-1}}{\omega(\epsilon)}=O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}\right).

Now, consider the scaled travelling-wave system written in component form

(3.13)

\begin{cases}-\omega(\epsilon)\left(w-b\epsilon^{\delta}w^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}w^{(iv)}\right)+z+c\epsilon^{\delta}z^{\prime\prime}+c_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}z^{(iv)}-\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}G_{1}(\eta^{\epsilon})=0,\\
-\omega(\epsilon)\left(z-b\epsilon^{\delta}z^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}z^{(iv)}\right)+w+a\epsilon^{\delta}w^{\prime\prime}+a_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}w^{(iv)}-\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}G_{2}(\eta^{\epsilon})=0.\end{cases}

where we use (
3.5
) and
(\eta^{\epsilon})^{T}=(z^{\epsilon},\epsilon^{\frac{\delta}{2}}(z^{\epsilon})^{\prime},\epsilon^{\delta}(z^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime},w^{\epsilon},\epsilon^{\frac{\delta}{2}}(w^{\epsilon})^{\prime},\epsilon^{\delta}(w^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime})
.
Applying the differential operator
\partial_{\epsilon}
to both equations in (
3.13
), using the product and chain rules, we obtain the linearized system. This system can be compactly written in matrix form as

(3.14)

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}\\
\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon}\end{pmatrix}-\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}\nabla G(\eta^{\epsilon})\cdot\theta^{\epsilon}=\begin{pmatrix}R_{1}\\
R_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
(\theta^{\epsilon})^{T}=(\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon},\epsilon^{\frac{\delta}{2}}(\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})^{\prime},\epsilon^{\delta}(\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime},\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\epsilon^{\frac{\delta}{2}}(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon})^{\prime},\epsilon^{\delta}(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime})
and the matrix differential operator
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}=(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}
acts on the derivative vector and is explicitly defined by its components

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,11}

\displaystyle=-\omega(\epsilon)\left(1-b\epsilon^{\delta}\partial_{y}^{2}+b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}\partial_{y}^{4}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,12}

\displaystyle=\left(1+c\epsilon^{\delta}\partial_{y}^{2}+c_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}\partial_{y}^{4}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,21}

\displaystyle=\left(1+a\epsilon^{\delta}\partial_{y}^{2}+a_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}\partial_{y}^{4}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,22}

\displaystyle=-\omega(\epsilon)\left(1-b\epsilon^{\delta}\partial_{y}^{2}+b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}\partial_{y}^{4}\right).

The non-homogeneous source terms
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
collect all terms resulting from the explicit differentiation of the
\epsilon
-dependent coefficients and the wave speed
\omega(\epsilon)

\begin{split}R_{1}=&\omega^{\prime}(\epsilon)\left(w^{\epsilon}-b\epsilon^{\delta}(w^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}(w^{\epsilon})^{(iv)}\right)+\omega(\epsilon)\left(-\delta b\epsilon^{\delta-1}(w^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime}+2\delta b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta-1}(w^{\epsilon})^{(iv)}\right)\\
&-\left(\delta c\epsilon^{\delta-1}(z^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime}+2\delta c_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta-1}(z^{\epsilon})^{(iv)}\right)+\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}\left(\frac{4}{p+1}G_{1}(\eta^{\epsilon})+\epsilon\nabla G_{1}(\eta^{\epsilon})\cdot\sigma^{\epsilon}\right),\\
R_{2}=&\omega^{\prime}(\epsilon)\left(z^{\epsilon}-b\epsilon^{\delta}(z^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime}+b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}(z^{\epsilon})^{(iv)}\right)+\omega(\epsilon)\left(-\delta b\epsilon^{\delta-1}(z^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime}+2\delta b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta-1}(z^{\epsilon})^{(iv)}\right)\\
&-\left(\delta a\epsilon^{\delta-1}(w^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime}+2\delta a_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta-1}(w^{\epsilon})^{(iv)}\right)++\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}\left(\frac{4}{p+1}G_{2}(\eta^{\epsilon})+\epsilon\nabla G_{2}(\eta^{\epsilon})\cdot\sigma^{\epsilon}\right),\end{split}

where
(\sigma^{\epsilon})^{T}=(0,\epsilon^{\frac{\delta}{2}}(z^{\epsilon})^{\prime},\epsilon^{\delta}(z^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime},0,\epsilon^{\frac{\delta}{2}}(w^{\epsilon})^{\prime},\epsilon^{\delta}(w^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime})
.
Given that
(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon})
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{H}_{2,\epsilon}
for small
\epsilon
, the leading order behavior of these residuals is dominated by the terms carrying the lowest power of
\epsilon
, which corresponds precisely to
\epsilon^{2\delta-1}=\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}
. Consequently, we have the uniform
L^{2}
-estimate:

(3.15)

\|R_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}},

where
C_{1}>0
is independent of
\epsilon
.

To derive the energy estimate, we test the matrix system (
3.14
) against the vector of derivatives
(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})^{T}
via the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
inner product

(3.16)

\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}\\
\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}\\
\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}=\langle R_{1},\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}\rangle+\langle R_{2},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon}\rangle.

Expanding the left-hand side of (
3.16
), we integrate by parts the higher-order spatial derivatives inside the diagonal operators
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,11}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,22}
. Exploiting the self-adjointness and coercivity of the fourth-order linear elliptic blocks yields:

-\omega\langle(1-b\epsilon^{\delta}\partial_{y}^{2}+b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}\partial_{y}^{4})\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}\rangle=-\omega\left(\|\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b\epsilon^{\delta}\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon})^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b_{2}\epsilon^{2\delta}\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon})^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

The off-diagonal coupling terms
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,12},\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,21}
and the localized linearized nonlinearity potentials containing
\nabla G(\eta^{\epsilon})
contribute lower-order terms that are strictly controlled by standard Sobolev embeddings due to the homogeneity of
F
. Thus, for
\epsilon
sufficiently small, the left-hand side of (
3.16
) matches the structure of the
\epsilon
-dependent energy norm and satisfies the coercive lower bound

(3.17)

\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}\\
\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon}\end{pmatrix}-\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}\nabla G(\eta^{\epsilon})\cdot\theta^{\epsilon},\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}\\
\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}\geq C_{2}\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2,\epsilon}}^{2}.

For the right-hand side of (
3.16
), we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality followed by Young’s inequality with
\gamma>0

\displaystyle\left|\langle R_{1},\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}\rangle+\langle R_{2},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|R_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\gamma}\left(\|R_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}+\gamma\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2,\epsilon}}^{2}
, choosing
\gamma
sufficiently small allows us to absorb the term
\gamma\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
directly into the left-hand side energy functional (
3.17
) without losing
\epsilon
-powers. Combining (
3.15
) and (
3.17
), we deduce

\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2,\epsilon}}^{2}\leq C_{3}\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}\right)^{2}.

Taking the square root of both sides yields

\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2,\epsilon}}\leq C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}.

Finally, multiplying both sides by the target weight
\epsilon^{\frac{p-3}{p+1}}
, we obtain

\epsilon^{\frac{p-3}{p+1}}\|(\partial_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon},\partial_{\epsilon}z^{\epsilon})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2,\epsilon}}\leq C_{4}\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}\epsilon^{\frac{p-3}{p+1}}=C_{4},

which is uniformly bounded as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, concluding the proof.
∎

J_{\omega}
for any
U\in G_{\omega}
. We may define

(4.1)

\omega(U)=S^{-1}(J_{1}(U))

for any such
U
. The continuity of this map follows from the continuity of
S^{-1}
and
J_{1}
.
∎

The key result needed in the stability analysis is the following:

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that
S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)>0
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for
\Psi\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}
and
V\in\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{G}_{\omega})
it follows that

\mathcal{H}(V)-\mathcal{H}\left(\Psi\right)+\omega(V)\left(\mathcal{Q}(V)-\mathcal{Q}\left(\Psi\right)\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)|\omega(V)-\omega|^{2}.

Proof.

Using that
S^{\prime}(\omega)=\mathcal{Q}(\Psi)
and Taylor expansion,

S(\omega_{1})\geq S(\omega)+S^{\prime}(\omega)(\omega_{1}-\omega)+\frac{1}{4}S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)|\omega_{1}-\omega|^{2}.

for
\omega_{1}
sufficiently close to
\omega
, which implies that

S(\omega(V))\geq H\left(\Psi\right)+\mathcal{Q}(\Psi)\omega(V)+\frac{1}{4}S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)|\omega(V)-\omega|^{2}.

for
V\in\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{G}_{\omega})
and
\varepsilon>0
but small enough. If we had
P_{\omega(V)}(V)\leq 0
, we chose
\Phi\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega(V)}
then,

\displaystyle S(\omega(V))

\displaystyle=J_{\omega(V),p}(\Phi)\leq J_{\omega(V),p}(V)=J_{\omega(V)}(V)-\frac{1}{p+2}P_{\omega(V)}(V)

\displaystyle\leq J_{\omega(V)}(V)-\frac{1}{2}P_{\omega(V)}(V)=J_{1}(V)=S(\omega(V)).

On the other hand, If we had
P_{\omega(V)}(V)\geq 0
, then

S(\omega(V))=J_{1}(V)=J_{\omega(V)}(V)-\frac{1}{p+2}P_{\omega(V)}(V)\leq J_{\omega(V)}(V)=\mathcal{H}(V)+\omega(V)\mathcal{Q}(V).

So, we find that all of the quantities above are equivalent, meaning that
V
achieves the same minimum as
\Phi
, and so
V\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega(V)}
. In other words,

S(\omega(V))=J_{\omega(V)}(V)=\mathcal{H}(V)+\omega(V)\mathcal{Q}(V).

Using this fact in a previous inequality, we get the desired result.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
0<\omega<\min\left\{1,\frac{-a}{b},\frac{-c}{b},\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}},\frac{c_{2}}{b_{2}}\right\}
and the wave speed
0<{\omega}<1
near
1
with
S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)>0
. Then the set of ground states
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
is stable.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. If
S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)>0
and the ground state set
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
is unstable, Then for
\Psi_{\omega}\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}

there exists a sequence of initial data
(U_{0}^{k})_{k}\subset\mathcal{V}_{\frac{1}{k}}(\mathcal{G}_{\omega})
, and
t_{k}>0
such that the solution
U_{k}(t)
of the Boussinesq system (
1.1
) with initial data
U(0)=U_{0}^{k}
satisfies (see Remark
4.2
)

(4.2)

\lim_{k\to\infty}||U_{0}^{k}-\Psi_{\omega}||_{X}=0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \inf_{V\in\mathcal{G}_{{\omega}}}\|U_{k}(t_{k})-V\|_{X}\geq\varepsilon>0.

Now, using Lemma
4.4
, the invariance of
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
and
\mathcal{Q}
on solutions and the continuity of
S
, we conclude that

\lim_{k\to\infty}J_{1}(U_{k}(t_{k}))=\lim_{k\to\infty}S(\omega(U_{k}(t_{k})))=S(\omega).

Moreover, we also have

\lim_{k\to\infty}J_{\omega}(U_{k}(t_{k}))=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega}(U_{k}(t_{k}))+\omega\mathcal{Q}(U_{k}(t_{k})))=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega}(U_{k}(0))+\omega\mathcal{Q}(U_{k}(0)))=S(\omega).

On the other hand, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}P_{\omega}(U_{k}(t_{k}))=\lim_{k\to\infty}(J_{\omega}(U_{k}(t_{k}))-J_{1}(U_{k}(t_{k})))=0,

which means that

is a minimizing sequence. Then, there is a subsequence

This clearly contradicts the condition (
4.2
), and thus the set of ground state solutions

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let

,

Next, using the same notation introduced in Section

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{p+1}}I_{2}^{\epsilon}(z^{\epsilon},w^{\epsilon})=

\displaystyle-2\omega(\epsilon)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(z^{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}+b\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}z_{x}^{\epsilon}w_{x}^{\epsilon}+b_{2}\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}z_{xx}^{\epsilon}w_{xx}^{\epsilon}\Big)\,dx.

Theorem 4.7
.

There exists
\omega_{0}\in(0,1)

From Theorem

On the other hand, from Lemma

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

Moreover, there is

, for

and

. In the case where

\omega*\in(0,1)

Proof.

Differentiating with respect to
\omega
the formula for

given by (

given by (

\frac{d}{d\omega}I_{\omega}(\Psi_{\omega})=\frac{I_{2,\omega}(\Psi_{\omega})}{\omega}

Then, differentiating with respect to
\omega

given (
4.5
) and using the previous fact, we get the desired formula for

Now, we point out that the first term of

is nonnegative, so we will determine the sign of

\frac{d}{d\omega}\left(\frac{I_{2,\omega}(\Psi_{\omega})}{\omega}\right).

Using the KdV scaling induced by (

Therefore,

where

From the previous notation, we have

(4.6)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\omega}\left(\frac{I_{2,\omega}(\Psi_{\omega})}{\omega}\right)&=\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\left(\frac{\epsilon^{\frac{3p+8}{p(p+1)}}}{\omega(\epsilon)}I_{2}^{\epsilon}(z,w)\right)\frac{d\epsilon}{d\omega}\\
&=(p+1)\omega(\epsilon)\epsilon^{\frac{p-3}{p+1}}\left(\frac{(8-p)}{p(p+1)}\epsilon^{-\frac{(p+4)(p-2)}{p(p+1)}}A(\epsilon)+\epsilon^{\frac{8-p}{p(p+1)}}A^{\prime}(\epsilon)\right)\\
&=\frac{(8-p)}{p}\omega(\epsilon)\epsilon^{\frac{8-p}{p(p+1)}}A(\epsilon)+(p+1)\omega(\epsilon)\epsilon^{\frac{8-p}{p(p+1)}}\epsilon^{\frac{p-3}{p+1}}A^{\prime}(\epsilon),\end{split}

where we are using
\omega^{2}=1-\epsilon^{\frac{4}{p+1}}
.

On the other hand, differentiating the functional
A(\epsilon)
yields terms involving

\partial_{\epsilon}z
,
\partial_{\epsilon}w
, as well as derivatives of the coefficients

\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p+1}}
and

and therefore

\epsilon^{\frac{p-3}{p+1}}\epsilon^{\frac{8-p}{p(p+1)}}R(z,w,\partial_{\epsilon}z,\partial_{\epsilon}w)=O\!\left(\epsilon^{\frac{8-p}{p(p+1)}}\right),

for

5.
Numerical experiments

In this section, we numerically investigate the stability of the family of solitary wave solutions discussed previously, focusing on the wave-velocity regime
0<\omega<1
and modeling parameters satisfying the condition
a+c+2b=0
.

To compute solitary wave solutions of the system (
1.1
) for arbitrary wave velocities, we adapt the numerical scheme introduced in
[
14
]
, originally developed for the fifth-order scalar KdV equation. This method is modified to approximate solutions of the system of traveling-wave equations (
1.3
), employing a Fourier spectral approach
augmented with stabilizing factors for both the wave elevation
\eta
and the fluid velocity
u
, thereby ensuring convergence of the iterative scheme used in our simulations.

The function
S^{\prime}(\omega)
is computed using the analytical expression
S^{\prime}(\omega)=I_{2}(\Psi_{\omega})
, obtained in Lemma
2.4
, while
S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)
is approximated using a second-order central difference scheme applied to
S^{\prime}(\omega)
. As established in the analytical results presented in the previous section, a traveling-wave solution corresponding to the wave velocity
\omega
is stable when
S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)>0
, and unstable when
S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)<0
.

To derive the numerical scheme, we apply the Fourier transform to equations (
1.3
), yielding

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\omega(\hat{v}_{k}+dk^{2}\hat{v}_{k}+d_{2}k^{4}\hat{v}_{k})+\hat{\psi}_{k}-ck^{2}\hat{\psi}_{k}+c_{2}k^{4}\hat{\psi}_{k}&=\widehat{G_{1}},\\
-\omega(\hat{\psi}_{k}+bk^{2}\hat{\psi}_{k}+b_{2}k^{4}\hat{\psi}_{k})+\hat{v}_{k}-ak^{2}\hat{v}_{k}+a_{2}k^{4}\hat{v}_{k}&=\widehat{G_{2}}.\end{cases}

We recall that
b=d>0
,
b_{2}=d_{2}>0
,
a,c<0
,
a_{2},c_{2}>0
, and
\widehat{G_{1}},\widehat{G_{2}}
denote the Fourier transforms of the nonlinear functions
G_{1}(\psi,\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime},v,v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})
and
G_{2}(\psi,\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime},v,v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})
, respectively.

Rewriting these equations, we get

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\omega(1+dk^{2}+d_{2}k^{4})\hat{v}_{k}+(1-ck^{2}+c_{2}k^{4})\hat{\psi}_{k}&=\widehat{G_{1}},\\
(1-ak^{2}+a_{2}k^{4})\hat{v}_{k}-\omega(1+bk^{2}+b_{2}k^{4})\hat{\psi}_{k}&=\widehat{G_{2}},\end{cases}

and thus, we can write

(5.1)

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{k}=\frac{\widehat{G_{1}}D_{22}-\widehat{G_{2}}D_{12}}{D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12}},

and

(5.2)

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{k}=\frac{\widehat{G_{2}}D_{11}-\widehat{G_{1}}D_{21}}{D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12}},

where

D=\begin{pmatrix}D_{11}&D_{12}\\
D_{21}&D_{22}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\omega(1+dk^{2}+d_{2}k^{4})&1-ck^{2}+c_{2}k^{4}\\
1-ak^{2}+a_{2}k^{4}&-\omega(1+bk^{2}+b_{2}k^{4})\end{pmatrix}.

On the other hand, by multiplying equation (
5.1
) by
\hat{v}_{k}
and equation (
5.2
) by
\hat{\psi}_{k}
, and then adding over
k
, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{1}(v):=\frac{\sum_{k}(D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12})\hat{v}_{k}^{2}}{\sum_{k}(\widehat{G}_{1}D_{22}-\widehat{G_{2}}D_{12})\hat{v}_{k}}=1,

and

\displaystyle P_{2}(\psi):=\frac{\sum_{k}(D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12})\hat{\psi}_{k}^{2}}{\sum_{k}(\widehat{G_{2}}D_{11}-\widehat{G_{1}}D_{21})\hat{\psi}_{k}}=1.

To compute approximate solutions to the traveling wave equations, we select initial values
\psi^{0}
and
v^{0}
. Then, for
s\geq 0
, we define the sequence

(5.3)

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{k}^{s+1}=\frac{\widehat{G_{1}}(\tilde{\psi^{s}},\tilde{\psi^{s}}^{\prime},\tilde{\psi^{s}}^{\prime\prime},\tilde{v^{s}},\tilde{v^{s}}^{\prime},\tilde{v^{s}}^{\prime\prime})D_{22}-\widehat{G_{2}}(\tilde{\psi^{s}},\tilde{\psi^{s}}^{\prime},\tilde{\psi^{s}}^{\prime\prime},\tilde{v^{s}},\tilde{v^{s}}^{\prime},\tilde{v^{s}}^{\prime\prime})D_{12}}{D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12}},

and

(5.4)

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{k}^{s+1}=\frac{\widehat{G_{2}}(\tilde{\psi^{s}},\tilde{\psi^{s}}^{\prime},\tilde{\psi^{s}}^{\prime\prime},\tilde{v^{s}},\tilde{v^{s}}^{\prime},\tilde{v^{s}}^{\prime\prime})D_{11}-\widehat{G_{1}}(\tilde{\psi^{s}},\tilde{\psi^{s}}^{\prime},\tilde{\psi^{s}}^{\prime\prime},\tilde{v^{s}},\tilde{v^{s}}^{\prime},\tilde{v^{s}}^{\prime\prime})D_{21}}{D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12}},

where
\tilde{v}^{s}:=\alpha_{s}v^{s}
,
\tilde{\psi}^{s}:=\beta_{s}\psi^{s}
, and
\alpha_{s}
,
\beta_{s}\in\mathbb{R}
are solutions of the following equations

(5.5)

\begin{cases}P_{1}(\alpha_{s}v^{s})=1,\\
P_{2}(\beta_{s}\psi^{s})=1.\end{cases}

We note that the quantities
\alpha_{s}
and
\beta_{s}
are stabilizing factors introduced to ensure the convergence of the iteration defined in (
5.3
) and (
5.4
).
In the numerical simulations, we initiate the iteration (
5.3
)-(
5.4
) with

v^{0}(x)=\psi^{0}(x)=e^{-0.05(x-50)^{2}},

and we take
N=2^{12}
points along the computational domain is
[0,L]=[0,100]
.

5.1.
Homogeneous case

Suppose that the functions
G_{1},G_{2}
are both homogeneous of order
p+1
. Then, given that the parameters
\alpha_{s}
,
\beta_{s}
are chosen at each iteration
s
such that
P_{1}(\alpha_{s}v^{s})=1
and

P_{2}(\beta_{s}\psi^{s})=1
, we obtain that

(5.6)

\begin{cases}&\frac{\sum_{k}(D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12})\alpha_{s}^{2}\hat{v}_{k}^{2}}{\sum_{k}\alpha_{s}^{p+1}(\widehat{G}_{1}D_{22}-\widehat{G_{2}}D_{12})\alpha_{s}\hat{v}_{k}}=1,\\
\\
&\frac{\sum_{k}(D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12})\beta_{s}^{2}\hat{\psi}_{k}^{2}}{\sum_{k}\beta_{s}^{p+1}(\widehat{G_{2}}D_{11}-\widehat{G_{1}}D_{21})\beta_{s}\hat{\psi}_{k}}=1.\end{cases}

From equations (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we obtain the explicit expressions for the parameters
\alpha_{s},\beta_{s}
given by

(5.7)

\begin{cases}\alpha_{s}&=M_{s}^{1/p},\\
\beta_{s}&=N_{s}^{1/p},\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle M_{s}:=\frac{\sum_{k}(D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12})\hat{v}_{k}^{2}}{\sum_{k}(\widehat{G}_{1}D_{22}-\widehat{G_{2}}D_{12})\hat{v}_{k}},

and

\displaystyle N_{s}:=\frac{\sum_{k}(D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12})\hat{\psi}_{k}^{2}}{\sum_{k}(\widehat{G_{2}}D_{11}-\widehat{G_{1}}D_{21})\hat{\psi}_{k}}.

As a consequence, the iteration defined by equations (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{k}^{s+1}=M_{s}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\frac{\widehat{G_{1}}(U)D_{22}-\widehat{G_{2}}(U)D_{12}}{D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12}},

and

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{k}^{s+1}=N_{s}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\frac{\widehat{G_{2}}(U)D_{11}-\widehat{G_{1}}(U)D_{21}}{D_{11}D_{22}-D_{21}D_{12}},

where
U=({\psi^{s}},{\psi^{s}}^{\prime},{\psi^{s}}^{\prime\prime},{v^{s}},{v^{s}}^{\prime},{v^{s}}^{\prime\prime})
.

S^{\prime\prime}(\omega)

5.2.
Non-homogeneous case

When the nonhomogeneous terms
G_{1},G_{2}

5.7
)
can no longer be used.
Instead, given the values of
\hat{v}_{k}^{s}
,
\hat{\psi}_{k}^{s}
at the iteration step
s
, we compute the stabilizing factors using, for instance, a Newton-type method. This involves solving the following nonlinear equations for
\alpha_{s},\beta_{s}
, at each step:

P_{1}(\alpha_{s}v^{s})=1,\quad\text{and}\quad P_{2}(\beta_{s}\psi^{s})=1.

Once the stabilizing parameters
\alpha_{s},\beta_{s}
are computed, we use the update formulas (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) to obtain the next iterates
\hat{v}_{k}^{s+1}
and
\hat{\psi}_{k}^{s+1}
for iteration
s+1
.
