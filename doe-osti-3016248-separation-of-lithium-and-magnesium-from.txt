 Summary of Research Results:

 The purpose of this work was to separate lithium and magnesium from lithium-containing brines
 by applying a zwitterionic simulated moving bed (zSMB) system that NLR developed. This work
 was needed to validate lithium and magnesium separation for future development at scale. Below
 is a summary of the tasks outlined in the Statement of Work and the results that were generated.

 Task 1: Contractor will perform a zSMB process demonstration with results achieving >75%
 yield and >90% purity of lithium from magnesium and calcium.

 Deliverable: Contractor will demonstrate zSMB operation to achieve target yield and purity
 of lithium from a model brine solution or brine sample provided by the Participant. The
 separation performance for at least one sample demonstration will be provided to Participant.
 Additional demonstration will be reported.

 Real brine loading tests results using QAC3CA resin

 Pretreated brackish water containing 770 ppm Li from Louisiana was conducted using a
 QAC3CA column. QAC3CA was chosen over other zwitterionic resins for large-scale separation
 because of its high resin stability and reproducibility. The long loading tests of the pre-treated
 Olokun Minerals brine (Figure 1) was done where the column length was 108 cm. The long
 loading test elution order of monovalent salts are as follows KCl, NaCl, and LiCl, which follows
 the decreasing order of ionic size and Δ𝐺ℎ𝑦𝑑. For reference, Δ𝐺ℎ𝑦𝑑 represents the Gibbs free
 energy of hydration of the ions in solution. If Δ𝐺ℎ𝑦𝑑 is only slightly negative, the ion is easily
 hydrated. Thus, monovalent cations form weak hydration shells due to slightly negative Δ𝐺ℎ𝑦𝑑
 values. While divalent cations form stronger hydration shells with more negative Δ𝐺ℎ𝑦𝑑 and this
 leads to bigger size hydration shells than the weakly hydrated monovalent cations in the mobile
 phase. This leads to divalent cations fully size-excluded and elutes faster than the monovalent
 cations. The rolled-up Li peak ahead of Mg and Ca fronts show that the Li displacement
 occurred by Ca and Mg fronts, indicated the nonlinear separation mechanisms. The separation
 window between Li and divalent cations became wide as salts moves down a long column
 length. As a result, 79.2% of Li is recovered with a 99.0% purity of Li over Mg and Ca, resulting
 in QAC3CA resin a promising material for large scale separations of lithium from Louisiana oil
 and gas produced water. Future work needs to focus on continuing to reduce the water
 consumption and increase the yield.

 1st 40-hour Loading Test using Synthetic Brine in a Multi-column Zwitterionic
 Chromatography (MZC)

 The MZC mineral recovery process successfully demonstrated lithium extraction capabilities that
 exceeded performance targets. The process utilized synthesized brine solutions from Olokun
 Minerals with an initial lithium concentration meeting the minimum target of 300 ppm. The
 system was operated continuously for 40 hours using a 3-3 column configuration (15 cm internal
 diameter, 60 cm length column) with a total volume of 63.62 L, processing feed at 3.9 L/min at
 130 psi with 19.85-minute cycle times. As shown in Figure 2, two configurations were tested:
 CZIC2 (Continuous Zwitterionic Chromatography) achieved 96% lithium recovery but only
 33.9% purity, while LL40 (Long Loading 40h MZC) showed 93% recovery with superior purity
 at 62.69% and better selectivity rations (Li/Mg of 137.8 vs. 91.2 for CZIC2) as shown in Figures
 3 and 4.

 The system demonstrated competitive energy efficiency at 2.57 kWh/kg LiCl and excellent water
 usage with a water-to-feed ratio of 1.72, positioning it favorably against other direct lithium
 extraction methods. The process achieved a throughput of 6.68 kg LiCl/day with a productivity
 of 0.664 kg LiCl/L/day and relatively low carbon intensity of 1.20 g CO2e/g LiCl. The
 continuous 40-hour operation validated the system’s stability and scalability potential, with
 elemental analysis performed using ICP-OES throughout the run. While technology shows
 promise for commercial lithium recovery from brine solutions, the results highlight a key trade-
 off between yield and purity that will require further optimization, particularly since both
 configurations fell short of the 75% purity target despite exceeding recovery goals.

 2nd Long Loading 20-h test using Real Brine in a Simulated Moving Bed Chromatography
 System with Zwitterionic Resins

 Pre-treated oil and gas produced water was used to conduct a 20-h test containing 207 ppm of
 lithium along with higher concentrations of competing ions – over 200 times more sodium
 (42,641 ppm) and nearly 150 times more calcium (30,330 ppm) than lithium, representing a
 much more challenging separation scenario than controlled synthetic brines. The process
 operated with three distinct zones at different flow rates over 7 cycles with 21.21-minute
 switching times using a multi-zone configuration designed to separate lithium from the complex
 ionic matrix (Figure 5).

 5

 Fig. 5. (A) zSMB configuration and operating conditions, and effluent profiles for (B) Extract and
 (C) Raffinate ports, respectively. The columns were preloaded with brine and eluted with water up
 to 8 column volume.

 The results demonstrate that the SMB configuration can handle real-world brine complexity,
 successfully separating lithium into the raffinate stream (136.77 ppm Li) while concentrating
 calcium and magnesium in the extract stream (14,109 ppm Ca and 295 ppm Mg). The system
 achieved a lithium productivity of 0.722 g Li/L/h, requiring a total column volume of 57.75 L to
 produce 1 kg Li/day. However, processing real brine came with significantly higher energy costs
 -15 kWh/kg Li and carbon intensity of 7 kg CO2e/kg Li compared to the synthetic brine results
 of 2.57 kWh/kg Li and 1.20 g CO2e/g Li). This increase likely reflects the increased difficulty of
 processing real brine with its higher ionic strength, competing ion concentrations, and trace
 contaminants like boron, sulfate, and phosphate that were absent in synthetic solutions, providing
 valuable scaling data as the technology progresses toward commercial deployment.

 As shown in Tables 2 and 3, the energy duty calculations reveal a dramatic increase in energy
 requirements when transitioning from synthetic to real brine processing, highlighting the
 significant challenges of real-world implementation. The 40-hour synthetic brine achieved
 remarkably low energy consumption of 2.57 kWh/kg LiCl, with the majority of energy
 consumed by the SMB valve system and minimal pump energy requirements. In contrast, the 20-
 hour real brine run required 15 kWh/kg Li – nearly a 6-fold increase in energy intensity – along
 with a proportionally higher footprint of 7 kg CO2e/kg Li compared to 1.20 g CO2e/g LiCl for
 synthetic brine. This substantial energy penalty reflects the increased complexity of separating
 lithium from real brine containing over 200 times more competing sodium ions and 150 times
 more calcium, along with trace contaminants like boron sulfate, and phosphate that create
 additional separation challenges. The energy distribution analysis shows that while pump energy
 remained minimal in both cases, the overall system energy requirements increased due to low Li
 concentration in feed. The separation mechanism required more energy to overcome the higher
 ionic strength and compositional complexity of using industrial brines with low Li concentration
 compared to controlled synthetic solutions, suggesting that the separation is efficient for the
 feedstock containing high Li concentration (>1,000 ppm).

 Task 3: Contractor will synthesize novel zwitterionic functionalized resins and conduct salt
 partitioning test to recover Li and other critical minerals (Co, Ni, Cu, etc.)

 Deliverable: Contractor and participant will report the screening results of novel zwitterionic
 resins for the recovery of Lithium and other critical minerals (Co, Ni, Cu, etc.). Upon successful
 results, Contractor will provide a report detailing the synthesis and procedures for scaling the
 resin and report zSMB separation performances in zSMB and the results of TEA an LCA using
 zSMB results using novel resins data from Task 3.

 The contractor synthesized new zwitterionic functionalized resins and performed salt partitioning
 tests to evaluate the recovery of lithium (Li) and other critical minerals (Co, Ni, Cu, etc.).

 During the early stages of CRADA development, the participant and contractor discussed
 candidate materials for synthesis. Commercial zwitterionic resins such as (QA⁺)C1(CA⁻) are
 available, but they cannot effectively separate LiCl from MgCl₂ or CaCl₂. Previously developed
 QAC3CA resins showed promising performance, and Olokun Minerals expressed interest in
 expanding their intellectual property (IP) strategy by producing new resins with comparable or
 improved separation capability. To support this effort, the following resins were synthesized:
 QACA2, QAC3PA, and QAC4CA.

 Salt partitioning tests were then conducted to evaluate their performance.

 8

 Figure 6 presents single-pulse tests of individual salts on columns packed with each resin.
 Results from QAC3CA are shown as a control. QAC4CA displayed salt partitioning behavior
 comparable to QAC3CA. QAC3PA also demonstrated a clear partitioning effect for monovalent
 salts; however, divalent salts eluted earlier than LiCl. QACA2 showed nearly identical retention
 across the five major salts (K, Na, Li, Ca, and Mg chlorides), while heavy metal salts exhibited
 progressively greater retention (Mn < Ni < Co).

 Figure 7 shows a Li-containing brine loading test using QAC3PA. Partial separation was
 observed: monovalent salts eluted first, followed by Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ salts. The Li peak appeared
 slightly ahead of Ca and Mg, creating a separation window between 1–1.5 BV. This indicates
 QAC3PA, like QAC3CA, can be applied to continuous operations for Li/divalent salt
 separations.

 Figure 8 presents a salt mixture test (Na, K, Li, Ca, and Mg chlorides, 10 g/L each) using
 QACA2. The retention times of the salts were closely aligned, suggesting that longer column
 lengths would be required to achieve meaningful separation between Li⁺ and divalent cations.

 The resin synthesis methods for QACA2, QAC4CA, and QAC3PA were described below (Filed
   ROI24-142, PCT 19-153A)

 10

 Modified IRC748 Chelating Resin (QACA2)

 A mixture of 30.00 g if IRC748 resign, DMF (150 ml) and Methyl iodide (20.0 ml, 300 mmol)
 was stirred in pressure flask at 70oC for 12 hours. The solids were filtered off, washed with water
 (3x50 ml) and methanol (2x50ml. The polymer was suspended in 10% KOH (150 ml), stirred for
 6 hours and the polymer was isolated by filtration, washed with water (2x75 ml) and methanol
 (2x75ml). After drying at under vacuum (0.2 torr) at 60o C for 5 hours 27.74 g of the titled resin
 was obtained.

 PSQAC4CA

 A mixture of PSDA (20.00 g, 80 mmol), methyl 5-bromovalerate (18.53 g, 95 mmol), DMF (90
 ml), and a catalytic amount of tetra-n-butylammonium iodide (0.46 g, 1.3 mmol) was stirred at
 90 °C for 40 hours. The resin was filtered off, washed with methanol (3x50 ml), and dried under
 a flow of nitrogen. The crude solid was stirred with a solution of KOH (6.0 g) in 150 ml of water
 for 7 hours before it was isolated by filtration. Subsequently, the resin was washed with water (3
 x 60 mL), and methanol (2x50 ml) and then dried under vacuum (0.1 torr) at 60 °C for 4 hours to
 yield 26.4 g of the titled resin.

 PSQAC3PA

 A mixture of PSDA (21.50 g, 86 mmol), Diethyl (3-bromopropyl)phosphonate

 (25.00 g, 96.5 mmol), DMF (10 ml), and a catalytic amount of tetra-n-butylammonium iodide
 (0.46 g, 1.3 mmol) was stirred at 90 °C for 40 hours. The resin was filtered off, washed with
 methanol (3x60 ml), and dried under a flow of nitrogen. The crude solid was mixed with dry
 dichloromethane (180 mL) and Trimethylsilyl iodide (30 ml, 210 mmol) was added over 10
 minutes at ca. 0 to 5oC. The reaction mixture was stirrer at ice bath for 3 hours, followed by 10
 hours at room temperature. The crude resin was filtered off, washed with dichloromethane (2x60
 ml), acetone (2x60 ml) followed by methanol (3x70 ml). The crud solid polymer was suspended
 in 10% solution of KOH (200ml) and the mixture was stirred for 2 hours. The solid was isolated
 by filtration, washed with water (2x80 ml), methanol (2 x 80 ml) and subsequently dried under
 vacuum (0.2 torr) at 60 °C for 6 hours to yield 28.9 g of the titled resin.

 Subject Inventions Listing:

 We report updated results for PCT19-153 subject invention. Here, new zwitterionic resin
 development and multi-column process for Li separation are reported. Lithium was separated
 from other mineral salts using carboxybetaine zwitterionic resin (QAC3CA) column under water
 elution in continuous operation. QAC3CA was prepared with different catalyst (KI), which
 showed the same separation results as the previously prepared one. New zwitterionic resins,
 QAC4CA, phosphoric acid-based zwitterionic resin (QAC3PA), and a quaternization of
 iminodiacetic acid resin (QACA2) were prepared and tested showing it's promising for mineral
 fractionation.
