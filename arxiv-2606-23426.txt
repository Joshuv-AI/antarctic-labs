Binary binomial equivalence via hyperplane arrangements Thanks: Supported by the FNRS Research grant T.196.23 (PDR) Thanks: The author is grateful to Michel Rigo for valuable comments and suggestions on an earlier version of this paper
Mehdi Golafshan

Abstract
. Rigo and Salimov (2015) proved that the number of binary 2 2 -binomial equivalence classes of words of length n n is the n th n^{\text{th}} cake number. We give a geometric explanation of this identity by constructing an explicit arrangement of n n planes in three-dimensional space whose chambers are naturally indexed by these equivalence classes. This arrangement is the three-dimensional member of an infinite family of hyperplane arrangements. In dimensions 1 1 , 2 2 , and 3 3 , the corresponding quotients recover abelian equivalence, a natural intermediate equivalence between abelian and 2 2 -binomial equivalence, and binary 2 2 -binomial equivalence itself. In higher dimensions, the same family realises natural refinements of 2 2 -binomial equivalence. We also determine the sizes of the resulting classes. Each size is given by a coefficient of a suitable Gaussian binomial coefficient. This yields the full class-size distribution for binary 2 2 -binomial equivalence and stabilisation results for the number of classes of any fixed cardinality.



Keywords:

k
-binomial equivalence;
hyperplane arrangement; cake numbers;
Gaussian binomial coefficient; class-size distribution.

1.
Introduction

The
binomial coefficient
of a word
\mathrm{u}
over a word
\mathrm{v}

counts the occurrences of
\mathrm{v}
as a scattered subword of
\mathrm{u}
. Building on
this notion, Rigo and Salimov
[
RS15
]
introduced the
k
-binomial
equivalences

\sim_{k}
on words over a finite alphabet: two words are

k
-binomially equivalent if every word of length at most
k
occurs as a
scattered subword of each with the same multiplicity. The relation

\sim_{1}
is abelian equivalence; larger
k
gives strictly finer partitions,
all coarser than equality. The binary fixed-length relations used in this
paper are fixed in
Section

1.1
.

The relation
\sim_{k}
has been studied from several angles. Lejeune et
al.
[
LRR20
]
studied the structure of

2
-binomial classes and proved that the number of

\sim_{2}
-classes of length-
n
words over an
m
-letter alphabet grows as

\Theta(n^{m^{2}-1})
; Chrisnata et al.
[
Chr23
]
sharpened the
asymptotic upper bound for
k\geq 3
; and Freydenberger et
al.
[
FGK15
]
gave polynomial-time algorithms
deciding
\sim_{k}
. In a different direction, Golafshan and
Rigo
[
GR25a
,
GR25b
]
extended the binomial coefficient to
two-dimensional rectangular arrays.

The motivating identity.
A basic non-trivial case for
\sim_{k}
is the binary
alphabet at
k=2
. Rigo and Salimov
[
RS15
, Lemma 4]
established

(1)

\#\bigl(\{\text{\tt a},\text{\tt b}\}^{n}\,/\,\sim_{2}\bigr)\;=\;\frac{n^{3}+5n+6}{6}\;=\;\sum_{r=0}^{3}\binom{n}{r}.

The right-hand side is the
n^{\text{th}}

cake number
(
A000125
): the
number of regions into which
n
planes in general position divide

\mathbb{R}^{3}
. More generally, the Steiner–Schläfli formula counts the
chambers of
n
hyperplanes in general position in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

\sum_{r=0}^{d}\binom{n}{r}

[
Sch01
]
; see
[
AW78
,
Sta07
]
for historical
and modern accounts.

Thus (
1
) shows that an algebraic enumeration of
equivalence classes coincides with the chamber count of a generic arrangement.
This numerical coincidence suggests a geometric model for the quotient

\{\text{\tt a},\text{\tt b}\}^{n}/{\sim_{2}}
. Richomme
[
Ric26
]
gave a geometric interpretation of certain binary

2
-binomial coefficients and described a lattice structure on the
representatives of a fixed
\sim_{2}
-class. The present point of view is
complementary: instead of studying the geometry inside one class, we construct
one hyperplane arrangement whose chambers are in bijection with the
quotient
\{\text{\tt a},\text{\tt b}\}^{n}/{\sim_{2}}
.

The work of Lejeune et al.
[
LRR20
]
gives an algebraic
model for the same quotient:
\mathcal{A}^{*}/{\sim_{2}}
identifies with the positive
submonoid of the free nilpotent group of class
2
. In the binary case this
description reduces to the complete invariant

(2)

\left(|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}},\binom{\mathrm{w}}{\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}}\right),

the binary fixed-length invariant used throughout the paper. Our construction is
complementary: it realises this binary quotient as the chamber set of a concrete
hyperplane arrangement.

1.1.
Main equivalence relations

Let

for the

for the number of occurrences

in
\mathrm{w}

and
\mathrm{v}
in

\mathcal{A}^{*}
, the binomial coefficient

\binom{\mathrm{w}}{\mathrm{v}}:=\#\{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{|\mathrm{v}|}\leq m:\mathrm{w}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathrm{w}_{i_{|\mathrm{v}|}}=\mathrm{v}\}.

We adopt the conventions
\binom{\mathrm{w}}{\varepsilon}=1
(
\varepsilon
the empty word) and
\binom{\mathrm{w}}{\mathrm{v}}=0
for

|\mathrm{v}|>|\mathrm{w}|
(see
[
Lot97
, Section 6.3]
).

Definition 1

(
[
RS15
, Definition 2]
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\mathrm{u},\mathrm{w}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. We say
\mathrm{u}

and
\mathrm{w}
are
k
-binomially equivalent
if

\binom{\mathrm{u}}{\mathrm{v}}=\binom{\mathrm{w}}{\mathrm{v}}
for every
\mathrm{v}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
with

|\mathrm{v}|\leq k
.

In this paper,
k
-binomial equivalence is denoted by
\mathrm{w}\sim_{k}\mathrm{u}
.
Since
\binom{\mathrm{u}}{\text{\tt a}}=|\mathrm{u}|_{\text{\tt a}}
for every
\text{\tt a}\in\mathcal{A}
, the relation

\sim_{1}
is abelian equivalence; and
\sim_{k+1}\subseteq\sim_{k}
for every

k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.

We focus on the binary alphabet
\mathcal{A}=\{\text{\tt a},\text{\tt b}\}
. Let
\mathrm{w},\mathrm{u}\in\{\text{\tt a},\text{\tt b}\}^{n}
; then we have

•

\mathrm{w}\sim_{1}\mathrm{u}\iff|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}=|\mathrm{u}|_{\text{\tt a}}
;

•

\mathrm{w}\sim_{2}\mathrm{u}\iff|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}=|\mathrm{u}|_{\text{\tt a}}
and

\binom{\mathrm{w}}{\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}}=\binom{\mathrm{u}}{\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}}
.

Example 1
.

Let
w_{1}=\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}\text{\tt b}\text{\tt a}
,
w_{2}=\text{\tt b}\text{\tt a}\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}
, and

w_{3}=\text{\tt a}\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}\text{\tt b}
. They are pairwise abelian
equivalent;
w_{1}\sim_{2}w_{2}
, while
w_{3}
is

2
-binomially equivalent to neither.

Set

\operatorname{strip}(\mathrm{w}):=\begin{cases}\Bigl\lfloor\binom{\mathrm{w}}{\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}}\bigm/|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}\Bigr\rfloor&\text{if }|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}>0,\\[2.0pt]
0&\text{if }|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}=0.\end{cases}

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathrm{u},\mathrm{w}\in\{\text{\tt a},\text{\tt b}\}^{*}
. We say that
\mathrm{u}
and
\mathrm{w}
are

3/2
-binomially equivalent
if

\mathrm{u}\sim_{1}\mathrm{w}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{strip}(\mathrm{u})=\operatorname{strip}(\mathrm{w}).

We denote this relation by
\mathrm{u}\sim_{3/2}\mathrm{w}
.

Remark 1
.

The notation
\sim_{3/2}
does not extend
k
-binomial equivalence to
non-integer
k
. It indicates that
\sim_{3/2}
lies strictly between the
first two integer levels of the
k
-binomial hierarchy:

\sim_{2}\subsetneq\sim_{3/2}\subsetneq\sim_{1}
.

Example 2
.

Let
w_{1}=\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}
,
w_{2}=\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}\text{\tt b}\text{\tt a}
, and

w_{3}=\text{\tt a}\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}\text{\tt b}
. They are pairwise abelian equivalent;

w_{1}\sim_{3/2}w_{2}
but
w_{1}\not\sim_{2}w_{2}
, while
w_{3}
is

3/2
-binomially equivalent to neither, because

\operatorname{strip}(w_{1})=\operatorname{strip}(w_{2})=1
and
\operatorname{strip}(w_{3})=2
.

Associate to each
\mathrm{w}\in\{\text{\tt a},\text{\tt b}\}^{n}
the Young diagram

\lambda(\mathrm{w}):=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}})
, where

is the number of

in
\mathrm{w}
; then

r_{1}\geq\cdots\geq r_{|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}}\geq 0

. Explicitly, writing

\mathrm{w}=\text{\tt b}^{i_{0}}\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}^{i_{1}}\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}^{i_{2}}\text{\tt a}\cdots\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}^{i_{|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}}},

we have
r_{j}=i_{j}+i_{j+1}+\cdots+i_{|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}}
.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathrm{w}\in\{\text{\tt a},\text{\tt b}\}^{n}
,

, and
\ell:=\min(s,|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}})
.
The

-tail
of

\mathcal{T}_{s}(\mathrm{w}):=(r_{|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}},\ldots,r_{|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}-\ell+1})
,

.

We let

denote the last entry of
\mathcal{T}_{s}(\mathrm{w})

and
|\mathcal{T}_{s}(\mathrm{w})|

. Note that

Example 3
.

Let
\mathrm{w}=\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}\text{\tt b}\text{\tt a}\text{\tt b}
, so
\lambda(\mathrm{w})=(3,1)
. Then

\mathcal{T}_{0}(\mathrm{w})=\varnothing
,
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathrm{w})=(1)

\mathcal{T}_{s}(\mathrm{w})=(1,3)

.

Let

1.3.
Relation to prior work

Binomial equivalence differs from
k
-abelian equivalence: both have abelian
equivalence as their first level, but
k
-abelian equivalence counts contiguous
factors, whereas
k
-binomial equivalence counts scattered subwords. The sizes of

k
-abelian classes were determined by Karhumäki et al.
[
KPRW17
]
; the present paper counts the binomial classes instead.

The paper draws on two classical ingredients. First, the arrangement

\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(d)}
is dual to the points
(i,i^{2},\ldots,i^{d})

for
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, and its chamber sign vectors are the positive-tope restrictions of the
alternating (or cyclic) oriented matroid
[
BLSWZ99
, Section 9.4]
. The run
description used here is therefore a word-combinatorial form of a classical
sign-variation phenomenon, familiar from total positivity
[
Kar68
]
and from Gale’s
evenness condition for cyclic polytopes
[
Gal63
]
; the novelty is not the phenomenon
but its use to model binary binomial quotients by chambers.

Second, the counting rests on a partition identity: the coefficient of
\mathrm{q}^{N}
in
the Gaussian binomial coefficient
\binom{r+c}{r}_{\!\mathrm{q}}
is the number of
partitions of
N
fitting in an
r\times c
rectangle (see Andrews
[
And98
]
or
Stanley
[
Sta12
, Section 1.8]
), with asymptotics refined by Melczer et al.
[
MPP20
]
. Here the Young-diagram encoding sends the free part of an

\approx_{s}
-class to exactly such a bounded partition, and the residual area
selects the required coefficient, again turning a classical identity into exact
class-size formulas.

2.
The arrangement
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(d)}
and chamber words

Let
d,n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.
A
hyperplane arrangement
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a finite collection

\mathcal{H}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\}
of affine hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
A
chamber
of
\mathcal{H}
is a connected component of the complement

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}
.
We write
\operatorname{Ch}(\mathcal{H})
for the set of chambers of

\mathcal{H}
.
For instance, three lines in general position in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
form an arrangement
with seven chambers: one bounded triangle and six unbounded regions; see

Figure

7
.

If each
H_{i}
is defined by the equation
L_{i}(\mathbf{x})=0
for some
affine form
L_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, the
sign vector
of a
point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}
is

\sigma(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\bigl(\sgn(L_{1}(\mathbf{x})),\,\ldots,\,\sgn(L_{n}(\mathbf{x}))\bigr)\in\{+,-\}^{n}.

Two points of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}
lie in the same
chamber iff they have the same sign vector; consequently, the
chambers of
\mathcal{H}
are in bijection with the sign vectors in the
image of
\sigma
.

An arrangement
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be in
general
position
, or
generic
, if, for every
r\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
, any
r

distinct hyperplanes of
\mathcal{H}
intersect in an affine subspace of
codimension
r
, and any
d+1
distinct hyperplanes of
\mathcal{H}
have
empty intersection.

We will need the following classical formula counting the chambers of a
generic arrangement.

Theorem 1

(Steiner
[
Ste26
]
; Schläfli
[
Sch01
]
)
.

For any arrangement
\mathcal{H}
of
n
hyperplanes in general position in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\#\operatorname{Ch}(\mathcal{H})\;=\;\sum_{r=0}^{d}\binom{n}{r}.

Modern expositions include Zaslavsky
[
Zas75
]
and
Stanley
[
Sta12
, Section 3.11]
; see also
[
Sta07
]
.

2.1.
The arrangement
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(d)}

We now give, for
d,n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, a concrete arrangement
in general position. For
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, let

H_{i}^{(d)}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the affine hyperplane defined below,
and set
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(d)}=\{H_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,H_{n}^{(d)}\}
:

(3)

H_{i}^{(d)}\colon\;1+ix_{1}+i^{2}x_{2}+\cdots+i^{d}x_{d}=0.

Proposition 1
.

The arrangement
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(d)}
is in general position.

Proof.

For each
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define the
polynomial
P_{\mathbf{x}}(t)=1+x_{1}t+x_{2}t^{2}+\cdots+x_{d}t^{d}
.
By (
3
), for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every

H_{i}^{(d)}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(d)}
,

\mathbf{x}\in H_{i}^{(d)}
iff
P_{\mathbf{x}}(i)=0
.
Since
P_{\mathbf{x}}(0)=1
and
\deg P_{\mathbf{x}}\leq d
, the polynomial

P_{\mathbf{x}}
is non-zero and has at most
d
roots; hence no point of

\mathbb{R}^{d}
lies on more than
d
of the hyperplanes
H_{i}^{(d)}
, and any

d+1
of them have empty intersection.

Fix
r\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
and
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n
. The
intersection
\bigcap_{s=1}^{r}H_{i_{s}}^{(d)}
is the solution set in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the linear system

\sum_{q=1}^{d}i_{s}^{q}\,x_{q}=-1
for
s\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
.
An
r\times r
minor of its coefficient matrix is a non-zero
Vandermonde-type determinant; hence the coefficient matrix has full rank
r
,
and the solution set is an affine subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of codimension
r
.

2.2.
Chamber sign words and runs

A
run
in a word
\mathrm{w}\in\{+,-\}^{*}
is a maximal block of consecutive
equal letters; equivalently, the number of runs of
\mathrm{w}
is one more than the
number of positions at which
\mathrm{w}
changes letter. For instance, the word

\mathord{+}\mathord{+}\mathord{-}\mathord{-}\mathord{+}
has three runs.

Throughout this subsection, sign words are read in the increasing order

H_{1}^{(d)},\ldots,H_{n}^{(d)}
of the hyperplanes. Since
P_{\mathbf{x}}(0)>0
,
we augment every chamber sign word
\mathbf{u}\in\{+,-\}^{n}
with a
fixed
+
at position
0
; this is why the run condition below is stated for
the augmented word
+\mathbf{u}
. To translate sign words into binary words,
we use
-\mapsto\text{\tt a}
and
+\mapsto\text{\tt b}
, the opposite choice merely
exchanging
a
and
b
.

For a chamber
C
of
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{(d)}
, recall the polynomial

P_{\mathbf{x}}
from the proof of
Proposition

1
and define the

sign word
of
C
by

\sigma(C)=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}\in\{+,-\}^{n},\qquad\sigma_{i}=\sgn P_{\mathbf{x}}(i),

for any
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in C
. Since

\mathbf{x}\in H_{i}^{(d)}
iff
P_{\mathbf{x}}(i)=0
and
C
misses every

H_{i}^{(d)}
, the value
P_{\mathbf{x}}(i)
is non-zero on
C
; being
continuous in
\mathbf{x}
, it is of constant sign on the connected set
C
, so

\sigma(C)
is well defined.

Proposition 2
.

The map

5.

Having counted the equivalence classes in the previous sections, we now study

\approx_{s}

\sim_{2}

\binom{c}{d}_{\mathrm{q}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-\mathrm{q}^{c-d+i}}{1-\mathrm{q}^{i}},

5.4.
Stabilisation of
s

Singleton

-classes were studied from a formal-language viewpoint by
Lejeune et al.
[

; we count them. By

Corollary

1
,
\approx_{s}
is the equality relation when

n\leq s+3
, where the count is immediate; the remaining case

reduces to the following fact.

Lemma 4
.

The value
A_{r,c}(u)
equals
1
for all
u\in\llbracket 0,rc\rrbracket
when

\min(r,c)\leq 1
, and only for
u\in\{0,1,rc-1,rc\}
when
\min(r,c)\geq 2
.

Proof.

The number
A_{r,c}(u)
counts the partitions of
u
contained in the

r\times c
rectangle. If
r=0
or
c=0
, only the empty partition fits,
so only
u=0
occurs. If
r=1
, the partitions
(u)
,
u\in\llbracket 0,c\rrbracket
,
give one partition for each area; the case
c=1
is symmetric.

Assume now that
r,c\geq 2
. Only
\emptyset
and
(1)
have areas
0

and
1
, respectively. By the symmetry of
A_{r,c}

(
Lemma

3
), the same holds for the complementary areas

rc
and
rc-1
. For
u\in\llbracket 2,rc-2\rrbracket
, symmetry lets us assume

u\leq rc/2
. Since the two partitions
(2)
and
(1,1)
both fit in the
rectangle,
A_{r,c}(2)\geq 2
; unimodality (
Lemma

3
)
then gives
A_{r,c}(u)\geq 2
. Hence
A_{r,c}(u)=1
exactly for

u\in\{0,1,rc-1,rc\}
.
∎

For
s,n\in\mathbb{N}
, put

S_{s}(n):=\#\bigl\{[\mathrm{w}]_{s}\in\{\text{\tt a},\text{\tt b}\}^{n}/\approx_{s}:\#[\mathrm{w}]_{s}=1\bigr\},

and let
\mathcal{S}_{s}(x):=\sum_{n\geq 0}S_{s}(n)\,x^{n}
be its generating function.

Theorem 6
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\mathcal{S}_{s}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\frac{x^{j}}{(1-x)^{j+1}}+\frac{x^{s+1}(1+x)(1+2x^{2})}{(1-x)^{s+2}}.

Proof.

By
Theorem

3
and
Lemma

4
,
a class is a singleton iff its size

A_{|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}-\ell,\,|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt b}}-\theta}(R_{s}(\mathrm{w}))
equals
1
, where

\ell=\min(s,|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}})
.

For
|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}\leq s
we have
\ell=|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}
, so the first index
is
0
and every such class is a singleton, contributing

\sum_{j=0}^{s}\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{n}{j}\,x^{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\frac{x^{j}}{(1-x)^{j+1}}.

For
|\mathrm{w}|_{\text{\tt a}}>s
we have
\ell=s
, and by
Lemma

4

a class is a singleton iff its residual area is a unit coefficient of the
residual rectangle’s Gaussian binomial—every area when a side of the
rectangle is at most
1
, and the four extreme areas otherwise. The
s
-tail is a weakly increasing tuple of non-negative integers, empty when

s=0
; for
s=0
it contributes the factor

1
, while for
s\geq 1
exactly
\binom{\theta+s-1}{s-1}
of these have
last entry
\theta
, so they contribute

\sum_{\theta\geq 0}\binom{\theta+s-1}{s-1}x^{\theta}=1/(1-x)^{s}
. Either way
the factor is
1/(1-x)^{s}
.
Weighting each residual rectangle (which has at least one row) by
x
raised
to the sum of its two side lengths and by its number of unit coefficients gives

x(1+x)(1+2x^{2})/(1-x)^{2}
. Since
n
equals
s
plus
\theta
plus
that sum, this case contributes

x^{s}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-x)^{s}}\cdot\frac{x(1+x)(1+2x^{2})}{(1-x)^{2}}=\frac{x^{s+1}(1+x)(1+2x^{2})}{(1-x)^{s+2}}.

Summing the two cases gives the stated generating function.
∎

Extracting coefficients gives the closed form

S_{s}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\binom{n}{j}+\binom{n}{s+1}+\binom{n-1}{s+1}+2\binom{n-2}{s+1}+2\binom{n-3}{s+1}.

In particular,
S_{s}(n)=2^{n}
for
n\leq s+3
, since then
\approx_{s}
is
the equality relation by
Corollary

1
.
