 ABSTRACT

 In this work the isotopic ratios and weight percentages of 238Pu, 239Pu, 240Pu, 241Pu and 242Pu were
determined using Isotope Dilution Mass Spectrometry. Two samples of C136A and C138A were sent to
the Analytical Research laboratories by the National Nuclear Security Administration Nuclear Reference
Material Program. The four samples were dissolved in concentrated hydrochloric acid. From the parent
solutions, eight aliquots were taken, diluted, spiked with 244Pu and submitted to separations. After the
samples were separated, they were analyzed using Multicollector Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass
Spectrometry. Along with the samples, two C126A Quality Control Samples were analyzed each day.
Mass bias standards were also analyzed at the beginning, during and at the end of the analysis. GUM
Workbench Pro software Version 2.4.1.406 was used to calculate the isotopic ratios relative to 239Pu, and
total Pu mass and the correlated expanded uncertainties.

 INTRODUCTION
 Samples of Certified Reference Materials (CRMs) C136A and C138A (Plutonium Isotopic Standards)
were provided by the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) Nuclear Reference Material
Program (NRMP) to the Analytical Research Laboratory (ARL) at the Materials and Fuels Complex at
Idaho National Laboratory (INL). Two independent samples of each CRM were dissolved in concentrated
hydrochloric acid. From each parent solution, two aliquots were taken, diluted, spiked with 244Pu and
submitted to separations. Separations were required to ensure the samples were elementally pure to
eliminate potential mass interferences with uranium and americium. After the samples were separated,
they were analyzed using Multicollector Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (MC-ICP-MS).
Along with the samples, two C126A Quality Control Samples were prepared and analyzed each day.
Mass bias standards were also analyzed at the beginning, during and at the end of the analysis. GUM
Workbench Pro software Version 2.4.1.406 was used to calculate the isotopic ratios relative to 239.
weight percentages of each of the Pu isotopes and total Pu weight in each sample and the expanded
uncertainties.
 PHYSICAL MEASUREMENTS
 All sample and solutions masses were measured using calibrated analytical balances. Balances in the
ARL are calibrated annually by the Standards and Calibration Laboratory at INL and they are assigned an
uncertainty for their measurement range. Daily checks are performed on these balances using National
Institute of Standards and Technology traceable external calibration weights, also calibrated by the
Standards and Calibration Laboratory at INL to confirm the balance’s functionality and accuracy before
use. Before use, balances calibrations are verified using several NIST traceable mass standards. Results
from are recorded on a control chart kept with each balance so that long term bias can be evaluated. All
sample dilutions were prepared gravimetrically on a mass-to-mass basis using NIST-traceable calibrated
balances. All balances used in this work were four decimal place balances with assigned uncertainties of
0.5 mg.

 SAMPLE PREPARATION
 Two C136A vials and two C138A vials containing approximately one milligram of material each
were received from NNSA NRMP, and Analytical Log Number (AL #) were assigned. The vials were
transferred to a radiological glovebox, transferred to perfluoroalkyl bottles by rinsing the original vials
with 6 M HCl. The weight of the empty bottles and the bottles with the solution were recorded, and the
solution weights were obtained by subtraction. The bottles were taken out the glovebox and stored prior
further preparation. All the identification and dissolution information are shown in Table 1.

 Samples were prepared for Isotope Dilution Mass Spectrometer IDMS by diluting a sample of the
parent solution and then quantitatively mixing it with an aliquot of a previously characterized spike
solution. Inside a radiological hood, the parent solutions were diluted up to a factor of 20 by taking 0.5
mL aliquots and diluting up to 10 mL with 6 M HNO3. Next, an aliquot of the parent solution was spiked.
Eight replicate spiked solutions were prepared by adding 3 mL of 8 M HNO3, followed by 1 mL aliquot
of the 1:20 samples and a 1 mL aliquot of the spike. The spike consists mainly of Pu-244 (97.8683 wt. %)
and has a total Pu concentration of 0.7975 ug g-1 ± 0.0010. All dilutions and aliquots were performed
gravimetrically. The spiking solution is calibrated yearly against CRM C126A. The minor plutonium
isotopic ratios in this spike are: 238/244 = 0.001035 ± 0.000270, 239/244 = 0.000437 ± 0.000009,
240/244 = 0.006885 ± 0.000009, 241/244 = 0.000133 ± 0.000007, 242/244 = 0.013552 ± 0.000011.
Along with the samples, four quality control samples (QC) were also prepared. Two of the QCs consisted
of CRM C126a (Plutonium metal, 93.9110% 239Pu) solution and 2 aliquots of SNL-B1 (93.9% 239Pu).
Prior to being spiked with 244Pu, SNL-B1 was diluted by a factor of 3. SNL B1 is prepared in-house and
contains approximately 93.9% 239Pu, along with lesser amounts of the minor isotopes of Pu. All dilution
weight sheets are shown in Appendix A.
 Plutonium was purified from the sample matrix using a TEVA resin. Prior to loading, samples were
treated with 1 mL of 1 M ascorbic acid to reduce Pu(VI) to Pu(IV), the oxidation state required for
retention on the TEVA resin. After a brief (5-10 min) reaction period, concentrated nitric acid was added
to adjust the solutions to approximately 3.5 M HNO₃ for resin loading. Samples were applied to
conditioned TEVA cartridges (2 mL bed volume), and matrix elements and isobaric interferences were
removed by rinsing the resin with 3 M HNO₃. Plutonium was eluted using 0.05 M HNO₃ containing 0.05
M HF and 0.02 M TiCl₃. The HF minimized hydrolysis during elution, and the TiCl₃ maintained Pu in the
Pu(III) oxidation state to prevent re-retention on the resin and ensure quantitative recovery. The
plutonium fractions were evaporated to dryness and reconstituted in 5 mL of 5% HNO₃ for MC-ICP-MS
analysis.

 MC-ICP-MS ANALYSIS
 Samples were analyzed on a Nu Plasma 3 MC-ICP-MS. Once the plasma started, the instrument was
allowed to warm up approximately 40 minutes. Simultaneously, a pre-amplifier gain calibration was
performed. Prior to analysis, the nebulizer gas flow and the peak alignment are optimized using a C128
solution (NBL), ensuring optimal instrumental response during analysis. After the instrument
optimization, the C128 solution was measured in the beginning, middle and at the end of the analysis of
each batch to determine the instrumental mass bias correction factor, which was applied to the isotopic
ratios. Immediately after the first mass bias, the samples and quality control sample were analyzed. The
isotopic ratios for 238Pu/239Pu, 240Pu/239Pu, 241Pu/239Pu,242Pu/239Pu and 244Pu/239Pu were measured in two
cycles of twenty-five measurements, with a total of fifty measurements per sample. The diluted samples
were analyzed throughout four days, with two samples plus the QCs analyzed on each day as described in
Table 2. The analysis dates are provided since no decay correction was performed on the samples results.

 The average and standard deviation of the fifty measurements were calculated by the instrument
software. The isotopic ratios, isotope weight percentages and uncertainties propagations were calculated
using GUM Workbench Pro software Version 2.4.1.406. Since the 244Pu standard had other plutonium
isotopes as minor constituents, the isotopic ratios had to be corrected. The equations used in GUM for
both the isotopic ratios and weight percentages are shown in Appendix B.
The results from the quality control samples were compared to control charts containing 2-sigma and 3-
sigma levels to confirm the quality of the data. If the results for the Pu total, 239Pu, and 240Pu are within
the 2-sigma limit, the QCs passed. 2-sigma and 3-sigma levels have been determined using at minimum
30 data points.

MS. All equipment was functional, properly calibrated and verified; all standards used are traceable;
pertinent procedures were followed; total expanded uncertainties were calculated using GUM. Results are
also provided as an Excel Spreadsheet in a separate file.
