Open-source Tip-and-Cue for Change-Detection Tasking

 Oak Ridge National Laboratory
  Matt McCarthy, PhD (mccarthymj@ornl.gov), David Hughes, PhD (hughesdc@ornl.glv), and
 Heidi Tubbs (tubbshc@ornl.gov)

Need: Currently, no tool exists to detect daily change across the globe, let alone to do so cost-
and resource-efficiently. The proposed tool will use open-source data to detect change in near-
real-time to identify targets of interest for tasking, providing a unique capability for constant
global situational awareness.

Goal: Open-source satellite data can be used to detect a variety of surface changes at
moderate resolution that could then prompt tasking of high-resolution commercial observations.
With daily global coverage, such open-source data could help prompt efforts to focus on the
reduced locations. Additionally, many relevant events (e.g., base construction, areas of conflict,
etc.) are publicized via open-web articles that could be scraped continuously and used similarly
to cue taskings when associated with locations. We intend to build a tool that evaluates daily
global event change to tip-and-cue tasking of high-resolution and high-spectral commercial
satellite data. Such a tool would be useful to save on costly taskings of non-events while
leveraging global imagery and articles to comprehensively detect changes of interest for
targeted evaluation.

Proposed: This approach refuses large areas from commercial-imagery tasking consideration
through filtering based on an agnostic change detection approach. Our approach uses a
combination of the large corpus of available open-source satellite-based sensors with various
spectral and spatial capabilities to identify areas that should receive more focus. A
complimentary webscraping capability retrieves public news reports and, by leveraging large
language models (LLMs), identifies potential areas of defined change. Web-scraped news
reports may also provide context for change observed in open-source satellite data, potentially
informing tasking priority. The areas identified by satellite and/or webscrape as relevant
indications of change will be identified for tasking.

Impact: This new capability would exist at the origin of change detection for rapid situational
awareness and reduce costs by filtering out areas of no relevant change. Further, the
webscrape component improves potential issues with temporal resolution informing when
events on the ground may signal imagery collection needs and learning when imagery may not
have been captured or obscured.

Risks: Harmonizing a definition of change across a multitude of sensors and platforms (e.g.,
multispectral and SAR) having different spectral and spatial characteristics will be challenging
and take time. Lack of ground-truth is always a risk in remotely sensed capabilities, and denied
areas will remain an issue. Webscraping will require an ontology from the sponsor describing
changes of interest and development of a database of key words to focus on and search.

Effort: First iteration would include a feasibility study for both open-source data access, rapid
ingestion and processing, and change mapping adapting to the constellation of sensors with
various attributes, combined with webscrape development and tuning for changes of interest.
Such an effort would be 8-12 months for 2.25 FTEs.

Success: Mid-term is a feasibility study to show: (1) what we can access for imagery, (2) can
we execute performant change detection with a reduced set of data or what will limit us in
spectral/spatial characteristics, and (3) can we baseline across sensors for change? A final
exam would be to take those sensors deemed appropriate and show that we can pull relevant
imagery in time and space, conduct change adaptive to the various available sensors, baseline
against the disparate sensors, and produce a validated “change is here and not there” map.
Expanding across this would be to expand adoption to other sensor modes beyond passive
sensing, conduct this process automated, and determine valid metrics for relevant change
detection.
