Hamiltonian semisprays on Lie algebroids
Misael Avendaño Camacho, Jhonny Kama Mamani, Eduardo Velasco Barreras

Abstract
We study the existence of Hamiltonian semisprays on Lie algebroids. This work is motivated by a problem studied by Vaisman for tangent bundles, and we extend this question to the setting of arbitrary Lie algebroids and provide a general solution. More precisely, given a Lie algebroid and a regular Lagrangian, we construct a family of Poisson brackets on the algebroid such that the Hamiltonian vector field associated with the corresponding energy function is a semispray. Our approach is based on the symplectic geometry of the prolongation of a Lie algebroid and a cohomological analysis of its vertical subbundle. The results provide a geometric framework for second-order Hamiltonian dynamics on Lie algebroids, extending some known facts in the classical tangent bundle case and revealing new interactions between Poisson geometry and algebroid structures.



1
Introduction

The relationship between Hamiltonian dynamics and second-order differential equations is a classical topic in geometric mechanics,
[
1
,
18
,
19
,
27
]
.
In particular, the problem of determining whether Hamiltonian vector fields can be of second order has been studied in the context of tangent bundles.
In the seminal work
[
30
]
, Vaisman showed that, in general, Poisson structures on the tangent bundle may not admit second-order differential equations as Hamiltonian, and provided both counterexamples and specific constructions for which this property holds.

This paper aims to extend this problem to the broader setting of Lie algebroids. Given that Lie algebroids provide a natural framework for generalizing geometric mechanics beyond tangent bundles
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
21
,
31
]
, it is natural to ask whether Vaisman’s problem admits a solution in this setting. More precisely, we address the following question: given a Lie algebroid, does there exist a Poisson structure admitting semisprays as Hamiltonian vector fields?

While Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalisms on Lie algebroids are well established
[
9
,
11
,
21
]
, the existence of Poisson brackets with this specific dynamical property has not, to our knowledge, been addressed in this generality. The extension of Vaisman’s results from the tangent bundle to general Lie algebroids is nontrivial,
since the tangent bundle of a Lie algebroid lacks several canonical structures present in a second tangent bundle,
and a general anchor and bracket introduce further difficulties.

Our main result shows that, for any regular Lagrangian on a Lie algebroid, one can construct a family of Poisson brackets such that the Hamiltonian vector field of the associated energy function is a semispray (Theorem
3.1
).

A key novelty of our approach lies in the use of the prolongation of a Lie algebroid
[
5
,
11
,
20
,
21
]
,
which allows us to overcome intrinsic obstructions present on general Lie algebroids, such as the absence of symplectic structures.
This constitutes the main difference between the work of Vaisman on the tangent bundle and our approach to the same problem on Lie algebroids.
In his paper, Vaisman shows that a certain subfamily of the
\mathbb{V}
-Lagrangian symplectic forms admits Hamiltonian second-order differential equations.

However, the arguments used by Vaisman cannot be directly extended to a general Lie algebroid.
Indeed, dimension considerations already impose restrictions: if the total space of a Lie algebroid has odd dimension, then it does not admit any symplectic structure. Already in the case of even dimension,
there is no canonical construction of a symplectic structure with a Hamiltonian semispray, in contrast with the tangent bundle case, where such a construction arises naturally.
For these reasons, we work on the prolongation of a Lie algebroid. In this setting, regular Lagrangians induce symplectic structures vanishing on the vertical subbundle such that its energy has for Hamiltonian section a second-order differential equation. In this paper, we enlarge and characterize the class of symplectic sections on the prolongation of a Lie algebroid in Propositions
7.2
and
7.3
, analogously to what Vaisman does in the tangent bundle. The final step in our construction is to prove that these symplectic sections on the prolongations induce Poisson brackets on the Lie algebroid with the properties established in Theorem
3.1
.

A central ingredient in our analysis is a cohomological study of vertical subbundles
arising from the prolongation. In particular, we prove that the cohomology associated
with the vertical component of the Lie algebroid differential vanishes in positive degrees.
This result is crucial for constructing global primitives of certain differential forms
and for obtaining a structural description of symplectic sections adapted to the vertical
subbundle. Although similar vanishing results can be obtained locally in coordinates, as in the
classical case considered by Vaisman, our approach provides a global and intrinsic
construction of the corresponding homotopy operator (Section
4
). This is achieved by exploiting
the geometry of pullback bundles and representations of Lie algebroids, leading to a
coordinate-free proof that may be of independent interest.

As a consequence of this cohomological analysis, we obtain a characterization of a family
of symplectic sections on the prolongation that vanish on vertical directions. This
description allows us to construct a family of Poisson brackets explicitly on the Lie
algebroid for which some Hamiltonian dynamics are described by semisprays on the Lie algebroid.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the basic notions on Lie
algebroids and semisprays. Section
3
contains the statement of the main result.
Section
4
is devoted to the cohomological analysis of vertical subbundles and the
construction of a homotopy operator, which plays a fundamental role in the sequel.
In Section
5
, we review the prolongation of a Lie algebroid and some geometric structures which are relevant for the purposes of this paper.
Section
6
deals with symplectic sections on the prolongation associated with regular Lagrangians. In Section
7
we classify symplectic sections vanishing in the vertical subbundle. And finally, in Section
8
, we complete the proof of the main theorem by constructing the relevant Poisson brackets.
We also provide an interesting example at the end of this section.

2
Preliminary Notions

Here, we recall the notions of Lie algebroids and semisprays.
For a comprehensive treatment of Lie algebroids, see
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie algebroid

A
over a smooth manifold
M
is a vector bundle
p:A\rightarrow M
equipped with an
\mathbb{R}
-Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(A)
on the
C^{\infty}(M)
-module of sections
\Gamma(A)
and a vector bundle homomorphism
\rho:A\rightarrow TM
, called anchor map, satisfying the Leibniz rule

[\sigma,f\eta]=f[\sigma,\eta]+(\mathcal{L}_{\rho(\sigma)}f)\eta,

for all
\sigma,\eta\in\Gamma(A)
,
f\in C^{\infty}(M).

We also denote by
\rho:\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
the induced
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear mapping on sections. Moreover, an immediate consequence of this definition is that the anchor map
\rho
is a Lie algebra homomorphism:

\rho([\sigma,\eta])=[\rho(\sigma),\rho(\eta)],\ \ \ \ \sigma,\eta\in\Gamma(A).

The image
\rho(A)=\mathcal{D}
of the anchor map is a smooth generalized distribution on
M
which is integrable in the sense of Stefan–Sussmann
[
28
,
29
]
.
The integral submanifolds of
\mathcal{D}
define a singular foliation of
M
called the
characteristic foliation
of
A
.

By fixing local coordinates
(U,x^{1},\ldots x^{n})
on
M
and a local basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
of sections
\Gamma(U)
we obtain local coordinates of
A
, called
adapted coordinates
:

A\ni a\mapsto(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n},y^{1},\ldots,y^{r}),

(2.1)

where
a=y^{j}\cdot e_{j}(x).
The smooth functions
\rho^{i}_{j}
,
C^{k}_{ij}
on
C^{\infty}(U)
defined by

\rho(e_{j})=\rho^{i}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}},\ \ \ \ [e_{i},e_{j}]=C^{k}_{ij}e_{k}

(2.2)

are called the
structure functions
of the Lie algebroid and satisfy the so-called
structure equations
of the Lie algebroid:

\rho^{i}_{j}\frac{\partial\rho^{k}_{l}}{\partial x^{i}}-\rho^{i}_{l}\frac{\partial\rho^{k}_{j}}{\partial x^{i}}=\rho_{i}^{k}C^{i}_{jl},\qquad\sum_{\mathrm{cyclic}(j,l,s)}\left(\rho^{i}_{j}\frac{\partial C^{k}_{ls}}{\partial x^{i}}+C^{t}_{ls}C^{k}_{jt}\right)=0

(2.3)

The simplest example of a Lie algebroid is the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold.

Example 2.2
.

For any manifold
M
, the tangent bundle
\tau_{M}:TM\rightarrow M
is a Lie algebroid with the standard Lie bracket of vector fields and anchor given by the identity map on
TM
.

Another example of Lie algebroids arises in the cotangent bundle of a
Poisson manifold

[
8
]
.

A
Poisson manifold
is a smooth manifold
M
equipped with a bivector field

\Pi\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(M)
satisfying the Jacobi identity

[\Pi,\Pi]_{\mathrm{SCH}}=0,

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{SCH}}
denotes the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket of multivector
fields on
M

[
15
]
.

Equivalently, a Poisson structure on
M
can be described by a
Poisson bracket
,
a bilinear operation
\{\cdot,\cdot\}:C^{\infty}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)

making
C^{\infty}(M)
into a Lie algebra and satisfying the Leibniz rule

\{f,gh\}=\{f,g\}h+g\{f,h\},\qquad\forall\,f,g,h\in C^{\infty}(M).

The correspondence between both descriptions is given by

\{f,g\}=\Pi(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g),\qquad\forall\,f,g\in C^{\infty}(M).

Example 2.3
.

Let
(M,\Pi)
be a Poisson manifold. The cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
carries a natural
Lie algebroid structure, with anchor map given by the vector bundle morphism

\Pi^{\sharp}:T^{*}M\rightarrow TM,\qquad\Pi^{\sharp}(\omega):=\mathrm{i}_{\omega}\Pi

and Lie bracket on
\Omega^{1}(M)
given by

[\sigma,\eta]_{\Pi}:=\mathcal{L}_{\Pi^{\sharp}(\sigma)}\eta-\mathcal{L}_{\Pi^{\sharp}(\eta)}\sigma-\mathrm{d}(\Pi(\sigma,\eta)),

for all
\sigma,\eta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. This Lie algebroid over
M
is called the

cotangent Lie algebroid
of
(M,\Pi)
.

Example 2.4
.

An involutive distribution
\mathcal{D}\subset TM
is a Lie algebroid with the inclusion
\mathcal{D}\hookrightarrow TM
as anchor map and the usual bracket of vector fields as Lie bracket.
More generally, if
A
is a given Lie algebroid and
V\subset A
is a subbundle such that
\Gamma(V)
is closed under the bracket on
A
,
then
V
is itself a Lie algebroid with anchor and bracket obtained from
A
by simple restriction.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}\ni\xi\mapsto\xi_{M}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
be a (right) action of a real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on the manifold
M
.
Its associated Lie algebroid consists of the trivial vector bundle
\mathrm{pr}_{1}:M\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow M
,
with anchor map
\rho:M\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow TM
defined by

\rho(m,\xi):=\xi_{M}(m).

By identifying the sections of
\mathrm{pr}_{1}
with the set of
\mathfrak{g}
-valued functions
v:M\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
,
a Lie bracket is defined by

[v,w](m):=[v(m),w(m)]+\langle\mathrm{d}w,(v(m))_{M}\rangle(m)-\langle\mathrm{d}v,(w(m))_{M}\rangle(m).

Definition 2.6
.

A vector field
\mathcal{V}\in\mathfrak{X}(A)
on a Lie algebroid
(p:A\rightarrow M,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
is called a
semispray
if

Tp\circ\mathcal{V}=\rho,

(2.4)

where
Tp:TA\rightarrow TM
is the tangent map of the projection.
A semispray
\mathcal{V}\in\mathfrak{X}(A)
is said to be a
spray

if the vector field
\mathcal{V}
satisfies the homogeneity condition

m^{*}_{k}\mathcal{V}=k\mathcal{V},\ \ \ \ \text{for }k>0,

(2.5)

where
m_{k}:A\rightarrow A
,
m_{k}(a):=ka
, is the dilation map.

To our knowledge, this notion was first introduced in
[
31
, Definition 2.2]
, and is closely related to

second-order differential equations
(SODEs) on Lie algebroids, which we recall in Section
5
.

In adapted coordinates
(U,x,y)
for
A
, a semispray
\mathcal{V}
has the form

\mathcal{V}(x,y)=y^{j}\rho^{i}_{j}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}+\nu^{k}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{k}},

for some local functions
\nu^{k}(x,y)\in C^{\infty}(A)
. If
\mathcal{V}(x,y)
is a spray, then
\nu^{k}(x,y)=\nu^{k}_{ln}(x)y^{l}y^{n}
. Note that every spray
\mathcal{V}
vanishes at the zero section of the vector bundle
p:A\rightarrow M
, that is,
\mathcal{V}(x,0)=0
.

Furthermore, the homogeneity condition (
2.5
) for a spray
\mathcal{V}
can be expressed in terms of the Liouville (Euler) vector field
\mathbf{E}\in\mathfrak{X}(A)
, which locally has the form

\mathbf{E}(x,y)=y^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{k}}.

Indeed, a semispray
\mathcal{V}\in\mathfrak{X}(A)
is a spray if and only if

[\mathbf{E},\mathcal{V}]=\mathcal{V}.

Let
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
be a smooth function on
A
. The
Legendre transform

\mathscr{L}:A\rightarrow A^{*}
induced by the
Lagrangian
function
L
is the map

A\ni a\mapsto\mathscr{L}(a)\in A^{*}

given by

\langle\mathscr{L}(a),b\rangle:=\left.\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}L(a+tb)\right)\right|_{t=0},

for all
b\in A
such that
p(b)=p(a).
The Lagrangian
L
is called
regular
if the Legendre transform is a diffeomorphism. In adapted coordinates, the Lagrangian is regular if and only if the matrix

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}\right)

is non-singular.
The
energy function

E_{L}\in C^{\infty}(A)
associated with
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
is given by

E_{L}:=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{E}}L-L,

(2.6)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{E}}
denotes the Lie derivative with respect to the Liouville (Euler) vector field.

Example 2.7
.

A Riemannian metric on a Lie algebroid
A
is a section
g\in\Gamma(A^{*}\otimes A^{*})
that is symmetric and positive definite.
The corresponding Lagrangian
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
is the fiberwise quadratic function
L(a):=g(a,a)
.
In adapted coordinates, we have
L=\tfrac{1}{2}g_{ij}y^{i}y^{j}
, where
g_{ij}:=g(e_{i},e_{j})
.
Thus,
\left(\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}\right)=(g_{ij})
, which is non-singular since
g
is positive definite.
Therefore, the Lagrangian
L
is regular.
Furthermore, since
L
is quadratic, we have
E_{L}=L
.
The same occurs if
g
is a pseudo-Riemannian metric.

3
Main Theorem

Now we present our main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

On a Lie algebroid
(p:A\rightarrow M,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
, let
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
be a regular Lagrangian
with associated energy function
E_{L}
.
Then, there exists a family of Poisson brackets
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
on
A
with the property that
for every function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
the Hamiltonian vector field of the function
E_{L}+p^{*}f
is a semispray.

One can deduce from Theorem
3.1
that Hamiltonian semisprays arise naturally
from the interaction between the Lie algebroid structure and regular Lagrangians,
giving rise to an intrinsic mechanism for generating second-order Hamiltonian dynamics in the Lie algebroid setting.

The rest of this paper is devoted to the proof of this theorem.
Before doing so, we provide a coordinate description of the family of Poisson brackets,
which makes the verification of their properties straightforward.

First, fix a local adapted coordinate system
(U,x,y)=(U,x^{1},\ldots,x^{n},y^{1}\ldots,y^{r})
on
A

and consider the structure functions
\rho_{j}^{i},

C_{ij}^{k}
of the Lie algebroid
A
given by (
2.2
).
Then, define the set of local functions
{M_{ij}}
,
M^{ij}
, and
N_{ij}
on
A
by

M_{ij}:=\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}},\ \ \ \ M_{ik}M^{kj}=\delta^{i}_{j},\quad\text{and}\quad N_{ij}:=\rho_{i}^{k}\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial x^{k}\partial y^{j}}-\rho_{j}^{k}\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial x^{k}\partial y^{i}}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial y^{k}}C^{k}_{ij}+\Theta_{ij}.

where
\Theta_{ij}
is any set of functions on
M
satisfying

\Theta_{ij}(x)=-\Theta_{ji}(x),\qquad\sum_{\mathrm{cyclic}(i,j,k)}\rho^{r}_{k}\frac{\partial\Theta_{ij}}{\partial x^{r}}-C^{r}_{jk}\Theta_{ri}=0.

(3.1)

Now, consider the Poisson bracket on
A
locally defined by the relations

\{x^{i},x^{j}\}=0,\ \ \ \ \{x^{i},y^{k}\}=-\rho^{i}_{r}M^{rk},\ \ \ \{y^{k},y^{l}\}=-M^{kr}N_{rs}M^{sl}.

(3.2)

The Hamiltonian vector field of the function

G(x,y)=E_{L}(x,y)+f(x)=\frac{\partial L}{\partial y^{k}}y^{k}-L(x,y)+f(x)

is the vector field

\mathcal{V}(x,y)=y^{a}\rho^{i}_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}-M^{sk}\left(\rho^{i}_{k}\frac{\partial G}{\partial x^{i}}+N_{jk}y^{j}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{s}},

which is a semispray on
A
.

In other words, given a regular Lagrangian
L
, the family of Poisson brackets in Theorem
3.1
is given by (
3.2
)
and is such that the Hamiltonian vector field of the function
G(x,y)
is a semispray.
This family is parameterized by the set of functions
\Theta_{ij}
satisfying the conditions in (
3.1
),
which has the following geometric interpretation:
the functions
\Theta_{ij}
are the local coefficients of a closed section
\theta\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}A^{*})
.
Indeed, as a consequence of Proposition
7.3
, Corollary
7.5
and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain that,
for a given regular Lagrangian
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
, the family of Poisson brackets in Theorem in
3.1

can be naturally identified with the set of closed sections
\theta\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}A^{*})
;
a correspondece that will be developed geometrically in Section 7.

We also notice that this procedure allows us to construct Hamiltonian sprays:
for the regular Lagrangian
L
given by the quadratic form associated with any pseudo-Riemannian metric on the Lie algebroid,
setting
\Theta_{ij}=0
and
f=0
implies that
\mathcal{V}
is a spray
(see Example
6.8
for more details).

The proof of the previous theorem follows from the statements:

(i)

The set of semisprays on the Lie algebroid
(p:A\rightarrow M,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)

are in one-to-one correspondence with the so-called
second-order differential equations
,
which are sections of the prolongation of the Lie algebroid
A
.
Indeed, the anchor map of the prolongation maps every of second-order differential equation into a semispray on
A
.

(ii)

One can characterize a family of
symplectic sections
on the prolongation of the Lie algebroid
(p:A\rightarrow M,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)

with the property that at least one of its Hamiltonian sections is a second-order differential equation.

(iii)

Each member of the family of Poisson structures on
A
in Theorem
3.1

is induced by a symplectic section of (ii) through the anchor map of the prolongation.

For the rest of this paper, we develop the above statements in detail.

4
Ehresmann Connections and Lie Algebroid Cohomology

In this section we establish the cohomological background for the construction of
the family of Poisson structures in Theorem
3.1
.
More precisely, as we show in Proposition
4.5
,
for any choice of an Ehresmann connection on the prolongation of a Lie algebroid,
the resulting vertical differential is cohomologically trivial in positive degrees.
This readily implies Proposition
5.8
, a sort of Poincaré Lemma for the vertical cohomology of the prolongation
that allows to globalize the constructions presented in Section
7
.

To highlight the intrinsic geometric nature of this construction and provide tools that may be of independent interest,
this section is developed in a slightly more general framework.
The proof of Proposition
4.5
is given via an explicit construction of homotopy operators for the (isomorphic) Lie algebroid cohomology
of a pullback vector bundle with coefficients in a Bott-type representation.
For further developments on connections on the prolongation of a Lie algebroid, see
[
24
]
.

Lie Algebroid Cohomology and Representations.

Let
\mathscr{A}\to E
be a Lie algebroid with anchor map
\varrho:\mathscr{A}\to TE
and Lie bracket
[\dcd]
.
Recall that its
de Rham differential

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}:\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathscr{A}^{*})\to\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathscr{A}^{*})

is the graded derivation
given by the standard Koszul formula

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\omega(\sigma_{0},\ldots,\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varrho(\sigma_{i})}(\omega(\sigma_{0},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma}_{i},\ldots,\sigma_{k}))

(4.1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leqslant i<j\leqslant k}(-1)^{i+j}\omega([\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}],\sigma_{0},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma}_{i},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma}_{j},\ldots,\sigma_{k}),

where
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*})
and
\sigma_{0},\ldots,\sigma_{k}\in\Gamma(\mathscr{A})
.
Moreover, we have
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{2}=0
, and the cohomology of the cochain complex
(\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathscr{A}^{*}),\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}})

is the
cohomology of the Lie algebroid

\mathscr{A}
, which we denote by
H^{\bullet}(\mathscr{A})
.
More generally, given a representation
\nabla
of
\mathscr{A}
on a vector bundle
F\to E
, that is, a flat
\mathscr{A}
-connection on
F
,
we get the cochain complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathscr{A};F),\mathrm{d}_{\nabla})

where
\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathscr{A};F):=\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathscr{A}^{*}\otimes F)
and
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\lambda(\sigma_{0},\ldots,\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\nabla_{\sigma_{i}}(\lambda(\sigma_{0},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma}_{i},\ldots,\sigma_{k}))

(4.2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leqslant i<j\leqslant k}(-1)^{i+j}\lambda([\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}],\sigma_{0},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma}_{i},\ldots,\widehat{\sigma}_{j},\ldots,\sigma_{k}),

for
\lambda\in\Omega^{k}(\mathscr{A};F)
and
\sigma_{0},\ldots,\sigma_{k}\in\Gamma(\mathscr{A})

[
16
, Chapter 7]
.
Its cohomology is called the
cohomology of the Lie algebroid

\mathscr{A}

with coefficients in the representation

\nabla
on
F
,
and is denoted by
H^{\bullet}(\mathscr{A};F)
.

4.1
Cohomology of Vertical Subbundles

Let
E\overset{\pi}{\to}B
be a vector bundle and
VE\subset TE
its vertical distribution:
VE=\ker\mathrm{d}\pi
.
Since
VE
is involutive, it inherits a Lie algebroid structure with the standard Lie bracket of tangent vector fields and inclusion as anchor.
In this part, we describe the Lie algebroid cohomology of
VE
with coefficients in any representation.
We base on the fact that
VE
is isomorphic to the pullback bundle
\pi^{*}E\to E
of
E
along
\pi:E\to B
,
which is equipped with canonical structures useful in the computation of the cohomology.

Recall that the fiber of the pullback bundle
\pi^{*}E\to E
at
v\in E
is just the fiber of
E
at
\pi(v)
:
(\pi^{*}E)_{v}=E_{\pi(v)}
.
Moreover,
\pi^{*}E
is generated by the pullback sections
\pi^{*}s\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}E)
,
s\in\Gamma(E)
,
given by
\pi^{*}s(v):=s(\pi(v))
.
The
vertical lift
is the map

\rho:\pi^{*}E\to TE,\qquad(v_{0},v)\mapsto\rho(v_{0},v):=v_{v_{0}}^{\vee},

(4.3)

where
v\in E
,
v_{0}\in E_{\pi(v)}
, and
v_{v_{0}}^{\vee}\in VE_{v_{0}}
is the tangent vector at
v_{0}
to the curve
t\mapsto v_{0}+tv
:

v_{v_{0}}^{\vee}F:=\left.\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}F(v_{0}+tv),\qquad F\in C^{\infty}(E).

The pullback bundle
\pi^{*}E\to E
is endowed with the following natural structures:

•

A
Lie algebroid structure
with the vertical lift
\rho:\pi^{*}E\to TE
as anchor
and the Lie bracket
\brk
on
\Gamma(\pi^{*}E)
characterized by
[\pi^{*}s_{1},\pi^{*}s_{2}]:=0
for
s_{1},s_{2}\in\Gamma(E)

•

A canonical representation
\nabla
on
\pi^{*}E
satisfying
\nabla_{X}\pi^{*}s:=0
for all
X\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}E),s\in\Gamma(E)

•

The
Euler section

\mathbf{e}\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}E)
, given on
v\in E
by
\mathbf{e}(v):=v
,
where the target
v
is viewed in the fiber
(\pi^{*}E)_{v}=E_{\pi(v)}

Since pullback sections generate
\pi^{*}E
, the Lie bracket
\brk
and the representation
\nabla
are well defined,
extending to arbitrary sections by
\mathbb{R}
-linearity and the Leibniz rule.
The vanishing of
\brk
and
\nabla
on pullback sections imply the flatness of
\nabla
.
Note that
\rho:\pi^{*}E\overset{\sim}{\to}VE
is a Lie algebroid isomorphism mapping
\mathbf{e}
to the Euler vector field on
E
,
implying that
[\mathbf{e},\pi^{*}s]=-\pi^{*}s
for all
s\in\Gamma(E)
.

Representations of
\bm{\pi^{*}E}
.

Let
\nabla
be a representation of
\pi^{*}E
on a vector bundle
F\to E
,
with associated cochain complex
\Omega^{\bullet}(\pi^{*}E;F):=\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}\pi^{*}E^{*}\otimes F)
of differential
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}
.
There is an induced
\pi^{*}E
-connection on
\wedge^{\bullet}\pi^{*}E^{*}\otimes F

given on
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)
, and for
X,u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}E)
by

(\nabla_{X}\omega)(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})=\nabla_{X}\big(\omega(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\big)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\omega(u_{1},\dots,\nabla_{X}u_{i},\dots,u_{k}).

(4.4)

Here,
\nabla
simultaneously denotes the induced connection
on
\wedge^{\bullet}\pi^{*}E^{*}\otimes F
, the representation of
\pi^{*}E
on
F
, and
the canonical flat
\pi^{*}E
-connection on
\pi^{*}E
; the context makes clear which one is meant.

Using the insertion operator we define the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{e}}:\Omega^{\bullet}(\pi^{*}E;F)\to\Omega^{\bullet}(\pi^{*}E;F)
along
\mathbf{e}

via Cartan’s magic formula
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{e}}:=i_{\mathbf{e}}\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}+\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}i_{\mathbf{e}}
.
It is straightforward to check that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{e}}
satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{e}}\omega=\nabla_{\mathbf{e}}\omega+k\omega,\qquad\omega\in\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F).

(4.5)

Scalling Cochain Map.

For fixed
v\in E
and
t\in(0,1]
, consider the
\pi^{*}E
-path
a:[t,1]\to\pi^{*}E

over the the radial curve
\gamma(s)=sv
on
E
given by
a(s)=\frac{1}{s}\mathbf{e}(\gamma(s))
.
Then, a parallel transport operator
\mathcal{P}_{t}:F_{tv}\to F_{v}
is defined by means of
the solution
\sigma:[t,1]\to F
to the
\pi^{*}E
-covariant differential equation

\nabla_{a}\sigma=0,\qquad\text{with initial condition }\quad\sigma(t)=\sigma_{0}\in F_{tv},

as
\mathcal{P}_{t}(\sigma_{0}):=\sigma(1)
.
This is a canonical isomorphism of vector spaces, that is, is independent of the choice of the path
a(s)
,
due to the flatness of
\nabla
.
Furthermore, since
a(s)=\frac{1}{s}\mathbf{e}(\gamma(s))=\frac{1}{s}(sv)=v
, we have that

a(s)
, as well as
\mathcal{P}_{t}
, are well defined and smooth at
t=0
.

Let
\mu_{t}:E\to E
be the radial scaling map
\mu_{t}(v):=tv
,
t\in(0,1]

and consider the Lie algebroid morphism
\Phi_{t}:\pi^{*}E\to\pi^{*}E
covering
\mu_{t}
, defined as

\Phi_{t}(u):=tu,\qquad u\in(\pi^{*}E)_{v},

where the result
tu
is viewed as an element of
(\pi^{*}E)_{tv}=E_{\pi(v)}
.
This induces a map
\Psi_{t}^{*}:\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)\to\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)

given for
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in(\pi^{*}E)_{v}
by

(\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega)_{v}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}):=\mathcal{P}_{t}\Big(\omega_{tv}(\Phi_{t}u_{1},\dots,\Phi_{t}u_{k})\Big)=t^{k}\mathcal{P}_{t}\Big(\omega_{tv}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\Big),

(4.6)

where the second equality uses
\Phi_{t}u_{i}=tu_{i}
.
Note that, since
\Phi_{t}
is a Lie algebroid morphism,
and
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is defined by
\nabla
-parallel transport along a
\pi^{*}E
-path,

\Psi_{t}^{*}
commutes with the differential
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}
:

\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega=\Psi_{t}^{*}\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\omega

(4.7)

Lemma 4.1
.

The map
\Psi_{t}^{*}:\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)\to\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)
satisfies the evolution equation

\frac{d}{dt}\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega=\frac{1}{t}\Psi_{t}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{e}}\omega),\qquad\omega\in\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F),\quad t\in(0,1].

(4.8)

Proof.

By differentiating the right-hand side of (
4.6
), we obtain from the product rule that

\frac{d}{dt}\left((\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega)_{v}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})\right)=\frac{k}{t}(\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega)_{v}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})+\frac{1}{t}(\Psi_{t}^{*}\nabla_{\mathbf{e}}\omega)_{v}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}),

where we have applied
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\sigma_{0})=\mathcal{P}_{t}\left(\nabla_{a(t)}\sigma_{0}\right)=\frac{1}{t}\mathcal{P}_{t}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{e}}\sigma_{0}\right)
,
obtained from the definition of parallel transport.
By (
4.5
), we get the desired evolution equation.
∎

Homotopy and Cohomology.

Given
k\geq 0
, define
h_{k}:\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)\to\Omega^{k-1}(\pi^{*}E;F)
by

h_{k}\omega:=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t}\Psi_{t}^{*}(i_{\mathbf{e}}\omega)\,dt.

(4.9)

To see that this defines a smooth
F
-valued section, note that
h_{0}
is the zero map,
and for
k>0
,
v\in E
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1}\in(\pi^{*}E)_{v}
that

(\Psi_{t}^{*}i_{\mathbf{e}}\omega)_{v}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1})=t^{k-1}\mathcal{P}_{t}\Big(\omega_{tv}(tv,u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1})\Big)=t^{k}\mathcal{P}_{t}\Big(\omega_{tv}(v,u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1})\Big),

so the integrand is exactly
t^{k-1}\mathcal{P}_{t}\Big(\omega_{tv}(v,u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1})\Big)
,
which is smooth and bounded near
t=0
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The maps
h_{k}
in (
4.9
)
give a homotopy between the identity and
\Psi_{0}^{*}:=\lim_{t\to 0}\Psi_{t}^{*}
:

h_{k+1}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}+\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\circ h_{k}=\mathrm{id}-\Psi_{0}^{*}

Moreover, for
k>0
, we have
h_{k+1}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}+\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\circ h_{k}=\mathrm{id}
,
and, for
k=0
, we get
h_{1}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}=\mathrm{id}-\mathcal{P}_{0}\circ p^{*}\circ\iota^{*}
,
where
\iota:B\to E
is the zero section of
E
, and
p:E\to B
is the bundle projection.

Proof.

Given
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)
, by (
4.7
),
and the evolution equation of Lemma
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle h_{k+1}(\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\omega)+\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}(h_{k}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t}\Big[\Psi_{t}^{*}i_{\mathbf{e}}\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\omega+\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\Psi_{t}^{*}i_{\mathbf{e}}\omega\Big]\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t}\Psi_{t}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{e}}\omega)\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}(\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega)\,dt=\Psi_{1}^{*}\omega-\lim_{t\to 0}\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega.

At
t=1
,
\Psi_{1}^{*}
is the identity operator. Since the integral converges,
\lim_{t\to 0}\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega
exists. Furthermore:

•

For
k>0
, we get
\lim_{t\to 0}(\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega)_{v}=\lim_{t\to 0}t^{k}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{t}\circ\omega_{tv}=0
.

•

For
k=0
, we have
\omega\in\Omega^{0}(\pi^{*}E;F)=\Gamma(F)
,
so
\lim_{t\to 0}(\Psi_{t}^{*}\omega)_{v}=\lim_{t\to 0}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\omega_{tv})=\mathcal{P}_{0}(\omega_{0_{\pi(v)}})
,
which is the parallel transport of
\omega
along the radial ray from
0_{\pi(v)}
to
v
.

∎

Consequently, the Lie algebroid cohomology vanishes in strictly positive degrees,
and the zeroth cohomology consists of parallel sections along the fibers:

Corollary 4.3
.

The Lie algebroid cohomology of
\pi^{*}E\to E
with coefficients in
F\to E
satisfies

H^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)=0\quad\text{for }k>0,\quad\text{and}\quad H^{0}(\pi^{*}E;F)\cong\Gamma(\iota^{*}F).

Proof.

If
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)
is
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}
-closed (
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}\omega=0
), then
it follows from Proposition
4.2
that

\omega=\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}(h_{k}\omega)+\Psi_{0}^{*}\omega.

If
k>0
, then
\Psi_{0}^{*}\omega=0
, so
\omega
is
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}
-exact, and thus
H^{k}(\pi^{*}E;F)=0
.
For
k=0
, we have
h_{0}=0
, and hence
\omega=\mathcal{P}_{0}(p^{*}\iota^{*}\omega)
, so

\omega
is the parallel extension of its restriction to the zero section.
Thus, the
\mathrm{d}_{\nabla}
-closed sections of
F
are the parallel sections along the fibers of
E
.
The restriction
\Gamma(F)\to\Gamma(\iota^{*}F)
induces the isomorphism
i^{*}:H^{0}(\pi^{*}E;F)\to\Gamma(\iota^{*}F)
, with inverse the parallel extension
\mathcal{P}_{0}\circ p^{*}
.
∎

4.2
Bott Connections and Bigrading of the de Rham Differential

Let
(\mathscr{A}\to E,\varrho,[\dcd])
be a Lie algebroid, and consider an involutive subbundle
V\subset\mathscr{A}
over
E
, that is,
[\Gamma(V),\Gamma(V)]\subset\Gamma(V)
.
Then, the restricted Lie bracket and anchor of
\mathscr{A}
endow
V
with a Lie algebroid structure,
and hence, with its own de Rham differential
\mathrm{d}_{V}
.

Consider the subbundle
V^{\circ}\subset\mathscr{A}^{*}
given as the annihilator of
V
in
\mathscr{A}^{*}
,
and the exterior algebra
\wedge^{\bullet}V^{\circ}\subset\wedge^{\bullet}\mathscr{A}^{*}
.
There exists a Bott-type representation of
V
on
\wedge^{\bullet}V^{\circ}
,

\nabla:\Gamma(V)\times\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}V^{\circ})\to\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}V^{\circ}),\qquad\nabla_{u}\alpha:=\mathcal{L}_{u}\alpha,\quad u\in\Gamma(V),\alpha\in\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}V^{\circ})

where the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{u}\alpha
is defined by the standard Cartan formula

\mathcal{L}_{u}\alpha:=\ii_{u}\mathrm{d}_{A}\alpha+\mathrm{d}_{A}\ii_{u}\alpha.

It is straightforward to check that
\nabla
is indeed a representation of
V
on
\wedge^{\bullet}V^{\circ}
.
In particular, for each
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have the cochain complex
(\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}V^{*}\otimes\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}),\mathrm{d}^{\nabla}_{V})

of the Lie algebroid
V
with coefficients in the representation on
\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}
induced by the Bott connection
\nabla
.

Vertical Subbundles and Ehresmann Connections.

Note that the sections of
\wedge^{\bullet}V^{*}
are
C^{\infty}(E)
-linear forms on
\Gamma(V)
,
which can be extended to sections of
\wedge^{\bullet}\mathscr{A}^{*}
by the choice of a (possibly non-linear) connection.

Given the Lie algebroid
\mathscr{A}
with involutive subbundle
V
,
an
Ehresmann connection
is a vector bundle projection
\gamma:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
on
V
, that is,
\gamma^{2}=\gamma
and
\operatorname{im}\gamma=V
.
We say that
V
is the
vertical subbundle
associated with
\gamma
,
and the
horizontal subbundle
is
H:=\ker\gamma
.
This gives rise to a direct sum decomposition
\mathscr{A}=H\oplus V
,
and the decomposition of the exterior algebra

\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*}=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{q}H^{\circ},

where
V^{\circ},H^{\circ}\subset\mathscr{A}^{*}
are the annihilators of
V
and
H
, respectively.
So, each
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*})
splits as

\omega=\sum_{p+q=k}\omega_{p,q},\quad\text{with }\omega_{p,q}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{q}H^{\circ});

here, we say that
\omega_{p,q}
is the component of
\omega
of
bidegree

(p,q)
.

Note that the choice of an Ehresmann connection
\gamma
for
V
in
\mathscr{A}

gives rise to a vector bundle isomorphism
H^{\circ}\cong V^{*}
.
Indeed, the direction
H^{\circ}\to V^{*}
is given by simple restriction to
V
;
while the inverse
\gamma^{*}:V^{*}\to H^{\circ}
is just extending by zero on
H
.
This induces an isomorphism of vector bundles
\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{q}H^{\circ}\cong\wedge^{q}V^{*}\otimes\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.
For each
\omega_{p,q}\in\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{q}H^{\circ}
, we denote by

\bar{\omega}_{p,q}\in\wedge^{q}V^{*}\otimes\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}
the corresponding element under this identification.

At the same time, the Ehresmann connection
\gamma
induces a
\gamma
-dependent bigrading of the de Rham differential
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}
as

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}=\mathrm{d}_{1,0}+\mathrm{d}_{0,1}+\mathrm{d}_{2,-1},

where the bidegrees of the components are indicated in the subscripts; that is, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{i,j}\big(\Gamma(\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{q}H^{\circ})\big)\subseteq\Gamma(\wedge^{p+i}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{q+j}H^{\circ})

for
(i,j)\in\{(1,0),(0,1),(2,-1)\}
and all
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The coboundary condition
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{2}=0
translates into the following relations among the components:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{0,1}^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{1,0}\mathrm{d}_{0,1}+\mathrm{d}_{0,1}\mathrm{d}_{1,0}

\displaystyle=0,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{0,1}\mathrm{d}_{2,-1}+\mathrm{d}_{2,-1}\mathrm{d}_{0,1}+\mathrm{d}_{1,0}^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{1,0}\mathrm{d}_{2,-1}+\mathrm{d}_{2,-1}\mathrm{d}_{1,0}

\displaystyle=0,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{2,-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=0

(4.14)

In particular, note from (
4.10
) that on
\Gamma(\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}H^{\circ},\mathrm{d}_{0,1})

is a cochain complex for each
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Under the identification
\Gamma(\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}H^{\circ})\cong\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}V^{*}\otimes\wedge^{p}V^{\circ})
,
the coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}_{0,1}
corresponds to the de Rham differential
\mathrm{d}_{V}^{\nabla}
of the Lie algebroid
V

with coefficients in the Bott representation
\nabla
on
\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}
.

This follows by direct inspection in the Koszul formula for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\omega_{p,q}

when evaluated on
p
sections of
H
and
q+1
sections of
V
:
the non-vanishing terms are precisely those given by the Koszul formula in the evaluation of
\mathrm{d}_{V}^{\nabla}\bar{\omega}_{p,q}
.
This result is the Lie algebroid analogue of the well-known fact that
the vertical component of the de Rham differential of a fiber bundle with respect to an Ehresmann connection
is the de Rham differential of the fibers with coefficients in the representation given by the Bott connection
(see, for instance,
[
4
, Section 2]
).

Vanishing of the
\bm{d_{0,1}}
-cohomology.

Consider the cochain complex
(\Gamma(\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}H^{\circ}),\mathrm{d}_{0,1})
for fixed
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In the case when
V
is isomorphic to the vertical subbundle of a vector bundle,
we can use the previous results to show that the cohomology of this complex vanishes in positive degrees.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathscr{A}\to E
be a Lie algebroid over a vector bundle
E\overset{\pi}{\to}M
,
and
V\subset\mathscr{A}
an involutive subbundle that is isomorphic as a Lie algebroid to the pullback bundle
p^{*}E
.
Then, for any choice of an Ehresmann connection
\gamma
for
V
in
\mathscr{A}
, the cohomology of the cochain complex

(\Gamma(\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}H^{\circ}),\mathrm{d}_{0,1})
vanishes in positive degrees, for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.4
, the complex
(\Gamma(\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}H^{\circ}),\mathrm{d}_{0,1})

is isomorphic to the cochain complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(V;\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}),\mathrm{d}_{V}^{\nabla})

of the Lie algebroid
V
with coefficients in the Bott-type representation on
\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}
.
In particular, their cohomologies are isomorphic.
Since, by hypothesis,
V
is isomorphic to
p^{*}E
,
by Corollary
4.3
, the cohomology of
\pi^{*}E
with coefficients in any representation vanishes in positive degrees,
and hence, so does the cohomology of
V
with coefficients in
\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}
:

H^{k}(V;\wedge^{p}V^{\circ})=0
for all
k>0
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular, the
\mathrm{d}_{0,1}
-cohomology of
\Gamma(\wedge^{p}V^{\circ}\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}H^{\circ})
vanishes in positive degrees.
∎

5
The Prolongation of a Lie Algebroid

In this section, we recall the prolongation of a Lie algebroid,
and some of its intrinsic geometric structures used in the rest of the paper.
The prolongation provides the natural framework to describe second-order dynamics
and to construct symplectic structures associated with regular Lagrangians.

The characterization of these symplectic sections, developed in Section
7
,
is the key to the proof of the main Theorem
3.1
.
Here we develop some tools towards this characterization:
Lemma
5.4
allows the application of the results of Section
4
,
leading in Proposition
5.8
to a “Poincaré lemma” for the vertical cohomology of the prolongation.

Further details on the Lie algebroid prolongation can be found in
[
5
,
11
,
20
,
21
]
.

For a Lie algebroid
(p:A\rightarrow M,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
, its
prolongation
over
A

is the vector bundle
p_{A}:\mathcal{T}A\rightarrow A
where

\mathcal{T}A:=\{(a,b,v)\in A\times A\times TA\mid p(a)=p(b);\,\rho(b)=T_{a}p(v)\}

(5.1)

and
p_{A}(a,b,v):=a
.
So, the fiber over
a\in A
is isomorphic to the vector space

(p_{A})^{-1}(a)\simeq\{(b,v)\in A_{p(a)}\times T_{a}A\mid\rho(b)=T_{a}p(v)\}.

Consider also the map
\widehat{p}:\mathcal{T}A\rightarrow A
given by
\widehat{p}(a,b,v):=b
.
The
vertical subbundle
of
\mathcal{T}A
is

\mathrm{Vert}(\mathcal{T}A):=\mathrm{ker}\ \widehat{p}=\{(a,b,v)\in\mathcal{T}A\mid b=0,\text{ and }\,T_{a}p(v)=0\}.

(5.2)

Elements of
\mathrm{Vert}(\mathcal{T}A)
are called
vertical
. Moreover:

Definition 5.1
.

A section
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{T}A)
is called
vertical
if
Z(a)\in\mathrm{Vert}(\mathcal{T}A)
for all
a\in A
.

Notice that a vertical section
Z
in
\mathcal{T}A
takes the form

Z=(\mathrm{id}_{A},0,X),

with
X\in\mathfrak{X}(A)
a vertical vector field on
A
, i.e.,
Tp\circ X=0.

A more general type of sections of
\mathcal{T}A
are the so-called
projectable
sections.

Definition 5.2
.

A section
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{T}A)
is said to be
projectable

if there exists
\sigma\in\Gamma(A)
such that

\widehat{p}\circ Z=\sigma\circ p.

(5.3)

One can deduce that every projectable section
Z
has the form

Z=(\mathrm{id}_{A},\sigma\circ p,X),

where
\sigma\in\Gamma(A)
and
X\in\mathfrak{X}(A)
are such that
X
and
\rho(\sigma)
are
p
-related vector fields.

A local coordinate system on the prolongation
\mathcal{T}A
can be obtained as follows:
fix an adapted coordinate system
(x^{i},y^{k})
on
A
associated with a local chart
(U,x^{1},x^{2},\ldots,x^{n})
on
M
and a basis
\{e_{l}\}
of local sections on
A
.
We choose a point
(a,b,v)\in\mathcal{T}A
. Since
p(a)=p(b)
the points
a,b\in A
have coordinates
(x^{i},u^{k})
and
(x^{i},y^{j})
, respectively. Now, the condition
\rho(b)=T_{a}p(v)
implies that
v
has the form
\displaystyle v=y^{j}\rho^{i}_{j}(x)\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right|_{a}+v^{l}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{l}}\right|_{a}
. Thus, one can assign

\mathcal{T}A\ni(a,b,v)\mapsto(x^{i},u^{k},y^{j},v^{l}),

(5.4)

as coordinates.
It follows from here that
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{T}A=2\,\mathrm{rank}\,A

and
\mathrm{rank}\,(\mathrm{Vert}(\mathcal{T}A))=\mathrm{rank}\,A
.

One can also define a local basis
\{\mathcal{E}_{i},\Upsilon_{j}\}
of sections of
\mathcal{T}^{A}A

as follows:

\mathcal{E}_{j}(a):=\left(a,e_{j}(p(a)),\rho^{i}_{j}(x)\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right|_{a}\right),\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \Upsilon_{k}(a):=\left(a,0,\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{k}}\right|_{a}\right)

(5.5)

Note that these local sections are projectable.
Moreover, the sections
\Upsilon_{k}
are vertical and determine a local basis for the subspace of vertical sections.

Lie algebroid structure of
\mathcal{T}A
.

The key property that allows us to define a Lie algebroid structure on
\mathcal{T}A
is the fact that
every section of
\mathcal{T}A
is locally written as a linear combination of projectable sections.
The Lie bracket can be easily defined in terms of projectable sections and extended by
\mathbb{R}
-linearity and the Leibniz rule.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
Z_{i}=(\mathrm{id}_{A},\sigma_{i}\circ p,X_{i})
be projectable sections of
\mathcal{T}A
with
\sigma_{i}\in\Gamma(A)
for
i=1,2.

The Lie bracket of
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
is

[Z_{1},Z_{2}](a):=(a,[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]\circ p(a),[X_{1},X_{2}](a)).

The Lie brackets of the elements of the basis (
5.5
) are given by

[\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{j}]=C^{k}_{ij}\mathcal{E}_{k},\qquad[\mathcal{E}_{i},\Upsilon_{k}]=0,\quad\text{and}\quad[\Upsilon_{j},\Upsilon_{k}]=0.

(5.6)

The anchor map
\widehat{\rho}:\mathcal{T}A\rightarrow TA
is just the projection onto the third factor,
\widehat{\rho}(a,b,v):=v
,
and can also be defined locally in terms of the basis (
5.5
) by

\widehat{\rho}(\mathcal{E}_{j})=\rho^{i}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}},\qquad\widehat{\rho}(\Upsilon_{k})=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{k}}.

(5.7)

Given
a,b\in A
in the same fiber, recall that the
vertical lift

(a,b)\mapsto b_{a}^{\vee}\in T_{a}A
is defined on
F\in C^{\infty}(A)
by

b_{a}^{\vee}F:=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}(F(a+tb)).

This gives rise to the isomorphisms

5.1
Liouville section, vertical endomorphism and SODEs

Definition 5.5
.

The
Liouville (Euler) section

\Delta\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Vert}(\mathcal{T}A))
of the vertical subbundle
of the prolongation of the Lie algebroid
(p:A\rightarrow M,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
is given by

\Delta(a):=\xi^{\vee}(a,a)=(a,0,a^{\vee}_{a}).

(5.9)

We notice that
\widehat{\rho}(\Delta)=\mathbf{E}
is the Liouville vector field on
A
.
On the other hand, the
vertical endomorphism
is the vector bundle map
J:\mathcal{T}A\rightarrow\mathcal{T}A
given by

J(a,b,v)=(a,0,b^{\vee}_{a}).

(5.10)

The following fact is clear due to the surjectivity of the vertical lifting map:

Lemma 5.6
.

The vertical endomorphism (
5.10
) satisfies
\mathrm{Im}\,J=\ker J=\mathrm{Vert}(\mathcal{T}A)
. Hence,
J^{2}=0
.

Let us denote by the same letter
J
the induced endomorphism on the module of sections
J:\Gamma(\mathcal{T}A)\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{T}A)
,
as well as the negative of its insertion operator
J:\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}(\mathcal{T}A)^{*})\to\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}(\mathcal{T}A)^{*})
,
which is a derivation of the tensor algebra of sections
\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}(\mathcal{T}A)^{*})
characterized by

J(L):=0,\ \ \ \ J(\sigma):=-\sigma\circ J,

for all
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
and
\sigma\in\Gamma((\mathcal{T}A)^{*})
.
In particular, for the dual basis
\{\Upsilon^{j},\mathcal{E}^{i}\}
of
\{\mathcal{E}_{i},\Upsilon_{j}\}
, we have

J\mathcal{E}^{i}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ J\Upsilon^{j}=-\mathcal{E}^{j},

and if
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}(\mathcal{T}A)^{*})
then

J\omega(Z_{1},Z_{2})=-\omega(J(Z_{1}),Z_{2})-\omega(Z_{1},J(Z_{2}))

for all
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{T}A)
.

Definition 5.7
.

A section
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{T}A)
is called a second-order differential equation (SODE) on the Lie algebroid
A
if

J\circ Z=\Delta,

where
J
is the vertical endomorphims (
5.10
) and
\Delta
is the Liouville section (
5.9
).

There is a biunivocal correspondence between SODEs and semisprays on
A
:

(i)

If
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{T}A)
is a SODE then the vector field
\mathcal{V}=\widehat{\rho}(Z)\in\mathfrak{X}(A)
is a semispray.

(ii)

Reciprocally, if
\mathcal{V}\in\mathfrak{X}(A)
is a semispray then
Z(a)=(a,a,\mathcal{V}(a))
,
a\in A
, is a SODE.

From these obsevations, it is clear that
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{T}A)
is a SODE on
A
if and only if
Z
is a section of the form

Z(a)=(a,a,\mathcal{V}(a)),

(5.11)

Locally, an SODE takes the following form

Z(x,y)=y^{j}\mathcal{E}_{j}(x,y)+\nu^{k}(x,y)\Upsilon_{k}(x,y).

(5.12)

5.2
Non-linear connections and bigraded decomposition

To simplify notation for the remainder of the paper, we denote by
\mathscr{A}:=\mathcal{T}A

the prolongation of the Lie algebroid
A
.
Recall form Lemma
5.4
that the vertical subbundle
\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})
is involutive in the Lie algebroid
\mathscr{A}
.
In this sense, following Section
4
, an Ehresmann connection is a vector bundle morphism
\gamma:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}

such that
\operatorname{Im}\gamma=\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})
and
\gamma^{2}=\gamma
,
giving rise to a horizontal subbundle
\mathbb{H}
, complementary to the vertical bundle
\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})
, on
\mathscr{A}
:

\mathscr{A}=\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})\oplus\mathbb{H}
.

For a fixed basis
\{\mathcal{E}_{i},\Upsilon_{j}\}
of local sections on
\mathscr{A}
as in (
5.5
),
with dual basis
\{\Upsilon^{j},\mathcal{E}^{i}\}
,
an Ehresmann connection is locally given by

\gamma=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathscr{A}}-\mathcal{E}^{i}\otimes(\mathcal{E}_{i}-\gamma^{j}_{i}\Upsilon_{j}).

(5.13)

The vertical and horizontal subbundles and their annihilators are locally generated as

\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})=\mathrm{span}\{\Upsilon_{j}\},\ \ \ \ \mathbb{H}=\mathrm{span}\{\mathrm{hor}_{i}:=\mathcal{E}_{i}-\gamma_{i}^{j}\Upsilon_{j}\},

\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{0}=\mathrm{span}\{\mathcal{E}^{j}\},\ \ \ \ \mathbb{H}^{0}=\mathrm{span}\{\nu^{i}:=\Upsilon^{i}+\gamma_{j}^{i}\mathcal{E}^{j}\}.

The choice of an Ehresmann connection
\gamma
induces a bigrading of
\Gamma(\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*})
,

\Gamma(\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*})=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\Gamma_{pq}(\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*}),

(5.14)

where
\Gamma_{pq}(\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*}):=\Gamma(\wedge^{p}\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{0}\otimes\wedge^{q}\mathbb{H}^{0})

denotes the set of sections of bidegree
(p,q)
.

The de Rham differential
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}
also has the bigraded decomposition

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}+\partial_{\mathscr{A}},

(5.15)

where each component
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
,
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
, and
\partial_{\mathscr{A}}

is of bidegree
(1,0)
,
(0,1)
, and
(2,-1)
, respectively.
The coboundary condition
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}=0
is equivalent to the relations

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=0,\ \ \ \partial_{\mathscr{A}}\circ\partial_{\mathscr{A}}=0,\ \ \ \ \mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\circ\partial_{\mathscr{A}}+\partial_{\mathscr{A}}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}=0,\ \ \ \ \mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\circ\partial_{\mathscr{A}}+\partial_{\mathscr{A}}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=0.

Observe that the component
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
in the decomposition (
5.15
) is also a coboundary operator.
Furthermore, as discussed in Section
4
, its cohomology is trivial in positive degrees.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\vartheta\in\Gamma_{pq}(\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*})
be a section of bidegree
(p,q)
,
q\geq 1
.
If
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\vartheta=0
, then there exists
\zeta\in\Gamma_{p,q-1}(\wedge^{k-1}\mathscr{A}^{*})
such that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\zeta=\vartheta.

Proof.

For the Lie algebroid
\mathscr{A}\to A
, the vertical subbundle
\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})
involutive
and isomorphic to the pullback bundle
p^{*}A
, due to Lemma
5.4
.
So, by Proposition
4.5
, the cohomology of

(\Gamma(\wedge^{p}\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{0}\otimes\wedge^{\bullet}\mathbb{H}^{0}),\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})

vanishes in degree
q
, meaning that every
\vartheta\in\Gamma_{pq}(\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*})
with
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\vartheta=0

is of the form
\vartheta=\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\zeta
for some
\zeta\in\Gamma_{p,q-1}(\wedge^{k-1}\mathscr{A}^{*})
.
∎

6
Symplectic Sections on the Prolongation

In this section, we recall the notion of
symplectic Lie algebroid

[
11
]
,
that is, a Lie algebroid equipped with a covariant 2-section with properties analogous to those of symplectic structures.
In particular, we recall their construction on the prolongation as the
Cartan 2-section
of a regular Lagrangian.

6.1
Symplectic Lie Algebroids

Given a Lie algebroid
(p:A\to M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\rho)
, with de Rham differential
\mathrm{d}_{A}
,
a section
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{k}A^{*})
is said to be
closed
if
\mathrm{d}_{A}\omega=0
.
If
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}A^{*})
, then we have the vector bundle morphism
\omega^{\flat}:A\rightarrow A^{*}

given by the insertion:
a\mapsto\mathrm{i}_{a}\omega
.
We say that
\omega
is
non-degenerate
if
\omega^{\flat}
is an isomorphism of vector bundles.

Definition 6.1
.

A
symplectic structure
on the Lie algebroid
A\rightarrow M
is a
closed and non-degenerate section
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}A^{*})
.
In this case, the pair
(A,\omega)
is called a
symplectic Lie algebroid
.

On a symplectic algebroid
(A,\omega)
, for each function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
there exists a unique
\sigma_{f}\in\Gamma(A)

called the
Hamiltonian section
of
f
, such that

\mathrm{i}_{\sigma_{f}}\omega=-\mathrm{d}_{A}f.

(6.1)

Furthermore, as is well known,
every symplectic Lie algebroid gives rise to a Poisson structure on the base manifold
M
.
This can be seen as an instance of Proposition 3.6 and Theorem 4.1 in
[
17
]

in the context of triangular Lie bialgebroids.
For completeness, we present the following proof.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
(A,\omega)
be a symplectic algebroid. The symplectic structure
\omega
defines a Poisson bracket on
M
given by

\{f,g\}_{\omega}:=\omega(\sigma_{f},\sigma_{g}),\qquad f,g\in C^{\infty}(M).

(6.2)

Furthermore, the anchor map relates the Hamiltonian section
\sigma_{f}
and vector field
X_{f}
as

\rho(\sigma_{f})=X_{f}

Proof.

It is clear that (
6.2
) defines a bivector field
\pi
on
M
.
Furthermore, if
\pi_{\omega}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}A)
is the Poisson structure on
A
induced by
\omega
,
then (
6.2
) implies that
\pi_{\omega}
and
\pi
are
\rho
-related:
\pi^{\sharp}=\rho\circ\pi_{\omega}^{\sharp}\circ\rho^{*}
.
Since the anchor map
\rho:\Gamma(A)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
is a Lie algebra homomorphism,
its extension to multisections
\wedge^{\bullet}\rho:\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}A)^{*}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(M)

is a graded Lie algebra homomorphism when equipped with their respective Schouten–Nijenhuis brackets.
The
\rho
-relatedness of
\pi
and
\pi_{\omega}
then implies that

[\pi,\pi]=[\wedge^{2}\rho\pi_{\omega},\wedge^{2}\rho\pi_{\omega}]=\wedge^{3}\rho[\pi_{\omega},\pi_{\omega}]=0.

For the second part, note for every
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
that

\mathcal{L}_{\rho(\sigma_{f})}g=\langle\mathrm{d}_{A}g,\sigma_{f}\rangle=\omega(\sigma_{f},\sigma_{g})=\{f,g\}_{\omega}=\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}g.

∎

Example 6.3
.

For the tangent Lie algebroid
(\tau_{M}:TM\rightarrow M)
, a symplectic section
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}T^{*}M)

is precisely a symplectic 2-form on
M
and the symplectic algebroid
(TM,\omega)
turns out to be a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
.

6.2
The Cartan 2-Section of a Regular Lagrangian

In this part, we describe how one can construct a symplectic section on the prolongation
\mathscr{A}:=\mathcal{T}A
of a Lie algebroid
(p:A\rightarrow M,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)

by means of a regular Lagrangian
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
.
To this end, we first introduce the Poincaré–Cartan 1-section.

Definition 6.4
.

Given a function
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
, the
Cartan 1-section

\theta_{L}\in\Gamma(\mathscr{A}^{*})
is defined by

\theta_{L}:=-J(\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}L).

The Cartan 1-section is horizontal, that is, it vanishes on
\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})
.
Locally, it takes the form

\theta_{L}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial y^{k}}\mathcal{E}^{k}.

Definition 6.5
.

The Cartan 2-section is the differential of
\theta_{L}
:

\omega_{L}:=\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\theta_{L}

(6.3)

The local expression of the Cartan 2-section is

\omega_{L}=M_{ij}\Upsilon^{i}\wedge\mathcal{E}^{j}+\frac{1}{2}N_{kl}\mathcal{E}^{k}\wedge\mathcal{E}^{l},

(6.4)

where

M_{ij}:=\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{kl}:=\rho^{r}_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial x^{r}\partial y^{l}}-\rho^{r}_{l}\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial x^{r}\partial y^{k}}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial y^{r}}C^{r}_{kl}.

From here, it is clear that
\omega_{L}
vanishes on the vertical subbundle, i.e.,

\left.\omega_{L}\right|_{\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})\times\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})}=0.

Moreover, it follows form (
6.4
) that
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
is a regular Lagrangian if and only if
the Cartan 2-section
\omega_{L}
(
6.3
) is a symplectic section.
In this case, since
\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{A}=2\operatorname{rank}\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})
,
we have that
\omega_{L}^{\flat}:\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})\to\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{0}
is an isomorphism.
Furthermore, the Hamiltonian section of its energy function

E_{L}:=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{E}}L-L=\frac{\partial L}{\partial y^{k}}y^{k}-L

(6.5)

is a second-order differential equation.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
be a regular Lagrangian with Cartan 2-section
\omega_{L}
.
Then, the Hamiltonian section
\sigma_{E_{L}}
of the energy function
E_{L}
,

\mathrm{i}_{\sigma_{E_{L}}}\omega_{L}=-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}E_{L},

(6.6)

is a SODE on
A
.

Proof.

We shall prove that
\sigma_{E_{L}}
has locally the form (
5.12
).
Let
\sigma_{E_{L}}=f^{a}\mathcal{E}_{a}+g^{b}\Upsilon_{b}
. By (
6.4
),

\mathrm{i}_{\sigma_{E_{L}}}\omega_{L}=-M_{ia}f^{a}\Upsilon^{i}+(M_{bj}g^{b}+f^{a}N_{aj})\mathcal{E}^{j}.

Since

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}E_{L}=M_{ia}y^{a}\Upsilon^{i}+\rho^{k}_{j}\frac{\partial E_{L}}{\partial x^{k}}\mathcal{E}^{j},

(6.7)

we have from (
6.6
) that

M_{ia}(y^{a}-f^{a})=0,\quad\text{for all $i$.}

(6.8)

The regularity of
L
implies that the unique solution of (
6.8
) is
f^{a}=y^{a}
, so
\sigma_{E_{L}}
is a SODE.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

If
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
is a regular Lagrangian,
then the Poisson structure
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\omega_{L}}
on
A
associated with the Cartan 2-section
\omega_{L}

has as Hamiltonian vector field the semispray
\mathcal{V}_{L}:=\widehat{\rho}(\sigma_{E_{L}})
.

This corollary is a particular instance of Theorem
3.1
.
Actually, in our proof of Theorem
3.1
, we construct a family of Poisson structures on
A

that contains that of Corollary
6.7
.

Further computations from the proof of Proposition
6.6

show that the local expressions of the Hamiltonian section
\sigma_{E_{L}}
and the semispray
\mathcal{V}_{L}
are

\displaystyle\sigma_{E_{L}}=y^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}-M^{rl}\left(\rho^{b}_{l}\frac{\partial E_{L}}{\partial x^{b}}+N_{sl}y^{s}\right)\Upsilon_{r},\qquad\mathcal{V}_{L}=y^{\alpha}\rho^{\beta}_{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\beta}}-M^{rl}\left(\rho^{b}_{l}\frac{\partial E_{L}}{\partial x^{b}}+N_{sl}y^{s}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{r}}.

These formulae allow us to give positive answers to the question of existence of Hamiltonian sprays on
A
:

Example 6.8
.

Let
g\in\Gamma(A^{*}\otimes A^{*})
be a pseudo-Riemannian metric on the Lie algebroid
(A,\rho,[\dcd])

and consider the regular (quadratic) Lagrangian
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
of Example
2.7
:
L(a):=g(a,a)=\tfrac{1}{2}g_{rs}(x)y^{r}y^{s}
.
Notice that the Hamiltonian semispray
\mathcal{V}_{L}
in Corollary
6.7
is actually a spray, since

M^{sk}=g^{sk}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{jk}=\left(\rho^{r}_{j}\frac{\partial g_{sk}}{\partial x^{r}}-\rho^{r}_{k}\frac{\partial g_{sj}}{\partial x^{r}}-g_{is}C^{i}_{jk}\right)y^{s},

where
g^{rs}
is the inverse of the metric
g_{rs}
on
A
.
From here, it is clear that the coefficients of
\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{s}}
in
\mathcal{V}_{L}
are fiberwise quadratic,
implying that
\mathcal{V}_{L}
is an spray on
A
.
We also observe that
N_{jk}
are the coefficients of
d_{A}(g^{\flat}\mathbf{E})
,
where
\mathbf{E}
is the Euler vector field.
In terms of the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu\lambda}
of the Levi-Civita
A
-connection of
g
,
the Hamiltonian spray
\mathcal{V}_{L}
is

\mathcal{V}_{L}=y^{\alpha}\rho^{\beta}_{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\beta}}+g^{sk}\left(\left(-\Gamma_{ab}^{\mu}g_{\mu k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma_{kb}^{\mu}g_{\mu a}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma_{ka}^{\mu}g_{\mu b}\right)y^{a}y^{b}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{s}}.

7
Symplectic Sections Vanishing on the Vertical Subbundle

In this section, we study the class of symplectic sections on the prolongation of a Lie algebroid that vanish on the vertical subbundle.
Concretely, we present two key results.
In Proposition
7.2
, we characterize those symplectic sections vanishing whenever both arguments are vertical.
Then, in Proposition
7.3
, we describe the subfamily annihilated by the dual extension of the vertical endomorphism.
This class of symplectic sections induce Poisson brackets on the Lie algebroid
A

having the properties stated in Theorem
3.1
, namely,
admitting a Hamiltonian semispray on
A
.

Recall that a section
Z\in\Gamma(\mathscr{A})
is called vertical if
Z(a)\in\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})
for all
a\in A
.
A section
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{k}\mathscr{A}^{*})
is called
horizontal
if
\mathrm{i}_{Z}\omega=0
for every vertical section
Z
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\Omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\mathscr{A}^{*})
be a symplectic section. We say that
\Omega
vanishes on the vertical subbundle if
\left.\Omega\right|_{\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})\times\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})}=0.

Now, we characterize the symplectic sections vanishing on the vertical subbundle.

Proposition 7.2
.

If a symplectic section
\Omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\mathscr{A}^{*})
vanishes on the vertical subbundle, then

\Omega=\Theta+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\zeta,

(7.1)

where
\zeta\in\Gamma(\mathscr{A}^{*})
and
\Theta\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\mathscr{A}^{*})
are horizontal,
\Theta
is
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}
-closed and
\mathrm{rank}(\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\zeta)=2r
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\mathscr{A}^{*})
be a symplectic section vanishing on the vertical subbundle.
Fix any Ehresmann connection
\gamma
and assume that
\Omega
has the following bigrading decomposition

\Omega=\Omega_{20}+\Omega_{11}+\Omega_{02}.

Since
\Omega
vanishes on the vertical subbundle,
\Omega_{02}\equiv 0
. Using the bigraded decomposition (
5.15
) of the exterior diferential
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}
and the closedness of
\Omega
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\Omega_{11}=0,

(7.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\Omega_{11}+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\Omega_{20}=0,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\Omega_{20}+\partial_{\mathscr{A}}\Omega_{11}=0.

(7.4)

By (
7.2
) and Proposition
5.8
, there exists
\zeta\in\Gamma_{10}(\mathscr{A}^{*})
such that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\zeta=\Omega_{11}
.
Now, from equations (
7.3
) and (
7.4
), we have

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\Omega_{11}+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\Omega_{20}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\zeta+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\Omega_{20}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}(\Omega_{20}-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\zeta)=0,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\Omega_{20}+\partial_{\mathscr{A}}\Omega_{11}=\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\Omega_{20}-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\zeta-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\partial_{\mathscr{A}}\zeta=\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}(\Omega_{20}-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\zeta)=0.

These relations imply that
\Theta:=\Omega_{20}-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\zeta
is a horizontal closed section.
Finally,

\Omega=\Omega_{20}+\Omega_{11}+\Omega_{02}=\Theta+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\zeta+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\zeta=\Theta+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\zeta.

∎

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
\Omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\mathscr{A}^{*})
be a symplectic section vanishing on the vertical subbundle. Then,
J(\Omega)=0
if and only if

\Omega=\Theta+\omega_{L},

where
\omega_{L}
is the Cartan 2-section for some regular Lagrangian
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
.

Proof.

First, we shall assume that
J(\Omega)=0
. By Proposition
7.2
,
\Omega=\Theta+\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\zeta
.
Since
\Theta
and
\zeta
are horizontal, Proposition
5.6
implies that

J(\Omega)=J(\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\zeta).

Then
J(\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\zeta)=0
.
Since
J:\mathbb{H}^{0}\to\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{0}
is an isomorphism,
there exists a unique
(0,1)
-section
\widetilde{\zeta}
such that
J(\widetilde{\zeta})=-\zeta
.
Furthermore, we have that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\widetilde{\zeta}=0
.
Indeed, If
\zeta=\zeta^{j}\mathcal{E}^{j}
, then
\widetilde{\zeta}=-\zeta^{j}\nu^{j}
and, by a direct computation, we get

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\widetilde{\zeta}=\frac{\partial\zeta^{j}}{\partial{y}^{i}}\nu^{i}\wedge\nu^{j}\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ J(\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\zeta)=-\frac{\partial\zeta^{j}}{\partial{y}^{i}}\mathcal{E}^{i}\wedge\mathcal{E}^{j}.

From here, we get that the vanishing of
J(\mathrm{d}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathscr{A}}\zeta)
implies
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\widetilde{\zeta}=0
.
Thus, by Proposition
5.8
, there exists
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
such that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}L=\widetilde{\zeta}
.
Since
J
vanishes on
\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{0}
, we get

\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\zeta=-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}J(\mathrm{d}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathscr{A}}L)=-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}J(\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}L)=\omega_{L}.

Thus,
\Omega=\Theta+\omega_{L}
.
Since
\Omega
is symplectic, the Lagrangian
L
has to be regular.
Conversely, if
\Omega=\Theta+\omega_{L}
, then by (
6.4
) we get

J(\Omega)=J(\omega_{L})=-\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}\mathcal{E}^{i}\wedge\mathcal{E}^{j},

which vanishes since
\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}
is symmetric while
\mathcal{E}^{i}\wedge\mathcal{E}^{j}
is skew-symmetric on
i
and
j
.
∎

Characterization of Horizontal Closed Sections in the Prolongation.

Following
[
21
]
, recall that the map
\widehat{p}:\mathscr{A}:=\mathcal{T}A\rightarrow A
is a Lie algebroid epimorphism over the projection
p:A\rightarrow M
.
In consequence, we have that
\widehat{p}^{\,*}
is a cochain complex monomorphism:
\widehat{p}^{\,*}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}=\mathrm{d}_{A}\circ\widehat{p}^{\,*}
.
Furthermore, since
\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})=\ker\widehat{p}
,
it follows that
\widehat{p}^{\,*}:\wedge^{\bullet}A^{*}\to\wedge^{\bullet}\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{\circ}
is an isomorphism.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\Theta\in\Gamma(\wedge^{k}\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{\circ})
be a horizontal section.
Then,
\Theta
is
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}
-closed if and only if
there exists a
\mathrm{d}_{A}
-closed section
\theta\in\Gamma(\wedge^{k}A^{*})
such that
\widehat{p}^{\,*}\theta=\Theta
.

Proof.

Since
\widehat{p}^{\,*}:\wedge^{\bullet}A^{*}\to\wedge^{\bullet}\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{\circ}
is an isomorphism,
there exists a unique
\theta\in\Gamma(\wedge^{k}A^{*})
such that
\widehat{p}^{\,*}\theta=\Theta
.
Taking into account that
\widehat{p}^{\,*}
is a morphism of cochain complex, we have that
\widehat{p}^{\,*}\mathrm{d}_{A}\theta=\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\Theta
.
From here, it is clear that
\Theta
is
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}
-closed if
\mathrm{d}_{A}\theta=0
.
Conversely, if
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}\Theta=0
, then
\widehat{p}^{\,*}\mathrm{d}_{A}\theta=0
.
Since
\widehat{p}^{\,*}
is injective, we conclude that
\mathrm{d}_{A}\theta=0
.
∎

The preceding proposition provides a simple intrinsic description of the horizontal closed sections of the prolongation
and allows us to construct them systematically.
With this in mind, we arrive at the following characterization of the symplectic sections vanishing on the vertical subbundle by means of objects on the base algebroid.

Corollary 7.5
.

A symplectic section
\Omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\mathscr{A}^{*})
vanishing on the vertical subbundle satisfies
J(\Omega)=0
if and only if
there exist a regular Lagrangian function
L\in C^{\infty}(A)
and a
\mathrm{d}_{A}
-closed section
\theta\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}A^{*})
such that

\Omega=\omega_{L}+\widehat{p}^{\,*}\theta.

This characterization provides the geometric framework underlying the parametrization of the family of Poisson structures introduced in Section
3
.
In particular, the closed sections obtained here correspond to the data that determine the family of Poisson brackets in Theorem
3.1
.
The precise relation between these objects will be established in Section
8
.

8
Proof of the Main Theorem

In this section, we present a proof of Theorem
3.1
by using
the characterizations of symplectic sections on the prolongation vanishing in the vertical distribution obtained in Section
7
.
We show that the Poisson brackets induced by these sections on the Lie algebroid have the properties stated in the theorem.
More precisely, we prove that each symplectic section satisfying the conditions of Proposition
7.3

gives rise to a Poisson bracket having semisprays Hamiltonian vector fields.
This establishes the link between the geometric framework developed in Section
7

and the coordinate description introduced in Section
3
.

We begin the proof by choosing a regular Lagrangian
L\in C^{\infty}(A)

and consider the Cartan 2-form
\omega_{L}
(
6.3
) associated with
L
. Let

\Omega=\Theta+\omega_{L},

where
\Theta
is an arbitrary
\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}
-closed horizontal section.
By Proposition
7.3
,
\Omega
is a
\Theta
-parameterized family of symplectic sections
vanishing on the vertical subbundle such that
J(\Omega)=0
.

Let
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\Omega}
be the Poisson bracket (
6.2
) on
A
induced by
\Omega
.
To show that the Hamiltonian vector field of
E_{L}+p^{*}f
,
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, is a semispray,
we shall show that it corresponds to a Hamiltonian section of
\Omega
which is also an SODE.

Consider the Hamiltonian section
\sigma_{E_{L}}
with respect to
\omega_{L}
,

\mathrm{i}_{\sigma_{E_{L}}}\omega_{L}=-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}E_{L}.

Recall from Proposition
6.6
that
\sigma_{L}
is a SODE on
A
.
For
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, let
Z\in\Gamma(\mathscr{A})
be the section given by

\mathrm{i}_{Z}\omega_{L}=-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}(p^{*}f)-\mathrm{i}_{\sigma_{L}}\Theta,

which is well-defined because of
\omega_{L}
is non-degenerate.
Since
\omega_{L}^{\flat}:\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})\to\mathrm{Vert}(\mathscr{A})^{0}
is an isomorphism,
we have that
Z
is a vertical section.
Taking into account that
\sigma_{E_{L}}
is a SODE, the sum
Z+\sigma_{E_{L}}
is again an SODE.
By direct computations, we get

\mathrm{i}_{Z+\sigma_{E_{L}}}\Omega=\mathrm{i}_{\sigma_{E_{L}}}\Theta+\mathrm{i}_{Z+\sigma_{E_{L}}}\omega_{L}=\mathrm{i}_{\sigma_{E_{L}}}\Theta-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}(p^{*}f)-\mathrm{i}_{\sigma_{E_{L}}}\Theta-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}E_{L}=-\mathrm{d}_{\mathscr{A}}(E_{L}+p^{*}f).

Thus,
Z+\sigma_{E_{L}}
is both an SODE on
A
and a Hamiltonian section for
\Omega
.
By Proposition
6.2
, the vector field
\mathcal{V}:=\widehat{\rho}(\sigma_{E_{L}}+Z)
is both a semispray on
A
and
the Hamiltonian vector field of the function the function
E_{L}+p^{*}f
with respect to the Poisson structure
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\Omega}
on
A
induced by
\Omega
.

We conclude the paper with a representative example that illustrates the scope
and geometric content of our main result. In this case, the construction can be
made explicit in local coordinates, revealing the interplay between the Poisson
structure and the induced second-order dynamics.

Example 8.1
.

Let
(M,\Pi)
be a Poisson manifold and consider its cotangent Lie algebroid

A=T^{*}M
(see Example
2.3
). In local coordinates
(U,x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})
on
M
, we write

\Pi=\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{ij}(x)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}.

In adapted coordinates
(x^{i},p_{i})
on
T^{*}M
, the structure functions (
2.2
) are

\displaystyle\rho^{i}_{j}=-\Pi^{ij}(x)
and
\displaystyle C^{k}_{ij}=\frac{\partial\Pi^{ij}}{\partial x^{k}}
.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M
, with inverse
g^{ij}(x)
, and consider the Lagrangian
\displaystyle L\in C^{\infty}(A)
defined by

L(x,p)=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}(x)\,p_{i}p_{j}.

The Lagrangian
L
is regular and coincides with its energy function:

E_{L}=L=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}(x)\,p_{i}p_{j}

Since
\Pi
is Poisson, we can think of
\Pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TM)
as a closed section.
By Lemma
7.4
, we have the horizontal closed section on the prolongation

\Theta=\widehat{p}^{\,*}\Pi=\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{ij}\mathcal{E}^{i}\wedge\mathcal{E}^{i}
.
Using the general construction described in Section 3, we obtaining a family of Poisson brackets on
T^{*}M
, locally given by

\{x^{i},x^{j}\}=0,\quad\{x^{i},p_{k}\}=-\Pi^{ir}g_{rk},\quad\{p_{k},p_{l}\}=-g_{kr}N_{rs}g_{sl},

where the functions
N_{ij}
take the form

N_{ij}=\left(\Pi^{ki}(\Gamma^{j}_{kr}g^{rl}+\Gamma^{l}_{kr}g^{jr})-\Pi^{kj}(\Gamma^{i}_{kr}g^{rl}+\Gamma^{l}_{kr}g^{ir})-g^{kl}\frac{\partial\Pi^{ij}}{\partial x^{k}}\right)p_{l}+\Pi_{ij},

where
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
denote the Christofel symbols of any connection satisfying
\nabla g=0
.
Thus, the Hamiltonian semispray of
E_{L}=L
is given by

\mathcal{V}(x,y)=p_{j}\Pi^{ji}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}+g_{ks}\left(N_{sr}p_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{si}\left(\Gamma^{r}_{ij}g^{jl}+\Gamma^{l}_{ij}g^{rj}\right)p_{r}p_{l}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}}.

As a semispray, the base dynamics of the vector field
\mathcal{V}(x,y)
is entirely determined by the Poisson tensor.
On the other hand, the fiber dynamic depends on the metric, the connection, and on the derivatives of the Poisson tensor.
Specifically, the metric enters in a covariant way while the Poisson tensor contributes through its explicit variation.

If we further assume that the Poisson tensor
\Pi
is

\nabla
-parallel,
then, using the identities

\partial_{k}\Pi^{ij}=-\Gamma^{i}_{kl}\Pi^{lj}-\Gamma^{j}_{kl}\Pi^{il},

one can rewrite the coefficients
N_{ij}
in terms of the Christoffel symbols of
\nabla
.
In this case, the resulting semispray is fully covariant.

This example highlights the geometric nature of our construction
and shows that the existence of Hamiltonian semisprays on Lie algebroids is not merely a formal extension of the tangent bundle case,
but rather a phenomenon intrinsically tied to the interplay between Poisson and algebroid structures.
