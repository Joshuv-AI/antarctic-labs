Introduction

Given an open set
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
, the Gagliardo seminorm of order
s\in[0,1]
is defined
by

\displaystyle|u|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p},&\text{if }s=0,\\
\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,&\text{if }0<s<1,\\
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p},&\text{if }s=1.\end{cases}

Throughout, we adopt the convention that
|u|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\infty
when
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\penalty\ \setminus\penalty\ W^{s,p}(\Omega)
. For
0<\sigma<\eta<s<1
with
\eta=\theta\sigma+(1-\theta)s
with
\theta\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+\eta p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+\theta\sigma p}|x-y|^{d+(1-\theta)sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Applying Hölder’s inequality yields the following well-known interpolation inequality, also recognized as a log-convex inequality

\displaystyle|u|_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d})}\leq|u|^{\theta}_{W^{\sigma,p}(\R^{d})}|u|^{1-\theta}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}.

This estimate also remains true up a constant, for
s=1
or
\sigma=0
. Roughly speaking, this estimate can be genuinely interpreted as a quantitative expression reflecting the interpolation identity
W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d})=\big[W^{\sigma,p}(\R^{d}),\,W^{s,p}(\R^{d})\big]_{\theta,p},

where the right-hand side denotes the real interpolation space via Lions-Peetre
K
-method; see for instance
[
BL76
,
Tri95
,
Lun18
]
. However, the above inequality is not robust, in the sense that it is not consistent as
\eta\to 1^{-}
or
\eta\to 0^{+}
. Indeed, it is a well-known fact that for any sufficiently smooth, non-constant function
u\in L^{p}(\R^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle|u|^{p}_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d})}\xrightarrow{\eta\to 1^{-}}\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad|u|^{p}_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d})}\xrightarrow{\eta\to 0^{+}}\infty.

The overarching goal of this paper is to establish a robust interpolation inequality, ensuring that the blow-up near
\eta=0
and
\eta=1
is avoided. To remedy these two anomalies, Brezis–Bourgain–Mironescu
[
BBM01
]
and Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova
[
MS02
]
showed, respectively, that the factor
\eta(1-\eta)
annihilates the singularities at
\eta=1
and
\eta=0
. More precisely, one has

(1.1)

\displaystyle\eta(1-\eta)|u|^{p}_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d})}\xrightarrow{\eta\to 1^{-}}K_{d,p}\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})},\qquad u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\eta(1-\eta)|u|^{p}_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d})}\xrightarrow{\eta\to 0^{+}}\frac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})},\qquad u\in\bigcup_{0<s<1}W^{s,p}(\R^{d}),

where the constant
K_{d,p}
, see
[
Fog23
]
, is given by

\displaystyle K_{d,p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|w_{d}|^{p}\,d\sigma_{d-1}(w)=\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{d}{2}\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{p+1}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{d+p}{2}\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)}.

The original proof of (
1.2
), i.e., the limit of the fractional seminorm as
\eta\to 0^{+}
due to Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova
[
MS02
]
is quite technical and uses advance tools like robust hardy inequalities. We present in Theorem
4.3
a relatively simple approach asymptotic of the limit as
\eta\to 0^{+}

as well as the proof of (
1.1
), i.e., the asymptotic as
\eta\to 1^{-}
, due to Brezis–Bourgain–Mironescu
[
BBM01
]
.
Note in passing that, the formula of Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova was recently extended to a more general setting in
[
DFGP25
]
.
In view of the aforementioned asymptotics, it is natural to opt for the following renormalized Sobolev seminorm

[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
of order
s\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)},&\text{if }s=0,\\
\displaystyle s(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,&\text{if }0<s<1,\\
\displaystyle K_{d,p}\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)},&\text{if }s=1.\end{cases}

The endpoint
p=\infty
may be viewed heuristically as the limit of
[u]_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}
or
|u|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}
as
p\to\infty
, thereby motivating

\displaystyle|u|_{W^{s,\infty}(\Omega)}=[u]_{W^{s,\infty}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},&\text{if }s=0,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\Omega,\,x\neq y}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}},&\text{if }0<s<1,\\
\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},&\text{if }s=1.\end{cases}

We must add the caveat that our convention for
[u]_{W^{0,p}(\Omega)}
, for a general
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
, does not necessarily reflect the limiting behavior of
[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
as
s\to 0^{+}
.
In fact, while the asymptotic behavior of
[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
as
s\to 1^{-}
is governed by the geometric regularity of
\Omega
, we shall in see Section
4
that the asymptotic behavior of
[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
as
s\to 1^{-}
depends on the geometric shape of
\Omega
.
Our primary motivation for considering the robust interpolation inequality stems from the study of the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the Dirichlet problem associated with the regional fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p,\Omega}
as
s\to 1^{-}
. Therefore, another natural choice for renormalizing the Gagliardo seminorm is to consider the normalization constant associated with the fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}\equiv(-\Delta)^{s}_{p,\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, where the regional operator
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p,\Omega}
is defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p,\Omega}u(x)&:=\widetilde{C}_{d,p,s}\pv\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad s\in(0,1)\\
(-\Delta)^{1}_{p,\Omega}u(x)&:=-\Delta_{p}u(x)=-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u(x)),\qquad\qquad s=1.\end{split}

Here following
[
Fog25
]
, our chosen normalizing constant is

(1.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{d,p,s}=\begin{cases}C_{d,p,s},&\text{if }sp>1,\\
C_{d,2,\frac{sp}{2}},&\text{if }sp\leq 1\end{cases}\quad\text{with}\quad C_{d,p,s}=\frac{s(1-2s)\pi^{\frac{1-d}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{d+sp}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1+sp}{2})\Gamma(p(1-s))\cos(s\pi)}.

Namely, we consider the renormalized seminorm

\displaystyle[u]^{*p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p},&\text{if }s=0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{C}_{d,p,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,&\text{if }0<s<1,\\
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p},&\text{if }s=1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle[u]^{*}_{W^{s,\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=[u]_{W^{s,\infty}(\Omega)}=|u|_{W^{s,\infty}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{ if }p=\infty.

In short, we can write
[u]^{*}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\big(B_{d,p,s}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)},

s\in[0,1]

where

\displaystyle B_{d,p,s}=\frac{\widetilde{C}_{d,p,s}}{2s(1-s)},\qquad B_{d,p,0}=\frac{p}{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{d,p,1}=\frac{p}{K_{d,p}|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}.

As we show in Section
4
, the normalization constant
\widetilde{C}_{d,p,s}
, is chosen so as to ensure that the following properties hold true for
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\R^{d})
.

•

\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s}u}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u}(\xi)
for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\R^{d})
, where
\widehat{u}(\xi)=\int_{\R^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
.

•

(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)\to-\Delta_{p}u(x)\quad
and
\quad[u]^{*}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}\to\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}
as
s\to 1^{-}
.

•

(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)\to|u(x)|^{p-2}u(x)\quad
and
\quad[u]^{*}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}\to\|u\|_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}
as
s\to 0^{+}
.

•

The following asymptotic behaviors hold

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{K_{d,p}C_{d,p,s}}{s(1-s)}=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\Gamma(p+1)C_{d,p,s}}{s(1-s)}=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{2C_{d,2,\frac{sp}{2}}}{s(1-s)}=\frac{2p}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}.

In particular we have
\widetilde{C}_{d,p,s}\asymp s(1-s)
. Others normalizing constants for the fractional
p
-Laplacian are proposed in
[
DGV21
]
and also in
[
DJS25
]
where the need for asymptotic near

p
-Laplacian, is considered.
Our first main result consists of the following robust interpolation inequality.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
and let
0\leq\sigma<\eta<s\leq 1
say
\eta=\theta s+(1-\theta)\sigma
with
\theta=\frac{\eta-\sigma}{s-\sigma}\in(0,1).

Then the following variants of the log-convex interpolation inequalities hold.

\mathbf{(I)}
. (Dimension free constant). For the renormalized seminorm
[\cdot]_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}
we have

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\varkappa N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)\,[u]_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}^{\theta}\cdot[u]_{W^{\sigma,p}(\R^{d})}^{1-\theta},

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{1/p}\varkappa N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)\,[u]_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d}_{+})}^{\theta}\cdot[u]_{W^{\sigma,p}(\R^{d}_{+})}^{1-\theta},

where
\tiny{\varkappa=\begin{cases}2^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{if }d=1,\\[-2.0pt]
1&\text{if }d\geq 2.\end{cases}}
and the dimension-free constant
N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)
is defined by

\displaystyle N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)=2^{\frac{(s-\eta)}{s-\sigma}(\eta+1-\sigma)}\left(\frac{s-\sigma}{\eta-\sigma}\right)^{\frac{\eta-\sigma}{(s-\sigma)p}}\left(\frac{s-\sigma}{s-\eta}\right)^{\frac{s-\eta}{(s-\sigma)p}},

and satisfies
1\leq N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)\leq 2^{1+\frac{1}{p}}
.

\mathbf{(II)}
. (Gagliardo seminorm).
For the Gagliardo seminorm
|\cdot|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}
we have

\displaystyle|u|_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\Upsilon_{d,p}(\sigma,\eta,s)|u|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}^{\theta}\cdot|u|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\theta},

\displaystyle|u|_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{1/p}\Upsilon_{d,p}(\sigma,\eta,s)|u|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d}_{+})}^{\theta}\cdot|u|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\R^{d}_{+})}^{1-\theta}.

where, we have
\Upsilon_{d,\infty}(\sigma,\eta,s)=N_{\infty}(\sigma,\eta,s),
while

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{d,p}(\sigma,\eta,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varkappa N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)}{[\eta(1-\eta)]^{\frac{1}{p}}}\times\begin{cases}\big[(s(1-s))^{\theta}(\sigma(1-\sigma))^{1-\theta}\big]^{\frac{1}{p}},&\sigma\neq 0,\ s\neq 1,\\
\big[(K_{d,p}\tfrac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p})^{\theta}(\sigma(1-\sigma))^{1-\theta}\big]^{\frac{1}{p}},&\sigma\neq 0,\,s=1,\\
\big[(s(1-s))^{\theta}(\tfrac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p})^{1-\theta}\big]^{\frac{1}{p}},&\sigma=0,\,s\neq 1,\\
\big[\tfrac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}K_{d,p}^{\theta}2^{1-\theta}\big]^{\frac{1}{p}},&\sigma=0,\,s=1.\end{cases}

\mathbf{(III)}
. (Renormalized seminorm). For the renormalized seminorm
[\cdot]^{*}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}
we have

\displaystyle[u]^{*}_{W^{\eta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\Upsilon^{*}_{d,p}(\sigma,\eta,s)[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{*\theta}[u]_{W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{*\,(1-\theta)},

\displaystyle[u]^{*}_{W^{\eta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{1/p}\Upsilon^{*}_{d,p}(\sigma,\eta,s)[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}^{*\theta}[u]_{W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}^{*\,(1-\theta)},

where, we have
\Upsilon^{*}_{d,\infty}(\sigma,\eta,s)=N_{\infty}(\sigma,\eta,s),
while

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{*}_{d,p}(\sigma,\eta,s)=\varkappa N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)\Big(\frac{B_{d,p,\eta}}{B_{d,p,\sigma}^{\theta}B_{d,p,s}^{1-\theta}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2^{1+\frac{2}{p}}\frac{B_{\max}}{B_{\min}},

with
B_{\max}=\max_{s\in[0,1]}B_{d,p,s}
and
B_{\min}=\min_{s\in[0,1]}B_{d,p,s}
.

It worth mentioning that, the cactus case
p=2
is particularly amenable to analysis using the Fourier transform. As obtained in Theorem
5.9
, we get
\Upsilon^{*}_{d,2}(\sigma,\eta,s)=1
, namely we have the following sharper estimate

\displaystyle[u]^{*}_{H^{\eta}(\R^{d})}\leq[u]^{*\,\theta}_{H^{s}(\R^{d})}\cdot[u]^{*\,(1-\theta)}_{H^{\sigma}(\R^{d})}.

However, the resulting constant
N_{2}(\sigma,\eta,s)
obtained with this method is less sharp when the dimension is large. Indeed for the seminorms
[\cdot]_{H^{t}(\R^{d})}
, we deduce

\displaystyle[u]_{H^{\eta}(\R^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq N_{2}(\sigma,\eta,s)[u]_{H^{s}(\R^{d})}^{\theta}\cdot[u]_{H^{\sigma}(\R^{d})}^{1-\theta}

where
N_{2}(\sigma,\eta,s)=\big(\tfrac{B_{d,2,\sigma}^{\theta}B_{d,2,s}^{1-\theta}}{B_{d,2,\eta}}\big)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{2d}.

It is worth emphasizing that, rather than interpolating norms, our interpolation inequality applies only to seminorms. More generally, inequalities of this type are known as Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities. We refer the interested reader to
[
BM18

for a thorough and comprehensive treatment of these inequalities. The robust log-convex inequality in the case

and

[

and the non-robust ones can be found in
[

.
Although we did not set out to address the open problem posed in
[

The key tool for establishing the robust interpolation inequality in Theorem
1.1
is the decomposition of the Gagliardo seminorm into short-range and long-range interactions. To this end, let us observe that passing through polar coordinates yields

\displaystyle\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}h\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
U:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is the averaged
L^{p}
-modulus of continuity of
u
,

\displaystyle U(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\R^{d}}|u(x+t\omega)-u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(\omega).

Definition 1.2

(Short and long range interaction)
.

We introduce the short-range (local-range) interaction
L_{p}(\cdot,u,r):[0,1]\to[0,\infty]
for
0\leq s<1
by

\displaystyle L_{p}(s,u,r)

\displaystyle:=\frac{(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(2r)^{(1-s)}}\Big(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(2r)^{(1-s)}}\Big(\iint_{\R^{d}\times B(x,r)}\hskip-4.30554pt\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The long-range (or nonlocal-range) interaction
T_{p}(\cdot,u,r):[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
also called the fractional tail is defined for
0<s\leq 1
by

\displaystyle T_{p}(s,u,r)

\displaystyle:=s^{\frac{1}{p}}r^{s}\Big(\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=s^{\frac{1}{p}}r^{s}\Big(\iint_{\R^{d}\times B^{c}(x,r)}\hskip-4.30554pt\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The definition of the endpoint cases
L_{p}(1,u,r)
and
T_{p}(0,u,r)
are fully justified by Theorem
4.1
, which establishes the following asymptotic limits

\displaystyle L_{p}(1,u,r)=\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}L_{p}(s,u,r)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}K_{d,p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}=[u]_{W^{1,p}(\R^{d})},

\displaystyle T_{p}(0,u,r)=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}T_{p}(s,u,r)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}=[u]_{W^{0,p}(\R^{d})}.

By heuristically taking the limit as
p\to\infty
, we analogously define the quantities

\displaystyle L_{\infty}(s,u,r)

\displaystyle:=(2r)^{-(1-s)}\hskip-8.61108pt\sup_{0<|x-y|\leq r}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\quad\qquad(0\leq s<1),

\displaystyle T_{\infty}(s,u,r)

\displaystyle:=r^{s}\sup_{|x-y|\geq r}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\quad\qquad\quad(0<s\leq 1),

\displaystyle L_{\infty}(1,u,r)

\displaystyle:=[u]_{W^{1,\infty}(\R^{d})},\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{\infty}(0,u,r):=[u]_{W^{0,\infty}(\R^{d})}.

The short-range interaction
L_{p}(s,u,r)
encodes the local (and hence the regularity of
u
) contribution to the fractional seminorm
|u|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}
by capturing the behavior of
u
at short scales inside the ball
B(0,r)
.
The long-range interaction
T_{p}(s,u,r)
encodes the non-local contribution to the fractional seminorm by capturing the behavior of
u
at long scales outside the ball
B(0,r)
. In other words
T_{p}(s,u,r)
captures the nonlocal effect of
|u|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}
.
The cornerstone of our approach to Theorem
1.1
is the following monotonicity result, which stands as our second main result.

Theorem 1.3

(Monotonicity of short and long range interaction)
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\R^{d})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
r>0
. The following assertions are true.

(1)

The map
s\mapsto L_{p}(s,u,r)
is continuous and increasing. Namely, we have

\displaystyle L_{p}(\eta,u,r)\leq L_{p}(s,u,r)\qquad\text{for \,\, $0\leq\eta<s\leq 1$}.

In fact for
p=\infty
we have the better estimate
2^{-\eta}L_{\infty}(\eta,u,r)\leq 2^{-s}L_{\infty}(s,u,r).

(2)

The map
s\mapsto T_{p}(s,u,r)
is continuous and for
0\leq\sigma<\eta\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle T_{p}(\eta,u,r)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\frac{1}{p}}\,\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}r^{\sigma}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+\sigma p}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=2^{1+\frac{1}{p}}r^{\sigma}(1-\sigma)^{-\frac{1}{p}}[u]_{W^{\sigma,p}(\R^{d})}.

(3)

If
d\geq 2
then the map
s\mapsto T_{p}(s,u,r)
is
2
-almost decreasing, that is,

\displaystyle T_{p}(\eta,u,r)\leq 2T_{p}(\sigma,u,r)\qquad\text{ for }\quad 0\leq\sigma<\eta\leq 1.

In fact, this estimate remains true provided that the following condition holds:

(G)

\displaystyle d\geq 2\quad\text{or}\quad\sigma=0\quad\text{or}\quad p=2\quad\text{or}\quad p=\infty.

2.
Chebyshev-type integral inequalities

The classical Chebyshev inequality
[
Paf82
]
, along with its historical development and key contributions, is thoroughly discussed in
[
MPF93
, Chapter X]
and
[
MV75
,
MP90
]
. Necessary and sufficient conditions for the validity of Chebyshev’s inequalities are established in
[
Shi11
]
. In probability theory, Chebyshev’s integral inequality is frequently referred to as the correlation inequality (see for instance Corollary
2.3
), reflecting the fact that comonotone functions of a random variable are always positively correlated. Beyond this, the inequality has broad applications across analysis, statistics, and information theory, where it underpins deviation bounds, the weak law of large numbers, and moment estimates.

2.1.
General Chebyshev-type integral inequalities

In this section,
f,g\in L^{1}(X)
are assumed to be two integrable functions on measure space
(X,\mu)
with a finite positive measure, i.e.,
0<\mu(X)<\infty
.

Theorem 2.1

(Chebyshev’s inequality)
.

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
with
0<\mu(X)<\infty
.

If
f
and
g
are
synchronous
(also called
comonotone
), that is,

\displaystyle(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))\geq 0,\quad\text{for all }x,y\in X,

then the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\geq\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\int_{X}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\int_{X}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

Equivalently, if
f
and
g
are
asynchronous
(also called
countermonotone
), that is,

\displaystyle(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))\leq 0,\quad\text{for all }x,y\in X,

then the reverse inequality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\int_{X}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\int_{X}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

Proof.

We only establish the first claim. Integrating

\displaystyle 0\leq(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))=f(x)g(x)+f(y)g(y)-f(x)g(y)-f(y)g(x)

over
X\times X
yields desired estimate since

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq 2\mu(X)\int_{X}f(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-2\int_{X}g(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\int_{X}f(x)\ d\mu(x).

∎

Remark 2.2
.

It is somewhat counterintuitive that comonotonicity for functions on
X\subset\R
does not necessarily imply that
f
and
g
are individually monotone. Rather, it means that
f
and
g
vary in the same direction–either both increasing or both decreasing–over the same subsets of
X
. In other words, the two functions move in sync. Consequently, the term synchronous functions may be more intuitive in many contexts, as it directly emphasizes tandem-movement rather than individual monotonicity. For example, for any function
k:\R\to\R
, the functions
f(x)=k^{2}(x)
and
g(x)=k^{4}(x)
share the same monotonicity on the same sets.

In probability space becomes Theorem
2.1
the following.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}:\Omega\to\R
be a random variable on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and
f,g:\R\to\R
be measurable functions such that
f(\mathbf{Z})
and
g(\mathbf{Z})
are integrable.

If
f
and
g
are synchronous, then the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{Z})g(\mathbf{Z})]\geq\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{Z})]\cdot\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{Z})].

If
f
and
g
are asynchronous, then the reverse inequality holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{Z})g(\mathbf{Z})]\leq\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{Z})]\cdot\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{Z})].

Next, we establish several Chebyshev-type integral inequalities with explicit constants, under relaxed variants of comonotonicity.

Definition 2.4

((b,c)-Synchronicity)
.

Consider two functions
f,g:X\to\R
,
b>0
and
c>0
. We say that the pair
(f,g)
is
(b,c)
-
synchronous
if

\displaystyle(f(x)-bf(y))(g(x)-cg(y))\geq 0\quad\text{for all }x,y\in X.

We say that
(f,g)
is
(b,c)
-
asynchronous
if

\displaystyle(f(x)-bf(y))(g(x)-cg(y))\leq 0\quad\text{for all }x,y\in X.

The result below generalizes the classical Chebyshev inequality, Theorem
2.1
; the proof is analogous and omitted for brevity.

Theorem 2.5

(Chebyshev’s inequality I)
.

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
and
b>0
and
c>0
.

If
f
and
g
are
(b,c)
-
synchronous
then

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\geq\frac{b+c}{1+bc}\cdot\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\Big(\int_{X}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)\Big(\int_{X}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big).

If
f
and
g
are
(b,c)
-
asynchronous
then

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\frac{b+c}{1+bc}\cdot\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\Big(\int_{X}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)\Big(\int_{X}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big).

In particular, if
b=1
or
c=1
one gets the Chebyshev inequality from Theorem
2.1
.

Definition 2.6

(Weak (b,c)-Synchronicity)
.

Consider two functions
f,g:X\to\R
,
b>0
and
c>0
. We say that the pair
(f,g)
is
weakly
(b,c)
-synchronous
(or
weakly
(b,c)
-comonotone
) if, for all
x,y\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\big(f(x)\leq bf(y)\quad\text{and}\quad g(x)\leq cg(y)\big)\quad\text{or}\quad\big(f(y)\leq bf(x)\quad\text{and}\quad g(y)\leq cg(x)\big).

Equivalently, there holds if for all
x,y\in X
,

\displaystyle(f(x)-bf(y))(g(x)-cg(y))\geq 0\quad\text{or}\quad(f(x)-b^{-1}f(y))(g(x)-c^{-1}g(y))\geq 0.

Similarly, we say that
(f,g)
is
weakly
(b,c)
-asynchronous
(or
weakly
(b,c)
-countermonotone
) if, for all
x,y\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\big(f(x)\leq bf(y)\quad\text{and}\quad cg(x)\geq g(y)\big)\quad\text{or}\quad\big(f(y)\leq bf(x)\quad\text{and}\quad cg(y)\geq g(x)\big).

Equivalently, there holds if for all
x,y\in X
,

\displaystyle(f(x)-bf(y))(g(x)-c^{-1}g(y))\leq 0\quad\text{or}\quad(f(x)-b^{-1}f(y))(g(x)-cg(y))\leq 0.

Remark 2.7
.

It is easy to verify that if
(f,g)
is
(b,c)
-synchronous (resp.
(b,c)
-asynchronous), then it is also weakly
(b,c)
-synchronous (resp. weakly
(b,c)
-asynchronous). The converse does not generally hold.
However, in the special case when
b=c=1
, all notions of synchrony and asynchrony coincide. That is,
f
and
g
are synchronous (resp. asynchronous) if and only if
(f,g)
is
(1,1)
-synchronous (resp.
(1,1)
-asynchronous), if and only if
(f,g)
is weakly
(1,1)
-synchronous (resp. weakly
(1,1)
-asynchronous).

Theorem 2.8

(Chebyshev’s inequality II)
.

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
with
f\geq 0
and
g\geq 0
. Let
b>0
and
c>0
. Define
b_{*}=\max(b,\tfrac{1}{b})
and
c_{*}=\max(c,\tfrac{1}{c})
.

If
(f,g)
is weakly
(b,c)
-synchronous then we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(x)g(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\geq\frac{b^{-1}_{*}+c^{-1}_{*}}{1+b_{*}c_{*}}\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\Big(\int_{X}g(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)\Big(\int_{X}f(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big).

If
(f,g)
is weakly
(b,c)
-asynchronous then we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(x)g(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\frac{b_{*}+c_{*}}{1+b^{-1}_{*}c^{-1}_{*}}\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\Big(\int_{X}g(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)\Big(\int_{X}f(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big).

The special case
b=c=1
, implies the classical Chebyshev inequality.

Proof.

Assume
(f,g)
is weakly
(b,c)
-asynchronous. For fixed
x,y\in X
, there are two possible cases. If
f(x)\leq bf(y)
and
cg(x)\geq g(y)
that is
(g(x)-c^{-1}g(y))(f(x)-bf(y))\leq 0,

then we have

\displaystyle g(x)f(x)+bc^{-1}g(y)f(y)\leq bg(x)f(y)+c^{-1}g(y)f(x).

If
f(y)\leq bf(x)
and
cg(y)\geq g(x)
then similarly we derive

\displaystyle g(x)f(x)+b^{-1}cg(y)f(y)\leq b^{-1}g(x)f(y)+cg(y)f(x).

Since
f\geq 0
and
g\geq 0
, each case implies the inequality

\displaystyle g(x)f(x)+(b_{*}\,c_{*})^{-1}g(y)f(y)\leq b_{*}g(x)f(y)+c_{*}g(y)f(x).

Integrating both sides with respect to
x
and
y
over
X\times X
implies

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(x)g(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\frac{b_{*}+c_{*}}{1+b^{-1}_{*}c^{-1}_{*}}\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\Big(\int_{X}g(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)\Big(\int_{X}f(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big).

Analogously, if
(f,g)
is weakly
(b,c)
-synchronous then we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}f(x)g(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\geq\frac{b^{-1}_{*}+c^{-1}_{*}}{1+b_{*}c_{*}}\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\Big(\int_{X}g(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)\Big(\int_{X}f(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big).

∎

Our purpose here is to analyze the situation where

Definition 2.9
.

Let

\displaystyle x\leq y\implies f(y)\leq bf(x)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in X.

We say that
(f,g)

(b,c)

We say that
(f,g)

(b,c)
-negatively monotone

-increasing and

Example 2.10
.

In general for a bounded function

Remark 2.11

(I)

(Chebyshev’s inequality III)

(Chebyshev’s inequality IV)

(b,c)
-monotone, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}f(t)g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq ar^{-a}\,\frac{b+c}{1+b^{-1}c^{-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{r}f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big),

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}f(t)g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq ar^{a}\,\frac{b+c}{1+b^{-1}c^{-1}}\Big(\int_{r}^{\infty}f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big).

Proof.

It is sufficient to apply Theorem
2.12
on measure spaces

(X=(0,r),\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(t)=t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t)
and

(X=(r,\infty),\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(t)=t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t)
.
∎

Definition 2.14
.

Let
g:X\to(0,\infty)
be a function,
X\subset\R
and
c>1
. We say that
g
is called

c
-adically decreasing
if

\displaystyle g(ct)\leq g(t)\quad\text{for all }t\in X\text{ such that }ct\in X.

Likewise,
g
is called
c
-adically increasing
if

\displaystyle g(ct)\geq g(t)\quad\text{for all }t\in X\text{ such that }ct\in X.

Example 2.15
.

Here are some non-trivial examples of
c
-adic monotone functions.

(1)

For
\tau>0
, function
g(t)=\frac{|\sin(t)|^{\tau}}{t^{\tau}}
,
t\in\R
dydically increasing but not monotone.

(2)

The function
g(t)=x^{\tau}\sin^{2}(\log_{c}(t)\pi)
with
\tau\in\R
and
c>1
is
c-
adically monotone but not monotone.

(3)

In general, let
f:X\to\R
be monotone. If
\phi:X\to[0,\infty)
is
c
-adically monotone with the same monotonicity, so is the function
g(t)=\phi(t)f(t)
.

Theorem 2.16

(Chebyshev’s inequality V)
.

Let
a,r\in(0,\infty)
and
b,c\in(1,\infty)
.
Let
f,g:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be two measurable functions. Assume
g
is
c
-adically decreasing.
If
f
is
b
-almost decreasing, then the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}g(t)f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a(cr)^{-a}}{b^{3}}\Big(\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{cr}f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big),

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a(cr)^{a}}{b^{3}}\Big(\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int^{\infty}_{cr}f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big).

If
f
is
b
-almost increasing then the estimates are reversed as follows

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}g(t)f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq a(cr)^{-a}b^{3}\Big(\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{cr}f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big),

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq a(cr)^{a}b^{3}\Big(\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int^{\infty}_{cr}f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big).

Moreover, if
g
is merely decreasing then one can simply replace
b^{3}
by
b
.

Proof.

Note that, if
g
is monotone then the sought inequalities follow immediately from Theorem
2.13
. Due to scaling invariance, it is enough to consider the case
r=1
. For a function
w:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, we use the following
c
-adic decomposition

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}w(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{c^{-(n+1)}}^{c^{-n}}t^{a-1}w(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\tfrac{1}{c}}^{1}\tau^{a-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}w(c^{-n}\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-a-1}w(t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{c^{n}}^{c^{n+1}}t^{-a-1}w(t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{1}^{c}\tau^{-a-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}w(c^{n}\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Since
f
is
b
decreasing, we have

b^{-1}f(c^{-n})\leq f(c^{-n}\tau)\leq bf(c^{-n-1})
for
\tau\in(\tfrac{1}{c},1)
,
while
b^{-1}f(c^{n+1})\leq f(c^{n}\tau)\leq bf(c^{n})
for
\tau\in(1,c)
.
Therefore using again monotonicity and the formulas
\int_{c^{-k}}^{c^{-(k-1)}}t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{c^{k-1}}^{c^{k}}t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{c^{a}-1}{ac^{ak}},

k\in\{n,n+1\}

we find that

\displaystyle\frac{ab^{-2}}{c^{a}-1}\int_{c^{-n}}^{c^{-(n-1)}}\hskip-4.30554ptf(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c^{an}}f(c^{-n}\tau)\leq\frac{ab^{2}c^{2a}}{c^{a}-1}\int_{c^{-n-2}}^{c^{-n-1}}\hskip-4.30554ptf(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\,\,(\tau\in(\tfrac{1}{c},1)),

\displaystyle\frac{ab^{-2}c^{2a}}{c^{a}-1}\int_{c^{n+1}}^{c^{n+2}}\hskip-4.30554ptf(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c^{an}}f(c^{n}\tau)\leq\frac{ab^{2}}{c^{a}-1}\int_{c^{n-1}}^{c^{n}}\hskip-4.30554ptf(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\,\,\qquad(\tau\in(1,c)).

Hence it follows that

\displaystyle b^{-2}\int_{0}^{c}f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{c^{a}-1}{a}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{f(c^{-n}\tau)}{c^{an}}\leq c^{2a}b^{2}\int_{0}^{c^{-1}}\hskip-4.30554ptf(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\,\,(\tau\in(\tfrac{1}{c},1)),

\displaystyle c^{2a}b^{-2}\int_{c}^{\infty}\hskip-4.30554ptf(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{c^{a}-1}{a}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{f(c^{n}\tau)}{c^{an}}\leq b^{2}\int_{c^{-1}}^{\infty}\hskip-4.30554ptf(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad(\tau\in(1,c)).

Since
f
is
b
-almost decreasing and
g(ct)\leq g(t)
, if we fix
\tau>0
,
then the sequence
(g(c^{-n}\tau))_{n}
is increasing and
(f(c^{-n}\tau))_{n}

b
-almost increasing,
while the sequence
(g(c^{n}\tau))_{n}
is decreasing and
(f(c^{n}\tau))_{n}
is
b
-almost decreasing.
Whence, applying Theorem
2.8
for discrete sums, with weights
\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{c^{an}}
and using

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}=\frac{c^{a}}{c^{a}-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}g(c^{-n}\tau)f(c^{-n}\tau)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c^{a}-1}{bc^{a}}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}g(c^{-n}\tau)\Big)\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}f(c^{-n}\tau)\Big),

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}g(c^{n}\tau)f(c^{n}\tau)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c^{a}-1}{bc^{a}}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}g(c^{n}\tau)\Big)\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}f(c^{n}\tau)\Big).

By exploiting these estimates inn conjunction with the
c
-adic decomposition we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g(t)f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c^{a}-1}{bc^{a}}\int_{\tfrac{1}{c}}^{1}\tau^{a-1}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}g(c^{-n}\tau)\Big)\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}f(c^{-n}\tau)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a}{b^{3}c^{a}}\int_{\tfrac{1}{c}}^{1}\tau^{a-1}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}g(c^{-n}\tau)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\Big(\int_{0}^{c}f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{b^{3}c^{a}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{c}f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big),

while, similarly, for the second case we get

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c^{a}-1}{b^{3}c^{a}}\int_{1}^{c}\tau^{-a-1}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}g(c^{n}\tau)\Big)\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}f(c^{n}\tau)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\geq\frac{ac^{a}}{b^{3}}\int_{1}^{c}\tau^{-a-1}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c^{an}}g(c^{n}\tau)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\Big(\int_{c}^{\infty}f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{ac^{a}}{b^{3}}\Big(\int_{1}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int_{c}^{\infty}f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big).

∎

Theorem 2.17

(Chebyshev’s inequality V
′
)
.

Let
a,r\in(0,\infty)
and
b,c\in(1,\infty)
.
Let
f,g:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be two measurable functions. Assume
g
is
c
-adically increasing

If
f
is
b
-almost increasing, then the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}g(t)f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a(r/c)^{-a}}{b^{3}}\Big(\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{r/c}f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big),

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a(r/c)^{a}}{b^{3}}\Big(\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int^{\infty}_{r/c}f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big).

If

\displaystyle\leq a(r/c)^{-a}b^{3}\Big(\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{r/c}f(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big),

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq a(r/c)^{a}b^{3}\Big(\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big(\int^{\infty}_{r/c}f(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big).

by

.

Proof.

Since
\tilde{g}(t)=g(1/t)
is

-adically decreasing, apply Theorem

for

and the function

Remark 2.18
.

Chebyshev-type inequalities similar to those in Theorem

and Theorem

can also be obtained in more complex settings, where the function

is simultaneously
c
-adically and

-almost monotone.
For instance, assume that
g:X\to[0,\infty)
is both
c
-adically decreasing and
b
-almost decreasing, meaning that

for all

Setting
q=\frac{\log b}{\log c},

so that

2.3.
Mononicity of singular integrals

In this section, we investigate various monotonicity properties involving integral operators. In the next result, we shall see that Chebyshev’s inequality surprisingly yields consequences for families of density functions with the Monotone Likelihood Ratio (MLR) property, a concept of genuine significance in statistics and probability theory. The study of the monotone likelihood ratio property dates back at least to the foundational statistical work by Karlin & Rubin
[
KR56
]
; a comprehensive exposition can be found in Lehmann & Romano
[
LR21
]
.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space with
X\subset\R
and
I\subset\R
be a nonempty set of indices. Let
b\geq 1
and
c\geq 1
. Assume
(p_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
is a family of probability densities on
X
with
b
-almost monotone likelihood ratio property in
x
, that is, for each
\alpha<\beta
the ratio mapping
x\mapsto g_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\frac{p_{\beta}(x)}{p_{\alpha}(x)}
is
b
-almost increasing on
X
. Let
f,g:X\to[0,\infty]
be such that
f
is
c
-almost increasing and
g
is
c
-almost decreasing. Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{X}f(x)p_{\beta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)&\geq\frac{b^{-1}+c^{-1}}{1+bc}\int_{X}f(x)p_{\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\\
\int_{X}g(x)p_{\beta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)&\leq\frac{b+c}{1+b^{-1}c^{-1}}\int_{X}g(x)p_{\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\end{split}

\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta\in I,\,\,\alpha<\beta).

In other words, if
\mathbf{X}_{\alpha}
is a random variable with law
p_{\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
then the mappings
\alpha\mapsto\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{X}_{\alpha})]
and
\alpha\mapsto\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X}_{\alpha})]
are almost monotone on
I
and we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{X}_{\beta})]&\geq\frac{b^{-1}+c^{-1}}{1+bc}\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{X}_{\alpha})]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X}_{\beta})]\leq\frac{b+c}{1+b^{-1}c^{-1}}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X}_{\alpha})]\end{split}

\displaystyle(\alpha<\beta).

Analogously, if
x\mapsto g_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\frac{p_{\beta}(x)}{p_{\alpha}(x)}

is
b
-almost decreasing, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X}_{\beta})]&\geq\frac{b^{-1}+c^{-1}}{1+bc}\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X}_{\alpha})]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{X}_{\beta})]\leq\frac{b+c}{1+b^{-1}c^{-1}}\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{X}_{\alpha})]\end{split}

\displaystyle(\alpha<\beta).

Proof.

The result follows by applying Theorem
2.12
to the couples
(f,g_{\alpha,\beta})
and
(g,g_{\alpha,\beta})
with respect to the measure
\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}(x)=p_{\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
; noting that

\displaystyle\int_{X}p_{\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=1=\int_{X}p_{\beta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\int_{X}g_{\alpha,\beta}(x)p_{\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

∎

The next result is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.19
and shows that the monotone likelihood ratio property implies the monotonicity of the expectations.

Theorem 2.20

(Monotone Likelihood Ratio)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space with
X\subset\R
and
I\subset\R
be a nonempty set. Assume
(p_{a})_{a\in I}
is a family of probability densities on
X
with monotone likelihood ratio in
x
, that is, for each
a<b
the ratio mapping
x\mapsto\frac{p_{b}(x)}{p_{a}(x)}
is increasing.
Let
c\geq 1
and
f,g:X\to[0,\infty]
be such that
f
is
c
-almost increasing and
g
is
c
-almost decreasing. Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{X}f(x)p_{a}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)&\leq c\int_{X}f(x)p_{b}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\\
\int_{X}g(x)p_{b}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)&\leq c\int_{X}g(x)p_{a}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\end{split}

\displaystyle(a,b\in I,\,\,a<b).

In other words, if
\mathbf{X}_{a}
is a random variable with law
p_{a}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
then the mappings
a\mapsto\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{X}_{a})]
and
a\mapsto\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X}_{a})]
is
c
-almost monotone on
I
, and we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{X}_{a})]\leq c\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{X}_{b})]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X}_{b})]\leq c\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X}_{a})]

\displaystyle(a,b\in I,\,\,a<b).

Proof.

The claim follows by taking
b=1
in Theorem
2.19
. For the special case
c=1
, see the different approach in
[
LR21
, Chapter 3]
.
∎

The next lemma provides an Abelian-type averaging result, establishing the limiting behavior of some singular integrals near
0
and
\infty
.

Lemma 2.21

(Averaging near
0
and
\infty
)
.

Let
r>0
and
g\in L^{q}_{\loc}((0,\infty))
, with
1<q\leq\infty
,
g:(0,\infty)\to\R
.
If
\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}g(t)=g(0)
exists in
\R\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
, then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}a\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=g(0).

If
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}g(t)=g(\infty)
exists in
\R\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
, then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}a\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=g(\infty).

Proof.

Since
q\in(1,\infty]
, we put
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}\in[1,\infty)
. Let us start with the cases where
g(0)
and
g(\infty)
are finite.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
0<\delta<r
such that
|g(t)-g(0)|<\varepsilon
for all
t\in(0,\delta).
Then for
a>0
Hölder inequality implies

\displaystyle a\Big|\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\frac{g(0)}{a}r^{a}\Big|=a\Big|\int_{0}^{r}(g(t)-g(0))t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(a\int_{0}^{\delta}+a\int_{\delta}^{r}\Big)|g(t)-g(0)|t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\delta^{a}+\|g-g(0)\|_{L^{q}((\delta,r))}\Big(\frac{a(r^{(a-1)q^{\prime}+1}-\delta^{(a-1)q^{\prime}+1})}{(a-1)q^{\prime}+1}\Big)^{1/q^{\prime}}.

Letting
a\to 0^{+}
, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{a\to 0^{+}}\Big|a\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t-g(0)r^{a}\Big|\leq\varepsilon.

Thus, using again the fact that
r^{a}\to 1
, we conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}a\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=g(0).

Analogously, given
\varepsilon>0
, if
g(t)\to g(\infty)
as
t\to\infty
, so there exists
R>r
such that

|g(t)-g(\infty)|<\varepsilon
for all
t>R.
Then for
a>0
Hölder inequality implies

\displaystyle a\Big|\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\frac{g(\infty)}{a}r^{-a}\Big|=a\Big|\int_{r}^{\infty}(g(t)-g(\infty))t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(a\int_{r}^{R}+a\int_{R}^{\infty}\Big)|g(t)-g(\infty)|t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\|g-g(\infty)\|_{L^{q}((r,R))}\Big(\frac{a\big(r^{(-a-1)q^{\prime}+1}-R^{(-a-1)q^{\prime}+1}\big)}{(a+1)q^{\prime}-1}\Big)^{1/q^{\prime}}+\varepsilon R^{-a}.

Letting
a\to 0^{+}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{a\to 0^{+}}\Big|a\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t-g(\infty)r^{-a}\Big|\leq\varepsilon.

Hence, since
r^{-a}\to 1
, we conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}a\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=g(\infty).

Last upon replacing
g
with
-g
, it is sufficient to deal with cases
g(0)=\infty
and
g(\infty)=\infty
. As previously we can choose
0<\delta<r
and
R>r
such that

\displaystyle g(t)\geq 1/\varepsilon\quad\text{for all $0<t<\delta$},\quad\text{resp.}\quad g(t)\geq 1/\varepsilon\quad\text{for all $t>R$}.

By splitting the integrals as previously, the following estimates hold respectively

\displaystyle a\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta^{a}}{\varepsilon}-a\|g\|_{L^{q}((\delta,r))}\Big(\frac{r^{(a-1)q^{\prime}+1}-\delta^{(a-1)q^{\prime}+1}}{(a-1)q^{\prime}+1}\Big)^{1/q^{\prime}},

\displaystyle a\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{R^{-a}}{\varepsilon}-a\|g\|_{L^{q}((r,R))}\Big(\frac{r^{(-a-1)q^{\prime}+1}-R^{(-a-1)q^{\prime}+1}}{(-a-1)q^{\prime}+1}\Big)^{1/q^{\prime}}.

From these, we deduce

\displaystyle\liminf_{a\to 0^{+}}a\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon},\quad\text{while}\quad\liminf_{a\to 0^{+}}a\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude:

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}a\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=g(0)=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}a\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=g(\infty)=\infty.

∎

Motivated by Lemma
2.21
can introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.22
.

Given
r>0
and a measurable function

we define the function
T,L:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
with

\displaystyle L(z)

\displaystyle=zr^{-z}\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{z-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\text{with}\qquad L(0)=g(0),

\displaystyle T(z)

\displaystyle=zr^{z}\int_{r}^{\infty}g(t)t^{-z-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\text{with}\qquad T(0)=g(\infty).

The conventions
L(0)=g(0)
and
T(0)=g(\infty)
are justified by Lemma
2.21
.

Theorem 2.23

(Monotonicity of singular integrals I)
.

Let
r>0
and
g:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
be measurable. If
g
is almost monotone, then so are
L(z)
and
T(z)
defined in Definition
2.22
. Namely if
g
is
c
-almost increasing,
c\geq 1
, then we have

\displaystyle L(a)\leq cL(b)\qquad\text{and}\qquad T(b)\leq cT(a)

\displaystyle 0\leq a\leq b.

\displaystyle 0\leq a\leq b.

Proof.

One can either apply Theorem

Theorem 2.24

(Monotonicity of singular integrals II)
.

If a measurable function
g:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is

-adically monotone (

), then the maps

and

\displaystyle 0\leq a\leq b.

since

3.1.
Funk–Radon transform

We will make use of the spherical slice transform identity, via the so called Funk–Radon transform introduced by Paul Funk in
[
Fun11
,
Fun13
]
, and which has since undergone significant development; see, for instance,
[
Rub15
,
Que20b
,
Que20a
]
. The Funk–Radon transform (also known as the Minkowski–Funk or spherical Radon transform) is an integral operator is defined in its normalized form for a function
g:\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to\R
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}g(v):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{v}}g(w)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-2}(w)}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-2}|}\qquad(v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}),

where
\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{v}=\{w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\langle v,w\rangle=0\}
denotes the great subsphere orthogonal to
v
.
The following result is likely well-known in the literature; however, as we were unable to find a precise reference, we provide a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem 3.1

(Slicing method)
.

For
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with
d\geq 2
there holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\hskip-4.30554ptg(w)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\hskip-4.30554pt\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{v}}g(w)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-2}(w)}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-2}|}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(v)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\hskip-4.30554pt\mathbf{F}g(v)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(v).

Proof.

it suffices to prove the claim for indicator functions

g=\mathds{1}_{A}
, where
A\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is a Borel measurable set. This can be done by considering the measure defined by

\displaystyle\mu(A)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{d-2}(A\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{v})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(v)}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-2}|}.

Given that the Hausdorff measures
\sigma_{d-1}
and
\sigma_{d-2}
are respectively rotation invariant, so does the measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Indeed, for each
v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
O\in\mathcal{O}(d)
, an orthogonal mapping on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, since
O^{\top}O=I_{d}
, one finds that

\displaystyle O(A)\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{v}=O(A\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{O^{\top}(v)}).

The rotation invariance of
\sigma_{d-2}
implies

\displaystyle\sigma_{d-2}(O(A)\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{v})=\sigma_{d-2}(O(A\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{O^{\top}(v)}))=\sigma_{d-2}(A\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{O^{\top}(v)}).

The rotation invariance of
\sigma_{d-1}
yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{d-2}(O(A)\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{v})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(v)}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-2}|}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{d-2}(A\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{O^{\top}(v)})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(v)}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-2}|}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sigma_{d-2}(A\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{v})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(O(v))}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-2}|},

in other words
\mu(O(A))=\mu(A)
. It follows that,
\mu=c\sigma_{d-1}
since rotation invariant Borel measures on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
are unique up to a multiplicative constant. On the other hand, once again the rotation invariance of
\sigma_{d-2}
implies that
\sigma_{d-2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-2}_{v})=\sigma_{d-2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-2})
so that
\mu(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})=\sigma_{d-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})=|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|
, yielding
\mu=\sigma_{d-1}
.
∎

\|\nabla u(\cdot+\tau t\omega)-\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}\leq 2^{p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}
and

\|\nabla u(\cdot+\tau t\omega)-\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}\to 0
as
t\to 0
by the continuity of the shift operator in
L^{p}(\R^{d})
. Next let
\varepsilon>0
and consider
t_{0}>0
such that
\big|\frac{U(t)}{t^{p}}-g(\infty)\big|<\varepsilon
for all
0<t<t_{0}
. For all
0<t<t_{0}
we find that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{(p+1)}{t^{p}}AU(t)-g(\infty)\Big|=\frac{(p+1)}{t^{p+1}}\int_{0}^{t}z^{p}\Big|\frac{U(z)}{z^{p}}-g(\infty)\Big|\,\mathrm{d}z\leq(p+1)\varepsilon.

We conclude that
(p+1)\frac{AU(t)}{t^{p}}\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}g(\infty)
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The approach used in Lemma
3.2
strongly relies on the averaged slicing method from Theorem
3.1
, thereby precluding the one-dimensional case
d=1
. Thus, it remains an open question whether, in dimension
d=1
, there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that the estimate

U(\theta s)\leq C_{p}U(s)
holds for all
s>0
and
\theta\in[0,1]
. We believe this does not always hold for
d=1
. However, as we see in Theorem
3.3
, one surrogate way to compensate for this one-dimensional gap consists in introducing the Hardy average

AU(s)=\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}U(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,

which satisfies
AU(\theta s)\leq 2^{p+1}AU(s)
for
d\geq 1
while encapsulating the most essential properties inherited from
U
.

4.
Asymptotics near
s=1
and
s=0

Let us make a brief digression to establish asymptotic results in the regimes
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
, for the normalized fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
and the associated seminorm, with respect to normalizing constant
\widetilde{C}_{d,p,s}
defined in (
1.3
). We present a relatively simple approach to the proofs of the asymptotic limits of the fractional seminorm
|\cdot|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}
as
s\to 1^{-}
, due to Brezis–Bourgain–Mironescu
[
BBM01
]
, and as
s\to 0^{+}
, due to Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova
[
MS02
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\R^{d})
and
r>0
. The following assertions are true.

(1)

If
u\in L^{p}(\R^{d})
then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\iint_{\R^{d}\times B^{c}(0,r)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}=\frac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})},

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\iint_{\R^{d}\,B^{c}(0,r)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}=0.

(2)

If
u\in\bigcup_{s\in(0,1)}W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\iint_{\R^{d}\times B(0,r)}\hskip-8.61108pt\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{0}^{r}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

(3)

If
u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)

\displaystyle\iint_{\R^{d}\times B(0,r)}\hskip-4.30554pt\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}=K_{d,p}\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.3
we know that
U(t)\to|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}
as
t\to\infty
and
U(t)\leq 2^{p}|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}
. Therefore, we deduce from Lemma
2.21
that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{sp}{p}\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}.

Furthermore, since
U(t)\leq 2^{p}|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\leq 2^{p}|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{(1-s)r^{-sp}}{sp}=0.

Assume
u\in W^{s_{0},p}(\R^{d})
for some
0<s_{0}<1
then, for
0<s<s_{0}
we have

\displaystyle s\int_{0}^{r}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\leq sr^{(s_{0}-s)p}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{U(t)}{t^{s_{0}p+1}}\leq sr^{(s_{0}-s)p}|u|^{p}_{W^{s_{0},p}(\R^{d})}\xrightarrow{s\to 0}0.

Now if
u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
, then
g(t)=\frac{U(t)}{t^{p}}
satisfies
g(t)\leq|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}
and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{U(t)}{t^{p}}=|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}=g(0).

Therefore, putting
a=(1-s)p
we deduce from Lemma
2.21
that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}\frac{a}{p}\int_{0}^{r}g(t)t^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{g(0)}{p}=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}K_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}.

∎

Definition 4.2
.

For
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
open, we define the space
BV_{p}(\Omega)
with
BV_{p}(\Omega)=W^{1,p}(\Omega)
if
1<p<\infty
and
BV_{1}(\Omega)=BV(\Omega)
, equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{BV_{p}(\Omega)}=(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+|u|^{p}_{BV_{p}(\Omega)})^{1/p}
with the seminorm
|\cdot|_{BV_{p}(\Omega)}
defined by

\displaystyle|u|_{BV_{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=|u|_{BV(\Omega)}

\displaystyle|u|_{BV_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=|u|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for }1<p<\infty.

Here we emphasize that
BV(\Omega)
is the space of functions of bounded variation and
|u|_{BV_{1}(\Omega)}=|u|_{BV(\Omega)}
is the total variation of
u
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\R^{d})
,
1\leq p<\infty
. The following assertions are true.

Maz’ya-Shaposhnikova:
We have
u\in\bigcup_{s\in(0,1)}W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}.

Brezis-Bourgain-Mirunescu:

Under the convention that
|u|_{BV_{p}(\R^{d})}=\infty
whenever

u\in L^{p}(\R^{d})\setminus BV_{p}(\R^{d})
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}s(1-s)\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\hskip-8.61108pt\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=K_{d,p}\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}.

In particular, the right-hand side limit is finite if and only if

u\in BV_{p}(\R^{d})
.

Proof.

For
u\in\bigcup_{s\in(0,1)}W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
Theorem
4.1
implies

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\Big(s\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}+s\int_{0}^{1}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)=\frac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})},

where we recall that

\displaystyle[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}=s(1-s)\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Conversely, if the above limit holds then
u\in\bigcup_{0<s<1}W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
since

\displaystyle s\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\big(1+\tfrac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}\big)\quad\text{for all $0<s<s_{0}$},

for some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
. Next for
u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
,
p\geq 1
, Theorem
4.1
implies

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)=K_{d,p}\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}.

The general case
u\in BV(\R^{d})
when
p=1
follows
analogously, see
[
Fog23
]
and
[
Dáv02
]
. Recall that by
[
BBM01
]
, see also
[
Fog23
, Theorem 1.1]
, we find that

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to 1^{-}}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}<\infty\implies\begin{cases}u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d}),&\text{if }1<p<\infty\\
u\in BV(\R^{d}),&\text{if }p=1.\end{cases}

In particular, if
u\notin W^{1,p}(\R^{d})
for
1<p<\infty
(resp.
u\notin BV(\R^{d})
for p=1) then

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to 1^{-}}s(1-s)\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\hskip-8.61108pt\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\infty,

that is we have
\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}=\infty
(resp.
|u|_{BV(\R^{d})}=\infty
).
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

For
u\in L^{p}(\R^{d})
,
1\leq p<\infty
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{C_{d,p,s}}{2}\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}\qquad\text{for $u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{d})$},

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{C_{d,2,\frac{sp}{2}}}{2}\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}\qquad\text{for $u\in\bigcup_{0<s<1}W^{s,p}(\R^{d})$}.

Proof.

This is directly implied by Theorem
4.3
and the fact that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{K_{d,p}C_{d,p,s}}{2s(1-s)}=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{C_{d,2,\frac{sp}{2}}}{s(1-s)}=\frac{p}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}.

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
be any open set and
x\in\Omega
.
Given
f\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\R^{d})
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we define

\displaystyle f_{\infty}(x,w)=\lim_{r\to\infty}f(x+rw)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{0}(x,w)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}g(x+rw)\qquad w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

Assume
f_{\infty}(x,w)
and
g_{0}(x,w)
exist in
\R\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
for a.e.
w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
then

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(y)\,\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d-(1-s)p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}g_{0}(x,w)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w),

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{\R^{d}\setminus\Omega}\frac{f(y)\,\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f_{\infty}(x,w)\mathds{1}_{S_{\Omega}(x)}(w)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w)

where
S_{\Omega}(x)=\{w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{c}}(x+rw)=1\}
is
the escape sector at
x
. In particular there hold that
S_{\Omega}(x)=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
if
\Omega
is bounded,
S_{\Omega}(x)=\emptyset
if
\Omega^{c}
is bounded, while
S_{\R^{d}_{+}}(x)=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{+}
with
\R^{d}_{+}=\{x\in\R^{d}\,:\,x_{d}>0\}
and
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{+}=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cap\R^{d}_{+}
.

Proof.

Since
x\in\Omega
we have
\delta_{x}=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>0
. Let
c_{1}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho}(x))}
and
c_{2}=\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. For arbitrarily large
\rho>\delta_{x}
and arbitrarily small
0<\delta<\delta_{x}
we have

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega^{c}\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\frac{|f(y)|\,\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\leq\int_{B_{\rho}(x)\setminus B_{\delta_{x}}(x)}\hskip-4.30554pt\frac{sc_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}=c_{1}\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}(\delta_{x}^{-sp}-\rho^{-sp})\xrightarrow{s\to 0^{+}}0,

\displaystyle(1-s)\int_{\Omega\cap B^{c}_{\delta}(x)}\frac{|g(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d-(1-s)p}}\leq\int_{B^{c}_{\delta}(x)}\frac{c_{2}(1-s)\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d-(1-s)p}}=c_{2}\frac{(1-s)|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{sp\delta^{(s-1)p}}\xrightarrow{s\to 1^{-}}0.

Using the previous remark, Lemma
2.21
and dominated convergence we get

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{\R^{d}\setminus\Omega}\frac{f(y)\,\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{B^{c}_{\rho}(x)}\frac{f(y)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{c}}(y)\,\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{\rho}^{\infty}f(x+rw)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{c}}(x+rw)r^{-sp-1}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\lim_{r\to\infty}f(x+rw)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{c}}(x+rw)\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f_{\infty}(x,w)\mathds{1}_{S_{\Omega}(x)}(w)\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w),

where we note that, for each
w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{c}}(x+rw)

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{S_{\Omega}(x)}(w)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x+rw\in\Omega^{c}\text{ for all sufficiently large }r,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Likewise, since
B_{\delta}(x)\subset B_{\delta_{x}}(x)\subset\Omega
, dominated convergence and Lemma
2.21
yield

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d-(1-s)p}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}\frac{a}{p}\int_{B_{\delta}(x)}\frac{g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d-a}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}\frac{a}{p}\int_{0}^{\delta}g(x+rw)r^{a-1}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}g_{0}(x,w)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w).

Finally, if
\Omega
is bounded then we have
\Omega\subset B_{R}(x)
for
R>0
large enough, so that
x+rw\in B_{R}^{c}(x)\subset\Omega^{c}
for all
w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
r>R.
Hence one readily gets that
S_{\Omega}(x)=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Likewise if
\Omega^{c}
is bounded say
\Omega^{c}\subset B_{R}(x)
for some
R>0
, then for all
r>R
and
w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we have
x+rw\in\Omega\subset B_{R}^{c}(x)
, that is,
\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{c}}(x+rw)=0
and hence
S_{\Omega}(x)=\emptyset
. Analogously it is not difficult to verify that
S_{\R_{+}}(x)=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{+}
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
be any open set and
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
1\leq p<\infty
. For
x\in\Omega
we denote
\delta_{x}=\dist(x,\partial\Omega)
,
S_{\Omega}(x)=\{w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{c}}(x+rw)=1\}
and

\displaystyle|S_{\Omega}(x)|:=\sigma_{d-1}(S_{\Omega}(x))=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathds{1}_{S_{\Omega}(x)}(w)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w).

The following assertions are true.

(1)

For
u\in\bigcup_{0<s<1}L^{p}(\Omega,\delta_{x}^{-sp})
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\int_{\R^{d}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,|S_{\Omega}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

(2)

For
u\in\bigcup_{0<s<1}W^{s,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega,\delta_{x}^{-sp})
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{2}{p}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|-|S_{\Omega}(x)|\big)|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(3)

Assume that either
\Omega
has a compact Lipschitz boundary, or that
\Omega=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x_{d}>\zeta(x^{\prime})\}
is epigraph of a Lipschitz function
\zeta:\R^{d-1}\to\R
. Then for
u\in\bigcup_{0<s<1}W^{s,p}(\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{2}{p}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|-|S_{\Omega}(x)|\big)|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(4)

Under the conditions of item (
2
) or item (
3
), the following hold

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{if $\Omega$ is bounded},

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{if $\Omega^{c}$ is bounded},

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s(1-s)\iint_{\R^{d}_{+}\times\R^{d}_{+}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d}_{+})}\qquad\text{if $\Omega=\R^{d}_{+}$}.

Proof.

(
1
)
Let
u\in L^{p}(\Omega,\delta_{x}^{-s_{0}p})
for some
0<s_{0}<1
. Lemma
4.5
implies

\displaystyle s\int_{\R^{d}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\xrightarrow{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{p}|S_{\Omega}(x)|\qquad x\in\Omega.

Moreover, for all
0<s\leq s_{0}
we have

\displaystyle s\int_{\R^{d}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\leq s\int_{\R^{d}\setminus B_{\delta_{x}}(x)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\delta_{x}^{-sp}\leq\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}(1+\delta_{x}^{-s_{0}p}).

Since
|u(x)|^{p}(1+\delta_{x}^{-s_{0}p})\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, the dominated convergence theorem yields

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\int_{\R^{d}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,|S_{\Omega}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

(
2
)
Assume
u\in W^{s_{0},p}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega,\delta_{x}^{-s_{0}p})
for some
0<s_{0}<1
. It appears that
\widetilde{u}\in W^{s_{0},p}(\R^{d})
where
\widetilde{u}
is the zero-extension of
u
, i.e.,
\widetilde{u}(x)=u(x)
for
x\in\Omega
and
\widetilde{u}(x)=0
for
x\in\Omega^{c}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+s_{0}p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+s_{0}p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{s_{0}p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\delta_{x}^{s_{0}p}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, since
\widetilde{u}\in W^{s_{0},p}(\R^{d})
, Theorem
4.3
implies

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s[\widetilde{u}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\frac{2}{p}|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\int_{\R^{d}}|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{2}{p}|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

This in conjunction with the foregoing gives

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}[\widetilde{u}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}-2\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}s\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\Big(\int_{\R^{d}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{p}\int_{\Omega}(|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|-|S_{\Omega}(x)|)|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(
3
)
If
\partial\Omega
is compact Lipschitz or
\Omega=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x_{d}>\zeta(x^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\Big)^{1/p}\qquad s\in[0,1].

Then for
0\leq\sigma<\eta<s\leq 1
there holds the estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{\eta,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\big(\varkappa N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)+1\big)\,\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\leq 9C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}.

This holds in particular, if
\Omega=\R^{d}_{+}
or if
\partial\Omega
is compact and Lipschitz.

Proof.

By Theorem
A.5
(see Appendix
A
), there exist a
C=C(d,p,\Omega)>0
and
\overline{u}\in L^{p}(\R^{d})
a suitable extension of
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that
\overline{u}|_{\Omega}=u
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\|\overline{u}\|_{W^{t,p}(\R^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{t,p}(\Omega)}
for all
t\in[0,1]
.
In view of Theorem
1.1
we find that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{\eta,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]^{p}_{W^{\eta,p}(\Omega)}\Big)^{1/p}\leq\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[\overline{u}]^{p}_{W^{\eta,p}(\R^{d})}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)[\overline{u}]^{\theta}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}[\overline{u}]^{1-\theta}_{W^{\sigma,p}(\R^{d})}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\|u\|^{\theta p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|u\|^{(1-\theta)p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+CN^{p}_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)\|u\|^{\theta p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\|u\|^{(1-\theta)p}_{W^{\sigma,p}(\Omega)}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{p}(\sigma,\eta,s)+1)\|u\|^{\theta}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\|u\|^{1-\theta}_{W^{\sigma,p}(\Omega)}\leq 9C\|u\|^{\theta}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\|u\|^{1-\theta}_{W^{\sigma,p}(\Omega)}.

∎

5.3.
Special case
p=2

The cactus case
p=2
is simpler to analyzed due to the availability of the Fourier transform.
Recall that the Fourier transform extends uniquely as a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\R^{d})
, defined for

u\in L^{2}(\R^{d})\cap L^{1}(\R^{d})
by

\displaystyle\hat{u}(\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\R^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad(\xi\in\R^{d}).

It is well-known that by Plancherel’s theorem, we have

(5.8)

\displaystyle[u]^{*\,2}_{H^{s}(\R^{d})}:=\frac{C_{d,s}}{2}\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{2}}{|h|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{\R^{d}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
C_{d,s}
is the usual normalization constant of the fractional Laplacian

\displaystyle C_{d,s}=\widetilde{C}_{d,2,s}=\frac{s(1-s)2^{2s}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+2s}{2}\right)}{\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(2-s)}=\Big(\int_{\R^{d}}\frac{1-\cos(h_{1})}{|h|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}h\Big)^{-1},

see
[
Fog20
, Section 2.3]
or
[
BV16
, Chapter 1]
.
We need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.8
.

Recalling
|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}
,
d\geq 1
, there holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{C_{d,s}}{s(1-s)}=\frac{2}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{C_{d,s}}{s(1-s)}=\frac{4d}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}.

For
d\geq 2
, the map
s\mapsto\frac{C_{d,s}}{s(1-s)}
is strictly increasing on
(0,1)
. In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}<\frac{C_{d,s}}{s(1-s)}<\frac{4d}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\qquad\quad\text{ for }\,\,s\in(0,1).

For
d=1
the map
s\mapsto\frac{C_{1,s}}{s(1-s)}
is strictly increasing on
(\tfrac{1}{16},1)
. Moreover we have

\displaystyle\frac{25}{26}

\displaystyle<\frac{2^{1/8}\Gamma\Big(\frac{9}{16}\Big)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\leq\frac{C_{d,s}}{s(1-s)}\leq 2\quad\qquad\text{ for\,\, $s\in(0,1)$}.

Proof.

Let us recall that the digamma function given by
\psi(x)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(x)}{\Gamma(x)}=\frac{d}{dx}\log\Gamma(x)
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
where it derivative
\psi^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x+n)^{2}}>0
is also called the trigamma function. Moreover
\psi
has only one positive root
x_{0}\approx 1.461632\in(1,\frac{3}{2})
. Hence the function
x\mapsto\Gamma(x)
is strictly increasing on
(x_{0},\infty)
and strictly decreasing on
(0,x_{0})
, in particular
\Gamma(x_{0})=\min_{x\in(0,\infty)}\Gamma(x)
. Furthermore, let
\gamma\approx 0.5772
be Euler-Mascheroni constant then some special values of
\psi
are given by

\displaystyle\psi(1)

\displaystyle=-\gamma\approx-0.5772,\qquad

\displaystyle\psi(1/2)=-\gamma-2\log 2\approx-1.9635,

\displaystyle\psi(0^{+})

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\psi(x)=-\infty,

\displaystyle\psi(x+1)=\psi(x)+\frac{1}{x},\quad x>0.

Define the function
G:(0,1)\to(0,\infty)
with

\displaystyle G(s):=\frac{s(1-s)}{C_{d,s}}=\frac{\pi^{d/2}\,\Gamma(2-s)}{2^{2s}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{d+2s}{2}\right)}.

For
d\geq 2
or
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1
, since
\psi
is increasing we have
\psi(\frac{d+2s}{2})\geq\psi(1)
and
\psi(2-s)\geq\psi(1)
. Thus, the logarithmic derivative of
G
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\log G(s)=\frac{G^{\prime}(s)}{G(s)}

\displaystyle=-\psi(2-s)-2\log 2-\psi\Big(\frac{d+2s}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-2\psi(1)-2\log 2=2\gamma-2\log 2<0.

Therefore, for
d\geq 2,

G
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1)
yielding that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{2d\,\Gamma(d/2)}<G(s)<\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)},\qquad\text{for }s\in(0,1),\;d\geq 2.

For
d=1
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq s\leq 1
we have
\frac{G^{\prime}(s)}{G(s)}<0
. Next, since the trigamma function
\psi^{\prime}(x)=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\log\Gamma(x)
is decreasing, the function
s\mapsto H(s)
with
H(s):=\big(\tfrac{G^{\prime}(s)}{G(s)}\big)^{\prime}=\psi^{\prime}(2-s)-\psi^{\prime}(\frac{1+2s}{2})
is increasing. Thus, using
\psi^{\prime}(1)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}
,
\psi^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}
and the formula
\psi^{\prime}(x+1)=\psi^{\prime}(x)-\frac{1}{x^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle H(s)\leq H(\tfrac{1}{2})=\psi^{\prime}(\frac{3}{2})-\psi^{\prime}(1)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-4<0

\displaystyle\text{for $0<s<\frac{1}{2}$}.

This implies that
s\mapsto\frac{G^{\prime}(s)}{G(s)}
is decreasing on
(0,\frac{1}{2})
, in particular

\displaystyle\frac{G^{\prime}(s)}{G(s)}\leq\frac{G^{\prime}(1/16)}{G(1/16)}<0,\qquad\frac{1}{16}<s<\frac{1}{2}.

Thus we have
\frac{G^{\prime}(s)}{G(s)}<0
for
\frac{1}{16}<s<1
. Altogether we find that
G
is strictly decreasing on
(\frac{1}{16},1)
. Recalling
G(s)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(2-s)}{2^{2s}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1+2s}{2}\right)}
and
\Gamma\left(\tfrac{31}{16}\right)=\tfrac{15}{16}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{15}{16}\right)\approx 0.97<1
we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}=G(1)<G(s)\leq G(\frac{1}{16})=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{1/8}\,\Gamma\big(\frac{9}{16}\big)}\Gamma\big(\frac{31}{16}\big)<\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{1/8}\,\Gamma\big(\frac{9}{16}\big)},\quad\text{$\frac{1}{16}\leq s<1$}.

It remains the case
d=1
and
0<s<\tfrac{1}{16}

we have
2-s\in(\tfrac{31}{16},2)\subset(x_{0},\infty)
and
\tfrac{1+2s}{2}\in(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{9}{16})\subset(0,x_{0}).

Since the digamma function is strictly increasing we find that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\log\Big(\frac{1}{2^{2s}\Gamma\big(\frac{1+2s}{2}\big)}\Big)=-2\log 2-\psi\big(\tfrac{1+2s}{2}\big)\geq-2\log 2-\psi\big(\frac{9}{16}\big)\approx 0.26>0.

Whence,
s\mapsto\frac{1}{2^{2s}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1+2s}{2}\right)}

is strictly increasing on
(0,\tfrac{1}{16}).
On the other hand, since
\Gamma
is increasing on
(x_{0},\infty)
we deduce that

\displaystyle\Gamma\Big(\frac{31}{16}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(2-s)\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad 1\leq\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{2s}\Gamma\Big(\frac{1+2s}{2}\Big)}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{1/8}\Gamma\Big(\frac{9}{16}\Big)}.

It follows that, for
0<s<\frac{1}{16}
,

\displaystyle\frac{24}{25}<0.97\approx\Gamma\Big(\frac{31}{16}\Big)\leq G(s)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(2-s)}{2^{2s}\,\Gamma\big(\tfrac{1+2s}{2}\big)}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{1/8}\Gamma\big(\tfrac{9}{16}\big)}<1.04=\frac{26}{25}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}<G(s)\leq\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{1/8}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{9}{16}\right)}<\frac{26}{25}\qquad\text{for \,\, $0<s<1$}.

∎

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
u\in L^{2}(\R^{d})
and
0\leq\sigma<\eta<s\leq 1
say
\eta=\theta s+(1-\theta)\sigma
with
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then we can choose
\Upsilon^{*}_{2,p}(\sigma,\eta,s)=1
. In particular we have

\displaystyle[u]^{*}_{H^{\eta}(\R^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq[u]^{*\,\theta}_{H^{s}(\R^{d})}\cdot[u]^{*\,(1-\theta)}_{H^{\sigma}(\R^{d})},

\displaystyle[u]_{H^{\eta}(\R^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{N}_{2}(\sigma,\eta,s)\,[u]_{H^{s}(\R^{d})}^{\theta}\cdot[u]_{H^{\sigma}(\R^{d})}^{1-\theta}

where
\widetilde{N}_{2}(\sigma,\eta,s)=\big(\tfrac{B_{d,\sigma}^{\theta}B_{d,s}^{1-\theta}}{B_{d,\eta}}\big)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{2d}

and we define
s\mapsto B_{d,2,s}
by

\displaystyle B_{d,2,s}=\frac{C_{d,s}}{2s(1-s)}=\frac{2^{2s-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+2s}{2}\right)}{\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(2-s)},\quad B_{d,2,0}=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{d,2,1}=\frac{2d}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}.

Proof.

Since
\eta=\theta\sigma+(1-\theta)s
then Hölder inequality implies

\displaystyle\int_{\R^{d}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2\theta\sigma}|\xi|^{2(1-\theta)s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\R^{d}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)^{\theta}\Big(\int_{\R^{d}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)^{1-\theta}.

In other words, by the identity (
5.8
) we get

[u]^{*}_{H^{\eta}(\R^{d})}\leq[u]^{*\,\theta}_{H^{s}(\R^{d})}[u]^{*\,(1-\theta)}_{H^{\sigma}(\R^{d})}.

Given that
[u]^{*\,2}_{H^{t}(\R^{d})}=B_{d,2,t}[u]^{2}_{H^{t}(\R^{d})}
, we conclude using Lemma
5.8
that

\displaystyle[u]_{H^{\eta}(\R^{d})}\leq\big(\tfrac{B_{d,2,\sigma}^{\theta}B_{d,2,s}^{1-\theta}}{B_{d,2,\eta}}\big)^{1/2}[u]_{H^{\sigma}(\R^{d})}^{\theta}\cdot[u]_{H^{s}(\R^{d})}^{(1-\theta)}\leq\sqrt{2d}[u]_{H^{\sigma}(\R^{d})}^{\theta}\cdot[u]_{H^{s}(\R^{d})}^{(1-\theta)}.

∎

5.4.
One dimensional conjecture gap

From our analysis, the following question remains open: Prove that for
d=1
there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\eta\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+\eta p}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{p}\,\sigma\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+\sigma p}}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\text{for }\,\,0\leq\sigma<\eta<1,

for all
u\in L^{p}(\R)
, where we note that
d=1
we have

\displaystyle U(t)=\int_{\R}|u(x+t)-u(x)|^{p}+|u(x-t)-u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{\R}|u(x+t)-u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

We make the following observations.

(1)

Natural guess for
C_{p}
.

Theorem
1.3
establishes that
C_{p}=2^{p}
when
d\geq 2
, which is thus a natural guess for the constant in our conjecture for
d=1
.

(2)

Special case
p=2\,\&\,d\geq 1
.

Thanks to the Fourier transform, the case
p=2
for
d\geq 1
boils down to proving the following inequality for
\xi\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\eta\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(t\xi)}{t^{1+2\eta}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{2}\,\sigma\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(t\xi)}{t^{1+2\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{for }\,\,0\leq\sigma<\eta<1.

We postulate that the sharper bound
C_{2}=2
can be obtained, thereby improving upon the constant
C_{2}=4
established in Lemma
5.2
.

(3)

Better constant.
In general, for
d\geq 1
, the case
\sigma=0
suggests that the sought estimate could hold with the sharper constant
C_{p}=2^{p-1}
. Indeed, by Theorem
3.3
, we have
U(t)\leq 2^{p}|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}=:2^{p-1}U(\infty).
and hence , applying Lemma
2.21
, we obtain

\displaystyle\qquad\eta\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+\eta p}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{2^{p-1}}{p}U(\infty)=2^{p-1}\Big(\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\sigma\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{U(t)}{t^{1+\sigma p}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big).

The same observation aligns with the conjecture in the case
p=2
.

(4)

From
d=1
to
d\geq 2
.
A relevant motivation for finding a better constant
C_{p}
in dimension
d=1
is that the same constant
C_{p}
also works in all dimensions
d\geq 2
via successive applications of the slicing method from Theorem
3.1
.

(5)

Relaxed inequality.

Although the inequality (
5.9
) is interesting in its own right, the interpolation inequality can also be obtained with a better constant through the following relaxed version:

(5.10)

\displaystyle(1-\eta)^{\frac{1}{p}}T_{p}(\eta,u,1)\leq 2(1-\sigma)^{\frac{1}{p}}T_{p}(\sigma,u,1).

A closer look at our proof of Lemma
5.2
, for the case
p=2
, hints that inequality (
5.10
) could be easier to prove than (
5.9
).

(6)

Another open question.

Although we are unable to construct a suitable counterexample, a related open question is to prove that
U
is not always
2^{p}
-almost increasing when
d=1
. Note that in Theorem
1.3
, the estimate
T_{p}(\eta,u,1)\leq 2^{p}T_{p}(\sigma,u,1)
for
d\geq 2
relies on the fact that
U
is
2^{p}
-almost increasing.

6.
Stability regional Dirichlet boundary value problem

In this section, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open, bounded Lipschitz domain, and assume that
sp>1
with
0<s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
, so that
\widetilde{C}_{d,p,s}=C_{d,p,s}
. Our goal is to establish the convergence of weak solutions to the Dirichlet problem associated with the normalized regional fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)_{p,\Omega}^{s}
.
The form
\cE^{s,p}_{\Omega}(\cdot,\cdot)
associated to
(-\Delta)_{p,\Omega}^{s}
is given, for
u,v\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
, by

\displaystyle\cE^{s,p}_{\Omega}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{d,p,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}(v(y)-v(x))\,\mathrm{d}y\,\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\cE^{1,p}_{\Omega}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla v(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Note that Theorem
4.8
implies that

\cE^{s,p}_{\Omega}(u,v)\xrightarrow{s\to 1^{-}}\cE^{1,p}_{\Omega}(u,v)
for
u,v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
; see, for instance
[
Fog26
]
.
Let us also recall that for
sp>1
, the trace operator
\gamma^{s}_{0}:W^{s,p}(\Omega)\to W^{s-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)
is continuous and surjective. Moreover, for
g\in W^{s-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)
, we consider the norm defined by

\displaystyle\|g\|_{W^{s-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)}=\inf\big\{\|u\|^{*}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:\gamma^{s}_{0}u=g\big\},

where, for practical reasons, we consider renormalized norm

\displaystyle\|u\|^{*}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\big(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\cE^{s,p}_{\Omega}(u,u)\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The fractional case is treated, for instance, in
[
DMT22
]
and
[
Leo23
, Chapter 9]
, while the classical case
s=1
can be found in
[
BF13
, Chapter III]
.

6.1.
Asymptotic compactness of Lions–Peetre type

The following result, originally proved in
[
BBM01
, Corollary 7]
, establishes a form of asymptotic Lions–Peetre-type compactness (cf.
[
LP64
, Theorems V.2.1 and V.2.2]
).

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is open bounded Lipschitz. Let
s_{0}\in[0,1)
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Assume
(u_{s})_{s\in[0,1)}\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
is a bounded family such that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in(s_{0},1)}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|u_{s}(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C_{d,p,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u_{s}(x)-u_{s}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)<\infty.

Then there exist a subsequence
s_{n}\to 1^{-}
and
u\in BV_{p}(\Omega)
such that

•

u_{s_{n}}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and for all
0\leq\eta<1
we have

\displaystyle\|u_{s}-u\|_{W^{\eta,p}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{s\to 1^{-}}0\quad\text{for all $0\leq\eta<1$}.

•

We have
u\in BV_{p}(\Omega)
(see Definition
4.2
) and satisfies

\displaystyle|u|^{p}_{BV_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{s_{n}\to 1^{-}}\frac{C_{d,p,s_{n}}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u_{s_{n}}(x)-u_{s_{n}}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+s_{n}p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

•

If
1<p<\infty
then for all
0<\tau<1
and
\tau p>1
we have

\displaystyle\|\gamma^{s_{n}}_{0}(u_{s_{n}})-\gamma^{1}_{0}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\gamma_{0}^{s_{n}}(u_{s_{n}})-\gamma^{1}_{0}(u)\|_{W^{\tau-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)}\xrightarrow{s_{n}\to 1^{-}}0.

Proof.

The existence of subsequence
s_{n}\to 1^{-}
such that
\|u_{s_{n}}-u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{s_{n}\to 1^{-}}0
for some
u\in BV_{p}(\Omega)
, which additionally satisfies the second claim, can be found in
[
Pon04
, Theorem 1.2]
; see also
[
BBM01
]
. Next, note that by
[
Fog23
, Lemma 2.12]
we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle s(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(d,p,\Omega)\begin{cases}\|u\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}&1<p<\infty\\
\|u\|_{BV(\Omega)}&p=1.\end{cases}

Thus by assumption we find that
\|u_{s_{n}}\|_{W^{s_{n},p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{W^{s_{n},p}(\Omega)}\leq C(d,p,\Omega)
. Let us consider
0\leq\eta<s_{n}<1
, for
n
large enough, so that
u_{s_{n}},u\in W^{\eta,p}(\Omega)
. The robust interpolation, Theorem
5.7
, with
\theta=\frac{\eta}{s_{n}}
implies

\displaystyle\|u_{s_{n}}-u\|_{W^{\eta,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{s_{n}}-u\|^{\frac{\eta}{s_{n}}}_{W^{s_{n},p}(\Omega)}\|u_{s_{n}}-u\|^{1-\frac{\eta}{s_{n}}}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u_{s_{n}}-u\|^{1-\frac{\eta}{s_{n}}}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

u_{s_{n}},u\in W^{\tau,p}(\Omega)
for
\frac{1}{p}<\tau\leq s_{n}<1
. By the continuity of the trace operator and the previous claims, we have

\displaystyle\|\gamma^{s_{n}}_{0}(u_{s_{n}})-\gamma^{1}_{0}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\|\gamma^{s_{n}}_{0}(u_{s_{n}})-\gamma^{1}_{0}(u)\|_{W^{\tau-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\tau}\|u_{s_{n}}-u\|_{W^{\tau,p}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0,

where
C_{\tau}=C(d,p,\Omega,\tau)>0
is a constant. This proves the third claim.
∎

Theorem 6.2

(Robust regional Poincaré-Friedrichs)
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary and
1<p<\infty
. Then there exist
s_{0}=s_{0}(d,p,\Omega)\in(\frac{1}{p},1)
and
B=B(d,p,\Omega)>1
such that for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
s_{0}\leq s<1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq Bs(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

In particular, letting
s\to 1^{-}
yields

\displaystyle\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Assume there exist no such
s_{0}
and
B
exist. Taking
s_{0}=1-\frac{1}{2^{n}}>\frac{1}{p}
with
n\geq 1
large and
B=2^{n}

s_{n}\in(1-\frac{1}{2^{n}},1)
and
u_{s_{n}}\in W^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{s_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1\quad\text{and}\quad s_{n}(1-s_{n})\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u_{s_{n}}(x)-u_{s_{n}}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+s_{s}p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x<\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

In particular,

Robust extension

In this section we prove that if the boundary
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz and compact, then
\Omega
is a robust extension domain. More precisely, we show that the Sobolev extension operator for
E:W^{s,p}(\Omega)\to W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
is bounded, with an operator norm that is bounded by a constant independent of
s
. For the convenience of the reader, we follow the approach presented in
[
DNPV12
]
. For this section we merely consider the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\big(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\big)^{1/p}\qquad s\in[0,1].

In general, an arbitrary open set is not necessarily a
W^{s,p}
-extension domain.
The following result shows that the half–space
\R^{d}_{+}
is a
W^{s,p}
-robust extension domain.

Lemma A.1

(Even reflection)
.

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open set which is symmetric with respect to the hyperplane
\{x_{d}=0\}
, i.e., for
(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\R^{d-1}\times\R
we have
(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\Omega
if and only if
(x^{\prime},-x_{d})\in\Omega
.
In particular,
\Omega=\R^{d}
is allowed. So that
\Omega=\Omega_{+}\cup\Omega_{-}
with
\Omega_{+}=\Omega\cap\R^{d}_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}=\Omega\cap(\R^{d}\setminus\R^{d}_{+})
where
\R^{d}_{+}=\{x\in\R^{d}\,:\,x_{d}>0\}
is the upper half space. Define refection of
u:\Omega_{+}\to\R
by

\displaystyle\overline{u}(x)=u(x^{\prime},|x_{d}|)=\begin{cases}u(x^{\prime},x_{d})&x_{d}\geq 0,\\
u(x^{\prime},-x_{d})&x_{d}<0.\end{cases}

Then the linear map
\overline{\cdot}:W^{s,p}(\Omega_{+})\to W^{s,p}(\Omega)

u\mapsto\overline{u}
is continuous and satisfies

\displaystyle[\overline{u}]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq 4^{1/p}[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega_{+})}\qquad\text{for all $s\in[0,1]$,\,\,\, $1\leq p\leq\infty$}.

In particular,
\R^{d}_{+}
is a
W^{s,p}
-robust extension domain.

Proof.

The case
s=1
albeit standard, is somewhat technical and can be adapted for instance from
[
Bre11
, Lemm 9.2]
so that
\partial_{x_{i}}\overline{u}=\overline{\partial_{x_{i}}u}
for
i=1,\dots,d-1
and
\partial_{x_{d}}\overline{u}=-\overline{\partial_{x_{d}}u}
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the change of variables

yields

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=2^{1/p}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{+})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=2^{1/p}\|\nabla u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{+})}.

Now we turn our attention to the case
0\leq s<1
. For
x\in\R^{d}_{+}
and
y\in\R^{d}_{-}
we have
(x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}\geq(x_{d}+y_{d})^{2}
and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\overline{u}(x)-\overline{u}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega_{-}}\int_{\Omega_{-}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},-x_{d})-u(y^{\prime},-y_{d})|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{+}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+2\int_{\Omega_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{-}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y^{\prime},-y_{d})|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{\Omega_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{+}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

By the same token, it readily follows that

\displaystyle[\overline{u}]_{W^{s,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq[u]_{W^{s,\infty}(\Omega_{+})}\qquad s\in(0,1).

Therefore,
\R^{d}_{+}
is a
W^{s,p}
-extension domain since if
\Omega_{+}=\R^{d}_{+}
then
\Omega=\R^{d}
.
∎

(Cutoff function)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\|\varphi\|^{p}_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\bigg(|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+s(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg).

Moreover, we have
\|u\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
. Finally, while the case
p=\infty
is obvious, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u\varphi\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1-1/p}(1+|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|)^{1/p}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}.

Now assume
\varphi\in C^{0,1}_{c}(\Omega)
with
\supp\varphi\subset K
. Let
\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-d}\zeta(\varepsilon^{-1}x)
,
\varepsilon>0
with
\zeta
satisfying
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))
,
\int\zeta=1
and
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
. There is bounded set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
such that
\delta:=\dist(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)>0
and for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small we have
\supp(u\varphi)*\eta_{\varepsilon}\subset\supp\varphi+B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subset\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}\subset\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}+B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subset\Omega
. It follows in particular that
[u\varphi]*\zeta_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\|[u\varphi]*\zeta_{\varepsilon}-[u\varphi]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0
.
Moreover, the function

(x,y)\mapsto U_{\varphi}(x,y)=([u\varphi](x)-[u\varphi](y))|x-y|^{-\frac{d}{p}-s}
satisfies, since
\Omega^{\prime}+B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subset\Omega
,
\|U_{\varphi}(\cdot-\varepsilon z,\cdot-\varepsilon z)-U_{\varphi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\Omega^{\prime})}\leq 2^{p}\|U_{\varphi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\Omega)}=2^{p}|u|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
and, by continuity of the shift in
L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\Omega^{\prime})
,
\|U_{\varphi}(\cdot-\varepsilon z,\cdot-\varepsilon z)-U_{\varphi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\Omega^{\prime})}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 1^{-}}0
, for each
z\in B_{1}(0)
. This in conjunction with dominated convergence theorem yield

\displaystyle\big|[u\varphi]*\zeta_{\varepsilon}-

\displaystyle[u\varphi]\big|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{|([u\varphi]*\zeta_{\varepsilon}-[u\varphi])(x)-([u\varphi]*\zeta_{\varepsilon}-[u\varphi])(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+2\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|[u\varphi]*\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)-[u\varphi](x)|^{p}\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{1}(0)}\hskip-8.61108pt\zeta(z)\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|U_{\varphi}(x-\varepsilon z,y-\varepsilon z)-U_{\varphi}(x,y)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle+2\|[u\varphi]*\eta_{\varepsilon}-[u\varphi]\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\int_{|h|>\delta}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}h}{|h|^{d+sp}}\,\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 1^{-}}0.

Thus we obtain
\|[u\varphi]*\zeta_{\varepsilon}-[u\varphi]\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and hence
u\varphi\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Zero-extension)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
be an open set, and
u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
with
s\in[0,1]
and

1\leq p\leq\infty
. Assume there exists a closed subset
K\subset\Omega
such that
u\equiv 0
in
\Omega\setminus K
, then the zero-extension function
\widetilde{u}
defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(x)=\begin{cases}u(x)\quad x\in\Omega\,,\\
\,0\qquad x\in\R^{d}\setminus\Omega\,\end{cases}

belongs to
W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
and we have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}\leq 2(1+|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|)\max(1,\delta^{-p})\|u\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
\delta=\dist(K,\partial\Omega)>0
, so that
|x-y|\geq\delta>0
for
x\in K
and
y\in\R^{d}\setminus\Omega
. In other words
\R^{d}\setminus\Omega\subset\R^{d}\setminus B_{\delta}(x)
for every
x\in K
. Hence for
x\in K
we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\R^{d}\setminus\Omega}\hskip-1.72218pt\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\leq\int_{B^{c}_{\delta}(0)}\hskip-1.72218pt\frac{\,\mathrm{d}h}{|h|^{d+sp}}

\displaystyle\leq\max(1,\delta^{-p})\int_{\R^{d}}\frac{1\land|h|^{p}}{|h|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}h=\max(1,\delta^{-p})\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}{s(1-s)p}.

Since
\supp u\subset K\subset\Omega
we can split the seminorm as follows

\displaystyle s(1-s)\int_{\R^{d}}\int_{\R^{d}}

\displaystyle\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=s(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+2s(1-s)\int_{K}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\R^{d}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq s(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+2|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\max(1,\delta^{-p})\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Beside this, it is readily seen that
\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, while the case
s=1
is standard and
\|\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\R^{d})}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
if
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Therefore, we find that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}\leq 2(1+|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|)\max(1,\delta^{-p})\|u\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)},

for
s\in[0,1]
and
1\leq p<\infty
. The case
p=\infty
and
s\in(0,1)
is straightforward.
∎

Lemma A.4

(Change of variables)
.

Let
T:U^{\prime}\to U
be a bi-Lipschitz mapping with
U\subset\R^{d}
and
U^{\prime}\subset\R^{d}
be open sets. Given
u:U\to\R
and
v:U^{\prime}\to\R
such that
v=u\circ T
, i.e.,
u=v\circ T^{-1}
then
u\in W^{s,p}(U)
, if and only if
u\circ T\in W^{s,p}(U^{\prime})
. Moreover there exists
C=C(d,p,T)>0
, such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}[u\circ T]_{W^{s,p}(U^{\prime})}&\leq C[u]_{W^{s,p}(U)}\\
[v\circ T^{-1}]_{W^{s,p}(U)}&\leq C[u]_{W^{s,p}(U^{\prime})}\end{split}\quad\quad\text{for $s\in[0,1]$ \,\, $1\leq p\leq\infty$}.

Proof.

We only prove the first estimate as the second one is similar. Since
T:U^{\prime}\to U
and
T^{-1}:U\to U^{\prime}
are Lipschitz with constants
\mathrm{Lip}(T)
and
\mathrm{Lip}(T^{-1})
, respectively, it follows from Rademacher’s theorem (see
[
Hei05
]
), that both
T
and
T^{-1}
are differentiable almost everywhere.Moreover , the Jacobian
DT
and
DT^{-1}
are bounded, with
\|DT\|_{L^{\infty}(U^{\prime})}\leq\mathrm{Lip}(T)
and
\|DT^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq\mathrm{Lip}(T^{-1})
. Let
M>1
such that

\displaystyle\|\det DT\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}+\|\det DT^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(U^{\prime})}+\mathrm{Lip}(T)+\mathrm{Lip}(T^{-1})\leq M.

While the case
p=\infty
is obvious, the case
s=1
is also standard since (by mollifying
T
) one obtains the chain rule
\nabla(u\circ T)=(\nabla u)\circ T\cdot DT
; see
[
BF13
, Theorem III.2.13.]
. Now we consider the case
1\leq p<\infty
and
s\in[0,1)
. It is easy to find that

\|u\circ T\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U^{\prime})}\leq M\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U)}
. Next, we have
|x-y|^{d+sp}\leq M^{d+p}|T^{-1}(x)-T^{-1}(y)|^{d+sp}
as since
T
is Lipschitz.
Using the change of variables
x=T(\hat{x})
and
y=T(\hat{y})
implies

\displaystyle\int_{U^{\prime}}\int_{U^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\frac{|u(T(\hat{x}))-u(T(\hat{y}))|^{p}}{|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{y}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{U}\int_{U}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|T^{-1}(x)-T^{-1}(y)|^{d+sp}}|\det DT^{-1}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}y|\det DT^{-1}(y)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq M^{d+p+2}\int_{U}\int_{U}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

That is
[u\circ T]_{W^{s,p}(U^{\prime})}\leq C[u]_{W^{s,p}(U)}
with
C=M^{\frac{d+2}{p}+1}
.
∎

Theorem A.5

(
W^{s,p}
-robust extension)
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\R^{d}
is an open set whose boundary
\partial\Omega

is compact and Lipschitz. Then
\Omega
is a robust
W^{s,p}
-
extension domain
, with
1\leq p\leq\infty
, in the following sense:

For any open set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\R^{d}
such that
\overline{\Omega}\subset\Omega^{\prime}
, there exist a constant
C=C(d,p,\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})>0
and a linear operator
E:L^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\R^{d})

for which the following properties hold.

•

For every
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
we have,
Eu|_{\Omega}=u
on
\Omega
and
\supp Eu\subset\Omega^{\prime}
.

•

For every
u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
with
s\in[0,1]
, we have
Eu\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})
.

•

For each
s\in[0,1]
, the operator
E:W^{s,p}(\Omega)\to W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
is bounded and

\displaystyle\|Eu\|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)},\qquad\text{ $u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)$}.

Proof.

Step 1: Local flattening.
Since
\partial\Omega
is compact Lipschitz and
\dist(\Omega,\partial\Omega^{\prime})>0
, there exists a finite number of open sets
U_{1},U_{2},\cdots,U_{k}
and a finite number of elements
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k}\in\partial\Omega
where
\overline{U}_{j}\subset\Omega^{\prime}
and
x_{j}\in\partial\Omega\cap U_{j}
, such that
\partial{\Omega}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}U_{j}
. Moreover, for each
j\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
there is a bi-Lipschitz isomorphism
T_{j}:Q\to U_{j}
with inverse
T^{-1}_{j}:U_{j}\to Q

such that

\displaystyle T_{j}(Q_{+})=U_{j}\cap\Omega,\qquad T_{j}(Q_{0})=U_{j}\cap\partial\Omega

where
Q_{0}:=\{x\in Q:x_{d}=0\}
and
Q_{+}=Q\cap\R^{d}_{+}
with

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle:=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\R^{d}:|x^{\prime}|<1\;\mbox{and}\;|x_{d}|<1\}.

Step 2: Partition of unity.
Clearly, we can write
\Omega^{\prime}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}U_{j}\cup\big(\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\partial{\Omega}\big)
. Accordingly, by the partition of unity, there exist
k+1
smooth functions
\psi_{0}
,
\psi_{1}
,…,
\psi_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\R^{d})
such that for each
j\in\{0,...,k\}
we have
\supp\psi_{0}\subset\R^{d}\setminus\partial{\Omega}
,
\supp\psi_{j}\subset U_{j}
,
0\leq\psi_{j}\leq 1
and
\sum_{j=0}^{k}\psi_{j}=1
. We obviously have
u=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\psi_{j}u.

Step 3: Pull-back.
Recall that
T_{j}:Q\to U_{j}
is the isomorphism of class
C^{0,1}
with
T_{j}(Q_{+})=U_{j}\cap\Omega
,
j\in\{1,...,k\}
. Let us consider the pull-back of
u|_{U_{j}\cap\Omega}
defined by

\displaystyle v_{j}(y):=u(T_{j}(y))\qquad\text{ for any }y\in Q_{+}.

According to Lemma
A.4
,
v_{j}\in W^{s,p}\left(Q_{+}\right)
and we have

(A.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}[v_{j}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(Q_{+})}&\leq C[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(U_{j}\cap\Omega)}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\|v_{j}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q_{+})}\leq C\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U_{j}\cap\Omega)}.\end{split}

Here and in the sequel, it will be clear from the context that
C=C(d,p,\Omega)>0
denotes a generic constant independent of
s
and possibly different step by step.

Step 4: Even reflection.
According to Lemma
A.1
, the even reflection
\overline{v}_{j}:Q\to\R
with
\overline{v}_{j}(x)=v_{j}(x^{\prime},|x_{d}|)
belongs to
W^{s,p}(Q)
and satisfies

\displaystyle[\overline{v}_{j}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq 4[v_{j}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(Q_{+})}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\|\overline{v}_{j}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q)}=2\|v_{j}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q_{+})}.

Step 5: Push-forward.
Next, after extending
v_{j}
to
Q
as
\overline{v}_{j}
we have to push the extension back to
\Omega
by defining

\displaystyle w_{j}(x):=\overline{v}_{j}\big(T_{j}^{-1}(x)\big)\quad\text{for all }x\in U_{j}.

It is worth noting that
w_{j}\equiv u
. Since
T_{j}
is bi-Lipschitz, by arguing as above using Lemma
A.4
, it follows that
w_{j}\in W^{s,p}(U_{j})
and

(A.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}[w_{j}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(U_{j})}&\leq C[\overline{v}_{j}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(Q)}\leq C[v_{j}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(Q_{+})}\leq C[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(U_{j}\cap\Omega)}\\
\|w_{j}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U_{j})}&\leq C\|\overline{v}_{j}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q)}\leq C\|v_{j}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q_{+})}\leq C\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U_{j}\cap\Omega)}.\end{split}

Step 6: Zero-extension.
By definition
\psi_{0}u
and
\psi_{j}w_{j}
,
j\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
, have compact support in
U_{j}\subset\Omega^{\prime}
so that Lemma
A.2
implies
\psi_{0}u,\psi_{j}w_{j}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(U_{j})
. By Lemma
A.3
their corresponding zero the extensions
\widetilde{\psi_{0}u}
and
\widetilde{\psi_{j}w_{j}}
to all
\R^{d}
belong to
W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
. Furthermore, since
\psi_{0}u,\psi_{j}w_{j}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(U_{j})
and
\overline{U}_{j}\subset\Omega^{\prime}
it is not difficult to verify that
\widetilde{\psi_{0}u},\widetilde{\psi_{j}w_{j}}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})
.
In sum,
\widetilde{\psi_{0}u},\widetilde{\psi_{j}w_{j}}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})\cap W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
and using Lemma
A.3
, Lemma
A.2
and the estimate (
A.2
) we get

(A.3)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi_{j}\,w_{j}}\|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}\leq C\|\psi_{j}\,w_{j}\|_{W^{s,p}(U_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|w_{j}\|_{W^{s,p}(U_{j})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega\cap U_{j})},

(A.4)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi_{0}\,u}\|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\,C\,\|\psi_{0}\,u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,C\,\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}.

Step 7: Extension.
Finally, we define the desired linear extension operator by

\displaystyle Eu=\widetilde{\psi_{0}u}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\psi_{j}w_{j}}.

It readily it follows that
\supp Eu\subset\Omega^{\prime}
, since
\supp\psi_{j}\subset U_{j}\subset\Omega^{\prime}
. Moreover, we have
Eu\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})\cap W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
since
\widetilde{\psi_{0}u},\widetilde{\psi_{j}w_{j}}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})\cap W^{s,p}(\R^{d})
. Next, since
w_{j}\equiv u
(and hence
\psi_{j}w_{j}\equiv\psi_{j}u
) on
U_{j}\cap\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle Eu(x)=\widetilde{\psi_{0}u}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\psi_{j}w_{j}}(x)=\psi_{0}u(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\psi_{j}w_{j}(x)=u(x)\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega$}.

That is
Eu|_{\Omega}=u
. Moreover, combining (
A.3
) with (
A.4
), we get

\displaystyle\|Eu\|_{W^{s,p}(\R^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}

with
C=C(d,p,\Omega)
independent of
s\in[0,1]
.
∎
