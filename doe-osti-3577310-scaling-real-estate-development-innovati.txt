Executive Summary
This report profiles 12 emerging developers
delivering affordable, high-performance build-
ings in markets ranging from Los Angeles
and Chicago to Billings, Montana, and Horry
County, South Carolina. The central finding
across the case studies is that innovation in
real estate development depends less on any
single technology than on how developers
structure partnerships, financing, and regula-
tory engagement. Rather than treating afford-
ability, profitability, and building performance
as trade-offs, these developers pursue all three
within project-specific strategies. They rede-
fine affordability not only through low rents but
through reduced utility costs, efficient opera-
tions, streamlined delivery, and long-term cost
of ownership.

Several developers lowered costs and delivery
risk through vertical integration and industri-
alized construction. 548 Development in Chi-
cago operates four in-house arms that include
development, capital, and energy. Its Humboldt
Park project, on track to be the city’s largest
Passive House building, layered $1.5 million in
Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (generating
$14.1 million in equity) with $18.1 million in tax
increment financing and other city assistance.
Rooftop solar is expected to cut the building’s
utility costs 33%–50%. SoLa Impact in Los
Angeles brought modular production in-house,
reducing the cost of a typical affordable unit
from $500,000 or more to roughly $250,000,
approximately 50% below business-as-usual
dollars-per-door. Module reports that its Last
Mile Network of regional production facilities
completes homes up to 40% faster and with up
to 80% lower energy demand. Its Black Street
project in Pittsburgh shortened the construc-
tion phase by 172 days, and Module estimates
the home’s high-performance systems would
save residents about $1,000 per year in utilities.
iUnit prototypes designs and systems before
committing to production. Its 40-unit Eliot Flats
in Denver comprises factory-built micro units
designed to operate as part of a microgrid.

Despite the promise of this novel approach, the project
faced initial scrutiny. The Horry County School Board
engaged in extensive deliberations before moving forward,
a response to the unconventional nature of the modular
delivery model. Firstfloor addressed this scrutiny directly,
providing detailed planning documentation and transparent

to approve the project.

Having navigated the approval process, Firstfloor moved
forward with a design approach that prioritized energy
performance from the outset. Central to the design process
was a holistic strategy focused on energy efficiency. By
reducing the exterior wall area per square foot by approx-
imately 50% compared to conventional school facilities,
the team minimized thermal bridging and reduced heating
and cooling loads. Additionally, Firstfloor engineered the
mechanical systems to be roughly 30% smaller per square
foot than those found in standard schools, partially enabled
by 10-inch hollow-core slabs, which reduced the condi-
tioned volume and provided thermal lag (ENR Southeast
2018; Schultes 2022). These reductions increased space
efficiency and contributed to each building’s low energy
demand (Figure 13). The National Laboratory of the Rock-
ies’ (NLR’s) profile of St. James Intermediate reports an
annual site energy use intensity of 14.3 kBtu/ft²·yr—well
below the median in NLR’s 88-school sample of zero-ener-
gy K–12 schools (NLR 2021)(Figure 14a).

Executing five large-scale buildings concurrently posed
significant logistical and labor challenges. Firstfloor ad-
dressed these issues with a coordinated strategy. They
employed a diverse range of structural systems—steel
frames, load-bearing masonry, hollow-core concrete slabs,
light gauge trusses, and glulam wood beams—to allow for
the segmentation of subcontractor scopes and to mitigate
scheduling conflicts (Figure 14b). Moreover, they incor-
porated prefabricated HVAC and electrical penthouses for
each building, streamlining installation and accelerating
project timelines.

The Horry County school project exemplifies Firstfloor’s
ability to integrate energy performance, cost-effectiveness,
and construction innovation within a demanding delivery
schedule. Their comprehensive process demonstrates a
scalable model for future educational infrastructure that
meets both cost and energy objectives.

case study for constrained urban sites where redevelop-
ment must address both physical barriers and long-stand-
ing community concerns about displacement.
