1 Introduction

My work on the contract DE-SC=0023274 (July, 2022 - July, 2025) was focused on understanding
the plasma fueling by NBI. On Dec. 1998 with Sergei Krasheninnikov (UCSD, San Diego, CA)
we initiated the Li Wall Fusion (LiWFusion) as a new concept of magnetic fusion [1]. The new

 1

concept was a reaction of the lack of luck of tokamak fusion with QDT = 1 on TFTR and JET. We
recognized the edge plasma cooling by recycling to be the route reason of the tokamak problems
on the way to burning plasma. LiWFusion relies on plasma pumping by a lithium layer on the
inner walls of the plasma chamber, combined with the plasma heating and fueling by the Neutral
Beam Injection (NBI). These two innovations eliminate the route problem of the tokmak fusion.
The concept became theoretically mature in 2006. In 2012, the technology of continuously Flowing
Liquid Lithium (24/7-FLiLi) was invented by me for the future implementations of the LWFusion
concept.
At the same time, my every presentation on LiWFusion to International Symposium on Lithium
Applications since 2010 was objected by some person with words “Everybody since the 1970s knows
that plasma fueling by NBI is impossible”. I dropped the name of the author of this objection and
ignored his views. My work on the current grant on NBI fueling of LTX-β not only clarified the
issue but resolved it in an astonishing way. The NBI fueling was fully understood (thus humiliating
the current dogmatic fusion community). The new future of LTX-β dedicated for decades to Li in
tokamaks, as well as of the entire magnetic fusion program was envisioned, in sharp contrast with
the fallure of OFES in the post-TFTR era of 21st century.
Several of my colleagues from LTX-β (PPPL) Dick Majeski, Anurag Maan, Dennis Boyle, and
Marina Gorelenkov and Alexei Polevoy from ITER helped me in understanding the NBI fueling.
Because my personal Section 4 of this report is highly critical to OFES, I did not listed the names
of these my colleagues as the coauthors of the report. I am acknowledging their contributions in
this introduction.

1.1 Brief summary of NBI fueling in LTX-β

LTX-β is unique in having the inner wall (shell) surface coated by the plasma pumping lithium (Li)
layer and 600 kW/20 kW neutral beam injection (NBI) . But because of its small size (R = 0.38 m,
a = 0.25 m, b/a≃ 1.5, and toroidal field Bt = 0.3T), LTX-β can only partially address the physics
of the low recycling regime. NBI cannot fuel the discharge in LTX-β İnstead, its contribution to
the main fueling by gas injection represented one of the topics of LTX research.
In 2019, my 3dOrb code, created for LTX-β, revealed the 100% loss of energetic NBI ions on LTX-β
at that time plasma current of 75 kA and created strong motivation for enhancing it to 100-150
kA. In 2020 the power supply system of LTX-β was upgraded to the plasma current 125 kA that
improved the capture of the hot ions from NBI. This was described in the progress reports on this
grant earlier.
Remarkably, R.Majeski’s suggested to test shifting NBI beam toward the lower field side in LTX-β.
As 3dOrb code has confirmed, this suggestion resulted in almost complete, 98%, capture of NBI
ions, thus eliminated the original deficiency of LTX-β in having high prompt losses of energetic
ions from NBI.
In the case of LTX-β, about a year ago, it became obvious, thanks to Dick, that the ≤ 20 keV
H0 -NBI still cannot fuel H+ plasma. The dominant mechanism of converting H0 from NBI to
energetic H+ is the Charge exchange (abbreviated here to Cx). The Ion Impact ionization (II) is
small compared to Cx at the ≃ 100 eV level of the ion temperature at LTX-β and its low beam
energy. The same is valid for ionization by electrons.

 2

Cx produces the secondary thermal atoms H0Cx . Unfortunately, the secondary H0Cx atoms escape
from the plasma much faster than being ionized by the residual ion impact or by electrons. I found
no kinetic or diffusion regime for the secondary Cx atoms in LTX-β plasma. These atoms simply
escape being not confined.
Three years ago, I discovered for myself in the burning plasma analysis, the importance of plasma
dilution by He2+ for NBI capture. This discovery has been immediately translated by Dick Majeski
and his LTX-β experience. He told that a noticeable fueling by NBI has been already observed in
LTX-β experiments with plasmas containing Li3+ as the impurity. We immediately found that the
cross-section of NBI beam capturing by Li3+ ia even bigger than that for He2+ . It is also much
bigger than the Cx of H0 with H+ . Both Cx and II collisions of H0 with He2+ and Li3+ in the
plasma lead to capturing the NBI beam and to plasma fueling. The GSV-code was created to
calculate the collision rates, mean free paths of NBI atoms and diffusion coefficients of secondary
Cx atoms.
Initially the GSV was based on the Barnett ORNL report [2]. In late 2023, Dr. D.B. King, the
head of NBI injection on JET (CCFE, Ca;ham, UK), has approved my approach but suggested to
approach Marina Gorelenkov from PPPL who has access to the widely used ADAS collision tables.
They are advanced than the older Barnett report. Marina introduced me to the ADAS tables
created for TFTR [Pankin04]. The GSV code was adopted to this data base of collisions, which
include H0 collisions with H+ , He2+ , Li3+ for the full range of MBI and fusion plasma parameters.
Using GSV, I’ve broken the long-standing community dogma of impossibility of beam fueling even
by low energy NBI. Just an 10% fraction of Li3+ ions in an H+ plasma is sufficient to achieve a
50% fueling fraction (!) of the H0 flux from 20 keV NBI in the plasma core. For us this was a
source of additional confidence in the success in our objectives on LTX-β and in my own objectives
regarding understanding the burning plasma [4, 5, 6].
Finally, I invented the toga device, which is the tokamak upgrade to two-chambers, suitable for
LiWFusion regime, where (a) plasma faces only Li without touching it, (b) the entire Plasma Surface
Interactions are eliminated, and (c) plasma in fueled by NBI, leaves nothing from the community
dogma even for future burning plasma. To my surprise, the OFES expressed no interest in learning
what toga is about even when the invention was submitted to the US PTO.

2 GSV code for ⟨σv⟩ analysis of NBI fueling

I’ve written the GSV code for the calculation of collision rate coefficients in application for NBI
fueling analysis to get the assessment of feeling as the issues for burning plasma. This Task was,
in fact, easier than interpretation of the actual LTX-β experiments. Initially, GSV has used its own
routines for generating the ⟨σv⟩ rate coefficients by averaging the discrete binary cross-sections σ
data (interpolated by cubic splines) with Maxwell distribution of plasma ions. Then, for the sake of
consistency with earlier adopted approaches, the Chebyshev representation from the C.F. Barnett
Report (1990) [2] was introduced later to GSV.
Recently, (Oct, 2023) by the advice of JET NBI experts (CCFE, UK) I incorporated the regular
table representation of ADAS (Atomic Data and Analysis Structure) generated earlier for the

 5

TRANSP code in PPPL [3].
Marina Gorelenkov (PPPL) prepared for me the requested data. Unfortunately, the TRANSP
ion temperatures and neutral atom energies in the tables are different for different collisions. GSV
remapped (by cubic spline interpolation) the TRANSP rate coefficients to a common set of tem-
peratures in order to calculate the rate coefficients for different mixtures of H+ , He2+ , and Li3+
ions in the plasma.

3 Li-doping effect to revolutionize the NBI use in magnetic fusion

For hydrogen NBI energy smaller than 42 keV, the Cx collisions dominate over Ion Impact Ioniza-
tion, while the only collision in pure H+ plasma which leads to fueling by NBI. In order on enhance
the fueling efficiency of NBI, the following standard energies 60 keV, 120 keV, and 180 keV were
determined for hydrogen, deuterium and tritium NBI atoms. This energies are relevant for the
burning plasma regimes.
For these standard energies, about 1/3 of neutral beam fueling is lost to the chamber wall. These
secondary Cx atoms have plasma ion temperature and hit the side walls of the vessel directly, what
represents a serious problem for utilizing NBI for both plasma heating and fueling. In particular,
this understanding leads to the community dogma of impossibility of using NBI for fueling and
only limited use for heating.
This dogma was broken by the LiWFusion concept, which introduced a thin Li layer
on the plasma facing walls. Approximately 100 mk of Li thickness is sufficient for
absorbing the secondary Cx atoms and protecting the wall from sputtering.

 8

 The Li coating was the driving idea of LTX device, introduced for arranging the plasma pumping. At
 the same time, the Li coating makes plasma chamber compatible with NBI regardless of secondary
 Cx atoms.

3.2 The concluding remarks on NBI fueling studies on LTX-β

Our work has injected the revolutionary understanding of applicability of NBI for magnetic fusion.
In contrast to dogmatic fusion community, which denies NBI for fueling burning plasma, we have
shown that in combination with Li coated walls NBI represents the best possible plasma heating
and fueling the plasma core.
While reduction of Cx at statndard NBI energy is essential for tokamaks with the solid (non-lithium)
walls, the residual secondary Cx atoms still will produce spattering of the wall and contamination
of the plasma.
My invention of the toga device has addressed the issue. Toga extended the notion of Li layer
to the Li environment for the plasma. This invention eliminates the PSI - the enemy of tokamak
performance since the mid 1970s. The LTX-β device also have the Li environment for the plasma,
but because of its single plasma chamber it cannot address the recycling problem properly. And
OFES, which always fundad LTX at the second priority, were blocking even the ideas of upgrading
the LTX program properly.
Unlike large devices for the burning plasma (TFTR, JET), the smaller research devices cannot use
the standard NBI energies because of shine-through. Even JET tokamk had limitations on NBI
duration because of shine-through. At NBI energies below its standard energy, the effect of fueling
is highly diminished.
After our discovery of Li-doping effect, the fusion community should forget about NBI
energies below the standard level: 60 keV for H-NBI, 120 keV for D-NBI, and 180
keV for T-NBI.
In application to my toga invention, which is insensitive to the secondary Cx atoms,
the Li-doping effect opens a high-way for toga development by utilizing the burning-
plasma NBI energies for heating and fueling studies in mid-size togas starting from
the early stage.
