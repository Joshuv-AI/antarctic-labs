Abstract

Kalton and Roberts proved K KR ⩽ 89 / 2 K_{\mathrm{KR}}\leqslant 89/2 , and Bondarenko, Prymak, and Radchenko improved the upper bound to 38.8 38.8 . We prove that K KR ⩽ 694,198,146,664,396 , 294 , 486 , 127 , 753 34,994,834,677,886 , 019 , 996 , 000 , 000 ≈ 19.837 . 1.
Introduction and finite reduction

Let
\Omega
be a set and let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebra of subsets of
\Omega
. Fix
\Delta>0
. A function
f\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
\Delta
-additive if
f(\varnothing)=0
and

(1)

|f(A)+f(B)-f(A\cup B)|\leqslant\Delta\qquad(A,B\in\mathcal{F},\ A\cap B=\varnothing).

The Kalton–Roberts theorem asserts that there is a universal constant
C
such that every
\Delta
-additive
f
is within
C\Delta
, uniformly on
\mathcal{F}
, of a finitely additive signed measure. We write
K_{\mathrm{KR}}
for the smallest such constant.

Kalton and Roberts proved in
[
10
]
that
K_{\mathrm{KR}}\leqslant 89/2
. Bondarenko, Prymak, and Radchenko improved the upper bound to
38.8
in
[
4
]
. Pawlik proved the first non-trivial lower bound,
K_{\mathrm{KR}}\geqslant 3/2
, in
[
13
]
; in joint work with Gnacik and Guzik, the second-named author later improved the lower bound to
3
in
[
7
]
. Related constants for approximately modular set functions were studied by Feige, Feldman and Talgam-Cohen in
[
6
]
.

The title refers to the original Kalton–Roberts upper bound
89/2
. The present constant is below
89/4
, so it more than halves their bound. The exact fraction displayed in the abstract is deliberately retained. It is the rational number obtained by balancing two affine inequalities at the end of the proof, after all expander parameters, intersection weights and the case-splitting point have been chosen rationally. Thus the large numerator and denominator are not artefacts of floating-point optimisation; they are the
exact
output of the certified argument. We write decimal approximations for readability, but all final numerical comparisons are rational.

The proof remains recognisably akin to the one from
[
10
]
in that its decisive step is an expander, or concentrator, recombination. What changes is the source collection fed into the recombination. The original proof minimises an oscillation, builds a submeasure from an extremal set, and applies a covering-index argument. Here we instead use the
\ell_{\infty}
-duality certificate for best approximation by additive measures. This certificate has positive and negative masses
p,q
, with
q\leqslant 1/2
, and therefore supplies low-frequency near-extremal multisets. Rational mixtures of neighbouring intersection levels lower the relevant frequencies still further.

In this paper a

has a concrete finite meaning. The dual certificate in

2.1
is a signed vector

For readability we also isolate the numerical core of the argument. The exact verifier checks, using rational arithmetic only, that

(1-\tau_{1})q_{0}^{3}+\tau_{1}q_{0}^{4}=\alpha_{1},\qquad(1-\tau_{2})p_{0}^{4}+\tau_{2}p_{0}^{5}=\alpha_{2},

Thus the search is not part of the proof; it is only the discovery mechanism which suggested the displayed values of

Theorem 1.1
.

For every set algebra
\mathcal{F}
and every
\Delta
-additive function
f\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{R}
, there is a finitely additive signed measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{F}
such that

\sup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}|f(A)-\mu(A)|\leqslant\frac{694198146664396294486127753}{34994834677886019996000000}\,\Delta.

Consequently,

K_{\mathrm{KR}}<\frac{9919}{500}=19.838.

The proof is finite-dimensional. We shall work on
2^{U}
, where
U
is finite, and then pass to general algebras by compactness. This compactness reduction is the same one used by Kalton and Roberts at the beginning of their proof of Theorem 4.1
[
10
, p. 809]
; we restate it to make the normalisation explicit.

Lemma 1.2

(Compact reduction)
.

Suppose that
Theorem

1.1
is known for every finite power-set algebra
2^{U}
, with a constant
C
in place of the displayed constant. Then it holds with the same constant
C
for every set algebra.

Proof.

It is enough to consider
\Delta>0
, since the case
\Delta=0
is exact additivity. By scaling, take
\Delta=1
. Let
f\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{R}
be
1
-additive, and assume the finite theorem with constant
C
. For each
A\in\mathcal{F}
set

I_{A}=[f(A)-C,f(A)+C]

and consider the compact product space

X=\prod_{A\in\mathcal{F}}I_{A}.

For disjoint
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
, the equation

\nu(A\cup B)=\nu(A)+\nu(B)

defines a closed subset of
X
. We claim that the family of all these closed additivity conditions has the finite intersection property. Indeed, choose finitely many such equations. They involve only finitely many sets of
\mathcal{F}
, hence are contained in a finite subalgebra
\mathcal{F}_{0}
. Identifying
\mathcal{F}_{0}
with a power set of its atoms, the finite theorem gives an additive signed measure
\nu_{0}
on
\mathcal{F}_{0}
with

|\nu_{0}(A)-f(A)|\leqslant C\qquad(A\in\mathcal{F}_{0}).

Extend
\nu_{0}
arbitrarily to a point of
X
on all remaining coordinates. This point satisfies the chosen additivity equations.

Compactness gives a point
\mu\in X
satisfying every finite-additivity equation. Thus
\mu
is a finitely additive signed measure on
\mathcal{F}
, and
\mu\in X
gives
|f(A)-\mu(A)|\leqslant C
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
.
∎

Henceforth
U
is finite and
\Delta=1
. Additive signed measures on
2^{U}
are identified with functions of the form

A\longmapsto\sum_{i\in A}a_{i}.

Let
\mathcal{L}
denote this linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{2^{U}}
.

Since
\mathbb{R}^{2^{U}}
is finite-dimensional and
\mathcal{L}
is closed, every
f\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{U}}
has a closest additive approximant in the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm. Indeed, after choosing any additive approximant, one may restrict the search to a bounded closed subset of
\mathcal{L}
, hence to a compact set.

2.
The extremal certificate

The following elementary duality lemma is the point at which the additive problem differs from the weak modular problem.

Lemma 2.1

(Dual certificate)
.

Let
f\colon 2^{U}\to\mathbb{R}
be
1
-additive. After subtracting a closest additive signed measure, suppose that

M:=\|f\|_{\infty}=\dist_{\infty}(f,\mathcal{L}).

Then there is a rational signed measure
\lambda
on
2^{U}
such that

(i)

\|\lambda\|_{1}=1
;

(ii)

\sum_{A\ni i}\lambda_{A}=0
for every
i\in U
;

(iii)

\lambda_{A}>0
only when
f(A)=M
, and
\lambda_{A}<0
only when
f(A)=-M
.

Proof.

The annihilator of
\mathcal{L}
is the set of all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{U}}
satisfying
\sum_{A\ni i}\lambda_{A}=0
for every
i\in U
. Since
0
is a best approximant to
f
from
\mathcal{L}
, the (finite-dimensional) Hahn–Banach criterion for best
\ell_{\infty}
-approximation gives
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{\perp}
with
\|\lambda\|_{1}=1
and
\langle\lambda,f\rangle=M
. Equality in Hölder’s inequality forces
\lambda
to be supported on the active coordinates
f=\pm M
, with the stated sign convention.

After fixing the positive and negative active supports and their signs, the equality
\langle\lambda,f\rangle=M
is automatic: by the sign convention it is just
M\|\lambda\|_{1}=M
. Thus the remaining constraints are only the zero-marginal equations, the support and sign restrictions, and
\|\lambda\|_{1}=1
. These define a non-empty bounded polytope with rational defining equations and inequalities. The polytope has a vertex; at a vertex, a maximal independent subsystem of active rational constraints determines the coordinates, which are rational by Cramer’s rule. Replacing
\lambda
by such a rational vertex proves the assertion.
∎

Let
p
and
q
denote the positive and negative masses of
\lambda
, respectively. Thus

p+q=1.

Replacing
f
by
-f
, if necessary, we may and shall assume

(2)

q\leqslant p,\qquad q\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

Put
u=f(U)
. If
P
is a positive active set, then
f(P)=M
,
|f(P^{c})|\leqslant M
, and
P\sqcup P^{c}=U
, so
1
-additivity gives
u\geqslant-1
. If
N
is a negative active set, the same argument gives
u\leqslant 1
. Hence

(3)

-1\leqslant u\leqslant 1.

We shall use weighted multisets. Since
\lambda
is rational, all weights can be cleared by duplication; thus every weighted multiset below may be replaced by an honest finite multiset without changing any averages or frequencies.

Lemma 2.2

(Low-frequency near-extremal multisets)
.

There are two finite multisets
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
of subsets of
U
with the following properties.

(i)

Every item of
U
appears in at most a
q
-fraction of the members of
\mathcal{A}
. The average deficit
M-f(A)
over
A\in\mathcal{A}
is at most
q(1-u)
.

(ii)

Every item of
U
appears in at most a
p
-fraction of the members of
\mathcal{B}
. The average surplus
M+f(B)
over
B\in\mathcal{B}
is at most
p(1+u)
.

Proof.

Write
\lambda=\lambda^{+}-\lambda^{-}
, where
\lambda^{+}
is supported on
f=M
and
\lambda^{-}
is supported on
f=-M
. For an item
i\in U
, let
m_{i}^{+}=\lambda^{+}(\{A:i\in A\})
and
m_{i}^{-}=\lambda^{-}(\{A:i\in A\})
. The condition
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{\perp}
says
m_{i}^{+}=m_{i}^{-}
.

Construct
\mathcal{A}
from the positive active sets and the complements of the negative active sets, with weights inherited from
\lambda^{+}
and
\lambda^{-}
. The total weight is
p+q=1
, and the frequency of item
i
is
m_{i}^{+}+(q-m_{i}^{-})=q
. Positive active sets have deficit zero. If
N
is negative active, then
N\sqcup N^{c}=U
, so
1
-additivity gives
|-M+f(N^{c})-u|\leqslant 1
; hence
M-f(N^{c})\leqslant 1-u
. Averaging with respect to the
\lambda^{-}
-weights gives the stated deficit bound for
\mathcal{A}
.

Similarly, construct
\mathcal{B}
from the negative active sets and the complements of the positive active sets. Its total weight is
1
, and the frequency of item
i
is
m_{i}^{-}+(p-m_{i}^{+})=p
. Negative active sets have surplus zero. If
P
is positive active, then
P\sqcup P^{c}=U
gives
|M+f(P^{c})-u|\leqslant 1
, whence
M+f(P^{c})\leqslant 1+u
. Averaging with respect to the
\lambda^{+}
-weights gives the stated surplus bound for
\mathcal{B}
.
∎

3.
Mixed intersections and recombination

We shall repeatedly use the following consequence of approximate additivity.

Lemma 3.1

(Approximate modularity)
.

Let
f\colon 2^{U}\to\mathbb{R}
be
1
-additive. Then, for all
A,B\subseteq U
,

(4)

|f(A)+f(B)-f(A\cup B)-f(A\cap B)|\leqslant 2.

Consequently, if
A
and
B
have deficits
a
and
b
, meaning
f(A)=M-a
and
f(B)=M-b
, then
A\cap B
has deficit at most
a+b+2
. The same assertion holds for surpluses after replacing
f
by
-f
.

Proof.

Set
I=A\cap B
,
X=A\setminus B
, and
Y=B\setminus A
. Then
A=I\sqcup X
,
B=I\sqcup Y
, and
A\cup B=I\sqcup X\sqcup Y
. Applying
1
-additivity to the disjoint unions gives

|f(A)-f(I)-f(X)|\leqslant 1,\qquad|f(A\cup B)-f(B)-f(X)|\leqslant 1.

Subtracting these two estimates gives (
4
).

If
f(A)=M-a
and
f(B)=M-b
, then
|f(A\cup B)|\leqslant M
, so (
4
) gives

f(A\cap B)\geqslant f(A)+f(B)-f(A\cup B)-2\geqslant M-a-b-2.

This is precisely the claimed deficit bound. The surplus version follows by applying the same argument to
-f
.
∎

Given a multiset
\mathcal{C}
, write
\mathcal{C}^{\cap\ell}
for the multiset of
\ell
-fold intersections of
\ell
independently chosen members of
\mathcal{C}
, equivalently for the product distribution on
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}
. For an integer
\ell\geqslant 1
and a rational
\tau\in[0,1]
, define

\mathcal{C}^{(\ell,\tau)}=(1-\tau)\mathcal{C}^{\cap\ell}+\tau\mathcal{C}^{\cap(\ell+1)}

as the corresponding weighted mixture. Since
\tau
is rational in our applications, this can again be realised as a finite multiset after clearing denominators.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose that every item appears in at most a
t
-fraction of the members of
\mathcal{C}
, and that the average deficit of
\mathcal{C}
is at most
E
. Then
\mathcal{C}^{(\ell,\tau)}
has item frequencies at most

(1-\tau)t^{\ell}+\tau t^{\ell+1}

and average deficit at most

\ell E+2(\ell-1)+\tau(E+2).

The same statement holds with deficits replaced by surpluses.

Proof.

Item frequencies multiply under independent intersections, so
\mathcal{C}^{\cap m}
has item frequencies at most
t^{m}
. Repeatedly applying
Lemma

3.1
shows that an
m
-fold intersection has deficit at most the sum of the
m
source deficits plus
2(m-1)
. Thus
\mathcal{C}^{\cap\ell}
has average deficit at most
\ell E+2(\ell-1)
, while
\mathcal{C}^{\cap(\ell+1)}
has average deficit at most
(\ell+1)E+2\ell
. Taking the weighted average with weights
1-\tau
and
\tau
gives the displayed bound. The surplus estimate is the same argument applied to
-f
.
∎

We use a standard expander-recombination lemma. An
(\alpha,r,\theta)
-expander will mean a bipartite multigraph
G=(V,W;E)
, parallel edges allowed, such that

|V|=2k,\qquad|W|=2\theta k,\qquad|E|=2rk,

and every set of at most
2\alpha k
vertices of
V
has at least that many distinct neighbours in
W
, where neighbours are counted as distinct right vertices, not as parallel edges. We say that such expanders exist if they exist for all sufficiently large admissible
k
, where admissible means that
2\theta k
and
2rk
are integers and the relevant biregular construction has integral degrees. For the rational parameters below the admissible sizes form an infinite arithmetic progression.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
(\alpha,r,\theta)
-expanders exist. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite multiset with item frequencies at most
\alpha
and average deficit at most
D
. Then, after recombination, one obtains a target multiset with item frequencies at most
\alpha/\theta
. If
D^{\prime}
denotes its average deficit, then

(5)

(1-\theta)M\leqslant D-\theta D^{\prime}+2r-1-\theta.

In particular,

(6)

M\leqslant\frac{D+2r-1-\theta}{1-\theta}.

The same assertions hold with deficits replaced by surpluses.

Proof.

By duplicating
\mathcal{C}
, we may assume that its cardinality is
2k
, where
k
is sufficiently large and admissible for the chosen expander family. This is possible because the admissible values of
2k
form an infinite arithmetic progression; choose an admissible multiple of the original multiset size. Duplication changes neither item frequencies nor average deficits.

Label the vertices of
V
by the source sets
(C_{v})_{v\in V}
. For each item
i\in U
, the set of source vertices containing
i
has cardinality at most
2\alpha k
. Hall’s condition, supplied by the expander property, gives a matching from these source occurrences into distinct neighbours in
W
. If several parallel edges join the matched pair
(v,w)
, choose one of them; parallel edges are merely separate labels for edge pieces. Put item
i
into the chosen edge piece. Doing this for every item partitions each source set
C_{v}
into edge pieces
(C_{e})_{e\ni v}
, and at each target vertex
w\in W
the incoming edge pieces are disjoint, because a fixed item is matched to at most one source occurrence ending at
w
. Let
T_{w}
be their union. The target item frequencies are at most
(2\alpha k)/(2\theta k)=\alpha/\theta
.

Let
d_{v}=M-f(C_{v})
and
d^{\prime}_{w}=M-f(T_{w})
. Iterated
1
-additivity gives, for each source vertex,

\sum_{e\ni v}f(C_{e})\geqslant f(C_{v})-(\deg v-1),

and, for each target vertex,

\sum_{e\ni w}f(C_{e})\leqslant f(T_{w})+(\deg w-1).

After summing over all source and target vertices and using
|E|=2rk
,
|V|=2k
, and
|W|=2\theta k
, we get

2k(M-D)-2k(r-1)\leqslant 2\theta k(M-D^{\prime})+2k(r-\theta).

This rearranges to (
5
). The surplus version follows by applying the same proof to
-f
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
(\alpha,r,\theta)
-expanders exist. Let
\mathcal{C}_{+}
and
\mathcal{C}_{-}
be two finite multisets, each with item frequencies at most
\alpha
. Assume that the average deficit of
\mathcal{C}_{+}
is
D
, and the average surplus of
\mathcal{C}_{-}
is
S
. After recombining both multisets, let
D^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime}
be the corresponding target average deficit and target average surplus. Then

(7)

(1-\theta)M\leqslant\frac{D+S}{2}-\theta\frac{D^{\prime}+S^{\prime}}{2}+2r-1-\theta.

The target item frequencies are at most
\alpha/\theta
.

Proof.

Apply
Lemma

3.3
to
f
and
\mathcal{C}_{+}
, and then to
-f
and
\mathcal{C}_{-}
, observing that surplus for
f
is deficit for
-f
. Average the two inequalities. The target-frequency assertion follows from the one-sided construction.
∎

4.
The four expander families

We need four explicit expander families. Their existence follows from the standard Pippenger random biregular construction. We include the verification because the final constant depends on these numerical parameters.

For
a>b>0
set

h(a,b)=a\log a-b\log b-(a-b)\log(a-b).

For an integer
r\geqslant 3
and
\theta=r/c
, where
c>r
is an integer, define

(8)

\Phi_{r,\theta}(x)=h(1,x)+h(\theta,x)+h(rx/\theta,rx)-h(r,rx).

Lemma 4.1

(Pippenger entropy check)
.

Let
0<\alpha<\theta<1
, let
r\geqslant 3
be an integer, and let
\theta=r/c
for an integer
c>r
. Suppose that there is
\delta\in(0,\alpha)
such that

(i)

e^{2}\theta^{1-r}\delta^{r-2}<1/20
;

(ii)

\Phi_{r,\theta}(x)<0
for every
x\in[\delta,\alpha]
.

Then
(\alpha,r,\theta)
-expanders exist for every sufficiently large admissible
k
.

Proof.

Let
N=2k
, where
N
is sufficiently large and admissible. Form a random bipartite multigraph by taking
r
copies of each of the
N
left vertices and
c=r/\theta
copies of each of the
\theta N
right vertices, and then choosing a uniformly random perfect matching between the two sets of copies.

Fix
m=xN
left vertices. If their neighbourhood has size less than
m
, then it is contained in some set of
m
right vertices; this deliberately overcounts the bad event, since a neighbourhood of size at most
m-1
is contained in many such sets. For one specified set of
m
right vertices, the
rm
left copies must be matched into its
cm
right copies. Hence the probability of containment is exactly

\frac{\binom{cm}{rm}}{\binom{rN}{rm}}.

Therefore the expected number of bad left sets of size
m
is at most

(9)

\binom{N}{m}\binom{\theta N}{m}\frac{\binom{cm}{rm}}{\binom{rN}{rm}}.

For the mid-range
\delta N\leqslant m\leqslant\alpha N
, use the elementary entropy bounds; see, for example,
[
3
, Appendix A]
,

\binom{n}{j}\leqslant\exp\bigl(nH(j/n)\bigr),\qquad\binom{n}{j}\geqslant\frac{1}{n+1}\exp\bigl(nH(j/n)\bigr),

where
H(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t)
. In the notation
h(a,b)=aH(b/a)
, these give from (
9
)

\mathbb{E}_{m}\leqslant(rN+1)\exp\bigl(N\Phi_{r,\theta}(x)\bigr).

By the assumption
\Phi_{r,\theta}<0
and compactness of
[\delta,\alpha]
, choose
\eta>0
such that
\Phi_{r,\theta}(x)\leqslant-\eta
throughout this interval. Hence

\sum_{\delta N\leqslant m\leqslant\alpha N}\mathbb{E}_{m}\leqslant N(rN+1)\exp(-\eta N)\longrightarrow 0.

In the four rows used below the certificate gives the explicit margin
\eta=1/1000
.

For
m=xN\leqslant\delta N
, first note the binomial-ratio bound

\frac{\binom{cm}{rm}}{\binom{rN}{rm}}\leqslant\left(\frac{cm}{rN}\right)^{rm}=\left(\frac{m}{\theta N}\right)^{rm}.

Indeed, factor the ratio as
\prod_{j=0}^{rm-1}(cm-j)/(rN-j)
; since
cm\leqslant rN
, each factor is at most
cm/(rN)
. The estimate
\binom{n}{j}\leqslant(en/j)^{j}
then gives

\binom{N}{m}\binom{\theta N}{m}\left(\frac{m}{\theta N}\right)^{rm}\leqslant\left(e^{2}\theta^{1-r}x^{r-2}\right)^{m}\leqslant\left(e^{2}\theta^{1-r}\delta^{r-2}\right)^{m}.

By assumption the sum of these bounds over all
m\geqslant 1
is at most

\sum_{m\geqslant 1}(1/20)^{m}=\frac{1}{19}.

Together with the mid-range estimate, this shows that the expected number of bad sets is less than
1
for all sufficiently large admissible
N
. Hence a graph with no bad set exists.
∎

Proof of the bound

We now prove the finite normalised theorem. By
Lemma

1.2
and scaling this suffices.

Let
f\colon 2^{U}\to\mathbb{R}
be
1

M=\|f\|_{\infty}.

We then use the notation
p,q,u
introduced above. Thus
q\leqslant 1/2
and
-1\leqslant u\leqslant 1
. Set

(12)

q_{0}=\frac{7437}{15625},\qquad p_{0}=1-q_{0}=\frac{8188}{15625},\qquad\alpha_{1}=\frac{1003}{10000},\qquad\alpha_{2}=\frac{47}{625}.

Define

(13)

\tau_{1}=\frac{q_{0}^{3}-\alpha_{1}}{q_{0}^{3}-q_{0}^{4}},\qquad\tau_{2}=\frac{p_{0}^{4}-\alpha_{2}}{p_{0}^{4}-p_{0}^{5}}.

The exact verifier checks
0\leqslant\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\leqslant 1
, and these inequalities are also clear numerically:

\tau_{1}=0.133233403\ldots,\qquad\tau_{2}=0.005861817\ldots.

Case 1:
q\leqslant q_{0}

Apply

3.2
to
\mathcal{A}
with
\ell=3
and

. The mixed multiset has item frequencies at most

(1-\tau_{1})q^{3}+\tau_{1}q^{4}\leqslant(1-\tau_{1})q_{0}^{3}+\tau_{1}q_{0}^{4}=\alpha_{1},

and average deficit at most

D_{1}:=6q_{0}+4+\tau_{1}(2q_{0}+2).

Here we used

, and the right-hand side is increasing in

.

Apply

. Let

be the target average deficit. By

,

The target item frequencies are at most

may be applied to the target multiset. If

M\leqslant 15+\frac{7}{3}D^{\prime}-\frac{4}{3}D^{\prime\prime}.

by the definition of

, dropping the negative term gives

Balancing (

) and (

7.
Further questions

The numerical value in
Theorem

1.1
is
highly unlikely
to be the limit of the method. We record several concrete directions.

(Q1)

Optimise the expander rows.
The four rows used here are chosen so that they fit the two mixed-intersection constructions and admit short rational certificates. One can ask for the optimal triples
(\alpha,r,\theta)
for the present recombination inequalities, either within Pippenger’s random biregular model or among more structured graph families. Even small improvements in the rows near
(1003/10000,4,1/3)
and
(47/625,4,2/7)
would feed directly into the final affine balance.

(Q2)

Use explicit lossless or unbalanced expanders.
The required graph property is one-sided vertex expansion for all left sets up to a fixed linear density. This is close in spirit to lossless expansion and unbalanced-expander constructions from theoretical computer science; see, for example,
[
5
,
8
]
. It would be useful to know whether such constructions give better certified constants, or whether their asymptotic advantages occur in parameter ranges too sparse for the present Kalton–Roberts application.

(Q3)

Can Ramanujan graphs help?
Ramanujan graphs optimise spectral expansion, and explicit constructions such as those of Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak and Morgenstern are among the strongest known bounded-degree expanders
[
11
,
12
]
. The present proof, however, needs small-set vertex expansion in a bipartite, imbalanced, possibly multi-graph setting. A natural question is whether spectral estimates, perhaps via the expander-mixing lemma or refined vertex-expansion inequalities, can certify rows competitive with the Pippenger rows used here. The general relationship between spectral expansion and the expansion properties needed in combinatorial applications is surveyed in
[
2
,
9
]
.

(Q4)

Search over deeper intersection patterns.
We only mix two neighbouring levels, namely triple/fourfold intersections in Case 1 and fourfold/fivefold intersections on one side in Case 2. Allowing mixtures of three or more levels would produce a finite-dimensional linear optimisation problem coupled to expander-row certification. It is open whether this extra flexibility lowers the constant substantially.

(Q5)

Lean-certified optimisation.
The repository
[
1
]
formalises the final selected proof. A more ambitious project would formalise the search procedure itself: generate candidate rational parameters, certify the entropy inequalities, and emit Lean terms proving the resulting bound. This would turn the numerical part of the Kalton–Roberts upper-bound problem into a reproducible proof-producing optimisation pipeline.
