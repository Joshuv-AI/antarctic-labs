Accomplishments

What are the major goals of the project?

 The major goals of this work are twofold: (1) to enable transformative basic understanding
of structure-property-performance relationships governing oxygen transport in oxygen-active
materials and (2) facilitate the discovery and rational design of new oxygen-active materials which
transport oxygen efficiently at low temperature. Transformative understanding and materials
design will be accomplished by synergistically combining materials data mining, machine learning,
high-throughput computation and targeted experiments.

What was accomplished under these goals?

Summary of Key Accomplishments

During this project, we have realized a number of key accomplishments, which we summarize
here. For brevity, in this final report we focus on only core components of the work, with a bias
toward the latest progress made in the last year of the grant. More technical details on how each
of these accomplishments were made are provided in the following subsections.
 1.) Discovery of new fast interstitial oxygen conductors: We have used a hierarchy of
 screening criteria and computationally discovered two new oxide structural families that
 are predicted to rapidly conduct oxygen with an interstitial-mediated diffusion mechanism:
 general structure formula A4B5C4O22+δ and AB2C4O12+δ, with specific investigated
 compositions of La4Mn5Si4O22+δ and ZrMn2Ge4O12+δ, respectively. These two new
 materials have very low Ab Initio Molecular Dynamics (AIMD)-calculated O migration
 barriers of 0.44 +/- 0.02 eV and 0.34 +/- 0.03 eV, respectively. From experiments, we found
 that La4Mn5Si4O22+δ has higher oxygen-ion conductivity than the widely used yttria-
 stabilized ZrO2, and among the highest surface oxygen exchange rates at the intermediate
 temperature of known materials.
 2.) Discovery of new fast vacancy-mediated oxygen conductors: We have combined data
 mining and unsupervised machine learning analysis and discovered a new structural class
 of vacancy-mediated oxygen conductor. This structure has the general formula Bi2MO4X
 (M= rare earth, X= halogen), where we focus on the specific composition LaBi2O4Cl. Its
 2D layered structure, featuring a “triple fluorite” layer, supports diffusion of both oxygen
 vacancies and interstitials. In addition to vacancy diffusion with a 0.1 eV barrier, ab initio
 studies show interstitial diffusion exhibits a modest barrier of 0.6−0.8 eV. Frenkel pairs are
 found to be the dominant defects in intrinsic LaBi2O4Cl, facilitating significant vacancy-
 mediated oxygen diffusion at elevated temperatures. With 2.8% oxygen vacancies,
 LaBi2O4Cl is predicted to achieve a conductivity of 0.3 S/cm at 25 °C in a single crystal.
 Experimental synthesis and characterization of polycrystalline LaBi2O4Cl and Sr-doped
 LaBi2O4Cl revealed conductivity exceeding that of YSZ and LSGM below 400 °C, with
 lower activation energies, achieving a total conductivity of 0.1−0.2 mS/cm at 300 °C.
 While these results confirm its potential of fast oxygen transport, we suggest further
 experimental optimization of LaBi2O4Cl, including aliovalent doping and microstructure
 refinement, could significantly enhance its performance, facilitating fast oxygen
 conduction approaching room temperature.

 3.) Understanding oxygen conduction in novel materials families: We have used NEB
 calculations and analysis of AIMD trajectories to elucidate the O defect migration
 pathways and diffusion mechanism in our promising new materials. In addition, we have
 fit machine learning interatomic potentials (IP) to our AIMD data to enable machine
 learning fitted IP molecular dynamics (IPMD) simulation of O diffusion in domains where
 AIMD simulations are not possible (e.g. low temperatures). We have shown results of O
 migration energies from these fitted machine learning potentials agree very well with the
 AIMD calculations, and are orders of magnitude faster to evaluate, thus providing a useful
 tool for accelerated and lower-temperature study of these promising systems. In particular,
 the use of machine learning fitted IPMD has demonstrated that Bi2LaO4Cl has a crossover
 in diffusion mechanism at about 1000K to a lower barrier mechanism. In addition, we find
 the fast oxygen kinetics in La4Mn5Si4O22+δ is the result of simultaneously active interstitial
 and interstitialcy diffusion pathways. We propose that the essential features for forming an
 effective interstitial oxygen conductor are the availability of electrons and structural
 flexibility, enabling sufficient accessible volume.

Computational discovery and experimental assessment of new La4Mn5Si4O22+𝜹 material
(A4B5C4O22+𝜹 family)

 In this work, we combined physically-motivated structure and property descriptors, density
functional theory (DFT) simulations, and experiments to demonstrate an approach to discover new
fast interstitial O conductors. Multiple new families were found which adopt completely different
structures from known O conductors. From these families, we synthesized and studied O kinetics
in La4Mn5Si4O22+𝜹 (LMS), a representative member of perrierite/chevkinite (A4B5C4O22+𝜹) family.
We found through electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA) measurements that LMS contains O
interstitials, has higher O ionic conductivity than the widely used yttria-stabilized ZrO2, and has
among the highest surface O exchange rates at intermediate temperature of known materials. The
fast O kinetics is the result of simultaneously active interstitial and interstitialcy diffusion pathways.
This work developed and demonstrated a powerful approach for discovering new families of
interstitial O conductors and suggests many more such materials remain to be discovered.
 We proposed a set of simple descriptors (features) for discovering interstitial O conductors
based on material structure, composition, and readily available property data. As shown in Figure
1, we screened nearly 34k oxide materials with the five descriptors and retained 519 compounds,
which were classified into 345 unique structural groups based on structural similarity analysis[1]
using the pymatgen package.[2] One candidate for each group was selected for further validation
with ab initio studies. It is striking to note the power of these simple descriptors based on physical-
intuition-guided hypotheses, as we quickly winnowed the field of 34k oxides down to 345, a 99%
reduction in the search space enabled by basic analysis of material structure and composition.

 DFT calculations were used to validate the findings of our simple descriptor approach
computationally by calculating O interstitial formation energy and migration barriers. From these
DFT calculations and extended AIMD runs, 3 compounds with migration barriers <0.5 eV were
identified as members of promising new families of interstitial O conductors, which were
K2Mn2(MoO4)3, LMS, and CeMn2Ge4O12. These three compounds are members of families of
double molybdates A2TM2(MoO4)3 (A=alkali metal, TM=transition metal),[3]
perrierite/chevkinite RE4TM5Si4O22 (RE=rare earth, TM=transition metal),[4] and germinates
RE1TM2Ge4O12 (RE= rare earth, TM=transition metal),[5] respectively. These new families are a
conservative estimate of the true number which could be uncovered with our present approach as
many compounds have not yet been fully studied by AIMD and no effort was made to explore
oxide structures not available in the Materials Project. Figure 2 shows the O diffusion path and
calculated diffusivity for LMS. LMS was selected as a representative member of the
perrierite/chevkinite family for experimental investigation due to its predicted fast interstitial O
diffusion, simple established synthesis method,[6] and inclusion of inexpensive, easy-accessible,
and non-toxic elements, which are all traits desirable for new O-active materials potentially useful
in a variety of applications.
 We note that all ab initio studies for LMS in this study were performed using the strongly
constrained and appropriately normed (SCAN) functional,[7] as it outperformed other functionals
in predicting all available experimental data such as lattice parameters, optical band gap, interstitial
oxygen concentration and diffusion barrier acquired in this study. Ab initio studies and simple
thermodynamic considerations suggest that excess oxygen with La4Mn5Si4O22+𝜹 ( 𝛿 ≈ 0.5) is
thermodynamically favorable under air conditions, while oxygen vacancies are very unfavorable

due to their high formation energy. In Figure 2a, the most stable interstitial site (Oi1) lies in
between two adjacent sorosilicate Si2O7 groups, connecting two Si tetrahedra, and the second most
stable interstitial site (Oi2) lies in the joint of the Si2O7 and the Mn32+ octahedra. These two
prevailing interstitial sites contribute two distinct and competitive diffusion pathways. In the
interstitial diffusion mechanism (yellow arrow in Figure 2a), the Oi hops between the Oi1 sites
through the channel between sorosilicate chains along the a-axis. A parallel active interstitialcy
(cooperative “knock-on”) mechanism is indicated by cyan arrows, in which the Oi moves along
the corner-sharing Si2O7-MnO2-Si2O7 framework along the b-axis. In the interstitialcy mechanism,
the Oi first hops from the Oi1 site to a lattice site by kicking a lattice oxygen to the Oi2 site, which
then moves to a lattice site by kicking another lattice oxygen to the next Oi1 site. The energy
landscape along the diffusion pathways reveals migration barriers of 0.69 eV and 0.74 eV for the
interstitial and interstitialcy mechanisms, respectively, calculated by Climbing Image Nudged
Elastic Band (CI-NEB) method. These values are in excellent agreement with the experimentally
determined activation energy of 0.78 eV discussed below. Both pathways contribute to the
diffusion, supported by their simultaneous occurrences AIMD simulations and the small energetic
difference (0.05 eV) in migration barriers for both mechanisms. To confirm the prevalence of these
two diffusion mechanisms across a broad temperature range, extensive molecular dynamics
simulations were conducted by AIMD using generalized gradient approximation exchange-
correlation functional of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE)[8] and machine learning-
trained interatomic potential molecular dynamics (ML-IPMD) simulations (we did not use SCAN
for long-time AIMD simulation due to its high computational cost). The oxygen tracer diffusion
coefficient achieved from GGA-PBE AIMD and ML-IPMD consistently confirms to the Arrhenius
fitting over a wide temperature range. The AIMD migration barrier shows excellent agreement
with the ML-IPMD results, as well as the GGA-PBE CI-NEB barriers. These results demonstrate
the dual diffusion mechanism dominates the oxygen ion diffusion at both high and low
temperatures. The DFT predictions of stable interstitials with low migration barriers indicate that
La4Mn5Si4O22+𝜹 is a fast oxygen conductor.
 Nearly all known interstitial oxygen conductors diffuse oxygen ions through an
interstitialcy mechanism. The prevalence of the interstitialcy mechanism is likely due to the large
size of the oxygen anion and the lack of available diffusion channels. A pure interstitial diffusion
mechanism through channels is rare, and, to our knowledge, only found in apatites and scheelite,
which both feature isolated polyhedra. LMS adopts a dual-diffusion mechanism, where the oxygen
diffusion consists of both interstitial and interstitialcy pathways. The pure interstitial diffusion
channel between the unconnected sorosilicate chains has a slightly lower energy barrier than the
interstitialcy pathway, with a difference of 0.05 eV according to the SCAN CI-NEB calculations.
The interstitial diffusion occurs due to the free space between the zigzag-arranged unconnected
sorosilicate chains and the adjacent Mn32+ ion, which create a stable channel for the interstitial
oxygen. The interstitialcy diffusion happens through the rotation of the corner-sharing framework
Si2O7-MnO2-Si2O7, which help stabilize the interstitial oxygen and facilitate diffusion. This dual
mechanism is distinct from any confirmed mechanisms for an interstitial oxygen conductor of
which we are aware.

Computational discovery and experimental assessment of new ZrMn 2Ge4O12+𝜹 material
(AB2C4O12+𝜹 family)

 The physical descriptor-based interstitial materials screening procedure shown in Figure 1
and used to discover the new interstitial diffusing material LMS detailed above also resulted in
finding of the material ZrMn2Ge4O12+𝜹 (ZMG). ZMG is isostructural with CeMn2Ge4O12, with
general formula AB2C4O12, and is an oxide material with the tetragonal space group P4/nbm (a =
b = 9.77 Å, c = 9.83 Å; 𝜶 = 𝜷 = 𝜸 = 90°).[10] Its bulk structure is shown in Figure 4a, and
experimental XRD scan confirming the structure is in Figure 4d. Members of AB2C4O12 which
have low interstitial O formation energies, include but are not limited to ZMG, ZrCo2Ge4O12,
ZrCo2Ge4O12, Y(YMn)Ge4O12, and Y(YCo)Ge4O12. Among these members, ZMG shows a very
low migration barrier of ≈0.33 eV from both AIMD simulation and NEB calculation.
 From AIMD simulations of ZMG, we found the O migration pathway shows
an interstitialcy (or “kick-out”) mechanism, shown in Figure 4a. NEB method shows that the
migration barrier is 0.33 eV (Figure 4b). Figure 4c shows the migration barrier predicted by fitting
an Arrhenius functional form to the O-ion diffusivity calculated by the AIMD and ML fitted IPMD
simulations using the moment tensor potential method,[11–13] fit to the AIMD data. The O

Unsupervised ML discovers new vacancy-mediated fast O conductor LaBi2O4Cl

 In this work, we conducted an unsupervised ML analysis of the >60k O-containing
compounds in the Materials Project database to find new fast O conductors.[14,15] Our search
identified the MBi2O4X (M=rare-earth element, X=halogen element) structure family as a
promising class of fast O conductors. By the coupled use of ab initio simulations and experiments,
the representative material LaBi2O4Cl (LBC) was revealed to exhibit fast O diffusion through an
O vacancy-mediated mechanism and to have an unusual mechanism for forming such vacancies

 8

from Frenkel pair defects. Our experiments reveal LBC has higher O conductivity than commonly
used solid-state O electrolyte materials at low temperatures of <400 °C, and that further
engineering of LBC, for example, through aliovalent doping or grain size refinement, may lead to
further significant improvements in O conductivity. Realizing the full predicted potential O
mobility of LBC at low temperatures presents an opportunity to expand the use of O-based energy
conversion technologies at low temperatures. These results demonstrate the power of combining
existing materials databases with unsupervised ML approaches to discover new classes of
functional materials.
 Oxygen ion mobility in the presence of various oxygen defects was examined through
AIMD simulations. We systematically examined the diffusion behavior for intrinsic LBC, LBC
with extrinsic oxygen vacancies (LBC+𝑉𝑂⋅⋅), and LBC with extrinsic interstitial oxygen (LBC+𝑂𝑖′′ ).
A fractional concentration of approximately 2.8% (or 1/36, which is one defect in a 3 × 3 × 1
supercell) for either vacancies or interstitials was examined. The MSD plots for all atoms suggest
that oxygen is the only species exhibiting significant diffusion in these systems, while other atoms
remain at their respective lattice sites over the studied temperature range from 600 K to 2000 K.
Figure 5a presents the self-diffusivity 𝐷 ∗ of oxygen species at different temperatures for the three
systems, in which extremely high diffusivity of oxygen in LBC+𝑉𝑂⋅⋅ is observed. The diffusion
mechanisms identified in AIMD simulation are further analyzed using Climbing Image Nudged
Elastic Band (CI-NEB) calculations to assess their migration barriers. As shown in Figure 5b-c,
the vacancy-mediated diffusion mechanism involves oxygen ions hopping through vacancies in
the Bi2O2 layer with a hop distance of 2.8 Å. The migration barriers are determined to be 0.09 eV
for extrinsic defect 𝑉𝑂⋅⋅ and 0.11 eV for 𝑉𝑂 associated with a Frenkel pair. In Figure 5d-e, the
interstitial-mediated (i.e., interstitialcy) diffusion mechanism involves an interstitial oxygen
displacing a lattice oxygen to a new interstitial site. The migration barriers for interstitial-mediated
mechanism are found as 0.60 eV for 𝑂𝑖 associated with a Frenkel pair and 0.80 eV for extrinsic
defect 𝑂𝑖′′ .
 For intrinsic LBC (yellow data in Figure 5a), the spontaneous formation of Frenkel pairs
is observed in AIMD studies at 1000K and above, within a total simulation time of about 400-800
ps. The oxygen diffusion is dominated by a vacancy-mediated mechanism, and the overall
activation barrier is 0.75 ± 0.01 eV, derived by fitting the Arrhenius relation. Using the Nernst-
Einstein relation, our simulations predict an ionic conductivity of 0.1 S/cm around 950˚C for
intrinsic LBC, with a thermally excited Frenkel pair concentration of approximately 0.59% (given
as a percentage of oxygen atom involved in Frenkel pair formation). This predicted conductivity
of intrinsic LBC is within the range of current best fast oxygen conductors.
 For LBC with interstitial oxygen (LBC+𝑂𝑖′′ ) (red data in Figure 5a), the energy barrier of
𝑂𝑖′′ diffusion via an interstitial-mediated mechanism is calculated to be 0.80 eV using CI-NEB.
This energy barrier is comparable to the formation energy of a Frenkel pair, which is calculated to
be 0.79 eV at T= 0 K, indicating that 𝑂𝑖′′ diffusion and Frenkel pair formation will occur
approximately together. However, oxygen diffusion will primarily occur through oxygen
vacancies once a Frenkel pair forms, as oxygen vacancies have a significantly lower migration
barrier than interstitials. AIMD studies further confirm that Frenkel pair formation in LBC+𝑂𝑖′′
and oxygen ion diffusion through vacancy-mediated mechanism occur at 1000K and above, within
a total simulation time of about 460-780 ps. Our studies show that oxygen diffusion in LBC+𝑂𝑖′′
system is dominated by vacancy-mediated diffusion from Frenkel pairs, despite the presence of
excess 𝑂𝑖′′ . This mechanism results in a total activation energy of 0.77 ± 0.06 eV, which closely
aligns to intrinsic LBC, as both are dominated by the same defect formation and migration

 9

mechanism. In addition, the fact that LBC+𝑂𝑖′′ gives similar values of diffusivities and activation
energy as intrinsic LBC suggests that excess oxygen interstitials do not significantly impact the
oxygen mobility in LBC. However, the extra interstitial does increase the overall conductivity due
to their significant concentration, and the LBC+𝑂𝑖′′ (with 2.8% extrinsic interstitials) is predicted
to have an ionic conductivity of 0.1 S/cm at about 850 ˚C, compared to an ionic conductivity of
0.1 S/cm at about 950 ˚C noted above for the intrinsic LBC.
 For LBC with oxygen vacancy (LBC+𝑉𝑂⋅⋅) (green data in Figure 5a), distinct diffusion
behaviors are observed at different temperature regimes, attributed to the presence of different
defect types. At low temperatures, 𝑉𝑂⋅⋅ is the dominant defect, and oxygen ions diffuse through the
vacancy-mediated mechanism. This low temperature diffusion regime is characterized by an ultra-
low migration barrier of 0.10 ± 0.02 eV, in excellent agreement with the CI-NEB-derived barrier
of 0.09 eV in Figure 5b. As temperature increases, Frenkel pairs begin to form, creating new fast
diffusing vacancies as well as some new but slower diffusing interstitials. The additional defects
created by spontaneous Frenkel pair formation begin to impact the overall diffusion at about 1100
˚C, changing the slope of self-diffusivity and leading to an increased activation energy of 0.45 ±
0.07 eV, as shown in Figure 5a. With the presence of about 2.8% extrinsic oxygen vacancies,
LBC is predicted to achieve conductivity of 0.3 S/cm at 25 ˚C.

pure LBC powder and SLBC powder using a flux synthesis method.[16] X-ray diffraction (XRD)
analysis indicated that the as-synthesized LBC powder exhibits a monoclinic phase, which then
transformed to a tetragonal phase after sintering in pellet form at 950oC for 10 hours in flowing Ar
gas (Figure 6a). The obtained lattice parameters of LBC were found to be consistent with the ab
initio calculation results and previous reports[16,17] of monoclinic and tetragonal LBC. Figure

content (2.9%, based on XRD refinement), we anticipate that its effect on the overall conductivity
will be minimal.

 We attempted to measure bulk conductivity via impedance spectroscopy but encountered
interference issues above 1M Hz at low temperature region (below 400°C) and were not able to
obtain conclusive results at this stage. Instead, we focused on measuring total conductivity using
the 4-probe DC method and compared it with the total conductivity of other state-of-the-art
oxygen-ion conductors. The total conductivity measured using the 4-probe method consists of both
ionic and electronic components, and the electronic conductivity is considered negligible based on
the following points: (1) LBC has a sizable bandgap of 2.65 eV, which makes electron conduction
via thermal excitation unlikely; (2) the cations La3+ and Bi3+ do not readily change valence states,
ruling out electronic conductivity through polaron hopping; (3) total conductivity under varying
oxygen partial pressures show minimal variation, confirming that both LBC and SLBC exhibit
ionic conductor behavior. We note that this same ionic conductor behavior was found in the recent
study on ionic conductivity of Te-doped LBC.[18] Hence, the electronic conductivity was
considered negligible, and the total conductivity was assumed to be due to ionic conductivity. In
Figure 6c, the total conductivity of LBC and SLBC is comparable to many state-of-the-art fast
oxygen-conducting electrolyte materials. LBC shows total conductivities of 4.6, 2.8, and 1.1
mS/cm at 650, 600, and 500 oC, respectively, and 0.4, 0.2, and 0.1 mS/cm at 400, 350, and 300 oC.
SLBC shows total conductivities of 0.6, 0.4, and 0.2 mS/cm at 400, 350, and 300 oC, respectively.
Below 400°C, LBC and SLBC exceeds the performance of the widely used YSZ, are comparable
to LSGM, and have lower activation energies (as 0.53 ± 0.01 eV for LBC, and 0.39 ± 0.01 eV for
SLBC) than both materials. Te-doped LBC, from a recent study by Yashima et al.[18] has higher
total conductivity than both LBC and SLBC above 200 °C. Te-doped LBC features an interstitial
diffusion mechanism with an activation energy of 0.68 eV, which aligns well with our ab initio
predicted activation energy of 0.77 eV for LBC with interstitials.
 Figure 6d displays an overall comparison across the experimental total conductivity to the
bulk conductivity predicted by ab initio studies. The ab initio-predicted bulk conductivity was
derived from simulations on a single, bulk crystal structure, without accounting for the effects of
grain boundaries, surfaces, secondary phases or other defects (e.g., other point defects or defect
clusters), which can affect the total conductivity experimentally. Despite this, the large gap
between predicted bulk conductivity and the experimental total conductivity indicates that the
ultra-high predicted conductivity in LBC+𝑉𝑂 is not being observed in our samples. We speculate
the low experimental conductivity in LBC and SLBC may be attributable to insufficient mobile
oxygen vacancies or grain boundary effects, factors that do not affect activation energy but can be
optimized through doping and microstructure control to achieve higher oxygen conductivity.

Impact

What is the impact on the development of the principal discipline(s) of the project?

The work to date suggests the following:
 1.) Oxides with interstitial defects and predominantly interstitial diffusion may be significantly
 more common than previously thought and that we will be successful in expanding the set
 of known such materials.

 15

 2.) There are new vacancy-mediated fast oxygen diffusing structural types than those currently
 known, potentially unlocking new oxide structural families to engineer fast oxygen
 conduction, especially at low temperatures.
 3.) The use of machine learning potentials fitted to AIMD data not only agree very well with
 AIMD-calculated oxygen diffusion over a wide temperature range, but can be fit in a few
 months and is orders of magnitude faster, thus enabling study of oxygen diffusion under
 conditions inaccessible with AIMD.

What is the impact on other disciplines?

 In the future, new materials discovered from this work may impact the types of chemical
reactions accessible at low temperature (impacting the chemistry field) and result in changes to
gas reformation plant process design (impacting the chemical engineering field).

historically underrepresented groups in science, technology, engineering and mathematics
(STEM), in this case a female postdoc and a student who is Hispanic and a member of The
Chickasaw Tribe.
