UniRank: Unified Rank Allocation for Low-Rank LLM Compression
Chao Han, Haozhe Hu, Fei Ma, Wei Zhang, Xiaoyu Shen

Abstract
Low-rank decomposition serves as a promising compression paradigm for large language models, however, rank allocation remains challenging: manual rules lack generalizability, and learning-based approaches incur heavy computational overhead. To address these issues, we formulate global low-rank allocation as a sorting-and-truncation pipeline, and score each singular component via dual criteria: Local singular energy ratio that quantifies the intrinsic importance within the decomposed parameter matrix and Global functional importance (measured by input-output cosine similarity) that evaluates the functional significance of decomposed modules. We verify the strong correlation between high input-output cosine similarity and low effective rank through geometric interpretation and experimental validation. Furthermore, we propose rank-preserving fine-tuning, which performs direct LoRA tuning on decomposed weights and avoids extra information loss caused by re-truncation in conventional merging pipelines. Empirical results confirm that our method delivers sustained performance enhancements when combined with models featuring distinct decomposition schemes, model sizes and architectural designs, e.g. in one-shot compression without further fine-tuning, our method reduces perplexity by up to 50% compared with uniform and heuristic allocation baselines. Code will be available at https://github.com/EIT-NLP/LLM-Pruning.

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved state-of-the-art results across various domains, including question answering
31
;
37
, code generation
7
;
54
, and mathematical reasoning
1
;
42
. However, their high inference cost remains a major barrier to practical deployment
17
. Low-rank approximation is an attractive structured compression technique because it replaces large linear transformations with compact factorized modules, preserves the original Transformer architecture, and can bring tangible inference acceleration
21
. Recent studies further reveal inherent low-rank structure in Transformer weights
43
;
40
, making low-rank compression a promising direction for efficient LLMs.

A practical low-rank pipeline must solve two coupled problems: how to decompose each weight matrix and how many singular components to keep for each matrix. While prior work has made substantial progress on decomposition design
23
;
19
;
53
, the rank allocation problem remains less systematically explored. Existing strategies typically fall into three categories. Uniform allocation
47
assigns the same rank ratio to all layers, overlooking the heterogeneous importance of different parameters. Heuristic allocation
28
relies on manually designed architectural or statistical rules, which may not generalize across models. Learning-based allocation
38
relaxes discrete rank selection into continuous mask optimization, but it introduces substantial training overhead and is sensitive to initialization. These limitations call for a rank allocation method that is fine-grained, generalizable, and inexpensive.

Ideally, the optimal rank allocation can be obtained by evaluating model performance over all combinations of reserved singular vectors for all parameter matrices, yet such exhaustive combinatorial search is computationally intractable and infeasible for large-scale language models. Instead, we simplify the intractable global optimization problem into a concise
Sorting-and-Truncation (S&T)
pipeline over all singular vectors derived from full model weight matrices, on the premise of constructing a reliable scalar metric to quantify the importance of each singular component. Different from previous rank assignment strategies, our paradigm integrates both structural reconstruction and functional impact for comprehensive measurement.

Our core insight is that the overall importance of a singular vector depends on two critical factors. The first is
local structural significance
, which measures the singular vector’s contribution to reconstructing the original weight matrix. Following the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem
10
, optimal low-rank reconstruction is driven by the magnitude of singular values, so we quantify this using the normalized squared singular value energy ratio,
\sigma_{k}^{2}/\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}^{2}
. The second is
global functional importance
, which assesses the host module’s impact on the overall network. To evaluate this without heavy computation, we exploit the intrinsic residual architecture of modern pre-norm LLMs
45
;
46
;
16
;
49
;
8
, formulated as
Y=X+f(X)
(Figure
1
(a)). Due to the widespread use of scale-invariant normalization (e.g., RMSNorm
52
), the semantic representation of information is strictly encoded in the directional vector space rather than its magnitude
11
. Consequently, we use layer-wise input-output cosine similarity computed on a small calibration set as a lightweight proxy to quantify module redundancy. Unlike previous methods that use similarity for layer pruning
4
;
33
;
55
;
9
;
18
, we establish it as a reliable indicator of functional effective rank. As illustrated in Figure
1
(b), a high input-output similarity implies that the layer transformation is either negligible (singular values
\approx 0
) or linearly correlated (effective rank
\approx 1
) with input features, both corresponds to a low effective rank.

Furthermore, existing low-rank compression methods suffer from a critical flaw when adapting to LoRA fine-tuning
20
. Since low-rank models are decomposed into two small factor matrices rather than full-rank weights, they fail to directly merge LoRA incremental parameters through standard weight reparameterization. Conventional schemes first reconstruct full-rank matrices by multiplying factors, integrate LoRA updates, then re-decompose and re-truncate to the target rank, which causes unavoidable information loss and extra computation
28
. To address this, we propose
Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning (RPFT)
, which splits the allocated rank
k
into a tunable part
k_{\text{LoRA}}
and a frozen part
k-k_{\text{LoRA}}
. This design eliminates re-decomposition and perfectly preserves fine-tuned information without truncation loss, enabling efficient adaptation and no extra information loss.

We evaluate our approach by instantiating a full compression and adaptation pipeline, which we name
UniRank
. UniRank is designed as a modular framework: S&T provides a plug-and-play rank allocation strategy, while RPFT enables post-compression tuning without exceeding the pre-defined rank budget. Our contribution can be summarized as:

•

We propose a global rank allocation framework that assigns low-rank budgets via a low-cost sorting-and-truncation pipeline.

•

We provide both geometric interpretation and experimental valiadition that connects high input-output cosine similarity to low effective rank, offering a lightweight criterion for low-rank compression.

•

We introduce Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning, a novel fine-tuning paradigm for low-rank models that enables efficient integration of LoRA updates on pre-decomposed factor matrices.

•

Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. With merely 2 minutes of rank allocation overhead, S&T reduces perplexity by up to 50% in zero-shot compression compared to handcrafted baselines. Equipped with RPFT, the downstream performance is further boosted to 90% of the dense model at 25% sparsity, consistently outperforming other SOTA methods.

Method

In this section, we present the proposed
UniRank
framework. We detail the
Sorting-and-Truncation (S&T)
module for adaptive rank budget assignment. Then, we describe the
Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning (RPFT)
scheme designed to efficiently fine-tune compressed models without extra information loss. Figure
2
illustrates how UniRank works.

3.1
Decomposition

Given a weight matrix
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
from LLM
linear
modules (we omit layer and type indices for clarity), we perform singular value decomposition (SVD) to factorize it into low-rank components:

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top},

(1)

where
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
and
\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
are orthogonal matrices of left and right singular vectors,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\text{diag}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})
with singular values
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{r}>0
, and
r=rank(W)\leq\min(m,n)
is the full rank. A rank-
k
approximation (
k<r
) is
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top}
, with approximation error proportional to the discarded singular values.

Sorting-and-Truncation (S&T).

We introduce
S&T
, a principled and architecture-agnostic strategy for adaptive rank allocation in low-rank decomposed LLMs. S&T operates by constructing a unified saliency metric across all parameters, enabling globally optimal rank assignment under budget constraint.

Figure 3:
Quantified relationship between performance drop (perplexity) and layer-wise global functional importance. A strong positive linear correlation is confirmed (Pearson
r=0.8792
).

In Section
1
, we have established that high input-output cosine similarity implies intrinsically low effective rank for a functional module, based on the geometric interpretation.
To further validate this intuition empirically, we conduct a layer-wise correlation analysis: we measure the performance drop caused by rank truncation while keeping 90% singular energy per layer, and compare it with
1-\mathbb{E}[\cos(x,y)]
computed on the calibration set.
As illustrated in Figure
3
, the two metrics exhibit a strong linear correlation with a Pearson coefficient of
0.8792
, corroborating that cosine similarity serves as a reliable indicator of layer sensitivity.

Accordingly, we define
global functional importance
as

\mathcal{I}_{l}=1-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{c}}\!\left[\cos(f_{l-1}(x),f_{l}(x))\right],

where
f_{l}(x)
denotes the output of layer
l
, and
\cos(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes average cosine similarity over tokens.
Intuitively, layers that induce larger feature transformations are assigned higher importance.

For local structural significance, we adopt the
singular energy ratio
: given decomposition results
U\Sigma V^{\top}
, each singular component
(u_{i},\sigma_{i},v_{i})
contributes normalized energy
\sigma_{i}^{2}/\|\Sigma\|_{F}^{2}
, reflecting how much variance it preserves.

S&T unifies these two factors into a aggregated importance score:

s_{l,i}=\mathcal{I}_{l}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{l,i}^{2}}{\|\Sigma_{l}\|_{F}^{2}},

(2)

which quantifies the importance of the
i
-th singular triplet in layer
l
.
All scores across the network are aggregated and sorted in descending order.
Notably, this pipeline requires no gradient-based optimization or handcrafted architectural priors.
Furthermore, S&T is fully decoupled from the base decomposition method and can be applied post-hoc to any SVD-based compressed model, making it a plug-and-play module for boosting rank efficiency.

Accumulative Rank Preservation

Following global sorting of all singular components, we observe that singular vectors from distinct weight matrices introduce heterogeneous parameter overhead. For an
m\times n
weight matrix, retaining one singular vector pair consumes
m+n
parameters. To address this variable parameter cost across matrices of different dimensions, we employ an accumulative rank preservation strategy. We first calculate the reserved parameter volume based on the total model parameters and target sparsity ratio, then iteratively select and aggregate the top singular vector pairs in descending order of importance until the total parameter consumption precisely reach the predefined budget.

3.2
Fine-Tuning

Low-rank compression decomposes weights into
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{\top}
, with
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
and
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
.

When LoRA meets Low-Rank Weights

In standard LoRA, a trainable low-rank incremental matrix
\Delta\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{A}_{\text{lora}}\mathbf{B}_{\text{lora}}^{\top}
is added to a frozen pre-trained weight
\mathbf{W}
, and during inference,
\Delta\mathbf{W}
is merged into
\mathbf{W}
via re-parameterization, i.e
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}=\mathbf{W}+\Delta\mathbf{W}
. However, for low-rank compressed model, the original matrix is already decomposed into low-rank matrices
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{\top}
, merging
\Delta\mathbf{W}
would require reconstructing a full matrix
[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}]^{\top}+\Delta\mathbf{W}
, and another SVD on the merged matrix, which would cause extra information loss (Appendix
A.2
gives an detailed analysis on rank dynamics).

Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning (RPFT)

We present RPFT to fix this issue, which optimizes partial singular components under constant reserved rank. Each compressed weight satisfies
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top}
. we divide retained singular vectors into two sets: (1)
\mathcal{I}_{\text{train}}
: a small number (typically 8 or 16) of singular vectors with low importance scores. (2)
\mathcal{I}_{\text{fix}}
: the remaining majority of singular vectors.

We directly optimize trainable vectors. The fine-tuned weight is written as:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{finetuned}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\text{fix}}}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\text{train}}}\sigma_{j}^{(t)}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{(t)}(\mathbf{v}_{j}^{(t)})^{\top},

(3)

where superscript
(t)
denotes trainable parameters. The rank stays bounded by
k
to avoid rank growth and information loss. RPFT uses sparse trainable units and discards re-decomposition, effectively reducing computation cost.

We conduct ablation on parameter grouping strategies for global rank allocation. We design three grouping settings: (1) parameter-level grouping, which sorts and truncates homogeneous projection parameters across all layers; (2) module-level grouping, which aggregates all parameters of the same functional module across layers; (3) our unified global setting, where all model parameters are ranked within a single group. Parameters across different groups are allocated rank budgets proportional to their original parameter sizes. Experimental results demonstrate that the unified global grouping consistently outperforms both parameter-level and module-level grouping. Separated grouping restricts cross-layer global singular component scheduling, leading to suboptimal rank allocation and inferior downstream performance. This validates the necessity of our holistic global rank assignment paradigm.

Hyperparameter Search for Global-Local Metric Balancing

While the base S&T metric, defined in Equation
2
exhibits strong generalization ability, we observe that minimal task-aware adaptation further enhances efficacy. We generalize S&T as:

s_{l,i}=(\mathcal{I}_{l})^{\alpha}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{l,i}^{2}}{\|\Sigma_{l}\|_{F}^{2}}

(4)

where adds
\alpha>0
for modulating the trade-off between structural importance and spectral energy concentration.

5
Conclusion

This paper presents an efficient inference framework
UniRank
for LLM via low-rank decomposition. We propose
Sorting-and-Truncation
, which adaptively assigns ranks using a aggregated metric combining global functional importance and local singular energy, eliminating manual limitations and reducing computational overhead. Additionally, we introduce
Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning
to enable parameter-efficient tuning without rank inflation or re-decomposition. UniRank demonstrates strong versatility by working seamlessly with various existing decomposition methods, whether applied to parameters or activations. Visualization reveals that the proposed method yields more aggressive rank distributions, validating its optimization efficacy. To sum up, UniRank offers a practical and generalizable solution for inference. Future work will extend its principles to even finer-grained compression (e.g. per-parameter importance evaluation) and broader architectural families.

Limitations

Despite promising practical performance, the proposed method is derived from geometric intuition rather than rigorous theoretical derivation such as error analysis. Moreover, the global functional importance is evaluated based on equivalent transformation of large modules instead of individual parameters. Such asymmetry constrains the performance ceiling. Specifically, trivial parameters may still exist inside critical modules, leaving further pruning potential untapped. We will explore this issue in future work and wish to provide insights for relevant research communities.
