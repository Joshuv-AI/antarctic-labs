Poster 63. Mid- to Long-Term Outcomes of Cartilage Restoration of the Knee with Matrix-induced Autologous Chondrocyte Implantation (MACI): A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: Trevor A. Poulson, Teron Nezwek, Andrew S. Bi, Peace Odiase, Jose Garcia, Adam B. Yanke, Brian J. Cole, Tom Minas, Jorge Chahla
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:This study evaluates the mid- to long-term outcomes of the FDA-approved MACI implant for treating large, symptomatic cartilage lesions of the knee. While third-generation MACI has been widely studied, most long-term data involve non-FDA-approved scaffolds, limiting the limiting the relevance of existing findings. This review focuses on the outcomes of the FDA-approved scaffold, assessing its durability in patient-reported outcomes, graft survivorship, and conversion rates to total knee arthroplasty.Methods:A systematic review was conducted per PRISMA guidelines using Embase, Scopus, PubMed, and CENTRAL to identify relevant studies published from January 1, 2008, to September 12, 2024. Included studies reported clinical outcomes following FDA-approved third-generation MACI performed in the knee with a minimum follow-up of 60 months, were published in English, and had a level of evidence I-IV. Exclusion criteria included non-knee procedures, non-FDA-approved scaffolds, first or second generation periosteal patches, augmentation with orthobiologics, case reports, reviews, and duplicate cohorts.Results:Nine studies compromising 500 patients with 504 cartilage defects in the knee were included, with a mean follow-up of >=60 months. The weighted mean age and BMI were 38.6 years and 26.4 kg/m², respectively. Defects were most located on the medial (65.9%) and lateral femoral condyles (20.4%), followed by the patella (5.4%) with a mean defect size of 3.5 cm². All studies utilized a standard two-stage third-generation MACI technique with porcine collagen membranes. Significant improvements were observed in all KOOS subscales, Lysholm, and IKDC scores at 5-year minimum follow-up. MOCART scores at mid-term showed a mean of 2.93 (95% CI: 2.52-3.34). The pooled mid-term failure rate was 9.0% (95% CI: 2.0-17.0%), reoperation rate was 14.0% (95% CI: 0.0-29.0%), and conversion to TKA was 1.0% (95% CI: 0.0-4.0%). One study reported sustained functional improvement at long-term follow-up.Conclusions:This review supports FDA-approved third generation MACI as an effective treatment for large, full-thickness knee cartilage defects, demonstrating significant, durable improvements in PROMs and functional recovery at mid- and long-term follow-up, making it a valuable joint-sparing technique.
