Non-classical Topological Evidence Logic
Igor Sedlár

Abstract
Topological Evidence Logic (TEL) is a recent approach to epistemic logic that uses topological tools to model coherent epistemic justification. Specifically, a hypothesis is coherently justified if and only if it is entailed by a dense open set. In its simplest form, TEL can be formulated as an extension of 𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{S4} with a global modality. All currently studied forms of TEL are based on classical propositional logic, which has been heavily criticised for misrepresenting the way in which ordinary agents reason. In this article, we show that the TEL approach is robust under modifications to the propositional base. We show that an extension of the intuitionistic modal framework recently introduced by de Groot and Shillito, incorporating a global modality, enables coherent justification to be expressed in an intuitionistic setting. Furthermore, we adapt the recent work of Standefer et al., which extends relevant logic with a global modality, to show that coherent justification can be expressed in a relevant setting if an interior-of-complement operator is added to the language. Our main technical result is a soundness and completeness theorem for relevant TEL based on the weak relevant modal logic 𝖡𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{BS4} .

Introduction

Topology has proven to be a useful tool in logic and computer science. McKinsey and Tarski’s famous results
[
20
,
21
,
39
]
establish a
topological semantics
for modal logic. Smyth’s topological perspective on the basic concepts of programming language semantics
[
32
]
led to the modelling of
observable properties
of computational systems by open sets
[
2
,
16
,
33
,
40
]
. This angle was further elaborated by Kelly
[
19
]
who provided a topological foundation of formal learning theory.

In topological semantics of the modal logic
\mathsf{S4}
, the set of possible worlds
X
is seen as a topological space and the proposition
\Box P
, for arbitrary
P\subseteq X
, is the
interior
of
P
. Hence,
x\in\Box P
iff there is an open set
U
such that
x\in U
and
U\subseteq P
. Assuming that open sets are interpreted as observable properties or
evidence
, then
\Box P
means that there is truthful evidence that supports
P
.

Topological Evidence Logic (TEL) is a recent extension of the topological approach to modal logic that explicitly uses topology to model epistemic notions; see
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
17
,
24
,
41
]
for example. The key concept in TEL is the topological representation of
coherent justification
. Given a space
X
, a hypothesis
P\subseteq X
is coherently justified if
U\subseteq P
for a dense open set
U
. Intuitively, this means that
P
is supported by evidence that is
consistent with all (consistent) evidence
. Accordingly,
P
is
known
in a state
x\in X
if it is coherently justified by an open set
U
such that
x\in U
.

A simple form of TEL is an extension of
\mathsf{S4}
with the global modality
\mathrm{A}
, representing truth in all states. Since
\Box P
is the interior of
P\subseteq X
, the proposition
\Diamond P
, defined as
(\Box(P^{c}))^{c}
, is the
closure
of
P
. It follows that
\mathrm{A}\Diamond P=X
iff
P
is dense. Consequently,
\mathrm{A}\Diamond\Box P=X
iff
P\subseteq U
for some dense open set
U
. In other words,
\mathrm{A}\Diamond\Box P
says that
P
is supported by some dense open set, i.e. that
P
is coherently justified.

All currently studied forms of TEL are based on classical propositional logic and the use of Boolean negation appears to be essential for TEL’s ability to express coherent justification. However, classical propositional logic has been heavily criticised for misrepresenting how agents reason. This has been a major theme in 20th century logic, giving rise to a plethora of non-classical logics such as intuitionistic, many-valued and relevant logics.
In recent years, many non-classical epistemic logics have emerged; see
[
4
,
11
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
37
,
38
]
. By using a non-classical propositional base, these epistemic logics avoid certain problematic closure principles such as ‘if an agent believes the consequent of an implication, then they believe the implication’ or ‘if an agent has inconsistent beliefs, then they believe everything’.
However, these logics lack the attractive evidence-based approach of TEL and are not able to represent coherent justification in the way TEL does.

In this article, we bridge this gap by
studying intuitionistic and relevant versions of TEL.

Recent work by de Groot and Shillito
[
14
]
marks the first step towards a non-classical version of TEL. They extend the Kripke-style relational semantics for propositional intuitionistic logic with the interior semantics for the modal operator
\Box
.
However, their logic
\mathsf{iS4}
lacks the resources to express the density-based notion of coherent justification that is central to TEL.
Our first contribution is to show that coherent justification can be expressed in an extension of
\mathsf{iS4}
with
\mathrm{A}
.

In the context of relevant logic, systems with
\mathrm{A}
have recently been considered by Standefer and co-authors
[
34
,
36
,
37
]
. However, completeness results for these logics have remained elusive.
Our second contribution is to show that density-based justification can be expressed within an extension of the a semantic framework for the weak relevant modal logic
\mathsf{BS4}
, in which the
\Box
operator is interpreted as interior and in which an interior-of-complement operator
\Box^{c}
and the global modality
\mathrm{A}
are introduced.
We also prove soundness and completeness of the resulting logic. Due to the modular nature of our axiomatisation, our result solves the problem of axiomatising relevant logic with
\mathrm{A}
, left open in
[
36
]
.

The article is structured as follows. Section
2
discusses intuitionistic TEL and shows that coherent justification can be expressed in an extension of de Groot and Shillito’s
\mathsf{iS4}
with a global modality. in Section
3
, we provide a sound and complete axiomatisation for intuitionistic TEL. Section
4
formulates a first naive attempt at relevant TEL and shows that it fails. Relevant TEL is introduced in Section
5
as an extension of the relevant modal logic
\mathsf{BS4}
with the global modality
\mathrm{A}
and the interior-of-complement operator
\Box^{c}
. A soundness and completeness result is established in Section
6
. Section
7
offers a summary of the paper and an outline of future work.
To ensure the paper remains self-contained for the non-expert reader, full proofs of two standard results in relevant logic are provided in the appendix.

2
Intuitionistic TEL

In this section, we present an intuitionistic version of TEL, extending the work of de Groot and Shillito
[
14
]
. We show that incorporating the global modality
\mathrm{A}
into their logic
\mathsf{iS4}
enables coherent justification to be expressed in a manner analogous to that in classical TEL. We assume that the reader is familiar with the basics of modal logic
[
13
]
and general topology
[
22
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathit{Prop}
be a countably infinite set of propositional variables. The
language
\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
is defined using the following grammar:

\varphi\coloneq p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\varphi\lor\varphi\mid\varphi\to\varphi\mid\Box\varphi\mid\mathrm{A}\varphi

where
p\in\mathit{Prop}
.
We define
\Diamond\varphi\coloneq\neg\Box\neg\varphi
and
\mathrm{E}\varphi\coloneq\neg\mathrm{A}\neg\varphi
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\langle X,\leq\rangle
be a pre-ordered set. An
up-set topology
on
\langle X,\leq\rangle
is a topology
\mathcal{T}
on
X
such that all
U\in\mathcal{T}
are closed upwards under
\leq
(up-sets). An
up-space
is
\langle X,\leq,\mathcal{T}\rangle
such that
\mathcal{T}
is an up-set topology on
\langle X,\leq\rangle
.
Given an up-space
\bm{S}=\langle X,\leq,\mathcal{T}\rangle
, the
\bm{S}
-interior
of
P\subseteq X
is
\mathit{Int}_{\bm{S}}(P)\coloneq\bigcup\{U\mid U\in\mathcal{T}\And U\subseteq P\}
and the
\bm{S}
-closure
of
P
is
\mathit{Cl}_{\bm{S}}(P)\coloneq\bigcap\{K\mid K^{c}\in\mathcal{T}\And P\subseteq K\}
, where
K^{c}
is the complement of
K
with respect to
X
.

We will write
\mathit{Int}
and
\mathit{Cl}
if
\bm{S}
is clear from the context or immaterial.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\langle X,\leq\rangle
be a pre-ordered set. The
Alexandroff topology
on
\langle X,\leq\rangle
is
\mathcal{T}_{\leq}
comprising all up-sets on
\langle X,\leq\rangle
. Then
\langle X,\leq,\mathcal{T}_{\leq}\rangle
is an up-space. In fact,
\mathcal{T}_{\leq}
is the
finest
up-set topology on
\langle X,\leq\rangle
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\langle\mathbb{R},\leq\rangle
be the set of real numbers with their standard ordering and choose a set
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}
that intuitively represents the ‘observable’ real numbers. We require that for each
a\in\mathbb{R}
there is
b\in O
such that
b\leq a
. Take
\mathcal{T}_{O}
to be the right-order topology determined by the basis
\mathcal{B}_{O}=\{[x,\infty)\mid x\in O\}
. The structure
\langle\mathbb{R},\leq,\mathcal{T}_{O}\rangle
is an up-space.
One example of this construction is
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, representing possible measurements of a real quantity using a device with integer outputs.

Example 2.5

(
[
33
,
40
]
)
.

Let
2^{\infty}
be the set of all finite and countably infinite binary words with the prefix ordering
\sqsubseteq
, where
w\sqsubseteq u
if
w
is a prefix of
u
.
Let
W
be a set of
finite
binary words containing the empty word, representing possible observations. This set may contain all finite binary words, but this is not a requirement. We assume that the observer can observe some finite words but not infinite ones.
Let
{\uparrow}w=\{u\in 2^{\infty}\mid w\sqsubseteq u\}
, comprising words that are ‘consistent’ with prefix
w
. Take the topology
\mathcal{T}_{W}
generated by the basis
\mathcal{B}_{W}=\{{\uparrow}w\mid w\text{ finite}\And w\in W\}
.
Then
\langle 2^{\infty},\sqsubseteq,\mathcal{T}_{W}\rangle
is an up-space. Open sets in this topology are the observable (or
semidecidable
) properties of binary words, i.e. properties that can be confirmed by observing a finite prefix.

Example 2.6

(
[
14
,
23
]
)
.

Let
\langle X,\leq,R\rangle
be a bi-relational Kripke model for intuitionistic modal logic
[
23
]
, where
R
is a preorder on
X
such that
x\leq y
implies
Rxy
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{R}
be the topology comprising all subsets of
X
that are upwards closed under the preorder
R
(the Alexandroff topology determined by
R
). Then
\langle X,\leq,\mathcal{T}_{R}\rangle
is an up-space. In fact, all up-spaces
\langle X,\leq,\mathcal{T}\rangle
give rise to a bi-relational Kripke model
\langle X,\leq,\leq_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle
where
\leq_{\mathcal{T}}
is the
specialisation order
of
\mathcal{T}
, in which
x\leq_{\mathcal{T}}y
iff
\forall U\in\mathcal{T}\colon
if
x\in U
, then
y\in U
. As discussed below, however, not all
\mathcal{T}
are identical to
\mathcal{T}_{\leq_{\mathcal{T}}}
.

Relational structures based on pre-ordered sets
\langle X,\leq\rangle
are often used in the semantics of non-classical logics. The usual informal interpretation of these structures is in terms of information support by partial situations:
x\leq y
iff all information supported by situation
x
is supported by situation
y
as well
[
15
,
18
,
26
]
. The Alexandroff topology
\mathcal{T}_{\leq}
makes this informal interpretation explicit: its open sets are all the pieces of information that
\leq
cares about since
x\leq y
iff
\forall U\in\mathcal{T}_{\leq}\colon
if
x\in U
, then
y\in U
.
Conversely, any topological space
\langle X,\mathcal{T}\rangle
can be transformed into a pre-ordered set
\langle X,\leq_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle
where
\leq_{\mathcal{T}}
is the specialisation order of
\mathcal{T}
(see Example
2.6
). However, a perfect match between the pre-order (implicit) and topological (explicit) model of available information cannot be expected in general.
The Alexandroff topology determined by the specialisation order
\leq_{\mathcal{T}}
of an arbitrary topology
\mathcal{T}
is the closure of
\mathcal{T}
under arbitrary intersections, not necessarily
\mathcal{T}
itself. However, if
\mathcal{T}
is already closed under arbitrary intersections (
\langle X,\mathcal{T}\rangle
is an Alexandroff space), then
\mathcal{T}
is the Alexandroff topology determined by
\leq_{\mathcal{T}}
.

In a general up-space
\langle X,\leq,\mathcal{T}\rangle
where
\mathcal{T}
is typically coarser than
\mathcal{T}_{\leq}
, the pre-order
\leq
is relative to
all information
while
\mathcal{T}
comprises the
observable information
. The latter is a subset of the former and so open sets of
\mathcal{T}
are up-sets with respect to
\leq
.
In general, not all information is observable. For example, while
\mathcal{T}_{\leq}
is closed under arbitrary intersections,
\mathcal{T}
does not have to be – an observer cannot usually make an infinite number of observations and combine them.
However,
\mathcal{T}
may differ from
\mathcal{T}_{\leq}
even if it is closed under arbitrary intersections. In Example
2.4
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
represent possible values of some quantity (e.g. temperature) and intervals
[x,\infty)
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}
represent temperature thresholds verifiable by a sensor with integer outputs. Collections of such intervals are closed under arbitrary intersections.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{U}(X,\leq)
be the collection of up-sets on
\langle X,\leq\rangle
. An
up-model
is
\langle X,\leq,\mathcal{T},\mathcal{V}\rangle
where
\mathcal{V}:\mathit{Prop}\to\mathcal{U}(X,\leq)
.
A
pointed up-model
is a pair
\langle\bm{M},x\rangle
where
\bm{M}
is an up-model on
X
and
x\in X
. The
satisfaction relation

\models
between pointed up-models and formulas
\chi\in\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
is defined by structural induction on
\chi
as follows (where
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}\coloneq\{x\in X\mid\bm{M},x\models\varphi\}
):

•

\bm{M},x\models p
iff
x\in\mathcal{V}(p)
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\neg\varphi
iff
y\notin\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
for all
y\geq x
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\varphi\land\psi
iff
\bm{M},x\models\varphi
and
\bm{M},x\models\psi
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\varphi\lor\psi
iff
\bm{M},x\models\varphi
or
\bm{M},x\models\psi
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\varphi\to\psi
iff, for all
y\geq x
, if
\bm{M},y\models\varphi
, then
\bm{M},y\models\psi
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\Box\varphi
iff
x\in\mathit{Int}_{\bm{M}}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}})
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\mathrm{A}\varphi
iff
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}=X
.

A formula
\varphi
is valid in an up-model
\bm{M}=\langle X,\leq,\mathcal{T},\mathcal{V}\rangle
iff
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}=X
. Validity in up-spaces and classes thereof is defined as usual.

We will often write
x\models\varphi
and
x\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
if
\bm{M}
is clear from the context or immaterial.
Recall that a set
P\subseteq X
is
dense
in
\langle X,\mathcal{T}\rangle
iff
\mathit{Cl}(P)=X
. Equivalently,
P
is dense iff
P\cap U\neq\emptyset
for all
U\in\mathcal{T}
such that
U\neq\emptyset
. Note that if
P
is dense and
P\subseteq Q
, then
Q
is dense.

Thanks to the fact that the
interior of complement
can be expressed using the interior operator and intuitionistic negation, support by a dense open set can be expressed in the same way as in classical TEL.

Lemma 2.8
.

\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket)=\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{c})
.

Proof.

We have
\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket)\subseteq\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{c})
since
\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{c}
. Conversely,
\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{c})\subseteq\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket
since all open sets are upwards closed under
\leq
. Hence,
\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{c})\subseteq\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket)
.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

The following hold for all pointed up-models
\bm{M},x
and all formulas
\varphi
:

1.

\bm{M},x\models\mathrm{A}\Diamond\varphi
iff
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
is a dense set;

2.

\bm{M},x\models\mathrm{A}\Diamond\Box\varphi
iff
U\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
for a dense open set
U
;

3.

\bm{M},x\models\Box\varphi\land\mathrm{A}\Diamond\Box\varphi
iff
U\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
for a dense open set
U
such that
x\in U
.

Proof.

1.
\llbracket\Diamond\varphi\rrbracket=X
iff
\forall x\colon x\notin\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket)
iff
\forall x\colon x\notin\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{c})
(Lemma
2.8
) iff
\mathit{Cl}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket)=X
iff
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
is dense.

2. By the first claim,
\llbracket\Diamond\Box\varphi\rrbracket=X
iff
\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket)
is dense. But clearly
\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket)
is dense iff there is a dense
U\in\mathcal{T}
such that
U\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
(since the collection of dense sets is closed under supersets).

3. By the second claim, we have
x\models\Box\varphi\land\mathrm{A}\Diamond\Box\varphi
iff
x\in U
for some open
U\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and there is a dense open
V
such that
V\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
. It follows that
U\cup V
is a dense open such that
x\in U\cup V\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
. Conversely, if
x\in U
for some dense open
U\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
, then obviously
x\models\Box\varphi
and
\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket)
is non-empty and dense, whence
x\models\mathrm{A}\Diamond\Box\varphi
.
∎

The upshot of this section is that TEL is easily extended to the intuitionistic setting. Take the usual Kripke-style semantics for intuitionistic propositional logic, add a topological interpretation of
\Box
as interior and extend the framework with the global modality
\mathrm{A}
. Even though negation is non-classical, we can still express interior-of-complement and therefore density.
In Section
4
, we consider TEL based on relevant logic. We will see that the situation is more interesting there.
However, before proceeding to relevant TEL, we will provide an axiomatisation of intuitionistic TEL.

3
Axiomatisation of Intuitionistic TEL

To the best of our knowledge, there is no explicit axiomatisation of intuitionistic modal logic with
\mathrm{A}
in the literature. As discussed in
[
36
, p. 1304]
, for example, intuitionistic propositional logic with
\mathrm{A}
can be axiomatised using Ono’s system
\mathit{L_{4}}
from
[
23
]
. Here, we extend this axiomatisation to fit the language with both
\mathrm{A}
and
\Box
.
While the completeness proof may be relatively unexciting for an expert reader, we include it for the sake of exposition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
be the Hilbert-style system that extends a fixed Hilbert-style system for intuitionistic propositional logic with the axioms and inference rules shows in Figure
1
. We write
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\varphi
to express that
\varphi
is a theorem of
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
.

\heartsuit(\varphi\to\psi)\to(\heartsuit\varphi\to\heartsuit\psi)

\heartsuit\varphi\to\varphi

\heartsuit\varphi\to\heartsuit\heartsuit\varphi

\dfrac{\varphi}{\heartsuit\varphi}

\mathrm{A}\varphi\lor\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi

\mathrm{A}\gamma\to\Box\mathrm{A}\gamma

\neg\mathrm{A}\gamma\to\Box\neg\mathrm{A}\gamma

Figure 1:
Modal axioms and rules of the system
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
, where
\heartsuit\in\{\Box,\mathrm{A}\}
.

The proof of the following lemma is straightforward, so we will omit it.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
\varphi\in\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
, if
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\varphi
, then
\varphi
is valid in all up-spaces.

Both modal operators clearly distribute over conjunctions,
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\heartsuit(\varphi\land\psi)\leftrightarrow(\heartsuit\varphi\land\heartsuit\psi)
, and so they are monotonic: if
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\varphi\to\psi
, then
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\heartsuit\varphi\to\heartsuit\psi
. We note that
\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi\to\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi
is provable using the axiom
\mathrm{A}\varphi\lor\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi
, distributivity of
\land
over
\lor
and the fact that
\bot\lor\psi\to\psi
is provable for each contradiction
\bot
.

Definition 3.3
.

An
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theory
is
\Gamma\subseteq\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
such that (a) if
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\varphi\to\psi
and
\varphi\in\Gamma
, then
\psi\in\Gamma
and (b) if
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\varphi
, then
\varphi\in\Gamma
. An
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theory
\Gamma
is
prime
if
\varphi\lor\psi\in\Gamma
only if
\varphi\in\Gamma
or
\psi\in\Gamma
. An
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theory
\Gamma
is
non-trivial
iff
\Gamma\neq\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
.

We denote as
|\varphi|
the set of non-trivial prime
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theories
\Gamma
such that
\varphi\in\Gamma
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The collection of sets of the form
|\Box\varphi|
for
\varphi\in\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
is a basis for an up-set topology on the set of all non-trivial prime
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theories ordered by set inclusion.

Proof.

A modification of the standard argument
[
3
]
for classical
\mathit{S4}
. Let
\Gamma
be any non-trivial prime
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theory. Firstly, by Necessitation,
\Box(p\to p)\in\Gamma
. Hence, every
\Gamma
belongs to a basic set.
Secondly, if
\Gamma
belongs to an intersection of two basic sets, i.e.
\Gamma\in|\Box\varphi|\cap|\Box\psi|
for some
\varphi,\psi
, then
\Gamma\in|\Box(\varphi\land\psi)|
since
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\Box\varphi\land\Box\psi\to\Box(\varphi\land\psi)
. Hence, it follows that
\Gamma
belongs to a basic subset of the intersection the two basic sets. Each basic set
|\Box\varphi|
is clearly closed under
\subseteq
.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

The
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-space
is
\langle X,\subseteq,\mathcal{T}\rangle
, where
X
is the set of all non-empty non-trivial prime
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theories and
\mathcal{T}
is the topology generated by the basis comprising sets
|\Box\varphi|=\{x\in X\mid\Box\varphi\in\Gamma\}
.

It follows from Lemma
3.4
that the
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-space is a well-defined up-space. It can be extended to an up-model
\bm{C}
by defining
\mathcal{V}
as usual, namely,
\mathcal{V}(p)\coloneq|p|
. We can prove the Truth Lemma, stating that
\bm{C},x\models\varphi
iff
\varphi\in x
, for all formulas without
\mathrm{A}
. However, as usual in modal logic, it is not the case that
\mathrm{A}\varphi\in x
for some
x\in X
iff
\varphi\in y
for all
y\in X
.
What we need to do is to
anchor
\bm{C}
in a fixed
w\in X
, that is restrict
\bm{C}
to prime theories that agree with

on all formulas of the form
\mathrm{A}\varphi
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
w
be any non-trivial prime
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theory. The
canonical up-model for
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
anchored in
w
is
\bm{C}^{w}=\langle X^{w},\subseteq^{w},\mathcal{T}^{w},\mathcal{V}^{w}\rangle
where

•

\displaystyle X^{w}\coloneq\bigcap_{\mathrm{A}\varphi\in w}|\mathrm{A}\varphi|\,\cap\,\bigcap_{\mathrm{A}\psi\notin w}|\neg\mathrm{A}\psi|
and
x\subseteq^{w}y
iff

•

\mathcal{T}^{w}
is the subspace topology on

(i.e.

•

.

Lemma 3.7
.

x\in X^{w}
iff
\mathrm{A}\varphi\in x\iff\mathrm{A}\varphi\in w
for all
\varphi
.

Proof.

If
x\in X^{w}
and
\mathrm{A}\varphi\in w
, then
\mathrm{A}\varphi\in x
by definition. If
\mathrm{A}\varphi\notin w
, then
\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi\in w
using the axiom
\mathrm{A}\varphi\lor\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi
of
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
. Then
\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi\in x
by definition, whence
\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi\in x
using the axiom
\mathrm{A}\psi\to\psi
. Since
x
is non-trivial and
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\psi\land\neg\psi\to\chi
, we have
\mathrm{A}\varphi\notin x
.

Conversely, assume that
x\notin X^{w}
. Then either there is
\mathrm{A}\varphi\in w
such that
\mathrm{A}\varphi\notin x
and we are done, or there is
\mathrm{A}\varphi\notin w
such that
\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi\notin x
. In the latter case, axiom
\mathrm{A}\varphi\lor\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi
makes sure that
\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi\in w
. However,
\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi\notin x
since that would entail
\neg\mathrm{A}\varphi\in x
using axiom
\mathrm{A}\psi\to\psi
.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

A pair
\langle\Delta,\nabla\rangle
of subsets of
\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
is
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-independent
if there are no
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}\in\Delta
and
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{m}\in\nabla
such that
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}\vdash\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{i}\,\to\,\bigvee_{j=1}^{m}\psi_{j}
.

Lemma 3.9

(Pair extension for
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
)
.

If
\langle\Delta,\nabla\rangle
is an
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-independent pair, then there is a prime
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theory
\Delta^{\prime}
such that
\Delta\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}
and
\nabla\cap\Delta^{\prime}=\emptyset
. If both
\Delta
and
\nabla
are non-empty, then
\Delta^{\prime}
is non-empty and non-trivial.

Proof.

See Appendix
A.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-empty set of formulas and
\psi
an arbitrary formula. If
\left(\bigcap_{\varphi\in\Gamma}|\varphi|\right)\subseteq|\psi|
, then there is a finite
\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
such that
\vdash\left(\bigwedge_{\delta\in\Delta}\delta\right)\to\psi
.

Proof.

Standard argument using Zorn’s lemma, the fact that
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
is finitary and Lemma
3.9
.
∎

We define
|\chi|^{w}\coloneq|\chi|\cap X^{w}
.

Lemma 3.11

(Truth Lemma)
.

For all
\chi\in\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
,
|\chi|^{w}=\llbracket\chi\rrbracket_{\bm{C}^{w}}
.

Proof.

Structural induction on
\chi
. The interesting cases are (i)
\chi=\varphi\to\psi
, (ii)
\chi=\Box\varphi
and (iii)
\chi=\mathrm{A}\varphi
.

(i)
\chi=\varphi\to\psi
. Assume that
\varphi\to\psi\in x
,
x\subseteq^{w}y
and, using the induction hypothesis,
\varphi\in y
. Then
\varphi\to\psi\in y
and
\psi\in y
since
y
is closed under modus ponens.
Conversely, if
\varphi\to\psi\notin x
, then there is
y
such that
x\subseteq y
,
\varphi\in y
and
\psi\notin y
by Lemma
3.9
since the pair
\langle x\cup\{\varphi\},\{\psi\}\rangle
is independent. We prove that
y\in X^{w}
as follows. If
\mathrm{A}\gamma\in w
, then
\mathrm{A}\gamma\in x
. Since
\mathrm{A}\gamma\to(\varphi\to\mathrm{A}\gamma)
is a theorem of
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
, we obtain
\varphi\to\mathrm{A}\gamma\in y
. Hence,
\mathrm{A}\gamma\in y
.
If
\mathrm{A}\gamma\notin w
, then
\neg\mathrm{A}\gamma\in x
. Since
\neg\mathrm{A}\gamma\to(\varphi\to\neg\mathrm{A}\gamma)
is a theorem of
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
, we obtain
\varphi\to\neg\mathrm{A}\gamma\in y
. Hence,
\neg\mathrm{A}\gamma\in y
.

(ii)
\chi=\Box\varphi
. If
\Box\varphi\in x
, then
x\in|\Box\varphi|^{w}\in\mathcal{T}^{w}
. Using the axiom
\Box\varphi\to\varphi
, we obtain
|\Box\varphi|\subseteq|\varphi|
and so
|\Box\varphi|^{w}\subseteq|\varphi|^{w}
.
Conversely, assume that there is
U\in\mathcal{T}^{w}
such that
x\in U\subseteq|\varphi|^{w}
. We know that
U=V\cap X^{w}
for some
V\in\mathcal{T}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x\in|\Box\psi|\cap X^{w}\subseteq|\varphi|
for some specific
\psi
.
By Lemma
3.10
, there are
\{\mathrm{A}\chi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq w\cap x
and
\{\mathrm{A}\chi^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subseteq w^{c}
such that

\vdash\Box\psi\,\land\,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{A}\chi_{i}\,\land\,\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\neg\mathrm{A}\chi^{\prime}_{j}\,\to\,\varphi

and
\{\neg\mathrm{A}\chi^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subseteq x
.
We can infer the following using axioms
\mathrm{A}\gamma\to\Box\mathrm{A}\gamma
,
\neg\mathrm{A}\gamma\to\Box\neg\mathrm{A}\gamma
and the fact that
\Box
is monotonic and distributes over conjunctions in
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
:

\vdash\Box\psi\,\land\,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{A}\chi_{i}\,\land\,\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\neg\mathrm{A}\chi^{\prime}_{j}\,\to\,\Box\varphi\,.

It follows that
\Box\varphi\in x
.

(iii)
\chi=\mathrm{A}\varphi
. If
\mathrm{A}\varphi\in x
, then
\mathrm{A}\varphi\in w
by Lemma
3.7
. Hence,
\mathrm{A}\varphi\in y
for all
y\in X^{w}
. Using the axiom
\mathrm{A}\varphi\to\varphi
, we infer that
\varphi\in y
for all
y\in X^{w}
.
Conversely, assume that
\mathrm{A}\varphi\notin x
. We show that the pair
\langle\{\mathrm{A}\psi\mid\mathrm{A}\psi\in w\}\cup\{\neg\mathrm{A}\psi^{\prime}\mid\mathrm{A}\psi^{\prime}\notin w\},\{\varphi\}\rangle
is independent. If not, then we have

\vdash\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{A}\psi_{i}\,\land\,\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\neg\mathrm{A}\psi^{\prime}_{j}\,\to\,\varphi

for some
\{\mathrm{A}\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq w
and
\{\mathrm{A}\psi^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subseteq w^{c}
.
We infer the following using axiom
\mathrm{A}\psi\to\mathrm{A}\mathrm{A}\psi
, the theorem
\neg\mathrm{A}\psi\to\mathrm{A}\neg\mathrm{A}\psi
and the fact that
\mathrm{A}
is monotonic and distributes over conjunctions in
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
:

\vdash\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{A}\psi_{i}\,\land\,\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\neg\mathrm{A}\psi^{\prime}_{j}\,\to\,\mathrm{A}\varphi\,.

Since the antecedent of this implication is in
x
(as
x\in X^{w}
), we conclude that
\mathrm{A}\varphi\in x
, which contradicts our assumption. Hence, the pair is independent and we have
y\in X^{w}
such that
\varphi\notin y
by Lemma
3.9
.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

A formula
\varphi
is valid in all up-spaces iff it is provable in
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
.

Proof.

Soundness is established by Lemma
3.2
. Completeness: if
\not\vdash\varphi
, then
\langle T,\{\varphi\}\rangle
is independent, where
T
is the set of all theorems of
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
. Then there is a non-trivial prime
\mathit{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
-theory
w
such that
\varphi\notin w
. By Lemma
3.11
,
\varphi
is not satisfied in
w
in
\bm{C}^{w}
. Hence,
\varphi
is not valid in all up-spaces.
∎

4
Towards relevant TEL

The results of Section
2
suggest a strategy for obtaining relevant TEL: first, take ordered frames for relevant logics
[
25
,
27
,
28
]
and endow them with an up-set topology; add a modal
\Box
to the language and interpret it as interior; add the global modality
\mathrm{A}
. In effect, this is the framework of
[
36
]
, extended with a topologically interpreted
\Box
.

Firstly, we will set up this framework in more detail. Secondly, we will show that it cannot express interior-of-complement and that it cannot express the fact that a given formula is supported by a dense open set. In the next section, we extend the framework so that these facts become expressible.

Definition 4.1
.

A
Routley–Meyer frame

[
28
,
35
]
is
\bm{F}=\langle X,\leq,N,R,\,^{*}\rangle
where
\langle X,\leq\rangle
is a pre-ordered set,
N\subseteq X
is closed upwards under
\leq
,
R
is a ternary relation on
X
and
\,{}^{*}
is a unary operation on
X
such that

(RM1)

if
x\in N
and
Rxyz
, then
y\leq z
;

(RM2)

for all
y
, there is
x\in N
such that
Rxyy
;

(RM3)

if
Rxyz
and
x^{\prime}\leq x
,
y^{\prime}\leq y
,
z\leq z^{\prime}
, then
Rx^{\prime}y^{\prime}z^{\prime}
;

(RM4)

if
x\leq y
, then
y^{*}\leq x^{*}
;

(RM5)

x=x^{**}
.

A
Routley–Meyer up-space
is a pair
\langle\bm{F},\mathcal{T}\rangle
where
\mathcal{T}
is an up-set topology on
\bm{F}
.

The motivation for adopting the frame conditions (RM1–5) is explained after Definition
4.4
. Before we get to the definition, we give two examples of RM up-models.

Example 4.2
.

Expanding Example
2.4
, take
\bm{F}_{\mathbb{R}}=\langle\mathbb{R},\leq,N,R,-\rangle
where
\leq
is the standard ordering of real numbers,
N=[0,\infty)
, and
Rxyz
iff
x+y\leq z
.

\bm{F}_{\mathbb{R}}
can be endowed by the right order topology
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Z}}=\{\emptyset,\mathbb{R}\}\cup\{[x,\infty)\mid x\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Example 4.3
.

Let
\Sigma^{*}
be the set of all finite words over an alphabet
\Sigma
, including the empty word
\epsilon
. Take
\bm{F}_{\Sigma^{*}}=\langle\mathcal{P}(\Sigma^{*}),\subseteq,N,R,\,^{c}\rangle
, where
A\in N
iff
\epsilon\in A
and
R(A,B,C)
iff
AB=\{wu\mid w\in A\And u\in B\}\subseteq C
.

\bm{F}_{\Sigma^{*}}
can be endowed with the topology of ‘observable properties’, generated by the sub-basis comprising sets
U_{w}=\{A\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\mid w\in A\}
for all
w\in\Sigma^{*}
. Intuitively, the open sets in this topology are properties of languages that can be verified by observing finite words.

Definition 4.4
.

A
RM up-model
is
\bm{M}=\langle\bm{F},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{V}\rangle
where
\mathcal{V}:\mathit{Prop}\to\mathcal{U}(X,\leq)
. Satisfaction is defined as follows (as before,
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}=\{x\mid\bm{M},x\models\varphi\}
):

•

\bm{M},x\models p
iff
x\in\mathcal{V}(p)
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\varphi\land\psi
iff
\bm{M},x\models\varphi
and
\bm{M},x\models\psi
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\varphi\lor\psi
iff
\bm{M},x\models\varphi
or
\bm{M},x\models\psi
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\varphi\to\psi
iff, for all
y,z
, if
Rxyz
and
\bm{M},y\models\varphi
, then
\bm{M},z\models\psi
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\neg\varphi
iff
\bm{M},x^{*}\not\models\varphi
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\Box\varphi
iff
x\in\mathit{Int}_{\bm{M}}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}})
;

•

\bm{M},x\models\mathrm{A}\varphi
iff
\bm{M},y\models\varphi
for all
y\in X
.

A formula
\varphi
is
valid
in an RM up-model
\bm{M}
iff
N\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
. Let
\mathsf{BS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
be the logic of all RM up-spaces, defined as the set of all
\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
-formulas valid in all RM up-models.

The next lemma, useful in proving validity of implicational formulas, provides a motivation for the frame conditions (RM1–4). The frame condition (RM5) is needed for the double negation law to hold.

Lemma 4.5
.

\varphi\to\psi
is valid in
\bm{M}
iff
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
.

Proof.

This holds thanks to (RM1), (RM2) and the fact that
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
us an up-set for all
\varphi
. The latter fact holds for
\varphi=\psi\to\chi
by (RM3) and for
\varphi=\neg\psi
by (RM4).
∎

We illustrate the satisfaction clauses of Definition
4.4
in the spaces discussed in previous examples.

Example
4.2
, continued.

In any model
\bm{M}
on
\bm{F}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
x\models\varphi\to\psi
iff, for all
y,z\in\mathbb{R}
, if
y\models\varphi
, then
x+y\models\psi
(‘adding’
\varphi
gives
\psi
). On the other hand,
x\models\neg\varphi
iff
-x\not\models\varphi
. Not all ‘properties’ are observable directly if our measurement device has only integer outputs. For example, if
\llbracket p\rrbracket=[0.5,\infty)
, then
\llbracket\Box p\rrbracket=[1,\infty)
, meaning that measuring
1
is the closest we can get to verifying

.

Example
4.3
, continued.

Formulas express properties of languages. In particular,
A\models\varphi\to\psi
if
AB\models\psi
for all
B
such that
B\models\varphi
(‘concatenating with a
\varphi
-language gives a
\psi
-language’). Not every property of languages can be verified by observing finite words. E.g. since every non-empty open set contains both finite and infinite languages,
\llbracket\Box p\rrbracket=\emptyset
if
\llbracket p\rrbracket=\{A\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\mid A\text{ is infinite}\}
.

Now we show that, over RM up-spaces,
\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
lacks the expressivity for the salient TEL concepts.

Proposition 4.6
.

For any given

:

1.

there is no
\varphi(p)\in\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
such that, for all
\bm{M}

\bm{M},x\models\varphi(p)
iff

there is no
\psi(p)\in\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}

\bm{M}
we have
\bm{M},x\models\psi(p)
iff there is a dense open
U

Proof.

1. We construct an RM up-model
\bm{M}
such that
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}\neq\mathit{Int}(\llbracket p\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}^{c})
for all

\mathcal{T}=\{\emptyset,\{1,2\},\{3,4\},X\}

and
\mathcal{V}(q)=\emptyset

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{\emptyset,\{1\},\{1,2\},\{1,2,4\},X\}

.
The claim is obvious for elements of
\mathit{Prop}
. The cases of the induction step corresponding to

2. We present a pair of RM up-models

5
Relevant TEL

The previous section shows that
\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A}}
needs to be extended in order to express the salient TEL concepts in the context of relevant logic. In this section, we introduce one such extension. It is perhaps the most direct one – we add a modal operator
\Box^{c}
, interpreted as
interior-of-complement
.

Definition 5.1
.

The
language
\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A},c}
is defined using the following grammar:

\varphi\coloneq p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\varphi\lor\varphi\mid\varphi\to\varphi\mid\Box\varphi\mid\mathrm{A}\varphi\mid\Box^{c}\varphi

where
p\in\mathit{Prop}
.
We define
\Diamond^{c}\varphi\coloneq\neg\Box^{c}\varphi
and
\mathrm{E}\varphi\coloneq\neg\mathrm{A}\neg\varphi
. We define
\bot\coloneq\mathrm{A}p\land\neg\mathrm{A}p
and
\top\coloneq\mathrm{A}p\lor\neg\mathrm{A}p
for some fixed
p\in\mathit{Prop}
.

Definition 5.2
.

In RM up-models, the satisfaction relation
\models
is defined for
\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A},c}
as before, with the following clause added:

•

\bm{M},x\models\Box^{c}\varphi
iff
x\in\mathit{Int}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{c})
.

Let
\mathsf{BS4}_{\mathrm{A},c}
be the logic of all RM up-models, defined as the set of all
\mathfrak{L}^{\Box}_{\mathrm{A},c}
-formulas valid in all RM up-models.

Note that
\bm{M},x\models\Diamond^{c}\varphi
iff
x^{*}\in\mathit{Cl}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}})
.
Moreover,
\bm{M},x\models\mathrm{E}\varphi
iff
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}\neq\emptyset
(for the right-to-left implication, we need to assume that every
y=z^{*}
for some
z
),
\bm{M},x\not\models\bot
and
\bm{M},x\models\top
for all
x\in X
.

With
\Box^{c}
at hand we can express density and coherent justification.

Proposition 5.3
.

The following hold for all pointed up-models
\bm{M},x
:

1.

\bm{M},x\models\mathrm{A}\Diamond^{c}\varphi
iff
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
is a dense set;

2.

\bm{M},x\models\mathrm{A}\Diamond^{c}\Box\varphi
iff
U\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
for a dense open set
U
;

3.

\bm{M},x\models\Box\varphi\land\mathrm{A}\Diamond^{c}\Box\varphi
iff
U\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\bm{M}}
for a dense open set
U
such that
x\in U
.

Proof.

1.
\llbracket\Diamond^{c}\varphi\rrbracket=X

7
Conclusion

This paper made the first steps in studying versions of Topological Evidence Logic (TEL) based on non-classical propositional logics. Specifically, we studied intuitionistic and relevant versions of TEL.
We have shown that an extension of de Groot and Shillito’s
[
14
]
modal logic
\mathsf{iS4}
with a global modality
\mathrm{A}
can express TEL-style coherent justification (existence of a dense open subset). We have argued that a language with
\Box
and
\mathrm{A}
is not sufficient for a relevant version of TEL since coherent justification is not expressible.
Accordingly, for relevant TEL we added an interior-of-complement operator
\Box^{c}
and we showed that coherent justification is expressible in the language even in a weak relevant background.
As our main technical result, we provided a sound and complete axiomatisation of a relevant TEL based on the weak modal relevant logic
\mathsf{BS4}
. We have also provided a sound and complete axiomatisation of intuitionistic TEL based on
\mathsf{iS4}
with
\mathrm{A}
.

The paper barely scratches the surface, leaving many interesting problems to be investigated in the future. Firstly, both
\mathsf{iS4}
and
\mathsf{BS4}
are decidable and we plan to extend the decidability result to
\mathsf{iS4}_{\mathrm{A}}
and
\mathsf{BS4}_{\mathrm{A},c}
. It should not be too difficult to combine proofs of the finite model property with the anchoring technique.
Secondly, it will be interesting to look at relevant TEL based on stronger propositional logics such as
\mathsf{R}
or
\mathsf{E}
. It is not clear how much the extra frame conditions needed for these logics interfere with the anchoring technique.
Thirdly, it is intriguing in the setting of RM frames to replace topological spaces seen as locales of up-sets, where evidence combination is represented by set intersection, by
quantales
of up-sets where evidence combination is represented by the ternary relation
R
, and investigate the corresponding modal logic.
