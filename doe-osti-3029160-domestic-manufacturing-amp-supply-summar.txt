Background and Context

 Background and Context

 Motivation

 Motivation

The US has seen increased electricity load growth, Most transmission and generation planning
and this is expected to further increase1 studies to date assume transmission
 equipment is readily available.

Large-scale transmission expansion can be a low- Our analysis aims to fill this gap by assessing
cost strategy to meet rising electricity demand and the ability of the U.S. supply chains to meet
maintain reliability2 demand of components and input materials
 through 2035

Domestic supply chain constraints for critical grid
components threaten this expansion3

 Circuit Breakers Estimates of Domestic Consumption and Availability
Data on facility output or total domestic manufacturing for high-voltage circuit breakers were not publicly available. Regional consumption and
import data used to estimate domestic total availability and import reliance.

• In 2024, North America deployed over 120,000 high-voltage circuit breakers led by U.S. substation
 upgrades and smart grid projects across California and Texas.43
 - Data on domestic manufacturing output or imports for high-voltage breakers not available from
 public data sources
 - Facility-specific output number to estimate of breaker output based on employee numbers or
 factory size also not available from public data sources
• Assume 25-75% of units consumed each year in the U.S. are imported
 - Import data for lower-voltage circuit breakers over 1000 V but less than 72.5 kV* showed that over
 the 2016 – 2020 period, import shares ranged from 75% to 26% based on number of units44
 - We assume the fraction of imports for high-voltage circuit breakers have a similar range, 75% - 25%

 * Based on HS codes: 853521; 853529                                                                             National Laboratory of the Rockies | 25
