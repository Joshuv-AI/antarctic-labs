Equivariant Quantum Cohomology of Grassmannians via the Clifford algebra
Christian Korff, Mikhail Vasilev

Abstract
. We construct an explicit equivariant quantum Satake map for Grassmannians, which enables us to express their torus-equivariant quantum cohomology in terms of that of projective space. We then consider the exterior algebra of the latter, which admits a canonical identification with a Clifford algebra. We describe the resulting action in several complementary ways: first, from a geometric perspective via push–pull maps, and second, in terms of the shuffle product, which also arises in the simplest cohomological Hall algebra associated with the A 1 A_{1} -quiver. Exploiting the Clifford algebra structure, we derive new recurrence relations among equivariant Gromov–Witten invariants, yielding a new method for their computation in terms of Wick’s Theorem. As an application, we provide combinatorial proofs of Graham positivity for both equivariant quantum Pieri rules, and in one case extend these results to quantum triple Schubert calculus.

1.1.
A geometric construction of the Clifford algebra

Therefore, we shall take a different approach in this article and directly identify the sum
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}qH_{T}^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))

for fixed

\mathcal{C}_{n}=\langle\psi_{i},\psi_{i}^{*}~:~i=1,\ldots,n\rangle/\sim
over

\psi_{i}\psi_{j}+\psi_{j}\psi_{i}=\psi_{i}^{*}\psi_{j}^{*}+\psi_{j}^{*}\psi_{i}^{*}=0,\qquad\psi_{i}\psi^{*}_{j}+\psi_{j}^{*}\psi_{i}=\delta_{ij}\;.

1.3.
New recurrence formulae for Gromov-Witten invariants

The maps (
1.3
) allow for a new combinatorics in quantum Schubert calculus which relates the quantum multiplication in
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
to either
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k-1;n))
or
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k+1;n))
and one arrives at recurrence relations for equivariant Gromov-Witten invariants of Grassmannians of different dimensions.

Previously established recurrence formulae and algorithms such as
[

Applying this recurrence repeatedly one then deduces a novel expression for the equivariant Gromov-Witten invariants purely in terms of the Clifford algebra maps (
1.3
). Before we can state this result we need to briefly recall two basic facts related to the Clifford algebra (further details are given in the main body of the article):

(i)

Given a group morphism
\alpha:G\to\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
there is a natural
G
-action on the Clifford algebra via automorphisms,
g.\psi_{i}=\sum_{j}\psi_{j}(g^{-1})_{ji}
and
g.\psi^{*}_{i}=\sum_{j}g_{ij}\psi^{*}_{j}
. In our case we will exploit this for
G=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes S_{n}
the extended affine Weyl group and the above group morphism
\alpha
is prescribed by a
G
-action on the quantum cohomology of projective space
qH^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
; see Lemma
3.4
.

(ii)

The Clifford algebra comes equipped with a natural functional
\langle\;\bullet\;\rangle:\mathcal{C}_{n}\to R[q^{\pm 1}]
called the
vacuum expectation value
which can be computed via a determinant formula called ‘Wick’s Theorem’,

\langle\psi_{i_{k}}\ldots\psi_{i_{1}}\psi^{*}_{j_{1}}\ldots\psi^{*}_{j_{k}}\rangle=\det(\langle\psi_{i_{a}}\psi^{*}_{j_{b}}\rangle)_{1\leq a,b\leq k}
with
\langle\psi_{i_{a}}\psi^{*}_{j_{b}}\rangle=\delta_{i_{a}j_{b}}
.

Recall that a skew diagram
\rho=\lambda/\mu
of two partitions
\lambda,\mu
is called a
horizontal strip
if the difference of their Young diagram contains at most one box in each column. Moreover, the content
c(\square)
of a box
\square\in\rho
is the difference of its column and row number in the Young diagram of
\lambda
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u=[u_{1}\ldots u_{n}],v=[v_{1}\ldots v_{n}],w=[w_{1}\ldots w_{n}]\in S^{k,n}
be Grassmannian permutations and set
\lambda_{i}=u_{k+1-i}+i-k-1
for
i=1,\ldots,k
. Then the equivariant Gromov-Witten invariants, the structure constants of
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
, are given by

(1.5)

C_{uv}^{w}(y,q)=\sum_{T(\lambda)}\langle\psi_{w_{k}}\cdots\psi_{w_{1}}(t^{\rho_{1}}.\psi^{*}_{v_{1}})\cdots(t^{\rho_{k}}.\psi^{*}_{v_{k}})\rangle

where the sum runs over all semi-standard tableaux
T
of shape
\lambda
, a partitioning of the Young diagram of
\lambda
into horizontal strips
\rho_{i}
, each of which defines a translation in the normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\simeq\langle t_{1},\ldots t_{n}\rangle\leq G
via
t^{\rho_{i}}=\prod_{\square\in\rho_{i}}t_{k+c(\square)}
.

To the best of the authors’ knowledge analogous expressions for the quantum invariants of higher flag varieties or their cotangent bundles are currently not known.

We note that the idea of obtaining such formulae via the Clifford algebra goes back to the work
[
32
, Section 11]
, where recurrence formulae for the non-equivariant cohomology ring
qH^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
were obtained; see also
[
33
]
. The new result here is their geometric description in terms of push-pull maps and the extension to equivariant quantum Schubert calculus which allows one to relate these formulae to the action of the extended affine Weyl group
W=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes S_{n}
on
qH^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
from Lemma
3.4
. Key in making this connection is an explicit quantum version of the equivariant Satake correspondence for Grassmannians.

1.4.
An equivariant quantum Satake map for Grassmannians

The main idea we are going to employ in our derivation of (
1.5
) and the underlying Clifford algebra structure is to describe the ring
qH_{T}^{*}(\operatorname{Gr}(k,n))
in terms of the (much simpler) quantum cohomology ring
qH_{T}^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
of projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}=\operatorname{Gr}(1,n)
; see e.g.
[
24
,
4
,
26
,
19
]
in the non-equivariant setting.

Let us briefly recall the construction. It follows from the geometric Satake construction
[
16
,
45
]
that when identifying the cohomology of
\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n)
as a minuscule Schubert cell in the affine Grassmannian of
\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
the latter corresponds to the
k
-fold exterior power of the cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
under the same identification. In particular, the Satake correspondence identifies
H^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
with the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module
\bigwedge^{k}V
and the multiplication by the first Chern class corresponds to the action by the principal nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. This picture has been extended to quantum cohomology in
[
9
,
19
]
by replacing the principal nilpotent element with the cyclic element in
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, which then describes the quantum Pieri rule in
qH^{*}(\operatorname{Gr}(k,n))
provided one sets
q=1
, i.e. one considers the so-called Verlinde algebra.

The construction of the Satake map extends to equivariant (non-quantum) Schubert calculus by considering the exterior
R
-algebra
\bigwedge V
with
V\simeq H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
as
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)
-modules; see e.g.
[
40
,
36
,
2
,
35
]
as well as references therein. In this article we state a quantum version of the equivariant Satake map for Grassmannians; see also
[
10
,
8
]
for related constructions albeit with a rather different focus on quantum difference equations.

Our construction will use instead ‘quantised Chern roots’
X^{q}_{1},\ldots,X^{q}_{k}\in\operatorname{End}_{R}V^{\otimes k}[q^{\pm 1}]
, a set of mutually commuting
n\times n
matrices with values in
R[q^{\pm 1}]
each satisfying the matrix identity

(1.6)

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X_{i}^{q}-y_{j}\cdot 1)=(-1)^{k-1}q\cdot 1\;,\qquad i=1,\ldots,k\;.

Apart from a sign factor in the deformation parameter
q
, the latter identity is the defining relation of the quantum cohomology of projective space and we refer to the matrices
X^{q}_{i}
as ‘quantised Chern roots’. This terminology is further justified by the following result. If we consider the double Schubert polynomial
\mathfrak{S}_{w}(X_{1}^{q},\ldots,X^{q}_{k}|y)\in\operatorname{End}_{R}V^{\otimes k}[q^{\pm 1}]
with
w\in S^{k,n}
a Grassmannian permutation then the latter commutes with the canonical projection
V^{\otimes k}[q^{\pm 1}]\twoheadrightarrow\bigwedge^{k}V[q^{\pm 1}]
and we prove the following:

Proposition 1.1
.

The
R[q^{\pm 1}]
-module
\bigwedge^{k}V[q^{\pm 1}]
together with the bilinear operation

\ket{w}\star\ket{u}:=\mathfrak{S}_{w}(X_{1}^{q},\ldots,X^{q}_{k}|y)\ket{u},\quad\ket{u}=v_{u_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{u_{k}},

where
u,w\in S^{k,n}
is a pair of Grassmannian permutations and
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\subset V
the standard basis of the free module
V\simeq R^{n}
, is a well-defined commutative associative unital graded ring, which is isomorphic to
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
. Moreover, the isomorphism is canonical, i.e. the basis vectors
\ket{w}=v_{w_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{w_{k}}
of
\bigwedge^{k}V[q^{\pm 1}]
map to Schubert classes.

The key ingredient which we will use in proving the above is Mihalcea’s result that the ring structure is fixed, once we can prove the quantum Chevalley rule holds
[
42
, Cor.7.1]
. As an immediate consequence we obtain an alternative expression for the equivariant Gromov-Witten invariants.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
u,v,w\in S^{k,n}
be Grassmannian permutations then

C^{w}_{uv}(y,q)=\bra{w}\mathfrak{S}_{u}(X_{1}^{q},\ldots,X_{n}^{q}|y)\ket{v}\;,

where the right hand side is computed via a natural pairing
\bigwedge^{k}V^{*}[q^{\pm 1}]\otimes\bigwedge^{k}V[q^{\pm 1}]\to R[q^{\pm 1}]
using Poincaré duality and
\bra{w}=v^{w_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge v^{w_{k}}
corresponds to the dual Schubert basis.

The last identity leads to another combinatorial algorithm involving semi-standard tableaux: recall that for Grassmannian permutations the double Schubert polynomial specialises to a factorial Schur polynomial and the latter have a presentation as a weighted sum over semi-standard tableaux; see Appendix
A
. Each term in this sum acts by simple matrix multiplication on
V^{\otimes k}[q^{\pm 1}]
and the final outcome of the computation can then simply be projected onto
\bigwedge^{k}V[q^{\pm 1}]
. This is a generalisation of the construction from
[
31
, Section 4.2 and 5.7]
for non-equivariant quantum cohomology.

From the realisation of equivariant quantum cohomology via the quantum Satake map one is then naturally led to consider the Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}_{n}(R)
: the latter is simply the exterior algebra
\bigwedge V[q^{\pm 1}]
and the geometric maps defined in (
1.3
) correspond to multiplication and contraction operators. In other words, the quantum Satake map extends to an isomorphism of Clifford algebra modules.

1.5.
Applications: Graham positivity and quantum triple Schubert calculus

As an application of our formalism we give new combinatorial proofs of Graham positivity for the quantum Pieri rules in
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
; see Section
4.3
. There is a known result originally due to Mihalcea
[
42
]
that the structure constants
C^{\nu,d}_{\lambda\mu}(y)
of the equivariant quantum cohomology ring
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
satisfy
Graham positivity
, i.e. the
C^{\nu,d}_{\lambda\mu}(y)
are non-negative integer polynomials in the torus weights
y_{i+1}-y_{i}
. A combinatorial proof of this statement or a manifestly positive combinatorial algorithm to compute the equivariant Gromov-Witten invariants is currently not known. More recently, Bertiger et al. introduced in
[
3
]
a statistics on Postnikov’s
[
48
]
cylindric tableaux which allows for a manifestly positive computation of quantum Pieri rules and we directly compare our formula against theirs in Section
4.4
.

We conclude by extending our construction to triple Schubert calculus and suggest a quantum version of it; see Section
4.5
. Recall that in
[
46
]
Molev and Sagan considered the problem of multiplying two factorial Schur polynomials with different sets of equivariant parameters

(1.7)

s_{\lambda}(x|z)s_{\mu}(x|y)=\sum\limits_{\nu}c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(y,z)s_{\nu}(x|y)

and derived a combinatorial formula for the coefficients
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(y,z)
, which is manifestly positive in the following sense:
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[y_{i}-z_{j}]
. Knutson and Tao then gave a geometric meaning for computing the expansion (
1.7
) in terms of so-called
triple Schubert calculus
; see Section 6 of
[
29
]
for details. In their setting of triple Schubert calculus the parameters
y
and
z
in the product (
1.7
) are the generators of the ring
H^{*}(\,\mathrm{Fl}_{n}\times\,\mathrm{Fl}_{n})\simeq H^{*}(\,\mathrm{Fl}_{n})\otimes H^{*}(\,\mathrm{Fl}_{n})
, namely
y_{i}
is the first Chern class of the
i
-th tautological line bundle over the first copy of
\,\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
and
z_{j}
is the first Chern class of the
j
-th tautological line bundle over the second copy of
\,\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
. Recently, the positivity of triple Schubert calculus for arbitrary partial flags was proved by geometric methods in
[
14
]
.

Prompted by these results in the literature, we consider an extension of our quantum Satake map from Proposition
1.1
to the base ring
\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
and define a triple quantum multiplication by replacing the
x
-variables in (
1.7
) with our quantised Chern roots satisfying (
1.6
). We conjecture that the resulting ring has structure constants that also satisfy a refined version of Graham positivity and prove a manifestly positive quantum Pieri rule in support of our conjecture.

1.6.
The connection with lattice models and Yang-Baxter algebras

For completeness, let us briefly outline the connection between our construction and the previous work on equivariant quantum cohomology of Grassmannians using Yang-Baxter algebras in
[
22
]
and
[
21
]
; a detailed discussion can be found in Appendix
C
.

Yang-Baxter algebras are unital associative bi-algebras whose defining relations are encoded in a matrix equation known as the
RTT
-equation,

R_{12}(x_{1}-x_{2})T_{1}(x_{1})T_{2}(x_{2})=T_{2}(x_{2})T_{1}(x_{1})R_{12}(x_{1}-x_{2})\;.

Historically, this is how quantum groups, such as Yangians, were first introduced by the Faddeev School and this method is now known as the Faddeev-Reshetikhin-Takhtajan (FRT)-construction of quantum groups
[
12
,
11
]
. Here
R
denotes a given solution of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation (the data defining the algebra) and
T
is called the
monodromy matrix
whose elements give the generators of the algebra.

In the case of the quantum cohomology of Grassmannians (as opposed to that of their cotangent bundles) the Yang-Baxter algebra
\operatorname{YB}
is not the Yangian
Y_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
, but instead the corresponding
R
-matrix describes a so-called exactly solvable five-vertex model in statistical mechanics
[
34
,
22
]
, it has been geometrically constructed in
[
21
]
including the monodromy matrix

(1.8)

T(x)=\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}(x)&T_{12}(x)\\
T_{21}(x)&T_{22}(x)\end{pmatrix}\;,

where the entries are currents whose coefficients are convolution operators: namely, the ‘quantum trace’ of the monodromy matrix,
t(x)=T_{11}(x)+qT_{22}(x)
, where
q
is the deformation parameter of the quantum cohomology ring, acts on
\mathcal{F}\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}qH_{T}^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
by quantum multiplication with the Chern polynomial of the tautological bundle on each individual summand, while the action of the off-diagonal elements
T_{12}(x),T_{21}(x)
involves push and pull maps of the type (
1.2
) and, thus, the latter map between different quantum cohomology rings.

The relation between the Yang-Baxter algebra construction from
[
21
]
and our construction using the quantum Satake map from Prop
1.1
is best summarised in terms of the following quantum version of a Cauchy-type identity for factorial Schur functions, which is a new result; compare with their non-equivariant quantum versions in
[
31
, Lemma 5.1 and Cor.5.40]
:

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
t(x)\in\operatorname{End}_{R}(qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
be the quantum multiplication by the Chern polynomial of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{S}_{k}
. Then

t(x_{1})\cdots t(x_{n-k})=\sum_{w\in S^{k,n}}\mathfrak{S}_{w}(X_{1}^{q},\ldots,X^{q}_{k}|y)\mathfrak{S}_{w^{*}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-k}|-y),

where
w^{*}=w\cdot(12\ldots n)^{k}\in S^{n-k,n}
is a Grassmannian permutation and the
X^{q}_{i}
are mutually commuting matrices satisfying (
1.6
).

Besides the above relation we give explicit formulae for all the Yang-Baxter algebra generators
T_{ij}(x)
in terms of the maps (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) in Appendix
C
of this work. In particular, we show that the image
\mathcal{Y}
of
\operatorname{YB}
in
\operatorname{End}\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}qH_{T}^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
, as well as its ‘dual’
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
under the ring isomorphism
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n)\simeq qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(n-k;n)
from Theorem
4.1
are both generated by the Clifford algebra and coincide; see Theorem
C.2
.

This shows that the Clifford algebra is a more fundamental algebraic structure underpinning the equivariant quantum cohomology of Grassmannians albeit the formulation in terms of Yang-Baxter algebras leads to a nice graphical calculus of lattice models which greatly simplifies some combinatorial descriptions.

Finally, we point out that the matrix identity (
1.6
) when rewritten in terms of eigenvalues of the matrices
X_{i}^{q}
are nothing else but the Bethe ansatz equations of the underlying lattice model. The latter are needed in the Bethe ansatz or quantum inverse scattering method construction of the idempotents for each of the quantum cohomology rings; see
[
34
,
22
,
21
]
for details.

Preliminaries

Most of the results in this section can be found in the literature but we collect them here to introduce our notation and keep this article self-contained. A new result is presented in Section

2.1.
Equivariant quantum cohomology of Grassmannians

Denote by
\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n)
the Grassmannian of
k
dimensional hyperplanes in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. There is a natural left
\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
action on
\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n)
. Fix a maximal torus
T\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
in
\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, the subset of
n\times n
invertible diagonal matrices with entries
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
which are called the
equivariant parameters
. We denote by

R=\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]\cong H^{*}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})

the equivariant cohomology of a point. Rather than recalling the geometric definition of the equivariant quantum cohomology ring
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
we shall work instead with its presentation as quotient ring using factorial Schur polynomials; see Appendix
A
. However, we give a dictionary between the algebraic and geometric definitions.

Consider the following generating function for factorial versions
e_{r}(x|y)
of the elementary symmetric polynomials,

\prod_{i=1}^{k}(z-x_{i})=\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}e_{r}(x|y)\;(z|y)^{k-r},\qquad(z|y)^{r}:=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(z-y_{i}),

where
(z|y)^{r}
are called the
falling factorial powers
. Geometrically, the above generating function corresponds to the Chern polynomial
z^{k}c_{-z^{-1}}(\mathcal{S}_{k})
of the
tautological bundle

\mathcal{S}_{k}
fixed by the short exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{S}_{k}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathcal{Q}_{n-k}\to 0
, where
\mathcal{Q}_{n-k}
denotes the
quotient bundle
. The variables
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}
are the first
k

Chern roots
. The following result is due to Mihalcea
[
44
, Thm 1.1]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Mihalcea)
.

The equivariant quantum cohomology ring
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
has the presentation,

(2.1)

qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n)\cong R[q][e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}]/\langle h_{k+1},\ldots,h_{n-1},h_{n}+(-1)^{k}q\rangle

where
q
is the quantum deformation parameter and
h_{r}=\det(\tau^{j-1}e_{1-i+j})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}
are the Chern classes of the quotient bundle with
\tau
denoting the shift operator; see Appendix
A
.

Furthermore, we recall that
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
has a distinguished additive
R[q]
-basis, the
Schubert classes

\sigma_{\lambda}
, where
\lambda
runs over all partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
whose Young diagram fits into a
k\times(n-k)
rectangle, i.e.
\lambda_{1}\leq n-k
. We shall denote this set by
\mathcal{P}_{k,n}
. Under the above isomorphism the Schubert classes are sent to the factorial Schur functions
s_{\lambda}=\det(\tau^{j-1}e_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}-i+j})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
with
\lambda^{\prime}
denoting the conjugate partition. The latter are specialisations of Lascoux and Schützenberger’s double Schubert polynomials to Grassmannian permutations; see Appendix
A
for details.

The main aim of this article is to give new combinatorial formulae for the computation of the structure constants
C_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}(y,q)
of the ring (
2.1
) in the Schubert basis defined via the expansion (we shall denote quantum multiplication by
\star
throughout for clarity)

(2.2)

\sigma_{\lambda}\star\sigma_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu}C^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu}(y,q)\sigma_{\nu}=\sum_{\nu}q^{d}C^{\nu,d}_{\lambda\mu}(y)\sigma_{\nu},

where the coefficients are called the
equivariant Gromov-Witten invariants
.

2.2.
The Satake correspondence for projective space

Let
V=R^{n}
be the free
R
-module with
n\geq 2
arbitrary but fixed and denote by
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\subset V
the standard basis. We use the notation
V^{*}
for its dual with basis
\{v^{1},\ldots,v^{n}\}
.

As
R
-modules we have the isomorphism
\Sigma_{1}:V\to H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
where

H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})\cong R[x]/\langle(x-y_{1})\cdots(x-y_{n})\rangle

and we identify the standard basis in
V
with the Schubert basis in
H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
via

(2.3)

\Sigma_{1}:\quad v_{i+1}\mapsto\sigma_{i}=(x-y_{1})\cdots(x-y_{i}),\qquad\forall i=0,1,\ldots,n-1\;.

We now turn the latter module isomorphism into an isomorphism of
R
-algebras: denote by
\{E_{ij}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)~:~1\leq i,j\leq n\}
the standard basis, the unit matrices, and define the following sum of a nilpotent and a (trivially) semi-simple element,

(2.4)

X=X_{\rm n}+X_{\rm s}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}E_{i+1,i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}E_{ii}\;.

Define an
R
-algebra structure on
V
by setting
v_{i}\odot v_{j}=(X-y_{1}\,1)\cdots(X-y_{i}\,1).v_{j}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The
R
-algebra
(V,\odot)
is associative and commutative. Moreover, the map (
2.3
) is an isomorphism of
R
-algebras
V\cong H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
and

\Sigma_{1}\circ(1+X)=c(\mathcal{S})\circ\Sigma_{1}\;,

where
c(\mathcal{S})
is the total Chern class of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

The proof uses the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
1\leq m<n
we have that

(X-y_{1}\cdot 1)\cdots(X-y_{m}\cdot 1)=\sum_{i-j=k}E_{ij}+\sum_{0\leq i-j<k}p_{ij}^{(m)}(y)E_{ij}

where the
p_{ij}^{(m)}(y)
are either zero if
j>i
or homogeneous polynomials in the equivariant parameters of degree
m-i+j
with

p_{ij}^{(m)}(y)=h_{m+j-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i}|y).

In particular, we have for
k=n
the matrix identity:

(2.5)

\displaystyle(X-y_{1}\cdot 1)\ldots(X-y_{n}\cdot 1)=0\;.

Proof.

We prove the first statement by induction in the factorial power
m
. The base of induction is trivial. Thus, we are left to complete the induction step. Using the induction assumption we have

\displaystyle(X-y_{1})\cdots(X-y_{m})(X-y_{m+1})v_{j}=(X-y_{m+1})\sum\limits_{i=j}^{j+m}h_{m+j-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i}|y)v_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=j}^{j+m}h_{m+j-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i}|y)v_{i+1}+\sum\limits_{i=j}^{j+m}(y_{i}-y_{m+1})h_{m+j-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i}|y)v_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=j+1}^{j+m+1}h_{m+j+1-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i-1}|y)v_{i}+\sum\limits_{i=j}^{j+m}(y_{i}-y_{m+1})h_{m+j-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i}|y)v_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=j}^{j+m+1}(h_{m+j+1-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i-1}|y)+(y_{i}-y_{m+1})h_{m+j-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i}|y))v_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=j}^{j+m+1}h_{m+1+j-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i}|y)v_{i},

where in the last equality we have applied the branching rule for complete factorial symmetric polynomials; see Appendix
A
.
∎

2.3.
The left Weyl group action

Recall that there is a natural
\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-action on
\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n)
which induces a left Weyl group action on
H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
,

(2.6)

(s_{i},\sigma_{w})\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{w}+(y_{i}-y_{i+1})\sigma_{s_{i}w},&\ell(s_{i}w)=\ell(w)-1\;\&\;s_{i}w\in S^{k,n}\\
\sigma_{w},&\text{ else}\end{array}\right.\;,

where
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
and
S^{k,n}
is the set of minimal length coset representatives of
S_{n}/S_{k}\times S_{n-k}
. Note that there is also a natural
S_{n}
-action on
R
(permuting the equivariant parameters) and since
H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
is an
R
-module it is tacitly understood that the action (
2.6
) is compatible with the ring action, i.e.
s_{i}.(r\sigma)=(s_{i}.r)(s_{i}.\sigma)
for all
r\in R
and
\sigma\in H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
. In other words,
H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
is a module for the skew group ring
R\#S_{n}
and this will be understood throughout the article including for our discussion of the quantum ring.

In light of the isomorphism (
2.3
), we wish to describe the above left Weyl group action for
k=1
in terms of the Lie group
\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
in order to then extend it to the tensor products of
R
-modules,
V^{\otimes k}
and
\bigwedge^{k}V
, below.

Lemma 2.3
.

The map
(s_{i},v_{j})\mapsto g_{i}.v_{j}
with

(2.7)

g_{i}:=\exp(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{\rm s}}(E_{i,i+1}))\in\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n-1

and
j=1,\ldots,n
defines a group action of
S_{n}
on
V
and the map (
2.3
) is an
R\#S_{n}
-module isomorphism with respect to the action defined via (
2.7
) on
H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
.

Proof.

If we specialise the left Weyl group action (
2.6
) to
H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
by setting
k=1
we arrive at

(2.8)

(s_{i},\sigma_{j})\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{j}+(y_{j}-y_{j+1})\sigma_{j+1},&j=i\\
\sigma_{j},&\text{ else}\end{array}\right.\;.

On the other hand, we have that
\operatorname{ad}_{X_{\rm s}}(E_{i,i+1})=(y_{i}-y_{i+1})E_{i,i+1}
and hence that

\exp(\operatorname{ad}_{X_{\rm s}}(E_{i,i+1}))=1+(y_{i}-y_{i+1})E_{i,i+1}\;.

From this explicit form of the action on
V
and (
2.3
) the claim is now obvious.
∎

Having identified the Lie algebra element
X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)
in (
2.4
) with the first Chern class of the tautological bundle via (
2.3
) it must be invariant under the left Weyl group action.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
g_{i}\in\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
be defined as in (
2.7
). Then we have the commutation relations

(2.9)

g_{i}^{-1}Xg_{i}=X^{s_{i}},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n-1,

where
X^{s_{i}}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)
denotes the element (
2.4
) with the equivariant parameters
y_{i}
and
y_{i+1}
exchanged.

Proof.

A simple case of matrix multiplication.
∎

2.4.
The Satake correspondence for Grassmannians

We remind the reader that under the Satake correspondence one identifies
H^{*}(\operatorname{Gr}(k,n))
with the
k
-fold exterior power of the cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. In particular, the correspondence is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-modules and the multiplication by the first Chern class corresponds to the action by the principal nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Here we recall the extension of the Satake correspondence to the torus-equivariant case; see for example
[
2
,
35
]
and references therein.

We start by identifying the direct sum of all cohomology rings with the exterior
R
-algebra of
V\simeq H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
as
R
-modules: namely, we consider the
R
-module isomorphism

(2.10)

\Sigma:\bigwedge V=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}V\to\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))

where for any subset
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
that is fixed by a Grassmannian permutation
w=[i_{1}\ldots i_{n}]\in S^{k,n}
we set

(2.11)

\Sigma:\quad\ket{w}=v_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{i_{k}}\mapsto\sigma_{w}

with
k=0,1,\ldots,n
. Here we have used that for each fixed
0\leq k\leq n
one can alternatively label Schubert classes by Grassmannian permutations as the latter are in bijection with partitions whose Young diagrams fit into the
k\times(n-k)
rectangle. The bijection is given by
w\mapsto\lambda(w)=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
, where
\lambda(w)
is the partition with parts
i_{j}=\lambda_{k+1-j}+j
. We will use both types of labels interchangeably throughout.

Example 2.5
.

Consider
k=6
,
n=10
and
\lambda=(4,4,2,2,1,0)
. The corresponding Grassmannian permutation
w(\lambda)
is recovered by drawing a lattice path prescribing the outline of the Young diagram of
\lambda
, where we record the positions
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
of each vertical and the positions
\{i_{k+1},\ldots,i_{n}\}
of each horizontal move in terms of black and a white-go stone, respectively. The resulting Grassmannian permutation in one-line notation is then given by the positions of the go-stones as
w(\lambda)=[i_{1}\ldots i_{k}i_{k+1}\ldots i_{n}]
; see Figure
4.1
. Alternatively, one can obtain
w(\lambda)
as a reduced expression in terms of simple reflections by reading out the content of the boxes in the Young diagram bottom to top and right to left. In our example,

w(\lambda)=s_{8}s_{9}\,s_{7}s_{8}\,s_{4}s_{5}s_{6}s_{7}\,s_{2}s_{3}s_{4}s_{5}s_{6}\;.

We will occasionally also need the image of Schubert classes under level-rank duality which in terms of Young diagrams is the conjugate partition
\lambda^{\prime}
obtained by swapping black and white go-stones and reading their positions from right to left; see Figure
4.1
.

Recall that there is natural action of the universal enveloping algebra
U
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)
on
V^{\otimes k}
via the coproduct,

\Delta^{k-1}(X)=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{k},\quad X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)

where
X_{i}=1\otimes\cdots 1\otimes X\otimes 1\cdots\otimes 1
acts non-trivially in the
i
th factor. This action descends to
\bigwedge^{k}V
because of Schur-Weyl duality. The following result for Grassmannians can be found in the literature; see e.g.
[
2
,
35
]
and references therein.

Theorem 2.2
.

The restriction
\Sigma_{k}=\Sigma|_{\bigwedge^{k}V}
of the map (
2.11
) to
\bigwedge^{k}V
is an isomorphism of
R
-algebras
\bigwedge^{k}V\cong H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
via

\Sigma_{k}\circ\Delta^{k-1}(X)=c_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{k})\circ\Sigma_{k}\;,

where
X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)
is defined in (
2.4
) and
c_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{k})
is the first Chern class of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{S}_{k}
. As the latter generates the ring
H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
, this fixes the isomorphism completely.

Let
\mathbb{X}_{k}=(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})^{\times k}
. In what follows we will make sometimes use of the fact that we can canonically identify the tensor product
V^{\otimes k}
with the equivariant cohomology
H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{X}_{k})\cong H_{T}^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})^{\otimes k}
(as
R
-algebras) and that multiplication by symmetric polynomials in the Chern roots ‘commutes’ with the natural projection
V^{\otimes k}\twoheadrightarrow\bigwedge^{k}V
in the following sense: let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
denote the
k
Chern roots of the
k
copies of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, then multiplication with any elementary symmetric polynomial
e_{r}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
in
H^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{X}_{k})
corresponds to multiplication by the
r
th Chern class
c_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{k})
of the tautological bundle in
H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Under the isomorphism (
2.11
) we have the identities

(2.12)

\Sigma_{k}\circ e_{r}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})=c_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{k})\circ\Sigma_{k},\qquad r=0,1,\ldots,k

and

(2.13)

\Sigma_{k}\circ\mathfrak{S}_{w}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k};y)=\sigma_{w}\circ\Sigma_{k},\qquad\forall w\in S^{k,n},

where
\mathfrak{S}_{w}(x;y)
is the double Schubert polynomial and the classes on the right hand side act by multiplication in
H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
.

In light of the first identity it is natural to define the analogue of the Chern polynomial.

Lemma 2.7
.

Fix
1\leq k\leq n-1
and let
u
be an indeterminate. Then

t(u)=(u\cdot 1+X_{1})\cdots(u\cdot 1+X_{k})\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)\otimes_{R}R[u]

commutes with the left Weyl group action on
\bigwedge^{k}V
and, thus, setting
t_{i}=t(-y_{i})\in\operatorname{End}_{R}\bigwedge^{k}V
we have

(2.14)

s_{i}t_{i}=t_{i+1}s_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad s_{i}t_{j}=t_{j}s_{i}\;\text{ if }|i-j|>1\;.

Here by abuse of notation we have denoted the image of the
s_{i}
in
\operatorname{End}\bigwedge^{k}V
by the same symbol. We note that the
t_{i}
are singular and that
t_{1}t_{2}\ldots t_{n}=0
.

Proof.

The claims follow directly from our previous Lemmata
2.2
and
2.4
as well as Schur-Weyl duality.
∎

2.5.
The exterior and the Clifford algebra

Consider the exterior
R
-algebra
\bigwedge V
from (
2.11
). The latter can be identified with the Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}_{n}(R)
over
V
with trivial quadratic form. Namely,
denote by
\psi_{i}^{*}=v_{i}\wedge
the multiplication operator
\bigwedge^{k}V\to\bigwedge^{k+1}V
with
v_{i}\in V
and by
\psi_{i}=\imath_{v^{i}}
the contraction operator
\bigwedge^{k}V\to\bigwedge^{k-1}V
with
v^{i}\in V^{*}
,

(2.15)

\imath_{v^{i}}(v_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{j_{k}})=\sum_{r=1}^{k}(-1)^{r-1}\delta_{ij_{r}}v_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{j_{r-1}}\wedge v_{j_{r+1}}\cdots\wedge v_{j_{k}}\;.

It is a standard fact that these operators satisfy the canonical anti-commutation relations (CAR) of free fermions,

(2.16)

\psi_{i}\psi_{j}+\psi_{j}\psi_{i}=\psi_{i}^{*}\psi_{j}^{*}+\psi_{j}^{*}\psi_{i}^{*}=0,\qquad\psi_{i}\psi^{*}_{j}+\psi_{j}^{*}\psi_{i}=\delta_{ij}\;.

As already pointed out in the introduction, the natural action of
\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
on
V
induces automorphisms of the Clifford algebra via

(2.17)

g.\psi^{*}_{i}:=\sum_{j}g_{ij}\psi_{j}^{*}\qquad\text{and}\qquad g.\psi_{i}:=\sum_{j}\psi_{j}(g^{-1})_{ji},\qquad\forall g\in\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)\;.

One verifies via a straightforward computation that
g.\psi_{i},g.\psi_{i}^{*}
still satisfy the CAR. In particular, any action of a group
G
on
V
induces via its representation map
G\to\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
a group action via automorphisms of the Clifford algebra,
g.\psi^{*}_{i}=\psi^{*}_{g.v_{i}}
and
g.\psi_{i}=\imath_{(g^{-1})^{T}.v^{i}}
. We now apply this to the case of the left Weyl group action with
G=S_{n}
using Lemma
2.3
. Note that the left Weyl group action naturally extends to an action on
\bigwedge V
under Schur-Weyl duality. The next lemma describes how this action on
\bigwedge V
interacts with that of the Clifford algebra.

Lemma 2.8
.

We have the commutation relations

s_{i}\circ\psi^{*}_{j}=(g_{i}.\psi_{j}^{*})\circ s_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{i}\circ\psi_{j}=(g_{i}.\psi_{j})\circ s_{i}

where the matrices
g_{i}\in\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
with
i=1,\ldots,n-1
are defined in (
2.7
). Explicitly,

g_{i}.\psi^{*}_{j}=\psi^{*}_{j}+\delta_{i+1,j}(y_{i}-y_{i+1})\psi^{*}_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{i}.\psi_{j}=\psi_{j}-\delta_{i,j}(y_{i}-y_{i+1})\psi_{i+1}\;.

Proof.

A straightforward computation using (
2.17
) and the definition (
2.7
).
∎

2.6.
Cyclic modules of the Clifford algebra: the Fock space and its dual

We recall the construction of some fundamental Clifford algebra modules as these might not be familiar to a wider mathematical audience. For this we now consider the (finite) Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}_{n}(R)
as abstract
R
-algebra generated by
\{\psi_{i},\psi_{i}^{*}~:~i=1,\ldots,n\}
subject to the CAR (
2.16
).

Define the following free
R
-modules,

(2.18)

\displaystyle W_{n}^{-}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}R\psi_{k},\qquad W_{n}^{+}=\bigoplus^{n}_{k=1}R\psi^{*}_{k},\qquad W_{n}=W_{n}^{-}\oplus W_{n}^{+}

and set

(2.19)

\mathcal{F}_{n}=\mathcal{C}_{n}/\mathcal{C}_{n}W^{-}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{F}^{*}_{n}=\mathcal{C}_{n}W^{+}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{n}\;.

The latter spaces are natural left and right
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-modules generated by the cyclic vectors

(2.20)

\ket{0}=1\!\!\mod\mathcal{C}_{n}W^{-}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bra{0}=1\!\!\mod W^{+}\mathcal{C}_{n}\;,

respectively. The cyclic vectors satisfy the relations

\psi_{i}\ket{0}=0,\qquad\bra{0}\psi_{i}^{*}=0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,n\;.

We call
\mathcal{F}_{n}
the
fermionic Fock space
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{n}
its dual, since there exists a symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{n}\otimes_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}\mathcal{F}_{n}\to R
called the
vacuum expectation value
(VEV) denoted by

\tilde{F}\otimes F\mapsto\bra{0}\tilde{F}F\ket{0}=\langle\tilde{F}F\rangle

The latter functional is defined via the following relations:

(2.21)

\displaystyle\langle 1\rangle=1,\quad\langle\psi_{i}\psi_{j}\rangle=\langle\psi_{i}^{*}\psi^{*}_{j}\rangle=\langle\psi^{*}_{i}\psi_{j}\rangle=0,\quad\langle\psi_{i}\psi^{*}_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}

and for
F_{i}\in\{\psi_{i},\psi^{*}_{i}\}
we set

\langle F_{1}\ldots F_{r}\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum\limits_{w}(-1)^{w}\langle F_{w_{1}}F_{w_{2}}\rangle\cdots\langle F_{w_{r-1}}F_{w_{r}}\rangle,&\;r\;\text{ even}\\
0,&\;r\;\text{ odd}\end{array}\right.\;,

where the sum runs over all permutations
w=[w_{1}\ldots w_{r}]\in S_{r}
such that
w_{i}<w_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
and
w_{1}<w_{3}<\ldots<w_{r-2}<w_{r}
. This relation is known as
Wick’s Theorem
in the physics literature, where it is used to compute density-density correlation functions for free fermion gases.

Proposition 2.9
.

Sending
\mathcal{F}_{n}\ni\ket{0}\mapsto 1\in R
provides an isomorphism of (left)
\mathcal{C}_{n}(R)
-modules.

Proof.

Since
\ket{0}
is cyclic, it suffices to show that the map
\psi^{*}_{i_{1}}\ldots\psi^{*}_{i_{k}}\ket{0}\mapsto v_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{i_{k}}
for any Grassmannian permutation
w=[i_{1}\ldots i_{n}]\in S^{k,n}
with
k=0,1,\ldots,n
intertwines the action of the Clifford algebra. But this is obvious from the definitions.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

By similar means one shows that
(\bigwedge V)^{*}\simeq\bigwedge V^{*}
has the structure of a
right

\mathcal{C}_{n}(R)
-module isomorphic to the dual Fock module
\mathcal{F}^{*}
. The Clifford generators naturally induce maps
\psi_{i}:\bigwedge^{k}V^{*}\to\bigwedge^{k+1}V^{*}
and
\psi^{*}_{i}:\bigwedge^{k}V^{*}\to\bigwedge^{k-1}V^{*}
. Moreover, for any
w=[i_{1}\ldots i_{n}]\in S^{k,n}
one can identify
\bra{0}\psi_{i_{k}}\cdots\psi_{i_{1}}\in\mathcal{F^{*}}
with the dual Schubert class
\sigma^{w}
satisfying
\langle\sigma^{u},\sigma_{w}\rangle=\delta_{uw}
with respect to the Poincaré bilinear form.

We have the following identification of maps under the

(2.22)

Let us prove the first formula in (

\psi^{*}_{1}(\sigma_{\lambda})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{(\lambda_{1}-1,\ldots,\lambda_{k}-1)},&\ell(\lambda)=k\\

∎

3.
Equivariant quantum Schubert calculus via the Clifford algebra

We now extend our construction to the quantum case. There are two ways of motivating the definition of the quantum ring
qH^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
from an algebraic/combinatorial perspective – both are of course interlinked:

(1)

Consider the loop algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[z,z^{-1}]=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)\otimes_{R}R[z,z^{-1}]
and its natural action on
V[z,z^{-1}]=V\otimes_{R}R[z,z^{-1}]
. Define a ‘quantised’ Chern root by considering the element

(3.1)

X^{q}=X^{q}_{\rm n}+X_{\rm s}=z\,E_{1,n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}E_{i+1,i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}E_{ii}\;,

where the loop parameter
z
is up to a sign the quantum parameter
q
. As we will show below the matrix (
3.1
) now satisfies the defining relations of
qH^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
. Thus, when extending the Satake map (
2.11
), the elementary symmetric polynomials
e_{r}(X^{q}_{1},\ldots,X^{q}_{k})
now describe the
quantum
multiplication by the tautological Chern classes
c_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{k})
in
qH^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
and the double Schubert polynomial
\mathfrak{S}_{w}(X_{1}^{q},\ldots,X_{k}^{q};y)
the
quantum
multiplication by a Schubert class.

(2)

Extend the left Weyl group action from Lemma
2.3
to an action of the extended affine Weyl group
W=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes S_{n}
, where the translations
t_{i}=t_{\epsilon_{i}}
generating the normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
act on
\bigwedge^{k}V\otimes_{R}R[z,z^{-1}]
by

t_{i}=t(-y_{i})=(X^{q}_{1}-y_{i}\cdot 1)\cdots(X_{k}^{q}-y_{i}\cdot 1),\quad i=1,\ldots,n\;;

compare with Lemma
2.7
. In particular, the affine Weyl reflection
s_{0}
acts according to

(3.2)

s_{0}\mapsto g_{0}=\exp\left(z^{-1}\operatorname{ad}_{X_{\rm s}}E_{n,1}\right)=1+z^{-1}(y_{n}-y_{1})E_{n,1}

and
g^{-1}_{0}.X^{q}.g_{0}=(X^{q})^{s_{0}}
where
(X^{q})^{s_{0}}
denotes the matrix
X^{q}
with the equivariant parameters
y_{1}
and
y_{n}
swapped; compare with Lemma
2.4
. In particular, we will show via Formula (
3.18
) that the action of the normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\leq W
for projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}=\,\mathrm{Gr}(1;n)
‘generates’ the quantum multiplication in
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n)
together with the action of the Clifford algebra.

Because both of these algebraic descriptions of the quantum multiplication can be expressed in terms of the Clifford algebra, this leads to a recurrence formula of the quantum product in
qH^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
in terms of push-pull maps.

3.1.
Quantised Chern roots

Set
V[z^{\pm 1}]=V\otimes_{R}R[z^{\pm 1}]
. The matrix
X^{q}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[z^{\pm 1}]
defined in (
3.1
) reads explicitly,

(3.3)

X^{q}=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}&0&\cdots&0&z\\
1&y_{2}&0&&0\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&1&y_{n-1}&0\\
0&\cdots&0&1&y_{n}\end{pmatrix}\;.

We shall identify the latter with the quantised Chern root of projective space because of the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
1\leq m\leq n
we have that

(X^{q}-y_{1}\cdot 1)\cdots(X^{q}-y_{m}\cdot 1)=\\
z\delta_{m,n}\cdot 1+\sum_{i-j=m}E_{ij}+z\sum_{i-j=m-n}E_{ij}+\sum_{0\leq i-j<m}p^{(m)}_{ij}(y)E_{ij}+z\sum_{i-j<m-n}p^{(m)}_{ij}(y)E_{ij}

where the
p^{(m)}_{ij}(y)
are either zero or homogeneous polynomials of degree
m-i+j
if
i>j
and of degree
m-n-i+j
if
i<j
, concretely

p_{ij}^{(m)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{m+j-i}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{i}|y),\quad i\geq j\\
h_{m-n-i+j}(y_{j},\ldots,y_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{i}|y),\quad i<j.\end{array}\right.

For
m=n
we have

(3.4)

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X^{q}-y_{j}\cdot 1)=z\cdot 1\;.

In particular,
\det(X^{q}-y_{j}\cdot 1)=z
and, thus, each
(X^{q}-y_{j}\cdot 1)\in\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R[z^{\pm 1}])
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines as for Lemma
2.2
, and thus we omit it.
∎

3.2.
The equivariant quantum Satake map for Grassmannians

In close analogy with the non-quantum case we now define the quantum Satake map starting with projective space. Recall that

qH^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})\cong R[q^{\pm 1}][x]/\langle(x-y_{1})\cdots(x-y_{n})-q\rangle

where we have assumed the quantum parameter to be invertible in order to define the action of the extended affine Weyl group below.

Define an isomorphism
\Sigma^{q}_{1}:V[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z-q\rangle\to qH^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
of
R[q^{\pm 1}]
-modules by sending the standard basis
\{v_{i}~:~1\leq i\leq n\}
in
V[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z-q\rangle
to the Schubert basis in
qH^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
as before,

(3.5)

\Sigma^{q}_{1}:\quad v_{i+1}\mapsto\sigma_{i}=(x-y_{1})\cdots(x-y_{i}),\qquad\forall i=0,1,\ldots,n-1\;.

However, we now define a different multiplication map on
V[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z-q\rangle
setting instead
v_{i}\star v_{j}=(X^{q}-y_{1}\,1)\cdots(X^{q}-y_{i-1}\,1).v_{j}
, where
X^{q}
is the matrix defined above.

Proposition 3.2
.

The
R[q^{\pm 1}]
-algebra
(V[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z-q\rangle,\star)
is associative and commutative. Moreover, the map (
3.5
) is an isomorphism of
R[q^{\pm 1}]
-algebras and

\Sigma^{q}_{1}\circ(1+X^{q})=c(\mathcal{S})\circ\Sigma^{q}_{1}\;,

where
c(\mathcal{S})
now denotes the quantum multiplication operator by the total Chern class of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Proof.

The proof is an immediate consequence of the matrix identity (
3.4
).
∎

Since
X^{q}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R[z^{\pm 1}])
there is a natural action of the symmetric polynomials in the quantised Chern roots

X_{i}^{q}=1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1\otimes\underset{i}{X^{q}}\otimes 1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1,\quad i=1,\ldots,k

on the
R[z^{\pm 1}]
-module
V^{\otimes k}\otimes_{R}R[z^{\pm 1}]\cong(V)^{\otimes k}[z^{\pm 1}]
using the standard coproduct formula,

\Delta^{k-1}(X^{q})=X_{1}^{q}+\cdots+X^{q}_{k},

where
\Delta
is an
R[z^{\pm 1}]
-algebra morphism.

Proposition 3.3
.

Define an isomorphism
\Sigma^{q}_{k}:\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z+(-1)^{k}q\rangle\to qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
of
R[q^{\pm 1}]
-modules by naturally extending the map (
2.11
) over the base
R[q^{\pm 1}]
.
Then this map becomes an isomorphism of
R[q^{\pm 1}]
-algebras if we in addition set

(3.6)

\Sigma^{q}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{S}_{w}(X^{q}_{1},\ldots,X^{q}_{k}|y)=\sigma_{w}\circ\Sigma^{q}_{k},\qquad\forall w\in S^{k,n},

where
\mathfrak{S}_{w}(x;y)
is the double Schubert polynomial and the Schubert classes
\sigma_{w}
on the right hand side act by quantum multiplication in
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
.

We split the proof of this proposition into several steps. First we prove the following, see
[
36
, Thrm 1.2]
:

Claim 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{w}(X^{q}_{1},\ldots,X^{q}_{k}|y)
be the double Schubert polynomial in the variables
X^{q}_{i}
, then for any Grassmannian permutation
w\in S^{k,n}
we have

(3.7)

\mathfrak{S}_{w}(X^{q}_{1},\dots,X^{q}_{n}|y)\ket{e}=\ket{w}\;,

where
e\in S^{k,n}
is the identity permutation.

Proof.

We start by observing that
\ket{e}
can be written in the following convenient form provided we consider
\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]
as a submodule of
V^{\otimes k}[z^{\pm 1}]
,

\ket{e}=v_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{k}=(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\Delta(X^{q})v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{1},

where
\Delta(X^{q})=\prod\limits_{i<j}^{k}(X^{q}_{i}-X^{q}_{j})
is the Vandermonde determinant. Recall that for Grassmannian permutations
w
we have that
\mathfrak{S}_{w}(x|y)=s_{\lambda(w)}(x|y)
, the factorial Schur polynomial; see Appendix
A
. Using the alternant formula for the factorial Schur polynomial we find

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(X^{q}_{1},\ldots,X^{q}_{k}|y)\ket{e}=\;

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\underset{k\times k}{\det}((X^{q}_{j}|y)^{\lambda_{i}+k-i})v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{1}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in S_{k}}(-1)^{\sigma}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{k}(X^{q}_{j}|y)^{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}+k-\sigma(j)}v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{1}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle v_{\lambda_{k}+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{\lambda_{1}+k}=\ket{\lambda}\;.

Here we have used in the last step that all terms involving the equivariant parameters cancel.
∎

Introduce the following
\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]
: we set
\deg y_{i}=1
,
\deg\ket{\lambda}=|\lambda|
and
\deg z=n
. Note that this the natural grading inherited from
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
under the linear isomorphism (
2.11
). Next we show that the ring structure on
\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]
is well-defined:

Claim 3.2
.

The pair
(\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}],\star)
with product

(3.8)

\ket{\lambda}\star\ket{\nu}=s_{\lambda}(X^{q}_{1},\ldots,X^{q}_{k}|y)\ket{\nu},

is a well-defined graded commutative associative ring with multiplicative identity
\ket{\emptyset}=\ket{e}=v_{1}\wedge v_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{k}
.

Proof.

We start by noting that the factorial Schur polynomials
s_{\lambda}(X^{q}|y)
and
s_{\mu}(X^{q}|y)
commute in
\operatorname{End}_{R}V[z^{\pm 1}]^{\otimes k}
, simply because the quantised Chern roots
X_{i}^{q}
mutually commute. But as the factorial Schur polynomials are symmetric in the
X_{i}^{q}
their restriction to the antisymmetric tensor product must also form an abelian family of operators in
\operatorname{End}_{R}\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]
.

To show that this implies commutativity of the ring structure
\star
, we now use formula (
3.7
), which immediately gives

\displaystyle\ket{\lambda}\star\ket{\mu}=s_{\lambda}(X^{q}|y)\ket{\mu}=\;

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(X^{q}|y)s_{\mu}(X^{q}|y)\ket{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle s_{\mu}(X^{q}|y)s_{\lambda}(X^{q}|y)\ket{\emptyset}=s_{\mu}(X^{q}|y)\ket{\lambda}=\ket{\mu}\star\ket{\lambda}.

Associativity follows from associativity of matrix multiplication and
\ket{e}
is a multiplicative identity because
s_{\emptyset}(X^{q}|y)=1
.

Finally, to show that this is a graded ring, we observe that each monomial
\prod_{\Box\in\lambda}(X^{q}_{T(\Box)}-y_{T(\Box)+c(\Box)})
acting on
\ket{\mu}
increases the degree by
|\lambda|
since multiplication by
X^{q}
either (1) shifts
v_{i_{T(\Box)}}
to
v_{i_{T(\Box)}+1}
(which amounts to adding a box), or (2) multiplies with
y_{i_{T(\Box)}}
, or (3) removes a maximal rim hook (by shifting
i_{k}=n
to
1
) of length
n-1
and multiplies with
z=(-1)^{k-1}q
.
∎

As a last step in the proof of Prop.
3.3
it remains to show that both graded ring structures coincide. Using a result of Mihalcea
[
42
, Cor. 7.1]
it suffices to show that the quantum Chevalley formula from
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
holds; this identifies the ring up to isomorphism (together with the natural grading inherited from
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
).

Claim 3.3
.

The quantum Chevalley formula holds in
(\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z+(-1)^{k}q\rangle,\star)
.

Proof.

Noting that

s_{\Box}(X^{q}|y)\ket{\lambda}=e_{1}(X^{q}|y)\ket{\lambda}=(X_{1}^{q}+\cdots+X_{k}^{q}-y_{1}-\cdots-y_{k})\ket{\lambda}

it suffices to consider the action of
X_{r}^{q}
on a state
\ket{\lambda}
. Using the bijection between Grassmannian permutations
w=[i_{1}\ldots i_{n}]\in S^{k,n}
and partitions
\lambda
with
i_{j}=\lambda_{k+1-j}+j
one easily verifies for
1\leq r<k
that either

X_{r}^{q}\ket{\lambda}=\ket{\lambda^{(r)}}+y_{i_{r}}\ket{\lambda}

where
\lambda^{(r)}
is the partition obtained by adding to the Young diagram of
\lambda
a single box with content
i_{r}-k
, or if that is not possible (
i_{r}+1=i_{r+1}
), then
X_{r}^{q}\ket{\lambda}=y_{i_{r}}\ket{\lambda}
. The case
r=k
is special: in this case
X_{r}^{q}\ket{\lambda}=q\ket{\lambda^{-}}+y_{i_{k}}\ket{\lambda}
where
\lambda^{-}
is the partition obtained by removing a maximal rim hook from the Young diagram of
\lambda
. This is only possible if
i_{1}>1
and
i_{k}=n
, otherwise the quantum term is absent. Thus, we arrive at

\ket{1}\star\ket{\lambda}=\sum_{\mu-\lambda=(1)}\ket{\mu}+(y_{i_{1}}+\cdots+y_{i_{k}}-y_{1}-\cdots-y_{k})\ket{\lambda}+q\ket{\lambda^{-}}\;.

This is the quantum Chevalley formula for
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
; see
[
42
, Thm 1]
.
∎

3.3.
The extended affine Weyl group action

Recall the left Weyl group action from Lemma
2.3
. We now extend this action to an action of the extended affine Weyl group
W=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes S_{n}
on
V[z^{\pm 1}]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Define a left
W
-action on
V[z^{\pm 1}]
via

(3.9)

(s_{i},v_{k})\mapsto g_{i}.v_{k}\quad\text{ and }\quad(t_{j},v_{k})\mapsto(X^{q}-y_{j}\cdot 1).v_{k}\;,

where
s_{i}
with
i=1,\ldots,n-1
are the simple reflections in
S_{n}
and
t_{j}
with
j=1,\ldots,n
the translations by the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Here it is understood that we interpret
V[z^{\pm 1}]
as module with respect to the skew group ring
R\#W
where the translations are acting trivially on
R
. We note that

(3.10)

t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n}=z\cdot 1\quad\text{and}\quad\varrho=s_{1}\cdots s_{n-1}t_{n}=zE_{1,n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}E_{i+1,i}\;.

Moreover, the affine Weyl reflection
s_{0}=\varrho s_{n-1}\varrho^{-1}
acts via the matrix (
3.2

Using the standard coproduct for the group algebra of
W
the action extends canonically to
V[z^{\pm 1}]^{\otimes k}
and
\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]
by acting diagonally. If we consider in particular the action of the cyclic element
\varrho
in (

\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]
we find that

\varrho.(v_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{i_{k-1}}\wedge v_{n})=(-1)^{k-1}z\;v_{1}\wedge v_{i_{1}+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{i_{k-1}+1}

and, thus, for positivity it is natural to define the quantum parameter for
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
as

.

The following lemma summarises the commutation relations between the extended affine Weyl group and the Clifford algebra.

Lemma 3.5
.

In addition to the relations from Lemma
2.8
, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle s_{0}\circ\psi^{*}_{i}=(g_{0}.\psi_{i}^{*})\circ s_{0},\quad g_{0}.\psi^{*}_{i}=\psi^{*}_{i}+\delta_{i1}z^{-1}(y_{n}-y_{1})\psi^{*}_{n}

(3.12)

\displaystyle s_{0}\circ\psi_{i}=(g_{0}.\psi_{i})\circ s_{0},\quad g_{0}.\psi_{i}=\psi_{i}-\delta_{in}z^{-1}(y_{n}-y_{1})\psi_{1}\;.

as well as

(3.13)

\begin{array}[]{c}\varrho\circ\psi^{*}_{i}=z^{\delta_{in}}\psi^{*}_{i+1}\circ\varrho=((-1)^{\operatorname{deg}-1}q)^{\delta_{in}}\psi^{*}_{i+1}\circ\varrho,\\

where

and indices are understood modulo

and deg denotes the ‘degree operator’ which assigns to a tensor its degree. Finally, for the translations

\displaystyle t_{j}\circ\psi^{*}_{i}=(t_{j}.\psi_{i}^{*})\circ t_{j},\qquad t_{j}.\psi_{i}^{*}=\psi^{*}_{i+1}+(y_{i}-y_{j})\psi_{i}^{*}=\psi^{*}_{t_{j}.v_{i}}

\displaystyle t^{-1}_{j}\circ\psi_{i}=(t^{-1}_{j}.\psi_{i})\circ t^{-1}_{j},\qquad t^{-1}_{j}.\psi_{i}=\psi_{i-1}+(y_{i}-y_{j})\psi_{i}=\psi_{(t_{j})^{T}.v_{i}}\;.

3.4.
The fermionic product formula

We now state a recurrence formula for the quantum product in
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
using the Clifford algebra. In the non-equivariant case (
y_{i}=0
) this formula was obtained in
[
33
, Eq (1.5) and Thm 1.3]
using the results from
[
32
, Section 11]
. The new result in the present article is its non-trivial extension to the equivariant case and the geometric interpretation of the Clifford algebra in terms of push-pull maps. To state the formula it is convenient to label the Clifford generators in light of (
3.13
) by arbitrary integers setting for any
1\leq i\leq n

(3.16)

\psi_{i+n}=z^{-1}\psi_{i}=(-1)^{\operatorname{deg}-1}q^{-1}\psi_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad\psi^{*}_{i+n}=z\psi^{*}_{i}=(-1)^{\operatorname{deg}-1}q\,\psi^{*}_{i}\;.

These ‘quasi-periodic’ boundary conditions where imposed
ad-hoc
in
[
32
,
33
]
but now can be interpreted naturally in light of the extended affine Weyl group action from Lemma
3.5
and (
3.13
). Henceforth, we shall use the quantum parameter
q=(-1)^{\operatorname{deg}-1}z
in formulae and it is always understood that we specialise the loop parameter
z
in the quantised Chern roots accordingly.

Remark 3.1
.

Recall that in the recursion relation (
1.3
) we used the classical (as opposed to quantum) product in
H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
. Let us now use the quantum product in
qH^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
instead to define,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}^{*}_{i+1}=c_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{k+1})\star_{k}\widetilde{\psi}^{*}_{i}-\widetilde{\psi}^{*}_{i}c_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{k})\star_{k-1}-y_{i}\circ\widetilde{\psi}_{i}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{i-1}=\widetilde{\psi}_{i}c_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{k})\star_{k}-c_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{k-1})\star_{k-1}\widetilde{\psi}_{i}-y_{i}\circ\widetilde{\psi}_{i}

in the rings

and

are related via a minus sign,
q_{k}=-q_{k-1}=(-1)^{k-1}z
, and we have therefore labelled the products to distinguish in which ring the multiplication is taken. We have the following result:
for
1\leq i\leq n
the action of the operators
\tilde{\psi}_{i}^{*},\tilde{\psi}_{i}:\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]\to\bigwedge^{k\pm 1}V[z^{\pm 1}]
coincides with the action of the classical ones
\psi^{*}_{i},\psi_{i}

\psi^{*}_{i}=\widetilde{\psi}_{i}^{*},\quad\psi_{n+1-i}=\widetilde{\psi}_{n+1-i}\;.

But for

\widetilde{\psi}^{*}_{i+n}=z\widetilde{\psi}^{*}_{i}=(-1)^{k}q\widetilde{\psi}_{i}^{*},\quad\widetilde{\psi}_{i-n}=z\widetilde{\psi}_{i}=(-1)^{k}q\widetilde{\psi}_{i}\;;

compare with (
3.16

\psi^{*}_{i},\psi_{i}
for the ‘quantum operators’ but it is now always tacitly understood that the relations (
3.16
) hold.

Recall from Lemma

that the matrices

are invertible if

\psi_{i}^{*}\mapsto t_{j}.\psi^{*}_{i}=\psi^{*}_{i+1}+(y_{i}-y_{j})\psi^{*}_{i}=\psi^{*}_{t_{j}.v_{i}},

and
\psi^{*}_{t_{j}.v_{i}}=(t_{j}.v_{i})\wedge

For

Remark 3.2

There is an alternative definition of the above product (

Proof.

We start by considering the branching rule (
A.5
) for factorial Schur polynomials (with respect to the last variable) which is a direct consequence of their tableaux representation:

s_{\lambda}(x|y)=\sum_{\mu}\prod\limits_{\square\in\lambda/\mu}(x_{k}-y_{k+c(\square)})s_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}|y),

\lambda/\mu

Given any

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(X^{q}|y)v\otimes v_{i}

3.5.
Fermions and the shuffle product

Let us mention yet another interpretation of the action of fermionic creation operators solely in terms of factorial Schur polynomials. To do that, we briefly recall the construction of cohomological Hall algebras due to Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
30
]
, for details, we refer to the original paper. Their construction works for any finite quiver
Q
, but here we are only interested in the simplest case
A_{1}
, namely the quiver
Q=\bullet
with one vertex and no arrows. To the quiver
Q
one associates the space
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\underline{k}}\mathcal{H}_{\underline{k}}
, where
\underline{k}
is a dimension vector of the quiver and
\mathcal{H}_{\underline{k}}
is the
\,\mathrm{GL}_{\underline{k}}
equivariant cohomology of the space of quiver representations of
Q
. In
[
30
]
the authors define a graded multiplication
m_{\underline{k}_{1},\underline{k}_{2}}:\mathcal{H}_{\underline{k}_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\underline{k}_{2}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\underline{k}_{1}+\underline{k}_{2}}
on the space
\mathcal{H}
, which they then explicitly calculate in terms of the
shuffle product
from
[
13
]
. In our case the dimension vector is simply a non-negative integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{k}=H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;\infty))\simeq R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}]^{S_{k}}
is the
T
-equivariant cohomology of the classifying space. The shuffle product is then given by

(3.26)

(f_{1}\ast f_{2})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k_{1}+k_{2}})=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|=k_{1}\\
|J|=k_{2}\\
I\cup J=[k_{1}+k_{2}]\end{subarray}}\frac{f_{1}(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k_{1}}})f_{2}(x_{j_{1}},\ldots,x_{j_{k_{2}}})}{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{k_{1}}\prod\limits_{r=1}^{k_{2}}(x_{j_{r}}-x_{i_{l}})},

where the sum is taken over all disjoint ordered subsets
I=\{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k_{1}}\}
and
J=\{j_{1}<\ldots<j_{k_{2}}\}
of
\{1,2,\ldots,k_{1}+k_{2}\}
.

Recall that the action of the fermionic creation operator produces from the (equivariant) Schubert class
\sigma_{w}\in qH_{T}^{*}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
labelled by the Grassmannian permutation
w\in S^{k,n}

by
\psi_{i}^{*}.w
. The following simple proposition explains the connection between the action of the fermionic creation operators and the shuffle product.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let

be the factorial Schur polynomial representing the corresponding equivariant Schubert class
\sigma_{w}
in the presentation (
2.1

(x_{1}|y)^{l-1}\ast s_{\lambda(w)}(x_{2},\ldots,x_{k+1}|y)=(-1)^{\#(\psi_{l}^{*}.w,w)}s_{\lambda(\psi^{*}_{l}.w)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1}|y)

if
l\notin\{w_{1},\ldots w_{k}\}
and
0
otherwise. In other words, computing the shuffle product of the factorial Schur polynomial with the factorial monomial
(x|y)^{l-1}
is the same as acting on the corresponding Grassmannian permutation by the fermionic creation operator.

Proof.

Let us compute the shuffle product

(3.28)

(x_{1}|y)^{l-1}\ast s_{\lambda(w)}(x_{2},\ldots,x_{k+1}|y)=\\
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k+1}\frac{(x_{i}|y)^{l-1}}{\prod\limits_{j\neq i}(x_{j}-x_{i})}s_{\lambda(w)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots x_{k+1}|y)=\\
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k+1}\frac{(-1)^{k+1-i}(x_{i}|y)^{l-1}}{\Delta(x)}a_{\lambda(w)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{k+1}|y),

where
\Delta(x)=\prod\limits_{i<j}^{k+1}(x_{i}-x_{j})
is the Vandermonde determinant and

a_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}|y)=\det((x_{i}|y)^{k+\lambda_{j}-j})

is the alternant in the definition of the factorial Schur polynomial; see Appendix
A
. On the other hand, the expression (
3.28

with respect to the row corresponding to the exponent

, which is zero if

because of two coinciding rows corresponding to the same exponent.
∎

4.2.
Equivariant Level-Rank Duality

It is well-known that Grassmannians of complementary dimensions are isomorphic
\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n)\simeq\,\mathrm{Gr}(n-k;n)
as algebraic varieties, the isomorphism can be easily understood in the following way: consider the complex vector space
V=\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a
k
-dimensional subspace
U\subset V
, consider the annihilator
{\rm Ann}(U)=\{\alpha\in V^{*}|\alpha(U)=0\}
of
U
. By basic linear algebra arguments it follows that
{\rm Ann}(U)
is an
n-k
dimensional subspace in
V^{*}
, thus we have
\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;V)\simeq\,\mathrm{Gr}(n-k,V^{*})
and this identification of algebraic varieties then implies a ring isomorphism
H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))\simeq H^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(n-k;n))
. Here we shall recall its quantum analogue which was obtained in
[
22
, Cor. 6.8.]
.

Define an involution on
R[q^{\pm 1}]
via
f\mapsto f^{\prime}
, where
f^{\prime}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n};q)=f(-y_{n},\cdots,-y_{1};q)
. We shall call an
R[q^{\pm 1}]
-module homomorphism
\varphi:M\to N

antilinear
if
\varphi(f\cdot m)=f^{\prime}\cdot\varphi(n)
for all
m\in M,n\in N
and
f\in R[q^{\pm 1}]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The anti-linear map
\Theta_{k}:qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))\to qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(n-k;n))
of
R[q^{\pm 1}]
-modules given by

(4.6)

f\cdot\sigma_{\lambda}\mapsto f^{\prime}\cdot\sigma_{\lambda^{\prime}},\quad\forall f\in R[q^{\pm 1}],

where
\lambda^{\prime}
denotes the partition conjugate to
\lambda
, is a ring isomorphism. In particular, we have the following equality for the equivariant Gromov-Witten invariants (
2.2
),

(4.7)

C_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu,d}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=C_{\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}^{\nu^{\prime},d}(-y_{n},\ldots,-y_{1})\;.

The proof of this result in
[
22
]
used the Bertram-Vafa-Intrilligator formula for the Gromov-Witten invariants and the Bethe ansatz. Here we wish to give an independent proof using the Clifford algebra and keeping this article self-contained.

A bijective proof of level-rank duality.

Let
u,w\in S^{k,n}
and define
u^{\prime},w^{\prime}\in S^{n-k,n}
to be the Grassmannian permutations corresponding to the conjugate partitions, i.e. they satisfy
\lambda^{\prime}(u)=\lambda(u^{\prime})
and
\lambda^{\prime}(w)=\lambda(w^{\prime})
. In terms of the Clifford algebra we have

(4.8)

\Theta_{k}\ket{w}=\Theta_{k}(v_{w_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{w_{k}})=\\
\Theta_{k}(\psi^{*}_{w_{1}}\cdots\psi^{*}_{w_{k}}.1)=\ket{w^{\prime}}=v_{n+1-w_{n}}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{n+1-w_{k+1}}=\\
(-1)^{k(n-k)+\ell(w)}\psi_{n+1-w_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n+1-w_{k}}v_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{n},

where we recall that
\ell(w)=|\lambda(w)|
. From (
4.8
) and the identities

(4.9)

\displaystyle|\psi_{i}^{*}.\lambda|=|\lambda|+i-k-1,\qquad|\psi_{i}.\lambda|=|\lambda|+k-i,

where by abuse of notation
\psi_{i}^{*}.\lambda
denotes the partition labelling the Schubert class
\psi^{*}_{i}(\sigma_{\lambda})
and, likewise,
\psi_{i}.\lambda
the one labelling
\psi_{i}(\sigma_{\lambda})
, we arrive at the commutation relations

(4.10)

\displaystyle\Theta_{k+1}\circ\psi^{*}_{i}=(-1)^{n-k-i}\psi_{n+1-i}\circ\Theta_{k},\qquad\Theta_{k-1}\circ\psi_{i}=(-1)^{n-k+1-i}\psi^{*}_{n+1-i}\circ\Theta_{k}\;.

4.3.
Graham positivity and quantum Pieri rules

In this section we use the Clifford algebra to prove that the structure constants
C^{\nu,d}_{\lambda\mu}(y)
of the equivariant quantum cohomology ring
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n))
satisfy
Graham positivity
, i.e. the
C^{\nu,d}_{\lambda\mu}(y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[y_{2}-y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}-y_{n-1}]
for
\lambda
being either a single column or a single row. These special cases are known as equivariant quantum Pieri rules. We note that the choice of torus weights
y_{i+1}-y_{i}
and, thus, the formulation of Graham positivity, depends on the conventions when fixing the Borel subgroup in the representation of
\,\mathrm{Gr}(k;n)=\,\mathrm{SL}_{n}/P
as homogeneous variety, where
P
is a maximal parabolic subgroup. Here we shall follow the conventions from
[
3
]
as we wish to compare directly with their combinatorial description of the quantum Pieri rules
[
3
, Thm 1.1]
and fix
B\subset\,\mathrm{SL}_{n}
to be the subgroup of
lower triangular
matrices, a choice which is already visible in the definition of the matrices (
2.4
) and (
3.1
).

We shall proceed as follows: we first prove Graham-positivity for products
\sigma_{(1)^{p}}\star\sigma_{\mu}
, where
(1)^{p}
is a single column of height
1\leq p\leq k
and
\mu
is arbitrary, by employing solely the fermionic product formula (
3.18
). We then explain the connection with the formula on cylindric shapes from
[
3
, Thm 1.1]
. To prove the other quantum Pieri formula for products of the form
\sigma_{p}\star\sigma_{\mu}
with
1\leq p\leq n-k
, we then employ the equivariant level-rank duality from
[
22
]
.

Corollary 4.6
.

(i) Let
\lambda=(1)^{p}
be single column of height
1\leq p\leq k
and
w=[w_{1}\ldots w_{n}]
a Grassmannian permutation with associated partition
\mu
. Then (
3.18
) simplifies to

\ket{(1)^{p}}\star\ket{\mu}=\sum_{\{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{p}\}\subset[k]}\psi^{*}_{w_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ(t_{i_{1}}.\psi^{*}_{w_{i_{1}}})\circ\cdots\circ(t_{i_{p}-p+1}.\psi^{*}_{i_{p}})\circ\cdots\circ\psi^{*}_{w_{k}}.1\,,

where the sum runs over all ordered
p
-subsets of
[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Since
i_{r}-r+1\leq w_{i_{r}}
and

t_{i_{r}-r+1}.\psi^{*}_{w_{i_{r}}}=\psi^{*}_{w_{i_{r}}+1}+(y_{w_{i_{r}}}-y_{i_{r}-r+1})\psi^{*}_{w_{i_{r}}},\qquad r=1,\ldots,p,

this formula is manifestly Graham positive.

(ii) Setting
\lambda=(p)
, a single row of length
1\leq p\leq n-k
, we apply the anti-linear ring isomorphism (
4.6
) to find that the expansion of
\ket{(p)}\star\ket{\mu}=\Theta_{n-k}(\ket{(1^{p})}\star\ket{\mu^{\prime}})
is also Graham positive.

Proof.

(i) We start by noting that since
\lambda=(1)^{p}
is a single column, the sum over the Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
in (
3.18
) becomes a sum over ordered subsets
\{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{p}\}\subset[k]
, where the
i_{r}
are the entries in the respective tableau. In particular, each horizontal strip
\rho_{j}=\lambda^{(j)}/\lambda^{(j-1)}
in (
3.18
) contains at most a single box and there are in total
p
of them.

To show Graham positivity recall that
1\leq w_{1}<w_{2}<\ldots<w_{k}\leq n
, since
w\in S_{n}
is Grassmannian. Hence, we must have that
i_{r}-r+1\leq w_{i_{r}}
as stated and, thus, the expansion of each term,

\psi^{*}_{w_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ(t_{i_{1}}.\psi^{*}_{w_{i_{1}}})\circ\cdots\circ(t_{i_{p}-p+1}.\psi^{*}_{i_{p}})\circ\cdots\circ\psi^{*}_{w_{k}}.1

gives a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-polynomial in the weights
(y_{w_{i_{r}}}-y_{i_{r}-r+1})
. Furthermore, due to the quasi-periodic boundary conditions we have that if
i_{p}=k
and
w_{k}=n
, then
t_{k-p+1}.\psi^{*}_{n}=(-1)^{k-1}q\psi^{*}_{1}+(y_{n}-y_{k-p+1})\psi^{*}_{n}
and, although a reordering of terms is required, the additional factor
(-1)^{k-1}
ensures positivity.

To show (ii) it suffices to note that if the expansion coefficients of the product
\ket{(1^{p})}\star\ket{\mu^{\prime}}
satisfy
f(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[y_{2}-y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}-y_{n-1}]
then their images
f^{\prime}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=f(-y_{n},\ldots,-y_{1})
must also be Graham positive by the anti-linearity property of the map (
4.6
).
∎

Appendix A
Factorial Schur Polynomials

Factorial Schur polynomials are a special case of double Schubert polynomials, which were originally introduced by Lascoux and Schützenberger. They are obtained by specialising the definition of double Schubert polynomials to Grassmannian permutations similar as Schur polynomials are a special case of single Schubert polynomials under the same specialisation. In the appendix we collect some basic known facts about factorial Schur polynomials and refer the reader to
[
37
]
for proofs and further details.

Let
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an infinite sequence of pairwise commuting variables (we assume
y_{j}=0
for
j>n
in the main body of the paper), then the factorial Schur polynomial is defined via the alternant formula similar to the usual Schur polynomials

(A.1)

s_{\lambda}(x|y)=\frac{\underset{n\times n}{\det}((x_{j}|y)^{\lambda_{i}+n-i})}{\Delta(x)},

where
\Delta(x)=\prod\limits_{i<j}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{j})
is the Vandermonde determinant and
(x|y)^{i}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{i}(x-y_{j})
is the factorial power. Alternatively, one can define factorial Schur polynomials combinatorially using semi-standard Young tableaux,

(A.2)

s_{\lambda}(x|y)=\sum\limits_{T(\lambda)}\prod\limits_{\square\in\lambda}(x_{T(\square)}-y_{T(\square)+c(\square)}),

where
c({\square})=j-i
is the content of the box
\square=(i,j)
. Setting all the parameters
y_{i}=0
recovers the usual Schur polynomial. The complete and elementary factorial symmetric polynomials are defined in the same manner as in the non-factorial case,

(A.3)

h_{k}(x|y)=s_{(k)}(x|y),\quad e_{k}(x|y)=s_{(1^{k})}(x|y).

The factorial versions of the Jacobi-Trudi and Nägelsbach-Kostka formulae are

(A.4)

s_{\lambda}(x|y)=\underset{n\times n}{\det}(h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}(x|\tau^{1-j}y)),\qquad s_{\lambda}(x|y)=\underset{n\times n}{\det}(e_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}-i+j}(x|\tau^{j-1}y)),

where
\tau(y_{i})=y_{i+1}
is a shift operator and
\lambda^{\prime}
is the conjugate partition.

Branching and reverse branching rules make it possible to express factorial Schur polynomials as a sum of factorial Schur polynomials dependent on fewer or more variables, respectively. First, we recall the well-known branching rule

(A.5)

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1},x_{k}|y)=\sum\limits_{\mu}\prod\limits_{\square\in\lambda/\mu}(x_{k}-y_{k+c(\square)})s_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}|y),

where the sum runs over all partitions
\mu\subseteq\lambda
such that
\lambda/\mu
is a horizontal strip. We also present the reverse branching rule
7
7

7

We were not able to find an exact reference for the reverse branching rule, thus we provide a proof, which uses only the above mentioned properties of the factorial Schur polynomials.

Proposition A.1
.

The factorial Schur polynomials satisfy the reverse branching rule

(A.6)

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}|y)=\sum\limits_{\mu}(-1)^{|\lambda/\mu|}\prod\limits_{\square\in\lambda/\mu}(x_{k}-y_{k+c(\square)})s_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1},x_{k}|y),

where the sum runs over all partitions
\mu\subseteq\lambda
such that
\lambda/\mu
is a vertical strip. Note that since factorial Schur polynomials are symmetric with respect to
x
variables, the branching rule (
A.5
) with respect to another

Proof.

We first prove the formula (
A.6
) for complete factorial symmetric polynomials. Indeed, to compute

we sum over the semistandard Young tableaux (SSYT) of shape

filled in with numbers at most

Now we use the Jacobi-Trudi formula to express the factorial Schur polynomials in terms of factorial complete symmetric polynomials

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}|y)=\det(h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}|\tau^{1-j}y)).

Notice that the factor

\lambda
and only one box deleted in the same row

\det(h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}|\tau^{1-j}y))=\sum\limits_{\lambda/\mu=(1^{r})}(-1)^{r}\prod\limits_{\square\in\lambda/\mu}(x_{k}-y_{k+c(\square)})s_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}|y),

which finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

The factorial Schur polynomials also satisfy a dual version of the Cauchy identity

where

is a generating function of factorial elementary symmetric polynomials (

) and the sum runs over all partitions
\lambda

rectangle with

is a partition obtained from

rectangle and transposing.

Appendix B
The local affine plactic algebra

We give an alternative combinatorial description of the Chern classes
c_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{k})
under the quantum Satake correspondence using the Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}_{n}(R)
. This description generalises the one of non-equivariant quantum cohomology from
[
48
]
in terms of the (affine) nil Temperley-Lieb algebra
\mathrm{nilTL}_{n}
. Namely, in the equivariant setting one obtains instead a (non-faithful) representation of the local affine plactic algebra considered in
[
32
]
(albeit over the ring
R
rather than the field
\mathbb{C}
).

Definition B.1
.

The local affine plactic algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is a free unital associative algebra generated by the set
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
modulo the relations

(B.1)

u_{i}u_{j}=u_{j}u_{i},\quad|i-j|\neq 1\;\;mod\;\;n,

(B.2)

u_{i}^{2}u_{i+1}=u_{i}u_{i+1}u_{i},\quad u_{i}u_{i+1}^{2}=u_{i+1}u_{i}u_{i+1},

where all the indices in the relation (
B.2
) are understood modulo
n
.

B.1.
Plactic polynomials

We recall the definition of a commutative subalgebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}\subset\mathcal{A}_{n}
in terms of plactic polynomials from
[
32
]
: for any monomial in the generators
u_{i}
we recall the notion of
clockwise
cyclic orientation (respectively
anticlockwise
cyclic orientation). Let
I
be a multiset, i.e. a set with possible repetitions, with elements
1\leq i\leq n
such that there exists at least one
1\leq j\leq n
with
j\not\in I
. Then we define a
clockwise ordered monomial

\prod\limits_{i\in I}^{\circlearrowright}u_{i}
to be a product of the
u_{i}
over the multiset
I
, such that
u_{i+1}
appears before
u_{i}
, where indices are understood modulo
n
.

Similarly, define the
anticlockwise ordered monomial

\prod\limits_{i\in I}^{\circlearrowleft}u_{i}
, such that
u_{i}
appears before
u_{i+1}
modulo
n
.

Next we define the plactic elementary and complete symmetric polynomials; compare with
[
32
]
.

Definition B.2
.

The affine plactic elementary and complete symmetric polynomials are defined by the cyclically ordered products

(B.3)

\boldsymbol{e}_{r}=\sum\limits_{|I|=r}\prod\limits_{i\in I}^{\circlearrowleft}u_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boldsymbol{h}_{r}=\sum\limits_{|I|=r}\prod\limits_{i\in I}^{\circlearrowright}u_{i},\quad 0<r<n,

where the first sum runs over all sets (without repetitions) of size
0<r<n
and the second one runs over all multisets of size
0<r<n
. For
r=0
we set
\boldsymbol{e}_{0}=\boldsymbol{h}_{0}=1
.

N.B. the definition (
B.3
) mimics the definition of the elementary and complete symmetric polynomials (modulo the plactic relations) in the case of commuting variables; see
[
39
]
.

Example B.1
.

Consider the case
n=3
, then we have

\boldsymbol{e}_{1}=\boldsymbol{h}_{1}=u_{1}+u_{2}+u_{3},\quad\boldsymbol{e}_{2}=u_{1}u_{2}+u_{2}u_{3}+u_{3}u_{1},\quad\boldsymbol{h}_{2}=u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}+u_{3}^{2}+u_{2}u_{1}+u_{3}u_{2}+u_{1}u_{3}\;.

We note that the quadratic terms
u_{i}^{2}
do not vanish in contrast to the polynomials defined in terms of the affine nil-Temperley-Lieb algebra for non-equivariant quantum cohomology; see
[
48
]
.

It was shown in
[
32
]
that the affine plactic elementary and complete symmetric polynomials commute.

Lemma B.2
.

The elements (
B.3
) generate an abelian subalgebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}\subset\mathcal{A}_{n}
, i.e.

\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\boldsymbol{e}_{i},\quad\boldsymbol{h}_{i}\boldsymbol{h}_{j}=\boldsymbol{h}_{j}\boldsymbol{h}_{i},\quad\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\boldsymbol{h}_{j}=\boldsymbol{h}_{j}\boldsymbol{e}_{i}

for all
0<i,j<n
.

B.2.
The Jordan-Schwinger map for fermions

Recall the Jordan-Schwinger map

(B.4)

\jmath:U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R))\to\operatorname{End}_{R}\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}V,\qquad x\mapsto\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\psi^{*}_{i}x_{ij}\psi_{j},\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R)\;.

This map is a Lie algebra morphism where the Lie bracket is the commutator. Moreover, if we consider the canonical action of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R))
on
V^{\otimes k}
and its projection onto
\bigwedge^{k}V
then we arrive at the commuting diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{End}_{R}V^{\otimes k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{End}_{R}\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}V}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{k-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\jmath}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{res}}

where
\Delta:U\to U\otimes_{R}U
is the familiar coproduct of the Hopf algebra
U=U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(R))
and
\operatorname{res}
is the restriction to
\bigwedge^{k}V\subset V^{\otimes k}
.

Proposition B.3
.

The map
\phi:\mathcal{A}_{n}\to\mathcal{C}_{n}\otimes_{R}R[z]
defined by

(B.5)

\displaystyle\phi(u_{i})=\psi^{*}_{i+1}\psi_{i}+y_{i}\psi^{*}_{i}\psi_{i},\quad 0<i<n,\qquad\phi(u_{n})=z\psi^{*}_{1}\psi_{n}+y_{n}\psi^{*}_{n}\psi_{n}\;,

is an algebra morphism. The representation of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is not faithful, but factors through the relations

u_{i}^{2}=y_{i}u_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad y_{i+1}\,u_{i}u_{i+1}u_{i}=y_{i}\,u_{i+1}u_{i}u_{i+1}\,,

where
i=1,\ldots,n
and indices are understood modulo
n
.

Remark B.1
.

The above representation of the affine plactic algebra was discussed in
[
22
, Corollary 3.25 and Remark 3.26]
albeit in the context of the affine nil Coxeter and the affine nil-Hecke algebra. Indeed, setting
y_{1}=\cdots=y_{n}=0
the above representation is a representation of the nil-Coxeter algebra which factors through the projection onto the nil Temperley-Lieb algebra. Setting
y_{1}=\cdots=y_{n}=1
instead, one obtains a representation of the affine nil-Hecke algebra.

Proof.

The proof consists of checking the relations of the plactic algebra using relations in the Clifford algebra. The relation (
B.1
) is obvious, because even number of fermions with distinct indices commute. Let us check one of the relations (
B.2
)

\displaystyle u_{i}^{2}u_{i+1}=(\psi^{*}_{i+2}+y_{i+1}\psi^{*}_{i+1})\psi_{i+1}(\psi^{*}_{i+2}+y_{i+1}\psi^{*}_{i+1})\psi_{i+1}(\psi^{*}_{i+3}+y_{i+2}\psi^{*}_{i+2})\psi_{i+2}

\displaystyle=-y_{i+1}(\psi^{*}_{i+2}+y_{i+1}\psi^{*}_{i+1})(\psi^{*}_{i+3}+y_{i+2}\psi^{*}_{i+2})\psi_{i+1}\psi_{i+2},\quad 0\leq i\leq n-3

and

\displaystyle u_{i}u_{i+1}u_{i}=(\psi^{*}_{i+2}+y_{i+1}\psi^{*}_{i+1})\psi_{i+1}(\psi^{*}_{i+3}+y_{i+2}\psi^{*}_{i+2})\psi_{i+2}(\psi^{*}_{i+2}+y_{i+1}\psi^{*}_{i+1})\psi_{i+1}

\displaystyle=y_{i+1}(\psi^{*}_{i+2}+y_{i+1}\psi^{*}_{i+1})(\psi^{*}_{i+3}+y_{i+2}\psi^{*}_{i+2})\psi_{i+2}\psi_{i+1},\quad 0\leq i\leq n-3,

which coincide due to the anticommutation relations. The relations including the parameter
q
are checked in the same way.
∎

B.3.
Chern classes in terms of the plactic algebra

We now identify the plactic elementary and complete symmetric polynomials (
B.3
) with multiplication operators by the Chern classes of the tautological and quotient bundles over Grassmannians.

Proposition B.4
.

Under the isomorphism from Proposition
3.3
the images
\phi(\boldsymbol{e}_{i}),\phi(\boldsymbol{h}_{j})\in\operatorname{End}_{R}\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z+(-1)^{k}q\rangle
of the plactic elementary and complete symmetric polynomials act by quantum multiplication with the Chern classes of the tautological and quotient bundle respectively,

(B.6)

\Sigma^{q}_{k}\circ\phi(\boldsymbol{e}_{i})=c_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{k})\circ\Sigma^{q}_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Sigma^{q}_{k}\circ\phi(\boldsymbol{h}_{j})=c_{j}(\mathcal{Q}_{k})\circ\Sigma^{q}_{k}\;,

where
1\leq i\leq k
,
1\leq j\leq n-k
and
0<k<n
.

If
k=0
or
k=n
then we define
\phi(\boldsymbol{e}_{i}),\phi(\boldsymbol{h}_{j})
through the identity (
B.6
).

Proof.

It suffices to show that

e_{r}(X_{1}^{q},\ldots,X^{q}_{k})=\phi(\boldsymbol{e}_{r})\qquad\text{and}\qquad h_{r}(X^{q}_{1},\ldots,X^{q}_{k})=\phi(\boldsymbol{h}_{r})\;.

Let us reinterpret the action of the elementary symmetric polynomials
e_{r}(X^{q})
in combinatorial terms. To do that, we first lift the action to
V^{\otimes k}[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z+(-1)^{k}q\rangle
and write the elementary symmetric polynomials explicitly

e_{r}(X^{q})=\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{r}\leq k}X^{q}_{i_{1}}\cdots X^{q}_{i_{r}}.

The action of each term on a basis vector
v_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{i_{k}}
is described as follows: we choose an
r
-subset of
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
and for each element in that set we simply multiply with
y_{i_{j}}
or swap
v_{i_{j}}
with
v_{i_{j}+1}
(unless it is already contained in the set
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
because then its projection onto
\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z+(-1)^{k}q\rangle
is zero). Summing up all the resulting vectors and projecting down to
\bigwedge^{k}V[z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z+(-1)^{k}q\rangle
, we obtain the result of the action of the elementary symmetric polynomial
e_{r}(X^{q})
.

Let us turn to the action of the plactic polynomial
\phi(\boldsymbol{e}_{r})
. Observe that due to the cyclic order in the definition (
B.3
) the combinatorial action of
\phi(\boldsymbol{e}_{r})
is exactly the same as
e_{r}(X^{q})
. Indeed, since the plactic elementary polynomials are ordered anti-clockwise and square-free, each basis vector
v_{i_{j}}
in
v_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{i_{k}}
is acted on by the same rule as described above, which proves the statement for the elementary symmetric polynomials. The proof for complete symmetric polynomials follows immediately from
[
32
, Def. 5.16, Cor. 6.9]
, where it was proved that plactic complete polynomials can be expressed in terms of plactic elementary polynomials by the Jacobi–Trudi formula, which finishes the proof.
∎

The following relates a general factorial Schur polynomial in the quantised Chern roots to the plactic polynomials:

Corollary B.5
.

Let
E(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{k}z^{r}\phi(\boldsymbol{e}_{r})
for
0<k<n
. Then we have the identity

(B.7)

E(z_{1})\cdots E(z_{n-k})=\sum_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}(X_{1}^{q},\ldots,X^{q}_{k}|y)s_{\lambda^{*}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-k}|-y)

where the sum runs over all partitions
\lambda
with Young diagram inside the
k\times(n-k)
rectangle and
\lambda^{*}=(\lambda^{\vee})^{\prime}
is obtained from
\lambda
by first taking the complement in the rectangle and then its transpose.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the known Cauchy type identity for factorial Schur polynomials, see e.g.
[
37
, Eqn 6.17]
.
∎

C.1.

Firstly, we recall the definition of the quantum

. Finally, the formula for
T_{22}(x)
follows from Theorem
C.1
.
∎

Appendix D
Another proof of Level-Rank Duality

We sketch an alternative proof of Theorem
4.1
using the quantum Chevalley formula.

Since our construction rests on the Satake correspondence let us first establish the duality for the case
k=1
, i.e.
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(1;n))\simeq qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(n-1;n))
or
(V,\star)\simeq(\bigwedge^{n-1}V,\star)
. Under the Satake map (
2.11
) the Schubert basis in
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(1;n))
corresponds to the standard basis in
V[q^{\pm 1}]=R^{n}[q^{\pm 1}]
,
v_{k}=\psi^{*}_{k}(1)\mapsto\sigma_{(k-1)}
, and the Schubert basis in
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(n-1;n))
to
v^{k}=(-1)^{k-1}\psi_{k}(v_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{n})\mapsto\sigma_{(1)^{n-k}}
with
k=1,\ldots,n
. We need to show that the map
\Theta_{1}:v_{k}\mapsto v^{n+1-k}
gives a ring isomorphism up to the stated transformation of the coefficients in
R
. Using the same argument from
[
42
, Cor. 7.1]
as previously, it suffices to show that the quantum Chevalley rule holds in both rings, because under the stated isomorphism
\Theta_{1}:\sigma_{(1)}\mapsto\sigma_{(1)}
. We have already done this for
(V,\star)
, so it suffices to compute

\Theta(v_{2})\star\Theta(v_{n+1-k})=v^{n-1}\star v^{k}=(X^{q}_{1}+\cdots+X^{q}_{n-1}-y_{1}\cdot 1-\cdots-y_{n-1}\cdot 1)v^{k}\\
=(X^{q}_{1}-y_{1}\cdot 1+\cdots+X^{q}_{n-1}-y_{n-1}\cdot 1)v_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{k-1}\wedge v_{k+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{n}

for all
k=1,\ldots,n
. One easily verifies that

(X^{q}_{i}-y_{i}\cdot 1)v^{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i<k-1\\
v^{k-1},&i=k-1\\
(y_{i+1}-y_{i})v^{k},&i>k-1\end{array}\right.\;,\qquad k=2,\ldots,n

and

(X^{q}_{i}-y_{i}\cdot 1)v^{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(y_{i+1}-y_{i})v^{1},&i<n-1\\
qv^{n}+(y_{n}-y_{n-1})v^{1},&i=n-1\end{array}\right.\;.

Hence, we arrive at

\displaystyle\Theta(v_{2})\star\Theta(v_{n+1-k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{n-1}\star v^{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v^{k-1}+(y_{n}-y_{k})v^{k},&k>1\\
qv^{n}+(y_{n}-y_{1})v^{1},&k=1\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta(v_{2}\star v_{n+1-k})

provided we impose the stated ‘anti-linearity’ property of the map which sends
f=(y_{n}-y_{k})\in R
to
f^{\prime}=(y_{n+1-k}-y_{1})\in R
. As the map
\Theta_{1}
is obviously degree preserving this establishes the ‘anti-linear’ ring isomorphism
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(1;n))\simeq qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(n-1;n))
or
(V,\star)\simeq(\bigwedge^{n-1}V,\star)
.

In light of the ‘dual’ quantum Chevalley rule (
D
) we can now repeat our previous construction step-by-step but starting from
qH^{*}_{T}(\,\mathrm{Gr}(n-1;n))\simeq\bigwedge^{n-1}V\simeq V^{*}
instead and use
Poincaré duality to describe the ring structure on
(\bigwedge^{n-k}V,\star)
. Namely, we have

\ket{\lambda}\star\ket{w}=\Theta(s_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\tilde{X}^{q}_{1},\ldots\tilde{X}^{q}_{k};y)v^{n+1-w_{n}}\wedge\ldots\wedge v^{n+1-w_{n-k+1}}),\qquad\tilde{X}^{q}=(X^{q})^{T}\;.

The last formula shows that under level-rank duality we obtain an equivalent description in terms of the transpose of the matrix (
3.3
), which corresponds to swapping the Borel subalgebra in our conventions when defining the quantum cohomology of Grassmannians.
