Technology Overview: Defining CHP
• Form of distributed generation (DG)
• Located at or near a building/facility
• Provides at least a portion of the electrical load
• Uses thermal energy for:
 • Space heating/cooling
 • Domestic hot water
 • Process heating/cooling
 Source: U.S. Department of Energy (DOE)
 https://www.energy.gov/cmei/ito/combined-heat-and-power-basics

Benefits of CHP Technologies
• CHP is more efficient than separate
  generation of electricity and heating/cooling
• Higher efficiency translates to lower
  operating costs (but requires capital
  investment)
• CHP can also increase energy reliability
  and enhance power quality if islanding
  capability is included in the installation (in
  the case of a grid disturbance or outage)
 Source: DOE CHP eCatalogue

CHP Site Qualifications Questions
 An answer of “yes” to these questions is a positive indicator for CHP:

1. Do you have significant thermal loads throughout the year (e.g., steam, hot water,
 chilled water, hot air)?

2. Does your facility have an existing central heating/cooling plant?

3. Have you implemented other energy efficiency measures (e.g., reduce heat loss)?

4. Do you expect to replace, upgrade, or retrofit central plant equipment within the next 3–5
 years?
5. Do you have access to on-site or nearby biomass resources? (e.g., landfill gas, farm
 manure, food processing waste)

Planning for Resilience with REopt
• REopt optimizes system sizing to meet site
 resilience goals during grid outages and
 provide electric bill savings during blue-
 sky operations
• REopt’s Energy Resilience Performance
 capability then calculates the probability that
 the system can support critical loads during
 outages of various durations, considering
 outage timing and associated conditions

 REopt can help answer questions such as:
 • What is the most cost-effective technology mix and sizing to sustain critical operations through a __-hour grid
 outage?
 • What is the probability ________ system can survive an outage of __ hours?

• Existing site conditions:
 • Electrical and gas service from a utility
 • Central heating and/or cooling plants
 • No major thermal distribution updates needed
• CHP system can operate in parallel with electric utility
 and centralized heating and/or cooling plants
• CHP can serve some, all, or none of the electric and
 heating loads
• No equipment redundancy requirements and factors of
 safety
• The year-long load profiles do not change year over
 year

Key Takeaways

 36

Key Takeaways for CHP Evaluation in REopt
 REopt can evaluate the cost-effectiveness of CHP at federal facilities and campuses
• Only a few inputs are required, but many inputs can be customized to perform a more
  detailed evaluation
• CHP can be cost-effective when electricity prices are high relative to natural gas prices
• CHP is a good technical fit if there is a central plant heating system with a large baseload
  heating demand that aligns with the electric load
• Absorption chiller can use CHP’s waste heat to provide cooling during warmer months
• CHP can be used to provide backup power during grid outages if configured with islanding
  capabilities
