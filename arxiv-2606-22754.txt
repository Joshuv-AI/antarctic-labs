Kreiss stability analysis of Hagstrom-Warburton nonreflecting boundary conditions for the first-order time-dependent Maxwell equations
Kaifang Liu, Matthias Schlottbom, J.J.W. van der Vegt, and Yan Xu

Abstract
We analyze the stability of the Hagstrom-Warburton nonreflecting boundary conditions (HW-NRBCs) for the first-order time-dependent Maxwell equations. The HW-NRBCs enjoy very small reflection coefficients and do not use high-order derivatives or nonlocal boundary operators, which makes them well-suited for high-order accurate numerical discretizations. The main result of this paper is an L 2 L^{2} a-priori bound on the solution in terms of initial and boundary data and volume sources in a half-space. To obtain this result, we first derive a mapping that takes outgoing components to ingoing components of the solution of the Maxwell equations with HW-NRBCs, which shows that the Kreiss condition does not hold uniformly. Next, to prove stability, several symmetrizers are constructed, which establishes well-posedness in a generalized sense.

Introduction

Scattering problems arise in many application fields, such as seismic wave simulations, acoustic and electromagnetic wave transmission, and are modeled mathematically as partial differential equations defined on an unbounded domain.
In practical computations, the unbounded domain is frequently truncated to a finite computational domain with an artificial boundary enclosing all sources and scatterers.
In order to minimize unphysical reflections at the boundary two popular approaches emerged, nonreflecting boundary conditions (NRBCs) and perfectly matched
layers (PMLs).

The perfectly matched layer
[
7
]
, originally introduced for the Maxwell equations, surrounds the computational domain with an absorbing layer that attenuates outgoing waves. Its effectiveness depends on the choice of absorption profile and layer thickness. After discretization, however, residual reflections from the outer boundary typically occur due to numerical dispersion and the finite PML thickness
[
46
]
, but see
[
8
,
35
]
for essentially reflectionless discrete PMLs on structured grids for the linear scalar wave equation. Although easily implemented within standard schemes such as the finite element method and finite difference time-domain method, the PML introduces a substantial number of additional degrees of freedom—particularly in three dimensions—and requires careful parameter tuning to minimize reflections
[
9
,
3
]
.

With NRBCs, the governing PDEs are solved on the truncated domain without modification, and the quality of the approximation depends on how effectively the NRBCs suppress spurious reflections at the artificial boundary.
A classical nonlocal NRBC formulation is the Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DtN) map
[
37
]
; see
[
18
,
22
,
23
,
28
]
for other types of nonlocal conditions. Although exact, such methods require solving a global problem and are therefore computationally expensive.
To reduce cost, local approximate NRBCs were introduced by Engquist and Majda
[
15
]
, with related formulations such as the Sommerfeld-type
[
17
]
and Bayliss–Turkel
[
5
]
conditions. These lower-order NRBCs are exact in one dimension but less accurate in higher dimensions, as they perfectly absorb only waves incident normal to the boundary. To keep reflections small, the artificial boundary must therefore be placed sufficiently far from scatterers and sources, which limits their practicality in numerical simulations.
Several locally defined NRBCs with high-order accuracy have since been developed.
Key contributions include those of Engquist and Majda
[
15
]
, Mur
[
45
]
, Collino
[
10
]
, Higdon
[
32
,
34
]
, Givoli and Neta
[
20
]
, and Hagstrom and Warburton
[
30
]
.
While the Engquist–Majda conditions become cumbersome at high order due to the presence of many derivatives, Collino
[
10
]
introduced local high-order boundary conditions using auxiliary variables, greatly simplifying their implementation. The Givoli–Neta
[
20
]
and Hagstrom–Warburton (HW)
[
30
]
formulations further advanced this approach.
Among these, the HW conditions are particularly attractive: they exhibit high-order decay of reflection coefficients compared with the Higdon
[
34
]
and Givoli–Neta
[
20
]
NRBCs, and they facilitate the treatment of corners and edges when multiple artificial boundaries are present. These advantages extend to general hyperbolic systems, including the elastic wave
[
33
]
, shallow water
[
21
]
, and Maxwell equations
[
30
]
. For recent developments on the HW-NRBCs, see
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
31
]
.

A central issue is the well-posedness of initial boundary value problems with NRBCs.
Energy estimates are commonly used to establish well-posedness for dissipative boundary conditions
[
40
]
but may fail in more general settings
[
40
, Section 7.4]
.
Energy estimate techniques have been used for the Higdon-NRBCs and the HW-NRBCs for the scalar wave equation
[
4
]
,
but a stability analysis for the time-dependent Maxwell equations with HW-NRBCs is not available.

An alternative approach to prove well-posedness relies on the uniform Kreiss condition – also known as the uniform Lopatinskii condition or the Kreiss-Sakamoto condition –
[
39
,
42
,
32
]
, which provides necessary and sufficient algebraic criteria for the strong well-posedness of hyperbolic systems with inhomogeneous boundary conditions.
In
[
39
]
, Kreiss established the theory for strictly hyperbolic systems with noncharacteristic boundaries, while Majda and Osher
[
42
]
extended it to characteristic boundaries, encompassing systems such as the time-dependent Maxwell and shallow water equations. Higdon
[
32
]
later offered a physical interpretation of this theory. For comprehensive treatments and recent developments, see
[
6
,
40
,
44
]
.
Failure of the uniform Kreiss condition leads to weaker notions of well-posedness, typically accompanied by a loss of derivatives.
Examples of such behavior arise, for instance, in
[
12
]
for Maxwell’s equations with (conservative) perfectly conducting boundary conditions, in
[
13
]
for general hyperbolic systems with conservative boundary conditions, and in
[
14
]
for constant-coefficient hyperbolic problems posed on half-spaces.
An approach based on symmetrizers for noncharacteristic boundaries is used in
[
11
, Section 2]
.

In this paper, we analyze the Hagstrom–Warburton nonreflecting boundary conditions for the first-order time-dependent Maxwell equations with general (non-smooth) material coefficients in the interior of the half-space
\Omega=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\,x_{1}<0\}
.
Our approach uses the energy method for the interior domain
[
6
]
where the coefficients can be non-smooth. The energy method requires the boundary conditions to be maximally dissipative, which is not the case for the HW-NRBCs. In the neighborhood of the boundary, where the coefficients are constant, we use therefore the Laplace–Fourier domain techniques developed in
[
39
,
42
]
.
A key element of the proof is the construction of eigensolutions of the interior Maxwell system via the bilateral Laplace–Fourier transform and converting the HW-NRBCs —originally time-dependent PDEs on the artificial boundary— into an algebraic system in the frequency domain.
Our analysis reveals that the uniform Kreiss condition, associated with this algebraic system, is not satisfied; consequently, one of the key assumptions in
[
42
]
fails, and their results on strong well-posedness with inhomogeneous boundary data do not apply. The failure of the uniform Kreiss condition arises from the presence of generalized eigenvalues.
For strictly hyperbolic systems with noncharacteristic boundaries, well-posedness can nevertheless be established for homogeneous boundary conditions, provided that the associated generalized eigenvalues satisfy certain additional structural properties
[
44
]
.
We show that the generalized eigenvalues corresponding to the HW–NRBCs for the Maxwell equations also satisfy the properties assumed in
[
44
]
.
In the case of inhomogeneous boundary data, well-posedness may still hold, though typically with a loss of derivatives
[
14
]
.
While
[
14
]
obtains a well-posedness result through the construction of explicit solutions, which, however, does not generalize to variable coefficients and cannot handle lower-order terms, we construct appropriate symmetrizers to obtain stability bounds also suitable for more general cases.
Particular attention is devoted to points on the imaginary axis where the Laplace–Fourier-transformed Maxwell matrix is non-diagonalizable or singular, significantly complicating the symmetrizer construction.
After the construction of a symmetrizer it is straightforward –using Gårding’s inequality– to obtain a bound on the solution in terms of the data, which proves the stability and well-posedness of the time-dependent Maxwell equations with HW-NRBCs.
As a byproduct of our analysis, we derive explicit reflection coefficients for the HW-NRBCs, extending analogous results for the scalar wave equation and the equations for acoustics
[
19
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. Section
2
introduces the Maxwell system together with the HW–NRBCs and states our main a priori estimate.
Sections
3
–
6
are devoted to the analysis of half-space problems. Section
3
constructs eigensolutions of the constant-coefficient Maxwell system and derives the associated reflection matrix, allowing us to express the HW–NRBCs as a mapping from outgoing to incoming modes in the Laplace–Fourier domain (Section
4
). This analysis also reveals the failure of the uniform Kreiss condition and shows that three distinct cases must be treated separately. In Section
5
, we construct symmetrizers adapted to these cases, which are then used in Section
6
to establish an a priori bound in half-spaces. In the appendices, we provide a detailed derivation of the HW-NRBCs, several technical lemmas, and Gårding inequalities used in the analysis.

Definition 3.2

\displaystyle\Lambda\left(\gamma_{j}^{+}\Psi_{L}\bm{c}_{j}+\gamma_{j}^{-}\Psi_{R}\bm{d}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\Lambda\left(\gamma_{j}^{-}\Psi_{L}\bm{c}_{j+1}+\gamma_{j}^{+}\Psi_{R}\bm{d}_{j+1}\right),

(3.19b)

for
1\leq j\leq J
.
Using that
\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}^{2}=|\bm{\omega}|^{4}I_{2}
and
|\bm{\omega}|^{2}=\kappa^{2}-s^{2}
, we see that

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}&(\kappa+s)^{2}I_{2}\\
-(\kappa+s)^{2}I_{2}&-\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\end{pmatrix}=16(s\kappa)^{2}(\kappa+s)^{4}\neq 0,

because
\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)\geq\mathrm{Re}{(}s)>0
by Lemma
C.1
.
The columns of
\Lambda\Psi_{L}
and
\Lambda\Psi_{R}
are thus linearly independent, and (
3.17
) is proven by comparing the coefficients in (
3.19
).
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Note that for
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}
we have
\gamma_{j}^{\pm}\neq 0
for
1\leq j\leq J
if
0<a_{j}<1
. This result follows from Lemma

, which states that
\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)\geq\mathrm{Re}{(}s)
. Hence,

\mathrm{Re}{(\gamma_{j}^{+})}=a_{j}\mathrm{Re}{(s)}+\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)\geq a_{j}\mathrm{Re}{(}s)+\mathrm{Re}{(}s)=\left(1+a_{j}\right)\mathrm{Re}{(}s)>0,

which gives
\gamma_{j}^{+}\neq 0

For

\mathrm{Re}{(\gamma_{j}^{-})}=a_{j}\mathrm{Re}{(}s)-\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)\leq a_{j}\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)-\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)=\left(a_{j}-1\right)\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)<0,

which gives
\gamma_{j}^{-}\neq 0

In addition, if

, then

if and only if

.
In that case, (

) shows that
\bm{c}_{j}=\bm{d}_{j+1}=\bm{0}
for
1\leq j\leq J
.

3.4
Reflection coefficients of HW-NRBCs

The interaction of the solution with the boundary is often described by the reflection coefficient, see
[
34
,
20
]
for example.
The reflection coefficient derived in the next result is instrumental in obtaining a Fourier-Laplace domain description of the HW-NRBC. Recall the definition of
\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}
given in (
3.7
).

Lemma 3.16

(Reflection coefficients)
.

Given the conditions stated Lemma
3.12
we have
\bm{c}_{0}=\mathrm{R}\bm{d}_{0}
with
\mathrm{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}
given by

\mathrm{R}=-\frac{\gamma_{J}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\quad\text{with}\quad\gamma_{J}=\frac{\gamma_{0}^{-}}{\gamma_{0}^{+}}\prod_{j=1}^{J}\left(\frac{\gamma_{j}^{-}}{\gamma_{j}^{+}}\right)^{2}.

(3.20)

Proof.

Lemma
3.12
shows that

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{J+1}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{L}\bm{c}_{J+1}+\Psi_{R}\bm{d}_{J+1}\quad\text{with}

\displaystyle\bm{c}_{J+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{0}^{+}}{s}\prod_{l=1}^{J}\frac{\gamma_{l}^{+}}{\gamma_{l}^{-}}\bm{c}_{0},\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{d}_{J+1}=\frac{\gamma_{0}^{-}}{s}\prod_{l=1}^{J}\frac{\gamma_{l}^{-}}{\gamma_{l}^{+}}\bm{d}_{0}.

The truncation condition (
3.14c
),
\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{J+1,-}=\bm{0}
, can be written as

\Pi_{-}\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{J+1}=\bm{0}
, with
\Pi_{-}=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,0,0,1,1)
.
Since

\Pi_{-}\Psi_{L}=\frac{1}{c_{\Psi}}(\kappa+s)^{2}I_{2}
and
\Pi_{-}\Psi_{R}=\frac{1}{c_{\Psi}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}
,
the truncation condition becomes after multiplication by
sc_{\Psi}

(\kappa+s)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{+}\prod_{l=1}^{J}\frac{\gamma_{l}^{+}}{\gamma_{l}^{-}}\bm{c}_{0}+\gamma_{0}^{-}\prod_{l=1}^{J}\frac{\gamma_{l}^{-}}{\gamma_{l}^{+}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\bm{d}_{0}=\bm{0}.

Hence
\bm{c}_{0}=\mathrm{R}\bm{d}_{0}
with
\mathrm{R}
as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

In view of Lemma
3.10
, we have for
s=-{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\omega_{f},\,\omega_{f}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle{\bm{\rho}}(\bm{x},t)=

\displaystyle A_{L}(\bm{x})\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}(\bm{k}_{L}\cdot\bm{x}-\omega_{f}t)}\Psi_{L}\bm{c}_{0}+A_{R}(\bm{x})\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}(\bm{k}_{R}\cdot\bm{x}-\omega_{f}t)}\Psi_{R}\bm{d}_{0},

with amplitude functions
A_{L}(\bm{x})=e^{\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)x_{1}}
,
A_{R}(\bm{x})=e^{-\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)x_{1}}
, and wave vectors
\bm{k}_{L}=(\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa),\omega_{2},\omega_{3})^{\top}
,
\bm{k}_{R}=(-\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa),\omega_{2},\omega_{3})^{\top}
.
By Lemma
C.1
, we have
\mathrm{Im}{(}s)\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa)\geq 0
, which shows that
{\bm{\rho}}(\bm{x},t)
is a left-moving wave if
\bm{d}_{0}=\bm{0}
, while
{\bm{\rho}}(\bm{x},t)
is a right-moving wave if
\bm{c}_{0}=\bm{0}
. Since
\kappa=\sqrt{|\bm{\omega}|^{2}-\omega_{f}^{2}}
, we observe that the contribution of
\Psi_{L}
is an evanescent plane wave if
|\bm{\omega}|^{2}>\omega_{f}^{2}
. If
|\bm{\omega}|^{2}<\omega_{f}^{2}
, then
\kappa
is purely imaginary and
{\bm{\rho}}(\bm{x},t)
is a propagating plane wave, and

\alpha=\kappa/s=\sqrt{1-|\bm{\omega}|^{2}/\omega_{f}^{2}}
is a real number. Lemma
3.16
then implies that the reflection coefficient of the HW-NRBCs for the Maxwell equations is

\mathtt{R}=\frac{1}{\omega_{f}^{2}(1+\alpha)^{2}}\frac{a_{0}-\alpha}{a_{0}+\alpha}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{J}\frac{a_{j}-\alpha}{a_{j}+\alpha}\right)^{2}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\quad\text{for }J\geq 0.

Since the cosine of the angle between the wave vector of the plane wave and the
x_{1}
-axis is
\alpha
, we see that the plane wave is annihilated at
\Gamma
if there is
j
such that
\alpha=a_{j}
.
Note, however, that for
\alpha=0
(waves parallel to the boundary
\Gamma
),
\mathtt{R}=\frac{1}{|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}
, which gives
|R|=1
. Waves nearly parallel to
\Gamma
require therefore larger values of
J
to obtain a small reflection coefficient
\mathtt{R}
.

4
On Kreiss’ condition for stability

As pointed out by Agmon, see
[
39
,
44
]
, a necessary condition for the well-posedness of the Maxwell equations with the HW-NRBCs on the half-space is that
solutions of the form

{\bm{\rho}}(\bm{x},t)=\mathrm{e}^{\kappa x_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\bm{\omega}\cdot\bar{\bm{x}}+st}\Psi_{L}\bm{c}_{0}

must vanish, i.e.,
\bm{c}_{0}=\bm{0}
.
As Lemma
3.16
shows, this necessary condition for well-posedness is satisfied.
A sufficient condition for (strong) well-posedness is the uniform Kreiss condition, see
[
39
,
42
]
.
To formulate the Kreiss condition in our context and to investigate its validity, we next derive the mapping that takes outgoing components to ingoing components of the solution of the Maxwell equations with HW-NRBCs.
This mapping is also instrumental in the following sections.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}
,
0<a_{0}\leq 1
and
0<a_{j}<1
,
j=1,\dots,J
.
Let
\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{j}
,
0\leq j\leq J+1
satisfy (
3.14
).
Then

\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{0,-}=\hat{S}_{\Gamma}\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{0,+}\quad\text{with}\quad\hat{S}_{\Gamma}=\frac{1}{\beta_{J}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\text{ and }\beta_{J}=\frac{(\kappa+s)^{2}-(\kappa-s)^{2}\gamma_{J}}{1-\gamma_{J}},

(4.1)

where
\gamma_{J}
is defined in (
3.20
), and
\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}
in (
3.7
).

Proof.

An application of Lemma
3.16
shows that

\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{0}=\Psi_{L}\mathrm{R}\bm{d}_{0}+\Psi_{R}\bm{d}_{0},

where
\mathrm{R}
is given in (
3.20
).
The above relation implies that

\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{0,+}=\left(\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\mathrm{R}+(\kappa+s)^{2}I_{2}\right)\bm{d}_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{0,-}=\left((\kappa+s)^{2}\mathrm{R}+\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\right)\bm{d}_{0},

which leads to

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{0,-}

\displaystyle=\left((\kappa+s)^{2}\mathrm{R}+\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\right)\left(\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\mathrm{R}+(\kappa+s)^{2}I_{2}\right)^{-1}\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{0,+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\gamma_{J}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}\left(1-\frac{(\kappa-s)^{2}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}\gamma_{J}\right)}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\hat{{\bm{\rho}}}_{0,+}.

Here, we have used that
\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}^{2}=|\bm{\omega}|^{4}I_{2}=(\kappa+s)^{2}(\kappa-s)^{2}I_{2}
.
Rearranging terms, we arrive at the claim.
∎

The uniform Kreiss condition requires that

\displaystyle\inf_{(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}}|\det\hat{N}_{J}(\bm{\omega},s)|>0,

where the matrix symbol
\hat{N}_{J}(\bm{\omega},s)
is defined by

\hat{N}_{J}(\bm{\omega},s)=\hat{S}_{\Gamma}\Psi_{L,+}-\Psi_{L,-}\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}.

The discussion preceding Lemma
4.1
shows that
|\det\hat{N}_{J}(\bm{\omega},s)|>0
for all
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}
.
In order to investigate the behaviour of
\hat{N}_{J}(\bm{\omega},s)
in the limiting case, it is convenient to introduce scaled variables,

\displaystyle s^{\prime}=\frac{s}{|\bm{\zeta}|},\quad\bm{\omega}^{\prime}=\frac{\bm{\omega}}{|\bm{\zeta}|},

(4.2)

where
\bm{\zeta}=(\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\eta,\xi)
and

|\bm{\zeta}|=\sqrt{\eta^{2}+\xi^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}
for
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}
.
The matrix symbol
\hat{N}_{J}
is homogeneous of degree zero,
that is
\hat{N}_{J}(\bm{\omega},s)=\hat{N}_{J}(\bm{\omega}^{\prime},s^{\prime})
.
The limiting cases, can thus be investigated by considering
\mathrm{Re}{(}s^{\prime})\to 0
. We provide the representation of
\hat{N}_{J}
in the next result.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}
we have that

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{J}(\bm{\omega},s)=-\frac{4\kappa s}{c_{\Psi}\left[1-\frac{(\kappa-s)^{2}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}\gamma_{J}\right]}I_{2}.

(4.3)

Proof.

Noticing

\Psi_{L,-}=\frac{(\kappa+s)^{2}}{c_{\Psi}}I_{2}
,

\Psi_{L,+}=\frac{1}{c_{\Psi}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}

and
\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}^{2}=|\bm{\omega}|^{4}I_{2}
, we obtain that

\hat{N}_{J}(\bm{\omega},s)=\frac{1}{c_{\Psi}}\left(\frac{|\bm{\omega}|^{4}(1-\gamma_{J})}{(\kappa+s)^{2}\left(1-\frac{(\kappa-s)^{2}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}\gamma_{J}\right)}-(\kappa+s)^{2}\right)I_{2}.

Using
|\bm{\omega}|^{2}=(\kappa-s)(\kappa+s)
proves the claim by a direct calculation.
∎

We note that no division by zero occurs in (
4.3
) for
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}
, because Lemma
C.3
ensures that

\left|\frac{(\kappa-s)^{2}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}\gamma_{J}\right|

is strictly smaller than one for

,
0\leq j\leq J
.
If
\mathrm{Re}{(}s^{\prime})\to 0
, then a division by zero may occur in (
4.3
).
The following asymptotic expansions of
\gamma_{J}
allow a more precise investigation.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
a_{0}=1,\,0<a_{j}<1
for
j=1,...,J
. The following asymptotics hold for

, with

\displaystyle\gamma_{J}

\displaystyle=1-c_{0}(\kappa/s)\frac{\kappa}{s}+O(\left|\frac{\kappa}{s}\right|^{2}),\quad

\displaystyle\text{with }c_{0}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{2(2-\delta_{0j})}{a_{j}+z},\quad

\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa-s}{\kappa+s}\gamma_{J}

\displaystyle=-1+c_{1}(\kappa/s)\frac{\kappa}{s}+O(\left|\frac{\kappa}{s}\right|^{2}),\quad

\displaystyle\text{with }c_{1}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{4}{a_{j}+z},\quad

\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle\frac{(\kappa-s)^{2}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}\gamma_{J}

\displaystyle=1-c_{2}(\kappa/s)\frac{\kappa}{s}+O(\left|\frac{\kappa}{s}\right|^{2}),\quad

\displaystyle\text{with }c_{2}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{2(2+\delta_{0j})}{a_{j}+z},\quad

\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Follows directly from

\frac{as-\kappa}{as+\kappa}=1-\frac{2}{a+\kappa/s}\kappa/s

and the definition of
\gamma_{J}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
a_{0}=1,\,0<a_{j}<1
for
j=1,...,J
. The following asymptotics hold for
|s/\kappa|\ll 1

\displaystyle\gamma_{J}

\displaystyle=-1+d_{0}(s/\kappa)\frac{s}{\kappa}+O(\left|\frac{s}{\kappa}\right|^{2}),\quad\text{with }d_{0}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{2(2-\delta_{0j})a_{j}}{a_{j}z+1},\

\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa-s}{\kappa+s}\gamma_{J}

\displaystyle=-1+d_{1}(s/\kappa)\frac{s}{\kappa}+O(\left|\frac{s}{\kappa}\right|^{2}),\quad\text{with }d_{1}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{4a_{j}}{a_{j}z+1},

\displaystyle\frac{(\kappa-s)^{2}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}\gamma_{J}

\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C}.

Remark 4.5
.

), which specifies the relation between the in- and out-going components of the solution to the Maxwell equations at the boundary.
Let us denote by
\hat{\bm{g}}
the Laplace-Fourier transform of the boundary data

in (

).
Since we assumed that
\hat{\bm{g}}_{0}=\hat{\bm{g}}_{+}=\bm{0}
, the considerations that led to Lemma

yield verbatim

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\rho}}_{0,-}=\hat{S}_{\Gamma}\hat{\bm{\rho}}_{0,+}-\hat{\bm{g}}_{-}.

(5.2)

Recall the definition of
\bm{\zeta}=(\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\eta,\xi)
in (
4.2
), where
\eta=\mathrm{Re}{(}s)
,

and
\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
.
For notational convenience, we partition a vector

\hat{\bm{y}}=[\hat{\bm{y}}_{+}^{\top},\hat{\bm{y}}_{-}^{\top}]=[\hat{\bm{y}}_{1}^{\top},\hat{\bm{y}}_{2}^{\top}]^{\top}

Definition 5.1

(Symmetrizer)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{U}
.
A matrix symbol
\hat{{R}}:\mathcal{U}\to(S_{1,0}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{4\times 4}
is called the symbol of a symmetrizer
{R}
for the matrix symbol
\hat{M}(\bm{\omega},s)\in(S_{1,0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{4\times 4}
on
\mathcal{V}
if there exists
\eta_{0}>0
such that the following properties hold:

(i)

\hat{{R}}
is homogeneous of degree zero and a uniformly bounded smooth function of
\bm{\zeta}
for
\eta\geq\eta_{0}
and
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{V}
. Moreover,
\hat{{R}}(\bm{\omega},s)
is Hermitian.

(ii)

There exist
\delta_{1}>0
and
C>0
such that
\hat{\bm{y}}^{\mathrm{H}}\hat{{R}}\hat{\bm{y}}\geq\delta_{1}|\hat{\bm{y}}|^{2}-C|\hat{\bm{g}}_{-}|^{2}
for all
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{V}
and

\hat{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{C}^{4}
,
\hat{\bm{g}}_{-}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
satisfying
\hat{\bm{y}}_{-}=\hat{S}_{\Gamma}\hat{\bm{y}}_{+}-\hat{\bm{g}}_{-}
;

(iii)

There exists
\delta_{2}>0
such that
\mathrm{Re}{(}(\hat{{R}}\hat{M})(\bm{\omega},s))\geq\delta_{2}\eta I_{4}
for all
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{V}
.

5.1
Symmetrizer symbol for
\hat{M}
on
\mathcal{U}_{1}

We employ the matrix symbol
\hat{P}^{-1}
arising from the spectral decomposition (
3.8
) and proceed according to the method described in
[
39
, Section 4]
. For a positive parameter
d\in\mathbb{R},\,d\geq 1
, whose value will be specified later, we define the symmetrizer symbol
\hat{{R}}_{1}
as follows:

\hat{{R}}_{1}=\hat{P}^{-{\mathrm{H}}}Q_{d}\hat{P}^{-1}\quad\text{with}\quad Q_{d}=\begin{pmatrix}-I_{2}&\\
&dI_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.3)

Since
\hat{{R}}_{1}
is zero-homogeneous we have
\hat{{R}}_{1}\in(S_{1,0}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{4\times 4}
.
It is important to determine the spectral properties of
\hat{P}^{-1}
, which we state next.
Recall the definition of
\mathcal{U}_{1}
with parameter
\nu>0
stated in (
5.1a
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Given
\hat{P}\in(S_{1,0}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{4\times 4}
, stated in (
3.10
).
For
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}
, the eigenvalues of
\hat{P}^{-\mathrm{H}}\hat{P}^{-1}
and the eigenvalues of
\hat{P}^{\mathrm{H}}\hat{P}
are the same, and they are given by
\hat{\lambda}_{1}=|s|/|\kappa|
and
\hat{\lambda}_{2}=|\kappa|/|s|
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\sqrt{2}}|\hat{\bm{y}}|\leq|\hat{P}^{-1}\hat{\bm{y}}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\nu}}|\hat{\bm{y}}|,\quad\text{for all }\hat{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{C}^{4}\text{ and }(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{1},

where
|\cdot|
denotes the spectral norm induced by the Euclidian norm.

Proof.

The matrix symbol
\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}
defined in (
3.7
),
\bm{\omega}\neq\bm{0}
, can be diagonalized as

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}=\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}\begin{pmatrix}|\bm{\omega}|^{2}&0\\
0&-|\bm{\omega}|^{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{2}&-\omega_{3}\\
\omega_{3}&\omega_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.4)

If
\bm{\omega}=\bm{0}
, we set
V_{0}=I_{2}
and the same decomposition holds.
The following decompositions hold

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}&(\kappa+s)^{2}I_{2}\\
(\kappa+s)^{2}I_{2}&\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}&\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}\\
-\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}&\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}&\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}\\
-\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}&\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}\end{pmatrix}\diag(-\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{-},-\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{+},\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{+},\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{-}),

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}&(\bar{\kappa}+\bar{s})^{2}I_{2}\\
(\bar{\kappa}+\bar{s})^{2}I_{2}&\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}&\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}\\
-\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}&\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}&\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}\\
-\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}&\hat{V}_{\bm{\omega}}\end{pmatrix}\diag(-\overline{\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{-}},-\overline{\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{+}},\overline{\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{+}},\overline{\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{-}}),

with
\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{+}=(\kappa+s)^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2}
and
\hat{\lambda}_{\bm{\omega}}^{-}=(\kappa+s)^{2}-|\bm{\omega}|^{2}
.
Therefore,
\hat{P}
and
\hat{P}^{\mathrm{H}}
share the same eigenvectors and the eigenvalues of
P^{\mathrm{H}}P
are given by

\displaystyle\frac{|(\kappa+s)^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2}|^{2}}{4|\kappa||s||\kappa+s|^{2}},\quad\frac{|(\kappa+s)^{2}-|\bm{\omega}|^{2}|^{2}}{4|\kappa||s||\kappa+s|^{2}},

each with algebraic and geometric multiplicities of two.
Since

\displaystyle(\kappa+s)^{2}=\kappa^{2}+2\kappa s+s^{2}=2s^{2}+2\kappa s+|\bm{\omega}|^{2},

we further obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}{(}(\kappa+s)^{2}-|\bm{\omega}|^{2})

\displaystyle=2\,\mathrm{Re}{(}s(\kappa+s)),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}{(}(\kappa+s)^{2}\pm|\bm{\omega}|^{2})

\displaystyle=2\,\mathrm{Im}{(}s(\kappa+s))=2\,\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa(\kappa+s)),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}{(}(\kappa+s)^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}{(}2s(\kappa+s)+2|\bm{\omega}|^{2})=2\,\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa(\kappa+s)),

where we used that
\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa^{2})=\mathrm{Im}{(}s^{2})
and
|\bm{\omega}|^{2}=(\kappa-s)(\kappa+s)
.
Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{|(\kappa+s)^{2}-|\bm{\omega}|^{2}|^{2}}{4|\kappa||s||\kappa+s|^{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{Re}{(}s(\kappa+s))^{2}+\mathrm{Im}{(}s(\kappa+s))^{2}}{|\kappa||s||\kappa+s|^{2}}=\frac{|s|}{|\kappa|},

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|(\kappa+s)^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2}|^{2}}{4|\kappa||s||\kappa+s|^{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa(\kappa+s))^{2}+\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa(\kappa+s))^{2}}{|\kappa||s||\kappa+s|^{2}}=\frac{|\kappa|}{|s|}.

Since the eigenvalues of
\hat{P}^{\mathrm{H}}\hat{P}
coincide with those of
\hat{P}\hat{P}^{\mathrm{H}}
, and the eigenvalues of
\hat{P}^{-\mathrm{H}}\hat{P}^{-1}
are the reciprocals of the eigenvalues of
\hat{P}\hat{P}^{\mathrm{H}}
, the assertion concerning the eigenvalues follows immediately.
Moreover, as
|\hat{P}^{-1}\widehat{\bm{y}}|^{2}=(\hat{P}^{-\mathrm{H}}\hat{P}^{-1}\widehat{\bm{y}},\,\widehat{\bm{y}})
, estimating
|\kappa/s|
and
|s/\kappa|
with the bounds
\nu/2\leq|s^{\prime}|,\,|\kappa^{\prime}|\leq 1
valid on
\mathcal{U}_{1}
by (
5.1a
) yields the desired result.
∎

on

Lemma 5.4
.

For any
\eta\geq\eta_{0}>0
the symmetrizer symbol
\hat{{R}}_{1}
defined in
(
5.3
) satisfies property
(ii)
on
\mathcal{U}_{1}
with
\delta_{1}=\nu/2
provided
d\geq 5
.

Proof.

Introduce
\hat{\bm{y}}=\hat{P}\hat{\bm{v}}
.
The boundary condition
\hat{\bm{y}}_{-}=\hat{S}_{\Gamma}\hat{\bm{y}}_{+}-\hat{\bm{g}}_{-}
then becomes in these variables

\displaystyle(\hat{P}_{21}-\hat{S}_{\Gamma}\hat{P}_{11})\hat{\bm{v}}_{1}=(\hat{S}_{\Gamma}\hat{P}_{12}-\hat{P}_{22})\hat{\bm{v}}_{2}-\hat{\bm{g}}_{-},

where
\hat{P}_{ij}
,
i,j=1,2
denote the corresponding blocks of
\hat{P}
.
Using
\hat{S}_{\Gamma}=\beta_{J}^{-1}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}
we compute

\hat{P}_{21}-\frac{1}{\beta_{J}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\hat{P}_{11}=\frac{\beta_{J}(\kappa+s)^{2}-|\bm{\omega}|^{4}}{2\beta_{J}(\kappa+s)\sqrt{s\kappa}}I_{2}.

Recalling the definition of
\beta_{J}
given in (
4.1
)
and that
|\bm{\omega}|^{2}=(\kappa+s)(\kappa-s)
, we compute

\displaystyle(\beta_{J}(\kappa+s)^{2}-|\bm{\omega}|^{4})^{-1}=\frac{1-\gamma_{J}}{4(\kappa+s)^{2}s\kappa},

and obtain

\displaystyle|{(\hat{P}_{21}-\frac{1}{\beta_{J}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\hat{P}_{11})^{-1}}|=\left|\frac{(1-\gamma_{J})\beta_{J}}{2(\kappa+s)\sqrt{s\kappa}}\right|=\left|\frac{(\kappa+s)(1-(\frac{\kappa-s}{\kappa+s})^{2}\gamma_{J})}{2\sqrt{s\kappa}}\right|,

The above term is bounded by some constant
C>0
on
\mathcal{U}_{1}
using Lemma
C.3
.
We next compute

\displaystyle(\hat{P}_{21}-\frac{1}{\beta_{J}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\hat{P}_{11})^{-1}(\frac{1}{\beta_{J}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}\hat{P}_{12}-\hat{P}_{22})=\frac{\beta_{J}-(\kappa+s)^{2}}{|\bm{\omega}|^{4}-(\kappa+s)^{2}\beta_{J}}\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}.

Similar as before, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{J}-(\kappa+s)^{2}}{|\bm{\omega}|^{4}-(\kappa+s)^{2}\beta_{J}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\gamma_{J})\beta_{J}-(1-\gamma_{J})(\kappa+s)^{2}}{(1-\gamma_{J})(\kappa+s)^{2}(\kappa-s)^{2}-(\kappa+s)^{2}\big((\kappa+s)^{2}-(\kappa-s)^{2}\gamma_{J}\big)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\kappa+s)^{2}-(\kappa-s)^{2}\gamma_{J}-(1-\gamma_{J})(\kappa+s)^{2}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}(\kappa-s)^{2}-(\kappa+s)^{2}(\kappa+s)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}\frac{\gamma_{J}\Big((\kappa+s)^{2}-(\kappa-s)^{2}\Big)}{(\kappa+s)^{2}-(\kappa-s)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{J}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}.

Because
|\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}|=|\bm{\omega}|^{2}=\kappa^{2}-s^{2}
,
we have that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\beta_{J}-(\kappa+s)^{2}}{|\bm{\omega}|^{4}-(\kappa+s)^{2}\beta_{J}}\right||\hat{M}_{\bm{\omega}}|=\left|\frac{\kappa-s}{\kappa+s}\gamma_{J}\right|\leq 1,

by Lemma
C.3
,
therefore, with
C>0
introduced above we have that

\displaystyle|\hat{\bm{v}}_{1}|\leq|\hat{\bm{v}}_{2}|+C|\hat{\bm{g}}_{-}|\quad\text{for all }(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{1}.

This allows us to verify property
(ii)
as follows,

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{y}}^{\mathrm{H}}\hat{{R}}_{1}\hat{\bm{y}}=\hat{\bm{v}}^{\mathrm{H}}\begin{pmatrix}-I_{2}&\\
&dI_{2}\end{pmatrix}\hat{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=-|\hat{\bm{v}}_{1}|^{2}+d|\hat{\bm{v}}_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\hat{\bm{v}}|^{2}+(d-1)|\hat{\bm{v}}_{2}|^{2}-2|\hat{\bm{v}}_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|\hat{\bm{v}}|^{2}+(d-5)|\hat{\bm{v}}_{2}|^{2}-4C^{2}|\hat{\bm{g}}_{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|\hat{\bm{v}}|^{2}-4C^{2}|\hat{\bm{g}}_{-}|^{2},

provided
d\geq 5
.
The claim follows from Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
\eta\geq\eta_{0}>0

defined in
(

on

A direct calculation using (

5.2
Symmetrizer symbol for
\hat{M}
on
\mathcal{U}_{2}

In view of Lemma
5.2
,
the matrix symbol
\hat{P}^{-1}
becomes singular if the scaled variable
\kappa^{\prime}
is close to

,
which occurs in a neighborhood of the points
(\bm{\omega}_{0}^{\prime},s_{0}^{\prime})

and
|\bm{\omega}_{0}^{\prime}|=1/\sqrt{2}

and

.
Therefore, another approach must be taken to transform the matrix symbol
\hat{M}(\bm{\omega},s)
into block form if
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{2}

We note that the points
(\bm{\omega}_{0},s_{0})
are called “special points” in
[
36
, Section 2.1]
.
Different from the procedure described in
[
39

(see also
[

Using the matrix decompositions as in
[
16

[
42

\hat{M}\in(S_{1,0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{4\times 4}

\displaystyle\hat{M}(\bm{\omega},s)=\hat{Q}_{\bm{\omega}}\hat{N}_{F}\hat{Q}_{\bm{\omega}}^{\mathrm{H}}

(5.5)

with

\hat{N}_{F}=\begin{pmatrix}&&&s\\
&&-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{s}&\\
&-s&&\\
\frac{\kappa^{2}}{s}&&&\\
\end{pmatrix},\ \hat{Q}_{\bm{\omega}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}|\bm{\omega}|}\begin{pmatrix}-\omega_{3}&-\omega_{2}&\omega_{2}&-\omega_{3}\\
\omega_{2}&-\omega_{3}&\omega_{3}&\omega_{2}\\
-\omega_{3}&-\omega_{2}&-\omega_{2}&\omega_{3}\\
\omega_{2}&-\omega_{3}&-\omega_{3}&-\omega_{2}\end{pmatrix},

\hat{Q}_{\bm{\omega}}

\displaystyle{B_{c}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1&0\\
0&1&0&-1\\
0&0&1&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad{B_{r}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\

we obtain the following block decomposition,

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{\bm{\omega}}^{-1}\hat{M}(\bm{\omega},s)\hat{T}_{\bm{\omega}}=I_{2}\otimes\hat{M}_{1},\qquad\hat{M}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&s\\
\kappa^{2}/s&0\end{pmatrix},

(5.7)

\otimes
denotes the usual Kronecker product of matrices.
We note that the matrix symbol
\hat{M}_{1}
is homogeneous of degree one, hence
\hat{M}_{1}\in(S_{1,0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{2\times 2}

\hat{T}_{\bm{\omega}}

The eigenvalues of
\hat{M}_{1}

Hence, an eigendecomposition of
\hat{M}_{1}

\hat{M}_{1}(\bm{\omega},\eta+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi)

\eta=0

Lemma 5.6
.

The matrix symbol
\hat{M}_{1}(\bm{\omega},s)\in(S_{1,0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{2\times 2}
has the following expansion

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{1}(\bm{\omega},\eta+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi)={\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi H-{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\frac{|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}{\xi}J^{\top}+\left(H+\frac{|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}{\xi^{2}}J^{\top}\right)\eta-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}^{k+1}\frac{|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}{\xi^{k+1}}\eta^{k}J^{\top}

for
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{2}
and
H=J+J^{\top}
.

Proof.

Because
\kappa^{2}=s^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{1}(\bm{\omega},s)=(\eta+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi)\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}+\frac{|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}{\eta+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

The result then follows from the Taylor expansion around
\eta=0
and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\eta^{k}}\frac{1}{\eta+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi}=(-1)^{k}\frac{k!}{(\eta+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi)^{k+1}}

and noting that
\eta^{k}/\xi^{k}=(\eta^{\prime}/\xi^{\prime})^{k}
is smaller than one for
\nu
sufficiently small by Lemma
C.4
. In fact, using Lemma
C.4
, there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}^{k+1}\frac{|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}{\xi^{k+1}}\eta^{k}\right|\leq\frac{|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}{\xi^{2}|\xi-\eta|}\eta^{2}=\frac{|\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}}{(\xi^{\prime})^{2}|\xi^{\prime}-\eta^{\prime}|}\eta^{\prime}\,\eta\leq C\nu\eta

for all

, where we used a geometric series in the first step.
∎

Next, we turn to the verification of property

Lemma 5.9

For

Denote by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1\\
&0&1\\
&1&0\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}.

\displaystyle(1-\gamma_{J})\beta_{J}=(\kappa+s)^{2}\left(1-\frac{(\kappa-s)^{2}}{(\kappa+s)^{2}}\gamma_{J}\right).

\gamma_{J}

{B}_{\Gamma}^{-}(\bm{n})\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 6}
.

Appendix C
Some technical Lemmas

The following results will be useful in the analysis.
The next lemma is a strengthened version of
[
38
, Lemma 2]
, which states that there is a constant
\delta>0
such that
\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)\geq\delta\mathrm{Re}{(}s)
for
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}
.
We show that
\delta=1
, which is sharp, as the choice
\bm{\omega}=\bm{0}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
shows.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}
.
Then
\kappa=\sqrt{s^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}
satisfies
\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)\geq\mathrm{Re}{(}s)
and
\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa)\mathrm{Im}{(}s)\geq 0
.

Proof.

The statement
\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa)\mathrm{Im}{(}s)\geq 0
is obvious, and we only show the first statement.
If
\mathrm{Im}{(}s)=0
, the result is clear.
Suppose
\mathrm{Im}{(}s)>0

and write
\kappa=|\kappa|\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\psi}

and
s=|s|\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\phi}
with
\psi,\phi\in(0,\pi/2)
.
Since
\kappa^{2}=s^{2}+\bm{\omega}^{2}
, we have
0\leq\psi\leq\phi
.

Case 1.
|\kappa|\geq|s|
.
Using the monotonicity of
\cos(t)
for
t\in[0,\pi/2]
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)=|\kappa|\cos(\psi)\geq|s|\cos(\phi)=\mathrm{Re}{(}s).

Case 2.
|\kappa|\leq|s|
.
Using the monotonicity of
\sin(t)
for
t\in[0,\pi/2]
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa)=|\kappa|\sin(\psi)\leq|s|\sin(\phi)=\mathrm{Im}{(}s).

Since
\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa^{2})=\mathrm{Im}{(}s^{2})
, we have
\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa)=\mathrm{Re}{(}s)\mathrm{Im}{(}s)
and thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)=\frac{\mathrm{Re}{(}s)\mathrm{Im}{(}s)}{\mathrm{Im}{(}\kappa)}\geq\mathrm{Re}{(}s).

The case
\mathrm{Im}{(}s)<0
can be treated similarly.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

There exists a continuous bijection
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
with
f(0)=1
and
f(1)=0
such that

\displaystyle|z-s|\leq f\left(\frac{2\mathrm{Re}{(}s\overline{z})}{|s|^{2}+|z|^{2}}\right)|z+s|,

for all nonzero
s,z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}{(}s\overline{z})\geq 0
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle|z+s|^{2}

\displaystyle=|z|^{2}+|s|^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}{(}s\overline{z}),

\displaystyle|z-s|^{2}

\displaystyle=|z|^{2}+|s|^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}{(}s\overline{z}).

By assumption
\mathrm{Re}{(}s\overline{z})\geq 0
, it therefore follows that
|z-s|\leq|z+s|
.
Suppose that
\mathrm{Re}{(}s\overline{z})>0
.
By squaring the previous identities and rearranging terms,
we see that
|z-s|\leq f|z+s|
for some
f\in(0,1)
if and only if

\displaystyle|z|^{2}+|s|^{2}\leq 2\frac{1+f^{2}}{1-f^{2}}\mathrm{Re}{(}s\overline{z}).

By choosing
f
as the solution to

\displaystyle\frac{1-f^{2}}{1+f^{2}}=2\frac{\mathrm{Re}{(}s\overline{z})}{|z|^{2}+|s|^{2}},

we deduce that the claimed inequality holds.
Note that
f
can be continuously extended with
f(0)=1
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma C.3
.

Let
0<a\leq 1
and
\bm{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}{(}s)>0
and
set
\kappa=\sqrt{s^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2}}
.
Assume that
|s|^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2}=1
.
Then it holds

\displaystyle\left|\frac{as-\kappa}{as+\kappa}\right|\leq f\left(\frac{2\mathrm{Re}{(}as\overline{\kappa})}{|as|^{2}+|\kappa|^{2}}\right)\leq f\left(\frac{2a}{1+a^{2}}(\mathrm{Re}{(}s))^{2}\right),

where
f
is the function from Lemma
C.2
.

Proof.

Using that
|s|\leq 1
and
|\kappa|\leq 1
as well as
\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)\geq\mathrm{Re}{(}s)
by Lemma
C.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{Re}{(}as\overline{\kappa})}{|as|^{2}+|\kappa|^{2}}\geq a\frac{\mathrm{Re}{(}s)\mathrm{Re}{(}\kappa)}{a^{2}+1}\geq\frac{a}{a^{2}+1}\mathrm{Re}{(}s)^{2},

and the result follows from Lemma
C.2
.
∎

Lemma C.4
.

For any
0<\nu<1/(2\sqrt{2})
the following inequalities hold,

\displaystyle|\kappa^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\text{for all }(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{3}.

(C.1)

\displaystyle|\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\text{for all }(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{2}\cup\mathcal{U}_{3}.

(C.2)

\displaystyle|\eta^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\nu^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for all }(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{2},

(C.3)

\displaystyle\left||\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 3\nu^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for all }(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{2},

(C.4)

Proof.

Case 1.

Suppose
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{3}
, i.e.,
|s^{\prime}|<\nu
.
From
|\bm{\zeta}^{\prime}|^{2}=1
, we deduce that
|\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}\geq 1-\nu^{2}
, which gives (
C.2
) for
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{3}
since
\nu<\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
.
Using
\kappa^{2}=|\bm{\omega}|^{2}+s^{2}
, the second triangle inequality implies that

\displaystyle|\kappa^{\prime}|^{2}=||\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}+(s^{\prime})^{2}|\geq|\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}-|s^{\prime}|^{2}\geq 1-2\nu^{2},

i.e., (
C.1
) holds, because
\nu<1/(2\sqrt{2})
.

Case 2.

Suppose
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{2}
, i.e.,
|\kappa^{\prime}|<\nu
.
Writing
s=\eta+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi
, we have
\kappa^{2}=|\bm{\omega}|^{2}+\eta^{2}-\xi^{2}+2{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\eta\xi
. Considering the real and imaginary parts of
\kappa
, we obtain the bounds

\displaystyle\nu^{2}>\big||\eta^{\prime}|^{2}+|\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\big|,\quad\nu^{2}>2|\xi^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}|.

(C.5)

From the first inequality in (
C.5
), we deduce that

\displaystyle 1=|\eta^{\prime}|^{2}+|\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}=|\eta^{\prime}|^{2}+|\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+2|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}<\nu^{2}+2|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},

(C.6)

i.e.,
(1-\nu^{2})/2<|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}
. From the second inequality in (
C.5
), it follows that
|\eta^{\prime}|^{2}<\nu^{2}/(1-\nu^{2})
.
Using that
\nu<1/(2\sqrt{2})
this shows that
|\eta^{\prime}|^{2}\leq 2\nu^{2}
, i.e., (
C.3
) holds.
The triangle inequality and the first inequality in (
C.5
) then show that (
C.4
) holds.
Finally, we prove the lower bound (
C.2
) for
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{2}
.
The second triangle inequality, (
C.4
) and the already shown bound
|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}>(1-\nu^{2})/2
imply that

\displaystyle|\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}=|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\geq|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-\left||\bm{\omega}^{\prime}|^{2}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\right|\geq\frac{1-7\nu^{2}}{2}\geq\frac{1}{16},

where we used
\nu<1/(2\sqrt{2})
in the last step, which gives (
C.2
) for
(\bm{\omega},s)\in\mathcal{U}_{2}

∎

Appendix D
Gårding’s inequalities

In this Appendix, we summarize several versions of Gårding’s inequality that are used in Section
6
to prove the stability of Maxwell equations with HW-NRBCs.
Special attention is given to symbols that contain a
1/|s|^{2}
singularity.

Recall the pseudo-differential operator
P\in(\OpsS_{1,0}^{m}(V\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{\ell\times\ell}
with
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
\ell,\,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

P(e^{-\eta t}\bm{v}(\bm{x},t))=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi t+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\bm{\omega}\cdot\bar{\bm{x}}}\hat{P}(\bm{x},t,\bm{\omega},\xi)\hat{\bm{v}}(x_{1},\bm{\omega},\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\quad\forall\bm{v}\in(C_{(0)}^{\infty}(\overline{V}))^{\ell},

where
\hat{\bm{v}}(x_{1},\bm{\omega},\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\xi t-{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}\bm{\omega}\cdot\bar{\bm{x}}-\eta t}\bm{v}(\bm{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}t
and
\bm{v}(\bm{x},t)=\bm{0}
for
(\bm{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{V}
. The space-time domain
V
is given by (
2.13
), see Section
2.1
, with
V=\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},
or a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with the segment property.

We first state an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma D.1

(
[
42
]
, Lemma 3.1)
.

The operator
({\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}D_{t}+\eta)^{-1}\in\OpsS_{1,0}^{-1}(\mathbb{R})
, defined by

({\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}D_{t}+\eta)^{-1}v(\cdot,t)=\int_{\gamma=0}^{t}e^{-\eta(t-\gamma)}v(\cdot,\gamma)\,\mathrm{d}\gamma,\quad\eta>0,

satisfies

\|({\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}D_{t}+\eta)^{-1}v\|_{0,V,\eta}\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\|v\|_{0,V,\eta}.

Recall
\langle\bm{v}\rangle=(1+|\bm{v}|^{2})^{1/2}
.
We have the following Gårding inequality for the pseudo-differential operator
P
.

Lemma D.2

(Gårding’s inequality)
.

Let
K
be a compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Given the pseudo-differential operator
P\in(\OpsS_{1,0}^{m}(K\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{\ell\times\ell}
with
m\in\mathbb{R},

\ell,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
P
has a Hermitian symbol
\hat{P}
and there exist constants
C_{0}>0
and
M\geq 0
such that the symbol
\hat{P}
satisfies

\hat{P}(\bm{x},t,\bm{\omega},\xi)\geq C_{0}\langle(\bm{\omega},\xi)\rangle^{m}I_{\ell}\quad\text{for all }|(\bm{\omega},\xi)|\geq M,\ (\bm{x},t)\in K,

with
I_{\ell}
the
\ell\times\ell
identity matrix.
Then there exist constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}
such that for all
{q}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathrm{Re}{(}P(e^{-\eta t}\bm{v}),e^{-\eta t}\bm{v})_{K}\geq C_{1}\|\bm{v}\|_{m/2,K,\eta}^{2}-C_{2}\|\bm{v}\|_{m/2-{q},K,\eta}^{2},\quad\forall\bm{v}\in(C_{(0)}^{\infty}(K))^{\ell},

(D.1)

with norm
\|\bm{v}\|_{m,K,\eta}^{2}=\|v_{1}\|_{m,K,\eta}^{2}+...+\|v_{\ell}\|_{m,K,\eta}^{2}
.

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward extension of the proof in
[
47
]
, Page 44, to Hermitian matrices of pseudo-differential operators using the
\|\cdot\|_{m,K,\eta}
norm on the space-time domain
K
.
∎

For
\eta
large enough, we can remove the second term on the right-hand side of (
D.1
). For
C_{2}\leq 0
this is trivial, therefore, we consider
C_{2}>0
.
Using the pseudo-differential operator
\Lambda_{\eta}^{m}\in(\OpsS_{1,0}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{\ell\times\ell}
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with symbol

\hat{\lambda}_{\eta}^{m}=(1+\eta^{2}+\xi^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2})^{m/2}I_{\ell},

we have the following relation

\|\bm{v}\|_{m,\eta}=\|\Lambda_{\eta}^{m}\bm{v}\|_{0}.

(D.2)

Using (
D.2
) and the approach outlined in
[
6
]
, Appendix C.2, we obtain

Lemma D.3
.

Given the open domain
V=\Sigma\times(0,\infty)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with boundary satisfying the segment condition and
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Under the conditions of Lemma
D.2
we have for all
\bm{v}\in(H_{\eta}^{m}(V))^{\ell},\,m\in\mathbb{R},\,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\eta\geq\eta_{0}=\sqrt{\max(0,4C_{2}^{2}/C_{1}^{2}-1)}
the Gårding inequality

\mathrm{Re}{(}P(e^{-\eta t}\bm{v}),e^{-\eta t}\bm{v})_{V}\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\|\bm{v}\|_{m/2,V,\eta}^{2},

(D.3)

where
C_{1},\,C_{2}
are the constants in (
D.1
).

Proof.

Using (
D.2
),
Gårding’s inequality (
D.3
) follows from (
D.1
) if we can prove that there exists
\alpha>0
such that for
\eta
sufficiently large the following inequality holds

C_{1}\hat{\lambda}_{\eta}^{m}-C_{2}\hat{\lambda}_{\eta}^{m-2{q}}\geq\alpha\hat{\lambda}_{\eta}^{m}.

(D.4)

Condition (
D.4
) is equivalent to

(C_{1}-\alpha)(1+\eta^{2}+\xi^{2}+|\bm{\omega}|^{2})^{{q}}\geq C_{2}\quad\text{for all}\ \xi\in\mathbb{R},\,\bm{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\,{q}\in\mathbb{R}.

Choosing
{q}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}C_{1}
gives the condition
\eta\geq\sqrt{\max(0,4C_{2}^{2}/C_{1}^{2}-1)}
and we obtain on the compact set
K\subset\overline{V}
the Gårding inequality

\mathrm{Re}{(}P(e^{-\eta t}\bm{v}),e^{-\eta t}\bm{v})_{K}\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\|\bm{v}\|_{m/2,K,\eta}^{2},\quad\forall\bm{v}\in(C_{(0)}^{\infty}(K))^{\ell}.

(D.5)

Next, we cover
\overline{V}
with a countable collection of compact sets
K_{j}
such that
\cup_{j}K_{j}^{\circ}=V
, with
K_{j}^{\circ}
being the open non-empty interior of

, see e.g.
[
24
]
Lemma 2.2. Using a partition of unity subordinate to
K_{j},
we then obtain that (
D.5
) applies to any
\bm{v}\in C_{(0)}^{\infty}(\overline{V})
. The claim then follows from the fact that
C_{(0)}^{\infty}(\overline{V})
is dense in
H_{\eta}^{1}(V)
if
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\,\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},
or a domain
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with boundary satisfying the segment condition,
[
24
]
Theorem 4.10, or
[
48
]
Theorem 3.6.
∎

For the stability analysis using the symmetrizer symbol

, which has a
