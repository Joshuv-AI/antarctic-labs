6. Introduction: The System Physics Advanced Reactor Critical (SPARC) facility is in design to be used as a zero-
 power criticality reactor at Idaho National Laboratory. It is a split table reactor assembly that will be used for
 testing fuel forms for support of reactor licensing. The purpose for this work package it to evaluate ceramic
 fuel forms that can be used for the fuel assembly.
7. Project Description:
 This work package evaluated several ceramic fuel forms for use in the SPARC facility’s experimental fuel
 assembly. Other candidate fuel forms, such as uranium-molybdenum alloys and tri-isotropic (TRISO) particle
 coated fuels are also being considered and are the topics of other work packages (FT-26IN08060101, FT-
 26OR08060106). The current effort focused on the use of high assay low enriched uranium (HALEU) dioxide
 (UO2), with various geometries being discussed initially. Discussions with team members at Idaho National
 Laboratory (INL) identified plate or brick fuel geometries highly desired because they provide greater uranium
 density at the fuel assembly faces relative to other geometries, such as cylindrical pellets. If successfully
 developed, UO2 fuel bricks would enable the SPARC facility to support the microreactor developers by
 generating experimental data for fuel, core design, licensing, and qualification efforts. This capability is
 essential to accelerating the deployment of the next generation fleet of reactor for the United States.
8. Accomplishments:
 The UO2 blocks originally had specifications to have geometry with 3”x2”x1” and to be at minimum 95 % of
 the theoretical density. To accomplish this unique shape, two routes have been taken. The first uses
 conventional pressing of green body pellets and a second uses a polymer injection molding (PIM) process.
 Conventional Pressing and Sintering
 Using the conventional pressing route, a rectangular shaped punch and die needed to be designed and
 purchased. Using ASME’s pressure vessel design equations, it was determined that the presses available at
 the Fuels Research Laboratory (FRL) at LANL cannot press this geometry and size at the loads required for high
 green body densities. The punch and dies then needed to be designed at a smaller scale. The final x,y
 dimensions were determined to be one half of the specifications (1.5”x1”). Using this information, engineers
 at INL designed a punch and die set for pressing novel rectangular fuel pellets from uranium dioxide powder,
 as opposed to traditional cylindrically shaped fuel pellets. The tooling set was fabricated by Thompson Tool &
 Die LLC. The final drawings are shown in Figure 1, and have been submitted for purchase. The delivery date
 for the accompanying milestone for this work package is after the first quarter of the FY27 due to delayed
 start times for the work and long lead times for the punch and die fabrication.

 1

 While awaiting the delivery of the rectangular punch and dies, on going works has been carried out to
 determine the lowest possible pressing loads that can be used to achieve high densities on cylindrical pellets.
 The amount of binder, ethylene bis-steramide (EBS), has been varying, as well as the pressing load, pressing
 time, and powder processing. The powder processing variations are shown in Table 1. Densities of > 95%
 were achieved after sintering when using 0.25 wt.% binder and milling the powder using a high energy ball
 mill for 45 minutes. A pressing load of 50 Mpa was applied and held for 3 minutes. Upon arrival of the
 rectangular punch and dies, these powder processing and pressing parameters will be used.
 Polymer Injection Molding (PIM)
 The second route, PIM, is a well-established industrial process used to fabricate ceramic, polymer, or metal
 components into complex geometries [Ashby, 2012]. It has been recently modified to fabricate dense UO2
 and UN cylindrical pellets (> 90 %TD) with a high success rate. Aluminum molds have been designed at one-
 third scale of the desired geometry and include variations of the design. The initial mold design includes six-1”
 by 2/3” rectangular mold to be machined from a single blank mold with corner radiuses ranging from 0 to
 0.04”. Two injection aperture sites are considered including a straight 0.12”-diameter opening and a 0.07”-
 diameter opening that transitions to a tapered opening with a 56-degree draft angle. 0.04” by 0.002” channels
 were created along all corners and vertices of the mold to allow for proper venting of trapped gasses within
 each tool cavity. The drawings of the mold are shown in Figure 3. At present the molds have been designed
 and sent to be machined, and they are expected to return in early-October 2026. With the milestone delivery
 date in FY27, the PIM processing work will be carried out in October and November. The polymer matrix will
 consist of 5.4, 26.9 and 2.7 vol.% of low-density polyethylene, paraffin wax, and stearic acid, respectively. This
 distribution of polymer constituents were determined from a UO2 study recently, and yielded a sintered
 density of 93.22 %TD.
 A comparison of the UO2 blocks formed by either conventional sintering or PIM will include an inspection of
 the density, formation of cracks near the corners if any, and structural integrity. This will be documented in
 the milestone report M3FT-26LA080601052.
