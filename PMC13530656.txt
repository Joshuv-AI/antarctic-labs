Poster 147. Pre-Operative Patient-Reported Outcomes Hold Predictive Value on Time to Return to Sport in NCAA Division I Collegiate Athletes after Hip Arthroscopy for Femoroacetabular Impingement
Authors: Elizabeth R Collinson, Andrea M Spiker, Elizabeth Zordani, Matthew L Kuik, Keith A Knurr, Haley E Smith
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:High-level athletes treated with hip arthroscopy (HA) for femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) demonstrate high rates of return to sport (RTS) and consistent improvements in patient-reported outcomes (PROs) post-operatively. However, the timing of RTS varies widely across studies. PROs collected pre-HA and early post-HA may have prognostic value to provide surgeons with estimates for the time needed to successfully RTS. This could enable surgeons to better guide RTS decision-making, and at the elite athlete level, provide an improved estimate of athlete availability and healthcare utilization to coaches and administrative staff. At the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) Division I level, accurate RTS prediction can influence Name, Image, and Likeness (NIL) opportunities, career progression, and team success during a limited window of eligibility. We hypothesize that higher/better PRO scores pre-HA and 3 months post-HA would be associated with quicker RTS in this population. This study aimed to determine if pre-HA or 3-month post-HA PROs can predict time to RTS in NCAA Division I collegiate athletes undergoing HA for FAI.Methods:This study was an IRB approved review of routinely collected healthcare and PRO data from a single institution of NCAA Division I collegiate athletes who underwent HA for FAI between January 2017 and July 2024. Athletes were excluded if they did not complete either the pre-HA or 3-month post-HA PROs, had no intention of returning to sport, or had undergone a lower extremity surgical procedure within 6 months prior to HA. All athletes completed rehabilitation at the same facility, following standardized post-operative rehabilitation protocols. PROs included the Modified Harris Hip score (mHHS), International Hip Outcome Tool - 12 Item Version (iHOT-12), Hip Outcome Score (HOS) with Activities of Daily Living (ADL) and Sports Specific (SS) subscales, Copenhagen Hip and Groin Outcome Score (HAGOS) with Symptoms, Pain, Activities of Daily Living (ADL), Sport, Physical Activity, and Quality of Life (QOL) subscales, Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) with Mobility, Pain Interference, Anxiety, Depression, Global Physical Health, and Global Mental Health subscales, Veterans RAND 12 Item Health Survey (VR-12) with Physical Component Summary (PCS) and Mental Component Summary (MCS) subscales, and Tampa Scale for Kinesiophobia (TSK-11). RTS date was explicitly documented as the date of clearance from a member of the medical team for return to full, unrestricted activity. Time-to-event Cox analyses examined the relationship between time to RTS and PRO scores at pre-HA and 3-month post-HA, separately. Hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) are reported. The level of significance was set at α <= 0.05.Results:Fifty-four NCAA Division I collegiate athletes from football (15), crew (10), soccer (8), track (5), wrestling (5), volleyball (3), swim (2), cross country (2), hockey (2), basketball (1), and tennis (1) were identified for inclusion (24 female, 24 bilateral, age 21.0±1.3 years, BMI 25.6 ± 4.7 kg/m2). Of those, 47 (20 female) had an explicitly documented RTS date with a median time to RTS of 4.35 [Interquartile Range (IQR), 3.57-5.39] months. Eight athletes without an explicit RTS date were right-censored during analysis. Mean pre-HA and 3-month post-HA PRO scores are reported in Table 1. Among pre-HA PROs, higher/better HAGOS QOL and HAGOS ADL subscale scores were associated with a quicker RTS (QOL: HR=1.04, 95% CI: 1.02-1.07, n=26, p<0.01, ADL: HR=1.03, 95% CI: 1.00-1.04, n=26, p=0.02). No other PROs pre-HA or post-HA were associated with time to RTS (all p>0.08). Forest plots describing pre-HA and 3 months post-HA PROs association with time to RTS are shown in Figure 1. Kaplan-Meier curves displaying time to return to sport stratified by median, 25th percentile, and 75th percentile groups of pre-HA HAGOS QOL and HAGOS ADL subscale scores are presented in Figure 2 and 3, respectively.Conclusions:Higher pre-HA HAGOS ADL and HAGOS QOL scores were predictors of quicker RTS in high-level Division I athletes undergoing HA for FAI. These findings support our hypothesis and suggest that HAGOS subscales may provide greater sensitivity in this high-level collegiate athlete cohort rather than traditional hip outcome measures, and that athletes with better baseline daily function and well-being may be positioned for more efficient recovery and quicker RTS. Notably, there was no association of PROs assessing sport-specific function, physical activity, or pain with time to RTS, contrary to our hypothesis, suggesting these instruments may not capture factors most relevant to RTS in this cohort. Although no 3-month post-HA PROs were significantly associated with RTS timing, several instruments had lower 3-month completion rates, limiting data interpretation. Overall, when advising high-level NCAA Division I athletes on expectations for their RTS timeline after HA for FAI, pre-operative PROs assessing ADL and/or QOL may contribute to more individualized guidance when interpreted alongside physical examination and patient history.
