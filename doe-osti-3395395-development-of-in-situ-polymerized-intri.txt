 ABSTRACT

Electrically conductive composites are emerging across diverse industries such as electronic,
automotive, aerospace, advanced air mobility, biomedical, infrastructure, defense and security
offering static charge dissipation, electromagnetic interference shielding, lighting protection,
sensing, dicing, corrosion monitoring, etc. Conductivity enhanced composites provide several
advantages compared to conventional metals including weight reduction, corrosion resistance,
energy efficient processability, tunable properties and multifunctionality. Polymers are typically
insulating in nature and require conducting filler for electron transport. However, dispersion and
polymer-filler interphases are critical and often disrupt conducting pathways. Besides, conductive
fillers such as graphene, carbon nanotube, MXene, silver nanowire, etc. are expensive, limiting
their wide adoption in composite industry. On the other hand, a limited number of intrinsically
conductive polymers are available among which polyaniline (PANI) has been widely studied due
to its high conductivity, thermal and chemical stability. However, PANI is difficult to process and
exhibits weak mechanical properties. In brief, there is a significant demand for electrically
conductive polymer formulation with cost-effective conducting fillers that offer processability in
scale to expand the market of conductivity enhanced materials.
 We are herein proposing a unique approach to develop electrically conductive formulation,
via in-situ polymerization of ELIUM initiated by polyaniline (PANI) enabling versatile processing
routes including coating, prepreg, and molding for composite manufacturing. To further enhance
electronic network formation, low-cost carbon allotrope developed by Minus 100 was explored as
a cost-effective alternative to conventional conducting fillers. In phase 1, conductive formulation
was developed with thermoplastic ELIUM resin by varying the concentrations of PANI and low
cost carbonaceous material, trade name CCM. ELIUM resin comprises liquid prepolymer of
methyl methacrylate which in-situ converts into thermoplastic polymethyl methacrylate (known
as PMMA). Low viscosity of ELIUM allows homogeneous dispersion of PANI and CCM, as
compared to viscous PMMA melt, resulting in a drastically high electrical conductivity. The
weight ratios of ELIUM, PANI and CCM were varied to measure the effect of individual
components. Increasing PANI loading from 30 wt.% to 50 wt.%, conductivity of the formulation
enhanced from 1.3 to 5 S.cm-1, which is closure to the metallic range. Incorporation of CCM
enhanced the electrical conductivity particularly at lower PANI loading, e.g. addition of 3 wt.%
concentration of CCM into 70:30 ELIUM/PANI (wt./wt.) increased the conductivity to 3.65 S.cm-1
showing 185% improvement. On the other hand, the blends with higher PANI concentration (50
wt.%), the effect of CCM was less significant. ELIUM/PANI blends showed higher storage
modulus at elevated temperature as compared to pristine ELIUM resin, (e.g. 70:30 ELIUM/PANI
showed 200% enhancement at 100 °C). The modulus further enhanced by the addition of CCM,
showcasing 300 % increase at 3 wt.% CCM concentration. The enhancement in thermal stability
prevented the drop in storage modulus at higher temperature offering high temperature
applications. The uncured resin was diluted with acetone to apply the coating on the glass/fiber
epoxy substrate. 50 MPa adhesive strength of the coating was noted as measured by the Dolly Test,
which is sufficient for wide range of applications.

 1. OBJECTIVES

The goal of this project is to propose a scalable and cost-effective solution for conductivity
enhanced materials to be used in EMI shieling, strain sensing and lightning strike protection
applications.
  Develop formulation with ELIUM, PANI and low-cost carbon allotropes from Minus 100
 to reach conductivity of 5 S.cm-1, and EMI shielding value of 50 dB.
  Employ the formulation as coating, composites and laminates, improving processability
 and mechanical performance.
  Evaluate strain sensing efficiency (gauge factor) and lighting strike protection performance
 and find potential applications in structural health monitoring and protective composites.
  Investigation of acrylate monomers as a potential alternative to ELIUM resin.
 2. TECHNOLOGY DESCRIPTION
Thermoplastic ELIUM resin (methyl methacrylate resin, PMMA) is gaining considerable attention
in multiple composite applications due to its unique conventional thermoset-like processability
and recyclability like thermoplastic. ELIUM prepolymer resin is polymerized in-situ via a
peroxide-initiated reaction after infusion, rather than undergoing conventional epoxy crosslinking
[1]. This free radical polymerization yields a thermoplastic PMMA matrix, thereby enabling
recyclability of the resulting composites. ELIUM resin offers excellent rigidity, dimensional
stability and resistance to oxidative photodegradation, making it ideal resin system for energy,
construction, civil engineering, transportation, marine, sports and consumers goods. ELIUM resin
is chosen as base material which is further modified by PANI and low-cost carbon allotropes
manufactured by Minus 100 to achieve high electrical conductivity. The lower viscosity of ELIUM
resin as compared to PMMA melt is leveraged in this work to obtain higher dispersibility of PANI
and CCM filler. Although conventional PMMA/PANI blends have been studied by several
researchers [2-4], the investigation on ELIUM resin in combination with PANI has never been
reported to the best of our knowledge. Typically, PMMA/PANI composites are prepared by melt
blending, solution blending, and co-polymerization. Compared to melt or solution blending, in-
situ polymerization is found to be more effective to obtain uniform dispersion. Ji et al. reported
PMMA/PANI gas sensor by polymerizing PANI on the electro spun film of PMMA [5]. Hamlaoui
et al. also in-situ polymerized PANI doped with malic acid in the presence of PMMA solution and
measured the electrical conductance [6]. While polymerization of PANI in presence of PMMA has
been extensively studied, conversely, the in-situ polymerization of PMMA in the presence of PANI
has never been reported. Shaari et al. co-polymerized PMMA and PANI by adding aniline
monomer into partially polymerized MMA [7]. The resulting co-polymer exhibited discontinuous
PANI network, leading to low electrical conductivity. In contrast, the polymerization of MMA in
the presence of pre-polymerized PANI could result in a continuous network of PANI in the matrix
of PMMA, a morphology which is difficult to obtain via conventional melt or solution blending.
 Doping agents play a significant role in enabling electronic conduction through PANI
molecules. The emeraldine base (EB) structure of PANI shows higher solubility in polar organic
solvent; however, it is insulating [8]. After the doping, EB is converted to emeraldine salt (ES)
forming a conductive phase. A suitable organic protonic acid can greatly enhance the conductance
of EB by donating proton (p-doping) while also increasing the solubility of as formed ES by acting
as a surfactant [8]. Dodecyl benzene sulphonic acid (DBSA) is found to be a promising doping

                                                                                          Page |3

agent due its plasticizing effect offered by the long chain moieties [9, 10]. In the current work,
liquid ELIUM was polymerized by EB in the presence of DBSA enabling simultaneous doping
reaction. The robust in-situ synthesis method of ELIUM resin led to an interpenetrating network
with PANI/DBSA offering diverse processing opportunities, such as coating, laminates,
compression molding, etc. ideal for making components for different industrial applications.
To further improve the conductivity of the developed resin system, a low-cost conductive carbon
allotrope by Minus 100 has been incorporated before polymerization via high shear mixing to
enhance the network for electron conduction. Minus 100 has actively contributed to the
development of value-added coal-based products and has been an integral part of the Penn State
Consortium for Premium Carbon Products from Coal (CPCPC). In its untreated state, coal exhibits
characteristics more aligned with insulation than electrical conductivity. Yet, research conducted
by Pennsylvania State University and others has established that through thermal treatment, coal
particles can be rendered electrically conductive. Collaborating with Minus 100, we are proposing
a conductive formulation to reach high electrically conductivity of 5 S.cm-1, avoiding the usage of
expensive fillers to escalate broader industrial adoption.

In-situ synthesis of ELIUM/PANI/DBSA (EPD) blend
 EPD blends were prepared by in-situ polymerization of liquid ELIUM resin containing
reactive diluent in the presence of PANI/DBSA at varying ratios under ambient temperature. At
first, the liquid ELIUM resin was blended with PANI by high shear mixing using Thinky mixer at
800 rpm for 90 s resulting in a uniform dispersion. Into the homogeneous mixture, measured
amount of DBSA was added with continuous stirring for 10 min and quickly casted on the mold
for polymerization under ambient temperature. With the incorporation of DBSA, a quick rise in
the viscosity was noted. Thus, acetone was used which acted as a diluent, suppressing the viscosity

                                                                                              Page |4

rise. In the EPD blends, the ratio of PANI and DBSA was always maintained at ~1:2 weight ratio
[11]. However, the composition of ELIUM and PANI/DBSA was varied to investigate the effect
of PANI/DBSA concentration Table 1. The schematic of preparation of the EPD is shown in Figure
1. Schematic of the development of conductive formulation and demonstration of various processability..
To prepare composites with CCM, carbon allotrope was added into the liquid ELIUM resin at the
beginning by using Thinky mixer at 800 rpm for 90 s. Then the PANI was added, and similar steps
were followed for curing. The ratios of CCM varied by 1, 3 and 5 wt.% of the total EPD blends,
described as part per hundred resin (phr). Compression molding was performed in Carver press at
160 °C. At first, the mold was preheated to 160 °C followed by placing the casted preform (EPD
resins) into the mold and compression load was applied for 5 min. After that, the sample was cool
under pressure to allow solidification. The casted perform and molded composites were
characterized by thermal properties, dynamic mechanical properties, and electrical conductivity.

 3. KEY RESULTS

 Liquid ELIUM resin undergoes polymerization by PANI/DBSA without adding peroxide in
the formulation. To understand the polymerization mechanism, liquid ELIUM resin was mixed
with PANI (EB) and DBSA separately. The mixture of liquid ELIUM and PANI exhibited
polymerization enhancing thermal stability (discussed latter). Alongside, the mixing of liquid
ELIUM resin with DBSA led to wax-like structure showing low thermal stability. The radical
scavenging effect of PANI has been reported in the literature through the donation of hydrogen
radical (H.) [12]. In this case, the donated H. is assumed to initiate the polymerization of liquid
ELIUM resin without requiring peroxide. Addition of DBSA accelerated the oxidation of PANI

enhancing the rate of polymerization [13]. Thus, a quick rise in viscosity was observed which was
countered by adding acetone for coating and preform casting, as shown in Figure 1. Schematic of
the development of conductive formulation and demonstration of various processability.. When only
DBSA was added to liquid ELIUM resin, DBSA might donate protons causing cationic
polymerization of ELIUM in absence of PANI. However, the extend of this reaction was low

 4. IMPACT
 Introducing a unique resin formulation with high electrical conductivity through in-situ liquid
 phase reaction allowing superior dispersion of PANI and low cost conducting fillers to
 enhance electron transportation.
 Enhancing multifunctionality by integrating electrical conductivity, EMI shielding and strain
 sensing within a single material platform expanding the scope of composites across
 electronics, aerospace, automotive, infrastructure and energy, while improving the durability
 and life span.
 High conductivity and easy applicability of the coating can potentially replace metals
 wire/mesh for lighting protection in large structures, featuring lightweight and corrosion
 resistant solution.
 Demonstration of drop in manufacturing compatibility with wide range of processing routes
 enabling easy adoption in industry scale.
 Demonstrate conversion of low cost byproduct into high value functional materials
 strengthening supply chain resilience and supporting US leadership in advanced
 manufacturing.

 5. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
In first phase of the proposed work, conductive formulation was successfully developed through
the simultaneous in-situ polymerization of ELIUM and doping of PANI (EB), resulting in the
formation of an inter connected conductive network. The effects of PANI and doping agent DBSA
were evaluated on the reaction kinetics, thermal stability, dynamic mechanical properties and
electrical conductivity. To enhance the conductivity, lost cost CCM filler derived from coal
byproduct was utilized which exhibited further improvement in both thermal stability and
electrical conductivity. The influence of CCM is more pronounced at lower PANI/DBSA
concentrations. Notably, addition of 3 phr of CCM into EPD70/30 resulted in a significant 185%
improvement in electrical conductivity. The highest conductivity of 5.66 S.cm-1 was achieved for
the EPD50/50 at 3 phr CCM concentration in the preform phase. However, after compression

                                                                                             P a g e | 17

molding, conductivity slightly decreased which can be attributed to the partial de-doping reaction
of PANI.

In the second phase, the following work will be performed:
1. Optimization of resin formulation for coating and laminates focusing on:
 - reduced PANI concentration
 - improved filer dispersion
 - investigate alternate carbon allotropes from Minus 100.
 - effect of coupling agent
 - effect of partial crosslinking by introducing vinyl group to improve mechanical performance.
2. Fabrication of coating and laminates
 - investigate adhesion strength for coating on different substrates.
 - investigate mechanical and interlaminar shear properties
3. Lighting strike protection and EMI shielding performance
 - Lightning strike test on coated surface and evaluate morphology and mechanical
 properties before and after
 - EMI shielding effectiveness of both coating and laminates
4. Strain sensing performance
 - strain sensing performance of the coating and laminates under cyclic tensile and/or
 flexural loading
 - effect of strain rate, deformation and oscillation cycle will be evaluated.
