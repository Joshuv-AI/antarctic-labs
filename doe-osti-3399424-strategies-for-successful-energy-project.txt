 Today’s Objectives

• We aim to equip you with an understanding of:
 1) How financing fits into the project development timeline/cycle
 2) Key characteristics of different ownership models and how they impact
 project economics and community impact
 3) Different private capital provider types and their main objectives
 4) Compliance considerations for braiding and stacking capital sources
• Key Takeaway: Achieving affordable, timely financing for projects
  requires a comprehensive understanding of (1) project objectives, (2)
  capital needs and constraints, (3) timeline goals and constraints, and
  (4) internal capacity.

Conclusion

 Takeaways

• A “project champion” spearheads project stakeholder engagement and applies
  expertise to seek incremental investment that progressively resolves key risks.
• Ownership model decisions are driven by a variety of factors ranging from
  community characteristics to technical expertise to regulatory influence.
• Understanding the characteristics and objectives of different private capital
  provider types is essential for efficiently seeking the appropriate
  funding/financing.
• When applying federal funding a project, it must always be applied and tracked
  separately from other sources.
• Successful financing for energy projects requires a strong understanding of (1)
  project objectives, (2) capital needs and constraints, (3) timeline goals and
  constraints, and (4) internal capacity.

 Sources for Finding Funding and Financing

• DSIRE: Database of State Incentives for Renewables and Efficiency. Example: Energy Efficiency
  Revolving Loan Fund (EE RLF) program
• Community Power Accelerator: An online platform developed by DOE and NLR, now managed by the
  Milken Institute, for distributed PV, storage, and microgrid projects to connect with capital
• DOE Better Buildings Financing Navigator: The Navigator is an online tool that helps public and private
  sector organizations find financing solutions for energy reduction and onsite generation projects.
• AFFORD Tool: A tool designed to identify, compare, and prioritize federal funding, tax credits, and other
  incentives.
• USDA Energy Programs: Our programs, authorized by the Agricultural Act of 2014, offer funding to
  complete energy audits, provide renewable energy development assistance, make energy efficiency
  improvements and install renewable energy systems.
• EPA Energy Financing Toolkit for Decisionmakers: This toolkit provides profiles of energy financing
  programs that state and local governments are pursuing to catalyze investment in energy. This introductory
  information is intended to help decisionmakers understand the basics of a range of financing programs
  across multiple sectors, view examples from state and local jurisdictions, and access additional resources
  to learn more.
