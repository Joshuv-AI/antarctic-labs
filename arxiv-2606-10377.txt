Bidirectional Random Projections
Chao Lan, Luyuan Yang

Abstract
This paper analyzes bidirectional random projections for ordinary least squares (OLS) regression under the fixed design setting. Let ( X , Y ) ∈ ℝ n × p × ℝ n (X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\times\mathbb{R}^{n} be a sample and R ∈ ℝ n 1 × n , W ∈ ℝ p × p 1 R\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n},W\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p_{1}} be two properly distributed random projections. We develop an expected excess loss bound for the OLS estimator built on ( W ​ X ​ R , W ​ Y ) (WXR,WY) . Compared to an established bound for OLS estimator built on ( X ​ R , Y ) (XR,Y) , the gap is approximately O ⁡ ( p 1 + C ​ 1 p 1 ) O\left(p_{1}+C\frac{1}{p_{1}}\right) , where C C scales with n 1 / n n_{1}/n and can be negative for small n 1 / n n_{1}/n . Its implications are confirmed by numerical results on real-world data.

1
Introduction

It is well-known that
ordinary least squares (OLS) regression
can be scaled up for high-dimensional data
by using random projection to reduce
data dimension. Excess loss for
the projected OLS (pOLS) estimator has been well studied

(
4
;
2
;
3
;
5
)
.
On the other hand, random projection
can also be used to reduce data number
for faster estimation

(
6
)
. As a result,
when working with large-scale high
dimensional data, it may be desirable to
apply random projection in both directions.
However, statistical properties of such
bi-directionally projected OLS (bOLS)
estimator remains unknown.

This paper develops an excess
loss bound for bOLS estimator.
By inspecting its gap to the
bound for pOLS estimator,
we obtain three implications:
(i) bOLS performs worse than pOLS;
(ii) as the projected data dimension increases, their gap
first decreases and then increases, and the pivot happens earlier
when the reduced data number is smaller;
(iii) as the projected data dimension
increases, both losses first decrease
and then increase, but bOLS’s
loss bounces back earlier. These
theoretical implications are confirmed
by numerical results on real-world data.

2
Preliminaries

Consider the fixed design setting.
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
be

n
fixed points and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
be their labels generated by

y_{i}=x_{i}^{T}\beta_{*}+\varepsilon_{i}
,
where
\beta_{*}\in R^{p}
is fixed
and
\varepsilon_{i}
’s are independent
random noises with
\mathbb{E}\varepsilon_{i}=0

and
var(\varepsilon_{i})=\sigma^{2}
for
some fixed
\sigma>0
. Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}

be a sample matrix with

x_{i}^{T}
on the
i_{th}
row,

Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a label
vector with
Y_{i}=y_{i}

and
E\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
noise vector with
E_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}
.
Then, the above setting
can be expressed as

Y=X\beta_{*}+E.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{P}(v^{2})
denote a Gaussian
distribution on
\mathbb{R}
with zero mean
and variance
v^{2}
.
Consider two random projections

W\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n}

and
R\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p_{1}}
.
For
W
, assume it has i.i.d.
entries following

W_{ij}\sim\mathcal{P}(1/n).

(2)

For
R
, assume it has i.i.d.
entries following

R_{ij}\sim\mathcal{P}(1/p_{1}).

(3)

Consider two OLS estimators.
Let
\hat{\theta}_{R}\in R^{p_{1}}

be a projected estimator built
on
(XR,Y)
i.e.,

\hat{\theta}_{R}=\arg\min_{\theta}\frac{1}{n}\parallel XR\theta-Y\parallel^{2},

(4)

where
||\cdot||
is
L_{2}
norm.
Let
\hat{\theta}_{RW}
be a bi-directionally
projected estimator built on
(WXR,WY)
i.e.,

\hat{\theta}_{RW}=\arg\min_{\theta}\frac{1}{n_{1}}\parallel WXR\theta-WY\parallel^{2}.

(5)

We are interested in comparing

\hat{\theta}_{RW}
with
\hat{\theta}_{R}
.
Comparison will be based on
two loss functions: the
expected square loss of
any estimator
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

is defined as

L(\beta)=\mathbb{E}_{Y}\ \frac{1}{n}\parallel X\beta-Y\parallel^{2}.

(6)

Given
R
, the expected square loss
of any estimator
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{1}}

is defined as

L_{R}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R}\ \frac{1}{n}\parallel XR\theta-Y\parallel^{2}.

(7)

Finally,
let
I_{s}
be an identity matrix of
size
s
; for matrix
G
, let

||\cdot||_{G}
be the induced
Mahalanobis norm,
\mathrm{tr}(G)
be its
trace, and
\lambda_{g\uparrow}

and
\lambda_{g\downarrow}
be its
largest and smallest singular values
respectively. (Lower case
of
G
is used for its singular values.)

\mathbb{E}_{Z}
means an expectation
is w.r.t. the randomness
in
Z
, and
\mathbb{E}_{Z\mid Q}
means
the expectation is conditioned on
Q
.
In consecutive arguments, an

\mathbb{E}
without subscript has the
same subscript as its preceding
\mathbb{E}
.

3
Main Result

The following is an established
loss bound for
\hat{\theta}_{R}
.

Theorem 3.1

(
3
)
.

Under the fixed design setting,
for
p_{1}<\min(p,n)
:

\mathbb{E}_{R,\hat{\theta}_{R}}[L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{R})]-L(\beta_{*})\leq\sigma^{2}\frac{p_{1}}{n}+\frac{1}{p_{1}}||\beta_{*}||^{2}_{M}\ :=B_{R},

(8)

where
M=\Sigma+(1+\kappa)\,\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)I_{p}
and

\Sigma=\frac{1}{n}X^{T}X
.

Our bound for
\hat{\theta}_{RW}
is
stated as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Under the fixed design setting,
there exists a

W_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n}

such that for
p_{1}<\min(p,n_{1})
:

\mathbb{E}_{R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}[L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]-L(\beta_{*})\leq C_{1}\sigma^{2}\frac{p_{1}}{n}+C_{2}\frac{1}{p_{1}}||\beta_{*}||_{M}^{2}+b\ :=B_{RW},

(9)

where
M
is specified in
Theorem
3.1
,

C_{1}=\mathbb{E}_{W}[\lambda^{2}_{w\uparrow}]\lambda^{-2}_{w_{0}\downarrow}
,

C_{2}=\frac{n_{1}}{n}\lambda^{-2}_{w_{0}\downarrow}

and
b=(C_{2}-1)L(\beta_{*})
.

It is not hard to see Theorem
3.2
recovers
Theorem
3.1

if
W=I_{n}
(so that
n_{1}=n

and all
\lambda
’s
equal to 1).
In general, we are interested
in the gap between two bounds
and how it behaves as
p_{1}
increases.
To this end, more concrete ideas are needed
on
\lambda_{w_{0}\downarrow}
.
Since
W
has i.i.d. entries from

N(0,1/n)
,
(
1
)
showed
that asymptotically
\lambda_{w\downarrow}

concentrates at

1-\sqrt{n_{1}/n}
while

\lambda_{w\uparrow}

is concentrated at

1+\sqrt{n_{1}/n}
.
Then, by the choice of

W_{0}
in (
22
),

\lambda^{2}_{w_{0}\downarrow}=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}\lambda^{2}_{w\downarrow}L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})}{\mathbb{E}_{R,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})}\approx(1-\sqrt{n_{1}/n})^{2}.

(10)

Let
r=\sqrt{n_{1}/n}
. The above implies

C_{1}=\mathbb{E}_{W}[\lambda^{2}_{w\uparrow}]\lambda^{-2}_{w_{0}\downarrow}\approx(\sqrt{n/n_{1}}+1)^{2}(\sqrt{n/n_{1}}-1)^{-2}=(1+r)^{2}(1-r)^{-2},

(11)

and

C_{2}=\frac{n_{1}}{n}\lambda^{-2}_{w_{0}\downarrow}\approx{(\sqrt{n/n_{1}}-1)^{-2}}=r^{2}(1-r)^{-2}.

(12)

Now we can evaluate the gap
between two bounds.
It is not hard to see

B_{RW}-B_{R}=(C_{1}-1)\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}p_{1}\ +\ (C_{2}-1)||\beta_{*}||_{M}^{2}\frac{1}{p_{1}}+b\approx\frac{4r}{(1-r)^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}p_{1}+\frac{2r-1}{(1-r)^{2}}||\beta_{*}||_{M}^{2}\frac{1}{p_{1}}+b.

(13)

The gap is generally positive
(e.g., for large
r
or
p_{1}
),
which suggests
\hat{\theta}_{RW}
performs
worse than
\hat{\theta}_{R}
.

To inspect how the gap behaves
as
p_{1}
increases, we consider
three scenarios: (i)
p_{1}
is
large, (ii)
p_{1}
is small and

r
is large, (iii)
p_{1}
is
small and
r
is small.
In (i), the gap is dominated by

\frac{2r}{(1-r)^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}p_{1}
and thus increases
as
p_{1}
grows. In (ii),
the gap is dominated by

\frac{2r-1}{(1-r)^{2}}||\beta_{*}||_{M}^{2}\frac{1}{p_{1}}
,
which has
2r-1>0
for large
enough
r
and thus decreases as
p_{1}
grows. In (iii), the gap is
dominated by the same term, which has
2r-1<0
for small enough
r
and thus increases as
p_{1}
grows.
It is noted that, although
r=0.5
is the pivot for
\frac{2r-1}{(1-r)^{2}}||\beta_{*}||_{M}^{2}\frac{1}{p_{1}}
to transit
between increasing and decreasing,
it is not necessarily the pivot for the gap since all constants are
approximated. Moreover, the pivot for the gap is not necessarily attainable.
For example, (
13
) suggests
the pivot is approximately at
p_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{2r-1}{4r}}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sigma}||\beta_{*}||_{M}
,
which is only achievable
if the right side
is bigger than 1 and
r>1/2
. Thus, when
r
is very small (depending on
\sqrt{n}||\beta_{*}||_{M}/\sigma
),
the gap will only increase
as
p_{1}
increases.
In summary, as
p_{1}
increases,
the gap generally decrease first
and then increase; when
r
is very small, the gap may only increase
as
p_{1}
increases.

Finally, we inspect the
pivot of each bound.
As
p_{1}
increases,
both bounds first decrease
and then increase. The pivot
point of
B_{R}
is
\sqrt{n||\beta_{*}||_{M}^{2}/\sigma^{2}}

and for
B_{RW}
it is
C_{2}/C_{1}\sqrt{n||\beta_{*}||_{M}^{2}/\sigma^{2}}
.
It is not hard to see

C_{2}/C_{1}\approx\frac{r^{2}}{(1+r)^{2}}\leq 1
, which implies the
loss of
\hat{\theta}_{RW}
will bounce back earlier than the loss of
\hat{\theta}_{R}
.

4
Proof of Theorem
3.2

Given
(R,W)
, let the expected
square loss of
\theta
be

L_{RW}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}\ \frac{1}{n_{1}}\parallel WXR\theta-WY\parallel^{2}.

(14)

Let
\hat{\theta}_{*}
be
a minimizer of
L_{RW}(\theta)
i.e.,

\hat{\theta}_{*}=\arg\min_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{1}}}L_{RW}(\theta).

(15)

4.1
Roadmap

We first bound
\mathbb{E}L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})

and then connect it to
\mathbb{E}[L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]
. Given
(R,W)
, it can be
shown (Claim
4.2
)

\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R,W}[\hat{\theta}_{RW}]=\hat{\theta}_{*},

(16)

which implies (Claim
4.3
)

\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R,W}[L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]\leq\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n_{1}}||WXR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-WXR\hat{\theta}_{*}||^{2}+L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{*}).

(17)

On the right side, the first term
can be bounded as (Claim
4.4
)

\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R,W}\frac{1}{n_{1}}||WXR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-WXR\hat{\theta}_{*}||^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}\frac{p_{1}}{n_{1}}\lambda_{w\uparrow}^{2}.

(18)

The second term can be bounded
as
L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{*})\leq L_{RW}(R^{T}\beta_{*})
since

\hat{\theta}_{*}
minimizes
L_{RW}(\theta)
.
Plugging both back to
(
17
) and
taking expectation w.r.t.

W
on both sides, we have

\mathbb{E}_{W,\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R}[L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]\leq\sigma^{2}\frac{p_{1}}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{W}[\lambda_{w\uparrow}^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{W}[L_{RW}(R^{T}\beta_{*})]=\sigma^{2}\frac{p_{1}}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{w\uparrow}^{2}]+L_{R}(R^{T}\beta_{*}),

(19)

where the second equality
is by the fact that

\mathbb{E}_{W}||Wz||^{2}=\frac{n_{1}}{n}||z||^{2}
for
any
z
not depending on
W
.

For the last term
L_{R}(R^{T}\beta_{*})
,
its expectation has been bounded
in
(
3
)
as
(Remark
4.6
)

\mathbb{E}_{R}[L_{R}(R^{T}\beta_{*})]\leq L(\beta_{*})+\frac{1}{p_{1}}||\beta_{*}||_{M},

(20)

where
M=\Sigma+(1+\kappa)\,tr(\Sigma)I_{p}
and

\Sigma=\frac{1}{n}X^{T}X
.
Plugging this in (
19
)
after taking expectation
w.r.t.
R
on both sides,

\mathbb{E}_{R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}[L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]\leq\sigma^{2}\frac{p_{1}}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{W}[\lambda_{w\uparrow}^{2}]+\frac{1}{p_{1}}||\beta_{*}||_{M}+L(\beta_{*}).

(21)

Finally, it can be shown (Claim
4.5
)
there exists a
W_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n}
depending on
R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW}

distributions such that

\mathbb{E}_{R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}[L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]\geq\mathbb{E}_{R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}[\frac{n}{n_{1}}\lambda^{2}_{w\downarrow}L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]=\frac{n}{n_{1}}\lambda^{2}_{w_{0}\downarrow}\mathbb{E}_{R,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}[L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]

(22)

Combining (
21
)
and (
22
) proves
the theorem.

4.2
Supporting Claims

We first remark two basic
observations without proof.
For any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}

(of any
k>0
), there is

\lambda^{2}_{w\downarrow}||z||^{2}\leq||Wz||^{2}\leq\lambda^{2}_{w\uparrow}||z||^{2}.

(23)

In addition,
the random noise vector
satisfies
\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}[E]=\vec{0}
and
\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}[EE^{T}]=\sigma^{2}I_{n}
.

Claim 4.1
.

L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})\leq\frac{n_{1}}{n}\frac{1}{\lambda_{w\downarrow}^{2}}L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})
.

Proof.

Since
||Wz||\geq\lambda_{w\downarrow}||z||
for any
z
, we have

L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}||WXR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-WY||^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\lambda_{w\downarrow}^{2}\mathbb{E}||XR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-Y||^{2}=\frac{n_{1}}{n}\lambda_{w\downarrow}^{2}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n_{1}}||XR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-Y||^{2}.

(24)

The last expectation is

L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})
. This proves the claim.
∎

Claim 4.2
.

\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}[\hat{\theta}_{RW}]=\hat{\theta}_{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{X}=WXR
and
\tilde{Y}=WY
.
Since
\hat{\theta}_{RW}
minimizes

\frac{1}{n_{1}}||\tilde{X}\theta-\tilde{Y}||^{2}
,
it is not hard to show

\hat{\theta}_{RW}=(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{Y},

(25)

and thus

\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}[\hat{\theta}_{RW}]=(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}].

(26)

For
\hat{\theta}_{*}
, recall it minimizes

L_{RW}(\theta)
and that

L_{RW}(\theta)=\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}||\tilde{X}\theta-\tilde{Y}||^{2}=\frac{1}{n_{1}}\left(||\tilde{X}\theta||^{2}+\mathbb{E}||\tilde{Y}||^{2}-2\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}]^{T}(\tilde{X}\theta)\right).

(27)

By taking derivative of the right
side w.r.t.
\theta
and setting it
to zero, it is not hard to see

\hat{\theta}_{*}=(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}[\tilde{Y}].

(28)

Combining (
26
)
and (
28
) proves the claim.
∎

Claim 4.3
.

\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R,W}[L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]\leq\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n_{1}}||WXR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-WXR\hat{\theta}_{*}||^{2}+L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{*})
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{X}=WXR
and
\tilde{Y}=WY
.
Observe that

\begin{split}n_{1}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R,W}[L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]&=\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW},Y\mid R,W}||\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{RW}-\tilde{Y}||^{2}\\
&=\mathbb{E}||\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{RW}-\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}+\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}-\tilde{Y}||^{2}\\
&=\mathbb{E}||\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{RW}-\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}||^{2}+\mathbb{E}||\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}-\tilde{Y}||^{2}+2\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{RW}-\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*})^{T}(\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}-\tilde{Y}).\end{split}

(29)

The last term is non-positive due
to the following arguments:

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW},Y\mid R,W}(\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{RW}-\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*})^{T}(\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}-\tilde{Y})&=\mathbb{E}(\hat{\theta}_{RW}^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}-\hat{\theta}_{RW}^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{Y}-\hat{\theta}_{*}^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}+\hat{\theta}_{*}^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{Y})\\
&=\mathbb{E}[\hat{\theta}_{RW}]^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}-\mathbb{E}[\hat{\theta}_{RW}^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{Y}]-\hat{\theta}_{*}^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}+\hat{\theta}_{*}^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}]\\
&=-\mathbb{E}[\hat{\theta}_{RW}^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{Y}]+\hat{\theta}_{*}^{T}\tilde{X}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}]\\
&=-\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}^{T}\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{Y}]+\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}]^{T}\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})\tilde{X}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}]\\
&=-\mathbb{E}[E^{T}W^{T}\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})\tilde{X}^{T}WE]\\
&\leq 0.\end{split}

(30)

In above, the third line
applies
\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R,W}[\hat{\theta}_{RW}]=\hat{\theta}_{*}
(Claim

4.2
) and the
fifth line applies

\tilde{Y}=WX\beta_{*}+WE
;
the last inequality holds
since
W^{T}\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})\tilde{X}^{T}W
is semi-positive
definite. Plugging
above back to (
29
)
proves the claim.
∎

Claim 4.4
.

\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R,W}\frac{1}{n_{1}}||WXR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-WXR\hat{\theta}_{*}||^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}\frac{p_{1}}{n_{1}}\lambda_{w\uparrow}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{X}=WXR
and
\tilde{Y}=WY
.
By design, there is

\tilde{Y}-\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}[\tilde{Y}]=(WX\beta_{*}+WE)-(WX\beta_{*}+W\mathbb{E}[E])=WE.

(31)

Combining above with
(
25
,

28
), we have

\hat{\theta}_{RW}-\hat{\theta}_{*}=(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}(\tilde{Y}-\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}[\tilde{Y}])=(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}WE.

(32)

Note the left side of this claim
is
\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R,W}||\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{RW}-\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}||^{2}
.
Plugging in the above, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\theta}_{RW}\mid R,W}||\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{RW}-\tilde{X}\hat{\theta}_{*}||^{2}&=\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}\ ||\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}WE||^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}\ \mathrm{tr}(E^{T}W^{T}\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}\ \tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}WE)\\
&=\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}\ \mathrm{tr}(\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}\ WEE^{T}W^{T})\\
&=\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}W\ \mathbb{E}[EE^{T}]\ W^{T})\\
&=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n_{1}}\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}WW^{T})\\
&\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n_{1}}\lambda^{2}_{w\uparrow}\ \mathrm{tr}(\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{T})\\
&=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n_{1}}\lambda^{2}_{w\uparrow}\ \mathrm{tr}(I_{p_{1}})=\sigma^{2}\frac{p_{1}}{n_{1}}\lambda^{2}_{w\uparrow}.\end{split}

(33)

In above, the fourth line is by
the linearity of expectation,
the fifth line is by the fact
that
\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,W}[EE^{T}]=\sigma^{2}I_{n}
, and the
sixth line is by an elementary
trace inequality
\mathrm{tr}(BA)\leq\lambda_{A\uparrow}\mathrm{tr}(B)
.
∎

Claim 4.5
.

There exists a
W_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n}
such that

\mathbb{E}_{R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}[L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]\geq\frac{n}{n_{1}}\lambda^{2}_{w_{0}\downarrow}\mathbb{E}_{R,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}[L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]
.

Proof.

First observe that

\mathbb{E}_{R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}[L_{RW}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})]=\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW},Y}||WXR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-WY||^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}\lambda^{2}_{w\downarrow}||XR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-Y||^{2}

(34)

By the weighted mean value theorem for integrals, there exists a
W_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n}
such that

\frac{1}{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{R,W,\hat{\theta}_{RW},Y}[\lambda^{2}_{w\downarrow}||XR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-Y||^{2}]=\frac{1}{n_{1}}\lambda^{2}_{w_{0}\downarrow}\mathbb{E}_{R,\hat{\theta}_{RW},Y}||XR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-Y||^{2}=\frac{n}{n_{1}}\lambda^{2}_{w_{0}\downarrow}\mathbb{E}_{R,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}[L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})],

(35)

where the last equality
holds because
L_{R}(\hat{\theta}_{RW})=\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid R,\hat{\theta}_{RW}}\frac{1}{n}||XR\hat{\theta}_{RW}-Y||^{2}
.
∎
