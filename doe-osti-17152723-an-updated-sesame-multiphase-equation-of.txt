 Abstract
 This report presents the updated multiphase sesame tabular EOS for Vascomax/Maraging
 250 steel (V/M250). Four phases are constructed independently to best describe the
 available measurements of V/M250 thermodynamic properties: the ambient alpha-
 martensite phase, the high-pressure epsilon phase, the high-temperature austenite
 phase, and the liquid phase. Compared with previous versions, this updated EOS better
 reflects the shock physics observed in experiments on V/M250 and similar martensitic
 steels. The updated sesame tabular EOS has been assigned material ID 4396.

1 Introduction
Vascomax 250, also referred to by the generic name Maraging 250 (hereafter V/M250), is
a steel with ultrahigh yield strength, reasonable toughness, and good wear resistance [1, 2].
These properties make it a candidate material for applications in extreme environments.
Despite its industrial use, very few thermodynamic EOS data are available for V/M250. A
sesame EOS was developed under material ID 4395 by Giulia De Lorenzi-Venneri in 2016.
However, no report was provided, and neither the EOS parameters nor the rationale for their
values is available for reference. A report is available for sesame EOS 4295, which describes
AM363 martensitic stainless steel [3]. Here, we present an updated V/M250 sesame EOS for
use in multiphysics simulations.
V/M250 is composed primarily of iron, with the following alloying elements (in weight per-
cent): 18.0 Ni, 8.2 Co, 4.9 Mo, 0.4 Ti, 0.05 Mn, 0.05 Cr, 0.05 Si, 0.05 Cu, 0.096 Al, and
0.003 C; iron makes up the balance. The high nickel and titanium content results in Ni3 Ti
precipitates, which contribute to the material’s high strength [1]. Under ambient conditions,
V/M250 steel exhibits an alpha-prime martensite crystal structure, which is a slight hexago-
nal distortion of the body-centered cubic alpha phase. Upon heating above 800 K, reversion
to austenite (the face-centered cubic gamma phase) occurs gradually [4]. Nearly complete
phase transformation is observed above 1093 K [1, 5].

2 Data
Few thermodynamic data are available for V/M250. Industry tables report ranges for quan-
tities such as heat capacity at constant pressure, the coefficient of volumetric thermal expan-
sion, and density under ambient conditions. The reported density is approximately 8.00–8.02
g/cm3 [6, 7]. The Marsh compendium lists a reference density of 8.129 g/cm3 , which is much
higher than the values in industry tables [8]. The previous sesame EOS uses this value as the
reference density. In this work, we have opted to reduce the reference density to 8.02 g/cm3 .
Bulk modulus measurements range from 140 to 166 GPa [9, 6, 8], with values commonly
reported in industry near the lower end of this range. Reported values of heat capacity
at constant pressure under ambient conditions fall within a narrow range, 4.27-4.52 ×10−4
MJ/(kg K) [10, 11, 7]. The Marsh LASL Shock Compendium [8] contains high-quality shock
Hugoniot data for V/M250 extending to high pressures. As in other martensitic steels, the
initial portion of the shock velocity versus particle velocity curve lies in a region that we
identify as the alpha-martensite phase. Above a pressure of ∼20 GPa, the particle velocity
drops slightly. This behavior has been observed in other martensitic steels, such as 17-4
PH [12]. It is also observed in pure iron, where the body-centered cubic (BCC) alpha phase
transforms under shock compression to a hexagonal close-packed (HCP) epsilon phase [13].
In 17-4 PH, measurements identify the transition as one from alpha martensite to HCP
epsilon. We therefore proceed under the assumption that the same shock-induced phase
transition occurs between 15 and 20 GPa along the V/M250 shock Hugoniot. The Hugoniot
measurements for Vascomax 250 show an additional feature not seen in iron: an apparent
transition to another solid phase around 80 GPa. This pressure is far too low to correspond
to shock melting, which is measured in iron near 270 GPa [14]. We therefore choose to model
this feature as a transition from epsilon to the face-centered cubic gamma/austenite crystal
structure. Under isobaric heating, the ferromagnetic alpha-martensite phase undergoes a
Curie transition to a paramagnetic state. Little information is available about the Curie
temperature, so we use a value of 950 K; this value should be revised as better data become
available. V/M250 steel melts from the gamma/austenite phase in the range of 1700–1750
K [15, 1].

Cold curves are generated using a Rose–Vinet EOS with the following input parameters: cold
density ρ0 , cold bulk modulus K0 , the pressure derivative of the bulk modulus at P = 0, K0′ ,
cold energy offset ∆E0 , Lennard-Jones coefficient lLJ , cohesive energy Ecoh. , and low- and
high-compression cutoffs η.
A Mie–Gruneisen + Debye model is used for the thermal ionic EOS. It requires the reference
Debye temperature Θref , the reference Gruneisen parameter γref , and the negative derivative
of the Gruneisen parameter with respect to the logarithm of volume, evaluated at the refer-
ence volume, γ ′ . These parameters are based largely on the multiphase sesame iron EOS [13]
and are modified to better capture the available V/M250 data and the three-phase shock
response. The melt curve is also tuned to resemble that of iron.
The thermal electronic EOS is determined using Kohn–Sham–Dirac density functional the-
ory average-atom calculations with the KSDT exchange-correlation functional. Separate
calculations were performed for Fe, Ni, Co, and Mo. A mixture was then constructed by
matching chemical potentials along isotherms, using atomic fractions of 0.717, 0.181, 0.073,
and 0.029, respectively. These calculations were performed using the Tartarus code [16, 17].

Figure 1 shows the updated sesame EOS for V/M250 along selected thermodynamic curves
for which data are available. Figure 1(a) shows the isobaric density change with temperature
at 1 atm. The EOS captures the trends in the available data for V/M350, which has a similar
composition and a closely matching reference density. Above 1000 K, our EOS transitions
to the austenite phase and captures the change in the density trend in the available data [7].
Figure 1(b) shows the isobaric heat capacity at 1 atm. Our EOS lies between the two heat-
capacity values taken from engineering data sheets for V/M250 [11, 10] and agrees well with
the heat-capacity data for V/M350. A broad peak extending up to 1000 K reflects the rise in
heat capacity associated with the ferromagnetic-to-paramagnetic transition. Sharp disconti-
nuities occur at the martensite-to-austenite and austenite-to-liquid transitions. Figure 1(c)
shows the shock velocity versus particle velocity along the principal Hugoniot, as reported
in the LASL Shock Data Compendium [8]. Our EOS traverses three distinct solid phases
along the shock Hugoniot: the martensite phase for up from 0 to ∼0.5 km/s, the epsilon
phase from 0.6 to 1.7 km/s, and the austenite phase from 2.2 km/s upward. For up from
1.7 to 2.2 km/s, the shock Hugoniot skirts the boundary between phases. Our chosen grid
and interpolation scheme produce a zigzag pattern in this region of the shock Hugoniot.
The same trend appears in Figure 1(d), which shows the shock Hugoniot in density–pressure
space. Our EOS captures the details of the available shock data.
Figure 2(a) shows the phase diagram of the updated V/M250 EOS. Consistent with the shock
Hugoniot measurements, the modeled shock Hugoniot passes through three solid phases
(martensite, epsilon, and austenite) before shock melting begins at 205 GPa. The shock-
melting transition is complete at 290 GPa, similar to values measured and simulated for
iron [13]. However, the shock Hugoniot of iron does not enter the austenite phase; in-
stead, shock melting occurs directly from epsilon to liquid. The thermal stabilization of the
austenite phase in V/M250 could be attributed to its high nickel concentration, although this
possibility requires further investigation. Figure 2(b) shows isotherms outlining the vapor
dome for V/M250 as described by the updated EOS. We predict a liquid–vapor critical point
at a density of 1.4 g/cm3 , a pressure of 1.27 GPa, and a temperature of 8890 K.

4 Conclusion
In this report, we present the updated Vascomax/Maraging 250 steel sesame tabular EOS.
This updated EOS retains three solid phases, as suggested by a detailed analysis of shock
Hugoniot measurements for V/M250 and related materials. Although the available data
for V/M250 are sparse, data for V/M350 and the iron EOS guided the construction of a
reasonable EOS for V/M250. The new EOS has been assigned sesame database material ID
4396 and is available in the development database as of the writing of this report.
