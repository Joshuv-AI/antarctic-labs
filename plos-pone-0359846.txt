Functional alterations of neutrophils from β-thalassemia patients exposed to Pythium insidiosum: An ex vivo study

Pasinee Sangsiwarit, Wisitsak Phoksawat, Nattiya Teawtrakul, Sonwit Phanabamrung, Parama Budmala, Ariya Chindamporn, Pratsanee Hiengrach

ABSTRACT
Patients with β-thalassemia are highly susceptible to Pythium insidiosum, the causative agent of human pythiosis, a severe and often life-threatening disease in tropical regions. This vulnerability has been associated with iron overload and immune dysregulation. However, the underlying mechanisms of impaired host defense, especially in neutrophils, remain incompletely understood. This study investigated neutrophil function in response to P. insidiosum zoospores, using neutrophils isolated from β-thalassemia patients and comparing their responses with those from healthy controls. Ex vivo study, isolated neutrophils were co-incubated with P. insidiosum zoospores, then evaluated for pathogen-killing activity, neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation, and phagocytic capacity. Neutrophils isolated from β-thalassemia patients exhibited significantly reduced fungicidal activity, as indicated by higher fungal burdens in comparison with healthy controls. In addition, NET formation was significantly diminished in the neutrophils isolated from the patient group, reflecting impaired extracellular pathogen containment. Similarly, phagocytic activity in the neutrophils isolated from the healthy group was significantly higher than in the patient group. These findings demonstrate that impairments of neutrophil functions, including killing activity, NET formation, and phagocytic capacity, contributed mechanistically to the increased susceptibility to P. insidiosum in patients with β-thalassemia. This study highlights the importance of targeting innate immune dysfunction in the development of therapeutic strategies for human pythiosis.

Introduction Pythiosis is a life-threatening infectious disease caused by Pythium insidiosum , an organism classified within the phylum Oomycota of the kingdom Chromista [ 1 ], distinct from true fungi because it produces motile, biflagellate zoospores [ 2 ]. Pythium zoospores facilitate infection by actively swimming in stagnant aquatic environments, which are prevalent in tropical regions, especially Thailand [ 3 , 4 ], to colonize exposed host tissues. High mortality rates have characterized pythiosis, and it is frequently misdiagnosed [ 5 ], as its clinical presentation mimics fungal infections but does not respond to conventional antifungal therapies. Previous reports suggest that a critical risk factor for pythiosis is thalassemia, especially β-thalassemia [ 6 , 7 ]. β-thalassemia is a hereditary blood disorder characterized by deficient or absent production of β-globin chains, which are essential components of hemoglobin [ 8 ]. This genetic impairment typically manifests as microcytic anemia. A primary consequence of this condition is the development of systemic iron overload [ 9 ], which triggers oxidative stress and cellular injury, directly contributing to clinical complications such as immune abnormalities and increased vulnerability to severe infections [ 10 ]. Within this context, neutrophils serve as the critical first line of the innate immune system. They are responsible for capturing and eliminating pathogens during the initial stages of fungal infection, acting as a vital defense before the activation of more complex humoral and lymphocyte-mediated responses. Neutrophils employ a combination of phagocytosis, reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, degranulation, and the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) to control the pathogen [ 11 ]. Previous reports suggest that the filamentous hyphae of P. insidiosum are often too large for ingestion, thereby necessitating reliance on extracellular mechanisms [ 12 ]. While the baseline activity of neutrophils against P. insidiosum is documented, their specific behavior within the β-thalassemia population has not been fully elucidated. We aim to assess the functional response of neutrophils isolated from β-thalassemia patients following exposure to P. insidiosum zoospores and compare them with those of neutrophils isolated from healthy individuals in an ex vivo study. Then, particular focus on phagocytic capacity, fungicidal activity, and NET formation, all of which are critical for controlling filamentous pathogens. Therefore, this study is the first ex vivo study to investigate the mechanisms underlying neutrophil dysfunction against P. insidiosum in patients with β-thalassemia. Additionally, this knowledge is essential for improving clinical outcomes. Methods Subject selection All eligible participants aged between 18 and 60 years were included. Blood samples were collected from 12 donors, six with β-thalassemia major and six healthy controls. The recruitment period was from 21 st December 2025–29 th May 2026. The diagnosis of β-thalassemia major was established based on clinical history, hematological parameters, and hemoglobin electrophoresis, in accordance with standard protocols employed at the Hematology Clinic, Srinagarind Hospital, Khon Kaen, Thailand. Following the obtainment of written informed consent, blood samples were collected via phlebotomy in accordance with the protocol approved by the Center for Ethics in Human Research at Khon Kaen University (HE681691). Inclusion criteria Inclusion criteria for the patient group required hemoglobin (Hb) levels less than 9 g/dL and serum ferritin levels higher than 1,000 g/dL. Conversely, individuals with coexisting autoimmune diseases or active infections were excluded from the study. The control group comprised healthy individuals aged 18–60 years with no history of blood disorders or chronic illnesses affecting iron metabolism [ 13 ]. Participants were required to have Hb levels between 12 and 18 g/dL [ 14 ] and serum ferritin levels within the normal range between 15 and 300 μg/L [ 15 ]. Exclusion criteria Any individual who was unable to provide informed consent or who had illnesses or treatments that might affect the biomarkers under investigation was excluded. Furthermore, women who were breastfeeding or pregnant were not allowed. Neutrophil isolation Heparinized blood from six patients with β-thalassemia and six healthy donors was recruited from Srinagarind Hospital, Khon Kaen, Thailand. Human neutrophils were isolated from whole blood using a standard density gradient separation method, as previously described [ 12 ]. Briefly, fresh heparinized blood was layered onto Polymorphprep™ (Axis-Shield, Norway) and centrifuged at 1,800 rpm for 30 min at 25°C, resulting in the separation of six distinct bands, including plasma, monocytes, neutrophils, isolation media, and a red blood cell pellet. Residual red blood cell contamination was eliminated using a red blood cell lysis buffer (Gibco-Invitrogen, USA). The resulting neutrophil pellet was resuspended in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco-Invitrogen) supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco-Invitrogen). Cell viability and purity were assessed via trypan blue (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) exclusion and Wright-Giemsa staining, respectively. To maintain viability, neutrophils were rested in complete RPMI 1640 at 37°C in a 5% CO 2 incubator before experimental use. However, because neutrophils have a short lifespan, all separation procedures were initiated within 2–3 h of blood collection. Cultivation of P. insidiosum and induction of zoospores A clinical strain of P. insidiosum , previously isolated from a patient with vascular pythiosis, was cultured in Sabouraud dextrose broth (SDB; Oxoid, UK) overnight. Zoospore production was performed as described by Mendoza and Prendas [ 16 ]. Briefly, the microorganism was cultured on sterile blanket grass (Axonopus compressus) on malt agar (Oxoid) at 37°C for 48 h. To induce the formation of encysted zoospores, the blanket grass colonized with Pythium hyphae was transferred to an induction medium containing calcium (Ca 2+ ) and magnesium (Mg 2+ ) ions, which facilitate Pythium zoospore production [ 17 ]. Finally, the encysted zoospores were washed with 1X phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) and quantified via trypan blue before use. Measurements of neutrophil-associated parameters Evaluation of the fungicidal activity of neutrophils. To specifically evaluate the killing capacity of neutrophils derived from β-thalassemia patients against P. insidiosum infection compared to healthy donors, isolated neutrophils were co-incubated with live Pythium zoospores in 24-well plates using complete RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated FBS. This co-culture was maintained at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 1 for durations of 0 and 60 min. Following incubation, 100 µL of the challenged samples were spread onto blood agar (Oxoid) and incubated overnight at 37°C, followed by manual colony enumeration. Assessment of NET formation. To evaluate the interaction between P. insidiosum zoospores and NET formation, the experiment was performed as described in the previous publication [ 12 ]. Zoospore isolates were heat-treated at 80°C for 30 min with gentle agitation, then centrifuged at 5,000 rpm for 10 min to harvest the cell pellet. Neutrophils isolated from healthy donors (control) and β-thalassemia patients were seeded onto poly-L-lysine-coated coverslips (Sigma-Aldrich) at a density of 10 5 cells. The cells were subsequently challenged with 100 ng/mL of 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA; Sigma-Aldrich) as a positive control, or heat-killed Pythium zoospores were added at an MOI of 1 and incubated for 2 h at 37°C in a 5% CO 2 atmosphere. Coverslips were washed with Tris-buffered saline (TBS; pH 7.6) containing 0.05% Tween 20 and fixed with 4% (v/v) paraformaldehyde for 10 min. The samples were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 1 min at room temperature, blocked with 3% (w/v) bovine serum albumin (BSA; Sigma-Aldrich) supplemented with 5% FBS in 1X TBS for 30 min, and subjected to three washes with 1X TBS containing 0.05% Tween 20. Neutrophil degranulation and NET structures were visualized using antibodies against myeloperoxidase (MPO; BioLegend, USA) and neutrophil elastase (NE; BioLegend), while nuclear morphology was counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI; Sigma-Aldrich). Fluorescent images were captured using fluorescence microscopy and independently evaluated by two blinded observers at 200 × magnification across 10 randomly selected fields. Evaluation of cellular phagocytic activity by flow cytometric analysis. Phagocytic activity was assessed using pHrodo-succinimidyl ester (Invitrogen, USA), which emits red fluorescence upon internalization into acidic phagosomes. Heat-killed Pythium zoospore preparation was prepared following a previously established protocol [ 18 ]. Briefly, the heat-killed zoospores were incubated with diluted pHrodo for 30 min, then washed three times with 1X PBS (pH 7.4). To evaluate phagocytosis, stained Pythium zoospores were co-incubated with isolated neutrophils from either healthy individuals or β-thalassemia patients at 37°C in a 5% CO 2 atmosphere for 30 min. The cold co-incubation at 4°C was used as a negative control following the previous publication [ 19 ]. Following incubation, samples were blocked with FACS buffer supplemented with human AB serum at 4°C for 20 min. Neutrophils were then labeled with 0.2 mg/mL of anti-CD11b antibody (APC; BioLegend). Flow cytometric analysis was performed using a BD FACSLyrics (BD Biosciences, USA). Phagocytosed zoospores within neutrophils were detected in the PE channel (575 nm), and the presence of PE-positive cells served as an indicator of phagocytic uptake. Statistical analysis Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). The normality of data distribution was first evaluated to determine the appropriate statistical tests. For normally distributed data, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed as appropriate, followed by suitable post hoc tests for multiple comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as a p-value < 0.05 . Discussion Pythiosis is a life-threatening infectious disease affecting both humans and animals [ 5 , 20 ], predominantly occurring in tropical, subtropical, and some temperate regions worldwide, with a particularly high incidence reported in Thailand [ 3 , 21 ]. The disease is caused by P. insidiosum , an aquatic oomycete pathogen that grows in swampy environments and stagnant water [ 16 , 22 ]. In humans, pythiosis is clinically classified into four major forms based on the anatomical site of infection and disease severity, including vascular pythiosis, ocular pythiosis, cutaneous pythiosis, and disseminated pythiosis [ 3 ]. Interestingly, vascular pythiosis is a severe clinical form of pythiosis, characterized by arterial invasion, thrombosis, and limb ischemia, often requiring surgical intervention and associated with substantial morbidity and mortality [ 3 , 21 , 23 ]. Notably, vascular pythiosis has been strongly linked to underlying hematological disorders, particularly β-thalassemia [ 24 ]. Several studies have reported a high prevalence of β-thalassemia among patients with vascular pythiosis, suggesting a disease-specific susceptibility in this population [ 6 , 24 , 25 ]. The pathophysiological basis for this association is not yet fully understood; however, it is thought to involve immune dysregulation, chronic hemolysis, and iron overload, all of which may compromise host defense mechanisms [ 26 ], especially neutrophil functions [ 27 ]. In particular, impaired neutrophil function and altered innate immune responses have been proposed as contributing factors that increase vulnerability to P. insidiosum infection in β-thalassemia patients. This study demonstrates that neutrophils isolated from patients with β-thalassemia exhibit a significant reduction in fungicidal activity against P. insidiosum compared with healthy controls. The resulting increase in fungal burden reflects an inefficiency in pathogen clearance, whereas neutrophils from healthy individuals effectively restricted P. insidiosum growth, indicating preserved effector function. Interestingly, the results demonstrated that after 60 min of incubation, Pythium zoospores co-incubated with neutrophils isolated from β-thalassemia exhibited a trend toward higher colony counts compared with the zoospore alone (control group). This might be associated with both neutrophil dysfunction in this group of patients [ 10 ] and the dual role in iron management by controlling the organism and, ironically, releasing iron into the surrounding environment as a nutrient source for microorganisms [ 28 ], specifically during the formation of NETs [ 29 , 30 ]. Therefore, neutrophils typically attempt to sequester iron via lactoferrin [ 31 ], host cell lysis or specific inflammatory signaling may paradoxically increase the availability of growth-limiting nutrients. This shift in the microenvironment likely fuels the ‘survivor zoospores’, which might be enabling them to proliferate more aggressively in the presence of immune cells than in their absence. Notably, Pythium growth is highly dependent on iron bioavailability [ 32 ], which might be another factor supporting Pythium . The iron levels in neutrophils against P. insidiosum infection should be further demonstrated. Additionally, the diminished fungicidal capacity in β-thalassemic neutrophils appears fundamentally linked to defects in NET formation. While both groups showed minimal baseline NET production, stimulation with PMA or P. insidiosum zoospores induced a robust NETotic response only in healthy neutrophils. Patients with β-thalassemia failed to mount a comparable response, suggesting a significant impairment in NETosis. Given that NETs are critical for immobilizing and neutralizing large extracellular pathogens, these findings suggest that defective NET formation is a primary contributor to the compromised innate immune defense in this population [ 33 , 34 ]. These observations align with previous literature indicating that iron overload, a hallmark of β-thalassemia, has deleterious effects on neutrophil physiology [ 35 ]. Additionally, excess iron disrupts intracellular redox balance and impairs the generation of ROS, which serve as central drivers of the NETotic pathway [ 36 ]. The generation of ROS in β-thalassemia patients in response to P. insidiosum infection should be further investigated. Furthermore, iron toxicity may interfere with key enzymes, such as NE and MPO, while chronic oxidative stress may desensitize signaling pathways to pathogenic stimuli [ 37 , 38 ]. The biological complexity of P. insidiosum further underscores the necessity of NET-mediated defenses. As an oomycete capable of forming large zoospores and filamentous hyphae, P. insidiosum often evades phagocytosis, requiring extracellular strategies such as NET deployment for containment [ 12 ]. Consequently, these results identify neutrophil dysfunction, specifically impaired killing and defective NETosis, as a critical factor in the increased susceptibility of β-thalassemia patients to P. insidiosum . Previous studies suggest that phagocytosis serves as a critical effector mechanism in neutrophil-mediated responses to pathogenic invasion [ 39 , 40 ]. In this study, the phagocytic efficacy of neutrophils against P. insidiosum was assessed in β-thalassemia patients and healthy controls. Although neutrophils from both groups were capable of engulfing heat-killed P. insidiosum zoospores, a significantly lower phagocytic capacity was observed in β-thalassemia patients compared with healthy individuals. Nevertheless, the overall level of phagocytic activity remained relatively low, suggesting that neutrophils are unable to efficiently sequester P. insidiosum zoospores. These findings indicate that phagocytosis is unlikely to represent the primary mechanism responsible for the clearance of this organism. As reported by Sriwarom et al. and Branzk et al., according to the size-dependent hypothesis, this inefficiency may be attributable to cellular dimensions [ 12 , 41 ]. Human neutrophils typically measure 10–12 µm in diameter [ 42 ], whereas P. insidiosum zoospores range from 8–12 µm [ 43 ], which is considerably larger than other common pathogens. Consequently, these findings suggest that neutrophil defense strategies are influenced by microbial size, with a preferential shift toward NET formation when encountering larger pathogens such as P. insidiosum . Furthermore, the significantly reduced phagocytic capacity observed in patients with β-thalassemia may reflect underlying neutrophil dysfunction. This interpretation is supported by previous reports demonstrating increased susceptibility to microbial colonization and infection in patients with thalassemia, including significantly higher oral Candida albicans colonization in individuals with thalassemia major compared with healthy controls [ 13 ]. Hence, the efficacy of this response is severely diminished in patients with β-thalassemia might be associated with chronic iron overload that generates excessive oxidative stress and leads to cellular dysfunction. This metabolic environment results in a reduced phagocytic capacity and defective NET formation, allowing P. insidiosum to evade immune clearance and establish persistent infection. To enhance the generalizability of these findings, subsequent research should also explore broader immunological parameters, including neutrophil-associated gene expression and cytokine responses. An additional limitation of this study is the difference in age distribution between the healthy donor and β-thalassemia groups. Although the difference in age between the groups was not statistically significant ( p = 0.0671 ), age may influence neutrophil functions, including fungicidal capacity, phagocytic activity, NETotic capacity, and other effector responses [ 44 , 45 ]. Therefore, the possibility that age-related differences contributed to the observed alterations in neutrophil function cannot be completely excluded. Future studies including larger cohorts with more closely age-matched participants or appropriately adjusted analyses will be important to further distinguish the effects of β-thalassemia-associated immune dysfunction from potential age-related effects. Such insights will be vital for understanding the disease and improving outcomes for patients with β-thalassemia. Conclusion These findings indicate diminished fungicidal capacity, phagocytic activity, and NETotic capacity in β-thalassemia patients, which may compromise extracellular antimicrobial defense against P. insidiosum . This impairment may partially explain the increased susceptibility to infection and the more severe clinical outcomes observed in patients with pythiosis. Supporting information S1 File Raw data 1.
