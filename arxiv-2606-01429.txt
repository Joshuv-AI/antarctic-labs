Pseudo-Gauge Stabilizers and Fibration Structure of the Cooper–Frye Map at Freeze-Out
Jiahua Tian

Abstract
We study the pseudo-gauge transformation (PGT) freedom at freeze-out in relativistic spin hydrodynamics. The Cooper–Frye map is shown to factor through the quotient of freeze-out data by a universal stabilizer, yielding a stratified fibration over the space of thermodynamic Lagrange multipliers. This classifies observables into base and fiber types, bounds the number of independent PGT-sensitive observables by the family-restricted fiber dimension, and implies cross-observable consistency relations. Applied to heavy-ion polarization data, the fibration structure provides a structural interpretation of the tension between Λ \Lambda polarization and ϕ \phi -meson spin alignment as evidence that the vorticity-dominated response sector may need to be enlarged with local field-correlation data. We show that Weyl-anomaly-induced currents studied recently are classified as base observables and recover the known Belinfante–canonical obstruction Ω a ​ b ≠ ϖ a ​ b \Omega_{ab}\neq\varpi_{ab} from the stabilizer condition.

3.1
Saturation at global equilibrium

In this subsection we derive the claim that
H_{\mathrm{univ}}=G_{\mathrm{PGT}}
at global equilibrium mentioned in section
2
. The argument proceeds by showing that at global equilibrium the generator
\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}
reduces to a linear combination of the total conserved charges of the Poincaré and baryon-number symmetries, and that these total charges are exactly pseudo-gauge invariant on
\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
with standard fall-off, so that
\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\rm GE}=0
and every
\Phi
lies in
H_{\mathrm{univ}}
.

Global thermodynamic equilibrium for a relativistic fluid with inverse temperature four-vector
\beta^{\mu}=u^{\mu}/T
is characterized by the Killing condition
6

\partial_{(\mu}\beta_{\nu)}=0\,,

(26)

which in flat spacetime is solved by

\beta^{\mu}(x)=b^{\mu}+\varpi^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\,x^{\nu}\,,\qquad\varpi_{\mu\nu}=-\partial_{[\mu}\beta_{\nu]}=\text{constant antisymmetric}\,.

(27)

This describes a uniformly rotating thermal fluid; the static case is the special instance
\varpi_{\mu\nu}=0
. Stationarity of
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}
under the dynamics then forces the spin potential at global equilibrium to be equal to the thermal vorticity
9
;
15
;
18
;
14
,

\Omega_{ab}=\varpi_{ab}\,.

(28)

The reason is that orbital and spin angular momenta are not separately conserved in an interacting theory; only their sum
\hat{J}^{ab}=\hat{L}^{ab}+\hat{S}^{ab}
commutes with the Hamiltonian. The combination
-\tfrac{1}{2}\varpi_{ab}\hat{L}^{ab}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Omega_{ab}\hat{S}^{ab}
that would otherwise appear in
\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}
is therefore a symmetry generator only when the coefficients in front of
\hat{L}^{ab}
and
\hat{S}^{ab}
coincide, i.e. when
\Omega_{ab}=\varpi_{ab}
. We will make this argument more explicit in a moment.

Inserting (
27
) and (
28
) into the explicit definition (
8
) of
\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}
, and using the constancy of the Lagrange multipliers
b^{\mu}
,
\varpi_{ab}
,
\xi
at global equilibrium, the generator takes the form

\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}\,=\,\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\,\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}\bigl(b_{\nu}+\varpi_{\nu\rho}x^{\rho}\bigr)\,-\,\xi\!\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\,\hat{j}^{\mu}\,-\,\tfrac{1}{2}\varpi_{ab}\!\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\,\hat{S}^{\mu,ab}\,.

(29)

The
b_{\nu}
piece of the stress-tensor term gives the total energy-momentum directly. The
x

\varpi_{\nu\rho}
,

\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\,\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}\,\varpi_{\nu\rho}\,x^{\rho}\,=\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,\varpi_{\nu\rho}\!\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\bigl(x^{\rho}\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}-x^{\nu}\hat{T}^{\mu\rho}\bigr)\,=\,-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\varpi_{\nu\rho}\,\hat{L}^{\nu\rho}\,,

(30)

which identifies the integrated orbital angular momentum. Combining (
30
) with the spin-tensor piece of (
29
) via the total angular momentum
\hat{J}^{\nu\rho}=\hat{L}^{\nu\rho}+\hat{S}^{\nu\rho}
, one obtains the compact form

\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\rm GE}\;=\;b_{\nu}\,\hat{P}^{\nu}\,-\,\xi\,\hat{Q}\,-\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,\varpi_{\nu\rho}\,\hat{J}^{\nu\rho}\,,

(31)

where the integrated total charges are defined as

\hat{P}^{\nu}\,=\,\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\,\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\hat{J}^{\nu\rho}\,=\,\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\bigl(x^{\nu}\hat{T}^{\mu\rho}-x^{\rho}\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}+\hat{S}^{\mu,\nu\rho}\bigr)\,,\qquad\hat{Q}\,=\,\int_{\Sigma}d\Sigma_{\mu}\,\hat{j}^{\mu}\,.

(32)

Equation (
31
) is the standard covariant form of the global-equilibrium statistical operator studied in
6
;
13
;
57
, where the generator is built entirely from the total charges of the Poincaré algebra together with the baryon-number generator.

It remains to establish that each of these total charges is pseudo-gauge invariant on
\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the standard fall-off conditions on the fields at spatial infinity. The baryon charge
\hat{Q}
is invariant by construction, since
\hat{j}^{\mu}
does not transform under (
2
). For the total momentum, the shift induced by the PGT is a 4-divergence
\partial_{\lambda}\hat{X}^{\lambda 0\nu}
integrated against
d^{3}x
; antisymmetry of
\hat{X}^{\lambda\mu\nu}
in its first pair kills the
\partial_{0}\hat{X}^{00\nu}
piece, and the spatial divergence
\partial_{i}\hat{X}^{i0\nu}
reduces by Gauss’s theorem to a surface integral on
S^{2}_{\infty}
that vanishes whenever
\hat{\Phi}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}
falls off faster than
1/r^{2}
, the standard assumption on pseudo-gauge generators acting on states of finite total energy-momentum. Hence

\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{P}^{\nu}\,=\,0\,.

(33)

The total angular momentum requires more care. The shift of its local density is

\Delta_{\Phi}\!\bigl(x^{\mu}\hat{T}^{\lambda\nu}-x^{\nu}\hat{T}^{\lambda\mu}+\hat{S}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}\bigr)\,=\,\partial_{\rho}\bigl[x^{\mu}\hat{X}^{\rho,\lambda\nu}-x^{\nu}\hat{X}^{\rho,\lambda\mu}\bigr]\,+\,\mathcal{R}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}\,,

(34)

where the first term is a total derivative obtained by the Leibniz identity
\partial_{\rho}(x^{\mu}A^{\rho})=A^{\mu}+x^{\mu}\partial_{\rho}A^{\rho}
and the residual is

\mathcal{R}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}\,=\,-\hat{X}^{\mu,\lambda\nu}\,+\,\hat{X}^{\nu,\lambda\mu}\,-\,\hat{\Phi}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}\,.

(35)

Substituting the definition
\hat{X}^{\lambda\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\hat{\Phi}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}-\hat{\Phi}^{\mu,\lambda\nu}-\hat{\Phi}^{\nu,\lambda\mu})
and using the antisymmetry
\hat{\Phi}^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}=-\hat{\Phi}^{\alpha,\gamma\beta}
in the last pair, one finds after a few lines

-\hat{X}^{\mu,\lambda\nu}+\hat{X}^{\nu,\lambda\mu}\,=\,\hat{\Phi}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}\,,

(36)

so that the residual vanishes identically,
\mathcal{R}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}=0
. The shift of the angular momentum density is therefore a pure total derivative. Integrating (
34
) over
\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}
, the total derivative becomes a boundary integral on
S^{2}_{\infty}
that vanishes by the same fall-off argument as for
\hat{P}^{\nu}
. Hence

\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{J}^{\mu\nu}\,=\,0\,.

(37)

Combining (
33
) and (
37

31

\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\rm GE}\,=\,b_{\nu}\cdot 0-\xi\cdot 0-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\varpi_{\nu\rho}\cdot 0\,=\,0\,,

(38)

which vanishes identically (and in particular equals
0\cdot\mathbf{1}
) for every
\Phi\in G_{\mathrm{PGT}}
. By the definition (
21
) we conclude

H_{\mathrm{univ}}\bigl[\beta_{\rm Killing},\,\Omega=\varpi\bigr]\,=\,G_{\mathrm{PGT}}\,.

(39)

We summarize the result as the following.

Proposition.

Let the freeze-out hypersurface
\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}
be diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and assume that the pseudo-gauge generators
\hat{\Phi}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}
decay faster than

\beta^{\mu}(x)=b^{\mu}+\varpi^{\mu}{}_{\nu}x^{\nu}
with constant antisymmetric
\varpi_{\mu\nu}

\Omega_{ab}=\varpi_{ab}

H_{\mathrm{univ}}=G_{\mathrm{PGT}}
. Equivalently, every Cooper–Frye observable map
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{\mathcal{O}}
descends in this limit all the way to the thermodynamic moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, the fiber
G_{\mathrm{PGT}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}
degenerates to a point, and pseudo-gauge ambiguity has no physical consequence.

The proposition makes precise the long-standing observation that the total Poincaré charges are PGT-invariant up to boundary terms

13

\hat{J}^{ab}

3.2
The Belinfante–canonical test

For a Dirac field, the canonical spin tensor is totally antisymmetric in its three indices,
\hat{S}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{can}}=-\hat{S}^{\mu,\lambda\nu}_{\mathrm{can}}=-\hat{S}^{\lambda,\nu\mu}_{\mathrm{can}}
. The Belinfante symmetrization procedure
16
;
46
;
28
replaces the canonical pair
(\hat{T}_{\mathrm{can}},\hat{S}_{\mathrm{can}})
by the symmetric Belinfante pair
(\hat{T}_{\mathrm{Bel}},0)
and corresponds to the PGT generator

\hat{\Phi}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}\;=\;\hat{S}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\hat{S}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{Bel}}=0\,.

(40)

The Belinfante-symmetrized tensor combination is

\hat{X}^{\lambda\mu\nu}\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\hat{S}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}-\hat{S}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu,\lambda\nu}-\hat{S}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\nu,\lambda\mu}\bigr)\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,\hat{S}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}\,,

(41)

where the second equality uses total antisymmetry of
\hat{S}_{\mathrm{can}}
, so that the second and third terms cancel against each other. Substituting (
40

10
), the canonical-to-Belinfante insertion is

\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}\;=\;\int_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}}d\Sigma_{\mu}\!\left[\beta_{\nu}\,\partial_{\lambda}\hat{X}^{\lambda\mu\nu}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Omega_{ab}\hat{S}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu,ab}\right]\,.

(42)

The first piece can be integrated by parts. On a planar
\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}

\int_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}}d\Sigma_{\mu}\,\beta_{\nu}\,\partial_{\lambda}\hat{X}^{\lambda\mu\nu}\;=\;-\int_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}}d\Sigma_{\mu}\,(\partial_{\lambda}\beta_{\nu})\,\hat{X}^{\lambda\mu\nu}

(43)

modulo edge terms that vanish for fields with appropriate fall-off at spatial infinity. Decomposing
\partial_{\lambda}\beta_{\nu}=\xi_{\lambda\nu}-\varpi_{\lambda\nu}
into the symmetric thermal shear

Proposition.

For a Dirac spin fluid on a planar freeze-out surface, ignoring edge contributions, the canonical-to-Belinfante PGT

4
Fibration Structure of Equivalence Relations

Two spaces are involved in the chain (
5
) and must be carefully distinguished. The first is the space of thermodynamic Lagrange multipliers:

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}\;:=\;\bigl\{\,(\beta^{\mu}(x),\,\Omega_{ab}(x),\,\xi(x))\;\text{on}\;\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}\,\bigr\}\,.

(47)

These parameterize the local-equilibrium state and are held fixed across pseudo-gauge representatives in the comparison scheme adopted here
6
. The second is the universal pseudo-gauge quotient of freeze-out data,

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}\;:=\;R_{\mathrm{FO}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}\,,

(48)

which collapses pseudo-gauge representatives related by the universal stabilizer (
21
). At fixed thermodynamic state
(\beta,\Omega,\xi)
, different pseudo-gauge choices generically give different density operators, and hence different physical observables
18
: this is precisely the statement that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}
are not the same object. Note that
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}=R_{\mathrm{FO}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}
removes only pseudo-gauge redundancies. It does not quotient by other possible parametrization redundancies of the freeze-out description, such as reparametrizations of
\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}
, local tetrad-frame choices, hydrodynamic field redefinitions, or operator-basis identities that leave
\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}
unchanged up to a c-number. Thus
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}
is the universal PGT quotient on which admissible Cooper–Frye observables are well-defined, but it is not asserted to be the minimal space of physically distinct local-equilibrium density operators.

It is convenient to fix terminology here, as it will be used throughout the rest of the paper. By a
physical state
on
\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}
we mean the full local-equilibrium specification needed to determine
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}
, i.e., a point
[R]\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}
rather than only a point of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}
. The thermal Lagrange multipliers
(\beta,\Omega,\xi)
alone do not fix
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}
: the local-equilibrium construction (
7
) additionally requires committing to a pseudo-gauge representative for the response operators that enter
\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}
. The non-triviality of the fibration structure developed below is precisely the statement that this pseudo-gauge information is needed
beyond
the thermodynamic data to fix observable values; in the language of the projection
\pi
defined just below,
\pi([R])\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}
recovers the thermal component of the physical state while the fiber direction records its residual pseudo-gauge component.

There is a natural projection

\pi:\;\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}

(49)

obtained by forgetting the residual pseudo-gauge class and remembering only the Lagrange multipliers. In the pseudo-gauge orbit sector of
R_{\mathrm{FO}}
considered here,
\pi
is a quotient projection with fiber

F_{(\beta,\Omega,\xi)}\;=\;G_{\mathrm{PGT}}\big/H_{\mathrm{univ}}[\beta,\Omega,\xi]\,,

(50)

the coset space of pseudo-gauge transformations modulo those that leave the density operator invariant at the given thermodynamic state. Equivalently, for each set of thermal parameters in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, this fiber enumerates the physically inequivalent pseudo-gauge representatives in
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}
over that point, since within a single
H_{\mathrm{univ}}
-orbit all representatives produce identical
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}
via (
48
). Because
H_{\mathrm{univ}}
depends on
(\beta,\Omega,\xi)
through (
22
), the fibration is non-trivial, with the fiber varying with the base point. The pseudo-gauge part of the fibration is

G_{\mathrm{PGT}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}[\beta,\Omega,\xi]\;\hookrightarrow\;\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}\;\xrightarrow{\;\pi\;}\;\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}\,.

(51)

Combining with the Cooper–Frye factorization (
25
), we arrive at the following structure:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&&G_{\mathrm{PGT}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}[\beta,\Omega,\xi]\\[5.0pt]
&&\big\downarrow\\[5.0pt]
R_{\mathrm{FO}}&\xrightarrow{\;q\;}&\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}&\;\xrightarrow{\;\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{\mathcal{O}}\;}&\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{O}}\\[5.0pt]
&&\big\downarrow\pi\\[5.0pt]
&&\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}\end{array}

(52)

The induced Cooper–Frye map
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{\mathcal{O}}
factors through the horizontal arrow
q
, but it need not descend through the vertical projection
\pi
. The quotient map
q
is universal, while the induced readout
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{\mathcal{O}}
depends on the chosen observable
\mathcal{O}
; the vertical projection
\pi:\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}
does not. The failure of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{\mathcal{O}}
to factor through

existed for the polarization observable, all observables of that type would be functions of

alone, and the canonical and Belinfante pseudo-gauges would give identical predictions for

Let us spell out the relation between the original response space
R_{\mathrm{FO}}
and the fiber
G_{\mathrm{PGT}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}[\beta,\Omega,\xi]

contains the full freeze-out surface data before quotienting: the thermodynamic Lagrange multipliers together with a choice of pseudo-gauge representative for the response operators entering

(53)

Choosing one representative

gives an orbit map

\alpha_{r_{b}}:\;G_{\mathrm{PGT}}\longrightarrow R_{\mathrm{FO},b},\qquad\Phi\longmapsto\Phi\cdot r_{b},

(54)

where
\Phi\cdot r_{b}
denotes the response data obtained from
r_{b}

Consequently the orbit map descends to a map of coset spaces

\bar{\alpha}_{r_{b}}:\;G_{\mathrm{PGT}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}[b]\longrightarrow\pi^{-1}(b),\qquad[\Phi]\longmapsto q(\Phi\cdot r_{b}).

When the fixed-

5.3
Weyl-Anomaly-Induced Current as a Base Response

As a concrete worked example of the base/fiber classification of section
4
, we analyze the Weyl-anomaly-induced transport current derived in
55
. That result obtains a vector current in an accelerated relativistic fluid from the boundary QFT on a Rindler horizon. We show that this current is a base observable in the sense of (
60
) and identify the criterion under which a local-equilibrium extension could promote it to a fiber observable.

The derivation of
55
is performed at global equilibrium:
\beta^{\mu}=u^{\mu}/T
is a Killing field, the thermal vorticity is constant, and the electromagnetic background satisfies the equilibrium constraints. In their conventions the anomaly-induced contribution is

j_{\rm W}^{\mu}=-4C_{\rm W}\,F^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}\,,

(100)

where
C_{\rm W}
is the Weyl-anomaly coefficient,
F_{\mu\nu}
is the external electromagnetic field strength, and
a^{\mu}=u^{\lambda}\nabla_{\lambda}u^{\mu}
is the fluid acceleration. At global equilibrium the velocity and temperature are determined by the Killing field
\beta^{\mu}=u^{\mu}/T
, so
a^{\mu}
is a functional of the same thermodynamic source data; for example
u^{\mu}=\beta^{\mu}/\sqrt{\beta^{2}}
and
T=(\beta^{2})^{-1/2}
in our mostly-minus convention. Thus the anomaly-induced current defines a map

\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{\rm W}:\;\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}^{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{j}\,,\qquad(\beta,\Omega,\xi;A_{\mu},C_{\rm W})\longmapsto-4C_{\rm W}F^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}\,,

(101)

where
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}^{A}
denotes the thermodynamic base enlarged by the external electromagnetic source and by the anomaly coefficient. The spin potential
\Omega
is included in the displayed domain only to match the rest of the paper; the leading Weyl-anomaly current in (
100
) does not depend on it.

This immediately gives the fibration statement. Standard pseudo-gauge transformations act on the stress-spin complex, not on the electromagnetic source or on the thermodynamic multipliers:

\delta_{\Phi}A_{\mu}=0,\qquad\delta_{\Phi}F_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad\delta_{\Phi}\beta^{\mu}=\delta_{\Phi}\xi=\delta_{\Phi}\Omega_{ab}=0\,.

(102)

Consequently the pullback of (
101
) to
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}
is constant along the pseudo-gauge fiber,

D\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{\rm W}(V_{\Phi})=0\qquad\text{for every }V_{\Phi}\in T\bigl(G_{\mathrm{PGT}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}[\beta,\Omega,\xi]\bigr)\,.

(103)

Equivalently,

\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{\rm W}=\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{\rm W}\circ\pi_{A}\,,

(104)

with
\pi_{A}:\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}^{A}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}^{A}
the source-enlarged thermal projection. This is precisely the definition of a base observable. At the global-equilibrium point used in
55
, section
3.1
gives the stronger statement
H_{\mathrm{univ}}=G_{\mathrm{PGT}}
, so the pseudo-gauge fiber itself degenerates to a point.

Relaxing global equilibrium to local equilibrium does not by itself make (
100
) a fiber observable. If it is promoted only as a local constitutive term built from
(\beta,A_{\mu},C_{\rm W})
, it remains a single-valued function on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}^{A}
. What local equilibrium can do is open non-trivial pseudo-gauge fibers in the density operator. The full vector-current expectation value may then have the schematic form

\langle\hat{j}^{\mu}\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}=j_{\rm W}^{\mu}[\beta,A]+j_{\rm rem}^{\mu}[\beta,\Omega,\xi;\mathrm{PG}]+\cdots\,,

(105)

where the first term is the anomaly-fixed base contribution and
j_{\rm rem}^{\mu}
denotes possible local-equilibrium dressing. The current becomes fiber-sensitive exactly when this remainder has a non-zero vertical derivative. In the BKM language of section
2
, the criterion is

\delta_{\Phi}\langle\hat{j}^{\mu}\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}=-\bigl\langle\hat{j}^{\mu};\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}\bigr\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\neq 0\qquad\text{for some }\Phi\notin H_{\mathrm{univ}}[\beta,\Omega,\xi]\,.

(106)

For the canonical-to-Belinfante direction this becomes, schematically,

(\Omega-\varpi)_{ab}\int_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}}d\Sigma_{\lambda}\,\bigl\langle\hat{j}^{\mu}(x);\hat{S}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\lambda,ab}(y)\bigr\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\neq 0\,,

(107)

up to the geometric and boundary terms displayed in (
10

(\beta,A_{\mu},C_{\rm W})
, the current remains base even away from global equilibrium. If it is non-zero, the correction is a fiber observable. Thus a current derived from a boundary or horizon construction need not be pseudo-gauge sensitive: the protected Weyl-anomaly coefficient and the global-equilibrium current (
100

106

6
Discussion

We have given a geometric formulation of the pseudo-gauge dependence of Cooper–Frye observables at freeze-out. The central object is the universal stabilizer
H_{\mathrm{univ}}
defined by the condition
\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}=c_{\Phi}\mathbf{1}
, which is necessary and sufficient for any observable computed from
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}
to be pseudo-gauge invariant. For each chosen observable
\mathcal{O}
, the Cooper–Frye readout
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
factors exactly through the quotient
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}=R_{\mathrm{FO}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}
, while the residual quotient is fibered over the space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}
of thermodynamic Lagrange multipliers with state-dependent fiber
G_{\mathrm{PGT}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}[\beta,\Omega,\xi]
. The non-trivial fiber is the obstruction underlying the Buzzegoli effect; its dimension upper-bounds the number of independent PG-sensitive observables and supplies a target for future searches beyond the canonical–Belinfante comparison.

The two-class classification of observables, base observables on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{phys}}
versus fiber observables on
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{FO}}
, gives a sharp criterion for which polarization moments are intrinsically pseudo-gauge robust and which carry residual pseudo-gauge dependence. The cross-observable consistency relations (
75
) provide multi-observable self-consistency tests that are absent from standard analyses and could be tested by simultaneous measurement of
\Lambda
hyperon polarization
3
;
2
and
\phi
-meson spin alignment
1
in the same kinematic bins. They also give a structural interpretation of the mechanism proposed in
51
: the large
\rho_{00}^{\phi}-1/3
signal need not contradict a narrow vorticity-based description of
\Lambda
polarization, but rather points to additional freeze-out response data that are approximately invisible to the
\Lambda
spin-vector observable and visible to the
\phi
-meson tensor-alignment observable.

For a fixed finite pseudo-gauge family and a chosen observable, one may still define an operational PG spread, for example

\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}}^{(\mathrm{can},\mathrm{Bel})}([R]):=\left\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{can}}([R])-\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{Bel}}([R])\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{O}}}\,.

(108)

This quantity vanishes at global equilibrium and is a useful indication that the chosen observable probes a residual fiber direction. It should not, however, be read as an equivalence criterion for equilibrium: it may vanish away from global equilibrium if the observable is insensitive to the chosen PG direction. The invariant statement is the stabilizer condition itself; quantities such as (
108
) are model- and observable-dependent probes of that geometry.

Several directions extend the present results. First, the full canonical-to-Belinfante computation on a curved
\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}
, rather than the planar idealization of section
3.2
, introduces extrinsic-curvature terms that modify the equilibrium relation (
46
). The recent analysis of curved freeze-out surfaces and tensor spin alignment
49
suggests these geometric corrections are quantitatively relevant for the
\rho_{00}
observable. Second, the explicit form of the fiber
G_{\mathrm{PGT}}/H_{\mathrm{univ}}
for general PGT generators
\Phi^{\lambda,\mu\nu}
(including a non-trivial spin superpotential
Z^{ab,\mu\rho}

53
) would identify additional PG-sensitive observables beyond the canonical-Belinfante one already used by
18
. Third, pairing this framework with realistic RSH simulations
23
;
24
;
30
would yield numerical predictions for family-restricted PG spreads such as (
108
) in actual collision kinematics and could turn the cross-observable consistency relations into quantitative experimental tests.

A further natural direction is the specialization to Bjorken-symmetric flow
17
, where one can compare the universal stabilizer condition (
22
) with the residual pseudo-gauge condition
\partial_{\lambda}\Phi^{\lambda,\mu\nu}=0
identified by
22
as the symmetric-to-symmetric condition. The two are logically distinct—(
22

\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}
, whereas Drogosz’s is a local spacetime constraint—and their relationship on a constant-proper-time hypersurface remains to be clarified.

Derivation of the density-operator variation.

Let

\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}(\epsilon)=\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}+\epsilon\,\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}+O(\epsilon^{2})\,,\qquad\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}(\epsilon)=\frac{e^{-\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}(\epsilon)}}{Z_{\mathrm{LE}}(\epsilon)}\,,\qquad Z_{\mathrm{LE}}(\epsilon)=\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}(\epsilon)}\,,

(109)

where
\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}
is the PGT insertion displayed in (
10

\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}
need not commute with
\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}
, the first variation of the exponential is given by the Duhamel formula

\delta e^{-\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}}=-\int_{0}^{1}ds\;e^{-(1-s)\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}}\left(\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}\right)e^{-s\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}}\,.

(110)

Dividing by
Z_{\mathrm{LE}}
and using
e^{-a\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}}=Z_{\mathrm{LE}}^{a}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\,a}
gives

\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{LE}}}\,\delta e^{-\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}}=-\int_{0}^{1}ds\;\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\,1-s}\left(\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}\right)\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\,s}\,.

(111)

The variation of the partition function is, by cyclicity of the trace,

\delta Z_{\mathrm{LE}}=\mathrm{Tr}\,\delta e^{-\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}}=-Z_{\mathrm{LE}}\,\langle\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\,,\qquad\delta\!\left(\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{LE}}}\right)=\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{LE}}}\,\langle\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\,.

(112)

Therefore

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{LE}}}\,\delta e^{-\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}}+\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}\,\langle\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}ds\;\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\,1-s}\left(\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}-\langle\Delta_{\Phi}\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\right)\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\,s}\,,

(113)

which is (
11
). The subtraction of

The Bogoliubov–Kubo–Mori inner product.

For a density operator
\hat{\rho}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and operators
X,Y
, the normalized BKM inner product is

(X,Y)_{\rho}\;:=\;\int_{0}^{1}ds\;\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\rho^{1-s}X^{\dagger}\rho^{s}Y\right)\,.

(114)

On the algebra of bounded operators on a finite-dimensional
\mathcal{H}
, this defines a positive-definite sesquilinear form
44
;
45
;
4
. The connected BKM correlator with respect to the local-equilibrium state
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}=e^{-\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}}/Z_{\mathrm{LE}}
that we use throughout the main text is

\langle X;Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\;:=\;(X,Y)_{\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}}\,-\,\langle X\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\langle Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}ds\;\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\,1-s}X^{\dagger}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\,s}Y\right)\,-\,\langle X\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\langle Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\,.

(115)

A direct computation, using
\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{1-s}\mathbf{1}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{s}Y)=\langle Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}
, shows that the connected form equals the uncentered form evaluated on
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}
-centered operators:

\langle X;Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\;=\;(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})_{\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}}\,,\qquad\tilde{X}:=X-\langle X\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\mathbf{1}\,,\qquad\tilde{Y}:=Y-\langle Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\mathbf{1}\,.

(116)

Non-degeneracy on hermitian operators modulo
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1}
.

The essential input to the necessity direction of (
24
) is the statement:
if

is hermitian and
\langle X;Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}=0
for every hermitian

, then

.
The hermiticity assumption on

is automatically satisfied in our application, since

is the variation of the hermitian operator
\hat{K}_{\mathrm{LE}}
under a real-valued PGT parameter

We give the proof in two steps. First, the BKM form
(X,X)_{\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}}
is non-negative on every operator

(X,X)_{\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}}\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}ds\;\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\,1-s}\,X^{\dagger}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{\,s}\,X\right)\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}ds\;\bigl\|\,\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{s/2}\,X\,\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}^{(1-s)/2}\,\bigr\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\;\geq\;0\,,

(117)

where

denotes the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Equality holds if and only if the integrand vanishes for almost all

s
by continuity, which for invertible
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}}
is therefore strictly positive-definite.

Second, by (

\langle X;Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}=(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})_{\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}}
where
\tilde{X}=X-\langle X\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\mathbf{1}

are the
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}

Y
are. We are given that
\langle X;Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}=0
for every hermitian
X
, which is equivalent to

(\tilde{X},\,\tilde{Y})_{\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}}\;=\;0\qquad\text{for every hermitian }\tilde{X}\text{ with }\langle\tilde{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}=0\,.

(118)

Since
\tilde{Y}
itself satisfies
\langle\tilde{Y}\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}=\langle Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}-\langle Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}=0
, the operator
\tilde{Y}
lies in the same subspace over which
\tilde{X}
is taken. We are therefore free to choose
\tilde{X}=\tilde{Y}
, giving

(\tilde{Y},\,\tilde{Y})_{\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LE}}}\;=\;0\,.

(119)

By positive-definiteness,
\tilde{Y}=0
, hence
Y=\langle Y\rangle_{\mathrm{LE}}\,\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{C}\,\mathbf{1}
. This is the precise non-degeneracy statement invoked in the proof of the universal stabilizer theorem.

Pseudo-gauge transformation conventions.

For completeness we record the standard pseudo-gauge transformation conventions following
28
;
9
;
53
;
22
. The most general PGT is parameterized by a rank-3 generator
\Phi^{\lambda,\mu\nu}
antisymmetric in its last pair,
\Phi^{\lambda,\mu\nu}=-\Phi^{\lambda,\nu\mu}
, together with an optional rank-4 superpotential
Z^{ab,\mu\rho}=-Z^{ba,\mu\rho}=-Z^{ab,\rho\mu}
. The transformation rules on the stress-energy and spin tensors are

\displaystyle T^{\prime\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\;=\;T^{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\lambda}X^{\lambda\mu\nu}\,,\qquad X^{\lambda\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\Phi^{\lambda,\mu\nu}-\Phi^{\mu,\lambda\nu}-\Phi^{\nu,\lambda\mu}\bigr)\,,

(120)

\displaystyle S^{\prime\lambda,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\;=\;S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}-\Phi^{\lambda,\mu\nu}+\partial_{\rho}Z^{\mu\nu,\lambda\rho}\,.

(121)

The combined transformation preserves the total angular momentum current
J^{\lambda,\mu\nu}=x^{\mu}T^{\lambda\nu}-x^{\nu}T^{\lambda\mu}+S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}
up to total derivatives, and the conserved baryon current
j^{\mu}
is invariant. In the planar
\Sigma_{\mathrm{FO}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
idealization used in the main text, the
Z
-superpotential term vanishes by antisymmetry of
Z^{ab,\mu\rho}
in its last pair and the surface integration measure
d\Sigma_{\mu}=n_{\mu}d^{3}\sigma
on a constant-time surface.
