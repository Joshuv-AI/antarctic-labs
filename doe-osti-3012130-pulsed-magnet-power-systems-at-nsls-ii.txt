 INTRODUCTION
 The NSLS-II booster injection pulsed power system pro-
vides injection of electrons from the linac pre-injector to
the booster tunnel at a nominal energy of 170 MeV. The
electron beam is accelerated in the booster tunnel by RF
systems to raise their energy to 3 GeV. The booster operates
in two modes: "intial fill" filling the storage ring from zero
current and continuously ramping at 1 Hz repetition rate
for upto 60 minutes, or "top-off" mode generating a bunch
train to maintain storage ring charge current. Depending on
the mode of operation, the extraction pulsed power system
periodically extracts the beam from booster tunnel and the
storage injection pulsed power system injects the beam into
the storage ring through a transfer line with minimal loss.

 OVERVIEW OF PULSED POWER
 SYSTEMS
 The pulsed power systems mainly comprise a combination
of septum, kicker magnets, energized by their own power
supplies or control unit and a pulser unit, which is frequently
used to inject and extract beams from each stage. Septum
magnet pulsed power systems are generally DC or slow AC
pulse used to provide a separate trajectory for the particle
beam during injection or extraction [1]. The kicker magnet
pulsed power systems produce rectangular pulses with fast
rise/fall times which provide relatively small deflection to
the beam orbit [2]. Additionally, there are bump magnet
pulsed power supply systems which are slow and provide
a small distortion to beam orbit. Figure 1 shows the layout

Pulser Unit - Septum and Bump
 The septum and bump pulser unit is based on a LC reso-
nant discharge, a thyristor, and a capacitor energy recovery
network on the negative current pulse as shown in Figure 3.
A capacitor charging power supply(CCPS), which is located
in the controls rack, is used to charge the capacitor to the
operating voltage value. The CCPS is protected from the
negative voltage swing of the capacitor bank using a parallel
diode and a series resistor is used to eliminate impact of
surge current from the power supply. The thyristor closes
upon receiving the trigger to its gate through a gate drive
board in the pulser unit which discharges the stored capacitor
energy to the inductive magnet. As the current goes negative,
an anti-parallel thyristor is triggered automatically, allowing
