Transition Crossing with Reduced Number of Jump Quadrupoles

 H. Lovelace III

                                          September 11, 2026

 Abstract
The Electron Ion Collider (EIC) Hadron Storage Ring (HSR) is the reconstructed Relativistic Heavy Ion
Collider (RHIC) where arcs of the accelerator will not change and all but one insertion region (IR) will be
slightly modified during commissioning. The experimental insertion region, IR6, will break the symmetry
of the γ-transition crossing scheme that was used in RHIC by reducing the number of effective compensa-
tion jump quadrupoles by four, eight, and with an additional experiment twelve. The reduction of jump
quadrupoles during transition may cause unwanted emittance growth, additional beam loss, or prevention
of crossing transition in the HSR. This tech note shows the results of the Accelerator Physics Experiment
2310 (APEX2310) using the 197 Au+79 ramp where four then eight, Q family, compensation γ-transition
quadrupoles located in the straight sections from a total 24 were turned off.

The results show that transition was crossed with 6% beam loss and minimal emittance growth for the
G24Q20 and G24Q16 configurations with 111 bunches. The metric for success is to show beam loss less than
10% and the minimal emittance growth shown in the experiment. These results add to the feasibility of effi-
cient transition crossing with unequal arc, G-family, and Q-family jump quadrupoles in the HSR. With the
loss of additional Q-family jump quadrupoles due to the addition of a second detector, alternative methods
of crossing transition will need to be explored.

1 Introduction
 The Electron Ion Collider (EIC) is currently under design and construction on the campus of Brookhaven
National Laboratory [1]. The EIC is composed of the new Electron Storage Ring, with its new injectors and
the modified Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) [2] ring serving as the new Hadron Storage Ring (HSR)
with its series of injectors. Figure 1 is of the EIC with the electron Proton Ion Collider (ePIC) detector
hosted in the 6 o’clock region of the EIC and a future detector to be designed and built in the 12 o’clock
region.
 The new HSR is similar to the RHIC yellow ring with modifications to the interaction regions where
the low/medium energy electron cooling and the experimental detector will be located. These modifications
reduce the number of Q-family jump quadrupoles which compensate for the tune shift and β-Twiss/η-
dispersion waves generated during the transition jump [3, 4]. The latest commissioning lattice design has
4 fewer jump quadrupoles than RHIC, where the ultimate design will have 8 fewer. Including a second
detector will mean that alternative methods of crossing transition will be needed [5, 6, 7]. If the number of
compensation jump quadrupoles falls below 16 the compensation quadrupoles will not have enough strength
to match the tunes before and after the transition jump.

2 Theory
 Besides the longitudinal phase jump that is needed at transition [8], the dispersion of the lattice is
distorted to change the value of the lattice γ-transition, γT to allow a transverse adiabatic jump of the γT . The
First Order Matched (FOM) scheme is a two “knob” method which uses a pair of jump quadrupole families,
G and Q, that becomes excited to perturb the dispersion of the lattice while simultaneously mitigating the
tune shift and distorted optics. Figure 2 shows the Twiss β, dispersion η, and layout of a RHIC sextant.
The G-family jump quadrupoles are located in the dispersive arcs while the Q-family jump quadrupoles are
hosted within the straight sections where dispersion is at a minimum. For RHIC, each sextant has 8 jump
quadrupoles, 4 in the arcs and 2 pairs of doublets in the straight sections. There is a total of 48 jump
quadrupoles in the ring, evenly matched into the G and Q families (G24Q24).

4 Accelerator Physics Experiment
 The Accelerator Physics Experiment (APEX) sessions were conducted on 3 separate days, August 6
(14 Ramps), October 15 (16 Ramps), and November 5 (8 Ramps) in 2025. For the experiments, marginal
changes were made to the strengths of the jump quadrupoles, amplitude of the voltages and timing [15]
of the jump quadrupoles polarity flip to force, at transition, all jump quadrupoles strengths to cross zero
at the same time. For the G24Q20 configuration, the yi2-qgt-ps power supply was turned off and for the
G24Q16 configuration the yi2-qgt-ps and the yo4-qgt-ps power supplies were turned off. Each power supply
energizes 4 Q-family jump quadrupoles. Figure 5 shows the integrated strengths of the jump quadrupoles
from their maximum (minimum) to the minimum (maximum) strengths. The maximum absolute strength
of the jump quadrupoles is 0.0078 m−1 . The crossing point is the moment of transition in the lattice of
the energy ramping accelerator where the longitudinal phase jump occurs. There are no ramp chromaticity
measurements for any ramp due to time constraints and slight modifications to the quadrupole and sextupole
strengths based on beam stability occurred during the sessions.

4.3 Beam Loss and Emittance Growth Mechanisms
 During transition in all configuration beam loss occurs. A possible reason for the beam loss is that the
beam capture during the longitudinal phase jump [16] is not as efficient for the different configurations.
Transition is a lattice parameter that is subject to change based on the optics and dispersion of the lattice.
As the jump quadrupoles are excited the optics deviate which during the jump causes transition to change
value and the time of transition on the order of milliseconds from one configuration to the next. Another
mechanism for beam loss and emittance growth is the chromaticity control through transition [17]. RHIC
has no normal operational method to measure the chromaticity [17] so that the value pre- and post-transition
remained unknown. The ramp efficiency of RHIC through transition after years of operation is near 98% to
99% with minimum emittance growth [18].

5 Conclusion
 The APEX2310 session took place over three days with results that show that transition with a reduced
number of jump quadrupoles is feasible for the operation of the EIC hadron storage ring. The inclusion
of a second detector within the EIC will make the current scheme for transition crossing infeasible without
additional upgrades to the jump quadrupole system. If the upgrade to the jump quadrupole system is not
possible, alternative methods of crossing transition such as the use of resonance islands will need to be further
explored.

 16

 During the sessions, it was observed that the tune feedback had an adverse effect on transition crossing
with no clear reason as to the cause. Further investigation led by the possibility tune mismatch theory, may
provide a reason to update the feedback systems to accommodate the asymmetric transition jump scheme.
From the experiments, the G24Q20 configuration beam loss during transition was 2% with 7.74% horizontal
emittance growth at a bunch intensity of 1.42×109 ions per bunch with 56 bunches. With 111 bunches the
beam loss was 2% and horizontal emittance growth was 10.10% for 1.5×109 ions per bunch. The G24Q16
configuration beam loss and horizontal emittance growth for 111 bunches at 2.2×109 were 6% and -11.54%.
