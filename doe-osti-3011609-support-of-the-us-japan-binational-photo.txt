Final Technical Report

Title: Support of the US-Japan Binational Photosynthesis Workshop
Award number: DE-SC0024720

Conference title: US-Japan Binational Photosynthesis Workshop (UJBPW)
Venue: Arizona State University (ASU), Tempe campus
Dates: November 6-8, 2023
Organizer: Kevin Redding (ASU)
Co-organizers: Graham Peers (Colorado State Univ.), Chihiro Azai (Chuo Univ., Tokyo)

Description: This workshop was held over the course of 3 days in early November of 2023 on
the campus of Arizona State University. Its primary goal was to facilitate interaction and productive
collaboration between scientists based in Japan and the U.S. who work on the mechanisms of
energy transduction carried out by photosynthetic organisms. Due to boundaries of geography
and culture, communication and collaboration between US and Japanese scientists working in
the field has not always been optimal. The UJBPW overcame these boundaries by holding a
relatively small conference where people could discuss results and ideas freely. The emphasis
was on scientific diversity and promotion of younger scientists and it gave graduate students the
opportunity to meet the people whose papers they have been reading and perhaps find a
postdoctoral position, which is a great way to forge scientific links across national boundaries.

Conference Venue
The Ballroom on the second floor of the Old Main building on the Tempe campus of ASU was
used for the talks, posters and lunches. It is located in the heart of ASU and there are many
amenities in convenient walking distance. Use of this venue greatly lowered the cost of the
workshop.

Registration and accommodations
We have set the registration costs for attendees were set thusly:
• Faculty/PI – $400 (early), $600 (regular)
• Students and Postdocs – $300 (early), $500 (regular)
Lists of nearby hotels within walking distance of campus and their price ranges were provided to
all attendees. We purposefully chose dates for the workshop in which there are no conflicts with
major sporting events or other events on campus or in the local area, so prices were relatively
inexpensive on the conference days.

Use of Funds Requested from the DOE
We received $7500 from DOE to partially cover the costs of US-based graduate students,
postdocs, and early career faculty to participate in the UJBPW. They were disbursed in the
following ways:
• $4200: full reimbursement of registration fees for 1 postdoc and 9 graduate students
• $1100: partial reimbursement of registration fees for 4 early career faculty
• $2200: partial reimbursement of travel costs for 1 postdoc and 2 graduate students

Selection of awardees and distribution of awards
All postdocs and graduate students were eligible to receive the registration awards. Participants
applying for support will need to submit the following application material:
1) An abstract they intended to submit to the meeting.
2) A brief paragraph stating why participation in the meeting will benefit their career.
3) An email from their primary advisor including a statement on available funds.
In the end, every US-based graduate student and postdoc was fully supported for registration
costs. Every US-based graduate student and postdoc traveling from outside Arizona with
financial need was partially supported for travel costs. Most of the US-based early career faculty
(our lowest priority) were partially supported for registration costs.
