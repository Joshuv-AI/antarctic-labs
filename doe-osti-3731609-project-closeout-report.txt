1. Project Closeout Scope
Following directions issued by ARPA-E, the SCALEUP project is required to end on or before November 10, 2025.
This termination precedes the originally scheduled end date of September 29, 2026. The Front End Engineering
Desing (FEED) level scope of this project started on September 29, 2023 with a planned three (3) year duration.
Due to the premature termination, the level of design completion varies across engineering disciplines,
Consequently, the status of deliverables and scope progress differs by task and by discipline.
This report aims to document only those activities, or parts of activities, that remain incomplete at the time of
project shutdown. All completed work, including results, data and knowledge gained to date, are captured in the
list of agreed deliverables. These are referenced through the truncated master deliverable registers (MDR’s) of
the tension leg platform (TLP) and semisubmersible (SEMI) concepts, provided in Appendix A and Appendix B,
respectively. The combination of this report and the agreed deliverables will aid future continuation and
development of the project. The technical foundation may be leveraged in subsequent offshore applications,
either in an Oil & Gas or Wind solution.

3. Naval Architecture
1. Designing a TLP or SEMI to be modular significantly enhances its constructability by breaking down the
 complex structure into manageable, transportable, and buildable units. This approach allows major
 components, such as the hull columns, pontoons, and box girders, to be fabricated concurrently in different
 yards or locations globally, thus optimizing resources and accelerating the overall schedule. Modularization
 also simplifies the lifting and integration phase at the final assembly site. The current design of the TLP is
 structurally optimized to meet the tower frequencies and global performance characteristics. However, a
 detailed review of the design in terms of constructability and modularity is necessary. Furthermore, pre-
 outfitting modules with piping, electrical systems, and machinery onshore minimizes hazardous and complex
 offshore hook-up and commissioning work, leading to improved quality control, better safety records, and
 ultimately, a substantial reduction in the TLP's total project time and cost. Along with modularization, all
 avenues for pre-outfitting modules should be investigated.
2. Model testing is a critical and indispensable step in the design and ultimate verification of Floating Offshore
 Wind Turbine (FOWT) platforms, providing the essential physical validation that purely numerical approaches
 cannot fully deliver. The necessity stems from the highly complex and multi-physics nature of a floating wind
 system. Unlike a fixed structure, an FOWT involves the continuous, dynamic interaction of three primary load
 sources:
 - Aerodynamic loads from the wind turbine generator (WTG).
 - Hydrodynamic loads from waves, currents, and vessel motion.
 - Restoring loads provided by the stationkeeping (mooring/tendon) system.
 The core importance of model testing is its ability to capture the coupled dynamic response of the system.
 While advanced computer simulations (like those based on tools using FAST or OrcaFlex) are powerful,
 they rely on inherent assumptions and simplifications of the governing physics. Physical experiments,
 typically performed at a scaled ratio in specialized wave tanks or towing basins, are necessary to:
 a. Validate Numerical Models: Testing provides real-world, measurable data (e.g., platform motion,
 tendon/mooring tension, and tower loads) that designers use to calibrate and confirm the accuracy of
 their numerical simulations, significantly reducing uncertainty.
 b. Capture Non-Linear Effects: Model tests are particularly adept at revealing non-linear phenomena such
 as wave slamming, second-order difference-frequency (slow-drift) motions, and viscous damping effects
 that are often difficult or computationally difficult to model accurately in software.
 c. Measure Extreme Loads: Designers must account for the platform’s performance under extreme dynamic
 load cases (e.g., a 100-year storm). The calibration with model tests measured maximum extreme loads
 experienced by the moorings and the platform structure is vital for ensuring structural integrity and
 safety.
 The SCALEUP project was originally planned to include a comprehensive series of model tests aimed at
 calibrating the numerical simulation models. However, due to the project’s early termination, this critical
 task remains incomplete. The current sizing of the TLP and SEMI is based on Kent’s extensive experience
 with model testing of similar oil and gas and floating wind platforms. While this provides a solid foundation
 for preliminary design, it is essential that a dedicated series of model tests, tailored specifically to the
 SCALEUP configurations, be conducted to validate and finalize the design.
3. Effective weight control is a critical aspect of the design and engineering of FOWT platforms, as it directly
 influences platform stability, hydrodynamic performance, and structural integrity. The distribution and
 magnitude of weight across the hull, tower, and turbine components must be carefully managed to maintain
 the desired center of gravity and ensure compliance with stability criteria under operational and extreme
 environmental conditions. Weight estimates must account for structural elements, outfitting systems, and
 allowances for future modifications or equipment additions. During the current phase, preliminary weight
 budgets were established. However, these need to be refined by incorporating inputs from naval architecture,
 mechanical, electrical, and safety disciplines, and vendor inputs. Furthermore, modular construction and pre-
 outfitting strategies necessitate rigorous tracking of weight contributions from individual modules to avoid
 cumulative discrepancies. Ultimately, precise weight control enhances the reliability, safety, and cost-
 effectiveness of the FOWT.

3.1. TLP
A novel synthetic tendon material was proposed during this phase to significantly reduce the mooring footprint
in shallow water environments. This innovation is expected to enhance the performance of tension leg platform,
particularly by simplifying installation procedure in shallow to deep waters. Due to its potential impact on load
distribution and dynamic response, the tendon system's influence on hull and tower/hull structural design
requires thorough evaluation through integrated analyses. Collaboration with vendors was initiated to obtain
critical performance data; however, the project concluded prematurely before reaching FEED-level maturity. As
a result, comprehensive data—such as line strength, fatigue resistance, and long-term creep behavior under
operational and environmental conditions—remains incomplete. This data is essential to validate the reliability of
the tendon system and ensure compliance with design standards, especially given its novel application.

3.2. SEMI
Evaluation of the impact of the novel mooring design: Shallow water mooring systems present unique challenges
in the design and deployment of FOWTs, primarily due to the need to balance structural integrity with spatial
efficiency. Achieving adequate mooring strength is essential to ensure platform stability under dynamic
environmental loads; however, this must be accomplished while minimizing the mooring line scope or anchor
radius. Reducing the anchor radius of individual platforms is critical to limiting the overall site footprint,
particularly when deploying multiple floating units within a constrained area.
A novel mooring system has been proposed in the phase with the objective of significantly reducing the mooring
footprint in shallow water environments. This system is expected to have a substantial influence on the
performance of the SEMI platform, particularly in terms of spatial efficiency and stationkeeping effectiveness.
Given its potential to alter load distributions and dynamic responses, the implications of this mooring
configuration on both the hull and tower/hull structural design must be thoroughly evaluated through future
integrated analyses. The proposed design incorporates synthetic mooring lines, which necessitate extensive
collaboration with vendors to obtain critical performance data, which, due to the premature termination of the
project, has not happened yet. This includes comprehensive characterization of the line strength, fatigue
resistance, and long-term creep behavior under operational and environmental loading conditions that has not
been completed yet. Such data is essential to validate the mooring system's reliability and ensure compliance
with design standards, particularly given the novel application in shallow water environments.

4. Hull Structural
4.1. TLP
Due to the premature termination of the project, several key tasks remain to be completed for the TLP FEED.
These tasks are summarized as follows:
• Hull Strength Analysis: The hull strength analysis should be conducted at two levels: global and local. The
 global strength analysis has been performed using assumed loads based on a previous project. Although
 the assumptions are considered conservative, the analysis should be updated using actual loads derived
 from coupled analyses to validate the structural adequacy. In addition, local finite element analyses (FEA)
 should be performed to verify critical connection details. It is anticipated that four local FEA models will be
 required to cover: typical pontoon, pontoon to column connection, box girder to column connections and
 tower foundation.
• Hull Fatigue analysis: Fatigue adequacy should also be evaluated at both the global and local levels. The
 global fatigue analysis will identify fatigue-critical areas, while local fatigue analyses will determine the
 actual fatigue life of these critical locations to ensure compliance with the required design life and Design
 Fatigue Factor (DFF). Local fatigue analyses are expected for the following key connections: tower
 foundation, top box girder to column connections, pontoon to column connections and tendon porch
 connections.
• Evaluation of the impact of the tower dampers: Model test results have demonstrated the effectiveness of
 the tower dampers. Their impact on both hull and tower structural design should be evaluated to ensure
 proper integration and to assess potential benefits in load reduction or fatigue improvement.
• Optimization of hull structures: Preliminary assessments indicate that the current hull design includes
 certain conservatisms and potential weight margins. Further optimization studies should be performed to
 reduce the structural weight and overall cost while maintaining required strength and fatigue performance.

4.2. SEMI
Due to the premature termination of the project, several key tasks remain to be completed for the SEMI FEED.
These tasks are summarized as follows:
• Hull Strength Analysis: The hull strength assessment should be performed at two levels: global and local.
 The global strength analysis has been conducted using assumed loads based on previous projects.
 Although the assumptions are considered conservative, FEA should be updated using actual loads from
 coupled analyses to validate the structural adequacy. In addition, local finite element analyses should be
 performed to verify the adequacy of key connection details. It is anticipated that four local FEA models will
 be required to cover typical pontoon, pontoon to tripod connection, pontoon to column connections and
 tower foundation.
• Hull Fatigue analysis: Fatigue evaluations should also be performed at both global and local levels to
 confirm the fatigue adequacy of the design. The global fatigue analysis will identify fatigue-critical regions,
 while local fatigue analyses will determine the actual fatigue life at these locations to ensure compliance
 with the design life and appropriate DFF. Local fatigue analyses are expected for the following critical
 connections: tower foundation and tripod to pontoon connections and mooring line foundations.
• Tower fatigue analysis in time domain: A frequency-domain fatigue analysis has been performed to assess
 the tower fatigue. While the current results are believed to be conservative, a time-domain fatigue analysis
 should be carried out to finalize and validate the tower design under realistic loading conditions.

10. Design
10.1. TLP
This section outlines the current status of the three-dimensional model development for the TLP structure. The
following design elements remain incomplete or require further refinement:
• The tower diameter is not updated to the revised outer diameter (OD) of 10,075 mm, as specified in the
 latest design drawings.
• Brackets, including tripping brackets, buckling stiffeners and flange stiffeners may be incomplete, missing
 or the wrong size/shape and layout. These elements serve as place holders only, which is typical for a
 FEED level design.
• Girder end connections are not fully developed at this stage of the project, which is typical for a FEED level
 design.

10.2. SEMI
This section outlines the current status of the three-dimensional model development for the SEMI structure. The
following design elements remain incomplete or require further refinement:
• Model progression stopped at the first configuration of the SEMI. Consequently, the latest SEMI model
 does not match the latest configuration of drawings, those of Configuration 250512.
• Brackets, including tripping brackets, buckling stiffeners and flange stiffeners may be incomplete, missing
 or the wrong size/shape and layout. They are place holders only, which is typical for a FEED level design.
• Box girder and tower support structure is a “shell only” model. No internal components have been
 modeled.
• Bulkheads and the frames (longitudinal, radial and transverse) are shown for general concept only, as the
 compartmentation was not confirmed by the Naval Architecture discipline prior to project termination.
• Model hierarchy, material grades, and descriptions are incomplete.
