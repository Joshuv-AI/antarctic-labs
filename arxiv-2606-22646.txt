Introduction

We consider the problem of finding the norms of embedding operators of the Sobolev space
{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}$}}{}_{2}^{n}[0;1]

in the space
{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}$}}{}_{\infty}^{k}[0;1]
.
By

{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}$}}{}_{p}^{n}[0;1]

(
n\in\mathbb{N}
)
we mean the space consisting of functions
f

all of whose derivatives up to order
n-1

are absolutely continuous
on the closed interval

[0;1]
,
f^{(n)}\in L_{p}[0;1]
,
and the following boundary conditions hold:

f^{(j)}(0)=f^{(j)}(1)=0,\quad j=0,1,\ldots,n-1.

By

\mathcal{H}

we shall denote the space
{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}$}}{}_{2}^{n}[0;1]

with norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left|f^{(n)}(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}.

For a fixed point

x\in(0;1)

and an integer

k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
,
we consider the problem of finding the norm of the functional

f\mapsto f^{(k)}(x)

in the space
\mathcal{H}
:

(1.1)

A_{n,k}(x):=\sup\left\{|f^{(k)}(x)|:\quad\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1\right\}.

The exact embedding constant is defined
by the formula

(1.2)

\Lambda_{n,k}:=\sup_{x\in[0;1]}A_{n,k}(x)

and is equal to the norm of the embedding operator

\mathcal{H}\to{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}$}}{}^{k}_{\infty}[0;1]
.
It is more convenient to calculate the quantities

A^{2}_{n,k}(x)
.
This is related to the fact that the quantities

A^{2}_{n,k}(x)

satisfy recurrence relations
in which the antiderivatives of the Legendre polynomials appear.

Estimates of derivatives of intermediate order
in terms of the norms of Sobolev spaces
arise in many problems.
In addition,
studies of embedding constants often involve
elegant relations with special functions,
in particular, with Legendre polynomials
(see
[
1
]
) and Bessel functions
(see
[
2
]
);
thus, studies of embedding constants are closely related
to function theory.
Certain questions in the embedding theory for Sobolev spaces
are of interest in connection with the spectral theory of operators,
for example, these are closely connected
with the problem of finding smallest eigenvalues for certain differential operators
(see
[
1
]
and the references therein).
The problem of finding exact embedding constants
has a rather long history.
In the present paper,
we are not concerned with questions
related to the embedding of the Sobolev spaces

{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}$}}{}_{p}^{n}[0;1]\hookrightarrow{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}$}}{}_{q}^{k}[0;1]

for arbitrary indices
p
and
q
.
A short survey of the history of such problems
was given in
[
3
]
.

In
[
1
]
, explicit expressions for

A^{2}_{n,0}(x)
,
A^{2}_{n,1}(x)
,
and

A^{2}_{n,2}(x)

for

x\in[-1;1]
were obtained,
their global maxima were found,

\Lambda^{2}_{n,0}
,
\Lambda^{2}_{n,1}
,
and

\Lambda^{2}_{n,2}
were calculated,
and it was conjectured that, for even
k
,
the maximum of

A^{2}_{n,k}

is attained
at the midpoint of a closed interval
and,
for odd

has a minimum
at the midpoint of a closed interval.

In
[
3
]
,
it was proved that

is the point at which the functions

have a local maximum for even

and a local minimum
for odd

.
In addition,

[

We note that, for

At the same time,

The approach in

2.
Recurrence relations for the antiderivatives of the Legendre polynomials.

The Legendre polynomials

P_{n}
,
n=0,1,\ldots

on the closed interval

[0;1]

are given by the Rodrigues formula

(2.1)

P_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{n!}\left((x^{2}-x)^{n}\right)^{(n)}.

The antiderivative of order
m\geqslant 0

is defined by the formula

(2.2)

P_{n}^{(-m)}(x):=\frac{1}{n!}\left((x^{2}-x)^{n}\right)^{(n-m)}.

As a rule,
we will work with Legendre polynomials
and their antiderivatives
using the variable

t:=x^{2}-x
.
At the same time,
according to formulas (
2.1
), (
2.2
),
the derivative will be regarded
as a derivative with respect to the variable
x
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The antiderivatives of shifted Legendre polynomials
satisfy the relation

P_{n+1}^{(k-n+1)}=2(k+1)P_{n}^{(k-n)}+(2x-1)P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}.

Proof.

Since

t=x^{2}-x

and

\dfrac{d}{dx}=(2x-1)\dfrac{d}{dt}
,
by the definition of the antiderivative of the Legendre polynomial,
we have

(2.3)

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}=\left(\dfrac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\right)^{(k+2)}=\left(\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}(2x-1)\right)^{(k+1)}=(2x-1)\left(\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)^{(k+1)}+2(k+1)\left(\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)^{(k)}.

On the other hand,

(2.4)

P_{n}^{(k-n)}=\left(\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)^{(k)},\qquad P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}=\left(\dfrac{t^{n}}{(n)!}\right)^{(k+1)}.

The assertion of the lemma follows from
relations (
2.3
), (
2.4
).
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The function

P_{n}^{(-m)}
,
the antiderivative of order
m\geqslant 0

of the shifted Legendre polynomial
P_{n}
,
satisfies the differential equation

(2.5)

(x-x^{2})y^{\prime\prime}+(2x-1)(m-1)y^{\prime}+(n+m)(n-m+1)y=0.

Proof.

1)
For

m=0
,
the shifted Legendre polynomial
P_{n}

satisfies the equation

(x-x^{2})y^{\prime\prime}-(2x-1)y^{\prime}+n(n+1)y=0.

2)
For the function

P_{n}^{(-m)}

(
m\geqslant 1
),
we shall search for the corresponding differential equation
in the form

(2.6)

(x-x^{2})y^{\prime\prime}+c_{n,m}(2x-1)y^{\prime}+d_{n,m}y=0

and find the recurrence relations
for the coefficients

c_{n,m}

and

d_{n,m}
.
It follows from Sec. 1 that

c_{n,0}=-1
,
d_{n,0}=n(n+1)
.

Differentiating Eq. (
2.6
),
we see that the polynomial

(P_{n}^{(-m)})^{\prime}=P_{n}^{(-(m-1))}

satisfies the equation

(x-x^{2})y^{\prime\prime}+(2x-1)(c_{n,m}-1)y^{\prime}+(d_{n,m}+2c_{n,m})y=0,

whence

c_{n,m}=c_{n,m-1}+1
,
d_{n,m}=d_{n,m-1}-2c_{n,m}
.
Solving these recurrence relations,
we obtain

c_{n,m}=m-1,\qquad d_{n,m}=(n+m)(n-m+1).

∎

Similar formulas for the antiderivatives of the classical Legendre polynomials
were obtained in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For the antiderivatives of shifted Legendre polynomials,
the following relations hold
:

(2.7)

2(2n+1)P_{n}^{(-m)}=P_{n+1}^{(-m+1)}-P_{n-1}^{(-m+1)}.

Proof.

The derivatives are regarded
as derivatives with respect to the variable
x
.
Since
t=x^{2}-x

and

\dfrac{d}{dx}=(2x-1)\dfrac{d}{dt}
,
by the definition of the antiderivative of the Legendre polynomial,
we have

P_{n+1}^{(-m+1)}=\left(\dfrac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\right)^{(n+2-m)}=\dfrac{1}{n!}(t^{n}(2x-1))^{(n+1-m)}=\dfrac{1}{n!}(nt^{n-1}(4t+1)+2t^{n})^{(n-m)}.

After simplifications, we see that

(2.8)

P_{n+1}^{(-m+1)}=2(2n+1)\left(\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)^{(n-m)}+\left(\dfrac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\right)^{(n-m)}.

Similarly, we obtain

(2.9)

P_{n-1}^{(-m+1)}=\left(\dfrac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\right)^{(n-m)},\quad P_{n}^{(-m)}=\left(\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)^{(n-m)}.

The assertion of the lemma follows from relations (
2.8
), (
2.9
).
∎

In particular,
for the antiderivative of order
m=n-k

of the Legendre polynomials
used in the embedding constants,
we obtain

(2.10)

2(2n+1)P_{n}^{(k-n)}=P_{n+1}^{(k-n+1)}-P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}.

3.
Relations between the Legendre polynomials
and the functions
A^{2}_{n,k}

Let us recall
that, in
[
4
]
,
the following relation was obtained:

(3.1)

A^{2}_{n,k}(x)=A^{2}_{n-1,k-1}(x)-(2n-1)\left(P_{n-1}^{(k-n)}(x)\right)^{2}.

Lemma 3.1
.

For the functions

A^{2}_{n,k}(x)
,
the following relation holds
:

\dfrac{d}{dx}(A^{2}_{n,k})=-P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}\cdot P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}.

Proof.

Let us carry out the proof by the method of mathematical induction.

1) Since
A^{2}_{n,1}(t)=\dfrac{-t^{2n-3}}{((n-1)!)^{2}(2n-3)}\left[(2n-1)(2n-3)t+(n-1)^{2}\right]

(see
[
4
]
),
after simplifications,
we obtain

\dfrac{d}{dx}(A^{2}_{n,1})=\dfrac{-t^{2n-4}(2a-1)(n-1)}{((n-1)!)^{2}}\left[2(2n-1)t+n-1\right].

On the other hand,

\displaystyle P_{n-1}^{(2-n)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\right)=\dfrac{t^{n-2}(2x-1)}{(n-2)!},

\displaystyle P_{n}^{(2-n)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\left(\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)=\dfrac{t^{n-2}}{(n-1)!}\left[2(2n-1)t+n-1\right].

Multiplying the last two equalities,
we see that the assertion holds
for

k=1

and any
n
.

2) Let the assertion hold
for some
k
and any
n
.
Since

A^{2}_{n+1,k+1}(x)=A^{2}_{n,k}(x)-(2n+1)\left(P_{n}^{(k-n)}\right)^{2},

differentiating with respect to
x
,
we obtain

(A^{2}_{n+1,k+1})^{\prime}=(A^{2}_{n,k})^{\prime}-2(2n+1)P_{n}^{(k-n)}\cdot P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}.

Replacing
(A^{2}_{n,k})^{\prime}

by the expression

(A^{2}_{n,k})^{\prime}=-P_{n-1}^{(k-n)}\cdot P_{n}^{(k-n)}
,
which is valid by the induction assumption,
and the multiplier

2(2n+1)P_{n}^{(k-n)}

given by formula (
2.10
)
by the difference

P_{n+1}^{(k-n+1)}-P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}
,
we obtain the required relation.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

In view of Eq. (
2.5
),
it follows from Sturm theory that,
between any two zeros of the polynomial

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}
,
there is at least one zero of

P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}
.

We shall need more subtle properties of the zeros of the polynomials

P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}

and

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

that do not follow from Sturm theory.
Let us prove a more general result.

Lemma 3.2
.

The roots of the polynomials

P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}

and

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

lying
on the interval

(0;1)

alternate.
The zeros of

P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}

are the minimum points of the function

A^{2}_{n,k}(x)

and
the zeros of

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

are its maximum points.

Proof.

Let us carry out the proof by the method of mathematical induction

1)
The induction base:
for any

n\geqslant 1

and

k=0
,
the given assertion holds.
Indeed,
in this case,

(A^{2}_{n,0})^{\prime}=-P_{n-1}^{(-n+1)}\cdot P_{n}^{(-n+1)}=-\dfrac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\cdot\dfrac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}(2x-1).

P_{n-1}^{(-n+1)}

has no zeros inside the interval

(0;1)
,
while

P_{n}^{(-n+1)}

has exactly one zero

x=1/2
,
and this is a maximum point.

2) Let,
for
some

n

and all

k\leqslant n-1
,
the assertion hold
for the pair of polynomials

P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}

and

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}
;
let us prove it
for

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

and

P_{n+1}^{(k-n+1)}

(i.e., we pass to the pair of indices

n+1

and

k+1
).
By Lemma (
3.1
) and formula (
2.10
),
we obtain

\displaystyle-(A^{2}_{n+1,k+1})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}P_{n+1}^{(k-n+1)}=-(A^{2}_{n,k})^{\prime}+2(2n+1)P_{n}^{(k-n)}P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

\displaystyle=P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}+2(2n+1)P_{n}^{(k-n)}P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}.

Since
P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

is the derivative of
P_{n}^{(k-n)}
,
it follows,
by Rolle’s theorem,
that their zeros alternate and, further,
the zeros of

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

are the maximum points of the function

(P_{n}^{(k-n)})^{2}

and the zeros of

P_{n}^{(k-n)}

are its minimum points.

By the induction hypothesis,
the zeros of

P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}

and

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

alternate.
The point

x_{0}={1}/{2}

is
a zero of

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

if and only if

k

is even,
and
it is known from
[
3
]
that the point

x_{0}={1}/{2}

is a local maximum of

A_{n,k}

for even
k

and a local minimum
for odd
k
.
Thus, the zeros of

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

are the maximum points of

A_{n,k}
.

Then
it follows from formula (
3.1
)
that the zeros of

P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}

are the minimum points of

A^{2}_{n+1,k+1}

and the zeros of

P_{n+1}^{(k-n+1)}

are the maximum points of

A^{2}_{n+1,k+1}
,
respectively,
which implies the assertion of the lemma.
∎

4.
The global maximum of
A^{2}_{n,k}

Theorem 4.1
.

The values of the quantities

A^{2}_{n,k}

at their local maximum points
lying
on the closed interval

[0,{1}/{2}]

constitute a nondecreasing sequence
and those
at the local maximum points
lying
on the closed interval

[\frac{1}{2},1]

constitute a nonincreasing sequence
(see the figure).

Proof.

For each fixed

n

and

k
,
we consider the function

B_{n,k}(x)=A^{2}_{n,k}(x)+(f(x)+g(x))(P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}(x))^{2}

with
some,
so far arbitrary, differentiable functions
f
and
g
.
The concrete form of these functions will be indicated in what follows.
Then

\displaystyle B^{\prime}_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle=(A^{2}_{n,k})^{\prime}+(f^{\prime}(x)+g^{\prime}(x))(P_{n}^{(k-n+1)})^{2}+2(f(x)+g(x))P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}P_{n}^{(k-n+2)}

\displaystyle=-P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}+(f^{\prime}(x)+g^{\prime}(x))(P_{n}^{(k-n+1)})^{2}+2(f(x)+g(x))P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}P_{n}^{(k-n+2)}

\displaystyle=P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}\left(-P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}+g^{\prime}(x)P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}+2(f(x)+g(x))P_{n}^{(k-n+2)}\right)+f^{\prime}(x)(P_{n}^{(k-n+1)})^{2}.

It follows from the recurrence relation (
2.7
)
and Lemma
3.1
that

-P_{n-1}^{(k-n+1)}=2(2n+1)P_{n}^{(k-n)}-P_{n+1}^{(k-n+1)}=2(2n+1)P_{n}^{(k-n)}-2(k+1)P_{n}^{(k-n)}-(2x-1)P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}.

Thus,

B^{\prime}_{n,k}(x)=P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}\left(2(2n-k)P_{n}^{(k-n)}+g^{\prime}(x)P_{n}^{(k-n+1)}+2(f(x)+g(x))P_{n}^{(k-n+2)}\right)+\\
+f^{\prime}(x)(P_{n}^{(k-n+1)})^{2}+(1-2x)(P_{n}^{(k-n+1)})^{2}.

We choose

f(x)

and

g(x)

so that the first summand
is equal to zero.
Taking into account the differential equation (
2.5
)
for

m=n-k
,
we obtain the system

\begin{cases}f(x)+g(x)=\dfrac{(x-x^{2})}{k+1},\\
g^{\prime}(x)=(2x-1)\dfrac{2(n-k-1)}{k+1}.\end{cases}

Therefore,

f^{\prime}(x)=(1-2x)\dfrac{2(n-k)}{k+1}.

Hence
