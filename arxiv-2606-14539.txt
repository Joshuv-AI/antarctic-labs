On a Conjecture for Parameterized s ​ t st -Orientations
Charalampos Papamanthou

Abstract
MaxSTN and MinSTN —proposed by Papamanthou and Tollis [ 9 , 10 ] —are two algorithms for producing s ​ t st -orientations of biconnected graphs with long and short longest paths respectively. Based on extensive experiments on planar and non-planar graphs of up to 5,000 nodes, it was conjectured (Conjecture 3.4.2 [ 8 ] ) that ℓ max ≥ ℓ min \ell_{\max}\geq\ell_{\min} for every biconnected graph G G , where ℓ max \ell_{\max} and ℓ min \ell_{\min} denote the longest-path lengths of the two orientations. This paper disproves this conjecture by exhibiting a biconnected graph on 9 vertices for which MaxSTN yields ℓ max = 6 \ell_{\max}=6 while MinSTN yields ℓ min = 7 \ell_{\min}=7 , regardless of how ties are broken in either algorithm.

Computing the

Papamanthou and Tollis

MinSTN
. The present paper disproves this

At any stage of the STN algorithm (Algorithm

3
Conjecture

It was observed experimentally (via experiments on planar and non-planar graphs of up to 5,000 nodes) that
MaxSTN
always
produced an orientation with a longest path at least as long as

MinSTN
. No formal proof for this was derived, and the following conjecture was put forth in
[
8
]
.

Conjecture 1

(Conjecture 3.4.2
[
8
]
)
.

Let
G
be a biconnected graph with source
s
and sink
t
. Let

\ell_{\max}
and
\ell_{\min}
denote the longest-path lengths
produced by
MaxSTN
and
MinSTN
respectively on
(G,s,t)
.
Then
\ell_{\max}\geq\ell_{\min}
.

This paper establishes that this conjecture is false by exhibiting a
strong
counterexample, a graph
G
, along with a sink
s
and
t
, for which there is no execution of
MaxSTN
that can outperform (in terms of longest-path length) some execution of
MinSTN
.

Tiebreak independence.

Since no ties arise at any step of either execution, the result

holds for

5
Statement on the use of AI

The counterexample was developed in an interactive session with
Claude Sonnet 4.6. No code was written by a human. Claude also helped with paper writing. We document our
experience here.

Claude read the paper
[
9
]
and wrote the Python STN code quickly. Some bugs were spotted and were corrected by further interaction. E.g., cut vertices were included as eligible, the sink block was not excluded, and the timestamp overwrite rule was implemented incorrectly.

Claude performed exhaustive
tests on
n\leq 6
but did not perform exhaustive search for
n=9
, claiming it was computationally infeasible, although in the end Claude did suggest another resource that could have made this possible
[
7
]
. During the process, Claude did produce some counterexamples that were confirmed wrong with external validation. Claude also drew some conclusions that we did not verify. E.g., Claude claimed that “
…exhaustive computer search over all
238
biconnected graphs on 5 vertices
(
4{,}760
ordered pairs
(s,t)
) and all
11{,}368
biconnected graphs on
6 vertices (
341{,}040
ordered pairs) found zero violations…
”, implying that
n=7
could be the first size where the conjecture does not hold.
