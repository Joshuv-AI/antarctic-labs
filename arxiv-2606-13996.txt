The sharp lifespan of small data smooth solutions to 2-D quadratic quasilinear wave equations in exterior domains
Bingbing Ding, Fei Hou, Huicheng Yin[12pt] 1. School of Mathematical Sciences and Mathematical Institute,Nanjing Normal University, Nanjing, 210023, China2. School of Mathematics, Nanjing University, Nanjing, 210093, China

Abstract
In the paper [M. Keel, H. Smith, C.D. Sogge, Almost global existence for quasilinear wave equations in three space dimensions. J. Amer. Math. Soc. 17 (2004), no. 1, 109-153], the authors prove that for the 3-D quadratic quasilinear wave equation in exterior domains with homogenous Dirichlet boundary value and small initial data of size ε \varepsilon , the lifespan T ¯ ε {\bar{T}}_{\varepsilon} of the smooth solution fulfills T ¯ ε ≥ e C / ε {\bar{T}}_{\varepsilon}\geq e^{C/\varepsilon} . However, for the corresponding 2-D quadratic quasilinear wave equation in exterior domains with homogenous Dirichlet or Neumann boundary value, so far it is still open whether the expected sharp lifespan T ε ≥ C ε 2 T_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}} holds or not. In this paper, we will solve this open question. Our main ingredients include: introducing the suitable Friedlander radiation field for the 2-D linear wave equation in exterior domains with homogenous Dirichlet or Neumann boundary value, constructing the delicate approximate solution, and establishing some crucial space-time decay estimates for the solutions of 2-D quasilinear wave equation in exterior domains. On the other hand, for the radial symmetric solutions to a class of 2-D quadratic quasilinear wave equation in exterior domains, the upper bound of the lifespan T ε ≤ C ε 2 T_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}} is derived and the sharp constant C C is also determined explicitly. Keywords. Lifespan, quadratic quasilinear wave equation, Friedlander radiation filed, L ∞ − L ∞ L^{\infty}-L^{\infty} estimate, approximate solution, weighted energy estimate 2020 Mathematical Subject Classification. 35L05, 35L20, 35L72

Proof.

\begin{split}\langle{t}\rangle\|\partial^{\leq 1}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}&\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\langle{t}\rangle^{\tilde{\varepsilon}}+\ln(2+t)\sum_{|a|\leq 1}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\langle{s}\rangle\|\partial^{a}[(\partial u)^{2}]\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{3})}\\

(5.12)

where

Lemma 5.3
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
and assumption (
5.1
), we have for

,

\sum_{|a|\leq 2N}\|\partial\partial^{a}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{3/2-3\tilde{\varepsilon}}.

Proof.

Set
\displaystyle E_{j}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{2N-j}\|\partial\partial_{t}^{k}v(t)\|_{H^{j}(\mathcal{K})}
with
0\leq j\leq 2N
.
Then one has that for
j\geq 1
,

\begin{split}E_{j}(t)&\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{2N-j}[\|\partial\partial_{t}^{k}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}+\sum_{1\leq|a|\leq j}(\|\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{a}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}+\|\partial_{x}\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{a}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})})]\\
&\lesssim E_{0}(t)+E_{j-1}(t)+\sum_{k=0}^{2N-j}\sum_{2\leq|a|\leq j+1}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{a}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})},\end{split}

(5.16)

where we have used the fact of

\sum_{k=0}^{2N-j}\sum_{1\leq|a|\leq j}\|\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{a}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{2N-j}\sum_{|a^{\prime}|\leq j-1}\|\partial_{x}\partial_{t}^{k+1}\partial_{x}^{a^{\prime}}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\lesssim E_{j-1}(t).

For the last term in (
5.16
), it can be deduced from the elliptic estimate (
2.1
) that

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{a}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}&\lesssim\|\Delta_{x}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{H^{|a|-2}(\mathcal{K})}+\|\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{H^{|a|-1}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})}\\
&\lesssim\|\partial_{t}^{k+2}v\|_{H^{|a|-2}(\mathcal{K})}+\|\partial_{t}^{k}\Box v\|_{H^{|a|-2}(\mathcal{K})}\\
&\quad+\|\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})}+\sum_{1\leq|b|\leq|a|-1}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{b}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})},\end{split}

(5.17)

where
\Delta_{x}=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Box
has been used.
Meanwhile, it follows from (
4.6
), (
4.7
), (
4.8
) and (
5.1
) with
|a|+k\leq 2N+1
that

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\Box v\|_{H^{|a|-2}(\mathcal{K})}&\lesssim\sum_{l\leq k}\varepsilon_{1}(\|\partial_{t}^{l}\partial v\|_{H^{|a|-1}(\mathcal{K})}+\|\partial_{t}^{l+2}v\|_{H^{|a|-2}(\mathcal{K})})+\varepsilon^{3/2}\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}[E_{j}(t)+E_{j-1}(t)]+\varepsilon^{3/2}.\end{split}

(5.18)

Combining (
5.16
)-(
5.18
) shows that for
j\geq 1
,

E_{j}(t)\lesssim E_{0}(t)+E_{j-1}(t)+\varepsilon_{1}E_{j}(t)+\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K}_{R+1})}.

(5.19)

Substituting (
5.10
) and (
5.19
) with
j=1
and the smallness of
\varepsilon_{1}
into (

E(t)=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sum_{i\leq 2N}\|\partial\partial_{t}^{i}v(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{3/2-2\tilde{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{E(s)ds}{\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2}}.

Applying Lemma
2.3
to the above inequality with (

which completes the proof of (
5.15
).
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
and assumption (
5.1
), we have for

\sum_{|a|\leq 2N-1}\|\partial Z^{a}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{3/2-4\tilde{\varepsilon}}\langle{t}\rangle^{1/2}.

(5.20)

Proof.

Analogously to Lemma
5.1
, one has that for any
|a|\leq 2N-1
,

\begin{split}&\sum_{|a|\leq 2N-1}\|\partial Z^{a}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}+\varepsilon_{1}\sum_{|a|\leq 2N-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial Z^{a}v(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\frac{ds}{\langle{s}\rangle^{1/2}}\\

\begin{split}|\mathcal{B}^{a}_{1}|&\lesssim\sum_{|b|\leq|a|}\int_{0}^{t}\|(1-\chi_{[1,2]}(x))\partial_{t}\partial^{b}v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{K})}\|(1-\chi_{[1,2]}(x))\partial_{x}\partial^{b}v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{K})}ds\\

where (
5.15
) has been used. Analogously,

5.21

(5.23)

where we have used (
4.8

2.3
to (
5.23

5.20

|\partial\partial^{\leq 1}(w_{B}-r^{1/2}F_{0})|\leq C(1+t)^{-3/2}\ln(2+t),\quad t\leq T_{\varepsilon},r\geq t/2+1.

(6.19)

According to the definition (
4.2
), we have
u_{ap}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon w_{B}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
.
This, together with (
6.18
) and (
6.19
), yields

\begin{split}W_{1}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})&=-\frac{1}{2c}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}r^{-1/2}\partial_{t}u+r^{1/2}\partial_{tr}^{2}u+\frac{1}{4}cr^{-3/2}u-cr^{-1/2}\partial_{r}u-cr^{1/2}\partial_{r}^{2}u\Big\}\Big|_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\\
&=-\frac{r^{1/2}}{2c}(\partial_{tr}^{2}u_{ap}-c\partial_{r}^{2}u_{ap})\Big|_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}+O(\varepsilon^{1.4})\\
&=-\frac{\varepsilon r^{1/2}}{2c}(\partial_{tr}^{2}w_{B}-c\partial_{r}^{2}w_{B})\Big|_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}+O(\varepsilon^{1.4})\\
&=\varepsilon F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{0}(r(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{\varepsilon})+O(\varepsilon^{1.4}),\end{split}

where
(\frac{1}{\varepsilon},r(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}))\in\Gamma^{+}_{\sigma_{0}}
.
It is easy to check that

r(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})=t_{0}+\sigma_{0}+\int_{t_{0}}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}c(\partial_{t}u(s))ds=\sigma_{0}+\int_{t_{0}}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}(c(\partial_{t}u(s))-1)ds+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

From this and (
6.11
), we arrive at

|r(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\sigma_{0}|\leq O(\varepsilon^{1/2}),

which leads to

W_{1}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{0}(\sigma_{0})+O(\varepsilon^{1.4})>K.

Applying Lemma
2.5
yields

\int_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}^{T_{\varepsilon}}a_{0}(t)dt=2c^{\prime}(0)(\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}})+O(\varepsilon\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon})<\frac{e^{8E\varepsilon^{3/2}}}{\varepsilon F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{0}(\sigma_{0})+O(\varepsilon^{1.4})}.

Thus, one has

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\varepsilon\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}\leq\frac{1}{2c^{\prime}(0)F_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\sigma_{0})}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
