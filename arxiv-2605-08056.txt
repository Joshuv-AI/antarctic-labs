An Exactly Solvable Absorbing Quantum Walk
Francisco Riberi

Abstract
We introduce and solve from first principles a continuous-time quantum walk with absorption generated by a Lindblad boundary sink of arbitrary strength. Tracing out the sink maps the problem onto a non-Hermitian tight-binding Hamiltonian with a rank-one imaginary defect on the semi-infinite line. We obtain closed-form expressions for the exact propagator, from which the first-passage statistics follow. Weak coupling limits absorption through inefficient transfer into the sink, whereas for strong dissipation, occupation at the boundary is stunted by the emergence of a localized non-Hermitian mode. Despite the different physical origin of these suppression mechanisms, we show their respective asymptotic absorption probabilities exhibit an exact duality. The evolution is conveniently visualized in phase-space, where the non-Hermitian mode produces a Wigner droplet exponentially confined near the edge site.

II
Model

We consider a continuous-time QW on the semi-infinite lattice
s\in\mathbb{N}
, with coherent hopping and absorption localized at the boundary. Evolution is generated by a nearest-neighbor tight-binding Hamiltonian with hopping rate
\Omega
,

\displaystyle H_{+}=\Omega\sum_{s\geq 1}\ket{s}\bra{s}-\frac{\Omega}{2}\sum_{s\geq 1}\left(\ket{s}\bra{s+1}+\ket{s+1}\bra{s}\right),

(1)

which produces ballistic propagation on the half-line.
Absorption is included by coupling the edge site
s=1
to a sink state
\ket{\emptyset}
through the jump operator

L_{\rm abs}=\sqrt{\kappa}\,\ket{\emptyset}\bra{1},

where
\kappa
is the absorption strength. The full density matrix obeys the following Lindblad master equation:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}=-i[H_{+},\rho]+L_{\rm abs}\rho L_{\rm abs}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{L_{\rm abs}^{\dagger}L_{\rm abs},\rho\}.

(2)

Averaging over the sink degree of freedom maps the problem onto a trace-decreasing evolution in the surviving walker sector, governed by the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{\rm eff}=H_{+}-\frac{i\kappa}{2}\ket{1}\bra{1}
:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{+}=-iH_{\rm eff}\rho_{+}+i\rho_{+}H_{\rm eff}^{\dagger},\qquad\rho_{+}={\rm Tr}_{\emptyset}\rho.

(3)

Thus the absorbing QW reduces to a tight-binding model with a rank-one imaginary defect.
The defect continuously removes probability amplitude reaching the boundary site.
Evolution is determined by the propagator

K_{\kappa}(s,s^{\prime};t)=\matrixelement{s}{e^{-iH_{\rm eff}t}}{s'},

and the reduced density matrix becomes:

\displaystyle\rho_{ss^{\prime}}^{(+)}(t)=\sum_{u,v\geq 1}K_{\kappa}(s,u;t)\,\rho_{uv}^{(+)}(0)\,K_{\kappa}^{*}(s^{\prime},v;t).

(4)

III
Analytic solution

We solve exactly for the propagator of the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H_{\rm eff}
, from which all dynamical observables follow in closed form.
In the absence of absorption (
\kappa=0
), the system reduces to a coherent QW on the half-line with a perfectly reflecting boundary at
s=1
. The corresponding propagator is obtained by applying the method of images
16
to the infinite lattice solution
37
,

\displaystyle K_{D}(s,s_{0};t)=e^{-i\Omega t}\left[i^{s-s_{0}}J_{s-s_{0}}(x)-i^{s+s_{0}}J_{s+s_{0}}(x)\right],

(5)

with
x=\Omega t
. The second term corresponds to the reflected trajectory and enforces the hard-wall boundary condition
K_{D}(0,s_{0};t)=0
. Absorption can be incorporated exactly at the resolvent level through the Green functions of the effective and reflecting Hamiltonian
15
:

\displaystyle G_{\kappa}(z)=\frac{1}{z-H_{\rm eff}},\qquad G_{D}(z)=\frac{1}{z-H_{+}}.

(6)

As the absorber acts only on the boundary site, the system is modified by a rank-one non-Hermitian defect,

V_{\rm abs}=-\tfrac{i\kappa}{2}\ket{1}\bra{1},

which can be resummed exactly by writing the Green functions in position-space (see Appendix). Propagation from
s_{0}
to
s
decomposes into a direct bulk term and processes involving repeated scattering from the absorbing boundary,

\displaystyle G_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};z)=G_{D}(s,s_{0};z)-\frac{i\kappa}{2}\frac{G_{D}(s,1;z)\,G_{D}(1,s_{0};z)}{1+\frac{i\kappa}{2}G_{D}(1,1;z)}.

We compute the bulk contribution in terms of the solution for the unconstrained walk on a line,
G_{D}(s,s_{0};z)=g_{s-s_{0}}(z)-g_{s+s_{0}}(z),
with

\displaystyle g_{n}(z)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{e^{ikn}}{z-\Omega(1-\cos k)}=-\frac{2}{\Omega}\frac{q^{|n|+1}}{1-q^{2}}.

(7)

Here
n
denotes the separation on the infinite lattice, and
q
is defined implicitly through
z=\Omega(1-\frac{1}{2}(q+q^{-1}))
. Substituting these expressions into
G_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};z)
, the resolvent simplifies to

\displaystyle G_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};z)=G_{D}(s,s_{0};z)-\frac{2i\eta}{\Omega}\frac{q^{s+s_{0}}}{1-i\eta q},

(8)

with
\eta=\kappa/\Omega
a
dimensionless absorption strength
that quantifies the competition between dissipation and coherent hopping.
We obtain the propagator by inverse Laplace transform,

\displaystyle K_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}dz\,e^{-izt}G_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};z),

(9)

where
\mathcal{C}
is a contour running horizontally in the complex
z
-plane above all poles and branch cuts of
G_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};z)
.
The inverse transform of
G_{D}
reproduces the hard-wall propagator in Eq. (
5
), while the scattering contribution encodes the absorber-induced boundary effects.

For
\eta<1
, the denominator in the second term of Eq. (
8
) admits the convergent geometric expansion

(1-i\eta q)^{-1}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(i\eta q)^{r}.

Computing the propagator reduces to a series of inverse transforms of powers
q^{\ell+r}
. This can be evaluated using the known relationship
G_{D}(r,1;z)\propto q^{r}
. The final result is:

\displaystyle K^{<}_{\kappa}=K_{D}+2\eta\,i^{\ell}e^{-i\Omega t}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-\eta)^{r}\frac{\ell+r}{x}J_{\ell+r}(x),

(10)

where
\ell=s+s_{0}
and
x=\Omega t
.

For
\eta>1
, the analytic structure changes qualitatively as the denominator

1-i\eta q

vanishes at

q_{p}=-i\eta^{-1},

with
|q_{p}|<1
. As a result, the contour integral used to evaluate Eq. (
9
) encloses a pole in addition to the continuum branch-cut contribution (see Appendix). The propagator separates into a continuum term and a discrete boundary-localized mode:

\displaystyle K^{>}_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};t)=K^{\rm cont}_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};t)+(1-q_{p}^{2})\,q_{p}^{s+s_{0}-2}e^{-iz_{p}t},

(11)

where

z_{p}=\Omega-\frac{i\Omega}{2}\left(\eta-\eta^{-1}\right).

We evaluate the continuum contribution by expanding

(1-i\eta e^{ik})^{-1}=-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}(i\eta e^{ik})^{-r},

which converges for
|\eta|>1
:

\displaystyle K^{\rm cont}_{\kappa}=K_{D}+2i^{N}e^{-i\Omega t}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{r+1}\eta^{1-r}\frac{\ell-r}{x}J_{\ell-r}(x).

(12)

IV
Discussion

The exact propagator determines all dynamical observables of the absorbing QW. For an initial state localized at
s_{0}
, the survival probability is

\displaystyle S(t|s_{0})=\sum_{s\geq 1}|K_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};t)|^{2},

(13)

while the complement
1-S(t|s_{0})
describes absorption at the boundary sink. Working in the momentum eigenbasis of the half-line tight-binding Hamiltonian, the total absorption probability

P_{\rm abs}(s_{0})=\lim_{t\to\infty}[1-S(t|s_{0})]

admits an exact integral representation (see Appendix):

\displaystyle P_{\rm abs}(s_{0})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}dk\,\sin^{2}(s_{0}k)\,\frac{4\eta\sin k}{1+\eta^{2}+2\eta\sin k}.

(14)

Equation (
14
) is invariant under the
\eta\leftrightarrow\eta^{-1}
transformation. Remarkably, this implies an exact weak–strong duality,
P_{\rm abs}(s_{0};\eta)=P_{\rm abs}(s_{0};\eta^{-1})
, see Fig.
1
. This symmetry, however, does not extend to the full transient dynamics.

Physically, evolution separates into three distinct regimes. For weak absorption (
\eta\ll 1
), the sink couples inefficiently to the walker, so
P_{\rm abs}\sim\tfrac{4}{\pi}\eta
. At the crossover point
\eta=1
, absorption is maximal,
approaching
1-\tfrac{2}{\pi}
for large
s_{0}
. In the limit of strong-dissipation (
\eta\gg 1
), transport into the edge site becomes dynamically suppressed, leading to decreased absorption,
P_{\rm abs}\sim\tfrac{4}{\pi\eta}
. This suppression originates from an emerging localized non-Hermitian boundary mode, and parallels the behavior observed in measurement-induced quantum Zeno absorption models
48
;
31
.

The first-passage density is determined directly by the probability current into the boundary site:

\displaystyle F(t|s_{0})=\kappa\,|K_{\kappa}(1,s_{0};t)|^{2}.

(15)

At short times,
F(t|s_{0})
exhibits oscillations arising from interference between direct and boundary-reflected propagation paths, as shown in Fig.
1
. Near
\eta\simeq 1
,
S(t)
decays rapidly as absorption is most efficient. For
\eta\gg 1
, however, the first-passage density decreases as the localized defect suppresses occupation of the absorbing site. This crossover admits a natural interpretation in phase space.

The dynamics can be conveniently visualized in terms of the doubled-lattice Wigner function (
m\geq 2
)

\displaystyle W_{+}(m,k,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\psi_{n}(t)\psi_{m-n}^{*}(t)e^{-i(2n-m)k},

(16)

where
\psi_{s}(t)=K_{\kappa}(s,s_{0};t)
for a localized initial condition.
Substituting the exact propagator yields an exact finite-sum Bessel-series representation of the Wigner function (see Appendix). For low to moderate absorption,
\eta<1
, the propagator separates into hard-wall and boundary-return contributions, leading to the following phase-space decomposition:

\displaystyle W_{+}=W_{DD}+(W_{DB}+W_{BD})+W_{BB}.

(17)

The terms correspond, respectively, to coherent bulk propagation, bulk–boundary interference, and repeated boundary-return processes. Equation (
17
) cleanly separates the competing effects contributing to the evolution.
For strong absorption (
\eta>1
), the boundary pole generates a localized phase-space contribution whose envelope decays exponentially away from the boundary,

\displaystyle|W_{\rm pole}(m,k,t)|\sim e^{-\Gamma_{p}t}\eta^{-m},\qquad\Gamma_{p}=\kappa-\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\kappa},

(18)

with characteristic localization length

\xi_{\rm loc}=1/\ln\eta.

The Wigner representation elucidates the competition between ballistic propagation and boundary interference effects: At short times the wavepacket spreads ballistically, while at later times absorption generates interference fringes and shows progressive depletion near the edge site. V
Conclusions

In this Letter, we introduced an exactly solvable continuous-time QW with absorption generated by a Lindblad boundary sink. The dynamics exhibits a crossover at
\kappa/\Omega=1
, separating weak coupling from a strongly dissipative regime in which transport into the boundary becomes dynamically suppressed. This transition is signaled by the emergence of a boundary-localized non-Hermitian mode, which produces an exponentially confined phase-space droplet near the edge site. Despite the fundamentally distinct microscopic mechanisms ruling the weak and strong coupling regimes, the asymptotic absorption probability remains invariant under the correspondence

\eta\leftrightarrow\eta^{-1}.

The exact Wigner representation further reveals how an active sink reshapes the phase-space structure of the walk through interference and probability depletion. Our framework provides a first-principles derivation of absorbing QW dynamics, and shows how phenomena previously introduced through heuristic rules emerge naturally from microscopic open-system evolution.

Several directions merit further investigation. Extending the model to entangled initial conditions, multiple absorbers or finite geometries may reveal nontrivial transport and trapping phenomena generated by interference between boundaries. Coupling the walk to bosonic environments is likewise relevant for realistic transport platforms and provides a natural setting to explore the interplay between coherent propagation, absorption, and decoherence. More broadly, our QW absorbing model offers a versatile arena for the study of non-Hermitian physics and open-system transport beyond measurement-based protocols.
