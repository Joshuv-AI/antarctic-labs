Inverted Dirac oscillator
Mustapha Maamache

Abstract
The Dirac oscillator is obtained from the Dirac Hamiltonian H D = ( c ​ α → ⋅ p → + m ​ c 2 ​ β ) \ H^{\mathrm{D}}=\left(c\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{p}+mc^{2}\beta\right) by modifying the momentum through a non-Hermitian substitution p → → p → ± i ​ ω ​ β ​ q → \overrightarrow{p}\rightarrow\overrightarrow{p}\pm i\omega\beta\overrightarrow{q} . Despite the non-Hermitian nature of this momentum operator, the full Hamiltonian remains Hermitian due to the presence of the Dirac matrix α → \vec{\alpha} . However, if one instead introduces a Hermitian modification of the form p → → p → ± ω ​ β ​ q → \vec{p}\rightarrow\vec{p}\pm\omega\beta\overrightarrow{q} , the resulting Hamiltonian is no longer Hermitian. In this case, the system corresponds to an inverted Dirac oscillator H r H^{\mathrm{r}} , where the potential becomes unbounded from below, the energy spectrum becomes continuous, and the eigenfunctions fail to be square-integrable, leading to normalization difficulties. We show that the Hamiltonian H r H^{\mathrm{r}} is a pseudo- 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} -symmetric operator, and we introduce an unbounded, non-unitary transformation that establishes a connection between H r H^{\mathrm{r}} and H D H^{\mathrm{D}} . The purpose of this work is to analyze this relativistic quantum system—known as the Dirac inverted oscillator—which, despite its various applications, admits an exact analytical solution.

4
Conclusion

A central result of this study is the establishment of a direct connection
between the inverted Dirac oscillator and the standard Dirac oscillator
through a Hermitian but non-unitary transformation operator
\rho
. This
mapping enables the transformation of a non-Hermitian problem into a more
tractable form associated with the conventional Dirac oscillator. As a
consequence, the spectral properties and eigenfunctions of the inverted
system can be obtained in a straightforward manner from the well-known
solutions of the Dirac harmonic oscillator. Furthermore, by considering the
non-relativistic limit, we have derived an approximate equation that clearly
reveals the underlying structure of the system, including the contributions
of the harmonic oscillator term and the spin-orbit coupling
\vec{S}\cdot\vec{L}
. The solutions were expressed in terms of standard radial
functions, spherical harmonics, and spinor components, consistent with the
coupling of orbital and spin angular momenta.

The present formulation opens the way for further investigations of
non-Hermitian relativistic systems and their physical implications. In
particular, the inverted Dirac oscillator may provide a useful framework for
exploring complex energy spectra, stability properties, and potential
applications in quantum systems where pseudo-Hermiticity and
PT
-symmetry
play a fundamental role. Future work may focus on extending this approach to
interacting systems, external fields, or higher-dimensional generalizations.
