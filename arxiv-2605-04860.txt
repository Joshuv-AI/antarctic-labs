Branching Brownian motion with rank-based selection and reaction-diffusion equations
Jacob Mercer

Abstract
We consider a family of branching-selection particle systems in which particles branch at time dependent rate r r and are killed with a probability which is dependent on their rank via some function ψ \psi . We show that, under fairly minimal conditions, the hydrodynamic limit of such a system is given by the reaction-diffusion equation U t = 1 2 ​ U x ​ x + r ⁡ ( t ) ​ G ​ ( U ) U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+r(t)G(U) with nonlinearity G ⁡ ( U ) G(U) which is a function of ψ \psi . This is a significant generalisation of the well-studied N N -BBM process, and is similar to the family of ‘ ( b , D ) (b,D) -BBM’ processes described by Groisman & Soprano-Loto in [ 15 ] . On the one hand, this allows us to understand common reaction-diffusion equations as limits of interacting particle systems with simple description. On the other hand, the asymptotic behaviour of solutions of the reaction-diffusion the PDEs can help us predict the asymptotic properties of the associated particle systems. We give general conditions under which the branching-selection particle system has an asymptotic velocity, and describe the velocity up to order ( log ⁡ N ) − 2 (\log N)^{-2} ; furthermore, we describe the connection between this velocity and the spreading speeds and travelling waves of the corresponding reaction-diffusion equation. This provides a partial weak selection principle.



1
Introduction

Reaction diffusion equations are a broad class of partial differential equations, which find applications in a number of areas of physical sciences, including ecology, cell biology, and flame propagation. We consider reaction-diffusion equations of the general form:

U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+Y(x,t,U),

where
Y:\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. A well-known example is the F-KPP equation, which was first studied in the 1930s independently by Fisher
[
11
]
and Kolmogorov, Petrovskii, and Piscunov
[
17
]
in the context of population genetics. The F-KPP equation is an equation of the form
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+G(U)
with
G(0)=G(1)=0
,
G(x)>0
for
x\in(0,1)
, and
G^{\prime}(x)\leq G^{\prime}(0)
for
x\in[0,1]
; a typical example being the Fisher equation
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+U(1-U)
. An important property of the F-KPP equation is the existence of travelling wave solutions and travelling fronts; it is well known that the F-KPP equation
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+G(U)
has a travelling wave solution for every speed
c\geq\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
. Thus the Fisher equation
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+U(1-U)
has travelling wave solution for all speeds
c\geq\sqrt{2}
. The situation is more complex in the general case, when
G
is not positive everywhere on
[0,1]
.

The PDE
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+U(1-U)
can also be understood as the hydrodynamic limit of an interacting particle system
[
13
]
which can be described as follows: consider
N
particles moving on
\mathbb{R}
as independent Brownian motions. At rate
N
, draw uniformly at random two particles from the population and move the leftmost of the two particles to the location of the rightmost particle.

Groisman and Soprano-Loto later generalised this when they describe their so-called ‘
(b,D)
-BBM’ process. In the
(b,D)
-BBM, particles behave as independent Brownian motions on
\mathbb{R}
, with branching described by the function
b:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
and deletion by the increasing function
D:[0,1]\to[0,1]
. More precisely, the
j
th
leftmost particle branches at rate
b\big(\frac{j-1}{N_{t}-1}\big)
, where
N_{t}
is the number of particles in the system at time
t
. Simultaneously to the
j

th
particle branching, the
i
th
leftmost particle, where
i<j
, is killed with probability
D\big(\frac{i}{j-1}-\big)-D\big(\frac{i-1}{j-1}-\big)
and this includes the possibility that no particle is deleted. The family of processes they describe is relatively general, and in particular includes branching Brownian motion (BBM), and the fixed population size
N
-BBM process (see
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
18
]
), among many others. In their work, they conjecture that the particle system has the hydrodynamic limit:

u_{t}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}u_{xx}(x,t)+u(x,t)\left(b(U(x,t))-\int_{U(x,t)}^{1}b(r)\frac{1}{r}D(dr)\right),

where
u
is the limiting empirical density and
U
the corresponding cumulative distribution.

Another generalisation of the
N
-BBM is studied by Atar
[
1
]
, who considers an
injection-branching-selection
system, in which the branching and selection steps are decoupled. The system proposed in
[
1
]
starts with
N
Brownian motions on
\mathbb{R}
, each branching at rate
\kappa\geq 0
, and subsequently particles are added to the system (
injected
) according to a random point process
\alpha^{N}(dx,dt)
and leftmost particles are deleted according to a random process
J^{N}
. As with the
(b,D)
-BBM, this setup allows for a variable number of particles. Subsequently Atar describes a weak formulation of the corresponding hydrodynamic limit.

In this paper we study a branching-selection particle system with rank-dependent selection which can be described as follows.
N
particles move as independent Brownian motions on
\mathbb{R}
, and each particle branches into two particles independently at time dependent rate
r(t)
. Simultaneously with each branching event, we kill a particle, killing the
i
th
leftmost particle with probability
\int_{(i-1)/N}^{i/N}\psi(s)ds
, where
\psi
is a positive, bounded, and continuous function with
\int_{0}^{1}\psi(s)ds=1
. We will call this process the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM. Note that unlike the
(b,D)
-BBM, this allows for the rank of the deleted particle to be greater than the rank of the branching particle. Then we will show that the empirical cumulative distribution function of the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM process converges to the unique solution to the following reaction-diffusion PDE:

U_{t}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}(x,t)+r(t)\left(U(x,t)-\int_{1-U(x,t)}^{1}\psi(s)ds\right).

(1)

The consequences of this result are two-fold. Firstly, any PDE
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+r(t)G(U)
, where
G(0)=G(1)=0
and
G^{\prime}(x)\leq 1
for
x\in[0,1]
can be understood as the hydrodynamic limit of a
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM with branching rate
r
and selection function
\psi(x)=1-G^{\prime}(1-x)
. Secondly, we may consider functions
G
for which the asymptotic behaviour of the equation
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+G(U)
is known, and discover what this implies about the asymptotic behaviour of the corresponding interacting particle system. Examples are given in Section
6
.

Another question about the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM which naturally arises is that of its asymptotic velocity. By coupling the process to versions of the
N
-BBM process, we prove that, when
r(t)\equiv 1
and under certain conditions on
\psi
, its asymptotic velocity,
v^{\psi}_{N}
, matches that of the
N
-BBM up to order
O((\log N)^{-2})
. That is,

v^{\psi}_{N}=\sqrt{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\sqrt{2}(\log N)^{2}}+o\left(\frac{1}{(\log N)^{2}}\right).

We may notice that when
r(t)\equiv 1
,
\psi(1)=0
, and and
\int_{0}^{x}\psi(s)ds\leq x
for all
x\in[0,1]
, then
\lim_{N\to\infty}v^{\psi}_{N}=\sqrt{2}=\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
is the minimal travelling wave speed for the PDE (
1
). This is an example of a
weak selection principle
; the
N
particle system has an asymptotic velocity which ‘selects’ the minimal travelling wave speed of the limiting PDE as
N\to\infty
. See for example
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
14
]
. Thus we aim to the answer the question of when the following diagram holds:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\psi,r,N)\text{-BBM Process}}

1
)
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v_{N}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Hydrodynamic limit}\atop N\to\infty}

Minimal wave speed

A discussion of this appears in Section

.

2
Construction of the process

Before stating the main results of this paper, we will formally construct the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM process as a measure-valued random process
(\mu^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
constructed from i.i.d Brownian motions, a Poisson process, and discrete random variables. We will write
\mu_{t}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{i}^{N}(t)}
where
X^{N}(t)=(X_{1}^{N}(t),X_{2}^{N}(t)\ldots,X_{N}^{N}(t))
is the vector of locations of the
N
particles at time
t
. Define the function
\Theta^{N}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
to be the function which sorts an
N
dimensional vector into increasing order. Furthermore, let
\Theta^{N}_{j}(\underline{x})
denote the
j
th
element of the vector
\Theta^{N}(\underline{x})
, which is the
j
th
smallest element of the vector
\underline{x}
.

Let the initial condition
\mu^{N}_{0}=\rho^{N}
where
\rho^{N}
is an atomic measure, the sum of
N
atoms of weight
1/N
, and suppose that
\rho^{N}
converges weakly to the probability measure
\rho
which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Now let
(\mathcal{N}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a Poisson process with time-dependent intensity
Nr(t)
, where
r
is continuous and bounded. Let
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots
be the discontinuities of the process, with
t_{0}=0
. At each time
t_{m}
we will order the elements of
X^{N}(t)
so that
X_{1}^{N}(t_{m})\leq X_{2}^{N}(t_{m})\leq\ldots\leq X_{N}^{N}(t_{m})
, but we may have
X_{i}(t)>X_{j}(t)
for
i<j
and
t\notin\{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},\ldots\}
. Let
\mathcal{I}=(I_{n})_{n=1,2,\ldots}
be a sequence of i.i.d. uniform random variables on
\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
, and let
\mathcal{J}=(J_{n})_{n=1,2,\ldots}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with
\mathbb{P}(J_{n}=k)=\int_{(k-1)/N}^{k/N}\psi(s)ds
for
k=1,2,\ldots,N
, where
\psi:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
is assumed to be positive and continuous with
\int_{0}^{1}\psi(s)ds=1
.

Let
\mathcal{W}:=((W_{i}(t))_{t\geq 0})_{i=1,2,\ldots,N}
be a family of
N
i.i.d Brownian motions. On each interval
(t_{m-1},t_{m})
for
m=1,2,\ldots
, the particle at location
X_{i}^{N}(t_{m-1})=\Theta^{N}_{i}(X^{N}(t_{m-1}))
is driven by the Brownian motion
(W_{i})_{t_{m-1}\leq t\leq t_{m}}
; so when
t\in(t_{m-1},t_{m})
, the
N
particles of the system are at locations
X^{N}_{i}(t_{m-1})+W_{i}(t)-W_{i}(t_{m-1})
for
i\in[N]
. Then at each time
t_{m}
, we move the particle at location
\Theta^{N}_{J_{m}}(X^{N}(t_{m}-))
to the location of
\Theta^{N}_{I_{m}}(X^{N}(t_{m}-))
. Note that this does not exclude the possibility that
I_{m}=J_{m}
. Repeating this inductively, we can describe the process
(X^{N}(t))_{t\geq 0}
as a function
X^{N}(t):=\Xi(\rho^{N},\mathcal{N},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},t)
for all times
t\geq 0
. Thus we have constructed the measure-valued process
(\mu^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

3
Main results

The first result we prove here is the hydrodynamic limit for the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM process under the conditions that
r
is continuous and bounded and
\psi
is positive, and continuous on
[0,1]
, with
\int_{0}^{1}\psi(u)du=1
. We will show, in a sense which we will make clear, that the hydrodynamic limit of the process
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM is the unique classical solution of the PDE:

\displaystyle U_{t}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}(x,t)+r(t)\left(U(x,t)-\int_{1-U(x,t)}^{1}\psi(s)ds\right)

(2)

It is a classical result due to Kolmogorov, Petrovskii, and Piscunov (§3, Theorem 1,
[
17
]
) that if
Y:\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, bounded, and Lipschitz continuous in the first and third variable, and
U(x,0)
is bounded and continuous, then
U_{t}=U_{xx}+Y(x,t,U)
has a unique classical solution. Note that if
r
is continuous and bounded on
[0,\infty)
and
\psi
is positive and continuous on
[0,1]
, then

Y(x,t,U):=r(t)\left(U-\int_{1-U}^{1}\psi(s)ds\right)\mathbbm{1}_{U\in[0,1]}

immediately satisfies these conditions.

Let us now define the assumption
(A1)
on
\psi
and
r
under which our first main result holds.

(A1)
:
\psi
, and
r
satisfy assumption
(A1)
if
\psi:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
is continuous with
\int_{0}^{1}\psi(s)ds=1
and
r
is continuous and bounded.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(\mu^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}\in\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{M}_{F}^{w}(\mathbb{R}))
be the empirical measure-valued process corresponding to the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM process with
\mu^{N}_{0}=\rho^{N}
. Let
Q^{N}_{T}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{w}_{F}(\mathbb{R})))
denote the distribution of
(\mu_{t}^{N})_{t\in[0,T]}
. Then under assumption (A1), for any fixed
T\in(0,\infty)
,
Q^{N}_{T}
converges weakly to
Q^{\infty}_{T}=\delta_{u}
, where
\delta_{u}
is a Dirac mass on
u(x,t)\in\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{w}_{F}(\mathbb{R}))
, and
U(x,t):=\int_{x}^{\infty}u(y,t)dy
is the unique classical solution to (
2
) with initial condition
U(x,0)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\rho(dy)
.

Next we define assumption
(A2)
, which is a sufficient condition on
\psi
and
r
for the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM process to have an asymptotic velocity
v_{N}
.

(A2)
:
\psi
and
r
satisfy assumption
(A2)
if
\psi(x)=0
for
x\in[1-p,1]
for some
0<p<1
and
\psi(x)>\epsilon
for
x\in[0,\epsilon]
for some
\epsilon>0
, and
r\equiv 1
.

By coupling our system to the
N
-BBM process, we can show that

agrees with the speed of the
N
-BBM up to order
(\log N)^{-2}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(X^{N}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM with any initial configuration. Then under the assumption (A2), the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM has asymptotic velocity:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Theta^{N}_{1}(X^{N}(t))}{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Theta^{N}_{N}(X^{N}(t))}{t}=v_{N}=\sqrt{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\sqrt{2}(\log N)^{2}}+o\left(\frac{1}{(\log N)^{2}}\right).

We may reasonably ask what happens if assumption
(A2)
fails. If the assumption that
\psi(x)>0
for
x\in[0,\epsilon]
is broken, then we never kill the leftmost particles of the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM, and accordingly, we would expect
\Theta_{1}^{N}(X^{N}(t))\to-\infty
as

so that no positive asymptotic speed exists. One the other hand, if
\psi(x)>0
for

, then the rightmost

. We do in fact expect that if

sufficiently quickly, then there is still an asymptotic velocity close to

(see Conjecture

), however the specific method of our proof does not apply in this setup.

In order to form a

, we also wish to relate
\sqrt{2}=\lim_{N\to\infty}v_{N}
to the travelling wave speed or spreading speed of the PDE (
2
). This is made precise in Section
6
, and summarised in the following Theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Under the assumptions (A1), (A2), the minimal spreading speed (in a sense which we will make precise) of the PDE (
2
) is
\sqrt{2}
. If, further, we have that
\int_{1-x}^{1}\psi(s)ds\leq x
for all
x\in[0,1]
, then the PDE (
2
) has a travelling wave with speed

for any

.

Together, Theorems

Next, we prove tightness of the sequence of laws of
(\mu_{t}^{N})_{t\geq 0}
. Specifically, we will prove that the sequence of laws is tight in the space
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{w}_{F}(\bar{\mathbb{R}}))
, where
\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is the extended real line
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
. The benefit of this method is that
\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is compact, and as a consequence
\{\mu:\mu(\bar{\mathbb{R}})=1\}
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{M}^{w}_{F}(\bar{\mathbb{R}})
. This will help us to prove a compactification condition required to show tightness.

Proposition 5
.

Fix
T>0
, and let
Q^{N}_{T}
denote the law of
(\mu_{t}^{N})_{t\in[0,T]}
. Then the sequence of
(Q^{N}_{T})_{N=1,2,\ldots}
is tight in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{M}_{F}^{w}(\bar{\mathbb{R}})))
with respect to the Skorokhod metric.

Proof.

In order to show that the sequence
((\mu_{t}^{N})_{t\geq 0})_{N=1,2,\ldots}
is tight in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{w}_{F}(\bar{\mathbb{R}})))
with respect to the Skorokhod topology, it is sufficient (Theorem 1.18,
[
9
]
) to show the following conditions:

(i)

Compact containment:
For all
\epsilon>0
there exists a compact subset

such that

\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}(\mu_{t}^{N}\in K_{\epsilon}\;\forall\,t\in[0,T])>1-\epsilon

(ii)

Tightness of real-valued processes:
The sequence of laws of
(\langle\mu_{t}^{N},f\rangle)_{0\leq t\leq T}
is tight in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R}))
for every function
f
in a dense subset of
C_{b}(\bar{\mathbb{R}})

Observe that the subset
\{\mu:\mu(\bar{\mathbb{R}})=1\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{F}^{w}(\bar{\mathbb{R}})
, the subset consisting of probability measures, is a compact subset since
\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is itself compact. This is easily seen by observing that any subset of
\{\mu:\mu(\bar{\mathbb{R}})=1\}
is tight, since
\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is a compact subset of
\bar{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\mu(\bar{\mathbb{R}})>1-\epsilon
for all
\mu
. Then since
\mu_{t}^{N}(\mathbb{R})=1
for all
t\in[0,T]
and

, the compact containment condition (i) holds immediately.

We now prove condition (ii). Since each
\mu_{t}^{N}
has support on
\mathbb{R}
, thus it is sufficient to show that
((\langle\mu_{t}^{N},f\rangle)_{t\geq 0})_{N=1,2,\ldots}
is tight for all functions in a dense subset of
C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
. We choose
\mathcal{BC}^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty),\mathbb{R})\cap C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
to be our dense subset of
C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
. By Aldous’ tightness criterion (see, for example, Theorem 16.10,
[
4
]
),
((\langle\mu_{t}^{N},f\rangle)_{t\geq 0})_{N=1,2,\ldots}
is tight if the following conditions hold:

A

For every
m>0
,
\lim_{a\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\sup_{0\leq t\leq m}|\langle\mu_{t}^{N},f\rangle|\geq a)=0
.

B

For each
\epsilon,\eta,m>0
, there exists
\delta_{0},N_{0}
such that if
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,
N\geq N_{0}
, and
\tau
is a stopping time such that
\tau\leq m
, then
\mathbb{P}(|\langle\mu_{\tau+\delta}^{N},f\rangle-\langle\mu^{N}_{\tau},f\rangle|\geq\epsilon)\leq\eta
.

Condition A follows immediately by observing that
|\langle\mu_{t}^{N},f\rangle|\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
, therefore since
f
is bounded, then for
a\geq\|f\|_{\infty}
, this probability is
0
. Now let us prove condition B. Since
f\in\mathcal{BC}^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty),\mathbb{R})\cap C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, thus
f_{t}=0
, and
f
,
f_{x}
, and
f_{xx}
are all bounded for
t\in[0,m+\delta]
, say by a constant

. So (
3
) gives that

\displaystyle|\langle\mu_{\tau+\delta}^{N},f\rangle-\langle\mu_{\tau}^{N},f\rangle|\leq(1+C_{r}+C_{r}C_{\psi})C_{f}\delta+|M_{\tau+\delta}^{N,W}-M_{\tau}^{N,W}|+|M_{\tau+\delta}^{N,P}-M_{\tau}^{N,P}|

Therefore by Markov’s inequality and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, there exists a constant
C
such that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\langle\mu_{\tau+\delta}^{N},

\displaystyle f\rangle-\langle\mu_{\tau}^{N},f\rangle|>\epsilon)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big((1+C_{r}+C_{r}C_{\psi})C_{f}\delta+|M_{\tau+\delta}^{N,W}-M_{\tau}^{N,W}|+|M_{\tau+\delta}^{N,P}-M_{\tau}^{N,P}|>\epsilon\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\{(1+C_{r}+C_{r}C_{\psi})C_{f}\delta\geq\epsilon/3\}}+\mathbb{P}(|M_{\tau+\delta}^{N,W}-M_{\tau}^{N,W}|>\epsilon/3)+\mathbb{P}(|M_{\tau+\delta}^{N,P}-M_{\tau}^{N,P}|>\epsilon/3)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\{(1+C_{r}+C_{r}C_{\psi})C_{f}\delta\geq\epsilon/3\}}+\frac{9}{\epsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}[|M_{\tau+\delta}^{N,W}-M_{\tau}^{N,W}|^{2}]+\frac{9}{\epsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}[|M_{\tau+\delta}^{N,P}-M_{\tau}^{N,P}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\{(1+C_{r}+C_{r}C_{\psi})C_{f}\delta\geq\epsilon/3\}}+\frac{9C}{\epsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}[[M^{N,W}]_{\delta}]+\frac{9C}{\epsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}[[M^{N,P}]_{\delta}]\xrightarrow[N\to\infty,\delta\to 0]{}0

Therefore condition B follows, hence the sequence

A very similar method to the above is employed by Etheridge (Section 1.4,
[

]

, instead of

(the two-point compactification of
\mathbb{R}
). However, since

are not distinguished in

, continuous bounded functions

must have

. However we wish to have convergence of

, which is why we use
\mathcal{M}^{w}_{F}(\bar{\mathbb{R}})
instead.

This generalisation from
\mathcal{M}_{F}^{w}(\mathbb{R})
to
\mathcal{M}_{F}^{w}(\bar{\mathbb{R}})
will not cause any additional difficulties for us. Since
(Q^{N}_{T})_{N=1,2,\ldots}
is tight, therefore by Prokhorov’s theorem (Theorem 5.1,
[
4
]
), there is a weakly convergent subsequence
(Q^{N_{k}}_{T})_{k=1,2,\ldots}
. By appealing to a comparison with branching Brownian motion, we can show that any subsequential limit
(\mu_{t}^{\infty})_{t\geq 0}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and has
\mu_{t}^{\infty}(\{-\infty,\infty\})=0
for all times

almost surely.

Lemma 6
.

Let
Q^{\infty}_{T}
be a subsequential limit of
(Q^{N}_{T})_{N\geq 1}
and let
(\mu^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\sim Q^{\infty}_{T}
. Then
\mu_{t}^{\infty}\ll\lambda
and
\mu^{\infty}_{t}(\{-\infty,\infty\})=0
for all
t\in[0,T]

Q_{T}^{\infty}
-a.s.

This proof essentially follows from the fact that the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM can be bounded above by a branching Brownian motion started from
N
particles, which has a well known hydrodynamic limit which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

We construct the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM from a branching Brownian motion as follows. Consider a branching Brownian motion
(X_{u}(t):u\in\mathcal{U}(t))_{t\geq 0}
starting from
N

\rho^{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}^{N}}
which branches at time-inhomogeneous rate

. Let
\mathcal{U}(t)
denote the set of particles at time
t
, and
\mathcal{U}^{i}(t)
be the subset of
\mathcal{U}(t)
consisting of the descendants of the particle which starts at location
x_{i}^{N}
. Now we will colour the particles of the BBM blue and red. Initially, all particles are blue. Then at each branching time of a blue particle, we colour exactly one blue particle red; the
j
th
leftmost blue particle being coloured red with probability
\int_{(j-1)/N}^{j/N}\psi(u)du
. Subsequently, blue particles always branch into blue particles and red into red. Clearly there are always exactly
N
blue particles, and the subset of blue particles describes a
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM.

Now let
\hat{\mu}^{N}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}^{i}(t)}\delta_{X_{u}(t)}
be the rescaled empirical measure of this coloured BBM process at time
t
. It is well-known (and proven in the Appendix, Theorem
16
, for completeness) that if
\hat{Q}_{T}^{N}
is the law of
(\hat{\mu}_{t}^{N})_{t\in[0,T]}
, then
\hat{Q}_{T}^{N}
converges weakly to
\hat{Q}_{T}^{\infty}
, and if
\hat{\mu}^{\infty}\sim\hat{Q}^{\infty}_{T}
, then
\hat{\mu}_{t}
has density
u(x,t)=\exp(\int_0^t r(s)ds)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbb{P}_{\rho}(B(t)\leq x)

\hat{Q}^{\infty}_{T}
-a.s., and
u
is the unique classical solution to the PDE
u_{t}=\frac{1}{2}u_{xx}+r(t)u
. Therefore since
\hat{\mu}_{t}^{\infty}\ll\lambda
and
\hat{\mu}_{t}^{\infty}(\{-\infty,\infty\})=0
for all
t\in[0,T]

\hat{Q}^{\infty}_{T}
-a.s., and
\mu_{t}^{N}
is dominated by
\hat{\mu}_{t}^{N}
for all
N
, thus
\mu_{t}^{\infty}\ll\lambda
and
\mu_{t}^{\infty}(\{-\infty,\infty\})=0
for all
t\in[0,T]

Q^{\infty}_{T}
-a.s..
∎

Since we know that
\mu_{t}^{\infty}
has no mass at
\infty

implies convergence in the space
\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{w}_{F}(\mathbb{R}))
, which is what we use from here onwards. Next we show, by Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion, that the map

is continuous on
[0,T]

Q^{\infty}_{T}
-a.s.. In particular:

Lemma 7
.

Let
Q^{\infty}_{T}
be a subsequential limit of
(Q^{N}_{T})_{N=1,2,\ldots}
and let
(\mu^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\sim Q^{\infty}_{T}
. Then the map

is continuous on
[0,T]
with respect to the weak topology
Q_{T}^{\infty}
-a.s..

Proof.

Since
\mu_{t}^{\infty}
is cadlag, we will show using the Kolmogorov continuity theorem to show that there exists a continuous modification of
\mu_{t}^{\infty}
, and therefore as a result
\mu_{t}^{\infty}
is almost surely continuous (see Theorem 1,
[
21
]
). So fix

and

. By the definition of convergence in the Skorokhod topology on
\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{w}_{F}(\mathbb{R}))

\lambda_{N}:[0,T]\to[0,T]

as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\langle\mu_{t_{1}}^{\infty},f\rangle-\langle\mu_{t_{2}}^{\infty},f\rangle|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\lim_{N\to\infty}|\langle\mu_{\lambda_{N}(t_{1})}^{N},f\rangle-\langle\mu_{\lambda_{N}(t_{2})}^{N},f\rangle|^{2}]\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\langle\mu_{\lambda_{N}(t_{1})}^{N},f\rangle-\langle\mu_{\lambda_{N}(t_{2})}^{N},f\rangle|^{2}]

5

\displaystyle|\langle\mu_{\lambda_{N}(t_{1})}^{N},f\rangle-\langle\mu_{\lambda_{N}(t_{2})}^{N},f\rangle|^{2}\leq C|\lambda_{N}(t_{1})-\lambda_{N}(t_{2})|^{2}+9|M_{\lambda_{N}(t_{1})}^{N,W}-M_{\lambda_{N}(t_{2})}^{N,W}|^{2}+9|M_{\lambda_{N}(t_{1})}^{N,P}-M_{\lambda_{N}(t_{2})}^{N,P}|^{2}

for some constant

. Then by (
5

\mathbb{E}[|M_{\lambda_{N}(t_{1})}^{N,W}-M_{\lambda_{N}(t_{2})}^{N,W}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\langle\mu_{\lambda_{N}(t_{1})}^{N},f\rangle

\displaystyle=\liminf_{N\to\infty}9C(|\lambda_{N}(t_{1})-t_{1}|^{2}+|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{2}+|t_{2}-\lambda_{N}(t_{2})|^{2})=9C|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{2},

since

. Therefore

\langle\mu_{t}^{\infty},f\rangle
has a continuous modification, and thus is almost surely continuous (Theorem 1,
[

). Therefore
\langle\mu_{t}^{\infty},f\rangle
is continuous for all

\displaystyle u_{t}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}u_{xx}(x,t)+r(t)u(x,t)\left(1-\psi\left(\int_{-\infty}^{x}u(y,t)dy\right)\right)

\displaystyle G(\mu,f,N,t):=\langle\mu_{t},f(\cdot,t)\rangle-\langle\mu_{0},f(\cdot,0)\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mu_{s},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}(\cdot,s)+f_{t}(\cdot,s)+r(s)f(\cdot,s)\rangle

\displaystyle-\big\langle\mu_{s},Nf(\cdot,s)r(s)\int_{(H\star\mu_{s})(\cdot)-1/N}^{(H\star\mu_{s})(\cdot)}\psi(u)du\big\rangle ds.

Therefore by Proposition

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[G(\mu^{N},f,N,t)^{2}]\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0.

5
General reaction-diffusion equations

So far we have shown that the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM process has a hydrodynamic limit described by the unique solution to the reaction-diffusion equation

U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+r(t)\left(U-\int_{1-U}^{1}\psi(s)ds\right).

In particular, this means that given any function
G(x)
with
G(0)=G(1)=0
and
G^{\prime}(x)\leq 1
, we can describe the solution of the PDE
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+G(U)
as the hydrodynamic limit of a branching-selection particle system with selection function defined by
\psi(x)=1-G^{\prime}(1-x)
. The author hopes that this introduces an intuitive way in which to understand a number of general reaction-diffusion equations, since
\psi
can be thought of as describing the relative probability of an individual in the population being out-competed as a function of ranked fitness.

Example 1:

A ubiquitous choice of
G
is the Fisher equation, when
G(x)=x(1-x)
, which has been studied since it was introduced by Fisher
[
11
]
. Choosing
\psi(x)=2-2x
gives us a particle system who’s hydrodynamic limit is the equation
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+U-U^{2}
. Simply, this particle system is the system of
N
particles, moving as Brownian motions, in which each particle branches at unit rate and simultaneously a particle is deleted with a probability which is directly proportional to its rank.

Example 2:

The Allen-Cahn equation
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+U(1-U)(U-\theta)
, for
\theta\in(0,1)
, models the
strong Allee effect
. In this case, to realise the PDE as the hydrodynamic limit of a rank-dependent branching selection system, we choose
\psi(x)=(2-\theta)(1-2x)+3x^{2}

Example 3:

Another common example we give is
G(x)=x(1-x)(1+\rho x)
, with
\rho\in[0,1]
. In this case, we can realise
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+U(1-U)(1+\rho U)
as the hydrodynamic limit of the rank-dependent branching selection system with selection function
\psi(x)=2+a-2x+4ax+3ax^{2}
.

U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+U-\int_{1-U}^{1}\psi(s)ds
under assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
.

It is a well known result (see
[
16
]
, for example) that if the function
G
satisfies the conditions
G(0)=G(1)=0
,
G(x)>0
for all
x\in(0,1)
, and
G^{\prime}(x)\leq G^{\prime}(0)
for all
x\in[0,1]
, then
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+G(U)
has a travelling wave solution
U(x,t)=\omega_{c}(x-ct)
with
\lim_{z\to-\infty}\omega_{c}(z)=1
and
\lim_{z\to\infty}\omega_{c}(z)=0
for all speeds
c\geq\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
. Therefore we can state the following:

Proposition 13
.

Let
\psi
be such that
\int_{1-x}^{1}\psi(s)ds<x
for all
x\in[0,1]
and
\psi(x)=0
for
x\in[1-p,1]
for some
p\in(0,1)
. Then the PDE (
2
) has a travelling wave solution for all speeds
c\geq\sqrt{2}
.

Proof.

Defining
G(x):=x-\int_{1-x}^{1}\psi(s)ds
, it immediately follows that
G(0)=0
, and
\int_{0}^{1}\psi(s)ds=1
gives that
G(1)=0
. Moreover, since
\psi
is positive and
\psi(x)=0
for
x\in[1-p,1]
,
G^{\prime}(x)=1-\psi(1-x)\leq 1=G^{\prime}(0)
for all
x\in[0,1]
. Finally, the condition
\int_{1-x}^{1}\psi(s)ds<x
ensures that
G(x)>0
for
x\in(0,1)
.
∎

The condition that
\psi
is
0
near
1
is equivalent to
G^{\prime}
being maximal
1
at
0
and hence that the minimal travelling wave speed is
\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}=\sqrt{2}
. Intuitively, if
\psi
were bounded below by
\delta\in(0,1)
on
[0,1]
, with
\psi(x)=\delta
for
x\in[1-p,1]
, the PDE would have minimal travelling wave speed
\sqrt{2(1-\delta)}
. In terms of the
N
-particle system, if we are killing the rightmost particle at a positive rate
N\delta
, this implies that the cloud of particles would then have the slower speed
\sqrt{2(1-\delta)}<\sqrt{2}
. We therefore conjecture the following:

Conjecture 14
.

Let
\psi
be a selection function such that
\psi(x)\geq\delta
for all
x\in[0,1]
and
\psi(x)=\delta
for all
x\in[1-p,1]
for some
p\in(0,1)
. Then the
(\psi,1,N)
-BBM has asymptotic velocity:

v^{\psi}_{N}=\sqrt{2(1-\delta)}-\frac{c}{(\log N)^{2}}+o\left(\frac{1}{(\log N)^{2}}\right)

for some positive constant
c
.

On the other hand, if
G(x)
is negative on
(0,1)
, we may not have a travelling wave at all. We recall the following result:

Theorem

(Ch. 1, §3, Theorem 3.14,
[
22
]
)
.

Consider the PDE
U_{t}=U_{xx}+G(U)
, with
G(0)=G(1)=0
and
G^{\prime}(0),G^{\prime}(1)>0
. Then there is no travelling wave solution
U(x,t)=w(x-ct)
of the PDE with
\lim_{x\to-\infty}w(x)=1
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}w(x)=0
.

Therefore we can conclude that
no weak selection principle holds in general
, since there exist selection functions
\psi
such that the
(\psi,1,N)
-BBM has asymptotic speed converging to
\sqrt{2}
but the PDE (
2
) does not have travelling wave solutions.

We may have in mind the case when
\epsilon\leq\psi(s)\leq\delta<1
for
s\in[0,\epsilon]
and
\psi(x)=0
for
x\in[1-p,1]
so that
\psi
satisfies assumption
(A2)
, and thus by Theorem
2
the
(\psi,1,N)
-BBM has an asymptotic velocity, but the PDE has no travelling wave solutions. The intuition behind this disparity is as follows. If
\psi(s)<1
for
s\in[0,\epsilon]
, then in the
N
-particle system, the leftmost
\lfloor\epsilon N\rfloor
particles branch more frequently than they are killed; rate
\epsilon
versus rate
\delta\epsilon
. When
N
is finite, this is controlled by the fact that we have rare events in which, for example, all particles come within distance
1
of one another. However the rarity of these events is such that, as
N\to\infty
, this is in some sense ‘insufficient to stop some of the mass diffusing to
-\infty
in the PDE’.

Whilst no weak selection principle holds in general, we may still prove that the solution of the PDE spreads to the right with speed at least
\sqrt{2}
in a sense which we will now define. For more complex equations, we may see more complex propagating fronts, and there exists ‘terrace solutions’ in which several travelling fronts of different speeds may be ‘stacked on top of each other’ (see
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
, for example).

Definition 2
.

Consider the PDE
U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+G(U)
with initial condition
U(x,0)=U_{0}(x)
such that
U_{0}(x)\to 1
as
x\to-\infty
and
U_{0}(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
. We say that the solution
U(x,t)
spreads up to (the value)
u^{\star}
at speed
c^{\star}
if:

1.

\lim_{t\to\infty}\inf_{x\leq ct}U(x,t)\geq u^{\star}
for all
c<c^{\star}
,

2.

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\geq ct}U(x,t)=0
for all
c>c^{\star}
.

More generally, we may say that the solution spreads up to (the value)
u^{\star}
with speed at least
c^{\star}
if just condition 1. holds.

This definition is a slight generalisation of the definition given by Hamel and Nadin in
[
16
]
, allowing for the possibility that the solution doesn’t spread up to the value
1
, but may spread up to a strictly smaller value
u^{*}\in(0,1)
. Let us observe also the somewhat trivial fact that, as
U
is monotonic decreasing, if
\lim_{t\to\infty}\inf_{x\leq ct}U(x,t)\geq a
, then also
\lim_{t\to\infty}\inf_{x\leq c^{\prime}t}U(x,t)\geq a
for all
c^{\prime}<c
.

Following
[
16
]
, we also introduce the following definition of a
front-like
initial condition
U_{0}(x)
:

Definition 3

(
[
16
]
, Definition 1.1)
.

We say that a function
U_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is front-like if
0\leq U_{0}(x)\leq 1
for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{y\geq x}|U_{0}(x)|=0
, and there exists
x_{-}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta>0
such that
U_{0}(x)\geq\delta
for almost all
x<x_{-}
.

Proposition 15
.

Let
G:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable function such that
G(0)=G(u^{\star})=G(1)=0
,
G(x)>0
for
x\in(0,u^{\star})
and
G^{\prime}(x)\leq G^{\prime}(0)
for
x\in[0,1]
, and consider the PDE

U_{t}=\frac{1}{2}U_{xx}+G(U).

(19)

Then any front-like initial condition
U_{0}(x)
such that
\lim_{x\to-\infty}U_{0}(x)=1
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}U_{0}(x)=0
spreads up to the value
u^{\star}
with speed at least
\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
. Moreover, the minimal spreading speed is attained; that is, there exists an initial condition
U_{0}(x)
which spreads up to the value
u^{\star}
at speed
\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
.

Proof.

We prove the above proposition by using comparison principles to bound the solution of the PDE (
19
) above and below. First we construct a lower bound to show that every initial condition
U_{0}
spreads up to
u^{\star}
with speed at least
\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
.
We prove the above proposition by using comparison principles to bound the solution of the PDE (
19
) above and below. First we construct a lower bound to show that every initial condition
U_{0}
spreads up to
u^{\star}
with speed at least
\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
.

To construct the lower bound, recall that solutions to the F-KPP equation are ordered with respect to their initial condition. That is, if
U_{0}(x)\leq\tilde{U}_{0}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
U(x,t)
and
\tilde{U}(x,t)
solve the PDE (
19
) with initial conditions
U_{0}(x)
and
\tilde{U}_{0}(x)
respectively, then
U(x,t)\leq\tilde{U}(x,t)
for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)
(see for example
[
17
]
, Theorem 2). Now fix a front-like initial condition
U_{0}
with
\lim_{x\to-\infty}U_{0}(x)=1
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}U_{0}(x)=0
, and define
U^{-}_{0}(x):=\min\{U_{0}(x),u^{\star}\}
, and let
U^{-}(x,t)
be the solution to the PDE (
19
) with initial condition
U^{-}_{0}
. We can consider this as a scaled F-KPP with source term
G
such that
G(0)=G(u^{\star})=0
and
G(x)>0
for
x\in(0,u^{\star})
and
G^{\prime}(x)
maximal at

. Moreover, the initial condition
U^{-}_{0}
is still front-like. Therefore the inequality (1.6) of
[
16
]
gives us that:

\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}\leq\sup\left\{c\in\mathbb{R}:\lim_{t\to\infty}\inf_{x\leq ct}U^{-}(x,t)=u^{\star}\right\},

so that
U^{-}
spreads up to

with speed at least

. Then by comparison:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\inf_{x\leq ct}U(x,t)\geq\lim_{t\to\infty}\inf_{x\leq ct}U^{-}(x,t)\geq u^{\star}

for all

, therefore
U(x,t)
spreads up to
u^{\star}
with speed at least
\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
.

Next, by bounding the solution above, we show that there exists an initial condition which spreads up to
u^{\star}
at speed
\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
. We do this by recalling that solutions to the F-KPP are ordered with respect to the source term
G
. That is, if
G(x)\leq H(x)
for all

,
U
solves

with initial condition
U_{0}
, and
V
solves

with initial condition

for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)
(see for example
[
17
]

So fix
\epsilon\in(0,u^{\star})
and let
G^{+}
be such that
G^{+}(x)=G(x)
for
x\in[0,\epsilon]
,
G^{+}(x)>0
and
G^{+}(x)\geq G(x)
for

, and

for
x\in[0,1]
. Therefore the PDE

has, for every speed

, a travelling wave solution
U^{+}(x,t)=U^{+}_{c}(x-ct)
such that
\lim_{z\to-\infty}U^{+}_{c}(z)=1
and
\lim_{z\to\infty}U^{+}_{c}(z)=0

, and let

be the solution to (
19
) with initial condition

. Then by comparison, for any

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\geq ct}U(x,t)\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\geq ct}U^{+}_{c^{\star}}(x-c^{\star}t)=0.

Combining this with the previous result confirms that the solution
U(x,t)
to (
19

U^{+}_{c^{\star}}(x)
spreads up to
u^{\star}
at speed
\sqrt{2G^{\prime}(0)}
, thus concluding the proof of the proposition. ∎

This therefore gives a weaker sense in which the weak selection principle holds under the assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
.

Appendix A
Hydrodynamic limit of branching Brownian motion

In this appendix, we provide a brief proof of a hydrodynamic limit result for branching Brownian motion for completeness. Although the author believes that the result is well-known in the field, a precise statement and proof of the specific result cannot be found. A somewhat similar result is proven in Appendix A of
[
2
]
. Many of the techniques are identical to those used in the main body of this paper.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\rho^{N}
be a sequence of probability measures such that
\rho^{N}
is the sum of
N
atoms of weight
1/N
, and such that
\rho^{N}\Rightarrow\rho\ll\lambda
as
N\to\infty
. Consider a branching Brownian motion
\{B^{N}_{i}(t):i\in\mathcal{U}^{N}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
which initially has
N
particles and empricial distribution
\rho^{N}
, and in which each particle branches at time-dependent rate
r(t)
. Define

\eta_{t}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{U}^{N}(t)}\delta_{B^{N}_{i}(t)},

so that
\eta_{0}^{N}=\rho^{N}
, and
(\eta_{t}^{N})_{t\geq 0}
is the empirical measure-valued process describing the system. Let
\mathcal{L}^{N}_{T}
be the law of
(\eta_{t}^{N})_{t\in[0,T]}
. Then
\mathcal{L}^{N}_{T}
converges weakly to
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{T}
in the Skorokhod topology, and if
(\eta_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\sim\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{T}
, then
\eta_{t}(dx)=u(x,t)dx
where
u
is the unique classical solution to the PDE
u_{t}=\frac{1}{2}u_{xx}+r(t)u
for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)
and
u(x,0)=\rho(x)
.

Proof.

Note that the BBM process with branching rate
r
is just the
(\psi,r,N)
-BBM without deletion of particles. Therefore reasoning exactly as in Proposition
4
, we can write

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{t}^{N},f(\cdot,t)\rangle=\langle\rho^{N},f(\cdot,t)\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\eta_{s}^{N},\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}(\cdot,s)+f_{t}(\cdot,s)+r(s)f(\cdot,s)\rangle ds+\tilde{M}_{t}^{N,W}+\tilde{M}^{N,P}_{t}

(20)

where
\tilde{M}^{N,W}
and
\tilde{M}^{N,P}
are local martingales with
\mathbb{E}[[\tilde{M}^{N,W}]_{t}]
and
\mathbb{E}[[\tilde{M}^{N,P}]_{t}]
converging to
0
as
N\to\infty
.

Next we prove tightness. We will prove tightness of the sequence
((\eta_{t}^{N})_{t\in[0,T]})_{N=1,2,\ldots}
in the space
\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{v}_{F}(\mathbb{R}))
, where
\mathcal{M}_{F}^{v}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of finite measures on
\mathbb{R}
with the vague topology. It is shown by Roelly-Coppoletta
[
20
]
that to show tightness in this space it is sufficient to show tightness of
(\langle\eta_{t}^{N},f\rangle)_{t\in[0,T]}
for all
f
in a dense subset of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, the space of continuous functions vanishing at infinity. We will take for our dense subset of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
the set
\mathcal{BC}^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty),\mathbb{R})\cap C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
. As in the proof of Proposition
5
, by Aldous’ criterion (Theorem 16.10,
[
4
]
), to prove tightness of
(\langle\eta_{t}^{N},f\rangle)_{t\in[0,T]}
, it is sufficient to show two conditions A and B.

Now the expected number of particles in the BBM with branching rate
r
and with initially
N
particles is
N\exp(\int_0^t r(s)ds)
. Therefore by the Markov inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sup_{0\leq t\leq m}|\langle\eta_{t}^{N},f\rangle|\geq a)\leq\frac{1}{a}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq m}|\langle\eta_{t}^{N},f\rangle|]\leq\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{a}\exp(\int_0^m r(s)ds),

so that for every
m
,
\lim_{a\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\sup_{0\leq t\leq m}|\langle\eta_{t}^{N},f\rangle|\geq a)=0
. This proves condition A. For condition B, take
\epsilon,\eta,m>0
and stopping time
\tau\leq m
. Then by (
20
), since
f
,
f_{xx}
and
r
are all bounded, say by
C
, we have:

\displaystyle|\langle\eta^{N}_{\tau+\delta},f\rangle-\langle\eta^{N}_{\tau},f\rangle|\leq\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\delta}(C+C^{2})\langle\eta_{s}^{N},1\rangle ds+|\tilde{M}_{\tau+\delta}^{N,W}-\tilde{M}_{\tau}|+|\tilde{M}_{\tau+\delta}^{N,P}-\tilde{M}_{\tau}^{N,P}|.

As before, we noted that
\langle\eta_{t}^{N},1\rangle
has expectation
\exp(\int_0^t r(s)ds)
, therefore by the Markov inequality an the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, there exists
\kappa
such that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\langle\eta_{\tau+\delta}^{N},f\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\eta_{\tau}^{N},f\rangle|\geq\epsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(C(1+C)\delta\langle\eta_{m+\delta}^{N},1\rangle\geq\epsilon/3)+\mathbb{P}(|\tilde{M}_{\tau+\delta}^{N,W}-\tilde{M}_{\tau}|^{2}>\epsilon^{2}/9)+\mathbb{P}(|\tilde{M}_{\tau+\delta}^{N,P}-\tilde{M}_{\tau}^{N,P}|^{2}>\epsilon^{2}/9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(3C(1+C)\epsilon\delta\mathbb{E}[\langle\eta_{m+\delta}^{N},1\rangle]+9\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{M}_{\tau+\delta}^{N,W}-\tilde{M}_{\tau}|^{2}]+9\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{M}_{\tau+\delta}^{N,P}-\tilde{M}_{\tau}^{N,P}|^{2}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(3C(1+C)\epsilon\delta e^{(m+\delta)\|r\|_{\infty}}+9\kappa\mathbb{E}[[\tilde{M}^{N,W}]_{\delta}]+9\kappa\mathbb{E}[[\tilde{M}^{N,P}]_{\delta}]\right)\xrightarrow[\delta\to 0,N\to\infty]{}0,

which proves condition B. Therefore we can conclude that the sequence
((\eta_{t}^{N})_{t\in[0,T]})_{N=1,2,\ldots}
is tight. Therefore
(\mathcal{L}^{N}_{T})_{N=1,2,\ldots}
has a subsequential limit
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{T}
to which it converges weakly. Since
N\langle\eta^{N}_{T},1\rangle
has the law of the sum of
N
independent geometric random variables with parameter independent of
N
, thus
\mathbb{E}[\langle\eta_{T}^{N},1\rangle^{2}]
has an upper bound independent of
N
. Thus for
0<s<t<T
, we can bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\langle\eta^{N}_{s},f\rangle-\langle\mu_{t}^{N},f\rangle|^{2}]\leq 9(C^{2}(1+C)^{2}(t-s)^{2}\mathbb{E}[\langle\eta_{T}^{N}\rangle^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{M}^{N,W}_{t}-\tilde{M}^{N,W}_{s}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{M}^{N,P}_{t}-\tilde{M}^{N,P}_{s}|^{2}]),

and hence, as in the proof of Proposition
7

\tilde{C}
such that

\mathbb{E}[|\langle\eta_{s}^{\infty},f\rangle-\langle\eta_{t}^{\infty}\rangle|^{2}]\leq\tilde{C}|s-t|^{2}

. Therefore by the Kolmogorov continuity theorem
\langle\eta_{t}^{\infty},f\rangle
has a continuous modification and hence, as it is also cadlag,

is almost surely continuous (see for example Theorem 1 of
[
21

, and hence
t\mapsto\eta_{t}^{\infty}
is almost surely continuous with respect to the vague topology. Now by the Skorokhod representation theorem, there exist
(\tilde{\eta}^{N}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(\tilde{\eta}^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

\tilde{\eta}^{N}

\tilde{\eta}^{\infty}

\mathbb{P}

(\tilde{\eta}^{N}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\sim\mathcal{L}^{N}_{T}
and
(\tilde{\eta}^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\sim\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{T}

(\tilde{\eta}^{N}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}

(\tilde{\eta}^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
in the Skorokhod topology and

,
\langle\tilde{\eta}^{N}_{t},f(\cdot,t)\rangle
converges
\langle\tilde{\eta}^{\infty}_{t},f(\cdot,t)\rangle
for all
t\in[0,T]
almost surely. Hence by the dominated convergence theorem:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(\tilde{\eta}^{N},f,t)

\displaystyle:=\langle\tilde{\eta}_{t}^{N},f(\cdot,t)\rangle-\langle\rho^{N},f(\cdot,t)\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{\eta}_{s}^{N},\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}(\cdot,s)+f_{t}(\cdot,s)+r(s)f(\cdot,s)\rangle ds

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}}\langle\tilde{\eta}_{t}^{\infty},f(\cdot,t)\rangle-\langle\rho,f(\cdot,t)\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{\eta}^{\infty}_{s},\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}(\cdot,s)+f_{t}(\cdot,s)+r(s)f(\cdot,s)\rangle ds.

Moreover, by (

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{G}(\tilde{\eta}^{N},f,t)^{2}]\leq 4\mathbb{E}[[\tilde{M}^{N,W}]_{t}]+4\mathbb{E}[[\tilde{M}^{N,P}]_{t}]
which converges to

as

\displaystyle|\tilde{G}(\tilde{\eta}^{N},f,t)|=|\langle\tilde{\eta}_{t}^{N},f(\cdot,t)\rangle-\langle\rho^{N},f(\cdot,t)\rangle

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{\eta}_{s}^{N},\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}(\cdot,s)+f_{t}(\cdot,s)+r(s)f(\cdot,s)\rangle ds|

\displaystyle\leq C\langle\tilde{\eta}^{N}_{t},1\rangle+C+(2C+C^{2})t\langle\tilde{\eta}^{N}_{t},1\rangle=:\Sigma_{N}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Sigma_{N}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\Sigma_{N}|\geq K\}}]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\Sigma_{N}^{2}}{K}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\Sigma_{N}|\geq K\}}\right]\leq K^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\Sigma_{N}^{2}].

\mathbb{E}[\langle\eta_{t}^{N},1\rangle]

(|G(\tilde{\eta}^{N},f,t)|)_{N=1,2,\ldots}

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{t}^{\infty},f(\cdot,t)\rangle=\langle\rho,f(\cdot,0)\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\eta_{s}^{\infty},\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}(\cdot,s)+f_{t}(\cdot,s)+r(s)f(\cdot,s)\rangle ds

(21)

for any
f(x,t)\in\mathcal{BC}^{2,1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
. Since the unique classical solution of the PDE
u_{t}=\frac{1}{2}u_{xx}+r(t)u
with
u(x,0)=\rho(x)
certainly solves (
21
), it only remains to show that there is at most one solution to (
21
).

Suppose that
\eta_{t}^{\infty}
and
\hat{\eta}_{t}^{\infty}
are two solutions to (
21
). Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth compactly supported test function, and let
f
be the solution of the backwards heat equation
f_{t}=\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}+rf
with terminal condition
f(x,t)=\phi(x)
. Then it immediately follows that
\langle\eta_{t}^{\infty},\phi\rangle=\langle\hat{\eta}_{t}^{\infty},\phi\rangle
. Thus
\eta_{t}^{\infty}=\hat{\eta}_{t}^{\infty}
for any
t
. Hence
\eta^{\infty}_{t}(dx)=u(x,t)dx
where
u
is the unique classical solution to the PDE
u_{t}=\frac{1}{2}u_{xx}+r(t)u
with
u(x,0)dx=\rho(dx)
, thus concluding the proof.
∎
