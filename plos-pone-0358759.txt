Robust bearing fault diagnosis for rotating machinery under noisy and variable operating conditions using a Wavelet-Mamba Network

Dayou Cui, Zhaoyan Xie, Zhixue Wang, Xiaowei Li, Xiangxuan Meng

ABSTRACT
Bearing fault diagnosis for rotating machinery remains challenging due to strong background noise and variable operating conditions in harsh industrial environments. To address these issues, this paper proposes a robust and lightweight bearing fault diagnosis framework, termed Multi-scale and Dual-scale Wavelet Mamba (MDW-Mamba), for noisy and non-stationary industrial applications. The proposed method employs a multi-stage “noise-feature decoupling” strategy, comprising a statistically guided three-channel input, a Multi-scale Omni-kernel Convolutions (MOM-Conv) backbone, and a Dual-Scale Wavelet Mamba (DSW-Mamba) module. This architecture explicitly separates fault components from broadband noise while enabling efficient long-term dependency modeling. Experimental results on three public datasets under variable-speed conditions demonstrate that MDW-Mamba consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods. It achieves an average classification accuracy exceeding 87% even at a signal-to-noise ratio of −6 dB, showing exceptional robustness against strong interference. Furthermore, the model maintains an ultra-lightweight footprint of 0.26 M parameters and a fast inference time of 1.285 ms, indicating strong potential for real-time, on-board deployment in resource-constrained industrial edge devices.

Introduction The efficient operation of modern industrial sectors relies heavily on the reliability of critical rotating assets, such as wind turbines, aerospace propulsion systems, and heavy-duty manufacturing equipment [ 1 , 2 ]. As critical “joints” of this equipment, the health status of rolling bearings directly determines the safety and efficiency of complex industrial processes [ 3 ]. Unlike stationary equipment in controlled laboratory environments, critical rotating machinery often operates for extended periods in harsh and unpredictable industrial settings. These machines face extremely challenging conditions, enduring violent vibrations, variable alternating impact loads, and complex environmental interference [ 4 ]. Statistics indicate that approximately 45%−55% of rotating machinery failures originate from bearing damage [ 5 ]. Consequently, failures during critical production phases result in severe downtime losses and catastrophic economic consequences. A major challenge is that rotational speeds of industrial equipment fluctuate in real-time according to varying load demands and complex duty cycles. The resulting non-stationary signals are easily submerged by strong background noise, making weak fault features difficult to extract [ 6 ]. Recently, with the advancement of Industrial 4.0 and Intelligent Manufacturing, Deep Learning (DL) methods have gradually overcome the limitations of traditional signal processing. Due to their adaptive feature extraction capabilities, they have become the mainstream research direction for fault diagnosis industrial rotating machinery [ 7 ]. Although deep learning has advanced mechanical fault diagnosis, existing algorithms face challenges under the intense time-varying and high-noise conditions common in complex industrial equipment. Classic CNNs, such as WDCNN [ 8 ] and ResNet18 [ 9 ], are widely used for feature extraction, while Multi-Scale CNNs (MSCNN [ 10 ]) capture multi-granularity fault patterns. However, their local receptive fields limit the capture of global temporal dependencies in long-sequence signals, making them prone to misdiagnosis under strong interference [ 11 ]. To fuse spatiotemporal features, hybrid models like CNN-LSTM [ 12 ] and DCA-BiGRU [ 13 ] utilize recurrent units; however, their serial computation leads to low efficiency and gradient vanishing issues. More recently, domain adaptation and incremental learning strategies have been introduced to handle cross-domain diagnosis under varying operating conditions [ 14 , 15 ], yet these approaches still rely on traditional backbone architectures without addressing the underlying noise-feature mixing problem. Recently, Transformers (e.g., TAR-1D [ 16 ]) have achieved global modeling via self-attention, but their quadratic complexity O ( L 2 ) imposes a heavy burden on resource-constrained industrial edge-side monitoring devices [ 17 ]. In the end, most of the current methods lack a clear frequency domain selection mechanism. In complex broadband noise environments, they often fail to effectively distinguish fault characteristics from random interference, resulting in a significant decrease in diagnostic accuracy. To solve the previously mentioned problems of computational bottlenecks and limited sensing fields, structured state space models (SSMs) represented by mamba have emerged in recent years, which have received great attention as a new sequence model [ 18 , 19 ]. Benefiting from its innovative Selective Scan Mechanism, Mamba not only possesses global long-range dependency capturing capabilities comparable to Transformers but also achieves linear computational complexity O ( L ) that grows with sequence length. This characteristic has shown great potential in fields such as computer vision [ 20 ] and long-sequence time series forecasting [ 21 ], providing an ideal infrastructure for the lightweight deployment of intelligent diagnostic systems for rotating machinery. Although the standard Mamba model has high computing efficiency, it still has inherent limitations when directly applied to fault diagnosis in noisy and complex industrial environments. Recent studies [ 22 , 23 ] show that the pure time domain architecture often fails to make full use of the sparseness of the frequency domain of mechanical vibration signals. As a state space sequence model, Mamba lacks a clear mechanism dedicated to frequency selection and decoupling. Therefore, in the broadband Gaussian noise environment omnipresent in modern industrial operations, it is difficult for the model to distinguish between high-frequency noise components and transient fault pulses, which leads to a serious “feature-noise” mixing phenomenon. To enhance feature extraction from non-stationary signals, the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) serves as a robust multi-resolution analysis tool [ 24 ]. By decomposing signals into sparse sub-bands, DWT facilitates the effective separation of noise from informative components. Therefore, integrating DWT-derived frequency-domain sparsity with Mamba’s global temporal modeling provides a promising framework for developing noise-resistant yet inference-efficient diagnostic architectures for various rotating machinery, such as the proposed MDW-Mamba model. Recent cross-domain research has begun exploring frequency-domain perception. In computer vision, WaveMamba [ 25 ] and IRSRMamba [ 26 ] integrated wavelet transforms with Mamba to enhance texture and structural modeling in 2D image tasks. Following this, Yang et al. [ 27 ] applied Discrete Multi-Wavelet Transform (DMWT) with Mamba to address few-shot fault diagnosis, where the primary challenge is data scarcity rather than noise interference. More recently, Lai et al. [ 28 ] combined CNN and Mamba-enhanced LSTM for bearing fault diagnosis, yet their approach focuses on multi-channel feature fusion without explicit frequency-domain noise decoupling. Despite these advances, existing wavelet-Mamba methods differ fundamentally from our work in two aspects. First, prior 1D fault diagnosis works [ 27 , 28 ] employ wavelets primarily as a feature enrichment tool under limited data conditions, rather than as a noise-feature decoupling mechanism under extreme noise. Second, they have not explored wavelet-guided selective state scanning for 1D strong background noise separation, nor designed specific frequency-drift compensation mechanisms for non-stationary industrial operating conditions where rotational speed varies continuously. The proposed MDW-Mamba addresses these gaps by integrating DWT as a frequency-selective prior that explicitly guides Mamba’s state update process to prioritize fault-related subbands while suppressing broadband noise, achieving robust diagnosis at SNR as low as −6 dB. Addressing these challenges, this paper proposes the MDW-Mamba network with the “noise-feature decoupling” mechanism as the core. Based on the research results of Lai et al. [ 28 ], the framework adopts a three-channel input strategy based on statistical filtering and combines with the mixed multi-view convolution (MOM-Conv) backbone network to capture local multi-scale features and achieve initial noise reduction. The core innovation point solves Mamba’s limitations in frequency domain selectivity on the basis of the framework proposed by Yang et al. [ 27 ].Unlike the previous “wavelet + Mamba” method, which mainly aimed at the complement of fewer sample features, this study uses the inherent frequency sparseness of wavelet transformation to guide the selective state scanning mechanism of Mamba. By focusing on a specific subband dominated by fault-induced transients, the model can achieve effective physical separation of fault-related characteristics and noise even under extreme conditions of −6 decibels. The main contributions of this paper are as follows: (1) The MDW-Mamba framework is proposed for the fault diagnosis of rotating machinery in high-noise industrial environments. By combining statistical filtering with MOM-Conv, the framework overcomes the problems of limited sensing field of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and high computational complexity of Transformer, and achieves an effective balance between diagnostic accuracy and reasoning efficiency. (2) The DSW-Mamba module has been developed to introduce frequency domain perception capabilities. Unlike prior wavelet-Mamba approaches that use wavelets primarily for feature enrichment under data scarcity [ 27 ] or vision tasks [ 25 ], our module employs discrete wavelet transformation (DWT) to explicitly realize “noise-feature decoupling” and guides Mamba’s selective state update mechanism to focus exclusively on the subband containing fault-related information. This design addresses the spectral mixing problem under broadband noise, significantly improving the robustness of the model under extreme noise conditions. (3) A large number of experiments conducted on three public data sets prove the superiority of MDW-Mamba. It has only 260,000 parameters, which is superior to the mainstream model in noisy environments and non-stationary operating conditions, which confirms its potential for real-time and edge-side deployment in industrial condition monitoring. Proposed method MDW-Mamba overview In order to achieve high-precision fault diagnosis of rotating machinery under strong noise and complex industrial environments, this paper proposes a hierarchical architecture called MDW-Mamba (Multi-scale and Dual-scale Wavelet Mamba). As shown in Fig 1 , this architecture is specially designed to cope with challenges such as speed fluctuations, load changes and broadband environmental noise common in modern industrial processes. It overcomes the limited sensing field of traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) [ 29 ] and the problem that Transformer calculates too much complexity when modeling long sequences [ 30 ]. These problems often hinder their actual deployment on resource-constrained industrial edge devices. 10.1371/journal.pone.0358759.g001 Fig 1 The overall architecture of the proposed MDW-Mamba framework. The hierarchical architecture for bearing fault diagnosis comprises four sequential stages: multi-channel input construction, a multi-scale feature extraction backbone (MOM-Conv), dual-scale refinement and fusion (DSW-Mamba), and a classification head. The workflow of MDW-Mamba includes three consecutive stages: multi-channel signal reconstruction, adaptive multi-scale feature extraction and global time-frequency refinement. First of all, the model does not directly process the original one-dimensional signal, but uses the prior knowledge of statistical filtering to reconstruct the single-variable sequence into a three-channel tensor with physical significance to improve the signal-to-noise ratio of the input signal. Then, the data enters the backbone network composed of stacked multi-scale Omni-Kernel Convolution (MOM-Conv) modules. Unlike fixed nuclear convolution, MOM-Conv integrates the characteristics of different expansion rate branches through the adaptive gate control mechanism, and can capture short-term transient signals and long-term periodic fluctuations at the same time. In order to further suppress noise and capture global dependencies, the Dual-Scale Wavelet Mamba (DSW-Mamba) module adopts Discrete Wavelet Transform, (DWT) decouples the input signal into low-frequency and high-frequency subbands. This module uses Mamba to model long program columns of low-frequency components, and filters out high-frequency noise with the help of the convolutional layer. Finally, local and global characteristics are integrated through residual connections, and the failure probability is generated through the Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer and the subsequent full connection layer. Multi-Scale Omni-Kernel Convolution (MOM-Conv) module The waveform of the bearing fault signal usually presents complex multi-scale characteristics: the accurate positioning of the weak early fault characteristics needs to be completed by relying on a small receptive field; while the capture of the periodic characteristics of the complex modulation edge frequency band requires a larger receptive field. The traditional convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture adopts a fixed convolutional kernel size, which is difficult to reconcile this inherent contradiction. In response to this problem, this study proposes a MOM-Conv module based on the idea of full-dimensional dynamic convolution. Dual-Scale Wavelet Mamba Transform (DSW-Mamba) Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are good at local feature extraction, but the limitations of their receptive fields make it difficult to effectively model the long-range dependent features of fault evolution. Although Transformer has excellent global modeling capabilities, it is limited by the complexity of quadratic computing, and it is difficult to adapt to long-sequence signal analysis tasks. The Mamba architecture based on the selective state space model (SSMs), although the computational complexity is reduced to the linear level, it is directly used for noisy high-frequency vibration signals, which is very prone to overfitting. The inherent suitability of SSMs for low-frequency modeling stems from their mathematical formulation. From a state-space perspective, the continuous-time dynamics 𝐀 in Eq 7 govern the rate at which past information is propagated or attenuated. Low-frequency signals, characterized by smooth temporal evolution and strong inter-sample correlations, align naturally with the Markovian structure of the hidden state h ( t ), where the state upda t e h ˙ ( t ) = 𝐀 h ( t ) + 𝐁 u ( t ) acts as an implicit low-pass filter by exponentially decaying high-frequency perturbations relative to the system’s eigendynamics. Furthermore, during discretization via Zero-Order Hold (ZOH), the timescale parameter Δ introduces a Nyquist-type constraint: frequencies exceeding 1 / ( 2 Δ ) are inherently aliased and cannot be reliably encoded [ 18 ]. This means that when raw high-frequency vibration signals containing broadband noise are directly fed into the Mamba encoder, the stochastic fluctuations in the noise-dominant bands force the state transition matrices to fit spurious high-frequency patterns, leading to overfitting. Conversely, the low-frequency approximation coefficients 𝐗 L produced by DWT possess a compact spectral distribution that matches the SSM’s implicit bandwidth constraints, enabling stable and robust long-range dependency modeling without overfitting. As illustrated in the low-frequency path of Fig 3 , the Mamba block employed in our DSW-Mamba module follows the structured state-space model (SSM) architecture introduced by Gu and Dao [ 18 ]. The block processes the low-frequency feature sequence 𝐗 L ∈ ℝ B × L l × D through the following stages: (1) Linear projection : The input is projected into a higher-dimensional space via a learnable linear layer, producing the activation x . (2) 1D convolution : A depthwise causal convolution with kernel size d c o n v = 4 is applied along the sequence dimension to incorporate local context, followed by a SiLU activation. (3) Selective state-space scan : The convolved features are discretized using the ZOH method ( Eq. 8 ) with a learnable timescale parameter Δ , and processed by the selective scan algorithm (SSM) that computes the output y via the recurrent dynamics in Eq. 7 . The key innovation of the selective mechanism lies in the input-dependent parameterization of Δ , 𝐁 , and 𝐂 , which allows the model to selectively focus on informative segments of the sequence while ignoring irrelevant noise. (4) Gated output : The SSM output is multiplied element-wise with a gated branch (the SiLU-activated z from a parallel linear projection), forming a gated linear unit (GLU)-style output. (5) Residual connection : The output is added to the original input via a skip connection, followed by layer normalization. This design enables the Mamba block to capture long-range dependencies efficiently with O ( L ) complexity, while its data-dependent gating mechanism naturally suppresses noise components when processing the fault-specific low-frequency subband. In addition to the core filter logic, this study introduces a dual skip connection architecture (marked as skip connection 1 and skip connection 2 in Fig 3 ) to optimize the gradient propagation process and achieve feature reuse. The dual-path design serves two functions: on the one hand, it can reduce the potential instability of deep network training, and on the other hand, it can ensure the priority learning of the purified signal envelope characteristics in the global modeling stage. By avoiding the interference from residual noise through this connection structure, the overall robustness of the model framework to external disturbances is significantly improved. Optimization objectives and learning strategy The MDW-Mamba framework adopts an end-to-end strategy for the accurate identification of rotating mechanical fault types. The model training process revolves around the calculation of the loss function and the iterative optimization of parameters, aiming to realize the convergence optimization of the model by minimizing the difference between the predicted distribution and the real label. Definition of loss function Let the training dataset be denoted as 𝒟 = { ( 𝐱 i , y i ) } i = 1 N , where 𝐱 i represents the input tensor, y i is the ground truth fault label, and C denotes the total number of classes. During the forward propagation phase, the classification head first outputs an unnormalized logit vector 𝐳 i = [ z i , 1 , . . . , z i , C ] ∈ ℝ C . To map this vector to a probability distribution, the Softmax function is introduced to calculate the predicted probability p ^ i , c of sample i belonging to class c : p ^ i , c = S o f t m a x ( z i , c ) = exp ( z i , c ) ∑ j = 1 C exp ( z i , j ) (10) To quantify the divergence between the predicted probability distribution and the true label distribution, the Multi-Class Cross-Entropy Loss ( ℒ C E ) is adopted as the global optimization objective. This function drives parameter updates by maximizing the log-likelihood of the correct class, defined mathematically as: ℒ C E = − 1 N ∑ i = 1 N ∑ c = 1 C 𝕀 ( y i = c ) log ( p ^ i , c ) (11) Here, 𝕀 ( · ) denotes the indicator function, when the condition is met, its value is 1, and if not, it is 0. The loss function promotes model learning fault characteristics with high discrimination by minimizing the KL dispersion (Kullback-Leibler) of the predicted distribution and the real label distribution. Parameter optimization strategy To guarantee stable convergence within the complex high-dimensional parameter space and avoid falling into a local optimal solution, this study adopts the AdamW optimizer [ 35 , 36 ]. Unlike the traditional Adam algorithm, AdamW decouples weight attenuation from the gradient update mechanism. Its mathematical expression is defined as follows: θ t + 1 = θ t − η t ( 𝐦 ^ t 𝐯 ^ t + ϵ + λ θ t ) (12) Here, the update for parameters θ at step t is driven by a learning rate η t and bias-corrected moment es t imates ( m ^ t , v ^ t ). Unlike standard Adam, the inclusion of the λ θ t term applies the weight decay coefficient λ directly to the current weights. This explicit constraint on the parameter norm acts as a safeguard against overfitting, a feature especially critical for maintaining MDW-Mamba’s diagnostic stability when processing heavily corrupted vibration signals. Materials and datasets Although the data set used by this research institute comes from a controllable laboratory environment, the non-steady speed characteristics and complex vibration laws it contains are highly compatible with the actual harsh operating conditions of industrial rotating machinery. This kind of data set can fully reflect the dynamic load change characteristics of heavy-duty operations, so it can be used as a representative benchmark set. In view of the difference between laboratory data and the random working conditions of real-world industrial systems, as well as the strong background interference caused by mechanical resonance and environmental disturbances during operation, this study introduces controllable add-on noise in the test stage. Through the systematic reproduction of complex interference in harsh industrial environments, the robustness of the model and the generalization ability in unconstrained actual working conditions can be verified. Aiming to validate of the proposed method, the research selected three mainstream public datasets, namely the University of Ottawa (Ottawa) dataset, Xi’an Jiaotong University (XJTU-SY) dataset and Jiangnan University (JNU) dataset. so as to match the unpredictability of real-world industrial environments, this study adopts a hybrid working condition division strategy to integrate the full-load and full-speed samples into a unified data set and randomly divide the training set and the test set according to 8:2. This setting can guide the model to learn the invariant characteristics of working conditions that are insensitive to rotational speed and load fluctuations, so as to verify the comprehensive generalization ability of the model in complex industrial scenarios, rather than the performance under single working conditions. Experimental results and validation In order to verify the effectiveness and generalized robustness of the MDW-Mamba model, this paper carries out a series of rigorous performance evaluation experiments on the proposed network architecture. The original design intention of this kind of experiment is to specifically test the anti-interference ability of the model under strong background noise and non-steady instantaneous shock signals prevalent in harsh industrial environments. Experimental setup and implementation details All experiments are implemented using PyTorch 2.6. Dataset preprocessing and segmentation. For all three benchmark datasets (Ottawa, XJTU-SY, and JNU), the raw vibration signals are segmented into non-overlapping samples with a fixed length of L = 1024 points per sample. No overlap ratio is applied during segmentation to ensure strict independence between training and test samples. The number of samples generated per class is determined by the total signal length of each fault type divided by the segment length, yielding approximately 500–2,000 samples per class depending on the dataset. The sampling frequencies of the original recordings are as follows: Ottawa dataset at 200 kHz, XJTU-SY at 25.6 kHz, and JNU at 50 kHz. To simulate realistic industrial noise environments, additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) at various signal-to-noise ratios (SNR: 6, 3, 0, −3, −6 dB) is injected into the test set only, leaving the training set in clean condition. Training configuration and hyperparameters. Each dataset is randomly split into training (80%) and test (20%) sets with a fixed random seed (seed = 42) to ensure reproducibility. To further enhance generalization under variable operating conditions, the training samples from all working conditions within each dataset are pooled together (mixed-condition training). The AdamW optimizer [ 35 , 36 ] is employed with an initial learning rate of 3 × 10 − 4 , weight decay coefficient of 1 × 10 − 4 , and betas = (0.9, 0.999). A cosine annealing scheduler with a minimum learning rate of 1 × 10 − 6 is used to adjust the learning rate over 100 training epochs. The batch size is set to 32. To ensure statistical reliability, each experiment is repeated 10 times with independent random seeds, and results are reported as mean ± standard deviation. Baseline model configuration. For fair comparison, all baseline models (WDCNN, CNNS-LSTM, MSCNN, ResNet18-1D, DCA-BiGRU, and TAR-1D) are trained using the same data splits, preprocessing pipeline, optimizer settings, and evaluation protocol described above. The specific architectural hyperparameters (e.g., number of layers, kernel sizes, hidden dimensions) for each baseline are adopted from their respective original publications. No dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning is performed for any baseline model to maintain consistency in cross-model comparison. The performance attenuation variant of “no DWT” has obvious performance attenuation, which shows that processing high-noise vibration signals only in the time domain often forms a performance bottleneck. Discrete wavelet transform (DWT) can effectively decompose the signal into sparse frequency bands, so that the Mamba module can focus on specific subbands rich in impact feature information for feature extraction. In addition, compared with the Transformer variant model, the selective scanning mechanism of MDW-Mamba is more robust. When dealing with long non-steady vibration sequences, Transformer is prone to distraction problems, and there are defects in square-level calculation complexity; and the linear recursion and selective filtering characteristics of Mamba are more suitable for capturing the instantaneous and intermittent impact features of rotating machinery fault signals. To further investigate the interaction effects between key components, we conduct combined ablation experiments by simultaneously removing multiple modules. Among the 26 possible multi-module combinations, we select four representative configurations based on a systematic criterion with three explicit constraints: (1) Orthogonal processing stages — each pair targets a distinct functional interaction axis (input preprocessing, time-domain feature extraction, frequency-domain refinement, training regularization), avoiding semantically overlapping combinations; (2) Full module coverage — all five key modules (3-Channel, MOMConv, DWT, Mamba, Aug) appear in at least one ablated combination; (3) Non-redundancy — combinations whose residual architectures would reduce to near-equivalent forms (e.g., three-module removals dominated by a shared two-module subset) are excluded, ensuring each selected combination yields a qualitatively distinct diagnostic signature. The selected configurations are: (1) w/o (MOMConv + DWT) : removes both the multi-scale feature extractor (MOMConv) and the wavelet-domain refiner (DWT), representing the interaction between the two core feature extraction layers operating in the time and frequency domains, respectively. For example, on the Ottawa dataset at − 6 dB, w/o (MOMConv + DWT) achieves 80.23 ± 0.53 %, which is lower than both w/o MOMConv ( 85.84 ± 0.95 %) and w/o DWT ( 81.67 ± 1.60 %), confirming that the two modules contribute complementary and non-redundant features. The w/o (DWT + Mamba) configuration exhibits a similar trend ( 80.48 ± 0.49 % on Ottawa), indicating comparable degradation when the wavelet refiner and the sequence model are removed together. The w/o (MOMConv + Aug) configuration, which removes both the multi-scale backbone and the data augmentation strategy, reveals a sharp performance decline across all three datasets. On Ottawa, this configuration drops to 27.82 ± 0.56 % at − 6 dB, demonstrating that the combination of architectural feature extraction and training-time enhancement is critical for noise robustness. On XJTU, the degradation is less severe ( 78.55 ± 0.61 % at − 6 dB), consistent with XJTU’s overall lower sensitivity to individual component removals. On JNU, the accuracy falls to 22.14 ± 1.23 %, approaching the random-guessing baseline, underscoring the essential role of both MOMConv and augmentation in challenging variable-speed scenarios. The triple-ablation w/o (3-Channel + MOMConv + DWT) further degrades performance to 76.25 ± 0.74 % on Ottawa, 76.95 ± 0.65 % on XJTU, and 28.73 ± 1.18 % on JNU, illustrating the cumulative effect of architectural simplification when the input preprocessing, multi-scale backbone, and frequency-domain refiner are simultaneously removed. These results collectively corroborate the single-ablation findings and confirm that each component in MDW-Mamba makes a distinct and indispensable contribution to overall noise robustness. Discussion This section provides an in-depth analysis of the performance of MDW-Mamba in rotating machinery operating environments from five dimensions: model mechanism, the contribution of data priors, interpretability, wavelet basis selection, and potential limitations. Synergy between frequency-domain decoupling and long-range dependencies Experimental results show that the performance of the MDW-Mamba model is significantly better than that of the traditional convolutional neural network (CNN) and Transformer benchmark models, especially in the environment of −6 dB low signal-to-noise ratio, its advantages are more significant. The core of the performance advantage of this model comes from the effective suppression of the phenomenon of “spectral mixing” - under the action of this phenomenon, fault-related transient signals and broadband environmental interference signals are prone to superimposed coupling, which makes it difficult to distinguish between the two. Because the traditional CNN model focuses on the capture of local high-frequency texture characteristics, it is easy to misjudge environmental noise as a fault signal. Although the Transformer model has a global sensing field, it lacks a clear frequency discrimination and screening mechanism, and it is difficult to adapt to complex noise interference scenarios. In response to the inherent defects of the above-mentioned existing model, the MDW-Mamba model has built an effective solution by introducing discrete wavelet transform (DWT) as a mechanism a priori filter. The model can decompose non-steady signals into sparse and clearly distinguishable frequency bands, thus achieving more targeted Selective Scan. This mechanism can guide the model to accurately avoid interference in the noise-dominated area and focus attention on specific subbands containing core diagnostic information. This design paradigm, which integrates physical a priori knowledge and data-driven learning, fundamentally improves the robustness and reliability of fault feature extraction in a low-SNR industrial environment. Role of statistical priors under extreme operating conditions The analysis results of the ablation experiment show that there are obvious performance bottlenecks in the pure data-driven model in a strong interference environment. When the signal-to-noise ratio is as low as −6 dB in extreme conditions, the diagnostic accuracy of the single-channel model drops sharply to 18.95%, almost failing; while the three-channel MDW-Mamba network can still maintain more than 87% robust performance. This performance difference highlights the inherent limitations of the traditional model in dealing with the environmental noise of random rotating machinery. When the fault-associated transient signal is submerged by the noise in the original signal, the auxiliary mean filter channel and the median filter channel can provide key low-frequency tracks and energy envelopes. This kind of statistical a priori can effectively act as a “soft attention” mechanism. Even if the main vibration data is seriously polluted, it can still guide the model to focus on the effective fault characteristics. In summary, in the dynamic and changing rotating machinery operation environment, the integration of multi-channel statistical a priori is the core prerequisite for ensuring the reliability of diagnosis. Interpretability considerations and connection to XAI In safety-critical industrial applications, understanding why a model reaches a particular diagnostic decision is as important as the classification accuracy itself [ 43 ]. The MDW-Mamba framework incorporates several design choices that inherently enhance its interpretability compared to purely black-box models. At the architectural level, the DSW-Mamba module’s “divide-and-conquer” strategy via DWT provides a physically meaningful decomposition of vibration signals into frequency subbands. The selective scanning mechanism of Mamba operating on the low-frequency approximation coefficients is explicitly guided by the spectral sparsity of fault-related transients, meaning that the model’s internal representations can be traced back to specific frequency components. This ante-hoc interpretability aligns with the taxonomy proposed in recent XAFD (Explainable AI for Fault Diagnosis) literature [ 43 ], which advocates for embedding physical priors into network design rather than relying solely on post-hoc explanation methods such as SHAP or Grad-CAM. Furthermore, the three-channel input construction (raw, mean-filtered, median-filtered) serves as an additional interpretable layer: the contribution of each channel’s statistical prior to the final prediction can be quantified through channel-wise ablation analysis. In extreme noise conditions, the mean and median channels consistently show higher importance weights, providing a clear mechanistic explanation for the model’s noise robustness. Recent work on Kolmogorov-Arnold-informed graph wavelet networks [ 44 ] has further demonstrated that integrating wavelet theory with learnable activation functions can yield both high diagnostic accuracy and model transparency. While MDW-Mamba currently employs fixed Haar wavelets rather than learnable wavelet bases, its frequency-domain decoupling strategy already embodies the same design philosophy: leveraging mathematical transforms with known physical interpretations to constrain and guide deep feature learning. A promising direction for future work would be to incorporate learnable wavelet parameters or attention-based frequency selection mechanisms [ 43 ], further bridging the gap between discriminative power and interpretability in deep learning-based fault diagnosis. Limitation analysis Although MDW-Mamba has achieved effective coordination of diagnostic accuracy and parameter efficiency, the computational loss caused by the frequency domain transformation process makes its single-sample reasoning delay reach 1.285 ms. The delay index is slightly higher than that of shallow network architectures such as WDCNN. In high-speed rotating machinery monitoring scenarios, sub-millisecond response is the core requirement, so this delay characteristic may become a performance bottleneck in practical applications. In addition, the model performance is still limited by high-quality annotation data sets. In the actual industrial operation process, there are many practical challenges in collecting labeled fault data. This dependence on annotation data seriously restricts the effective deployment of the model in unconstrained industrial environments. Conclusions In view of the bearing fault diagnosis problem caused by strong background noise and variable working condition coupling in the industrial operating environment, this paper proposes an MDW-Mamba network. The network framework embeds a multi-stage noise-feature decoupling mechanism to suppress broadband interference in the state space model from the mechanism level. By introducing discrete wavelet transform (DWT), the non-steady vibration signal is decomposed into multiple frequency bands, so that the Mamba architecture has clear frequency selectivity; at the same time, statistical filtering priors are integrated into the input stage to ensure that even if the fault features are severely masked by noise, the network can still maintain a high sensitivity to the fault features. The empirical evaluation results based on the Ottawa, XJTU-SY and JNU data sets show that the MDW-Mamba network performs well under mixed working conditions. Especially when the signal-to-noise ratio is as low as −6 dB, the average diagnostic accuracy of the network can still reach more than 87%. Compared with the standard ResNet and Transformer models, the performance has improved by about 20%. In addition, the network parameter scale is only 0.26M, which achieves an excellent balance between lightweight design and diagnostic robustness. Future work will focus on addressing current limitations: firstly, by employing model pruning and operator acceleration to reduce the computational latency of frequency-domain transforms, facilitating deployment on low-power embedded devices in industrial monitoring systems; and secondly, by integrating unsupervised domain adaptation or few-shot learning techniques to reduce reliance on large-scale labeled data, thereby enhancing the practical utility of MDW-Mamba in unconstrained industrial scenarios. This study provides a novel paradigm of “Multi-Channel Input + Time-Frequency Fusion” for the diagnosis of non-stationary vibration signals in rotating machinery.
