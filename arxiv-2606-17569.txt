Search for a Time-Dependent Z ′ Z^{\prime} Resonance in the Dimuon Channel
Marlon P. Brade 1,2,3, Jeremiah D. Juevesano 2,3,4, Venus Abbegaile S. Carbonel 2,3, Karen E. Bustamante 2,3, Dennis C. Arogancia 2,3, Jan Mickelle V. Maratas 2,3

Abstract
We present a time-domain search strategy for resonances with periodically varying masses in high-energy collider data. Conventional resonance searches rely on time-integrated event samples and are therefore insensitive to signals whose properties evolve during data taking. To address this limitation, we develop a two-dimensional unbinned likelihood framework in invariant mass and time, allowing the reconstruction of signals that trace nontrivial trajectories in the ( m , t ) (m,t) plane rather than appearing as stationary resonances. As a benchmark scenario, we consider a Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson arising from a gauged U ​ ( 1 ) B − L U(1)_{B-L} extension of the Standard Model and introduce a phenomenological model in which the mediator mass undergoes periodic temporal modulation. The resulting signal manifests as a resonance whose position changes with time, producing distinctive patterns that are inaccessible to conventional analyses based solely on invariant-mass information. The method is implemented using dimuon events from the CMS Open Data corresponding to the Run G data-taking period at s = 13 ​ TeV \sqrt{s}=13~\mathrm{TeV} , with an integrated luminosity of 7.54 ​ fb − 1 7.54~\mathrm{fb}^{-1} . We derive upper limits on the gauge coupling g ′ g^{\prime} as a function of the Z ′ Z^{\prime} mass for several choices of the modulation amplitude. The results demonstrate that incorporating temporal information can enhance sensitivity relative to standard time-integrated searches, particularly in regions with sufficient signal statistics.

Among these possibilities, ultralight bosonic dark matter has attracted significant interest due to its distinctive wave-like behavior on macroscopic scales
[

. For sufficiently small masses, the large occupation number allows it to be described as a coherent classical field rather than a collection of individual particles. This field undergoes oscillations with a frequency set by its mass and can remain coherent over astrophysical timescales. Such behavior arises naturally in scenarios where the dark matter abundance is generated via the misalignment mechanism. A direct consequence is the possibility of inducing time-dependent effects in particle physics observables, leading to signatures that are not captured by conventional time-integrated searches. These time-varying phenomena provide a complementary avenue for probing dark matter interactions.The possibility that ultralight scalar dark matter induces a time-dependent modulation of vector boson masses has been explored in previous studies, particularly in the context of dark photon models where the effect arises through kinetic mixing

In this work, we extend this framework to the case of a

(m,t)

To address this, we develop a time-domain search strategy based on a two-dimensional likelihood in invariant mass and time. This approach retains the correlation between these observables and allows for the direct reconstruction of the time-dependent signal.

Although the analysis is presented in the context of the
B-L
model, the methodology is formulated at the level of collider observables and can be applied to a broader class of scenarios involving time-dependent mediator properties. This provides a complementary strategy for probing new physics that would otherwise remain hidden in conventional time-integrated searches. The method is implemented using dimuon events from the CMS Open Data corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
7.54\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
from the Run G data-taking period. The use of a single data-taking period provides a relatively consistent set of detector and running conditions while preserving the temporal structure necessary for a time-dependent analysis. Although Run G consists of multiple individual runs, the dataset retains a well-defined time profile that can be incorporated through the time-dependent normalization procedure described later. This choice reduces additional complications associated with combining heterogeneous data-taking periods and provides a controlled environment for establishing the feasibility of the proposed time-domain search strategy.

1.1
Model

We consider an extension of the Standard Model by an additional Abelian
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge symmetry with gauge boson
Z^{\prime}_{\mu}
. The effective Lagrangian is given by

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}Z^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}Z^{\prime\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{0Z^{\prime}}^{\,2}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}Z^{\prime\mu}+g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{Z^{\prime}}\,,

(1)

where
Z^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}Z^{\prime}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}
,
g_{Z^{\prime}}
is the gauge coupling, and
J^{\mu}_{Z^{\prime}}
is the corresponding fermionic current.

We introduce a complex scalar field
\phi
that constitutes the dark matter and carries
U(1)^{\prime}
charge
Q_{\phi}
. The field evolves according to

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+m_{\phi}^{2}\phi=0\,,

(2)

and at late times behaves as a coherently oscillating classical background,

\phi(t)\simeq\phi_{1}\cos(m_{\phi}t)+\phi_{2}\sin(m_{\phi}t)\,.

(3)

The coupling of
\phi
to the gauge field arises through the covariant derivative,

D_{\mu}\phi=\left(\partial_{\mu}-ig_{Z^{\prime}}Q_{\phi}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\right)\phi\,,

(4)

which generates the interaction

|D_{\mu}\phi|^{2}\supset(g_{Z^{\prime}}Q_{\phi})^{2}|\phi|^{2}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}Z^{\prime\mu}\,.

(5)

This term contributes to the effective mass of the
Z^{\prime}
in the presence of the oscillating background,

m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}(t)=m_{0Z^{\prime}}^{\,2}+(g_{Z^{\prime}}Q_{\phi})^{2}|\phi(t)|^{2}\,.

(6)

Using the oscillatory solution, the scalar bilinear can be expressed as a periodic function, leading to a time-dependent
Z^{\prime}
mass of the form

m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}(t)=\tilde{m}_{0}^{\,2}\left(1+\kappa\cos^{2}(m_{\phi}t)\right),

(7)

where
\tilde{m}_{0}
is an effective mass parameter and
\kappa
quantifies the modulation amplitude. This parametrization captures the essential feature of a periodically varying mediator mass induced by the ultralight dark matter field.

2
Methodology

In this work, we develop a time-dependent search strategy for a neutral vector boson
Z^{\prime}
in scenarios where its effective mass is modulated by a coherently oscillating ultralight scalar background. This leads to a phenomenology that differs substantially from conventional resonance searches, where the signal is assumed to appear as a stationary peak in invariant mass distributions. In the present scenario, the resonance mass evolves in time, producing a signal with a non-trivial structure in the joint invariant mass–time plane. Consequently, analyses based solely on time-integrated invariant mass spectra can significantly dilute the signal, particularly when the modulation amplitude is large and the resonance sweeps across a broad mass range. To retain sensitivity to this behavior, we construct a two-dimensional likelihood in the observable space
(m,t)
, where
m\equiv m_{\mu\mu}
is the dimuon invariant mass and
t
is the event time. The signal probability density function (PDF) is explicitly defined in this two-dimensional space, allowing the correlation between invariant mass and time to be treated directly within the likelihood. Unlike standard bump hunts, where sensitivity arises from localized excesses in invariant mass, the signal considered here appears as a time-dependent trajectory in the
(m,t)
plane. Preserving this structure is therefore essential, motivating the use of a fully unbinned likelihood that maximizes the information retained from the data.

Signal and Background Probability Distribution Function

The analysis is performed in the two-dimensional observable space defined by the invariant mass
m
and event time
t
. Figure
1
shows the invariant mass distribution of the selected dimuon events together with the fitted background model. Good agreement is observed over the full mass range considered in the analysis, including both the resonant
Z
-boson peak and the non-resonant Drell–Yan continuum at higher masses.

The signal PDF is constructed to model the time-dependent mass of the
Z^{\prime}
boson induced by the oscillating scalar field background. At fixed time, the invariant mass distribution is described by a Gaussian function,

S(m,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}}\exp\left[-\frac{(m-\mu(t))^{2}}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}}\right],

(8)

where
\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}
denotes the detector mass resolution. For simplicity, the resolution is taken to scale linearly with the nominal resonance mass according to
\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}=0.01\,m_{0}
,
which approximates the expected detector behavior over the mass range relevant to this study. The time-dependent mean of the resonance is parameterized as

\mu(t)=m_{0}\sqrt{1+\kappa\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi t}{\tau}+\delta\right)},

(9)

where
m_{0}
denotes the nominal resonance mass,
\kappa
controls the modulation amplitude,
\tau
is the oscillation period, and
\delta
is an arbitrary phase offset. This parametrization provides a minimal phenomenological description of a resonance whose mass undergoes periodic modulation due to the coherent oscillation of the scalar dark matter field. The explicit time dependence induces a non-factorizable correlation between invariant mass and event time, providing the key experimental signature targeted in this work. We consider a benchmark scalar mass of

m_{\phi}\simeq 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{eV}
,
which corresponds to a modulation period of

\tau\simeq 20660~\mathrm{s}
(
\approx 5.7
hours). This benchmark is particularly attractive because it lies in a regime where the oscillation is neither too rapid to be washed out by limited event statistics nor too slow to produce only a fraction of a cycle during data taking. Instead, the associated timescale permits the observation of numerous oscillation periods across the Run G dataset while retaining sufficient statistics within each period to reconstruct the evolving resonance structure. Moreover, the expected coherence time of a field with this mass greatly exceeds the duration of the dataset, ensuring that the modulation remains phase coherent and can be modeled as a stable periodic signal throughout the analysis.

To account for variations in data-taking conditions, the signal PDF is multiplied by a time-dependent acceptance function
T(t)
,

P_{\mathrm{sig}}(m,t)=S(m,t)\,T(t).

(10)

The background contribution is modeled as the sum of two components corresponding to resonant
Z
-boson production and the non-resonant Drell–Yan continuum,

P_{\mathrm{bkg}}(m,t)=P_{Z}(m,t)+P_{\mathrm{DY}}(m,t).

(11)

The resonant
Z
-boson contribution is modeled using a double Crystal Ball (DCB) function, which describes the Gaussian core together with asymmetric non-Gaussian tails arising from detector resolution effects and final-state radiation. The DCB shape parameters are fixed using CMS Open Data simulation samples. The non-resonant Drell–Yan contribution is modeled using a log-curved power-law function,

B_{\mathrm{DY}}(m)\propto m^{-(n+b\log m)},

(12)

which provides a flexible parametrization of the smoothly falling invariant mass spectrum over a broad mass range. Both background components are multiplied by a common time-dependent acceptance function
T(t)
, shown in Figure
2
. This function is constructed as a stepwise constant distribution over individual data-taking runs. For each run
i
, defined over the interval
[t_{i}^{\min},t_{i}^{\max})
, the acceptance is assigned the value

T(t)=\frac{N_{Z}^{(i)}}{\Delta t_{i}},\qquad t\in[t_{i}^{\min},t_{i}^{\max}),

(13)

where
N_{Z}^{(i)}
denotes the number of events in the invariant mass window

80~\mathrm{GeV}<m_{\mu\mu}<100~\mathrm{GeV},

(14)

and

\Delta t_{i}=t_{i}^{\max}-t_{i}^{\min}

(15)

is the corresponding run duration. This procedure provides a data-driven estimate of the relative normalization across runs and effectively accounts for variations in instantaneous luminosity and detector efficiency throughout data taking. The background PDFs are therefore expressed as

\displaystyle P_{Z}(m,t)

\displaystyle=Z(m)\,T(t),

(16)

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{DY}}(m,t)

\displaystyle=B_{\mathrm{DY}}(m)\,T(t).

-boson event yield in individual data-taking runs. Each step corresponds to a distinct run with an approximately constant event rate. The figure displays only a small portion of the full analysis time interval (

The full likelihood model is constructed as an extended sum of signal and background contributions with independent yield parameters determined from the fit. The use of a common acceptance function for both background components reflects the assumption that Standard Model processes follow the same underlying data-taking conditions. In contrast, the signal retains a non-factorizable dependence through the time-dependent mean
\mu(t)
, producing correlations between invariant mass and time that are absent in the background model. This non-factorizable structure forms the central feature exploited by the analysis to distinguish a time-dependent signal from Standard Model backgrounds. The overall structure of the background model and the observed data in the
(m,t)
plane are illustrated in Figure
3
, where the temporal variations induced by the acceptance function and the invariant-mass dependence of the event distribution can be clearly seen.

2.1
Statistical Analysis

Statistical inference is performed using a modified frequentist approach based on the profile likelihood method
[
14
,
25
]
. The analysis employs an extended unbinned likelihood constructed in the two-dimensional observable space of invariant mass and event time, allowing the signal and background hypotheses to be compared while preserving the full temporal information of the dataset. Systematic uncertainties are incorporated through nuisance parameters, which are profiled in the likelihood fit.

The profile likelihood ratio test statistic is defined as

q_{\mu}=-2\ln\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}(\mu,\hat{\hat{\theta}})}{\mathcal{L}(\hat{\mu},\hat{\theta})}\right),

(17)

where
\mu
denotes the signal strength parameter,
\hat{\mu}
and
\hat{\theta}
correspond to the values of the signal strength and nuisance parameters that globally maximize the likelihood function, and
\hat{\hat{\theta}}
represents the conditional maximum-likelihood estimator of the nuisance parameters for a fixed value of
\mu
.

In the absence of a statistically significant deviation from the background-only expectation, upper limits are derived using the
CL_{s}
prescription. Exclusion limits at the 95% confidence level are obtained for the signal hypothesis parameterized by
\mu
, which in the present analysis corresponds to the product of the signal production cross section and branching ratio. Signal hypotheses yielding
CL_{s}<0.05
,
are considered excluded at the 95% confidence level.

3
Results and discussion

Figure 4:
Expected cross section times branching ratio as a function of the mediator mass for different values of the modulation parameter
\kappa
. The signal rate decreases rapidly with increasing mass.

The analysis uses reconstructed muon candidates from the CMS Open Data corresponding specifically to the RunG data-taking period, with selection criteria applied to define the dimuon sample. Events are required to contain at least two muons. Each muon must satisfy
p_{T}>10\,\mathrm{GeV}
and
|\eta|<2.4
, consistent with the CMS muon detector acceptance. Opposite-sign muon pairs are selected to ensure compatibility with a neutral resonance hypothesis and suppress same-sign backgrounds. Dimuon candidates are formed from all such pairs passing these requirements. The invariant mass of each pair is required to lie between
50\,\mathrm{GeV}
and
3000\,\mathrm{GeV}
, removing the low-mass region dominated by QCD backgrounds while covering the search region relevant for heavy
Z^{\prime}
resonances. All selected dimuon candidates are used to construct the invariant mass and kinematic distributions employed in the time-dependent analysis.

The two-dimensional event distribution in the
(m,t)
plane exhibits the expected structure associated with the time-dependent normalization function incorporated in the likelihood model. The observed variations in event density as a function of time primarily reflect changes in data-taking conditions, including differences in luminosity and run duration, rather than genuine physical modulations. As illustrated in Figure
3
, these temporal structures are consistently reproduced by the background model, indicating that the normalization function accurately captures the time dependence of the dataset. The agreement between the observed data and the modeled background in the
(m,t)
plane provides an important validation of the treatment of detector and data-taking effects within the time-dependent analysis framework.

As shown in Figure
4
, the expected signal rate decreases rapidly with increasing mediator mass. This behavior reflects the falling production cross section and constitutes a primary limitation for conventional time-integrated searches, where sensitivity is driven predominantly by event yield. In the high-mass region, the number of expected signal events becomes small, reducing the statistical power available to distinguish a potential signal from the Standard Model background. The corresponding decrease in
\sigma\times\mathrm{BR}
is observed for all values of
\kappa
, although the time-dependent scenarios retain improved sensitivity relative to the time-integrated case.

The inclusion of time-dependent information through the parameter
\kappa
introduces an additional observable that enhances sensitivity beyond conventional analyses. Instead of relying solely on localized excesses in invariant mass, the present method exploits correlations in the
(m,t)
plane arising from the periodic modulation of the resonance mass. As the resonance evolves in time, the signal acquires a characteristic non-factorizable structure that cannot be reproduced by Standard Model backgrounds, whose time dependence is governed only by the data-taking profile encoded in the normalization function. This effect is particularly visible in the low- and intermediate-mass regions, where the event statistics are sufficiently large to resolve the temporal modulation. In these regions, the signal occupies a well-defined trajectory in the
(m,t)
plane, allowing the likelihood fit to separate signal-like and background-like structures more effectively. Consequently, the time-dependent analysis gains sensitivity even without increasing the total number of signal events. This demonstrates that temporal information itself can act as an additional discriminating observable in collider searches.

The resulting upper limits on the coupling
g^{\prime}
are shown in Figure
5
. The conventional time-integrated scenario (
\kappa=0
) is represented by the dashed curve, while the time-dependent cases corresponding to
\kappa=8
and
\kappa=24
are shown by the solid curves. A clear and systematic improvement in sensitivity is observed as
\kappa
increases. For a fixed mediator mass, larger modulation amplitudes lead to lower excluded values of
g^{\prime}
, indicating that the time-dependent analysis is capable of probing weaker couplings than standard approaches. The improvement becomes more pronounced as the modulation amplitude increases because larger values of
\kappa
generate stronger correlations between invariant mass and time. Physically, this corresponds to a larger excursion of the resonance mass during the oscillation cycle, producing a more distinctive structure in the two-dimensional observable space. The likelihood fit is therefore able to exploit this additional structure to enhance signal discrimination relative to the background-only hypothesis. At higher mediator masses, the limits on
g^{\prime}
become weaker for all values of
\kappa
. This behavior is driven primarily by the rapidly decreasing signal rate, which limits the number of events available to reconstruct the time-dependent modulation. In this regime, statistical fluctuations become increasingly important, reducing the relative impact of the temporal information. As a result, the separation between the different
\kappa
scenarios gradually decreases at high masses.

Although the current study is performed using a limited subset of CMS Open Data, the observed trends already demonstrate the viability of the time-domain approach. The sensitivity is expected to improve with larger integrated luminosities, finer time resolution, and optimized detector calibrations. In particular, extending the analysis to larger datasets would significantly improve the statistical precision in the high-mass region, where event yields currently limit the reach of the search.

Overall, these results demonstrate that incorporating temporal information into collider analyses provides a systematic gain in sensitivity to new physics scenarios involving time-dependent mediator properties. The analysis establishes that time-domain methods offer a complementary strategy to conventional resonance searches and can probe regions of parameter space that may otherwise remain inaccessible in standard time-integrated approaches.

4
Summary and Conclusions

In this work, we have developed a time-dependent search strategy for vector mediators whose masses are modulated by a coherently oscillating ultralight scalar dark matter background. We considered a
Z^{\prime}
gauge boson arising from a gauged
U(1)_{B-L}
extension of the Standard Model as a benchmark scenario, in which the interaction with the scalar field induces a periodic variation in the effective mediator mass.

Unlike conventional resonance searches that rely on time-integrated invariant mass distributions, the signal in this framework exhibits a non-trivial structure in the joint
(m,t)
space, appearing as a time-dependent trajectory rather than a stationary peak. To capture this behavior, we constructed a two-dimensional unbinned likelihood in invariant mass and time, allowing for a direct treatment of the correlation between these observables. This approach preserves the full information content of the data and avoids the loss of sensitivity associated with one-dimensional projections.

The method was implemented using dimuon events from the CMS Open Data corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
7.54\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
. A data-driven background model was employed, together with a time-dependent acceptance function derived from
Z
-boson yields to account for variations in data-taking conditions. The resulting analysis demonstrates that incorporating time-dependent information leads to a systematic improvement in sensitivity compared to conventional time-integrated searches.

The results show that larger modulation amplitudes lead to improved sensitivity, particularly in the low- and intermediate-mass regions where sufficient statistics allow the temporal structure of the signal to be resolved. At higher masses, the improvement is reduced due to the rapidly decreasing signal yield.

In summary, this work demonstrates that time-domain analyses provide a complementary and effective strategy for probing new physics at colliders. By exploiting correlations between invariant mass and time, it is possible to access regions of parameter space that are less constrained by conventional searches. Future studies using larger datasets and improved experimental inputs are expected to further enhance the sensitivity of this approach.
