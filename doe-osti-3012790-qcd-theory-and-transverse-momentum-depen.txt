Executive Summary
A major challenge for QCD theorists is to understand how to different categories of QCD physics
contribute to a single observable. This is the goal of QCD factorization theory. Today, QCD
physicists are tasked with describing increasingly detailed properties of transverse momentum
dependent particle distributions, including their shapes and normalizations, their variation with
energy and other kinematical variables, the identity of particles involved, correlations with spin,
and the relationship between transverse momentum distributions in different processes.
Embedded within that broad effort are specific fundamental questions at the heart of QCD theory.
Namely, how does one give precise, quantitative meaning to quark and gluon concepts in hard
scattering events, and what are the limits of those concepts?

In the past two decades, there has been a renaissance in TMD QCD, with a broad spectrum of
motivations driving the study of TMD observables. TMD QCD studies are in a transformative
stage; subfields that traditionally progressed nearly independently of one other, such as hadronic
structure studies and the study of perturbative QCD gluon radiation in high-energy collisions, are
becoming intertwined. It is thus becoming increasingly clear that unified approaches -- those that
incorporate all the relevant phenomena into a single formalism rooted in QCD fundamentals --
will be critical to the mission to expand the current understanding of QCD nuclear/hadronic
dynamics. TMD QCD factorization theorems and their generalizations will be central to these
efforts. The factorization theorems, in addition, provide the practical prescription for applying
specific calculations to phenomenology.

The goal of this project was to unify and push the boundaries of traditional TMD research and
prepare it for use in future phenomenological applications.

Findings

Early in the project, a major focus was on the transverse momentum dependence of processes as
large transverse momentum. Our early phenomenological analyses [13,15] in the first year of the
project found strong tension between cross section data and calculations performed at both
leading and next-to-leading order using standard collinear pdfs as input. We identified part of the
problem as arising due to heightened sensitivity at large transverse momentum to regions of
collinear parton distribution and fragmentation functions that are almost completely unknown,
and this led to an exploration of possible solutions. One possibility that we suggested was that, to
arrive at a correct treatment of large transverse momentum, a refined treatment of fragmentation
functions was needed, but this case could not be made adequately without a consideration of e+e‐
annihilation to back‐to‐back hadrons. To investigate this, an analysis of the e+e‐ annihilation to
back‐to‐back hadrons analogous and complementary to our work on the large transverse
momentum in semi‐inclusive deep inelastic was completed over the summer of 2019, and with
results completed and published in [11]. This work confirmed that there is tension between
collinear factorization direct perturbation theory calculations with standard fragmentation
functions and e+e‐ annihilation to back‐to‐back hadrons as one goes to large transverse
momentum. The utilities we used for this are now included in the web resources that we set up in
year 2. A case is now firmly established that existing collinear parton correlation functions are in
tension with large transverse momentum observables, and that a consistent treatment of TMD
factorization requires the tightening up of the phenomenology of collinear parton distribution and
fragmentation functions in large transverse momentum differential cross sections. This work has
been folded together with the online tools we have made available at a site dedicated to the
treatment of large transverse momentum:
https://jeffersonlab.github.io/BigTMD/_build/html/index.html.

One of the complications revealed by work on the project up to this stage was that collinear
fragmentation function extractions were not precisely constrained enough for use in TMD
applications. One of the next steps, therefore, was the completion of a dedicated
phenomenological analysis extracting new collinear parton distribution and fragmentation
functions that allow for a consistent transition from the small to large transverse momentum
region. A paper presenting and explaining these results was published in [8].

In parallel to this phenomenological work, and since the phenomenological treatment of the non‐
perturbative intrinsic transverse momentum will need to be a part of this work, it was important to
develop, in parallel, studies of the expected physics of non‐perturbative transverse momentum, to
allow for improved constraints in the fitting of process and phenomenological aspects of the
project generally. Especially important work in this direction, was published in [12,14]. This
work addressed the problem of how to treat the QCD evolution when sensitivity to intrinsic
parton transverse momentum is achieved by considering weighted cross sections rather than cross
sections directly differential in a measured transverse momentum. Many of the observables of
greatest interest in spin physics are of exactly this type. Since they involve TMD parton densities
but are inclusive observables in transverse momentum, questions have plagued phenomenological
treatments about what kind of QCD mechanisms are actually the appropriate description for such
observables. In [9,10], the project addressed this question by demonstrating that a common

relation connecting collinear and TMD correlation functions, derived within simplified parton
model assumptions fails in full QCD, and that the corrections are actually non‐perturbative in
size. We concluded from this that it is really the TMD factorization and evolution rather than
collinear or higher twist that is appropriate for transverse moments of observables that are
sensitive to intrinsic parton transverse momentum, despite the inclusive integration over all
transverse momentum. Going forward, this will have a significant impact on the identification of
non‐perturbative components of TMD pdfs in phenomenological treatments. In year 2 of the
project, I and my colleagues had set up a system for estimating transitions from very large scales
(such as at the large hadron collider (LHC)) to the moderate energies relevant for hadron structure
studies at facilities like Jefferson Lab and a future electron‐ion collider (EIC) [12].

The ultimate goal of this project was to rigorously formulate and implement the factorization
theorems in ways that ensure that plans for the future of institutions like Jefferson Lab and the
Electron Ion Collinear experiments will be on a firm theoretical footing and that the data will be
interpretable [7] in terms of quark/gluon structure. The major outcome of this direction in the
project was the formulation of a phenomenological program for applying TMD factorization in a
way that fully incorporates nonperturbative theoretical treatments of intrinsic small transverse
momentum that is universally applicable to all relevant experimental observations while
permitting an understanding of measurements in terms of the intrinsic quark/gluon structure of
hadrons. This “hadron structure oriented” (HSO) TMD program was assembled over a sequence
of articles published in the last three years [1,2,4], and with details and results made public
through this website,

The outcome of this recent work is that: One may implement TMD evolution in the standard
Collins-Soper-Sterman style, but now in a way that allows us to incorporate constraints from
concrete models, effective field theories, and lattice QCD theory in an approach that allows for
hadron structure interpretations.

 • There is now an internally consistent framework for confronting and interpreting
 the phenomenological tension between existing measurements and data. For
 example, there is a pattern of mismatches in the very large transverse momentum
 dependence with purely collinear factorization descriptions. As it now stands, the
 resolution to this tension between measurements and theoretical calculations
 remains an open issue. One conjecture is that it arises due to inadequate
 constraints on the existing extractions of collinear parton correlation functions,
 especially the collinear FFs. The investigation into the question of how much
 new fits like these help in the treatment of large transverse momentum is still on-
 going, but the HSO approach provides an internally consistent framework for
 understanding this and similar issues.
 • One is able now to use this new framework to translate and compare results
 obtained using different approaches to TMD factorization such as soft-collinear
 effective theory and resummation techniques.

More broadly, the project has elucidated the more subtle aspects of the abstract formalism of
TMD and related quark and gluon correlation functions [3]. For example, current efforts in TMD
physics are focused on modeling or describing the quark and gluon structure of hadrons in fully
nonperturbative ways. However, a difficulty that confronts any interpretation of QCD
calculations in a hadron structure framework is that the parton correlation functions associated
with hadron structure are beset by ultraviolet (UV) and rapidity divergences. It is necessary,
therefore, to introduce renormalization and/or regularization schemes, and these can modify or
spoil the naivest versions of partonic structure interpretations. Retaining an intrinsic structure
interpretation thus demands that one carefully consider the treatment of UV behavior. In a recent
collaboration [5], my colleagues and I investigated an especially striking example of this in the
context of ordinary collinear, unpolarized PDFs. This was the issue of whether PDFs can be
negative. Common sense intuition suggests that a parton distribution, if it is a literal number
density, would have to be strictly positive or zero. However, the PDFs of quantum field theory
are not literal number densities, partly due to the UV divergence issue discussed above, and we
showed in a simple quantum field theory (Yukawa theory) that nothing about the definition of a
collinear PDF or the factorization theorem requires that they always be positive in the MS-bar
renormalization scheme, and they can even be negative in physically relevant kinematical
regimes, particularly at the moderate hard scales where hadron structure studies often occur.
Given that positivity is sometimes imposed as a constraint in phenomenological applications, it is
important to understand its domain of theoretical validity. An interesting feature found in [8] was
that, at least within the simple Yukawa field theory, positivity is strictly preserved if one instead
defines the collinear PDF as the cutoff integral of a TMD PDF, as is often done in applications of
TMD factorization to phenomenology. This gives another example, in addition to the large
transverse momentum matching issue discussed below, of how the need to refine the current
theoretical understanding even of collinear PDFs and FFs overlaps with parallel efforts to
improve the hadron structure interpretation of TMD PDFs and FFs.

The work has paved the way for a deeper understanding of quark and gluon structure in the
proton to be achieved from planned future experiments. It has benefitted from the projects
support of two graduate students, both of whom have received PhDs, three postdoctoral
assistants, and two undergraduate research assistants.
