Analysis of Maxwell nanofluid model with convective variations, variable-density and heat generation in a porous medium

Mohammed Alrehili

Abstract

This study introduces a novel analysis of Maxwell nanofluid flow over a stretching surface, integrating multiple coupled physical mechanisms previously overlooked in the literature. The comprehensive model incorporates boundary convection, internal heat generation, Ohmic heating, temperature-dependent transport properties (viscosity, density, and diffusivity), and viscous dissipation effects. This holistic approach distinguishes the current work from earlier studies, which have not simultaneously examined the coupled effects of these interacting mechanisms within a unified Maxwell nanofluid model. A mathematical model is developed and solved numerically using similarity transformations, the shooting method, and the fourth-order Runge-Kutta scheme. Validation against established benchmarks confirms the method’s reliability. Further, the results demonstrate the coupled influence of key parameters on flow, thermal, and concentration profiles, along with their effects on skin friction, heat transfer (Nusselt number), and mass transfer (Sherwood number) rates. This study addresses a significant research void while providing useful theoretical guidance for the design and optimization of polymer processing, heat exchanger systems, and nanofluid-based thermal management technologies. A central revelation of this research is the dual role of temperature-sensitive density: it simultaneously amplifies thermal distribution while attenuating both nanoparticle concentration and fluid velocity. This parameter further enhances thermal and mass transfer rates while increasing the surface drag, indicating its important role in the coupled transport behavior of Maxwell nanofluids. Additionally, heat generation and convective mechanisms, as anticipated, elevate temperature profiles but inhibit flow momentum, resulting in suppressed velocity and refined control over transport behavior. Quantitatively, increasing the porous parameter enhances the skin-friction coefficient, local Nusselt number, and local Sherwood number by approximately 20.8%, 45.6%, and 3.0%, respectively, while increasing the density parameter raises these quantities by about 75.1%, 79.8%, and 69.2%, respectively. These quantitative improvements highlight the theoretical potential of the proposed model to support the design and optimization of heat exchangers, polymer processing, and nanofluid-based thermal management systems, while accounting for the associated increase in surface drag.

Introduction

The analysis of non-Newtonian fluid dynamics has attracted considerable interest owing to its essential applications in industrial and technological operations-including polymer processing, surface coating, and thermal management systems. This practical relevance has stimulated substantial research aimed at elucidating the underlying transport phenomena of these fluids under diverse physical conditions. As a widely recognized non-Newtonian fluid model, the Maxwell formulation is particularly valued for its capacity to represent elastic behavior and stress relaxation phenomena in complex flow environments. Contemporary studies have markedly advanced the theoretical and practical understanding of this category of non-Newtonian fluids. Recent research in non-Newtonian Maxwell fluid dynamics has expanded significantly across multiple interdisciplinary fronts. Abdelsalam et al. [] conducted a comparative assessment of rheological properties over permeable stretching surfaces, emphasizing interactions between fluid microstructure and boundary conditions. Parallelly, Venkatesh et al. [] investigated augmented thermal and species transport by incorporating nanoparticles, radiative heat transfer, and chemical reaction effects. Mandal et al. [] examined mixed convection in magnetohydrodynamic flow under time-dependent stretching, whereas Gurejala et al. [] analyzed the influence of viscous dissipation in electromagnetic nanofluid systems. Further extending the scope, Basit et al. [] introduced a model coupling bioconvection with Darcy-Forchheimer porous resistance and Lorentz forces, offering new perspectives on flow-porous media interactions. In a contemporary contribution, Zeeshan et al. [] explored hydrodynamic stability in thermally magnetized flow over unsteady stretching geometries, enriching the theoretical framework for time-dependent fluid phenomena. Together, these studies reflect the expanding scope of current investigations and confirm the continued relevance of non-Newtonian transport phenomena in advancing both fundamental theory and technological applications.

The importance of nanofluids has grown significantly owing to their superior thermal conductivity, which makes them ideal for application in heat transfer, thermal management, energy systems, cooling systems, and processing technologies. It is, therefore, not surprising that studies on the flow, heat, and mass transfer properties of nanofluids have been carried out extensively due to their versatility in various fluid models and configurations. Anwar [] used fractional derivatives and fractional relaxation times in the Buongiorno model of heat and mass transfer in viscoelastic nanofluids. The study by Irfan et al. [] was based on the mass flux principle for the estimation of convective heat transfer mechanism in Carreau nanofluid taking into account nonlinear mixed convection effects. On the other hand, Sarfraz and Khan [] conducted the analysis of second-law analysis of hybrid nanofluid with silver and titania particles having heat generation and buoyancy forces over an inclined stretching sheet. Moreover, Ohmic heating and activation energy effects were also taken into account in the study of Jeffrey nanofluid flow with gyrotactic microorganisms by Muhammad et al. []. In Rajhi et al. [], the thermal study of tetra-nanofluid is done within a vertically aligned needle with both convection and porous media effects. In addition to that, Gizachew et al. [] performed a study on time-dependent MHD Williamson nanofluid flow with an inclined stretching surface within a porous medium. The study on the bioconvective Darcy-Forchheimer flow of Ree-Eyring nanofluid flow with velocity and thermal slip effects was performed by Puneeth et al. []. Furthermore, the triple-diffusive radiated Prandtl nanofluid flow with multiple thermal effects and varying thermophysical properties was modeled mathematically by Khan et al. []. Oldroyd-B nanofluid flow in a non-inertial frame of reference was studied using Cattaneo-Christov heat-flux model by Yasir et al. []. Finally, Majeed et al. [] recently did the study on bioconvective hydromagnetic Ree-Eyring nanofluid flow with Cattaneo-Christov heat-flux and gyrotactic microorganisms with Darcy-Forchheimer resistance.

Boundary interactions play a critical role in fluid transport phenomena, as they exert direct control over heat and mass transfer efficiencies in numerous engineering and industrial applications. This fundamental importance has prompted increased investigative focus on fluid dynamic behavior under realistic physical conditions, including non-ideal thermal boundary conditions and slip flow effects, to more accurately represent practical operational environments. Illustrating this trend, Baag et al. [] examined the effects of surface stretching and internal heat generation on nanofluid dynamics, yielding valuable insights into thermal performance optimization. Building on the study of non-Newtonian behavior, Abbas et al. [] examined Powell-Eyring fluid flow over a stretching sheet, integrating velocity slip and viscous dissipation to elucidate the intricate dynamics of viscoelastic fluids. Further expanding this research direction, Dhlamini et al. [] analyzed second-grade nanofluids with bioconvective effects, highlighting the synergistic interaction between microbial motility and hydrodynamic boundaries. In a related investigation, Gundagani et al. [] explored nano-enhanced Powell-Eyring fluid transport within porous media, focusing on the coupling of magnetohydrodynamic forces and thermal energy transfer mechanisms. Francis et al. [] expanded the computational modeling of bioconvective nanofluids over conical surfaces, revealing how magnetic fields influence hydrodynamic stability. In the most recent contribution, Panda et al. [] investigated hybrid nanofluids exhibiting tangent hyperbolic rheological behavior, incorporating simultaneous effects of magnetism, porous media resistance, and wall slip conditions. Together, these studies underscore the critical importance of boundary-mediated transport phenomena and illustrate the growing sophistication of contemporary models in pushing the frontiers of thermal and mass transfer science.

Fluid density plays a pivotal role in heat and mass transfer phenomena, significantly influencing thermal stability, buoyancy-induced convection, and overall energy and species transport efficiency in nanofluid applications. Recognizing this importance, researchers have increasingly explored its behavior under varied physical constraints. For instance, Yadav et al. [] analyzed the direct dependence of nanofluid density on temperature and concentration variations, providing a foundational understanding for subsequent research. Expanding on this, Abbas et al. [] integrated density variation and thermal radiation into a magnetohydrodynamic Sakiadis flow model, demonstrating their synergistic effect on transport dynamics. Gamaoun et al. [] expanded upon this research direction by evaluating heat transfer in nanofluids along stretching surfaces under magnetic exposure, utilizing the Keller Box numerical technique to achieve high computational accuracy. Concurrently, Ullah et al. [] explored the behavior of chemically reactive nanofluids, demonstrating the coupled influence of density gradients and viscous dissipation on the evolution of thermal and concentration boundary layers. Further contributions include Ullah et al. [], who studied Carreau nanofluid flows with chemical reactions and heat generation under realistic heating conditions, and Khedher et al. [], who emphasized conductivity and density effects on second-grade nanofluids over polymer surfaces. These studies underscore the fundamental influence of density-dependent phenomena on nanofluidic transport behavior, driving ongoing research to optimize thermohydraulic models for cutting-edge engineering systems.

Although previous studies have significantly advanced the understanding of Maxwell nanofluid transport under various physical conditions, they have primarily examined thermal convective boundary conditions, internal heat generation, viscous dissipation, Joule heating, and temperature-dependent thermophysical properties separately or in limited combinations. Consequently, the coupled influence of these interacting mechanisms on flow, heat, and mass transfer remains insufficiently understood. To address this gap, the present study develops a unified mathematical model that simultaneously incorporates these interacting mechanisms. A comprehensive review of the available literature further indicates that no previous study has simultaneously incorporated all of these interacting mechanisms within a single Maxwell nanofluid framework, thereby confirming the originality and necessity of the present investigation. The novelty of the present work lies in providing a comprehensive analysis of their coupled effects within a single Maxwell nanofluid framework, thereby offering a deeper understanding of the underlying transport characteristics and supplying useful guidance for the design and optimization of advanced thermal engineering systems.

Flow analysis

This section outlines the mathematical framework governing the flow, heat, and mass transfer of a non-Newtonian Maxwell fluid across a stretching sheet. The model (Fig. ) incorporates temperature-dependent density and viscosity, along with thermophysical properties such as thermal conductivity and mass diffusivity that vary with both temperature and solute concentration. The stretching-sheet configuration considered in the present study is widely adopted to model boundary-layer transport phenomena encountered in polymer extrusion, continuous coating, wire drawing, and cooling processes. Although the stretching-sheet geometry is well established, the novelty of the present study lies in employing this configuration as a unified platform to simultaneously investigate the coupled effects of thermal convective boundary conditions, internal heat generation, Ohmic heating, viscous dissipation, and temperature-dependent transport properties in Maxwell nanofluids. This distinguishes the present model from previous studies that considered these mechanisms individually or in limited combinations. Consequently, the proposed configuration provides new physical insights into the coupled momentum, heat, and mass transfer characteristics under realistic operating conditions. Therefore, the proposed model represents a realistic engineering configuration for advanced thermal processing and nanofluid cooling systems, where these physical mechanisms may coexist simultaneously.

Findings and interpretation

This section decodes the intricate interplay of physical phenomena within a Maxwell nanofluid flowing over a stretching surface. We integrate and analyze five key coupled mechanisms: convective boundary heating, internal energy generation, Ohmic dissipation, temperature-sensitive properties (viscosity, density, diffusivity), and viscous thermal effects, revealing how these forces collectively shape flow dynamics, thermal distribution, and mass transfer in ways previously unexamined. This study harmonizes a suite of twelve dimensionless parameters, each representing a distinct physical force-to fully characterize nanofluid behavior. From the fluid’s elastic memory (Maxwell parameter β) and magnetic influence M to molecular diffusion (Schmidt Sc, Prandtl Pr), thermophoretic ξt and Brownian nanoparticle ξr dynamics, convective energy exchange Υ, the heat source/sink parameter γ, viscous heating (Eckert Ec), and boundary suction ω, each parameter contributes a unique voice to the collective description of flow, thermal, and concentration fields. Concluding remarks

This study pioneers a holistic model for Maxwell nanofluid flow, integrating convection, Ohmic heating, variable properties, and dissipation into a unified framework. The numerical approach proved robust and accurate. A limitation of the present study is that it is restricted to steady two-dimensional flow under boundary-layer assumptions, without considering transient or three-dimensional effects. Key findings reveal that: The temperature-dependent density parameter intensifies the thermal field while diminishing nanofluid velocity and concentration, and simultaneously enhances heat/mass transfer rates and skin friction.Heat generation and convection parameters amplify the temperature distribution but suppress fluid motion, leading to reduced velocity.The suction parameter acts as a kinetic amplifier, accelerating fluid motion while simultaneously suppressing thermal and concentration levels. This dynamic leads to a notable reduction in skin friction and heat transfer efficiency. Conversely, mass transfer rates experience a significant boost.The thermal conductivity parameter acts as a hidden accelerator for fluid motion while simultaneously dampening thermal and solutal distributions. This dual behavior effectively reduces skin friction drag and attenuates both heat and mass transfer rates.The Eckert number serves as a transformative agent: it suppresses flow momentum while significantly elevating nanofluid temperature. This thermal amplification, driven by viscous dissipation, subsequently increases skin friction resistance and amplifies both heat and mass transfer rates.The observed increase in thermal distribution with the porous, density, viscosity, magnetic field, and heat generation parameters supports the elevated operating temperatures required during polymer processing and contributes to improved thermal management in heat exchangers and nanofluid-based cooling systems.In future studies, the present model may be extended to transient three-dimensional Maxwell nanofluid flows with additional effects, such as nonlinear thermal radiation, velocity slip, and hybrid nanofluids. Physics-informed deep learning may also be explored to solve more complex coupled transport problems.
