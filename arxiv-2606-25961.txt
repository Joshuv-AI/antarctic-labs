Well-posedness of magnetic Zakharov system on 2D bounded domain
Oleksiy S. Shcherbina

Abstract
The paper deals with initial boundary value problems for the Magnetic Zakharov system arising in plasma physics in two-dimensional domains. It was proved the global well-posedness of these problems in some Sobolev type classes.

1
Introduction

We consider the dissipative magnetic Zakharov system in a smooth (2D) bounded domain

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}iE_{t}+\Delta E-nE+iE\times B+i\gamma_{1}E=g_{1}(x,t),&x\in\Omega,\\
n_{tt}+\gamma_{2}n_{t}-\Delta\left(n+|E|^{2}\right)=g_{2}(x,t),&x\in\Omega,\\
B_{tt}-\gamma_{3}\Delta B_{t}+\Delta^{2}\left(B+iE\times\overline{E}\right)=g_{3}(x,t),&x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1)

Here the function
E=E(x,t):\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\times\{0\}
is the slowly varying amplitude of the
high-frequency electric field, and
n=n(x,t):\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to R
denotes the fluctuation
of the ion-density from its equilibrium value, and
B=B(x,t):\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\{0\}\times\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}

is the self-generated
magnetic field. E denotes the conjugate complex of E, and the notation

\times
in (
1
) means the cross product for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or
\mathbb{C}^{3}
valued vectors.
Parameters
\gamma_{i}
are nonnegative and functions
g_{i}(x,t)
are given.

We supplement this system with initial data

E(x,0)=E_{0}(x),\,n_{t}(x,0)=n_{1}(x),\,n(x,0)=n_{0}(x),\,B_{t}(x,0)=B_{1}(x),\,B(x,0)=B_{0}(x),

(2)

and with the boundary conditions

\begin{array}[]{lll}E|_{\partial\Omega}=0,&n|_{\partial\Omega}=0,&B|_{\partial\Omega}=\Delta B|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{array}

(3)

After omitting the magnetic field
B
the system (
1
) is reduced to the standard Zakharov system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}iE_{t}+\Delta E-nE+i\gamma_{1}E=g_{1},&x\in\Omega,&t>0,\\
n_{tt}+\gamma_{2}n_{t}-\Delta\left(n+|E|^{2}\right)=g_{2},&x\in\Omega,&t>0,\end{array}\right.

which was proposed by Zakharov
[
18
]
for the description of wave phenomena in plasma.

In the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 1
),
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0
and
g_{1}(x,t)=g_{2}(x,t)=0
the Cauchy problem for (
1
)
was studied by many authors (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
7
]

and the references therein). The local well-posedness was shown in appropriate
Sobolev classes (see
[
7
]
for the most advanced results)
and there are solutions which blow up in finite time for
d=2

[
8
]
. In the case
d\leq 3
for some special class of initial data
the global well-posedness is also known
[
2
]
.

In the case when
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is bounded domain(
d=1,2
) and
\gamma_{i}\geq 0
the Cauchy problem for (
1
) for
d=1
was studied by
[
6
,
9
,
15
]
.
It was proved the well-posedness of (
1
) for Dirichlet and periodic bounded conditions,
existence of the compact global attractor
\mathcal{A}
and (in the case of periodic boundary conditions) analyticity of elements
\mathcal{A}
.
In 2D case system (
1
) was studied in
[
5
]
for different types of boundary conditions.
It was proved the global well-posedness in some Sobolev type classes and studied properties of the solutions.
In the dissipative case the existence of a global attractor is established.

The system (
1
) at first was derived by X. He
[
10
]
for description of the the pondermotive force and magnetic field
generation effects resulting from the non-linear interaction between plasma-wave and particles.
In the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
),
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0
and
g_{1}(x,t)=g_{2}(x,t)=0
the Cauchy problem for (
1
)
was studied by
[
19
]
.
It was proved the local existence and uniqueness of the solution for this system.

In this paper we prove that system (
1
) has a unique solution on
[0,T]
, where either
T\sim\|g_{1}\|_{L_{2}}^{-1}
in a conservative case
\gamma_{1}=0
or
T=\infty
in the dissipative case
\gamma_{1}>0
.
To prove the existence of solutions we use Galerkin approximations
and the standard compactness method. The proof of their uniqueness
relies on the method which was developed in the theory of shallow
shells for the case of critical nonlinearities
(see
[
14
]
and also
[
3
]
).

2
Statement of the main results

Below we denote by
\|\cdot\|
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
the norm and the inner product correspondently in the space
H=L_{2}(\Omega)
and consider
the minus Laplace operator with the Dirichlet boundary conditions:

Au=-\Delta u,~~~u\in D(A)=H^{2}\left(\Omega\right)\bigcap H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right).

The operator
A
is positive and has a discrete spectrum, i.e.,
there exists an orthonormal basis
\{e_{k}\}
in
H
consisting of eigenfunctions
of
A
:

Ae_{k}=\lambda e_{k},~~k=1,2,\ldots,~~0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots,~~\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}=+\infty.

We also use the notation
H_{s}=D(A^{s/2})
for
s\geq 0
and endow this space
with the graph norm
\|\cdot\|_{s}=\|A^{s/2}\cdot\|
.
If
s<0
, then
H_{s}
denotes the completion of
H_{0}
with respect to

\|\cdot\|_{s}=\|A^{s/2}\cdot\|
.

We also mention that we deal with both real and complex versions
of the spaces
H_{s}
. We keep notation
H_{s}
for real case
and denote by
\overline{H_{s}}
its complexification. For norms and inner products
we use the same notations.

We understand solutions to problem
(
1
)–(
2
) in the sense of the following
definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A triple
(n;B;E)
is said to be a

semi-strong
if

(n_{t};n;B_{t};B;E)\in L_{\infty}\left(0,T;\,\mathcal{H}\right),~~\mathcal{H}\equiv H_{0}\times H_{1}\times{H_{-1}}\times{H_{1}}\times\overline{H}_{2}.

(6)

We also note that by (
1
) and (
6
) we have that

n_{tt}\in L_{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H_{-1}\right)\quad B_{tt}\in L_{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H_{-3}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad E_{t}\in L_{\infty}\left([0,T];\,\overline{H}\right).

(7)

Therefore (after changing on a set of zero measure in
[0,T]
) the fiver
(n_{t};n;B_{t};B;E)
is a weakly continuous function with values
in
\mathcal{H}
(see
[
12
, Lemma 8.1]
).
In particular, this means that initial data (
2
) have a sense.
Moreover, (
6
) and (
7
), Aubin’s embedding theorem (see
[
16
, Corollary 4]
) imply that

(n_{t};n;B_{t};B;E_{t};E)\in C\left([0,T];\,H_{-\sigma}\times H_{1-\sigma}\times H_{-\sigma}\times{H_{-1}}\times{H_{1}}\times H_{2-\sigma}\right)

(8)

for every
\sigma>0
.

Our first result is the following assertion on the existence of a semi-strong solution of problem (
1
)–(
2
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(n_{1},n_{0},B_{1},B_{0},E_{0})\in\mathcal{H}

and
(g_{1},\,g_{2},\,g_{3})\in\mbox{\huge{???}}
.
Then problem (
1
)–(
2
) has a
semi-strong solution on an interval [0,T] provided that

•

either
\gamma_{1}\geq 0
and
\|E_{0}\|+T\cdot\|g_{1}\|<\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
,

•

or else
\gamma_{1}>0
, and

\max\left\{\|E_{0}\|,\gamma_{1}^{-1}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|g_{1}(t)\|\right\}<\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
.

Our next result is the following uniqueness theorem for semi-strong solutions of problem (
1
)–(
2
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Problem (
1
)–(
2
) on every interval
[0,T]
has at most one semi-strong solution.

3
The proof of existence theorem

Before proceeding to the proof, we recall the following classical embedding in two dimensional case

H^{1-2/p}(\Omega)\subset L_{p}(\Omega),\quad 2<p<\infty.

(9)

By interpolation, (
9
) implies that for an any function
u\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
we get

\|u\|_{L_{p}}\leq C_{p}(\Omega)\|u\|^{2/p}\|\nabla u\|^{1-2/p},\quad 2<p<\infty.

(10)

In particular, for
p=4
we recall the following Sobolev-type estimate

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
u\in H_{1}
remains true

\|u\|^{4}_{L_{4}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|u\|^{2}\cdot\|\nabla u\|^{2}.

(11)

The proof of this lemma can be found in Appendix of
[
5
]
.

Let us prove now Theorem
2.1
by the compactness method.
We consider the Galerkin approximations of the magnetic Zakharov problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}iE_{t}^{N}-AE^{N}-P_{N}(n^{N}E^{N})+iP_{N}(E^{N}\times B^{N})+i\gamma_{1}E^{N}=P_{N}g_{1}(x,t),\\
n^{N}_{tt}+\gamma_{2}n^{N}_{t}+A\left(n^{N}+P_{N}|E^{N}|^{2}\right)=P_{N}g_{2}(x,t),\\
B^{N}_{tt}+\gamma_{3}AB^{N}_{t}+A^{2}\left(B^{N}+iP_{N}(E^{N}\times\overline{E^{N}})\right)=P_{N}g_{3}(x,t),\end{array}\right.

(12)

Here and bellow
P_{N}
is the orthoprojector on Span
\{e_{k}\,:\,k=1,2,\ldots,N\}
, where
\{e_{k}\}
are the eigenfunctions of
A
.
To simplify notations we omit below the index
N
for functions
(n,B,E)
.

We note that the product
E\times\overline{E}
is
pure imaginary.
Therefore

\Im\left(i(E\times B)\cdot\overline{E}\right)=\Re\left((\overline{E}\times E)\cdot B\right)=0
.

In order to prove the initial a priori estimate,
let us multiply the first equation from (
12
) by
2\overline{E}

and integrate the imaginary part of the result over
\Omega
. Then
we have

\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\|E\|^{2}+2\gamma_{1}\|E\|^{2}=2\Im(g_{1},E)\leq 2\|E\|\|g_{1}\|.

(13)

If
\gamma_{1}=0
then we obtain that
\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\|E\|\leq\|g_{1}\|
. Hence,

\|E(t)\|\leq\kappa\equiv\|E\|_{0}+T\|g_{1}\|.

(14)

In the case
\gamma_{1}>0
we can estimate the r.h.s of (
13
) such as

2\|E\|\|g_{1}\|\leq\gamma_{1}\|E\|^{2}+\gamma_{1}^{-1}\|g_{1}\|^{2}.

After integrating (
13
) over
[0,t]
we have the initial a priori estimate

\displaystyle\|E(t)\|^{2}\leq\|E_{0}\|^{2}e^{-\gamma_{1}t}+\gamma_{1}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|g_{1}(\tau)\|^{2}e^{-\gamma_{1}(t-\tau)}\mbox{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\|E_{0}\|^{2},\gamma_{1}^{-2}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|g_{1}(t)\|^{2}\right\}\equiv\kappa^{2}.

(15)

Now we can proceed to prove the next a priori estimate.
Let us define now the functional

G_{1}(t)=\|\nabla E\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|n_{t}\|^{2}_{-1}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|n\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|B_{t}\|^{2}_{-2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|B\|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}n|E|^{2}+i\int_{\Omega}(E\times\overline{E})\cdot B.

(16)

Taking into account that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\int_{\Omega}n|E|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}n_{t}|E|^{2}+2\Re\int_{\Omega}nE\cdot\overline{E_{t}},

\displaystyle i\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\int_{\Omega}(E\times\overline{E})\cdot B=i\int_{\Omega}(E\times\overline{E})\cdot B_{t}-2\Re\int_{\Omega}i(E\times B)\cdot\overline{E_{t}},

it is straightforward to check that

\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}G_{1}(t)+2\gamma_{1}\|\nabla E\|^{2}+\gamma_{2}\|n_{t}\|_{-1}^{2}+\gamma_{3}\|B_{t}\|_{-1}^{2}=R_{0}(t),

(17)

where

R_{0}(t)=2\gamma_{1}\int_{\Omega}(iE\times B-nE)\cdot\overline{E}-2\Re\int_{\Omega}g_{1}\cdot\overline{E_{t}}-2\gamma_{1}\Re\int_{\Omega}g_{1}\cdot\overline{E}+\int_{\Omega}g_{2}A^{-1}n_{t}+\int_{\Omega}g_{3}\cdot A^{-2}B_{t}.

(18)

By integration from (
17
) we have that

G_{1}(t)\leq G_{1}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}R_{0}(\tau)d\tau.

(19)

Now we estimate from bellow the functional
G_{1}(t)
. From Hölder inequality, (
11
) and (
3
) we get

\left|\int_{\Omega}n|E|^{2}+i\int_{\Omega}(E\times\overline{E})\cdot B\right|\leq\left(\|n\|+\|B\|\right)\|E\|^{2}_{L_{4}}\leq\sqrt{2}\kappa(\|n\|+\|B\|)\|\nabla E\|.

(20)

We note that if
\kappa<\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
then there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\|\nabla E\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|n\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|B\|^{2}-\sqrt{2}\kappa(\|n\|+\|B\|)\|\nabla E\|\geq\alpha\left(\|\nabla E\|^{2}+\|n\|^{2}+\|B\|^{2}\right).

Therefore from (
16
) we can conclude that

G_{1}(t)\geq\alpha\left(\|\nabla E\|^{2}+\|n\|^{2}+\|B\|^{2}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|n_{t}\|^{2}_{-1}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|B_{t}\|^{2}_{-2}\equiv H_{0}(t).

(21)

Now we need to estimate the r.h.s of (
19
) by
C_{1}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}H_{0}(\tau)d\tau
.
Then from (
19
), (
21
) we get

H_{0}(t)\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}H_{0}(\tau)d\tau.

From Gronwall’s lemma we obtain our the second a priori estimate

\|\nabla E\|^{2}+\|n_{t}\|^{2}_{-1}+\|n\|^{2}+\|B_{t}\|^{2}_{-2}+\|B\|^{2}\leq C,\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

(22)

where
C
does not depend on
N
.

To do this let us estimate
R_{0}(t)
.
From the first equation of (
12
) it follows that

E_{t}=-iAE-iP_{N}(nE)-P_{N}(E\times B)-\gamma_{1}E-iP_{N}g_{1}
.
Substituting it into

\int_{\Omega}g_{1}\cdot\overline{E_{t}}
we get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left|\Re\int_{\Omega}g_{1}\cdot\overline{E_{t}}\right|&\leq&\|\nabla g_{1}\|\|\nabla E\|+(\|n\|+\|B\|)\|E\|_{L_{4}}\|g_{1}\|_{L_{4}}+\gamma_{1}\|g_{1}\|\|E\|\\
&&\leq C(\|n\|+\|B\|+\|\nabla E\|^{1/2})\|\nabla E\|^{1/2}\leq\varepsilon H_{0}(t)+C_{\varepsilon}.\end{array}

(23)

Taking into account that

\left|2\gamma_{1}\Re\int_{\Omega}g_{1}\cdot\overline{E}+\int_{\Omega}g_{2}A^{-1}n_{t}+\int_{\Omega}g_{3}\cdot A^{-2}B_{t}\right|\leq C\left(1+\|n_{t}\|^{2}_{-1}+\|B_{t}\|^{2}_{-2}\right),

from (
20
) and (
23
) we can conclude the estimation

R_{0}\leq C_{1}+C_{2}H_{0}(t),

which is enough for us.

Now we are ready to proceed to prove the main a priori estimate.
Let us multiply
the first equation from (
12
) by
-2A\overline{E_{t}}-2\gamma_{1}A\overline{E}

and integrate the real part of the result over
\Omega

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\left\{\|AE\|^{2}+2\Re\int_{\Omega}g_{1}\cdot A\overline{E}\right\}+2\gamma_{1}\|AE\|^{2}+2\Re\int_{\Omega}nE\cdot A\overline{E_{t}}-2\Re\int_{\Omega}i(E\times B)\cdot A\overline{E_{t}}

\displaystyle=2\gamma_{1}\Re\int_{\Omega}(iE\times B-nE-g_{1})\cdot A\overline{E}+2\Re\int_{\Omega}g_{1t}\cdot A\overline{E}.

(24)

Let us multiply the second and the third equation from (
12
) by
n_{t}

and by
A^{-1}B_{t}
correspondingly, then let us add and integrate the result over
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\left\{\|\nabla n\|^{2}+\|n_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla B\|^{2}+\|B_{t}\|^{2}_{-1}\right\}+\gamma_{2}\|n_{t}\|^{2}+\gamma_{3}\|B_{t}\|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}n_{t}A|E|^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}iA(E\times\overline{E})\cdot B_{t}=\int_{\Omega}n_{t}P_{N}g_{2}+\int_{\Omega}P_{N}g_{3}\cdot A^{-1}B_{t}.

Now we define the functional
H_{1}(t)
such that

H_{1}(t)=\|AE\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla n\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|n_{t}\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla B\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|B_{t}\|^{2}_{-1}.

(26)

Taking into account (
26
) by adding (
3
) and (
25
) we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\left\{H_{1}(t)+2\Re\int_{\Omega}g_{1}\cdot A\overline{E}\right\}+2\gamma_{1}\|AE\|^{2}+\gamma_{2}\|n_{t}\|^{2}+\gamma_{3}\|B_{t}\|^{2}+2\Re\int_{\Omega}nE\cdot A\overline{E_{t}}

(27)

\displaystyle\qquad-2\Re\int_{\Omega}i(E\times B)\cdot A\overline{E_{t}}+\int_{\Omega}n_{t}A|E|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}iA(E\times\overline{E})\cdot B_{t}=R_{1}(t),

where

\displaystyle R_{1}(t)=2\gamma_{1}\Re\int_{\Omega}(iE\times B-nE-g_{1})\cdot A\overline{E}+2\Re\int_{\Omega}g_{1t}\cdot A\overline{E}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}n_{t}P_{N}g_{2}+\int_{\Omega}P_{N}g_{3}\cdot A^{-1}B_{t}.

(28)

It is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}n_{t}A|E|^{2}+2\Re\int_{\Omega}nE\cdot A\overline{E_{t}}-2\Re\int_{\Omega}i(E\times B)\cdot A\overline{E_{t}}+\int_{\Omega}iA(E\times\overline{E})\cdot B_{t}

(29)

\displaystyle\qquad=\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\left\{2\Re\int_{\Omega}nE\cdot A\overline{E}+\|P_{N}(nE)\|^{2}-2\Re\int_{\Omega}i(E\times B)\cdot A\overline{E}+\|P_{N}(E\times B)\|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-2\Re\int_{\Omega}iP_{N}(E\times B)\cdot(n\overline{E})\right\}-R_{2}(t),

where

\displaystyle R_{2}(t)=2\int_{\Omega}n_{t}|\nabla E|^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}i(\partial_{1}E\times\partial_{1}\overline{E}+\partial_{2}E\times\partial_{2}\overline{E})\cdot B_{t}

(30)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\Re\int_{\Omega}P_{N}(nE-iE\times B)\cdot(n_{t}\overline{E}+i\overline{E}\times B_{t})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\Re\int_{\Omega}P_{N}(n\overline{E_{t}}+i\overline{E_{t}}\times B)\cdot(i\gamma_{1}E-P_{N}g_{1}).

Let us define now the functional

\displaystyle H_{2}(t)=2\Re\int_{\Omega}(nE+g_{1})\cdot A\overline{E}+\|P_{N}(nE)\|^{2}-2\Re\int_{\Omega}i(E\times B)\cdot A\overline{E}+\|P_{N}(E\times B)\|^{2}

(31)

From (
27
), (
29
) and (
31
) it follows that

\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\left\{H_{1}(t)+H_{2}(t)\right\}+2\gamma_{1}\|AE\|^{2}+\gamma_{2}\|n_{t}\|^{2}+\gamma_{3}\|B_{t}\|^{2}=R_{1}(t)+R_{2}(t).

Integrating this equality over
[0,T]
yields

\displaystyle H_{1}(t)+H_{2}(t)+2\gamma_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|AE(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau+\gamma_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|n_{t}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau+\gamma_{3}\int_{0}^{t}\|B_{t}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=H_{1}(0)+H_{2}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(R_{1}(\tau)+R_{2}(\tau)\right)d\tau.

(32)

At first we estimate
H_{2}
as

\displaystyle|H_{2}(t)|\leq C\|AE\|\|E\|_{L_{4}}(1+\|n\|_{L_{4}}+\|B\|_{L_{4}})+C\|E\|^{2}_{L_{4}}(\|n\|_{L_{4}}+\|B\|_{L_{4}})^{2}

(33)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq C\|AE\|(1+\|\nabla n\|^{1/2}+\|\nabla B\|^{1/2})+C\|\nabla n\|+C\|\nabla B\|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\delta H_{1}(t)+C_{\delta},

where
\delta>0
is arbitrary.
Now we estimate the terms in the r.h.s of (
3
). We note that (
10
) and
(
22
) imply that
\|E\|_{L_{p}}\leq C
for all
2\leq p<\infty
. Therefore,
choosing sufficiently small
\varepsilon
,
from (
3
), (
10
) and (
22
) we get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|R_{1}(t)|&\leq&C\|\Delta E\|\|E\|_{L_{4}}(\|n\|_{L_{4}}+\|B\|_{L_{4}})+C\|\Delta E\|+C\|n_{t}\|+C\|B_{t}\|\\
&\leq&C\|\Delta E\|(1+\|\nabla n\|^{1/2}+\|\nabla B\|^{1/2})+C\|n_{t}\|+C\|B_{t}\|\\
&\leq&CH_{1}(t)+\varepsilon\|B_{t}\|^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}.\end{array}

(34)

In the same way we obtain

\displaystyle|R_{2}(t)|\leq C\|\nabla E\|^{2}_{L_{4}}(\|n_{t}\|+\|B_{t}\|)+C(\|n_{t}\|+\|B_{t}\|)\|E\|_{L_{4}}\|E\|_{L_{8}}(\|n\|_{L_{8}}+\|B\|_{L_{8}})

(35)

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|\Delta E-nE+iP_{N}(E\times B)+i\gamma_{1}E-P_{N}g_{1}(x,t)\|(\|n\|_{L_{4}}+\|B\|_{L_{4}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq C(\|\Delta E\|^{2}+\|n_{t}\|^{2})+\varepsilon\|B_{t}\|^{2}+C(\|\nabla n\|^{3/2}+\|\nabla B\|^{3/2})+C

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq CH_{1}(t)+\varepsilon\|B_{t}\|^{2}+C.

From (
3
), (
34
), (
33
) and (
35
) we get

\displaystyle(1-\delta)H_{1}(t)+(\gamma_{3}-2\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\|B_{t}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}H_{1}(\tau)d\tau.

(36)

From Gronwall’s lemma and this relation we conclude the final a priori estimate

\|AE\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla n\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|n_{t}\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla B\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|B_{t}\|_{-1}^{2}\leq C,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(37)

Furthermore, we note that (
36
) and (
37
) imply an additional a priori estimate.

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\|B_{t}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau\leq C.

(38)

Taking into account (
37
) we can pass to the limit as
N\to\infty
in (
12
) and obtain the existence of semi-strong solution on
[0,T]
.

4
The proof of uniquness theorem

We use the method suggested by
Sedenko
[
14
]
in the theory of elastic shells
(see also
[
3
,
5
]
). Bellow we need the following lemma, where
the orthoprojector
P_{N}
is the same as in (
12
).

Lemma 4.1
.

(a) Let
u(x)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then there exists
N_{0}>0
such that

Proof.

Let us prove (

\|uv\|_{H^{-s}}=\sup\limits_{\|\varphi\|_{H^{s}}=1}\left|\int_{\Omega}uv\varphi\right|\leq\|u\|_{H^{-s}}\|v\varphi\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{-s}}\left(\|v\|_{L_{\infty}}+\|v\|_{H^{1}}\right).

∎

Let
(E^{(i)}),n^{(i)};B^{(i)})

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}iE_{t}-AE=G_{1}(t)\equiv n^{(1)}E+nE^{(2)}-iE^{(1)}\times B-iE\times B^{(2)}+i\gamma_{1}E,\\

B_{tt}+\gamma_{3}AB_{t}+A^{2}B=G_{3}(t)\equiv-iA^{2}\left(E^{(1)}\times\overline{E}+E\times\overline{E^{(2)}}\right).\end{array}\right.

(42)

with zero initial data.

Let us consider the solution

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}iE^{N}_{tt}-AE^{N}_{t}=P_{N}G_{1t}(t),\\
iE^{N}_{t}-AE^{N}=P_{N}G_{1}(t),\\
n^{N}_{tt}+An^{N}=P_{N}G_{2}(t),\\

Multiplying the first two equations in (

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{dt}}\left\{\|E^{N}_{t}\|^{2}_{-s}+\|E^{N}\|^{2}_{1-s}\right\}\leq 2\left(\|P_{N}G_{1,t}\|_{-s}\|E^{N}_{t}\|_{-s}+\|P_{N}G_{1}\|_{1-s}\|E^{N}\|_{1-s}\right).

\displaystyle\|E_{t}(t)\|^{2}_{-s}+\|E(t)\|^{2}_{1-s}\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|G_{1,t}(\tau)\|_{-s}\|E_{t}(\tau)\|_{-s}+\|G_{1}(\tau)\|_{1-s}\|E(\tau)\|_{1-s}\right)d\tau.
