Semicontinuity of Lyapunov exponents and entropy

We consider a
C^{\infty}
smooth diffeomorphism
f
of a compact surface (more precisely, a boundaryless and compact two-dimensional
C^{\infty}
Riemannian manifold). Given an invariant Borel probability measure
\mu\in\mathbb{P}(f)
, its
top Lyapunov exponent
describes the asymptotics of the norm of the iteration of the differential:

\lambda^{+}(\mu):=\int\lambda^{+}(x)\,d\mu(x)\text{ where }\lambda^{+}(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|D_{x}f^{n}\|.

The set
\mathbb{P}(f)
of
f
-invariant Borel probability measures is equipped with the weak (star) topology which turns it into a compact metrizable space. One can ask:
what are the continuity properties of the map
\mu\mapsto\lambda^{+}(\mu)
over
\mathbb{P}(f)
?

It is an important and popular fact that this map is upper semicontinuous but may fail to be lower semicontinuous. The first, positive statement follows from Kingman’s ergodic theorem for subadditive processes that we recall below. The second, negative statement is a consequence of classical perturbative techniques in so-called Newhouse domains (see Proposition
0.0.1
).

Another equally important functional defined over
\mathbb{P}(f)
is the
Kolmogorov-Sinaï entropy

h(\mu)
. For now, we will just mention the word ‘‘complexity’’ and note that the entropy vanishes if the measure is carried by a periodic orbit. Entropy and exponents are closely linked. For instance, the Ruelle-Margulis inequality
3
3

3

In fact, the Ledrappier-Young formula
[
26
]
expresses exactly the entropy as a sum of the exponents weighted by some dimensions.
(Theorem
3.5.1
) states:

\forall\mu\in\mathbb{P}(f)\quad h(\mu)\leq\int\max\left(\lambda^{+}(x),0\right)\,d\mu(x).

Since we are assuming
f
to be
C^{\infty}
smooth, the function
\mu\in\mathbb{P}(f)\longmapsto h(\mu)
is upper semicontinuous by a theorem of Newhouse (Theorem
3.2.5
, based on Yomdin’s analysis of the iteration of
C^{\infty}
smooth maps, Theorem
4.3.1
). Unless the entropy function is identically zero over
\mathbb{P}(f)
, it is not lower semicontinuous by a closing lemma on surfaces due to Katok
[
22
]
.

The results

Lower semicontinuity ratios of exponent and entropy

Let us observe that, in the setting of
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms, the Kolmogorov-Sinaï entropy and the Lyapunov exponent turn out to satisfy
the same semicontinuity properties
, though for different reasons.
Our results will show that the two continuity defects are nevertheless quantitatively related by a kind of “relative Ruelle-Margulis inequality”.

Our statements deal with sequences of ergodic measures and their weak star limit. Our results take the best and simplest form when this limit happens to be ergodic as in the following statement:

Theorem A
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism of a closed surface.
4
4

4

A closed manifold is a compact and boundaryless manifold.

Let
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of ergodic invariant probability measures for
f
. Assume that their top exponents and their entropies converge to some positive limits. If
\mu
is an ergodic accumulation point of
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in the weak star topology, then

(0.1)

0<^{(1)}\lim_{k}\frac{h(\nu_{k})}{h(\mu)}\leq^{(2)}\lim_{k}\frac{\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})}{\lambda^{+}(\mu)}\leq^{(3)}1.

In particular, continuity of the entropy:
h(\mu)=\lim_{k}h(\nu_{k})>0
, implies continuity of the exponent:
\lambda^{+}(\mu)=\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})>0
.

Note that the assumption
\lim_{k}h(\nu_{k})>0
together with upper semicontinuity and the Ruelle inequality ensure that all terms in the above inequality are well-defined.

Our new result is the middle inequality (2) which can be compared to the Ruelle inequality. The other inequalities are obvious or classical: (1) is the positivity assumption, and (3) is the upper semicontinuity of the Lyapunov exponent (recalled below, see Proposition
1.2.7
).

The above statement is sharp in the following sense:

Proposition 0.0.1
.

On any closed surface, there is a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism
f
such that for any pair
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
, one can find a sequence of ergodic
\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{P}(f)
converging weakly to an ergodic measure
\mu
, and such that

\lim_{k}h(\nu_{k})=\alpha\cdot h(\mu),\text{ and }\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})=\beta\cdot\lambda^{+}(\mu)>0.

One can also arrange that, additionally,
h(\mu)=0
or that
h(\mu)>0
.

Proof.

This is
[
6
, Prop 8.1]
extended to the cases
h(\mu)=0
or
\alpha=0
. These extensions do not require any change in the proof given in
[
6
]
.
∎

In particular, this construction shows that when
\lim_{k}h(\nu_{k})=h(\mu)=0
, the discontinuity of the exponent is arbitrary (
\beta
can take any value in
(0,1]
). Thus, the assumption that
\lim_{k}h(\mu_{k})>0
cannot be dropped from Theorem
A
.

The assumption that the limit is ergodic is a strong restriction. It can be removed at the price of a somewhat more abstract conclusion as follows.

Theorem B
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism of a closed surface.
Let
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of ergodic invariant probability measures for
f
. Assume that their top exponents and their entropy converge to some positive limits. If
\mu
is an arbitrary accumulation point of
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in the weak star topology, then there is a decomposition

\mu=(1-\beta)\mu_{0}+\beta\mu_{1}\text{ with }0<\beta\leq 1\text{ and }\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{P}(f)

satisfying
h(\mu_{1})>0
and

(0.2)

0<\lim_{k}\frac{h(\nu_{k})}{h(\mu_{1})}\leq\beta=\lim_{k}\frac{\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})}{\lambda^{+}(\mu_{1})}.

Deduction of Theorem
A
from Theorem
B
.

Let
f,\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots,\mu
be as in the hypothesis of Theorem
A
. We apply Theorem
B
and get the decomposition
\mu=(1-\beta)\mu_{0}+\beta\mu_{1}
. The ergodicity of
\mu
implies that this decomposition is trivial:
\mu_{1}=\mu
since
\beta>0
. The inequalities in (
0.2
) reduce to those in (
0.1
).
∎

Four applications

We obtain the semicontinuity of some dimensions of invariant probability measures and use it for the construction of natural measures.

Semicontinuity of the Hausdorff dimension of ergodic measures

We deduce from Theorem
A
the semicontinuity of the Hausdorff dimension over ergodic invariant measures with entropy bounded away from zero.

We first recall some basic facts.
We denote by
\operatorname{HD}(X)
the classical Hausdorff dimension of any subset
X
of a metric space
[
18
]
. This notion is extended to any probability Borel measure
\nu
by setting

\operatorname{HD}(\nu):=\inf\{\operatorname{HD}(X):\nu(X)=1\}.

Given a non necessarily ergodic measure
\nu\in\mathbb{P}(f)
whose top exponent
\lambda^{+}(\nu)
and bottom exponent
5
5

5

The bottom exponent
\lambda^{-}(\nu)
is the top exponent with respect to the inverse
f^{-1}
.

\lambda^{-}(\nu)
are both nonzero, the “thermodynamical” unstable and stable dimensions of
\mu
are defined as

\delta^{+}(\nu):=\frac{h(\nu)}{\lambda^{+}(\nu)}\text{ and }\delta^{-}(\nu):=-\frac{h(\nu)}{\lambda^{-}(\nu)}.

When
\nu\in\mathbb{P}(f)
is ergodic and
hyperbolic of saddle type
(i.e. with both positive and negative exponents, none vanishing), these numbers are indeed (nonnegative) Hausdorff dimensions and we have L.-S. Young formula
[
43
]

(0.3)

\operatorname{HD}(\nu)=\delta^{+}(\nu)+\delta^{-}(\nu).

Let
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)\subset\mathbb{P}(f)
be the subset of ergodic measures and, given
h>0
, let
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)\subset\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)
be the further subset of those ergodic invariant probability measures of
f
with entropy at least
h
.

Corollary 0.0.2
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism of a closed surface. For each
0<h\leq h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)
, the Hausdorff dimension
\nu\mapsto\operatorname{HD}(\nu)
is upper semicontinuous over
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
: Given any sequence of measures
\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
admitting a weak star limit
\mu
, if that limit is ergodic, then

\operatorname{HD}(\mu)\geq\limsup_{k}\operatorname{HD}(\nu_{k}).

Remark 0.0.3
.

By Newhouse theorem
3.2.5
, the limit of any weakly converging sequence in
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
has entropy at least
h
. However to apply Theorem
A
, we need ergodicity which we have to assume, since it is automatic in our setting. Now, in contrast to
\{\nu\in\mathbb{P}(f):h(f,\nu)\geq h\}
,

\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
is not compact, hence the above does not yield the existence of an ergodic measure maximizing the Hausdorff dimension, even in restriction to
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
for
h>0
.

We are able to remove the ergodicity assumption only when dealing with the more technical, thermodynamical dimensions
\delta^{+},\delta^{-}
.

Proof of the Corollary.

Let
f
and
h
as above. From the variational principle and the existence of MMEs for
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms, we have
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)\neq\emptyset
. Let
(\nu_{k})
be a sequence of measures in
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
. Assume that it converges in the weak star topology to some limit
\mu
. Since
f
is
C^{\infty}
, Newhouse theorem gives
h(\mu)\geq h
. By assumption,
\mu
is ergodic so
\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
.

Given
H:=\limsup_{k}\operatorname{HD}(\nu_{k})=\limsup_{k}\delta^{+}(\nu_{k})+\delta^{-}(\nu_{k})
, we can find a subsequence such that the following limits exist and satisfy the following conditions:

\lim_{k}\delta^{+}(\nu_{k})+\delta^{-}(\nu_{k})=H,\;\lim_{k}h(\nu_{k}),\;\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k}),\;\lim_{k}\lambda^{-}(\nu_{k}).

By Ruelle inequality,
\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})\geq h>0
, hence we can apply Theorem
A
. The inequality from this theorem is equivalent to

\lim_{k}\delta^{+}(\nu_{k})=\frac{\lim_{k}h(\nu_{k})}{\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})}\leq\frac{h(\mu)}{\lambda^{+}(\mu)}=\delta^{+}(\nu).

Note that this argument shows the upper semicontinuity of
\delta^{+}
in
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
.

Noting that
\lambda^{-}(f,\cdot)=-\lambda^{+}(f^{-1},\cdot)
and
h(f^{-1},\cdot)=h(f,\cdot)
, we see that
\delta^{-}(f,\cdot)=\delta^{+}(f^{-1},\cdot)
, hence the same applies to
\delta^{-}
. We obtain

\limsup_{k}\delta^{+}(\nu_{k})+\delta^{-}(\nu_{k})\leq\delta^{+}(\mu)+\delta^{-}(\mu).

Finally, the Ruelle-Margulis inequality implies that every
\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
, for any
h>0
, is hyperbolic of saddle type, hence L.S. Young formula (
0.3
) applies. It yields the upper semicontinuity of the Hausdorff dimension on
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
.
∎

Maximizing the unstable dimension

In this case, we are able to deal with nonergodic limits to find a measure maximizing the unstable dimension. Indeed, the non-necessarily ergodic version of our result, Theorem
B
, gives the following existence result:

Corollary 0.0.6
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism of a closed surface. For each
h>0
such that
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)\neq\emptyset
, the function
\mu\mapsto\delta^{+}(\mu)
achieves its supremum over
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
.

Note that this is not an immediate consequence of the upper semicontinuity of
\delta^{+}
over
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
, since the latter is not necessarily compact.

We first make the following two observations:

Lemma 0.0.7
.

If measures
\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)
weakly converge to
\mu
in the weak topology with
\nu_{k}(\{x:
sink or source
\})=0
for all large
k
, then the same holds for
\mu
, i.e.,
\mu(\{x:
sink or source
\})=0
.

Proof of the Lemma.

Assume by contradiction that
\mu(\{p\})>0
for some source or sink
p
in the setting of the lemma. Let
B
be the forward or backward basin of
p
. The convergence
\nu_{k}\to\mu
forces
\nu_{k}(B)>0
for large
k
. By ergodicity, a.e.
\nu_{k}
-typical orbit enters
B
. Hence
\nu_{k}
is the periodic measure defined by
p
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 0.0.8
.

Let
f
be a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism of a closed surface and let
\mu\in\mathbb{P}(f)
be such that its ergodic decomposition does not contain any periodic sink. If
\delta^{+}(\mu)>0
, then hyperbolic ergodic components
m^{+}
with
\delta^{+}(m^{+})\geq\delta^{+}(\mu)
appear with positive measure in the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
.

Proof.

Let
\mu=\int\mu_{\xi}\,d\xi
be the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
. Note that
\lambda^{+}(\mu_{\xi})\geq 0
a.e. (otherwise
\mu_{\xi}
would be a sink). Let

H:=\{\xi:\lambda^{+}(\mu_{\xi})>0>\lambda^{-}(\mu_{\xi})\}.

We have

\lambda^{+}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\int\lambda^{+}(\mu_{\xi})\,d\xi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{\geq}}\int_{H}\lambda^{+}(\mu_{\xi})\,d\xi\text{ and }h(\mu)=\int h(\mu_{\xi})\,d\xi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{=}}\int_{H}h(\mu_{\xi})\,d\xi,

where (1) follows from
\lambda^{+}(\mu_{\xi})\geq 0
a.e. and (2) follows from Ruelle-Margulis inequality applied to
f
and
f^{-1}
.
Therefore

\delta^{+}(\mu)=\frac{\int h(\mu_{\xi})\,d\xi}{\int\lambda^{+}(\mu_{\xi})\,d\xi}\leq\frac{\int_{H}\delta^{+}(\mu_{\xi})\cdot\lambda^{+}(\mu_{\xi})\,d\xi}{\int_{H}\lambda^{+}(\mu_{\xi})\,d\xi}.

Understanding the right hand side as an average, this inequality implies that the set
\{\xi:\delta^{+}(\mu_{\xi})\geq\delta^{+}(\mu)\}
has positive measure for
\lambda^{+}\cdot d\xi
hence for
d\xi
.
∎

Proof of the Corollary.

Let
\nu_{k}\to\mu
with
\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
for some
h>0
.
Since
\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})\geq h(\nu_{k})\geq h>0

B
applies to a subsequence with converging exponents and entropies and yields a decomposition

satisfying

\limsup_{k}\delta^{+}(\nu_{k})=\limsup_{k}\frac{h(\nu_{k})}{\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})}\leq\frac{h(\mu_{1})}{\lambda^{+}(\mu_{1})}=\delta^{+}(\mu_{1}).

Note that
\mu_{1}
is not necessarily ergodic or equal to
\mu
.

We apply this to a sequence of measures
\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)
such that

\lim_{k}\delta^{+}(\nu_{k})=\sup\{\delta^{+}(\nu):\nu\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)\}>0.

To conclude, we first note that if

Existence of S.R.B. measures

Our results give some natural characterizations of the existence of hyperbolic
Sinai-Ruelle-Bowen measure
(SRB for short), a fundamental class of measures in smooth ergodic theory
[
25
,
26
]
. These measures
\mu
are a subject of large interest
[
44
]
in part because, according to
[
36
]
, they are
physical measures
: their ergodic basin, i.e., the set of points
x
such that the weak limit
\lim_{n}(1/n)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{k}x}
exists and is
\mu
, has positive volume.

Definition 0.0.9
.

For surface diffeomorphisms, the hyperbolic
SRB measures
are the ergodic measures
\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)
such that

h(\mu)=\lambda^{+}(\mu)>0.

The above is equivalent to
\delta^{+}(\mu)
being well-defined and equal to
1
.
Therefore Corollary
0.0.6
yields:

Corollary 0.0.10
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism of a closed surface. There exists a hyperbolic SRB measure if and only if there is some
h>0
satisfying

\sup\{\delta^{+}(\mu):\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)\}=1.

One can translate the above in terms of
geometric pressure
and the corresponding variational problem. If
\mu\in\mathbb{P}(f)
has no sinks or sources in its ergodic decomposition, its unstable space is a.e. one-dimensional and therefore its geometric pressure is

P_{\rm geo}(\mu):=h(\mu)-\lambda^{+}(\mu)\leq 0.

This quantity is always nonnegative by Ruelle-Margulis inequality. Observe that among hyperbolic measures, the pressure is zero if and only if the thermodynamical unstable dimension satisfies
\delta^{+}(\mu)=1
. Thus:

Corollary 0.0.11
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism of a closed surface. There exists a hyperbolic SRB measure if and only if there is some
h>0
satisfying

\sup\{P_{\rm geo}(\mu):\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}^{h}(f)\}=0.

The above characterizations using Hausdorff dimension and geometric pressure are rather abstract and seem difficult to check. A further work
[
8
]
using, not the results of
[
6
]
, but the underlying techniques and ideas explained in these lectures established a celebrated conjecture of Viana
[
40
]
in the case of
C^{\infty}
smooth surface diffeomorphisms:
positive Lyapunov exponents on a set of positive Lebesgue measure imply the existence of an SRB measure with positive entropy.

Burguet
[
5
]
independently established this result (together with a control on the ergodic basins).

Entropy-continuity of the exponent

The original motivation of
[
6
]
was to establish some uniform hyperbolicity estimates for ergodic measures with large entropy. These estimates lead to a spectral gap and many consequences

These estimates can be deduced from the following consequence of Theorem

Corollary C
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
smooth diffeomorphism of a closed surface with
h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)>0
.
Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots
be ergodic invariant Borel probability measures with weak star limit
\mu
.

If
\lim_{k\to\infty}h(\mu_{k})=h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)
, then the top Lyapunov exponents also converge, as follows

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(\mu_{k})=\lambda^{+}(\mu).

By Newhouse theorem
3.2.5

h(f,\mu)=h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)
, i.e.,
\mu
is a measure maximizing the entropy (or MME).

Proof.

Let
f
,
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots
, and
\mu
be as above. Theorem
B
yields some decomposition
\mu=(1-\beta)\mu_{0}+\beta\mu_{1}
with
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{P}(f)
and

and such that

h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)=\lim_{k}h(\nu_{k})\leq\beta\cdot h(\mu_{1})\text{ and }\beta=\lim_{k}\frac{\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})}{\lambda^{+}(\mu_{1})}.

Since
h(\mu_{1})\leq h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)
, we have
0\neq h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)\leq\beta\cdot h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)
so

. Thus
\mu=\mu_{1}
and
\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})=\lambda^{+}(\mu)
.
∎

Proof of the results and organization of the lectures

Let us summarize our argument for Theorem

Projective dynamics and linear theory

In chapter
1.1
, we review the basic theory of Lyapunov exponents of linear cocycles and explain the classical formulas expressing the Lyapunov exponents as averages of the continuous dilation function with respect to well-chosen measures. These expressions involve the projective dynamics
\widehat{f}
on
\widehat{M}:=\{(x,E):x\in M,\;E
one-dimensional linear subspace of
T_{x}M\}
defined by
(x,E)\mapsto(fx,Df_{x}E)
and the dilation function

\varphi:\widehat{M}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad(x,E)\longmapsto\log|D_{x}f_{|E}|_{x,fx}\;,

where
|A|_{x,fx}

with respect to the Euclidean norms on
T_{x}M
and
T_{fx}M

M
. Since

\|D_{x}f_{|E}\|

Given an ergodic measure

\widehat{\Gamma}^{+}:=\left\{(x,E)\in\widehat{M}:\exists\lim_{n\to\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|Df^{n}_{x}|E\|>0\right\}.

In light of these remarks, the starting point of the proof of Theorem

Part 1: Discontinuity and neutral blocks

Indeed, we first observe that any discontinuity of the Lyapunov exponent

\lim_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha\to 0\\

Part 2: Entropy from parametrizations of curves

To exploit the action of the differential
Df
on the unstable direction we bound the entropy of a measure using the behavior of its generic unstable curves. In fact (see §
3.7.8
), there is a
formula of Ledrappier and Young
expressing the entropy of an ergodic measure as the complexity of its unstable disintegrations along unstable Pesin manifolds. These Pesin manifolds are, in our case, smooth curves tangent to the unstable bundle at typical points and the dynamics along this bundle is precisely what is controlled by the neutral blocks.

To implement this control, we will use “good parametrizations”, i.e., parametrizations at small scales (both in position and direction) with bounds on their derivatives. This is provided by
Yomdin’s theory
applied to the projective dynamics which we will explain in Chapter
4
.

Part 3: Nonlinear estimates

The last part of the proof of Theorem
D
is more delicate. We need to estimate the growth of the number of good reparametrizations of typical unstable curves. The information at our disposal is two-fold:

(1)

the long neutral blocks;

(2)

the topological entropy.

How to use (1) is the technical core of the proof. It is presented in Chapter
5
. Of course, the differential controls the expansion along the unstable curve on the surface. But to use the differential at
one point
requires the tangent to the curve to be almost constant, while, in general, even curves whose lengths stay small may have exponentially growing oscillations.
To solve this difficulty, we use that the unstable direction is a measurable section.
Now, a measurable function has some modulus of continuity over a set of measure close to
1
by Lusin theorem.
Thus a tight control in position will yield some control in direction. We will propagate these two joint controls using what we call below a double scale: one on the surface and one on the projective extension. This will show that neutral blocks do not contribute to entropy even in the projective extension.

Outside of these neutral blocks, we will use (2), yielding a control in terms of dynamical balls by the topological entropy. A standard application of Yomdin’s theory will translate the number of dynamical balls into an estimate on the number of reparametrizations so that we will be able to use alternately (1) and (2), as explained in Chapter
6
.

In the final chapters
7
and
8
, we explain the additional ideas needed for the more powerful Theorems
A
and
B
and then point out some further developments and a few open problems.

1.2.1.
Top Lyapunov exponent

Definition 1.2.1

The

is the following limit (if it exists):

The

at

\lambda^{+}(F,x):=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{n}\log\|F^{n}_{x}\|_{x,f^{n}(x)}.

The key remark is that:

\varphi(x,n+m)\leq\varphi(x,m)+\varphi(f^{m}x,n).

for all

if

for all

The

\gamma(\varphi):=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\int\varphi(x,n)\,\mu(dx).

It is easily checked that
\gamma(\varphi)\in[-\infty,\int_{X}\varphi(x,1)\,d\mu]
if the process is integrable and that

1.2.2.
Existence of the average top exponent

The average exponent has very nice properties as a direct consequence of the subadditivity and Fekete lemma.

Proposition 1.2.3
.

Let
F:V\to V
be a bundle endomorphism of a bundle with a Riemannian structure.

If
\log^{+}\|F_{x}\|_{x\in X}\in L^{1}(\mu)

We note that the average exponent

The proof the above proposition relies on the following elementary fact:

(Fekete)
.

If

is a sequence of real numbers which is subadditive
(\forall n,m\;a_{m+n}\leq a_{m}+a_{n})
then the following limit exists:

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{n}a_{n}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}a_{n}\in[-\infty,a_{1}].

Proof of the Proposition.

The existence of the average exponent follows from the subadditivity of the sequence

1.2.3.
Semicontinuity of the average top exponent

Given a cocycle

The key point is that the functions appearing in (

Proposition 1.2.7

The average top Lyapunov exponent

1.2.5.
Multiplicative ergodic theorem

The Lyapunov exponents can be given a more precice geometric interpretation as rates of growth of vectors under iteration of a vector bundle morphism. This is provided by the
multiplicative ergodic theorem
(see, eg,
[

For this statement, we define the angle between two subspaces of the same fiber as

\angle(A,B):=\min\{\arccos(a\cdot b)\geq 0:(a,b)\in A\times B,\|a\|=\|b\|=1\}\in[0,\pi/2].

Theorem 1.2.13

(Oseledets)

Let
p:V\to X
be a measured finite-dimensional vector bundle and let

be a bundle automorphism over a measure-preserving automorphism
(X,\mu,f)

\log\|F_{x}\|_{x,fx}\in L^{1}(\mu).

Then there exist a set

of full
\mu
-measure, a measurable function

•

When necessary, one writes

Let

2

2

Similarly, if all its exponents are positive, then it must be a source.

The proof of this lemma will require the following important result. The Pliss Lemma allows one to deduce uniform estimates from simple averages by restricting oneself to a subset of positive measure.

Theorem 1.2.16

(Pliss Lemma)
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
be a finite sequence of real numbers with average
\alpha:=\frac{1}{N}(a_{1}+\dots+a_{N})
. Let
A:=\max(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})
.

We have, for any
\beta<\alpha
,

\#\{1\leq k\leq N:\forall 0\leq\ell\leq k\;(a_{\ell}+a_{\ell+1}+\dots+a_{k})\geq\beta\cdot\alpha\}\geq\frac{\alpha-\beta}{A-\beta}\cdot N.

Remark 1.2.17
.

Pliss Lemma implies the lower bound:

m:=\#\{1\leq k\leq N:a_{k}\geq\beta\cdot\alpha\}\geq\frac{\alpha-\beta}{A-\beta}\cdot N\;,

which is obvious and optimal: consider the case where
a=(\beta-\epsilon,\dots,\beta-\epsilon,A,\dots,A)
for some arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
and notice that

(N-m)(\beta-\epsilon)+m\cdot A\geq N\alpha\;,

so
m(A-\beta+\epsilon)\geq N(\alpha-\beta+\epsilon)
, which yields the Pliss bound above as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Proof of Pliss Lemma.

Say that an integer interval in
\{1,\dots,N\}
is bad if
(\#I)^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}<\beta
. Otherwise say that the interval is good. Say that an integer
1\leq k\leq N
is Pliss if for all
0\leq\ell\leq k
, the interval
\{k-\ell,\dots,k\}
is good. We have to show that there are at least
N\cdot(\beta-\alpha)/(A-\alpha)
Pliss integers.

To this end, notice that any non-Pliss integer
1\leq k\leq N
is contained in a bad interval
\{\ell(k),\dots,k\}
for some
1\leq\ell(k)\leq k
which we can assume to be maximal. We claim that such bad intervals are disjoint or nested. Otherwise, there would be integers
k,k^{\prime}
such that
\ell(k)<\ell(k^{\prime})\leq k<k^{\prime}
. Note that
\{k+1,\dots,k^{\prime}\}
is good by maximality of
\ell(k^{\prime})
. This implies that
\{\ell(k^{\prime}),\dots,k\}
is bad. But this would contradict the maximality of
\ell(k)
.

Thus the non-Pliss integers are contained in a disjoint union
B
of bad intervals. The average
(\#B)^{-1}\sum_{i\in B}a_{i}
is therefore less than
\beta
. Thus

N\cdot\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\leq(\#B)\cdot\beta+(N-\#B)\cdot A=(N-(N-\#B))\cdot\beta+(N-\#B)\cdot A\;,

hence

N\cdot(\alpha-\beta)\leq(N-\#B)\cdot(A-\beta).

Therefore the number of Pliss integers is at least
N-\#B\geq N(\alpha-\beta)/(A-\beta)
, proving the claim.
∎

, we get some not necessarily ergodic lift
\widehat{\mu}\in\mathbb{P}(f)
. Write its ergodic decomposition
\widehat{\mu}=\int_{\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(\widehat{f})}\widehat{\nu}\,d\widehat{P}
. Taking the image by
\widehat{\pi}_{*}
which is continuous, we get

\mu=\int_{\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(\widehat{f})}\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\nu})\,d\widehat{P}.

Since
\mu
is ergodic, it is an extreme point of
\mathbb{P}(f)
. Hence
\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\nu})=\mu
a.e. Thus, a.e. ergodic component
\widehat{\nu}
is an ergodic lift of
\mu
, proving item (ii).

To prove item (iii), we rely on the following identity:

(1.7)

\forall(x,v)\in V\setminus 0\;\forall n\geq 1\quad\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{\varphi}(\widehat{f}^{k}(x,v))=\log\|F^{n}.v\|_{f^{n}x}-\log\|v\|_{x}.

This and the Birkhoff theorem imply that for
\widehat{\mu}
-a.e.
\widehat{v}=(x,\mathbb{R}.v)\in\widehat{V}

\widehat{\mu}(\widehat{\varphi})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{\varphi}(\widehat{f}^{k}(\widehat{v}))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|F^{n}_{x}.v\|_{f^{n}x}=\lambda(x,v).

Item (iii) is proved.

We turn to item (iv). First, if
\widehat{\mu}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(\widehat{f})
is some ergodic lift of
\mu
, then one has item (iii), i.e.,
\widehat{\mu}(\widehat{\varphi})=\lambda(x,v)
for
\widehat{\mu}
-a.e.
\widehat{v}
, that is,
\widehat{\mu}(\widehat{\varphi})
belongs to the spectrum of
(F,\mu)
.

Conversely, let us see that
\lambda^{1}(\mu)
is an average
\widehat{\mu}(\widehat{\varphi})
for some ergodic lift
\widehat{\mu}
of
\mu
.
Pick some
\mu
-generic
x\in X_{\rm reg}
and fix some
v\in E^{1}_{x}\setminus 0
. Using the weak topology on
\mathbb{P}(\widehat{V})
, take any accumulation point

\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}=\lim_{j}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{j}-1}F^{k}_{*}\delta_{(x,v)}.

Since
\widehat{\varphi}
is continuous, it holds that

\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}(\widehat{\varphi})=\lim_{j}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{j}-1}\widehat{\varphi}(\widehat{v}^{k}\widehat{v})=\lambda(x,v)=\lambda^{1}(\mu).

Since
\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}
might fail to be ergodic, we consider its ergodic decomposition
\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}=\int\widehat{\nu}\,d\widehat{P}
. We must have

\lambda^{1}(\mu)=\int\widehat{\nu}(\widehat{\varphi})\,d\widehat{P}\;.

Therefore the inequality
\widehat{\nu}(\widehat{\varphi})\leq\lambda^{1}(\mu)
must be an equality for
\widehat{P}
-a.e.
\widehat{\nu}
: a.e. ergodic component
\widehat{\mu}
of
\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}
is an ergodic lift
\widehat{\mu}
with
\widehat{\mu}(\widehat{\varphi})=\lambda^{1}(\mu)
.

The previous argument applied to
F^{-1}
gives an ergodic lift
\widehat{\mu}
realizing the bottom exponent:
\lambda^{r(\mu)}(\mu)=\widehat{\mu}(\widehat{\varphi})
.

For
r(\mu)\leq 2
, this is enough to prove item (iv).

To prove item (v), let
\widehat{\mu}=\int\widehat{\nu}\,d\widehat{P}
be the ergodic decomposition of
\widehat{\mu}
. Since
\widehat{\pi}_{*}:\mathbb{P}(\widehat{f})\to\mathbb{P}(f)
is continuous and affine, we have

\mu=\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\mu})=\int_{\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(\widehat{f})}\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\nu})\,d\widehat{P}=\int_{\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)}\nu\,d\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{P})\;.

Since the ergodic decomposition
\mu=\int\nu\,dP
is unique, we have
\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{P})=P
.

Consider the Rokhlin disintegration of
\widehat{P}
corresponding to the map
\widehat{\pi}:\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(\widehat{f})\to\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)
:

\widehat{P}=\int_{\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)}\widehat{P}_{\nu}\,d\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{P})\text{ with }\widehat{P}_{\nu}(\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(\widehat{f})\cap\widehat{\pi}_{*}^{-1}(\nu))=1\text{ $\widehat{P}$-a.e.}

Hence, the ergodic decomposition of
\widehat{\mu}
can be rewritten as follows:

\widehat{\mu}=\int_{\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(\widehat{f})\cap\widehat{\pi}_{*}^{-1}(\nu)}\,d\widehat{P}_{\nu}(\widehat{\nu})\right)\,dP,

which proves item (v).

Item (vi) is an immediate consequence of (v).
∎

1.4.
Reading the simple Lyapunov spectrum

We consider a linear invertible cocycle
A
of a vector bundle
p:V\to X
.
Recall that
X_{\rm reg}
is the set of Oseledets regular points. The following is the set of points with
simple Lyapunov spectrum
:

X_{\#}:=\{x\in X_{\rm reg}:r(x)=\dim V\}\;.

We also consider

X_{=}:=\{x\in X_{\rm reg}:r(x)=1\}=\{x\in X_{\rm reg}:\lambda^{+}(F,x)=\lambda^{-}(F,x)\}.

On
X_{=}
, the Oseledets theorem reduces to the following equality:

\lim_{|n|\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|F^{n}_{x}.v\|=\lambda^{+}(F,x)=\lambda^{-}(F,x)\text{ for all }v\in V_{x}\setminus 0.

Over
X_{\#}
, the situation is more interesting. To describe it, it is convenient to use the projective dynamics as follows. We give the general statement though we are only going to consider the case where
\dim V=2
and
X_{\rm reg}=X_{=}\sqcup X_{\#}
is a partition.

Recall that
\widehat{V}^{i}
(
1\leq i\leq\dim V
) is the measurable sub-bundle defined by Oseledets theorem.

Proposition 1.4.1
.

Let
\widehat{V}^{i}_{\#}
be the restriction of the bundle
\widehat{V}^{i}
to
X_{\#}
.
Let
\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)
with
\mu(X_{\#})=1
. Then the following are true:

(1)

\mu
admits exactly
d=\dim V
ergodic lifts
\widehat{\mu}^{1},\dots,\widehat{\mu}^{d}

\widehat{\mu}^{i}:=\int_{X_{\#}}\delta_{(x,V^{i}_{x})}\,d\mu(x)\quad(i=1,\dots,d).

(2)

For each
i=1,\dots,d
,
\widehat{\mu}^{i}(\widehat{\varphi})=\lambda^{i}(A,\mu)
.

(3)

The ergodic components of any invariant lift are, a.s., ergodic lifts.

Proof.

We first prove (1), starting with showing that each
\widehat{\mu}^{i}
,
1\leq i\leq r(\mu)=d
, is an ergodic lift.

We recall Oseledets theorem. Since
\mu(X_{\#})=1
, for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
,
E^{i}_{x}
is one-dimensional and satisfies
Df_{x}(E^{i}_{x})=E^{i}_{fx}
. Therefore, there is a unique measurable section
\Gamma^{i}:X_{\#}\to\widehat{V}
,
x\mapsto E^{i}_{x}
. In particular,
\Gamma^{i}\circ f=F\circ\Gamma^{i}
, so
\widehat{\mu}^{i}:=\Gamma^{i}_{*}(\mu)\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(\widehat{f})
(defined in the above statement) is an ergodic lift. Obviously,
\widehat{\mu}^{i}(\widehat{\varphi})=\int_{X_{\#}}\widehat{\varphi}(E^{i}_{x})\,d\mu(x)=\lambda^{i}(\mu)
.

Conversely, let
\widehat{\mu}
be an arbitrary ergodic lift of
\mu
. We are going to show that it must be
\widehat{\mu}^{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq r(\mu)
, which in particular implies that
\widehat{\mu}(\widehat{\varphi})=\lambda^{i}(\mu)
.

Let
(x,\mathbb{R}.v)
be some
\widehat{\mu}
-typical point. We can assume that
\|v\|_{x}=1
. We write
v=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}v^{i}_{x}
where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
v^{i}_{x}
a unit vector of
E^{i}_{x}
(unique up to sign). Let
I
be the smallest integer such that
\alpha^{I}\neq 0
. By ergodicity, this integer is a.e.-constant.

If
I=d
, then
\widehat{\mu}=\widehat{\mu}^{d}
and the claim is proved. Otherwise, note that
\lambda^{I}>\lambda^{I+1}>\dots>\lambda^{d}
for all
i>I
. Using the Euclidean distance on the unit sphere in
V_{x}
, we obtain

d\left(\frac{Df^{n}.v}{\|Df^{n}.v\|},E^{I}_{f^{n}x}\right)=d\left(\widehat{f}^{n}(v),\widehat{f}^{n}(E^{I}_{x})\right)\leq C(x)e^{n(\lambda^{I}-\lambda^{I+1})}\to 0.

By invariance of
\widehat{\mu}
, this implies the distance must be zero for each
n
. Thus,
\widehat{v}=E^{I}_{x}
, i.e.,
\widehat{\mu}=\widehat{\mu}^{I}
and (1) is proved.

For each
1\leq i\leq d
, the Oseledets theorem gives
A_{x}(V^{i}_{x})=V^{i}_{f(x)}
, which implies the invariance:
\widehat{f}_{*}(\widehat{\mu}^{i})=\widehat{\mu}^{i}
. Since each space
E^{i}_{x}
is one-dimensional, we have
\log|Df^{n}_{x}|=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log|Df|_{E^{i}_{f^{k}x}}|
. Since
\mu
is invariant and
\log\|Df|_{E}\|
is bounded, the following holds:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}^{i}(\widehat{\varphi})

\displaystyle=\int_{X_{\#}}\log|Df|_{E^{i}_{x}}|\,d\mu=\int_{X_{\#}}\frac{1}{n}\log|Df^{n}|_{E^{i}_{x}}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{X_{\#}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Df^{n}|_{E^{i}_{x}}|\,d\mu=\int_{X_{\#}}L^{i}_{x}\,d\mu=\lambda^{i}(A,\mu).

This proves (2).

Let
\widehat{\mu}=\int\widehat{\mu}_{\xi}\,d\xi
be the ergodic decomposition of a lift
\widehat{\mu}\in\mathbb{P}(\widehat{f})
of
\mu\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)
. Since
\widehat{\pi}_{*}
is a continuous and linear map, we get

\widehat{\mu}=\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\mu})=\int\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\mu}_{\xi})\,d\xi\;.

Hence by ergodicity of
\widehat{\mu}
, for a.e.
\xi
,
\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\mu_{\xi})=\mu
, the ergodic components of the lift are ergodic lifts of
\widehat{\mu}
. This proves (3).
∎

1.5.
Continuity properties of the top Lyapunov exponents

1.5.1.
The setting

We are going to apply the preceding results to the continuity of the top Lyapunov exponent.
Given
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)
weakly converging to some
\mu\in\mathbb{P}(f)
, we consider the average top exponents

(1.8)

\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)-\limsup_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})\geq 0

(the inequality is Proposition
1.2.7
). By ergodicity, the average top Lyapunov exponent of
\nu_{k}
coincides with its top exponent a.e.,

\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})=\lambda^{1}(f,x)\text{ for $\nu_{k}$-a.e. }x\in M,

while, for not necessarily ergodic limit measures, we have

\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)=\int\lambda^{+}(f,x)\,d\mu.

In this section, we express (
1.8
) in terms of suitable projective lifts of
\nu_{k},\mu
, restricting, when necessary, to surface diffeomorphisms.

1.5.2.
Top Lyapunov exponents of
\nu_{k}
’s and their limit

In our applications, each ergodic
\nu_{k}
will have positive entropy for the surface diffeomorphism
f
, hence have, almost everywhere, exactly one positive and one negative exponent. In particular, there is no harm assuming that

(1.9)

\text{each $\nu_{k}$, $k\geq 1$, has simple Lyapunov spectrum almost everywhere}.

Thus, Proposition
1.4.1
gives the existence of the lift
\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{1}
of
\nu_{k}
to
\widehat{\Gamma}^{1}
. Moreover,

\forall k\geq 1\quad\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})=\int\varphi\,d\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{1}.

By compactness, perhaps after passing to a subsequence, the measures
\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{1}
converge to some
\widehat{\mu}\in\mathbb{P}(\widehat{f})
. By definition of the weak convergence and the continuity of
\varphi:\widehat{M}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda^{+}(f,\nu_{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\nu}_{k}(\varphi)=\widehat{\mu}(\varphi).

Obviously,
\widehat{\mu}
is a lift of
\mu
. However,
\widehat{\mu}
does not need to coincide with the lift
\widehat{\mu}^{1}
. Indeed, such a lift may fail to exist as
\dim(E^{1}_{x})
may be greater than
1
on a set of positive
\mu
-measure.

1.5.3.
Decomposition into hyperbolic and neutral parts

Recall that
M_{\#}:=\{x\in M_{\rm reg}:r(f,x)=\dim M\}
is the set of (Oseledets regular) points with simple Lyapunov spectrum. Since possibly

, we cannot apply Proposition
1.3.3
directly. We consider the decomposition (distinct from the neutral decomposition to be discussed later):

\mu=t\cdot\mu_{\#}+(1-t)\cdot\mu_{*},\text{ where }\mu:=\mu|_{M_{\#}},\;\mu_{*}:=\mu|_{M_{\rm reg}\setminus M_{\#}},\text{ and }0\leq t\leq 1.

where we use the following notation for any invariant measurable subset
X
, we let

\mu|_{X}:=\frac{\mu(\cdot\cap X)}{\mu(X)}\text{ if }\mu(X)>0\text{ and }\mu|_{X}:=\mu\text{ otherwise.}

We are going to control separately the two components
\widehat{\mu}_{\#},\widehat{\mu}_{*}
.

1.5.4.
Control of the neutral part in dimension

When
\dim M=2
, we control
\mu_{*}
by using the fact that

coincides with the set

(1.10)

h(f,\mu)=t\cdot h(f,\mu_{\#})\text{ and }\lambda^{+}(f,\mu)=t\cdot\lambda^{+}(f,\mu_{\#}).

Lemma 1.5.1
.

Let
f
be a diffeomorphism of a closed manifold
M
and let
\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)

(k\geq 1)
weakly converge to some
\mu

\nu_{k}
are non atomic.

Then
L^{1}(f,x)=\dots=L^{d}(f,x)=0

\mu
-a.e.

Proof.

Up to replacing

, we can assume by contradiction that

for a set of points

\mu
contains (with positive measure) elements which have only negative exponents, hence are sinks by Lemma

By weak convergence,
\nu_{k}(B)>0
for all large

\nu_{k}
is ergodic, this implies that
\nu_{k}
-a.e. orbit visits

∎

Chapter 2
Neutral decomposition

Recall our general strategy: given a sequence of ergodic measures
\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)
weakly converging to some
\mu\in\mathbb{P}(f)
,
we want to use discontinuities of the top exponent of measures to derive a non-trivial bound on their entropies.
This chapter accomplishes the linear part of this strategy, deducing from any discontinuity of the exponent

(2.1)

\lambda^{+}(\mu)-\limsup_{k}\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})\qquad(\text{nonnegative by upper semicontinuity}),

the existence of a

of the measure
\mu
whose non-expanding properties will be used in later chapters to bound the entropy.

More precisely, we consider the unstable lifts
\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(\widehat{f})
converging to some
\widehat{\mu}\in\mathbb{P}(\widehat{f})
. As we have observed in the previous chapter, since
\widehat{\varphi}
is continuous on
\widehat{M}
, we get

\lim_{k}\lambda^{+}(\nu_{k})=\lim_{k}\widehat{\nu}_{k}^{+}(\widehat{\varphi})=\widehat{\mu}(\widehat{\varphi}).

Hence, any discontinuity of the exponent means that
\widehat{\mu}
differs from the unstable lift
\widetilde{\mu}^{+}
of
\mu
. In Theorem

, this will lead to a

\widehat{\mu}=(1-\beta)\widehat{\mu}_{0}+\beta\widehat{\mu}_{1}\quad(0\leq\beta\leq 1,\;\widehat{\mu}_{0},\widehat{\mu}_{1}\in\mathbb{P}(\widehat{f})),

where
\widehat{\mu}_{0}
has some non-expanding properties at the level of the differential
Df
.

The neutral part
(1-\beta)\widehat{\mu}_{0}
is obtained by taking a limit of the empirical measure defined by
\nu_{k}
-typical orbits restricted to certain segments. Those are the
neutral orbit segments
during which the unstable direction does not expand going forward.

We will later show that the lack of expansion within neutral orbit segments prevents them from contributing to the entropy, which yields a bound of the following form:

\limsup_{k}h(\nu_{k})\leq\beta\cdot h(\mu_{1})\qquad(\mu_{1}:=\widehat{\pi}_{*}(\widehat{\mu}_{1})).

We will define and build an abstract neutral decomposition in the setting of an additive cocycle:

–

T:X\to X
is a homeomorphism of a compact metric space;

–

\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function;

–

\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(T)
weakly converge to
\mu\in\mathbb{P}(T)
.

We will apply this construction to the projective dynamics of a smooth surface diffeomorphisms by taking:

–

T:X\to X
, the projective extension of the surface diffeomorphism;

–

\varphi
, the logarithm of its dilation;

–

\nu_{k}
, the unstable lift of a hyperbolic measure in
\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(f)
.

Our study of Lyapunov exponents in the previous chapter will allow us to relate the term
(1-\beta)\widehat{\mu}_{0}
of the neutral decomposition to the discontinuity (
2.1
).

For

-neutral blocks for

Proposition 2.1.3

Given

-neutral blocks that are maximal for the inclusion. Moreover, it is equivariant, i.e.,

Let

∎

Definition 2.1.4

The set of

The set

2.2.
Neutral decomposition

We define and discuss the neutral decomposition of a sequence of measures.
By the Birkhoff ergodic theorem, one can reconstruct an ergodic measure from its typical orbits. More precisely, if
\nu\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(T)
, then the Birkhoff ergodic theorem gives, for
\nu
-a.e.
x\in X
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{T^{k}x}=\mu\;.

We will obtain the neutral decomposition by splitting the above sums into neutral blocks and the remainder. We will then let
\alpha\to 0
(to capture all expansion) and
L\to\infty
(to get invariance).

Recall that a sequence
\nu_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{{\operatorname{erg}}}(T)\to\mu\in\mathbb{P}(T)
has been fixed together with a continuous function
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(2.3)

\liminf_{k}\int_{X}\varphi\,d\nu_{k}>0.

We will consider
\alpha>0
arbitrarily small, and in particular smaller than the above limit.

We consider
subprobability Borel measures
on
X
, i.e., positive Borel measures with total mass at most
1
. The set of such measures is compact for the weak (star) topology which is metrizable (for the same reasons as the set of probability measures).

Some notations.

For a subprobability measure
\nu
and an integrable, nonnegative function
g\in L^{1}(\nu)
, we write
g\cdot\nu
for the subprobability measure
2
2

2

Often the measure
g\cdot\nu
is denoted by
g\,d\nu
.
defined by
(g\cdot\nu)(E)=\int_{E}g\,d\nu
for all measurable set
E\subset X
.

When
g
is a measurable function, either nonnegative or integrable, we also write
\nu(g)
for
\int g\,d\nu
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

A
\varphi
-
neutral decomposition
of the sequence
(\nu_{k})
is:

\mu=m_{0}+m_{1}
where
m_{0},m_{1}
are both
T
-invariant, Borel subprobability measures satisfying, for some sequence

\mu^{0}_{\alpha,L,k_{i}}:=1_{\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)}\cdot\nu_{k_{i}}\hskip-19.91684pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}(\alpha,L)\to(0,\infty)\\
i\to\infty\end{array}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hskip-19.91684ptm_{0}\text{ and }\mu^{1}_{\alpha,L,k_{i}}:=(1-1_{\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)})\cdot\nu_{k_{i}}\hskip-19.91684pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}(\alpha,L)\to(0,\infty)\\
i\to\infty\end{array}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hskip-19.91684ptm_{1}.

More explicitly, for any pair of neighborhoods

We call

the

and we let

and

(if

Under the positivity assumption (

Theorem 2.2.2
.

Given a sequence

satisfying (

\varphi
-neutral decomposition

(B)

for

-a.e.

\liminf_{n}\frac{1}{n}S_{n}\varphi(x)>0
.

On the one hand,
\beta>0

We can rewrite the conclusion of the theorem as follows:

Corollary 2.2.3
.

In the setting of the theorem above, we also define

(B)

2.3.
Construction of a neutral decomposition

In this section, we build a
\varphi
-neutral decomposition for a given sequence
\nu_{k}\to\mu
and function
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
, proving Theorem
2.2.2
.

Recall that
\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)\subset X
is the measurable set of points in
X
that are contained in some
\alpha
-neutral block of length at least
L
. In particular,

(2.5)

\forall 0<\alpha\leq\alpha^{\prime}\;\forall L\geq L^{\prime}\quad\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)\subset\mathfrak{N}(\alpha^{\prime},L^{\prime}).

For this proof it is convenient to say that a subsequence
k_{i}\to\infty
is

if for any

the following weak limit exists:

m_{0}^{\alpha,L}:=\lim_{i}\left(1_{\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)}\cdot\nu_{k_{i}}\right)

Note that the above weak limit always belong to the set
\operatorname{S\mathbb{P}}(X)
of Borel sub-probabilities on

.
Note also that this asks for the convergence of uncountably many sequences
(\alpha_{j},L_{j})\to(0,\infty)
but the monotonicity (

) will save the day.

The first step of the proof of the theorem is to build an admissible sequence.

Lemma 2.3.1
.

There exists an admissible sequence
k_{i}\to\infty
.

Proof.

In this proof, we use the notation
\mu_{\alpha,L,\ell}:=1_{\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)}\cdot\nu_{\ell}
.

We rely on the classical Cantor’s diagonal argument in compact metric spaces such as
[0,1]
or
\operatorname{S\mathbb{P}}(M)
. Let us first remark that since
L
takes only countably many values, it is enough to find, for each

, a subsequence
(k_{i})
so that
(\mu_{\alpha,L,k_{i}})
converges (note that
k_{i}
depends on
L
). Indeed, the diagonal Cantor argument will then conclude.

Thus we fix
L\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
We let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a dense countable subset of
(0,1)
. An application of Cantor’s diagonal argument yields a strictly increasing sequence of integers
\ell_{i}
such that:

\forall\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}\quad\text{the limit }m_{0}^{\alpha,L}:=\lim_{i}\mu_{\alpha,L,\ell_{i}}\text{ exists}.

We extend the function
\alpha\mapsto m_{0}^{\alpha}
from
{\mathcal{A}}
to
(0,1)
.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be a countable dense subset of the continuous nonnegative functions on
X
.
We fix

\alpha\in(0,1)\longmapsto\widetilde{m}_{0}^{\alpha,L}(u):=\sup\{m_{0}^{\beta,L}(u):\beta\in{\mathcal{A}}\cap(0,\alpha]\}.

Since

\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}

\widetilde{m}_{0}^{\alpha,L}(u)=m_{0}^{\alpha,L}(u)
for all

.

The function
\alpha\mapsto\widetilde{m}_{0}^{\alpha,L}(u)

is countable and so is
\Delta_{L}:=\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\Delta_{L,u}

Therefore Cantor’s diagonal argument can be applied again, extracting
(k_{i})
from
(\ell_{i})
such that

does exist for all
\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}\cup\Delta
and all

.

Now, let

be arbitrary (no longer contained in a countable set). We will show the existence of
\lim_{i}\mu_{\alpha_{0},L,k_{i}}
. If
\alpha_{0}\in{\mathcal{A}}\cup\Delta
, this is obvious. So we can assume that
\alpha_{0}\notin{\mathcal{A}}\cup\Delta
. In particular,

is a point of continuity of

for each

. Fix some

and

\widetilde{m}_{0}^{\alpha_{0},L}(u)-\epsilon<\widetilde{m}_{0}^{\alpha_{-},L}(u)\leq\widetilde{m}_{0}^{\alpha_{+},L}(u)<\widetilde{m}_{0}^{\alpha_{0},L}(u)+\epsilon.

Without lost of generality, we can assume that

and

\forall i\geq i_{0}\quad\widetilde{m}_{0}^{\alpha_{0},L}(u)-\epsilon<\mu_{\alpha_{-},L,k_{i}}(u)\leq\mu_{\alpha_{+},L,k_{i}}(u)<\widetilde{m}_{0}^{\alpha_{0},L}(u)+\epsilon\;,

.

Note that

\displaystyle m_{0}^{\alpha,L}(u)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i}\nu_{k_{i}}(1_{\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)}u)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}1_{\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)}(T^{m}x_{i})u(T^{m}x_{i})

Proposition 2.3.3
.

Fix some admissible subsequence
k_{i}\to\infty
. For each
\alpha>0
and

, let

be the corresponding limit in

m_{0}:=\lim_{(\alpha,L)\to(0,\infty)}m_{0}^{\alpha,L}\;.

Proof.

Let

be some non-negative continuous function on

imply that

\forall\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\;\forall L_{1}\leq L_{2}\quad m_{0}^{\alpha_{2},L_{2}}(u)\leq m_{0}^{\alpha_{1},L_{1}}(u).

\lim_{(\alpha,L)\to(0,\infty)}m_{0}^{\alpha,L}(u)=\inf_{\alpha>0,L\geq 1}m_{0}^{\alpha,L}(u)

By Lemma

2.4.
Properties of the neutral decomposition

To prove the theorem, we apply Lemma
2.3.1
to get an admissible sequence, and Proposition
2.3.3
to get corresponding subprobability measures
m_{0},m_{1}
. It remains to show that this decomposition has
m_{1}\neq 0
and satisfies the claims (A) and (B).
To simplify notations, we replace
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
by the subsequence
(\nu_{k_{i}})_{i\geq 1}
defined by the admissible sequence.

Proof of (A) and
m_{1}\neq 0

We assume
m_{0}\neq 0
.
(A) is a simple consequence of the boundedness of
\varphi
and the maximality of the neutral blocks. By the Birkhoff theorem,
m_{0}^{\alpha,L}(\varphi)
is an average over
x
of the limits:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}1_{\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)}(T^{k}x)\varphi(T^{k}x)

In particular, it is an average of
\varphi(T^{k}x)
for
k\in N_{x}(\alpha,L)
(i.e.,
T^{-k}x\in\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)
). To estimate
m_{0}^{\alpha,L}(\varphi)
it is therefore enough to estimate its average over neutral blocks.

Now, if
\;[\!\!\![\;a,b\;[\!\!\![\;
is an
(\alpha,L)
-neutral block for

, then

\frac{1}{b-a}\sum_{k=a}^{b-1}\varphi(T^{k}x)\leq\alpha\;,

whereas the maximality of neutral blocks implies
\sum_{k=a}^{b}\varphi(T^{k}x)>(b-a+1)\alpha
so

\frac{1}{b-a}\sum_{k=a}^{b-1}\varphi(T^{k}x)>\alpha+\frac{\alpha-\varphi(T^{b}x)}{b-a}.

Hence

\alpha-\frac{\alpha+\|\varphi\|_{\sup}}{L}\leq m_{0}^{\alpha,L}(\varphi)\leq\alpha

Thus, in any limit where
L\to\infty
,
m_{0}(\varphi)=\alpha
and
m_{0}
is obtained as
\alpha\to 0
, proving (A).

Therefore
\mu(\varphi)=m_{0}(\varphi)+m_{1}(\varphi)=m_{1}(\varphi)
, but this is positive by assumption
2.3
. Thus
m_{1}\neq 0
or
\beta\neq 0
.

Proof of (B)

We proceed by contradiction, assuming

\gamma:=\frac{1}{2}m_{1}\left\{x\in X:\liminf_{n\to\infty}(1/n)S_{n}\varphi(x)\leq 0\right\}>0.

We are going to use Pliss lemma (Theorem
1.2.16
) to deduce from the above assumption the existence of many
(\alpha_{0},L_{0})
-neutral blocks disjoint from all
(\alpha_{1},L_{1})
-neutral blocks for

and

. This will contradict the construction of
m_{0}
as the limit of
m_{0}^{\alpha,L}
.

This convergence gives
\alpha_{0}>0
and
L_{0}\geq 1
such that

\forall 0<\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}\;\forall L\geq L_{0}\quad\left|m_{0}^{\alpha,L}(X)-m_{0}(X)\right|<\frac{\gamma}{100}.

Note that we can assume that
\alpha_{0}
does not belong to the countable set of values
\alpha
such that
\mu\{x:\exists n\geq 1
s.t.
(1/n)S_{n}\varphi(x)=\alpha\}>0

For

Chapter 4
Yomdin theory

In this chapter, we introduce the notions and techniques that we need from Yomdin’s theory. These ideas are very relevant to our goals in two respects:

First, Yomdin theory implies that, thanks to the
C^{\infty}
smoothness, the tail entropy vanishes (Theorem
4.1.1

\nu_{k}

Second, the reparametrization results (Theorem

curves under the dynamics. More precisely, they imply that the number of reparametrizations needed to control the smooth size of the iterations of a curve does not grow much faster than the number of dynamical balls needed to cover that curve.

4.1.
Motivation and key ideas of Yomdin theory

The key insight of Yomdin theory may be the observation that high smoothness forces low local complexity. The first applications were:

–

the proof of the Shub entropy conjecture for

self-maps of compact manifolds
[
42
]
;

–

]
.

The latter application can be deduced from the following result from

\lambda(f):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|Df^{n}\|_{\sup}\leq\log{\operatorname{Lip}}(f).

Here

\|\cdot\|_{x}

In particular,

This gives a direct proof (i.e., not using Pesin theory) of Newhouse’s Theorem

According to

Fundamental results of Yomdin theory

4.3.1.
Local image of a small curve

Yomdin theory shows that, for smooth dynamics, coverings by admissible reparametrizations and coverings by dynamical balls have comparable cardinalities. Thus we will be able to consider smooth curves and use the tangent dynamics in our entropy estimates.

–

–

Part III
Nonlinear estimates

Introduction

The third and final part of these lectures is where everything comes together to prove our main result, Theorem
D
. Recall that this theorem is an entropy bound for a sequence of measures in terms of the neutral decomposition of Chapter
2
.
The proof combines two types of information:

(1)

the nonexpanding property of the differential along the unstable direction within neutral orbit segments given by Theorem
2.5.1
;

(2)

the number of dynamical balls within the remaining, arbitrary orbit segments given by the topological entropy.

Converting the linear information (1) to an entropy bound is quite delicate. It is performed in Chapter
5
, the technical core of our approach. It is this argument which requires the consideration of the projective dynamics, the study of unstable curves, and the use of double scales.

The information (2) is just the formula for the topological entropy, adapted to the projective bundle, see Section
3.8
.

Chapter
6
concludes the proof of Theorem
D
by applying Ledrappier-Young entropy formula (Theorem
3.7.7
) to an appropriate unstable curve. The estimates are obtained by subdividing the orbits of typical points into long neutral orbit segments and the rest, using the relevant estimate (1) or (2) above for each orbit segment.

We stress that the various arguments mix dynamical balls and smooth reparametrizations. The translation between these two languages is provided by Yomdin theory (especially Corollary
4.3.2
).

Finally, Chapter
7
outlines the extra difficulties entailed by the proof of the stronger Theorem
B
of our original paper
[
6
]
and how they are solved.

5.2.
Strategy of proof

By Definition
5.1.1
, the iterates of a neutral tangent vector
\widehat{x}\in\widehat{M}
stay small throughout the whole of the neutral block (for instance the iterates first become very small then grow back to unit size). The same will be true of the length of the iterates of a small unstable curve
as long as the whole curve stays small and approximately straight
. This will give the required bound on the diameter of the iterates of the curve
\sigma
on the surface
M
.

However, this argument is incomplete: as we noted, it requires that the iterates of the curve stay approximately straight. We would like to see that
\sigma
having a small length implies that the diameter of
\widehat{\sigma}
does not become large. However, this implication does not hold in general, but because the iterates of
\sigma
are unstable curves, we can make the following argument.

Since the map is not uniformly hyperbolic, it is not true that an unstable curve with small length is always approximately straight. However we are considering a measure
\nu_{k}
which is weakly close to some limit measure
\mu
. This limit measure can be decomposed into a zero entropy part and a hyperbolic part in the sense of Pesin. By Lusin theorem the hyperbolic part will contain a uniform subset with large measure on which small length will imply small diameter in
\widehat{M}
.
By the general theory, zero entropy part will be controlled by a small number of dynamical balls.

This leads to using two scales
0<\epsilon\ll\widehat{\epsilon}
; their separation comes from the (arbitrarily bad) modulus of continuity of the Oseledets spaces produced by Lusin theorem. Here
\widehat{\epsilon}>0
just needs to be small given the map
Df
, whereas
\epsilon>0
is chosen so small that for some large

, with high probability with respect to the hyperbolic part of
\mu

d(x,y)<\epsilon\implies d(E^{+}_{f^{N}x},E^{+}_{f^{N}x})<\widehat{\epsilon}.

Finally, the necessity to mix the control by dynamical balls (for the zero entropy part) and with the control by the tangent dynamics (for the hyperbolic part) will require Yomdin theory and high smoothness.

We implement this strategy as follows:

–

we split the limiting measure
\widehat{\mu}\in\mathbb{P}(\widehat{f})
into a hyperbolic and a non-hyperbolic part;

–

we prove by induction that, as long as the diameter of the lifted curve
\widehat{f}^{k}(\widehat{\sigma})
stays small, the length of its trace on
M
is given by the derivative at any point (Lemma
5.4.1
);

–

we show that near the hyperbolic part, a very small diameter of
\sigma
on the surface
M
implies that the
\widehat{M}
-diameter of the image
\widehat{f}(\widehat{\sigma})
stays small (Lemma
5.5.1
);

–

we observe that the non-hyperbolic part of
\widehat{\mu}
has only zero Lyapunov exponents and therefore zero entropy. It follows that the corresponding orbit segments can be covered by a small number of dynamical balls (Lemma
5.6.1
);

–

we decompose the orbit into segments controlled by (1) the hyperbolic part, (2) the zero entropy part, (3) the rest;

–

the number of such decomposition is not too large (Lemma
5.7.1
) and therefore one can deal with typical orbits having a given decomposition;

–

we split by induction a parametrization of a unstable curve, restricting to typical points and keeping the
C^{r}

x\mapsto(x,E^{-}_{x})
. By Lusin theorem, the functions

,
*=\pm
and
1\leq k\leq N
, are all continuous on some compact set

with
\mu_{\#}(K_{\#})>1-\gamma^{2}
. Let

\widehat{K}_{+}:=\{(x,E^{+}_{x}):x\in K_{\#}\},\;\widehat{K}_{-}:=\{(x,E^{-}_{x}):x\in K_{\#}\},\text{ and }\widehat{K}_{\pm}:=\widehat{K}_{-}\sqcup\widehat{K}_{+}.

These are compact subsets of

. By Proposition

,
\widehat{\mu}_{\#}(\widehat{K}_{\pm})=1
.

5.5.3.
First
N
iterates starting near
\widehat{K}_{\pm}

The set
\widehat{K}_{\pm}
is the disjoint union of the graphs of two continuous functions. Hence, near
\widehat{K}_{\pm}
, a tight control for the distance on the surface (up to a very small
\epsilon

\widehat{\epsilon}
), as stated in the lemma below:

Lemma 5.5.1
.

Given
N\geq 1
,
\delta>0
, let
K_{\#},\widehat{K}_{+},\widehat{K}_{-}
be as above. Set

\widehat{\epsilon}:=\frac{1}{10}\min(\delta,\epsilon_{Y},\epsilon_{*},d(\widehat{K}_{+},\widehat{K}_{-}))>0,

where
\epsilon_{Y}
and
\epsilon_{*}
are given by Corollary
4.3.2
and Lemma
5.4.1
.

Then, there is
0<\epsilon(N,\widehat{\epsilon},K_{\#})<\delta
such that, for any small enough open neighborhood
\widehat{W}_{\#}
of
\widehat{K}_{\pm}
with
\widehat{\mu}(\partial W_{\#})=0
, we have,

\forall 0\leq k\leq N\quad{\operatorname{diam}}(\widehat{f}^{k}(\widehat{\sigma}\cap\widehat{W}_{\#}))<\widehat{\epsilon}

where
\sigma
is any regular curve
\sigma
with
C^{r}
size at most
(\epsilon,\widehat{\epsilon})
.

Proof.

By the continuity of
x\mapsto E^{\pm}_{x}
over

, there is
0<\epsilon<\widehat{\epsilon}
such that, for all

with

\widehat{x}^{+}:=(x,E^{+}_{x})
and
\widehat{x}^{-}:=(x,E^{-}_{x})
, one has

\forall 0\leq k\leq N\quad d(\widehat{f}^{k}(\widehat{x}^{+}),\widehat{f}^{k}(\widehat{y}^{+}))<\widehat{\epsilon}/2\text{ and }d(\widehat{f}^{k}(\widehat{x}^{-}),\widehat{f}^{k}(\widehat{y}^{-}))<\widehat{\epsilon}/2.

Since
\widehat{\epsilon}<d(\widehat{K}_{+},\widehat{K}_{-})
, for all
\widehat{x},\widehat{y}\in\widehat{K}_{\pm}
with

, it must be that

(\widehat{x}=\widehat{x}^{+}\text{ and }\widehat{y}=\widehat{y}^{+})\text{ or }(\widehat{x}=\widehat{x}^{-}\text{ and }\widehat{y}=\widehat{y}^{-}).

Hence:

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{x},\widehat{y}\in\widehat{K}_{\pm}\\

Using the compactness of
\widehat{M}
, we find

such that

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{x},\widehat{y}\in B(\widehat{K}_{\pm},\delta_{*})\\
d(\widehat{x},\widehat{y})\leq\widehat{\epsilon}\text{ and }d(x,y)\leq\epsilon\end{array}\right\}\implies\forall 0\leq k\leq N\quad f(\widehat{f}^{k}\widehat{x},\widehat{f}^{k}\widehat{y})<\widehat{\epsilon},

where

. We conclude by letting
\widehat{W}_{\#}:=B(\widehat{K}_{\pm},\delta_{**})
for some

such that
\widehat{\mu}(\partial B(\widehat{K},\delta_{**}))=0

∎

5.6.
Control of the first iterates near
\widehat{\mu}_{0}

Since all Lyapunov exponents vanish
\mu_{0}
-a.e., Ruelle-Margulis inequality implies that the entropy of
\mu_{0}
vanishes. Since
\widehat{\pi}:\widehat{M}\to M
preserves entropy, this is also the case for
\widehat{\mu}_{0}
. The following is folklore (but the proof is instructive):

Lemma 5.6.1
.

If
\widehat{\nu}\in\mathbb{P}(\widehat{f})
satisfies

, then for any
\overline{\gamma},\overline{\epsilon},\widehat{\epsilon}>0
and large enough integer

\widehat{M}_{0}
with
\widehat{\nu}(\widehat{M}_{0})>1-\overline{\gamma}
such that

\frac{1}{n}\log r_{f}(\widehat{M}_{0},\overline{\epsilon},\widehat{\epsilon}/2,n)<\frac{\eta}{10}.

Proof.

The above is obvious from Bowen’s formula if
\widehat{\nu}
is ergodic. The general case requires a bit of extra work.
Let
P
a finite partition of
\widehat{M}
into measurable sets, each contained in doubly scaled balls of size
(\overline{\epsilon}/2,\widehat{\epsilon}/4)
.

The Shannon-McMillan-Breiman theorem and more precisely its non-ergodic version (Theorem
3.4.2
), shows that, for
\widehat{\nu}
-a.e.
x\in\widehat{M}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\widehat{\nu}(P^{n}(x))=h(x)\;,

for some
\widehat{f}
-invariant, integrable nonnegative function such that
\int h\,d\mu=0
. Obviously
h=0
a.e., so this implies that there are a set
\widehat{M}_{0}\subset\widehat{M}
and an integer
N_{0}
such that
\nu(\widehat{M}_{0})>1-\overline{\gamma}
and for all
n\geq N_{0}
, the following holds:

\forall x\in\widehat{M}_{0}\quad\widehat{\nu}(P^{n}(x))>e^{-n\eta/10}.

Hence, the set of elements of
P^{n}
that intersect
\widehat{M}_{0}
has cardinality less than
e^{n\eta/10}
and joint measure at least that of
\widehat{M}_{0}
. But each element in this set is contained in an
(\overline{\epsilon},\widehat{\epsilon}/2,n)
-dynamical ball, so the minimal number of balls covering
\widehat{M}_{0}
is less than
e^{n\eta/10}
.
∎

This lemma applied to
\widehat{\mu}_{0}
gives a large multiple integer
n
of
N
(in particular

) and a compact set
\widehat{K}_{0}
such that

\widehat{\mu}_{0}(\widehat{K}_{0})>1-\gamma^{2}\text{ and }\frac{1}{n}\log r_{\widehat{f}}(\widehat{K}_{0},n,\epsilon/3,\widehat{\epsilon}/3)<\frac{\eta}{10}.

Denote by
\widehat{C}_{0}
the cover implicit in the definition of
r_{\widehat{f}}(\widehat{K}_{0},n,\epsilon/3,\widehat{\epsilon}/3)
. Pick
\epsilon/3<\rho\leq\epsilon/2
and
\widehat{\epsilon}/3<\widehat{\rho}\leq\widehat{\epsilon}/2
such that,

\widehat{W}_{0}:=\bigcup_{\widehat{x}\in\widehat{C}_{0}}B_{\widehat{f}}(\widehat{x},n,\rho,\widehat{\rho})\text{ satisfies }\widehat{\mu}(\partial\widehat{W}_{0})=0.

We set
n_{*}:=n
and note, in conclusion, that the open set
\widehat{W}_{0}\subset\widehat{M}
satisfies:

(5.3)

\widehat{\mu}_{0}(\widehat{W}_{0})>1-\gamma^{2},\;\widehat{\mu}(\partial\widehat{W}_{0})=0,\;\text{ and }\frac{1}{n_{*}}\log r_{\widehat{f}}(\widehat{W}_{0},n_{*},\epsilon/2,\widehat{\epsilon}/2)<\frac{\eta}{10}.

5.7.1.
Partitionning orbits

For each tangent vector
\widehat{x}

\;[\!\!\![\;0,n\;[\!\!\![\;
into segments starting by a visit to

(so the derivative controls the expansion) or a visit to
\widehat{W}_{0}
(so the entropy vanishes). Recall that we are considering a

, that is,
(\widehat{x},\dots,\widehat{f}^{n-1}\widehat{x})
such that

(5.4)

\frac{1}{n}\#\{0\leq k<n:\widehat{f}^{k}\widehat{x}\in\widehat{U}_{0}\}>1-\gamma.

Lemma 5.7.1
.

Any good orbit segment with length

can be decomposed into disjoint orbit sub-segments of the following three kinds:

(1)

of length
N
with initial point in
\widehat{W}_{\#}

(2)

of length
n_{*}
with initial point in
\widehat{W}_{0}

(3)

of length

such that the orbit segments of kind (3) altogether contain at most

The
kind
of each sub-segment defined above will determine how it is controlled. Below we will define the
type
of a segment which will describe how the segment is split into subsegments and the kinds of each.

Proof.

We define inductively times
n_{0}=0<\dots<n_{\ell}=n
, setting
n_{0}:=0
and then

(1)

n_{i+1}:=n_{i}+N
if
\widehat{f}^{n_{i}}\widehat{x}\in\widehat{W}_{\#}
and
n_{i}+N\leq n

(2)

n_{i+1}:=n_{i}+n_{*}
if
\widehat{f}^{n_{i}}\widehat{x}\in\widehat{W}_{0}\setminus\widehat{W}_{\#}
and
n_{i}+n_{*}\leq n

(3)

n_{i+1}:=n_{i}+1
otherwise.

Note that this defines a decomposition into the three announced kinds.
We stop when
i+1=\ell
.
Case (3) only occurs outside
\widehat{U}_{0}:=\widehat{W}_{0}\cup\widehat{W}_{\#}
or within the integer interval
\;[\!\!\![\;n-n_{*},n\;[\!\!\![\;
. By (
5.4

\gamma\cdot n
times. Thus, case (3) occurs at most
\gamma\cdot n+n_{*}<2\gamma\cdot n
times.
∎

The finite sequence of splitting spots in Lemma
5.7.1

(n_{i})_{i=0}^{\ell}
(in the notations of the above proof) is called the
type of the orbit segment
. Since the three lengths

are pairwise distinct, the type uniquely defines which kind (1), (2), or (3) occur and at which positions.
We define

\Theta(n):=\{(n_{i})_{i=0}^{\ell}:\forall 0\leq i<\ell\;n_{i+1}-n_{i}\in\{N,n_{*},1\},\;n_{0}=0,\text{ and }n_{\ell}=n\}.

Since the sub-segments
\;[\!\!\![\;n_{i},n_{i+1}\;[\!\!\![\;

Lemma 5.7.2

There is some

such that, for all integers

, the number of possible types of good orbit segments of length

is bounded by
\exp(H(4\gamma)n)

The curious reader may take a look to the proof of the similar Lemma

5.7.2.
Inductive scheme

Combining the lower bounds

Case

Additional steps for Theorem B

In this short chapter, we indicate how to strengthen Theorem

7.1.
The goal

Both these theorems yields a decomposition

\mu=(1-\beta)\mu_{0}+\beta\cdot\mu_{1}\qquad(0<\beta\leq 1,\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{P}(f))

We want to strengthen the entropy bound

\limsup_{k\to\infty}h(f,\nu_{k})\leq\beta\cdot h_{\operatorname{top}}(f)

into

\limsup_{k\to\infty}h(f,\nu_{k})\leq\beta\cdot h(f,\mu_{1}).

There are two stages to obtain this.

7.2.
Bounding by the entropy of an ergodic measure

In the first stage, let us assume that the measure
\mu_{1}
is ergodic (for instance if
\mu
itself is). In this rather simple case, it is enough to use Katok’s entropy formula: for all large
n\geq 1
, there is a union of at most
\exp(h(f,\mu_{1})n)
dynamical balls with
\mu_{1}
-measure arbitrarily close to
1
. Since these balls are open sets, this is still true for the measures
(1-1_{\mathfrak{N}(\alpha,L)})\cdot\nu_{k}
for
k
large.
Note here that it is necessary to first fix the iterate
n
before deciding how large
k
must be. Hence one has to split long non-neutral blocks into a concatenation of blocks with that fixed length
n
. One can adapt in this way the argument explained in the previous chapter as Proposition
6.4.1
.

7.3.
Bounding by the entropy of an arbitrary measure

In the second stage, we remove the artificial assumption that the measure
\mu_{1}
is ergodic (there is no reason why it should be!). Katok’s entropy formula may then fail. It is replaced by the following: for all large
n\geq 1
, there is a union of at most
\exp(\overline{h}(f,\mu_{1})n)
dynamical balls with
\mu_{1}
-measure arbitrarily close to
1
.

The new quantity that appears here:
\overline{h}(f,\mu_{1})
, is the
essential supremum
of the entropies of the ergodic components of
\mu_{1}
. This is unavoidable. In fact, this quantity is what Katok’s entropy formula yields when applied to a non-ergodic measure. Of course, it is usually much larger than the usual entropy, which is the
average
of these entropies.

To solve this problem, we split the ergodic decomposition
\mu=\int\mu_{\xi}\,d\xi
according to the value of
h(f,\mu_{\xi})
. Thus,

\mu=a_{1}\mu_{1}+\dots+a_{N}\mu_{N}\text{ with }0\leq\overline{h}(f,\mu_{n})-h(f,\mu_{n})\leq\eta.

The weak convergence
\nu_{k}\to\mu
implies that
\nu_{k}
-typical orbits can be split into intervals, each of which is close to being
\mu_{n}
-typical for some
1\leq n\leq N
. Moreover the fraction of the time spent close to
\mu_{n}
is close to
a_{n}
(in particular, the leftover is a small fraction).

One can apply Katok’s formula for each
\mu_{n}
in order to control the corresponding intervals. Since the measures
\mu_{n}
are invariant, the previous intervals are very long and therefore do not create significant entropy.

Chapter 8
Some further results and questions

In these notes we have tried to present the main ideas of
[
6
]
in a way that avoids as many difficulties as possible, but still capturing the main ideas and perhaps the most important case, when the entropies of the measures converge to the maximum, i.e., to the topological entropy.

The interested reader is invited to read the original paper
[
6
]
to understand the additional twists involved in other cases, already when the limiting measure is ergodic, and finally when it is not, as we believe that the added difficulties are somewhat transverse to the ideas presented here.

We would like to draw the reader’s attention to the related but different approach of Burguet
[
5
]
. Instead of focusing on neutral orbit segment, Burguet defines “hyperbolic” orbit segments. He obtains a similar but different decomposition with more control. In subsequent works he has been able to obtain precise, often sharp, results in finite smoothness, at least near the measures of maximal entropy.

In our opinion, it is an interesting open problem to understand if there are other relevant variants of the neutral decomposition, especially decomposition that would satisfy additional properties, such as invariance under time reversal. Not only would this potentially improve the entropy bound, but it might open the door to attacking the problem of finding ergodic measures that maximize the Hausdorff dimension, for which our approach only gives partial results.

Several natural extensions of
[
6
]
have been already obtained by now:

•

Hengyi Li has dealt with the situation where critical points are allowed, generalizing our results in the one-dimensional case (see
[
27
]
for non-flat critical points; further results are being written in collaboration with Alexandre Delplanque
[
14
]
). This later work relies on the Burguet’s decomposition and reparametrization lemma mentioned above.

•

As noticed by Yuntao Zang
[
45
]
and Liu Chuyi and Dawei Yang in
[
28
]
, one can use Burguet’s approach as long as the entropy is still be given by curves: for three dimensional flows or diffeomorphisms by using some symmetries.

•

Karina Marin, Mauricio Poletti, and Filiphe Veiga
[
30
]
have considered certain skew products which can be considered as “random composition of surface diffeomorphisms”.

To finish, we wish to point out how much remains to be done, especially in settings where the entropy can no longer be computed from the dynamics on curves.
