Phase transition of Schott’s statistic for high-dimensional heavy-tailed data
Hantao Chen, Guangming Pan, Cheng Wang

Abstract
Consider Schott’s statistic ( Schott 2005 ) defined as the squared Frobenius norm of the sample correlation matrix for data from α \alpha -regularly varying populations. We investigate its asymptotic distribution in a general framework characterized by data dimension p, sample size n, and regularly varying coefficients α \alpha . In particular, we identify a phase transition phenomenon in the asymptotic behavior. For light-tailed populations ( α > 3 \alpha>3 ), we revisit the α \alpha -free asymptotic distribution but relax the constraint on the ratio of p / n p/n . For heavy-tailed populations ( α < 3 \alpha<3 ), we derive a new asymptotic normal distribution whose variance explicitly depends on α \alpha . We also propose a consistent estimator for the asymptotic variance such that the standardized Schott’s test statistic remains applicable for unknown location parameters and all α > 0 \alpha>0 .

1
Introduction

Suppose that the observations
{\bf Z}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Z}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) from a
p
-dimensional population
{\bf Z}=(Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{p})
. Denote the data matrix by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\bf Z}_{1}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
{\bf Z}_{n}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}Z_{11}&\cdots&Z_{1p}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
Z_{n1}&\cdots&Z_{np}\end{pmatrix}=\left({\bf X}_{1},\cdots,{\bf X}_{p}\right).

(1)

Consider the hypotheses testing problem

\displaystyle H_{0}:\mbox{The covariance matrix},\mbox{cov}({\bf Z}),~\mbox{is diagonal}.

Equivalently, the population correlation matrix is an identity matrix, i.e.

\displaystyle H_{0}:\mbox{cor}({\bf Z})={\bf I}_{p}.

Define the sample covariance matrix

\displaystyle{\bf S}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf Z}_{i}{\bf Z}_{i}^{\top},

and the corresponding sample correlation matrix

\displaystyle{\bf R}=

\displaystyle\mbox{diag}({\bf S})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf S}\mbox{diag}({\bf S})^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where

\displaystyle\mbox{diag}({\bf S})=\frac{1}{n}\mbox{diag}\left(\|{\bf X}_{1}\|^{2},\cdots,\|{\bf X}_{p}\|^{2}\right).

Schott 2005
proposed a statistic (Schott’s statistic) for the above testing problem

\displaystyle T=\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2}).

(2)

Note that
\mbox{tr}({\bf R})=p
. Schott’s statistic eventually evaluates the squared Frobenius norm of the difference between the sample correlation matrix
{\bf R}
and the identity matrix
{\bf I}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle T=\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})-2\mbox{tr}({\bf R})+2p=\|{\bf R}-{\bf I}_{p}\|_{F}^{2}+p.

Schott 2005
obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 1 of
Schott 2005
)
.

Suppose
{\bf Z}\sim N(\bf{0},{\bf I}_{p})
and
p/n\to y\in(0,\infty)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2p}\left(\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})-p-\frac{p(p-1)}{n}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1).

(3)

In random matrix theory, studying the limiting behavior of the linear spectral statistics (including
\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})
and
\log|{\bf R}|
) of sample correlation matrices has been a central topic. Specifically, assuming
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{p}
are i.i.d. random variables,
Gao et al. 2017
show the asymptotic normality (
3
) still holds whenever
\mathbb{E}(Z_{ij}^{4})<\infty
. See also
Mestre and Vallet 2017
and
Zheng et al. 2019
.

Since the sample correlation matrix is self-normalized, it is interesting to study the robustness of its linear spectral statistics to heavy-tailed populations.
Heiny and Parolya 2024
firstly observed a surprising fact that unlike the CLT for
\log|{\bf S}|
, the CLT for
\log|{\bf R}|
holds even for populations with infinite fourth moment. Recently,
Li et al. 2024
proposed a necessary and sufficient condition

\displaystyle x^{3}P(|X_{1}|>x)\to 0,\quad x\to\infty,

(4)

under which the asymptotic distribution of linear spectral statistics is still normal. By the main result of
Li et al. 2024
, the CLT (
3
) for
\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})
holds under the condition (
4
) and p and n are in the same order. But it is an open question whether there is CLT and how it is dependent on
\alpha
when
\alpha
is less then three.

Note that nonparametric rank statistics such as
Kendall’s tau and Spearman’s rho perform well for continuous heavy-tailed distributions. They become much more involved when handling ties which are inherent in discrete data.
To the best of our knowledge, no theoretical results exist for high-dimensional
nonparametric statistics under discrete heavy-tailed distributions.

In view of the above, this work is to establish the CLT for Schott’s statistics for general
\alpha
-regularly varying populations. It turns out that CLT of
\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})
holds for general
\alpha>0
.
Roughly speaking, when
\alpha>3
, the asymptotic distribution is
\alpha
-free and only depends
n
and
p
. When
\alpha<3
, a phase transition happens where the leading term of the variance depends on
\alpha
. Therefore, we find a new renormalization constant dependent on
\alpha
to ensure the validity of central limit theorem when
\alpha<3
. Our proof does not rely on the framework of random matrix theory such that
p
and
n
are not required to be comparable magnitude. In other words, we allow
p
to be much larger than
n
once
\alpha
is larger enough.

The overall proof strategy is as follows. Rewrite the sample correlation matrix as

\displaystyle{\bf R}=\begin{pmatrix}\|{\bf X}_{1}\|^{-1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\|{\bf X}_{p}\|^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\left({\bf Z}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Z}_{n}\right)\begin{pmatrix}{\bf Z}_{1}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
{\bf Z}_{n}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\|{\bf X}_{1}\|^{-1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\|{\bf X}_{p}\|^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Consider the Gram matrix of
{\bf R}
. By (
1
) we have

\displaystyle{\bf B}=\left({\bf X}_{1},\cdots,{\bf X}_{p}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\|{\bf X}_{1}\|^{2}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\|{\bf X}_{p}\|^{2}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
{\bf X}_{p}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{{\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}}{\|{\bf X}_{i}\|^{2}}.

This is the so-called spatial-sign covariance matrix proposed by
Locantore et al. 1999
. A key observation is that
\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})=\mbox{tr}({\bf B}^{2})
so that the CLT for
\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})
in (
3
) can be established by studying the linear spectral statistics of
{\bf B}
. When
\alpha<1
, the mean of the underlying random variables does not exist so that it is challenging to investigate the Schott statistic for non-symmetric heavy tailed distributions. Hence we turn to centralized Schott’s statistic for general heavy tailed distributions. We use an important fact that the magnitudes of the maximum of i.i.d heavy tailed random variables and the sum of i.i.d heavy tailed random variables share the same order. We also make use of the exchangeability of self-normalized random variables and a key fact that the sum of the centralized self-normalized random variables is equal to zero.

To our best knowledge, our work is the first one which systematically derives the asymptotic normality of
\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})
for arbitrary
\alpha>0
. We also relax the constraint on the ratio of the data dimension
p
and sample size
n
. In particular, the test statistics are applicable to any dimension
p
if the underlying population has enough moments (e.g.,
\alpha>5
) and the sample size
n
tends to infinity. An important implication of our main results is that Scott’s statistic after standardization still works well for the heavy tailed distributions unlike nonparametric rank statistics such as Kendall’s tau and Spearman’s rho perform well for continuous heavy-tailed distributions.
We summarize our contributions below:

1.

We obtain the asymptotic normality for symmetric distributions and all
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})-p-p(p-1)/n}{\sqrt{V_{n}(\alpha)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1),

where the explicitly definition of
V_{n}(\alpha)
is given in (
8
) below.
There exists a phase transition for the leading term of
V_{n}(\alpha)
as
\alpha
changes. When condition (
4
) holds, we recover the result of
Li et al. 2024
, e.g.,

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2p}\left(\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})-p-\frac{p(p-1)}{n}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1).

and when
\alpha<3
,
V_{n}(\alpha)
depends on
\alpha
. Particularly, for
\alpha<2
, we have a neat new CLT as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2n}}{(2-\alpha)p}\left(\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})-p-\frac{p(p-1)}{n}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1),~0<\alpha<2.

2.

To ensure the asymptotic normality the conditions about
n,p,\alpha
are imposed as follows

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{n}{p^{2}}\to 0,&\alpha<2,\\
\frac{n^{\alpha-1+\epsilon}}{p^{2}}\to 0,&2\leq\alpha\leq 3,\\
\frac{n^{5-\alpha+\epsilon}}{p^{2}}\to 0,&3<\alpha\leq 5,\\
n\to\infty,&\alpha>5,\end{cases}

for some
\epsilon>0
. Generally speaking, the greater the value of
\alpha
, the less restriction on
p
. In particular, when
\alpha>5
, we only need
n\to\infty
which means the Schott’s test is valid for all different dimensions
p
.

3.

To accommodate unknown location parameters, we consider the centralized Pearson’s sample correlation matrix
\widetilde{{\bf R}}
and obtain the following CLT for general distributions and all
\alpha>0
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}^{2})-p-p(p-1)/(n-1)}{\sqrt{V_{n}(\alpha)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1).

This extends the existing CLTs to allow for unknown means and to the best of our knowledge, this is the first such a result valid for all regularly varying indices
\alpha>0
.

The following notations are used in the subsequent exposition. For a vector
{\bf x}
,
\|{\bf x}\|
is the Frobenius norm. For two sequence of real positive numbers
\{a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{n}\}
, write
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
if
a_{n}/b_{n}\to 1
. Let
C,C_{1},\ldots
be a sequence of generic constants which may take different values at various places.

2
Main results

Assumption
.

Let
\{X_{ij},i=1,\ldots,p,j=1,\ldots,n\}
be an array of i.i.d. random variables from the population
X
. We assume that
X
is regularly varying with index
\alpha>0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle P(|X|>x)\sim x^{-\alpha}l(x),\quad x\to\infty,

where
l
is a slowly varying function, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{l(\lambda x)}{l(x)}\to 1,\quad x\to\infty,\quad\forall\lambda>0.

We further define another slowly varying function

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}l(t)/tdt,

(5)

where
l(x)/\widetilde{l}(x)\to 0
and
a_{n}
is a positive sequence satisfying
2na_{n}^{-1}\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{a_{n}})\to 1
. More details can be found in
Bingham, Goldie and Teugels 1989
.

Write

\displaystyle{\bf X}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}X_{i1}\\
\vdots\\
X_{in}\end{pmatrix},\quad i=1,\ldots,p.

The Schott statistic can be rewritten as

\displaystyle T=\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\frac{\left({\bf X}_{i}^{\top}{\bf X}_{j}\right)^{2}}{\|{\bf X}_{i}\|^{2}\|{\bf X}_{j}\|^{2}}=p+\sum_{i\not=j}\frac{\left({\bf X}_{i}^{\top}{\bf X}_{j}\right)^{2}}{\|{\bf X}_{i}\|^{2}\|{\bf X}_{j}\|^{2}}.

We centralize the sample vectors for general distributions with unknown means, yielding the form of Schott’s statistic

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}=p+\sum_{i\not=j}\frac{\left(({\bf X}_{i}-\bar{X}_{i}\mathbf{1}_{n})^{\top}({\bf X}_{j}-\bar{X}_{j}\mathbf{1}_{n})\right)^{2}}{\|{\bf X}_{i}-\bar{X}_{i}\mathbf{1}_{n}\|^{2}\|{\bf X}_{j}-\bar{X}_{j}\mathbf{1}_{n}\|^{2}},

where

\displaystyle\bar{X}_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{ij},\quad\mathbf{1}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

2.1
Symmetric distribution case

This part is to consider the case when
X
is symmetrically distributed, i.e.,

\displaystyle P(X>x)=P(X<-x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

Define the self-normalized variables

\displaystyle{\bf Y}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}Y_{i1}\\
\vdots\\
Y_{in}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{{\bf X}_{i}}{\|{\bf X}_{i}\|},i=1,\ldots,p.

The Schott’s statistic is

\displaystyle T=\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\left({\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{j}\right)^{2}=p+\sum_{i\not=j}\left({\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{j}\right)^{2}.

Note that the expectation of odd powers of the self-normalized variables is zero in this case. As a consequence, the expectation and variance of Schott’s statistics can be derived explicitly.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
X
is a regularly varying variable with index
\alpha>0
and
X\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}}\,-X
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(T)=

\displaystyle p+\frac{p(p-1)}{n},

(6)

\displaystyle\mbox{var}(T)=

\displaystyle 2p(p-1)\left[\frac{n(n+2)}{n-1}\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{11}^{4}\right)^{2}-\frac{6}{n-1}\mathbb{E}Y_{11}^{4}+\frac{2n+1}{n^{2}(n-1)}\right].

(7)

As can be seen, the expectation remains independent of
\alpha
, while the variance is determined by
\mathbb{E}Y_{11}^{4}
, which is influenced by
\alpha
. To be more precise, by Proposition
A.1
below, we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Y_{11}^{4}\sim\begin{cases}(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})n^{-1},&\alpha<2,\\
\frac{1}{k-1}l(\sqrt{a_{n}})a_{n}^{-1},&\alpha=2,~\mathbb{E}X^{2}=\infty,\\
\frac{\alpha\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}{2\Gamma(k)}l(\sqrt{n})n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},&2\leq\alpha<4,~\mathbb{E}X^{2}<\infty,\\
2k\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{n})n^{-2},&\alpha=4,~\mathbb{E}X^{4}=\infty,\\
\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{4}n^{-2},&\alpha\geq 4,~\mathbb{E}X^{4}<\infty,\end{cases}

where
\Gamma()
is the Gamma function and
\tilde{l}()
is the slowly varying function defined in (
5
). As shown in (
7
), the limiting behavior of variance
\mbox{var}(T)
depends on the scale of
n\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{11}^{4}\right)^{2}
and a phase transition occurs when
\alpha=3
. When
\alpha>3
, only the
2/n^{2}
term matters, whereas for
\alpha\leq 3
, the
n\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{11}^{4}\right)^{2}
term contributes, and its effect varies dependent on the value of
\alpha
. The leading terms of
\mbox{var}(T)
are summarized as follows

\displaystyle V_{n}(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}2(1-\alpha/2)^{2}p^{2}/n,&\alpha<2,\\
l^{2}(\sqrt{a_{n}})p^{2}/(2n\widetilde{l}^{2}(\sqrt{a_{n}})),&\alpha=2,\\
\alpha^{2}\Gamma^{2}(2-\alpha/2)\Gamma^{2}(\alpha/2)l^{2}(\sqrt{n})/\left(2n^{\alpha-1}\right),&2<\alpha<3,\\
4p^{2}/n^{2}+9\pi^{2}l^{2}(\sqrt{a_{n}})p^{2}/(8n^{2}),&\alpha=3,\\
4p^{2}/n^{2},&\alpha>3.\end{cases}

(8)

We next present the CLT of Schott’s statistic when
X
is symmetrically distributed.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
X
is a regularly varying variable with index
\alpha>0
,
X\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}}\,-X
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{n}{p^{2}}\to 0,&0<\alpha<2,\\
\frac{n^{\alpha-1}}{p^{2}}\frac{\tilde{l}^{2}(\sqrt{a_{n}})}{l^{2}(\sqrt{a_{n}})}\to 0,&\alpha=2,~\mathbb{E}X^{2}=\infty,\\
\frac{n^{\alpha-1}}{p^{2}}\frac{1}{l^{2}(\sqrt{n})}\to 0,&2\leq\alpha<3,~\mathbb{E}X^{2}<\infty,\\
\frac{n}{p}\frac{l(\sqrt{n})}{l^{2}(\sqrt{n})+2}\to 0,&\alpha=3,\\
\frac{n^{5-\alpha}l^{2}(\sqrt{n})}{p^{2}}\to 0,&3<\alpha\leq 5,\\
n\to\infty,&\alpha>5.\end{cases}

(9)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})-p-p(p-1)/n}{\sqrt{V_{n}(\alpha)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1).

A phase transition occurs at
\alpha=3
. When
\alpha>3
or
\alpha=3
with
l(x)\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle V_{n}(\alpha)\sim 4p^{2}/n^{2},

such that

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2p}\left(\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})-p-\frac{p(p-1)}{n}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1).

Observe that
\alpha>3
and
\alpha=3
with
l(x)\to 0
imply that

\displaystyle x^{3}P(|X_{1}|>x)\to 0,\quad x\to\infty.

Our result is consistent with that in
Li et al. 2024
in this case. In particular,
Li et al. 2024
proved that the above condition is necessary and sufficient for general linear spectral statistics and the asymptotic CLT depends on the positive ratio
p/n
only. Here we relax the constraint on the ratio of
p/n
such that the ratio could either tend to zero or infinity.

When
\alpha<3
, the variance
V_{n}(\alpha)
is dominated by
2p^{2}n\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{11}^{4}\right)^{2}
and we need a new renormalization constant to ensure the validity of CLT. For
\alpha<3
, the variance
V_{n}(\alpha)
increases as
\alpha
decreases, indicating that heavier tails lead to larger fluctuations in the Schott’s statistic. For
2<\alpha<3
, the CLT is complex and for
\alpha<2
, we have a neat new CLT as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2n}}{(2-\alpha)p}\left(\mbox{tr}({\bf R}^{2})-p-\frac{p(p-1)}{n}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1),~0<\alpha<2.

Remark 2.1

(Ratio condition)
.

To remove the slowly varying functions involved in (
9
), we can roughly impose the following ratio conditions between
p
and
n
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{n}{p^{2}}\to 0,&\alpha<2,\\
\frac{n^{\alpha-1+\epsilon}}{p^{2}}\to 0,&2\leq\alpha\leq 3,\\
\frac{n^{5-\alpha+\epsilon}}{p^{2}}\to 0,&3<\alpha\leq 5,\\
n\to\infty,&\alpha>5,\end{cases}

for some
\epsilon>0
. Generally speaking, the greater the value of
\alpha
, the weaker restriction on
p
. For example, when
\alpha>5
, we only need
n\to\infty
which means the Schott’s test is valid for any dimension
p
. If we view Schott’s statistics as an U-statistic of order two based on the sample correlation matrix, our results can be viewed as a kind of an extension of
Chen, Zhang and Zhong 2010
which studied the U-statistics based on sample covariance matrices.

2.2
General distribution cases for centralized data

This section is devoted to the CLT for Schott’s statistic for centralized data. We first define the centralized self-normalized variables as follows

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}=\frac{{\bf X}_{i}-\bar{X}_{i}\mathbf{1}_{n}}{\|{\bf X}_{i}-\bar{X}_{i}\mathbf{1}_{n}\|}=\frac{{\bf Y}_{i}-\bar{Y}_{i}\mathbf{1}_{n}}{\sqrt{1-n\bar{Y}_{i}^{2}}},\quad i=1,\ldots,p,

(10)

where

\displaystyle\bar{X}_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}{\bf X}_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{ik},\quad\bar{Y}_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{ik},

(11)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}_{ik}=\frac{X_{ki}-\bar{X}_{i}}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{ki}^{2}-n\bar{X}_{i}^{2}}}=\frac{Y_{ik}-\bar{Y}_{i}}{\sqrt{1-n\bar{Y}_{i}^{2}}}.

The centralized Pearson’s sample correlation matrix is defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\bf R}}=\left(\widetilde{r}_{ij}\right)_{p\times p},\quad\widetilde{r}_{ij}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{ki}X_{kj}-n\bar{X}_{i}\bar{X}_{j}}{\sqrt{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{ki}^{2}-n\bar{X}_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{kj}^{2}-n\bar{X}_{j}^{2}\right)}}.

In the form of self-normalized variables
\widetilde{r}_{ij}
can be also written as

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}_{ij}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{ik}Y_{jk}-n\bar{Y}_{i}\bar{Y}_{j}}{\sqrt{\left(1-n\bar{Y}_{i}^{2}\right)\left(1-n\bar{Y}_{j}^{2}\right)}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\widetilde{Y}_{ik}\widetilde{Y}_{jk}.

Thus the centralized Schott’s statistic is

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}=\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}^{2})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\left(\widetilde{r}_{ij}\right)^{2}=p+\sum_{i\not=j}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\widetilde{Y}_{ik}\widetilde{Y}_{jk}\right)^{2}=p+\sum_{i\not=j}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}^{\top}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{j}\right)^{2}.

Note that
\widetilde{Y}_{i1},\cdots,\widetilde{Y}_{in}
are identically distributed random variables and exchangeable. A key observation is that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\widetilde{Y}_{ik}=0,\quad\sum_{k=1}^{n}\widetilde{Y}_{ik}^{2}=1.

Hence we have the trivial moment results

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}=0,\quad\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{2}=\frac{1}{n},\quad\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{Y}_{1}\widetilde{Y}_{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{2}=-\frac{1}{n(n-1)},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{2}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{4},\quad\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{3}\widetilde{Y}_{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{4}.

Due to this elegant property we have for any
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}^{2})=p+\frac{p(p-1)}{n-1}.

We are now in a position to state CLT for the centralized data.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
X
is a regularly varying variable with index
\alpha>0
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{n}{p^{2}}\to 0,&0<\alpha<2,\\
\frac{n^{\alpha-1}}{p^{2}}\frac{\tilde{l}^{2}(\sqrt{a_{n}})}{l^{2}(\sqrt{a_{n}})}\to 0,&\alpha=2,~\mathbb{E}X^{2}=\infty,\\
\frac{n^{\alpha-1}}{p^{2}}\frac{1}{l^{2}(\sqrt{n})}\to 0,&2\leq\alpha<3,~\mathbb{E}X^{2}<\infty,\\
\frac{n}{p}\frac{l(\sqrt{n})}{l^{2}(\sqrt{n})+2}\to 0,&\alpha=3,\\
\frac{n^{5-\alpha}l^{2}(\sqrt{n})}{p^{2}}\to 0,&3<\alpha\leq 5,\\
n\to\infty,&\alpha>5.\end{cases}

(12)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}^{2})-p-p(p-1)/(n-1)}{\sqrt{V_{n}(\alpha)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1).

Compared with Theorem
2.1
, the only difference is the asymptotic mean and there we use
(n-1)
to replace
n
. This is another instance of the substitution principle
(
Zheng, Bai and Yao 2015
)
.

2.3
Consistent estimation for the asymptotic variance

We need to estimate the asymptotic variance
V_{n}(\alpha)
to implement the above CLT results in practice. However, it is challenging to estimate
V_{n}(\alpha)
directly since it depends on the regularly varying index
\alpha
and the slowly varying function
l(x)
, both of which are unknown in advance. In extreme value theory, estimating the regularly varying index
\alpha
has been extensively studied. For instance, two commonly used estimators are the Hill estimator
Hill 1975
and the Pickands estimator
(
Pickands III 1975
)
.

Instead of estimating the variance through estimating
\alpha
and
l(x)
we turn to directly construct an estimator for
\mathbb{E}Y_{11}^{4}
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{np}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{Y}_{ij}^{4}.

Plugging it into (
7
) yields

\displaystyle\widehat{V}=\frac{2}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{Y}_{ij}^{4}\right)^{2}+\frac{4p^{2}}{n^{2}}.

Theorem 2.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}^{2})-p-p(p-1)/(n-1)}{\sqrt{\widehat{V}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1).

3
Application

An important application of correlation matrices is to test mutual independence of a p-dimensional random vector
{\bf X}=\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{p}\right)^{\top}
,

\displaystyle H_{0}:X_{1},\cdots,X_{p}\text{ are independent}.

Based on the empirical correlation matrices such as Pearson, Spearman and Kendall’s correlation matrices, we can consider the test statistics based on the maximum norm or the Frobenius norm of the correlation matrices. In particular, Schott’s statistic is based on the Frobenius norm of Pearson’s correlation matrix. Existing studies on testing correlation structures can be categorized according to the choice of the norm and the type of the correlation measure. For the Frobenius norm, results for the sample correlation matrix are given by
Gao et al. 2017
;
Zheng et al. 2019
; those for Kendall’s tau are established in
Leung and Drton 2018
;
Li, Wang and Li 2021
; and the results for Spearman’s rho are derived in
Bao et al. 2015
;
Leung and Drton 2018
;
Chen and Wang 2025
. For the maximum norm, the sample correlation matrix is studied in
Zhou 2007
, while both Kendall’s tau and Spearman’s rho are investigated in
Han, Chen and Liu 2017
.

By Theorem
2.3
we can construct a distribution-free test statistic based on Pearson’s correlation matrix as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}^{2})-p-p(p-1)/(n-1)}{\sqrt{\widehat{V}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N(0,1),

which is valid for regularly varying index
\alpha>0
.

3.1

To examine the finite sample performance of our proposed statistics, we conduct null hypotheses from the following types of populations:

(1).

Student’s t-distribution:
X_{ij}
are i.i.d.
t(\alpha)
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq p
.

(2).

Pareto distribution:
X_{ij}
are i.i.d.
\mathrm{Pareto}(\alpha)
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq p
.

In details, Student’s t-distribution is a symmetric population and Pareto distribution is asymmetric. As for numerical experiments, we take different combinations of sample size
n
, data dimension
p
and regularly varying index
\alpha
. We adopt the data dimensionality settings from
Chen, Zhang and Zhong 2010
, where the feature dimension
p
satisfies
p=c\exp(n^{\eta})
. For sample sizes
n=20,40,60,80
, the corresponding feature dimensions are
p=35,62,91,124
for

for

.

3.2
Discrete heavy-tailed distribution

While nonparametric rank statistics such as Spearman’s
\rho
and Kendall’s
{\bf K}
perform well for continuous heavy-tailed distributions, they rely on the continuity assumption and thus fail to account for ties, which are inherent in discrete data. To the best of our knowledge, no theoretical results exist for high-dimensional nonparametric statistics under discrete heavy-tailed distributions. In contrast, our analysis of Schott’s statistic for the Pearson’s correlation matrix imposes no continuity assumptions and is therefore directly applicable to both continuous and discrete heavy-tailed distributions.

In this part, we consider Zipf distribution which is a discrete heavy-tailed distribution supported on positive integers.
For a tail index
\alpha>0
and truncation
N
, its probability mass function is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X=k)=\frac{1}{k^{\alpha+1}H_{N}(\alpha)},\qquad k=1,2,\dots,N,

where
H_{N}(\alpha)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}k^{-\alpha-1}
denotes the generalized harmonic number.
As
N\to\infty
and
\alpha>0
, its tail follows a power law

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X>x)\sim Cx^{-\alpha},\quad x\to\infty,

indicating regular variation with index
\alpha
.

Table
2
presents
the empirical sizes of the tests at a nominal significance level of
5\%
based on 1000 replications. For different values of the heavy-tailed index
\alpha
, we find that the empirical sizes are consistently close to
5\%
. This fully demonstrates the validity of our proposed statistic for discrete heavy-tailed variables.

A
Asymptotic results for self-normalized variables

For i.i.d. random variables
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
, we define the self-normalized variables
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{n}

\displaystyle Y_{i}=\frac{X_{i}}{\sqrt{X_{1}^{2}+\cdots,X_{n}^{2}}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

By the identity of Gamma function

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{\beta}}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\beta-1}e^{-sx}ds,

and Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots Y_{r}^{k_{r}}\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\frac{k}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}X_{i}^{k_{i}}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}X_{1}e^{-sX_{1}^{2}}\right)^{n-r}ds,

where
k=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}
. Asymptotic behavior concerning the moments of the self-normalized variables depends on the Laplace transformation
\varphi(s)=\mathbb{E}e^{-sX^{2}}
. See
Albrecher and Teugels 2007
for more details.

We first collect two important lemmas about the Laplace transformation of heavy-tailed distributions and then present the main results of self-normalized variables.

Lemma A.1

(Corollary 8.1.7, Theorem 8.8.1 of
Bingham, Goldie and Teugels 1989
and Page 7 of
Albrecher and Teugels 2007
)
.

Assume that
P(|X|>x)\sim x^{-\alpha}l(x)
where
l
is a slowly varying function. Let
\varphi(s)=\mathbb{E}e^{-sX^{2}}
, then

\displaystyle 1-\varphi(s)\sim\begin{cases}\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}l(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}),&\alpha<2,\\
2s\widetilde{l}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}),&\alpha=2,\mathbb{E}X^{2}=\infty,\\
\mathbb{E}X^{2}s,&\alpha\geq 2,\mathbb{E}X^{2}<\infty,\end{cases}\quad(s\to 0^{+})

and

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\varphi^{(k)}(s)\sim\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha}{2}\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-k}l(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}),&2k>\alpha,\\
2k\widetilde{l}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}),&2k=\alpha,\mathbb{E}X^{2k}=\infty,\\
\mathbb{E}X^{2k},&2k\leq\alpha,\mathbb{E}X^{2k}<\infty,\end{cases}\quad(s\to 0^{+})

where
\varphi^{(k)}(s)=\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\varphi(s)
. Here

\widetilde{l}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}l(t)/tdt
is itself a slowly varying function and
l(x)/\widetilde{l}(x)\to 0
.

Lemma A.2

(Pages 349 and 373 of
Bingham, Goldie and Teugels 1989
)
.

Let
F
be a distribution with positive support, and
\varphi
be its Laplace transformation. For any
\beta\in(0,1]
, if

\displaystyle 1-\varphi(s)\sim s^{\beta}l(\frac{1}{s}),\quad s\to 0^{+},

then, there exists a positive sequence
(a_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
a_{n}\to\infty
, such that

\displaystyle na_{n}^{-\beta}l(a_{n})\to 1.

Proposition A.1
.

Assume that
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots
are i.i.d. with
P(|X_{1}|>x)\sim x^{-\alpha}l(x)
where
l
is a slowly varying function. For
k\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{2k}\sim\begin{cases}\frac{\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(k)\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}n^{-1},&\alpha<2,\\
\frac{1}{k-1}\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{a_{n}})a_{n}^{-1},&\alpha=2,~\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2}=\infty,\\
\frac{\alpha\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}{2\Gamma(k)}l(\sqrt{n})n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},&2\leq\alpha<2k,~\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2}<\infty,\\
2k\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{n})n^{-k},&\alpha=2k,~\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}=\infty,\\
\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}n^{-k},&\alpha\geq 2k,~\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}<\infty,\end{cases}

where
a_{n}
is a positive sequence satisfying
2na_{n}^{-1}\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{a_{n}})\to 1
.

Proof.

Following
Albrecher and Teugels 2007
, the expectation can be represented as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{2k}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(s)\varphi^{n-1}(s)ds,

where
\varphi(s)=\mathbb{E}e^{-sX_{1}^{2}}
.

If
0<\alpha<2
, by Lemma
A.2
, we can choose
a_{n}\to\infty
such that

\displaystyle na_{n}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}l(\sqrt{a_{n}})\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})\to 1,

and by Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle\varphi^{n}(\frac{t}{a_{n}})=\exp\left\{n\log\varphi(\frac{t}{a_{n}})\right\}\sim\exp\left\{-n\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})\left(\frac{t}{a_{n}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}l(a_{n})\frac{l(\frac{a_{n}}{t})}{l(a_{n})}\right\}\sim\exp\left\{-t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right\}.

This, together with Lemma
A.1
, implies that

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(s)\varphi^{n-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{a_{n}}\right)^{k-1}\varphi^{\left(k\right)}\left(a^{-1}_{n}t\right)\varphi^{n-1}\left(a_{n}^{-1}t\right)\frac{1}{a_{n}}dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k-1}}{a_{n}^{k}}\left[\frac{\alpha}{2}\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})(\frac{t}{a_{n}})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-k}l(\frac{\sqrt{a_{n}}}{\sqrt{t}})\right]\varphi^{n}(\frac{t}{a_{n}})dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})}{2\Gamma(k)\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\cdot\frac{l(\sqrt{a_{n}})\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}{a_{n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\frac{l(\sqrt{a_{n}}/\sqrt{t})}{l(\sqrt{a_{n}})}\varphi^{n}(\frac{t}{a_{n}})dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})}{2\Gamma(k)\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\exp\left\{-t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right\}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})}{n\Gamma(k)\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}.

If
\alpha=2
and
\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2}=\infty
, by Lemma
A.1
and Lemma
A.2
, we can choose
a_{n}
such that

\displaystyle 2na_{n}^{-1}\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{a_{n}})\to 1,

and

\displaystyle\varphi^{n}(\frac{t}{a_{n}})=\exp\left\{n\log\varphi(\frac{t}{a_{n}})\right\}\sim\exp\left\{-2n\frac{t}{a_{n}}\widetilde{l}(a_{n})\frac{\widetilde{l}(\frac{a_{n}}{t})}{\widetilde{l}(a_{n})}\right\}\sim e^{-t}.

And by Lemma
A.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(s)\varphi^{n-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{t}{a_{n}})^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(\frac{t}{a_{n}})\varphi^{n-1}(\frac{t}{a_{n}})\frac{1}{a_{n}}dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k-1}}{a_{n}^{k}}\left[\frac{\Gamma(k-1)t^{1-k}l(\frac{\sqrt{a_{n}}}{\sqrt{t}})}{a_{n}^{1-k}}\right]e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{[l(\sqrt{a_{n}})]}{\Gamma(k)a_{n}}\Gamma(k-1)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{\Gamma(k)n}\cdot\frac{l(\sqrt{a_{n}})}{2\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{a_{n}})}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{a_{n}})}{(k-1)a_{n}}.

When
\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2}<\infty
and
\alpha<2k
, by Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(s)\varphi^{n-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{t}{n})^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(\frac{t}{n})\varphi^{n-1}(\frac{t}{n})\frac{1}{n}dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k-1}}{n^{k}}\left[\frac{\alpha}{2}\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})(\frac{t}{n})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-k}l(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{t}})\right]e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})l(\sqrt{n})}{2\Gamma(k)n^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}{2\Gamma(k)}n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}l(\sqrt{n}).

When
\alpha=2k
and
\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}=\infty
, by Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(s)\varphi^{n-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{t}{n})^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(\frac{t}{n})\varphi^{n-1}(\frac{t}{n})\frac{1}{n}dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k-1}}{n^{k}}\left[2k\widetilde{l}(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{t}})\right]e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{2k\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{n})}{\Gamma(k)n^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k-1}e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2kn^{-k}\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{n}).

When
\alpha\geq 2k
and
\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}<\infty
, by Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(s)\varphi^{n-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{t}{n})^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}(\frac{t}{n})\varphi^{n-1}(\frac{t}{n})\frac{1}{n}dt

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k-1}}{n^{k}}\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}n^{-k}.

Collecting all the terms, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{2k}\sim\begin{cases}\frac{\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(k)\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}n^{-1},&\alpha<2,\\
\frac{1}{k-1}\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{a_{n}})a_{n}^{-1},&\alpha=2,\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2}=\infty,\\
\frac{\alpha\Gamma(k-\frac{\alpha}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}{2\Gamma(k)}l(\sqrt{n})n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},&2\leq\alpha<2k,~\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2}<\infty\\
2k\widetilde{l}(\sqrt{n})n^{-k},&\alpha=2k,\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}=\infty,\\
\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}n^{-k},&\alpha\geq 2k,~\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{2k}<\infty.\end{cases}

∎

Proposition A.2
.

Assume that
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots
are i.i.d. regularly varying with
\alpha>1
and
\mathbb{E}X_{1}=0
. For positive numbers
k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}
, we denote
I_{j}=\{1\leq i\leq r:k_{i}=j\}
,
j=1,2,\cdots
.

•

If
1<\alpha\leq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots Y_{r}^{k_{r}}\right)=O(n^{-r}).

•

If
2<\alpha\leq 3
, for any
\beta<\alpha
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots Y_{r}^{k_{r}}\right)=O(n^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(r-|I_{2}|)-|I_{2}|}).

•

If
\alpha>3
, for any
\beta<\alpha
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots Y_{r}^{k_{r}}\right)=O(n^{-|I_{1}|-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\lceil\alpha\rceil-1}i|I_{i}|-\frac{\beta}{2}(r-\sum_{i=1}^{\lceil\alpha\rceil-1}|I_{i}|)}).

Proof.

For any
k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots Y_{r}^{k_{r}}\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\frac{k}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{k_{i}}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\varphi^{n-r}(s)ds.

Firstly, we consider the case
1<\alpha\leq 2
. When
k_{i}=1
, by the identity

\displaystyle sx^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}e^{-s\theta x^{2}}=1-e^{-sx^{2}},\quad\forall sx^{2}>0,

where
\theta
is uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
, we can get

\displaystyle X_{i}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}=X_{i}\left(1-sX_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}e^{-s\theta X_{i}^{2}}\right),

which yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)=\mathbb{E}X_{i}-s\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left(X_{i}^{3}e^{-s\theta X_{i}^{2}}\right)\right\}.

Thus, by Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|=

\displaystyle s\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{3}e^{-s\theta_{i}X_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|\leq s\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}\left(X_{i}^{3}e^{-s\theta_{i}X_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}\left(X_{i}^{2}e^{-s\theta_{i}X_{i}^{2}}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}\left(X_{i}^{4}e^{-s\theta_{i}X_{i}^{2}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle s\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\left((\theta_{i}s)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}l\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{i}s}}\right)\cdot(\theta_{i}s)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-2}l\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{i}s}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\left[\theta_{i}^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}}l\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{i}s}}\right)\right],

where
\theta_{i}
is uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
and independent of
X_{i}
.

Next when
k_{i}
is odd and
k_{i}\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{k_{i}}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{2}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{2k_{i}-2}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}l(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})\cdot s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-k_{i}+1}l(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=s^{\frac{\alpha-k_{i}}{2}}l(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}).

Combing the two pieces, by Lemma
A.2
, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots Y_{r}^{k_{r}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\prod_{i\in I_{1}}\left[s^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\left(\theta_{i}^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}}l(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{i}s}})\right)\right]\prod_{1\leq i\leq r;i\not\in I_{1}}\left[s^{\frac{\alpha-k_{i}}{2}}l(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})\right]\varphi^{n-r}(s)ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{i\in I_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\theta_{i}^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}}\right]\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}r-1}\left[l(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})\right]^{r}\varphi^{n-r}(s)ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{a_{n}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}r-1}\left[l(\sqrt{a_{n}})\right]^{r}\varphi^{n-r}(\frac{t}{a_{n}})\frac{1}{a_{n}}dt\lesssim n^{-r}.

Secondly, we consider the case
2<\alpha\leq 3
. For
i\in I_{1}
, we choose
\beta\in(2,\alpha)
arbitrarily. Since that
\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{\beta}<\infty
and

\displaystyle f(x)=x^{3-\beta}e^{-s\theta x^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{3-\beta}{2s\theta}\right)^{\frac{3-\beta}{2}}e^{-\frac{3-\beta}{2}},

(13)

we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|=

\displaystyle s\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{3}e^{-s\theta_{i}X^{2}}\right)\right|=s\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{i}|^{\beta}\cdot|X_{i}|^{3-\beta}e^{-s\theta_{i}X^{2}}\right)\lesssim\mathbb{E}\theta_{i}^{\frac{\beta-3}{2}}s^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\lesssim s^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}.

For
i\in I_{2}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{2}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{2}\lesssim 1.

For
i\in I_{3}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{k_{i}}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{i}|^{\beta}\cdot|X_{i}|^{k_{i}-\beta}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\lesssim s^{-\frac{k_{i}-\beta}{2}}.

So we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots Y_{r}^{k_{r}}\right)\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\cdot s^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}|I_{1}|}\prod_{i\in I_{3}}s^{-\frac{k_{i}-\beta}{2}}\varphi^{n-r}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{\beta}{2}(r-|I_{2}|)+|I_{2}|-1}\varphi^{n-r}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{t}{n})^{\frac{\beta}{2}(r-|I_{2}|)+|I_{2}|-1}\varphi^{n-r}(\frac{t}{n})\frac{1}{n}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle n^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(r-|I_{2}|)-|I_{2}|}.

Finally, we deal with the case
\alpha>3
. For convenience, we denote
H=\cup_{2\leq j\leq\lceil\alpha\rceil-1}I_{j}
and
\Lambda=\cup_{j\geq\lceil\alpha\rceil}I_{j}
. For
i\in I_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|=s\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{3}e^{-s\theta_{i}X^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim s\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{3}\lesssim s.

For
i\in H
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{k_{i}}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{k_{i}}\lesssim 1.

For
i\in\Lambda
, with similar process in (
13
),

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}^{k_{i}}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{i}|^{\beta}\cdot|X_{i}|^{k_{i}-\beta}e^{-sX_{i}^{2}}\right)\lesssim s^{-\frac{k_{i}-\beta}{2}}.

So we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots Y_{r}^{k_{r}}\right)\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\cdot s^{|I_{1}|}\prod_{i\in\Lambda}s^{-\frac{k_{i}-\beta}{2}}\varphi^{n-r}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{|I_{1}|+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in H}k_{i}+\frac{\beta}{2}|\Lambda|-1}\varphi^{n-r}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{t}{n})^{|I_{1}|+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in H}k_{i}+\frac{\beta}{2}|\Lambda|-1}\varphi^{n-r}(\frac{t}{n})\frac{1}{n}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle n^{-|I_{1}|-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in H}k_{i}-\frac{\beta}{2}|\Lambda|}.

Proposition B.1
.

Assuming
X\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}}\,-X
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}({\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{2})^{2}=\frac{1}{n},\quad\mbox{cov}\left(({\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{2})^{2},({\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{3})^{2}\right)=0,

\displaystyle\mbox{var}\left(({\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{2})^{2}\right)=\frac{n(n+2)}{n-1}\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}\right)^{2}-\frac{6}{n-1}\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}+\frac{2n+1}{n^{2}(n-1)}.

Noting

\displaystyle\mbox{cov}({\bf Y})=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\bf Y}{\bf Y}^{\top}=\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n},

(14)

we can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}({\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{2})^{2}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mbox{tr}({\bf Y}_{1}{\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{2}{\bf Y}_{2}^{\top})=\mbox{tr}\left(\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{n}.

For the covariance, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{cov}\left(({\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{2})^{2},({\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{3})^{2}\right)=\mbox{var}\left({\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}\left(\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}^{\top}\right){\bf Y}_{1}\right)=0,

\displaystyle\mbox{var}\left(\left({\bf Y}_{1}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{2}\right)^{2}\right)=\mbox{cov}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}Y_{1i}Y_{1j}Y_{2i}Y_{2j},\sum_{{i^{\prime}},{j^{\prime}}=1}^{n}Y_{1{i^{\prime}}}Y_{1{j^{\prime}}}Y_{2{i^{\prime}}}Y_{2{j^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\mbox{cov}\left(Y^{2}_{11}Y^{2}_{21},\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}Y_{1i}Y_{1j}Y_{2i}Y_{2j}\right)+n(n-1)\mbox{cov}\left(Y_{11}Y_{12}Y_{21}Y_{22},\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}Y_{1i}Y_{1j}Y_{2i}Y_{2j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\mbox{var}\left(Y^{2}_{11}Y^{2}_{21}\right)+n(n-1)\mbox{cov}\left(Y^{2}_{11}Y^{2}_{21},Y^{2}_{12}Y^{2}_{22}\right)+2n(n-1)\mbox{var}\left(Y_{11}Y_{12}Y_{21}Y_{22}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{n(n+2)}{n-1}\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}\right)^{2}-\frac{6}{n-1}\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}+\frac{2n+1}{n^{2}(n-1)},

where the lase equality follows from the fact that

\displaystyle 1=\mathbb{E}(Y_{1}^{2}+\cdots+Y_{n}^{2})^{2}=n\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}+n(n-1)\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}.

(15)

Proposition B.2
.

Assuming
X\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}}\,-X

{\bf B}=(b_{ij})_{n\times n}

\displaystyle\mbox{cov}\left({\bf Y}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf Y},{\bf Y}^{\top}{\bf B}{\bf Y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n(n+2)\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}-3}{n(n-1)}\mbox{tr}\left({\bf A}\circ{\bf B}\right)+\frac{1-n^{2}\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}}{n^{2}(n-1)}\mbox{tr}({\bf A})\mbox{tr}({\bf B})+\frac{2-2n\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}}{n(n-1)}\mbox{tr}({\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}).

A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\mbox{cov}\left({\bf Y}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf Y},{\bf Y}^{\top}{\bf B}{\bf Y}\right)=\mbox{cov}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}Y_{i}Y_{j},\sum_{{i^{\prime}},{j^{\prime}}=1}^{n}b_{{i^{\prime}{j^{\prime}}}}Y_{{i^{\prime}}}Y_{{j^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\mbox{cov}\left(\sum_{i}^{n}a_{ii}Y^{2}_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{jj}Y^{2}_{j}\right)+4\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}b_{ij}\mbox{var}(Y_{i}Y_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{var}(Y_{1}^{2})\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}b_{ii}+\mbox{cov}\left(Y_{1}^{2},Y_{2}^{2}\right)\sum_{i\neq j}a_{ii}b_{jj}+4\mbox{cov}(Y_{1}Y_{2})\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}b_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mbox{var}(Y_{1}^{2})-\mbox{cov}\left(Y_{1}^{2},Y_{2}^{2}\right)-2\mbox{cov}(Y_{1}Y_{2})\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}b_{ii}+\mbox{cov}\left(Y_{1}^{2},Y_{2}^{2}\right)\sum_{i,j}a_{ii}b_{jj}

\displaystyle+2\mbox{cov}(Y_{1}Y_{2})\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}b_{ji}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}-3\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}\right)\mbox{tr}\left({\bf A}\circ{\bf B}\right)+\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\mbox{tr}({\bf A})\mbox{tr}({\bf B})+2\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}\mbox{tr}({\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}).

This, together with (

), completes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
V_{n}=\mbox{var}(T)
. Define the martingale difference as follows

\displaystyle M_{n,k}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{V_{n}}}\left(\mathbb{E}[T|{\bf Y}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Y}_{k}]-\mathbb{E}[T|{\bf Y}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Y}_{k-1}]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{V_{n}}}\mbox{tr}\left(\sum_{i<k}{\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}+\frac{p-k}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right)\left({\bf Y}_{k}{\bf Y}_{k}^{\top}-\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{V_{n}}}\left(\sum_{i<k}\left({\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{k}\right)^{2}-\frac{k-1}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{V_{n}}}\sum_{i<k}{\bf Y}_{k}^{\top}\left({\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}-\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right){\bf Y}_{k},

where we use the facts that
{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{i}=1
, (
14
) and that

\displaystyle T=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\left({\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{j}\right)^{2}=\mbox{tr}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}{\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}{\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}\left(\sum_{i<k}{\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}+{\bf Y}_{k}{\bf Y}_{k}^{\top}+\sum_{i>k}{\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}\right)\left(\sum_{j<k}{\bf Y}_{j}{\bf Y}_{j}^{\top}+{\bf Y}_{k}{\bf Y}_{k}^{\top}+\sum_{j>k}{\bf Y}_{j}{\bf Y}_{j}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}\left(\sum_{i\neq k}{\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}\right)\left(\sum_{j\neq k}{\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}\right)+1+2\mbox{tr}\left(\sum_{i<k}{\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}+\sum_{i>k}{\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}\right)\left({\bf Y}_{k}{\bf Y}_{k}^{\top}\right).

To apply the martingale central limit theorem, we need to verify that

(i).

\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}M_{n,k}^{2}\to 1
.

(ii).

\mbox{var}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}[M_{n,k}^{2}|{\bf Y}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Y}_{k-1}]\right)\to 0
.

(iii).

\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}M_{n,k}^{4}\to 0
.

First, (i) is obvious since

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}M_{n,k}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mbox{var}\left(M_{n,k}\right)=\mbox{var}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}M_{n,k}\right)=\frac{\mbox{var}(T)}{V_{n}}=1.

For (ii), note that

\displaystyle M_{n,k}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{V_{n}}}{\bf Y}_{k}^{\top}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left({\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}-\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right)\right){\bf Y}_{k},

and that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[M_{n,k}|{\bf Y}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Y}_{k-1}]=\frac{2}{n\sqrt{V_{n}}}\mbox{tr}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left({\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}-\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right)\right)=0.

Proposition
B.2
implies that

\displaystyle\frac{V_{n}}{4}\mathbb{E}[M_{n,k}^{2}|{\bf Y}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Y}_{k-1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}-3\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}\right)\right)\sum_{i,j=1}^{k-1}\mbox{tr}\left(\left({\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}-\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right)\circ\left({\bf Y}_{j}{\bf Y}_{j}^{\top}-\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}\right)\sum_{i,j=1}^{k-1}\mbox{tr}\left(\left({\bf Y}_{i}{\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}-\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right)\left({\bf Y}_{j}{\bf Y}_{j}^{\top}-\frac{1}{n}{\bf I}_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}-3\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}\right)\right)\sum_{i,j=1}^{k-1}\left(\left\|{\bf Y}_{i}\circ{\bf Y}_{j}\right\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{1}{n}\right)+2\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}\right)\sum_{i,j=1}^{k-1}\left(\left({\bf Y}_{i}^{\top}{\bf Y}_{j}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}[M_{n,k}^{2}|{\bf Y}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Y}_{k-1}]=\frac{4\left(\mathbb{E}Y_{1}^{4}-3\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{1}^{2}Y_{2}^{2}\right)\right)}{V_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(p-i\right)\left(\left\|{\bf Y}_{i}\circ{\bf Y}_{i}\right\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{1}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}\circ\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}\right\|^{2}_{2},\quad\left\|\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}\circ\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}\right\|^{2}_{2},\quad\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}^{\top}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{j}\right)^{2},

Noting

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{n,k}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{V_{n}}}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}^{\top}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{k}^{\top}-\frac{1}{n-1}{\bf I}_{n}+\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\right)\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{V_{n}}}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}^{\top}{\bf A}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}

we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{n,k}^{4}=

\displaystyle\frac{16}{V_{n}^{2}}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}=1}^{k-1}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i_{1}}^{\top}{\bf A}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i_{1}}\right)\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i_{2}}^{\top}{\bf A}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i_{2}}\right)\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i_{3}}^{\top}{\bf A}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i_{3}}\right)\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i_{4}}^{\top}{\bf A}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i_{4}}\right).

Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}^{\top}{\bf A}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}\mid\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{k}\right)=\mbox{tr}\left(\frac{1}{n-1}{\bf I}_{n}-\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\right){\bf A}_{k}=0,~i=1,\cdots,k-1,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\widetilde{M}_{n,k}^{4}=\frac{16}{\widetilde{V}_{n}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}^{\top}{\bf A}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}\right)^{4}+\frac{96}{\widetilde{V}_{n}^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}^{\top}{\bf A}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{i}\right)^{2}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{j}^{\top}{\bf A}_{k}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{j}\right)^{2}

For the first term, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}^{\top}{\bf A}_{2}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}\right)^{4}=\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}^{\top}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{4}\leq 8\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}^{\top}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{2}\right)^{8}+\frac{1}{n^{4}}\right).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}^{\top}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{2}\right)^{8}=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{8}=1}^{n}\widetilde{Y}_{1i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{Y}_{1i_{8}}\widetilde{Y}_{2i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{Y}_{2i_{8}}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{|\{i_{1}\}\cup\cdots\cup\{i_{8}\}|=1}+\sum_{|\{i_{1}\}\cup\cdots\cup\{i_{8}\}|=2}+\sum_{|\{i_{1}\}\cup\cdots\cup\{i_{8}\}|=3}+\sum_{|\{i_{1}\}\cup\cdots\cup\{i_{8}\}|\geq 4}\right)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{Y}_{i_{8}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{8}\right)^{2}+n(n-1)\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=8,k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1}\frac{8!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!}\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{k_{1}}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{k_{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+n(n-1)(n-2)\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=8,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\geq 1}\frac{8!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{k_{1}}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{k_{2}}\widetilde{Y}_{3}^{k_{3}}\right)^{2}+O(n^{-4}),

where the last step is due to the fact that

by Proposition
C.2
. For the moments with odd powers, we can bound them by the moments with even powers. For example,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{7}\widetilde{Y}_{2}=-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{8},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{5}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{3}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{4}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{2}\left(\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{2}+\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{4}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{6}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{2}.

For

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{k+1}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{7-k}+\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{k}\widetilde{Y}^{8-k}_{2}+(n-1)\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{k}\widetilde{Y}^{7-k}_{2}\widetilde{Y}_{3},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{k}\widetilde{Y}^{7-k}_{2}\widetilde{Y}_{3}=-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{k+1}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{7-k}-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{k}\widetilde{Y}_{2}^{8-k},~k=1,\cdots,6.

Combining all the pieces together, we can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}^{\top}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{2}\right)^{8}

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}^{\top}{\bf A}_{3}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{1}\right)^{2}\left(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{2}^{\top}{\bf A}_{3}\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{2}\right)^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left(c_{1}\mbox{tr}({\bf A}_{3}\circ{\bf A}_{3})+2c_{2}\mbox{tr}({\bf A}_{3}^{2})\right)^{2}

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

We need to verify the convergence of our statistic

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}_{n}^{2})-\mathbb{E}\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}_{n}^{2})}{\sqrt{\widehat{V}}}=\frac{\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}_{n}^{2})-\mathbb{E}\mbox{tr}(\widetilde{{\bf R}}_{n}^{2})}{\sqrt{\frac{2p^{2}}{n^{2}}\left[n^{3}\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{11}^{4}\right)^{2}+2\right]}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{n^{3}\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{11}^{4}\right)^{2}+2}{n^{3}H^{2}+2}}.

\displaystyle\frac{n^{3}\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{11}^{4}\right)^{2}+2}{n^{3}H^{2}+2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\to}}1.

If
\alpha>3
, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle n^{\frac{3}{2}}H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\to}}0.

If
\alpha\leq 3
, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{1}^{4}}{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\to}}1.

Consider the case when
\alpha\leq 3
first. By Markov’s inequality

\displaystyle P\left(\left|\frac{H}{\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{11}^{4}}-1\right|>\varepsilon\right)=P\left(\left|H-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{11}^{4}\right|>\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{11}^{4}\right)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left|H-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{11}^{4}\right|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{11}^{4}\right)^{2}}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|H-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{11}^{4}|^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}p^{2}}\sum_{i,k=1}^{p}\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(\widetilde{Y}_{ij}^{4}-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{ij}^{4})(\widetilde{Y}_{kl}^{4}-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Y}_{kl}^{4})
