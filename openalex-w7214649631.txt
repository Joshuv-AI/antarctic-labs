Abstract
Cyptides refer to a rapidly growing class of ribosomally synthesized and post-translationally modiﬁed peptides (RiPPs)
containing biaryl cyclophanes installed by P450 enzymes. Here, we further investigated P450 cyptide synthase KwwB from
Kitasatospora sp. GAS204B, which expands the KwwB-catalyzed chemical repertoire to non-native cross-link between TyrC3 and Trp-N1 at the YxW motif. In addition, we demonstrated that KwwB tolerates leader peptide mutations at positions
-11 through -2, with the exception of position -4, which contains a conserved Pro residue. This result provides an additional
toolkit for cross-linked peptide modiﬁcation.

Cyclic peptides have achieved great success in therapeutics with an average of one cyclic peptide drug being
approved every year [5, 6]. Recently, substantial efforts
have been dedicated to identifying promiscuous macrocyclases as a key strategy for generating diverse cyclic
peptides for drug discovery [7–9]. One of these promiscuous macrocyclases is the cytochrome P450 enzyme
associated in the RiPP pathway [9, 10]. P450-RiPP
enzymes are a rapidly expanding class of posttranslational modiﬁcation proteins involved in cyptide
biosynthesis. The name cyptides has been proposed for
biaryl cyclophane-containing peptides formed by P450RiPP enzymes [11], where the biaryl cyclophane is generated between two aromatic side chains [12, 13]. In this
context, C–C, C–N, or C–O bond connecting two aromatic
side chains can be formed, generating diverse biaryl
cyclophane structures containing two to six amino acids
within the cross-linked region on the precursor peptides
(Fig. 1A) [9–11, 14–19]. Biaryl cyclophanes were thought
to be exclusively installed by P450-RiPP enzymes until a
recent discovery extended this reaction to the DUF3328
enzymes [20], while monoaryl cyclophanes are generated
by radical S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) [21], BURPdomain [22], DUF3328 [23], and α-ketoglutaratedependent (αKG) enzymes [24].

Yield of cross-linked peptide fragments
The estimated cross-linking yield was calculated by dividing
the EIC peak intensity of cross-linked peptide fragment against
EIC peak intensity of both cross-linked and unmodiﬁed peptide
fragments. Yield = modiﬁed peptide fragments/(modiﬁed
peptide fragments + unmodiﬁed peptide fragments). To normalize calculated yield to the cross-linked peptide fragment of
wild-type KwwA, the calculated yield of each cross-linked
peptide fragment from precursor variants is divided by the
calculated yield to the cross-linked peptide fragment of wildtype KwwA, and then multiplied by 100%.

Results and discussion
To determine the cross-linked structure of the YxW motif
generated by KwwB, we carried out large-scale protein
expression of His6-SUMO-KwwA W1Y + KwwB at 48 L
and obtained 3.0 mg mixture of peptide fragments 1 and 2
(ratio 1:2) for NMR characterization (Figs. 2B and S1-S2).

The residues within the precursor sequence are numbered
‘+1’ from the start of the core peptide. 2D NMR analysis of
mixture in DMSO-d6 does not affect our ability to elucidate
the structure of cross-linked peptide fragment 1
(Figs. S3–S8). Unlike unmodiﬁed fragment 2, Tyr1 in
modiﬁed fragment 1 does not have any magnetically
equivalent nuclei, indicating the presence of meta- or orthosubstitution (Table S7). Based on HMBC correlation of
Tyr1-H2 (δH 6.59) and Tyr1-H6 (δH 6.91) to Tyr1-Cβ (δC
36.9), we deduced that Tyr1 must be meta-substituted
(Fig. 2B). Due to the overlap signals of Tyr1-H5 and Tyr1H6, no COSY correlation was observed in the aromatic ring
of Tyr1, nevertheless early HMBC evidence concluded a
meta-substituted Tyr1. Phe2 was assigned as unmodiﬁed,
and we focused our attention on Trp3. Unlike unmodiﬁed
fragment 2, the indole of Trp3 in peptide fragment 1 lacks
an obvious downﬁeld proton signal at the N1 position (δH 10.81 at Trp3-N1 in unmodiﬁed fragment 2),
suggesting that Trp3 is substituted at the N1 position
(Table S7). This deduction was supported by the COSY
correlations that connected Trp3-H4 (δH 7.62), Trp3-H5 (δH
7.11), Trp3-H6 (δH 7.13) and Trp3-H7 (δH 7.16) as one spin
system. The positions of these signals in the indole Trp3
were determined by the HMBC correlation of Trp3-H2 to
Trp3-C3 (δC 113.4), Trp3-C3a (δC 128.9) and Trp3-C7a (δC
137.7); Trp3-H4 to Trp3-C6 (δC 122.0) and Trp3-C7a;
Trp3-H5 to Trp3-C3a and Trp3-C7 (δC 112.6); Trp3-H6 to
Trp3-C4 (δC 118.9) and Trp3-C7a; Trp3-H7 to Trp3-C3a
and Trp3-C5 (δC 119.6). Based on the above ﬁndings, we
concluded that the newly formed cross-link was between
Tyr1-C3 and Trp3-N1. The key NOESY correlation of
Tyr1-H2 (δH 6.59) to Trp3-H2 (δH 7.02) conﬁrmed this
conclusion (Fig. 2B). Another key NOESY correlation of
Trp3-H2 to Phe2-Hα (δH 4.98) indicates that Tyr1-H2,
Trp3-H2 and Phe2-Hα lie on the same face, while Tyr1-H5,
Tyr1-H6, Trp3-H4, Trp3-H5, Trp3-H6 and Trp3-H7 lie on
the opposite face (Fig. 2B). The NMR signals of the crosslinked residues in peptide fragment 1 are near identical to
the Tyr-C3–Trp-N1 cross-linked structures generated by
other P450 enzymes (Table S8).
Similar non-native Tyr-C3–Trp-N1 cross-linking has
been observed in P450Blt (Micromonospora sp. MW-13)
[31, 32], while native Tyr-C3–Trp-N1 cross-linking is
generated by SlpB (Streptomyces sp. LaPpAH-199) [11],
SalP (Schoenoplectus californicus NRRL 2423) [15] and
MpoB (Micromonospora polyrhachis DSM 45886) [33].
From a chemical synthesis perspective, this Tyr-C3–Trp-N1
cross-link can be achieved via Larock macrocyclization
[34]. Since cross-links can be installed at the YxW and
WxY motifs, we substituted Tyr with Phe, to ask whether
the hydroxyl unit is essential for cross-linking formation.
Coexpression of His6-SUMO-KwwA W1F or W3F +
KwwB, showed that no cross-linking was observed in either

of the precursor variant (Figs. S9–S10). This result is consistent with the proposed biaryl cross-linking mechanism
(Fig. S11), which involves a second hydrogen atom
abstraction on the hydroxyl group at Tyr-OH4 [12].
In our previous study, we found that KwwB accepts
diverse precursor peptides with C-terminal mutations, (1)
single amino acid mutation at position -1, KwwA N-1A/T/H,
(2) single amino acid mutation at position +2, KwwA F2A/
V/D/T/R/C/H, and (3) single amino acids mutation at position +4, KwwA V4A/T/R/P/D/F [10]. Notably, only the
precursor variant KwwA F2P with the WPW motif was not
accepted by KwwB. To date, numerous studies have reported on the tolerance of P450-RiPP to C-terminal mutations in
precursor peptides [9, 14, 17, 19, 31, 33, 35, 36], but
extensive N-terminal mutations are limited to biarylitide
precursors composed of ﬁve amino acids [35]. In addition,
studies on precursor peptide swapping have also been
reported [9, 11, 17]. The second objective of this study is to
determine whether KwwB could tolerate leader peptide
mutations. Based on the precursor peptide sequence logo
extracted from the P450 proteins highly related to KwwB
(Fig. S12), we found that the N-terminal residues were more
conserved than other regions, especially at positions -11/10/-4 (Fig. 2A). Thus, we focused on a single amino acid
mutation at positions -11 through -2 in the leader peptide
region.
To investigate the promiscuity of KwwB on the leader
peptide region of KwwA, we designed 30 precursor variants, each consisting of a single amino acid mutation at
positions -11 through -2 on the precursor peptide KwwA.
Taking into account that the native residues at these
positions containing non-polar (Ala/Ile/Leu), polar (Asn),
charged (Lys/Arg/His) and rigid (Pro) amino acids, we
systematically designed the precursor variants containing
all four substituted residues Ala, Asn, Lys, and Pro at each
mutated position to cover non-polar, polar, charged and
rigid amino acids (Fig. 2C). Coexpression of all 30 precursor variants, His6-SUMO-KwwA H-11A, H-11N, H11P, R-10A, R-10N, R-10P, I-9K, I-9N, I-9P, L-8K, L-8N,
L-8P, N-7A, N-7K, N-7P, R-6A, R-6N, R-6P, K-5A, K5N, K-5P, P-4A, P-4K, P-4N, A-3K, A-3N, A-3P, R-2A,
R-2N or R-2P + KwwB, followed by Ni-afﬁnity puriﬁcation, trypsin digestion and LC-MS analysis showed
that cross-linking was detected in all precursor variants
except for any mutation at position -4 (Figs. 2D and S1343). We reason that the absence of cross-linking in the
precursor variants KwwA P-4A/K/N is due to the substitution of Pro at position -4, resulting in the loss of the
rigid structure provided by proline. Rigid structure is an
essential feature for the formation of sharp bends or kinks
in the precursor peptides, and this feature appears to be
necessary for cross-linking. Although this is only speculation, the importance of this Pro residue is also

manifested in their conservation (Figs. 2A and S12). We
used AlphaFold 3 to generate complex structure of KwwA
with KwwB [37], but the model had low conﬁdence and
unable to provide any mechanistic insights (Fig. S44). At
present, it remains unclear how Pro residue at position -4
involves in cross-link formation. Interestingly, we still
detected cross-linked products when additional rigidity
was introduced with two consecutive Pro residues (ProPro) at position -5/-4 in KwwA K-5P or -4/-3 in KwwA
A-3P. Nevertheless, this ﬁnding presents the P450 enzyme
KwwB capable of tolerating mutations at the leader peptide region. One possible application is to create chimeric
RiPP products, expanding the structural diversity as seen
in thioamidated biarylitide [35], where biarylitide is
formed by P450-RiPP enzyme.

Conclusions
In conclusion, we further investigated P450 cyptide synthase KwwB, which expands the KwwB-catalyzed chemical repertoire to non-native Tyr-C3–Trp-N1 cross-link.
In addition, KwwB shows broad substrate speciﬁcity for
mutations at positions -11 through -2 in the leader peptide
region, except at position -4 containing a conserved Pro
residue, highlighting the versatility of P450 enzymes.
Among all the tested precursor variants, KwwA R-2P has
the highest cross-linking yield, while KwwA K-5A and
R-2A had the lowest cross-linking yields (Fig. 2D and
Table S9). At present, how the Pro residue at position -4
participates in cross-link formation remains unclear, and
whether it plays a role in substrate recognition or peptide
conformation requires future investigation.
