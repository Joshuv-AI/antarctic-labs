Rates of Decay for ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -Ritt-Kreiss Operators
Loris Arnold

Abstract
. This paper investigates the growth of the sequences ( ‖ T n ​ ( I − T ) k ‖ ) n ≥ 1 (\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|)_{n\geq 1} for bounded linear operators on Banach spaces under various resolvent conditions. Focusing on α \alpha -Ritt operators that are also strongly Kreiss bounded, we show that the growth estimates established by Nevanlinna for Kreiss bounded operators can be significantly refined within the Hilbert space setting, nearly attaining the optimal rates obtained by Seifert for the more restrictive power-bounded case. We further introduce the class of ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -RK operators as a generalization of both Ritt and Kreiss-type conditions. For these operators, we derive comprehensive growth estimates which, for certain ranges of α \alpha and β \beta , yield improvements over existing bounds in the literature. Particular attention is given to β \beta -Kreiss operators, for which we provide a characterization via Cesàro-type means. We show that, in contrast to the well-known case β = 1 \beta=1 , the power growth estimate ‖ T n ‖ = O ⁡ ( n β ) \|T^{n}\|=O(n^{\beta}) is sharp whenever β > 1 \beta>1 . The optimality of our estimates is discussed in several cases, relying on constructions and techniques developed by Nevanlinna, Spijker, and Borovykh. We conclude by providing a characterization of Ritt operators that appears to be absent from the literature.

1.
Introduction

The classical Katznelson-Tzafriri theorem asserts that for a power-bounded operator
T
on a Banach space
X
, the sequence
\|T^{n}(I-T)\|
converges to zero as
n\to\infty
if and only if the spectrum of
T
satisfies
\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{T}\subset\{1\}
. To obtain quantitative rates for this decay, additional conditions on the resolvent are required. A prominent class in this context is that of
\alpha
-Ritt operators for
\alpha\geq 1
, defined by the condition:

(1)

\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|R(\lambda,T)\|\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|^{\alpha}},\quad 1<|\lambda|<2.

Under the assumption of power-boundedness, Seifert
[
20
]
proved that

(2)

\|T^{n}(I-T)\|=O(n^{-1/\alpha}\log(n+1)).

The case
\alpha=1
corresponds to the classical Ritt operators, which can be characterized as those power-bounded operators for which the optimal decay
\|T^{n}(I-T)\|=O(n^{-1})
holds. More generally, Ritt operators admit the following equivalent characterizations:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T\in B(X)
with
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

T
is a Ritt operator;

(ii)

For
\alpha,\beta>0
with
\alpha+\beta=1
,

\|R(\lambda,T)\|\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|^{\alpha}(|\lambda|-1)^{\beta}},\quad 1<|\lambda|<2;

(iii)

T
is power-bounded and, for some (or all)
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|=O(n^{-k})
.

A natural question is whether similar estimates for
\alpha
-Ritt operators hold when the assumption of power-boundedness is relaxed. An operator
T\in B(X)
is said to be Kreiss bounded if

(3)

\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|R(\lambda,T)\|\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|-1},\quad|\lambda|>1.

While Kreiss boundedness is equivalent to power-boundedness in finite dimensions, it is strictly weaker in infinite-dimensional spaces. In the latter case, the growth of
\|T^{n}\|
can be as fast as
O(n)
, although more refined bounds exist depending on the geometry of the space
X
. For example, if
X
is a UMD space with finite cotypes
q
and
q^{*}
for
X
and
X^{*}
respectively, and we set
s=\min(q,q^{*})
, Theorem 3.1 in
[
7
]
states that

\|T^{n}\|=O\Bigg(\frac{n}{\log^{1/s}(n+1)}\Bigg).

A stronger property, known as strong Kreiss boundedness, was introduced by McCarthy
[
16
]
. It is defined by the following estimate on the powers of the resolvent:

\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|R(\lambda,T)^{n}\|\leq\frac{C}{(|\lambda|-1)^{n}},\quad|\lambda|>1,\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

This condition has been the subject of extensive study, and various growth estimates for
\|T^{n}\|
have been obtained depending on the geometry of the underlying space
X
(see
[
19
,
6
,
1
,
8
]
):

\|T^{n}\|=\begin{cases}O(n^{1/2}),&\text{in general},\\
O(\log^{\kappa}(n+1)),&X\text{ a Hilbert space},\\
O\big(n^{|1/2-1/p|}\log^{\kappa}(n+1)\big),&X\text{ an }L^{p}\text{-space},\\
o(n^{1/2}),&X\text{ a UMD space}.\end{cases}

One of the primary goals of this work is to study
\alpha
-Ritt operators that satisfy these weaker resolvent conditions. For Kreiss bounded
\alpha
-Ritt operators, Nevanlinna
[
19
, Theorem 9.]
established the following growth estimate:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T
be Kreiss bounded and satisfying (
1
). Then, for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

(4)

\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|=O\big(n^{\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}}\big).

The paper is organized as follows.

In Section 2, we show that these estimates (
4
) can be significantly refined by assuming strong Kreiss boundedness and employing moment inequalities. In the Hilbert space setting, we derive a bound analogous to (
2
) for strongly Kreiss bounded
\alpha
-Ritt operators:

\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|=O\left(\frac{\log^{\kappa}(n+1)}{n^{k/\alpha}}\right),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Section 3 is devoted to a complete proof of Theorem
1.2
. This result serves as a foundational tool for the derivations presented in the subsequent sections.

In Section 4, we introduce the class of
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK operators, defined by the resolvent estimate

\|R(\lambda,T)\|\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|^{\alpha}(|\lambda|-1)^{\beta}},\quad 1<|\lambda|<2,

which unifies Ritt-type (
\beta=0
) and Kreiss-type (
\alpha=0
) conditions. For the parameter range
\alpha>0
,
0\leq\beta<1
with
\alpha+\beta>1
, we establish in Theorem
4.4
a general upper bound for
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|
that directly improves and generalizes the estimates from
[
15
, Corollary 3.3, Case 4]
. Specifically, for
k>\alpha-1
, we prove that

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O(n^{\frac{\alpha+(\beta-1)(k+1)}{\alpha}}).

By setting
\beta=0
, these results provide, as a special case, refined estimates for
\alpha
-Ritt operators (see Corollary
4.7
).

Section 5 focuses on
\beta
-Kreiss operators, i.e.,
(0,\beta)
-RK operators. To analyze these operators, we introduce a generalization of classical Cesàro boundedness, called
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}
-boundedness, defined by the growth condition
\|S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)\|=O(n^{\gamma+\beta-1})
for
\gamma>0
and
\beta\geq 1
, where
S_{n}^{\gamma}(T):=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{n-j}^{\gamma-1}T^{j}
denotes the Cesàro sums of order
\gamma
. We establish a characterization of
\beta
-Kreiss operators in terms of the uniform boundedness of these generalized Cesàro means for
\gamma>1
. Next, we introduce the property of uniform
\beta
-Kreiss boundedness and show its equivalence to the uniform
C_{1}^{(\beta)}
-boundedness of
\lambda T
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
. For the special class of positive operators acting on Banach lattices, we show that
\beta
-Kreiss boundedness, uniform
\beta
-Kreiss boundedness, and
C_{1}^{(\beta)}
-boundedness are all equivalent, extending a classical result from the case
\beta=1
. Finally, adapting a construction from
[
3
]
, we exhibit an explicit example of a
\beta
-Kreiss operator on a Hilbert space whose powers satisfy the lower bound
\|T^{n}\|\geq c\,n^{\beta}
for some constant
c>0
and for all
n\geq 1
. This proves that for any
\beta>1
, the power growth estimate
\|T^{n}\|=O(n^{\beta})
is sharp, a behavior that stands in sharp contrast to the classical case
\beta=1
which implies
\|T^{n}\|=o(n)
in Hilbert spaces.

Section 6 is dedicated to examples. We first study an example by Nevanlinna
[
19
, Example 5]
to show that the estimates in (
4
) are sharp for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Next, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the multiplication operator
M_{z}
on a specific Banach space of analytic functions shows that an
m
-Kreiss operator is not necessarily
C_{1}^{(m)}
-bounded providing also the necessity of the
\log(n+1)
factor in the estimate
\left\|S_{n}^{m}(T)\right\|=O(n^{m}\log(n+1))
for
m
-Kreiss operator. Next, for
m
-Ritt operators, we use matrices from
[
5
]
to show that the rate
O(n^{m-k-1})
is sharp when
k\leq m-2
. We then use a larger version of the Assani matrix to prove that Cesàro-
m
boundedness does not imply
m
-Kreiss boundedness. Furthermore, an
\ell^{2}
-direct sum of finite-dimensional blocks, constructed from the examples of Borovykh and Spijker
[
5
]
, gives an
(m,1)
-RK operator with the maximal growth rate
O\big(n^{m}\log(n+1)\big)
. Finally, we generalize the Tomilov–Zemánek matrix construction. We prove that for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, an operator
T
is power bounded if and only if a triangular matrix
\mathcal{T}_{N+1}
built from
T
is Cesàro-
N
bounded. This provides Cesàro-
N
bounded operators with power growth
n^{N}
such that
\Big(n^{-N}S^{N}_{n}\big(\mathcal{T}_{N+1}\big)\Big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly, while
\Big(n^{-N}S^{N}_{n}\big(\mathcal{T}_{N+1}\big)\Big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not.

2.
\alpha
-Ritt and strongly Kreiss bounded operators in Hilbert spaces

This section is devoted to establishing a sharp upper bound for the growth of the sequences
\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|
when
T
is an
\alpha
-Ritt operator on a Hilbert space. We show that under the stronger assumption of strong Kreiss boundedness, the classical polynomial decay can be balanced against a logarithmic growth factor.

The following lemma establishes the control of the orbit sums for strongly Kreiss bounded operators in Hilbert spaces (see
[
6
, Lemma 4.8]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T
be a strongly Kreiss bounded operator on a Hilbert space
H
. Then there exist constants
C,\kappa>0
such that for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|T^{n}x\|\leq CN\log(N+1)^{\kappa}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|(T^{*})^{n}x\|\leq CN\log(N+1)^{\kappa}.

As a direct consequence of Lemma
2.1
, we can bound
\left\|T^{N}\right\|
. Indeed, for any unit vectors
x,y\in H
, we use the identity
N\langle T^{N}x,y\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle T^{n}x,(T^{*})^{N-n}y\rangle
. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, together with Lemma
2.1
, implies

|\langle T^{N}x,y\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{N}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|T^{n}x\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|(T^{*})^{n}y\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq C\log(N+1)^{\kappa}.

Taking the supremum over
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
, we establish the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
T
be a strongly Kreiss bounded operator on a Hilbert space
H
. Then there exist constants
C,\kappa>0
such that for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\|T^{N}\right\|\leq C\log(N+1)^{\kappa}.

In the sequel, we will need to deal with fractional powers of
(I-T)
. Recall that when
I-T
is sectorial, the operator
(I-T)^{\alpha}
is defined via the standard sectorial functional calculus (see
[
11
]
for further details).

For our purposes, alongside Lemma
2.1
, we will require the following result, which follows directly from the moment inequality (see
[
10
, Corollary 7.2]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T\in B(X)
be such that
I-T
is sectorial. Then for
\alpha>1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|\leq C\,\|T^{n}\|^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k\alpha}\|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

where
C
depends only on
\alpha
and
k
.

Before stating the main result of this section, we recall the definition of
\alpha
-Ritt operators

Definition 2.4
.

We say that
T\in B(X)
is an
\alpha
-Ritt operator for
\alpha>1
if
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(5)

\left\|R(\lambda,T)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|^{\alpha}},\quad 1<|\lambda|<2.

The smallest constant
C
satisfying (
5
) is denoted by
R_{\alpha}(T)
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
T
be an
\alpha
-Ritt operator on a Hilbert space
H
.
If
T
is strongly Kreiss bounded, then there exists
\kappa>0
such that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|=O\Big(\frac{\log^{\kappa}(n+1)}{n^{k/\alpha}}\Big).

We begin with a preliminary lemma, the proof of which can be found in
[
20
, Lemma 3.9]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\alpha>1
and let
T\in B(X)
be an
\alpha
-Ritt operator such that
I-T
is sectorial. Then there exists
M>0
, depending on
R_{\alpha}(T)
such that

\|(I-T)^{\alpha}R(\lambda,T)\|\leq M,\quad 1<|\lambda|<2.

Remark 2.7
.

In
[
20
]
, the proof of Lemma
2.6
is originally provided under the assumption that
T
is power-bounded. However, the condition of power-boundedness is not used in the proof.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2.5
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
T
be an
\alpha
-Ritt operator.
The first step consists in showing that

(6)

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{n+k}{k}^{2}\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k\alpha}x\|^{2}\leq C\sum_{n=0}^{N}\|T^{n}x\|^{2},\quad N\in\mathbb{N},

where
C
depends only on
k
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|>1
and define

\varphi_{N,k}(\lambda):=\lambda^{k}R(\lambda,T)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{T^{n}}{\lambda^{n}},\quad N\in\mathbb{N}.

A straightforward computation based on the identity

\lambda^{k}R(\lambda,T)^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}\frac{T^{n}}{\lambda^{n}}

leads to

\varphi_{N,k}(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{n,N\}}\binom{n-j+k-1}{k-1}\frac{T^{n}}{\lambda^{n}}.

Applying Parseval’s identity yields

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\Bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n-j+k-1}{k-1}\Bigg)^{2}\frac{\|T^{n}x\|^{2}}{\rho^{2n}}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\varphi_{N,k}(\rho e^{i\theta})x\|^{2}\,d\theta,

for every
x\in H
and
\rho>1
.

Using

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{r+j}{r}=\binom{r+m+1}{r+1},

we obtain

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n-j+k-1}{k-1}=\binom{n+k}{k}.

It follows that

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{n+k}{k}^{2}\frac{\|T^{n}x\|^{2}}{\rho^{2n}}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\varphi_{N,k}(\rho e^{i\theta})x\|^{2}\,d\theta.

Substituting
x
by
(I-T)^{k\alpha}x
and using Lemma
2.6
,
together with Parseval’s identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{\binom{n+k}{k}^{2}}{\rho^{2n}}\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k\alpha}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{2k}\rho^{2k}}{2\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{\|T^{n}x\|^{2}}{\rho^{2n}}.

Letting
\rho\to 1^{+}
yields (
6
).

For
(x,y)\in H\times H^{*}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\binom{N+k+1}{k+1}\langle T^{N}(I-T)^{k\alpha}x,y\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\left\langle\binom{n+k}{k}T^{n}(I-T)^{k\alpha}x,(T^{*})^{N-n}y\right\rangle.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, it follows

(7)

\displaystyle|\langle T^{N}(I-T)^{k\alpha}x,y\rangle|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\binom{N+k+1}{k+1}^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{n+k}{k}^{2}\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k\alpha}x\|^{2}\Bigg)\Bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{N}\|(T^{*})^{N-n}y\|^{2}\Bigg).

Now assume that
T
is strongly Kreiss bounded.
By Lemma
2.1
and (
6
),
there exists
\kappa>0
such that

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{n+k}{k}^{2}\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k\alpha}x\|^{2}\leq CN\log^{\kappa}(N+1)\|x\|^{2},

and

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\|(T^{*})^{N-n}y\|^{2}\leq CN\log^{\kappa}(N+1).

Combining these estimates with (
7
) and taking the supremum over
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
, we obtain

\|T^{N}(I-T)^{k\alpha}\|\leq\frac{CN\log^{\kappa}(N+1)}{\binom{N+k+1}{k+1}},\quad N\in\mathbb{N}.

Since

\frac{1}{\binom{N+k+1}{k+1}}\leq\frac{(k+1)!}{N^{k+1}},

it follows that

\|T^{N}(I-T)^{k\alpha}\|\leq\frac{C\log^{\kappa}(N+1)}{N^{k}}.

for
C
depending only on
k
.
Finally, applying Lemma
2.3
and Proposition
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|T^{N}(I-T)^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\|T^{N}\|^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}\|T^{N}(I-T)^{k\alpha}\|^{1/\alpha}\leq\frac{\log^{\kappa}(N+1)}{N^{k/\alpha}}.

e^{i\theta}-z\in\mathcal{D}_{T,e^{i\theta}}
. In this case, using (
8
) and the
\alpha
-Ritt condition, we have

\displaystyle\|R(e^{i\theta}-z,T)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(e^{i\theta}-z-e^{i\theta})^{k}R(e^{i\theta},T)^{k+1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R_{\alpha}(T)}{|e^{i\theta}-1|^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{R_{\alpha}(T)^{k}|z|^{k}}{|e^{i\theta}-1|^{k\alpha}}.

By choosing
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|z|\leq\frac{|e^{i\theta}-1|^{\alpha}}{2R_{\alpha}(T)}
, we ensure that
e^{i\theta}-z\in\mathcal{D}_{T,e^{i\theta}}
and

(10)

\|R(e^{i\theta}-z,T)\|\leq\frac{2R_{\alpha}(T)}{|e^{i\theta}-1|^{\alpha}}.

Furthermore, since
1-e^{-x}\leq x
for every real number
x
, we have

1-e^{-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}\leq c|\theta|^{\alpha},\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].

Utilizing the standard inequality

(11)

|\theta|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\times 2\left|\sin\Big(\frac{\theta}{2}\Big)\right|=\frac{\pi}{2}|1-e^{i\theta}|,\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi],

it follows that by setting
z=e^{i\theta}-\Gamma(\theta)
, we obtain

|z|=1-e^{-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}\leq c\left(\frac{\pi}{2}|1-e^{i\theta}|\right)^{\alpha}\leq\frac{|1-e^{i\theta}|^{\alpha}}{2R_{\alpha}(T)}.

Since
e^{i\theta}-z=e^{i\theta-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}
, inequality (
10
) implies that

\|R(\Gamma(\theta),T)\|\leq\frac{2R_{\alpha}(T)}{|e^{i\theta}-1|^{\alpha}},\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].

We note that since
\alpha>1
,

|1-e^{i\theta-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}|\leq\Big(1+\frac{c\pi^{\alpha}}{2}\Big)|1-e^{i\theta}|,\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi],

which yields

(12)

\|R(\Gamma(\theta),T)\|\leq\frac{2R_{\alpha}(T)}{\Big(1+\frac{c\pi^{\alpha}}{2}\Big)|e^{i\theta-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}-1|^{\alpha}}=\frac{2R_{\alpha}(T)}{\Big(1+\frac{c\pi^{\alpha}}{2}\Big)|\Gamma(\theta)-1|^{\alpha}},\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].

Finally, consider the function

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}u&:&\Omega:=\{\lambda\in\operatorname{ext}(\Gamma),\;|\lambda|\leq 2\}&\to&\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
&&\lambda&\mapsto&|\lambda-1|^{\alpha}\|R(\lambda,T)\|.\\
\end{array}

Since
\lambda\mapsto(\lambda-1)^{\alpha}R(\lambda,T)
is analytic on
\Omega
(where we use an appropriate determination of logarithm), then by
[
12
, Theorem 3.13.1]
,
u
is subharmonic on
\Omega
. Moreover
u
is bounded on
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda|=2\}
as well as on
\{\Gamma(\theta):\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]\}
by (
12
), the required estimate (
9
) follows directly from the maximum principle.
∎

To facilitate the presentation of the main proof, we establish two technical lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a>0
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
, and
d>0
. Then

(13)

\int_{\big(\frac{d}{n}\big)^{1/a}}^{\infty}e^{-nd\theta^{a}}\theta^{b}\,d\theta\leq Cn^{-\frac{1+b}{a}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
C
depends only on
a,b
, and
d
.

Proof.

Performing the change of variables
t=nd\theta^{a}
, we obtain

\theta=\Big(\frac{t}{nd}\Big)^{1/a},\qquad d\theta=\frac{1}{a}(nd)^{-1/a}t^{\frac{1}{a}-1}\,dt.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\big(\frac{d}{n}\big)^{1/a}}^{\infty}e^{-nd\theta^{a}}\theta^{b}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}(nd)^{-\frac{1+b}{a}}\int_{d^{2}}^{\infty}t^{\frac{1+b}{a}-1}e^{-t}\,dt,

and the estimate (
13
) follows.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

If
b>-1
, then
\frac{1+b}{a}-1>-1
. Since
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s}e^{-t}\,dt<\infty
for any
s>-1
, we obtain

(14)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nd\theta^{a}}\theta^{b}\,d\theta\leq Cn^{-\frac{1+b}{a}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\,a>0,\,b>-1,

where
C
depends on
a,b
, and
d
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
r>0
. Then

(15)

|re^{i\theta}-1|\geq\frac{1}{2}|e^{i\theta}-1|,\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].

Proof.

Let
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
. We observe that

|re^{i\theta}-1|^{2}=r^{2}-2r\cos\theta+1,

whose minimum over
r\geq 0
occurs at
r=\max(\cos\theta,0)
.

If
\cos\theta\leq 0
, then
|re^{i\theta}-1|\geq 1
. Since
|e^{i\theta}-1|=2|\sin(\tfrac{\theta}{2})|\leq 2
, estimate (
15
) immediately follows.

If
\cos\theta>0
, then

|re^{i\theta}-1|\geq|\cos(\theta)e^{i\theta}-1|=|\sin(\theta)|.

Using the identity
\sin\theta=2\sin(\tfrac{\theta}{2})\cos(\tfrac{\theta}{2})
and noting that
\cos(\tfrac{\theta}{2})\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
whenever
\cos\theta>0
, we obtain

|re^{i\theta}-1|\geq|\sin\theta|=2\left|\sin\Big(\frac{\theta}{2}\Big)\right|\left|\cos\Big(\frac{\theta}{2}\Big)\right|\geq\left|\sin\Big(\frac{\theta}{2}\Big)\right|=\frac{1}{2}|e^{i\theta}-1|,

which establishes inequality (
15
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For any integer
n>1
, let
\Gamma_{n}:=\big(1+\frac{1}{n}\big)\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is the curve defined in Lemma
3.1
. Then
\Gamma_{n}([-\pi,\pi])\subset\operatorname{ext}(\Gamma)\cap D(0,2)
. By the Dunford–Riesz operational calculus, we can write

T^{n}(I-T)^{k}=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}\lambda^{n}(1-\lambda)^{k}R(\lambda,T)\,d\lambda.

Taking the norm yields

\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\pi}^{\pi}\big|\Gamma_{n}^{\prime}(\theta)\big|\big|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)\big|^{n}\big|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)\big|^{k}\|R(\Gamma_{n}(\theta),T)\|\,d\theta.

For
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, we have
|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|=\big(1+\frac{1}{n}\big)e^{-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}
and

|\Gamma^{\prime}_{n}(\theta)|=\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)\Big|-c\alpha\operatorname{sign}(\theta)|\theta|^{\alpha-1}+i\Big|e^{-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}\leq 2(c\alpha\pi^{\alpha-1}+1).

Using (
9
) along with the inequality
\big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{n}\leq e
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we find

\displaystyle\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|\leq C\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-nc|\theta|^{\alpha}}|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|^{k}\|R(\Gamma_{n}(\theta),T)\|\,d\theta,

where
C
depends on
c
and
\alpha
.

For integers
n>1
, we define
\theta_{n}:=\big(\frac{c}{n}\big)^{1/\alpha}
. For any
\theta
satisfying
0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}
, we observe that

|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|\geq|\Gamma_{n}(\theta_{n})|=\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)e^{-c^{2}/n}\geq\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{n}\Big)=1+\frac{1-c^{2}}{n}-\frac{c^{2}}{n^{2}}\geq 1+\frac{1-2c^{2}}{n}.

Thus, assuming
c<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
, we ensure that
|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|>1
for all
0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}
.

We now partition the interval of integration into two regions:

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-nc|\theta|^{\alpha}}|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|^{k}\|R(\Gamma_{n}(\theta),T)\|\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}}\cdots\,d\theta+\int_{\theta_{n}\leq|\theta|\leq\pi}\cdots\,d\theta

(16)

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

On the interval
0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}
, we exploit the hypothesis that
T
is Kreiss bounded (since
|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|>1
there). On the remaining interval
\theta_{n}\leq|\theta|\leq\pi
, we leverage the
\alpha
-Ritt condition. This yields the bounds

I_{1}\leq\int_{0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}}e^{-nc|\theta|^{\alpha}}\frac{|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|^{k}}{|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|-1}\,d\theta\quad\text{and}\quad I_{2}\leq\int_{\theta_{n}\leq|\theta|\leq\pi}e^{-nc|\theta|^{\alpha}}|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|^{k-\alpha}\,d\theta.

For any
\theta
such that
0<|\theta|\leq\pi
, we can write

\displaystyle\Big|\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)e^{-c|\theta|^{\alpha}+i\theta}-1\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|e^{i\theta}-1|+\Big|\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)e^{-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}-1\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|\theta|+|1-e^{-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}|+\frac{e^{-c|\theta|^{\alpha}}}{n}

\displaystyle\leq|\theta|+c|\theta|^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{n}.

Consequently, we obtain the estimate

(17)

\big|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)\big|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C\Big(|\theta|+\frac{1}{n}\Big)&\text{if }0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n},\\
C|\theta|&\text{if }\theta_{n}\leq|\theta|\leq\pi,\end{array}\right.

where
C
depends on
\alpha
and
c
.

To bound
I_{1}
, we use
e^{-nc|\theta|^{\alpha}}\leq 1
and the lower bound
|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|-1\geq\frac{1-2c^{2}}{n}
valid for
0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}
. It follows that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{k}n}{1-2c^{2}}\int_{0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}}\Big(|\theta|+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{k}\,d\theta=\frac{2C^{k}n}{(1-2c^{2})(k+1)}\left[\Big(\frac{c}{n^{1/\alpha}}+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{k+1}-\frac{1}{n^{k+1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}n^{1-\frac{{k+1}}{\alpha}}=C^{\prime}n^{\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}},

where
C^{\prime}
depends on
\alpha,c
and
k
.

To bound
I_{2}
, we apply inequalities (
15
) and (
11
) to obtain

(18)

|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|e^{i\theta}-1|\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{\pi}|\theta|=\frac{1}{\pi}|\theta|,\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].

By combining (
17
) (when
k-\alpha\geq 0
) and (
18
) (when
k-\alpha<0
), we deduce that

\int_{\theta_{n}\leq\theta\leq\pi}e^{-nc\theta^{\alpha}}|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|^{k-\alpha}\,d\theta\leq C^{\prime\prime}\int_{\theta_{n}\leq\theta\leq\pi}e^{-nc\theta^{\alpha}}\theta^{k-\alpha}\,d\theta,

where
C^{\prime\prime}
depends on
\alpha,c
and
k
.
Finally, using (
13
), we conclude that

I_{2}\leq 2C^{\prime\prime}\int_{\big(\frac{c}{n}\big)^{1/\alpha}}^{\infty}e^{-nc\theta^{\alpha}}\theta^{k-\alpha}\,d\theta\leq Mn^{-\frac{1+k-\alpha}{\alpha}}=Mn^{\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We conclude this section with a proposition stating that in the case
\alpha=2
, the estimates given in (
4
) are sharp. The proof is provided in
[
19
, Example 5]
for
k\in\{0,1\}
. We provide further details in Section
6.1
, including the cases
k\geq 2
.

Proposition 3.5
.

There exist a Banach space
X
and an operator
T\in B(X)
which is Kreiss bounded and
2
-Ritt, such that for some constant
C>1
and all
n\geq 1
:

\frac{1}{C}n^{\frac{1-k}{2}}\leq\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|\leq Cn^{\frac{1-k}{2}}.

4.
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK Operators and Power Growth Estimates

In this section, we study a class of bounded linear operators that generalizes both the Ritt and Kreiss boundedness conditions. This
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK condition was originally introduced by Borovykh and Spijker
[
4
, Section 4]
in the framework of numerical stability for initial value problems, and has since been further investigated by Mahillo and Rueda
[
15
]
. Our primary objective is to establish sharp bounds on the growth of the sequence
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|
for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
by using techniques employed in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
T\in B(X)
is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK operator for
\alpha,\beta>0
if
\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(19)

\left\|R(\lambda,T)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|^{\alpha}(|\lambda|-1)^{\beta}},\quad 1<|\lambda|<2.

In the limiting case where
\beta=0
,
T
reduces to an
\alpha
-Ritt operator, whereas for
\alpha=0
,
T
is said to be
\beta
-Kreiss bounded. We denote by
R_{\alpha,\beta}(T)
the smallest constant
C
satisfying (
19
).

Let
T
be an
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK operator. If
\alpha+\beta<1
, then
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=O(e^{-\omega n})
for some
\omega>0
(see
[
15
, Corollary 3.3. case 1]
. If
\alpha+\beta=1
, then
T
is a Ritt operator. Furthermore, provided that
\beta\geq 1
, then by
[
15
, Corollary 3.3.]
,
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=O(G_{\alpha,\beta}(n))
, where the auxiliary function
G_{\alpha,\beta}(n)
is defined as

G_{\alpha,\beta}(n)=\begin{cases}n^{\beta},&\text{if }0\leq\alpha<1,\\
n^{\beta}\log(n+1),&\text{if }\alpha=1,\\
n^{\beta+\alpha-1},&\text{if }\alpha>1.\end{cases}

Since
\beta
-Kreiss boundedness constitutes a less restrictive condition than
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK boundedness, one cannot generally expect an estimate sharper than
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O(G_{\alpha,\beta}(n))
for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Motivated by these observations, the subsequent analysis focuses on the non-trivial regime where
\alpha+\beta>1
. We first state the following result:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\alpha>0
such that
\alpha+\beta>1
. Let
T\in B(X)
be an
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK operator. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the following asymptotic estimates hold:

(1)

If
k<\alpha-1
, then

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O\Big(n^{\alpha+\beta-k-1}\Big).

(2)

If
k=\lfloor\alpha-1\rfloor
, then

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=\begin{cases}O\Big(n^{\beta}\log(n+1)\Big),&\text{if }\alpha\in\mathbb{N},\\
O\Big(n^{\beta}\Big),&\text{if }\alpha\notin\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By the Riesz-Dunford calculus, for
\rho>1
, we have

T^{n}(I-T)^{k}=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho^{n+1}e^{i(n+1)t}(1-\rho e^{it})^{k}R(\rho e^{it},T)\,dt,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Applying the
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK condition (
19
) yields

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|\leq R_{\alpha,\beta}(T)\frac{\rho^{n+1}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{|\rho e^{it}-1|^{\alpha-k}(\rho-1)^{\beta}}\,dt.

Choosing
\rho=1+\frac{1}{n}
and using Lemma
1.3
,
we obtain the desired result.
∎

For the regime
\beta<1
, we are able to establish explicit estimates for
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|
when the integers satisfy
k>\alpha-1
, exploiting the fact that
T
is of Ritt-type in this case. More precisely, according to
[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
, we have the following result:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
T
be an
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK operator on
X
with
\beta<1
and
\alpha>0
. Then
T
is an
\alpha_{\beta}
-Ritt operator, where
\alpha_{\beta}:=\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}
.

This allows us to derive the following bounds :

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
0\leq\beta<1
such that
\alpha+\beta>1
. Let
T\in B(X)
be an
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK operator. For any
k>\alpha-1
, we have :

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O\Big(n^{\frac{\alpha+(\beta-1)(k+1)}{\alpha}}\Big).

To establish the Theorem
4.4
, we require the following estimate.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
b>-1
and
a>1
, then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
a,b
and
d
such that

(20)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nd\theta^{a}}\Big(\theta+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{b}\,d\theta\leq Cn^{-\frac{1+b}{a}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
C
depends only on
a,b
, and
d
.

Proof.

Applying the change of variables
t=nd\theta^{a}
, the integral can be rewritten as

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nd\theta^{a}}\Big(\theta+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{b}\,d\theta=\frac{1}{a}(nd)^{-\frac{b+1}{a}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\frac{b+1}{a}-1}\left(1+n^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{a}\right)}d^{\frac{1}{a}}t^{-\frac{1}{a}}\right)^{b}\,dt.

Next, we use the property
(1+x)^{b}\leq C_{b}(1+x^{b})
for
x\geq 0
, where
C_{b}:=\max(1,2^{b-1})
. Furthermore, since
a>1
and
b>0
, we have
b\left(1-\frac{1}{a}\right)>0
, which immediately implies that
n^{-b\left(1-\frac{1}{a}\right)}\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying these properties, we can bound the integral on the right-hand side uniformly with respect to
n
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\frac{b+1}{a}-1}\left(1+n^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{a}\right)}d^{\frac{1}{a}}t^{-\frac{1}{a}}\right)^{b}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{b}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\frac{b+1}{a}-1}\left(1+n^{-b\left(1-\frac{1}{a}\right)}d^{\frac{b}{a}}t^{-\frac{b}{a}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{b}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\frac{b+1}{a}-1}\left(1+d^{\frac{b}{a}}t^{-\frac{b}{a}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=C_{b}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\frac{b+1}{a}-1}\,dt+C_{b}d^{\frac{b}{a}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\frac{1}{a}-1}\,dt.

Since
\frac{b+1}{a}>0
and
\frac{1}{a}>0
, the exponents satisfy
\frac{b+1}{a}-1>-1
and
\frac{1}{a}-1>-1
. Thus, both integrals on the right-hand side converge, which yields (
20
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Assume that
k>\alpha-1
. Since
T
is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK operator with
\beta<1
, Lemma
4.3
ensures that
T
is an
\alpha_{\beta}
-Ritt operator with
\alpha_{\beta}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}
. Following the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we define
\Gamma_{n}:=\big(1+\frac{1}{n}\big)e^{i\theta-c|\theta|^{\alpha_{\beta}}}
for a sufficiently small parameter
c>0
. This yields

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|\leq C\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\Gamma^{\prime}_{n}(\theta)||1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|^{k}\left\|R(\Gamma_{n}(\theta),T)\right\|\,d\theta=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where the integrals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are partitioned at
\theta_{n}:=\big(\frac{c}{n}\big)^{1/\alpha_{\beta}}
, analogously to (
3
). Since
T
is
\alpha_{\beta}
-Ritt, the same arguments used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
show that the integral
I_{2}
satisfies

I_{2}\leq C_{2}n^{-\frac{1+k-\alpha_{\beta}}{\alpha_{\beta}}}=C_{2}n^{\frac{\alpha_{\beta}-k-1}{\alpha_{\beta}}}=C_{2}n^{\frac{\alpha+(\beta-1)(k+1)}{\alpha}},

where
C_{2}
depends on
\alpha,\beta,c
and
k
.
To estimate the integral
I_{1}
, we invoke the
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK condition. For
0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}
, the contour satisfies
|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|>1
and
|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|-1\geq\frac{1-2c^{2}}{n}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}}e^{-nc|\theta|^{\alpha_{\beta}}}\frac{|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|^{k-\alpha}}{(|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|-1)^{\beta}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}n^{\beta}\int_{0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}}e^{-nc|\theta|^{\alpha_{\beta}}}|1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|^{k-\alpha}\,d\theta,

where
C^{\prime}
depends on
\alpha,\beta,c
, and
k
.

If
k-\alpha\geq 0
, we apply the estimate (
17
) and Lemma
4.5
to obtain

I_{1}\leq C^{\prime}n^{\beta}\int_{0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}}e^{-nc|\theta|^{\alpha_{\beta}}}\Big(|\theta|+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{k-\alpha}\,d\theta\leq C_{1}n^{\beta-\frac{k+1-\alpha}{\alpha_{\beta}}}.

If
-1<k-\alpha<0
, we utilize the lower bounds (
15
) along with (
14
) to find

I_{1}\leq C^{\prime}n^{\beta}\int_{0\leq|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}}e^{-nc|\theta|^{\alpha_{\beta}}}|\theta|^{k-\alpha}\,d\theta\leq C_{1}n^{\beta-\frac{k+1-\alpha}{\alpha_{\beta}}},

where
C_{1}
depends on
\alpha,\beta,c
, and
k
.
Observing that

\displaystyle\beta-\frac{k+1-\alpha}{\alpha_{\beta}}=\frac{\alpha+(\beta-1)(k+1)}{\alpha},

we conclude that
I_{1}\leq C_{1}n^{\frac{\alpha+(\beta-1)(k+1)}{\alpha}}
, completing the proof of the third item.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

(1)

Setting
\beta=0
in Theorem
4.4
provides the rates for an
\alpha
-Ritt operator with
\alpha>1
:

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=\begin{cases}O(n^{\alpha-k-1}),&\text{if }k<\alpha-1,\\
O(\log(n+1)),&\text{if }k=\alpha-1,\\
O(n^{\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}}),&\text{if }k>\alpha-1.\end{cases}

In particular, for
1<\alpha<2
, an
\alpha
-Ritt operator satisfies

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=\begin{cases}O(n^{\alpha-1}),&\text{if }k=0,\\
O(n^{\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}}),&\text{if }k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

This clarifies the link with Theorem
1.2
: in this case, adding the Kreiss boundedness assumption only improves the rate at the boundary
k=0
, where it reduces the power growth from
O(n^{\alpha-1})
down to
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=O(n^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}})
.

(2)

In the regime where
0<\alpha,\beta<1
and
\alpha+\beta>1
, Theorem
4.4
yields for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
:

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O\big(n^{\frac{\alpha+(\beta-1)(k+1)}{\alpha}}\big).

In particular, for
k=0
, our bound reads

(21)

\left\|T^{n}\right\|=O(n^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-1}{\alpha}}).

This improves the bounds available in the literature. For instance, in
[
15
, Corollary 3.3, Case 4]
, the authors obtained
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=O\big(n^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-1}{\alpha}}\log(n+1)\big)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{-1}:=\big\{\frac{1}{m},\;m\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and, for
\alpha\notin\mathbb{N}^{-1}
,

\left\|T^{n}\right\|=\begin{cases}O\Big(n^{(\alpha+\beta-1)(1+\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\rfloor)}\Big),&\text{if }\frac{1}{\alpha}-\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\rfloor\geq\frac{\alpha+\beta-1}{\alpha},\\
O\Big(n^{\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\rfloor(\beta-1)+1}\Big),&\text{if }\frac{1}{\alpha}-\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\rfloor<\frac{\alpha+\beta-1}{\alpha}.\end{cases}

Our bound (
21
) unifies and improves these estimates for all parameters.

Let
\alpha>1
. If
T
is
\alpha
-Ritt and Kreiss bounded, then for
1<|\lambda|\leq 2
and any
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have

\left\|R(\lambda,T)\right\|=\left\|R(\lambda,T)\right\|^{\gamma}\left\|R(\lambda,T)\right\|^{1-\gamma}\leq\frac{C}{(|\lambda|-1)^{\gamma}|\lambda-1|^{\alpha(1-\gamma)}}.

It follows that
T
is
(\alpha(1-\gamma),\gamma)
-RK bounded for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
Moreover, if
T
is
(\alpha(1-\gamma),\gamma)
-RK bounded for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
, then Lemma
4.3
implies that
T
is
\alpha
-Ritt. Consequently, for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have the following chain of implications:

(22)

\text{ $\alpha$-Ritt and Kreiss bounded}\implies\text{ $(\alpha(1-\gamma),\gamma)$-RK bounded}\implies\text{ $\alpha$-Ritt}.

In view of Remark
21
and the discussion above, we can provide a weaker condition than being both
\alpha
-Ritt and Kreiss bounded that still ensures the rate
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O\big(n^{\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}}\big)
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\alpha>1
and let
\gamma\in(0,1)
satisfy
\gamma>\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}
. Assume that
T
is
(\alpha(1-\gamma),\gamma)
-RK bounded. Then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O\big(n^{\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}}\big).

Remark 4.8
.

If
1\leq\alpha<2
and
0<\beta<1
, it follows from the Theorem
4.4
(with
k=1
) that if
T
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK, then

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)\right\|=O\Big(n^{\frac{\alpha+2\beta-2}{\alpha}}\Big).

In
[
15
, Theorem 3.4]
, the best bounds they obtain for this case are:

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)\right\|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O\big(n^{2\beta-1}\log(n+1)\big)&\mbox{if }\alpha=1\\
O(n^{\beta})&\mbox{if }1<\alpha<2\text{ and }3-2\alpha\leq\beta<1\\
O(n^{2\alpha+2\beta-3})&\mbox{if }1<\alpha<2\text{ and }0<\beta<3-2\alpha\\
\end{array}\right.

In all cases, our bounds are better, as we have the following comparisons: For
1<\alpha<2
and
0<\beta<1

\frac{\alpha+2\beta-2}{\alpha}-\beta=\Big(\frac{2}{\alpha}-1\Big)(\beta-1)<0

and

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha+2\beta-2}{\alpha}-(2\alpha+2\beta-3)=\frac{-2(\alpha-1)(\alpha+\beta-1)}{\alpha}<0.

Finally, in the case
\alpha=1
, the power rate in
n
is the same as in
[
15
]
, but our estimate removes the logarithmic term.

To conclude this section, we illustrate our results for the specific case
\alpha=2
. Recall that from (
22
), for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have the hierarchy:

T\text{ is $2$-Ritt and Kreiss bounded}\implies T\text{ is $(2(1-\gamma),\gamma)$-RK bounded}\implies T\text{ is $2$-Ritt}.

According to Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
4.4
, the asymptotic behavior of
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|
can be categorized as follows:

•

Case 1:
If
T
is
2
-Ritt and Kreiss bounded, or if
T
is
(2(1-\gamma),\gamma)
-RK bounded for
\frac{1}{2}<\gamma<1
, then

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O\big(n^{\frac{1-k}{2}}\big),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

•

Case 2:
If
T
is
(1,1/2)
-RK, the rate at the boundary
k=0
involves a logarithmic factor:

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=\begin{cases}O(n^{1/2}\log(n+1)),&\text{if }k=0,\\
O\big(n^{\frac{1-k}{2}}\big),&\text{if }k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

•

Case 3:
If
T
is
(2(1-\gamma),\gamma)
-RK with
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
, then since
2(1-\gamma)>1
and
2(1-\gamma)+\gamma-1=1-\gamma
, we find:

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=\begin{cases}O(n^{1-\gamma}),&\text{if }k=0,\\
O\big(n^{\frac{1-k}{2}}\big),&\text{if }k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

•

Case 4:
Finally, if
T
is merely a
2
-Ritt operator, the estimates are less restrictive:

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=\begin{cases}O(n),&\text{if }k=0,\\
O(\log(n+1)),&\text{if }k=1,\\
O\big(n^{\frac{1-k}{2}}\big),&\text{if }k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}.\end{cases}

5.
Focus on
\beta
-Kreiss bounded operator

We recall that a
\beta
-Kreiss bounded operator is a
(0,\beta)
-RK operator; that is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(23)

\left\|R(\lambda,T)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{(|\lambda|-1)^{\beta}},\quad 1<|\lambda|<2.

The smallest constant
C
satisfying (
23
) is denoted by
K_{\beta}(T)
. The case
\beta<1
is of less interest in this context as it implies
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=O(e^{-\omega n})
for some
\omega>0
. The case
\beta=1
corresponds to the classical Kreiss boundedness condition.

By Theorem
4.4
, we know that if
T
is
\alpha
-Ritt for
\alpha>1
, then

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O(n^{\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}}),\quad\text{for }k>\alpha-1.

When
T
is further assumed to be Kreiss bounded (
\beta=1
), this bound also holds for integers
k\leq\alpha-1
. This leads us to investigate the growth of
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|
for
k<\alpha-1
when
T
is both
\alpha
-Ritt and
\beta
-Kreiss bounded with
\beta>1
. Since
\alpha
-Ritt boundedness implies
\alpha
-Kreiss boundedness, we naturally assume
1<\beta<\alpha
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
1<\beta<\alpha
. Suppose
T
is
\alpha
-Ritt and
\beta
-Kreiss bounded. Then for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k<\alpha-1
,

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O\left(n^{\beta\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}}\right).

Proof.

Let
k<\alpha-1
, so that
k-\alpha<-1
. Following the method from Theorem
1.2
, we write:

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\Gamma^{\prime}_{n}(\theta)||1-\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|^{k}\left\|R(\Gamma_{n}(\theta),T)\right\|d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{|\theta|\leq\tilde{\theta}_{n}}\dots d\theta+\int_{\tilde{\theta}_{n}<|\theta|\leq\pi}\dots d\theta

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where we set
\tilde{\theta}_{n}:=cn^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}
with
c<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
. We recall that for
0\leq|\theta|\geq\theta_{n}=cn^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
we have
|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|>1
and
|\Gamma_{n}(\theta)|-1\leq\frac{1-c^{2}}{n}
, so that, since
\tilde{\theta}_{n}\leq\theta_{n}
, these estimations are also valid for
0\leq|\theta|\leq\tilde{\theta}_{n}
. Using the
\beta
-Kreiss condition:

I_{1}\leq Cn^{\beta}\int_{0}^{\tilde{\theta}_{n}}\left(\theta+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{k}d\theta\leq C^{\prime}n^{\beta}\tilde{\theta}_{n}^{k+1}=C_{1}n^{\beta-\beta\frac{k+1}{\alpha}}=C_{1}n^{\beta\frac{\alpha-k-1}{\alpha}}.

where
C_{1}
depends on
\alpha,K_{\beta}(T),c
and
k
.
For the second integral, using the
\alpha
-Ritt condition:

I_{2}\leq C\int_{\tilde{\theta}_{n}}^{\pi}\theta^{k-\alpha}d\theta\leq C_{2}\tilde{\theta}_{n}^{k-\alpha+1}=C_{2}\left(n^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\right)^{k-\alpha+1}=C_{2}n^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(\alpha-k-1)},

where
C_{2}
depends on
\alpha,c
and
k
.
Both terms yield the same rate, which concludes the proof.
∎

5.1.
Connection to generalized Cesàro boundedness

One of the central themes in the ergodic theory of
T
concerns the convergence and the growth properties of various means of the discrete semigroup
\{T^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. For
\gamma>-1
, the Cesàro means of order
\gamma
(or Cesàro-
\gamma
means) are defined by

(24)

S_{n}^{\gamma}(T):=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{n-j}^{\gamma-1}T^{j},

where the binomial coefficients
A_{n}^{\gamma}
are given by the generating relation
(1-z)^{-(\gamma+1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}^{\gamma}z^{n}
for
|z|<1
. These coefficients satisfy
A_{n}^{\gamma}=\binom{n+\gamma}{n}=\frac{(\gamma+1)(\gamma+2)\dots(\gamma+n)}{n!}
and behave asymptotically as
A_{n}^{\gamma}\sim\frac{n^{\gamma}}{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}
for
n\to\infty
.

Traditionally, an operator
T
is called Cesàro-
\gamma
bounded if
\|S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)\|=O(n^{\gamma})
. In the context of
\beta
-Kreiss bounded operators, it is natural to investigate a more general condition where the growth rate also depends on the parameter
\beta
. This leads us to the following definition, which generalizes the classical notion.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
\beta\geq 1
. An operator
T\in B(X)
is said to be
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}
-bounded if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)\|\leq Cn^{\gamma+\beta-1},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

We denote by
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}(T)
the smallest constant
C
satisfying (
5.2
). When
\beta=1
, we recover the classical Cesàro-
\gamma
boundedness.

Remark 5.3
.

In the following,
C_{1}^{(\beta)}
-boundedness will play a central role. It is worth noting the connection between
C_{1}^{(\beta)}
-boundedness and the classical Cesàro-
\beta
boundedness. Indeed, using the property
S_{n}^{\gamma+\delta+1}(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{n-j}^{\delta}S_{j}^{\gamma}(T)
, we can write:

S_{n}^{1}(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{n-j}^{-\beta}S_{j}^{\beta}(T).

If we assume that
T
is Cesàro-
\beta
bounded (i.e.,
\|S_{j}^{\beta}(T)\|\leq Cj^{\beta}
), it follows that:

\|S_{n}^{1}(T)\|\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{n}|A_{n-j}^{-\beta}|j^{\beta}\leq Cn^{\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{n}|A_{n-j}^{-\beta}|\leq Cn^{\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|A_{j}^{-\beta}|.

For
\beta>1
,
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|A_{j}^{-\beta}|<\infty
according to
[
22
,
(1\cdot 16)
p.77]
. Consequently, any Cesàro-
\beta
bounded operator is necessarily
C_{1}^{(\beta)}
-bounded.

Our first result connects
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}
-boundedness to Kreiss boundedness.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\beta,\gamma\geq 1
and let
T\in B(X)
be
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}
-bounded. Then
T
is
\gamma+\beta
-Kreiss bounded.

Proof.

Let
f(\lambda):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n}A^{\gamma}_{n-j}T^{j}\Big)\lambda^{-n}
. Then

\displaystyle f(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}A^{\gamma-1}_{n-j}T^{j}\lambda^{-n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}T^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A^{\gamma-1}_{n}\lambda^{-n-j}=\lambda\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{T^{j}}{\lambda^{j}}\Big)\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A^{\gamma-1}_{n}\lambda^{-n}\Big)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\gamma+1}R(\lambda,T)(\lambda-1)^{-\gamma}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{|\lambda|^{\gamma+1}}{|\lambda-1|^{\gamma}}\left\|R(\lambda,T)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|f(\lambda)\right\|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\|S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)\right\||\lambda|^{-n}\leq C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}(T)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{\gamma+\beta-1}|\lambda|^{-n}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}^{\gamma+\beta-1}=C\frac{|\lambda|^{\gamma+\beta}}{(|\lambda|-1)^{\gamma+\beta}},

with
C
depending only
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}(T)
. Finally we obtain

\left\|R(\lambda,T)\right\|\leq C\frac{|\lambda|^{\beta-1}|\lambda-1|^{\gamma}}{(|\lambda|-1)^{\gamma+\beta}}

which gives that
T
is
\gamma+\beta
-Kreiss bounded.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

If
T\in B(X)
is Cesàro-
\gamma
bounded for some
\gamma>0
, it is well known that
\|T^{n}\|=O(n^{\gamma})
. However, if
T
is additionally assumed to be
\alpha
-Ritt, combining Propositions
5.1
and
5.4
yields the estimate

\|T^{n}\|=O\left(n^{(\gamma+1)\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\right).

In particular, this ensures that
\|T^{n}\|=o(n^{\gamma})
provided that
\alpha<\gamma+1
.

Conversely the next result establishes that
\beta
-Kreiss boundedness is a sufficient condition for
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}
-boundedness for any
\gamma>1
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
T
be a
\beta
-Kreiss bounded operator with constant
K_{\beta}(T)
. Then for each
\gamma>1
,
T
is
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}
-bounded. Furthermore
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}(T)
depends only on
\gamma
and
K_{\beta}(T)
.

Proof.

Let
0<\rho<1
. By the Cauchy inversion formula and the generating function identity

(25)

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{m}S_{m}^{\gamma}(T)=(1-z)^{-\gamma}(I-zT)^{-1},\quad|z|<1,

the means
S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)
can be represented as:

\displaystyle 2\pi\rho^{n}S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(1-\rho e^{it})^{-\gamma}e^{int}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\rho e^{it})^{j}T^{j}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{i(n+1)t}(1-\rho e^{it})^{-\gamma}R\Big(\frac{e^{it}}{\rho},T\Big)\,dt.

The
\beta
-Kreiss boundedness of
T
implies
\|R(\lambda,T)\|\leq K_{\beta}(T)(|\lambda|-1)^{-\beta}
for
|\lambda|>1
. Setting
\lambda=e^{it}/\rho
, we have
|\lambda|-1=\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}
, which yields:

\displaystyle\|S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K_{\beta}(T)}{2\pi\rho^{n+1}(\frac{1}{\rho}-1)^{\beta}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{|\rho e^{it}-1|^{\gamma}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{\beta}(T)}{2\pi\rho^{n+1-\beta+\gamma}(1-\rho)^{\beta}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{|\frac{e^{it}}{\rho}-1|^{\gamma}}\,dt.

Using Lemma (
1.3
), for
\gamma>1
and
r=1/\rho>1
, we have

(26)

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|1-re^{it}|^{-\gamma}dt\leq C_{\gamma}(r-1)^{1-\gamma}.

We arrive at

\|S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)\|\leq\frac{\rho^{\beta-1}C_{\gamma}K_{\beta}(T)}{2\pi\rho^{n+1}(1-\rho)^{\beta-1+\gamma}}.

Since
\rho^{\beta-1}\leq 1
for
0<\rho<1
, taking
\rho=1-\frac{1}{n}
, we obtain
\|S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)\|=O(n^{\gamma+\beta-1})
, which proves (
5.2
).
∎

Remark 5.7
.

(1)

If
T
is
\beta
-Kreiss bounded, the limit case
\gamma=1
leads to

\|S^{1}_{n}(T)\|=O\big(n^{\beta}\log(n+1)\big).

We show in Section
6.2
that this behavior is optimal. Specifically, we provide an example of a
\beta
-Kreiss bounded operator for which

\|S^{1}_{n}(T)\|\geq C^{-1}n^{\beta}\log(n+1).

(2)

If
T
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK with
\alpha>0
, the same argument shows that for
\gamma\geq 1
:

\|S_{n}^{\gamma}(T)\|\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{n+1-\beta+\gamma}(\rho-1)^{\beta}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{|\frac{e^{it}}{\rho}-1|^{\alpha+\gamma}}\,dt.

In particular, for
\gamma=1
, using (
26
) and
\rho=1-\frac{1}{n}
, we find
\|S_{n}^{1}(T)\|=O(n^{\beta+\alpha})
. Thus, any
(\alpha,\beta)
-RK operator is
C_{1}^{(\alpha+\beta)}
-bounded. This implies that
\alpha
-Ritt operators (
\alpha>1
) are
C_{1}^{(\alpha)}
-bounded, while for
\alpha=1
(Ritt case),
T
is power-bounded and thus Cesàro bounded.

Proposition 5.8
.

For an operator
T\in B(X)
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

T
is
\beta
-Kreiss bounded.

(2)

For each
\gamma>1
,
\lambda T
is
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}
-bounded uniformly for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
, i.e.,

(27)

\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}(\lambda T)<\infty.

(3)

Condition (
27
) holds for at least one
\gamma>1
.

Proof.

(i)\implies(ii)
: According to Proposition
5.6
, this follows from the fact that
K_{\beta}(\lambda T)=K_{\beta}(T)
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
, since the constant
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}(\lambda T)
depends only on
\alpha
and
K_{\beta}(\lambda T)
.

(ii)\implies(iii)
is trivial.

To prove
(iii)\implies(i)
, let
\lambda=re^{i\theta}
with
r>1
and
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
. Using the identity (
25
), we can write the resolvent as:

\displaystyle R(re^{i\theta},T)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\theta}R(r,e^{-i\theta}T)=e^{-i\theta}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(e^{-i\theta}T)^{j}}{r^{j+1}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{e^{-i\theta}}{r}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{S_{j}^{\gamma}(e^{-i\theta}T)}{r^{j}}.

By assumption
(iii)
, there exists
\gamma>1
such that
\lambda T
is
C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}
-bounded uniformly in
\mathbb{T}
. Let
M=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}C_{\gamma}^{(\beta)}(\lambda T)
. Then, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\|S_{j}^{\gamma}(e^{-i\theta}T)\|\leq M(j+1)^{\gamma+\beta-1}
.

Recall that for
\delta\geq 0
and
|z|<1
, we have the power series
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}z^{j}A_{j}^{\delta}=(1-z)^{-(\delta+1)}
and the comparison
(j+1)^{\delta}\leq D_{\delta}A_{j}^{\delta}
. It follows that:

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda,T)\|

\displaystyle\leq M\frac{(r-1)^{\gamma}}{r^{\gamma+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(j+1)^{\gamma+\beta-1}}{r^{j}}\leq C\frac{(r-1)^{\gamma}}{r^{\gamma+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{A_{j}^{\gamma+\beta-1}}{r^{j}}

\displaystyle=C\frac{(r-1)^{\gamma}}{r^{\gamma+1}}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)^{-(\gamma+\beta)}=MD_{\beta}\frac{(r-1)^{\gamma}}{r^{\gamma+1}}\frac{r^{\gamma+\beta}}{(r-1)^{\gamma+\beta}}=C\frac{r^{\beta-1}}{(r-1)^{\beta}},

where
C
depends on
\gamma,\beta
and
M
. Thus we obtain:

\|R(\lambda,T)\|\leq\frac{K}{(|\lambda|-1)^{\beta}},\quad 1<|\lambda|<2.

This shows that
T
is
\beta
-Kreiss bounded, concluding the proof.
∎

5.2.
Uniform
\beta
-Kreiss boundedness

A strengthened condition of Kreiss boundedness was introduced in
[
17
]
, known as
uniform Kreiss boundedness
. It is well established that an operator
T
is uniformly Kreiss bounded if and only if

\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|(n+1)^{-1}S^{1}_{n}(\lambda T)\right\|<\infty.

In this section, we extend this concept to the
\beta
-order case.

Definition 5.9
.

An operator
T\in B(X)
is said to be uniformly
\beta
-Kreiss bounded if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(28)

\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{T^{j}}{\lambda^{j+1}}\right\|\leq\frac{C}{(|\lambda|-1)^{\beta}},\quad\text{for all }1<|\lambda|<2\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}.

We denote by
K_{U}^{(\beta)}(T)
the smallest constant
C
satisfying (
28
).

Following the approach in
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
9
, Theorem 2]
, we provide a characterization of this property in terms of uniform Cesàro means.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
T\in B(X)
and
\beta\geq 1
. Then
T
is uniformly
\beta
-Kreiss bounded if and only if
T
is uniformly
C_{1}^{(\beta)}
-bounded on the unit circle, i.e.,

(29)

\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|(n+1)^{-\beta}S^{1}_{n}(\lambda T)\right\|<\infty.

Proof.

Assume first that
T
is uniformly
\beta
-Kreiss bounded. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and
r>1
. By applying Abel’s summation formula, we have:

S_{n}^{1}(\lambda T)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(\lambda T)^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}r^{j}\frac{(\lambda T)^{j}}{r^{j}}=r^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(\lambda T)^{j}}{r^{j}}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(r^{j}-r^{j+1})\sum_{l=0}^{j}\frac{(\lambda T)^{l}}{r^{l}}.

Using the assumption (
28
) (noting that
\left\|\sum\frac{(\lambda T)^{j}}{r^{j}}\right\|\leq K_{U}^{(\beta)}(T)r(r-1)^{-\beta}
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\left\|S_{n}^{1}(\lambda T)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq K_{U}^{(\beta)}(T)\left(\frac{r^{n+1}}{(r-1)^{\beta}}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{r^{j+1}(r-1)}{(r-1)^{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{U}^{(\beta)}(T)}{(r-1)^{\beta}}\left(r^{n+1}+(r-1)\frac{r(r^{n}-1)}{r-1}\right)\leq\frac{2K_{U}^{(\beta)}(T)r^{n+1}}{(r-1)^{\beta}}.

Setting
r=1+\frac{1}{n+1}
, we find
\left\|S_{n}^{1}(\lambda T)\right\|\leq C(n+1)^{\beta}
, where
C
is independent of
n
and
\lambda
. Thus, (
29
) holds.

Conversely, assume that
T
satisfies (
29
) with constant
M
. Let
\lambda=re^{i\theta}
with
1<r<2
. Then:

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{T^{j}}{\lambda^{j+1}}=\frac{e^{-i(n+1)\theta}}{r^{n+1}}S^{1}_{n}(e^{-i\theta}T)+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{j+1}}-\frac{1}{r^{j+2}}\right)e^{-i(j+1)\theta}S^{1}_{j}(e^{-i\theta}T).

Applying the bound
\|S_{j}^{1}(e^{-i\theta}T)\|\leq M(j+1)^{\beta}
, we get:

\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{T^{j}}{\lambda^{j+1}}\right\|\leq M\left(\frac{(n+1)^{\beta}}{r^{n+1}}+\frac{r-1}{r}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(j+1)^{\beta}}{r^{j+1}}\right).

The function
x\mapsto x^{\beta}r^{-x}
attains its maximum at
x=\beta/\log(r)
. Using the inequality
\log(r)\geq(r-1)/r
, we have:

\frac{(n+1)^{\beta}}{r^{n}}\leq\left(\frac{\beta}{\log(r)e}\right)^{\beta}\leq\left(\frac{\beta r}{e}\right)^{\beta}\frac{1}{(r-1)^{\beta}}.

Furthermore, using the comparison with binomial coefficients
(j+1)^{\beta}\leq D_{\beta}A_{j}^{\beta}
, we have:

\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(j+1)^{\beta}}{r^{j+1}}\leq\frac{D_{\beta}}{r}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{A_{j}^{\beta}}{r^{j}}=\frac{D_{\beta}}{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)^{-(\beta+1)}=\frac{D_{\beta}r^{\beta}}{(r-1)^{\beta+1}}.

Combining these estimates, we obtain:

\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{T^{j}}{\lambda^{j+1}}\right\|\leq\frac{C}{(|\lambda|-1)^{\beta}},\quad 1<|\lambda|<2,

the constant
C
is independent of
n
and
\lambda
. This concludes the proof.
∎

5.3.
The case of positive operators

In this section, we focus on the class of positive operators. A well-known result (see e.g.,
[
6
, Proposition 5.13.]
) states that for a positive operator
T\in B(X)
, Cesàro boundedness is equivalent to Kreiss boundedness. Outside the setting of positive operators, this equivalence fails: there exist operators that are Cesàro bounded but not Kreiss bounded, and vice versa. We now establish an analogous result within our
\beta
-framework.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
X
be a Banach lattice and let
T\in B(X)
be a positive operator. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

T
is
C_{1}^{(\beta)}
-bounded.

(2)

T
is uniformly
\beta
-Kreiss bounded.

(3)

T
is
\beta
-Kreiss bounded.

Proof.

(i)\implies(ii)
: Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and
x\in X
. Since
T
is positive, we have for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(\lambda T)^{j}x\right|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}|\lambda|^{j}T^{j}|x|=\sum_{j=0}^{n}T^{j}|x|.

Taking the norm and using the fact that
\||x|\|=\|x\|
in a Banach lattice, we obtain:

\|S_{n}^{1}(\lambda T)x\|\leq\|S_{n}^{1}(T)|x|\|\leq C_{1}^{(\beta)}(T)(n+1)^{\beta}\|x\|.

This shows that
\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|(n+1)^{-\beta}S_{n}^{1}(\lambda T)\|<\infty
. By Proposition
5.10
,
T
is uniformly
\beta
-Kreiss bounded.

(ii)\implies(iii)
is immediate from the definitions.

(iii)\implies(i)
: Since
T
is positive, for any
x\in X_{+}
and any
r>1
, we have:

S_{n}^{1}(T)x=\sum_{j=0}^{n}T^{j}x\leq r^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{T^{j}}{r^{j+1}}x\leq r^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{T^{j}}{r^{j+1}}x=r^{n+1}R(r,T)x.

Taking the norm and applying the
\beta
-Kreiss boundedness of
T
:

\|S_{n}^{1}(T)x\|\leq r^{n+1}\|R(r,T)\|\|x\|\leq K_{\beta}(T)\frac{r^{n+1}}{(r-1)^{\beta}}\|x\|.

By choosing
r=1+\frac{1}{n+1}
, we obtain:

\|S_{n}^{1}(T)\|\leq K_{\beta}(T)\left(1+\frac{1}{n+1}\right)^{n+1}(n+1)^{\beta}\leq eK_{\beta}(T)(n+1)^{\beta},

which concludes the proof that
T
is
C_{1}^{(\beta)}
-bounded.
∎

5.4.
Sharpness of the power growth for
\beta
-Kreiss bounded operators

Recall that if
T
is a Kreiss bounded operator on a Hilbert space, then

\left\|T^{n}\right\|=o(n).

Since a
\beta
-Kreiss bounded operator
T
satisfies
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=O(n^{\beta})
, we are naturally led to the following question:

Question 5.12
.

Does every
\beta
-Kreiss bounded operator on a Hilbert space satisfy
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=o(n^{\beta})
?

In
[
3
]
, the authors constructed, for each
0<\epsilon<1
, a uniformly Kreiss bounded operator satisfying
\left\|T^{n}\right\|\geq Cn^{1-\epsilon}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Our approach to Question
5.12
relies on this construction; in what follows, we present it while extending it to the
\beta
-Kreiss setting, thereby providing a negative answer to this question.

Let
\ell^{2}_{N}
denote the space
\mathbb{C}^{2N}
endowed with the norm
\left\|(x(j))_{j=1}^{2N}\right\|_{2}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2N}|x(j)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
. For
j\in\{1,\dots,2N\}
, let
e_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{2N}
be the standard basis vectors. For
r>0
, we define the weights:

\omega_{j}=\begin{cases}j^{r},&\text{if }j=1,\dots,N,\\
\frac{N^{2r}}{(2N-j+1)^{r}},&\text{if }j=N+1,\dots,2N,\end{cases}

and consider the weighted shift
T_{N}
on
\ell^{2}_{N}
given by:

T_{N}e_{j}=\begin{cases}\frac{\omega_{j+1}}{\omega_{j}}e_{j+1},&\text{if }j=1,\dots,2N-1,\\
0,&\text{if }j=2N.\end{cases}

Since
\left\|T_{N}^{m}\right\|=\max_{1\leq j\leq 2N-m}\{\frac{\omega_{j+m}}{\omega_{j}}\}
, it follows that:

\left\|T_{N}^{m}\right\|=\begin{cases}(m+1)^{r},&\text{if }m=1,\dots,N,\\
\frac{N^{2r}}{(2N-m)^{2r}},&\text{if }m=N+1,\dots,2N-1.\end{cases}

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the norm of the Cesàro mean is given by:

\|S^{1}_{n}(T_{N})\|=\sup\left\{\,|\langle S^{1}_{n}(T_{N})x,y\rangle|:x\in\ell^{2}_{N},y\in\ell^{2}_{N},\ \|x\|_{2}=\|y\|_{2}=1\right\}.

Let
x=\sum_{j=1}^{2N}\alpha_{j}e_{j}
and
y=\sum_{j=1}^{2N}\beta_{j}e_{j}
satisfy
\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1
and
\left\|y\right\|_{2}=1
. We decompose
x=x_{1}+x_{2}
and
y=y_{1}+y_{2}
where
x_{1},y_{1}
are supported on
\{1,\dots,N\}
and
x_{2},y_{2}
on
\{N+1,\dots,2N\}
. Since
\langle S^{1}_{n}(T_{N})x_{2},y_{1}\rangle=0
, we have:

|\langle S^{1}_{n}(T_{N})x,y\rangle|\leq A_{n}+B_{n}+C_{n},

where
A_{n}:=\bigl|\langle S^{1}_{n}(T_{N})x_{1},y_{1}\rangle\bigr|
,
B_{n}:=\bigl|\langle S^{1}_{n}(T_{N})x_{2},y_{2}\rangle\bigr|
, and
C_{n}:=\bigl|\langle S^{1}_{n}(T_{N})x_{1},y_{2}\rangle\bigr|
. Following
[
3
]
, we have:

A_{n}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{j^{\prime}=j}^{\min(N,j+n)}|\alpha_{j}||\beta_{j^{\prime}}|\Big(\frac{j^{\prime}}{j}\Big)^{r},\quad B_{n}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{j^{\prime}=\max(1,j-n)}^{j}|\alpha_{2N-j+1}||\beta_{2N-j^{\prime}+1}|\left(\frac{j^{\prime}}{j}\right)^{r}

and

C_{n}\leq\begin{cases}N^{2r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}j^{-2r}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{N}l^{-2r}\right)^{1/2},&\text{if }n+1\geq\frac{N}{2},\\
2\left(\sum_{j=N-n+1}^{N}|\alpha_{j}|\right)\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}=N+1}^{N+n}|\beta_{j^{\prime}}|\right),&\text{if }n+1<\frac{N}{2}.\end{cases}

We observe that:

(30)

\left(\sum_{j=N-n+1}^{N}|\alpha_{j}|\right)\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}=N+1}^{N+n}|\beta_{j^{\prime}}|\right)\leq(n+1)^{1/2}\left\|x_{1}\right\|_{2}(n+1)^{1/2}\left\|y_{2}\right\|_{2}\leq n+1.

Let
V
be the unilateral weighted backward shift on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
, defined by
Vf_{1}:=0
and
Vf_{k}:=\big(\frac{k}{k-1}\big)^{r}f_{k-1}
for
k>1
. Let
z=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z_{j}f_{j}
with
\left\|z\right\|_{2}=1
. Then, as shown in
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\|V^{j}z\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=j+1}^{\infty}|z_{l}|^{2}\left(\frac{l}{l-j}\right)^{2r}\leq\sum_{l=1}^{2n}|z_{l}|^{2}{l}^{2r}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}(l-j)^{-{2r}}+2^{2r}(n+1).

Since

(31)

\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}(l-j)^{-{2r}}=\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}j^{-{2r}}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{l^{1-2r}}{1-2r}&\text{if }0<r<1/2,\\
1+\frac{1}{2r-1}&\text{if }r>1/2,\end{cases}

it follows that:

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\|V^{j}z\|_{2}^{2}\leq\begin{cases}\big(\frac{1}{1-2r}+2^{2r}\big)(n+1)&\text{if }0<r<1/2,\\
\big(\frac{1}{2r-1}+1+2^{2r}\big)(n+1)^{2r}&\text{if }r>1/2.\end{cases}

By Jensen’s inequality, we have:

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\|V^{j}z\|_{2}\leq(n+1)\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\|V^{j}z\|^{2}_{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\begin{cases}C(n+1)&\text{if }0<r<1/2,\\
C(n+1)^{r+1/2}&\text{if }r>1/2,\end{cases}

where
C
depends on
r
. Since
\left\|S^{1}_{n}(V)z\right\|_{2}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\|V^{j}z\|_{2}
, it follows that
V
is Cesàro bounded if
0<r<1/2
and
C_{1}^{(r+1/2)}
-bounded if
r>1/2
. In particular, if
r>1/2
, a simple comparison shows that
2r>r+1-1/2
, and therefore
V
is
C_{1}^{(2r)}
-bounded.

The adjoint
V^{*}
, acting on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
, is given by the unilateral weighted forward shift
V^{*}f_{j}=\left(\frac{j+1}{j}\right)^{r}f_{j+1}
. For
u=\sum\delta_{j}f_{j}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
and
v=\sum\gamma_{j}f_{j}\in\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})
with
\|u\|_{2}=\|v\|_{2}=1
and non-negative coefficients, there exists a constant
C
such that:

(32)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j^{\prime}=j}^{\min(N,j+n)}\delta_{j}\gamma_{j^{\prime}}\left(\frac{j^{\prime}}{j}\right)^{r}=|\langle S^{1}_{n}(V^{*})u,v\rangle|\leq\|S^{1}_{n}(V)\|\leq\begin{cases}C(n+1)&\text{if }0<r<1/2,\\
C(n+1)^{2r}&\text{if }r>1/2.\end{cases}

Applying (
32
), we immediately obtain:

A_{n}\leq\begin{cases}C(n+1),&\text{if }0<r<1/2,\\
C(n+1)^{2r},&\text{if }r>1/2,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{n}\leq\begin{cases}C(n+1),&\text{if }0<r<1/2,\\
C(n+1)^{2r},&\text{if }r>1/2.\end{cases}

Furthermore, by applying (
31
) for
n+1\geq\frac{N}{2}
and (
30
) for
n+1<\frac{N}{2}
, we have:

C_{n}\leq\begin{cases}C(n+1),&\text{if }0<r<1/2,\\
C(n+1)^{2r},&\text{if }r>1/2.\end{cases}

This demonstrates that
T_{N}
is Cesàro bounded if
0<r<1/2
and
C_{1}^{(2r)}
-bounded if
r>1/2
.

Given
\beta>0
, set
r=\beta/2
. On the space

\mathcal{H}:=\bigoplus_{N=1}^{\infty}\ell^{2}_{N}=\left\{x=(x_{N})_{N\geq 1}:x_{N}\in\ell^{2}_{N}\text{ and }\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}:=\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\|x_{N}\|_{2}^{2}<\infty\right\},

we define the block-diagonal operator
T
by:

T=\bigoplus_{N=1}^{\infty}T_{N},

which acts on any
x=(x_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{H}
as
T(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)=(T_{1}x_{1},T_{2}x_{2},\dots)
.

The block-diagonal structure of
T
implies that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, its
n
-th power and its first-order Cesàro mean are also block-diagonal, specifically:

T^{n}=\bigoplus_{N=1}^{\infty}T_{N}^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{n}^{1}(T)=\bigoplus_{N=1}^{\infty}S_{n}^{1}(T_{N}).

Consequently, the norm of these operators can be computed as the supremum of the norms of their components:

\|T^{n}\|=\sup_{N\geq 1}\|T_{N}^{n}\|\quad\text{and}\quad\|S_{n}^{1}(T)\|=\sup_{N\geq 1}\|S_{n}^{1}(T_{N})\|.

Then
T\in B(\mathcal{H})
and:

\|S^{1}_{n}(T)\|=\sup_{N}\|S^{1}_{n}(T_{N})\|\leq\begin{cases}C(n+1),&\text{if }\beta<1,\\
C(n+1)^{\beta},&\text{if }\beta>1.\end{cases}

Now assume that
\beta>1
. Since
T
is positive, Proposition
5.11
implies
T
is uniformly
\beta
-Kreiss bounded. Finally, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
:

\|T^{2N-1}\|\geq\|T_{N}^{2N-1}\|=N^{\beta}=\frac{1}{2^{\beta}}(2N)^{\beta}

and

\|T^{2N}\|\geq\|T_{N+1}^{2N}\|\geq\frac{\|T_{N+1}^{2N+1}\|}{\|T_{N+1}\|}>\frac{1}{2^{3\beta/2}}(2N+1)^{\beta}.

This proves
\|T^{N}\|\geq C(N+1)^{\beta}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, answering Question
5.12
negatively:

Proposition 5.13
.

For each
\beta>1
, there exists a positive, uniformly
\beta
-Kreiss bounded operator
T_{\beta}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
such that
\left\|T_{\beta}^{N}\right\|\geq CN^{\beta}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

6.1.
Proof of Proposition
3.5

Let
Y
be the Banach space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
that are continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{Y}=\|f\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{1},

where

\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{|z|\leq 1}|f(z)|\quad\text{and}\quad\|f^{\prime}\|_{1}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f^{\prime}(e^{it})|dt.

It turns out that
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
is an algebra norm, meaning there exists a constant
C_{Y}>0
such that
\|fg\|_{Y}\leq C_{Y}\|f\|_{Y}\|g\|_{Y}
.

Let
T\in B(Y)
be the multiplication operator defined by

(Tf)(z)=m(z)f(z)\quad\text{with}\quad m(z):=\frac{1+z}{2}.

Then
T
is Kreiss bounded and
2
-Ritt.

Indeed let
|\lambda|\geq 1
with
\lambda\neq 1
. For any
f\in Y
, the resolvent operator is given by

R(\lambda,T)f(z)=(\lambda-m(z))^{-1}f(z).

We have
\|R(\lambda,T)f\|_{Y}\leq C_{Y}\|(\lambda-m)^{-1}\|_{Y}\|f\|_{Y}
. Thus, it suffices to estimate

\|(\lambda-m)^{-1}\|_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad\|((\lambda-m)^{-1})^{\prime}\|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\|(\lambda-m)^{-2}\|_{1}.

First, using the residue formula, for
r>1
and
t\in[-\pi,\pi]
, or
r=1
and
t\neq 0
, we have:

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|re^{it}-\frac{1+e^{i\theta}}{2}\right|^{-2}d\theta=\frac{2\pi}{r(r-\cos(t))}.

Consequently, for
|\lambda|>1
, we obtain:

\|(\lambda-m)^{-2}\|_{1}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\lambda-m(e^{i\theta})\right|^{-2}d\theta\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|-1}.

For
\lambda=e^{it}
with
t\neq 0
, we have:

\|(\lambda-m)^{-2}\|_{1}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|e^{it}-m(e^{i\theta})\right|^{-2}d\theta=\frac{2\pi}{1-\cos(t)}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|^{2}}.

Next, we estimate the
L^{\infty}
-norm. For
r>1
and
t,\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, we have:

\displaystyle\left|re^{it}-m(e^{i\theta})\right|^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left|re^{it}-\frac{e^{i\theta}+1}{2}\right|^{-1}=\left|re^{i(t-\frac{\theta}{2})}-\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right|^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\Big|r-cos\Big(\frac{\theta}{2}\Big)\Big|^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{r-1}.

For
t\in[-\pi,\pi]\setminus\{0\}
and
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, we have:

|e^{it}-m(e^{i\theta})|^{-1}=2\left|(2e^{it}-1)-e^{i\theta}\right|^{-1}\leq\frac{2}{\left||2e^{it}-1|-1\right|}\leq\frac{C}{|e^{it}-1|^{2}}.

Therefore, for
|\lambda|>1
,
\|(\lambda-m)^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|-1}
, and for
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{1\}
,
\|(\lambda-m)^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|^{2}}
.

Finally, we obtain:

\|R(\lambda,T)\|_{B(Y)}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|-1},\quad|\lambda|>1,

which shows that
T
is Kreiss bounded, and

\|R(\lambda,T)\|_{B(Y)}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda-1|^{2}},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{1\},

which shows that
T
is
2
-Ritt.

Furthermore, Moreover, it satisfies:

(33)

\frac{1}{C}n^{\frac{1-k}{2}}\leq\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|_{B(Y)}\leq Cn^{\frac{1-k}{2}},\quad k\geq 0

where
C
depends only on
k
.

Indeed by Theorem
1.2
, we have the upper bound:

\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|_{B(Y)}\leq Cn^{\frac{1-k}{2}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

where
C
depends only on
k
. To establish the lower bound, let
\mathbf{1}
denote the constant function equal to
1
on
\mathbb{D}
. Then,

T^{n}(I-T)^{k}(\mathbf{1})(z)=m(z)^{n}(1-m(z))^{k},

so that

\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}(\mathbf{1})\|_{Y}=\|m^{n}(1-m)^{k}\|_{\infty}+\|(m^{n}(1-m)^{k})^{\prime}\|_{1}.

First, we show that:

(34)

\|m^{n}(1-m)^{k}\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{C}n^{-k/2},

with
C
depending only on
k
. We note that
|m(e^{i\theta})^{n}(1-m(e^{i\theta}))^{k}|=|\cos(\theta/2)|^{n}|\sin(\theta/2)|^{k}
so that
\left\|m^{n}(1-m)^{k}\right\|_{\infty}=\underset{\theta\in[0,\pi/2]}{\sup}{\cos^{n}(\theta)\sin^{k}(\theta)}
. Let
g(\theta):=\cos^{n}(\theta)\sin^{k}(\theta)
for
\theta\in[0,\pi/2]
. The function
f(\theta):=\log(g(\theta))=n\log(\cos\theta)+k\log(\sin\theta)
satisfies:

f^{\prime}(\theta)=-n\tan(\theta)+k\cot(\theta),\quad\theta\in(0,\pi/2).

The maximum of
f
(and thus of
g
) is attained at
\theta_{0}=\theta_{0}(n,k)
satisfying
\tan^{2}(\theta_{0})=k/n
. This implies
\cos^{2}(\theta_{0})=\frac{n}{n+k}
and
\sin^{2}(\theta_{0})=\frac{k}{n+k}
. We then have:

\|m^{n}(1-m)^{k}\|_{\infty}=g(\theta_{0})=\left(\frac{n}{n+k}\right)^{n/2}\left(\frac{k}{n+k}\right)^{k/2}=\frac{n^{n/2}k^{k/2}}{(n+k)^{(n+k)/2}},

which yields (

). Moreover, the derivative is given by:

\displaystyle(m^{n}(1-m)^{k})^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=nm^{\prime}(z)m^{n-1}(z)(1-m(z))^{k}-km^{\prime}(z)m^{n}(z)(1-m(z))^{k-1}

\displaystyle=m^{\prime}(z)m^{n-1}(z)(1-m(z))^{k-1}\big(n(1-m(z))-km(z)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1+z}{2}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{1-z}{2}\right)^{k-1}\left(1-\left(1+\frac{k}{n}\right)\frac{z+1}{2}\right),

so that for

(m^{n}(1-m)^{k})^{\prime}(e^{it})=\frac{n}{2}\cos^{n-1}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\sin^{k-1}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\left(1-\left(1+\frac{k}{n}\right)\frac{e^{it}+1}{2}\right).

Since

\displaystyle\left|1-\left(1+\frac{k}{n}\right)\frac{1+e^{it}}{2}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\text{Im}\left(1-\left(1+\frac{k}{n}\right)\frac{1+e^{it}}{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{k}{n}\right)\frac{|\sin(t)|}{2}\geq\frac{|\sin(t)|}{2}=\left|\cos\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right|\left|\sin\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right|,

it follows that:

\displaystyle\|(m^{n}(1-m)^{k})^{\prime}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{2}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|\cos\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right|^{n}\left|\sin\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right|^{k}dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2^{k-1}n}{\pi^{k}}\int_{0}^{\pi}t^{k}\left|\cos\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right|^{n}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k-1}n}{\pi^{k}n^{(k+1)/2}}\int_{0}^{{\pi}\sqrt{n}}t^{k}\left|\cos\left(\frac{t}{2\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|^{n}dt.

As

, the integral converges to
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}e^{-t^{2}/8}dt>0
. Consequently, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
k
such that:

\|(m^{n}(1-m)^{k})^{\prime}\|_{1}\geq\frac{1}{C}n^{\frac{1-k}{2}}.

Combined with (
34
), this establishes the lower bound in (

).

6.2.
An
m
-Kreiss operator which is not

-bounded

Let
\beta>1
and denote by
Y_{m}
the space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
which are continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
up to their
m
-th derivative, endowed with the norm:

\left\|f\right\|_{Y_{m}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left\|f^{(j)}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|f^{(m)}\right\|_{1}.

Note that
\|\cdot\|_{m}
is an algebra norm; thus, there exists a constant
C_{m}>0
such that for all
f,g\in Y_{m}
,
\|fg\|_{m}\leq C_{Y_{m}}\|f\|_{m}\|g\|_{m}
.
The multiplication operator
M_{z}
on
Y_{m}
, defined by
(M_{z}f)(z)=zf(z)
, satisfies:

(i)

M_{z}
is
m
-Kreiss bounded,

(ii)

\|S^{1}_{n}(M_{z})\|_{B(Y_{m})}\geq Cn^{m}\log(n+1)
.

Proof.

(i)
: Let
|\lambda|>1
. The resolvent operator
R(\lambda,M_{z})
is the multiplication operator by the function
g_{\lambda}(z)=(\lambda-z)^{-1}
. We have:

\|R(\lambda,M_{z})f\|_{m}\leq C_{Y_{m}}\|f\|_{Y_{m}}\|(\lambda-z)^{-1}\|_{m}.

To show that
M_{z}
is
m
-Kreiss bounded, we must estimate, for
|\lambda|>1
:

\|(\lambda-z)^{-1}\|_{\infty},\dots,\|(\lambda-z)^{-m}\|_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad\|((\lambda-z)^{-1})^{(m)}\|_{1}.

Note that the
m
-th derivative of
(\lambda-z)^{-1}
is
m!(\lambda-z)^{-(m+1)}
.
Let
\lambda=re^{it}
with
r>1
. For any
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, we have:

|re^{it}-e^{i\theta}|^{-j}\leq(r-1)^{-j}=\frac{1}{(|\lambda|-1)^{j}}.

Furthermore, for the
L^{1}
term, we have, by Lemma (
1.3
):

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|re^{it}-e^{i\theta}|^{-(m+1)}d\theta\leq\frac{C_{m}}{(r-1)^{m}}.

Summing these estimates, we obtain
\|(\lambda-z)^{-1}\|_{Y_{m}}\leq\frac{C}{(|\lambda|-1)^{m}}
, which proves that
M_{z}
is
m
-Kreiss bounded.

(ii)
: To establish the lower bound, we test the operator on the constant function
\mathbf{1}\in Y_{m}
. We have:

\|S_{n}^{1}(M_{z})\|_{B(Y_{m})}\geq\|S_{n}^{1}(M_{z})(\mathbf{1})\|_{m}=\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{n}z^{j}\right\|_{m}\geq\left\|\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}z^{j}\right)^{(m)}\right\|_{1}.

Let
D_{n}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}z^{j}
denote the Dirichlet-type kernel. It is a well-known result in Fourier analysis that the
L^{1}
-norm of the
j
-th derivative of
D_{n}
satisfies:

C^{-1}n^{j}\log(n+1)\leq\|D_{n}^{(j)}\|_{1}\leq Cn^{j}\log(n+1)

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Taking
j=m
, we obtain:

\|S_{n}^{1}(M_{z})\|_{B(Y_{m})}\geq C^{-1}n^{m}\log(n+1),

which completes the proof.
∎

6.3.
On the sharpness of the
m
-Ritt growth estimates

We are interested in
m
-Ritt operators with
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It is known that in this case,

(35)

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O(n^{m-k-1}),\quad k\in\{0,\dots,m-2\}.

As noted in
[
5
]
, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an
m
-dimensional space
X
and an operator
T\in B(X)
that is
m
-Ritt such that

\left\|T^{n}\right\|\leq n^{\alpha-1}.

Indeed, let
T_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
be defined by

T_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
1&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&1&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&1&1\end{pmatrix},

then according to
[
5
, Lemma]
, its resolvent is given by

(\lambda I-T_{m})^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\lambda-1}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)^{2}}&\frac{1}{\lambda-1}&0&\cdots&0\\
\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)^{3}}&\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)^{2}}&\frac{1}{\lambda-1}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&0\\
\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)^{m}}&\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)^{m-1}}&\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)^{m-2}}&\cdots&\frac{1}{\lambda-1}\end{pmatrix},

which shows that
T_{m}
is
m
-Ritt. Furthermore, writing for
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
:

J_{m,n}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\binom{n}{1}&1&0&\cdots&0\\
\binom{n}{2}&\binom{n}{1}&1&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\binom{n}{m-1}&\binom{n}{m-2}&\cdots&\binom{n}{1}&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m},

where
\binom{n}{l}:=0
if
l>n
, we clearly have
\left\|J_{m,n}\right\|\leq Cn^{m-1}
.
Again, according to
[
5
, Lemma]
, for
n\geq m
, we have

T_{m}^{n}=J_{m,n},

and then, for
k\leq m-1
,

(T_{m}-I)^{k}T_{m}^{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
J_{m-k,n}&0\end{array}\right),

which implies that
\left\|(I-T_{m})^{k}T_{m}^{n}\right\|\geq Cn^{m-k-1}
.

This demonstrates that the estimate (
35
) is sharp for
k\leq m-2
.

6.4.
A Cesàro-
m
bounded operator which is not
m
-Kreiss

The Assani example
A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}

provides an example of a Cesàro bounded operator that is not Kreiss bounded. Following this idea, let

B_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
1&-1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&-1&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&1&-1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{(m+1)\times(m+1)}.

Then, by
[
5
, Lemma 3.1 (b)]
, the powers of
B_{m}
are given by:

B_{m}^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}(-1)^{n}&0&\cdots&0\\
(-1)^{n-1}\binom{n}{1}&(-1)^{n}&\cdots&0\\
(-1)^{n-2}\binom{n}{2}&(-1)^{n-1}\binom{n}{1}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&0\\
(-1)^{n-m+1}\binom{n}{m}&(-1)^{n-m+2}\binom{n}{m-1}&\cdots&(-1)^{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Now, since

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{j}{m}=P_{m}(n),

where
P_{m}
is a polynomial of degree
m
, and for each
l\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{j}{m-l}=Q_{m,l}(n),

where
Q_{m,l}
is a polynomial of degree at most
m-1
, it follows that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{n^{m}}{C}\leq\left\|S^{1}_{n}(B_{m})\right\|\leq Cn^{m}.

Consequently,
B_{m}
is Cesàro-
m
bounded, which further implies that it is also
C_{1}^{(m)}
-bounded.

Moreover,
B_{m}
is not
m
-Kreiss bounded. Indeed, according to
[
4
, Theorem 4.2.]
, if
B_{m}
were
m
-Kreiss bounded, it would satisfy:

\left\|B_{m}^{n}\right\|\leq C_{m}n^{m-1},

where
C_{m}
depends only on
m
. However, the explicit form of
B_{m}^{n}
shows that

\left\|B_{m}^{n}\right\|\geq Dn^{m}

for some
D>0
. Therefore,
B_{m}
cannot be
m
-Kreiss bounded.

6.5.
Optimality of the growth bound for
(m,1)
-RK operators

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It is known that if
T
is an
(m,1)
-RK operator, then

(36)

\left\|T^{n}\right\|=O(n^{m}\log(n+1)).

We now provide an example showing that this estimate is sharp.
According to
[
5
]
, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a bounded operator
S_{N}
on
X_{N}:=(\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{N})
which is
(m,1)
-RK with constant
R_{m,1}(S_{N})
depending only on
m
(and thus independent of
N
), such that

\left\|S_{N}^{N}\right\|_{N}\geq CN^{m}\log(N+1),\quad N\geq m+1,

where
C
depends only on
m
. The fact that
R_{m,1}(B_{N})
depends only on
m
follows from
[
5
, Lemma 3.1.]
.
Let
X
be the
\ell^{2}
-direct sum of the spaces
X_{N}
:

X:=\left(\bigoplus_{N=m+1}^{\infty}X_{N}\right)_{\ell^{2}},

endowed with the norm
\left\|x\right\|=\left(\sum_{N=m+1}^{\infty}\left\|x_{N}\right\|_{N}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
. Let
B
be the operator defined on
X
by

S:=\bigoplus_{N=m+1}^{\infty}S_{N}.

Then
S\in B(X)
is
(m,1)
-RK since
\sup_{N}C_{RK}(B_{N})<\infty
. Moreover, for each
N\geq m+1
, we have:

\left\|S^{N}\right\|\geq\left\|S_{N}^{N}\right\|_{N}\geq CN^{m}\log(N+1).

This demonstrates that the estimate (
36
) is sharp.

6.6.
On a generalization of the Tomilov-Zemanek example to higher order Cesàro means

Let
X
be a Banach space. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the space
\mathcal{X}_{N+1}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N}X
equipped with the norm

\|x_{0}\oplus x_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus x_{N}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{N+1}}:=\sqrt{\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left\|x_{j}\right\|^{2}}.

Define the bounded linear operator
\mathcal{T}_{N+1}
on
\mathcal{X}_{N+1}
by the operator matrix:

\mathcal{T}_{N+1}=\begin{pmatrix}T&0&0&\cdots&0\\
I-T&T&0&\cdots&0\\
0&I-T&T&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&I-T&T\end{pmatrix}.

Direct computations show that the
n
-th power of
\mathcal{T}_{N+1}
is given by:

\mathcal{T}_{N+1}^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{0}(T)&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\rho_{1}(T)&\rho_{0}(T)&0&\cdots&0\\
\rho_{2}(T)&\rho_{1}(T)&\rho_{0}(T)&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\rho_{N}(T)&\rho_{N-1}(T)&\rho_{N-2}(T)&\cdots&\rho_{0}(T)\end{pmatrix},

where
\rho_{i}(T):=\binom{n}{i}T^{n-i}(I-T)^{i}
. Following the approach in
[
21
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have the following result:

Proposition 6.1
.

For
N\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{T}_{N+1}
be defined as above. Then:

(1)

T
is power bounded if and only if
\mathcal{T}_{N+1}
is Cesàro-
N
bounded.

(2)

(T^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in the strong operator topology of
X
if and only if the sequence
\big(n^{-N}S_{n}^{N}(\mathcal{T}_{N+1})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in the strong operator topology of of
\mathcal{X}_{N+1}
.

Proof.

We provide the proof for the case
N=2
(the
3\times 3
case). We have:

S^{2}_{n}(\mathcal{T}_{3})=\begin{pmatrix}S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{0}(T))&0&0\\
S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{1}(T))&S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{0}(T))&0\\
S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{2}(T))&S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{1}(T))&S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{0}(T))\end{pmatrix}.

Clearly,
S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{0}(T))=S^{2}_{n}(T)
. For the first off-diagonal term, we compute:

\displaystyle S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{1}(T))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(n-j+1)jT^{j-1}(I-T)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-2j)T^{j}-nT^{n}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j+1)T^{j}-(n+2)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^{j}-nT^{n}

\displaystyle=2S_{n-1}^{2}(T)-(n+2)S_{n-1}^{1}(T)-nT^{n}.

For the second off-diagonal term, we have:

\displaystyle S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{2}(T))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(n-j+1)\frac{j(j-1)}{2}T^{j-2}(I-T)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-3j-1)T^{j}+\frac{n(n-2)}{2}T^{n}

\displaystyle=3\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j+1)T^{j}-(2n+4)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^{j}+\frac{n(n-2)}{2}T^{n}

\displaystyle=3S_{n-1}^{2}(T)-(2n+4)S_{n-1}^{1}(T)+\frac{n(n-2)}{2}T^{n}.

If
T
is power-bounded, the above identities show that
n^{-2}S_{n}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{3})
is uniformly bounded, so
\mathcal{T}_{3}
is Cesàro-
2
bounded.

Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{T}_{3}
is Cesàro-
2
bounded. Let
x\in X
with
\left\|x\right\|=1
. Since

S_{n}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{3})\begin{pmatrix}x\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}S_{n}^{2}(T)x\\
S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{1}(T))x\\
S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{2}(T))x\end{pmatrix},

it follows that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|S_{n}^{2}(T)x\right\|\leq Cn^{2}
,
\left\|S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{1}(T))x\right\|\leq Cn^{2}
, and
\left\|S^{2}_{n}(\rho_{2}(T))x\right\|\leq Cn^{2}
.

The first estimate implies that
T
is Cesàro-
2
bounded. This further implies that
\left\|S^{1}_{n}(T)x\right\|\leq C^{\prime}n^{2}
because
S_{n}^{1}(T)=S_{n}^{2}(T)-S_{n-1}^{2}(T)
. Using the previous expansion of
S_{n}^{2}(\rho_{1}(T))
, we have:

\left\|nS_{n-1}^{1}(T)x\right\|-\left\|2S_{n-1}^{2}(T)x-2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^{j}x\right\|\leq\left\|S_{n}^{2}(\rho_{1}(T))x\right\|\leq Cn^{2},

which leads to:

\left\|nS_{n-1}^{1}(T)x\right\|\leq C^{\prime}\left(n^{2}+\left\|S_{n-1}^{2}(T)x\right\|+\left\|S_{n-1}^{1}(T)x\right\|\right)\leq C^{\prime\prime}n^{2}.

Dividing by
n
, we obtain that
T
is Cesàro bounded (i.e.,
\left\|S_{n}^{1}(T)\right\|=O(n)
).

In the same manner, using the fact that
T
is Cesàro bounded and Cesàro-
2
bounded, it follows that:

\frac{n(n-2)}{2}\left\|T^{n}x\right\|\leq Cn^{2}+3\left\|S_{n-1}^{2}(T)x\right\|+(2n+4)\left\|S_{n-1}^{1}(T)x\right\|\leq C^{\prime\prime\prime}n^{2}.

This proves that
T
is power bounded. Part
(2)
regarding the strong convergence is obtained by following the same steps, replacing the norm estimates with strong operator topology convergence arguments.
∎

Following the ideas of
[
21
]
, we can now construct an operator
\mathcal{T}_{N+1}
such that:

(i)

\left\|\mathcal{T}^{n}_{N+1}\right\|\geq Cn^{N}
,

(ii)

\mathcal{T}_{N+1}
is Cesàro-
N
bounded,

(iii)

\big(n^{-N}S^{N}_{n}(\mathcal{T}_{N+1})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in the strong operator of
\mathcal{X}_{N}
,

(iv)

\big(n^{-N}S^{N}_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{*}_{N+1})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not converge in the in the strong operator of
\mathcal{X}^{*}_{N}
.

Let
T
be the forward shift on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
, which is power-bounded and converges strongly to
0
. Its adjoint
T^{*}
(the backward shift) does not converge strongly. According to the proposition,
\mathcal{T}_{N+1}
satisfies (ii) and (iii). Moreover, the growth condition (i) is satisfied since:

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{N+1}^{n}\right\|\geq\left\|\rho_{N}(T)\right\|\geq\binom{n}{N}\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{D}}\big|\lambda^{n}(1-\lambda)^{d}\big|=2^{d}\binom{n}{N}\geq Cn^{N}.

Finally, for (iv), we observe that
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{N+1}
is given by the upper triangular operator matrix:

\mathcal{T}^{*}_{N+1}=\begin{pmatrix}T^{*}&I-T^{*}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&T^{*}&I-T^{*}&\cdots&0\\
0&0&T^{*}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&I-T^{*}\\
0&0&\cdots&0&T^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

The same arguments used for
\mathcal{T}_{N+1}
apply to this upper triangular structure. Consequently, the result of the Proposition (
6.1
) holds for
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{N+1}
as well. Since
T^{*}
does not converge strongly to
0
, it follows that the sequence
\big(n^{-N}S_{n}^{N}(\mathcal{T}^{*}_{N+1})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not converge in the strong operator of
\mathcal{X}^{*}_{N}
.

7.
Final remarks and open questions

The proves of
[
15
, Theorem 3.2. and 3.4]
allows for the following slight improvement of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
T\in B(X)
with
\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{T}\subset\{1\}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

T
is a Ritt operator;

(ii)

For some (or all)
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha,\beta>0
with
\alpha+\beta=m
, we have

\|R(\lambda,T)^{m}\|\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda-1|^{\alpha}(|\lambda|-1)^{\beta}}
for
1<|\lambda|<2
;

(iii)

T
is power-bounded and, for some (or all)
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\|=O(n^{-k})
.

The implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
is straightforward.

For
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: Recall that by the Riesz-Dunford calculus, for each
r>1
and
\varphi\in H^{\infty}(r\mathbb{D})
, we have

(37)

\varphi^{(m-1)}(T)=\frac{(m-1)!}{2\pi i}\int_{|\lambda|=\rho}\varphi(\lambda)R(\lambda,T)^{m}d\lambda,\quad 1<\rho<r.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k\alpha>2
. For
\varphi_{1}(z)=z^{n+km-1}
and
\varphi_{2}(z)=z^{n+km}-\big(1+\frac{km}{n+1}\Big)z^{n+km-1}
, the derivatives are given by

\varphi^{(km-1)}_{1}(z)=\frac{(n+km-1)!}{n!}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi^{(km-1)}_{2}(z)=\frac{(n+km)!}{(n+1)!}(z^{n+1}-z^{n}).

Using (
37
) and setting
\rho_{1}=1+\frac{1}{n}
and
\rho_{2}=1+\frac{km}{n+1}
, we obtain:

T^{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi\binom{n+km-1}{km-1}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\rho_{1}^{n+km}e^{i(n+km)t}R(\rho_{1}e^{it},T)^{km}dt,

and

T^{n}(I-T)=\frac{1}{2\pi\binom{n+km}{km-1}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\rho_{2}^{n+km+1}e^{i(n+km)t}\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\Big(1+\frac{km}{n+1}\Big)-e^{it}\right)R(\rho_{2}e^{it},T)^{km}dt.

From
[
15
, Lemma 3.1.]
), we have:

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{|(1+\frac{1}{n})e^{it}-1|^{k\alpha}}dt\leq Cn^{k\alpha-1},\quad\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{|e^{it}-1|}{|(1+\frac{km}{n+1})e^{it}-1|^{k\alpha}}dt\leq Cn^{k\alpha-2},

and using the property
\binom{n+l}{l}\geq Cn^{l}
for
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha+\beta=m
, it follows that:

\displaystyle\left\|T^{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-(km-1)}n^{k\beta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{|(1+\frac{1}{n})e^{it}-1|^{k\alpha}}dt

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-km+1}n^{k\beta}n^{k\alpha-1}=Cn^{k(\alpha+\beta-m)}=C,

and

\displaystyle\left\|T^{n}(I-T)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-(km-1)}n^{k\beta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{|e^{it}-1|}{|(1+\frac{km}{n+1})e^{it}-1|^{k\alpha}}dt

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-km+1}n^{k\beta}n^{k\alpha-2}=Cn^{k(\alpha+\beta-m)-1}=\frac{C}{n}.

Regarding
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
: If
T
is power-bounded and
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|=O(n^{-k})
, then the moment inequality (Lemma
2.3
) implies
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)\right\|=O(n^{-1})
. It is well known that a power-bounded operator satisfying this condition is a Ritt operator (see
[
18
, Theorem 4.5.4]
).

Remark 7.2
.

An operator
T\in B(X)
is said to be
\mathcal{R}
-Ritt if the set

\left\{(\lambda-1)R(\lambda,T):|\lambda|>1\right\}

is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded. We refer to
[
13
, Chapter 8]
for a detailed treatment of
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness. Using standard techniques from
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness and adapting classical Ritt theory, one can show that
T
is
\mathcal{R}
-Ritt if and only if the set
\{T^{n}:n\geq 0\}
is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded and the set
\{nT^{n}(I-T):n\geq 1\}
is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded. Consequently, the
\mathcal{R}
-bounded version of Theorem
7.1
also holds. Further details on
\mathcal{R}
-Ritt operators can be found in
[
14
]
.

Concluding Questions

Let
T\in B(X)
be a Kreiss bounded operator, so that the moment inequality (Lemma
2.3
) holds. Assume further that
T
is
\alpha
-Ritt for some
\alpha>1
. According to Nevanlinna’s theorem, we have
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=O(n^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}})
.

Let
f
be a function bounded away from zero such that
\left\|T^{n}\right\|\leq f(n)
. By the moment inequality, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have:

\displaystyle\left\|T^{n}(I-T)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\left\|T^{n}\right\|^{\frac{k-1}{k}}\left\|T^{n}(I-T)^{k}\right\|^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle\leq D_{k}f(n)n^{\frac{\alpha-k-1}{k\alpha}}=D_{k}f(n)n^{\frac{\alpha-1}{k\alpha}}n^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

This implies that for every
\varepsilon>0
,
\left\|T^{n}(I-T)\right\|=O(n^{-1/\alpha}f(n)n^{\varepsilon})
. This observation is particularly relevant for strongly Kreiss bounded operators on
L^{p}
, where
f(n)=\log^{\kappa}(n+1)n^{|1/2-1/p|}
. Note that if the constants satisfy
D_{k}\leq D\cdot k
, then choosing
\log(n)\leq k\leq\log(n+1)
leads to

(38)

\left\|T^{n}(I-T)\right\|=O(f(n)n^{-1/\alpha}\log(n+1)).

This comparison with Seifert’s theorem
[
20
, Corollary 3.1]
motivates the following question:

Question 7.3
.

Under the assumptions that
T
is
\alpha
-Ritt for
\alpha>1
, Kreiss bounded and
\left\|T^{n}\right\|\leq f(n)
for
f
satisfying
\lim_{n\to\infty}f(n)=\infty
with
f(n)=o\big(n^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\big)
, does the estimate (
38
) hold?

Another independent question concerns the sharpness of the estimates obtained in Theorem
1.2
. As recalled in Section 6, Nevanlinna provided an example of a Kreiss bounded and
