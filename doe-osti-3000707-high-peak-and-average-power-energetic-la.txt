Goals, Accomplishments, and Plans
• Major R&D goals:
  • Demonstrate Joule-Level amplification from a Tm:YLF CPA system (Tasks 1.1 – 1.3 in the proposal)
  • Create a conceptual design of ultrashort MWIR and LWIR systems producing 1 – 10 J pulse energies with
 repetition rates of 1 - 10 kHz (Task 3.1)
 • Demonstrate a LWIR OPCPA compressible to fs durations (Task 3.2 and 3.3)

• Accomplishments under these goals:
  • Demonstrated Task 1.1 milestone: Modify short pulse front-end to support broad bandwidth operation
  • Sub-scale probe experiment with samples of the nonlinear crystal types to be used full-scale
 experiments in Task 3.3

• Plans for the next 12 months:
  • Implement spectral shaping of seed source (Task 1.2)
  • Crystal development to improve quality and scale aperture
  • Characterize 10μm beam from probe experiments using ZGP and BGSE with long-wavelength diagnostics
 Project impact: The source that will be developed in year 3 of this project could be used as a compact and short-pulse
 seed for CO2 power amplifiers which could impact laser plasma acceleration drivers by simplifying the architecture of 10
 µm systems and enabling shorter pulse durations.

Facilities impacts
• Plumbing and electrical accommodations for a large aperture Tm:YLF amplifier will
  consolidate space and limit unnecessary beam path. That infrastructure development
  will positively impact future projects in that lab space. Additionally, new diagnostics for
  accurate measurements of energy and spectrum in the LWIR will be acquired which will
  positively impact the overall S&T capabilities of LLNL’s kW technologies laboratory.

 13 Energy.gov/science

Impact to other disciplines
• Success on this project has the potential to improve EUV sources for lithography where
  a Tm:YLF DPSSL alternative to conventional CO2 lasers could be significantly more
  efficient (wall plug efficiency), more compact and easier to use. Furthermore, the
  ponderomotive force scaling makes longer wavelength (MWIR and LWIR) high energy
  lasers attractive for High Harmonic Generation and secondary radiation generation
  processes.
