The invariance principle for inhomogeneous Diophantine approximations
Songzi Li

Abstract
We establish the central limit theorem and the invariance principle for the inhomogeneous Diophantine approximations. The proof employs the cumulant method, which was developed by Björklund and Gorodnik to prove the central limit theorem in the homogeneous setting. Our approach also relies on the effective mixing of expanding translates for high-order correlations on the affine lattice space, extending the previous result by Kim.



Key words
: Invariance principle; Diophantine approximations; homogeneous flows

: Primary 60F17, 11K60; Secondary 37A17

Introduction and main results

In the 1920s, Khintchine
[
11
]
established the celebrated theorem illustrating the Borel-Cantelli property in Diophantine approximations. Since then, various universal limit theorems from probability theory have been demonstrated to hold under various conditions in Diophantine geometry. In this work, we extend the central limit theorem (CLT) and the invariance principle (IP) to the inhomogeneous, non-simultaneous setting.

Given
u\in M_{m,n}({\mathbb{T}})
and
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
, define the following inhomogeneous linear forms on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\displaystyle L^{(i)}_{(u,{\mathbf{x}})}(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})=\sum^{n}_{j=1}u_{ij}q_{j}+x_{i},

(1.1)

for
i=1,\dots,m
. For positive constants
\{\theta_{i},\ i=1,\dots,m\}
and
\{\omega_{i},\ i=1,\dots,m\}
with
\sum^{m}_{i=1}\omega_{i}=n
, we consider the Diophantine inequalities given by

\displaystyle|p_{i}+L^{(i)}_{(u,{\mathbf{x}})}(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})|<\theta_{i}\|{\mathbf{q}}\|^{-\omega_{i}},\ i=1,\dots,m,

(1.2)

for
{\mathbf{p}}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and
{\mathbf{q}}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
. Let
\|\cdot\|
be a norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The special case
\omega_{1}=\dots=\omega_{m}
corresponds to the simultaneous case.

Classical results imply that there are infinitely many solutions
({\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\})
for the Diophantine inequalities. To describe the distribution of the number of solutions, let
\Delta_{T}(u,{\mathbf{x}})
denote the counting function, defined as

\displaystyle\Delta_{T}(u,{\mathbf{x}})=\sharp\{({\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}),\ 0<\|{\mathbf{q}}\|<T,\ |p_{i}+L^{(i)}_{(u,{\mathbf{x}})}(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})|<\theta_{i}\|{\mathbf{q}}\|^{-\omega_{i}},\ i=1,\dots,m\}.

For the homogeneous case
{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{0}}
, Schmidt
[
18
]
established an asymptotic formula for
\Delta_{T}(u,{\mathbf{0}})
as
T\rightarrow\infty
, which can be viewed the law of large numbers. Leveque
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
, Philipp
[
17
]
, Fuchs
[
10
]
derived the CLT of
\Delta_{T}(u,{\mathbf{0}})
for the one-dimensional case. An IP was also derived by Fuchs
[
9
]
in this situation. Dolgopyat-Fayad-Vinogradov
[
7
]
studied the CLT for the simultaneous case in high dimensional case, while Björklund and Gorodnik
[
4
]
proved the CLT for the non-simultaneous approximations.

The inhomogeneous analogues of Khintchine’s theorem were proven by Cassels
[
6
]
and Szüsz
[
19
]
. The Schmidt theorem and related definitions have been developed under a much more general circumstances, see for example Beresnevich-Velani
[
2
]
, Badziahin-Beresnevich-Velani
[
1
]
. We mention that Dolgopyat-Fayad-Vinogradov
[
7
]
proved the CLT for the inhomogeneous, simultaneous case.

Inspired by the work of Björklund-Gorodnik
[
4
]
, we prove the CLT for the inhomogeneous, non-simultaneous case. Throughout this paper, we assume that
m\geq 2
,
n\geq 1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
(u,{\mathbf{x}})
is uniformly distributed on
{\mathbb{T}}^{mn}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{T}(u,{\mathbf{x}})-\sigma^{2}_{m,n}\log T}{\sigma_{m,n}\sqrt{\log T}}\Longrightarrow{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)\ in\ distribution,

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where

\displaystyle\sigma_{m,n}^{2}=2^{m}(\prod^{m}_{i=1}\theta_{i})n{\rm{vol}}_{n}.

(1.3)

and
{\rm{vol}}_{n}
is the Euclidean volume of the unit ball with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|
.

Via Dani correspondence, the CLT for the counting function in Diophantine approximations reduces to a CLT for flows on the affine lattice space, or more precisely, to the CLT for Siegel transforms of piecewise smooth functions. In
[
7
]
, the authors employed the martingale approach, which was initiated by Le Borgne
[
5
]
, to prove the CLT for diagonal flows on the lattice space. However, the method fails to extend to the non-simultaneous case. We adopt the cumulant method developed in
[
4
]
for the homogeneous, non-simultaneous setting. The cumulant method relies on the estimates for high-order correlations of expanding flows on the homogeneous space, tracing back to the seminal work by Kleinbock-Margulis
[
14
]
. Based on Kim’s recent work
[
12
]
, which established an analogue mixing result on the affine lattice space, we derive the exponential mixing for high-order correlations, thus rendering the cumulant method applicable for the inhomogeneous case.

Furthermore, for
t\in[0,1]
, we construct a piecewise linear function
X_{N,t}
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{mn}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
that interpolates between
0
and
\Delta_{N}
(see Section
5
for the precise definition of
X_{N,t}
). Using the cumulant method, we can more easily verify the moment condition required for tightness of the distributions, which yields the following IP.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
v=(u,{\mathbf{x}})
is uniformly distributed on
{\mathbb{T}}^{mn}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{X_{N,t}(u,{\mathbf{x}})-\sigma^{2}_{m,n}t\log N}{\sigma_{m,n}\sqrt{\log N}}\Longrightarrow W_{t}\ in\ distribution,

as
N\rightarrow\infty
in
{\mathcal{C}}([0,1])
, where
\{W_{t},\ t\in[0,1]\}
is the standard Brownian motion on
{\mathbb{R}}
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is devoted to the mixing property of the flows on the affine lattice space. As a consequence, we derive the exponential mixing for high-order correlations, which implies the CLT for the smooth functions. In Section
3
we extend the CLT to smooth Siegel transforms. We then prove the main result, Theorem
1.1
in Section
4
. In the last Section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Mixing property of higher order correlations

Let
G=\rm{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
,
\Gamma=\rm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
, and let
X=G/\Gamma
be the uni-modular lattice space. Let
\hat{G}=\rm{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\hat{\Gamma}=\rm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and
Y=\hat{G}/\hat{\Gamma}
be the affine lattice space. The multiplication law on
\hat{G}
is given by

\displaystyle(g_{1},{\mathbf{v}}_{1})(g_{2},{\mathbf{v}}_{2})=(g_{1}g_{2},{\mathbf{v}}_{1}+g_{1}{\mathbf{v}}_{2}).

The action of
(g,{\mathbf{v}})\in\hat{G}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is defined as

\displaystyle(g,{\mathbf{v}}){\mathbf{z}}=g{\mathbf{z}}+{\mathbf{v}}.

Denote by
d_{G}
and
d_{\hat{G}}
the right invariant Riemannian metric on
G
and
\hat{G}
, which induces metrics
d_{X}
and
d_{Y}
on
X
and
Y
, respectively. Define the norm on
G
as
\|g\|=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\{|g_{ij}|,|g^{-1}_{ij}|\}
. Denote
m_{X}
,
m_{Y}
by the normalized Haar measure on
X
,
Y
, respectively. Let

\displaystyle H=\{M|M=\begin{pmatrix}{\rm{Id}}_{m}&u\\
0&{\rm{Id}}_{n}\end{pmatrix},u\in M_{m,n}\}

(2.4)

and
m_{H}
be the Haar measure on
H
. Notice that H is a unipotent abelian subgroup of
G
which is expanding horospherical with respect to

\displaystyle a_{t}={\rm diag}\{e^{nt}{\mathbf{1}}_{m},e^{-mt}{\mathbf{1}}_{n}\}

(2.5)

for
t>0
. For a subset
V\subset H
with compact closure, define
m_{V}=\frac{1}{m_{H}(V)}m_{H}
.

The effective ergodicity theorem proved by Kleinbock-Margulis
[
14
]
shows the exponential mixing property of the expanding translates of
H
on
X
.

Theorem 2.1

(Kleinbock-Margulis)
.

Let
V\subset H
be a fixed neighborhood of the identity in
H
with smooth boundary and compact closure. For any compact set
L\subset X
and
x_{0}\in L
, there exist constants
\lambda=\lambda(m,n)>0
and
T(L)\geq 0
such that for any
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(X)
and
t\geq T(L)
,

\displaystyle\int_{V}f(a_{t}ux_{0})dm_{V}(u)-\int_{X}fdm_{X}=O({\mathcal{S}}(f)e^{-\lambda t}),

(2.6)

where
{\mathcal{S}}
is a Sobolev norm on
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(X)
and the constant depends on
m
,
n
,
V
.

Kim
[
12
]
recently extended Kleinbock-Margulis’ result to the space of affine lattices. He introduced the function
\zeta:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
, given by

\displaystyle\zeta({\mathbf{b}},T)=\min\{N\in{\mathbb{N}},\min_{1\leq|q|\leq N}|q{\mathbf{b}}|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\frac{N^{2}}{T}\},

(2.7)

where
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes the supremum distance from
0\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.

Theorem 2.2

(Kim)
.

Let
V\subset H
be a fixed neighborhood of the identity in
H
with smooth boundary and compact closure. There exists a constant
\delta=\delta(d)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{V}f(a_{t}uy_{0})dm_{V}(u)-\int_{Y}fdm_{Y}=O({\mathcal{S}}(f)\zeta({\mathbf{b}}_{0},e^{\frac{nt}{2}})^{-\delta}),

(2.8)

holds for
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
, where
{\mathcal{S}}
is a Sobolev norm on
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
,
y_{0}=(g_{0},{\mathbf{0}})(1,{\mathbf{b}}_{0})\hat{\Gamma}\in Y
with
g_{0}\in\rm{SL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
,
{\mathbf{b}}_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and
t\geq 0
such that
\|g_{0}\|\leq\zeta({\mathbf{b}}_{0},e^{\frac{nt}{2}})^{\delta}
. The constant depends on
V
and
d
.

We consider

g_{t}={\rm diag}\{e^{\alpha_{1}t},\dots,e^{\alpha_{m}t},e^{-\alpha_{m+1}t},\dots e^{-\alpha_{m+n}t}\}

(2.9)

for
t>0
and
\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m+n})
with positive numbers
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m+n}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}=\sum^{m+n}_{i=m+1}\alpha_{i}.

Define

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}=\min_{1\leq i\leq d}\alpha_{i}.

The effective equidistribution results with respect to
g_{t}
also hold on
\rm{SL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})/\rm{SL}(d,\mathbb{Z})
and
\rm{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}/\rm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}

[

]
and

]

(Björklund-Gorodnik)

Let

\displaystyle\int_{V}f(u)\big(\prod^{k}_{i=1}f_{i}(g_{t_{i}}ux_{0})\big)dm_{V}(u)-\int_{V}fdm_{V}\prod^{k}_{i=1}(\int_{X}f_{i}dm_{X})=O(\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{k}}\prod^{k}_{i=1}{\mathcal{S}}(f_{i})e^{-\lambda^{\prime}D(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}),

We prove the following effective equidistribution for high-order correlations on

Theorem 2.4

Let

2.1.2
Diophantine type vectors

A vector
{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is said to be of Diophantine type
\kappa\geq 1
if there exists
c_{\kappa}>0
such that

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{b}}-\frac{{\mathbf{p}}}{q}|>c_{\kappa}q^{-\kappa}

(2.16)

for any
{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
|\cdot|
is the supremum norm of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

For
c>0
, define

\displaystyle D(\kappa,c)=\{{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},|q{\mathbf{b}}|_{\mathbb{Z}}>cq^{-\kappa+1},\ \forall q\in{\mathbb{N}}\}.

Then for
c_{1}<c_{2}
,
D(\kappa,c_{2})\subset D(\kappa,c_{1})
. Moreover, we have the following estimate.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
\kappa>d+1

\displaystyle m_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus D(\kappa,c))\ll c\sum^{\infty}_{q=1}q^{-\kappa+d}.

(2.17)

Proof.

For
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define

\Pi_{q}=\{{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},|q{\mathbf{b}}|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq cq^{-\kappa+1}\}=\{{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},|q{\mathbf{b}}-{\mathbf{p}}|\leq cq^{-\kappa+1},\ for\ some\ {\mathbf{p}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}.

Then

m_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus D(\kappa,c))\leq\sum_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}}m_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}(\Pi_{q}).

Notice that

\displaystyle m_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}(\Pi_{q})\ll c\sum_{{\mathbf{p}}=O(|q|)}q^{-\kappa}\ll cq^{-\kappa+d},

which leads to (
2.17
).
∎

For

\displaystyle m_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus\bigcup^{\infty}_{c\geq\epsilon}D(\kappa,c)\big)=m_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus D(\kappa,\epsilon)\big)\ll\epsilon.
