Fixed-treatment duration venetoclax following covalent BTKi progression in chronic lymphocytic leukemia

TO THE EDITOR:

Covalent Bruton tyrosine kinase inhibitors (cBTKis) are commonly used targeted agents in early lines of therapy for chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) and small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL)1,2; however, treatment following disease progression on cBTKis remains a challenge in clinical practice.3,4 Venetoclax is a B-cell lymphoma-2 inhibitor approved for CLL and SLL treatment, either as continuous monotherapy or as a fixed-treatment duration (FTD) regimen in combination with anti-CD20 antibodies (FTD Ven+anti-CD20; frontline, 12 months; relapsed/refractory, 24 months).4,5 Venetoclax has demonstrated durable responses and outcomes among patients with previous cBTKi exposure, both in clinical trials (eg, M14 032 and VENICE-1)6,7 and in real-world studies.8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15

Although these prior analyses included a proportion of patients who discontinued their cBTKi due to disease progression, real-world evidence on venetoclax used specifically among patients who previously progressed on a cBTKi is limited. Although an analysis from the CLL Collaborative Study of Real-World Evidence (CORE) was conducted to assess clinical outcomes among patients who initiated venetoclax-based therapy after having stopped cBTKi for a variety of reasons (eg, intolerance and disease progression), it did not explicitly focus on patients treated with FTD Ven+anti-CD20 in post-cBTKi progression settings nor did it assess overall survival (OS).13 With the expanding CLL and SLL treatment landscape, most recently with the availability in the post-cBTKi setting of the noncovalent BTKi pirtobrutinib,4 there is a need for real-world evidence on existing therapies such as FTD Ven+anti-CD20 after previous cBTKi progression. Therefore, this study was conducted to assess clinical outcomes, including OS, among patients who initiated FTD Ven+anti-CD20 following progression on a cBTKi.

This study used data from patient medical records collected from 23 medical centers in the United States, Canada, and Europe as part of the international CORE database (1 June 2018 to 17 March 2025).16 Patients were included if they initiated FTD Ven+anti-CD20 in the line of therapy following progression on a cBTKi. cBTKi progression was defined as documentation in the medical record of either (1) disease progression during the cBTKi line, as determined by the treating physician or (2) reason for discontinuation of the cBTKi was “due to progression” and/or “due to relapsed/refractory disease.” Patients were excluded if they had <1 month of follow-up, with follow-up duration defined as the time between the date of initiation of FTD Ven+anti-CD20 and the latest of the date last seen, patient referral to another institution, or last treatment date. Patient consent was not required because the data collected were de-identified and retrospective. Approval was received from institutional review boards from each of the centers included in the CORE database.

Clinical outcomes included physician-determined overall response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), time to next treatment or death (TTNT-D), and OS. Descriptive results were reported overall (ie, all patients who received FTD Ven+anti-CD20) and for the nested subgroup of patients who received VR (venetoclax+rituximab), with further stratification for second line of therapy (2L) or third line of therapy (3L). Due to small sample sizes and limited follow-up duration, subgroup results for the 23 patients who received venetoclax+obinutuzumab were not reported. Additional methods details are provided in the supplemental Methods.

Among 2696 patients in CORE, 175 initiated a venetoclax-based therapy in the line of therapy following progression on a cBTKi, of whom 79 received FTD Ven+anti-CD20 (VR, 56 [70.9%]) and were included in the study. Patients had a median age of 67.9 years (interquartile range [IQR], 60.6-75.7), and among those with available genetic testing data, 25 of 36 (69.4%) had unmutated immunoglobulin heavy-chain variable region gene and 11 of 54 (20.4%) had del(17p) and/or TP53 mutation. FTD Ven+anti-CD20 was commonly initiated in 29 of 79 (36.7%) patients in 2L (VR, 23/56 [41.1%]) and in 36 of 79 (45.6%) patients in 3L (VR, 24/56 [42.9%]), with the remaining patients being initiated on FTD Ven+anti-CD20 in the fourth line or later. The most common prior cBTKi received was ibrutinib (FTD Ven+anti-CD20, 77.2%; VR, 89.3%). Furthermore, 54.4% of patients had prior chemotherapy (CT) or chemoimmunotherapy (CIT) exposure (VR, 55.4%).

The median (IQR) follow-up duration from treatment initiation was 18.4 months (IQR, 9.7-27.9) for the overall cohort (2L, 17.8; 3L, 21.1; VR, 21.0). Median (IQR) active treatment duration was 13.6 months (IQR, 6.1-22.7; 2L, 12.2; 3L, 18.3; VR, 14.7). Almost half (45.6%) of patients were still on FTD Ven+anti-CD20 at the end of follow-up (VR, 44.6%). Additional treatment characteristics are described in the supplemental Results and supplemental Table 1.

The ORR was measured as the proportion of patients with a complete or partial response among those with available information on responses in their medical charts based on physician assessment. Although CT scans and bone marrow biopsies were not mandated to assess response, abstractors were provided with the 2018 International Workshop on Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia response criteria for reference when completing data collection.

PFS was defined as the time from the start of venetoclax-based therapy to disease progression or death (ie, event). Patients were censored at the earliest of the date of last follow-up or initiation of a subsequent line of therapy; 18-month rates were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method.

TTNT-D was defined as the time from the start of venetoclax-based therapy to initiation of a subsequent line of therapy or death (ie, event). Patients were censored at the earliest of the date of last follow-up or initiation of a subsequent line of therapy; 18-month rates were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method.

OS was defined as the time from the start of venetoclax-based therapy to death (ie, event). Patients were censored at the date of last follow-up; 18-month rates were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method.

N indicates the number of patients with available data for assessment of the clinical outcome.

Clinical outcomes were also reported for select subgroups of patients with (nonmutually exclusive) higher-risk genetic features, including (1) del(17p) and/or TP53 mutation, (2) complex karyotype (≥3 chromosomal abnormalities), and (3) unmutated IGHV. Information on chromosomal and/or molecular abnormalities was collected as documented in the patient chart based on physician report.

In the VR subgroup, the ORR was 72.2% (clinical complete response, 33.3%; partial response, 38.9%). In terms of line of therapy, the ORR was 84.6% in 2L and 68.8% in 3L. At 18 months, the PFS rate was 78.2% (2L, 89.7%; 3L, 84.0%), the TTNT-D rate was 81.4% (2L, 89.7%; 3L, 85.9%), and the OS rate was 87.0% (2L, 89.7%; 3L, 95.5%).

In this international, real-world study, patients demonstrated strong clinical outcomes with FTD Ven+anti-CD20, including VR, following cBTKi progression. Although the existing real-world literature describes clinical outcomes with FTD Ven+anti-CD20 after cBTKi discontinuation in smaller patient cohorts, only a proportion of patients in these studies discontinued due to disease progression.10,12, 13, 14, 15 To our knowledge, this analysis is the first to specifically focus on patients with prior cBTKi progression. Additionally, relative to prior literature,10,12, 13, 14, 15 this study included a sizeable cohort of 45.6% of patients without prior CT/CIT exposure. Of note, some study limitations should be considered (see supplemental Material).

Four retrospective studies evaluated VR following the discontinuation of a prior cBTKi for any reason, including intolerance or disease progression.10,12,13,15 Although our findings were generally consistent with previous studies, including the prior CORE analysis by Ghosh et al13 and 3 smaller cohort studies of 13 to 36 patients,10,12,15 direct comparability was limited by differences in patient populations. For example, Lew et al included a more heavily pretreated VR cohort (89% with prior CT/CIT exposure), and none of the prior studies evaluated VR among patients who discontinued cBTKi due to disease progression.10,12,13,15 Our findings build on existing literature to provide real-world evidence on outcomes with FTD Ven+anti-CD20 specifically following progression on a cBTKi.

Notably, patients in our study achieved promising 18-month outcomes, including OS rates of more than 80%. Furthermore, the 18-month rates of 78.9% for PFS and 80.0% for TTNT-D highlighted the considerable length of time before subsequent treatment was required. Given established patient preferences for time-limited therapy, including the chance for continued remission while off treatment, time without burdensome side effects, and lower drug costs,17,18 the clinical benefits observed in this study provide compelling evidence for the use of FTD Ven+anti-CD20 following progression on a cBTKi, even as newer treat-to-progression agents emerge.

In conclusion, the results of this real-world, international, multicenter study suggest that patients treated with FTD Ven+anti-CD20, including VR, in the relapsed/refractory setting following progression on a cBTKi can achieve deep responses, as well as meaningful PFS, TTNT-D, and OS across lines of therapy. As further data collection occurs, continued follow-up of these patients will enable additional analyses to inform clinical practice in the relapsed/refractory setting.
