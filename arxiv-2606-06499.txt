Mock-pre-Lie bialgebras
Shuai Hou, Zafar Normatov, Lina Song

Abstract
. In this paper, we systematically develop the theory of mock-pre-Lie bialgebras from multiple perspectives. We introduce the notion of a phase space of a mock-Lie algebra, and show that a mock-Lie algebra admits a phase space if and only if it is sub-adjacent to a mock-pre-Lie algebra. We introduce the notions of Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras and mock-pre-Lie bialgebras, and prove the equivalences between mock-pre-Lie bialgebras, Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras, certain matched pairs of mock-pre-Lie algebras, certain matched pairs of mock-Lie algebras and phase spaces of a mock-Lie algebra, which lays a theoretical foundation for subsequent research. Next, we investigate coboundary mock-pre-Lie bialgebras, and derive an analogue of the classical Yang-Baxter equation. In addition, we introduce two important special classes of mock-pre-Lie bialgebras: quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebras and factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebras. We show that quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebras naturally induce relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight − 1 . -1. Finally, we provide a new perspective for the study of triangular and factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebras by introducing the concept of quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebras of arbitrary weight.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to establish the bialgebra theory for mock-pre-Lie algebras from multiple perspectives,
and to focus on quasi-triangular and factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebras.

1.1.
Mock-Lie algebras and mock-pre-Lie algebras

Mock-Lie algebras
[
36
]
, also called Jacobi-Jordan algebras
[
13
]
, are a class of commutative algebras satisfying the Jacobi identity. Jordan algebras were originally introduced in order to achieve an axiomatization for the algebra of observables in quantum mechanics
[
22
]
.
Mock-Lie algebras are a special case of Jordan algebras: a mock-Lie algebra is a Jordan algebra in any characteristic. Furthermore, the class of mock-Lie algebras coincides with the class of commutative nilalgebras of nilindex at most
3
. In addition, mock-Lie algebras can be regarded as a subclass of the Jordan-Lie superalgebras
[
30
]
. In recent years, there has been some related research on mock-Lie algebras.
In
[
1
,
2
]
, the authors started from arbitrary
3
-nilpotent Jacobi-Jordan algebras to construct a new family of solutions to the quantum Yang-Baxter equation. Beyond that, they also constructed a non-abelian cohomological type object in order to classify certain mock-Lie algebras.
In
[
36
]
, the author studied the problems concerning faithful representations of mock-Lie algebras.
The dual algebras of mock-Lie algebras have been investigated in
[
14
]
.
The cohomology theory of mock-Lie algebras has been studied in
[
9
,
35
]
. In
[
8
]
, the authors introduced the notion of pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras, which are also called mock-pre-Lie algebras and have a close relationship with mock-Lie admissible algebras. Some properties and double constructions of mock-pre-Lie algebras have been investigated in
[
18
]
. Furthermore, cohomologies and linear deformations of mock-pre-Lie algebras have been studied in
[
3
]
.

1.2.
Bialgebras

For a given algebraic structure on a finite-dimensional vector space
\mathfrak{g}
, to construct its corresponding bialgebra structure, one only needs to endow the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with an algebraic structure of the same type, while imposing that the algebraic structures on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
satisfy the corresponding compatibility conditions.
Beyond the direct construction of bialgebra structures, the significance of bialgebras also stems from the fact that the compatibility conditions admit two other equivalent formulations, namely, the equivalence relation among bialgebras, Manin triples and certain matched pairs. At present, many bialgebraic theories of algebra have been established and studied. V. G. Drinfel’d introduced the notion of Lie bialgebras, which is closely related to Poisson-Lie groups
[
23
]
and quantum universal enveloping algebras
[
17
]
. In
[
4
]
, C. Bai developed the theory of pre-Lie bialgebras and established the connections with symplectic Lie algebras and phase spaces of Lie algebras.
The bialgebra theory of mock-Lie algebras has been investigated in
[
11
,
16
,
28
]
. The matched pair of mock pre-Lie algebras has also been studied in
[
29
]
. For further research on relevant bialgebra theory, refer to
[
12
,
19
,
20
,
26
,
27
,
33
]
.

1.3.
Rota-Baxter operators and the classical Yang-Baxter equation

Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras originated in the study of fluctuation theory in probability theory. In recent years, Rota-Baxter operators have found wide-ranging applications, including Connes-Kreimer’s work on the renormalization theory of quantum field theory
[
15
]
.
The theory of the Yang-Baxter equation was first discovered independently by the physicist C. N. Yang in 1967
[
34
]
and R. J. Baxter
[
10
]
in 1971, respectively. The classical Yang-Baxter equation can be regarded as the semi-classical limit of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation. Semenov-Tian-Shansky established the relationship between the classical Yang-Baxter equation and Rota-Baxter operators, showing that the operator form of the skew-symmetric classical Yang-Baxter equation is precisely corresponds to a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on a Lie algebra under certain conditions
[
32
]
.
B. A. Kupershmidt introduced the notion of
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators on Lie algebras
[
24
]
, which are also called relative Rota-Baxter operators
[
6
]
. On the one hand, a skew-symmetric solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation naturally gives rise to a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the coadjoint representation
[
24
]
. On the other hand, any relative Rota-Baxter operator can give rise to a skew-symmetric solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation
[
5
]
. Rota-Baxter operators also play an important role in the theory of bialgebras
[
25
,
31
]
. For a more detailed introduction to Rota-Baxter operators, refer to
[
21
]
.

1.4.
Main results and outline of the paper

The purpose of this paper is to develop the theory of mock-pre-Lie bialgebras. First, we introduce the notion of
quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebras, and show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between symplectic mock-Lie algebras
[
7
]
and quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebras. We study phase spaces of a mock-Lie algebra and prove that a mock-Lie algebra has a phase space if and only if it is sub-adjacent to a mock-pre-Lie algebra. We introduce the notion of a Manin triple of mock-pre-Lie algebras. Motivated by the general principle governing the equivalence between Manin triples and bialgebras, we further develop the theory of mock-pre-Lie bialgebras and establish the equivalence among Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras, mock-pre-Lie bialgebras, the phase spaces of mock-Lie algebras, as well as the matched pair of mock-pre-Lie and mock-Lie algebras. We also introduce the notions of quasi-triangular and factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebras, showing that the former give rise to relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
-1
. Finally, we use quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebras of weight
\lambda
to characterize triangular and factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebras respectively.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notion of a phase space of a mock-Lie algebra, and characterize this structure in terms of mock-pre-Lie algebras and Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras. In Section
3
, we introduce the notion of mock-pre-Lie bialgebras and establish their equivalence with Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras, phase spaces of the sub-adjacent Lie algebras, as well as certain matched pairs of mock-pre-Lie and mock-Lie algebras. We also study coboundary mock-pre-Lie bialgebras, leading to an analogue of the classical Yang-Baxter equation. In Section
4
, we introduce the notion of quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebras, which can give rise to a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1
. In Section
5
, we focus on quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebras and show that factorizable and triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebras
can be induced by such algebras.

Throughout this paper, all vector spaces are finite-dimensional over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
. For any vector space
V
,
V^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}(V,\mathbb{K})
denotes its dual.

2.
The phase space of mock-Lie algebras and Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras

In this section, first we introduce the notion of a phase space of a mock-Lie algebra. Then we prove that a mock-Lie algebra admits a phase space if and only if it is sub-adjacent to a mock-pre-Lie algebra. Moreover, we introduce the notion of a Manin triple of mock-pre-Lie algebras and show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the phase spaces of mock-Lie algebras and Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras.

To begin with, we recall some fundamental results concerning mock-Lie algebras and mock-pre-Lie algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
8
]
)
A
mock-Lie algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
together with a symmetric bilinear map
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

such that

(1)

\displaystyle\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}}-\{y,x\}_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(2)

\displaystyle\{x,\{y,z\}_{\mathfrak{g}}\}_{\mathfrak{g}}+\{y,\{z,x\}_{\mathfrak{g}}\}_{\mathfrak{g}}+\{z,\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}}\}_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\quad\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
9
]
)
A representation of a mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a pair
(V;\rho),

where
V
is a vector space and
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a linear map such that

(3)

\displaystyle\rho(\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}})=-\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(V;\rho)
be a representation of a mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}}).
Define
\rho^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V^{*})
by

\langle\rho^{*}(x)\alpha,u\rangle=-\langle\alpha,\rho(x)u\rangle,\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},\alpha\in V^{*},u\in V.

Then
(V^{*};-\rho^{*})
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}}),
which is called the dual representation of
(V;\rho).

Example 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-Lie algebra. Define
\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by

\mathrm{ad}_{x}y:=\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, which is called the adjoint representation. Furthermore,
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};-\mathrm{ad}^{*})
is also a representation of
\mathfrak{g},
which is called the coadjoint representation.

Lemma 2.5
.

(
[
9
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-Lie algebra. Then
(V;\rho)
is a representation of

(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
if and only if there is a mock-Lie algebra structure (called the semi-direct product) on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
together with the multiplication defined by

(4)

\displaystyle\{x+u,y+v\}_{\ltimes}:=\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho(x)v+\rho(y)u,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in V.

This semi-direct product mock-Lie algebra is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}V.

Definition 2.6
.

(
[
11
]
)
Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
a representation. A linear map
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
with respect to
(V;\rho)

if
T
satisfies

\displaystyle\{Tu,Tv\}_{\mathfrak{g}}=T(\rho(Tu)v+\rho(Tv)u),\quad\forall u,v\in V.

The notion of mock-pre-Lie algebras (also called pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras) was introduced in
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

A
mock-pre-Lie algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
together with a bilinear multiplication
\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, such that for any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, the anti-associator
Aass(x,y,z)=(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z)
is skew-symmetric in
x,y,
i.e.
Aass(x,y,z)=-Aass(y,x,z)
or equivalently,

(5)

\displaystyle(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z)=-(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z-y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z).

Proposition 2.8
.

(
[
8
]
)
Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra. Then the operation

(6)

\{x,y\}_{c}:=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

defines a mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{c})
, which is called the
sub-adjacent mock-Lie algebra
of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}_{c}.

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
is called a
compatible mock-pre-Lie algebra

on the mock-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
. Furthermore, for
x\in\mathfrak{g},
the linear map
L:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
defined by
L_{x}y=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y
gives a representation of the mock-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}.

Example 2.9
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two mock-pre-Lie algebras. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\oplus})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra,
where the multiplication
\cdot_{\oplus}
is defined by

\displaystyle(x_{1},y_{1})\cdot_{\oplus}(x_{2},y_{2}):=(x_{1}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{2},y_{1}\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}y_{2}),\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g},y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathfrak{h}.

This mock-pre-Lie algebra is called the direct sum mock-pre-Lie algebra.

Definition 2.10
.

(
[
3
]
)
A
representation
of a mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a triple
(V;\rho,\mu),
where
V
is a vector space,
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent mock-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
on
V
and
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a linear map satisfying

(7)

\displaystyle\rho(x)\mu(y)+\mu(y)\rho(x)=-\mu(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)-\mu(y)\mu(x),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

The following semidirect product characterization of representation was given in
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

(
[
3
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra,
V
is a vector space and
\rho,\mu:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
are linear maps. Then
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of a mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
if
and only if there is a mock-pre-Lie algebra structure on the direct sum of vector spaces
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
with the multiplication defined by

(8)

(x+u)\cdot_{(\rho,\mu)}(y+v):=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+\rho(x)v+\mu(y)u,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in V.

This semidirect product mock-pre-Lie algebra is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}V
.

Example 2.12
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{g};L,R)
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}),
which is called the
regular representation
, where
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is the right multiplication defined by
R_{x}y=y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x,
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. If there is a mock-Lie algebra structure on the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{*},
we denote the left and right multiplications by
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
respectively.

Next we consider the dual representation of a mock-pre-Lie algebra. Let
(V;\rho,\mu)
be a representation of a mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
. For all
x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V,\xi\in V^{*},
define

\rho^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V^{*})
and
\mu^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V^{*})
by

\displaystyle\langle\rho^{*}(x)\xi,u\rangle=-\langle\xi,\rho(x)u\rangle,\quad\langle\mu^{*}(x)\xi,u\rangle=-\langle\xi,\mu(x)u\rangle.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
(V;\rho,\mu)
be a representation of a mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Then

(V^{*};-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*},\mu^{*})

is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
, which is called the
dual representation
of
(V;\rho,\mu).

Proof.

For all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V,\xi\in V^{*},
by (
3
) and (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\langle(-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*})(\{x,y\}_{c})\xi+(-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*})(x)(-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*})(y)\xi+(-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*})(y)(-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*})(x)\xi,u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi,(\rho(\{x,y\}_{c})+\mu(\{x,y\}_{c})+\rho(y)\rho(x)+\rho(y)\mu(x)+\mu(y)\rho(x)

\displaystyle+\mu(y)\mu(x)+\rho(x)\rho(y)+\rho(x)\mu(y)+\mu(x)\rho(y)+\mu(x)\mu(y))u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that
-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*}
is a representation of the sub-adjacent mock-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{c}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\langle(-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*})(x)\mu^{*}(y)\xi+\mu^{*}(y)(-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*})(x)\xi+\mu^{*}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\xi+\mu^{*}(y)\mu^{*}(x)\xi,u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi,(\mu(x)\mu(y)-\mu(y)(\rho(x)+\mu(x))-(\rho(x)+\mu(x))\mu(y)-\mu(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y))u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

implies that (
7
) holds.
Therefore,
(V^{*};-\rho^{*}-\mu^{*},\mu^{*})
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
.
∎

Example 2.14
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra. By Proposition
2.13
,
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};-L^{*}-R^{*},R^{*})
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
, which is called the
coregular representation
, where two linear maps
L^{*},R^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
are respectively defined by

\displaystyle\langle L^{*}_{x}(\xi),y\rangle=-\langle\xi,x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y\rangle,\quad\langle R^{*}_{x}(\xi),y\rangle=-\langle\xi,y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x\rangle,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Definition 2.15
.

(
[
7
]
)
A
symplectic structure
on a mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a non-degenerate
skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
satisfying

(9)

\displaystyle\omega(\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}},z)+\omega(\{y,z\}_{\mathfrak{g}},x)+\omega(\{z,x\}_{\mathfrak{g}},y)=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

A mock-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called
symplectic mock-Lie algebra
if it is endowed with a symplectic
form
\omega
, and it is denoted by
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
.

Proposition 2.16
.

(
[
7
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
be a symplectic mock-Lie algebra. Then there exists a compatible mock-pre-Lie algebra structure
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
given by

(10)

\displaystyle\omega(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,z)=\omega(y,\{x,z\}_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Next we introduce the concept of quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebras, which are not only closely related to symplectic mock-Lie algebras, but also serve as the natural background for Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras.

Definition 2.17
.

A
quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
equipped with a nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that the following invariant condition holds:

(11)

\displaystyle\omega(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,z)=\omega(y,\{x,z\}_{c}),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Remark 2.18
.

Actually, by (
11
), we also have

(12)

\displaystyle\omega(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,z)=\omega(z\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,x).

Proposition
2.16
tells us that quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebras are the underlying structures of symplectic mock-Lie algebras.

Proposition 2.19
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between symplectic mock-Lie algebras and quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebras.

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebra. By (
6
) and
(
11
), we have

(13)

\displaystyle\omega(\{x,y\}_{c},z)+\omega(\{y,z\}_{c},x)+\omega(\{z,x\}_{c},y)=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Thus,
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{c},\omega)
is a symplectic mock-Lie algebra.

Conversely, let
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a symplectic mock-Lie algebra. By Proposition
2.16
, there is a compatible quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebra structure
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
given by

\displaystyle\omega(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,z)=\omega(y,\{x,z\}_{\mathfrak{g}}).

The proof is finished.
∎

Let
V
be a vector space and
V^{*}
its dual space. Then there is a natural nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega
on
V\oplus V^{*}
given by:

(14)

\displaystyle\omega(x+\xi,y+\eta)=\langle\xi,y\rangle-\langle\eta,x\rangle,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Next we introduce the notion of a phase space of a mock-Lie algebra.

Definition 2.20
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-Lie algebra and let
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be dual space of
\mathfrak{g}
.
If there is a mock-Lie algebra structure on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\},\omega)
is a symplectic mock-Lie algebra, where
\omega
is given by (
14
), and
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\}|_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
are mock-Lie subalgebras of
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
, then the symplectic mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\},\omega)
is called a
phase space of the mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Mock-pre-Lie algebras play an important role in the study of phase spaces of mock-Lie algebras.

Theorem 2.21
.

A mock-Lie algebra has a phase space if and only if it is sub-adjacent to a mock-pre-Lie algebra.

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra. By Proposition
2.8
, the left multiplication
L
is a representation of the mock-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. By Lemma
2.3
,
-L^{*}
is a representation of the sub-adjacent mock-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Thus, we have the semidirect product mock-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{c}\ltimes_{-L^{*}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}=(\mathfrak{g}_{c}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\ltimes})
. By (
14
), for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},
we have

\displaystyle\omega(\{x+\xi,y+\eta\}_{\ltimes},z+\zeta)+\omega(\{y+\eta,z+\zeta\}_{\ltimes},x+\xi)+\omega(\{z+\zeta,x+\xi\}_{\ltimes},y+\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(\{x,y\}-L^{*}_{x}\eta-L^{*}_{y}\xi,z+\zeta)+\omega(\{y,z\}-L^{*}_{y}\zeta-L^{*}_{z}\eta,x+\xi)+\omega(\{z,x\}-L^{*}_{z}\xi-L^{*}_{x}\zeta,y+\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle-L^{*}_{x}\eta-L^{*}_{y}\xi,z\rangle-\langle\zeta,\{x,y\}_{c}\rangle+\langle-L^{*}_{y}\zeta-L^{*}_{z}\eta,x\rangle-\langle\xi,\{y,z\}_{c}\rangle+\langle-L^{*}_{z}\xi-L^{*}_{x}\zeta,y\rangle-\langle\eta,\{z,x\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\eta,L_{x}z\rangle+\langle\xi,L_{y}z\rangle-\langle\zeta,\{x,y\}_{c}\rangle+\langle\zeta,L_{y}x\rangle+\langle\eta,L_{z}x\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\xi,\{y,z\}_{c}\rangle+\langle\xi,L_{x}y\rangle+\langle\zeta,L_{x}y\rangle-\langle\eta,\{z,x\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that
(\mathfrak{g}_{c}\ltimes_{-L^{*}}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\omega)
is a symplectic mock-Lie algebra. Moreover,
(\mathfrak{g}_{c},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{c})
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{c}\ltimes_{-L^{*}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is an abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{c}\ltimes_{-L^{*}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Thus, the symplectic mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{c}\ltimes_{-L^{*}}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\omega)
is a phase space of the sub-adjacent mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{c},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{c})
.

Conversely, let
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\},\omega)
be a phase space of a mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
. By Proposition
2.16
, there exists a compatible mock-pre-Lie algebra structure
\cdot
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
given by

\displaystyle\omega((x+\xi)\cdot(y+\eta),z+\zeta)=\omega(y+\eta,\{x+\xi,z+\zeta\}).

Since
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a subalgebra of
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(x\cdot y,z)=\omega(y,\{x,z\})=\omega(y,\{x,z\}_{\mathfrak{g}})=\langle y,0\rangle-\langle 0,\{x,z\}_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Thus,
x\cdot y\in\mathfrak{g},
which implies that
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot|_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a subalgebra of the mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot)
. Its sub-adjacent mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{c},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{c})
is exactly the original mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
.
∎

Corollary 2.22
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\},\omega)
be a phase space of a mock-Lie algebra and
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot)
the associated mock-pre-Lie algebra. Then both
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot|_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot|_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
are subalgebras of the mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot)
.

Corollary 2.23
.

Let
(V;\rho)
be a representation of the mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
and
(V^{*};-\rho^{*})

the dual representation. If
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\ltimes},\omega)
is a phase space of
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}),
where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\ltimes}
is given by (
4

\displaystyle x\cdot y:=\rho(x)y,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

defines a mock-pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, by (
11
) and (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\xi,x\cdot y\rangle=\omega(\xi,x\cdot y)=-\omega(x\cdot y,\xi)=-\omega(y,\{x,\xi\}_{\ltimes})=\omega(y,\rho^{*}(x)\xi)=-\langle\rho^{*}(x)\xi,y\rangle=\langle\xi,\rho(x)y\rangle.

Therefore, we have

∎

At the end of this section, we introduce the notion of a Manin triple of mock-pre-Lie algebras.

Definition 2.24
.

A
Manin triple of mock-pre-Lie algebras
is a triple
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
, where

(i)

(\mathcal{G},\cdot,\omega)
is a quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebra;

(ii)

both
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
are isotropic subalgebras of
(\mathcal{G},\cdot)
, that is,
\omega(x,y)=\omega(u,v)=0
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
;

(iii)

\mathcal{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
as vector spaces.

In a Manin triple of mock-pre-Lie algebras
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
, since the skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega
is nondegenerate,
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
can be identified with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
via

\langle\xi,x\rangle:=\omega(\xi,x),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}.

Thus,
\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
naturally and the bilinear form
\omega
is exactly given by
(
14
).

Theorem 2.25
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras and phase spaces of mock-Lie algebras. More precisely, if
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})

(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\},\omega)

(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}}),
where
\omega
is given by (
14
). Conversely, if
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}},\omega)
is a phase space of a mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}}),
then
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a Manin-triple of mock-pre-Lie algebras, where the mock-pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by (
11

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
be a Manin triple of mock-pre-Lie algebras. Here
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot,\omega)
is a quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebra and
\omega
is given by (
14
).
Denote by
\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
be the mock-pre-Lie algebra structures on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, respectively, and denote by
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
the corresponding sub-adjacent mock-Lie algebra structures on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, respectively. It is straightforward to deduce that the corresponding mock-Lie algebra structure
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by

(15)

\displaystyle\{x+\xi,y+\eta\}=\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}}-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\xi}y-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\eta}x+\{\xi,\eta\}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}-L^{*}_{x}\eta-L^{*}_{y}\xi,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},

it is obvious that
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}})
are subalgebras of
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\})

By Proposition
2.19
,
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\},\omega)
is a symplectic mock-Lie algebra, which is a phase space of the mock-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}}).

Conversely, let
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}},\omega)

(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
. By Proposition
2.16

\cdot
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}

∎

3.
Mock-pre-Lie bialgebras and the mock-pre-Lie classical Yang-Baxter equation

In this section, first we introduce the notion of mock-pre-Lie bialgebras. Then we establish the equivalence between mock-pre-Lie bialgebras, Manin triples of mock-pre-Lie algebras, phase spaces of the sub-adjacent Lie algebras, certain matched pairs of mock-pre-Lie algebras and certain matched pairs of mock-Lie algebras. Furthermore, we introduce the notion of coboundary mock-pre-Lie bialgebras, which yields the mock-pre-Lie classical Yang-Baxter equation, as an analogue of the classical Yang-Baxter equation.

3.1.

(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}),

\displaystyle\langle\alpha(x),\xi\otimes\eta\rangle=\langle x,\xi\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\eta\rangle

Definition 3.1

\displaystyle+\{r_{23},r_{12}\}+\{r_{13},r_{21}\}-\{r_{13},r_{23}\}\Big)+\sum_{j}\Big(L_{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}a_{j}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}-{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes L_{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}a_{j}}+L_{x}L_{a_{j}}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}

\displaystyle-{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes L_{x}L_{a_{j}}-L_{x}\otimes L_{a_{j}}+L_{a_{j}}\otimes L_{x}\Big)(r-\tau(r))\otimes b_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q(x)[[r,r]]+\sum_{j}[P_{-}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}a_{j})+P_{+}(x)P_{-}(a_{j})](r-\tau(r))\otimes b_{j}.

Therefore, we finished the proof.
∎

With the discussion above, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra and
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. Then the map
\alpha
defined by equation (
29
) induces
a mock-pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a
mock-pre-Lie bialgebra if and only if the following two conditions
are satisfied:

(i)

[P_{-}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)+P_{+}(x)P_{-}(y)](r-\tau(r))=0

\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
;

(ii)

Q(x)[[r,r]]=0
,

where for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},

P_{\pm}(x),Q(x)
and
[[r,r]]
are defined by Proposition
3.14
.

The equation
[[r,r]]=-r_{13}\cdot r_{12}-r_{23}\cdot r_{21}+\{r_{23},r_{12}\}+\{r_{13},r_{21}\}-\{r_{13},r_{23}\}=0
is called the
mock-pre-Lie classical Yang-Baxter equation
, as an analogue of the classical Yang-Baxter equation.

4.
Quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebras and relative Rota-Baxter operators

In this section, we introduce the notions of quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie algebras and relative Rota-Baxter operators of arbitrary weight on mock-pre-Lie algebras. We show that a quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie algebra can give rise to a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1.

Let
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},
we define
r_{+},r_{-}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
by

(36)

\displaystyle\langle r_{+}(\xi),\eta\rangle=r(\xi,\eta),\quad\langle r_{-}(\xi),\eta\rangle=r(\eta,\xi),\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Explicitly, writing
r=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, the corresponding map
r_{+}
is

(37)

\displaystyle r_{+}(\xi)=\sum_{i}\langle\xi,a_{i}\rangle b_{i},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Then the mock-pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by (
29
) in Theorem
3.15
is given by

(38)

\displaystyle\xi\cdot_{r}\eta=-(L^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)})\eta+R^{*}_{r_{-}(\eta)}\xi,\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

We will denote it by
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{r})
, or simply by
\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{r}.

Now we introduce the notion of
(L,L+R)
-invariance of
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, which is the main ingredient in the definition
of a quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra. An element
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is called
(L,L+R)
-invariant
if

(39)

\displaystyle\Big(L_{x}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}-{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes(L_{x}+R_{x})\Big)r=0,\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra and
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}.
Then
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant if and only if

(40)

\displaystyle r_{+}(L^{*}_{x}\xi)+\{x,r_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}=0.\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Proof.

For all
x\in\mathfrak{g},\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},
we have

(41)

\displaystyle\langle r_{+}(L^{*}_{x}\xi)+\{x,r_{+}(\xi)\}_{c},\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle r,L^{*}_{x}\xi\otimes\eta\rangle+\langle L_{x}(r_{+}(\xi))+R_{x}(r_{+}(\xi)),\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle r,L^{*}_{x}\xi\otimes\eta\rangle-\langle r,\xi\otimes(L^{*}_{x}+R^{*}_{x})\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\Big(L_{x}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}-{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes(L_{x}+R_{x})\Big)r,\xi\otimes\eta\rangle,

which implies that
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant if and only if
r_{+}(L^{*}_{x}\xi)+\{x,r_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}=0.

∎

Next, for convenience in the subsequent discussion, we write
r=r_{a}+r_{s},
where
r_{a}
denotes the skew-symmetric part of
r

and
r_{s}
denotes the symmetric part of
r.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra and
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}.
If the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant, then we have
[P_{-}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)+P_{+}(x)P_{-}(y)](r_{a})=0
, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

Since the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant, by Lemma
4.2
and (
5
), we have

\displaystyle[P_{-}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)+P_{+}(x)P_{-}(y)](r_{a})(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(L_{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}-{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes L_{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y}+L_{x}L_{y}\otimes{{\rm{Id}}}-{{\rm{Id}}}\otimes L_{x}L_{y}-L_{x}\otimes L_{y}+L_{y}\otimes L_{x}\Big)(r_{a})(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-r_{a}(L^{*}_{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y}\xi,\eta)+r_{a}(\xi,L^{*}_{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y}\eta)+r_{a}(L^{*}_{y}(L^{*}_{x}\xi),\eta)-r_{a}(\xi,L^{*}_{y}(L^{*}_{x}\eta))-r_{a}(L^{*}_{x}\xi,L^{*}_{y}\eta)+r_{a}(L^{*}_{y}\xi,L^{*}_{x}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle(r_{a})_{+}(L^{*}_{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y}\xi),\eta\rangle+\langle(r_{a})_{+}(\xi),L^{*}_{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y}\eta\rangle+\langle(r_{a})_{+}(L^{*}_{y}(L^{*}_{x}\xi),\eta\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle(r_{a})_{+}(\xi),L^{*}_{y}(L^{*}_{x}\eta)\rangle-\langle(r_{a})_{+}(L^{*}_{x}\xi),L^{*}_{y}\eta\rangle+\langle(r_{a})_{+}(L^{*}_{y}\xi),L^{*}_{x}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}-(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)+\{y,\{x,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}\}_{c}

\displaystyle-x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\xi))-y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\{x,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\{y,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c},\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that
[P_{-}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)+P_{+}(x)P_{-}(y)](r_{a})=0.

∎

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra. If
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
[[r,r]]=0
and the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant, then the mock-pre-Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{r}^{*})
induced by
r
is called a
quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra
. Moreover, if
r
is symmetric, it is called a
triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra
.

Denote by
I
the operator

(42)

\displaystyle I=r_{+}-r_{-}:\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}.

Note that
I^{\ast}=-I
. Actually,
\frac{1}{2}I
is the contraction of the skew-symmetric part of
r
.
If
r
is symmetric, then
I=0
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra and
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}.
The skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant if
and only if
I\circ L_{x}^{\ast}=-(L_{x}+R_{x})\circ I
, or
L_{x}\circ I=-I\circ(L_{x}^{*}+R_{x}^{*}),
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, where
I
is
given by (
42
).

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant if
and only if

(r_{a})_{+}(L^{*}_{x}\xi)+\{x,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}=0.
Using
\frac{1}{2}I=(r_{a})_{+},
we can deduce that

the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant if
and only if
I\circ L_{x}^{\ast}=-(L_{x}+R_{x})\circ I.
Moreover, by
I^{\ast}=-I,
we have
L_{x}\circ I=-I\circ(L_{x}^{*}+R_{x}^{*}).

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra and
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}.
If the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant, then

(43)

\displaystyle\langle\xi,x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle+\langle\eta,\{x,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}\rangle=0,\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Furthermore, the mock-pre-Lie algebra multiplication
\cdot_{r}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
given by (
38
) reduces to

(44)

\displaystyle\xi\cdot_{r}\eta=-(L^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)})\eta+R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)}\xi,\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Proof.

Since the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant, by Proposition
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\xi,x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle+\langle\eta,\{x,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle L^{*}_{x}\xi,(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle+\langle\eta,\{x,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\eta,(r_{a})_{+}(L^{*}_{x}\xi)+\{x,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}\rangle=0,

which implies that (
43
) holds.
Moreover, by (
43
), we have

\displaystyle\langle(L^{*}_{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)})\eta+R^{*}_{(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)}\xi,x\rangle=-(\langle\xi,x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle+\langle\eta,\{x,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}\rangle)=0.

By (
38
), we have

\displaystyle\xi\cdot_{r}\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(L^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)})\eta+R^{*}_{r_{-}(\eta)}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(L^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)})\eta-(L^{*}_{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)})\eta+R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)}\xi-R^{*}_{(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(L^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)})\eta+R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)}\xi.

The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra and
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. Assume that the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant. Then
r
satisfies
[[r,r]]=0
if and only if
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\cdot_{r})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra and
the linear maps
r_{+},r_{-}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
are both mock-pre-Lie algebra homomorphisms from
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{r})
to
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Proof.

If
r
satisfies
[[r,r]]=0,
then by Theorem
3.15
and Proposition
4.3
,
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\cdot_{r})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra.
By a direct calculation, for all
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},
we have

\displaystyle[[r_{s},r_{s}]](\xi,\eta,\zeta)=\langle\eta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\rangle+\langle\xi,(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,\{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi),(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\}_{c}\rangle.

Moreover, by (
43
), we have

\displaystyle[[r_{a},r_{a}]](\xi,\eta,\zeta)=-\langle\zeta,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi),(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\}_{c}\rangle

and

\displaystyle[[r_{s},r_{a}]](\xi,\eta,\zeta)+[[r_{a},r_{s}]](\xi,\eta,\zeta)=0,

which implies that

\displaystyle[[r,r]](\xi,\eta,\zeta)=[[r_{s}+r_{a},r_{s}+r_{a}]](\xi,\eta,\zeta)=[[r_{s},r_{s}]](\xi,\eta,\zeta)+[[r_{a},r_{a}]](\xi,\eta,\zeta).

Next we show that the following equation holds

(45)

\displaystyle r_{+}(\xi\cdot_{r}\eta)-r_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{+}(\eta)=-[[r,r]](\xi,\cdot,\eta).

One the one hand, for the left part, by (
44
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\zeta,r_{+}(\xi\cdot_{r}\eta)-r_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{+}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\zeta,((r_{s})_{+}+(r_{a})_{+})(-L^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)}\eta-R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)}\eta)+R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)}\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\zeta,((r_{s})_{+}+(r_{a})_{+})(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}((r_{s})_{+}+(r_{a})_{+})(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(-L^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)}\eta-R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)}\eta)\rangle+\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)}\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(-L^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)}\eta-R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)}\eta)\rangle+\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(R^{*}_{(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)}\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\eta,\{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi),(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\}_{c}\rangle-\langle\xi,(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\eta,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta),(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}\rangle+\langle\xi,(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle

One the other hand, we have

\displaystyle[[r,r]](\xi,\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[r_{s},r_{s}]](\xi,\zeta,\eta)+[[r_{a},r_{a}]](\xi,\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle+\langle\xi,(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\eta,\{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi),(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\}_{c}\rangle-\langle\eta,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi),(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta)\}_{c}\rangle.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\langle\zeta,r_{+}(\xi\cdot_{r}\eta)-r_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{+}(\eta)+[[r,r]](\xi,\cdot,\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\eta,\{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi),(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\}_{c}\rangle-\langle\xi,(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\eta,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta),(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\xi,(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle+\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\xi,(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\eta,\{(r_{s})_{+}(\xi),(r_{s})_{+}(\zeta)\}_{c}\rangle-\langle\eta,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi),(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta)\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\uwave{-\langle\eta,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta),(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\}_{c}\rangle}+\langle\xi,(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle\uwave{-\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle}

\displaystyle-\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\eta,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi),(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta)\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta),(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\}_{c}\rangle-\langle\xi,(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta)\rangle-\langle\zeta,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi),(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\zeta,(r_{s})_{+}(\eta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\rangle-\langle\zeta,(r_{a})_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{a})_{+}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\eta,\{(r_{a})_{+}(\xi),(r_{a})_{+}(\zeta)\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\eqref{skew-invariant}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that (
45
) holds. Thus, if
r
satisfies
[[r,r]]=0
,
then
r_{+}
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra homomorphism. Similarly, we can also prove that
r_{-}
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra homomorphism.

Conversely, if
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\cdot_{r})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra and
r_{+}
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra homomorphism, by (
45
), we have
[[r,r]]=0
.
∎

Next we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
from a mock-pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
to a mock-pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu).

Definition 4.8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two mock-pre-Lie algebras.
Let two linear maps
\rho,\mu:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be a representation of the mock-pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on the vector space
\mathfrak{h}
.
If for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h},

(46)

\displaystyle{}\mu(x)(u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}v)+u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mu(x)v)+v\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mu(x)u)+\mu(x)(v\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(47)

\displaystyle{}(\mu(x)u)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}v+u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(x)v)+(\rho(x)u)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}v+\rho(x)(u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

then the pair
(\rho,\mu)
is called an
action
of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
on
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}).

Example 4.9
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra. Then the regular representation
(\mathfrak{g};L,R)
which is defined in Example
2.12
is an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself.

Let
(\rho,\mu)
be an action of a mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}).
Define a new operation
\cdot_{(\rho,\mu)}
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
by

(48)

\displaystyle{}(x+u)\cdot_{(\rho,\mu)}(y+v)=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+\rho(x)v+\mu(y)u+u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}v,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 4.10
.

With above notations,
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{(\rho,\mu)})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra, which is called the semidirect product of the mock-pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the mock-pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
with respect to the action
(\rho,\mu).

Proof.

It follows from straightforward computations, and we omit details.
∎

Definition 4.11
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two mock-pre-Lie algebras and
(\rho,\mu)
be an action of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
on
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Then a linear map
B:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
from a mock-pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
to a mock-pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
if
B
satisfies the following equation:

(49)

(Bu)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(Bv)=B(\rho(Bu)v+\mu(Bv)u+\lambda u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}v),\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra.
A linear map
B:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a
Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on a mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
if

\displaystyle(Bx)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(By)=B((Bx)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(By)+\lambda x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Moreover, a mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
equipped with a Rota-Baxter operator
B
is called a
Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
, which is denoted by
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
be a Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then there is a new mock-pre-Lie multiplication
\cdot_{B}
on
\mathfrak{g}
defined by

\displaystyle x\cdot_{B}y=(Bx)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(By)+\lambda x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y.

The mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
is called the descendent mock-pre-Lie algebra and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}_{B}
. Furthermore,
B
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra homomorphism from the descendent mock-pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{B}
to
\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 4.13
.

Note that a Rota-Baxter operator on a mock-pre-Lie algebra is a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the regular action
(\mathfrak{g};L,R)
.
If the mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
is abelian, then the action
(\rho,\mu)
reduce to a representation, and the relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
reduce to the relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
(also called an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator). See
[
29
]
for more details.

By
I=r_{+}-r_{-},
the mock-pre-Lie algebra multiplication
\cdot_{r}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
given by (
38
) reduces to

(50)

\displaystyle\xi\cdot_{r}\eta=-(L^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)})\eta+R^{*}_{r_{+}(\eta)}\xi-R^{*}_{I(\eta)}\xi,\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Define a new multiplication
\cdot_{+}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as follows:

(51)

\xi\cdot_{+}\eta=R_{I(\eta)}^{*}\xi,\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a mock-pre-Lie algebra and
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}.
If the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant, then
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{+})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra.

Proof.

By (
5
) and Proposition
4.5
, for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},
we have

\displaystyle\langle(\xi\cdot_{+}\eta)\cdot_{+}\zeta+\xi\cdot_{+}(\eta\cdot_{+}\zeta)+(\eta\cdot_{+}\xi)\cdot_{+}\zeta+\eta\cdot_{+}(\xi\cdot_{+}\zeta),x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle R^{*}_{I\zeta}R^{*}_{I\eta}\xi+R^{*}_{I(R^{*}_{I\zeta}\eta)}\xi+R^{*}_{I\zeta}R^{*}_{I\xi}\eta+R^{*}_{I(R^{*}_{I\zeta}\xi)}\eta,x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi,(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}I\zeta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}I\eta-x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(I\eta\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}I\zeta)-\{x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}I\zeta,I\eta\}_{c}-\{x,I\eta\}_{c}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}I\zeta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that
(\xi\cdot_{+}\eta)\cdot_{+}\zeta+\xi\cdot_{+}(\eta\cdot_{+}\zeta)=-(\eta\cdot_{+}\xi)\cdot_{+}\zeta-\eta\cdot_{+}(\xi\cdot_{+}\zeta)
. Thus
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{+})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra.
∎

Lemma 4.15
.

(-L^{*}-R^{*},R^{*})
is an action of the mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
on the mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{+})
given in Proposition
4.14
.

Proof.

By (
5
) and Proposition
4.5
, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},
we have

\displaystyle\langle R^{*}_{x}(\xi\cdot_{+}\eta)+R^{*}_{x}(\eta\cdot_{+}\xi)+\xi\cdot_{+}(R^{*}_{x}\eta)+\eta\cdot_{+}(R^{*}_{x}\xi),y\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle R^{*}_{x}R^{*}_{I\eta}\xi+R^{*}_{x}R^{*}_{I\xi}\eta+R^{*}_{I(R^{*}_{x}\eta)}\xi+R^{*}_{I(R^{*}_{x}\xi)}\eta,y\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\xi,(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}I\eta-\{y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x,I\eta\}_{c}-y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(I\eta\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x)-\{y,I\eta\}_{c}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

From the above, equation (
46
) holds, and (
47
) holds by direct computation. Consequently,

(-L^{*}-R^{*},R^{*})
is an action of the mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
on
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{+}).

∎

Furthermore, we prove that quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebras naturally induces a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1

with respect to the coregular representation
(-L^{*}-R^{*},R^{*})
.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{r}^{*})
be a quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra induced by
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. Then
r_{+}:(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{+})\to(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
-1
with
respect to the action
(-L^{*}-R^{*},R^{*})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.7
and (
50
), for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},
we have

\displaystyle(r_{+}\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{+}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{+}(\xi\cdot_{r}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{+}(-(L^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)})\eta+R^{*}_{r_{+}(\eta)}\xi-R^{*}_{I(\eta)}\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{+}(-(L^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)}+R^{*}_{r_{+}(\xi)})\eta+R^{*}_{r_{+}(\eta)}\xi-\xi\cdot_{+}\eta),

which implies that
r_{+}:(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{+})\to(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
-1
with
respect to the action
(-L^{*}-R^{*},R^{*})
.
∎

Taking a symmetric
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
into Theorem
4.16
, then we have the following conclusion.

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{r}^{*})
be a triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra induced by a symmetric
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. Then
r_{+},r_{-}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
are relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
with
respect to the coregular representation
(-L^{*}-R^{*},R^{*})
.

5.
Factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebras and quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebras

In this section, we introduce the notions of factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebras and quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebras of weight
\lambda
. We establish a one-to-one correspondence between factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebras and quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebras of nonzero weight. Moreover, we show that quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebras of weight
0
can give rise a triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra.

Definition 5.1
.

A quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{r}^{*})
is called
factorizable
if the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is non-degenerate, which means that the linear map
I:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
defined in (
42
) is a linear isomorphism of vector spaces.

Consider the map

\mathfrak{g}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{+}\oplus r_{-}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,y)\longmapsto x-y}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{r}^{\ast})
be a factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebra. Then
\mathrm{Im}(r_{+}\oplus r_{-})
is a mock-pre-Lie subalgebra of the direct sum mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\oplus})
, which is given by Example
2.9
, and
r_{+}\oplus r_{-}:(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{r})\rightarrow\mathrm{Im}(r_{+}\oplus r_{-})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra isomorphism. Moreover, any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
has a unique decomposition

(52)

x=x_{+}-x_{-},

where
(x_{+},x_{-})\in\mathrm{Im}(r_{+}\oplus r_{-})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.7
, we have both
r_{+}
and
r_{-}
are mock-pre-Lie algebra homomorphisms, then
\mathrm{Im}(r_{+})
and
\mathrm{Im}(r_{-})
are mock-pre-Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}.

Consider the direct sum mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\oplus})
, therefore we have
\mathrm{Im}(r_{+}\oplus r_{-})
is a mock-pre-Lie subalgebra of
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\oplus}).
Since
I=r_{+}-r_{-}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}
is nondegenerate, it follows that
r_{+}\oplus r_{-}:(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{r})\rightarrow\mathrm{Im}(r_{+}\oplus r_{-})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra isomorphism.

Since
I:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}
is nondegenerate, then for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},
there exist a unique
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},
such that

I(\xi)=(r_{+}-r_{-})(\xi)=x,
thus we have

r_{+}I^{-1}(x)-r_{-}I^{-1}(x)=(r_{+}-r_{-})I^{-1}(x)=x,

which implies that
x=x_{+}-x_{-},
where
x_{+}=r_{+}I^{-1}(x)
and
x_{-}=r_{-}I^{-1}(x)
.
The uniqueness also follows from the fact that
I:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is nondegenerate.
∎

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
be a mock-pre-Lie bialgebra. Define a multiplication
\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}
on
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by

(53)

(x,\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}(y,\eta)=\Big(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y-(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\xi}+\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\xi})y+\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\eta}(x),\xi\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\eta-(L_{x}^{*}+R_{x}^{*})\eta+R_{y}^{*}(\xi)\Big),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

By Proposition
3.3
,
(\mathfrak{d},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra, which is called the
mock-pre-Lie double of the mock-pre-Lie bialgebra

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*}),
and denoted by
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}\bowtie_{-L^{*}-R^{*},R^{*}}^{-\mathcal{L}^{*}-\mathcal{R}^{*},\mathcal{R}^{*}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
be a mock-pre-Lie bialgebra. Suppose that
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\{e_{1}^{*},e_{2}^{*},\dots,e_{n}^{*}\}
is the dual basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then
r=\sum_{i}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{*}\subset\mathfrak{d}\otimes\mathfrak{d}
induces a mock-pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{d}^{*}=\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
such that
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}_{r}^{*})
is a quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra, where the mock-pre-Lie algebra structure
(\mathfrak{d}_{r}^{*},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}_{r}^{*}})
is given by

(54)

(\xi,x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}_{r}^{*}}(\eta,y)=(\xi\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\eta,x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Moreover,
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}_{r}^{*})
is also a factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebra.

Proof.

We first prove that the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(e_{i}\otimes e_{i}^{\ast}-e_{i}^{\ast}\otimes e_{i})
of
r
is
(\tilde{L},\tilde{L}+\tilde{R})
-invariant, where
\tilde{L},\tilde{R}
are the left and right multiplication operators of the mock-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{d},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}})
respectively. For all
(\xi,x)\in\mathfrak{d}^{\ast}
, we have
({r_{a}})_{+}(\xi,x)=\frac{1}{2}(-x,\xi)\in\mathfrak{d}.
Furthermore, by a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\{(x,\xi),({r_{a}})_{+}(\eta,y)\}_{\mathfrak{c}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\{x,y\}_{c}-\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{*}x+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{*}y,\{\xi,\eta\}_{c}-L_{x}^{*}\eta+L_{y}^{*}\xi\Big),

\displaystyle\tilde{L}^{\ast}_{(x,\xi)}(\eta,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(-\{\xi,\eta\}_{c}+L_{x}^{*}\eta-L_{y}^{*}\xi,-\{x,y\}_{c}-\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{*}x+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{*}y\Big).

Thus we have

({r_{a}})_{+}\Big(\tilde{L}^{\ast}_{(x,\xi)}(\eta,y)\Big)+\{(x,\xi),({r_{a}})_{+}(\eta,y)\}_{\mathfrak{c}}=0.

By Lemma
4.6
, the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(\tilde{L},\tilde{L}+\tilde{R})
-invariant. We also have

\displaystyle[[r,r]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(-e_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}e_{j}\otimes e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}^{*}-e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}e_{j}\otimes e_{i}^{*}+\{e_{i},e_{j}^{*}\}_{\mathfrak{c}}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{i}^{*}+e_{i}\otimes\{e_{i}^{*},e_{j}\}_{\mathfrak{c}}\otimes e_{j}^{*}

\displaystyle-e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\otimes\{e_{i}^{*},e_{j}^{*}\}_{\mathfrak{c}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(-e_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}e_{j}\otimes e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}^{*}-e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}e_{j}\otimes e_{i}^{*}-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{e_{j}^{*}}e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{i}^{*}-L^{*}_{e_{i}}e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle-e_{i}\otimes L^{*}_{e_{j}}e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{j}^{*}-e_{i}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{*}_{e_{i}^{*}}e_{j}\otimes e_{j}^{*}-e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\otimes\{e_{i}^{*},e_{j}^{*}\}_{\mathfrak{c}}).

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(e_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}e_{j}\otimes e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}^{*}+e_{i}\otimes L^{*}_{e_{j}}e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{j}^{*})=0;

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}e_{j}\otimes e_{i}^{*}+L^{*}_{e_{i}}e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{i}^{*})=0;

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{e_{j}^{*}}e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{i}^{*}-e_{i}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{*}_{e_{i}^{*}}e_{j}\otimes e_{j}^{*}-e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\otimes\{e_{i}^{*},e_{j}^{*}\}_{\mathfrak{c}})=0.

Thus
[[r,r]]=0.
Hence
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}^{\ast}_{r})
is a quasi-triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra.

Moreover, note that
r_{+},r_{-}:\mathfrak{d}^{\ast}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{d}
are given respectively by

r_{+}(\xi,x)=(0,\xi),\quad r_{-}(\xi,x)=(x,0),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

This implies that
I(\xi,x)=(-x,\xi)
, which means that the linear map
I:\mathfrak{d}^{\ast}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{d}
is a linear isomorphism of vector spaces. Therefore,
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}^{\ast}_{r})
is a factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebra.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
be a Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
and
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
a quadratic mock-pre-Lie algebra. Then the quadruple
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},B,\omega)
is called a
quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
if the following compatibility condition holds:

(55)

\displaystyle\omega(Bx,y)+\omega(x,By)+\lambda\omega(x,y)=0,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

The following theorem shows that a factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebra naturally gives rise to a quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra of nonzero weight.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{r}^{*})
be a factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebra with
I=r_{+}-r_{-}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},B,\omega_{I})
is a quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
, where the linear map
B:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega_{I}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are defined respectively by

(56)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda r_{-}\circ I^{-1},

(57)

\displaystyle\omega_{I}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle I^{-1}x,y\rangle,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

Since
r_{+},r_{-}:(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{r})\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
are both mock-pre-Lie algebra homomorphisms, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

(58)

\displaystyle I(I^{-1}x\cdot_{r}I^{-1}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r_{+}-r_{-})(I^{-1}x\cdot_{r}I^{-1}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((I+r_{-})(I^{-1}x\cdot_{r}I^{-1}y)-r_{-}(I^{-1}x\cdot_{r}I^{-1}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((I+r_{-})I^{-1}x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}((I+r_{-})I^{-1}y)-(r_{-}I^{-1}x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{-}I^{-1}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r_{-}I^{-1}x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{-}I^{-1}y)+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle B(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}B(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}(r_{-}I^{-1}x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{-}I^{-1}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}r_{-}(I^{-1}x\cdot_{r}I^{-1}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}r_{-}I^{-1}\Big(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(r_{-}I^{-1}y)+(r_{-}I^{-1}x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(B(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}B(y)+\lambda x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y),

which implies that
B
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Next we show that
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},B,\omega_{I})
is a quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra. Since
I^{*}=-I
, we have

\omega_{I}(x,y)=\langle I^{-1}x,y\rangle=-\langle x,I^{-1}y\rangle=-\omega_{I}(y,x),

which means that
\omega_{I}
is skew-symmetric.

Since the skew-symmetric part
r_{a}
of
r
is
(L,L+R)
-invariant, by Proposition
4.5
,
we can deduce that
I^{-1}\circ L_{x}=-(L_{x}^{*}+R_{x}^{*})\circ I^{-1}
. Thus

\displaystyle\omega_{I}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,z)-\omega_{I}(y,\{x,z\}_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle I^{-1}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y),z\rangle-\langle I^{-1}y,\{x,z\}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle I^{-1}\circ(L_{x}y)+(L^{*}_{x}+R^{*}_{x})\circ(I^{-1}y),z\rangle=0,

which implies that (
11
) holds.

Moreover, by using
r^{\ast}_{-}=r_{+}
and
I=r_{+}-r_{-}
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{I}(x,By)+\omega_{I}(Bx,y)+\lambda\omega_{I}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\Big(\langle I^{-1}(x),r_{-}I^{-1}(y)\rangle+\langle I^{-1}r_{-}I^{-1}(x),y\rangle+\langle I^{-1}x,y\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\langle(-I^{-1}r_{+}I^{-1}+I^{-1}r_{-}I^{-1}+I^{-1})(x),y\rangle=0,

which implies that (
55
) holds.

Therefore,
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},B,\omega_{I})
is a quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
.
∎

At the end of this section, we show that a quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
can give rise to a triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra and factorizable mock-pre-Lie bialgebra, respectively .

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},B,\omega)
be a quadratic Rota-Baxter mock-pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
and

\mathcal{I}_{\omega}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
the induced linear isomorphism given by
\langle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}x,y\rangle:=\omega(x,y).
Then
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
defined by

(59)

r^{B,\omega}_{+}:=B\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g},\quad r^{B,\omega}_{+}(\xi)=r^{B,\omega}(\xi,\cdot),\quad\forall\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},

satisfies
[[r,r]]=0
, and gives rise to a triangular (if
\lambda=0
) or a factorizable (if
\lambda\neq 0
) mock-pre Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{r^{B,\omega}}^{*})
.

Proof.

Since
\omega
is skew-symmetric, let
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}x=\xi,
we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{*}\eta,\xi\rangle=\langle\eta,\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\xi)\rangle=\omega(\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\eta),\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\xi))=-\omega(\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\xi),\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\eta))=-\langle\xi,\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\eta)\rangle,

which means
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=-\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{\ast}
.

Case (1):
When
\lambda=0,
by the fact that
\omega(x,By)+\omega(Bx,y)=0
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\langle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}x,By\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}\circ B(x),y\rangle=0,

which implies that
B^{*}\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}+\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}\circ B=0,
and then

\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\circ B^{*}+B\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=0.

We have

r^{B,\omega}_{-}:=(r^{B,\omega}_{+})^{\ast}=-\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\circ B^{*}=B\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=r^{B,\omega}_{+}.

Thus
r^{B,\omega}\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is symmetric. Define a multiplication
\cdot_{r^{B,\omega}}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by

(60)

\displaystyle\xi\cdot_{r^{B,\omega}}\eta=-(L_{r^{B,\omega}_{+}(\xi)}^{*}+R_{r^{B,\omega}_{+}(\xi)}^{*})\eta+R_{r^{B,\omega}_{-}(\eta)}^{*}\xi.

By the fact that
\omega(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,z)-\omega(y,\{x,z\}_{c})=0
for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\langle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}\circ L_{x}(y),z\rangle+\langle(L_{x}^{*}+R_{x}^{*})\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}(y),z\rangle=0,

which implies that
L_{x}\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=-\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\circ(L_{x}^{*}+R_{x}^{*}).
For all
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\xi\cdot_{r^{B,\omega}}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\Big(-(L_{r^{B,\omega}_{+}(\xi)}^{*}+R_{r^{B,\omega}_{+}(\xi)}^{*})\eta+R_{r^{B,\omega}_{-}(\eta)}^{*}\xi\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{r^{B,\omega}_{+}(\xi)}\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\eta)+\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\circ R_{r^{B,\omega}_{-}(\eta)}^{\ast}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{r^{B,\omega}_{+}(\xi)}\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\eta)+R_{r^{B,\omega}_{-}(\eta)}\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{B,\omega}_{+}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\eta)+\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}r^{B,\omega}_{+}(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\eta)+(\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(B\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\xi)\cdot_{B}(\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\eta),

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}(\xi\cdot_{r^{B,\omega}}\eta)=(\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\xi)\cdot_{B}(\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\eta).

Thus
\cdot_{r^{B,\omega}}
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra multiplication and
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra isomorphism from
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{r^{B,\omega}})
to
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{B})
. By
B:(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{B})\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a mock-pre-Lie algebra homomorphism, we have

r^{B,\omega}_{+}=r^{B,\omega}_{-}=B\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}:(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{r^{B,\omega}})\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})

are both mock-pre-Lie algebra homomorphisms. Therefore, by Theorem
4.7
, we have
[[r,r]]=0
and
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{r^{B,\omega}}^{*})
is a triangular mock-pre-Lie bialgebra.

Case (2):
When
\lambda\neq 0,
by the fact that
\omega(x,By)+\omega(Bx,y)+\lambda\omega(x,y)=0
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\langle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}x,By\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}\circ B(x),y\rangle+\lambda\langle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}x,y\rangle=0,

which implies that
B^{*}\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}+\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}\circ B+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{\omega}^{-1}=0,
and then

\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\circ B^{*}+B\circ\mathcal{I}_{\omega}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=0.
