Sums of Apostol’s Möbius functions of order k k
Reo Terada

Abstract
In 1970, T. M. Apostol introduced the Möbius function μ k \mu_{k} of order k k for all positive integer k k , as a generalization of the Möbius function μ = μ 1 \mu=\mu_{1} . For any integer k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , he proved ∑ n ≤ x μ k ​ ( n ) = A k ​ x + O k ​ ( x 1 / k ​ log ⁡ x ) \sum_{n\leq x}\mu_{k}(n)=A_{k}x+O_{k}\left(x^{1/k}\operatorname{log}x\right) where A k A_{k} is a positive constant. In 2001, A. Bege conjectured both the conditional and unconditional estimates for the sum ∑ n ≤ x , ( n , q ) = 1 μ k ​ ( n ) \sum_{n\leq x,(n,q)=1}\mu_{k}(n) for any positive integer q q . In this paper, we give affirmative solutions to the conditional version of Bege’s conjecture completely and the unconditional one partially. We also give a mean square estimate for the error term.

Introduction

For any complex number
s=\sigma+it
where
\sigma,t\in\mathbb{R}
, we let
\zeta(s)
denote the Riemann zeta-function.
In 1970, T. M. Apostol
[
1
]
introduced the following generalization of the Möbius function
\mu
.
For each fixed positive integer
k
, the Möbius function
\mu_{k}
of order
k
is defined by

\mu_{k}(n)\coloneq\begin{cases}1&if $n=1$,\\
(-1)^{r}&if $n=(p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r})^{k}\prod_{i>r}p_{i}^{a_{i}}$ with $0\leq a_{i}<k$,\\
0&if $p^{k+1}\mid n$ for some prime $p$,\\
1&otherwise.\end{cases}

(1)

Here
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},\ldots
are distinct primes.
In other words,
\mu_{k}(n)
vanishes if
n
is divisible by
p^{k+1}
for some prime
p
;
otherwise,
\mu_{k}(n)
is
1
unless the prime factorization of
n
contains the
k
-th powers of exactly
r
distinct primes,
in which case
\mu_{k}(n)=(-1)^{r}
.
If
k=1
,
\mu_{k}(n)
is the usual Möbius function, that is
\mu_{1}(n)=\mu(n)
.
The asymptotic formula for the summatory function
\sum_{n\leq x}\mu_{k}(n)
was first considered by Apostol
[
1
]
.
For any integer
k\geq 2
and any real number
x\geq 2
, he proved that

\sum_{n\leq x}\mu_{k}(n)=A_{k}x+E_{k}(x)

(2)

holds, where the error term
E_{k}(x)
is
O_{k}\left(x^{1/k}\operatorname{log}x\right)
, and the constant
A_{k}
is given by

A_{k}=\prod_{p}\left(1-2p^{-k}+p^{-(k+1)}\right).

(3)

In 1977, under the Riemann Hypothesis, D. Suryanarayana
[
9
]
showed that

E_{k}(x)=O\left(x^{4k/(4k^{2}+1)}\operatorname{exp}\left(C_{0}\frac{\operatorname{log}x}{\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x}\right)\right),

(4)

where
C_{0}
is an absolute positive constant.

We now consider a more general case by adding the condition that
n
is coprime to
q
.
Let
x\geq 1
, we write for integers
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 1
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\mu_{k}(n)=A_{k,q}x+E_{k,q}(x),

where
E_{k,q}(x)
denotes the error term.
In 2001, A. Bege
[
4
]
proposed the following conjecture on the size of
E_{k,q}(x)
.

Conjecture
.

For any real number
x\geq 3
and any integers
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 1
, we have

E_{k,q}(x)\ll\theta(q)x^{1/k}\operatorname{exp}\left(-D\frac{(\operatorname{log}x)^{3/5}}{(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{1/5}}\right)

(5)

where

A_{k,q}\coloneq\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\prod_{p\nmid q}\left(1-2p^{-k}+p^{-(k+1)}\right),

(6)

\varphi(q)
is Euler’s totient function,
\theta(q)
is the number of positive squarefree divisors of
q
and
D
is an absolute positive constant.
In particular, when
q=1
the conjecture is

E_{k}(x)\ll x^{1/k}\operatorname{exp}\left(-D\frac{(\operatorname{log}x)^{3/5}}{(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{1/5}}\right).

(7)

If the Riemann Hypothesis is true, then the estimate is improved to

E_{k,q}(x)\ll\theta(q)x^{2/(2k+1)}\operatorname{exp}\left(A\frac{\operatorname{log}x}{\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x}\right)

(8)

for any real number
x\geq 3
and any integer
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 1
. Here
A
is an absolute positive constant.
In particular, when
q=1
the conjecture is

E_{k}(x)\ll x^{2/(2k+1)}\operatorname{exp}\left(A\frac{\operatorname{log}x}{\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x}\right).

(9)

In 2023, D. Banerjee, Y. Fujisawa, T. M. Minamide and Y. Tanigawa
[
3
]
improved Apostol’s result
E_{k}(x)\ll_{k}x^{1/k}\operatorname{log}x
to

E_{k}(x)\ll_{k}x^{1/k}\operatorname{exp}\left(-D_{0}k^{-13/5}\frac{(\operatorname{log}x)^{3/5}}{(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{1/5}}\right)

(10)

unconditionally, where
D_{0}
is an absolute positive constant.
This result solves the unconditional part of Bege’s conjecture when
q=1
in a version which the
O
-constant and the constant
D
in (
7
) may depend on
k
.

Recently, in 2025, under the Riemann Hypothesis, G. Martin and C. H. Yip
[
7
]
show that

E_{k}(x)\ll_{k,\varepsilon}x^{1/(k+1)+\varepsilon}

(11)

for any real number
x\geq 1,\varepsilon>0
and any integer
k\geq 2
.
This result gives an affirmative solution to the conditional part of Bege’s conjecture with
q=1
in a version where the
O
-constant in (
9
) may depend on
k
.
They also give the following oscillation result:

E_{k}(x)=B_{k}x^{1/(k+1)}+\operatorname{\Omega}_{\pm}\left(x^{1/(2k)}\operatorname{log}x\right),\quad x\to+\infty.

(12)

where

B_{k}\coloneq\frac{\zeta(1/(k+1))}{\zeta^{2}(k/(k+1))}\prod_{p}\frac{\left(1-2p^{-k/(k+1)}+p^{-1}\right)\left(1-p^{-1}\right)}{\left(1-p^{-k/(k+1)}\right)^{2}}.

(13)

The purpose of this paper is to generalize Banerjee, Fujisawa, Minamide and Tanigawa’s result (
10
) and Martin and Yip’s result (
11
) to the case
q\geq 1
. we also estimate the mean square of the error term.

Theorem 1
.

For any real number
x\geq 3
and any integers
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 1
, we have

E_{k,q}(x)\ll_{k}\theta(q)x^{1/k}\operatorname{exp}\left(-D_{0}k^{-8/5}\frac{(\operatorname{log}x)^{3/5}}{(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{1/5}}\right),

(14)

where
D_{0}
is an absolute positive constant.

Remark
.

Theorem
1
answers a version of the unconditional part of Bege’s conjecture in which the
O
-constant and the constant
D
in (
5
) may depend on
k
in the affirmative.
If
q=1
, it also slightly improves Banerjee, Fujisawa, Minamide and Tanigawa’s result (
10
).

Theorem 2
.

Assume that the Riemann Hypothesis is true.
For any real number
x\geq 3
and any integers
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 1
,

E_{k,q}(x)\ll\theta(q)x^{1/(k+1)}\operatorname{exp}\left(C_{0}\frac{\operatorname{log}x}{\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x}\right)

holds. Here

Remark

Theorem

If

Next, we consider the mean square of the function

\Xi_{k,q}(x)\coloneq E_{k,q}(x)-B_{k,q}x^{1/(k+1)}

(16)

where

\displaystyle B_{k,q}

\displaystyle\coloneq\frac{\varphi(q)J\left(1/(k+1),q\right)q^{(k-2)/(k+1)}\zeta(1/(k+1))}{J^{2}\left(k/(k+1),q\right)\zeta^{2}(k/(k+1))}

(17)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{p\nmid q}\frac{\left(1-2p^{-k/(k+1)}+p^{-1}\right)\left(1-p^{-1}\right)}{\left(1-p^{-k/(k+1)}\right)^{2}}

In other words,

measures the discrepancy of the size of the error term

.

For any real number

and any integers

Note

For any real number
