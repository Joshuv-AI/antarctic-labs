 The research grant addressed in this final report began its most recent 3-year segment June
1, 2022 and terminated May 31, 2025. The work funded by the grant supported our group at
the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) in its collaboration on the nEDM@SNS (SNS
refers to the Spallation Neutron Source) project at the Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL).
This project had as its goal the measurement of a non-zero value of the electric dipole moment
of the neutron (nEDM). If indeed the neutron has a non-zero electric dipole moment, it will tell
us very important information about the Standard Model of Nuclear and Particle Physics. The
Standard Model is remarkably successful in describing much of the world we live in, but we
know it is not complete. A non-zero nEDM would indicate the existence of strong CP violation,
which would be a major addition to the Standard Model.

 It was decided by DOE and NSF late in calendar 2023 not to continue funding the
nEDM@SNS experiment. The precise reasons for this decision were perhaps more financial
than scientific. Certainly the experiment was taking significant resources and time to
implement. But we were also making significant progress and the independent annual review
in the summer of 2023 was quite positive. In any event, following the decision to terminate the

experiment, the grant of relevance here was used to help bring the project at MIT to a close in a
sensible fashion. This included helping the postdoctoral associate (Prajwal Mohanmurthy)
transition to the next stage of his career in as straightforward a way as possible. The PI did not
submit a proposal for a new research grant, as he is now retired and not planning to continue
research in any significant way. We have now used all of the remaining funds to close down
our role in the previous experiment and, as described above, to help our last postdoctoral
associate in as smooth a transition as possible to the next stage of his career.

 This experiment was certainly very important but it was also very technically challenging. A
significant technical challenge was the need for a high-flux low-energy beam of polarized 3He.
Such a source of polarized 3He was needed to provide the final signal that would indicate a non-
zero nEDM. The chief MIT responsibility in the nEDM@SNS experiment was to provide the
source of polarized 3He and we were in fact making very good progress toward this goal. Some
details about what was accomplished during this funding period are given below.

 Our project was building an Atomic Beam Source (ABS) of polarized 3He which would provide
the needed signal in the nEDM@SNS experiment. The ABS equipment items and engineering
manpower were supported by a subaward from the University of Illinois-Urbana Champaign’s
NSF grant, while the postdoctoral effort was supported by this DOE award. The ABS consisted
of three main components: (i) a cryogenic chamber (Figure 1) generating a ~1K beam via a two-
pot cooling system and a Microchannel Plate (MCP) nozzle, (ii) a quadrupole magnet section for
polarization, and (iii) a characterization section with residual gas analyzers. Major upgrades
could include larger pots, a new MCP nozzle with steering actuators, ultrahigh vacuum
capabilities, and a gas-handling panel integrating precision mass flow controllers and actuated
valves. These possible upgrades are expected to be described in an imminent publication in the
Review of Scientific Instruments.

  By April 2023, installation of temperature sensors, actuated valves, and gas panel
components was completed. Vacuum tests demonstrated pressures as low as 15 nTorr after
chamber bake-out, sufficient for stable polarized beam transport. The ABS incorporates key
advances enabling long-term stable delivery of polarized 3He.

 Additional work on the project included construction of a mezzanine platform (Figure 2) that
provided structural and utility support for the ABS, including gas lines, power outlets, and
electronic racks. The mezzanine was intended to support the ABS in an orientation that would
have provided the polarized 3He beam in a vertical direction as required by the nEDM@SNS
experiment. The design work for vertical operation was complete, necessary parts were
ordered, and assembly work was underway when the experiment was cancelled. Components
have been safely stored at MIT-Bates for potential future use on other experiments that require
a source of polarized 3He.

Figure 1: Engineering drawing of the cryogenic chamber, showing the cold head, 4K and 1K
pots, MCP nozzle with actuation, and skimmers.

Figure 2: Layout of the mezzanine at the MIT Bates Laboratory, including electrical racks, gas
lines, and integration of turbo pumps with analyzer and quadrupole sections.

  Complementary experiments which are also designed to measure a non-zero nEDM are
underway at several laboratories around the world. Our experiment had important features
which, if functioning at the planned level, would have made it the most sensitive of all of the
experiments. It is of course not possible to predict precisely how sensitive any complicated
experiment will be in the end.
