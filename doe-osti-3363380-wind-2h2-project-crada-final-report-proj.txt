 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 The Xcel-NLR Wind Electrolysis R&D Project is designed to gain knowledge and insight about
 producing hydrogen from electricity for use as a vehicle fuel and as a “stored wind”. The project
 will analyze producing hydrogen directly from wind as well as from the electric grid. In addition,
 it will look at storing hydrogen and then using it to put electricity back onto the grid.

 Task 7: Solar Commercialization Testing Facility

 The purpose of this task is to develop an initial scoping document for a Solar Commercialization
 Test Facility (SoComm). NLR shall work with Xcel, the University of Colorado, the Colorado
 School of Mines, and Colorado State University to develop this report.

 This task will result in a report that details the following information:

 • Purpose for the facility that synthesizes each entity's objectives into a vision/mission-type
 statements.
 • Catalog of potential solar energy technologies to be tested at SoComm.
 • Recommendations for the prospective area needed to house the facility. Included in this
 section will be an assessment of the viability of using the NLR Visitor Center site as the
 location for SoComm. The prospect of having multiple sites will be explored (e.g. PV at
 NLR site, CSP at a university site or at the NLR NWTC).
 • Estimate the facilities and testing/evaluation infrastructure that will be needed to operate
 the facility including: land prep at the NLR site, grid interconnection requirements,
 hardware mounting requirements, and catalog of testing, computer, data
 storage/processing, communications equipment needed at the facility.
 • Estimate the personnel and other expenses necessary to operate SoComm. This will
 include an estimate of FTEs with consideration for student participation as well as
 incidental operations and maintenance requirements.
 • Prospective budget for start-up and operations of SoComm.

 Task 7 Results:

 A description, mission, and vision of the Solar Commercialization Test Facility (SoComm) was
 developed. The SoComm facility was renamed the Solar Technology Advancement Center
 (SolarTAC) during the project. SolarTAC is a collaboration among Xcel Energy, the National
 Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR), Colorado State University (CSU), Colorado School of Mines,
 and the University of Colorado at Boulder (CU) formed to accelerate pre-commercial and early
 commercial solar energy technologies to the marketplace. The plan for SolarTAC consisted of a
 central testing and showcase/education facility, and possible satellite sites that might also have
 some testing and education/outreach activities.

 Task 10: Supporting Analysis for the Wind Predictor Tool

 Xcel Energy will provide proprietary information to NLR for projects supporting the
 development of the Wind Predictor (WiP) Tool. The proprietary information includes turbine-
 level and metrological data associated with wind plants in Xcel Energy’s service territory. The
 project has three components: (I) Quality control of the data stream, (2) wind power plant
 analysis, and (3) analysis of wind events. Step one is to develop a quality control methodology to
 ensure data stream integrity to be implemented at Xcel Energy. Step two is to develop, wind
 plant by wind plant, power curves for based on wind speed, wind direction, turbine availability,
 and air density (derived from barometric pressure data), and other related proprietary
 information. These empirical power curves will be implemented in the Wind Predictor Tool. In
 step three, NLR will analyze historical wind plant data and associated meteorlogical data to
 evaluate wind plant events. This will include characterization and analysis of wind plant ramping

 4

 events for short-term forecasting purposes. Metrics for analyzing individual wind plant events
 will be developed and incorporated into Wind Predictor Tool.

 Task 10 Results:

 This work was used to develop and validate the NLR Wind Integration National Dataset (WIND)
 Toolkit. The WIND Toolkit spans the years from 2007 to 2013 using advanced numerical
 weather prediction (NWP) methods run on a nationwide 2-kilometer grid with 5-minute
 resolution, providing data at more than 110,000 onshore and offshore wind power production
 sites. The WIND Toolkit was produced with the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF)
 model version 3.4.1. By the end of the project, it will provide high-resolution NWP model output
 throughout a seven-year timeframe (2007 to 2013) at 5-min temporal resolution. This data is
 converted into publicly available wind power production time series and simulated operational
 forecasts for 1-hour, 4-hour, 6-hour, and day-ahead forecast horizons at the 110,000 sites
 selected. The available parameters will include power, barometric pressure, wind speed and
 direction (at 100 meters), relative humidity, temperature, and air density.

 Task 11: Wind Farm Wake Measurements and Modeling

 Xcel Energy will provide Proprietary information to NLR for projects supporting the
 development of models to study the Wake effects from wind turbines. The proprietary
 information includes turbine-level and metrological data associated with wind plants in Xcel
 Energy’s service territory. These models will be used to enhance both the design and operation
 of wind farms. Wake effects from wind turbines operating within wind farms are poorly
 understood, particularly in “large’ wind farms, meaning those with more than 4 rows on a side.
 This lack of understanding and also a lack of accurate modeling tools directly contribute to an
 underperformance problem that many wind farms in the US are experiencing, where energy
 production can be 10% lower than originally estimated. Poor wind farm design can result in large
 financial losses for wind farm owners and operators. For those systems that already in operation,
 the models can be used to predict the effect that wakes have on their specific farm, which when
 coupled with forecasting models may lead to better estimates of power plant performance. A
 byproduct of the analysis is an estimate of individual power curves for each operating turbine.
 NLR researchers will analyze Xcel Energy operational data to identify the wake strength of each
 turbine in the farm as a function of many variables, including inflow wind speed, wind direction,
 number of upwind turbines, atmosphere stability, etc. Ideally, the data will consist of 10 minute
 (or better) averages of individual turbine operational and metrological data that can be mined to
 quantify the wake effects. Wake analyses will be compared to standard tool predictions of wind
 turbine wakes (e.g. WaSP) and also more advanced tools currently being developed (statistically
 and CFD based). Results from these comparisons will help quantify the uncertainty of current
 wake models and also be used to develop the next generation of models for future operations.
 Any data that is collected as part of this task will be considered Proprietary Information and
 treated in the manner as described in the subject CRADA, Article VII, Obligations as to
 Proprietary information.

 5

 Task 11 Results:

 The information provided was used to develop a methodology for analyzing how ambient and
 wake turbulence affects the power generation of a single wind turbine within an array of
 turbines. Using monitoring data from a wind power plant, we selected two sets of wind and
 power data for turbines on the edge of the wind plant that resemble (i) an out-of-wake scenario
 (i.e., when the turbine directly faces incoming winds) and (ii) an in-wake scenario (i.e., when the
 turbine is under the wake of other turbines). For each set of data, two surrogate models were then
 developed to represent the turbine power generation (i) as a function of the wind speed; and (ii)
 as a function of the wind speed and turbulence intensity. Support vector regression was adopted
 for the development of the surrogate models.

 Three types of uncertainties in the turbine power generation were also investigated: (i) the
 uncertainty in power generation with respect to the published/reported power curve, (ii) the
 uncertainty in power generation with respect to the estimated power response that accounts for
 only mean wind speed; and (iii) the uncertainty in power generation with respect to the estimated
 power response that accounts for both mean wind speed and turbulence intensity.

 Task 12: Western Wind and Solar Integration Study

 Xcel Energy will provide proprietary information to NLR to support the Western Wind and Solar
 Integration Study (WWSIS). The Proprietary Information includes information regarding power
 plant operation and costs in the PSCO territory, which can help the WWSIS model the WECC
 grid with more fidelity. This includes information regarding the operations of the PSCO coal
 units and how much flexibility can be extracted from them to accommodate wind and solar
 energy. It also includes costs of extracting this flexibility from the coal units, since there will be
 increased wear and tear on those units, resulting in higher O&M costs. This also includes wind
 and solar output information that is used in the WWSIS to improve modeling of those resources
 and development of hypothetical wind and solar plant output datasets. Any data that is collected
 as part of this task will be considered Proprietary Information and treated in the manner as
 described in the subject CRADA, Article VII, Obligations as to Proprietary information.

 Task 12 Results:

 Information was used to support the Western Wind and Solar Integration Study (WWSIS) which
 investigated the operational impact of up to 35% energy penetration of wind, photovoltaics (PV),
 and concentrating solar power (CSP) on the power system operated by the WestConnect group of
 utilities in Arizona, Colorado, Nevada, New Mexico, and Wyoming1. WWSIS was conducted
 over two and a half years by a team of researchers in wind power, solar power, and utility
 operations, with oversight from technical experts in these fields. This report discusses the
 development of data inputs, the design of scenarios to address key issues, and the analysis and
 sensitivity studies that were conducted to answer questions about the integration of wind and
 solar power on the grid.
