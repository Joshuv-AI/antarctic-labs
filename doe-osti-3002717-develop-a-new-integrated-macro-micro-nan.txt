 1. ABSTRACT

Bending tests provide a means to study plane strain fracture performance of 6000 series aluminum alloys.
Metrics from bending tests have been correlated with self-pierce riveting (SPR) performance of a high
strength AA6111 automotive aluminum alloy in previous works [1]. Using the ORNL HPC resources,
this project developed an innovative macro→micro←nano (MMN) multiscale microstructure-based finite
element (FE) code to further the understanding of microstructural relationship to fracture properties of
high strength 6000 series alloys.
This work started with microstructural characterization in both mesoscale and nanoscale and bending
performance characterization of AA6111 HS2-T6 alloy at Ford, the MMN framework was applied to this
alloy to simulate 3-point VDA bending. From the results of the macro-modeling of 3-point VDA bending,
the critical region of fracture was identified, and the region geometry was used to construct the micro-
model. The fracture criterion of micron-scale precipitates and aluminum matrix which contains submicron
and nano particles (AL-SMP-NP) in the micro-model was calibrated and validated by comparing
simulated and measured bending results. With the AL-SMP-NP fracture strain obtained, the fracture
strain of Al-matrix containing nano particles (ALNP) will similarly be determined by a submicron scale-
model using an edge-constrained FE modeling approach developed by Hu et al. [5], [6]. With the ALNP
fracture strain obtained, the fracture strain of Al-matrix containing no particles will similarly be
determined by a Nano scale-model using an edge-constrained FE modeling approach.
After the MMN framework is built and fracture criterion calibrated, nano-model FE simulations with
virtual microstructures was performed to obtain a reduced order model (ROM) of the fracture criterion of
the ALNP as a function of volume fraction, size, and distribution of the nanoparticle. This
nano→submicron→micro modeling part allows exploration of the influence of different material
nanostructures from different process conditions on the bending properties within the multiscale bending
simulation framework and the ROM of material bendability as a function of nanoparticle size and shape
was established.
This obtained reduced order model (ROM) could help guide the design and selection materials to improve
existing SPR process models that could replace trial-and-error rivet/die selection and help to design new
rivet/die combinations capable of robustly joining new higher strength 6000 series alloys in automotive
body structures. This would enable lightweighting of Ford vehicles leading to greater fuel efficiency and
reduce manufacturing time and energy.
 2. STATEMENT OF OBJECTIVES

The objective and technical scope of this proposed project is to develop and apply the MMN
multiscale finite element framework to predict the bending performance of high strength
automotive aluminum sheet alloys and guide the design of its optimum process conditions for
best SPR rivet-ability for vehicle light weighting. This will start with the acquisition of an
AA6111 alloy with T6 temper condition. After microstructural characterization in both
mesoscale and nanoscale and bending performance characterization at Ford, the MMN
framework will be applied to this alloy to simulate 3-point bending. Starting from macro-model
of 3-point bending, the critical region of fracture will be identified, and the region geometry will
be used to construct the micro-model. The fracture criterion of micron-scale precipitates and
ALNP matrix in the micro-model will be calibrated and validated by comparing simulated and
measured bending results. With the ALNP fracture strain obtained, the fracture strain of Al-
matrix in ALNP will similarly be determined by the nano-model using an edge-constrained FE
modeling approach (Fig. 2) developed by Hu et al. [5], [6].

 4

After the MMN framework is built and fracture criterion calibrated, massive number of nano-
model FE simulations will be performed to obtain a reduced order surrogate model (ROSM) of
the fracture criterion of the ALNP as a function of volume fraction, size, and distribution of the
nanoparticle and PFZ. This nano → micro modeling part allows exploration of the influence of
different material nanostructures from different process conditions on the bending properties
within the multiscale bending simulation framework. Further experimental validations will be
done to verify the simulated bending performance of the material with different process
conditions.
 3. BENEFITS TO THE FUNDING DOE OFFICE’S MISSION

The project will ensure accurate prediction of the microstructure effects on bendability and rivetability of
high strength automotive Al sheet alloys, giving guidance to the process optimization of the alloy process
to reach the department of energy goal considerable vehicle light-weighting and cost-cutting and promote
the EV adoption and substantial decarbonization.

4.2.1 Tension test
Before setting up for the macro model, it is important to obtain the material plastic flow behavior and it is
usually performed through tensile tests. For this purpose, Ford has tensile-tested the AA6111 HS2-T6
alloy using … tensile test machine at a constant cross-head speed of … with ASTM E-8 standard tensile
specimen as shown in Fig. 5, which has a gauge length of 57 mm and width of 12.5 mm.
Flat ASTM E8 tensile coupons were electro discharged machined (EDM’d) from the supplied sheet
materials. The coupons were cut in three different directions, where the tensile axis was either 0°
(longitudinal, L), 45° (diagonal, D), or 90° (transverse, T) to the rolling direction. A portion of the tensile
coupons were artificially aged at 180°C +/- 2°C for 8 hours to a T6 temper using a Blue M electric
convection air furnace. Tensile specimens were tensile tested at a constant rate of 3.0 mm/min until
failure using a servohydraulic MTS load frame. Surface strain of the gauge region was measured during
the tensile tests using an ARAMIS 3D 5M stereo camera digital image correlation (DIC) system using
GOM Correlate Pro 2021 software. Two Schneider Xenoplane 23 mm f1.4 camera lens were used, each
mounted to an ARAMIS 5M (GigE), 5 Megapixel sensor. The two sensor-lens assemblies were mounted
on an adjustable bar so that the camera distance—defined as—the minimum distance between the center
of the sensor housings, was 165 mm. The working measurement distance—defined as—the distance from
the midpoint of the camera distance to the middle of the tensile specimen, was 460 mm. The angle
between the two optical camera axes was 25. 189°. Using this configuration, the system was calibrated
using the cp20-175mmx140mm calibration panel, which resulted in a calibrated measurement volume of
145 mm long, 125 mm wide, and 105 mm deep. Where the long edge of the volume was parallel with the
tensile axis of the tensile specimen and the center of the volume coincided with the midpoint of the tensile
gauge region. The calibration deviation (epipolar error) was 0.026 pixels and minimum displacement
sensitivity was 0.002 mm. Further the region of interest (ROI) was defined as gauge area of the tensile
specimen. A random speckle pattern was applied to the gauge region of the specimens prior to testing.
This was done by first masking the specimens, then spraying painting them with a flat white spray paint,

following by applying a black speckles using flat black spray paint, removing mask so that the ROI was
covered in a random speckle patter.

During tensile testing multiple image capture rates were used. This was done to minimize the number of
saved images to facilitate speedier post processing while not compromising on accurate characterization
of the elastic, initial yield and fracture portions of the stress strain curve. During tensile testing of the T4
specimens a fixed frame rate of 9 Hz was used to capture elastic and initial yield behavior. After 90
images were captured, the image capture rate reduced to 5 Hz with a 50-frame ring buffer. This means
that at any given point after the post yield phase only the last 50 images were saved at a 5 Hz frame rate,
while frames prior to the last 50 images were saved at a reduced frame rate of 1/3 Hz. The T6 specimens
used a similar image capture regime. However for the T6 specimens a 7 Hz frame rate was used for the
elastic and initial yield portion up to 170 images. After 170 images were captured, frame rate switched to

images were processed using a facet size of 21 pixels and a point distance of 9 pixels. Facet matching was
done against the previous stage for the collected image sequence. The center of the facets corresponded to
nodes of a triangular mesh. The full 3D displacement field was then measured throughout the test. Strain
was computed from the displacement measuremetns. Stress-strain curves were generated where strain was
defined as average major strain in the ROI (gauge region) at each image index.

specimens and capture true plain strain behavior in the center region of the specimen. A random speckle
pattern was applied to the center of the bend specimens. This was done in a similar way as above by first

speckle patter. During three point bend testing images were captured at a rate of 20 Hz with a ring buffer
of 260. Frames prior to the last 260 were captured at a reduced rate of 5 Hz. Collected DIC images were
processed using a facet size of 21 pixels and a point distance of 8 pixels. This resulted in 3-5 features per
facet, resulting in a pixel resolution of ~25μm/pixel. Facet matching was done against the previous stage
for the collected image sequence. The center of the facets corresponded to nodes of a triangular mesh.
The full 3D displacement field was then measured throughout the test. Equivalent plastic strain was then
computed from the displacement measurements.
The fracture initiation stage was then visually identified by the user. This was done by first identifying the
largest crack to occur in the test. Once the largest crack was identified the user worked backwards in the
DIC image sequence until the largest crack fully closed. Then working forwards the first opening of this
largest crack was visually identified. An opening was defined as a region of separated paint just exposing
the metal substrate. This first opening was typically less than 21 pixels (facet length). The image frame
just prior to the first opening was selected as the fracture initiation stage. Then a script was used to (1)
label the selected point as the fracture initiation point; (2) draw multiple square regions around the point.
The square regions were 0.250, 0.500, 1.000, and 2.000 mm in length. In each square region the average
major strain, average minor strain, and average effective plastic strain was computed. The final step (3)
was to output time, force, and the average strains in each box for each DIC frame. Crosshead
displacement was calculated from the time data by multiplying by 1 mm/s.

For the AA6111 HS2-T6 alloy, we use the Ludwik law to represent its flow behavior, while the elasticity
is represented by a Young’s modulus of 70GPa and 0.33, which is typical for aluminum alloys. Since the
bending direction, i.e. the tension side (the top surface) tensile direction, is along the L direction, we

bending simulations. The results of using the parameters of sample L03 (see Table 1), the bending loads
calculated are similar to those of experiments initially, but the value increase faster. The calculated peak

 16

load (2223N) is about 17% higher than those of experiments (~1900N) (see Fig. 15a). This could be due
to deformation anisotropy.
The bending deformation is in plane strain deformation mode with tension on the tension side and
compression on the compression side, while uniaxial tension is closer to plane stress tension. The
different deformation mode could lead to different dislocation accumulation rate and hence different work
hardening rate. Therefore, the work hardening parameters need to be modified for bending deformation to
reflect the strain mode difference with uniaxial tension.
Since the initial loads are similar at the elastic-plastic transition of the load-displacement curves between
FE calculations and experiments, a parametric study for the macro-bending simulation has been
performed on k in equation 4 and Table 1 and with a factor of 0.65, the predicted load-displacement
curves match well with the experiments. The modified parameters (319.27, 295.54*0.65,0.5993) will then
be used for all the other models of different scales.

 8. CONCLUSIONS

This developed multiscale macro→micro←nano or MMN modeling approach is successfully
used to obtain the correlations between material microstructure of different scale of AA6111
alloys and bendability in terms of crack initiation strain on the surface. A reduced order model
(RPM) is hence obtained to relate the material nano-scale microstructure to its bendability, while
assuming the submicron scale and meso-scale microstructure are similar and It was successfully
used to predict the bendability of 6HS2-T6 and 6HS4-T6 alloys, but predicts higher crack
initiation strain in the 6HS2-T4 and 6HS4-T4 alloys than DIC measurement. The reason of this
deviation may be due to the different microstructure and properties of the material on the surface
than that of the bulk, since the surface cracks does not connects and propagate through the
thickness.
