Executive Summary
This final technical report synthesizes major research results for DOE award DE-SC0022207. The project sought to
identify metabolic control points and bottlenecks that regulate flux to free fatty acids (FFAs) in Picosynechococcus
sp. PCC 7002 by treating lipid metabolism as modular pathways upstream and downstream of acetyl-CoA.
Deletion of the acyl-ACP synthetase gene (aas) together with expression of a modified Cuphea palustris
thioesterase (CupTE) established a C8-FFA production platform, and co-expression of the initiating ketosynthase
chKASIII and the elongating ketosynthase ecFabF* increased relative carbon flux to octanoic acid (C8-FFA) by
approximately six-fold while lowering modeled minimum selling price by approximately four-fold, despite slower
growth and lower total carbon fixation.
Complementary work delivered enabling systems-biology tools. An anion-exchange chromatography (AEX)
workflow improved microbial acyl-ACP quantification and exposed a previously underappreciated role for AAS in
membrane lipid remodeling. A Fast-Pass desorption electrospray ionization-mass spectrometry imagining (DESI-
MSI) method reduced untargeted metabolic phenotyping to roughly 40 s per sample. CRISPRi screens at high cell
density and elevated CO2 nominated candidate regulatory and physiological targets for improved industrial fitness.
Taken together, the supplied outputs show that fatty acid overproduction in PCC 7002 is limited not by a single
enzyme alone but by interacting constraints across fatty acid initiation, elongation, acyl-chain recycling, precursor
balance, carbon-nitrogen homeostasis, and stress physiology. The project therefore advanced both mechanistic
understanding and a practical engineering framework for photosynthetic oleochemical production.

1. Project objectives and scope
The overall objective of the project was to apply systems biology to identify metabolic control points and
bottlenecks that regulate FFA production in PCC 7002, with lipid metabolism organized conceptually into pathways
upstream and downstream of the nodal metabolite acetyl-CoA. The two specific aims were to identify upstream
control points governing acetyl-CoA precursor availability and to assess downstream bottlenecks in the fatty acid
biosynthesis (FAB) pathway.

 Central hypothesis of the project: cyanobacterial lipid metabolism can be modularized into pathways upstream
 and downstream of acetyl-CoA, enabling separate systems-level analysis and coordinated "push-pull"
 optimization of carbon flux to free fatty acids.

2. Enabling systems-biology toolkit
PCC 7002 is a compelling photosynthetic chassis because of its rapid growth, halotolerance, and increasingly
mature synthetic-biology toolkit, including inducible gene-expression systems and genome-engineering methods
(Clark et al., 2018; Markley et al., 2015; Zess et al., 2016). The project combined this host with multiomics,
targeted acyl-ACP measurements, high-throughput ambient mass spectrometry, and pooled CRISPRi screening to
move beyond one-gene/one-phenotype engineering logic.

2.1 Fast untargeted phenotyping by DESI-MSI
We developed a DESI-MSI "Fast-Pass" line-scan workflow to accelerate untargeted metabolic phenotyping
(Shepard et al., 2025). Although the demonstration system used engineered E. coli FFA strains, the method
addresses the same design-build-test bottleneck faced in cyanobacterial oleochemical engineering: it is faster to
generate new engineered strains by high-throughput mutational techniques (e.g., transposon or CRISPRi libraries)
than to characterize them analytically.

Figure 2. DESI Fast-Pass workflow for rapid, location-resolved untargeted phenotyping. Adapted from Shepard et al. (2025), Figure 3.

The workflow preserved untargeted chemical information while reducing acquisition time to approximately 40 s
per sample and eliminating the need for post-acquisition image segmentation to define regions of interest. We
showed that the method resolved both target FFA products and broader metabolic differences among strains,
enabling a rapid screening platform that can be followed by deeper LC-MS or ion-mobility workflows when
warranted.

3. Major technical results in fatty-acid pathway engineering
In cyanobacteria, residual FFAs are normally reactivated by acyl-ACP synthetase (AAS), so productive FFA secretion
generally requires aas disruption together with expression of a heterologous thioesterase (Kaczmarzyk & Fulda,
2010; Kahn et al., 2023; Ruffing, 2014). Prior in vitro work suggested that the initiating ketosynthase FabH was the
dominant control point in PCC 7002 FAB, and prior in vivo engineering showed that a diatom-derived initiating
enzyme could improve medium-chain fatty acid production (Kuo & Khosla, 2014; Gu et al., 2016). We extended
this logic by integrating aas deletion and the highly active C8-selective CupTE thioesterase, then testing whether
initiation and elongation could be amplified sequentially by expressing chKASIII and ecFabF* enzymes (Hernandez
Lozada et al., 2018; Zuniga et al., 2026).
CupTE expression from a single genomic locus enabled C8-FFA accumulation in PCC 7002 without strong toxicity,
establishing the base production condition (denoted as “T” for “termination”) used for later pathway analysis. This
was a major milestone because it supplied a robust C8-termination module in an industrially promising marine
cyanobacterial host.
The critical mechanistic question was whether control would shift downstream once initiation and termination
activities were both enhanced. The answer was yes: ecFabF* improved C8-FFA production only when initiation
and termination had already been amplified, revealing that the value of amplifying the elongation step depends
on the pathway state into which it is introduced.

 Figure 3. Sequential pathway amplification shows that relative flux to C8-FFA rises strongly when initiation, elongation, and
 termination are co-optimized. Adapted from Zuniga et al. (2026), Figure 3.

Figure 3 captures the central downstream result. The IT condition (CupTE + chKASIII) sharply reduced growth and
absolute C8 production but increased relative flux toward product. Adding ecFabF* to generate the IET condition
restored absolute C8-FFA production to approximately the level of the T condition while driving the C8/biomass
flux ratio above 1. In other words, product became the major carbon sink. Relative carbon flux to C8-FFA increased
 Page 5

by roughly six-fold versus the base CupTE strain, consistent with a new elongation bottleneck emerging once
initiation control had been relieved.
Flux-control analysis supported this interpretation. ecFabF* exhibited unfavorable control when added to the T
background alone, but a positive control coefficient when added to the IT background. This is an important
systems-biology result: the sign and magnitude of control depended on pathway context, not simply on the
nominal function of the engineered enzyme.
The same study also clarified why better carbon partitioning did not automatically yield a healthier production
phenotype. Acyl-ACP measurements showed persistent accumulation of C4:0 and C6:0 intermediates and
depletion of the C8:0 thioesterase substrate, indicating that chain termination was not the dominant residual
limitation and that at least one elongation-step bottleneck remained unresolved. Central-metabolite and
proteomic analyses further revealed depletion of TCA-cycle-linked intermediates, alpha-ketoglutarate-associated
carbon-nitrogen imbalance, downregulation of nitrogen assimilation proteins, antioxidant-enzyme induction, and
broader proteotoxic stress under IET expression (Forchhammer & Selim, 2020; Zhang et al., 2018; Zuniga et al.,
2026).
This systems view is particularly relevant to the project's upstream objective. Feeding labeled acetate and
pyruvate increased intracellular acetyl-CoA abundance and delivered substantial 13C into lipogenic fatty acids, yet
it had little effect on C8-FFA synthesis and did not fully rescue growth. The available evidence therefore argues
against a simple acetyl-CoA-limitation model and instead points to integrated precursor, redox, and biomass-
balance constraints upstream of product formation.

4. AAS biology and acyl-ACP analytics
A major enabling advance of the project was the refinement of acyl-ACP measurement for microbial systems.
Building on earlier LC-MS/MS methods for acyl-ACP discovery and quantification (Nam et al., 2020; Jenkins et al.,
2021), we developed an anion-exchange chromatography (AEX) workflow that improved recovery of long-chain
and low-abundance microbial acyl-ACP intermediates. The method also incorporated boiling of cell pellets to
suppress endogenous thioesterase activity and better preserve the native acyl-ACP distribution.
Applied to PCC 7002, the AEX workflow showed that low-temperature acclimation in wild-type cells was
accompanied by higher levels of unsaturated glycerolipids and unsaturated acyl-ACPs together with stronger
desaturase and lipid-turnover signatures. By contrast, the aas knockout displayed lower pools of short- to long-
chain acyl-ACPs, diminished induction of lipid-remodeling proteins, slower growth at reduced temperature, and a
constrained membrane-remodeling response.

 Figure 4. Acyl-ACP pools and lipid-turnover proteins in wild-type PCC 7002 versus the aas knockout at 30°C and 37°C. Adapted from
 Jaroensuk et al. (2026), Figure 5.

One of the most important observations was the persistence of unsaturated and even polyunsaturated acyl-ACP
species in the aas knockout. Because cyanobacterial fatty-acid synthesis does not normally generate
polyunsaturated acyl-ACPs directly, these measurements imply that alternative acyl-activating routes or related
compensatory mechanisms remain active when AAS is absent. For metabolic engineering, this means that aas
deletion is essential for FFA secretion but not physiologically neutral: acyl-chain recycling and membrane
remodeling must be treated as part of strain design, especially under temperature, salinity, light, or density stress.
These findings strengthen the interpretation of the C8-production study. The same acyl-ACP-centered view
explains both why downstream edits changed product partitioning and why membrane lipid physiology remained
tightly coupled to strain fitness. In practical terms, the project converted acyl-ACP measurement from a niche
pathway assay into a core diagnostic tool for cyanobacterial lipid engineering.

5. Industrial-condition CRISPRi findings
The project also extended pooled CRISPR interference to industrially relevant conditions. The screening
framework built on the high-density PCC 7002 CRISPRi library reported by Hren et al. (2025) and was applied in
photobioreactor or chemostat formats to high cell density, 4% CO2, and 30% CO2 conditions using inducible sgRNA
and dCas9 expression (Abraham, Ph.D. dissertation Chapter 6; Hren et al., 2025).

 Page 7

 Figure 5. Experimental design for pooled CRISPRi screening of PCC 7002 under industrially relevant conditions. Adapted from
 Abraham, Ph.D. dissertation, Figure 6.1.

Across these screens, enriched guides pointed toward a diverse set of candidate beneficial knockdowns. At high
cell density, candidate targets included photosynthetic and energy-balance functions such as psbL and ndhF, the
translation-release factor prfC, and several regulatory genes. Under 4% CO2, enriched guides included regulatory
proteins such as SixA and SasA, motility-related functions, crtR, and cpcB. Under 30% CO2, strong enrichment was
observed for cheY, dut, and several regulatory or DNA/RNA-handling genes, including recA, degT, tig, a DNA
helicase, and sensory or response regulators.
We propose these genes as targets for follow-up strain construction rather than fully validated causal genes. Even
so, the screen outcomes are valuable because they shift attention from pathway enzymes alone to whole-cell
traits that control industrial performance: motility cost, photosystem balance, chemotaxis, nucleotide metabolism,
and stress regulation. In the context of our current project, these results provide a path toward combining high-
yield lipid production with better growth under dense or carbon-rich cultivation.

6. Process modeling and technoeconomic significance
To connect pathway engineering to process-level impact, we coupled experimental rate measurements to the
BioSTEAM simulation framework and to the linear-phase growth formalism previously developed for PCC 7002
cultures (Clark et al., 2018; Cortes-Pena et al., 2020; Zuniga et al., 2026). The model treated production as the
second stage of a two-stage process in which biomass is first accumulated and then induced for C8-FFA synthesis
during the linear-growth phase.

  Figure 6. Two-stage process model and relative minimum selling price advantage of the IET condition. Adapted from Zuniga et al.
                                                        (2026), Figure 9.

The analysis showed that economics depended more strongly on carbon partitioning than on absolute volumetric
productivity alone. Although IET cultures did not substantially outperform the T strain in absolute C8-FFA
production rate, they directed a much larger fraction of fixed carbon to product and therefore achieved a modeled
minimum selling price approximately four-fold lower than the T condition. By contrast, the IT condition improved
relative allocation but penalized volumetric productivity so severely that reactor-related costs dominated.
This result matters beyond a single strain comparison. It shows that in photosynthetic bioprocesses, growth
suppression is not automatically detrimental if carbon is diverted productively during the production phase and
pre-induction growth remains unchanged. The model therefore provides a principled basis for prioritizing
partitioning, secretion, and downstream recovery together rather than optimizing titer or growth in isolation.

7. Integrated conclusions and future directions
Taken together, the research findings support three central conclusions. First, the downstream "pull" module of
cyanobacterial fatty-acid production can be engineered rationally: CupTE established effective C8 chain
termination in PCC 7002, chKASIII and ecFabF* shifted pathway control, and acyl-ACP measurements made the
resulting bottlenecks visible. Second, the physiological cost of rerouting carbon is substantial and distributed
across central metabolism, nitrogen balance, redox stress, and membrane remodeling. Third, the project produced
enabling analytics and screening platforms that make these interacting constraints experimentally tractable in
future design-build-test-learn cycles.
For the upstream "push" module, the research fundings suggest that the relevant control problem is broader than
acetyl-CoA pool size. Exogenous acetate and pyruvate readily entered lipogenic acetyl-CoA, yet C8-FFA output
remained constrained, indicating that future push strategies must co-optimize precursor balance across PEP,
oxaloacetate, and TCA-cycle intermediates as well as regulatory responses linked to alpha-ketoglutarate and
nitrogen assimilation.
 Page 9

The most immediate next steps suggested by the project are to identify the residual elongation-phase
bottleneck(s), test additional dynamic or condition-responsive control of fatty-acid and central-carbon nodes, and
validate CRISPRi-derived targets that improve fitness under high-density and high-CO2 operation. The combination
of AEX acyl-ACP profiling, pooled CRISPRi, and faster phenotyping by Fast-Pass DESI-MSI provides a strong
platform for that next phase of modular metabolic engineering.
