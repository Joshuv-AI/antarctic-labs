Commercial integration of advanced nuclear energy with Artificial Intelligence (AI):
Possible implications

Zhehui Wang, zwang@lanl.gov;
Los Alamos National Laboratory, Los Alamos, NM 87545, USA

(First version: Nov. 3, 2025; Updated on Jan. 12, 2026)

Summary: The integration of advanced nuclear technologies (both fission and fusion) with
artificial intelligence (AI) presents unprecedented national security challenges and opportunities.
As fusion energy approaches commercial viability alongside advanced Small Modular Reactors
(SMRs), their integration with AI and Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) systems could
fundamentally transform the global energy and AI landscapes — two pillars of national security.
This document briefly examines how AI could accelerate nuclear energy development and
deployment while altering existing power structures, a lot could be done to deepen the discussions.
Simultaneously, it observes how nuclear-powered AI may expedite advances toward AGI and
beyond. These issues are deeply interconnected and thus need to be examined as a whole and more
comprehensively than what’s being summarized here. For instance, AI-powered autonomous
operation of nuclear facilities could reduce human error but introduce new cybersecurity
vulnerabilities and uncertainties. Further investigation would also address how AI-enhanced
nuclear technologies might complicate proliferation concerns through advanced fuel cycle
management, nuclear materials production and safeguard. The strategic advantage gained by first
entities achieving successful AI-nuclear integration could reshape global and national security
framework. Timely analysis of these implications may be crucial for policymakers seeking to
harness these technologies' benefits while effectively mitigating their potential risks.

Background: Conventional energy sources, whether fossil fuels or renewable alternatives such as
solar, hydroelectric, and wind, face fundamental power density limitations that could prove
insufficient for the massive computational infrastructure required by advanced AI systems and
hyperscale data centers. Nuclear energy technologies, encompassing both fission and fusion
concepts, have consequently attracted renewed global attention and substantial investment from
technology giants, including Google, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Meta, and OpenAI. These companies
recognize that next-generation computing and data facilities could require energy solutions with
unprecedented density and reliability. For example, the energy demands of advanced AI training
runs have grown exponentially, with GPT-4 training estimated to consume over 50 GWh of
electricity. Microsoft and TerraPower announced a partnership in 2023 to develop nuclear-powered
data centers, recognizing that future AI systems will require enormous computational resources

Implications: The pairing of commercial fusion and advanced fission energy with artificial
intelligence may reach the inflection point within the coming decade, see Figure 1, and will
therefore have a significant impact on national security and policymaking in multiple dimensions,
including but not limited to: 1.) Strategic power redistribution could occur as nations with
advanced energy-AI capabilities gain advantages regardless of traditional geopolitical assets.

strategic leverage. 4.) Nuclear nonproliferation frameworks would require comprehensive
reimagining as fusion technologies blur the line between civilian and military applications. The
dual-use nature of these technologies will challenge existing control regimes and verification
mechanisms. 5.) Critical infrastructure protection would focus increasingly on securing energy-AI
nexus points that become prime targets for adversaries. The concentration of computational power
enabled by advanced energy sources creates new vulnerabilities that demand novel security
approaches. 6.) Intelligence capabilities may transform as AI systems powered by reliable,
abundant energy enable real-time processing of vast data streams. This would create both
opportunities for enhanced intelligence gathering and challenges in maintaining information
security. 7.) Policy makers may need to develop new governance frameworks that address these
converging technologies while balancing innovation, security, and international stability. Those
who successfully navigate this complex landscape will secure decisive advantages in future
security competitions.

Risk matrix:
The risk-opportunity matrix, Figure 2, describes a landscape where AI-nuclear integration
technologies cluster into distinct actionable categories. Quick Wins in the upper-left quadrant—
including Nuclear-Powered AI Data Centers, Digital Twins for Nuclear Operations, and AI for
Nuclear Materials Detection—represent high-value, lower-risk opportunities for immediate
investment. Strategic Priorities in the upper-right quadrant, such as Quantum Computing with
Nuclear Power and Nuclear-Powered AGI Systems, offer transformative potential but require
careful risk management and regulatory oversight. Mid-range technologies like AI-Designed
Nuclear Fuel and AI-Managed Safety Systems occupy the strategic middle ground, balancing
moderate risks with substantial opportunities. Notably, AI for Proliferation Activities appears in
the High Risk, Low Reward quadrant with the most unfavorable risk-opportunity balance (colored
red), signaling technologies to avoid or strictly regulate. The matrix's color gradient effectively
visualizes this balance, with deeper blue indicating more favorable risk-opportunity ratios. This
assessment framework provides decision-makers with a clear prioritization tool for resource
allocation and strategic planning in the emerging AI-nuclear technology integration.
