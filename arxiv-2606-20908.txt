On Itô-Stratonovich formula for rough sheets Thanks: AMS 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary: 60L10, 60L20, 60L70; Secondary: 60L90.
Youssef Hakiki and Samy Tindel

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore a new strategy towards an Itô-Stratonovich type formula for rough sheets. Historically, planar integration for irregular paths has been notoriously cumbersome. The emergence of “mixed differential” terms in 2D leads to overlapping iterated integrals, which previously required the construction of exhaustive combinatorial structures. As an example of this kind of structure, let us mention the massive 36-element planar signature introduced by K. Chouk and M. Gubinelli (Rough sheets. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.7748, 2014 2014 ). In this work we propose a simplified setting for rough calculus in the plane, which relies on elementary Taylor expansions in a more fundamental way. We claim that this simple trick allows us to significantly streamline the complexity of planar algebraic integration. We illustrate this methodology for a specialized Rough-Young framework: we extend the classical planar change-of-variable formula to paths possessing an asymmetric Hölder regularity: γ 1 > 1 / 3 \gamma_{1}>1/3 in the first direction and γ 2 > 1 / 2 \gamma_{2}>1/2 in the second direction. Relying on a structured controlled path expansions, we rigorously minimize the set of iterated integrals needed in the signature, and express the resulting planar change-of-variable formula as the explicit limit of Riemann sums.



1.
Introduction

For processes indexed by a one-dimensional parameter, Lyons’ theory of rough paths (see
[
8
,
7
]
) provides a highly effective framework for understanding complex maps, such as the stochastic integral or the Itô map. This type of method has been instrumental in simplifying proofs and establishing new results across stochastic analysis, enabling pathwise solutions of SDEs, the differentiation of the Itô map, and the extension of calculus to complex noises like fractional Brownian motion. In this 1D setting, Gubinelli introduced the notion of a controlled path to abstract the basic structures necessary for integrating rough signals, and highlighting the existence of a
sewing map
that serves as the core of the integration process.

Extending these robust 1D structures to processes indexed by the plane (or higher-order objects) is notoriously a cumbersome topic. In order to get an idea of this fact, let us start from the simplest situation of a smooth function
x
indexed by
[0,1]^{2}
and a regular function
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then some elementary computations show that

(1.1)

[\delta\varphi(x)]_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}}=\int_{[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}\varphi^{(1)}(x_{u;v})\,d_{uv}x_{u;v}+\int_{[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}\varphi^{(2)}(x_{u;v})\,d_{u}x_{u;v}d_{v}x_{u;v},

for all
0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}\leq 1
and
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq 1
, where we have set
[\delta y]_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}}
for the planar increment of
y
in the rectangle
[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]
, namely

(1.2)

[\delta y]_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}}\equiv y_{s_{2};t_{2}}-y_{s_{1};t_{2}}-y_{s_{2};t_{1}}+y_{s_{1};t_{1}},

and where
d_{u}x
is a shorthand for
(\partial x/\partial u)du
.
This simple formula already exhibits the extra term
\int\varphi^{(2)}(x_{u;v})\,d_{u}x\,d_{v}x
with respect to integration in
\mathbb{R}
, and the mixed differential term
d_{u}x\,d_{v}x
is one of the main source of complications when one tries to extend (
1.1
) to more complex situations.

Before detailing our approach, we emphasize that the study of two-dimensional fields and their signatures has recently seen
substantial developments. We distinguish three main lines of investigations:

(a)

In relation to possible applications in data science and image processing, a proper notion of 2D signatures for images has been developed and applied as a low-dimensional feature set for texture classification
[
21
,
6
]
. Concurrently, discrete two-parameter extensions of iterated-sums signatures have been tightly linked to the theory of quasisymmetric functions
[
5
]
. Deep topological and higher-algebraic properties of these multiparameter objects have also been uncovered; for instance, generalized mapping space signatures have been constructed by adapting Chen’s cochain constructions to cubical mapping spaces
[
10
]
. Furthermore, the laws of random surfaces have been elegantly framed in
[
14
]
, relying on algebraic and topological arguments. Those last references mostly use the algebra of Jacobians, as opposed to the rectangular increments we wish to handle.

(b)

From a pure geometric measure theory perspective, robust notions of integrating nonsmooth rough differential 2-forms over planes have been formalized, bridging Young and Züst integration with Itô and Stratonovich-type sums evaluated over two-dimensional simplexes
[
18
,
1
]
.

(c)

Within the realm of stochastic analysis, two-parameter rough differential equations have recently been developed to study signature and Schwinger-Dyson kernels
[
2
]
, as well as to establish Hörmander’s theorem for semilinear SPDEs
[
9
]
. However, we stress that these particular frameworks primarily deal with a very specific, factorized type of dynamic of the form
d_{12}x_{u,v}=d_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}x_{u,v}=dX_{u}dY_{v}
, where the planar driving noise is built from the tensor product of two independent one-parameter rough paths
X
and
Y
.
We should also note the interesting Malliavin calculus approaches of
[
19
,
20
,
16
]
. However, this type of method does not give raise to pathwise integrals and relies on Skorohod integration, which is sometimes seen as non physical (see
[
4
]
for Skorohod-Stratonovich corrections).

Despite the progress reported above, the analytic challenge of rigorously solving (
1.1
) for highly irregular stochastic fields remains daunting.
Among the works in this direction, the foundational paper of Chouk and Gubinelli
[
3
]
is our primary source of inspiration. Before describing it in more detail, let us set up some notation that will be used throughout the paper. Following the convention of
[
3
,
4
]
, we carefully separate the two coordinate directions, labeling them direction 1 and direction 2. The evaluation of a function
f:[0,1]^{2k}\to\mathbb{R}
is written
f_{s_{1}\cdots s_{k};\,t_{1}\cdots t_{k}}
. We also distinguish two types of differential elements in (
1.1
): the bidirectional differential
d_{12}x\equiv d_{uv}x
, and the product differential
d_{1}x\,d_{2}x
, which we further shorten to
d_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}x
. Setting
y=\varphi(x)
and
y^{(j)}=\varphi^{(j)}(x)
for
j\geq 1
, the formula (
1.1
) takes the compact form

(1.3)

\delta y=\int_{1}\int_{2}y^{(1)}\,d_{12}x+\int_{1}\int_{2}y^{(2)}\,d_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}x,

where
\delta=\delta_{1}\delta_{2}
is the rectangular increment operator (with
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
defined precisely in Section
3.2
). We further denote by
\mathcal{P}_{k,l}(V)
the space of functions with
k
variables in direction 1 and
l
variables in direction 2 satisfying vanishing conditions on diagonals; the most important instance is
\mathcal{P}_{2,2}(V)
, on which we introduce Hölder norms. Namely, given
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
and
f\in\mathcal{P}_{2,2}(V)
, we set

\|f\|_{\gamma_{1};\,\gamma_{2}}=\sup\left\{\frac{|f_{s_{1}s_{2};\,t_{1}t_{2}}|}{|s_{2}-s_{1}|^{\gamma_{1}}|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\gamma_{2}}}\,;\,s_{1},s_{2},t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]\right\},

and we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{2,2}^{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(V)
the subspace of
\mathcal{P}_{2,2}(V)
on which this norm is finite.

With this set of preliminary notation in hand, the approach of
[
3
]
rests on two complementary principles which can be roughly summarized as follows:

(1)

The 2D sewing map (see Proposition
3.3
below) is understood as a tensorization of the classical 1D sewing map (Proposition
2.2
), applied iteratively in each coordinate direction.

(2)

In order to invoke the 2D sewing map, rectangular increments such as
[\delta\varphi(x)]_{s_{1}s_{2};\,t_{1}t_{2}}
in (
1.1
) are first systematically dissected through (
1.1
), until the resulting remainder falls within the domain of the sewing lemma.

While this global strategy is conceptually easy to summarize, its execution meets several nontrivial obstacles. The article
[
3
]
treats fields
x
with
\delta x\in\mathcal{P}_{2,2}^{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}
,
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>1/3
, and encounters the following three sources of difficulty:

(a)

Giving separate rigorous meaning to the two integral terms in (
1.3
), involving
d_{12}x
and
d_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}x
respectively, requires distinct analytical treatments.

(b)

The Taylor expansion of rectangular increments generates boundary terms, that is, integrals over subdomains
[0,s_{1}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]
and
[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[0,t_{1}]
of the rectangle, which must be tracked carefully at each step.

(c)

When expanding, overlapping iterated integrals in directions 1 and 2 inevitably appear. Otherwise stated, one often has to start a new iterated integral in direction 2 while still integrating in direction 1 (or the other way around).

The last obstacle
(c)
above is in fact the most serious. To describe the objects involved, we introduce a compact index notation for iterated integrals of
x
. Each element
\mathbf{x}^{e}
encodes its integration pattern via a superscript: indices
1
and
\hat{1}
record a step in direction 1 against
d_{1}x
or
d_{\hat{1}}x
respectively; indices
2
and
\hat{2}
similarly record a step in direction 2. A dot
\cdot
marks the start of a new independent integration factor. And
0
signals no integration in the given direction at that step. As a first illustration,

(1.4)

\mathbf{x}^{1\hat{1};0\cdot\hat{2}}=\int_{1}d_{1}x\int_{2}d_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}x,\quad\text{that is}\quad\mathbf{x}^{1\hat{1};0\cdot\hat{2}}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}}=\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\int_{s_{1}}^{\sigma_{2}}d_{1}x_{\sigma_{1};t_{1}}\right)d_{1}x_{\sigma_{2};\tau_{1}}d_{2}x_{\sigma_{2};\tau_{1}},

which belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{2,2}
. As a second example,
\mathbf{x}^{1\cdot\hat{1};0\hat{2}}\in\mathcal{P}_{3,2}
is defined by

(1.5)

\mathbf{x}^{1\cdot\hat{1};0\hat{2}}=\int_{1}d_{1}x\int_{1}\int_{2}d_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}x,\quad\text{that is}\quad\mathbf{x}^{1\cdot\hat{1};0\hat{2}}_{s_{1}s_{2}s_{3};t_{1}t_{2}}=\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}d_{1}x_{\sigma_{1};t_{1}}\int_{s_{2}}^{s_{3}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}d_{1}x_{\sigma_{2};\tau_{1}}d_{2}x_{\sigma_{2};\tau_{1}}.

Then notice in (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) that the break in integration can occur at different steps in directions 1 and 2: these are precisely the overlapping iterated integrals generated during expansion.
Those integrals render the rough analysis substantially more complex.
In
[
3
]
, they are resolved by a technique called
splitting
, which decomposes them into pieces amenable to the 2D sewing map, but at the cost of much longer expansions. In the case
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>1/3
, this forces 36 distinct elements (according to our count) in the signature
\mathbb{X}
of
x
so that one can define a rough integral. This large number has to be compared to just 2 elements in the corresponding 1D setting for
\gamma>1/3
. Furthermore, it appears difficult to extend the approach in
[
3
]
to the regime
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\leq 1/3
.

Let us now turn to our own strategy. It differs from
[
3
]
in one key respect: we invest considerably more effort in Taylor-expanding the rectangular increments
before
invoking the sewing maps. We believe that this simple trick allows a more efficient dissection of the planar integrals.
For sake of conciseness, we will deal with Young regularity
\gamma_{2}>1/2
in direction 2. In this simplified context we hope the reader can see what Taylor expansions can bring to the picture, and how they avoid splitting operations (see Lemma
4.4
and (
4.4
) below for a first concrete instance).
Then the overall outcome of our approach is fourfold:

(i)

We establish an exact change-of-variable formula for
y=\varphi(x)
, expressing
\delta y
as the limit of modified Riemann sums (see (
4.81
) in Proposition
4.14
below). The geometric rough sheet
\mathbb{X}
required for this formula consists solely of the 14 elements listed in Table
1
and Table
2
, a drastic reduction from the 36 elements of
[
3
]
.

(ii)

We introduce a notion of controlled process (Definition
4.7
) adapted to the Rough-Young setting, which appears to be a natural and robust concept for planar integration. In the context of a rough-Young regularity setting, controlled processes form a reasonably compact structure.

(iii)

Although we restrict the analysis to the Rough-Young regime
\gamma_{1}>1/3
,
\gamma_{2}>1/2
for conciseness, the same strategy should extend to the fully rough case
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>1/3
. The reason is that the mechanism at the heart of our method (Taylor-expanding the rectangular increments before invoking the sewing maps) is symmetric in the two directions and does not rely on
\gamma_{2}>1/2
. In the present setting, direction 2 enters only through a first-order Young expansion (see Lemma
4.4
and (
4.4
)), while direction 1 is handled by the full rough apparatus of second-order controlled processes together with the sewing map
\Lambda_{1}
acting at order
3\gamma_{1}>1
. To reach
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>1/3
one would simply carry the direction-2 expansion to the same depth as the direction-1 one, so that both variables are treated as second-order controlled processes. The overlapping iterated integrals discussed around (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) would then be handled, exactly as here, by pushing the Taylor expansion one step further rather than by the splitting technique of
[
3
]
. The price to pay is heavier combinatorial bookkeeping and a moderately larger signature
\mathbb{X}
, but we don’t expect new conceptual obstructions along the road. A complete treatment of the fully rough regime, including the precise description of the enlarged signature, is deferred to subsequent publications.

(iv)

As a primary stochastic application, we show that the fractional Brownian sheet with Hurst parameters
H_{1}>1/3
and
H_{2}>1/2
can be lifted to a geometric rough sheet satisfying Hypotheses
3.7
–
4.13
below, via multiparameter Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey inequalities (see Proposition
5.11
for the precise statement). This places our Itô-Stratonovich formula in a concrete probabilistic setting.

To summarize, this paper develops a self-contained rough calculus in the plane under the Rough-Young regularity assumption
\gamma_{1}>1/3
,
\gamma_{2}>1/2
. Thanks to deeper Taylor expansions of rectangular increments, we are allowed to keep the signature small and the proofs transparent, at the cost of more careful bookkeeping in the expansion steps. The fBs application in Section
5
shows that the framework is not merely theoretical: the rough sheet
\mathbb{X}
can be concretely constructed for a natural Gaussian field.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
recalls the algebraic integration framework in dimension 1: increments, products of increments, iterated integrals, and the 1D sewing map (Proposition
2.2
). Section
3
develops the 2D counterpart of this framework: planar increments and the 2D sewing map (Proposition
3.3
), together with the signature elements listed in Table
1
. Section
4
is devoted to the dissection of planar integrals: we introduce the controlled path notion (Definition
4.7
), construct the rough-Young integrals
z^{1}
and
z^{2}
(Theorem
4.11
), and establish the change-of-variable formula (Proposition
4.14
). Section
5
is devoted to the application to the fractional Brownian sheet: after establishing a generalized multiparameter GRR inequality, we prove that the fBs admits a lift to a geometric rough sheet satisfying Hypotheses
3.7
–
4.13
(Proposition
5.11
).

2.
Algebraic integration in dimension 1

We recall here the minimal amount of notation concerning algebraic integration theory in
\mathbb{R}
, in order to prepare the ground for further developments in the plane. We refer to
[
11
,
12
]
for a more detailed introduction.

2.1.
Increments

The integration theory developed below rests on the algebraic framework of
increments, originally introduced in
[
11
,
12
]
, which we briefly recall.
For a vector space
V
and an integer
k\geq 1
, define
\mathcal{C}_{k}(V)
as the
set of functions
g:[0,1]^{k}\to V
that vanish whenever two consecutive
arguments coincide:

g_{t_{1}\cdots t_{k}}=0\quad\text{whenever }t_{i}=t_{i+1}\text{ for some }i\leq k-1.

Elements of
\mathcal{C}_{k}(V)
are called
(k-1)
-increments
, and we set

\mathcal{C}_{*}(V)=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{C}_{k}(V)
. The algebraic structure on

\mathcal{C}_{*}(V)
is governed by the coboundary operator

(2.1)

\delta:\mathcal{C}_{k}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{k+1}(V),\qquad(\delta g)_{t_{1}\cdots t_{k+1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{k-i}\,g_{t_{1}\cdots\hat{t}_{i}\cdots t_{k+1}},

where
\hat{t}_{i}
indicates that the
i
-th argument is omitted. A direct
computation confirms that
\delta^{2}=0
, i.e. the composition

\delta:\mathcal{C}_{k}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{k+1}(V)
followed by

\delta:\mathcal{C}_{k+1}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{k+2}(V)
is the zero map. We accordingly set

\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}_{k}(V):=\ker\delta\cap\mathcal{C}_{k}(V),\qquad\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}_{k}(V):=\mathrm{Im}\,\delta\cap\mathcal{C}_{k}(V).

The two instances of (
2.1
) that appear most frequently in
this paper are
g\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(V)
and
h\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(V)
. In this case, for

s,u,t\in[0,T]
we get:

(2.2)

\delta g_{st}=g_{t}-g_{s},\qquad\delta h_{sut}=h_{st}-h_{su}-h_{ut}.

Since
\delta^{2}=0
, every exact increment belongs to the kernel of

\delta
. In fact the converse also holds:
\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}_{k+1}(V)=\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}_{k}(V)

for all
k\geq 1
. The special case
k=1
is worth stating explicitly:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}_{2}(V)
. Then there exists a (non-unique)

f\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(V)
such that
h=\delta f
.

In other words,
\delta|_{\mathcal{C}_{2}(V)}
measures the obstruction for a

1
-increment to be a genuine finite difference:
h_{st}=f_{t}-f_{s}
for
some
f
if and only if
\delta h=0
.

Our analysis will mainly involve
k
-increments with
k\leq 2
, equipped with
Hölder-type norms. That is, for
f\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(V)
, set

(2.3)

\|f\|_{\mu}=\sup_{s,t\in[0,T]}\frac{|f_{st}|}{|t-s|^{\mu}},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\mu}(V):=\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(V)\,:\,\|f\|_{\mu}<\infty\bigr\}.

The Hölder norm on
\mathcal{C}_{1}(V)
is defined by transport through
\delta
:
for a continuous
g\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(V)
,

(2.4)

\|g\|_{\mu}:=\|\delta g\|_{\mu},

and
g\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mu}(V)
iff this quantity is finite. Note that

\|\cdot\|_{\mu}
is only a semi-norm on
\mathcal{C}_{1}(V)
. For

h\in\mathcal{C}_{3}(V)
, the analogous definition reads

(2.5)

\|h\|_{\mu}=\sup_{s,u,t\in[0,T]}\frac{|h_{sut}|}{|t-s|^{\mu}},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\mu}(V):=\bigl\{h\in\mathcal{C}_{3}(V)\,:\,\|h\|_{\mu}<\infty\bigr\}.

We set
\mathcal{C}_{3}^{1+}(V):=\bigcup_{\mu>1}\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\mu}(V)
, along with the
corresponding closed-increment subspace
\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}_{3}^{1+}(V)
.
With those notions in hand, the following inversion result for
\delta
is the cornerstone of the
pathwise integration theory we will use; a concise proof can be found in
[
12
]
.

Proposition 2.2

(The
\Lambda
-map)
.

There exists a unique linear map
\Lambda:\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}_{3}^{1+}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{2}^{1+}(V)
such that

\delta\Lambda=\mathsf{id}_{\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}_{3}^{1+}(V)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Lambda\delta=\mathsf{id}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{1+}(V)}.

Equivalently, every
h\in\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}_{3}^{1+}(V)
has a unique preimage

\Lambda h\in\mathcal{C}_{2}^{1+}(V)
under
\delta
. Furthermore, for any

\mu>1
, the map
\Lambda
is continuous from
\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\mu}(V)
to

\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\mu}(V)
, with the explicit bound

(2.6)

\|\Lambda h\|_{\mu}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\mu}-2}\|h\|_{\mu},\qquad h\in\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\mu}(V).

The following corollary spells out how Proposition
2.2
connects
the algebraic framework to Riemann-type integration.

Corollary 2.3
.

For any
1
-increment
g\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(V)
such that
\delta g\in\mathcal{C}_{3}^{1+}
,
set
\delta f:=(\mathsf{id}-\Lambda\delta)g
. Then the increments of
f
can be written as limits of Riemann type sums:

\delta f_{st}=\lim_{|\Pi_{st}|\to 0}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}g_{t_{i}\,t_{i+1}},

where the limit is over any partition
\Pi_{st}=\{t_{0}=s,\dots,t_{n}=t\}

of
[s,t]
as its mesh tends to zero. Thus
\delta f
is the indefinite
integral of the
1
-increment
g
.

2.2.
Products of increments

Throughout this section we specialize to the scalar case
V=\mathbb{R}
and write

\mathcal{C}_{k}^{\gamma}
in place of
\mathcal{C}_{k}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R})
. The natural product
of two increments in
(\mathcal{C}_{*},\delta)
is obtained by identifying the
last variable of the first factor with the first variable of the second
(see e.g.
[
11
]
):

Definition 2.4
.

For
g\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
and
h\in\mathcal{C}_{m}
, denote by
gh
the element of

\mathcal{C}_{n+m-1}
given by

(2.7)

(gh)_{t_{1},\dots,t_{m+n-1}}=g_{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}}\,h_{t_{n},\dots,t_{m+n-1}},\quad t_{1},\dots,t_{m+n-1}\in[0,T].

The operator
\delta
interacts with this product through the following
Leibniz-type rules.

Proposition 2.5
.

Consider the product introduced in Definition
2.4
.

(i)

If
g,h\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
, then
gh\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and

(2.8)

\delta(gh)=\delta g\,h+g\,\delta h.

(ii)

If
g\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
h\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
, then
gh\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
and

(2.9)

\delta(gh)=-\delta g\,h+g\,\delta h.

(iii)

If
g\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
and
h\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
, then
gh\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
and

(2.10)

\delta(gh)=\delta g\,h+g\,\delta h.

Proof.

We verify (
2.8
); the remaining cases are equally
straightforward. For
g,h\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
s,t\in[0,T]
,

[\delta(gh)]_{st}=g_{t}h_{t}-g_{s}h_{s}=g_{s}(h_{t}-h_{s})+(g_{t}-g_{s})h_{t}=g_{s}\,\delta h_{st}+\delta g_{st}\,h_{t},

which is (
2.8
).
∎

While Definition
2.4
and the
notation (
2.7
) cover most situations arising in this
paper, some later computations require identifying more than one variable.
We record this generalization for future reference.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
,
h\in\mathcal{C}_{m}
, and
k\leq\min(n,m)
. The increment

g*_{k}h\in\mathcal{C}_{m+n-k}
is defined by

(2.11)

(g*_{k}h)_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m+n-k}}=g_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\,h_{t_{n-k},\ldots,t_{n+m-k}}.

2.3.
Iterated integrals as increments

Iterated integrals of smooth functions on
[0,T]
are obviously
particular cases of elements of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
, which will be of interest
for us. A typical example of this kind of object is given as follows: consider
f^{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
and
0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}\leq 1
. For
n\geq 1
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2})
the simplex

(2.12)

\mathcal{S}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2})=\left\{(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})\in[0,T]^{n};\,s_{1}<\sigma_{1}<\cdots<\sigma_{n}<s_{2}\right\}

and we set

(2.13)

h_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{1,\ldots,n}\equiv\int_{\mathcal{S}_{n}(s_{1},s_{2})}df_{\sigma_{1}}^{1}\cdots df_{\sigma_{n}}^{n}=\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\int_{s_{1}}^{\sigma_{n-1}}\cdots\int_{s_{1}}^{\sigma_{2}}df_{\sigma_{1}}^{1}\cdots df_{\sigma_{n}}^{n}.

With these conventions in mind, the following relations between multiple integrals and the operator
\delta
will also be useful. The reader is sent to
[
12
]
for its elementary proof.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let

3.
Algebraic integration in the plane

This section is devoted to recall the elements of algebraic integration necessary to define an integral of the form

3.1.
Some general notation

The conventions used in this section were set up in Section

is written

y=\varphi(x)
and
y^{(j)}=\varphi^{(j)}(x)
for
j\geq 1

reads as in (

(3.1)

\delta y=\int_{1}\int_{2}y^{(1)}\,d_{12}x+\int_{1}\int_{2}y^{(2)}\,d_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}x,

3.2.
Planar increments

We consider here increments of a variable
s
(also called direction 1) and a variable
t
(also called direction 2), with

(3.2)

In the particular case

Some partial difference operators

\delta_{2}

\delta_{1}:\mathcal{P}_{k,l}(V)\to\mathcal{P}_{k+1,l}(V),\qquad\delta_{1}g_{s_{1}\cdots s_{k+1};\,t_{1}\cdots t_{l}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{k-i}g_{s_{1}\cdots\hat{s}_{i}\cdots s_{k+1};\,t_{1}\cdots t_{l}},

\delta_{2}
similarly. The planar increment
\delta

\delta=\delta_{1}\,\delta_{2}

(3.3)

\delta f_{s_{1}s_{2};\,t_{1}t_{2}}=f_{s_{2};\,t_{2}}-f_{s_{2};\,t_{1}}-f_{s_{1};\,t_{2}}+f_{s_{1};\,t_{1}},

which is the usual rectangular increment of a function

Notation 3.1

For

and

\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{P}_{k,l}
for
\mathcal{P}_{k,l}\cap\ker(\delta)
and
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{k,l}
for

As in the 1-d case, the Hölder regularity of planar increments is an essential feature of our generalized integration theory. On

Related to this type of quantity, we introduce a family of norms for functions defined in

Definition 3.2

Let

4.1.
Decomposition for the increments of a function of

Hypothesis 4.1

Remark 4.2

Condition (

(i)

This section is devoted to showing that a fractional Brownian sheet (fBs)

5.1.

\mathbb{X}
in the next subsection.

First for sake of comparison, let us recall a version of Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey (GRR for short) lemma generalized to

Remark 5.5
.

It is worth emphasizing that Lemma

requires only

and

Since we are mainly interested in constructing a rough sheet above the fBs, we need to check that all the assumptions of

are satisfied.
The

is not trivial.
Specifically,
the analytical difficulty in bounding the
\mathcal{P}_{2,3}
-Hölder norm of the term

stems from its asymmetric structure: it acts as a

-variables increment in the

3
-variables increment in the

-direction.
Standard GRR bounds (

) and (

and
\mathcal{P}_{2,2}\equiv\mathcal{C}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{2}

\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}

,

and

. This splitting procedure allows us to treat the

-variables increment
\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}
, for fixed

, then we have:

(5.18)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}-\,\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}\right\rVert_{\kappa_{1};\kappa_{2}}\leq c\Bigl(U^{3}_{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2},p}\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\otimes j}\bigl)\ +\ \bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\mathbf{x}^{i;j}-\mathbf{z}^{1;2}\mathbf{z}^{i;j}\bigl\rVert_{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}}\Bigl).

Proof.

To establish this regularity, we combine and iterate the generalized 1D and 2D GRR lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
. First, applying Lemma
5.1
to
(s_{1},s_{2})\mapsto\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}
for fixed
t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}
, we get

(5.19)

\sup_{0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}\leq 1}\frac{\bigl\lvert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}\bigr\rvert^{2p}}{|s_{2}-s_{1}|^{2\kappa_{1}p}}\\
\leq c\Bigl(U^{1}_{\kappa_{1},p}\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{\cdot;t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{\cdot;t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}\bigl)^{2p}+\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1;2}_{\cdot;t_{1}t_{2}}\mathbf{x}^{i;j}_{\cdot;t_{2}t_{3}}-\mathbf{z}^{1;2}_{\cdot;t_{1}t_{2}}\mathbf{z}^{i;j}_{\cdot;t_{2}t_{3}}\bigl\rVert_{\kappa_{1}}^{2p}\Bigr),

where we have used the fact that
\delta_{1}\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}=\mathbf{x}^{1\cdot\,i;2\cdot j}=\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\mathbf{x}^{i;j}
. Dividing (
5.19
) by
|t_{3}-t_{1}|^{2\kappa_{2}p}
, taking the supremum over
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}\leq 1
, and using the definitions of
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\kappa_{1};\kappa_{2}}
and
U^{1}_{\kappa_{1},p}(\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\kappa_{2}})
, we obtain

(5.20)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}-\,\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}\right\rVert_{\kappa_{1};\kappa_{2}}^{2p}\leq c\Bigl(U^{1}_{\kappa_{1},p}\bigl(\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}\bigl\rVert_{\kappa_{2}}\bigl)^{2p}+\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\mathbf{x}^{i;j}-\mathbf{z}^{1;2}\mathbf{z}^{i;j}\bigl\rVert^{2p}_{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}}\Bigl).

We will now focus on bounding the right hand side above.

Let us first bound the second term in the right hand side of (
5.20
). To this aim, we write

\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\mathbf{x}^{i;j}-\mathbf{z}^{1;2}\mathbf{z}^{i;j}=\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{1;2}-\mathbf{z}^{1;2}\bigr)\mathbf{x}^{i;j}+\mathbf{z}^{1;2}\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{i;j}-\mathbf{z}^{i;j}\bigr).

Since
x,z\in\mathcal{P}_{1,1}^{\gamma_{1}+\varepsilon,\gamma_{2}}
, both pairs
\mathbf{x}^{1;2},\mathbf{z}^{1;2}
and
\mathbf{x}^{i;j},\mathbf{z}^{i;j}
belong to
\mathcal{P}_{2,2}^{\gamma_{1}+\varepsilon,\gamma_{2}}
. Standard product estimates for Hölder functions then yield
\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\mathbf{x}^{i;j}-\mathbf{z}^{1;2}\mathbf{z}^{i;j}\rVert_{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}}<\infty
, by an argument analogous to the one carried out for the term
\lVert\mathbf{z}^{1;0}\mathbf{z}^{i;j}-\mathbf{x}^{1;0}\mathbf{x}^{i;j}\rVert_{2\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}
in the proof of item
(ii)
of Lemma
5.4
.

Let now turn to the first term in the right hand side of (
5.20
), which needs to be estimated further thanks to our splitting mechanism. Indeed, owing to the geometric assumption observe that for
t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}
we have
\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}=\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2};t_{2}t_{3}}
, where
\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}
is defined in (
5.16
). Then, for
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,1]
fixed, the Hölder norm
\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot}\rVert_{\kappa_{2}}
can be estimated as follows:

(5.21)

\displaystyle\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot}\bigl\rVert_{\kappa_{2}}\leq\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot;\cdot}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot;\cdot}\bigr\rVert_{\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2}}

The idea behind embedding the increment
\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}
into the one
\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2};t_{2}t_{3}}
, defined in (
5.16
), is that the latter can be viewed, for
s_{1},s_{2}
fixed, as an increment in
\mathcal{P}_{2,2}
which we can write as an “exact” rectangular increment. Then for this rectangular increment, we can apply (
5.5
) directly. Indeed, setting
f_{r,t}:=\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};0\,r;0\,t}
and recalling from (
3.3
) that
\delta f_{r_{1}r_{2};t_{1}t_{2}}=f_{r_{2},t_{2}}-f_{r_{1},t_{2}}-f_{r_{2},t_{1}}+f_{r_{1},t_{1}}
, the definition (
5.16
) and bilinearity of the product give

\displaystyle\delta f_{r_{1}r_{2};t_{1}t_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{s_{1}<\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}<s_{2}}\Bigl[\Bigl(\int_{0}^{r_{2}}-\int_{0}^{r_{1}}\Bigr)d_{12}x_{\sigma_{1};\tau_{1}}\Bigr]\Bigl[\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t_{2}}-\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\Bigr)d_{ij}x_{\sigma_{2};\tau_{2}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\int_{s_{1}<\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}<s_{2}}\Bigl(\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}d_{12}x_{\sigma_{1};\tau_{1}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}d_{ij}x_{\sigma_{2};\tau_{2}}\Bigr)=\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};r_{1}r_{2};t_{1}t_{2}}.

Therefore using (
5.5
) we obtain

(5.22)

\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot;\cdot}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot;\cdot}\bigr\rVert_{\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2}}\leq c_{2}\,U^{2}_{\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2},p}\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot;\cdot}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{s_{1}s_{2};\cdot;\cdot}\bigl).

Hence recalling that we are bounding the term
U
in the right hand side of (
5.20
) and plugging (
5.22
), we discover that

\displaystyle U^{1}_{\kappa_{1},p}\bigl(\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}\bigl\rVert_{\kappa_{2}}\bigl)^{2p}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{u_{1}u_{2};\cdot}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}_{u_{1}u_{2};\cdot}\bigl\rVert_{\kappa_{2}}^{2p}}{|u_{2}-u_{1}|^{2\kappa_{1}p+2}}du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\frac{U^{2}_{\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2},p}\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{u_{1}u_{2};\cdot;\cdot}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{u_{1}u_{2};\cdot;\cdot}\bigl)^{2p}}{|u_{2}-u_{1}|^{2\kappa_{1}p+2}}du_{1}du_{2}.

Expanding
U^{2}_{\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2},p}
according to its expression (
5.4
) in Definition
5.2
, we then get

\displaystyle U^{1}_{\kappa_{1},p}\bigl(\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}\bigl\rVert_{\kappa_{2}}\bigl)^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{[0,1]^{6}}\frac{\lvert\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2};w_{1}w_{2}}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\otimes j}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2};w_{1}w_{2}}\rvert^{2p}}{|u_{2}-u_{1}|^{2\kappa_{1}p+2}|v_{2}-v_{1}|^{2\kappa_{2}p+2}|w_{2}-w_{1}|^{2\kappa_{2}p+2}}du_{1}du_{2}dv_{1}dv_{2}dw_{1}dw_{2}

(5.23)

\displaystyle=c\,U^{3}_{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2},p}\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\otimes j}\bigl)^{2p},

where
U^{3}_{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2},p}\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\otimes j}\bigl)
is defined in (
5.17
). Inserting (
5.23
) for the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.20
), and taking the
2p
-th root of the resulting inequality, we conclude (
5.18
). This finishes our proof.
∎

The bound (
(ii)
) in Lemma
5.4
-(ii) still involves the norm
\lVert\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\cdot j}-\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\cdot j}\rVert_{2\gamma_{1}+\varepsilon;\gamma_{2}}
on its right-hand side, which is itself an intermediate quantity rather than a directly checkable condition on the input paths. Lemma
5.7
was designed precisely to control this term via (
5.18
). Substituting (
5.18
) into (
(ii)
) and taking the
2p
-th root, we obtain the following self-contained estimate.

Corollary 5.8
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
5.4
-(ii) and Lemma
5.7
, the Hölder norm of
\mathbf{x}^{1i;0\cdot j}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;0\cdot j}
is controlled entirely by the integrability functionals
U^{2}
,
U^{3}
and product Hölder norms of the input paths
x
and
z
:

\displaystyle\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1i;0\cdot j}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;0\cdot j}\bigr\rVert_{2\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\Bigl(U^{2}_{2\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},p}(\mathbf{z}^{1i;0\cdot j}-\mathbf{x}^{1i;0\cdot j})+U^{3}_{2\gamma_{1}+\varepsilon,\gamma_{2},\gamma_{2},p}\bigl(\mathbf{x}^{1i;2\otimes j}-\mathbf{z}^{1i;2\otimes j}\bigl)

(5.24)

\displaystyle\qquad+\bigl\lVert\mathbf{z}^{1;0}\,\mathbf{z}^{i;j}-\mathbf{x}^{1;0}\,\mathbf{x}^{i;j}\bigr\rVert_{2\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}+\ \bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\mathbf{x}^{i;j}-\mathbf{z}^{1;2}\mathbf{z}^{i;j}\bigl\rVert_{2\gamma_{1}+\varepsilon,\gamma_{2}}\Bigr).

5.2.
Fractional Brownian sheet

With the multiparameter GRR estimates of the preceding subsection in hand, we now carry out the main construction. That is, as mentioned in the introduction to this section, we consider a fractional Brownian sheet
x
. We will lift this field to a geometric rough sheet
\mathbb{X}
satisfying Hypotheses
3.7
,
4.8
, and
4.13
, so that the rough-Young integral of Theorem
4.11
and the change of variable formula of Proposition
4.14
apply to
x
. In this section we will use a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
on which
x
is defined. The relevant Hurst parameter regime is
H_{1}>1/3
and
H_{2}>1/2
.

The construction follows the regularization strategy of Remark
3.8
. Using the harmonizable representation of
x
(see (
5.26
) below), we approximate
x
by a family of smooth random fields
x^{N}
, obtained by restricting the spectral integral to frequencies
|\xi|,|\eta|\leq N
. For each
N
, the associated rough sheet
\mathbb{X}^{N}
is constructed explicitly by computing the iterated integrals via the partial derivatives of
x^{N}
. The GRR bounds of Lemma
5.4
and Corollary
5.8
then imply that
(\mathbb{X}^{N})_{N\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in the appropriate Hölder topology, and the limit defines the desired rough sheet
\mathbb{X}
above
x
.
We begin with the precise definition of the fBs.

Definition 5.9
.

The process
\{x_{s;t}:(s,t)\in[0,1]^{2}\}
defined on the probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
is called a fractional Brownian sheet (fBs) with
Hurst parameters
H_{1},H_{2}\in(0,1)
if
x
is a centered Gaussian process with
covariance function

R_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}}=\mathbb{E}[x_{s_{1};t_{1}}x_{s_{2};t_{2}}]
defined by

(5.25)

\displaystyle R_{s_{1}s_{2};t_{1}t_{2}}=\frac{1}{4}\left(|s_{1}|^{2H_{1}}+|s_{2}|^{2H_{1}}-|s_{1}-s_{2}|^{2H_{1}}\right)\left(|t_{1}|^{2H_{2}}+|t_{2}|^{2H_{2}}-|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{2H_{2}}\right).

With this definition, the fBs
x
can be represented (see e.g.
[
17
]
) as
follows:

(5.26)

x_{s;t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathbf{c}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{e^{is\xi}-1}{|\xi|^{H_{1}+1/2}}\frac{e^{it\eta}-1}{|\eta|^{H_{2}+1/2}}\hat{W}(d\xi,d\eta),

where
\hat{W}
is the Fourier transform of the white noise
W
and the constant
\mathbf{c}
depends on
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
only. The
representation (
5.26
) is called harmonizable
representation for the fBs.

Remark 5.10
.

Since
x
is defined via the Fourier transform
\hat{W}
of a white noise
W
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we work throughout Section
5
with the Wiener space built on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. For
n\geq 1
and a symmetric kernel
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes n}
, we write
I_{n}(f)
for the
n
-th Wiener iterated integral with respect to
\hat{W}
. These integrals generate the Wiener chaos decomposition
L^{2}(\Omega)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{n}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is the
n
-th Wiener chaos. A key identity used below (see (
5.41
)) is the product formula: for
f,g\in\mathfrak{H}
,

(5.27)

I_{1}(f)\,I_{1}(g)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_{1}(f)\,I_{1}(g)\bigr]+I_{2}(f\otimes g),

where
f\otimes g
denotes the standard tensor product. We refer to
[
15
]
for a systematic treatment of Malliavin calculus, Wiener chaos, and iterated integrals.

Since our rough sheet
\mathbb{X}
in Table
1
is defined by
regularization, see e.g. Remark
3.8
, we introduce a regularizing parameter
N\geq 1
and the
following sequence of random fields approximating the fBs
x
:

(5.28)

\displaystyle x_{s;t}^{N}:=\mathbf{c}\int_{D_{N}}\frac{e^{is\xi}-1}{|\xi|^{H_{1}+1/2}}\frac{e^{it\eta}-1}{|\eta|^{H_{2}+1/2}}\hat{W}(d\xi,d\eta),

where
\mathbf{c}
and
\hat{W}
appear in (
5.26
), and
D_{N}:=[-N,N]^{2}
. It is easily seen that
x^{N}
is a smooth field, and we have

(5.29)

\partial_{1}x^{N}_{u;v}=\mathbf{c}\int_{D_{N}}\mathbf{K}_{1}(u,v,\xi,\eta)\,\hat{W}(d\xi,d\eta),\quad\partial_{2}x^{N}_{u;v}=\mathbf{c}\int_{D_{N}}\mathbf{K}_{2}(u,v,\xi,\eta)\,\hat{W}(d\xi,d\eta),

where, for
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we define the following kernels:

(5.30)

\mathbf{K}_{1}(u,v,\xi,\eta):=\frac{i\xi\,e^{iu\xi}(e^{iv\eta}-1)}{|\xi|^{H_{1}+1/2}|\eta|^{H_{2}+1/2}},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{K}_{2}(u,v,\xi,\eta):=\frac{(e^{iu\xi}-1)\,i\eta\,e^{iv\eta}}{|\xi|^{H_{1}+1/2}|\eta|^{H_{2}+1/2}}.

In the same way, one can write

(5.31)

\partial_{12}x_{s;t}^{N}=\mathbf{c}\int_{D_{N}}\mathbf{K}_{12}(s,t,\xi,\eta)\,\hat{W}(d\xi,d\eta),\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbf{K}_{12}(s,t,\xi,\eta):=\frac{-\xi\eta\,e^{is\xi+it\eta}}{|\xi|^{H_{1}+1/2}|\eta|^{H_{2}+1/2}}.

Denote by
\mathbb{X}^{N}
the rough sheet containing iterated integrals constructed from
x^{N}
. Our main goal in this section is to show that the fBs can be enhanced in a rough sheet
\mathbb{X}
, where all the associated iterated integrals are constructed as limit of the integrals of
\mathbb{X}^{N}
.

Now, let
\gamma_{1}\in(1/3,H_{1})
and
\gamma_{2}\in(1/2,H_{2})
. Using Lemma
5.4
with
z=x^{N}
, we have the following approximation of integrals of the rough sheet
\mathbb{X}
by integrals of
\mathbb{X}^{N}
:

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
x
be a fBs with Hurst parameters
(H_{1},H_{2})
such that
1/3<H_{1}<1/2
,
H_{2}>1/2
, as introduced in Definition
5.9
. Let
\gamma_{1}\in(1/3,H_{1})
,
\gamma_{2}\in(1/2,H_{2})
, and
p>1
. Then the following convergences hold true:

(5.32)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{N;1;2}-\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\rVert_{\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}^{2p}\Bigl]=0,

(5.33)

\displaystyle\lim_{N,M\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{M;\hat{1};\hat{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{N;\hat{1};\hat{2}}\rVert_{\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}^{2p}\Bigl]=0,

(5.34)

\displaystyle\lim_{M,N\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{M;11;0\cdot 2}-\mathbf{x}^{N;11;0\cdot 2}\bigl\rVert^{2p}_{2\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}\Bigl]=0,

(5.35)

\displaystyle\lim_{M,N\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{M;1\hat{1};0\cdot\hat{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{N;1\hat{1};0\cdot\hat{2}}\bigl\rVert^{2p}_{2\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}\Bigl]=0.

Proof.

We prove the four convergences (
5.32
)-(
5.35
) in turn. The arguments for (
5.32
) and (
5.33
) rest on direct second-moment estimates using the harmonizable representation, combined with dominated convergence. The proof of (
5.34
) additionally requires the GRR bound of Corollary
5.8
. The argument for (
5.35
) is entirely analogous and is left to the reader.

Step 1: Convergence of
\mathbf{x}^{N;1;2}
. We follow the same lines as
[
3
, Section 6.1, Step 1, p. 43]
. Namely by (
5.13
) and Gaussian hypercontractivity we have

(5.36)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{N;1;2}-\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\rVert_{\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}^{2p}\Bigl]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\big(U^{2}_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},p}(\mathbf{x}^{N;1;2}-\mathbf{x}^{1;2})\big)^{2p}\Bigl]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{4}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|\mathbf{x}^{N;1;2}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{1;2}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}|^{2p}\Bigl]}{|u_{2}-u_{1}|^{2\gamma_{1}p+2}|v_{2}-v_{1}|^{2\gamma_{2}p+2}}du_{1}du_{2}dv_{1}dv_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{4}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|\mathbf{x}^{N;1;2}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{1;2}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}|^{2}\Bigl]^{p}}{|u_{2}-u_{1}|^{2\gamma_{1}p+2}|v_{2}-v_{1}|^{2\gamma_{2}p+2}}du_{1}du_{2}dv_{1}dv_{2}.

The harmonizable representations of
x
and
x^{N}
(see (
5.26
) and (
5.28
)), plus an elementary change of variable, ensure that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|\mathbf{x}^{N;1;2}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{1;2}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}|^{2}\Bigl]=\mathbf{c}\int_{|\xi|\vee|\eta|\geq N}\frac{\rvert e^{iu_{2}\xi}-e^{iu_{1}\xi}\lvert^{2}\rvert e^{iv_{2}\eta}-e^{iv_{1}\eta}\lvert^{2}}{|\xi|^{2H_{1}+1}|\eta|^{2H_{2}+1}}d\xi d\eta

(5.37)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathbf{c}|u_{2}-u_{1}|^{2H_{1}}|v_{2}-v_{1}|^{2H_{2}}\,I_{N}\bigl(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2}\bigl),

where we set

I_{N}\bigl(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2}\bigl):=\int_{\bigl\{|\xi|\vee|\eta|\geq N|u_{2}-u_{1}||v_{2}-v_{1}|\bigl\}}\frac{|e^{i\xi}-1|^{2}|e^{i\eta}-1|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2H_{1}+1}|\eta|^{2H_{2}+1}}d\xi\,d\eta.

Plugging (
5.2
) into (
5.36
), we thus obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Bigl[\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{N;1;2}-\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\rVert_{\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}^{2p}\Bigl]

(5.38)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]^{4}}{|u_{2}-u_{1}|^{2(H_{1}-\gamma_{1})p-2}|v_{2}-v_{1}|^{2(H_{2}-\gamma_{2})p-2}}I_{N}\bigl(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2}\bigl)^{p}du_{1}du_{2}dv_{1}dv_{2}.

Notice that for all
H_{1},H_{2}>1/3
, we have the following uniform bound over
(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\in[0,1]^{4}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
:

I_{N}\bigl(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2}\bigl)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|e^{i\xi}-1|^{2}|e^{i\eta}-1|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2H_{1}+1}|\eta|^{2H_{2}+1}}d\xi\,d\eta<\infty.

Hence by dominated convergence we get

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}I_{N}\bigl(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2}\bigl)=0.

Since
\gamma_{1}<H_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}<H_{2}
, we can choose
p
large enough and use the dominated convergence theorem again, so that the integral in (
5.2
) converge to zero as
N\rightarrow 0
. This yields

(5.39)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{N;1;2}-\mathbf{x}^{1;2}\rVert_{\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}^{2p}\Bigl]=0.

Step 2: Convergence of
\mathbf{x}^{N;\hat{1};\hat{2}}
. Since
\mathbf{x}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}
is not automatically defined (unlike
\mathbf{x}^{1;2}
) as a rectangular increment of
x
, we show that
\{\mathbf{x}^{N;\hat{1};\hat{2}}\,:\,N\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2p}(\Omega,\mathcal{P}_{2,2}^{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}})
. To this aim, let
M,N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M<N
. Using (
5.13
) and the Gaussian hypercontractivity again, we get

(5.40)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\lVert\mathbf{x}^{N;\hat{1};\hat{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{M;\hat{1};\hat{2}}\rVert_{\gamma_{1};\gamma_{2}}^{2p}\Bigl]\leq\int_{[0,1]^{4}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[|\mathbf{x}^{N;\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{M;\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}|^{2}\Bigl]^{p}}{|u_{2}-u_{1}|^{2\gamma_{1}p+2}|v_{2}-v_{1}|^{2\gamma_{2}p+2}}du_{1}du_{2}dv_{1}dv_{2}.

Recalling (
5.29
), it is readily checked that the product formula (
5.27
) implies

(5.41)

\displaystyle\partial_{1}x^{N}_{u;v}\partial_{2}x^{N}_{u;v}-\partial_{1}x^{M}_{u;v}\partial_{2}x^{M}_{u;v}=\mathcal{J}_{MN}(u,v)+I_{2}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{D_{N}^{2}\setminus D_{M}^{2}}\mathbf{K}(u,v;\cdot)\bigr),

where
D_{N}
,
\mathbf{K}_{1}
,
\mathbf{K}_{2}
are as in (
5.30
),
D_{N}^{2}:=D_{N}\times D_{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
, and we set

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}(u,v;\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{K}_{1}(u,v,\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\otimes\mathbf{K}_{2}(u,v,\xi_{2},\eta_{2})

(5.42)

\displaystyle\phantom{:}=\frac{-\xi_{1}\eta_{2}\,e^{iu\xi_{1}}(e^{iu\xi_{2}}-1)\,e^{iv\eta_{2}}(e^{iv\eta_{1}}-1)}{\prod_{j=1}^{2}|\xi_{j}|^{H_{1}+1/2}|\eta_{j}|^{H_{2}+1/2}},

as well as

(5.43)

\mathcal{J}_{MN}(u,v):=\int_{D_{N}\setminus D_{M}}\mathbf{K}_{1}(u,v,\xi,\eta)\,\overline{\mathbf{K}_{2}(u,v,\xi,\eta)}\,d\xi\,d\eta.

Therefore we have obtained

(5.44)

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{N;\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{M;\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}\bigl(\partial_{1}x^{N}_{u;v}\,\partial_{2}x^{N}_{u;v}-\partial_{1}x^{M}_{u;v}\,\partial_{2}x^{M}_{u;v}\bigr)du\,dv

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{E:partial-diff}}{=}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}\Bigl[\mathcal{J}_{MN}(u,v)+I_{2}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{D_{N}^{2}\setminus D_{M}^{2}}\mathbf{K}(u,v;\cdot)\bigr)\Bigr]du\,dv

Next starting from (
5.44
), we set

(5.45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}(\cdot)

\displaystyle:=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}\mathbf{K}(u,v;\xi_{1},\cdot)\,du\,dv,

(5.46)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{MN}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}(u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2})

\displaystyle:=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}\mathcal{J}_{MN}(u,v)\,du\,dv,

and we apply the stochastic Fubini theorem. This yields

(5.47)

\mathbf{x}^{N;\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{M;\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}=I_{2}\Bigl(\mathbf{1}_{D_{N}^{2}\setminus D_{M}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}\Bigr)+\mathcal{J}_{MN}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}(u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}).

Hence using the fact that
\mathcal{J}_{MN}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}
is deterministic, the second moment of (
5.47
) reads

(5.48)

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl|\mathbf{x}^{N;\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{M;\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}\Bigr|^{2}\Bigr]=\mathcal{A}^{1}_{MN}(u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2})+\mathcal{A}^{2}_{MN}(u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}),

where we set

(5.49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{1}_{MN}(u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2})

\displaystyle:=\bigl(\mathcal{J}_{MN}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}(u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2})\bigr)^{2},

(5.50)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2}_{MN}(u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2})

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[I_{2}\Bigl(\mathbf{1}_{D_{N}^{2}\setminus D_{M}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr].

Below we provide separate bounds on
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{MN}
and
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{MN}
, respectively.

Step 3: Bound on
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{MN}
.

We first bound the deterministic term (
5.49
), where
\mathcal{J}_{MN}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}
is given by (
5.46
). To this aim, observe that according to (
5.41
) and (
5.43
), we have
\mathcal{J}_{MN}(u,v)=\mathcal{J}_{N}(u,v)-\mathcal{J}_{M}(u,v)
, where
\mathcal{J}_{N}(u,v)
can be expressed as

\mathcal{J}_{N}=\,\int_{D_{N}}\frac{i\xi e^{iu\xi}(e^{iv\eta}-1)}{|\xi|^{H_{1}+1/2}|\eta|^{H_{2}+1/2}}\frac{(-i\eta)e^{-iv\eta}(e^{-iu\xi}-1)}{|\xi|^{H_{1}+1/2}|\eta|^{H_{2}+1/2}}d\xi d\eta.

Hence some elementary manipulations show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{N}

\displaystyle=\,{\int_{-N}^{N}\frac{\xi(1-e^{iu\xi})}{|\xi|^{2H_{1}+1}}d\xi}{\int_{-N}^{N}\frac{\eta(1-e^{-iv\eta})}{|\eta|^{2H_{2}+1}}d\eta}=-(2i)^{2}\int_{0}^{N}\frac{\sin(u\xi)}{|\xi|^{2H_{1}}}d\xi\int_{0}^{N}\frac{\sin(u\eta)}{|\eta|^{2H_{2}}}d\eta

(5.51)

\displaystyle=4u^{2H_{1}-1}v^{2H_{2}-1}\,\mathcal{S}_{H_{1}}(uN)\,\mathcal{S}_{H_{2}}(vN),

where for
i=1,2
we define

\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(A):=\int_{0}^{A}\frac{\sin(x)}{x^{2H_{i}}}dx.

Now, using the elementary identity
AB-ab=A(B-b)+b(A-a)
we obtain

(5.52)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{MN}(u,v)=\mathcal{J}_{N}(u,v)-\mathcal{J}_{M}(u,v)

\displaystyle=4u^{2H_{1}-1}v^{2H_{2}-1}\Bigl[\mathcal{S}_{H_{1}}(uN)\bigl(\mathcal{S}_{H_{2}}(vN)-\mathcal{S}_{H_{2}}(vM)\bigr)+\mathcal{S}_{H_{2}}(vM)\bigl(\mathcal{S}_{H_{1}}(uN)-\mathcal{S}_{H_{1}}(uM)\bigr)\Bigr].

Since
\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(x)
converges to a finite limit as
x\to\infty
, the differences
\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(B)-\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(A)
are uniformly bounded. Specifically, for
i=1,2
we have

(5.53)

\sup_{0<A<B<\infty}\bigl|\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(B)-\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(A)\bigl|\leq C,

where
C
is a positive constant depends on
H_{i}
only. Next we use integration by parts in order to control the term
\mathcal{S}_{H_{1}}(uN)-\mathcal{S}_{H_{1}}(uM)
in terms of
M
. Namely we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(B)-\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(A)\Bigl\rvert

\displaystyle=\Bigl\rvert\int_{A}^{B}x^{-2H_{i}}\sin(x)dx\Bigl\rvert

(5.54)

\displaystyle=\Bigl\rvert\Bigl[-x^{-2H_{i}}\cos(x)\Bigr]_{A}^{B}-\int_{A}^{B}2H_{i}x^{-2H_{i}-1}\cos(x)dx\Bigl\lvert\leq CA^{-2H_{i}}.

Combining (
5.53
) and (
5.2
), then for any
\theta\in(0,1)
as small as possible but fixed, we have (recall that
C
is a generic constant which can change from line to line):

\Bigl\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(B)-\mathcal{S}_{H_{i}}(A)\Bigl\rvert\leq C^{1-\theta}A^{-2\theta H_{i}}.

Plugging this inequality in (
5.52
), we obtain

(5.55)

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{J}_{MN}(u,v)\bigl|\leq C\,M^{-2\theta H_{1}}u^{2(H_{1}-\theta)-1}v^{2(H_{2}-\theta)-1}.

We now choose
\theta\equiv\varepsilon<(H_{1}-\gamma_{1})\wedge(H_{2}-\gamma_{2})
small enough. By definition (
5.46
) of
\mathcal{J}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{MN}
and the triangle inequality,

\bigl|\mathcal{J}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{MN}(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})\bigr|\leq\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}\bigl|\mathcal{J}_{MN}(u,v)\bigr|\,du\,dv.

Inserting the pointwise bound (
5.55
) and using the fact that the integrand factors, we compute each integral explicitly:

\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}u^{2(H_{1}-\varepsilon)-1}\,du=\frac{u_{2}^{2(H_{1}-\varepsilon)}-u_{1}^{2(H_{1}-\varepsilon)}}{2(H_{1}-\varepsilon)},\qquad\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}v^{2(H_{2}-\varepsilon)-1}\,dv=\frac{v_{2}^{2(H_{2}-\varepsilon)}-v_{1}^{2(H_{2}-\varepsilon)}}{2(H_{2}-\varepsilon)},

which gives

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{J}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{MN}(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CM^{-2\varepsilon H_{1}}}{4(H_{1}-\varepsilon)(H_{2}-\varepsilon)}\left(u_{2}^{2(H_{1}-\varepsilon)}-u_{1}^{2(H_{1}-\varepsilon)}\right)\left(v_{2}^{2(H_{2}-\varepsilon)}-v_{1}^{2(H_{2}-\varepsilon)}\right)

(5.56)

\displaystyle\leq c\,M^{-2\varepsilon H_{1}}\bigl|u_{2}-u_{1}\bigl|^{H_{1}-\varepsilon}\bigl|v_{2}-v_{1}\bigl|^{H_{2}-\varepsilon}.

Recalling from (
5.49
) that
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{MN}=\bigl(\mathcal{J}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{MN}\bigr)^{2}
, we square (
5.56
) to conclude:

(5.57)

\mathcal{A}^{1}_{MN}(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})\leq C\,M^{-4\varepsilon H_{1}}\,|u_{2}-u_{1}|^{2(H_{1}-\varepsilon)}\,|v_{2}-v_{1}|^{2(H_{2}-\varepsilon)}.

This completes the bound on
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{MN}
.

Step 4: Bound on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{MN}
.
We now bound the Wiener chaos term (
5.50
). By the Itô isometry applied to
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{MN}
,

(5.58)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2}_{MN}(u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2})=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[I_{2}\Bigl(\mathbf{1}_{D_{N}^{2}\setminus D_{M}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}\Bigl)^{2}\Bigl]\leq 2\bigl\lVert\mathbf{1}_{D_{N}^{2}\setminus D_{M}^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}\bigl\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

Recall from (
5.45
) that
\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1},\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2};\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}\mathbf{K}(u,v;\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\,du\,dv
, where
\mathbf{K}
is given by (
5.2
). Substituting (
5.2
) and observing that the integrand factors into a function of
(u,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
times a function of
(v,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
, we get

\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1},\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2};\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\\
=\frac{-\xi_{1}\,\eta_{2}}{\prod_{j=1}^{2}|\xi_{j}|^{H_{1}+1/2}|\eta_{j}|^{H_{2}+1/2}}\left(\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}e^{iu\xi_{1}}(e^{iu\xi_{2}}-1)\,du\right)\left(\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}e^{iv\eta_{2}}(e^{iv\eta_{1}}-1)\,dv\right).

Setting

(5.59)

\mathcal{I}^{\hat{1}}_{u_{1}u_{2}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}):=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}e^{iu\xi_{1}}(e^{iu\xi_{2}}-1)\,du,\qquad\mathcal{I}^{\hat{2}}_{v_{1}v_{2}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}):=\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}e^{iv\eta_{2}}(e^{iv\eta_{1}}-1)\,dv,

we obtain

(5.60)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1},\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2};\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=\frac{-\xi_{1}\mathcal{I}^{\hat{1}}_{u_{1}u_{2}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\,\eta_{2}\mathcal{I}^{\hat{2}}_{v_{1}v_{2}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})}{\prod_{j=1}^{2}|\xi_{j}|^{H_{1}+1/2}|\eta_{j}|^{H_{2}+1/2}}.

The expressions for
\mathcal{I}^{\hat{1}}_{u_{1}u_{2}}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\hat{2}}_{v_{1}v_{2}}
will be further decomposed as follows. We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

\displaystyle:=i\xi_{2}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}e^{iu\xi_{1}}\int_{u_{1}}^{u}e^{iu^{\prime}\xi_{2}}du^{\prime}du,\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

\displaystyle:=\bigl(e^{iu_{1}\xi_{2}}-1\bigl)\frac{e^{iu_{2}\xi_{1}}-e^{iu_{1}\xi_{1}}}{i\xi_{1}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{\hat{2}^{\prime}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})

\displaystyle:=i\eta_{1}\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}e^{iv\eta_{2}}\int_{v_{1}}^{v}e^{iv^{\prime}\eta_{1}}dv^{\prime}dv,\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{\hat{2}^{\prime\prime}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})

\displaystyle:=\bigl(e^{iv_{1}\eta_{1}}-1\bigl)\frac{e^{iv_{2}\eta_{2}}-e^{iv_{1}\eta_{2}}}{i\eta_{2}}.

Then according to (
5.59
) we have

(5.61)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\hat{1}}_{u_{1}u_{2}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}e^{iu\xi_{1}}\bigl(e^{iu\xi_{2}}-1\bigl)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}e^{iu\xi_{1}}\bigl(e^{iu\xi_{2}}-e^{iu_{1}\xi_{2}}\bigl)du\,+\,\bigl(e^{iu_{1}\xi_{2}}-1\bigl)\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}e^{iu\xi_{1}}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})+\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}).

In the same way, we let the reader check that

(5.62)

\mathcal{I}^{\hat{2}}_{v_{1}v_{2}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{2}}e^{iv\eta_{2}}\bigl(e^{iv\eta_{1}}-1\bigl)dv=\mathcal{I}_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{\hat{2}^{\prime}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})+\mathcal{I}_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{\hat{2}^{\prime\prime}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}).

We now simplify
\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime}}
by a linear change of variables. To this aim, observe from (
5.61
) that

(5.63)

\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=i\xi_{2}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}e^{iu\xi_{1}}\int_{u_{1}}^{u}e^{iu^{\prime}\xi_{2}}du^{\prime}du

Then we set

s=\frac{u-u_{1}}{u_{2}-u_{1}},\qquad t=\frac{u^{\prime}-u_{1}}{u_{2}-u_{1}},

so that
u=u_{1}+s(u_{2}-u_{1})
and
u^{\prime}=u_{1}+t(u_{2}-u_{1})
. After some elementary computations we end up with

(5.64)

\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=i\xi_{2}\,(u_{2}-u_{1})^{2}\,e^{iu_{1}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}\,\mathcal{D}\bigl((u_{2}-u_{1})\xi_{1},(u_{2}-u_{1})\xi_{2}\bigl),

where we have set

(5.65)

\mathcal{D}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\int_{0}^{1}e^{iux_{1}}\int_{0}^{u}e^{iu^{\prime}x_{2}}du^{\prime}du.

Similarly, one can also check that

(5.66)

\mathcal{I}_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{\hat{2}^{\prime}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=i\eta_{1}\,(v_{2}-v_{1})^{2}\,e^{iv_{1}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})}\,\mathcal{D}\bigl((v_{2}-v_{1})\eta_{2},(v_{2}-v_{1})\eta_{1}\bigl).

Now recall from (
5.58
) and (
5.60
) that we wish to integrate kernels like
\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime}},\,\mathcal{I}_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{\hat{2}^{\prime}}
. Having this in mind, we define a new function

\phi_{\alpha}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\frac{|\mathcal{D}(z_{1},z_{2})|^{2}}{|z_{1}|^{2\alpha-1}|z_{2}|^{2\alpha-1}}.

Then another elementary change of variables reveals that

(5.67)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lvert\xi_{1}\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\bigl\rvert^{2}}{|\xi_{1}|^{2H_{1}+1}|\xi_{2}|^{2H_{1}+1}}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=(u_{2}-u_{1})^{4H_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{H_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2})\,dx_{1}\,dx_{2}=c\,(u_{2}-u_{1})^{4H_{1}},

(5.68)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lvert\eta_{2}\mathcal{I}_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{\hat{2}^{\prime}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\bigl\rvert^{2}}{|\eta_{1}|^{2H_{2}+1}|\eta_{2}|^{2H_{2}+1}}d\eta_{1}d\eta_{2}

\displaystyle=(v_{2}-v_{1})^{4H_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{H_{2}}(y_{1},y_{2})\,dy_{1}\,dy_{2}=c\,(v_{2}-v_{1})^{4H_{2}},

where for the second identities in (
5.67
) and (
5.68
) we have used the following fact, which can be checked by elementary integration tools for any
1/3<\alpha<1
(for our purposes
\alpha\in\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
):

(5.69)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{\alpha}(z_{1},z_{2})\,dz_{1}\,dz_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lvert\mathcal{D}(z_{1},z_{2})\bigl\lvert^{2}}{|z_{1}|^{2\alpha-1}|z_{2}|^{2\alpha-1}}\,dz_{1}\,dz_{2}<\infty.

Let us now focus on the terms
\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime\prime}},\,\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{2}^{\prime\prime}}
in (
5.61
)-(
5.62
).
For those terms we follow the same kind of elementary change of variables and integrations as for (
5.67
)-(
5.68
). We end up with integrals of the form

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lvert e^{iaz_{k}}-1\bigl\lvert^{2}\bigl\lvert e^{iz_{\ell}}-1\bigl\lvert^{2}}{|z_{k}|^{2\alpha+1}|z_{\ell}|^{2\alpha+1}}dz_{k}dz_{\ell}.

The analysis of those integrals involve the interpolation inequality
\lvert e^{ix}-1\rvert\leq 2^{1-\theta}|x|^{\theta}
for
|x|<1
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, which implies that for any
1/3<\alpha<1
,
\varepsilon>0
small and
(k,\ell)=(1,2),\ (2,1)
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lvert e^{iaz_{k}}-1\bigl\lvert^{2}\bigl\lvert e^{iz_{\ell}}-1\bigl\lvert^{2}}{|z_{k}|^{2\alpha+1}|z_{\ell}|^{2\alpha+1}}dz_{k}dz_{\ell}\leq c\,|a|^{2(\alpha+\varepsilon)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\psi_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z_{k},z_{\ell})dz_{k}dz_{\ell},

where

\psi_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\frac{\bigl\lvert e^{iz_{1}}-1\bigl\lvert^{2}}{|z_{1}|^{2\alpha+1}}\Bigl(|z_{2}|^{-2\alpha-1}\textbf{1}_{\{|z_{2}|>1\}}+|z_{2}|^{-1+2\varepsilon}\textbf{1}_{\{|z_{2}|\leq 1\}}\Bigl).

This type of arguments easily yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lvert\xi_{1}\mathcal{I}_{u_{1}u_{2}}^{\hat{1}^{\prime\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\bigl\rvert^{2}}{|\xi_{1}|^{2H_{1}+1}|\xi_{2}|^{2H_{1}+1}}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=(u_{2}-u_{1})^{4H_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lvert e^{ix_{1}}-1\bigl\lvert^{2}\bigl\lvert e^{i\frac{u_{1}}{(u_{2}-u_{1})}x_{2}}-1\bigl\lvert^{2}}{|x_{1}|^{2H_{1}+1}|x_{2}|^{2H_{1}+1}}dx_{1}dx_{2}

(5.70)

\displaystyle\leq\,(u_{2}-u_{1})^{2(H_{1}-\varepsilon)}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\psi_{H_{1},\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2},

as well as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lvert\eta_{2}\mathcal{I}_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{\hat{2}^{\prime\prime}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\bigl\rvert^{2}}{|\eta_{1}|^{2H_{2}+1}|\eta_{2}|^{2H_{2}+1}}d\eta_{1}d\eta_{2}

\displaystyle=(v_{2}-v_{1})^{4H_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\bigl\lvert e^{i\frac{v_{1}}{(v_{2}-v_{1})}y_{1}}-1\bigl\lvert^{2}\bigl\lvert e^{iy_{2}}-1\bigl\lvert^{2}}{|y_{1}|^{2H_{2}+1}|y_{2}|^{2H_{2}+1}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

(5.71)

\displaystyle\leq\,(v_{2}-v_{1})^{2(H_{2}-\varepsilon)}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\psi_{H_{2},\varepsilon}(y_{2},y_{1})dy_{1}dy_{2}.

We now assemble the
L^{2}
bound on
\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}
. Squaring (
5.60
) and using
|AB|^{2}=|A|^{2}|B|^{2}
, the integrand already factors:

\bigl\lvert\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2};\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\bigl\rvert^{2}=\frac{\bigl\lvert\xi_{1}\,\mathcal{I}^{\hat{1}}_{u_{1}u_{2}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\bigl\rvert^{2}}{|\xi_{1}|^{2H_{1}+1}|\xi_{2}|^{2H_{1}+1}}\cdot\frac{\bigl\lvert\eta_{2}\,\mathcal{I}^{\hat{2}}_{v_{1}v_{2}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\bigl\rvert^{2}}{|\eta_{1}|^{2H_{2}+1}|\eta_{2}|^{2H_{2}+1}}.

Plugging the decompositions (
5.61
)–(
5.62
) into each factor and applying
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
, we bound the
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
-factor by a sum involving
\mathcal{I}^{\hat{1}^{\prime}}_{u_{1}u_{2}}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\hat{1}^{\prime\prime}}_{u_{1}u_{2}}
, and similarly for the
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
-factor. Reporting the bounds (
5.67
)–(
5.70
) for the
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
-factor and (
5.68
)–(
5.71
) for the
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
-factor into the full integral, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}

\displaystyle\Bigl\lvert\mathcal{K}^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}_{u_{1}u_{2};v_{1}v_{2}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2};\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\Bigl\lvert^{2}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}d\eta_{1}d\eta_{2}

(5.72)

\displaystyle\leq(u_{2}-u_{1})^{2(H_{1}-\varepsilon)}(v_{2}-v_{1})^{2(H_{2}-\varepsilon)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\Psi^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}(x_{1},x_{2};y_{1},y_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}dy_{1}dy_{2}<\infty},

where
\Psi^{\hat{1};\hat{2}}
is spelled out as

(5.73)
