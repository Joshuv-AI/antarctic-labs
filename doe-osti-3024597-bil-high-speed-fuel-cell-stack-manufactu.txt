Executive Summary
General Motors LLC (GM) was awarded a project to develop and implement technologies for
manufacturing 20,000 units of Fuel Cell Stacks per year on two shifts per day basis. GM
leveraged prior in-house expertise in designing the Fuel Cells, deploying the manufacturing
process steps in the laboratory environment as well as the deployment in the industrial
environment on a smaller scale. The project focus was to design, build, and deploy a
manufacturing line consisting of an anode and cathode electrode processing, unitized electrode
assembly, fuel cell stacking, compression, testing, and final assembly of the fuel cell stack. The
project was terminated in the first budget period.

Scope
The project objective was to design, build, and deploy a manufacturing system to produce
20,000 next generation fuel cell stacks per year. The manufacturing line consisted of anode and
cathode electrode processing, Unitized Electrode Assembly (UEA), stacking, compression, testing
and final assembly of the fuel cell stack. The manufacturing system would help develop
workforce talent, manufacturing technology, and processes to execute large-scale
commercialization of next-generation fuel cells for numerous applications across the
transportation industry.

Approach

The project was divided into two phases (budget periods) approximately 18 months each.
 • Phase 1: Design, Build, and Runoff of Machinery, Tooling, and Equipment (MT&E)
           (10/1/24 - 12/31/25)
 • Phase 2: Machinery Tooling and Equipment Shipped, Installed, and Validated (1/1/26 -
 6/30/27)

Phase 1 (Budget Period 1) was for the design, build, and test of the equipment, tooling, and
runoff of machinery at the supplier. The machinery and equipment for executing the process
steps was purchased from outside suppliers. GM retained oversight of the entire project to
design, build and commission the equipment based on the specification developed in-house.
The equipment design incorporated over 100 years of manufacturing lessons learnt. Thus, the
primary tenets included safety, ergonomics, maintainability and ease of use. The manufacturing
line incorporated the features that will ensure that the defective product will not land in the
hands of a customer. The ability to track and trace the parts going into the stack was
incorporated in the process design. Process data was to be captured and retained for future use
in problem containment and elimination.

Scaling-up of the manufacturing process involved developing innovative approaches to execute
the individual process steps. The project team was well placed to assess the building blocks of
the technology and make it suitable for fuel cell manufacturing. The access to vast pool of
engineers within GM, with collective expertise in mechanical, controls and IT technologies would
make it possible to take some bold innovative steps and make it work.

Phase 2 (Budget Period 2) was not completed. This phase was to be focused on the shipping of
the equipment, tooling, and machinery to be shipped to GM and installed and validated.

Results
GM completed all the process development and design activities outlined in Statement of
Project Objectives (SOPO) under Phase 1 and started assembly of the system at the machine
builder prior to the project termination in December 2025.

Key achievements and completion dates are listed below:

• Sourcing Complete to OEM for automation system April 2024
 • Started design activities April 2024
 • Identification and ordering Long Lead systems June 2024
 • Completed system design November 2024
 • Machine Build and Assembly December 2024 – September 2025

• All processes validation completed November 2024
 • Laser Cutting
 • Inline Inspection
 • Material Handling Tooling
 • Laser cutting, inline inspection systems

In December 2025, GM requested the program be terminated as GM was cancelling its next
generation fuel cell program. The equipment was decommissioned and no processes were tested
or validated on the production equipment. GM did not bill or receive any federal funds for the
equipment or any related project costs.
