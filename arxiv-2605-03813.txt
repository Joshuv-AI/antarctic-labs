Large Homomorphisms on the Homotopy Lie Coalgebra
Andrew J. Soto Levins, Ryan Watson

Abstract
. We introduce and study a notion of large homomorphisms on the homotopy lie coalgebra; these homomorphisms are a variant of the large homomorphisms of Levin. As a consequence of our work, we establish new cases of a homotopy lie coalgebra analog of a conjecture of Quillen as proposed by Briggs.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 13D03, 13D05 Secondary: 16E45

Introduction

Throughout this paper, ring means unital commutative noetherian local ring. In 1978 Avramov
[
Avr78
]
introduced the notion of small homomorphisms in commutative algebra. These are local homomorphisms
\varphi\colon(R,\mathfrak{m},k)\to(S,\mathfrak{n},k)
such that the induced map,
\varphi_{*}\colon\tor^{R}(k,k)\to\tor^{S}(k,k)
is injective. Inspired by this, Levin
[
Lev80
]
defined the dual notion of large homomorphisms which are local homomorphisms in which the map
\varphi_{*}
is surjective. In this paper, we define a notion of large homomorphisms on the homotopy lie coalgebra (see
1.4
and
1.5
for the definition of the homotopy lie coalgebra).

Given a local map
\varphi\colon(R,\mathfrak{m},k)\rightarrow(S,\mathfrak{n},\ell)
, we say
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
if the natural map

\varphi_{n}\colon\pi_{n}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell\rightarrow\pi_{n}(S)

is surjective. By
[
Bri18
, Example 7]
, if
\varphi
is large, then
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
for all
n
. This gives many examples of homomorphisms that are
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
for a given
n
; also see
[
GT21
]
. The main result of this paper is the following, which is a consequence of
Theorem

3.5
. To make sense of it we first recall the notion of upper c.i. dimension. This is a variant of the usual notion of c.i. dimension (see
[
AGP97
,
Maj16
]
) but the faithfully flat extension in the quasi-deformation is also required to have regular closed fiber. We denote the upper c.i. dimension of a map by
\uppercidim{\varphi}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow(S,\mathfrak{n},\ell)
be a local homomorphism with
\uppercidim{\varphi}
finite. If
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{2n+1}-large}
for some
n\geq 1
, then
\varphi
is quasi-complete intersection at
\mathfrak{n}
.

When
\varphi
has finite flat dimension, the condition of being
\operatorname{\pi_{2n+1}-large}
is equivalent to
\varphi
being complete intersection at
\mathfrak{n}
by
Lemma

2.4
. In
Example

3.8
we give classes of surjective large homomorphisms with infinite projective dimension and finite upper complete intersection dimension, and thus providing examples of homomorphisms satisfying the assumptions in
A
that are not complete intersection at
\mathfrak{n}
.

In
[
Bri18
, Theorem 33]
, Briggs showed that
\varphi
is quasi-complete intersection at
\mathfrak{n}
if and only if
\varphi_{n}
is an isomorphism for
n\geq 3
, and in
[
Bri18
, Question on page 85]
asked must
\varphi
be quasi-complete intersection at
\mathfrak{n}
if
\varphi_{n}
is an isomorphism for
n\gg 0
? When
\uppercidim{\varphi}
is finite,
A
says that the answer to Briggs’ question is yes. Since
\varphi_{n}
is an isomorphism for
n\gg 0
if and only if
\widetilde{\pi}_{n}(\varphi)=0
for
n\gg 0
, where
\widetilde{\pi}_{n}(\varphi)
is a variant of the homotopy lie coalgebra defined in
[
Bri18
]
, this question of Briggs can be thought of as a homotopy lie coalgebra analog of a conjecture of Quillen, which we now explain.

Let
\operatorname{D}_{n}(S|R;-)
denote the
nth
André-Quillen homology functor on the category of
S
-modules, defined by André
[
And71
]
and Quillen
[
Qui70
]
. In
[
Qui70
, Conjecture 5.6]
Quillen conjectured that if
\varphi
is of finite type, then
\operatorname{D}_{n}(S|R;-)=0
for
n\gg 0
implies
\operatorname{D}_{n}(S|R;-)=0
for
n\geq 3
. This was also conjectured by Avramov in
[
Avr99
, pg. 459]
for maps not necessarily of finite type. In other words,
\varphi
is quasi-complete intersection when the André-Quillen homology functors eventually vanish. We note here that Quillen’s conjecture is known to hold when
S
is an algebra retract of
R

[
AI03
]
, when
\varphi
is onto and
S
has finite complete intersection dimension over
R

[
Sot00
, Proposition 10]
, and when
S
has finite flat dimension over
R

[
Avr99
]
. Using
[
BI23
]
, it can be shown that if
\uppercidim{\varphi}
is finite, then
\operatorname{D}_{n}(S|R;\ell)
being zero for some
n\geq 2
implies
\varphi
is quasi-complete intersection at
\mathfrak{n}
which establishes the conjecture of Quillen when
\varphi
is of finite type and has finite upper c.i. dimension (see
Proposition

3.10
).

1.
Background

In this section, we review necessary background for the paper. Throughout this secion,
\varphi\colon(R,\mathfrak{m},k)\rightarrow(S,\mathfrak{n},\ell)
is a local homomorphism of noetherian local rings.

1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
R
. The ring
k(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}S
is the fiber of
\varphi
at
\mathfrak{p}
. The fibers of the completion map
R\rightarrow\widehat{R}
are called the formal fibers of
R
. The closed fiber of
\varphi
is
S/\mathfrak{m}S
.

1.2
.

We now recall the definition of a Cohen factorization. A local homomorphism is called
weakly regular
if it is flat with regular closed fiber. By
[
AFH94
, Theorem 1.1]
the composition of
\varphi
with the completion map
S\rightarrow\widehat{S}
admits a
Cohen factorization

R\xrightarrow{\dot{\varphi}}R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{\prime}}\widehat{S},

where
\dot{\varphi}
is weakly regular,
R^{\prime}
complete, and
\varphi^{\prime}
surjective. Cohen factorizations reduce the problem of studying ring homomorphisms to only considering surjections and flat homomorphisms with regular closed fiber.

1.3
.

Assume
\widehat{R}\cong Q/I
where
Q
is a regular local ring and
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{Q}^{2}
, such a
Q
and
I
exist by Cohen’s structure theorem. A
minimal Cohen model of
R
is a semi-free extension
Q\to Q[X]
such that
Q[X]\to\widehat{R}
is a surjective quasi-isomorphism and
\partial(\mathfrak{m}_{Q[X]})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{Q}+\mathfrak{m}_{Q[X]}^{2}
. These always exist (see
[
Avr10
, Section 7.2]
).

Let
R\to R^{\prime}\to\widehat{S}
be a Cohen factorization of
\varphi
. By
[
Avr10
, Section 7.2]
, there exists a semi-free extension
R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime}[X]
such that
R^{\prime}[X]\to\widehat{S}
is a surjective quasi-isomorphism and
\partial(\mathfrak{m}_{R^{\prime}[X]})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{R^{\prime}}+\mathfrak{m}_{R^{\prime}[X]}^{2}
. The factorization
R\to R^{\prime}[X]\to\widehat{S}
is called the
minimal Cohen model for
\varphi
.

1.4
.

Assume
\widehat{R}\cong Q/I
, where
Q
is a regular local ring and
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{Q}^{2}
. Let
Q[X]\to\widehat{R}
be a minimal Cohen model. The
homotopy lie coalgebra
of
R
is defined to be

\pi_{*}(R)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\pi_{*}(Q[X])=\Sigma(\mathfrak{m}_{Q[X]}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}_{Q[X]}).

1.5
.

Let
R\to R^{\prime}[X]\rightarrow\widehat{S}
be a minimal Cohen model for
\varphi
. The
homotopy lie coalgebra
of
\varphi
is defined to be

\pi_{*}(\varphi)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\pi_{*}(A)=\Sigma(\mathfrak{m}_{A}/\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{2})

where
A=k\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}[X]
. Moreover, there is an induced map,
\pi_{*}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell\to\pi_{*}(S)
, and following the notation of
[
Bri18
]
, we set

\widetilde{\pi}_{*}(\varphi)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=H_{*}\left(\operatorname{cone}(\pi_{*}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell\to\pi_{*}(S))\right).

From this we get a long exact sequence of
\ell
vector spaces
[
Bri18
, (3.4)]

\cdots\to\widetilde{\pi}_{n+1}(\varphi)\to\pi_{n}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell\to\pi_{n}(S)\to\widetilde{\pi}_{n}(\varphi)\to\pi_{n-1}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell\to\cdots.

1.6
.

We now collect some facts about
\widetilde{\pi}_{*}(\varphi)
.

(1)

There is a natural map
\pi_{*}(\varphi)\to\widetilde{\pi}_{*}(\varphi)
, and when
\operatorname{fd}_{R}S
is finite, the map is an isomorphism
[
Bri18
, Theorem 30]
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{fd}_{R}S
is finite then, we obtain six term exact sequences
[
Bri18
, Theorem 25 and (3.2)]

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2i}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2i}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2i}(\varphi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2i-1}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2i-1}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2i-1}(\varphi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

(3)

By
[
Bri18
, Theorem 32]
, this measures the singularity of
\varphi
at the maximal ideal of
S
.

(a)

\varphi
is weakly regular if and only if
\widetilde{\pi}_{\geq 2}(\varphi)=0

(b)

\varphi
is c.i. at
\mathfrak{n}
if and only if
\widetilde{\pi}_{\geq 3}(\varphi)=0

(c)

\varphi
is q.c.i. at
\mathfrak{n}
(see
3.4
) if and only if
\widetilde{\pi}_{\geq 4}(\varphi)=0
.

1.7
.

If
\dot{\varphi}\colon(R,\mathfrak{m},k)\to(R^{\prime},\mathfrak{n},\ell)
is weakly regular, then
\pi_{n}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell\cong\pi_{n}(R^{\prime})
for all
n\geq 2
. Indeed,
\pi_{n}(\varphi)=0
for all
n\geq 2
by
[
AI03
, Proposition 2.6]
, so the claim follows by
1.6
(2) as
R^{\prime}
has finite flat dimension over
R
.

2.
Definitions and Basic Properties

In this section, we define
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
homomorphisms and prove facts we use throughout the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon(R,\mathfrak{m},k)\rightarrow(S,\mathfrak{n},\ell)
be a local homomorphism. We say
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
if the natural map

\pi_{n}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell\rightarrow\pi_{n}(S)

is surjective.

As mentioned in the introduction, every large homomorphism is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
for all
n
. The following example gives a homomorphism that is not large, but is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
for almost all
n
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
R=k[[x,y]]/(x^{3},x^{2}y)
and
S=k[[x,y]]/(x^{2},y^{2})
. The map
\varphi\colon R\twoheadrightarrow S
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
for
n\neq 2
. Indeed, since
S
is complete intersection,
\pi_{n}(S)=0
for
n\geq 3
(see
[
Avr10
, Section 7.3 and Theorem 10.2.1]
) and thus
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
for
n\geq 3
. Lastly, one can check that
\pi_{1}(R)\to\pi_{1}(S)
is an isomorphism, and
\pi_{2}(R)\to\pi_{2}(S)
is the zero map with
\pi_{2}(S)\neq 0
. Indeed, the map on
\pi_{1}
maps between the minimal generators of the maximal ideals of
R
and
S
respectively, and the map on
\pi_{2}
maps between the minimal generators of the ideals defining
R
and
S
respectively.

2.3
.

We now give the definition of locally complete intersection maps
[
Avr99
]
. Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
be a local homomorphism.

(1)

Let

R\xrightarrow{\dot{\varphi}}R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{\prime}}\widehat{S},

be a Cohen factorization of
\varphi
. The map
\varphi
is
complete intersection
(c.i.) if
\ker{\varphi^{\prime}}
is generated by a regular sequence. By
[
Avr99
, Remark 3.3]
this property does not depend on the choice of Cohen factorization.

(2)

The map
\varphi
is c.i. at a prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
S
if the localization
\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}\colon R_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow S_{\mathfrak{q}}
is c.i. where
\mathfrak{p}=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})
, and
\varphi
is
locally c.i.
if
\varphi
is c.i. at every prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
S
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow(S,\mathfrak{n},\ell)
be a local homomorphism. Consider the following conditions

(1)

\varphi
is locally complete intersection.

(2)

\operatorname{fd}_{R}S<\infty
and
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
for all
n\geq 3
.

(3)

\operatorname{fd}_{R}S<\infty
and
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{2n+1}-large}
for some
n\geq 1
.

(4)

\varphi
is complete intersection at
\mathfrak{n}
.

Then
1\implies 2\iff 3\iff 4
.

Proof.

The fact that
(1)
implies
(4)
is by definition, so we just need to show the equivalence of
(2)
,
(3)
, and
(4)
. It is clear that
(2)
implies
(3)
, so we now show
(3)
implies
(4)
.

Assume
\operatorname{fd}_{R}S<\infty
and
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{2n+1}-large}
for some
n\geq 1
. As the flat dimension of
S
over
R
is finite, by
1.6
, we have exact sequences for all
i\geq 0
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2i+1}(R)\otimes_{k}\ell}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2i+1}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2i+1}(\varphi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

As
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{2n+1}-large}
, we have
\pi_{2n+1}(\varphi)=0
. Hence by
[
AI03
, Corollary 5.5]
,
\varphi
is c.i. at
\mathfrak{n}
.

Lastly, we show
(4)
implies
(2)
. As
\varphi
is c.i. at
\mathfrak{n}
, the equality
\pi_{n}(\varphi)=0
holds for all
n\geq 3
by
[
AI03
, 2.8]
. Moreover, since
\varphi
is c.i.,
S
has finite flat dimension over
R
by
[
AFH94
, Lemma 3.2]
so by
1.6
,
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
for
n\geq 3
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

By
[
Avr99
, Lemma 5.12.1]
when the formal fibers of
R
are locally c.i., then condition
4
implies condition
1
so all four conditions in the lemma are equivalent. However, in
[
FR70
, Remarque 3.2.1]
the authors give an example that is c.i. at
\mathfrak{n}
but not locally c.i., so in general the four statements in the lemma are not all equivalent.

3.
Main Result

In this section we prove
A
. We first recall the definition of maps with finite upper c.i. dimension
[
Maj16
]
and
[
Tak04
]
.

3.1
.

A local map
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
has
finite upper c.i. dimension
, denoted by
\uppercidim{\varphi}<\infty
, if there exists a Cohen factorization

R\rightarrow R^{\prime}\rightarrow\widehat{S}

and a diagram
R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{h}R^{\prime\prime}\xleftarrow{f}Q
of local homomorphisms so that
h
is weakly regular,
f
is surjective,
\ker{f}
is c.i., and
\operatorname{pd}_{Q}\widehat{S}\otimes_{R^{\prime}}R^{\prime\prime}
is finite. Such a diagram is called a
quasi-deformation
. Note that if
\operatorname{pd}_{R^{\prime}}\widehat{S}<\infty
, then
\uppercidim_{R^{\prime}}\widehat{S}<\infty
. In particular, if
\operatorname{fd}_{R}S<\infty
, then
\uppercidim{\varphi}<\infty
by
[
AFH94
, Lemma 3.2]
.

We next prove the following lemma, which allows us to pass the property of being
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
through quasideformations.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
be a local homomorphism. Let

R\rightarrow R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{\prime}}\widehat{S}

be a Cohen factorization of
\varphi
and assume there is a diagram

R^{\prime}\rightarrow R^{\prime\prime}\leftarrow Q

of local homomorphisms with the first map weakly regular and the second map onto. Consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{S}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R^{\prime\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{S}\otimes_{R^{\prime}}R^{\prime\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

If
f
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
for some
n\geq 2
, then
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
if and only if
gf
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
.

Proof.

Let
k
be the residue field of
R
,
\ell
the residue field of
S
, and
L
the residue field of
Q
. Since
R\to R^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime\prime}
are both weakly regular, the composite
R\to R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime\prime}
is also weakly regular
[
AF98
, construction 4.4]
. Similarly,
S\to\widehat{S}\otimes_{R^{\prime}}R^{\prime\prime}
is weakly regular as well. Consider the following diagram induced by the functoriality of

,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{n}(R)\otimes_{k}L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{n}(S)\otimes_{\ell}L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{n}(Q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{n}(R^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{n}(\widehat{S}\otimes_{R^{\prime}}R^{\prime\prime}).}

Since

and

are weakly regular, the vertical maps are isomorphisms for all

by

. Hence,
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
if and only if
g
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
. Assuming
f
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
, it follows that
gf
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
if and only if
\varphi
is
\operatorname{\pi_{n}-large}
.
∎

Before stating the theorem, we recall some definitions.

3.3
.

André-Quillen homology was introduced independently by André
[
And71
]
and Quillen
[
Qui70
]
. For a ring homomorphism
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
one can attach to it a complex
\operatorname{L}_{S|R}
, known as the cotangent complex, and given an
S

the

over

with coefficients in

\operatorname{D}_{n}(S|R;M)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\operatorname{H}_{n}(\operatorname{L}_{S|R}\otimes_{S}M).

3.4
.

We now give the definition of
quasi-complete intersection
(q.c.i) maps. See
[
AHŞ13
, Section 1]
for a discussion of q.c.i ideals. For an ideal

in a noetherian local ring

, the ideal

is q.c.i. if and only if
\operatorname{D}_{n}(R/I|R;-)=0
for

by

\varphi\colon R\rightarrow(S,\mathfrak{n},\ell)
be a local homomorphism. The map

is q.c.i at

if in some Cohen factorization

R\rightarrow R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{\prime}}\widehat{S}

the ideal
\ker{\varphi^{\prime}}
is q.c.i. By
[
AHŞ13
, Lemma 7.2]
this property does not depend on the choice of Cohen factorization.

\varphi

for all

. Before getting to the example, we first need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local complete intersection and

a nonzero ideal so that

. If

has infinite projective dimension and
