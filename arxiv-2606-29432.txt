Squeezing-enhanced Pairwise Fusion of Photonic Qudits
Pradip Laha, Peter van Loock

Abstract
Pairwise fusion gates are linear-optical measurements that herald Bell projections onto two-rail subspaces of two d d -rail single-photon qudits. Without ancillary input photons, these passive measurements succeed with probability 1 − d − 1 1-d^{-1} , with all failures confined to the diagonal logical subspace. We show that identical single-mode squeezers applied to the 2 ​ d 2d interferometer outputs before photon-number-resolving detection recover part of this structured failure sector. Photon-number parity preserves the successful off-diagonal fusion signatures, while selected all-even patterns yield POVM elements proportional to definite pairwise Bell projectors. We derive the exact logical-space POVM and prove that a diagonal pattern is accepted if and only if its photon-number-imbalance vector has exactly two nonzero components of equal magnitude. The resulting closed elliptic-integral expression increases the ideal success probability, for instance, from 75 % 75\% to 79.62 % 79.62\% for d = 4 d=4 , and from 83.33 % 83.33\% to 87.15 % 87.15\% for d = 6 d=6 . With a representative finite detector saturation threshold, n sat = 7 n_{\rm sat}=7 , the respective certified values remain 78.84 % 78.84\% and 86.71 % 86.71\% . These results establish active Gaussian processing as a method for recycling structured measurement failures without ancillary input photons, at the cost of 2 ​ d 2d squeezing operations and a larger photon-number range at detection.

Predetection squeezing has, in particular, recently been applied to generalized high-dimensional Bell analyzers, where the active squeezing layer before photon-number-resolving (PNR) detection reshapes photon-counting signatures associated with generalized Bell states
Bianchi et al. 2025

Our starting point is the pairwise fusion gate (PFG) introduced by Yamazaki and Azuma

d
-rail photonic qudits, the ancilla-free PFG projects onto Bell states supported on known two-rail subspaces with success probability
1-d^{-1}

2(2^{k}-1)

k\geq 1

1-d^{-(k+1)}
. Crucially, the ancilla-free failure is not generic: passive interference already converts the entire off-diagonal, or different-rail, logical sector into pairwise Bell projections, whereas all failure probability is confined to the
d
-dimensional diagonal, or same-rail, sector. This structure leads to the central question of this work: can an active optical layer act selectively on this diagonal failure sector while leaving the already successful pairwise projections intact?

Here, we demonstrate that squeezing can perform precisely this failure-sector engineering. As shown in Fig.
1
, identical single-mode squeezers are appended to all
2d
outputs of the ancilla-free PFG before PNR detection. In the ideal lossless case, squeezing preserves photon-number parity, so the odd-parity signatures that identify the off-diagonal PFG outcomes remain protected. In the all-even diagonal sector, however, squeezing redistributes the photon-number amplitudes and creates additional patterns whose restricted Kraus vectors are proportional to definite pairwise Bell states.

For
d>2
, the
global
qualification is essential: pairwise diagonal Bell states associated with different rail pairs are not mutually orthogonal because they share diagonal basis states. The accepted probability therefore cannot be obtained by optimizing embedded two-dimensional subspaces separately and adding the results.
Instead, we classify the POVM on the full
d
-rail logical space. Every all-even count pattern defines a difference vector whose components are the half-photon-number imbalances between the two output ports of each rail. An outcome is a valid pairwise diagonal Bell projection
if and only if
this vector has exactly two nonzero components of equal magnitude. This criterion gives an exact closed elliptic-integral expression for the ideal-PNR success probability.

We find that finite photon-number resolution suppresses, but does not eliminate, the squeezing-induced advantage.

II
Pairwise fusion with squeezing

II.1
Logical space and pairwise Bell projections

In a
d
-rail single-photon encoding, one logical qudit is represented by one photon occupying one of
d
orthogonal modes, with all other modes in vacuum. We consider two such photonic qudits,
A
and
B
. Their respective input creation operators are denoted by
a_{i}^{\dagger}
and
b_{i}^{\dagger}
. The computational basis states are
|i\rangle_{A}=a_{i}^{\dagger}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle_{A}
and
|j\rangle_{B}=b_{j}^{\dagger}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle_{B}
, with
i=0,\ldots,d-1
.
In the two-register Fock space, we write
|\mathrm{vac}\rangle=|\mathrm{vac}\rangle_{A}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle_{B}
for the joint multimode vacuum.
Since the input contains exactly one photon in each register, the two-qudit logical state lies in the two-photon subspace

\mathcal{H}_{2\gamma}^{(d)}=\operatorname{span}\left\{a_{i}^{\dagger}b_{j}^{\dagger}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle:i,j=0,\ldots,d-1\right\}.

(1)

Equivalently,
|i\rangle_{A}|j\rangle_{B}=a_{i}^{\dagger}b_{j}^{\dagger}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle
, where
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
label the logical single-photon modes of the two qudits, not photon occupation numbers. For each unordered pair of distinct rails
i<j
, the pairwise Bell states are

\displaystyle|\Psi_{ij}^{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(|i\rangle_{A}|j\rangle_{B}\pm|j\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}\Big),

(2)

\displaystyle|\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}\pm|j\rangle_{A}|j\rangle_{B}\Big).

(3)

The states
|\Psi_{ij}^{\pm}\rangle
belong to the off-diagonal, or different-rail, sector, whereas the states
|\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}\rangle
belong to the diagonal, or same-rail, sector. It is useful to introduce the diagonal and off-diagonal projectors

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm diag}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}{}_{A}\!\langle i|{}_{B}\!\langle i|,

(4)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm off}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{d-1}|i\rangle_{A}|j\rangle_{B}{}_{A}\!\langle i|{}_{B}\!\langle j|.

(5)

Their sum

\Pi_{2\gamma}=\Pi_{\rm off}+\Pi_{\rm diag}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}|i\rangle_{A}|j\rangle_{B}{}_{A}\!\langle i|{}_{B}\!\langle j|,

(6)

is the projector from the full optical Fock space onto the two-photon logical subspace

\mathcal{H}_{2\gamma}^{(d)}
, and hence acts as the identity operator when restricted to that subspace.

There are
\binom{d}{2}
unordered rail pairs and two signs for each pair, so each of the two labelled families contains
d(d-1)
states. The passive PFG success probability, however, cannot be obtained by counting these labels. The states
|\Psi_{ij}^{\pm}\rangle
are mutually orthogonal and form a basis of the
d(d-1)
-dimensional off-diagonal sector. By contrast, for
d>2
, the collection of states
|\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}\rangle
is neither mutually orthogonal nor a Bell basis of the diagonal sector. For example, for three distinct rails
i,j,k
,
\langle\Phi_{ij}^{+}|\Phi_{ik}^{+}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}
.
Different rail pairs therefore share diagonal basis components. A PFG does not implement a complete BM on the full
d^{2}
-dimensional two-qudit space. Rather, each successful outcome heralds a Bell projection supported on a definite two-rail subspace
(i,j)
; all remaining outcomes are treated as erasures.

The passive ancilla-free PFG
Yamazaki and Azuma 2025
succeeds with unit probability when the input is supported entirely in the off-diagonal sector. Equivalently, its successful outcomes implement restricted Kraus bras proportional to
\langle\Psi_{ij}^{\pm}|
for every
i<j
, and their POVM elements sum to
\Pi_{\rm off}
. For an arbitrary logical input state
\rho
, the passive success probability is therefore

P_{\rm f}^{\rm(pass)}(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\Pi_{\rm off}\rho\right].

For the maximally mixed logical input,

\rho_{\rm mix}=\Pi_{2\gamma}/d^{2}
, this becomes the off-diagonal fraction of the logical space,

P_{\rm f}(d)=\frac{d(d-1)}{d^{2}}=1-\frac{1}{d}.

(7)

Equation (
7
) is the state-averaged benchmark used throughout this work. The aim of the squeezed construction is to retain unit success conditioned on the off-diagonal sector while recovering a well-defined subset of the diagonal failure sector.

II.2
Rail-wise PFG interferometer

The passive interferometer is a product of balanced (50:50) beam splitters (BSs), one for each matched input-rail pair
(a_{i},b_{i})
. We use the BS phase convention

\displaystyle a_{i}^{\dagger}\longmapsto\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(c_{i}^{\dagger}+d_{i}^{\dagger}\right),\qquad b_{i}^{\dagger}\longmapsto\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(c_{i}^{\dagger}-d_{i}^{\dagger}\right).

(8)

Here
c_{i}
and
d_{i}
denote the two output modes associated with rail
i
. Other phase conventions differ only by fixed local phases and give the same success probabilities.

The rail-wise interference gives the diagonal and off-diagonal sectors distinct output-parity structures. Each off-diagonal Bell state produces exactly two odd-parity output modes. For
|\Psi_{ij}^{+}\rangle
, these modes are either
(c_{i},c_{j})
or
(d_{i},d_{j})
, whereas for
|\Psi_{ij}^{-}\rangle
they are either
(d_{i},c_{j})
or
(c_{i},d_{j})
. Their locations therefore identify both the rail pair
(i,j)
and the sign
\pm
. These parity patterns constitute the passive PFG success outcomes.

By contrast, a diagonal basis input
|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}
is mapped to the two-mode bunched state

|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}\longmapsto\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(|2\rangle_{c_{i}}|0\rangle_{d_{i}}-|0\rangle_{c_{i}}|2\rangle_{d_{i}}\big).

(9)

Passive photon counting can identify that the event originated from a same-rail input because the photons remain confined to the output pair
(c_{i},d_{i})
. However, it also reveals the occupied diagonal rail
i
, and therefore implements a same-rail projection proportional to
\langle i|_{A}\langle i|_{B}
, rather than erasing the rail information needed to project coherently onto
|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}\pm|j\rangle_{A}|j\rangle_{B}
, i.e., onto
|\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}\rangle
. The passive failure probability
1/d
is thus not an unstructured erasure: it is confined entirely to the diagonal sector.

II.3
Local squeezing and effective POVM

We now append identical single-mode squeezers to all
2d
output modes before PNR detection, as shown in Fig.
1
. For a single mode
c
,

S_{c}(r)=\exp\left[\tfrac{r}{2}\left(c^{2}-c^{\dagger 2}\right)\right],

(10)

and the full local Gaussian layer is

\mathcal{S}(r)=\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}S_{c_{i}}(r)\,S_{d_{i}}(r).

(11)

A direct spatial implementation therefore uses one squeezing operation on each PFG output mode, for a total of
2d
single-mode squeezing operations. Throughout,
r
is taken to be real and identical across all modes. Mode-dependent squeezing amplitudes and phases define a broader optimization problem but are not required for the dimension-independent mechanism considered here.

Although the logical input contains exactly two photons, the squeezing layer produces superpositions over higher photon-number sectors. One key property is that it preserves photon-number parity locally. For every output mode
\mu
,

\left[S_{\mu}(r),(-1)^{\hat{n}_{\mu}}\right]=0,

(12)

where
\hat{n}_{\mu}
is the photon-number operator of that mode. Hence, in the lossless measurement, the two odd-mode signatures of the passive off-diagonal PFG outcomes remain disjoint from the all-even diagonal sector. Squeezing can therefore redistribute diagonal-sector amplitudes without invalidating the passive PFG outcomes.

The physical readout remains photon counting, while the inserted Gaussian layer changes the POVM induced on the original two-photon logical input space. For a detected photon-number pattern

\bm{n}=\left(n_{c_{0}},n_{d_{0}},\ldots,n_{c_{d-1}},n_{d_{d-1}}\right),

(13)

the restricted Kraus bra is

\langle\kappa_{\bm{n}}(r)|=\langle\bm{n}|\mathcal{S}(r)\,U_{\rm PFG}\,\Pi_{2\gamma}\,.

(14)

The operators in Eq. (
14
) act from right to left: the logical-subspace projector
\Pi_{2\gamma}
defined in Eq. (
6
) restricts the input,
U_{\rm PFG}=\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}U_{i}^{\rm BS}
applies the rail-wise interferometer,
\mathcal{S}(r)
applies the local squeezing layer, and
\langle\bm{n}|
selects the observed photon-counting outcome.

The amplitude for a logical input
|\psi\rangle
to produce the detection pattern
\bm{n}
is therefore

\langle\kappa_{\bm{n}}(r)|\psi\rangle
, and the corresponding rank-one POVM element on the logical input space is

E_{\bm{n}}(r)=|\kappa_{\bm{n}}(r)\rangle\langle\kappa_{\bm{n}}(r)|.

(15)

A detection pattern is accepted as a successful PFG outcome only when its restricted Kraus bra is nonzero and proportional to one target pairwise Bell bra, i.e.,

\langle\kappa_{\bm{n}}(r)|\propto\langle\Psi_{ij}^{\pm}|\qquad\text{or}\qquad\langle\kappa_{\bm{n}}(r)|\propto\langle\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}|

for a definite rail pair
i<j
and a definite sign. All other outcomes are declared erasures; in particular, patterns whose restricted Kraus bras contain contributions from more than one pairwise Bell projection are not counted as PFG successes. This is an operator-level declaration: a successful pattern identifies the pairwise Bell projection implemented by that measurement outcome, rather than discriminating an ensemble of mutually nonorthogonal diagonal states.

With this convention, the accepted photon-counting patterns define the coarse-grained success POVM element

M_{\rm suc}(r)=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathcal{U}(r)}E_{\bm{n}}(r),

(16)

where
\mathcal{U}(r)
is the set of accepted photon-number outcomes. Averaging this POVM element over the maximally mixed logical input,
\rho_{\rm mix}=\Pi_{2\gamma}/d^{2}
, gives

P_{\rm f}(d,r)=\operatorname{Tr}\big[M_{\rm suc}(r)\rho_{\rm mix}\big]=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{2\gamma}^{(d)}}\big[M_{\rm suc}(r)\big].

(17)

This averaging convention follows Ref.
Yamazaki and Azuma 2025
: it weights the actual dimensions of the logical input sectors rather than counting the nonorthogonal labels
|\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}\rangle
.

For the lossless ideal-PNR measurement, the coarse-grained success element has a simple sector structure. The accepted off-diagonal patterns are exactly the passive PFG success signatures, so the off-diagonal contribution is
\Pi_{\rm off}
. Squeezing does not change this part of the measurement. Its only effect on the success probability is to make some all-even diagonal-sector patterns implement valid pairwise diagonal Bell projections. These outcomes occur only probabilistically; by rail symmetry, their total accepted probability is the same for every fixed diagonal basis input. Denoting this probability by
p_{\rm diag}(d,r)
, we obtain

M_{\rm suc}(r)=\Pi_{\rm off}+p_{\rm diag}(d,r)\,\Pi_{\rm diag},

(18)

where the exact value of
p_{\rm diag}(d,r)
is derived in Sec.
III.2
.

Equation (
18
) also gives the success probability for an arbitrary logical input state
\rho
:

P_{\rm f}(\rho;d,r)=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\Pi_{\rm off}\rho\right]+p_{\rm diag}(d,r)\operatorname{Tr}\left[\Pi_{\rm diag}\rho\right].

(19)

For the maximally mixed input, these weights are
1-1/d
and
1/d
, respectively. Since
p_{\rm diag}(d,0)=0
, the passive result is recovered at
r=0
.

III
Exact POVM theory and ideal-PNR performance

III.1
Diagonal sector as a difference-vector measurement

We now classify the accepted all-even diagonal outcomes and evaluate their total probability. Let
|D_{i}\rangle\equiv|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}=a_{i}^{\dagger}b_{i}^{\dagger}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle
denote a diagonal computational-basis input. As shown in Eq. (
9
), the rail-wise BS maps
|D_{i}\rangle
to a two-photon bunched superposition in the output modes
c_{i}
and
d_{i}
. Accordingly, diagonal inputs contribute only to all-even outcomes.
We write an all-even photon-counting outcome as

n_{c_{i}}=2q_{i}^{c},\qquad n_{d_{i}}=2q_{i}^{d},\qquad q_{i}^{c},q_{i}^{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(20)

For a single mode, define the squeezed-vacuum and squeezed-two-photon amplitudes

v_{q}(r)=\langle 2q|S(r)|0\rangle,\qquad u_{q}(r)=\langle 2q|S(r)|2\rangle.

(21)

The required matrix elements are given in Appendix
A
. For
r>0
, the ratio
u_{q}(r)/v_{q}(r)
is affine in
q
:

R_{q}(r)\equiv\frac{u_{q}(r)}{v_{q}(r)}=\frac{\tanh r}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{2}\,q}{\sinh r\cosh r}.

(22)

The passive endpoint
r=0
is evaluated directly from the original amplitudes, or equivalently by taking the continuous limit of the final probability expressions; the ratio in Eq. (
22
) is not evaluated at
r=0
.

For an all-even pattern

\bm{q}=\left(q_{0}^{c},q_{0}^{d},\ldots,q_{d-1}^{c},q_{d-1}^{d}\right),

(23)

the Kraus bra restricted to

\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{span}\{|D_{i}\rangle\}_{i=0}^{d-1}

is

\langle K_{\bm{q}}|=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}A_{i}(\bm{q})\langle D_{i}|,

(24)

where
A_{i}(\bm{q})=\langle K_{\bm{q}}|D_{i}\rangle
. Since
|D_{i}\rangle
enters the squeezing layer as the bunched superposition of Eq. (
9
), while all rails
\ell\neq i
are initially in vacuum,
A_{i}(\bm{q})
factorizes as

A_{i}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[u_{q_{i}^{c}}\,v_{q_{i}^{d}}-v_{q_{i}^{c}}\,u_{q_{i}^{d}}\right]\prod_{\ell\neq i}v_{q_{\ell}^{c}}\,v_{q_{\ell}^{d}}.

(25)

For compactness, the argument
r
of
u_{q}
and
v_{q}
is suppressed in intermediate expressions. The relative minus sign is inherited from the BS phase convention in Eq. (
9
). Using
u_{q}=R_{q}v_{q}
, the bracket in Eq. (
25
) becomes

v_{q_{i}^{c}}v_{q_{i}^{d}}\left[R_{q_{i}^{c}}-R_{q_{i}^{d}}\right]=-\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(q_{i}^{c}-q_{i}^{d}\right)}{\sinh r\cosh r}v_{q_{i}^{c}}v_{q_{i}^{d}}.

Therefore, for
r>0
,

A_{i}(\bm{q})=C_{\bm{q}}(r)\,m_{i}
, where

m_{i}=q_{i}^{c}-q_{i}^{d}=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(n_{c_{i}}-n_{d_{i}}\big)

(26)

is the half-photon-number imbalance between the two output ports of rail
i
, and

C_{\bm{q}}(r)=-\frac{1}{\sinh r\cosh r}\prod_{\ell=0}^{d-1}v_{q_{\ell}^{c}}(r)v_{q_{\ell}^{d}}(r)

(27)

is a common amplitude prefactor for the pattern
\bm{q}
. Equation (
24
) therefore becomes

\langle K_{\bm{q}}|=C_{\bm{q}}(r)\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}m_{i}\,\langle D_{i}|.

(28)

Although the separated prefactor in Eq. (
27
) is not defined at
r=0
, the physical amplitudes in Eq. (
25
) have a regular passive limit. All statements based on Eqs. (
22
)–(
28
) are therefore understood for
r>0
, with
r=0
treated by continuity at the probability level.

Each all-even squeezed detection outcome is thus represented in the diagonal subspace by the integer rail-imbalance vector

\bm{m}=(m_{0},\ldots,m_{d-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(29)

The prefactor
C_{\bm{q}}(r)
determines the probability weight, whereas the direction of
\bm{m}
determines the projected diagonal state. In particular,
\bm{m}=\bm{0}
gives a vanishing Kraus bra on the diagonal logical subspace.

Let
\bm{e}_{i}
denote the
i
-th standard basis vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and let
h=1,2,\ldots
be the common magnitude of the nonzero imbalances. A nonzero all-even outcome implements a pairwise diagonal Bell projection if and only if

\bm{m}=\pm h\bm{e}_{i}\pm h\bm{e}_{j},\qquad i<j.

(30)

Equation (
30
) is exactly the requirement that the restricted Kraus bra have support on only two diagonal basis states,
\langle D_{i}|
and
\langle D_{j}|
, with coefficients of equal magnitude. Equal signs give a bra proportional to
\langle D_{i}|+\langle D_{j}|
, and hence to
\langle\Phi_{ij}^{+}|
while opposite signs give a bra proportional to
\langle D_{i}|-\langle D_{j}|
, and hence to
\langle\Phi_{ij}^{-}|
, up to an ignorable overall phase. The criterion is independent of the squeezing strength:
r
changes the probability assigned to each pattern but not whether its imbalance vector represents a target pairwise Bell projection. The necessity and sufficiency of Eq. (
30
) are proved in Appendix
B
.

All other all-even outcomes are rejected by the PFG success rule. A single nonzero component gives a Kraus bra proportional to
\langle D_{i}|
for one rail
i
; two nonzero components of unequal magnitude give a nonmaximally weighted two-rail superposition; and support on more than two rails gives a higher-dimensional diagonal superposition. Such outcomes may be relevant for other measurements, but they are not accepted as pairwise fusion successes here.

III.2
Exact ideal-PNR success probability

We now sum the probabilities of all all-even outcomes satisfying (
28
). Let

w_{q}(r)=|v_{q}(r)|^{2}=\operatorname{sech}r\,\frac{(2q)!}{2^{2q}(q!)^{2}}\tanh^{2q}r

(31)

be the probability of detecting
2q
photons from a squeezed-vacuum input. Consider a fixed diagonal input
|D_{i}\rangle
. For an all-even pattern
\bm{q}
, Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
) give

\left|\langle K_{\bm{q}}|D_{i}\rangle\right|^{2}=\frac{m_{i}^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r\cosh^{2}r}\prod_{\ell=0}^{d-1}w_{q_{\ell}^{c}}(r)w_{q_{\ell}^{d}}(r).

(32)

The diagonal recovery probability is obtained by summing Eq. (
32
) over all accepted imbalance patterns. For each partner rail
j\neq i
, imbalance magnitude
h\geq 1
, the accepted patterns satisfy

m_{i}=\pm h,\quad m_{j}=\pm h,\quad m_{\ell}=0\quad(\ell\neq i,j).

Thus,

\displaystyle p_{\rm diag}(d,r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{q}:\,|m_{i}|=h,\\
|m_{j}|=h,\\
m_{\ell}=0;(\ell\neq i,j)\end{subarray}}\left|\langle K_{\bm{q}}|D_{i}\rangle\right|^{2}.

(33)

This sum factorizes rail by rail. To evaluate it, define the squeezed-vacuum autocorrelations

Z_{h}(r)=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}w_{q+h}(r)\,w_{q}(r),\qquad h=0,1,2,\ldots.

(34)

For
h\geq 1
,
Z_{h}(r)
is the weight associated with one specified signed imbalance between two squeezed-vacuum modes; summing over the two signs gives
2Z_{h}(r)
. The zero-imbalance weight is
Z_{0}(r)
.

For the occupied rail
i
, the factor
m_{i}^{2}
in Eq. (
32
) contributes
h^{2}
, and summing over the two signs of
m_{i}
gives

\frac{2h^{2}Z_{h}(r)}{\sinh^{2}r\cosh^{2}r}.

For the partner rail
j
, summing over the two signs of
m_{j}
gives
2Z_{h}(r)
. Each of the remaining
d-2
spectator rails has zero imbalance and contributes
Z_{0}(r)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle p_{\rm diag}(d,r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{2h^{2}Z_{h}(r)}{\sinh^{2}r\cosh^{2}r}\right]\!\!\big[2Z_{h}(r)\big]Z_{0}(r)^{d-2}

\displaystyle=(d-1)\frac{4Z_{0}(r)^{d-2}}{\sinh^{2}r\cosh^{2}r}\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{2}Z_{h}(r)^{2}.

(35)

Introducing
y=\tanh^{2}(2r)
with
0\leqslant y<1
, the above equation reduces exactly to

p_{\rm diag}(d,r)=\frac{d-1}{4}\,y\,(1-y)^{(d-1)/2}\left[\frac{2\,\mathrm{K}(y)}{\pi}\right]^{d-2}\,.

(36)

Here,
\mathrm{K}(y)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}d\theta/\sqrt{1-y\sin^{2}\theta}

is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind in the parameter convention. The
reduction
to Eq. (
36
) and the absolute convergence of Eq. (
35
) are established in Appendix
C
.

Equation (
36
) is the exact ideal-PNR probability that a fixed diagonal basis input produces an accepted pairwise Bell projection. By rail-permutation symmetry, it is independent of the occupied rail and is the diagonal-sector coefficient of the success POVM introduced in Sec.
II
.

The full state-averaged success probability is obtained by adding the unchanged off-diagonal contribution and weighting the diagonal contribution by the diagonal fraction
1/d
:

P_{\rm f}^{\rm id}(d,r)=1-\frac{1}{d}+\frac{1}{d}\,p_{\rm diag}(d,r)\,.

(37)

At
r=0
,
y=0
and hence
p_{\rm diag}(d,0)=0
, so Eq. (
37
) reduces to the passive value
1-1/d
. For every finite
r>0
, the expression in Eq. (
36
) is strictly positive.

III.3
Optimization and physical scaling

Note that
y\to 1
as
r\to\infty
and therefore
y
never reaches 1 at finite
r
. For optimization over nonzero finite squeezing, we have
0<y<1
.
Figure
2
shows the ideal-PNR performance obtained from Eq. (
37
). The diagonal contribution vanishes at
r=0
and again in the limit
r\rightarrow\infty
, and therefore reaches a maximum at a finite squeezing strength. Physically, squeezing must generate equal-magnitude imbalances in the occupied rail and one partner rail while leaving all remaining rails with zero imbalance.

Since the off-diagonal contribution in
Eq. (
37
) is independent of
r
, maximizing the total success probability is equivalent to maximizing
p_{\rm diag}(d,r)
. Writing

y_{\star}=\tanh^{2}(2r_{\star}),

the unique finite-squeezing optimum is determined by

(d-2)\frac{\mathrm{E}(y_{\star})}{\mathrm{K}(y_{\star})}=d-4+3y_{\star}\,,

(38)

where
\mathrm{E}(y)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sqrt{1-y\sin^{2}\theta}\,d\theta

is the complete elliptic integral of the second kind. The derivation of Eq. (
38
), the uniqueness of its solution, and its asymptotic expansion are given in Appendix
C
.

The optimized success probability
P_{\rm f}^{\rm id}(d,r_{\star})
, with
r_{\star}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{arctanh}\sqrt{y_{\star}}
, is displayed in Fig.

(b). It exceeds the passive value
1-1/d
for every dimension considered. The absolute improvement is largest at small and moderate dimensions. In particular, the success probability increases from
0.75
to
0.7962
for

0.8333
to

for
d=6

At large

\displaystyle y_{\star}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{d}+O(d^{-2}),

(39)

\displaystyle r_{\star}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}+O(d^{-2}),

(40)

\displaystyle p_{\rm diag}(d,r_{\star})

\displaystyle=e^{-1}+O(d^{-1}).

(41)

Thus, the optimized measurement asymptotically recovers a fraction
e^{-1}
of the diagonal failure sector. Since this sector has weight

IV
Finite detector resolution

The ideal-PNR analysis of Sec.
III
assumes lossless detection with unbounded photon-number resolution. We now keep the measurement lossless but impose a finite detector saturation threshold
n_{\rm sat}
: photon numbers
0,1,\ldots,n_{\rm sat}-1
are resolved, while the outcome
\nu=n_{\rm sat}
represents the saturation bin
n\geq n_{\rm sat}
. Because finite resolution does not change the parity of resolved counts, the odd-parity off-diagonal signatures remain disjoint from the all-even diagonal sector within the resolved window. We therefore use a conservative certified rule: any event containing a saturated detector is declared an erasure, and every resolved event is accepted only if it satisfies the same pairwise imbalance criterion as in the ideal-PNR analysis.

For the lossless case, saturation discards high-count events without altering resolved counts. The largest resolved even and odd half-photon indices are

Q_{e}=\left\lfloor\tfrac{1}{2}\left(n_{\rm sat}-1\right)\right\rfloor,\qquad Q_{o}=\left\lfloor\tfrac{1}{2}\left(n_{\rm sat}-2\right)\right\rfloor,

(42)

where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
is the floor function. Thus,

2q<n_{\rm sat}
for
q\leqslant Q_{e}
, while

2q+1<n_{\rm sat}
for
q\leqslant Q_{o}
.

For an off-diagonal PFG outcome to remain within the trusted finite window, the two odd modes must lie inside the resolved odd window, while the remaining
2d-2
even modes must lie inside the resolved even window. The resulting probability is

p_{\rm off}^{(n_{\rm sat})}(d,r)=\left[\sum_{q=0}^{Q_{o}}o_{q}(r)\right]^{2}\left[\sum_{q=0}^{Q_{e}}w_{q}(r)\right]^{2d-2}.

(43)

Here, for
q=0,1,2,\ldots
,

o_{q}(r)=\left|\langle 2q+1|S(r)|1\rangle\right|^{2}=\operatorname{sech}^{3}r\,\frac{(2q+1)!}{2^{2q}\,(q!)^{2}}\tanh^{2q}r,

(44)

is the probability for a squeezed single-photon, as derived in Appendix
A
.

V
Conclusion and outlook

We have shown that local single-mode squeezing can enhance an ancilla-free PFG for
d
-rail photonic qudits without changing the encoding or supplying ancillary input photons. The mechanism uses the structure of the passive PFG: the off-diagonal sector is already converted into pairwise Bell projections, while all passive failures lie in the diagonal sector. In the lossless setting, the squeezing layer leaves the successful off-diagonal signatures intact and converts a certified subset of all-even diagonal outcomes into additional pairwise Bell projections. The accepted diagonal outcomes are characterized by a global rail-imbalance criterion: an all-even pattern is accepted if and only if its imbalance vector has exactly two nonzero components of equal magnitude. This avoids overcounting among the nonorthogonal diagonal pairwise states and yields an exact success POVM with a closed elliptic-integral expression for the recovered diagonal contribution.

The resulting improvement is moderate but systematic. In the ideal-PNR limit, the success probability increases from
75\%
to
79.62\%
for
d=4
, and from
83.33\%
to
87.15\%
for
d=6
. At large
d
, the optimized measurement recovers a fraction
e^{-1}
of the diagonal failure sector, giving an absolute gain
1/(ed)+O(d^{-2})
over the passive PFG. Finite photon-number resolution alone does not remove the advantage: for saturation threshold
n_{\rm sat}=7
, resolving photon numbers
0,\ldots,6
, the certified lossless probabilities remain
78.84\%
for
d=4
and
86.71\%
for
d=6
. These gains require
2d
squeezing operations, phase stability, low insertion loss, and increased PNR dynamic range.

Several extensions are natural. More general Gaussian layers, including passive multimode mixing, rail-dependent squeezing, and optimized phases, may recover a larger part of the structured failure space. The all-even outcomes rejected by the present pairwise criterion may also be useful, since many correspond to known higher-dimensional diagonal superpositions rather than featureless failures. Hybrid squeezing–ancilla PFGs provide another possible route, but their advantage should be established by a PFG-specific optimization rather than by resource counting alone. Beyond the lossless model considered here, realistic imperfections remain crucial: post-squeezer loss can change photon-number parity and mix signatures that are disjoint in the ideal analysis. Quantifying the effects of detector inefficiency, insertion loss, nonuniform squeezing, phase drift, beam-splitter imbalance, mode mismatch, PNR errors, and possible approximate declaration rules is therefore an important next step toward assessing experimental feasibility.

More broadly, this work illustrates a strategy of failure-space engineering: preserve the part of a measurement that already succeeds, and act selectively on the structured support of its failure POVM. Here, high-dimensional encoding supplies a large passively successful sector, while local Gaussian processing converts a rigorously characterized part of the remaining failure space into certified entangling outcomes. The same idea may be useful for other photonic measurements whose failures occupy an identifiable subspace that can be selectively reshaped by accessible optical operations.

This appendix gives the single-mode matrix elements used in Sec.

S(r)=\exp\left[\frac{r}{2}\left(a^{2}-a^{\dagger 2}\right)\right],\qquad r\geq 0.

(48)

With this convention,

\displaystyle S(r)=\exp\left[-\frac{\tanh r}{2}a^{\dagger 2}\right]

(49)

\displaystyle v_{q}(r)\equiv\langle 2q|S(r)|0\rangle=\sqrt{\operatorname{sech}r}\,\frac{\sqrt{(2q)!}}{2^{q}q!}\left[-\tanh r\right]^{q},

(50)

with
q=0,1,2,\ldots
. Taking the modulus square gives

\displaystyle w_{q}(r)

\displaystyle\equiv|v_{q}(r)|^{2}=\operatorname{sech}r\,\frac{(2q)!}{2^{2q}(q!)^{2}}\tanh^{2q}r.

(51)

We also need the two-photon input matrix element

u_{q}(r)\equiv\langle 2q|S(r)|2\rangle.

(52)

The rightmost exponential in Eq. (
49
) gives

\exp\left[\frac{\tanh r}{2}a^{2}\right]|2\rangle=|2\rangle+\frac{\tanh r}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle.

(53)

Substituting this into Eq. (
49
) and projecting onto
|2q\rangle
gives

u_{q}(r)=v_{q}(r)\left[\frac{\tanh r}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{2}q}{\sinh r\cosh r}\right].

(54)

Therefore

R_{q}(r)\equiv\frac{u_{q}(r)}{v_{q}(r)}=\frac{\tanh r}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{2}q}{\sinh r\cosh r}.

(55)

For the finite-resolution analysis in Sec.

o_{q}(r)=\left|\langle 2q+1|S(r)|1\rangle\right|^{2},\qquad q=0,1,2,\ldots,

(56)

\displaystyle\langle 2q+1|S(r)|1\rangle

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sech}^{3/2}r\,\frac{\sqrt{(2q+1)!}}{2^{q}q!}\left[-\tanh r\right]^{q},

Appendix B
Proof of the difference-vector criterion

This appendix proves the difference criterion used in Sec.
III.1
. We work only in the diagonal subspace

\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{span}\left\{|D_{i}\rangle\equiv|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}\right\}_{i=0}^{d-1}.

(58)

For an all-even detection pattern, the main-text derivation gives the restricted Kraus bra

\langle K_{\bm{q}}|=C_{\bm{q}}(r)\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}m_{\ell}\langle D_{\ell}|,\qquad m_{\ell}=q_{\ell}^{c}-q_{\ell}^{d}.

(59)

The scalar
C_{\bm{q}}(r)
fixes the probability scale but not the normalized state selected inside
\mathcal{D}
. Thus the relevant object for Bell-projectability is the integer rail-imbalance vector
\bm{m}=(m_{0},\ldots,m_{d-1})
.

A valid pairwise diagonal Bell projection requires
\langle K_{\bm{q}}|
to be proportional to

\langle\Phi_{ij}^{\pm}|=\frac{\langle D_{i}|\pm\langle D_{j}|}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad i<j.

Equivalently, there must exist a nonzero scalar
\lambda
, a rail pair
i<j
, and a sign
s=\pm 1
such that

C_{\bm{q}}(r)m_{\ell}=\lambda\left(\delta_{\ell i}+s\,\delta_{\ell j}\right)\quad\text{for all }\ell.

(60)

Because the same scalar
C_{\bm{q}}(r)
multiplies every component, Eq. (
60
) can hold only if
\bm{m}
has exactly two nonzero components, on rails
i
and
j
, and those two components have equal magnitude. Thus

\bm{m}=h\bm{e}_{i}+sh\bm{e}_{j},\quad i<j,\quad h\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\quad s=\pm 1.

(61)

Conversely, if Eq. (
61
) holds, then

\langle K_{\bm{q}}|=hC_{\bm{q}}(r)\left(\langle D_{i}|+s\langle D_{j}|\right),

(62)

which is proportional to
\langle\Phi_{ij}^{+}|
for
s=+1
and to
\langle\Phi_{ij}^{-}|
for
s=-1
, up to fixed local phases. This proves necessity and sufficiency.

Absorbing the overall sign of
h
into the irrelevant proportionality constant, the accepted rail-imbalance vectors are therefore

\bm{m}=\pm h\bm{e}_{i}\pm h\bm{e}_{j},\qquad i<j,\qquad h=1,2,\ldots.

(63)

Equal signs select the
+
Bell sign and opposite signs select the
-
Bell sign. All other all-even patterns are rejected by the PFG success rule.

Appendix C
Evaluation and optimization of the diagonal success probability

Starting from the autocorrelation representation in Eq. (
35
), we first establish its absolute convergence and then reduce it in terms of complete elliptic integrals. Throughout this appendix, we use

x=\tanh^{2}r,\qquad 0\leqslant x<1.

Since

\frac{(2q)!}{2^{2q}(q!)^{2}}=\frac{1}{4^{q}}\binom{2q}{q}\leqslant slant1,

and
\operatorname{sech}r=\sqrt{1-x}
, Eq. (
31
) implies

w_{q}(r)\leqslant\sqrt{1-x}\,x^{q}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle Z_{h}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}w_{q+h}\,w_{q}\leqslant(1-x)\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}x^{q+h}x^{q}=\frac{x^{h}}{1+x}.

Consequently,

\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{2}Z_{h}(r)^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{(1+x)^{2}}\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{2}x^{2h}

Now, for
|x|<1
, it can be shown that

\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{2}x^{2h}=\frac{x^{2}(1+x^{2})}{(1-x^{2})^{3}}.

Substituting this, we obtain

\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{2}Z_{h}(r)^{2}\leqslant\frac{x^{2}(1+x^{2})}{(1+x)^{2}(1-x^{2})^{3}}<\infty

for
0\leqslant x<1
, equivalently for every finite
r
. This bound is used only to establish absolute convergence and to justify the Fourier manipulations below; the exact value of the weighted autocorrelation sum is derived in Eq. (
74
).

C.1
Reduction to an elliptic-integral expression

With
x=\tanh^{2}r
, the squeezed-vacuum probabilities can be written as

w_{q}(r)=\sqrt{1-x}\,\frac{1}{4^{q}}\binom{2q}{q}x^{q}.

(64)

Using the binomial series identity

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u}}=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^{q}}\binom{2q}{q}u^{q},\qquad|u|<1,

the generating function of the weights is

W_{x}(z)\equiv\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}w_{q}(r)z^{q}=\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{1-xz}}.

(65)

On the unit circle, define

\displaystyle F_{x}(\theta)

\displaystyle=W_{x}(e^{i\theta})W_{x}(e^{-i\theta})=\frac{1-x}{\sqrt{1-2x\cos\theta+x^{2}}}.

(66)

On the other hand, expanding the product of generating functions gives

\displaystyle F_{x}(\theta)=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}w_{m}(r)w_{n}(r)e^{i(m-n)\theta}.

(67)

Now group terms by the difference
h=m-n
. For
h=0
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n}^{2}(r)=Z_{0}(r).

For
h>0
, set
m=n+h
. Then the
+h
terms give

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n+h}(r)w_{n}(r)e^{ih\theta}=Z_{h}(r)e^{ih\theta},

and similarly the
-h
terms give
Z_{h}(r)e^{-ih\theta}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle F_{x}(\theta)=Z_{0}(r)+2\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}Z_{h}(r)\cos(h\theta),

(68)

so the autocorrelations
Z_{h}(r)
are the Fourier coefficients of

F_{x}(\theta)
.

The zero-imbalance coefficient is

\displaystyle Z_{0}(r)

\displaystyle=(1-x)\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{4^{q}}\binom{2q}{q}\right]^{2}x^{2q}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(1-x)}{\pi}\mathrm{K}(x^{2}),

(69)

where we used the standard series representation of the complete elliptic integral of the first kind,

\mathrm{K}(m)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1-m\sin^{2}\theta}}=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{4^{q}}\binom{2q}{q}\right]^{2}m^{q}.

(70)

Here
m
denotes the elliptic-integral parameter.

The weighted autocorrelation sum can be evaluated using Parseval’s
identity. Differentiating Eq. (
68
) gives

F_{x}^{\prime}(\theta)=-2\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h\,Z_{h}(r)\sin(h\theta),

and hence

\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{2}Z_{h}(r)^{2}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|F_{x}^{\prime}(\theta)\right|^{2}d\theta.

(71)

From Eq. (
66
),

F_{x}^{\prime}(\theta)=-\frac{x(1-x)\sin\theta}{\left(1-2x\cos\theta+x^{2}\right)^{3/2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{2}Z_{h}(r)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}(1-x)^{2}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\!\frac{\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta}{\left(1-2x\cos\theta+x^{2}\right)^{3}}.

(72)

For completeness, introduce

J(a,b)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a-b\cos\theta}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2}}},\qquad a>|b|.

Since

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta}{(a-b\cos\theta)^{3}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}J}{\partial a^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}J}{\partial b^{2}}\right),

setting
a=1+x^{2}
and
b=2x
gives

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta}{\left(1-2x\cos\theta+x^{2}\right)^{3}}=\frac{\pi}{(1-x^{2})^{3}}.

(73)

Substitution into Eq. (
72
) yields

\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{2}Z_{h}(r)^{2}=\frac{x^{2}}{4(1-x)(1+x)^{3}}.

(74)

Using

\sinh^{2}r\cosh^{2}r=\frac{x}{(1-x)^{2}},

Eqs. (
35
),
(
69
), and
(
74
) give

p_{\rm diag}(d,r)=(d-1)\frac{x(1-x)}{(1+x)^{3}}\left[\frac{2(1-x)}{\pi}\mathrm{K}(x^{2})\right]^{d-2}.

(75)

We now introduce

y=\frac{4x}{(1+x)^{2}}=\tanh^{2}(2r).

(76)

The quadratic transformation

\mathrm{K}(x^{2})=\frac{1}{1+x}\mathrm{K}\left(\frac{4x}{(1+x)^{2}}\right)

and the identity

1-y=\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)^{2}

reduce Eq. (
75
) to

p_{\rm diag}(d,r)=\frac{d-1}{4}\,y(1-y)^{(d-1)/2}\left[\frac{2\mathrm{K}(y)}{\pi}\right]^{d-2},

(77)

which is Eq. (
36
) of the main text.

At
r=0
, one has
y=0
and hence

p_{\rm diag}(d,0)=0
. For every finite
r>0
,

0<y<1
, so Eq. (
77
) is strictly positive.
In the opposite limit
r\rightarrow\infty
,
y\rightarrow 1
, and
the algebraic factor
(1-y)^{(d-1)/2}
dominates the logarithmic
divergence of
\mathrm{K}(y)^{d-2}
, giving

p_{\rm diag}(d,r)\rightarrow 0
.

C.2
Exact optimization condition

Because
y=\tanh^{2}(2r)
is strictly increasing for
r\geq 0
,
maximizing
p_{\rm diag}(d,r)
over
r
is equivalent to maximizing
Eq. (
77
) over
0<y<1
. Its logarithmic
derivative is

\frac{d}{dy}\ln p_{\rm diag}=\frac{1}{y}-\frac{d-1}{2(1-y)}+(d-2)\frac{\mathrm{K}^{\prime}(y)}{\mathrm{K}(y)}.

(78)

Introduce the complete elliptic integral of the second kind,

\mathrm{E}(y)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sqrt{1-y\sin^{2}\theta}\,d\theta.

(79)

Using

\mathrm{K}^{\prime}(y)=\frac{\mathrm{E}(y)-(1-y)\mathrm{K}(y)}{2y(1-y)},

(80)

the stationary condition

d\ln p_{\rm diag}/dy=0
becomes

(d-2)\frac{\mathrm{E}(y_{\star})}{\mathrm{K}(y_{\star})}=d-4+3y_{\star},\quad r_{\star}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{arctanh}\sqrt{y_{\star}},

(81)

which is Eq. (
38
) of the main text.

The solution of Eq. (
81
) is unique. To
see this, define

\displaystyle R(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{E}(y)}{\mathrm{K}(y)},

(82)

\displaystyle G_{d}(y)

\displaystyle=(d-2)R(y)-d+4-3y.

(83)

Differentiating
R(y)
gives

R^{\prime}(y)=-\frac{\left[R(y)-(1-y)\right]^{2}+y(1-y)}{2y(1-y)}<0

(84)

for
0<y<1
. Hence

G_{d}^{\prime}(y)=(d-2)R^{\prime}(y)-3<0.

Moreover,

\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}G_{d}(y)=2,\qquad\lim_{y\to 1^{-}}G_{d}(y)=1-d<0.

Thus,
G_{d}(y)
has exactly one zero in
(0,1)
. Since

p_{\rm diag}
vanishes at both endpoints and is positive in the interval, this zero gives the unique global maximum.

Example cases

For
d=2
, Eq. (
81
) reduces to

-2+3y_{\star}=0,

so

y_{\star}=\frac{2}{3},\qquad r_{\star}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{arctanh}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}.

(85)

Equation (
77
) then gives the exact analytical expression

p_{\rm diag}(2,r_{\star})=\frac{1}{6\sqrt{3}}.

Thus

P_{\rm f}^{\rm id}(2,r_{\star})=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{12\sqrt{3}}=0.5481125\ldots.

(86)

For
d=4
, Eq. (
77
) becomes

p_{\rm diag}(4,r)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\,y(1-y)^{3/2}\,\mathrm{K}^{2}(y).

(87)

The stationarity condition in Eq. (
81
)
reduces to
2\,\frac{\mathrm{E}(y_{\star})}{\mathrm{K}(y_{\star})}=3y_{\star}
,
or equivalently
2\mathrm{E}(y_{\star})=3y_{\star}\mathrm{K}(y_{\star})
.
This equation has a unique solution in
0<y_{\star}<1
, which we compute numerically. We obtain

y_{\star}=0.489762\ldots,\quad r_{\star}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{arctanh}\sqrt{y_{\star}}=0.433483.

At this point,

\displaystyle p_{\rm diag}(4,r_{\star})

\displaystyle=0.184796,

\displaystyle P_{\rm f}^{\rm id}(4,r_{\star})

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4}p_{\rm diag}(4,r_{\star})=0.796199.

(88)

C.3
Large-
d
expansion

For small
y
,

\frac{\mathrm{E}(y)}{\mathrm{K}(y)}=1-\frac{y}{2}-\frac{y^{2}}{16}-\frac{y^{3}}{32}+O(y^{4}).

(89)

Substituting the ansatz

y_{\star}=\frac{a_{1}}{d}+\frac{a_{2}}{d^{2}}+\frac{a_{3}}{d^{3}}+O(d^{-4})

into Eq. (
81
) and matching successive
powers of
1/d
gives

y_{\star}=\frac{4}{d}-\frac{18}{d^{2}}+\frac{90}{d^{3}}+O(d^{-4}).

(90)

Since

r_{\star}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{arctanh}\sqrt{y_{\star}}
,
this implies

r_{\star}^{2}=\frac{1}{d}-\frac{11}{6d^{2}}+O(d^{-3}).

(91)

Finally, using

\ln\left[\frac{2\mathrm{K}(y)}{\pi}\right]=\frac{y}{4}+\frac{7y^{2}}{64}+O(y^{3})

in Eq. (
77
) gives

p_{\rm diag}(d,r_{\star})=e^{-1}\left[1-\frac{13}{4d}+O(d^{-2})\right].

(92)

The corresponding gain over the passive PFG is

\Delta P_{\rm f}^{\rm id}(d)=\frac{p_{\rm diag}(d,r_{\star})}{d}=\frac{1}{ed}\left[1-\frac{13}{4d}+O(d^{-2})\right].
