Genome-wide identification of the WRKY gene family in apricot (Prunus armeniaca L.) and functional characterization of PaWRKY57 in salt stress tolerance
Authors: Ying Li, Xueping Han, Huajun Gao, Jingyuan Yang, Qinglin Niu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSoil salinization severely restricts the growth, yield, and industrial development of apricot (Prunus armeniaca L.). WRKY transcription factors play crucial roles in plant responses to abiotic stresses. However, the genome-wide characteristics of the WRKY family in apricot and their functional mechanisms in salt tolerance remain unclear. In this study, we identified 58 PaWRKY genes in apricot and characterized their phylogeny, chromosomal distribution, and gene structure. Based on transcriptomic and phylogenetic analyses, PaWRKY57 (homologous to AtWRKY33) was selected as a key salt-responsive candidate gene. Heterologous overexpression of PaWRKY57 in tobacco significantly enhanced salt tolerance; compared with the wild type, the transgenic lines exhibited a higher germination rate, better growth phenotype, lower accumulation of malondialdehyde (MDA) and reactive oxygen species (ROS), higher proline content, and stronger activities of antioxidant enzymes (SOD, POD, and APX). Protein-protein interaction prediction showed PaWRKY57 interacts with MPK3/4/6 and other stress-related proteins, and GO enrichment implicated it in osmotic stress and protein phosphorylation. This study provides preliminary genetic resources and a theoretical basis for research on the molecular mechanism of salt tolerance and stress-resistant molecular breeding in apricot.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-09618-z.Keywords: Apricot (Prunus armeniaca L.), WRKY transcription factor, PaWRKY57, Salt stress

Introduction

Apricot (Prunus armeniaca L.), a stone fruit tree of significant economic value within the Rosaceae family, is recognized for its high edibility, economic profitability, and potential for industrial applications. It is widely cultivated in regions possessing favorable climatic and edaphic conditions globally, serving as a pivotal species in the fruit tree industry [1, 2]. However, the growth, yield, and quality of apricot production are frequently constrained by abiotic stress factors, particularly drought, elevated temperatures, and soil salinization. Among these, soil salinization, exacerbated by the expansion of saline-alkali land, has become a major environmental limiting factor restricting apricot cultivation and sustainable production [3, 4]. Therefore, systematic dissection of the molecular mechanisms underlying apricot salt stress responses is essential for breeding salt-tolerant cultivars and the sustainable development of the apricot industry.

Transcription factors serve as crucial molecular regulators within the complex networks governing plant stress responses. They perceive upstream stress signals and orchestrate the expression of numerous downstream functional genes, thereby enabling precise and efficient adaptive mechanisms to both abiotic and biotic stressors [5]. The WRKY transcription factor family, a large and plant-specific group, is distinguished by a highly conserved WRKYGQK heptapeptide sequence alongside a zinc-finger motif. These structural elements constitute the WRKY domain, which specifically recognizes and binds to W-box cis-elements in the promoter regions of target genes, activating or repressing their transcription [6, 7]. Since the first WRKY gene was identified, extensive studies have demonstrated the family’s broad involvement in regulating fundamental physiological processes, including growth, development, and secondary metabolism. Notably, WRKY proteins are integral to plant defense responses against both biotic stresses, such as pathogen invasion, and abiotic stresses, including drought, salinity, and extreme temperature conditions [8]. In Arabidopsis thaliana, AtWRKY33 acts as a key regulatory hub integrating salt stress and pathogen resistance pathways. It substantially enhances plant tolerance to salt stress and overall stress resilience by mediating hormone signaling pathways (including jasmonic acid and ethylene) and controlling the transcription of reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging genes [9–11]. This functional characterization provides a valuable framework for exploring the roles of WRKY genes in conferring salt tolerance in fruit tree species.

To date, following the successive completion of genome sequencing in various plant species such as A. thaliana [12], Oryza sativa [13], and Malus domestica [14], genome-wide identification, classification, and bioinformatics analyses of the WRKY transcription factor family have become foundational and standard approaches for elucidating their functional characteristics and evolutionary patterns. Significant advancements in this field have highlighted the pivotal regulatory roles of WRKY family members in stress response mechanisms across a broad range of species. Presently, the WRKY family has been extensively identified and functionally characterized in model plants (e.g., A. thaliana, O. sativa) [15, 16] as well as in several economically important fruit trees, including apple, peach (Prunus persica), and Citrus reticulata [14, 17, 18]. Their essential involvement in regulating plant growth, development, secondary metabolism, and responses to biotic and abiotic stresses is well documented. Recently, the WRKY gene family in apricot has been identified, and some members have been implicated in responses to drought stress [19]. Nevertheless, systematic research focusing on apricot WRKYs under salt stress, together with in vivo functional validation of salt-responsive PaWRKY genes, remains lacking. Identification and functional validation of key salt-responsive WRKY genes in apricot are urgently needed. This knowledge gap hinders the elucidation of WRKY-mediated stress adaptation mechanisms and impedes the genetic improvement of salt tolerance in apricot.

In this study, a comprehensive genome-wide identification and analysis of the WRKY transcription factor family in apricot was conducted. Employing bioinformatics approaches, we thoroughly examined the physicochemical properties, chromosomal distribution, phylogenetic relationships, gene structural features, and conserved motif patterns of the family members. Through integration of transcriptomic data and phylogenetic analysis, PaWRKY57—a homolog of the A. thaliana core salt stress-responsive transcription factor AtWRKY33—was identified as a principal candidate gene. This study aims to characterize the apricot WRKY family and reveal the regulatory function of PaWRKY57 in salt stress response. These findings offer a theoretical framework and valuable genetic resources for elucidating salt tolerance mechanisms in apricot.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Four-year-old salt-tolerant elite apricot (P. armeniaca ‘Jintaiyang’ × ‘Zhenzhuyouxing’) plants were grown at the Wanjishan Experimental Demonstration Base of the Shandong Institute of Pomology. According to preliminary experiments and previous studies [20, 21], healthy annual shoots were treated with distilled water (control) or 500 mmol L-¹ NaCl for 12 h. Leaves were harvested for transcriptome sequencing and named H12-WT (control) and H12-5‰ (NaCl treatment).

Nicotiana benthamiana is maintained in this laboratory and grown at 25 °C, with 65% relative humidity, under a 16 h light / 8 h dark photoperiod.

Transcriptome sequencing

Transcriptome libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform to generate 150 bp paired-end reads. Three biological replicates were set for each treatment group, named H12-WT-1, H12-WT-2, H12-WT-3, H12-5‰-1, H12-5‰-2 and H12-5‰-3. Raw reads in fastq format were filtered using fastp software, and clean reads were obtained after removing low-quality reads for subsequent analysis. The HISAT2 software was applied to align clean reads against the reference genome, and gene expression levels were calculated based on FPKM values. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened via DESeq2. Genes with q-value < 0.05 and fold change > 2 or fold change < 0.5 were identified as DEGs.

WRKY gene family analysis

The apricot genome data (P. armeniaca Genome v1.0) was downloaded from the Genome Database for Rosaceae (GDR, https://www.rosaceae.org/). A. thaliana genome data and WRKY protein sequences were obtained from the TAIR database (https://www.arabidopsis.org/). The hidden Markov model (HMM) profile of the WRKY domain was built using HMM Build, and homologous proteins were identified by searching the apricot protein database with HMM Search.

Physicochemical properties of PaWRKY proteins were predicted using the ExPASy online tool (https://web.expasy.org). Chromosomal locations, phylogeny, gene structures, and conserved motifs were analyzed using TBtools [22]. Phylogenetic tree was visualized using the iTOL platform (https://itol.embl.de/help.cgi).

Salt stress treatment of transgenic tobacco

Wild-type (WT) and transgenic tobacco plants with consistent growth were exposed to 100 mmol L−¹ or 200 mmol L−¹ NaCl for 14 days, after which physiological parameters were assessed.

Seed Germination Rate: Healthy, full tobacco seeds (50 seeds per group) were planted on MS medium containing 0 mmol L−¹ (control), 100 mmol L−¹, or 200 mmol L−¹ NaCl. Germinated seeds were recorded at 5 days after sowing, and germination rate was calculated.

Phenotypic observation: WT and transgenic plants were irrigated with 200 mmol L−¹ NaCl solution. Growth status was observed and photographed after 7 days of treatment.

Physiological index measurements

Malondialdehyde (MDA): Measured by the thiobarbituric acid (TBA) method.

Proline: Measured by the acidic ninhydrin colorimetric method.

Hydrogen Peroxide (H₂O₂): Measured by the titanium complex colorimetric method.

Superoxide Anion (O₂⁻) Production Rate: Measured by the hydroxylamine oxidation method.

Peroxidase (POD): Measured by the guaiacol method.

Superoxide Dismutase (SOD): Measured by the NBT photoreduction method.

Ascorbate Peroxidase (APX): Measured by the UV spectrophotometric method.

Protein-protein interaction network and GO enrichment analysis

The online platform STRING (https://string-db.org) was utilized to predict proteins that interact with PaWRKY57, and the resulting interaction network was visualized and refined using Cytoscape.

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of the interacting proteins was performed using Metascape (https://metascape.org).

Data analysis

Nine candidate genes (PARG00404, PARG12518, PARG18989, PARG16720, PARG27085, PARG27849, PARG02893, PARG18979, PARG21633) were chosen to perform RT-qPCR verification of transcriptome data. RT-qPCR primers were designed using NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast) and listed in Supplementary Table 1. The reaction procedure was as follows: 95℃ 30 s; 95℃ 5 s, 56℃ 34 s, 95℃ 15 s, 40 cycles; 60℃ 1 min, 95℃ 30 s. Relative gene expression was quantified based on the 2 −ΔΔCt calculation approach. Fold changes of candidate genes from transcriptome and RT-qPCR were transformed into log2FC. Paired log2FC values were subjected to simple linear regression analysis. The regression equation and coefficient of determination (R2) were applied to evaluate the concordance between two datasets and verify the reliability of RNA-seq data.

All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation from three biological replicates. GraphPad Prism 9.5 was used for graph plotting and statistical analysis. Significant differences were evaluated using Student’s t-test. * indicates significant difference at P < 0.05, and ** indicates highly significant difference at P < 0.01.

Results

Transcriptome analysis and screening of salt-responsive WRKY genes in apricot

Transcriptome sequencing yielded a total of 312.84 million raw reads. Each sample exhibited a Q30 score exceeding 93%, with GC content ranging from 45% to 47%, indicating high sequencing quality. A total of 6,092 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between the salt stress treatment and control groups, including 2,535 upregulated and 3,557 downregulated genes (Fig. 1A).

Identification and functional enrichment analysis of DEGs in apricot leaves under salt stress. Note: (A) Volcano plot of DEGs, (B) GO analysis of DEGs, (C) KEGG analysis of DEGs

GO analysis (Fig. 1B) classified DEGs into biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). Upregulated DEGs were mainly enriched in protein phosphorylation, lipoxygenase biosynthesis, and response to hypoxia (BP); plasma membrane, membrane components, and peroxisome (CC); and protein serine/threonine kinase activity and UDP-glucosyltransferase activity (MF). Downregulated DEGs were mainly associated with photosynthesis, chloroplast, thylakoid membrane, and chlorophyll binding. Notably, 67 upregulated DEGs were enriched in “response to abscisic acid”, a key pathway for salt tolerance.

KEGG analysis (Fig. 1C) showed that upregulated DEGs were mainly enriched in the plant MAPK signaling pathway, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism, and galactose metabolism. In contrast, downregulated DEGs were markedly enriched in photosynthesis and carbon fixation.

To identify candidate genes potentially responsible for salt tolerance, we conducted GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of all differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Based on these enrichment results, seven significantly upregulated candidate genes (PARG00404, PARG12518, PARG18989, PARG19646, PARG27085, PARG27849, PARG29259) were selected for subsequent functional characterization. Among them, PARG00404 and PARG29259 belong to the WRKY transcription factor family and were prioritized for subsequent family-specific analysis.

To verify the reliability of the RNA-Seq data, nine DEGs were randomly selected for RT-qPCR validation (Fig. 2A). Among them, four (PARG00404, PARG12518, PARG18989, and PARG27849) overlapped with the seven functionally selected candidates, and the remaining five were randomly chosen to ensure unbiased representation. Simple linear regression was performed using paired log2FC values from RNA-seq and RT-qPCR (Fig. 2B). The two datasets displayed a significant positive linear correlation with a high coefficient of determination, demonstrating excellent consistency in expression alteration trends and validating the credibility of transcriptomic results.

RT-qPCR validation of differentially expressed genes. Note: (A) RT-qPCR analysis of genes, (B) Linear regression of RNA-Seq and RT-qPCR data

Genome-wide identification of the WRKY gene family in apricot and selection of PaWRKY57

A total of 58 WRKY gene family members were identified in the apricot genome and named PaWRKY01 to PaWRKY58 according to their chromosomal locations (Fig. 3). Physicochemical analysis (Supplementary Table 2) showed that the predicted PaWRKY proteins varied considerably in length (162–1085 amino acids), molecular weight (18.76-120.01 kDa), and isoelectric point (pI 18.76-120.01). Most were unstable proteins (instability index 39.97–66.97) and hydrophilic (negative GRAVY), consistent with WRKY properties in other fruit trees.

Chromosomal distribution of WRKY gene family members in apricot. Note: Each vertical bar represents an individual chromosome, with corresponding chromosome numbers labeled on the left side

To elucidate the evolutionary relationships within the apricot WRKY family, a phylogenetic tree was constructed using WRKY protein sequences from both apricot and A. thaliana (Fig. 4). This analysis classified the WRKY family into seven subfamilies. Groups I and II-c were the most populous, each comprising 13 members, whereas Group II-a was the smallest, containing only two members. Groups II-b, III, II-d, and II-e included eight, eight, seven, and seven members, respectively. Further examination of conserved motifs and gene structural features (Fig. 5) demonstrated that members within the same subfamily exhibited similar exon-intron arrangement and conserved motif distribution patterns, while these characteristics varied significantly across different subfamilies.

Phylogenetic tree of WRKY genes from apricot and A. thaliana. Note: The outer colored arcs denote distinct subfamilies; red dots on the inner circle represent apricot genes, while blue triangles indicate A. thaliana genes

Analysis of conserved motifs and gene structures of WRKY gene family members in apricot. Note: (A) ML phylogenetic tree of apricot WRKY genes; (B) Conserved motifs of genes. Different colors represent distinct conserved motifs (motif 1-motif 10); (C) Intron/exon structure of genes. Green boxes indicate UTRs, yellow boxes indicate exons, and gray lines indicate introns

Phylogenetic analysis showed that PaWRKY57 (PARG29259) clustered closely with AtWRKY33, a well-studied gene known to participate in salt stress responses in A. thaliana. WRKY33 homologs have been widely reported to regulate plant salt stress tolerance [9, 23]. Two candidate genes, PaWRKY25 (PARG00404) and PaWRKY57, were initially identified via transcriptome sequencing for subsequent research. RT-qPCR assays demonstrated that the transcript level of PaWRKY25 was increased by 15.4-fold, while PaWRKY57 was upregulated by 22.1-fold under salt treatment (Fig. 6). Combining the phylogenetic relationship and expression profiles, PaWRKY57 was therefore chosen for subsequent functional characterization to clarify its role in salt stress response in apricot.

Subcellular localization of PaWRKY57

The PaWRKY57-GFP fusion protein was specifically localized in the nucleus, whereas the control GFP signal was distributed ubiquitously in the whole cell (Fig. 7). This confirms that PaWRKY57 is a nuclear-localized transcription factor [24], consistent with its regulatory function.

Overexpression of PaWRKY57 enhances salt tolerance in transgenic tobacco

Fifteen T0 transgenic tobacco lines were obtained by resistance screening and PCR identification. RT-qPCR of T2 lines identified two high-expression lines, designated GMO-7 and GMO-9, which were used for subsequent experiments (Fig. 8A).

Identification of transgenic tobacco and phenotypic analysis under salt stress. Note: (A) Relative expression levels of PaWRKY57 in transgenic tobacco lines; (B) Seed germination rates under salt stress; (C) Plant phenotypes after salt treatment

To evaluate the salt tolerance of the transgenic tobacco, phenotypic responses under salt stress conditions were examined. Seed germination assays were conducted on MS medium supplemented with 0, 100, or 200 mmol L−¹ NaCl, with germination rates recorded on the fifth day. Results indicated a decline in germination rates across all seeds as NaCl concentration increased (Fig. 8B). Under control conditions, no significant differences in germination rates were observed between transgenic and WT seeds. However, under salt stress, both PaWRKY57-overexpressing lines demonstrated significantly higher germination rates compared to WT. Furthermore, plants were subjected to irrigation with 200 mmol L−¹ NaCl solution (Fig. 8C). After seven days of treatment, WT plants exhibited pronounced leaf wilting and chlorosis, whereas transgenic plants maintained predominantly green foliage and exhibited robust growth.

Role of PaWRKY57 in modulating physiological responses under salt stress

MDA serves as a critical marker for membrane lipid peroxidation, and proline acts as an important osmoprotectant in plant adaptation to stress. Changes in the levels of these compounds reflect the severity of cellular damage and the plant’s stress tolerance capacity [25].Under salt stress, MDA content increased in all tobacco plants but was lower in transgenic lines than in WT. Notably, under 200 mmol L−¹ NaCl, the MDA concentration in the GMO-7 transgenic line was reduced compared to the WT, suggesting that overexpression of PaWRKY57 may alleviates oxidative damage to cellular membranes (Fig. 9A). Proline accumulation also rose with increasing salt concentrations in all plants, however, the GMO-7 line demonstrated higher proline levels than WT under both 100 mmol L−¹ and 200 mmol L−¹ NaCl treatments, indicating that the transgenic plants possess certain advantages in osmotic adjustment capacity (Fig. 9B).

Analysis of physiological parameter alterations in tobacco subjected to salt stress. Note: (A) MDA content changes; (B) Proline content changes; (C) H₂O₂ content changes; (D) O₂⁻ production rate changes; (E) POD activity changes; (F) SOD activity changes; (G) APX activity changes.

Salt stress induced significant increases in both H₂O₂ content and O₂⁻ production rates in WT and transgenic plants. Nevertheless, H₂O₂ accumulation was markedly lower in the transgenic lines compared to WT under salt stress conditions (Fig. 9C). Similarly, the rate of O₂⁻ production was significantly suppressed in transgenic tobacco at all tested salt concentrations (Fig. 9D). These findings suggest that the transgenic plants possess a more effective mechanism for reducing ROS accumulation under saline stress.

Following exposure to salt stress, the activities of the antioxidant enzymes POD, SOD, and APX increased in both WT and transgenic plants in response to elevated NaCl concentrations. Compared to WT, transgenic plants exhibited significantly higher activities of these enzymes under salt stress. Specifically, POD activity in the GMO-7 line was significantly higher than that in the WT under 100 mmol L−¹ NaCl (Fig. 9E). Furthermore, activities of SOD (Fig. 9F) and APX (Fig. 9G) were significantly greater in the transgenic lines than in WT under both salt stress conditions. Collectively, these results preliminarily indicate that overexpression of PaWRKY57 may enhance physiological adaptation to salt-induced oxidative stress, at least partially through the upregulation of key antioxidant enzyme activities.

Protein-protein interaction network and GO enrichment analysis of the PaWRKY57

To explore the potential molecular mechanisms underlying PaWRKY57 function, a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed via the STRING database based on sequence homology and conserved structural domains. This in silico prediction yielded ten putative candidate interactors of PaWRKY57: ATG18A, MED16, MPK4, SIB2, WRKY25, MKS1, MPK6, SIB1, CYP71B15, and MPK3 (Fig. 10A). To obtain preliminary transcriptional clues supporting the potential functional coordination of these predicted interactors, RT-qPCR was conducted to profile the expression patterns of PaWRKY57 and the ten candidate partners under salt stress (Fig. 10B). All ten predicted candidate interactors and PaWRKY57 were significantly upregulated under salt stress. Their synchronous transcriptional response suggests they may operate within a common salt stress regulatory network. However, direct physical binding between these proteins requires further experimental validation. These candidates are prioritized for follow-up biochemical verification and functional characterization.

Analysis of PaWRKY57 interacting proteins. Note: (A) PPI network of PaWRKY57; (B) Expression pattern analysis of interacting proteins; (C) GO enrichment analysis of PaWRKY57-interacting proteins

Subsequent GO enrichment analysis of the interacting proteins identified four significantly enriched terms, which covered biological processes (BP) and molecular functions (MF) (Fig. 10C). Within the BP category, terms including camalexin biosynthetic process, response to osmotic stress, and regulation of response to stress were prominently enriched. The camalexin biosynthetic process is intimately linked to the production of phytoalexins during biotic and abiotic stress responses, whereas the response to osmotic stress is directly related to cellular dehydration induced by salt stress. In the MF category, protein kinase activity was significantly enriched, which is consistent with the presence of multiple MPKs in the interaction network; this further reinforces the involvement of this network in stress signal perception and transduction.

Discussion

Soil salinization is a major global threat to agriculture and ecology, markedly limiting fruit tree growth and the sustainable development of the fruit industry. As an important economic fruit tree, apricot requires the identification of salt-tolerance genes and dissection of underlying molecular mechanisms to alleviate salt stress and promote high-quality development of the apricot industry [26, 27]. Transcription factors are core regulators of plant stress responses, and the WRKY family attracts extensive attention for its roles in stress resistance, growth, development, and secondary metabolism [28, 29]. Although genome-wide identification and functional characterization of WRKY family members have been conducted in various fruit trees, including apple, peach, and citrus [17, 18, 30], comprehensive studies focusing on this family in apricot remain absent, thereby constraining progress in molecular breeding aimed at enhancing salt tolerance in this species.

This study presents a systematic genome-wide analysis of the WRKY family in apricot, identifying 58 PaWRKY genes (designated PaWRKY01 to PaWRKY58). This number is closely comparable to the WRKY family sizes reported in phylogenetically related stone fruit species, such as peach (58 members) and Prunus salicina (55 members) [17, 31]. This similarity suggests a relatively conserved family size within the genus Prunus (Rosaceae) and implies the absence of significant gene expansion or loss events during its evolutionary history [32]. PaWRKY proteins showed wide variation in sequence length, molecular weight, and isoelectric point; most were unstable and hydrophilic, similar to those reported for apple and Pyrus spp WRKYs [33, 34]. These findings suggest that PaWRKY proteins may participate in intracellular signal transduction and protein-protein interactions, relying on their hydrophilic characteristics. Their inherent instability could be associated with rapid degradation and dynamic regulatory processes under stress conditions [35, 36].

Phylogenetic analysis categorized the 58 PaWRKY members into seven distinct subfamilies. Among these, Group I and Group II-c were the most populous, each comprising 13 members, whereas Group II-a was the smallest, containing only two members. This classification is consistent with the well-established categorization criteria of the WRKY family identified in A. thaliana, rice, and other fruit tree species [37, 38], thereby reinforcing the evolutionary conservation of the WRKY family. Members within the same subfamily exhibited similar gene structures and conserved motif patterns, which are closely associated with the functional preservation of WRKY proteins. Notably, conserved motifs—particularly the WRKYGQK heptapeptide sequence and the zinc-finger domain—constitute the critical regions enabling WRKY proteins to specifically bind to the W-box cis-acting elements located in the promoters of target genes, thereby facilitating transcriptional regulation [39, 40]. Previous studies have demonstrated that WRKY members from the same subfamily often share analogous roles in plant stress responses. For instance, most Group I members participate in plant responses to abiotic stresses such as salinity and drought [41, 42]. Comparative functional analysis of homologous genes across species revealed that WRKY33 homologs from different Rosaceae tree species exhibit both functional conservation and distinct species-specific regulatory divergence. The peach WRKY33 homolog is primarily involved in the regulation of cold stress response [43], while its apple counterpart predominantly participates in defense against fungal diseases [33].

In the present study, Group I contained 13 PaWRKY members, which provides valuable clues for the targeted identification of WRKY genes associated with other abiotic stress responses. Of particular interest, PaWRKY57 clustered closely with AtWRKY33, a key gene involved in the salt stress response in A. thaliana. AtWRKY33 has been shown to significantly enhance plant salt tolerance by modulating hormone signaling pathways and ROS scavenging mechanisms [23, 44]. Given that PaWRKY57 (PARG29259) was markedly upregulated under salt stress conditions in transcriptomic analyses, it is hypothesized that PaWRKY57 may perform a comparable regulatory function in enhancing salt tolerance, analogous to AtWRKY33.

Subcellular localization confirmed that PaWRKY57 is specifically localized in the nucleus, consistent with the nuclear localization of transcription factors [45–48] and salt-responsive WRKYs such as MdWRKY55 (apple) and FvWRKY75 (Fragaria × ananassa) [49, 50]. This further substantiates the hypothesis that PaWRKY57 participates in the salt stress response by regulating the expression of downstream genes associated with salt tolerance within the nucleus. Functional analysis in transgenic tobacco demonstrated that PaWRKY57 overexpression significantly improves seed germination under salt stress. Additionally, transgenic plants displayed reduced wilting and chlorosis, confirming that PaWRKY57 markedly enhances salt tolerance in transgenic tobacco. This functional role is conserved among homologous WRKY33 genes implicated in salt tolerance across species such as A. thaliana and rice [9, 23]. For instance, overexpression of MsWRKY33 improves alfalfa growth under salt stress [51], while WRKY33 overexpression enhances rice growth performance under similar conditions [23], collectively reinforcing the conserved regulatory function of WRKY33 homologs in salt stress tolerance.

Physiological analysis showed that PaWRKY57 regulates salt tolerance through antioxidant defense systems and osmotic balance regulation. Under salt stress conditions, MDA levels in PaWRKY57-overexpressing lines were significantly reduced compared to wild-type plants, suggesting mitigated membrane lipid peroxidation and preserved cell membrane integrity [52, 53]. Concurrently, proline content was markedly elevated in the overexpression lines relative to WT, indicating enhanced maintenance of cellular osmotic pressure and alleviation of salt-induced damage [54, 55]. This regulatory pattern parallels findings reported for Arachis hypogaea AhWRKY75 and Ipomoea batatas IbWRKY2 [56, 57]. Given that excessive accumulation of ROS under salt stress can inflict cellular damage, plants typically counteract this by activating antioxidant enzymes such as SOD, POD, and APX [58, 59]. In the present study, PaWRKY57-overexpressing tobacco exhibited significantly decreased H₂O₂ content and O₂⁻ production rates, alongside a notable increase in the activities of the aforementioned antioxidant enzymes. These results indicate that PaWRKY57 mitigates ROS accumulation and oxidative stress by enhancing antioxidant enzyme activity. This mechanism aligns with previous reports demonstrating that WRKY transcription factors confer salt tolerance through the regulation of antioxidant enzyme gene expression [60, 61]. It is therefore postulated that PaWRKY57 may augment plant stress resilience by transcriptionally modulating downstream antioxidant enzyme genes.

Protein-protein interaction prediction and functional enrichment analysis provided preliminary insights into potential upstream signaling components associated with PaWRKY57. The predicted network suggested that PaWRKY57 may potentially associate with MAPK family proteins, including MPK3, MPK4, and MPK6, which is consistent with the well-established role of the MAPK cascade as a core module for perceiving and transducing salt stress signals in plants [62, 63]. In Arabidopsis, WRKY33 has been demonstrated to be phosphorylated by MPK3/MPK6 and to interact with MKS1 and WRKY25 to regulate defense responses [42, 64, 65]. Given the close phylogenetic relationship between PaWRKY57 and AtWRKY33, it is plausible that PaWRKY57 may participate in similar protein complexes; however, this hypothesis remains to be experimentally verified. Other predicted candidate proteins, such as WRKY25, MKS1, and CYP71B15, have been reported in other species to be synergistically involved in multiple physiological processes, including gene expression regulation and phytoalexin biosynthesis [23, 64, 65]. The co-expression patterns of these candidate genes with PaWRKY57 under salt stress provide preliminary support for their potential functional association, but do not constitute evidence of direct protein-protein interaction. Future studies employing Y2H, BiFC, or co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assays are warranted to confirm these predicted interactions and to dissect the precise molecular mechanisms by which PaWRKY57 integrates into the salt stress signaling network.

This study investigated the functional role of PaWRKY57 in the salt stress response of apricot, with several inherent limitations remaining. First, as a transcription factor, the core function of PaWRKY57 is to regulate the transcription of downstream target genes. However, the downstream target genes directly regulated by PaWRKY57 have not been systematically identified in the present work, and no validated, functionally relevant target gene has been established to mechanistically explain the observed physiological phenotypes. This restricts the in-depth elucidation of its molecular regulatory mechanism underlying salt tolerance. Second, all functional characterizations of PaWRKY57 depend on heterologous overexpression in tobacco. We did not carry out reverse verification assays such as gene silencing or gene knockout in native apricot materials, resulting in relatively single evidence supporting its biological function. Third, the protein-protein interaction network generated in this study relies solely on in silico prediction and correlative transcriptional expression analysis. Direct biochemical assays including Y2H, BiFC, or Co-IP are still needed to verify physical binding between PaWRKY57 and its putative partner proteins. Accordingly, the proposed interaction network should only be regarded as a preliminary theoretical model pending further experimental validation. Future studies will integrate transcriptome profiling and DAP-seq to screen the direct target genes of PaWRKY57. Meanwhile, genetic editing and transient gene suppression assays in apricot will be performed to further validate gene function. These follow-up works will clarify the complete regulatory cascade of PaWRKY57 and supply valuable gene resources for salt-tolerant apricot molecular breeding.

Conclusion

This study presents a systematic genome-wide analysis of the WRKY gene family in apricot, identifying 58 members and revealing their physicochemical properties, phylogeny, and gene structures. PaWRKY57 was confirmed as a nuclear-localized transcription factor that positively regulates salt tolerance by maintaining cell membrane integrity, enhancing antioxidant enzyme activities, scavenging ROS, and promoting proline accumulation. These findings provide a theoretical basis and gene resources for salt tolerance mechanism research and molecular breeding in apricot.
