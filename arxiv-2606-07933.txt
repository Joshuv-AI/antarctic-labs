Finite-Blocklength Lossy Joint Source-Channel Coding over Unknown Channels
Adeel Mahmood Harish Viswanathan Jinfeng Du Nokia Bell Labs Nokia Bell Labs Nokia Bell Labs Murray Hill, NJ, USA Murray Hill, NJ, USA Murray Hill, NJ, USA

Abstract
We analyze the finite-blocklength performance of lossy joint source-channel codes (JSCC) in an unknown-channel framework, where the true channel is unknown but the source distribution is known. We establish achievability results for mismatched-design JSCC, where the code design is based on a channel Q Y | X Q_{Y|X} but deployed over a different channel P Y | X P_{Y|X} . Our mismatched-design achievability result allows nonstationary channel laws and arbitrary standard Borel alphabets for the source, reproduction, channel input and channel output. The achievability bound is given in terms of the rate-distortion and rate-dispersion functions, as well as two channel-dependent quantities that we call the mismatched-design rate and mismatched-design rate-dispersion. For block erasure channels, our result shows that channel mismatch incurs no penalty. We then show a second-order universal family of source-channel codes over the set of block erasure channels. Our code construction uses Poisson functional representations of suitable conditional probability measures to produce the encoder and decoder outputs. We use a parameterized family of Gibbs posteriors as the decoder-side kernels, whose envelope recovers the generalized mutual information.

Introduction

With the advent of AI, joint source–channel coding (JSCC) has attracted significant recent interest
[
6
,
19
,
1
,
20
]
. In AI-based JSCC, encoder models are trained to map source data directly to channel inputs, while decoder models are trained to reconstruct the source data with the highest possible fidelity. Image and video datasets, combined with synthetic additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channels, are commonly used for training. Prior studies have shown that encoder and decoder models trained at a single nominal channel signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) can perform reasonably well over a range of SNR values
[
1
,
19
]
. This behavior contrasts with separate source and channel coding, which exhibits a waterfall effect, characterized by a sharp degradation in reconstruction fidelity when the channel capacity falls below the source encoder rate. These findings suggest that JSCC may offer robustness to channel uncertainty. Motivated by this observation, in this paper, we investigate achievable performance bounds for lossy JSCC when the source distribution is assumed to be known but the actual channel statistics are unknown to both the encoder and the decoder.

Let
P_{Y|X}
denote the unknown channel, which we will also call the true channel. In this unknown-channel framework, we study a
mismatched-design
class of JSCC in which the JSCC design is based on a channel
Q_{Y|X}
fixed at design time. Both the channel input distribution
Q_{X}^{*}
and the decoder kernel are based on
Q_{Y|X}
, while the parameters of
P_{Y|X}
play no role in the JSCC construction. We call this a
Q_{Y|X}
-based source-channel code. Through refined asymptotic analysis based on the normal approximation, we analyze the finite-blocklength performance of the
Q_{Y|X}
-based source-channel code when the code is run over the channel
P_{Y|X}
. Our results characterizing an achievable performance region under the channel mismatch will be called
mismatched-design
results.

Such mismatched-design JSCC for unknown channels is driven by the needs of real deployments. On the one hand, for transmission over mobile channels, only an estimate
Q_{Y|X}
of the true channel
P_{Y|X}
may be known, so that the JSCC design may be
Q_{Y|X}
-based. On the other hand, replacing separate source and channel coding by JSCC in real communication networks is difficult
[
18
]
because practical networks are inherently modular: application providers responsible for source coding and network providers responsible for channel coding are typically distinct entities connected through standardized packet interfaces. It is impractical to relay the real-time channel state information to the application provider to enable channel-aware source coding.

Recent works
[
18
]
,
[
4
]
proposed a new standard interface, called the multi-level reliability interface, between the application layer and the network in order to abstract the network channel into multiple reliability levels for the application provider. The source coder at the application layer is then trained as a JSCC for a channel abstraction
Q_{Y|X}
derived from the multi-level reliability interface. The channel coder at the network optimizes the transmission of the message packets based on packet importance weights
[
13
]
that are also derived from the same interface. The channel abstraction used for training at the application is chosen to be block erasure channels (BECs) with different erasure probabilities in
[
4
]
. The motivation for this choice is that in real packet networks, decoding failures are naturally represented as packet erasures, with the decoder explicitly knowing which blocks/packets are missing. Hence, the true channel
P_{Y|X}
can be modeled as a nonstationary BEC, with different packet erasure probabilities that are determined by factors such as channel conditions and scheduling policies. Understanding the achievable finite-blocklength performance in the channel-mismatched framework provides valuable insights in optimizing the design and operation: the application uses a JSCC trained for a channel abstraction
Q_{Y|X}
while the network chooses its parameters

1

These parameters could include orthogonal resource allocation weights or different power allocations in a power-domain superposition scheme
[
13
]
.
and policies, including unequal error protection (UEP) and selective packet dropping, to optimize the effective channel

are block erasure channels, with

(0,1)

-based source-channel codes achieves the same first- and second-order asymptotic performance as the family of optimal
P_{Y|X}
-matched codes.

To derive our mismatched-design result, we consider a specific construction of a
Q_{Y|X}
-based JSCC and analyze its achievable performance over a different channel
P_{Y|X}
. We focus on a complete channel-mismatched setting where the parameters of the true (unknown) channel
P_{Y|X}
play no role in the JSCC construction. In particular, this makes the problem different from the analysis of mismatch capacity in the mismatched decoding framework
[
5
]
, which entails optimizing over the channel input law while the decoding metric is arbitrary but fixed. Our JSCC construction departs from the conventional framework of shared i.i.d. source and channel codebooks between the encoder and decoder. Instead, following
[
11
]
, we have a Poisson point process as shared randomness, and the encoder and decoder obtain the channel input and source reconstruction, respectively, using the Poisson functional representation
[
11
, Definition 1]
of suitable conditional probability measures. The source distribution
P_{S}
is assumed to be fully known at design time, so this paper does not focus on source mismatch.

Our one-shot results in the mismatched-design framework encompass arbitrary sources and channels on standard Borel spaces, albeit with certain technical assumptions which ensure, in particular, that the information density of the joint distribution
Q_{X}^{*}\times Q_{Y|X}
is well-defined under the mismatched distribution
Q_{X}^{*}\times P_{Y|X}
. On the other hand, our block coding results based on the normal approximation encompass memoryless, stationary sources and memoryless, but not necessarily stationary, channels, whose single-letter probability laws are arbitrary but satisfy mild regularity conditions. We prove finite-blocklength achievability bounds in the memoryless setting in terms of the usual source-related quantities, the rate-distortion and rate-dispersion functions
[
7
]
, as well as two channel-related single-letter quantities, which we call the
mismatched-design rate
and the
mismatched-design rate-dispersion
. Mismatched-design performance outside of the channel regularity conditions is discussed from an operational perspective.

For block erasure channels (BECs), the mismatched-design rate and mismatched-design rate-dispersion are simply the usual capacity and dispersion of the true block erasure channel
P_{Y|X}
as long as
Q_{Y|X}
has an erasure probability in
(0,1)
, thus proving that there is no penalty of channel mismatch for BECs. We use this observation to formalize a notion of second-order universal source-channel codes and then construct such codes over the set of block erasure channels. At a high level, a JSCC scheme is second-order channel-universal over a set of channels
\mathcal{W}
if its encoder and decoder do not depend on the true
P_{Y|X}\in\mathcal{W}
, yet for every
P_{Y|X}\in\mathcal{W}
, it achieves the same first- and second-order asymptotic performance as the
P_{Y|X}
-matched optimal JSCC scheme. This universal result should be understood as a property of a family of codes, indexed by the channel blocklength
n

and source blocklength
k
. Our universal results do not imply the existence of a single source-channel code that is optimal for every channel in a given set
\mathcal{W}
. This is because a single source-channel code
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
in the family is associated with an operating point
(n,k)
and also depends on the target distortion level
d
. Then for a certain channel
P_{Y|X}
in a channel set
\mathcal{W}
, the code
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
might be optimal in the sense that the rate
R=k/n
is the best achievable rate for that channel and the distortion level

, while for other channels in
\mathcal{W}
, a higher rate might be achievable, making the code
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
suboptimal. We make the notion of universality rigorous in Section
IV-A
and present our universal results in Section
IV-B
.

I-A

Related Work

Mismatched-design frameworks have been studied in several works under a variety of closely related formulations and mostly in the context of channel coding. Second-order asymptotics of the achievable rate based on the normal approximation were studied in
[
17
]
for the case when a channel code optimized for AWGN channels (i.e., one that uses spherical codebooks and nearest neighbor decoding) is deployed over arbitrary additive (non-Gaussian) noise memoryless channels; first-order results for this same framework were previously derived by Lapidoth
[
9
]
. The
“mismatch”
in
[
17
]
and
[
9
]
is that between a Gaussian model and a non-Gaussian model for the additive noise in the channel. However, most works on mismatched-design frameworks have focused on mismatched decoding in the context of channel coding, where the decoding rule is the argmax of a fixed, possibly suboptimal, decoding metric. In this mismatched maximum-metric decoding formulation, first-order asymptotically achievable rates given by the generalized mutual information (GMI) and the lower bound on mismatched (LM) capacity have been derived; see the survey paper
[
15
]
and references therein. In particular,
[
5
]
derives lower bounds to the mismatch capacity in several settings, which include general alphabet channels with memory and non-single-letter decoding metrics. Second-order asymptotics of achievable rates (second-order coding rates) with mismatched decoding are studied in
[
16
]
, featuring the GMI and the LM rate together with the dispersion as the second-order term in the asymptotic expansion
[
16
, Theorem 5]
.

Our mismatched-design formulation is closer in spirit to the mismatched-decoding setting of
[
5
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
, but differs in several aspects. First, we derive results for JSCC rather than for channel coding, which requires proof techniques different from the random-coding union bound used in
[
16
, Theorem 1]
. Second, we restrict our attention to a parameterized family of decoding metrics induced by the design channel
Q_{Y|X}
rather than completely arbitrary decoding metrics. However, this restriction is not a limitation of our proof techniques; the same methods can be extended to arbitrary decoding metrics. Third, unlike
[
5
]
, our mismatched-design results are in the finite-blocklength setting similar to the second-order asymptotic result in
[
16
, Theorem 5]
. Unlike
[
16
, Theorem 5]
, our main mismatched-design result (Theorem
4
) applies to nonstationary channels, specifies an explicit third-order term and is in the JSCC framework instead of the channel coding framework. Our mismatched-design result recovers the GMI as the envelope over a parameterized family of
Q_{Y|X}
-based JSCC designs; however, the LM rate is not the focus of this work.

Literature on mismatched JSCC frameworks is relatively sparse, especially in the finite-blocklength regime. Optimal mismatched analysis, or even standard matched analysis, in JSCC in the finite-blocklength regime is harder than in channel coding because an optimal JSCC decoder in the finite-blocklength regime does not generally reduce to a maximum likelihood channel decoder
[
8
, p. 2552]
. Zhou, Tan and Motani
[
21
]
studied a JSCC architecture called NN-JSCC that is constrained to use random codebooks (for both source reproduction and channel codewords) that are either i.i.d. Gaussian or spherical. However, instead of nearest neighbor encoding and decoding rules that are optimal for Gaussian sources and channels, they use modified minimum distance and nearest neighbor schemes that serve to maximize the communication rate when transmitting an unknown arbitrary memoryless source over an unknown arbitrary additive noise memoryless channel, although the source power and noise power are known and fixed. Such modifications ensure a robust performance over a range of source and channel laws; a naive JSCC construction designed optimally only for Gaussian sources and channels (i.e., one that uses plain nearest neighbor schemes) may not necessarily perform well for arbitrarily distributed sources and additive channels.

The NN-JSCC is part of the mismatched-design class of JSCC. Indeed, the fact that the NN-JSCC uses random codebooks optimized for Gaussian channels represents exactly a channel-mismatch design because such codebooks are not necessarily optimal for general additive non-Gaussian noise channels. Hence, results for NN-JSCC characterize the performance under mismatch between a Gaussian model and a non-Gaussian model for the source and channel, while the source power and noise power are matched and known. Furthermore, when the true source and channel are Gaussian, the NN-JSCC achieves the optimal first- and second-order performance. However, the performance of NN-JSCC is not shown to be optimal for an arbitrary non-Gaussian source and an additive non-Gaussian noise channel. Hence, it is not clear whether NN-JSCC can be said to be a universal construction over any non-trivial set of channels, especially since the NN-JSCC design is based on a fixed and known channel noise power
[
21
, (2)]
. Nevertheless, the NN-JSCC remains
“robust”
in the sense that for any arbitrary source with fixed source power (or second-moment)
\sigma^{2}
and an additive channel with fixed SNR
P
, the first-order asymptotic rate (number of source symbols per channel use) achieved by the NN-JSCC is
C(P)/R_{d}(\sigma^{2})
, where
C(P)
and
R_{d}(\sigma^{2})
are the Gaussian capacity and rate-distortion function, respectively. A similar notion of robustness was proved in
[
9
]
for channel codes and in
[
10
]
for lossy source codes. In
[
9
]
, the rate
C(P)
was shown to be achievable over all additive (non-Gaussian) channels with SNR
P
using a channel code based on Gaussian codebook and nearest neighbor decoding. In
[
10
]
, the compression rate
R_{d}(\sigma^{2})
was shown to be achievable for all (non-Gaussian) sources with second-moment
\sigma^{2}
using a source code based on a Gaussian codebook. However, universality is a stronger notion than robustness.

IV
Results on universal source-channel codes

In the first part of this section, we give the definition of universal source-channel codes, where the universality is only w.r.t. a given set of channels. In the second part, we present a universal source-channel code construction for block erasure channels.

IV-A

Definition of Second-Order Universality

The notion of universal codes was first introduced in the source coding literature
[
3
]
. Universal source codes have two defining characteristics: they do not have knowledge
of the source distribution, and they achieve the same asymptotic
performance as optimal codes that do. For joint source-channel codes, we refer to the analogous first property as
channel-blindness
. For the second property, we strengthen the usual notion from universal source coding: instead of requiring only first-order asymptotic optimality, we require the channel-blind JSCC construction to achieve both first- and second-order asymptotically optimal performance, matching an optimal channel-matched JSCC scheme. This is motivated by the fact that first-order optimality is already achieved by separate source-channel coding, so the study of first-order universal joint source-channel codes
over a set of channels can simply be reduced to a universal channel coding problem.

We now rigorously define the notion of second-order universality of joint source-channel codes over the set of memoryless, nonstationary channels. Let
\mathcal{W}
be any set of single-use channels
P_{Y|X}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})
. Let

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{W})\coloneqq\left\{\left(P_{Y|X}^{(i)}\right)_{i\geq 1}:P_{Y|X}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{W}\right\}

denote the set of all memoryless, nonstationary channels with channel laws in
\mathcal{W}
. We will sometimes write
\mathbf{P}\coloneqq\left(P_{Y|X}^{(i)}\right)_{i\geq 1}
as shorthand. Given any
\mathbf{P}\in\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{W})
, denote its
n
-block prefix channel by
P_{Y^{n}|X^{n}}^{\mathbf{P}}
where

\displaystyle P_{Y^{n}|X^{n}}^{\mathbf{P}}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}P_{Y|X}^{(i)}.

Then let

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}P_{Y|X}^{(i)}:P_{Y|X}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{W}\right\}

(33)

denote the set of all
n
-block prefix channels, or equivalently, the set of
n
-block memoryless, nonstationary channels with channel laws in
\mathcal{W}
. We also write
P_{n}\coloneqq P_{Y^{n}|X^{n}}^{\mathbf{P}}
as shorthand.

To simplify the notation, assume that the source distribution
P_{S^{k}}=P_{S}^{\otimes k}
, distortion level

and distortion measure

have been fixed as in
(\ref{rep1})
such that

A source-channel code

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{k^{*}\left(d,\epsilon,n|P_{Y|X}^{\otimes n}\right)-k^{*}_{\mathcal{U}^{(d)}}\left(d,\epsilon,n|P_{Y|X}^{\otimes n}\right)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)=0.

(37)

Remark 7

We note that universality is a property of a channel-blind family of source-channel codes, and it is an asymptotic notion. A single code
U_{n,k}^{(d)}
in the family is not what one calls universal. Rather, a universality result in the above framework asserts that the same channel-blind
family contains codes whose performance is asymptotically near-optimal for every
channel in the class. This does not, however, remove the need to select an appropriate operating point
(n,k)
for the channel at hand. In our second-order universal construction (next section), the channel-blind family of codes is generated by a common rule
whose operating point
(n,k)
can be adjusted. That is, we provide a single code-generation rule over a set of channels that is reused across all
(n,k)
to obtain the channel-blind family of codes. This is in contrast to a
purely existence result in which the codes
U_{n,k}^{(d)}
in the family could be unrelated across different blocklength pairs. In this sense, our universality result can be viewed as a form of construction-level separation: the rule used to generate the source-channel code is fixed uniformly over the set of channels, while channel adaptation is reduced to selecting the source blocklength
k
for a given channel blocklength
n
.

Lastly, the definition of universality in Definition
10
requires pointwise convergence to the optimal limit for each fixed sequence
\mathbf{P}
as opposed to uniform convergence over the set
\mathscr{C}_{\text{reg}}(\mathcal{W})
:

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\sup_{\mathbf{P}\in\mathscr{C}_{\text{reg}}(\mathcal{W})}\left(\frac{k^{*}\left(d,\epsilon,n|P_{n}\right)-k^{*}_{\mathcal{U}^{(d)}}(d,\epsilon,n|P_{n})}{\sqrt{n}}\right)=0.

(38)

This parallels the distinction in source coding: weak universality requires convergence for each fixed source law, whereas strong universality, or minimax universality, requires uniform convergence over the source class.
Such a distinction is not merely technical; it leads to different convergence rates
[
12
]
. In this paper, we only enforce a pointwise, weak-universality requirement over channel sequences
\mathbf{P}\in\mathscr{C}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{W})
.

V
Conclusion and Future Directions

Our mismatched-design result (Theorem
4
) proves the scheme-envelope achievability of the generalized mutual information in the JSCC setting and for nonstationary channels over a fixed general alphabet. Theorem
4
also subsumes achievability results for nonstationary channels in the traditional matched JSCC. For future directions, one can study mismatched decoding involving an arbitrary decoding metric

We also defined a new notion of universal JSCC and showed that a channel-blind family of JSCC is second-order universal over the set of memoryless and stationary block erasure channels with erasure probability

Whenever

\check{Z}
drawn from the distribution

satisfies
\operatorname{d}(s,\check{Z})\leq d
almost surely. The core idea is that the encoder and decoder both have access to a Poisson point process as shared randomness. Given a source realization
S=s
, the encoder can obtain a point
(X,\check{Z})
from the Poisson point process with the target conditional joint distribution
Q_{X}\times P_{\check{Z};s}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z})

K_{X|Y=y}\times P_{Z}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z})

\hat{Z}

\epsilon(d)=\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{d}(S,\hat{Z})>d)
, which can be upper bounded by
\mathbb{P}(\check{Z}\neq\hat{Z})

Let

(\ref{unif_enc})

Then since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})=\Theta(n)
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})=\Theta(n)
, we have
\frac{\hat{k}_{n}}{n}=\Theta(1)
. Hence, there exists an
N>0
such that
\operatorname{S}=\left\{(n,\hat{k}_{n}):n\geq N\right\}
is a valid (in the sense of Definition
8
) admissible source-channel blocklength set for the result
(\ref{n350c})
. Next, we show that for sufficiently large
n
, inequality

(\ref{n350c})
is satisfied for
k=\hat{k}_{n}
. Indeed, the LHS of
(\ref{n350c})
evaluated at
k=\hat{k}_{n}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})-\hat{k}_{n}R_{d}(P_{S})

\displaystyle=Q^{-1}(\epsilon)\sqrt{\frac{V_{d}(P_{S})}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})}+\overline{c}\log(n)+\tilde{c}.

(67)

The RHS of
(\ref{n350c})
evaluated at
k=\hat{k}_{n}
is

\displaystyle Q^{-1}(\epsilon)\sqrt{\hat{k}_{n}V_{d}(P_{S})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})}+\overline{c}\log(\hat{k}_{n})+c.

(68)

Hence, it suffices to show that for sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle Q^{-1}(\epsilon)\sqrt{\frac{V_{d}(P_{S})}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})}+\overline{c}\log(n)+\tilde{c}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\geq Q^{-1}(\epsilon)\sqrt{\hat{k}_{n}V_{d}(P_{S})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})}+\overline{c}\log(\hat{k}_{n})+c.

(69)

Case 1:
If
\epsilon\leq 1/2
, then
(\ref{choiceofkn})
implies that there exists a constant
N_{0}^{\prime}
such that for
n\geq N_{0}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\hat{k}_{n}\leq\frac{1}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)}).

(70)

Then the RHS of
(\ref{finalsufficientkn})
can be upper bounded by

\displaystyle Q^{-1}(\epsilon)\sqrt{\frac{V_{d}(P_{S})}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})}+\overline{c}\log\left(\frac{1}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})\right)+c

\displaystyle\leq Q^{-1}(\epsilon)\sqrt{\frac{V_{d}(P_{S})}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})}+\overline{c}\log\left(\frac{n\log(2^{m})}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\right)+c.

Therefore,
(\ref{finalsufficientkn})
holds for
n\geq N_{0}^{\prime}
.

Case 2:
For
\epsilon>1/2
,
(\ref{choiceofkn})
implies that there exists
N_{1}^{\prime}
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\hat{k}_{n}\geq\frac{1}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})

(71)

and

\displaystyle\hat{k}_{n}\leq\frac{2n\log(2^{m})}{R_{d}(P_{S})}.

(72)

Using
(\ref{sjha2fgdhaf})
and
(\ref{sjnidadhaf})
, the RHS of
(\ref{finalsufficientkn})
can be upper bounded by

\displaystyle Q^{-1}(\epsilon)\sqrt{\frac{V_{d}(P_{S})}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})}+\overline{c}\log\left(\frac{2n\log(2^{m})}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\right)+c.

Hence,
(\ref{finalsufficientkn})
holds for
n\geq N_{1}^{\prime}
.

Combining both cases, we have that for every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle k^{*}_{\mathcal{U}^{(d)}}(d,\epsilon,n|P_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})-\frac{Q^{-1}(\epsilon)}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sqrt{\frac{V_{d}(P_{S})}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}C(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(P_{Y|X}^{(i)})}-\mbox{}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\frac{\overline{c}}{R_{d}(P_{S})}\log(n)-\frac{\tilde{c}}{R_{d}(P_{S})}

for sufficiently large
n
.

Appendix F
Examples of Sources

The main results in the paper apply to a source distribution
P_{S}
, distortion level
d
and distortion measure
\operatorname{d}
satisfying Assumptions

(\ref{assumpr1_source})
-
(\ref{assumpr3_source})
and Conditions
(\ref{cond1dee})
-
(\ref{weird9thmomcond})
. In this section, we give examples of source-distortion tuples
(P_{S},d,\operatorname{d})
that satisfy these conditions.

F-A

Gaussian source with squared-error distortion measure

For a Gaussian source
S\sim P_{S}
with squared-error distortion measure
\operatorname{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle P_{S}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{S}^{2}),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\sigma_{S}^{2}<\infty,

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}(s,z)

\displaystyle=(s-z)^{2}.

For any distortion level
d\in(0,\infty)
, the
rate-distortion function of the Gaussian source is

\displaystyle R_{d}(P_{S})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\sigma_{S}^{2}}{d},&0<d<\sigma_{S}^{2},\\[5.0pt]
0,&d\geq\sigma_{S}^{2}.\end{cases}

(73)

Hence,
d_{\min}=0
and
d_{\max}=\sigma_{S}^{2}
. For
d\in(0,\sigma_{S}^{2})
, the infimum in
(\ref{64})
is uniquely achieved by the following conditional distribution
[
2
, 10.3.2]
:

\displaystyle P_{Z|S}^{*}(\cdot|s)=\mathcal{N}\!\left(\left(1-\frac{d}{\sigma_{S}^{2}}\right)s,d\left(1-\frac{d}{\sigma_{S}^{2}}\right)\right),

(74)

and the reproduction marginal is
P_{Z}^{*}=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{S}^{2}-d).
Equivalently, the rate-distortion-achieving joint law
P_{S,Z}^{*}=P_{S}\times P_{Z|S}^{*}
admits the backward
representation
S=Z+N
, where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{S}^{2}-d),N\sim\mathcal{N}(0,d)
and
Z\perp\!\!\!\perp N
. Assumption
(\ref{assumpr3_source})
can be verified to hold by simply taking
E=\{0\}
. To verify that Condition
(\ref{weird9thmomcond})
holds, let
S\sim P_{S}
and
Z\sim P_{Z}^{*}
be independent. Then since
S-Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,2\sigma_{S}^{2}-d)
, we have

\mathbb{E}\!\left[(\operatorname{d}(S,Z))^{9}\right]=\mathbb{E}[(S-Z)^{18}]<\infty
. Furthermore, for
d\in(0,\sigma_{S}^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{d}R_{d}(P_{S})=\frac{1}{2d},

\displaystyle\jmath_{d}(s,P_{S},\operatorname{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{S}^{2}}{d}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{s^{2}}{\sigma_{S}^{2}}-1\right),

\displaystyle V_{d}(P_{S})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle\overline{c}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{d^{2}}{\sigma_{S}^{4}-d^{2}},

where
\overline{c}
was defined in
(\ref{c28f})
.

F-B

Bernoulli source with Hamming distortion measure

For a Bernoulli source
S\sim P_{S}
with Hamming distortion measure
\operatorname{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Z}=\{0,1\},

\displaystyle P_{S}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Bern}(p),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<p<1,

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}(s,z)

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}\{s\neq z\}.

For any distortion level
d\in[0,\infty)
, the
rate-distortion function for a Bernoulli
(p)
source is given by

\displaystyle R_{d}(P_{S})=\begin{cases}H_{b}(p)-H_{b}(d),&0\leq d\leq\min\{p,1-p\},\\[5.0pt]
0,&d>\min\{p,1-p\}.\end{cases}

(75)

Hence,
d_{\min}=0
and
d_{\max}=\min\{p,1-p\}
. For
d\in(0,d_{\max})
, the infimum in
(\ref{64})
is uniquely achieved by the following conditional distribution
[
2
, 10.3.1]
:

P_{Z|S}^{*}(z|s)

z=0

z=1

s=0

\frac{(1-d)(1-p-d)}{(1-p)(1-2d)}

\frac{d(p-d)}{(1-p)(1-2d)}

s=1

\frac{d(1-p-d)}{p(1-2d)}

\frac{(1-d)(p-d)}{p(1-2d)}
.

The reproduction marginal is
P_{Z}^{*}=\left(\frac{1-p-d}{1-2d},\frac{p-d}{1-2d}\right).
Equivalently, the rate-distortion-achieving joint law
P_{S,Z}^{*}=P_{S}\times P_{Z|S}^{*}
admits the backward
representation
S=Z\oplus N
, where
Z\sim P_{Z}^{*},N\sim\operatorname{Bern}(d)
and
Z\perp\!\!\!\perp N
. Clearly, Assumption
(\ref{assumpr3_source})
and Condition
(\ref{weird9thmomcond})
hold. Furthermore, for
0<d<\min\{p,1-p\}
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{d}R_{d}(P_{S})=\log\left(\frac{1-d}{d}\right),

\displaystyle\jmath_{d}(s,P_{S},\operatorname{d})

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{1}{P_{S}(s)}\right)-H_{b}(d),

\displaystyle V_{d}(P_{S})

\displaystyle=p(1-p)\log^{2}\left(\frac{1-p}{p}\right),

\displaystyle\overline{c}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{d(1-d)(2p-1)^{2}}{(p-d)(1-p-d)}.
