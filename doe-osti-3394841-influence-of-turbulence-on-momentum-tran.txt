ABSTRACT

The interface between a river and the groundwater has been identified as being a critical
component of the ecohydrology of watersheds. The spatially and temporally fluctuating
region where surface waters and groundwaters mix is termed, loosely, the hyporheic zone.
The hyporheic zone has been identified as being one of the most important components
controlling the exchange of nutrients (including carbons sources, electron donors, and
electron acceptors such as oxygen) and other aqueous components (such as colloids and
dissolved contaminants) between the surface and groundwater systems. The fluctuating
motions of fluids, known as turbulence, has been identified as a major process affecting
transport across the sediment-water interface. In this work, we conducted a closer
examination of the influence of turbulence near the sed at resolutions that have been
previously not tractable. This exploration included the following. 1. Direct experimental
observations of the flow structures that increase momentum (and mass) transport in the
region of the hyporheic zone. 2. A combination of high-resolution computer simulations of
the flow near the sediment-water interface. These were used both to interpret experimental
observations, and to provide necessary data and analysis for the development of theory. 3.
Development of new mathematical models that represent the transport of fluid and
dissolved components near the sediment-water interface. 4. A nonlinear closure scheme
illustrating a proof-of-principle for scalar variables was developed; this represents a new
method for closure in upscaling efforts. The results of this work were (1) A suite of
experimental data (available to other scientists) that reports direct measurements of the
influence of turbulence at the sediment-water interface, (2) numerical simulations that
support and help interpret these experiments, and (3) a number of peer-reviewed journal
articles that report the results to the broader scientific community.

SUMMARY OF PROJECT ACTIVITIES

Objectives/Hypotheses

In the proposed work, we present several novel ideas that will allow a closer examination of
the influence of turbulence near the SWI at resolutions that have been previously not
tractable. This proposal will include the following components that address the problem of
observation and upscaling turbulent flows near the free fluid-porous media interface.
Among the novel ideas we propose are the following:

  1. Direct experimental observations of the flow structures that increase momentum
 (and mass) transport in the region of the hyporheic zone. We will use our expertise in
 index-of-refraction matched fluid-solid porous media systems (Patil 2012, Patil 2013,
 Wood 2015) to conduct these experiments. We have shown previously (e.g., Wood
 2015) that these methods generate high-fidelity data regarding the flow field within
 porous media.

 2

  2. A combination of high-resolution large eddy simulation (LES) and fully-resolved
 DNS used to understand the turbulent characteristics of flows near the SWI. We
 have extensive expertise in the simulation of turbulent flows near and within porous
 materials. Our high-resolution direct numerical simulations (DNS) combined with large
 eddy simulations (LES) will be used both to interpret experimental observations, and to
 provide necessary data and analysis for the development of theory. The DNS
 simulations will be performed at the Kolmogorov microscale (or below), so that there is
 no closure approximation adopted in solving the Navier-Stokes equations.

  3. Development specific theory that yield upscaled models that can represent this
 process in multi-scale hydrologic modeling frameworks. The groundwork for this theory
 has been outlined by our group in a recent publication in the Annual Reviews of Fluid
 Mechanics. This theory supplies additional sources for the Reynolds stress tensor that
 have not been conventionally considered in upscaling approaches but are essential for
 consistency with the existing experimental data. Development of upscaled models for
 these applications is needed, because it is still impractical to use DNS simulation on a
 routine basis for representing momentum transport at the SWI.

  4. We will use novel artificial neural network learning approaches to address the
 well-known closure problem in turbulence theory as part of our upscaling process.
 This is a novel method whose utility has only just begun to be recognized. In particular,
 this approach will allow us to effectively handle the nonlinear closure of the Navier-
 Stokes equations. The nonlinear closure is the most difficult component of upscaling
 the Navier-Stokes equations. Machine learning methods have only just begun to be
 investigated as closure schemes for turbulent flows; they represent a true paradigm
 shift in the methods used to effect closure.

All objectives were explored during the research. However, objective 4 was reduced in
scope due to unexpected changes in personnel (Dr. James Liburdy passed away during the
program of research). Thus, Objective 4 was completed for a nonlinear closure, but this
work was done illustrating only a proof-of-principle for the numerical closure scheme for
nonlinear systems. It was not completed for the full tensorial closure required to describe
turbulent flows.

Methods

The methods and approaches used in this research are summarized below. Detailed
descriptions of these methods are available in the associated peer-reviewed papers
associated with this research. These papers are reported as separate scientific and
technical information (STI) documents hosted by DOE OSTI ( www.osti.gov ).

 1. High-Performance Computing
 a. Pore-Resolved Simulations: Researchers run direct numerical simulations
 (DNS) across a variety of permeability Reynolds numbers (ReK).
 b. Turbulence Modeling: The computational structure maps exact open-channel
 fluid dynamics passing over randomly packed porous beds.

 3

 c. Interface Layer Evaluation: Simulations test the top sediment layer as
 roughness elements over an impermeable wall to trace pressure variations.
  2. Experimental Validation
 a. Index-Matching Fluid Dynamics: The team deploys refractive-index-matched
 Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV) to visually capture clear flow velocities inside
 the sediment voids.
 b. Solute Mapping: Planar Laser-Induced Fluorescence (PLIF) maps tracer
 concentrations to isolate turbulent mixing patterns.

  3. Upscaling & Machine Learning
 a. Double-Averaging Methodology: Time-space averaging equations isolate the
 specific impacts of Reynolds stresses, form-induced stresses, and mixing
 lengths.
 b. Physics-Informed Neural Networks: Machine learning data analysis computes
 the subgrid-scale quantities to upscale findings into macro-scale nonlinear
 partial differential equations (PDEs).

 Summary of Findings Related to Each Objective

  1. Objective 1. Refractive Index Matched Flow Visualization. A suite of refractive-
 index matched PIV experiments were successfully conducted on transitional and
 turbulent open channel flow over randomly packed spherical particle bed. Detailed
 data near the particle-flow interface was used for validation of continuum models in
 large-eddy simulation predictions. This work has been published in two theses
 (Jordan Strahl and Zeeshan Memon). A journal article combining experimental data
 and volume averaged LES simulations is under review (Memon, Z., Wood, BD, Apte,
 SV, "Momentum Transport and Interfacial Turbulence across the Sediment–Water
 Interface in Rough Permeable Beds”, Transport Phenomena, under review.)

 4

2. Objecrtive 2. Upscaled LES Turbulence Theory. A new, single-domain
 formulation based on volume averaging was developed to model the free flow-
 particle bed region based on a set of governing equations with variations in porosity
 in the interface region. One of the major findings was that the standard Ergun-type
 permeability-porosity relation is inaccurate in capturing the net drag force in the flow-
 particle interface region. These closure models are developed for large
 representative elemental volumes that result in homogeneous porosity. However,
 near the flow-particle interface, porosity varies rapidly and the standard drag force
 closures are significantly inaccurate. This was evaluated by performing large-eddy
 simulation of transitional and turbulent flow over a particle bed and comparing the
 mean and turbulence statistics against the RIM PIV data. In the interface region,
 mismatch in the mean velocity as well turbulent stresses were observed. These
 findings are documented in PhD thesis of Zeeshan Memon and in a journal article
 (Memon, Z., Wood, BD, Apte, SV, "Momentum Transport and Interfacial Turbulence
 across the Sediment–Water Interface in Rough Permeable Beds”, Transport
 Phenomena, under review.)

3. Objective 3. Upscaled Turbulence Closure for Porous Media. Improved closure
 models for drag force in the interface region, pore-resolved direct numerical
 simulations of inertial flow over a randomly packed bed of spherical particles were
 computed for a range of free stream bulk Reynolds numbers (3-300) and different
 bed porosity (0.41, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7). The detailed flow and stress data was explicitly
 filtered using different kernels (box, cellular, quartic, Gaussian) and kernel widths
 proportional to the particle sizes. The standard Carman-Kozeny based permeability-
 porosity correlation significantly overpredicts the drag force in the interface region. A
 kernel with varying filter width that is proportional to the pore size deep within the bed
 and smoothly transitions to grid resolution in the free flow is devised by using a
 Lipschitz constraint to prevent unphysical local maxima or minima in permeability. A
 physics-informed, two-parameter correction based on Brinkman solution of
 exponential velocity within the bed is developed to correct the permeability in the
 transition region with rapid porosity variation. This corrected model is shown to give
 very good results compared to the resolved PR-DNS data. The variable filter
 introduces commutation errors and a thorough analysis of commutation errors in the
 interface region was performed. For the flow Reynolds numbers studied, these errors
 were small compared to the drag force; however, in general cases these errors
 should be accounted for in the model. These findings are documented in a journal
 article (Daniel Fust, Sourabh V. Apte, Brian D. Wood, S. Balachandar, “Modeling Drag
 Force in Porous Media: A Physics-Informed Closure for Inhomogeneous Porosity”
 Journal of Fluid Mechanics, 2026, under review.) A posteriori analysis of the
 improved closure model is being conducted.

4. Objective 4. Machine Learning Applications.

 Machine learning for Nonlinear Closures (scalar valued). Applications of machine
 learning methods to engineering problems is still an area of active research. As part
 of this project, we developed a closure scheme that used data to learn the effective
 behavior of a nonlinear system. There are few existing methods for achieving
 closures for nonlinear problems, especially in problems where perturbative methods
 5

 cannot be used to linearize the processes involved. In this work, we developed a
 nonlinear closure scheme for a transport and reaction problem as a proof-of-principle
 for closing (scalar valued) nonlinear systems. The method proved to be capable of
 accurate closures for even highly nonlinear processes. This work is reported in
 Ehsan Taghizadeh, Helen M.Byrne, Brian D.Wood, "Explicit physics-informed neural
 networks for nonlinear closure: The case of transport in tissues ", Journal of
 Computational Physics, Volume 449, 110781, 2022.

 Machine Learning for Nonlinear Closures of Roughness Effects. Roughness at the
 sediment-water interface presents a significant problem for turbulence theory. In this
 region, the length scales involved break classical averaging and closure schemes.
 To capture these effects of roughness at the sediment-water interface (and, hence,
 the turbulence penetration across the flow-particle interface), a three-dimensional
 filter-kernel with local roughness-capturing porosity and permeability variation is
 necessary. A machine-learning based nonlinear model is has been investigated to
 relate permeability and drag force to porosity, particle bed characteristics (pore size,
 surface area density), gradients in porosity and superficial velocity, tortuosity, among
 others. This work remains in progress.
