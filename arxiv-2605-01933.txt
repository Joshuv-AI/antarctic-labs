1.
Introduction

The underdamped, or kinetic, Langevin dynamics is a basic model for sampling
and nonequilibrium relaxation. Given a potential
U:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and friction

\gamma>0
, it is the stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=V_{t}\,\,\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}V_{t}

\displaystyle=-\nabla U(X_{t})\,\,\mathrm{d}t-\gamma V_{t}\,\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\gamma}\,\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},

where
W_{t}
is a standard
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Brownian motion. Its invariant
measure is the product Gibbs measure

\mu(\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)\propto\exp\{-U(x)-\lvert v\rvert^{2}/2\}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v.

For a reference probability measure
\nu
, write the entropy

\Ent_{\nu}(f):=\int f\log f\,\mathrm{d}\nu
for probability densities
f
(with

0\log 0=0
), and write the relative entropy
\Ent(\eta\mid\nu):=\Ent_{\nu}(\,\mathrm{d}\eta/\,\mathrm{d}\nu)
for
probability laws
\eta\ll\nu
, with value
+\infty
otherwise.
Let
p_{t}
be the law at time
t
of the underdamped Langevin dynamics with
initial datum
p_{0}
. The Langevin dynamics is a prototypical example of
hypocoercivity: for smooth solutions, the entropy dissipation is

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Ent(p_{t}\mid\mu)=-\gamma I_{v}(p_{t}/\mu):=-\gamma\int\frac{\lvert\nabla_{v}(p_{t}/\mu)\rvert^{2}}{p_{t}/\mu}\,\mathrm{d}\mu,

so the dissipation controls only velocity derivatives. Spatial relaxation is
therefore produced indirectly, through the Hamiltonian transport

v\cdot\nabla_{x}-\nabla U\cdot\nabla_{v}
. Hypocoercivity refers to methods that
quantify this transfer of dissipation from velocity to position
[
28
]
.

In this work we obtain quantitative entropy convergence estimates for the
underdamped Langevin dynamics. If
\mu_{x}\propto\mathrm{e}^{-U}\,\mathrm{d}x
satisfies a
logarithmic Sobolev inequality with constant
\rho
, the natural benchmark is
the quadratic potential
U(x)=\rho\lvert x\rvert^{2}/2
. With damping parameter

\gamma=\Gamma\sqrt{\rho}
, one may explicitly calculate that the best possible
order of relaxation is
\sqrt{\rho}
. In comparison, the overdamped Langevin
dynamics relaxes with rate
\rho
, and thus the underdamped dynamics exhibits
diffusive-to-ballistic acceleration. The aim of the present work is to obtain
such accelerated rates of convergence in relative entropy for general convex potentials, with constants depending only on
\Gamma
and
\rho
.

Our main result (Theorem
2.3
) is the explicit entropy decay

\Ent(p_{t}\mid\mu)\leq\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\rho}\,t}\,\Ent(p_{0}\mid\mu),\qquad t\geq 0,

with the explicit constants

\lambda_{\Gamma}=\frac{\theta}{2(1+\theta)},\qquad\theta=\min\Bigl\{\tfrac{\Gamma}{12},\tfrac{1}{4\Gamma}\Bigr\},

valid for arbitrary finite-entropy initial data
p_{0}
and any
\Gamma>0
. In particular, the rate of convergence is of sharp order
\sqrt{\rho}
.

The main new ingredient is a nonlinear corrector that couples the velocity current to the Brenier displacement of the position marginal (see
\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}
defined in (
2.18
)), which is the time derivative of
\tfrac{1}{2}W_{2}^{2}(q\mu_{x},\mu_{x})
. This is motivated by the modified
L^{2}
approach
[
15
,
18
]
and displacement convexity
[
27
,
24
]
. The corrector replaces the usual local mixed derivative with a Wasserstein-gradient quantity adapted to entropy. Its derivative supplies the missing spatial coercivity, and as a result, the constants in the final entropy estimate only involve the LSI constant
\rho
.

The qualitative mechanism behind such estimates goes back to the work of
Desvillettes–Villani on entropy-dissipating kinetic equations

[
12
]
and to the spectral and hypoelliptic analysis of
kinetic Fokker–Planck operators by Hérau–Nier and Hérau

[
22
]
,
[
23
]
.
Villani’s hypocoercivity memoir
[
28
]
then gave a systematic functional framework:
one modifies the natural norm, entropy, or Fisher information by adding mixed
position–velocity terms, and proves a closed Lyapunov inequality for the
modified functional. These arguments are robust
and apply to many degenerate diffusions, but the constants are typically tied
to auxiliary Sobolev norms, derivative bounds on the coefficients, or abstract
coercivity constants. As a result, extracting a sharp dependence on the
spatial Poincaré or log-Sobolev constant requires additional work.

A large literature has further developed this modified-functional idea in
Hilbert spaces to obtain more quantitative estimates. Dolbeault, Mouhot, and Schmeiser introduced an abstract
micro–macro
L^{2}
hypocoercivity method with computable rates for linear
kinetic equations conserving mass
[
14
,
15
]
and the approach has been adapted to underdamped Langevin dynamics
[
26
,
18
]
. In particular, a
very recent gap-shifted modified
L^{2}
method
[
18
]
provides a simple proof of a sharp
O(\sqrt{m})
convergence rate estimate, where
m
is the Poincaré constant of the spatial measure. Related Hilbert-space and
Bakry–Émery-type approaches clarify domain issues and produce explicit
hypocoercive estimates for Kolmogorov and Langevin generators

[
21
,
5
]
. More recently, the space-time Poincaré inequality approach of Albritton, Armstrong, Mourrat, and Novack

[
1
]
and the Langevin refinement
[
9
]
have led to explicit
L^{2}
rates based on time-augmented functional inequalities. In particular, Cao, Lu, and Wang obtained the sharp
O(\sqrt{m})
rate when the spatial measure has Poincaré constant
m
and the potential is convex
[
9
]
. There are also resolvent and space-time Poincaré–Lions approaches that are well suited to explicit constants and to weakly confining regimes

[
6
]
,
[
8
]
. These results are closest in spirit to the rate question addressed here, but they are primarily
L^{2}
or
resolvent estimates and use linear correctors or Hilbert projections rather
than a nonlinear entropy functional adapted to the evolving density.

Entropy hypocoercivity is more subtle because the dissipation is nonlinear in the
solution. Villani’s entropic method and the sharp entropy analysis of
Arnold–Erb for linear-drift Fokker–Planck equations show how modified
entropy or modified entropy-production functionals can recover exponential
convergence, with sharp rates in the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck case

[
28
]
,
[
4
]
. For nonlinear confinement, the
entropic multipliers method of Cattiaux, Guillin, Monmarché, and Zhang removes
some bounded-Hessian assumptions by replacing the standard log-Sobolev input
with a weighted log-Sobolev inequality
[
10
]
.
These approaches differ from the present one in two important ways: their
correctors are local differential multipliers, and the closing functional
inequalities generally encode more information than the sole LSI constant

\rho
of the position marginal. The estimate proved below instead uses, at the level of constants, only
convexity and the ordinary LSI for
\mu_{x}
; the additional tame-growth
assumption is used solely for the approximation argument.

Probabilistic and optimal transport methods give another family of quantitative kinetic estimates. Coupling constructions yield contractions for modified Wasserstein distances and can be highly explicit, especially near the boundary between overdamped and underdamped regimes
[
16
,
11
]
. On compact position spaces, phase-space Wasserstein contraction estimates can also be proved by exploiting the explicit Langevin flow
[
13
]
. These results quantify convergence in transport metrics on phase space, but they do not directly yield the entropy estimate pursued here and often do not yield sharp estimates.

2.
Main results and outline

Let

\mu(\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)=\mu_{x}(\,\mathrm{d}x)\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v),\qquad\mu_{x}(\,\mathrm{d}x)=r(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=Z_{x}^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-U(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\lvert v\rvert^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}v.

If
p_{t}(x,v)
denotes the Lebesgue density of the law of the underdamped
Langevin diffusion, then
p_{t}
evolves according to the Fokker–Planck equation

(2.1)

\partial_{t}p_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}p_{t}-\nabla U(x)\cdot\nabla_{v}p_{t}=\gamma\nabla_{v}\cdot\bigl(vp_{t}+\nabla_{v}p_{t}\bigr).

Let
\varrho_{\infty}(x,v)=r(x)(2\pi)^{-d/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\lvert v\rvert^{2}/2}
denote the Lebesgue
density of
\mu
. It is more convenient to work with the density ratio

g_{t}=p_{t}/\varrho_{\infty}
, which satisfies

(2.2)

\partial_{t}g=-\mathcal{L}_{a}g+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{s}g,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{a}=v\cdot\nabla_{x}-\nabla U\cdot\nabla_{v},\qquad\mathcal{L}_{s}=\Delta_{v}-v\cdot\nabla_{v}.

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}
is skew-adjoint in
L^{2}(\mu)
, and
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is the symmetric Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator in
v
.

Adding a constant to
U
changes only the normalizing constant
Z_{x}
, so without loss of generality we assume
U\geq 0
. In addition, we make the following standing assumptions on
U
.

Assumption 2.1

(Convex potential with logarithmic Sobolev inequality)
.

The potential
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is convex on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the probability
measure
\mu_{x}=Z_{x}^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-U}\,\mathrm{d}x
satisfies the logarithmic Sobolev
inequality with constant
\rho>0
:

(2.3)

\Ent_{\mu_{x}}(f)\leq\frac{1}{2\rho}\int\frac{\lvert\nabla f\rvert^{2}}{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x},\qquad\forall\,f\geq 0,\qquad\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=1.

We also impose the following quantitative regularity bounds on
U
, following
Hérau–Nier
[
22
]
; these are used only to invoke the kinetic
Fokker–Planck semigroup theory in
Section

7
. The bounds
(
2.4
)–(
2.5
) below coincide
with Hypothesis 1 of
[
22
]
after a physical-parameter rescaling.

Assumption 2.2

(Tame confining potential)
.

The potential
U
satisfies the following quantitative confining bounds for some
n\geq 1
. Write
\langle x\rangle=(1+\lvert x\rvert^{2})^{1/2}
.

(A1)

There are constants
C_{\alpha}<\infty
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, such that

(2.4)

\lvert\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U(x)\rvert\leq C_{\alpha}\left(1+\langle x\rangle^{2n-\min\{\lvert\alpha\rvert,2\}}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In particular,
U
has at most polynomial growth of order
2n
,
\nabla U
has
at most order
2n-1
, and all derivatives of order at least two have at most
order
2n-2
.

(A2)

There are constants
0<C_{0},C_{1}<\infty
such that

(2.5)

U(x)\geq C_{0}^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{2n}-C_{0},\qquad\lvert\nabla U(x)\rvert\geq C_{1}^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{2n-1}-C_{1},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Consequently
Z_{x}=\int\mathrm{e}^{-U(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty
.

Our main result is the following sharp entropy hypocoercivity decay estimate. The remainder of this section sketches the proof idea and sets up the notation and the regularity class used in its proof.

Theorem 2.3

(Entropy hypocoercivity)
.

Assume Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
. Let
\gamma=\Gamma\sqrt{\rho}
with
\Gamma>0
and choose

(2.6)

0<\theta\leq\min\left\{\frac{\Gamma}{12},\ \frac{1}{4\Gamma}\right\}.

Set

(2.7)

\lambda_{\Gamma}=\frac{\theta}{2(1+\theta)}.

Then every finite-entropy initial density
g_{0}\geq 0
,
\int g_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=1
, satisfies

(2.8)

\Ent_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}_{t}g_{0})\leq\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}\exp\{-\lambda_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\rho}\,t\}\Ent_{\mu}(g_{0}),\qquad t\geq 0,

where
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is the semigroup generated by (
2.2
).

The proof uses a modified entropy functional in the spirit of the modified
L^{2}
approach of
[
15
]
. We first introduce notation
for decomposing a density
g
into its marginal and conditional parts. Let

\Pi_{v}
denote averaging over the velocity variable against
\kappa
, that is,

(2.9)

(\Pi_{v}\phi)(x)=\int\phi(x,v)\,\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v)

for any scalar-, vector-, or tensor-valued function
\phi
for which the integral makes sense. For a probability density
g
with respect to
\mu
, set its spatial marginal

(2.10)

q=\Pi_{v}g.

On the set
\{x\mid q(x)>0\}
write the conditional density

(2.11)

h_{x}(v)=\frac{g(x,v)}{q(x)}.

If
\Pi_{v}(\lvert v\rvert\,g)(x)<\infty
for
\mu_{x}
-a.e.
x
, define

(2.12)

j=\Pi_{v}(v\,g),\qquad m(x)=\frac{j(x)}{q(x)}.

On the set
\{q=0\}
choose
h_{x}
to be
1
and set
j=0
and
m=0
.
If the first moment
\Pi_{v}(\lvert v\rvert\,g)(x)
fails to be finite on a set of positive

q\mu_{x}
-measure, all quantities involving
j
or
m
are assigned the value

+\infty
unless a separate regularity assumption supplies these fields. In
particular, finite conditional entropy implies existence of the first moment by Lemma
3.1
below.

Similarly, when

\Pi_{v}(\lvert v\rvert^{2}g)(x)<\infty
for
\mu_{x}
-a.e.
x
, define the second-moment
field and the (centered) stress tensor

(2.13)

M=\Pi_{v}(v\otimes v\,g),\qquad\Theta=M-\frac{j\otimes j}{q}-q\,I_{d},

with
j\otimes j/q
interpreted as
0
on
\{q=0\}
. By construction,

I_{d}+\Theta/q
coincides with the conditional covariance of
v
given
x
on

\{q>0\}
; in particular
\Theta
is symmetric and
I_{d}+\Theta/q\geq 0
a.e.

With the convention above, the relative entropy admits the decomposition

(2.14)

\Ent(g):=\Ent_{\mu}(g)=\Ent_{x}(q)+\Ent_{v}(g),

where

(2.15)

\Ent_{x}(q):=\Ent_{\mu_{x}}(q),\qquad\Ent_{v}(g):=\int q(x)\Ent_{\kappa}(h_{x})\,\mu_{x}(\,\mathrm{d}x).

The identity (
2.14
) is understood in the extended sense; if

\Ent(g)<\infty
, both terms on the right are finite.

Whenever
\Ent_{x}(q)<\infty
, Talagrand’s inequality following from
the log-Sobolev inequality (
2.3
) gives
W_{2}(q\mu_{x},\mu_{x})<\infty
; see
[
25
]
and

[
19
]
. Since
q\mu_{x}\ll\,\mathrm{d}x
, Brenier’s theorem gives a

q\mu_{x}
-a.e. unique optimal map
T_{q}=\nabla\varphi_{q}
transporting
q\mu_{x}

to
\mu_{x}
; see
[
7
]
and
[
27
]
. Denote

(2.16)

\xi_{q}(x)=x-T_{q}(x).

We also use the following spatial notation throughout the paper. For vector
fields
F
, let

(2.17)

\nabla_{x}^{*}F=-\divop_{x}F+\nabla U\cdot F

be the adjoint of
\nabla_{x}
in
L^{2}(\mu_{x})
. For matrix fields
M
,

\nabla_{x}^{*}M
is understood row by row:

(\nabla_{x}^{*}M)_{i}=-\sum_{k}\partial_{x_{k}}M_{ik}+\sum_{k}(\partial_{x_{k}}U)M_{ik}.

For some
\epsilon>0
to be specified later, we define the Wasserstein current corrector and modified entropy as

(2.18)

\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g)=\int j(x)\cdot\xi_{q}(x)\,\mu_{x}(\,\mathrm{d}x),\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(g)=\Ent(g)+\epsilon\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g),

whenever the integral is finite. Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2

below show that this is automatic for finite entropy.

The intuition behind this choice of corrector is that
\xi_{q}=x-T_{q}
is the Wasserstein gradient, with respect to the first argument
q\mu_{x}
, of
\frac{1}{2}W_{2}^{2}(q\mu_{x},\mu_{x})
.
Formally, along a smooth solution, if
\nu_{t}=q_{t}\mu_{x}
and
m_{t}=j_{t}/q_{t}
, then the marginal equation is the continuity equation

\partial_{t}\nu_{t}+\divop(m_{t}\nu_{t})=0,

and the first-variation formula for
D(t)=\frac{1}{2}W_{2}^{2}(\nu_{t},\mu_{x})
gives
(see
Section

4
)

D^{\prime}(t)=\int\xi_{q_{t}}\cdot m_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}=\int\xi_{q_{t}}\cdot j_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t}).

Thus the corrector pairs the position error, measured by the optimal
transport displacement back to
\mu_{x}
, with the velocity current that moves the
position marginal. It is the Wasserstein analogue of the position–velocity
cross term in classical hypocoercivity: its derivative produces the missing
coercive contribution in
\Ent_{x}(q_{t})
due to displacement convexity of the relative entropy
[
27
,
24
]
, while its size remains controlled by the entropy through the current bound and Talagrand’s inequality, as stated in Proposition
2.4
.

We will establish exponential decay of the modified entropy
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}
and
its equivalence with the relative entropy
\Ent
for
\epsilon
small enough. The
key estimate is stated first for regular finite-entropy solutions. This
regularity class is used to separate the presentation of key ideas and calculations from technical details; the precise definition is placed after the proof sketch, and the result for arbitrary finite-entropy
data is obtained by approximation in
Section

7
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
, let
\gamma
and

\theta
be as in Theorem
2.3
, and set
\epsilon=\theta\sqrt{\rho}
.
For every regular
finite-entropy solution, the corrector
\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})
is
well-defined and

(2.19)

(1-\theta)\Ent(g_{t})\leq\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(g_{t})\leq(1+\theta)\Ent(g_{t}),

and
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}
satisfies

(2.20)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(g_{t})\leq-\lambda_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\rho}\,\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(g_{t})

in the sense of distributions on the time interval.

Proof Sketch

The two assertions in Proposition
2.4
are proved as follows.

The equivalence (
2.19
) is a direct consequence of the
corrector bound
\lvert\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g)\rvert\leq\rho^{-1/2}\Ent(g)
established
in Lemma
3.2
, combined with the choice
\epsilon=\theta\sqrt{\rho}
.

For the differential inequality (
2.20
), write

\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(g_{t})=\Ent(g_{t})+\epsilon\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})
and differentiate
the two terms separately. The entropy dissipation identity
(
3.1
) gives

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Ent(g_{t})=-\gamma I_{v}(g_{t}).

The time derivative of the Wasserstein corrector

\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})
is obtained from
the distributional Wasserstein second-variation inequality for the squared
distance
W_{2}^{2}(q_{t}\mu_{x},\mu_{x})
(
Section

4
), together with the
stress estimate for Brenier maps (
Section

5
), which controls the
singular contribution of the Hessian of the convex transport potential.
Assembling these two pieces yields (see (
6.2
))

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})\leq-\Ent_{x}(q_{t})-\gamma\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})+3I_{v}(g_{t}).

Combining the two derivatives and using the corrector
bound to absorb the residual terms closes the Lyapunov inequality
(
Section

6
) and gives (
2.20
) with the explicit rate

\lambda_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\rho}
.

Theorem
2.3
for arbitrary finite-entropy initial data is then obtained from Proposition
2.4
by entropy-dense approximation with regular solutions and by lower semicontinuity of the entropy and Fisher information; this is carried out in
Section

7
.

Regularity class used in the proof

The regularity class used in Proposition
2.4
is the
following. It is not an additional assumption on the initial datum in
Theorem
2.3
; it is only used to facilitate the various calculations and estimates.

(Regular finite-entropy solution)
.

Let

be a compact interval with nonempty interior. A solution

of (

) on

(R1)

There is
c_{I}>0
such that

for all

. Moreover

,

solves
(

) pointwise,
\Ent(g_{t})<\infty
,

, and

is absolutely continuous with

(2.21)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Ent(g_{t})=-\gamma I_{v}(g_{t})\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }t\in I.

All integrations by parts in

(R2)

The fields

are

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{x}q_{t}+\nabla_{x}^{*}\left(\frac{j_{t}\otimes j_{t}}{q_{t}}\right)+\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta_{t}-\gamma j_{t}

). The

(R3)

The continuity equation

has the classical characteristic representation on

exists globally, transports

to

, and satisfies

(2.24)

X_{s}(x)=x+sm_{t}(x)+\frac{s^{2}}{2}a_{t}(x)+o_{L^{2}(q_{t}\mu_{x})}(s^{2}),\qquad a_{t}=\partial_{t}m_{t}+(m_{t}\cdot\nabla)m_{t}.

The remainder is locally uniform for
t
in compact subsets of the interior of

I
, and
m_{t},a_{t}\in L^{2}(q_{t}\mu_{x})
locally uniformly in
t
.

(R4)

Let

. The fields

and
\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta_{t}
belong to

locally

. There exists a standard sequence of cutoff functions

\displaystyle\int\chi_{R}\nabla q_{t}\cdot\xi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}\longrightarrow\int\nabla q_{t}\cdot\xi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x},

\displaystyle\int\xi_{t}\cdot\nabla_{x}^{*}(\chi_{R}\Theta_{t})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}\longrightarrow\int\xi_{t}\cdot\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x},

3.
Entropy, current, and size of the corrector

For regular solutions,

(3.1)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Ent(g_{t})=-\gamma I_{v}(g_{t}),

where
I_{v}
is the velocity Fisher information, given by

(3.2)

I_{v}(g)=\int\frac{\lvert\nabla_{v}g\rvert^{2}}{g}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

For general finite-entropy initial data, the corresponding integrated
inequality is obtained by approximation from the regular identity (see
Corollary
7.5
below):

(3.3)

\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{t}g_{0})+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}I_{v}(\mathcal{P}_{s}g_{0})\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\Ent(g_{0}),\qquad t\geq 0.

The Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality of Gross
[
20
]
, applied fiberwise, gives

(3.4)

\Ent_{v}(g)\leq\frac{1}{2}I_{v}(g).

For the spatial marginal
q=\Pi_{v}g
and current
j=\Pi_{v}(vg)
, define the average current energy

(3.5)

J(g)=\int\frac{\lvert j(x)\rvert^{2}}{q(x)}\,\mu_{x}(\,\mathrm{d}x).

Recall the convention
j=0
on
\{q=0\}
. If the conditional first moment in
(
2.12
) is not finite on a set of positive
q\mu_{x}
-measure, then

J(g)
is defined to be
+\infty
.

Lemma 3.1

(Current bounds)
.

For every probability density ratio
g
,

(3.6)

J(g)\leq 2\Ent_{v}(g),\qquad J(g)\leq I_{v}(g),

where the inequalities are understood in the extended sense. In particular,
finite velocity entropy (and hence finite total entropy) makes the current
j

well-defined and gives
J(g)<\infty
.

Proof.

Disintegrate
g\mu
as
q(x)\mu_{x}(\,\mathrm{d}x)h_{x}(v)\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v)
. For fixed
x

with
q(x)>0
and
\Ent_{\kappa}(h_{x})<\infty
, use the variational formula

(3.7)

\Ent_{\kappa}(h_{x})=\sup_{\phi}\left\{\int\phi h_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\kappa-\log\int\mathrm{e}^{\phi}\,\mathrm{d}\kappa\right\},

or equivalently

\int\phi h_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\kappa\leq\Ent_{\kappa}(h_{x})+\log\int\mathrm{e}^{\phi}\,\mathrm{d}\kappa

whenever the exponential moment on the right is finite. Applying this to

\phi(v)=\lambda\lvert v\rvert
with any
\lambda>0
first gives

\int\lvert v\rvert h_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\kappa<\infty
, since Gaussian measures have finite
exponential moments of linear growth. Applying it next to
\phi(v)=a\cdot v

then yields

a\cdot\int vh_{x}(v)\,\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v)\leq\Ent_{\kappa}(h_{x})+\log\int\mathrm{e}^{a\cdot v}\,\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v)=\Ent_{\kappa}(h_{x})+\frac{\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{2}.

Optimizing over
a
gives

\Bigl\lvert\int vh_{x}(v)\,\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v)\Bigr\rvert^{2}\leq 2\Ent_{\kappa}(h_{x}).

Since
j(x)=q(x)\int vh_{x}(v)\,\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v)
on
\{q>0\}
and
j=0
on
\{q=0\}
, multiplication by
q
and integration gives
J\leq 2\Ent_{v}
. The Fisher-information bound
J\leq I_{v}
then follows by combining this with the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
3.4
).
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Corrector bound)
.

For every finite-entropy probability density ratio
g
,

(3.8)

\lvert\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g)\rvert\leq\sqrt{J(g)}\,W_{2}(q\mu_{x},\mu_{x})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\Ent(g).

Moreover, if
I_{v}(g)<\infty
, then

(3.9)

\lvert\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g)\rvert\leq\sqrt{I_{v}(g)}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho}\Ent_{x}(q)}.

Proof.

Talagrand’s inequality following from the log-Sobolev inequality (
2.3
)
yields (see
[
25
]
and
[
19
]
)

(3.10)

W_{2}^{2}(q\mu_{x},\mu_{x})\leq\frac{2}{\rho}\Ent_{x}(q).

The Brenier map realizes the Wasserstein-
2
distance, so

(3.11)

\int q\lvert\xi_{q}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=W_{2}^{2}(q\mu_{x},\mu_{x}).

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Lemma
3.1
give

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{J(g)}\,W_{2}(q\mu_{x},\mu_{x})\leq\sqrt{2\Ent_{v}(g)}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho}\Ent_{x}(q)}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{-1/2}(\Ent_{x}(q)+\Ent_{v}(g))=\rho^{-1/2}\Ent(g),

where the last inequality uses Young’s inequality.
The estimate (
3.9
) follows in the same way from
J\leq I_{v}
.
∎

With
\epsilon=\theta\sqrt{\rho}
, (
3.8
) immediately implies the
equivalence (
2.19
) for every finite-entropy density.

4.
Moment equations and the Wasserstein acceleration inequality

We begin by recording the moment equations for
q
and
j
, using the adjoint

\nabla_{x}^{*}
from (
2.17
). With
M
and
\Theta
defined in
(
2.13
), set

S=M-q\,I_{d}=\frac{j\otimes j}{q}+\Theta
. For regular solutions, integrating
(
2.2
) against
1
and
v
gives

(4.1)

\partial_{t}q=\nabla_{x}^{*}j,

(4.2)

\partial_{t}j=-\nabla_{x}q+\nabla_{x}^{*}S-\gamma j,

which, in terms of
\Theta
and
m=j/q
, reads

(4.3)

\partial_{t}j=-\nabla_{x}q+\nabla_{x}^{*}\left(\frac{j\otimes j}{q}\right)+\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta-\gamma j.

Write
\nu_{t}=q_{t}\mu_{x}
and
T_{t}=T_{q_{t}}
for the Brenier map from
\nu_{t}
to

\mu_{x}
, and set
\xi_{t}=x-T_{t}
. The equation (
4.1
) is equivalent to the
continuity equation

(4.4)

\partial_{t}\nu_{t}+\divop(m_{t}\nu_{t})=0,\qquad m_{t}=\frac{j_{t}}{q_{t}}.

Define

(4.5)

D(t)=\frac{1}{2}W_{2}^{2}(\nu_{t},\mu_{x}),\qquad C(t)=\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})=\int\xi_{t}\cdot m_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}.

By Definition
2.5

(R3)
,
m_{t}\in L^{2}(q_{t}\mu_{x})

locally uniformly in time and (
4.4
) holds in the classical
distributional sense. The
Benamou–Brenier continuity-equation estimate therefore implies that

t\mapsto\nu_{t}
is a
2
-absolutely continuous curve in
(\mathcal{P}_{2},W_{2})
,
with metric derivative bounded by
\lVert m_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}(\nu_{t})}
. For absolutely
continuous Wasserstein curves, the first-variation formula for the squared
Wasserstein distance applies; see, for instance,
[
3
]
and

[
27
]
. Hence

(4.6)

D^{\prime}(t)=C(t)\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }t.

Next we calculate the Wasserstein acceleration, i.e., the second derivative of
D(t)
.

Lemma 4.1

(Acceleration inequality)
.

For every regular solution, the following inequality holds in the sense of
distributions in time:

(4.7)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})\leq J(g_{t})+\int\xi_{t}\cdot\left[\partial_{t}j_{t}-\nabla_{x}^{*}\left(\frac{j_{t}\otimes j_{t}}{q_{t}}\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}.

If the Wasserstein curve is twice differentiable at
t
and the right-hand side
of (
4.7
) is finite at that time, the same inequality holds
pointwise.

Proof.

Fix
t
in the interior of the time interval. By
Definition
2.5

(R3)
, the flow
X_{s}
generated by

m_{t+s}
satisfies
(X_{s})_{\#}\nu_{t}=\nu_{t+s}
and

X_{s}(x)=x+sm_{t}(x)+\frac{s^{2}}{2}a_{t}(x)+o_{L^{2}(\nu_{t})}(s^{2}),\qquad a_{t}=\partial_{t}m_{t}+(m_{t}\cdot\nabla)m_{t}.

Since
(X_{s})_{\#}\nu_{t}=\nu_{t+s}
and
(T_{t})_{\#}\nu_{t}=\mu_{x}
, the map

x\mapsto(X_{s}(x),T_{t}(x))
defines an admissible coupling of
\nu_{t+s}
and

\mu_{x}
. Therefore

\displaystyle D(t+s)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\lvert X_{s}(x)-T_{t}(x)\rvert^{2}\,\nu_{t}(\,\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=D(t)+s\int\xi_{t}\cdot m_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}\int\bigl(\lvert m_{t}\rvert^{2}+\xi_{t}\cdot a_{t}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}+o(s^{2}).

The
o(s^{2})
term follows from the locally uniform
L^{2}(\nu_{t})
remainder in
(
2.24
), together with the local
L^{2}(\nu_{t})
bounds on

\xi_{t}
,
m_{t}
, and
a_{t}
. Define

Q(t)=\int\bigl(\lvert m_{t}\rvert^{2}+\xi_{t}\cdot a_{t}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}.

Applying the estimates with
s=h
and
s=-h
gives

D(t+h)+D(t-h)-2D(t)\leq h^{2}Q(t)+o(h^{2})

locally uniformly. Let
\omega\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I)
be nonnegative. Multiplying by

\omega(t)
, integrating in
t
, changing variables in the two shifted terms, and
letting
h\downarrow 0
yields

(4.8)

\int D(t)\omega^{\prime\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int Q(t)\omega(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Thus
D^{\prime\prime}\leq Q
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(I)
. Combining this with
(
4.6
) gives
C^{\prime}\leq Q
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(I)
.

It remains to identify the expression of
Q(t)
more explicitly. Recall that
r(x)=Z_{x}^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-U(x)}
is the
Lebesgue density of
\mu_{x}
, so the continuity equation (
4.4
)
for
\nu_{t}=q_{t}\mu_{x}
reads

\partial_{t}(rq_{t})+\divop(rq_{t}m_{t})=0,

and the conservative identity is

\partial_{t}(rq_{t}m_{t})+\divop(rq_{t}m_{t}\otimes m_{t})=rq_{t}a_{t}.

Dividing by
r
and using the definition of
\nabla_{x}^{*}
gives

(4.9)

q_{t}a_{t}=\partial_{t}j_{t}-\nabla_{x}^{*}\left(\frac{j_{t}\otimes j_{t}}{q_{t}}\right).

Finally,
\int\lvert m_{t}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}=J(g_{t})
, so
C^{\prime}\leq Q
is exactly
(
4.7
).
∎

Using (
4.3
) in (
4.7
), we obtain, in the sense of
distributions,

(4.10)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})\leq J(g_{t})-\gamma\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})-\int\nabla q_{t}\cdot\xi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}+\int\xi_{t}\cdot\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}.

The last two spatial terms on the right-hand side will be estimated next.

5.
Stress estimate for Brenier maps

The purpose of this section is to estimate the two spatial terms in
(
4.10
). For a density
q
with Brenier map
T_{q}
and

\xi_{q}=x-T_{q}
, and for a stress field
\Theta
, as defined in (
2.13
), write

(5.1)

\mathfrak{A}(q)=\int\nabla q\cdot\xi_{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x},\qquad\mathfrak{S}(q,\Theta)=\int\xi_{q}\cdot\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x},

whenever the integrals are well-defined. Our goal is to bound the combination

-\mathfrak{A}(q)+\mathfrak{S}(q,\Theta)
by entropy terms.

We first record the fiberwise Gaussian estimate used to control the conditional
covariance.

Lemma 5.1

(Conditional covariance duality)
.

Let
0<\beta<1/2
. Let
h
be a probability density with respect to
\kappa
,
with mean
m
and covariance matrix
\Sigma
. Let
K=K^{T}\leq I_{d}
. Then

(5.2)

(\Sigma-I_{d}):K\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\Ent_{\kappa}(h)+\frac{1}{\beta}\Lambda_{\beta}(K),

where

(5.3)

\Lambda_{\beta}(K)=\log\int\exp\{\beta((z\otimes z-I_{d}):K)\}\,\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}z).

If the eigenvalues of
K
are
1-\lambda_{i}
with
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
, then

(5.4)

\frac{1}{\beta}\Lambda_{\beta}(K)\leq\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-1-\log\lambda_{i}),

with the convention that the right-hand side is
+\infty
when some
\lambda_{i}=0
.

Proof.

Let
Y
have law
h\kappa
and let
W=Y-m
. If
\bar{h}
is the density of
W

with respect to
\kappa
, then

\Ent_{\kappa}(\bar{h})=\Ent_{\kappa}(h)-\frac{\lvert m\rvert^{2}}{2}\leq\Ent_{\kappa}(h).

The entropy variational formula (
3.7
), applied to
\bar{h}
and

\beta((z\otimes z-I):K)
under
\kappa
, yields (
5.2
); the
condition
K\leq I_{d}
and
\beta<1/2
ensure that the Gaussian exponential moment
is finite.

Diagonalizing
K
gives

\Lambda_{\beta}(K)=-\beta\tr K-\frac{1}{2}\log\det(I_{d}-2\beta K).

If the eigenvalues of
K
are
1-\lambda_{i}
, then

\Lambda_{\beta}(K)=\sum_{i}\left[\beta(\lambda_{i}-1)-\frac{1}{2}\log\bigl(1+2\beta(\lambda_{i}-1)\bigr)\right].

Since
0<2\beta<1
, concavity of
s\mapsto s^{2\beta}
gives

1+2\beta(\lambda-1)\geq\lambda^{2\beta},\qquad\lambda\geq 0.

This proves (
5.4
).
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Localized covariance bound)
.

Let
0<\beta<1/2
. Let
G=G^{T}\geq 0
,
0\leq\chi\leq 1
, and

K_{\chi}=\chi(I_{d}-G)
. If
h
has covariance
\Sigma
, then

(5.5)

(\Sigma-I_{d}):\chi(I_{d}-G)\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\Ent_{\kappa}(h)+\chi\bigl(\tr G-d-\log\det G\bigr),

with the convention that the last term is
+\infty
if
\det G=0
.

Proof.

The eigenvalues of
K_{\chi}
are
1-\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}
, where

\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}=(1-\chi)+\chi\lambda_{i},

and
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
G
. Applying Lemma
5.1
to
K_{\chi}
gives the entropy term plus

\sum_{i}\bigl(\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}-1-\log\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}\bigr).

The function
f(s)=s-1-\log s
is convex on
(0,\infty)
and
f(1)=0
, hence

f\bigl((1-\chi)\cdot 1+\chi\lambda_{i}\bigr)\leq\chi f(\lambda_{i}).

Summing over
i
gives (
5.5
); the singular case follows by monotone limiting.
∎

Lemma 5.3

(Stress bound)
.

Assume that
U
is convex and satisfies Assumption
2.2
. Let
q
be a positive
C^{\infty}
probability density with respect to
\mu_{x}
,
with
\Ent_{x}(q)<\infty
. Let
T=T_{q}=\nabla\varphi
be the Brenier map from

q\mu_{x}
to
\mu_{x}
, and write
\xi=x-T
. Let

g(x,v)=q(x)h_{x}(v)
be a probability density with respect to

\mu_{x}\otimes\kappa
such that the conditional mean

m(x)=\int vh_{x}(v)\,\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v)

and the conditional covariance matrix

\Sigma(x)=\int(v-m(x))\otimes(v-m(x))h_{x}(v)\,\kappa(\,\mathrm{d}v)

exist for a.e.
x
, and set
\Theta=q(\Sigma-I_{d})
. Assume that
\Theta\in C^{1}_{\rm loc}

as a matrix field. For
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
define the localized pairings by the integrals

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{\chi}(q)

\displaystyle=\int\chi\nabla q\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x},

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\chi}(q,\Theta)

\displaystyle=\int\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}^{*}(\chi\Theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}.

Assume that the following limits exist for a standard cutoff sequence

\chi_{R}
:

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(q)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathfrak{A}_{\chi_{R}}(q),

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(q,\Theta)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathfrak{S}_{\chi_{R}}(q,\Theta),

(5.10)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int q\nabla\chi_{R}\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}.

Then, for every

0<\beta<1/2
,

(5.11)

-\mathfrak{A}(q)+\mathfrak{S}(q,\Theta)\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\Ent_{v}(g)-\Ent_{x}(q).

Proof.

We use the standard BV facts for gradients of convex functions and the
Alexandrov Monge–Ampère identity for Brenier maps; see

[
2
]
for BV integration by parts,
[
17
]
for Alexandrov
second derivatives and Hessian measures of convex functions, and

[
27
]
for Brenier maps and the Alexandrov change-of-variables
formula. Since

T=\nabla\varphi
with
\varphi
convex, its
distributional derivative is the positive semidefinite matrix-valued Radon
measure

DT=D^{2}\varphi=G(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+D^{s}T,\qquad D^{s}T=N\,\sigma,

where
G(x)
is the Alexandrov derivative and
N(x)
is symmetric positive
semidefinite for
\sigma
-a.e.
x
. The Alexandrov Monge–Ampère identity
for
T_{\#}(q\mu_{x})=\mu_{x}
is

(5.12)

q(x)r(x)=r(T(x))\det G(x)\qquad q\mu_{x}\text{-a.e.}

Since
q>0
and
r>0
, this identity gives
\det G>0
for
q\mu_{x}
-a.e.
x
and
therefore

(5.13)

\log q(x)=U(x)-U(T(x))+\log\det G(x)\qquad q\mu_{x}\text{-a.e.}

Fix
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
0\leq\chi\leq 1
. The compactly supported field

r\chi\Theta
is
C^{1}
, while
\xi=x-T
is locally BV. Since

(\nabla_{x}^{*}(\chi\Theta))r=-\divop(r\chi\Theta)
row by row, the BV
integration-by-parts formula gives

(5.14)

\mathfrak{S}_{\chi}(q,\Theta)=\int\chi\Theta:(I_{d}-G)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}-\int r\chi\Theta:N\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

For the absolutely continuous part, apply Lemma
5.2

pointwise with
\Sigma=I_{d}+\Theta/q
:

(5.15)

\chi\Theta:(I_{d}-G)\leq\frac{q}{\beta}\Ent_{\kappa}(h_{x})+\chi q\bigl(\tr G-d-\log\det G\bigr)\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }x.

For the singular part,
I_{d}+\Theta/q=\Sigma\geq 0
and
N\geq 0
imply

(5.16)

-r\chi\Theta:N=r\chi q\bigl(I_{d}-\Sigma\bigr):N\leq r\chi q\tr N.

Combining (
5.14
)–(
5.16
) yields

(5.17)

\mathfrak{S}_{\chi}(q,\Theta)\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\Ent_{v}(g)+\mathfrak{B}_{\chi}(q),

where

(5.18)

\mathfrak{B}_{\chi}(q):=\int\chi q\bigl(\tr G-d-\log\det G\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}+\int r\chi q\tr N\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

The estimate is localized. On
\supp\chi
, the smooth density
q
and the
weight
r
are bounded above and below, while
D^{2}\varphi
is a finite
matrix-valued Radon measure. The singular trace term is therefore finite on

\supp\chi
. The term
-\log\det G
is locally integrable in the extended sense
because (
5.13
) rewrites it as
-\log q+U-U\circ T
. Here
q
is bounded above and below and
U
is bounded
on
\supp\chi
, and the only potentially nonlocal term is harmless: since

0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
U\geq 0
, and

T_{\#}(q\mu_{x})=\mu_{x}
,

\int U(T)\chi q\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}\leq\int U(T)q\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=\int U\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}<\infty,

the last finiteness following from the polynomial upper growth of
U
in
(
2.4
) and the confining lower bound in
(
2.5
). Thus the localized quantities are well-defined.

Using
r\nabla q=\nabla(qr)+qr\nabla U
and applying the scalar BV
integration-by-parts formula to
\xi=x-T
gives

(5.19)

\mathfrak{A}_{\chi}(q)=\int\chi q\bigl(\tr G-d+\nabla U(x)\cdot(x-T(x))\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}+\int r\chi q\tr N\,\mathrm{d}\sigma-\int q\nabla\chi\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}.

Let
\Ent_{\chi}(q)=\int\chi q\log q\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}
. Subtracting
(
5.19
) from (
5.18
) and using (
5.13
) gives

(5.20)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{\chi}(q)-\mathfrak{A}_{\chi}(q)

\displaystyle=-\Ent_{\chi}(q)-\int\chi q\Bigl[\nabla U(x)\cdot(x-T(x))-U(x)+U(T(x))\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}

\displaystyle+\int q\nabla\chi\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}.

Convexity of
U
gives

\nabla U(x)\cdot(x-T(x))-U(x)+U(T(x))\geq 0.

Therefore (

) and (

)
combine to the localized inequality

(5.21)

\mathfrak{S}_{\chi}(q,\Theta)-\mathfrak{A}_{\chi}(q)\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\Ent_{v}(g)-\Ent_{\chi}(q)+\int q\nabla\chi\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}.

Choose the cutoff sequence
\chi_{R}
. The assumptions
(

)–(

) give convergence
of the two localized left-hand terms and of the boundary term. Since

\Ent_{x}(q)<\infty
, the positive part of

is integrable and the negative

; hence
\Ent_{\chi_{R}}(q)\to\Ent_{x}(q)
by dominated
convergence applied separately to the positive and negative parts. Letting

R\to\infty
in (

(
5.11
).
∎

6.
Entropy dissipation estimate

For a regular solution, Lemma
5.3
applied at time
t
with

\Theta=\Theta_{t}
and the cutoff convention in Definition

(R4)
identifies

\mathfrak{A}(q_{t})
and
\mathfrak{S}(q_{t},\Theta_{t})
with the two spatial terms
in (
4.10
). We now
take
\beta=\tfrac{1}{4}
in (
5.11
). Combining
(
4.10
) with Lemma

gives, in the sense of
distributions in time,

(6.1)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})\leq-\Ent_{x}(q_{t})-\gamma\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})+J(g_{t})+4\Ent_{v}(g_{t}).

By Lemma

and (

),

(6.2)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})\leq-\Ent_{x}(q_{t})-\gamma\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})+3I_{v}(g_{t}).

Together with (

), this yields

(6.3)

-\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(g_{t})\geq(\gamma-3\epsilon)I_{v}(g_{t})+\epsilon\Ent_{x}(q_{t})+\epsilon\gamma\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t}).

The last term is controlled by (
3.9
):

(6.4)

\epsilon\gamma\mathcal{C}_{\rm OT}(g_{t})\geq-\epsilon\gamma\sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho}I_{v}(g_{t})\Ent_{x}(q_{t})}.

Using
\gamma=\Gamma\sqrt{\rho}
and
\epsilon=\theta\sqrt{\rho}

-\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(g_{t})\geq\sqrt{\rho}\Bigl[(\Gamma-3\theta)I_{v}+\theta\Ent_{x}-\theta\Gamma\sqrt{2I_{v}\Ent_{x}}\Bigr].

The choice of
\theta
in (

\Gamma-3\theta\geq\frac{3\Gamma}{4},

and, by Young’s inequality together with

7.
Removal of regularity assumptions

This section removes the regularity assumptions by approximation, extending the
estimates from regular solutions to arbitrary finite-entropy data.
We rely on the positive-time weighted smoothing theorem of Hérau–Nier

[
22
]
. We first put their operator in the normalization used here.
Let

\varrho_{\infty}(x,v)=Z_{x}^{-1}(2\pi)^{-d/2}\exp\{-U(x)-\lvert v\rvert^{2}/2\}

be the Lebesgue density of
\mu
. Consider the Lebesgue-density equation

(7.1)

\partial_{t}p_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}p_{t}-\nabla U\cdot\nabla_{v}p_{t}-\gamma\nabla_{v}\cdot(\nabla_{v}p_{t}+vp_{t})=0.

If
p_{t}
solves (
7.1
) and

f_{t}=\varrho_{\infty}^{-1/2}p_{t}
, then a direct conjugation gives

(7.2)

\partial_{t}f_{t}+\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}f_{t}=0,

where

(7.3)

\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}=v\cdot\nabla_{x}-\nabla U\cdot\nabla_{v}+\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(-\partial_{v_{j}}+\frac{v_{j}}{2}\right)\left(\partial_{v_{j}}+\frac{v_{j}}{2}\right).

Indeed, since

\nabla_{v}\varrho_{\infty}^{1/2}=-(v/2)\varrho_{\infty}^{1/2}
,

(\nabla_{v}+v)(\varrho_{\infty}^{1/2}f)=\varrho_{\infty}^{1/2}(\nabla_{v}+v/2)f,

and therefore

-\varrho_{\infty}^{-1/2}\nabla_{v}\cdot\bigl((\nabla_{v}+v)(\varrho_{\infty}^{1/2}f)\bigr)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(-\partial_{v_{j}}+\frac{v_{j}}{2}\right)\left(\partial_{v_{j}}+\frac{v_{j}}{2}\right)f.

For the Hamiltonian part,

\varrho_{\infty}^{-1/2}(v\cdot\nabla_{x}-\nabla U\cdot\nabla_{v})(\varrho_{\infty}^{1/2}f)=(v\cdot\nabla_{x}-\nabla U\cdot\nabla_{v})f,

because

v\cdot\nabla_{x}(U+\lvert v\rvert^{2}/2)-\nabla U\cdot\nabla_{v}(U+\lvert v\rvert^{2}/2)=0
.

Lemma 7.1

(Hérau–Nier smoothing)
.

Assume Assumption
2.2
(A1)
,
(A2)
.
For every
f_{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the weak Cauchy problem
(
7.2
) with initial datum
f_{0}
admits the unique
semigroup solution

f_{t}=\mathrm{e}^{-t\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}}f_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),\qquad t>0.

Moreover
t\mapsto f_{t}
belongs to
C^{\infty}((0,\infty);\mathcal{S})
, and for
every compact interval
I_{0}\subset(0,\infty)
and every
N,k\geq 0
,

(7.4)

\sup_{t\in I_{0}}\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert+\lvert\eta\rvert\leq k}\sup_{x,v}(1+\lvert x\rvert+\lvert v\rvert)^{N}\lvert\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\eta}f_{t}(x,v)\rvert<\infty.

Proof.

The operator
\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}
is exactly Hérau–Nier’s normalized
Fokker–Planck operator with
m=\beta=1
. The growth hypotheses are their
Hypothesis 1 in the positive-potential case; compare
(
2.4
)–(
2.5
) with
Hypothesis 1 of
[
22
]
. If
f_{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, choose

r\in\mathbb{R}
with

in the isotropic Sobolev scale of

[
22
]
. Hérau–Nier’s Theorem 0.1(1) and Corollary 3.3
give the unique weak Cauchy semigroup solution in that space, and their
Theorem 4.2 gives

\mathrm{e}^{-t\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{S}
for every
t>0
. The estimate
(
7.4
) follows from the quantitative

H^{s,s}
-bounds in Theorem 0.1(2) of
[
22
]
, applied
with an order larger than both the desired output order and the distribution
order of
f_{0}
, together with weighted Sobolev embedding. In the
positive-potential case the possible equilibrium projection is a fixed Schwartz
function. The explicit polynomial factors in that theorem are bounded when

t
stays in
I_{0}\subset(0,\infty)
. Since

\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}
has
C^{\infty}
polynomially bounded coefficients and maps

\mathcal{S}
continuously into itself, (
7.2
) also
gives
C^{\infty}
time-dependence with values in
\mathcal{S}
.
∎

Under Assumption
2.2
, the operator in (
2.2
),

-\mathcal{L}_{a}+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{s}
, generates a
positivity-preserving, mass-preserving,
L^{1}(\mu)
-strongly continuous Markov
semigroup
(\mathcal{P}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on density ratios. Equivalently, it is the

L^{2}(\mu)
-adjoint of the kinetic Langevin backward semigroup with generator

\mathcal{L}_{a}+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{s}
. The measure
\mu
is invariant,
and for every convex
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with
\Phi(0)=0
,

(7.5)

\int\Phi(\mathcal{P}_{t}f)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\int\Phi(f)\,\mathrm{d}\mu

whenever

and the right-hand side is finite. In particular,

(7.6)

\Ent_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}_{t}f)\leq\Ent_{\mu}(f),\qquad t\geq 0.

We now use Lemma
7.1
to establish the short-time smoothing
property of the semigroup.

Proposition 7.2
.

Fix a positive floor
\zeta\in(0,1)
and set

(7.7)

f=\zeta+(1-\zeta)h,

where
h\geq 0
,
\int h\,\mathrm{d}\mu=1
, and

is a bounded
C^{\infty}
density ratio
with compact support in
(x,v)
. Then for every
\delta>0
the shifted solution

s\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{s+\delta}f
is regular on each interval
[0,T]
in the sense
of Definition
2.5
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{P}_{t}1=1
,

(7.8)

\mathcal{P}_{t}f=\zeta+(1-\zeta)\mathcal{P}_{t}h,\qquad\mathcal{P}_{t}f\geq\zeta.

Let
\widetilde{p}_{t}=(\mathcal{P}_{t}h)\varrho_{\infty}
and

\widetilde{f}_{t}=\varrho_{\infty}^{-1/2}\widetilde{p}_{t}
, thus
\widetilde{f}_{t}
solves
(
7.2
) with initial datum

\widetilde{f}_{0}=h\varrho_{\infty}^{1/2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\subset\mathcal{S}
.
The Markov solution lies in the weak Cauchy class used by Hérau–Nier. Indeed,

0\leq\mathcal{P}_{t}h\leq\|h\|_{\infty}
and

\|\widetilde{f}_{t}-\widetilde{f}_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)}^{2}\leq 2\|h\|_{\infty}\|\mathcal{P}_{t}h-\mathcal{P}_{s}h\|_{L^{1}(\mu)},

so

is continuous in

by the

strong
continuity of
\mathcal{P}_{t}
. By the uniqueness part of Lemma

, the
distributional solution obtained from the Markov semigroup coincides after

\mathrm{e}^{-t\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}}\widetilde{f}_{0}
. Therefore

7.1
gives, for every

I_{0}\subset(0,\infty)
and every

,

(7.9)

\sup_{t\in I_{0}}\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert+\lvert\eta\rvert\leq k}\sup_{x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+\lvert x\rvert+\lvert v\rvert)^{N}\lvert\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\eta}\widetilde{f}_{t}(x,v)\rvert<\infty.

The same estimate holds with
\widetilde{f}_{t}
replaced by any time derivative

\partial_{t}^{\ell}\widetilde{f}_{t}
, because

\partial_{t}^{\ell}\widetilde{f}_{t}=(-\mathcal{K}_{\gamma})^{\ell}\widetilde{f}_{t}
and

\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}
maps
\mathcal{S}
continuously into itself.

Since

\widetilde{g}_{t}=\mathcal{P}_{t}h=\widetilde{p}_{t}/\varrho_{\infty}=\varrho_{\infty}^{-1/2}\widetilde{f}_{t}
,

) imply the following

): for every compact interval

where the family

is bounded in the Schwartz topology,
uniformly for

. Therefore, for every polynomial

,

(7.10)

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\,\Pi_{v}\bigl(b(v)\widetilde{g}_{t}\bigr)(x)=\mathrm{e}^{U(x)/2}S_{\alpha,b,t}(x),

where

is a Schwartz function of

, again uniformly for

, and continuously in

in every Schwartz seminorm. Squaring
(

) and multiplying by

\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=Z_{x}^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-U(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x
cancels the factor
\mathrm{e}^{U(x)}
. Thus, for every

, every multi-index
\alpha
, every compact interval

I\subset(0,\infty)
, and
\ell=0,1
,

(7.11)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t\in I}\int_{\{\lvert x\rvert>R\}}\left\lvert\partial_{t}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\,\Pi_{v}\bigl(b(v)\widetilde{g}_{t}\bigr)(x)\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=0.

For

this follows by writing
\partial_{t}\widetilde{g}_{t}
from the

and
polynomially bounded functions of

), and
multiplication by such coefficients preserves Schwartz decay of the functions

S_{\alpha,b,t}
.

Because the floor in (

) is the invariant density
1
,
the moments decompose as

q_{t}=\zeta+(1-\zeta)\widetilde{q}_{t},\qquad j_{t}=(1-\zeta)\widetilde{j}_{t},\qquad M_{t}=\zeta I_{d}+(1-\zeta)\widetilde{M}_{t}.

Define

The Markov

contraction and Gaussian moment bounds give uniform

on compact positive time intervals. Together with

.
Combining this boundedness,

, and the tail estimate
(

m_{t}=\frac{j_{t}}{q_{t}}\in L^{\infty},\qquad\partial_{t}m_{t},\ \nabla m_{t},\ a_{t}=\partial_{t}m_{t}+(m_{t}\cdot\nabla)m_{t}\in L^{2}(q_{t}\mu_{x}),

and, for every compact interval

,

(7.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t\in I}\int_{\{\lvert x\rvert>R\}}

\displaystyle\Biggl(\frac{q_{t}^{2}+\lvert\nabla q_{t}\rvert^{2}+\lvert j_{t}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\Theta_{t}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta_{t}\rvert^{2}}{q_{t}}

\displaystyle+q_{t}\lvert m_{t}\rvert^{2}+q_{t}\lvert\partial_{t}m_{t}\rvert^{2}+q_{t}\lvert\nabla m_{t}\rvert^{2}+q_{t}\lvert a_{t}\rvert^{2}\Biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=0.

Indeed, derivatives of

are finite sums of products of bounded
factors and derivatives of

and

divided by powers of the positive

. The tensor
\Theta_{t}
and its first

and their first

, and the polynomial

is absorbed by the Schwartz factors in
(

and

and integrating in

estimates above justify the integrations by parts.

The entropy dissipation identity follows directly. Because

\mathcal{P}_{t}f\geq\zeta
and
\mathcal{P}_{t}f
is bounded above due to the maximum principle,

is bounded on compact positive time intervals. For

\lvert\eta\rvert=1

\partial_{v}^{\eta}\mathcal{P}_{t}f=(1-\zeta)\varrho_{\infty}^{-1/2}R_{0,\eta,t},

with

The same
\varrho_{\infty}^{-1/2}R
representation for spatial and velocity

and

bounds needed for the standard cutoff
passage. Thus one may multiply (
2.2
) by
\log(\mathcal{P}_{t}f)
and let
the cutoff radius tend to infinity in all integrations by parts. The Hamiltonian

(
2.21
).

We now verify
(R3)
in Definition
2.5
. The vector field
m_{t}
is smooth and
bounded in

on every compact positive time interval; hence the classical

solves the smooth continuity equation, the method of characteristics gives

(X_{s})_{\#}(q_{t}\mu_{x})=q_{t+s}\mu_{x}
.

We now prove the second-order expansion. Fix a compact interval

I\subset(0,\infty)
and restrict

to a slightly smaller compact interval so

for

\lvert\sigma\rvert\leq s_{0}

Therefore, for

X_{s}(x)-x-sm_{t}(x)=\int_{0}^{s}(s-\sigma)a_{t+\sigma}(X_{\sigma}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

The same formula, with the obvious orientation change, holds for negative

.
It is enough to show

\lVert a_{t+\sigma}(X_{\sigma})-a_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}(q_{t}\mu_{x})}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }\sigma\to 0,

locally uniformly in

. On every fixed ball this follows from smoothness and

. For the complement of a large ball, boundedness of

. This

. Hence (

) holds.

For the cutoff terms containing the Brenier displacement, let

A_{R}=\{x:\lvert x\rvert\geq R\}

. Since

has finite second moment,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t\in I}\int_{A_{R}}q_{t}\lvert\xi_{t}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=0.

Indeed, use
\lvert x-T_{t}(x)\rvert^{2}\leq 2\lvert x\rvert^{2}+2\lvert T_{t}(x)\rvert^{2}
and split the

-integral into
\{\lvert y\rvert\leq L\}
and

.

The coefficients multiplying

in the cutoff errors are

,
\nabla q_{t}
,

,
\Theta_{t}
, and
\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta_{t}

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t\in I}\int_{A_{R}}\frac{\lvert B_{t}\rvert^{2}}{q_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=0,\qquad B_{t}\in\{q_{t},\nabla q_{t},j_{t},\Theta_{t},\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta_{t}\}.

.

)

. Finally, the product rule gives

where

((\nabla\chi_{R})\cdot\Theta_{t})_{i}=\sum_{k}(\partial_{x_{k}}\chi_{R})(\Theta_{t})_{ik}
.

\displaystyle\left|\int\xi_{t}\cdot\nabla_{x}^{*}(\chi_{R}\Theta_{t})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}-\int\xi_{t}\cdot\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{R}}\lvert\xi_{t}\rvert\lvert\nabla_{x}^{*}\Theta_{t}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}+\frac{C}{R}\int_{A_{R}}\lvert\xi_{t}\rvert\lvert\Theta_{t}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}\longrightarrow 0,

. This proves (

-boundary terms in the

The final ingredient is an entropy approximation with a positive floor.

Lemma 7.3

(Entropy-dense smooth densities with a positive floor)
.

Let

,
\int g_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=1
, and
\Ent(g_{0})<\infty

such that

(7.18)

f_{n}:=\zeta_{n}+(1-\zeta_{n})h_{n}\longrightarrow g_{0}\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\mu),\qquad\Ent(f_{n})\longrightarrow\Ent(g_{0}).

Proof.

Write
\varrho_{\infty}
for the Lebesgue density of
\mu
. We first build a
bounded compactly supported recovery sequence without the positive floor. Let

\psi(z)=z\log z
and let
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be the Euclidean ball. Since

\Ent(g_{0})<\infty
, the probability measure
g_{0}\mu
is tight and the positive part

\psi(g_{0})_{+}
is integrable. Choose
R\to\infty
and
L\to\infty
so that, for

u_{R,L}=\min\{g_{0},L\}\mathbf{1}_{B_{R}},\qquad c_{R,L}=\int u_{R,L}\,\mathrm{d}\mu,

one has
c_{R,L}\to 1
and

\Ent(c_{R,L}^{-1}u_{R,L})\to\Ent(g_{0})
. This follows by first truncating the
positive and negative parts of
\psi(g_{0})
and then using

c\,\psi(c^{-1}u)=u\log u-u\log c
.

Fix such a bounded compactly supported ratio
u
with
\int u\,\mathrm{d}\mu=1
.
The Lebesgue density
p=u\varrho_{\infty}
is bounded, compactly supported, and has
unit integral with respect to
\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v
. Extend
p
by zero and mollify with
a nonnegative compactly supported Friedrichs kernel:

p_{\delta}=\eta_{\delta}*p
. Then
p_{\delta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}
,
p_{\delta}\geq 0
,

\int p_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v=1
, and
p_{\delta}\to p
in
L^{1}(\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v)
. Set

h_{\delta}=p_{\delta}/\varrho_{\infty}
. For small
\delta
all supports lie in one
compact set, where
\varrho_{\infty}
is smooth and bounded above and below; hence

h_{\delta}
is a bounded smooth compactly supported density ratio and

h_{\delta}\to u
in
L^{1}(\mu)
.

The entropy also converges. Since
z\mapsto z\log z
is convex, Jensen’s
inequality for convolution gives

\int p_{\delta}\log p_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v\leq\int p\log p\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v.

Lower semicontinuity of the same convex integral under
L^{1}
convergence gives
the reverse liminf inequality, so

\int p_{\delta}\log p_{\delta}\to\int p\log p
. Moreover

\log\varrho_{\infty}
is bounded on the common compact support and therefore

\int p_{\delta}\log\varrho_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v\longrightarrow\int p\log\varrho_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v.

Using

\int h_{\delta}\log h_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int p_{\delta}\log p_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v-\int p_{\delta}\log\varrho_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v,

we get
\Ent(h_{\delta})\to\Ent(u)
. A diagonal choice gives bounded compactly
supported smooth probability density ratios
h_{n}
such that

h_{n}\to g_{0}
in
L^{1}(\mu)
and
\Ent(h_{n})\to\Ent(g_{0})
.

Choose
\zeta_{n}\downarrow 0
and define

f_{n}=\zeta_{n}+(1-\zeta_{n})h_{n}
. Then
f_{n}
is smooth, bounded from
below by
\zeta_{n}
, and has unit mass. The
L^{1}
convergence is immediate.
Convexity of relative entropy gives

\Ent(f_{n})=\Ent\bigl((1-\zeta_{n})h_{n}+\zeta_{n}\cdot 1\bigr)\leq(1-\zeta_{n})\Ent(h_{n}).

Together with lower semicontinuity of entropy under
L^{1}(\mu)
convergence, this
proves
\Ent(f_{n})\to\Ent(g_{0})
.
∎

Lemma 7.4

(Lower semicontinuity of the velocity Fisher information)
.

Let
(Y,\mathfrak{m})
be either
(\mathbb{R}^{2d},\mu)
or

([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d},\,\mathrm{d}t\otimes\mu)
. If
f_{n}\geq 0
and

f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}(Y,\mathfrak{m})
, then

(7.19)

\int_{Y}\frac{\lvert\nabla_{v}f\rvert^{2}}{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{Y}\frac{\lvert\nabla_{v}f_{n}\rvert^{2}}{f_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}.

The integrand has the usual convex lower-semicontinuous extension: it is zero
at
(f,\nabla_{v}f)=(0,0)
and
+\infty
at
(0,p)
for
p\neq 0
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the assertion on the space-time cylinder; the
time-independent case is identical. Let

\nabla_{v}^{*,\kappa}\psi=-\divop_{v}\psi+v\cdot\psi
be the adjoint of

\nabla_{v}
in
L^{2}(\kappa)
, acting only on the velocity variables. For smooth
positive
u
and every

\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int\frac{\lvert\nabla_{v}u\rvert^{2}}{u}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mu\geq\int\left[2u\nabla_{v}^{*,\kappa}\psi-u\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mu,

with equality after optimizing over
\psi=\nabla_{v}u/u
and truncating. By the
standard relaxation of this identity, the Fisher functional equals the dual
quantity

(7.20)

\mathcal{I}_{v}(u)=\sup_{\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}}\int\left[2u\nabla_{v}^{*,\kappa}\psi-u\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mu

for every nonnegative
u\in L^{1}
; if the supremum is finite, the distributional
velocity derivative is absolutely continuous with density satisfying the usual
Fisher bound. For each fixed test field
\psi
, the right-hand side of
(
7.20
) is continuous under
L^{1}
convergence because

\psi
,
\nabla_{v}^{*,\kappa}\psi
, and
\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}
are bounded and compactly
supported. Taking the supremum over
\psi
gives (
7.19
).
∎

Corollary 7.5

(Integrated entropy dissipation)
.

For every probability density
g_{0}
with
\Ent(g_{0})<\infty
and every
t\geq 0
,
(
3.3
) holds.

Proof.

We use the approximation of Lemma
7.3
. First let

f=\zeta+(1-\zeta)h
be one of the smooth positive-floor densities
appearing in Proposition
7.2
. For every
\delta>0
, the
curve
s\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{s+\delta}f
is regular on
[0,t]
, so integrating
(
2.21
) gives

\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{t+\delta}f)+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}I_{v}(\mathcal{P}_{s+\delta}f)\,\mathrm{d}s=\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{\delta}f).

As
\delta\downarrow 0
, strong continuity and the semigroup property give

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\mathcal{P}_{s+\delta}f-\mathcal{P}_{s}f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{\delta}f-f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\longrightarrow 0,

and also
\mathcal{P}_{t+\delta}f\to\mathcal{P}_{t}f
in
L^{1}(\mu)
. Entropy is lower
semicontinuous in
L^{1}(\mu)
, and the space-time Fisher functional is lower
semicontinuous by Lemma
7.4
. Since

\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{\delta}f)\leq\Ent(f)
by (
7.6
), we obtain

\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{t}f)+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}I_{v}(\mathcal{P}_{s}f)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\Ent(f).

Now take
f=f_{n}
from Lemma
7.3
. The
L^{1}
contraction
gives
\mathcal{P}_{s}f_{n}\to\mathcal{P}_{s}g_{0}
in
L^{1}([0,t]\times\mu)
and

\mathcal{P}_{t}f_{n}\to\mathcal{P}_{t}g_{0}
in
L^{1}(\mu)
. Applying entropy lower
semicontinuity and Lemma
7.4
, and using

\Ent(f_{n})\to\Ent(g_{0})
, proves
(
3.3
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
g_{0}
be an arbitrary probability density with
\Ent(g_{0})<\infty
, and
choose
f_{n}
as in Lemma
7.3
. Fix
n
and
\delta>0
.
By Proposition
7.2
, the shifted solution

s\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{s+\delta}f_{n}
is regular on compact intervals. Applying
Proposition
2.4
to this regular solution gives, for every

t\geq 0
,

(7.21)

\displaystyle\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\delta}f_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}\exp\{-\lambda_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\rho}\,t\}\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{\delta}f_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}\exp\{-\lambda_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\rho}\,t\}\Ent(f_{n}),

where the second line uses entropy contraction. Since

\mathcal{P}_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\delta}=\mathcal{P}_{t+\delta}
and

\mathcal{P}_{s}f_{n}\to\mathcal{P}_{t}f_{n}
in
L^{1}(\mu)
as
s\downarrow t
, letting

\delta\downarrow 0
and using lower semicontinuity of entropy gives

(7.22)

\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{t}f_{n})\leq\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}\exp\{-\lambda_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\rho}\,t\}\Ent(f_{n}).

Finally
\mathcal{P}_{t}f_{n}\to\mathcal{P}_{t}g_{0}
in
L^{1}(\mu)
by
L^{1}
-contraction and
strong continuity of the semigroup. A second application of lower
semicontinuity and (
7.18
) gives

\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{t}g_{0})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Ent(\mathcal{P}_{t}f_{n})\leq\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}\exp\{-\lambda_{\Gamma}\sqrt{\rho}\,t\}\Ent(g_{0}),

which proves (
2.8
).
∎
