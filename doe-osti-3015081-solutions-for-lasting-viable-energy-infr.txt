Executive Summary
The Solutions for Lasting, Viable Energy Technologies (SOLVE IT) Prize aimed to enable local
organizations to identify and implement innovative energy solutions in a way that works for their unique
needs and challenges. The competition awarded $3,740,000 to winning teams across two phases.

The prize was designed to support local stakeholders and organizations as they identified and
implemented innovative energy solutions. In doing so, the SOLVE IT Prize looked to promote the
commercialization of promising energy technologies that will lead to reliable, affordable energy across
the U.S.

The prize awarded competitors with a demonstrated history of productive work with stakeholders. In
Phase 1: Embark, competitors identified a region, a local challenge facing that community that could be
addressed by an energy technology solution, and a clear plan for engagement. In Phase 2: Engage,
teams engaged with their stakeholders to develop the vision for their future energy project. Phase 3:
Establish was planned, but not completed, due to the competition being terminated in April 2025.

A total of 28 Phase 1 winners were awarded $80,000 each for describing their experience in local
stakeholder engagement, identifying an area seeking creative energy solutions, and developing a high-
level plan to engage local stakeholders.

A total of 10 Phase 2 winners were awarded $150,000 each for collaborating with stakeholders to
identify energy projects that can address local priorities.

The SOLVE IT Prize benefited the industry and public by incentivizing local organizations to work with
their stakeholders to identify and begin implementing innovative energy solutions.

Background and Project Objectives
Under the IIJA TCF, OTC pursues activities that broadly support the commercialization of promising
energy technologies while simultaneously enhancing and improving American infrastructure,
competitiveness, opportunity, and energy access. Together with OCED and EERE, the SOLVE IT Prize was
created to help local organizations identify and implement innovative energy solutions that work for
their specific needs and challenges and to create benefits such as economic growth, workforce
opportunities, greater energy independence, and local energy resilience.

The prize mechanism was engaged to accelerate these objectives, providing a more aggressive timeline
for eligible competitors to develop and implement their plans, and for those who were successful and
created the highest quality results, a reward of fast and flexible funding. The mechanism also allows for
information collection around a range of challenges and technical solutions across many different
localities, creating a broad dataset to study the variety of approaches to multi-stakeholder project
implementation and effectiveness.

The expected outcomes included:

 • Moving one or more innovative energy solutions toward implementation in the identified
 geographic area. Competitors provided proof of feedback from technologists, local government,
 and other stakeholders to improve technical and other co-benefits.
 • Identification, development, and sharing of prize participant practices and lessons learned
 regarding the implementation of energy technology innovations to facilitate replication and

The originally planned results and awards for the SOLVE IT Prize included:

 • Phase 1: Approximately 25 winners at $80k each (for describing engagement experience,
 identifying an area seeking creative energy solutions, and developing a high-level plan to engage
 local stakeholders).
 • Phase 2: Approximately 10 winners at $150k each (for collaborating with stakeholders to
 identify energy projects that can address local priorities).
 • Phase 3: Approximately 3 winners at $500k each (for demonstrating proof of obtaining
 confirmed commitments to carry out at least one specific, technically novel energy project).

The realized results were:

 • Phase 1: 28 winners at $80k each
 • Phase 2: 10 winners at $150k each

The prize competition was successful in engaging a large number of teams interested in identifying and
implementing innovative energy solutions. In total, Phase 1 of the prize (which was open to the public,
following eligibility requirements) received an above-average number of submissions for Phase 1 of an
American-Made prize. Phase 1 was able to award a higher number of teams due to the strength of
plans, progress, and capabilities of the competing teams.

Phase 3 was unable to award any teams due to prize termination in April 2025.

Project Design and Execution, Accomplishments, and Conclusions

Winning Teams and Awards:

Phase 1 was able to award a higher number of teams than anticipated due to the strength of plans,
progress, and capabilities of the competing teams. Phase 2 saw many quality submissions, making it a
challenging process to select only ten winners. While Phase 3 did not end up finishing, based on the
quality of the Phase 2 winners it is anticipated that the Phase 3 decisions would have been equally
challenging.

Competitor Engagement:

In addition to the results of the winning teams, the prize competition was successful in engaging a very
large number of teams interested in identifying and implementing innovative energy solutions. In total,
Phase 1 of the prize (which was open to the public, pending eligibility requirements) received an above-
average number of submissions for Phase 1 of an American-Made prize.

Technical Support and Mentorship:

 8

The competition saw success in the ability to provide technical support and mentorship to competing
teams through leveraging Power Connectors – organizations subcontracted with NLR who are part of
the American-Made Network.

Power Connectors provided a variety of support opportunities for competing teams. In Phase 1 of the
prize, the focus was on promoting the competition to engage as many competitors as possible, while
also answering questions, helping teams navigate the submission portal, and providing feedback on
their ideas. In Phase 2 of the prize, Power Connectors began with a needs assessment survey that
participating teams completed, which provided additional context for what types of support would be
most beneficial. Power Connectors then used this data to put together a series of virtual peer exchange
and guest expert sessions, to which all competing teams were invited (but not required) to attend. In
addition to these sessions, Power Connectors offered custom mentorship to the Phase 2 teams such as
brainstorming strategies for engagement, providing guidance and advice on specific stakeholders to
engage, discussing how to approach conversations, suggesting methods of receiving feedback, building
accountability frameworks, and more as needed by the teams.

Of the 28 teams selected as winners of Phase 1 and thus eligible to compete in Phase 2, 20 teams
participated in at least one of the virtual peer exchange or guest expert sessions (six total sessions were
provided during Phase 2). Sixteen teams set up 1:1 office hours with Power Connectors to discuss their
projects and receive mentorship. One Power Connector offered the dedicated support of an intern
through their organization, and six competing teams in Phase 2 took advantage of this opportunity.

Challenges and Lessons Learned:

 • Improve Power Connector supports. Despite a wide range of support offerings and consistent
 promotion, team engagement with Power Connectors in Phase 2 was lower than expected,
 especially in the first half of the six-month phase. Although participation improved toward the
 end, the overall utilization remained limited. This suggests that either teams felt confident
 proceeding independently or the timing and structure of support didn’t align with their needs.
 Future efforts could benefit from a more targeted, phased approach to support delivery and
 stronger incentives to participate, and ensuring that the Power Connector cohort is a match for
 the projects targeted by the prize.
 • Flexible reviewer pools. The number of submissions received outstripped the plan for reviewers,
 resulting in a rapid expansion to meet project deadlines. Given typically tight timelines and
 increasing popularity of prizes as reflected by the increased submissions, going forward it is
 recommended that prize projects are designed to have a flexible review plan that includes a
 scenario for quickly scaling up the reviewer pool.
 • Improve/enhance requirements for letters of support. Future prizes should strengthen the
 review of partnership claims in submissions by requiring clearer proof of collaboration (e.g.,
 verified letters of support) and ensuring access to reviewers with relevant expertise in
 partnership structures and stakeholder dynamics. The prize team encountered challenges with a
 Phase 1 winning team due to internal communication issues and misalignment on project goals
 among its members. While DOE/NLR appropriately refrained from intervening in internal team
 matters, this situation highlights the need for more rigorous vetting of partnership claims and
 better-informed review processes to help prevent similar issues in future phases.

Pivots or Reduction in Scope:

 • More Phase 1 winners were selected than originally estimated, which required careful
 monitoring and management of the budget to prepare for future phases and selections.
 • The evaluation component of the prize was unable to be completed or result in the planned
 final deliverable due to termination of the prize in April 2025.
 • No Phase 3 awards were made, due to termination of the prize in April 2025.

Best Practices and Success Stories:

In addition to the overall successful competitor results and awards made as detailed above, the prize
was created using lessons learned from previous prize efforts, resulting in a very streamlined and
effective process from the writing of the rules document, to preparing website and promotional
materials, to receiving and reviewing submissions. While the initial ideation process was challenging
(many different ideas from many different stakeholders), developing a summary document that
concisely explained the goals, structure, and anticipated outcomes or achievements proved to be a
successful approach. This “executive summary” allowed the subsequent parts of the prize development
process (as mentioned above: rules, websites, and promotion) to move quickly.

The SOLVE IT Prize has been referenced or highlighted in many instances since its launch as an exemplar
of a robust, straightforward, and well-executed prize competition within the American-Made program.

Project Path Forward
There are no immediate plans to continue the SOLVE IT Prize or the evaluation effort. The competition
had a successful creation, launch, Phase 1, and Phase 2.
