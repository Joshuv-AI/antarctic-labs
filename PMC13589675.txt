Novel Use of Electronic Admission Notifications for Timely Care Management of Accountable Care Organization Clients in Emergency Departments
Authors: Ellen McCreedy, Ann Reddy, Laura Dionne, Grace F. Wittenberg, Nichole E. Stetten, Nate Hunkins, Martha Etzell, Aimee van Denberghe, Ahjay Stivland, Abby Munson, Alyssa Heath, Peter Serina
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary Key points ○Hospital admissions increase the risk of delirium, falls, and accelerated cognitive and physical decline in older adults.○Providing real‐time clinical and discharge support information to emergency room providers may reduce unnecessary hospitalizations.○This pilot study demonstrated that electronic admission, discharge, and transfer notifications can be used to alert accountable care organization providers when patients enter the emergency room to provide timely care management. Why does this paper matter? ○This is the first pilot study to use electronic admission, discharge, and transfer notifications, coupled with a structured care management response, to reduce unnecessary hospitalizations in a community‐based accountable care organization.○Case managers self‐reported a hospital diversion rate of 11%; a formal efficacy study is needed to validate these results.

Introduction

For older adults with multiple comorbid conditions, hospitalizations are associated with an increased risk of delirium [1], falls [2], and accelerated cognitive decline [3]. Providing complete and timely clinical information to emergency department (ED) providers may reduce unnecessary hospitalizations, particularly among older adults transferring from congregate living settings [4, 5]. Bluestone Accountable Care Organization (ACO), a national physician group predominately serving assisted living clients, is using admission, discharge, and transfer (ADT) notifications to provide real‐time, structured information to ED providers to reduce unnecessary hospitalizations. The primary aims of this pilot study were to determine the feasibility of using ADT notifications as a trigger for a structured case management intervention, and to characterize adherence for a future embedded, pragmatic, randomized controlled trial (ePCT).

Methods

The Bluestone ED Early Response Initiative has two components, a structured phone script used by care managers to provide up‐to‐date clinical information to the ED providers, followed by a one‐page, faxed form. Materials are based on the INTERACT Nursing Home to Hospital Transfer Form [6] and include information on client comorbidities, baseline mental and physical functioning, results of recent Bluestone ACO visits (including diagnostics), care team contact information, and outpatient resources. Eligible ED visits were identified by receipt of ADT notification indicating ED registration during care manager working hours (8:00 am to 4:00 pm local time), or an assisted living center directly notifying a care manager of patient transfer. When Bluestone ACO received an ADT notification that their client had registered in the ED, the systems manager generated an email to the assigned case manager.

Bluestone care managers recorded information about successful contacts with ED providers in a secure spreadsheet, including: time from ADT notification to successful call with ED provider (within 1 h, 1–2 h, 2+ hours); who the care manager spoke with (name, role); the likely disposition of the client (hospital admission, return to assisted living, discharge to nursing home, other); and whether the care manager believed the information that they provided prevented a hospitalization (subjective effectiveness measure).

Protocols were registered in clinicaltrials.gov (protocol #NCT06933849) and approved by Advarra (STUDY00000191). Appendix A. details the Bluestone ED Early Response workflow.

Results

Between November 1, 2023 and June 30, 2024, 1989 eligible ED registrations for 1376 Bluestone ACO clients were identified. The plurality of eligible visits (1641 of 1989, or 83%) were identified through electronic ED admission notifications, while 348 of the 1989 (17.5%) were identified through direct communication between assisted living staff and care managers, with no corresponding ADT notification found.

Clients with eligible visits were 81.9 years old (standard deviation, SD: 12.4), mostly female (67%) and white (85%). Eligible clients had an average of 1.4 ED visits during the pilot period (SD 1.0, Range 1–21). Almost half (46%) of all eligible clients were in Florida, and 63% had a dementia diagnosis.

Care managers made successful contact with an ED provider by phone for 851 of the 1989 eligible ED registrations (43%). Of the 851 successful contacts, 442 (51.9%) were admitted or held for observation, 310 (36.4%) discharged back to the assisted living center, 94 (11.0%) discharged back to the assisted living center with likely hospital avoidance reported by the Bluestone care manager, and 5 (< 1%) were admitted to a skilled nursing facility or died. Table 1 describes variation in age, dementia diagnosis, time to successful contact with ED provider, and reason for ED visit, by disposition status. Successful contacts that resulted in admission were more likely than discharged cases to be caused by altered mental states, breathing difficulties, or a change in condition. Over a third of case manager‐reported hospitalization avoidances were for ED visits related to a fall.

Discussion

While only 43% of potentially eligible visits had a successful phone call between the Bluestone ACO care manager and an ED provider during the pilot study, our qualitative findings suggest the successful contact rate could be increased by detailing ED providers about the program and focusing on patients with frequent transfers [7]. We argue that the success rate is impressive for the busy ED setting and, if the “true” avoidance rate (as measured by claims) is comparable to the self‐reported rate found in this pilot study, the intervention would be largely beneficial and cost‐saving. An ePCT is needed to generate cost‐effectiveness evidence.

The use of vendor‐supplied ADT notifications to trigger a near real‐time care management response is relatively novel [8, 9, 10]. Almost 20% of eligible ED visits were identified through direct communication between the assisted living staff and Bluestone ACO providers. No related ADT notifications were found for these events, even when expanding the type of acute registration to include observation and direct hospital admissions. There were no apparent time or state patterns to the variation in ADT availability. However, some case managers had a higher rate of non‐ADT events than others, which may reflect existing informal communication patterns—a theory that needs further exploration. Validating ADT notifications against claims‐based ED records is an important methodological next step.

ED Early Response is a promising intervention leveraging electronic admission notifications to provide real‐time, targeted care management services. This type of low‐cost, scalable intervention will appeal to policymakers and providers, especially those in cost‐sharing relationships.
