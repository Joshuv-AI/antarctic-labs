The Huang–Yang formula for a two-dimensional Fermi gas: upper bound
Christian Hainzl, Fabian Saxler, Robert Seiringer

Abstract
We compute an upper bound on the ground state energy of a dilute two-dimensional Fermi gas with repulsive short-range interactions. Our bound can be viewed as the two-dimensional analogue of a formula derived by Huang and Yang in the three-dimensional case. It captures the first three terms in an asymptotic expansion for small ϱ ​ a 2 \varrho a^{2} , where ϱ \varrho denotes the density and a a the scattering length of the interaction potential.

Introduction and main result

Consider
N
identical fermions of spin
\frac{1}{2}
in a box
\Lambda=[-L/2,L/2]^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with periodic boundary conditions for concreteness. The fermions interact via a non-negative potential

The relevant Hamiltonian acts on

that are antisymmetric under permutations. In suitable units, it is given by

(1.1)

where

is the Laplacian for the
i

is bounded from below (in fact, non-negative)
and we are interested in the ground state energy

E(N_{\uparrow},N_{\downarrow},L)=\inf_{\psi\in{\mathfrak{H}}(N_{\uparrow},N_{\downarrow})}\frac{\langle\psi,H_{N}\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi,\psi\rangle}

(1.2)

where
{\mathfrak{H}}(N_{\uparrow},N_{\downarrow})
denotes the subspace of
\bigwedge^{N}L^{2}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C}^{2})
with
N_{\uparrow}
particles of spin
\uparrow

N_{\downarrow}=N-N_{\uparrow}
particles of spin
\downarrow

.
It follows from standard methods
[
27
]
that the ground state energy density in the thermodynamic limit

e(\varrho_{\uparrow},\varrho_{\downarrow})=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\rightarrow\infty\\
N_{\sigma}/L^{2}=\varrho_{\sigma},\ \sigma\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}\end{subarray}}\frac{E(N_{\uparrow},N_{\downarrow},L)}{L^{2}}

(1.3)

exists and is independent of boundary conditions.

We assume

Let

\displaystyle=\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\ln\pi-\frac{(1+t)^{2}}{\pi^{3}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2\times 3}}\chi(k\sqrt{1+t})\chi(q\sqrt{1+1/t})

1.1
Strategy of the proof

As in previous work on either the bosonic
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
9
,
11
,
25
,
26
,
29
]
or fermionic case
[
6
,
7
,
10
,
14
,
18
,
22
]
, the basic strategy consists of constructing a trial state that has the correct correlation structure at small distances, taking the two-body interactions into account. Without it, one would obtain a wrong expression for the energy. In three dimensions, the effect is less severe, effectively the correlation structure replaces the appearance of
\int V
in the formulas by
8\pi a
(with
a
the three-dimensional scattering length), as already remarked in the famous footnote in Bogoliubov’s original paper
[
5
]
. In two dimensions, however, the effect is more pronounced, as
\int V
gets replaced by

\frac{8\pi}{|\ln(\varrho a^{2})|}
which goes to zero in the dilute limit
a^{2}\varrho\to 0
.

On the technical level, this means that if one tries to estimate the interaction by
\int V
in some error terms, one effectively loses a factor
|\ln a^{2}\varrho|^{-1}
, which may render the estimate useless. Consequently, in the two-dimensional case one faces similar difficulties as one encounters in three dimensions for interaction potentials that are not integrable.

To remedy this, we follow the method in
[
3
,
11
]
and construct the trial state via a two-step procedure. In the first step, one introduces a Jastrow factor that effectively leads to a problem where the original interaction
V_{\infty}
has been replaced by a softer one
W_{\infty}

with the same scattering length, but whose integral is of the right order
|\ln a^{2}\varrho|^{-1}
. In a second step, one constructs a trial state inspired by second order perturbation theory (for the soft potential
W_{\infty}
), applying an analysis similar to
[
14
]
.

Due to lack of a good control on the norm of the trial function because of the Jastrow factor, we need to localize particles to small boxes and cannot directly work in the thermodynamic limit, similarly as in
[
26
,
11
]
. This is done in the first step in

Section

2
. In the second step, we introduce the Jastrow factor in
Section

3
, with the estimate on its effect on the norm of the trial state postponed to
Section

5
. This effectively replaces the original interaction
V
by a softer one
W
; the main bound on the ground energy of the resulting Hamiltonian will be given in
Section

4
. Finally, we collect all estimates and complete the proof of
1.2
in
Section

6
.

For convenience, we shall in the following sections use the notation

\delta=|\ln(\varrho a^{2})|^{-1}

(1.10)

which we shall always assume to be small.

2
Step 1. Reduction to smaller boxes

A standard method
[
27
]
to obtain an upper bound is to employ a trial state that considers particles localized in individual boxes. Small corridors are introduced to limit the interaction between particles in different boxes. Moreover, Dirichlet boundary conditions are needed to be able to glue together the state on small boxes to a state on the big box, but the effect of the change in boundary conditions can be quantified, as follows.

Recall the definition (
1.2
) for the ground state energy
E(N_{\uparrow},N_{\downarrow},L)
of the Hamiltonian
H_{N}
in (
1.1
), defined with periodic boundary conditions on
[-L/2,L/2]^{2}
. Following
[
26
, Sects. II.C and II.D]
(and using the monotonicity in the particle number), one finds under
1.1
that

e(\varrho_{\uparrow},\varrho_{\downarrow})\leq\ell^{-2}E(N_{\uparrow},N_{\downarrow},\ell-R_{1}-2R_{2})+\frac{4\varrho}{R_{2}^{2}}+C\frac{\varrho^{2}(1-R_{1}/\ell)^{2}}{(1-R_{1}/\ell-2R_{2}/\ell)^{4}}(a/R_{1})^{\varepsilon_{0}}

(2.1)

for any
R_{2}>0
,
R_{1}\geq R_{0}
and
\ell>R_{1}+2R_{2}
, and particle numbers
N_{\uparrow}\geq\varrho_{\uparrow}\ell^{2}
,
N_{\downarrow}\geq\varrho_{\downarrow}\ell^{2}
. Here the localization error
4\varrho/R_{2}^{2}
comes from switching between Dirichlet and periodic boundary conditions, and the last term bounds the interaction between different boxes that are separated by a distance
R_{1}
, taking our decay assumption on
V_{\infty}
into account.

The reduction in box size from
\ell
to
\ell-R_{1}-2R_{2}
, and the resulting increase in density, leads to an error of order
\varrho^{2}(R_{1}+R_{2})\ell^{-1}
in the energy density. In combination with the error term
\varrho R_{2}^{-2}
, the optimal choice of
R_{2}
is thus
R_{2}\sim(\ell/\varrho)^{\frac{1}{3}}
and the resulting error is of oder
\varrho^{2}(\varrho\ell^{2})^{-\frac{1}{3}}
. Given the last term in (
2.1
), the optimal choice of
R_{1}
is
R_{1}\sim(a^{\varepsilon_{0}}\ell)^{\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{0}}}
, with a resulting error order
\varrho^{2}(a/\ell)^{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\varepsilon_{0}}}
. These error terms will thus be negligible if we choose
\ell\sim\varrho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-\gamma_{\ell}}
for some
\gamma_{\ell}\geq 9/2
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

\frac{4\pi}{\ln(b/a_{b})}=\inf\left\{\int_{B_{b}}\left(2|\nabla f|^{2}+V_{\infty}|f|^{2}\right)\ \Big|\ f\in H^{1}(B_{b}),\ f|_{\partial B_{b}}=1\right\}

[
17
]

The minimizer

-2\Delta f_{\infty,b}+V_{\infty}f_{\infty,b}=0

We have

, and

. Moreover,

is radial and increasing in

.

In the following, we shall consider

\varphi_{\infty,b}=1-f_{\infty,b}
, which satisfies

\varphi_{\infty,b}|_{\partial B_{b}}=0
.
We will extend

by zero outside

It satisfies

2\Delta\varphi_{\infty,b}+V_{\infty}(1-\varphi_{\infty,b})=\frac{2}{R\ln(b/a_{b})}\delta_{|x|=b}\quad\mathrm{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

(3.3)

Importantly, we find via the divergence theorem that

\int_{B_{b}}V_{\infty}(1-\varphi_{\infty,b})=\frac{4\pi}{\ln(b/a_{b})}.

(3.4)

Let

for some
\gamma_{b}\geq 1
.
For any
\varrho_{\uparrow}
,
\varrho_{\downarrow}
such that

N_{\sigma}=\varrho_{\sigma}L^{2}=|B_{\sigma}|
for
\sigma\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}
, there is a normalized state
\Phi
in
\bigwedge^{N}L^{2}([-L/2,L/2]^{2})
satisfying

{\mathcal{N}}_{\sigma}\Phi=N_{\sigma}\Phi

and

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\Phi,\mathcal{H}_{W}\Phi\rangle}{L^{2}}\leq 2\pi(\varrho_{\uparrow}^{2}+\varrho_{\downarrow}^{2})+8\pi\delta\varrho_{\uparrow}\varrho_{\downarrow}+16\pi\delta^{2}F(\varrho_{\downarrow}/\varrho_{\uparrow})\varrho_{\uparrow}\varrho_{\downarrow}+C\varrho^{2}\delta^{3}\left(\ln\delta^{-1}\right)^{2}

(4.13)

for
\varrho a^{2}
small enough.
Here
F
is the function defined in (
1.5
).

The trial state
\Phi
in
4.1
will be chosen as a unitary transformation
RT
of the Fock space vacuum
\Omega
, where
R
is the particle-hole transformation in (
4.10
), and
T
will be defined in the next subsection. Our task is thus to compute
T^{*}R^{*}\mathcal{H}_{W}RT

and then take its expectation value in the vacuum
\Omega
.

Similarly as in
[
14
]
, the unitary transformation
T

can be viewed as a quasi-Bogoliubov transformation
implementing correlations among the particles, taking into account the presence of the Fermi ball.
It is actually substantially simpler than the one employed in
[
14
]
, where the correlations for both small and large length scales were implemented by quasi-bosonic Bogoliubov transformations, whereas the correlations on small length scales are already taken care of by the Jastrow factor of Step 2 in our approach.

Our method of proof is applicable to a more general class of soft potentials
W
depending on a parameter
b
, not only the one introduced in (
3.6
). In fact, what we shall need in the proof is that
W\geq 0
,

\int W\leq C\delta

(4.14)

as well as

\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{p}\frac{|\hat{W}(p)|}{p^{2}+\lambda}\leq C\delta\ln(\lambda b^{2})^{-1}

(4.15)

for small
\lambda b^{2}
and
\lambda L^{2}\gtrsim 1
.
In our case,

\hat{W}(p)=4\pi J_{0}(|p|b)/\ln(b/a)

(4.16)

where
J_{0}
is the zeroth spherical Bessel function, which is bounded and decays like
|p|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for large
p
, hence these conditions are all satisfied.

4.4
Trial state

Our trial state will be constructed with the aid of the following unitary transformation.

Definition 4.2
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, let
T_{\lambda}={\rm e}^{\lambda(B-B^{\ast})}
with

\displaystyle B=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{p,r,r^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{*}}\hat{\eta}_{r,r^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon}(p)\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow},

(4.17)

where

\hat{\eta}^{\varepsilon}_{r,r^{\prime}}(p)=\hat{W}(p)\frac{\hat{u}_{\uparrow}(r+p)\hat{v}_{\uparrow}(r)\hat{u}_{\downarrow}(r^{\prime}-p)\hat{v}_{\downarrow}(r^{\prime})}{|r+p|^{2}+|r^{\prime}-p|^{2}-|r|^{2}-|r^{\prime}|^{2}+2\varepsilon}

(4.18)

and
\varepsilon=\delta\varrho
.
We shall write
T
for
T_{1}
for brevity.

The trial state is then
given by

\Phi=RT\Omega

(4.19)

and fulfills
{\mathcal{N}}_{\sigma}\Phi=N_{\sigma}\Phi
with
N_{\sigma}=|B_{\sigma}|
, as can be seen from the fact that
R\Phi
has this property by construction, and that
R^{*}BR
commutes with
{\mathcal{N}}_{\sigma}
for
\sigma\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}
.

4.5
The correlation Hamiltonian

From the identity
R\Omega=\psi_{\rm FFG}
it follows that the energy of the ground state of the free Fermi gas equals
E_{\mathrm{FFG}}=\langle\psi_{\rm FFG},{\mathcal{H}}_{W}\psi_{\rm FFG}\rangle=\langle R\Omega,\mathcal{H}_{W}R\Omega\rangle
. We compute
R^{*}{\mathcal{H}}_{W}R
explicitly as in
[
10
]
, applying (
4.10
) and normal ordering the resulting terms. The result is as follows.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
be a normalized state satisfying
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}\psi=N_{\sigma}\psi
for
\sigma\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}
.
Then

\langle\psi,\mathcal{H}_{W}\psi\rangle=E_{\mathrm{FFG}}+\langle R^{\ast}\psi,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{corr}}R^{\ast}\psi\rangle

(4.20)

where
E_{\mathrm{FFG}}
is the energy of the free Fermi gas satisfying

\frac{E_{\mathrm{FFG}}}{L^{2}}=2\pi(\varrho_{\uparrow}^{2}+\varrho_{\downarrow}^{2})+\hat{W}(0)\varrho_{\uparrow}\varrho_{\downarrow}+O\left(\delta b^{2}\varrho^{3}\right)+O\left({\varrho^{\frac{3}{2}}}{L^{-1}}\right)

(4.21)

and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{corr}}=\penalty\ \mathbb{H}_{0}\penalty\ +\penalty\ \mathbb{X}\penalty\ +\penalty\ \sum_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{Q}_{i}
is the correlation Hamiltonian given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{k}||k|^{2}-(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2}|\hat{a}_{k,\sigma}^{\ast}\hat{a}_{k,\sigma},

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)v_{\sigma}(x-y)\left(a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{x})a_{\sigma}(u_{y})-a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(v_{y})a_{\sigma}(v_{x})\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)\Bigl[a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{x})a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(v_{x})a_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v_{y})a_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u_{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\tfrac{1}{2}a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(v_{x})a^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v_{y})a_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v_{y})a_{\sigma}(v_{x})-a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{x})a^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v_{y})a_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v_{y})a_{\sigma}(u_{x})\Bigl],

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{x})a^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u_{y})a^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v_{y})a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(v_{x})+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)\Bigl[a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{x})a^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v_{y})a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(v_{x})a_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v_{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{x})a^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u_{y})a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(v_{x})a_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u_{y})\Bigl]+\mathrm{h.c.},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y){a}^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{x}){a}^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u_{y}){a}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u_{y}){a}_{\sigma}(u_{x}).

(4.22)

Equation

4.21
is obtained via a straightforward calculation
[
10
]
that yields

\frac{E_{\mathrm{FFG}}}{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{k\in B_{\sigma}}|k|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\left(\hat{W}(0)\varrho_{\sigma}\varrho_{\sigma^{\prime}}-\frac{\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}}{L^{4}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in B_{\sigma}}\hat{W}(k-k^{\prime})\right).

(4.23)

From the explicit form of
W
in (
3.6
)
one finds that
\hat{W}(p)=\hat{W}(0)+O(\delta b^{2}|p|^{2})
and thus

\frac{1}{L^{4}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in B_{\sigma}}\hat{W}(k-k^{\prime})=\hat{W}(0)\varrho_{\sigma}^{2}+O(\delta b^{2}\varrho^{3}).

(4.24)

The sum in the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.24
) is easily seen to equal
2\pi(\varrho_{\uparrow}^{2}+\varrho_{\downarrow}^{2})+O(\varrho^{\frac{3}{2}}L^{-1})
, hence leading to (
4.21
).

We further decompose
\mathbb{Q}_{2}=\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\parallel}+\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow}
with
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\parallel}
including only same-spin interactions, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\parallel}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{x})a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{y})a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(v_{y})a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(v_{x})+\mathrm{h.c.}

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u_{x})a^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u_{y})a^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v_{y})a^{\ast}_{\sigma}(v_{x})+\mathrm{h.c.}

(4.25)

We shall see in the following that we can restrict our attention to the
effective correlation Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{0}+\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow}

with only a negligible error. In fact,
as in
[
10
, Proposition 3.3]
,

|\langle\psi,\mathbb{X}\psi\rangle|\leq C\hat{W}(0)\varrho\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle,\qquad|\langle\psi,\mathbb{Q}_{1}\psi\rangle|\leq C\hat{W}(0)\varrho\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle,

(4.26)

and
\hat{W}(0)\leq C\delta
in our case. Moreover, as in
[
14
]
,
\mathbb{Q}_{3}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\parallel}
vanish on the considered trial state:

\langle T\Omega,\mathbb{Q}_{3}T\Omega\rangle=\langle T\Omega,\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\parallel}T\Omega\rangle=0\,.

(4.27)

To see this note that
B
creates or annihilates four particles, two with spin up and two with spin down. The operator

\mathbb{Q}_{3}
creates/annihilates two particles, while
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\parallel}
creates/annihilates four
particles of the same spin, hence
\mathbb{Q}_{3}T\Omega
and
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\parallel}T\Omega
are orthogonal to
T\Omega
.

In
Section

4.10
it will be shown via a more involved computation that the contribution of
\mathbb{Q}_{4}
is small as well. In contrast, for the three-dimensional case considered in
[
14
]
,
\mathbb{Q}_{4}
is essential for the renormalization of the interaction at high momenta and negligible only for small momenta. Of course, the soft potential considered here should be thought of as already renormalized.
In our case, the operator
B
is determined by solving the commutator equation

[\mathbb{H}_{0},B-B^{*}]\approx-\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow},

(4.28)

which corresponds to the second (low momentum) quasi-bosonic Bogoliubov transformation in
[
14
]
, or the part of the Bogoliubov transformation acting on low momenta in
[
2
]
, respectively.

4.6
Configuration space representation and
t
-integral bounds

Following
[
14
]
, it will be very useful to write

\frac{1}{|r+p|^{2}+|r^{\prime}-p|^{2}-|r|^{2}-|r^{\prime}|^{2}+2\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2t\varepsilon}{\rm e}^{-t|r+p|^{2}}{\rm e}^{t|r|^{2}}{\rm e}^{-t|r^{\prime}-p|^{2}}{\rm e}^{t|r^{\prime}|^{2}}

(4.29)

on the support of
\hat{u}_{\uparrow}(p+r)\hat{u}_{\downarrow}(r^{\prime}-p)\hat{v}_{\uparrow}(r)\hat{v}_{\downarrow}(r^{\prime})
.
By applying this in (
4.18
), we obtain the configuration space representation

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2t\varepsilon}\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)a_{\uparrow}(u_{t,x})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,x})a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,y})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,y})

(4.30)

for the operator
B
defined in (
4.17
), with
u_{t,\sigma}
and
v_{t,\sigma}
defined in (
4.3
).
Using this representation, there often arises a need for bounds of the following type throughout the proof.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
0<\varepsilon<(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2}
and
L>k_{\rm F}^{\sigma}/\varepsilon
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2\varepsilon t}\|u_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}\leq C\ln\left(1+\tfrac{(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right).

(4.31)

Moreover, for
\lambda\geq 1
,

\int_{(\lambda k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{-2}}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{2t((k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2}-\varepsilon)}\|u_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\lambda\ln\left(1+\tfrac{(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right).

(4.32)

Proof.

Let
f(t)=t^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm e}^{2t}/(1+t^{\frac{1}{2}})
for
t>0
. By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2\varepsilon t}\|u_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2\varepsilon t}\|u_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}f(t(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2\varepsilon t}\|v_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}f(t(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2})^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.33)

For the first term on right-hand side, we can bound

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2(1+x)t}f(t)\leq\frac{C}{x\sqrt{1+x}}

(4.34)

for
x>0
, which yields

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2\varepsilon t}\|u_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}f(t(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2})\leq\frac{C}{L^{2}}\sum_{|k|>k_{\rm F}^{\sigma}}\frac{1}{|k|^{2}-(k_{\mathrm{F}}^{\sigma})^{2}+\varepsilon}\frac{k_{\rm F}^{\sigma}}{|k|}\leq C\ln\left(1+\tfrac{(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right).

(4.35)

The last estimate is obtained by bounding the sum via the corresponding integral, which can easily justified under the stated assumption
L>k_{\rm F}^{\sigma}/\varepsilon
.
The bound (
4.32
) follows immediately from (
4.35
) since
f(t)\geq{\rm e}^{2t}/(2\lambda)
for
t\geq 1/\lambda^{2}
and
\lambda\geq 1
.

For the second term in (
4.33
), on the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2\varepsilon t}\|v_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}^{2}f(t(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2L^{2}}\sum_{|k|\leq k_{\rm F}^{\sigma}}\frac{1+\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{1-(|k|^{2}-\varepsilon)/(k_{\mathrm{F}}^{\sigma})^{2}}}{(k_{\mathrm{F}}^{\sigma})^{2}-|k|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq C\ln\left(1+\tfrac{(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right),

(4.36)

hence proving (
4.31
).
∎

For our choice of
\varepsilon=\delta\varrho
, we have
\ln(1+\tfrac{(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma})^{2}}{\varepsilon})\leq C\ln\delta^{-1}
.

4.7
Conjugation of
{\mathcal{N}}

We start by computing bounds for
T^{*}{\mathcal{N}}T
.

Proposition 4.5
.

For
\lambda\in[0,1]

\langle T_{\lambda}\Omega,{\mathcal{N}}T_{\lambda}\Omega\rangle\leq CL^{2}\varrho\left(\delta\ln\delta^{-1}\right)^{2}\,.

(4.37)

Proof.

We have
\langle\Omega,{\mathcal{N}}\Omega\rangle=0
and

\partial_{\lambda}\langle\psi,T_{\lambda}^{*}{\mathcal{N}}T_{\lambda}\psi\rangle=\langle\psi,T_{\lambda}^{*}[{\mathcal{N}},B-B^{*}]T_{\lambda}\psi\rangle=-4\langle\psi,T_{\lambda}^{*}(B+B^{*})T_{\lambda}\psi\rangle

(4.38)

for any normalized
\psi\in{\mathcal{F}}
.
We will apply the representation (
4.30
) for
B
,
and proceed as in
[
14
, Prop. 3.5]
.
We have

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,B\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\int{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2t\varepsilon}\|u_{t,\uparrow}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\uparrow}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}\int{\rm d}y\,\|a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,y})\psi\|\int W\,.

(4.39)

Since
\|v_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}\leq{\rm e}^{t(k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow})^{2}}\|v_{\downarrow}\|_{2}
and
0\leq\hat{u}_{t,\downarrow}(k){\rm e}^{t(k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow})^{2}}\leq 1
,
the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle\|v_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}\int{\rm d}y\,\|a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,y})\psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{\downarrow}\|_{2}L\left(\int{\rm d}y\,\|a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,y})\psi\|^{2}{\rm e}^{2t(k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|v_{\downarrow}\|_{2}L\|{\mathcal{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|.

(4.40)

Using further that

\int W\leq C\delta
for our choice of parameters,

4.4
thus implies that

|\langle\psi,B\psi\rangle|\leq CL\varrho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\mathcal{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\delta\ln\delta^{-1}\,.

(4.41)

Equation

4.37
then follows from (
4.38
) via Grönwall’s Lemma.
∎

4.8
Conjugation of
\mathbb{H}_{0}

We shall now show that, up to a negligible error, the commutator of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
with
B-B^{*}
satisfies (

).

Proposition 4.6
.

We have

\partial_{\lambda}T_{\lambda}^{*}\mathbb{H}_{0}T_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}^{*}(-\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow}+{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{H}_{0}})T_{\lambda}

(4.42)

with
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{H}_{0}}=2\varepsilon(B+B^{*})

(4.43)

for any normalized

Proof.

A simple computation shows that

The expectation value of

∎

4.9
Conjugation of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow}

In this subsection, we compute the contribution due to
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow}
, and prove that it is given by the correct constant up to sub-leading terms.

Proposition 4.7
.

We have

\partial_{\lambda}T_{\lambda}^{*}\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow}T_{\lambda}=-\frac{2}{L^{4}}\sum_{p,r,r^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{*}}\hat{W}(p)\hat{\eta}^{\varepsilon}_{r,r^{\prime}}(p)\hat{u}_{\uparrow}(r+p)\hat{u}_{\downarrow}(r^{\prime}-p)\hat{v}_{\uparrow}(r)\hat{v}_{\downarrow}(r^{\prime})+T_{\lambda}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}T_{\lambda}

(4.45)

with

|\langle\psi,{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\psi\rangle|\leq C\varrho\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle\delta^{2}\ln\delta^{-1}+C\varrho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\|{\mathcal{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}\ln\delta^{-1}

(4.46)

for any
\psi\in{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

The derivative is given by

\partial_{\lambda}T_{\lambda}^{*}\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow}T_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}^{*}[\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow},B]T_{\lambda}+\mathrm{h.c.}

(4.47)

Recalling the definitions of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow}
and
B
in in (
4.25
) and (
4.17
), respectively, we see that the relevant commutator to be calculated is

[\hat{a}_{-s^{\prime},\uparrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}_{s^{\prime}-k,\uparrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}_{-s,\downarrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}_{s+k,\downarrow}^{\ast},\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}]

(4.48)

for

,

,

s^{\prime}-k,r+p\not\in B_{\uparrow}

15

\displaystyle\delta_{s^{\prime},r}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s^{\prime}-k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{-s,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{s+k,\downarrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}+\delta_{s,r^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{-s^{\prime},\uparrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s^{\prime}-k,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{s+k,\downarrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}

\displaystyle+\delta_{s^{\prime}-k,r+p}\hat{a}_{-s^{\prime},\uparrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{-s,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s+k,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}+\delta_{s+k,r^{\prime}-p}\hat{a}_{-s^{\prime},\uparrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s^{\prime}-k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{-s,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}

\displaystyle-\delta_{s^{\prime},r}\delta_{s,r^{\prime}}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s^{\prime}-k,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{s+k,\downarrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}-\delta_{s^{\prime}-k,r+p}\delta_{s+k,r^{\prime}-p}\hat{a}_{-s^{\prime},\uparrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{-s,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}

\displaystyle-\delta_{s,r^{\prime}}\delta_{s^{\prime}-k,r+p}\hat{a}_{-s^{\prime},\uparrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s+k,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}-\delta_{s^{\prime},r}\delta_{s+k,r^{\prime}-p}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s^{\prime}-k,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{-s,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}

\displaystyle-\delta_{s,r^{\prime}}\delta_{-k,p}\hat{a}_{-s^{\prime},\uparrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s^{\prime}-k,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}-\delta_{s^{\prime},r}\delta_{-k,p}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{-s,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s+k,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}

\displaystyle+\delta_{s,r^{\prime}}\delta_{s^{\prime},r}\delta_{-k,p}\left(\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s+k,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{s+k,\downarrow}+\hat{a}^{\ast}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}+\hat{a}^{\ast}_{-s,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-s,\downarrow}+\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}^{\ast}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}-1\right)

(4.49)

and we will split
[\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\uparrow\downarrow},B]=\sum_{i=1}^{15}\mathrm{I}_{i}
accordingly. The constant term in (

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}

The following bounds are analogous to similar ones in the three-dimensional case in
[

.
For a given term
\mathrm{I}_{j}

\mathrm{I}^{t}_{j}

\mathrm{I}_{j}=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2t\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{I}^{t}_{j}

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{1}^{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{r}\hat{v}_{t,\uparrow}(r)\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}r\cdot(x-z)}W(x-y)W(z-z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times a_{\uparrow}^{*}(u_{x})a_{\downarrow}^{*}(u_{y})a_{\downarrow}^{*}(v_{y})a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(u_{t,z}).

(4.50)

Using that

the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields the bound

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}^{t}_{1}\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\ {\rm e}^{t(k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow})^{2}}\left(\int{\rm d}x\left\|\int{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)a_{\downarrow}(v_{y})a_{\downarrow}(u_{y})a_{\uparrow}(u_{x})\psi\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int{\rm d}z\left\|\int{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,W(z-z^{\prime})a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(u_{t,z})\psi\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.51)

The first factor on the right-hand side can be bounded, again via Cauchy–Schwarz, as

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}x\left\|\int{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)a_{\downarrow}(v_{y})a_{\downarrow}(u_{y})a_{\uparrow}(u_{x})\psi\right\|^{2}\leq\|v_{\downarrow}\|_{2}^{2}\langle\psi,\mathbb{Q}_{4}\psi\rangle\int W.

(4.52)

In the second factor we can bound

\displaystyle\left\|\int{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,W(z-z^{\prime})a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(u_{t,z})\psi\right\|\leq\|u_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}\left\|a_{\uparrow}(u_{t,z})\psi\right\|\int W.

(4.53)

In combination with the second inequality in (
4.40
), this shows that

|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}^{t}_{1}\psi\rangle|\leq\|u_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}\|v_{\downarrow}\|_{2}\|\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\|{\mathcal{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\left(\int W\right)^{3/2}.

(4.54)

With the aid of
4.4
and (
4.14
), this finally yields

|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}_{1}\psi\rangle|\leq C\varrho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\|{\mathcal{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}\ln\delta^{-1}\,.

(4.55)

The term
\mathrm{I}_{2}
can be bounded in the same way, simply exchanging
\uparrow
and
\downarrow
.

For
\mathrm{I}_{3}
, we similarly have

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{3}^{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{r}\hat{u}_{t,\uparrow}(r)\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}r\cdot(x-z)}W(x-y)W(z-z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ \times a_{\uparrow}^{*}(v_{x})a_{\downarrow}^{*}(u_{y})a_{\downarrow}^{*}(v_{y})a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z}).

(4.56)

Using that
0\leq\hat{u}_{t,\uparrow}(r)\leq{\rm e}^{-t(k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow})^{2}}
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, its expectation value can be bounded by

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}^{t}_{3}\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{-t(k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow})^{2}}\left(\int{\rm d}x\left\|\int{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)a_{\downarrow}(v_{y})a_{\downarrow}(u_{y})a_{\uparrow}(v_{x})\psi\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int{\rm d}z\left\|\int{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,W(z-z^{\prime})a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z})\psi\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.57)

The first factor can be bounded, again via Cauchy–Schwarz, as

\int{\rm d}x\left\|\int{\rm d}y\,W(x-y)a_{\uparrow}(v_{x})a_{\downarrow}(u_{y})a_{\downarrow}(v_{y})\psi\right\|^{2}\leq\left(\int W\right)^{2}\|v_{\uparrow}\|_{2}^{2}\|v_{\downarrow}\|_{2}^{2}\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle.

(4.58)

For the second we can proceed as for
\mathrm{I}_{1}
above, using (
4.53
) with
v_{t,z}
in place of
u_{t,z}
, as well as

{\rm e}^{-2t(k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow})^{2}}\int dz\left\|a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z})\psi\right\|^{2}\leq\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle.

(4.59)

Applying again
4.4
, we conclude that

|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}_{3}\psi\rangle|\leq C\varrho\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle\delta^{2}\ln\delta^{-1}\,.

(4.60)

The term
\mathrm{I}_{4}
is bounded in the same way, exchanging
\uparrow
and
\downarrow
.

We proceed with
\mathrm{I}_{5}
, for which we have

\mathrm{I}^{t}_{5}\!=\!\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,W(x-y)W(z-z^{\prime})v_{t,\downarrow}(y-z^{\prime})v_{t,\uparrow}(x-z)a^{*}_{\uparrow}(u_{x})a^{*}_{\downarrow}(u_{y})a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(u_{t,z}).

(4.61)

Again we can use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to bound it as

|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}^{t}_{5}\psi\rangle|\leq C\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}\varrho^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\|{\mathcal{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\|u_{t,\uparrow}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\uparrow}\|_{2},

(4.62)

and hence
4.4
shows that also this term satisfies the bound (
4.55
).

The term
\mathrm{I}_{6}
has a similar structure, but with interchanged roles of
u
and
v
, i.e.,

\mathrm{I}^{t}_{6}\!=\!\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,W(x-y)W(z-z^{\prime})u_{t,\downarrow}(y-z^{\prime})u_{t,\uparrow}(x-z)a^{*}_{\uparrow}(v_{x})a^{*}_{\downarrow}(v_{y})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z}).

(4.63)

We shall utilize two different bounds, depending on the magnitude of
t
. On the one hand, a simple Cauchy–Schwarz inequality shows that

|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}^{t}_{6}\psi\rangle|\leq C\min\{\varrho_{\uparrow},\varrho_{\downarrow}\}\left(\int W\right)^{2}\|u_{t,\uparrow}\|_{2}\|u_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}{\rm e}^{t(k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow})^{2}+t(k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow})^{2}}\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle.

(4.64)

Let
k_{\rm F}=\max\{k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow},k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow}\}
. We shall integrate (
4.64
) over
t\geq k_{\rm F}^{-2}
using
4.4
. In fact, with a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality the bound (
4.32
) implies that

\int_{k_{\rm F}^{-2}}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,\|u_{t,\uparrow}\|_{2}\|u_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}{\rm e}^{t(k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow})^{2}+t(k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow})^{2}}\leq C\left(\frac{k_{\rm F}}{\min\{k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow},k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow}\}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ln\delta^{-1}

(4.65)

which, after multiplication by
\min\{\varrho_{\uparrow},\varrho_{\downarrow}\}
, is bounded by
\varrho\ln\delta^{-1}
.
On the other hand,
writing

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}^{t}_{6}=\frac{1}{L^{4}}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}}\hat{u}_{t,\uparrow}(r)\hat{u}_{t,\downarrow}(r^{\prime})\int

\displaystyle{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,W(x-y)W(z-z^{\prime}){\rm e}^{{\rm i}r\cdot(x-z)}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}r^{\prime}\cdot(y-z^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times a^{*}_{\uparrow}(v_{x})a^{*}_{\downarrow}(v_{y})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z})

(4.66)

we can bound
0\leq\hat{u}_{t,\sigma}(k)\leq{\rm e}^{-t|k|^{2}}
and thus

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}^{t}_{6}\psi\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{L^{4}}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-t(|r|^{2}+|r^{\prime}|^{2})}\left\|\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}r\cdot x}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}r^{\prime}\cdot y}a_{\downarrow}(v_{y})a_{\uparrow}(v_{x})\psi\right\|

\displaystyle\times\left\|\int{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,W(z-z^{\prime}){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}r\cdot z}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}r^{\prime}\cdot z^{\prime}}a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z})\psi\right\|.

(4.67)

The function
g_{t}
with Fourier coefficients
\hat{g}_{t}(k)={\rm e}^{-t|k|^{2}}
is the periodization of a Gaussian, hence non-negative and integrates to
1
, independently of
t
.
Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the sum over
r
and
r^{\prime}
, it suffices to bound

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{4}}\sum_{r,r^{\prime}}{\rm e}^{-t(|r|^{2}+|r^{\prime}|^{2})}\left\|\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,W(x-y){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}r\cdot x}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}r^{\prime}\cdot y}a_{\downarrow}(v_{y})a_{\uparrow}(v_{x})\psi\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}x^{\prime}{\rm d}y^{\prime}\,W(x-y)W(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})g_{t}(x-x^{\prime})g_{t}(y-y^{\prime})\left\langle\psi,a^{*}_{\uparrow}(v_{x^{\prime}})a^{*}_{\downarrow}(v_{y^{\prime}})a_{\downarrow}(v_{y})a_{\uparrow}(v_{x})\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{\downarrow}\|_{2}^{2}\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}x^{\prime}{\rm d}y^{\prime}\,W(x-y)W(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})g_{t}(x-x^{\prime})g_{t}(y-y^{\prime})\|a_{\uparrow}(v_{x^{\prime}})\psi\|\|a_{\uparrow}(v_{x})\psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{\downarrow}\|_{2}^{2}\|W*g_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle

(4.68)

where the last step follows from
2\|a_{\uparrow}(v_{x^{\prime}})\psi\|\|a_{\uparrow}(v_{x})\psi\|\leq\|a_{\uparrow}(v_{x^{\prime}})\psi\|^{2}+\|a_{\uparrow}(v_{x^{\prime}})\psi\|^{2}
and (
4.59
). An analogous bound holds with
v_{t}
in place of
v
, and we conclude that

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}^{t}_{6}\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C\varrho\,{\rm e}^{t(k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow})^{2}}{\rm e}^{t(k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow})^{2}}\|W*g_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=C\varrho\,\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k}|\hat{W}(k)|^{2}{\rm e}^{-2t|k|^{2}}{\rm e}^{t(k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow})^{2}}{\rm e}^{t(k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow})^{2}}.

(4.69)

For the integration over
0<t<k_{\rm F}^{-2}
we shall use that

\int_{0}^{k_{\rm F}^{-2}}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-t(2|k|^{2}+2\varepsilon-(k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow})^{2}-(k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow})^{2})}\leq\frac{C}{|k|^{2}+k_{\rm F}^{2}}

(4.70)

which can be obtained from an explicit calculation, using
\varepsilon>0
and
k_{\rm F}^{\sigma}\leq k_{\rm F}
.
With
|\hat{W}(k)|\leq C\delta
and (
4.15
), we thus conclude that
\rm I_{6}
satisfies the same bound as
\rm I_{3}
in (
4.60
).

For the term
\mathrm{I}_{7}
, we find

\mathrm{I}^{t}_{7}=\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,W(x-y)W(z-z^{\prime})v_{t,\downarrow}(y;z^{\prime})u_{t,\uparrow}(x;z)a^{*}_{\uparrow}(v_{x})a^{*}_{\downarrow}(u_{y})a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z}).

(4.71)

It can be bounded as

|\langle\psi,\mathrm{I}^{t}_{7}\psi\rangle|\leq\left(\int W\right)^{2}\|u_{t,\uparrow}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}\|v_{\uparrow}\|_{2}^{2}\langle\psi,{\mathcal{N}}\psi\rangle

(4.72)

and hence satisfies (
4.60
) after integration over
t
. The same applies to
\mathrm{I}_{8}
.
The term
\mathrm{I}_{9}
has a somewhat different structure, and can be written as

\mathrm{I}^{t}_{9}=\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,W(x-y)W(z-z^{\prime})v_{t,\downarrow}(y;z^{\prime})u_{t,\uparrow}(y;z^{\prime})a^{*}_{\uparrow}(v_{x})a^{*}_{\uparrow}(u_{x})a_{\uparrow}(u_{t,z})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z}).

(4.73)

With Cauchy–Schwarz, it can bounded in exactly the same was as (
4.72
), however.
The same bound applies to
\mathrm{I}_{10}
, interchanging
\uparrow
and
\downarrow
.

We are left with studying the quadratic terms which, by translation-invariance, are necessarily of the form
d\Gamma(\omega)
for a (spin-dependent) Fourier multiplier
\omega
. Explicitly,

\mathrm{I}_{11}+\mathrm{I}_{12}+\mathrm{I}_{13}+\mathrm{I}_{14}=\sum_{k,\sigma}\omega_{\sigma}(k)\hat{a}_{k,\sigma}^{*}\hat{a}_{k,\sigma}

(4.74)

with

\displaystyle\omega_{\sigma}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{4}}\sum_{p,r}\hat{W}(p)^{2}\left(\frac{\hat{v}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\hat{u}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r-p)\hat{v}_{\sigma}(k-p)\hat{u}_{\sigma}(k)}{|r-p|^{2}-|r|^{2}+|k|^{2}-|k-p|^{2}+2\varepsilon}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{\hat{v}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\hat{u}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r-p)\hat{u}_{\sigma}(k+p)\hat{v}_{\sigma}(k)}{|r-p|^{2}-|r|^{2}+|k+p|^{2}-|k|^{2}+2\varepsilon}\right)

(4.75)

(where
\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma
). They can thus be bounded by
{\mathcal{N}}\max_{k,\sigma}\omega_{\sigma}(k)
. Let us show that
\max_{k,\sigma}\omega_{\sigma}(k)\leq C\varrho\delta^{2}\ln\delta^{-1}
. We can drop the positive terms
|k|^{2}-|k-p|^{2}
and
|k+p|^{2}-|k|^{2}
in the denominators, yielding

\omega_{\sigma}(k)\leq\frac{1}{L^{4}}\sum_{p,r}\hat{W}(p)^{2}\frac{\hat{v}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\hat{u}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r-p)}{|r-p|^{2}-|r|^{2}+2\varepsilon}.

(4.76)

By proceeding similarly as in the proof of
4.4
, bounding the sum by the corresponding integral, and distinguishing between the cases
|p|\gtrsim k_{\rm F}^{\sigma^{\prime}}
and
|p|\lesssim k_{\rm F}^{\sigma^{\prime}}
, one readily checks that

\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{r}\frac{\hat{v}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\hat{u}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r-p)}{|r-p|^{2}-|r|^{2}+2\varepsilon}\leq C\min\left\{\frac{(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma^{\prime}})^{2}}{|p|^{2}+(k_{\rm F}^{\sigma^{\prime}})^{2}},\ln\delta^{-1}\right\}.

(4.77)

In combination with (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), this implies the result.

The last term,
\mathrm{I}_{15}
, gives the constant first term on the right-hand side of (
4.45
). This completes the proof.
∎

4.10
Conjugation of
\mathbb{Q}_{4}

In this subsection, we show that
\mathbb{Q}_{4}
does not contribute to the energy to the relevant order of
\delta^{2}\varrho^{2}
. We will follow analogous bounds in the three-dimensional case in
[
14
, Props. 4.5 & 5.3]
, but again there will be notable differences. For later use, we shall formulate the next proposition for more general operators that have the same structure as
\mathbb{Q}_{4}
, but
W
is replaced by a general function
w
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{4}|_{w}
denote the same operator as
\mathbb{Q}_{4}
in (
4.3
), but with
W_{\infty}
replaced by an even and integrable function
w:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with support contained inside
\Lambda
. Then, for any
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

|\langle T_{\lambda}\Omega,{\mathbb{Q}_{4}|_{w}}T_{\lambda}\Omega\rangle|\leq CL^{2}\left(\int w\right)\varrho^{2}\left(\delta\ln\delta^{-1}\right)^{2}\,.

(4.78)

Proof.

As in
[
14
, Prop. 5.3]
, the first step will be to replace the functions
\hat{u}_{\sigma}
in the definition of
\mathbb{Q}_{4}|_{w}

by smooth functions with smaller support. Let again
k_{\rm F}=\max\{k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow},k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow}\}
, and let
\hat{\zeta}^{\gtrless}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
be smooth functions with
\hat{\zeta}^{<}+\hat{\zeta}^{>}=1
such that

\hat{\zeta}^{<}(k)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $|k|\leq 5k_{\rm F}$}\\
0&\text{if $|k|\geq 6k_{\rm F}$}\end{cases}

(4.79)

Let
\zeta^{\gtrless}
be the corresponding functions on
\Lambda
with Fourier coefficients
\hat{\zeta}^{\gtrless}
, and also
u_{\sigma}^{\gtrless}
with Fourier coefficients
\hat{u}_{\sigma}^{\gtrless}(k)=\hat{u}_{\sigma}(k)\zeta^{\gtrless}(k)
. Note that
u_{\sigma}^{>}=\zeta^{>}
. Since
u_{\sigma}=u_{\sigma}^{>}+u_{\sigma}^{<}
, we can split

\mathbb{Q}_{4}|_{w}=\mathbb{Q}^{>}_{4}|_{w}+\mathbb{Q}^{<}_{4}|_{w}

(4.80)

with

\mathbb{Q}^{>}_{4}|_{w}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,w(x-y){a}^{\ast}_{\sigma}(u^{>}_{x}){a}^{\ast}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u^{>}_{y}){a}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u^{>}_{y}){a}_{\sigma}(u^{>}_{x})

(4.81)

and
\mathbb{Q}^{<}_{4}|_{w}
containing all the remaining terms. Using
\|u_{\sigma}^{<}\|_{2}\leq C\varrho^{\frac{1}{2}}
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality implies that

\mathbb{Q}^{<}_{4}|_{w}\leq\mathbb{Q}^{>}_{4}|_{w}+C\varrho{\mathcal{N}}\int w.

(4.82)

The second term on the right-hand side can be bounded with the aid of
4.5
, and we are hence left with bounding
\mathbb{Q}^{>}_{4}|_{w}
.

We shall do this via Grönwall’s lemma, and start by computing the derivative,

\partial_{\lambda}T^{\ast}_{\lambda}\mathbb{Q}^{>}_{4}|_{w}T_{\lambda}=T^{\ast}_{\lambda}[\mathbb{Q}^{>}_{4}|_{w},B]T_{\lambda}+\mathrm{h.c.}

(4.83)

By
4.2
and (
4.81
), we thus need to compute the commutator

[\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s+k,\sigma}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s^{\prime}-k,\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{s^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{s,\sigma},\hat{a}_{p+r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-p+r^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}]

(4.84)

in case
s,s+k\not\in B_{\sigma}
,
s^{\prime},s^{\prime}-k\not\in B_{\sigma^{\prime}}
,
p+r\not\in B_{\uparrow}
,
r\in B_{\uparrow}
,
r^{\prime}-p\not\in B_{\downarrow}
,
r^{\prime}\in B_{\downarrow}
. After normal ordering, we obtain
6
terms, given by

\displaystyle-\delta_{s+k,r+p}\delta_{\sigma,\uparrow}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s^{\prime}-k,\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{s^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{s,\sigma}+\delta_{s^{\prime}-k,r+p}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\uparrow}\hat{a}_{s+k,\sigma}^{\ast}\hat{a}_{r^{\prime}-p,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{s^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{s,\sigma}

\displaystyle-\delta_{s^{\prime}-k,r^{\prime}-p}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s+k,\sigma}\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{s^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{s,\sigma}+\delta_{s+k,r^{\prime}-p}\delta_{\sigma,\downarrow}\hat{a}^{\ast}_{s^{\prime}-k,\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{r+p,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{s^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{s,\sigma}

\displaystyle+\delta_{s+k,r+p}\delta_{s^{\prime}-k,r^{\prime}-p}\delta_{\sigma,\uparrow}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{s^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{s,\sigma}-\delta_{s+k,r^{\prime}-p}\delta_{s^{\prime}-k,r+p}\delta_{\sigma,\downarrow}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\uparrow}\hat{a}_{-r^{\prime},\downarrow}\hat{a}_{-r,\uparrow}\hat{a}_{s^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{s,\sigma}

(4.85)

Exploiting the reflection symmetry of
\hat{w}(k)
and
\hat{\eta}^{\varepsilon}_{r,r^{\prime}}(p)
, we can combine
the first two terms, the third and the forth as well as the last two
by a suitable change of variables.
Accordingly, we shall write

[\mathbb{Q}^{>}_{4}|_{w},B]=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathrm{J}_{i}\,.

(4.86)

Similarly as in the proof of
4.7
, we shall denote by
\mathrm{J}^{t}_{j}
an expression such that
\mathrm{J}_{j}=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2t\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{J}^{t}_{j}
.

The first term to consider is

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{1}^{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,\zeta_{t}^{>}(z-z^{\prime})w(x-z)W(y-z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times a_{\sigma}^{*}(u^{>}_{x})a_{\downarrow}(u_{t,y})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,y})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(u^{>}_{z})a_{\sigma}(u^{>}_{x})

(4.87)

where we introduced the function
\zeta^{>}_{t}
with Fourier coefficients
\hat{\zeta}^{>}(k){\rm e}^{-t|k|^{2}}
.
We in fact have

\mathrm{J}_{1}=\sum_{\sigma}\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}z\,w(x-z)a_{\sigma}^{*}(u^{>}_{x})\mathcal{O}_{z}a_{\uparrow}(u^{>}_{z})a_{\sigma}(u^{>}_{x})

(4.88)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{p,r,s}\hat{W}(p)\frac{\hat{\zeta}^{>}(p+s)\hat{u}_{\downarrow}(p-r)\hat{v}_{\downarrow}(r)\hat{v}_{\uparrow}(s)}{|p-r|^{2}+|p+s|^{2}-|r|^{2}-|s|^{2}+2\varepsilon}\hat{a}_{p-r,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{r,\downarrow}\hat{a}_{s,\uparrow}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}z\cdot(s+p)}.

(4.89)

Since
|p+s|\geq 5k_{\rm F}
for all the summands, and also
|r|\leq k_{\rm F}^{\downarrow}
and
|s|\leq k_{\rm F}^{\uparrow}
, we have
|p-r|\geq 3k_{\rm F}
, and hence we can replace
\hat{u}_{\downarrow}(p-r)
by the characteristic function of
|p-r|\geq 3k_{\rm F}
, which we shall denote by
\hat{\eta}
. For
t>0
, let us introduce

\hat{\eta}_{t}(k)=\begin{cases}{\rm e}^{-t|k|^{2}}&\text{for $|k|\geq 3k_{\rm F}$}\\
0&\text{for $|k|<3k_{\rm F}$}\end{cases}

(4.90)

The conclusion is thus that we can equivalently write

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{1}^{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}\,\zeta_{t}^{>}(z-z^{\prime})w(x-z)W(y-z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times a_{\sigma}^{*}(u^{>}_{x})a_{\downarrow}(\eta_{t,y})a_{\downarrow}(v_{t,y})a_{\uparrow}(v_{t,z^{\prime}})a_{\uparrow}(u^{>}_{z})a_{\sigma}(u^{>}_{x})

(4.91)

and hence

|\langle\psi,\mathrm{J}_{1}^{t}\psi\rangle|\leq\left(\int w\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int W\right)\|\zeta_{t}^{>}\|_{1}\|\eta_{t}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\downarrow}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\uparrow}\|_{2}\|\mathbb{Q}^{>}_{4}|_{w}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|\|{\mathcal{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|

(4.92)

by Cauchy–Schwarz. We have
\|\zeta^{>}_{t}\|_{1}\leq C
uniformly in
t>0
, as shown in
[
14
, Lemma A.6]
. Moreover, by proceeding as in the proof of
4.4
, one readily shows that

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}t\,{\rm e}^{-2t\varepsilon}\|\eta_{t}\|_{2}\|v_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}{\rm e}^{tk_{\rm F}^{2}}\leq C.

(4.93)

(Using
\|\eta_{t}\|_{2}\leq\|u_{t,\sigma}\|_{2}
an application of (
4.31
) would lead to an additional factor
\ln\delta^{-1}
on the right-hand side; this results from momenta close to the Fermi sphere, hence does not appear here since
\hat{\eta}
is supported well outside the Fermi balls, however.)

) and can thus be bounded in the same way, with the result that, after integration over
t
,

|\langle T_{\lambda}\Omega,\mathrm{K}_{7}T_{\lambda}\Omega\rangle|\leq CL\varrho^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\int w\right)\|(\mathbb{S}_{6}^{>})^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{\lambda}\Omega\|\left(\delta\ln\delta^{-1}\right)^{2}.

(5.17)

The remaining terms of order
6
can be treated the same way.
This completes the proof.
∎

6
Step 5. Conclusion of
1.2

We now have all the tools in hand in order to give the
proof of
1.2
. In the first step in
Section

2
, we have shown that it suffices to consider finite systems of size
\ell
instead of taking the thermodynamic limit, see Eq. (
2.1
). This results in two error terms for the energy density of the order
\varrho^{2}(\varrho\ell^{2})^{-\frac{1}{3}}
and
\varrho^{2}(a/\ell)^{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\varepsilon_{0}}}
, respectively.

In the second step in
Section

3
, we have shown that instead of the original interaction potential
V
, one can work instead with the soft potential
W
(defined in (
3.6
)). According to
3.2
, the price to pay is an additive error of order
\varrho^{2}(\ell/b)^{2}(a/b)^{\varepsilon_{0}}
, as well as one resulting from
{\mathcal{R}}_{3}
, which is estimated in
5.2
. For its application, we shall need that

\|f\nabla f\|_{1}\leq C\frac{b}{\ln(b/a)}

(6.1)

which was shown in
[
25
, Eq. (2.14)]
. Hence the resulting error term is of order
\varrho^{3}b^{2}\delta^{2}
. Moreover, according to
3.3
, we also have to take into account the change in norm due to the introduction of the Jastrow factor. This was bounded in
5.1
, and we shall need that

\|1-f^{2}\|_{1}\leq C\frac{b^{2}}{\ln(b/a)}

(6.2)

which follows from
[
25
, Eq. (2.12)]
. This adds another error term of order
\varrho^{2}(b\ell\varrho)^{2}\delta
.

We shall choose
\ell\sim\varrho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-\gamma_{\ell}}
and
b\sim\varrho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{\gamma_{b}}
. All the above mentioned error terms are thus bounded by
\varrho^{2}\delta^{3}
if
\gamma_{\ell}\geq\frac{9}{2}
and
\gamma_{b}\geq\gamma_{\ell}+1
.

Finally, we still have to consider the constraint of filled Fermi balls in
4.1
, i.e., the assumption that
N_{\sigma}=|B_{\sigma}|
. In order to fulfill it, it may be necessary to add more particles to the system, leading to a slightly increased density. It is easy to see that resulting densities are bounded by
\varrho_{\sigma}(1+O(N_{\sigma}^{-\frac{1}{2}}))
, leading to an error term of the order
\varrho^{3/2}\ell^{-1}
, which is negligible for our choice of
\ell
above, however.

We hence obtain the desired bound in (
1.4
) as a consequence of
4.1
.
