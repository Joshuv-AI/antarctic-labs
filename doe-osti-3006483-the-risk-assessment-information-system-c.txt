 3. CHEMICAL FOOTNOTES

 1. Screening value is for filtered (dissolved) metals. A conversion factor (CF) was used to convert the screening
 value for total metals in surface water to a screening value for dissolved metals in surface water. CMC
 (dissolved) = CMC (total) × CF.
 2. The freshwater screening value is hardness dependent. The screening value shown is for dissolved metals
 assuming a hardness of 50 mg/L as CaCO3. See Table 1C for screening values for varying degrees of water
 hardness.
 3. Freshwater criteria for pentachlorophenol are pH Dependent. Values displayed are for a pH of 7.8.
 4. Criteria for ammonia are pH, temperature, and lifestage dependent.
 5. Selenium concentrations in water do not reflect dietary sources to aquatic life and screening against these
 numbers may not be adequately protective.
 6. pH of 6.5−9.0
 7. Bioaccumulative chemical
 8. Sum for the Toxic Unit Approach. (See Table 2c at Region 4 Ecological Risk Assessment Supplemental
 Guidance)
 9. Total value for Low Molecular Weight PAHs.
10. Total value for High Molecular Weight PAHs.
11. Subject to a narrative assessment. See Region 4 Ecological Risk Assessment Supplemental Guidance.
12. For hardness−dependent metals, freshwater chronic criteria are based on soft water with a total hardness of 50
 mg/L as CaCO3. Soft water is common within Region 5; this water ESL may be recalculated when site
 specific water hardness is less than 50 mg/L.
13. Freshwater criteria for pentachlorophenol are pH Dependent. Values displayed are for a pH of 6.5.
14. The freshwater BTAG for Hydrazine depends on the hardness level. For hardness < 50, the BTAG is 5 ug/L.
 For hardness => 50, the BTAG is 10 ug/L.
15. Potential ecological risks associated with aluminum in soils are based on the measured soil pH. Where
 aluminum is a COC, it should only be retained for those soils with a pH less than 5.5. U.S. EPA. 2003.
 Ecological soil screening level for aluminum. Interim final. OSWER directive 9285.7−60. Washington.
16. Iron is not expected to be toxic to plants in well−aerated soils between pH 5 and 8. Importance is based less
 on its direct chemical toxicity than on its effect as a mediator in the geochemistry of other toxic metals and
