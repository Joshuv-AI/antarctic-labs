Qiming Fang, Sihong Shao, Yuxuan Wu

Abstract
: Using the concepts of Eulerian-spanning set and coboundary operator, we generalize Hadlock’s conversion of the maxcut problem on planar graphs to one on general graphs with non-negative weights. Using our conversion, we can explore algorithms for maxcut beyond the class of planar graphs. We obtain a Fixed-Parameter Tractable algorithm for
k
-contraction apex graphs. Specifically, our algorithm can be applied to graphs with crossing number
k
, giving an
O(2^{k}(n+k)^{3/2}\log(n+k))
-time algorithm that matches the best known results when restricted to non-negative weights.

Keywords:
maxcut, planar graphs, Eulerian-spanning set, coboundary operator, Fixed-Parameter Tractable algorithm.

1
Introduction

The famous maxcut problem asks for a partition of vertices of a given graph into two sets such that the number of edges that cross between two parts attains maximum. It is an NP-complete problem
[
11
]
, thus being unlikely to be solved efficiently in general. However, a major breakthrough by Hadlock
[
10
]
showed that the maximum cut can be found in polynomial time on planar graphs. He showed that finding the maximum cut in a planar graph is equivalent to finding a minimum odd-circuit cover, which is further equivalent to finding a minimum odd-vertex pairing in the dual graph. While the problem of minimum odd-vertex pairing can be converted to a minimum matching problem, which can be solved using Edmonds’ Blossom Algorithm
[
5
]
, he concluded that we can find the maximum cut in a planar graph within polynomial time.

In this paper, we start with Hadlock’s conversion, and generalize it beyond the setting of planar graphs. The second step of Hadlock’s conversion relies heavily on planar duality, whose generalization to general graphs remains to be established. With regard to this, we take a step back and see what happens. We noticed that on planar graphs, an odd-vertex pairing in the dual graph corresponds to an edge set in the original graph satisfying the following coboundary condition: its coboundary (over the field
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
) is the set of odd faces. Therefore, a minimum odd-circuit cover is simply a minimum edge set satisfying the coboundary condition. But this coboundary condition can be generalized to the setting of general graphs by replacing the concept of face with our concept of
Eulerian-spanning set
. This idea rises from the fact that the boundaries of faces in a planar graph span its cycle space, i.e. the space of Eulerian subgraphs. Therefore we generalize Hadlock’s conversion on planar graphs to one that applies to maxcut on general graphs. Moreover, when restricted to planar graphs, our conversion is almost identical to Hadlock’s (c.f. Section
2
). Figure
1
illustrates our generalization, and conversion of the maxcut problem.

Our contribution.

By noticing the coboundary condition on planar graphs, and generalizing it with the concept of Eulerian-spanning set, we obtain a conversion of maxcut on general graphs with non-negative weights. Using our conversion, the maxcut problem is eventually converted to a
weighted set symmetric difference problem (WSSD)
, which is polynomial-time solvable under specific conditions. We obtain a fixed-parameter algorithm that solves maxcut on graphs with a Eulerian-spanning set satisfying a
k
-frequent appearance condition
, which is equivalent to the graph being a
k
-contraction apex graph (the concept of
k
-contraction apex graphs is a generalization of the concept of contraction apex graphs in
[
14
]
, c.f. Definition
1.1
). This result can be modified to cover the case of graphs with crossing number
k
.

Definition 1.1
.

An undirected graph
G=(V,E)
is a
k
-contraction apex graph, if we can contract at most
k
edges in
G
and get a planar graph.

Throughout this paper, we will use
P_{k}(G)
to denote an edge set of cardinality at most
k
such that after contracting
P_{k}(G)
,
G
becomes a planar graph.

In this work, we give an algorithm for maxcut parameterized by the number
k
, which comes down to the following main theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is an undirected, simple,
k
-contraction apex graph,
w:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is a weight function, and
P_{k}(G)
is included in the input. Let
n=|V|,m=|E|
Then there is an
O(2^{k}\max\{m,n\}^{3/2}\log\max\{m,n\})
-time algorithm finding the maximum weighted cut of
G
.

It is easy to see that planar graphs are simply the case of
k=0
in Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, in Section
7
, we will show that an immediate corollary (Corollary
6.8
) of this theorem covers the case of graphs with crossing number
k
, giving an
O(2^{k}(n+k)^{3/2}\log(n+k))
-time algorithm (c.f. Theorem
7.5
), which matches the results of
[
12
,
4
]
restricted to the setting of non-negative weights. We note that our algorithm is essentially different from those in
[
12
,
4
]
, which directly branch the original maxcut problem to multiple maxcut problems.

Related work.

Polynomial-time algorithms for max-cut have also been found in other classes of graphs, such as graphs not contractible to
K_{5}

[

]
, graphs without long odd cycles
[

]
, graphs of bounded treewidth
[

]
, line graphs
[

]

.

Basic assumptions.

All graphs considered in this paper are finite and undirected, but are allowed to have multiple edges and self-loops unless otherwise specified. When we talk about a planar graph, we will always assume that its planar embedding is arbitrarily fixed unless otherwise specified. Throughout this paper, copies of the same instance in a multiset are considered to be different.

2
Our conversion on planar graphs

In this section we briefly illustrate our conversion on planar graphs. The purpose is to prepare the reader for understanding our conversion on general graphs. We consider unweighted maximum cut on simple planar graphs for simplicity. We set up some definitions and notations first.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose
S
is a finite multiset. Then
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{S}
is the linear space over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(the field with two elements) with dimension
|S|
. Moreover, every element in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{S}
is regarded as an indicator function of some subset of
S
.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
S_{1},S_{2}
are two subsets of the same multiset
S
. Then
S_{1}\triangle S_{2}
is the symmetric difference between
S_{1},S_{2}
.

From now on, for a subset
S_{1}\subseteq S
, the term
S_{1}
denotes both the subset, and the corresponding indicator vector in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{S}
. Therefore,
\forall S_{1}\subseteq S,S_{2}\subseteq S
,
S_{1}+S_{2}
has the same meaning as
S_{1}\triangle S_{2}
. We use “+” when we want to emphasize the addition with respect to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and use “
\triangle
” otherwise.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a graph. Then
\partial_{1}:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{V}
is the unique linear operator such that
\forall e\in E
, if
e=(v_{1},v_{2})
, then
\partial_{1}(\{e\})=\{v_{1}\}+\{v_{2}\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a planar graph. We define the boundary of a face
f
to be the set
\{e\in E:\text{only one of the two faces on $e$'s two sides is }f\}
. We say that
f
is odd, if the boundary of
f
consists of an odd number of edges, and even otherwise.

Definition 2.5

(
coboundary operator for planar graphs)
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a planar graph. Let
F
be the set of faces of
G
. Then
\delta_{p}:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
is the unique linear operator such that
\forall e\in E
, if
f_{1},f_{2}
are the two faces on two sides of
e
, then
\delta_{p}(\{e\})=\{f_{1}\}+\{f_{2}\}
.

It is easy to see that the image of a pendant edge under
\delta_{p}
is zero, because two faces on two sides of a pendant edge are the same and therefore cancel out. Now we begin converting the maxcut problem on planar graphs. First, Hadlock showed that finding the maximum cut in a planar graph is equivalent to finding a minimum odd-circuit cover:

Theorem 2.6

(
[
10
]
,Corollary 1)
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a simple graph. An edge set
E_{1}\subseteq E
is a maximum cut if and only if
E\setminus E_{1}
is a minimum odd-circuit cover.

Now we show that a minimum odd-circuit cover is also a minimum cardinality set whose image under
\delta_{p}
is the set of odd faces.

Theorem 2.7
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a simple planar graph, and
F
is the set of faces of
G
. Let
F_{o}
be the set of odd faces in
G
. We have (1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3):

(1)
E_{1}
is a minimum odd-circuit cover of
G
.

(2)
\delta_{p}(E_{1})=F_{o}
. Or equivalently,
\forall f\in F
, if
C_{f}
is the boundary of
f
, then
|C_{f}\cap E_{1}|\equiv|C_{f}|\pmod{2}
.

(3)
E_{1}
is an odd-circuit cover of
G
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): Suppose
C_{f}\cap E_{1}=\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{t}\}
, where
t=0
is allowed. Because
E_{1}
is minimal,
\forall i\in\{1,2,\cdots,t\},E_{1}\setminus\{e_{i}\}
is not an odd-circuit cover. Therefore, there is an odd circuit
C_{i}
that intersects
E_{1}
only in
e_{i}
. Let us consider
C_{tot}=C_{f}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}C_{i}
. Then
C_{tot}\in C(G)
. Therefore
C_{tot}
is the disjoint union of some circuits (it can happen that
C_{tot}=\emptyset
). Moreover,
C_{tot}\cap E_{1}=\emptyset
. Therefore,
|C_{tot}|
must be even, or there is an odd circuit
C_{o}^{\prime}\subseteq C_{tot}
that does not intersect
E_{1}
, a contradiction. But
\forall i\in\{1,2,\cdots,t\},|C_{i}|
is odd. We then have that the parity of
|C_{f}|
must be the same as
t
, which is precisely the conclusion we want.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3): We only need to prove that every cycle in
G
that does not intersect
E_{1}
is even, because circuits can be decomposed to disjoint cycles. Suppose
C
is a cycle in
G
that does not intersect
E_{1}
. Then
C
enclose a region in the plane. Let those faces in that region be
f_{1},\cdots,f_{t}
, and their boundaries be
C_{1},\cdots,C_{t}
. Then we have
C=\sum_{i=1}^{t}C_{i}
, where
\forall i,C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
. Therefore
C\cap E_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(C_{i}\cap E_{1})
. Then
|C|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{t}|C_{i}|\pmod{2},|C\cap E_{1}|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{t}|(C_{i}\cap E_{1})|\pmod{2}
. Since
|C\cap E_{1}|=0
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{t}|(C_{i}\cap E_{1})|\equiv 0\pmod{2}
. But
|C_{i}\cap E_{1}|\equiv|C_{i}|\pmod{2}
by (2). Therefore
|C|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{t}|C_{i}|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{t}|(C_{i}\cap E_{1})|\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a simple planar graph. Let
F_{o}
be the set of odd faces in
G
. Then an edge set
E_{1}\subseteq E
is a minimum odd-circuit cover if and only if it is a minimum cardinality set such that
\delta_{p}(E_{1})=F_{o}
.

Therefore, we need only to find a minimum cardinality set
E_{1}
such that
\delta_{p}(E_{1})=F_{o}
. Let the set of faces of
G
be
F
. Then
\forall e\in E,\delta_{p}(\{e\})\subseteq F
. Moreover,
\forall E_{s}\subseteq E,\delta_{p}(E_{s})=\triangle_{e\in E_{s}}\delta_{p}(e)
. Therefore, the problem can be further converted to the following
set symmetric difference problem (SSD):

Problem 2.9

(
SSD)
.

Suppose
S
is a set, and
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{S}
is a multiset consisting of subsets of
S
. Suppose we are given a target set
T\subseteq S
. The goal is to find a minimum cardinality multiset
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
, such that
\triangle_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{T}}S_{i}=T
, or claim that no such
\mathcal{T}
exists.

If we let
S=F,\mathcal{S}=\{\delta_{p}(\{e\}):e\in E\},T=F_{o}
, then this instance of SSD is equivalent to finding a minimum cardinality set
E_{1}
such that
\delta_{p}(E_{1})=F_{o}
. Obviously the conversion can be done in polynomial time. Because
G
is planar, we have
|\delta_{p}(\{e\}|\in\{0,2\},\forall e\in E
. But sets of size 0 can simply be discarded. Therefore we only need to solve SSD in polynomial time when
|S_{i}|=2,\forall S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
. But if we view elements in
S
as vertices, subsets
S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
as edges, we get the following vertex pairing problem:

Problem 2.10

(
vertex pairing problem)
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, and a vertex set
V_{t}\subseteq V
, find the minimum cardinality edge set
F\subseteq E
such that
\partial_{1}(F)=V_{t}
, or claim that no such
F
exists.

This vertex pairing problem can be easily solved in polynomial time by Dijkstra’s shortest path algorithm and weighted perfect matching algorithm. For a complete proof, see Lemma
5.1
. We note that the vertex pairing problem we get eventually is the same as the odd-vertex pairing problem as in Hadlock’s work
[
10
]
.

Therefore, we have illustrated our conversion on planar graphs. The conversion on general graphs is similar, only that the faces are replaced by our concept of Eulerian-spanning set. The rest of the paper is devoted to proving our main results.

3
Eulerian-spanning set and coboundary operator

Now we describe our conversion on general graphs. We consider weighted maxcut on simple graphs, because multiple edges and self-loops can be handled easily. We set up some definitions, some of which are standard and are included only for completeness.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, a Eulerian subgraph of
G
is a subgraph
G_{1}=(V,E_{1}),E_{1}\subseteq E
, such that every vertex
v\in V
is of even degree in
G_{1}
.

The cycle space of
G
, denoted by
C(G)
, is the set of the edge sets of its Eulerian subgraphs.
C(G)
is a subspace of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{E}
.

It can be easily verified that every element in
C(G)
is the disjoint union of some cycles in
G
.

From now on, we may use a subset
E_{1}\subseteq E
to denote a subgraph of
G
if the vertex set does not affect our deduction, or is clear from context.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, a Eulerian-spanning set of
G
is a finite multiset
\mathcal{C}
consisting of elements in
C(G)
, such that the span of elements in
\mathcal{C}
is
C(G)
.

By this definition, every Eulerian subgraph of
G
is the symmetric difference between some elements in
\mathcal{C}
. In the setting of planar graphs, the boundary of all faces consists a Eulerian-spanning set. With regard to this, one may find that our subsequent conversion comes down to what we described in Section
2
when restricted to planar graphs.

Remark 3.3
.

We do not require elements in
\mathcal{C}
to be linearly independent, because finding a maximum independent subset can be computationally costly (Gaussian elimination takes time
O(n^{3})
, where
n
is the order of the matrix). Removing the independence condition also makes it easier to prove some theorems.

Remark 3.4
.

We allow multiple edges and self-loops to appear in the above definition. This is because in Section
6
, we will construct Eulerian-spanning set on graphs possibly with multiple edges and self-loops.

We then give the definition of
k
-frequent appearance condition:

Definition 3.5
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is an undirected graph, and
\mathcal{C}
is a Eulerian-spanning set of
G
. We say
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the
k
-frequent appearance condition, if
|\{e\in E:|\{C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}:e\in C_{i}\}|\geq 3\}|\leq k
.

Definition 3.6

(
coboundary operators for general graphs with a Eulerian-spanning set)
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and a Eulerian-spanning set
\mathcal{C}
of
G
, we make the following definitions:

(i)
\partial_{1}:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{V}
is the unique linear operator such that
\forall e\in E
, if
e=(v_{1},v_{2})
, then
\partial_{1}(\{e\})=\{v_{1}\}+\{v_{2}\}
.

(ii)
\delta_{1}:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\mathcal{C}}
is the unique linear operator such that
\forall e\in E,\delta_{1}(\{e\})=\{C\in\mathcal{C}:e\in C\}
.

By the above definition, it is obvious that

\forall E_{1}\subseteq E,\delta_{1}(E_{1})=\{C\in\mathcal{C}:|C\cap E_{1}|\equiv 1\pmod{2}\}

We additionally note that we do not need a Eulerian-spanning set to define the operator
\partial_{1}
.

4
Converting weighted maxcut to WSSD

The first step is similar to that in Section
2
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
10
]
,Theorem 1)
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a simple graph. An edge set
E_{1}\subseteq E
is a subset of a cut if and only if
E\setminus E_{1}
is an odd-circuit cover.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a simple graph,
w:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is a weight function. Let
n=|V|,m=|E|
. Suppose we are given a set
E_{1}\subseteq E
such that
E\setminus E_{1}
is a minimum weighted odd-circuit cover, then we can find a maximum weighted cut in time
O(m+n)
.

Proof.

Because
E\setminus E_{1}
is an odd-circuit cover,
(V,E_{1})
is bipartite. Fix any partition
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2},V_{1}\cap V_{2}=\emptyset
such that all edges in
E_{1}
have one endpoint in
V_{1}
and the other in
V_{2}
. Then the partition
(V_{1},V_{2})
induces a cut
E_{3}
in
G
. Moreover,
E_{3}\supseteq E_{1}
. Now we prove that
E_{3}
is a maximum weighted cut of
G
. Suppose
E_{4}
is a cut in
G
with total weight greater than
E_{3}
. Then by Theorem
4.1
,
E\setminus E_{4}
is an odd-circuit cover with total weight smaller than
E\setminus E_{3}
. But
E\setminus E_{3}
has total weight no more than
E\setminus E_{1}
. Then
E\setminus E_{4}
is an odd-circuit cover with total weight smaller than
E\setminus E_{1}
, a contradiction. Therefore
E_{3}
is a maximum weighted cut, and can obviously be found in time
O(m+n)
if
E_{1}
is given.
∎

By Theorem
4.2
, we only need to find a minimum weighted odd-circuit cover of
G
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, we distinguish elements in the cycle space
C(G)
by a measure of parity to be described below, which is similar to the way we distinguish odd and even faces in the setting of planar graphs:

Definition 4.3
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a graph, and
C\in C(G)
. We say that
C
is odd, if
C
has an odd number of edges. Otherwise we say that
C
is even.

Now we prove the analogues of Theorem
2.7
and Corollary
2.8
for Eulerian-spanning set on general graphs.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a simple graph, and
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{i}\}_{i\in[m]}
is a Eulerian-spanning set of
G
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{o}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be the multiset of odd elements in
\mathcal{C}
. Suppose
E_{1}\subseteq E
. We have (1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3):

(1)
E_{1}
is a minimal odd-circuit cover of
G
with respect to set inclusion. That is,
E_{1}
itself is an odd-circuit cover, but
\forall E_{2}\subsetneq E_{1}
,
E_{2}
is not an odd-circuit cover.

(2)
\delta_{1}(E_{1})=\mathcal{C}_{o}
. Or equivalently,
\forall C\in\mathcal{C},|C\cap E_{1}|\equiv|C|\pmod{2}
.

(3)
E_{1}
is an odd-circuit cover of
G

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): Suppose
C\cap E_{1}=\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{t}\}
, where
t=0
is allowed. Because
E_{1}
is minimal with respect to set inclusion,
\forall i\in\{1,2,\cdots,t\},E_{1}\setminus\{e_{i}\}
is not an odd-circuit cover. That is,
\forall i\in\{1,2,\cdots,t\}
, there is an odd circuit
C_{i}
that intersects
E_{1}
only in
e_{i}
. Now let us consider
C_{tot}=C+\sum_{i=1}^{t}C_{i}
. Then
C_{tot}\in C(G)
. Therefore
C_{tot}
is the disjoint union of some circuits (it can happen that
C_{tot}=\emptyset
). Moreover,
C_{tot}\cap E_{1}=\emptyset
. Therefore,
|C_{tot}|
must be even, or there is an odd circuit
C_{o}^{\prime}\subseteq C_{tot}
that does not intersect
E_{1}
, a contradiction. But
\forall i\in\{1,2,\cdots,t\},|C_{i}|
is odd. We then have that the parity of
|C|
must be the same as
t
, which is precisely the conclusion we want.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3): Suppose
C
is a circuit in
G
that does not intersect
E_{1}
. By definition of
\mathcal{C}
, we have
C=\sum_{i=1}^{t}C_{i}
, where
\forall i,C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
. Therefore
C\cap E_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(C_{i}\cap E_{1})
. Then
|C|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{t}|C_{i}|\pmod{2},|C\cap E_{1}|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{t}|(C_{i}\cap E_{1})|\pmod{2}
. Since
|C\cap E_{1}|=0
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{t}|(C_{i}\cap E_{1})|\equiv 0\pmod{2}
. But
|C_{i}\cap E_{1}|\equiv|C_{i}|\pmod{2}
by (2). Therefore
|C|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{t}|C_{i}|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{t}|(C_{i}\cap E_{1})|\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a simple graph,
w:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is a weight function, and
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{i}\}_{i\in[m]}
is a Eulerian-spanning set of
G
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{o}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be the set of odd elements in
\mathcal{C}
. Let
w_{t}:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{E}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\},\forall E_{s}\subseteq E,w_{t}(E_{s})=\sum_{e\in E_{s}}w(e)
. Suppose
E_{1}\subseteq E
. Then
E_{1}
is a minimum weighted odd-circuit cover of
G
if and only if

\exists E_{3}\subseteq E_{1},w_{t}(E_{3})=w_{t}(E_{1}),E_{3}=\operatorname{argmin}_{F\subseteq E:\delta_{1}(F)=\mathcal{C}_{o}}w_{t}(F)

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
: Suppose
E_{1}
is a minimum weighted odd-circuit cover. By possibly removing some edges of zero weight in
E_{1}
, we get a set
E_{3}
that is a minimal odd-circuit cover with respect to set inclusion. By Theorem
4.4
,
\delta_{1}(E_{3})=\mathcal{C}_{o}
. Moreover, if there is an
E_{4}
such that
\delta_{1}(E_{4})=\mathcal{C}_{o},w_{t}(E_{4})<w_{t}(E_{3})
, then by Theorem
4.4
,
E_{4}
is an odd-circuit cover with total weight less than
E_{3}
, a contradiction.

(\Leftarrow)
:Suppose
E_{3}\subseteq E_{1}
satisfies

w_{t}(E_{3})=w_{t}(E_{1}),E_{3}=\operatorname{argmin}_{F\subseteq E:\delta_{1}(F)=\mathcal{C}_{o}}w_{t}(F)

Then
E_{3}
is an odd-circuit cover by Theorem
4.4
. Moreover, if there is an odd-circuit cover
E_{4}
with total weight less than
E_{3}
, there must be an edge set

that is a minimal odd-circuit cover with respect to set inclusion. Therefore
\delta_{1}(E_{5})=\mathcal{C}_{o}
by Theorem
4.4
. But
E_{5}
has total weight less than

, which is a contradiction.
∎

The next step is also similar to that in Section
2
. For each edge
e\in E
,
\delta_{1}(e)
is a subset of
\mathcal{C}
. By Theorem
4.4
, it suffices to find an

with minimum weight such that

, because

itself would be a minimum weighted odd-circuit cover. Since

, the problem of finding a minimum odd-circuit cover is converted to the following ”weighted set symmetric difference” problem, or WSSD:

Problem 4.6

(

.

Suppose
S
is a finite multiset, and
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{S}
is a multiset consisting of subsets of
S
. Moreover, there is a weight function
w:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
. Suppose we are given a target set

. The goal is to find a multiset
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
, such that
\triangle_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{T}}S_{i}=T
, and the total weight of sets in
\mathcal{T}
is minimal, or claim that no such
\mathcal{T}
exists.

The decision version of Problem
4.6
is as follows:

Problem 4.7

(
decision version of Problem
4.6
)
.

Suppose
S
is a finite multiset, and
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{S}
is a multiset consisting of subsets of
S
. Moreover, there is weight function

. Suppose we are given a target set

\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
, such that

Remark 4.8
.

We will prove in Appendix

that Problem

Now we show that we can convert a weighted maxcut problem to an instance of WSSD problem efficiently:

Theorem 4.9
.

Suppose we are given a simple graph
G=(V,E)
, a weight function
w_{G}:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
, and a Eulerian-spanning set
\mathcal{C}
of
G
. Let
n=|V|,m=|E|
. Then the problem of finding the minimum weighted odd-circuit cover of
G
can be converted to an instance of Problem
4.6
in time
O(m|\mathcal{C}|+nm)
. Moreover, if we have the answer of the converted problem, we can recover the solution of the minimum weighted odd-circuit cover in time
O(m|\mathcal{C}|)

Proof.

Pick any bijection
h:\mathcal{C}\to[|\mathcal{C}|]
. Let
S=[|\mathcal{C}|]
. For every
e\in E
, a copy of
h(\delta_{1}(e))
is included in
\mathcal{S}
, and weighted by
w_{G}(e)
. Let
T=h(\mathcal{C}_{o})
. Then we get an instance of Problem
4.6
.

Moreover, we mark each set
h(\delta_{1}(e))
with the edge
e
corresponding to it during our transformation.

Now we consider the computational complexity. Because each element in
\mathcal{C}
has length at most
m
, the reductions described above can be done in time
O(m|\mathcal{C}|+nm)
. Moreover, since we keep track of the edges that correspond to sets in
\mathcal{S}
, we can recover the minimum weighted odd-circuit cover from the solution of the converted problem in time
O(m|\mathcal{C}|)
.
∎

5
WSSD is P when
\forall S_{i},|S_{i}|\leq 2

When
\forall S_{i},|S_{i}|\leq 2
, we can convert the WSSD problem to a weighted vertex pairing problem as in Section
2
, and solve it in polynomial time. For completeness, we first present a polynomial-time algorithm solving the weighted vertex pairing problem.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose we are given a graph
G=(V,E)
, a weight function
w:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
, and a target vertex set
V_{1}\subseteq V
. Let
n=|V|,m=|E|
. Then there is an
O(n^{2}m+n^{3})
algorithm finding the edge set
F\subseteq E
of minimum total weight such that
\partial_{1}(F)=V_{1}
, or claiming that no such
F
exists.

Proof.

Because
\partial_{1}(F)=V_{1}
, the subgraph
G_{1}=(V,F)
can be decomposed into disjoint 2-paths connecting vertices in
V_{1}
, and cycles. We can remove all cycles, because the image of a cycle under
\partial_{1}
is zero, and
F
is of minimum total weight. Therefore, to find
F
, we only need to find the shortest path connecting every pair of vertices in
V_{1}
(there can be no such paths), and then compute a minimum weighted perfect matching. The procedure is described formally as follows:

We construct a graph
G_{path}=(V_{1},E_{path})
, where
(v_{i},v_{j})\in E_{path}
if and only if there is a path connecting
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
, and we weight
(v_{i},v_{j})
by the minimum total weight among all paths connecting
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
. Then we compute the minimum weighted perfect matching of
G_{path}
. The symmetric difference of paths corresponding to edges in the perfect matching is the
F
we want (we note that the paths can only intersect in edges with zero weight, otherwise the symmetric difference of these paths has boundary
V_{1}
, and therefore corresponds to a perfect matching in
G_{path}
with total weight even smaller than that of the minimum perfect matching we just computed, a contradiction).

Now we consider the computational complexity. The shortest path problem can be solved by Dijkstra’s Algorithm in time
O(n^{2}m)
. Deciding whether there is a perfect matching in
G_{path}
, and computing the minimum weighted one when there is can both be done in time
O(n^{3})
by the weighted matching algorithm in
[
6
]
. Therefore the total running time is
O(n^{2}m+n^{3})
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose
S
is a finite multiset,
|S|=n
, and
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{S}
is a multiset consisting of subsets of
S
. Moreover, every set in
\mathcal{S}
has size at most 2. We are also given a weight function
w:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
. Suppose there is a target set
T\subseteq S
. Then there is an
O(n^{2}m+n^{3})
-time algorithm finding
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
such that
\triangle_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{T}}S_{i}=T
and the total weight of sets in
\mathcal{T}
is minimum, or claiming that no such
\mathcal{T}
exists.

Proof.

Obviously we can ignore the sets of size 0, i.e. empty sets. Therefore we assume that all sets in
\mathcal{S}
are of size 1 or 2. Let
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}:|S_{i}|=1\},\mathcal{S}_{2}=\{S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}:|S_{i}|=2\}
. We construct a graph
G_{s}=(V_{s},E_{s})
with a weight function
w_{s}:E_{s}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
as follows:

V_{s}=S\cup\{u\},E_{s}=E_{A}\cup E_{B}

E_{A}=\{(s_{i},s_{j}):\{s_{i},s_{j}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\}

E_{B}=\{(s_{i},u):\{s_{i}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\}

\forall(s_{i},s_{j})\in E_{A},w_{s}(s_{i},s_{j})=w(\{s_{i},s_{j}\})

\forall(s_{i},u)\in E_{B},w_{s}(s_{i},u)=w(\{s_{i}\})

Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between sets in
\mathcal{S}
and edges in
G_{s}
. For every
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
, denote its corresponding edge set by
E_{\mathcal{T}}
. Then the total weight of edges in
E_{\mathcal{T}}
is equal to the total weight of sets in
\mathcal{T}
. Moreover,
\triangle_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{T}}S_{i}=T
if and only if
\partial_{1}(E_{\mathcal{T}})\in\{T,T\cup\{u\}\}
. Therefore, it suffices to find an edge set
F\subseteq E_{s}
of minimum total weight such that
\partial_{1}\in\{T,T\cup\{u\}\}
. By Lemma
5.1
, this can be done in time
O((n+1)^{2}m+(n+1)^{3})=O(n^{2}m+n^{3})
.
∎

6
Reducing the running time by planarity

We first give a fixed-parameter polynomial algorithm for Eulerian-spanning set
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the
k
-frequent appearance condition.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a simple graph,
w:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is a weight function, and
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a Eulerian-spanning set of
G
included as input. Assume the number of edges that appear in at least three elements in
\mathcal{C}
is
k
. Let
n=|V|,m=|E|
. Then there is an algorithm finding the maximum weighted cut of
G
that runs in time
O(2^{k}(\max\{|\mathcal{C}|,n\}^{2}m+\max\{|\mathcal{C}|,n\}^{3})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
, we only need to find the minimum weighted odd-circuit cover of
G
. By Theorem
4.5
,
4.9
, it suffices to solve an instance
\mathcal{I}
of Problem
4.6
as follows: Pick any bijection
h:\mathcal{C}\to[|\mathcal{C}|]
. Let
S=[|\mathcal{C}|]
. For every
e\in E
, a copy of
h(\delta_{1}(e))
is included in
\mathcal{S}
, and weighted by
w(e)
. Let
T=h(\mathcal{C}_{o})
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{l}
be the multiset consisting of copies of
h(\delta_{1}(e))
for every
|\delta_{1}(e)|\geq 3
. then
|\mathcal{S}_{l}|=k
by our assumption. Our algorithm to solve the problem is as follows: for every
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{l}
, we find a
\mathcal{T}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l}
with minimum total weight such that
\triangle_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}S_{i}=T\triangle(\triangle_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{R}}S_{i})
, or claim that no such
\mathcal{T}_{1}
exists. But the above problem satisfies the condition in Theorem
5.2
, and therefore can be solved in time
O(\max\{|\mathcal{C}|,n\}^{2}m+\max\{|\mathcal{C}|,n\}^{3})
. It is obvious that
\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{T}_{1}
is a candidate solution of
\mathcal{I}
. Then, among our candidate solutions, we choose the one with minimum total weight, which must be the solution to
\mathcal{I}
.

We then consider the computational complexity of the whole procedure. Converting the problem takes time
O(m|\mathcal{C}|+nm)
. Finding all candidate solutions takes time
O(2^{k}(\max\{|\mathcal{C}|,n\}^{2}m+\max\{|\mathcal{C}|,n\}^{3})
, and choosing the best one takes at most the same quantity of time. Recovering the minimum weighted odd-circuit cover takes time
O(m|\mathcal{C}|)
, and recovering the maximum weighted cut from the minimum weighted odd-circuit cover takes time
O(m)
. Therefore, the total running time is
O(2^{k}(\max\{|\mathcal{C}|,n\}^{2}m+\max\{|\mathcal{C}|,n\}^{3})
.
∎

We will prove that we can find a Eulerian-spanning set satisfying
k
-frequent appearance condition for
k
-contraction apex graphs efficiently later. Despite this, we cannot apply the above theorem in a black-box way to get Theorem
1.2
, because the running time is far from what we desired. In fact, we have to utilize the planarity of

to cut down the running time. The remaining of this section is devoted to proving Theorem

.

We present definitions and set up notations first.

Definition 6.2

(
edge contraction for graphs)
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a graph. For
E_{1}\subseteq E
, we denote the resulting graph after contracting all edges in
E_{1}
while preserving multiple edges and self-loops by
G/E_{1}
. When
E_{1}=\{e\}
is a set of a single edge, we use
G/e
as a shorthand for
G/\{e\}
.

Therefore the contraction of an edge set
E_{1}
can be viewed as contracting edges in
E_{1}
one by one.

We need a definition of edge contraction for subgraphs.

Definition 6.3

(
edge contraction for subgraphs)
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a graph, and
H=(V_{2},E_{2})
is a subgraph of
G
. Let
e\in E,e=(v_{1},v_{2})
. If
e\in E_{2}
, the contraction
H/e
is defined exactly the same way as in Definition
6.2
. When
e\notin E_{2}
, the contraction is defined as follows:
H/e
is the graph obtained by seeing
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
as equal, while preserving multiple edges and self-loops. The contraction of a subgraph with respect to an edge set
E_{1}\subseteq E
, which we will denote by
H/E_{1}
, is just the graph obtained by contracting edges in
E_{1}
one by one.

Remark 6.4
.

One can easily verify that
H/E_{1}
is well defined. Moreover, for any subgraph
H
and edge set
E_{1}\subseteq E
,
H/E_{1}
can be viewed as a subgraph of
G/E_{1}
.

We still need a lemma before we explain how to find a Eulerian-spanning set for
k
-contraction apex graphs.

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a graph,
e\in E
, and
\mathcal{C}
is a Eulerian-spanning set of
G
. Then
\mathcal{C}/e\triangleq\{C_{i}/e:C_{i}\in\mathcal{C},C_{i}/e\text{ has at least one edge}\}
is a Eulerian-spanning set of
G/e
. Moreover, for any edge
e_{1}
in
G/e
, the number of elements in
\mathcal{C}/e
containing it is no more than the number of elements in
\mathcal{C}
containing its corresponding edge in
G
.

Proof.

Obviously
\mathcal{C}/e\subseteq C(G/e)
. Suppose that
C\in C(G/e)
, then
\exists C^{\prime}\in C(G),C^{\prime}/e=C
. If
C^{\prime}
is the symmetric difference between
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{l}\in\mathcal{C}
, then
C
is obviously the symmetric difference between
C_{1}/e,C_{2}/e,\cdots,C_{l}/e
. The latter statement is obvious as well since we do not merge multiple edges or remove self-loops.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a graph. Let
n=|V|,m=|E|
. Then the following two statements are equivalent:

(1) There is a Eulerian-spanning set
\mathcal{C}
of
G
such that the number of edges that appear in at least three elements in
\mathcal{C}
is at most
k
.

(2)
G
is
k
-contraction apex.

Moreover, if
G
is simple, we have:

(I) If condition (2) is satisfied and
P_{k}(G)
is given, we can compute a Eulerian-spanning set for
G
which has size
O(m)
and satisfies condition (1) in time
O(\max\{m,n\}(k+1)^{2})
.

(II) If condition (1) is satisfied, we can find an edge set
P_{k}(G)
within time
O(m|\mathcal{C}|)
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): We set
P_{k}(G)
to be the set of edges that appear in at least three elements in
C
, and consider
G/P_{k}(G)
. By repeatedly applying Lemma
6.5
, we get that
G/P_{k}(G)
has a Eulerian-spanning set
\mathcal{C}_{1}
such that each edge in
G/P_{k}(G)
is contained in at most two elements in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, because edges in
P_{k}(G)
are no longer in
G/P_{k}(G)
. We then transform
\mathcal{C}_{1}
to a cycle basis such that each edge in
G/P_{k}(G)
is contained in at most two cycles in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, and by the Mac Lane Planarity Criterion conclude the proof.

Suppose there is a n element
C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
such that
C_{i}
is not a cycle. Then
C_{i}
is the disjoint union of cycles. Take any cycle
C_{i1}\in C_{i}
. We distinguish between three cases:

(i) If
C_{i1}
cannot be represented by the symmetric difference of elements in
\mathcal{C}_{1}\setminus\{C_{i}\}
, we replace
C_{i}
by
C_{i1}
in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

(ii) If (i) doesn’t hold, and
C_{i}\setminus C_{i1}
cannot be represented by the symmetric difference of elements in
\mathcal{C}_{1}\setminus\{C_{i}\}
, we replace
C_{i}
by
C_{i}\setminus C_{i1}
in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

(iii) If neither (i) nor (ii) holds, we remove
C_{i}
from
\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

By basic linear algebra, after the modification,
\mathcal{C}_{1}
will still be a Eulerian-spanning set of
G/P_{k}(G)
. Moreover, after the modification, the total size of elements in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
decreases, and the number of appearance of any edge in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
cannot increase. If we repeat the above procedure until we cannot do it anymore, we will finally get a Eulerian-spanning set consisting of cycles, such that any edge cannot appear in more than two cycles in the basis. After picking a maximum independent subset, we get the cycle basis we want.

Moreover, since we can find
P_{k}(G)
by simply counting the number of appearance of any edge in
\mathcal{C}
, which can be done in time

, we prove (II).

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): We construct a Eulerian-spanning set
\mathcal{C}
for
G
such that the number of edges that appear in at least three elements in

is at most

.
\mathcal{C}
consists of two parts:

(i) Partition

Now we consider the computational complexity of this part when

cannot exceed that of

. Sweeping and recording all faces of

takes time at most
O(m)
, because every edge in

is on the boundary of at most 2 faces. Because each edge in

can appear in at most two elements in

, the total size of elements in

cannot exceed

. In the construction of

takes at most the same amount of time. Therefore, computing

Finally, the total running time is the sum of that in (i) and (ii), which is

and

∎

We first present the definition of

[

]
and crossing number of a given embedding.

Definition 7.1
.

A graph

is
s

times. The crossing number of an embedding is obtained in the following way: count the crossings separately for each pair of edges, and for each crossing point of the pair of edges.

We say a

has a

-planar embedding of crossing number

, if
G
can be embedded in the plane such that each edge is crossed at most
s
times, and the crossing number of this embedding is
k

We define the crossing number of

to be the minimum crossing number among all embeddings of

Remark 7.2
.

Given an embedding of
G
on the plane, if there are at least three edges that intersect in one point, we can adjust the edges a little in a local way such that each pair of edges intersect in a different point, without changing the total number of crossings. Therefore, we will always assume that
G
is drawn on the plane so that situations in which three edges intersect in one point do not occur.

Lemma 7.3

Theorem 7.5
.

is a simple graph with a planar embedding of crossing number

. Then there is an

G

For any edge

do the following: let a point move from

to

8
Conclusion and discussion

In this work, we generalize Hadlock’s conversion of maxcut on planar graphs to one on general graphs with non-negative edge weights. We first convert the weighted maxcut problem to minimum weighted odd-circuit cover problem by means similar to Hadlock’s. Then, by introducing the concept of Eulerian-spanning set and coboundary operator, we convert the problem of finding minimum weighted odd-circuit cover to finding a minimum weighted edge set with specific coboundary. By enumerating edges that appear at least three times in the Eulerian-spanning set, and solving the rest of the problem using Lemma
5.1
, we finally solve the problem and recover the answer to maxcut.

We obtain an algorithm finding the maximum cut of
k
-contraction apex graphs parameterized by
k
. We modified our algorithm to find a maximum cut on a graph with crossing number
k
.

However, after we proved our result on
k
-contraction apex graphs, we realized that there is a direct algorithm finding the weighted maxcut within the same time limit. Specifically, we can enumerate the all possible partition within each connected component in
V,P_{k}(G)
in time
O(2^{k})
. After we fixed a partition within each connected component, the rest of the problem can be solved using the maxcut algorithm on planar graphs in time
O(\max\{m,n\}^{3/2}\log\max\{m,n\})
. Therefore the total running time is
O(2^{k}\max\{m,n\}^{3/2}\log\max\{m,n\})
. We haven’t found results describing the above algorithm for
k
-contraction apex graphs yet.

There are several possible directions of future work. First, a shortcoming of our result is that the set
P_{k}(G)
is either included as input, or found efficiently in special cases, such as the graph being 2-planar. In general, we can only apply a brute force search to find the
P_{k}(G)
if it is not revealed to us, which induces a heavy
\Omega(n^{k+1})
running-time overhead. In future work, one may try to develop a more efficient algorithm finding the edge set
P_{k}(G)
when it is not included in the input. Second, our conversion only covers the case of non-negative weights. One may try to generalize it to the setting of real weights. Third, one may try to directly generalize the planar duality to the setting of general graphs.

Appendix A
NP-completeness of WSSD

Unfortunately, solving the decision version of WSSD, i.e. Problem
4.7
, turns out to be NP-complete, even for unweighted version, and even if we restrict the size of any
S_{i}
to be either 2 or 3. We will prove the above results in this section.

Throughout this section, we will use
S,\mathcal{S},T,w,r
to denote an instance of Problem
4.7
. If the weight function is omitted, we mean that the weight of any subset is 1.

First, we notice that Problem
4.7
is in NP since any
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
satisfying
\triangle_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{T}}S_{i}=T
, and
|\mathcal{T}|\leq r
is a proof. Therefore, we only need to prove the NP-hardness in the following theorems.

Theorem A.1
.

Problem
4.7
under the restriction that
w\equiv 1
is NP-complete.

Proof.

To see that the problem is NP-hard, we reduce the decision version of finding a maximum cut in a Hamiltonian graph (where the Hamiltonian cycle can be included in the input) to the problem in the theorem, where the former has been proven to be NP-complete.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a simple graph with Hamiltonian cycle
C_{H}
. We construct a Eulerian-spanning set
\mathcal{C}
of
G
as follows:

First we include
C_{H}
into
\mathcal{C}
. Then, for every edge
e\notin C_{H}
, take a cycle
C_{e}\subseteq C_{H}\cup e
such that
e\in C_{e}
and include it into
\mathcal{C}
. We need to prove
\mathcal{C}
is indeed a Eulerian-spanning set.

If the sum of elements in
\mathcal{C}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is the empty set, then none of

Theorem A.2

Proof.

Step 1. In step 1, we eliminate all subsets of size 1. Suppose

S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{S_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,S_{m}\},\ S_{k}^{\prime}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &S_{k},\ if\ s\notin S_{k}\\

Then

. Moreover, the number of sets of size 1 in

. Therefore, by repeating the above procedure, we will finally get an instance

\mathcal{S}^{(1)}
are of size at least 2.

Step 2. We give every set in
\mathcal{S}^{(1)}
a weight of
p=|S^{(1)}|+2
, and denote the weight function by

. Now we have an instance, i.e.
S^{(1)},\mathcal{S}^{(1)},T^{(1)},w,pr

S^{(1)},\mathcal{S}^{(1)},T^{(1)},r

\mathcal{S}
in this step. Suppose
