In 1951 Ugine G s president, Andre Perrin, a man in his sixties,
was very concerned ever US economic policy toward France, which
he felt was in many ways a/greater threat to French stability
than Communism. His fears seemed to stem from a feeling that
the US was t rying to Americanise French industry toorapidly.

He thought that a vast amount of modem US industrial equip-
ment had been installed in France which resulted in a marked
increase in French industrial production and activity. He felt,
however, that the increase in production was coming too rapidly
for the French economy to stand, much more rapidly than a similar
increase had ever taken place in US industry.

He was of the opinion that US authorities had failed to .
consider French industrial traditions and the comparative poverty
of the French resources P He was sceptical of the practicability
of a US-type economy in France 0 He was convinced that there
would be serious social disorders among the working forces if
