DE-FG02-02ER45998: Growth in heterogeneous, evolving macromolecular networks:
 toward functional, biomimetic material

 Technical Report 2026

 This report summarizes research carried out by the Balazs and Matyjaszewski Labs at Pitt
and CMU, respectively, during 2025-26, supported by the DOE grant. It has produced the
following 3 research articles and 2 review publications that acknowledged grant ER45998:

 Computational Modeling of Hyperbranched Polymers

The Balazs lab, in collaboration with the Matyjaszewski lab, extended the theoretical
understanding of hyperbranched macromolecules synthesized by ATRP via meso-scale Dissipative
Particle Dynamics (DPD) simulations. The study modeled the kinetics and structural evolution of
hyperbranched polymers formed from inibramers. These simulations provided a predictive
framework for correlating reaction parameters with branching density and molecular weight
distributions, offering a powerful tool for designing complex polymer topologies that are difficult
to characterize purely through experimentation.

Biswas, S.; Liu, Y.; Yashin, V. V.; Lin, T.-C.; Kapil, K.; Kowalewski, T.; Matyjaszewski, K.; Balazs,
A. C., Modeling hyperbranched polymer formation via ATRP using dissipative particl
dynamics, Polymer 2024, 312, 127620.

http://dx.doi.org/https://doi.org/10.1016/j.polymer.2024.127620.

 Synthesis of Structurally Tailored Networks

The Matyjaszewski lab combined Living Ring-Opening Metathesis Polymerization (ROMP) with
Atom Transfer Radical Polymerization (ATRP). By utilizing a dual-functional inimer, the team
synthesized Structurally Tailored and Engineered Macromolecular (STEM) networks. This method
allowed for the precise transition from zero-generation (via ROMP) to first-generation networks
(via photoinduced ATRP), yielding materials with tunable swelling and mechanical properties.
This dual-mechanism approach resolves prior selectivity issues inherent in RAFT/ATRP
combinations.

Yasir, M.; Hu, B.; Lin, T.-C.; Matyjaszewski, K., Synergistic Combination of Living Ring-Opening
Metathesis Polymerization and Atom Transfer Radical Polymerization to Synthesize Structurally
Tailored and Engineered Macromolecular Networks, Langmuir 2025, 41, 378-
      http://dx.doi.org/10.1021/acs.langmuir.4c03654.

3. Sustainable and Oxygen-Tolerant Catalysis

The Matyjaszewski lab developed a robust heterogeneous catalytic system for oxygen-tolerant
photoredox ATRP. The researchers covalently immobilized Eosin Y onto a crosslinked
poly(ethylene glycol)-based ChemMatrix resin (CM-EY). This solid-supported photocatalyst
mediated efficient polymerization under green light in open-to-air conditions, offering superior
stability and recyclability compared to non-covalently bound systems. This advancement
significantly facilitates the removal of catalysts from final products, a crucial step for industrial
applications.

Kapil, K.; Sun, M.; Lin, T.-C.; Murata, H.; Szczepaniak, G.; Kim, K.; DiLuzio, S.; Jeong, J.;
Baumer, M.; Bernhard, S.; Kowalewski, T.; Matyjaszewski, K., Heterogenous catalysis for oxygen
tolerant photoredox atom transfer radical polymerization and small-molecule
dehalogenation, Polymer Chemistry 2024, 15, 4264-4280.
http://dx.doi.org/10.1039/D4PY00899E.
