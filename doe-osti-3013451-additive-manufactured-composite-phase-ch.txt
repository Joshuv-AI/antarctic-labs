 ABSTRACT

Phase-change materials play a critical role in industrial energy storage applications to drive efficiency
improvements, thermal energy management, and carbon emissions reductions. Recently, it has been
shown that rapid solidification of alloys with metastable immiscibility in the liquid phase has the potential
to form unique microstructures in which a low-melting phase is uniformly distributed in a high-melting
matrix. This feature can be exploited using additive manufacturing to produce components with complex
geometries containing such unique phase-change microstructures. Phase-field simulations utilizing high-
performance computing were used to provide a detailed description of the evolution of the active phase
during service in terms of their morphology and composition in different polycrystalline matrix grain
morphologies that are typically produced during additive manufacturing. Phase field simulations were
performed using, MEUMAPPS-SL (Microstructure Evolution Using Massively Parallel Phase-field
Simulations – Solid Liquid) code that was developed in-house by the Oak Ridge National Laboratory.
The simulations utilized the capabilities of the Kestrel supercomputer at the National Renewable Energy
Laboratory. The simulation results were compared with experimental results generated at Siemens
Energy, Inc. The results indicate that the kinetics of liquid spreading along grain boundaries is largely
determined by the mobility of the triple line along the intersection of the grain boundary liquid and the
grain boundary plane.

 1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 BACKGROUND

The increased emphasis on clean manufacturing makes the role of phase change materials (PCM) in
energy storage and waste-heat recovery increasingly significant in recent years [1-2]. PCMs using
metallic alloys have a significant advantage compared to other non-metals because of their higher thermal
conductivity and volumetric latent heat content [3]. The potential for designing metallic PCMs where the
need for a container structure can be eliminated has recently been demonstrated using an Al-Sn alloy [4]
that exhibits metastable immiscibility in the liquid, as shown in figure 1.

Microstructures in which the Sn phase, which acts as the active PCM, can be distributed in a continuous,
matrix Al -phase that acts the container material, have been produced in simple geometries using a
powder bed fusion based additive manufacturing (AM) process [4]. While the Al-Sn system can be used
for applications just above the melting point of Sn (501.3K), the study initiated a new paradigm for
designing a class of container-less, composite PCMs (CC-PCM)s over a range of operating temperatures
using the large number of alloys that are known to demonstrate metastable liquid immiscibility [5]. The
significant advantage of the CC-PCM over conventional C-PCM is that the matrix phase, that now acts as
the container, can be rendered multi-functional. Since AM processes are ideally suited for manufacturing
components with complex geometries with internal features such as cooling channels, it is possible to
design the active AM component such that it acts as the heat exchanger. Given the flexibility of the AM
processes to changes in internal details, it is possible to design the optimum distribution of the cooling
channels to achieve the maximum allowable heat transfer characteristics the metallic system can offer.

This research addressed one of the main limitations of AM processed CC-PCMs, namely, the wetting of
the active phase on the matrix grain boundaries that weakens the structural integrity of the matrix during
repeated melting and solidification [4] due to the formation of long, continuous grain boundary films of
liquid Sn. In addition, the long liquid columns can potentially be extruded to the free surface during
service resulting in the loss of the active phase. The proposed strategy to address this challenge is to
trigger a columnar to equiaxed transition (CET) in the matrix phase that has proved to be highly
beneficial in preventing solidification cracking during AM processing [6]. In addition, the tortuous nature
of grain boundary surfaces in fine equiaxed matrix grains may reduce the tendency for the formation of
long liquid films. Existing literature indicates that CET in Al-Sn alloys could be triggered by intermetallic
particles that precipitate in the liquid in Al-Sn-Mn [7] and Al-Sn-Ti [8] systems that help in distributing
Sn on Al grain boundaries in a discontinuous fashion, thereby reducing the formation of continuous grain
boundary liquid films. Interestingly, it has been shown that when the liquid droplet size is reduced, the
wettability of Sn liquid on Al grain boundaries is reduced [9]. Similar strategies could be explored in
other miscibility gap alloys by optimizing the alloy composition and AM process parameters. In this
research, the focus was to utilize high performance computing (HPC) based phase field (PF) simulations
to develop a fundamental insight into the influence of thermal excursions above the melting point of the
active phase on the microstructural stability of the active phase in the PCM microstructure. The
simulations considered various microstructural scenarios in which the morphology matrix grains were
varied from columnar to equiaxed. The PF simulations were performed using the MEUMAPPS code
developed at the Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) [10, 11]. The model alloy system used in this
research was Fe-Cu binary alloy [12] that shows liquid immiscibility like Al-Si. However, the working
temperature of the alloy is the melting temperature of Cu which is significantly higher than that for Si in
the Al-Si alloy.

 2. APPROACH

In phase field models, the interface between two phases is represented as a diffuse boundary along with
the concentration field and the phase fraction field (described by an order parameter) continuously
varying one phase to the other. The representation of the diffuse interface allows the interface evolution to
be tracked without explicitly considering flux boundary conditions across the moving interface. This
simplicity comes at the cost of making some assumptions used to link the concentration and the order
parameter across the interface. The phase field simulation effort in this research was accomplished in two
stages. In the first stage, a hypothetical AB binary alloy was chosen with a simple parabolic
approximation for the Gibbs energies of the solid and liquid phases as a function of solute concentration.
The objective of these simplified simulations was to demonstrate wetting of grain boundary liquid as a
function of the microstructural and kinetic parameters. For this effort, a multi-phase field model proposed
by Chang and Moelans [13] was integrated with the MEUMAPPS phase field code.

2.2 KIM-KIM-SUZUKI MODEL

In the second phase of the project, the phase field simulations were performed for a real alloy system of
interest in thermal-energy storage applications. After initial considerations of Al-Sn and Fe-Cu alloy
systems, Fe-Cu was chosen as the candidate alloy. Fe-Cu alloy also shows liquid immiscibility as shown
for Al-Sn (Figure 1). However, it allows a higher working temperature compared to the Al-Sn alloy,
which was of interest. For this purpose, the phase field model proposed by Kim, Kim and Suzuki (KKS)
[14], which is the default model used in MEUMAPPS was used. The governing equations in the KKS

interface is assumed to be made up of different ratios of solid and liquid concentrations for which the
chemical potentials of the solute(s) are equal in the solid and liquid phases. Accordingly,

 3. SIMULATIONS

As mentioned previously, the major focus of the simulations was to investigate the wetting and spreading
of the grain boundary liquid that determines the stability of the phase change microstructure. The
simulations considered three different initial geometric conditions: (a) planar grain boundary to capture
the wetting phenomenon in columnar grains, (b) isolated spherical grain and (c) grain boundary surfaces
in a polycrystalline grain structure to capture the effect of boundary curvature on wetting behavior. Initial
simulations were performed using the Chang-Moelans model for a hypothetical AB binary. After
demonstrating the wetting behavior in the hypothetical alloy, the simulations were extended to the Fe-Cu
binary alloy with two different compositions, 15 and 30 mole percent Cu. These simulations were
performed using the KKS and the LKKS model. The simulations were performed by varying the

3.2.1 Model Validation

As mentioned previously, the standard KKS model could not handle the presence of multiple phases at a
location without the presence of the matrix phase because the formulation of the standard KKS model
assumed that multiple phases at any given location always co-existed with the matrix phase. Therefore,
the LKKS was implemented into the MEUMAPPS phase field code and used for investigating the Fe-Cu
system. As a first step, the LKKS model was validated by simulating the growth of a single spherical
liquid phase situated at the center of cubic volume of 100 × 100 × 100. The Cu concentration was 0.15
mole fraction and the temperature was 1380K. All the simulations using the LKKS model were performed
using a mesh resolution of 1 × 10-9 m.

In earlier simulations using the Chang-Moelans model, the dihedral angle between the liquid and the grain
boundary under wetting conditions (s-s energy = 2 × s-l energy) did not approach the equilibrium value.
Under these conditions, the contact angle according to Young’s law should be zero and the liquid should
completely wet the boundary. The contact angle, however, stabilized around 45°, as shown in Figures 2
and 3. Similar results were obtained using LKKS model in Fe-Cu system as shown in Figure 13. The
simulations shown in Figure 13 were performed in a 160 × 160 × 400 simulation volume. In order to
eliminate kinetic effects due to solute diffusion, the liquid was considered as a phase with the same
composition as the matrix so that the evolution of the liquid was governed primarily by the imbalance in
the surface energies. However, there is a thermodynamic driving force as well as a capillary force that
tends to change of the volume of the liquid phase during its morphological evolution. In this case, the
liquid phase of the same composition as the solid had a lower Gibbs energy and therefore the liquid phase
tends to grow with time. The grain boundary energy in the current simulation was 3.3 times higher than

line. If the critical value is set too small, then a fairly large number of points that are outside the triple line
will be included, while a too high a value will exclude some of the triple line sites. In the current
simulations, a critical value of 0.001 was used after much trial and error.

 4. EXPERIMENTS

The objective of this effort was to investigate the morphology of liquid phase in Fe-Cu and Fe-Cu-Cr
alloys during Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF) additive manufacturing (AM) to provide experimental
support for validating the phase field simulations. AM experiments were performed on Fe-10wt% Cu and
Fe-10wt%Cu-20wt%Cr alloys, including powder synthesis by gas atomization, microstructural
evolution, and phase stability. The experimental effort was initiated by Siemens Corporation and
performed at The University of Alabama.

4.1 METHODS

4.1.1 Powder Production

Fe-10Cu and Fe-10Cu-20Cr powders were produced using an inert close-coupled gas atomizer (
HERMIGA 75/5 VI, Phoenix Scientific Industries Ltd, UK) in the Alabama Atomization Facility
at the University of Alabama. Iron bars (99.8% purity), copper rectangular bars (99.9% purity),
and Fe-Cr chunks (99.8% purity) were loaded in an alumina crucible and remelted by vacuum
induction melting in the gas atomizer. Once the molten material reached the superheat temperature,
the melt chamber was overpressure to 0.25 bar, and the molten material was fed through a 2.5 mm
diameter transfer tube into an argon gas atomization nozzle. The atomization gas pressure varied
between runs to optimize particle size distribution. Powders were collected at the bottom of the
atomization chamber and allowed to naturally cool in place under an argon atmosphere. Powders
were sieved into >200 µm, 200–45 µm, 15–45 µm, and <15 µm fractions using a high energy
sifting machine (FS-UC 401, ELCAN Industries, Tuckahoe, NY, USA). The morphology of the
Fe-10Cu powders was investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with an Apreo FE-
SEM from Thermo Scientific.

4.1.2 LPBF processing

An AconityMID Laser Powder Bed Fusion machine was used for building the 3D objects selected
for this study. A laser power of 240 W, scan velocity of 1142 mm/s, a hatch spacing of 80 m, and
a layer per thickness of 30 m was used to print Fe-10Cu 5 X 10 X 5mm blocks. Fe-10Cu-20Cr
cylinders of 11 mm diameter by 16.5 mm length were printed using 175 W laser power, 1200 mm/s
scan velocity, hatch spacing of 80 m, and a layer per thickness of 30 m.

4.1.3 Characterization

Heat treatment of the LPBF samples was performed in a tube furnace following a two-step
treatment: 950 °C for 12 h (Argon cool), then 1150 °C for 15 min (water quench). Thermal cycling
of the LPBF Fe-10Cu-20Cr was performed in a Gleeble machine following: 0–1300 °C with 10
°C/s heating rate and 10-s hold. LPBF samples (as- printed and as heat-treated) were sectioned, hot
mounted and polished to a 0.05 um colloidal silica finish. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with a
Apreo FE-SEM from Thermo Scientific was used for analysis of the microstructure of the cross-section.
Composition mapping was performed using x-ray energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) conducted on an
Apreo FE-SEM from Thermo Scientific. Electron back scatter diffraction was performed using an Oxford
HKLNordlys EBSD camera with a step size of 200 um. A Phillips X’Pert x-ray diffractometer was
used with a Cu-Kα x-ray source and an angular step size of 0.05° at a collection time of 50 s/°for
phase identification of the Fe-10Cu powder and LPBF sample.

 5. SUMMARY

Two multi-phase phase-field models, the Chang-Moelans and the LKKS models, were integrated with the
MEUMAPPS phase-field code in order to investigate the wetting behavior of Cu-rich liquid on grain
boundaries in FCC iron. Three different grain boundary geometries were considered in order to cover the

 24

range of grain morphologies that result in powder-bed based AM processing of Fe-Cu alloys. Planar grain
boundaries were used to investigate spreading of liquid on columnar grains. A spherical grain
morphology was used to investigate the effect of grain boundary curvature in the absence of grain
boundary triple lines. Polycrystalline grain structures were used to investigate the spreading kinetics in
equiaxed grain structures.

Simulations using the Chang-Moelans model were used to demonstrate wetting behavior of a hypothetical
B-rich liquid in a A-B binary alloy. The following key findings were obtained using the Chang-Moelans
model for the A-B binary alloy:

 (a) increasing the grain boundary diffusion coefficient compared to the diffusion in bulk results in
 preferential coarsening of the grain boundary liquid, with the larger liquid phase particles
 consuming the smaller ones, leading to the formation of large and discrete liquid phase
 morphology on the grain boundaries.
 (b) Liquid phase particles below the critical nucleus size dissolved in the matrix through diffusion in
 the matrix.
 (c) In all simulations where the grain boundary energy was higher than the soli-liquid interfacial
 energy, significant spreading as expected based on the theoretical dihedral angle considerations
 did not occur, although under extreme wetting conditions, a lower dihedral angle compared to the
 isotropic case was obtained.
 (d) A lower dihedral angle was also associated with liquid phase dissolution on the grain boundary

While the Chang-Moelans model provided some key findings on grain boundary liquid evolution based
solely on hypothetical parameters, extending the model to real alloys integrating the appropriate alloy
thermodynamics is not straightforward. PF formulation based on the KKS [14] allow for efficient
coupling of the diffuse interface between phases with the alloy thermodynamics and the kinetics of
interface migration.

The implementation of the LKKS model in MEUMAPPS and investigation of the Fe-Cu led to the key
finding that in order to kinetically capture the wetting of a low-energy s-l interface with a high-energy
grain boundary, the phase field mobility of the triple junction has to be increased by a factor that depends
on the relative interfacial energies. Although, the factor cannot be calculated at the mesoscale, it captures
the principle that the triple point mobility is related to the mechanisms of atom transfer across the junction
that becomes more efficient when the bounding interfaces have a more disordered structure. This finding
was in clear agreement with experimental experiments that showed that highly elongated liquid
morphologies were found on random, high-angle grain boundaries. The simulations also appeared to be in
agreement with the experimental finding that the extent of wetting is reduced on interfaces that were
associated with the BCC phase that promotes high back diffusion.

Since the LKKS model provides a convenient and well-founded approach for thermodynamic integration,
it is possible to extend the simulations to more complex alloys that are known to exhibit metastable liquid
immiscibility that can be processed by rapid solidification to yield unique phase-change microstructures.
More recently, a neural net (NN) based thermodynamic model has been integrated into MEUMAPPS [21]
and has been successfully used to investigate solidification microstructures during AM of the Ni-base
superalloy IN 718.

Both the Chang-Moelans model and the LKKS model indicated that the intragranular liquid phase retains
the spherical morphology, in agreement experimental results, although rare instances of non-spherical
morphologies were observed in the simulations due to the coalescence of closely spaced particles.

 25

The simulations provide important guidelines for PCM alloy design. First, decreasing the particle size to a
value less than the critical nucleus size will promote the dissolution of the grain boundary particles and
eliminate wetting. Therefore, it would be beneficial to have many fine particles through rapid
solidification approaches such as additive manufacturing. Alloys with metastable miscibility gap such as
Fe-Cu are known to produce such fine microstructures as shown in the literature, as well as by the current
experimental effort. However, the grain boundaries must be pinned by an inert grain boundary phase
particle through the Zener pinning effect, so that once the interacting liquid phase particles dissolve by
diffusion, the inert particles would still be intact and prevent the boundaries from migrating and capturing
more of the liquid phase particles situated in the grain interior. Low-angle grain boundaries with low
interfacial energies are clearly beneficial.
