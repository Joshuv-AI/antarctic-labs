Prospective Evaluation of Cross-linked Sodium Hyaluronate Gel for Injection in Treating Moderate-to-Severe Nasolabial Folds: A Randomized Controlled Evaluator-Blind Non-inferiority Study
Authors: Xinlyu Huang, Yankun Zhang, Jie Dong, Xiaowei Qin, Shixin Yang, Jun Li, Lanying Qin, Fang Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThis study evaluates a novel lidocaine-premixed hyaluronic acid (HA) gel for nasolabial fold (NLF) correction, addressing pain management and longevity challenges in facial rejuvenation. The aim is to assess the non-inferiority, efficacy, and safety of this investigational HA gel versus an established comparator.MethodsIn this multicenter, evaluator-blinded randomized controlled trial (RCT), 324 Chinese adults with moderate-to-severe NLFs (Wrinkle Severity Rating Scale [WSRS] 3–4) received either test product (22 mg/mL HA + 3 mg/mL lidocaine) or control. The primary endpoint was ≥ 1 grade WSRS improvement at 6 months.ResultsThe test product showed non-inferiority at 6 months (full analysis set [FAS]: 88.27% versus 85.19%, P = 0.392; per‑protocol set (PPS): 91.45% versus 88.24%, P = 0.363), with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) exceeding a −10% margin. Pain scores were comparable at 0.5 h/1 h post-injection (P > 0.05) but higher at 2 h post-touch-up (P < 0.05). Adverse event (AE) rates were similar, with induration/swelling being most common.ConclusionsThe lidocaine-premixed HA gel is non-inferior for NLF correction, offering similar safety and efficacy with integrated anesthesia.

Introduction

Skin aging is characterized by rhytides and laxity, with nasolabial folds (NLFs) being among the most prominent age-related changes. These folds extend from the nasal alae to oral commissures and worsen owing to midface volume loss from fat atrophy and bone resorption [1, 2]. Hyaluronic acid (HA), a naturally occurring glycosaminoglycan, provides structural support in the dermal matrix, maintaining skin hydration and elasticity [3, 4]. Among the diverse array of minimally invasive esthetic interventions that either complement or serve as viable alternatives to traditional surgical rhytidectomy, the use of hyaluronic-acid-based dermal fillers has garnered exponentially increasing attention in contemporary dermatologic practice [5].

In recent years, advanced chemical modification techniques have enabled the development of cross-linked sodium hyaluronate formulations that demonstrate substantially increased molecular weight while remarkably preserving the exceptional biocompatibility profile inherent to native HA molecules [6]. This molecular stabilization through cross-linking confers enhanced resistance to enzymatic degradation primarily via hyaluronidases while simultaneously prolonging the residence duration in vivo, thereby significantly expanding the potential biomedical applications to include not only soft-tissue augmentation but also roles as anti-adhesion barriers, tissue engineering matrices, and advanced drug delivery vehicles [7, 8]. Various commercial products (e.g., Restylane®, Yvoire®, and Juvéderm VOLIFT™) demonstrate effective wrinkle correction and lip augmentation, with effects lasting 6–12 months and an excellent safety profile [9–11].

Pain during injection remains a major patient concern. While topical anesthetic preparations are widely employed in clinical settings, they characteristically exhibit delayed onset of action and frequently demonstrate incomplete analgesic efficacy. To address these limitations, innovative modified sodium hyaluronate gel filler formulations premixed with lidocaine hydrochloride have been developed, offering the dual advantages of rapid-onset pain reduction during injection, minimization of pretreatment preparation time, and accelerated postoperative recovery.

This study evaluates a cross-linked sodium hyaluronate gel for injection (Jinan Grandwill Medical Technology Co., Ltd.) containing 22 mg/mL HA and 3 mg/mL lidocaine. A prospective, multicenter, randomized, controlled non-inferiority trial was conducted in Chinese adults with moderate-to-severe NLFs (WSRS 3–4), comparing the test product with Q-Med AB’s control. The study complied with Chinese regulatory and ethical standards.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This clinical trial adopted a rigorously controlled prospective, multicenter, evaluator-blinded, randomized, controlled non-inferiority design with 1:1 allocation randomization. Participants meeting all inclusion criteria were randomly assigned to receive either the test product (cross-linked sodium hyaluronate gel for injection manufactured by Jinan Grandwill Medical Technology Co., Ltd.) or the active control product (modified sodium hyaluronate gel for injection produced by Q-Med AB) administered through standardized mid-to-deep dermal injection techniques in the facial region, specifically targeting correction of moderate-to-severe nasolabial folds. The study protocol incorporated two distinct phases: an initial treatment/correction phase (encompassing the injection procedure and immediate follow-up within 1 month post-injection) and a subsequent comprehensive observation phase (extending from 1 month post-injection until final follow-up completion), with scheduled efficacy and safety evaluations conducted at baseline and at precisely defined intervals of 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after the final injection procedure. The study involved five sites. The same camera setup was used by all five sites. Unified imaging protocols were used across centers.

Eligible participants included male or female subjects aged between 18 and 65 years with visibly prominent nasolabial folds or contour depressions on both sides of the face that were deemed appropriate for dermal filler correction, as objectively evidenced by a Wrinkle Severity Rating Scale (WSRS) grade of 3–4 for each fold (with allowance for reasonable asymmetry between left and right sides), who voluntarily provided informed consent by signing institutional review board-approved consent forms, demonstrated willingness and ability to comply with all study protocol requirements and complete all mandated follow-up visits, and explicitly agreed to abstain from undergoing any additional facial cosmetic procedures or unauthorized medical treatments during the entire study period.

Key exclusion criteria comprehensively included: active infections, open wounds, or neoplastic lesions in the proposed treatment area; history of prior permanent or semi-permanent filler injections or major facial surgical procedures within 12 months preceding enrollment; recent receipt of anti-wrinkle treatments (≤ 6 months); diagnosed immunosuppression or active inflammatory skin diseases; severe systemic or immune disorders; bleeding diathesis or chronic anticoagulant use (unless discontinued ≥ 2 weeks prior); pregnancy or lactation status; planned major facial or dental procedures during study participation; significant weight-altering interventions; recent dental surgeries (≤ 3 months) or participation in other clinical trials (≤ 3 months); documented hypersensitivity to lidocaine or HA components; and any other condition deemed clinically significant by the principal investigator that would contraindicate participation.

The control product is composed of a gel particle suspension consisting of sodium hyaluronate cross-linked by 1,4-Butanediol diglycidyl ether (BDDE), lidocaine hydrochloride, sodium chloride, sodium dihydrogen phosphate, disodium hydrogen phosphate, and water for injection. The sodium hyaluronate is prepared by microbial fermentation, with a labeled concentration of cross-linked sodium hyaluronate of 20 mg/mL and a labeled concentration of lidocaine hydrochloride of 3 mg/mL. The package is equipped with 1–2 needles (29G TW × 1/2″) for injection.

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Chao‑Yang Hospital, Capital Medical University (approval no. 2022‑7) and by the ethics committees of all participating centers: Shanxi Provincial People’s Hospital (approval no. [2022] provincial medical device review no. 9), Shanxi Bethune Hospital (approval no. [2022] ethics review for medical devices no. (09)), Nanjing Women and Children’s Healthcare Hospital (approval no. 2022FYYL017‑01), and Cangzhou People’s Hospital (approval no. 2022‑040‑02). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. Consent for publication of identifiable material was obtained from all patients.

Procedures

Prior to treatment, all participants underwent thorough medical evaluations, including detailed medical histories and current medication reviews. Standardized pretreatment photographs of the nasolabial folds were captured using consistent imaging equipment and lighting across study sites. A single certified investigator, blinded to treatment assignments, performed all Wrinkle Severity Rating Scale (WSRS) assessments. Triplicate photographs of each treatment site were systematically collected at every follow-up.

An independent, masked review committee of three board-certified physicians evaluated all images. Two primary evaluators, trained in WSRS scoring, independently assessed photographs per predefined criteria. Discrepancies (≥ 1 point WSRS difference) were resolved by a third adjudicator, with the majority score determining the final rating.

The test product was administered using 27G needles supplied with the device, while the control product was administered using 29G needles as per the manufacturer’s specifications. All injections were performed using standardized mid-to-deep dermal injection techniques. Optional touch-up procedures were permitted on the basis of investigator assessment at the 1-month follow-up visit.

The primary efficacy endpoint assessed non-inferiority in wrinkle improvement at 6 months post-treatment via blinded photographic evaluation. Secondary outcomes included investigator and subject ratings of wrinkle severity, esthetic improvement, dose required for optimal correction, and treatment duration. Safety assessments comprised documented injection-site reactions, subject-reported symptoms, investigator-recorded adverse events (AEs/SAEs), and laboratory tests (hematology, chemistry, and urinalysis). The study was powered to compare the test product’s safety and efficacy versus the control for nasolabial fold correction.

Efficacy Endpoints

The study employed both full analysis set (FAS) and per-protocol set (PPS) analytical approaches to rigorously evaluate treatment effects across all predefined endpoints.

The primary endpoint focused on the WSRS improvement rate at 6 months post-final injection as assessed by blinded independent photographic evaluation compared to baseline measurements. Treatment responders were strictly defined as subjects demonstrating ≥ 1 grade improvement in bilateral WSRS scores from baseline at all post-treatment assessment timepoints.

Secondary endpoints systematically included: subject-reported visual analog scale (VAS) pain scores recorded at 0.5 h, 1 h, and 2 h post-treatment; serial WSRS score changes (assessed via both blinded photographic evaluation and on-site investigator assessments) at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months; WSRS improvement rates (photographic assessment at 1, 3, and 12 months; on-site investigator assessment at all intervals); and both investigator- and subject-rated Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale (GAIS) improvement rates at all scheduled follow-ups.

Safety Assessment

Local adverse events were defined as any undesirable clinical sign or symptom occurring at or in the immediate vicinity of the injection site, including expected post-injection reactions such as induration, swelling, tenderness, erythema, bruising, and pruritus. All events were captured regardless of anticipated nature, resulting in cumulative incidence rates that reflect the comprehensive capture of transient injection-site reactions over the entire 12-month follow-up period. Severity was graded as: mild (transient, no interference with daily activities, no intervention required), moderate (some interference with daily activities, possible symptomatic treatment), and severe (marked interference with daily activities, active intervention required).

Statistical Analysis

For intergroup comparisons of baseline characteristics, continuous variables were analyzed using independent t-tests (for normally distributed data with homogeneity of variance confirmed by Levene’s test) or nonparametric Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, while categorical data were assessed by χ2 tests. Ordinal data analyses employed either Wilcoxon rank-sum or Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel (CMH) tests as appropriate. All statistical tests were specified as two-sided with α = 0.05 unless otherwise noted, where P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Additional exploratory analyses were conducted as detailed in the prespecified statistical analysis plan (SAP). The trial was statistically powered at 80% (1 − β = 0.80) with a one-sided significance level of α = 0.025 for primary endpoint analysis, incorporating appropriate adjustments for multiple comparisons.

Results

Injection Procedures (FAS)

All participants received single bilateral nasolabial fold injections (utilizing 27G needles for treatment group versus 29G for controls), with optional touch-up procedures permitted based on investigator assessment. Mean injection duration was approximately 4 min per treated side. The treatment group received statistically greater injection volumes than controls (left side: 0.91 ± 0.30 mL versus 0.84 ± 0.29 mL; right side: 0.90 ± 0.29 mL versus 0.81 ± 0.28 mL), resulting in correspondingly higher total doses (1.81 ± 0.57 mL versus 1.65 ± 0.55 mL).

Touch-up injections were performed in 77 participants (24 treatment, 53 control), all targeting bilateral nasolabial folds. The treatment group consistently used 27G needles (versus 29G in controls), with mean injection duration of approximately 3 min per side. Mean injection volumes for touch-up procedures were comparable between groups (left: 0.41 ± 0.12 mL [treatment] versus 0.45 ± 0.11 mL [control]; right: 0.41 ± 0.14 mL versus 0.42 ± 0.10 mL), yielding similar total touch-up doses (0.83 ± 0.18 mL versus 0.87 ± 0.18 mL).

When combining initial and touch-up procedures, mean total injection volumes did not differ significantly between groups for left (0.97 ± 0.36 versus 0.99 ± 0.40 mL; P = 0.860) or right folds (0.96 ± 0.34 versus 0.95 ± 0.39 mL; P = 0.548), or aggregate total dose (1.93 ± 0.68 versus 1.94 ± 0.77 mL; P = 0.903), with identical volume ranges observed (left/right: 0.30–2.00/2.10 mL; total: 0.60–4.00 mL).

Discussion

Cross-linked sodium hyaluronate represents a significant advancement in dermal filler technology, achieving substantially higher molecular weight while meticulously preserving the intrinsic biocompatibility of natural HA molecules. This molecular optimization enhances resistance to enzymatic degradation and prolongs in vivo residence time. The primary clinical application of these formulations is as soft-tissue dermal fillers, with multiple internationally registered products now commercially available [12, 13].

This rigorously conducted randomized controlled trial demonstrates that the investigational cross-linked sodium hyaluronate gel for injection exhibits both clinically meaningful efficacy in correcting moderate-to-severe nasolabial folds and an acceptably favorable safety profile consistent with established products.

In our study population, baseline demographic and clinical characteristics—including age, sex, race, weight, height, history of prior/current head/face treatments, comprehensive medical history, allergy profile, and screening assessments (vital signs, pregnancy tests, urinalysis, complete blood count, blood chemistry panels, and coagulation profiles)—were well-balanced between treatment groups, confirming appropriate comparability for valid statistical analysis.

Primary non-inferiority analysis conclusively demonstrated that there were no significant between-group differences in WSRS improvement rates in either FAS or PPS populations at month 6 post-treatment (left/right sides, all P > 0.05). Detailed statistical analysis confirmed that the lower bound of the 95% confidence interval for treatment difference (−4.29%) exceeded the pre-specified non-inferiority margin of −10%, thereby establishing non-inferiority of the investigational treatment relative to the active comparator (FAS population). These findings were robustly corroborated in the PPS population analysis, where the 95% CI lower bound (−3.56%) similarly surpassed the −10% margin, providing additional confirmation of non-inferiority.

Regarding secondary endpoints, VAS pain scores and blinded photographic WSRS responder rates showed no significant intergroup differences at most timepoints. However, investigator‑assessed WSRS changes favored the treatment group at most follow‑up visits, and both investigator‑ and subject‑rated GAIS responder rates showed significant advantages for the test product at several timepoints (months 6 and 12 for subject‑rated GAIS; months 1, 3, and 12 for investigator‑rated GAIS in the FAS population). These findings suggest a trend toward superior perceived esthetic improvement with the investigational filler, although the primary non‑inferiority objective was met on the basis of the blinded photographic assessment.

Pain management remains a critical consideration in hyaluronic acid-based dermal filler treatments. This study systematically evaluated a novel lidocaine-premixed modified sodium hyaluronate gel formulation, demonstrating generally comparable VAS pain scores between treatment and control groups at 0.5 h and 1 h post-initial injection (bilaterally, all P > 0.05) and following touch-up procedures (all P > 0.05 in both FAS and PPS populations). However, at the 2 h post-touch-up assessment timepoint, significantly higher VAS scores were observed in the treatment group for: left-side PPS analysis (P < 0.05) and right-side FAS and PPS analyses (both P < 0.05), though left-side FAS showed no significant difference (P > 0.05). These results collectively indicate that there is no significant difference in overall pain experience between the trial group and the control group, with the pain levels being well tolerated by participants in both arms. Further comparative studies evaluating pain profiles relative to other commercially available injectable hyaluronic acid products may provide additional valuable insights.

Safety assessment represents another critically important consideration in aesthetic medicine. The comprehensive safety evaluations in this study demonstrated that both treatment groups exhibited comparably favorable safety and tolerability profiles, with no statistically or clinically significant differences in the incidence or severity of adverse events between the FAS or PPS populations.

Our findings corroborate and extend the existing body of evidence indicating that modified sodium hyaluronate gel formulations can effectively correct moderate-to-severe NLFs through precise intradermal volumization [14, 15]. Previous clinical research that recruited 50 patients with moderate to severe NLFs has demonstrated that amino acid-cross-linked HA derivatives show particular promise as advanced biomaterials combining excellent biocompatibility with optimal viscoelastic characteristics for both research and clinical applications [16]. The current study significantly expands upon these earlier findings by incorporating a larger sample size, rigorous comparator-controlled design, and comprehensive 12-month follow-up period, thereby providing more robust evidence to support the use of these innovative filler materials in routine clinical practice.

An important consideration when interpreting the VAS pain score data is the different needle gauges used between groups (27G for the test product versus 29G for the control), which may have influenced subjective pain perception. While both products contained equivalent concentrations of lidocaine (3 mg/mL), the larger gauge needle used in the test group could theoretically contribute to greater tissue trauma and associated discomfort. Despite this potential confounding factor, the overall pain profiles were largely comparable between groups, with no significant differences at most assessed timepoints. This suggests that the analgesic properties of the test formulation are clinically acceptable, though future studies employing standardized needle sizes would be valuable for more precise comparative pain assessment."

Conclusions

This study confirms the non-inferiority of the lidocaine-premixed HA gel compared with an established control for NLF correction, with comparable safety profiles and generally similar pain outcomes, thereby supporting its use in clinical aesthetic practice. Limitations include the single-ethnicity population, a slight age imbalance between groups, and a 12-month follow-up period that precludes assessment of longer-term durability. We acknowledge that photographic variability across centers could introduce potential measurement errors. Future studies with extended follow-up beyond 12 months, standardized needle gauges for pain assessment, and comparative effectiveness evaluations against other lidocaine-containing fillers in diverse populations would further strengthen the evidence base.
