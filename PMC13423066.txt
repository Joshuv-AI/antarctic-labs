Promoting factors of adherence to annual mass drug administration for schistosomiasis among farmers in an endemic Southeast Asian setting: a cross-sectional study in Oriental Mindoro, Philippines
Authors: Takao Suzuki, Gernan Santiago Alejandro, Yuichi Chigusa, Junko Yasuoka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesMass drug administration (MDA) is essential for schistosomiasis elimination; however, evidence on factors promoting adherence in the Philippines is limited. This study aims to investigate these factors among high-risk farmers.DesignA community-based cross-sectional study using a questionnaire survey and abdominal ultrasonography.SettingThis study was conducted in selected barangays in Victoria, Oriental Mindoro, Philippines where the highest prevalence was reported.ParticipantEligible participants were farmers aged ≥18 years who had been directly engaged in paddy farming in Victoria for at least 5 years. Individuals unable to complete the questionnaire or provide informed consent were excluded. A total of 342 participants were enrolled in this study.Primary and secondary outcome measuresThe primary outcome was adherence to annual MDA. Secondary outcome measures demographic characteristics, liver lesion images, schistosomiasis-related history and knowledge (12-item questionnaire).ResultsAmong 342 participants, 65.2% adhered to annual MDA. Ultrasonography findings potentially suggestive of schistosomiasis (Omae type 3) were observed in 37.7% overall (30.5% in non-adherent vs 51.3% in adherent participants). Self-reported history of schistosomiasis was reported by 51.0% (35.5% vs 51.3%). Family history was reported by 41.9% (55.5% vs 34.7%, p<0.001). Knowledge about schistosomiasis was high overall (79.1%) and higher among adherent participants (77.3% vs 82.4%, p<0.001). Variables with p<0.20 in bivariate analysis were included in a multiple logistic regression. Self-reported history showed the strongest association with MDA adherence (adjusted OR 3.608, 95% CI 1.940 to 6.711), whereas ultrasonography findings and family history were not significant.ConclusionA self-reported history of schistosomiasis was most strongly associated with MDA adherence, indicating that subjective awareness of infection is a key factor promoting participation. However, despite high overall knowledge levels, understanding of clinical manifestations remained limited. These findings highlight priority areas for targeted health education, particularly improving recognition of schistosomiasis symptoms, to strengthen participation in control programmes.

Introduction

Schistosomiasis is a zoonotic parasitic disease caused by Schistosoma spp. that is transmitted through skin contact with contaminated freshwater containing cercariae released from snail intermediate hosts and infects humans and other mammals, including livestock.1 2 In the Philippines, schistosomiasis japonica remains a major public health concern, with transmission documented in 28 of 81 provinces and approximately 12.4 million people at risk.1 3 4 A nationwide barangay-level survey conducted between 2014 and 2018 using the Kato-Katz technique reported an overall prevalence of 4.8%, highest in Oriental Mindoro (9.5%), although the true prevalence may be higher due to the method’s limited sensitivity compared with immunoserological assays.5–7 The government launched the National Schistosomiasis Control and Elimination Programme (NSCEP) in 2019, aiming for elimination by 2025, but this goal had not been achieved by 2026. A key challenge is agriculture-driven transmission in endemic areas, where farmers’ prolonged contact with freshwater in paddy fields and irrigation canals that harbour intermediate host snails leads to repeated exposure and elevated infection risk, making farmers a critical target for control efforts.4 8

Current WHO guidelines identify mass drug administration (MDA) with praziquantel as the central strategy for schistosomiasis elimination.9 MDA aims to reduce individual morbidity and suppress transmission in endemic areas by periodically administering antiparasitic drugs to high-risk populations.9 In the Philippines, MDA was implemented in all 28 endemic provinces under a programme initiated in 2007; however, because schistosomiasis is a zoonotic infection involving non-human reservoirs, MDA alone proved insufficient for elimination or sustained control.4 Consequently, the NSCEP adopted a One Health-oriented comprehensive strategy, combining continued MDA targeting residents aged 5–65 years in high-endemic barangays with interventions addressing animal reservoirs, snail control, water, sanitation and hygiene improvements and health education.4 Although the role of MDA has evolved from a stand-alone intervention to a core component of an integrated strategy, it remains indispensable because it can rapidly and cost-effectively reduce morbidity and the human infectious reservoir, thereby serving as a bridge until longer-term interventions take effect.9 10

The success of MDA depends largely on coverage and compliance, and numerous studies have therefore examined facilitators and barriers to uptake.11 12 Reported barriers include limited understanding of the disease, weaknesses in drug supply and programme delivery, reluctance to take praziquantel and post-treatment adverse effects.12 The large size and bitter taste of praziquantel tablets, together with post-treatment symptoms such as abdominal pain, diarrhoea, vomiting and headache resulting from immune responses to dying parasites, may discourage uptake; measures such as administering the drug after meals or providing snacks have been reported to mitigate these effects and improve participation.13–15 Greater knowledge of schistosomiasis and higher perceived risk have consistently been associated with increased participation in MDA.12 Longitudinal studies of compliance have suggested higher adherence among school-aged children, women, individuals with greater awareness of disease risk, and those with a history of infection.11 Evidence from the Philippines is limited. A study conducted in Northern Samar in 2015 by Inobaya et al reported a higher risk of non-compliance among women, whereas farmers and individuals with prior participation in MDA showed greater compliance, and respondents who had heard of schistosomiasis or had higher knowledge levels were less likely to be non-compliant.16

One major evidence gap in Southeast Asia is that most studies on participation in and compliance with MDA have been conducted in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA), while evidence on schistosomiasis japonica in Southeast Asia, particularly in the Philippines, remains limited. Differences in parasite subspecies and sociocultural contexts may restrict the applicability of findings from African settings. In addition, many studies rely mainly on questionnaire data, and few have examined both objectively measured infection status and participants’ subjective understanding of symptoms in relation to MDA participation. Therefore, this study aims to address these gaps by investigating demographic characteristics, schistosomiasis-related history, and knowledge associated with MDA adherence to inform strategies for improving coverage. As schistosomiasis prevalence remains substantial, strengthening elimination strategies centred on MDA is essential. This study also contributes to the limited evidence from Southeast Asia, particularly the Philippines, and, to our knowledge, is the first investigation conducted in Oriental Mindoro, one of the country’s highest prevalence provinces.

Methods

Study design

This was a cross-sectional study conducted in selected barangays along the shoreline of Lake Naujan in Victoria, Oriental Mindoro, from September to October 2025. Data were collected through a questionnaire survey and abdominal ultrasonography among farmers, a high-risk population for schistosomiasis.

Study site

This study was conducted in Victoria, Oriental Mindoro, Philippines, which reported the highest national prevalence of schistosomiasis based on the 2014–2018 Department of Health prevalence survey.7 Within the province, schistosomiasis-endemic municipalities are concentrated along the shoreline of Lake Naujan, including Pola, Socorro, Naujan and Victoria.17 In 2023, three chronic schistosomiasis cases were reported in Victoria according to the Field Health Services Information System, supporting its selection as the study site.18 Eight shoreline barangays (Urdaneta, San Narciso, Malabo, Duongan, Pakyas, Jose Leido Jr., Bambanin and Merit) were selected as study sites. According to the 2020 census, their populations were 1384, 2001, 2138, 1416, 1922, 2134, 1451 and 491, respectively.19

Participant selection

Eligible participants were farmers directly involved in paddy activities, including land preparation, planting, irrigation, weeding, harvesting or the supervision of these activities; had been engaged in farming in Victoria for at least 5 years; and were aged 18 years or older. Individuals were excluded if they had any cognitive impairment or other conditions limiting their ability to respond to the questionnaire, or if they declined to provide informed consent. We assumed that 40% of participants adhered to annual MDA, consistent with previous research.16 Allowing for up to seven independent variables in the multivariable logistic regression model and applying a conservative events-per-variable criterion of 20, we required at least 140 events, corresponding to a sample size of 350 participants, which was set as the recruitment target to account for missing data. A predefined sampling frame was constructed from the registry of farmers maintained by the Department of Agriculture, Regional Field Office No. 48. We aimed to recruit 50 participants per barangay, except for Barangay Merit (30 participants), due to its smaller population. Two trained staff randomly selected individuals from the registry and contacted them to confirm participation until targets for each barangay were met.

Data collection

Data were collected using a structured questionnaire to assess demographic characteristics, schistosomiasis-related history and knowledge of schistosomiasis, and abdominal ultrasonography to assess the objective status of chronic schistosomiasis. One survey day was scheduled for each barangay. Participants gathered at the designated barangay health centre or barangay hall on the scheduled date.

Questionnaire

The questionnaire was developed and standardised based on an extensive review of the literature and existing surveys. We collected data on demographic characteristics, including age, sex, barangay of residence, marital status, job status (full-time or part-time), educational attainment, monthly household income and primary water source. We also collected data on schistosomiasis-related history, including prior awareness of schistosomiasis, personal and family history of schistosomiasis, number of times participated in MDA in the past 5 years, and willingness to participate in future MDA. Knowledge of schistosomiasis was assessed using 12 multiple-choice questions (three options each), across domains including understanding of the pathogen, clinical manifestations, transmission routes, intermediate host, non-human definitive hosts, awareness of the role of open defecation in transmission, perceived preventability of schistosomiasis, knowledge of appropriate infection prevention methods, transmission prevention methods and tools, the importance of MDA and appropriate interventions for livestock. The questionnaire used in this study is provided as online supplemental file 1.

Content validity was established through expert review for relevance and clarity by five experienced researchers, and the Scale-level Content Validity Index for the questionnaire was 0.90. The questionnaire was initially developed in English and subsequently translated into Filipino, and forward and backward translation procedures were performed to ensure linguistic and conceptual equivalence. Both language versions were then reviewed and approved by the ethics committees in Japan and the Philippines before data collection. The questionnaire was subsequently pilot tested among 20 farmers in Nueva Ecija, Philippines, and the reliability of the questionnaire was assessed using Kuder-Richardson Formula 20 for the knowledge of schistosomiasis section, yielding a coefficient of 0.73, indicating acceptable reliability.

Barangay health workers served as interviewers and underwent standardised orientation on study objectives, ethical considerations and uniform administration of the questionnaire before the survey. Face-to-face interviews were conducted to ensure completeness and accuracy of responses, with interviewers recording participants’ answers directly at the time of the interview.

Abdominal ultrasonography

Abdominal ultrasonography was performed on the same day using a standardised imaging protocol to identify chronic schistosomiasis japonica by assessing liver lesions associated with Schistosoma japonicum infection. Imaging criteria were defined a priori based on Omae’s classification, and a participant was classified as positive when the characteristic network pattern (Omae type 3, figure 1), which is highly specific for schistosomiasis japonica, was observed.20 21 Absence of this finding was classified as negative.

Omae’s classification type 3.

Abdominal ultrasonography was performed using the Vscan Air CL (GE HealthCare). All images were independently reviewed and validated by two physicians licensed in Japan. Examinations were conducted in private rooms to ensure participant privacy, and a female medical staff member was present during examinations of female participants.

Participants identified as positive on ultrasonography were referred to the Municipal Health Office (MHO), in accordance with prior guidance from the MHO. These procedures were independent of the study, and the resulting data were not collected or analysed for the present study.

Statistical analysis

Data were analysed using descriptive and inferential statistical methods. Descriptive statistics were used to summarise demographic characteristics, schistosomiasis-related history and knowledge levels. Continuous variables were presented as means and SD, while categorical variables were summarised using frequencies and percentages.

For adherence to annual MDA, respondents who, in response to a question on the frequency of MDA participation over the past 5 years, reported participating five or more times were classified as adherent to annual MDA in this study, while those who did not were classified as non-adherent. Knowledge level was defined as the proportion of correct answers to 12 questionnaire items assessing knowledge of schistosomiasis japonica. Each item was scored dichotomously (1 point for a correct response, 0 for an incorrect response), yielding a total raw score ranging from 0 to 12. The knowledge level was calculated as the percentage of correct responses. Knowledge level was subsequently classified based on percentile cut-offs derived from the distribution of scores in the study population, with participants categorised as having very high (≥81st percentile), high (61st–80th percentile), moderate (41st–60th percentile), low (21st–40th percentile) or very low (≤20th percentile) knowledge.

Comparisons between participants adherent and non-adherent to annual MDA were conducted to examine associations with demographic characteristics, schistosomiasis-related history and knowledge of schistosomiasis. Welch’s t-test was used for continuous variables, while categorical variables were analysed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Multiple logistic regression was conducted to assess the adjusted relationship between adherence to annual MDA and variables in the bivariate analyses, with p values of less than 0.20. Age was entered into the model as a continuous variable, and knowledge level was entered as a continuous variable using the percentile score. Logistic regression analyses were conducted using listwise deletion, with cases containing missing values for any independent variable excluded from the analyses. Variables related to marital status, awareness of schistosomiasis and willingness to participate in future MDA were excluded from the multiple logistic regression model due to the imbalanced sample distribution. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in any stage of this study.

Results

Demographic characteristics

Table 1 summarises the demographic characteristics of the participants. Of the 380 individuals recruited, 342 participated in the study. Among these, 119 (34.8%) adhered to annual MDA in accordance with the Philippine national guidelines. The mean age was 51.3 years (SD 12.8) overall and was higher in the group adherent to annual MDA (54.8 years, SD 10.4) than in the group not adherent to annual MDA (49.3 years, SD 13.5), with older age groups more prevalent in the adherent group. The overall sex distribution (37.1% female and 62.6% male) was broadly similar to that of the DA registry population in Victoria (21.5% women and 78.5% men). In the group adherent to annual MDA, the proportion of men was higher (74.8%), as were the proportions of full-time farmers (64.7%) and participants with educational attainment above secondary level (60.5%). In contrast, the group not adherent to annual MDA showed a more balanced sex distribution, a slightly higher proportion of part-time farmers (52.0%), and 52.0% with secondary-level or higher education. In both groups, approximately 85% of participants reported a monthly household income below 7500 Philippine pesos (US$130), which corresponds to the lowest income class in the questionnaire, and no significant difference was observed between the group adherent to annual MDA and the group not adherent to annual MDA.

Ultrasonography findings and schistosomiasis-related history

Diagnosis of chronic schistosomiasis obtained via abdominal ultrasonography and schistosomiasis-related history collected through questionnaire surveys are summarised in table 2. Of 342 participants, 213 (62.3%) tested negative and 129 (37.7%) tested positive by abdominal ultrasonography, suggesting a possible history of prior infection in more than one-third of the participants. In the group adherent to annual MDA, the proportions of positive and negative results were comparable, whereas in the group not adherent to annual MDA, negative results predominated (69.5%). Overall, a high level of awareness of schistosomiasis was observed, with 95.0% of participants reporting having heard of the disease and 91.7% expressing willingness to participate in future MDA if given the opportunity. These tendencies were more pronounced in the group adherent to annual MDA, in which 97.5% reported having heard of schistosomiasis and 99.1% indicated willingness to participate in future MDA.

Overall knowledge level, calculated from the proportion of correct responses across 12 items, was 79.1% (SD 13.1), corresponding to a classification of ‘high’, indicating a generally high level of knowledge in the study population. Across the 12 items, 10 items showed high correct response rates approaching 80% (range 69.3–97.4). By contrast, the items assessing knowledge of clinical manifestations and non-human definitive hosts had lower correct response rates (29.5% and 56.7%, respectively) than the other items. For the clinical manifestations item, which assessed recognition of correct symptoms of schistosomiasis, response options included blood in stool, headache, sudden weight loss and other. Overall, 35.8% selected headache, whereas 31.5% selected the correct response, blood in stool. Several participants also provided incorrect free-text responses, including stomachache. In the group not adherent to annual MDA, 39.9% selected headache and 26.3% selected blood in stool. In the group adherent to annual MDA, 41.7% selected blood in stool, representing the most frequent response in that group. A similar pattern was observed for the item assessing knowledge of non-human definitive hosts. When asked which of carabao, waterfowl or fish could serve as a definitive host, 56.9% of the total sample selected carabao. The proportion selecting carabao was 72.0% in the group adherent to annual MDA and 48.9% in the group not adherent to annual MDA.

Multiple logistic regression

Table 4 shows the results of the multiple logistic regression analysis. Age, sex, job status, educational attainment, diagnosis by abdominal ultrasonography, history of schistosomiasis, family history of schistosomiasis and knowledge level were included, as these demonstrated differences with p<0.20 in the bivariate analysis. Variables related to awareness of schistosomiasis and willingness to participate in future MDA were excluded from the model because of imbalanced sample distribution. In the multiple logistic regression analysis, age, job status, educational attainment, history of schistosomiasis and knowledge level in percentile were significantly associated with adherence to annual MDA. Higher age, full-time farming (compared with part-time farming), higher educational attainment and higher knowledge level were each associated with greater adherence to annual MDA. Among independent variables, self-reported history of schistosomiasis showed the strongest association with adherence: participants with a reported history had higher odds of adherence compared with those without (OR 3.608, 95% CI 1.940 to 6.711). The odds associated with a diagnosis of schistosomiasis based on abdominal ultrasonography were not significant, whereas a stronger association was observed for self-reported history.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this study is the first epidemiological research on MDA of schistosomiasis in Oriental Mindoro, Philippines, which is reported to have the highest prevalence of schistosomiasis in the country. We comprehensively assessed factors promoting adherence to MDA. Overall, the community demonstrated high interest in schistosomiasis and a generally high knowledge level. Higher adherence to MDA was observed among participants who were older, employed full-time rather than part-time, had higher educational attainment and possessed a higher knowledge level. The strongest association was observed for self-reported history of schistosomiasis. Despite the high knowledge level, understanding of clinical manifestations and non-human definitive hosts was insufficient, suggesting the need to improve knowledge in this area.

Our findings indicate that infection history, particularly participants’ own awareness of their infection as reflected by self-reported history of schistosomiasis, was the strongest promoter of adherence to MDA. In other words, a high level of personal concern about schistosomiasis was strongly associated with participation in MDA. Previous studies have reported that past infection history and perceived disease severity are important factors promoting participation in MDA programmes for schistosomiasis, and our results support these observations.11 12 In addition to self-reported history, we evaluated objective infection history assessed by abdominal ultrasonography and family history. In bivariate analyses, the proportion of participants reporting a history of infection, a positive network pattern and a family history of schistosomiasis was higher in the group adherent to annual MDA. However, in multivariable analysis, only a self-reported history of schistosomiasis remained independently associated with adherence. No multicollinearity was detected among these variables, suggesting that self-reported history of schistosomiasis had the strongest association with MDA adherence, independent of the other variables. These findings reinforce existing evidence and suggest that awareness of personal infection may encourage desirable health practices.

Although subjective recognition of infection was important, understanding of clinical manifestations was insufficient and requires improvement. In this sample population, the overall knowledge level was ‘High’, with correct response rates of approximately 80% for most questions. By contrast, the correct response rate for questions regarding clinical manifestations was markedly lower (29.5% overall and 37.8% even among the group adherent to annual MDA). Without accurate knowledge of symptoms, infected individuals may be unable to suspect schistosomiasis and therefore may not seek appropriate care, indicating room for improvement in this domain. Similar concerns have been raised in the Philippines. A study by Bertulfo in Surigao del Sur reported that perceived severity of schistosomiasis promotes health-seeking behaviour.22 The influence of perceived severity on MDA participation has also been documented in studies conducted in SSA.12 23 Awareness of correct symptoms is essential for individuals to recognise possible infection and to become aware of the need for diagnosis and the availability of MDA. Despite the importance of awareness, educational opportunities remain limited in many endemic areas. Victoria, the study site, is no exception. At present, the only regular educational opportunity is health education provided during the annual MDA campaign through lectures and informational materials. Participation in these activities is voluntary and may therefore be limited to residents who are highly interested in schistosomiasis or who are available to attend during the campaign. Our findings support the need for policies that expand educational opportunities and improve community awareness of schistosomiasis.

As a secondary finding, transmission of schistosomiasis in this area appears to remain at a level that cannot be neglected, underestimating the need for further intervention. Abdominal ultrasonography revealed a high prevalence of chronic schistosomiasis (37.3%). A limitation of ultrasonographic diagnosis is that liver lesions develop years after infection and therefore do not indicate current active infection.24 25 Furthermore, the interpretation of ultrasonographic images is inherently subjective, and such pathological changes may have causes other than S. japonicum infection. Therefore, they cannot be definitively attributed to schistosomiasis and should be interpreted with caution. Moreover, liver lesions may not progress in individuals who receive early treatment or participate in MDA despite repeated infections.24 Nevertheless, at least 37.3% of participants suspected they had experienced schistosomiasis at some point, a finding that should not be overlooked. As the Philippines undergoes epidemiological transition with increasing non-communicable diseases, attention to neglected tropical diseases has reportedly declined, particularly since the COVID-19 pandemic.26–28 The nationwide prevalence of 4.8% reported in recent surveys was derived from stool examinations with limited sensitivity in a broader population, and the disease burden in high-risk populations should not be underestimated.5 6 Current guidelines emphasise the importance of a comprehensive approach beyond MDA alone, including interventions targeting livestock as zoonotic reservoirs.9 However, 43.3% of participants were unable to correctly identify non-human definitive hosts. As the Philippines promotes treatment and management of livestock, understanding and cooperation among farmers who use livestock are increasingly important.7 Our findings suggest insufficient awareness of the zoonotic nature of schistosomiasis and highlight the need to improve awareness of this aspect in future control strategies.

Limitation

This study has several limitations. First, as a cross-sectional study using questionnaires and abdominal ultrasonography, it is subject to limitations inherent to both methods and the study design. Questionnaire-based data collection may have introduced recall bias and social desirability bias due to interviewer involvement. Also, temporality cannot be established; the direction of causality between adherence to annual MDA and associated factors cannot be determined.

Second, abdominal ultrasonography alone cannot definitively diagnose schistosomiasis. The gold standard for the definitive diagnosis of schistosomiasis is the Kato-Katz technique. Therefore, the proportion of participants with suggestive ultrasonographic findings in this study is not based on definitive diagnosis and is not comparable to previous studies. Ultrasonographic findings are subject to interpretation of observers, and although the network pattern is relatively specific to schistosomiasis, it is not exclusive to Schistosoma japonicum infection. In addition, ultrasonography may miss early or previously treated infections without established liver lesions, potentially underestimating the true prevalence of infection.

Conclusion

This study provides rare evidence on MDA adherence from a high-transmission area of Oriental Mindoro Province, the region with the highest prevalence in the Philippines, thereby addressing the scarcity of data from Southeast Asia. Analyses using questionnaire surveys and abdominal ultrasonography showed that self-reported history of schistosomiasis had the strongest association with MDA adherence, indicating that subjective awareness of infection is a key promoter of participation. At the same time, despite high overall knowledge levels, understanding of clinical manifestations and non-human definitive hosts was insufficient, highlighting the need for targeted interventions to address these knowledge gaps as part of comprehensive control strategies. These findings will contribute to strengthening schistosomiasis control in endemic areas of Southeast Asia through evidence-based health education and policy development in high-burden settings. Schistosomiasis remains a disease that cannot be neglected, and continued efforts will be crucial to advance progress toward the elimination of schistosomiasis.
