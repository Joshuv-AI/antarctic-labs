The Extended KdV Equation: Augmented Lagrangian and Variational Solitary Waves with Applications to Dispersive Hydrodynamics
Saleh Baqer, Hamid Said

Abstract
In this work, we extend the method of averaged Lagrangian to the study of the general second-order (non-conservative) extended Korteweg–de Vries equation, known as the eKdV equation. Building on the framework introduced in [ 18 ] , we construct a master (augmented) Lagrangian, modeled on Luke’s Lagrangian, that incorporates the governing constraints at the appropriate asymptotic orders via the method of Lagrange multipliers. Averaging the resulting Euler-Lagrange equations in the traveling wave setting yields the existence of a (single) solitary wave solution with a sech 2 \mathrm{sech}^{2} profile. Explicit second-order formulas are obtained for the height of the solitary wave, together with the solitary wave velocity and inverse width, in terms of a fixed amplitude parameter. A key feature of the derived expressions is their asymptotic reduction to the classical KdV results when the first-order terms are retained. To assess the robustness and utility of the variational solitonic solutions, the derived formulas are subsequently applied, via the dispersive shock equal amplitude approximation method, to estimate the height and velocity of the leading solitary wave edge of dispersive shock waves governed by the eKdV Riemann problem. Theoretical predictions for the relevant wave parameters in both the eKdV solitary wave and dispersive shock wave problems are compared with direct numerical simulations and found to be in strong agreement.

In 1967 J.C. Luke formulated a variational principle to derive the shallow water waves equations

While they were successful in obtaining a perturbed Lagrangian for the problem, it alone did not produce the eKdV equation. (23)

Or, equivalently

\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{1}}-\dfrac{d}{d\theta}\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{1}^{\prime}}\right)+\dfrac{d^{2}}{d\theta^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\right)-\cdots+(-1)^{m}\dfrac{d^{m}}{d\theta^{m}}\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{1}^{(m)}}\right)=0,\\[4.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{2}}-\dfrac{d}{d\theta}\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{2}^{\prime}}\right)+\dfrac{d^{2}}{d\theta^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\right)-\cdots+(-1)^{m}\dfrac{d^{m}}{d\theta^{m}}\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{2}^{(m)}}\right)=0,\\[4.0pt]
\vdots\\[4.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{n}}-\dfrac{d}{d\theta}\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{n}^{\prime}}\right)+\dfrac{d^{2}}{d\theta^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\right)-\cdots+(-1)^{m}\dfrac{d^{m}}{d\theta^{m}}\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial z_{n}^{(m)}}\right)=0.\end{array}

(24)

for each
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. Now, we suppose that in addition to the field
u
(and its derivative), the problem under consideration is dependent on another field
\phi
(and its derivative) through a set of
k
point-wise constraints of the form

\begin{array}[]{c}G_{1}(u,\cdots,u_{m\theta},\phi,\cdots,\phi_{m\theta})=0,\\[4.0pt]
G_{2}(u,\cdots,u_{m\theta},\phi,\cdots,\phi_{m\theta})=0,\\[4.0pt]
\vdots\\[4.0pt]
G_{k}(u,\cdots,u_{m\theta},\phi,\cdots,\phi_{m\theta})=0.\end{array}

(25)

Then, the Lagrangian that is to be extremized in this case (which we term the
augmented Lagrangian
) becomes

L_{\mathrm{aug}}(u,u_{\theta},\cdots,u_{m\theta},\phi,\phi_{\theta},\cdots,\phi_{m\theta})=L(u,u_{\theta},\cdots,u_{m\theta})+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}G_{i}(u,\cdots,u_{m\theta},\phi,\cdots,\phi_{m\theta})

(26)

where each Lagrange multiplier may be
\theta
dependent, that is
\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{i}(\theta)
, for every
i=1,2,\cdots k
. Therefore, under the ansatz

u=u(z_{1}(\theta),z_{2}(\theta),...,z_{n}(\theta)),\qquad\phi=\phi(z_{1}(\theta),z_{2}(\theta),...,z_{n}(\theta)),

(27)

the Euler-Lagrange equations (
24
) are satisfied with
L
replaced by
L_{\mathrm{aug}}
.

Remark 2
. Throughout this paper as was done in
[
18
]
, all fields are taken, in addition, to have support over
(-a,a)
or to decay sufficiently fast enough in the limit
|x|\to\infty
if the spatial domain is taken to be
\mathbb{R}
.

3
Variational solitary waves

We are now in position to detail our variational study of single solitary wave solutions to the eKdV equation (
1
). As such, we begin by defining the phase
\theta=x-V_{s}t
, where
V_{s}
denotes the travelling wave velocity. The Lagrangian (
2.1
) in the phase
\theta

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=-V_{s}\phi_{\theta}+\alpha\left(-V_{s}u\phi_{\theta}+\dfrac{u^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{\phi_{\theta}^{2}}{2}\right)+\dfrac{V_{s}}{6}\beta\phi_{3\theta}+\dfrac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}u\phi_{\theta}^{2}-\dfrac{V_{s}}{120}\beta^{2}\phi_{5\theta}

\displaystyle\mbox{}~~~+\alpha\beta\left(\dfrac{V_{s}}{2}u\phi_{3\theta}+\dfrac{1}{6}\phi_{\theta\theta}^{2}-\dfrac{1}{6}\phi_{\theta}\phi_{3\theta}\right)+\alpha^{2}\beta\left(\dfrac{V_{s}}{2}u^{2}\phi_{3\theta}+\dfrac{1}{2}u\phi_{\theta\theta}^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}u\phi_{\theta}\phi_{3\theta}\right)

\displaystyle\mbox{}~~~+\alpha\beta^{2}\left(\dfrac{-V_{s}}{24}u\phi_{5\theta}+\dfrac{1}{40}\phi_{3\theta}^{2}-\dfrac{1}{30}u\phi_{\theta\theta}\phi_{4\theta}+\dfrac{1}{120}u\phi_{\theta}\phi_{5\theta}\right).

As mentioned previously, the Lagrangian (

u
and
\phi

1
). By requiring equation (

G(u,...,u_{4\theta},\phi_{\theta})=\phi_{\theta}-\left(u-\dfrac{1}{4}\alpha u^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\beta u_{\theta\theta}+C_{1}\alpha^{2}u^{3}+C_{2}\beta^{2}u_{4\theta}+\alpha\beta\left(C_{3}u_{\theta}^{2}+C_{4}uu_{\theta\theta}\right)\right)=0.

General coefficients
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
, and

B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
, and
B_{4}
. See Appendix
A
for explicit relations between these coefficients; in fact it is shown that the eKdV equation (in terms of phase
\theta
) can be obtained without any reference to the coefficient

. Henceforth we assume that
C_{3}\equiv 0
.

As we have already mentioned in Section
2
, the Lagrangian (
3
) together with constraint (
29
) alone cannot reproduce the eKdV equation. Therefore, careful inspection of a suitable constraint in the form of equation (
19
) is required. Firstly, we reiterate that the orders at which the corrections in the Euler-Lagrange equations, particularly equation (
18
)
2
, are necessary are
\beta,\beta^{2}
, and
\alpha\beta
. However, the pertinent terms in the constraint (
19
) only appear at the orders: zero,
\alpha
and
\beta
. Another associated consideration is related to the use of the averaging method (see Subsection
3.1
). The application of the method entails having a Lagrangian in terms of one field only. For our problem, it is natural to make this choice for
u
(and its derivatives), implying that
\phi_{\theta}
needs to be expressed in terms of
u
. This is in fact the case in the constraint (
29
) in contrast to (
19
). The latter consideration is dealt with by substituting equation (
29
) into
(

)
and retaining terms up to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha,\beta)

(u-V_{s}u)+\dfrac{3\alpha}{4}u^{2}+\dfrac{\beta}{6}u_{\theta\theta}=0.

(30)

However, we are still left with the issue of the discrepancies in the orders of magnitude. To remedy the situation, we multiply the previous equation by
\alpha\beta
in order to correct the Euler-Lagrange equations at
\mathcal{O}(\beta,\beta^{2},\alpha\beta)
. Hence, we define the constraint

H(u,u_{\theta\theta})=(u-V_{s}u)\alpha\beta+\dfrac{3\alpha^{2}\beta}{4}u^{2}+\dfrac{\alpha\beta^{2}}{6}u_{\theta\theta}=0,

(31)

where it is expected that, as in the calculations undertaken in Section
2
a division by
\alpha
will ensue in the final equations.

The

for our problem, finally, becomes

L_{\rm aug}=L+\lambda G+\sigma H,

(32)

where
\lambda
and
\sigma
denote two Lagrange multipliers associated with the constraints. We conclude this subsection by making two remarks. First, since the problem, as formulated in terms of the above Lagrangian
L_{\mathrm{aug}}
, depends on the wave velocity
V_{s}
, it is expected that the coefficients
C_{1},C_{2}
, and
C_{4}
that need to be determined, and the Lagrange multipliers
\lambda
and
\sigma
, all depend on
V_{s}
as well. This is illustrated for the case of the water wave problem in Appendix
A
. Second, as the classical KdV equation (
9
) demonstrates, the velocity
V_{s}
can depend on the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
. As we are considering the eKdV equation (
1
), one expects that the dependence on
\alpha
and
\beta
should go up to second-order, namely,

V_{s}=1+\alpha\dfrac{a_{0}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2},\beta^{2},\alpha\beta).

(33)

An explicit formula will be found later in this section.

3.1
Variational method

We start by implementing the averaged Lagrangian method
[

\alpha

3.1.1
An equivalent Lagrangian

We begin by substituting the constraint (

\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3},\alpha^{2}\beta,\alpha\beta^{2})

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=-V_{s}u+\left(\dfrac{-3V_{s}}{4}u^{2}+u^{2}\right)\alpha-\left(\dfrac{V_{s}}{6}u_{\theta\theta}\right)\beta+\left(\dfrac{V_{s}}{4}u^{3}+\dfrac{1}{4}u^{3}-V_{s}C_{1}u^{3}\right)\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\mbox{}~~~+\left(\dfrac{17V_{s}}{360}u_{4\theta}-V_{s}C_{2}u_{4\theta}\right)\beta^{2}+\Bigg(\dfrac{1}{6}uu_{\theta\theta}-\dfrac{V_{s}}{12}u_{\theta}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{6}u_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{V_{s}}{12}uu_{\theta\theta}-V_{s}C_{4}uu_{\theta\theta}\Bigg)\alpha\beta

\displaystyle\mbox{}~~~+\left(\frac{5}{12}uu_{\theta}^{2}-\frac{1}{8}u^{2}u_{\theta\theta}++C_{4}u^{2}u_{\theta\theta}-\frac{V_{s}}{4}uu_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{V_{s}}{4}u^{2}u_{\theta\theta}-V_{s}C_{4}u^{2}u_{\theta\theta}+V_{s}C_{1}uu_{\theta}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\mbox{}~~~\left.+\frac{V_{s}}{2}C_{1}u^{2}u_{\theta\theta}\right)\alpha^{2}\beta+\left(\dfrac{7}{90}u_{\theta}u_{3\theta}-\dfrac{17}{360}uu_{3\theta}+\dfrac{V_{s}}{80}u_{\theta\theta}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{40}u_{\theta\theta}^{2}+\dfrac{V_{s}}{60}u_{\theta}u_{3\theta}+\dfrac{31V_{s}}{240}uu_{4\theta}\right.

\displaystyle\mbox{}~~~\left.+\dfrac{V_{s}}{6}C_{4}u_{\theta\theta}^{2}-V_{s}C_{2}uu_{4\theta}+\dfrac{V_{s}}{3}C_{4}u_{\theta}u_{3\theta}+\dfrac{V_{s}}{6}C_{4}uu_{4\theta}+C_{2}uu_{4\theta}\right)\alpha\beta^{2}

\displaystyle\mbox{}~~~+\left(C_{1}u^{4}-\dfrac{7}{32}u^{4}-V_{s}C_{1}u^{4}\right)\alpha^{3}.

(35)

is now a function of the phase
\theta

and its derivatives, and the velocity potential

. Here, the functions

3.1.2
Averaging method

The parameters
a_{0}
and

must satisfy an Euler-Lagrange system resulting from the Lagrangian (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 7.22743pt\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial a_{0}}+\lambda\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial a_{0}}+\sigma(\theta)\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial a_{0}}=0,\\
\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial w_{s}}+\lambda\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial w_{s}}+\sigma(\theta)\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial w_{s}}=0.\end{array}\right.

In these equations, the quantity
\sigma
can be expressed in terms of

and

by employing the solitary wave solution (

\alpha
and
\beta

The Euler-Lagrange equation corresponding to taking variation with respect to

(i.e. equation (
37
)
1

\displaystyle\left(-2\dfrac{V_{s}}{w_{s}}-2\dfrac{\lambda}{w_{s}}\right)+\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{\lambda a_{0}}{w_{s}}-2\dfrac{V_{s}a_{0}}{w_{s}}+\dfrac{8}{3}\dfrac{a_{0}}{w_{s}}\right)\alpha+\left(\dfrac{64}{45}w_{s}B_{3}a_{0}(1-V)+\dfrac{32}{15}V_{s}C_{4}a_{0}w_{s}\right.

\displaystyle\left(2\dfrac{V_{s}a_{0}}{w_{s}^{2}}+2\dfrac{a_{0}\lambda}{w_{s}^{2}}\right)+\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\dfrac{\lambda a_{0}^{2}}{w_{s}^{2}}+\dfrac{V_{s}a_{0}^{2}}{w_{s}^{2}}-\dfrac{4}{3}\dfrac{a_{0}^{2}}{w_{s}^{2}}\right)\alpha+\left(\dfrac{32}{45}a_{0}^{2}(1-V_{s})(B_{3}-2B_{4})-\dfrac{8}{45}V_{s}a_{0}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\mbox{}\left.+\dfrac{16}{15}\lambda C_{4}a_{0}^{2}+\dfrac{16}{15}V_{s}C_{4}a_{0}^{2}\right)\alpha\beta+\left(-\dfrac{4}{15}\dfrac{a_{0}^{3}}{w_{s}^{2}}+\dfrac{16}{15}\dfrac{a_{0}^{3}V_{s}C_{1}}{w^{2}_{s}}+\dfrac{16}{15}\dfrac{a_{0}^{3}\lambda C_{1}}{w^{2}_{s}}-\dfrac{4}{15}\dfrac{a_{0}^{3}V_{s}}{w^{2}_{s}}\right)\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\mbox{}+\left(\dfrac{32}{315}a_{0}^{3}(B_{3}-2B_{4})+\dfrac{128V_{s}}{105}a_{0}^{3}C_{4}-\dfrac{16V_{s}}{35}a_{0}^{3}+\dfrac{128}{315}a_{0}^{3}\right)\alpha^{2}\beta+\left(\dfrac{64}{63}a_{0}^{2}w^{2}(2B_{4}-B_{3})\right.

\displaystyle\mbox{}\left.-\dfrac{32}{35}a_{0}^{2}w_{s}^{2}-\dfrac{64}{7}V_{s}a_{0}^{2}C_{2}w_{s}^{2}+\dfrac{8}{7}V_{s}a_{0}^{2}w_{s}^{2}+\dfrac{64}{7}a_{0}^{2}C_{2}w_{s}^{2}\right)\alpha\beta^{2}+\left(\dfrac{1}{5}\dfrac{a_{0}^{4}}{w^{2}_{s}}+\dfrac{32}{35}\dfrac{C_{1}a_{0}^{4}V_{s}}{w^{2}_{s}}\right.

\displaystyle V_{s}

\displaystyle=1+\dfrac{2}{5}a_{0}\alpha+\dfrac{2}{15}w_{s}^{2}\beta+\dfrac{4}{35}B_{1}a_{0}^{2}\alpha^{2}+\left(-\dfrac{32}{105}B_{4}a_{0}w_{s}^{2}+\dfrac{8}{21}B_{3}a_{0}w_{s}^{2}\right)\alpha\beta

\displaystyle\mbox{}~~~+\left(-\dfrac{48}{7}B_{2}w_{s}^{4}+\dfrac{16}{21}B_{3}w_{s}^{4}-\dfrac{32}{21}B_{4}w_{s}^{4}\right)\beta^{2}.

(40)

Substituting the above expression into the Euler-Lagrange equation (
3.1.2
), and retaining terms up to second-order in
\alpha
and
\beta
gives a quartic polynomial in the unknown parameter

\left(\dfrac{64}{21}a_{0}\beta^{2}B_{2}-\dfrac{64}{189}a_{0}\beta^{2}B_{3}+\dfrac{16}{189}a_{0}\beta^{2}B_{4}\right)w_{s}^{4}+\left(-\dfrac{16}{315}a_{0}^{2}\alpha\beta B_{3}-\dfrac{88}{315}a_{0}^{2}\alpha\beta B_{4}-\dfrac{4}{45}a_{0}\beta\right)w_{s}^{2}\\

(41)

The four roots of this polynomial are

w_{s}=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{30}}{40\sqrt{\beta K_{1}}}\sqrt{\alpha K_{2}\mp\sqrt{K_{3}\alpha^{2}+K_{4}\alpha+49}+7},

(42)

where the coefficients

\displaystyle K_{1}=9B_{2}-B_{3}+2B_{4},

(43)

\displaystyle K_{2}=a_{0}(4B_{3}+22B_{4}),

(44)

\displaystyle K_{3}=8a_{0}^{2}\left(-360B_{1}B_{2}+40B_{1}B_{3}-80B_{1}B_{4}+2B_{3}^{2}+22B_{3}B_{4}+\dfrac{121}{2}B_{4}^{2}\right),

(47)

w_{s}=\dfrac{\sqrt{30}}{40\sqrt{\beta K_{1}}}\sqrt{\alpha K_{2}-\sqrt{K_{3}\alpha^{2}+K_{4}\alpha+49}+7},

and its expansion can be found

\boxed{w_{s}=w_{0}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{7}a_{0}\overline{B}\alpha-\dfrac{1}{98}a_{0}^{2}\overline{B}^{2}\alpha^{2}\right),}

(50)

By substituting equation (

) into the formula for the velocity (

\boxed{V_{s}=1+\dfrac{1}{2}a_{0}\alpha+\left(\dfrac{4}{35}a_{0}^{2}B_{1}+\dfrac{1}{35}a_{0}^{2}\overline{B}-\dfrac{38}{35}a_{0}^{2}B_{4}+\dfrac{5}{7}a_{0}^{2}B_{3}-\dfrac{27}{7}a_{0}^{2}B_{2}\right)\alpha^{2}.}

(51)

For future reference, we denote the coefficient of the second-order term in the previous expression by

\overline{V}=\dfrac{4}{35}a_{0}^{2}B_{1}+\dfrac{1}{35}a_{0}^{2}\overline{B}-\dfrac{38}{35}a_{0}^{2}B_{4}+\dfrac{5}{7}a_{0}^{2}B_{3}-\dfrac{27}{7}a_{0}^{2}B_{2}.

3.2
Energy method: eKdV velocity-amplitude relation

Now to find an explicit expression for the actual (higher order) solitonic height, one can obtain an additional and distinct velocity-amplitude relation by deriving an energy estimate for the eKdV equation (
1
). We refer to
[
28
]
and
[
18
]
for the application of the method to the Kawahara equation and to the conservative-eKdV equation, respectively. To start, we assume a general travelling wave solution in the form
u=u(x-V_{s}t)
and substitute it into equation (
1
). This implies

-V_{s}u^{\prime}+u^{\prime}+\dfrac{3}{4}(u^{2})^{\prime}\alpha+\dfrac{1}{6}u^{(3)}\beta+\frac{1}{3}B_{1}(u^{3})^{\prime}\alpha^{2}+B_{2}u^{(5)}\beta^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}B_{3}(u^{\prime 2})^{\prime}+B_{4}uu^{(3)}\right)\alpha\beta=0,

(52)

and by integrating the above equation we get

-V_{s}u+u+\dfrac{3}{4}u^{2}\alpha+\dfrac{1}{6}u^{\prime\prime}\beta+\frac{1}{3}B_{1}u^{3}\alpha^{2}+B_{2}u^{(4)}\beta^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}(B_{3}-B_{4})u^{\prime 2}+B_{4}uu^{\prime\prime}\right)\alpha\beta=E_{1},

(53)

where
E_{1}
is a constant of integration that can be determined by the boundary condition

\lim_{\theta\to{-\infty}}u=0\quad\implies\quad E_{1}=0.

(54)

Now, we multiply equation (
53
) by
u^{\prime}
and integrate, giving the energy balance

-\frac{V_{s}}{2}u^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}u^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}u^{3}\alpha+\frac{1}{12}u^{\prime 2}\beta+\frac{1}{12}B_{1}u^{4}\alpha^{2}+B_{2}\left(u^{\prime}u^{(3)}-\frac{1}{2}u^{\prime\prime 2}\right)\beta^{2}\\
+\left(\frac{B_{3}-B_{4}}{2}\int u^{\prime 2}u^{\prime}+B_{4}\int uu^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}\right)\alpha\beta=E_{2},

(55)

where again the constant
E_{2}
can be determined similarly by the boundary condition

\lim_{\theta\to{-\infty}}u=0\quad\implies\quad E_{2}=0.

(56)

On using integration by parts, one can put the integrals in (
55
) in terms of the other:

-\frac{V_{s}}{2}u^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}u^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}u^{3}\alpha+\frac{1}{12}u^{\prime 2}\beta+\frac{1}{12}B_{1}u^{4}\alpha^{2}+B_{2}\left(u^{\prime}u^{(3)}-\frac{1}{2}u^{\prime\prime 2}\right)\beta^{2}\\
+\left(\frac{B_{3}-B_{4}}{2}u^{\prime 2}u+(2B_{4}-B_{3})\int uu^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}\right)\alpha\beta=0.

(57)

Equation (
57
) cannot be additionally integrated to obtain an exact differential equation. This is expected, and it is connected to the fact that the eKdV equation does not conserve the kinetic energy quantity
u^{2}/2
. However, one can derive a local energy equation accurate to second-order
[
10
]
. This is achieved by utilizing the classical KdV equation
asymptotically
, that is equation (
1
) up to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha,\beta)
only

\displaystyle(2B_{4}-B_{3})\alpha\beta\int uu^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(2B_{4}-B_{3})}{6}\int\left(\dfrac{2V_{s}}{3}u^{\prime}\beta-\dfrac{2}{3}u^{\prime}\beta-\dfrac{1}{9}u^{(3)}\beta^{2}\right)u^{\prime\prime}

(58)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2B_{4}-B_{3}}{3}\left(V_{s}u^{\prime 2}\beta-u^{\prime 2}\beta-\dfrac{1}{6}u^{\prime\prime 2}\beta^{2}\right),

(59)

which leads to the asymptotic energy equation

-\frac{V_{s}}{2}u^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}u^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}u^{3}\alpha+\left(\frac{1}{12}u^{\prime 2}+\dfrac{(2B_{4}-B_{3})V_{s}}{3}u^{\prime 2}-\dfrac{2B_{4}-B_{3}}{3}u^{\prime 2}\right)\beta\\

A_{s}

(61)

and

(66)

Application to a dispersive hydrodynamic problem

Generally speaking, solutions describing dispersive shocks are obtained using Whitham modulation theory
[
23
,
39
,
41
]
, which is based on averaged conservation laws or, equivalently, averaged Lagrangian. However, the determination of exact modulation equations (a system of PDEs governing the slowly varying wave parameters of the underlying wavetrain) requires that the modulation system to be expressed in Riemann invariant form. This formulation is possible only when the nonlinear dispersive wave model under consideration is integrable
[
42
]
.
As previously stressed, the full eKdV equation (
1
) is a non-integrable model. Consequently, alternative approximate methods are required to determine the associated DSW solutions.

One particularly powerful approach in dispersive hydrodynamics—and a central focus of the present work—is the DSW equal amplitude approximation developed by Smyth and Marchant in
[
35
]
. This method enables the determination of certain macroscopic properties of DSWs, notably the height and velocity of the leading solitary wave edge, without requiring full knowledge of the associated modulation equations. Importantly, the method is applicable regardless of whether the governing equation is integrable. A wide range of applications to nonlinear dispersive wave problems can be found in
[
10
,
13
,
36
]
. It is also worth mentioning another related approach, the DSW fitting method developed by El
[
43
]
. This method can provide predictions for a broader range of macroscopic and microscopic DSW properties, again without requiring explicit knowledge of the full modulation system. However, we do not consider the fitting method further here, as it is not directly relevant to the present work. In particular, unlike the equal amplitude approximation, the fitting method does not require the explicit existence of a solitary wave solution in its formulation. Interested readers can refer to
[
23
]
for further details and applications of the DSW fitting method to both integrable and non-integrable dispersive systems.

The DSW equal amplitude approximation for dispersive shocks is typically applied to nonlinear dispersive wave equations in a moving reference frame. Specifically, we apply a Galilean transformation,
\xi=x-t
and
\tau=t
, to the eKdV equation (
1
), which eliminates the transport term
u_{x}
. The eKdV equation then takes the form (after replacing
\xi
and
\tau
by
x
and
t

u_{t}+\dfrac{3}{2}uu_{x}\alpha+\dfrac{1}{6}u_{3x}\beta+B_{1}u^{2}u_{x}\alpha^{2}+B_{2}u_{5x}\beta^{2}+\left(B_{3}u_{x}u_{xx}+B_{4}uu_{3x}\right)\alpha\beta=0.

(67)

In this case, the eKdV equation (
67

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[\frac{3}{4}\alpha u^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\beta u_{xx}+\frac{1}{3}\alpha^{2}B_{1}u^{3}+B_{2}\beta^{2}u_{4x}+\alpha\beta B_{4}uu_{xx}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta\left(B_{3}-B_{4}\right)u^{2}_{x}\right]=0.

(68)

On multiplying the eKdV equation (
67

and integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right]+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[\frac{1}{2}\alpha u^{3}+\frac{1}{6}\beta uu_{xx}-\frac{1}{12}\beta u^{2}_{x}+\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{2}B_{1}u^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta B_{3}u^{2}u_{xx}+\beta^{2}B_{2}uu_{4x}\right.

(70)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\frac{1}{2}u^{2}-\alpha(2B_{4}-B_{3})u^{3}\right]+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha u^{3}+\frac{1}{6}\beta uu_{xx}-\frac{1}{12}\beta u^{2}_{x}+\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{2}B_{1}u^{4}\right.+

(71)

which is valid asymptotically. We note that if

5.2
Comparisons with previous works

One observes from the variational solitary wave parameter solutions (
49
), (
51
), and (
66
) that they possess the important property of
asymptotic reduction
. By this we mean that, when the higher order effects (second-order approximation) in the eKdV equation are neglected, the solutions (
49
), (
51
), and (
66
) reduce directly to the corresponding (first-order) solutions associated with the classical KdV equation, namely

w_{s}=\sqrt{\frac{3\alpha}{4\beta}a_{0}},\quad V_{s}=1+\frac{a_{0}}{2}\alpha,\quad A_{s}=a_{0}.

(80)

The availability of higher order solitonic solutions that retain this asymptotic reduction property is crucial for a broad range of applications in dispersive hydrodynamics; as illustrated in the present paper, in the authors’ previous work
[
18
]
, and in
[
20
]
.

In
[
3
,
4
]
, Karczewska
et al.
produced a single solitary wave solution to the eKdV equation (
1
) with the shallow water wave coefficients (
2
) in the form traveling wave solutions. Specifically, by substituting for
u(x,t)=A_{s}\mathrm{sech}^{2}(w_{s}\,(x-V_{s}t))
into the equation, the authors obtained

\quad w_{s}\approx\sqrt{\dfrac{0.145137}{\beta}},\quad V_{s}\approx 1.11455,\quad A_{s}\approx\dfrac{0.242399}{\alpha},

(81)

thereby determining the three unknown wave parameters. A comparison with the results reported reveals several quantitative and qualitative differences. First, the solutions (
81
) do not appear to reduce (directly) to the classical KdV expressions when the higher order terms in the eKdV equation are omitted, in contrast to the results obtained in the present work and in the study
[
18
,
20
]
. As emphasized earlier, such asymptotic reduction is essential when solitonic solutions are employed in the derivation of DSW solutions. Evidently, the previous expression for
A_{s}
captures the qualitative behavior of the amplitude in the presence of resonant effects, although significant quantitative discrepancies remain when compared with direct numerical solutions as presented in Figure
2
(b). On the other hand, the solitary wave velocity
V_{s}
is reported to remain fixed at approximately
V_{s}\approx 1.11455
, independently of changes in the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
. This behavior is valid only for small values of
\alpha
and
\beta
, which, in fact, has been the standing assumption throughout Sections
3
and
4
, enabling the asymptotic expansion. In this case, Figure
2
(a) indicates that this wave velocity given provides a reasonable approximation. When these parameters increase—while still satisfying the fundamental assumption
\alpha,\beta<1
underlying the derivation of the eKdV equation and its associated solutions—the velocity deviates noticeably from this constant value, as can be seen in Figure
2
(a). Furthermore, in the context of the application considered in Section
4
, a constant velocity is not well suited when the single solitonic solution is applied to analyze the DSW solutions, where the wave velocity depends monotonically on the magnitude of the initial discontinuity
\Delta
(and implicitly on
\alpha
and
\beta
), as presented in Figure
4
(a).

A generalization of the results of
[
3
,
4
]
was give by Khusnutdinova
et al.

[
5
]
, where a single solitary wave solution was found for the full eKdV equation (
1
), not limited to the shallow water wave coefficients. Here again, the authors substituted a single solitary wave solution in the form of
\mathrm{sech}^{2}
into the eKdV equation and, through careful algebraic manipulations, demonstrated the existence of a
family
of solitons, among which are the so-called
embedded solitons
—isolated localized waves lying within the continuous spectrum of the corresponding linear wave system—and studied their numerical solutions. However, these solutions appear to exist only for a specific range of choices of the coefficients
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
, and
B_{4}
(see equations (23)–(24) in
[
5
]
).

The above observations highlight the advantages and practical relevance of the solitonic solutions derived in the present work through the techniques of the calculus of variations. In general, this approach allows us to construct single solitary wave solutions for the eKdV equation (
1
) without imposing restrictions on the coefficients of the second-order terms, namely
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
, and
B_{4}
, in contrast to previous works; while at the same time providing explicit second-order formulas for the width, velocity, and height of the solitary wave, making the solution more amenable to applications involving DSWs.

6
Conclusions

In
[
18
]
, the present authors asked whether the method of averaged Lagrangian, which was used to obtain single solitary wave solutions for the
conservative
-eKdV equation, can be extended to produce a single traveling solitary wave solution to the eKdV equation (
1
) for general coefficients
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
, and
B_{4}
. In the present work, we showed that a master or augmented Lagrangian, modeled on the known Luke’s Lagrangian, can indeed be constructed, allowing the variational method to determine the complete set of wave parameters for this solitary wave.

Notably, a careful inspection of the derivation of the eKdV equation for shallow water waves (
