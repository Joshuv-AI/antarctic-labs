Prefrontal hemodynamic responses to astringency aftertaste across alcoholic and alcohol-free matrices: A functional near-infrared spectroscopy study
Authors: Xin Chen, Ran Tao, Xue Gong, Hang Liang, Yujia Yang, Fengnan Niu, Lingqi Li, Yang Ye, Dejing Xin, YiDi Yang, Siying Liu, Peng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Astringency is an important mouthfeel attribute in many beverages, yet its neurophysiological correlates remain poorly understood. It also remains unclear how beverage matrix shapes frontal hemodynamic responses to astringency over time. This study used functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) to characterize prefrontal hemodynamic responses to proanthocyanidin (PAC)-induced astringency in three matrices: water, ethanol, and red wine. To capture temporal aspects of the perceptual experience, responses were assessed separately during the induction and aftertaste phases. Sensory evaluation showed that perceived astringency increased with PAC concentration, although both the magnitude and time course of this increase varied across matrices. During the induction phase, frontal HbO responses were generally limited and less consistent. By contrast, the aftertaste phase was associated with broader and stronger prefrontal HbO responses. Region-of-interest analysis further revealed distinct matrix-related response patterns in channels corresponding anatomically to the orbitofrontal cortex (OFC), frontopolar area (FPA), and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), suggesting that lingering astringency may involve distributed evaluative processing rather than a simple representation of perceived intensity. Overall, prefrontal HbO responses to astringency differed between the induction and aftertaste phases and varied across beverage matrices. This work extends current understanding of astringency as a matrix-sensitive sensory process and provides useful neurophysiological evidence concerning the frontal hemodynamic correlates of persistent mouthfeel attributes in food systems.

Introduction

Astringency is a complex oral sensation involving trigeminal signaling rather than a basic taste. Drying and roughness are commonly regarded as perceptual sub-qualities of astringency (Rossetti et al., 2009; Schöbel et al., 2014). It is expressed across diverse beverages and foods, including fruits, tea, coffee, chocolate, and red wine, and plays a decisive role in product quality and consumer acceptance (He et al., 2015; Lesschaeve and Noble, 2005; Linne and Simons, 2017; Ye et al., 2022). In wines, moderate astringency can add structural complexity and balance, whereas excessive astringency reduces liking and purchase intent (Ma et al., 2014). Therefore, studying the sensory properties and underlying neurophysiological mechanisms of astringency is important both for basic research and for quality control and product development in the food and beverage industry.

The perception of astringency is a dynamic oral sensation whose intensity and sub-qualities evolve over time during oral exposure. This temporal development reflects ongoing interactions among astringent compounds, saliva, and oral surfaces, together with changes in oral lubrication, which contribute to the onset, persistence, and dissipation of the sensation (Wang et al., 2024c). The extent of these interactions depends on the chemical structure of phenolic compounds, particularly their molecular size, degree of polymerization, galloylation, and structure-related hydrophobicity. Beyond these compound-level characteristics, astringency is also modulated by the surrounding beverage matrix. Previous studies of astringency elicited by phenolic compounds have mainly focused on two beverage matrices: simple aqueous solutions and alcoholic beverage systems (Deng et al., 2022; González-Muñoz et al., 2022). Wine represents a particularly complex alcoholic matrix in which pH, viscosity, tartaric acid concentration, ethanol content, sugars, native tannins, aromatic compounds, and other constituents may collectively influence astringency perception (Rudge et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024b; Zhao et al., 2023a). Among these factors, ethanol changes solvent polarity and the hydrogen-bonding environment, which may alter tannin solvation, conformation, and self-association, as well as the balance of hydrogen-bonding and hydrophobic interactions with salivary proteins. These changes may consequently modify tannin-protein association and aggregation, with subsequent effects on oral lubrication and perceived astringency (Laguna et al., 2017; Vidal et al., 2004). Therefore, the presence of alcohol may modify the peak intensity and persistence of astringency, including its temporal evolution after expectoration or swallowing. Most previous studies concentrated primarily on the temporal intensity profiles of astringency while the stimulus remains in the oral cavity; limited attention has been paid to the persistence and changes in oral astringency sensations following expectoration or swallowing (Qi et al., 2023). Assessing post-expectoration sensations may therefore provide a more complete description of the temporal course of astringency, including its induction and persistence.

Human sensory evaluation remains the reference method for investigating astringency perception because it directly captures human perceptual responses (Deng et al., 2022). However, sensory evaluation alone cannot characterize the physiological processes associated with this perception. Functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) is an optical imaging method extensively utilized to investigate cerebral hemodynamic dynamics. fNIRS measures concentration changes in oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO) and deoxygenated hemoglobin (HbR), markers closely correlated with neuronal metabolic activity occurring within superficial cortical layers (Li et al., 2022). HbO and HbR exhibit distinct and sensitive absorption properties within the near-infrared spectral range of 600–1000 nm (Oropallo et al., 2025). Variations in optical signals can be detected by strategically positioning sensor arrays on the scalp over the cortical areas of interest. Concentration changes in HbO and HbR are computed using the modified Beer–Lambert law, providing indirect measures of cerebral neural activity (Scholkmann et al., 2014). Compared with electroencephalography (EEG) and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), fNIRS offers fewer physical constraints (Li et al., 2022) and demonstrates notable benefits in terms of sensitivity to electromagnetic interference and motion artifacts (Balardin et al., 2017; Pinti et al., 2019). Such advantages enable the effective use of fNIRS in naturalistic environments and with patient populations typically unsuitable for EEG or fMRI imaging. A recent fNIRS study examined cortical hemodynamic responses to astringency elicited by tannic acid and plant proteins (Kew et al., 2025). However, how alcoholic matrices modulate the temporal evolution of astringency, particularly its residual perception after ingestion, remains unclear.

This study aimed to investigate the prefrontal cortical hemodynamic responses induced by astringent compounds across three different beverage matrices (aqueous solution, alcoholic solution, and red wine) using fNIRS. Proanthocyanidins (PACs) were selected as the primary astringent stimuli because they are widely present in wine, play a critical role in wine astringency, and can be readily standardized and quantified (Xia et al., 2024). We compared how water, ethanol, and red wine modulate prefrontal hemodynamic responses associated with astringency intensity and hedonic appraisal across the induction and aftertaste phases.

Materials and methods

Materials

The purified water utilized for this research was supplied by Wahaha Co. (Hangzhou, China). Food-grade 98% PACs were sourced from Jiahe Xuri Co. (Shenzhen, China). Food-grade 95% ethanol was obtained from Hanyong Co. (Henan, China).

Selection and characterization of the red wine matrix

Selection of red wine

To select a base wine suitable for controlled PAC addition, we reviewed published chemical and sensory data for red wines. Pavez et al. (2022) characterized 37 red wines from different varieties, origins, and styles and identified tannin content as the chemical parameter most strongly associated with overall astringency and dryness. The Pinot Noir wine included in their dataset showed relatively low tannin content and perceived astringency. Guided by these findings, a commercial Burgundy Pinot Noir wine (12.5% v/v), purchased from a local Sam's Club (Nanjing, China), was selected as the red wine matrix. Because tannin concentrations can vary among individual wines, the selected wine was subsequently characterized using the MCP tannin and TPAC assays described below.

Methyl Cellulose Precipitable (MCP) Tannin assay

Tannins were quantified using the MCP tannin assay originally developed and validated by Sarneckis et al. (2006), with minor procedural modifications following Pavez et al. (2022). In brief, 50 μL of red wine sample was mixed with 600 μL of 0.04% w/v methylcellulose solution, 400 μL of saturated ammonium sulfate solution, and 950 μL of distilled water. The mixture was then allowed to stand at room temperature for 10 min before being centrifuged at 6000 ×g for 5 min. The supernatant was retained, and its absorbance was measured at 280 nm using a UV–vis spectrophotometer (UV-2450, Shimadzu, Japan). A calibration curve was established using solutions of (+)-catechin at 0.10–0.25 g/L. The results were reported in g/L of (+)-catechin equivalents (g CE/L). Analyses were performed in triplicate.

Total proanthocyanidin content (TPAC) analysis

Following a modified approach from Delcour and Varebeke (1985) and Treutter (1989), the samples were diluted 50-fold with a 90% ethanol solution. Subsequently, 70 μL of the diluted sample was mixed with 210 μL of 0.1% 4-dimethylaminocinnamaldehyde reagent (comprising 12.5% concentrated HCl and 68.25% ethanol in acidified ethanol). The reaction was allowed to proceed for 15 min, after which the absorbance was measured at 640 nm using a Spark® Multimode Microplate reader (1 aA-5082, Tecan, Austria). (+)-Catechin was utilized as the calibration standard, and the results were quantified in terms of (+)-catechin equivalents (g CE/L). The Pinot Noir wine had an alcohol content of 12.5% (v/v) and a pH of 3.6. Its MCP tannin and TPAC contents were 1.27 ± 0.11 and 0.57 ± 0.14 g CE/L, respectively.

Viscosity analysis

Viscosity was measured according to Cai et al. (2022) using a rotational rheometer (Physica MCR301, Anton Paar, Graz, Austria) equipped with 50-mm parallel plates. Measurements were performed at 25 °C with a plate gap of 1 mm while the shear rate was increased from 0.1 to 1000 s−1 over 330 s. Apparent viscosity at 100 s−1 was used for statistical analysis, and each sample was measured three times.

PAC was dissolved in purified water to prepare two aqueous conditions containing 2 g/L PAC (LP) and 4 g/L PAC (HP). For the alcoholic conditions, 12.5% (v/v) ethanol was used as the solvent, with PAC concentrations of 2 g/L (ALP) and 4 g/L (AHP). A commercial red wine (12.5% ABV) was used as the red-wine matrix. The wine contained baseline tannins as follows: MCP tannin, 1.27 ± 0.11 g catechin equivalents (CE)/L; and TPAC, 0.57 ± 0.14 g CE/L. The maximum additional PAC in the wine was limited to 2 g/L to avoid exceeding physiologically and sensorially relevant concentrations and to ensure experimental feasibility. The red wine without added PAC was therefore designated RLP, whereas the wine containing 2 g/L added PAC was designated RHP.

The viscosity results showed significant differences among the aqueous samples (0.89–0.92 mPa s), alcoholic samples (1.38–1.41 mPa s), and red wine samples (1.53–1.57 mPa s) (p < 0.05). Within each matrix, viscosity did not differ significantly between the low- and high-PAC samples (p > 0.05).

All solutions were freshly prepared on the day of the experiment, stored at room temperature, and presented to participants at a standardized serving temperature of approximately 25 °C. At this temperature, all stimuli were transparent, with no visible sediment. Each sample was administered in 8 mL aliquots during fNIRS recording sessions to ensure consistency in stimulus delivery across all experimental conditions.

Participants

A total of 24 participants were voluntarily recruited through an internal recruitment notice circulated among students and staff of the College of Food Science and Technology, Nanjing Agricultural University. Candidates were right-handed adults with previous experience in food sensory evaluation and prior training in recognizing the basic tastes. They were also required to be familiar with sensory-testing procedures, to consume alcoholic beverages without adverse reactions, and to report good general and oral health, normal salivary function, and no known alcohol allergy.

Screening consisted of a questionnaire, a task-specific PAC astringency-ranking test, and an fNIRS signal-quality assessment during a mock trial. Eight candidates were excluded because of poor fNIRS signal quality associated with thick hair or excessive movement during expectoration (n = 4), medication use that could affect taste (n = 2), or failure to correctly rank the PAC reference samples according to astringency intensity (n = 2). The final sample comprised 16 healthy adults (9 males and 7 females; mean age, 24.5 ± 2.1 years; mean body mass index, 21.3 ± 3.8 kg/m2). Participants received monetary compensation.

Prior to testing, they were instructed to refrain from eating, drinking, and smoking for 1 h. The experiment was conducted in a sensory laboratory, which contained individual booths with opaque partitions and was maintained at 25 ± 1 °C. The testing area was odor-free and sound-insulated. The lighting system provided warm-, neutral-, and cool-white settings, and uniform neutral-white lighting was used throughout the experiment. Before the formal experiment, participants received an explanation of the study procedures and completed a questionnaire to provide relevant background information, including any previous experience with fNIRS studies and their availability for the full experimental session. The experimental protocol was reviewed and approved (no. NJAU2026-43, approved on August 1, 2025) by the Ethics Committee of the College of Food Science and Technology, Nanjing Agricultural University, and all procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

fNIRS measurement

fNIRS measurements were conducted using a noninvasive optical imaging technique to record frontal hemodynamic responses in the prefrontal cortex (PFC). The NirSmart II-3000 system (Danyang Huichuang Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) was used to detect hemodynamic signals by emitting near-infrared light at wavelengths of 730 and 850 nm. The optode configuration consisted of 15 emitters and 16 detectors strategically arranged to form 48 measurement channels positioned over the bilateral prefrontal cortical regions (Fig. 1A). Optodes were securely fitted onto participants’ scalps using a comfortable head cap, ensuring consistent inter-optode distances of approximately 3 cm. Prior to formal data collection, each participant completed a brief mock acquisition session (pre-recording phase). This session was intended to familiarize participants with both the fNIRS apparatus and the experimental procedure, thereby minimizing state anxiety and novel-environment artifacts during the actual trial. Participants were seated comfortably in a quiet, dimly lit room and were instructed to keep their head still, close their eyes, and avoid unnecessary swallowing or other motor movements throughout the scanning period.

Depicted in Fig. 1B, the experimental procedure began with a 60 s prerest baseline where the participants sat quietly without oral stimulation. Next, they received an 8 mL sample of the astringent stimulus (PAC in aqueous solution, alcoholic solution, or red wine) delivered via a disposable syringe within 5 s. After sample delivery, participants were instructed to hold the sample in the mouth and gently move their tongue against the hard palate for 10 s without swallowing (Velázquez-Martínez et al., 2023). After the induction phase, the sample was spat. To thoroughly perceive the residual astringency during the aftertaste phase, participants were instructed to gently rub their tongue against the hard palate for another 20 s. They then immediately evaluated the intensity and pleasantness of astringency using rating scales after the initial tasting and expectoration. A 300 s resting period followed, during which the participants rinsed their mouths with distilled water to remove any lingering taste and prevent carryover effects. Throughout each trial, fNIRS continuously recorded frontal HbO responses to the stimuli across the different matrices. The participants underwent three randomized trials for each beverage matrix, with adequate breaks to reduce sensory fatigue and minimize interference from previous stimuli.

Sensory evaluation

Sensory evaluation was conducted in a dedicated sensory laboratory equipped with individual booths. Responses were collected using paper forms. Astringency intensity was rated on a ten-point numerical scale ranging from 0 (no perceived astringency) to 9 (extremely strong astringency within the reference range), whereas pleasantness was rated on a bipolar nine-category scale ranging from −4 (extremely unpleasant) through 0 (neutral) to +4 (extremely pleasant). This bounded response format was selected to facilitate repeated ratings and consistent use of predefined reference anchors. Similar nine-point scales have been used in previous studies of wine astringency (Pavez et al., 2022; Rinaldi et al., 2021). For intensity-scale familiarization, aqueous PAC solutions were assigned to four reference positions: 0 g/L PAC to score 0, 2 g/L to score 3, 4 g/L to score 6, and 6 g/L to score 9. To rigorously control for expectancy effects and visual or olfactory cues associated with red wine, such as its characteristic color and aroma, all samples were administered under double-blind conditions. Specifically, each sample was loaded into a black syringe and, following the experimental instruction, was delivered into the participant's mouth within a predefined time window. Participants wore nose clips throughout the entire sensory evaluation to eliminate potential interference from aroma perception.

Astringency perception was strictly divided into two phases: the induction phase and the aftertaste phase, and ratings were recorded separately for each phase. The induction phase was defined as the period from sample entry into the mouth until swallowing or expectoration, whereas the aftertaste phase referred to the period following swallowing or spitting. Immediately after each signal acquisition session, participants were asked to complete the rating scales for both phases. The interstimulus interval was set at 300 s, during which participants thoroughly rinsed their mouths with purified water. A preliminary pilot test conducted prior to the formal experiment (n = 5) confirmed that a 300 s rest period, combined with thorough mouth rinsing, was sufficient to allow the perceived astringency elicited by both proanthocyanidin and red wine samples to return to baseline levels.

Statistical analysis

The results were analyzed using NIRS-KIT (Hou et al., 2021) plugin in MATLAB. Preprocessing procedures included detrending, motion correction, and bandpass filtering (0.01–0.2 Hz). Compared with HbR, HbO is more sensitive to cerebral blood flow variations and provides a superior signal-to-noise ratio, thus offering a more intuitive reflection of cognitive activation signals (Tian et al., 2024). General linear modeling analysis was conducted to examine HbO changes for each participant, and β-values corresponding to different task conditions were computed (Hou et al., 2021).

Following the method described by Wang et al. (2024a), β values for each task condition were calculated by subtracting the β value of the corresponding trial baseline from that of the target condition, that is, taste condition β value − baseline β value or spitting condition β value − baseline β value. To identify channels showing significant task-related HbO responses, one-sample t-tests were conducted on the β values of each channel within the experimental group for the two task contrasts (taste − baseline and spitting − baseline). Finally, false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure implemented in NIRS-KIT, and statistical significance was defined as FDR-corrected p < 0.05.

Data were entered into Microsoft Excel for Microsoft 365 (Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA) for collation. Statistical significance tests were conducted using SPSS 27 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and data were visualized utilizing NIRS-KIT, BrainNet Viewer, and Origin2021 Pro software (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, MA, USA). Normality was checked using Shapiro–Wilk tests and Q–Q plots. Post hoc comparisons following the repeated-measures ANOVA were adjusted using Tukey's HSD procedure. Friedman tests were also conducted for the six conditions within each ROI (Eremenko et al., 2025). Given the final sample size, a sensitivity analysis was conducted for a two-sided paired comparison. With n = 16 and α = 0.05, the study had 80% power to detect a standardized within-participant effect of dz = 0.75.

Results

Subjective hedonic evaluation

This study evaluated astringency across matrices and PAC levels using two scales: intensity (0 [no sensation] to 9 [very strong sensation]) and pleasantness (−4 [very unpleasant] to 4 [very pleasant]) (Tao et al., 2025). As shown in Fig. 2A, intensity ratings during the induction phase differed significantly among treatments (p < 0.05). With increasing added PAC concentration, astringency intensity increased significantly within each matrix (LP vs HP, ALP vs AHP, and RLP vs RHP; p < 0.05). During the induction phase, ALP and AHP elicited significantly lower intensity ratings than LP and HP, respectively (p < 0.05), indicating that the simple alcoholic matrix attenuated perceived astringency relative to the aqueous PAC solutions at the same added PAC concentrations. Interestingly, this attenuation was not observed in the red-wine conditions: RLP was comparable to LP and RHP was comparable to HP (p > 0.05), whereas RLP and RHP elicited significantly higher intensity ratings than ALP and AHP, respectively (p < 0.05). Thus, despite having the same ethanol content, the red-wine matrix produced a sensory pattern distinct from that of the simple alcoholic matrix. During the aftertaste phase, astringency intensity continued to increase with added PAC concentration within all three matrices (Fig. 2B). Frontal HbO responses during the two phases of astringency perception

To characterize the spatial distribution of HbO responses, the measurement channels were assigned to anatomical ROIs using the Brodmann area template. Using anatomical landmarks as a basis, we partitioned hemispheric regions of interest (ROIs) by adopting the S9 midline as the boundary to assess lateralization, quantifying left-versus right-hemisphere activation strength across distinct phases of astringency perception.

During the astringency induction phase (Table 2), only a limited number of channels showed significant responses, and the overall pattern was characterized by a mixture of activation and deactivation. In contrast, the red-wine control without added PAC (RLP) elicited minimal immediate neural engagement, with significant activation detected only in a single channel within the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC, CH10). Notably, the channel responses observed in the temporal region under several conditions may not exclusively reflect cortical processing of astringency. Instead, they may partly arise from mechanical friction between the tongue and palate during astringency perception, which can recruit the temporalis and other masticatory muscles, thereby contributing to signal changes in channels overlying the temporal area. Taken together, these findings suggest that, as astringency evolves from the induction stage to the aftertaste stage, the OFC, FPA, and DLPFC may play important roles in integrating complex sensory feedback.

ROI analysis of key prefrontal cortices during the aftertaste phase

A few Shapiro–Wilk tests for the HbO β residuals were significant. However, the corresponding Q–Q plots showed only minor deviations, and no extreme outliers were observed. Discussion

Astringency is a dynamic percept with a pronounced temporal structure. Previous fNIRS studies of food perception have often analyzed responses within a single time window (Eremenko et al., 2025; Meyerding et al., 2024; Minematsu et al., 2018; Zhou et al., 2023). We separated astringency into induction and aftertaste phases and compared prefrontal HbO responses across aqueous, alcoholic, and red-wine matrices.

Temporal dynamics of prefrontal HbO responses during astringency perception

The sensory data (Fig. 2) showed that perceived astringency was shaped by PAC concentration, beverage matrix, and perceptual phase. In general, astringency intensity increased as PAC concentration increased. However, this linear relationship was modulated by the beverage matrix. During the induction phase, ALP and AHP received lower intensity ratings than LP and HP, suggesting that ethanol may dampen the immediate expression of astringency in the model solutions. This finding is consistent with Fontoin et al. (2008), who reported that increasing ethanol concentration reduced the perceived overall astringency of grape-seed tannin oligomers in model wine solutions. Zhao et al. (2023b) further showed that increasing ethanol from 10% to 15% weakened the hydrophobic contribution to tannin–protein interactions. Therefore, under the present 12.5% ethanol condition, ethanol may have weakened PAC–salivary protein association and subsequent aggregation. The red-wine conditions showed a different pattern during induction: RLP did not differ significantly from LP, and RHP did not differ significantly from HP, whereas RLP and RHP showed higher intensity ratings than ALP and AHP, respectively. A plausible explanation is that the more complex physicochemical matrix of wine may compensate for, or partly counteract, the masking effect of ethanol, thereby maintaining the perceived intensity of astringency during the initial stage of oral stimulation (Fontoin et al., 2008; González-Muñoz et al., 2022; Laguna et al., 2017; Vidal et al., 2004; Wang et al., 2024b). The significantly higher viscosity of the alcoholic conditions (ALP and AHP) and red-wine conditions (RLP and RHP) may have contributed to the differences in astringency perception and prefrontal hemodynamic responses among the three matrices by affecting oral coating and lubrication. This interpretation is consistent with Kew et al. (2025). However, because viscosity did not differ significantly between the two conditions within each matrix, viscosity is unlikely to explain the within-matrix differences.

The fNIRS findings also pointed to a marked phase difference (Table 2, Table 3). During the induction phase, significant cortical responses were relatively sparse, and the overall pattern included both activation and deactivation. This suggests that, under the present recording conditions, the initial emergence of astringency may involve only limited cortical engagement. One possible explanation is that astringency develops progressively through interactions between PACs, salivary proteins, and the mucosal pellicle. Consistent with this interpretation, Kew et al. (2025) showed in TR146-MUC1 and saliva-coated TR146 models that tannic acid and pea protein depleted cell-associated mucin and salivary proteins, supporting salivary-film disruption and lubrication loss as mechanisms contributing to astringency. After expectoration, removal of the bulk liquid while residual PAC–salivary protein complexes and the altered salivary film remained on the oral surfaces may have further increased tongue-to-palate friction. These progressive oral interactions may help explain the stronger and more widespread responses observed during aftertaste (Wang et al., 2024c). Although the exact channels showing significant effects differed across beverage matrix and PAC concentrations, prefrontal activity was clearly stronger and more widespread during the aftertaste phase than during induction (Fig. 3). In particular, the OFC, FPA, and DLPFC showed prominent involvement during aftertaste. Kew et al. (2025) demonstrated the temporal characteristics of astringency-related prefrontal hemodynamic responses by dividing the 60-s post-ingestion period into three consecutive intervals. Pea protein samples elicited significant right DLPFC responses during 0–20 s, whereas the response to tannic acid emerged later in the right DLPFC during 20–40 s and persisted in the left DLPFC during 40–60 s. Although their temporal segmentation differed from the induction–aftertaste distinction used in the present study, the delayed and prolonged responses reported for tannic acid are broadly consistent with our observation. These findings suggest that the lingering phase of astringency engages coordinated processing across multiple prefrontal regions to a greater extent than the initial stage of sensation.

ROI-specific and matrix-associated prefrontal HbO responses during astringency aftertaste

RM-ANOVA analysis revealed significant Region × Matrix × Concentration interactions, indicating that neural activity during the aftertaste phase did not simply reflect a general increase in prefrontal engagement. Rather, it showed a differentiated response pattern across the OFC, FPA, and DLPFC.

The OFC is generally regarded as an important site for integrating multimodal flavor information and assigning subjective value to oral sensations, particularly by linking sensory input with affective meaning (Bartra et al., 2013; Kringelbach, 2005; Rolls, 2004). In the present study, OFC activity during the aftertaste phase was clearly shaped by the matrix. The aqueous conditions (LP and HP) elicited lower HbO β values than the four ethanol-containing conditions (ALP, AHP, RLP, and RHP), and the strongest response in the right OFC was observed under the RHP condition. This pattern suggests that OFC involvement becomes more pronounced when astringency is experienced in an ethanol-containing matrix, particularly in the more complex context of red wine. This finding is broadly consistent with earlier reports showing OFC involvement in flavor-related valuation (Kishi et al., 2017). The correlation results further support this interpretation, while also suggesting that OFC activity is not a simple readout of sensory intensity. In the aqueous matrix (LP/HP), activation in the left OFC was negatively associated with added PAC concentration and astringency intensity, but positively associated with pleasantness. In the red-wine matrix (RLP/RHP), OFC activity also remained negatively associated with astringency intensity. These findings suggest that, during aftertaste, the OFC is more closely related to the evaluative matrix than to astringency strength alone.

The FPA showed a different pattern. Relative to the aqueous conditions (LP and HP), all four ethanol-containing conditions (ALP, AHP, RLP, and RHP) elicited stronger activation in the left FPA, whereas the largest response in the right FPA was observed under the RLP condition. The interpretation of the FPA findings requires some caution. Minematsu et al. (2018) reported that, within the ventral anterior prefrontal cortex (aPFC), pleasant food and drink stimuli tended to elicit decreases in HbO, whereas unpleasant stimuli tended to elicit increases. However, the same study also showed that freely tasted simple sweet and bitter solutions did not produce consistent aPFC responses across participants, and emphasized that the aPFC is not a primary gustatory region but is more closely related to higher-order cognitive and emotional processing. In addition, other fNIRS studies have reported relative decreases in HbO in the frontopolar and adjacent prefrontal regions during both pleasant and aversive taste stimulation, indicating that the direction of prefrontal hemodynamic change is not uniform across paradigms (Hu ChengHong et al., 2014). These previous findings indicate that FPA hemodynamic responses cannot be explained by pleasantness in a simple one-to-one manner. In the present study, all four ethanol-containing conditions (ALP, AHP, RLP, and RHP) elicited greater left-FPA activation than the aqueous conditions (LP and HP), whereas the strongest right-FPA response was observed under RLP rather than RHP. The aqueous conditions (LP/HP) primarily differed in PAC concentration, whereas the alcoholic conditions (ALP/AHP) also elicited ethanol-related oral sensations, and the red-wine conditions (RLP/RHP) contained additional sensory components, including organic acids and native tannins. Thus, the sensory input during aftertaste was not determined by PAC concentration alone. The observed FPA responses may therefore reflect the combined processing of residual astringency and other oral sensations rather than the direct encoding of pleasantness or a single sensory attribute.

The DLPFC serves as a key executive node involved in regulation and cognitive reappraisal (Morawetz et al., 2017; Nejati et al., 2021). RLP elicited the highest HbO β values in both hemispheres. In the left DLPFC, RLP was significantly higher than LP, HP, ALP, AHP, and RHP; in the right DLPFC, it was significantly higher than LP, HP, AHP, and RHP, but not ALP. Within the red-wine matrix, RHP showed lower DLPFC activation than RLP after addition of 2 g/L PAC, which does not support a simple intensity-coding interpretation. A more cautious interpretation is that DLPFC recruitment was greatest when the lingering sensation required more active evaluation within a complex matrix. This view is consistent with previous work linking the DLPFC to cognitive control and reappraisal (Golkar et al., 2012), as well as with wine neuroimaging studies reporting DLPFC involvement during the aftertaste phase (Pazart et al., 2014). The correlation pattern in the red-wine matrix (RLP/RHP) points in the same direction: right DLPFC activity was negatively associated with added PAC concentration and pleasantness.

In summary, prefrontal HbO responses during the aftertaste phase were not determined by astringency intensity alone, but were strongly shaped by matrix context and differed across prefrontal ROIs. The OFC, FPA, and DLPFC appeared to contribute to distinct aspects of aftertaste processing, suggesting that the hemodynamic response pattern of lingering astringency reflects a coordinated evaluative process.

Implications for the hemodynamic correlates of astringency

While astringency originates from physicochemical interactions among PACs, saliva, and oral surfaces, the present results suggest that astringency perception engages prefrontal processes extending beyond the oral periphery. This was most evident during the aftertaste phase, when matrix-dependent differences became clearer in both ROI and brain–behavior correlation analyses. These findings support the interpretation that PAC-induced astringency evolves from an initially oral sensation into a broader evaluative process. Consequently, this multi-stage perception engages a distributed prefrontal network rather than a single process linked to perceived intensity alone. The OFC, FPA, and DLPFC may contribute in different but overlapping ways to processing residual astringency and other oral sensations present during the aftertaste phase, with their relative involvement varying as a function of both PAC concentration and beverage matrix.

From an applied perspective, this framework may help explain how moderate astringency contributes structure and complexity to the sensory profile of some beverages, whereas excessive or persistent astringency may become a dominant oral sensation. The pleasantness of astringency appears to depend not only on its intensity, but also on how matrix composition shapes its persistence and evaluative processing. This may be particularly relevant for non-alcoholic beverages, where moderate oral stimulation can enhance sensory structure, whereas harsh astringency may diminish overall product quality.

Limitations and future directions

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the present findings. First, the final sample included 16 young adults. Although the repeated-measures design was sensitive to relatively large within-participant effects (dz ≥ 0.75 at 80% power), smaller effects may have gone undetected, and the findings may not generalize to a broader population. Second, although the study distinguished between the induction and aftertaste phases of astringency, the regional and correlation analyses were concentrated primarily on hemodynamic responses during the aftertaste phase. Consequently, the mechanistic interpretation is more developed for aftertaste than for the initial induction stage. In addition, the matrix conditions were not manipulated in a fully orthogonal manner. Differences between red wine and the water or alcohol solutions may reflect acidity, phenolic composition, and other matrix constituents. Residual taste and flavor differences could not be excluded despite minimizing visual and olfactory cues with black syringes and nose clips; thus, the responses reflect astringency within beverage matrices rather than isolated astringency. Future studies using simplified model systems with independently controlled matrix variables would allow stronger causal inference. Third, although fNIRS is well suited to ecologically valid tasting experiments, it has limited spatial resolution and is susceptible to extracranial artifacts. Because short-separation channels and concurrent systemic physiological measurements were unavailable, superficial and autonomic contributions to the frontal HbO signals could not be excluded; accordingly, the ROI labels indicate only the cortical regions anatomically corresponding to the measurement channels. This is particularly relevant in temporal regions, where muscle activity may contaminate cortical signals. Building on the salivary and cellular analyses of Kew et al. (2025), participant-matched salivary, tribological, and electromyographic measurements could further link peripheral oral processes with fNIRS responses across beverage matrices. Finally, the present study focused mainly on HbO β values in predefined prefrontal ROIs. Future work could extend this framework by incorporating functional connectivity, multivariate pattern analysis, and individual differences in astringency preference, thereby providing a more comprehensive account of how beverage matrices shape astringency perception over time.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that PAC-induced astringency is associated with distinct prefrontal hemodynamic response patterns that vary with both perceptual phase and beverage matrix. Relative to induction, aftertaste evoked broader and stronger prefrontal HbO responses, suggesting that lingering astringency may involve more extensive evaluative processing. The distinct response profiles observed in channels corresponding anatomically to the OFC, FPA, and DLPFC further suggest that aftertaste perception across matrices may reflect coordinated higher-order processing rather than a simple readout of astringency intensity. Together, these findings provide new neurophysiological evidence concerning the prefrontal hemodynamic correlates of astringency and underscore the importance of matrix design in shaping residual oral sensations. These findings may support the rational design of astringent beverages by helping optimize aftertaste-related mouthfeel, particularly in alcohol-free drinks and low-alcohol products.
