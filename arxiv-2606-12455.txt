King Function for Shifted Gaussian: Laguerre Structure, Spectral Theory and Density Journal: Applied Mathematics Letters
Yanpeng Wang, Zhe Gao

Abstract
We study King function arising as radial kernels in the laboratory-frame spherical harmonic expansion of shifted Gaussian distributions. We first clarify their relation with the co-moving Laguerre hierarchy by means of a King–Laguerre expansion. We then derive the King differential equation and show that the associated self-adjoint operator in a Gaussian-weighted Hilbert space is unitarily equivalent to the free radial Schrödinger operator on the half-line. This yields the spectral representation and generalized eigenfunction. Finally, we prove that real-parameter King function, lies in the resolvent set, form a dense non-orthogonal system in a natural radial velocity space, providing an approximation-theoretic basis for King mixture representations. Weighted L 1 L^{1} -integrability criteria and closed-form moment formulas are also derived, justifying the normalization of King function.



1
Introduction

The evolution of a charged-particle distribution function

f(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)
under transport, electromagnetic forces and
collisions is commonly described by the Vlasov–Fokker–Planck
equation
[
16
,
22
,
20
]
.
When spatial transport and macroscopic fields are neglected, the
collisional relaxation is governed by the velocity-space
Fokker–Planck equation
[
16
,
8
,
22
]

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f(\bm{v},t)=\mathcal{C}[f].

(1)

Here
\mathcal{C}
is the Fokker–Planck collision operator written
in the canonical divergence form

\mathcal{C}[f]=\nabla_{\bm{v}}\cdot\Bigl[A(\bm{v})f+D(\bm{v})\cdot\nabla_{\bm{v}}f\Bigr],

(2)

where
A(\bm{v})
and
D(\bm{v})
are the velocity-space friction vector and diffusion tensor, respectively. In this paper we focus on the reduced form of the collision operator, namely the Lenard–Bernstein operator
[
11
]
.

A standard way to represent three-dimensional velocity (3V) distributions is to separate the angular variables by means of spherical harmonics
[
1
,
7
]
. In spherical velocity coordinates

\bm{v}=(v,\theta,\phi)
with
v=|\bm{v}|
, any velocity distribution function that is square-integrable on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
for almost every
v>0
admits the
spherical-harmonic expansion, also known as the Laplace series
[
1
]
,

f(\bm{v})=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}f_{l}^{m}(v)\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta,\phi)

(3)

with convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
.
Throughout the paper we use the spherical harmonics without the factor

N_{l}^{m}=\sqrt{\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}\,\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}}
, denoted as

\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta,\phi)=P_{l}^{m}(\cos\theta)\,e^{\mathrm{i}m\phi},

(4)

where
P_{l}^{m}(\cdot)
denotes the associated Legendre function with the Condon–Shortley phase convention. With this unnormalized convention, the radial coefficients in
(
3
) are given by

f_{l}^{m}(v)=(N_{l}^{m})^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(v,\theta,\phi)\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta,\phi)}\,\,\mathrm{d}\Omega,\qquad\,\mathrm{d}\Omega=\sin\theta\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\,\mathrm{d}\phi.

(5)

Thus the 3V problem is reduced to the choice and analysis of suitable radial function
f_{l}^{m}(v)
.

For near-equilibrium problems
[
5
,
10
,
22
,
4
]
, the classical radial bases are Hermite and Laguerre functions
[
19
,
2
]
. They arise naturally after setting the velocity origin to the shift velocity of a Gaussian. More precisely, the Lenard–Bernstein operator becomes an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator in the co-moving variable
[
21
,
22
]
. Hermite polynomials diagonalize this generator in co-moving Cartesian coordinates
[
16
,
19
]
, while Laguerre function diagonalize its radial part in co-moving spherical coordinates
[
17
]
.

However, this spectral picture is intrinsically tied to the co-moving frame. In laboratory spherical coordinates, a Shifted Gaussian is no longer centered at the origin. Consequently, laboratory spherical harmonics do not diagonalize the shifted Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator. Moreover, fixed-center low-order Hermite or Laguerre truncations may be inefficient for finite-shift components, high-energy tails and multi-peak distributions
[]
. This motivates radial representations adapted to Shifted Gaussian profiles in the laboratory frame.

Motivated by this issue and by the Gaussian mixture model
[
12
,
3
]
(GMM), Wang and collaborators
[
23
,
24
]
introduced the
King mixture model
(KMM) for kinetic velocity distributions. The basic radial building block is the
King
function

[
24
]
. In dimensionless form, the King function of order
l
with a real parameter
k
can be written as
[
24
]

\mathcal{K}_{l}(v;k)\propto\frac{1}{\sqrt{v}}\,e^{-v^{2}}\,I_{l+1/2}(kv),\qquad k>0,

(6)

where
I_{l+1/2}(\cdot)
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind
[
25
,
15
]
. Equivalently, up to normalization, the essential radial profile is

e^{-v^{2}}i_{l}(kv),\qquad k>0,

(7)

where
i_{l}(\cdot)
is the modified spherical Bessel function.

Despite their practical motivation, the mathematical theory of King
function and KMM remains largely undeveloped. The present work
addresses three basic gaps.

1)

Representation gap.

Hermite and Laguerre function is well understood as co-moving
spectral functions of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck collision operator.
The King function, in contrast, arises from the laboratory-frame
spherical-harmonic expansion of a Shifted Gaussian. A systematic
account of the relation between these two representations is missing.

2)

Operator-theoretic gap.

King function satisfies a natural second-order differential equation. However, the associated Sturm–Liouville operator, its self-adjoint realization and its spectrum have not been systematically analyzed.

3)

Approximation-theoretic gap.

The real-parameter King function used in KMM is not spectral
eigenfunction of the corresponding self-adjoint operator. Therefore
their approximation power cannot be inferred directly from the
spectral theorem. A separate density analysis is needed as a
first step toward a rigorous convergence theory for King mixture
representations.

The purpose of this paper is to fill these gaps. We first clarify the
King–Laguerre connection by showing that a King function is an infinite
superposition of Laguerre radial mode. We then introduce the King
differential expression and prove that, after a Gaussian-weighted
Liouville transform, the corresponding self-adjoint operator is unitarily
equivalent to the free radial Schrödinger operator on the half-line
[
26
,
18
]
. This gives the complete spectral structure of the King operator. Finally, we
prove that the real-parameter King function forms a dense non-orthogonal
system in a natural radial Hilbert space. These results provide an
analytic foundation for King function in non-equilibrium velocity-space representations.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2

recalls the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck structure of the Lenard–Bernstein
operator, the associated Laguerre radial mode, and states the main
results. Section
3
derives the King function from the
laboratory-frame spherical harmonic expansion of a Shifted Gaussian and
proves the King–Laguerre relation. Section
4
develops the corresponding self-adjoint operator and its spectral representation. Section
5
proves the density of the real-parameter King family. Section
6
discusses
consequences for King mixture representations, and Section
7
concludes with open directions.

2
Preliminaries and main results

We recall the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck structure of the Lenard–Bernstein
operator and the corresponding Laguerre radial mode. The material is
standard and included only to fix notation and normalization.

2.1
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck structure

Under the assumptions of detailed balance, isotropy and linear
relaxation toward a shifted Gaussian, the Fokker–Planck collision
operator (
2
) reduces to the Lenard–Bernstein
operator
[
11
]

\mathcal{C}_{\rm LB}[f]=\nu\,\nabla_{\bm{v}}\!\cdot\!\bigl[(\bm{v}-\bm{u})f+D\,\nabla_{\bm{v}}f\bigr],

(8)

where
\bm{u}
denotes the shift velocity,
\nu>0
is the collision frequency, and
D>0
is the velocity-space diffusion coefficient. The shifted Gaussian

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}(\bm{v})=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}\varsigma^{3}}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{|\bm{v}-\bm{u}|^{2}}{\varsigma^{2}}\Bigr)

(9)

is the equilibrium state: with
D=\varsigma^{2}/2
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{\rm LB}[M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}]=0
. In the case

\bm{u}=0
, it reduces to the standard Gaussian, commonly referred to as the Maxwellian.

For a distribution of the form

f(\bm{v})=M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}(\bm{v})\,\mathfrak{f}(\bm{v})

with
\mathfrak{f}=f/M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}
, a direct calculation
gives

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}^{-1}\,\mathcal{C}_{\rm LB}[M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}\mathfrak{f}]=\nu\Bigl[\frac{\varsigma^{2}}{2}\Delta_{\bm{v}}\mathfrak{f}-(\bm{v}-\bm{u})\!\cdot\!\nabla_{\bm{v}}\mathfrak{f}\Bigr].

(10)

Introducing the co-moving dimensionless velocity

\widehat{\bm{w}}=(\bm{v}-\bm{u})/\varsigma
and noting that

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}(\bm{v})\,\,\mathrm{d}\bm{v}=\pi^{-3/2}e^{-|\widehat{\bm{w}}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\widehat{\bm{w}}
,
Eq. (
10
) simplifies to

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}^{-1}\,\mathcal{C}_{\rm LB}[M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}\mathfrak{f}]=\nu\mathcal{A}\mathfrak{f}(\widehat{\bm{w}}),\qquad\mathcal{A}:=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\widehat{\bm{w}}}-\widehat{\bm{w}}\!\cdot\!\nabla_{\widehat{\bm{w}}}.

(11)

The operator
\mathcal{A}
is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator with invariant Gaussian measure

\pi^{-3/2}e^{-|\widehat{\bm{w}}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\widehat{\bm{w}}

[
21
,
13
]
.
Thus, while
\mathcal{C}_{\rm LB}
acts in the original velocity

\bm{v}
, its Gaussian-conjugated form acts on the relative
distribution
\mathfrak{f}(\widehat{\bm{w}})
as an
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator.

2.2
Laguerre radial mode

We diagonalize
\mathcal{A}
using spherical harmonics. In co-moving spherical coordinates

\widehat{\bm{w}}=(\widehat{w},\theta_{w},\phi_{w})

with
\bm{e}_{\widehat{\bm{w}}}=\widehat{\bm{w}}/\widehat{w}
,
we separate variables by

\Upsilon(\widehat{\bm{w}})=R(\widehat{w})\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\bm{e}_{\widehat{\bm{w}}})~.

Using

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}=-l(l+1)\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}
,
the generator
\mathcal{A}
reduces to the radial operator
[
19
,
17
]

\mathcal{A}_{l}R=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[R^{\prime\prime}(\widehat{w})+\frac{2}{\widehat{w}}R^{\prime}(\widehat{w})-\frac{l(l+1)}{\widehat{w}^{2}}R(\widehat{w})\Bigr]-\widehat{w}R^{\prime}(\widehat{w}).

(12)

The eigenvalue problem
\mathcal{A}_{l}R=-\Lambda R
reduces to the generalized Laguerre equation; polynomial solutions force
\Lambda=l+2p
with
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(see, e.g.,
[
17
,
19
]
).

The eigenvalue problem
\mathcal{A}_{l}R=-\Lambda R
reduces to the generalized Laguerre equation; polynomial solutions force
\Lambda=l+2p
with
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

[
17
,
19
,
9
]
. Hence the spherical eigenfunction is

\Upsilon_{p,l,m}(\widehat{\bm{w}})=R_{p,l}^{\mathrm{Lag}}(\widehat{w})\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\bm{e}_{\widehat{\bm{w}}}),\qquad R_{p,l}^{\mathrm{Lag}}(\widehat{w})=\widehat{w}^{\,l}L_{p}^{(l+1/2)}(\widehat{w}^{2}),

(13)

and they satisfy

\mathcal{A}\Upsilon_{p,l,m}=-(l+2p)\,\Upsilon_{p,l,m}~.

For each fixed
l
, the family

\{R_{p,l}^{\mathrm{Lag}}\}_{p=0}^{\infty}
forms an orthogonal basis of the weighted Hilbert space
[
19
,
2
,
14
]

\mathscr{H}_{\rm rad}^{\rm co}=L^{2}\big((0,\infty);\,\widehat{w}^{2}e^{-\widehat{w}^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\widehat{w}\big)~.

(14)

Remark 1

(Limitation of the Laguerre representation)
.

The Laguerre spectral decomposition diagonalizes
\mathcal{A}

exclusively in the
co-moving
variables

\widehat{\bm{w}}=(\bm{v}-\bm{u})/\varsigma
.
In laboratory spherical coordinates

\hat{\bm{v}}=\bm{v}/\varsigma,\quad\hat{\bm{u}}=\bm{u}/\varsigma,\quad\hat{v}=|\hat{\bm{v}}|,\quad\hat{u}=|\hat{\bm{u}}|,

with the polar axis along
\hat{\bm{u}}
, a direct calculation gives

\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\partial_{\hat{v}}^{2}+\frac{2}{\hat{v}}\partial_{\hat{v}}+\frac{1}{\hat{v}^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Bigr)-\bigl(\hat{v}-\hat{u}\cos\theta\bigr)\partial_{\hat{v}}-\frac{\hat{u}\sin\theta}{\hat{v}}\,\partial_{\theta}.

Thus the shifted term couples radial and angular variables. Unless

\hat{\bm{u}}=0
, laboratory spherical harmonics do not diagonalize

\mathcal{A}
. This motivates the laboratory-frame King representation.

2.3
Main results

To state the main results we first introduce the central objects.
Throughout, we work with the dimensionless radial coordinate

\hat{v}=v/\varsigma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Definition 2

(Complex-parameter King function)
.

For
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\varsigma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
k\in\mathbb{C}
,
the
complex-parameter King function
is

\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};k,\varsigma):=A_{l}(k,\varsigma)\,e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\,i_{l}\!\bigl(k\hat{v}\bigr),

(15)

where the normalization factor

A_{l}(k,\varsigma):=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}\varsigma^{3}}\,(2l+1)e^{-(k/2)^{2}}.

(16)

The coefficient
A_{l}
satisfies the normalization condition of kinetic moments (see Proposition
30
).
Two specializations are of primary interest.

•

Imaginary branch
(
k=\mathrm{i}\kappa,\;\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
):
up to a nonzero constant factor, this family yields the spectral mode

\Phi_{l,\kappa}(\hat{v}):=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}j_{l}(\kappa\hat{v}).

(17)

•

Real branch
(
k\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
):
up to the
\hat{v}
-independent prefactor
A_{l}
, this reduces to
the radial profile of a Shifted Gaussian,

\Psi_{l,k}(\hat{v}):=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}i_{l}(k\hat{v}).

(18)

Remark 3

(Zero-parameter limit and threshold renormalization)
.

From the small-argument asymptotics of the modified spherical Bessel
function,

i_{l}(z)\sim\frac{z^{l}}{(2l+1)!!},\qquad z\to 0,

(19)

we obtain

\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};k,\varsigma)\sim A_{l}(k,\varsigma)\,\frac{k^{l}}{(2l+1)!!}\,\hat{v}^{\,l}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}},\qquad k\to 0.

Consequently, for
l\geq 1
the King function vanishes in the
zero-shift limit, while for
l=0
it reduces to the
non-Shifted Gaussian. We record the zero-parameter case as

\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};0,\varsigma):=\delta_{l,0}\,\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}\varsigma^{3}}\,e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}.

(20)

For the continuous spectrum, the analogous threshold behavior is
captured by the renormalized limit

\mathcal{K}_{l}^{\mathrm{ren}}(\hat{v};0,\varsigma):=\lim_{k\to 0^{+}}k^{-l}\,\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};k,\varsigma)=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}\varsigma^{3}}\,\frac{\hat{v}^{\,l}}{(2l-1)!!}\,e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}.

(21)

Note that
\mathcal{K}_{l}^{\mathrm{ren}}
represents only the
lowest-order non-vanishing term in the small-
k
asymptotics
and does not belong to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
space for the continuous spectrum.

Definition 4

(King differential expression)
.

For
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\hat{v}>0
, the
King
differential expression
is

\tau_{l}f(\hat{v})=-\frac{1}{\omega(\hat{v})}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}}\Bigl(\omega(\hat{v})\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}f}{\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}}\Bigr)+V_{l}(\hat{v})\,f(\hat{v}),

(22)

where
\omega(\hat{v})=\hat{v}^{2}e^{2\hat{v}^{2}}
and

V_{l}(\hat{v})=\frac{l(l+1)}{\hat{v}^{2}}-(4\hat{v}^{2}+6)
.
The associated Hilbert space is

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}=L^{2}\big((0,\infty);\,\omega(\hat{v})\,\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}\big)~.

(23)

The main results are presented as follows.
The first result bridges the laboratory and co-moving representations;
the next two concern the operator theory of
\tau_{l}
; the last
establishes the approximation property of the real-parameter family.

Theorem 5

(King–Laguerre expansion)
.

For every
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
k\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,

\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};k,\varsigma)=M_{0,\varsigma}(\hat{v})\,\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}(2l+1)\Bigl(\frac{k\hat{v}}{2}\Bigr)^{\!l}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}(k/2)^{2p}}{\Gamma(p+l+3/2)}\,L_{p}^{(l+1/2)}(\hat{v}^{2}),

(24)

where
M_{0,\varsigma}(\hat{v})=\pi^{-3/2}\varsigma^{-3}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}

and
L_{p}^{(\alpha)}(\cdot)
is the
generalized Laguerre polynomial.

Theorem 6

(Unitary equivalence and self-adjointness)
.

The Liouville transformation

(\mathcal{U}f)(\hat{v})=\hat{v}e^{\hat{v}^{2}}f(\hat{v})

maps
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
unitarily onto
L^{2}(0,\infty)
, and on

C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)

\mathcal{U}\tau_{l}\mathcal{U}^{-1}=h_{l}:=-\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}^{2}}+\frac{l(l+1)}{\hat{v}^{2}}.

For
l\geq 1
, the endpoints
0
and
\infty
are limit-point, and
the unique self-adjoint realization is

\mathcal{L}_{l}=\mathcal{U}^{-1}h_{l}\mathcal{U},\qquad\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\mathcal{U}^{-1}\operatorname{Dom}(h_{l}).

For
l=0
, the origin is limit-circle; choosing the regular Dirichlet
condition
\psi(0)=0
, with
\psi=\mathcal{U}f
, gives

\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{0})=\left\{f\in\operatorname{Dom}_{\max}(\tau_{0}):\lim_{\hat{v}\to 0^{+}}\hat{v}f(\hat{v})=0\right\}.

With these domains,
\mathcal{L}_{l}
is self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
.

Theorem 7

(Spectral resolution)
.

For every
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
the self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{l}
has purely absolutely
continuous spectrum

\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\sigma_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal{L}_{l})=[0,\infty),\qquad\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\varnothing.

The generalized eigenfunction
\Phi_{l,\kappa}(\hat{v})
defined by Eq.
(
17
), satisfies the
\delta
-orthogonality

\bigl\langle\Phi_{l,\kappa},\,\Phi_{l,\kappa^{\prime}}\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}}=\frac{\pi}{2\kappa^{2}}\,\delta(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime})~.

They are complete in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
, giving the King transform

f(\hat{v})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{f}(\kappa)\,\Phi_{l,\kappa}(\hat{v})\,\,\mathrm{d}\kappa,\qquad\widetilde{f}(\kappa)=\frac{2\kappa^{2}}{\pi}\bigl\langle f,\Phi_{l,\kappa}\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}}.

Theorem 8

(Density of the real-parameter King family)
.

Let

H_{\rm rad}=L^{2}\big((0,\infty);\,\hat{v}^{2}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}\big)~.

(25)

For every
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the family

\mathscr{K}_{l}^{\mathbb{R}}=\bigl\{\Psi_{l,k}(\hat{v})=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}i_{l}(k\hat{v})\mid k\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\bigr\}

has a dense linear span in
H_{\rm rad}
.

5
addresses the representation gap,
Theorems

–

the

the

3
King representation

Instead of attempting to diagonalize the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator
in the laboratory frame, we
directly expand the Shifted Gaussian in spherical harmonics. The
resulting radial coefficients is the
King function
. We also derive
the second-order differential equation satisfied by this function.

3.1
Radial function in the laboratory frame

Let

v=|\bm{v}|,\;u=|\bm{u}|,\;\bm{e}_{\bm{v}}=\bm{v}/v,

and, when
u>0
,

\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}=\bm{u}/u.

Proposition 9

(King function from the Shifted Gaussian)
.

For
u>0
, the shifted Gaussian

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}
admits the spherical-harmonic expansion

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}(v,\theta,\phi)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\mathcal{K}_{l}(v;u,\varsigma)\,\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta_{u},\phi_{u})}\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta,\phi),

where
(\theta_{u},\phi_{u})
are the angular coordinates of
\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}
, and the
l
-th radial factor is the
King function

\mathcal{K}_{l}(v;u,\varsigma)=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}\varsigma^{3}}(2l+1)\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{u^{2}+v^{2}}{\varsigma^{2}}\Bigr)\,i_{l}\!\Bigl(\frac{2uv}{\varsigma^{2}}\Bigr),\qquad u>0.

(26)

The case
u=0
is obtained by taking the limit
u\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

For
u>0
. From (
9
) we have

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}(v,\bm{e}_{\bm{v}})=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}\varsigma^{3}}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{v^{2}+u^{2}}{\varsigma^{2}}\Bigr)\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{2uv}{\varsigma^{2}}\,\bm{e}_{\bm{v}}\!\cdot\!\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}\Bigr).

Applying the plane-wave expansion
[
1
,
15
]

e^{z\,\bm{e}_{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}}=4\pi\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}i_{l}(z)\,(N_{l}^{m})^{2}\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\bm{e}_{\bm{v}})\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\bm{e}_{\bm{u}})},

(27)

with
z=2uv/\varsigma^{2}
. Using the explicit normalization

4\pi(N_{l}^{m})^{2}=(2l+1)\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}
, we obtain

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}(v,\bm{e}_{\bm{v}})=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}\varsigma^{3}}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{v^{2}+u^{2}}{\varsigma^{2}}\Bigr)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}(2l+1)\,\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}\,i_{l}\!\Bigl(\frac{2uv}{\varsigma^{2}}\Bigr)\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\bm{e}_{\bm{v}})\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\bm{e}_{\bm{u}})}.

(28)

Writing the angular arguments explicitly,

\bm{e}_{\bm{v}}=(\theta,\phi)
and
\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}=(\theta_{u},\phi_{u})
,
Eq. (
28
) becomes

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}(v,\theta,\phi)=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}\varsigma^{3}}e^{-(v^{2}+u^{2})/\varsigma^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}(2l+1)\,\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}\,i_{l}\!\Bigl(\frac{2uv}{\varsigma^{2}}\Bigr)\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta,\phi)\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta_{u},\phi_{u})}.

(29)

When the polar axis is aligned with the shift direction
(
\theta_{u}=0
), the identity

\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}}(0,\phi_{u})=\delta_{m,0}
collapses the sum
over
m
to the single term
m=0
, recovering the expected
azimuthal symmetry.

Writing the spherical-harmonic expansion of the shifted Gaussian as

M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}(v,\theta,\phi)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}M_{l}^{m}(v)\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta,\phi),

the radial coefficient of the
l
-th angular mode is

M_{l}^{m}(v)=\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\bm{e}_{\bm{u}})}\;\mathcal{K}_{l}(v;u,\varsigma),

(30)

with
\mathcal{K}_{l}
given by (
26
).
If the polar axis is aligned with the shift direction, then

\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(0,\phi_{u})=\delta_{m,0}~.

So only the
m=0
term remains and the usual Legendre expansion is
recovered. Finally,
u=0
follows from

i_{0}(0)=1
and
i_{l}(0)=0
for
l\geq 1
.

Remark 10

(Reduction to the standard King function)
.

Introducing the dimensionless variables

\hat{v}=v/\varsigma

,

\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};k,\varsigma)=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}\varsigma^{3}}\,(2l+1)e^{-(k/2)^{2}}\,e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\,i_{l}(k\hat{v}),\qquad k\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

(31)

This is precisely the real branch of the complex-parameter King
function introduced in Definition
2
.

3.2
Proof of Theorem
5

Proof.

Recall the Bessel-Laguerre generating identity
[
15
,
9
]

e^{-t}(xt)^{-\alpha/2}I_{\alpha}(2\sqrt{xt})=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-t)^{p}}{\Gamma(p+\alpha+1)}\,L_{p}^{(\alpha)}(x),\qquad t\in\mathbb{C},

(32)

valid as an identity of entire functions in
t
for each fixed

x>0
. Set
\alpha=l+1/2
,
x=\hat{v}^{2}
and

t=(k/2)^{2}
, so that
2\sqrt{xt}=k\hat{v}
.
Using the relation

i_{l}(z)=\sqrt{\pi/(2z)}\,I_{l+1/2}(z)
, the left-hand side of
(
32
) becomes

\displaystyle e^{-(k/2)^{2}}\bigl[\hat{v}^{2}(k/2)^{2}\bigr]^{-(l+1/2)/2}I_{l+1/2}(k\hat{v})

\displaystyle=e^{-(k/2)^{2}}\,i_{l}(k\hat{v})\;\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\;2^{\,l+1/2}\,(k\hat{v})^{-l}.

Equating this with the right-hand side of
(
32
) and solving for the

\hat{v}
-dependent factor yields

e^{-(k/2)^{2}}\,i_{l}(k\hat{v})=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\Bigl(\frac{k\hat{v}}{2}\Bigr)^{l}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}(k/2)^{2p}}{\Gamma(p+l+3/2)}\,L_{p}^{(l+1/2)}(\hat{v}^{2}).

(33)

Absolute convergence of the series for every
\hat{v}>0
follows from
the standard estimates for the Bessel-Laguerre identity
[
17
,
15
]
.
Substituting the above equation into (

)
yields the King–Laguerre relation (

).
∎

Remark 11

(Relation between King and Laguerre functions)
.

Formula (
33
) shows that the King function
is not a single Laguerre mode. It is an infinite superposition of
Laguerre radial mode with coefficients controlled by the parameter
k
.
Thus Laguerre functions provide the co-moving spectral hierarchy, whereas King functions
represent shifted Gaussian structures in the laboratory frame.
This dictionary between the two representations is fundamental for the subsequent spectral analysis.

4
The King operator and its spectral representation

In this section we prove Theorems
6

and
7
. We begin by deriving the second-order differential equation satisfied by the King functions,
then construct the associated self-adjoint operator and its spectral resolution.

4.1
The King equation

Proposition 12

(King equation)
.

For every
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
k\in\mathbb{C}
, the
function
e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}i_{l}(k\hat{v})
satisfies the following second-order linear ordinary differential equation

f^{\prime\prime}(\hat{v})+\Bigl(\frac{2}{\hat{v}}+4\hat{v}\Bigr)f^{\prime}(\hat{v})+\Bigl[-\frac{l(l+1)}{\hat{v}^{2}}+4\hat{v}^{2}+6-k^{2}\Bigr]f(\hat{v})=0.

(34)

We refer to (
34
) as the
King equation
.

Proof.

Set
z=k\hat{v}\in\mathbb{C}
. The modified spherical Bessel function satisfies
[
15
, §10.49]

[
25
, Ch. 3]

z^{2}i_{l}^{\prime\prime}(z)+2zi_{l}^{\prime}(z)-\bigl[z^{2}+l(l+1)\bigr]i_{l}(z)=0.

(35)

With
f(\hat{v})=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}i_{l}(k\hat{v})
we compute

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\hat{v})

\displaystyle=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\bigl[ki_{l}^{\prime}(z)-2\hat{v}i_{l}(z)\bigr],

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(\hat{v})

\displaystyle=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\bigl[k^{2}i_{l}^{\prime\prime}(z)-4k\hat{v}i_{l}^{\prime}(z)+(4\hat{v}^{2}-2)i_{l}(z)\bigr].

Substituting into the left-hand side of (
34
) and
simplifying,

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(\hat{v})+\Bigl(\frac{2}{\hat{v}}+4\hat{v}\Bigr)f^{\prime}(\hat{v})+\Bigl[-\frac{l(l+1)}{\hat{v}^{2}}+4\hat{v}^{2}+6-k^{2}\Bigr]f(\hat{v})

\displaystyle=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\Bigl\{k^{2}i_{l}^{\prime\prime}(z)+\frac{2k}{\hat{v}}i_{l}^{\prime}(z)-\Bigl[k^{2}+\frac{l(l+1)}{\hat{v}^{2}}\Bigr]i_{l}(z)\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}}{\hat{v}^{2}}\Bigl\{z^{2}i_{l}^{\prime\prime}(z)+2zi_{l}^{\prime}(z)-\bigl[z^{2}+l(l+1)\bigr]i_{l}(z)\Bigr\}.

The bracket vanishes identically by (
35
). Hence (
34
) holds.
∎

4.2
Unitary equivalence and self-adjointness

To place the King equation in an operator-theoretic framework, we
rewrite it in Sturm-Liouville form. Multiplying
(
34
) by the integrating factor

\exp\!\int\!({2}/{\hat{v}}+4\hat{v})\,\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}=\hat{v}^{2}e^{2\hat{v}^{2}}

yields the differential expression
\tau_{l}
introduced in
Definition
4
, which we recall for convenience:

\tau_{l}f(\hat{v})=-\frac{1}{\omega(\hat{v})}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}}\Bigl(\omega(\hat{v})\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}f}{\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}}\Bigr)+V_{l}(\hat{v})\,f(\hat{v}),

(36)

with
\omega(\hat{v})=\hat{v}^{2}e^{2\hat{v}^{2}}
and

V_{l}(\hat{v})={l(l+1)}/{\hat{v}^{2}}-(4\hat{v}^{2}+6)
.
The natural Hilbert space is

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}=L^{2}\big((0,\infty);\,\omega(\hat{v})\,\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}\big)
, defined by Eq. (
23
) .

Lemma 13

(Unitary reduction to the radial Schrödinger operator)
.

The Liouville transformation

(\mathcal{U}f)(\hat{v})=\hat{v}\,e^{\hat{v}^{2}}f(\hat{v})

maps
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
unitarily onto
L^{2}(0,\infty)
and
satisfies

\mathcal{U}\,\tau_{l}\,\mathcal{U}^{-1}=h_{l}=-\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}^{2}}+\frac{l(l+1)}{\hat{v}^{2}}\quad\text{on }C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty).

The right-hand side is the radial half-line Schrödinger operator
[
26
,
18
]
on
L^{2}(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The unitarity follows from

\|\mathcal{U}f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl|\hat{v}\,e^{\hat{v}^{2}}f(\hat{v})\bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(\hat{v})|^{2}\,\hat{v}^{2}e^{2\hat{v}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}}^{2}.

For the operator identity, substitute

f(\hat{v})=\hat{v}^{-1}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\psi(\hat{v})

into (
36
). A direct computation gives

\tau_{l}f=\hat{v}^{-1}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\Bigl[-\psi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{l(l+1)}{\hat{v}^{2}}\,\psi\Bigr],

hence
\mathcal{U}\tau_{l}\,\mathcal{U}^{-1}\psi=-\psi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{l(l+1)}{\hat{v}^{2}}\,\psi
.
∎

To obtain a self-adjoint operator we must specify a domain.
Let
\operatorname{Dom}_{\max}(\tau_{l})
be the maximal domain.

Definition 14

(Maximal domain)
.

The maximal domain of the differential expression
\tau_{l}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
is

\operatorname{Dom}_{\max}(\tau_{l}):=\Bigl\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}\;\Big|\;f,\;\omega f^{\prime}\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty),\;\tau_{l}f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}\Bigr\}.

(37)

Lemma 15

(Self-adjoint realization)
.

For each
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define
\mathcal{L}_{l}f:=\tau_{l}f

with the following domain.

•

If
l\geq 1
:
\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\operatorname{Dom}_{\max}(\tau_{l})
.

•

If
l=0
:

\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{0}):=\{f\in\operatorname{Dom}_{\max}(\tau_{0})\mid\lim_{\hat{v}\to 0^{+}}\hat{v}\,f(\hat{v})=0\}
.

Then
\mathcal{L}_{l}
is self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
13
, self-adjoint realizations of

\mathcal{L}_{l}
correspond bijectively to those of the
half-line radial Schrödinger operator
h_{l}
on
L^{2}(0,\infty)
. In Weyl’s endpoint classification
[
26
,
18
]
,
infinity is limit-point for all
l
. At the origin:

•

l\geq 1
: limit-point — no boundary condition needed;

•

l=0
: limit-circle — we impose the Dirichlet condition

\psi(0)=0
for
\psi=\mathcal{U}f
, which in the
original variable reads
\lim_{\hat{v}\to 0^{+}}\hat{v}\,f(\hat{v})=0
.

Since
h_{l}
with the above domain is self-adjoint and

\mathcal{U}
is unitary,
\mathcal{L}_{l}=\mathcal{U}^{-1}h_{l}\,\mathcal{U}

is self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
.
∎

Lemmas
13
and
15

together establish Theorem
6
.

4.3
Spectrum and generalized eigenfunctions

We now determine the spectral resolution of
\mathcal{L}_{l}
,
proving Theorem
7
.

Proposition 16

(Absolutely continuous spectrum)
.

For every
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the self-adjoint operator

\mathcal{L}_{l}
has purely absolutely continuous spectrum

\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\sigma_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal{L}_{l})=[0,\infty),\qquad\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\varnothing.

Proof.

By unitary equivalence (Lemma
13
),
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\sigma(h_{l})
.
The operator
h_{l}
is the free radial Schrödinger operator on the half-line, diagonalized by the spherical Hankel transform
[
25
,
15
]
of order
l+1/2
. Its spectrum is purely absolutely continuous and equals
[0,\infty)
. Moreover,
h_{l}\geq 0
excludes negative
eigenvalues. Positive-energy solutions oscillate at infinity and are
not square-integrable; the regular solution at
\lambda=0
is
likewise not square-integrable. Hence there is no point spectrum.
∎

The generalized eigenfunctions correspond to
\lambda=-k^{2}\geq 0
. That is the imaginary
branch of Definition
2
(up to the
constant prefactor):

\Phi_{l,\kappa}(\hat{v}):=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}j_{l}(\kappa\hat{v}),\qquad\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

(38)

Since
j_{l}(-\kappa\hat{v})
is linearly dependent on

j_{l}(\kappa\hat{v})
, it suffices to consider
\kappa\geq 0
.
In the sense of distributions on
(0,\infty)
these functions
satisfy
\mathcal{L}_{l}\Phi_{l,\kappa}=\kappa^{2}\Phi_{l,\kappa}
.

Lemma 17

(Generalized orthogonality)
.

For every
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the generalized eigenfunctions satisfy the weighted
\delta
-orthogonality

\bigl\langle\Phi_{l,\kappa},\,\Phi_{l,\kappa^{\prime}}\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}}=\frac{\pi}{2\kappa^{2}}\,\delta(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}).

Proof.

Using
\omega(\hat{v})=\hat{v}^{2}e^{2\hat{v}^{2}}
and the definition of

\Phi_{l,\kappa}
,

\displaystyle\bigl\langle\Phi_{l,\kappa},\Phi_{l,\kappa^{\prime}}\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}j_{l}(\kappa\hat{v})\,e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}j_{l}(\kappa^{\prime}\hat{v})\,\hat{v}^{2}e^{2\hat{v}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{v}^{2}\,j_{l}(\kappa\hat{v})\,j_{l}(\kappa^{\prime}\hat{v})\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{l}\delta(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}),

where the last equality is the standard orthogonality of spherical
Bessel functions
[
25
,
15
]
. The normalized factor is
\mathcal{N}_{l}(\kappa)=\pi/(2\kappa^{2})
.
∎

Lemma 18

(Completeness and the King transform)
.

For each
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the family

\{\Phi_{l,\kappa}\mid\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}

is complete in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
. More precisely, for every
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
there exists
a unique spectral coefficient

\widetilde{f}\in L^{2}((0,\infty);\mathcal{N}_{l}(\kappa)\,\,\mathrm{d}\kappa)

such that the
King transform

f(\hat{v})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{f}(\kappa)\,\Phi_{l,\kappa}(\hat{v})\,\,\mathrm{d}\kappa

(39)

holds in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
. The spectral coefficient is recovered via the inverse
transform

\widetilde{f}(\kappa)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{l}(\kappa)}\bigl\langle f,\Phi_{l,\kappa}\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}},

(40)

and the Parseval identity

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl|\widetilde{f}(\kappa)\bigr|^{2}\,\mathcal{N}_{l}(\kappa)\,\,\mathrm{d}\kappa

(41)

holds.

Proof.

By Lemma
13
,
the problem reduces to the completeness of the spherical Hankel transform
[
15
]
of order
l+1/2
on
L^{2}(0,\infty)
. The normalization factor follows from Lemma
17
.
∎

Proposition
16
,
Lemma
17
and
Lemma
18
together constitute the proof of
Theorem
7
.

5
Density of the real-parameter King functions

The spectral theory of Section
4
concerns the
imaginary-parameter branch

\Phi_{l,\kappa}(\hat{v})
presented by Eq. (
38
) and is developed in the
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
space. In the KMM, however,
one works with the
real-parameter
King function

\Psi_{l,k}(\hat{v})=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}i_{l}(k\hat{v}),\qquad k\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

This function corresponds to the negative spectral parameter

\lambda=-k^{2}<0
. Therefore, it lies in the resolvent set

\varrho(\mathcal{L}_{l})=\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
of operator
\mathcal{L}_{l}
, rather than in its spectral family.

We prove that the real-parameter family is nevertheless dense in the physically natural radial Hilbert space

H_{\rm rad}=L^{2}\big((0,\infty);\,\hat{v}^{2}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}\big)~.

(42)

The inner product (conjugate-linear in the second argument) is
denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H_{\rm rad}}
.
The weight
\hat{v}^{2}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}
combines the spherical volume
element in 3V space with the Gaussian decay of the Maxwellian equilibrium.

5.1
Two auxiliary lemmas

The proof of density rests on the analyticity of the modified
spherical Bessel function
[
25
,
15
]
and the uniqueness of the Fourier transform of finite complex measures. We isolate the key steps below.

Lemma 19

(Entire function generated by a test function)
.

Let
h\in H_{\rm rad}
. For
z\in\mathbb{C}
, set

\Psi_{l,z}(\hat{v})=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}i_{l}(z\hat{v}),

and define

F(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(\hat{v})\Psi_{l,z}(\hat{v})\hat{v}^{2}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}.

Then
F
is entire and admits the expansion

F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n,l}z^{2n+l}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(\hat{v})\hat{v}^{2n+l+2}e^{-2\hat{v}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v},

(43)

where

a_{n,l}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{2n+l+1}n!\Gamma(n+l+3/2)}\neq 0.

(44)

Proof.

The proof follows by inserting the absolutely convergent Taylor expansion

i_{l}(w)=w^{l}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n,l}w^{2n},

where
a_{n,l}
given by Eq. (
44
).
Fix
R>0
and let
|z|\leq R
.
From (
44
) we have

|a_{n,l}|\leq C_{l}/(\Gamma(n+l+3/2)\,n!)

for some
C_{l}>0
independent of
n
.
Consequently, for every
\hat{v}>0
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n,l}|R^{2n+l}\hat{v}^{2n+l}\leq C_{l}R^{l}\hat{v}^{l}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(R^{2}\hat{v}^{2})^{n}}{\Gamma(n+l+3/2)\,n!}\leq C_{l,R}(1+\hat{v})^{l}e^{R\hat{v}},

where
C_{l,R}>0
depends only on
l
and
R
.
Hence

|h(\hat{v})\,i_{l}(z\hat{v})|\,\hat{v}^{2}e^{-2\hat{v}^{2}}\leq C_{l,R}\,|h(\hat{v})|(1+\hat{v})^{l}e^{R\hat{v}}\hat{v}^{2}e^{-2\hat{v}^{2}}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(\hat{v})|(1+\hat{v})^{l}e^{R\hat{v}}\hat{v}^{2}e^{-2\hat{v}^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}\leq\|h\|_{H_{\rm rad}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+\hat{v})^{2l}\hat{v}^{2}e^{2R\hat{v}-3\hat{v}^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}\Bigr)^{\!1/2}<\infty,

since
e^{-3\hat{v}^{2}}
dominates
e^{2R\hat{v}}
as
\hat{v}\to\infty
.
By dominated convergence, summation and integration may be
interchanged, yielding the absolutely and uniformly convergent power series (
43
) for
|z|\leq R
. Since
R
is
arbitrary,
F
is entire.
∎

Lemma 20

(Finite complex measure with vanishing moments)
.

Let
\varepsilon
be a finite complex Borel measure on
(0,\infty)

and let
|\varepsilon|
denote its total variation measure. Assume
that there exists
\eta>0
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\eta x}\,\,\mathrm{d}|\varepsilon|(x)<\infty.

(45)

If

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon(x)=0\qquad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}~.

(46)

The Fourier transform is injective on the space of finite
complex Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}

[
6
]
; therefore
\varepsilon\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Condition (
45
) guarantees that the Laplace
transform of
\varepsilon
,

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{tx}\,\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon(x),

is well defined and holomorphic in the open half-plane

\{t\in\mathbb{C}:\Re(t)<\eta\}
.
Differentiating under the integral sign gives

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n}e^{tx}\,\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon(x),\qquad\Re(t)<\eta.

Evaluating at
t=0
and using (
46
) yields

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}(0)=0
for all
n\geq 0
.
Hence
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
vanishes identically in a
neighbourhood of the origin; by analytic continuation it vanishes on
the whole half-plane
\Re(t)<\eta
.

In particular, setting
t=\mathrm{i}\chi
with
\chi\in\mathbb{R}
gives

0=\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{i}\chi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\mathrm{i}\chi x}\,\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon(x)=\widehat{\varepsilon}(\chi),

where
\widehat{\varepsilon}
is the Fourier transform of the finite
complex measure
\varepsilon
(extended by zero to the negative
half-line). The Fourier transform is injective on the space of finite
complex Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}

[
6
]
; therefore
\varepsilon\equiv 0
.
∎

5.2
Proof of Theorem
8

Proof.

Let
g\in H_{\rm rad}
be orthogonal to every
\Psi_{l,k}
with

k>0
. Since the inner product is conjugate-linear in the second

h=\overline{g}
. Then the function
F
in

19
satisfies

for every

. Since

\mathbb{C}
.

Thus Lemma

Because the density

Therefore the orthogonal complement of

\operatorname{span}\mathscr{K}_{l}^{\mathbb{R}}

.
∎

5.3
Consequences and the Laguerre connection

The restriction
k\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
covers all physical shift
amplitudes (
u=\varsigma k/2\geq 0
). By analyticity in
k
,
the density conclusion extends immediately to any larger parameter
set containing
[0,\infty)
, for instance
\Re(k)\geq 0
or all

k\in\mathbb{C}
.
A multiplicative renormalization reveals the direct link to the classical Laguerre basis.

Corollary 21

(Density of the renormalized King function)
.

For every
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the renormalized family

\widetilde{\mathscr{K}}_{l}=\bigl\{\widetilde{\Psi}_{l,k}(\hat{v})=\hat{v}^{-l}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}i_{l}(k\hat{v})\mid k\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\bigr\}

is dense in the polynomial-weighted space

\widetilde{H}_{\rm rad}^{\,l}=L^{2}\big((0,\infty);\,\hat{v}^{2l+2}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}\big).

(48)

Proof.

The multiplication operator

V:H_{\rm rad}\to\widetilde{H}_{\rm rad}^{\,l}
,

(Vf)(\hat{v})=\hat{v}^{-l}f(\hat{v})
,
is unitary because for all
f,g\in H_{\rm rad}
,

\langle Vf,Vg\rangle_{\widetilde{H}_{\rm rad}^{\,l}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{v}^{-l}f(\hat{v})\,\overline{\hat{v}^{-l}g(\hat{v})}\,\hat{v}^{2l+2}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\hat{v}=\langle f,g\rangle_{H_{\rm rad}}.

By Theorem
8
,
\mathscr{K}_{l}^{\mathbb{R}}
is
dense in
H_{\rm rad}
; hence its image under
V
is dense in

\widetilde{H}_{\rm rad}^{\,l}
.
∎

Remark 22

(Laguerre-space correspondence)
.

Under the change of variable
x=\hat{v}^{2}
, the renormalized family
maps to
x^{-l/2}e^{-x}i_{l}(k\sqrt{x})
, and

\widetilde{H}_{\rm rad}^{\,l}
is isometrically isomorphic to the Laguerre space

\mathscr{L}_{l+1/2}^{2}=L^{2}\big((0,\infty);\,x^{l+1/2}e^{-x}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\big).

Corollary
21
is therefore
equivalent to the density of

\{x^{-l/2}e^{-x}i_{l}(k\sqrt{x})\mid k>0\}

in the Hilbert space naturally associated with the Laguerre basis
L_{p}^{(l+1/2)}(x)
.

6
Applications to King mixture representations

The main results of Section
2.3
establish two
complementary perspectives on the complex-parameter King function

\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};k,\varsigma)
introduced in
Definition
2
:

•

For
k\in\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(imaginary branch), the
functions
\mathcal{K}_{l}
is the generalized
eigenfunction of the self-adjoint King operator

\mathcal{L}_{l}
and are complete in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}

(Theorems
6
–
7
).

•

For
k\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(real branch), they are the
radial profiles of Shifted Gaussian and are dense in

H_{\rm rad}
(Theorem
8
).

We briefly discuss the modeling implications of the preceding analysis for
King mixture representations.

Remark 23

(Role of general complex parameters)
.

For a general complex parameter

k\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}_{>0})
,
the family remains mathematically well-defined and inherits the
density of the real branch by analyticity, but adds no independent
modeling power. We therefore focus on the real and imaginary
branches, which provide, respectively, the approximation basis and
the spectral basis for the theory.

6.1
Laplace-King representation

For
k=\mathrm{i}\kappa
with
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the King
function reduces to the generalized eigenfunction

\Phi_{l,\kappa}(\hat{v})=e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}j_{l}(\kappa\hat{v})
of

\mathcal{L}_{l}
(up to a constant prefactor). If
f_{l}^{m}(\hat{v})\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}
(decaying
faster than
e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}
as
\hat{v}\to\infty
),
Theorem
7
provides the orthogonal King
transform pair

\displaystyle f_{l}^{m}(\hat{v})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{f}_{l}^{\,m}(\kappa)\,\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};\mathrm{i}\kappa,\varsigma)\,\,\mathrm{d}\kappa,

(49)

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{l}^{\,m}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|a_{l}(\kappa)|^{2}}\frac{2\kappa^{2}}{\pi}\bigl\langle f_{l}^{m}(\cdot),\,\mathcal{K}_{l}(\cdot;\mathrm{i}\kappa,\varsigma)\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{K}}},

(50)

together with the Parseval identity.
Coefficient
a_{l}(\kappa)
satisfies
\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};\mathrm{i}\kappa,\varsigma)=a_{l}(\kappa)\Phi_{l,\kappa}(\hat{v})
.
This can be viewed as a

continuous KMM
: a spectral integral over the imaginary branch
that diagonalizes the King operator
\mathcal{L}_{l}
.

For the real branch (
k\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
), let
f_{l}^{m}(\hat{v})
be a radial amplitude in
the laboratory-frame spherical-harmonic expansion
(
3
) of a distribution
f(\bm{\hat{v}})
.
If
f_{l}^{m}(\hat{v})\in H_{\rm rad}
,
Theorem
8
immediately yields the following.

Theorem 24

(Universal approximation of radial mode)
.

Let
f_{l}^{m}(\hat{v})\in H_{\rm rad}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
there
exist an integer
S
, positive parameters
k_{1},\dots,k_{S}
and weights
\mathfrak{n}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{n}_{S}
such that

\Bigl\|f_{l}^{m}(\hat{v})-\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathfrak{n}_{s}\,\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};k_{s},\varsigma)\,\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta_{k_{s}},\phi_{k_{s}})}\Bigr\|_{H_{\rm rad}}<\varepsilon.

(51)

Choose directions
(\theta_{k_{s}},\phi_{k_{s}})
such that

\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta_{k_{s}},\phi_{k_{s}})\neq 0
.
Then the angular factors can be absorbed into the weights
\mathfrak{n}_{s}
.

The finite sum in (
51
) defines the
King mixture
model
(KMM) for the radial amplitude
f_{l}^{m}(\hat{v})
— a
discrete, non-orthogonal superposition of Shifted Gaussian radial
profiles. For fixed angular momentum
l
, this is the exact radial
analog of the universal approximation theorem for GMM
[
12
,
3
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with fixed covariance. The centrifugal factor
i_{l}(k\hat{v})\sim(k\hat{v})^{l}
automatically adapts the mixture to the angular-momentum barrier, a feature absent in standard GMM.

Remark 25
.

The condition
f_{l}^{m}(\hat{v})\in H_{\rm rad}
permits function growing slightly slower than
e^{\hat{v}^{2}/2}
as
\hat{v}\to\infty
.
The Gaussian weight
\hat{v}^{2}e^{-\hat{v}^{2}}
in the measure precisely compensates such growth. From Theorem
24
,

f_{l}^{m}(\hat{v})\approx\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathfrak{n}_{s}\,\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};k_{s},\varsigma)\,\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta_{k_{s}},\phi_{k_{s}})}~.

(52)

Inserting either the discrete or the continuous KMM back into the
spherical-harmonic expansion (
3
) yields a
finite- or infinite-dimensional representation of the full 3V
velocity distribution. In the discrete case one obtains a
superposition of Shifted Gaussian components sharing the same
thermal speed
\varsigma
but with distinct shift amplitudes:

f(\hat{v},\theta,\phi)\;\approx\;\sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\sum_{s=1}^{S_{l,m}}\mathfrak{n}_{s}\,\mathcal{K}_{l}(\hat{v};k_{s},\varsigma)\,\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta_{k_{s}},\phi_{k_{s}})}\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta,\phi).

(53)

Here

are the spherical angles of the shift vector associated with the parameter
k

m=0
term survives. We refer to (

Laplace-King
representation. Its continuous analog is obtained by replacing
\sum_{s}\mathfrak{n}_{s}
with

\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{f}_{l}^{\,m}(\kappa)\,\,\mathrm{d}\kappa

and setting
k_{s}=\mathrm{i}\kappa_{s}
.

Remark 26
.

The representation (
53
) should be understood as a modal Laplace–King expansion. If the same shift vectors and weights are imposed consistently across all angular modes, it reduces to a genuine Shifted-Gaussian mixture. Otherwise, it is a more general modal King representation.

6.2
Comparison with the Laguerre expansion

The classical Laguerre spectral method expands the
relative

distribution
\mathfrak{f}=f/M_{\bm{u},\varsigma}
in the co-moving
frame:

\mathfrak{f}(\widehat{\bm{w}})=\sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\sum_{p=0}^{P_{l,m}}c_{p,l}^{\,m}\,R_{p,l}^{\mathrm{Lag}}(\widehat{w})\,\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta_{w},\phi_{w}),

where
R_{p,l}^{\mathrm{Lag}}(\widehat{w})=\widehat{w}^{\,l}L_{p}^{(l+1/2)}(\widehat{w}^{2})

as in (
13
). The expansion coefficients are

c_{p,l}^{\,m}=\frac{2(p!)}{\Gamma(p+l+3/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{f}_{l}^{\,m}(\widehat{w})\,R_{p,l}^{\mathrm{Lag}}(\widehat{w})\,\widehat{w}^{2}e^{-\widehat{w}^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\widehat{w},

(54)

with angular projections

\mathfrak{f}_{l}^{\,m}(\widehat{w})=\Bigl(N_{l}^{m}\Bigr)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\mathfrak{f}(\widehat{w},\theta_{w},\phi_{w})\,\overline{\widetilde{Y}_{l}^{m}(\theta_{w},\phi_{w})}\,\,\mathrm{d}\Omega.

The King–Laguerre identity (Theorem
5
) shows that each King profile is an infinite Laguerre superposition. Thus a King mixture may be viewed as a nonlinear reorganization of the fixed-center Laguerre hierarchy, with the shift parameter partially resumming infinitely many Laguerre modes.

The two representations are therefore complementary. The Laguerre basis is adapted to the co-moving spectral structure of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator, while the King basis is adapted to shifted Gaussian components in the laboratory frame. The former is particularly natural near a single equilibrium Maxwellian; the latter is useful for representing finite-shift or moderately non-equilibrium radial structures. Together with the continuous King transform, they form a complete toolkit for non-equilibrium velocity-space representations.

7
Discussion and Conclusion

We have developed a spectral and approximation framework for King
function associated with shifted Gaussian distribution. The central
observation is that King function is not another co-moving Hermite or Laguerre
spectral mode, but laboratory-frame radial kernels obtained from the
spherical-harmonic decomposition of shifted Gaussian profiles. In particular, the King–Laguerre expansion shows that
each King function is an infinite superposition of Laguerre radial mode,
which can be interpreted as a shift-adapted resummation of the fixed-center Laguerre hierarchy.

The operator-theoretic analysis identifies the natural
self-adjoint King operator. After a Gaussian-weighted Liouville
transform, the King differential expression is unitarily equivalent to
the free radial Schrödinger operator on the half-line. Hence its
self-adjoint realization has purely absolutely continuous spectrum

[0,\infty)
. The corresponding generalized eigenfunction is the
imaginary-parameter King function, and the associated spectral
representation is King transform, a Gaussian-conjugated spherical Hankel transform.
In contrast, the real-parameter King function correspond to negative spectral parameter, which lie in the resolvent set.

This distinction yields a useful dual interpretation of the complex-parameter King function. The imaginary branch provides an orthogonal continuous transform, whereas the real branch provides a non-orthogonal
approximation dictionary. Although the real-parameter King function is
not spectral eigenfunction, their span is dense in the natural radial
space
H_{\rm rad}
. This density property gives a rigorous approximation-theoretic
basis for real-parameter King mixture model (KMM). In addition, the weighted

L^{1}
-integrability criteria and closed-form moment formulas is derived, justifying the normalization of the King
kernel and provide explicit moment constraints for finite King mixture.

Several questions remain open. The density theorem is qualitative and
does not provide convergence rates for finite mixture. Conditioning of
the non-orthogonal King dictionary, stability of parameter fitting,
positivity preservation, optimal selection of shift parameters, and
efficient enforcement of moment constraints require further analysis.
Extensions to variable thermal speed, and more general nonlinear Fokker–Planck collision operator
are natural directions for future work.
