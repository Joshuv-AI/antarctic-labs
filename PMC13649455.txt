CT-based deep learning auto-segmentation of high-risk clinical target volume in CT-guided cervical cancer brachytherapy: a single-center pragmatic study

Introduction

Cervical cancer remains a major global health burden, and image-guided adaptive brachytherapy is essential for curative treatment for locally advanced disease (, ). Accurate high-risk clinical target volume (HR-CTV) delineation underpins target coverage and organ-at-risk sparing (OAR) sparing (–). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is preferred because of superior soft tissue contrast; nevertheless, computed tomography (CT)-guided planning remains common where access, scheduling, applicator compatibility, or workflow constraints limit MRI at each fraction (–).

Manual HR-CTV contouring on CT is time-consuming and observer dependent (, ). Prior studies have evaluated deep-learning segmentation in CT-based tandem-and-ovoid brachytherapy, postoperative vaginal brachytherapy, MRI-based workflows, and OAR implementation (–). More recent work has benchmarked convolutional neural network (CNN)-, transformer-, and state-space architectures in larger CT cohorts and incorporated physician acceptability and dosimetric endpoints (, ). Thus, CT-based auto-segmentation itself is no longer the unresolved question; the remaining need is transparent evaluation under deployment-relevant preprocessing, patient-separated testing, target-specific clinical review, and explicit uncertainty for small subgroups.

This study was designed as a pragmatic single-center benchmark rather than a new architecture proposal or a replacement for MRI-guided target definition. Its incremental contribution is the comparison of three representative architecture classes—slice-wise CNN, volumetric CNN, and volumetric transformer—within the same CT-only, applicator-in-situ workflow, covering intact-cervix tandem-and-ovoid and postoperative vaginal-cylinder target definitions. The primary objective was to compare geometric performance for intact-cervix HR-CTV (HR-CTVc); the postoperative vaginal stump HR-CTV (HR-CTVv) results were exploratory. The secondary objective was to characterize physician consensus edit burden as a clinically relevant implementation endpoint.

Methods

Study design and data partitioning

This retrospective single-center model development and internal validation study included 544 CT-guided brachytherapy fractions from 182 patients, including 509 fractions from 163 intact-cervix patients treated with tandem-and-ovoid applicators and 35 fractions from 19 postoperative patients treated with vaginal cylinders. All fractions from each patient were assigned to one partition only. HR-CTVc fractions were divided 325/82/102 and the HR-CTVv fractions were divided 22/6/7 into training/validation/testing sets, respectively. The HR-CTVv test set contained seven fractions, and thus this subgroup was analyzed descriptively as exploratory.

CT was acquired with the applicator in situ on a Philips Brilliance Big Bore (Philips Medical Systems (Cleveland) Inc., Cleveland, OH, USA) scanner at 120 kVp and approximately 325 mAs, without intravenous contrast. Native in-plane resolution was approximately 0.66 × 0.66 mm and slice thickness was 3 mm.

Reference contouring

The clinical reference contours followed ICRU Report 89 and IBS–GEC-ESTRO–ABS CT-based recommendations (, ). Contours were generated and reviewed by two senior radiation oncologists specializing in gynecologic radiotherapy, each with at least 10 years of clinical radiotherapy experience. One physician generated the initial contour, and the other performed slice-by-slice clinical review and modification as needed. Review incorporated examination findings, diagnostic MRI obtained before brachytherapy when available, external-beam records, and prior brachytherapy fractions. Disagreements were resolved by joint review before treatment approval. These CT-based consensus contours represent a pragmatic clinical reference rather than MRI-defined ground truth.

An ancillary analysis was available for 20 test fractions (16 HR-CTVc and 4 HR-CTVv), in which the two physicians delineated HR-CTV independently while unaware of the other contour. Pairwise physician agreement was Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) 0.80 ± 0.06 and 95th percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95) 3.4 ± 1.8 mm. The corresponding 3D U-shaped convolutional neural network architecture (U-Net)-to-observer DSCs were 0.81 ± 0.06 and 0.80 ± 0.06. This geometric analysis provides an interobserver benchmark distinct from the consensus clinical-usability endpoint. The four-fraction HR-CTVv component was reported descriptively.

Models and label-independent preprocessing

The evaluated architectures were two-dimensional (2D) U-Net, three-dimensional (3D) U-Net, and nnFormer (–). The 2D model processed slices independently; the 3D model used volumetric convolutions; and nnFormer used volumetric self-attention. The applicator remained visible, but no applicator mask or digitization channel was supplied (, ). The task-specific 3D U-Net encoder–decoder configurations for HR-CTVc and HR-CTVv are shown in and , respectively.

The two clinical target types were handled as separate tasks. Each task was resampled to the median native spacing of its training cohort—3.0 × 0.6003 × 0.6003 mm3 for HR-CTVc and 3.0 × 0.5814 × 0.5814 mm3 for HR-CTVv—using linear interpolation for CT and nearest-neighbor interpolation for masks. In accordance with the nnU-Net CT-normalization strategy (), the 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles, mean, and standard deviation were calculated once from pooled HR-CTV foreground voxels in the training partition. These task-level parameters were then fixed and applied unchanged to all training, validation, and test images. After resampling, images were cropped or padded to 32 × 256 × 256 voxels around the image-matrix center; this operation did not use an HR-CTV contour. Validation and test labels were accessed only after inference for metric calculation. Thus, no target label was required at inference.

Training used Adam (initial learning rate 0.001), plateau-based decay, batch size 2 for volumetric models and 8 for the 2D model, a Dice-plus-cross-entropy loss, and random rotation, flipping, and intensity perturbation. Training continued for 300 epochs, and the checkpoint with the best validation DSC was selected before test evaluation ().

Clinical usability and dosimetric illustration

The test-set contours were reviewed by the same two senior radiation oncologists specializing in gynecologic radiotherapy using a consensus-adjudication design. For each contour, one final category was assigned. “No correction” indicated immediate clinical acceptability. “Minor correction” indicated localized boundary editing without complete redelineation. “Major correction” indicated substantial multi-slice editing, correction of a clinically important geographic miss, or complete or near-complete redelineation (, , ). Because this design generated one adjudicated category rather than paired physician-level ordinal ratings, weighted Cohen’s kappa was not applicable. The assisted pathway combined treatment-planning-system OAR auto-contouring, 3D U-Net HR-CTV inference, and physician review and correction of the generated structures. Dose-volume histograms (DVHs) from one tandem-and-ovoid case and one vaginal-cylinder case were selected to demonstrate how contour variation may propagate to treatment-planning metrics. This component was qualitative by design and was not an equivalence or noninferiority test.

Statistical analysis

Segmentation performance was evaluated using DSC, HD95, and average surface distance (ASD) (–). Test-set results are reported as mean ± SD, consistent with the locked aggregate analysis. The large nnFormer HR-CTVc HD95 dispersion was interpreted as an outlier-sensitive distribution in accordance with current validation guidance (, ). Because the retained output set comprised aggregate rather than case-level metrics, median, IQR, and empty-prediction frequency were not estimated retrospectively. reports the original fraction-level pairwise comparisons. Given within-patient clustering of repeated fractions, these P values are interpreted as exploratory descriptive evidence; architecture selection was based on the combined geometric and clinical-review findings rather than significance testing alone.

Results

Cohort characteristics

The intact-cervix cohort included International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics stages IB3–IVA. No interstitial or hybrid-needle cases were included. The median number of fractions per patient was 3 in the intact-cervix group and 2 in the vaginal-cylinder group ().

Physician consensus review

On consensus review, 3D U-Net required no or minor correction in 91/102 HR-CTVc fractions (89.2%) and 6/7 HR-CTVv fractions (85.7%). The HR-CTVv estimate is exploratory because it was based on seven test fractions. The consensus-adjudication design generated one final category per contour, so weighted kappa was not applicable. Descriptive workflow timing

In the predefined 20-fraction workflow evaluation, full manual HR-CTV and OAR delineation required 24.3 ± 2.9 min per fraction (median, 24.0 min; IQR, 22.1–26.5 min). The 3D U-Net inference time was 5.4 ± 0.8 s, and the total assisted contouring workflow required 3.2 ± 0.5 min (median, 3.1 min; IQR, 2.8–3.5 min). The corresponding aggregate absolute reduction was 21.1 ± 2.8 min and the relative reduction was 86.8% ± 3.4% (). These results are reported as a descriptive implementation endpoint, consistent with the archived analysis plan.

Discussion

The central finding is clinically pragmatic: within a CT-only, applicator-in-situ cohort, the volumetric 3D U-Net produced the most favorable overall balance of overlap, boundary agreement, and consensus edit burden for intact-cervix cases. This result prioritizes 3D U-Net for physician-supervised implementation and external validation in comparable brachytherapy workflows.

Prior CT-guided brachytherapy studies addressed applicator-aware segmentation, postoperative target delineation, and dedicated tandem-and-ovoid HR-CTV models (–, , ). Wang et al. reported a postoperative HR-CTV DSC of 0.87 and combined geometric, physician, and dosimetric assessment (). Kraus et al. prospectively evaluated OAR auto-contouring in CT-based gynecologic brachytherapy and emphasized the distinction between technical inference and clinically verified workflow efficiency (). In a 2025 five-model benchmark of 694 CT scans, Zang et al. reported an HR-CTV DSC of 0.862 and HD95 of 3.638 mm, together with physician acceptability and cohort-level dosimetry (). Related cervical cancer segmentation studies in planning CT, MRI, and dual-modality workflows have broadened technical and clinical validation across target and OAR settings (–, –). The present study therefore provides a patient-separated, within-cohort comparison across three architecture classes and two target definitions rather than a first demonstration of CT auto-segmentation.

CT-guided workflow remains clinically relevant because radiotherapy and imaging resources are unequally distributed (, ), while applicator-in-situ MRI may be constrained by access, scheduling, and compatibility (). Hybrid MRI/CT strategies can reduce dependence on MRI at every fraction (). Nevertheless, CT-based automation still requires explicit recognition of the limitations of CT-defined HR-CTV.

The apparent advantage of 3D U-Net is technically plausible because HR-CTV definition depends on volumetric relationships among residual disease, cervix or vaginal cuff, uterus, parametria, and applicator geometry (–). Slice-wise models cannot directly enforce inter-slice coherence, whereas transformer-based models may be more sensitive to cohort size and optimization (, , , ). Semi-supervised MRI-based OAR segmentation has expanded potential training strategies (), but it does not remove the target-definition limitations of CT-only HR-CTV segmentation. The very large nnFormer HR-CTVc HD95 dispersion illustrates why a single mean can obscure clinically important failure behavior. Recent validation guidance recommends matching metrics to the intended clinical use, examining distributions and outliers, and combining geometric results with structured expert review rather than relying on one aggregate measure (, , ).

The ancillary physician-to-physician analysis provides a clinically relevant benchmark for contour variability in this CT-based workflow and complements the consensus edit categories. In a 2026 multicenter study, Peng et al. reported a baseline interobserver DSC of 0.88 and HD95 of 3.2 mm, with further improvement after limited clinician prompting (). Differences from the present DSC of 0.80 and HD95 of 3.4 mm are plausible given the CT-only target definition, cohort composition, and delineation procedure. Together, these findings support supervised use and explicit reporting of observer variability.

The predefined 20-fraction workflow evaluation demonstrated a substantial reduction in contouring time within the implemented clinical pathway. This endpoint represents an aggregate implementation signal rather than a prospective time-motion comparison because the assisted workflow combined OAR automation, HR-CTV inference, and physician review. A prospective study with case-level paired timing and standardized workflow definitions would establish the precision and transportability of this efficiency estimate.

The seven postoperative test fractions provide a preliminary feasibility signal for vaginal stump auto-segmentation. Their limited anatomical coverage and statistical precision do not support definitive architecture ranking, but the observed performance provides a clinically relevant basis for expanding postoperative cohorts and prospective workflow evaluation (, ).

The two DVH examples illustrate how geometric contour differences propagate to treatment-planning metrics; they were not designed to estimate population-level dosimetric impact. The appropriate confirmatory assessment is locked-protocol replanning of the complete test cohort with paired HR-CTV D90 and OAR D2cc differences, confidence intervals, and prespecified clinically meaningful thresholds (, , , ).

Several design features define the scope of inference. Reference contours were CT-based clinical consensus contours rather than MRI-defined targets, and internal validation was performed within a single acquisition and contouring environment with repeated fractions and a small postoperative subgroup (, ). The primary intact-cervix findings therefore establish performance within the intended institutional workflow, while multicenter testing is required to assess transportability. Inference itself was label independent, although the training-derived intensity fingerprint remains institution and task specific (). Clinical usability was evaluated by consensus adjudication, for which weighted kappa is not applicable. The archived nnFormer and timing outputs were aggregate, so post hoc case-level distributional and paired timing analyses were not introduced. These boundaries support physician-supervised implementation and define the next validation steps without altering the reported internal test results.

Conclusions

In this internally validated CT-guided brachytherapy cohort, 3D U-Net showed the most favorable overall performance among the evaluated architectures for intact-cervix cases and is the leading candidate for physician-supervised implementation in this workflow. The postoperative vaginal stump results provide a preliminary feasibility signal that requires confirmation in a larger cohort. Multicenter external validation, interobserver benchmarking, and cohort-level dosimetric assessment are the appropriate next steps. MRI-guided target definition remains preferred where available.
