A Coupled Theoretical and Experimental Approach to Elucidating
the Mechanisms of Methyl Esters
 Final Technical Report
 Nicole J. Labbe
 University of Colorado Boulder

Abstract

The goal of this project was to probe fundamental kinetics questions regarding the gas phase
reactive behavior of oxygenates. In order to succeed, our program necessitated both the
development of new experimental tools and the development of theory based kinetic models to
explain the chemistry of oxygenate fuels. By using advanced hybrid additive manufacturing/
traditional manufacturing techniques to create specialized ceramic micro-reactors, the team was
able to simulate extreme high-temperature environments with much greater precision and
durability than was previously possible. These tiny, high-tech tubes allow researchers to capture
and identify "fleeting" chemical species—molecules that exist for only a fraction of a
millisecond—using sophisticated light sources and mass spectrometry. Additionally, the team
supported the development of a tabletop VUV laser system for isomer detection without a
synchrotron. Through this work, we have successfully mapped out the specific chemical pathways
of various oxygen-rich fuels, solving long-standing mysteries about how these substances break
down kinetically. Ultimately, this research provides the fundamental knowledge needed to design
next-generation engines and fuels that are better for the environment.

Technical Description

The provided documents outline significant technical achievements in the field of gas-phase
chemical kinetics that were accomplished through this project, focusing on the development of
novel micro-reactors and the elucidation of complex chemical mechanisms for oxygenated
fuels. These advancements were made through a coupled theoretical and experimental approach
at the University of Colorado Boulder.

1. Advanced Micro-Reactor Development
A major achievement is the reinvention and enhancement of the Chen Nozzle, a radical-
generating micro-reactor used for gas-phase chemical physics. While prior iterations of the Chen
Nozzle were fundamentally just a tube, this work focused on developing unique manufacturing
techniques for silicon carbide ceramics to create repeatable, high-quality tubes with modifiable
end features to create true nozzles. The goal of this was to be able to stabilize pressure conditions
within the body of the reactor to provide our group and other scientists with more repeatable and
constable conditions for kinetics measurements.

2. Diagnostic Advances
To resolve very short-time (50–200 µsec) thermal decomposition chemistry, the program
successfully integrated several advanced diagnostic tools. Prior to the start of this project, the
experimental setup available in our lab utilized both time-of-flight photoionization mass
spectrometry (PIMS) and matrix-isolated infrared spectroscopy (IR), providing a universal and
sensitive method to detect nearly all polyatomic products. However, isomer resolution remained
a challenge in the PIMS setup. In collaboration with the Kapteyn-Murnane group, a new tunable,
tabletop Vacuum Ultraviolet (VUV) light source was acquired and tested. This source uses four-
wave-mixing in a xenon-filled fiber to produce spectral lines spaced by 1.2 eV. This light source
enabled the separation of select isomers, which is critical for identifying specific combustion
intermediates. Further details may be found in Couch et al., Detection of the Keto-Enol
Tautomerization in Acetaldehyde, Acetone, Cyclohexanone, and Methyl Vinyl Ketone with a
Novel VUV Light Source. Proc. Combust. Inst. 38, 2021
(https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1778226).

3. Chemical Mechanism Elucidation
This research has clarified several critical mechanisms related to oxygenates used as biofuels or
other combustibles. The contributions to this work are outlined below:

Keto-Enol Tautomerization: As a proof of concept for the new VUV source, researchers detected
the keto-enol isomerization in combustion intermediates such as acetone, acetaldehyde,
cyclohexanone, and methyl vinyl ketone. Notably, they experimentally observed the thermal
tautomerization of acetone to propen-2-ol for the first time. Further details may be found in
Couch et al., Detection of the Keto-Enol Tautomerization in Acetaldehyde, Acetone,
Cyclohexanone, and Methyl Vinyl Ketone with a Novel VUV Light Source. Proc. Combust. Inst.
38, 2021 (https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1778226).

Propionic Acid Pyrolysis: The team resolved a discrepancy in existing literature regarding the
formation of methyl ketene from propionic acid. While previous works suggested direct
dehydrogenation, this study used a combined theoretical and experimental approach to identify a
well-skipping reaction through a diol intermediate (propene-1,1-diol) as the true source. Further

details may be found at Rogers et al. Diol Isomer Revealed as a Source of Methyl Ketene from
Propionic Acid Unimolecular Decomposition. International Journal of Chemical Kinetics. 52,
2021 (https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1976352).

Methyl Ester Decomposition and Sooting: The study examined how the presence and location of
a double bond in methyl esters (e.g., methyl 3-hexenoate vs. methyl 5-hexenoate) affects their
reactivity and sooting tendency (Yield Sooting Index). For methyl hexanoate, the research
determined that weakened C-C bond homolytic fissions dominate the pyrolysis. The program
produced the first quantitative atmospheric pressure combustion data for methyl hexanoate
through collaboration with the University of Duisburg-Essen. Further details may be found at
Rogers et al. Probing the Low-Temperature Chemistry of Methyl Hexanoate: Insights from
Oxygenate Intermediates. Proc. Combust. Inst. 38. 2021
(https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1778228)

Keto-hydroperoxides for THF: The goal of this work was to pinpoint the source of literature
inconsistencies for the low temperature oxidation chemistry of THF. While theoretical
calculations were generally in line with the disputed literature studies, the slight difference were
not enough to compensate for the ~4 orders of magnitude difference between the experimental
and model predictions in the literature. However, we were able to that the uncertainties in the
decomposition mechanisms likely lie with the uncertainty in abstraction chemistry for these
models. Further details may be found at Lockwood and Labbe. Insights on Keto-Hydroperoxide
Formation from O2 Addition to the Beta-Tetrahydrofuran Radical. Proc. Combust. Inst. 38. 2021.
(https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1778229)

Presentations

Generation Microreactors for Rapid Reaction Speciation Data, Nicole J. Labbe, WSSCI Spring
Meeting, Stanford, CA, March 2022), a local meeting (A theory based low temperature ignition
mechanism for 2-butanol, Katie S. Lockwood, Sheik F. Ahmed, Thomas D. Foust, Nicole J.

Digital Manufacturing Method for Silicon Carbide, Jatinder Sampathkumar, Nik Ninos, Gregory
Whiting, Nicole J. Labbe, MRS Fall Meeting, Boston, MA, November 2021), and three
international presentations (1. Detection of the Keto-Enol Tautomerization in Acetaldehyde,
Acetone, Cyclohexanone, and Methyl Vinyl Ketone with a Novel VUV Light Source, D. E.
Couch, Q. L. Nguyen, D. D. Hickstein, H. C. Kapteyn, M. M. Murnane, N. J. Labbe.
International Symposium on Combustion, Adelaide, Australia, January 2021; 2. Probing the
Low-Temperature Chemistry of Methyl Hexanoate: Insights from Oxygenate Intermediates, C.
O. Rogers, D. Kaczmarek, T. Kasper, N. J. Labbe. International Symposium on Combustion,
