Hyperon-pair spin tomography beyond scalar spin correlations
Lei Wang

Abstract
Spin correlations measured after hadronization probe how quantum information is transported through confinement. We formulate a process-independent spin-tomography framework for Λ ​ Λ ¯ \Lambda\bar{\Lambda} pairs in which the weak-decay angles determine the full two-spin tensor rather than only its scalar trace. Applied to the short-range STAR correlation in unpolarized p ​ p → Λ ​ Λ ¯ ​ X pp\to\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}X , the framework shows that the published scalar P Λ ​ Λ ¯ P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}} leaves a continuous density-matrix degeneracy: separable tensor completions and completions that violate the positive-partial-transpose (PPT) criterion can have the same trace. The missing observables are the transverse and longitudinal tensor components and their anisotropy A C = C ⟂ − C ∥ A_{C}=C_{\perp}-C_{\parallel} . A local P 0 3 {}^{3}P_{0} string-breaking benchmark turns the STAR trace into the falsifiable pattern C ⟂ > 0 C_{\perp}>0 , C ∥ < 0 C_{\parallel}<0 , and A C > 0 A_{C}>0 . We further show that feed-down must be treated as a tensor response rather than as a universal scalar dilution. In the complementary e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} calibration channel, the same benchmark predicts a centrally enhanced A C A_{C} of order 0.3 0.3 in Belle II kinematics, measurable with about 10 4 10^{4} selected pairs for nominal spin-transfer parameters. A claim of entanglement requires the reconstructed two-spin density matrix, not the scalar trace alone, to violate the PPT bound.

II
Hyperon-pair density matrix

The observable information in a hyperon-pair event is most transparently
organized at the density-matrix level. For two spin-
1/2
particles the
spin state can be expanded as

\rho_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=\frac{1}{4}\left[I\otimes I+P_{i}\sigma_{i}\otimes I+\bar{P}_{j}I\otimes\sigma_{j}+C_{ij}\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}\right],

(1)

where
I
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix in spin space,

\sigma_{i}
are Pauli matrices,
i,j=1,2,3
label Cartesian spin axes,

P_{i}
and
\bar{P}_{j}
are the single-spin polarizations, and

C_{ij}=\langle\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}\rangle
is the two-spin correlation
tensor. Repeated Cartesian indices are summed. The weak decays convert this
spin tensor into a joint angular distribution,

\displaystyle W(\hat{\bm{n}},\hat{\bar{\bm{n}}})=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\Big[

\displaystyle 1+\alpha_{\Lambda}P_{i}\hat{n}_{i}+\alpha_{\bar{\Lambda}}\bar{P}_{j}\hat{\bar{n}}_{j}

(2)

\displaystyle+\alpha_{\Lambda}\alpha_{\bar{\Lambda}}C_{ij}\hat{n}_{i}\hat{\bar{n}}_{j}\Big],

where
W
is normalized as

\int d\Omega\,d\bar{\Omega}\,W(\hat{\bm{n}},\hat{\bar{\bm{n}}})=1
, and

\hat{\bm{n}}
and
\hat{\bar{\bm{n}}}
are unit vectors along the proton and antiproton
momenta in the respective
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
rest frames. Unless stated
otherwise, numerical projections use the current BESIII/PDG analyzing-power
convention

\alpha_{\Lambda}=0.732\pm 0.014
and impose CP symmetry,

\alpha_{\bar{\Lambda}}=-\alpha_{\Lambda}

27
;
29
;
67
. The quoted uncertainty is smaller
than the spin-transfer and feed-down systematics explored below. Comparisons
with older measurements should nevertheless rescale the published spin
correlations to the same analyzing-power convention, since many early
hyperon-polarization analyses used smaller historical values of

\alpha_{\Lambda}
. For ideal angular acceptance the tensor follows directly
from the moments

C_{ij}=\frac{9}{\alpha_{\Lambda}\alpha_{\bar{\Lambda}}}\left\langle\hat{n}_{i}\hat{\bar{n}}_{j}\right\rangle.

(3)

Equation (
3
) is the basic tomography map. In a real detector
the same relation is implemented with a response matrix or an event-level
likelihood, because acceptance and reconstruction efficiencies distort the raw
moments but not the tensor decomposition itself. The brackets in
Eq. (
3
) denote the event or probability-distribution average
over the selected, acceptance-corrected hyperon-pair sample.

The remaining convention is the choice of axes. For short-range
fragmentation we define the basis event by event in the
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
pair
center-of-mass frame. The axis
\hat{e}_{3}
is chosen along the local pair
axis, taken here to be the
\Lambda
three-momentum; in the
e^{+}e^{-}
benchmark
the angle between this axis and the event thrust axis is denoted by

\theta_{T}
. The transverse axes may be fixed by the projected beam or
thrust direction. Once the azimuth about
\hat{e}_{3}
is averaged, the
observables used below are insensitive to the residual orientation of

\hat{e}_{1}
and
\hat{e}_{2}
. The daughter baryon directions are then
projected onto the transported triad in the two hyperon rest frames. More
explicitly, we first construct the orthonormal triad in the pair center-of-mass
frame and then transport it to each hyperon rest frame with the corresponding
rotation-free Lorentz boost. This fixes the helicity-frame convention used
for the decay angles; other choices, such as a Gottfried-Jackson or
beam-plane basis, are related by event-dependent rotations and should be
reported together with the measured tensor. With this convention the axially
averaged tensor becomes

C=\mathrm{diag}(C_{\perp},C_{\perp},C_{\parallel}),\qquad A_{C}\equiv C_{\perp}-C_{\parallel}.

(4)

The decay distribution contains two independent tensor structures,

C_{ij}\hat{n}_{i}\hat{\bar{n}}_{j}=C_{\perp}\sin\theta\sin\bar{\theta}\cos(\phi-\bar{\phi})+C_{\parallel}\cos\theta\cos\bar{\theta}.

(5)

Thus
C_{\perp}
is measured by an azimuthal decay-plane correlation, whereas

C_{\parallel}
is measured by a longitudinal polar-angle correlation. Their
difference
A_{C}
has a direct physical meaning in this basis: it tests
whether spin information transverse to the local fragmentation axis survives
differently from spin information along that axis.
Here
(\theta,\phi)
and
(\bar{\theta},\bar{\phi})
are the polar and
azimuthal angles of
\hat{\bm{n}}
and
\hat{\bar{\bm{n}}}
in the transported

(\hat{e}_{1},\hat{e}_{2},\hat{e}_{3})
triad.

The axial average is a projection, not a statement that the underlying event
or detector is exactly cylindrically symmetric. It is unbiased when the
physics distribution and the detector response are symmetric under rotations
around
\hat{e}_{3}
, or when residual azimuthal anisotropies are corrected by
an event-level response matrix. If the reaction plane, thrust reconstruction,
or tracking acceptance introduces a preferred transverse direction, the full

3\times 3
tensor and its covariance matrix should be extracted before
forming
C_{\perp}=(C_{11}+C_{22})/2
. The difference between the two
transverse eigenvalues then gives a direct systematic check of the axial
projection.

III
Relation to the STAR scalar

The STAR observable is obtained by projecting the two decay directions onto a
single relative angle,
\cos\theta^{\ast}=\hat{\bm{n}}\cdot\hat{\bar{\bm{n}}}
. The measured
distribution is

\frac{1}{N}\frac{dN}{d\cos\theta^{\ast}}=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\alpha_{\Lambda}\alpha_{\bar{\Lambda}}P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}\cos\theta^{\ast}\right].

(6)

Here
N
is the number of selected
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
pairs in the one-dimensional
projection. Integrating Eq. (
2
) at fixed

\hat{\bm{n}}\cdot\hat{\bar{\bm{n}}}
reduces the full tensor to its trace,

P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{Tr}C=\frac{2C_{\perp}+C_{\parallel}}{3}.

(7)

The published scalar therefore fixes one linear combination of

C_{\perp}
and
C_{\parallel}
, not the tensor itself.

Two limiting tensor completions make this loss of information explicit:

\displaystyle C_{\rm iso}

\displaystyle=P\,I,

(8)

\displaystyle C_{T_{0}}

\displaystyle=3P\,\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1).

(9)

Both reproduce the same
P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=P
. The first is an isotropic
same-sign tensor. The second is a triplet-
m=0
-like tensor conditioned on
the local pair axis. Their anisotropies are

A_{C}^{\rm iso}=0,\qquad A_{C}^{T_{0}}=6P.

(10)

The scalar trace cannot distinguish these states even though their angular
patterns and separability properties are different.

For vanishing single-spin polarizations, a diagonal
C
describes a
Bell-diagonal two-spin state. Positivity then requires the Bell eigenvalues

\lambda_{i}
, written in terms of the diagonal entries

(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
of
C
,

\lambda_{1}=\frac{1+c_{1}-c_{2}+c_{3}}{4},\quad\lambda_{2}=\frac{1-c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3}}{4},

\lambda_{3}=\frac{1+c_{1}+c_{2}-c_{3}}{4},\quad\lambda_{4}=\frac{1-c_{1}-c_{2}-c_{3}}{4}

to be non-negative. If sizable single-spin polarizations are present, the PPT
test must instead be applied to the full density matrix in
Eq. (
1
); the connected tensor
C_{ij}-P_{i}\bar{P}_{j}
remains a
useful correlation diagnostic but is not, by itself, a complete separability
test. The positive-partial-transpose criterion is necessary and sufficient
for two qubits. The quantity entering the Bell-diagonal test is
the nuclear norm
\|C\|_{\ast}
, the sum of the singular values of the
correlation tensor. It is invariant under local spin-basis rotations

C\to R_{1}CR_{2}^{T}
, with
R_{1,2}\in SO(3)
, which correspond to local
unitary transformations of the two spins. Thus the PPT witness used below
does not depend on how the two transverse axes are oriented, provided the same
local tensor is reconstructed. In the diagonal axial basis the PPT condition
becomes

\|C\|_{\ast}\leq 1,

(11)

where
\|C\|_{\ast}
is the sum of singular values. We define

{\cal E}_{\mathrm{PPT}}\equiv\|C\|_{\ast}-1.

(12)

For the published short-range STAR value
P=0.181
, the isotropic benchmark
gives
{\cal E}_{\mathrm{PPT}}=3P-1=-0.457
, whereas the
T_{0}
-like benchmark gives

{\cal E}_{\mathrm{PPT}}=9P-1=0.629
. The same scalar input can therefore be
completed into tensors with qualitatively different PPT behavior.

Figure 1:
Trace degeneracy in the axially symmetric tensor plane. The STAR
scalar
P=(2C_{\perp}+C_{\parallel})/3
fixes a line rather than a unique
density matrix. Motion along this line leaves the scalar unchanged while
changing
A_{C}
and the PPT witness. The isotropic and
T_{0}
-like
benchmarks are two tensor completions of the same scalar input.

The geometry in Fig.
1
illustrates why a tensor
measurement is needed. A fixed
P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}
is a line in the

(C_{\perp},C_{\parallel})
plane. Moving along that line leaves the STAR
scalar unchanged but changes the density matrix, the anisotropy, and the PPT
witness. Increasing the precision of the scalar measurement would narrow the
allowed band around the line, but it would still not locate the physical
tensor on that band. The missing coordinates are precisely the transverse
and longitudinal decay moments.

IV
Spin transfer and local
{}^{3}P_{0}
dynamics

The tensor reconstructed from hyperon decays is not the partonic tensor
itself. It is the result of spin transport through fragmentation, possible
spin production during confinement, and feed-down from heavier hyperons. For
a fragmenting quark of flavor
q
, the most general one-body spin map acts
affinely on the Bloch vector,

P_{a}^{\Lambda}=r_{a}^{q\to\Lambda}+S_{ai}^{q\to\Lambda}P_{i}^{q}.

(13)

Here
P_{i}^{q}
is the incoming quark polarization,
P_{a}^{\Lambda}
is the
outgoing hyperon polarization,
S_{ai}^{q\to\Lambda}
is the spin-transfer
matrix,
r_{a}^{q\to\Lambda}
denotes polarization generated from an unpolarized
quark, and
a,i
label Cartesian spin axes in the chosen local basis.
After subtracting single-spin products,
or equivalently for a selected sample with negligible single-spin
polarizations, the two-body part of the map gives

C_{ab}^{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=\frac{\sum_{q}w_{q}\left\langle S_{ai}^{q\to\Lambda}C_{ij}^{q\bar{q}}\bar{S}_{bj}^{\bar{q}\to\bar{\Lambda}}\right\rangle_{q}}{\sum_{q}w_{q}}+C_{ab}^{\rm conf}.

(14)

The weights
w_{q}
include hard flavor rates, fragmentation probabilities,
and event selection. The bracket
\langle\cdots\rangle_{q}
denotes a
conditional average over the accepted fragmentation phase space for flavor

q
, including the relevant
z
, transverse momentum, event-shape, and
azimuthal variables in the chosen bin. In Eq. (
14
),

C_{ij}^{q\bar{q}}
is the pre-fragmentation quark-antiquark spin tensor,

\bar{S}_{bj}^{\bar{q}\to\bar{\Lambda}}
is the antiquark-to-antihyperon transfer
matrix, and
C_{ab}^{\rm conf}
denotes spin correlations generated locally
by confinement, for example by string-breaking
s\bar{s}
production, that are
not represented by independent one-body spin transfer.

The simplest quantitative limit is leading-
\Lambda
production with strange
spin dominance. This is an effective phenomenological benchmark, not a claim
that all reconstructed
\Lambda
hyperons in
pp
collisions are prompt or
strange-quark dominated. Nonleading fragmentation, light-quark initiated

\Lambda
production, and decays from excited hyperons can dilute the tensor
and can make the effective transfer coefficients depend on kinematics and
event selection. With this limitation explicit, azimuthal averaging around
the pair axis gives the bin-averaged transfer matrix

S^{s\to\Lambda}=\mathrm{diag}(T_{T},T_{T},T_{L}),

(15)

and similarly for the antiquark. The tensor map becomes

C_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=\mathrm{diag}(T_{T}\bar{T}_{T}C_{xx}^{s\bar{s}},T_{T}\bar{T}_{T}C_{yy}^{s\bar{s}},T_{L}\bar{T}_{L}C_{zz}^{s\bar{s}})+C^{\rm conf}.

(16)

The bars on
\bar{T}_{T}
and
\bar{T}_{L}
denote the corresponding
antiquark-to-
\bar{\Lambda}
transfer coefficients; they are set equal to the
unbarred coefficients when CP-symmetric transfer is assumed. The

x,y,z
labels denote the
\hat{e}_{1},\hat{e}_{2},\hat{e}_{3}
axes introduced in
Sec.
II
.
This form is more informative than a scalar dilution. If

T_{T}\neq T_{L}
, fragmentation changes the tensor anisotropy even when the
trace is held fixed. The transfer coefficients are therefore effective
averages over the accepted phase space,

\displaystyle T_{a}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{a}}{{\cal N}},\qquad a=T,L,

\displaystyle N_{a}

\displaystyle=\int dz\,d^{2}p_{T}\,{\cal A}D_{1}^{q\to\Lambda}T_{a}^{q\to\Lambda},

\displaystyle{\cal N}

\displaystyle=\int dz\,d^{2}p_{T}\,{\cal A}D_{1}^{q\to\Lambda},

(17)

where
z
is the hyperon momentum fraction,
p_{T}
is its transverse
momentum relative to the fragmenting parton or jet axis,
{\cal A}
is the
acceptance and selection weight in the bin,
D_{1}^{q\to\Lambda}
is the
unpolarized fragmentation function, and
T_{a}^{q\to\Lambda}
is the
kinematic spin-transfer coefficient. The effective transfer coefficients are
therefore not universal constants of fragmentation. Additional nonstrange
fragmentation channels or higher-order corrections can be absorbed, at this
level, into flavor weights and into a broader response matrix. They would
increase the phenomenological uncertainty of
T_{T}
and
T_{L}
, but they do
not change the tensor observables that the weak-decay moments determine.

Existing data constrain the longitudinal component more directly than the
transverse one. Longitudinal
\Lambda
polarization at LEP and polarized
spin-transfer measurements in
pp
collisions support a moderate strange
spin-retention scale. We use the conservative working interval

0.30<T_{L}<0.70.

(18)

The transverse transfer coefficient is less constrained. The STAR
D_{TT}

measurement is consistent with zero within uncertainties, while the Belle and
phenomenological “polarizing-fragmentation” constraints primarily describe
spin generation from an unpolarized quark,
D_{1T}^{\perp}
, rather than the
polarized-quark transfer coefficient in Eq. (
13
).
Accordingly, the dependence on transverse transfer is parameterized by

r\equiv T_{L}^{2}/T_{T}^{2}.

(19)

The interval
0.5<r<1.5
is used below as a representative SU(6)-guided
band in which transverse and longitudinal spin retention are comparable. It
is not a direct experimental constraint. In an experimental analysis this
single effective band can be replaced by a
z
- and
p_{T}
-dependent
polarized-fragmentation fit.

{}^{3}P_{0}
tensor and, if large enough,
change either the magnitude or the sign of
C_{\parallel}
. The value of
Eq. (
26
) is therefore its falsifiability: observing the opposite
sign pattern would not contradict the tomography formalism, but would rule
out a dominant local-
{}^{3}P_{0}
interpretation of the short-range STAR
trace.

Eliminating
\lambda
in favor of the measured trace and defining

r=T_{L}^{2}/T_{T}^{2}
gives

\displaystyle C_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\frac{3P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}}{2-r},

\displaystyle C_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3rP_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}}{2-r},

(27)

\displaystyle A_{C}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(1+r)P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}}{2-r},

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{\mathrm{PPT}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(2+r)P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}}{2-r}-1.

(28)

The
r=1
point is exactly the benchmark

C=3P\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1)
.

and

are stable throughout the physical
region
r<2
, whereas the magnitude of
A_{C}
and the PPT witness grow as

falsifiable tensor prediction. A measured
C_{\parallel}\geq 0
, or
an
A_{C}
much smaller than the band allowed by reasonable
r
, would
disfavor this local-
{}^{3}P_{0}
interpretation even if the scalar trace were
reproduced.

V
STAR tensor projections

The published STAR result contains the scalar curve
P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}(\Delta R)
,
but not the event-level decay directions required to extract

C_{\perp}
and
C_{\parallel}
. The numerical analysis therefore has two
roles. It first propagates the published scalar uncertainties into
trace-degenerate tensor completions. It then converts the same input into
event-level targets for a future tensor measurement. The goal is not to
extract a tensor from the published trace alone, but to state precisely which
additional weak-decay moments would remove the trace degeneracy.

Figure 3:

The bin-by-bin projection in Fig.

is a consistency diagnostic
rather than a fit. The published
P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}
values are

Their identical scalar projection but different
C_{\perp}
,

,

A_{C}
, and
{\cal E}_{\mathrm{PPT}}
identify the missing experimental
discriminator. A STAR or LHC pair analysis gains precisely this information
by retaining the decay-plane and polar-angle moments instead of reducing each
event to
\cos\theta^{\ast}
.

For each accepted event define

g(\hat{\bm{n}},\hat{\bar{\bm{n}}})=\frac{\hat{n}_{x}\hat{\bar{n}}_{x}+\hat{n}_{y}\hat{\bar{n}}_{y}}{2}-\hat{n}_{z}\hat{\bar{n}}_{z}.

(29)

The
x,y,z
components again refer to the transported

(\hat{e}_{1},\hat{e}_{2},\hat{e}_{3})
axes.
The sample estimator for the anisotropy is then

\widehat{A}_{C}=\frac{9}{\alpha_{\Lambda}\alpha_{\bar{\Lambda}}}\,\langle g\rangle_{N},

(30)

where
\langle\cdots\rangle_{N}
denotes the average over selected and
response-corrected events.
For ideal angular acceptance and diagonal benchmark tensors,

\sigma(A_{C})\simeq\frac{3\sqrt{3/2}}{|\alpha_{\Lambda}\alpha_{\bar{\Lambda}}|\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}}.

(31)

Here
N_{\rm eff}
is the effective number of selected signal pairs after
background dilution, feed-down dilution, and acceptance losses have been
folded into the moment variance.
Using the STAR short-range value as the signal scale, an ideal sample requires

N_{5\sigma}\simeq 1.0\times 10^{3}
selected pairs to distinguish

C_{\rm iso}
from
C_{T_{0}}
. A STAR-like scenario with 15% unpolarized
background and moderate acceptance loss gives

N_{5\sigma}\simeq 2.8\times 10^{3}
, while a conservative scenario with
stronger dilution requires
\sim 5.3\times 10^{4}
pairs.

The statistical reach follows the same hierarchy. Because the separation
between tensor hypotheses scales with
P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}
, the first
short-range bin dominates the sensitivity in Fig.
4
.
Larger
\Delta R
bins rapidly lose discrimination as the scalar correlation
approaches zero. They remain valuable, but mainly as control regions for
detector, combinatorial, and collective backgrounds.

The analytic sensitivity formula has been checked with event-level
pseudo-experiment ensembles that generate weak two-body decays, apply
nonuniform angular acceptance, add unpolarized background, and calibrate the
moment response with an unpolarized sample. These closure tests recover the

1/\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}
scaling in Eq. (
31
) after response
correction and confirm that acceptance biases are a standard calibration
problem. They are, however, validation studies rather than independent
physics results. The decisive experimental information remains the measured
tensor
C_{ij}
, its covariance matrix, and the response model used to map
raw decay-angle moments onto physics-level tensor components.

VI
Feed-down, backgrounds, and physical-region checks

Observed
\Lambda
hyperons are not all prompt. A substantial fraction can
come from heavier hyperons, and their decays transmit spin with channel
dependent coefficients. Feed-down must therefore be treated as a tensor
response,

C_{ij}^{\rm obs}=\sum_{\mu\nu}f_{\mu\nu}D^{\mu}_{ik}\bar{D}^{\nu}_{jl}C_{kl}^{\mu\nu}+C_{ij}^{\rm bkg},

(32)

where
\mu,\nu
label the prompt or parent categories of the reconstructed

\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
,
f_{\mu\nu}
is the corresponding pair fraction,

D_{ik}^{\mu}
and
\bar{D}_{jl}^{\nu}
are daughter-spin response
matrices,
C_{kl}^{\mu\nu}
is the parent or prompt tensor before daughter
decay, and
C_{ij}^{\rm bkg}
is the residual background tensor
contribution. A scalar dilution is recovered only in the special case where
every decay-transfer matrix is proportional to the identity and every
category carries the same tensor pattern. Generically, feed-down changes the
trace, anisotropy, and PPT witness by different amounts.

The inputs required for Eq. (
32
) are experimental: the prompt
fraction, parent yields, branching fractions, and spin-transfer matrices for
decays such as
\Sigma^{0}\to\Lambda\gamma
and
\Xi\to\Lambda\pi
. The
radiative
\Sigma^{0}
channel, for instance, carries a spin-transfer factor
of order
-1/3
in the usual isotropic retention approximation. That
number should not be used as a universal tensor correction if the parent

\Sigma^{0}
sample is itself polarized, aligned, or selected in an
anisotropic phase-space region. In the general case the decay response is a
matrix
D_{ik}^{\mu}
acting on the parent spin tensor, and off-diagonal or
unequal transverse components can be retained in Eq. (
32
). A
large uncorrected parent fraction can therefore dilute and reshape the
primary tensor at the same time. Because the published STAR scalar result
does not provide an event-level parent composition, the present study scans
daughter-level transfer factors
d_{\perp},d_{\parallel}
. An experimental
analysis can replace this scan with measured prompt and parent fractions for
the selected pair sample.

single number once tensor observables are used. Transverse and longitudinal
transfer factors act on different components, so the same primary tensor can
move in different directions in the
(C_{\perp},C_{\parallel})
plane depending
on the parent mixture. In particular, feed-down can move an observed tensor
across the
{\cal E}_{\mathrm{PPT}}=0
boundary without multiplying the trace by a
common factor. A publication-level tensor measurement should therefore quote
either prompt-corrected tensor components or the response matrix that maps
prompt tensors into observed tensors.

The remaining experimental effects enter with different levels of severity.
Unpolarized background mainly dilutes the moments. Feed-down and
axis/acceptance smearing are more dangerous because they can change the tensor
shape itself. A scalar estimate gives
N_{5\sigma}\simeq 1.0\times 10^{3}

selected pairs in the ideal case,
2.8\times 10^{3}
for a STAR-like
background and acceptance scenario, and
\sim 5.3\times 10^{4}
for a
conservative diluted scenario. These numbers should be read as design scales,
not as replacements for an experimental likelihood. The experimental
checklist is correspondingly concrete: constrain the background fraction,
build a tensor-level feed-down response, and validate the angular response
with unpolarized or mixed-event controls before interpreting

{\cal E}_{\mathrm{PPT}}
.

VII
Clean
e^{+}e^{-}
benchmark and relation to top-quark
D

The top-quark measurements provide a useful reference point for interpreting
trace observables. In the top threshold region the
D
variable used by
ATLAS and CMS is a trace-type projection of
C_{ij}^{t\bar{t}}
in a basis
where the Standard Model predicts a nearly singlet-like tensor. With the
convention used in those analyses,
D<-1/3
is a sufficient entanglement
marker

19
;
15
;
40
. The STAR scalar
P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=\mathrm{Tr}C/3
is also a
trace projection, but it is positive and below the isotropic threshold

1/3
. It therefore cannot witness entanglement by itself. Hyperons
require tensor anisotropy, not merely another trace measurement.

The same tensor program becomes especially controlled when the pre-fragmentation
spin state is known. This makes
e^{+}e^{-}
fragmentation a natural
calibration channel for the STAR-motivated observables. CMS measurements of
local
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
polarization in
p{\rm Pb}
collisions

41
constrain the single-spin vectors

P_{i}
and
\bar{P}_{i}
. A pair analysis would extend the same weak-decay
likelihood to
C_{ij}
, separating local single-spin polarization,
collective backgrounds, and genuine pair-spin correlations. In
hemisphere-tagged
e^{+}e^{-}\to q\bar{q}\to\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}X
, the hard spin tensor
is calculable before fragmentation. At leading order for photon exchange,

\displaystyle C_{q\bar{q}}^{e^{+}e^{-}}(\theta_{T})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}\!\left(\kappa(\theta_{T}),\kappa(\theta_{T}),-1\right),

\displaystyle\kappa(\theta_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{T}}{1+\cos^{2}\theta_{T}}.

(33)

After diagonal spin transfer this gives

C_{\perp}^{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=DT_{T}^{2}\kappa(\theta_{T}),\qquad C_{\parallel}^{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=-DT_{L}^{2},

(34)

where
D
denotes prompt fraction, hemisphere purity, and residual dilution.
Thus the hard process predicts a characteristic central-thrust enhancement of

A_{C}
, which is more differential than an integrated Bell or PPT witness.

The angle
\theta_{T}
is experimentally accessible in the usual event-shape
sense. In a Belle II analysis one would reconstruct the thrust axis from the
full hadronic final state after removing the
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
decay
daughters, require the two hyperons to populate opposite hemispheres, and form

\theta_{T}
from the angle between the
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
pair axis in the pair-CM
frame and the thrust axis. Thrust-axis resolution, hemisphere mistags, and
non-two-jet backgrounds are represented here by the dilution factor
D
;
experimentally they can be constrained or unfolded with event-shape
response matrices.

The most robust observable consequence is the anisotropy peak around the
central thrust region. At
\theta_{T}\simeq 90^{\circ}
, the prediction is

A_{C}=0.33^{+0.22}_{-0.16}
for the central 68% interval, with a 5–95%
range
0.13<A_{C}<0.70
. The PPT witness is positive only in the upper part
of the band, so the robust near-term target is the anisotropy
A_{C}
, not an
unconditional entanglement claim. The same calculation gives a median ideal
selected-pair requirement
N_{5\sigma}\simeq 1.1\times 10^{4}
, with a 5–95%
interval of roughly
2.4\times 10^{3}
–
7.3\times 10^{4}
pairs for a

5\sigma

A_{C}
observation.

The required integrated luminosity depends on the inclusive rate for producing
reconstructible opposite-hemisphere
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
pairs. A useful
bookkeeping relation is

N_{\rm sel}={\cal L}\,\sigma_{q\bar{q}}\,R_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}^{\rm hemi}\,{\cal B}(\Lambda\to p\pi^{-})^{2}\,\epsilon_{\rm reco}\,\epsilon_{\rm sel},

(35)

where
{\cal L}
is the integrated luminosity,
\sigma_{q\bar{q}}
is the
light-quark continuum cross section,
R_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}^{\rm hemi}
is the pair
rate per hadronic event after hemisphere and kinematic requirements,

{\cal B}(\Lambda\to p\pi^{-})
is the weak-decay branching fraction, and the
efficiencies include tracking, vertexing, particle identification, and
event-shape selections. Equation
(
35
) is deliberately written in terms of selected pairs
because
R_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}^{\rm hemi}
is analysis dependent. For illustration,
at
{\cal L}=50~{\rm ab}^{-1}
and
\sigma_{q\bar{q}}\simeq 3.5~{\rm nb}
,
one has
1.75\times 10^{11}
light-quark continuum events. An effective
selected-pair probability of
10^{-6}
already yields

{\cal O}(10^{5})
pairs, comfortably above the median requirement, whereas a
probability near
10^{-7}
remains near the edge of the broad
spin-transfer band. A Belle II feasibility study should therefore determine

R_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}^{\rm hemi}\epsilon_{\rm reco}\epsilon_{\rm sel}
directly
from data or tuned simulation.

This benchmark also clarifies the relation to the Bell-witness program of
Gong, Parida, Tu, and Venugopalan. That work established the general
PPT/Bell framework for hyperon spin correlations. The present analysis
addresses the measurement layer needed to use that framework with collider
data: the trace degeneracy of scalar observables, the directly measurable
anisotropy
A_{C}
, the hard-process angular dependence in
e^{+}e^{-}
, a
literature-motivated spin-transfer band, and moment-level closure tests. The
result is a concrete measurement target,

A_{C}(\theta_{T})\quad\hbox{with}\quad C_{\perp}>0,\;C_{\parallel}<0,

rather than only the general statement that hyperon pairs can test
entanglement.

VIII
Conclusions

We have developed a hyperon-pair spin-tomography framework that turns scalar
spin correlations into a well-defined tensor measurement problem. The STAR
short-range
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
correlation fixes the trace of the two-spin tensor,
but not the density matrix. The missing information is carried by the
weak-decay moments that determine
C_{\perp}
,
C_{\parallel}
, and

A_{C}=C_{\perp}-C_{\parallel}
.

The central conclusion is that tensor structure is essential. An
isotropic same-sign tensor and a local-
{}^{3}P_{0}
-motivated tensor can
reproduce the same STAR scalar while giving different PPT behavior. The local

{}^{3}P_{0}
benchmark predicts
C_{\perp}>0
,
C_{\parallel}<0
, and

A_{C}>0
in the local pair-axis basis. This is a falsifiable dynamical
hypothesis for STAR, CMS, and Belle II, not a claim that current scalar data
already establish entanglement.

The experimental path is correspondingly well defined. At RHIC and the LHC,
short-range
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
pairs can be analyzed in a local pair-axis basis and
reported as tensor components with covariance matrices and feed-down and
acceptance response. At Belle II, the calculable hard tensor in

e^{+}e^{-}\to q\bar{q}
provides a controlled calibration benchmark: the predicted

A_{C}(\theta_{T})
band is positive, centrally enhanced, and measurable with
selected-pair samples of order
10^{4}
for nominal spin-transfer parameters.
Together, these measurements would determine whether spin correlations
observed after confinement are isotropic, tensor-anisotropic but separable, or
compatible with a PPT-violating two-hyperon density matrix.
