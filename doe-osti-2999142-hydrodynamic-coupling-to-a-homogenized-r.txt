ABSTRACT: (250-word limit)
Resolving radiation transport fields subject to opacity profiles with strong, oscillatory line
structure while potentially falling under intermediate optical depth conditions presents a
numerical challenge in radiation transport modeling. The Young measure-based homogenization
technique formulated by Haut et al. (2017) was investigated as a candidate method for resolving
radiation fields under such conditions more accurately. The method was compared against
frequently-utilized mean opacity methods as the Rosseland and Planck formulations. In this
work, all methods were tested through radiation slab calculations separately comprised of
aluminum, copper, and krypton, each for different thermodynamic conditions. Following these
offline radiation slab calculations, demonstrations shifted towards the SCEPTRE radiation
transport code and, subsequently, the multiphysics ALEGRA code for approximately-coupled
radiation-material simulations. Throughout all the simulations shown in this study, for a fixed
computational cost, the homogenized method was observed to be more accurate than any of the
solutions determined through traditional mean opacity approaches.

INTRODUCTION AND EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF RESULTS:
A shortened introduction and executive summary will be provided here, given that a
comprehensive entry for this section is found in the journal draft submission located in the
addendum.

Many multiphysics simulations rely on radiation transport calculations to accurately resolve the
transport of radiation fields within a given system. These systems can range from hypersonic re-
entry phenomenon to nuclear fusion experiments to energetic astrophysical events taking place
far from our solar system (Jo, et al., 2023; Eitner, et al., 2024; Brown, et al., 2009). In any of
these systems, the radiation field can carry a significantly large fraction of the total energy and
influence the overall dynamics of the multiphysics system. These radiation transport calculations
rely on opacity profiles to describe the extent to which photons stream and eventually become
absorbed by particles within the system. However, accounting for the complete set of physics in
a simulation can become very expensive due to the large parameter space being investigated. The
opacity profile is one detailed aspect of the radiation transport component that can lead to
expensive multiphysics simulations, so it is often simplified to accelerate these calculations. As a

result, plenty of historical and state-of-the-art simulations have relied on averaged opacities to
produce results in reasonable computational times.

Examples of averaged-opacity descriptions include the Rosseland and Planck mean opacity
formulations (Castor, 2004). Several similarly-derived mean opacities tend to occupy a specific
regime of optical depths, leading to erroneous calculations when incorrect optical depths are
assumed (Pomraning, 1971; Clouet, 2000). The Rosseland and Planck mean opacities, in
particular, address the optically-thick and optically-thin limits only, and are questionable beyond
these limits. In this work, Haut et al.’s Young measure-based homogenization algorithm was
introduced to accommodate the domain of intermediate optical depths while tackling strong line
structures that can occupy the opacity profile. By assessing this methodology’s accuracy through
multiple stages of test cases, the algorithm’s viability as an alternative opacity treatment method
was showcased in this study.

Using the PrOpacEOS opacity data files for multiple elements at several thermodynamic
conditions, the advantages of the homogenization approach were conveyed through the multiple
demonstration stages in this work (MacFarlane, et al., 2013). This research study began with
multiple radiation slab calculations performed offline to confirm the method’s accuracy gain
over conventional mean opacity methods. Following the analyses of these radiation slab
calculations, similar simulations employing the same configuration were conducted with
SCEPTRE to demonstrate the algorithm’s sustained performance gains under a production-level
transport code. This work then concluded through the first homogenized radiation-material
simulations using SCEPTRE-ALEGRA, where the improvements of homogenization were
discerned through a coupled multiphysics simulation.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT AND
METHODOLOGY:
Descriptions of the homogenization algorithm’s implementation, mean opacity formulations, and
research approach are provided in the journal submission draft provided in the addendum.
Readers seeking rigorous, theoretical underpinnings of the homogenization methodology can
find these discussions in the Haut et al. publication (Haut, et al., 2017).

An important objective of this study is to demonstrate the Young measure-based homogenization
algorithm’s benefits over the Rosseland and Planck mean opacity formulations. The 1D radiation
slab calculations provide a simple, yet informative way of exhibiting this novel algorithm’s
advantages over conventional mean opacity approaches. Since the process of demonstrating
these advantages through multiple frameworks is paramount, i.e. from offline calculations to
production-level capabilities to a coupled, multiphysics simulation, it is important to relay these
advantages succinctly through the multiple stages of test cases performed in this study; the 1D

radiation slab configuration is a convenient platform from which to relay those results at each
stage.

The 1D radiation slab solution was determined by integrating the steady form of the radiation
transport equation, under the assumptions that the temperature and density along the slab are
constant. For a constant temperature and density, the opacity profile becomes fixed, allowing one
to focus on the differences created by a change in opacity treatment. The intensity, or flux,
calculations then take place under these prescriptions. Along each stage or set of simulations,
solutions for the Rosseland and Planck mean opacities are always included for direct
comparisons against the homogenization approach. This ensures that the advantages of the
homogenization approach are consistently provided along this research project’s progression.

The first set of results shown in this study are the offline radiation slab calculations. The initial
slab calculations assumed Planck radiation law emissions that propagate towards the front and
rear of the slab, leading to symmetric flux profiles along the width of the slab. Under the
SCEPTRE framework, asymmetric sources as functions of location were used to construct finite
element solutions of the calculated flux (Pautz, et al., 2009). The sources employed in the
SCEPTRE calculations, however, are very similar to the initial, offline slab calculations in that
only one direction of the offline source profile was used. The performance of the homogenized
method may then be observed for an asymmetric source across the width of the slab. Finally, the
radiation flux profiles generated from SCEPTRE were used as energy deposition inputs for
ALEGRA’s 1D radiation-material simulations (Niederhaus, et al., 2023). Energy deposition into
the slab was set up to occur over nanosecond timescales, after which the solid dynamics of the
slab was monitored for differences resulting from the different opacity treatment methods.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION:
Detailed results and analyses of the simulations performed in this study are provided in the
journal submission draft and presentation, both found in the addendum of this report.

The first set of results comprise multiple, offline radiation slab calculations. Comparisons
between the mean opacity formulations and the Young measure-based homogenization method
were conducted for various elements under different thermodynamic conditions. The elements
represented in this work include aluminum, copper, and krypton, all of which may be found in
experiments held at Sandia National Laboratories (Hall, et al., 2001; Moore, et al., 2021;
Mattsson, et al., 2014; Jennings, et al., 2015). By applying these methods over multiple elements
and thermodynamic conditions, a rigorous approach was taken to demonstrate the accuracy gains
of homogenization over conventional mean opacity methods for a variety of conditions.

Each set of radiation slab calculations in the addendum’s manuscript draft includes a line-by-line
solution, where the radiation transport solver was applied over every data point available in the
opacity profile, the Rosseland and Planck mean opacity solutions, and the homogenization
approach calculated for multiple enumerations of opacity bands. The results show several
noteworthy features in the different opacity treatments’ slab results. In the radiation slab
calculations for a single energy group, the homogenization method was generally observed to
produce more accurate solutions in comparison to the mean opacity formulations. Additionally,
the homogenization-based solution was shown to improve for a greater number of opacity band
divisions. These results provide an initial glimpse of the homogenization benefits over
conventional methods.

Following these results, another set of radiation slab calculations was conducted, albeit under the
SCEPTRE radiation transport framework. This stage of calculations provided an opportunity to
ensure that the homogenization method retained its advantages over the Rosseland and Planck
mean opacity solutions. However, an additional step was taken in these calculations to equate the
number of opacity bands used in the homogenization approach to the number of opacity
subgroups in the mean opacity methods. This allows the reader to fairly compare the different
methods without concern for differences in the number of opacity bands opposite to the number
of photon energy subgroups. Despite the increase in number of photon energy subgroups, the
homogenization approach persistently yielded the lowest relative error among the different
methods.

Lastly, radiation-material simulations were generated and compared under the SCEPTRE-
ALEGRA framework. Using a slightly higher density condition for the opacity profile, multiple
radiation flux profiles were generated under differently assumed opacity treatment methods. The
fluxes were then converted to energy deposition profiles to instantiate the slab responses and
ensuing dynamics. In this section, the effect of different opacity methods was sufficiently
apparent that different pressure profiles and rear surface velocities were realized during the
slab’s expansion and contraction cycles. Through these approximately-coupled simulations, the
improvement in radiation-material dynamics was discerned for a homogenized radiation
transport algorithm over average opacity-based transport calculations.

ANTICIPATED OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS:
The results of this study were compiled into a journal draft submission titled “Homogenization of
Radiation Transport Calculations for Multiphysics Simulations”. This journal draft has been
reviewed by the team members and will be submitted for potential publication with the Journal
of Computational Physics. With this work burgeoning as its own opacity treatment method,
unique with respect to the mean opacity methods that have been employed for multiple decades,
there are a wide range of opportunities for exploration and development relative to the Young

measure-based homogenization algorithm. Discussion of the opportunities described below can
be also found in the addendum where the journal draft is located.

While the results consistently show several performance advantages, there are multiple research
opportunities that explore various facets of the Young measure-based homogenization approach.
In this work, the results were confined to a single elemental material. However, actual systems
involve mixtures, even for experiments that are intended to assess a single material element,
possibly due to minute contamination. The test cases in this report also assumed constant
temperature and density, whereas actual experiments and observations undergo variations in
thermodynamic conditions. Therefore, a proper homogenized radiation transport capability
requires a thorough implementation of the method to enable fully-coupled radiative simulations.
Additionally, the opacity band and photon energy group structures were selected in this work to
ensure that comparisons with the mean opacity formulations were equivalent from the
subdivisions of the opacity profile. Full realization of the method’s advantages will require
optimization of opacity bands and photon energy groups relative to the homogenization method.

Beyond the details of the homogenization algorithm, there are numerous application
opportunities where the homogenized radiation transport method can be exercised. The radiation-
material simulation performed with SCEPTRE-ALEGRA in this work used an approximately-
coupled radiation physics calculation. However, plenty of high-energy-density systems have
strongly-coupled radiation physics, so a thorough implementation of the algorithm should be
pursued for future applications. In the arena of nuclear detonation (NUDET) modeling where
radiation-hydrodynamic simulations are relevant, the Air Force Technical Applications Center
(AFTAC) and the Defense Threat Reduction Agency (DTRA) have interests in more accurately-
resolved optical signals as a result of incorporating the homogenization routine. Funding is
currently pending to reach Sandia National Laboratories to continue exploring and developing
homogenization capabilities for such codes.

As mentioned, other applications where a homogenized radiation transport algorithm will have
an impact can include radiation-material experiments such as ablation modeling where the
subsequent solid dynamics can be studied. There are numerous experiments performed internally
where a range of radiation-material experiments under various conditions are explored
(Niederhaus, et al., 2023; Lemke, et al., 2009; Moore, et al., 2022; Moore, et al., 2025). The
domain of spectroscopic modeling through the use of collisional-radiative models is also
significantly influenced by radiation transport simulations (Chung, et al., 2005; Scott & Hansen,
2010; Ralchenko, 2016; Abrantes, et al., 2018; Schaeuble, et al., 2025). Due to the proximity of
both physics fields, a natural progression is to explore the influence of the homogenized
radiation transport module on atomic/molecular state distribution functions and the resultant
opacity and emissivity profiles.

CONCLUSION: (400 word limit)
The conclusion stated herein was partially extracted from the journal submission draft, which is
found in the addendum.

The treatment of opacity profiles is a challenging aspect of solving the radiation transport
equation. In the context of deterministic radiation transport equations, the Planck and Rosseland
mean opacity formulations were revisited as commonly-used opacity averaging methods in the
multigroup setting. However, these two formulations are valid primarily in the optically-thin and
optically-thick limits, respectively. The Young measure-based homogenization approach by Haut
et al. was explored in this study to address intermediate optical depths that may also have strong
line structures in the opacity profile (Haut, et al., 2017).

To prove the method's viability under these conditions, the opacity treatments above, the
Rosseland mean opacity, Planck mean opacity, and homogenization approaches, were
investigated for a variety of atomic elements, temperatures, and densities. The element types
included aluminum, copper, and krypton, all of which are used in Sandia's high-energy-density
(HED) or pulsed power facility experiments. The radiation slab calculations showed that the
homogenization approach outperformed the conventional mean opacity methods when compared
to equivalent line-by-line calculations along the opacity profile. Increases in opacity band
resolution for similar respective groups showed further improvements in accuracy as well.

Successful demonstrations of homogenization through offline radiation slab calculations led to
its application in the SCEPTRE and ALEGRA frameworks. The radiation slab configuration was
first employed in the SCEPTRE radiation transport code, which uses a finite element
discretization of the domain. Improvements were replicated in the SCEPTRE framework as the
opacity treatments mentioned above were deployed and compared in this setting. This then led to
the radiation-material simulations with the ALEGRA code. Approximate radiation-material
calculations were performed using each of the opacity treatment's radiation fluxes as energy
deposition profiles to monitor the slab dynamics. The accuracy of the homogenization method
was realized in this coupled simulation through visualizations of velocity and pressure profiles.

Altogether, these results show promise for improving radiation transport calculations in coupled
radiation physics simulations. In the preceding section detailing future endeavors for this work,
multiple avenues of research remain that include optimizing the homogenization algorithm,
implementing the method fully into production-level codes, as well as deploying the method for
a wide range of applications. With plenty of positive results gleaned from this study, the team
plans to pursue a multitude of research tasks and applications to enable the method’s capability
across the lab and its partners.

1. Introduction

 Radiation transport is present in many energetic physical systems, such

as astrophysics (Hui-Bon-Hoa, A., 2021; Nagayama et al., 2017; Eitner et al.,

2024), nuclear fusion (Brown et al., 2009; Jones et al., 2008, 2014; Ampleford

et al., 2015), and general planetary reentry including hypersonics (Jo et al.;

Le Maout et al., 2025; Park, 2013). The coupled, multi-physics nature of

these systems means that an accurate representation of the radiation eld

is paramount to ensure that the proper dynamics are resolved throughout

the course of a simulation. However, the parametric space of a generalized

radiation eld can be highly complex and expansive, thereby requiring simpli-

cations in multiple respects before one can even begin to solve the radiation

transport equation (Mihalas and Weibel-Mihalas, 1999). One feature of the

radiation transport equation that may be simplied to reduce the overall

computational cost is the opacity description.

 The opacity prole is an important component of a radiation transport

solve as the opacity dictates the extent to which photons are either absorbed

or transmitted in a given medium. Ideally, the transport equation would

be solved over every single opacity data point available along a prole to

obtain the most accurate radiation eld representation possible. But this

quickly becomes intractable, especially for coupled multi-physics equations.

Therefore, in the context of deterministic radiation transport algorithms,

one should seek to strike a balance between providing a suciently-resolved

 2

radiation ux eld and delivering the aforementioned solution in reasonable

time. The user then has the discretion to prescribe the appropriate averaged

opacity formulation suitable for solving the radiation transport equation for

given opacity data.

 While multiple mean opacity formulations have been deployed throughout

the past decades to reduce the computational expense of solving the radiation

transport equation, there are certain limitations associated with using these

formulations. For instance, if one uses the well-known Planck and Rosse-

land mean opacity formulations, these descriptions are intended to be used

for optically-thin and optically-thick conditions, respectively (Castor, 2004).

Towards intermediate optical depths, i.e. between the optically-thin and

optically-thick limits, utility of the Rosseland and Planck mean opacity for-

mulations becomes increasingly questionable and possibly invalid. Alterna-

tive formulations have been devised throughout the eld's history to accom-

modate a wider range of optical depths, such as Pomraning's expansion-based

set of equations or Clouet's phase-space construction of averaged opacities

involving Fourier and Laplace transforms (Pomraning, 1971; Clouet, 2000).

But, these numerical descriptions either require specic qualitative features

in their opacity proles, e.g. smooth, or have a restrictive range of applica-

bility in optical depths.

 Multiple studies across dierent elds have employed these average opac-

ity expressions in the radiation transport evaluations of their multi-physics

simulations. Hypersonics is one such domain where the dynamics of atmo-

spheric re-entry requires coupled physics modules to resolve the complex

shock structure ahead of a re-entering object or vehicle (Jo et al.; Le Maout

 3

et al., 2025). In this setting, the air is heated and compressed from up-

per atmospheric conditions as it passes through the vehicular shock front;

this implicates a transition across optical depths where photon transport can

be aected. At high-energy-density (HED) conditions where high tempera-

tures and pressures are prevalent, astrophysical systems and fusion experi-

ments can still encounter intermediate optical depth conditions. Examples

of widely-used state-of-the-art codes that are currently employed for these

applications are given through the following references: MacFarlane et al.

(2006); Jennings et al. (2015); Eitner et al. (2024). Suciently strong lines

in the opacity prole that are not drowned by high-density eects, for exam-

ple, may be poorly captured by conventional mean opacity methods like the

Rosseland and Planck formulations.

 In this study, the Young measure-based homogenization approach is in-

troduced as a means of more accurately resolving the radiation ux at inter-

mediate optical depth conditions where conventional mean opacity formula-

tions can be quickly invalidated (Haut et al., 2017). This method also has

its advantages on opacity group proles where there can be an abundance of

opacity lines. Another advantage of this homogenization approach is that its

implementation can be developed from a pre-existing multigroup radiation

transport algorithm. These aspects and benets of the Young measure-based

homogenization algorithm were explored in this work, and exercises were

chosen to demonstrate its advantages in the radiation transport context over

traditional opacity averaging approaches.

2. Methodology

 To introduce the various opacity treatments used in this study, an overview

 The Young measure-based homogenization approach by Haut et al. (2017)

extends from the multigroup formalism presented above. The homogeniza-

tion methodology in this work will specically refer to the formulation by

 7

Haut et al. (2017), even though its concept has been known since its initial

construction by Tartar (1990) with subsequent extensions by others, such

as Bernard et al. (2010) and Mathiaud and Salvarani (2013). In the multi-

group formalism, the energy axis is discretized into groups or bins. The

homogenization approach uses the multiband setting by building from the

multigroup formalism and further discretizing the opacity axis into opacity

 1
bands. The multiband formalism has been in the radiation transport reper-

toire for many decades; however, the system's closure has, at times, required

elaborate numerical procedures that increase computation times (Cullen and

Pomraning, 1980; Ripoll and Wray, 2008). The method by Haut et al. (2017),

however, allows one to easily keep the system closed through opacity-based

tabulations, which will be discussed shortly. A sample schematic of the type

of divisions constructed along both the energy and opacity axes for a ctional

opacity prole is provided in Figure 1.

One should note that the schematic shown is not simply a checkered grid,

despite the discretizations along both axes. Several aspects of the opac-

ity grid were ne-tuned to ensure that the calculations contributing to the

eventual homogenized solution are accurate as possible. First, each photon

energy group's opacity bands are limited to the minimum and maximum

opacity values in the respective group. This avoids wasteful computations

since calculations performed outside of these limits have no contribution to

the homogenized solution. Additionally, discretization along the opacity axis

may warrant a logarithmic division of opacity bands, rather than linear; this

 This discretization modies the multigroup radiation transport equation

into an expression that accounts for the opacity bands prescribed in each

photon energy group. This equation is shown in Equation 5.

      Ω · ∇ Ii,j + σi,j (Ei , T (x)) Ii,j = σi,j (Ei , T (x)) · Si (T (x) , Ei )   (5)

In the equation, an index, j , has been added as a subscript to the intensity

and absorption/opacity variables. This indicates that an intensity value will

be calculated for each opacity band in each energy group. The opacity values

representing each band is simply the centered value of each opacity band in

this work. The reader will note that the source function is discretized only

in energy; since the source contribution to the equation is strictly dependent

on energy and not the opacity discretization, there is no addition opacity

subscript on the source function. Likewise, the energy parameter itself is

completely independent of the opacity so it is neither subscripted by the

opacity discretization.

 At this point, one expects the computational time under homogenization

to increase due to the computation duration's linear correlation to the num-

ber of opacity bands assigned for each group. This will certainly increase

relative to the use of a mean opacity description for each group. However,

it will be shown in Subsections 3.2 and 3.3 that the accuracy achieved with

the homogenization method still outperforms the mean opacity methods for

an equal number of subdivisions in a photon energy group. To describe this

alternatively, the number of opacity bands within a group for the homoge-

nization method will be equal to the number of further-divided subgroups if

 10

the mean opacity methods are utilized for the same group, e.g. in a photon

energy group, 4 opacity bands for homogenization would be used versus using

the Rosseland mean opacity for each subgroup created by subdividing the

same photon energy group into 4 subgroups. The benets of homogenization

under an equivalent number of opacity subdivisions will be consistently shown

in the aforementioned subsections. Additionally, the photon energy groups

are not necessarily optimized for the homogenization approach discussed in

this work for the purpose of comparing the opacity treatment methods more

fairly (Till et al., 2014, 2022). Therefore, one could expect an overall drop in

compute times if optimal homogenization groups are accommodatingly wider

than the photon group widths chosen in this study.

 In the multigroup context, an intensity or ux value is produced from

each energy group's radiation transport calculation. As mentioned, multi-

ple band intensities are generated in each energy group under the multiband

scenario; therefore, these values must be weighed and summed to yield the

similar group-wise intensities obtained in the multigroup case. In the ho-

mogenization approach by Haut et al. (2017), Young measures are used to

weigh the individual intensity contributions, which simplies the weighted

summation and keeps the weights self-contained to the opacity data. This

contrasts with the aforementioned multiband methods that rely on complex

closure techniques to construct homogenized intensity solutions.

3.2. Radiation Transport with SCEPTRE

 Following the analyses of the radiation slab calculations, continued demon-

strations of the homogenization algorithm's performance took place through

a production-level transport code: SCEPTRE (Pautz et al., 2009). Since

part of the objective of this study is to assess the impact of a homogenized

radiation transport algorithm on multi-physics simulations, SCEPTRE's pre-

existing connection to other physics codes provides a convenient platform

from which to continue this endeavor. SCEPTRE, which stands for Sandia's

Computational Engine for Particle Transport for Radiation Eects, is a suite

of deterministic codes purposed to solve the linear, steady-state Boltzmann

transport equation (Lathrop and Carlson, 1966). The platform's general-

 30

izability allows the user to pursue particle transport problems, in addition

to radiation transport problems. Since this work is focused on solving the

radiation transport problem eciently, the discussion will be focused on the

radiation-relevant capabilities in SCEPTRE.

 SCEPTRE solves the transport equation with several implementation

ideologies in consideration. First, the Boltzmann transport equation can be

 st st
rendered in the 1 -order form, where only 1 -order derivatives are present,

 nd nd
or the 2 -order form which includes 2 -order derivatives in its description.

The code is capable of solving both forms where the user designates either or

 st
both forms for dierent particle or energy groups; in this study, only the 1 -

order form for radiation is solved from which the discretizations follow. The

discretizations lead to a multigroup rendition of the energy axis, a discrete

ordinates approach over all the solid angles, and a nite-element treatment

of the spatial discretization. While the multigroup and discrete ordinates ap-

proaches are common treatments of the radiation transport equation (Grant

and Corner, 1962), the nite-element framework enables the code and its

results to couple seamlessly with uid and/or material models that use un-

structured, nite element-based meshes to describe the elds. Therefore,

this section was included to ensure that the advantages of homogenization

are retained when transitioning from the proof-of-concept demonstrations

shown in the previous subsection to the production-level outputs delivered

by SCEPTRE.

3.3. Approximate Radiation-Material Simulations

 Dierences in radiation ux proles were evident throughout the preced-

ing radiation slab calculations depending upon the type of opacity treatment

that was deployed. Actual experiments and radiative systems in nature are

characteristically multiphysics by description, so the impact of variations

in the radiation ux prole can be magnied depending upon the type of

system that is being modeled. This section builds on the previous sections

to determine how the resulting changes in radiation ux due to the type

of opacity treatment can inuence multiphysics systems. In this case, a

radiation-material setting will be simulated that primarily captures the ef-

fect of radiation physics on material dynamics. By delivering an energy pulse

to a slab, several parameters can be compared and assessed between the var-

ious opacity methodologies employed for radiation transport calculations.

 The computational modeling tool that was used for these calculations

is the multiphysics ALEGRA code (Niederhaus et al., 2023). ALEGRA,

which stands for Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian General Research Applica-

tion, is a nite-element code founded on solid dynamics calculations and was

eventually expanded to other numerical capabilities that include magnetohy-

drodynamics (MHD) and thermal transport. These features were developed

to simulate experiments performed at the Sandia National Laboratories' fa-

cilities such as pulsed power devices, hypervelocity impact, and general ra-

diation eects (Ampleford et al., 2015; Jones et al., 2008, 2014; Moore et al.,

2021, 2022). Given that these experiments can be highly complex due to the

physics involved, this platform provides a simpler radiation-material cong-

uration that can be pursued for the objectives of this investigation.

 38

 The setup of this radiation-material study is mainly a 1D calculation of

the solid dynamics following the rapid deposition of radiation energy over the

2 mm width of the aluminum slab. For these calculations, the energy depo-

sition process takes place suciently fast enough relative to the dynamics of

the solid material that an approximate coupling can be assumed between the

radiation physics and solid dynamics. As such, the SCEPTRE radiation ux

proles can be determined a priori and provided as an input to the ALEGRA

calculations. Similar congurations are found for ablation experiments where

thermal responses are monitored due to high radiative (photon) or particle

(proton) impingement (Moore et al., 2022). Relevant simulations of laser

irradiation on metallic slabs have also been documented in the general liter-

ature (MacFarlane et al., 2003). Before proceeding with the analysis of the

slab's solid dynamics, the radiation ux proles calculated with SCEPTRE

will be introduced for context.

4. Conclusion

 The treatment of opacity proles is a challenging aspect of solving the

radiation transport equation. In the context of deterministic radiation trans-

port equations, the Planck and Rosseland mean opacity formulations were

revisited as commonly-used opacity averaging methods in the multigroup set-

ting. However, these two formulations are valid primarily in the optically-thin

and optically-thick limits, respectively. The Young measure-based homoge-

 45

Figure 14: Rear surface velocity as a function of time produced from the ALEGRA sim-

ulation. The multiple proles correspond to the dierent ux calculations using dierent

opacity treatments in SCEPTRE: line-by-line, Rosseland mean, Planck mean, and 4-band

homogenization.

 46

nization approach by Haut et al. (2017) was explored in this study to address

intermediate optical depths that may also have strong line structures in the

opacity prole.

 To prove the method's viability under these conditions, all of the opac-

ity treatments above, the Rosseland mean opacity, Planck mean opacity,

and homogenization approaches, were investigated for a variety of atomic

elements, temperatures, and densities. The type of elements included alu-

minum, copper, and krypton, all of which are present in Sandia National

Laboratories' range of high-energy-density (HED) or pulsed power facility

experiments. The radiation slab calculations showed that the homogeniza-

tion approach outperformed the conventional mean opacity methods when

compared to equivalent line-by-line calculations along the opacity prole.

Increases in opacity band resolution for similar respective groups showed

further improvements in accuracy as well.

 Successful demonstrations of homogenization through the radiation slab

calculations led to its application in the SCEPTRE and ALEGRA frame-

works. The radiation slab conguration was rst employed in the SCEP-

TRE radiation transport code, which uses a nite element discretization of

the domain. Improvements were replicated in the SCEPTRE framework as

the opacity treatments mentioned above were deployed and compared in this

setting. This then led to the radiation-material simulations conducted us-

ing the ALEGRA code. Approximate radiation-material calculations were

performed using each of the opacity treatment's radiation uxes as energy

deposition proles to monitor the subsequent slab dynamics. The accuracy

of the homogenization method was realized in this coupled setting through

 47

visualizations of velocity and pressure proles from these simulations.

 The results of this study have signicant implications for coupled mul-

tiphysics simulations that include radiation transport physics. While the

benets have been clearly shown throughout the report, there are multiple

simplications assumed with other potential avenues for improvement. For

instance, the radiation slab calculations have assumed a xed temperature-

density condition all along the width of the slab. Actual systems, however,

will be subject to spatially-varying and temporally-evolving temperature-

density conditions. The simulations also assumed mono-elemental materials,

whereas real experiments and applications will have mixtures of species. Rel-

evance to actual systems will require thermodynamic variations and mate-

rial mixtures through follow-on simulations. Numerically, the photon energy

groups were also xed for changing temperatures and densities for a given el-

ement. Groups need to be adjusted for temperature-density variations where

opacity lines may either enumerate or saturate. As such, the tabulations

and algorithmic implementation would need to account for changes in pho-

ton energy group denitions or opacity band descriptions. Fortunately, these

challenges are not restricted solely to homogenization as the method ex-

tends itself from a generalized multigroup framework. Therefore, radiation

transport codes that employ a deterministic, multigroup foundation for their

algorithms can build towards these capabilities, not just SCEPTRE and/or

ALEGRA.

 The transport of accurate radiation elds aects the overall energy trans-

port which can strongly inuence the hydrodynamics and/or solid dynam-

ics evolution. Since the coupling in this study has been primarily conned

 48

to an approximate coupling description, future work will need further de-

velopment and implementation of the homogenization method for strongly-

coupled radiation-hydrodynamic, radiation-material, or even plasma simula-

tions (Niederhaus et al., 2023; Lemke et al., 2009; Moore et al., 2022, 2025).

The inuence of homogenized radiation transport modeling on collisional-

radiative systems and spectroscopy is also evident, requiring its own atten-

tion for future study (Chung et al., 2005; Scott and Hansen, 2010; Ralchenko,

2016; Abrantes et al., 2018; Schaeuble et al., 2025). These examples show-

case the impact of accurately-resolved radiation elds at multiple levels from

microscopic atomic processes to the macroscopic dynamics of uid and solid

systems. By capturing the opacity eects scrupulously through radiation

transport calculations, greater condence may be gleaned from radiative,

multiphysics simulations that guide real experiments and physical applica-

tions of interest.
