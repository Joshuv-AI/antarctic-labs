Effective Computation of Mutation Paths and Generators of Cluster Automorphism Groups
Jindong Zhao, Haiyan Zhu

Abstract
In this paper, we improve the marked-vertex strategy introduced by Fu and Liang, and design the algorithm to compute all mutation paths and generator elements of cluster automorphism groups efficiently. As an application, we get generators of cluster automorphism groups of cluster algebras of finite mutation type of rank 4.



Keywords:
Cluster algebra, Cluster automorphism group, Marked-vertex strategy , Finite mutation type

Introduction

Cluster algebras, introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
1
]
, are commutative rings endowed with a combinatorial structure determined by seeds and mutations.
Their rich combinatorial and algebraic properties establish profound connections to a wide range of mathematical fields, including representation theory, integrable systems, and algebraic geometry.
In
[
1
]
, Assem, Schiffler and Shramchenko introduced and characterized cluster automorphisms of a cluster algebra, and computed the cluster automorphism groups for finite and affine types.
Since then, cluster automorphism groups have been extensively investigated.
Blanc and Dolgachev
[
2
]
used geometric methods to determine the cluster automorphism groups of cluster algebras of rank two.
Chang and Zhu
[
9
]
explicitly described the cluster automorphism groups of finite type cluster algebras via folding techniques.
The cluster automorphism groups for cluster algebras arising from marked surfaces have been further studied in
[
21
,
3
,
11
]
.
Recently, Fu and Liang
[
20
]
used a pseudo
\mathbb{N}
-grading to compute the generating sets of cluster automorphism groups for cluster algebras of rank
3
.

However, the computation of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
of cluster algebras of rank 4 presents substantial challenges:
(1) the lack of a complete classification framework,
(2) severe combinatorial explosion and intrinsic computational complexity in determining generating sets,
(3) the highly intricate polynomial relations among potential generators render manual verification and simplification a significant challenge, even for small rank 4 cases.

Motivated by the approach of Fu and Liang, we proceed with a similar strategy.
For the cluster algebra of Dynkin type
\mathbb{D}_{4}
, the mutation graph contains multiple disjoint cyclic paths, as illustrated in Figure
2.1
.
Consequently, the procedure becomes trapped in an infinite loop under Assumptions (
\diamond
).

To resolve this issue and establish a valid termination condition, we improve the
marked-vertex strategy
to refine the generating set constructed by Fu and Liang.
We then design effective algorithms to compute the generating sets of cluster automorphism groups. And as an application, we get generators of finite mutation type cluster algebras of rank 4.
Subsequently, we eliminate redundant generators — a process similar to computing a reduced Gröbner basis, which reveals the importance of essential generators — to obtain the essential generators of each cluster automorphism group.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the basic definitions relative to cluster algebras and cluster automorphisms.
Then we use an example to illustrate Fu-Liang’s method and highlight the necessity of algorithm optimization in cluster algebras of rank 4. In Section
3
, we generalize the approach of Fu-Liang by adding marked vertices in Theorems
3.3
. After introducing the marked-vertex strategy, we establish algorithms for calculating cluster automorphism groups. In Appendix
A
, according to
[
14
]
, all diagrams of finite-mutation skew symmetrizable matrices are listed in Table
5
.
As an application of Algorithms
2
, we determine generators of the cluster automorphism groups of finite mutation types of rank 4 with indecomposable exchange matrices in Section
4
. Furthermore, we characterize the relations among generators.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall basic definitions in cluster algebras and restrict our attention to cluster algebras with trivial coefficients, which were introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky in
[

]
and let

2.1
Cluster algebras

Definition 2.1
.

For an
n\times n
integer matrix
B=(b_{ij})_{n\times n}
:

(1)

B
is
skew-symmetric
if
B^{\top}=-B
.

(2)

B
is
skew-symmetrizable
if there exists a positive-integer diagonal matrix
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{n})
such that
DB=(d_{i}b_{ij})_{n\times n}
is skew-symmetric. In this case,
D
is called a
skew-symmetrizer
of
B
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a purely transcendental extension field of
\mathbb{Q}
. A
labeled
seed is a pair
(\mathbf{x},B)
consisting of:

•

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, a transcendence basis of
\mathcal{F}
over
\mathbb{Q}
; elements of
\mathbf{x}
are called
cluster variables
, and
\mathbf{x}
itself is termed a
cluster
.

•

B=(b_{ij})
, an
n\times n
skew-symmetric integer matrix, referred to as the
exchange matrix
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a seed
(\mathbf{x},B)
and an index
k\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, the mutation
\mu_{k}
in direction
k
transforms
(\mathbf{x},B)
into a new seed
(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},B^{\prime})=\mu_{k}(\mathbf{x},B)
, where:

-
Mutation of cluster variables
: The new cluster
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,x_{n}^{\prime})
satisfies
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}
for all
i\neq k
, while
x_{k}^{\prime}
is determined by the exchange relation:

x_{k}x_{k}^{\prime}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\\
b_{ik}>0\end{subarray}}x_{i}^{b_{ik}}+\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\\
b_{ik}<0\end{subarray}}x_{i}^{-b_{ik}}.

-
Mutation of the exchange matrix
: The entries of the new exchange matrix
B^{\prime}=(b_{ij}^{\prime})
are given by:

b_{ij}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}-b_{ij}&\text{if }i=k\text{ or }j=k,\\
b_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\left(|b_{ik}|b_{kj}+b_{ik}|b_{kj}|\right)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The mutation is an involution, i.e.,
\mu_{k}\mu_{k}(\mathbf{x},B)=(\mathbf{x},B)
.
Let
\mathbb{T}_{n}
be the
n
-regular tree whose edges are labeled by the numbers
1,\ldots,n
such that the
n
edges emanating from each vertex carry distinct labels. For each vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, there is a unique reduced sequence
i_{k}\ldots i_{1}
such that
t_{0}
and
t
are connected by edges labeled by
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}
, i.e.,
t_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{1}}}{{\rule[2.15277pt]{10.00002pt}{0.4pt}}}\cdot\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{2}}}{{\rule[2.15277pt]{10.00002pt}{0.4pt}}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{k}}}{{\rule[2.15277pt]{10.00002pt}{0.4pt}}}t.

Definition 2.4
.

The
cluster algebra

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{x}_{t_{0}},B_{t_{0}})
associated with an initial seed
(\mathbf{x}_{t_{0}},B_{t_{0}})
is the subalgebra of
\mathcal{F}
generated by all cluster variables from seeds obtained via arbitrary sequences of mutations starting from
(\mathbf{x}_{t_{0}},B_{t_{0}})
.

Moreover, a cluster algebra is called of finite type if it has finitely many cluster variables, while a cluster algebra with only finite number of distinct exchange matrices is called of the finite mutation type.

Definition 2.5
.

A collection of labeled seeds
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\{\Sigma_{t}=(\mathbf{x}_{t},B_{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}
indexed by the
n
-regular tree
\mathbb{T}_{n}
is called a
cluster pattern
or seed pattern of rank
n
if for any pair of vertices
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
that are
k
-adjacent, i.e.,
\Sigma_{t^{\prime}}=\mu_{k}(\Sigma_{t})
.

2.2
Diagrams of exchange matrices

In this section, we recall the diagrams of exchange matrices, which were introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky in
[
17
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
B=(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
be an
n\times n
skew-symmetrizable matrix. The diagram
\Gamma(B)
associated with
B
is a weighted directed graph where the vertex set is
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. A directed edge from vertex
i
to vertex
j
exists precisely when
b_{ij}>0
, and such an edge is assigned a weight of
|b_{ij}b_{ji}|
. We call
B
indecomposable if its corresponding diagram
\Gamma(B)
is connected.

Definition 2.7
.

For any vertex
k
in a diagram
\Gamma:=\Gamma(B)
(where
B
is a skew-symmetrizable matrix), the mutation
\mu_{k}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
at
k
is a weighted directed graph constructed from
\Gamma
through the following steps:

•

Edge reversal:
All edges incident to vertex
k
have their orientations reversed, with weights preserved.

•

Path composition:
For any pair of vertices
i
and
j
connected via a 2-edge path
i\to k\to j
or
i\leftarrow k\leftarrow j
in
\Gamma
:

\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{a}\pm\sqrt{a^{\prime}}=\sqrt{bc}

b
= weight of edge
(i,k)
or
(k,i)
,
c
= weight of edge
(k,j)
or
(j,k)

a
= weight of edge
(i,j)
,
a^{\prime}
= new weight of edge
(i,j)

Signs determined by cycle orientations (The sign before
a
is “+” if
i\to k\to j
, and is “-” otherwise. And the sign before
a^{\prime}
is opposite to that before
a
)

•

Preservation:
All other edges and weights remain unchanged.

This diagram mutation rule encodes the signed adjacency changes during matrix mutation.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\Sigma=(\mathbf{x},B)
be a labeled seed of rank
n
, and let
\Gamma(B)
be the diagram corresponding to
B
. For any
\sigma\in S_{n}
:

•

The action on the seed is:

\sigma(\Sigma)=(\sigma\mathbf{x},\sigma(B)),\quad\begin{cases}x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}\\
b_{ij}^{\prime}=b_{\sigma^{-1}(i)\sigma^{-1}(j)}\end{cases}

•

The action on the diagram
\sigma(\Gamma(B))
relabels vertices via
\sigma
while preserving edge weights and directions between corresponding vertices.

This group action formalizes vertex relabeling and is compatible with mutation operations.

Furthermore, for skew-symmetrizable matrices
B
and
B^{\prime}
, we write
B\sim B^{\prime}
if there exists a
\sigma\in S_{n}
such that
B^{\prime}=\sigma(B)
.
In the following,
\Gamma(B)
and
\Gamma(B^{\prime})
are said to be
sign permutation equivalent
if
B^{\prime}\sim\pm B
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\Sigma=(\mathbf{x},B)
be a labeled seed of rank
n
, let
\Gamma(B)
be the diagram of
B
, and let
1\leq k\leq n
. The following statements hold for any
\sigma\in S_{n}
:

1.

\sigma(\Gamma(B))=\Gamma(\sigma(B))
(diagram isomorphism)

2.

The actions of mutation and permutation commute:

\sigma\circ\mu_{k}=\mu_{\sigma(k)}\circ\sigma.

That is:

\sigma(\mu_{k}(\Sigma))=\mu_{\sigma(k)}(\sigma(\Sigma))\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(\mu_{k}(\Gamma(B)))=\mu_{\sigma(k)}(\sigma(\Gamma(B))).

Cluster automorphism

In this section, we recall the approach introduced by Fu and Liang
[
20

for calculating cluster automorphism groups of cluster algebras.

Firstly, we recall the definition of cluster automorphisms, which were introduced by Assem, Schiffler and Shamchenko in
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.10

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(\Sigma):=\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{x},B)
be a cluster algebra, and
f:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra.
f
is called a
cluster automorphism
of
\mathcal{A}
if there exist another seed
\Sigma^{\prime}:=(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of
\mathcal{A}
and a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
, such that:

(1)

\sigma f(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}^{\prime},

(2)

\sigma(f(\mu_{k}(\mathbf{x})))=\mu_{\sigma(k)}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
for
k=1,\ldots,n.

In fact, the condition
(2)
is superfluous that was conjecture by Chang and Schiffler and proved in
[
5
]
.

In fact, every cluster automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
can be written as a quadruple

f=(\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2},\sigma,\varepsilon),

where
\Sigma_{1}=(\mathbf{x}_{1},B_{1})
and
\Sigma_{2}=(\mathbf{x}_{2},B_{2})
are labeled seeds,
\sigma\in S_{n}
, and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}
such that
\sigma B_{1}=\varepsilon B_{2}
. The automorphism
f
is uniquely determined by the condition

\sigma f(\mathbf{x}_{1})=\mathbf{x}_{2}
,
which was proven in
[
20
]
.

Denote the set of all cluster automorphisms of
\mathcal{A}
by
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
which forms a group under composition, cf.
[
1
]
.

Recall that, we have, fixed a cluster pattern
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\{\Sigma_{t}=(\mathbf{x}_{t},B_{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}
with root vertex
t_{0}
, for any two vertices
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, let
p(t,t^{\prime})
be the unique path in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
from
t
to
t^{\prime}

p(t,t^{\prime})=t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{1}}}{{\rule[2.15277pt]{10.00002pt}{0.4pt}}}\cdot\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{2}}}{{\rule[2.15277pt]{10.00002pt}{0.4pt}}}\cdot\cdots\cdot\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{m}}}{{\rule[2.15277pt]{10.00002pt}{0.4pt}}}t^{\prime}
, for any
\sigma\in S_{n}
, we denote

\mu_{p(t,t^{\prime})}:=\mu_{i_{m}}\cdots\mu_{i_{1}}\text{ and }\mu_{\sigma(p(t,t^{\prime}))}:=\mu_{\sigma(i_{m})}\cdots\mu_{\sigma(i_{1})}.

For a fixed vertex
s\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, we define the weight of
p(t,t^{\prime})
with respect to
s
as follows

\mathbf{w}_{s}(t,t^{\prime}):=\#\left\{t^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\mid\text{the path }p(t,t^{\prime})\text{ passes through the vertex }t^{\prime\prime}\text{ and }B_{t^{\prime\prime}}\sim\pm B_{s}\right\}.

For any positive integer

\mathcal{P}_{m}(t_{0}):=\{p(t_{0},t)\mid\mathbf{w}_{t_{0}}(t_{0},t)=m+1\text{ and }B_{t}\sim\pm B_{t_{0}}\}.

Furthermore, for a certain cluster automorphism

g_{i_{k}\cdots i_{1}}^{\sigma,\varepsilon}\coloneqq(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Sigma_{t},\sigma,\varepsilon)\text{ or }\psi_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Sigma_{t_{0}},\sigma,\varepsilon).

Let
m
be a positive integer, define

H_{m}(t_{0}):=\{f_{p(t_{0},t)}\mid p(t_{0},t)\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(t_{0})\}\subset G_{m}(t_{0}),

where

G_{m}(t_{0}):=\left\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{A})\middle|\begin{array}[]{l}\exists s\in\mathbb{T}_{n},\sigma\in S_{n}\text{ and }\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}\\
\text{such that }\mathbf{w}_{t_{0}}(t_{0},s)=m+1\text{ and }\\
f=(\Sigma_{t_{0}},\Sigma_{s},\sigma,\varepsilon)\end{array}\right\}.

Assume that there is a (marked) vertex
t_{\diamond}
satisfyies

Assumption (
\diamond

For any

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n,m}^{t_{\diamond}}(t_{0})

\displaystyle:=\{p(t_{0},t)\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(t_{0})\mid\mathbf{w}_{t_{\diamond}}(t_{0},t)=m\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{n}(t_{0}),

Marked-vertex strategy and algorithms

3.1
Multiple marked vertices strategy

Inspired by the Assumption(
\diamond

t_{0}\triangleq t_{\diamond_{0}}

t_{\diamond}\triangleq t_{\diamond_{1}}(n>2)

Assumption (
\diamond_{n}
)

\mathbf{w}_{t_{\diamond_{i}}}(t_{0},t_{\diamond_{i}})=\mathbf{w}_{t_{0}}(t_{0},t_{\diamond_{i}})=1

(
[

]
)
.

Let

For any mutation sequence

\displaystyle:=\{f_{p(t_{0},t)}\mid p(t_{0},t)\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n})(t_{0})\},

\displaystyle G_{1}(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n})(t_{0})

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{A})\middle|\begin{array}[]{l}\exists p(t_{0},t)\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n})(t_{0}),\sigma\in S_{n},\\

.
