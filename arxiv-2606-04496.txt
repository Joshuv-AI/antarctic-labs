1
Introduction

The study of time-dependent
C\!P
violation in the radiative decay
B\rightarrow K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}\gamma
provides a unique probe for physics beyond the Standard Model (SM). The decay occurs predominantly through
b\rightarrow s\gamma
loop transitions, which makes it susceptible to contributions from heavy, virtual particles
11
;
28
. As a result, this decay channel can explore physics at energy scales significantly larger than those directly accessible in current collider experiments.

The time-dependent
C\!P
asymmetry arises due to interference between decay amplitudes with and without
B^{0}
–
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
mixing, where a nonzero phase appearing in the quark-mixing matrix causes differing decay rates for
B^{0}
and
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
over time.
For coherent
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pair production at the
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
, in which one of the
B
mesons (
B_{\text{sig}}
) decays to a final state
f_{C\!P}\gamma
, where
f_{C\!P}
is a
C\!P
eigenstate, and the other
B
meson (
B_{\text{tag}}
) decays to a flavour-specific final state, this asymmetry is given as

{\cal A}_{C\!P}(\Delta t)=\frac{\Gamma(B_{\text{tag}=B^{0}}(\Delta t)\rightarrow f_{C\!P}\gamma)-\Gamma(B_{\text{tag}=\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}^{0}}(\Delta t)\rightarrow f_{C\!P}\gamma)}{\Gamma(B_{\text{tag}=B^{0}}(\Delta t)\rightarrow f_{C\!P}\gamma)+\Gamma(B_{\text{tag}=\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}^{0}}(\Delta t)\rightarrow f_{C\!P}\gamma)},

(1)

where
\Gamma(B_{{\rm tag}=B^{0}/\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}^{0}})
is the decay rate of
B_{\text{sig}}
to
f_{C\!P}\gamma
when
B_{\text{tag}}
has been identified as a
B^{0}/\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
meson, and
\Delta t
is the proper time difference between the decays of
B_{\text{sig}}
and
B_{\text{tag}}
. This asymmetry can be parameterised as

{\cal A}_{C\!P}(\Delta t)=S\sin(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t)-C\cos(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t),

(2)

where
\Delta m_{d}
is the mass difference between the two
B^{0}
mass eigenstates, and
S
and
C
are the mixing-induced and direct
C\!P
-violating parameters, respectively.

In the SM, the photon emitted in
b\rightarrow s\gamma
processes is predominantly left-handed due to the chiral structure of the weak interaction. The presence of a polarised photon in the final state suppresses the interference, leading to small
C\!P
-violating effects. In particular, the SM predicts that
S
should be suppressed by a factor of
m_{s}/m_{b}
due to helicity suppression, with deviations expected at the level of a few percent
13
. Here,
m_{s}
(
m_{b}
) is the mass of the strange (bottom) quark. The
S
value is calculated to be
(-2.3\pm 1.6)\%
for the resonant
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{*0}(892)\gamma
channel, which can, however, be enhanced to
{\cal O}(10\%)
in nonresonant
B\rightarrow K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}\gamma
decays through long-distance effects, such as charm loop contributions
26
;
27
. The
C
value is expected to be smaller than 1%
32
; however, this estimate may not be robust due to potentially large uncertainties in predicting strong phases
13
.

Any significant contribution from the right-handed photon can increase the
S
value beyond the previously mentioned predictions, indicating evidence for new physics, such as supersymmetry, extended Higgs sectors, or models with vector-like fermions
45
;
21
;
31
. Therefore, a precise measurement of the time-dependent
C\!P
asymmetry in
B\rightarrow K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}\gamma
provides a stringent test of the SM and a powerful probe for new sources of
C\!P
violation from physics beyond the SM. Moreover, the different mechanisms at play in the theoretical predictions
13
;
26
;
27
for the
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{*0}(892)\gamma
and nonresonant decays motivate separate investigation of these channels.

The previous measurements from Belle, BaBar, and Belle II are summarised in Table
1
. While these results are consistent with SM predictions, they exhibit significant uncertainties. The objective of our study is to enhance the measurement precision by utilizing a larger dataset and advanced algorithms. In particular, we have implemented an improved, Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based flavour Tagger
3
across both the Belle and Belle II datasets, along with an improved
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
selection algorithm specifically for the Belle dataset. We have also incorporated information from events with poor time resolution in a time-integrated fit, which contributes to reducing the uncertainty on
C
.

2
Detectors and data samples

The Belle and Belle II detectors both have a cylindrical geometry whose symmetry (
z
) axis is nearly aligned with the electron beam direction at the interaction point (IP). The polar angle
\theta
is defined relative to the
z
axis.

The Belle detector
1
was located at the KEKB accelerator
34
, which collided electrons and positrons with beam energies of 8.0 GeV and 3.5 GeV, respectively. It recorded data from 1999 to 2010. Belle was a large-solid-angle magnetic spectrometer composed of a silicon vertex detector (SVD), a central drift chamber (CDC), an array of aerogel threshold Cherenkov counters (ACC), a barrel-like arrangement of time-of-flight (TOF) scintillation counters, and an electromagnetic calorimeter (ECL) comprised of CsI(Tl) crystals, all located inside a superconducting solenoid coil that provided a magnetic field of 1.5 T. The SVD and CDC were used to reconstruct charged particle tracks and vertices, while the ACC and TOF, along with specific ionization (
dE/dx
) measurements from the CDC, were used for charged particle identification (PID) purposes, and photons were reconstructed from calorimetric clusters in the ECL. An iron flux-return yoke, placed outside the coil, was instrumented with resistive-plate chambers to detect
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}
mesons and muons.

The Belle II detector
2
represents a significant upgrade from Belle, and is currently operational at the SuperKEKB accelerator
8
, which collides electrons and positrons with beam energies of 7.0 GeV and 4.0 GeV, respectively.
Belle II features a two-layer pixel detector (PXD) that is surrounded by a four-layer SVD
6
and a 56-layer CDC. These subdetectors help reconstruct the tracks of charged particles; the SVD and CDC additionally provide
dE/dx
information for PID purposes. Enclosing the CDC are a time-of-propagation counter (TOP)
10
located in the central region and an aerogel-based ring-imaging Cherenkov counter (ARICH) situated in the forward region. Together, these subdetectors constitute the primary PID system. Surrounding both the TOP and ARICH is the ECL, made from the same CsI(Tl) crystals as Belle but with faster electronics, which measures energy and time for photons and electrons.
Outside the ECL is a superconducting solenoid magnet that provides a magnetic field of 1.5 T, parallel to the
z
axis.
The flux return of the magnet is equipped with resistive-plate chambers and plastic scintillator modules for the detection of muons,
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}
mesons, and neutrons.

The data samples used in our study were collected at the
\Upsilon(4S)
resonance, which decays predominantly into
B\overline{B}
pairs. The Belle dataset corresponds to an integrated luminosity of
711\,{\rm fb}^{-1}
, containing approximately
772\times 10^{6}

\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
decays. Similarly, the Belle II dataset used corresponds to an integrated luminosity of
493\,{\rm fb}^{-1}
, containing approximately
521\times 10^{6}

\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
decays. About 81% of the latter sample was collected with both the PXD and SVD operational, while the remaining 19% was collected with the PXD off.

The analysis strategy and fitting procedure are developed using simulated Monte Carlo (MC) samples. Data control samples and sidebands are then used to validate the procedures before examining the signal region in the data, which is accessed only after the analysis procedures have been finalized. MC simulation samples for the process
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Upsilon(4S)\rightarrow B\overline{B}
are generated using the
EvtGen

35
package, designed for
B
meson decays. The
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\overline{q}
(
q\in[u,d,s,c]
) continuum events are generated using the
KKMC

29
package. Both packages are interfaced with
Pythia6

41
(
Pythia8

40
) for Belle (Belle II).
The detector response is modeled with the
Geant3

17
framework for Belle and
Geant4

7
for Belle II. We use MC samples from generic
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions, i.e., combining
B^{0}\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
,
B^{+}B^{-}
, and
q\overline{q}
events, as well as specific samples of
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events, where one of the
B
mesons decays into a specified mode of interest, for example, the signal. The Belle II analysis software framework
16
;
25
is used to process both the simulated and real data samples.

3
Reconstruction and event selection

The reconstruction of
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}\gamma
candidates is performed with a bottom-up approach, where
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidates are first reconstructed from tracks in the PXD (Belle II), SVD, and CDC, while
\pi^{0}
and
\gamma
candidates are reconstructed from clusters in the ECL. The remaining tracks and clusters are assigned to the
B_{\rm tag}
candidates.

We reconstruct
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
using two oppositely charged tracks, assumed to be pions and constrained to come from a common vertex, and that their invariant mass lies within
\pm 30\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
of the known
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
mass
38
. To suppress misreconstructed
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidates, two multivariate (MVA) classifiers are used. The first one selects
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
–like candidates by primarily using the kinematic properties of the
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidate and its decay pions, together with its flight length. The second MVA is designed to reduce the contamination from
\Lambda^{0}\rightarrow p^{+}\pi^{-}
decays by using the proton PID likelihood of each candidate decay pion, and the reconstructed
\Lambda^{0}
mass, obtained by assigning the proton mass hypothesis to one of the pion candidates. In Belle, these classifiers
37
are based on a NeuroBayes Neural Network
23
package while in Belle II, LightGBM
33
is employed.

We reconstruct
\pi^{0}
candidates from two photon candidates with invariant mass satisfying
m_{\gamma\gamma}\in[90,160]\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, with further constraints on their shower shape in the ECL and the opening angle between them. For Belle II, we also require the ECL cluster time for each photon candidate to be within
\pm 200\,{\rm ns}
of the beam-crossing time. A mass-constrained fit is applied to improve the momentum resolution of
\pi^{0}
candidates. To reject misreconstructed
\pi^{0}
candidates, separate MVA classifiers based on XGBoost
18
are employed for Belle and Belle II utilizing features related to the kinematics of the
\pi^{0}
candidates and the shower shapes of their decay products. The key discriminating features are the
\chi^{2}
probability of the mass-constrained fit and the opening angle between the
\pi^{0}
decay products.

Prompt photon candidates are selected from high-energy ECL clusters with energies satisfying
E^{*}>1.4\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
. The asterisk (
*
) denotes quantities calculated in the
e^{+}e^{-}
center-of-mass frame. To ensure the cluster shape is consistent with an electromagnetic shower, we require that the ratio
E_{9}
/
E_{21}
exceeds 0.9, where
E_{9}
and
E_{21}
are the energies deposited in a
3\times 3
array of crystals and a
5\times 5
array excluding the four corners, respectively, centred on the crystal with the highest energy. Furthermore, for Belle II, we require the ECL cluster time for each photon candidate to be within
\pm 200\,{\rm ns}
of the beam-crossing time. To suppress backgrounds from
\pi^{0}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
and
\eta\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
decays, separate MVA classifiers based on XGBoost are employed for Belle and Belle II
14
. These classifiers combine the kinematic information of the prompt photon candidate with other clusters from the rest of the event to identify potential
\pi^{0}
or
\eta
candidates. If such candidates are found, then the prompt photon candidate is rejected. The most discriminating features are the reconstructed
\pi^{0}/\eta
mass and the
\chi^{2}
probability of the corresponding mass-constrained fit.

The
B_{\rm sig}
candidates are reconstructed by combining the selected
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
,
\pi^{0}
, and prompt photon candidates. A vertex fit is performed to the entire decay chain with the
B_{\rm sig}
candidates constrained to originate from the IP
15
. Two kinematic variables are used to identify
B_{\rm sig}
candidates: the beam-constrained mass
M_{\mathrm{bc}}=(1/c^{2})\sqrt{s/4-(|\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}^{*}_{B}|c)^{2}}
and the energy difference
\Delta E=E^{*}_{B}-\sqrt{s}/2
, where
\sqrt{s}/2
is the beam energy, and
E^{*}_{B}
and
\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}^{*}_{B}
are the energy and momentum of the reconstructed
B_{\rm sig}
candidate, respectively. Owing to the presence of a high-energy photon in the final state, the
\Delta E
distribution is asymmetric, with a long tail on the negative side arising primarily due to shower leakage in the ECL. This effect also causes a correlation between
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
and
\Delta E

22
, which can be mitigated by redefining
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
as follows:

\displaystyle E_{\rm res}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{s}/2-E^{*}_{K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}},

\displaystyle E_{\pi^{0}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{\rm res}}{E^{*}_{\pi^{0}}+E^{*}_{\gamma}}\times E^{*}_{\pi^{0}},\ E_{\gamma}^{\prime}=\frac{E_{\rm res}}{E^{*}_{\pi^{0}}+E^{*}_{\gamma}}\times E^{*}_{\gamma},

\displaystyle{\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}_{\pi^{0}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(E_{\pi^{0}}^{\prime}/c)^{2}-(M_{\pi^{0}}c)^{2}}\times\frac{\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}^{*}_{\pi^{0}}}{|\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}^{*}_{\pi^{0}}|},\ {\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}_{\gamma}^{\prime}}=(E_{\gamma}^{\prime}/c)\times\frac{\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}^{*}_{\gamma}}{|\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}^{*}_{\gamma}|},

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{bc}}

\displaystyle=(1/c^{2})\sqrt{s/4-(|\vec{p}_{K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}}+\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}_{\pi^{0}}^{\prime}+\vec{p}\mkern 2.0mu\vphantom{p}_{\gamma}^{\prime}|c)^{2}}.

(3)

By rescaling the
\pi^{0}
and photon energies using the precisely measured kinematics of the
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
and the
e^{+}e^{-}
collision system, we minimize the dependence of
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
on the measured energies of the
\pi^{0}
and photon candidates, which are prone to biases from shower leakage. This rescaling significantly reduces the correlation between
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
and
\Delta E
in the background events without affecting the signal, and improves the signal
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
resolution by 3–7% depending on the dataset. Candidates are required to satisfy
M_{\mathrm{bc}}>5.23\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
-0.3\penalty\ \mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}<\Delta E<0.2\penalty\ \mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
. The invariant mass of the
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}
system,
M_{K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}}
, is required to lie within the range
[0.8,1.8]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
. The dataset is divided into two regions:
M_{K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}}\in[0.8,1.0]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, denoted as MR1, and
M_{K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}}\in(1.0,1.8]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, denoted as MR2. The
K^{*0}(892)
resonance is the primary contributor in MR1.

We reconstruct the
B_{\rm tag}
vertex using well-reconstructed tracks not associated with the
B_{\rm sig}
candidate. A further constraint is applied to ensure that the vertex lies in a tube originating from the IP, along the
B_{\rm tag}
flight direction calculated using the
B_{\rm sig}
momentum
20
. We retain events where both
B_{\rm sig}
and
B_{\rm tag}
vertices are successfully reconstructed. A GNN-based flavour tagging (FT) algorithm
3
is employed to determine the flavour of
B_{\rm tag}
at the time of its decay. This algorithm outputs a flavour tag
q
(where
q=+1
for
B^{0}
and
-1
for
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
) and a quality factor
r
that indicates the confidence level of the flavour assignment.

The data include contributions from backgrounds associated with continuum events and
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
decays other than signal. To suppress the continuum background, we train MVA classifiers based on XGBoost, using event shape variables that leverage the topological differences between
q\overline{q}
events, which exhibit a boosted jet-like topology, and
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events, characterised by a nearly isotropic distribution. Separate classifiers are trained for Belle and Belle II, as well as for MR1 and MR2, to account for differences in detector performance and background composition. The most discriminating feature in these classifiers is the angle between the thrust axes of the
B_{\rm sig}
candidate and the rest of the event. The selection criteria for all MVA classifiers are determined by maximizing a figure-of-merit
N_{\rm S}/\sqrt{N_{\rm S}+N_{\rm B}}
, where
N_{\rm S}
and
N_{\rm B}
are the numbers of signal and background events, respectively, expected in the signal region that is defined by
M_{\mathrm{bc}}>5.27{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
-0.2\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}<\Delta E<0.1\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
.
The asymmetric range for
\Delta E
is considered to account for the shower leakage in the ECL.

The output of the continuum MVA classifier, after applying the selection criteria, is used as a variable in the final fit. This MVA output exhibits a strong peak near 1.0 for the signal, making it challenging to model. We therefore transform it using the following relation to obtain a variable that can be more readily modeled with analytical functions:

C^{\prime}=\ln\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{out}}-C_{\mathrm{out}}^{\mathrm{min}}}{C_{\mathrm{out}}^{\mathrm{max}}-C_{\mathrm{out}}}\right),

(4)

where
C_{\mathrm{out}}
is the original MVA output, and
C^{\mathrm{min}}_{\mathrm{out}}
and
C^{\mathrm{max}}_{\mathrm{out}}
are the minimum and maximum values of the output, respectively.

After applying all selection criteria, 10–12% of events have multiple signal candidates, depending on the dataset. Since these ambiguities are primarily due to misreconstructed
\pi^{0}
candidates, the candidate with the highest
\pi^{0}
MVA output is retained in such instances; for the remaining events with multiple candidates, a candidate is arbitrarily chosen. We find that this approach selects the correctly reconstructed signal decay in events with multiple candidates approximately 75% of the time based on simulation studies.

The overall reconstruction and selection efficiency for signal events is estimated with simulated datasets. For Belle, the efficiency is approximately 19% in MR1 and 13% in MR2. For Belle II, the corresponding values are higher, around 22% in MR1 and 14% in MR2; the increase is due to improved detector performance and reconstruction algorithms.

4
Likelihood fit and results

The distribution of the proper decay time difference
\Delta t
between
B_{\rm sig}
and
B_{\rm tag}
encodes the
C\!P
asymmetries [Eq. (
2
)]. This variable is calculated using the formula
\Delta t=\Delta\ell/\beta\gamma c
, where
\Delta\ell
is the distance between the two reconstructed vertices along the boost direction, and
\beta\gamma
, the Lorentz boost of the
\Upsilon(4S)
, equals
0.425
(
0.284
) for Belle (Belle II).

Based on the quality of
\Delta t
reconstruction, events are categorised into two groups: well- and poorly-reconstructed
\Delta t
events. For an event to be classified as well-reconstructed, both the
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
decay products must register at least one hit in the SVD layers for Belle, and in either the PXD or SVD layers for Belle II, and the uncertainty in
\Delta t
must be less than
3.0\,{\rm ps}
for both Belle and Belle II. Such events account for approximately 50% (75%) of the total events selected in the Belle (Belle II) dataset. The larger fraction in Belle II arises from the larger volume of the vertex detector (PXD and SVD) and the smaller Lorentz boost in comparison to Belle, which enhance the likelihood of the
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
decaying within the vertex detector.

The well-reconstructed
\Delta t
events are used for time-dependent (TD) fits to extract both
S
and
C
parameters, while the poorly-reconstructed
\Delta t
events are used for time-integrated (TI) fits to extract only the
C
parameter. To extract
S
and
C
, we perform a simultaneous extended unbinned maximum-likelihood fit to the TD and TI datasets in the two
B_{\rm tag}
flavours. The TD fit involves
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
,
\Delta E
,
C^{\prime}
, and
\Delta t
, taking per-event dependence on the
\Delta t
uncertainty (
\sigma_{\Delta t}
) and flavour-tagging quality factor
r
into account. The TI fit uses
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
,
\Delta E
, and
C^{\prime}
, considering per-event dependence on
r
. We use the same fit strategy for the Belle and Belle II datasets in both MR1 and MR2. The fit model includes three components: signal (both correctly and partially reconstructed events with a well-reconstructed
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
),
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
, and
q\overline{q}
backgrounds. The probability density functions (PDFs) for all components are modelled with simulated events and validated with control channels.

Based on MC simulation studies, we find
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
,
\Delta E
, and
C^{\prime}
variables have negligible correlation with
\Delta t
, allowing us to factorise the full fit PDFs into the two parts: the signal extraction PDF and the
C\!P
asymmetry PDF. Being a function of
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
,
\Delta E
, and
C^{\prime}
, the first PDF aims to discriminate between various components and obtain the per-event signal probability. On the other hand, the second PDF models the
\Delta t
distribution with per-event dependence on
\sigma_{\Delta t}
and
r
to determine
S
and
C
. The full PDFs for TD and TI datasets are given by:

\displaystyle P_{\rm{full}}^{\rm TD}

\displaystyle(M_{\mathrm{bc}},\Delta E,C^{\prime},\Delta t\mid\sigma_{\Delta t},q,r)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}N_{i}^{q}(q,r)f_{i}^{\rm TD}P_{i}^{\rm SE}(M_{\mathrm{bc}},\Delta E,C^{\prime})P_{i}^{\rm CPV}(\Delta t\mid\sigma_{\Delta t},q,r)

(5)

and

\displaystyle P_{\rm{full}}^{\rm TI}

\displaystyle(M_{\mathrm{bc}},\Delta E,C^{\prime}\mid q,r)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}N_{i}^{q}(q,r)(1-f_{i}^{\rm TD})P_{i}^{\rm SE}(M_{\mathrm{bc}},\Delta E,C^{\prime}).

(6)

Here,
i\in[1,3]
represents the signal,
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
, and
q\overline{q}
components, respectively;
N_{i}^{q}(q,r)
are the yields of components for the two
B_{\rm tag}
flavours, expressed in terms of the direct
C\!P
-violation parameter
C
for signal and
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background as described later;
f_{i}^{\rm TD}
are the fractions of events in the TD dataset, obtained from simulation and calibrated with sidebands and control channels. Lastly,
P_{i}^{\rm SE}
are the signal extraction PDFs, and
P_{i}^{\rm CPV}
are the
C\!P
asymmetry PDFs for the three components.

4.1
Signal extraction PDF

We model the shape in
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
for correctly reconstructed signal using a Crystal Ball function
24
with a Gaussian core and a polynomial tail on one side
42
, and its
\Delta E
shape using a Johnson’s function
30
. The correlation between
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
and
\Delta E
can range from 5% to 20% depending on the dataset and is modelled in the fit by parameterising the width and tail parameters of the
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
PDF as functions of
\Delta E
. The
C^{\prime}
distribution for signal is modelled with a Johnson’s function. The signal extraction PDF for signal is then the
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
–
\Delta E
PDF multiplied by the
C^{\prime}
PDF. The parameters describing the means of the
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
and
\Delta E
functions are floated in the full fit. For the partially reconstructed signal with a well-reconstructed
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
, known as self cross-feed (SCF), we use a Crystal Ball function for
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
, and an asymmetric Gaussian function for
\Delta E
and
C^{\prime}
. The signal extraction PDF for the SCF component is the product of the individual
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
,
\Delta E
, and
C^{\prime}
PDFs. The final signal extraction PDF for the signal component (
P_{1}^{\rm SE}
) is the sum of the correctly reconstructed and SCF signal PDFs, weighted by their fractions expected from MC simulation.

The
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background exhibits a peaking structure in
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
near the known
B
meson mass
38
; however, such a structure is absent in
\Delta E
. To address the residual correlation between
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
and
\Delta E
for this background, we model its joint
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
–
\Delta E
PDF using a two-dimensional Kernel Density Estimator (KDE)
19
. The
C^{\prime}
distribution is represented by an asymmetric Gaussian function.
The signal extraction PDF for the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background (
P_{2}^{\rm SE}
) is then the
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
–
\Delta E
KDE PDF multiplied with the
C^{\prime}
PDF.

We model the
q\overline{q}
background
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
shape using an ARGUS function
9
, and its
\Delta E
shape using a first-order polynomial. The
C^{\prime}
distribution for the
q\overline{q}
background is modelled with a Johnson’s function.
Due to the negligible correlation among the three fit variables, we define the signal extraction PDF for the
q\overline{q}
background (
P_{3}^{\rm{SE}}
) as the product of the individual PDFs for
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
,
\Delta E
, and
C^{\prime}
.

The final signal extraction PDF is the sum of signal,
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
, and
q\overline{q}
background signal extraction PDFs, weighted by their respective yields, which are floated in the fit:

P^{\rm{SE}}_{\rm tot}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}N_{i}P_{i}^{\rm{SE}}(M_{\mathrm{bc}},\Delta E,C^{\prime}).

(7)

To validate the signal extraction fit strategy in the data, we use the
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{*0}[K^{+}\pi^{-}]\gamma
control channel, using the same fit strategy as the signal channel. This channel is chosen because of its relatively large sample and clean signature. It mimics the signal channel due to the presence of a high-energy photon in the final state. The means of the signal and
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}

C^{\prime}
PDFs, and the widths of the signal
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
,
\Delta E
, and
C^{\prime}
PDFs are floated in both data and simulation fits of the control channel to take into account possible data-MC differences. We extract as correction factors differences in the means and ratios in the widths, which are of the order of 1–3% for the signal and around 10% for the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background. These correction factors are then applied to the full fit in the signal channel.

The projections of signal extraction PDFs (
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
,
\Delta E
, and
C^{\prime}
) obtained from the full fit to data in the signal channel are shown in Figures
1
and
2
for Belle and Belle II, respectively.

4.2
C\!P
asymmetry PDF

The
C\!P
asymmetry PDF for signal comes into play in the fit performed to the
\Delta t
distribution to measure
S
and
C
. The physics-motivated model for
C\!P
violation is modified to incorporate the effect of incorrect flavour assignment by the FT algorithm, and is convolved with a resolution function to account for finite detector resolution and various physical effects, such as secondary decays of charmed particles
3
:

P_{\rm sig}(\Delta t,q\mid\sigma_{\Delta t},r)=\frac{\exp(-|\Delta t|/\tau_{B})}{4\tau_{B}}\{1-q\Delta w+q\mu(1-2w)\\
+[q(1-2w)+\mu(1-q\Delta w)]\left[S\sin(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t)-C\cos(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t)\right]\}\otimes\mathcal{R}(\Delta t\mid\sigma_{\Delta t}).

(8)

Here,
\tau_{B}
is the lifetime of
B^{0}
and
w
,
\Delta w
, and
\mu
are the three FT parameters that represent the fraction of wrong tags, the asymmetry of wrong tags, and the asymmetry of the tagging efficiency, respectively. They are determined in seven bins of
r
from a control channel of self-tagged
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{*}
and
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
decays for Belle and Belle II, respectively
3
. The effective tagging efficiencies are (
33.6\pm 1.2)\%
and (
37.4\pm 0.4)\%
for Belle and Belle II, respectively. We use linear interpolation to model the FT parameters as a function of
r
and use a per-event dependence on
r
in the fit.

(12)

The parameters of the resolution functions in data and simulation are determined from fits to a control channel of

\displaystyle N_{\rm sig}^{q}(q=\pm 1,r)=\frac{N_{\rm sig}}{2}\biggl(

\displaystyle 1-q\Delta w+q\mu(1-2w)

\displaystyle-C\frac{q(1-2w)+\mu(1-q\Delta w)}{1+\Delta m_{d}^{2}\tau_{B}^{2}}\biggr).

(13)

We model the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background
\Delta t

and

). We also use the same resolution function as that used for the signal, except for the width of the core Gaussian, which is determined from a fit to simulated

\tau_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}

We consider several sources of systematic uncertainties that could affect our measurement of the

Systematic uncertainties due to the FT parameters (
w
,
\Delta w
, and
\mu
), physics parameters (
\tau_{B}
and
\Delta m_{d}
),
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}

C\!P
asymmetries (
S_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}
and
C_{B\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}
), and the SCF fraction are estimated by sampling these parameters from Gaussian distributions and refitting the final dataset with these sampled values. The widths of the resulting distributions of fitted
S
and
C
values are then taken as the systematic uncertainties. For each FT parameter, the sampling Gaussian is centered at the mean value and assigned a width equal to the corresponding uncertainty, which arises from the limited size of calibration data samples. For physics parameters, the means and widths of the sampling Gaussians are set to their known values and uncertainties
38
. For
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}

C\!P
asymmetries, the sampling Gaussian is centered at the values expected from MC simulation, with widths corresponding to the uncertainties in these parameters, typically around 10–20%. For the SCF fraction, the sampling Gaussian is likewise centered at its MC expected value, with a width set to 20% of that value.

The systematic uncertainties due to the fixed parameters of the resolution function and PDF shapes are estimated by sampling these parameters from the simulation-fit likelihood, and refitting the final dataset with these sampled values. The widths of the resulting distributions of fitted
C
and
S
values are taken as the systematic uncertainties. The systematic uncertainties due to non-parametric
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
KDE PDFs are estimated by generating new PDFs using datasets bootstrapped from simulated events. This method introduces Poisson fluctuations on the PDF shape. The final dataset is refitted using these generated KDE PDFs and the widths of the resulting distributions of fitted
C
and
S
values are taken as the systematic uncertainties.

Systematic uncertainties related to the
B
meson vertex reconstruction stem from possible misalignment of vertex detectors, uncertainties in the IP profile, and corrections applied to charged track trajectories. These uncertainties are estimated using the same methodology as outlined in Refs.
5
;

Summary

We report the measurement of time-dependent
C\!P
violation parameters in
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}\gamma
decays using
770\times 10^{6}
and
521\times 10^{6}

B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events from Belle and Belle II, respectively. The combined values of the
C\!P
violation parameters in the
K^{*0}(892)
region (
M_{K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}}\in[0.8,1.0]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
) are

S=0.09\pm 0.16\pm 0.02,

and

C=-0.09\pm 0.08\pm 0.04.

Similarly, the combined values of the
C\!P
violation parameters in the non-
K^{*0}(892)
region (
M_{K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}}\in(1.0,1.8]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
) are

S=-0.32\pm 0.33\pm 0.09,

and

C=-0.07\pm 0.17\pm 0.08.

These results represent the most precise measurements to date for
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}\gamma
, improving upon the combination of the previous Belle
43
and Belle II
4
results by approximately 24% for
S
and 31% for
C
in the
K^{*0}(892)
region, and supersede the earlier measurements
43
;
4
for the same channel. These findings are consistent with the predictions of the Standard Model.
