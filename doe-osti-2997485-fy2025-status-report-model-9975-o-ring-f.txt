 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Leak testing experiments to monitor the aging performance of Viton® GLT and GLT-S O-rings used in the
model 9975 shipping package have been ongoing since 2004 at Savannah River National Laboratory.
Seventy tests using mock-up 9975 primary containment vessels (PCVs) with GLT O-rings were assembled
and heated to temperatures ranging from 200 to 450 °F. Due to material substitution, fourteen tests with
GLT-S O-rings were initiated in 2008 and heated to temperatures ranging from 200 to 400 °F. The
conditioning temperatures are elevated compared to the calculated maximum O-ring temperature in a 9975
package in storage, 158 °F, to accelerate aging and enable observations of O-ring failures in a reasonable
time frame.

The mock-up PCV fixtures are leak tested periodically, and all GLT O-ring fixtures aged at 350 °F or above
have failed to maintain a leak-tight seal. Eight GLT O-ring fixtures aged at 300 °F have failed after 2.8 to
5.7 years at temperature while the remaining fixtures at 300 °F were retired from testing following more
than five years of aging without failure. Two of these retired fixtures were returned to testing and heated to
350 °F to evaluate the impact of additional heating at higher temperature for aged O-rings. Fixture #20
failed after 3 months while fixture #18 failed after 9 months at 350 °F. These O-rings demonstrated that
aged and in-service O-rings can continue to be used, even at higher temperatures, after being in storage,
and their leak performance are consistent with other samples at 350 °F.

There has been one GLT O-ring fixture which failed after 13.4 years of aging at 200 °F. However, 20 other
GLT O-rings aging at 200 °F have remained leak-tight for over 16.9 years and remain in test. There are two
GLT O-ring fixtures at 270 °F; one fixture has failed after 12.9 years while the other fixture remains in test
after 12.5 years. All GLT-S O-ring fixtures aged at 300 °F or above have failed their leak test. No failures
have yet been observed in GLT-S O-ring fixtures aging at 250 °F for 14.9 years, while one GLT-S O-ring
fixture failed after 12.4 years at 200 °F.

The leak testing data to date suggest the GLT and GLT-S O-rings aging in the K-Area Complex (KAC)
storage at temperatures of 158 °F might maintain a leak-tight seal for up to 59 years. Data from the O-ring
fixtures are generally consistent with results from compression stress-relaxation testing and provide
confidence in the predictive models based on those results. However, uncertainty exists in extrapolating
these elevated temperature results to the lower temperatures of interest for normal storage in KAC. The
collective data from these test efforts suggest the minimum O-ring service life at KAC normal storage
conditions should be at least 34 years for GLT and GLT-S O-rings.

Measurement of compression set in O-rings removed from failed fixtures, compared to that from KAC
surveillance O-rings, indicate significant margin remains for O-rings still in service in 9975 packages in
KAC. Aging and periodic leak testing will continue for the remaining 24 mock-up PCV fixtures.

1.0 Introduction
Special nuclear materials are stored within 9975 shipping packages in the K-Area Complex (KAC) at the
Savannah River Site (SRS). The 9975 shipping package consists of a 35 gallon stainless steel drum,
Celotex® fiberboard insulation, lead shield, and primary and secondary containment vessels. The 9975
shipping package design, performance, and analysis for safe transport of radioactive material are described
in the Safety Analysis Report for Packaging (SARP) [1]. The primary and secondary containment vessels
(PCV and SCV) are sealed with dual Viton® GLT or GLT-S fluoroelastomer O-rings. The outer O-rings
are credited with being leak-tight while in transport. The 3013 container is credited with providing
containment of special nuclear materials while stored inside a 9975 package. For non-3013 containers
stored in a 9975 package, the containment vessel outer O-rings are credited with maintaining containment
[2]. The SRS Surveillance Program monitors material performance to establish a basis for service life and
ensures the continued integrity and safe storage of 9975 packages [3-4].

O-ring seals were identified as susceptible to aging and degradation under storage conditions, and
experiments to monitor the aging performance of GLT O-rings have been ongoing since 2004 at Savannah
River National Laboratory (SRNL). Due to product discontinuation, the O-rings were substituted with
GLT-S polymers and tests with GLT-S O-rings were initiated in 2008. The experiments include leak testing,
compression stress-relaxation (CSR) and oxygen consumption testing [5-6]. These experiments evaluate
the performance of O-rings conditioned at elevated temperatures to predict their service life in 9975
packages stored in KAC [7-8]. The sealing performance of O-rings is evaluated by periodically leak testing
mock-up PCV fixtures after conditioning at elevated temperatures. Laboratory tests and service life
estimates supported increasing the approved storage life for 9975 packages from the initial 10 year storage
life. The 9975 shipping package is currently approved for a storage period of 40 years with 3013 containers
and 20 years with non-3013 containers in KAC [9-11].

Laboratory testing and 9975 package surveillance activities to support the 9975 Surveillance Program are
described in reference 8. Long-term testing of O-ring leak performance and experimental results of Viton®
O-rings through July 2024 were reported in reference [12]. This report presents the data collected since the
previous status report through July 2025.

2.0 Experimental Procedure
2.1 Fixture assembly and conditioning
Test fixtures were assembled with either Parker Seals V0835-75 (Viton® GLT) O-rings or Parker Seals
VM835-75 (Viton® GLT-S) O-rings in a mock-up PCV. The assembly and setup have been described in
detail in previous reports [13-14]. The two-piece lid of the mock-up PCV was machined to be identical to
the actual PCV lid used in the 9975 shipping package. The body of the mock-up PCV is 3.5 inches tall with
a thread hole machined in the bottom to provide a port for evacuating and filling the vessel with gas. A
PCV test fixture with the dual O-rings installed in the lid is shown in Figure 1a. The mock-up PCV fixtures
were assembled per the requirements described in the SARP [1]. The fixture is heated with a flexible heater
and capped with insulation at the exposed ends. The assembled fixture is shown in Figure 1b. The assembled
fixture was leak tested initially to confirm leak-tightness before conditioning at 200, 250, 270, 300, 350,
400, or 450 °F.

2.2 Irradiation
If the fixture required irradiation per test matrix, it was placed in a Co-60 gamma cell and irradiated at one
of two dose rates to reach a total dose of 2 E5 rad. This is equivalent to a ten year dose at the bounding dose
rate expected for the PCV O-rings (2 rad/hr). The fixture was irradiated at either a low dose rate of
approximately 667 to 830 rad/hr (lasting ~240 hours) or a higher rate of ~1.7 E5 rad/hr (lasting 72 minutes),
as Viton® and other polymers are known to be sensitive to dose rate. After irradiation, the fixture was leak
tested again to ensure that irradiation alone did not affect leak-tightness and then heated to test temperature.

Additional irradiations were performed in 2014, 2018, and 2022 at the higher dose rate to maintain an
approximate balance relevant to storage conditions between the thermal and radiation degradation
mechanisms. The fixtures were leak tested pre- and post-irradiation. Three fixtures (#9, 41, 42) received an
additional 2 E5 rad and will continue to receive an additional 2 E5 rad every four years, and three other
fixtures (#53, 54, 55) received an additional 1 E5 rad and will continue to receive an additional 1 E5 rad
every four years. As of August 2025, three fixtures have a total dose of 8 E5 rad and three fixtures have a
total dose of 5 E5 rad.

2.3 Leak Testing
The O-ring fixtures are leak-tested after initial assembly, after irradiation, and periodically after
conditioning at elevated temperatures to the same leak-tight criterion as the 9975 PCV and SCV O-rings.
The fixtures are leak tested per American National Standards Institute (ANSI) standard N14.5 at room
temperature, generally between 68-77 °F, using a Varian 959 helium mass spectrometer leak detector [15].
Fixtures with leakage rates less than 1 E-7 ref·cm3/sec air (or 2 E-7 ref·cm3/sec He) are considered leak-
tight. A helium gas filled envelope test, as defined in ANSI N14.5-2022, is used for the mock-up PCV
fixtures. Both O-rings are tested simultaneously with failure of either O-ring causing a failure of the test.
Although this approach differs from annual certification testing, it gives results that are valid and
comparable and accommodates the difference in set up of the actual PCV and SCV and mock-up PCV
fixture. If a leak is found, it is possible to determine which O-ring (i.e., inner and/or outer O-ring) is leaking
by selectively directing helium to either the interior or exterior of the fixture.

2.4 Test Matrix
Eighty-four fixtures have been assembled and evaluated since 2004 in three test matrices. The first test
matrix was developed to determine the importance and effect of several variables on the condition of the
GLT O-rings over time inside the KAC storage facility. The variables investigated include O-ring
temperature, radiation/dose rate, and O-ring lubrication, and are detailed in Table A-1. There are 62 fixtures
and 22 separate sets of conditions in the first test matrix. Fixtures for the second test matrix were assembled
using GLT O-rings in 2008 and aged at temperatures ranging from 350 to 450 °F to provide some O-ring

In March 2024, two GLT O-ring fixtures, #18 and #20, leak tight and unopened after >5 years at 300 °F
and 11.7 years at room temperature, were returned to testing. The fixtures were heated at 350 °F to evaluate
the impact of additional heating at higher temperature for aged O-rings. Fixtures #18 and #20 failed after
0.7 and 0.2 years, respectively, at 350 °F. The examination of fixture #18 is discussed in this report while
the results for fixture #20 were previously reported [12].

Two fixtures conditioned at 200 °F failed after 12.4 and 13.4 years for GLT-S and GLT O-rings,
respectively [21-22]. All of the other 23 O-ring fixtures aging at 200 °F or 250 °F have remained leak-tight,
with total times at temperature surpassing 14.9 years; the majority of these fixtures remain leak-tight after
17 to 18.5 years (Table 3). One fixture, #21W, failed after 12.9 years at 270 °F. GLT and GLT-S O-rings
in the remaining fixtures aged at ≥300 °F failed or were retired. In 2012, eight fixtures were removed from
testing after five years at 300 °F as their leak performance had exceeded the times to failures of several
300 °F samples as reported in Table 2. In 2024, two of the retired fixtures, #18 and #20, were taken out of
retirement and conditioned at 350 °F to assess the time to failure for previously in service (used and aged)
O-rings compared to new O-rings. Leak rate histories can be found in Table 3 for fixtures in test since the
last status report in July 2024.

The dual O-rings in fixture #18 maintained leak-tightness after 5.2 years at 300 °F and 11.7 years of storage
at room temperature. The fixture was conditioned at 350 °F and leak tested monthly. The O-rings were
leak-tight after 216 days but failed after 250 days at 350 °F (Table 2). Neither O-ring would successfully
pass the leak test. For new O-rings conditioned at 350 °F, the times to failure ranged from 114-594 days
(Table 2). The aged O-rings performed remarkably well compared to new and pristine O-rings at 350 °F.
The O-rings in fixture #18 demonstrated that in-service and leak-tight O-rings can continue to be used, even
at higher temperatures, after being in storage. The O-rings in fixture #18 performed better than those in
fixture #20 which failed after 5.9 years at 300 °F and 73 days at 350 °F [12].

Fixture #18 was opened to examine the aged O-rings. The O-rings showed no discernable spring back
within 5 minutes of opening the fixture as shown in Figure 2a. Small white pieces of solidified silicone
high-vacuum grease were on the O-rings (Figure 2d). White grease residue was previously observed on
other opened fixtures. The O-rings were removed, and subsequent dimensional measurements were used to
calculate the compression set. Compression set measures the amount of O-ring deformation after removal
of force and was calculated using ASTM D395, Method B, as:
 compression set (%) = (ti-tf) / (ti – groove depth) * 100
where ti and tf are the initial and final radial thicknesses, respectively. The groove depth for all fixtures is
0.0995 inch. A new O-ring should have a compression set of approximately 0% while a heavily degraded
O-ring will have a compression set approaching 100%. Within thirty minutes after opening fixture #18, the
compression set was 97% for both the inner and outer O-rings. Compression set values remained at 97%
after one week. These compression set values are similar to those of failed O-rings aged at 300 °F or 350 °F
as shown in Figure 3.

Fixture #18 was challenging to open and white residue was observed coming up through the threads during
the opening process. The O-rings were difficult to remove from the plug grooves due to being adherent to
the plug. The O-rings were black with flattened surfaces, and both O-rings had a section with a brown stain
(Figure 2c). There were stains on the plug grooves and fixture body, likely a combination of oxidized grease
and material transferred from the O-rings, that was darker than typical for failed fixtures (Figure A-2).

The first fixture conditioned at 270 °F to fail after 12.9 years, #21W, was opened to examine the aged GLT
O-rings. The O-rings showed no discernable spring back within 6 minutes of opening the fixture as shown
in Figure 4a. Within 20 minutes after opening fixture #21W, the compression set was 87% and 84% for the
inner and outer O-rings, respectively. Compression set values slightly decreased to 84% and 83% after one

 5

week. The compression set values are consistent with other failed O-rings aged at 200 °F or above
(Figure 3). Additional observations of fixture #21W include the O-rings were black, flattened, and had
white grease residue (Figure 4). No defects were visually observed on the O-rings. There were minimal
stains on the plug and fixture body (Figure A-3). The other GLT O-rings aging at 270 °F remain in test after
passing the leak test after 12.5 years.

Figure 5 depicts the time at temperature for fixtures that failed the leak test and those that are still in test. A
trendline provides a lower bound to the failure data to illustrate a potential extrapolation of the observed
behavior. The wide range of scatter in the leak failure data indicate there is significant uncertainty for
extrapolations and degradation rates. Fixture #18 was the second sample, after #20, to fail after being
conditioned at two different temperatures. The time to failure for the O-rings in fixture #18 was after 1,912
days at 300 °F and 250 days at 350 °F. Ideally, degradation rates would be used to correlate degradation at
one temperature to another (e.g., 1,912 days at 300 °F corresponds to X days at 350 °F) but there is high
uncertainty in this method due to the variance in failure data. Hence, fixture #18 was estimated by
conservatively assuming that the degradation from conditioning at 300 °F for 1,912 days was comparable
to conditioning at 350 °F for 63 days (lower bound trendline value for 350 °F). This assumption was utilized
as the time to failure for O-ring samples conditioned at 300 °F and 350 °F exceeded the lower bound
trendline values of 217 and 63 days, respectively. Therefore, it is conservative to assume that the
degradation from time spent at 300 °F for fixture #18, which remained leak-tight after conditioning at
300 °F well in excess of 217 days, corresponds to a minimal of 63 days at 350 °F. Thus, the minimal total
time to failure for fixture #18 at 350 °F is (63 +250) 313 days. This result is within the range of leak failures
for O-rings at a constant temperature of 350 °F (Figure 5). Note that fixture #20 failed after 2,152 days at
300 °F and 73 days at 350 °F, or conservatively, (63+73) 136 days at 350 °F. The leak performance of
fixtures #18 and #20 with varying elevated temperatures are consistent with other samples at a constant
temperature of 350 °F (Figure 5).

No influence on time to failure has been observed from the irradiations performed on test fixtures. Six
fixtures received additional irradiation in November 2022 and will continue to receive an additional dose
every four years. As of July 2025, three fixtures have a total dose of 8 E5 rad and three fixtures have a total
dose of 5 E5 rad (Table 3). It is recognized that these irradiations present a simplistic approach that ignores
potential synergistic effects from the simultaneous exposure to elevated temperature and radiation.

Recent thermal analysis of the 9975 package used 95 °F as the long-term average ambient temperature in
KAC [23]; based on the storage temperature history of KAC, 95 °F is a conservatively bounding average
ambient temperature [24]. For a package with a maximum heat load of 19 W, the calculated O-ring
temperature is 156 °F at beginning of life and 158 °F after 20 years of service with bounding package
degradation [23]. O-rings aging at 250 °F and 270 °F have exceeded the lower bound trendline in Figure 5;
these O-rings have been conditioning at temperature for over 12.5 years. These O-rings demonstrate that
the trendline maintains a lower bound to leak test data at least to 250 °F. Furthermore, there are 21 (GLT
and GLT-S) O-ring fixtures currently aging at 200 °F that have maintained a leak-tight seal for at least
15.4 years, with many fixtures leak-tight for over 18 years. The degree of scatter in the data would suggest
significant uncertainty for extrapolations over a large temperature range. Nevertheless, this trendline
suggests the possibility that O-rings aging at 158 °F (KAC storage temperature) might maintain a leak-tight
seal for up to 59 years.

Figure 6 shows the lower bound trendline from leak testing results and predictions from compression stress-
relaxation (CSR) data which utilized a time-temperature superposition approach and CSR failure criterion
of 90% loss of initial counterforce [5]. The dashed lines extrapolate the O-ring behavior through
temperatures for which partial data exist (i.e., samples have not yet reached a failure point). Given the range
of scatter observed in both leak testing and CSR testing, it is recommended that any extrapolated service
life estimates not exceed the minimum of these projections. CSR results indicate GLT and GLT-S O-rings
will perform their required function in KAC storage conditions for at least 34 years. As additional data are
collected, and the aging models refined, these service life estimates may be revised. However, at present,
both GLT and GLT-S O-rings are expected to provide leak-tight seals well in excess of the current approved
20 year KAC service life for 9975 packages with non-3013 containers.

Compression set measures the amount of O-ring deformation after removal of force and was calculated
using ASTM D395, Method B. A new O-ring should have a compression set of approximately 0% while a
heavily degraded O-ring will have a compression set approaching 100%. The compression set of PCV
O-rings in 9975 packages in KAC were measured previously during field surveillance and presented in
Figure 3 along with the values from failed leak testing O-ring fixtures. There is clear separation in
compression set values for surveillance O-rings and failed fixture O-rings; the latter have compression set
values greater than 50%. While there are no data to specifically show how long it will take at storage
conditions for the compression set to increase to that seen from failed test fixtures, if surveillance O-rings
continue to show similar behavior, they likely retain significant margin to reaching a failure condition.

4.0 Conclusions
The leak performance of GLT and GLT-S O-rings were evaluated in mock-up PCV fixtures between 200
to 450 °F. Fixtures for GLT and GLT-S O-rings aged at 300 °F and above have experienced leak test failures
or have been removed from test. Two of the formerly retired and leak-tight fixtures aged at 300 °F were
recently returned to testing and heated to 350 °F. Fixture #20 failed after 3 months of testing while fixture
#18 failed after 9 months. These results were within the range of time to leak failures for O-rings
conditioned at 350 °F. These were These O-rings demonstrated that used and in-service O-rings can
continue to be used, even at higher temperatures, after being in storage, and their leak performance are in
line with other samples at 350 °F.

There have been two observed leak test failures for samples aged at 200 °F after 12.4 years and 13.4 years
for GLT-S and GLT O-rings, respectively. However, 21 other GLT and GLT-S O-ring fixtures aging at
200 °F have maintained a leak-tight seal for over 14 years, with most over 17.5 years, and remain in test.
For GLT-S O-rings, no failures have been observed for fixtures aging at 250 °F for 14.9 years. This year,
there was one fixture which failed after 12.9 years at 270 °F . The remaining 24 fixtures remain conditioning
at elevated temperatures and will be periodically leak tested.

 11

The PCV O-ring maximum temperature in KAC is 156-158 °F based on an average ambient temperature
of 95 °F and maximum payload of 19 W. The leak testing data to date suggest the GLT and GLT-S O-rings
aging in KAC storage at temperatures of 158 °F might maintain a leak-tight seal for up to 59 years. Further
consideration should be given to results from CSR testing which predict a service life of up to 34 years.
Analysis of compression set measurements of O-rings removed during KAC surveillance, compared to
failed O-rings from laboratory testing, indicate that significant margin remains for O-rings still in service
in KAC. Both GLT and GLT-S O-rings are expected to provide leak-tight service in excess of the current
approved 20 year KAC storage life for 9975 packages with non-3013 containers.

Aging and periodic leak testing will continue for the remaining 24 fixtures.
