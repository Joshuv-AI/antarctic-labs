Unilateral Pneumocystis-associated immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome: A case illustrated
Authors: Reiko Masubuchi, Akira Kawashima, Katsuji Teruya, Hiroyuki Gatanaga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A man in his 50 s presented with severe Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP) with newly diagnosed advanced HIV infection. Initial chest computed tomography (CT) revealed diffuse bilateral ground-glass opacities that improved after standard therapy. Seven days after antiretroviral therapy initiation, fever, dyspnea, and hypoxemia recurred, and repeat CT revealed new predominantly left-sided reticular and fibrosis-like opacities. Paradoxical PCP-associated immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) was diagnosed based on the clinical course and lack of a more likely alternative diagnosis. This case expands the radiologic spectrum of PCP-associated IRIS by demonstrating a markedly asymmetric, fibrosis-like presentation following the apparent resolution of bilateral PCP.

A man in his 50 s presented with a two-week history of progressive dyspnea and was hospitalized for severe hypoxemia. Chest computed tomography (CT) on admission showed diffuse bilateral ground-glass opacities with peripheral sparing (Fig. 1A). HIV screening was positive and subsequently confirmed by positive HIV RNA testing. A CD4 count was 5/μL (reference >500/μL), saliva P. jirovecii PCR was 200,000 copies/mL (reference <200 copies/mL), and β-D-glucan was 194 pg/mL (reference <40 pg/mL), consistent with severe Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP). Trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole and systemic corticosteroids were initiated. After three days of 1000 mg methylprednisolone pulse therapy, the patient’s respiratory symptoms and oxygenation improved. Corticosteroids were gradually tapered and discontinued, and a 21-day course of trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole was completed. Because of a generalized drug eruption, secondary prophylaxis was switched to atovaquone. Antiretroviral therapy (ART) with bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide was initiated 28 days after admission. Seven days later, fever, dyspnea, and hypoxemia recurred. Repeat CT revealed new predominantly left-sided reticular and fibrosis-like opacities (Fig. 1C). Given the temporal association with ART initiation, previously documented PCP, and negative microbiological and viral testing for alternative infections, paradoxical PCP-associated immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) was diagnosed. Atovaquone was continued, and prednisolone 40 mg/day was initiated and gradually tapered over 53 days, resulting in symptomatic and radiologic improvement without recurrence at the 6-month follow-up.

Discussion

PCP is a common opportunistic infections in people living with HIV [1]; however, PCP-associated IRIS develops in only 5–12% of patients after ART initiation [2]. Typically, it occurs in patients with PCP with bilateral, symmetrical ground-glass or reticular opacities [3]. However, its radiologic spectrum includes uncommon unilateral or asymmetric infiltrates, nodular lesions, and cystic changes [3].

Radiologic descriptions of PCP‑associated IRIS usually remains limited. Most reported cases demonstrate recurrent ground-glass or consolidative abnormalities within previously affected lung fields [4]. Although unilateral PCP-associated IRIS has occasionally been described [5], [6], predominantly unilateral fibrosis-like opacities with cystic change following apparent resolution of bilateral PCP appear to be distinctly uncommon.

The fibrosis-like pattern observed in our patient differs from the more typical recurrent ground-glass pattern and may represent an exaggerated organizing or fibroproliferative inflammatory response rather than recurrent infection. Although similar immune-mediated tissue remodeling has been proposed in tuberculosis-associated IRIS [7], the mechanisms underlying PCP-associated IRIS remain uncertain.

Owing to the lack of validated diagnostic criteria, the diagnosis of PCP-associated IRIS remains clinical and requires careful exclusion of more likely alternative causes. Management of PCP-associated IRIS generally includes continuation of ART and anti-PCP therapy. Adjunctive corticosteroids are commonly used for clinically significant inflammatory organ involvement; however, the optimal dose and tapering schedule remain undefined [8].

This case expands the radiologic spectrum of PCP-associated IRIS by demonstrating that immune reconstitution may present with markedly asymmetric fibrosis-like pulmonary abnormalities after the apparent resolution of bilateral PCP. Recognition of this atypical presentation may facilitate timely diagnosis and appropriate corticosteroid treatment while avoiding unnecessary escalation of antimicrobial therapy.
