A Weil Sum Approach to Permutation Polynomials over Quadratic Extensions of Finite Fields
Bidushi Sharma, DHIREN KUMAR BASNET*

Abstract
. In this article, we introduce several classes of permutation polynomials over 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} . More precisely, we characterize permutation polynomials of the forms x q + b ​ x 2 + c ​ x + d x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d and x q + 1 + b ​ x q + c ​ x + d x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d over 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} . To this end, we determine the exact number of zeros of these polynomials using existing results on certain special Weil sums. We also present the compositional inverses of the permutation polynomials obtained in this paper.

1.
Introduction

A permutation polynomial (PP) over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a polynomial that induces a bijection on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Permutation polynomials have attracted the interest of many researchers due to their wide applications in coding theory
[
11
,
6
]
, cryptography
[
12
,
13
]
, and combinatorial design
[
7
]
. For a comprehensive survey of recent advancements on this topic, we refer the readers to
[
8
,
9
,
14
,
17
]
.

The central idea used in this paper is that a polynomial
f
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if the equation
f(x)=t
has exactly one solution in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Equivalently, for every
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, the polynomial
f(x)+t
has exactly one zero in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Thus, in the first part of this article, we focus on determining the exact number of zeros of certain special polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. More precisely, we study polynomials of the form
x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d
and
x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d
. Note that none of the polynomials considered here are of Wan-Lidl type, that is, of the form
x^{s}f(x^{r})
with
s,r\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\mid(q-1)
, nor they are affine
q
-polynomial. It is worth noting that the polynomial
x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d
, over fields of even characteristic, behaves as an affine
2
-polynomial, however, it is not affine
q
-polynomial.

An important tool in determining the number of zeros of polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is the Weil sum. A Weil sum is a character sum over a finite field in which the character is evaluated at a polynomial argument. In our case, we use Weil sums associated with the canonical additive character, namely

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(f(x)\big),

where
\chi
is the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}.

Let
M
denote the number of zeros of
f(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then

M=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(yf(x)\big),

which provides a convenient tool for determining
M
. Using character sums to study permutation behavior has been employed in various works to study permutation polynomials and permutation rational functions. For instance, in
[
10
]
, the permutation behavior of affine
q
-polynomials of the form

f(x)=a_{t}x^{q^{t}}+a_{t-1}x^{q^{t-1}}+\cdots+a_{1}x^{q}+a_{0}x+a

is investigated. In
[
2
]
, over fields of even characteristic, the permutation behavior of polynomials of the form
\operatorname{Tr}(Ax^{q+1})+L(x)
, where
L
is
q
-linearized and
A\neq 0
, is studied. Further applications of this method can be found in
[
1
]
.

In Section 2 of this article, we collect all the preliminary results required for our work.Here
\operatorname{Tr}
and
\operatorname{N}
denotes the trace and norm maps from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In Section 3, we begin by determining the number of roots of the polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and conclude by listing explicit cases in which it permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and finding their inverses.

2.
Preliminaries

In Lemmas
2.1
–
2.4
, we present all the Weil sums that arise in the process of determining the number of roots over both odd and even ordered fields.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
1
]

Let
q
be odd,
\chi
denote the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, and let
\eta
denote the quadratic character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Let
A,B,C\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with
B\neq 0
, and define
D=\operatorname{Tr}(A)^{2}-4N(B)
. If
D\neq 0
, then

\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(Aw^{q+1}+Bw^{2}+Cw\big)=-\eta(D)\,q\,\chi\big(-A\theta^{q+1}-B\theta^{2}\big),

where

\theta=\frac{-\operatorname{Tr}(A)C^{q}+2B^{q}C}{\operatorname{Tr}(A)^{2}-4N(B)},

that is,
\theta
is the unique root of the equation
\operatorname{Tr}(A)x^{q}+2Bx+C=0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
5
]

Let
q
be even and
\chi
denote the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Let
A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with
A\neq 0
. Then

\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(Aw^{q+1}+Bw\big)=\begin{cases}q^{2},&\text{if }A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\text{ and }B=0,\\
0,&\text{if }A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\text{ and }B\neq 0,\\
-q\,\chi\left(\dfrac{AB^{q+1}}{\operatorname{Tr}(A)^{2}}\right),&\text{if }A\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.1
.

A nonzero polynomial
C(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x]
of degree
n
is called a self-conjugate reciprocal polynomial if

x^{n}C^{(q)}\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\beta C(x)

for some
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Here,
C^{(q)}
denotes the polynomial obtained by raising each coefficient of
C(x)
to its
q
-th power.

A comprehensive account of SCR polynomials is given in
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
8
]

Let
q
be even and
C(x)=\alpha x^{2}+\beta x+\alpha^{q}

be a self-conjugate reciprocal polynomial, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Let
\mu_{q+1}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}:x^{q+1}=1\}.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

C(x)
has at least one root in
\mu_{q+1}
.

(2)

C(x)
has two distinct roots in
\mu_{q+1}
.

(3)

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\!\left(\dfrac{\alpha^{q+1}}{\beta^{2}}\right)=1.

The following two lemmas help us in finding the inverse of the permutation polynomials we construct in this paper.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
18
]

Let
L(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}X^{q^{i}}
be a
q
-linearized permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, and let
D_{L}
be its associated Dickson matrix. Then

L^{-1}(X)=(\det D_{L})^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\bar{\alpha_{i}}X^{q^{i}},

where
\bar{\alpha_{i}}
is the
(i,0)
-th cofactor of the matrix
D_{L}
. The Dickson matrix
D_{L}
is given by

D_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}&\alpha_{n-1}^{q}&\alpha_{n-2}^{q^{2}}&\cdots&\alpha_{1}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{0}^{q}&\alpha_{n-1}^{q^{2}}&\cdots&\alpha_{2}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{1}^{q}&\alpha_{0}^{q^{2}}&\cdots&\alpha_{3}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{n-1}&\alpha_{n-2}^{q}&\alpha_{n-3}^{q^{2}}&\cdots&\alpha_{0}^{q^{n-1}}\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 2.7
.

[
16
]

Consider any polynomial
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[x]
, any additive polynomials
\phi,\psi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[x]
, any
q
-polynomial
\overline{\psi}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[x]
satisfying
\phi\circ\psi=\overline{\psi}\circ\phi
and
|\psi(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})|=|\overline{\psi}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})|
, and any polynomial
h\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[x]
such that

h(\psi(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}))\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}.

Let

f(x)=h(\psi(x))\,\phi(x)+g(\psi(x))\text{ and }\bar{f}(x)=h(x)\phi(x)+\overline{\psi}(g(x)).
Assume that
f
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, and further assume that
|S_{\psi}|=|S_{\overline{\psi}}|
and

\ker(\phi)\cap\psi(S_{\psi})=\{0\}
. Then
\phi
induces a bijection from
S_{\psi}
to
S_{\overline{\psi}}
. Let
\bar{f}^{-1},\,\phi^{-1}\!\big|_{S_{\overline{\psi}}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[x]
induce the inverses of
\bar{f}\!\big|_{\psi(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})}
and
\phi\!\big|_{S_{\psi}}
, respectively. Then the compositional inverse of
f
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is given by

f^{-1}(x)=\bar{f}^{-1}(\overline{\psi}(x))+\phi^{-1}\!\big|_{S_{\overline{\psi}}}\left(\frac{x-\overline{\psi}(x)-g(\bar{f}^{-1}(\overline{\psi}(x)))+\overline{\psi}(g(\bar{f}^{-1}(\overline{\psi}(x))))}{h(\bar{f}^{-1}(\overline{\psi}(x)))}\right).

Furthermore, if
\phi
induces a bijection from
\psi(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
to
\overline{\psi}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
, then
\phi
is a permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and the compositional inverse of
f
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is given by

f^{-1}(x)=\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{x-g(\bar{f}^{-1}(\overline{\psi}(x)))}{h(\bar{f}^{-1}(\overline{\psi}(x)))}\right).

3.
Main Results

Notation

Throughout this section, we use the following notation:

•

\psi
denotes the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

•

\chi
denotes the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, defined by
\chi=\psi\circ\operatorname{Tr}
, where
\operatorname{Tr}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the trace map given by
\operatorname{Tr}(x):=x^{q}+x.

•

\eta
denotes the quadratic character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.

•

\eta^{\prime}
denotes the quadratic character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
, defined by
\eta^{\prime}(x)=\eta(x^{q+1}).

•

\mu_{q+1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}:x^{q+1}=1\}
, the set of
(q+1)
-th roots of unity.

•

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}

denotes the absolute trace function from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

We first explicitly determine the number of zeros of the polynomials under consideration. In particular, Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
are employed to analyze the polynomial
x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d
for odd
q
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
q=p^{k}
and let
c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then
M
, the number of zeroes of
x^{q}+cx+d

in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is given by

M=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }c\notin\mu_{q+1},\\[2.84526pt]
q,&\text{if }c\in\mu_{q+1}\text{ and }d=cd^{q},\\[2.84526pt]
0,&\text{if }c\in\mu_{q+1}\text{ and }d\neq cd^{q}.\end{cases}

Proof.

\displaystyle Mq^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uf(w)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi(uw^{q}+cuw)\chi(du)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big((u^{q}+cu)w\big)\chi(du)\quad\text{(since $\chi(u)=\chi(u^{q})$ for all $u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}$)}

\displaystyle=q^{2}+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big((u^{q}+cu)w\big).

First consider the case
c\notin\mu_{q+1}
. Then for any
u\neq 0
, we have

u^{q}+cu\neq 0,

and hence
Mq^{2}=q^{2}.

Now suppose that
c\in\mu_{q+1}
, and let
N
be the set of zeros of the
q
-polynomial
u^{q}+cu
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then
N
is a
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of dimension
1
, i.e.,

N=\{\alpha u:u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},
where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
satisfies

\alpha^{q}+c\alpha=0.

Again,

\displaystyle Mq^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}\cap N}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}1+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}\setminus N}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big((u^{q}+cu)w\big)

\displaystyle=q^{2}+q^{2}\sum_{u\in N\setminus\{0\}}\chi(du)

\displaystyle=q^{2}+q^{2}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(d\alpha u)

\displaystyle=q^{2}+q^{2}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\psi\big(u\,\operatorname{Tr}(d\alpha)\big)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}q^{2}+q^{2}(q-1),&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(d\alpha)=0,\\[2.84526pt]
q^{2}-q^{2},&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(d\alpha)\neq 0.\end{cases}

Now, since
\alpha^{q}=-c\alpha
, we have
\operatorname{Tr}(d\alpha)=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad d=cd^{q}.

Therefore, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q
be odd,
b,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with
b\neq 0
, and
\alpha
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then the number of zeros of

x^{q}+bx^{2}+d
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is

1-\eta(-b^{q+1})\sum_{i\in K}(-1)^{i},

where

K=\{\,i\mid 0\leq i\leq q,\ h(\alpha^{i(q-1)})=0\,\},
and
h(x)=b^{q}x^{3}-4b^{q+1}d^{q}x^{2}-4b^{q+1}dx+b
.

Proof.

Let
M
denote the number of zeros of
f(x)=x^{q}+bx^{2}+d
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle Mq^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uf(w)\big)

\displaystyle=\text{
}q^{2}+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(buw^{2}+u^{q}w\big).

Applying Lemma
2.1
to the inner sum, we have
D=-4b^{q+1}u^{q+1}=0
if and only if
u=0
. Hence, for
u\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(buw^{2}+u^{q}w\big)

\displaystyle=-\eta(-4b^{q+1}u^{q+1})\,q\,\chi\big(-bu\theta_{u}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=-\eta(b^{q+1})\,q\,\chi\left(-\frac{u^{2q-1}}{4b}\right)\eta^{\prime}(u),

where
\theta_{u}=-\dfrac{u^{q}}{2bu}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle Mq^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}-q\eta(-b^{q+1})\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}}\chi\left(\frac{4bdu-u^{2q-1}}{4b}\right)\eta^{\prime}(u).

Now every nonzero element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
can be written in the form
\alpha^{i}u
, where
0\leq i\leq q
and
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Also,

\eta^{\prime}(\alpha^{i}u)=\eta^{\prime}(\alpha)^{i}.

Since
\alpha
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, we have

\eta^{\prime}(\alpha)=-1.

Hence,

\displaystyle Mq^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}-\eta(-b^{q+1})q\sum_{i=0}^{q}(-1)^{i}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi\left(\frac{4bd\alpha^{i}-(\alpha^{i})^{2q-1}}{4b}u\right)

\displaystyle=q^{2}-\eta(-b^{q+1})q\sum_{i=0}^{q}(-1)^{i}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\psi\left(u\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{4bd\alpha^{i}-(\alpha^{i})^{2q-1}}{4b}\right)\right).

Let
K\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,q\}
such that

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\dfrac{4bd\alpha^{i}-(\alpha^{i})^{2q-1}}{4b}\right)=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad i\in K.

This gives

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\psi\left(u\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{4bd\alpha^{i}-(\alpha^{i})^{2q-1}}{4b}\right)\right)=\begin{cases}q-1,&\text{if }i\in K,\\
-1,&\text{if }i\notin K.\end{cases}

Hence,

\displaystyle Mq^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}-\eta(-b^{q+1})q\left[\sum_{i\in K}(-1)^{i}(q-1)-\sum_{i\notin K}(-1)^{i}\right]

\displaystyle=q^{2}-\eta(-b^{q+1})q^{2}\sum_{i\in K}(-1)^{i}.

Furthermore,

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{4bd\alpha^{i}-(\alpha^{i})^{2q-1}}{4b}\right)=0

if and only if

4b^{q+1}d\alpha^{i}-(\alpha^{i})^{2q-1}b^{q}+4b^{q+1}d^{q}\alpha^{iq}-(\alpha^{i})^{2-q}b=0,

which is equivalent to

4b^{q+1}d-b^{q}x^{2}+4b^{q+1}d^{q}x-bx^{-1}=0,

where
x=\alpha^{i(q-1)}
. Hence the result follows.
∎

In Lemma
3.2
, one might question how the evaluation of the character sum differs from that in
[
1
]
. We note that in
[
1
]
, in all cases the discriminant is of the form
D=(u^{q}-u)^{2}
, which implies that
D\neq 0
if and only if
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and in this case
\eta^{\prime}(D)=1
always.

However, in our setting,
D=0
if and only if
u=0
. Consequently, the evaluation of
\eta^{\prime}(D)
for
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
presents additional difficulties. To overcome this, we employ a different representation of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
than that used in
[
1
]
.

In Lemmas
3.3
-
3.4
, we consider the case where
q
is even for two different polynomials, while in Lemma
3.3
we treat the case where
q
is odd. Each problem is addressed using number theoretic arguments or purely character sum estimates.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
q=2^{k}
and
b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
b\neq 0
. Let
\chi
be the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Let
M
denote the number of zeroes of

f(x)=x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x]
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then the following hold

(1)

If
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
,
c=0
, and
k
is even, then

M=1+\chi(dx_{1}),

where
x_{1}
is the unique root of

x^{2q-1}=b

in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

(2)

If
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
,
c=0
, then

M=1+\chi(dx_{1})+\chi(dx_{2})+\chi(dx_{3}),

where
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
are the three distinct roots of
x^{2q-1}=b
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

(3)

If
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
c=1
, then

M=1.

(4)

If
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
with

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{(c+1)^{4}}\right)=0,

then

M=1+\chi\!\left(\dfrac{db}{(c+1)^{2}}\right).

(5)

If
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
with

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{(c+1)^{4}}\right)=1,

then

M=1+\chi\!\left(\frac{db}{(x_{1}+c)^{2}}\right)+\chi\!\left(\frac{db}{(x_{2}+c)^{2}}\right)+\chi\!\left(\frac{db}{(c+1)^{2}}\right),

where
x_{1},x_{2}
are the two distinct roots of

x^{2}+(c+1)^{2}x+1=0
in
\mu_{q+1}\setminus\{1\}
.

Proof.

We begin with

Mq^{2}=q^{2}+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\!\left(buw^{2}+(u^{q}+cu)w\right).

By Lemma
2.3
, the inner sum equals
q^{2}
if

u^{2q}+c^{2}u^{2}+bu=0,

and equals
0
otherwise. Hence, if we define

H=\left\{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}:u^{2q}+c^{2}u^{2}+bu=0\right\},

then

M=1+\sum_{u\in H}\chi(du).

Therefore, our next goal is to study the set
H
.
First suppose that
c=0
. Then we need to determine the solutions of

u^{2q-1}=b

in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and write

b=\alpha^{s}

for some integer
s
. We need to determine the number of integers
r
with

0\leq r\leq q^{2}-2

such that
u=\alpha^{r}
satisfies

\alpha^{r(2q-1)}=\alpha^{s},

i.e.,

r(2q-1)\equiv s\pmod{q^{2}-1}.
This congruence has a solution if and only if

\gcd(2q-1,q^{2}-1)\mid s,

and the number of such integers
r
is exactly

\gcd(2q-1,q^{2}-1).

Since
q=2^{k}
with
k
even, we have

\gcd(2q-1,q^{2}-1)=1\mid s.

Hence for every
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
, the equation

u^{2q-1}=b

has a unique solution
x_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
. Similarly, when
k
is odd, we have

\gcd(2q-1,q^{2}-1)=3.

Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\langle\alpha^{q+1}\rangle\subseteq\langle\alpha^{3}\rangle,

it follows that for every
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, the equation

u^{2q-1}=b

has exactly three distinct solutions
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}.

Now when
c=1
, we examine the number of zeroes of

u^{2q}+u^{2}+bu
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
Let

r=bu
. Therefore

u^{2q}+u^{2}=\operatorname{Tr}(u)^{2}=r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Substituting
u=r/b
, we obtain

\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\dfrac{r}{b}\right)^{2}=r.

Since
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, this yields

r=0.

Hence
u=0
and consequently
H=\phi
.

Now, let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
. Then
u^{2q-1}+c^{2}u+b=0\iff u^{2(q-1)}u+c^{2}u+b=0
. Let
u^{q-1}=r\in\mu_{q+1}
. We obtain
(r+c)^{2}u=b
and since
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
, we obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle u=\frac{b}{(r+c)^{2}}.

Equation
3.1
together with the fact that
u^{q-1}=r
, yields

\displaystyle r^{3}+c^{2}r^{2}+c^{2}r+1=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle(r+1)\bigl(r^{2}+(c+1)^{2}r+1\bigr)=0.

Notice that

r^{2}+(c+1)^{2}r+1\neq 0

when
r=1
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.5
, the polynomial

r^{2}+(c+1)^{2}r+1

has two distinct roots
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mu_{q+1}\setminus\{1\}
if

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\left(\dfrac{1}{(c+1)^{4}}\right)=1,

and has no root in
\mu_{q+1}\setminus\{1\}
otherwise. Since

u=\dfrac{b}{(r+c)^{2}},

the possible values of
u
satisfying
u^{2q-1}+c^{2}u+b=0
are

\frac{b}{(c+1)^{2}},\quad\frac{b}{(x_{1}+c)^{2}},\quad\frac{b}{(x_{2}+c)^{2}}.

Therefore, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q
be even,
b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then
M
, the number of zeros of

x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d

in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is given by

M=\begin{cases}2-|N|,&\text{if }b^{q}\neq c,\\[2.84526pt]
q+1-|N|,&\text{if }b^{q}=c\text{ and }\operatorname{Tr}(d)=0,\\[2.84526pt]
1-|N|,&\text{if }b^{q}=c\text{ and }\operatorname{Tr}(d)\neq 0,\end{cases}

where

N=\{\,i\mid 1\leq i\leq q,\ h(\alpha^{i(q-1)})=0\,\}

and

h(x)=(d^{q}+b^{q}c^{q})x^{2}+(d^{q}+d+b^{q+1}+c^{q+1})x+(d+bc).

Proof.

Proceeding as in Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle Mq^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}u^{q}+cu)w\big)

\displaystyle=q^{2}+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}+c)uw\big)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}u^{q}+cu)w\big).

When
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
b^{q}=c
, by Lemma
2.2
we have

\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}+c)uw\big)=q^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi(uw^{q+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(du)\,q^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\psi\big(u\,\operatorname{Tr}(d)\big)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}q^{2}(q-1),&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(d)=0,\\[2.84526pt]
-q^{2},&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(d)\neq 0.\end{cases}

When
b^{q}\neq c
, by Lemma
2.2
we have

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}+c)uw\big)=0.

Again, by Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}u^{q}+cu)w\big)=-q\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi\left(\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(u)^{2}du+u(b^{q}u^{q}+cu)^{q+1}}{\operatorname{Tr}(u)^{2}}\right).

Since every element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be written in the form
\alpha^{i}u
, where
1\leq i\leq q
and
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}u^{q}+cu)w\big)=

\displaystyle-q\sum_{i=1}^{q}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\chi\left(u\,\frac{d\alpha^{i}\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{i})^{2}+\alpha^{i}(b^{q}\alpha^{iq}+c\alpha^{i})^{q+1}}{\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{i})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-q\sum_{i=1}^{q}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\psi\left(u\operatorname{Tr}(f(\alpha^{i}))\right),

where
f(\alpha^{i})=\dfrac{d\alpha^{i}\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{i})^{2}+\alpha^{i}(b^{q}\alpha^{iq}+c\alpha^{i})^{q+1}}{\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^{i})^{2}}.

Let
N\subseteq\{1,\dots,q\}
be such that

\operatorname{Tr}(f(\alpha^{i}))=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad i\in N.

Therefore,

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\psi\big(u\,\operatorname{Tr}(f(\alpha^{i}))\big)=\begin{cases}q-1,&\text{if }i\in N,\\[2.84526pt]
-1,&\text{if }i\notin N.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{q}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\psi\big(u\,\operatorname{Tr}(f(\alpha^{i}))\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in N}(q-1)+\sum_{i\notin N}(-1)

\displaystyle=q(|N|-1).

i.e.,

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\big(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}u^{q}+cu)w\big)=q^{2}(1-|N|).

Also note that

\operatorname{Tr}(f(\alpha^{i}))=0

if and only if

(d^{q}+b^{q}c^{q})x^{2}+(d+d^{q}+b^{q+1}+c^{q+1})x+(d+bc)=0,

where

x=\alpha^{i(q-1)}.

Thus, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
q
be odd and suppose that
b^{q}=c
. Then
M
, the number of zeros of

x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d

in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x]
is given by

M=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }b=c=d=0,\\[2.84526pt]
q+1,&\text{if }b=c=0,\ d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\\[2.84526pt]
0,&\text{if }b=c=0,\ d\notin\mathbb{F}_{q},\\[2.84526pt]
1,&\text{if }b^{q}=c\neq 0,\ d\in\mathbb{F}_{q},\ d=b^{q+1},\\[2.84526pt]
q+1,&\text{if }b^{q}=c\neq 0,\ d\in\mathbb{F}_{q},\ d\neq b^{q+1},\\[2.84526pt]
0,&\text{if }b^{q}=c\neq 0,\ d\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since

Mq^{2}=q^{2}+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\bigl(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}u^{q}+cu)w\bigr),

we first consider the case
b=c=d=0
. In this case, it follows immediately from Lemma
2.4
that

M=1.

Now assume that
b=c=0
and
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Since
\ker(\operatorname{Tr})
is a one-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, if
t\neq 0
satisfies

\operatorname{Tr}(t)=0,

then

\ker(\operatorname{Tr})=\{ut:u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

By Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle Mq^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}+q^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\ker(\operatorname{Tr})\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi(du)-q\sum_{u\notin\ker(\operatorname{Tr})}\chi(du)

\displaystyle=q^{2}+(q^{2}+q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\ker(\operatorname{Tr})\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi(du)-q\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi(du)+q

\displaystyle=q^{2}+(q^{2}+q)\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\psi\bigl(du\,\operatorname{Tr}(t)\bigr)+q

\displaystyle=q^{2}+q^{3}.

Similarly, if
b=c=0
and
d\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then

M=0.

For
b^{q}=c\neq 0
and
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
d=c^{q+1}
, by Lemma
2.4
we have

\displaystyle Mq^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\ker(\operatorname{Tr})\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi(uw^{q+1})+\sum_{u\notin\ker(\operatorname{Tr})}\chi(du)\sum_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\chi\bigl(uw^{q+1}+(b^{q}u^{q}+cu)w\bigr)

\displaystyle=q^{2}+q^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\ker(\operatorname{Tr})\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi(du)-q\sum_{u\notin\ker(\operatorname{Tr})}\chi\bigl((d-c^{q+1})u\bigr)

\displaystyle=q^{2}+q^{2}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\psi\bigl(du\,\operatorname{Tr}(t)\bigr)-q(q^{2}-q)

\displaystyle=q^{2}.

In a similar way, the cases
b^{q}=c\neq 0
,
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
d\neq c^{q+1}
, and
b^{q}=c\neq 0
,
d\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}
, follow.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
q
be odd. Then the polynomial

x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x]

is a permutation polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if

b=0

and

c\notin\mu_{q+1}.

Proof.

First consider the case when
b=0
.
By Lemma
3.1
, the equation
x^{q}+cx+d+t=0.
has exactly one solution in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for every
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if

c\notin\mu_{q+1}.

Next assume that
b\neq 0
. Observe that

x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d=g\!\left(x+\dfrac{c}{2b}\right),

where

g(x)=x^{q}+bx^{2}+D,

with

D=d-\dfrac{c^{2}}{4b}-\dfrac{c^{q}}{2b^{q}}.

Since a translation preserves permutation behavior, therefore, it suffices to restrict our attention to polynomials of the form

g(x)=x^{q}+bx^{2}+D.

Thus, the equation

g(x)+t=0

has exactly one solution in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for every
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if

\sum_{i\in K_{t}}(-1)^{i}=0

for every
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, where

K_{t}=\left\{\,i\mid 0\leq i\leq q,\;h_{t}\!\left(\alpha^{\,i(q-1)}\right)=0\right\},

and

h_{t}(x)=b^{q}x^{3}-4b^{q+1}(D+t)^{q}x^{2}-4b^{q+1}(D+t)x+b.

Here,
\alpha
denotes a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. This follows directly from Lemma
3.2
.

Notice first that

J=\left\{\alpha^{i(q-1)}\mid 0\leq i\leq q\right\}

is a subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
of order
q+1
, generated by
\alpha^{q-1}
.

We first consider the case
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. For

t=-D-\dfrac{3}{4b},

we have

h_{t}(-1)=0\quad\text{and}\quad h_{t}^{\prime}(-1)=0.

Hence,
-1
is a root of
h_{t}(x)=0
of multiplicity at least
2
. Since
-1\in\mu_{q+1}
and the product of all roots of
h_{t}(x)
is

-\dfrac{b}{b^{q}}=-1,

the third root must also be
-1
. Therefore,
-1
is in fact a root of multiplicity
3
.

Now

-1=(\alpha^{q-1})^{\dfrac{q+1}{2}},

and therefore

K_{t}=\left\{\dfrac{q+1}{2}\right\}.

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then
\dfrac{q+1}{2}
is odd, whereas if
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, then
\dfrac{q+1}{2}
is even. In either case,

\sum_{i\in K_{t}}(-1)^{i}\neq 0.

Similarly, assume that
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
\operatorname{Tr}(b)=0
. Take

t=\dfrac{2b^{1-q}-1}{4b}-D.

Then one readily verifies that
1
is a root of
h_{t}(x)=0
of multiplicity
3
. Consequently,

K_{t}=\{0\}.

Therefore,

\sum_{i\in K_{t}}(-1)^{i}\neq 0.

Finally, assume that
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
\operatorname{Tr}(b)\neq 0
. For

t=\dfrac{1}{4b}-D,

the roots of
h_{t}(x)=0
are
1
,
-1
, and
\gamma
, where
\gamma\neq,1-1
. Hence,

K_{t}=\{r,j,k\}.

For all possible parity distributions of
r
,
j
and
k
, we have

\sum_{i\in K_{t}}(-1)^{i}\neq 0.

Thus the result follows.

∎

It follows directly from Theorem
3.6
that whenever
b\neq 0
and
q
is odd, there exists no permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of the form
x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d
. However, this is not the case when
q
is even, as evident from Theorem
3.7
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
q=2^{k}
and
b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Then
x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x]
is a permutation polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if either
b=0
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{1\}
or
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
c=1
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.3
and
3.1
, when
b=0
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{1\}
or
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
c=1
, the equation

x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d+t=0

has exactly one solution in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
for every
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Hence
x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

For all remaining cases, take
t=d
to see the polynomial in question fail to display permutation behavior due to Lemma
3.3
.
∎

We note that in Theorem 14
[
2
]
, it is shown that when
\operatorname{Tr}(A)=1
, the polynomial
\operatorname{Tr}(Ax^{q+1})+L(x)
does not permute
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. This implies that our polynomial
x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d
never permute
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
when
q
is even. For completeness and to keep the paper self-contained, we also provide an independent proof of this fact i.e., Proposition
3.8
. However, our approach is entirely different from that in
[
2
]
, thereby preserving the novelty of our method.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
q
be even. Then the polynomial

x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x]

is never a permutation polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Proof.

First assume that
b^{q}=c
. Choose
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that

\operatorname{Tr}(d+t)=0\quad\text{and}\quad t\neq d+b^{q+1}.

Note that such a choice is always possible. Put

\beta_{t}:=d+b^{q+1}+t.

Then, by Lemma
3.4
,

h_{t}(x)=\beta_{t}^{q}x^{2}+\beta_{t}.

Since
\beta_{t}\neq 0
, the polynomial
h_{t}(x)
has exactly one root in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, counted with multiplicity
2
.

Now note that

\left\{\alpha^{i(q-1)}\mid 1\leq i\leq q\right\}=\mu_{q+1}\setminus\{1\}.

Hence, if the root of
h_{t}(x)
lies in
\mu_{q+1}\setminus\{1\}
, then
|N|=1
, otherwise,
|N|=0
. Therefore, the equation

x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d+t=0

has either
q
or
q+1
roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Next, we consider the case
b^{q}\neq c
. Then

h_{t}(x)=\beta_{t}^{q}x^{2}+\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}(d+t)+b^{q+1}+c^{q+1}\bigr)x+\beta_{t},

where

\beta_{t}=d+bc+t.

We claim that

h_{t}(1)\neq 0

for all
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Indeed, if
h_{t}(1)=0
, then

\beta_{t}^{q}+\beta_{t}+\operatorname{Tr}(d+t)+b^{q+1}+c^{q+1}=0,

which simplifies to
b^{q}=c
, a contradiction.

Next, choose
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that

\operatorname{Tr}(d+t)\neq b^{q+1}+c^{q+1}.

Then
h_{t}(x)
is either a linear polynomial or a quadratic polynomial with nonzero linear term.

In the linear case, clearly
|N|=0
. In the quadratic case, Lemma
2.5
shows that if
h_{t}(x)
has a root in
\mu_{q+1}
, then it has two distinct roots in
\mu_{q+1}
. Since
h_{t}(1)\neq 0
, the only possible values are

|N|=0\quad\text{or}\quad 2.

Therefore, the equation

x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d+t=0

has either
2
or
0
roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

This completes the proof.∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
q
be odd and suppose that
b^{q}=c
. Then

x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x]

is never a permutation polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Proof.

The equation

x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d+t=0

has exactly
q+1
solutions when
b=c=0
and
t=1-d
, and has no solution when
b^{q}=c\neq 0
and
t=t^{\prime}-d
for some
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
∎

We note that computational evidence (via SageMath) suggests that when
b^{q}\neq c
and
q
is odd, there exists no permutation polynomial of the form
x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. However, we have not yet been able to prove this result with our existing tools. It would be interesting to investigate this problem further and determine a theoretical explanation. These observations motivate the following conjecture.

Conjecture 3.10
.

Let
q
be odd and let
b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then

f(x)=x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d

is not a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

In the following two propositions, we present the compositional inverses of the two permutation polynomials obtained in Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.7
. Note that it is sufficient to compute the inverses in the case
d=0
.

Proposition 3.11
.

The compositional inverse of the permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
in Theorem
3.6
is given by

f^{-1}(x)=\frac{1}{c^{q+1}-1}\left(c^{q}x+x^{q}\right).

Proof.

The result follows directly from Lemma
2.6
.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
q=2^{k}
. The compositional inverse of the permutation polynomial in Theorem
3.7
is given as follows:

(1)

If
b=0
, then

f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{1}{c^{2}+1}\left(cx+x^{q}\right).

(2)

If
b\neq 0
, then

f^{-1}(x)=\phi^{-1}\!\left(x+\bar{f}^{-1}(\operatorname{Tr}(x))\right),

where

\phi^{-1}(x)=\left(b^{-1}x\right)^{2^{2k-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{f}^{-1}(x)=\left(b^{-1}x\right)^{2^{k-1}}.

Proof.

(1) The result follows directly from Lemma
2.6
.

(2) In Lemma
2.7
, take
g=x
,
\phi=bx^{2}
, which permute
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
since
q
is even,
\psi=\operatorname{Tr}(x)
,
\overline{\psi}=\operatorname{Tr}(x)
, and
h(x)=1
. Then

\psi(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})=\overline{\psi}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})=\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Hence,

f(x)=bx^{2}+x^{q}+x,\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{f}(x)\big|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=bx^{2}.

Therefore,

\bar{f}^{-1}(x)=(b^{-1}x)^{2^{k-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi^{-1}(x)=(b^{-1}x)^{2^{2k-1}}.

Thus, the result follows.

∎

4.
Conclusion

We study special permutation polynomials of the forms
x^{q}+bx^{2}+cx+d
and
x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
by employing character sums, more specifically Weil sums, to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for these polynomials to be permutation polynomials and also present their compositional inverses.

For future research, one may further investigate the case
b^{q}\neq c
with
q
odd for the polynomial
x^{q+1}+bx^{q}+cx+d
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, with or without the use of character sums. Moreover, the general strategy of determining the number of roots to characterize permutation behavior can be extended to other classes of polynomials as well.
