1.0 Executive Summary

The Karuk Tribe Community Solar Systems project consisted of a 948-kilowatt direct current (kW-DC) solar
photovoltaic (PV) system for the Casino, solar + storage systems installed on 39 Elder homes designed around
home energy usage and critical power supply during outages, and an 18 kW-DC solar PV system for the Happy
Camp Wellness Center. The Casino array was a ground mounted, fixed tilt solar system installed adjacent to the
Casino in unused land not planned for development. The Elder Homes were in three different towns: Yreka, CA,
Happy Camp, CA, and Orleans, CA. Each solar system for the Elder homes included battery energy storage for
critical backup of medical equipment. The Wellness Center was also in Happy Camp, CA and was a new
construction gym and wellness clinic that was installed with a small solar PV system. This project targeted both
large energy using facilities, as well as critical infrastructure.

The project objectives were to reinforce financial stability of Tribally owned businesses by maximizing resources
and offsetting operational costs by use of solar energy systems and to provide stable power to homes serving
the most vulnerable community members, Karuk Tribe Elders, so they can maintain use of critical medical
equipment and basic necessities (heating/cooling/cooking/food storage) during frequent and extended power
outages. This also allowed the Karuk Tribe to improve their environmental footprint and reduce the impact of
Tribal energy usage through traditional power sources i.e. utility. The solar systems are slated to save the Tribe
over $200,000 annually by producing 1,959,675 kWh for the first year.

The goal summary for the Karuk Tribe is a few visions as it relates to clean energy and promoting sustainability
within the Tribe. A quote from the Business Contact for Tribal Energy goals: “Expand environmentally friendly
energy infrastructure through-out the service area to improve energy efficiency, reduce energy costs and
reinforce tribal sovereignty.” Given the problems the Karuk Tribe has with both Pacific Power and Light (PPL)
and Pacific Gas and Electric (PGE) utilities, the need for clean and consistent energy is abundant. The Tribe
continues to use environmental methods throughout the Tribal organization (such as the reduce, reuse, recycle
method.) The next logical step is to produce energy from the sun. With the DOE’s financial assistance, the
project has become a reality and lives are changing due to the resilience of energy and, in some cases, battery
storage. The meaningful solar systems installed on Karuk Tribal Lands are a necessary step forward in their
sustainability goals and progression to not rely on Utility power fully.

2.0 Background

The Karuk Tribe of California is one of the largest Tribes in CA. It was federally recognized in 1978 and has 4,800
members. They have demonstrated their ability to administer a multitude of social, cultural and economic
programs effectively, earning the status of a "Self-Governance Tribe." The Tribal government currently employs
more than 100 people in administrative, child welfare, community/economic development, education, elders,
energy assistance, health, housing, human services and natural resources programs. In little more than a
decade, the Karuk Tribe has developed housing divisions, health clinics and Head Start programs in its three
major population centers: Orleans, Happy Camp and Yreka. Through the tribally chartered Karuk Community
Development Corporation, the Karuk Tribe administered salmon fisheries enhancement projects, acquired and
expanded a retail business, planned a small manufacturing plant, assisted several local people in starting small
business enterprises and staffs Workforce Development personnel at Community Computer Centers in its
communities of Orleans, Happy Camp and Yreka.

With the assistance of the Department of Energy, these solar energy systems will provide a good return on

vision of the Tribe looking ahead.

3.0 Project Objectives

The Karuk Tribe are continuing to follow through with their renewable energy initiative with the installation of
1,275.6-kW of solar PV capacity serving 3 commercial buildings and 39 Elder homes on the Tribal Reservation.
Each of these buildings has its own interconnection point with the Utility and, by using proven solar PV
technology in the northern California climate, this system is projected to offset 95% of total combined building
energy consumption for these Tribal facilities. The solar PV system is estimated to save the tribe $13,691,985
over a 30-year period. The Karuk Tribe – along with SunRenu- have analyzed numerous energy saving
technologies, with solar PV and solar systems having the greatest benefit to the Tribe. The tribe, situated in
Northern California, supports one of the highest production values from solar in the state. This technology best
supports the Tribes’ goals.

With the Tribes’ significant need for basic services, it is an accepted goal of the Tribe to attempt to acquire
support for projects that the Tribe sees as vital for the community. This solar + storage project will help the Tribe
achieve the goals necessary to better care for their members and provide a better quality of life.

Project Summary: The buildings specified for the Karuk community solar project are as follows:

 1. Tribal Casino Facility and trailer (Solar PV): 948 kW-DC Solar PV system, ground mounted in ideal open
 flat land adjacent to facility. Approximately 95% offset.
 2. Tribal Elder Homes (Solar PV & Battery Storage): 310-kW Solar PV & 580-kWh battery storage in total for
 all 39 homes. These will be roof mounted with 4 different battery storage kit types. 100% Offset on each
 home, based on energy data at time of development.
 3. Tribal Wellness Center (Solar PV) – 18.4kW Solar PV System roof mounted facing due southeast to
 maximize peak loads. 96% offset on this facility based on usage at time of development.

4.0 Description of Activities Performed

Prior to the grant award, analytics (i.e. development and utility) were completed prior to the start of the project.
Upon receiving the final grant award, the process began with plant design. During the Design phase, vendors
such as WattHub Renewables and SunRenu Solar paid careful attention to the constructability, equipment,
location, and further analyzing energy usage to maximize value. Decisions such as inter row spacing, distance of
home run wires, access, tilt angles, battery sizing, orientation, and use of power optimizers, and interconnection
methods were all considered in the Design phase.

The Engineering and Permitting phase included using Professional Engineers on both electrical and structural
facilities. These followed carefully designed solar systems plans identified during the Design phase. The
engineering designs were shared by the Karuk Tribe to DOE for review and comment. Upon approval, these
designs were submitted to the Utility for the interconnection application and with the Tribe’s internal team for a
permit in lieu of City Plan review.

The Notice to Proceed for Construction was given by following a formal process. This involved an internal Tribal
review of the Design and Engineering plans as well as the interconnection applications acceptance by the Utility.
The utility companies were: Pacific Gas and Electric and Pacific Corp.

5.0 Conclusions and Recommendations
The project was accomplished between May 2023 and December 2023 for all facets of work. The systems were
granted permission to operate (PTO) at different times, with the latest being March 2024 due to PPL delays on
four Elder Homes in Happy Camp, CA due to a transformer that had to be upsized/replaced before the systems
could be activated. All systems are commissioned and operating as expected with slight deviations in
performance based on normal sun irradiance and general routine maintenance.

The Karuk Tribe and SunRenu worked very well together and fostered a positive working relationship. The solar
systems being implemented in a short time span also provided a favorable step forward in implementing aspects
of the Karuk Tribe energy vision. This is the second major solar project for the Karuk Tribe with plans to continue
accelerating the energy vision for the Tribe and provide resilience to critical facilities.

All solar panels (VSUN along with inverters,) SolArk and SolarEdge are bankable technology that are mainstream
in industry. The type of equipment being used extends longevity and lifecycle of the solar systems. Each system

6.0 Lessons Learned
There were a few challenges to the implementation of the project, one during construction and another related
to the net metering which impacted customer savings. Both challenges turned to lessons learned in the scope of
the project timeline. Some of the challenges are as follows:

 • Site conditions for ground mount – The array was moved slightly to accommodate a flat, already graded
 area for the Casino ground mount. The move (which amounted to a few hundred feet) added to the
 length of the home run. We mitigated the voltage drop from the array to the interconnection point
 (approx. 1000’) by stepping up the voltage of the output from the inverters at the array, to minimize
 voltage drop to under 2%.

 • Elder Home Coordination – having to install 39 Elder homes with solar and storage took a concerted
 effort to communicate with the Elders, coordinate installations that fit within normal business hours,
 and segment the installations per City to maximize efficiency. Vendor and subcontractors worked hard
 to install all 39 solar and storage systems in a 4-month period. A major challenge to completing this
 segment of the project was that not all the Elder Homes had internet connectivity which calls for
 commissioning the inverter and battery system on site. Remote troubleshooting becomes impossible
 without internet service. To address this issue, SunRenu had several meetings with Tribal facilities
 employees to train them on how to operate and perform high level troubleshooting for these solar and
 storage systems. Lessons learned from this situation are to prepare for remote locations without
 internet and work with Facilities employees early in the process to assist with operations and
 maintenance of these systems.

 • A big challenge to the installation of the Casino array was that when we applied for the grant in 2021,
 Pacific Corp (Utility serving the Casino) offered a net metering program that was favorable to solar. As
 time went on through the grant review, award negotiation, and final award process, Pacific Corp had
 changed their net metering rate structure to a less favorable export rate. The site was designed to cover
 92% of the Casino’s energy consumption, which counted on the original net metering program. Pacific
 Corp did not notify anyone of the change and still had the nomenclature of “net metering” when the
 Tribe applied for interconnection. Under the current net metering program, the Tribe receives a low
 buyback on exported energy from the Casino solar array (5 – 7 cents per kWh). This impacted the solar
 savings by approximately 25%. Lessons learned are to keep up to speed on Utility rate structures
 throughout the tenure of the grant program. The Tribe is planning on applying for a future grant to
 implement battery energy storage systems for the Casino, along with new solar PV and energy storage
 for the new Hotel. This supplemental project will provide enhanced savings for the existing Casino array
 allowing the Tribe to reap the full benefits of the original net metering program and more.
