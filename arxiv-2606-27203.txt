1
Introduction

Let
h
denote the absolute logarithmic Weil height on the field of algebraic numbers
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. A celebrated conjecture due to Lehmer predicts that, once roots of unity are excluded, there is a universal positive lower bound for
\deg(\alpha)h(\alpha)
, as
\alpha
varies in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
. Lehmer’s conjecture still remains unproved in its generality; nevertheless, for some special classes of fields, it can even be sharpened. This point of view was considered for the first time in
[
6
]
, where the authors introduced the notion of fields with the
Bogomolov property
(property (B) for short), where the height is uniformly bounded below outside torsion points; in other words, zero is isolated in the set of heights on the multiplicative group of the field.

For a number field, property
{\rm(B)}
is a consequence of Northcott’s theorem. For infinite extensions, however, the question becomes subtler and often reflects arithmetic features of the field, such as the local behaviour of the extension, the structure of its Galois group, or its relation with objects of geometric origin.

This note is concerned with the following perspective. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field, let
G_{\mathbb{K}}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{K})
, and let

\rho:G_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow\Omega

be a continuous representation into a separated topological group. The field

\mathbb{K}(\rho):=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\ker(\rho)}

is the algebraic extension of
\mathbb{K}
cut out by the representation
\rho
. We say that
\rho
has property (B) if the field
\mathbb{K}(\rho)
has the Bogomolov property. This language includes classical examples, such as cyclotomic extensions, abelian extensions of number fields, as well as non-abelian extensions coming from torsion points of elliptic curves.

Indeed, one of the main motivations for this approach is an important theorem due to Habegger
[
14
]
: if
E
is an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then the field
\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathrm{tors}})
generated by all torsion points of
E
has property
{\rm(B)}
. This is naturally a theorem about the Galois representation on the adelic Tate module associated with
E
, since the field cut out by this representation is precisely the field obtained by adjoining to
\mathbb{Q}
the coordinates of the torsion points of
E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

In this article, we present some extensions of this viewpoint beyond the scope of elliptic curves, in particular to Galois representations attached to modular forms, following
[
3
,
17
]
, and to
p
-adic representations with large local image, not necessarily of geometric origin, following
[
11
,
10
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
recalls the basic definitions and examples concerning the Bogomolov property, together with the ADZ criterion and its interpretation in terms of Galois representations. Section
3
is devoted to modular Galois representations and to the known results toward property (B) in this setting. In Section
4
we describe the strategy of the proof in the strong supersingular case and discuss two directions in which the argument can be extended, separately treating the
p
-part and the prime-to-
p
part of the adelic representations.
Section
5
concerns
p
-adic representations with large local image and illustrates the scope of the main criterion through a selection of applications.

This note is expository. It records the main definitions and statements presented in the talk, and it sketches some main tools used in the proof mechanisms. Full proofs and more details can be found in the cited papers, especially
[
14
,
3
,
11
]
.

2
The Bogomolov property of algebraic fields

2.1
Weil height

Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field containing
\alpha
. The absolute logarithmic Weil height is defined as

h(\alpha)=\frac{1}{[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{v}[\mathbb{K}_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v},\hskip 20.00003pt\log^{+}(x)=\log\max\{x,1\},

where
v
runs over all places of
\mathbb{K}
, the absolute values are normalized to satisfy the product formula, and
\mathbb{K}_{v}
denotes the completion of
\mathbb{K}
at a place
v
. This definition is independent of the chosen field
\mathbb{K}
containing
\alpha
.

The following elementary properties are easily proved:

•

h(\alpha\beta)\leq h(\alpha)+h(\beta)
,

•

h(\alpha+\beta)\leq h(\alpha)+h(\beta)+\log 2
,

•

h(\alpha^{n})=|n|h(\alpha)\hskip 20.00003pt(n\in\mathbb{Q})
,

•

h(\sigma(\alpha))=h(\alpha)\hskip 20.00003pt(\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Moreover, the following results are well known:

•

Kronecker’s theorem
:
h(\alpha)=0
for
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
if and only if
\alpha
is a root of unity.

•

Northcott’s theorem
: there are only finitely many algebraic numbers of bounded degree and bounded height.

A classical conjecture, proposed by Lehmer in 1933, predicts that the product of the degree of a non-torsion algebraic number and its height is uniformly bounded from below.

C o n j e c t u r e 2.1

(Lehmer)
.

There exists a universal constant
c>0
such that

[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]\,h(\alpha)\geq c

for every
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}\setminus{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\infty}
.

Although Lehmer’s conjecture remains open in its full generality, certain fields of algebraic numbers are known to satisfy a stronger property: the height is uniformly bounded from below outside torsion.

D e f i n i t i o n  2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{L}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
be a field. We say that
\mathbb{L}
has the
Bogomolov property
, or property (B), if there exists a constant
c_{\mathbb{L}}>0
such that

h(\alpha)\geq c_{\mathbb{L}}

for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{L}^{\times}\setminus{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\infty}
.

2.2
Fields with property (B).

It has often been observed that most of the known examples of fields with property (B) fall into two broad families.

Fields satisfying some local boundedness conditions.

By Northcott’s theorem, number fields have property (B). A non trivial example is the compositum
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}
of all totally real fields (the original proof was obtained by Schinzel, applying
[
24
, Corollary
1^{\prime}
]
to the linear polynomial
P(z)=z-\alpha
to get the optimal lower bound). In
[
6
]
Bombieri and Zannier also show that totally
p
-adic fields, or more generally fields with bounded local degrees at some finite place have Property (B). By a result of Checcoli
[
9
]
, a Galois extension has uniformly bounded local degrees at
every
finite place if and only if its Galois group has finite exponent. Thus (infinite) Galois extensions with Galois group of bounded exponent have Property (B).

Fields obtained by adding torsion to some base field.

Zannier conjectured that the maximal abelian extension
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}
of the rational field also satisfies property (B), and this was proved in
[
2
]
; by Kronecker-Weber theorem
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}
is the field obtained by adjoining all torsion points of the multiplicative group. By a special case of
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
, property (B) also holds for
\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{ab}}
, for any number field
\mathbb{K}
(notice however, that
\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{ab}}
is not in general obtained by adding torsion points of some algebraic group).
An important class of non abelian extensions with Property (B) has been exhibited by Habegger in
[
14
]
: let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
; then the field
\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathrm{tors}})
generated by the coordinates of all torsion points of
E
has the Bogomolov property.

2.3
Property (B) under the compositum of fields and ADZ fields.

Property (B) is not preserved under taking composita, even in the basic case of the compositum of an infinite extension and a finite one, as the following example, taken from
[
1
, Example 5.3]
, shows.

E x a m p l e 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}
be the maximal totally real extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. As already mentioned in Section
2.2
, it has property (B) by
[
24
]
. However, its compositum with the quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(i)
is the maximal totally CM extension
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{tr}}(i)
of
\mathbb{Q}
, which contains a sequence of elements whose heights tend to zero:

\alpha_{k}=\left(\frac{2+i}{2-i}\right)^{1/k}.

We recall a result of Amoroso, David and Zannier
[
1
, Lemma 1.5]
. This is a very useful criterion, since it can be used to establish property B for infinite fields which are neither abelian nor of bounded local degree at a fixed place, but rather, in some sense, cut across the two families described above.

D e f i n i t i o n  2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field,
v
a non-archimedean place of
\mathbb{K}
, and

\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
a Galois extension
with Galois group
\mathbb{G}
. We say that the extension
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
is ADZ at
v
if

\mathbb{L}^{Z(\mathbb{G})}/\mathbb{K}

has local degrees uniformly bounded over
v
.

(
Z(\mathbb{G})
denotes the center of the group
\mathbb{G}).

L e m m a  2.5

(ADZ)
.

[
1
, Lemma 1.5]

ADZ
fields have property
(B)
.

An interesting feature of ADZ fields is that they are stable under composition with finite extensions. More generally, let
(\mathbb{L}_{i}/\mathbb{K})
be a family of extensions that are all ADZ at the same place
v
. Assume that the local degrees at
v
of the fixed fields by
Z(\mathrm{Gal}(L_{i}/K))
are bounded independently of
i
. Then the compositum
\bigcup_{i}L_{i}
is again ADZ at
v

[
17
, Lemma 5.7]
. In particular, the compositum of two extensions of
\mathbb{K}
that are both ADZ at
v
is itself ADZ at
v
.

2.4
Property (B) for Galois representations

Habegger’s theorem initiated the study of the Bogomolov property in non-abelian Galois extensions arising from arithmetic geometry; it will be the starting point for our exposition.

T h e o r e m  2.6

(Habegger 2013,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
E
be a non CM elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then the field
\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathrm{tors}})
has property
(B)
.

If
E
has complex multiplication then
\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathrm{tors}})
is an abelian extension of a quadratic number field and thus it has the property (B) by the quoted result of Amoroso and Zannier on
\mathbb{K}^{\mathrm{ab}}

[
4
]
. However for a non-CM elliptic curve there are no number fields
\mathbb{K}
such that
\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathrm{tors}})
is contained in the maximal abelian extension of
\mathbb{K}
(cf. the discussion in the Introduction of
[
14
]
).

Notice that

\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathrm{tors}})=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\ker{\widehat{\rho}_{E}}},

where

\widehat{\rho}_{E}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{T}(E))\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})

is the representation given by the action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
over the adelic Tate module associated to
E
.
The representation
\widehat{\rho}_{E}

is the product of all the
\ell
-adic representations

\rho_{E,\ell}:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow Aut(T_{\ell}(E))=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})

where
T_{\ell}(E)
is the
\ell
-adic Tate module. Indeed, by Galois correspondence
\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell^{n}])
is the subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
fixed by the kernel of the action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
on the
\ell^{n}
-torsion points of
E
; moving to the projective limit we see that
\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell^{\infty}])=\mathbb{Q}(\rho_{E,\ell})
. Since
\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathrm{tors}})
is the compositum of
\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell^{\infty}])
, for every prime
\ell
, then it coincides with the field fixed by
\bigcap_{\ell}\ker(\rho_{E,\ell})=\ker(\widehat{\rho}_{E})
.

This point of view naturally leads to the following generalization. For any field
\mathbb{K}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, we introduce the following

D e f i n i t i o n  2.7
.

Let
\Omega
be a separated topological group,
\mathbb{K}
be a subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
G_{\mathbb{K}}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{K})
. We say that a continuous homomorphism

\rho:G_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow\Omega

has
property
(B)

if the field cut out by
\rho
,

\mathbb{K}(\rho):=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\ker(\rho)},

has property
\mathrm{(B)}
.

In this perspective, the main result of
[
2
]
implies (by Galois theory) that every homomorphism of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
taking values in an abelian group has Property (B); and Habegger’s theorem establishes property (B) for the representation
\widehat{\rho}_{E}
when
E
is an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

We shall be particularly interested in the case where
\rho
is a Galois representation, that is, when
\Omega=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(A)
, where
A
is a topological ring.

As an immediate consequence of the ADZ lemma, particularly simple to state in the language of Galois representations, we obtain the following

L e m m a  2.8

(ADZ, representation variant)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field, and let

\rho:G_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{d}(A)

be a Galois representation. If the image of a decomposition group
G_{v}
in the projective linear group
\mathrm{PGL}_{d}(A)
is finite for some non-Archimedean place
v
of
\mathbb{K}
, then
\rho
has property
\mathrm{(B)}
.

One of the main purposes of this article is to re-interpret some known results on property (B) in terms of Galois representations, with the aim of providing a unifying framework for a part of the theory.

Modular Galois representations

3.1
Modular forms and Galois representations

Let
f=\sum_{n}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
be a normalized eigenform for the Hecke algebra, of weight
k\geq 2
, level
N
and nebentypus
\chi
;
let
\mathbb{K}_{f}
be the Hecke field of
f
, that is, th number field generated by the coefficients
a_{n}
; and
\mathcal{O}_{f}
its integer ring.
A construction due to Shimura and Deligne attaches to
f
a family of compatible Galois representations

\rho_{f,v}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f,v})

indexed by the finite places
v
of
\mathcal{O}_{f}
.

Each
\rho_{f,v}
is characterized by the following properties

•

\rho_{f,v}
is continuous and unramified at primes
p
not dividing
N\ell
, where
\ell
is the rational prime which
v
divides;

•

for each prime
p
not dividing
N\ell
, the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f,v}(Frob_{p})
is

X^{2}-a_{p}X+\chi(p)p^{k-1}.

(3.1)

For a rational prime
p
, we let

\rho_{f,p}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\prod_{v|p}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f,v}).

Moreover, let

\widehat{\rho}_{f}=\prod_{v}\rho_{f,v}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{f}),

where
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{f}
is the profinite completion of
\mathcal{O}_{f}
.

Motivated by Habegger’s theorem and the results obtained so far, the following conjecture is ventured in
[
3
]

C o n j e c t u r e 3.1
.

Let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
be a normalized eigenform. Then,

a)

for every non-Archimedean place
v
,
\rho_{f,v}
has property (B);

b)

\widehat{\rho}_{f}
has property (B).

Notice that
\mathbb{Q}(\widehat{\rho}_{f})
is the compositum of the fields

\mathbb{Q}(\widehat{\rho}_{f})=\bigcup_{v}\mathbb{Q}(\rho_{f,v}),

so that part b) of Conjecture
3.1
implies part a). However, since property (B) is not preserved under composita of fields, the converse is not immediate.

Abelian varieties of type
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.

The following conjecture, due to S. David, may be viewed as a natural extension of Habegger’s theorem; see
[
13
, p. 114]
.

C o n j e c t u r e 3.2

(S. David)
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field. Then
\mathbb{Q}(A_{\rm tors})
has property (B).

Conjectures
3.1
and
3.2
overlap to some extent.
Indeed, when
f
is a cuspidal modular eigenform of weight
2
, the representation
\widehat{\rho}_{f}
is the Galois representation of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
on the adelic Tate module
\widehat{\mathrm{T}}(A_{f})
, where
A_{f}
is an abelian variety defined over
\mathbb{Q}
of dimension
[\mathbb{K}_{f}:\mathbb{Q}]
, equipped with an action of
\mathcal{O}_{f}

[
29
]
.
Such abelian varieties are called
of type
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, after
[
22
]
.

Conversely, as a consequence of Serre’s modularity conjecture, proved by Khare and Wintenberger in 2009
[
15
, Corollary 10.2]
, every abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type over
\mathbb{Q}
is modular.

Therefore, Conjecture
3.1
predicts, in particular, property
\mathrm{(B)}
for abelian varieties of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

3.2
Property (B) for modular representations

Despite the generality of Conjecture
3.1
, the available results proving property (B) for adelic representations attached to modular forms hold only under strong restrictions.

By Habegger’s theorem, (B) holds for modular forms of weight 2 and Hecke field equal to
\mathbb{Q}
, because the abelian varieties of type
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
they give rise are elliptic curves in this case.

Some examples in the case where the Hecke field has degree greater than one over
\mathbb{Q}
are provided by the following

T h e o r e m  3.3
.

[
3
, Theorem 1.4]

Let
f=\sum_{n}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a normalized eigenform.

Assume that there exists a prime
p
satisfying the following conditions

(P0)

p\not|N
and
p\geq 2k-1
;

(P1)

a_{p}=0
;

(P2)

the reduction
\overline{\rho}_{f,p}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p})
has image

\widetilde{G}=\{\alpha\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p})\ |\ \det\alpha\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})^{k-1}\}.

Then

\widehat{\rho}_{f}
has property
(B)
.

A generalization to the case with Nebentypus (that is,
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
) has been obtained by Piras in
[
17
]
. When the Nebentypus is nontrivial, the situation is substantially more delicate. Indeed, the presence of inner twists prevents condition
(P2)
from holding in its present form, so that the argument cannot be extended by a straightforward adaptation of the proof. Instead, both the statement and the proof must be reformulated by passing to the restriction of the representation to the number field fixed by the group of inner twists.

A recent result establishes property (B) for a class of modular forms that are supercuspidal at a prime
p
. A brief account of this theorem will be given in Section
4.3
.

About hypotheses
(P0)
,
(P1)
,
(P2)
.

If
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
has rational Hecke eigenvalues, then it falls within the scope of Habegger’s theorem. The latter is unconditional, since for an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
the existence of a prime

satisfying hypotheses
(P0)
,
(P1)
, and
(P2)
follows from the combination of two fundamental results.

•

Serre’s big image theorem

[
28
, Théorème 2]
: Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field and let
E_{/\mathbb{K}}
be an elliptic curve without complex multiplication. Then the mod-
p

\rho_{E,p}:G_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(E[p])\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

is surjective for all but finitely many primes
p
.

•

Elkies’ theorem on supersingular primes
[
12
, Theorem 1]

: For every elliptic curve
E_{/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
, there exist infinitely many supersingular primes.

For the sake of simplicity, we focus on a modular form

. In this case, an analogue of Serre’s theorem, due to Ribet
[

, ensures that property

is satisfied for every sufficiently large prime

.

However, the analogue of Elkies’ theorem, namely the existence of infinitely many primes satisfying the strong supersingular condition

, is far from being established.

On the contrary, for non-CM modular forms of weight strictly greater than

, this condition appears to be rather exceptional. Evidence for this comes from two complementary directions. On the one hand, Calegari and Sardari
[

]
proved that, for a fixed prime

of a given level prime to

. On the other hand, for a fixed eigenform

and varies

, or fixes

This phenomenon is the main factor limiting the range of applicability of

.

4
The proof strategy and further progress

4.1
Idea of the proof

Although property (B) is not preserved under taking composita, it is useful, for the purpose of analyzing the proof of Theorem
3.3
—which closely follows Habegger’s argument—to distinguish two separate components once a prime
p
satisfying the hypotheses of the theorem has been fixed:

•

the
prime-to-
p
part,
concerning
\widehat{\rho}_{f}^{(p)}:\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\prod_{\ell\not=p}\mathcal{O}_{f}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
.

•

the

p
-part,
concerning
\rho_{f,p}:\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Inspired by Habegger’s argument, a criterion ensuring the Bogomolov property for the product of these two representations has been developed
[
3
, Proposition 3.4]
. A further generalization was subsequently obtained in
[
10
, Theorem 4.2]
.

The prime-to-
p
part.

The ADZ Lemma
2.8
is enough to prove Property (B) for the prime-to-
p
part.
Indeed, the proof can be outlined as follows:

\displaystyle p\not|N\hbox{ and }a_{p}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\widehat{\rho}^{(p)}\hbox{ unramified and }\mathrm{Trace}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})=0

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\Longleftrightarrow}\limits^{\eqref{eq:polcar}}}\widehat{\rho}^{(p)}\hbox{ unramified and }\mathrm{Frob}_{p}^{2}\hbox{ is central}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\Longrightarrow}\limits^{{\mathrm{(ADZ)}}}}\hbox{property }{\rm(B)}\hbox{ for the prime-to-$p$ part.}

The
p
-part.

This is the most delicate part of the proof; it is based on the study of the
p
-
p
representation
\rho_{p}|_{G_{p}}
, where
G_{p}
is a decomposition group at
p
. We only provide a brief overview of the key steps of the proof; the interested reader is referred to
[
14
,
3
,
17
]
for the details.

•

Since
p
does not divide the level
N
, it is known by local Langlands correspondence that the representation
\rho_{p}|_{G_{p}}
is crystalline at
p
, and that the characteristic polynomial of the crystalline Frobenius coincides with that of
\rho_{p}(\mathrm{Frob_{p}})
, namely (
3.1
);

•

A description due to Breuil
[
7
, Proposition 3.2]
shows then that
\rho_{p}|_{G_{p}}
is, up to a twist by an unramified character, induced by a power of a Lubin-Tate character:
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
.

•

One may then appeal to Lubin-Tate theory (in particular
[
16
, Proposition 6.1]
) to control the higher ramification groups of the local extension
L=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\rho_{p}|_{G_{p}})
.

•

This leads to establish, for every
\alpha\in L
, a
local metric inequality:

0<|\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha|_{p}<C,\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt0<C<1,

for a suitable
\sigma
in
G_{p}
.

•

The next step is to pass from the local setting to the global one by propagating the local metric inequality to a positive proportion of the primes
v\mid p
. This is the point at which the big image hypothesis
(P2)
on
\bar{\rho}
becomes essential.

•

Then the product formula yields a lower bound for the height outside a small exceptional set. Dealing with the latter requires an additional descent argument, which we shall not discuss here.

4.2
Working on the
p
-part:
p
-adic Lie groups and Sen’s Theorem

As discussed above, the condition
(
P1) considerably limits the scope of Theorem
3.3
. In order to verify Conjecture
3.1
, one is naturally led to seek weaker substitutes for the condition
a_{p}=0
, replacing it with assumptions that are expected to hold more frequently.

If we examine the outline of the proof given in the previous section, we see that condition
(P1)
enters the argument in two distinct contexts:

•

In the
prime-to-
p
part
in order to know that a power of Frobenius is central and apply the ADZ lemma;

•

In the
p
-part
in order to apply Lubin-Tate theory, control ramification and produce the local metric inequality.

Although the restriction to
G_{p}
of a
p
-adic Galois representation is usually the most challenging aspect to analyze, it is there that the most substantial progress has been made in relaxing the strong supersingularity condition.

A problem of Serre.

In the final part of his paper
[
27
]
, Serre posed the following problem.
Let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension,

\mathcal{O}
be a
p
-adic ring, and let

\rho:G_{K}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathcal{\mathcal{O}})

be a continuous homomorphism.
Put
L=K(\rho)
,
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)\simeq\mathrm{im}(\rho)
.

Assume that
L/K
is totally ramified.

There are two natural filtrations on
G
:

•

the
Lie filtration
, coming from the codomain of
\rho
,

\ldots G_{n+1}\subseteq G_{n}\subseteq G_{n-1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq G_{0}=G

where

G_{n}=G\cap(1+p^{n}\mathrm{M}_{d}(\mathcal{O})).

•

the
higher ramification groups filtration
(upper notation), coming from the domain of
\rho
,

\ldots G^{n+1}\subseteq G^{n}\subseteq G^{n-1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq G^{0}=G

Serre asked how these two filtrations are related and, in particular, how the corresponding valuations on
G
depend on each other and on the ramification index
e_{K}
of the base field.

The same question can be formulated for any totally ramified
p
-adic Lie extension

L/K
, that is, an extension whose Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
is a
p
-adic Lie group. Indeed, the
p
-adic Lie structure endows
G:=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
with a
Lie filtration

(G_{n})
in the sense of
[
26
, §3]
, which can then be compared with the ramification filtration arising from the arithmetic structure of the extension.

Sen’s theorem

The answer to Serre question was provided in
[
25
, §4]

T h e o r e m  4.1

(Sen)
.

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic 0 and residue characteristic
p
. Let
L/F
be a totally ramified extension.
Assume that the Galois group
G=Gal(L/F)
is a
p
-adic Lie group, with
\dim(G)>0
.

Then, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

G^{ne+c}\subseteq G_{n}\subseteq G^{ne-c}

for all
n
, with
G^{r}=G
for
r<0
.

R e m a r k  4.2
.

Although Sen’s theorem was originally formulated for extensions of local fields, the requirement that the residue field is finite does not enter in a substantial way in the proof. In fact, as shown in
[
19
]
, the statement remains valid for totally ramified Galois extensions of fields of characteristic
0
, complete with respect to a discrete non-Archimedean valuation and with perfect residue field of characteristic
p>0
.
In particular, it holds when
F
is the completion of an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with a finite ramification index.

Sen’s theorem says that in a
p
-adic Lie extension the layers of a Lie filtration control the upper indices of the higher ramification groups. By Herbrand theorem, one can then pass to the lower indices and provide local metric inequalities as for the Lubin-Tate extensions (which in fact are a particular case). Details can be found in
[
11
, §1]
for the case of finite residue field, and in
[
10
, §2]
in the general case.

In other words, Sen’s theorem allows one to replace the condition

a_{p}=0

by the assumption that

G\hbox{ is a $p$-adic Lie group}

in the
p
-part of the proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Property (B) for
p
-adic Lie extensions.

Using Sen’s theorem on the local part, as outlined above, has led to criteria ensuring the Bogomolov property for
p
-adic Lie extensions of number fields.

We present here the main result of
[
11
]
.
Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field,

\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
be a Galois extension with
\mathbb{G}=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K})
a (compact)
p
-adic Lie group.
Fix a non-Archimedean place
v
of
\mathbb{K}
dividing
p
; let
G=G_{v}
be a decomposition group of
\mathbb{G}
at
v
and
I_{v}\subset G_{v}
be its inertia subgroup.

The following conditions on
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
are introduced

(LPTR)

Locally potentially totally ramified
:
I_{v}
is open in
G_{v}
.

(NC)

Normal closure
: For a Lie filtration
(\mathbb{G}_{n})
of
\mathbb{G}
, and the associated local filtration
(G_{n})
of
G
, the normal closure in
\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{G}_{n}
of
G_{n-1}/G_{n}
coincides with
\mathbb{G}_{n-1}/\mathbb{G}_{n}
.

(CE)

Central element
:
\exists\,\tau\in Z(\mathbb{G})
acting on
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
via
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{g}
with
g\in\mathbb{Z},g>1
.

T h e o r e m  4.3
.

[
11
, Theorem 1]

If
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{K}
satisfies
(LPTR)
,
(NC)
, and
(CE)
at some
v
dividing
p
, then
\mathbb{L}
has the Bogomolov property
(B)
.

Recently, Theorem
4.3
has been generalized to the case where the base field
\mathbb{K}
is not necessarily a number field, but has finite ramification index at
v
, see
[
10
, Theorem 3.2]
.

4.3
Working on the prime-to-
p
part: supercuspidal modular forms

In the proof of Theorem
3.3
, the strong supersingular condition
a_{p}=0
was used in dealing with the prime-to-
p
-part in order to deduce that a power of
\mathrm{Frob}_{p}
is central, and then exploit ADZ lemma. At present, no new ideas have emerged that would allow the proof to be extended to the full generality predicted by Conjecture
3.1
.

Nevertheless, the ADZ lemma can be successfully applied to another important class of modular forms in order to handle the prime-to-
p
part of the Bogomolov property. Namely, one may consider modular forms that are
supercuspidal
at
p
, i.e. those whose local Weil–Deligne representation at
p
is irreducible.
By
[
30
, §2.2.1]
, irreducibility implies that the projective image of such a representation is finite, leading to the following

P r o p o s i t i o n  4.4
.

[
10
, Proposition 5.2]

Let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
be an eigenform, and let

\widehat{\rho}_{f}^{(p)}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}\Big(\prod_{\lambda\nmid p}\mathbb{K}_{f,\lambda}\Big)

be the prime-to-
p
part of the adelic Galois representation associated to
f
.
Let
\pi=\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
be the automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
associated to
f
and assume that its
p
-component
\pi_{p}
is supercuspidal.
Then the projective image of
G_{p}
through
\widehat{\rho}_{f}^{(p)}
is finite, hence
\widehat{\rho}_{f}^{(p)}
has
(B)
by Lemma
2.8
.

On the other hand the study of the
p
-part

\widehat{\rho}_{f,p}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{K}_{f}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}),

for
f
supercuspidal at
p
, is generally involved; indeed its restriction to
G_{p}
is a product of potentially crystalline representations which can be different from each other, although they share the same inertial type; it does not seem easy to show conditions (LPTR), (NC) and (CE) in this case. A partial result proving property (B) for the whole adelic representation associated to some
p
-supercuspidal forms is presented in
[
10
, Theorem A]
.

5
Representations with big local image

In the remainder of this article, we present some applications of Theorem
4.3
to
p
-adic Galois representations, without assuming that they arise from modular forms or from geometry.

Indeed, the theorem can be applied to an extension
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}(\rho)
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a number field and
\rho
is a
continuous
p
-adic representation of
G_{\mathbb{K}}
.

A particularly simple situation arises when the image of the inertia at a place over
p
is
full
;

T h e o r e m  5.1
.

[
11
, Theorem 3.3]

Let
\rho:G_{\mathbb{K}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be a continuous representation
such that the image of an inertia subgroup at a place
v
over
p
is open in
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Then
\rho
has property
(B)
.

Indeed, an open inertia image is an open
p
-adic Lie subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, hence has the same dimension as
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Conditions (LPTR), (NC) and (CE) then follow readily.

5.1
The two-dimensional case

Recall that a representation of a profinite group
\Omega
is said to be
strongly absolutely irreducible
if its restriction to every open subgroup of
\Omega
is absolutely irreducible. Of course, if
\rho(I_{v})
is open in
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
then
\rho|_{G_{v}}
is strongly absolutely irreducible. When
d=2
, an analysis of the possible Lie algebras attached to the image of
\rho
leads to the following partial converse (see
[
11
, Corollary B.1.3 (
i
) and Proposition B.2.10]
):

P r o p o s i t i o n  5.2
.

Let
\rho:G_{\mathbb{K}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be a continuous representation.
Let
v
be a
p
-adic place of
\mathbb{K}
, with
decomposition group
G_{v}
and inertia subgroup
I_{v}
.

Assume that
\rho|_{G_{v}}
is strongly absolutely irreducible, and
that
\det\rho(I_{v})
is infinite.

Then
\rho(I_{v})
is open in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, and hence
\rho
has
(B)
.

A criterion for strong absolute irreducibility in dimension
2
.

When
d=2
, strong absolute irreducibility can be detected by means of the following characterization
[
11
, Corollary B.1.3]
:

P r o p o s i t i o n  5.3
.

A two-dimensional representation of a profinite group with values in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is strongly absolutely irreducible if and only if
it is absolutely irreducible, not induced from a character, and not a twist of a finite-image representation.

5.2
Applications

In this section, we present some applications of the criteria discussed above which, in our opinion, are particularly noteworthy. The interested reader is referred to
[
11
, Sections 4–7]
for a more extensive exposition.

The
p
-part of supersingular modular forms.

We recall that a normalized cuspidal eigenform

f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))

is said to be
supersingular
at
p
if
p
does not divide
N
and
v_{p}(a_{p})>0
. In this case the local
p
-adic representation is crystalline. When
k=2
and
a_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
, as is the case for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, the Hasse bound forces
a_{p}=0
; this is the situation referred to as
strong supersingularity
. For general weights and Hecke fields
\mathbb{K}_{f}
, supersingularity no longer implies strong supersingularity, and forms with
v_{p}(a_{p})>0
can satisfy
a_{p}\neq 0
.

The classification of irreducible two-dimensional crystalline representations with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
given in
[
7
, Prop. 3.1.1]
, combined with Proposition
5.3
, leads to the following

T h e o r e m  5.4
.

Let
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
be a normalized Hecke eigenform of weight
k\geq 2
, with Hecke field
\mathbb{K}_{f}
. Let
p
be a prime not dividing
N
and
v
a splitting
p
-adic place of
\mathbb{K}_{f}
such that
f
is supersingular at
v
.
If
a_{p}\not=0
then
\rho_{f,v}
has
(B)
.

It is worth noting that this criterion points in a direction opposite to that of Habegger’s and Amoroso–Terracini’s arguments: the strong supersingularity condition
a_{p}=0
occurs precisely when the local representation is induced from a character, a situation excluded by strong absolute irreducibility.

Elliptic curves over a number field.

Habegger’s argument does not extend to elliptic curves defined over
an arbitrary number field, essentially because Elkies’ theorem is not known in
such generality. In fact, it is proved in
[
23
, Theorem 1.4]
that
the Bogomolov property holds for the adelic representation

\widehat{\rho}_{E}:G_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})

associated with an elliptic curve
E
defined over a number field
\mathbb{K}
,
provided that the curve has infinitely many supersingular primes. In particular,
this applies to every elliptic curve when
\mathbb{K}
has at least one real
embedding
[
23
, Corollary 1.5]
.

Outside this setting, Theorem
4.3
sometimes allows us to
recover property (B) for the
p
-adic representation

\rho_{E,p}:G_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

or, equivalently, for the field
\mathbb{K}(E[p^{\infty}])
.

T h e o r e m  5.5
.

[
11
, Theorem 6.1]

Assume that
E_{/\mathbb{K}}
is non-CM and that there exists a rational prime
p

such that:

i)

for some
p
-adic place
v
of
\mathbb{K}
, the restriction

\rho_{E,p}|_{G_{v}}

is absolutely irreducible;

ii)

the residual representation

\bar{\rho}_{E,p}:G_{\mathbb{K}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

is surjective.

Then
\rho_{E,p}
has property

(B)
.

By Serre’s big image theorem, for any fixed non-CM elliptic curve
E
, condition
ii)
holds for all sufficiently large primes
p
.

Property (B) in families.

One of the advantages of the methods described above is that they behave well in
p
-adic analytic families of Galois representations.

This framework encompasses several well-known examples, including:

•

universal deformation spaces attached to a fixed residual representation
\bar{\rho}
;

•

Hida families;

•

non-ordinary components of the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve.

Let

\{\rho_{x}:G_{\mathbb{K}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\}_{x\in X}

be such a family over a rigid-analytic space
X
, and fix a place
v
of
\mathbb{K}
, dividing
p
.

After removing a finite union of proper analytic subsets, and under suitable hypotheses, one obtains an open subset
U\subset X
such that for every
x\in U
:

1.

\rho_{x}|_{G_{v}}
is absolutely irreducible and not induced;

2.

the Hodge–Tate–Sen weights satisfy
k_{x,1}\neq k_{x,2}
;

3.

\det\rho_{x}(I_{v})
has infinite image in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

By applying Theorem
4.3
, the following

generic Bogomolov property
is deduced:

For every
x\in U
, the representation
\rho_{x}
has property
(B)
.

The interested reader can find precise statements and complete proofs in Section 7 of
[
11
]
.

A natural question concerns the existence of a uniform lower bound for the height throughout the family. We hope to return to this problem in future work.

Beyond condition (CE): the
p
-adic Rémond conjecture.

Although somewhat ad hoc and rather mysterious, the central element condition plays a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
4.3
. For example, one can easily verify that the extension
\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{Q}
, with

\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}\bigl(\mu_{p^{\infty}},\,\sqrt[p^{n}]{2}\,:\,n\in\mathbb{N}\bigr)

satisfies conditions (LPTR) and (NC), whereas the central element condition (CE) fails, because the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{Q})
is the semidirect product
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, whose center is trivial.
Obviously,
\mathbb{L}
does not have (B), since

h\bigl(\sqrt[p^{n}]{2}\bigr)=\frac{\log 2}{p^{n}}\to 0\hbox{ for }n\to\infty.

Other sequences in
\mathbb{L}
whose heights tend to zero can of course be obtained from
(\sqrt[p^{n}]{2})
by multiplying its terms by roots of unity. A natural question is whether all sequences in
\mathbb{L}^{\times}
whose heights tend to
0
are essentially of this form.

This question would have an affirmative answer if the following more general conjecture, due to Rémond
[
20
, Conjecture 3.4]
, were proved.

C o n j e c t u r e 5.6

(Rémond)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field, and

\Gamma\leq\mathbb{K}^{\times}
be a non-torsion finitely generated subgroup.
Put
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}(\Gamma^{\rm{div}})
.
The Weil height is bounded from below on
\mathbb{L}^{\times}\setminus\Gamma^{\rm{div}}
.

Here
\Gamma^{\rm{div}}
denotes the divisible subgroup of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
generated by
\Gamma
:

\Gamma^{\rm{div}}=\{\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}\ |\ \alpha^{n}\in\Gamma,\hbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Although Rémond’s conjecture appears to lie beyond the scope of our methods, its
p
-adic counterpart seems approachable via the techniques on
p
-adic Lie extensions described above.

In other words, we can replace ”divisible” by ”
p
-divisible” in Conjecture
5.6
and formulate the following weaker version:

C o n j e c t u r e 5.7

(Rémond,
p
-adic version)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field, and

\Gamma\leq\mathbb{K}^{\times}
be a non-torsion finitely generated subgroup.
Put
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{K}(\Gamma^{p-\rm{div}})
.
The Weil height is bounded from below on
\mathbb{L}^{\times}\setminus\Gamma^{p-\rm{div}}
.

Here
\Gamma^{p-\rm{div}}
denotes the
p
-divisible subgroup of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
generated by
\Gamma
:

\Gamma^{p-\rm{div}}=\{\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}\ |\ \alpha^{p^{n}}\in\Gamma,\hbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

When
\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)=1
, Conjecture
5.7
has been proved by Amoroso
[
5
]
, for
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q},\,\Gamma=\langle\alpha\rangle
where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
and
p
is an odd rational prime not satisfying the Wieferich condition (that is,
p
does not divide
\alpha
and
p^{2}
does not divide
\alpha^{p-1}-1
).
A generalization is due to Plessis
[
18
, Théorème 1.8]
, who established the same result when
\mathbb{K}
is any number field,
\alpha
is any element of
\mathbb{K}
, and
p
is any odd rational prime not dividing the discriminant of
\mathbb{K}
.

A modified version of Theorem
4.3
, which does not require condition (CE), appears to be well suited to the study of Conjecture
5.7
in the general case.

More precisely, it should allow one to:

•

establish Conjecture
5.7
when

\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)>1
; unlike the rank-one case, the verification of conditions (LPTR) and (NC) are no longer immediate, but can nevertheless be carried out in full generality;

•

replace a single prime
p
by a finite set
S
of primes through a recursive argument.

These results, obtained in collaboration with Andrea Conti, Ilaria Del Corso, and Arnaud Plessis, are currently being finalized and will appear in a forthcoming paper.
