Genetic dissection of leg and back feather follicle density in yellow-feathered broiler: integrative GWAS and transcriptome analysis reveal the regulatory role of SERPINF1
Authors: Xiaohuan Chao, Shuya Chen, Tong OuYang, Chutian Ye, Jie Chen, Jiongwen Wu, XueRong Ma, Aijun Liu, Weiming Liang, Tianxi Cui, Lulu Xia, Kaifan Xiao, Youcheng He, Xiquan Zhang, Cheng Fang, Qingbin Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn recent years, chilled chicken has emerged as a new market trend. Skin feather follicle density, an important carcass appearance trait characterized by "fine and dense" features, is increasingly preferred by consumers. This study analyzed hair follicle density in the leg and back regions of 1,382 yellow-feathered broiler. The results showed that back feather follicle density was significantly higher than that of the leg (P < 0.01), with a significant positive correlation between the two regions. Meanwhile, feather follicle density in both the leg and back showed negative correlations with abdominal fat weight, body weight, dressed weight, full-eviscerated weight, semi-eviscerated weight. mRNA-seq analysis of leg and back skin tissues from four individuals identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) affecting feather follicle density traits across different anatomical locations, including GTF2A1, RPSA2, COX3, and CNKSR2. Furthermore, Genome-Wide Association Study (GWAS) analysis of 505 chickens identified 223 single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) significantly associated with leg feather follicle density and 107 SNPs associated with back feather follicle density, including candidate SNPs such as C19014967G, C17514817T, G1847078C, and C34389948G, as well as candidate genes including ELF5, MAP3K1, HIF1AN, and SERPINF1. mRNA-seq analysis of skin tissues from high and low feather follicle density groups in the leg (TG vs. TD) and back (BG vs. BD) identified 541 leg-related DEGs and 1,130 back-related DEGs, respectively. Joint analysis of TG vs. TD and BG vs. BD revealed 174 commonly DEGs, including candidate genes such as NSA2, IGF2, RPL17, and NRP1. Through integrated GWAS and RNA-seq analysis, five genes significantly associated with leg feather follicle density and twelve candidate genes associated with back feather follicle density were identified. Notably, individuals with the AA genotype at the SERPINF1 SNP locus Chr19:5636537 showed extremely significantly higher feather follicle density than those with the GG genotype, and both mRNA and protein expression levels of SERPINF1 were significantly upregulated in high feather follicle density individuals.Keywords: Feather follicle density, GWAS, mRNA-seq, Chr19:5636537, SERPINF1

Introduction

With the rapid development of chilled marketing models for yellow-feathered broiler, consumers are paying increasing attention to carcass appearance traits such as skin color, hair follicle size, and hair follicle density. The poultry hair follicle is a dynamic mini-organ that integrates functions of morphogenesis, environmental adaptation, and social communication (Bavananthasivam et al., 2022; Schneider et al., 2009; van Staaveren et al., 2021). Hair follicles are typically associated with sebaceous glands, whose secretions help lubricate feathers and maintain skin moisture (Lee and Choi, 2024; Veniaminova et al., 2023). Unlike mammals, birds lack sweat glands and primarily regulate body temperature through respiration and feathers (Goel, 2021). Nevertheless, the genetic regulatory mechanisms underlying feather follicle density in yellow-feathered broilers remain poorly understood, which limits effective selection for this trait in breeding programs. Thus, there is a need to systematically elucidate its molecular basis at the genomic level.

The multilayered structure of the feather follicle, derived from epidermal and dermal tissues, provides physical protection and coordinately regulates feather growth, differentiation, and regeneration through the interaction between its inner layer cells and bulb cells (Alibardi, 2010; Alibardi and Toni, 2008; An et al., 2022; Muller-Rover et al., 1998; Yu et al., 2002; Yue et al., 2012). The dermal papilla, located at the base of the follicle and composed of dense mesenchymal cells, supplies essential nutrients and signaling molecules to the follicle and directly modulates its cyclic activity and feather quality (Alibardi, 2017; Chuong et al., 2013; Painter et al., 2021). Follicle development comprises two critical stages: the embryonic and postnatal periods, each characterized by distinct biological features and regulatory mechanisms (Li et al., 2025a; Ma et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). The morphogenesis of feather follicles primarily occurs between embryonic days 9 and 17 (Feng et al., 2022). Postnatally, follicle activity follows a cyclic pattern, successively progressing through anagen (active growth), catagen (apoptosis-mediated regression), and telogen (relative quiescence), corresponding to rapid structural expansion, follicle atrophy and growth arrest, and regenerative preparation, respectively (Botchkarev and Paus, 2003; Kinde et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2022).

In recent years, high-throughput sequencing technologies have provided effective approaches for dissecting the genetic basis of feather follicle traits in poultry. Liu et al. performed transcriptome analysis of follicles from Bashang Long-tailed chickens with different plumage colors using RNA-Seq, identifying pigmentation-related candidate genes, including KRT75, SOX10, and SLC45A2 (Liu et al., 2019). Ji et al. conducted transcriptome sequencing of follicle tissues from the back and leg skin of chickens, revealing significant differences in follicle density between these two anatomical regions, and further confirmed through SNP association analysis that the AC genotype of the Wnt3a SNP g.255361 C>A significantly increased leg follicle density (Ji et al., 2021). These studies have elucidated local regulatory mechanisms of follicle development; however, genome-wide genetic dissection and molecular regulatory network studies targeting follicle density in yellow-feathered broilers remain scarce, particularly lacking systematic investigations that integrate population genetic analysis with gene expression regulation.

Based on this, the present study used yellow-feathered broilers as the research subject. First, by analyzing the coefficient of variation for follicle density across three generations, we confirmed the presence of abundant genetic variation within the population, providing a foundation for genetic improvement of this trait. Subsequently, by integrating GWAS with mRNA-seq technology, we systematically identified candidate SNP loci and functional genes associated with follicle density, aiming to elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanisms of follicle density in yellow-feathered broilers from both genetic variation and gene expression perspectives. The findings will provide candidate markers and theoretical support for early molecular selection of follicle density in Yellow-feathered broilers, offer scientific underpinning for the breeding of high-quality chilled chicken products that meet consumer aesthetic preferences, and enrich the theoretical understanding of genetic regulatory mechanisms for avian skin appendages, thereby holding significant applied value and scientific importance.

Materials and methods

Animal testing and ethical statements

In this study, a total of 1,382 three-generation TC line of medium-speed yellow-feathered broiler, provided by Guangzhou Kwangfeng Seed Industry Technology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China), were raised under identical environmental conditions following standardized management protocols. At 77 days of age, blood sample collection and euthanasia were performed. Feather follicle density in the leg and back regions was measured using a 2-cm square wire mesh grid. The number of follicles within the grid area was counted and divided by the enclosed area to calculate follicle density, expressed as follicles per square centimeter (feather follicle/cm²). All animal procedures were approved by the Animal Care Committee of South China Agricultural University [SCAU#2021F058; March 4, 2021].

Genome-wide resequencing and GWAS

Genomic DNA was extracted from 505 chickens and subjected to whole-genome resequencing (6 × coverage) at Beijing Biomarker Technologies Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China), the reference genome GRCg6a_release95 was used. Genome-wide association analysis was performed using the linear mixed model (LMM) implemented in GEMMA software, integrating phenotypic and genotypic data to identify SNPs associated with feather follicle density traits. mRNA-seq

Yellow-feathered broiler (77-day-old) from the same batch were selected, with four tissue samples per group. The experimental groups were designated as follows: BG group (high back feather follicle density), BD group (low back feather follicle density); TG group (high leg feather follicle density), and TD group (low leg feather follicle density); B group (back feather follicle tissue) and T group (leg skin tissue from the same individuals as group B). The feather follicle density phenotype is shown in Supplementary Table 1. Samples were sent to Beijing Biomarker Technologies Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) for transcriptome sequencing. The obtained reads were mapped to the reference genome Gallus_gallus.GRCg7b, followed by Principal component analysis (PCA) and Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA). DEGs were identified using the criteria of |Fold Change| > 1.5 and FDR < 0.05. Subsequent analyses included Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses and Protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis.

Western bolt

For immunoblotting analysis, total protein lysates were prepared in ice-cold RIPA buffer. Equal amounts of protein were resolved on 12% SDS-PAGE gels (EpiZyme, Shanghai, China) under 120 V constant voltage for 1 h, followed by electrotransfer to PVDF membranes (Bio-Rad, CA, USA) at 330 mA. Following blocking with Western Blocking Buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 1 h at room temperature and subsequent triple TBST washes, membranes were cut according to target protein sizes. The sections were then probed overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against SERPINF1 (1:1000, A11782, Abcam) and GAPDH (1:5000, AC054, Abcam). After three TBST rinses, the membranes were exposed to HRP-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG at room temperature for 60 minutes, washed thrice with TBST, and developed using an a high-sensitivity ECL chemiluminescence kit. Immunoreactive bands were captured via ChemiDoc XRS+ imaging system (Bio-Rad) and densitometrically analyzed using ImageJ. Each experiment was performed with at four biological replicates.

Data statistics and analysis

Phenotypic data were preliminarily organized using Microsoft Excel. Subsequently, IBM SPSS Statistics 26 was employed to calculate means, standard deviations (SD), and coefficient of variation (CV). One-way ANOVA was conducted to test the significance of differences among the data.

Results

Analysis of feather follicle density trait phenotypic data in yellow-feathered broiler

To investigate the genetic variation characteristics of feather follicle density and its relationship with slaughter traits in yellow-feathered broiler, this study measured the leg and back feather follicle density of 1,382 birds across three generations. The results showed that back feather follicle density was significantly higher than leg density (P<0.01), with a significant positive correlation between the two traits. Leg feather follicle density exhibited highly significant sexual dimorphism (P<0.01). Additionally, the CV for feather follicle density exceeded 10% in all three generations, indicating substantial genetic variation for this trait (Table 1). Correlation analysis further revealed that both leg and back feather follicle density were negatively correlated with abdominal fat weight, body weight, dressed weight, full-eviscerated weight, semi-eviscerated weight in both males and females (Fig. 1 A-B).

Comparative mRNA-seq of skin tissues from leg regions (T) and back regions (B)

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms contributing to the significant difference in feather follicle density between the leg regions and back, we performed mRNA-seq on skin tissues collected from four randomly selected yellow-feathered broiler. DEGs were identified using criteria of |log₂FC| > 1.5 and FDR < 0.05. Comparative analysis revealed 169 up-regulated and 82 down-regulated genes in leg skin relative to back skin (Fig. 2 A-C). The top 15 significantly DEGs are listed in Table 2, including GTF2A1, RPSA2, COX3, and CNKSR2. Functional enrichment analyses (GO and KEGG) showed that these genes were mainly enriched in cytoplasm, nucleus, metabolic pathway, and oxidative phosphorylation pathways (Fig. 2 D-E). Furthermore, PPI network analysis identified HBEGF, ND6, NDUFC2, and ND5 as core hub genes (Fig. 2 F).

GWAS of feather follicle traits in yellow-feathered broiler

To identify key genetic loci controlling feather follicle density, whole-genome resequencing was performed on 505 chickens, and genome-wide association analysis was conducted for feather follicle traits in the leg and back regions of yellow-feathered broiler.

Using the threshold of -log10(p) > 5, a total of 223 SNP loci significantly associated with leg feather follicle density traits were identified (Fig. 3 A-B). These SNPs were distributed across 15 chromosomes including Chr 1, Chr 3, Chr W, and Chr Z (Fig. 3 C). Annotation of these 223 loci revealed that they were primarily located within 282 genes. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses showed that these genes were mainly enriched in ATP binding, cytoplasm, metabolic pathways, and calcium signaling pathways (Fig. 3 D-E). Table 3 lists candidate loci including C7468861T, C19014967G, and C17514817T, along with genes such as NFAT5, ELF5 and MAP3K1 associated with leg feather follicle density.

mRNA-seq of skin tissues with low and high leg feather follicle density in yellow-feathered broiler

To further elucidate the genetic mechanisms underlying leg feather follicle density, skin tissues from the leg region were collected from chickens with low (TD, n = 4) and high (TG, n = 4) leg feather follicle density for mRNA sequencing. PCA of the mRNA-seq data demonstrated tight clustering of biological replicates within each group, indicating high reproducibility (Fig. 5 A). WGCNA revealed that the brown2 module (MEbrown2) was significantly associated with leg feather follicle density (P = 8 × 10⁻⁵), comprising 4,791 genes, including SERPINF1, WNT11, and SLC26A2 (Fig. 5 B-C). Subsequently, DEGs were identified using the criteria of fold change > 1.5 and FDR < 0.05. A total of 541 DEGs were detected between the TD and TG groups, including 291 upregulated genes (Fig. 5 D-E). Such as KLF12, CHD1, SMPDL3B, SLC33A1, and ARHGEF7. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of these DEGs revealed significant enrichment in oxidative phosphorylation and the phosphatidylinositol signaling system (Fig. 5 F). GO analysis indicated that the DEGs were primarily enriched in cellular process, cellular anatomical entity, and binding (Fig. 5 G).

mRNA-seq of back skin tissues with low and high feather follicle density in yellow-feathered broiler

To further elucidate the genetic mechanisms governing back feather follicle density, back skin tissues were collected from chickens with high (BG) and low (BD) density groups (n = 4 per group) for mRNA sequencing. PCA of the transcriptome data revealed tight clustering of biological replicates within each group, indicating high reproducibility (Fig. 6 A). WGCNA identified the darkseagreen2 module (MEdarkseagreen2) as significantly associated with back feather follicle density (P = 0.009), comprising 5,024 genes including SERPINF1, TMEM104, and SLC4A11 (Fig. 6 B). Statistical cutoffs of |fold change| > 1.5 and FDR < 0.05 were applied for differential expression analysis, resulting in the identification of 1,130 DEGs between the BG and BD groups, comprising 540 upregulated and 590 downregulated genes (Fig. 6 C-D). Such as SLC8A3, SREBF2, MPP7, and SERPINF1. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed that these DEGs were significantly enriched in focal adhesion, MAPK signaling pathway, and neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction (Fig. 6 E). GO enrichment analysis indicated that the DEGs were primarily associated with cellular process, cellular anatomical entity, and catalytic activity (Fig. 6 F).

Joint analysis of DEGs in the leg and back

To further investigate key genes influencing feather follicle density in both leg and back skin of yellow-feathered broiler, a joint analysis was conducted on DEGs identified from TD vs. TG (TDEGs) and BG vs. BD (BDEGs) comparisons. The results revealed 174 overlapping DEGs common to both comparisons (Fig. 7 A-B). GO enrichment analysis showed that these genes were significantly enriched in membrane raft, plasma membrane, and zinc ion binding (Fig. 7 C). PPI network analysis identified NSA2, IGF2, RPL17, and NRP1 as core hub genes located in the central region of the network (Fig. 7 D).

Integrative GWAS and mRNA analysis of feather follicle density in yellow-feathered broiler

By integrating GWAS and mRNA data, we conducted an in-depth analysis of the genetic architecture and molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying feather follicle density in yellow-feathered broiler. Our results identified five genes significantly associated with leg feather follicle density and twelve candidate genes related to back feather follicle density (Fig. 8 A-D). PPI network analysis further revealed that SERPINF1 interacts with WDR81, and CHAC1 interacts with ATP10D (Fig. 8 E). To validate the regulatory role of SERPINF1 in back feather follicle density, we performed genotyping of significantly associated SNP loci and found that the polymorphism at Chr19:5636537 was significantly correlated with the phenotype, with individuals carrying the AA genotype exhibiting significantly higher follicle density than those with the GG genotype (Table 5). Additionally, both mRNA and protein expression levels of SERPINF1 were significantly upregulated in high-back feather follicle density individuals (Fig. 8 D, F-G), suggesting that this gene may regulate feather follicle development through a dosage-dependent mechanism.

Discussion

Feather follicle density is an important economic trait in poultry, regulated by multiple factors including genetics, nutrition, disease, and environment (Chen et al., 2019; Humberto Vilar Da Silva et al., 2019; Vilar da Silva et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2015). In this study, yellow-feathered broiler were used as the research object. GWAS and mRNA-seq technologies were employed to genetically dissect feather follicle density in the leg and back regions, respectively. Significantly associated candidate loci and functional genes were identified. These findings will provide a theoretical basis for revealing the molecular genetic mechanisms of feather follicle development in poultry, and lay the foundation for molecular breeding of yellow-feathered broiler.

In this study, through GWAS screening, the loci C7468861T, C19014967G, and C17514817T were identified, along with candidate genes including NFAT5, ELF5, and Map3k1 associated with feather follicle density. Studies have shown that ELF5 regulates the proliferation and differentiation of keratinocytes and is specifically highly expressed in the hair follicle bulge region and hair germ stem cells, playing important roles in skin and hair development and cycle regulation (Hu et al., 2025). Furthermore, as a key regulator of the MAPK signaling pathway, Map3k1 has crucial functions in hair follicle cycle transition. Li et al. confirmed that Fgf21 knockout induces upregulation of miR-136-5p expression, which subsequently targets and inhibits Map3k1 translation, leading to delayed transition of hair follicles from telogen to anagen (Li et al., 2025b). These findings suggest that C19014967G and C17514817T may serve as potential genetic markers associated with feather follicle density in yellow-feathered broiler.

Through integrated analysis of DEGs between the TD and TG groups (TDEGs) as well as between the BG and BD groups (BDEGs), and construction of a PPI protein-protein interaction network to screen for core hub genes, IGF2 was identified as a key regulatory gene. Previous studies have demonstrated that IGF2 exhibits differential expression patterns during hair follicle development in Cashmere goats (Qin et al., 2025); Bhat et al. further confirmed that IGF2 is significantly upregulated during the active growth phase of hair follicles in Pashmina goats (Capra hircus) (Bhat et al., 2021). The present findings are consistent with these reports: IGF2 expression levels were significantly elevated in both the TG group and the BG group. Based on these results, we hypothesize that IGF2 may regulate the telogen-anagen transition efficiency and regenerative capacity of feather follicles, thereby influencing the number of functionally active follicles in leg and back skin regions of yellow-feathered broiler.

SERPINF1 (Serpin family F member 1) is a member of the serpin superfamily but lacks serine protease inhibitory activity (Steele et al., 1993). It plays crucial roles in various biological processes, including osteoporosis and neuronal differentiation (Rajagopal et al., 2016; Song et al., 2023; Tian et al., 2025). Previous studies have identified SERPINF1 as a characteristic marker gene of the hair follicle bulge region, utilizing it to evaluate the impact of spaceflight on the hair follicle stem cell microenvironment, where its expression was found to be upregulated in certain individuals (Terada et al., 2016). Liu et al. reported that SERPINF1 is expressed at low levels in occipital hair follicles but is highly expressed in the vertex region; their study further demonstrated that downregulation of SERPINF1 promotes hair follicle growth, whereas recombinant SERPINF1 protein significantly inhibits hair follicle growth (Liu et al., 2022). Interestingly, our findings contradict those of Liu et al. In the present study, both mRNA and protein expression levels of SERPINF1 were significantly upregulated in the back skin of chickens with high feather follicle density. We hypothesize that, in chickens, SERPINF1 may promote the formation of high-density feather follicles through regulating inter-follicular competition or extracellular matrix remodeling, rather than through direct inhibition of cell proliferation.

In conclusion, this study, through GWAS and RNA-seq technologies, elucidated the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying feather follicle density traits in yellow-feathered broiler, identified multiple associated candidate SNP loci and genes, and revealed that the AA genotype at Chr19:5636537 and high expression of SERPINF1 are both associated with high feather follicle density. These findings provide a theoretical basis and potential targets for in-depth investigation into the genetic mechanisms of feather follicle density and molecular breeding in yellow-feathered broiler.
