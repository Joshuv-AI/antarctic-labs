On Ramanujan Primes for Hecke-Maass cusp forms
Tinghao Huang and Shifan Zhao

Abstract
. For a primitive Hecke-Maass cusp form ϕ \phi of level N N with the n n -th Hecke eigenvalue λ ϕ ​ ( n ) \lambda_{\phi}(n) and a prime number p ∤ N p\nmid N , the celebrated Ramanujan conjecture at p p asserts the following sharp upper bound: | λ ϕ ​ ( p ) | ≤ 2 . |\lambda_{\phi}(p)|\leq 2. 1.
Introduction

The celebrated Ramanujan conjecture for a primitive holomorphic cuspidal Hecke eigenform
f
of weight
k\geq 2
and level
N
asserts that for primes
p\nmid N
, the following sharp bound for the
p
-th Hecke eigenvalue
\lambda_{f}(p)
for
f
holds:

|\lambda_{f}(p)|\leq 2.

This conjecture has been solved affirmatively by Deligne in
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
as a consequence of his solution to the Weil conjectures.

Now for a primitive Hecke-Maass cusp form
\phi
of level
N
with the
n
-th Hecke eigenvalue
\lambda_{\phi}(n)
and a prime number
p\nmid N
, the Ramanujan conjecture for
\phi
at
p
predicts the following sharp upper bound:

|\lambda_{\phi}(p)|\leq 2.

This conjecture, though unsolved, is believed to be true for any prime not dividing
N
, just as in the holomorphic case. This is an outstanding unsolved problem in number theory, which would follow from the solution to the Langlands functoriality conjectures.
The current best result towards the Ramanujan conjecture for
\phi
is due to Kim and Sarnak
[
7
]
, which states that for
p\nmid N

(1.1)

|\lambda_{\phi}(p)|\leq p^{\frac{7}{64}}+p^{-\frac{7}{64}}.

Let
\phi
be a primitive Hecke-Maass cusp form of level
N
and central character
\chi_{\phi}
. Define

\text{RP}(\phi):=\{p\nmid N:|\lambda_{\phi}(p)|\leq 2\}

to be the set of primes
p
at which the Ramanujan conjecture holds for
\phi
.
In
[
10
]
, Luo and Zhou investigated two problems related to the Ramanujan conjecture for primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms:

(1)

Given
\phi
of level
N
, can we find an upper bound for the
least

p\in\text{RP}(\phi)
?

(2)

Given
\phi
of level
N
, can we find a lower bound for the
lower natural density
for
\text{RP}(\phi)
in the set of all prime numbers?

The authors provided affirmative answers to both of the above questions, and we refer the readers to
[
10
, Theorem 1, Theorem 2]
for more details.
We also remark that both of the above problems have been investigated in higher rank settings, see for example
[
15
, Theorem B]
and
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
.

The goal of this paper is to investigate the above questions in the context of multiple primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms.

In the first part of this paper, we determine upper bounds for the least prime at which the Ramanujan conjecture is true for two or three distinct primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms
simultaneously
.
Our results state the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be two primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms of levels
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
and Laplacian eigenvalues
\frac{1}{4}+t_{\phi_{1}}^{2}
and
\frac{1}{4}+t_{\phi_{1}}^{2}
, we may find a prime number
p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cap\text{RP}(\phi_{2})
such that

p\ll[N_{1}N_{2}(1+|t_{\phi_{1}}|)(1+|t_{\phi_{2}}|)]^{0.447374}

where the implied constant is absolute.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
and
\phi_{3}
be three primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms of levels
N_{1},N_{2}
and
N_{3}
and Laplacian eigenvalues
\frac{1}{4}+t_{\phi_{1}}^{2},\frac{1}{4}+t_{\phi_{2}}^{2}
and
\frac{1}{4}+t_{\phi_{3}}^{2}
, we may find a prime number
p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cap\text{RP}(\phi_{2})\cap\text{RP}(\phi_{3})
such that

p\ll[N_{1}N_{2}N_{3}(1+|t_{\phi_{1}}|)(1+|t_{\phi_{2}}|)(1+|t_{\phi_{3}}|)]^{0.778798},

where the implied constant is absolute.

In the second part of this paper, we shall investigate the (lower) natural density of the set of prime numbers at which the Ramanujan conjecture holds for at least one element in a given family of primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms.

Before stating our results we review some necessary backgrounds on natural densities of sets.
Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of prime numbers, and
\pi(X):=\sum_{p\leq X}1
be the prime counting function. For a subset
A\subset\mathcal{P}
, recall that the upper natural density
\overline{d}(A)
and the lower natural density
\underline{d}(A)
of
A
inside
\mathcal{P}
are defined by

\displaystyle\overline{d}(A):=\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{|\{p\leq X:p\in A\}|}{\pi(X)},

\displaystyle\underline{d}(A):=\liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{|\{p\leq X:p\in A\}|}{\pi(X)},

respectively. If
\overline{d}(A)=\underline{d}(A)
, then we define
d(A):=\overline{d}(A)=\underline{d}(A)
and call it the natural density of
A
inside
\mathcal{P}
.

In
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
, Luo and Zhou proved

(1.2)

\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi))\geq\frac{34}{35}.

Now let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be two distinct primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms of level
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
as before. Two natural questions on
\text{RP}(\phi_{1})
and
\text{RP}(\phi_{2})
arise:

(1)

How large can
\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cap\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))
be? In other words, what is the lower natural density of the set of primes at which the Ramanujan conjecture holds for
both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
?

(2)

How large can
\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cup\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))
be? In other words, what is the lower natural density of the set of primes at which the Ramanujan conjecture holds for
at least one of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
?

Note that for either question above, it is natural to assume
\{p:p\nmid N_{2},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\}\neq\{p:p\nmid N_{1},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{2})\}
. Since otherwise
\text{RP}(\phi_{1})
and
\text{RP}(\phi_{2})
differs only by a finite set of primes at most, and the question is no longer interesting.

For question (1) above, by the Pigeonhole principle and (
1.2
) we have

\displaystyle\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cap\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))

\displaystyle\geq\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{1}))+\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))-\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cup\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{34}{35}+\frac{34}{35}-1=\frac{33}{35}.

For question (2) above, by (
1.2
) we have trivially

(1.3)

\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cup\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))\geq\max\{\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{1})),\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))\}\geq\frac{34}{35}.

In section 3 we shall improve the bound (
1.3
) by establishing the following theorem:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be two primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms of level
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
. Assuming
\{p:p\nmid N_{2},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\}\neq\{p:p\nmid N_{1},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{2})\}
, we have

(1.4)

\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cup\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))\geq\frac{43}{44}.

Our method of proving Theorem
3
generalizes from two forms to any finite number of primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms:

Theorem 4
.

Let
\phi_{i}
be primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms for
i=1,...,m
of levels
N_{1},...,N_{m}
respectively. Assuming
\{p:p\nmid N_{j},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{i})\}\neq\{p:p\nmid N_{i},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{j})\}
for any
i\neq j
, we have

(1.5)

\underline{d}(\cup_{j=1}^{m}\text{RP}(\phi_{j}))\geq 1-\frac{1}{26+9m}.

Note that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{26+9m}=0
. Thus a direct corollary of Theorem
4
is the following:

Corollary 1
.

Let
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an infinite family of primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms, whose levels are respectively
N_{i}
’s for
i\in I
. Assuming
\{p:p\nmid N_{j},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{i})\}\neq\{p:p\nmid N_{i},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{j})\}
for any
i,j\in I,i\neq j
, we have

(1.6)

d(\cup_{i\in I}\text{RP}(\phi_{i}))=1.

From Corollary
1
it can be stated that for almost all primes
p
(in the sense of natural density), there exists some
i\in I
, such that
\phi_{i}
is unramified at
p
, and the Ramanujan conjecture holds for
\phi_{i}
at
p
.

2.
Proof of Theorem 1 and 2

2.1.
Preliminaries

Before proceeding the proof of the Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
, we review some necessary knowledge of the adjoint square
L
-functions of primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms.
Suppose
\phi
is a primitive Hecke-Maass cusp form of level
N
and central character
\chi_{\phi}
whose
n
-th Hecke eigenvalue is denoted as
\lambda_{\phi}(n)
, then the adjoint square
L
-function of
\phi
, denoted by
L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi))
, is defined as the following absolutely convergent Dirichlet series for
\Re(s)>1
:

L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi)):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{A_{\phi}(n)}{n^{s}}

where
A_{\phi}(n)=\sum_{k^{2}|n}\lambda_{\phi}(n^{2}/k^{4})\overline{\chi_{\phi}(n/k^{2})}
if
(n,N)=1
and
A_{\phi}(n)=0
otherwise. The
L
-function
L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi))
could be analytically continued to the entire complex plane, which satisfies a functional equation of the
s\mapsto 1-s
type.
Just as
\lambda_{\phi}(\cdot)
, the Dirichlet coefficient
A_{\phi}(\cdot)
is also multiplicative.
The
L
-function satisfies an Euler product expansion for
\Re s>1
:

(2.1)

L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi))=\prod_{p\nmid N}\bigg[1-\frac{A_{\phi}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{A_{\phi}(p)}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\bigg]^{-1}

Suppose the Laplacian eigenvalue of
\phi
is
\frac{1}{4}+t_{\phi}^{2}
, we shall set the conductor of
\phi
as

Q_{\phi}:=N^{2}(1+|t_{\phi}|)^{2}.

Then, as
\operatorname{Ad}(\phi)
is shown to be a cusp form for
GL(3)
in
[
3
]
whenever
\phi
is not of dihedral type, we have in this case the following convexity bound (see for example
[
4
, (5.20)]
and of
[
12
, Proposition 3.3]
) for
L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi))
on vertical lines
\Re s=\sigma
where
0<\sigma<1
:

(2.2)

L(\sigma+it,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi))\ll_{\epsilon}\big[Q_{\phi}\cdot(1+|t|)^{3}\big]^{\frac{1-\sigma}{2}+\epsilon}.

Now we prove Theorem 1.
We only need to determine an upper bound for
y\geq 2
, where
y
is taken such that for any prime number
p\leq y
with
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
, at least one of
|\lambda_{\phi_{1}}(p)|
and
|\lambda_{\phi_{2}}(p)|
is greater than
2
. We may also assume that both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are not dihedral, as otherwise either one of
\phi_{1}
or
\phi_{2}
already satisfies the Ramanujan conjecture at all unramified primes.

For this purpose, as in
[
13
]
and
[
10
]
, we firstly pick up the crucial observation that: if the Ramanujan conjecture is not satisfied at a prime
p\nmid N_{i}
, then the (twisted) Hecke eigenvalue shall be large at
p^{2}
by satisfying:
\lambda_{\phi_{i}}(p^{2})\overline{\chi_{\phi_{i}}(p)}>3
,
i=1,2
. This follows directly from the Hecke relation

(2.3)

\lambda_{\phi_{i}}(p^{2})\overline{\chi_{\phi_{i}}(p)}=|\lambda_{\phi_{i}}(p)|^{2}-1

in which the right hand side will be greater than 3 if
|\lambda_{\phi_{i}}(p)|>2
, and is not less than
-1
at all time as
|\lambda_{\phi_{i}}(p)|^{2}\geq 0
. We shall hence consider a relevant summation involving
\lambda_{\phi_{i}}(p^{2})\overline{\chi_{\phi_{i}}(p)}
’s whose length is controlled by a parameter
x
which is associated to
y
. Denoting this summation as
S(x)
for now, we determined an upper bound for
S(x)
using the analytic properties of the adjoint sqaure
L
-functions for
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
, and a lower bound by utilizing the observation (
2.3
) and the sieving machinery in
[
11
]
.

Let
y
be as before such that for any prime number
p\leq y
we have
|\lambda_{\phi_{1}}(p)|>2
or
|\lambda_{\phi_{2}}(p)|>2
, and we set
x=y^{U}
where
U\geq 1
is to be determined later. We define
B(\cdot)
as the Dirichlet convolution of
A_{\phi_{1}}(\cdot)
and
A_{\phi_{2}}(\cdot)
:

B(n):=[A_{\phi_{1}}*A_{\phi_{2}}](n)=\sum_{r|n}A_{\phi_{1}}(r)A_{\phi_{2}}(n/r).

Hence
B(\cdot)
is again multiplicative, and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{B(n)}{n^{s}}=L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1}))\cdot L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2}))

for
\Re s>1
.
In particular, for
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
,

B(p)=A_{\phi_{1}}(p)+A_{\phi_{2}}(p)=\lambda_{\phi_{1}}(p^{2})\overline{\chi_{\phi_{1}}(p)}+\lambda_{\phi_{2}}(p^{2})\overline{\chi_{\phi_{2}}(p)}

as
A_{\phi_{i}}(p)=\lambda_{\phi_{i}}(p^{2})\overline{\chi_{\phi_{i}}(p)}
for
i=1,2
.
By (
2.3
) we have
B(p)>3+(-1)=2
if (
) fails, and
B(p)\geq-2
in all cases.

We shall be considering the following:

S(x):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq x\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}B(n)\log(x/n),

where
\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}
denotes summing over square-free numbers.

We shall derive an upper bound for
S(x)
of the shape

x^{\sigma+\epsilon}\cdot Q_{\phi_{1}}^{\frac{1-\sigma}{2}+\epsilon}Q_{\phi_{2}}^{\frac{1-\sigma}{2}+\epsilon}

where
\sigma<1
for
\epsilon>0
small,
as well as a lower bound

S(x)\gg_{U}x(\log x)^{2}

where
U\geq 1
is a suitable constant to be determined.
From the these bounds Theorem
1
follows directly.

2.2.
Upper bound for
S(x)

Using the analytic properties of
L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{i}))
’s, we shall prove the following:

Lemma 1
.

We have for any
\frac{23}{32}+\epsilon<\sigma<1
:

S(x)\ll_{\epsilon}x^{\sigma+\epsilon}\cdot Q_{\phi_{1}}^{\frac{1-\sigma}{2}+\epsilon}Q_{\phi_{2}}^{\frac{1-\sigma}{2}+\epsilon}

where the implied constant depends only on
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

We define the following auxiliary Euler product:

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle:=\prod_{p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}}\bigg[\bigg(1-\frac{A_{\phi_{1}}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{A_{\phi_{1}}(p)}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{A_{\phi_{2}}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{A_{\phi_{2}}(p)}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\bigg)\bigg]\cdot\bigg[1+\frac{B(p)}{p^{s}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid N_{1}\\
p|N_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigg(1-\frac{A_{\phi_{1}}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{A_{\phi_{1}}(p)}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\bigg)\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid N_{2}\\
p|N_{1}\end{subarray}}\bigg(1-\frac{A_{\phi_{2}}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{A_{\phi_{2}}(p)}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\bigg)

By (
1.1
) and (
2.3
), we have

(2.4)

|A_{\phi_{i}}(p)|\leq p^{\frac{7}{32}}+p^{\frac{-7}{32}}+1.

Then, upon expanding the factors and making use of (
2.4
), we see that for
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\bigg[\bigg(1-\frac{A_{\phi_{1}}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{A_{\phi_{1}}(p)}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{A_{\phi_{2}}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{A_{\phi_{2}}(p)}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\bigg)\bigg]\cdot\bigg[1+\frac{B(p)}{p^{s}}\bigg]=1+O(p^{-2\sigma+\frac{7}{16}}),

where
\sigma:=\Re(s)
.
We therefore conclude that for
\sigma>\frac{23}{32}+\epsilon
,
G(s)
converges absolutely and we have

|G(s)|\ll\tau(N_{1})\tau(N_{2})\cdot\prod_{p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}}(1+O(p^{-1-2\epsilon}))\ll_{\epsilon}(N_{1}N_{2})^{\epsilon},

where the implied constant depends only on
\epsilon>0
and
\tau(\cdot)
is the divisor function.

Now, as for
\sigma>1
we have

L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1}))L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2}))G(s)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{B(n)}{n^{s}}

by (
2.1
), for
c>1
we could relate
S(x)
to a line integral as

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1}))L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2}))G(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s^{2}}ds.

As
L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1})),L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2}))
are continued and
G(s)
is absolutely convergent for
\sigma>\frac{23}{32}+\epsilon
, we may shift the integral line left to any vertical line with real part
\sigma
satisfying
\frac{23}{32}+\epsilon<\sigma<1
and conclude

S(x)\ll_{\epsilon}x^{\sigma+\epsilon}\cdot(Q_{\phi_{1}}Q_{\phi_{2}})^{\frac{1-\sigma}{2}+\epsilon}

in the view of the convexity bounds for
\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1})
and
\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2})
as in (
2.2
).

∎

2.3.
Lower bound for
S(x)

Following
[
10
, Lemma A.2, Lemma A.3]
, we define a multiplicative function
h(\cdot)
supported on square-free positive integers, by setting
h(1)=1
and

h(p)=\begin{cases}2,&p\leq y,\\
-2,&p>y\end{cases}

and then multiplicatively extend to all square-free numbers. We set
h(n)=0
if
n
is not square-free.

In the following lemma, we transform the problem of bounding
S(x)
from below to that of bounding a summation of
h(n)
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
z>1
.
If for all
t\leq z
and positive integers
r\leq z
, we have
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq t\\
(n,rN_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)\geq 0
, then
S(z)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq z\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)\log(z/n)
.

Proof.

We firstly work with
S_{0}(z)
, which is defined as

S_{0}(z):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq z\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}B(n).

In view of the Mobius inversion, we define a new multiplicative function
g(\cdot)
by

B(n)=\sum_{d|n}h(d)g(n/d).

In particular
g(1)=1
and
g(p)=B(p)-h(p)\geq 0
for
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
.

Thus for any
1<t\leq z

\displaystyle S_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq t\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{d|n}h(d)g(n/d)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\leq t\\
(r,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}g(r)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq t/r\\
(d,rN_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(d)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq t\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n),

where in the last step by the positivity of
g(\cdot)
over square-free numbers and the positivity assumption on
h(\cdot)
we dropped all the terms in the double summation except for that corresponding to
r=1
.

Finally, as
S(z)=\int^{z}_{1}S_{0}(t)\frac{dt}{t}
and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq z\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)\log(z/n)=\int^{z}_{1}\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq t\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)\big)\frac{dt}{t}

by partial summation, we conclude

S(z)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq z\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)\log(z/n).

∎

The following lemma guarantees the required positivity in the assumption of Lemma
2
by providing an asymptotic formula for mean value of
h(\cdot)
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
U\geq 1
and
y,h(\cdot)
be as before, and let
r\leq y^{U}
be a positive integer, we then have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y^{u}\\
(n,rN_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)=c(rN_{1}N_{2})\cdot\big[\sigma_{2}(u)+o_{U}(1)\big](\log y)y^{u}

uniformly for
u\in[U^{-1},U]
, where
\lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}o_{U}(1)=0
and

c(a)=\frac{\varphi(a)^{2}}{a^{2}}\prod_{p\nmid a}(1-1/p)^{2}(1+2/p)\gg(\log\log a)^{-2}.

The continuous function
\sigma_{2}(u)
is the solution to the differential-difference equation

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(u)=u,\,\,0<u\leq 1,

\displaystyle\big(u^{-1}\sigma_{2}(u)\big)^{\prime}=-\frac{4}{u^{2}}\sigma_{2}(u-1),\,\,u>1.

Proof.

This is
[
11
, Lemma 6]
, in whose notation we take:
K=1,x_{0}=0,x_{1}=1,\chi_{0}=2,\chi_{1}=-2
.

We also remark here that by
[
11
, Remark 7]
and
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
, the original co-primary condition
(n,r)=1
could be modified to
(n,rN_{1}N_{2})=1
for
r\leq y^{U}
, as doing so only creates an extra factor which could be bounded by
(N_{1}N_{2})^{\epsilon}
for arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
in the right hand side of the weaker assumption prescribed in
[
11
, Remark 7]
, which is admissible for our purpose.
∎

Lastly, recalling
x=y^{U}
, we link
\sum_{n\leq x}h(n)
and
\sum_{n\leq x}h(n)\log(x/n)
together via partial summation. The lemma below is essentially
[
10
, Lemma A.5]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
U\geq 1
be such that
\sigma_{2}(u)>0
for
1<u\leq U
, then for

y\gg_{U}1
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y^{U}\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)\log(y^{U}/n)\gg_{U}y^{U}(\log y)^{2}.

Proof.

Defining
H(t):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq t\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)
, by partial summation we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y^{U}\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)\log(y^{U}/n)

\displaystyle=\int^{y^{U}}_{1}H(t)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\int^{U}_{0}H(y^{u})\log(y)du\geq\int^{U}_{U^{-1}}H(y^{u})\log(y)du,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that for
p\leq y
and
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
,
h(p)=2

and
U\geq 1
.
On the other hand, uniformly for
U^{-1}\leq u\leq U
Lemma
3
gives

H(y^{u})=c(N_{1}N_{2})(\sigma_{2}(u)+o_{U}(1))\cdot(\log y)y^{u}.

Thus for
y\gg_{U}1
large, we conclude

\int^{U}_{U^{-1}}H(y^{u})\log(y)du\gg_{U}c(N_{1}N_{2})y^{U}(\log(y))^{2}

∎

Notice that the above implies that if
U
is taken such that the assumptions in Lemma
4
are satisfied (notice that by Lemma
3
the assumptions in Lemma
2
are also satisfied with
z=x=y^{U}
), then for
y
larger than a constant (absolute once
U
is fixed), we may invoke Lemma
2
to conclude

S(x)=S(y^{U})\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y^{U}\\
(n,N_{1}N_{2})=1\end{subarray}}h(n)\log(y^{U}/n)\gg y^{U}(\log y)^{2}\gg x(\log x)^{2}.

This provides the desired lower bound for
S(x)
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
3
and
4

Throughout this section, we assume that all the involved primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms are not of dihedral, tetrahedral or octahedral type, as otherwise the Ramanujan conjecture is already satisfied at every unramified primes.

3.1.
Preliminaries

We introduce the following notation for the twisted symmetric fourth power of a primitive Hecke-Maass cusp form
\phi
of level
N
with central character
\chi_{\phi}
:

A^{4}(\phi):=\text{Sym}^{4}(\phi)\otimes\overline{\chi_{\phi}}^{2}.

We also denote the usual symmetric
m
-th power of
\phi
by
\text{Sym}^{m}(\phi)
. In particular, under our assumption that
\phi
is not of dihedral, tetrahedral or octahedral type,
\text{Sym}^{3}(\phi)
and
A^{4}(\phi)
correspond to cuspidal automorphic representations on
GL_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
and
GL_{5}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
respectively (see
[
7
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
). Denoting the local parameters for
\phi
at
p\nmid N
to be
\{\alpha_{p},\beta_{p}\}
, then the local parameters for
\operatorname{Ad}(\phi)
,
\text{Sym}^{3}(\phi)
and
A^{4}(\phi)
at
p
are respectively given by

\{\alpha_{p}/\beta_{p},1,\beta_{p}/\alpha_{p}\}\,,\{\alpha_{p}^{3},\alpha_{p}^{2}\beta_{p},\alpha_{p}\beta_{p}^{2},\beta_{p}^{3}\},

and

\{\alpha_{p}^{2}/\beta_{p}^{2},\alpha_{p}/\beta_{p},1,\beta_{p}/\alpha_{p},\beta_{p}^{2}/\alpha_{p}^{2}\}.

Just as
\operatorname{Ad}(\phi)
, we may define the
L
-functions for
\text{Sym}^{3}(\phi)
and
A^{4}(\phi)
by Euler products, which could be further expanded as Dirichlet series:

\displaystyle L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\phi))

\displaystyle:=\prod_{p\nmid N}\big[(1-\alpha_{p}^{3}p^{-s})(1-\alpha_{p}^{2}\beta_{p}p^{-s})(1-\alpha_{p}\beta_{p}^{2}p^{-s})(1-\beta_{p}^{3}p^{-s})\big]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
(n,N)=1\end{subarray}}A^{[3]}_{\phi}(n)n^{-s}

and

\displaystyle L(s,A^{4}(\phi))

\displaystyle:=\prod_{p\nmid N}\big[(1-\alpha_{p}^{2}\beta_{p}^{-2}p^{-s})(1-\alpha_{p}\beta_{p}^{-1}\beta_{p}p^{-s})(1-p^{-s})(1-\beta_{p}\alpha_{p}^{-1}p^{-s})(1-\beta_{p}^{2}\alpha_{p}^{-2}p^{-s})\big]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
(n,N)=1\end{subarray}}A^{[4]}_{\phi}(n)n^{-s},

for
\Re(s)>1
.

To prove Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
, we firstly establish some necessary lemmas.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
be primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms of level
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
. Assuming
\{p:p\nmid N_{2},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\}\neq\{p:p\nmid N_{1},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{2})\}
, we have
\phi_{1}\neq\phi_{2}
,
\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1})\neq\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2})
, and
A^{4}(\phi_{1})\neq A^{4}(\phi_{2})
.

Proof.

The assertion that
\phi_{1}\neq\phi_{2}
is trivial. Let
\alpha_{p}^{(i)},\beta_{p}^{(i)}
be the local parameters of
\phi_{i}
at
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
,
i=1,2
. If
\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1})=\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2})
were to hold, then for every prime
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
we have

\left\{\frac{\alpha_{p}^{(1)}}{\beta_{p}^{(1)}},1,\frac{\beta_{p}^{(1)}}{\alpha_{p}^{(1)}}\right\}=\left\{\frac{\alpha_{p}^{(2)}}{\beta_{p}^{(2)}},1,\frac{\beta_{p}^{(2)}}{\alpha_{p}^{(2)}}\right\}.

However, by
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
we know
p\notin\text{RP}(\phi_{i})\Leftrightarrow\frac{\alpha_{p}^{(1)}}{\beta_{p}^{(1)}}\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
. It follows that
\text{RP}(\phi_{1})=\text{RP}(\phi_{2})
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1})\neq\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2})
. By the same argument we also conclude
A^{4}(\phi_{1})\neq A^{4}(\phi_{2})
.
∎

Note that if
\text{Sym}^{2n}(\phi_{1})
and
\text{Sym}^{2n}(\phi_{2})
were known to exist as cuspidal automorphic representations on
\text{GL}_{2n+1}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, then we also have
\text{Sym}^{2n}(\phi_{1})\otimes\overline{\chi_{\phi_{1}}}^{n}\neq\text{Sym}^{2n}(\phi_{2})\otimes\overline{\chi_{\phi_{2}}}^{n}
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\phi
be a primitive Hecke-Maass cusp form of level
N
. Then as
X\to\infty
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid N\end{subarray}}A_{\phi}(p)=o(\pi(X)),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid N\end{subarray}}A^{[4]}_{\phi}(p)=o(\pi(X)),

\displaystyle\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid N\end{subarray}}|A^{[3]}_{\phi}(p)|^{2}}{\pi(X)}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid N\end{subarray}}|A^{[4]}_{\phi}(p)|^{2}}{\pi(X)}\leq 1.

Proof.

The first two relations follow from the fact that
\operatorname{Ad}(\phi)
and
A^{4}(\phi)
cuspidal automorphic representations on
\text{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\text{GL}_{5}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
respectively, and the Prime Number Theorem for automorphic
L
-functions
[
4
, Theorem 5.13]
. The last two relations are
[
10
, Remark 3.3]
.
∎

The key new input of our work is the following lemma:

Lemma 7
.

Let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be primitive Hecke-Maass cusp forms of levels
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
. Assuming
\{p:p\nmid N_{2},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\}\neq\{p:p\nmid N_{1},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{2})\}
, then as
X\to\infty
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\end{subarray}}A_{\phi_{1}}(p)A_{\phi_{2}}(p)=o(\pi(X)),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\end{subarray}}A_{\phi_{1}}(p)A^{[4]}_{\phi_{2}}(p)=o(\pi(X)).

Proof.

Let
\chi
be an Hecke character on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
. Since we have assumed
\{p:p\nmid N_{2},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\}\neq\{p:p\nmid N_{1},p\in\text{RP}(\phi_{2})\}
, by Lemma
5
we have
\phi_{1}\neq\phi_{2}
and
A^{4}(\phi_{1})\neq A^{4}(\phi_{2})
. Under these conditions, by the recent work of Thorner and Zhao
[
14
, Theorem 1.3 (1a)(3)]
, the Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions
L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1})\times\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2})\otimes\chi)
and
L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1})\times A^{4}(\phi_{2})\otimes\chi)
have no Landau-Siegel zeros relative to an absolute constant
c_{1}>0
. Explicitly, these
L
-functions are non-vanishing in the following real interval:

\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{\log(Q_{\phi_{1}}Q_{\phi_{2}}Q_{\chi})},1\right).

Here
Q_{\chi}
is the analytic conductor of
\chi
. In particular, taking
\chi=|\cdot|^{it},t\in\mathbb{R}
to be the Archimedean characters, we conclude that the Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions
L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1})\times\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{2}))
and
L(s,\operatorname{Ad}(\phi_{1})\times A^{4}(\phi_{2}))
are non-vanishing in the following standard zero-free region:

\left\{s=\sigma+it:\sigma\geq 1-\frac{c_{2}}{\log(Q_{\phi_{1}}Q_{\phi_{2}}(|t|+3))}\right\},

where
c_{2}>0
is another absolute constant. The lemma now follows from the Prime Number Theorem for automorphic
L
-functions
[
4
, Theorem 5.13]
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
3

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
3
:

Proof.

For a prime
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
we define

U(p):=(1+3A_{\phi_{1}}(p)+3A_{\phi_{2}}(p)+5A^{[4]}_{\phi_{1}}(p))^{2},

and consider the partial sum
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\end{subarray}}U(p)
.

On one hand, by
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have

p\notin\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cup\text{RP}(\phi_{2}),p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\Rightarrow A_{\phi_{1}}(p),A_{\phi_{2}}(p)>3,\hskip 8.53581ptA^{[4]}_{\phi_{1}}(p)>5.

Thus we have

(3.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\end{subarray}}U(p)\geq 44^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\notin\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cup\text{RP}(\phi_{2})\\
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\end{subarray}}1.

On the other hand, by the Hecke relations we have for
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
:

\displaystyle A_{\phi_{i}}(p)^{2}

\displaystyle=A^{[4]}_{\phi_{i}}(p)+A_{\phi_{i}}(p)+1,\hskip 8.53581pti=1,2,

\displaystyle A_{\phi_{1}}(p)A^{[4]}_{\phi_{1}}(p)

\displaystyle=|A_{\phi_{1}}^{[3]}(p)|^{3}-1.

Therefore we can write

\displaystyle U(p)

\displaystyle=-11+15A_{\phi_{1}}(p)+15A_{\phi_{2}}(p)+19A^{[4]}_{\phi_{1}}(p)+9A^{[4]}_{\phi_{2}}(p)

\displaystyle+18A_{\phi_{1}}(p)A_{\phi_{2}}(p)+30A^{[4]}_{\phi_{1}}(p)A_{\phi_{2}}(p)

\displaystyle+30|A^{[3]}_{\phi_{1}}(p)|^{2}+25|A^{[4]}_{\phi_{1}}(p)|^{2}.

Thus by Lemma
6
and Lemma
7
we have

(3.2)

\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\end{subarray}}U(p)}{\pi(X)}\leq 44.

Finally, combining (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) we have

\overline{d}((\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cup\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))^{c})\leq\frac{1}{44},

which is equivalent to

\underline{d}(\text{RP}(\phi_{1})\cup\text{RP}(\phi_{2}))\geq\frac{43}{44}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.
∎
