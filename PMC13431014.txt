Chloroplast genome sequence characteristics and phylogenetic analysis of Amorphophallus josefbogneri Hett 2006
Authors: Haoliang Shi, Wei Wang, Kai Chen, Ying Qi, Penghua Gao, Min Yang, Feiyan Huang, Jianrong Zhao, Lifang Li, Lei Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe complete chloroplast genome of Amorphophallus josefbogneri Hett 2006 was sequenced, assembled, and annotated. The genome is 172,957 bp in length and exhibits a typical circular quadripartite structure with LSC 94,009 bp, SSC 14,946 bp, and two IRs of 32,001 bp each. The overall GC content is 34.96%. A total of 128 genes were annotated, including 83 protein-coding genes, 8 rRNA genes, and 37 tRNA genes, of which 110 are single-copy genes. Phylogenetic analysis places A. josefbogneri in a clade A. muelleri, A. kiusianus, A. yunnanensis, A. coaetaneus, and A. tonkinensis. This chloroplast genome provides a resource for developing molecular markers for species identification and phylogenetic studies within Amorphophallus.

Introduction

Amorphophallus is a genus within the Araceae family that holds significant economic and ecological importance (Huang et al. 2022; Li et al. 2024a). The tubers of some species serve as traditional food sources and are key producers of glucomannan, while many species with distinctive morphologies and large, striking inflorescences are highly valued in the ornamental plant market and for biodiversity conservation (Gao et al. 2022; Islam et al. 2023).

Amorphophallus josefbogneri Hett 2006, a tuberous geophyte, is native to southwestern Thailand and grows primarily in the wet tropical biome. The plant features a short, smooth petiole with a uniform dull green to greyish-green coloration. Its leaf blade consists of few leaflets borne on short, fully winged rachises. Individual leaflets are elliptic to slightly elongate obovate, with a slightly leathery texture and an acute apex. The upper leaf surface is a dark, rich green with a narrow purple margin, while the lower surface is greyish-green (Hetterscheid 2006) (Figure 1). Despite this morphological characterization, the phylogenetic placement of A. josefbogneri within Amorphophallus remains unresolved, and no genomic resources, including chloroplast genome sequences, have been reported for this species to date.

This uncertainty stems from broader taxonomic challenges within the genus., taxonomic and phylogenetic studies of this genus have long faced challenges. Due to the high plasticity of vegetative organ morphology, coupled with the short flowering period and the difficulty of preserving complete inflorescence characteristics in herbarium specimens, relying solely on traditional morphological traits makes it difficult to clarify species boundaries and infrageneric relationships. This has led to persistent controversies in the classification system and left the evolutionary positions of some groups ambiguous (van der Ham et al. 1998; Grob et al. 2004).

The chloroplast genome has emerged as a powerful tool for resolving these taxonomic uncertainties within Amorphophallus, as it moderate mutation rate and conserved structure provide informative sites well-suited for phylogenetic inference at the species level (Provan et al. 2001; Shaw et al. 2007). However, complete chloroplast genome data for this highly species-rich genus remain extremely scarce. As of January 1, 2026, only 13 complete chloroplast genomes of Amorphophallus have been deposited in GenBank (Li et al. 2024a). This scarcity substantially limits our ability to understand the phylogenetic history, species diversification patterns, and evolutionary dynamics of key traits in this genus from a genomic perspective. Recent chloroplast genome analyses have delineated three major phylogenetic groups within the genus, namely the East Asian continental CA-I clade, the Southeast Asian SEA clade, and the CA-II clade (Li et al. 2024a).

This study focuses on A. josefbogneri as the research subject. The primary objectives are to assemble its complete chloroplast genome using high-throughput sequencing technology, to perform systematic structural and functional annotation of the genome, and to construct a high-resolution phylogenetic tree by integrating the newly generated data with publicly available chloroplast genomes from other Amorphophallus species and representative Araceae taxa. The expected outcomes will provide preliminary molecular evidence for clarifying the phylogenetic relationships and taxonomic positions of A. josefbogneri within the genus Amorphophallus, thereby contributing a useful genomic reference for future comparative and phylogenetic studies within this group.

Materials and methods

A. josefbogneri was cultivated at Kunming University. Fresh leaves were collected from the university’s konjac germplasm nursery in Yunnan Province, China (coordinates: 24.97406°N, 102.79605°E), and a voucher specimen (No. YBMY20251201; collector: Lei Yu; email: HaoliangShi@kmu.edu.cn) was deposited in the Herbarium of the Key Laboratory of Araceae Biology, Yunnan Province. Total genomic DNA was extracted from the fresh leaf tissue using the DNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The purity and concentration of the DNA were measured using a NanoDrop™ 1000 spectrophotometer and a Qubit™ fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). A SMRTbell library with an insert size of approximately 15 kb was prepared using the SMRTbell Express Template Preparation Kit 2.0 (PacBio, Menlo Park, CA, USA). The library construction workflow included DNA shearing, purification with AMPure PB beads, removal of single-stranded overhangs, damage and end repair, ligation of hairpin adapters, and final library purification. Following quality control, the library was sequenced on a PacBio Revio system.

Raw subreads were processed into high-fidelity (HiFi) reads using the Circular Consensus Sequencing (CCS) algorithm (v6.0.0) with the following parameters: –minPasses 3, –minPredictedAccuracy 0.99, and –maxLength 21000. For chloroplast genome assembly, the PacBio HiFi data (>3 Gb, with an average read length >13 kb) were first aligned to a reference chloroplast genome using minimap2 (v2.15-r905) (Li 2018). Reads aligned to the reference with a coverage depth below 50× were discarded to ensure sufficient depth for robust assembly and to minimize potential contamination from low-coverage nuclear plastid DNA insertions (NUPTs) or sequencing artifacts (Zhou et al. 2023). The filtered reads were then assembled with Flye (v2.6) in a reference-guided mode, with the A. titanum (NC_056329.1) chloroplast genome provided as the reference (Kolmogorov et al. 2020). This graph was visualized and manually edited using Bandage (v0.8.1) to resolve redundancies and circularize the sequence. Assembly completeness and circularity were confirmed by the presence of a single contiguous path with uniform coverage depth across the entire structure, yielding the complete chloroplast genome (Wick et al. 2015). The chloroplast genome assembly was annotated using the Plastid Genome Annotator (PGA) pipeline, followed by manual curation to correct the annotations (Qu et al. 2019). Finally, a circular graphical map of the chloroplast genome was generated using OGDRAW (Greiner et al. 2019).

To investigate the phylogenetic position of A. josefbogneri within Amorphophallus, we reconstructed a maximum likelihood phylogeny using complete chloroplast genomes. Our dataset comprised 14 taxa, including A. josefbogneri, 11 congeneric species, two additional species from Araceae (Spirodela polyrhiza and Colocasia esculenta), which served as the outgroup. Sequences were aligned with MAFFT and refined using TrimAl in TBtools-II (Chen et al. 2023). Phylogenetic inference was performed with IQ-TREE (v.1.6.10), employing the best-fit model (TVM+F + R2) as selected by the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), with branch support measured from 1000 bootstrap replicates (Nguyen et al. 2015). The final trimmed alignment comprised 14 sequences and was 156,378 bp in length, all of which were used for phylogenetic inference.

Results

PacBio HiFi high-fidelity long-read sequencing generated 510,197 high-quality reads, producing 7.95 Gb of data. The assembled chloroplast genome of A. josefbogneri is 172,957 bp in length, consisting of a large single-copy (LSC) region of 94,009 bp, a small single-copy (SSC) region of 14,946 bp, and two inverted repeat (IR) regions of 32,001 bp each. The overall GC content is 34.96%. The genome harbors 128 genes, of which 110 are unique. These include 83 protein-coding genes (77 unique), 37 tRNAs (29 unique), and 8 rRNAs (4 unique) (Supplementary Table S1, Figure 2). The average coverage depth of sequencing reads across the genome was 940.10× (Supplementary Figure S1). Functional annotation identified 45 photosynthesis-related genes (including photosystem I/II subunits, NADH dehydrogenase, cytochrome b/f complex, ATP synthase, and Rubisco large subunit) and 72 self-replication genes, comprising 45 RNA genes (37 tRNAs and 8 rRNAs) and 27 protein-coding genes. 11 genes contained cis-splicing introns, including rps16, atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, clpP, petB, petD, rpl16, and ndhB (Supplementary Table S1 and Figure S2). While the rps12 gene exhibits trans-splicing (Supplementary Figure S3). These 14 sequences, including that of A. josefbogneri, were used to reconstruct a maximum likelihood tree (Figure 3). S. polyrhiza and C. esculenta were designated as the outgroup and accordingly occupied the basal position in the tree, Following the phylogenetic framework established in previous studies (Cyrille et al. 2017; Li et al. 2024a), three major groups were recognized within the genus: the East Asian continental CA-I clade, the Southeast Asian SEA clade, and the CA-II clade. Specifically, A. titanum and A. paeoniifolius were placed in the SEA clade (bootstrap = 68%), whereas A. albus, A. konjac, A. kachinensis, and A. krausei were resolved in the CA-II clade (bootstrap = 60%). The newly sequenced A. josefbogneri clustered with A. muelleri, A. kiusianus, A. yunnanensis, A. coaetaneus, and A. tonkinensis within the CA-I clade (bootstrap = 100%), indicating their close phylogenetic affinity.

Discussion and conclusion

The plastome of A. josefbogneri (172,957 bp, 34.96% GC, 128 genes) fits well within the range previously reported for 11 other Amorphophallus species (164,417–177,076 bp, 34.5%–36.0% GC, 126–131 genes; Supplementary Table S2). A direct comparison with previously published Amorphophallus plastomes reveals that its genome size is larger than those of A. konjac (161,647 bp; Liu et al. 2019) and A. yunnanensis (164,417 bp; Yin and Gao 2023a), yet slightly smaller than that of A. albus (175,728 bp; Li et al. 2024a). Its GC content (34.96%) is nearly identical to that of A. coaetaneus (34.90%; Gao et al. 2023) and marginally lower than that of A. kiusianus (36.00%; Gao and Yin 2024b). Similarly, the gene count (128) matches exactly those reported for A. kiusianus and A. tonkinensis (Gao and Yin 2024b; Yin et al. 2024), while it differs slightly from the 126 genes found in A. yunnanensis and the 131 in A. coaetaneus (Gao et al. 2023; Yin and Gao 2023a) (Supplementary Table S2). Together, these consistencies in size, gene number, and base composition strongly support the deep evolutionary conservation of plastome architecture within the genus, as noted in previous comparative surveys (Li et al. 2024a). While the overall plastome architecture is highly conserved across the genus, the presence or absence of specific protein-coding genes may reflect differential selective pressures or adaptive significance. The A. josefbogneri chloroplast genome retains both rpl33 and rps15, consistent with the gene complement observed in most angiosperm plastomes (Rogalski et al. 2008).

The clustering pattern observed in the phylogenetic tree, where A. josefbogneri (originating from southwestern Thailand) groups with the East Asian continental CA-I clade (including A. muelleri, A. kiusianus, A. yunnanensis, A. coaetaneus, and A. tonkinensis), indicates a close phylogenetic relationship between this Thai endemic species and taxa distributed in East Asia (Li et al. 2024a; Gao and Yin 2024b). The phylogenetic groupings recovered in this study, including the CA-I, SEA, and CA-II clades, are consistent with the classification framework established in previous studies based on both complete chloroplast genomes and traditional plastid markers (Li et al. 2024a; Cyrille et al. 2017). Over evolutionary time, the phylogenetic clustering of taxa from geographically proximate regions suggests a shared common ancestry, which is reflected in their close genetic affinities observed in the plastome-based phylogeny (Abd Rahim 2017; Gao et al. 2018; Pouchon et al. 2023).
