Multi-omics reveals the pituitary-mediated neuroendocrine mechanisms during the transition from the onset-of-birth to the laying period in Wanxi white geese
Authors: X.J. Li, X. Yuan, S. Chen, M.M. Hou, M. Ren, S.H. Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The dynamic remodeling of the pituitary endocrine state is a critical pivot driving the transition of the reproductive cycle in geese. In this study, pituitary tissues from Wanxi white geese at the onset-of-birth and the laying period were investigated. Using serum hormone assays, transcriptomics, and dual-luciferase reporter assays, the mRNA and miRNA expression profiles of the pituitary during these periods were constructed. The results revealed that serum levels of FSH, LH, E2, and P4 were significantly elevated during the laying compared to the onset-of-birth period. In contrast, the PRL level was significantly decreased. Integrated omics analysis identified 453 differentially expressed genes and 73 differentially expressed miRNAs. Functional enrichment analysis demonstrated that during the laying period, the pituitary mediates the robust synthesis and release of gonadotropins by activating ECM-receptor interaction, calcium and MAPK signaling pathways. Furthermore, it regulates the pituitary tissue microenvironment and upstream signal transduction through a post-transcriptional network centered on miR-32-x and miR-338-y. This network targeted key genes, including NTRK2, CAMK4, COL1A2 and ADCY2. Furthermore, dual-luciferase reporter assays supported a sequence-specific interaction between miR-338-y and the ADCY2 3′UTR. In conclusion, our findings suggest that the synergistic effect of endocrine pathways and the miRNA post-transcriptional network provides a potential multi-dimensional regulatory framework for the transduction of pituitary reproductive signals, which may drive the transition of Wanxi White geese from the onset of birth to the laying period. This study provides preliminary insights into the molecular regulatory network underlying pituitary gonadotropin secretion in poultry and identifies potential candidate targets for future breeding strategies evaluating egg-laying performance in geese.

Introduction

Egg-laying performance significantly impacts the economic profitability of the goose industry. The hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis primarily initiates avian reproduction and maintains the laying cycle (He et al., 2024). Stimulation of the pituitary gland, a crucial signaling hub within the neuroendocrine network, by the gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) secreted from the hypothalamus, synthesizes and releases robust amounts of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH). This process ultimately drives the development, maturation, and ovulation of ovarian follicles (Chang et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025). During the transition from the onset of birth and the laying period, the endocrine network of the pituitary undergoes molecular reprogramming to adapt to and maintain continuous ovulation and egg production (Ouyang et al., 2021). Therefore, the dynamic remodeling of the pituitary endocrine is a key driving force for the transition of the avian reproductive cycle and a critical determinant of egg-laying performance (Ma et al., 2025). Elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying the dynamic remodeling of the pituitary endocrine system and tissue microenvironment between the onset of birth to the laying period is significant for improving the egg-laying performance of geese.

The feedback regulation of reproductive hormones govern the periodic transition of the pituitary's endocrine state, which also relies on intracellular transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulatory networks (Chen et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2022). The recent and rapid development of multi-omics sequencing technologies and post-transcriptional regulation, particularly by microRNAs (miRNAs), has played a crucial role in the field of avian reproductive endocrinology. miRNAs are a class of endogenous, non-coding, single-stranded RNAs of approximately 20-24 nucleotides in length. They predominantly bind with high specificity to the 3′ untranslated region (3′UTR) of target mRNAs, causing the targeted post-transcriptional degradation or translational repression of downstream mRNAs (Nguyen et al., 2025). Evidence indicates that miRNAs are broadly involved in tissue microenvironment remodeling, cell proliferation and apoptosis, and hormone synthesis in poultry (Pozovnikova et al., 2024; Ran et al., 2023). For instance, in vitro transcriptomes of chicken ovarian granulosa cells revealed that miR-223 affects steroid hormone secretion by targeting and repressing the CRIM1 gene and interfering with the AKT signaling pathway (Xiang et al., 2024). Similarly, integrated mRNA and miRNA profiles of rooster epididymal tissues confirmed that miRNA-mediated post-transcriptional regulatory networks play a vital role in sperm development and motility (Xing et al., 2022). However, the molecular mechanisms by which miRNA-dominated post-transcriptional networks regulate the pituitary endocrine system during the transition from the onset-of-birth to the laying period in geese remain largely unclear.

Thus, this study investigated the pituitary tissues from Wanxi white geese (WWG) at the onset-of-birth and the laying period. The study generated the transcriptome profiles of miRNA and mRNA of the pituitary gland during these two periods, and constructed an miRNA-mRNA interaction network associated with reproductive signal transduction. Further, this study aimed to characterize stage-associated genes and potential molecular regulatory networks related to pituitary gonadotropin secretion across different reproductive stages. The findings provide a foundation for future functional validation of candidate genes and regulatory relationships associated with reproductive and egg-laying traits in WWG.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

Six healthy WWG with similar body weights were randomly selected from both periods (onset-of-birth (3-year-old) and laying period (3-year-old)), and were fasted for 12 h before blood sampling. Blood samples were collected via the wing vein and allowed to stand at 4°C for 24 h before centrifuging at 3,000 × g for 10 min. The serum supernatant was carefully collected into 2 mL centrifuge tubes and stored at −80°C for subsequent determination of reproductive hormone levels. After blood collection, three geese from each experimental group were randomly selected and euthanized. Following euthanasia, the geese were dissected to examine their ovarian morphology. The specific criteria for ovarian assessment were defined as follows: in the onset-of-birth period, the ovaries were small and lacked identifiable large yellow preovulatory follicles or a distinct hierarchical follicle sequence. During the laying period, the ovaries contained a clearly identifiable hierarchy of large yellow preovulatory follicles, including F1–F5 follicles (a detailed comparison of ovarian morphology is provided in Figure S1). The skulls of the euthanized geese were rapidly opened to completely excise the intact pituitary glands. The extracted pituitary glands were washed with physiological saline, placed into cryotubes, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and finally transferred to a −80°C freezer for subsequent mRNA and miRNA transcriptome sequencing. Pituitary samples from the pre-laying and laying groups were designated KC and CC, respectively, with individual samples labeled KC1–KC3 and CC1–CC3.

Determination of reproductive hormone levels

Serum concentrations of FSH, LH, estradiol (E2), progesterone (P4), and prolactin (PRL) were measured using commercial Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) following the manufacturer's instructions.

RNA library construction and sequencing

The mRNA sequencing libraries were constructed from purified total RNA through sequential mRNA isolation, fragmentation, first- and second-strand cDNA synthesis, end repair, 3′ A-tailing, adapter ligation, and PCR enrichment. Library concentrations were quantified using a Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and fragment size distributions were assessed using an Agilent 4200 TapeStation (Agilent Technologies). After library quality assessment, the validated libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform using a paired-end 150-bp strategy.

For small-RNA sequencing, libraries were constructed from total RNA using the QIAseq miRNA Library Kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 3′ and 5′ adapters were sequentially ligated to small-RNA molecules, followed by reverse transcription, PCR amplification, and size selection of the target library fragments. Library concentrations were quantified using a Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer, and fragment-size distributions were assessed using an Agilent 4200 TapeStation. The qualified libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform using a single-end 50-bp strategy.

Transcriptome data analysis

The raw data were initially subjected to quality control using the fastp (v0.23.4), yielding high-quality clean reads (Chen et al., 2018). The clean reads were mapped to the reference genome (NCBI_GCF_002166845.1) using HISAT2 (v2.0.4) (Kim et al., 2019). Transcript assembly was then performed using StringTie (v1.3.5) (Pertea et al., 2015). Transcript expression levels were quantified using the Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads (FPKM) method, and the fold change (FC) between groups was calculated accordingly. Finally, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified based on the threshold |log₂FC| > 0.58 and FDR < 0.05.

Quality control and miRNA data analysis

Following small-RNA sequencing, raw reads were processed using fastp (v0.23.4) (Chen et al., 2018). Adapter sequences were trimmed, and reads corresponding to adapter dimers, reads lacking inserts, reads containing excessive ambiguous nucleotides, and low-quality reads were removed. After adapter trimming and quality filtering, reads of 18–32 nt were retained. Reads derived from rRNAs, tRNAs, snRNAs, snoRNAs, and repetitive elements were identified by comparison with Rfam (v14.0) (Kalvari et al., 2021). The remaining clean reads were aligned to the goose reference genome (NCBI_GCF_002166845.1) using Bowtie (v2.0) (Langmead et al., 2009). Genome-mapped reads were compared with mature and precursor miRNA sequences deposited in miRbase (v22) to identify known miRNAs (Kozomara et al., 2019). Unannotated reads were subsequently analyzed using miRDeep2 to predict candidate novel miRNAs based on their genomic positions and precursor hairpin structures (Friedländer et al., 2012). MiRNA expression levels were normalized and compared across samples utilizing the Transcripts Per Million (TPM) method (Wagner et al., 2012). Differential expression analysis was performed using the DESeq2 package (Love, et al., 2014), with differentially expressed miRNAs (DEMs) defined by the thresholds of |log₂FC| > 0.26 and FDR < 0.1. Finally, to elucidate the regulatory roles of these DEMs, their putative target genes were predicted using two independent algorithms: miRanda (v3.3a) and TargetScan (v7.0) (Agarwal et al., 2015; Enright et al., 2003). The intersection of the prediction results from both algorithms was defined as the final set of miRNA target genes.

GO and KEGG enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis of the DEGs and the target genes of DEMs were performed using the clusterProfiler (v3.10.1) (Yu et al., 2012) and KOBAS (v2.0) (Xie et al., 2011) software. A P-value < 0.05 was considered as the threshold for significant enrichment of GO terms and KEGG pathways, whereas a P-value < 0.01 indicated highly significant enrichment.

Integrated miRNA-mRNA analysis

An integrated analysis of the DEMs and their predicted target genes was conducted to elucidate the interaction relationships among DEGs and the regulatory mechanisms of miRNAs. The interacting miRNA-mRNA pairs were screened and identified based on the principle of significant negative correlation between the expression levels of miRNAs and their corresponding target mRNAs (Muniategui et al., 2013). Finally, the miRNA-mRNA interaction network was constructed and visualized using the Cytoscape software (v3.8.0) (Shannon et al., 2003).

Validation by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Six DEGs and four DEMs were randomly selected for validation of the sequencing data via reverse transcription-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR). Specific primers (Supplementary Table S1) were designed using Oligo 7 software and synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). The RT-qPCR was performed in a 20 μL reaction volume using a real-time PCR detection system. The thermocycling conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 95°C for 3 min, 45 cycles of denaturation at 95°C for 15 s, and annealing/extension at 60°C for 30 s. All samples were analyzed in triplicate. The relative expression levels were calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT method, with GAPDH and U6 serving as the internal reference gene.

Dual luciferase detection

Based on the predicted binding site between ADCY2 and miR-338-y, dual-luciferase reporter plasmids containing the wild-type ADCY2 3′UTR sequence (ADCY2-3′ UTR-WT) or mutant sequence (ADCY2-3′ UTR-MUT) were designed and synthesized using the psiCHECK2 vector (Promega, WI, USA). 293T cells (Procell, CA, USA) were seeded into 24-well plates and cultured for 24 h until they reached approximately 80% confluence. The cells were co-transfected with 1 μg of the reporter plasmid and 15 pmol of miR-338-y mimic or negative control (NC) using the Lip3000 reagent (Invitrogen, CA, USA). After 48 h of transfection, the cells were lysed and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4°C. Subsequently, 20 μl of the supernatant was mixed with the dual-luciferase reporter assay (Promega, WI, USA), and the activities of firefly luciferase and Renilla luciferase were measured at 560 nm and 465 nm, respectively.

Statistical analyses

The relative expression of DEGs and DEMs was calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT method, normalized to the internal reference genes GAPDH and U6, respectively. All experimental data are expressed as mean ±SD. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 27.0 software (IBM Corp., NY). Differences between groups were analyzed using an independent sample t-test or one-way ANOVA, with P < 0.05 indicating statistically significant differences. Visual statistical analysis and graph plotting were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9 (v9.4.0) and Cytoscape (v3.8.2).

Serum hormone levels revealed significantly elevated concentrations of FSH, E2, LH, and P4 in WWG during the laying period (P < 0.05) compared to the onset-of-birth. Quality control of sequencing data

Transcriptome sequencing of the six pituitary tissue samples generated 515,383,404 raw and 513,422,472 high-quality clean reads before and after stringent filtering and quality control procedures, respectively. The sequencing quality across all samples was exceptionally high, with the percentage of Q20 and Q30 bases exceeding 97.61% and 93.62%, respectively. Furthermore, the GC content of the samples were 48.55% to 51.56%, indicating a stable and balanced base composition without negligible sequencing bias. Subsequent alignment of these clean reads to the goose reference genome revealed that the mapping rate for all individual samples was >83.36% (Supplementary Table S2). Collectively, these results demonstrate the high reliability and quality of the sequencing data, fully satisfying the requirements for the subsequent identification of DEGs and downstream bioinformatics analyses.

Discussion

As the central hub of the HPG axis, the pituitary gland plays a crucial role in regulating avian follicular development, ovulation, and broodiness by synthesizing and secreting gonadotropins, such as FSH, LH, and PRL (Liu et al., 2018; Tong et al., 2025). The present study utilized the pituitary tissues of WWG at the onset-of-birth and laying periods to characterize potential molecular changes by which the pituitary gland regulates follicular development during the physiological transition between these two stages. The analysis involved integration of serum hormone profiles, transcriptomics, miRNA interaction networks, and dual-luciferase reporter assays. Hormones serve as critical phenotypic indicators reflecting the reproductive status of the organism. During the avian laying period, increased secretion of GnRH from the hypothalamus stimulates the pituitary gland to release substantial amounts of FSH, supporting continuous follicular recruitment and development. Concurrently, elevated LH levels are maintained to induce the ovulation of mature follicles and promote P4 production. The abundant secretion of E2 by granulosa cells serves as a pivotal endocrine signal that stimulates oviductal development, facilitates eggshell calcification, and ensures successful ovulation (Hanlon et al., 2022; Yan et al., 2022). Conversely, the relatively high levels of PRL observed at the onset-of-birth period may be attributed to its negative regulatory role within the reproductive cycle. Elevated PRL concentrations can prevent premature follicular development by suppressing the secretion of GnRH and gonadotropins (Li et al., 2026). Consistent with this mechanism, this study revealed that the serum concentrations of FSH, LH, E2, and P4 were significantly elevated, whereas the PRL level was markedly decreased during the laying period. Therefore, the pituitary endocrine network probably undergoes profound transcriptional remodeling during the transition from the onset-of-birth to the laying period to successfully initiate and sustain the egg-laying mode.

This study performed mRNA and miRNA transcriptome sequencing on the pituitary tissues of WWG to elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying the transcriptional remodeling of the pituitary endocrine network during the transition from the onset of birth period to the laying period in poultry. A total of 453 DEGs were identified and were significantly enriched in several KEGG pathways associated with neuroendocrine signal transduction, extracellular matrix remodeling and protein processing, included the calcium signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, focal adhesion and ECM-receptor interaction. The Calcium signaling pathway and MAPK signaling pathway were two important signal transduction pathways enriched in this study. In the HPG axis, pituitary gonadotropes must precisely receive and decode upstream GnRH pulse signals and subsequently activate intracellular signaling cascades to regulate gonadotropin synthesis and secretion (Li et al., 2024; Stamatiades and Kaiser, 2018). In this study, the MAPK signaling pathway was significantly enriched, consistent with its established role as a major downstream effector of GnRH signaling. Related studies have demonstrated that GnRH-induced gonadotropin synthesis and secretion (such as FSH and LH) primarily, are largely mediated by MAPK cascades, depends on the MAPK cascade, particular, the ERK, JNK, and p38 MAPK branches regulate the transcription of gonadotropin-associated genes, including LHB, FSHB, and the gene encoding the common glycoprotein hormone α-subunit (Perrett and McArdle, 2013). Notably, RASGRF1 and NTRK2, two MAPK pathway-associated genes involved in signal transduction and neuroendocrine regulation, were significantly upregulated during the laying period. RASGRF1 functions as an upstream activator of Ras/MAPK signaling and facilitates the transmission of extracellular signals to downstream MAPK cascades (Krapivinsky et al., 2003). NTRK2 encodes a neurotrophin receptor that transduces neurotrophin-mediated signals through several intracellular pathways, including the MAPK cascade (Przybył et al., 2021). Collectively, the upregulation of RASGRF1 and NTRK2 may indicate enhanced neuroendocrine signal responsiveness in the pituitary of WWG during the laying period, thereby contributing to pituitary functional remodeling and the maintenance of reproductive endocrine activity. Furthermore, the calcium signaling pathway predominantly drives the release of pituitary gonadotropins (such as FSH and LH). Hormone secretion by pituitary gonadotrophs is highly dependent on Ca²⁺ signaling, and GnRH stimulation induces intracellular Ca²⁺ mobilization and voltage-dependent Ca²⁺ influx, thereby contributing to the synthesis and exocytotic release of LH and FSH (Durán-Pastén and Fiordelisio, 2013). In this study, key genes associated with Ca²⁺ signal transduction and downstream signaling in the calcium signaling pathway, including CAMK4 and ADCY2, were significantly upregulated during the laying period. CAMK4, a member of the Ca²⁺/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase family, can transmit intracellular Ca²⁺ signals to downstream transcriptional regulatory processes (Redmond et al., 2002; Sun et al., 1994). ADCY2 encodes adenylyl cyclase 2, a membrane-associated enzyme that catalyzes cAMP production and may contribute to functional crosstalk between cAMP and Ca²⁺-dependent signaling pathways (Ding et al., 2004; Shen and Cooper, 2013). Therefore, the significant enrichment of the calcium signaling pathway may be associated with signaling processes involved in gonadotropin synthesis and secretion of LH and FSH. At the post-transcriptional level, this study identified 73 DEMs whose predicted target genes significantly enriched in several pathways, including focal adhesion, ECM-receptor interaction, protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum and N-glycan biosynthesis. Among these pathways, focal adhesion and ECM–receptor interaction are closely associated with the maintenance of pituitary tissue architecture and the local endocrine microenvironment (Kuchenbauer et al., 2001; Prasad, 2019). The extracellular matrix not only provides structural support for pituitary cells but also transmits extracellular cues through membrane receptors such as integrins to intracellular focal-adhesion and cytoskeletal systems, thereby potentially influencing pituitary cell adhesion, proliferation, differentiation, and secretory activity (Scully et al., 2016). Protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum and N-glycan biosynthesis are closely related to the biosynthesis and maturation of pituitary secretory proteins. The endoplasmic reticulum is a major site for the folding, modification, assembly, and quality control of secretory proteins, in which N-linked glycans contribute to chaperone-assisted protein folding and quality control (Hammond et al., 1994; Hebert et al., 1995). FSH and LH are pituitary glycoprotein hormones, and their glycosylation patterns can influence hormone assembly, secretion, receptor-mediated signaling, biological activity, and circulating half-life (Wang et al., 2016; Zariñán et al., 2020). Therefore, the significant enrichment of miRNA target genes in pathways related to cell–matrix interactions and secretory protein processing suggests that miRNA-mediated post-transcriptional regulation may contribute to the establishment and maintenance of reproductive endocrine function in WWG during the laying period by modulating the pituitary microenvironment and the processing and maturation of gonadotropins.

By constructing an miRNA-mRNA interaction network, this study identified negative regulatory correlations between 56 miRNAs and 137 mRNAs. These target genes were abundantly enriched in several KEGG pathways, including ECM-receptor interaction, the calcium signaling pathway, N-Glycan biosynthesis, and focal adhesion. Therefore, the massive release of gonadotropins by the pituitary gland during the laying period relies on intracellular hormone synthesis and also necessitates the dynamic remodeling of the extracellular microenvironment to establish an efficient vesicular transport network. Furthermore, the integrated multi-omics analysis (Sankey diagram) unveiled the multidimensional interactive relationships among miRNAs, target genes, key pathways, and hormone phenotypes. The hub node miR-32-x was predicted to target DEGs such as NTRK2, CAMK4, and COL1A2 which primarily enriched the ECM-receptor interaction, calcium and MAPK signaling pathway. These molecular changes occurred concurrently with increased serum LH and FSH concentrations. In this study, miR-32-x expression was negatively correlated with the expression levels of the candidate target genes NTRK2, CAMK4 and COL1A2. NTRK2 encodes the neurotrophin receptor TrkB, which activates the downstream Ras/MAPK signaling pathway upon ligand binding and may enhance the responsiveness of pituitary cells to neurotrophin- and MAPK-related signal (Minichiello et al., 1998).CAMK4 encodes calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase IV, which transmits intracellular Ca²⁺ signals to nuclear transcriptional programs by phosphorylating and activating transcription factors such as CREB, thereby triggering the exocytotic release of LH and FSH (Sun et al., 1994).COL1A2 encodes the α2 subunit of type I collagen, an important component of the extracellular matrix that can influence cell proliferation, differentiation, morphogenesis, and hormone secretion (Lukashev and Werb, 1998). Collectively, the miRNA-32-x-centered post-transcriptional regulatory network may be associated with coordinated changes in neurotrophin/MAPK signaling, Ca²⁺-dependent transcriptional responses, and extracellular matrix remodeling in pituitary cells and may participate in the functional adaptation of the pituitary associated with gonadotropin synthesis and secretion during the laying period.

In the present study, a dual-luciferase reporter assay confirmed the direct targeting of ADCY2 by miR-338-y, which represents one of the central candidate regulatory pairs identified in the integrated miRNA–mRNA network. This experimental evidence provides support for the reliability of the target-prediction and network-prioritization strategy, confirmed the direct targeting of ADCY2 by miR-338-y. Previous studies have shown that miR-338 expression changes during the pituitary response to GnRH stimulation, suggesting that it may participate in reproductive and neuroendocrine regulation (Ye et al., 2013). ADCY2 encodes adenylyl cyclase 2, which catalyzes the conversion of ATP into cyclic adenosine monophosphate (Gray et al., 2024). cAMP is a major intracellular second messenger that interacts with Ca²⁺-dependent signaling pathways and transmits extracellular neuroendocrine stimuli to downstream transcriptional and secretory responses (Halls and Cooper, 2011). In pituitary endocrine cells, the coordinated actions of cAMP and Ca²⁺ signaling may contribute to cellular responses to hypothalamic inputs and to the synthesis and release of pituitary hormones (Hezareh et al., 1997). Moreover, Ca²⁺ signaling is essential for stimulus–secretion and stimulus–transcription coupling in pituitary endocrine cells, particularly for the synthesis and exocytotic release of LH and FSH in gonadotrophs (Durán-Pastén and Fiordelisio, 2013). Therefore, the validated targeting of ADCY2 by miR-338-y provides preliminary evidence that miR-338-y may participate in post-transcriptional regulation of the pituitary during the transition from the onset-of-laying period to the laying period. The downregulation of miR-338-y accompanied by the upregulation of ADCY2 suggests that reduced miRNA-mediated repression may be associated with enhanced cAMP-related signal transduction in the pituitary. This regulatory relationship may contribute to the remodeling of neuroendocrine signaling associated with changes in gonadotropin secretion. Nevertheless, further studies are required to determine whether the interaction between miR-338-y and ADCY2 directly alters ADCY2 protein abundance, intracellular cAMP levels, Ca²⁺ signaling activity, or FSH and LH secretion.

Conclusion

By integrating multidimensional data—including serum hormone profiles, transcriptomes, miRNA expression profiles, and multi-omics joint networks—this study provides a preliminary molecular atlas detailing the transition of pituitary endocrine function from the onset of birth to the laying period in WWG. These findings indicate demonstrate that during the laying period, the pituitary gland not only activates key endocrine signaling pathways, such as the ECM-receptor interaction, calcium, and MAPK signaling pathways, to mediate the massive synthesis and exocytotic release of gonadotropins, but also relies on a post-transcriptional regulatory network centered on hub miRNAs, including miR-32-y and miR-338-y. By targeting specific genes such as NTRK2, CAMK4, COL1A2 and ADCY2, this miRNA network potentially modulates the pituitary tissue microenvironment and upstream signal transduction. The synergistic coordination of these mechanisms may ultimately drives the physiological transition of the geese from the onset-of-birth to the laying period. In conclusion, this study provides novel insights into the complex molecular regulatory networks governing gonadotropin secretion in the avian pituitary gland and identifies potential candidate targets for future breeding strategies aimed at enhancing the egg-laying performance of geese.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the omics analyses were based on two reproductive stages with three biological replicates per group; therefore, the findings primarily represent stage-associated molecular differences and may not capture the continuous temporal dynamics of pituitary regulation. Second, most miRNA–mRNA relationships were inferred from target prediction and inverse expression patterns. Although the dual-luciferase reporter assay supported a sequence-specific interaction between miR-338-y and the ADCY2 3′UTR, additional experiments are required to determine its effects on endogenous ADCY2 expression, intracellular cAMP and Ca²⁺ signaling, and gonadotropin secretion. Finally, the identified genes and miRNAs should be regarded as candidates rather than validated markers for breeding. Their relevance to reproductive traits requires functional validation and genotype–phenotype association analyses in larger, independent goose populations.
