For a generalized digroup D D , we construct an associative enveloping algebra A D A_{D} and prove that 𝖱𝖾𝗉 ⁡ ( D ) \mathsf{Rep}(D) is equivalent to the category of left A D A_{D} -modules. Introduction

Maschke’s theorem (
[
3
, Chapter 8]
,
[
8
, Section 2]
) is one of the foundational results of finite-dimensional representation theory.
If
G
is a finite group and the characteristic of the ground field does not divide the order of
G
,
then every finite-dimensional
G
-module is semisimple. Equivalently, every
G
-submodule
admits a
G
-stable complement, or, in homological terms, every short exact sequence of

G
-modules splits.

The strength of Maschke’s theorem lies not only in its statement but also in its mechanism.
Given a
G
-submodule
W\subseteq V
, one starts with an arbitrary linear projection

\pi:V\to W
, which need not be
G
-equivariant. Averaging over the group produces the operator

\pi_{G}=\frac{1}{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{g\in G}g\cdot\pi\cdot g^{-1},

which is
G
-equivariant and still projects onto
W
. Hence
\ker(\pi_{G})
is a
G
-stable
complement of
W
. In this way, semisimplicity is obtained by a canonical averaging process on
the group side
[
3
, Chapter 8, pp. 70–77]
.

The aim of this article is to understand how far this philosophy extends to generalized digroups.
Generalized digroups were introduced as a two-sided extension of groups, governed by two
associative products together with a nontrivial set of bar-units, called the halo

[
7
]
. Their structure is richer than that of ordinary groups in
two essential ways: they carry two products,
\vdash
and
\dashv
, linked by mixed
associativity identities, and they may have several bar-units rather than a single one. A
basic structural theorem shows that every generalized digroup is realized in product form

G\times E
, where
G
is a group acting on the halo
E

[
5
]
. This
product description is not merely a convenient model; it is the general form of the theory.

The representation theory of generalized digroups reflects this two-layered structure. A
representation consists of two operator families,
\lambda
and
\rho
, encoding the two
products and interacting through compatibility relations. Once one passes to the structural form

D\simeq G\times E
, the operators on the
\rho
-side depend only on the group component. Thus
the
\rho
-side retains a genuinely group-theoretic character and still admits a Maschke-type
averaging argument. The
\lambda
-side, by contrast, remains sensitive to the halo and carries
the extra data that distinguishes generalized digroups from groups.

This already suggests the form of the answer. One should not expect a full Maschke theorem in the
category of generalized digroup representations. What survives is a partial splitting phenomenon:
the group-theoretic side still behaves semisimply, while the halo side supports the obstruction to
full splitting. The main purpose of the paper is to isolate that obstruction and describe it
cohomologically
[
1
, Chapter III]
[
9
, Chapter 2]
.

Our first main result is categorical. To every generalized digroup
D
we associate an
associative enveloping algebra
A_{D}
and prove that the category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
of representations
of
D
is equivalent to the category of left
A_{D}
-modules. This places the theory in an
abelian framework with enough injectives and projectives, and allows extension groups to be treated
by standard homological methods
[
9
, Chapter 2]
[
2
, Chapter IV]
.

Our second main result is a Maschke-type splitting theorem on the
\rho
-side. Under the natural
hypothesis that the group component
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, every short
exact sequence in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
admits a
\rho
-equivariant splitting. The obstruction to a full
splitting in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is then concentrated entirely in the off-diagonal behaviour of the

\lambda
-operators. We show that this obstruction is encoded by an explicit family of cocycles,
which leads to the concrete identification

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)\cong Z^{1}(Q,W)/B^{1}(Q,W)

in the sense of extension classes
[
10
]
[
9
, Chapter 2]
.

Our third main contribution is structural. We construct explicit nonsplit extensions, showing that

\mathsf{Rep}(D)
need not be semisimple even when the group component lies in a classical Maschke
regime. We then derive a spectral sequence separating the group-theoretic and halo-theoretic
contributions to cohomology. Moreover, the forgetful functor to the halo algebra

B_{E}
preserves injective objects, so the
E_{2}
-page is expressed in terms of
group cohomology with coefficients in
\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{q}(Q,W)
. In the Maschke regime,
this spectral sequence collapses and yields criteria for splitting and for
non-semisimplicity.

The guiding theme is therefore clear. Averaging explains why the group side splits. What remains
after averaging is a genuinely halo-theoretic obstruction, invisible in the classical group case
and intrinsic to generalized digroups. The cocycle model for
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
and the spectral sequence
developed here provide a homological description of that residual obstruction.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the structural
theory of generalized digroups and fix the corresponding notion of representation. In
Section
3
we define the enveloping algebra
A_{D}
and prove the equivalence

\mathsf{Rep}(D)\simeq A_{D}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. In Section
4
we establish the
Maschke-type splitting on the
\rho
-side and obtain the cocycle description of

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)
. Section
5
provides explicit nonsplit extensions.
Finally, in Section
6
we derive the spectral sequence and its consequences for
splitting and non-semisimplicity.

2
Structural preliminaries on generalized digroups

In this section we recall the structural features of generalized digroups that will be used
throughout the paper. Our aim is twofold. First, we fix the product model
G\times E
that
underlies the classical structure theory and that will later allow us to separate the group side
from the halo side. Second, we introduce the notion of representation in the form needed for the
homological developments of the following sections. Although most of the material recalled here is
structural, it plays a decisive role in the later construction of the enveloping algebra and in the
cohomological analysis of extension classes.

2.1
Generalized digroups and their structural form

We begin by recalling the classical notion of a generalized digroup
[
7
, Definition 5]
.

Definition 1
.

A
generalized digroup
is a quadruple

(D,\vdash,\dashv,E),

where
D
is a nonempty set,
\vdash,\dashv:D\times D\to D
are two associative
binary operations, and
E\subseteq D
is a nonempty subset, called the
halo

or the set of
bar-units
, such that the following conditions hold:

(GD1)

For every
e\in E
and every
x\in D
,

x\dashv e=x,\qquad e\vdash x=x.

(GD2)

For every
e\in E
and every
x\in D
, there exist elements

x^{-1}_{l_{e}},\,x^{-1}_{r_{e}}\in D

such that

x^{-1}_{l_{e}}\dashv x=e,\qquad x\vdash x^{-1}_{r_{e}}=e.

(GD3)

For all
x,y,z\in D
,

x\vdash(y\dashv z)=(x\vdash y)\dashv z,

and

x\dashv(y\dashv z)=x\dashv(y\vdash z),\qquad(x\vdash y)\vdash z=(x\dashv y)\vdash z.

Remark 2
.

The halo
E
need not be a singleton. Thus a generalized digroup differs from a group not only
because it is governed by two products instead of one, but also because it may carry several
bar-units. This extra geometric direction is one of the basic algebraic sources of the extension
phenomena studied later.

For a fixed
e\in E
, the left and right inverses with respect to
e
organize
distinguished group parts of the generalized digroup.

Definition 3
.

Let
D=(D,\vdash,\dashv,E)
be a generalized digroup and let
e\in E
.
Define

G_{l}^{e}:=\{x^{-1}_{l_{e}}\mid x\in D\},\qquad G_{r}^{e}:=\{x^{-1}_{r_{e}}\mid x\in D\}.

These are called, respectively, the
left group at
e
and the

right group at
e
.

Remark 4
.

The notation above is standard in the structure theory of generalized digroups.
For each fixed bar-unit
e
, the sets
G_{l}^{e}
and
G_{r}^{e}
carry natural group
structures induced by
\dashv
and
\vdash
, respectively (
[
7
, Theorem 2]
). In the present article
the right component will be the one relevant for Maschke-type arguments, whereas the
structural decomposition is naturally expressed using the left group.

A key fact in the classical theory is that generalized digroups admit a product-type
description with an action on the halo. Since this theorem is structural and will be
used repeatedly later, we record it explicitly (
[
7
, Theorem 4 and 5]
).

Theorem 5

(Structure theorem)
.

Let
D=(D,\vdash,\dashv,E)
be a generalized digroup and let
e\in E
.
Then there exists a natural left action

G_{l}^{e}\times E\to E,\qquad(g,\alpha)\mapsto g\bullet\alpha,

such that
D
is isomorphic, as a generalized digroup, to

G_{l}^{e}\times E

endowed with the operations

(g,\alpha)\vdash(h,\beta)=(gh,\,g\bullet\beta),\qquad(g,\alpha)\dashv(h,\beta)=(gh,\,\alpha).

(1)

In particular, every generalized digroup is, up to isomorphism, of this form.

Remark 6
.

Theorem
5
shows that product constructions are not merely illustrative examples.
They constitute the general structural model of the classical theory. Accordingly, later arguments
may be developed in the product picture without loss of generality.

We next isolate the product construction appearing in Theorem
5
.

Construction 7
.

Let
G
be a group with identity
1
, let
E
be a nonempty set, and let

G\times E\to E,\qquad(g,\alpha)\mapsto g\bullet\alpha

be a left action of
G
on
E
.
Define two binary operations on
G\times E
by

(g,\alpha)\vdash(h,\beta):=(gh,\,g\bullet\beta),\qquad(g,\alpha)\dashv(h,\beta):=(gh,\,\alpha),

(2)

for all
(g,\alpha),(h,\beta)\in G\times E
. Define also

\overline{E}:=\{(1,\alpha)\mid\alpha\in E\}\subseteq G\times E.

Proposition 8
.

Let
G
be a group acting on a nonempty set
E
, and endow
G\times E
with the
operations

(g,\alpha)\vdash(h,\beta)=(gh,\,g\bullet\beta),\qquad(g,\alpha)\dashv(h,\beta)=(gh,\,\alpha),

together with the halo

\overline{E}:=\{(1,\alpha)\mid\alpha\in E\}.

Then
(G\times E,\vdash,\dashv,\overline{E})
is a generalized digroup.

Proof.

This is standard in the structure theory of generalized digroups. The verification of the
axioms follows by a direct computation using associativity in
G
and the action identity

(gh)\bullet\gamma=g\bullet(h\bullet\gamma)\qquad(g,h\in G,\ \gamma\in E).

For completeness, one checks that
\vdash
and
\dashv
are associative, that every

(1,\alpha)\in\overline{E}
is a bar-unit, and that the left and right inverses relative to

(1,\beta)
are given by

(g,\alpha)^{-1}_{l_{(1,\beta)}}=(g^{-1},\beta),\qquad(g,\alpha)^{-1}_{r_{(1,\beta)}}=(g^{-1},g^{-1}\bullet\beta).

The mixed identities are immediate from the definitions of
\vdash
and
\dashv
.
See, for instance,
[
7
, Proposition 7]
.
∎

Remark 9
.

For every
\alpha\in E
, the right group at the bar-unit
(1,\alpha)
is naturally identified
with
G
. Indeed, by the proof above,

(g,\xi)^{-1}_{r_{(1,\alpha)}}=(g^{-1},\,g^{-1}\bullet\alpha),

and therefore

G_{r}^{(1,\alpha)}=\{(g^{-1},\,g^{-1}\bullet\alpha)\mid g\in G\}.

The map

G\longrightarrow G_{r}^{(1,\alpha)},\qquad g\longmapsto(g^{-1},\,g^{-1}\bullet\alpha),

is bijective, and it transports the group structure of
G
to the group structure of

G_{r}^{(1,\alpha)}
induced by
\vdash
. This is the group component to which the classical
Maschke theorem will later be applied.

2.2
Representations of generalized digroups

We now fix the representation-theoretic language used throughout the article
[
5
, Definition 6]
.

Definition 10
.

Let
D=(D,\vdash,\dashv,E)
be a generalized digroup. A
representation
of
D

over
\mathbb{K}
is a triple

(V,\lambda,\rho),

where
V
is a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space,

\lambda:D\to\operatorname{End}(V),\qquad\rho:D\to\operatorname{Aut}(V),

are maps satisfying, for all
x,y\in D
and all
e\in E
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{x\dashv y}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y},\qquad

\displaystyle\rho_{x\vdash y}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}\rho_{y},\qquad

\displaystyle\rho_{e}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}_{V},

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\lambda_{y}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\vdash y},\qquad

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\dashv y}.

Remark 11
.

With this convention, the
\rho
-side reflects the group component in the usual order.
In particular, once one passes to the structural form
D\simeq G\times E
and proves that

\rho_{(g,\alpha)}
depends only on
g
, the resulting map

g\longmapsto\rho_{g}

is a genuine representation of the group
G
.

Remark 12
.

The asymmetry between
\lambda
and
\rho
is structural. The operators
\rho_{x}
are
required to be invertible, whereas no invertibility is assumed for
\lambda_{x}
. This is the
mechanism that later permits triangular and nilpotent behavior to coexist with semisimplicity
on the group side.

Definition 13
.

Let
(V,\lambda,\rho)
and
(W,\lambda^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be representations of
D
.
A
morphism of representations

f:(V,\lambda,\rho)\longrightarrow(W,\lambda^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})

is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map
f:V\to W
such that, for every
x\in D
,

f\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{\prime}_{x}f,\qquad f\rho_{x}=\rho^{\prime}_{x}f.

Proposition 14
.

Generalized digroup representations over
\mathbb{K}
, together with their morphisms,
form a category, which we denote by
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
.

Proof.

Let
(V,\lambda,\rho)
be a representation of
D
. The identity map
\mathrm{id}_{V}
is
clearly a morphism, because

\mathrm{id}_{V}\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{x}\mathrm{id}_{V},\qquad\mathrm{id}_{V}\rho_{x}=\rho_{x}=\rho_{x}\mathrm{id}_{V}

for every
x\in D
.

Now let

f:(V,\lambda,\rho)\to(W,\lambda^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}),\qquad g:(W,\lambda^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})\to(U,\lambda^{\prime\prime},\rho^{\prime\prime})

be morphisms of representations. Then
g\circ f
is
\mathbb{K}
-linear and, for every
x\in D
,

\displaystyle(g\circ f)\lambda_{x}

\displaystyle=g(f\lambda_{x})=g(\lambda^{\prime}_{x}f)=(g\lambda^{\prime}_{x})f=(\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{x}g)f=\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{x}(g\circ f),

and similarly

\displaystyle(g\circ f)\rho_{x}

\displaystyle=g(f\rho_{x})=g(\rho^{\prime}_{x}f)=(g\rho^{\prime}_{x})f=(\rho^{\prime\prime}_{x}g)f=\rho^{\prime\prime}_{x}(g\circ f).

Thus
g\circ f
is again a morphism of representations.

Associativity of composition and the identity laws are inherited from the category of

\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces. Therefore
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is a category.
∎

Remark 15
.

Section
2
fixes the structural and representation-theoretic language
used in the remainder of the paper. In the next section we replace the category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)

by a more algebraically tractable model, namely the category of modules over an associative
enveloping algebra.

3
The enveloping algebra and the category of representations

The purpose of this section is to place the representation theory of a generalized digroup
inside a standard homological framework. We do this by attaching to every generalized digroup

D
an associative algebra
A_{D}
whose module category is equivalent to
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
.

3.1
The enveloping algebra

Let
D=(D,\vdash,\dashv,E)
be a generalized digroup. Consider two formal copies of the set
D
,
denoted by

\{L_{x}\mid x\in D\},\qquad\{R_{x}\mid x\in D\}.

Let
W_{D}
be the free
\mathbb{K}
-vector space with basis

\mathcal{X}_{D}:=\{L_{x},R_{x}\mid x\in D\}.

We denote by

T(W_{D})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}W_{D}^{\otimes n}

the tensor algebra of
W_{D}
. Thus
T(W_{D})
is the free unital associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
generated by the symbols
L_{x}
and
R_{x}
,
x\in D
.

Definition 16
.

Let
I_{D}
be the two-sided ideal of
T(W_{D})
generated by the elements

\displaystyle L_{x\dashv y}-L_{x}L_{y},

(3)

\displaystyle R_{x\vdash y}-R_{x}R_{y},

(4)

\displaystyle R_{e}-1\qquad(e\in E),

(5)

\displaystyle R_{x}L_{y}-L_{x\vdash y},

(6)

\displaystyle L_{x}R_{y}-L_{x\dashv y},

(7)

for all
x,y\in D
. The
enveloping algebra
of
D
is the quotient algebra

A_{D}:=T(W_{D})/I_{D}.

Remark 17
.

Relations (
3
)–(
7
) are the formal shadows of the axioms of a
representation. The symbols
L_{x}
encode the operators
\lambda_{x}
, while the symbols

R_{x}
encode the operators
\rho_{x}
. Accordingly, the algebra
A_{D}
is designed so that
a representation of
D
is exactly the same thing as a left
A_{D}
-module.

3.2
Universal property

The construction above is best understood through its universal property.

Proposition 18
.

Let
B
be a unital associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. Then to give a unital algebra morphism

\Phi:A_{D}\longrightarrow B

is equivalent to giving two families

(\ell_{x})_{x\in D}\subseteq B,\qquad(r_{x})_{x\in D}\subseteq B

such that, for all
x,y\in D
and all
e\in E
,

\displaystyle\ell_{x\dashv y}

\displaystyle=\ell_{x}\ell_{y},

(8)

\displaystyle r_{x\vdash y}

\displaystyle=r_{x}r_{y},

(9)

\displaystyle r_{e}

\displaystyle=1,

(10)

\displaystyle r_{x}\ell_{y}

\displaystyle=\ell_{x\vdash y},

(11)

\displaystyle\ell_{x}r_{y}

\displaystyle=\ell_{x\dashv y}.

(12)

Proof.

Assume first that
\Phi:A_{D}\to B
is a unital algebra morphism. Let

\pi:T(W_{D})\longrightarrow A_{D}

be the quotient map, and define

\ell_{x}:=\Phi(\pi(L_{x})),\qquad r_{x}:=\Phi(\pi(R_{x})).

Since every generator of the ideal
I_{D}
maps to zero in
A_{D}
, its image under
\Phi

must be zero in
B
. Therefore

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Phi\bigl(\pi(L_{x\dashv y}-L_{x}L_{y})\bigr)=\ell_{x\dashv y}-\ell_{x}\ell_{y},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Phi\bigl(\pi(R_{x\vdash y}-R_{x}R_{y})\bigr)=r_{x\vdash y}-r_{x}r_{y},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Phi\bigl(\pi(R_{e}-1)\bigr)=r_{e}-1,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Phi\bigl(\pi(R_{x}L_{y}-L_{x\vdash y})\bigr)=r_{x}\ell_{y}-\ell_{x\vdash y},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Phi\bigl(\pi(L_{x}R_{y}-L_{x\dashv y})\bigr)=\ell_{x}r_{y}-\ell_{x\dashv y}.

Hence the families
(\ell_{x})
and
(r_{x})
satisfy (
8
)–(
12
).

Conversely, suppose that families
(\ell_{x})
and
(r_{x})
in
B
are given and satisfy
(
8
)–(
12
). Since
T(W_{D})
is the tensor algebra of
W_{D}
, the assignment

L_{x}\longmapsto\ell_{x},\qquad R_{x}\longmapsto r_{x}

extends uniquely to a unital algebra morphism

\widetilde{\Phi}:T(W_{D})\longrightarrow B.

We claim that
I_{D}\subseteq\ker(\widetilde{\Phi})
. Indeed, each generator of
I_{D}
is sent to
zero:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(L_{x\dashv y}-L_{x}L_{y})

\displaystyle=\ell_{x\dashv y}-\ell_{x}\ell_{y}=0,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(R_{x\vdash y}-R_{x}R_{y})

\displaystyle=r_{x\vdash y}-r_{x}r_{y}=0,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(R_{e}-1)

\displaystyle=r_{e}-1=0,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(R_{x}L_{y}-L_{x\vdash y})

\displaystyle=r_{x}\ell_{y}-\ell_{x\vdash y}=0,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(L_{x}R_{y}-L_{x\dashv y})

\displaystyle=\ell_{x}r_{y}-\ell_{x\dashv y}=0.

Therefore
\widetilde{\Phi}
factors uniquely through the quotient
A_{D}
, yielding a unital
algebra morphism

\Phi:A_{D}\longrightarrow B.

The two constructions are inverse to each other by inspection. This proves the claim.
∎

3.3
A structural lemma

Before passing to the equivalence of categories, we isolate a simple lemma which guarantees that
the operators
R_{x}
act invertibly on every
A_{D}
-module. This point is essential, because in
Definition
10
the map
\rho
takes values in
\operatorname{Aut}(V)
, not merely in

\operatorname{End}(V)
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
D=(D,\vdash,\dashv,E)
be a generalized digroup and let
x\in D
. Then there exists an
element
x^{\sharp}\in D
such that

x\vdash x^{\sharp}\in E\qquad\text{and}\qquad x^{\sharp}\vdash x\in E.

Proof.

By Theorem
5
, the generalized digroup
D
is isomorphic to a structural model

G\times E

with operations

(g,\alpha)\vdash(h,\beta)=(gh,\,g\bullet\beta),\qquad(g,\alpha)\dashv(h,\beta)=(gh,\,\alpha).

Write the image of
x
under such an isomorphism as
(g,\alpha)
. Define

(g,\alpha)^{\sharp}:=(g^{-1},g^{-1}\bullet\alpha).

Then

(g,\alpha)\vdash(g^{-1},g^{-1}\bullet\alpha)=(1,\,g\bullet(g^{-1}\bullet\alpha))=(1,\alpha)\in\overline{E},

and also

(g^{-1},g^{-1}\bullet\alpha)\vdash(g,\alpha)=(1,\,g^{-1}\bullet\alpha)\in\overline{E}.

Transporting this element back through the isomorphism yields an element
x^{\sharp}\in D
such
that

x\vdash x^{\sharp}\in E\qquad\text{and}\qquad x^{\sharp}\vdash x\in E.

This proves the lemma.
∎

3.4
Equivalence with modules over
A_{D}

We now prove the main result of the section.

Theorem 20
.

There is an equivalence of categories

\mathsf{Rep}(D)\simeq A_{D}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},

where
A_{D}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
denotes the category of left
A_{D}
-modules.

Proof.

We construct functors in both directions and prove that they are quasi-inverse.

Step 1: from representations to modules.

Let
(V,\lambda,\rho)
be a representation of
D
. Set

\ell_{x}:=\lambda_{x}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(V),\qquad r_{x}:=\rho_{x}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(V)

for every
x\in D
. Since
(V,\lambda,\rho)
satisfies the axioms of
Definition
10
, we have

\displaystyle\ell_{x\dashv y}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\dashv y}=\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}=\ell_{x}\ell_{y},

\displaystyle r_{x\vdash y}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x\vdash y}=\rho_{x}\rho_{y}=r_{x}r_{y},

\displaystyle r_{e}

\displaystyle=\rho_{e}=\mathrm{id}_{V},

\displaystyle r_{x}\ell_{y}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{x\vdash y}=\ell_{x\vdash y},

\displaystyle\ell_{x}r_{y}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}=\lambda_{x\dashv y}=\ell_{x\dashv y}.

Thus Proposition
18
applies with
B=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(V)
, and yields a unique
unital algebra morphism

\Phi_{(\lambda,\rho)}:A_{D}\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(V)

such that

\Phi_{(\lambda,\rho)}(L_{x})=\lambda_{x},\qquad\Phi_{(\lambda,\rho)}(R_{x})=\rho_{x}

for all
x\in D
. In other words,
V
becomes a left
A_{D}
-module.

Now let

f:(V,\lambda,\rho)\longrightarrow(W,\lambda^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})

be a morphism in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Then, for every
x\in D
,

f\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{\prime}_{x}f,\qquad f\rho_{x}=\rho^{\prime}_{x}f.

Since the generators
L_{x}
and
R_{x}
act on
V
and
W
by
\lambda_{x},\rho_{x}
and

\lambda^{\prime}_{x},\rho^{\prime}_{x}
, respectively, it follows that
f
commutes with the action of every
generator of
A_{D}
, hence with the action of every element of
A_{D}
. Therefore
f
is

A_{D}
-linear.

We have thus defined a functor

\mathcal{F}:\mathsf{Rep}(D)\longrightarrow A_{D}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}.

Step 2: from modules to representations.

Let
M
be a left
A_{D}
-module. For each
x\in D
, define

\lambda_{x}(m):=L_{x}\cdot m,\qquad\rho_{x}(m):=R_{x}\cdot m,\qquad m\in M.

We first verify that
\rho_{x}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}(M)
for every
x\in D
. By Lemma
19
,
there exists
x^{\sharp}\in D
such that

x\vdash x^{\sharp}\in E\qquad\text{and}\qquad x^{\sharp}\vdash x\in E.

Using the defining relations of
A_{D}
, we compute

\displaystyle R_{x}R_{x^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=R_{x\vdash x^{\sharp}}=1,

because
x\vdash x^{\sharp}\in E
, and similarly

\displaystyle R_{x^{\sharp}}R_{x}

\displaystyle=R_{x^{\sharp}\vdash x}=1,

because
x^{\sharp}\vdash x\in E
. Hence
R_{x}
is invertible in
A_{D}
, with inverse

R_{x^{\sharp}}
. Therefore the operator
\rho_{x}
on
M
is invertible, so indeed

\rho_{x}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}(M).

We next verify the five axioms of a representation.

For
(R1)
, let
m\in M
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{x\dashv y}(m)

\displaystyle=L_{x\dashv y}\cdot m

\displaystyle=(L_{x}L_{y})\cdot m

\displaystyle=L_{x}\cdot(L_{y}\cdot m)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}(\lambda_{y}(m)).

Thus

\lambda_{x\dashv y}=\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}.

For
(R2)
,

\displaystyle\rho_{x\vdash y}(m)

\displaystyle=R_{x\vdash y}\cdot m

\displaystyle=(R_{x}R_{y})\cdot m

\displaystyle=R_{x}\cdot(R_{y}\cdot m)

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}(\rho_{y}(m)),

so

\rho_{x\vdash y}=\rho_{x}\rho_{y}.

For
(R3)
, if
e\in E
, then

\rho_{e}(m)=R_{e}\cdot m=1\cdot m=m.

Hence
\rho_{e}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
.

For
(R4)
,

\displaystyle(\rho_{x}\lambda_{y})(m)

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}(\lambda_{y}(m))

\displaystyle=R_{x}\cdot(L_{y}\cdot m)

\displaystyle=(R_{x}L_{y})\cdot m

\displaystyle=L_{x\vdash y}\cdot m

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\vdash y}(m).

Therefore

\rho_{x}\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{x\vdash y}.

For
(R5)
,

\displaystyle(\lambda_{x}\rho_{y})(m)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}(\rho_{y}(m))

\displaystyle=L_{x}\cdot(R_{y}\cdot m)

\displaystyle=(L_{x}R_{y})\cdot m

\displaystyle=L_{x\dashv y}\cdot m

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\dashv y}(m).

Hence

\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}=\lambda_{x\dashv y}.

Thus
(M,\lambda,\rho)
is a representation of
D
.

Now let
g:M\to N
be an
A_{D}
-linear map. For every
x\in D
and every
m\in M
,

g(\lambda_{x}(m))=g(L_{x}\cdot m)=L_{x}\cdot g(m)=\lambda^{\prime}_{x}(g(m)),

and similarly

g(\rho_{x}(m))=g(R_{x}\cdot m)=R_{x}\cdot g(m)=\rho^{\prime}_{x}(g(m)).

Hence
g
is a morphism of representations. We have therefore defined a functor

\mathcal{G}:A_{D}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Rep}(D).

Step 3: verification that the functors are quasi-inverse.

Let
(V,\lambda,\rho)
be a representation. Applying
\mathcal{F}
endows
V
with the

A_{D}
-module structure for which

L_{x}\cdot v=\lambda_{x}(v),\qquad R_{x}\cdot v=\rho_{x}(v).

Applying
\mathcal{G}
to this module recovers exactly the original operators
\lambda_{x}

and
\rho_{x}
. Hence

\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}.

Conversely, let
M
be a left
A_{D}
-module. Applying
\mathcal{G}
defines operators

\lambda_{x}
and
\rho_{x}
by the actions of
L_{x}
and
R_{x}
. Applying
\mathcal{F}

to the resulting representation recovers the original module structure, because
A_{D}
is
generated by the classes of the symbols
L_{x}
and
R_{x}
. Therefore

\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{G}=\mathrm{id}_{A_{D}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}}.

The two functors are thus quasi-inverse equivalences of categories.
∎

3.5
Homological consequences

The categorical equivalence above has immediate formal consequences which will be used throughout
the paper.

Corollary 21
.

The category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is abelian.

Proof.

By Theorem
20
, the category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is equivalent to the category of left
modules over the associative algebra
A_{D}
. The latter is an abelian category. Since abelianness
is invariant under equivalence of categories,
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is abelian.
∎

Corollary 22
.

The category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
has enough projectives.

Proof.

The category of left modules over any associative algebra has enough projectives. By
Theorem
20
,
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is equivalent to such a module category. Hence

\mathsf{Rep}(D)
also has enough projectives.
∎

Corollary 23
.

The category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
has enough injectives.

Proof.

By Theorem
20
, the category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is equivalent to the category of left
modules over the associative algebra
A_{D}
. The latter has enough injectives. Hence

\mathsf{Rep}(D)
also has enough injectives.
∎

Corollary 24
.

For all objects
Q,W\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
and every integer
n\geq 0
, the extension group

\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)

is well defined.

Proof.

By Corollary
21
, the category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is abelian, and by
Corollary
23
, it has enough injectives. Therefore the right derived functors of

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,-)

exist. By definition, these derived functors are precisely the groups

\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W).

∎

Remark 25
.

Section
3
provides the formal bridge from generalized digroups to homological
algebra. From this point on, extension classes in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
may be studied either categorically
or through the algebra
A_{D}
. This is the framework in which the Maschke splitting and the
cocycle model for
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
will be developed in the next section.

4
Maschke splitting and a cocycle model for
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}

We now turn to the cohomological core of the paper. The main point is that, under a Maschke-type
hypothesis on the group component, every short exact sequence splits on the
\rho
-side. The
remaining obstruction to splitting in the full representation category is then forced to live in
the
\lambda
-side, where it can be encoded by an explicit family of cocycles. The purpose of
this section is to make that obstruction precise and to identify it with
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
.

4.1
The group part of the
\rho
-action

The first point is that the
\rho
-operators depend only on the group component.

Proposition 26
.

Let
(V,\lambda,\rho)
be a representation of
D=G\times E
. Then, for every
g\in G
and
every
\alpha,\beta\in E
,

\rho_{(g,\alpha)}=\rho_{(g,\beta)}.

Consequently, one may define

\rho_{g}:=\rho_{(g,\alpha)}\qquad(g\in G),

independently of the choice of
\alpha\in E
. Moreover,

\rho_{gh}=\rho_{g}\rho_{h}\qquad(g,h\in G),

so that
g\mapsto\rho_{g}
is a genuine representation of the group
G
on
V
.

Proof.

Fix
g\in G
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in E
. Since
G
acts on
E
, we may write

\alpha=g\bullet(g^{-1}\bullet\alpha).

Therefore,

(g,\alpha)=(g,\gamma)\vdash(1,g^{-1}\bullet\alpha).

Applying axiom
(R2)
and then
(R3)
, we obtain

\rho_{(g,\alpha)}=\rho_{(g,\gamma)}\,\rho_{(1,g^{-1}\bullet\alpha)}=\rho_{(g,\gamma)}\,\mathrm{id}_{V}=\rho_{(g,\gamma)}.

Since
\gamma
is arbitrary, this proves that
\rho_{(g,\alpha)}
depends only on
g
.

Now let
g,h\in G
and choose any
\alpha,\beta\in E
. Then

(g,\alpha)\vdash(h,\beta)=(gh,\,g\bullet\beta),

so by
(R2)
,

\rho_{gh}=\rho_{(gh,g\bullet\beta)}=\rho_{(g,\alpha)}\,\rho_{(h,\beta)}=\rho_{g}\rho_{h}.

Thus
g\mapsto\rho_{g}
is a genuine representation of
G
.
∎

Remark 27
.

If we write

L_{\alpha}:=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}\qquad(\alpha\in E),

then for every
g\in G
and
\alpha\in E
one has

\lambda_{(g,\alpha)}=L_{\alpha}\,\rho_{g}.

Indeed, for any
\beta\in E
,

(1,\alpha)\dashv(g,\beta)=(g,\alpha),

and therefore axiom
(R5)
gives

\lambda_{(g,\alpha)}=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)\dashv(g,\beta)}=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}\rho_{(g,\beta)}=L_{\alpha}\rho_{g}.

We shall not use this identity immediately, but it will later clarify the role played by the halo.

4.2
Maschke splitting on the
\rho
-side

We now impose the hypothesis under which the classical Maschke theorem may be applied.

Standing hypothesis.

Throughout the rest of this section, we assume that
G
is finite and that

\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
.

Under this hypothesis, the group algebra
\mathbb{K}[G]
is semisimple
[
3
, Theorem 8.7]
. In particular, every

\mathbb{K}[G]
-module is semisimple, and therefore every short exact sequence of
G
-modules splits.

Proposition 28
.

Let

0\longrightarrow W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Q\longrightarrow 0

(13)

be a short exact sequence in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Then there exists a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map

s:Q\longrightarrow V

such that

\pi\circ s=\mathrm{id}_{Q}

and

\rho_{x}\circ s=s\circ\rho_{x}^{Q}\qquad(x\in D).

Equivalently, every short exact sequence in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
splits on the
\rho
-side.

Proof.

By Proposition
26
, the operators
\rho_{x}
depend only on the group
component
g\in G
, and the assignment

g\longmapsto\rho_{g}

is a genuine representation of
G
. Thus (
13
) is, in particular, a short exact
sequence of
G
-modules.

Since
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, Maschke’s theorem applies, and there
exists a
G
-equivariant linear section

s:Q\longrightarrow V

such that

\pi\circ s=\mathrm{id}_{Q}

and

\rho_{g}\circ s=s\circ\rho_{g}^{Q}\qquad(g\in G).

Finally, because
\rho_{x}
depends only on the group component of
x\in D=G\times E
, the same
identity holds for every
x\in D
. Thus
s
is
\rho
-equivariant on all of
D
.
∎

Fix once and for all such a
\rho
-equivariant splitting
s
. Then every element
v\in V

admits a unique decomposition

v=\iota(w)+s(q)\qquad(w\in W,\ q\in Q),

and hence
V
is identified, as a vector space, with
W\oplus Q
.

Lemma 29
.

With respect to the decomposition
V\cong W\oplus Q
induced by the splitting
s
, the operators

\rho_{x}
and
\lambda_{x}
have the form

\rho_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{x}^{W}&0\\
0&\rho_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\lambda_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}&\theta_{x}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix},

where
\theta_{x}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(Q,W)
for every
x\in D
.

Proof.

Since
W
is a subrepresentation, it is stable under all
\rho_{x}
and all
\lambda_{x}
. Hence
the lower-left block of both operators is zero.

Because the chosen splitting
s
is
\rho
-equivariant, one has

\rho_{x}(s(q))=s(\rho_{x}^{Q}(q))\qquad(x\in D,\ q\in Q),

so no
W
-component is produced from the
Q
-part under
\rho_{x}
. Therefore the upper-right
block of
\rho_{x}
is also zero. Thus

\rho_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{x}^{W}&0\\
0&\rho_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

For
\lambda_{x}
there is a uniquely
determined linear map

\theta_{x}:Q\to W,

which records the
W
-component of
\lambda_{x}(s(q))
. Hence

\lambda_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}&\theta_{x}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

∎

Remark 30
.

The family
\theta=\{\theta_{x}\}_{x\in D}
is the obstruction to extending the
\rho
-equivariant
splitting to a full splitting in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. If
\theta_{x}=0
for every
x\in D
, then the image
of
s
is stable under all
\lambda_{x}
and all
\rho_{x}
, and therefore (
13
)
splits in the full representation category.

4.3
The cocycle space
Z^{1}(Q,W)

We now determine the identities satisfied by the family
\theta
.

Definition 31
.

Let
Q,W\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Define
Z^{1}(Q,W)
to be the set of all families

\theta=\{\theta_{x}\}_{x\in D},\qquad\theta_{x}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(Q,W),

such that, for all
x,y\in D
,

\displaystyle\theta_{x\dashv y}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}+\theta_{x}\lambda_{y}^{Q},

(14)

\displaystyle\theta_{x\vdash y}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}^{W}\theta_{y},

(15)

\displaystyle\theta_{x\dashv y}

\displaystyle=\theta_{x}\rho_{y}^{Q}.

(16)

Lemma 32
.

Let (
13
) be a short exact sequence in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
, choose a
\rho
-equivariant
splitting
s
, and let
\theta=\{\theta_{x}\}_{x\in D}
be the corresponding off-diagonal family
from Lemma
29
. Then
\theta\in Z^{1}(Q,W)
.

Proof.

We compare the upper-right blocks in the representation identities.

Step 1: the identity (
14
).

Axiom
(R1)
gives

\lambda_{x\dashv y}=\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}.

Using Lemma
29
,

\lambda_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}&\theta_{x}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\lambda_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{y}^{W}&\theta_{y}\\
0&\lambda_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

Multiplying these matrices yields

\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}\lambda_{y}^{W}&\lambda_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}+\theta_{x}\lambda_{y}^{Q}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\lambda_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

On the other hand,

\lambda_{x\dashv y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{W}&\theta_{x\dashv y}\\
0&\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

Comparing the upper-right blocks gives

\theta_{x\dashv y}=\lambda_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}+\theta_{x}\lambda_{y}^{Q}.

Step 2: the identity (
15
).

Axiom
(R4)
gives

\rho_{x}\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{x\vdash y}.

Since
\rho_{x}
is block diagonal,

\rho_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{x}^{W}&0\\
0&\rho_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\lambda_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{y}^{W}&\theta_{y}\\
0&\lambda_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix},

so

\rho_{x}\lambda_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{x}^{W}\lambda_{y}^{W}&\rho_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}\\
0&\rho_{x}^{Q}\lambda_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

Comparing the upper-right blocks with

\lambda_{x\vdash y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x\vdash y}^{W}&\theta_{x\vdash y}\\
0&\lambda_{x\vdash y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}

gives

\theta_{x\vdash y}=\rho_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}.

Step 3: the identity (
16
).

Axiom
(R5)
gives

\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}=\lambda_{x\dashv y}.

Using the block forms,

\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}&\theta_{x}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{y}^{W}&0\\
0&\rho_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}\rho_{y}^{W}&\theta_{x}\rho_{y}^{Q}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\rho_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

Comparing the upper-right blocks with
\lambda_{x\dashv y}
yields

\theta_{x\dashv y}=\theta_{x}\rho_{y}^{Q}.

Thus
\theta
satisfies all defining identities of
Z^{1}(Q,W)
.
∎

4.4
Coboundaries and the quotient
Z^{1}/B^{1}

We next describe the dependence of
\theta
on the choice of
\rho
-equivariant splitting.

Definition 33
.

Let
Q,W\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Define

\operatorname{Hom}_{\rho}(Q,W):=\{t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(Q,W)\mid\rho_{g}^{W}t=t\rho_{g}^{Q}\text{ for all }g\in G\}.

For
t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\rho}(Q,W)
, define a family

\delta t=\{(\delta t)_{x}\}_{x\in D}

by

(\delta t)_{x}:=\lambda_{x}^{W}t-t\lambda_{x}^{Q}.

(17)

Let
B^{1}(Q,W)
be the set of all families of the form
\delta t
,
t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\rho}(Q,W)
.

Lemma 34
.

For every
Q,W\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
, one has

B^{1}(Q,W)\subseteq Z^{1}(Q,W).

Proof.

Let
t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\rho}(Q,W)
, and define
\theta:=\delta t
by (
17
). We verify the
three cocycle identities.

Verification of (
14
).

Using axiom
(R1)
for
W
and
Q
, we compute

\displaystyle\theta_{x\dashv y}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{W}t-t\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}^{W}\lambda_{y}^{W}t-t\lambda_{x}^{Q}\lambda_{y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}^{W}(\lambda_{y}^{W}t-t\lambda_{y}^{Q})+(\lambda_{x}^{W}t-t\lambda_{x}^{Q})\lambda_{y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}+\theta_{x}\lambda_{y}^{Q}.

Verification of (
15
).

Using axiom
(R4)
for
W
and
Q
, and the fact that
t
intertwines the
\rho
-action,
we obtain

\displaystyle\theta_{x\vdash y}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\vdash y}^{W}t-t\lambda_{x\vdash y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}^{W}\lambda_{y}^{W}t-t\rho_{x}^{Q}\lambda_{y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}^{W}\lambda_{y}^{W}t-\rho_{x}^{W}t\lambda_{y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}^{W}(\lambda_{y}^{W}t-t\lambda_{y}^{Q})

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}.

Verification of (
16
).

Using axiom
(R5)
for
W
and
Q
, again together with the intertwining property of
t
,
we compute

\displaystyle\theta_{x\dashv y}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{W}t-t\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}^{W}\rho_{y}^{W}t-t\lambda_{x}^{Q}\rho_{y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}^{W}t\rho_{y}^{Q}-t\lambda_{x}^{Q}\rho_{y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{x}^{W}t-t\lambda_{x}^{Q})\rho_{y}^{Q}

\displaystyle=\theta_{x}\rho_{y}^{Q}.

Therefore
\theta\in Z^{1}(Q,W)
, and hence
B^{1}(Q,W)\subseteq Z^{1}(Q,W)
.
∎

Lemma 35
.

Let (
13
) be a short exact sequence in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
, let
s:Q\to V
be a

\rho
-equivariant splitting, and let
\theta\in Z^{1}(Q,W)
be the corresponding cocycle.
For
t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\rho}(Q,W)
, define

s_{t}:=s+\iota t.

Then
s_{t}
is again
\rho
-equivariant, and the cocycle associated with
s_{t}
is

\theta+\delta t.

Proof.

First observe that

\pi s_{t}=\pi s+\pi\iota\,t=\mathrm{id}_{Q},

so
s_{t}
is again a linear section of
\pi
. Next, for every
g\in G
,

\displaystyle\rho_{g}s_{t}

\displaystyle=\rho_{g}s+\rho_{g}\iota t

\displaystyle=s\rho_{g}^{Q}+\iota\rho_{g}^{W}t

\displaystyle=s\rho_{g}^{Q}+\iota t\rho_{g}^{Q}

\displaystyle=(s+\iota t)\rho_{g}^{Q}

\displaystyle=s_{t}\rho_{g}^{Q},

because
s
is
\rho
-equivariant and
t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\rho}(Q,W)
. Since
\rho_{x}
depends only on
the group component, this proves that
s_{t}
is
\rho
-equivariant for every
x\in D
.

Let
\Phi_{t}:W\oplus Q\to W\oplus Q
be the linear automorphism

\Phi_{t}(w,q)=(w+t(q),\,q).

Then the block matrix of
\lambda_{x}
with respect to the splitting
s_{t}
is

\Phi_{t}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}&\theta_{x}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}\Phi_{t}.

A direct computation gives

\Phi_{t}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}&\theta_{x}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}\Phi_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}&\theta_{x}+\lambda_{x}^{W}t-t\lambda_{x}^{Q}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence the new off-diagonal block is

\theta_{x}+(\delta t)_{x}.

Therefore the cocycle associated with
s_{t}
is
\theta+\delta t
.
∎

4.5
The cocycle model for
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}

We may now identify
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
with the quotient
Z^{1}/B^{1}
.

Theorem 36
.

Let
Q,W\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Under the standing hypothesis that
G
is finite and

\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, there is a bijection

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)\cong Z^{1}(Q,W)/B^{1}(Q,W).

Moreover, the zero class corresponds exactly to split short exact sequences.

Proof.

By Corollary
24
, the group
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)
is defined in the abelian
category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
, which has enough injectives and projectives. We use the Yoneda interpretation
of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
(
[
9
, Section 3.4]
or
[
10
]
), namely that

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)
classifies short exact sequences

0\to W\to V\to Q\to 0

up to the usual equivalence of extensions.

Step 1: from an extension to a cocycle class.

Let

0\to W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\to}}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}Q\to 0

be a short exact sequence in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. By Proposition
28
, there exists a

\rho
-equivariant splitting
s:Q\to V
. The corresponding family

\theta=\{\theta_{x}\}_{x\in D}

obtained from Lemma
29
belongs to
Z^{1}(Q,W)
by
Lemma
32
. We assign to the extension the class

[\theta]\in Z^{1}(Q,W)/B^{1}(Q,W).

Step 2: independence of the choice of splitting.

If
s^{\prime}
is another
\rho
-equivariant splitting, then

s^{\prime}=s+\iota t

for a unique
t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(Q,W)
. Since both
s
and
s^{\prime}
are
\rho
-equivariant, the map

t
lies in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\rho}(Q,W)
. By Lemma
35
, the cocycle attached to
s^{\prime}

is

\theta+\delta t.

Thus the class of the cocycle in
Z^{1}(Q,W)/B^{1}(Q,W)
is independent of the chosen

\rho
-equivariant splitting.

Step 3: from a cocycle to an extension.

Conversely, let
\theta\in Z^{1}(Q,W)
. Define a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space

V_{\theta}:=W\oplus Q

and define operators on
V_{\theta}
by

\rho_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{x}^{W}&0\\
0&\rho_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\lambda_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}&\theta_{x}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

We claim that these operators define a representation of
D
.

Indeed,
\rho_{x}\in\operatorname{Aut}(V_{\theta})
because
\rho_{x}^{W}
and
\rho_{x}^{Q}
are invertible. Axiom

(R2)
follows immediately from block diagonality and the fact that
W
and
Q
are already
representations:

\rho_{x\vdash y}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{x\vdash y}^{W}&0\\
0&\rho_{x\vdash y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{x}^{W}\rho_{y}^{W}&0\\
0&\rho_{x}^{Q}\rho_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}=\rho_{x}\rho_{y}.

Similarly,
\rho_{e}=\mathrm{id}
follows from
\rho_{e}^{W}=\mathrm{id}_{W}
and
\rho_{e}^{Q}=\mathrm{id}_{Q}
.

For axiom
(R1)
, we compute

\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}\lambda_{y}^{W}&\lambda_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}+\theta_{x}\lambda_{y}^{Q}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\lambda_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\theta\in Z^{1}(Q,W)
, identity (
14
) gives

\lambda_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}+\theta_{x}\lambda_{y}^{Q}=\theta_{x\dashv y},

while
W
and
Q
satisfy
(R1)
. Hence

\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{W}&\theta_{x\dashv y}\\
0&\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda_{x\dashv y}.

For axiom
(R4)
,

\rho_{x}\lambda_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{x}^{W}\lambda_{y}^{W}&\rho_{x}^{W}\theta_{y}\\
0&\rho_{x}^{Q}\lambda_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\theta
satisfies (
15
), and
W,Q
satisfy
(R4)
, we obtain

\rho_{x}\lambda_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x\vdash y}^{W}&\theta_{x\vdash y}\\
0&\lambda_{x\vdash y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda_{x\vdash y}.

For axiom
(R5)
,

\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}\rho_{y}^{W}&\theta_{x}\rho_{y}^{Q}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\rho_{y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}.

Using (
16
) and the fact that
W,Q
satisfy
(R5)
, we deduce

\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{W}&\theta_{x\dashv y}\\
0&\lambda_{x\dashv y}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda_{x\dashv y}.

Thus
V_{\theta}
is a representation of
D
. The canonical maps

0\to W\to W\oplus Q\to Q\to 0

define a short exact sequence in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
.

Step 4: passage to the quotient by
B^{1}
.

If
\theta^{\prime}=\theta+\delta t
for some
t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\rho}(Q,W)
, define

\Phi_{t}:W\oplus Q\to W\oplus Q,\qquad\Phi_{t}(w,q)=(w+t(q),q).

Then
\Phi_{t}
is the identity on the subobject
W
and induces the identity on the quotient

Q
. Moreover, the
\rho
-operators are block diagonal, so
\Phi_{t}
trivially intertwines the

\rho
-actions. By the computation in Lemma
35
,
\Phi_{t}
conjugates the

\lambda
-operators attached to
\theta
to those attached to
\theta^{\prime}
. Therefore

\Phi_{t}
is an isomorphism of extensions. Hence cocycles differing by an element of

B^{1}(Q,W)
define equivalent extensions.

Step 5: the two constructions are inverse.

Start with an extension

0\to W\to V\to Q\to 0

and choose a
\rho
-equivariant splitting
s
. The associated cocycle
\theta
records the
off-diagonal block of the
\lambda
-operators with respect to the decomposition

V\simeq W\oplus Q
induced by
s
. Reconstructing the extension from
\theta
yields exactly
the same block matrices for
\lambda
and
\rho
, and therefore an equivalent extension.

Conversely, start with
\theta\in Z^{1}(Q,W)
. In the extension

0\to W\to V_{\theta}\to Q\to 0,

the obvious section

s_{0}:Q\to V_{\theta},\qquad s_{0}(q)=(0,q),

is
\rho
-equivariant, and the off-diagonal block of
\lambda_{x}
with respect to the resulting
decomposition
V_{\theta}=W\oplus s_{0}(Q)
is precisely
\theta_{x}
. Thus the cocycle recovered from
this extension is exactly
\theta
.

Hence the two constructions are inverse to each other, and we obtain a bijection

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)\cong Z^{1}(Q,W)/B^{1}(Q,W).

Step 6: characterization of split extensions.

Suppose first that the extension corresponding to
\theta
is split in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Then there
exists a splitting which is both
\rho
-equivariant and
\lambda
-equivariant. With respect to
such a splitting, all off-diagonal blocks vanish, so the corresponding cocycle is zero. Hence

[\theta]=0
in
Z^{1}(Q,W)/B^{1}(Q,W)
.

Conversely, assume that
[\theta]=0
. Then
\theta=\delta t
for some
t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\rho}(Q,W)
.
By Lemma
35
, replacing the chosen
\rho
-equivariant splitting
s
by

s^{\prime}=s-\iota t

changes the cocycle from
\theta
to

\theta-\delta t=0.

Hence the new splitting is both
\rho
-equivariant and
\lambda
-equivariant, so the extension
splits in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
.

Therefore the zero class in
Z^{1}(Q,W)/B^{1}(Q,W)
corresponds exactly to split short exact
sequences.
∎

Remark 37
.

Theorem
36
is the precise form of the failure of Maschke semisimplicity in the
representation theory of generalized digroups. Classical Maschke theory annihilates the obstruction
on the group side, but the off-diagonal
\lambda
-data survives and is measured by an explicit
cohomology class.

5
Explicit nonsplit extensions

The abstract cohomological description obtained in the previous section becomes more meaningful
once one sees it in a concrete example. The purpose of this section is therefore to construct an
explicit nonsplit extension in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
, showing that the category need not be semisimple even
when the group side lies in a classical Maschke regime. This example also makes the cocycle
description of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
completely tangible.

5.1
The underlying generalized digroup

Let

G=C_{2}=\{1,s\},\qquad E=\{e_{0},e_{1}\},

and let
G
act trivially on
E
, that is,

g\bullet e_{i}=e_{i}\qquad(g\in G,\ i\in\{0,1\}).

By Construction
7
and Proposition
8
, the set

D:=G\times E

is a generalized digroup with operations

(g,e_{i})\vdash(h,e_{j})=(gh,e_{j}),\qquad(g,e_{i})\dashv(h,e_{j})=(gh,e_{i}),

(18)

and halo

\overline{E}=\{(1,e_{0}),(1,e_{1})\}.

Since
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, the standing hypothesis of
Section
4
is satisfied.

5.2
A two-dimensional representation

Let
V=\mathbb{K}^{2}
, with ordered basis

v_{1}=\binom{1}{0},\qquad v_{2}=\binom{0}{1}.

Let

\chi:G\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}

be the nontrivial character of
C_{2}
, given by

\chi(1)=1,\qquad\chi(s)=-1.

Define two matrices

P_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad P_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 38
.

The matrices
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
satisfy

P_{i}P_{j}=P_{i}\qquad(i,j\in\{0,1\}).

In particular,

P_{0}^{2}=P_{0},\qquad P_{1}^{2}=P_{1},\qquad P_{0}P_{1}=P_{0},\qquad P_{1}P_{0}=P_{1}.

Proof.

A direct matrix computation gives

P_{0}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=P_{0},

and

P_{1}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=P_{1}.

Similarly,

P_{0}P_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=P_{0},

and

P_{1}P_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=P_{1}.

Hence
P_{i}P_{j}=P_{i}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
.
∎

We now define maps

\rho:D\to\operatorname{Aut}(V),\qquad\lambda:D\to\operatorname{End}(V),

by

\rho_{(g,e_{i})}:=\chi(g)\,I_{2},

(19)

and

\lambda_{(g,e_{0})}:=\chi(g)\,P_{0},\qquad\lambda_{(g,e_{1})}:=\chi(g)\,P_{1}.

(20)

Proposition 39
.

The triple
(V,\lambda,\rho)
is a representation of the generalized digroup
D
.

Proof.

We verify the five axioms of Definition
10
.

Let

x=(g,e_{i}),\qquad y=(h,e_{j})

be arbitrary elements of
D
, with
i,j\in\{0,1\}
.

Verification of
(R1)
.

Since the action of
G
on
E
is trivial, (
18
) gives

x\dashv y=(gh,e_{i}).

Therefore

\lambda_{x\dashv y}=\lambda_{(gh,e_{i})}=\chi(gh)\,P_{i}.

On the other hand,

\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}=(\chi(g)P_{i})(\chi(h)P_{j})=\chi(g)\chi(h)\,P_{i}P_{j}.

By Lemma
38
,

P_{i}P_{j}=P_{i},

hence

\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}=\chi(gh)\,P_{i}=\lambda_{x\dashv y}.

Verification of
(R2)
.

Again by (
18
),

x\vdash y=(gh,e_{j}).

Thus

\rho_{x\vdash y}=\rho_{(gh,e_{j})}=\chi(gh)\,I_{2}.

Since
\chi
is a group character,

\chi(gh)=\chi(g)\chi(h),

and therefore

\rho_{x}\rho_{y}=(\chi(g)I_{2})(\chi(h)I_{2})=\chi(g)\chi(h)\,I_{2}=\chi(gh)\,I_{2}=\rho_{x\vdash y}.

Verification of
(R3)
.

Let
(1,e_{i})\in\overline{E}
. Then

\rho_{(1,e_{i})}=\chi(1)\,I_{2}=I_{2}=\mathrm{id}_{V}.

Verification of
(R4)
.

Since
x\vdash y=(gh,e_{j})
, we have

\lambda_{x\vdash y}=\chi(gh)\,P_{j}.

On the other hand,

\rho_{x}\lambda_{y}=(\chi(g)I_{2})(\chi(h)P_{j})=\chi(gh)\,P_{j}=\lambda_{x\vdash y}.

Verification of
(R5)
.

Since
x\dashv y=(gh,e_{i})
, we have

\lambda_{x\dashv y}=\chi(gh)\,P_{i}.

On the other hand,

\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}=(\chi(g)P_{i})(\chi(h)I_{2})=\chi(gh)\,P_{i}=\lambda_{x\dashv y}.

All five axioms are satisfied. Hence
(V,\lambda,\rho)
is a representation of
D
.
∎

5.3
A short exact sequence

Consider the one-dimensional subspace

W:=\operatorname{span}\{v_{2}\}\subseteq V.

Proposition 40
.

The subspace
W
is a subrepresentation of
V
.

Proof.

Let
x=(g,e_{i})\in D
. Since
\rho_{x}=\chi(g)I_{2}
, one has

\rho_{x}(v_{2})=\chi(g)v_{2}\in W.

It remains to check stability under
\lambda_{x}
. If
i=0
, then

\lambda_{(g,e_{0})}(v_{2})=\chi(g)P_{0}\binom{0}{1}=\chi(g)\binom{0}{0}=0\in W.

If
i=1
, then

\lambda_{(g,e_{1})}(v_{2})=\chi(g)P_{1}\binom{0}{1}=\chi(g)\binom{0}{0}=0\in W.

Thus
W
is stable under all
\lambda_{x}
and all
\rho_{x}
, so
W
is a subrepresentation.
∎

Let

Q:=V/W

and denote by

\overline{v}_{1}:=v_{1}+W

the class of
v_{1}
modulo
W
. Then
Q
is one-dimensional, generated by
\overline{v}_{1}
,
and we have a short exact sequence in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
:

0\longrightarrow W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Q\longrightarrow 0.

(21)

5.4
The extension is nonsplit

We now show that (
21
) does not split in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
.

Proposition 41
.

The short exact sequence (
21
) is nonsplit in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
.

Proof.

Suppose, for contradiction, that (
21
) splits in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Then there exists
a one-dimensional subrepresentation
U\subseteq V
such that

V=W\oplus U.

Since
W=\operatorname{span}\{v_{2}\}
, every one-dimensional complement
U
of
W
is of the form

U=\operatorname{span}\{u\},\qquad u=v_{1}+av_{2}

for a unique scalar
a\in\mathbb{K}
.

Because
U
is a subrepresentation, it must be stable under
\lambda_{(1,e_{1})}
. Now

\lambda_{(1,e_{1})}u=P_{1}\binom{1}{a}=\binom{1}{1}.

Hence
\lambda_{(1,e_{1})}u\in U
, so there exists
c\in\mathbb{K}
such that

\binom{1}{1}=c\binom{1}{a}.

Comparing the first coordinates gives
c=1
, and therefore the second coordinates give

a=1.

Thus necessarily

U=\operatorname{span}\{v_{1}+v_{2}\}.

We now impose stability under
\lambda_{(1,e_{0})}
. Since

\lambda_{(1,e_{0})}(v_{1}+v_{2})=P_{0}\binom{1}{1}=\binom{1}{0}=v_{1},

stability would require
v_{1}\in U
. But
U=\operatorname{span}\{v_{1}+v_{2}\}
, and
v_{1}
is not a scalar
multiple of
v_{1}+v_{2}
. This is impossible.

The contradiction shows that no
D
-stable complement of
W
exists. Therefore
(
21
) is nonsplit in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
.
∎

5.5
The corresponding class in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}

We finally identify the cocycle associated with the extension (
21
).

Fix the linear section

s:Q\to V,\qquad s(\overline{v}_{1})=v_{1}.

Since
\rho_{(g,e_{i})}=\chi(g)I_{2}
is scalar, this section is automatically
\rho
-equivariant.
Thus Theorem
36
applies.

With respect to the decomposition

V=W\oplus s(Q)=\operatorname{span}\{v_{2}\}\oplus\operatorname{span}\{v_{1}\},

the induced actions on
W
and
Q
are as follows.

Lemma 42
.

For every
g\in G
and
i\in\{0,1\}
,

\rho_{(g,e_{i})}^{W}=\chi(g)\mathrm{id}_{W},\qquad\rho_{(g,e_{i})}^{Q}=\chi(g)\mathrm{id}_{Q},

\lambda_{(g,e_{i})}^{W}=0,\qquad\lambda_{(g,e_{i})}^{Q}=\chi(g)\mathrm{id}_{Q}.

Proof.

The formulas for
\rho^{W}
and
\rho^{Q}
are immediate from the fact that
\rho_{(g,e_{i})}

is the scalar matrix
\chi(g)I_{2}
.

For
\lambda^{W}
, we have already seen in the proof of Proposition
40

that
\lambda_{(g,e_{i})}(v_{2})=0
. Hence

\lambda_{(g,e_{i})}^{W}=0.

For
\lambda^{Q}
, we compute modulo
W
. If
i=0
, then

\lambda_{(g,e_{0})}(v_{1})=\chi(g)P_{0}\binom{1}{0}=\chi(g)\binom{1}{0},

so

\lambda_{(g,e_{0})}^{Q}(\overline{v}_{1})=\chi(g)\overline{v}_{1}.

If
i=1
, then

\lambda_{(g,e_{1})}(v_{1})=\chi(g)P_{1}\binom{1}{0}=\chi(g)\binom{1}{1},

and since
\binom{1}{1}\equiv\binom{1}{0}\pmod{W}
, it follows that

\lambda_{(g,e_{1})}^{Q}(\overline{v}_{1})=\chi(g)\overline{v}_{1}.

Thus, in both cases,

\lambda_{(g,e_{i})}^{Q}=\chi(g)\mathrm{id}_{Q}.

∎

Let

\beta=\{\beta_{x}\}_{x\in D}

be the off-diagonal family attached to the splitting
s
. Thus

\lambda_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{x}^{W}&\beta_{x}\\
0&\lambda_{x}^{Q}\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the decomposition
V=W\oplus s(Q)
.

Proposition 43
.

The family
\beta
is given by

\beta_{(g,e_{0})}=0,\qquad\beta_{(g,e_{1})}(\overline{v}_{1})=\chi(g)v_{2}.

In particular,

\beta_{(1,e_{1})}(\overline{v}_{1})=v_{2}\neq 0.

Hence the class of (
21
) in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)
is nonzero.

Proof.

By definition,
\beta_{x}
is the
W
-component of
\lambda_{x}(s(\overline{v}_{1}))
.

If
x=(g,e_{0})
, then

\lambda_{(g,e_{0})}(v_{1})=\chi(g)P_{0}\binom{1}{0}=\chi(g)\binom{1}{0}.

This vector lies entirely in
s(Q)=\operatorname{span}\{v_{1}\}
, so its
W
-component is zero. Hence

\beta_{(g,e_{0})}=0.

If
x=(g,e_{1})
, then

\lambda_{(g,e_{1})}(v_{1})=\chi(g)P_{1}\binom{1}{0}=\chi(g)\binom{1}{1}=\chi(g)v_{1}+\chi(g)v_{2}.

Thus the
W
-component is
\chi(g)v_{2}
, so

\beta_{(g,e_{1})}(\overline{v}_{1})=\chi(g)v_{2}.

In particular,

\beta_{(1,e_{1})}(\overline{v}_{1})=v_{2}\neq 0.

By Theorem
36
, the extension class of (
21
) corresponds to
the class of
\beta
in
Z^{1}(Q,W)/B^{1}(Q,W)
. If this class were zero, the extension would split
in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. This contradicts Proposition
41
. Therefore the class is
nonzero.
∎

Remark 44
.

Proposition
43
gives a completely explicit obstruction to Maschke
semisimplicity in the category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. The
\rho
-side splits, as predicted by
Section
4
, but the off-diagonal
\lambda
-data survives and defines a nontrivial
class in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)
.

6
A spectral sequence for generalized digroups

Having obtained an explicit cocycle model for first extensions, we now move to a more global
cohomological viewpoint. The purpose of this section is to separate the group-theoretic and
halo-theoretic contributions to extension theory by means of a spectral sequence. This provides a
conceptual explanation for the phenomenon already visible in the previous sections: the group side
may become homologically trivial under a Maschke hypothesis, while the halo side continues to
support nontrivial extension classes.

Throughout this section, fix a structural presentation

D\simeq G\times E

as in Theorem
5
. We write the operations as

(g,\alpha)\vdash(h,\beta)=(gh,\,g\bullet\beta),\qquad(g,\alpha)\dashv(h,\beta)=(gh,\,\alpha).

6.1
The halo algebra

We first isolate the algebra generated by the halo.

Definition 45
.

Let
B_{E}
be the unital associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra generated by symbols

\{\varepsilon_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in E\}

subject to the defining relations

\varepsilon_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\beta}=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\qquad(\alpha,\beta\in E).

(22)

Remark 46
.

Since
B_{E}
is defined as a quotient of a free associative algebra, associativity is automatic.
Relation (
22
) implies that every
\varepsilon_{\alpha}
is idempotent and that the
product of two generators remembers only the left factor. Thus
B_{E}
algebraically records the
left-zero behaviour of the halo.

The action of
G
on
E
induces a family of algebra automorphisms of
B_{E}
.

Lemma 47
.

For every
g\in G
, the assignment

\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{\alpha}):=\varepsilon_{g\bullet\alpha}\qquad(\alpha\in E)

extends uniquely to an algebra automorphism of
B_{E}
. Moreover,

\tau_{gh}=\tau_{g}\circ\tau_{h}\qquad(g,h\in G).

Proof.

Since
B_{E}
is defined by generators and relations, it is enough to verify that the assignment on
generators preserves the defining relations. For
\alpha,\beta\in E
,

\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\beta})=\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{\alpha})=\varepsilon_{g\bullet\alpha},

whereas

\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{\alpha})\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{\beta})=\varepsilon_{g\bullet\alpha}\,\varepsilon_{g\bullet\beta}=\varepsilon_{g\bullet\alpha}.

Hence
\tau_{g}
extends uniquely to an algebra endomorphism of
B_{E}
.

It is invertible, with inverse
\tau_{g^{-1}}
, because

\tau_{g^{-1}}(\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{\alpha}))=\tau_{g^{-1}}(\varepsilon_{g\bullet\alpha})=\varepsilon_{g^{-1}\bullet(g\bullet\alpha)}=\varepsilon_{\alpha}.

Finally, for
\alpha\in E
,

(\tau_{g}\circ\tau_{h})(\varepsilon_{\alpha})=\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{h\bullet\alpha})=\varepsilon_{g\bullet(h\bullet\alpha)}=\varepsilon_{(gh)\bullet\alpha}=\tau_{gh}(\varepsilon_{\alpha}),

because
\bullet
is a left action of
G
on
E
.
∎

6.2
The auxiliary semilinear category

We now introduce the auxiliary category that isolates the halo module structure together with the
genuine action of the group component.

Definition 48
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
be the category whose objects are pairs

(M,t),

where
M
is a left
B_{E}
-module and

t_{g}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}(M)\qquad(g\in G)

is a family satisfying

\displaystyle t_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}_{M},

(23)

\displaystyle t_{g}t_{h}

\displaystyle=t_{gh},

(24)

\displaystyle t_{g}(bm)

\displaystyle=\tau_{g}(b)\,t_{g}(m)\qquad(b\in B_{E},\ m\in M).

(25)

A morphism

f:(M,t)\longrightarrow(N,s)

in
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
is a
B_{E}
-linear map
f:M\to N
such that

ft_{g}=s_{g}f\qquad(g\in G).

We now relate
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
to
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
.

Proposition 49
.

Let
(V,\lambda,\rho)\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Define a left
B_{E}
-module structure on
V
by

\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot v:=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}(v)\qquad(\alpha\in E,\ v\in V),

and define

t_{g}:=\rho_{g}\qquad(g\in G).

Then
(V,t)
is an object of
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
.

Proof.

First,

\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}\lambda_{(1,\beta)}=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)\dashv(1,\beta)}=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)},

so the assignment
\varepsilon_{\alpha}\mapsto\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}
defines a left

B_{E}
-module structure.

Next, by Proposition
26
, one has

t_{1}=\rho_{1}=\mathrm{id}_{V}\qquad\text{and}\qquad t_{g}t_{h}=\rho_{g}\rho_{h}=\rho_{gh}=t_{gh}.

Hence (
23
) and (
24
) hold.

It remains to prove (
25
). Let
\alpha\in E
and
v\in V
. By axiom
(R4)
,

\rho_{g}\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}=\lambda_{(g,g\bullet\alpha)}.

Using Remark
27
, we rewrite the right-hand side as

\lambda_{(g,g\bullet\alpha)}=\lambda_{(1,g\bullet\alpha)}\rho_{g}.

Hence

\rho_{g}\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}=\lambda_{(1,g\bullet\alpha)}\rho_{g}.

Equivalently,

t_{g}(\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot v)=\varepsilon_{g\bullet\alpha}\cdot t_{g}(v)=\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{\alpha})\cdot t_{g}(v).

This proves (
25
).
∎

Proposition 50
.

Let
(M,t)\in\mathcal{C}(E,G)
. Define maps

\rho:D=G\times E\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{K}}(M),\qquad\lambda:D=G\times E\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(M)

by

\rho_{(g,\alpha)}:=t_{g},\qquad\lambda_{(g,\alpha)}(m):=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot t_{g}(m).

Then
(M,\lambda,\rho)
is a representation of
D
. Moreover, this construction is inverse
to that of Proposition
49
. Consequently, the categories

\mathsf{Rep}(D)
and
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
are equivalent.

Proof.

We verify the five axioms of Definition
10
.

Let

x=(g,\alpha),\qquad y=(h,\beta)

be elements of
D=G\times E
, and let
m\in M
.

Verification of
(R1)
.

Since

x\dashv y=(gh,\alpha),

we have

\lambda_{x\dashv y}(m)=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot t_{gh}(m).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(\lambda_{y}(m))

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}(\varepsilon_{\beta}\cdot t_{h}(m))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot t_{g}(\varepsilon_{\beta}\cdot t_{h}(m))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{\beta})\,t_{g}t_{h}(m)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot\varepsilon_{g\bullet\beta}\,t_{gh}(m)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot t_{gh}(m),

because
\varepsilon_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{g\bullet\beta}=\varepsilon_{\alpha}
. Therefore

\lambda_{x\dashv y}=\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}.

Verification of
(R2)
.

Since

x\vdash y=(gh,g\bullet\beta),

we have

\rho_{x\vdash y}=t_{gh}=t_{g}t_{h}=\rho_{x}\rho_{y}.

Verification of
(R3)
.

If
e=(1,\alpha)\in\overline{E}
, then

\rho_{e}=t_{1}=\mathrm{id}_{M}.

Verification of
(R4)
.

Using (
25
), we compute

\displaystyle\rho_{x}(\lambda_{y}(m))

\displaystyle=t_{g}(\varepsilon_{\beta}\cdot t_{h}(m))

\displaystyle=\tau_{g}(\varepsilon_{\beta})\,t_{g}t_{h}(m)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{g\bullet\beta}\cdot t_{gh}(m)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{(gh,g\bullet\beta)}(m)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\vdash y}(m).

Verification of
(R5)
.

We compute

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(\rho_{y}(m))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot t_{g}(t_{h}(m))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot t_{gh}(m)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{(gh,\alpha)}(m)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x\dashv y}(m).

Thus
(M,\lambda,\rho)
is a representation of
D
.

We now verify that this construction is inverse to that of
Proposition
49
.

Start with
(V,\lambda,\rho)\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Proposition
49
defines
the
B_{E}
-action by

\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot v=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}(v)

and
t_{g}=\rho_{g}
. Applying the present construction then gives

\rho^{\prime}_{(g,\alpha)}=t_{g}=\rho_{g}=\rho_{(g,\alpha)},

because
\rho_{(g,\alpha)}
depends only on
g
, and

\lambda^{\prime}_{(g,\alpha)}(v)=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot t_{g}(v)=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}\rho_{g}(v)=\lambda_{(g,\alpha)}(v)

by Remark
27
.

Conversely, start with
(M,t)\in\mathcal{C}(E,G)
. The present construction defines

\rho_{(g,\alpha)}=t_{g},\qquad\lambda_{(g,\alpha)}(m)=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot t_{g}(m).

Applying Proposition
49
, the induced
B_{E}
-action is

\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot m=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}(m)=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot t_{1}(m)=\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot m,

so it is the original one, and the induced operators are

t^{\prime}_{g}=\rho_{g}=t_{g}.

Thus both constructions are mutually inverse on objects, and the same is immediate for morphisms.

Therefore
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
and
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
are equivalent categories.
∎

6.3
The
G
-action on
\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,W)

Let
Q,W\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. By Propositions
49

and
50
, we regard them as objects

(Q,t^{Q}),\qquad(W,t^{W})

of
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
. In particular,
Q
and
W
are left
B_{E}
-modules, where

\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot q=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}^{Q}(q),\qquad\varepsilon_{\alpha}\cdot w=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}^{W}(w),

and they are endowed with genuine representations

t_{g}^{Q}=\rho_{g}^{Q},\qquad t_{g}^{W}=\rho_{g}^{W}\qquad(g\in G),

satisfying

t_{g}^{Q}t_{h}^{Q}=t_{gh}^{Q},\qquad t_{g}^{W}t_{h}^{W}=t_{gh}^{W}.

Definition 51
.

For
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,W)
and
g\in G
, define

(g\cdot f)(q):=t_{g}^{W}\bigl(f((t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(q))\bigr)\qquad(q\in Q).

(26)

Lemma 52
.

Formula (
26
) defines a left action of
G
on
\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,W)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,W)
,
g,h\in G
, and
q\in Q
. Then

\displaystyle\bigl(g\cdot(h\cdot f)\bigr)(q)

\displaystyle=t_{g}^{W}\Bigl((h\cdot f)\bigl((t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(q)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=t_{g}^{W}t_{h}^{W}\Bigl(f\bigl((t_{h}^{Q})^{-1}(t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(q)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=t_{gh}^{W}\Bigl(f\bigl((t_{gh}^{Q})^{-1}(q)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=((gh)\cdot f)(q).

Also,

(1\cdot f)(q)=t_{1}^{W}(f((t_{1}^{Q})^{-1}(q)))=f(q),

so
1\cdot f=f
.

It remains to check that
g\cdot f
is again
B_{E}
-linear. Let
b\in B_{E}
and
q\in Q
.
Since
t_{g}^{Q}
is semilinear with twisting
\tau_{g}
, its inverse is semilinear with twisting

\tau_{g^{-1}}
. Therefore

(t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(bq)=\tau_{g^{-1}}(b)\,(t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(q).

Hence

\displaystyle(g\cdot f)(bq)

\displaystyle=t_{g}^{W}\Bigl(f\bigl((t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(bq)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=t_{g}^{W}\Bigl(f\bigl(\tau_{g^{-1}}(b)\,(t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(q)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=t_{g}^{W}\Bigl(\tau_{g^{-1}}(b)\,f((t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(q))\Bigr)

\displaystyle=b\,t_{g}^{W}\Bigl(f((t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(q))\Bigr)

\displaystyle=b\,(g\cdot f)(q).

Hence
g\cdot f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,W)
.
∎

Lemma 53
.

One has

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)=\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,W)^{G}.

Proof.

Assume first that
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)
. Then
f
commutes with every

\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}
, so
f
is
B_{E}
-linear. It also commutes with every
\rho_{g}
, hence
with every
t_{g}
. Therefore

(g\cdot f)(q)=t_{g}^{W}\bigl(f((t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}(q))\bigr)=t_{g}^{W}\bigl((t_{g}^{W})^{-1}(f(q))\bigr)=f(q),

so
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,W)^{G}
.

Conversely, let
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,W)^{G}
. The invariance condition means that

t_{g}^{W}f(t_{g}^{Q})^{-1}=f\qquad(g\in G).

Equivalently,

t_{g}^{W}f=ft_{g}^{Q}\qquad(g\in G).

Since
t_{g}=\rho_{g}
, this shows that
f
commutes with all
\rho_{g}
, hence with all

\rho_{x}
, because
\rho_{x}
depends only on the group component by
Proposition
26
. It also commutes with all

\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}
, because it is
B_{E}
-linear. Finally, by
Remark
27
,

\lambda_{(g,\alpha)}=\lambda_{(1,\alpha)}\rho_{g}.

Hence
f
commutes with every
\lambda_{(g,\alpha)}
as well. Therefore

f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)
.
∎

6.4
The halo functor and the spectral sequence

Fix
Q\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. Define

\mathcal{F}(-):=\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,-),

where the target is the category of left
G
-modules endowed with the action of
Definition
51
.

Lemma 54
.

The functor
\mathcal{F}:\mathsf{Rep}(D)\to\mathbb{K}G\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is left exact.

Proof.

The functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,-)
is left exact on the underlying category of
B_{E}
-modules, and the

G
-action defined in (
26
) is functorial. Hence
\mathcal{F}
is left exact.
∎

Proposition 55
.

Assume that
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
. Then, for every injective
object
I\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
, the
G
-module

\mathcal{F}(I)=\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,I)

is
(-)^{G}
-acyclic.

Proof.

Since
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, the group algebra
\mathbb{K}G
is
semisimple. Hence

H^{p}(G,M)=0\qquad(p>0)

for every left
\mathbb{K}G
-module
M
. Therefore every
G
-module is
(-)^{G}
-acyclic.
In particular,
\mathcal{F}(I)
is
(-)^{G}
-acyclic for every injective

I\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
.
∎

Theorem 56
.

Assume that
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
.
Let
D
be a generalized digroup and let
Q,W\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
.
Then there exists a first-quadrant spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}\cong H^{p}\!\bigl(G,R^{q}\mathcal{F}(W)\bigr)\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{p+q}(Q,W),

where
\mathcal{F}(W)=\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,W)
is endowed with the
G
-action of
Definition
51
.

Proof.

By Lemma
53
, the composite of
\mathcal{F}
with the invariants functor

(-)^{G}:\mathbb{K}G\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{K}}

is precisely

W\longmapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W).

The invariants functor
(-)^{G}
is left exact, and its right derived functors are the cohomology functors
H^{p}(G,-)
; see Brown, Chapter III, especially the preliminary discussion. By proposition
55
, the Grothendieck spectral sequence (
[
6
, Theorem 10.47]
) applies to
the composite
(-)^{G}\circ\mathcal{F}
, and yields

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}\!\bigl(G,R^{q}\mathcal{F}(W)\bigr)\Longrightarrow R^{p+q}\!\bigl((-)^{G}\circ\mathcal{F}\bigr)(W).

Since
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is abelian with enough injectives by
Corollaries
21
and
23
, the right derived functors of

W\longmapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}(Q,W)

are precisely
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{p+q}(Q,W)
. Therefore

E_{2}^{p,q}\cong H^{p}\!\bigl(G,R^{q}\mathcal{F}(W)\bigr)\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{p+q}(Q,W).

∎

Proposition 57
.

The forgetful functor

U:\mathsf{Rep}(D)\longrightarrow B_{E}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},

obtained via Propositions
49

and
50
, sends injective objects of
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
to injective

B_{E}
-modules.

Proof.

By Propositions
49
and
50
, the category

\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
. It is therefore enough to prove the claim for
the forgetful functor

U:\mathcal{C}(E,G)\longrightarrow B_{E}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}.

Define a functor

L:B_{E}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}(E,G)

as follows. For a left
B_{E}
-module
M
, let

L(M):=\bigoplus_{g\in G}M_{g},

where each
M_{g}
is a copy of
M
as a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space, endowed with the twisted

B_{E}
-action

b\cdot m_{g}:=\bigl(\tau_{g^{-1}}(b)m\bigr)_{g}\qquad(b\in B_{E},\ m\in M).

Define operators

t_{h}:L(M)\to L(M)\qquad(h\in G)

on homogeneous elements by

t_{h}(m_{g}):=m_{hg}.

We check that
L(M)
is an object of
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
. Clearly
t_{1}=\mathrm{id}
and

t_{h}t_{k}=t_{hk}
. Moreover,

t_{h}(b\cdot m_{g})=t_{h}\bigl((\tau_{g^{-1}}(b)m)_{g}\bigr)=(\tau_{g^{-1}}(b)m)_{hg},

while

\tau_{h}(b)\cdot t_{h}(m_{g})=\tau_{h}(b)\cdot m_{hg}=\bigl(\tau_{(hg)^{-1}}(\tau_{h}(b))m\bigr)_{hg}=(\tau_{g^{-1}}(b)m)_{hg},

so the semilinearity condition holds.

We now show that
L
is left adjoint to
U
. Let
M\in B_{E}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
and

(N,t)\in\mathcal{C}(E,G)
. Given a morphism

\Phi:L(M)\to(N,t)

in
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
, define

f_{\Phi}:M\to U(N),\qquad f_{\Phi}(m):=\Phi(m_{1}).

Since
\Phi
is
B_{E}
-linear,
f_{\Phi}
is
B_{E}
-linear.

Conversely, given a
B_{E}
-linear map

f:M\to U(N),

define

\widetilde{f}:L(M)\to N

on homogeneous elements by

\widetilde{f}(m_{g}):=t_{g}(f(m)).

This map is
B_{E}
-linear, because

\widetilde{f}(b\cdot m_{g})=t_{g}\bigl(f(\tau_{g^{-1}}(b)m)\bigr)=t_{g}\bigl(\tau_{g^{-1}}(b)f(m)\bigr)=b\,t_{g}(f(m))=b\,\widetilde{f}(m_{g}),

and it is
G
-equivariant by construction. The assignments

\Phi\longmapsto f_{\Phi},\qquad f\longmapsto\widetilde{f}

are inverse to one another, so

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}(E,G)}(L(M),(N,t))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(M,U(N))

naturally.

Hence
L
is left adjoint to
U
. Finally,
L
is exact: twisting a module structure by an algebra
automorphism is exact, and direct sums are exact in module categories. Therefore
U
, being a
right adjoint to an exact functor, preserves injective objects.
∎

Lemma 58
.

The forgetful functor

U:\mathcal{C}(E,G)\longrightarrow B_{E}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}

is exact. Consequently, via the equivalence

\mathsf{Rep}(D)\simeq\mathcal{C}(E,G)
, the functor

U:\mathsf{Rep}(D)\longrightarrow B_{E}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}

is exact.

Proof.

Kernels and cokernels in
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
are computed on the underlying

B_{E}
-modules, endowed with the induced
G
-action by restriction and passage to
quotients. Therefore a sequence in
\mathcal{C}(E,G)
is exact if and only if it is
exact after forgetting the
G
-action. Hence
U
is exact.
∎

The next proposition identifies the
q
-direction with genuine halo cohomology
by using the fact that the forgetful functor preserves injective objects.

Proposition 59
.

For every
q\geq 0
,

R^{q}\mathcal{F}(W)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{q}(Q,W)

as left
G
-modules. Consequently, the spectral sequence of
Theorem
56
takes the form

E_{2}^{p,q}\cong H^{p}\!\bigl(G,\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{q}(Q,W)\bigr)\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{p+q}(Q,W).

Proof.

Let

0\to W\to I^{0}\to I^{1}\to\cdots

be an injective resolution in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
. By Lemma
58
and Proposition
57
,
the complex
U(I^{\bullet})
is an injective resolution of
W
in

B_{E}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. Hence the cohomology of the complex

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,U(I^{0}))\to\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,U(I^{1}))\to\cdots

computes
\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{q}(Q,W)
. Since
U
does not change the underlying

B_{E}
-module, this is exactly the complex

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,I^{0})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{B_{E}}(Q,I^{1})\to\cdots

computing
R^{q}\mathcal{F}(W)
. The
G
-action on the complex is the one from
Definition
51
, so the resulting identification is

G
-equivariant.
∎

6.5
Corollaries

We now extract the consequences most relevant for Maschke-type splitting.

Corollary 60

(Collapse in the Maschke regime)
.

Assume that
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
. Then the spectral sequence collapses at the

E_{2}
-page, and for every
n\geq 0
,

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{n}(Q,W)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{n}(Q,W)^{G}.

Proof.

Since
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, the group algebra
\mathbb{K}[G]
is semisimple.
Hence

H^{p}(G,M)=0\qquad(p>0)

for every left
\mathbb{K}G
-module
M
. In particular, Proposition
55

applies. Moreover, by Proposition
59
, the
E_{2}
-page of the spectral
sequence is

E_{2}^{p,q}\cong H^{p}\!\bigl(G,\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{q}(Q,W)\bigr).

Since
H^{p}(G,-)=0
for every
p>0
, all terms with
p>0
vanish. Therefore the spectral
sequence collapses at the
E_{2}
-page, and for every
n\geq 0
,

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{n}(Q,W)\cong E_{2}^{0,n}=H^{0}\!\bigl(G,\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{n}(Q,W)\bigr)=\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{n}(Q,W)^{G}.

∎

Corollary 61

(Splitting criterion)
.

Assume that
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
. If

\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{1}(Q,W)^{G}=0,

then every short exact sequence

0\to W\to V\to Q\to 0

in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
splits.

Proof.

By Corollary
60
,

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{1}(Q,W)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{1}(Q,W)^{G}.

Hence the hypothesis implies
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{1}(Q,W)=0
. In any abelian category, this is
equivalent to the splitting of every short exact sequence

0\to W\to V\to Q\to 0.

∎

Corollary 62

(Criterion for non-semisimplicity)
.

Assume that
G
is finite and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
. If there exist objects
Q,W\in\mathsf{Rep}(D)
such that

\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{1}(Q,W)^{G}\neq 0,

then the category
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is not semisimple.

Proof.

Again by Corollary
60
,

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{1}(Q,W)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{B_{E}}^{1}(Q,W)^{G}.

If the latter is nonzero, then
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathsf{Rep}(D)}^{1}(Q,W)\neq 0
. Therefore there exists a nontrivial
extension of
Q
by
W
in
\mathsf{Rep}(D)
, and such an extension cannot split. Hence

\mathsf{Rep}(D)
is not semisimple.
∎

Remark 63
.

Theorem
56
and its corollaries isolate the precise mechanism behind the failure of
a full Maschke theorem for generalized digroups. The group side controls the invariant functor and
therefore the
p
-direction of the spectral sequence, while the halo algebra controls the

q
-direction. The obstruction to semisimplicity is therefore genuinely two-layered.
