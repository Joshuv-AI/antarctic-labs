Towards the Erdős–Kleitman Problem: from Erdős matching conjecture perspective
Cheng Chi, Yan Wang

Abstract

Introduction

Let
[n]\coloneqq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
For a set
X
and an integer
k
, write
\binom{X}{k}
for the family of
all
k
-element subsets of
X
, and write
\binom{X}{\geq k}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\geq k}\binom{X}{i}
.
For an integer
n
, we also write
\binom{n}{\geq k}\coloneqq\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}
.
A
matching
in a family is a collection of pairwise disjoint sets. An
s
-matching
is a matching of size
s
. For a family
\mathcal{F}
, its
matching number
is the size of a largest matching in it.

Extremal problems for matching numbers in set systems go back to the well-known Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem
[
5
]
.
In the uniform setting, one asks for the largest size of a
k
-uniform family with bounded matching number.
For integers
N,k,t
with
N\geq kt
, define

e_{k}(N,t)\coloneqq\max\left\{|\mathcal{G}|:\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[N]}{k}\text{ and }\nu(\mathcal{G})<t\right\}.

The Erdős matching conjecture (EMC, for short) asserts that

e_{k}(N,t)=\max\left\{\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-t+1}{k},\,\binom{kt-1}{k}\right\}.

Erdős proved that for every fixed
k
and
t
, the conjecture holds for sufficiently large
N

[
3
]
.
The conjecture is known for
k=2
as the Erdős–Gallai theorem

[
4
]
.
For
k=3
, it was proved for sufficiently large
N
by Łuczak and Mieczkowska
[
17
]
and then fully solved by Frankl
[
6
]
.
Despite this substantial progress, the full conjecture remains open for every
k\geq 4
.

In this paper we study the corresponding non-uniform problem.
For integers
n\geq s\geq 2
, define

e(n,s)\coloneqq\max\{|\mathcal{F}|:\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}\text{ and }\nu(\mathcal{F})<s\}.

The study of this problem, now called the Erdős–Kleitman problem, dates back to a question of Erdős answered by Kleitman, who determined
e(sm-1,s)
and
e(sm,s)
for all
m,s\geq 1

[
12
]
.
In contrast with these two residue classes, the other values of
e(n,s)
remain unknown and may depend sensitively on
n\pmod{s}
.

Throughout the paper we fix
m\geq 3
and write
n=ms+c=(m+1)s-\ell
and
\ell=s-c
.
The connection between the Erdős–Kleitman problem and EMC is captured by the following construction.
If

\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{m}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{G})<\ell
, then
\mathcal{G}\cup\binom{[n]}{\geq m+1}
has no
s
-matching.
Indeed, any
s
-matching using
q
sets from
\mathcal{G}
has total size at least
qm+(s-q)(m+1)=(m+1)s-q
.
Since
n=(m+1)s-\ell
, this forces
q\geq\ell
, contradicting

\nu(\mathcal{G})<\ell
.

Let
L^{\prime}
be a subset of
[n]
with
|L^{\prime}|=m\ell-1
.
Applying the above construction with
\mathcal{G}=\binom{L^{\prime}}{m}
gives the
following family
P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
, that is

P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell;L^{\prime})\coloneqq\binom{L^{\prime}}{m}\cup\binom{[n]}{\geq m+1}.

(1.1)

Its size is independent of the choice of
L^{\prime}
, and we denote this value by
|P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)|
.

Nevertheless, this construction is not the only natural candidate.
When
\ell
is small, a different construction gives larger families that contain sets with size strictly less than
m
.
For
L\in\binom{[n]}{\ell-1}
, define

P(m,s,\ell;L)\coloneqq\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|+|A\cap L|\geq m+1\}.

The defining inequality immediately gives
\nu(P(m,s,\ell;L))<s
, since
for any
s
pairwise disjoint sets the sum of
|A|+|A\cap L|
is at most
n+|L|=(m+1)s-1
.
In our earlier paper
[
2
]
, we showed that, for every fixed
m\geq 3
, the only extremal families are
P(m,s,\ell;L)
when
1\leq\ell\leq\left(\frac{m+1}{2m+1}-o(1)\right)s
, which answers a question of Frankl and Kupavskii affirmatively.

The first contribution of this paper is to prove that for
m\geq 3
and sufficiently large
s
, the family
P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
is extremal when
c=s-\ell
is moderately small compared to
s
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
m\geq 3
. There exist constants
\beta_{m},\delta_{m}>0
and an integer

s_{0}=s_{0}(m)
such that the following holds for all
s\geq s_{0}
.
Let
n=ms+c,\ell=s-c
and
\beta_{m}s^{(m-1)/m}\leq c\leq\delta_{m}s
.
If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)|
.
Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}=P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
for some
L^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{m\ell-1}
.

Let
t(s)
be the root of
|P(3,s,\ell)|=|P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell)|
with
t(s)>1
, that is,

t(s)\coloneqq\frac{17-18s+\sqrt{49-852s+1284s^{2}}}{20}.

For
m=3
, Chi and Wang
[
2
]
proved that there exists
L\in\binom{[n]}{\ell-1}
and
L^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{3\ell-1}
such that
P(3,s,\ell;L)
is extremal when
1\leq\ell<t(s)
, while
P(3,s,\ell;L)
and
P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
are both extremal when
t(s)
is an integer and
\ell=t(s)
.
Moreover, from the definition of
t(s)
, one may see that
|P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell)|
dominates
|P(3,s,\ell)|
when
s\geq\ell>t(s)
.

Our second result shows that
P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
is indeed extremal for almost all admissible ranges of
\ell
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists an integer
s_{0}
such that the following holds for all

s\geq s_{0}
.
Suppose
n=4s-\ell
with
t(s)<\ell<s-1.59s^{2/3}
.
If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell)|
.
Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}=P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
for some
L^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{3\ell-1}
.

.

Definitions and lemmas

In this section we collect some lemmas and theorems used later. For a family

G
, let
\tau(G)
denote its
vertex-cover number
; we use the convention

\tau(\emptyset)=0
.

Lemma 2.1

(Baranyai
[
1
]
)
.

The complete
q
-uniform hypergraph on
qt
vertices can be decomposed
into
\binom{qt-1}{q-1}
perfect matchings.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q\geq 2
, let
G\subseteq\binom{X}{q}
, and suppose that

|X|=qt+d
, where
t\geq 1
and
d\geq 0
. Put

Z=\binom{X}{q}\setminus G
. If
\nu(G)<t
, then

|Z|\geq\max\left\{\frac{1}{t}\binom{qt+d}{q},\binom{d+q}{q}\right\}.

(2.1)

Proof
.

Choose a uniformly random
qt
-subset
U
of
X
. By
Section

2
,

\binom{U}{q}
decomposes into
\binom{qt-1}{q-1}
perfect matchings. Since

G
has no matching of size
t
, every such decomposition contains at
least one member of
Z\cap\binom{U}{q}
. Hence

\mathbb{E}|Z\cap\binom{U}{q}|\geq\binom{qt-1}{q-1}
. On the other hand,

\mathbb{E}|Z\cap\binom{U}{q}|=|Z|\frac{\binom{qt+d-q}{qt-q}}{\binom{qt+d}{qt}}.

Rearranging gives
|Z|\geq t^{-1}\binom{qt+d}{q}
. To prove

|Z|\geq\binom{d+q}{q}
, let
M
be a maximum matching in
G
. Then
|M|\leq t-1
. By maximality,
every edge of
G
meets
V(M)
. The set
X\setminus V(M)
has size at
least
d+q
, so every
q
-set inside any fixed
(d+q)
-subset of

X\setminus V(M)
belongs to
Z
. This proves (
2.1
).
∎

For
k\geq 3
, put

\eta_{k}=\frac{1}{2k^{2k+1}}.

(2.2)

Theorem 2.3
.

For every integer
k\geq 3
, there is an integer
t_{k}
such that the
following holds. If
t\geq t_{k}
,
kt\leq N<(k+\eta_{k})t
, and

\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[N]}{k}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{G})<t
, then
|\mathcal{G}|\leq\binom{kt-1}{k}
.
Moreover, if equality holds, then
\mathcal{G}=\binom{U}{k}
for some
U\in\binom{[N]}{kt-1}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
k\geq 3
. There are constants
\kappa_{k}>0
and
\gamma_{k}
such that
the following holds. Let
\gamma\geq\gamma_{k}
, let
\tau\geq 1
and

\rho\geq 0
be integers with
\gamma=\tau+\rho
, and let
W
be a set of
size
k\tau+\rho
. If
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\binom{W}{k}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{A})<\tau

and
\mathcal{B}=\binom{W}{k}\setminus\mathcal{A}
, then

|\mathcal{B}|\geq\kappa_{k}(\rho+1)\gamma^{k-1}
.

Proof
.

Put
\theta=\eta_{k}/(2+\eta_{k})
.
We choose
\gamma_{k}
sufficiently large such that
Theorem

2.3
applies whenever
\gamma\geq\gamma_{k}
.

First suppose that
\rho\leq\theta\gamma
. Since
\gamma=\tau+\rho
, this gives
\rho\leq\theta(\tau+\rho)
, and hence
\rho\leq\theta\tau/(1-\theta)={\eta_{k}}\tau/2
.
Therefore

|W|=k\tau+\rho\leq\left(k+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\right)\tau<(k+\eta_{k})\tau.

Also, since
\rho\leq\theta\gamma
, we have
\tau=\gamma-\rho\geq(1-\theta)\gamma
.
Thus, by the choice of
\gamma_{k}
,
\tau
is large enough
for
Theorem

2.3
to apply.
Applying
Theorem

2.3
to
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\binom{W}{k}
, we obtain
|\mathcal{A}|\leq\binom{k\tau-1}{k}
. Consequently,

|\mathcal{B}|\geq\binom{k\tau+\rho}{k}-\binom{k\tau-1}{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\binom{k\tau-1+i}{k-1}\geq(\rho+1)\binom{k\tau-1}{k-1}

Since
\rho\leq{\eta_{k}}\tau/2
, we have
\gamma=\tau+\rho\leq\left(1+\eta_{k}/2\right)\tau\leq(1+\eta_{k})\tau
.
Therefore,
\tau\geq\frac{\gamma}{1+\eta_{k}}
.
For every
0\leq j\leq k-2
, since
\gamma_{k}
is sufficiently large,

k\tau-1-j\geq\frac{k\gamma}{1+\eta_{k}}-(k-1)\geq\frac{k\gamma}{3(1+\eta_{k})}.

Thus

\binom{k\tau-1}{k-1}=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k-2}(k\tau-1-j)}{(k-1)!}\geq\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\left(\frac{k\gamma}{3(1+\eta_{k})}\right)^{k-1}.

Hence in this case

|\mathcal{B}|\geq\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\left(\frac{k}{3(1+\eta_{k})}\right)^{k-1}(\rho+1)\gamma^{k-1}.

Suppose instead that
\rho>\theta\gamma
. Let
M
be a maximum matching
in
\mathcal{A}
. Since
\nu(\mathcal{A})<\tau
, the matching
M
has at most

\tau-1
edges and covers at most
k(\tau-1)
vertices. Therefore at
least

|W|-k(\tau-1)=k\tau+\rho-k(\tau-1)=\rho+k

vertices remain uncovered. By maximality of
M
, no
k
-set contained in the uncovered set belongs to
\mathcal{A}
as otherwise such a
k
-set could be added to
M
, contradicting the maximality of
M
.
Hence every
k
-set contained in the uncovered set belongs to
\mathcal{B}
, and so
|\mathcal{B}|\geq\binom{\rho+k}{k}
.
Since
\rho>\theta\gamma
, we have
\rho^{k-1}>\theta^{k-1}\gamma^{k-1}
.
Also
\rho\geq 1
, because
\rho
is an integer and

\rho>\theta\gamma>0
. Hence,
\rho\geq(\rho+1)/2
.
Therefore

\binom{\rho+k}{k}=\frac{(\rho+1)(\rho+2)\cdots(\rho+k)}{k!}\geq\frac{\rho^{k}}{k!}\geq\frac{\theta^{k-1}}{2k!}(\rho+1)\gamma^{k-1}.

Thus in this case

|\mathcal{B}|\geq\frac{\theta^{k-1}}{2k!}(\rho+1)\gamma^{k-1}.

Taking
\kappa_{k}
to be the smaller of the two positive constants obtained
in the two cases proves the lemma.
∎

Comparison setup

Write
n=ms+c=(m+1)s-\ell
and
\ell=s-c
unless otherwise specified.
For a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
and
k\geq 0
, put

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{F}\cap\binom{[n]}{i},\quad\mathcal{F}_{<m}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{F}_{i},\quad H=\mathcal{F}_{m},\quad\mathcal{F}_{\geq k}=\mathcal{F}\cap\binom{[n]}{\geq k},\quad\mathcal{Y}_{i}=\binom{[n]}{i}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{i}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\geq s\geq 2
, and let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfy
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
. If

\emptyset\in\mathcal{F}
, then there is a family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
such that

|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|=|\mathcal{F}|
,
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})<s
,
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, and

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof
.

Since
n\geq s
and
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, not all singletons can belong to
\mathcal{F}
.
Choose
x\in[n]
with
\{x\}\notin\mathcal{F}
, and set

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{F}\setminus\{\emptyset\})\cup\{\{x\}\}
.
Clearly
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|=|\mathcal{F}|
,
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}\neq\emptyset
. If
an
s
-matching in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
avoids
\{x\}
, then it is already an

s
-matching in
\mathcal{F}
. If it uses
\{x\}
, replacing
\{x\}
by

\emptyset
gives an
s
-matching in
\mathcal{F}
. Thus
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})<s
.
∎

The next lemma reduces the extremal problem to paying for the lower layers and
the excess in the critical layer by missing upper-layer sets.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
m,s,c,\ell,n
be fixed parameters with
n=ms+c
and

\ell=s-c
, and let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
.
If

\sum_{i=0}^{m}|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\leq\binom{m\ell-1}{m}+\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|,

then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)|
.

Proof
.

For
i\geq m+1
, we have
|\mathcal{F}_{i}|=\binom{n}{i}-|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|
. Hence

|\mathcal{F}|=\sum_{i=0}^{m}|\mathcal{F}_{i}|+\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}-\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|\leq\binom{m\ell-1}{m}+\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}=|P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)|.

This proves the lemma.

The next numerical estimate will be used in Case 1 of the proof of

Theorem

1.1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
m\geq 3
, assume
c\leq\delta_{m}s
, put
\ell=s-c
and
n=ms+c
.
For all sufficiently large
s
, if
1\leq j\leq m-1
and

p=\ell+j-m-1
, then

\binom{m\ell-1}{m}-\binom{mp-1}{m}>(j+m)\binom{n}{m-1}.

Proof
.

The choice of
\delta_{m}
implies
\delta_{m}\leq 1/(100m(m+1))
.
Put
x=m\ell-1
and
r=m(m+1-j)
. Since
mp-1=x-r
,

\binom{m\ell-1}{m}-\binom{mp-1}{m}=\sum_{a=0}^{r-1}\binom{x-a-1}{m-1}
,
and this sum is at least
r\binom{x-r}{m-1}
.
We compare
\binom{x-r}{m-1}
with
\binom{n}{m-1}
. Since

x=m(s-c)-1
,
n=ms+c
, and
r=m(m+1-j)
, we have

n-(x-r-m+2)=(m+1)c+r+m-1
. Moreover,
r\leq m(m+1)
, and by the
choice of
\delta_{m}
,
(m+1)c\leq s/(100m)
. Hence, after increasing the
lower bound on
s
if necessary,

n-(x-r-m+2)\leq s/(50m)\leq n/(16m^{2})
, where the last inequality uses

n\geq ms
. Therefore

(x-r-m+2)/n\geq 1-1/(16m^{2})
.

We have

\frac{\binom{x-r}{m-1}}{\binom{n}{m-1}}=\prod_{i=0}^{m-2}\frac{x-r-i}{n-i}\geq\left(\frac{x-r-m+2}{n}\right)^{m-1}\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{16m^{2}}\right)^{m-1}\geq 1-\frac{1}{16m}.

Here the last inequality follows from Bernoulli’s inequality. Thus

\binom{x-r}{m-1}\geq(1-1/(16m))\binom{n}{m-1}
.

It remains only to check the coefficient. Since
r=m(m+1-j)
, it is enough
to prove

m(m+1-j)(1-1/(16m))>j+m
. Indeed,

m(m+1-j)\left(1-\frac{1}{16m}\right)-(j+m)=m(m+1-j)-\frac{m+1-j}{16}-(j+m)\geq\frac{7}{8},

where the last inequality follows from taking derivative and letting
j=m-1
.
Hence the desired strict inequality follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Fix
m\geq 3

\eta_{m}

2.2
), and choose
\delta_{m}
as in (
3.1

Let
\kappa=\kappa_{m+1}
be the constant from
Section

2
with
k=m+1
.
Choose
\beta_{m}
sufficiently large such that, for all
s\geq 1
and all
n\leq(m+1)s
,

2m\binom{n}{m-1}\leq\beta_{m}^{m}s^{m-1}\quad\text{and}\quad 4m\binom{n}{m-1}\leq\kappa\beta_{m}^{m}s^{m-1}.

(4.1)

Then we choose
s_{0}=s_{0}(m)
sufficiently large such that all later applications of some theorems and lemmas are valid and all asymptotic estimates below hold.

Let
s\geq s_{0}
, let
\beta_{m}s^{(m-1)/m}\leq c\leq\delta_{m}s
, put

\ell=s-c
and
n=ms+c
, and let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfy

\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
. We use the notation from
Section

3
.
By
Section

3
, we may and do assume that
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
.
By
Section

3
, it is enough to prove that

|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|+|H|-\binom{m\ell-1}{m}\leq|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|,

(4.2)

since
|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\leq\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|
. Also, as
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
,

|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\binom{n}{i}\leq m\binom{n}{m-1}.

(4.3)

The behavior of the
m
-layer is decisive.
We split according to whether
H
has an
\ell
-matching.

4.1
Case 1:
\nu(H)<\ell

In this case, we have
\nu(H)<\ell
.
Since
n=m\ell+(m+1)c
,
Section

3
gives

m\ell\leq n<(m+\eta_{m})\ell
.
Applying
Theorem

2.3
gives

|H|\leq\binom{m\ell-1}{m}.

(4.4)

If
|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|=0
, then (
4.2
) follows from
(
4.4
). Assume
|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|>0
, and choose

E\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq m-1
.

Put
p=\ell+j-m-1
. The value of
p
is chosen so that one
lower-layer set,
p
disjoint
m
-sets, and
c+m-j
disjoint

(m+1)
-sets would together form an
s
-matching. For sufficiently
large
s
, we have
p\geq 1
, and

1+p+(c+m-j)=s,\qquad n-j-mp=(m+1)(c+m-j).

(4.5)

Let
H(\overline{E})=\{Q\in H:Q\cap E=\emptyset\}
.
There are two subcases.

Subcase 1a:

H(\overline{E})
contains a
p
-matching.

In this case, choose pairwise disjoint sets
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{p}\in H(\overline{E})
.
Then
E,Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{p}
are pairwise disjoint members of
\mathcal{F}
. Let

W=[n]\setminus(E\cup Q_{1}\cup\cdots\cup Q_{p})
and
\tau=c+m-j
.
By (
4.5
),
|W|=n-j-mp=(m+1)(c+m-j)=(m+1)\tau
and

1+p+\tau=1+(\ell+j-m-1)+(c+m-j)=\ell+c=s
.
If
\mathcal{F}_{m+1}\cap\binom{W}{m+1}
contained
\tau

pairwise disjoint sets, then these sets together with

E,Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{p}
would form an
s
-matching in
\mathcal{F}
, a
contradiction. Hence
\mathcal{F}_{m+1}\cap\binom{W}{m+1}
has no

\tau
-matching.

Since
|W|=(m+1)\tau
, applying
Section

2
gives

|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq\left|\binom{W}{m+1}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{m+1}\right|\geq\tau^{-1}\binom{(m+1)\tau}{m+1}\geq\tau^{m}\geq c^{m}
, where the last inequality follows from

\tau=c+m-j\geq c
. Since
c\geq\beta_{m}s^{(m-1)/m}
, (
4.1
)
and (
4.3
) give

|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|\leq m\binom{n}{m-1}\leq\beta_{m}^{m}s^{m-1}\leq c^{m}\leq|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|
.
Together with (
4.4
), this proves (
4.2
) in
this subcase.

Subcase 1b:
H(\overline{E})
has no
p
-matching.

The family
H(\overline{E})
is a subfamily of
\binom{[n]\setminus E}{m}
and
|[n]\setminus E|=N=n-j=mp+(m+1)(c+m-j)
.
Our goal here is to bound
|H(\overline{E})|
by
Theorem

2.3
.
It remains to verify
N<(m+\eta_{m})p
, equivalently
(m+1)(c+m-j)<\eta_{m}p
.
Using
p=\ell+j-m-1
, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{m}p-(m+1)(c+m-j)

\displaystyle=\eta_{m}\ell-(m+1)c-\eta_{m}(m+1-j)-(m+1)(m-j)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta_{m}^{2}}{m+1+2\eta_{m}}s-\eta_{m}(m+1-j)-(m+1)(m-j)

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta_{m}^{2}}{m+1+2\eta_{m}}s-O(1),

where the inequality follows from
Section

3
.
Since
s_{0}
is sufficiently large, we have
mp\leq N<(m+\eta_{m})p
.

Theorem

2.3
gives
|H(\overline{E})|\leq\binom{mp-1}{m}
.
The number of
m
-sets meeting
E
is at most

\binom{n}{m}-\binom{n-j}{m}\leq j\binom{n}{m-1}
.
Therefore
|H|\leq j\binom{n}{m-1}+\binom{mp-1}{m}
.
Combining this with (
4.3
) and
Section

3
gives

|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|+|H|\leq(m+j)\binom{n}{m-1}+\binom{mp-1}{m}<\binom{m\ell-1}{m}
.
This proves (
4.2
) in this subcase.

4.2
Case 2:
\nu(H)\geq\ell

Write
\nu(H)=\ell+d
.
Since
\mathcal{F}
has no
s
-matching and

s=\ell+c
, we must have
d\leq c-1
.
Thus
0\leq d\leq c-1
.
The family
H
has no
(\ell+d+1)
-matching. Since
d\leq c-1
,
we have
m(\ell+d+1)\leq n
. Also

n-m(\ell+d+1)=(m+1)c-m(d+1)\leq(m+1)c<\eta_{m}\ell\leq\eta_{m}(\ell+d+1),

by
Section

3
. Thus

m(\ell+d+1)\leq n<(m+\eta_{m})(\ell+d+1)
, and
Theorem

2.3
gives

|H|\leq\binom{m(\ell+d+1)-1}{m}
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle|H|-\binom{m\ell-1}{m}\leq\sum_{a=0}^{m(d+1)-1}\binom{m\ell+a-1}{m-1}\leq m(d+1)\binom{n}{m-1}.

Together with (
4.3
), this gives

|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|+|H|-\binom{m\ell-1}{m}\leq 2m(d+1)\binom{n}{m-1}.

(4.6)

Choose
\ell+d
pairwise disjoint members
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{\ell+d}
of

H
.
Let
W=[n]\setminus(Q_{1}\cup\cdots\cup Q_{\ell+d})
and
\tau=c-d
.
Then
\tau\geq 1
and
|W|=(m+1)\tau+d
. If

\mathcal{F}_{m+1}\cap\binom{W}{m+1}
contained
\tau
pairwise disjoint sets,
then these sets together with
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{\ell+d}
would form an

s
-matching in
\mathcal{F}
. Hence,
\nu(\mathcal{F}_{m+1}\cap\binom{W}{m+1})<\tau
.
Applying
Section

2
to
\mathcal{F}_{m+1}\cap\binom{W}{m+1}

with
k=m+1
,
\rho=d
, and
\gamma=\tau+d=c
, we obtain

|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq\kappa(d+1)c^{m}
.
Since
c\geq\beta_{m}s^{(m-1)/m}
, (
4.1
) gives

|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq\kappa(d+1)\beta_{m}^{m}s^{m-1}\geq 4m(d+1)\binom{n}{m-1}.

Together with (
4.6
), this proves (
4.2
) in
Case 2.

Therefore
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)|
for every family
\mathcal{F}
with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
.

4.3
Equality cases

First suppose that

\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
and
|\mathcal{F}|=|P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)|
.
Since
|\mathcal{F}|=|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|+|H|+\binom{n}{\geq m+1}-\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|
and

|P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)|=\binom{m\ell-1}{m}+\binom{n}{\geq m+1}
, equality gives

\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|=|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|+|H|-\binom{m\ell-1}{m}.

(4.7)

Equality cannot occur in Case 2 since we have

|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq 4m(d+1)\binom{n}{m-1}>2m(d+1)\binom{n}{m-1}\geq|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|+|H|-\binom{m\ell-1}{m}

in Case 2.
Since
\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|\geq|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|
, this contradicts
(
4.7
).

Thus equality must occur in Case 1. If
|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|>0
, take

E\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
with
1\leq j\leq m-1
, as in the proof of Case 1. In
Subcase 1a, we obtain

|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq c^{m}\geq\beta_{m}^{m}s^{m-1}\geq 2m\binom{n}{m-1}>|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|.

Together with
|H|\leq\binom{m\ell-1}{m}
, this again contradicts
(
4.7
). In Subcase 1b, we obtain
|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|+|H|<\binom{m\ell-1}{m}
, making the right-hand side of (
4.7
) negative, while
\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|\geq 0
. Hence equality implies
|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|=0
.

With
|\mathcal{F}_{<m}|=0
, equality is possible only in Case 1 and hence

\nu(H)<\ell
.
By
Theorem

2.3
,
|H|\leq\binom{m\ell-1}{m}
.
Equation (
4.7
) becomes

\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|=|H|-\binom{m\ell-1}{m}.

The left-hand side is non-negative, whereas the right-hand side is non-positive.
Therefore both sides are zero.
Hence
|H|=\binom{m\ell-1}{m}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{i}=\emptyset
for every
i\geq m+1
.

Theorem

2.3
gives a set
L^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{m\ell-1}
such that
H=\binom{L^{\prime}}{m}
.
This implies that
\mathcal{F}=P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
.

It remains to exclude extremal families containing
\emptyset
. Suppose that such a family exists. By
Section

3
, one
can replace
\emptyset
by a missing singleton and obtain an extremal
family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}\neq\emptyset
. The
equality classification just proved forces

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
for some
L^{\prime}
, but
P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
has no singleton
because
m\geq 3
, a contradiction.

Conversely, the construction verified after (
1.1
)
shows that every
P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
has no
s
-matching and has size

|P^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)|
.
This completes the proof.

Proof of
Theorem

We prove the theorem in the following equivalent form. Let
s
be sufficiently large and let
\ell
satisfy
t(s)<\ell<s-1.59s^{2/3}
.
Put
n=3s+c=4s-\ell,\ell=s-c,a=\ell-1
and
r=n-a=2s+2c+1
.
Then
s-t(s)>c>1.59s^{2/3}
. Also
c<0.11s
for all
sufficiently large
s
.

Throughout this section, take
m=3
in
Section

3
.
For each family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
, write

\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}=\binom{[n]}{\geq 4}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\geq 4}=\bigcup_{i=4}^{n}\mathcal{Y}_{i}
.
For a family
\mathcal{G}
and a set

, write
\mathcal{G}(\overline{E})=\{G\in\mathcal{G}:G\cap E=\emptyset\}

Define
A_{3}=\binom{3\ell-1}{3}
,

\Lambda_{3}=\binom{a}{2}+\binom{n}{3}-\binom{r}{3}

B=(s-1)+(s-1)(2n-s)/2
.
The range

, equivalently
\ell>t(s)
, gives

A_{3}>\Lambda_{3}.

(5.1)

Indeed, direct expansion gives

\Lambda_{3}-A_{3}=\frac{\ell-1}{3}\left(24s^{2}-6s-6-(18s-17)\ell-10\ell^{2}\right).

The factor in parentheses is the negative of

10\ell^{2}+(18s-17)\ell-24s^{2}+6s+6
. Since
t(s)
is the positive root
of this quadratic and
\ell>t(s)
, this proves
(
5.1
).

For

, put

p_{i}=\ell+i-4,\qquad N_{i}=n-i,\qquad D_{i}=\binom{n}{3}-\binom{n-i}{3}.

(5.2)

Thus
D_{i}
is the number of triples meeting a fixed

-set. We shall use

\displaystyle A_{3}-D_{i}-h_{3}(N_{i},p_{i}-1)

\displaystyle>B,

(5.3)

\displaystyle A_{3}-D_{i}-\binom{3p_{i}-1}{3}

\displaystyle>B.

(5.4)

Their verification is deferred to
Appendix

A
.

5.1
Reduction to the key inequality

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfy
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
.
Since the family
P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
contains all sets of size at least
4
,
the only possible surplus over
P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
comes from layers
1,2,3
,
and this surplus must be paid for by missing sets above the
3
-layer.

By
Section

3
, we may and do assume that
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}

from now on.
Since
n=3s+c
and
c<0.11s
, we have
2n-s=5s+2c<5.22s
for all sufficiently
large
s
. Hence

B<3s^{2}.

(5.5)

In the rest of the proof, we will show that

|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|\leq A_{3}+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|.

(5.6)

This implies

|\mathcal{F}|=|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|+\binom{n}{\geq 4}-|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|\leq A_{3}+\binom{n}{\geq 4}=|P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell)|.

We first bound the number of
1
-sets and
2
-sets in
\mathcal{F}
.
The family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
has no
s
pairwise disjoint members, so
|\mathcal{F}_{1}|\leq s-1
.
Also
\nu(\mathcal{F}_{2})<s
. By
Section

2
, and since
n=3s+c
with
c>0
,

|\mathcal{F}_{2}|\leq\max\left\{\binom{2s-1}{2},\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-s+1}{2}\right\}=\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-s+1}{2}=\frac{(s-1)(2n-s)}{2}.

Thus, by the definition of
B
,

|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|\leq B.

(5.7)

Now we split into the three cases

\tau(H)\leq a,\qquad\nu(H)\leq a<\tau(H),\qquad\nu(H)\geq\ell.

5.2
Case 1:
\tau(H)\leq a

In this case, let
A
be a vertex cover of
H
with
|A|=a
.
Put
R=[n]\setminus A
and
M=\binom{[n]}{3}\setminus H
.
Since
A
covers
H
, every triple contained in
R
is missing from
H
, so

\binom{R}{3}\subseteq M
.

Define

B_{1}(M)=\left\{x\in[n]:|M(\overline{\{x\}})|\geq\binom{r+2}{3}\right\}

and

B_{2}(M)=\left\{E\in\binom{[n]}{2}:|M(\overline{E})|\geq\binom{r}{3}\right\}.

For a singleton
E=\{x\}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
, call
E
bad if
x\in B_{1}(M)
. For

E\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
, call
E
bad if
E\in B_{2}(M)
. All other sets in

\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}
are called good.

Put
M^{+}=M\setminus\binom{R}{3}
and
\xi=|M^{+}|=|M|-\binom{r}{3}
.
Every pair contained in
A
belongs to
B_{2}(M)
, because
\binom{R}{3}\subseteq M
; these pairs contribute exactly
\binom{a}{2}
.
We claim that

|B_{1}(M)|+|B_{2}(M)|\leq\binom{a}{2}+|M|-\binom{r}{3}.

(5.8)

It remains to bound the bad singletons and the bad pairs not wholly contained
in
A
by
\xi
.
Split them into the four classes

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{1}

\displaystyle=B_{2}(M)\cap\{E:|E|=2,|E\cap A|=1\},\qquad\mathcal{U}_{2}=B_{2}(M)\cap\binom{R}{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{3}

\displaystyle=B_{1}(M)\cap A,\qquad\mathcal{U}_{4}=B_{1}(M)\cap R.

For

, the number of possible elements in

is at most

, where

L_{1}=ar,\quad L_{2}=ar+\binom{r}{2},\quad L_{3}=ar+\binom{r}{2}+a,\quad L_{4}=ar+\binom{r}{2}+a+r.

If
\mathcal{U}_{j}
is non-empty, then the defining lower bound for

B_{1}(M)
or
B_{2}(M)
forces

, where

T_{1}=\binom{r-1}{2},\quad T_{2}=(r-2)^{2},\quad T_{3}=r^{2},\quad T_{4}=\frac{3r^{2}-3r+2}{2}.

These four thresholds are exactly the deficits between the relevant threshold

\binom{r+2}{3}
or
\binom{r}{3}
and the triples already supplied by

\binom{R}{3}
. Since
r-2a=4c+3
, we have

for all sufficiently

. The thresholds are increasing, because

T_{2}-T_{1}=(r-3)(r-2)/2
,
T_{3}-T_{2}=4(r-1)
, and

T_{4}-T_{3}=(r-2)(r-1)/2
. For fixed

, each difference

increases

, and at

4a+15
,
4
,

7a+28
, and

. Hence

for every

.
If

, all four classes are empty. If
T_{j}\leq\xi<T_{j+1}

some

. If
\xi\geq T_{4}
, the whole union has

L_{4}\leq T_{4}-1\leq\xi
. This proves
(
5.8
).

Subcase A: there are no good sets.

In this subcase, we have
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq B_{1}(M)

\mathcal{F}_{2}\subseteq B_{2}(M)

By (

In this subcase, let

.
Put

and

.
If

(5.9)

If

contains
p

-matching in

Since

for all sufficiently large
s
. By (
5.5
),
|\mathcal{Y}_{4}|>B
. Also

, because
A
covers
H

. Using (

), we obtain

|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|<A_{3}+|\mathcal{Y}_{4}|\leq A_{3}+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|
.

It remains to handle the subcase in which
H(\overline{E})
contains no

p
pairwise disjoint triples.
Here
N-3p+1=4(s-\ell)+13-4i>0
, so

Theorem

2.6
applies to
H(\overline{E})
with matching parameter

p
.
Since (
5.9
) is strictly larger than

\binom{N}{3}-\binom{N-p+1}{3}
, the first alternative in
Theorem

2.6

cannot be the active upper bound. Hence

Theorem

2.6
gives
|H(\overline{E})|\leq\binom{3p-1}{3}
. At most
D_{i}
triples of
H
meet

E
, hence
|H|\leq D_{i}+\binom{3p_{i}-1}{3}
. By (
5.7
) and
(
5.4
), we have
|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|<A_{3}
.
This completes Case 1.

5.3
Case 2:
\nu(H)\leq a<\tau(H)

In this case,
Theorem

2.7
applies, since

n-(3a+2)=4s-\ell-(3\ell-1)=4(s-\ell)+1>0
.
Since
A_{3}=\binom{3\ell-1}{3}=\binom{3a+2}{3}
,

|H|\leq\max\left\{h_{3}(n,a),\binom{3a+2}{3}\right\}=\max\{h_{3}(n,a),A_{3}\}.

Now we consider two subcases.

Subcase A:

|H|\leq h_{3}(n,a)
.

We first record the comparison needed in this subcase.

A_{3}-h_{3}(n,a)>B.

(5.10)

This is precisely the
i=2
instance of (
5.3
), since

D_{2}+h_{3}(N_{2},p_{2}-1)=h_{3}(n,a)

by (
5.2
) and
a=\ell-1
. The verification of
(
5.3
) is deferred to
Appendix

A
.

In this subcase, (
5.7
) and
(
5.10
) give
|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|<B+h_{3}(n,a)<A_{3}
.
Hence (
5.6
) holds.

Subcase B:

h_{3}(n,a)<|H|\leq A_{3}
.

In this subcase, if
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}=\emptyset
, then

|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|=|H|\leq A_{3}
, so (
5.6
) holds. Suppose
therefore that
E\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
, and put
p=p_{i}
and

N=N_{i}
.

If
H(\overline{E})
contains
p
pairwise disjoint triples, the completion
argument from Case 1 gives
|\mathcal{Y}_{4}|>B
. Since
|H|\leq A_{3}
,
(
5.7
) implies

|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|<A_{3}+|\mathcal{Y}_{4}|\leq A_{3}+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|
.

It remains to consider the case
\nu(H(\overline{E}))\leq p-1
. We claim that

\tau(H(\overline{E}))>p-1
. Otherwise, a vertex cover of
H(\overline{E})

of size at most
p-1
, together with the vertices of
E
, would cover every
triple in
H
. Its size would be at most

(p-1)+i=\ell+2i-5\leq\ell-1=a
,
contradicting
\tau(H)>a
.

Thus
\nu(H(\overline{E}))\leq p-1<\tau(H(\overline{E}))
. Since

N-(3(p-1)+2)=N-(3p-1)=4(s-\ell)+13-4i>0
,
Theorem

2.7
gives

|H(\overline{E})|\leq\max\{h_{3}(N,p-1),\binom{3p-1}{3}\}
. Therefore

|H|\leq D_{i}+\max\{h_{3}(N_{i},p_{i}-1),\binom{3p_{i}-1}{3}\}
. The shifted
comparisons (
5.3
) and (
5.4
),
together with (
5.7
), give
|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|<A_{3}
.
This completes Case 2.

5.4
Case 3:
\nu(H)\geq\ell

Before entering the proof of Case 3, we record an important lemma here.
Later, we will use it to bound
|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|
.
The proof of this lemma is fairly tricky, and hence it is given in
Appendix

B
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
H
contains
\ell+d
pairwise disjoint triples for some
d\geq 0
, then

|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|>B+\frac{1351}{100}(d+1)s^{2}.

(5.11)

Write
\nu(H)=q=\ell+d
with
d\geq 0
. Since
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, we have
q\leq s-1
.
Since
3(q+1)-1\leq 3s-1<n
, applying
Theorem

2.6
to
H
gives

|H|\leq\max\left\{\binom{n}{3}-\binom{n-q}{3},\binom{3q+2}{3}\right\}.

For the alternative
\binom{n}{3}-\binom{n-q}{3}
, we have

n-q=r-1-d
. Since
A_{3}>\Lambda_{3}
,

\binom{n}{3}-\binom{n-q}{3}-A_{3}<\binom{r}{3}-\binom{r-1-d}{3}\leq(d+1)\binom{r}{2}.

Because
r=2s+2c+1<2.23s
, the bound
(d+1)\binom{r}{2}
is less than

3(d+1)s^{2}
, and hence is smaller than
(1351/100)(d+1)s^{2}
.
For the alternative
\binom{3q+2}{3}
, the inequality
q\leq s-1
gives

\binom{3q+2}{3}-A_{3}\leq(3d+3)\binom{3s}{2}<\frac{27}{2}(d+1)s^{2}<\frac{1351}{100}(d+1)s^{2}.

Thus

|H|-A_{3}<\frac{1351}{100}(d+1)s^{2}.

(5.12)

By (
5.7
),
|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|\leq B
.
Section

5.4

gives

|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|>B+\frac{1351}{100}(d+1)s^{2}.

Together with (
5.12
), this yields

|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|<A_{3}+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|
. Hence (
5.6
) holds
strictly in Case 3.

5.5
Equality cases

The three cases prove (
5.6
), hence
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell)|
.
It remains to discuss equality.

Suppose equality holds.
Cases 1 and 3 were strict.
In Case 2, the subcase
|H|\leq h_{3}(n,a)
is strict, and all subcases with

\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\neq\emptyset
are strict. Therefore equality can occur only
when
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{2}=\emptyset
,
|H|=A_{3}
, and
\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}=\emptyset
. Since equality can occur only in Case 2, we have

\nu(H)\leq a=\ell-1
.
Since
\nu(H)<\ell
and
n\geq 3\ell-1
,
Theorem

2.6
applied to
H
with
matching parameter
\ell
gives

|H|\leq\max\left\{\binom{n}{3}-\binom{n-\ell+1}{3},\binom{3\ell-1}{3}\right\}.

The first alternative is

\binom{n}{3}-\binom{n-\ell+1}{3}=\binom{n}{3}-\binom{r}{3}
, which is strictly smaller
than
A_{3}
, because
A_{3}>\Lambda_{3}=\binom{a}{2}+\binom{n}{3}-\binom{r}{3}
. Hence the
equality statement in
Theorem

2.6
gives
H=\binom{L^{\prime}}{3}
for some

L^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{3\ell-1}
. Since
\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}=\emptyset
, we obtain

\mathcal{F}=\binom{L^{\prime}}{3}\cup\binom{[n]}{\geq 4}=P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
.

Finally, no extremal family can contain
\emptyset
. If one did, the initial
replacement by a missing singleton would produce an extremal family containing
a singleton, whereas the equality family just obtained contains no singleton.
Thus equality holds exactly for the families
P^{\prime}(3,s,\ell;L^{\prime})
with

L^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{3\ell-1}
. This proves
Theorem

1.2
.
