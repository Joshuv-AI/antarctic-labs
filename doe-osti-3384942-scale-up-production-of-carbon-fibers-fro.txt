 ABSTRACT

This work investigates the scale-up of pitch precursors for the carbon fiber market using mesophase pitch
formulated and produced by Advanced Carbon Products Technologies’ (ACPT’s) patented mesophase
pitch processing technology. Through use of its patented process, ACPT developed strategic materials by
converting petroleum-based pitch into a high-value, low-cost, carbon-rich feedstock for carbon fiber and
other materials critical to our national security. The team successfully developed processing criteria for
the tailored mesophase pitch material that can be processed further into precursor and carbon fiber.
Processability of the mesophase pitch into precursor and carbon fiber was demonstrated at scale. The
resulting materials sequester the carbon that would otherwise be burned and released into the atmosphere,
making this an environmentally friendly way to produce these materials in the United States.

Pitch-based carbon fibers offer a promising pathway toward cost-effective, high-performance materials
for structural and high-modulus composite applications; however, adoption has been limited by
challenges in precursor processability and scale-up. In this work, tailored isotropic and mesophase
petroleum-derived pitch materials were developed and evaluated for precursor and carbon fiber
production through a collaborative effort between Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) and ACPT.
Processing conditions were established at ORNL’s Carbon Fiber Technology Facility to enable stable
melt-blowing of pitch-based precursors under continuous operation. Mesophase pitch precursor fibers
were produced following oxidation and carbonization, corresponding to a diameter shrinkage of
approximately 12%–13% and an estimated mass yield of about 75%–80%. Continuous melt-blowing
steady-state operation was demonstrated over time, indicating robust process stability. Melt-blowing was
achieved at throughput rates of approximately 20 lb/h⁻¹, validating the commercial viability of petroleum-
derived pitch feedstocks for fiber production. Further studies are required to tailor carbon fiber
microstructure and properties for specific composite applications.

 1. INTRODUCTION

The quest for advanced carbon-based materials with unique properties and unparalleled performance has
spurred significant research into the development and synthesis of liquid crystal mesophases. These
mesophases, derived from polyaromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), exhibit extraordinary thermal stability,
mechanical strength, and anisotropic properties, making them indispensable in the production of display
technologies, optoelectronics, and high-performance structural composites such as carbon fibers. These
characteristics make them ideal precursors to produce high-strength carbon fibers that play a pivotal role
in critical industries, including aerospace, automotive, and renewable energy, in which their exceptional
stiffness, strength, and lightweight characteristics are essential.

The production of mesophase pitch, a precursor to carbon fibers, remains a challenging and resource-
intensive process. Traditional methods rely on batch reactors operating under pressurized conditions for
extended durations, often requiring hazardous catalysts like super acids (e.g., HF/BF3). Although these
approaches have demonstrated the feasibility of mesophase formation, they suffer from limitations,
including compositional variability of raw materials, inefficient reaction kinetics, and environmental
hazards. For instance, the HF/BF3 process commercialized for PAH polymerization was discontinued
owing to the extreme toxicity and corrosiveness of the reagents involved.

Carbon-based materials are of utmost importance in modern society, enabling a remarkable number of
highly engineered applications in nuclear shielding, performance composites, and adsorbents. One of the
most demanding is pitch-based carbon fibers, which require a molecularly ordered liquid mesophase to
form highly oriented connected basal planes that grant carbon fibers their extraordinary stiffness and
strength [1]. A critical limitation of pitch-based carbon fibers is the formation of the mesophase, a

disperse mixture of PAHs commonly from coal- or petroleum-based hydrocarbon feedstocks. The
synthesis of pitch from the hydrocarbon fractions of petroleum and coal has been previously demonstrated
for the fabrication of pitch-based carbon fiber [2,3]. The polydispersity and natural compositional
variation in coal and petroleum grades resulting from the breakdown of plant and animal matter, coupled
with an inability to reliably predict and separate mesogenic compounds from pitches, have continued to
present practical developmental challenges to the realization of highly graphitizable phases [4]. Previous
attempts to create synthetic mesophase pitch with a well-controlled population of anisotropic PAHs
consistently achieved optically active phase domains above 90%. However, those efforts required toxic
chemicals/catalysts [5]. A process that was commercialized for a short time was discontinued over the
toxicity concerns and practical challenges of handling the highly corrosive chemicals at high temperatures
(HTs). As a result, a serious need exists for invention of alternative approaches to form highly controlled
mesophases from synthetic feedstocks without the use of legacy chemicals.

Advanced Carbon Products Technologies (ACPT) has developed and patented continuous processes
[6,7,8] to produce both isotropic and mesophase pitch from low-cost aromatic petroleum-based
feedstocks. ACPT’s continuous approach can convert feedstock into mesophase pitch more efficiently
and at significantly lower cost and cycle time than those of other approaches. However, optimization of
pitch properties for precursors and carbon fiber manufacturing requires integration with fiber spinning,
stabilization, and conversion processes. Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) provided the
manufacturing-relevant infrastructure and expertise necessary to tailor pitch characteristics to meet the
stringent requirements of continuous precursor fiber production and downstream carbon fiber conversion.

This project was established using ORNL’s fiber research integrated multiscale approach by establishing
two working mechanisms

 A Strategic Partnership Project (SPP), #NFE-22-09159, focused on targeted optimization of pitch
 properties based on melt-spinning behavior and fiber conversion performance at bench scale

 A Cooperative Research and Development Agreement (CRADA), focused on a systematic reduction
 of uncertainties and risks associated with scaling by linking pitch formulation to fiber processing
 performance at pilot scale

1.1 EXPERIMENTAL

The high-level tasks of the project were the following:

 Expansion of a pitch production pilot plant

 Demonstration of continuous processing of the participant’s mesophase pitch, including melt-
 blowing; oxidation; and low-temperature (LT)/HT carbonization/graphitization, sizing, and chopping

 Characterization and batch processing of mat material obtained at various stages of the project

1.2 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

This project was executed under the Carbon Fiber Technology Facility (CFTF) strategic framework in
alignment with the US Department of Energy’s Office of Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energy1

Advanced Materials and Manufacturing Office2 (AMMO) objectives to advance manufacturing readiness
levels (MRLs) and accelerate the transition of advanced materials from laboratory-scale development to
manufacturing-relevant environments. The effort focused on the development and scale-up of a pitch-
based feedstock for carbon fiber manufacturing, with an emphasis on systematically reducing technical
and manufacturing risks that impede commercialization.

Under the SPP (#NFE-22-09159), activities advanced the technology from laboratory formulation toward
proof-of-concept manufacturing feasibility at the bench scale. Efforts included pitch material design,
characterization, and initial melt-spinning trials to demonstrate the ability to produce continuous
precursor fibers with convertibility to carbon fibers. These activities addressed early-stage risks
associated with material chemistry, thermal behavior, and basic process compatibility.

Continued SPP execution enabled validation of melt-spinning/blowing and precursor handling processes
using lab-scale infrastructure (Error! Reference source not found.). Process parameters were developed
and refined to improve material consistency, spinnability, and precursor quality, generating repeatable
data relevant to downstream carbonization. This phase reduced risks related to process stability,
reproducibility, and precursor-to-carbon-fiber conversion performance. Data generated at this stage were
used as the baseline for subsequent stages.

In parallel, CFTF’s melt-spinning/blowing infrastructure was upgraded to enable sustained HT processing
of pitch-based materials. These upgrades expanded facility capability to support technical readiness level
advancement, provided an industry-accessible platform for derisking scale-up before private-sector
capital investment, and enabled hands-on workforce development in advanced fiber manufacturing
operations. In addition, the upgrades strengthened CFTF’s role as a national manufacturing test bed
supporting AMMTO technology-to-market objectives while also enabling Vehicle Technologies Office
(VTO)–relevant evaluation of materials for lightweight, high-performance transportation applications.
Collectively, the project reduced manufacturing risk, advanced technology toward commercial readiness,
and supported workforce development in advanced fiber manufacturing aligned with the missions of both
AMMO and VTO.

Building on SPP outcomes, a follow-on CRADA advanced the technology into manufacturing-relevant
environments. Activities focused on scaling the pitch material; developing process conditions for fiber
melt-blowing and carbon fiber processing; evaluating process robustness under elevated temperatures and
extended operating conditions; and identifying scale-dependent uncertainties affecting yield, throughput,
and quality. These efforts directly supported progression toward MRL 5 and MRL 6 by demonstrating
performance at the pilot scale or production-representative equipment.

Process conditions were developed and optimized at the CFTF to enable stable melt-blowing of ACPT-
derived isotropic and mesophase pitch materials for precursor fiber production and subsequent carbon
fiber processing. Continuous operation was achieved for periods exceeding 5 h under steady-state
conditions, with processing terminated solely because of exhaustion of the available feedstock, indicating
robust process stability.

1.2.1 Petroleum-to-Mesophase-Pitch Production Process

ACPT’s patents and expertise are centered on continuous mesophase pitch production (e.g., chemistry,
conversion, throughput). ORNL provided the following unique capabilities to support the project:

 Pilot-scale melt-blowing of pitch into continuous precursor fiber
 Fiber-relevant property targets (e.g., rheology, spinnability window, defect tolerance)
 Feedback loops between pitch chemistry ↔ fiber processability ↔ carbon fiber conversion
 Manufacturing-relevant risk reduction before commercialization

The partnership between ACPT and ORNL focused on advancing the translation from pitch production to
fiber manufacturing. ORNL facilitated this process by offering pilot-scale fiber manufacturing capabilities
and essential process-property feedback, which allowed for precise tailoring of the pitch to meet specific
requirements for carbon fiber applications.

ACPT’s ACP10 pilot unit in Hitchins, KY has been expanded to produce approximately 25 lbs/hr. of
isotropic pitch. Additionally, the ACP20 pilot unit now produces about 12 lbs/hr. of mesophase pitch. In
addition, ACPT built the fully automated ACP-20 mesophase pitch production pilot unit with a capacity
of 76 lbs/hr. at the facility in Ashland, KY. Attached is a recent photo of the skid-mounted unit under
construction (Fig. 6).

 2. CONCLUSIONS

Based on iterative feedback from precursor fiber formation and carbon fiber conversion trials, the ORNL
team established and refined processing conditions enabling stable and repeatable conversion of ACPT’s
tailored pitch materials into precursor and carbon fibers. Continuous operation was achieved under
steady-state conditions, producing mesophase pitch precursor fibers with a controlled mean diameter,
shrinkage of approximately 12%–13%, and an estimated mass yield of about 75%–80%. These results
demonstrate strong coupling among pitch chemistry, melt-processing behavior, and fiber morphology,
with diameter control and conversion yield serving as key indicators of process robustness and material
quality.

In parallel, ACPT expanded its pitch production operations to increase material throughput, supporting
improved feedstock consistency and scalability. Collectively, the demonstrated control over fiber
diameter, shrinkage behavior, and conversion efficiency indicates a viable pathway toward scale-up and
technology transfer of pitch-based carbon fibers for composite manufacturing. Further studies are
required to tailor the resultant carbon fiber microstructure and properties—including texture development,
modulus, and defect population—to meet the distinct performance requirements of high-modulus
applications versus cost-sensitive structural composites.
