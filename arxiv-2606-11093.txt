Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following Cauchy problem for a semilinear evolution equations with scale-invariant time-dependent damping:

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+L_{w^{2}}u+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}=\Delta^{\theta}f(u),&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=0,\,\,\,\,u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\mu>0
,
\theta\in\{0,1\}
and the
operator
L_{w^{2}}
is defined via the Fourier transform by
L_{w^{2}}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(w(\xi)^{2}\hat{u})
, where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform on the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
its inverse.
Throughout the paper, the nonlinearity is regarded as a perturbation of the corresponding linear equation.
We assume that the nonlinearity
f
satisfies

f(0)=0,\quad|f(u)-f(v)|\leq C\,|u-v|\big(|u|^{\alpha-1}+|v|^{\alpha-1}\big),

(1.2)

for some
\alpha>1
. Assumption (
1.2
) has the advantage of relying on Lipschitz-type regularity condition; several nonlinearities of classical interest, such as
|u|^{\alpha}
,
\pm u|u|^{\alpha-1}
, satisfy (
1.2
).
In order to treat the nonlinearity
\Delta|u|^{\alpha}
in the framework of (
1.2
), we will consider initial data in homogeneous Sobolev spaces of negative order. Following
[
5
]
and the references therein, this assumption is commonly referred to as a “vanishing condition”.

The Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is often regarded as a
critical model
in the sense that the asymptotic behavior of its solutions strongly depends on the size of the parameter
\mu
. Moreover, different choices of
w(\xi)
lead to various well-known partial differential equations with scale-invariant damping, including the semilinear wave equation (Section
1.1
), the plate equation (Section
1.2
), and the generalized Boussinesq equation (Section
1.3
).

Wave equations with scale-invariant damping arise naturally in several problems from mathematical physics. In particular, such damping terms appear in compressible Euler equations with time-dependent dissipation, in the analysis of supersonic flows in divergent nozzles, and in generalized Tricomi-type models; see
[
29
]
for a recent overview and further references. From the analytical viewpoint, the damping term
\frac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}
is critical in the sense that it separates diffusion-like behavior from predominantly hyperbolic dynamics. This critical structure is also reflected in the qualitative properties of solutions and in the determination of critical exponents for global existence and blow-up phenomena.

The main goal of this paper is to investigate the global (in time) existence of small data energy solutions to the semilinear problem (
1.1
) under suitable assumptions on the initial data, the damping parameter
0<\mu<1
, the operator
L_{w^{2}}
, and the nonlinearity exponent
\alpha>1
.

Our analysis for the semilinear problem (
1.1
) is based on
L^{p}-L^{q}
decay estimates for the corresponding linear problem. In particular, for the plate-type model we employ
L^{1}-L^{q}
estimates, and for the Boussinesq equation, by using dispersive estimates with the choice

q=1+\alpha,\qquad p=q^{\prime}=1+\frac{1}{\alpha},

we are able to treat the nonlinear terms as a small perturbation of the associated linear problem, provided that the initial data is sufficiently small and the exponent
\alpha
lies in the supercritical range.

The asymptotic profile of solutions to (
1.1
) strongly depends on the size of the damping parameter
\mu
. In the case
\mu>1
, the damping term produces stronger dissipative effects and the associated linear problem typically exhibits diffusion-like decay properties, which facilitate the treatment of the nonlinear terms. In contrast, the regime
0<\mu<1
considered in this paper corresponds to a weakly damped hyperbolic behavior, where the dissipative mechanism is not sufficiently strong to dominate the dispersive effects. Consequently, the derivation of sharp decay estimates becomes substantially more delicate, especially for operators with non-homogeneous symbols such as those appearing in generalized Boussinesq-type equations. This weak damping regime therefore requires a finer analysis of the interplay between dispersion and dissipation.

The Strauss and the Fujita exponents

The problem of determining the critical exponent for the semilinear wave equation

u_{tt}-\Delta u=|u|^{\alpha}

goes back to
[
23
]
, who showed in dimension
n=3
that global (in time) solutions with small initial data exist for
\alpha>1+\sqrt{2}
, while finite-time blow-up occurs for
\alpha\leq 1+\sqrt{2}
under a suitable sign condition on the data. In
[
30
]
, it was conjectured that the critical exponent for the wave equation is given by
\gamma(n-1)
, where
\gamma(n)
is the positive root of the equation

\frac{n}{2}\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

This conjecture was later confirmed in
[
18
]
with the complete high-dimensional theory. Moreover, in
[
5
]
it was proved that
\gamma(n)
is the critical exponent for the Boussinesq equation. The shift from
\gamma(n)
to
\gamma(n-1)
is related to the structure of the phase function: the Hessian of
w(\xi)=|\xi|
has rank
n-1
for all
\xi\neq 0
.

On the other hand, the problem of determining the critical exponent for the semilinear damped wave equation

u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|u|^{\alpha}

was extensively studied in the literature. In particular, it was shown that the critical exponent coincides with the Fujita exponent
\alpha_{\text{Fuj}}=1+\frac{2}{n}
, that is well-known to be the critical exponent for the semilinear heat equation with power nonlinearity. More precisely, if
\alpha>\alpha_{\text{Fuj}}
, then global (in time) solutions with sufficiently small initial data exist, whereas for
\alpha\leq\alpha_{\text{Fuj}}
solutions blow up in finite time under suitable assumptions on the initial data (see
[
34
]
and
[
42
]
). This reflects the diffusion phenomenon exhibited by the damped wave equation, whose asymptotic behavior is closely related to that of the heat equation.

1.1
Wave equation with scale-invariant time-dependent damping

When
w(\xi)=|\xi|
, the operator
L_{w^{2}}
reduces to the Laplacian,
L_{w^{2}}=-\Delta
, and (
1.1
) becomes the semilinear wave equation with scale-invariant damping:

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}=|u|^{\alpha},&t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
\mu>0
and
\alpha>1
. This model has been extensively studied in the literature. It is conjectured that the small data Cauchy problem (
1.3
) with
u_{1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
admits a critical exponent of the form

\alpha_{\text{crit}}=\max\left\{\gamma(n+\mu)\,,\,\alpha_{\text{Fuj}}(n)\right\},\qquad\text{that is},\qquad\alpha_{\text{crit}}=\begin{cases}\gamma(n+\mu)&\text{if}\quad 0<\mu<\mu_{*}(n),\\
\alpha_{\text{Fuj}}(n)&\text{if}\quad\mu\geq\mu_{*}(n),\end{cases}

where
\alpha_{\text{Fuj}}(n)=1+2/n
and

\mu_{*}(n)=\frac{n^{2}+n+2}{n+2}

satisfying
\gamma(n+\mu_{*})=\alpha_{\text{Fuj}}(n)
. This conjecture has been partially verified in
[
6
,
7
,
12
,
13
,
21
]
.

Recently, in
[
8
]
it was proved that
\alpha_{\text{crit}}=1+\frac{4}{n}
is the critical exponent to (
1.3
) under the assumption of small data
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mu\geq 1
if
n=1,2,3
or
\mu\geq\frac{2n}{n+3}
if
n\geq 4
.

Cauchy problems for the semilinear scale-invariant damping
wave models with time-dependent speed of propagation
u_{tt}-(1+t)^{2\ell}\Delta u+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}=|u|^{\alpha}
, with
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, have been studied by many
authors in a series of papers (see
[
1
,
31
,
35
]
and references therein).
These models belong to the family of equations arising in Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) spacetimes and are used to describe accelerating/decelerating expanding universe models.

1.2
\sigma
-evolution equation with scale-invariant time-dependent damping

If
w(\xi)=|\xi|^{\sigma}
with
\sigma>0
, then
L_{w^{2}}=(-\Delta)^{\sigma}
, and (
1.1
) becomes the following semilinear
\sigma
-evolution equation with scale-invariant time-dependent damping:

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}=|u|^{\alpha},&t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
\mu\geq 0
,
\sigma>0
, and
\alpha>1
. The choice
w(\xi)=|\xi|^{2}
yields
L_{w^{2}}=\Delta^{2}
and this describes the dynamics of a plate with a time-dependent damping mechanism.

For
\sigma>1
and
\mu=0
, assuming small initial data in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the authors of
[
14
]
proved that, for
\sigma<n\leq 2\sigma
, the exponent
\alpha=\frac{n+\sigma}{(n-\sigma)_{+}}
is the critical exponent distinguishing blow-up and global existence for (
1.4
).

The model (
1.4
) with
\sigma>1
has been recently investigated in
[
15
]
, where some global existence results for small data solutions were obtained. In particular, the authors proved that

•

if
1\leq n<2\sigma
and
\mu>\max\{\frac{4n}{n+2\sigma}\,,\,1\}
, then the Fujita-type exponent
p=1+\frac{2\sigma}{n}
is the critical exponent distinguishing blow-up and global existence by assuming small initial data in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
where
r\in[1,2]
;

•

if
1\leq n<\sigma
and
1-n/\sigma<\mu<\min\{1\,,\,\mu^{\star}\}
, then the critical exponent is a shift of Fujita exponent
\alpha=\alpha_{\text{Fuj}}(\mu+n/\sigma-1)
by assuming small initial data in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here
\mu^{\star}
is defined by
\mu^{\star}=\frac{1}{2}\big(1-n/\sigma+\sqrt{(1-n/\sigma)^{2}+8(1-n/\sigma)}\big)
.

1.3
Boussinesq equation with scale-invariant time-dependent damping

Choosing
w(\xi)=\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4}}
, leads to
L_{w^{2}}=-\Delta+\Delta^{2}
, where the low frequency and high frequency behaviors are governed by different Laplacians. Namely, we consider

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+\Delta^{2}u+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}=\Delta f(u),&t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.5)

where
0<\mu<1
and
f(u)=|u|^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>1
. For discussions on the physical background and modeling aspects of the damped Boussinesq equations, we refer the reader to
[
39
]
, where the authors studied the following Cauchy problem for the generalized Boussinesq equation with weak damping:

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+\Delta^{2}u+u_{t}=\Delta f(u),&t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.6)

where the nonlinear term
f(u)
behaves as a power type satisfying
f(u)=O(|u|^{2})
as
u\to 0
. They established the global existence and time-decay rates of solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.6
) by applying the energy method together with suitable interpolation inequalities. Moreover, the existence, uniqueness, and asymptotic stability of time-periodic solutions to the equation in (
1.6
) were investigated in
[
38
]
.

Although the coefficient in front of the second-order operator is constant in (
1.5
), time-dependent coefficients naturally arise in several related beam and plate models. For instance, the authors in
[
9
]
investigated damped plate equations with a time-dependent coefficient of the form
u_{tt}-\lambda(t)\Delta u+\Delta^{2}u+u_{t}=0
, while, in
[
41
]
, the authors studied the asymptotic behavior of solutions to damped beam equations with variable coefficients, which may be regarded as the case
n=1
of the previous model. More recently, the asymptotic behavior of solutions to a nonlinear beam equations with two time-dependent coefficients,
u_{tt}-\lambda(t)\partial_{x}^{2}u+\partial_{x}^{4}u+b(t)u_{t}=\partial_{x}f(\partial_{x}u)
, was studied in
[
19
]
. Motivated by these developments, it is natural to investigate related higher-order models with time-dependent damping. Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, the Cauchy problem (
1.5
) with scale-invariant damping has not yet been addressed in the literature.

On the other hand, the undamped Boussinesq equation (that is, when
\mu\equiv 0
) have drawn much attention by many researchers due to the wide applications in the real world (see
[
3
,
5
,
17
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
). The classical form

u_{tt}-u_{xx}+u_{xxxx}=(u^{2})_{xx},

was first derived by Boussinesq in 1872 in
[
4
]
to describe the propagation of long waves with small amplitude on the surface of shallow water, where
u=u(x,t)
denotes the elevation of the free fluid surface. Beyond water waves, the Boussinesq equation also models nonlinear vibrations of strings and electromagnetic waves in nonlinear dielectric materials (see
[
36
,
37
]
), among other physical phenomena.

2
Main results

Our first goal is to fill some gaps in the global existence results for small data solutions to (
1.4
) obtained in the previous works
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
. Applying the dispersive estimates derived in Proposition
3.2
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\sigma\geq 2
,
1\leq n<2\sigma
and
\big(1-\frac{n}{\sigma}\big)_{+}<\mu<\min\big\{1,2-\frac{n}{\sigma}\big\}
. Assume that

1+\frac{2}{\mu+\dfrac{n}{\sigma}-1}<\alpha.

Then, for sufficiently small initial data

u_{1}\in\mathcal{A}:=L^{1}(\mathbb R^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb R^{n})

there is a unique global (in time) solution
u\in\mathcal{C}\big([0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb R^{n})\cap L^{r}(\mathbb R^{n})\big)
for all
r\in(\alpha,\infty)
to (
1.4
).
Moreover, the solution satisfies the following decay estimates for all
t\geq 0
and
q\in[r,\infty)
:

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\mu-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{A}},

(2.1)

for any
\delta>0
such that
(1+\delta)\alpha<r
.

Remark 2.1
.

Theorem
2.1
improves the global existence result stated in Theorem 2.1 in
[
15
]
in several aspects. First, the assumption on the space dimension is relaxed from
n<\sigma
to
n<2\sigma
. Second, the upper bound for damping parameter is simplified. In
[
15
]
, the parameter
\mu
is required to satisfy

1-\frac{n}{\sigma}<\mu<\min\{1,\mu^{\star}\},

where
\mu^{\star}
is defined at the end of Section
1.2
.

Moreover, the restriction on the exponent of the nonlinearity is replaced by

1+\frac{2}{\mu+\frac{n}{\sigma}-1}<\alpha,

instead of the interval

1+\frac{2}{\mu+\frac{n}{\sigma}-1}<\alpha\leq\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{(2\gamma_{1}+\mu-2)_{+}},\quad\gamma_{1}=\frac{n}{\sigma}

appearing in
[
15
]
. Namely, we have a result without imposing an upper bound on the power of the nonlinearity in Theorem 2.1.

Theorem
2.1
yields a simpler description of the decay behavior together with a broader range of admissible parameters. Finally, duo to the lack of
L^{1}-L^{q}
estimates in Proposition 3.2, we have the loss of decay
(1+t)^{\frac{n\delta}{\sigma(1+\delta)}}
in (
2.1
) for solutions to (
1.4
).

Our second goal is to derive the Strauss-type exponent to (
1.5
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mu\in(0,1)
. Assume that

\alpha_{\text{crit}}<\alpha<1+\frac{4}{(n-2)_{+}},

where
\alpha_{\text{crit}}
is the positive solution to

\frac{n}{2}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}+\frac{\mu}{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\Big(1+\frac{\mu}{2}\Big).

(2.2)

If
n\geq 2
, then for sufficiently small initial data

u_{1}\in\mathcal{A}:=\dot{H}^{-1,1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

there is a unique global (in time) solution
u\in L^{\infty}\big([0,\infty),L^{\alpha+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big)
to (
1.5
).

Moreover, the solution satisfies the following decay estimates:

\|\,u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha+1}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}-\frac{\mu}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}=\frac{n}{2}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}.

(2.3)

In space dimension
n=1
, the result holds replacing
\mathcal{A}
by

\mathcal{A}:=\dot{H}^{-\frac{3}{2},1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{-1,1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Remark 2.2
.

In the case
\mu=0
in (
2.2
), we find the classical Strauss exponent
\gamma(n)
and our result corresponds to the result obtained in
[
5
, Theorem 2]
.

Remark 2.3
.

In the present paper, we investigated the weakly damped regime
0<\mu<1
in (
1.5
), where the asymptotic behavior of solutions remains essentially hyperbolic. In this case, the competition between dispersion and weak dissipation leads to a Strauss-type critical exponent for the Boussinesq equation in Theorem
2.2
.

On the other hand, for larger damping parameters
\mu>1
in (
1.5
), one may expect a qualitatively different behavior. Indeed, the damping term is sufficiently strong to produce diffusion-like effects in the low-frequency region, similarly to what occurs for damped wave and beam equations with effective dissipation. In view of this phenomenon, it is natural to conjecture that the critical exponent associated with (
1.5
) for
\mu>1
and the nonlinearity
|u|^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>1
should be of Fujita type. This expectation is also consistent with the decay structure observed for weakly damped beam equations in
[
22
,
32
,
33
]
, where the low-frequency part exhibits heat-like behavior and stronger dissipative effects dominate the long-time behavior.

3
Dispersive estimates

The corresponding
s-
parameter linear model to (
1.1
) with vanishing right-hand side is given by

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+L_{w^{2}}u+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}=0,&t\geq s\geq 0,\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(s,x)=0,\,\,\,\,u_{t}(s,x)=u_{1}(s,x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

We apply the partial Fourier transformation
\hat{u}(t,\xi)=\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}(u)(t,\xi)
with respect to spatial variables and obtain

\begin{cases}\widehat{u}_{tt}+w(\xi)^{2}\widehat{u}+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}\widehat{u}_{t}=0&t\geq s\geq 0,\,\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\widehat{u}(s,\xi)=0,\,\,\,\,\widehat{u}_{t}(s,\xi)=\widehat{u}_{1}(s,\xi),&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Introducing the new variable
\tau:=(1+t)w(\xi)
and making the ansatz
\widehat{u}(t,\xi)=v(\tau)
, the Cauchy problem (
3.2
) reduces to the following Cauchy problem for second order ODE:

\begin{cases}v_{\tau\tau}+v+\dfrac{\mu}{\tau}v_{\tau}=0,&t\geq s\geq 0,\,\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v((1+s)w(\xi))=0,\,\,\,\,v_{\tau}((1+s)w(\xi))=\dfrac{\hat{u}_{1}(s,\xi)}{w(\xi)},&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

(3.3)

According to
[
40
]
, the solution to the transformed equation (
3.3
) can be expressed in terms of Hankel functions
H^{\pm}_{\rho}
. This representation captures the oscillatory and decaying behavior of solutions at both low and high frequencies and is crucial for deriving sharp pointwise estimates in the Fourier space (see Lemma
A.1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
u
solves the Cauchy problem (
3.1
). Then, the Fourier transform
\hat{u}(t,s,\xi)
can be represented as

\widehat{u}(t,s,\xi)=\psi(t,s,\xi)\widehat{u}_{1}(s,\xi),

where the multiplier
\psi=\psi(t,s,\xi)
satisfies

\psi(t,s,\xi)=\frac{\pi}{4i}\frac{(1+t)^{\rho}}{(1+s)^{\rho-1}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}H^{-}_{\rho}\left((1+s)w(\xi)\right)&H^{-}_{\rho}\left((1+t)w(\xi)\right)\\

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

, that is,
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfy

|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}a(\xi)|\leq C\,\langle\xi\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-|\gamma|},\quad\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}.

Let us define

h(t,\xi):=((1+t)g(\xi))^{\frac{1}{2}}a((1+t)g(\xi)),\qquad t\geq 0,

and satisfy (

h(t,\cdot)\in M_{p}^{p}

, and there exists a constant

\|h(t,\cdot)\|_{M_{p}^{p}}\leq C

Proof.

We prove that
h
satisfies the Mikhlin–Hörmander condition uniformly with respect to
t\geq 0
, namely,

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}h(t,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb N^{n}.

Hence, the multiplier estimate follows.

If the multiplier

is homogeneous, then

\|h(t,\cdot)\|_{M_{p}^{p}}=\|h(0,\cdot)\|_{M_{p}^{p}}
and we may reduce the problem to time-independent multiplier.

In the following, our analysis include also non-homogeneous multipliers. Set

F(r):=r^{\frac{1}{2}}a(r),\qquad r>0.

Since
a\in S^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, one has

|F^{(k)}(r)|\leq C_{k}r^{-k},\qquad k\in\mathbb N_{0}.

Moreover,

h(t,\xi)=F((1+t)g(\xi)).

By the chain rule, for every multi-index
\alpha\neq 0
,

\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}h(t,\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{|\alpha|}F^{(k)}((1+t)g(\xi))(1+t)^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{k}=\alpha\\
|\beta_{j}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}C_{\alpha,\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{j}}g(\xi).

Since

|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}g(\xi)|\leq C_{\gamma}|\xi|^{\eta-|\gamma|},

we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}h(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{|\alpha|}((1+t)g(\xi))^{-k}(1+t)^{k}|\xi|^{k\eta-|\alpha|}\leq C_{\alpha}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}.

Hence,

satisfies the Mikhlin–Hörmander condition, and therefore
\|h(t,\cdot)\|_{M_{p}^{p}}\leq C
,
p\in(1,\infty)
with

independent of

. ∎

Remark 3.2

(General framework)

More generally (see for instance

where

•

•

In particular, for the fractional Laplacian

We preliminarily notice that, thanks to the homogeneity assumption of

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}w(\xi)|

\displaystyle={\mathrm{|\xi|}}^{\sigma-|\gamma|}|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}w(\xi^{\prime})|\leq C_{\gamma}\,{\mathrm{|\xi|}}^{\sigma-|\gamma|},

We are interested in the case whether

4
Proof of global in time existence results

Let us begin by introducing some notations that will be used throughout this section. First, we denote by
u^{\lin}=u^{\lin}(t,x)
the solution to the linear Cauchy problem (
3.1
) with
s=0
, and by
K=K(t,\tau,x)
the corresponding fundamental solution. Next, we define the operator
N
as follows:

N:u\in X(T)\rightarrow Nu(t,x)=u^{\lin}(t,x)+u^{\nl}(t,x),

where
X(T)
is a suitable function space in which we seek solutions, and

\displaystyle u^{\nl}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}K(t,\tau,x)\ast_{(x)}f(u)d\tau,

where
f(u)=|u(\tau,x)|^{\alpha}
or
f(u)=\Delta|u(\tau,x)|^{\alpha}
. By Duhamel’s principle, the solution to (
1.1
) can be represented as a fixed point of the operator
N
.

To establish the existence of such a fixed point, we will prove that
N
satisfies the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|Nu\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{A}}+C_{1}(t)\|u\|_{X(T)}^{\alpha},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|Nu-Nv\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(t)\|u-v\|_{X(T)}\big(\|u\|_{X(T)}^{\alpha-1}+\|v\|_{X(T)}^{\alpha-1}\big),

(4.2)

where
\mathcal{A}
denotes the function space of the initial data
u_{1}
. These estimates enable us to apply the contraction mapping principle, thereby obtaining a unique solution
u\in X(T)
to the equation
Nu=u
. In particular,

•

for large initial data, we obtain local (in time) existence by exploiting the fact that
C_{1}(t),C_{2}(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
;

•

for small initial data, we obtain global-in-time existence provided that
\max\{C_{1}(t)\,,\,C_{2}(t)\}\leq C
for all
t\in[0,\infty)
, where
C
is a suitable constant.

In the next results the following lemma comes into play.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\nu>-1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, it holds

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\nu}\,(1+s)^{\beta}\,ds\lesssim\begin{cases}(1+t)^{\nu},&\mbox{ if }\,\,\beta<-1,\\
(1+t)^{\nu}\,log(e+t),&\mbox{ if }\,\,\beta=-1,\\
(1+t)^{1+\nu+\beta},&\mbox{ if }\,\,\beta>-1,\end{cases}

and

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\nu}\,e^{-c(t-s)}(1+s)^{\beta}\,ds\lesssim(1+t)^{\beta}.

Moreover, the estimate is also valid if
(t-s)^{\nu}
is replaced by
(1+t-s)^{\nu}
in the integral.

4.1
Application to the nonlinear plate-type equation

In this section, we consider the Cauchy problem for semilinear plate-type equation

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}=|u|^{\alpha}&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
\sigma\geq 2
and
\alpha>1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We define the solution space

X(T)=\big\{u\in\mathcal{C}\big([0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big):\ \|u\|_{X(T)}<\infty\big\},\quad\text{where}\quad r\in(\alpha,\infty).

The corresponding norm

\|u\|_{X(T)}=\sup_{t\in[0,T],\,q\in[2,r]}(1+t)^{\mu-1+\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u\|_{L^{q}}.

Since
u_{1}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\mathcal{A}
, from Proposition
3.2
, for
0<\mu<\min\left\{1,2-\frac{n}{\sigma}\right\}
we immediately conclude

\|u^{\lin}\|_{X(T)}=\|K(t,0,x)\ast u_{1}(x)\|_{X(T)}\leq C_{0}\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{A}}.

Therefore, to conclude (
4.1
), it remains to prove

\|u^{\nl}\|_{X(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{X(T)}^{\alpha}.

From Proposition
3.2
, for
0<\mu<\min\left\{1,2-\frac{n}{\sigma}\right\}
we have for all
2\leq q\leq r

\displaystyle\|u^{\nl}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|K(t,s,\cdot)\ast|u(s,\cdot)|^{\alpha}\|_{L^{q}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t)^{1-\mu-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}(1+s)^{\mu}\|\,|u(s,\cdot)|^{\alpha}\|_{L^{1+\delta}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\mu-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{\mu}\|u(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha(1+\delta)}}^{\alpha}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\mu-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{\mu+\alpha\left(1-\mu-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta}-\frac{1}{\alpha(1+\delta)}\right)\right)}ds.

The last integral is finite under the condition

\mu+\alpha\Big(1-\mu-\frac{n}{\sigma(1+\delta)}\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\Big)\right)<-1,\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad\alpha>1+\frac{2}{\mu+\frac{n}{\sigma}-1}.

Therefore, we conclude

\|u^{\nl}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim(1+t)^{1-\mu-\frac{n}{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{\alpha}.

Next we turn to the proof of the Lipschitz condition (
4.2
). Due to the fact that

|\,|u(\tau,x)|^{\alpha}-|v(\tau,x)|^{\alpha}|\lesssim|u(s,x)-v(s,x)|\big(|u(s,x)|^{\alpha-1}+|v(s,x)|^{\alpha-1}\big),

from Hölder’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\big\||u(s,\cdot)|^{\alpha}-|v(s,\cdot)|^{\alpha}\big\|_{L^{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(s,\cdot)-v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha(1+\delta)}}\big(\|u(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha(1+\delta)}}^{\alpha-1}+\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha(1+\delta)}}^{\alpha-1}\big).

Thus, following the same ideas to what we did to estimate
\|u^{\nl}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}
, we can obtain (
4.2
). In this way the proof of Theorem
2.1
is completed.
∎

4.2
Application to the Boussinesq equation

In this section, we consider the Cauchy problem for semilinear Boussinesq equation

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+\Delta^{2}u+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}=\Delta|u|^{\alpha}&t>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\end{cases}

(4.3)

where
\alpha>1
. We define the operator

(Fu)(t,\cdot)=\int_{0}^{t}\Delta\,E(t-s)\ast f(u(s,\cdot))\,ds,

where
E=E(t)
is the fundamental solution to the linear problem (
3.14

.

If

, applying Proposition
3.3
, with

and

\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\,(1+t)^{-\delta_{BQ}-\frac{\mu}{2}}\|\langle D\rangle^{nd-1}|D|^{-1}u_{1}\|_{L^{p}}.

If
n=1
, we apply
Proposition
3.3
, with

,

\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\,(1+t)^{-\delta_{BQ}-\frac{\mu}{2}}\|\langle D\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}|D|^{-\frac{3}{2}}u_{1}\|_{L^{p}}.

Thanks to Remark

,
\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}=\frac{nd}{2}
.
Therefore, to maximize the decay rate we shall fix

and

on the conjugate line, that is,

p=1+\frac{1}{\alpha},\quad q=1+\alpha,\qquad\text{hence},\qquad d(p,q)=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}\,.

The critical exponent
\alpha_{\text{crit}}
for the existence of global-in-time solutions is obtained when

\alpha\Big(\frac{nd}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\Big)=1+\frac{\mu}{2},\qquad\text{i.e.},\qquad\frac{n}{2}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}+\frac{\mu}{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\Big(1+\frac{\mu}{2}\Big).

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We define the solution space

X=\big\{u\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),L^{\alpha+1}):\ \|u\|_{X}\leq R\big\}

for
R>0
that will be fixed later, and the corresponding norm

\|u\|_{X}=\sup_{t>0}(1+t)^{\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}+\frac{\mu}{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\alpha+1}},\quad\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}=\frac{n}{2}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}.

Let
E(t,s,\cdot)\ast u_{1}
be the solution to the linear problem (
3.14
).
We notice that
nd<2
is equivalent to
\alpha<1+4/(n-2)
when
n\geq 3
, so that from the estimate above we derive

\|E(t,0,\cdot)\ast u_{1}\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{1}(1+t)^{-\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}-\frac{\mu}{2}}\,\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{A}}.

In particular, we fix
R=2C_{1}\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{A}}
, so that
\|E(t,0,\cdot)\ast u_{1}\|_{X}\leq R/2
. We now prove that the operator

(Nu)(t,\cdot)=E(t,0,\cdot)\ast u_{1}(t,\cdot)+(Fu)(t,\cdot)

is a contraction on
X
. Let
g(\tau,\cdot)=f(u(\tau,\cdot))-f(v(\tau,\cdot))
. We modify the estimate to reduce the regularity of
\Delta g
, namely, in Proposition
3.3
we set
\nu=\nu_{0}=1
at low frequencies and
\nu=\nu_{0}=0
at high frequencies, to get

\|E(t,s,\cdot)\ast\Delta g(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha+1}}\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}(1+s)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}\begin{cases}C\,(1+t-s)^{-\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}}\,\||D|^{-2}\Delta g(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\,,&t-s\geq 1,\\
C\,(t-s)^{-\frac{nd}{2}}\,\|\langle D\rangle^{-2}\Delta g(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\,,&t-s\in(0,1].\end{cases}

Recalling that
nd<2
, the singularity
(t-s)^{-\frac{nd}{2}}
is integrable. Hence,

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t-1}\|E(t,s,\cdot)\ast\Delta g(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha+1}}ds\leq C\,(1+t)^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\int_{0}^{t-1}(1+s)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}(t-s)^{-\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}}\,\|g(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\,ds,\\
&\int_{t-1}^{t}\|E(t,s,\cdot))\ast\Delta g(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha+1}}ds\leq C\,(1+t)^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\int_{t-1}^{t}(1+s)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{nd}{2}}\,\|g(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\,ds.\end{split}

Since
u,v\in X
, we get

\begin{split}\|\langle D\rangle^{-2}\Delta g(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}&\leq\||D|^{-2}\Delta g(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\approx\|g(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\\
&\leq C\,R^{\alpha-1}\|u-v\|_{X}\,(1+s)^{-\alpha\left(\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)}.\end{split}

We notice that

\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}\leq\frac{nd}{2}<1,

hence, using
\alpha\big(\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}+\frac{\mu}{2}\big)>1+\frac{\mu}{2}
, from Lemma
4.1
we immediately obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t-1}(t-s)^{-\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}}\,(1+s)^{-\alpha\left(\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)+\frac{\mu}{2}}\,ds\lesssim(1+t)^{-\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}},\\
&\int_{t-1}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{nd}{2}}\,(1+s)^{-\alpha\left(\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)+\frac{\mu}{2}}\,ds\lesssim(1+t)^{-\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}}.\end{split}

In turn, we get

\|(Nu-Nv)(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha+1}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|E(t,s,\cdot)\ast\Delta g(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha+1}}ds\leq C_{2}\,R^{\alpha-1}\|u-v\|_{X}\,(1+t)^{-\delta_{\mathrm{BQ\,}}-\frac{\mu}{2}}.

Recalling that
R=2C_{1}\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{A}}
and letting
\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{A}}
sufficiently small to obtain
C_{2}\,R^{\alpha-1}\leq 1/2
, we derive

\|(Nu-Nv)\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\|u-v\|_{X}.

Therefore,
F:X\to X
and it is a contraction. By Banach’s contraction principle, there is a unique
u\in X
such that
Fu=u
, that is,
u
is a solution to (
4.3
). Moreover,
\|u\|_{X}\leq R=2C_{1}\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{A}}
, that is, we proved (
2.3
).
∎

Appendix

In this section, we will recall some tools from special functions that have been applied in the treatments of the linear Cauchy problem (
3.1
).

Lemma A.1

(Bessel, Weber and Hankel functions,
[
2
]
)
.

For the solutions of the Bessel equation of order
\gamma
the following asymptotic estimates hold:

(i)

The Bessel function
J_{\gamma}(\tau)

\Gamma_{\gamma}(\tau)=\tau^{-\gamma}J_{\gamma}(\tau),

is entire in
\gamma
and
\tau
. In particular, for small arguments we have

|J_{\gamma}(\tau)|\lesssim\tau^{\gamma},\quad 0<\tau<1.

(ii)

The Weber function
Y_{\gamma}(\tau)
satisfies for every integer
n

Y_{n}(\tau)=\frac{2}{\pi}J_{n}(\tau)\ln\tau+A_{n}(\tau),

where
\tau^{n}A_{n}(\tau)
is entire, non-null for
\tau=0
and

|A_{n}(\tau)|\lesssim\tau^{-n},\quad 0<\tau<1.

(iii)

The Hankel functions
H^{\pm}_{\gamma}=J_{\gamma}\pm iY_{\gamma}
satisfy

2(H^{\pm}_{\gamma})^{\prime}(\tau)=H^{\pm}_{\gamma-1}(\tau)-H^{\pm}_{\gamma+1}(\tau)\quad and\quad\tau(H^{\pm}_{\gamma})^{\prime}(\tau)=\tau H^{\pm}_{\gamma-1}(\tau)-\gamma H^{\pm}_{\gamma}(\tau).

(iv)

For large arguments
\tau\geq K>0
,
H^{\pm}_{\gamma}(\tau)
can be written as

H^{\pm}_{\gamma}(\tau)=e^{\pm i\tau}a_{\gamma}^{\pm}(\tau),

where
a_{\gamma}^{\pm}(\tau)\in S^{-\frac{1}{2}}(K,\infty)
is a classical symbol of order
-\frac{1}{2}
. More precisely, we have the following asymptotic behaviors
[
28
, Equations (10.2.5) and (10.2.6)]
:

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{+}(\tau)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\tau}}e^{i\left(\tau-\frac{1}{2}\gamma\pi-\frac{1}{4}\pi\right)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{\gamma}^{-}(\tau)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\tau}}e^{-i\left(\tau-\frac{1}{2}\gamma\pi-\frac{1}{4}\pi\right)}.

(v)

For small arguments
0<\tau\leq K<1
, we have

\displaystyle|H^{\pm}_{\gamma}(\tau)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\tau^{-|\gamma|}&\text{if}\quad\gamma\neq 0,\\
-\ln(\tau)&\text{if}\quad\gamma=0.\end{cases}
