On determinantal formulas for hermitian random matrices
Di YANG, Jiayi ZHAO, Jian ZHOU

Abstract
In this paper, we give a direct proof of determinantal formulas for connected k k -point functions for hermitian matrix models. We also give a new proof of KP integrability for them. From the viewpoint of KP hierarchy, we further give a new proof of the explicit formula for the corresponding affine coordinates. Furthermore, duality for some hermitian matrix models is proved.

Introduction

Consider a positive measure
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu
over
\mathbb{R}
. Let
\pi_{k}(\lambda)=\lambda^{k}+\cdots
,
k\geq 0
, be the associated monic orthogonal polynomials with respect to
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu
. Denote

h_{k}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\pi_{k}(\lambda)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\lambda),\qquad k\geq 0.

(1)

The orthogonal polynomials satisfy the following three-term recurrence relations:

\lambda\pi_{l}(\lambda)=\pi_{l+1}(\lambda)+\beta_{l}\pi_{l}(\lambda)+\gamma_{l}\pi_{l-1}(\lambda),\quad l\geq 1,

(2)

where
\beta_{l},\gamma_{l}\in\mathbb{R}
. In the 19th century, motivated from numerical integration and approximation theory, the Christoffel–Darboux kernel
K_{n}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
was introduced:

K_{n}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\frac{\pi_{l}(\lambda_{1})\pi_{l}(\lambda_{2})}{h_{l}},\quad n\geq 0,

(3)

which was shown
[
11
,
12
]
to satisfy the following identity: for any
n\geq 1
,

K_{n}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\frac{\pi_{n}(\lambda_{1})\pi_{n-1}(\lambda_{2})-\pi_{n-1}(\lambda_{1})\pi_{n}(\lambda_{2})}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}.

(4)

Since the mid-twentieth century, to understand energy level repulsion in complex heavy nuclei, Wigner, Dyson, Mehta and others established the random matrix theory, where the probability distributions of eigenvalues are important subjects. Dyson
[
17
]
studied the joint probability density of
k
eigenvalues of the hermitian matrix with respect to
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu

\rho_{k}(n;\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-k}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\lambda_{k+1})\cdots\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\lambda_{n})}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\lambda_{1})\cdots\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\lambda_{n})},

(5)

and further showed
[
18
]
that it can be written as a determinant using the Christoffel–Darboux kernel:

\rho_{k}(n;\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})=\frac{1}{(n-k+1)_{k}}\det(K_{n}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{k},

(6)

where
(n)_{k}:=n(n+1)\cdots(n+k-1)
denotes the increasing Pochhammer symbol.

The notion of correlators was known
[
32
,
33
]
to have important relations with field theory of strong interactions. For the Gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE), where
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\lambda)=e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

k
-point correlators

k
-point correlators. Explicit formula for

29
]
. Explicit formula for

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{k-1}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{i+1},\lambda_{i})\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{y-\lambda_{1}}\mathcal{J}_{n}(y,\lambda_{k})K_{n}(y,\lambda_{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{k-1}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{i+1},\lambda_{i})\right)\left(\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{k})\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1})+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{1}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\right)\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{i+1},\lambda_{i})+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{1}}\right)\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{k})\prod_{i=2}^{k-1}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{i+1},\lambda_{i})\right).

(79)

Thus

\displaystyle\ell_{k,(k)}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell_{k,(k-1)}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{1}}\right)\ell_{k-1,(k-1)}(\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{i+1},\lambda_{i})+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{1}}\right)\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{k})\prod_{i=2}^{k-1}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{i+1},\lambda_{i})\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{1}}\right)\left((-1)^{k-1}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{k})\prod_{i=2}^{k-1}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{i+1},\lambda_{i})-\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{i+1},\lambda_{i})-\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})}.

(80)

The lemma is proved.

Lemma 7
.

For
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\ell_{k}(n;\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\lambda_{\sigma(k)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{\sigma(i)},\lambda_{\sigma(i+1)})+\frac{\delta_{k,2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\sum_{1\leq a_{1}<\cdots<a_{t}\leq k}\left(\sum_{\sqcup_{s=1}^{t}P_{s}=\mathbf{Z}_{k}\backslash\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{t}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}Q_{a_{i},P_{i}}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})\right)\frac{1}{t}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{t}}\ell_{t}(n;\lambda_{a_{\sigma(1)}},\cdots,\lambda_{a_{\sigma(t)}}).

(97)

Proof.

By (
83
), noting that
\ell_{k}
and
\ell_{k,(k)}
are invariant under the cyclic group
C_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}/C_{k}}\ell_{k}(\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\lambda_{\sigma(k)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}/C_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{J}_{n}(\lambda_{\sigma(i)},\lambda_{\sigma(i+1)})-\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}/C_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}-\lambda_{\sigma(i+1)}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}/C_{k}}\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\sum_{1\leq a_{1}<\cdots<a_{t}\leq k}R_{\sigma(a_{1}),\cdots,\sigma(a_{t})}(\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\lambda_{\sigma(k)})\ell_{t}(\lambda_{\sigma(a_{1})},\cdots,\lambda_{\sigma(a_{t})}).

(98)

For
k\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}/C_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}-\lambda_{\sigma(i+1)}}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k-1}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}-\lambda_{\sigma(i+1)}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{\sigma(k-1)}-\lambda_{k}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\sigma(1)}-\lambda_{k}}\right)=0.

(99)

Given
1\leq a_{1}<\cdots<a_{t}\leq k
,
\ell_{t}(\lambda_{a_{\sigma(1)}},\cdots,\lambda_{a_{\sigma(t)}})
has the same rational function before it for any
\sigma\in S_{t}/C_{t}
, which is

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqcup_{s=1}^{t}P_{s}=\mathbf{Z}_{k}\backslash\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{t}\}\\
P_{s}:=\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{g_{s}}\}\end{subarray}}\prod_{s=1}^{t}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{g_{s}}}\left(\prod_{b=1}^{g_{s}-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{\sigma(b)}}-\lambda_{p_{\sigma(b+1)}}}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{\sigma(g_{s})}}-\lambda_{a_{s}}}.

(100)

Now we claim that for any set
P=\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{s}\}\subset\mathbf{Z}_{k}
and
a\in\mathbf{Z}_{k}\backslash P

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{s}}\left(\prod_{b=1}^{s-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{\sigma(b)}}-\lambda_{p_{\sigma(b+1)}}}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{\sigma(s)}}-\lambda_{a}}=\prod_{b=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{b}}-\lambda_{a}},

(101)

where the RHS is just
Q_{a,P}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})
. We prove (
101
) by induction on s. The base case
s=1
is straightforward. Assume that (
101
) holds for
s\geq 1
. Then for
s+1
, we consider the two sides of (
101
) as meromorphic functions in
\lambda_{a}
, treating the other
\lambda_{j}
’s as fixed parameters. Both rational functions possess only simple poles at
\lambda_{p_{1}},\cdots,\lambda_{p_{s+1}}
. Therefore, it suffices to verify that the residues at these poles match. Without loss of generality, we focus on the coefficient of the term
\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{s+1}}-\lambda_{a}}
. Specifically, the coefficient on the LHS of (
101
) is

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{s+1}\\
\sigma(s+1)=s+1\end{subarray}}\prod_{b=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{\sigma(b)}}-\lambda_{p_{\sigma(b+1)}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{s}}\left(\prod_{b=1}^{s-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{\sigma(b)}}-\lambda_{p_{\sigma(b+1)}}}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{\sigma(s)}}-\lambda_{p_{s+1}}}=\prod_{b=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p_{b}}-\lambda_{p_{s+1}}},

(102)

which is the same as the coefficient on the RHS of (
101
). These prove the lemma.
∎

Now we are ready to finish the proof of Theorem
1
. For
k\geq 2

\displaystyle C_{k}(n;\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\text{partition }\Pi\text{ of }\mathbf{Z}_{k}}\sum_{(a_{1},\cdots,a_{|\Pi|})\in\Pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\Pi_{|\Pi|}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{|\Pi|}Q_{a_{i},\Pi_{i}\backslash\{a_{i}\}}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})\right)\frac{1}{|\Pi|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{|\Pi|}}\ell_{|\Pi|}(n;\lambda_{a_{\sigma(1)}},\cdots,\lambda_{a_{\sigma(|\Pi|)}})

(103)

where the last equation follows from (
97

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{K}
the adjoint matrix of

\displaystyle-\frac{a_{j-i-1}(j-2)}{h_{j-2}}-\frac{a_{n-i+1}(n)a_{n-j}(n-1)-a_{n-i}(n-1)a_{n-j+1}(n)}{h_{n-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=j-1}^{n-1}\frac{a_{k-i+2}(k)a_{k-j+1}(k)-a_{k-i+1}(k)a_{k-j+2}(k)}{h_{k}}=0.

(128)

By (

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{k}}(a_{k-i+2}(k)a_{k-j+1}(k)-a_{k-i+1}(k)a_{k-j+2}(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{k}}\biggl(\left(a_{k-i+2}(k+1)+\beta_{k}a_{k-i+1}(k)+\frac{h_{k}}{h_{k-1}}a_{k-i}(k-1)\right)a_{k-j+1}(k)

Then the LHS of (

(131)

Thus the lemma is proved.

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{\Delta_{n-1}}\det H_{(0,1,\cdots,n-1;0,1,\cdots,\widehat{k},\cdots,n)},

4.2
Properties of connected correlators

We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For any integers
i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\geq 1
,
\langle\tr M^{i_{1}}\cdots\tr M^{i_{k}}\rangle_{c}(n)
is a polynomial of
a_{j}(m)
,
1\leq j\leq i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}
,
n-1\leq m\leq n+i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}
. Here
a_{j}(m)
are defined in (
117
).

Proof.

First, note that
A_{0,j}(n)=-a_{j+1}(n)
. Also by comparing the coefficients of
x^{n}
and
x^{n-1}
in the three-term recurrence relation (
2
), we have that

\displaystyle\beta_{n}=

\displaystyle a_{1}(n)-a_{1}(n+1),

(140)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}=

\displaystyle a_{2}(n)-a_{2}(n+1)-\beta_{n}a_{1}(n),

(141)

are polynomials in
a_{j}(m)
,
j=1,2
,
m=n,n+1
.

Next,
A_{i,0}(n)=c_{i+1}(n+i)
, where
c_{j}(n)
are defined in (
117
). We prove that for any
j\geq 0
,
c_{j}(n)
is a polynomial in the coefficients of the orthogonal polynomials by induction on
j
. For
j=0
,
c_{0}(n)=1
. Assume that
c_{k}(n)
is a polynomial in the coefficients of the orthogonal polynomials for any
k<j
. Then by definition,

c_{j}(n)=-\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}c_{k}(n)a_{j-k}(n-k)

(142)

is a polynomial in
a_{j}(m)
. Hence
A_{i,0}(n)
is also a polynomial in
a_{j}(m)
,
1\leq j\leq i+1
,
n\leq m\leq n+i
.

Finally, letting
A_{0,-1}(n)=-a_{0}(n)=-1
and
A_{-1,0}(n)=c_{0}(n)=1
, for
k,l\geq 1
,

T_{k,l}(n)=-A_{0,l-1}(n)A_{k-1,0}(n)+A_{0,l-2}(n-1)A_{k-2,0}(n+1)\gamma_{n}

(143)

is a polynomial in
a_{j}(m)
,
1\leq j\leq\max\{2,k,l\}
,
n-1\leq m\leq\max\{n+1,n+k-1\}
. Thus by (
120
),
A_{k,l}(n)
are polynomials in
a_{j}(m)
,
1\leq j\leq k+l+1
,
n-1\leq m\leq n+k+l
. Now by comparing the coefficients in (
19
), we obtain the theorem.
∎

As a direct corollary, we obtain:

Corollary 2
.

If the orthogonal polynomials
\{\pi_{l}\}_{l\geq 0}
satisfy that for any
k\geq 0
,
a_{k}(n)
is rational (resp. polynomial, holomorphic, meromorphic) in
n
, then for any
i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\geq 1
,
\langle\tr M^{i_{1}}\cdots\tr M^{i_{k}}\rangle_{c}(n)
is rational (resp. polynomial, holomorphic, meromorphic) in
n
.

In introduction, we mentioned the KP dual for hermitian matrix models which is characterized by

\widetilde{\langle s_{\rho}\rangle}(n)=(-1)^{|\rho|}\langle s_{\rho^{\prime}}\rangle(-n),\qquad n\geq\max\{2,|\rho|\}.

(155)

Now we prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

The three-term recurrence coefficients associated to
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu
read

\beta_{n}=\beta,n\geq 0,\qquad\gamma_{n}=\begin{cases}\gamma,&n\geq 1,\\
0,&n=0.\end{cases}

(156)

The associated orthogonal polynomials
\pi_{n}
read

\pi_{n}(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}(n)\lambda^{n-k},\qquad\lambda^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}(n)\pi_{n-k}(\lambda),

(157)

where
a_{0}(n)\equiv 1,c_{0}(n)\equiv 1
, and for
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle a_{k}(n)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{[k/2]}(-1)^{k-j}\frac{\beta^{k-2j}\gamma^{j}}{j!(k-2j)!}(n-k+1)_{k-j},

(158)

\displaystyle c_{k}(n)=

\displaystyle(n-k+1)\sum_{j=0}^{[k/2]}\frac{\beta^{k-2j}\gamma^{j}}{j!(k-2j)!}(n-k+j+2)_{k-j-1}.

(159)

Let

\widetilde{a}_{k}(n):=c_{k}(-n-1+k).

(160)

Then the polynomials

\widetilde{\pi}_{n}(\lambda):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\widetilde{a}_{k}(n)\lambda^{n-k},\qquad n\geq 0,

(161)

satisfy the three-term recurrence relation

\lambda\widetilde{\pi}_{n}(\lambda)=\widetilde{\pi}_{n+1}(\lambda)+\widetilde{\beta}_{n}\widetilde{\pi}_{n}(\lambda)+\widetilde{\gamma}_{n}\widetilde{\pi}_{n-1}(\lambda),\qquad n\geq 0,

(162)

where for
n\geq 0
,

\widetilde{\beta}_{n}=\beta,\qquad\widetilde{\gamma}_{n}=\begin{cases}\gamma+\delta_{n,1}\gamma,&n\geq 1,\\
0,&n=0.\end{cases}

(163)

The corresponding measure is
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}
.
Let

\widetilde{c}_{k}(n):=a_{k}(-n-1+k).

(164)

Then

\lambda^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\widetilde{c}_{k}(n)\widetilde{\pi}_{n-k}(\lambda).

(165)

For the hermitian matrix models
M,\widetilde{M}
associated to
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}
, the affine coordinates satisfy

\widetilde{A}_{0,i}(n)=-A_{i,0}(-n),\qquad\widetilde{A}_{i,0}(n)=-A_{0,i}(-n),\qquad|n|\geq i.

(166)

Thus for
|n|\geq\max\{2,i,j\}
,

\widetilde{T}_{i,j}(n)=T_{j,i}(-n).

(167)

Therefore, for
|n|\geq\max\{2,i,j\}
,

\widetilde{A}_{i,j}(n)=-A_{j,i}(-n).

(168)

Then using Theorem
1
, for
|n|\geq\max\{2,i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}\}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\langle\tr M^{i_{1}}\cdots\tr M^{i_{k}}\rangle}_{c}(n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\langle\tr M^{i_{1}}\cdots\tr M^{i_{k}}\rangle_{c}(-n),

(169)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\langle\tr M^{i_{1}}\cdots\tr M^{i_{k}}\rangle}(n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\langle\tr M^{i_{1}}\cdots\tr M^{i_{k}}\rangle(-n).

(170)

Now for partition
\rho=(\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{k})
,
l(\rho):=k,|\rho|:=\rho_{1}+\cdots+\rho_{k}
. Let
p_{\rho}(M):=\prod_{j=1}^{l(\rho)}\tr M^{\rho_{j}}
. Then

\widetilde{\langle p_{\rho}\rangle}(n)=(-1)^{l(\rho)}\langle p_{\rho}\rangle(-n),\qquad|n|\geq\max\{2,|\rho|\}.

(171)

By Frobenius character formula
[
39
, Section 1.7]
, the Schur polynomial of
\rho
reads

s_{\rho}=\sum_{|\lambda|=|\rho|}\frac{\chi^{\rho}_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}}p_{\lambda},

(172)

where
\chi^{\rho}_{\lambda}
is the character of the irreducible representation of the symmetric group
S_{|\rho|}
on
\lambda
. By
[
39
, Section 1.7]
,

\chi_{\lambda}^{\rho}=\chi_{\lambda}^{\rho^{\prime}}(-1)^{|\lambda|-l(\lambda)},

(173)

where
\rho^{\prime}
is the conjugate partition of
\rho
. Then for
|n|\geq\max\{2,|\rho|\}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\langle s_{\rho}\rangle}(n)=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\lambda|=|\rho|}\frac{\chi^{\rho}_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}}(-1)^{l(\lambda)}\langle p_{\lambda}\rangle(-n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\lambda|=|\rho|}\frac{\chi^{\rho^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(-1)^{|\lambda|-l(\lambda)}}{z_{\lambda}}(-1)^{l(\lambda)}\langle p_{\lambda}\rangle(-n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\rho|}\sum_{|\lambda|=|\rho^{\prime}|}\frac{\chi^{\rho^{\prime}}_{\lambda}}{z_{\lambda}}\langle p_{\lambda}\rangle(-n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\rho|}\langle s_{\rho^{\prime}}\rangle(-n).

(174)

Thus
\widetilde{M}
is the conjugate dual of
M
.
∎

Remark 6

(Relations with Kerov map)
.

The Jacobi fraction of
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu
writes

J(t)=\dfrac{1}{1-\beta t-\dfrac{\gamma t^{2}}{1-\beta t-\dfrac{\gamma t^{2}}{\cdots}}}.

(175)

Then

J(t)=\frac{1}{1-\beta t}E\left(\frac{t}{1-\beta t}\right),

(176)

where

E(z):=\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{\gamma z^{2}}{1-\dfrac{\gamma z^{2}}{\cdots}}}.

(177)

Consider the Kerov map
[
36
,
61
]

\mathcal{K}:J(t)\mapsto 1+t\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\log J(t).

(178)

Then let
z:=\frac{t}{1-\beta t}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(J)(t)=

\displaystyle 1+\beta z+(1+\beta z)z\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\log E(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\beta z+(1+\beta z)(\mathcal{K}(E)(z)-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\beta z)\mathcal{K}(E)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\beta t}\mathcal{K}(E)\left(\frac{t}{1-\beta t}\right).

(179)

Now
E(z)
satisfies the relation

E(z)=\frac{1}{1-\gamma z^{2}E(z)},\qquad E(0)=1,

(180)

which implies that

E(z)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\gamma z^{2}}}{2\gamma z^{2}}.

(181)

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(E)(z)=

\displaystyle 1+z\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\log E(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4\gamma z^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-2\gamma z^{2}E(z)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{2\gamma z^{2}}{1-\dfrac{\gamma z^{2}}{1-\dfrac{\gamma z^{2}}{\cdots}}}}.

(182)

Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(J)(t)=\dfrac{1}{1-\beta t-\dfrac{2\gamma t^{2}}{1-\beta t-\dfrac{\gamma t^{2}}{1-\beta t-\dfrac{\gamma t^{2}}{\cdots}}}}.

(183)

This is exactly the Jacobi fraction of
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}
.

When
\beta=0,\gamma=1
, the corresponding Jacobi fractions are the generating functions of Dyck paths and of grand Dyck paths. When
\beta=1,\gamma=1
, the corresponding Jacobi fractions are the generating functions of Motzkin paths and of grand Motzkin paths. When
\beta=2,\gamma=1
, the corresponding Jacobi fractions are related to the generating functions of Catalan numbers of type A and of type B. When
\beta=3,\gamma=2
, the corresponding Jacobi fractions are related to the generating functions of big Schröder paths and of central Delannoy paths. These examples are conjectured in
[
61
,
62
]
, which are now proved.
