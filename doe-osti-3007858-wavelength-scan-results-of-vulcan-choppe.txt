 ABSTRACT

 We scanned VULCAN chopper by different wavelength band center and band width. Three commonly used
settings 60, 30, and 20 Hz are investigated for existing detector banks. The full scanned results in wavelength unit are
included in this report. From the result, one can decide what chopper setting can be used to meet experiment
requirement while avoiding time-of-flight frame overlapping or leakage, which generate artifacts in collected neutron
data.

 1. Introduction

 The VULCAN instrument [1] at the SNS, ORNL, is a diffractometer and located on beamline 7 of FTS and
uses high resolution ambient water moderator of 30x30 mm2. The instrument flight path is 43.75 m and several
detectors at different 2 theta diffraction angles are used to collect wide q range diffractions. The source frequency is
60 Hz, and two pairs of double disc choppers are equipped for flexible instrument operation as the opening window
can be changed. The first pair resides at 6.551m and 6.561 m from the source, and the second pair resides at 9.616 m
and 9.626 m from the source. Of each pair, the upstream one spins in clockwise direction and the downstream one
spins in counterclockwise direction. This spinning allows the chopper opening window to be adjustable. The first pair
of double disc choppers are used mainly for experiment to choose different incident energy while the second pair
mainly serve as the emergency backup for the first pair. In operation, 20, 30, and 60 Hz chopper settings with
different wavelength band centers are used to provide max band width allowable for broad materials science and
engineering research. The chopper serves to clean frame overlap or leakage for such long time-of-flight
diffractometer. The chopper’s performance can be modeled based on the engineering design[2], but the practical
performance needs to be commissioned considering the potential engineering errors and other unexpected factors.
Because of the flexibility of the double disc chopper, chopper scan with different settings, such as various frequencies,
different band width, and different band center, provides a full picture of neutron energy distribution collected at
detector locations. And the scanned results serve as a good reference to select most appropriate chopper settings
without frame overlap or leakage to ensure the reliable neutron data collection. In this report we include the
comprehensive scan of the first double disc chopper by measuring a pure incoherent scattering sample VNb alloy
which does not show observable Bragg peaks thanks to the neutron null scattering material design [3], where the
addition of Nb cancel the weak coherent diffraction peaks from V.

 2. Experiment

 The VNb alloy was used for measuring the scattering of different chopper settings. We changed the chopper
speeds at 20, 30 and 60 Hz while the band width was set from, 2.92 to 4.32 Å, 1.48 to 2.88 Å, and 0.24 to 1.44 Å, and
the band center was set from 2.8 to 4.0 Å, 2.0 to 4.0 Å, 1.5 to 3.0 Å, respectively. The band center wavelength change
step is nominally 0.2 Å while a couple of scan steps are increased at the ends of the scan range for 30 and 60 Hz
chopper speeds. The details are listed in the tables in chapter 3. Measurement time is 5, 4, 3 mins of neutron time for
20, 30, 60 Hz, respectively, to ensure enough statistics collected at detectors. A measurement scan table was built in
the instrument control system EPICS while the SNS accelerator operated at a nominal 2 MW power. The scattering
data were histogrammed in neutron wavelength by taking the corrections of detector pixel geometrical locations,
which are built in the VDRIVE software [4]. The data reduction process is straight forward. First, bin the tof data at
sample position by using VBIN by setting the keyword “sample_tof=1”, and to plot the intensity data in wavelength,
use the VIEW command by setting “wavelen =1” and “maxv =9” to generate the intensity data plot in wavelength.
We plot all the measurement of the results collected in bank 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 which have the center 2-theta angles of -
90o, 90 o, 120 o, 150 o, and -65 o, respectively. The full spectrum of VULCAN was also measured when SNS operates
at 10 Hz frequency of the accelerator. At lower frequency, the chance of pulse frame overlap is greatly reduced.
 We show below the results the sub sections, and highlight the chopper settings where no obvious frame
overlap or leakage is observed while maximizing the bandwidth.

 3. Results

 The spectrum measured while the accelerator operated at 10 Hz is shown in Fig. 3.0.1 in both regular scale and
logarithmic scale of intensity. The neutron optics setting is high intensity guide and the No. 9 guide [5]. The neutron
wavelength intensity distribution changes with different guide settings, but the TOF range will not be impacted much.
As shown in Fig. 3.0.1, the presented range of the neutron wavelength is from ~0.5 to 8.5 Å with 1.5 Å being the
highest flux. Longer wavelength above 8.5 Å is available, but the absolute flux is too low to be useful for practical
operation. The spectrum collected at low frequency accelerator operation serves as a clean reference of the instrument
spectrum.

3.1 RESUTLS OF 20 HZ CHOPPER SPEED

 Table 1 lists the scanned band width at different band centers. The max band width is 4.32 Å, which is the
standard band width of 20 Hz in operation and the standard band center is 2.8 Å. When shifting the band center to a
larger value 3.0 Å, leakage starts to show up immediately although the max allowable wavelength range increases. To
use a larger wavelength while avoiding leakage or band overlap, smaller band width needs to be considered. For
example, when band width is 3.92 Å, leakage starts to show up at 3.4 Å band center, the max allowable wavelength
increases from ~4.5 to 5.0 Å. When band width reduces to 3.12 Å, leakage was not seen for all the shifted band
centers. This however is at the cost of reduced band width, and subsequently reduced d or q range.

 4. Summary

 We showed the wavelength intensity distribution of different chopper settings of VULCAN. The effective band
width without neutron leakage from the chopper depends on the band center. When shifting center wavelength to a
larger band center to cover larger wavelength, a reduction of band width may be required to avoid leakage or frame
overlap. This does not depend on the detectors but each detector may have different d or q coverage thanks to its
location and coverage. When choosing chopper setting not included in this report, a quick assessment of the chopper
setting can be done by measuring a VNb sample and using VDRIVE commands to bin the data and viewing them in
wavelength. The data in d scale may lead wrong decision in selecting chopper setting because the time focused data
smear the leaked neutrons in diffraction patterns.
