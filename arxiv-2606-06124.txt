Two discs and a missing triangle: the maximally extended Kerr black hole revisited
M. A. H. MacCallum

Abstract
The region spaning the ring singularity in the Kerr black hole solution is described here as two (flat) discs rather than one. From this viewpoint it follows that the usual depiction of the worldsheet θ = π / 2 \theta=\pi/2 , ϕ \phi constant (in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates) is missing (multiple copies of) the region r < 0 r<0 , representable as a triangle in the usual conformal diagram style.

Introduction

Despite containing two commuting Killing vectors, and therefore
depending on only two essential coordinates, the Kerr black hole solution’s
geometry and its physical consequences have been
surprisingly tricky to understand. There have been many papers
exploring various aspects, and advanced textbooks discuss the
solution and its properties.

Surfaces of constant Boyer and Lindquist coordinates

Kerr and Schild
(
16

15

had considered metrics of
the form
g_{ab}=\eta_{ab}+2Hk_{a}k_{b}
where
\eta_{ab}

one of the PNDs of the Kerr solution. Boyer and Lindquist denote the similar
coordinate system based on the other PND by

Boyer and Lindquist then pass to a form they describe as “a generalization of the
Eddington form of the Schwarzschild metric”, which they call the (E) frame
(their (2.7)). Later they transform again to what they call
“Schwarzschild-like coordinates” (their (2.13)), which are now known
as Boyer-Lindquist coordinates and in which the metric is (
1.1

Boyer and Lindquist discuss in their Sect. 2 a pictorial
representation of surfaces of constant

\theta

are diffeomorphic to but in general not isometric to the corresponding
surfaces in the Kerr geometry. In this representation, the constant

surfaces are confocal ellipsoids. (For
3m^{2}/4<a^{2}<m^{2}

is misleading in that the Gaussian curvature of the event horizon
becomes negative near the poles

23

25

The constant

surfaces (other than the axis
\theta=0

represented by half-hyperboloids of one sheet, confocal to the
ellipsoids. The “half” arises because Boyer and Lindquist, at this point in their

The constant

Apropos of the limit

Using the specialization of (

The maximal analytic extension and its missing triangle

To present the full MAE in a good way is not simple. It helps that the angular
coordinate
\phi
is ignorable, and all diagrams in the literature do
ignore it, so each point shown is really a circle of points. I shall
follow the same practice here. Following
5
, the temporal and
radial structure is usually presented in two-dimensional conformal
diagrams of worldsheets with one space and one time dimension, such as
Fig.
1
: these are surfaces of constant
\theta
and
\phi
.

In
6
, which appeared after that of Boyer and Lindquist, the global
structure for the Kerr-Newman family is considered. By adaptation and
generalization of the arguments in
5
, Carter gave the
more complicated coordinate transformations required to find the
four-dimensional maximal analytic extension (MAE).

That paper also integrates the geodesics using the Carter constant, a
quadratic found by considering separability of the Hamilton-Jacobi
equations (see also
6
;
7
), which was later shown to
arise from a Killing tensor
(
26
)
.

Carter could then show that all geodesics in the extended manifolds
that do not reach the singularities (plural here due to the infinitely
many repeats of the basic blocks) are complete, and that causal
geodesics that do reach the singularities lie in the equatorial
planes. The paper did not include diagrams like those of the 1966
paper, which had by then been generalized by Boyer and Lindquist.

Carter further showed that in a region near the singular ring the
periodic Killing vector
\partial_{\phi}
is timelike, so that there the
circles
0\leq\phi\leq 2\pi
are closed timelike lines and imply
causality violation. For more on that and the behaviour of geodesics

Further remarks

It has been clarified above that the two-surfaces spanning the ring
singularity in the Kerr black hole solution are a pair of discs, not
just one, and both are flat, and it has also been shown that the usual
conformal diagram for the
\theta=\pi/2
section of the MAE is missing
(multiple copies of) a triangle of points with
r<0
(in
Boyer and Lindquist coordinates).

A number of other recent developments relate to the issues discussed
above, and deserve mention to interested readers.
9

analytic extensions and the geometry of the Kerr family: these were
presented in Chruściel’s talk at the Carter Fest.
21

metric form for all Petrov type D solutions. In his talk at the
CarterFest, Riazuelo showed some enjoyable computer graphics of the
Kerr family MAEs (Cf.
22
.)

It is remarkable that 54 years on from Carter’s seminal 1966 paper, the MAE
of the Kerr solution remains an object of study prompting new work.
