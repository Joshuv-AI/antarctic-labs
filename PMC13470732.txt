Enhancing Ammonium-Ion Storage Capability of NH4V3O8 via an Interlayer Defect-Modulation Strategy
Authors: Yu-Ting Wang, Pengcheng Su, Kang Zhang, Duolin Xu, Wenhui Chen, Mengyan Zhang, Hongwei Tang, Yihui Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ammonium vanadates (NVO) have presented great potential in serving as host materials for ammonium ions, yet they are still impeded by sluggish ammonium-ion kinetics. Herein, an interlayer cation-modulation strategy is proposed to promote the ammonium-ion storage capability of the NH4V3O8 cathode. By rationally modulating the number of interlayer ammonium ions, the proposed NVO possesses an expanded interlayer distance and weakened electrostatic repulsion between the stored NH4 + and the NVO host, resulting in enhanced performance in both the three-electrode system and full-cell system. This work is anticipated to boost the application potential of NVO cathodes in aqueous ammonium-ion batteries.

The escalating demands of inherently safe energy storage systems accelerate the development of aqueous batteries. , To date, several kinds of aqueous metallic carriers have been explored, such as Na+, K+, Zn2+, and Ca2+. − As promising alternatives, aqueous nonmetallic carriers, including H+, H3O+, and NH4 +, have gained the researchers’ interest in recent years by virtue of their smaller ionic sizes and fast diffusion kinetics in aqueous electrolytes. Among those nonmetallic carriers, NH4 + possesses a low molar mass (18 g mol–1) and the smallest hydrated ionic size of 3.31 Å, guaranteeing its rapid transfer in rocking-chair aqueous ammonium-ion batteries (AIBs). Besides, compared to H+ and H3O+, NH4 + owns comparatively moderate acidity, which is conducive to creating a mildly acidic or neutral electrolyte environment and thus resulting in the mitigated side reaction of the hydrogen evolution reaction and less corrosion to cells. Therefore, AIBs exhibit great potential in inherently safe energy storage systems for grid-scale energy-related applications in the future. Nevertheless, AIBs still suffer from relatively lower specific capacities, and NH4 + host materials with favorable conductivity, abundant active sites, and migration pathways are highly desirable to further promote the potential of AIBs.

To date, various electrode materials of AIBs have been explored, including the Prussian blue analogues, vanadium-based oxides, manganese-based oxides, and 2D materials (such as MoS2 and COFs). , Specifically, most vanadium-based oxides possess typical layered structures with open frameworks, variable valence states of vanadium, and abundant active oxygen sites, enabling them to be regarded as appealing electrode materials of AIBs, and they have substantiated their potential in storing ammonium ions in recent years. By virtue of the enlarged interlayer distance and strengthened structural stability, ammonium vanadates (NVO), such as NH4V3O8 and NH4V4O10, are regarded as the most promising host materials for NH4 + among those vanadium-based cathodes. , However, the excess NH4 + in the [VOn] layers of NVO would magnify its electrostatic interaction with the intercalated NH4 +, resulting in sluggish kinetics and inferior NH4 + storage capability of NVO and further limiting their potential in AIBs.

To strengthen the competitiveness of NVO-based electrodes in energy storage systems, numerous molecular engineering strategies have been proposed, and the defect engineering strategy is regarded as an effective and facile way to significantly improve their electrochemical properties. In our previous study, we suggested the great potential of modulating the number of interlayer NH4 + deficiencies in alleviating the Zn2+ storage capability of NVO, and this strategy may also be able to improve its NH4 + storage performance. Herein, we have used the same strategy on NVO and revealed its specific influence on boosting the NH4 + storage performance of the NVO cathode. Consequently, by rationally tailoring the defect number of interlayer NH4 +, the interlayer distance of NVO is significantly expanded and more active sites are available for external NH4 + to be stored, leading to the enhanced NH4 + storage capability of the NVO cathode in both the three-electrode and full-cell systems.

Results and Discussion

The modulation of interlayer NH4 + defects can be realized by a facile calcination process (Figure S1). By altering the corresponding calcination temperatures, the number of NH4 + defects in the [VOn] layers can be tailored, accompanied by the variation of its morphology and structure. To verify the NH4 + removal process of NVO during calcination procedure, the thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) test was conducted (Figure a), and two weight loss stages were observed: the first weight loss stage (∼0.3%, 20–250 °C) is due to the loss of adsorbed water and crystal water, while the main weight loss stage at 300–400 °C (∼9.2%) is caused by the transformation of interlayer NH4 + to the released NH3; hence, 325 °C, 350 °C, and 375 °C were selected to reveal the relationship between NH4 + deficiency and NH4 + storage capability (denoted as 325NVO, 350NVO, and 375NVO, respectively). In the Raman spectra, those four samples possess the same characteristic peaks with different intensities (Figure b), which may be ascribed to the variation of crystallinity integrity and morphologies caused by the calcination process. The subsequent X-ray diffraction (XRD, Figure c) further confirms the structural variation of NVO during the calcination process. The pristine NVO owns the typical peaks of the standard card of NH4V3O8 (JCPDS: 88-1473), indicating its favorable purity and crystallinity. By contrast, the (001) peaks of 325NVO and 350NVO shift to a lower angle of 8.0° (corresponding to the interlayer distance of ∼11 Å), accompanied by the emergence of the characteristic peaks of V2O5 (JCPDS: 41-1426), suggesting the enlarged interlayer distance and the formation of vanadium oxides due to partial removal of interlayer NH4 +. With the rise of calcination temperature, the excess removal of NH4 + at the higher calcination temperature would aggravate its irreversible transformation to V2O5 with the decreased interlayer distance (375NVO) and inferior structural stability (and it would completely convert to the vanadium oxides at 500 °C), which is adverse to its ammonium-ion storage capability. To quantify the content of V2O5 in various calcined NVO samples, the Rietveld-refined XRD analyses are conducted (Figure d–f). With the rise of calcination temperature, the content of V2O5 increases from 36.3% (weight ratio) to 42.6%, which is in accordance with the results of a previous report. Generally, V2O5 possesses comparatively large active sites by virtue of its abundant hydrogen-bond sites (O sites) but suffers from poor structural stability. In contrast, the NVO has enhanced structural stability because of the existence of interlayer NH4 + “pillars”, while those NH4 + “pillars” may also lead to the enlarged electrostatic repulsion effect and the reduced accommodation space for external NH4 +, resulting in inferior kinetics and electrochemical properties. Consequently, a moderate calcination temperature is desired to balance the NH4 + storage sites and structural stability. To verify the influence of calcination on the valence variation of NVO, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) tests were conducted. Specifically, the content of V4+ in NVO is significantly increased (Figure g), and the area ratio of V5+/V4+ is decreased from 2.568 (pristine NVO) to 1.026 (350NVO), which may be caused by the generation of oxygen defects during the calcination process. Meanwhile, the relevant O 1s spectra further verify the increased content of oxygen defects (∼531.4 eV), and the ratio of defect oxygen/lattice oxygen is increased from 22.38% to 23.09% (Figure h), demonstrating the existence of extra oxygen defects created by calcination treatment. Generally, the existence of oxygen defects in NVO is deemed an advantageous factor for its electrochemical properties since it can weaken the electrostatic interaction between O2– and the intercalated ions and promote the conductivity of NVO. In the N 1s spectra (Figure i), a distinct N 1s peak is detected at ∼401.5 eV, corresponding to the characteristic peak of NH4 + in NVO. In contrast, the N 1s signal of 350NVO shows decreased intensity and slightly shifts to the higher binding energy area, which may be ascribed to the removal of NH4 + and the reconstruction of the [VOn] frame due to the partial reduction of V5+. Note that this phenomenon is also found in other reported NVO-based electrodes with NH4 + defects. In addition, compared to pristine NVO, the signal intensity of N 1s of all the calcined NVOs is significantly reduced (Figure S2), and the N/V atomic ratio is decreased from 0.52 (NVO) to 0.17 (375NVO), indicating the successful and controllable removal of NH4 + after the calcination process (Figure S3). , Besides the crystal structures, the morphology of NVO is also influenced by the calcination treatment (Figure S4). The pristine NVO shows typical bundle-like morphology, while 325NVO and 350NVO present the aggregated bulk structures due to the calcination treatment. However, 375NVO exhibits a fragmentized morphology, which can be ascribed to the structural collapse of NVO caused by the excess removal of NH4 +. In addition, the morphology variation also leads to differences in their surface areas; the surface area of NVO increases with the rise of the calcination temperature (the degree of interlayer NH4 + deficiency), and 375NVO exhibits the largest surface area of 3.78 m2 g–1. Though the larger surface area is beneficial to electrolyte infiltration, structural stability plays a more important role in promoting the electrochemical properties of active materials. Furthermore, the high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) test is conducted to reveal the crystal structure of 350NVO (Figure j,k). Specifically, 350NVO possesses a significantly enlarged interlayer distance of 11.2 Å, which is in favor of the intercalation of NH4 +, and this phenomenon is in accordance with the XRD spectra. In addition, all the elements are uniformly distributed in 350NVO, indicating its successful fabrication.

To verify the enhanced ammonium-ion storage capability of the calcined NVO samples, their electrochemical properties are first tested in a three-electrode system by adopting the Pt and saturated calomel electrode (SCE) as the counter electrode and reference electrode, respectively, with a 1 M (NH4)2SO4 solution as the electrolyte. To further probe the optimal calcination temperature of NVO, the electrochemical properties of extra 340 NVO and 360NVO are tested, and the performances of pristine NVO and pure V2O5 are also revealed as a comparison (Figure S6). Typically, the pristine NVO exhibits three pairs of redox peaks (−0.6/–0.25 V, −0.4/0 V, 0.47/0.53 V, Figure a), corresponding to the multistep vanadium redox transitions. , However, the cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves of these five calcined NVO electrodes are quite different from the curve of pristine NVO: 340NVO, 350NVO, 360NVO, and 375NVO present similar CV curves, while 325NVO shows a transitional CV curve between pristine NVO and 350NVO. As mentioned before, the removal of NH4 + would lead to the partial transformation of NVO to vanadium oxides; in other words, the calcined NVO samples are mixtures which are comprised of NVO and vanadium oxides. Consequently, besides the typical redox peaks of NVO, the extra redox peaks (located at ∼0.3 V, 0.4 V, 0.6 V, and −0.1 V), which are contributed by the vanadium oxides (V2O5), − are also detected in these calcined NVO electrodes at higher temperatures, and this phenomenon is also found in NVO-based zinc-ion batteries (the pristine NVO and calcined NVO electrodes exhibit distinct discrepancies in their CV curves). Among those NVO electrodes, 350NVO owns the largest CV area, indicating its favorable ammonium-ion storage capability, which is also verified by the subsequent galvanostatic charge/discharge (GCD) tests. Specifically, the pristine NVO and pure V2O5 deliver the initial capacities of 150.3 and 234.7 mAh g–1, respectively; the capacities of 40.7 and 51.7 mAh g–1 are retained after 50 cycles at 0.1 A g–1 (Figures b and S7), while all the calcined NVO electrodes exhibit promoted ammonium-ion storage capabilities, and 350NVO has the best electrochemical properties with an initial capacity of 300.9 mAh g–1 and a reversible capacity of 100.4 mAh g–1 remaining after 50 cycles. However, all the NVO-based electrodes exhibit fast capacity loss at the initial stage (about the first ten cycles), which may be caused by the following reasons: (1) the significant volumetric expansion in V2O5 during the intercalation/deintercalation of large-radius NH4 +. V2O5 generally suffers from its inferior stability, and the introduction of large Zn2+ or NH4 + would lead to the volumetric expansion of the V2O5 lattice and the significant capacity loss at the initial stage. In fact, the pure V2O5 cathode also exhibits the obvious capacity loss at the initial stage (Figure S7). Due to the existence of V2O5, these calcined NVO electrodes would also be influenced by its inferior initial cycling stability. By contrast, the pristine NVO possesses comparatively slight capacity loss, which may be ascribed to the improved structural stability of NVO (without the existence of V2O5); (2) the irreversible deintercalation process of intercalated NH4 + in calcined NVO electrodes. The calcination process repels partial interlayer NH4 + and endows NVO with abundant NH4 + storage sites, while some of them may act as the “dead site”, and the deintercalation behavior of intercalated NH4 + is irreversible, resulting in fast capacity decay. This phenomenon is often found in those layered hosts with abundant artificial active sites, such as modified NVO and MoS2-based electrodes. ,, After the initial fast capacity-loss stage, those NVO-based electrodes show comparatively steady performance after about 10 cycles, and distinct plateaus are also detected in the GCD curve of 350NVO after 50 cycles (Figure c), suggesting its favorable stability and reversibility. Figure d presents the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) curves of four NVO electrodes, and all the curves are comprised of a semicircle in the high-frequency region and an oblique line in the low-frequency region, corresponding to the charge transfer resistance (R ct) and NH4 + diffusion impedance (Z w), respectively. Among those NVO electrodes, 350NVO presents the smallest R ct value and Warburg impedance (Figure S8), implying its enhanced NH4 + diffusion kinetics, which may be attributed to its enlarged interlayer distance and the larger active space for NH4 + to be accommodated. To reveal the NH4 + storage behaviors of 350NVO, the power-law equation (i = av b) is adopted to evaluate its b value by analyzing its CV curves at various scan rates (Figure e), and almost all the b values range from 0.5 to 1 (Figure f), indicating the coexistence of diffusion-controlled process and surface-dominated pseudocapacitive behavior. Furthermore, the capacity contribution is further quantified by using the following equation: i = k 1 v + k 2 v 1/2, where k 1 v and k 2 v 1/2 represent the pseudocapacitive contribution and diffusion contribution, respectively. For the 350NVO electrode, the diffusion process (the intercalation/deintercalation of NH4 +) plays a major role at the small scan rates (Figure S9). With the rise of the scan rate, the pseudocapacitive behavior gradually dominates the NH4 + storage process (65% at 10 mV s–1), which is beneficial to its rate performance.

To evaluate the practical potential of the proposed 350NVO, its electrochemical properties are also revealed in a full-cell system, and MoS2, which is regarded as a promising host for NH4 +, is hired as an anode material. By comparing their potential windows (Figure a), 0–1.4 V is adopted as the working voltage of the NVO//MoS2 system (Figure b), and distinct redox peaks are detected, indicating the typical intercalation/deintercalation processes of NH4 +. Moreover, the corresponding plateaus are also found in their GCD curves (Figure c), further confirming the intercalation/deintercalation behaviors of NH4 + in NVO and MoS2 hosts. Compared to pristine NVO, all the calcined NVO electrodes present enhanced capacity in full-cell systems (Figure S10). Specifically, 350NVO can deliver an initial capacity of 78.3 mAh g–1 at 0.1 A g–1, which is far larger than pristine NVO (25.8 mAh g–1) and pure V2O5 (27.5 mAh g–1), and a high reversible capacity of 71.7 mAh g–1 can be retained after 100 cycles, corresponding to a favorable capacity retention of 91.6% (Figure d). Moreover, 350NVO also exhibits the highest rate performance among those NVO-based full cells, and capacities of 70.2, 54.1, 41.1, 25.8, and 22.8 mAh g–1 can be delivered at 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, and 2 A g–1, respectively. When the current density returns to 0.1 A g–1, a stable capacity of 68.6 mAh g–1 is realized, corresponding to a superb retention of 97.7% (Figure e). By contrast, pristine NVO only possesses an inferior retention of 86.1% as the current density returns to 0.1 A g–1. Moreover, the proposed 350NVO//MoS2 full cell exhibits favorable long-term cycling stability with high Coulombic efficiencies (Figure f), and a reversible capacity of 46.9 mAh g–1 can be reached after 1500 cycles at 0.5 A g–1, suggesting its promoted structural stability. By analyzing its CV curves at various scan rates (Figure g), 350NVO still shows the intermediate b values (Figure h), implying its dual-mode NH4 + storage behavior in the three-electrode system remains in the full-cell system. Similarly, the diffusion-controlled process also dominates the capacity contribution at small scan rates (1 mV s–1) and possesses considerable contributions at the larger scan rates compared to other reported electrodes for AIBs (Figure i), − indicating the significance of the intercalation/deintercalation processes of NH4 + in 350NVO. By pairing with other promising anodes (such as organic HBF), its performance in a full-cell system may be further improved.

To decipher the NH4 + storage mechanism of 350NVO, the relevant ex situ characterizations are conducted in a full-cell system. In the ex situ XRD spectra (Figure a), the (001) peak of 350NVO shifts to a lower angle during the charging process, which may be caused by the intercalation of NH4 + in the 350NVO cathode, and it returns to the initial angle after the discharging process, implying the reversible intercalation/deintercalation of NH4 +. Moreover, other characteristic peaks show negligible variation during the electrochemical process, indicating the favorable structural stability of 350NVO. The intercalation process of NH4 + in 350NVO is also verified by the ex situ Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra (Figure b). The intensities of N–H signals (∼3150 and 1400 cm–1) and V = O···H vibrations (∼1050 cm–1) are gradually increased during the charging process, which can be ascribed to the intercalation of NH4 + and the formation of hydrogen bonds between 350NVO and the intercalated NH4 +, and their intensities decrease in the subsequent discharging process, suggesting the reversible behaviors of NH4 + during the electrochemical processes. In the ex situ XPS spectra (Figure c), the contents of V4+ and V–O–H are significantly increased at the fully charged state, which is caused by the introduction of NH4 + in the interlayer of 350NVO, and these intensities return to their initial states at the following fully discharged state, further confirming the high reversibility of the intercalation of NH4 +. In addition, only one peak located at 401.8 eV is detected in the N 1s spectrum at the initial state, corresponding to the N–H–O signal from the interaction between the interlayer NH4 + and [VOn] layers. At the fully charged state, an extra peak located at ∼399.7 eV is detected, corresponding to the −N–H- signal from NH3, which is the commonly existing form of the intercalated NH4 + in AIBs. ,, At the subsequent fully discharged state, the signal of −N–H- disappears, indicating the reversibility of the intercalated NH4 +. Consequently, the reversible intercalation/deintercalation behaviors of NH4 + in the 350NVO host are verified. Based on the above discussion, it can be concluded that the interlayer intercalation of NH4 + plays a significant role in contributing to the capacity of NVO. By releasing partial interlayer NH4 +, the calcined 350NVO is able to provide more interlayer space and active sites to accommodate external NH4 +, resulting in enhanced capacity and mitigated electrostatic repulsion between the interlayer NH4 + and intercalated NH4 + (Figure d). In addition, the residual interlayer NH4 + can serve as “pillars” to maintain the structural stability of NVO. Consequently, the proposed 350NVO exhibits comparable NH4 + storage capability (Table S1) and great potential in AIBs.

Conclusions

In summary, a controllable interlayer cation modulation strategy is realized in NVO via a facile calcination process. By tailoring the number of interlayer NH4 + deficiencies, the structural variation of NVO and the emergence of V2O5 synergistically endow 350NVO with an expanded interlayer space, extra NH4 + storage active sites, and enhanced structural stability, thus resulting in promoted NH4 + transfer kinetics and improved NH4 + storage capability in both the three-electrode and full-cell systems. This work demonstrates an effective way to improve the potential of NVO-based electrodes in AIBs.

Experimental Section

Materials

The pristine NH4VO3, (NH4)2SO4, and ammonium molybdate tetrahydrate ((NH4)6Mo7O24·4H2O)) powders were obtained from Shanghai Aladdin, and the thiourea powder was purchased from Shanghai Macklin. All chemicals were used directly without further purification.

Fabrication of NVO Samples

Typically, 1.18 g of NH4VO3 powder was added to 50 mL of DI water and stirred for 30 min at 60 °C to obtain a light-yellow solution. Then, a 2 M HCl solution was added to the above solution dropwise to adjust the pH value to 3, stirred for 30 min, and the solution gradually turned red. Afterward, the mixture was transferred into a Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave and heated at 160 °C for 24 h. The product was washed with ethanol and DI water several times, and the NH4V3O8 (NVO) precursor was prepared. Then, the NVO powder was placed into a tubular furnace and calcined at a specific temperature (325 °C, 350 °C, and 375 °C, denoted as 325NVO, 350NVO, and 375NVO, respectively) for 2 h with a heating rate of 2 °C min–1, and the proposed NVO samples with various NH4 + defects were obtained.

Fabrication of O-Doped MoS2

O-doped MoS2 was fabricated via a typical solvothermal method based on a previous report. Typically, 0.408 g of ammonium molybdate tetrahydrate ((NH4)6Mo7O24·4H2O) was dispersed in 20 mL of DI water and stirred for 1 h. Then, 0.754 g of thiourea (CH4N2S) was dissolved in 10 mL of DI water and stirred for 30 min, and then it was mixed with the above solution and stirred for another 30 min to obtain the homogeneous solution. Afterward, the mixed solution was transferred into a Teflon-lined stainless autoclave and kept at 180 °C for 24 h; the product was washed with DI water and ethanol several times and dried at 80 °C for 12 h; and the proposed O-doped MoS2 powder was obtained.

Characterizations

X-ray diffraction (XRD) with CuKα radiation (Bruker-D8 ADVANCE) was adopted to verify the crystal structures of the proposed NVO samples, and the morphologies and structures of the NVO were studied by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-6390LV) and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM, JEM 2100). The thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) was conducted on a NETZSCH.

An STA449C simultaneous thermal analyzer was used with protective argon gas. The Raman spectra and X-ray photoelectron spectra were measured by a Horiba HR Evolution and Thermo Scientific ESCALAB250Xi instruments, respectively.

Electrochemical Performance Tests

The typical slurry-coating procedures were used to fabricate the electrodes of AAIBs. Specifically, the NVO, Super P, and PVDF were thoroughly mixed with the weight ratio of 7:2:1 and ground in the N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP) solvent. Afterward, the slurry was coated on the Ti foil, dried in a vacuum oven at 80 °C for 12 h, and cut into circular electrodes with a diameter of 12 mm, with a mass loading of 1–1.6 mg cm–2; the fabrication of the MoS2 anode was similar to that of the NVO anode. The Whatman GF/D glass microfiber membrane was adopted as the separator, and a 1 M (NH4)2SO4 solution was chosen as the electrolyte. In the three-electrode system, the NVO electrode, Pt electrode, and saturated calomel electrode (SCE) were adopted as the working electrode, counter electrode, and reference electrode, respectively, while the full-cell tests were conducted on the assembled CR2032 coin cells with the mass loading ratio of 1:3 (NVO: MoS2, based on their CV areas in Figure a). The CHI660E electrochemical workstation was used to perform the cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests, and the LANHE CT 3002A battery test system was used to evaluate the electrochemical performance of the proposed electrodes; all specific capacities were calculated based on the weight of NVO in the NVO electrode.
