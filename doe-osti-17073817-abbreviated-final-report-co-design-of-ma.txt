 Co-design of Materials and Systems for Carbon Conversion
 Christopher Hahn

Project Overview
The energy transition requires that we transform the way chemicals and materials are
manufactured- electricity must be used to drive chemical transformations in electrochemical,
rather than thermochemical reactors. Today, these “electrolyzers” typically make desired
products at the cathode, but more than 80% of the energy is unavoidably used at the anode
where only a low value product (O2) is made. Here, we aimed to develop an electrolyzer that
leverages an alternative anode reaction that reduces that energy demand while generating
valuable products. We established a strategy to co-design electrolyzers by bridging optimization
of materials, systems, and processes through a multidisciplinary approach. We built new
foundational understanding of alcohol oxidation mechanisms and phase evolution behaviors,
developed new machine learning models to accelerate the discovery of novel catalysts, and
established a 1st of its kind multi-scale model connecting alcohol oxidation to industrially-relevant
conditions. These enabled us to deliver a paired electrolysis reactor with unprecedentedly low
energy input, while establishing a co-design framework that expands LLNL’s leadership in
optimization and materials.

Mission Impact
We delivered a co-design framework that enables more accurate digital twins, establishing new
tools to address DOE’s energy and environmental security missions and advance LLNL’s core
competencies in Advanced Materials and Manufacturing and High-Performance Computing,
Simulation, and Data Science. We addressed federal priorities of the DOE Applied Energy Offices
by providing materials and systems that significantly decrease the energy intensity of chemicals
manufacturing, a key gap for scale-up between benchtop and industry. Throughout the project,
we developed strategic international partnerships with Korea to develop science and technology
tools and capabilities to meet future national security challenges.
