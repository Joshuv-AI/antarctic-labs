 SUMMARY

This document establishes the test plan for the testing of the Seal Wire Verifier System in different
environmental conditions while maximizing the utility of the 15 seals available for tests. The plan
considers the maximum length of wire for which the system can detect the presence of a splice and
investigates performance at different temperatures, applications to different materials and containers, and
performance under different levels of tension.

 1. BACKGROUND

In 2024, ORNL determined that ultrasonic pulses around 80 kHz were the best method to detect the
presence of a splice in the wires that are used for the International Atomic Energy Agency’s (IAEA’s)
Field Verifiable Passive Seal (FVPS) [1]. In 2025, ORNL developed the Seal Wire Verifier System
(SWVS), which can perform wire verification and integrates with existing equipment used by the IAEA
[2]. Since then, ORNL has been trying to improve the SWVS by making a more optimized, custom circuit
board rather than relying on larger development-board components. ORNL has also developed a 3D
printed clip that can easily and repeatedly connect the transducers to the wires.

 5

This document describes the testing used to explore the limits of the SWVS. To support the testing, the
IAEA has provided ORNL with 15 FVPSs.

 2. SPLICING

In Enders et al. (2024) [1], ORNL described the method that resulted in the most difficult-to-detect splice.
ORNL will follow that method for all splices in the proposed tests. Variability is expected from splice to
splice; however, a proliferator would also have to expect a similar amount of variation.

 3. BEST PARAMETERS

Multiple SWVS parameters can be adjusted to provide the optimum response,1 including voltage (5 to
200 V), number of pulses, duty cycle of pulses, frequency, and transmitting wave (e.g., square wave or
sine wave). A 4 m wire is assumed to be a common wire length for which a splice should be detectable.

1. Use a 4 m wire spliced at the 2 m point as the base case. Analysis will be performed in the time
 domain, where the splice return is apparent without having to use a reference / verification spectra. If
 the time domain signal is insufficient to reliably detect a splice, switch to the frequency domain,
 where reference / verification spectra will be required.

2. Set up the SWVS to average 128 samples or more.

3. Amplitude: test the response at 110, 120, 140, 160, 180, and 200 volts.

4. Number of pulses: test the response using a pulse train of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 10 pulses.

5. Frequency: test the response using a frequency of 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 85, 90, and 100 kHz.

6. Use signals from the Acoustic Control Systems A1220 system as references to identify the splice
 reflections collected by SWVS. Demonstrate that the custom board is able to discern a splice in a
 longer wire than the commercially available A1220 device could discern.

7. Record the parameters from Steps 3-5 because they will become the operational parameters for the
 SWVS.

 4. MAXIMUM LENGTH

The ability to detect a splice without having to remove the seal is increasingly valuable as wire length
increases. The IAEA has not published a maximum allowable length, but the propagation of ultrasonic
pulses down a wire will determine a maximum length.

1. Using the operational parameters determined in Section 3, take a reference measurement of a 20 m
 wire.

2. Splice the seal wire at the halfway point.

1 The parameters are not intended to be adjusted by the user. This laboratory testing will determine the best overall values; then,

these values will be fixed for all operations.

 6

3. Using the operational parameters, determine if the splice can be detected in a 20 m wire.
 a. If yes—perform steps 1–3 with a wire length increased by 2 m.
 b. If no—perform steps 1–3 with a wire length decreased by 2 m.

4. Repeat steps 1–3 to find the maximum length, down to the nearest 1 m.

 5. BASELINE NOISE TESTING

The purpose of this testing is to observe the effects of the wire, the seal body, and the splice on the signal
received. The seals used in this portion will be directly usable in the next section, Temperature Testing.

 1. Record the input on two different SWVS systems with transducers attached to the HM-6 but no
 wire between them. This becomes the background noise of the system.

 2. Connect each SWVS to a 2 m wire with no seal attached. Record the response. This measures the
 noise floor of the wire itself.

 3. Close Seals 391 and 392 on each wire. Record the response. This is the pristine measurement.

 4. Splice Seals 391 and 392 at the halfway point. Record the response. This allows for a differential
 measurement between a pristine seal and a spliced seal.

Save Seals 391 and 392 for use in the Temperature Testing.

 6. TEMPERATURE TESTING

Seal wires are expected to be verified in both hot and cold conditions because dry storage casks (for
example) may need to be verified outside under hot or cold conditions.

1. Place two pristine seals (384, 387) with 2 m wire lengths inside the temperature chamber.

2. Refer to the reference measurements of Seals 391 and 392 with 2 m wire lengths; these are the
 pristine measurements already collected for Seals 391 and 392.

3. Place the spliced seals (Seals 391 and 392) inside the temperature chamber.

4. Connect the SWVS transducers to each seal (four SWVSs total) inside the temperature chamber.

5. Place the actual SWVS units outside the temperature chamber. The chamber has cable pass-throughs
 to enable this setup.

6. Set up the SWVSs to automatically take readings every 10 min. Temperature will be recorded using
 either separate temperature sensors or the chamber data logger.

7. Cycle the chamber through the following:
 a. Increase from 20°C to 50°C at 10°C/h.
 b. Hold at 50°C for 2 h.
 c. Ramp down to −20°C at 10°C/h.
 d. Hold at −20°C for 2 h.
   e. Ramp up to 20°C at 10°/h.

 7

 f. Repeat steps a–e twice, for a total of three cycles (about 3 days of testing).

8. Compare the recorded signals and look for signs of signal drift that correlate to temperature.

9. Look for signs of thermal aging of the wires. (Thermal aging is not expected because the working
 temperature range of the wires is listed as −40°C to 82°C.)

 7. MATERIALS TESTING

Seal wires may be wrapped tightly around some objects, placed in conduit, or used with a small bend
radius. This testing will determine whether these conditions affect the signal response.

 1. Close Seals 400 and 402 on 2 m wires.

 2. Pass a wire of 1 m less than the maximum length determined in Section 4 through metal conduit,
 and close Seal 404.

 3. Take a reference seal wire measurement on all three seals.

 4. Wrap Seal 400 around a metallic 55 gal drum, putting the wire in close contact with the outer
 surface of the cylinder.

 5. Wrap Seal 402 into a tight-radius bend, with bend radius of 1 cm.

 6. Take another reference measurement of Seals 400 and 402.

 7. Splice all three seals. There may need to be a break in the conduit (i.e., threaded pipe) to support
 the splice.

 8. Measure all three seals to see if the splice is detectable.

 9. Place the objects (cylinder and tight-radius bend) in a temperature chamber and conduct the tests
       at −10°C and +45°C.

 8. TENSION TESTING

Seal wire performance will be tested under varying levels of tension. Although the tension on the wire is
not expected to change under normal operating conditions, item movement could conceivably increase or
decrease the tension on the wire to make it different from the as applied condition. This test will map the
effect (if any) of varying tension on the wire.

1. Close Seals 406 and 407 on separate 2 m wires.

2. Measure the response via the SWVS.

3. Record the SWVS response for both seals under approximately 1, 2, 5, and 10 kg of force.

4. Splice Seal 407 slightly offset from the halfway point to enable weights to be added to the wire.

5. Hold the seals at the ends of the wires.

 8

6. Record the SWVS response for both seals under approximately 1, 2, 5, and 10 kg of force. (The splice
 may break before 10 kg of force is reached, which is acceptable.)

 9. ALTERNATE LOCATION TESTING

Splice a 4 m wire at 5% along the length, splice another wire at 20% along the length, and close two seals
on the two wires. Check for the ability to detect the splice at 2.5%, 20%, 80%, and 97.5% along the length
by switching the transmission and receiving transducers.

All splices to this point will have been performed at the midpoint of the wires. This testing will evaluate
the ability to detect splices at other locations along the wire: 2.5%, 20%, 80%, and 97.5%.

1. Close Seal 418 on a 4 m wire, and close Seal 419 on another 4 m wire.
2. Measure the response of the wires.
3. Splice the wire on Seal 418 at 2.5% along the length (i.e., 10 cm into the wire or from the
 transmitter).
4. Splice the wire on Seal 419 at 20% along the length (i.e., 0.8 m into the wire or from the transmitter).
5. Determine whether the splices can be detected on Seals 418 and 419.
6. Switch the position of the transmitting and receiving transducers.
7. Remeasure the spliced wires, now effectively looking for splices at the 80% and 97.5% locations.

 10. REPORTING

At the conclusion of testing, a test report will document the results of the testing described in this report.
