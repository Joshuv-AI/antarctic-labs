Poster 125. The Effect of Femoroplasty Resection Depth on Clinical Outcomes in Borderline Dysplastic Patients Undergoing Hip Arthroscopy
Authors: Stephanie Buza, Mason Uvodich, Malik Dancy, Hayden Baker, Alex Capitano, Akshay Raghuram, Bryan T Kelly, Struan Coleman, Danyal H Nawabi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:The study purpose was to evaluate the relationship between the extent of cam femoroplasty and postoperative outcomes following hip arthroscopy in borderline dysplastic patients. The primary hypothesis is that a deeper femoroplasty negatively affects postoperative outcomes.Methods:One-hundred one patients (107 hips) between the ages of 14-50 with a lateral center edge angle >=18 and <=25 undergoing primary hip arthroscopy with femoroplasty were identified. The change in modified Harris Hip Score (mHHS), achievement of a minimal clinically important difference (MCID), substantial clinical benefit (SCB), and patient acceptable symptomatic state (PASS) were evaluated. Evaluation of postoperative Dunn lateral radiographs characterized the femoroplasty by resection depth percentage (resection depth/femoral head diameter), proximal resection angle, and postoperative alpha angle. Regression analyses evaluated outcome variables relative to resection characteristics while controlling for age, sex, preoperative patient-reported outcome measures, acetabular and femoral version, and labral repair.Results:The mean resection depth for the cohort was 6.7% ± 3%, mean postoperative alpha angle was 38° ± 6.5°, and mean proximal resection angle was 77.8° ± 7°. Ninety-nine percent of patients had 1-year post-operative outcomes, and 64% had >=2-year outcomes. A deeper resection depth was negatively associated with postoperative change in mHHS at 1-year (est. -1.71,p=0.007) but not associated with 2-year change in mHHS (est. 0.19, p=0.73). Resection depth was associated with a lower odds of achieving 1-year MCID, SCB, and PASS (OR 0.78, 95% Confidence Interval (CI) 0.61-0.99, p=0.047, OR 0.083, 95% CI0.69-0.99, p=0.046, OR 0.8, 95% CI 0.66-0.97, p=0.024, respectively), however, it was not associated with lower 2-yearoutcomes. Neither postoperative alpha angle nor proximal resection angle were associated with any of the postoperative outcomes at any timepoint.Conclusions:A deeper femoroplasty resection negatively affects 1-year postoperative functional outcomes and lower likelihood of achieving MCID, SCB, and PASS at 1-year postoperatively. In the setting of borderline dysplasia, caution should be exercised to remove an impinging cam lesion and little more.
