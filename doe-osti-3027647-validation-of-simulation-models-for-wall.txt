Executive Summary:
 This is the final report for the DOE award DE-SC0021657, titled “Validation of Simulation
Models for Wall-Connected Scrape off Layer Currents during MHD Activity in the HBT-EP
Tokamak” for the period September 1, 2020 – April 30, 2025. This award is part of a broader
collaborative project, which supported training of a PhD student at Columbia University under the
joint supervision of Dr. Levesque and Dr. Hansen (on award DE-SC0021325). The award
supported research into computational models for plasma and 3D conducting structures in the
vicinity relevant to tokamak disruptions and other mode activity (eg. RWMs) using the
NIMROD1,2,3 and PSI-Tet4,5,6 codes. Additional detail on progress in support of these tasks is
provided below.

Full Report:
Student training on NIMROD and 3D MHD
 During this period, Dr. Hansen worked with Mr. David Arnold (PhD candidate at
Columbia) and trained him on usage of NIMROD for HBT-EP and other tokamak applications.
This included how to modify the code to implement new boundary conditions and adjust existing
conditions as required to enable spatially variable wall resistivity in NIMROD resistive wall
models. Mr. Arnold started working on this project in late Spring 2021 once the Columbia
University side of the project was finalized and has performed most of the work outlined below
under the supervision of, and with support from, Dr. Hansen.
 Dr. Levesque also worked with Mr. Arnold to provide data and interpretation from relevant
experiments on HBT-EP to use for code validation. These formed the baseline cases chosen for
simulation comparisons with HBT-EP.

Development of comprehensive synthetic diagnostic suite for validation
One of the primary advantages of HBT-EP for this study is the extensive diagnostics available to
measure the plasma and currents between the plasma and the first wall. To fully leverage this
capability, we have developed “synthetic” versions of all diagnostics in NIMROD to generate
predicted signals from our modeling. This includes measurement of plasma mode activity through
internal (EUV) and external (magnetics) diagnostics and measurements of current flow through
diagnosed limiters and wall tiles. All synthetic diagnostics are being implemented using the new
“NIMPY” library, which is developed and supported by the NIMROD team. See Figure 1 for
illustrations of each of the synthetic diagnostics used thus far in this investigation.

  Figure 1- Synthetic diagnostics for comparison with magnetic field (left), poloidal EUV (center) and current tile
 (right) diagnostics in HBT-EP.

Linear simulations of non-disruptive external kink activity in HBT-EP
Linear simulations of non-disruptive external kink activity in HBT-EP were performed in this
period. Comparison to experimental diagnostics was made, showing good agreement with internal
measurements, shown in Figure 2 and Figure 3, and external measurements, shown in Figure 4.
Very good agreement was found particularly on the internal structure as measured by a poloidal
array of EUV diodes, which are used in HBT-EP for both observation and control. This capability
is now being used to further study nonlinear simulations, and experimental observations in HBT-
EP by members of the HBT-EP group7,8,9.

Nonlinear simulations of non-disruptive external kink activity in HBT-EP
In HBT-EP and many tokamak devices, MHD activity is observed in a nonlinearly saturated state.
As a result, for detailed validation and understanding of experimental results, replication of such
states in model environments is required. However, this is often a challenging situation to
reproduce as it depends on a balance of different effects to enable saturation of a linearly unstable
mode. Such simulations were developed during this period, building on the linear simulations
presented above. These simulations use a resistive wall, which is a more realistic boundary
condition than the conventional ideal wall boundary condition, and Hall effects to enable mode
rotation consistent with experimental observations. Figure 5 shows an example of such a
simulation where a m=3, n=1 mode (initially a resistive wall mode) grows up and saturates with
large-scale islands visible in the equilibrium. Further simulations (see Figure 6) have also
developed cases with both edge and core activity as observed experimentally7, providing improved
confidence in simulations’ predictions of wall currents and other dynamics and further elucidation
of experimental results.

 Figure 5- Synthetic diagnostic signals (left) and island structures (right) from a NIMROD simulation of HBT-EP
 with saturated mode activity similar to experimental observations.

Modification of NIMROD resistive wall model to enable 3D wall resistivity
The resistive wall model implemented in NIMROD was extended in this work to enable 3D
resistivity (poloidal and toroidal variation). Simulations have been performed to identify a suitable
resistive wall mode (RWM) in HBT-EP geometry, shown in Figure 7. In the future this mode will
be used as the basis of linear and nonlinear validation of the “thin-wall” resistive wall model
formulation in NIMROD. As part of this validation, toroidally asymmetric wall effects will be
studied for the first time with this boundary condition type and compared to experimental data in
HBT-EP and simulations in PSI-Tet. This work is proceeding in collaboration with other members
of the NIMROD team and community members interested in 3D wall effects.

Figure 7- Example linear simulation using NIMROD with 3D wall effects to capture the 10-fold toroidal symmetry
 of the first wall in HBT-EP.
