The material described in this report is based upon work supported by the U.S. Department of

SC0018310.

A major challenge that limits the efficiency of the overall water splitting reaction is the oxidation
half-reaction, a process that releases oxygen and electrons, which in turn can reduce either protons
or carbon dioxide to make hydrogen or carbon-based fuel. In this project, the major objectives are
to increase the stability of water oxidation catalysts (WOCs), to develop ways to study catalyst
stability, and to enable WOC performance in acidic media.
The project was originally led by Principal Investigator Dr. Douglas Grotjahn. Tragically, Dr.
Grotjahn passed away in 2023. In 2024, Dr. Jing Gu took over as PI and guided the project through
a challenging transition period. With a no-cost extension into 2025, the team successfully
stabilized the research effort, achieving a smooth and productive transition that allowed work to
continue effectively. Other students from the original group made smooth transitions to join other
research labs to continue their PhD research.
Between 2024-2025, we got four manuscripts published, and below I will explain each project
in detail:

 1. Among mononuclear WOC, ruthenium catalysts incorporating 2,2’-bipyridine-6,6’-
 dicarboxylato ligands (bda2−) (Figure 1a) with ceric ammonium nitrate, Ce (IV), as a
 sacrificial oxidant have shown impressive water oxidation rates. Although the rate of water
 oxidation catalysis is important, in the long run, the durability of WOC remains a challenge.
 For example, there is evidence that at pH 7, the tetradentate bda ligand will dissociate from
 the metal centre in the presence of a sacrificial oxidant, photosensitizer, and light, to create
 a trinuclear complex. To address the stability problems mentioned above, here we
 introduce a family of complexes with a new pentadentate ligand bcpq2−(Figure 1b). The
 P(V) center of the phosphinic acid moiety of the pentadentate ligand bears a quinoline side
 arm that is designed to reduce axial ligand loss. As far as we are aware, there is no previous
 example attempting to use the extra valency on P(V) to connect a linker arm in WOC.
 The bcpq2− ligand introduces several benefits, including (1) facile synthesis (2) the Ru
 catalysts exhibit impressive water oxidation activity using Ce (IV) in acid, and at pH 7
 electrochemically, (3) following a single site mechanism, and (4) the
 pentadentate bcpq catalyst remains homogeneous in bulk electrolysis experiments at pH 7,
 whereas the bda analog becomes heterogeneous. This work is published on
      ChemCatChem 2024 (ChemCatChem 2024, 16, e202301644)

 Figure 1. Use of Pv as a connecting element to design the new pentadentate bcpq ligand.

2. In addition to the two common water oxidation catalysis mechanisms (I2M and WNA)
 already mentioned, a third potential mechanism, oxide relay (OR), has recently been
 proposed. The three mechanisms are depicted in Scheme 1. OR was computationally
 shown to be energetically favorable when compared to WNA and I2M for the catalyst
 species B. In this work, A series of ruthenium complexes containing both a carboxylate
 and a sulfonate group were synthesized and tested as water oxidation catalysts (Figure
 2). These catalysts achieved similar rates and improved TON under sacrificial oxidant
 conditions compared to their dicarboxylate counterparts. Moreover, they exhibited
 improved electrochemical performance under neutral and moderately acidic conditions
 compared to the dicarboxylate catalyst while operating at a sufficiently low
 potential. Our results have also provided insights into the varying mechanisms for the
 same catalyst under sacrificial oxidant and electrochemical conditions or at different
 concentrations. Based on the second-order dependence of the rate on the catalyst
 concentration in the CAN experiments, we infer that catalysts 1 a and 2 a should undergo
 I2M under sacrificial oxidant conditions. In contrast, under electrochemical conditions,
 the essential role of buffer base in achieving significant catalytic activity and the absence
 of isotope-substituted catalyst in experiments using 18O labelled water suggest that WNA
 should be the dominant mechanism under our tested conditions, although the
 predominant mechanism may change as the electrochemical potential varies. The
 mechanisms for catalysts 1 b and 2 b are debatable and could also vary as the reaction
 condition changes. However, for 1 b, the initial rate is clearly first-order with respect to
 catalyst concentration in sacrificial oxidant experiments, indicating that I2M is not
 favorable under that condition. Computational results based on 2 a suggest that WNA is
 the most favorable mechanism energetically compared to OR and I2M under the
 assumption that the O−O bond formation step is the RDS. This work was published in
 2024 (Eur. J. Inorg. Chem. 2024, 2, e202400265) by Jake Kerkhof, a previous graduate
 student of Dr. Grotjahn, with Prof. Yuezhi Mao, a collaborator of Dr. Grotjahn.

 Scheme 1. Commonly proposed mechanisms for ruthenium water oxidation catalysts from

 (I2M), and oxide relay (OR).

3. Recently, Doug’s group has developed a Ru-based water oxidation catalyst (WOC) with
 pendant sulfonate (1, Ru(4′-X-terpyridine)(phenanthroline-SO3)OTf (X = H, 1a) that
 shows high activity under both sacrificial oxidant (CAN, Ce(NH4)2(NO3)6, CeIV) and
 electrocatalytic conditions, in both acidic and neutral media. Here, we demonstrate that the
 functionalization of the 4′-X-terpyridine ligands with an electron-donating substituent X =
 OEt (1b) makes potentials of RuII/RuIII redox catalysis more negative, whereas when X =
 NO2 (1c) and CF3 (1d), potentials are more positive. For 1b, full conversion of the
 sacrificial oxidant CeIV occurred in 0.4 h (7 h for 1a), with an initial rate of 2.07 μmol O2 s–
 1
 and a turnover frequency of 7.6 s–1, which is 30-fold faster than that for 1a at [cat]0 = 20
 μM. Under electrocatalytic conditions, water oxidation by 1b is three times faster than that
 by the parent catalyst 1a at close to the same potential. Extensive computations have
 identified differences in the initial PCET steps of the water oxidation by catalysts 1a,
 1b, and 1d, and demonstrated the increased probability of the O2 formation via the oxide

 relay pathway in the order 1b< 1a < 1d (Figure 3). This work was recently published in
   Inorganic Chemistry 2025 (Inorganic Chemistry, 2025, 64, 7, 3188-3195)

 Figure 3. Mono-Ru-based water oxidation catalysts (WOC) 1b–1d with pendant sulfonate
 are reported in this paper and other recently reported analogs.

The project also made important contributions to education and workforce development as well.
One notable success was PhD student Colton J. Breyer, who graduated with his PhD degree in
Sep. 2024 from the joint doctoral program of UCDS/SDSU. His thesis, entitled "Instrumentation
and Methodology for the Challenging Analysis of Water Oxidation Stability and Performance,"
advanced techniques for evaluating catalysts in energy-related reactions like water splitting. It
trained early-career scientists, including a Hispanic PhD student, Miguel Ibanez, who
synthesized new ligands, passed his candidacy exam, and is progressing toward his degree under
Dr. Yong Yan's mentorship at SDSU. This DOE project helped Colton to successfully finish his
PhD and Miguel transition to a new research group. By providing mentorship during a difficult
leadership change and supporting diverse trainees, the project strengthened the next generation
of chemists.
Despite the challenges from the PI transition, this DOE-supported project enabled recovery and
meaningful progress. This award advanced fundamental knowledge in catalysis, promoted
sustainable chemistry, and supported education and student success— outcomes that enhance
U.S. scientific capabilities and benefit society.
