B. Project Scope

This was a collaborative effort between Lawrence Livermore National Security, LLC (LLNS), as
manager and operator of Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) and Gamma
Technologies, LLC (GT or Participante), to incorporate the ability to access LLNL chemical
kinetics technologies while using GT-SUITE, GT’s market leading engine simulation software.

At the end of the project, LLNL has released Zero-RK version 3.5 with zero- and one-
dimensional (0-D and 1-D) solver functionality that interfaces with GT’s GT-SUITE v2023 and
later releases. GT has tested its product to assure their customers that the interface can provide
reduction in chemistry solution time for detailed chemistry simulations. The process has also
positioned GT to easily benefit from future improvements of the Zero-RK suite of tools
developed under the DOE Vehicle Technologies Office.

C. Technical Accomplishments

The specific technical accomplishments follow from the Task, Milestones and Deliverables as
specified in Section B. First, after discussions and preliminary testing between GT and LLNL,
benchmark simulations were selected as the baseline for comparison between starting and ending

performance for both 0D and 1D simulations. The simulations were taken from GT’s standard
examples for engine simulation with combustion and were chosen as industry relevant problems
that can be run in a moderate amount of time. These simulations were run using GT-SUITE
standard solvers.

In parallel to the initial benchmark case selection and evaluation, LLNL and GT worked together
to specify two sides of the application programming interface (API) that would enable stable and
efficient coupling of Zero-RK to GT-SUITE. These discussions resulted in an API specifications
that were quickly implemented in both codes and documented in code as C-language headers.
Since the initial specifications, some changes have been made to the Zero-RK interface over the
course of the CRADA to accommodate additional features that were agreed on between the
parties as desirable. Future changes to the Zero-RK interfaces are expected to be minimal and
easy to accommodate. The current APIs is documented as part of the public release of Zero-RK
(https://github.com/llnl/zero-rk) with both a C-language headers and mini-applications which
exercise the key functionality for the zero- (zerork_cfd_plugin_tester) and one-dimensional
(zerork_flame_api_tester) solvers.

The use of Zero-RK solvers for solution of chemical kinetically limited simulations can have a
dramatic impact on solution time with no loss in accuracy. For the zero-dimensional benchmark
simulation with a 47 species mechanism, Zero-RK can reduce simulation time by almost 2 times
(from 320 seconds with the built-in GT-SUITE solver down to 170 seconds with Zero-RK) with
negligible effect on prediction of peak pressure, engine power and burn rate(s). Increasing the
model size to 198 species, to be able to better capture emissions and fuel variability, resulted in
approximately 5 times reduction in solution time (from more than 150 minutes with the built-in
GT-SUITE solver down to 32 minutes with Zero-RK).

D. Expected Economic Impact

Through the collaborative efforts of LLNL and Gamma Technologies, we have extended the
reach of Zero-RK, a groundbreaking tool designed to significantly accelerate the calculation
process of solving chemical kinetics for large-size mechanisms. This collaboration has enabled
us to bring the accelerated simulation capabilities of Zero-RK to a wider user base, fostering
innovation and unlocking cost savings across various sectors. The expanded market reach of
Zero-RK ensures that more companies can now benefit from the enhanced efficiency and
productivity offered by this advanced tool, revolutionizing their approach to chemical kinetics
simulations. It has far-reaching economic implications for various industries and markets, with
notable impacts including:

1. Our fast chemical kinetics solver greatly reduces simulation time for companies involved in
complex fuel development, clean fuel testing, and fuel additive analysis. This accelerated
simulation process enables these companies to optimize their fuel formulations and additive
compositions more efficiently, leading to cost savings and faster time-to-market.

2. The availability of our tool opens up new possibilities for companies engaged in low carbon
fuel development. Previously, these companies often relied on expensive experimental methods
due to the limitations of the speed of existing simulation software. With our fast chemical

3. Clients studying ash formation in combustion engines will greatly benefit from our tool. Ash

reliance on experimental testing leads to significant savings on equipment, materials, and
personnel expenses. While the exact dollar amount will vary depending on specific project
requirements and industry dynamics, it is reasonable to expect considerable cost savings for
companies utilizing our fast chemical kinetics solver.

D.1 Specific Benefits

Benefits to DOE

Enhanced LLNL’s reputation by incorporating DOE-developed simulation capabilities to
improve industry leading simulation software. Enhanced U.S. competitiveness by utilizing
DOE-developed simulation capabilities to improve the performance of U.S. developed
combustion devices and related industries.

Benefits to Industry

Enhanced participant’s flagship product by accelerating combustion kinetics calculations using
DOE-developed technology. The result of this project improves the predictive capability of GT-
SUITE for detailed chemical kinetic analysis and accelerates the use of simulation tools for the
design of automotive powertrains. GT is also positioned to easily benefit from future
improvements of the Zero-RK suite of tools developed under the DOE Vehicle Technologies
Office.

E. Participant Contribution

GT contributions were in three major areas. First, they defined scope for benchmarking
simulations and provided the relevant input files for them. Second, they provided detailed
guidance on the specification of the application programming interface (API) that provides the
bridge between GT-SUITE and Zero-RK. Finally, GT engineers updated GT-SUITE to
incorporate Zero-RK as an option for solution of chemical kinetics in combustion simulations.
GT will work with LLNL to promote the new capability via presentations and publications and
will provide user support to their customers who want to utilize the Zero-RK solver in
simulations.
