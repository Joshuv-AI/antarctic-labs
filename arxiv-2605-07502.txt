Introduction

The objective of this paper is to establish asymptotics for the broken
k
-diamond partition function and further show that this function satisfies the Turán inequalities, the Laguerre inequalities, and asymptotically complete monotonicity, when
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The broken
k
-diamond partitions were introduced by Andrews and Paule
[
2
]
.
Let
\Delta_{k}(n)
denote the number of broken
k
-diamond partitions of
n
. Andrews and Paule
[
2
]
gave the following generating function for
\Delta_{k}(n)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(1-q^{2n}\right)\left(1-q^{(2k+1)n}\right)}{\left(1-q^{n}\right)^{3}\left(1-q^{(4k+2)n}\right)}.

(1.1)

The broken
k
-diamond partition function
\Delta_{k}(n)
is known for its many elegant arithmetic properties. In particular, numerous Ramanujan-like congruences satisfied by
\Delta_{k}(n)
were proved by many authors, including Andrews and Paule
[
2
]
, Chan
[
8
]
, Chen, Fan and Yu
[
10
]
, Hirschhorn
[
23
]
, Paule and Radu
[
29
]
and so on.

A sequence
\{\alpha(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
of real numbers satisfies the (second order) Turán inequalities or to be log-concave if for
n\geq 1
,

\alpha^{2}(n)\geq\alpha(n-1)\alpha(n+1).

As stated by Chen, Jia and Wang
[
11
]
and Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier
[
21
]
, the higher order Turán inequalities are conveniently formulated in terms of the Jensen polynomials. The Jensen polynomials
J_{\alpha}^{d,n}(X)
of degree
d
and shift
n
associated to the sequence
\left\{\alpha(n)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
are defined by

J_{\alpha}^{d,n}(X):=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom{d}{j}\alpha(n+j)X^{j}.

We say that the sequence
\left\{\alpha(n)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the order
d
Turán inequality at
n
if and only if
J_{\alpha}^{d,n-1}(X)
is hyperbolic.
Additionally, A sequence
\{\alpha(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
is said to satisfy the Laguerre inequalities of order
m
if for all
n\geq 0
,

L_{m}(\alpha(n)):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}(-1)^{k+m}{{2m}\choose k}\alpha(n+k)\alpha(2m-k+n)\geq 0.

The Turán inequalities and the Laguerre inequalities arise from the study of the Laguerre–Pólya class, which are closely related to the Riemann hypothesis. We refer the interested readers to
[
13
,
16
,
17
,
31
,
32
]
. Consequently, this area has received widespread attention. Many researchers have been investigating the Turán inequalities
[
11
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
27
,
28
]
and the Laguerre inequalities
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
for different
kinds of sequences.

Define

\Delta^{r}\left(\alpha(n)\right):=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom{r}{k}(-1)^{r+k}\alpha(n+k).

We say that the sequence
\left\{\alpha(n)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies
r
-order monotonicity if the sign of
\Delta^{r}\left(\alpha(n)\right)
remains invariant. If
\left\{\alpha(n)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies
r
-order monotonicity for any
r
, we say that it possesses complete monotonicity. Moreover,
\left\{\alpha(n)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
is said to satisfy asymptotically complete monotonicity if, for any
r
, there exists an integer
N(r)
such that
\left\{\alpha(n)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies
r
-order monotonicity for
n>N(r)
. It should be noted that the complete monotonicity is another property related to the Laguerre–Pólya class, see
[
12
]
. Good
[
19
]
conjectured and Gupta
[
22
]
later proved, that for each
r\geq 1
,
\Delta^{r}\left(p(n)\right)>0
holds for sufficiently large
n
, where
p(n)
denotes the ordinary partition function. Furthermore, Wang, Xie and Zhang
[
33
]
established the asymptotically complete monotonicity for the overpartition function.

Recently, inequalities for the broken
k
-diamond partition function have also received significant attention from many scholars.
Dong, Ji and Jia
[
18
]
obtained an asymptotic formula for
\Delta_{k}(n)
, where
k=1
or
2
. Based on this asymptotic formula, they showed that
\Delta_{k}(n)
satisfies the Turán inequalities of any order for sufficiently large

and is log-concave for

. They also conjectured that these results still hold for

. Applying the asymptotic formula given in
[
18
]
, when

or
2
, Jia
[
24
]
proved that
\Delta_{k}(n)
satisfies the third order Turán inequalities for

and Yang
[
36
]
established the Laguerre inequalities of order
2
and determinantal inequalities of order

for
\Delta_{k}(n)
.

However, the existing asymptotics and inequalities of
\Delta_{k}(n)
only hold for
k=1
or
2
. In this paper, we intend to extend these results to any positive integer
k
and thus resolve the conjectures proposed by Dong, Ji and Jia
[
18
]
.

Asymptotic formula for
\Delta_{k}(n)

In this section, we present an exact formula for
\Delta_{k}(n)
by a powerful result of Zuckerman
[

[

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that

Suppose that

\left.F\right|_{\kappa}\gamma_{h,j}(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{h,j}(n)q^{\frac{n+\lambda_{h,j}}{c_{j}}}

\displaystyle a(n)

\displaystyle=2\pi(n+\lambda)^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq h<j\\

Theorem 3.1

(Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier)
.

Let
\{\alpha(n)\}
,
\{A(n)\}
, and
\{\delta(n)\}
be sequences of positive real numbers with

tending to zero and satisfying

\log\left(\frac{\alpha(n+j)}{\alpha(n)}\right)=A(n)j-\delta^{2}(n)j^{2}+o\left(\delta^{d}(n)\right)\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty

for some

and all

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\delta^{-d}(n)}{\alpha(n)}J_{\alpha}^{d,n}\left(\frac{\delta(n)X-1}{\exp(A(n))}\right)\right)=H_{d}(X)

uniformly for

in any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}

are the Hermite polynomials. Furthermore, this
implies that the polynomials

are hyperbolic for all but finitely many values of

L_{r}(\alpha(n))=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j+r}{2r\choose j}\alpha(n+j)\alpha(n+2r-j)>0.

where

A_{1}(n)=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}(n)}-\frac{5\pi^{2}}{6x_{k}^{2}(n)}=\Theta(\delta(n)),\quad R(n,j)=O(\delta(n)^{2r-1})=o(\delta(n)^{r}),

and for
m\geq 2
,

A_{m}(n)=\left(-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}(2m-3)!!}{2^{m}m!x_{k}^{2m-1}(n)}+\frac{5}{4mx_{k}^{2m}(n)}\right)\left(-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}\right)^{m}=\Theta(\delta(n)^{2m-1})=o(\delta(n)^{m}).

Based on Theorem
3.3
, it follows that for sufficiently large
n
,

A_{1}(n)^{r}\Delta^{r}\left(\Delta_{k}(n)\right)>0,

which implies that
\Delta^{r}\left(\Delta_{k}(n)\right)>0
.
∎

4
Log-concavity

In this section, we show that
\Delta_{k}(n)
is log-concave in a certain range by applying Theorem
1.2
. To this end, we need some lemmas. Let

\Theta(n):=\frac{\Delta_{k}(n-1)\,\Delta_{k}(n+1)}{\Delta_{k}^{2}(n)},\qquad\Theta_{M}(n):=\frac{M_{k}(n-1)\,M_{k}(n+1)}{M_{k}^{2}(n)},\qquad\varepsilon_{n}:=\frac{|R_{k}(n)|}{M_{k}(n)}.

We first give a sufficient condition for
\Theta(n)\leq 1
, i.e.,
\Delta_{k}^{2}(n)\geq\Delta_{k}(n-1)\,\Delta_{k}(n+1)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\varepsilon_{n-1},\varepsilon_{n},\varepsilon_{n+1}<1
. Then

\Theta(n)\leq\Theta_{M}(n)\left(1+\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}}{(1-\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast})^{2}}\right),

(4.1)

where
\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}:=\max\{\varepsilon_{n-1},\varepsilon_{n},\varepsilon_{n+1}\}
.
In particular, a sufficient condition for
\Theta(n)\leq 1
is

1-\Theta_{M}(n)\geq\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}}{(1-\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast})^{2}}.

(4.2)

Moreover, if
\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
, it suffices that

1-\Theta_{M}(n)\geq 16\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}.

(4.3)

Proof.

By the decomposition
\Delta_{k}=M_{k}+R_{k}
and the definition of
\varepsilon_{n}
,

\Delta_{k}(n)\leq M_{k}(n)\bigl(1+\varepsilon_{n}\bigr),\qquad\Delta_{k}(n)\geq M_{k}(n)\bigl(1-\varepsilon_{n}\bigr),

for every integer
n
. In particular,

\Delta_{k}(n-1)\leq M_{k}(n-1)(1+\varepsilon_{n-1}),\qquad\Delta_{k}(n+1)\leq M_{k}(n+1)(1+\varepsilon_{n+1}).

Then

\Theta(n)=\frac{\Delta_{k}(n-1)\Delta_{k}(n+1)}{\Delta_{k}^{2}(n)}\leq\frac{M_{k}(n-1)M_{k}(n+1)}{M_{k}^{2}(n)}\cdot\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{n-1})(1+\varepsilon_{n+1})}{(1-\varepsilon_{n})^{2}}.

Since
0\leq\varepsilon_{n\pm 1},\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}<1
, we have

1+\varepsilon_{n\pm 1}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast},\qquad 1-\varepsilon_{n}\geq 1-\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}>0.

As
u\mapsto u^{-2}
is decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, we obtain

\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{n-1})(1+\varepsilon_{n+1})}{(1-\varepsilon_{n})^{2}}\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast})^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast})^{2}}.

For any
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
,

\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}=1+\frac{4\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}.

Applying this with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}
gives (
4.1
).

From (
4.1
),

\Theta(n)\leq\Theta_{M}(n)\bigl(1+A\bigr),\qquad A:=\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}}{(1-\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast})^{2}}.

If
A\leq 1-\Theta_{M}(n)
, then

\Theta_{M}(n)(1+A)\leq\Theta_{M}(n)+\Theta_{M}(n)\bigl(1-\Theta_{M}(n)\bigr)=2\Theta_{M}(n)-\Theta_{M}(n)^{2}\leq 1,

because
t(2-t)\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
(equivalently,
(t-1)^{2}\geq 0
).
This proves (
4.2
). Finally, if
\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
, then

A=\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}}{(1-\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast})^{2}}\leq\frac{4\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}}{(1/2)^{2}}=16\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast},

so the cruder sufficient condition (
4.3
) follows.
∎

According to Lemma
4.1
, we need an upper bound for
\varepsilon_{n}
in order to prove the log-concavity of
\Delta_{k}(n)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\delta_{k}:=\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}-\sqrt{\beta_{k}}>0
. For
\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}(n)\geq 4
, we have

\varepsilon_{n}\leq C_{k}\,x_{k}(n)\,e^{-\delta_{k}\,x_{k}(n)},\qquad C_{k}:=\frac{432\sqrt{2}k^{3}e^{\pi\sqrt{k/3}}}{\pi^{\frac{5}{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\frac{3}{4}}}.

(4.4)

Proof.

From (
1.7
), it follows that

\varepsilon_{n}=\frac{|R_{k}(n)|}{M_{k}(n)}\leq\frac{216k^{3}e^{\pi\sqrt{k/3}}}{\pi^{3}\alpha_{k}}\cdot\frac{x_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}(n)\exp\left(\sqrt{\beta_{k}}x_{k}(n)\right)}{I_{2}\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}x_{k}(n)\right)}.

(4.5)

We claim that for all
t\geq 4
,

I_{2}(t)\ \geq\ \frac{e^{t}}{2\sqrt{2\pi t}}.

(4.6)

To see this, use the large-argument asymptotic expansion (fixed order
\nu
) from the DLMF
[
26
, §10.40(i)-(ii)]
, with the remainder controlled in absolute value (see also
[
25
]
). For
\nu=2
and
t\geq 4
,

I_{2}(t)=\frac{e^{t}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\left(1-\frac{15}{8t}+\frac{105}{128\,t^{2}}+R_{3}(t)\right),\qquad|R_{3}(t)|\leq\frac{945}{3072\,t^{3}}.

Since the third coefficient is positive, to obtain a uniform lower bound we discard the positive term
+\frac{105}{128\,t^{2}}
(worst case) and replace
+R_{3}(t)
by
-|R_{3}(t)|
. Hence, for
t\geq 4
,

\frac{I_{2}(t)}{e^{t}/\sqrt{2\pi t}}\ \geq\ 1-\frac{15}{8t}-\frac{945}{3072\,t^{3}}\ \geq\ 1-\frac{15}{32}-\frac{945}{196{,}608}=\frac{103{,}503}{196{,}608}\ >\ \frac{1}{2},

which proves (
4.6
).

By (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), for
\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}(n)\geq 4
,

\varepsilon_{n}\leq\frac{432\sqrt{2}k^{3}e^{\pi\sqrt{k/3}}}{\pi^{\frac{5}{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\frac{3}{4}}}\,x_{k}(n)\exp\left(-\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}-\sqrt{\beta_{k}}\right)x_{k}(n)\right),

which is (
4.4
).
∎

In order to prove the log-concavity of
\Delta_{k}(n)
, based on Lemma
4.1
, we also need a lower bound for
1-\Theta_{M}(n)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
n
with
x_{k}(n-1)\geq\dfrac{26}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}

, we have

1-\Theta_{M}(n)\geq\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,\pi^{4}}{36\,x_{k}^{3}(n+1)}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Let
f(n):=\log\left(M_{k}(n)\right)
, and regard
n
as a continuous variable. Then

\Delta^{2}f(n):=f(n+1)-2f(n)+f(n-1)=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-|s|)\,f^{\prime\prime}(n+s)\,ds,

(by the integral form of the second-order Taylor remainder / Peano kernel; see, e.g.,
[
14
]
)

hence

\Delta^{2}f(n)\leq\sup_{|s|\leq 1}f^{\prime\prime}(n+s),\qquad\Theta_{M}(n)=\exp\big(\Delta^{2}f(n)\big).

(4.8)

Write

f(n)=\log\left(\frac{\alpha_{k}\pi^{3}}{18}\right)-2\log\left(x_{k}(n)\right)+\phi(t),

with
\phi(t):=\log\left(I_{2}(t)\right)
and
t:=\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}(n)
. Using

x_{k}^{\prime}(n)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}(n)},\qquad x_{k}^{\prime\prime}(n)=-\frac{\pi^{4}}{9x_{k}(n)^{3}},\qquad t^{\prime}=\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}^{\prime}(n),\quad t^{\prime\prime}=\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}^{\prime\prime}(n),

a straightforward calculation yields

f^{\prime\prime}(n)=\frac{4\pi^{4}}{9x_{k}(n)^{4}}+\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)\frac{\alpha_{k}\pi^{4}}{9x_{k}(n)^{2}}-\phi^{\prime}(t)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,\pi^{4}}{9x_{k}(n)^{3}}.

(4.9)

We claim that for
t\geq 8
,

\Big|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\Big(1-\frac{1}{2t}\Big)\Big|\leq\frac{2}{t^{2}},\qquad\Big|\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{2t^{2}}\Big|\leq\frac{4}{t^{3}}.

(4.10)

By
[
26
, §10.40(i)-(ii)]
together with the error estimates in
[
26
, §10.41]
, for
t\geq 4
,

I_{\nu}(t)=\frac{e^{t}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\!\left(1-\frac{\mu-1}{8t}+\frac{(\mu-1)(\mu-9)}{2!\,8^{2}\,t^{2}}+R_{3}^{(\nu)}(t)\right),\quad\mu:=4\nu^{2},

with a remainder
R_{3}^{(\nu)}(t)=O(t^{-3})
; for fixed
\nu
and
t\geq 4
this implies

|R_{3}^{(\nu)}(t)|\leq C_{\nu}/t^{3}
for some explicit constant
C_{\nu}
(e.g. bounded by a constant multiple of the next coefficient).
Specializing to
\nu=2
and
\nu=3
gives

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{t}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\left(1-\frac{15}{8t}+\frac{105}{128t^{2}}+R_{3}^{(2)}(t)\right),

\displaystyle|R_{3}^{(2)}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{t^{3}},

\displaystyle I_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{t}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\left(1-\frac{35}{8t}+\frac{945}{128t^{2}}+R_{3}^{(3)}(t)\right),

\displaystyle|R_{3}^{(3)}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{3}}{t^{3}},

for some explicit
C_{2},C_{3}
(one may take, for instance, the absolute value of the first neglected coefficient times a harmless factor; any concrete choice works for
t\geq 4
).

Using the standard identity

I_{\nu}^{\prime}(t)=I_{\nu+1}(t)+\frac{\nu}{t}I_{\nu}(t)\qquad\text{(see DLMF \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Olver-Lozier-Boisvert-Clark-2010}{}{}, (10.29.2)]})},

we obtain for
\nu=2

I_{2}^{\prime}(t)=I_{3}(t)+\frac{2}{t}I_{2}(t).

Substituting the expansions and collecting terms up to
t^{-2}
gives

I_{2}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{e^{t}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\left(1-\frac{19}{8t}+\frac{465}{128t^{2}}+S_{3}(t)\right),\qquad|S_{3}(t)|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{t^{3}},

for some explicit
C^{\prime}
depending only on the fixed orders (here
2,3
).
Dividing by the corresponding expansion of
I_{2}(t)
and using

\dfrac{1+a}{1+b}=(1+a)\,(1-b+b^{2}-\cdots)

with
a,b=O(t^{-1})
(and the above remainder bounds), a short algebraic check yields

\phi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{I_{2}^{\prime}(t)}{I_{2}(t)}=1-\frac{1}{2t}+\frac{15}{8t^{2}}+E_{3}(t),\qquad|E_{3}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{t^{3}}\quad(t\geq 8).

In particular, for
t\geq 8

\left|\phi^{\prime}(t)-\left(1-\frac{1}{2t}\right)\right|\leq\frac{15}{8t^{2}}+|E_{3}(t)|\leq\frac{2}{t^{2}},

proving the first inequality in (
4.10
).

The modified Bessel equation

t^{2}I_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}+tI_{\nu}^{\prime}-(t^{2}+\nu^{2})I_{\nu}=0\qquad\text{(see DLMF \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Olver-Lozier-Boisvert-Clark-2010}{}{}, \lx@sectionsign 10.25(i)]})}

implies the Riccati identity for
\phi(t)=\log\left(I_{2}(t)\right)
:

\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)=1+\frac{4}{t^{2}}-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(t)}{t}-\big(\phi^{\prime}(t)\big)^{2}.

Write
\phi^{\prime}(t)=1-\tfrac{1}{2t}+u(t)
, where by the first inequality in (
4.10
), we have
|u(t)|\leq 2/t^{2}
and, more sharply,
u(t)=\tfrac{15}{8t^{2}}+O(t^{-3})
. A direct substitution gives

\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2t^{2}}-2\,v(t)-u(t)^{2},\qquad\text{where }v(t):=u(t)-\frac{15}{8t^{2}}.

Hence
|v(t)|\leq 1/t^{3}
(for
t\geq 8
) and
u(t)^{2}\leq 4/t^{4}\leq 1/t^{3}
, which yields

\left|\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{2t^{2}}\right|\leq 2|v(t)|+u(t)^{2}\leq\frac{4}{t^{3}}

for
t\geq 8
. This proves (
4.10
).

Using (
4.9
) and the upper bounds from (
4.10
), with
t=\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}(n)
, we have

f^{\prime\prime}(n)\ \leq\ -\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\pi^{4}}{9x_{k}^{3}(n)}\ +\ \frac{5\pi^{4}}{9x_{k}^{4}(n)}\ +\ \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{\pi^{4}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}^{5}(n)},

whenever
t\geq 8

(and in particular for
t\geq 26
)
. Applying the same bound to
f^{\prime\prime}(n+s)
(with
x_{k}(n)
replaced by
x_{k}(n+s)
and
t
by
\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}(n+s)
), and then taking the supremum in (
4.8
), the monotonicity in
x_{k}(n)
yields

\mathcal{D}(n):=\Delta^{2}f(n)\leq-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\pi^{4}}{9\,x_{k}^{3}(n+1)}\ +\ \frac{5\pi^{4}}{9\,x_{k}^{4}(n-1)}\ +\ \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{\pi^{4}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}^{5}(n-1)}.

As in the original argument, it suffices to ensure that the positive remainders are at most one half of the main (negative) term, namely

\frac{5\pi^{4}}{9\,x_{k}^{4}(n-1)}+\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{\pi^{4}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}^{5}(n-1)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\pi^{4}}{9\,x_{k}^{3}(n+1)},

which holds for all
x_{k}(n-1)\geq 26/\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}
(equivalently,
t(n-1)\geq 26
). Consequently

\mathcal{D}(n)\leq-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\pi^{4}}{18\,x_{k}^{3}(n+1)}<0.

From (
4.10
) we also have, for
t\geq 8
,

\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2t^{2}}-\frac{4}{t^{3}},\qquad\phi^{\prime}(t)\ \leq\ 1-\frac{1}{2t}+\frac{2}{t^{2}}.

Plugging these into (
4.9
) yields the matching lower bound

f^{\prime\prime}(n)\ \geq\ -\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\pi^{4}}{9x_{k}^{3}(n)}\ -\ \frac{5\pi^{4}}{9x_{k}^{4}(n)}\ -\ \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{\pi^{4}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}^{5}(n)},

so that
|f^{\prime\prime}(n)|\leq c/x_{k}^{3}(n)
for some explicit constant
c
when
t\geq 26
. Therefore,

|\mathcal{D}(n)|=\left|\int_{-1}^{1}(1-|s|)f^{\prime\prime}(n+s)\,ds\right|\leq c/x_{k}^{3}(n)<1

for
x_{k}(n-1)\geq 26/\sqrt{\alpha}_{k}
.

Finally, for
|\mathcal{D}(n)|<1
,

1-e^{\mathcal{D}(n)}\geq-\mathcal{D}(n)-\tfrac{\mathcal{D}^{2}(n)}{2}\geq\tfrac{-\mathcal{D}(n)}{2}.

Hence

1-\Theta_{M}(n)\ =\ 1-e^{\Delta^{2}f(n)}\ =\ 1-e^{\mathcal{D}(n)}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\pi^{4}}{18\,x_{k}^{3}(n+1)}=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\pi^{4}}{36\,x_{k}^{3}(n+1)},

which is (
4.7
).
∎

In light of Lemmas
4.1
-
4.3
, we derive another sufficient condition for the log-concavity of
\Delta_{k}(n)
, which can be used directly to prove Theorem
1.5
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Define

A_{k}:=\frac{1728\,C_{k}}{\pi^{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}
.
If
x_{k}(n-1)\geq\dfrac{26}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}
and

e^{\delta_{k}\,x_{k}(n)}\ \geq\ A_{k}x_{k}^{4}(n)\qquad\big(\text{equivalently }~\delta_{k}x_{k}(n)\geq\log A_{k}+4\log x_{k}(n)\big),

(4.11)

then
\Delta_{k}^{2}(n)\geq\Delta_{k}(n-1)\Delta_{k}(n+1)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, for
\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,x_{k}(j)\geq 4
we have

\varepsilon_{j}\ =\ \frac{|R_{k}(j)|}{M_{k}(j)}\ \leq\ C_{k}\,x_{k}(j)\,e^{-\delta_{k}\,x_{k}(j)}\qquad(j=n-1,n,n+1).

Since
x_{k}(n)
is increasing in
n
, we have
x_{k}(n-1)\leq x_{k}(n)\leq x_{k}(n+1)
and therefore

\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}=\max\{\varepsilon_{n-1},\varepsilon_{n},\varepsilon_{n+1}\}\leq C_{k}\,x_{k}(n+1)\,e^{-\delta_{k}x_{k}(n-1)}.

(4.12)

Using
x_{k}(n)=\frac{\pi}{6}\sqrt{24n-(2k+2)}
, we have

x_{k}(n\pm 1)=x_{k}(n)\sqrt{1\pm\frac{24}{24n-(2k+2)}}=x_{k}(n)\sqrt{1\pm\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}^{2}(n)}},

hence

x_{k}(n)-x_{k}(n-1)=x_{k}(n)\Big(1-\sqrt{1-\tfrac{2\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}^{2}(n)}}\Big)\leq\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}(n)}.

Therefore,

e^{\delta_{k}(x_{k}(n)-x_{k}(n-1))}\ \leq\ \exp\!\Big(\frac{2\delta_{k}\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}(n)}\Big)\ \leq\ \exp\!\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}(n)}\Big)\ \leq\ \exp\!\Big(\frac{\alpha_{k}\pi^{2}}{39}\Big)=:\rho_{k}

and

x_{k}(n+1)\leq x_{k}(n)\sqrt{1+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}\cdot\frac{\alpha_{k}}{26^{2}}}<\frac{3}{2}x_{k}(n),

where we use that
\delta_{k}\leq\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}
,
x_{k}(n)\geq 26/\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}
and
\alpha_{k}<\frac{5}{2}
. These together with (
4.12
) give that

\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}\leq C_{k}\,x_{k}(n+1)\,e^{\delta_{k}(x_{k}(n)-x_{k}(n-1)-x_{k}(n))}<\frac{3}{2}\,\rho_{k}\,C_{k}\,x_{k}(n)\,e^{-\delta_{k}x_{k}(n)}.

(4.13)

Assume (
4.11
). Then by (
4.13
) and
x_{k}(n)\geq 26/\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}
,

\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}<\frac{3\rho_{k}\,C_{k}}{2A_{k}\,x_{k}^{3}(n)}=\frac{\pi^{4}\rho_{k}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}{1152}\cdot\frac{1}{x_{k}^{3}(n)}<\frac{1}{2}.

so
0\leq\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}<\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Then by Lemma
4.1
, a sufficient condition for

\Theta(n)\leq 1

is

1-\Theta_{M}(n)\geq 16\,\varepsilon_{n}^{\ast}.

(4.14)

According to (
4.13
), to prove (
4.14
), it suffices to show

1-\Theta_{M}(n)\geq 24\,\rho_{k}\,C_{k}\,x_{k}(n)\,e^{-\delta_{k}x_{k}(n)}.

(4.15)

Using Lemma
4.3
, it is enough that

\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,\pi^{4}}{36\,x_{k}^{3}(n+1)}\geq 24\,\rho_{k}\,C_{k}\,x_{k}(n)\,e^{-\delta_{k}x_{k}(n)},

i.e.,

e^{\delta_{k}x(n)}\geq\frac{864\,\rho_{k}\,C_{k}}{\pi^{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\;x_{k}(n)x_{k}^{3}(n+1).

Moreover, since

\frac{x_{k}^{3}(n+1)}{x_{k}^{3}(n)}=\Bigl(1+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}^{2}(n)}\Bigr)^{\!3/2}\leq\exp\Bigl(\frac{\pi^{2}}{x_{k}^{2}(n)}\Bigr)\leq\exp\Bigl(\frac{\pi^{2}\alpha_{k}}{676}\Bigr)=:\tilde{c}_{k},

we have

\frac{864\,\rho_{k}\,C_{k}}{\pi^{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\;x_{k}(n)x_{k}^{3}(n+1)\leq\frac{864\,\tilde{c}_{k}\,\rho_{k}\,C_{k}}{\pi^{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\;x_{k}^{4}(n).

Since
\tilde{c}_{k}\rho_{k}<2
, the right-hand side is
\leq\dfrac{1728\,C_{k}}{\pi^{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\,x_{k}^{4}(n)=A_{k}x_{k}^{4}(n)
, which is exactly the hypothesis (
4.11
). Hence (
4.15
) holds, so (
4.14
) holds, and therefore
\Theta(n)\leq 1
, i.e.,

\Delta_{k}^{2}(n)\geq\Delta_{k}(n-1)\,\Delta_{k}(n+1).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Having established an available sufficient condition for
\Delta_{k}^{2}(n)\geq\Delta_{k}(n-1)\,\Delta_{k}(n+1)
, we are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.5
directly.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By Lemma
4.4
, it suffices to verify, for a given
n
,

\text{(i)}\quad x_{k}(n-1)\ \geq\ \frac{26}{\sqrt{\alpha}_{k}},\qquad\text{(ii)}\quad e^{\delta_{k}\,x_{k}(n)}\ \geq\ A_{k}\,x_{k}(n)^{4},

(4.16)

where
\delta_{k}=\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}-\sqrt{\beta_{k}}>0
and

A_{k}=\frac{1728\,C_{k}}{\pi^{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}},\qquad C_{k}=\frac{432\sqrt{2}k^{3}e^{\pi\sqrt{k/3}}}{\pi^{\frac{5}{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\frac{3}{4}}}.

Since

x_{k}(n-1)=\frac{\pi}{6}\sqrt{\,24(n-1)-(2k+2)\,},

the condition
x_{k}(n-1)\geq 26/\sqrt{\alpha}_{k}
is equivalent to

24(n-1)-(2k+2)\ \geq\ \frac{26^{2}\cdot 36}{\pi^{2}\alpha_{k}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\ \geq\ 1+\frac{k+1}{12}+\frac{1014}{\alpha_{k}\pi^{2}}.

Hence, for any integer
n\geq\left\lceil\dfrac{1014}{\alpha_{k}\pi^{2}}+\dfrac{k+1}{12}+1\right\rceil
,
(
4.16
)(i) holds.

Let
F(n):=\dfrac{e^{\delta_{k}x_{k}(n)}}{x_{k}^{4}(n)}
. Since
x_{k}^{\prime}(n)=\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}(n)}>0
,

\frac{d}{dn}\log\left(F(n)\right)=\delta_{k}x_{k}^{\prime}(n)-\frac{4x_{k}^{\prime}(n)}{x_{k}(n)}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3x_{k}(n)}\Big(\delta_{k}-\frac{4}{x_{k}(n)}\Big).

Hence
F(n)
is strictly increasing whenever
x_{k}(n)\geq 4/\delta_{k}
. At the threshold

n_{0}(k):=8\,k^{3}+\frac{k+1}{12},

we have

x_{k}\left(n_{0}(k)\right)=\frac{\pi}{6}\sqrt{24n_{0}(k)-(2k+2)}=\frac{\pi}{6}\sqrt{24\Big(n_{0}(k)-\frac{k+1}{12}\Big)}=\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\,k^{3/2},

(4.17)

while

\frac{4}{\delta_{k}}\leq\frac{4(2k+1)}{6}\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}=\frac{2\sqrt{10}}{3}\,k+\frac{\sqrt{10}}{3}

since

\delta_{k}=\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}-\sqrt{\beta_{k}}=\frac{\alpha_{k}-\beta_{k}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}+\sqrt{\beta_{k}}}=\frac{12}{(2k+1)\,(\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}+\sqrt{\beta_{k}})}\ \geq\ \frac{6}{2k+1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}.

(4.18)

Thus for
k\geq 3
,

x_{k}(n_{0})\geq\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\,k^{3/2}\geq 4\pi\,k\ >\ \frac{2\sqrt{10}}{3}\,k+\frac{\sqrt{10}}{3}\ \geq\ \frac{4}{\delta_{k}},

and consequently
F(n)
is increasing for all
n\geq n_{0}(k)
.

Combining with the displayed formula for
A_{k}
and the elementary bound

\alpha_{k}^{-5/4}\leq 1
, we get

A_{k}\ \leq\ \widetilde{A}_{k}:=\frac{1728}{\pi^{\frac{13}{2}}}\,\cdot 432\sqrt{2}\;k^{3}\,e^{\pi\sqrt{k/3}}.

(4.19)

When
n\geq n_{0}(k)
, the monotonicity of
F(n)
and (
4.19
) give that a sufficient condition for (
4.16
)(ii) is

\delta_{k}\,x_{k}(n_{0}(k))\geq\log\widetilde{A}_{k}+4\log\left(x_{k}(n_{0}(k))\right).

(4.20)

Applying (
4.17
) and (
4.18
), for
k\geq 3
,
we obtain the explicit uniform lower bound

\delta_{k}\,x_{k}\left(n_{0}(k)\right)\geq 24\pi\sqrt{\frac{2}{15}}\cdot\frac{k^{3/2}}{2k+1}\geq\frac{72\pi}{7}\sqrt{\frac{2k}{15}}.

On the other hand, from (
4.17
) and (
4.19
),

\log\widetilde{A}_{k}+4\log\left(x_{k}\left(n_{0}(k)\right)\right)=\pi\sqrt{k/3}+9\log k+\mathrm{const},

where

\mathrm{const}:=\log\!\Big(\frac{1728}{\pi^{\frac{13}{2}}}\,\cdot 432\sqrt{2}\Big)+4\log\!\Big(\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\Big)<14.4.

Consequently, (
4.20
) follows from the inequality

\left(\frac{72\pi}{7}\sqrt{\frac{2}{15}}-\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\right)\sqrt{k}\ \geq\ 9\log k+14.4,

which holds for all
k\geq 16
.
For
k=3
and for
5\leq k<16
, we verify (
4.16
)(ii)
at the threshold

n=n_{0}(k)
by a finite computation using the exact constants
C_{k}
(thus the exact
A_{k}
). By the monotonicity of
F(n)
established above, this implies that (
4.16
)(ii) then holds for all
n\geq n_{0}(k)
and each such
k
.

For
k=4
, condition (
4.16
)(ii) at

n\geq 526
is verified by a computation
using the exact constants
C_{k}
(hence
A_{k}
).

Note that for
k\geq 3
,

\max\left\{\left\lceil 8k^{3}+\frac{k+1}{12}\right\rceil,\left\lceil\frac{1014}{\alpha_{k}\pi^{2}}+\frac{k+1}{12}+1\right\rceil\right\}=\left\lceil 8k^{3}+\frac{k+1}{12}\right\rceil.

Therefore, for all
k\geq 3
and
n\geq\max\left\{\left\lceil 8k^{3}+\frac{k+1}{12}\right\rceil,526\right\}
,
the two hypotheses (
4.16
) of Lemma
4.4
are satisfied.
Therefore
\Delta_{k}^{2}(n)\geq\Delta_{k}(n-1)\Delta_{k}(n+1)
, which completes the proof.
∎
