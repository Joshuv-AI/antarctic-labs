Quasi-Gorenstein Morphisms of commutative local dg-algebras
Zachary Nason, Andrew J. Soto Levins, Ryan Watson

Abstract
. We introduce quasi-Gorenstein morphisms of commutative local dg-algebras and use a Gorenstein version of the virtually small property to characterize them, a result which is new even for homomorphisms of local rings. In a different direction, we characterize exact sequences in a noetherian local ring, in the sense of Avramov, Henriques, and Şega, in terms of quasi-Gorenstein morphisms involving Koszul complexes.

Primary: 13B10, 13D09, 13H10, 16E35, 16E45

1.
Introduction

In
[
3
]
Avramov and Foxby introduced quasi-Gorenstein homomorphisms of noetherian local rings. These are the homomorphisms that capture the Gorenstein property: for a noetherian local ring
R
with residue field
k
,
R\rightarrow k
is quasi-Gorenstein if and only if
R
is Gorenstein. Moreover, if a local homomorphism
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
of noetherian local rings is quasi-Gorenstein, then
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
S
is Gorenstein.

In this paper we introduce quasi-Gorenstein morphisms of commutative local dg-algebras, extending work of Avramov and Foxby. We first review necessary background for the paper in
Section

2
, and in
Section

3
we prove dg-analogs of results about quasi-Gorenstein ring homomorphisms that will be used throughout the paper.

The following is our first main result - it is new even when both
R
and
S
are rings.

Theorem A

(
Proposition

3.4
and
Theorem

4.6
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
be a morphism of commutative local dg algebras with
S_{0}
a noetherian local ring and
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}S<\infty
. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

\varphi
is quasi-Gorenstein.

(2)

Every dg
S
-module
M
with bounded and degreewise finite length homology that has finite Gorenstein dimension over
R
is virtually
G
small over
S
.

This theorem parallels results of Briggs, Iyengar, Letz, and Pollitz
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
and of Dwyer, Greenlees, and Iyengar
[
13
, Theorem 9.1]
that show if
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
is a surjective homomorphism of noetherian rings of finite flat dimension, then
\varphi
is locally complete intersection if and only if any
S
-complex with finite length homology that is proxy small as an
R
-complex is also proxy small over
S
.

The following is our second main result. Recall that a sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in a noetherian local ring
A
is exact if for
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
x_{i}
is
A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1})
-regular or is an exact zero divisor on
A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1})
.

Theorem B

(
Theorem

5.10
)
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local ring, let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a sequence of elements in
A
, and let
R
be the Koszul complex on
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Then
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is quasi-Gorenstein and
\operatorname{Gdim}_{A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})}A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})<\infty
for
1\leq i\leq n
if and only if the sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is exact.

In particular, this theorem gives a sufficient condition for an ideal to be quasi-complete intersection: by
[
4
, Theorem 3.7]
ideals generated by exact sequences are quasi-complete intersection. Moreover,
Theorem

5.10
and
Corollary

5.12
give examples where
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is quasi-Gorenstein and
R
is a Koszul complex. We provide more examples of quasi-Gorenstein morphisms of dg-algebras in
Section

6

We end the paper with
Section

7
by using results in previous sections to give ways of producing nonfree Gorenstein projective dg-modules over a Koszul complex on a sequence of exact zero divisors. In particular, if the dg-algebra is local-Cohen-Macaulay, we give examples of nonfree maximal local-Cohen-Macaulay dg-modules, see
Remark

7.6
.

In this paper we work under the following setup.

Setup 1.1
.

When we say
R
is a dg-algebra, we mean a nonnegative strongly graded-commutative dg-algebra such that
(\operatorname{H}_{0}(R),\mathfrak{m},k)
is a noetherian local ring and such that
\operatorname{H}(R)
is finitely generated over
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
. When we say
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
is a morphism of dg-algebras, we mean
\varphi
is a morphism of dg-algebras such that the induced map
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(S)
is a local homomorphism of noetherian local rings.

2.
Background

In this section we review necessary background for the paper. We work in the derived category
\mathsf{D}(R)
of dg
R
-modules, and focus on the subcategory
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
, which consists of all dg
R
-modules
M
such that
\operatorname{H}(M)
is bounded and degreewise finitely generated over
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
. We write
\simeq
for isomorphisms in the derived category, and write
\sim
for isomorphisms in the derived category up to a shift. We reserve the symbol
\cong
to denote isomorphisms of dg-modules. The supremum and infimum of a dg-module
M
are

\sup{M}=\sup\{n\mid\operatorname{H}_{n}(M)\neq 0\}\quad\quad\inf{M}=\inf\{n\mid\operatorname{H}_{n}(M)\neq 0\}.

The amplitude of
M
is
\operatorname{amp}{M}=\sup{M}-\inf{M}
.

2.1
.

A subcategory of
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
is thick if it is closed under taking shifts, finite direct sums, summands, and triangles. The thick closure of a dg-module
M
, denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Thick}}_{R}M
, is the smallest thick subcategory containing
M
.

2.2
.

Let
M
be a dg-module in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
. Following Bird, Shaul, Sridhar, and Williamson
[
6
]
, the
projective dimension
of a dg-module
M
is

\displaystyle\operatorname{projdim}_{R}M\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0

\displaystyle\text{ for any }N\in\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)

\displaystyle\text{ and any }i>n-\inf{N}\}.

The following are equivalent by
[
23
, Proposition 2.2]
and
[
24
, Theorem 2.22]
:

(1)

\operatorname{projdim}_{R}M<\infty
.

(2)

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{n}(k,M)=0
for
n\gg 0
.

(3)

M
is in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Thick}}_{R}R
.

We also will use the Poincaré series of
M
, which is defined to be

\mathsf{P}_{M}^{R}(t)=\sum\dim_{k}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{n}(k,M)t^{n}.

Similarly, the
injective dimension
of
M
is defined to be

\displaystyle\operatorname{injdim}_{R}M\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(N,M)=0

\displaystyle\text{ for any }N\in\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)

\displaystyle\text{ and any }i>n+\sup{{N}}\}.

By
[
23
, Corollary 2.31]

\operatorname{injdim}_{R}M<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{n}(k,M)=0
for
n\gg 0
. The Bass series of
M
is defined to be

\mathsf{I}^{M}_{R}(t)=\sum\dim_{k}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{n}(k,M).

2.3
.

A Gorenstein dimension for dg-modules was defined by Hu, Yang, and Zhu in
[
19
]
. Let
M\in\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
be a derived reflexive dg-module.

(1)

The
Gorenstein dimension
of
M
, denoted by
\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M
, is
-\inf{\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,R)}
.

(2)

The dg-module
M
is in the class
\mathcal{G}
if either
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}M=\inf{M}
or
M=0
, and
M
is in the class
\mathcal{G}_{0}
if it is an object of
\mathcal{G}
with the additional properties that
\operatorname{amp}{M}\geq\operatorname{amp}{A}
and
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}M=\inf{M}=0
, or
M=0
. The dg-modules in
\mathcal{G}_{0}
are called Gorenstein projective.

By
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]

\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}M<\infty
if and only if
M
is in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Thick}}_{R}\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

2.4
.

A dg-module
D
is a
dualizing
dg-module over
R
if
D
has finite injective dimension and if the canonical morphism

D\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(D,D)

is an isomorphism in the derived category. The dg-algebra
R
is
Gorenstein
if
\operatorname{injdim}_{R}R<\infty
, see
[
14
,
15
]
. The following are equivalent, see
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
:

(1)

R
is Gorenstein.

(2)

R
is a dualizing dg-module for
R
.

(3)

\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}M<\infty
for all
M
in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
.

(4)

\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}k<\infty
.

3.
Quasi-Gorenstein morphisms of dg-algebras

In this section we prove dg-analogs of results about quasi-Gorenstein ring homomorphisms that will be used throughout the paper. Gorenstein and quasi-Gorenstein morphisms of rings were first studied in
[
2
,
3
]
, and Gorenstein morphisms of dg-algebras were defined in
[
15
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
be a morphism of dg-algebras with
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}S<\infty
. We say
\varphi
is
quasi-Gorenstein
if
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R)
is isomorphic in
\mathsf{D}(S)
to a shift of
S
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
be a module finite local homomorphism of noetherian local rings with
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}S<\infty
. By
[
3
, Theorem 7.8]
,
\varphi
is quasi-Gorenstein if and only if
\varphi
is quasi-Gorenstein in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

The following was proved in
[
10
, Theorem 17.1.23]
when
R
is a ring.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R
be a dg-algebra and let
M
a dg-module in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
. If there exists an isomorphism

\phi\colon M\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,R),R),

then the canonical morphism

\delta_{R}^{M}\colon M\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,R),R)

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
10
, Proposition 17.1.23]
to the dg-setting. Let
(-)^{\dagger}
be the functor
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(-,R)
. The functor
(-)^{\dagger}
is self-adjoint on the right with unit
\delta_{R}^{-}
. Consider the composition of morphisms
(\delta_{R}^{M})^{\dagger}\circ\delta_{R}^{M^{\dagger}}
. Through the zig-zag identities, we have that
(\delta_{R}^{M})^{\dagger}\circ\delta_{R}^{M^{\dagger}}=1_{R}^{M^{\dagger}}
, and so we have that
\operatorname{H}((\delta_{R}^{M})^{\dagger})\circ\operatorname{H}(\delta_{R}^{M^{\dagger}})=\operatorname{H}(1_{R}^{M^{\dagger}})
by applying the homology functor. By
[
10
, Theorem 17.1.22]
, we have that
\operatorname{H}(\delta_{R}^{M^{\dagger}})
is an isomorphism of
\operatorname{H}(R)
-complexes, which, by
[
31
, Corollary 7.2.13]
implies that
\delta_{R}^{M^{\dagger}}
is an isomorphism in
\mathsf{D}(R)
. Through the same reasoning, we obtain that
\delta_{R}^{M^{\dagger\dagger}}
is an isomorphism in
\mathsf{D}(R)
.

As biduality
\delta_{R}^{-}
is a natural transformation, we have the commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\dagger\dagger}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\dagger\dagger}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\dagger\dagger\dagger\dagger}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{R}^{M}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{R}^{M^{\dagger\dagger}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{\dagger\dagger}}

Since
\phi
,
\phi^{\dagger\dagger}
, and
\delta_{R}^{M^{\dagger\dagger}}
are isomorphisms, the commutativity of the diagram forces
\delta_{R}^{M}
to be an isomorphism as well.
∎

The following was proved in
[
3
, Theorem 7.11]
when both
R
and
S
are rings and
M
is a finitely generated
S
module.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
be a quasi-Gorenstein morphism of dg-algebras and let
M
be in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(S)
. Then

\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}M=\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}S+\operatorname{Gdim}_{S}M.

In particular,
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}M<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{Gdim}_{S}M<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
n=\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}S
. Since
\varphi
is quasi-Gorenstein,
S
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,\Sigma^{n}R)
. We now have the following isomorphisms

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(M,S)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(M,\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,\Sigma^{n}R))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M\otimes_{S}^{\mathsf{L}}S,\Sigma^{n}R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,\Sigma^{n}R)

and so
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(M,S)
is in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(S)
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,\Sigma^{n}R)
is in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
. In particular, if
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension over
R
and
S
, then

\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}M=\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}S+\operatorname{Gdim}_{S}M.

We have the following isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,R),R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,\Sigma^{n}R),\Sigma^{n}R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(M,S),\Sigma^{n}R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(M,S)\otimes_{S}^{\mathsf{L}}S,\Sigma^{n}R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(M,S),\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,\Sigma^{n}R))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(M,S),S).

Now applying
Lemma

3.3
and
[
31
, Corollary 7.2.13]
, and the same reasoning in
[
10
, Proposition 12.3.19]
, shows that
M
is derived reflexive over
R
if and only if it is derived reflexive over
S
, and so the proof is finished.
∎

The next two results were proved in
[
3
, 8.10.a]
and
[

, 8.9]
, respectively, when

,

, and

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
Q\xrightarrow{\psi}R\xrightarrow{\varphi}S
be morphisms of dg-algebras. If
\varphi\psi
and
\psi
are quasi-Gorenstein, then
\varphi
is quasi-Gorenstein.

Proof.

Note that
S
has finite Gorenstein dimension over
R
by
Proposition

3.4
since
S
has finite Gorenstein dimension over

. Also, we have the following isomorphisms in
\mathsf{D}(S)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R)\sim\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{Q}(R,Q))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{Q}(S\otimes_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}R,Q)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{Q}(S,Q)

\displaystyle\sim S.

The first and fourth isomorphisms hold as both
\psi
and
\varphi\psi
are quasi-Gorenstein, and the second isomorphism is in
\mathsf{D}(S)
by
[
31
, Proposition 12.10.12]
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
Q\xrightarrow{\psi}R\xrightarrow{\varphi}S
be morphisms of dg-algebras. If
\psi
and
\varphi
are quasi-Gorenstein, then
\varphi\psi
are quasi-Gorenstein.

Proof.

Note that
S
has finite Gorenstein dimension over
Q
by
Proposition

3.4
since
S
has finite Gorenstein dimension over
R
. Also, we have the following isomorphisms in
\mathsf{D}(S)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{Q}(S,Q)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{Q}(S\otimes_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}R,Q)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{Q}(R,Q))

\displaystyle\sim\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R)

\displaystyle\sim S

where the second isomorphism is in
\mathsf{D}(S)
by
[

, Proposition 12.10.12]
, and the third and fourth isomorphisms hold as both
\varphi
and

The following was proved in
[
3
, 7.7.2]
when both
R
and
S
are rings, and it was proved for Gorenstein morphisms of dg-algebras in
[
15
, Theorem 3.6 and
Theorem 3.10]
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
be a quasi-Gorenstein morphism of dg-algebras. Then
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
S
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

Let
k
be the residue field of
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
and let
l
be the residue field of
\operatorname{H}_{0}(S)
. Since
S
is in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
,
l
is a finite dimensional
k
-vector space. This gives the sixth isomorphism below:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(l,S)\sim\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(l,\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(l\otimes_{S}^{\mathsf{L}}S,R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(l,R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(l\otimes_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}k,R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{k}(l,\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(k,R))

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{\text{finite}}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(k,R).

The first isomorphism holds as
\varphi
is quasi-Gorenstein. It is now clear that
R
has finite injective dimension over itself if and only if
S
does.
∎

4.
Virtually G Small

In this section we use a Gorenstein version of virtually small to give a sufficient condition for a morphism of dg-algebras to be quasi-Gorenstein, which is new even for homomorphisms of local rings. Virtually G small complexes were first studied by Dywer, Greenlees, and Iyengar
[
13
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
R
be a dg-algebra. A dg-module
M\in\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
is virtually
G
small if there exists
F_{M}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Thick}}_{R}M
so that
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}F_{M}<\infty
.

4.2
.

Let
R
be a dg-algebra and let
x
be an element in
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
. Under the identification

\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)\simeq\operatorname{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(R,R))\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{D}(R)}(R,R),

x
corresponds to an endomorphism of
R
in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
. Denote the cone of this morphism by
R/\!/x
. In
[
23
, Section 2.2]
and
[
26
, Section 5]
it is shown that
R/\!/x
has the structure of a dg-algebra. If
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is a sequence in
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
, then
R/\!/\underline{x}
is defined inductively as
(R/\!/(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}))/\!/x_{n}
. Moreover, we have
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R/\!/\underline{x})\cong\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)/\underline{x}
and that
\operatorname{H}(R/\!/\underline{x})
is finitely generated over
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R/\!/\underline{x})
. In particular,
\operatorname{H}(R/\!/\underline{x})
has finite length if
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R/\!/\underline{x})
is artinian.

A dg
R
-module
M
is in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{fl}}(R)
if
M
is in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
and
\operatorname{H}(M)
has finite length as an
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
-module.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
R
be a dg-algebra. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

R
is Gorenstein.

(2)

Every
M\in\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
is virtually G small.

(3)

Every
M\in\mathsf{D_{b}^{fl}}(R)
is virtually G small.

Note that 1 implies 2 by
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
. To see that 3 implies 1, let
\underline{x}
be a system of parameters for
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
. By
[
6
, Lemma 7.12]
there exists a dg-module
E
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(R/\!/\underline{x},E)
has finite injective dimension and so that
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(R/\!/\underline{x},E)
is in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{fl}}(R)
. By assumption there is a dg-module
F_{M}
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Thick}}_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(R/\!/\underline{x},E)
that has finite Gorenstein dimension. Since
F_{M}
also has finite injective dimension,
R
is Gorenstein by
[
29
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
be a morphism of dg-algebras with
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}S<\infty
. Then there is a isomorphism in
\mathsf{D}(S)

\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R),\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R))\simeq S.

Proof.

Just note that we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R),\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R)\otimes_{S}^{\mathsf{L}}S,R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(S,\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(S,R),R))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{S}(S,S)

\displaystyle\simeq S,

where the first and second isomorphisms are in
\mathsf{D}(S)
by
[
31
, Proposition 12.10.12]
, and the third isomorphism holds by the same reasoning in
[
10
, Proposition 12.3.19]
since
S
is derived reflexive over
R
.
∎

The following was proved in
[

, Theorem 6.2]

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
R
be a dg-algebra with

a noetherian local ring and let

be a dg-module. If
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,M)
has finite projective dimension and

\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,M)
and

Proof.

This result was proved in

The following is new even when both
R
and
S
are rings.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow S
be a morphism of dg-algebras with

5.
The canonical augmentation of the Koszul complex

In this section, for a dg-algebra
R
, we study the quasi-Gorensteinness of the canonical augmentation
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
, focusing on when
R
is a Koszul complex.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that for a dg-algebra
R
, if the canonical augmentation
R\to\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is quasi-Gorenstein, then
\operatorname{amp}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{0}(R),R)=0
. Since
\sup\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{0}(R),R)=\sup R
by
[
25
, Proposition 3.3]
, we have that
\inf\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{0}(R),R)=\sup R
. Thus, by the definition of Gorenstein dimension, we have that
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)=-\sup R
. This unintuitively implies that the Gorenstein dimension of
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
with respect to
R
is always nonpositive if it is finite.

It is not always the case that
R
and
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
are simultaneously Gorenstein, and so by
Proposition

3.7
it is not always the case that the canonical augmentation is quasi-Gorenstein. A consequence of
[
28
, Proposition 5]
is that it is always possible to write down a dg-algebra
R
that is not Gorenstein such that
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is Gorenstein. The next example shows that it is possible for a dg-algebra
R
to be Gorenstein without
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
being Gorenstein.

Example 5.2
.

Let
A=k[[x,y]]
where
k
is a field and let
R
be the Koszul complex on
x^{2},xy
. Then
A
is Gorenstein, and so
R
is Gorenstein by
[
15
, Theorem 4.9]
, but
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)=A/(x^{2},xy)
is not Gorenstein. Now by
Proposition

3.7

R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is not quasi-Gorenstein.

The following gives an example where the canonical augmentation is quasi-Gorenstein.

Example 5.3
.

Let
A=k[[x,y]]/(xy)
where
k
is a field. Let
R
be the Koszul complex on
x
, and consider the natural morphisms
A\xrightarrow{\psi}R\xrightarrow{\varphi}\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
. Since both
A
and
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
are Gorenstein,
\varphi\psi
is quasi-Gorenstein by
[
3
, 7.7.2]
. Also,
\psi
is quasi-Gorenstein by
[
15
, Lemma 4.8]
, and so
\varphi
is quasi-Gorenstein by
Proposition

3.5
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
R
be a dg-algebra. If
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is quasi-Gorenstein, then
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
and
\operatorname{H}_{\sup{R}}(R)
are isomorphic as
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
-modules.

Proof.

The morphism
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
being quasi-Gorenstein gives an isomorphism between
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{0}(R),R)
and a shift of
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
, and
[
25
, Proposition 3.3]
gives an isomorphism

\operatorname{H}_{\sup{R}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{H}_{0}(R),R))\cong\operatorname{H}_{\sup{R}}(R),

and so
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
and
\operatorname{H}_{\sup{R}}(R)
must be isomorphic.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local ring, let
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a sequence in
A
, let
R
be the Koszul complex on
\underline{x}
, and assume
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\neq 0
. If
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is quasi-Gorenstein, then
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\cong A/(\underline{x})
. Moreover, we have
\operatorname{ann}_{A}\left({\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})}\right)=(\underline{x})
,
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)=-n
, and
\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}A/(\underline{x})=0
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{H}_{n}(R)\cong\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\neq 0
, we have the equality
\sup{R}=n
, and so we have

\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\cong\operatorname{H}_{n}(R)\cong\operatorname{H}_{\sup{R}}(R)\cong\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)\cong A/(\underline{x}),

where the third isomorphism is by
Lemma

5.4
.

Moreover, the isomorphism
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\cong A/(\underline{x})
gives an equality
\operatorname{ann}_{A}\left({\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})}\right)=(\underline{x})
and
Remark

5.1
gives the first equality below

\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)=-\sup{R}=-n.

Finally,
Proposition

3.4
gives the first equality below

\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)=\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}R+\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)=n-n=0,

finishing the proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The converse of
Proposition

5.5
is false. Take
A=k[[x,y,z]]/(xy,xz)
and consider the sequence
y,z
. We have that
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(y,z)=(x)
and that
A/(y,z)\cong(x)
as
A
-modules, and so
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(y,z)\cong A/(y,z)
. However,
A
is not Gorenstein and
A/(y,z)
is Gorenstein, and so the map
A\to A/(y,z)
cannot be quasi-Gorenstein. Thus, the map from the Koszul complex
R
on
(y,z)
to
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
cannot be quasi-Gorenstein by
Proposition

3.6
.

Remark 5.7
.

Let
A
be an artinian local ring that is not Gorenstein. Then there exists an ideal
I
such that
\operatorname{ann}_{A}\left({\operatorname{ann}_{A}I}\right)\neq I
, see
[
8
, Exercise 3.2.15]
. Let
R
be the Koszul complex on a generating set for
I
. Since
A
is artinian, every nonzero ideal has a nonzero annihilator, and so
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is not quasi-Gorenstein by
Proposition

5.5
. This gives examples where the canonical augmentation of the Koszul complex is not quasi-Gorenstein.

Below we recall the definition of an exact sequence, see
[
12
,
18
,
22
]
.

5.8
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local ring. An element
x\in A
is an exact zero divisor if it satisfies

A\neq\operatorname{ann}_{A}(x)\cong A/(x)\neq 0.

This is equivalent to saying that there is a nonzero element
y\in A
such that
\operatorname{ann}_{A}x=(y)
and
\operatorname{ann}_{A}y=(x)
. A sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is a sequence of exact zero divisors if
x_{i}
is an exact zero divisor on
A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})
for
1\leq i\leq n
. The element
x
is called exact if
x
is
A
-regular or is an exact zero divisor. A sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is called exact if
x_{i}
is exact on
A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Remark 5.9
.

The assumption on the annihilator in
Proposition

5.5
happens often. Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a sequence in a noetherian local ring
A
.

(1)

If the length of the longest
A
-regular sequence in
(\underline{x})
is zero, then
\sup{R}=n
by the depth sensitivity of the Koszul complex
[
8
, Theorem 1.6.17]
. Thus, we have that
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\cong\operatorname{H}_{n}(R)\neq 0
.

(2)

By
[
4
, Theorem 3.7]
if
\underline{x}
is a sequence of exact zero divisors, then the length of the longest
A
-regular sequence in
(\underline{x})
is zero.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local ring, let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a sequence in
A
, and let
R_{i}
be the Koszul complex on
x_{1},\dots,x_{i}
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})\rightarrow A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})
is quasi-Gorenstein for
1\leq i\leq n
.

(2)

R_{i}\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R_{i})
is quasi-Gorenstein for
1\leq i\leq n
.

(3)

R_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R_{n})
is quasi-Gorenstein and
\operatorname{Gdim}_{A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})}A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})
is finite for
1\leq i\leq n
.

(4)

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is an exact sequence.

Proof.

The equivalence of (1) and (2) follows from Propositions
3.5
and
3.6
, and (2) implies (3) by definition. To see that (3) implies (1), consider the composition
A\rightarrow R_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R_{n})
. The first arrow is quasi-Gorenstein by
[
15
, Lemma 4.8]
and the second arrow is quasi-Gorenstein by assumption, and so
A\to\operatorname{H}_{0}(R_{n})
is quasi-Gorenstein by
Proposition

3.6
. Therefore
A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})\rightarrow A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})
is quasi-Gorenstein for all
1\leq i\leq n
by repeated applications of
[
3
, Theorem 8.10.b]
.

We now show the equivalence of (1) and (4). We have (4) implies (1) by
[
4
, 3.1 and Theorem 3.7]
and
[
16
, Remark 8]
. We now show (1) implies (4). If
\operatorname{ann}_{A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})}(x_{i})=0
, then
x_{i}
is a nonzerodivisor on
A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})
. If
\operatorname{ann}_{A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})}(x_{i})\neq 0
, then consider the composition

A/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})\longrightarrow K_{A/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})}(x_{i})\longrightarrow A/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})

where
K_{A/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})}(x_{i})
is the Koszul complex on
x_{i}
over
A/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})
. The first arrow is quasi-Gorenstein by
[
15
, Lemma 4.8]
, and the composition is quasi-Gorenstein by assumption. Thus, the second arrow is quasi-Gorenstein by
Proposition

3.5
, and so
\operatorname{ann}_{A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})}(x_{i})
and
A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i})
are isomorphic by
Proposition

5.5
. This tells us that
x_{i}
is an exact zero divisor by definition.
∎

The following example illustrates that all the maps
A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})\rightarrow A/(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})
in
Theorem

5.10
must be quasi-Gorenstein for the sequence to be exact, and it is taken from
[

, Example 4.8]
.

Example 5.11
.

Let
k
be a field and let
A=k[[x,y,z]]/(x^{2},y^{2}+xz,z^{2})
. Then
A
is artinian and Gorenstein. The sequence
x,y,z
is exact, as
A/(x)
,
A/(x,y)
, and
A/(x,y,z)
are all Gorenstein. However, the map
A\to A/(y)
is not quasi-Gorenstein, as
A/(y)
is not Gorenstein. As expected, the sequence

is not exact by
[
12
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Now using
Theorem

5.10
we can build new quasi-Gorenstein morphisms of dg-algebras from old ones.

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
R
be a dg-algebra and let
x
be in
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
. If
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is quasi-Gorenstein and

is exact, then
R/\!/x\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R/\!/x)
is quasi-Gorenstein.

Proof.

Consider the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R/\!/x}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}_{0}(R/\!/x)\cong\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)/(x)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

Since

is quasi-Gorenstein by

, and so

is quasi-Gorenstein by

. This implies that

is quasi-Gorenstein. Since

has finite projective dimension and is isomorphic to a shift of

∎

6.
More (non)examples of quasi-Gorenstein morphisms

We now provide more examples of dg-algebra morphisms, some of which are quasi-Gorenstein and some of which are not.

6.1.
dg-algebra resolutions

Theorem

5.10
gives examples where
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is quasi-Gorenstein and
R
is a Koszul complex. In
Proposition

6.1
we give a way of producing dg-algebras
R
that are not Koszul complexes such that
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is quasi-Gorenstein. In the following proposition, when we say a homomorphism is Gorenstein, we mean it is quasi-Gorenstein and has finite projective dimension.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local ring, let
I\subseteq J
be ideals in
A
such that
A\rightarrow A/I
is Gorenstein and
A\rightarrow A/J
is quasi-Gorenstein. If
R
is a free resolution of
A/I\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}A/J
that has a dg-algebra structure, then both
A\rightarrow R
and
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
are quasi-Gorenstein and the amplitude of
R
is positive.

We prove the proposition after the following remark.

Remark 6.2
.

Here we give a way of finding explicit
A,I,J,R
as in
Proposition

6.1
.

(1)

Under the setup of
Proposition

6.1
, one would expect
A/I\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}A/J
to have infinite projective dimension over
A
. This is because
A/I\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}A/J
has finite projective dimension if and only if
A/J
has finite projective dimension by
[
10
, Proposition 16.4.17]
, and there exist quasi-Gorenstein ideals
J
of infinite projective dimension.

(2)

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a Gorenstein local ring that is not regular and let
I
be an ideal such that
A\rightarrow A/I
is Gorenstein. Since
A
is Gorenstein,
A\rightarrow k
is quasi-Gorenstein, and so
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
are ideals that satisfy the assumptions in
Proposition

6.1
. Since
A
is not regular,
k
has infinite projective dimension. Thus,
A/I\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}k
has infinite projective dimension by the paragraph above, which implies
A/I\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}k
cannot be isomorphic to a Koszul complex. See
[
20
, Example 3.2]
for an explicit example of a noetherian local ring
A
and an ideal
I
such that both
A
and
A/I
are Gorenstein but not regular,
A/I
has finite projective dimension, and
I
is not generated by a regular sequence.

Writing down a Gorenstein ideal that is not generated by a regular sequence is far from straightforward. We now explain a way to search for such an ideal. By the Buchsbaum-Eisenbud structure theorem
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
an ideal
I
of grade 3 in a noetherian local ring
A
is Gorenstein if and only if
I
is the ideal of
(n-1)
th order pfaffians of some
n\times n
alternating matrix of rank
n-1
. Moreover, such an
I
is minimally generated by
n
elements. It is easy to see that a Gorenstein ideal of grade 1 is generated by a nonzerodivisor, and it is a consequence of the Hilbert-Burch theorem that a Gorenstein ideal of grade 2 is generated by a regular sequence, see
[
8
, Theorem 1.4.17]
.

Let
I
be an ideal in a Cohen-Macaulay local ring
A
. Then the height of
I
is at most the minimal number of generators of
I
, and the two are equal if and only if
I
is generated by a regular sequence. This implies that a Gorenstein ideal of grade 3 coming from an
n\times n
alternating matrix where
n>\dim{A}
cannot be generated by a regular sequence. One can use Macaulay2
[
17
]
to compute pfaffians of matrices and the grade of an ideal.

(3)

Such an
R
in
Proposition

6.1
exists. To see this, let
I
and
J
be ideals in a ring
A
. Then by
[
1
, Proposition 2.1.10]
both
A/I
and
A/J
have a free resolution with a dg-algebra structure. Now take
R
to be the tensor product of two such resolutions.

Note that it follows from
[
9
, Proposition 1.3]
that the minimal free resolution of a cyclic module of projective dimension at most three can be given a dg algebra structure, but by
[
1
, Theorem 2.3.1]
there exists a cyclic module whose minimal free resolution admits no dg-algebra structure. This tells us that if both
I
and
J
are Gorenstein ideals of grade at most 3,
R
can be taken to be the tensor product of the minimal free resolutions of
A/I
and
A/J
.

Proof of
Proposition

6.1
.

We first show that
R\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
is quasi-Gorenstein. Consider

A\xrightarrow{f}R\xrightarrow{g}\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)\cong A/J.

Since
gf
is quasi-Gorenstein, by
Proposition

3.5
it is enough to show
f
is quasi-Gorenstein. Since
A/I
has finite projective dimension and since
A/J
has finite Gorenstein dimension,
A/I\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}A/J
has finite Gorenstein dimension. Also, the following isomorphisms hold:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(R,A)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(A/I\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}A/J,A)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(A/I,\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(A/J,A))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(A/I,A)\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(A/J,A)

\displaystyle\sim A/I\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}A/J

\displaystyle\simeq R.

The third isomorphism is by
[
10
, Corollary 12.3.23]
since
A/I
has finite projective dimension. This shows that
f
is quasi-Gorenstein. To see that the amplitude of
R
is positive, just note that
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{1}(A/I,A/J)\neq 0
since
IJ\neq I\cap J
by Nakayama’s lemma.
∎

6.2.
Trivial extensions

In this subsection we give a way of producing morphisms of dg-algebras of positive amplitude, some of which are quasi-Gorenstein and some of which are not, by using trivial extensions. For a dg-algebra
R
and a dg-module
M
, the trivial extension of
R
by
M
, denoted by
R\ltimes M
, is the dg-module
R\oplus M
with multiplication defined by

(r_{1},m_{1})\cdot(r_{2},m_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(r_{1}r_{2},r_{1}m_{2}+m_{1}r_{2}).

By
[
26
, Proposition 7.1]
if
M
is in
\mathsf{D_{b}^{f}}(R)
and
\inf{M}>0
, then
\operatorname{H}_{0}(R\ltimes M)\cong\operatorname{H}_{0}(R)
and
R\ltimes M
is a dg-algebra as in
1.1
. For more on trivial extensions in the dg-algebra setting, see
[
21
]
and
[
26
, Section 7]
.

It is a well-known fact in commutative algebra that a noetherian local ring has a canonical module if and only if R is the homomorphic image of a Gorenstein local ring. This fact has been extended by Jørgensen to show that a noetherian local ring has a dualizing complex if and only if it is the homomorphic image of a Gorenstein dg-algebra
[
21
, Theorem 2.2]
. We now adapt Jørgensen’s proof to show that a dg-algebra has a dualizing dg-module if and only if it is the homomorphic image of a Gorenstein dg-algebra.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
R
be a dg-algebra and let
D
be a dg
R
-module with
\inf{D}>0
. Then
D
is a dualizing dg
R
-module if and only if
R\ltimes D
is a Gorenstein dg-algebra.

Proof.

First assume
D
is a dualizing dg
R
-module. In the canonical morphism
R\to R\ltimes D
, the induced homomorphism on the zeroth homologies is an isomorphism. Thus, by
[
30
, Proposition 7.5]
, we have that
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(R\ltimes D,D)
is a dualizing dg (
R\ltimes D
)-module. Now a similar argument as in
[
21
, Theorem 2.2]
shows that
R\ltimes D
is a Gorenstein dg-algebra.

Now suppose that
R\ltimes D
is a Gorenstein dg-algebra. Then
R\ltimes D
is a dualizing dg (
R\ltimes D
)-module. In the canonical morphism
R\ltimes D\to R
, the induced homomorphism on the zeroth homologies is an isomorphism. Thus, by
[
30
, Proposition 7.5]
, we have that
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R\ltimes D}(R,R\ltimes D)
is a dualizing dg
R
-module.
∎

For the next result, recall that if
R
is homologically
\mathfrak{m}
-adically complete, then
R
has a dualizing dg-module by
[
25
, Proposition 7.21]
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
R
be a dg-algebra. Then the following hold.

(1)

Assume
D
is a dualizing dg-module with
\inf{D}>0
. Then
R\rightarrow R\ltimes D
is quasi-Gorenstein if and only if
R
is Gorenstein.

(2)

The canonical morphism
R\rightarrow R\ltimes\Sigma^{n}R
is quasi-Gorenstein.

Proof.

We first prove (1). By
Proposition

6.3

R\ltimes D
is Gorenstein, and so
R\rightarrow R\ltimes D
being quasi-Gorenstein implies
R
is Gorenstein by
Proposition

3.7
. Now if
R
is Gorenstein, then
R
is a dualizing dg
R
-module, and so
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(R\ltimes D,R)
is a dualizing dg
(R\ltimes D)
-module by
[
30
, Proposition 7.5]
. Since dualizing dg modules are unique in the derived category up to shift by
[
30
, Corollary 7.16]
and since
R\ltimes D
is Gorenstein by
Proposition

6.3
,
R\rightarrow R\ltimes D
is quasi-Gorenstein.

We now prove (2). The following isomorphisms hold in
\mathsf{D}(R)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(R\ltimes\Sigma^{n}R,R)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(R\oplus\Sigma^{n}R,R)\simeq R\oplus\Sigma^{-n}R

\displaystyle\simeq\Sigma^{-n}(R\oplus\Sigma^{n}R)

\displaystyle\simeq\Sigma^{-n}(R\ltimes\Sigma^{n}R).

This shows that
\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(R\ltimes\Sigma^{n}R,R)\sim(R\ltimes\Sigma^{n}R)
in
\mathsf{D}(R)
, and a similar argument as
[
21
, Theorem 2.2]
gives that this isomorphism arises from a morphism in
\mathsf{D}(R\ltimes D)
. Thus, the canonical morphism is quasi-Gorenstein.
∎

7.
Gorenstein projective modules over Koszul complexes

In this section we give ways of producing nonfree Gorenstein projective dg modules over Koszul complexes.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local ring, let
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a sequence of exact zero divisors, and let
R
be the Koszul complex on
\underline{x}
. Then we have the equalities
\operatorname{amp}{R}=n
,
\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}A/(\underline{x})=0
, and
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}A/(\underline{x})=-n
.

Proof.

Consider the composition
A\xrightarrow{f}R\xrightarrow{g}A/\underline{x}
. By
Theorem

5.10

g
is quasi-Gorenstein, and by
Remark

5.9

\operatorname{H}_{n}(R)=\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\neq 0
. This implies
\operatorname{amp}{R}=n
. Also, by
Proposition

5.5
we have
\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}A/\underline{x}=0
and
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}A/(\underline{x})=-n
.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local ring, let
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a sequence of exact zero divisors, and let
R
be the Koszul complex on
\underline{x}
. If
M
is a finitely generated
A/\underline{x}
-module with finite Gorenstein dimension, then the following equalities hold.

(1)

\operatorname{amp}{\Sigma^{n-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M\oplus\Sigma^{-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M}=\operatorname{amp}{R}
.

(2)

\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}\Sigma^{n-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M\oplus\Sigma^{-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M=0
.

Proof.

Consider the composition
A\xrightarrow{f}R\xrightarrow{g}A/\underline{x}
. For (1), just note that
Lemma

7.1
gives the second equality below

\operatorname{amp}{\Sigma^{n-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M\oplus\Sigma^{-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M}=n=\operatorname{amp}{R}.

For (2), it is by
Lemma

7.1
that we have
\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}A/(\underline{x})=0
. Also,
f
is quasi-Gorenstein by
[
15
, Lemma 4.8]
, which implies
gf
is quasi-Gorenstein by
Proposition

3.6
. Now
[
3
, Theorem 7.11]
gives the first equality below

\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M=\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}A/\underline{x}+\operatorname{Gdim}_{A/\underline{x}}M=\operatorname{Gdim}_{A/\underline{x}}M,

and so
\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}\Sigma^{n-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M\oplus\Sigma^{-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M=n
. Therefore,
f
being quasi-Gorenstein gives the first equality below by
Proposition

3.4

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}\Sigma^{n-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M\oplus\Sigma^{-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}\Sigma^{n-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M\oplus\Sigma^{-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}M}M

\displaystyle\hskip 15.00002pt-\operatorname{Gdim}_{A}R

\displaystyle=0.\qed

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local ring, let
R
be a Koszul complex on
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}
, and let
M
be a dg
R
-module. Then

\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,R)\simeq\Sigma^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(M,A).

Proof.

Just note that we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,R)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M,\Sigma^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(R,A))

\displaystyle\simeq\Sigma^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(M\otimes_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}R,A)

\displaystyle\simeq\Sigma^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(M,A).\qed

Recall that by
Remark

5.9
, if
\underline{x}
is a sequence of exact zero divisors, then
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\neq 0
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
A
be a Gorenstein local ring and let
R
be a Koszul complex on a sequence
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. If
M
is a finitely generated Gorenstein projective
A
-module, then
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R=0
. Moreover, if
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\neq 0
and
(0\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}_{M}(\underline{x}))\neq 0
, then the equality
\operatorname{amp}{M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R}=\operatorname{amp}{R}
holds.

Proof.

Since
A
is Gorenstein,
R
is Gorenstein by
[
15
, Theorem 4.8]
, and so
M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R
has finite Gorenstein dimension over
R
. We now compute the Gorenstein dimension of
M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R
. We have the following isomorphisms

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R,R)\simeq\Sigma^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R,A)

\displaystyle\simeq\Sigma^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(M,\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(R,A))

\displaystyle\simeq\Sigma^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(M,\Sigma^{-n}R)

\displaystyle\simeq\Sigma^{n-n}\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{A}(M,R)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,R),

where the first isomorphism is by
Lemma

7.3
and the fifth isomorphism is by
[
11
, Theorem 2.8]
since
M
is Gorenstein projective and
R
is a complex of free
A
modules. Note that

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,R)=\cdots\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,A)\rightarrow 0

where
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,A)
is in degree zero and we have an isomorphism

\operatorname{H}_{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,R))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,A)/(\underline{x})\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,A).

Since
M
is Gorenstein projective over
A
,
M
is reflexive, and so
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,A)
is nonzero, which implies
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,A)/(\underline{x})\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,A)
is nonzero by Nakayama’s Lemma. Therefore

\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R=-\inf{\operatorname{\mathsf{R}Hom}_{R}(M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R,R)}=-\inf{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,R)}=0.

Moreover, we have

\operatorname{H}_{n}(R)\cong\operatorname{ann}_{A}(\underline{x})\neq 0\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\operatorname{H}_{n}(M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R)\cong(0\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}_{M}(\underline{x}))\neq 0,

which implies
\operatorname{amp}{M\otimes_{A}^{\mathsf{L}}R}=n=\operatorname{amp}{R}
, finishing the proof.
∎

The following example shows that rings and modules as in
Proposition

7.4
exist.

Example 7.5
.

Let
k
be a field, let
A=k[[x,y]]/(xy)
, and let
M=A/(x)
. Then both
\operatorname{ann}_{A}(x)
and
(0\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}_{M}(x))
are nonzero. Since the depth of
M
is one and since
A
is a one dimensional Gorenstein local ring,
M
is Gorenstein projective.

Remark 7.6
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local ring, let
R
be the Koszul complex on a sequence
\underline{x}
, and assume
R
is local-Cohen-Macaulay; see
[
26
, Definition 4.2]
for the definition of local-Cohen-Macaulay. If
A
is Cohen Macaulay, then
R
is local-Cohen-Macaulay by
[
27
, Theorem 4.2]
, but the converse is not true, see
[
27
, Remark 4.13]
. By
[
19
, Corollary 1.5]
if
M
is a Gorenstein projective dg-module over
R
such that
\operatorname{amp}{M}=\operatorname{amp}{R}
, then
M
is a maximal local-Cohen-Macaulay dg-module; see
[
27
, Definition 6.1 and Definition 6.4]
for the definition of maximal local-Cohen-Macaulay dg-module. This tells us that if
A
is Cohen-Macaulay and if
\underline{x}
is a sequence of exact zero divisors, then
Proposition

7.2
and
Proposition

7.4
give examples of nonfree maximal local-Cohen-Macaulay dg-modules over local-Cohen-Macaulay dg-algebras.
