 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Savannah River National Laboratory Glass Composition-Properties (ComPro) database is an extensive
database containing pertinent composition and durability data to support the accelerated clean-up mission
at the Defense Waste Processing Facility. The activities described in this data qualification report were
performed to support the information contained in the database.

There were two objectives of the original data qualification process. The first objective was to review
supporting documentation to determine if DOE/RW-0333P Quality Assurance Requirements and
Description had been implemented during the original work. If the DOE/RW-0333P Quality Assurance
Requirements and Description had not been directly implemented during the original work, the second
objective was to determine if the controls that were used were adequate to meet the intent of the DOE/RW-
0333P Quality Assurance Requirements and Description. The results of these two objectives and the
activities performed to support these decisions are described in this document.

An assessment of each dataset was made to determine if the data were RW-0333P Compliant, RW-0333P
Equivalent or Non-RW-0333P Compliant. The original data qualification was performed in accordance
with E7, Conduct of Engineering Manual, Procedure 3.70, Revision 4, Qualification of Data. The specific
method that was used was Equivalent Controls as described in E7, 3.70.

Revision 2 of this document adds supporting information for the RW-0333P Compliant datasets added to
Revision 3 of the database.

1.0 Introduction
The Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) supports glass composition development for the Defense
Waste Processing Facility (DWPF) at the Savannah River Site (SRS). Taylor et al. developed an extensive
database containing pertinent glass composition and durability information to aid in this work. 1 This
database is the Glass Composition – Properties (ComPro) database.

The datasets in the ComPro database were obtained from several laboratories including Idaho National
Engineering & Environmental Laboratory (INEEL), Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL),
SRNL, Vitreous State Laboratory (VSL), and West Valley Demonstration Project (WVDP).

WSRC-RP-2004-00704, “The SRNL Glass Composition – Properties (ComPro) Database” provides a
detailed discussion of the database development efforts including the primary objective of the database, its
intended end use, the verification and validation process used to provide a confidence statement with respect
to the database entries, and a protocol to maintain and update the database as additional information is
obtained.1 SRNL-STI-2009-00093, “The User Guide for the ComPro Database” provides a roadmap for
use of the database in glass development and other studies.2

An assessment of each dataset was made to determine if the data were RW-0333P Compliant, RW-0333P
Equivalent or Non-RW-0333P Compliant. The original data qualification was performed in accordance
with E7, Conduct of Engineering Manual, Procedure 3.70, Revision 4, Qualification of Data. The specific
method that was used was Equivalent Controls as described in E7, 3.70.

Revision 2 of this document adds supporting information for the RW-0333P Compliant datasets added to
Revision 3 of the database.

1.1 Identification of the Datasets to be Qualified
An assessment was performed on all datasets contained in the ComPro database to determine if the controls
in place during the original work were RW-0333P Compliant. The datasets to be qualified are those which
were not classified as RW-0333P Compliant during the initial assessment.

1.2 Proposed Use of Qualified Data
The primary objective of the ComPro database is to provide comprehensive information that includes glass
composition and durability data to support the development of glass compositions for DWPF. However,
this is not the only possible use of these data. The End User of the ComPro database is certainly free to
utilize these data to support other efforts not related to this primary objective.

The datasets identified as RW-0333P Compliant or RW-0333P Equivalent may be used for waste form
affecting activities.

It is not the intent to restrict the use of the reported data to the limited focus of the specific DWPF task at
hand. These data are being made available to potential End Users as an aid to their research of various
composition and property relationships which may be outside the effects of composition on durability. It is,
however, noted that the rigor in which the ComPro database was developed is only applicable to the
composition and durability information. Therefore, any use of the additional physical property data, visual
observation information, and/or physical characterization data (such as X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning
electron microscopy (SEM), and/or transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis) to support model
development or other relationships with the stated compositions should be performed with caution. This
supplemental information has not been subjected to the verification and validation process associated with
the electronic entries of the ComPro database.

2.0 Approach

2.1 Process Used to Collect the Data
A major portion of the ComPro database development process (collecting data) was identifying the
numerous source documents containing relevant data to the stated objective, obtaining an approved copy
of each document, and electronically entering the appropriate data into the database. When possible,
electronic versions of the documents were used to import data into the ComPro database. When an
electronic version of the document was not available, the data were either entered by hand or electronic
spreadsheets were obtained from the authors and the data were “copied and pasted” into the ComPro
database.

2.2 Qualification Method Used and Rationale
The datasets to be qualified have been assessed and qualified, if possible, using the process in E7, Conduct
of Engineering and Technical Support, Procedure 3.70, Revision 4, Qualification of Existing Data. The
specific technique that was used for qualifying data from this procedure was Equivalent Controls.

Equivalent Controls are defined by E7, Procedure 3.70 as
 Qualifying non-qualified data by citing equivalent controls, by:
  examining the difference in controls between the non-qualified data and current
 requirements and,
  determining how much impact the difference in controls could have on the non-
 qualified data.
 If it is concluded that the difference in controls had no significant technical effect, that decision is
 then documented in the conclusion section of the Data Qualification Report.

Because most, if not all, of the data were generated under programs that had some level of Quality
Assurance (QA), it was determined that the Equivalent Controls method was the most cost-effective means
to use for data qualification.

2.3 Data Qualification Process
Three QA Levels were established:
  RW-0333P Compliant – Data originally obtained under a program that met the DOE/RW-0333P
 QARD3 or its predecessor RW-0214. Implementation of all revisions of the DOE/RW-0333P
 QARD is accepted in this category. Since work is this category was initially RW-0333P Compliant,
 there is no need to re-qualify these data. No additional level of confidence in the data would be
 obtained by qualifying the data to later revisions relative to Revision 0 of the DOE/RW-0333P
 QARD.
  RW-0333P Equivalent – Data obtained under a program that did not clearly identify compliance
 with the DOE/RW-0333P QARD. These are the data that were qualified to the DOE/RW-0333P
 QARD as described in this report.
  Non-RW-0333P Compliant – Insufficient evidence was available to determine that these data were
 collected under a QA Program that either implemented at the time or met the intent of the
        DOE/RW-0333P QARD.

Using these three QA Levels the following steps were implemented to classify the datasets in the ComPro
database.

Source documents referenced in the ComPro database were collected from various laboratories. A review
of the documents was performed to determine if a QA Level, e.g. DOE/RW-0333P, had already been
identified in these documents.

 2

If no QA Level was identified in the source documents, a detailed review was performed on each document
to identify controls that were implemented during data generation. Examples of such controls are measuring
& test equipment (M&TE), procedures, planning documents, standards, equipment listings, sample
identification, etc. The results of this extensive review are documented in Table 2-1. After identifying
controls that were implemented during the work and documenting this information in Table 2-1, these
controls were assessed to determine if they met the intent of RW-0333P requirements that would be
applicable to the task. A decision was also made as to whether the lack of implementation of some controls
would have had an adverse impact on the data quality. If the controls that were implemented met the intent
of applicable controls and the lack of evidence of applicable controls had no adverse impact, the data were
categorized as RW-0333P Equivalent.

If enough information was not provided in the associated report to make the determination that the data
were RW-0333P Equivalent, then the data set was classified as Non-RW-0333P Compliant.

If there was no hard copy of the source document to review, the controls could not be assessed and that is
noted in Table 2-1.
