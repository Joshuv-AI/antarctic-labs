Negative diffusivity of excitons in electron-hole plasmas
H. Terças, V. N. Mantsevich

Abstract
We develop a minimal hydrodynamic framework to describe exciton transport in the presence of an electron–hole plasma in two - dimensional semiconductors. Treating excitons, electrons, and holes as coupled fluids, we show that exciton diffusion is strongly renormalized by momentum exchange with the plasma. In the collisional regime, mutual diffusion leads to a nontrivial redistribution of transport coefficients but preserves the positivity of the exciton diffusivity. In contrast, when plasma inertia and collective charge oscillations are accounted for, the exciton diffusive mode hybridizes with acoustic plasma modes, giving rise to a dynamical instability manifested as an effective negative diffusion coefficient. We demonstrate that this instability originates from the non - equilibrium coupling between slow excitons and fast plasma degrees of freedom, rather than from nonlinear diffusion or thermodynamic effects. Our results provide a unified physical mechanism for negative exciton diffusivity reported in recent experiments and establish collective plasma dynamics as a key control parameter of exciton transport in two - dimensional materials.

In this work, we introduce a minimal theoretical framework to investigate exciton diffusion in the presence of an electron–hole plasma. Treating excitons, electrons, and holes as coupled fluids, we analyze how momentum exchange and collective plasma dynamics modify exciton transport. We first consider the diffusive regime in a quasi-neutral plasma, neglecting plasmons, and derive the corresponding effective diffusion equations. We then incorporate plasma inertia and collective charge oscillations, demonstrating the emergence of an exciton bubble instability associated with an effective negative diffusion coefficient.

II
Basic model

We start by constructing the most basic, yet phenomenologically correct theory describing the diffusive processes of excitons in a two-dimensional electron-hole plasma. The goal is to provide a theoretical justification for the renormalization of the intrinsic exciton diffusion coefficient,
\displaystyle D_{X}=k_{B}T/(m_{X}\gamma_{X})
, arising from the coupled dynamics of excitons, electrons, and holes, where
\displaystyle\gamma_{X}
accounts for intrinsic processes such as finite lifetime and impurity scattering. We consider a two-dimensional semiconductor (e.g., a TMD) in which these three fluids coexist and interact. The exciton fluid is governed by the following equations

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial n_{X}}{\partial t}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(n_{X}{\bf u}_{X}\right)=0\\
\\
\left(\frac{D}{Dt}+\gamma_{X}\right){\bf u}_{X}=-k_{B}T_{X}\frac{\bm{\nabla}n_{X}}{m_{X}n_{X}}-\sum_{\alpha=\{e,h\}}\gamma_{\alpha}\left({\bf u}_{X}-{\bf u}_{\alpha}\right),\end{array}

(1)

where
\displaystyle D/Dt=\partial/\partial t+\bf v\cdot\bm{\nabla}
is the Lagrange derivative,
\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha}
is the rate at which elastic collisions with the
\displaystyle\alpha-
th species take place. Conversely, the electrons/holes are described by the following equations

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial n_{\alpha}}{\partial t}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(n_{\alpha}{\bf u}_{\alpha}\right)=0\\
\\
\frac{D}{Dt}{\bf u}_{\alpha}=\frac{q_{\alpha}{\bf E}}{m_{\alpha}}-k_{B}T_{\alpha}\frac{\bm{\nabla}n_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}}+\gamma_{\alpha}\left({\bf u}_{X}-{\bf u}_{\alpha}\right),\\
\\
\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bf E}=\frac{e}{\epsilon_{0}}\left(\rho_{h}-\rho_{e}\right)=\frac{e}{\epsilon_{0}}\left(n_{h}-n_{e}\right)\delta(z),\end{array}

(2)

where the Dirac-delta
\displaystyle\delta(z)
assures that the physics is happening in two dimensions.

III
Exciton diffusion in collisional plasmas

As a first approach, we shall consider that quasi-neutrality is satisfied,
\displaystyle n_{e}\simeq n_{h}
, such that the Poisson Eq. in (
2
) can be neglected. In an intermediate situation, the so-called
ambipolar regime
, an ambipolar electric field
\displaystyle{\bf E}_{\rm amb}
is produced, keeping the electron-hole plasma moving together. However, this effect is particular prominent in heavy-hole systems,
\displaystyle m_{h}/m_{e}\gg 1
. While we may consider this variation quite straigthforwardly, for the purpose of the current estimations we assume the symmetric effective mass case,
\displaystyle m_{e}\sim m_{h}
, such that
\displaystyle{\bf E}_{\rm amb}\sim 0
. The situation of close effective masses of electrons and holes is realized, in particular, in Mo
\displaystyle-
based TMDs
Kormnyos2015
. Since electrons and holes move as whole, it is therefore convenient to work with reduced variables,

\displaystyle\displaystyle n_{p}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{m_{e}n_{e}+m_{h}n_{h}}{m_{e}+m_{h}}\simeq n_{e}+n_{h},

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\bf u}_{p}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{m_{e}{\bf u}_{e}+m_{h}{\bf u}_{h}}{m_{e}+m_{h}}\simeq\frac{{\bf u}_{e}+{\bf u}_{h}}{2}.

(3)

From Eq. (
2
), we may write

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial n_{p}}{\partial t}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(n_{p}{\bf u}_{p}\right)\simeq 0\\
\\
\frac{D}{Dt}{\bf u}_{p}\simeq-k_{B}T_{p}\frac{\bm{\nabla}n_{p}}{m_{p}n_{p}}+\gamma_{p}\left({\bf u}_{X}-{\bf u}_{p}\right).\end{array}

(4)

where
\displaystyle T_{p}=(m_{e}n_{e}T_{e}+m_{h}n_{h}T_{h})/(m_{e}n_{e}+m_{h}n_{h})\simeq(T_{e}+T_{h})/2
, is the plasma temperature, and
\displaystyle\gamma_{p}=(\gamma_{e}+\gamma_{h})/2
. In that case, the exciton momentum conservation equation reads

\left(\frac{D}{Dt}+\gamma_{X}\right){\bf u}_{x}=-k_{B}T_{X}\frac{\bm{\nabla}n_{X}}{m_{X}n_{X}}-\gamma_{p}\left({\bf u}_{X}-{\bf u}_{p}\right).

(5)

We now assume that the balanced gas condition, in which the exciton and plasma fractions are comparable,
\displaystyle\alpha_{X}\sim\alpha_{p}
. In that case, if one system behaves diffusively, the other will behave similarly. Mathematically, diffusive behavior is expected when the fluid varies slowly in the Lagrange frame, i.e.
\displaystyle D/Dt\ll\gamma_{X,p}
. As such, Eq. (
4
) provides

{\bf u}_{X}^{(\rm diff)}\simeq{\bf u}_{p}^{(\rm diff)}+\frac{k_{B}T_{p}}{n_{p}m_{p}\gamma_{p}}\bm{\nabla}n_{p}.

(6)

Similarly, from Eq. (
5
), the same procedure yields

{\bf u}_{X}^{(\rm diff)}\simeq\frac{\gamma_{p}}{\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{p}}{\bf u}_{p}^{(\rm diff)}-\frac{k_{B}T_{X}}{m_{X}n_{X}\left(\gamma_{X}+\gamma_{p}\right)}\bm{\nabla}n_{X}.

(7)

Solving for both velocities, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\bf u}_{p}^{(\rm diff)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{k_{B}T_{X}}{m_{X}n_{X}\gamma_{X}}\bm{\nabla}n_{X}-\frac{k_{B}T_{p}}{m_{p}n_{p}\widetilde{\gamma}}\bm{\nabla}n_{p},

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\bf u}_{X}^{(\rm diff)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{k_{B}T_{X}}{m_{X}n_{X}\gamma_{X}}\bm{\nabla}n_{X}-\frac{k_{B}T_{p}}{m_{p}n_{p}\gamma_{X}}\bm{\nabla}n_{p},

(8)

where
\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}=\gamma_{X}\gamma_{p}/(\gamma_{X}+\gamma_{p})
is the reduced collision rate. Putting in more explicit grounds, we get the diffusive two-fluid model

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial n_{X}}{\partial t}-D_{X}\nabla^{2}n_{X}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(D_{Xp}\bm{\nabla}n_{p}\right)=0,\\
\\
\frac{\partial n_{p}}{\partial t}-D_{p}\nabla^{2}n_{p}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(D_{pX}\bm{\nabla}n_{X}\right)=0,\end{array}

(9)

where
\displaystyle D_{X}=k_{B}T_{X}/(m_{X}\gamma_{X})
and
\displaystyle D_{p}=k_{B}T_{p}/(m_{p}\widetilde{\gamma})
are the self-diffusion coefficients, and the
\displaystyle D_{Xp}
and
\displaystyle D_{pX}
are the mutual diffusion coefficients,

D_{Xp}=\frac{k_{B}T_{X}}{m_{X}\gamma_{X}}\frac{n_{X}}{n_{p}},\quad D_{pX}=\frac{k_{B}T_{p}}{m_{p}\gamma_{X}}\frac{n_{p}}{n_{X}}.

(10)

III.1
Dilute plasma case: adiabatic ellimination

As a consistency check, we should try to observe if the plasma may, indeed, lead to negative diffusivity of excitons. To see that, let us adiabatically eliminate the plasma from the dynamics, assuming
\displaystyle\partial_{t}n_{p}\ll\partial_{t}n_{X}
. By doing that, we get
\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}n_{p}\simeq-(D_{pX}/D_{p})\bm{\nabla}n_{X}
. Plugging back in the exciton diffusion coefficient in Eq. (
9
), we obtain

\frac{\partial n_{X}}{\partial t}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(\widetilde{D}_{X}\bm{\nabla}n_{X}\right)=0,

(11)

where the effective excition diffusion coefficient is given by

\widetilde{D}_{X}=D_{X}-\frac{D_{pX}D_{Xp}}{D_{p}}=D_{X}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{\gamma_{p}+\gamma_{X}}\right)>0.

(12)

This shows that any negative exciton diffusion must be balanced by positive plasma diffusion. Thus, negative diffusivity is restricted to the exciton subsystem, and the overall system remains positively diffusive.

III.2
Dense plasma case: full self-consistent dynamics

To retrieve such phenomenology, we should couple the plasma dynamics. Coming back to Eq. (
9
), and performing perturbations around some equilibria,
\displaystyle n_{j}\simeq n_{j,0}+n_{j,1}
, we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial n_{X,1}}{\partial t}-D_{X}\nabla^{2}n_{X,1}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(D_{Xp}\bm{\nabla}n_{p,1}\right)=0,\\
\\
\frac{\partial n_{p,1}}{\partial t}-D_{p}\nabla^{2}n_{p,1}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(D_{pX}\bm{\nabla}n_{X,1}\right)=0.\end{array}

(13)

where the diffusion coefficients are intended to be evaluated at zero-th order,
\displaystyle D_{ij}[n_{i},n_{j}]=D_{ij}[n_{i,0},n_{j,0}]
. By performing a Fourier analysis, we get a secular equation of the form
\displaystyle{\rm det}M=0
, where
\displaystyle M
reads

M=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega+iD_{X}k^{2}&-iD_{Xp}k^{2}\\
\\
-iD_{pX}k^{2}&\omega+iD_{p}k^{2}\end{array}\right].

(14)

Observing that
\displaystyle D_{Xp}D_{pX}=D_{X}D_{P}\widetilde{\gamma}/\gamma_{X}
, the density dependence is explicitly eliminated in the secular equation (although it may still exist implicitly via the dependence of the collision rates
\displaystyle\gamma_{X}
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{p}
). Finally, the characteristic polynomial provides

(\omega+iD_{X}k^{2})(\omega+iD_{p}k^{2})-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{\gamma_{p}+\gamma_{X}}D_{p}D_{X}k^{4}=0.

(15)

In the absence of the collisions with the plasma,
\displaystyle\gamma_{p}\to 0
, the excitons exhibit a diffusive mode,
\displaystyle\omega=-iD_{X}k^{2}
, corresponding to pure damping. When the two systems are coupled, however, the dispersion relation reads

\displaystyle\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{ik^{2}}{2}\left[(D_{X}+D_{p})\right.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left.\pm\sqrt{4\frac{\gamma_{p}}{\gamma_{p}+\gamma_{X}}D_{p}D_{X}+(D_{X}-D_{p})^{2}}\right].

(16)

Since
\displaystyle\gamma_{p}/(\gamma_{p}+\gamma_{X})<1
, the imaginary part is always negative, meaning that negative diffusion is absent in diffusive plasmas. In what follows, we unlock an extra degree of freedom of the system, the plasmons, and show how they are at the origin of negative diffusivity of excitons.

III.3
Effect of mass asymmetry: the ambipolar correction

The symmetric-mass approximation
\displaystyle m_{e}\simeq m_{h}
adopted above is
particularly well justified for Mo-based TMDs
Kormnyos2015
, but is less accurate in
W-based compounds (e.g. WSe
2
or WS
2
) where the ratio

\displaystyle m_{h}/m_{e}
can differ from unity by up to
\displaystyle\sim 30\%

Kormnyos2015
.
When
\displaystyle m_{e}\neq m_{h}
, the two carriers no longer drift at the same
speed in the absence of an external field, and charge separation
builds a Dember (ambipolar) electric field
\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\text{amb}}

that enforces quasi-neutrality
van Roosbroeck 1950
;
Meyer and Bartoli 1980
. To incorporate this effect within the present framework, we retain the
full Poisson equation for
\displaystyle\mathbf{E}
and define the ambipolar velocity
\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{p}^{\text{amb}}=-D_{a}\bm{\nabla}n_{p}/n_{p}
and diffusion coefficient in the standard
way
Meyer and Bartoli 1980
;
Nelson et al. 1982
,

D_{a}=\frac{(D_{e}/m_{e}+D_{h}/m_{h})\,m_{e}m_{h}}{m_{e}+m_{h}}\simeq\frac{2D_{e}D_{h}}{D_{e}+D_{h}},

(17)

where
\displaystyle D_{e}=k_{B}T_{e}/(m_{e}\gamma_{e})
and

\displaystyle D_{h}=k_{B}T_{h}/(m_{h}\gamma_{h})
are the single-species diffusion coefficients, and the second equality in Eq. (
17
) holds for
\displaystyle T_{e}=T_{h}
and
\displaystyle m_{e}\simeq m_{h}
. In the presence of mass asymmetry, the plasma continuity equation is modified: the self-diffusion coefficient
\displaystyle D_{p}

is replaced by
\displaystyle D_{a}
, while the cross-diffusion term
\displaystyle D_{pX}
acquires an additional prefactor

\eta=\frac{2m_{e}m_{h}}{(m_{e}+m_{h})^{2}},

(18)

which satisfies
\displaystyle\eta\leq 1
(with
\displaystyle\eta=1/2
in the equal-mass limit). The effective exciton diffusion coefficient
then reads

D_{X}^{\text{eff}}=D_{X}\!\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{\gamma_{p}+\gamma_{X}}\,\eta\right)>0,

(19)

showing that mass asymmetry
reduces
the magnitude of the plasma renormalization relative to the equal-mass case but does not qualitatively alter the physics.

IV
Exciton diffusion in collisionless plasmas: effect of the plasmons

In what follows, we may be interested in the regime in which advection dominates over plasma diffusion, but still subdominant in respect to exciton diffusion,
\displaystyle\gamma_{X}\simeq D/Dt\gg\gamma_{p}
. In such a regime, let us analyze the coupled dynamics of the plasma and exciton subsystems. For such a description, we relax the quasi-neutrality condition in Eqs. (
2
), and conveniently use Poisson’s equation in Fourier space,

\widetilde{\phi}({\bf k})=i\frac{e}{2\varepsilon_{0}k}(\widetilde{n}_{h}-\widetilde{n}_{e}),

(20)

with
\displaystyle n_{0e}=n_{0h}\equiv n_{0}
and
\displaystyle\widetilde{f}(k)=\int d^{2}xf({\bf x})e^{i\bf k\cdot x}
denoting the Fourier transform, which relates to the electric field as
\displaystyle{\bf}\widetilde{E}({\bf k})=i{\bf k}\widetilde{\phi}(k)
.

For excitons, we assume the diffusive limit
\displaystyle\partial_{t}\textbf{u}_{X}\ll\gamma_{X}\textbf{u}_{X}
, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{X}\simeq-\frac{v_{X}^{2}}{\gamma_{X}n_{0X}}\bm{\nabla}n_{X}+\sum_{\alpha}\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{\gamma_{X}}\textbf{u}_{\alpha}.

(21)

Substituting Eq. (
21
) to the continuity equation for excitons, one can get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\partial n_{X}}{\partial t}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(D_{X}\bm{\nabla}n_{X}\right)+\sum_{\alpha}\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{\gamma_{X}}\textbf{u}_{\alpha}=0.

(22)

Taking the second derivative of the electron/hole continuity equation, and putting Eqs. (
20
) and (
21
) together, one can eliminate
\displaystyle\textbf{u}_{X}
from the dynamics of plasma subsystem, thus obtaining

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\omega^{2}n_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\pm\omega_{p}^{2}(n_{e}-n_{h})-i\gamma_{\alpha}\omega n_{\alpha}+v_{\alpha}^{2}k^{2}n_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+i\gamma_{\alpha}n_{0\alpha}{\bf k}\cdot\left[-i\frac{v_{X}^{2}{\bf k}}{n_{0x}\gamma_{X}}n_{X}+\sum_{\alpha}\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{\gamma_{X}}\textbf{u}_{\alpha}\right]=0,

(23)

where
\displaystyle\omega_{p}=\sqrt{e^{2}n_{0}k/(2\varepsilon_{0}m_{e})}
is the 2D (dispersive) plasma frequency. Since
\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha}/\gamma_{X}\ll 1
, the last term in Eq. (
23
) can be neglected, and together with Eq. (
22
), yields the secular system

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[\omega^{2}-(\omega_{p}^{2}+v_{e}^{2}k^{2}+i\gamma_{e}\omega)\right]\widetilde{n}_{e}+\omega_{p}^{2}\widetilde{n}_{h}+\frac{\gamma_{e}}{\gamma_{X}}\bar{v}_{X}^{2}k^{2}\widetilde{n}_{X}=0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[\omega^{2}-\left(\omega_{p}^{2}+v_{h}^{2}k^{2}+i\gamma_{h}\omega\right)\right]\widetilde{n}_{h}+\omega_{p}^{2}\widetilde{n}_{e}+\frac{\gamma_{h}}{\gamma_{X}}\bar{v}_{X}^{2}k^{2}\widetilde{n}_{X}=0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle-i\omega\widetilde{n}_{X}+D_{X}k^{2}\widetilde{n}_{X}+i\omega\frac{n_{0X}}{n_{0}}\left(\frac{\gamma_{e}}{\gamma_{X}}\widetilde{n}_{e}+\frac{\gamma_{h}}{\gamma_{X}}\widetilde{n}_{h}\right)=0,

(24)

with
\displaystyle\bar{v}_{X}=v_{X}\sqrt{n_{0}/n_{0X}}
denoting the renormalized exction thermal speed. The dispersion relation contains three roots, which, in the absence of coupling (
\displaystyle\gamma_{e}=\gamma_{h}=0
), correspond to the bare modes
\displaystyle\omega={\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}}
, where

\omega_{1}=-iD_{X}k^{2},\quad\omega_{2}=v_{\rm th}k,\quad\omega_{3}=\sqrt{2\omega_{p}^{2}+v_{\rm th}^{2}k^{2}},

(25)

with
\displaystyle v_{\rm th}\simeq(v_{e}+v_{h})/2
, respectively corresponding to the exciton diffusive mode, and to the plasma acoustic and optical modes. For finite values of
\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha}
, the bare modes hybridize. Interestingly, the diffusive mode mixes with the acoustic mode of the plasma and acquires a positive imaginary part, which is associated to a dynamical instability (or gain), as depicted in Fig.
1
. Such a gain can be reinterpreted as the emergence of a negative diffusion coefficient.

To explicitly observe the effects of the plasma in the diffusion processes, we introduce the auxiliary functions

\displaystyle A_{e}=\omega^{2}-(\omega_{p}^{2}+v_{e}^{2}k^{2}+i\gamma_{e}\omega)
and
\displaystyle A_{h}=\omega^{2}-(\omega_{p}^{2}+v_{h}^{2}k^{2}+i\gamma_{h}\omega)
, such that the determinant of the secular matrix in Eq. (
24
)
reads

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(-i\omega+D_{X}k^{2}\right)\left(A_{e}A_{h}-\omega_{p}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-i\omega\,\frac{\bar{v}_{X}^{2}k^{2}}{\gamma_{X}^{2}}\left(\gamma_{e}^{2}A_{h}+\gamma_{h}^{2}A_{e}-2\gamma_{e}\gamma_{h}\omega_{p}^{2}\right)=0.

(26)

This allows us to define an effective exciton diffusion coefficient in Fourier
space as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[-i\omega+\widetilde{D}_{X}(k,\omega)k^{2}\right]\widetilde{n}_{X}=0,

(27)

with

\widetilde{D}_{X}(k,\omega)=D_{X}-i\omega\,\frac{\bar{v}_{X}^{2}}{\gamma_{X}^{2}}\frac{\gamma_{e}^{2}A_{h}+\gamma_{h}^{2}A_{e}-2\gamma_{e}\gamma_{h}\omega_{p}^{2}}{A_{e}A_{h}-\omega_{p}^{4}}.

(28)

Equation (
28
) makes explicit that the exciton diffusivity is
renormalized by the full collective response of the electron–hole plasma,
including both acoustic and optical branches. In the symmetric limit,
\displaystyle\gamma_{e}=\gamma_{h}\equiv\gamma
and

\displaystyle v_{e}=v_{h}\equiv v_{\rm th}
, the optical branch decouples exactly by symmetry, and one finds

\widetilde{D}_{X}(k,\omega)=D_{X}-i\omega\,\frac{2\gamma^{2}\bar{v}_{X}^{2}}{\gamma_{X}^{2}\left(\omega^{2}-v_{\rm th}^{2}k^{2}-i\gamma\omega\right)}.

(29)

The onset of the instability can be estimated from Eq. (
29
)
by requiring
\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(\omega)>0
, leading to the approximate condition
\displaystyle\gamma^{2}\bar{v}_{X}^{2}\gtrsim\gamma_{X}^{2}v_{\rm th}^{2}

in the long-wavelength limit. Therefore, the effective exciton diffusion coefficient is controlled by the
acoustic plasma response, while the optical plasmon remains present in the full secular equation but does not couple to the exciton mode in the perfectly symmetric case. Physically, this instability can be understood as a delayed feedback mechanism:
density gradients in the exciton subsystem excite plasma waves, which in turn induce currents that reinforce the original perturbation, leading to amplification instead of dissipation.

V
Effective temperature of electron-hole plasma and excitons

We now estimate the effective temperature of two sub-systems based on typical TMD experimental parameters. In analyzing the electron–hole plasma, we perform the estimates considering only electrons and assume that the hole temperature is the same. Such an approximation implies that electron and hole effective masses are taken to be equal
Kormnyos2015
, and that their scattering times are also the same. We therefore use the relation
\displaystyle D_{X(e-h)}=k_{B}T_{X(e-h)}(\tau_{s}^{X(e-h)}/m_{X(e-h)})
for the diffusion coefficient
Mouri et al. 2014
;
Kulig et al. 2018

VII
Conclusion

We have developed a minimal hydrodynamic framework to describe exciton transport in the presence of an electron–hole plasma in two-dimensional semiconductors. Treating excitons, electrons, and holes as coupled fluids, we have shown that momentum exchange with the plasma provides a natural and unified mechanism for the strong renormalization of exciton diffusion observed in experiments.

In the collisional, quasi-neutral regime, mutual diffusion between excitons and plasma leads to a redistribution of transport coefficients while preserving the positivity of the effective exciton diffusivity. By contrast, when plasma inertia and collective charge dynamics are taken into account, the exciton diffusive mode hybridizes with acoustic plasma excitations. This coupling gives rise to a dynamical instability, manifested as an effective negative diffusion coefficient. Importantly, the instability does not rely on nonlinear diffusion laws or thermodynamic anomalies, but instead emerges from the non-equilibrium coupling between slow excitonic motion and fast plasma degrees of freedom.

A key experimental fingerprint of the mechanism proposed here is the transient spatial contraction of the exciton cloud at early times, followed by re-expansion once the plasma sufficiently relaxes. Such non-monotonic dynamics is qualitatively distinct from the scenario proposed by Wietek
et al.

Wietek et al. 2024
, where negative diffusivity originates from a nonlinear coupling between a rapidly expanding electron–hole plasma and slower excitonic populations across the Mott threshold. In that picture, the effective diffusion coefficient becomes density dependent and the sign reversal is transient and spatially homogeneous. In contrast, the present mechanism predicts a fundamentally
wavevector-selective
instability: density fluctuations satisfying

\gamma^{2}v_{X}^{2}\gtrsim\gamma_{X}^{2}\left(v_{\rm th}^{2}k^{2}+\omega_{p}^{2}/k^{2}\right)

are amplified, whereas longer- and shorter-wavelength modes remain stable. Such momentum selectivity should be experimentally accessible using spatially resolved pump–probe or transient-grating spectroscopy in highly excited TMD monolayers.

Our results therefore provide a transparent physical interpretation of recent observations of effective negative exciton diffusion in multicomponent systems, while clarifying the distinct roles played by collisional transport and collective plasma dynamics. More broadly, they highlight the importance of treating excitons as part of an interacting many-fluid system rather than as isolated diffusive quasiparticles.

The present work also calls attention to the role of
collective
plasma excitations, rather than individual carrier scattering events, as a dominant control parameter of exciton transport. While exciton–phonon and exciton–exciton interactions have been extensively investigated, the coupling between excitonic density fluctuations and the acoustic branch of the electron–hole plasma has so far remained largely unexplored. Since the present framework can be naturally extended to include disorder, finite recombination, and external driving fields, it may serve as a starting point for a unified description of the broad phenomenology of exciton transport in two-dimensional materials, including recently observed superfluid-like propagation
del Aguila et al. 2023b
and viscous hydrodynamic flow
Mantsevich and Glazov 2024
.
