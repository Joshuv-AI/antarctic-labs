Design Document Summary – IM3 / Snow-Vegetation interactions
Summary prepared by Rich Fiorella
V1: 8/14/24, LA-UR-24-28788
V2: 3/11/25, LA-UR-24-28788 version 2
V3: 9/15/25

We targeted three categories of improvements to the snow-vegetation interactions in ELM
under NGEE Arctic IM3:

 1) Atmospheric downscaling from gridcell to topounit using the coupler bypass.
 Existing capability allows for downscaling of atmospheric forcing from the gridcell to
 the topounit level of the subgrid hierarchy but required that the atmospheric forcing
 was passed to ELM through the coupler. Updates have been made to handle the
 case when meteorological data is passed to ELM using the coupler bypass in
 lnd_import_export.F90
 2) Four new vegetation parameters have been added to pftvarcon. Four new
 parameters – taper, stocking, bendresist, and vegshape – have been added to
 pftvarcon.
 a. The ﬁrst two – taper and stocking – were local variables in the
 VegStructUpdate subroutine used to calculate vegetation height from wood
 density and the deadstem C pool for that PFT. Stocking refers to the
 individual plant density on the grid cell (e.g., stems/area) and taper is an
 estimate of above-ground deadstem C height-to-radius ratio in the plant.
 Default values of stocking were 1000 stems/ha everywhere, and taper values
 were 200 for trees and 10 for shrubs (not used for grasses). Moving these
 values to pftvarcon allows di^erent values to be speciﬁed on the ELM
 parameters ﬁle.
 b. Bendresist and vegshape are new parameters and are used only in the
 VegStructUpdate subroutine to calculate the fraction of leaf/stem area that is
 buried for a given snow depth. Bendresist refers to the ability of a shrub to
 resist bending under snow load (or seasonally even without snow load in the
 case of some Arctic shrubs) (Ménard et al., 2014; Ray et al., 2022), and varies
 between 0 and 1, where 1 is the default and corresponds to no bending.
 Vegshape is a parameter added suggested by Sturm et al. (2005) and Liston
 and Hiemstra (2011) that tries to account for uneven distribution of LAI/SAI
 within the plant along its height. Suggested values for this are 1 and 2 and are
 noted to correspond to parabolic and hemispheric vegetation shapes,
 respectively. Other values may make more sense if an analytic relationship
 between vegshape and taper can be made, but a challenge here is that
 vegshape refers to the vegetation crown while taper refers to the whole plant.
 3) Vegshape (b) and bendresist (g) are used to calculate the fraction of LAI/SAI buried
 by snow for a given snow depth for shrub PFTs only. The relevant equation in
 VegStructUpdate has been changed from:

*Version 2 update: Note that fb is deﬁned in the Fortran as the fraction buried, but in
equation 1 fb represents the fraction of vegetation exposed. We have modiﬁed these
equations to preserve the meaning of fb as fraction buried, and modiﬁed the elai and esai
(exposed LAI and SAI) expressions that follow as:

Original:
                              elai(p) = max(tlai(p)*fb, 0.0_r8)
                              esai(p) = max(tsai(p)*fb, 0.0_r8)
Revised:
                          elai(p) = max(tlai(p)*(1._r8 – fb), 0.0_r8)
                          esai(p) = max(tsai(p)*(1._r8 – fb), 0.0_r8)
