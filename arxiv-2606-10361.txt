Near-Exponential Convergence Rates for kNN Classification based on Boltzmann Margin
Luyuan Yang, Shayan Shafaei, Chao Lan

Abstract
Convergence-rate analysis for classifiers is often conducted under either Tsybakov margin or Massart margin. The former is a relatively weak condition that typically yields polynomial rates, while the latter is substantially stronger but can guarantee exponential rates. In this paper, we introduce a new condition, called Boltzmann margin , that bridges the gap between these two regimes. It is weaker than Massart margin, generally stronger than Tsybakov margin, and can imply many of their properties under suitable conditions. We apply Boltzmann margin to the analysis of kNN classifiers and establish the first near-exponential convergence rates for kNN classification. We also present extensions of the main results and provide numerical evidence supporting the main theoretical implications.

Introduction

In machine learning, a fundamental question
is how fast the classification error converges
to the Bayes error as training data
size increases. The convergence rate has
been widely studied under two margin
conditions:
Tsybakov margin

(
34
)
and

Massart margin

(
26
)
.
Tsybakov margin is a relatively weak condition
that assumes data decay polynomially
fast towards the Bayes decision boundary
and typically yields polynomial rates
(
6
;
12
)
.
Massart margin is substantially stronger,
as it assumes no data exist near the boundary,
but can guarantee exponential rates

(
5
;
35
)
.

There remains a substantial gap between
the two margins. To establish any super-polynomial
convergence rate, existing analyses typically
require Massart margin, which may be
unnecessarily strong for many problems.

Can there be a more balanced margin
condition that yields super-polynomial
convergence rates without assuming an
absence of data near the boundary?

This question motivates our study.

In this paper, we introduce
Boltzmann margin
,
a more balanced condition that assumes
data decay exponentially fast towards the
Bayes decision boundary. It is weaker than Massart
margin, generally stronger than Tsybakov margin,
and can imply many of their properties under
suitable conditions.
Figure
1
illustrates
the relations among the three margins.
In general, the decay behavior under
Boltzmann margin lies between those under Massart
and Tsybakov margins. Moreover, through suitable
choices of its parameters, Boltzmann margin
can resemble the decay behavior of
either margin in certain regions, making
it a flexible framework for convergence
rate analysis.

2.1
kNN Classification

Early studies on kNN convergence rates
are based on relatively mild assumptions,
notably Hölder smoothness of the posterior
function
\eta
.

(
23
, Corollary 1)

presents a
O(n^{-\frac{2\upsilon}{D}})
convergence
rate of 1NN error (to twice the Bayes
error) when both
\eta
and the
conditional label variance are Hölder.

(
14
)
presents an
asymptotic rate of
O(n^{-\frac{\upsilon}{2\upsilon+D}})

for kNN when
\eta
is generalized
Hölder (which, informally, requires
the average posterior within any open
ball to approach the posterior at the
center of the ball as the ball radius
shrinks, with the convergence rate
controlled by a Hölder-type parameter
\upsilon
)
and
k=n^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+D}}
.

Later studies establish faster rates for kNN
under Tsybakov margin and additional
density assumptions.

(
21
, Theorem 6)
presents
a
\tilde{O}(n^{-\frac{\upsilon(1+\beta)}{2\upsilon+D}})

rate when
\eta
is Hölder,
the data domain is bounded, the data density
is bounded away from zero (i.e., strong
density), and

k=\tilde{O}(n^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+D}})
.

(
32
, Theorem 1)

presents a
O(n^{-\frac{2(1+\beta)}{4+D}})
rate
when
\eta
is Hölder, the data density
has minimal mass (which, informally, requires
any open ball centered at any point
to have a mass that scales with
its radius),
and
k=O(n^{\frac{4}{4+D}})
.

(
13
, Theorem 3.1)
presents
a
O(n^{-\frac{1+\beta}{2+D}})

rate when
\eta
is Lipschitz continuous,
the data density has minimal mass,
and
k=O(n^{\frac{2}{2+D}})
.

Recent studies remain rooted in the Tsybakov
margin and focus on relaxing conditions.

(
6
, Theorem 4)
presents
a
O(n^{-\frac{\upsilon(1+\beta)}{2\upsilon+D}})

rate when
\eta
is generalized
Hölder and
k=O(n^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+D}})
.

(
12
, Theorem 1)
presents
a
O(n^{-\frac{1+\beta}{2+D}})
rate
when
\eta
is modified Lipschitz (which,
informally, requires the posterior gap
between two points to decrease as the
radius of an open ball that contains them
decreases) and
k=O(n^{\frac{2}{2+D}})
.

(
15
, Theorem 6)
presents
a
O(n^{-\frac{\upsilon(1+\beta)}{2\upsilon+D}})
rate for
multi-class kNN in a metric space when

\eta
is (weighted) Hölder, the
distribution function is continuous,
and
k=O(n^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+D}})
.
Recently,
(
37
, Theorem 4)

shows that the convergence rate can
vary across different phases
and reach
O(Dn^{-3/4})
when
n\leq D^{2}
,
provided that
\eta
is Hölder,

D>10
, and
k=O(n^{3/4})
.
An exception appears in the
appendix of
(
6
)
,
where the established polynomial rate
is extended to an asymptotic exponential
rate under Massart margin.

Convergence rates have also been
studied for several kNN variants.
Under Tsybakov margin,

(
29
, Theorem 4.3)
presents
an asymptotic rate of
\tilde{O}(n^{-\frac{\upsilon(\beta+1)}{2\upsilon+1}})

for robustified kNN when
\eta
is
modified Lipschitz and
k=\tilde{O}(n^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+1}})
;

(
38
, Theorem 5)

presents a
O(n^{-\frac{1+\beta}{1+d}})

rate for compressed kNN when
\eta

is modified Lipschitz, the compressed
data dimension is sufficiently large,
and
k=O(n^{\frac{2}{2+d}})
. Without
a margin assumption,

(
30
, Theorem 4.8)

presents an asymptotic rate of

O(n^{-\frac{1}{2+d}})

for kNN trained in a
bounded transformed domain
when
\eta
is probabilistic
Lipschitz and
k=O(n^{\frac{2}{2+D}})
.

2.2
Ensemble kNN Classification

Ensemble learning is a popular strategy
for improving classification performance
by combining weak classifiers, typically learned
from bootstrap samples, into a strong classifier

(
11
;
39
)
.
Let
\tilde{n}
be the bootstrap sample size
and
m
be the number of weak classifiers.

Early studies on ensemble kNN assume
m=\infty
.

(
1
, Corollary 10)
shows
a bagged 1NN regression estimator has a

O(n^{-\frac{2}{2+D}})
error rate when

\eta
is Lipschitz, the label variance is
bounded,
D\geq 3
,
\tilde{n}\propto n^{\frac{D}{2+D}}
, and
k=O(n^{\frac{2}{2+D}})
.
(The same rate applies to the induced bagged
1NN classifier.)

(
31
, Corollary 4)
shows
that under an implied Tsybakov margin, a
bagged 1NN classifier has a
O(n^{-\frac{4}{4+D}})

rate when
\eta
is continuous, the data density
has minimal mass, and
n^{r}\leq\tilde{n}\leq n^{1-r}
for any
r\in(0,1/2)
.

Recent studies remain rooted in Tsybakov margin
but do not require
m=\infty
.

(
36
)
considers a special
bagged 1NN classifier, where each bootstrap
sample is relabeled using the training set
and the kNN classification
rule with
k=\tilde{O}(n^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+d}})
.
It shows that the classifier has an asymptotic
rate of
\tilde{O}(n^{-\frac{\upsilon}{2\upsilon+d}})+\tilde{O}(\tilde{n}^{-\frac{\upsilon}{d}})

when
\eta
is Hölder and the data density
has minimal mass, and that the first term
dominates when

\tilde{n}/n=\Omega(n^{-\frac{\upsilon}{2\upsilon+d}})
.

(
27
)
presents a

O(n^{-\frac{\upsilon(1+\beta)}{2\upsilon+D}})
rate
for bagged kNN when
\eta
is modified
Hölder and
k=O(\tilde{n}^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+D}}m^{-\frac{D}{2\upsilon+D}})\to\infty
as

n\to\infty
.
In addition to standard ensemble kNN,

(
17
, Theorem 3)

designs an under-bagging kNN classifier
for imbalanced classes; it shows that
the classifier has an asymptotic rate of

\tilde{O}(n^{-\frac{\upsilon(\beta+1)}{2\upsilon+D}})

when
\eta
is Hölder,

k=\tilde{O}(m\tilde{n}^{-\frac{D}{2\upsilon+D}})
,
and
m
scales at a rate of

\tilde{O}(n^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+D}})
.

2.3
Consistency for ekNN Classifier

Bayes consistency is a desirable property
of classifiers. Informally, a classifier is

weakly consistent
if its expected
error converges to the Bayes error,
and
strongly consistent
if
its error converges to the Bayes error
with probability one (over the random choice
of training data). Strong consistency
implies weak consistency

(
10
)
.

Weak consistency is often easier to establish.
In fact, most studies reviewed in Section
2.2
already imply weak consistency, since their convergence rates
imply that the expected classification error
converges to the Bayes error.
A few other studies also establish
weak consistency for ekNN classifiers.

(
16
)
studies
a bag of infinitely many 1NN classifiers,
each trained on
\tilde{n}_{+}
positive
points sampled from
n_{+}
candidates
and
\tilde{n}_{-}
negative points sampled
from
n_{-}
candidates, and shows that the
classifier is weakly
consistent if
\tilde{n}_{+},\tilde{n}_{-}

diverge and
\frac{\tilde{n}_{+}}{n_{+}},\frac{\tilde{n}_{-}}{n_{-}},\frac{\tilde{n}_{+}}{n_{+}}-\frac{\tilde{n}_{-}}{n_{-}}
all converge
to zero.
(
3
)

shows that a bag of
m
1NN classifiers
is weakly consistent if
m,\tilde{n}

diverge and
\frac{\tilde{n}}{n}\to 0
.

(
2
)
further shows that
a probabilistic version of this
classifier, where
\tilde{n}
depends
on a sampling probability
q_{n}
for
each candidate point, is weakly consistent
if
\mathbb{E}\tilde{n}=nq_{n}
diverges
and
q_{n}\to 0
.

Strong consistency often requires stronger
conditions. kNN is shown to be strongly
consistent if
k\to\infty
and
k/n\to 0

(
10
, Chapter 11)
.
1NN is not strongly consistent

(
8
)
, but its
variants based on compressed
training data are strongly consistent

(
22
;
18
)
.
To the best of our knowledge, strong consistency
for ekNN has not been established.
This paper presents the first such
guarantee.

3
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce
notation and concepts.
When clarity is needed, a probability
(or expectation) is denoted by
\Pr_{Z}

(or
\mathbb{E}_{Z}
) when taken with respect
to the randomness of
Z
.
As in prior studies, we focus on
binary classification.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a data space equipped with
norm
||\cdot||
and
\mathcal{Y}=\{0,1\}
be a
label set. Let
B(x,r)=\{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X};||x-x^{\prime}||\leq r\}
denote a closed ball centered
at
x
with radius
r
. For
\mathcal{X}
,
let
D
be its input dimension
and
d
be its
intrinsic dimension

introduced in
(
36
)
,
i.e., an integer for which there exists
a constant
C_{d}>0
such that
\Pr_{z}\{z\in B(x,r)\}\geq\min(C_{d}r^{d},1)

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
r>0
.
While most known rates depend on
D
,
our rates depend on
d
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}
be a random data point
and
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
be its label. The posterior
function
\eta
is defined by

\eta(x)=\mathbb{E}_{Y}[Y\mid X=x].

The following assumption is made
throughout the analysis.

Assumption

(Hölder Smooth)
.

The posterior function
\eta

is
(\lambda,\upsilon)
-Hölder smooth
for constants
\lambda,\upsilon>0
,
i.e., for any
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
,
we have
|\eta(x)-\eta(x^{\prime})|\leq\lambda\cdot||x-x^{\prime}||^{\upsilon}
.

Let
\hat{y}
be a classifier
and
er(\hat{y})={\Pr}_{X,Y}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq Y\}
be
its error. Let
\hat{y}_{*}
be a

Bayes classifier
, i.e.,
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
:

\hat{y}_{*}(x)=\mathbb{I}\{\eta(x)\geq 1/2\},

where
\mathbb{I}
is the indicator function.

For a classifier
\hat{y}
trained on
a random sample
Z\subseteq\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
,
its
expected excess error
is defined as

||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}:=\mathbb{E}_{Z}[er(\hat{y})]-er(\hat{y}_{*}).

(1)

Unless otherwise specified, all convergence
rates discussed in this paper are w.r.t.
the expected excess error in (
1
).

3.1
Boltzmann Margin

We first revisit two popular margin conditions.
Let
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
,
t_{*}
be positive constants.
A distribution on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
is said to
have a
t_{*}
Massart margin

(
26
)
if

{\Pr}_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t_{*}\}=0,

and a
(\alpha,\beta)

Tsybakov margin

(
34
)
if

\forall t>0,\ \ {\Pr}_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\}\leq\alpha t^{\beta}.

It is clear that Massart margin is
much stronger than Tsybakov margin.
In the following, we introduce a new margin
condition that interpolates between them.
It is named after the Boltzmann factor
in physics
(
24
)

due to the similarity of their forms.

Definition

(Boltzmann Margin)
.

A distribution on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
is said
to have a
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann
margin if

\forall t>0,\ {\Pr}_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\}\leq\alpha\exp(-\gamma t^{-\beta}).

(2)

Figure
1
illustrates
the relations among the margins.
The three Boltzmann curves are generated
with
\beta=0.1,0.4,0.8
, respectively,
while fixing
\alpha,\gamma
. As
\beta
increases,
the Boltzmann curve approaches the
Massart curve. As
\beta
decreases,
it approaches the Tsybakov curve and
even becomes weaker in certain regions.
These relations illustrate the flexibility
of the Boltzmann margin in convergence analysis.

We now formalize the relations among
the margins. The first lemma shows
that Massart margin implies Boltzmann
margin, and the reverse holds under
suitable parameter choices.

Lemma 3.1
.

A
t_{*}
Massart margin implies a

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann
margin for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0

satisfying
\alpha\exp(-\frac{\gamma}{t_{*}^{\beta}})=1
.
Reversely, for any
t_{*}>0
,
a
(1,\beta,t_{*}^{\beta})
Boltzmann margin
approaches the
t_{*}
Massart margin
condition as
\beta\to\infty
, provided
that
|1/2-\eta(X)|\neq t_{*}

almost everywhere.

The next lemma shows Tsybakov margin
implies Boltzmann margin and the reverse
holds far from the decision boundary.

Lemma 3.2
.

A
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin
implies a
(\frac{\gamma}{\alpha},\beta)
Tsybakov margin.
Reversely, a
(\alpha,\beta)
Tsybakov margin
satisfies the
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})

Boltzmann margin property (
2
)
for
t\in[(\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\ln(\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha)})^{1/\beta^{\prime}},0.5]
, if
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}>0
and
\gamma^{\prime}\leq\ln(\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha)
.

Figure
2

illustrates the reversed relation.
The decay is much slower under Boltzmann
margin except near the decision boundary.
Although this may seem less significant
from the margin perspective, where the
focus is often on near-boundary properties,
it is highly relevant to machine learning.
The reason is that far-boundary properties
often govern non-asymptotic model behavior,
which is particularly important in practice
because training data are finite.

Figure 2
:
Example Data Densities

To better understand the above implication,
we first establish a monotonicity property
of the Boltzmann margin.

Lemma 3.3
.

A
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin implies
a
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
Boltzmann margin whenever
(i)
\alpha^{\prime}\geq\alpha
, (ii)
\beta^{\prime}\geq\beta
or (iii)
\gamma^{\prime}\leq\gamma
, with the remaining parameters fixed.

Now consider a model trained on
n
data
points. Suppose data distribution has a

(\alpha,\beta)
Tsybakov margin, and set

t=1/n
.
Then Lemma
3.2

implies the distribution also satisfies
the
(\alpha^{\prime},1,\gamma^{\prime})
Boltzmann margin
property whenever

2\leq n\leq\gamma^{\prime-1}\ln(\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha).

By Lemma
3.3
, one may pick

\gamma^{\prime}\leq 0.001
and
\alpha^{\prime}\geq\alpha^{2}
,
yielding the range
n\in[2,2000]
.
This suggests the model may exhibit
Boltzmann-margin behavior for moderate
n
.

Remark 3.4
.

It is worth noting that in Lemma

3.2
, for
a
(\alpha,\beta)
Tsybakov margin to imply
a Boltzmann margin, the constraint on
t

does not depend on
\beta
. This is because
we set
t\leq 0.5
so that

t^{\beta}\leq 1
for any
\beta
.
This setting is often sufficient;
for example, when
t=1/n
,
it covers all
n\geq 2
.

4
Results for kNN Classifier

Let
S
be an i.i.d. sample of
n
points
from
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
. The kNN regression
estimator for
\eta(x)
based on
S
is

\hat{\eta}(x;S)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in S}\mathbb{I}\{x^{\prime}\in N_{k}(x;S)\wedge y^{\prime}=1\},

where
N_{k}(x;S)
is the set of
k

nearest neighbors of
x
in
S
.
The induced kNN classifier is
defined by

\hat{y}(x)=\mathbb{I}\{\hat{\eta}(x;S)\geq 1/2\}.

As in prior studies, we assume ties occur
with probability zero e.g., ties in the
distance to the
k
th nearest neighbor
or in the decision rule
\eta(x)=1/2
.

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose data distribution has a

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin.
Let
C_{\upsilon}=2+d/\upsilon
.
If
k=O(n^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
,
then for
n\geq(C^{\prime}\ln n)^{1+C_{\upsilon}/\beta}
:

||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq C_{\alpha}\exp\left(-C_{\gamma}n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right),

where
C_{\alpha}=2+\alpha
,
C_{\gamma}=\min(\gamma,1/C^{\prime})

and
C^{\prime}>0
is a constant depending on
\lambda,\upsilon

and the structure of
\mathcal{X}
.

Theorem
4.1
establishes
the first near-exponential convergence rate
for kNN classification, and the exponent
depends on
\beta,d
and
\upsilon
.
In the following, we discuss its implications.

Larger
\beta
makes the rate closer to
exponential. This makes sense because,
by Lemma
3.1
,
increasing
\beta
causes Boltzmann
margin to approach Massart margin,
and the latter is known to warrant
exponential rates
(
5
;
35
)
.
(However, this does not trivially imply that kNN
has an exponential rate under Massart margin.
We establish such a result later.)

Smaller
d/\upsilon
makes the rate closer
to exponential. This is consistent with prior
results, which show smaller
D/\upsilon
implies
faster rates (Table
1
).
A minor difference is that our rate depends
on the intrinsic dimension
d
instead of
D
.

The theorem also yields several
other observations.
First, larger
\upsilon\beta/d
permits a slower
growth rate for
k
but also leads to
slower convergence – a similar tradeoff
appears in prior kNN convergence guarantees.
Second, the assumed scaling rate for

k
is slightly faster than in prior
results, as we assume
k=O(n^{\frac{2+\beta}{2+\beta+d/\upsilon}})
while they
assume
k=O(n^{\frac{2}{2+d/\upsilon}})
or

O(n^{\frac{2}{2+D/\upsilon}})
.
Third, our rate holds for large
n
,
which is a common constraint in
existing rates that are either asymptotic

(
14
;
29
;
30
;
36
)
or multi-phase
37
.
The next remark shows that, under
suitable conditions, the large-
n

condition in Theorem
4.1

can be removed without affecting the
convergence rate.

Remark 4.2
.

Theorem
4.1
implies
that, for
n\geq 1
:

(i) If
k=O(n^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
,
then there exists a constant
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}>0
such
that
||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\exp\left(-C_{\gamma}n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right)
.

(ii) Take
\beta\to\infty
. Then

||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq C_{\alpha}^{\prime}\exp\left(-C_{\gamma}n\right)

for
k=O(n)
,
where
C_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\tilde{C}_{\alpha}<\infty
.

Claim (ii) suggests that Boltzmann margin may
warrant an exponential rate under sufficiently
fast data decay.
This is not a trivial consequence of (i),
since one must verify that
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}

remains bounded as
\beta\to\infty
.
Combined with Lemma
3.1
,
this implies an exponential and non-asymptotic
convergence rate for kNN classification
under Massart margin, which is formalized
in the next subsection.
Hereafter, unless otherwise
specified, the constants
C_{\gamma}
,
C^{\prime}
,
C_{\upsilon}

are as defined in Theorem
4.1
,
while
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}
,
C_{\alpha}^{\prime}
are as
defined in Remark
4.2
.

4.1
Extended Results

In this section, we present three
corollaries of Theorem
4.1
.
The first one provides a
probabilistic convergence rate.

Corollary 4.3

(Probabilistic)
.

Suppose data distribution has a

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin.
If
k=O(n^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
,
then for any
\delta>0
and
n\geq 1
:

er(\hat{y})-er(\hat{y}_{*})\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\delta^{-1}\exp\left(-C_{\gamma}n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right),

with probability at least
1-\delta

over the random choice of
S
.

This probabilistic rate is near-exponential,
in contrast to the polynomial probabilistic rates
in
(
29
;
30
)
.
Moreover, the dependence on
\delta
is
multiplicative in our bound, whereas it is
additive in the prior bounds, e.g.,

\tilde{O}(n^{-\frac{\upsilon(1+\beta)}{2\upsilon+1}}+\delta)
. With the common choice

\delta=O(1/n)
, our bound remains
near-exponential.

Theorem
4.1
also
yields convergence rates under other margins.
The first is under Tsybakov margin.

Corollary 4.4

(Tsybakov)
.

Suppose data distribution has
a
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
Tsybakov margin
and let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
be
constants satisfying

\gamma\leq\ln(\alpha/\alpha^{\prime})
.
If
k=O(n^{\frac{2+\beta}{C_{\upsilon}+\beta}})
,
then

||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\exp(-C_{\gamma}n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}),

for
2\leq n\leq\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}\ln(\alpha/\alpha^{\prime})\right)^{1+C_{\upsilon}/\beta}
.

This can be viewed as a multi-phase
convergence rate,
where the rate is near-exponential
within the above range and
becomes polynomial beyond it.
Multi-phase rates have recently been studied
in
(
37
)
. The authors show
that the rate can be improved to
O(n^{-3/4})

when
n\leq D^{2}
, but all rates remain
polynomial. By contrast, we show
the rate can be near-exponential
over a finite range of
n
, and
this range can be
O(D^{2})
when

\alpha
is large or
\gamma
is small.

It is worth noting that
\beta^{\prime}

does not appear in the error bound
or the range of
n
, for the reason
explained in Remark
3.4
.
Moreover, under suitable conditions,
the bound may be expressed in terms
of the Tsybakov-margin parameters and
the admissible range only. For example,
when
\alpha
is large,
\gamma
is small,
and
\beta\to\infty
, it follows
that the range becomes nearly

n\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\ln(\alpha/\alpha^{\prime}):=B

and the error bound becomes
\frac{n}{B}\exp(-\ln(\alpha/\alpha^{\prime})+\ln(\alpha+2))
;
optimizing it over
\alpha
yields a bound
independent of the Boltzmann
margin parameters.

The last corollary specializes the
rate under Massart margin.

Corollary 4.5

(Massart)
.

Let
k=O(n)
. If data distribution
has a
t_{*}
Massart margin, then

||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq C\exp(-C_{*}n),

(3)

for all
n\geq C_{*}^{-1}\ln n
,
where
C
and
C_{*}\propto t_{*}^{C_{\upsilon}}

are positive constants.
Moreover, for every
t_{*}>0
,
there exists a data distribution
satisfying the
t_{*}
Massart margin
for which (
3
)
holds for all
n\geq 1
(possibly
with different constants).

The corollary establishes an exponential
convergence rate for kNN classification
under Massart margin.
We can compare it with the rate in

(
6
, Appendix C.4)
.
First, the two rates are developed using
fundamentally different techniques (ours
via Boltzmann margin). Second, both rates
are
O(\exp(-Cn))
, where
C\propto t_{*}^{p}

for some constant
p
.
The prior rate has
p=2+\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}
,
where
\alpha^{\prime}=\upsilon/D
is the
generalized Hölder parameter,
while we have
p=C_{\upsilon}=2+\frac{d}{\upsilon}=2+\frac{d}{D}\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}
.
Since
d/D<1
, our
p
is smaller,
implying a larger
t_{*}^{p}
for

t_{*}\in(0,1)
. Consequently, the
exponential constant in our bound
increases slightly faster with
t_{*}
.
Finally, both rates require
k=O(n)
.

The corollary contains two distinct
claims: one holds under arbitrary Massart
margins but only yields an asymptotic rate,
while the other only holds under certain
Massart margins but yields a non-asymptotic
rate. Moreover, condition
k=O(n)

is stronger than

O(n^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})

used in near-exponential rates.

5
Results for ekNN Classifier

In this section, we present new convergence
rates for ensemble kNN (ekNN) classification
under Boltzmann margin.

For ensemble classifiers, the excess error
definition (
1
) requires a
slight adjustment to account for the additional
randomness introduced by bootstrapping.
There are two common settings.
One assumes a training set is sampled
from the population and bootstrap samples
are drawn from it. In this case, one can
make claims that account for the additional
bootstrapping randomness, e.g.,

(
36
)
establishes error rates
with high probability over the random choices
of both the training set and bootstrap samples.
The other assumes bootstrap
samples are drawn directly from the
population and treats their collection
as the training set, e.g.,
(
3
)
;
in this case, (
1
) incorporates
all randomness, so no special treatment is
needed. For convenience, we adopt the second
setting, but our results also apply
to the first setting
(Remark
5.2
).

Let
\tilde{S}:=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}\}

be a collection of
m
samples, where
each
S_{j}
contains
\tilde{n}
points i.i.d.
sampled from
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
,
and all samples are drawn i.i.d.
Hence
n=m\tilde{n}
.
Consider the following bagged
kNN regression estimator

(
33
;
19
;
17
)
based on
\tilde{S}
:

\hat{\eta}_{e}(x;\tilde{S})=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\hat{\eta}(x;S_{j}).

The induced ekNN classifier is

\hat{y}_{e}(x)=\mathbb{I}\{\hat{\eta}_{e}(x;\tilde{S})\geq 1/2\}.

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose data distribution has a

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin.
If
k=O(\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
, then

||\hat{y}_{e}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq C_{\alpha}\exp(-C_{\gamma}\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}),

(4)

for
\tilde{n}\geq\left(2\max\{\ln\tilde{n},2\ln m\}/C^{\prime}\right)^{1+C_{\upsilon}/\beta}
.

Moreover, if
m=O(\sqrt{\tilde{n}})
,
then (
4
) holds for
all
\tilde{n}\geq 1
, with
C_{\alpha}

replaced by a possibly different
constant
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}>0
.

We have some interesting observations from
the theorem. First, recall that the known
convergence rates for ekNN classifiers
are polynomial and typically assume

\tilde{n}=\Theta(n^{g})

for some constant
g
, e.g.,
\frac{D}{2+D}
or
\frac{\upsilon+d}{2\upsilon+d}

(Section
2.2
).
Under the same choice of
\tilde{n}
,
our theorem yields

||\hat{y}_{e}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}=O(e^{-C_{\gamma}n^{\frac{g\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}}).

This rate is slightly weaker than the one we
established for kNN classification, but can be
close when
g
is near 1, e.g., under
the choice
g=\frac{D}{2+D}
with large
D
.
As a result, it is generally faster than
the known polynomial rates.

Second, unlike
(
1
;
31
)
,
our rate does not require
m=\infty
.
In fact, if
m=\infty
, our rate
only holds for
\tilde{n}=\infty
,
which limits its practical relevance.
That said, Theorem
5.1

does allow
m
to diverge no
faster than
\tilde{O}(e^{\tilde{n}})
.
A related condition appears in

(
27
)
, which requires

m
to diverge no faster than

O(\tilde{n}^{2\mathcal{V}/D})
to ensure

k=O(\tilde{n}^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+D}}m^{-\frac{D}{2\upsilon+D}})\to\infty
.
Our condition is weaker in two respects:
(i) it allows but does not require the
divergence of
m
; (ii)
e^{\tilde{n}}

grows exponentially faster than

\tilde{n}^{2\mathcal{V}/D}
, allowing
m
to
diverge substantially faster.

Finally, Theorem
5.1

also applies to the alternative bootstrap
setting discussed earlier in this section.
In the following remark, conditions (ii)
and (iii) are standard in ekNN analysis,
e.g.,
(
36
;
27
)
.

Remark 5.2
.

Theorem
5.1

also applies when
(i) a training set
S
is sampled
i.i.d. from the population, (ii)
subsets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}

are drawn i.i.d. from
S
,
and (iii) in each subset, points
are sampled without replacement.

5.1
Other Results

Theorem
5.1
also
implies a probabilistic convergence rate.

Corollary 5.3

(Probabilistic)
.

Suppose data distribution has
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)

Boltzmann margin. If

k=O(\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})

and
m=O(\sqrt{\tilde{n}})
, then for
any
\delta>0
and
\tilde{n}\geq 1
,

er(\hat{y}_{e})-er(\hat{y}_{*})\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\delta^{-1}\exp(-C_{\gamma}\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}),

with probability at least
1-\delta

over the random choice of
\tilde{S}
,
where
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}
is as defined
in Theorem
5.1
.

We can compare the above with the
asymptotic rate for the bagged 1NN classifier
in
(
36
)
, which is

\tilde{O}\left(n^{-1}\ln(1/\delta)\right)^{\frac{1}{2+d/\upsilon}}
if

\frac{\tilde{n}}{n}=\Omega(n^{-\frac{\upsilon}{2\upsilon+d}})
.
Under the same choice of
\tilde{n}/n
,
our bound becomes

O(e^{-C_{\gamma}n^{\frac{1+d}{C_{\upsilon}}\cdot\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}})
.
This rate is weaker than the asymptotic
rate we established for kNN,
but can be close when
d/\upsilon
is small or

\beta
is large – in either case, our bound
remains near-exponential and can be
faster than the prior polynomial rate.

Our next result is a strong consistency
guarantee for ekNN. Recall that a classifier

\hat{y}
trained on a random sample
Z
is
strongly Bayes consistent
if
\Pr_{Z}\{\lim_{|Z|\to\infty}er(\hat{y})=er(\hat{y}_{*})\}=1

(
10
)
.

Theorem 5.4

(Strong Consistency)
.

Suppose data distribution has a

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin.
Then
\hat{y}_{e}
is strongly
Bayes consistent
if
k=O(\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})

and
m=\tilde{O}(e^{\tilde{n}})
.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the
first result that formally establishes strong
consistency for ekNN classification.
We can compare its condition on
k

with those for weak consistency, such as

O(n^{\frac{4}{4+D}})
or

O(n^{\frac{\upsilon(1+\beta)}{2\upsilon+D}})

(Section
2.2
).
Our
O(\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+2+d/\upsilon}})

has similar implications:
k
needs to
scale with
\tilde{n}
, and a larger
data dimension allows
it to scale more slowly.
A major difference is in the impact of
\beta
:
in prior conditions, an arbitrarily large
\beta

can require
k
to scale arbitrarily fast
alongside
n
, e.g.,
k=O(n^{5})
; in our condition,

k=O(\tilde{n})
even when
\beta\to\infty
.

7
Conclusion and Discussion

This paper introduces Boltzmann margin,
a new margin condition
that enables near-exponential
convergence rates for classification.
We demonstrate its application
to kNN and ekNN classifiers and establish
their first near-exponential rates,
as well as the first strong
consistency guarantee for ekNN.
Extending Boltzmann margin to broader
hypothesis classes remains an
important direction for future research.

Besides its theoretical contributions,
the present study also has practical
implications.
For example, Lemma
3.2

suggests that, for distributions satisfying
a Tsybakov margin, one may expect faster
error decay in the small-data regime
than that implied by the margin alone.
Furthermore, improving error rates
may not require finding a feature space
(e.g., through data embedding) in which
a strong Massart margin holds. Such a
representation can be difficult to obtain
and may involve substantial tradeoffs. Instead,
one may identify a feature space satisfying
Boltzmann margin while still achieving
near-exponential error convergence.

Lemma
3.1
.

A
t_{*}
Massart margin implies a

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann
margin for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0

satisfying
\alpha\exp(-\frac{\gamma}{t_{*}^{\beta}})=1
.
Conversely, for any
t_{*}>0
,
a
(1,\beta,t_{*}^{\beta})
Boltzmann margin
approaches a
t_{*}
Massart margin
condition as
\beta\to\infty
, provided
that
|1/2-\eta(X)|\neq t_{*}

almost everywhere.

Proof.

Suppose data distribution has a
t_{*}
Massart margin
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
satisfies
\alpha e^{-\gamma t_{*}^{-\beta}}=1
.
For any
t\geq t_{*}
,

\Pr\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\}\leq t\}\leq 1=\alpha e^{-\gamma t_{*}^{-\beta}}\leq\alpha e^{-\gamma t^{-\beta}}.

(6)

When
t<t_{*}
, the above inequality
also holds because the left side is
zero due to Massart margin. This proves
the first claim.

Reversely, for any
t_{*}>0
,
suppose the distribution has a

(\alpha,\beta,t_{*}^{\beta})

Boltzmann margin. Then, for any
t>0
:

\Pr\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\}\leq\alpha\exp(-t_{*}^{\beta}t^{-\beta})=\alpha\exp\left(-(t_{*}/t)^{\beta}\right):=\Delta.

(7)

Consider three cases for
t
.

– If
t<t_{*}
, then
t_{*}/t>1
so
that
\Delta\xrightarrow{\beta\to\infty}0
.

– If
t>t_{*}
, then
t_{*}/t<1
so
that
\Delta\xrightarrow{\beta\to\infty}\alpha
.

– Case
t=t_{*}
happens with probability
zero by assumption.

Putting all together, we have

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\Pr\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\}\leq\left\{\begin{matrix}0&\text{if }t\leq t_{*}\\[5.0pt]
\alpha&\text{if }t>t_{*}\end{matrix}\right.

(8)

This proves the Boltzmann margin
converges to a
t_{*}
Massart margin.
∎

Lemma
3.2
.

A
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin
implies a
(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma},\beta)
Tsybakov margin.
Reversely, a
(\alpha,\beta)
Tsybakov margin
satisfies the
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})

Boltzmann margin property (
2
)
for
t\in[(\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\ln(\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha)})^{1/\beta^{\prime}},0.5]
, if
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}>0
and
\gamma^{\prime}\leq\ln(\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha)
.

Proof.

Suppose a distribution has
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)

Boltzmann margin. Then for any
t>0
,

\Pr\{|1/2-\eta(x)|\}\leq t\}\leq\alpha e^{-\gamma t^{-\beta}}\leq\alpha\frac{1}{\gamma t^{-\beta}}=\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}t^{\beta},

(9)

which implies
(\alpha/\gamma,\beta)
Tsybakov margin.
This proves the first claim.

Reversely, suppose a distribution has

(\alpha,\beta)
Tsybakov margin.
Let
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}>0
be
any constants satisfying

\gamma^{\prime}\leq\ln(\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha)
.
Then, one can verify that

(\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\ln(\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha)})^{1/\beta^{\prime}}\leq t\leq 0.5

implies

\alpha t^{\beta}\leq\alpha\leq\alpha^{\prime}\exp(-\gamma^{\prime}t^{-\beta^{\prime}}),

(10)

where the first inequality follows

t^{\beta}\leq 1
and the second
follows the lower bound of
t
.
Hence the
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})

Boltzmann margin. Note that
\beta

is not included in the condition
on
t
because we set
t\leq 0.5
,
in which case
t^{\beta}\leq 1

for any
\beta>0
.
∎

Apparently, the right side increases

Lemma A.1

of
S
, there is

\mathbb{E}_{S}[er(\hat{y})]-er(\hat{y}_{*})\leq{\Pr}_{X,S}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\};

(12)

and for any
t>0
,

{\Pr}_{S}\{er(\hat{y})-er(\hat{y}_{*})>t\}\leq\frac{1}{t}{\Pr}_{X,S}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}.

(13)

Proof.

Let
Y
be the label of
X
.
Since
\hat{y}(X)\neq Y

implies
\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)

or
\hat{y}_{*}(X)\neq Y
, there is

{\Pr}_{X,Y}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq Y\}\leq{\Pr}_{X}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}+{\Pr}_{X,Y}\{\hat{y}_{*}(X)\neq Y\},

(14)

which implies

{\Pr}_{X}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\geq{\Pr}_{X,Y}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq Y\}-{\Pr}_{X,Y}\{\hat{y}_{*}(X)\neq Y\}=er(\hat{y})-er(\hat{y}_{*}).

(15)

This proves the first claim. The second
claim follows by taking expectation w.r.t.

S
on both sides of (
15
),
and the third claim follows by further
employing the Markov inequality (based on
the fact that
er(\hat{y})-er(\hat{y}_{*})\geq 0
).
∎

The above lemma shows

{\Pr}_{X}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}

is key for bounding excess error.
The following lemma provides an
observation that is useful for bounding

{\Pr}_{X}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\hat{y}
be a classifier
defined as
\hat{y}(x)=\mathbb{I}\{\hat{y}(x;S)\geq 1/2\},\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
.
Then, almost surely, any
x\in X

satisfying
|\hat{\eta}(x;S)-\eta(x)|\leq|1/2-\eta(x)|
also satisfies

\hat{y}(x)=\hat{y}_{*}(x)
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\hat{y}(x)=\hat{y}_{*}(x)

if
\hat{\eta}(x;S)
and
\eta(x)
fall
on the same side of 1/2, which is guaranteed
if
|\hat{\eta}(x;S)-\eta(x)|\leq|1/2-\eta(x)|
. This proves the lemma.
∎

A.3
Other Tools

We borrow the following kNN regression bound
from prior work. The original
bound is uniform and very strong,
but we only need its implied pointwise
bound. The following is a paraphrase of

[
36
, Proposition 1]
.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the VC dimension of the class
of balls on
\mathcal{X}
.
Let
\delta<1
and

k=O((\ln\frac{2|Z|}{\delta})^{\frac{d}{2\upsilon+d}}(C_{d}|Z|)^{\frac{2\upsilon}{2\upsilon+d}})
. With probability at least
1-2\delta
over the random sampling of
Z
,
we have simultaneously for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
:

|\hat{\eta}(x;Z)-\eta(x)|\leq C\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}\ln(2|Z|/\delta)}{C_{d}|Z|}\right)^{\frac{\upsilon}{2\upsilon+d}},

(16)

where
C
is a function of
\upsilon,\lambda
,
and
C_{d}
is a constant depending on intrinsic
data dimension
d
.

Corollary A.4
.

Let
C_{\upsilon}=2+\frac{d}{\upsilon}

and
C^{\prime}=\frac{C_{d}}{\mathcal{V}C^{C_{\upsilon}}}
.
Proposition
A.3
implies
for any
t>0
, if
k=O(t^{\frac{d}{\upsilon}}|Z|C_{d}^{\frac{2\upsilon+2d}{2\upsilon+d}})
then

{\Pr}_{X,Z}\{|\hat{\eta}(X;Z)-\eta(X)|>t\}\leq 2|Z|\exp\left(-C^{\prime}|Z|t^{C_{\upsilon}}\right).

(17)

Proof.

Proposition
A.3
implies
for any
x\in X
, (
16
)
holds with high probability.
Setting the right side of (
16
)
to
t
implies
{\Pr}_{Z}\{|\hat{\eta}(x;Z)-\eta(x)|>t\}\leq 2|Z|\exp\left(-\frac{C_{d}}{\mathcal{V}}|Z|\left(\frac{t}{C}\right)^{C_{\upsilon}}\right)
.
Taking expectation w.r.t.
x
on both sides
gives the corollary.
∎

We will also apply the classic Borel-Cantelli
Lemma to prove storng consistency. It is quoted
as follows.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
E_{1},E_{2},\ldots
be a sequence of
events. If
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Pr\{E_{i}\}<\infty
,
then
\Pr\{{\lim\sup}_{i\to\infty}E_{i}\}=0
.

Appendix B
Results for kNN Classification

Theorem
4.1
.

Suppose data distribution has a

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin.
Let
C_{\upsilon}=2+d/\upsilon
.
If
k=O(n^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
,
then for
n\geq(C^{\prime}\ln n)^{1+C_{\upsilon}/\beta}
:

||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq C_{\alpha}\exp\left(-C_{\gamma}n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right),

where
C_{\alpha}=2+\alpha
,
C_{\gamma}=\min(\gamma,1/C^{\prime})

and
C^{\prime}>0
is a constant depending on

\lambda,\upsilon
and the structure of
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.1
and Lemma

A.2
, for any
t>0
,

\begin{split}||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}&\leq{\Pr}_{X,S}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\\[5.0pt]
&\leq{\Pr}_{X,S}\{|\hat{\eta}(X;S)-\eta(X)|>|1/2-\eta(X)|\}\\[5.0pt]
&\leq{\Pr}_{X,S}\{|\hat{\eta}(X;S)-\eta(X)|>t\}+{\Pr}_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\},\end{split}

(18)

where the last inequality is by
case-studying
|1/2-\eta(X)|

based on
t
.

On the right side of (
18
),
the second term can be bounded based
on Boltzmann margin i.e,

{\Pr}_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\}\leq\alpha\exp(-\gamma t^{-\beta}).

(19)

The first term can be bounded using Corollary
A.4
. This corollary sets
C_{\upsilon}=2+d/\upsilon
and
C^{\prime}=\frac{C_{d}}{\mathcal{V}C^{C_{\upsilon}}}
, where

C_{d}>0
is a constant depending on
d

and
C
is the constant in Lemma
A.3

that depends on
\lambda
and
\upsilon
. (This means

C^{\prime}
depends on
\lambda,\upsilon,d,\mathcal{V}
. )
The corollary says if
k=O(t^{\frac{d}{\upsilon}}nC_{d}^{\frac{2\upsilon+2d}{2\upsilon+d}})

then

{\Pr}_{X,S}\{|\hat{\eta}(X;S)-\eta(X)|>t\}\leq 2n\exp(-C^{\prime}nt^{C_{\upsilon}}).

(20)

Plugging this and (
19
) back to
(
18
), we have

{\Pr}_{X,S}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq 2n\exp(-C^{\prime}nt^{C_{\upsilon}})+\alpha\exp(-\gamma t^{-\beta}):=\Delta.

(21)

To merge the two terms in
\Delta
,
we first assume
n\geq\frac{2}{C^{\prime}}t^{-C_{\upsilon}}\ln n
,
which is realizable for large enough
n

and a proper choice of
t
. Then, we have

n\leq\exp(\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}nt^{C_{\upsilon}})
and
thus

\Delta\leq 2\exp(-\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}nt^{C_{\upsilon}})+\alpha\exp(-\gamma t^{-\beta}).

(22)

Set
nt^{C_{\upsilon}}=t^{-\beta}
.
Then
t=n^{-\frac{1}{C_{\upsilon}+\beta}}
and
the above implies

\Delta\leq(2+\alpha)\exp\left(-\min(\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime},\gamma)\cdot n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right).

(23)

Also, based on the above choice of
t
, the
conditions on
n
and
k

become
n\geq(\frac{2}{C^{\prime}}\ln n)^{\frac{C_{\upsilon}+\beta}{\beta}}
and

k=O(n^{\frac{2+\beta}{C_{\upsilon}+\beta}})
.
The theorem is proved by
substituting
\frac{2}{C^{\prime}}
by
C^{\prime}
,
and noting that
C^{\prime}
depends on
\lambda,\upsilon

as well as
d,\mathcal{V}
(structure of
\mathcal{X}
).
∎

Remark
4.2
.

Theorem
4.1
implies
that, for
n\geq 1
:

(i) If
k=O(n^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
,
then there exists a constant
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}>0
such
that
||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\exp\left(-C_{\gamma}n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right)
.

(ii) Take
\beta\to\infty
. Then

||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq C_{\alpha}^{\prime}\exp\left(-C_{\gamma}n\right)

for
k=O(n)
,
where
C_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\tilde{C}_{\alpha}<\infty
.

Proof.

To prove (i), we start with arguments
(
21
) and (
23
)
in the proof of Theorem
4.1
,
which imply

{\Pr}_{X,S}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq(2+\alpha)\exp\left(-\min(\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime},\gamma)\cdot n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right)

(24)

when
n\geq(\frac{2}{C^{\prime}}\ln n)^{\frac{C_{\upsilon}+\beta}{\beta}}
. This constraint can
be satisfied for all
n\geq N
, where
N>0
is
a constant depending on
\lambda,\upsilon,d,\mathcal{V},\beta
.
In other words, when
n\geq N
, we have
(
24
).

Set

C_{N}:=(2+\alpha)\exp\left(-\min(\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime},\gamma)\cdot N^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right).

(25)

Then, when
n\leq N
, there is

\begin{split}{\Pr}_{X,S}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq 1&=\frac{1}{C_{N}}(2+\alpha)\exp\left(-\min(\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime},\gamma)\cdot N^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{C_{N}}(2+\alpha)\exp\left(-\min(\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime},\gamma)\cdot n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right).\end{split}

(26)

Combining (
24
)
and (
26
) with

\tilde{C}_{\alpha}:=\max(1,C_{N}^{-1})\cdot(2+\alpha)
, we have

{\Pr}_{X,S}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\exp\left(-\min(\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime},\gamma)\cdot n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right).

(27)

This proves claim (i) in the remark.

To prove claim (ii), it is tempting
to simply take the limit of
\beta
on both sides
of (
27
). However, we need to
first make sure
\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\tilde{C}_{\alpha}<\infty
which is not obvious since
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}

also depends on
\beta
.

Recall
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}=\max(1,C_{N}^{-1})\cdot(2+\alpha)
where
C_{N}
is set in (
25
),
it is clear a sufficient condition for
\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\tilde{C}_{\alpha}<\infty
is
\lim_{\beta\to\infty}N^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}<\infty
.
This is true for the following reason.

First, as
\beta\to\infty
,
N
remains bounded
because now we need every
n\geq N
to satisfy

n\geq\lim_{\beta\to\infty}(\frac{2}{C^{\prime}}\ln n)^{\frac{C_{\upsilon}+\beta}{\beta}}=\frac{2}{C^{\prime}}\ln n,

(28)

which is easy to fulfill by a finite
N
.
Let
N_{*}
be such an integer. Then,

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}N^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}=N_{*}
.

Now we can safely take the limit of
\beta
on both sides
of (
27
), which proves claim (ii).
∎

Corollary
4.3
.

Suppose data distribution has

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin
and
k=O(n^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
.

S
.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
A.1

and argument (
27
)
for Remark
4.2
,
for any
t>0
and
n\geq 1
,

{\Pr}_{S}\{er(\hat{y})-er(\hat{y}_{*})>t\}\leq\frac{1}{t}\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\exp(-C_{\gamma}n^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}),

(29)

Setting the right side to
\delta
and
solving for
t
proves the corollary.
∎

Corollary
4.4
.

Suppose data distribution has
a
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
Tsybakov margin
and let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
be
constants satisfying

\gamma\leq\ln(\alpha/\alpha^{\prime})

If
k=O(n^{\frac{2+\beta}{C_{\upsilon}+\beta}})
,

3.2

Revisit the proof of Theorem
4.1

based on this implied Boltzmann margin.
It sets

(30)

∎

Corollary
4.5
.

Let
k=O(n)
. If data distribution
has a
t_{*}
Massart margin, then

||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq C\exp(-C_{*}n),

(31)

for all
n\geq C_{*}^{-1}\ln n
,
where
C
and
C_{*}\propto t_{*}^{C_{\upsilon}}

Moreover, for every
t_{*}>0
,
there exists a data distribution
satisfying the
t_{*}
Massart margin

31

holds for all
n\geq 1
(possibly

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, the

Boltzmann margin as long as

Revisit the proof of Theorem
4.1

based on this implied Boltzmann margin.

Arguments (
18
)
(

) imply that, if

k=O(t^{\frac{d}{\upsilon}}nC_{d}^{\frac{2\upsilon+2d}{2\upsilon+d}})

||\hat{y}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq{\Pr}_{X,S}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq 2n\exp(-C^{\prime}nt^{C_{\upsilon}})+{\Pr}_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\}.

(32)

Set
t=t_{*}
. Then, the right probability

(33)

Appendix C
Results for Ensemble kNN Classification

Theorem
5.1
.

Suppose data distribution has a

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin.
If
k=O(\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
, then

||\hat{y}_{e}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq C_{\alpha}\exp(-C_{\gamma}\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}),

(34)

for
\tilde{n}\geq\left(2\max\{\ln\tilde{n},2\ln m\}/C^{\prime}\right)^{1+C_{\upsilon}/\beta}
.

Moreover, if
m=O(\sqrt{\tilde{n}})
,
then (
34
) holds for
all
\tilde{n}\geq 1
, with
C_{\alpha}

replaced by a possibly different
constant
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}>0
.

Proof.

Recall
\hat{y}_{e}
is trained on
\tilde{S}
,
which is a collection of
m
samples
S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}

each containing
\tilde{n}
i.i.d. sampled points.

By Lemma
A.1
and Lemma

A.2
, for any
t>0
,

\begin{split}||\hat{y}_{e}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}&\leq{\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\{\hat{y}_{e}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\\[1.99997pt]
&\leq{\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\{|\hat{\eta}_{e}(X;\tilde{S})-\eta(X)|>t\}+{\Pr}_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\}.\end{split}

(35)

The second term can be bounded using Boltzmann
margin. For the first term,

\begin{split}{\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\{|\hat{\eta}_{e}(X;\tilde{S})-\eta(X)|>t\}&={\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\left\{\frac{1}{m}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\hat{\eta}(X;S_{j})-\eta(X)\right|>t\right\}\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\Pr}_{X,S_{j}}\left\{\left|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{j})-\eta(X)\right|>t\right\}\\
&=m\cdot{\Pr}_{X,S_{1}}\left\{\left|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{1})-\eta(X)\right|>t\right\},\end{split}

(36)

where the inequality follows a union bound
and the last equality is based on the
i.i.d. sampling of
S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}
.

The right side of (
36
)
can be bounded using Corollary
A.4
.
Recall
C_{\upsilon}=2+d/\upsilon

and
C^{\prime}=\frac{C_{d}}{\mathcal{V}C^{C_{\upsilon}}}
.
The corollary says
if
k=O(t^{\frac{d}{\upsilon}}\tilde{n}C_{d}^{\frac{2\upsilon+2d}{2\upsilon+d}})
then

{\Pr}_{X,S_{1}}\left\{\left|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{1})-\eta(X)\right|>t\right\}\leq 2\tilde{n}\exp\left(-C^{\prime}\tilde{n}t^{C_{\upsilon}}\right)
.
Plugging this back to (
36
),
we have

\begin{split}{\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\{|\hat{\eta}_{e}(X;\tilde{S})-\eta(X)|>t\}\leq 2m\tilde{n}\exp\left(-C^{\prime}\tilde{n}t^{C_{\upsilon}}\right)\leq 2m\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}\tilde{n}t^{C_{\upsilon}}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}C^{\prime}\tilde{n}t^{C_{\upsilon}}\right),\end{split}

(37)

where the second inequality holds when

\tilde{n}\leq\exp(\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}\tilde{n}t^{C_{\upsilon}})
and the third
holds when
m\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}C^{\prime}\tilde{n}t^{C_{\upsilon}}\right)
.

Plugging the above back to
(
35
), we have

{\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\{\hat{y}_{e}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}C^{\prime}\tilde{n}t^{C_{\upsilon}}\right)+\alpha\exp(-\gamma t^{-\beta}).

(38)

To merge terms, set
\tilde{n}t^{C_{\upsilon}}=t^{-\beta}
. Then

t=\tilde{n}^{-\frac{1}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}

and the above implies

{\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\{\hat{y}_{e}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq(2+\alpha)\exp\left(-\min\left(\frac{1}{4}C^{\prime},\gamma\right)\cdot\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right).

(39)

Based on this choice of
t
,
the above constraints on
\tilde{n}
and
n

can be merged into

\tilde{n}\geq\left(\frac{2}{C^{\prime}}\max\{\ln\tilde{n},2\ln m\}\right)^{1+C_{\upsilon}/\beta}.

(40)

This proves the first claim of the theorem.

The second claim follows from the fact that,
if
m=O(\sqrt{\tilde{n}})
,
then (
40
) becomes

\tilde{n}\geq(C^{\prime\prime}\ln\tilde{n})^{1+C_{\upsilon}\beta}

for some constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
. It is clear this
new constraint can be satisfied for large
enough
\tilde{n}
. This means there exists an
integer
\tilde{N}>0
such that
n\geq\tilde{N}
implies

\tilde{n}\geq(C^{\prime\prime}\ln\tilde{n})^{1+C_{\upsilon}\beta}

and thus (
39
). Set

C_{\tilde{N}}=(2+\alpha)\exp\left(-\min\left(\frac{1}{4}C^{\prime},\gamma\right)\cdot\tilde{N}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right).

(41)

Then, when
n\leq\tilde{N}
, we have

\begin{split}{\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\{\hat{y}_{e}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq 1&=\frac{1}{C_{\tilde{N}}}(2+\alpha)\exp\left(-\min\left(\frac{1}{4}C^{\prime},\gamma\right)\cdot\tilde{N}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{C_{\tilde{N}}}(2+\alpha)\exp\left(-\min\left(\frac{1}{4}C^{\prime},\gamma\right)\cdot\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}\right).\end{split}

(42)

Finally, combining (
39
)
and (
42
) with

\tilde{C}_{\alpha}:=\max\{2+\alpha,\frac{1}{C_{\tilde{N}}}(2+\alpha)\}
proves the second claim.
∎

.

Theorem

Boltzmann margin and

Remark
5.2
.

Theorem
5.1

also applies when
(i) a training set
S
is sampled
i.i.d. from the population, (ii)
subsets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}

S
,
and (iii) in each subset, points

Proof.

Consider the alternative setting.
Let
S
be a training set, whose elements are sampled i.i.d. from the population. Let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}
subsets of
S
that are independently sampled, and points in each
subset are sampled without replacement.
Let
\tilde{S}
be the collection of
these subsets.
Let
\hat{y}_{b}
be the ensemble kNN classifier built on above
\tilde{S}
. Its expected excess error is

||\hat{y}_{b}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{S},S}[er(\hat{y}_{b})]-er(\hat{y}_{*}).

(44)

We will show this error has the same bound
presented in Theorem
5.1
.

First, by the arguments in Lemma
A.1
proof, we have

er(\hat{y})-er(\hat{y}_{*})\leq{\Pr}_{X}\{\hat{y}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}.

(45)

Taking expectation on both sides of
the above w.r.t.
S
and
\tilde{S}
,
we have

||\hat{y}_{b}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq\Pr_{X,\tilde{S},S}\{\hat{y}_{b}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}=\mathbb{E}_{S}\Pr_{X,\tilde{S}\mid S}\{\hat{y}_{b}(X)\neq{\hat{y}}_{*}(X)\}.

(46)

Second, by the arguments in Lemma

A.2
proof, we have

\mathbb{E}_{S}\Pr_{X,\tilde{S}\mid S}\{\hat{y}_{b}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq\mathbb{E}_{S}\Pr_{X,\tilde{S}\mid S}\{|\hat{\eta}_{b}(X;\tilde{S})-\eta(X)|>t\}+\Pr_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\}.

(47)

Next, by following argument (
36
)
in Theorem 5.1 proof, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{S}\Pr_{X,\tilde{S}\mid S}\{|\hat{\eta}_{b}(X;\tilde{S})-\eta(X)|>t\}&=\mathbb{E}_{S}\Pr_{X,\tilde{S}\mid S}\left\{\frac{1}{m}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\hat{\eta}(X;S_{j})-\eta(X)\right|>t\right\}\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}_{S}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Pr_{X,S_{j}\mid S}\{|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{j})-\eta(X)|>t\}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}_{S}\Pr_{X,S_{j}\mid S}\{|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{j})-\eta(X)|>t\}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Pr_{X,S_{j}}\{|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{j})-\eta(X)|>t\}\\[1.00006pt]
&=m\Pr_{X,S_{1}}\{|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{1})-\eta(X)|>t\}.\end{split}

(48)

In above,
\Pr_{X,S_{j}\mid S}
only
treats
S_{j}
as a random subset given
S
,
while
\Pr_{X,S_{j}}
treats
S_{j}
as an
i.i.d. sample of the population (because
its elements are sampled from
S
without replacement).
The last equality holds because
S_{j}
’s
are sampled i.i.d. from
S
and thus now
treated as i.i.d. samples of the population.
Plugging the above back to (
47
)
then (
49
), we have

||\hat{y}_{b}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq m\Pr_{X,S_{1}}\{|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{1})-\eta(X)|>t\}+\Pr_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\}.

(49)

Comparing this bound with the
bound (
35

+
36
)
presented in Theorem

5.1
proof i.e.,

||\hat{y}_{e}-\hat{y}_{*}||_{e}\leq m\cdot\Pr_{X,S_{1}}\left\{\left|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{1})-\eta(X)\right|>t\right\}+\Pr_{X}\{|1/2-\eta(X)|\leq t\},

(50)

we see
\hat{y}_{b}
has the
same excess error bound as

\hat{y}_{e}
. It is also noted
the rest analysis on
\Pr_{X,S_{1}}\left\{\left|\hat{\eta}(X;S_{1})-\eta(X)\right|>t\right\}

is the same for both classifiers,
since
S_{1}
is treated as an i.i.d.
sample from the population in both cases.
∎

.

.

A.1
and

, we

Theorem
5.4
.

Suppose data distribution has a

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin.
Then,
\hat{y}_{e}
is strongly
Bayes consistent
if
k=O(\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})

and
m=\tilde{O}(e^{\tilde{n}})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.1
, for any
\tilde{S}
,

er(\hat{y}_{e})-er(\hat{y}_{*})\leq{\Pr}_{X}\{\hat{y}_{e}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}:=Q(\tilde{S}).

(52)

We will prove strong consistency based
on the following definition i.e., for any
t>0
,

{\Pr}_{\tilde{S}}\{{\lim}_{|\tilde{S}|\to\infty}Q(\tilde{S})>t\}=0.

(53)

According to the Borel-Cantelli Lemma

A.5
, it suffices to show

\sum_{|\tilde{S}|=N_{*}}^{\infty}{\Pr}_{\tilde{S}}\{Q(\tilde{S})>t\}<\infty,

(54)

for some integer
N_{*}
.

Recall
|\tilde{S}|=m\tilde{n}
.
Our observation is that, in order to
verify (
54
),
it is sufficient to verify the following
with
m=1
:

\sum_{\tilde{n}=N_{*}}^{\infty}{\Pr}_{\tilde{S}}\{Q(\tilde{S})>t\}<\infty,

(55)

The reason is, the sequence of

|\tilde{S}|
generated with
divergent
m
and divergent
\tilde{n}

is a subsequence of
|\tilde{S}|

generated with
m=1
and divergent
\tilde{n}
.
Therefore, if the latter sequence
converges, the former sequence also
converges.

Fix
m=1
. We will set
N_{*}
in (
55
)
from two perspectives.

Let
t_{s}>0
be a variable depending
on
\tilde{S}
, and inspect each
{\Pr}_{\tilde{S}}\{Q(\tilde{S})>t_{s}\}
.
Remark
C.1
implies
that for
k=O(\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta+2}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
,

m=\tilde{O}(e^{\tilde{n}})
(which includes

m=1
) and large enough
\tilde{n}

that satisfies
\tilde{n}\geq\left(2\ln\tilde{n}/C^{\prime}\right)^{1+C_{\upsilon}/\beta}
(which sets
N_{*}
):

{\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\{\hat{y}_{e}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\exp(-C_{\gamma}\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}).

(56)

Then, by Lemma
A.1
, we have

{\Pr}_{\tilde{S}}\{Q(\tilde{S})>t_{s}\}\leq\frac{1}{t_{s}}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{S}}Q(\tilde{S})=\frac{1}{t_{s}}{\Pr}_{X,\tilde{S}}\{\hat{y}_{e}(X)\neq\hat{y}_{*}(X)\}\leq\frac{\tilde{C}_{\alpha}}{t_{s}}\exp(-C_{\gamma}\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}).

(57)

Set
t_{s}=\exp(-\frac{C_{\gamma}}{2}\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})
.
Then
t_{s}\to 0
as
\tilde{n}\to\infty
.
This means for any
t>0
, when
\tilde{n}
is large enough (which
sets
N_{*}
from another perspective),
there is
t_{s}<t
and hence

{\Pr}_{\tilde{S}}\{Q({\tilde{S}})>t\}\leq{\Pr}_{\tilde{S}}\{Q({\tilde{S}})>t_{s}\}\leq\frac{\tilde{C}_{\alpha}}{t_{s}}\exp(-C_{\gamma}\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})=\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}C_{\gamma}\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}}).

(58)

Plugging the above back to
(
55
),
we have that for any
t>0
,

\sum_{\tilde{n}=N_{*}}^{\infty}{\Pr}_{\tilde{S}}\{Q({\tilde{S}})>t\}\leq\sum_{\tilde{n}=N_{*}}^{\infty}{\Pr}_{\tilde{S}}\{Q({\tilde{S}})>t_{s}\}\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\sum_{\tilde{n}=N_{*}}^{\infty}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}C_{\gamma}\tilde{n}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+C_{\upsilon}}})<\infty.

(59)

This verifies (
55
)
and further (
54
) with
any divergent
m
. Finally, note we still need

m=\tilde{O}(e^{\tilde{n}})
in order to apply
Remark
C.1
and obtain
(
56
). This proves the theorem.
∎

Appendix D
Numerical Results

D.1
Density Design

Recall
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]

and
\eta(X)=X
.
Given constants
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
,
our goal is to design a density
function
f_{b}
on
[0,1]
that satisfies

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
Boltzmann margin.
The basic idea is threefold: data
decay near decision boundary, data
are constant elsewhere (for better
visualization and does not affect
Boltzmann margin), and density
is Hölder continuous.

Pick a threshold
T\in(0,0.5)

and consider two cases.

When
|X-0.5|\leq T
, we ask data
to decay as if under Boltzmann margin.
This implies

\Pr\{|\eta(x)-0.5|\leq t\}=\Pr\{|x-0.5|\leq t\}=2\int_{0.5}^{0.5+t}f_{b}(x)dx\leq\alpha\exp(-\gamma t^{-\beta}).

(60)

For the last inequality, taking derivative
of
t
on both sides and setting
t^{\prime}=0.5+t

yields

f_{b}(t^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta\gamma e^{-\gamma(t^{\prime}-0.5)^{-\beta}}(t^{\prime}-0.5)^{-\beta-1}.

(61)

A mirrored result of (
60
)
can be obtained by taking integral
on
[0.5-t,0.5]
instead of

[0.5,0.5+t]
, which yields

f_{b}(t^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta\gamma e^{-\gamma(0.5-t^{\prime})^{-\beta}}(0.5-t^{\prime})^{-\beta-1}.

(62)

Combining both results, we can set

f_{b}(x)=\frac{1}{N_{T}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta\gamma e^{-\gamma|x-0.5|^{-\beta}}|x-0.5|^{-\beta-1},

(63)

where
N_{T}
is a normalizer (to be specified
later). This completes the density design
for
|X-0.5|\leq T
.

When
|x-0.5|\geq T
, we ask density
to stay constant i.e.,
f_{b}(x)=\frac{C_{T}}{N_{T}}

for some constant
C_{T}
.

It remains to specify
N_{T}
and
C_{T}
.
Since
f_{b}(x)
is continuous
at
|0.5-X|=T
, based on (
63
)
we have

C_{T}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta\gamma e^{-\gamma T^{-\beta}}T^{-\beta-1}.

(64)

Further,
\int_{0}^{1}f_{b}(x)=1
implies
that (through straight calculations)

N_{T}=\alpha\exp(-\gamma T^{-\beta})+2C_{T}(0.5-T).

Putting all together,
we complete the density
design on
\mathcal{X}
, i.e.,

f_{b}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma}{2N_{T}}\cdot\frac{e^{-\frac{\gamma}{|x-0.5|^{\beta}}}}{|x-0.5|^{\beta+1}},&|x-0.5|\leq T\\[7.5pt]
\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma}{2N_{T}}\cdot\frac{e^{-\gamma T^{-\beta}}}{T^{\beta+1}},&|x-0.5|>T.\end{cases}

(65)
