Evaluating the efficiency of a progesterone radioimmunoassay to predict pregnancy and fetal numbers in ewes
Authors: Sara C Gurule, Sergio A Soto-Navarro, Jennifer A Hernandez Gifford
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccuracy of pregnancy diagnosis in late gestation is essential for adjusting nutritional demands and management protocols in sheep production. The objective of this study was to evaluate the efficiency of progesterone (P4) concentrations measured via radioimmunoassay for determining pregnancy status and estimating the number of lambs expected. Blood samples were collected via jugular venipuncture at day 100 of gestation (term approximately 150 d) from 423 ewes over an 8-year period. Serum P4 concentration thresholds for predicting lamb numbers were established: non-pregnant (0 to 3.8 ng/mL), single lamb (3.9 to 12.5 ng/mL), and multiple lambs (12.6 ng/mL and above). Thresholds were compared with actual lambing outcomes to validate the effectiveness of the assay in detecting number of lambs. We hypothesized that using these P4 thresholds would provide reasonable accuracy in predicting the number of lambs, enabling more precise resource allocation and improved management decisions. The P4 radioimmunoassay demonstrated 99% accuracy for pregnancy detection and 73% overall accuracy in predicting number of lambs. Multiparous ewes demonstrated greater accuracy (84%) than nulliparous ewes (73%) in prediction of lamb number. Maternal serum P4 was positively associated with the number of lambs born (P < 0.001). The P4 × lamb birth weight interaction (P = 0.02) indicated that lamb birth weight modified the association between maternal P4 concentration and the number of lambs born, providing a biological explanation for some of the overlap in P4 concentrations between singleton- and twin-bearing ewes. Accurate prediction of pregnancy status and fetal number can improve flock management by identifying non-pregnant ewes for culling and enabling nutritional and management strategies tailored to ewes carrying single or multiple fetuses, ultimately improving animal welfare, productivity, and profitability. The reliability of the model across parities and years supports its efficacy in mixed-age flocks.

Introduction

Efficient reproductive management in sheep is important for economic and sustainable success of operations. Sheep are seasonally polyestrus animals, highlighting the importance of accurate pregnancy diagnosis, as loss of a breeding opportunity means loss of one productive year (Ganaie et al. 2009). Accurate pregnancy determination and expected lamb number allows producers to modify management practices such as maternal nutritional adjustments and resource allocation, which can enhance both maternal health and lamb survival rates (Simões et al. 2021). Progesterone is the hormone primarily responsible for maintaining pregnancy, where it is initially produced by the ovarian corpus luteum and transitions to the placenta between days 50 and 70, with a second phase of increase between days 90 and 120 of gestation (Ricketts and Flint 1980). This physiological transition makes P4 concentrations particularly informative when measured near or after this gestational timepoint. Progesterone concentrations can suggest the number of developing lambs, which can significantly improve management practices, especially in the critical last six weeks of gestation when fetal growth intensifies, demanding precise nutritional strategies to support both ewe and developing lambs (Redmer et al. 2004).

Traditional transabdominal ultrasonography is a valuable tool for pregnancy diagnosis in sheep, but often limited by equipment availability, trained personnel, and difficulty detecting pregnancy early in gestation (Barbagianni et al. 2017; Ali et al. 2024). Accuracy of fetal number determination varies with gestational age, fetal position, and operator experience (Chauhan and Waziri 1991; Karen et al. 2001). Measuring serum P4 via radioimmunoassay provides an alternative approach for pregnancy determination and fetal number prediction using a single blood sample collected at a strategic time of gestation. The radioimmunoassay measures circulating P4 concentrations with high specificity and sensitivity, offering precise predictive thresholds that can effectively guide management decisions in sheep operations. Importantly, producers generally prefer minimal animal handling, especially those in extensive production systems, making a single blood collection particularly advantageous by reducing stress for both animal and handler. This method not only confirms pregnancy status, but also assists in predicting number of lambs, thereby enabling producers the ability to adjust feed rations and manage space requirements effectively.

A previous study evaluated the use of a commercial P4 radioimmunoassay kit to predict pregnancy and fetal numbers in ewes (Schneider and Hallford 1996), but has since been discontinued by the manufacturer, allowing needed reinvestigation into the efficiency of a currently available radioimmunoassay to detect pregnancy over a longer study duration and increased ewe numbers. The current study re-examines and refines serum P4 thresholds for pregnancy and lamb number prediction and the potential for changed assay dynamics or flock characteristics. This re-validation ensures that producers have reliable and modern standards for management decisions. Establishing reliable P4 concentration thresholds can provide sheep producers with a valuable tool to make informed decisions that could lead to increased profitability through optimized resource allocation and improved lambing rates. Therefore, the aim of this study was to determine the accuracy of serum P4 concentrations at day 100 of pregnancy, in predicting pregnancy status and the number of lambs born. We hypothesized that specific P4 concentration thresholds will accurately predict ewes that are non-pregnant, carrying a single, or multiple lambs based on serum P4 concentrations measured at day 100 of gestation.

Materials and methods

Site and animals

All procedures were approved by the New Mexico State University Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC # 2209000058).

This study was conducted on the campus of New Mexico State University in Las Cruces, New Mexico, at the West Sheep Unit research facility. A total of 423 Rambouillet ewes aged 2 to 7 years old were evaluated over this 8-year study (98 nulliparous, 275 multiparous). The ewe population was supplemented annually with long-yearling nulliparous replacement ewes (18 mo of age). Distribution of ewes by year is demonstrated in Table 1. The diet consisted of 1.60 kg/ewe of alfalfa and 0.23 kg/ewe of corn daily with ad libitum access to shade, water, mineral, and salt.

Breeding and sample collection

Ewes were bred via natural mating, where a fertile sternal rattle-painted ram was introduced to ewes for a 34-d breeding period beginning on October 15th each year. Breeding marks on the rump of ewes were evaluated twice daily and used to predict lambing dates. Blood samples were collected annually via jugular venipuncture targeting approximately day 100 of gestation (term ∼150 d). Across this eight-year study, the actual mean gestational age at blood collection was 105.5 ± 0.9 d. Serum was collected after centrifugation at 1500 × g for 15 min at 4°C and stored at −20°C for no longer than 30 d prior to assay. Samples were thawed only once for P4 analysis.

Radioimmunoassay

Serum P4 concentrations were quantified via a commercially available radioimmunoassay kit (Coat-A-Count, MP Biomedicals, Solon, OH) modified for ovine serum, previously described by (Castañon 2012; Gurule et al. 2021). Briefly, this kit utilized polypropylene tubes coated with an antibody against P4 and 125I-P4 as the tracer. A standard stock of P4 (Sigma, St. Louis, MO) was prepared at a concentration of 10 ng/mL and assayed in duplicate into the antibody-coated tubes to create a standard curve at 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6, and 3.2 ng of P4 per tube. Serum samples were assayed in duplicate at 100 µL, and tubes were normalized to 0.5 mL using assay buffer. Each tube subsequently received 1.0 mL P4-tracer. Tubes were then vortexed and incubated at room temperature for 24 h. Tubes were decanted and counted for 1 min on gamma counter (Packard Cobra II). Detection limit (95% of maximum binding) of the assay was 0.1 ng/mL. Serum P4 had an inter-assay coefficient of variation (CV) of 10.2% and an intra-assay CV of 7.2%.

Data collection

Lambing data were comprehensively recorded each spring during the lambing season (mid-March through April) over the 8-year study period from 2017 to 2024. For each parturition event, the number of lambs born per ewe was documented, along with individual lamb birth weights and sex. Multiple births were managed with individual identification to ensure accurate designation of lambs to their respective dams. Lambing outcome data were systematically organized to track individual ewes across multiple lambing seasons. This approach allowed for assessment of year-to-year variations and parity effects on the progesterone-fetal number relationship. The outcome data included ewe identification, age, parity status (nulliparous or multiparous), lambing date, number of lambs born (0, 1, or 2+), individual weights, and sex of each lamb.

To validate the predictive accuracy of the P4 thresholds established in the study, lambing outcomes (non-pregnant, single, or multiple lambs) were matched with corresponding serum P4 concentrations determined at day 100 of gestation using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA). Predictive accuracy was calculated as the percentage of correct classifications within each category (pregnancy status and lamb number prediction). For each threshold category, the number of correct predictions was divided by the total number of ewes in that category to determine category-specific accuracy rates. Overall accuracy was calculated as the total number of correct predictions across all categories divided by the total number of ewes evaluated.

Statistical analyses

Data were analyzed using SAS (Version 9.4; SAS Institute, Inc., Cary, NC). To evaluate the relationship between maternal serum P4 concentration, lamb birth weight, and the number of lambs born, PROC GLIMMIX assuming a Poisson distribution with a log link function were used. The initial model included serum P4 concentration as a fixed effect to evaluate its association with the number of lambs born. A second model included serum P4 concentration, lamb birth weight, and their interaction as fixed effects to determine whether lamb birth weight influenced the relationship between maternal P4 concentration and the number of lambs born. To account for autocorrelation arising from repeated measurements, a first-order Toeplitz [TOEP(1)] covariance structure was specified. Post hoc estimates and hypothesis testing were performed using PROC PLM. Statistical significance was denoted by P ≤ 0.05, and P > 0.05 < 0.1 reflect trends.

Results and discussion

Model creation

Using the P4 concentrations from all ewes throughout the 8-year study, a predictive model was generated to determine pregnancy status and estimate number of fetuses born according to their circulating serum P4 on day 100 of gestation. This model predicted non-pregnant ewes to have a P4 concentration ≤ 3.8 ng/mL and pregnant ewes with a P4 concentration ≥ 3.9 ng/mL. Additionally, the model predicted the number of lambs born based on: (1) ewes having a P4 concentration ≤3.8 ng/mL were predicted to be non-pregnant, (2) ewes having a P4 concentration ≥3.9 ≤ 12.5 ng/mL were predicted to be carrying one lamb, and (3) ewes having a P4 concentration ≥12.6 ng/mL were predicted to have two or more lambs. These thresholds were developed through comprehensive analysis of the relationship between serum P4 values and actual lambing outcomes across the study period.

The threshold values established in the present study support previous findings demonstrating that circulating P4 concentrations increase with advancing gestation and fetal number. However, the thresholds identified in the current study were greater than those reported by Schneider and Hallford (1996), who established pregnancy determination thresholds of ≥2.5 ng/mL for pregnant ewes, 2.5 to 10.9 ng/mL for single-bearing ewes, and ≥11.0 ng/mL for multiple-bearing ewes at day 100 of gestation. In comparison, the present study identified thresholds of ≥3.9 ng/mL for pregnancy determination, 3.9 to 12.5 ng/mL for single-bearing ewes, and ≥12.6 ng/mL for multiple-bearing ewes at day 100 of gestation.

Pregnancy determination

To determine the ability of the P4 radioimmunoassay to accurately determine pregnancy, P4 concentrations obtained on day 100 of gestation throughout the 8-year study were fitted to the model and checked against the actual number of lambs born. Table 1 presents the annual distribution of ewes used for pregnancy determination, categorized by parity status as either multiparous or nulliparous. The present study included a total of 423 ewes over the 8-year period, with multiparous ewes comprising majority of the study population. This diverse sampling across multiple years and parities provides confidence in the consistency of the model.

Pregnancy was determined with high accuracy using the established P4 threshold (Table 2), with an overall accuracy of 99% across both multiparous and nulliparous ewes throughout the 8-year study. The accuracy of the threshold demonstrates the reliability of the P4 radioimmunoassay technique for pregnancy diagnosis when performed at day 100 of gestation. Our findings closely align with previous research by Schneider and Hallford (1996), who reported 98% accuracy in detecting pregnancy in mature ewes and 99% accuracy in ewe lambs using serum P4 concentrations at a similar stage of gestation. Quantifying P4 at day 100 captures stabilized concentrations, as P4 synthesis has transitioned from the corpus luteum to the placenta (Ricketts and Flint 1980). By this timepoint in gestation, most embryonic and fetal losses have already occurred, making pregnancy diagnosis highly reliable, as pregnancies are highly likely to be carried to term. Majority of pregnancy loss in sheep occurs during approximately the first six weeks of development, with fetal loss between days 0 and 30 reported to be as high as 40% (O’Connell et al. 2016; Juengel et al. 2023). Therefore, a positive diagnosis at day 100 nearly guarantees the ewe will lamb. Although rebreeding open ewes is not feasible at day 100 due to the seasonal polyestrous nature of sheep, reliable pregnancy confirmation at this stage enables producers to implement key management strategies including nutritional adjustments, sorting for lambing, and early culling, which improve flock health and overall profitability.

Progesterone concentrations of pregnant ewes were greater than those of open ewes, allowing a clear separation characterized by the P4 ≤ 3.8 ng/mL threshold, resulting in greater accuracy for pregnancy diagnosis. Across the 8-year study, misclassification rates were minimal, only five classification errors were identified among 423 ewes evaluated (99% overall accuracy). All misclassifications were false negatives (pregnant ewes misclassified as open), while no false positives (open ewes misclassified as pregnant) were observed. Specifically, one error occurred in multiparous ewes in 2018, three errors occurred in multiparous ewes in 2021, and one error occurred in nulliparous ewes in 2022. The absence of false positives was likely due to the consistently low P4 concentrations in open ewes (≤3.8 ng/mL), which demonstrated distinct separation from pregnant ewes. In contrast, false negatives likely reflect biological and animal variation in P4 levels, particularly in pregnant ewes with concentrations near the lower diagnostic threshold.

The current study demonstrates that day 100 P4 measurements offer reliable results compared with early detection methods, which show 50% margin of error when P4 concentrations between 1.01 and 1.46 ng/mL were used (Susmel and Piasentier 1992). The consistently greater accuracy across years and parity status indicates that the radioimmunoassay method is reliable regardless of age or previous reproductive history of the ewe. This consistency is particularly valuable for producers managing mixed-age flocks, as it provides a single reliable diagnostic tool applicable to their entire breeding population.

Prediction of fetal numbers

To determine the accuracy of the P4 radioimmunoassay to detect the number of lambs born, P4 concentrations were also fitted to the model and compared with the actual number of lambs born. Average serum P4 concentration at day 100 of gestation by parity status and number of lambs born are listed in Table 3. These data indicate that P4 concentrations were consistently greater in ewes carrying multiple lambs compared to those carrying a single lamb across all years of the study. This pattern can be explained by increased placental mass and consequently greater P4 synthesis associated with multiple pregnancies, as P4 concentrations during the second half of pregnancy have been positively associated with both placental mass and lamb birth weight in sheep (Wallace et al. 1997). Mean P4 concentration in ewes carrying multiple lambs was approximately 1.5 to 2 times greater than ewes carrying singletons, providing a clear biological marker for distinguishing between single and multiple pregnancies.

The accuracy of the model in predicting fetal numbers reached an overall efficiency of 73% across study duration and parity, with nulliparous and multiparous ewes demonstrating 92% and 84% accuracy, respectively (Table 4). Kalkan et al. (1996) reported a positive relationship between fetal numbers and mean serum P4 concentrations during the second half of pregnancy, but no difference based on lamb sex. Similarly, Chauhan and Waziri (1991) reported serum P4 concentrations increased with fetal number, averaging 9.2, 19.2, and 29.9 ng/mL in ewes carrying single, twin, and triplet pregnancies, respectively. Yotov (2007) also demonstrated greater circulating P4 concentrations in twin-bearing ewes (26.9 ng/mL) compared to single-bearing ewes (20.1 ng/mL) at day 60 of gestation. However, Yotov (2007) reported lower predictive accuracy (66.5%) when using an ELISA-based P4 assay for fetal number determination at day 60 of gestation, whereas the present study utilized a radioimmunoassay at day 100 of gestation. Comparable with the overall accuracy rate of 73% in the current study, Schneider and Hallford (1996) demonstrated an overall accuracy of 79% across three years, with accuracies of 88% and 74% reported for ewe lambs and mature ewes, respectively. These findings suggest that both assay methodology and timing of sample collection may substantially influence accuracy of predicting fetal numbers in sheep.

The present study addressed a critical gap by evaluating P4 thresholds for pregnancy and fetal number prediction using a currently available radioimmunoassay kit to provide a necessary update since the foundational work of Schneider and Hallford. Notably, the P4 threshold concentrations established in this study (≤3.8 ng/mL for non-pregnant, 3.9 to 12.5 ng/mL for a single lamb, and ≥12.6 ng/mL for multiples) were considerably greater and more distinct than those originally reported by Hallford and colleagues (≤2.4 ng/mL for non-pregnant ewes, 2.5 to 10.9 ng/mL for those carrying a single lamb, and ≥11.0 ng/mL for multiple lambs). This discrepancy likely stems from differences in assay sensitivity and calibration standards. Repeating and expanding this work was essential, as it ensures the diagnostic thresholds remain accurate and practical for current sheep production systems, and demonstrates the necessity of periodic reassessment as both technologies and sheep populations may change.

The ability to distinguish between non-pregnant, single-bearing, and multiple-bearing ewes with reasonable accuracy represents a significant management advantage for sheep producers. The last third of gestation is characterized by exponential fetal growth accounting for approximately 90% of total fetal development, dramatically increasing the nutritional requirements of the dam to support both maternal metabolism and developing lamb(s) (Redmer et al. 2004). Undernutrition during this critical period predisposes ewes, particularly those carrying multiple lambs, to metabolic disorders such as pregnancy toxemia or ketosis due to increased fetal glucose demand (Robinson et al. 1971; Baird 1981; Andrews 1997). Additionally, maternal undernutrition during gestation can impair fetal growth and negatively impact postnatal lamb development and metabolic function (Hoffman et al. 2016). Therefore, the ability to estimate fetal number using circulating P4 concentrations enables tailored feeding regimens that help prevent these metabolic challenges.

Lamb weight and lamb number impact on progesterone concentration

As fetal weight increases in utero, placental surface area and placental mass must increase to support greater nutrient exchange (Özyürek and Türkyilmaz 2020; Urun and Şirin 2023). As the placenta becomes the major source of P4 production during mid- to late gestation in sheep, variation in placental growth associated with fetal size may influence circulating maternal P4 concentrations independent of fetal number. To investigate whether lamb birth weight influenced P4 concentrations and potentially affected the accuracy of fetal number prediction, the relationships among serum P4 concentration at day 100 of gestation, number of lambs born per ewe, and lamb birth weight were evaluated using a generalized linear mixed model that accounted for repeated measures within individual ewes.

The initial generalized linear mixed model demonstrated a significant relationship between maternal P4 concentration and number of lambs born (P < 0.0001). These results indicate that as the number of lambs increase, circulating maternal P4 concentrations also increase. To determine whether lamb birth weight contributed to this relationship, lamb birth weight, and its interaction with serum P4 concentration were added to the model. A significant P4 concentration and lamb birth weight interaction was detected (P = 0.02). This interaction indicates that lamb birth weight modifies the relationship between maternal P4 concentration and number of lambs born.

Importantly, these findings provide explanation for some of the misclassification observed when predicting fetal number using circulating P4 concentrations alone. While ewes carrying multiple fetuses generally exhibited greater P4 concentrations due to increased placental mass and endocrine activity, some singleton pregnancies producing larger lambs may also have developed larger placentas capable of producing comparatively elevated P4 concentrations. Consequently, some singleton pregnancies producing larger lambs may have hormonally resembled twin-bearing ewes, resulting in overlap between predictive threshold ranges. This overlap likely contributed to the reduced accuracy observed when distinguishing between single and multiple-bearing ewes and highlights the important influence of placental and fetal development on maternal endocrine profiles during pregnancy.

Limitations and future directions

The present study characterized serum P4 concentrations to establish predictive thresholds for pregnancy status and fetal number using a radioimmunoassay in a research capacity. These findings demonstrate the use of P4 concentrations as predictors of pregnancy outcomes and provide a foundation for future diagnostic applications. However, implementation in commercial sheep operations is currently limited due to the accessibility of conducting the assay.

Future research should evaluate the applicability of these thresholds using other commercially available P4 assays and determine if comparable predictive performance can be achieved across methodologies. Validation of these thresholds in additional flocks, production systems, and sheep breeds will also be important to determine the broader applicability of P4-based pregnancy and fetal number prediction.

Conclusion

These data demonstrate that serum P4 concentrations measured via radioimmunoassay at day 100 of gestation provide a highly accurate method for pregnancy diagnosis (99%) and a reasonably accurate approach for predicting number of lambs born (73%). The established P4 thresholds (≤3.8 ng/mL for non-pregnant, 3.9−12.5 ng/mL for singletons, and ≥12.6 ng/mL for multiples) offer sheep producers a valuable tool for management decisions during the second half of pregnancy. The findings herein demonstrated that maternal serum P4 concentrations were associated with the number of lambs born, but that this relationship was influenced by lamb birth weight, which explains some of the variability in predictive accuracy and highlights the complex physiological factors influencing maternal hormone profiles during pregnancy. This method serves as an alternative to pregnancy diagnosis techniques and fetal number prediction, allowing for improved resource allocation and specialized management practices that can enhance productive outcomes for sheep producers.
