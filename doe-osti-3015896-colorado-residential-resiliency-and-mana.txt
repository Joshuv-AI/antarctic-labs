 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 Validate and model impacts of electric vehicle (EV) home charging on service transformers;
 analyze diverse grid locations and conﬁgurations to determine ‘risk factors’; ﬁnalize/deliver new
 design tools and typical EV load curves; improve/issue new construction standards for
 transformers, secondaries, and services. To ensure residential charging equity, smart charge
 management strategies will be studied and analyzed with the goal of creating aﬀordable demand
 charges for customers.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Task 1: Charging Proﬁle Evaluation

 NLR will capture charging proﬁles of several (~5) EVs under diﬀerent conditions (temperature,
 initial state of charge (SOC), etc.) with several (~5) electric vehicle supply equipment (EVSEs)
   Level-1 (L1) (120V 1.4 kW), Level-2 (L2) (40A 10kW, 80A ~19.2 kW), DC fast charging
 (DCFC) (~50 kW) and extreme fast charging (XFC) (~350kW) under diﬀerent voltage
 conditions to develop input data for vehicle / EVSE models. NLR will work with Xcel to identify
 existing EVs available within NLR or Xcel’s ﬂeet to perform evaluation without procuring
 vehicles. If Xcel ﬂeet vehicles will be loaned to NLR, the attached Appendix C-Loaned
 Participant’s Personal Property for Modiﬁcation 24 will be populated with vehicle speciﬁcs. The
 developed charging models will be used to accurately represent vehicle charging for further
 widescale distribution system impacts. The high-ﬁdelity EV charging system models will be
 created for integration into a Python-based toolkit.

 Task 1 Results:

 This project performed a laboratory testing of several electric vehicles using L2 charging and
 collected high-ﬁdelity data to be processed by larger EV grid integration analysis. The data have
 been collected at EVRI laboratory at NLR for several EVs. The data has been used as an input
 for the open-source EVI-DiST: Electric Vehicle Infrastructure – Distribution System Integration
 Tool available at (https://www.nrel.gov/transportation/electric-vehicle-infrastructure-
 distribution-system-integration-tool). The data can be accessed through EVI-DiST repository.

 The work completed and outcomes of Task 1 are documented in the following technical report:
 https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy26osti/94231.pdf

 Task 2: Develop EV Charging Requirements

 Develop a PEV charging load dataset for Xcel territory in Colorado using GPS travel data from
 conventional vehicles traveling across each region. This dataset will include charge requirement
 results for various scenarios that include (1) EV adoption rates from today, (2) a mix of vehicle
 ranges, and (3) a mix of EV charging infrastructure for both home-dominant and public-
 dominant charging. The dataset will associate the charging requirement for each EV to its
 location to allow load to be disaggregated to individual feeders in the distribution network. The
 disaggregation work will help assess clustering eﬀects by leveraging a consumer choice model
 with current adoption trends.

 Task 3: Reﬁning Smart Charge Control Strategies

 NLR’s RECHARGE project has developed several control strategies: (1) TOU, (2) Randomized
 TOU, (3) Feeder Peak Minimization, (4) Behind-the-Meter DER, and (5) V/Var. This task will
 look at these strategies to determine if these control strategies are applicable to Xcel’s Colorado
 system. The target amount of control strategies shall be ﬁve, but if more are required or there is a
 more promising strategy, it will be added. Some modiﬁcations may be required to reﬁne these
 strategies to account for diﬀerences. A series of simulations will be run to compare the eﬃcacy
 of ﬁve control approaches selected jointly by NLR and Xcel Energy teams in delivering multiple
 grid services in various future scenarios.

 Task 4: Feeder Selection

 Perform analysis using metadata for the feeders in Colorado and identify prototypical feeders: In
 this task, the metadata for the feeders in Xcel Energy’s territory in Colorado will be analyzed to
 identify reference or prototypical feeders (up to 10). The metadata would include information
 such as feeder lengths, location types (rural, suburban, or urban), customer load types
 (residential, commercial, or industrial), expected EV adoption (low, medium, or high), advanced
 metering infrastructure (AMI) deployment territory, and other factors identiﬁed by the Xcel and
 NLR teams. “Heavily” industrialized areas will be outside of the scope of this study. The
 parameters for the secondary network modeling will also be determined. These parameters
 include customer load proﬁles, allocation of non-EV load at the individual customer level, and
 secondary line lengths and line parameters. In some cases, several feeders on the same substation
 will need to be studied to look at impacts when there is a need to balance loads. The NLR team
 will leverage previously developed open-source tools to develop the secondary networks for the
 selected feeders.

 Task 5: EV charging and distribution system modeling

 Detailed distribution grid models will be implemented to understand the locational impact of EV
 charging on distribution grid operations and identify voltage or thermal constraints. The
 modeling will take place in OpenDSS, a powerful open-source software conducive to both
 translating results to commercial software and to releasing developed tools and models to the
 research community. The project team will modify these models to account for diﬀerent EV
 penetrations and their load proﬁles. A Python-based toolkit (using OpenDSS as the power ﬂow
 engine) will be developed for providing additional capabilities, including interfacing with
 vehicle/charger models, developing multiple penetration scenarios, receiving inputs from smart
 charging controls, etc.

 Task 5 Results:

 The work completed and outcomes of Task 5 are documented in the following technical report:
 https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy26osti/94231.pdf. Chapters 5 and 6 of this report specifically address
 Task 5.

 Task 6: Grid Analysis for the selected feeders

 In this task, the NLR team will work with Xcel Energy to develop a baseline for comparing the
 diﬀerent scenarios. The baseline will consider the presence of other DERs for the selected
 timeframe, expected load growth, and EV loading proﬁles from the previous tasks. NLR team
 will select a few representative weeks (seasonal/holiday) to study the impact of EV ﬂeets and the
 eﬀectiveness of the diﬀerent control strategies. The NLR team will deﬁne a few metrics to
 compare performance across at least three diﬀerent scenarios and compute these metrics from
 simulation data. These scenarios may include but are not limited to typical ~50th percentile
 transportation loads, worst-case grid loading scenarios, and a high travel period. These scenarios
 will be selected with Xcel and may involve examining scenarios and factors that generate high
 coincident loading.

 Task 6 Results:

 The work completed and outcomes of Task 6 are documented in the following technical report:

 Task 7: Extend analysis to Xcel Energy’s Public Service Company of Colorado territory

 The NLR team will take the analysis results from Tasks 4 through 6 to extend the outcomes to
 the rest of Xcel Energy’s territory. The NLR team will determine a “risk factor” based on the
 metadata analysis and grid simulation analysis and assign a risk factor to all the feeders in Xcel
 Energy’s territory. Further, a reduced-order analysis will be performed to identify systemic risks,
 including coincidental charging on multiple feeders within the same substation. NLR will model
 the feeders and then turn the “knobs” of vehicle adoption and DER adoption to create a more
 holistic representation of the prototypical feeders.

 Task 8: Transformer thermal loading

 Leveraging the results from Tasks 5 and 6, NLR will create synthetic load proﬁles for the
 evaluation of the thermal characteristics by Xcel (or their supplier) to quantify the life impacts of
 EV charging on transformers. NLR will work with Xcel to deliver load proﬁles consistent with
 the IEEE C57.12.00 standard.
