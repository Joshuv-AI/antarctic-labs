Precision renormalisation and improvement of N f = 3 N_{\rm f}=3 lattice QCD with Wilson fermions
Rainer Sommer

Abstract
We renormalise (and improve) the flavour non-singlet axial current, pseudo-scalar density, vector current and tensor current, as well as quark masses, in O ⁡ ( a ) {\rm O}(a) improved lattice QCD with three massless flavours and lattice spacings down to 0.01 ​ fm 0.01\,{\rm fm} . To this end, we tune a number of lattices with Schrödinger functional boundary conditions and resolutions 8 ≤ L / a ≤ 64 8\leq L/a\leq 64 to lines of constant physics with massless quarks and fixed gradient flow coupling g ¯ GF 2 ​ ( L i ) , i = 0 , 1 , 2 \bar{g}_{\mathrm{GF}}^{2}(L_{i}),\;i=0,1,2 , corresponding to L 0 ≈ 0.25 ​ fm L_{0}\approx 0.25\,{\rm fm} , L 1 = 2 ​ L 0 L_{1}=2L_{0} and L 2 = 4 ​ L 0 L_{2}=4L_{0} . We further renormalise and improve the quark mass of additional heavy quarks for use in the B-physics programme of the collaboration [ 1 ] . Our somewhat technical results enable first-principles strategies for solving multi-scale problems involving, e.g., the b-quark mass [ 2 ] or a large temperature [ 3 ] . Comparing also to other determinations of the axial current renormalisation constant Z A Z_{\rm A} , we have a precise confirmation of how renormalisation and the restoration of chiral symmetry work out with Wilson fermions at small a a . In particular, the accurate restoration of chiral symmetry and the exact flavour symmetry lead to practically negligible uncertainties in observables determined from Ward identities: four to five significant digits are achieved for Z A , Z V Z_{\rm A},Z_{\rm V} . We provide an explanation for the strong suppression of their statistical variances.

Introduction

Across the energy scales, QCD displays very different phenomena from light
hadron physics at low energy to the perturbative region in terms of weakly
interacting quarks and gluons at asymptotically high energy. Exploiting the
full predictive power of the fundamental formulation of the theory, lattice
QCD, requires connecting these regimes. Since available numerical resources do
not allow largely different energy scales to be treated in a common lattice
“simulation”, the ALPHA collaboration has developed the step-scaling
technique. It connects energy scales iteratively. In addition to the
renormalisation problems motivating the original development

[
4
,
5
]
, we would like to mention mastering aspects
of QCD at high temperature
[
3
]
as well as our approach to
treating the large b-quark mass scale in lattice
simulations
[
6
,
7
,
1
]
. We refer to
reviews
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
as well as to
the most advanced application
[
11
]
for an account of the
historical development and more references.

The basic idea
[
4
]
is to introduce a finite,
small

volume with linear size
L
where
L/a={\rm O}(10)
allows for small lattice
spacings,
a
, and therefore large energy scales. The finite-volume physics is
related iteratively,
L_{0}\to L_{1}=2L_{0}\to L_{2}=2L_{1}=2^{2}L_{0}
(and, depending on the
application, also more steps), to the large-volume physics of interest. We note
that in this process the larger but finite volumes with
L\approx 1\,{\rm fm}
are
particularly challenging, as the guidance from perturbation theory can be
grossly misleading
[
12
]
.

Here we carry out precise simulations with
L=L_{0},L_{1},L_{2}
, as specified above,
with
L_{0}\approx\hbox{$1\over 4$}\,{\rm fm}
and three massless flavours of
{\rm O}(a)
improved
Wilson quarks. We compute the improvement and renormalisation coefficients,
which are needed to renormalise the
charm
and
bottom
quark
masses as a prerequisite for the strategy described in
Ref.
[
1
]
and already used in our preliminary
reports
[
2
,
13
]
. We further compute
renormalisation factors for the flavour non-singlet axial-vector current, the
pseudo-scalar density, as well as the vector and tensor currents. In the case of
the axial and vector currents, we also provide results for the coefficients of
the associated
{\rm O}(a)
improvement and mass-improvement terms, respectively. Our
simulations of the finite-volume theory with Schrödinger functional boundary
conditions
[
14
,
15
]
set a new standard in terms of the
resolutions achieved. We go up to
L/a=64
, working with lattice spacings
between
0.007\,{\rm fm}
and
0.08\,{\rm fm}
.

In this paper we describe our simulations, including the non-trivial setup of
the proper lines of constant physics and the determination of the above-mentioned
coefficients. In addition, we present step-scaling functions for the
renormalised coupling and quark mass, improving further on the results of

[
16
,
17
]
in terms of precision and resolution.
The small lattice spacings allow a check that the continuum limit is achieved
smoothly and the extrapolations of
[
16
,
17
]

were safe. Similarly, we check and advance the improvement and
renormalisation of the axial current. We demonstrate rather precisely how
different renormalisation conditions yield compatible results once their
intrinsic discretisation errors are accounted for. All quantities determined
by Ward identities (WIs) are very precise when the lattice spacing is small.
We explain in appendix
D
that this is due to the fact that,
close to the continuum, the statistical variance of quantities determined from
WIs is strongly dominated by contact terms and these are typically suppressed
by
g_{0}^{2}
- and
a/L
-factors.

2.1
Line of constant physics

A non-perturbative interpolating fit formula for the
{\rm O}(a)

, was determined for our
three-flavour lattice QCD action in ref.
[

. As in the case
of

, it naturally applies to the parameter range where the
underlying data were generated, i.e., to the large-coupling region (resp. the
associated
\beta
-range) of state-of-the-art large-volume computations, while

. Therefore, this once again raises the question of the reliability of
the interpolation formula found in
[
25
]
beyond its immediate
scope of applicability.

To clarify this question, we have calculated

directly on our gauge field
ensembles, adopting an alternative improvement condition compared
to
[
25
]
. As originally suggested in
ref.
[
26

as advantageous, it amounts to varying the (spatial)
periodicity angle

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(x+\hat{k}L)=e^{{\rm i}\theta}\psi_{i}(x)\,,\quad\overline{\psi}_{i}(x+\hat{k}L)=\overline{\psi}_{i}(x)e^{-{\rm i}\theta}\,,\quad k=1,2,3\,.

(2.27)

More specifically, we add quenched quarks, mass-degenerate with the sea quarks
but with different
\theta
. Adding an argument
\theta
, we consider their
PCAC masses
m_{ij}(x_{0},\theta)
and parameterise their values at
x_{0}=T/2
in
the form

\displaystyle m_{12}(T/2,\theta)=r(\theta)+ac_{\rm A}s(\theta)\,.

(2.28)

The improvement coefficient
c_{\rm A}
can then be estimated from

\displaystyle c_{\rm A}=-{r(\theta)-r(\theta^{\prime})-\left[r^{(0)}(\theta)-r^{(0)}(\theta^{\prime})\right]\over a(s(\theta)-s(\theta^{\prime}))}\,,

(2.29)

where
r^{(0)}
denotes the value at
g^{2}_{0}=0
as listed in table
10
.
In particular we consider
\theta=0.5
and
\theta^{\prime}=0
. We display the results
for
L\approx L_{1}
and compile them as a function of
g_{0}^{2}
alongside the fit
formula from
[
25
]
on the left hand side of
figure
5
. The comparison demonstrates that the fit formula is
accurate throughout the relevant range of
g_{0}^{2}
up to the always present

a/L
ambiguity. This ambiguity is illustrated on the right hand side of the
figure, where we show the difference between our results and the interpolating
formula together with fits to a linear function in
a/L
. We therefore use the
existing fit formula from ref.
[
25
]
in this paper and
recommend it for future applications.

3
Massive quenched flavours

Quenched heavy quarks are essential for the planned B-physics programme. Again
the starting point is to fix the LCP, now including the bare heavy quark mass.
Furthermore, a first application will be the determination of the b-quark mass
[
13
]
, and we thus need to compute the
renormalisation factor connecting bare quark masses to the renormalisation
group invariant (RGI) quark masses (cf. section
3.2
).
Here we briefly review the renormalisation and improvement of the quark masses
in a standard mass-independent SF scheme and then also in partially massive
schemes, where some mass-enhanced
a^{2}
-effects are absorbed into
renormalisation factors and improvement coefficients. A complete
mass-dependent scheme as in
[
35
]
would also require the
determination of mass-dependent coefficients
c_{\mathrm{A}}(g_{0}^{2},am_{\rm q})
and

c_{\mathrm{sw}}(g_{0}^{2},am_{\rm q})
. It is beyond the scope of our project.

We refer to ref.
[
36
]
(appendix A) for a detailed
discussion of the connection between the renormalisation of the unitary theory
and that of additional quenched quarks.

3.1
Quark mass renormalisation

Wilson fermions break chiral symmetry of QCD. An additive mass-counterterm is
needed to restore it in the continuum limit. For any bare quark mass of
flavour
i=1,\ldots,N_{\rm f}
in the lattice action, one defines the bare
subtracted quark mass

\displaystyle m_{\mathrm{q,}i}\equiv m_{0,i}-m_{\rm crit}(g^{2}_{0})

(3.30)

with a linearly divergent counterterm,
m_{\rm crit}(g^{2}_{0})\sim a^{-1}(k_{1}g_{0}^{2}+\ldots)
. The standard definition for
m_{\rm crit}(g^{2}_{0})
is that all

m_{\mathrm{q,}i}
vanish for vanishing PCAC masses
m_{ij}=0
,
i,j\in\{1,\dots,N_{\rm f}\}
.
In terms of the subtracted masses
m_{\mathrm{q,}i}
, the renormalised,
{\rm O}(a)
improved
quark mass is given by

[
37
,
26
,
38
]

\displaystyle m_{i,\rm R}

\displaystyle=Z_{\rm m}\left\{\left[\,m_{\mathrm{q,}i}+(r_{\rm m}-1){\mathrm{Tr}[M_{\rm q}]}/{N_{\rm f}}\,\right]+aB_{i}\right\}+{\rm O}(a^{2})\,,

(3.31)

\displaystyle B_{i}

\displaystyle=b_{\mathrm{m}}m_{\mathrm{q,}i}^{2}+\bar{b}_{\rm m}m_{\mathrm{q,}i}\mathrm{Tr}[M_{\rm q}]+(r_{\rm m}d_{\rm m}-b_{\mathrm{m}}){\mathrm{Tr}[M_{\rm q}^{2}]}/{N_{\rm f}}+(r_{\rm m}\bar{d}_{\rm m}-\bar{b}_{\rm m}){\mathrm{Tr}[M_{\rm q}]^{2}}/{N_{\rm f}}\,,

where
M_{\rm q}={\rm diag}(m_{{\rm q},1},\ldots,m_{{\rm q},N_{\rm f}})
is the bare
subtracted mass matrix of the sea quarks, and
B_{i}
is a combination of
Symanzik counterterms cancelling
{\rm O}(a)
mass-dependent cutoff effects.
Z_{\rm m}

depends on a renormalisation scale
\mu
, but all other coefficients are only
functions of
g^{2}_{0}
when lattice spacing effects can be neglected.

As already alluded to above, one can also renormalise and
{\rm O}(a)
improve the
current (aka PCAC) quark masses,

\displaystyle m_{ij,\rm R}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Z_{\mathrm{A}}}{Z_{\mathrm{P}}}\dfrac{1+b_{\rm A}\,am_{\mathrm{q},ij}+\bar{b}_{\rm A}\,a\mathrm{Tr}[M_{\rm q}]}{1+b_{\rm P}\,am_{\mathrm{q},ij}+\bar{b}_{\rm P}\,a\mathrm{Tr}[M_{\rm q}]}\cdot m_{ij}+{\rm O}(a^{2})\,,

(3.32)

with
m_{\mathrm{q},ij}=(m_{\mathrm{q},i}+m_{\mathrm{q},j})/2
. The explicit appearance of the quark
mass matrix in such equations reflects the fact that a mass-independent
renormalisation scheme has been chosen. Accordingly, all renormalisation
factors and improvement coefficients are independent of the masses in the
action at leading order in
a
, while improvement terms involve positive
powers of the mass matrix.
3
3

3

For completeness we mention that in the
{\rm O}(a)
improved theory,
and in a mass-independent renormalisation scheme, varying masses at
fixed lattice spacing requires keeping
\tilde{g}_{0}^{2}=g_{0}^{2}(1+b_{\rm g}(g_{0}^{2})a\mathrm{Tr}[M_{\rm q}])
fixed. Renormalisation factors such as

Z_{\rm A},Z_{\rm P}
are therefore taken to depend on
\tilde{g}_{0}^{2}
. This
mass-dependence is relevant for heavy sea quarks as, e.g., used in

[
11
]
, while we here have
\mathrm{Tr}[M_{\rm q}]=0
.

We now consider massive valence quarks in our target
M_{\rm q}=0
theory where
eq. (
3.31
) simplifies to

\displaystyle m_{i,\rm R}

\displaystyle=Z_{\rm m}\left\{m_{\mathrm{q,}i}+ab_{\mathrm{m}}m_{\mathrm{q,}i}^{2}\right\}+{\rm O}(a^{2})\,.

(3.33)

For the remaining improvement coefficients needed in the quenched sector, we
follow standard practices and determine them by requiring the continuum PCAC
relation to be satisfied up to
{\rm O}(a^{2})
,

\displaystyle m_{ij,\rm R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\hbox{$1\over 2$}\left[m_{i,\rm R}+m_{j,\rm R}\right]+{\rm O}(a^{2})\,.

(3.34)

We explicitly employ the procedure originally developed in
ref.
[
39
]
with the improvements suggested in
ref.
[
36
]
. Imposing (
3.34
),
eqs. (
3.33
) and (
3.32
) lead to the following
relation between the bare current and subtracted quark masses at fixed gauge
coupling
g_{0}^{2}
,

\displaystyle m_{ij}

\displaystyle=Z\left\{m_{{\rm q},ij}+ab_{\mathrm{m}}\hbox{$1\over 2$}({m_{{\rm q},i}^{2}+m_{{\rm q},j}^{2}})-a(b_{\rm A}-b_{\rm P})m_{{\rm q},ij}^{2}\right\}+{\rm O}(a^{2})\,,

(3.35)

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle={Z_{\rm m}(\mu)Z_{\rm P}(\mu)}/{Z_{\rm A}}\,,

which allows to determine the set of coefficients
Z
,
b_{\mathrm{m}}
and

b_{\mathrm{AP}}\equiv b_{\mathrm{A}}-b_{\mathrm{P}}
non-perturbatively.
Note that the
\mu
-dependence cancels in
Z_{\rm m}Z_{\rm P}
and that one cannot
disentangle the coefficients
b_{\mathrm{A}}
and
b_{\mathrm{P}}
, since only their difference
appears after expanding the denominator for small
am_{\mathrm{q},ij}

in (
3.32
).
Furthermore, higher-order mass effects can be ignored, as they contribute only
at order
a^{2}
.

As explained in detail in section 2 of ref.
[
36
]
, it is
convenient to consider the specific set of masses

\displaystyle m_{{\rm q},i}=\epsilon\,,\,\text{for}\;i\leq N_{\rm f}=3\,,\quad m_{{\rm q},4}=m_{{\rm q},5}=2\Delta+\epsilon\,,\quad m_{{\rm q},6}=m_{{\rm q},7}=\Delta+\epsilon\,,

(3.36)

which depend on two mass-parameters
\Delta
and
\epsilon
. For
\epsilon=0
,
we have three massless sea quarks and two doublets of degenerate valence quarks
with masses
2\Delta
and
\Delta
, respectively. Using eq. (
3.35
)
for flavour combinations
(i,j)\in\{(1,2)
,
(1,4)
,
(4,5)
,
(6,7)\}
, one
constructs the explicit estimators

\displaystyle R_{Z}

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{m_{45}-m_{12}}{2\Delta}+a\left(R_{\rm AP}-R_{\rm m}\right)(m_{45}+m_{12})\,,

(3.37a)

\displaystyle R_{\rm m}

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{2\left(m_{14}-m_{67}\right)}{(m_{45}-m_{12})\,a\Delta}\,,

(3.37b)

\displaystyle R_{\rm AP}

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{2m_{14}-m_{45}-m_{12}}{(m_{45}-m_{12})\,a\Delta}\,.

(3.37c)

They determine
Z
up to corrections of order
a^{2}
, and
b_{\mathrm{m}}
and
b_{\mathrm{AP}}
up to

{\rm O}(a)
, respectively. The mix of non-degenerate (
m_{14}
) and degenerate
(
m_{45},m_{67}
) PCAC masses is needed to separate
b_{\mathrm{m}}
from
b_{\mathrm{AP}}
.
The estimators are also valid for any finite
\epsilon={\rm O}(m_{12})
. We
utilise them only with small values of
\epsilon
to account for our
mistunings of the sea quark masses, i.e., for the fact that they are not exactly zero.

Taking the limit
\Delta\to 0
defines our mass-independent scheme for
Z

and the two
b
-parameters.
The so-defined functions
Z(\tilde{g}_{0}^{2})
,
b_{\mathrm{m}}(\tilde{g}_{0}^{2})
and

b_{\mathrm{AP}}(\tilde{g}_{0}^{2})
still have a residual dependence on the details of the
definitions of the PCAC masses,
namely, on our choice of an SF scheme with specific angles
\theta
and
classically-improved finite difference operators as in
refs.
[
40
,
39
,
41
,
42
,
36
]

which are advantageous for heavy quarks.
These
dependences are
{\rm O}(a^{2})
for
Z
and
{\rm O}(a)
for the
b
-parameters and
change only
{\rm O}(a^{2})
effects in the improved theory.
Below we define what we call a partially massive scheme (or “massive scheme”
for short), which absorbs some of the
(am_{\rm q})^{n}
-effects,
n>1
, into
mass-dependent
Z,\,b_{\mathrm{m}},\,b_{\mathrm{AP}}
and evaluate them numerically.
This scheme is similar to the one in

[
36
,
42
,
41
]
and goes part of the
way to a completely massive scheme whose advantages have been discussed
in
[
35
]
.

3.2
Partially massive scheme

It is not necessary to take the limit
\Delta\to 0
in the above estimators.
Instead it is expected to be advantageous to choose
\Delta
to be of the order
of the (bare) quark mass under consideration. This then introduces higher
orders in
am_{\rm q}
, which will absorb some of the higher-order cutoff effects.
Such a definition of a massive scheme is not complete, and mass-dependent
cutoff effects will remain, since additional improvement terms with a
different structure are required. Let us just give one example. Beyond the
first order in
am_{\rm q}
, terms
a^{2}m_{\mathrm{q},i}m_{\mathrm{q},j}
are needed in numerator and
denominator of eq. (
3.32
). These are not absorbed by mass-dependent

Z,\,b_{\mathrm{m}},\,b_{\mathrm{AP}}
. Nevertheless it has been observed in

[
42
,
41
]
that taking
\Delta
around the quark
mass of interest reduces cutoff effects compared to
\Delta=0
.

For the practical choice of
\Delta
, one needs to add a condition keeping the
heavy
renormalised
quark mass fixed and determine
a\Delta
for each

a/L
on the desired LCP. We are interested in extending LCP-0,
table
1
, in this way.

As in

[
32
,
43
,
17
,
42
,
44
,
33
]

we fix the RGI heavy quark mass,
M_{\mathrm{h}}
, which is scale and scheme
independent.
4
4

4

Here, the symbol
M
is not to be confused with the matrix of bare
subtracted sea quark masses appearing in the foregoing subsection.

The dimensionless variable

\displaystyle z_{\mathrm{h}}

\displaystyle=L_{0}M_{\mathrm{h}}\,,

(3.38)

then has to remain constant as
a/L_{0}
is varied. In terms of the
subtracted
quark mass
m_{\rm h,R}(\mu)
, renormalised at scale
\mu
, the
z
-variable
reads

\displaystyle z_{\mathrm{h}}

\displaystyle=L_{0}\,h(\mu)\,m_{\rm h,R}(\mu)

\displaystyle=L_{0}\,h(\mu)\,\dfrac{Z_{\mathrm{A}}(g_{0}^{2})}{Z_{\mathrm{P}}(g_{0}^{2},a\mu)}Z(g_{0}^{2},a\Delta_{\mathrm{h}})\left\{1+ab_{\mathrm{m}}(g_{0}^{2},a\Delta_{\mathrm{h}})m_{\mathrm{q,h}}\right\}m_{\mathrm{q,h}}\,,

(3.39)

where the flavour-independent function
h(\mu)=M/m_{\rm R}(\mu)
connects the
PCAC quark mass renormalised at scale
\mu
to its renormalisation group
invariant counterpart. For the three-flavour theory, this factor has been computed
non-perturbatively in the continuum limit in ref.
[

\mu=1/L_{0}
it evaluates to

\displaystyle h(1/L_{0})

\displaystyle\equiv\left.\dfrac{M}{m_{\mathrm{R}}(\mu)}\right|_{\mu=1/L_{0}}=1.4744(87)\,.

(3.40)

To fix
z_{\mathrm{h}}
to a prescribed value at finite lattice spacing, we still need
Z

and

on each lattice as a function of the mass parameter
\Delta_{\mathrm{}}
, and to

\Delta_{\mathrm{}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{h}}

Scaling of current quark masses involving bottom quarks

The estimators
R_{X}
, relevant for mass-improvement, have been derived from the
equality of (renormalised and improved) current and subtracted quark masses,
cf. eq. (
3.35
). While the latter is used to fix the heavy
LCP, we can ask the question for residual cutoff effects on current quark
masses. The corresponding RGI current quark masses are defined as

\displaystyle z_{ij}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{L}{2}(M_{i}+M_{j})=h(\mu)\lim_{a\to 0}\left[\dfrac{Z_{\mathrm{A}}(g_{0}^{2})}{Z_{\mathrm{P}}(g_{0}^{2},a\mu)}(1+b_{\mathrm{AP}}(g_{0}^{2},a\Delta)am_{\mathrm{q},ij})Lm_{ij}\right]\,.

(3.44)

Using eq. (
3.44
), we define

\displaystyle z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{hh}}

\displaystyle=z_{\mathrm{hh}}\,,

\displaystyle z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{h1}}

\displaystyle=2z_{\mathrm{h1}}-z_{\rm 12}\,.

(3.45)

At finite lattice spacing, both choices provide an estimator for the RGI mass
of the heavy quark flavour that should agree in the continuum with the value of

z_{\mathrm{h}}
used to set up the heavy LCP. Moreover, we can employ the leading-order
relationship
Zm_{\mathrm{q},ij}=m_{ij}
to replace the mass-improvement term
b_{\mathrm{AP}}am_{\mathrm{q},ij}
through
\tilde{b}_{\mathrm{AP}}am_{ij}
, where
\tilde{b}_{\mathrm{AP}}=b_{\mathrm{AP}}/Z

(

(a/L_{0})^{2}

4
Conclusions and outlook

We have carried out the renormalisation of massless
N_{\rm f}=3
QCD along three
lines of constant physics, defined by the gradient flow coupling with
Schrödinger functional boundary conditions. The specific choices are listed
in table
6
. Renormalisation factors of the flavour non-singlet
bilinears have been computed: axial-vector, pseudo-scalar, vector, tensor. Also
several improvement coefficients
b_{X},\,c_{X}
have been determined. In
table
6
we provide the pointers to tables and figures with
detailed results.

Since our primary goal was to perform the renormalisations for the ALPHA
collaboration B-physics project
[
1
]
, which is based on
step-scaling
[
4
]
, the lattice spacings are mostly rather
small. They are too small for direct large-volume computations, cf.
figure
2
. One might, however, devise step-scaling approaches
similar to
[
1
]
for applications other than B-physics.

A quick look at the “precision” column of the summary table reveals very high
to extremely high precision for quantities determined by Ward identities
(WIs). This can be understood theoretically. Variances are given by squaring
the path integral observable. For those quantities
Y
determined by a WI,
written in the form
\langle W(Y)\rangle=0
, the WI can be used in the path
integral representation of the variance. This removes the largest part of the
variance, leaving the remaining terms due to
{\rm O}(a^{n})
violations of the WI as well
as contact terms. We provide more details in appendix
D
.

Since chiral symmetry breaking by the Wilson term is restored by the chiral
WIs, one may also say that close to the continuum limit chiral symmetry
breaking plays no major role after renormalisation and
{\rm O}(a)
improvement. Of
course, the determination of the various coefficients has to be carried out.
Here we have not determined the coefficients of
{\rm O}(a)
terms that involve
the sea quark masses since these are not needed in the step-scaling functions
of
[
1
]
. Some of them can be determined through relations of
PCAC masses at finite and small sea quark masses. A general principle for
determining these coefficients is to impose the absence of linear terms in the
quark mass in a finite periodic volume
[
48
]
or at short
distances
[
49
]
.

We have computed all necessary renormalisations with a precision that will be
subdominant in the physics observables of the B-physics
project
[
1
,
2
,
13
]
and expect that
selected results will be useful beyond this application. We have further
determined renormalisation and improvement coefficients in a partially massive
valence scheme in section
3.2
.
In addition to previous
implementations
[
36
,
42
,
41
]
, we
determined a full
{\rm O}(a)
improved LCP for heavy valence quarks at the scale
of the heavy quark (
\Delta_{\rm h}=\Delta(z_{\mathrm{h}})
), which effectively includes
some relevant massive higher-order cutoff effects.
This scheme is tailored for B-physics, and we have demonstrated that it
does reduce higher-order discretisation errors in this context. It has contributed to the good
scaling observed in ref.
[
13
]
.

Finally, in our simulations we have varied the lattice spacing by a significant
factor, keeping physics scales constant. This allowed us to verify the expected
critical slowing down
\sim a^{-2}
of the HMC algorithm, cf.
figures
11
and
12
in appendix
E
.

A.3
Projection to
Q=0

The topological charge is defined as in
[
16
]
,

\displaystyle Q=\dfrac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\,a^{4}\sum_{x}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathrm{Tr}\left[G_{\mu\nu}^{a}(x,t)G_{\rho\sigma}^{a}(x,t)\right]^{\rm cl}_{\sqrt{8t}=0.3\,L}\,,

(A.46)

with the clover definition, “cl”, for the lattice field tensor at flow time

\sqrt{8t}=cL
with
c=0.3
, using the Zeuthen flow. The projector is taken as

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}(Q)=\begin{cases}1&|Q|<0.5\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,,

(A.47)

such that projected expectation values are

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}\rangle_{Q=0}={{\langle{\cal O}\,\hat{\delta}(Q)\rangle}\over{\langle\hat{\delta}(Q)\rangle}}\,.

(A.48)

In
[
54
]
it has been shown that polynomials of flowed gauge
fields do not require renormalisation (beyond the one of coupling and quark
mass). One may worry that (
A.48
) is not just a
polynomial in the fields, but it has further been demonstrated numerically that
configurations away from integer
Q
are suppressed with a high power of
a
in
the lattice path integral
[
55
]
. Therefore
(
A.48
) preserves the renormalisation properties of

\langle{\cal O}\rangle
.

, the

As in previous work, see e.g.
[

(C.49)

with
F_{1}

2.16

Appendix D
On the variance of PCAC quark masses

Here we present a qualitative discussion of the variance of the quark masses,
as an example of a quantity computed from Ward identities. Ignoring details
such as
Q=0
projection, improvement, (and initially) the sum over different
time slices, we rewrite the condition for the PCAC mass,
m
, as

\displaystyle F(m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0,\quad\text{with}\quad F(m)=\langle W^{12}(x_{0})\rangle\,,

(D.59)

where

\displaystyle W^{12}(x_{0})=a^{3}\sum_{\bf x}w^{12}(x)\,\mathcal{O}^{21}\,,\quad w^{12}(x)={\partial}_{0}A^{12}_{0}(x)-2m\,P^{12}(x)\,,

(D.60)

and note that the external operators
{\mathcal{O}}^{ji}={a^{6}\over L^{3}}\sum_{\bf x,y}\bar{\zeta}_{j}({\bf x})\gamma_{5}\zeta_{i}({\bf y})
have vanishing
mass dimension,
[{\mathcal{O}}^{ji}]=0
, and create only space-momentum zero
states. The variance
\Sigma^{2}(m)
of
m
is related to that of
F
by

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(m)\cdot(F^{\prime}(m))^{2}=\Sigma^{2}(F(m)),

(D.61)

where
F^{\prime}(m)
is the sensitivity of the WI to
m
. We are from now on
interested only in the qualitative behaviour at small
a
, i.e. the dependence
on powers of
a
. In other words, we ignore multiplicative
a
-independent or
only logarithmically varying terms and denote this by the
\sim
symbol. Since

F^{\prime}(m)={\rm O}(a^{0})
we have

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(m)\sim\Sigma^{2}(F(m))\,.

(D.62)

The variance related to fluctuations of only the gauge field is given by

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[W^{12}(x_{0})]_{\psi}[W^{12}(x_{0})]_{\psi}\rangle_{U}-\langle[W^{12}]_{\psi}\rangle_{U}^{2}

(D.63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[W^{12}(x_{0})]_{\psi}[W^{12}(x_{0})]_{\psi}\rangle_{U}\,,

where
[\bullet]_{\psi}
is the (exact) Grassmann “average” over the fermion
fields and
\langle.\rangle_{U}
the gauge average. Introducing a quenched copy
of the fermions with indices 4,5, we can avoid splitting fermion and gauge
average, and write

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle W^{12}(x_{0})\,W^{45}(x_{0})\rangle\,

(D.64)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{6}\sum_{\bf x,x^{\prime}}\langle w^{12}(x_{0},{\bf x})\,w^{45}(x_{0},{\bf x^{\prime}})\,\mathcal{O}^{21}\,\mathcal{O}^{54}\rangle\,.

Close to the continuum limit, we may use the PCAC operator relation

w^{12}(x)\approx 0
at all points except
\bf x=\bf x^{\prime}
and get

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(F)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle a^{6}\sum_{\bf x}\langle a^{d-8}\ \mathcal{O}^{d}(x_{0},{\bf x})\,\mathcal{O}^{21}\,\mathcal{O}^{54}\rangle\,,

(D.65)

where
\mathcal{O}^{d}(x_{0},{\bf x})
is a dimension
d
Lorentz-scalar operator
which can arise from mixing with
w^{12}(x_{0},{\bf x})\,w^{45}(x_{0},{\bf x})
.
Since higher-dimensional operators are suppressed by powers of
a
, only the
lowest
d
is relevant. Due to the (non-singlet) flavour structure

\overline{\psi}_{1}\psi_{2}\,\overline{\psi}_{4}\psi_{5}
of
\mathcal{O}^{d}
we have
d=6
. Using
translation invariance

\displaystyle a^{3}\sum_{\bf x}\langle\mathcal{O}^{d}(x_{0},{\bf x})\,\mathcal{O}^{21}\,\mathcal{O}^{54}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{3}\langle\mathcal{O}^{d}(x_{0},{\bf x})\,\mathcal{O}^{21}\,\mathcal{O}^{54}\rangle\,,

(D.66)

we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(LF)~\sim~a^{d-5}~\sim~{a\over L}\,.

(D.67)

The last equation is just due to dimensional reasoning and therefore valid up to
some dimensionless function
f(g_{0})
for small
m
. Clearly, to get a
non-vanishing contribution in perturbation theory, we need two gluon lines,
connecting the 12 and 54 parts. This provides a factor
g_{0}^{4}
and we find

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(LF)\sim g_{0}^{4}{a\over L}\,.

(D.68)

Finally, let us take into account the average over
|I_{T}|
values of
x_{0}
in
eq. (
2.9
). The contact term in the variance only appears for
equal-time contributions, which also means that the correlation between
estimates
m(x_{0})
at
x_{0}
with those at
y_{0}\neq x_{0}
vanishes to the
accuracy considered here. Thus we get another suppression

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}(Lm_{\eqref{e:x0average}})\sim g_{0}^{4}{a\over L}\dfrac{1}{|I_{T}|}\sim g_{0}^{4}{a^{2}\over LT}\,.

(D.69)

We note that in several steps, e.g. in (
D.63
) or going from

In all cases under consideration here, the integrated autocorrelation time

A special source for autocorrelation is the freezing of the topological charge
at small lattice spacing
[
60
,
61
]
. Charges that
differ from zero are exponentially suppressed in small
volumes
[
62
]
. This helps to some extent, but for the
L_{2}

lattices we face a more challenging situation. Here, non-zero charges do
occur with a probability of a few tens of percent, as clearly seen on the
coarser lattices where rather frequent tunnellings are observed. But the
sampling of the topological charge strongly depends on the lattice spacing.
The charge is frozen to zero on the very fine lattices of the ensembles tuned
to LCP-0 and LCP-0s in tables
1
and
2
.
Because we define our observables in the zero charge sector, cf.
eq. (
A.48
), the frozen situation is not the difficult
one. Most problematic are the ensembles in the intermediate region, where the
charge changes once in a while and the simulation may be stuck in sectors of
non-zero charge for a number of trajectories. This can have a negative effect
on the autocorrelation times.

It is then reassuring that all in all the observables defined by
eq. (
A.48
) show rather standard critical slowing down,
as illustrated for the ones with the largest autocorrelations in
figures
11
,
12
. Note that the freezing of the
charge happens around
a\approx 0.05\,{\rm fm}

[
60
,
61
]
. For
L\leq 0.5\,{\rm fm}
, shown in
fig.
11

0.05\,{\rm fm}
and complete
agreement with
\tau_{\mathrm{int}}\propto a^{-2}
is observed. On the other
hand, for
L\approx 1\,{\rm fm}
the transition to freezing is at
L/a\approx 20
.

\tau_{\mathrm{int}}[F_{1}]
on LCP-2 and
\tau_{\mathrm{int}}[F_{1}]

despite these LCPs being very close. We interpret this as a sign of
insufficient statistics to properly determine the uncertainty of

\tau_{\mathrm{int}}
in the difficult transition region. We emphasise again that
in the figures we showed the observables with the strongest autocorrelations.
