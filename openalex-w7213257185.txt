ARTICLE

Environments of quasi-stationary linear precipitation system
associated with Baiu frontal depressions
Eigo Tochimoto1

· Hiroshi Niino2 · Yasutaka Hirockawa1

Received: 14 April 2026 / Accepted: 3 September 2026
© The Author(s) 2026

Abstract
Heavy rainfall in Japan is often caused by quasi-stationary linear precipitation systems (QSLPSs), which frequently occur
in association with Baiu frontal depressions (BFDs). While the importance of the roles of BFDs in QSLPS occurrence
has been suggested by previous case studies, there is no systematic analysis to clarify the differences between the environments of BFDs that cause QSLPSs (QSL-BFDs) and those that do not (NQSL-BFDs). Using ERA5 reanalysis data,
this study statistically compares the characteristics of QSL-BFDs and NQSL-BFDs, based on composite analyses of 36
QSL-BFDs and 101 NQSL-BFDs, respectively, detected over and west of the Kyushu Islands in June and July between
1989 and 2020. The results show that QSL-BFDs are characterized by significantly stronger low-level moisture flux and
convergence in the eastern part of mainland China and over the East China Sea, leading to enhanced large-scale lifting
and mid-troposphere moistening prior to QSLPS occurrence. Between 6 and 12 h before QSLPS occurrence, significant
differences in low-level moisture, convergence, and conditional instability (especially in terms of Entraining CAPE)
between QSL-BFDs and NQSL-BFDs emerge, suggesting that these quantities can be useful precursors for prediction of
the potential of QSLPSs. In contrast, storm-relative environmental helicity (SREH) does not differ significantly between
the two types of the BFDs, indicating that it is not an effective discriminator.
Keywords Quasi-stationary linear precipitation system · Baiu frontal depression · Heavy rainfall

1 Introduction
Organized mesoscale convective system often cause heavy
rainfall events, resulting in catastrophic damage (e.g.,
Schumacher and Rasmussen 2020). In Japan, more than
50% of heavy rainfall events are associated with quasistationary linear precipitation system (QSLPS) (e.g., Tsuguti and Kato 2014; Hirockawa et al. 2020a; Hirockawa
and Kato 2022a), which are characterized by a precipitation area in length of 50–300 km and in width of 20–50 km
(e.g., Kato 2020), stagnating and anchoring near the same
area for several hours. Since QSLPSs are caused by various
meso-alpha to synoptic scale disturbances such as stationary

fronts, tropical cyclones, extratropical cyclones and their
associated fronts (Hirockawa et al. 2020a), the understanding of the processes that occur in association with these
disturbances and create favorable environments for QSLPS
occurrence is important to provide more insight into the key
atmospheric conditions leading to the formation of QSLPSs.
During boreal summer, a quasi-stationary front, called
Baiu/Meiyu front (hereafter simply referred to as Baiu front),
emerges around East Asia. A large number of QSLPSs occur
along or south of Baiu frontal zone (e.g., Hirockawa et al.
2020b; Kato 2020). Along the periphery of a northwestern
Pacific subtropical high, abundant low-level moisture is
transported from the East China Sea to Baiu frontal zone
(Ninomiya and Akiyama 1992), which contributes to the
formation and maintenance of QSLPSs. Occasionally, an
approaching upper-level trough associated with a subtropical jet contributes to enhance QSLPSs through inducing the
dynamical forcing of ascent and/or destabilizing the atmosphere (Tsuji et al. 2020; Tochimoto et al. 2022; Ueno and
Takemi 2025).

One of the important meso-alpha to synoptic scale disturbances linked to QSLPS occurrence is the Baiu frontal
depression (hereafter referred to as BFD), which often forms
and develops along Baiu frontal zone. In the western parts
of Baiu frontal zone, especially west of Japan Islands, BFDs
are known to be intensified mainly through diabatic heating
due to the condensation of water vapor, with relatively little
contribution of baroclinicity (Ninomiya and Kurihara 1987;
Tagami et al. 2007; Tochimoto and Kawano 2012, 2017a,
2017b; Tochimoto and Yanase 2023). An upper-level trough
approaching from west also is known to intensify a BFD
(Akiyama 1984; Chang et al. 1998; Shibagaki and Ninomiya
2005; Tochimoto and Kawano 2012, 2017b; Tochimoto and
Yanase 2023; Ueno and Takemi 2025). Since an existence of
a BFD over the East China Sea increases the zonal pressure
gradient between the BFD and the subtropical high, northward moisture flux toward Japan Islands is intensified (e.g.,
Ogura et al. 1985; Araki et al. 2021; Tochimoto et al. 2022;
Manda et al. 2025; Ueno and Takemi 2025). In early July
in 2020, for example, several BFDs successively developed
between mainland China and the East China Sea, enhanced
low-level moisture flux and its convergence in the southeast quadrant of the BFDs, leading to the formation of nine
QSLPSs in the Kyushu Islands (Araki et al. 2021). During
this period, the formation of a BFD that caused a QSLPS
leading to a serious flood in Kumamoto prefecture on 3–4
July in 2020 was contributed by an upper-level trough
(Tochimoto et al. 2022). The role of an upper-level trough
in enhancing a BFD and also inducing ascent affecting a
heavy rainfall event in 2018 was also demonstrated (Tsuji et
al. 2020; Ueno and Takemi 2025). While several case studies highlighted the relationship between BFDs and heavy
rainfall events, to the best of our knowledge, no systematic study on the environments of QSLPSs associated with
BFDs has been made.
Favorable environments for heavy rainfall events associated with QSLPSs in Japan have been examined in previous
studies. For example, the importance of moisture and its flux
at low-levels (e.g., at 500 m height) was demonstrated (e.g.,
Kato 2018; Tsuguti et al. 2019; Araki et al. 2021). Furthermore, several studies suggested that mid-tropospheric moisture plays important roles in heavy rainfall events (Unuma
and Takemi 2016; Tsuji et al. 2021, 2023). Kato (2020) proposed 6 necessary conditions for the occurrence of QSLPSs
(hereafter, referred to as 6-conditions) as follows:
1. Moisture flux at 500 m height > 150 g m s−1.
2. Storm relative environmental helicity (SREH) > 100 m2
s−2.
3. 400 km area-averaged vertical velocity > 0 m s−1.
4. Relative humidity at 700 and 500 hPa > 60%.
5. Equilibrium height > 3000 m.

6. The distance from 500 m height to the level of free

Since QSLPSs are a type of mesoscale deep convection,
their environment is related to conditional instability, which
is often measured by CAPE. For the assessment of QSLPS
environments, however, entraining CAPE (E-CAPE; e.g.,
Sueki and Niino 2016; Tochimoto et al. 2019) that considers
the effect of mid-tropospheric moisture may be more appropriate than conventional CAPE, since mid-tropospheric
moisture is included in the 6-conditions. Although BFDs
may affect these environmental conditions for the formation
and maintenance of QSLPSs, not all BFDs cause QSLPSs.
Thus, it is desired to elucidate differences between the environments and structure of BFDs that cause QSLPSs and
those that do not, in order to gain a better understanding of
QSLPSs environments associated with BFDs.
The aim of the present study is to obtain a comprehensive
understanding of the environments of QSLPSs associated
with BFDs through a statistical analysis. This study mainly
focuses on QSLPSs that occurred in the Kyushu Islands since
the largest number of QSLPSs take place there. We examine
the evolution processes that establish favorable conditions
for QSLPS occurrences. The data and the methods used in
the present paper are described in Sect. 2. The characteristics of BFDs that cause QSLPSs (QSL-BFDs) and those that
do not (NQSL-BFDs) are introduced in Sect. 3. Results of
the composite analysis are shown in Sect. 4, and summary
and discussion are provided in Sect. 5.

2 Methods
2.1 Dataset of BFDs and QSLPS
Dataset for BFD positions and their 3-hourly tracks in
June and July between 1989 and 2020, which are detected
based on Japanese 55-year reanalysis (JRA-55; Harada et
al. 2016) in Tochimoto and Yanase (2023), is used in the
present study. In their study, BFDs are detected by Hodges’s
algorithm (Hodges 1994, 1995, 1999) using 3-hourly sea
level pressure (SLP) fields [6-hourly analysis (00, 06, 12,
18 UTC) and 6-hourly 3-hour forecast (03, 09, 15, 21 UTC)
SLP fields]. To detect meso-α-scale BFDs, the SLP data are
spectrally filtered using a bandpass filter of T42–T106 spectral resolution (200–500 km). The SLP minima are linked
between consecutive steps to form tracks by minimizing a
cost function that depends on the track smoothness.
Following Hirockawa et al. (2020a, 2020b), Hirockawa and Kato (2022a, 2022b), QSLPS between 1989 and
2020 are detected based on four-connected neighboring
algorithm (Samet and Tamminen 1988) applied to radar/

raingauge-analyzed precipitation amounts (RAP; Nagata
2011). The detection procedure is briefly summarized as
follows: A four connected component labelling (Samet and
Tamminen 1988) is used to identify candidate rainfall systems. A three-hourly accumulated precipitation of at least
80 mm is required to define a heavy rainfall area. QSLPSs
are objectively extracted with considering spatio-temporal
continuity of heavy rainfall areas. In the present study,
the candidates with durations of more than five hours are
defined as QSLPSs. The occurrence time of a QSLPS is
determined from 3-hour accumulated precipitation data. If
the time when a heavy rainfall area is first detected at the
time of t1, the occurrence time of the QSLPS is defined by
t1 – 3 h. A detailed description of the procedure for detecting
QSLPSs can be found in Hirockawa et al. (2020a, 2020b,
2026), submitted Hirockawa and Kato (2022a; 2020b). In
the present study, we only consider QSLPSs detected in
the area of 129–132°E, and 30–34°N to focus on QSLPSs
around Kyushu Islands.

2.2 Detection of QSL-BFD and NQSL-BFDs
In this subsection, the procedure for detecting BFDs that
cause QSLPS is described (Fig. 1). The nearest time of
3-hourly JRA-55 time to QSLPS occurrence is defined
as time t0. BFDs, whose positions at t0, are located within
650 km of the position of the maximum of 3-hour accumulated precipitation (P3Max) associated with QSLPSs
are identified. Although a distance threshold of 500 km was
used to extract QSLPSs associated with low-pressure systems in Hirockawa et al. (2020a), we adopted a threshold of
650 km to ensure the inclusion of typical QSLPSs associated with the BFDs observed in July 2020 (e.g., Araki et al.
2021; Hirockawa et al. 2020b; Tochimoto et al. 2022). If
there are more than one BFDs satisfying this condition, the
nearest one is extracted as the QSL-BFD (Fig. 1a). Next, a
BFD that is located within 100 km from a QSL-BFD center
and does not cause QSLPS, is extracted. This threshold was
selected to extract NQSL-BFDs occurring sufficiently close
to the locations of QSL-BFDs. To compare cyclones of
similar strength between QSL-BFD and NQSL-BFDs, only
BFDs whose central SLP differs from that of corresponding
QSL-BFD by less than 1.5 hPa are selected (Fig. 1b). This
criterion ensures that the difference in central SLP between
the two groups is not statistically significant. Furthermore,
we have imposed an additional criterion that the date of
occurrence of NQSL-BFDs must be within one week from
that of the corresponding QSL-BFD, though the year can
be different. In the present study, we focus on QSL- and
NQSL-BFDs located within the area of 124–131°E, and
30–35°N to examine the characteristics of BFDs around
and west of the Kyushu Islands. Finally, 36 QSL-BFDs and

2.3 Composite analysis
A composite analysis is performed to clarify the differences
in atmospheric conditions between QSL-BFDs and NQSLBFDs. To examine the environments of QSLPSs, this study
utilizes ERA5 reanalysis (Hersbach et al. 2023) with a horizontal grid spacing of 0.25° and a temporal resolution of
3 h, instead of JRA-55, between t0 – 48 h and t0 + 48 h. A
comparison of the composite fields between JRA-55 and
ERA5 shows that the overall characteristics are similar.
However, the ERA5 data set is suitable for investigating
mesoscale environments because of its finer spatiotemporal
resolution than that of JRA-55, which has a horizontal resolution of approximately 60 km horizontal resolution and a
temporal interval of 6 h. To assess whether the differences
in atmospheric variables between QSL-BFDs and NQSLBFDs are statistically significant, a permutation test (Efron
and Tibshirani 1993) is applied.

3 Characteristics of QSL-BFDs and NQSLBFDs
Figure 2 shows locations of the QSL-BFD and NQSLBFD centers at t0, as well as P3Max of QSLPSs. Positions
of BFD centers at t0 are quite similar between QSL-BFDs
and NQSL-BFDs. QSLPSs generally occur in the western
part of the Kyushu Islands since warm moist air associated
with BFDs is transported from the East China Sea (e.g.,
Ninomiya and Akiyama 1992). Figure 3 shows the tracks
of QSL- and NQSL-BFDs. QSL-BFDs mainly form over
mainland China and the East China Sea, and then they move
eastward and reach their locations at t0 (blue dots). Thereafter, some of them move into the Northwestern Pacific after
passing through the ocean south of Japan Islands, while others move to the Sea of Japan across the Japan Islands or
Tsushima Strait. The tracks of NQSL-BFDs are generally
similar to those of the QSL-BFDs: the majority of NQSLBFDs also form over mainland China and the East China
Sea and subsequently move eastward.

In the present study, environmental conditions favorable for
an occurrence of quasi-stationary linear precipitation system
(QSLPS) associated with Baiu frontal depressions (BFDs)
have been examined by means of composite analyses. BFDs
that lead to the formation of QSLPSs (QSL-BFDs) around
Kyushu Islands and those that do not (NQSL-BFDs) in June
and July between 1989 and 2020 were compared to clarify
the differences in their environmental ingredients, where

environmental helicity (SREH), which is related to vertical
wind shear and is one of the 6-conditions proposed by Kato

and NQSL-BFDs to the west of the Kyushu Islands. This
suggests that SREH alone is not necessarily an effective discriminator between QSL-BFDs and NQSL-BFDs.

differences between QSL-BFDs and NQSL-BFDs. However, the underlying physical processes that strengthen

mainland China remain unclarified. To reveal these processes is an important subject for the future work. Moreover, formation and maintenance mechanism of QSLPSs
associated with BFDs need to be clarified, which might
be possible if QSLPSs are reproduced in a high-resolution
numerical simulation in which the composite fields of QSLBFDs are used as initial and boundary conditions.
