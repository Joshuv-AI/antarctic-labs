Inverse Hessian Curvature Flow in Minkowski Space I: Cocompact Hypersurfaces Thanks: The second author is supported by NSFC Grant No.12141105.
Dake Li, Zhizhang Wang, Shiqi Yin

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the cocompact inverse σ k \sigma_{k} curvature flow in Minkowski space. We prove the longtime existence and convergence of this flow. As a consequence, quermassintegral inequalities are established.



1.
Introduction

Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
is
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
endowed with the pseudo-metric

ds^{2}=dx_{1}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{n}^{2}-dx_{n+1}^{2}.

i.e.
\left<{X},{Y}\right>=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}-x_{n+1}y_{n+1}
. A nonzero vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
is said to be spacelike, timelike, or null respectively if
\left<{X},{X}\right>>0,<0
or
=0
respectively. Denote
E_{n+1}=(0,\cdots,0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. We regard
x_{n+1}
as the time function. If the
(n+1)
-th component of
X
is positive, or equivalently,
\left<{X},{E_{n+1}}\right><0
, we say that
X
is future directed.

In this paper, we studied the inverse Hessian curvature flow.

Definition 1.1

(ICF in Minkowski space)
.

A family of time-dependent spacelike hypersurfaces
M_{t}=X:M\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
is said to be an inverse curvature flow if it satisfies following equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}X(\cdot,t)=-\frac{1}{F(\cdot,t)}\nu(\cdot,t),\quad\forall t\in(0,T),\\
&X(\cdot,0)=X_{0}(\cdot),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
F=F(h,g)=f(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
is the curvature function depending on the first and second fundamental forms
g,h
, or the principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
of
M_{t}
.
Moreover,
f(\kappa)
is strictly increasing with respect to each
\kappa_{i}
, concave, and of homogeneous degree
1
, normalized by
f(1,1,\cdots,1)=1
.
\nu
is the future directed (timelike) unit normal vector, and
F>0
for all
(p,t)\in M\times[0,T)
.

In this paper we only studied
F=s_{k}^{1/k}\equiv(\sigma_{k}/\binom{n}{k})^{1/k}
,
k=1,2.\cdots,n
, where the Hessian curvature
\sigma_{k}
is defined by the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial of the principal curvatures:

\sigma_{k}(\kappa)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\kappa_{i_{2}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{k}}.

For example, if
F(p,t)=s_{1}(\kappa)(p,t)=\frac{g^{ij}h_{ij}}{n}
is the mean curvature, then (
1.1
) is called the inverse mean curvature flow.

The curvature flow problem in Euclidean space has been extensively studied in the literature. In
[
22
]
, Huisken studied the mean curvature flow, which has been generalized to Gauss curvature and a large family of curvature flows by Chow
[
12
]
and Andrews
[
2
]
. In
[
15
]
and
[
33
]
, Gerhart and Urbas respectively considers the inverse curvature flow in Euclidean space, which is a similar flow equation as (
1.1
) with the opposite sign. Roughly speaking, they have proved that, for any compact initial hypersurface
M_{0}
, the ICF will expand
M_{0}
and it will convergence to some sphere by a suitable scaling. For entire graphical flows, Daskalopoulos-Huisken
[
13
]
studied the inverse mean curvature flow.

In Minkowski space, Andrews, Chen, Fang and McCoy
[
3
]
first studied the curvature flow for cocompact hypersurfaces. More precisely, they consider the curvature flow:

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}X(\cdot,t)=F(\cdot,t)\nu(\cdot,t),\quad\forall t\in(0,T),

where
F
is a homogenous degree 1 curvature function defined in Definition
1.1

and the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
is spacelike, co-compact, and strictly convex. Aarons
[
1
]
, Bayard-Schnürer
[
6
]
and Bayard
[
5
]
studied the mean curvature flow, Gauss curvature flow and scalar curvature flow with a forcing term in Minkowski space. For the non-compact hypersurfaces, the second author and Xiao
[
36
,
39
]
studied the Hessian curvature self-expanders and the Hessian curvature flows of entire space-like hypersurfaces in Minkowski space. Then, Qu, the second author and Wo
[
27
]
generalized a similar result to Hessian curvature translating solutions. Moreover, the second author and Xiao
[
37
]
also studied some fully nonlinear curvature flows of noncompact spacelike hypersurfaces in Minkowski space. One may see
[
31
,
30
,
35
,
38
]
for related prescribed curvature problem in Minkowski space.

An important applicaition of the inverse curvature flow is to prove quermassintegral inequalities. By using
[

,
33
]
, Guan-Li
[

]
establishes the quermassintegral inequalities for star-shaped compact hypersurfaces in Euclidean space.
In hyperbolic space, the inverse curvature flow and

[

[

Brendle-Guan-Li

[

establishes the full quermassintegral inequalities for compact strictly hypersurfaces if its principal curvatures are less than 1. If we would like to remove the upper bound of the principal curvatures, they can prove part of quermassintegral inequalities. For more reference, the reader

In this paper, we discuss the ICF of standard co-compact hypersurfaces. Following
[
3
]
, we have

Definition 1.2

(standard co-compact)
.

A spacelike hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
is said to be standard co-compact if there exists a discrete subgroup
G
of the future preserved Lorentz group
\mathrm{O}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n,1})
that acts freely and properly discontinuously on
M
, such that the quotient Riemannian manifold
M/G
is compact. Here

\displaystyle\mathrm{O}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n,1})=

\displaystyle\Big\{\text{linear }T:\mathbb{R}^{n,1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n,1}\mid\left<{TX},{TY}\right>=\left<{X},{Y}\right>,\forall X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n,1},\left<{TE_{n+1}},{E_{n+1}}\right><0\Big\}

is the future preserved Lorentz group.

All orientable closed surfaces with negative Gauss curvature can be isometric embedded into Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{2,1}
as some spacelike strictly convex standard co-compact surfaces by a theorem of Chen-Yin
[
10
]
. Therefore, spacelike strictly convex standard cocompact hypersurfaces can be viewed as closed manifolds with negative sectional curvatures. Moreover, these manifolds have nontrivial topological structure induced by the group
G
.

A special class of solutions to equation (
1.1
) are the self-shrinkers, which are defined by

Definition 1.3

(self-shrinker)
.

A spacelike hypersurface
X:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
is called a self-shrinker if it satisfies the equation

F=-\frac{1}{\left<{X},{\nu}\right>}.

(1.2)

It is obvious that the hyperboloid
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n,1}\mid\left<{X},{X}\right>=-1\}
satisfying the above equation. The self-shrinker defined here is corresponding to the self-shrinker of curvature flow for closed compact hypersurfaces in Euclidean space.
We can prove the following rigidity result for any co-compact self-shrinker:

Theorem 1.4
.

A
k
-convex co-compact spacelike self-shrinker of the ICF (
1.1
), where
F=s_{k}^{1/k}
, must be a branch of hyperboloid
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
.

Here
k
-convex means:

Definition 1.5
.

A
C^{2}
regular hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
is
k
-convex,
if the principal curvatures of
M
at
X\in M
satisfy
\kappa[X]\in\Gamma_{k}
for all
X\in M
, where
\Gamma_{k}

is the Gårding cone

\Gamma_{k}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\sigma_{m}(\lambda)>0,m=1,\cdots,k\}.

One may compare Theorem
1.4
with Gao-Li-Wang
[
14
]
.
Let

u(x,t)=\sqrt{e^{-2t}+|x|^{2}}.

One may easily check that
X_{t}=(x,u(x,t))
satisfying (
1.1
). As
t
goes to infinity, we can see the hypersurface
M_{t}
convergences to the light cone, whose principal curvatures are infinite. Now we take a normalized flow

\tilde{X}_{t}=e^{t}X_{t},

then
\tilde{X}_{t}
convergences to the hyperboloid. In general, similar to the above baby model, we can prove:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
F=s_{k}^{1/k}
in the ICF (
1.1
), where
s_{k}={\sigma_{k}}/{\binom{n}{k}}
is the normalized k-th elementary symmetric polynomial. If the initial spacelike hypersurface is strictly convex and standard co-compact, then the flow (
1.1
) has a unique long-time solution
X:M\times[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
. After scaling by
\tilde{X}(p,t)=e^{t}X(p,t)
, this solution converges in
C^{\infty}
-topology to the upper branch of a hyperboloid
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}(r_{\infty})
with radius
r_{\infty}
. In addition, the limit hyperboloid
M^{\infty}
satisfies the volume relation:

\operatorname{Vol}(M^{\infty}/G)\leq\operatorname{Vol}(M_{0}/G),

with equality when
F(\kappa)=s_{1}(\kappa)
.

Similar to Guan-Li
[
16
]
, we can prove the quermassintegral inequalities by using the above theorem:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
M
be a standard co-compact convex spacelike hypersurface with related Lorentz group
G
,
\overline{M}=M/G
. Let
\left\lvert{\overline{M}}\right\rvert:=\int_{\overline{M}}1\text{d}\mu_{M}
be the
n
-dimensional volume of
\overline{M}
. Then for
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we have

{\left(\int_{\overline{M}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)\text{d}\mu_{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k}}}\leq{(C_{n}^{k})^{\frac{1}{n-k}}\left\lvert{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G}\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{n-k}-\frac{1}{n}}}\cdot\left\lvert{\overline{M}}\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{n}}.

(1.3)

The equality holds if and only if
M
is exactly a part of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
.

Remark 1.8
.

When
k=n
, the inequality (
1.3
) becomes

\int_{\overline{M}}\sigma_{n}(\kappa)d\mu_{M}=|\mathbb{H}^{n}/G|.

(1.4)

Geometrically,
\sigma_{n}(\kappa)d\mu_{M}
corresponds to the volume form of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
pulled back via the Gauss map
N:M\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Since the Gauss map induces a global diffeomorphism between the compact quotients
\overline{M}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}/G
, the total integral of the Gauss-Kronecker curvature
\sigma_{n}(\kappa)
equals to the volume of
\mathbb{H}^{n}/G
, which is called the
hyperbolic volume
.

Remark 1.9
.

By Mostow’s theorem (see
[
7
]
), the hyperbolic volume
\left\lvert{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G}\right\rvert
on the right-hand side of (
1.3
) is a topological invariant of
M/G
.

Remark 1.10
.

When
n
is an even number, the hyperbolic volume can be explicitly expressed by the Euler characteristic class (
[
28
]
Theorem 11.3.2, Gauss-Bonnet Theorem)

|{\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G|=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot\text{Vol}(S^{n})}{2}\chi({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G),

where
\text{Vol}(S^{n})
is the Euclidean volume of the
n
-dimensional unit sphere. Since
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G
is homeomorphic to
M/G
under the flow,
\chi({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)=\chi(M/G)
.
When
n
is an odd number, we do not have an explicit formula for the hyperbolic volume. However, it is still a topological invariant of
M/G
.

Remark 1.11
.

As Guan and Yuan
[
17
]
pointed out, if
M^{n}
is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, each
\sigma_{k}(\kappa[M])
is intrinsic. We will give an analogous result in Minkowski space (Lemma
8.2
).

Since all we known quermassintegral inequalities hold for topological spheres, we think our inequalities are the first quermassintegral inequalities for manifolds with nontrivial topological structure, i.e. “high genus” manifolds. Therefore, we believe that inequalities (
1.3
) are interesting.

At last, let’s give two questions.
A compact manifold with negative sectional curvature is not necessarily able to be embedded in Minkowski space as a spacelike hypersurface of codimension
1
. Moreover, by Remark
1.11
,
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
can be expressed as a polynomial of the Riemmanian curvature tensor
Riem_{g}
, then the integral in (
1.3
) is still meaningful.
Therefore, it is a natural question that
does the geometric inequalities (
1.3
) hold for all closed manifolds
(N,g)
with negative sectional curvature?

Another question is that can we extend Theorem
1.6
to
F=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{k-1}}
and then give the full quermassintegral inequalities as
[
16
]
? The difficulty here is that we can not give the upper bound of the principal curvatures in Minkowski space. In our second paper
[
23
]
, we can give some counterexamples for non cocompact hypersurfaces. In fact, we can construct some strictly convex initial hypersurface
M_{0}
, and the flow equation has a solution
M_{t}
such that, at finite time, the curvatures will blow up. However, we do note find appropriate counterexample for cocompact case. Note that, in
[
19
]
, a similar difficulty occurs in de Sitter space.

The organization of the paper is as follows. Section 2 gives the proof of rigidity of self-shrinkers. In Section 3, we will derive the evolution equations of geometric quantities. In Section 4, we will rewrite our flow equation in hyperboloid by Gauss map. Section 5 proves the longtime existence of (
1.1
). We will estimate the support function, speed function, gradient of the support function and principal curvatures. In Section 6, we derive the normalized flow equation and establish the uniform curvature estimate. Section 7 proves the convergence of the normalized flow. In the last Section, we establish the quermassintegral inequalities and prove that
\sigma_{k}
curvatures also are intrinsic in Minkowski space.

2.
Rigidity of Self-shrinkers

Let
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
be a local coordinate of
M
. Denote
X_{i}:=\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x_{i}}},X_{ij}:=\frac{\partial^{2}X}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},\nu_{j}

:=\frac{\partial{\nu}}{\partial{x_{j}}}
, then we have the Gauss-Weingarten formula:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{ij}=\Gamma_{ij}^{k}X_{k}+h_{ij}\nu,\\
&\nu_{j}=A_{j}^{k}X_{k},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where
A_{j}^{k}=g^{kl}h_{lj}
is the shape operator,
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
is the Christoffel symbol and
\nu
is the future directed (timelike) unit normal vector.

The main part of this section is to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:
Let
\phi:=\left<{X},{\nu}\right>
. We will prove that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F^{ij}\phi_{ij}\leq\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}\left<{X},{\nabla\phi}\right>,

where
F^{ij}=\frac{\partial{F}}{\partial{h_{ij}}}
. For a fixed point
p\in M
, choose a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
such that
\nabla e_{j}=0,\forall j=1,2,\cdots,n
. Then
F^{ij}(p)=\operatorname{diag}\{F^{11},\cdots,F^{nn}\}
, and

\displaystyle\phi_{i}:

\displaystyle=\nabla_{e_{i}}\phi=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij}\left<{X},{e_{j}}\right>,

\displaystyle\phi_{ii}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nabla_{i}h_{ij}\left<{X},{e_{j}}\right>+\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij}\left<{e_{i}},{e_{j}}\right>+\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij}h_{ji}\left<{X},{\nu}\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nabla_{j}h_{ii}\left<{X},{e_{j}}\right>+h_{ii}+\kappa_{i}^{2}\left<{X},{\nu}\right>,

where we have used Codazzi equation
\nabla_{i}h_{ij}=\nabla_{j}h_{ii}
. By differentiating the self-shrinker equation (
1.2
), we have
\nabla F=\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}\nabla\phi
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\phi_{ii}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}F^{ii}\sum_{j}\nabla_{j}h_{ii}\left<{X},{e_{j}}\right>+\sum_{i}F^{ii}h_{ii}+\sum_{i}F^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}\left<{X},{\nu}\right>

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}F\left<{X},{e_{j}}\right>+F+\sum_{i}F^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}\left<{X},{\nu}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<{X},{\nabla F}\right>+F+\sum_{i}F^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}\left<{X},{\nu}\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}\left<{X},{\nabla\phi}\right>+\left(F-\frac{1}{F}\sum_{i}F^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}\right),

where we have used Euler’s identity
\sum_{i,j}F^{ij}h_{ij}=F
in the second equality, and the equation (
1.2
) again in the last equality. Since
F=s_{k}^{1/k}
,
F^{ii}=\frac{1}{k}s_{k}^{1/k-1}s_{k}^{ii}
, we have

\frac{1}{F}\sum_{i}F^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}=\frac{1}{ks_{k}}\sum_{i}s_{k}^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}.

From the well-known identity

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}^{ii}(\kappa)\kappa_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa|i)\kappa_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}(\kappa)\sigma_{k}(\kappa)-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}(\kappa),

we deduce that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{k}^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}=ns_{1}(\kappa)s_{k}(\kappa)-(n-k)s_{k+1}(\kappa).

By Newton-Maclaurin’s inequality,
s_{1}\geq s_{k}^{1/k}
,
s_{k+1}\leq s_{k}^{(k+1)/k}
, and the equality holds if and only if all principal curvatures are equal to each other. We conclude that
F-\frac{1}{F}\sum_{i}F^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}\leq 0
and then
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F^{ij}\phi_{ij}\leq\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}\left<{X},{\nabla\phi}\right>
from (
2.2
). By strong maximum principle,
\phi
must be constant on
M
. Then
\nabla^{2}\phi=\nabla\phi\equiv 0
, which implies
F-\frac{1}{F}\sum_{i}F^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}\equiv 0
then
M
is totally umbilical. Thus
M
is a branch of hyperboloid
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
.
∎

3.
Evolution Equations

In a local coordinate system
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
, the first and second fundamental forms are defined by

g_{ij}=\left<{X_{i}},{X_{j}}\right>,\quad h_{ij}=-\left<{\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}X},{\nu}\right>.

Here
\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}X=X_{ij}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}X_{k}
.
The mean curvature is defined by
H=\operatorname{tr}_{g}(h)=g^{ij}h_{ij}
. Recall that ICF stands for the following evolution:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}X=-S\nu:=-F(h,g)^{-1}\nu,\quad\forall t\in(0,T),\\
&X(\cdot,0)=X_{0}(\cdot).\end{aligned}\right.

For a spacelike hypersurface
(M^{n},g)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
, let
\nabla
be its Levi-Civita connection. The Riemannian curvature tensor is defined by

{R}(X,Y)Z=-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z+\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z+\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.

(3.1)

By using a local frame
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
, we define

{R}_{ijkl}=g(R(e_{i},e_{j})e_{k},e_{l}),\quad{R}_{ikj}^{\hskip 8.19447ptp}={R}_{ikjm}g^{mp}.

(3.2)

Recall the following two fundamental geometric equations:

R_{ijkl}=-(h_{ik}h_{jl}-h_{il}h_{jk})\quad\text{(Gauss equation)}.

(3.3)

\nabla_{k}h_{ij}=\nabla_{j}h_{ik}\quad\text{(Codazzi equation)}.

(3.4)

We will denote
\dot{F}^{ij}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_{ij}}
,
\ddot{F}^{ij,kl}=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial h_{ij}\partial h_{kl}}

and
\dot{S}^{ij}=\frac{\partial S}{\partial h_{ij}}
,
\ddot{S}^{ij,kl}=\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial h_{ij}\partial h_{kl}}
. We firstly derive evolution equations for some geometric quantities.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under ICF (
1.1
), the following evolution equations hold:

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}=-2Sh_{ij}
,

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\nu=-\nabla S
,

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{ij}=-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}S-SA_{i}^{p}A_{j}^{q}g_{pq}
,

(4)

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}F=F^{-2}\dot{F}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}F-2F^{-3}\dot{F}^{ij}\nabla_{i}F\nabla_{j}F+F^{-1}\dot{F}(h^{2})
.

Proof.

We follow the calculations in
[
3
]
. Using Gauss-Weingarten formula (
2.1
), the evolution of the induced metric
g
is given by:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\left<{\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{i}}}},{\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{j}}}}\right>

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\left<{\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{i}}}(-S\nu)},{\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{j}}}}\right>+\left<{\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{i}}}},{\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{j}}}(-S\nu)}\right>

\displaystyle=-SA_{i}^{k}g_{kj}-Sg_{ik}A_{j}^{k}

\displaystyle=-2Sh_{ij}.

The evolution for the unit future directed normal
\nu
is given by (notice that
\nu\cdot\nu=-1
):

\displaystyle\left<{\frac{\partial{\nu}}{\partial{t}}},{\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{i}}}}\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\left<{\nu},{\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{i}}}}\right>-\left<{\nu},{\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{i}}}(-S\nu)}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<{\nu},{\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{i}}}(S\nu)}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<{\nu},{\frac{\partial{S}}{\partial{x^{i}}}\nu+SA_{i}^{k}\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{k}}}}\right>

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial{S}}{\partial{x^{i}}},

which leads to

\frac{\partial{\nu}}{\partial{t}}=-\frac{\partial{S}}{\partial{x^{i}}}g^{ij}\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{j}}}=-\nabla S.

(3.6)

The evolution for the second fundamental form
h
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\left<{\frac{\partial{\nu}}{\partial{x^{i}}}},{\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{j}}}}\right>

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\left<{\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{i}}}\left(\frac{\partial{\nu}}{\partial{t}}\right)},{\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{j}}}}\right>+\left<{\frac{\partial{\nu}}{\partial{x^{i}}}},{\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{j}}}\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{t}}}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<{\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{i}}}\left(-\frac{\partial{S}}{\partial{x^{k}}}g^{jl}\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{l}}}\right)},{\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{j}}}}\right>+\left<{\frac{\partial{\nu}}{\partial{x^{i}}}},{\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x^{j}}}(-S\nu)}\right>

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial{S}}{\partial{x^{k}}}\right)-\left<{\frac{\partial{\nu}}{\partial{x^{i}}}},{S\frac{\partial{\nu}}{\partial{x^{j}}}}\right>

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}S-SA_{i}^{p}A_{j}^{q}g_{pq}.

Introduce the canonical spacetime connection (see
[
4
]
, Section 6.3)

\nabla_{t}\partial_{i}:=\sum_{p=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}g^{pj}\partial_{t}g_{ij}\right)\partial_{p}=-S\sum_{p=1}^{n}A_{i}^{p}\partial_{p},

(3.8)

where
\partial_{i}:=\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{x^{i}}}
(
i=1,\cdots,n
). Then
\nabla_{t}g_{ij}=0
, and we have:

\nabla_{t}h_{ij}=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{ij}-h(\nabla_{t}\partial_{i},\partial_{j})-h(\partial_{i},\nabla_{t}\partial_{j})=-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}S+SA_{j}^{p}h_{ip}.

(3.9)

Since
\nabla_{t}g_{ij}=0
, we have

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}S(h,g)=\dot{S}^{ij}\nabla_{t}h_{ij}=-\dot{S}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}S+S\dot{S}(h^{2}),

(3.10)

where
\dot{S}(h^{2})=\dot{S}^{ij}A_{j}^{p}h_{pi}
.
From Gauss-Codazzi equations and Ricci’s identity,

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}h_{kl}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}h_{jl}

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}h_{jl}+R_{ikj}^{\hskip 8.19447ptp}h_{pl}+R_{ikl}^{\hskip 8.19447ptp}h_{jp}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{ij}+h_{jk}h_{i}^{p}h_{pl}-h_{ij}h_{k}^{p}h_{pl}+h_{kl}h_{i}^{p}h_{jp}-h_{il}h_{k}^{p}h_{jp}.

Plug it into (
3.9
), then we get:

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}h_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{i}(\dot{S}^{kl}\nabla_{j}h_{k}l)+SA_{j}^{p}h_{ip}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=-\dot{S}^{kl}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}h_{kl}-\ddot{S}^{kl,mn}\nabla_{i}h_{mn}\nabla_{j}h_{kl}+SA_{j}^{p}h_{ip}

\displaystyle=-\dot{S}^{kl}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{ij}-h_{ij}h_{k}^{p}h_{pl}+h_{kl}h_{i}^{p}h_{jp})-\ddot{S}(\nabla_{i}h,\nabla_{j}h)+SA_{j}^{p}h_{ip}

\displaystyle=-\dot{S}^{kl}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{ij}-\ddot{S}(\nabla_{i}h,\nabla_{j}h)+(S-\dot{S}(h))h_{i}^{p}h_{jp}+h_{ij}\dot{S}(h^{2}),

where
\dot{S}(h)=\dot{S}^{kl}h_{kl},\dot{S}(h^{2})=\dot{S}^{ij}h_{j}^{p}h_{pi}
. Since
S=F^{-1}
,

\dot{S}^{kl}=-F^{-2}\dot{F}^{kl},\quad\ddot{S}^{kl,mn}=2F^{-3}\dot{F}^{mn}\dot{F}^{kl}-F^{-2}\ddot{F}^{kl,mn},

then (
3.12
) can be expressed

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}h_{ij}=

\displaystyle F^{-2}\dot{F}^{kl}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{ij}+F^{-2}\ddot{F}(\nabla_{i}h,\nabla_{j}h)-2F^{-3}\nabla_{i}F\nabla_{j}F

(3.13)

\displaystyle+2F^{-1}h_{i}^{p}h_{jp}-F^{-2}h_{ij}\dot{F}(h^{2}),

where we have used Euler’s identity and
\dot{F}^{kl}h_{kl}=F
.

From (
3.13
) we deduce

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{ij}=F^{-2}\dot{F}^{kl}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{ij}+F^{-2}\ddot{F}(\nabla_{i}h,\nabla_{j}h)-2F^{-3}\nabla_{i}F\nabla_{j}F-F^{-2}h_{ij}\dot{F}(h^{2}).

(3.14)

∎

4.
Gauss Map Parameterization

For a strictly convex spacelike hypersurface
M
, define its support function
u:{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

u(z)=\inf\{-\left<{z},{X}\right>:X\in M\}.

If
M
is co-compact, strictly convex,
u(z)
is bounded on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, and is written as:

u(z)=-\left<{z},{X(\nu^{-1}(z))}\right>,

where we regard
z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
as the position vector of the future branch of hyperboloid.
X(\nu^{-1}(z))
means the position vector of
M
whose unit future directed normal is exactly
z
.

Remark 4.1
.

For a cocompact hypersurface, by a translation along the
x_{n+1}
-direction, we can assume that the support function
u
is positive for all
z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
.

Under the flow (
1.1
), we will derive the evolution equation for
u=u(z,t)
. Differentiating
u
with respect to
t
, we have:

\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{t}}(z,t)=\left<{-\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{t}}\big|_{\nu^{-1}(z)}-X_{*}\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\nu^{-1}(z)\right)},{z}\right>=-S\big|_{\nu^{-1}(z)}.

(4.1)

Here
X_{*}
is the push-forward of
X
.

If we use the image of Gauss map to reparameterize the hypersurface
M
, then the position vector of
M
can be recovered by its support function
[
3
]
:

\bar{X}=uz-z_{*}(\overline{\nabla}u)\in\mathbb{R}^{n,1},

(4.2)

where
\overline{\nabla}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
with respect to the standard hyperbolic metric
\bar{g}
(the induced metric of hyperboloid from
\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
), and
z_{*}
is the push-forward of the map
z:{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
.
Moreover, the eigenvalues
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
of the following tensor

\tau_{ij}:=u\bar{g}_{ij}-\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{j}u

(4.3)

with respect to
\bar{g}
are exactly the principle radii of
M
(the reciprocal of
\kappa_{i}
), and it satisfies the Codazzi-type equality
\overline{\nabla}_{i}\tau_{jk}=\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{ik}
following from Ricci’s identity.

Since we assume that
M
is strictly convex, then
\tau_{ij}
is positive definite. Therefore, we can define the dual function
F_{*}
of
F
:

F_{*}(\tau,\bar{g}):=F(\bar{g},\tau)^{-1}=\left(\frac{s_{n}}{s_{n-k}}\right)^{1/k}(r).

Since
S(z,t)=F(h,g)^{-1}=F_{*}(\tau,\bar{g})
by using the Gauss map parameterization, the evolution equation of
u
can be written as:

\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{t}}(z,t)=-F_{*}(\tau)(z,t),

(4.4)

where we omitted
\bar{g}
in
F_{*}(\tau,\bar{g})
since
\bar{g}
does not change under the flow, and we may also omit
\tau
in the following context.

Denote
\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}=\frac{\partial{F_{*}}}{\partial{\tau_{kl}}}
. The evolution equation (
4.4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}u

\displaystyle=-F_{*}=-\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\tau_{kl}\text{ (since $\operatorname{degree}F_{*}=1$) }

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}u-u\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\bar{g}_{kl}\text{ (by the definition of $\tau_{kl}$)}.

Remark 4.2
.

Under the co-compactness assumption, the maximum and minimum of
\kappa_{i}
can be attained. In addition,
F_{*}
and
u
have positive upper and lower bound at every moment.

Proposition 4.3
.

The evolution for
F_{*}=F_{*}(\tau)
under ICF (
1.1
) is given by:

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}F_{*}=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}F_{*}-F_{*}\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}{\bar{g}}_{kl}
,

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}F_{*}^{-1}=F_{*}^{-1}\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\bar{g}_{kl}-2F_{*}\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}F_{*}^{-1}\overline{\nabla}_{l}F_{*}^{-1}+\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}F_{*}^{-1}
.

where
\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}:=\frac{\partial{F_{*}}}{\partial{\tau_{kl}}}
is the partial derivative of
F_{*}
(as a function with the matrix components
\tau_{ij}
as its variable).

Proof.

From (
4.5
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}F_{*}

\displaystyle=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\tau_{kl}

\displaystyle=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\left(u\bar{g}_{kl}-\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}u\right)

\displaystyle=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\left(\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{t}}\bar{g}_{kl}-\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{t}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}F_{*}-F_{*}\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\bar{g}_{kl}.

The second equation can be easily obtained by the first equation.
∎

5.1.
Short-Time Existence

Since the equation (
1.1
) is invariant for
G
action, the flow equation actually deforms the quotient manifold
M/G
. Thus we have:

Proposition 5.1
.

The ICF (
1.1
) admits a short-time solution
M_{t},t\in[0,T)
which is co-compact.

For details, see section 5 of
[
3
]
for reference.

5.2.
Estimates on Support Function

Let
u(z,t)
be the support function of a standard co-compact, strictly convex spacelike hypersurface
X:M\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
. Then
u
has positive lower and upper bound at initial time
t=0
. Therefore, we obtain the following estimate.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
be standard co-compact, then the support function
u(z,t)
satisfies:

e^{-t}\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(z,0)\leq u(z,t)\leq e^{-t}\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(z,0),\quad\forall(z,t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times[0,T).

(5.1)

Proof.

Let
w(t)=u_{+}e^{-t}
, where
u_{+}=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(z,0)
is a constant. Then
\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}w(t)=-w(t)
.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}(u-w)

\displaystyle=-F_{*}(\tau)+w

\displaystyle=-F_{*}(\tau)+F_{*}(w\bar{g})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}F_{*}((1-s)\tau+sw\bar{g})\text{d}s

\displaystyle=a^{kl}(-\tau_{kl}+w\bar{g}_{kl})

\displaystyle=a^{kl}\left(\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}(u-w)-\bar{g}_{kl}(u-w)\right),

where
a^{kl}=\int_{0}^{1}F_{*}^{kl}\big|_{(1-s)\tau+sw\bar{g}}\text{d}s
is symmetric and positive-definite. Since at the initial time
u(z,0)-w(0)\leq 0
, by the maximum principle of parabolic equations,

u(z,t)-w(t)\leq 0,\forall z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n},\forall t\in[0,T).

That is,
u(z,t)\leq u_{+}e^{-t}
. Similar, we can prove the lower bound of
u
.
∎

5.3.
Estimates on Speed Function

We establish the
C^{0}
-estimate on speed function
F_{*}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that the initial hypersurface
X_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
is standard co-compact, then
F_{*}(z,t)
satisfies:

\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}uF_{*}^{-1}(z,0)\leq uF_{*}^{-1}(z,t)\leq\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}uF_{*}^{-1}(z,0).

(5.2)

Therefore, we have two positive constants
C_{-},C_{+}
, such that

C_{-}e^{-t}\leq F_{*}(\tau)(z,t)\leq C_{+}e^{-t}.

(5.3)

Proof.

Let
w(z,t)=uF_{*}^{-1}
, by (
4.5
) and Proposition
4.3
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}w

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{t}}F_{*}^{-1}+u\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}F_{*}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}w-2F_{*}\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}F_{*}^{-1}\overline{\nabla}_{l}w.

Since
\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}
is positive definite, by maximum principle, we have
\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}w(z,0)\leq w(z,t)\leq\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}w(z,0)
. By co-compactness,
w(z,0)
has positive lower and upper bounds. Thus
C_{1}\leq w(z,t)\leq C_{2}
. Then by (
5.1
), we get (
5.3
).
∎

5.4.
Gradient Estimate

We establish an upper bound for
\left\lvert{\overline{\nabla}u}\right\rvert
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that
X:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
is spacelike, strictly convex, and standard co-compact, then its support function
u(z)
satisfies:

\left\lvert{\overline{\nabla}u}\right\rvert^{2}\leq u^{2}.

(5.4)

Proof.

Consider
\phi(z)=\left<{X},{X}\right>=\left\lvert{\overline{\nabla}u}\right\rvert^{2}-u^{2}
. Our goal is to prove
\phi\leq 0
. Due to the cocompactness,
\phi
achieves its maximum at some
y_{0}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. Choose an normal coordinate
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
around
y_{0}
. We have, at
y_{0}
:

0=\phi_{i}=2\left<{X_{i}},{X}\right>,\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,n.

By strict convexity of the hypersurface,
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are linearly independent, forming a basis for
T_{p}M
, where
p=\nu^{-1}(y_{0})\in M
. Then we conclude that
X
is parallel to its normal direction
\nu
at
y_{0}
. Since
X=\overline{\nabla}u-uz
, we have
\overline{\nabla}u(y_{0})=0
. Therefore,
\phi\leq\phi(y_{0})=-u^{2}(y_{0})\leq 0
.

∎

5.5.
Estimates on Principle Curvatures

We firstly derive the evolution equation for
\tau_{ij}
. We will denote
\dot{F}_{*}(\bar{g}):=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\bar{g}_{kl}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Under the ICF (
1.1
), there holds:

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\tau_{ij}=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}\tau_{ij}+\ddot{F}_{*}^{kl,mn}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\tau_{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{mn}-2F_{*}\bar{g}_{ij}+\dot{F}_{*}(\bar{g})\tau_{ij}.

(5.5)

Proof.

By definition,
\tau_{ij}=u\bar{g}_{ij}-\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{j}u
. Then from (
4.4
) we get:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\tau_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{t}}\bar{g}_{ij}-\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{t}}

\displaystyle=-F_{*}\bar{g}_{ij}+\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{j}F_{*}

\displaystyle=-F_{*}\bar{g}_{ij}+\overline{\nabla}_{i}\left(\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{kl}\right)

\displaystyle=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{kl}+\ddot{F}_{*}^{kl,mn}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\tau_{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{mn}-F_{*}\bar{g}_{ij}.

Set
\bar{R}(X,Y)Z=-\overline{\nabla}_{X}\overline{\nabla}_{Y}Z+\overline{\nabla}_{Y}\overline{\nabla}_{X}Z+\overline{\nabla}_{[X,Y]}Z
, and
\bar{R}_{ijkl}=\bar{g}(R(e_{i},e_{j})e_{k},e_{l})
,
\bar{R}_{ikj}^{\hskip 8.19447ptp}=\bar{R}_{ikjm}g^{mp}
. By Ricci’s identity, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{kl}

\displaystyle=\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\tau_{jl}

\displaystyle=\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\tau_{jl}+\bar{R}_{ikj}^{\hskip 8.19447ptp}\tau_{pl}+\bar{R}_{ikl}^{\hskip 8.19447ptp}\tau_{jp}

\displaystyle=\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}\tau_{ij}+\bar{g}_{kj}\tau_{il}-\bar{g}_{ij}\tau_{kl}+\bar{g}_{kl}\tau_{ij}-\bar{g}_{il}\tau_{kj},

Using the above equality and noting that
\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\tau_{kl}=F_{*}
, we have

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\tau_{ij}=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}\tau_{ij}+\ddot{F}_{*}^{kl,mn}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\tau_{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{mn}-2F_{*}\bar{g}_{ij}+\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\bar{g}_{kl}\tau_{ij}.

∎

To estimate the eigenvalues of
\tau
, we need the following tensor maximum principle (
[
18
]
, Theorem 9.1, or
[
2
]
, Theorem 3.1):

Lemma 5.6

(
[
18
]
, Theorem 9.1)
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold without boundary, equipped with a (may be time-dependent) Riemannian metric
g
, whose Levi-Civita connection is
\nabla
. Let
T_{ij}
be a smooth symmetric tensor field on
(M,g)
, which satisfies the evolution equation

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}T_{ij}=a^{kl}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}T_{ij}+u^{k}\nabla_{k}T_{ij}+N_{ij},

(5.6)

where
a^{kl}
and
u
are smooth,
a^{kl}
is positive-definite everywhere, and the residue part
N_{ij}
satisfies the null eigenvector condition: wherever
T_{ij}(p_{0},t_{0})\leq 0
(negative definite), for any null eigenvector
v^{j}
(
T_{ij}v^{j}=0
), there holds
N_{ij}v^{j}(p_{0},t_{0})\leq 0
. Then if at the initial time
t=0
,
T_{ij}(p,0)\leq 0,\forall p\in M
, then for any
t>0
, we have
T_{ij}(p,t)\leq 0,\forall p\in M
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

1\leq\dot{F}_{*}(\bar{g})\leq c(n,k)
is uniformly bounded. Moreover,
\dot{F}_{*}(\bar{g})=1
if and only if
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}
.

Proof.

Choose a local orthonormal frame such that
\bar{g}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
. Let
f_{*}(\overline{g})=(\frac{s_{n}}{s_{n-k}})^{1/k}
.
We only need to show that

1\leq\dot{f}_{*}(\bar{g})(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{f_{*}}}{\partial{\lambda_{i}}}\leq c(n,k),

where
c(n,k)>1
is a universal constant. Since
\dot{f}_{*}(\bar{g})
is of homogeneous degree
0
in
\lambda
,
\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\dot{f}_{*}=\max_{\lambda_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}^{2}=1}\dot{f}_{*}=c(n,k)
. Then we get
\dot{f}_{*}\leq c(n,k)
. To prove the lower bound, notice that
f_{*}(\lambda)
is concave and of homogeneous degree
1
, then

\displaystyle f_{*}(1)-f_{*}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq Df_{*}(\lambda)\cdot(1-\lambda)\quad\text{(concavity)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{f_{*}}}{\partial{\lambda_{i}}}(\lambda)\cdot 1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{f_{*}}}{\partial{\lambda_{i}}}(\lambda)\cdot\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=\dot{f}_{*}(\lambda)-f_{*}(\lambda)\quad\text{(Euler's identity for homogeneous function)}.

Then we get
1\leq\dot{f}_{*}(\lambda)
. By the strictly concavity of
f_{*}
we know that the equality occurs if and only if
\lambda
is a multiple of
(1,1\cdots,1)
.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

There exists a time-independent constant
C>0
(only depending on
X_{0}
), such that
\tau_{ij}
has the upper bound:

\tau_{ij}\leq Ce^{c(n,k)t}\bar{g}_{ij},

(5.7)

where
c(n,k)
is the uniform constant given in Lemma
5.7
.

Proof.

Let
T_{ij}=\tau_{ij}-Ce^{c(n,k)t}\bar{g}_{ij}
, where
C>0
is a sufficiently large constant only depending on
X_{0}
(independent of
t
), such that at
t=0
,
T_{ij}\leq 0
. By the evolution equation (
5.5
) and the concavity of
F_{*}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}T_{ij}

\displaystyle=\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}T_{ij}+\ddot{F}_{*}^{kl,mn}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\tau_{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{mn}-2F_{*}\bar{g}_{ij}+\dot{F}_{*}(\bar{g})\tau_{ij}-Cc(n,k)e^{c(n,k)t}

\displaystyle\leq\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}T_{ij}+c(n,k)T_{ij},

where we used the concavity:
\ddot{F}_{*}^{kl,mn}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\tau_{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{mn}\leq 0
, and
\dot{F}_{*}(\bar{g})\leq c(n,k)
. By Lemma
5.6
, we obtain
T_{ij}\leq 0,\forall t\in[0,T)
, i.e.
\tau_{ij}\leq Ce^{c(n,k)t}
.
∎

Proposition 5.9
.

There exists a time-independent constant
C>0
, such that
\tau_{ij}
has the lower bound:

\tau_{ij}\geq Ce^{-c_{1}(n,k)t}\bar{g}_{ij},

(5.8)

where
c_{1}(n,k)>c(n,k)

Proof.

Notice that

Ce^{-kt}\leq F_{*}(\tau)^{k}=\frac{s_{n}}{s_{n-k}}\leq c\frac{\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n-k}}\leq c\cdot\lambda_{1}^{k-1}\lambda_{n}.

By the previous proposition,
\lambda_{1}\leq Ce^{c(n,k)t}
, then we obtain
\lambda_{n}\geq Ce^{-(k+c(n,k)(k-1))t}
.
∎

By now we have obtained
C_{1}(T)\leq\tau_{ij}\leq C_{2}(T)
. Since the principle radii will not go to

or

in finite time, we conclude the long-time existence:

.

6.
Normalized Equation

In the next two sections, we work on the normalized hypersurface:
\tilde{X}(p,t):=e^{t}X(p,t)
. We consider the normalized flow:

\frac{\partial{\tilde{u}}}{\partial{t}}(y,t)=-F_{*}(\tilde{\tau})(y,t)+\tilde{u}(y,t),\quad\forall y\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n},t\in(0,\infty).

Here
\tilde{u}
is the support function of
\tilde{X}
and
\tilde{\tau}=\tilde{u}\overline{g}-\overline{\nabla}^{2}\tilde{u}

is the curvature radius tensor of
\tilde{X}
. To simplify the notations, we may drop the tildes and simply write the normalized equation as

\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{t}}(y,t)=-F_{*}(\tau)(y,t)+u(y,t),\quad\forall y\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n},t\in(0,\infty).

(6.1)

Let
G:M\to{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be the Gauss map of the strictly convex cocompact hypersurface
M
in the ambient Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
. Denote
\mathscr{L}=\partial_{t}-F_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}
with respect to the hyperbolic metric
\bar{g}
.
Let
Z:=\dot{F_{*}}(\bar{g})-1
, then we know that
0\leq Z\leq c(n,k)
, and
Z=0
if and only if
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=\cdots=\kappa_{n}
.

We first derive some evolution equations for the normalized flow.

Lemma 6.1
.

The normalized evolution equation for the support function
u
is

\mathscr{L}u=\partial_{t}u-F_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}u=-Zu.

Lemma 6.2
.

The normalized evolution equation for speed
F_{*}
is

\mathscr{L}F_{*}=\partial_{t}F_{*}-F_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}F_{*}=-F_{*}\left(\dot{F_{*}}(\bar{g})-1\right)=-ZF_{*}.

Lemma 6.3
.

The normalized evolution equation for curvature radii
\tau_{ij}
is given by

\mathscr{L}\tau_{ij}=\partial_{t}\tau_{ij}-F_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}\tau_{ij}=F_{*}^{kl,mn}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\tau_{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\tau_{mn}-2F_{*}\bar{g}_{ij}+\dot{F_{*}}(\bar{g})\tau_{ij}+\tau_{ij}.

The above equations can be easily verified by using
\tilde{u}=e^{t}u
,
\tilde{F_{*}}=e^{t}F_{*}
and
\tilde{\tau}_{ij}=e^{t}\tau_{ij}
. Moreover, by Proposition
5.2
, Proposition
5.3
, and Proposition
5.4
, we have:

Proposition 6.4
.

The normalized equation (
6.1
) satisfies

\begin{aligned} &C_{1}\leq u(y,t)\leq C_{2},\\
&C_{1}\leq F_{*}(y,t)\leq C_{2},\\
&\left\lvert{\overline{\nabla}u}\right\rvert^{2}(y,t)\leq u^{2}(y,t),\end{aligned}\quad\forall(y,t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times(0,T)

(6.2)

for some positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
independent of

.

6.1.
Estimates on Principal Curvature

Now we are going to bound
\tau_{ij}
from above. We will consider the test function

\varphi={u^{\alpha}}\cdot{\lambda_{1}},

where
\lambda_{1}
is the largest eigenvalue of
\tau
, and
\alpha>0
is a positive constant to be determined.

We will first extend the lemma 5 in
[

]
to the parabolic case:

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose that
h=h(t)
is a time-dependent symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor on a manifold
M
with a time-dependent metric
g(t)
. Let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection of
g(t)
. Suppose the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of
h
(with respect to the metric
g
) has multiplicity
m
at
(y_{0},t_{0})\in M\times\mathbb{R}
. Then for any smooth function
\varphi(y,t)
such that
\varphi\geq\lambda_{1}
in
M\times\mathbb{R}
, and
\varphi=\lambda_{1}
at
(y_{0},t_{0})
, we have, at
(y_{0},t_{0})
,
\forall i=1,\cdots,n
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \delta_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{i}\varphi=\nabla_{i}h_{\alpha\beta},\quad 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq m\\
\nabla_{i}\nabla_{i}\varphi\geq\nabla_{i}\nabla_{i}h_{11}+2\sum_{p>m}\frac{h_{1pi}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p}}\end{aligned}\right.

(6.3)

In addition, if
\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}=\Phi(y,t)h_{ij}
for some function
\Phi(y,t)
, then at
(y_{0},t_{0})
,

\delta_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial{\varphi}}{\partial{t}}=\nabla_{t}h_{\alpha\beta},\quad 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq m.

(6.4)

Here
\nabla_{t}
is the canonical spacetime connection defined by (
3.8
).

Proof.

The first assertion follows directly from the lemma 5 in
[
8
]
. We only need to prove (
6.4
). Let
(y^{1},\cdots,y^{n},t)
be a local coordinate of
M\times\mathbb{R}
around
(y_{0},t_{0})
such that
g
and
h
are diagonal at
(y_{0},t_{0})
, and
g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
at
(y_{0},t_{0})
. By switching the indices, we also assume that
\lambda_{1}=h_{11}=\cdots=h_{mm}
at
(y_{0},t_{0})
. Let
\gamma(t)=(y_{0},t),t\in(t_{0}-\epsilon,t_{0}+\epsilon)
be a curve passing through
(y_{0},t_{0})
, with
\dot{\gamma}(t)=\partial_{t}
. Let

v(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v^{i}(t)\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{y^{i}}}(y_{0},t)

be a spatial vector field along
\gamma(t)
, such that
v(t_{0})\in\text{span}(e_{1},\cdots,e_{m})
and
v^{\prime}(t_{0})\in\text{span}(e_{m+1},\cdots,e_{n})
. That is,

\displaystyle v^{k}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=0,\quad m<k\leq n,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{v^{\alpha}}}{\partial{t}}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=0,\quad 1\leq\alpha\leq m.

Since

t\mapsto h(v(t),v(t))-\varphi(\gamma(t))\cdot g(v(t),v(t))

attains its maximum at
t=t_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\bigg|_{t=t_{0}}\left[h(v(t),v(t))-\varphi(y_{0},t)\cdot g(v(t),v(t))\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{ij}\right)v^{i}v^{j}+2h_{ij}\frac{\partial{v^{i}}}{\partial{t}}v^{j}-\frac{\partial{\varphi}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}v^{i}v^{j}

\displaystyle-\varphi\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}\right)v^{i}v^{j}-2\varphi g_{ij}\frac{\partial{v^{i}}}{\partial{t}}v^{j}\bigg|_{t=t_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{ij}\right)v^{i}(t_{0})v^{j}(t_{0})-\frac{\partial{\varphi}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}v^{i}(t_{0})v^{j}(t_{0})-\varphi\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}\right)v^{i}(t_{0})v^{j}(t_{0}).

By assumption,
\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}=\Phi h_{ij}
is diagonal at
(y_{0},t_{0})
. Combined with the arbitrary choice of
v(t_{0})=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}v^{\alpha}(t_{0})\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{y^{\alpha}}}\in\text{span}(e_{1},\cdots,e_{m})
and the symmetry of
\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{ij}
,
g_{ij}
, and
\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}
, we conclude that

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{\partial{\varphi}}{\partial{t}}g_{\alpha\beta}-\varphi\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}g_{\alpha\beta}=0,\quad\forall 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq m.

Hence
\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{\alpha\beta}\right)
is diagonal at
(y_{0},t_{0})
. Moreover, for
\alpha=\beta
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}h_{\alpha\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{\alpha\alpha}-2h(\nabla_{t}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{y^{\alpha}}},\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{y^{\alpha}}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{\alpha\alpha}-2h(\frac{1}{2}g^{kj}\partial_{t}g_{\alpha j}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{y^{k}}},\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{y^{\alpha}}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{\alpha\alpha}-h_{\alpha\alpha}\partial_{t}g_{\alpha\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{\alpha\alpha}-\varphi\partial_{t}g_{\alpha\alpha}.

Therefore,
\frac{\partial{\varphi}}{\partial{t}}=\nabla_{t}h_{\alpha\alpha}
. In conclusion,
\delta_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial{\varphi}}{\partial{t}}=\nabla_{t}h_{\alpha\beta},1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq m
.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

There exists a large constant
\alpha>1
(depending only on
\sup F_{*}
) such that

u^{\alpha}\cdot\lambda_{1}\leq C,\quad\forall y\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n},t\geq 0,

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n,\alpha,\inf_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times\{0\}}u,\sup_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times\{0\}}u
, and
\sup_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times\{0\}}\left\lvert{\overline{\nabla}u}\right\rvert
.

Proof.

To simplify the calculation, take
\log
and turn to the test function

\phi=\alpha\log u+\log\lambda_{1}.

At the maximum point
(y_{0},t_{0})
of
\phi
in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\times[0,T]
, suppose that
t_{0}>0
. Choosing a normal coordinate around
y_{0}
, by Lemma
6.5
, we have at
(y_{0},t_{0})
,

0=\phi_{i}=\alpha\frac{u_{i}}{u}+\frac{(\lambda_{1})_{i}}{\lambda_{1}},

\displaystyle\phi_{ii}

\displaystyle=\alpha\frac{u_{ii}}{u}-\alpha\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}+\frac{(\lambda_{1})_{ii}}{\lambda_{1}}-\frac{(\lambda_{1})_{i}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\frac{u_{ii}}{u}-\alpha\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\left(\tau_{11ii}+2\sum_{p>m}\frac{\tau_{1pi}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p}}\right)-\frac{\tau_{11i}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{L}\phi\leq\frac{\mathscr{L}\tau_{11}}{\lambda_{1}}+\frac{\alpha\mathscr{L}u}{u}+\alpha\sum_{i}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}-2\sum_{i}\sum_{p>m}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}\tau_{1pi}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p})}+\sum_{i}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}\tau_{11i}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ddot{F_{*}}(\overline{\nabla}_{1}\tau,\overline{\nabla}_{1}\tau)}{\lambda_{1}}-\frac{2F_{*}}{\lambda_{1}}+(Z+2)-\alpha Z

\displaystyle+\alpha\sum_{i}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}-2\sum_{i}\sum_{p>m}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}\tau_{1pi}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p})}+\sum_{i}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}\tau_{11i}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}.

Since

\displaystyle\ddot{F_{*}}(\overline{\nabla}_{1}\tau,\overline{\nabla}_{1}\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}{F_{*}^{ii,jj}\tau_{ii1}\tau_{jj1}}-2\sum_{p>q}\frac{F_{*}^{pp}-F_{*}^{qq}}{\lambda_{q}-\lambda_{p}}\tau_{1pq}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j}{F_{*}^{ii,jj}\tau_{ii1}\tau_{jj1}}-2\sum_{p>1}\frac{F_{*}^{pp}-F_{*}^{11}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p}}\tau_{1p1}^{2},

and

-2\sum_{i}\sum_{p>m}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}\tau_{1pi}^{2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p})}\leq 2\sum_{p>m}\frac{F_{*}^{11}\tau_{1p1}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p}},

and
\tau_{1p1}=0
for
1<p\leq m
(by (
6.3

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-2\sum_{p>m}\frac{F_{*}^{pp}\tau_{11p}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p})}-\frac{2F_{*}}{\lambda_{1}}+(Z+2)-\alpha Z

\displaystyle+\alpha\sum_{i}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}+\sum_{i}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}\tau_{11i}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{p>m}\frac{F_{*}^{pp}\tau_{11p}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{p})}-\frac{2F_{*}}{\lambda_{1}}+(Z+2)-\alpha Z

\displaystyle+\alpha\sum_{i}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}+\frac{F_{*}^{11}\tau_{111}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}.

By the first order condition
0=\phi_{i}
, we have
\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}=\frac{\tau_{11i}^{2}}{\alpha^{2}\lambda_{1}^{2}}
, then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq(-1+\frac{1}{\alpha})\sum_{p>m}\frac{F_{*}^{pp}\tau_{11p}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}+(\alpha+\alpha^{2})\frac{F_{*}^{11}u_{1}^{2}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2F_{*}}{\lambda_{1}}+(Z+2)-\alpha Z.

Note that
F_{*}
has a uniform upper bound by Proposition
6.4
. Assume that
\lambda_{1}\geq 2\sup F_{*}
, then
\frac{F_{*}(\lambda)}{\lambda_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Since
g(\lambda):=\frac{F_{*}(\lambda)}{\lambda_{1}}
is a continuous function of
\lambda
with
g(1,1,\cdots,1)=1
, there exist an open neighborhood

of
(1,1,\cdots,1)
such that
g(\lambda)\geq\frac{3}{4}
in U. Then we conclude that
\{\lambda\in\Gamma_{n}|\lambda_{1}\geq 2\sup F_{*}\}\cap U=\emptyset
. By strict concavity of

,
Z=0
if and only if
\lambda
is proportional to
(1,1,\cdots,1)
. Since
Z
is of homogeneous degree

, we conclude that
Z\geq\delta
as
\lambda\in\{\lambda\in\Gamma_{n}|\lambda_{1}\geq 2\sup F_{*}\}
for some constant

, which only depends on
\sup F_{*}
. With the assumption

, we obtain

0\leq\alpha(\alpha+1)\frac{F_{*}^{2}|\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}}{\lambda_{1}u^{2}}-((\alpha-1)Z-2).

Take

, then

\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{C\alpha(1+\alpha)}{(\alpha-1)\delta-2}.

Therefore,

\lambda_{1}\leq\min\left\{2\sup F_{*},\frac{C\alpha(1+\alpha)}{(\alpha-1)\delta-2}\right\}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

In general, for a Banach space
X
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
C^{m}(I,X)
the space consisting of all
C^{m}
-maps
u
from the interval

to

with finite norm, where the norm of

is defined by

\|u\|_{C^{m}(I,X)}:=\sup_{t\in I;0\leq l\leq m}\|\partial_{t}^{l}u(t)\|_{X}.

(6.5)

In particular, for
X=C^{k}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)
,
I=[0,T]
and
u:({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
, the norm is given by

\|u\|_{C^{m}\left([0,T],C^{k}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\right)}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T];0\leq l\leq m}\|\partial_{t}^{l}u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{k}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)}.

(6.6)

Similarly, for
X=C^{k,\alpha}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)
(
0<\alpha<1
) and
u:({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
,

\|u\|_{C^{m}\left([0,T],C^{k,\alpha}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\right)}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T];0\leq l\leq m}\|\partial_{t}^{l}u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{k,\alpha}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)}.

(6.7)

Combining all the estimates above (Proposition
6.4
and Lemma
6.6
), by Evans-Krylov theory, for any

, the normalized support function

satisfies

\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2,\alpha}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)}\leq C,

where

is a uniform constant independent of

. Then by (

\|u\|_{C^{1}\left([0,T],C^{2,\alpha}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\right)}\leq C.

(6.8)

It follows a priori estimates for higher-order derivatives immediately.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
u(y,t)
satisfies the normalized flow (

) with

, then for any integer

\|u\|_{C^{1}([0,T],C^{k}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G))}\leq C,

where

, and independent of

.

7.
Convergence

In this section we will show the convergence of the normalized flow. By Lemma
6.1
, the normalized evolution equation is

\mathscr{L}u=-Zu,

where
Z=\dot{F_{*}}(\bar{g})-1\in[0,c(n,k))
,
\mathscr{L}=\partial_{t}-\dot{F}_{*}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\overline{\nabla}_{l}
. Since we already have the uniform estimate on the coefficients of
\mathscr{L}
, independent of
T
, the following strong maximum principle (
[
24
]
Theorem 2.7) holds:

Lemma 7.1

(Strong Maximum Principle)
.

If
w(x,t)
satisfies

\mathscr{L}w\leq c^{i}(x,t)\overline{\nabla}_{i}w,

Then the supremum of
w(x,t)
is attained on
M\times\{t=0\}
. Moreover, if
w(x,t)
attains its supremum in the interior of
M\times(0,T)
for some
T>0
, then
w
must be constant in
M\times(0,T)
.

We first show that the right hand side
-F_{*}+u
of the equation will converge to
0
as
t\to+\infty
, by using a similar idea of
[
21
]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
w=\log u-\log F_{*}
, then
w\to\text{const}
, locally uniformly as
t\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Choose a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
,

w_{i}=\frac{u_{i}}{u}-\frac{(F_{*})_{i}}{F_{*}},\qquad w_{t}=\frac{u_{t}}{t}-\frac{(F_{*})_{t}}{F_{*}},

w_{ii}=\frac{u_{ii}}{u}-\frac{(u_{i})^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{(F_{*})_{ii}}{F_{*}}+\frac{(F_{*})_{i}^{2}}{F_{*}^{2}},

\displaystyle\implies\mathscr{L}w

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathscr{L}u}{u}-\frac{\mathscr{L}{F_{*}}}{F_{*}}+\sum_{i}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}(F_{*})_{i}^{2}}{F_{*}^{2}}-\sum_{i}\frac{F_{*}^{ii}u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}

(7.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}F_{*}^{ii}\left(\frac{(F_{*})_{i}}{F_{*}}-\frac{u_{i}}{u}\right)\left(\frac{(F_{*})_{i}}{F_{*}}+\frac{u_{i}}{u}\right)

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i}c^{i}w_{i}.

Thus by the strong maximum principle,
w
attains its supremum and infimum for
({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\times[t_{0},+\infty)
only on the boundary
({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\times\{t=t_{0}\}
. Then we can further conclude that

osc(w)(t):=\max_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}w(z,t)-\min_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}w(z,t)

is strictly decreasing. This monotonicity leads to a convergence as
t\to+\infty
:

\lim_{t\to+\infty}osc(w)(t)=\epsilon\geq 0

If
\epsilon=0
, then the lemma has been proved. Assume not, that is,
\epsilon>0
. Fix
T>0
. For any sequence
\{t_{n}\}\to+\infty(n\to\infty)
, define a sequence of functions,

w_{n}(z,t):=w(z,t+t_{n}),\quad z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n},t\in(-t_{n},+\infty).

By Corollary
6.7
, we have

\left\lVert{w_{n}}\right\rVert_{C^{1}((-t_{n},T),C^{2,\alpha}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G))}\leq C
, where
C>0
is a uniform constant independent of
T
. By the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, there exists a subsequence of
\{t_{n}\}
, again denoted by
\{t_{n}\}
, and a limit function
w_{\infty}^{T}(z,t)
such that

w_{n}(z,t)\to w_{\infty}^{T}(z,t),\quad\text{in }C^{0}\left([-T,T],C^{2,\beta}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\right)

for some
0<\beta<\alpha
. By a standard Cantor’s diagonal argument, there is a subsequence of
\{t_{n}\}
, again denoted by
\{t_{n}\}
, and a limit function
w_{\infty}(z,t)
such that

w_{n}(z,t)\to w_{\infty}(z,t),\quad\text{in }C_{loc}^{0}\left((-\infty,+\infty),C^{2,\beta}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\right)

for some
0<\beta<\alpha
. Here,
w_{n}
converges to
w_{\infty}
in
C_{loc}^{0}\left((-\infty,+\infty),C^{2,\beta}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\right)
means that for any
M>0
, we have

w_{n}(z,t)\to w_{\infty}(z,t),\quad\text{in }C^{0}\left([-M,M],C^{2,\beta}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\right).

Then
w_{\infty}(z,t)
also satisfies equation (
7.1
). Notice that
osc(w_{n})(t)=osc(w)(t+t_{n})
, then

osc(w_{\infty})(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}osc(w)(t+t_{n})=\lim_{t\to+\infty}osc(w)(t)=\epsilon>0,\quad\forall t\in(-\infty,+\infty).

However, by the strong maximum principle,
osc(w_{\infty})(t)
should be strictly decresing until it reaches zero, which is a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
F_{*}
and
u
be the speed function and support function of the rescaled solution
\tilde{X}(p,t)=e^{t}X(p,t)
, then

-F_{*}+u\to 0,\quad\text{locally uniformly as }t\to+\infty.

Proof.

We first conclude that
uF_{*}^{-1}\to 1
. If not, by Lemma
7.2
,
w=\log u-\log F_{*}\to const
, suppose that
uF_{*}^{-1}\to c\neq 1
. Then
u_{t}=-F_{*}+u=F_{*}(uF_{*}^{-1}-1)\to F_{*}(c-1)
as
t\to+\infty
. Note that
0<C_{1}\leq F_{*}\leq C_{2}
, if
c>1
, then
\exists T_{1}>0
such that
u_{t}\geq\frac{C_{1}(c-1)}{2}>0
when
t>T_{1}
. This implies
u\to+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
, which contradicts with
u\leq C
. Similarly,
c<1
will leads to
u\to-\infty
, which is also a contradiction. Therefore,
uF_{*}^{-1}\to 1
. By the uniform bounds for
F_{*}
, we conclude that
-F_{*}+u=F_{*}(uF_{*}^{-1}-1)\to 0
.
∎

Now we can prove the convergence of the flow.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
:
We first show that the volume of the rescaled hypersurface converges. In fact, the normalized ICF is volume decreasing: the normalized volume element
\text{d}\tilde{\mu}_{t}=\sqrt{\det\tilde{g}}\text{d}x=e^{nt}\sqrt{\det g}\text{d}x
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\det\tilde{g}}}{\partial{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\left(e^{2nt}\det g\right)

\displaystyle=e^{2nt}\left(\frac{\partial{\det g}}{\partial{t}}+2n\det g\right)

\displaystyle=e^{2nt}\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}g_{ij}\cdot g^{ij}\cdot\det g+2n\det g\right)

\displaystyle=2e^{2nt}\det g\left(-\frac{H}{s_{k}^{1/k}}+n\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

where we used the Newton-Maclaurin inequality
s_{1}(\kappa)\geq s_{k}^{1/k}(\kappa)
. Especially,
\frac{\partial{\det\tilde{g}}}{\partial{t}}\equiv 0
when
S=s_{1}^{-1}
, i.e.
F(\kappa)=s_{1}(\kappa)
. Therefore, the volume of
M/G
converges to a constant
V^{\infty}\geq 0
(later we will see
V^{\infty}\neq 0
), uniquely determined by the initial co-compact hypersurface and the speed function
F(\kappa)
.

By Corollary
6.7
, the normalized support function
u(z,t)
satisfies that for any
k\geq 1
, there exist a positive constant
C_{k}>0
such that

\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{C^{1}\left((0,+\infty),C^{k}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)\right)}\leq C_{k}.

By Arzela-Ascoli theorem and a diagonal subsequence argument, for any
\{t_{n}\}\to+\infty
, there exist a subsequence
\{u(\cdot,t_{n_{k}})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges in
C^{\infty}
-topology to a limit function
u^{\infty}(\cdot)
. Since
u_{t}=-F_{*}+u\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
, any convergent subsequence of
u(\cdot,t)
will converge to a self-shrinker whose support function denoted by
u^{\infty}(\cdot)
. By the rigidity (Theorem
1.4
), we conclude that
u^{\infty}(\cdot)\equiv r^{\infty}
is a constant. Moreover,
r^{\infty}
and the corresponding limit spacelike hypersurface
M^{\infty}
should satisfy the volume relation:

0<\operatorname{vol}(M^{\infty}/G)=V^{\infty}\leq\operatorname{vol}(M_{0}/G).

(7.2)

Especially, the equality holds when
F(\kappa)=s_{1}(\kappa)
. Hence
u(\cdot,t)
converges in
C^{\infty}
-topology to the constant
r^{\infty}
which satisfies (
7.2
). ∎

8.
Application on Geometric Inequalities

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.7
. We define the isoperimetric ratio

\mathcal{I}_{k}(\overline{M})=\frac{\left\lvert{\overline{M}}\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\left(\int_{\overline{M}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)\text{d}\mu_{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k}}}

(8.1)

This ratio is scaling-invariant: if
\widetilde{M}
is defined by a scaling of the position vector of
M
, then
\mathcal{I}_{k}(\widetilde{M})=\mathcal{I}_{k}(M)
.

Our goal is to prove that for any standard co-compact convex spacelike hypersurface
M
, we have

\mathcal{I}_{k}(M/G)\geq\mathcal{I}_{k}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}/G)

(8.2)

where
G
is the Lorentz subgroup such that
M/G
is compact.

8.1.
Some Calculations

Let
S(h,g)=-F^{-1}(h,g)
. By proposition
3.1
, we have

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}\text{d}\mu_{g}=-S\sigma_{1}(\kappa)\text{d}\mu_{g},

(8.3)

\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{t}}h_{j}^{i}=-\nabla^{i}\nabla_{j}S+S(h^{2})_{j}^{i},

(8.4)

and for
1\leq m\leq n
