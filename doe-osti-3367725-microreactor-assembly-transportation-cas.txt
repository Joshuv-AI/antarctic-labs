 ABSTRACT
 Criticality safety analyses are completed on a transportation cask used for microreactor assembly
shipment to provide an example of model and analysis to industry for reproducing this type of study
on their microreactor fuel shipment. The fuel assembly considered is based on a gas-cooled
microreactor (GC-MR), which utilizes HALEU fuel in the form of TRISO particles and utilizes
various design options considered in industry designs. Various versions of this GC-MR assembly
were studied, with and without YH2 moderator, providing similar conclusions. The shipment cask
design is revised based on an existing ES-3100 design, developed by Y-12 for the transport of
highly enriched uranium (HEU), but is enlarged to hold the GC-MR fuel assembly. Criticality
safety analysis for the cask/GC-MR fuel assembly package was performed using the CSAS6
sequence of SCALE6.3.2, utilizing the ENDF/B-VII.1 based continuous energy neutron library,
and the analysis strictly follows the guideline from NRC reference reports. Different scenarios, e.g.
normal operation, undamaged cask with water flooded, damaged cask with optimal water
moderation, have been analyzed and it could be concluded the package would always have a large
margin of subcriticality even packed in an infinite array. Sensitivity and similarity analyses are also
performed using the TSUNAMI sequence of SCALE6.3.2, and the similarity analysis uses all the
experiments from the ICSBEP Handbook with Highly Enriched Uranium (HEU), Intermediate and
Mixed Enriched Uranium (IEU) and Low Enriched Uranium (LEU) systems, together with
additional ones that are sponsored by the DNCSH program, and selected IRPhEP experiments using
TRISO fuel and graphite moderator. These similarity analyses indicate that the dry nominal design
has no similar benchmark experiments (ck values greater than 0.8), which may become problematic
if more assemblies are shipped together (or a fully loaded core is shipped) and margin to criticality
is reduced. However, the cask models with flooded assemblies exhibited similarities to many
experiments with ck values greater than 0.8.

1. Introduction
 As part of a Department of Energy (DOE) initiative to increase the availability of High-Assay
Low Enriched Uranium (HALEU) fuel, the DOE/NRC Criticality Safety for Commercial-Scale
HALEU Fuel Cycle and Transportation (DNCSH) project aims to support the Nuclear Regulatory
Commission (NRC) in providing data for criticality safety validation of reactor designs. This report
focuses on criticality safety analyses on transportation packages for HALEU fueled microreactors.
 The advancement of microreactor technology has highlighted the need for specialized fuel
transportation packaging, as existing casks are not licensed to transport the wide range of fuel
packages considered in these advanced reactors. Most microreactors concepts rely on a form of
HALEU fuel, and some even consider transportation of fully loaded fresh reactor.
 This study provides an example of criticality safety analysis performed on a hypothetical
microreactor assembly transportation package that is developed and analyzed based on revised ES-
3100 [1] transportation cask and gas-cooled microreactors (GC-MR) assembly [2]. This model is
aimed for release to the DNCSH GitLab repository to support industry and streamline their
analyses.
 Criticality safety assessments were conducted using SCALE 6.3.2 [3] to analyze the criticality
margin for an enlarged ES-3100 cask with GC-MR assembly under various operational conditions.
Tools such as CSAS6 with KENO-VI are used for keff calculations, while TSUNAMI-3D/IP was
employed for sensitivity and similarity analyses, utilizing ENDF/B-VII.1 data library. The study
evaluated many configurations representing different operational states, including undamaged,
water-intruded, and damaged casks, noting that the goal of this report is not to do an extensive
safety evaluation, but to showcase the methodology on the most likely scenarios to be considered.
Parametric studies assessed design variations to provide conclusions that are applicable to a wider
range of microreactor concepts.

1.1 Review of Microreactor Technologies
 The field of microreactor development is rapidly evolving, with numerous innovative designs
emerging from leading players in the nuclear industry. These technologies are characterized by
their compactness, transportability, and scalability, utilizing a variety of fuel types and forms,
coolants, and moderators to address diverse operational needs. Microreactors are expected to play
a crucial role across a broad spectrum of applications – from delivering power to remote
communities to supplying reliable energy for military bases and serving various industrial
operations.

various development phases. Table 1-1 provides a summary description of some prominent

conceptual designs to pre-application phases. It is important to note that the list of microreactor
projects is not exhaustive, as the field is rapidly evolving with new initiatives continually emerging.
Nonetheless, the reactors highlighted reflect the industry's growing focus on advanced fuel types,
particularly the widespread interest in fuel options like TRISO. In parallel, innovative cooling

1.3 Review of Assembly Packages
 The deployment of microreactors necessitates the development and certification of packaging
systems capable of safely transporting microreactors utilizing HALEU fuel, both in fresh and
irradiated states. Robust transport solutions must comply with stringent regulations set by NRC and
the Department of Transportation (DOT). Since microreactor components are typically
manufactured offsite and shipped to operational locations, specialized packaging is crucial to
ensure safety during transit across various transportation modes. These measures are designed to
maintain subcriticality and prevent accidental criticality under both normal and extreme conditions
(such as package damage and water flooding).
 Due to the higher enrichment levels of HALEU compared to conventional reactor fuel, criticality
safety analysis is essential for certifying transport packages. This analysis relies on computational
modeling and simulation to ensure subcriticality with sufficient uncertainty margin. With
enrichment levels reaching up to 20%, HALEU fuel presents elevated criticality risks, necessitating
rigorous evaluation. NRC regulations require thorough assessments of fissile material
configurations, orientations, and potential moderators to guarantee subcriticality is maintained.
 Table 1-4 provides a sub-selection of various NRC-transportation casks that were found to be
most relevant for microreactors. Although the NRC has approved various packaging solutions for
different nuclear materials, existing packages differ in size and payload capacity, covering materials
such as TRIGA fuel elements, unirradiated uranium-zirconium fuel, spent nuclear fuel, plutonium
oxide and metal, PWR uranium dioxide fuel, irradiated HTGR fuel, low-enriched uranium oxide,
mixed oxide fuel, uranium hexafluoride (UF6), and transuranic waste. However, no current package
is certified to transport microreactors with HALEU, highlighting the need to develop tailored
packaging solutions.
 This study considers a modified design of the ES-3100 cask, initially developed for transporting
highly enriched uranium (HEU). The ES-3100 is a 30-gallon drum-style package featuring a
containment vessel and confinement assembly. Its structural integrity is reinforced by steel
boundaries and solid fill materials. However, the standard ES-3100 is too small to accommodate
the GC-MR fuel assembly, which required an enlarged version. The modified cask increases the
inner radius of the confinement vessel to approximately 12.8 cm and extends its length to about
200 cm. The outer containment vessel measures 30.3 cm in radius and 240 cm in length.

2. Model Description
 In this analysis, the major calculation tool is SCALE6.3.2 [3]. The Criticality Safety Analysis
Sequences (CSAS6) with KENO-VI, were utilized for keff calculations. The SCALE sensitivity and
uncertainty analysis sequences, known as the Tools for Sensitivity and Uncertainty Analysis
Methodology Implementation (TSUNAMI), are used for sensitivity and similarity analysis.
TSUNAMI-3D with KENO-VI was utilized to calculate the sensitivity data files (SDFs), while
TSUNAMI-IP (TSUNAMI Indices and Parameters) was used with the generated SDFs for
similarity analysis. For both CSAS6 and TSUNAMI-3D sequences, the ENDF/B-VII.1 based
continuous energy neutron library is used for KENO-VI calculations.

2.1 GC-MR Fuel Assembly Model
 The GC-MR model was developed at ANL as a modeling experiment that gathers several
expected modeling challenges encountered by the U.S. industry. The GC-MR assembly uses a
graphite structure, TRISO fuel blocks with U-235 enriched by 19.95% at HALEU fuel, YH₂
moderator pins with FeCrAl envelope, and upper/lower BeO reflector regions. Additional modeling
specificities were implemented in the GC-MR such as burnable poison blocks, helium coolant
channels and central control and shutdown rod. ANL had already developed the GC-MR assembly
neutronic models using Monte Carlo code Serpent [7]. Because the ANL staff working on this
project are new users of CSAS6/KENO-VI, it was desired to first build a single GC-MR fuel
assembly model using CSAS6 and verify the newly built CSAS6 model by using the Serpent Monte
Carlo result before building the cask/GC-MR package model.
 The GC-MR fuel assembly, selected for this analysis, has a total length of 200 cm and active
length of 160 cm. It contains ~ 2.04 kg of U-235 and ~ 8.31 kg of U-238. The fuel blocks are
formed by TRISO fuel particles, with 40% packing fraction, dispersed in graphite matrix. The

2.2 Cask Model
 The shipment cask design is revised based on the existing ES-3100 [1] design, developed by Y-
12 for the transport of HEU. The fuel element specification as discussed in previous section is
shown in reference [8]. The ES-3100 is a 30-gal drum-type package, with a containment vessel and
confinement assembly to protect fissile material during transport and to maintain structural integrity
[9]. The package largely consists of the steel used to form these boundaries and a solid filling
material to prevent damage from physical deformation as well as to act as insulation from thermal
stress.
 The original ES-3100 cask is too small for the GC-MR fuel assembly, and an enlarged version
was introduced increasing the radius and length of the confinement (inner) assembly, and with the
distance between the confinement assembly and containment (outer) vessel roughly preserved. The
radial configurations of the original ES-3100 and the enlarged cask are shown in Figure 2-2. For
the enlarged cask, the (inner) radius and (inner) axial length of the confinement vessel is ~12.8 cm
and ~200 cm respectively, while those for the confinement vessel are ~30.3 cm and ~240 cm
respectively.
 Between the inner (confinement) and outer (containment) vessels, Kaolite 1600™ (a mixture of
Portland cement, water, and vermiculite) is the nominal filling material of the ES-3100. Water was
modeled to consider water intrusion. The material definition of Kaolite was determined from a
Thermal Ceramics specification sheet included in the first revision of the Safety Analysis Report
for Packaging (SARP) [1] [10], and the Kaolite density in this study is 0.625 g/cm3. All the
compositions are at room temperature (293 K).

be optimally moderated with the fissile content in its most reactive credible configuration [11].
 This water-reflected, optimally moderated containment vessel analysis should be compared with
one where the water reflector is replaced by the package material (including water flooding in
voids) that surrounds the containment system [11]. Demonstrating that these two single, undamaged

 Following these guidelines, the values of keff for single package are calculated and shown in

 Criticality Safety Index (CSI) [12] is a dimensionless number (rounded up to the next tenth)
assigned to and placed on the label of a fissile material package, to designate the degree of control
of accumulation of packages, overpacks or freight containers containing fissile material during
transportation.
 For a shipment of multiple packages containing fissile material, the sum of the CSIs must be
less than or equal to 50 for shipment on a nonexclusive use conveyance and less than or equal to
100 for shipment on an exclusive use conveyance.
 A fissile material package must be controlled by either the shipper or the carrier during transport
to assure that an array of such packages remains subcritical. To enable this control, the designer of
a fissile material package shall derive a number "N" based on all the following conditions being
satisfied, assuming packages are stacked together in any arrangement and with close full reflection
on all sides of the stack by water:
 (1) Five times "N" undamaged packages with nothing between the packages would be
 subcritical,
 (2) Two times "N" damaged packages, if each package were subjected to the tests specified
 in § 71.73 ("Hypothetical accident conditions") [12] would be subcritical with optimum
 interspersed hydrogenous moderation,
 (3) The value of "N" cannot be less than 0.5.
 The CSI must be determined by dividing the number 50 by the value of "N" derived using the
procedures specified above. The value of the CSI may be zero provided that an unlimited number
of packages are subcritical, such that the value of "N" is effectively equal to infinity [12].
 For the current design of cask holding GC-MR fuel assembly, the value of "N" is infinite because
the system is subcritical with a comfortable margin even using infinite array of packages, for either
undamaged or damaged cask, for all the scenarios analyzed. Therefore, for this design the CSI could
be deemed as zero.

3.2 Sensitivity and Similarity Analysis
 The objective of the similarity analysis is to identify validation gaps and quantify uncertainties
and biases in nuclear data and methods by comparing applications to similar benchmark critical
experiments which have been analyzed, reviewed, and validated by nuclear criticality safety experts
[13]. A similarity index, named ck, is calculated by correlating calculated keff sensitivities for a
given application and a reference benchmark, yielding a value less than or equal to one. If two
compared systems are very similar, ck > 0.9, then the application is assumed to behave like the
benchmark and have similar uncertainties and biases, helping analysts and regulators to assess the
safely operation of the application. The sensitivity calculation is a required preliminary step to

                                                     23                                   ANL/NSE-25/9 Rev 3

generate SDFs which would be used to compute similarity coefficients in the following
calculations.

3.2.1 Sensitivity analysis

 TSUNAMI calculations were performed for four configurations, including the most reactive

3.2.2 Similarity analysis
 With generated SDFs, the applications were examined against a swath of ICSBEP critical
experiments [6] using TSUNAMI-IP. The four generated sensitivity profiles, in conjunction with
the ENDF/B-VII.1 56-group covariance library included in SCALE 6.3.2, were used in TSUNAMI-
IP to produce estimated nuclear data–induced uncertainties [9]. How similar the shared data-
induced uncertainties between the applications and experiments are then determined with a
correlation coefficient called similarity index, ck. The value ck is one of several integral similarity
indices considered valuable for validation. The use of ck, calculated by TSUNAMI-IP, provides a
rigorous quantitative approach to selecting experiments beyond the more qualitative, yet informed,
approach completed in Section 1.2 where system characteristics such as spectrum, fissile form, and
enrichment, among other physical features, are used to determine suitable experiments. The use of
ck as a selection criterion has supported the selection of applicable experiments for validation by
choosing those with a ck greater than or equal to 0.9, indicating a high degree of similarity [13].
Although not ideal, a ck greater than or equal to 0.8, considered marginally similar, may also be
used to demonstrate marginal applicability where high-similarity experiments do not exist [13].
 The neutron spectrum plays an important role in determining the similarity between two
systems. The neutron spectra of the four GCMR assembly configurations and PWR are compared
in Figure 3-10, with neutron flux tallied using the SCALE 252-energy-group structure. Compared
to a PWR neutron spectrum, the GCMR dry scenario has a very hard neutron spectrum, while the
two undamaged cask flooded configurations have over-moderated neutron spectra. The impact of
neutron spectra on similarity analysis will be discussed in later part of this section.

4. Results for Varied GC-MR Design Parameters
 A parametric study was conducted to assess the impact of varying GC-MR design parameters
on keff and extend the criticality safety analysis performed above to a wider range of microreactor
concepts. This involved CSAS6/KENO-VI estimations with different design parameters applied to
the GC-MR assembly model and the infinite array of dry casks, as detailed in Section 4.1.
Additionally, sensitivity and similarity analyses were performed for both dry and damaged casks,
considering a graphite moderator in place of YH₂ in the GC-MR assembly, as described in Section
4.2. TSUNAMI-3D simulations were used to evaluate sensitivity coefficients and TSUNAMI-IP
calculations were conducted to perform similarity analyses, comparing the dry and damaged casks
to ICSBEP benchmark data.

4.1 Criticality Safety Eigenvalue Calculations with CSAS6
4.1.1 Parametric study for the assembly model

 Table 4-1 compares the keff for the GC-MR fuel assembly model under various conditions:
removal of the B₄C poison material, replacing the yttrium dihydride (YH₂) moderator with a
graphite moderator, and reducing the enrichment to 15 at%. These cases are compared to the
reference design previously discussed in Section 2.1. Additionally, the results are analyzed for two
modeling approaches:
 • A simplified CSAS6 model where all TRISO fuel particles are smeared with the graphite
 matrix inside the fuel. Also, the absorber particles are smeared with the graphite matrix
 inside burnable poison blocks.
 • A CSAS6 model where the fuel and absorber particles are arranged in a cubic array.
 These two cases were previously demonstrated in Figure 2-1.
 It was observed that removing the B₄C poison material from the GC-MR assembly increases
keff by approximately 12,525 pcm in the heterogeneous TRISO model. This effect is slightly
underestimated in the homogenous model where 12,465 pcm change in keff was noticed. Replacing
the YH₂ moderator pins with graphite moderator pins reduced keff by 2,821 pcm in the
heterogeneous model and 3,293 pcm in the homogeneous model. Notably, YH₂ provided better
moderation than graphite in the GC-MR system. While reducing the enrichment of HALEU TRISO
fuel blocks from 19.95% to 15% led to a keff decrease of 4,387 pcm in the heterogeneous model.
The homogeneous model estimated a larger decrease of 4,990 pcm, further demonstrating the
tendency of the homogeneous model to underestimate keff.
  Key findings from this comparison highlight that the homogeneous model consistently
underpredicted keff, underscoring the need for a heterogeneous model to achieve more accurate

4.1.2 Parametric study for an infinite array of dry casks
 An analysis was also conducted for an infinite array of dry casks. The study explored various
design parameters of the GC-MR. Similar to the previous analysis, the packages were compactly
arranged in a hexagonal array, with the hexagonal surfaces aligned against the containment vessel’s
outer boundary. To simulate the infinite configuration, the model used a single package with
reflective boundary conditions. The radial geometry features a hexagonal domain with reflective
boundaries, while the axial symmetry of the GC-MR fuel assembly allows modeling only half of
the cask, and reflective boundary conditions were applied at the top and bottom boundaries.
  Figure 4-1 shows the 3D and radial configurations, representing the infinite hexagonal package
model used in the analysis. A detailed description of the cask model was provided in Section 2.2.
 Table 4-2 presents a parametric study of various GC-MR designs within an infinite array of dry
casks. The reference case represents the dry cask scenario under normal conditions (without water
intrusion), resulting in a keff of 0.57743. Reducing the packing fraction (PF) of TRISO particles
from 40% to 30%, while maintaining the reference enrichment (U-235 enriched at 19.95% HALEU

4.1.3 Optimal water moderation in damaged cask with graphite-moderated GC-MR
 The primary objective of this section is to identify the optimal water moderator for the damaged
cask containing the graphite-moderated GC-MR assembly. The optimal water moderation might be
different from the nominal GC-MR design. This is achieved by estimating the keff of the damaged
cask under confinement water intrusion, evaluating a range of water densities. Figure 4-2 illustrates
the process of determining the optimal water density.
 The results show that the highest reactivity occurs at 60% water density. This contrasts with the
damaged reference cask, where the YH₂-moderated GC-MR assembly exhibits peak reactivity at
50% water density. The difference arises from the better moderation of YH₂ compared to graphite.
In the damaged cask with GC-MR YH₂-moderated assembly, over-moderation occurs beyond 50%
water density, while graphite requires higher water density beyond 60% for the system to become
over-moderated.

4.2.1 Sensitivity analysis
 This section presents the results of the sensitivity analysis for infinite arrays of both dry and
damaged casks, each cask containing a GC-MR assembly moderated by graphite rather than YH₂.
The analysis examines the impact of this alternative moderator on neutron behavior and system
reactivity under different cask conditions. For the dry cask scenario, Table 4-3 highlights the
sensitivity coefficients for total cross section, for selected isotopes with the highest values. The
analysis revealed that U-235, Fe-56, and graphite exhibited significant sensitivity coefficients.
 In the thermal energy region, U-235 and graphite demonstrated positive sensitivity, while Fe-56
showed negative sensitivity. Notably, spikes in the sensitivity of graphite were observed in the
epithermal energy range. These spikes correlate with U-238 resonances within this energy range,
where increased sensitivity of graphite's elastic scattering occurs as it impacts the resonance escape
probability of U-238.

5. Conclusions
 Criticality safety analyses were completed on a transportation cask representative of a
microreactor assembly shipment, to provide an example model and analysis for reproducing this
type of study on similar microreactor fuel shipment. The fuel assembly considered in this study is
based on a GC-MR model developed by ANL under the NEAMS program, which utilizes HALEU
fuel in the form of TRISO particles and utilizes various design options considered in some of the
proposed designs that are either actively being developed or at various stages of certification. In
order to represent a wide range of applications, various versions of this GC-MR assembly were
studied, with and without YH2 moderator, providing similar conclusions. The shipment cask design
was revised based on an existing design ES-3100, developed by Y-12 for the transport of HEU, but
is enlarged to hold the GC-MR fuel assembly.
 This work was performed to illustrate modeling workflow that would need to be reproduced by
industry for criticality safety on microreactor fuel packages. Criticality safety analysis for the GC-
MR fuel assembly package was performed using the CSAS6 sequence of SCALE6.3.2, utilizing
the ENDF/B-VII.1 based continuous energy neutron library. Initial code-to-code comparison was
completed with Serpent to verify developed SCALE model. The criticality-safety analysis strictly
follows the guideline from NRC reference reports. Different scenarios, e.g. normal operation,
undamaged cask with water flooded, damaged cask with optimal water moderation, have been
analyzed and it could be concluded the package would always have large margin of subcriticality
even packed in infinite array. Additional scenarios may need to be considered by industry for their
package design, for instance considering different compaction or preferential flooding scenarios.
 Sensitivity and similarity analyses were also performed using the TSUNAMI sequence of
SCALE6.3.2, and the similarity analysis uses all the experiments from ICSBEP Handbook with
high, intermediate and mixed, and low enriched uranium systems, together with the THETA and
Deimos experiment models developed under the DNCSH program, and some selected IRPhEP
experiments using TRISO particles fuel and graphite moderator.
 These similarity analyses against ICSBEP Handbook experiments indicate that normal
conditions of transport (dry cases) have no similar benchmark experiments (ck values greater than
0.8) for the GCMR assembly nominal design, which may become problematic if more assemblies
are shipped together (or a fully loaded core is shipped) and margin to criticality is reduced. Similar
criticality safety analyses have been performed on full-core microreactor transportation casks in
Ref. [18]. The reason for this gap is a lack of benchmark experiments with SDFs in the ICSBEP
that are using similar TRISO-fueled (HALEU) graphite moderated technology, and it is also due to
the under-moderated neutron spectrum of GCMR design (under the dry scenario): the current
ICSBEP doesn’t have experiments with similar neutron spectra and material selection. However,
hypothetical accident conditions with flooded assemblies showed many similar experiments with
ck values greater than 0.8, or even greater than 0.9. Those similar experiments are found among the
abundant water-moderated experiments in ICSBEP handbooks, many of which have similar

                                                     50                                   ANL/NSE-25/9 Rev 3

neutron spectra to this hypothetical GCMR assembly cask under accident condition. The recently
developed THETA experiments were found marginally similar (ck>0.8) to a hypothetical accident
condition with flooded GCMR assembly configurations. None of the TRISO-fueled graphite
moderated experiments included in this study are similar (ck > 0.8) to the normal conditions of
transport of a GCMR assembly.
 Overall, more critical benchmarks may be needed to validate the assembly transportation model
to justify the reduced upper subcritical limits (USLs) that are imposed due to a lack of validations,
in case the subcriticality of the system is not sufficient
