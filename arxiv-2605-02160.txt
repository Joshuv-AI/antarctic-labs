Continuity of Lyapunov Exponent for Quasi-Periodic Gevrey Cocycles
Xueyin Wang

Abstract
. It is shown that for the quasi-periodic cocycles in Gevrey space G s G^{s} and subexponential Brjuno class frequency Ω ⁡ ( η ) \Omega(\eta) , the Lyapunov exponent is continuous provided that 1 < s + η < 2 1<s+\eta<2 .



1.
Introduction and results

In this paper, we study the continuity of the Lyapunov exponent for one-frequency quasi-periodic
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
cocycles

(\alpha,A):\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad(\theta,x)\mapsto(\theta+\alpha,A(\theta)x),

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
and
A(\cdot):\mathbb{T}\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. An important example is Schrödinger cocycle, where
A(\theta)=A_{E}^{V}(\theta)\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}E-V(\theta)&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
.

For
N\geqslant 1
, we write

A_{N}(\theta)=A(\theta+(N-1)\alpha)\cdots A(\theta+\alpha)A(\theta),

and define the finite-scale Lyapunov exponent by

L_{N}(\alpha,A)=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\ln\|A_{N}(\theta)\|\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

By subadditivity, the Lyapunov exponent

L(\alpha,A)=\lim_{N\to\infty}L_{N}(\alpha,A)=\inf_{N\geqslant 1}L_{N}(\alpha,A)

exists.

The regularity of the Lyapunov exponent is a central problem in dynamical systems and spectral theory. It is well-known that the regularity depends delicately on both the arithmetic properties of the frequency and the regularity of the cocycle
[
12
]
. We focus on the cocycles in Gevrey class. For
\rho>0
and
s>1
,
let
G^{s}_{\rho}(\mathbb{T},*)
be the space of
*
-valued functions

f(\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{f}_{k}e^{2\pi ik\theta}\quad\text{with}\quad\|f\|_{s,\rho}\coloneq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{f}_{k}|e^{\rho|2\pi k|^{\frac{1}{s}}}<\infty.

We now introduce the frequency class used in this paper. Let
p_{n}/q_{n}
be the continued fraction approximants of
\alpha
.
For
0<\eta<1
, we say that
\alpha\in\Omega(\eta)
if there exists
C_{\alpha}>0
such that for all
n
,

\ln q_{n+1}\leqslant C_{\alpha}q_{n}^{\eta}.

(1.1)

We call
\Omega(\eta)
a subexponential Brjuno class. This condition is weaker than any Diophantine condition, but it is stronger than the usual Brjuno condition.

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\rho>0
,
1<s<2
,
0<\eta<2-s
and
\alpha\in\Omega(\eta)
. Then the map

A\mapsto L(\alpha,A)

is continuous in
G^{s}_{\rho}(\mathbb{T},\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}))
.

Let us compare our result with some known results. In the analytic setting,
[
1
]
proved the continuity of the Lyapunov exponent for one-frequency quasi-periodic Schrödinger cocycles for arbitrary irrational frequencies. This result was later extended to general
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
cocycles in
[
6
]
. In contrast, in the non-analytic setting, a surprising result due to
[
11
]
shows that the Lyapunov exponent may be discontinuous in the
C^{\infty}
topology. This dichotomy naturally motivates the study of the regularity of the Lyapunov exponent for cocycles in Gevrey classes
[
7
,
8
,
2
,
10
,
3
]
, which provide a natural intermediate regularity between the analytic and
C^{\infty}
topologies.

The regularity index
s
of the Gevrey class
G^{s}
plays a key role in the study of the Lyapunov exponent. For
1<s<2
,
[
7
]
first proved that the Lyapunov exponent is continuous for Schrödinger cocycles with strong Diophantine (
SDC
) frequencies. Here,
\alpha
is said to be
SDC
if there exist
C_{\alpha}>0
and
\tau>1
such that

q_{n+1}\leqslant C_{\alpha}q_{n}(\ln q_{n})^{\tau}.

Later,
[
2
]
obtained corresponding continuity results for
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
cocycles and Diophantine (
DC
) frequencies, which satisfy

q_{n+1}\leqslant C_{\alpha}q_{n}^{\tau}

for some
C_{\alpha}>0
and
\tau>1
. Since
SDC\subseteq DC\subseteq\Omega(\eta)
for any
\eta>0
, our Theorem
1.1
covers all frequencies considered in
[
7
,
2
]
when
1<s<2
.

In contrast, one cannot expect continuity for Gevrey cocycles when
s>2
. The first counterexample was constructed in
[
4
]
. More precisely, they proved that for any bounded type frequency, there exists a Schrödinger cocycle whose corresponding Lyapunov exponent is discontinuous. Here,
\alpha
is called bounded type if there is
C_{\alpha}>0
such that

q_{n+1}\leqslant C_{\alpha}q_{n}.

Recently,
[
9
]
further constructed discontinuity examples for general
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
cocycles covering all
\alpha\in SDC
. Since the
SDC
class has full Lebesgue measure, the result in
[
9
]
reveals that
s=2
is a critical transition point of continuity for almost all frequencies.

For the Schrödinger cocycle, we denote
L(\alpha,E)\coloneq L(\alpha,A_{E}^{V})
. Then we have the following corollary.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
\rho>0
,
1<s<2
,
0<\eta<2-s
,
\alpha\in\Omega(\eta)
, and
V\in G^{s}_{\rho}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})
. Then
L(\alpha,E)
is continuous with respect to
E
.

Both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
imply the competition between the smoothness of the cocycle and the arithmetic of the frequency. A similar phenomenon was also observed by
[
9
]
, where the authors demonstrated that lower smoothness combined with rapid denominator growth can result in discontinuity (see Theorems 1.3–1.5 in
[
9
]
).
Our Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
quantitatively capture this competition, implying that the larger
s
is (i.e., the lower the smoothness), the smaller
\eta
must be (i.e., the slower the denominator growth) to ensure the continuity of the Lyapunov exponent.

Finally, we briefly describe the idea of the proof. The starting point is the large deviation theorem for Gevrey cocycles, which is available in a scale window
C_{1}q_{s}^{\sigma}<N<C_{2}q_{s}^{\sigma_{1}}
. In the Diophantine case, the continued fraction denominators grow at most polynomially. To connect these scale windows, the multi-scale induction developed in
[
2
]
relies on choosing consecutive scales with a subexponential growth ratio, specifically
N_{s+1}/N_{s}>\exp(cq_{s}^{c})
(see
[
2
]
*Eq. (8.30)). This rapid growth subsequently yields a subexponentially decaying error bound between the Lyapunov exponent and its finite-scale approximation. However, for
\alpha\in\Omega(\eta)
, such subexponential estimates are invalid because the gaps between consecutive denominators can be significantly larger.

The novelty of our approach lies in a refined multi-scale induction scheme that successfully bridges these larger gaps. Our key observation is that one can delicately choose the parameters in the Gevrey large deviation theorem so that the Brjuno condition (
1.1
) still allows the scale windows to be connected. Instead of forcing a subexponential bound, we establish a polynomial growth bound for the successive scale ratios:
N_{s+1}/N_{s}>q_{s}^{c}
. Consequently, as demonstrated in Theorem
4.1
, this slower growth leads to an approximation error with polynomial decay, rather than subexponential decay. Nevertheless, this polynomial error remains sufficient to establish the desired continuity. This is because our final argument ultimately only requires that
L(\alpha,A)
be approximated by a fixed finite-scale expression,
2L_{2N_{0}}(\alpha,A)-L_{N_{0}}(\alpha,A)
, with an error tending to zero.

2.
Large deviation theorem

Let us recall the following large deviation theorem. For simplicity, we suppress the explicit dependence on
\alpha
in the
L_{N}(\alpha,A)
and
L(\alpha,A)
since
\alpha\in\Omega(\eta)
is fixed. In the notation of
[
2
]
, our
G^{s}_{\rho}
corresponds to
G^{\nu}_{\rho}
with
\nu=1/s\in(1/2,1)
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\rho>0
,
1<s<2
,
0<\kappa<1
. Assume
A\in G^{s}_{\rho}(\mathbb{T},\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}))
, and

\bigg|\alpha-\frac{a}{q}\bigg|<\frac{1}{q^{2}},\quad\gcd(a,q)=1.

Then there exist
c,C_{i}(\kappa)>0,i=1,2
,
\sigma_{1}>\sigma>1>\gamma>0
and
q_{0}(\kappa,\rho,s,\|A\|_{s,\rho})>0
such that for
q\geqslant q_{0}
and
C_{1}(\kappa)q^{\sigma}<N<C_{2}(\kappa)q^{\sigma_{1}}
,

\operatorname{meas}\bigg\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}:\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\ln\|A_{N}(\theta)\|-L_{N}(A)\bigg|>\kappa\bigg\}<e^{-cq^{\gamma}}.

Remark 2.2
.

The constants are uniform in a neighborhood of
A
in
G^{s}_{\rho}
.

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
2
]
*Sect 8.1.3, the parameters
\sigma_{1},\sigma,\gamma
in Theorem
2.1
are chosen as follows: Let

\delta\in(s-1,1).

(2.1)

Let
\sigma>1
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that

1<\sigma<\frac{1}{\delta}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\delta\sigma}{\sigma-1}<p<\frac{1}{\sigma-1}.

(2.2)

Define

\gamma=1+p(1-\sigma)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{1}=\frac{p(\sigma-1)}{\delta}.

(2.3)

The parameter choices detailed in Remark
2.3
are designed for the Avalanche Principle in the Diophantine setting. Relaxing this frequency condition to the subexponential Brjuno class (
1.1
) requires confining the parameters to a strictly tighter regime. More precisely, we replace (
2.1
) with (because
s+\eta<2
)

\delta\in(s-1,1-\eta).

Then
\sigma,p,\gamma,\sigma_{1}
are chosen as in (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
\delta\in(s-1,1-\eta)
, there exist parameters
\sigma,p,\gamma,
and
\sigma_{1}
satisfying (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and

\eta\sigma_{1}<\gamma\sigma.

(2.4)

Proof.

For
\sigma>1
, we choose the integer

p=\bigg\lfloor\frac{\delta\sigma}{\sigma-1}\bigg\rfloor+1.

Note that as
\sigma\to 1
, we have
p(\sigma-1)<1
and
p(\sigma-1)\to\delta
. Consequently, it follows that

\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma}=\frac{p(\sigma-1)}{\delta\sigma}\to 1\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=1+p(1-\sigma)\to 1-\delta.

Since
\eta<1-\delta
, it follows that (
2.4
) holds provided
\sigma
is chosen sufficiently close to
1
.
∎

3.
Applications of avalanche principle

In this section, we establish the error estimate on two scales.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
|\alpha-a/q|<1/q^{2}
with
\gcd(a,q)=1
. Let
q\geqslant q_{0}
and
N
satisfying
C_{1}(\kappa)q^{\sigma}<N<2N<C_{2}(\kappa)q^{\sigma_{1}}
be as in Theorem
2.1
. Further assume that
L_{N}(A)>90\kappa>0
and
L_{2N}(A)>\frac{9}{10}L_{N}(A)
. Then, for sufficiently large
q_{0}
and for any
N^{\prime}
such that
N\mid N^{\prime}
and
m\coloneq N^{\prime}/N<e^{\frac{c}{10}q^{\gamma}}
, we have

|L_{N^{\prime}}(A)+L_{N}(A)-2L_{2N}(A)|<e^{-\frac{c}{2}q^{\gamma}}+\frac{2L_{N}(A)}{m}.

The proof is based on the following avalanche principle.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
M_{1},\cdots,M_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\|M_{j}\|\geqslant\mu>n,\quad j=1\cdots,n,

and for
j=1,\cdots,n-1
,

|\ln\|M_{j}\|+\ln\|M_{j+1}\|-\ln\|M_{j+1}M_{j}\||<\frac{1}{2}\ln\mu.

Then

\bigg|\ln\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}M_{j}\|+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\ln\|M_{j}\|-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\ln\|M_{j+1}M_{j}\|\bigg|<C\frac{n}{\mu}.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

For brevity, we write
L_{N}\coloneq L_{N}(A)
. We apply Proposition
3.2
to the matrices
M_{j}(\theta)\coloneq A_{N}(\theta+jN\alpha)
,
j=0,\cdots,m-1
. By Theorem
2.1
, there exists a subset
\Omega_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
satisfying

\operatorname{meas}(\mathbb{T}\setminus\Omega_{N})<2me^{-cq^{\gamma}},

such that for all
\theta\in\Omega_{N}
,

\begin{split}|\ln\|M_{j}\|-NL_{N}|&<N\kappa,\\
|\ln\|M_{j+1}M_{j}\|-2NL_{2N}|&<2N\kappa.\end{split}

Observe that for
\theta\in\Omega_{N}
, we have

\|M_{j}\|>e^{N(L_{N}-\kappa)}>e^{\frac{89}{90}NL_{N}}

and

\begin{split}\big|\ln\|M_{j}\|+\ln\|M_{j+1}\|&-\ln\|M_{j+1}M_{j}\|\big|\\
&<4N\kappa+2N|L_{N}-L_{2N}|<\frac{1}{4}NL_{N}.\end{split}

Apply Proposition
3.2
with
\mu=e^{\frac{89}{90}NL_{N}}
, one can see that
\mu>e^{\frac{c}{10}q^{\gamma}}>m
and
\frac{1}{4}NL_{N}<\frac{1}{2}\ln\mu
due to the largeness of
q_{0}
, and thus for
\theta\in\Omega_{N}
,

\bigg|\ln\bigg\|\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}M_{j}\bigg\|+\sum_{j=1}^{m-2}\ln\|M_{j}\|-\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\ln\|M_{j+1}M_{j}\|\bigg|<Cme^{-\frac{89}{90}NL_{N}}.

Integrating over
\Omega_{N}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{N}}\ln\|A_{N^{\prime}}(\theta)\|\,\mathrm{d}\theta&+\sum_{j=1}^{m-2}\int_{\Omega_{N}}\ln\|A_{N}(\theta+jN\alpha)\|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&-\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\int_{\Omega_{N}}\ln\|A_{2N}(\theta+jN\alpha)\|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigg|<Cme^{-\frac{89}{90}NL_{N}}.\end{split}

Combining this estimate with the integral over
\mathbb{T}\setminus\Omega_{N}
, we find

\bigg|L_{N^{\prime}}+\frac{m-2}{m}L_{N}-2\frac{m-1}{m}L_{2N}\bigg|<C\frac{m}{N^{\prime}}e^{-\frac{89}{90}NL_{N}}+2\|A\|_{C^{0}}me^{-cq^{\gamma}}<e^{-\frac{c}{2}q^{\gamma}}.

It follows from the subadditivity of
L_{N}
that

|L_{N^{\prime}}+L_{N}-2L_{2N}|<e^{-\frac{c}{2}q^{\gamma}}+\frac{2L_{N}}{m}.

∎

4.
Multi-scale induction

We establish the local uniform error estimate between Lyapunov exponent and its finite-scale approximation.

.

Assume that
\alpha\in\Omega(\eta)

m=m_{j+1}
,
N=N_{j}
, and
q=\tilde{q}_{j}
, we get

|L_{N_{j+1}}+L_{N_{j}}-2L_{2N_{j}}|<e^{-\frac{c}{2}\tilde{q}_{j}^{\gamma}}+\frac{2L_{N_{j}}}{m_{j+1}}<C_{0}\tilde{q}_{j}^{\sigma_{1}-\zeta\sigma}.

(4.14)

Applying Theorem
3.1
once more with
N^{\prime}=2N_{j+1}
,
m=2m_{j+1}
,
N=N_{j}
, and
q=\tilde{q}_{j}
, we find

|L_{2N_{j+1}}+L_{N_{j}}-2L_{2N_{j}}|<C_{0}\tilde{q}_{j}^{\sigma_{1}-\zeta\sigma}.

(4.15)

Combining (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) yields

|L_{2N_{j+1}}-L_{N_{j+1}}|<2C_{0}\tilde{q}_{j}^{\sigma_{1}-\zeta\sigma}.

Furthermore, the triangle inequality gives

\begin{split}|L_{N_{j+1}}-L_{N_{j}}|&\leqslant|L_{N_{j+1}}+L_{N_{j}}-2L_{2N_{j}}|+2|L_{2N_{j}}-L_{N_{j}}|<10C_{0}\tilde{q}_{j-1}^{\sigma_{1}-\zeta\sigma}.\end{split}

Thus, (
4.6
), (
4.7
), and (
4.8
) hold for
s=j
.

Consequently, utilizing the telescopic sum
L-L_{N_{1}}=\sum_{s\geqslant 1}(L_{N_{s+1}}-L_{N_{s}})
and the growth condition
\tilde{q}_{s+1}>\tilde{q}_{s}^{\zeta}
with
\zeta>1
, we deduce

\begin{split}|L+L_{N_{0}}-2L_{2N_{0}}|&\leqslant|L_{N_{1}}+L_{N_{0}}-2L_{2N_{0}}|+\sum_{s\geqslant 1}|L_{N_{s+1}}-L_{N_{s}}|\\
&\leqslant C_{0}\tilde{q}_{0}^{\sigma_{1}-\zeta\sigma}+10C_{0}\sum_{s\geqslant 1}\tilde{q}_{s-1}^{\sigma_{1}-\zeta\sigma}\leqslant\tilde{q}_{0}^{-c^{\prime}},\end{split}

where
c^{\prime}\coloneq\frac{1}{2}(\zeta\sigma-\sigma_{1})>0
, and the final inequality holds for sufficiently large
\tilde{q}_{0}
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Assume that
A,A_{n}\in G^{s}_{\rho}(\mathbb{T},\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}))
and
A_{n}\to A
as
n\to\infty
. If
L(A)=0
, then the continuity of
L(\cdot)
at
A
follows directly from upper semi-continuity:

0\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}L(A_{n})\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}L(A_{n})\leqslant L(A)=0.

Therefore, we may assume
L(A)>100\kappa>0
.

By Theorem
4.1
, for sufficiently large
n
, we simultaneously have

\begin{split}|L(A)+L_{N_{0}}(A)-2L_{2N_{0}}(A)|&<\tilde{q}_{0}^{-c^{\prime}},\\
|L(A_{n})+L_{N_{0}}(A_{n})-2L_{2N_{0}}(A_{n})|&<\tilde{q}_{0}^{-c^{\prime}}.\end{split}

It follows that

\begin{split}|L(A)-L(A_{n})|&\leqslant|L_{N_{0}}(A)-L_{N_{0}}(A_{n})|+2|L_{2N_{0}}(A)-L_{2N_{0}}(A_{n})|+2\tilde{q}_{0}^{-c^{\prime}}\\
&\leqslant C(N_{0})\|A_{n}-A\|_{C^{0}}+2\tilde{q}_{0}^{-c^{\prime}}.\end{split}

Consequently,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|L(A)-L(A_{n})|\leqslant 2\tilde{q}_{0}^{-c^{\prime}}.

Letting
\tilde{q}_{0}\to\infty
implies the result.
∎
