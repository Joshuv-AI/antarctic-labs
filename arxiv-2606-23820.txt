Jeehyun Yang, Vighnesh Nagpal, Michael Zhang, Qiao Xue, Eliza M.-R. Kempton, Jacob L. Bean, Michael R. Line, Jonathan J. Fortney, Peter Gao, Matthew C. Nixon, Caroline Piaulet-Ghorayeb, Kevin B. Abstract
Sulfur chemistry has emerged as an important probe of exoplanet atmospheres in the JWST era, although observational constraints have thus far been largely limited to SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and H 2 ​ S \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S} in warm and hot exoplanets. Recent JWST observations have revealed CS 2 \text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} in several cooler gas-giant exoplanets, yielding a new tracer of sulfur chemistry. However, the detailed chemical pathways responsible for the formation of CS 2 \text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} remain poorly understood. Here, we use TOI-6894 b, a temperate gas giant with evidence for CS 2 \text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , as a test case for one-dimensional photochemical kinetic-transport modeling and sensitivity analyses of CS 2 \text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} chemistry. We show that CS 2 \text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} is produced through coupled thermochemical and photochemical processes involving CH 4 \text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} and H 2 ​ S \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S} as the primary carbon and sulfur reservoirs, with S 2 \text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} photolysis driving disequilibrium sulfur chemistry. Our models provide a self-consistent explanation for the observed CS 2 \text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} feature in TOI-6894 b. Extending our analysis to gas giant exoplanets spanning a wide range of T eq T_{\rm eq} , we find that CS 2 \text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} abundance peaks in temperate to warm atmospheres ( T eq ∼ 500 T_{\rm eq}\sim 500 – 700 K), and declines toward both lower and higher temperatures. This temperature dependence provides a unified framework for interpreting current CS 2 \text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} observations, accounting for reported detections in temperate to warm planets and the lack of detections in colder and hotter giant exoplanets. Our results establish CS 2 \text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} as a complementary probe of sulfur inventories and atmospheric metallicity in cool gas giant exoplanets.

Chemical kinetic models inherently contain uncertainties arising from imperfect knowledge of reaction rate coefficients. These uncertainties propagate into the model output and therefore represent one of the major caveats in atmospheric chemical modeling. Owing to decades of developments in combustion chemistry, our understanding of the chemistry of C-H-O species, and to some extent nitrogen chemistry, has been significantly improved through laboratory measurements of key reactions and
ab initio

(
23

32

(

For example, sulfur-bearing species are often highly reactive and can contaminate or damage chemical kinetic measurement systems (e.g., reactor walls and mass spectrometers), making laboratory studies particularly challenging. For this reason, sensitivity analysis is routinely performed in chemical engineering applications to evaluate the robustness of chemical kinetic models against uncertainties in reaction rate coefficients. Such analyses not only assess the reliability of model predictions but also identify the reactions that exert the strongest control over the chemical system.

III.1
Overall Atmospheric Chemistry of TOI-6894 b

Figure
1
shows the vertical volume mixing ratio (VMR) profiles for the fiducial TOI-6894 b model described in Section
II.3
. In the deep atmosphere (
P\geq 10
bar), the C-, N-, O-, and S-bearing species are present in their typical forms expected for a

\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}

\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{NH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}

(
44
)
. In the middle atmosphere (
P\sim 10^{-2}-1
bar), vertical mixing controls the overall composition, producing nearly uniform VMR profiles for the major C-, N-, O-, and S-bearing species. In the upper atmosphere (
P\lesssim 10^{-2}
bar), photochemistry controls the overall composition.

A key result is the formation of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the upper atmosphere, represented by the magenta solid line in Figure
1
. Within the grey-shaded JWST-observable region
(
51
,
P\sim 0.1
–2 mbar;)
, sulfur initially stored as
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
in the deep atmosphere is progressively converted primarily into
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
toward higher altitudes, with smaller fractions forming
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is approximately a factor of two lower than the deep-atmosphere VMR of
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecule contains two sulfur atoms, whereas each
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
molecule contains only one. This result indicates that sulfur originally locked in
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
in the deep atmosphere is largely redistributed into
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the upper atmosphere of TOI-6894 b. In the next section, we further identify the production pathways of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{S}

2

\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{SH}

\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{SH}

\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CS}

\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

As briefly mentioned in Section

III.3
Sensitivity Analysis of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
Formation to Rate-Coefficient Uncertainty

Based on the dominant reactions involved in
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
formation identified through the rate analysis described in Figure
2
b and Section
III.2
, we subsequently performed sensitivity calculations for each reaction following the procedure described in Section
II.4
. The resulting sensitivity coefficients are shown in Figure
2
a. The model sensitivity is quantified using the sensitivity coefficient (Eqn.
1
). Briefly speaking, if the sensitivity coefficient of the
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance with respect to reaction
i
is equal to 1 (i.e.,
S_{i,{}\mathrm{CS}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}}
=1), reducing the rate coefficient of reaction
i
by a factor of 10 results in a corresponding factor of 10 decrease in the predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance, indicating that the reaction positively contributes to
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production. Conversely, if
S_{i,{}\mathrm{CS}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}}=-1
, reducing the rate coefficient by a factor of 10 increases the predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance by a factor of 10, indicating that the reaction suppresses
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
formation. In practice, reactions with small absolute sensitivity coefficients have a limited influence on the model output. For example,
|S_{i,{}\mathrm{CS}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}}|\leq 0.1
implies that even a factor of 10 perturbation in the rate coefficient changes the predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance by only
\sim
20 %, while
|S_{i,{}\mathrm{CS}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}}|\leq 0.01
corresponds to only
\sim
2 % variation.

Figure
2
a shows that among the 27 dominant reactions associated with
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
formation, 25 reactions possess
|S_{i,{}\mathrm{CS}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}}|\lesssim 0.05
, corresponding to uncertainties of only
\lesssim 25
% in the predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance even under order-of-magnitude perturbations to their individual rate coefficients. The SH+H
\rightarrow
S+
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
reaction exhibits the second largest sensitivity (
S_{i,{}\mathrm{CS}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}}=0.1670
), corresponding to approximately a factor of two variation in
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance even if the reaction rate is perturbed by two orders of magnitude. Overall, these results demonstrate the robustness of the predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production against uncertainties in most reaction rates, noting that the order-of-magnitude perturbations adopted here are conservative for many kinetic parameters constrained theoretically or experimentally.

The only truly influential reaction is
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis,

{}\mathrm{S}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\xrightarrow{h\nu}{}\mathrm{S}+{}\mathrm{S}

(2)

whose sensitivity coefficient approaches unity (
S_{i,{}\mathrm{CS}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}}=0.7871
). This indicates that
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis acts as the primary controlling reaction for
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production in the atmosphere of TOI-6894 b, suggesting that
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
formation is primarily photochemically driven.

\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis proceeds through a predissociation mechanism. Briefly speaking, predissociation occurs when dissociation takes place at energies below the nominal dissociation threshold due to strong spin-orbit coupling, which induces state mixing between bound and dissociative electronic states. The ground state of
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
X^{3}\Sigma^{-}_{g}
) is first excited into its quantum mechanically allowed bound excited state
B^{3}\Sigma^{-}_{u}
. However, the strong spin-orbit coupling in
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
causes significant state mixing between
B^{3}\Sigma^{-}_{u}
and its quantum mechanically forbidden state
B^{{}^{\prime\prime}3}\Pi^{-}_{u}
. Since
B^{{}^{\prime\prime}3}\Pi^{-}_{u}
possesses a substantially lower dissociation threshold (
\sim 280
nm) than
B^{3}\Sigma^{-}_{u}
(
\sim 230
nm), state mixing enables photodissociation of
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at significantly longer wavelengths.

This
B/B^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
–
X
system of
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
has been extensively investigated both experimentally
(
24
;
25
;
53
)
and theoretically
(
28
)
. In
EPACRIS
, we adopt the
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photodissociation cross sections from
27
, which were derived from experimental measurements by
53
at 370 K with estimated uncertainties below 30%. Therefore, even under conservative assumptions, the uncertainty in the
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis rate is unlikely to exceed a factor of two. Combining this with the sensitivity coefficient of the
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis reaction (
S_{i,{}\mathrm{CS}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}}=0.7871
), the predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance would vary by at most
\sim
40%. This result further supports the robustness of the photochemically produced
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance in the modeled atmosphere of TOI-6894 b.

Sensitivity Analysis of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Figure

3

K_{\rm zz}
values up to
10^{8}
cm
2

cm

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and an enhanced transport of
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
from the deeper atmosphere. In other words, the region dominated by vertical mixing extends toward higher altitudes and replaces part of the upper atmosphere that is photochemically controlled under lower
K_{\rm zz}
conditions. As discussed in Sections
III.2
and
III.3
, this further supports our conclusion that
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is primarily a photochemical product in the atmosphere of TOI-6894 b and is preferentially produced in the upper atmosphere where photochemical processes dominate.

Varying the atmospheric metallicity primarily controls the maximum abundance of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
formed in the atmosphere, whereas the conversion of sulfur from
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

P\sim 10^{-3}

\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
,
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, and
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
. Consequently, the absolute abundance of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
increases with metallicity, while the photochemical conversion efficiency from
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
to
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
remains largely unchanged. This behavior likely occurs because the photochemistry is primarily controlled by available UV photons in the upper atmosphere rather than by the absolute sulfur inventory. These results suggest that sulfur in the atmosphere of TOI-6894 b can be efficiently converted into
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
via photochemistry over the explored metallicity range from 0.1
\times
to 30
\times Z_{\odot}
.

Varying
T_{\rm int}
primarily affects the deep atmospheric chemical composition, particularly the abundances of
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, CO, and
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

(
43
;
19
;
67

. As discussed in Section

,
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
acts as the primary carbon source for
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

T_{\rm int}

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

–400 K for the 1
\times Z_{\odot}

\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
abundance above
\sim 100
ppmv (VMR
\gtrsim 10^{-4}

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production throughout the
T_{\rm int}
range of 30–400 K in the atmosphere of TOI-6894 b. For planets with higher
T_{\rm eq}
and
T_{\rm int}
, however, thermochemical depletion of
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
may eventually limit
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

III.6
.

Although not shown, varying the stellar spectrum from GJ 876 to the more UV-active AD Leo enhances
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production deeper (
>
1 mbar) in the atmosphere by increasing the availability of 100–400 nm photons and extending the region where photochemistry dominates, increasing

abundances by up to an order of magnitude. However, the resulting changes near the

abundances increasing by less than a factor of three and producing almost no discernible change in the model transmission spectra (Section

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Because
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
to
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
) and sulfur atoms from initial
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
, respectively. At lower temperatures, such as for TOI-199 b (
T_{\rm eq}=350
K), the atmosphere lacks sufficient thermal energy for efficient
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production despite the high abundance of
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

T_{\rm eq}
increases, thermochemical conversion becomes more efficient, leading to enhanced
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production that peaks at intermediate
T_{\rm eq}

T_{\rm eq}
, however, the decreasing abundance of
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
now limits the available carbon source for
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
formation, resulting in declining
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
column densities.

The potential detectability of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
can be estimated empirically from our transmission spectrum calculations. By varying the atmospheric
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance in our fiducial model, we find that a
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
column density of
\gtrsim 10^{18}
molecules cm
-2
within the observable atmosphere is required to generate a spectral feature with an amplitude comparable to the current JWST measurement uncertainties for TOI-6894 b (
\sim 1\sigma
), as shown by black dashed line in Figure
4
. In terms of abundance, this roughly corresponds to a volume mixing ratio of
\sim
1 ppmv. From this perspective, the absence of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
feature in the JWST observation of TOI-199 b
(
7
)
is consistent with the low predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
column densities in both the 1
\times
and 10
\times Z_{\odot}
cases.

In contrast, both TOI-6894 b and V1298 Tau e exhibit substantial predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
column densities (see Figure
5
). More importantly, for the 1
\times Z_{\odot}
scenarios, the predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
column densities exceed those of CO in both atmospheres. This distinction is particularly important for observational detectability because the major absorption bands of CO and
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
overlap near
\sim
4.6
\mu
m. Although the CO absorption lines are relatively sparse in this wavelength region, their absorption cross sections are approximately an order of magnitude larger than those of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Consequently, a higher
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
column density relative to CO is favorable for distinguishing
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
absorption from overlapping CO features. This result is consistent with the clear detections of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in both TOI-6894 b and V1298 Tau e
(
14
, M. Zhang
et al.
, submitted;)
. However, the attenuated
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
features and enhanced
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
absorption reported for V1298 Tau e by
14
may indicate a lower C/O ratio than the solar value of 0.55
(
37
)
,
T_{\rm int}\geq 100
K, or a combination of both.

V1298 Tau b represents the opposite regime, where the CO column density exceeds that of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Although its
T_{\rm eq}
=666 K lies within the regime where our models predict potentially observable
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, recent JWST observations revealed prominent CO and
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
absorption together with weaker
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
features and a tentative detection of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

(
5
)
. The enhanced CO abundance may partially obscure the 4.6
\mu
m
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
feature, but this alone is unlikely to fully explain the apparent absence of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, particularly given the reported detection of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the hotter planet WASP-80 b. Instead, V1298 Tau b may illustrate the importance of additional atmospheric parameters beyond
T_{\rm eq}
. As a young giant planet, V1298 Tau b is expected to possess a substantial internal heat flux, and atmospheric retrievals suggest either a high internal temperature (
T_{\rm int}\lesssim 500
K) and/or a sub-solar C/O ratio of 0.22
(
5
)
. Both effects reduce atmospheric
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
abundances, thereby suppressing
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production in our models. Consistent with this interpretation, our
T_{\rm int}=400
K models predict lower
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
abundances, significantly enhanced CO, comparable
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
column densities (both close to the estimated detectability threshold), and reduced
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
/CO contrast compared to the
T_{\rm int}=100
K cases. Given that our
T_{\rm int}=400
K models qualitatively reproduce the key spectroscopic characteristics of V1298 Tau b, we therefore speculate that the apparent absence of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in V1298 Tau b reflects the combined influence of internal heating, atmospheric composition, and spectral masking by CO-bearing species, rather than
T_{\rm eq}
alone.

At the
T_{\rm eq}
of WASP-80 b (825 K), the atmosphere enters a regime where the abundances of CO and
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
become comparable. Recent JWST/NIRCam and MIRI observations of WASP-80 b identified a prominent absorption feature near 4.6 and 6.5
\mu
m that may be attributed to
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

(
55
)
. The relative column density ratios among
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, CO, and
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in WASP-80 b are similar to those predicted for V1298 Tau b, although the absolute column densities are approximately an order of magnitude lower (Figure
5
). From our modeling perspective, the predicted
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
column density in WASP-80 b remains above
10^{18}
[molecules cm
-2
] even for the 1
\times Z_{\odot}
scenario, while the 10
\times Z_{\odot}
model predicts an additional order-of-magnitude enhancement in
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance. However, the CO column density is also predicted to increase substantially, exceeding that of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
by approximately a factor of 10 for the 1
\times Z_{\odot}
scenario and by nearly two orders of magnitude for the 10
\times Z_{\odot}
scenario (Figure
5
). These results suggest that the atmosphere of WASP-80 b can maintain a detectable abundance of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. However, the presence of substantial amounts of CO, despite its relatively sparse absorption lines in this wavelength region, may complicate the distinction between
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and CO features near 4.6
\mu
m. This interpretation is broadly consistent with the JWST observations reported by
55
.

At the
T_{\rm eq}
of WASP-39 b (1116 K),
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
is completely depleted from the atmosphere, with the carbon inventory primarily locked in CO. Thus,
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
formation becomes limited by the lack of available
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, leading to the lowest
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
column densities (see Figure
5
). In contrast,
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
becomes abundant at 10
\times Z_{\odot}
. These predictions are consistent with the extensive JWST observations of WASP-39 b, which have revealed prominent
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
features with no
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
features
(
30
;
51
;
1
;
50
)
. Together with the
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
detections in WASP-80 b, these results suggest the existence of a transition
T_{\rm eq}
, between 825 and 1116 K, at which the dominant observable sulfur-bearing species shifts from
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
to
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

Overall, our photochemical modeling spanning a wide range of
T_{\rm eq}
suggests that detectable
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundances are favored in gas giants with
T_{\rm eq}\sim 400
–800 K. A number of additional planets within this
T_{\rm eq}
range have already been or are planned to be observed with JWST using modes capable of probing
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
absorption (e.g., NIRSpec/G395M, G395H, and PRISM), making them promising targets for future
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
detections (see labels along the top
x
-axis of Figure
5
).

Furthermore, recent studies have shown that
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is highly sensitive to atmospheric metallicity and serves as a powerful tracer of heavy-element enrichment in warm and hot giant exoplanets
(
57
;
13
;
50
;
12
)
. Our results suggest that
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
may play an analogous role in cooler gas giants, where
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
becomes less abundant. Although the
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance can be influenced by factors such as sub-solar C/O ratios, elevated
T_{\rm int}
, and stellar UV activity, its strong dependence on metallicity makes it a promising sulfur-based metallicity tracer. Together,
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
provide complementary probes of atmospheric sulfur chemistry and metallicity across a wide range of gas giants.

IV
Conclusions

In this Letter, we performed one-dimensional self-consistent atmospheric modeling of TOI-6894 b, along with an extensive sensitivity analysis to investigate the photochemical production of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Our models show very good agreement with the JWST NIRSpec/PRISM observations and predict substantial
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundances in TOI-6894 b’s atmosphere. The formation of
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
in the deep atmosphere is essential for subsequent
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production, since these species serve as the primary carbon and sulfur reservoirs. Through the combined effects of thermochemical and photochemical processes, carbon and sulfur initially stored in
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
are progressively converted into
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CS}
, HCS, CS, and ultimately
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Our sensitivity analysis identifies
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis as a key process driving the disequilibrium chemistry that leads to
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
formation in TOI-6894 b.

By extending our analysis to a broader
T_{\rm eq}=350
–1116 K, we find that detectable
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundances are favored in temperate-to-warm gas-giant atmospheres (
T_{\rm eq}\sim 400
–800 K). This
T_{\rm eq}
range encompasses 17 gas giant exoplanets that have already been or are planned to be observed with JWST using observing modes sensitive to
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, making them promising targets for future detections (Figure
5
). In particular, our models predict substantial
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production in TOI-6894 b, V1298 Tau e, and WASP-80 b, consistent with the recent detection of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in these planets
(
55
;
14
, M. Zhang
et al.
, submitted;)
, as well as the absence of
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in V1298 Tau b
(
5
)
. In summary, our modeling results suggest that
\text{CS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
can serve as a valuable tracer of the sulfur inventory in temperate to warm gas giants (
T_{\rm eq}\sim 400
– 800 K), complementing
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which is expected to be a more effective sulfur tracer in hotter giant exoplanets
(
57
;
13
;
50
;
12
)
.
