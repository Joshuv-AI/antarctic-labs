Global research trends of digital applications in medical education
Authors: Bingxin Chen, Xiuli Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The integration of digital applications into medical education has transformed traditional pedagogical approaches by offering innovative solutions for knowledge and skill acquisition. Given the profound impact of digital applications, this study employed bibliometric analysis to map global trends, identify key contributors, and delineate emerging themes in digital applications in medical education.Methods:We identified 2096 articles published from January 1, 2014, to December 31, 2024, from the Web of Science Core Collection. Using Microsoft Office Excel (Microsoft Corp.) and VOSviewer 1.6.19, we visualized annual publication trends, countries, organizations, journals, authors, and keywords.Results:From January 1, 2014, to December 31, 2024, annual publications surged from 85 to 448 articles. The United States of America dominated contributions, with the most publications (732 documents) and the most citations (12,525). Cooperation among organizations was characterized by partnerships between universities and hospitals, with the University of Toronto as the most prolific contributor (48 documents and 1659 citations). BMC Medical Education (117 documents and 1598 citations) and Anatomical Sciences Education (51 documents and 1827 citations) displayed high publication volumes and prominent citations. Konge Lars published the most documents, totaling 51, and led research on digital applications in medical education with 1141 citations. The keywords mainly focused on medical education, virtual reality, and simulation.Conclusions:This study collected articles from the past decade and revealed key contributors and global trends. The findings could help researchers and medical educators identify potential collaborators and provide direction for future research.

1. Introduction

Medical education is the cornerstone of meeting the evolving needs of clinical medicine.[1] However, traditional educational methods increasingly show the limitations of medical teaching equipment.[2] Traditional medical education relies on static textbooks and a one-way teaching mode, resulting in a lag in knowledge update rates and difficulty for students in grasping cutting-edge diagnostic and treatment technologies.[3–5] Meanwhile, clinical skills training is limited by the scarcity of real case resources, especially in rare diseases.[3–5] Therefore, traditional medical education has long faced multiple challenges.

In the past period, the exponential development of digital applications has facilitated development in various sectors, including medical education.[6–9] Digital applications, such as virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and e-learning platforms, served as transformative modalities in medical education.[10–12] By creating virtual spaces for low-risk clinical rehearsal, digital applications bridged the gap between theoretical knowledge and real clinical practice.[5] Therefore, digital applications provided immersive, interactive learning experiences that simulate authentic clinical environments with high fidelity.[13,14]

Existing studies have focused on discrete digital applications, but there is a lack of comprehensive analysis of digital applications in medical education. Bibliometric analysis can summarize bibliometric traits of a research field to aid understanding of trends and guide deeper research.[15] Therefore, leveraging Microsoft Office Excel and VOSviewer for visual analysis, our study conducts a bibliometric analysis of 10 years of global research on digital applications in medical education. Our research delineated global trends and informed evidence-based innovation in digital applications in medical education.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Research overview

This study aims to analyze the research hotspots, global trends, and development directions of digital applications in medical education over the past 10 years. We summarized and visualized the countries, organizations, journals, authors, and keywords of the included articles to provide a comprehensive understanding for future research.

2.2. Data sources

This was a descriptive bibliometric research study. Data were obtained from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database, which covered multidisciplinary fields and provided core functions such as literature search, citation analysis, and research impact assessment. WoSCC was searched to track relevant academic research.

2.3. Search strategy

When searching the WoSCC database, the search formula was as follows: (TS=(“Digital application*” OR “Digital tool*” OR “Digital platform*” OR “Digital solution*” OR “virtual reality” OR “virtual reality” OR “augmented reality” OR “augmented reality” OR “interface” OR “modeling”) AND TS=(“medical education” OR “medical training” OR “medical curriculum” OR “clinical education” OR “health professions education” OR “simulation training”)) AND DOP=(2014-01-01/2024-12-31).

2.4. Data collection

After the initial search, we obtained 3120 articles. Then, 2 researchers with academic research experience independently read the titles and abstracts of these articles, retaining those whose main topic was “digital applications in medical education.” After screening, the full texts of these articles were downloaded and read to determine their research content. Studies focusing on digital applications in medical education were included in the research scope. The screening work was carried out by 2 independent reviewers to minimize subjective bias. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion. In total, 2096 articles were identified. The inclusion criteria were the articles focused on digital applications in medical education and were published between January 1, 2014, and December 31, 2024. Exclusion criteria comprised the following publication types: editorials, letters, conference abstracts, reviews, news items, and retracted articles. Additionally, studies were classified as irrelevant and excluded if they did not use digital applications as a central educational intervention for medical learners – for example, those focusing exclusively on treatment, workflow, or administration, or those that merely mentioned digital tools without meaningful integration into teaching or training.

2.5. Data analysis

Included articles were imported into Microsoft Office Excel (Microsoft Corp.) to draw publication data charts. VOSviewer (version 1.6.19) was utilized for bibliometric analysis, and countries, organizations, journals, authors, and keywords were further analyzed.

3. Results

3.1. Analysis of annual publication trends

The increase in research on digital applications in medical education reflects the growing academic and practical interest in this field. From the WoSCC database, a total of 2096 publications were included in our study (Fig. 1). The period from 2014 to 2019 showed a slow growth phase, with annual publications increasing from 85 to 126 (Table 1 and Fig. 2). In 2020, 2021, and 2022, annual publications exceeded 200 articles per year (Table 1 and Fig. 2). Annual publications reached 308 articles per year in 2023, and continued to grow rapidly, peaking at 448 in 2024 (Table 1 and Fig. 2). This surge coincided with advances in digital applications such as VR, AR, and big data platforms, which were reshaping medical education.[16–18] Nevertheless, it should be noted that this quantitative growth merely reflects the expanding scope of this research field and the sustained rise in academic attention. The quality and practical educational effectiveness of relevant studies remain to be further verified.

3.2. Analysis of countries

A total of 103 countries have contributed to the research on digital applications in medical education (Table 2). The United States of America (USA) led in this field with 732 documents, followed by Canada (183), England (179), Germany (173), and China (164). The country with the most citations was the USA (12,525), followed by Canada (4642), England (2317), Germany (2204), and Denmark (1927). The results reflected that the USA has occupied an important position in this research field for a long time and contributed a considerable amount of academic research. It was particularly noteworthy that the USA and European countries have built a solid cooperation framework and formed a close partnership (Fig. 3).

3.3. Analysis of organizations

From January 1, 2014, to December 13, 2024, 3084 organizations participated in research on digital applications in medical education (Table 3). The top 5 organizations in terms of publication volume were the University of Toronto (48 documents), Copenhagen University (41 documents), Rigshospitalet (39 documents), the University of Michigan (36 documents), and Mayo Clinic (36 documents). The organization with the most citations was the University of Toronto (1659 citations), followed by Mayo Clinic (1478 citations), Rigshospitalet (1075 citations), the University of Ottawa (974 citations), and Copenhagen University (966 citations). These results underscored the importance of collaboration between universities and hospitals in advancing digital applications in medical education. Such partnerships were vital for fostering innovation and enhancing the integration of technology into educational practices, ultimately contributing to more effective and modern medical education (Fig. 4).

4. Discussion

This bibliometric analysis elucidated the dynamic evolution of digital applications in medical education over the past decade, revealing distinct growth phases and research trends.

Between 2014 and 2019, the field was in the exploratory phase with slow growth, as researchers and organizations explored the feasibility of digital tools in medical education. From 2020 onward, annual publications surged, and this acceleration aligned with the rapid development of digital applications such as large language models, VR, and AR, which gained traction for their ability to simulate clinical environments and enhance training efficiency. At the same time, coronavirus disease-2019 may have acted as a catalyst to accelerate the use of digital applications in medical education due to the need for distance learning.[19] The driving role of rapid technological development in recent years cannot be ignored. The peak of 448 publications in 2024 underscored the maturation of digital applications and their integration into medical education. Notably, advancements in data-driven platforms and artificial intelligence (AI)-powered tools further expanded the scope of digital medical education, enabling personalized learning and real-time feedback mechanisms.[20–22] Nowadays, integrating AI into medical education should go beyond superficial digital teaching. It is essential to translate AI training into clinicians’ real-world clinical decision-making within curricula, develop programs to foster prudent and rational AI use, and mitigate risks such as overreliance on algorithms. Integrating AI into medical education has indeed brought many practical opportunities. However, it must be recognized that it still faces many limitations, including a weak theoretical foundation, insufficient outcome assessment, insufficient validation of adaptive systems, algorithmic biases, inequality gaps, and regulatory uncertainties, which require further development.[23]

A total of 103 countries contributed to this field, with the USA dominating in both publications (732 documents) and citations (12,523). Canada, England, Germany, and Denmark followed, reflecting their robust research infrastructures and technological support. The USA is in an important position and participates in extensive international cooperation. European countries, particularly England and Germany, demonstrated strong partnerships, likely facilitated by cross-border cooperation initiatives such as European Union-funded projects.[24] China, as an Asian country, had 164 publications and 1724 citations, demonstrating considerable potential in digital applications in medical education. These results emphasized the importance of promoting close global cooperation. Cheng et al also found that most research and institutional practices on AI integration into undergraduate medical curricula originate from North America, with limited evidence from Europe, Asia, Africa, and South America.[25] Such regional disparities highlight equity concerns in global medical education and underscore the need for future studies to examine region-specific barriers, multifaceted challenges, and emerging opportunities for AI adoption in these underrepresented regions.

Among 3084 organizations, the University of Toronto emerged with the most publications (48 documents) and citations (1659), underscoring its sustained research efforts in digital medical education. The excellent performance of universities and hospitals, such as Mayo Clinic, Copenhagen University, and Rigshospitalet, highlighted key synergies between academic research and clinical practice. Such partnerships enabled the development of digital applications that address real-world challenges in medical education, from diagnostic accuracy to surgical simulation.[26]

Journals such as Medical Education and Anatomical Sciences Education demonstrated high publication volumes and citations, which reflected the active academic exchanges among journals in the digital applications of medical education. As for authors, Konge Lars (51 documents, 1141 citations) worked as an important researcher in this research field. Sorensen Mads Solvsten and Andersen Steven Arild Wuyts also had high numbers of documents and citations. Identifying top journals and authors in this field enabled quick access to cutting-edge achievements, enhanced research efficiency, and expanded cooperative networks.

Keyword analysis displayed that multiple nodes and links formed clusters of different colors. The nodes of the visual map were densely distributed with countless connections, indicating many connections among these keywords. Among them, “medical education” was the largest node, followed closely by “virtual reality” and “simulation,” which indicated the important research directions. The above analysis demonstrated that digital applications profoundly transformed medical teaching and learning by enhancing engagement and providing immersive, data-driven experiences. Visual immersive tools have notable advantages in anatomy teaching and surgical skills training, enabling repeated simulation exercises, while their application in nursing education adopts more context-based interactive models to enhance the cultivation of clinical communication skills.[27–29] Computational analysis is also required in areas such as the discovery of biomarkers, vaccine development, research on autoimmune diseases, diagnosis of infectious diseases, and cancer immunotherapy.[30] Overall, these digital methods are gradually addressing the long-standing limitations of traditional medical education, such as limited specimen resources, insufficient surgical simulation opportunities, and difficulty in recreating rare clinical scenarios.

5. Limitations

Although this bibliometric analysis displayed valuable insights, several limitations remain. First, only English-language publications were included, excluding non-English studies and compromising the comprehensiveness of the findings. Second, the predominance of research and institutional practices originating from North America may introduce geographical bias. Third, this study only retrieved publications from the WoSCC, which may introduce potential retrieval bias. This study mainly evaluated academic influence based on the number of publications and the total number of citations. In subsequent research, more indicators should be included.

6. Knowledge gaps

Currently, most studies remain confined to superficial digital teaching practices, without thoroughly examining the substantive impact of digital applications on clinical decision-making competence. Consequently, there is an urgent need for longitudinal follow-up studies to systematically elucidate effective integration pathways of digital applications in medical education, so as to genuinely enhance learners’ clinical reasoning and decision-making skills. On this basis, curriculum frameworks should be deliberately developed to foster rational and critical digital applications, alongside the design of intervention strategies aimed at effectively mitigating risks such as overreliance on algorithms.

7. Future directions

Future research should prioritize global collaboration to reduce geographical disparities and promote equity in digital applications for medical education, particularly by collecting empirical evidence from underrepresented continents. Longitudinal work should explore digital applications in medical education. Further efforts should develop curricula to nurture critical AI competency and mitigate risks of algorithm overreliance. Standardized evaluation metrics ought to be established to assess the sustained impacts of digital medical education on clinical practice.

8. Conclusion

Digital applications have played a pivotal role in the modernization of medical education. This bibliometric analysis charts the rapid evolution of research concerning digital applications in medical education, propelled by technological advances. The analysis of countries reveals that North American and European institutions dominate relevant scholarship. Therefore, sustained cross-regional collaboration is essential to facilitate balanced progress across global settings.
