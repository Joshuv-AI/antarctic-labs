Abstract
By a well-known characterisation, in a presheaf topos every internal suplattice is a presheaf of suplattices, but not every presheaf of suplattices is an internal suplattice (and similarly for frames). In this paper, we construct the free internal suplattice/frame on an presheaf of suplattices/frames, yielding a left adjoint to the forgetful functor from the respective internal structures to presheaves of structures. The description of this left adjoint has also appeared in recent work of Henry and Townsend, in connection to a different universal property, namely that of turning a lax natural transformation between poset-enriched functors to a strict one. As an application of our construction, we investigate conditions on frames internal to a presheaf topos, such as being locally compact, compact, stably locally compact or Hausdorff, in terms of properties of their sections in the base topos. In the first three cases, it is necessary that all the sections have the respective properties, while the Hausdorff property is not transferred to the sections. Moreover for local compactness it is necessary that the transition maps preserve the way-below relation. Finally, for an internal locally compact frame in presheaves we analyse the connection of its way-below relation to the respective relations of its sections.

1
Introduction-Preliminaries

The aim of this work is to contribute to the study of locales (frames) that are internal to a presheaf topos in terms of their ‘external’ description. By their ‘external’ description, we mean the consequence of the well-known characterisation, due to Joyal and Tierney
[
9
]
, that in the topos of presheaves over a small category (with finite limits), the internal frames are precisely presheaves of frames such that the transition maps have left adjoint maps satisfying the Beck-Chevalley condition (accounting for the internal completeness of the underlying sup-lattice) that also satisfy the Frobenious reciprocity condition (accounting for the distributivity of internal suprema with finite infima).

In this paper, we show that the forgetful functor from internal sup-lattices (respectively, internal frames) to presheaves of sup-lattices (resp., frames) has a left adjoint, which we denote by
\mathfrak{z}
(
Theorem

2.3
, resp.,
Theorem

2.8
). The existence of the left adjoint
\mathfrak{z}
, for the case of frames, is a special case of an internal ‘generalised ideal construction’, as studied in
[
2
]
and in the work of the third named author
[
18
,
19
]
(for the non-internal version, see
[
7
, Proposition II.2.11]
). The underlying construction of
\mathfrak{z}
giving the free internal sup-lattice (resp., frame) associated with a presheaf of sup-lattices (resp., frames) has also appeared in the work of Henry and Townsend
[
4
]
, but in connection to a different universal property it enjoys:
\mathfrak{z}
universally turns lax natural transformations between functors, valued in suitable poset-enriched categories, into (strict) natural transformations (for a comparison of these two universal properties, see
Remark

2.4
). The existence of a left adjoint to the forgetful functor from internal suplattices/frames to presheaves of suplattices/frames can be leveraged to give a clear, algebraic account of how to compute, among other things, the internal power-object and the internal ideal-object (
Corollary

2.5
and
Corollary

2.10
). This connection between the free suplattice completion
\mathfrak{z}
and internal power-objects and ideal-objects is also present in
[
4
]
; in contrast to
[
4
]
, our proof is straightforwardly algebraic. Moreover, we show that the free internal frame completion
\mathfrak{z}
is idempotent if and only if all presheaves of frames are internal frames, if and only if the presheaf topos is Boolean (
Proposition

2.13
).

Although
[
4
]
provides an equivalence between the category of compact regular frames that are internal in a presheaf topos and the category of presheaves with values in the category of compact regular frames, this equivalence is not obtained taking the underlying presheaf of an internal frame,
à la
Joyal–Tierney, and so it remains a meaningful question to investigate compactness and Hausdorffness conditions in terms of properties of the sections of the internal frame. We use the explicit description of the free frame completion
\mathfrak{z}
to analyse conditions such as those of being locally compact, compact, stably locally compact, or Hausdorff for locales internal to a presheaf topos. The importance of these respective subclasses of internal locales is, we believe, manifested by the various applications they have found in the literature. For instance, compact Hausdorff locales are crucial in formulating a Gelfand-Neimark duality internally in a presheaf topos
[
5
,
1
]
, which is then used in
[
6
,
13
]
in a discussion of topos-theoretic foundations to algebraic quantum theory.

The explicit computation of the sections of the internal ideal-object provides the first stepping stone for relating properties of the sections of an internal frame
L
to internal properties of
L
. For example, compactness of the frame
L
amounts to the equalizer of the supremum and the constant to top map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{idl}}L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigvee}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

being just
\{L\}
. By using
\mathfrak{z}
to calculate internal ideal-objects, it can easily be derived that the sections of a compact internal frame are compact (
Proposition

3.9
). Further, local compactness amounts to the existence of a map
\Lambda\colon L\to{\mathrm{idl}}L,
left adjoint to the supremum map, and from that we can derive a section-wise left adjoint to the supremum map,

\lambda_{a}\colon La\to{\mathrm{idl}}(La)

yielding the local compactness of the sections of
L
(
Proposition

3.1
), and it can further be calculated that the transition maps preserve the way-below relation of the sections (
Proposition

3.2
), or equivalently the localic transition maps are finitary. However, we show that the internal way-below relation of an internal locally compact frame is not reducible to the way-below relations of its sections.
In a similar fashion, stable local compactness (preservation of finite infima by the extra left adjoint) can be transferred to the sections of the internal locale (
Proposition

3.8
).
In connection to the Hausdorff condition, i.e the closedness of the localic diagonal

L\to L\times L
, we obtain that, for a presheaf
M
of Hausdorff frames, the associated internal frame
\mathfrak{z}M
is internally Hausdorff (
Proposition

3.13
). In contrast, a counterexample, suggested to us by S. Henry, shows that the Hausdorffness of an internal frame need not be transferred to the sections.

Terminology, notation and conventions.

We now fix some terminology and notation that will be used hereafter.

Firstly, given an arrow
f
in a category
\mathcal{C}
, we use
\vartheta_{0}(f)
to denote the source of
f
, and
\vartheta_{1}(f)
for the target. Often we will work with objects internal to the presheaf topos
[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
, where we adopt the convention that
\mathcal{C}
has at least (weak) pullbacks. Instances at an object
a
of a natural transformation
\varphi
may be denoted either as
\varphi_{a}
or as
\varphi^{a}
, especially when a map
\varphi^{a}_{*}
adjoint to the latter occurs.

A
frame
is a complete lattice
L
satisfying the infinite distributive law

\textstyle u\wedge\bigvee_{i\in I}{v_{i}}=\bigvee_{i\in I}{(u\wedge v_{i})}

for all
u\in L
and subsets
\{v_{i}\mid i\in I\}\subseteq L
. A
frame homomorphism
is a map
L\to M
between frames that preserves finite infima and arbitrary suprema (in particular, frame homomorphisms preserve
0
and
1
). The category of frames and their homomorphisms is denoted by
\mathsf{Frm}
, and is complete and cocomplete (
[
12
, §IV.3&4]
).

The category
\mathsf{Loc}
is defined as the opposite of
\mathsf{Frm}
; the objects of
\mathsf{Loc}
are referred to as
locales
and its morphisms as
localic maps
.
We usually do not distinguish notationally between an object
L
in the category of locales and the same object in the category of frames (although it is customary when we denote by
X
the former, to write
OX
to denote the same object, this time regarded as a frame). Accordingly, the frame homomorphism corresponding to a localic map
f\colon L\to M
is denoted by
f
while we write
f_{*}
for its right adjoint (direct image of the localic map), which exists
since
f
preserves (finite infima and) all suprema.

An ideal of a lattice
L
is a subset
I\subseteq L
such that

0\in I,\qquad x\leq y\in I\Rightarrow x\in I,\quad\text{and}\quad x,\;y\in I\Rightarrow x\vee y\in I.

We denote by
{\mathrm{idl}}L
the poset of ideals, ordered by inclusion, which is a frame when
L
is a distributive lattice. A morphism of lattices
f\colon L\to M

induces a pair if adjoint maps
f[-]\dashv f^{-1}[-]\colon{\mathrm{idl}}M\to{\mathrm{idl}}L.
Here
f^{-1}[-]
denotes the inverse image of
I,
which is
an ideal when
I
is, and
f[I]
is
the ideal generated
by the set-theoretic direct image (we adopt this convention to make our notation less cumbersome). When needed we denote by

\langle S\rangle=\{x\in L\;|\;\exists s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}\in S\;x\leq s_{1}\vee\cdots\vee s_{n}\}

the ideal generated by
S\subseteq L.
In particular

\langle\bigcup_{i}J_{i}\rangle

is the ideal generated by a union of ideals. When
L,

M
are distributive lattices,
f^{-1}[-]
is a frame morphism.

The way-below relation
\ll
on a poset with directed suprema is defined as

x\ll y
if, whenever
y\leq\bigvee S,
with
S
directed, there is an
s\in S
so that
x\leq s.
A complete lattice is continuous if for all
x,
the set

\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{270.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}\!x=\{y\;|\;y\ll x\}
is directed and
x=\bigvee\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{270.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}\!x.

This can be equivalently expressed (
[
7
]
, VII 2.1) as requiring that the
supremum map
{\mathrm{idl}}L\to L
has a left adjoint (
x\mapsto\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{270.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}\!x
)
A frame is locally compact if it is a continuous lattice.

The concepts and results that we recalled above make sense and remain valid in the internal logic of a topos. As we shall see, concepts such as
local compactness and compactness will be defined internally in a topos via the poset of ideals.

2
Adjoining internal suprema

Frames that are internal in a presheaf topos
[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
are presheaves of frames satisfying further conditions (
[
9
, §VI.4]
,
[
8
, §C1.6.9]
, where we recall our convention that
\mathcal{C}
has all pullbacks).
In particular, the internal completeness of the poset
L
is captured by the existence, for each
f\colon b\to a\in\mathcal{C},

of a left adjoint
\Sigma_{f}\dashv Lf\colon La\to Lb,
such that a Beck-Chevalley condition is satisfied for pullbacks in
\mathcal{C}.

A morphism of internal sup-lattices
\varphi\colon L\to M
has to preserve suprema indexed by objects in the topos, so that all squares

\textstyle{Lb\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Sigma^{L}_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\varphi^{b}}
\textstyle{Mb\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Sigma^{M}_{f}}
\textstyle{La\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi^{a}}
\textstyle{Ma}

must commute. If
L
is moreover a frame, then
\Sigma_{f}\dashv Lf
is further required to satisfy the Frobenious reciprocity condition
\Sigma_{f}(Lf(u)\land v)=u\land\Sigma_{f}v
for all
u\in La
and
v\in Lb
(so that
L
as a functor takes values in the category of frames and open maps). We remark here that, as it is not too hard to see, these characterisations in fact remain valid if
\mathcal{C}
only has
weak
pullbacks and the Beck-Chevalley condition is required for the latter. (For cases where
\mathcal{C}
lacks even weak pullbacks, the reader is directed to
[
2
,
20
]
.)

As a result, there are forgetful functors

\displaystyle U\colon\mathsf{SupLat}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{SupLat}],

\displaystyle U\colon\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Frm}],

which are however not full. Indeed, not every natural transformation between suplattice or frame-valued functors coming from internal suplattices or frames respectively need preserve suprema in the internal sense described above. As aforementioned, as a particular case of the “geometrisation” procedure described in
[
2
,
18
,
19
]
, we can construct left adjoints to these forgetful functors. In this section, we provide a complete description of the left adjoint, as well as explicit proofs of its universal property.

Remark 2.1
.

Recall from
[
10
]
that the left adjoints
\Sigma_{f}
, satisfying Beck-Chevalley and Frobenius, are used in categorical logic to interpret existential quantification. From the logical perspective, a presheaf of frames can be understood as encoding the algebraic syntax of some theory of geometric logic
without
existential quantification (see
[
8
, §D1]
for more on the syntax of geometric logic). Our free internal frame on a presheaf of frames is therefore similar in spirit to the existential completions found in
[
17
,
21
]
, a connection that will be made concrete in
Remark

2.11
.

2.1
The free internal suplattice on a presheaf of suplattices.

For suplattices, a functor
\mathfrak{z}
in the opposite direction is defined as follows:

\mathfrak{z}L(a)=\left\{(u_{f})\in\prod_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\;L(\vartheta_{0}f)\;\middle|\;\forall g\colon\vartheta_{0}(g)\to\vartheta_{0}(f)\;(L(g)(u_{f})\leq u_{fg})\right\}.

For an arrow
r\colon b\to a
in
\mathcal{C},

\mathfrak{z}L(r)\colon\mathfrak{z}L(a)\to\mathfrak{z}L(b)
sends the tuple

(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
to
(u_{rh})_{\vartheta_{1}h=b}
. Each
\mathfrak{z}L(a)
is then a complete lattice with suprema given componentwise, as it is easy to verify. Since
L
takes values in
\mathsf{SupLat}
, a left adjoint
\Sigma^{\mathfrak{z}L}_{r}
to
\mathfrak{z}Lr
can be defined as follows

\textstyle\Sigma_{r}^{\mathfrak{z}L}(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=b}=\left(\bigvee_{rf=g}u_{f}\right)_{\vartheta_{1}g=a},

which we can easily confirm yields a left adjoint:

\displaystyle\textstyle\left(\bigvee_{rf=g}u_{f}\right)_{\vartheta_{1}g=a}\leq(v_{g})_{\vartheta_{1}g=a}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\textstyle\forall g\;\bigvee_{rf=g}u_{f}\leq v_{g}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall g\;\forall f\;(rf=g\Rightarrow u_{f}\leq v_{g}\;)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall f\;(u_{f}\leq v_{rf})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=b}\leq\mathfrak{z}L(r)(v_{rf})_{\vartheta_{1}f=b}.

Remark 2.2
.

Being a suplattice morphism, each

\mathfrak{z}\varphi_{*}^{a}\colon\mathfrak{z}M(a)\to\mathfrak{z}L(a).

\mathfrak{z}\varphi_{*}^{a}((v_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a})=(\varphi_{*}^{\vartheta_{0}f}(v_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}),

where, for every

denotes the right adjoint of the suplattice morphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M(\vartheta_{0}f)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L(\vartheta_{0}g)}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\ast}^{\vartheta_{0}f}}

Theorem 2.3
.

The functor
\mathfrak{z}\colon[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{SupLat}]\to\mathsf{SupLat}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
is left adjoint to the forgetful functor
U\colon\mathsf{SupLat}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]\to[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{SupLat}]
.

Proof.

The unit of the adjunction will be the morphism
\eta_{L}\colon L\to\mathfrak{z}L
defined for each
a\in\mathcal{C}
and
x\in La
by
\eta_{L}^{a}(x)\coloneqq(Lf(x))_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
. Note that this is well-defined and natural in
a
simply by functoriality of
L
, while it preserves suprema because the latter are computed componentwise in
\mathfrak{z}L(a)
and the transition maps
Lf
are morphisms in
\mathsf{SupLat}
. In addition, for any natural transformation of presheaves of suplattices
\varphi\colon L\to M\in[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{SupLat}]
and
a\in\mathcal{C}
,

we have that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}\varphi^{a}(\eta_{L}^{a}(x))

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{z}\varphi^{a}(Lf(x))_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}

\displaystyle=(\varphi^{\vartheta_{0}f}(Lf(x)))_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}

\displaystyle=(Mf(\varphi^{a}(x)))_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}

\displaystyle=\eta_{M}^{a}(\varphi^{a}(x)),

showing that
\eta
is natural in
L
.

Now consider a morphism
\varphi\colon L\to M\in[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{SupLat}]
with
M\in\mathsf{SupLat}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
an internal suplattice. We want to show that
\varphi
extends uniquely along
\eta_{L}
to a morphism
\bar{\varphi}\colon\mathfrak{z}L\to M
in
\mathsf{SupLat}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
. For each
a\in\mathcal{C}
, define

\textstyle\bar{\varphi}^{a}(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}=\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma^{M}_{f}\varphi^{\vartheta_{0}f}(u_{f}).

Concerning the naturality of
\bar{\varphi}
we have to show that, for

the square

\textstyle{\mathfrak{z}La\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{z}Lr}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\varphi}^{a}}
\textstyle{Ma\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Mr}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{z}Lb\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\varphi}^{b}}

2.2
The free internal frame on a presheaf of frames.

Next, we want to show that the adjunction established above restricts to one between frame-valued presheaves and internal frames in
[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
.

First of all, if
L\in[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Frm}]
, then it is easy to see that for every
a\in\mathcal{C}
the suplattice
\mathfrak{z}L(a)
also has finite infima. In particular, given elements
(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
and
(v_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
, the family
(u_{f}\wedge v_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
is also an element of
\mathfrak{z}L(a)
because for any
g
with
\vartheta_{1}g=\vartheta_{0}f
we have
Lg(u_{f}\wedge v_{f})\leq Lg(u_{f})\wedge Lg(v_{f})\leq u_{fg}\wedge v_{fg}
. Since both finite infima and arbitrary suprema are given componentwise, it immediately follows that
\mathfrak{z}L(a)
is a frame.

To check Frobenious reciprocity, consider an arrow
r\colon b\to a\in\mathcal{C}
and elements
(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
and
(v_{g})_{\vartheta_{1}g=b}
of
\mathfrak{z}L(a)
and
\mathfrak{z}L(b)
respectively. Then using the infinite distributive law which holds in the sections of
L
, we have that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{r}^{\mathfrak{z}L}\left(\mathfrak{z}Lr(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\wedge(v_{g})_{\vartheta_{1}g=b}\right)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{r}^{\mathfrak{z}L}\left((u_{rg})_{\vartheta_{1}g=b}\wedge(v_{g})_{\vartheta_{1}g=b}\right)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{r}^{\mathfrak{z}L}(u_{rg}\wedge v_{g})_{\vartheta_{1}g=b}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\left(\bigvee_{rg=f}(u_{rg}\wedge v_{g})\right)_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\left(\bigvee_{rg=f}(u_{f}\wedge v_{g})\right)_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\left(u_{f}\wedge\left(\bigvee_{rg=f}v_{g}\right)\right)_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}

\displaystyle=\textstyle(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\wedge\left(\bigvee_{rg=f}v_{g}\right)_{\vartheta_{1}g=b}

\displaystyle=\textstyle(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\wedge\Sigma_{r}^{\mathfrak{z}L}(v_{g})_{\vartheta_{1}g=b}.

Finally, given any
\varphi\colon L\to M\in[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Frm}]
, it is easy to see that each
\mathfrak{z}\varphi^{a}
preserves finite infima, simply because each
\varphi^{a}
does and they are given componentwise in
\mathfrak{z}L(a)
and
\mathfrak{z}M(a)
. Hence,
\mathfrak{z}\varphi
is a morphism in
\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
, and so we have shown that
\mathfrak{z}
restricts also to a functor
[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Frm}]\to\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
. We can then state the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.8
.

The functor
\mathfrak{z}\colon[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Frm}]\to\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
is left adjoint to the forgetful
U\colon\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]\to[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Frm}]
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the unit and co-unit of the adjunction at the level of suplattices restrict to the corresponding subcategories.

If
L\colon\mathcal{C}^{op}\to\mathsf{Frm}
is a presheaf of frames, then it is easy to see that each
\eta_{L}^{a}\colon La\to\mathfrak{z}L(a)
preserves finite infima, by virtue of them being given componentwise in
\mathfrak{z}L(a)
and the fact that all
Lf
preserve them.

For the co-unit
\varepsilon_{L}\colon\mathfrak{z}L\to L
, for any internal frame
L\in\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
, we need to show that each
\varepsilon_{L}^{a}
now also preserves finite infima. We have to show that for every
(u_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
and
(v_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
in
\mathfrak{z}L(a)

\textstyle\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}u_{f}\wedge\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}v_{f}\leq\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}(u_{f}\wedge v_{f}),

since the other inequality clearly holds. But

\textstyle\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}u_{f}\wedge\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}v_{f}=\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}g=a}(\Sigma_{f}u_{f}\wedge\Sigma_{g}v_{g})

for arrows
f,

g
with common codomain
a\in\mathcal{C}.
We show that each
\Sigma_{f}u_{f}\wedge\Sigma_{g}v_{g}

is dominated by
\Sigma_{fk}(u_{fk}\wedge v_{fk})
, where
k
is the map in the pullback

\textstyle{\cdot\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{k}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{b\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{\cdot\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{a.}

Indeed, we readily calculate that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{fk}(u_{fk}\wedge v_{fk})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Sigma_{fk}(u_{fk}\wedge Lkv_{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f}\Sigma_{k}(u_{fk}\wedge Lkv_{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f}(\Sigma_{k}u_{fk}\wedge v_{f})\quad\mbox{(Frobenious reciprocity)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f}(\Sigma_{k}u_{gh}\wedge v_{f})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f}(\Sigma_{k}Lhu_{g}\wedge v_{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f}(Lf\Sigma_{g}u_{g}\wedge v_{f})\quad\mbox{(Beck-Chevalley)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{g}u_{g}\wedge\Sigma_{f}v_{f},

completing the proof.

3

With the construction

in hand, we now turn to a study of the interaction of properties of an internal locale and properties on their sections.

3.1
Local compactness

An internal locale
L
is (internally) locally compact if there exists a map
\Lambda\colon L\to{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}}L
which is left adjoint to the
supremum map
, which we denote
S\colon{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}}{L}\to L
. We show that an internal locale that is locally compact has locally compact sections, and moreover transition maps preserve the way-below relation, but we also show that the internal way-below relation is not given pointwise.

Before continuing, note that an adjunction of maps (i.e. natural transformations) between internal posets can be entirely checked by passing to sections, i.e. an adjunction
\varphi\dashv\psi\colon X\to Y\in\mathsf{Pos}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
gives, for all
a\in\mathcal{C}
, an adjunction
\varphi_{a}\dashv\psi_{a}\colon Xa\to Ya
, and vice versa.

First, we explicitly describe the internal supremum map
S\colon{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}}L\to L
, using the fact that
{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}}L=\mathfrak{z}({\mathrm{idl}}\circ L)
. The map
S
is left adjoint to the canonical inclusion
\zeta\colon L\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}}L
, which is given by
\zeta_{a}(u)=(\downarrow\!Lf(u))_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\in\mathfrak{z}({\mathrm{idl}}\circ L)(a)
. Hence, the component of
S
at
a\in\mathcal{C}
must be given by
S_{a}\left((I_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\right)=\bigvee\{\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}[I_{f}]\}
(where we recall our convention that
\Sigma_{f}[I_{f}]
denotes the ideal generated by the direct image of
I_{f}
under
\Sigma_{f}
) since, given
u\in La
, by a direct calculation we have that:

\displaystyle S_{a}\left((I_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\right)\leq u

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\forall f\,\forall v\in I_{f}\,(\Sigma_{f}v\leq u),

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\forall f\,\forall v\in I_{f}\,(v\leq Lf(u)),

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\forall f\,(I_{f}\subseteq\,\downarrow\!Lf(u)),

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(I_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\leq\zeta_{a}(u).

Proposition 3.1
.

If
L\in\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
is locally compact, then all its sections are locally compact.

Proof.

We define a left adjoint
\lambda_{a}\colon La\to{\mathrm{idl}}(La)
to the supremum map
\bigvee\colon{\mathrm{idl}}(La)\to La
(of the section)
by
\lambda_{a}(u)=\langle\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}[(\Lambda_{a}u)_{f}]\rangle
.
Indeed, we have that

\displaystyle\textstyle\lambda_{a}(u)=\langle\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}[(\Lambda_{a}u)_{f}]\rangle\subseteq I

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall f\;(\Sigma_{f}[(\Lambda_{a}u)_{f}]\subseteq I)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall f\;((\Lambda_{a}u)_{f}\subseteq Lf[I])

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\Lambda_{a}(u)\leq(Lf[I])_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle u\leq S_{a}((Lf[I])_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}).

Finally, we note that
S_{a}((Lf[I])_{\vartheta_{1}f=a})=\bigvee\{\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}Lf[I]\}=\bigvee I
, since
I\subseteq\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}Lf[I]
and
\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}Lf[I]\subseteq I
, where the latter follows from the fact that
\Sigma_{f}
is left adjoint to
L_{f}
. Hence,
\lambda_{a}
does define a left adjoint. In other words, we have shown that
\langle\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}[(\Lambda_{a}u)_{f}]\rangle
defines the way-below segment of the element

∎

Example 3.5

(The internal way-below relation)
.

We have seen that local compactness of an internal frames implies local compactness of its sections. In a similar fashion, the internal way-below relation
\ll_{L}
of an internal locally compact frame
L
is contained in the way-below relation of each of its sections. More precisely, the internal way-below relation defines a subobject
{\ll_{L}}\subseteq L\times L
, and so for each object
a\in\mathcal{C}
we obtain a relation
{\ll_{L}}(a)\subseteq La\times La
that is contained in the way-below relation
{\ll_{La}}\subseteq La\times La
of the (locally compact) section
La
. Indeed
(u,v)\in{\ll_{L}}(a)
entails that for all
(I_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\in{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}}L(a)
, if

v\leq S_{a}((I_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a})=\bigvee\{\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}[I_{f}]\}
then
(\downarrow\!Lf(u))_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\leq(I_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
, which is equivalent to requiring that
u\in I_{\mathrm{id}_{a}}
. Now if
v\leq\bigvee I
, for an ideal
I\in{\mathrm{idl}}(La)
, then

\bigvee I\leq\bigvee\{\bigvee_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}[Lf[I]]\}
, and hence
u\in I
. Thus,
(u,v)\in{\ll_{La}}
.

However, the inclusion
{\ll_{L}}(a)\subseteq{\ll_{La}}
may be strict, as we now show in an example. Thus, the internal way-below relation is, in general, markedly weaker than the way-below relation of each section.

Consider the presheaf topos on
\mathbf{2}=\{0\leq 1\}
and the subobject classifier
\Omega\in[\mathbf{2}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
, which is a compact, and hence locally compact, internal frame. (Indeed, the subobject classifier is a compact frame in any topos, see
[
15
]
, in essence because
\Omega
is the internalisation of the one-point space; recall also that the topos of presheaves on
\mathbf{2}=\{0\leq 1\}
is equivalent to the topos of sheaves on the Sierpiński space.) A simple calculation (using
[
11
, §I.4]
or
Example

2.6
if necessary) shows that the sections of the subobject classifier
\Omega
are given by

\Omega_{0}=\{0\leq 1\},\qquad\Omega_{1}=\{0\leq\#\leq 1\}.

Since both sections are finite chains, the way-below relation on each of them coincides with the order relation, and in particular

{\ll_{\Omega_{1}}}=\{(0,0),(0,\#),(0,1),(\#,\#),(\#,1),(1,1)\}.

We will show that
(\#,\#)\not\in{\ll_{\Omega}}(1)
.

Note that the ideals on the sections
\Omega_{0}
,
\Omega_{1}
are, respectively,

{\mathrm{idl}}(\Omega_{0})=\{\{0\},\{0,1\}\},\qquad{\mathrm{idl}}(\Omega_{1})=\{\{0\},\{0,\#\},\{0,\#,1\}\},

and recall that an element of
{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathbf{2}}}\Omega(1)=\mathfrak{z}({\mathrm{idl}}\circ\Omega)(1)
, i.e. an internal ideal, consists of a pair
(I_{\leq},I_{\mathrm{id}_{1}})
where

,

, satisfying the lax-compatibility condition

. To demonstrate that

, it suffices to find such a pair

such that

but for which

. Taking

to be
\{0,1\}
and

to be

satisfies these conditions, since

but clearly

.

For any subsets
S,\;T\subseteq L
of a distributive lattice, for the corresponding ideals they generate in
L
we have that

\langle S\cap T\rangle=\langle S\rangle\cap\langle T\rangle.

Proof.

We only need to show the inclusion of
\langle S\rangle\cap\langle T\rangle
into
\langle S\cap T\rangle
. Let
x\in\langle S\rangle\cap\langle T\rangle
be such that

x\leq s_{1}\vee\cdots\vee s_{m},
with
s_{i}\in S
and
x\leq t_{1}\vee\cdots\vee t_{n},
with
t_{j}\in T.

Then

\textstyle x\leq\bigvee_{i}s_{i}\wedge\bigvee_{j}t_{j}=\bigvee_{i,j}(s_{i}\wedge t_{j}),

with each
s_{i}\wedge t_{j}\in S\cap T
, and hence
x\in\langle S\cap T\rangle.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For
L\in\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}],

if
I\subseteq La,

J\subseteq Lb
are ideals and
f\colon a\to b\in\mathcal{C},
then

\Sigma_{f}[I\cap Lf[J]]=\Sigma_{f}[I]\cap J,

i.e. we have Frobenius reciprocity for section-wise ideals.

Proof.

For the non-trivial inclusion, let

v\in\Sigma_{f}[I]\cap J,
so that
v\leq\Sigma_{f}u
with
u\in I
and
v\in J.

Then

v=v\wedge\Sigma_{f}u=\Sigma_{f}(Lfv\wedge u),

where
Lfv\wedge u\leq Lfv\in Lf[J],
while
Lfv\wedge u\leq u\in I.

This means that
Lfv\wedge u\in Lf[J]\cap I,
and so

v=\Sigma_{f}(Lfv\wedge u)\in\Sigma_{f}[I\cap Lf[J]]
, completing the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

If
L\in\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
is stably locally compact, then all its sections
La
are stably locally compact.

Proof.

The internal frame
L
being stably locally compact means that
\Lambda\colon L\to{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}}L
preserves binary infima, and hence
\Lambda_{a}\colon La\to{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}}L(a)
preserves binary infima for all objects
a\in\mathcal{C}
. Using the description of the pointwise way-below relation from
Proposition

3.1
and
Lemma

3.6
, we then have that

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(u)\cap\lambda_{a}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\langle\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}(\Lambda_{a}u)_{f}\rangle\cap\langle\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}(\Lambda_{a}w)_{f}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left({\textstyle\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}(\Lambda_{a}u)_{f})\cap\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}(\Lambda_{a}w)_{f}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{\textstyle\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=\vartheta_{1}g=a}}\left(\Sigma_{f}(\Lambda_{a}u)_{f}\cap\Sigma_{g}(\Lambda_{a}w)_{g}\right)\right\rangle

From this point on, the Frobenius reciprocity for pointwise-ideals from
Lemma

3.7
ensures that we can still follow the calculation in the proof of
Theorem

2.8
, and so we deduce that each
\Sigma_{f}(\Lambda_{a}u)_{f}\cap\Sigma_{g}(\Lambda_{a}w)_{g}
is contained in

\Sigma_{fk}[(\Lambda_{a}u)_{fk}\cap(\Lambda_{a}w)_{fk}]=\Sigma_{fk}[(\Lambda_{a}(u\wedge w)_{fk}]\subseteq\lambda_{a}(u\wedge w),

where
k
denotes the pullback of
g
along
f
. This completes the non-trivial inclusion
\lambda_{a}(u)\cap\lambda_{a}(w)\subseteq\lambda_{a}(u\land w)
, and thus we conclude that
\lambda_{a}(u)\cap\lambda_{a}(w)=\lambda_{a}(u\land w)
as desired.
∎

3.3
Compactness

A frame
L
is
compact
if whenever the supremum of an ideal is the top element of
L
, then the ideal contains the top element, and thus must be given by
\{L\}
itself. In other words the, equalizer

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{idl}}L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigvee}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

in the category of partially ordered sets,
of the supremum and the constantly equal to the top element is
\{L\}
. From this characterisation, we derive that the sections of an internally compact frame are compact as well, but we show also that the converse does not hold.

Proposition 3.9
.

If an internal frame
L\in\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
is compact, then all its sections
La
are compact.

Proof.

The diagrammatic condition expressing compactness above remains an equalizer when evaluated at any
a\in\mathcal{C}
, since limits and colimits in
[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
are computed pointwise.
Thus, whenever a lax compatible family
(I_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\in{\mathrm{idl}}_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}}L(a)=\mathfrak{z}({\mathrm{idl}}\circ L)(a)
is chosen for which the (internal) supremum

S_{a}((I_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a})=\bigvee{\textstyle\{\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}[I_{f}]\}}

is equal to the top element
1\in La
, then
(I_{f})_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
equals the family
(L(\vartheta_{0}f))_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
.
Now given an ideal
I\subseteq La
with
\bigvee I=1_{La}
, the family
(Lf[I])_{\vartheta_{1}=a}\in{\mathrm{idl}}L(a)
has the property that
S_{a}((Lf[I])_{\vartheta_{1}=a})=1
, because

I
participates in the union (via the
f=\mathrm{id}_{a}
component) and so

\bigvee I\leq\bigvee{\textstyle\{\bigcup_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}\Sigma_{f}[Lf[I]]\}}\leq\bigvee I=1.

Hence, by the internal compactness of
L
, we have
(Lf[I])_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}=(L(\vartheta_{0}f))_{\vartheta_{1}f=a}
. By taking the
\mathrm{id}_{a}
component of the tuple, we conclude that
I=La
, demonstrating that
La
is compact.
∎

By combining
Proposition

3.9
and
Proposition

3.8
, we get the following corollary:

Corollary 3.10
.

If
L\in\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
is stably compact, then so too are all its sections.

Example 3.11
.

The converse to
Proposition

3.9
does not hold. Consider the constant presheaf
L\colon\mathbf{2}^{op}\to\mathsf{Frm}
on a (non-trivial) compact frame
L
, which defines an internal frame of
[\mathbf{2}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
(recall that
\mathbf{2}
denotes the category
\{0\leq 1\}
). We can show that
L
is not internally compact by demonstrating that the equalizer of sets

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{z}({\mathrm{idl}}\circ L)({1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L({1})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{\mathbf{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

contains, in addition to the maximal internal ideal
(L)_{\vartheta_{0}f={1}}
, a non-maximal tuple
(I_{f})_{\vartheta_{0}f={1}}\in\mathfrak{z}({\mathrm{idl}}\circ L)({1})
.

We take the family
(I_{\leq},I_{\mathrm{id}_{1}})
where
I_{\mathrm{id}_{1}}=\{0\}\neq L
and
I_{\leq}=L
, which is a lax-compatible family of ideals, and so
(I_{\leq},I_{\mathrm{id}_{1}})\in\mathfrak{z}({\mathrm{idl}}\circ L)({1})
. We then calculate that

S_{1}((I_{\leq},I_{\mathrm{id}_{1}}))=\bigvee\left\{{\Sigma_{\leq}[L]\cup\Sigma_{\mathrm{id}_{1}}[\{0\}]}\right\}=\bigvee\{L\cup\{0\}\}=1,

and yet
(L)_{\vartheta_{0}f={1}}\neq(I_{f})_{\vartheta_{0}f={1}}
, proving that
L
is not internally compact as desired.

3.4
Hausdorffness

A locale is Hausdorff if its diagonal
\Delta\colon L\to L\times L
is closed.
A map of locales
f\colon L\to M
is closed if, for all
u\in L,

v\in M,

f_{*}(u\vee f^{*}v)=f_{*}u\vee v
, which can be expressed diagrammatically as the commutativity of the following diagram of posets

\textstyle{L\times M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{*}\times\mathrm{id}_{M}}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\textstyle{M\times M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{-\vee-}
\textstyle{L\times L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{-\vee-}
\textstyle{L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{*}}
\textstyle{M.}

Hence a map
f\colon L\to M\in\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
is closed if and only if

f_{a}\colon La\to Ma
is closed for all
a\in\mathcal{C}
. We show that if
L
is a presheaf of Hausdorff frames, then
\mathfrak{z}L
is an internally Hausdorff frame. As a consequence, we derive explicitly the well-known fact that the subobject classifier of a presheaf topos is internally compact and Hausdorff, and thus we are able to give an example, suggested to us by S. Henry, where the Hausdorffness of an internal frame is
not
inherited by its sections.

Proposition 3.12
.

Given a natural transformation
\varphi\colon L\to M\in[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Frm}]
such, that for all objects
a\in\mathcal{C}
, the component
\varphi^{a}\colon La\to Ma
is closed,
then the induced map
\mathfrak{z}\varphi^{a}\colon\mathfrak{z}L(a)\to\mathfrak{z}M(a),
is a closed frame map, and hence
\mathfrak{z}\varphi\colon\mathfrak{z}L\to\mathfrak{z}M
is a closed morphism of internal locales.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the fact that the frame structure on both
\mathfrak{z}L(a)
and
\mathfrak{z}M(a)
, the action of
\mathfrak{z}\varphi^{a}
, and the action of
\mathfrak{z}\varphi^{a}_{\ast}
are all computed pointwise (see
Section

2
).
∎

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
L\in[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Frm}]
be a presheaf of frames such that each section
La
is Hausdorff. Then
\mathfrak{z}L
is Hausdorff.

Proof.

Colimits in
[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Frm}]
are computed pointwise, and so the coproduct of two presheaves of frames
L
,
M
is given by the presheaf
a\mapsto La\otimes Ma
, which we denote by
L\otimes M
.
The functor
\mathfrak{z}
, being a left adjoint, preserves colimits and so
\mathfrak{z}(L\otimes M)\cong\mathfrak{z}L\sqcup\mathfrak{z}M,
where
\mathfrak{z}L\sqcup\mathfrak{z}M
denotes the coproduct of
\mathfrak{z}L,\mathfrak{z}M\in\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]
as internal frames.
Since each
La
is Hausdorff, we have that the co-diagonal

\nabla_{La}\colon La\otimes La\to La
is closed. By
Proposition

3.12
, each

\mathfrak{z}(L\sqcup L)(a)\cong\mathfrak{z}(La\otimes La)\to\mathfrak{z}La
is closed and thus
the co-diagonal
\mathfrak{z}L\sqcup\mathfrak{z}L\to L
is closed, i.e
\mathfrak{z}L
is Hausdorff.
∎

In particular, consider the constant presheaf
\mathbf{2}\colon\mathcal{C}^{op}\to\mathsf{Frm}
on the two element frame, as considered in
Example

2.6
. Each section is Hausdorff, and so
\mathfrak{z}\mathbf{2}
, i.e. the subobject classifier
\Omega\in\mathsf{Frm}[\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
, is Hausdorff too.

Example 3.14
.

We close with an example that the sections
La
of an internally Hausdorff frame
L
need not be Hausdorff. Our thanks go to S. Henry for pointing this out to us. Consider the example above of the subobject classifier
\Omega
for the particular presheaf topos
[\mathbf{2}^{op},\mathsf{Set}]
. We have just seen that
\Omega
is a Hausdorff internal frame. But it is easily calculated that
\Omega(1)
has three elements, and hence is isomorphic to the Sierpiński frame, which is obviously not Hausdorff.
