Optimizing the Principal Coefficient of Elliptic Equations using L p L^{p} -regularity, p < ∞ p<\infty
Ala’ Alalabi, Lorena Bociu, Paul Manns

Abstract
We study coefficient identification problems for elliptic partial differential equations with total variation regularization and control constraints. Existing related literature relies on continuity and differentiability properties of the control-to-state operator with respect to the L ∞ L^{\infty} -norm. While this is sufficient for deriving optimality conditions, it is not well-suited for numerical algorithms, as it neglects the spatial extent of perturbations and leads to a qualitative discrepancy compared to L q L^{q} -norms with q < ∞ q<\infty . In this work, we address this gap by exploiting W 1 , s W^{1,s} -regularity results to establish differentiability properties of the control-to-state operator with respect to L q L^{q} -norms for finite q q . Based on this framework, we derive first- and second-order differentiability results for the reduced objective functional and establish first-order optimality conditions involving a restricted subdifferential characterization of the total variation seminorm and corresponding regularity of the associated multipliers. Building on this, we analyze a nonsmooth trust-region method based on an L r L^{r} -trust region for r > 0.5 ​ q r>0.5q and prove its convergence to first-order stationary points.

Although it is well known in the community (see, e.g.,

We believe that this may be explained by the fact that the optimality conditions satisfied at the points one aims to recover are essentially unchanged, whether or not additional regularity of the control-to-state operator has been verified.

The situation changes, however, when one aims to prove (global) convergence of nonlinear programming-type algorithms to stationary points. If a globalization mechanism such as a trust region or a proximal operator is employed, the analysis must exploit regularity with respect to the norm associated with the trust region or proximal operator.
We believe that using the

-norm for this purpose is impractical, because it accounts only for the maximal pointwise height difference between two successive control iterates, while completely neglecting the measure of the region on which this difference occurs. In particular, a step that changes the control from

to

u_{\ell}
to
u_{u}

on the whole domain

-norm
is the only one with this behavior so that there is a qualitative gap between using the
an

-norm with

In this article we address this gap in the literature and make the following contributions.
Using the aforementioned regularity theory of Gröger

2
Analysis of State and Adjoint Equations

We begin the section by introducing the notation used throughout
the paper.

Let
C
be a convex set and

be a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
.
We say that a function

is differentiable at

with respect to

if there exists

such that

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\to 0\\
u+\phi\in C\end{subarray}}{\lim}\frac{\big|j(u+\phi)-j(u)-\langle j^{\prime}(u),\phi\rangle_{X^{*},X}\big|}{\|\phi\|_{X}}=0.

Remark 2.1
.

Although we define differentiability in terms of feasible variations around the linearization point

, we note that one could alternatively embed the feasible set into a larger set that is open in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)

and then work with this enlarged set as it is done, for example, in
[
6
]
. However, this does not eliminate the main difficulties in our analysis, which arise from the fact that the differentiation at

is performed with respect to
L^{q}(\Omega)
-norm, with

If

for

, we write

\nabla_{R}j(u)
for the representative of
j^{\prime}(u)\in X^{*}
in

, where

is the Hölder conjugate of

.

We recall that for an open set
\Omega
,

is
the space of regular Borel measures with finite variation
norm, which is the dual space of
C_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

For an integrability index

, we denote its Hölder conjugate index by

.
By

be the dual of

We use

In the interest of a clean exposition, we often drop the domain of a space
when stating the Lebesgue or Sobolev norm in

Control-to-state Operator

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

1.

Remark 2.6

By inspecting the statement and proof of Theorem 1 in

Lemma 2.8

Let

For all

Let

with

defined in (

If we test with

from above implies

\displaystyle u_{\ell}\|\nabla(y_{1}-y_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{u_{1}}\nabla(y_{1}-y_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\nabla(y_{1}-y_{2})\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla y_{2}\|_{L^{s}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2s/(s-2)}}

where

C_{1}(s,f_{D},f)\coloneqq C(s)\bigl(\|f\|_{W^{-1,s}(\Omega)}+\|f_{D}\|_{W^{1-1/s,s}(\tilde{\Gamma})}\bigr)
.
From the definition of
S
in (
8

y_{1}-y_{2}=\bigl(A(u_{1})^{-1}F_{u_{1}}-A(u_{2})^{-1}F_{u_{2}}\bigr)\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega),

and hence, by the Poincaré inequality on

(see, e.g., Theorem 1.9 in
[

\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\tilde{y}_{1}-\tilde{y}_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{2}(\Omega,\tilde{\Gamma})\|\nabla(\tilde{y}_{1}-\tilde{y}_{2})\|_{L^{2}}=C_{2}(\Omega,\tilde{\Gamma})\|\nabla(y_{1}-y_{2})\|_{L^{2}},

2.3
Adjoint Equation

General adjoint calculus (formally) gives (under sufficient differentiability assumptions):

(F\circ S)^{\prime}(u)=-F^{\prime}(S(u))\bigl(\partial_{1}E(S(u)-\tilde{f}_{D},u)\bigr)^{-1}\,\partial_{2}E(S(u)-\tilde{f}_{D},u).

Since

(\partial_{1}E(S(u)-\tilde{f}_{D},u))^{*}=A(u)

must hold when both sides are well defined, we can introduce the adjoint
state
p=p(y,u)\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
for
y=S(u)

as the solution to

A(u)p=-F^{\prime}(y)\in W^{-1,2}(\Omega),

which in variational form is given by

\int_{\Omega}u\,\nabla p\cdot\nabla v\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\langle-F^{\prime}(y),v\rangle\quad\text{ for all }v\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega),

and corresponds to the mixed boundary value problem

\begin{cases}\operatorname{div}(u\nabla p)=-F^{\prime}(y)&\text{in }\Omega\\
p=0&\text{on }\tilde{\Gamma}\\
u\nabla p\cdot\nu=0&\text{on }\Gamma.\end{cases}

(10)

We note that the regularity considerations for the state
equation also apply here and yield a solution

p\in W^{1,s}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
for the same exponent
s>2
as in
Proposition

2.5
,
if we assume that
-F^{\prime}(y)\in W^{-1,s}(\Omega)
holds. Thus, the implied adjoint solution
operator

S_{p}:W^{1,s}(\Omega)\times U_{\textrm{ad}}\to W^{1,s}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega),\ \ S_{p}(y,u)=p

is well defined and bounded provided that
-F^{\prime}(y)
is uniformly
bounded in
W^{-1,s}(\Omega)
for
y\in\{S(u):u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}\}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
F:W^{1,2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable
with Lipschitz continuous derivative
F^{\prime}:W^{1,2}(\Omega)\to W^{-1,2}(\Omega)

and
\sup\{\|F^{\prime}(S(u))\|_{W^{-1,s}}:u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}\}<\infty
.
Then the following properties hold:

1.

For all
q\geq\frac{2s}{s-2}
and all
y
with
-F^{\prime}(y)\in W^{-1,s}(\Omega)
, the map
S_{p}(y,\cdot):(U_{\textrm{ad}},\|\cdot\|_{L^{q}})\to W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)

is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant depending on

,

\|F^{\prime}(y)\|_{W^{-1,s}}
, and the coercivity constant
u_{\ell}
.

2.

For all
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
, the map
S_{p}(\cdot,u):W^{1,2}(\Omega)\to W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)

is Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

The first claim follows from arguments similar to those in
Lemma

2.8
.
The second claim follows from the assumed Lipschitz continuity of
F^{\prime}

and
Proposition

2.5
.
∎

The next corollary follows directly from

2.10
.

.

,

,

, and the data.

,

and combine the Lipschitz continuity of

from

2.4
First Order Derivatives of
S
and
F\circ S

By Proposition
2.5
we know that there exists
s>2
such that the control-to-state map

S:U_{ad}\to W^{1,s}(\Omega)
is well-defined. Moreover, by Lemma
2.8
we have that for all
q\geq\frac{2s}{s-2}
,

S:(U_{\textrm{ad}},\|\cdot\|_{L^{q}})\to W^{1,2}(\Omega)

is Lipschitz continuous. Next we prove that
S
is of class
C^{1}
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
and assume that
q\geq\frac{2s}{s-2}
.
Then the control-to-state operator

S:(U_{\textrm{ad}},\|\cdot\|_{L^{q}})\to W^{1,2}(\Omega)

is Fréchet differentiable at
u

w.r.t. feasible variations. Moreover, given
u\in U_{ad}
and
\phi\in L^{q}(\Omega)
s.t.
u+\phi\in U_{ad}
, we have that
S^{\prime}(u)[\phi]
is the unique solution to

\int_{\Omega}u\nabla z\cdot\nabla v\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla y\cdot\nabla v\,dx,\quad\text{ for all }v\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega).

(11)

Proof.

Let
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
, and let
\phi\in L^{q}(\Omega)
such that
u+\phi\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
.
The bilinear form

a(z,v):=\int_{\Omega}u\nabla z\cdot\nabla v\,dx

is continuous on

W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)\times W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
since

u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and coercive because

u\geq u_{\ell}>0
almost everywhere in
\Omega
.
Let
G_{\phi}:W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be given by
\langle G_{\phi},v\rangle:=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla y\cdot\nabla vdx
.
Using
Hölder’s Inequality with exponents
q
,
t
and
2
such that

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{2}=1,
we obtain (due to the fact that
t\in(2,s]
) that

\left|\langle G_{\phi},v\rangle\right|\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla y\|_{L^{t}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla y\|_{L^{s}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}},

which shows that
G_{\phi}\in W^{-1,2}(\Omega)
. Hence, by Lax–Milgram Theorem,
there exists a unique solution

z_{u,\phi}\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
to (
11
).
Moreover, we have that
G_{\phi}\in W^{-1,s}(\Omega)
. Indeed,
using Hölder’s inequality together with the
W^{1,s}
-regularity of
y

and the
L^{\infty}
-regularity of
\phi
, we obtain

|\langle G_{\phi},v\rangle|\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla y\|_{L^{s}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{s^{\prime}}}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|y\|_{W^{1,s}}\|v\|_{W^{1,s^{\prime}}}.

Thus Proposition
2.5
implies that

z_{u,\phi}\in W^{1,s}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega),
and there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\|z_{u,\phi}\|_{W^{1,s}}\leq C_{1}\|G_{\phi}\|_{W^{-1,s}}\leq C_{1}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|y\|_{W^{1,s}}.

Therefore, the unique solution

z_{u,\phi}\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
satisfies

\|z_{u,\phi}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}\|y\|_{W^{1,s}}.

Now for all
v\in W_{\tilde{\Gamma}}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, the homogenized states
\tilde{y}=y-\tilde{f}_{D}
and
\tilde{y}_{\phi}=y_{\phi}-\tilde{f}_{D}
satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\nabla\tilde{y}\cdot\nabla v\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx-\int_{\Omega}u\nabla\tilde{f}_{D}\cdot\nabla v\,dx

(12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(u+\phi)\nabla\tilde{y}_{\phi}\cdot\nabla v\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(u+\phi)\nabla\tilde{f}_{D}\cdot\nabla v\,dx

(13)

Subtracting (
12
) from (
13
) yields the identity

\int_{\Omega}u\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y})\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla\tilde{y}_{\phi}\cdot\nabla v\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla\tilde{f}_{D}\cdot\nabla v\,dx.

Subtracting
\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla\tilde{y}_{\phi}\cdot\nabla v\,dx
from both sides and
using
\tilde{y}_{\phi}+\tilde{f}_{D}=y_{\phi}
, the identity becomes

\int_{\Omega}u\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y})\cdot\nabla v\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla y_{\phi}\cdot\nabla v\,dx.

(14)

Combining (
14
) with (
11
) we obtain

\int_{\Omega}u\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y}-z_{u,\phi})\cdot\nabla v\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla(y_{\phi}-y)\cdot\nabla v\,dx,\ \forall v\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega).

(15)

From (
15
), using the lower bound on
u
and the fact that
y_{\phi}-y=\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y}
, we obtain

\displaystyle u_{\ell}

\displaystyle\|y_{\phi}-y-z_{u,\phi}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}}\leq C_{p}\Big|\int_{\Omega}u\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y}-z_{u,\phi})\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y}-z_{u,\phi})dx\Big|

\displaystyle=C_{p}\Big|\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y})\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y}-z_{u,\phi})dx\Big|\leq C_{p}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y})\|_{L^{t}}\|\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y}-z_{u,\phi})\|_{L^{2}},

where
t\in(2,s)
is such that

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{2}=1
. Then using interpolation between
L^{2}
and
L^{s}
, there exists

\theta=\frac{2(s-t)}{t(s-2)}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle u_{\ell}

\displaystyle\|y_{\phi}-y-z_{u,\phi}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}}\leq C_{p}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla(y_{\phi}-y)\|_{L^{2}}^{\theta}\|\nabla(y_{\phi}-y)\|_{L^{s}}^{1-\theta}\|\nabla(\tilde{y}_{\phi}-\tilde{y}-z_{u,\phi})\|_{L^{2}}.

(17)

Finally, using the the Lipschitz continuity of the control-to-state operator
from Lemma
2.8
we obtain

\displaystyle u_{\ell}

\displaystyle\|y_{\phi}-y-z_{\phi}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}^{1+\theta}C(s)(\|f\|_{W^{-1,s}}+\|f_{D}\|_{W^{1-1/s,s}(\tilde{\Gamma})})^{1-\theta}\|y_{\phi}-y-z_{\phi}\|_{W^{1,2}},

for real constant
C>0
, which allows us to conclude that

\frac{\|y_{\phi}-y-z_{\phi}\|_{W^{1,2}}}{\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}}\to 0,\qquad\text{as }\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}\to 0.

Thus
S
is Fréchet differentiable at
u
and

S^{\prime}(u)[\phi]=z_{u,\phi}
.

∎

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
q>\frac{2s}{s-2}
, and
F:W^{1,2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable with Lipschitz
continuous derivative
F^{\prime}:W^{1,2}(\Omega)\to W^{-1,2}(\Omega)
and
\sup\{\|F^{\prime}(S(u))\|_{W^{-1,s}}:u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}\}<\infty
.
Then the mapping
F\circ S:(U_{\textrm{ad}},\|\cdot\|_{L^{q}})\to\mathbb{R}
is
Fréchet differentiable at
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
with respect to feasible variations. Specifically, with
y=S(u)
and
p=S_{p}(y,u)
, we have

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}\to 0\\
u+\phi\in U_{\textrm{ad}}\end{subarray}}{\lim}\frac{|F(S(u+\phi))-F(y)-(\phi\nabla y,\nabla p)_{L^{2}}|}{\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}}=0

Moreover, the mapping

U_{\textrm{ad}}\ni u\mapsto\nabla y(u)\cdot\nabla p(u)\in L^{\frac{2s}{s+2}}(\Omega)

is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the
L^{q}(\Omega)
-norm.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.12
and the assumptions on
F
we obtain that
(F\circ S)^{\prime}(u)[\phi]=\langle F^{\prime}(y),z_{u,\phi}\rangle
, for
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
, and
\phi\in L^{q}(\Omega)
such that
u+\phi\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
.

Now we use
the adjoint equation (
10
) (tested with
z_{u,\phi}
) and the variational form (
11
) of

z_{u,\phi}
(tested with
p
) to deduce that

\langle F^{\prime}(y),z_{u,\phi}\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}u\nabla p\cdot\nabla z_{u,\phi}=\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla y\cdot\nabla p.

Now, let
u_{1}
,
u_{2}\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
and set

y_{1}\coloneqq S(u_{1})
,
y_{2}\coloneqq S(u_{2})
,

p_{1}\coloneqq S_{p}(y_{1},u_{1})
, and

p_{2}\coloneqq S_{p}(y_{2},u_{2})
.
By Hölder’s inequality with

\frac{s+2}{2s}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{s},

we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla y_{1}\cdot\nabla p_{1}-\nabla y_{2}\cdot\nabla p_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{2s}{s+2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\nabla y_{1}-\nabla y_{2})\cdot\nabla p_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{2s}{s+2}}}+\|\nabla y_{2}\cdot(\nabla p_{1}-\nabla p_{2})\|_{L^{\frac{2s}{s+2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla y_{1}-\nabla y_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla p_{1}\|_{L^{s}}+\|\nabla y_{2}\|_{L^{s}}\|\nabla p_{1}-\nabla p_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

By Proposition
2.5
, there exists a constant
C_{y}>0
,
depending only on the data, the domain, and the admissible bounds

u_{\ell},u_{u}
, such that

\|y(u)\|_{W^{1,s}(\Omega)}\leq C_{y},\;\forall u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}.

Similarly, the assumption

\sup_{u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}}\|F^{\prime}(S(u))\|_{W^{-1,s}(\Omega)}<\infty

is used to obtain a uniform
W^{1,s}
-bound for the adjoint variables.
Note that
p(u)
solves

A(u)p(u)=-F^{\prime}(S(u)),

the
W^{1,s}
-regularity estimate for the adjoint equation yields

\|p(u)\|_{W^{1,s}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F^{\prime}(S(u))\|_{W^{-1,s}(\Omega)}

for all
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
. Hence, there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\|p(u)\|_{W^{1,s}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\;\forall u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}.

Hence,
\|\nabla p_{1}\|_{L^{s}}\leq C_{p},\;\|\nabla y_{2}\|_{L^{s}}\leq C_{y}.
Combining these uniform bounds with

Lemmas

2.8
and
2.11
, we obtain

\|\nabla y_{1}-\nabla y_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla p_{1}-\nabla p_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{q}}.

Therefore,

\|\nabla y_{1}\cdot\nabla p_{1}-\nabla y_{2}\cdot\nabla p_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{2s}{s+2}}}\leq C\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{q}},

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
u_{1},u_{2}\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
.
This proves the final claim.

∎

.

Let
j:U_{\textrm{ad}}\to\mathbb{R},

For all
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
and
\phi

2.13

2.5
Second Order Derivatives of
S
and
F\circ S

From the previous section we have that for
u\in U_{ad}
and
\phi\in L^{q}(\Omega)
such that
u+\phi\in U_{ad}
,
z_{u,\phi}=S^{\prime}(u)[\phi]\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
is the unique solution to (
11
). Now we obtain the following property for
z_{u,\phi}
, which will be used in the proof of the second-order
differentiability of the control-to-state operator.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
q\geq\frac{2s}{s-2}
and let
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)

be a feasibly approximable direction as in

Theorem

2.13
.
For every
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
, let
y(u)=S(u)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
denote the state.
Then there exists a constant
L>0
such that for all

u_{1},u_{2}\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
we have

\|z_{u_{1},\phi}-z_{u_{2},\phi}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq L\max\{1,\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
u_{1}
,
u_{2}\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
be arbitrary and set

y_{i}\coloneqq S(u_{i}),\;z_{i}\coloneqq z_{u_{i},\phi}=S^{\prime}(u_{i})[\phi]

for
i\in\{1,2\}
. By definition,
z_{i}
satisfies (
11
) with
u=u_{i}
and
y(u_{i})=y_{i}
.
We therefore subtract (
11
) for
i=2
from (
11
) for
i=1

for arbitrary test functions
v\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
and obtain

\int_{\Omega}u_{1}\nabla z_{1}\cdot\nabla v\,dx-\int_{\Omega}u_{2}\nabla z_{2}\cdot\nabla v\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla(y_{1}-y_{2})\cdot\nabla v\,dx.

Subtracting
\int_{\Omega}u_{1}\nabla z_{2}\cdot\nabla v\,dx
from both sides and reorganizing terms yields

\int_{\Omega}u_{1}\nabla(z_{1}-z_{2})\cdot\nabla v\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\nabla(y_{1}-y_{2})\cdot\nabla v\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(u_{1}-u_{2})\nabla z_{2}\cdot\nabla v\,dx.

The choice
v=z_{1}-z_{2}\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
then yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{1}|\nabla(z_{1}-z_{2})|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\nabla(y_{1}-y_{2})\cdot\nabla(z_{1}-z_{2})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(u_{1}-u_{2})\nabla z_{2}\cdot\nabla(z_{1}-z_{2})\,dx.

Using the fact that
u_{1}\geq u_{\ell}>0
a.e. in
\Omega,
together with similar estimates
as in the proof of
Lemma

2.8
, we obtain

u_{\ell}\|\nabla(z_{1}-z_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla(y_{1}-y_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla z_{2}\|_{L^{t}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

(18)

with
t\coloneqq 2q/(q-2)
so that
1/q+1/t+1/2=1.
Since
q\geq\frac{2s}{s-2}
, we have
t\leq s
. Hence, by Hölder’s inequality and the continuous embedding
L^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{t}(\Omega)
,
\|\nabla z_{2}\|_{L^{t}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|\nabla z_{2}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Using
Lemma

2.8
, we obtain that
\|\nabla(y_{1}-y_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq L_{S}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, inequality (
18
) yields

u_{\ell}\|\nabla(z_{1}-z_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\Big(L_{S}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C\|\nabla z_{2}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

It remains to uniformly bound
\|\nabla z_{2}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}
in terms of
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}.
Define the functional
G\in W^{-1,s}(\Omega)
by

\langle G,v\rangle_{W^{-1,s},W^{1,s^{\prime}}}:=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla y_{2}\cdot\nabla v\,dx\qquad\forall v\in W^{1,s^{\prime}}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega).

Then

\displaystyle\|G\|_{W^{-1,s}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|v\|_{W^{1,s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\left|\langle G,v\rangle_{W^{-1,s},W^{1,s^{\prime}}}\right|\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla y_{2}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Consequently, the same regularity theory as for the state equation
applies and we
deduce from
Proposition

2.5

and the assumptions on
\phi
that

\|z_{2}\|_{W^{1,s}}\leq K_{2}(s)\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla y_{2}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K_{2}(s)\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}C_{y}

with
C_{y}\coloneqq\|\tilde{f}_{D}\|_{W^{1,s}}+K_{2}(s)\bigl(\|f\|_{W^{-1,s}}+u_{u}\|\nabla\tilde{f}_{D}\|_{L^{s}}\bigr)
.
∎

To prove second-order differentiability of the control-to-state
operator, we later need to estimate terms of the form

\phi\nabla z_{u,\psi}

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. For this purpose, the
W^{1,2}
-regularity of

z_{u,\psi}
is not sufficient, and we require the following improved

W^{1,\sigma}
-regularity result for first variations. This is presented in the next lemma.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
u\in U_{\rm ad}
and let
y=S(u)
.
Let
\sigma\in(2,s)
and set
\sigma^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}
.
Choose
\hat{q}=\hat{q}(\sigma)\in(1,\infty)
such that

1/s+1/{\hat{q}}=1/\sigma
.
Then, for every
\phi\in L^{\hat{q}}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique

z_{u,\phi}=S^{\prime}(u)[\phi]\in W^{1,\sigma}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)

satisfying

\int_{\Omega}u\nabla z_{u,\phi}\cdot\nabla v\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla y\cdot\nabla v\,dx\qquad\forall v\in W^{1,\sigma^{\prime}}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on

\Omega
,
\sigma
, the admissible bounds

u_{\ell},u_{u}
, and the data
f,f_{D}
, such that

\|z_{u,\phi}\|_{W^{1,\sigma}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{\hat{q}}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

First note that

\hat{q}=\frac{s\sigma}{s-\sigma}.

Since
\sigma\in(2,s)
, we obtain

\hat{q}>\frac{2s}{s-2}.

Hence, the requirement from

Theorem

2.13

is satisfied. Let
\phi\in L^{\hat{q}}(\Omega)
.
Since
y=S(u)\in W^{1,s}(\Omega)
, we have that
\nabla y\in L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}).

By the choice of
\hat{q}
,
1/s+1/{\hat{q}}=1/\sigma
,
and therefore Hölder’s inequality yields

\phi\nabla y\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We now define the functional

G_{1}\in W^{-1,\sigma}(\Omega)

by

\langle G_{1},v\rangle_{W^{-1,\sigma},W^{1,\sigma^{\prime}}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}}:=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\nabla y\cdot\nabla v\,dx\qquad\forall v\in W^{1,\sigma^{\prime}}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega).

Then, for all

v\in W^{1,\sigma^{\prime}}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
,

|\langle G_{1},v\rangle|\leq\|\phi\nabla y\|_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\sigma^{\prime}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and hence

|\langle G_{1},v\rangle|\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\hat{q}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla y\|_{L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{W^{1,\sigma^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

Consequently,

\|G_{1}\|_{W^{-1,\sigma}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{\hat{q}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla y\|_{L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}.
Since
\sigma\in(2,s)
, Proposition 2.4 applies.
Therefore, there exists a unique

z_{u,\phi}\in W^{1,\sigma}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)

such that

\int_{\Omega}u\nabla z_{u,\phi}\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\langle G_{1},v\rangle\qquad\forall v\in W^{1,\sigma^{\prime}}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega),

and

The following theorem establishes
the second-order differentiability of the reduced objective

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
q>\frac{2s}{s-2}
and
\tilde{q}>\tilde{q}_{*}(q)
(see
Lemma

2.17
). Let
F:W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}

be twice continuously Fréchet differentiable and assume that

F^{\prime}(y)\in W^{-1,2}(\Omega),\qquad F^{\prime\prime}(y)\in\mathcal{L}(W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega),W^{-1,2}(\Omega))\qquad\text{for all }y\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega),

with
F^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime\prime}
Lipschitz continuous on
W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
.

Then
j\coloneqq F\circ S:(U_{\textrm{ad}},\|\cdot\|_{L^{\tilde{q}}(\Omega)})\to\mathbb{R}

is twice Fréchet differentiable on
U_{\textrm{ad}}
for feasible variations.
More precisely, for
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
, let

y\coloneqq S(u),\qquad p\coloneqq S_{p}(y,u),\qquad z_{u,\phi}\coloneqq S^{\prime}(u)[\phi],\qquad z_{u,\psi}\coloneqq S^{\prime}(u)[\psi],\qquad z_{u,\phi,\psi}\coloneqq S^{\prime\prime}(u)[\phi,\psi].

Then, for all
\phi
,
\psi\in U-u
, we have

\displaystyle j^{\prime}(u)[\phi]=\langle F^{\prime}(y),z_{u,\phi}\rangle_{W^{-1,2},W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}}=\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\nabla y\cdot\nabla p\,dx,

(30)

and

\displaystyle j^{\prime\prime}(u)[\phi,\psi]

\displaystyle=\langle F^{\prime\prime}(y)z_{u,\psi},z_{u,\phi}\rangle_{W^{-1,2},W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}}+\int_{\Omega}\psi\,\nabla z_{u,\phi}\cdot\nabla p\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\nabla z_{u,\psi}\cdot\nabla p\,dx.

(31)

Moreover,

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\to 0\\
u+\eta\in U_{\textrm{ad}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\bigl|j(u+\eta)-j(u)-j^{\prime}(u)[\eta]-\frac{1}{2}\,j^{\prime\prime}(u)[\eta,\eta]\bigr|}{\|\eta\|_{L^{\tilde{q}}(\Omega)}^{2}}=0,

and the bilinear map
j^{\prime\prime}(u):L^{\tilde{q}}(\Omega)\times L^{\tilde{q}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}

is bounded.

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.18
, the control-to-state operator

S:(U_{\textrm{ad}},\|\cdot\|_{L^{\tilde{q}}})\to W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)

is twice Fréchet differentiable for feasible variations.

Due to the assumed twice continuous Fréchet differentiability of
F
and the fact that
the remainder term estimate for the inner derivative holds for feasible variations,
the chain rule in Banach spaces may be applied to deduce that the reduced functional

j
is twice Fréchet differentiable for feasible variations.

Specifically, we obtain for
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
and

that

for the first derivative and, recalling

,

\displaystyle j^{\prime\prime}(u)[\phi,\psi]=\langle F^{\prime\prime}(y)z_{u,\psi},z_{u,\phi}\rangle+\langle F^{\prime}(y),S^{\prime\prime}(u)[\phi,\psi]\rangle=\langle F^{\prime\prime}(y)z_{u,\psi},z_{u,\phi}\rangle+\langle F^{\prime}(y),z_{u,\phi,\psi}\rangle

for the second derivative, where

W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)
and
W^{-1,2}(\Omega)

Recall that the adjoint state

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\nabla p\cdot\nabla v\,dx=-\langle F^{\prime}(y),v\rangle\qquad\text{for all }v\in W^{1,2}_{\tilde{\Gamma}}(\Omega)

from (

To rewrite the second term in

\displaystyle\langle F^{\prime}(y),z_{u,\phi,\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u\nabla p\cdot\nabla z_{u,\phi,\psi}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\psi\,\nabla z_{u,\phi}\cdot\nabla p\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\nabla z_{u,\psi}\cdot\nabla p\,dx.

The second-order expansion

(U_{\textrm{ad}},\|\cdot\|_{L^{\tilde{q}}(\Omega)})

3

(

For deriving first-order optimality conditions, we consider the following
abstraction of (

\displaystyle\min_{u}\ j(u)+\TV(u)\ \operatorname{s.t.}\ u\in U_{\textrm{ad}},

′
)

where we recover (

) with the choice

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
j:L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded below and lower semicontinuous. Then (

) admits a solution.

Proof.

That follows using the direct method of calculus of variations.
∎

For (
P

follows from the continuity of the solution operator of the state equation asserted
in
Lemma

2.8
. Again, we consider some
s>2
, where we have in mind the situation of
Remark

2.7
and

Proposition

2.5
, and
q>\frac{2s}{s-2}
. Then
2<q<\infty
and the Hölder conjugate
q^{\prime}

satisfies
1<q^{\prime}<2
. Moreover, the convex subdifferential of the indicator function on
\BV(\Omega)\cap L^{q}(\Omega)

reads:

\partial\delta_{U_{\textrm{ad}}}(\bar{u})=\{u^{*}\in(\BV(\Omega)\cap L^{q}(\Omega))^{*}\,:\,\langle u^{*},u-\bar{u}\rangle\leq 0\text{ for all }u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}\}.

In the following, we consider locally optimal solutions to (
P′
), where locally means in a neighborhood with respect
to the norm-topology. Since the choice of the topology in
\BV(\Omega)
(weak
∗
, strict, or norm) usually matters a lot,
we briefly recall that if a function is locally optimal in a weak
∗
-neighborhood or strict neighborhood of some point in

\BV(\Omega)
, it is also locally optimal in a neighborhood with respect to the norm topology of
\BV(\Omega)
so that
it makes sense to consider local optimality in this weakest sense.

Lemma 3.2
.

\bar{u}\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
is locally optimal for (
P′
)
with respect to
\BV(\Omega)\cap L^{1}(\Omega)
if and only if it is locally
optimal with respect to
\BV(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
for
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{u}\in\BV(\Omega)\cap U_{\textrm{ad}}
be locally optimal with respect to

\BV(\Omega)\cap L^{1}(\Omega)
, that is, there exists
r>0
such that

j(\bar{u})+\TV(\bar{u})\leq j(u)+\TV(u)

holds for all
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
with
\max\{\TV(u-\bar{u}),\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}}\}\leq r
.
The continuous embedding
L^{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega)
gives some
c>0

such that

\max\{\TV(u-\bar{u}),\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}}\}\leq\max\{c,1\}\max\{\TV(u-\bar{u}),\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}}\}.

Consequently,
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
with
\max\{\TV(u-\bar{u}),\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}}\}\leq\frac{r}{\max\{1,c\}}

implies
j(\bar{u})+\TV(\bar{u})\leq j(u)+\TV(u)
.
For the reverse direction, we observe that the box constraints imply

\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}}\|(u-\bar{u})^{p-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}}(u_{u}-u_{\ell})^{p-1}.

Consequently, for all
u\in U_{\textrm{ad}}
with
\max\{\TV(u-\bar{u}),\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}}\}\leq r

we have

\displaystyle\hskip-10.00002pt\max\{\TV(u-\bar{u}),\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}}\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{1,u_{u}-u_{\ell}\}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\max\{\TV(u-\bar{u}),\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{1,u_{u}-u_{\ell}\}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\max\{\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p}},\TV(u-\bar{u})^{\frac{1}{p}},\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}},\TV(u-\bar{u})\}
