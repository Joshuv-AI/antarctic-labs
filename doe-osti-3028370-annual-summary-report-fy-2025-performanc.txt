 Executive Summary
The purpose of this Annual Summary Report (ASR) for fiscal year (FY) 2025 is to
evaluate the continued adequacy of the Integrated Disposal Facility (IDF) Performance
Assessment (PA) and Disposal Authorization Statement (DAS). This report consolidates
relevant monitoring data, modeling analyses, and regulatory reviews to demonstrate a
reasonable expectation that the PA objectives and performance measures will be met, as
required under DOE O 435.1, Radioactive Waste Management. The ASR follows the
guidance in DOE-STD-5002-2017, Disposal Authorization Statement and Tank Closure
Documentation, which provides a framework for maintaining the validity of the DAS
through periodic assessment of facility performance and compliance with waste disposal
requirements.

The IDF is a near-surface disposal facility designed to receive and permanently dispose
of low-level waste (LLW) and mixed low-level waste (MLLW) generated from Hanford Site
operations. The facility consists of two double-lined disposal cells equipped with leak
detection and leachates recovery systems to ensure environmental protection. Waste
planned for disposal includes vitrified low-activity waste (LAW) and solid secondary waste
(SSW) from the Hanford Waste Treatment and Immobilization Plant (WTP).

At the end of FY 2025, the IDF had not yet received any waste, as it remains in a pre-
operational state. Disposal activities will begin with the hot commissioning of the WTP
LAW Vitrification Facility using the Direct-Feed Low-Activity Waste (DFLAW) near the
end of the calendar year (CY) 2025.

During FY 2025, selected elements of the IDF PA technical basis and implementing
documentation were updated. The IDF Waste Acceptance Criteria (IDF-00002, Waste
Acceptance Criteria for the Integrated Disposal Facility) was revised to Rev. 1 (published
February 2025) to incorporate updated waste acceptance requirements and limits in
support of upcoming operations.

The Operating Disposal Authorization Statement (ODAS, DOE-EM, 2021, Operating
Disposal Authorization Statement for the Integrated Disposal Facility Hanford Site,
Washington) for the IDF remains in effect, with no outstanding conditions or key issues
affecting its implementation. Based on the comprehensive review of PA analyses,
monitoring data, the Radioactive Waste Management Basis (RWMB), and regulatory
compliance activities, this ASR concludes that the IDF remains in compliance with DOE
O 435.1, and there is reasonable assurance that the PA performance objectives will be
met once disposal operations commence at the beginning of FY 2026.

 1 Changes Potentially Affecting the Performance Assessment
 or the Disposal Authorization Statement
This chapter documents potential or actual changes, discoveries, proposed actions, and new information
identified during fiscal year (FY) 2025 (October 1, 2024, through September 30, 2025) that are relevant to
the Integrated Disposal Facility (IDF) Performance Assessment (PA).

The IDF PA Change Control Process (IDF-PRO-EN-54165, Change Control Process) is used to evaluate
whether new information identified through PA maintenance activities, model updates, research and
development (R&D) efforts, or operational planning could result in releases from the IDF that would not
comply with the performance objectives specified in DOE M 435.1-1, Radioactive Waste Management
Manual, or other applicable Federal, State, and local regulatory requirements. This process includes the
use of Unreviewed Disposal Questions (UDQ) to determine whether a full evaluation or Special Analysis
(SA) is required.

During FY 2025, three Unreviewed Disposal Question evaluations (UDQEs) were active under the IDF PA
Change Control Process. Each of these UDQEs represents a change potentially affecting the IDF PA, as
identified and filtered through the UDQ screening process. The ongoing evaluations focus on determining
whether the identified changes remain bounded by the assumptions and results in the approved IDF PA
and supporting SAs, or whether additional PA actions (e.g., SA, PA revision, or other change control
outcomes) are warranted. Brief summaries of the ongoing UDQEs are provided below, and their statuses
are summarized in Table 1.
• UDQ-2025-001 – IDF Process Model Update (UDQE in progress). This evaluation was performed
 to update the IDF Process Model base case to incorporate past changes made to the system model
 to align with the recently updated system model. In particular, the IDF Process Model was updated to
 include a new long-term glass corrosion rate, updated inventory, and updated dose coefficients. This
 UDQE was initiated in FY 2025 and will be completed and documented in FY 2026.
• UDQ-2025-002 – Plateau-to-River Groundwater Flow and Transport Model Update (UDQE in
 progress). This UDQE is the evaluation phase associated with a screening initiated in FY 2024
 related to revisions to the Plateau-to-River (P2R) groundwater flow and transport model
 (CP-57037 Rev. 03, Model Package Report: Plateau-to-River Groundwater Model Version 9.1), which
 is a regional-scale groundwater model that predicts future conditions. The evaluation is assessing the
 significance of identified changes to saturated zone flow and transport parameters near the IDF and
 whether updated P2R model results remain consistent with the groundwater modeling framework
 used to support the approved IDF PA and related special analyses. The UDQE remained in progress
 throughout FY 2025 and will be completed and documented in FY 2026.
• UDQ-2025-003 – Updated Glass Data Package (UDQE in progress). This evaluation addresses
 new waste glass information (e.g., composition, corrosion behavior, and/or release-rate relationships)
 from an updated PNNL glass data package. The UDQE is assessing whether use of the updated
 glass data would change modeled waste form release rates and, if so, whether those changes could
 affect long-term PA results relative to the approved IDF PA basis. This UDQE was initiated in
 FY 2025 and will be completed and documented in FY 2026.

All three items summarized above are in the evaluation phase because they represent the subset of
screened changes that could potentially affect the PA and therefore warrant additional technical review.
The evaluations are ongoing and will be reported in greater detail upon completion in FY 2026.
Because the evaluations are still in progress, no final determinations regarding impacts to IDF PA
performance objectives are made in this ASR. The FY 2025 ASR documents the identification of new
information, the basis for initiating evaluations, and the status of ongoing analyses. Results and
conclusions from these evaluations, including any identified impacts and recommended actions, will be
documented in the FY 2026 ASR and associated supporting technical documentation.

 4 Monitoring

Air and groundwater monitoring in the vicinity of the IDF are used to define pre-disposal conditions and
establish baseline environmental conditions prior to receipt of waste. During FY 2025, air and
groundwater monitoring were not conducted for the purpose of compliance monitoring. Performance
monitoring at the IDF remains limited to evaluation of liner system performance through review of liquid
volumes collected in the leak detection sumps. Monitoring data are also used, as appropriate, for model
validation and PA maintenance activities, as described in Chapter 6.

This chapter summarizes IDF-relevant monitoring results applicable to FY 2025. Environmental
monitoring results are maintained in an online database (https://ehs.hanford.gov/eda) referred to as the
Environmental Dashboard Application (EDA). All monitoring results discussed in annual reports are
available through the EDA; however, interpretation of the most recent data by subject matter experts
(SMEs) is constrained by annual reporting cycles.

The most recent sitewide environmental monitoring report is DOE/RL-2025-12, Hanford Annual Site
Environmental Report for Calendar Year 2024. The most recent groundwater monitoring report is
DOE/RL-2024-29, Hanford Site Groundwater Monitoring Report for 2024. Both documents are available
online at https://www.hanford.gov/page.cfm/SoilGroundwaterAnnualReports.

4.2 Groundwater Monitoring
Groundwater at the Hanford Site is monitored under multiple sampling and analysis plans that are
reviewed and/or approved by the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), the Washington State Department of
Ecology (Ecology), and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA). The IDF is monitored under a
facility-specific groundwater monitoring plan, DOE/RL-2019-29, Groundwater Monitoring Plan for the
Integrated Disposal Facility. Ecology approved DOE’s request to initiate baseline sampling under the
revised monitoring program in 2020, and the revised program is in the process of being incorporated into
the Hanford Facility Resource Conservation and Recovery Act of 1976 (RCRA) Permit (WA7890008967,
Hanford Facility RCRA Permit, Dangerous Waste Portion, Revision 8C).

Because of the reporting cycle associated with the groundwater monitoring program, the groundwater
results discussed in this section reflect sampling and analyses completed during calendar year (CY)
2025, as documented in DOE/RL-2024-29, Hanford Site RCRA Groundwater Monitoring Report for 2024.
Figures and tables presented in that report describe the IDF groundwater monitoring network and
monitoring results applicable to the CY 2025 reporting period. Fiscal year 2025 maximum groundwater
concentrations for samples collected from the IDF groundwater monitoring network are summarized in
Table 4.

The current monitoring objective for the IDF is to collect baseline groundwater information prior to waste
disposal operations. Groundwater beneath the IDF is monitored in accordance with Title 40, Code of
Federal Regulations, Part 264 (40 CFR 264), “Standards for Owners and Operators of Hazardous Waste
Treatment, Storage, and Disposal Facilities,” Subpart F, “Releases from Solid Waste Management Units.”
During FY 2025, groundwater monitoring at the IDF continued to be conducted in accordance with the
requirements specified in the Hanford RCRA Permit, including the annual sampling schedule, as well as
the revised groundwater monitoring plan, which includes semiannual sampling.

The IDF groundwater monitoring network, as defined collectively under the Hanford RCRA Permit
(WA7890008967) and DOE/RL-2019-29, consists of three upgradient wells and eight downgradient wells
(Figure 1 and Table 3). However, changes in groundwater flow direction since issuance of the Hanford
RCRA Permit have resulted in reclassification of several wells. Wells 299-E17-23, 299-E17-25, and
299-E17-26 are currently considered cross-gradient, and well 299-E17-22 is considered cross-gradient or
downgradient under current flow conditions. Well 299-E24-21 is designated as upgradient in
Part III, OUG-11, Chapter 5.0 of the Hanford RCRA Permit and as downgradient in the revised
groundwater monitoring plan (DOE/RL-2019-29). Based on current groundwater flow conditions, well
299-E24-21 functions as a downgradient well. Table 4 provides the FY 2025 maximum groundwater
concentrations for samples in the IDF groundwater monitoring network.

4.3 Leak Detection Monitoring
Washington Administrative Code (WAC) 173-303-665, “Landfills,” and the IDF RCRA permit conditions
require leakage rate monitoring and implementation of an approved Response Action Plan. Leakage from
each disposal cell is monitored in accordance with procedure IDF-PRO-PM-53955, Data Collection
Process, and monitoring results are documented in the IDF Operating Record. The Response Action
Plan, which is incorporated into the IDF RCRA Permit, requires notification to Ecology within 7 days if the
action leakage rate for the primary leak detection system is exceeded.

During the Pre-Active Life phase, monitoring for the presence of liquid in the secondary leak detection
system (LDS) sumps is performed quarterly, and monitoring of the action leakage rate for the primary leak
detection system is performed quarterly and following storm events, as specified in Table 6-2 of the
Hanford RCRA Permit (WA7890008967). A storm is defined as any atmospheric disturbance resulting in
either wind gusts of 56.3 km per hour (35 miles per hour) or greater, or precipitation exceeding 0.5 inches
within a 24-hour period. During the Active Life phase, monitoring of the action leakage rate for the primary
LDS will be performed weekly and following storm events, and monitoring for the presence of liquids in
the secondary leak detection system sumps will be performed monthly.

In December 2024, March 2025, June 2025, and September 2025, water removed from the leachate
collection and recovery system (LCRS) and water levels in the LDS sumps of Cell 1 and Cell 2 were
monitored in accordance with IDF-PRO-PM-53955.
• The volumes of water removed from the LCRS for Cell 1 for the four reporting periods was
      2,587 gal/day, 4,386 gal/day, 2,091 gal/day, and 2,178 gal/day, respectively.
• The volumes of water removed from the LCRS for Cell 2 for the four reporting periods was
 411 gal/day, 2,828 gal/day, 1150 gal/day and 1,126 gal/day, respectively.
• The leakage rate from the LDS in each cell (Cell 1 and Cell 2) during these monitoring periods was
 0 gal/day and 26 gal/day, respectively, which is less than the approved Action Leakage Rate of
 206 gal/acre/day established in the Response Action Plan in the IDF RCRA Permit.

 5 Research and Development
The R&D section of this ASR focuses on work conducted and documented in FY 2025. The research is
aligned with the PA Maintenance activities presented in Table 6-1 of the PA Maintenance Plan
(CHPRC-03348, Performance Assessment Maintenance Plan for the Integrated Disposal Facility).

5.1 Waste Form Lysimeters
The field-scale lysimeter study is a long-term (years) experiment intended to provide corroborating data
on waste form durability, contaminant release from both glass and grout waste forms, and transport in the
near-field environment relevant to that expected at the Hanford Site IDF. The status of the field-scale
lysimeter study conducted at the Hanford Site to investigate the durability of glass and grout waste forms
is summarized in PNNL-36443, Field-Scale Lysimeter Studies of Low-Activity Waste Forms Degradation.
The dataset generated from this study will be used to refine and improve process model descriptions of
waste form release and near-field transport used in IDF performance assessment calculations.

The study tests a variety of relevant cementitious and glass waste forms targeted for IDF disposal and is
observing their performance under IDF-relevant near-field conditions. Waste forms currently being tested

 12

include a baseline and an enhanced waste glass formulation, cementitious liquid secondary wastes, and
a cementitious supplemental LAW. Water infiltration rates span a range representing accelerated
exposures compared to expected IDF conditions to allow more rapid data collection for comparison to
model predictions and more accurate projections of long-term performance.

Initial results indicate that, as expected, glass is more durable than grout waste forms; however, the
enhanced knowledge of grout performance will better inform the risk budget tool and comparisons to IDF
PA projections. It is still too early to predict if performance will meet or exceed IDF PA projections, though
incorporating the results in the RBT will help manage compliance with the PA conclusions by selecting
appropriate grout formulations and managing contaminant inventories.

5.2 ILAW Glass Disposal in the IDF
The current Risk Budget Tool (RBT) was developed based on assumptions regarding waste form
performance and contaminant inventories that were analyzed in the 2017 IDF PA. The purpose of the
work documented in PNNL-38147, Technical Basis Updates Supporting ILAW Glass Disposal Modeling
at Hanford’s Integrated Disposal Facility – FY25, is to add flexibility to the RBT to better represent actual
waste forms and inventories produced and more accurately reflect contaminant releases relative to the
available risk budget at any point in time.

Work in FY 2025 included updates to (1) enhance the IDF RBT to be more robust and technically
accurate; (2) extend the simulation time of IDF release calculations for Enhanced Waste Glass (EWG) to
10,000 years and capture associated sensitivity analyses; (3) evaluate composition–parameter correlation
models used to predict glass dissolution over disposal timescales; 4) evaluate the Integrated Glass
Corrosion Model at multiple glass-surface-area to aqueous-solution-volume ratios that approach
hydraulically unsaturated conditions; and (5) perform testing and model updates for an expanded glass
composition space.

Key findings and resulting implications for each category of activities were summarized. For LAW glass
the notable outcome was a reduction in projected glass corrosion and contaminant release rates by
orders-of-magnitude and realistic projection of potential impact of Stage III glass dissolution behavior.
The PA is not impacted in that contaminant releases from LAW glass are expected to be substantially
reduced creating a greater “buffer” in the risk budget for release from other waste forms

The report HFO-72080Immobilized Low-Activity Waste Technical Requirements Document (IWTRD),
represents an update to the IWTRD which is required by the IDF Permit to be updated when new ILAW
glasses, such as EWG, are planned to be disposed of in the IDF. The previous IWTRD addressed
baseline waste glass (BWG) compositions and did not include the larger EWG composition space. This
paves the way for the WTP to begin producing EWG compositions in the future. EWG glasses generally
perform as well, or better than BWG glasses with significantly higher waste loading, especially for waste
feeds containing elevated sulfate. For this reason the incorporation of EWG glasses will not adversely
affect the results of the 2017 IDF PA analyses.

5.3 Product Consistency Tests of ILAW Glass
The updated results of on-going Long-Term Product Consistency Tests (PCTs) of immobilized low-activity
waste (ILAW) glasses are presented in VSL-25R5750-1, Final Report: FY2025 Long-Term PCT of ILAW
Glasses. The report evaluates the long-term performance using PCTs for ILAW glasses. The report
presents experimental data and analyses that assess the durability and stability of ILAW glasses under
conditions relevant to long-term storage. These findings are critical for understanding how ILAW glasses
will behave over time and ensuring the safe management and disposal of nuclear waste. The study’s
structured format, including tables, figures, and a list of abbreviations, demonstrates a rigorous approach
and helps to advance research and development in nuclear waste management strategies.

5.4 Effluent Treatment Facility Grout Testing
The current baseline treatment strategy for dispositioning ETF brine after WTP startup is to ship the
stream off site to Perma-Fix Northwest (PFNW) for treatment and stabilization followed by disposing of
the final stabilized waste form on site at the IDF. The report PNNL-38161, Effluent Treatment Facility
Grout Waste Form Evaluation for Hanford Integrated Disposal Facility Disposal, details work performed to
support this strategy by gathering data on the performance of the PFNW waste form to evaluate its
performance relative to the IDF PA requirements. Grout waste forms were prepared from simulated ETF
brine waste using the formulation prescribed by PFNW. The following performance tests were completed
to gather model inputs for the IDF PA: EPA Method 1315 leach testing, contaminant partition coefficient
testing, saturated hydraulic conductivity testing, water characteristic curve measurements, reduction
capacity testing, and physical properties characterization such as compressive strength, porosity, density,
and bulk mineralogical composition. The results showed the PFNW grout had acceptable fresh and cured
properties which exceeded regulatory requirements. Other performance tests revealed property values
comparable to previously studied baseline grouts and underscore the potential of the PFNW ETF grout
waste form as a viable option for safely immobilizing ETF brines.

5.5 Other Grout Testing for Potential Future Grout Disposal in IDF
A portion of LAW and / or pre-treated waste (PTW) from West Area will be mixed with cementitious
materials to produce immobilized grout waste forms. Evaluations are ongoing regarding the potential
impact of receiving and disposing of the LAW/PTW immobilized in grout in the IDF. Several FY 2025
reports (PNNL-38271, EPA Method 1311 Retention Factors for RCRA Regulated Inorganics in Grout
Waste Forms; PNNL-38151, Measuring the Spatial Rate of Grout Waste Form Oxidation; PNNL-38170,
Comparison of the Capture and Hold of Technetium and Iodide Using Commercial and Simple Materials;
SRNL-STI-2025-00222, Evaluation of Pozzolan-Substituted Cast Stone for Hanford Pretreated Low
Activity Tank Waste; SRNL-STI-2025-00598, LDR Inorganic Spiked Simulants in Pozzolan-Substituted
Cast Stone for Hanford Pretreated Low-Activity Waste) assessed the viability of this potential future
pathway by testing various long-term grout performance properties.
