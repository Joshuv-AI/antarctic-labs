Computational time-dependent acoustic scattering in a rectangular membrane–porous waveguide

Muna Elsadig, Muhammad Safdar, Hala H Taha, Muhammad Afzal

ABSTRACT
A semi-analytical, computational framework is developed for the time-dependent propagation, scattering and dissipative decay of a broadband acoustic pulse in a two-dimensional rectangular waveguide whose central segment carries a porous material core bracketed by two clamped flexible membrane strips. The transient treatment follows the wavepacket–Fourier approach. About the centre line the field splits into symmetric and anti-symmetric subproblems; the duct and porous segments are expanded on rigid-wall cosine modes and each membrane on a clamped sine Galerkin basis, whose projection yields a closed-form coupling matrix. The block linear system is validated by point-wise reconstruction of pressure and velocity continuity at every matching plane, by a truncation convergence study, and by two independent energy checks: the absorbed power fraction is recomputed from a dissipation audit of the power fluxes entering and leaving the porous core, and the lossless limit of the porous model is shown to return the incident power in full. The time-dependent field is recovered as a single Gaussian-weighted matrix product over a precomputed wavenumber grid, so that each snapshot costs one matrix–vector product. Numerical experiments delineate transmission, mixed and reflection regimes, locate the membrane–cavity hybrid resonances of maximum absorption, and visualise the transfer of pulse energy to the membranes and porous filler during transit.

1 Introduction The control of broadband acoustic disturbances inside ducted systems is a recurrent design problem in heating, ventilation and air-conditioning networks, in exhaust mufflers, in compact loudspeaker enclosures, and in the architectural and aerospace treatment of partitions, building components and aircraft nacelles. Two complementary engineering principles dominate the analytical response. The first is to break the duct with a dissipative porous insert whose fibrous or microcellular skeleton converts acoustic energy into heat through viscothermal losses. The second is to deploy thin flexible elements – membranes, micro-perforated panels, compliant plates – that couple the acoustic field to a structural resonance and so introduce a reactive impedance jump that selectively reflects narrow bands of frequency. Hybrid configurations that combine both elements – a porous core bracketed by flexible terminations – exploit the complementary strengths of the two strategies and yield filters whose reflection, transmission and absorption fractions can be tailored over a broad frequency band. A substantial literature has grown around the semi-analytical modelling of such hybrid devices. Expansion-chamber silencers were analysed by Selamet and Radavich [ 1 ], topology optimisation of muffler partitions by Lee and Kim [ 2 ], the interaction of micro-perforated panels with surrounding fluid layers by Maxit et al. [ 3 ] and by Yu et al. [ 4 , 5 ], and double-layer and subdivided-cavity micro-perforated absorbers by Zhang and Gu [ 6 ] and by Toyoda and Takahashi [ 7 ]. Single-layer liners backed by porous honeycombs were measured by Hillereau et al. [ 8 ]. More recent work has addressed trifurcated and branched configurations [ 9 ], parallel micro-perforated sub-absorbers [ 10 , 11 ], the continuity of velocity at perforation boundaries [ 12 ], and building-acoustic applications of flexible-absorbent ducts [ 13 ]. Dissipative silencers lined with poroelastic materials and containing mean flow have been treated by Nennig et al. [ 14 ] and benchmarked against numerical methods by Kirby [ 15 ]. Within this body of work the analytical line most directly relevant to the present paper combines the mode-matching method (MMM) with a Galerkin treatment of the membrane discontinuity. Two-dimensional acoustic propagation in a duct bounded by elastic membranes was analysed by Nawaz et al. [ 16 ]; acoustic propagation in three-dimensional rectangular ducts with flexible walls and porous linings was treated by Lawrie [ 17 ], and Lawrie and Afzal introduced the tailored Galerkin technique for waveguides with height discontinuities bridged by membranes [ 18 ]. A sequence of extensions has since refined the methodology to wave-bearing cavities [ 19 ], to partitioned membrane-bounded cavities with material contrast [ 20 ], to silencing membrane cavities with different edge conditions [ 21 ], to flexural attenuation with absorbent linings and different edge conditions [ 22 ], to flexible waveguides with absorbent material along flanged junctions [ 23 ], to coaxial lined chambers with membrane discs [ 24 ], to sound-source modelling with structural variations and sound-absorbent materials [ 25 ], to bifurcated flexible shells [ 26 ] and dynamical surfaces tailored in a flexible shell [ 27 ], and to the optimisation of acoustic liner conditions with flexible components [ 28 ]. A cylindrical mode-filter combining a porous central cavity with two clamped flexible membrane discs has likewise been reported by Alahmadi et al. [ 29 ]. Time-dependent visualisation of the same kind of mode-matching problem has progressed in parallel. Rather than reporting a single frequency response, the frequency-domain solution is evaluated on a dense wavenumber grid and recombined against a localised wavepacket envelope through a Fourier integral. The methodology was brought to the rectangular-waveguide-with-corner setting by Aldosri and Meylan [ 30 ], who emphasised both the qualitative insight a moving snapshot reveals and the algorithmic observation that the spatial matrix can be cached so that each new time instant costs only one matrix–vector product. In the frequency domain, the filtering of higher-order modes by dissipative screens in a rigid duct has recently been analysed by Lawrie and Afzal [ 31 ], who considered an inlet–outlet system carrying a finite-length section fitted with resistive wire-mesh perforates and characterised the configurations of optimum higher-mode attenuation in terms of the coalescence of duct eigenmodes at exceptional points, the plane wave passing essentially unimpeded. It is worth stating precisely what separates the present study from its closest antecedents. In the corner-scatterer study of Aldosri and Meylan [ 30 ] – the time-domain antecedent, which already treats a rectangular geometry with the same wavepacket–Fourier reconstruction – the scattering surfaces are rigid, and in the wire-mesh filtering study of Lawrie and Afzal [ 31 ] the dissipative screens are purely resistive: in both cases the interface conditions are algebraic relations between the acoustic traces on either side, so each frequency solve reduces to mode matching alone, with no structural unknowns. Here, by contrast, the terminations of the dissipative core are elastic membranes that carry their own dynamics: each frequency solve is a coupled fluid–structure problem in which the membrane displacement is an additional unknown field governed by its own differential equation with clamped edges. The methodological contribution is the treatment of this coupling in the Cartesian rectangular setting: the membrane displacement is expanded on a sine basis that satisfies both clamped edges identically, the projection of the rigid-duct cosine modes onto that basis is obtained in closed form, and the resulting Galerkin systems – one per parity subproblem – absorb the porous-core impedance through an exact two-term elimination, so that the only linear system solved has the dimension of the membrane basis. None of these ingredients appears in the rigid-scatterer or resistive-screen studies cited above, and their combination with the wavepacket–Fourier reconstruction, together with a parity selection rule that governs which transverse modes a symmetric filter can pass, is to the best of our knowledge new. Beyond this methodological gap, two further gaps motivate the study. First, the time-domain analysis cited above concentrates on rigid stepped or right-angle scatterers [ 30 ], and the dissipative screens of the frequency-domain filtering study [ 31 ] are purely resistive; the broadband transient behaviour of a composite reactive (membrane) and dissipative (porous bulk) filter remains, to the best of our knowledge, undocumented. Second, the behaviour of a transient pulse near the membrane and cavity resonances is qualitatively different from the steady-state energy curves usually plotted – a transient frame can make absorption and reflection visible as ringing rather than as a single number. The present article addresses these gaps by formulating, solving, validating and reporting the time-dependent acoustic scattering problem for a rectangular waveguide that carries two clamped flexible membrane strips bracketing a porous central cavity. The solution is built from a symmetric and an anti-symmetric subproblem, a Galerkin projection of the membrane equation onto a clamped basis, and the energy bookkeeping of reflected, transmitted and absorbed power, all derived directly for the Cartesian rectangular geometry. Time-dependence is introduced through the Gaussian-wavepacket reconstruction of [ 30 ], specialised to the present hybrid filter, so that the spatial coefficient matrix is computed once and re-used at every time instant. Validation precedes all reported numbers and figures: a point-wise interface reconstruction, a truncation convergence study, and an independent energy audit – a dissipation integral over the porous core together with a lossless-limit check – must all pass before the driver writes a single result file. The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. The physical model, governing equations, boundary conditions and membrane conditions are stated first, followed by the symmetric and anti-symmetric mode-matching solution and the matrix system. The time-dependent reconstruction and the power identities – including the independent energy audit – are then described. The numerical results are reported in a single integrated section that opens with the validation of the truncated solution, then exposes the role of higher-order acoustic modes, the modal-filtering response to individual higher-order incident modes, the parametric heatmaps of the absorbed fraction against the porous half-length, the membrane tension and the duct half-width, the steady-state spatial field over the full geometry, the time-domain snapshots of the broadband wavepacket, and finally the transient energy stored in the porous core during transit. In this regime the correlations of Eq (4) extrapolate smoothly toward a transparent medium, so the porous core behaves as a weakly dissipative equivalent fluid: at the reference frequency the realised constants are Γ = 1.0030 + 0.0093 i and τ = 1.0049 + 0.0037 i , and both tend to unity as the frequency increases. The porous core is therefore to be read throughout as a weakly resistive filler described by an empirical complex propagation constant and effective-density ratio of the Delany–Bazley form. The offset between the two truncations deserves a comment, since the convergence of coupled Galerkin–mode-matching schemes is known to depend on the ratio of the structural to the acoustic basis size. To quantify the energy stored in (and absorbed by) the porous filler during the transit, we define the time-dependent absorbed-power proxy W a ( t ) : = ∫ − L L ∫ − a a φ 2 ( x , y , t ) d x d y ( m 6 / s 2 ) , (33) where φ ( x , y , t ) is the real-valued time-domain velocity potential obtained from Eq (26) with the Gaussian wavepacket envelope of Eq (25) ; since φ is real, no modulus is needed in the integrand. The integral runs over the porous core | x | < L , | y | < a . With the potential in m 2 /s, the squared integrand carries m 4 /s 2 and the double integral over the core area yields the stated unit m 6 /s 2 . We stress that W a ( t ) is a field-intensity proxy for the energy content of the core, not the acoustic energy density itself – the latter would combine | ∇ φ | 2 with ( ∂ t φ ) 2 / c 2 – and it is likewise distinct from the dimensionless steady-state absorbed fraction E a b s of Eq (27) . For the narrow-band wavepackets used here the two energy-like measures are monotonically related, so W a ( t ) serves as a faithful comparative indicator of the instantaneous field energy stored in (and being dissipated by) the porous filler: it peaks while the pulse is inside the core and decays as the residual energy is radiated out through the membranes. The formulation combines a decomposition into symmetric and anti-symmetric subproblems, a Galerkin treatment of the membrane discontinuity, and a Delany–Bazley model of the porous filler, developed directly for the Cartesian rectangular setting. The Gaussian-wavepacket Fourier reconstruction of the time-dependent field has been organised as a single matrix-vector product per snapshot through the time-independent spatial matrix, so each new time instant incurs only one matrix–vector product once the family of frequency-domain solutions has been precomputed. Validation gates the whole pipeline: a point-wise interface reconstruction, a truncation convergence study, and an independent energy audit – in which the absorbed power recomputed from a dissipation integral over the porous core agrees with the power deficit of the solved amplitudes, the net power delivered to each conservative membrane vanishes, and the lossless limit returns the incident power in full – must all succeed before any quantitative result is emitted. Frequency-domain results document a low-frequency transmission band, an intermediate mixed regime, and a high-frequency reflection-dominated regime, with a localised absorption peak that identifies the membrane-cavity co-tuning. Time-domain snapshots visualise how the wavepacket loses energy to the membranes and to the porous core as it crosses the structure, showing reflection, attenuated transmission and post-transit ring-down in a single sequence of frames. The combined geometry-and-time treatment is, to the best of our knowledge, new and provides both a quantitative design tool and a qualitative insight into how broadband transients interact with hybrid reactive–dissipative filters.
