Artificial intelligence in immune checkpoint inhibitor research: A bibliometric analysis of the landscape
Authors: Jian Kang, Rui Tang, Dongqi Li, Li Ma, Zhiqiang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis bibliometric analysis examines the transformative role of artificial intelligence (AI) in immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) research. Using VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and Bibliometrix, we analyzed 1,938 publications from the Web of Science Core Collection (2015–2026), revealing a dramatic rise in AI-related ICI studies, led by China and the USA. Key findings demonstrate AI’s integration in predicting treatment response, optimizing dosing strategies, and managing immune-related adverse events. Through keyword co-occurrence and citation analyses, we identify critical AI applications including digital pathology and tumor microenvironment characterization. This comprehensive overview provides valuable insights into research trends and emerging frontiers for AI-driven innovations in cancer immunotherapy.KEYWORDS: Artificial intelligence, immune checkpoint inhibitors, bibliometric

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) significantly improve patient prognoses by activating the immune system.1–3 However, their efficacy varies widely, while they may induce immune-related adverse events (irAEs), underscoring the critical need for precise dosing studies.4–9 Traditional immune biomarker detection methods are costly, and their accuracy has been questioned.10 Meanwhile, these methods struggle to capture the heterogeneity and dynamic changes within the tumor microenvironment (TME).10–12 Recently, artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a transformative tool to address these challenges.13–18 By integrating multi-omics data, spatial genomics analysis, and clinical outcomes, AI is increasingly unraveling the complex interplay between immune checkpoint inhibitor dosage, therapeutic efficacy, and toxicity.19–23

The rapid expansion of AI applications in immuno-oncology has generated a large and increasingly diverse body of literature. Although several bibliometric studies have examined either AI in oncology or immunotherapy-related research, they have primarily focused on publication characteristics or general research trends. A comprehensive analysis specifically addressing the evolving intersection of AI and ICIs, including collaboration networks, emerging research themes, and the translation of AI technologies toward precision immunotherapy, remains limited. Consequently, researchers lack a systematic overview of how this interdisciplinary field has evolved and where future opportunities are emerging.

Bibliometric analysis provides a quantitative framework for mapping scientific development, identifying influential contributors, revealing collaboration patterns, and detecting emerging research hotspots.24–29 When combined with knowledge mapping techniques, it can further characterize the evolution of AI methodologies, their applications in ICIs research, and their relationships with clinically important topics such as treatment response prediction, biomarker discovery, dose optimization, and immune-related adverse events.13,15,20,30–32

Therefore, this study presents a comprehensive bibliometric and visual knowledge-mapping analysis of global research on AI and ICIs. By evaluating publication trends, collaboration networks, influential authors and institutions, research hotspots, and emerging frontiers, we aim to provide an updated overview of the field, identify current knowledge gaps, and highlight future directions for integrating AI into precision immunotherapy.

Methods

Search strategy

We conducted a systematic literature search in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database (https://www.webofscience.com/wos/woscc/basic-search) on 20 June 2026. To ensure reproducibility, the complete search strategy (topic search, TS) was as follows:

TS=(“Artificial Intelligence” OR “Intelligent Systems” OR “Machine Learning” OR “Deep Learning” OR “Dimensionality Reduction” OR “Ensemble Learning” OR “Federated Learning” OR “Reinforcement Machine Learning” OR “Reinforcement Machine Learning” OR “Representation Machine Learning” OR “Supervised Machine Learning” OR “Transfer Machine Learning” OR “Unsupervised Machine Learning” OR “Particle Swarm Optimization” OR “Pattern Analysis, Machine” OR “Prediction Methods, Machine” OR “Predictive Learning Models” OR “Sentiment Analysis” OR “Generative Artificial Intelligence”) AND TS=(“immune checkpoint inhibitor” OR “immune checkpoint inhibitors” OR “PD-1” OR “PD-L1” OR “CTLA-4” OR “nivolumab” OR “pembrolizumab” OR “ipilimumab”).

The explicit inclusion criteria were1: document types strictly limited to articles and reviews2; language restricted to English3; no publication year restrictions. The exclusion criteria were as follows: conference abstracts, editorials, letters, case reports, book chapters, and non-English publications were all excluded from the analysis.

Furthermore, to ensure that the screening process fully corresponded with the flowchart shown in Figure 1 and to eliminate any ambiguity in methodological description, we implemented a standardized multi-stage screening procedure. First, all initially retrieved records were imported into reference management software for deduplication. Second, two independent reviewers performed an initial screening based on titles and abstracts, removing records that clearly did not meet the inclusion criteria. Finally, the remaining records underwent full-text review to confirm their final eligibility against the stated inclusion criteria. After this systematic screening process, a total of 1,938 publications were ultimately selected for subsequent bibliometric analysis.

Data analysis

VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) was utilized for bibliometric analysis, extracting key information from numerous ICIs-related publications.33–35 This software facilitated keyword co-occurrence analysis. The following parameters were applied: counting method was set to full counting; the minimum number of occurrences of a keyword was set to 5; among the 5529 identified keywords, 598 met this threshold; the relevance score was used to select the most representative terms; the association strength normalization method was chosen; and the clustering resolution was set to 1.0. The maps generated by VOSviewer used nodes to represent entities like keywords; the size of these nodes indicated the number of ICIs publications associated with each item, and the color signified the classification.33–35

CiteSpace (version 7.0.R0), developed by Professor Chen, was another tool used for bibliometric analysis and visualization in immunosuppressant research.36–38 Specifically, it was used to analyze references with “Citation Bursts” to identify significant and influential publications in the field. For the citation burst detection, the following parameters were employed: the time slice length was set to 1 y; the g-index (k = 25) was used as the selection criterion; the minimum duration of a burst was set to 2 y; and the γ value was set to 0.5 to adjust the burst scaling. The reference with citation bursts analysis was specifically used to identify significant and influential publications that experienced sharp increases in citation frequency.

The R package “bibliometrix” (version 5.0) was employed to analyze thematic evolution and to construct a global distribution network of ICIs publications.39–43 For the thematic evolution analysis, the parameters included: co-occurrence network based on author keywords; the number of keywords per cluster was set to a minimum of 3, and the fractional counting method was applied for country collaboration networks. The bibliometrix package also comprehensively analyzed ICIs research across countries, institutions, publishers, and authors, offering insights into research output, impact, keywords, collaborative networks, and publication trends. The quartile and impact factor of journals were obtained from the Journal Citation Reports 2025 and were used to assess the quality and influence of ICIs-related publications.

Additionally, Microsoft Office Excel 2021 was used for quantitative analysis of ICIs publications, enabling researchers to organize, process, and analyze data efficiently. By applying AI techniques, researchers gained deeper insights into ICIs research trends, collaborations, and knowledge evolution, enabling a comprehensive understanding of the current status and future directions of AI-driven ICIs innovation.

Discussion

The integration of AI and bibliometrics in the study of ICIs represents a significant advancement in immunotherapy research. It provides a comprehensive framework to analyze the evolution, current trends, and future directions of ICIs research.22,44–48 Our bibliometric analysis unveils the rapid growth in publications related to AI applications in ICIs, highlighting the escalating interest and investment in this domain. This section further explores the implications of our findings, focusing on the specific applications of AI in ICIs research, including the identification of novel gene targets, cell receptor structures, design of new ICIs structures, simulation of ICIs experiments, monitoring of drug efficacy, and the application of multimodal fusion for personalized treatment in real-world settings.

Figure 2 illustrates the rapid increase in publications over the past decade, which indicates growing enthusiasm and tangible progress in the field. This growth in research marks a fundamental shift in the approach to complex biological problems. AI has become an indispensable tool, enabling researchers to unravel the intricate mechanisms underlying ICIs and their interactions with the TME. The identification of novel gene targets and cell receptor structures, along with the design of new ICIs molecules, is an area where AI has shown particular promise.

However, the rapid proliferation of AI applications calls for cautious optimism. While the quantitative growth is impressive, the qualitative impact, especially regarding clinical translation, remains uneven. Our analysis suggests that many studies remain in the preclinical or early clinical validation stage, which highlights a persistent gap between computational promise and therapeutic reality. This finding underscores the need for more robust, interdisciplinary collaboration that prioritizes clinical relevance alongside algorithmic innovation.

The application of AI in ICIs is rapidly evolving. Various types of AI tools are being deployed to address different tasks.13,19 Convolutional neural networks (CNN) are commonly used for image analysis tasks, such as identifying tumor characteristics from radiological data.49 Graph-based models are useful for analyzing complex biological networks, including interactions between immune cells and cancer cells.50,51 Each AI tool has its own strengths and limitations, and the choice depends on the specific task.

We believe that selecting AI methodologies should consider not only technical performance but also biological plausibility and clinical interpretability. For instance, while deep learning models often achieve high predictive accuracy, their “black-box” nature can reduce clinician trust and hinder their integration into routine practice. We advocate for a balanced approach that combines high-performance AI with explainable AI (XAI) techniques, ensuring that predictions are both accurate and interpretable – a crucial step toward building clinician confidence and facilitating real-world adoption.

Discovering novel gene targets and predictive biomarkers

AI and machine learning algorithms have been instrumental in identifying genomic and transcriptomic signatures that correlate with ICIs response. For example, mutational landscape analysis revealed that a higher nonsynonymous mutation burden is associated with sensitivity to PD-1 blockade in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).52 Pan-cancer immunogenomic studies have further established relationships between genotype and immunophenotype, enabling the prediction of checkpoint blockade response.53 Genomic correlates of response to CTLA-4 blockade in melanoma have also been identified,54 while transcriptomic features, such as IFN-γ-related mRNA profiles, have been shown to predict clinical outcomes.55 Integrative analyses of T-cell dysfunction and exclusion signatures provide additional biomarkers for immunotherapy response.56 Moreover, deconvolution of RNA-seq data can reveal molecular and pharmacological modulators of the tumor immune contexture.57 These AI-driven approaches enable the discovery of novel gene targets and robust predictive biomarkers, although challenges such as data heterogeneity and batch effects remain and must be addressed through standardized protocols and domain-guided feature selection.

Optimizing ICIs treatment strategies and clinical trial design

AI facilitates the design of personalized immunotherapy plans by integrating multi-omics and clinical data to predict which patients are most likely to benefit from specific ICIs regimens. The concept of the “cancer-immune set point” provides a framework for understanding the determinants of response, and AI models can incorporate this to stratify patients.58 Landmark clinical trials have established the efficacy of anti-PD-1/PD-L1 agents in various settings,59–66 and AI can leverage these real-world data to refine treatment selection. For instance, integrative molecular and clinical modeling has been applied to predict outcomes to PD-1 blockade in melanoma,67 and pan-tumor genomic biomarkers have been developed to guide therapy.68 Explainable AI techniques can validate these models, ensuring scientific credibility and streamlining clinical trial design by identifying promising patient subgroups and combination strategies.

Characterizing the tumor microenvironment and immune landscape

A comprehensive understanding of the TME is critical for ICIs efficacy. AI-powered analyses of bulk and single-cell data have revealed the immune landscape of cancer,69 highlighting the roles of immune cell infiltration, cytokine networks, and stromal interactions. For example, TGF-β signaling has been shown to attenuate response to PD-L1 blockade by promoting T-cell exclusion.70 Spatial organization of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, assessed through deep learning on pathology images, correlates with molecular features and patient outcomes.71 Additionally, deconvolution methods can estimate immune cell fractions from RNA-seq data, identifying modulators of the immune contexture.57 These AI-driven characterizations help unravel resistance mechanisms and guide the development of combination therapies that remodel the TME for improved ICI responsiveness.

Predicting ICIs efficacy and toxicity via in silico simulations

AI models enable virtual simulations of ICIs effects, allowing predictions of drug efficacy and adverse events before clinical implementation. Radiomics approaches, for instance, have been used to non-invasively assess tumor-infiltrating CD8+ cells and predict response to anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy from imaging data.72 Deep learning on histopathology images can classify NSCLC and predict somatic mutations,73 offering surrogate biomarkers for immunotherapy sensitivity. Integrative models combining molecular and clinical features have also been developed to forecast patient outcomes.67 Furthermore, AI can simulate pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic profiles and model interactions within the TME, thereby optimizing dosing regimens and minimizing toxicity. Such in silico experiments accelerate the translation of preclinical findings and reduce the need for costly animal studies.

Monitoring ICI drug efficacy and managing immune-related adverse events

Continuous monitoring of treatment response and early detection of irAEs are essential for safe and effective ICI therapy. AI-powered imaging analysis, such as radiomics, can dynamically assess tumor burden and treatment response non-invasively.72 Moreover, a recent scoping review highlights the growing role of AI in ICI safety, including risk prediction, early identification, and clinical decision support for irAEs.74 Machine learning algorithms integrated with wearable devices and electronic health records can analyze vital signs, laboratory results, and symptom reports to detect early signs of toxicity, enabling timely interventions. AI-based tools also facilitate the interpretation of complex imaging and laboratory data, helping clinicians adjust treatment strategies and improve patient outcomes.

Multimodal fusion of real-world data for personalized immunotherapy

The integration of diverse data types – genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, imaging, and clinical – offers a holistic view of patient heterogeneity and treatment response. Pan-cancer immunogenomic analyses,53 integrative molecular modeling,67 and pan-tumor genomic biomarker studies68 exemplify how multimodal data can be combined to build robust predictive models. AI algorithms excel at fusing these high-dimensional datasets to uncover patterns that guide individualized treatment decisions. For example, combining radiomic features with molecular profiles may enhance prediction of ICI outcomes.72 Despite its promise, multimodal fusion faces challenges related to data privacy, interoperability, and computational infrastructure. Overcoming these barriers will require interdisciplinary collaboration and policy support, ultimately enabling truly personalized immunotherapy regimens that maximize efficacy while minimizing adverse effects.

Several landmark clinical trials have already changed clinical practice by demonstrating the superiority of ICIs over chemotherapy in various settings.59–66 AI builds upon these foundational studies by offering tools for biomarker discovery, response prediction, and toxicity management, thus bridging the gap between research and patient care. Future efforts should focus on validating AI applications in prospective clinical settings and ensuring their equitable implementation.

Deploying AI models safely is a critical issue in the field of ICIs. Ethical concerns arise when AI models are applied in clinical settings because they significantly impact patient care. Ensuring model transparency is essential because it helps clinicians and researchers understand how AI models make decisions. Additionally, tackling dataset biases is crucial so that AI models remain fair and generalizable across diverse populations. Strategies to address these challenges include rigorously validating AI models, training them on diverse and representative datasets, and implementing ethical guidelines for AI use in healthcare.

AI has great potential to bridge the gap between preclinical research and clinical application, which is a critical need in immunotherapy.20,48,75 Efficiently translating lab findings into clinical practice is essential. AI-driven approaches can streamline this process. They help translate promising discoveries into tangible benefits for patients. However, integrating AI into clinical practice requires technical advancements. It also demands a shift in mind-set among clinicians and researchers. Robust validation and regulatory frameworks must be established to ensure AI-driven tools are reliable, accurate, and safe.

Limitation

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, our literature search was confined to the Web of Science Core Collection, which may exclude relevant publications available in other databases such as Scopus, PubMed/MEDLINE, and Embase. Second, we restricted our inclusion to English‑language articles and reviews, which may introduce language bias and omit important contributions published in other languages. Third, the search was performed on 20 June 2026. Finally, citation‑based indicators reflect impact within the scholarly community but do not directly measure clinical relevance or the quality of individual studies. Despite these limitations, our analysis offers a robust and current mapping of AI applications in ICI research, providing valuable insights into the evolution and emerging frontiers of this interdisciplinary field.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this bibliometric analysis reveals a rapid and sustained expansion of AI-related ICI research over the past decade, with China and the USA emerging as the leading contributors. While the field exhibits robust quantitative growth, meaningful clinical translation remains an ongoing endeavor that will require rigorous model validation, interpretable AI architectures, and equitable data representation. Future efforts should prioritize bridging the gap between computational findings and bedside application, ensuring that AI-driven innovations ultimately translate into measurable improvements in patient outcomes.
