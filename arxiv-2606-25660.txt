Lattice non-invertible symmetry from non-commuting transfer matrices
Eric Vernier, Yuan Miao, Masahito Yamazaki

Abstract
We establish a direct connection between Onsager symmetry, duality defects, and quantum integrability in the XXZ spin chain at roots of unity, Δ = ( q + q − 1 ) / 2 \Delta=(q+q^{-1})/2 with q N = ± 1 q^{N}=\pm 1 . Using a non-Abelian algebra of transfer matrices governed by an unbalanced version of the Yang–Baxter/RLL relation, we construct an explicit lattice realization of the Onsager algebra and its duality automorphism. The duality is represented by a matrix product operator related to the transfer matrices of the τ 2 \tau_{2} model. We show that this operator obeys ℤ N \mathbb{Z}_{N} Tambara–Yamagami fusion rules and therefore realizes on the lattice the topological defect lines of the free compactified boson conformal field theory. Our results identify non-Abelian integrability as a natural framework for the emergence of the Onsager symmetry and categorical dualities in lattice models.



Introduction.
— Symmetry and integrability are deeply intertwined in the study of quantum many-body systems. On the one hand, quantum integrability is defined by the celebrated Yang–Baxter equation

;

;

;
Aasen et al. 2016
;

;

;
Chen 2025

In this Letter, we provide a novel connection between
symmetry and integrability. In short, we introduce a non-Abelian generalization of the Yang–Baxter equation in integrable lattice models whose conserved quantities do not all commute with one another and show that this leads to an explicit construction of TDLs satisfying non-invertible fusion rules, thus realizing non-invertible symmetries on the lattice.

We discuss the one-dimensional spin-1/2 XXZ quantum chain, with transfer matrices inherited from its two-dimensional classical counterpart, the six-vertex model

;
Baxter 1982

We focus on the root-of-unity points, which are dense in the gapless phase of the XXZ model. In this regime, the non-semisimplicity of the underlying quantum group

Duality.
—
We showed that the quantum XXZ model at root of unity possesses the Onsager algebra symmetry. At the free-fermionic point (
N=2
), the generators boil down to their original Ising representation,
{\mathbf{A}}_{0}=\sum_{j}{\sigma_{j}^{z}}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{1}=\sum_{j}{\sigma_{j}^{x}\sigma_{j+1}^{x}}
, and get exchanged by the Kramers–Wannier duality
Kramers and Wannier 1941a
;
Kramers and Wannier 1941b
.
The latter can be implemented on the lattice as a non-invertible MPO

, verifying

D_{\sigma}\sigma^{z}_{n}=\sigma^{x}_{n}\sigma^{x}_{n+1}D_{\sigma}\;,\,\,D_{\sigma}\sigma^{x}_{n}\sigma^{x}_{n+1}=\sigma^{z}_{n+1}D_{\sigma}\;

(18)

The duality defect, together with the spin flip defect
D_{\psi}=\prod_{j=1}^{L}\sigma_{j}^{z}
, obey the well-known fusion rules
Aasen et al. 2016

\begin{split}&D_{\sigma}\cdot D_{\sigma}=\left(1+D_{\psi}\right)\cdot\mathbb{T}\;,\\
&D_{\sigma}\cdot D_{\psi}=D_{\psi}\cdot D_{\sigma}=D_{\rm\sigma}\end{split}

(19)

which, modulo the lattice translation operator
\mathbb{T}=\prod_{n=1}^{L-1}\mathcal{P}_{n,n+1}
, are known as those of the Tambara–Yamagami fusion category
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})

Tambara and Yamagami 1998

and

are symmetries of

For

\mathbf{A}_{n}\to\pm\mathbf{A}_{1-n}\,,\mathbf{G}_{n}\to\mp\mathbf{G}_{1-n}
, with

, which keeps the algebraic structure intact. As the second main result of this Letter, we show that operators

,
T(0)
is invertible and plays an analogous role to the translation operator in (
19
).
The fact that there are two copies of the
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
relations follows from the existence of an additional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, combination of a global spin flip (
\pi
-rotation about the
x
axis) and of time reversal, which exchanges
D_{\pm}
.

For anisotropy parameter
-1<\Delta<1
, the XXZ spin chain is gapless and flows in the IR to the compactified boson CFT with radius
R
, given by the relation
2R^{2}=\pi/\gamma
in the notation of Ref.
Ginsparg 1988
, where
q=e^{\mathrm{i}\gamma}
. For instance,
R=1
for
\gamma={\pi}/{2}
(free fermion point) and
R=\sqrt{{3}/{2}}
for
\gamma={\pi}/{3}
. Therefore, the root-of-unity values of
q
correspond to rational values of
R^{2}
, which are known to correspond to rational CFTs and have a wealth of TDLs implementing non-invertible symmetries
Frohlich et al. 2007
. The existence of the
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
TDLs for
R=\sqrt{{N}/{2}}
(corresponding to
\gamma=\pi/N
) can be understood with help of T-duality
Ginsparg 1988
(which is an invertible operation in CFT).
We start with the CFT with
R=\sqrt{{N}/{2}}
, and perform the gauging of the discrete symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, a subgroup of the
U(1)
momentum symmetry (which is the IR limit of the magnetization
U(1)
symmetry of the lattice model). The resulting CFT has radius
R^{\prime}={R}/{N}={1}/{\sqrt{2N}}
. The “domain wall” between the two theories is denoted as
\mathcal{D}
, satisfying the fusion algebra of the non-Abelian object of
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
category. The new CFT with radius
R^{\prime}={1}/{\sqrt{2N}}
coincides with the original CFT after T-duality
\mathcal{T}
, which shows the equivalence between the CFT with radius
R^{\prime}
and the CFT with radius
R^{\prime\prime}={1}/{(2R^{\prime})}=\sqrt{{N}/{2}}=R
. Therefore, for the CFT with radius
R=\sqrt{{N}/{2}}
, the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{D}
becomes a symmetry satisfying

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\circ\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{Z}^{n}\;,

(24)

where
\mathcal{Z}
is the generator of the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry.
Comparing with our findings, we conclude that the duality operator
D_{\pm}
can be regarded as a lattice realization of the symmetry operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
in
c=1
free compactified boson CFT, sharing the same fusion rules as the non-Abelian object of
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
category.

Outlook.
---
We have studied the interplay between integrability and symmetry in models with non-Abelian conserved charges, focusing on the spin-1/2 XXZ chain at roots of unity. Using unbalanced Yang--Baxter/RLL relations, we identified the symmetry generated by (typically quasilocal) conserved charges as the Onsager algebra. Since this algebra arises from a seemingly unrelated corner of exactly solvable models, our results suggest that Onsager-type structures may be a more general feature of integrable systems with enhanced degeneracies
3
3

3

This connection appears unrelated to the appearance of the
q
-Onsager algebra in open XXZ chains
Baseilhac and Koizumi 2005
.
. Extending this framework to higher-rank, higher-spin, and other root-of-unity integrable models remains an open direction. In particular, the relation to
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
symmetry with open boundaries
Pasquier and Saleur 1990
;
Gainutdinov et al. 2015
;
Gainutdinov and Nepomechie 2016
and to affine Temperley–Lieb representations
Pinet and Saint-Aubin 2023
;
Hu et al. 2026
remains to be understood.

We constructed the duality operators
D_{\pm}
as MPOs realizing the
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
fusion algebra on the lattice. However, the fusion algebra does not fix the underlying fusion category, since the associator (F-symbols) remains undetermined. Tensor-network methods
Lootens et al. 2024
;
Rubio and Kull 2023
provide a route to extract this data and reconstruct the full fusion category. We leave a detailed analysis of its relation to integrability for future work.

A central question is whether every non-invertible symmetry (i.e., fusion algebra) admits a lattice realization as a descendant of a non-Abelian integrable structure. A geometric viewpoint suggests that many fusion categories, particularly those associated with quantum groups at roots of unity
Reshetikhin and Turaev 1991
, arise from three-dimensional Chern–Simons theory
Witten 1989
. This structure can be “Baxterized” via a field-theoretic version of T-duality
Yamazaki 2019
, leading to the topological-holomorphic four-dimensional Chern–Simons theory, which unifies several aspects of integrability
Costello et al. 2018a
;
Costello et al. 2018b
;
Costello and Yamazaki 2019
. While most discussions focus on generic
q
, Ref.
Moosavian et al. 2025
studied the chiral Potts model at roots of unity and its connections to hyperbolic monopoles
Atiyah and Murray 1995
, higher-dimensional holomorphic Chern–Simons theories
Costello 2020
;
Bittleston and Skinner 2023
, and string theory embeddings
Costello and Yagi 2020
;
Ashwinkumar et al. 2020
. Extending these ideas to a unified framework of higher-dimensional quantum field theory and string theory is a compelling direction for future research.

Appendix A
Reminders on the asymmetric 6-vertex model

A.1
Transfer matrix and Hamiltonian

The R-matrix of the asymmetric 6-vertex model is given as

R_{1,2}(u)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{a}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\omega\mathsf{b}&\mathsf{c}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\mathsf{c}&\mathsf{b}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\mathsf{a}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(SA.1)

where, as in the main text,
\mathsf{a}=e^{2u}\omega-1
,
\mathsf{b}=e^{2u}-1
,
\mathsf{c}=e^{u}(\omega-1)
.
It is straightforward to check the Yang–Baxter equation

R_{1,2}(u-v)R_{1,3}(u)R_{2,3}(v)=R_{2,3}(v)R_{1,3}(u)R_{1,2}(u-v)\;,

(SA.2)

which implies the commutativity of the asymmetric 6-vertex transfer matrices

\left[T_{\rm 6v}(u),T_{\rm 6v}(v)\right]=0\;,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathbb{C}\;.

(SA.3)

The logarithmic derivative of the transfer matrix yields the Hamiltonian used in the main text,

\,\left.\partial_{u}\log T_{\rm 6v}(u)\right|_{u=0}=\frac{2}{q-q^{-1}}H+\frac{L(3q^{2}-1)}{2(q^{2}-1)}\;,\qquad H=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(q\sigma^{+}_{j}\sigma^{-}_{j+1}+q^{-1}\sigma^{-}_{j}\sigma^{+}_{j+1}+\frac{q+q^{-1}}{4}\sigma^{z}_{j}\sigma^{z}_{j+1}\right)\;,

(SA.4)

unitarily related to the usual XXZ Hamiltonian with twisted boundaries,

H^{\prime}=UHU^{-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(q^{L\delta_{j,L}}\sigma^{+}_{j}\sigma^{-}_{j+1}+q^{-L\delta_{j,L}}\sigma^{-}_{j}\sigma^{+}_{j+1}+\frac{q+q^{-1}}{4}\sigma^{z}_{j}\sigma^{z}_{j+1}\right)\,,\qquad U=\prod_{j=1}^{L}q^{\frac{j}{2}\sigma_{j}^{z}}\;,

(SA.5)

coinciding with eq. (
1
) in the main text.
The commutation of 6-vertex transfer matrices together with the definition of the Hamiltonian imply
\left[H,T_{\rm 6v}(u)\right]=0
for all
u
.
The Hamiltonian
H
possesses the global
U(1)
symmetry associated with the conservation of the magnetization

S^{z}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sigma^{z}_{j}\;,

(SA.6)

and a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry that is the combination of the spin-flip and the time-reversal symmetry,

H=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{L}\sigma^{x}_{j}\cdot H\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{L}\sigma^{x}_{j}\right)^{\ast}\;.

(SA.7)

Together, they consist of a representation of a global
O(2)=U(1)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry will play a role when identifying the duality operators in the lattice setup.

A.2
Auxiliary transfer matrices at root of unity

At a root of unity (
q^{N}=\pm 1
), additional transfer matrices can be defined, related to the (semi)cyclic or nilpotent representations of the quantum group
U_{q}(sl_{2})
.
The cyclic transfer matrices
T_{\rm c}(u,v,s)
, also known as the column-to-column transfer matrices of the
\tau_{2}
model, are defined in the main text. They involve an auxiliary space
a=\mathbb{C}^{N}
, on which operators
X_{a}
and
Z_{a}
act, satisfying the algebra
Z_{a}X_{a}=\omega X_{a}Z_{a}
,
Z_{a}^{N}=X_{a}^{N}=1
. For concreteness, we may work in the basis

Z_{a}|n\rangle=\omega^{n}|n\rangle\,,\qquad X_{a}^{\pm 1}|n\rangle=|n\pm 1\mod N\rangle\,,\qquad n=1,\ldots N\,,

(SA.8)

The asymmetric 6-vertex R matrix and the
\tau_{2}
Lax operator satisfy the RLL relation,

\mathcal{L}_{a,1}(u_{1}-u_{2},v,s)\mathcal{L}_{a,2}(u_{1},v,s)R_{1,2}(u_{2})=R_{1,2}(u_{2})\mathcal{L}_{a,2}(u_{1},v,s)\mathcal{L}_{a,1}(u_{1}-u_{2},v,s)\;.

(SA.9)

which implies that the asymmetric 6-vertex transfer matrix, and therefore the Hamiltonian
H
, commutes with the
\tau_{2}
transfer matrix

\left[T_{\rm 6v}(x),T_{\rm c}(u,v,s)\right]=\left[H,T_{\rm c}(u,v,s)\right]=0\;,\quad x,u,v,s\in\mathbb{C}\;.

(SA.10)

Transfer matrices based on the nilpotent quantum group representations (sometimes referred to as “complex spin” representations) can conveniently be expressed by introducing in the
a=\mathbb{C}^{N}
auxiliary space raising and lowering nilpotent operators
S_{a}^{\pm}
such that
X_{a}^{\pm 1}=S_{a}^{\pm}+(S_{a}^{\mp})^{N-1}
. In the basis (
SA.8
), these act as
S_{a}^{+}|n\rangle=\delta_{n<N}|n+1\rangle
and
S_{a}^{-}|n\rangle=\delta_{n>1}|n-1\rangle
.
The transfer matrices are defined as

\displaystyle T_{\rm n}(u,w)=\mathrm{Tr}_{a}\left(\mathcal{L}_{a,1}(u,w)\ldots\mathcal{L}_{a,L}(u,w)\right)

(SA.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a,j}(u,w)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{-u-w}+e^{u+w}Z_{a}&\omega(e^{-2w}-e^{2w}Z_{a})S_{a}^{-}\\
S_{a}^{+}(1-Z_{a})&\omega e^{u-w}+e^{-u+w}Z_{a}\end{array}\right)_{j}\,.

The asymmetrix 6-vertex R matrix and Lax operators
\mathcal{L}_{a,j}(u,w)
satisfy the RLL relation,

\mathcal{L}_{a,1}(u_{1}-u_{2},w)\mathcal{L}_{a,2}(u_{1},w)R_{1,2}(u_{2})=R_{1,2}(u_{2})\mathcal{L}_{a,2}(u_{1},w)\mathcal{L}_{a,1}(u_{1}-u_{2},w)\;,

(SA.14)

which implies the commutativity of transfer matrices

\left[T_{\rm 6v}(x),T_{\rm n}(u,w)\right]=\left[H,T_{\rm n}(u,w)\right]=0\;,\quad x,u,w\in\mathbb{C}\;.

(SA.15)

Remark.
The RLL relations considered above also ensure the commutativity between two inhomogeneous transfer matrices. Define the inhomogeneous asymmetric 6-vertex transfer matrix as

T_{\rm 6v}^{\rm inh}(u,\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L})=\mathrm{Tr}_{a}\left(R_{a1}(u-\xi_{1})R_{a2}(u-\xi_{2})\cdots R_{aL}(u-\xi_{L})\right)\;,

(SA.16)

and analogously the inhomogeneous
\tau_{2}
and nilpotent transfer matrices

\displaystyle T_{\rm c}^{\rm inh}(u,v,s,\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{a}\left(\mathcal{L}_{a1}(u-\xi_{1},v,s)\mathcal{L}_{a2}(u-\xi_{2},v,s)\ldots\mathcal{L}_{aL}(u-\xi_{L},v,s)\right)\;.

(SA.17)

\displaystyle T_{\rm n}^{\rm inh}(u,w,\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{a}\left(\mathcal{L}_{a1}(u-\xi_{1},w)\mathcal{L}_{a2}(u-\xi_{2},w)\ldots\mathcal{L}_{aL}(u-\xi_{L},w)\right)\;.

(SA.18)

The RLL relation implies that

\left[T_{\rm 6v}^{\rm inh}(x,\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L}),T_{\rm c}^{\rm inh}(u,v,s,\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L})\right]=\left[T_{\rm 6v}^{\rm inh}(x,\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L}),T_{\rm n}^{\rm inh}(u,w,\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L})\right]=0,\quad x,u,v,s,w\in\mathbb{C}\;,

(SA.19)

with fixed inhomogeneities
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L}
. It allows in particular to extend our results to higher-spin scenarios with transfer matrix fusion.

B.1
Elementary intertwiners

Let us start by rewriting the cyclic/
\tau_{2}
Lax operator in factorized form. Denoting
t={(u+v)}/{2}
and
w={(u-v)}/{2}
,
it is straightforward to check that

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{a,j}(u,v,s)&=\begin{pmatrix}1&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&X_{a}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}e^{-t}&e^{t}\\
e^{t}&-e^{-t}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}1&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&Z_{a}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}e^{s}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&e^{-s}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}e^{-w}&e^{w}\\
e^{w}&-e^{-w}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}1&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\omega X_{a}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\\
&:=\big\{t,s,w\big\}_{a,j}\;,\end{split}

(SB.22)

where we have introduced the short-hand notation
\{t,s,w\}_{a,j}
.
Our strategy for deriving intertwining relations between generic pairs of Lax operators follows the same line as Refs.
Miao et al. 2021
;
Weston 2026
, namely, we will decompose the intertwining relations as sequences of elementary operations. Let us start by listing these elementary operations, which are also summarized in Fig.
S1
.
The first building block corresponds to a pair of operators acting in
a=\mathbb{C}^{N}
,

\mathsf{M}_{a}(x)\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{2x}-\varepsilon^{-1}\omega^{i-1}}{1-\varepsilon^{-1}\omega^{i}e^{2x}}Z_{a}^{n}\;,\qquad\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{a}(x)\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{2x}\omega^{i-1}-\varepsilon^{-1}}{\omega^{i}-\varepsilon^{-1}e^{2x}}Z_{a}^{n}\;.

(SB.23)

Here and in the following we use
\varepsilon=e^{\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{N}}
.
These operators are unnormalized inverses of each other (namely
\mathsf{M}_{a}(x)\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{a}(x)=\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{a}(x)\mathsf{M}_{a}(x)\propto\mathrm{id}_{a}
), and intertwine
\{t,0,w\}
and
\{w,0,t\}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{a}(t-w)\big\{t,0,w\big\}_{a,j}=\varkappa_{j}\cdot\big\{w,0,t\big\}_{a,j}\cdot\varkappa^{-1}_{j}\mathsf{M}_{a}(t-w)\;,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{a}(t-w)\varkappa_{j}\cdot\big\{w,0,t\big\}_{a,j}\cdot\varkappa_{j}^{-1}=\big\{t,0,w\big\}_{a,j}\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{a}(t-w)\;,\qquad\varkappa_{j}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}_{j}\;.

(SB.24)

Relations (
SB.24
) can be directly checked by writing all sides as
2\times 2
matrices, and using the algebra of
X_{a},Z_{a}
.

The other elementary intertwiners that we define act on the tensor product
a\otimes b
of two auxiliary spaces,

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{a,b}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\varepsilon^{-1}(e^{2t}-\omega^{i-1})}{1-e^{2t}\omega^{i}}(\omega X_{a}^{-1}X_{b})^{n}\;,

(SB.25)

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}_{a,b}(w)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\varepsilon(e^{2w}-\omega^{i-1})}{1-e^{2w}\omega^{i}}(\omega X_{a}^{-1}X_{b})^{n}\;,

(SB.26)

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{2s}-\omega^{i-1}}{e^{2s}-\omega^{i}}(\omega X_{a}^{-1}X_{b})^{n}\;.

(SB.27)

These operators have (unnormalized) inverses obtained by negating their argument,
\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(-s)\propto\mathrm{id}_{a,b}
(and similarly after replacing
\mathsf{S}
by
\mathsf{T}
or
\mathsf{K}
), and can be checked to obey the following relations

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{a,b}(t-t^{\prime})~\varkappa_{j}\cdot\big\{w,0,t\big\}_{a,j}\cdot\varkappa_{j}^{-1}\big\{t^{\prime},s,w^{\prime}\big\}_{b,j}=\varkappa_{j}\cdot\big\{w,0,t^{\prime}\big\}_{a,j}\cdot\varkappa_{j}^{-1}\big\{t,s,w^{\prime}\big\}_{b,j}~\mathsf{S}_{a,b}(t-t^{\prime})\;.

(SB.28)

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}_{a,b}(w-w^{\prime})\big\{t,s,w\big\}_{a,j}\varkappa_{j}\cdot\big\{w^{\prime},0,t^{\prime}\big\}_{b,j}\cdot\varkappa_{j}^{-1}=\big\{t,s,w^{\prime}\big\}_{a,j}\varkappa_{j}\cdot\big\{w,0,t^{\prime}\big\}_{b,j}\cdot\varkappa_{j}^{-1}~\mathsf{T}_{a,b}(w-w^{\prime})\;.

(SB.29)

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)~\big\{t,s,0\}_{a}\big\{0,s^{\prime},w\big\}_{b}=\big\{t,0,0\}_{a}\big\{0,s+s^{\prime},w\big\}_{b}\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)\;.

(SB.30)

\big\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}t},0,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}w}\big\}
\mathsf{M}
\big\{w,0,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}t}\big\}\big\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}t^{\prime}},s,w^{\prime}\big\}
\mathsf{S}
\big\{t,s,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}w}\big\}\big\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}w^{\prime}},0,t^{\prime}\big\}
\mathsf{T}
\big\{t,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}s},0\big\}\big\{0,s^{\prime},w\big\}
\mathsf{K}

B.2
The unbalanced RLL relations

By constructing sequences of the five intertwiners
\mathsf{M},\,\tilde{\mathsf{M}},\,\mathsf{S},\,\mathsf{T},\,\mathsf{K}
, we can now readily derive the various unbalanced RLL relations obeyed by the cyclic/
\tau_{2}
Lax operators.
The first such relation is given by

\begin{split}&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}~\mathcal{L}_{aj}(u,v,s)\mathcal{L}_{bj}(0,0,0)=\mathcal{L}_{aj}\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u+v}{2},s\right)\mathcal{L}_{bj}\left(\tfrac{u-v}{2},\tfrac{v-u}{2},0\right)\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\;,\\
&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\equiv\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{b}(\tfrac{u-v}{2})\mathsf{T}_{a,b}(\tfrac{u-v}{2})\mathsf{M}_{b}(0)\;,\end{split}

(SB.31)

and follows from the sequence,

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{aj}(u,v,s)\mathcal{L}_{bj}(0,0,0)&=\{t,s,w\}_{a,j}\cdot\{0,0,0\}_{b,j}\\
&\xrightarrow{\mathsf{M}_{b}(0)}\{t,s,w\}_{a,j}\cdot\varkappa_{j}\{0,0,0\}_{b,j}\varkappa^{-1}_{j}\\
&\xrightarrow{\mathsf{T}_{a,b}(\tfrac{u-v}{2})}\{t,s,0\}_{a,j}\cdot\varkappa_{j}\{w,0,0\}_{b,j}\varkappa^{-1}_{j}\\
&\xrightarrow{\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{b}(\tfrac{u-v}{2})}\{t,s,0\}_{a,j}\cdot\{0,0,w\}_{b,j}=\mathcal{L}_{aj}\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u+v}{2},s\right)\mathcal{L}_{bj}\left(\tfrac{u-v}{2},\tfrac{v-u}{2},0\right)\;,\end{split}

(SB.32)

with
t=\tfrac{u+v}{2}
and
w=\tfrac{u-v}{2}
.
By using the train argument as described in the main text, this yields the first step of the factorization for the cyclic/
\tau_{2}
transfer matrix

T_{\rm c}(u,v,s)T_{\rm c}(0,0,0)=T_{\rm c}\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u+v}{2},s\right)T_{\rm c}\left(\tfrac{u-v}{2},\tfrac{v-u}{2},0\right)\;.

(SB.33)

A similar relation is

\begin{split}&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\mathcal{L}_{aj}\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u+v}{2},s\right)\mathcal{L}_{bj}\left(\tfrac{u-v}{2},\tfrac{v-u}{2},0\right)=\mathcal{L}_{aj}(0,0,0)\mathcal{L}_{bj}(u,v,s)~\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\;,\\
&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\equiv\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{a}(0)\mathsf{S}_{a,b}({\tfrac{u+v}{2}})\mathsf{M}_{a}(\tfrac{u+v}{2})\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)\;.\end{split}

(SB.34)

The derivation of the intertwiner
\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}
is analogous to the sequence in (
SB.32
).
By using the train argument and combining with (
SB.33
), we have

T_{\rm c}(u,v,s)T_{\rm c}(0,0,0)=T_{\rm c}\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u+v}{2},s\right)T_{\rm c}\left(\tfrac{u-v}{2},\tfrac{v-u}{2},0\right)=T_{\rm c}(0,0,0)T_{\rm c}(u,v,s)\;,

(SB.35)

i.e.
T_{\rm c}(0,0,0)
commutes with all
\tau_{2}
transfer matrices with arbitrary spectral parameters.

Another relation is

\begin{split}&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\mathcal{L}_{aj}\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u+v}{2},0\right)\mathcal{L}_{bj}\left(\tfrac{u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}}{2},\tfrac{u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}}{2},0\right)=\mathcal{L}_{aj}\left(\tfrac{u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}}{2},\tfrac{u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}}{2},0\right)\mathcal{L}_{bj}\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u+v}{2},0\right)\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\;,\\
&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\equiv\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{a}(\tfrac{u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}}{2})\mathsf{S}_{a,b}(\tfrac{u+v-u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}}{2})\mathsf{M}_{a}(\tfrac{u+v}{2})\;,\end{split}

(SB.36)

which implies that

\left[T_{+}(u),T_{+}(v)\right]=0\;,

(SB.37)

i.e. transfer matrices
T_{+}
are in involution.

Similarly, we have

\begin{split}&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\mathcal{L}_{aj}\left(\tfrac{u-v}{2},\tfrac{v-u}{2},0\right)\mathcal{L}_{bj}\left(\tfrac{u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}}{2},\tfrac{v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}}{2},0\right)=\mathcal{L}_{aj}\left(\tfrac{u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}}{2},\tfrac{v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}}{2},0\right)\mathcal{L}_{bj}\left(\tfrac{u-v}{2},\tfrac{v-u}{2},0\right)\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\;,\\
&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\equiv\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{b}(\tfrac{v-u}{2})\mathsf{T}_{a,b}(\tfrac{u-v-u^{\prime}+v}{2})\mathsf{M}_{b}(\tfrac{v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}}{2})\;,\end{split}

(SB.38)

which implies that

\left[T_{-}(u),T_{-}(v)\right]=0\;,

(SB.39)

i.e. transfer matrices
T_{-}
are in involution.

Finally,

\begin{split}&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\mathcal{L}_{aj}\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u+v}{2},0\right)\mathcal{L}_{bj}\left(\tfrac{u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}}{2},\tfrac{v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}}{2},0\right)=\mathcal{L}_{aj}\left(\tfrac{u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}}{2},\tfrac{v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}}{2},0\right)\mathcal{L}_{bj}\left(\tfrac{u+v}{2},\tfrac{u+v}{2},0\right)\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\;,\\
&\check{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\equiv\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{a}(\tfrac{v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}}{2})\mathsf{S}_{a,b}(\tfrac{u+v}{2})\mathsf{M}_{a}(\tfrac{u+v+v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}}{2})\tilde{\mathsf{M}}_{b}(0)\mathsf{T}_{a,b}(\tfrac{v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}}{2})\mathsf{M}_{b}\big(\tfrac{v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}}{2}\big)\;,\end{split}

(SB.40)

which implies that

\left[T_{+}(u),T_{-}(v)\right]=0\;,

(SB.41)

i.e. transfer matrices
T_{+}
and
T_{-}
commute with each other.

B.3
The duality relations

The duality operators in the main text are defined as certain limits of the cyclic/
\tau_{2}
transfer matrix,

D_{\pm}=\lim_{u\to\mp\infty}\varepsilon^{\frac{L}{2}\mp S^{z}}e^{\pm u\,L}T_{\rm c}(u,u,0)=\mathrm{Tr}_{a}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{L}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}_{a,j}\right)\;.

(SB.42)

where we recall the definition
\varepsilon=e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{N}}
. The associated Lax operators
\mathcal{L}_{a,j}^{\pm}
are

\mathcal{L}^{+}_{a,j}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega X_{a}^{-1}\\
-\varepsilon X_{a}Z_{a}&\varepsilon Z_{a}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}^{-}_{a,j}=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon Z_{a}&-\varepsilon X_{a}^{-1}Z_{a}\\
X_{a}&\omega\end{pmatrix}_{j}\,.

(SB.43)

We start from the intertwining relation (
SB.30
), with
s^{\prime}=w=0
:

\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)~\big\{t,s,0\}_{a}\big\{0,0,0\big\}_{b}=\big\{t,0,0\}_{a}\big\{0,s,0\big\}_{b}\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)\;.

(SB.44)

Taking the limit
t\to\pm\infty
and noticing that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{\mp t}\{t,s,0\}_{a}=e^{\mp s\sigma_{j}^{z}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1\pm\sigma_{j}^{z}}{2}}\mathcal{L}_{a,j}^{\mp}\,,

(SB.45)

we obtain

\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)~e^{\mp s\sigma_{j}^{z}}\mathcal{L}_{a,j}^{\mp}\mathcal{L}(0,0,0)_{b,j}=\mathcal{L}_{a,j}^{\mp}\mathcal{L}(0,s,0)_{b,j}\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)

(SB.46)

Using the train argument and the centrality of
T_{\rm c}(0,0,0)
, we obtain the first set of duality relations

D_{\pm}T_{\rm c}(0,0,s)=e^{\pm 2sS^{z}}D_{\pm}T_{\rm c}(0,0,0)\;.

(SB.47)

The second set of relations can be proved similarly: observing that
\{\widetilde{Z}_{a},\widetilde{X}_{a}\}=\{Z_{a},-\varepsilon X_{a}Z_{a}^{-1}\}
satisfy the same algebra as
Z_{a},X_{a}
, we have

\lim_{w\to\pm\infty}e^{\mp w}\{0,s,w\}_{a}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{a,j}^{\mp}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1\pm\sigma_{j}^{z}}{2}}e^{\mp s\sigma_{j}^{z}}\,,

(SB.48)

where
\simeq
here stands for a unitary change of basis in the auxiliary space
a
, implementing the change
\{{Z}_{a},{X}_{a}\}\to\{\widetilde{Z}_{a},\widetilde{X}_{a}\}
. Setting now
s^{\prime}=t=0
in the intertwining relation (
SB.30
) and taking the limits
w\to\pm\infty
yields

T_{\rm c}(0,0,s)D_{\pm}=D_{\pm}e^{\pm 2sS^{z}}T_{\rm c}(0,0,0)\;.

(SB.49)

Inserting in (
SB.47
), (
SB.49
) the expansion of
T_{\rm c}(0,0,s)
in terms of the Onsager generator
{\mathbf{A}}_{1}
recovers respectively the duality relations
D_{\pm}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}=\pm{\mathbf{A}}_{0}D_{\pm}
,
{\mathbf{A}}_{1}D_{\pm}=\pm D_{\pm}{\mathbf{A}}_{0}
given in the main text.

Appendix C
Relating the transfer matrices with the Onsager algebra

C.1
Basics about the Onsager algebra

The Onsager algebra
Onsager 1944
;
El-Chaar 2012
is defined from two infinite families of generators,
\mathbf{A}_{m}
,
\mathbf{G}_{l}
(
m,l\in\mathbb{Z}
), with
\mathbf{G}_{-l}=-\mathbf{G}_{l}
, obeying the following commutation relations

[\mathbf{A}_{l},\mathbf{A}_{m}]=4\mathbf{G}_{l-m}\,,\qquad[\mathbf{G}_{l},\mathbf{A}_{m}]=2\mathbf{A}_{l+m}-2\mathbf{A}_{m-l}\,,\qquad[\mathbf{G}_{l},\mathbf{G}_{m}]=0\,,\,.

(SC.50)

There are many other possible ways to organize the generators.
Onsager’s original presentation, displayed above, gives a “symmetric” role to
{\mathbf{A}}_{0}
and
{\mathbf{A}}_{1}
, related by Kramers-Wannier duality (while the
\mathbf{G}_{l}
s are odd under duality).
We here briefly review an alternative presentation, introduced in Ref.
Vernier et al. 2019
, which in contrast organizes the generators according to their behavior with respect to the
U(1)
charge
{\mathbf{A}}_{0}
. The generators are now
\mathbf{Q}^{\alpha}_{m}
,
\alpha\in\{0,\pm\}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, linearly related to the former through

\mathbf{Q}^{0}_{m}=\frac{\mathbf{A}_{m}+\mathbf{A}_{-m}}{2}\,,\qquad\mathbf{Q}^{\pm}_{m}=\frac{\mathbf{A}_{m}-\mathbf{A}_{-m}\pm 2\mathbf{G}_{m}}{4}\,,

(SC.51)

(and conversely,
\mathbf{A}_{m}=\mathbf{Q}_{m}^{0}+\mathbf{Q}_{m}^{+}+\mathbf{Q}_{m}^{-}
and
\mathbf{G}_{m}=\mathbf{Q}_{m}^{+}-\mathbf{Q}_{m}^{-}
, see Ref.
Miao 2021
for a proof of the isomorphism). Each of the three families
\{\mathbf{Q}_{m}^{\alpha}\}
for
\alpha=0,+,-
forms an abelian subalgebra, each with a different behaviour with respect to
{\mathbf{A}}_{0}
:
\mathbf{Q}_{m}^{0}
commutes with it, while
\mathbf{Q}_{m}^{\pm}
raise (lower) it by
\pm 4
.

C.2
Proof of the first Dolan–Grady relation

We start by introducing in the auxiliary space
a=\mathbb{C}^{N}
raising and lowering nilpotent operators
S_{a}^{\pm}
such that
X_{a}^{\pm 1}=S_{a}^{\pm}+(S_{a}^{\mp})^{N-1}
, namely which in the basis (
SA.8
) act as
S_{a}^{\pm}|n\rangle=|n\pm 1\rangle
, see the discussion above eq. (
SA.11
). Defining further
U_{a}|n\rangle=e^{\alpha n}|n\rangle
, we thus have
U_{a}S_{a}^{\pm}U_{a}^{-1}=e^{\pm\alpha}S_{a}^{\pm}
. This allows us to write

\displaystyle(e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}\sigma_{j}^{z}}U_{a})\cdot\mathcal{L}_{a,j}(u,v,s)\cdot(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\sigma_{j}^{z}}U_{a}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{s-u}+e^{u-s}Z_{a}&\omega(e^{s-v}-e^{v-s}Z_{a})(S_{a}^{-}+e^{N\alpha}(S_{a}^{+})^{N-1})\\
(S_{a}^{+}+e^{-N\alpha}(S_{a}^{-})^{N-1})(e^{v+s}-e^{-v-s}Z_{a})&\omega e^{u+s}+e^{-u-s}Z_{a}\end{array}\right)_{j}\,.

Noting that for
v=s=0
the

(1-Z_{a})(S_{a}^{+})^{N-1}=(S_{a}^{-})^{N-1}(1-Z_{a})=0

C.2
) recovers the Lax operator
\mathcal{L}_{a,j}(u,0,0)
.
Using this identity repeatedly yields

e^{\alpha S^{z}}T(u,0,0)e^{-\alpha S^{z}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{a}(U_{a}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{a,1}(u,0,0)U_{a}\ldots U_{a}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{a,L}(u,0,0)U_{a})=T(u,0,0)\,,

(SC.54)

proving commutation of the matrix
T(u)=T_{\rm c}(u,0,0)
with the global magnetization
S^{z}
.

Taking into account now the derivative of the cyclic transfer matrix with respect to
s
at
s=0

(SC.55)

where

\begin{split}\widetilde{(\partial_{s}\mathcal{L})}_{a,j}(u,\alpha)&=(e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}\sigma_{j}^{z}}~U_{a})\cdot\partial_{s}\mathcal{L}(u,0,s)_{a,j}|_{s=0}\cdot(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\sigma_{j}^{z}}~U_{a}^{-1})\\

(S_{a}^{-})^{N-1}&0\end{array}\right)_{j}+2\sinh(N\alpha)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\omega(S_{a}^{+})^{N-1}\\
-(S_{a}^{-})^{N-1}&0\end{array}\right)_{j}\,.\end{split}

(SC.56)

At the expense of yet another rotation in the auxiliary space, we similarly show that

\partial_{v}T_{\rm c}(u,0,v)|_{v=0}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathrm{Tr}_{a}(\mathcal{L}_{a,1}(u,0,0)\ldots\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-2\omega(S_{a}^{+})^{N-1}\\
2(S_{a}^{-})^{N-1}&0\end{array}\right)_{j}\ldots\mathcal{L}_{a,L}(u,0,0))\,.

(SC.57)

Expanding the l.h.s. of (
SC.56

Furthermore, we get from comparing with (

C.3
Proof of the second Dolan–Grady relation

We now turn to establishing the second Dolan–Grady relation.
Consider the product

T_{\rm c}(0,0,s){\mathbf{A}}_{0}T_{\rm c}(0,0,-s)=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathrm{Tr}_{a\otimes b}\left(\mathcal{L}_{a,b,1}(s)\ldots\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b,j}(s)\ldots\mathcal{L}_{a,b,L}(s)\right)\,,

(SC.59)

where the two-row matrices
\mathcal{L}_{a,b,j}(s)
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b,j}(s)
are defined as

\mathcal{L}_{a,b,j}(s)=\mathcal{L}_{a,j}(0,0,s)\mathcal{L}_{b,j}(0,0,-s)\,,\qquad\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b,j}(s)=\mathcal{L}_{a,j}(0,0,s)\sigma_{j}^{z}\mathcal{L}_{b,j}(0,0,-s)\,,

(SC.60)

matrix products being taken in the physical space
j
while the entries of
\mathcal{L}_{a,j}(0,0,s)
(resp.
\mathcal{L}_{b,j}(0,0,s)
) act respectively on each tensorand of the doubled auxiliary space. See Fig.
S2
for a pictorial representation.
Using the RLL equation (
SB.30
) with
t=w=0
,
s^{\prime}=-s
and repeating the train argument allows us to rewrite

T_{\rm c}(0,0,s){\mathbf{A}}_{0}T_{\rm c}(0,0,-s)=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathrm{Tr}_{a\otimes b}\left(\mathcal{L}_{a,b,1}(0)\ldots\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b,j}(s)\ldots\mathcal{L}_{a,b,L}(0)\right)\,,

(SC.61)

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b,j}(s)=\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(-s)\mathcal{L}_{a}(0,0,s)\sigma_{j}^{z}\mathcal{L}_{b}(0,0,-s)\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(s)\,,

(SC.62)

with
\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}
proportional to
\mathsf{K}_{a,b}(s)
defined in (
SB.27
), obeying
\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(s)\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(-s)=1
.
In the matrix product, the only operators that do not commute with
\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(s)
are
Z_{a}
and
Z_{b}
, and they appear through the combinations
e^{2s}Z_{a}
,
e^{-2s}Z_{b}
,
Z_{a}Z_{b}
. Moreover, the only dependence in
s
is through these combinations.
Using the identities :

\begin{split}\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(s)(e^{2s}Z_{a})\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(-s)&=(1-(e^{2Ns}-1)\Pi_{a,b})Z_{a}\\
\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(s)(e^{-2s}Z_{b})\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(-s)&=Z_{b}(1-(e^{-2Ns}-1)\Pi_{a,b})\\
\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(s)(Z_{a}Z_{b})\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(-s)&=Z_{a}Z_{b}\,,\end{split}

(SC.63)

where we have introduced the projector
\Pi_{a,b}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X_{a}^{k}X_{b}^{-k}
, we see that the only dependence in
s
of the matrix product (
SC.61
) is as a linear combination of
e^{2Ns}
,
e^{-2Ns}
, and an
s
-independent term.
Introducing the exponential form of
T_{\rm c}(0,0,s)=T(0)e^{\frac{sN}{2}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}}
and comparing with the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula, we then recover the second Dolan–Grady relation.

C.4
Expansion of
T_{\pm}(u)
in terms of the Onsager generators

The factorization formula for the cyclic/
\tau_{2}
transfer matrix, given in eq. (
8
) of the main text, fixes that

T_{+}(u)T_{-}(u)=T(0)T_{\rm c}(u,u,0)=T(0)^{2}T(u)=T(0)^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}(u)}\,,

(SC.64)

therefore we can freely define
T_{\pm}(u)=T(0)e^{\pm\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}(u)+\frac{i}{2}\mathcal{H}(u)}
, as in the main text. Furthermore
[\mathcal{G}(u),\mathcal{G}(v)]=[\mathcal{H}(u),\mathcal{H}(v)]=[\mathcal{G}(u),\mathcal{H}(v)]
for all
u,v
, as a result of the commutation of the families
T_{\pm}(u)
, Eqs. (
SB.37
), (
SB.39
), (
SB.41
).
Plugging this expression (as well as the exponential form of
T_{\rm c}(u,0,s)
into the identity (
SC.58
) results in

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(u)=-\frac{N}{4}[{\mathbf{A}}_{0},e^{\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}(u)}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}(u)}]\,,

(SC.65)

the identity given in main text.
Expanding
\mathcal{G}(u)
in series with respect to
u
(with
\mathcal{G}(0)=0
) shows that the first order is proportional to
[{\mathbf{A}}_{1},{\mathbf{A}}_{0}]=4\mathbf{G}_{1}
. More generally, at a given order
n
, the coefficient of in the l.h.s. involves commutators of the form
[{\mathbf{A}}_{0},[\ldots,{\mathbf{A}}_{1}]]
, where
\ldots
denotes (possibly iterated) commutations with the lower-order terms in
\mathcal{G}
. Since commuting any
\mathbf{G}_{l}
with some
\mathbf{A}_{m}
yields a linear combination of
\mathbf{A}_{n}
’s, which, commuted again with
{\mathbf{A}}_{0}
, yield some linear combinations of
\mathbf{G}_{l}
’s, all orders in the series expansion of
\mathcal{G}(u)
can be written as linear combinations of the
\mathbf{G}_{l}
’s. We are therefore left with the task of determining the coefficients of this series expansion.

An elegant way to check that eq. (
14
) indeed furnishes the correct series expansion is to recast the Onsager commutation relations between
\mathbf{G}_{l}
and
\mathbf{A}_{m}
generators in terms of a linear operator
\Xi
acting on the space of Onsager generators,

[\mathbf{G}_{l},\mathbf{A}_{m}]=2\mathbf{A}_{m+l}-2\mathbf{A}_{m-l}=2(\Xi^{l}-\Xi^{-l})\mathbf{A}_{m}\,,\qquad\qquad\Xi^{\pm 1}\mathbf{A}_{m}\equiv\mathbf{A}_{m\pm 1}\,.

(SC.66)

With the ansatz
\mathcal{G}(u)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\tanh(Nu)^{m}}{m}\mathbf{G}_{m}
, we then have

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}(u)}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}(u)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp[\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\tanh(Nu)^{m}}{m}(\Xi^{m}-\Xi^{-m})]{\mathbf{A}}_{1}

(SC.67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\tanh(Nu)\Xi^{-1}}{1-\tanh(Nu)\Xi}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}

(SC.68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(Nu)}\sum_{m\geq 1}\tanh(Nu)^{m-1}\mathbf{A}_{m}-\tanh(Nu){\mathbf{A}}_{0}

(SC.69)

Commuting again with
{\mathbf{A}}_{0}
,

[{\mathbf{A}}_{0},e^{\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}(u)}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}(u)}]=\frac{-4}{\cosh^{2}(Nu)}\sum_{m\geq 1}\tanh(Nu)^{m-1}\mathbf{G}_{m}=-\frac{4}{N}\mathcal{G^{\prime}}(u)\,,

(SC.70)

indeed satisfying the condition (
SC.65
).
We therefore have proved the expression of
\mathcal{G}(u)
as a series expansion in the generators
\mathbf{G}_{m}
.

C.5
The nilpotent representations

The transfer matrices built from nilpotent (“complex spin”) representations were defined above, eq. (
SA.11
).
Clearly, they reduce to
T(u)
for
w=0
, but more generally they commute with the Hamiltonian, and with the (half)-integers spin transfer matrices.
We further check

\displaystyle[T_{\rm n}(u,w),T_{\rm n}(u^{\prime},w^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(SC.71)

\displaystyle T_{\rm n}(u,w)T(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\rm n}\left(\frac{u+w}{2},\frac{u+w}{2}\right)T_{\rm n}\left(\frac{u-w}{2},\frac{w-u}{2}\right)\equiv T_{\rm L}(\frac{u+w}{2})T_{\rm R}(\frac{u-w}{2})

(SC.72)

As for the cyclic transfer matrices, the factorization (
SC.72
) follows from an RLL equation of the form

\displaystyle\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(u,w)\mathcal{L}_{a}(u,w)\mathcal{L}_{b}(0,0)=\mathcal{L}_{a}\left(\frac{u+w}{2},\frac{u+w}{2}\right)\mathcal{L}_{b}\left(\frac{u-w}{2},\frac{w-u}{2}\right)\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(u,w)

(SC.73)

\displaystyle\check{\mathcal{R}}_{a,b}(u,w)=\Omega_{b}(u,w)~\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{e^{w-u}-e^{u-w}\omega^{1-j}}{1-\omega^{-j}}\right)(S^{-}_{a}S^{+}_{b})^{k}\,,

(SC.74)

where the matrix
\Omega_{b}
acts diagonally in the
Z
-basis (
SA.8
) of
b=\mathbb{C}^{N}
as

\Omega_{b}(u,w)|n\rangle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1-\omega^{-i}}{e^{w-u}-e^{u-w}\omega^{-i}}\right)|n\rangle
.
Using this factorization, it is then easy to work out the relation with the Onsager generators: with the same reasoning as for the cyclic representations, we can freely define

T_{\mathrm{L}/\mathrm{R}}(u)=T(0)\exp\left({\pm\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{Q}(u)+\frac{i}{2}\mathcal{H}(u)}\right)\,,

(SC.75)

where
\mathcal{H}(u)
is the central generating function used above (see eq.
7
), while
\mathcal{Q}(u)
is yet to be computed. We now turn to the derivative
\partial_{w}T_{\rm n}(u,w)|_{w=0}
. On the one hand, using the factorized form (
SC.72
),

\partial_{w}T_{\rm n}(u,w)|_{w=0}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\left(\tfrac{u}{2}\right)T(u)\,.

(SC.76)

On the other hand, in matrix product form,

\partial_{w}T_{\rm n}(u,w)|_{w=0}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathrm{Tr}_{a}(\mathcal{L}_{a,1}(u,0)\ldots\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-e^{-u}+e^{u}Z_{a}&2\omega(Z_{a}-1)S_{a}^{-}\\
S_{a}^{+}(1-Z_{a})&-\omega e^{u}+e^{-u}Z_{a}\end{array}\right)_{j}\ldots\mathcal{L}_{a,L}(u,0))\,.

(SC.77)

Comparing with the calculations of Sec.
C.2
, in particular with eq. (
SC.56
), yields the identity

\partial_{w}T_{\rm n}(u,w)|_{w=0}=-\partial_{s}T_{\rm c}(u,0,s)|_{s=0}+\frac{1}{16}[{\mathbf{A}}_{0},[{\mathbf{A}}_{0},\partial_{s}T_{\rm c}(u,0,s)|_{s=0}]]

(SC.78)

Plugging in the expansion of
\partial_{s}T_{\rm c}(u,0,s)|_{s=0}
in terms of Onsager generators yields an expansion for
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}(u)
.
Integrating, we find

\mathcal{Q}(u)=-\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\tanh(Nu)^{m}}{m}(\mathbf{A}_{m}+\mathbf{A}_{-m})+\tanh(Nu)^{2}{\mathbf{A}}_{0}\right)\,.

(SC.79)

Appendix D
The duality operators

The duality operators
D_{\pm}
are defined on the lattice as limits of the
\tau_{2}
transfer matrix, see Eqs. (
SB.42
), (
SB.43
). In this Appendix we study the fusion rules of these operators, and relate them to those of the fusion category
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
.

D.1
Reminders on the fusion category
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})

In this section, we briefly introduce the basic properties of the fusion category
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, which we argue to be related to the duality operators.
The Tambara–Yamagami category over the finite abelian group
\mathbb{Z}_{N}

Tambara and Yamagami 1998
, denoted as
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\chi,\epsilon)
, is a fusion category
Etingof et al. 2015
equipped with group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, a nondegenerate bicharacter
\chi
and the Frobenius–Schur indicator
\epsilon=\pm 1
. The nondegenerate bicharacter
\chi
is a map

\chi\;:\;G\times G\,\rightarrow\,U(1)\;,

(SD.80)

satisfying

\chi(gh,k)=\chi(g,k)\chi(h,k)\;,\quad\chi(g,hk)=\chi(g,k)\chi(h,k)\;,\quad g,h,k\in G\;.

(SD.81)

The bicharacter
\chi
and Frobenius–Schur indicator
\epsilon
determine the F-symbol (associator) of the category
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\chi,\epsilon)
. In the scope of the Letter, we focus only on the fusion ring (algebra) of the category. We encourage the interested readers to consult Ref.
Etingof et al. 2015
. We abbreviate the category
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\chi,\epsilon)
as
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
for a choice of
\chi(g,h)=\varepsilon^{2gh}
and
\epsilon=1
. The latter must not be confused with
\varepsilon^{2}=e^{\frac{2\mathrm{i}\pi}{N}}
is the principal
N
-th root of unity.

The simple objects of the category
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\chi,\epsilon)
are

\{0,1,2,\dots,N-1,\alpha\}\;,

(SD.82)

where
0,1,2,\dots,N-1\in G=\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, and
\alpha
is the non-Abelian object with quantum dimension
\sqrt{N}
.

The fusion ring (algebra) of the category
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\chi,\epsilon)
is given as

x\otimes y=(x+y)\,\mathrm{mod}\,N\;,\quad x\otimes\alpha=\alpha\otimes x=\alpha\;,\quad\alpha\otimes\alpha=\bigoplus_{x=0}^{N-1}x\;,

(SD.83)

where
x,y\in G=\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.

As we shall show in the following section, the duality operator (of the Onsager algebra) can be seen as a representation of the non-Abelian object
\alpha
up to normalization.

D.2
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry and the duality operators

We start by defining the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry of the model,
a subgroup of the
U(1)
symmetry. It can be generated by either choice of

\mathsf{Z}_{+}=\prod_{j=1}^{L}\varepsilon^{1-\sigma^{z}_{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{L}\begin{pmatrix}1&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\varepsilon^{2}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\;,\qquad\mathsf{Z}_{-}=\prod_{j=1}^{L}\varepsilon^{1+\sigma^{z}_{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{L}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon^{2}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&1\end{pmatrix}_{j}\;,

(SD.84)

which are related by the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
spin-flip symmetry.
In this paragraph, we show that the operators
D_{\pm}
defined in the main text (see also eq. (
SB.42
) and (
SB.43

\mathsf{Z}_{\pm}\cdot D_{\pm}=D_{\pm}\cdot\mathsf{Z}_{\pm}=D_{\pm}\;,

(SD.85)

SD.83

(SD.86)

where

V_{a}=(X_{a})^{1/M}\,,\qquad W_{a}^{\pm}=(X_{a})^{1/M}(Z_{a})^{\pm 1/M}\,,\qquad q=\exp\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi M}{N}\right)=\varepsilon^{M}\,.

(SD.87)

(Z_{a})^{1/M}X_{a}(Z_{a})^{-1/M}=\varepsilon^{2}X_{a}

D.3
Fusion of two duality operators

In this section we prove the last set of fusion rules, associated with the fusion of two duality operators.
Start with the product

\begin{split}D_{-}\cdot D_{-}&=\mathrm{Tr}_{a,b}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{L}\mathcal{L}_{aj}^{-}\mathcal{L}_{bj}^{-}\right)\\
&=\mathrm{Tr}_{a,b}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{L}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon^{2}Z_{a}Z_{b}-\varepsilon\omega Z_{a}X_{a}^{-1}X_{b}&-\varepsilon^{2}\omega Z_{a}Z_{b}X_{b}^{-1}-\varepsilon\omega^{2}Z_{a}X_{a}^{-1}\\
\varepsilon Z_{b}X_{a}+\omega X_{b}&-\varepsilon\omega Z_{b}X_{a}X_{b}^{-1}+\omega^{2}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\right)\;.\end{split}

(SD.89)

Noting that all entries in the product of Lax operators
\mathcal{L}_{aj}^{-}\mathcal{L}_{bj}^{-}
commute with
\mathcal{K}=Z_{a}X_{a}^{-1}X_{b}
, we can decompose the doubled auxiliary space as
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N-1}k
, where
k
denotes the
N
-dimensional space where
\mathcal{K}
has eigenvalue
\mu_{k}=\varepsilon^{N-1}\omega^{k}=-\varepsilon\omega^{k}
, namely

D_{-}\cdot D_{-}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathrm{Tr}_{k}\prod_{j=1}^{L}\left.(\mathcal{L}_{aj}^{-}\mathcal{L}_{bj}^{-})\right|_{k}\,,

(SD.90)

where
(\mathcal{L}_{aj}^{-}\mathcal{L}_{bj}^{-})|_{k}
denotes the projection of the doubled Lax operator onto the auxiliary subspace with eigenvalue
\mu_{k}
.
The form of the eigenvalues
\mu_{k}
can be deduced from the observation that
(\mathcal{K})^{N}=\omega^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}=\varepsilon^{N(N-1)}
. Within a
k
-subspace, the action of all remaining operators can be recast in terms of a pair of new operators,
Z_{k}=\varepsilon^{-2}\omega^{-k-1}Z_{a}Z_{b}
,
X_{k}=X_{b}
, satisfying the usual
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
-clock algebra
Z_{k}X_{k}=\omega X_{k}Z_{k}
,
Z_{k}^{N}=X_{k}^{N}=1
. We then have

\left.(\mathcal{L}_{aj}^{-}\mathcal{L}_{bj}^{-})\right|_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{k+1}(1+Z_{k})&\omega^{k+2}(1-Z_{k})X_{k}^{-1}\\
\omega X_{k}(1-Z_{k})&\omega(Z_{k}+\omega)\end{pmatrix}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{k+1}&0\\
0&\omega\end{pmatrix}_{j}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{kj}(0,0,0)=\begin{pmatrix}1+Z_{k}&\omega(1-Z_{k})X_{k}^{-1}\\
X_{k}(1-Z_{k})&\omega+Z_{k}\end{pmatrix}_{j}\,.

(SD.91)

Recognizing the rightmost matrix in the above formula as
\mathcal{L}_{k,j}(0,0,0)
, we can therefore factorize the product
D_{-}\cdot D_{-}
as

D_{-}\cdot D_{-}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\prod_{j=1}^{L}\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{k+1}&0\\
0&\omega\end{pmatrix}_{j}\right)T(0)=\omega^{L}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\omega^{k\left(S^{z}+\tfrac{L}{2}\right)}\right)~T(0)

(SD.92)

hence proportional to the projector onto the sector with magnetization
S^{z}=-\tfrac{L}{2}\,~\mathrm{mod}\,N
.
Noticing that this corresponds to the sector where
\mathsf{Z}_{-}=1
, we can also write

D_{-}\cdot D_{-}=\omega^{L}~\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(\mathsf{Z}_{-})^{k}\right)~T(0)\,.

(SD.93)

When
T(0)
is invertible, we recover an explicit representation of the fusion algebra of the
\mathrm{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
category.
Analogously, we can prove the following relation for the fusion of
D_{\pm}
,

D_{+}\cdot D_{+}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(\mathsf{Z}_{+})^{k}\right)~T(0)\,.

(SD.94)
