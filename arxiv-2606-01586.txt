Abstract
. For a given subset S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) of a graph G G , the pair ( G , S ) (G,S) is knitted if for every partition of S S into non-empty subsets S 1 , S 2 , … , S t S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{t} , there exist pairwise disjoint connected subgraphs C 1 , C 2 , … , C t C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{t} in G G such that S i ⊆ V ⁡ ( C i ) S_{i}\subseteq V(C_{i}) for all 1 ≤ i ≤ t 1\leq i\leq t . A graph G G is ℓ \ell -knitted if ( G , S ) (G,S) is knitted for every subset S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) of size ℓ \ell . In this paper, we prove that every 8 ​ ℓ 8\ell -connected graph is ℓ \ell -knitted. We subsequently apply this result to Hadwiger’s Conjecture, which states that every k k -chromatic graph contains a K k K_{k} -minor. Specifically, we demonstrate that the vertex connectivity of any minimal counterexample to Hadwiger’s Conjecture is at least ⌈ k / 8 ⌉ \lceil k/8\rceil , improving upon the previous lower bound of ⌈ 2 ​ k / 27 ⌉ \lceil 2k/27\rceil established by Kawarabayashi (2007). Our proof corrects a gap in the argument of Kawarabayashi-Yu (2013) and establishes the claim stated without proof in Liu–Rolek–Yu (2019).



1.
Introduction

One of the most profound problems in graph theory is Hadwiger’s Conjecture, which posits that
every
k
-chromatic graph contains a
K_{k}
-minor
. A graph
H
is a minor of a graph
G
if
H
can be obtained from a subgraph of
G
by contracting edges.

It is well known that Hadwiger’s Conjecture holds for
k\leq 6
. In 1937, Wagner
[
12
]
proved that the case
k=5
is equivalent to the Four Color Theorem. Nearly 60 years later, Robertson, Seymour, and Thomas
[
9
]
proved that the case
k=6
is also equivalent to the Four Color Theorem. In their proof, minimal counterexamples—often referred to as “contraction-critical non-complete graphs”—play a foundational role.

Kawarabayashi and Toft
[
5
]
showed that any
7
-chromatic graph contains either a
K_{7}
-minor or a
K_{4,4}
-minor, a result for which the connectivity properties of minimal counterexamples are once again pivotal. Extensive research has been dedicated to the connectivity properties of contraction-critical graphs. Dirac
[
2
]
proved that every
k
-contraction-critical graph is
5
-connected for
k\geq 5
. Mader
[
8
]
extended this deep structural result, establishing that every
k
-contraction-critical graph is
7
-connected for
k\geq 7
, and every
6
-contraction-critical graph is
6
-connected. Toft
[
11
]
proved that
k
-contraction-critical graphs are
k
-edge-connected. Kawarabayashi
[
4
]
established the first general linear bound on the vertex connectivity of minimal counterexamples to Hadwiger’s Conjecture:

Theorem 1

(Kawarabayashi
[
4
]
)
.

For all positive integers
k
, every minimal (with respect to the minor relation)
k
-chromatic counterexample to Hadwiger’s Conjecture is
\lceil\frac{2k}{27}\rceil
-connected.

The primary tool used in the proof of Theorem
1
was the concept of
k
-linked graphs. A graph
G
is
k
-linked
if for every
2k
distinct vertices
u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2},\ldots,u_{k},v_{k}
, there exist
k
disjoint paths
P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{k}
such that
P_{i}
connects
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
. The study of
k
-linked graphs has a rich history and remains central to structural graph theory.

In this paper, we improve the bound in Theorem
1
by investigating a related notion called a “knitted graph,” introduced by Bollobás and Thomason
[
1
]
. For
1\leq m\leq k\leq|V(G)|
, a graph is
(k,m)
-knit
if, whenever
S
is a set of
k
vertices of
G
and
S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}
is a partition of
S
into
t\geq m
non-empty parts,
G
contains vertex-disjoint connected subgraphs
C_{1},\ldots,C_{t}
such that
S_{i}\subseteq V(C_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq t
. Clearly, a
(2k,k)
-knit graph is
k
-linked. In
[
1
]
, Bollobás and Thomason proved that if a
k
-connected graph
G
contains a minor
H
with minimum degree at least
0.5(|V(H)|+\lfloor 5k/2\rfloor-2-m)
, then
G
is
(k,m)
-knit. They used this to show that
22k
-connected graphs are
k
-linked, providing the first linear upper bound on the connectivity required for a graph to be
k
-linked.

We consider a natural generalization of the
(k,m)
-knit property.

Definition 1
.

For a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
, the pair
(G,S)
is knitted
if for every partition of
S
into non-empty subsets
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{t}
, there exist pairwise disjoint connected subgraphs
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{t}
in
G
such that
S_{i}\subseteq V(C_{i})
. A graph
G
is
\ell
-knitted
if
(G,S)
is knitted for all
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|=\ell
.

It is clear that an
\ell
-knitted graph is
(\ell,m)
-knit for all
m\leq\ell
. In this paper, we establish a connectivity threshold for a graph to be
\ell
-knitted.

Definition 2
.

A pair
(A,B)
of vertex sets is a
separation
of
G
if
V(G)=A\cup B
and there are no edges between
A\setminus B
and
B\setminus A
. The
order
of a separation
(A,B)
is
|A\cap B|
. If
S\subseteq A
, we say that
(A,B)
is a separation of
(G,S)
.

Our main structural result is the following:

Theorem 2
.

Let
k
and
\ell
be positive integers, and let
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|=\ell<k/8
. If there is no separation of
(G,S)
of order less than
\ell
, and every vertex in
G-S
has degree at least
k-1
, then
(G,S)
is knitted.

Note that in
[
6
]
, we claimed a proof of the above result for
|S|=\ell<k/9
. However, we later discovered a gap in the argument, arising from an overly strong conclusion asserted in Theorem
6
. Subsequently, by replacing the sufficient degree condition used in
[
6
]
with the stronger condition established in
[
7
]
, we claimed, without proof, that the above result also holds for
|S|=\ell<k/8
in
[
7
]
.

Theorem
9
on edge-density (presented in Section
3
) is logically stronger than Theorem
2
and will serve as our primary tool. We are now ready to state and prove our main result on the connectivity of minimal counterexamples to Hadwiger’s Conjecture.

Theorem 3
.

For all positive integers
k
, every
k
-chromatic minimal (with respect to the minor relation) counterexample to Hadwiger’s Conjecture is
\lceil\frac{k}{8}\rceil
-connected.

Proof.

Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that the statement fails. Let
G
be a minimal
k
-chromatic graph that contains no
K_{k}
-minor and has vertex connectivity strictly less than
k/8
. Let
S
be a minimum vertex cutset of
G
. Thus,
|S|=\ell<k/8
. Let
A_{1}
be the vertex set of a connected component of
G-S
, and let
A_{2}=V(G)\setminus(S\cup A_{1})
.

By the minimality of
G
, both induced subgraphs
G[A_{1}\cup S]
and
G[A_{2}\cup S]
are proper subgraphs, and therefore have chromatic number at most
k-1
.

We partition
S
into independent sets greedily: let
S_{1}
be a maximal independent set in
G[S]
, and for
i\geq 2
, let
S_{i}
be a maximal independent set in the induced subgraph
G[S\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}S_{j}]
. Let
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{t}
be the resulting partition.

Observe the structural implication of this greedy maximal choice: for any
i>j
, every vertex in
S_{i}
must have at least one neighbor in
S_{j}
, because adding any vertex of
S_{i}
to
S_{j}
would violate the independence of
S_{j}
. Consequently, if we find pairwise disjoint connected subgraphs
C_{1},\ldots,C_{t}
in
G
such that
S_{i}\subseteq V(C_{i})
, contracting each
C_{i}
into a single vertex will form a clique
K_{t}
on these contracted vertices.

Since
G
is a minimal
k
-chromatic graph, its minimum degree is at least
k-1
. Thus, every vertex in
A_{p}
(for
p\in\{1,2\}
) has at least
k-1
neighbors in
A_{p}\cup S
. Note also that any separation of
(A_{1}\cup S,S)
or
(A_{2}\cup S,S)
is a valid separation of
(G,S)
. Since
S
is a minimum cutset of
G
, neither
(A_{1}\cup S,S)
nor
(A_{2}\cup S,S)
can have a separation of order less than
\ell
.

Therefore, the pairs
(A_{1}\cup S,S)
and
(A_{2}\cup S,S)
both satisfy the conditions of Theorem
2
. By Theorem
2
, both pairs are knitted.

This guarantees the existence of disjoint connected subgraphs
C_{i}\subseteq A_{1}\cup S
such that
S_{i}\subseteq V(C_{i})
, and similarly, disjoint connected subgraphs
D_{i}\subseteq A_{2}\cup S
such that
S_{i}\subseteq V(D_{i})
. We contract the components
C_{i}
within
A_{1}\cup S
into single vertices; by our earlier observation, these contracted vertices form a complete graph on the sets
S_{1},S_{2},\dots,S_{t}
. Let
G_{1}
be the graph obtained from this contraction combined with the uncontracted vertices of
A_{2}
. Similarly, we can contract the components
D_{i}
within
A_{2}\cup S
to form a complete graph on the same partition sets, yielding a graph
G_{2}
combined with
A_{1}
.

Both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are proper minors of
G
. By the minimality of
G
, we have
\chi(G_{1})\leq k-1
and
\chi(G_{2})\leq k-1
. Crucially, the contracted vertices corresponding to
S_{1},\dots,S_{t}
form a clique in both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Therefore, any
(k-1)
-coloring of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
must assign all
t
contracted vertices strictly distinct colors. Since the cliques in
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
represent the exact same partition of
S
, we can naturally permute the colors of
G_{2}
so that they perfectly match the colors assigned to the clique in
G_{1}
. Fusing these colorings yields a valid proper
(k-1)
-coloring for the entire graph
G
, contradicting the assumption that
G
is
k
-chromatic. This completes the proof.
∎

The remainder of the paper is dedicated to proving Theorem
2
. We achieve this in two steps: first (Section
3
), we establish that the graphs under study are either knitted or contain a highly dense subgraph; second (Section
4
), we demonstrate that such dense subgraphs inherently contain a knitted subgraph. This approach structurally mirrors the techniques utilized by Thomas and Wollan
[
10
]
.

2.
A theorem on forests with a boundary set

In this section, we establish the following results concerning the interaction between a forest with a prescribed boundary set and a vertex outside the forest. Although the result is developed for use in our proof, it may also be of independent interest.

Theorem 4

(General Reduction)
.

Let
W
be a graph, and let
S^{\prime}\subseteq V(W)
be a boundary set with
|S^{\prime}|\geq 2
. Let
F
be a forest in
W
with
c\geq 1
connected components such that
S^{\prime}\subseteq V(F)
and all leaves of
F
belong to
S^{\prime}
. Let
u\in V(W)\setminus V(F)
. Suppose that
d(u,F)\geq|S^{\prime}|+k
, where
k=2
if
c=1
, and
k=1
if
c\geq 2
. Then the induced subgraph
W[V(F)\cup\{u\}]
contains a forest
F_{0}
with
u\in V(F_{0})
such that
S^{\prime}\subseteq V(F_{0})
, all leaves of
F_{0}
in
S^{\prime}
, and at least one of the following holds:

(1)

|V(F_{0})|<|V(F)|

(Strict Order Reduction)

(2)

|V(F_{0})|=|V(F)|
and
c(F_{0})<c(F)

(Strict Component Reduction)

Proof.

Partition
S^{\prime}
across the components
T_{1}\dots T_{c}
of
F
as
S^{\prime}_{i}=S^{\prime}\cap V(T_{i})
. Since all leaves of
F
are in
S^{\prime}
, each
T_{i}
is minimally spanned by
S^{\prime}_{i}
.
Let
N=N_{W}(u)\cap V(F)
. We have
|N|\geq|S^{\prime}|+k
. Let
I
be the index set of components containing at least one vertex of
N
.

For each
i\in I
and
s\in S^{\prime}_{i}
, let
P_{s}
be a shortest path in
T_{i}
from
s
to
N
. Let
y_{s}\in N
be its terminus. Define
F_{\text{ess}}^{(i)}=\bigcup_{s\in S^{\prime}_{i}}P_{s}
, and let
Y_{i}=\{y_{s}\mid s\in S^{\prime}_{i}\}
. Let
Y=\bigcup Y_{i}
. Clearly,
|Y|\leq|S^{\prime}|
.

We construct an
intermediate graph

F^{\prime}_{0}
:

F^{\prime}_{0}=\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}F_{\text{ess}}^{(i)}\right)\cup\{u\}\cup\{uy\mid y\in Y\}\cup\left(\bigcup_{j\notin I}T_{j}\right)

While
F^{\prime}_{0}
connects the active components through
u
and spans
S^{\prime}
, it might contain cycles if paths in
F_{\text{ess}}^{(i)}
coalesce and then diverge to multiple targets in
Y_{i}
. We resolve this by taking
F_{0}
to be a
minimal spanning sub-forest
of
F^{\prime}_{0}
with respect to
S^{\prime}
. By minimality,
F_{0}
is cycle-free and all leaves of
F_{0}
are in
S^{\prime}
.

We must guarantee
u\in V(F_{0})
. If
u
were removed during minimization,
F_{0}
would be entirely contained within
F\setminus N
. This implies
\bigcup F_{\text{ess}}^{(i)}
inherently spans
S^{\prime}
, which forces
F_{\text{ess}}^{(i)}=T_{i}
and
N=Y
. However, our threshold guarantees
|N|\geq|S^{\prime}|+1
, while
|Y|\leq|S^{\prime}|
, a contradiction. Thus,
u
survives minimization.

Because
F_{0}\subseteq F^{\prime}_{0}
, any neighbor of
u
in
N\setminus Y
is completely excluded from
F_{0}
.

If
c=1
,
k=2
. Thus
|N\setminus Y|\geq|S^{\prime}|+2-|S^{\prime}|=2
. The order strictly decreases:
|V(F_{0})|\leq|V(F)|-2+1<|V(F)|
, satisfying the requirement of our theorem.

If
c\geq 2
,
k=1
. If
|I|=1
, then
d(u,T_{i})\geq|S^{\prime}|+1\geq|S_{i}^{\prime}|+2
for some sole component
T_{i}
. It follows that
W[T_{i}\cup\{u\}]
has a smaller tree with desired properties. So
|I|\geq 2
. Then the components merge into one component. Thus
c(F_{0})<c(F)
. Furthermore,
|V(F_{0})|\leq|V(F)|-|N\setminus Y|+1\leq|V(F)|
.
∎

We now classify the exact tight structure when
F
is a single tree (
c=1
), the degree is lowered to exactly
d(u,F)=|S^{\prime}|+1
, and
no
reduction is possible. We will not use this result in our proof, but again, it may be of independent interest.

Theorem 5

(Extremal Structure)
.

Let
F
be a tree bounding
S^{\prime}
such that
\text{Leaves}(F)\subseteq S^{\prime}
. Let
u\notin V(F)
such that
d(u,F)=|S^{\prime}|+1
. Suppose
W[V(F)\cup\{u\}]
contains NO strictly smaller forest
F_{0}
spanning
S^{\prime}
with
u\in V(F_{0})
and
\text{Leaves}(F_{0})\subseteq S^{\prime}
.
Then
F
is a subdivided star with a unique central vertex
z
of degree
|S^{\prime}|
, and
N_{W}(u)\cap V(F)
consists exactly of
z
and its
|S^{\prime}|
immediate neighbors.

Proof.

Let
N=N_{W}(u)\cap V(F)
with
|N|=|S^{\prime}|+1
. For any valid assignment of each
s\in S^{\prime}
to a closest neighbor
y_{s}\in N
, we can construct the intermediate graph
F^{\prime}_{0}=\bigcup P_{s}\cup\{u\}\cup\{uy_{s}\}
and extract its minimal spanning sub-forest
F_{0}
.

As established,
|V(F_{0})|\leq|V(F)|-|N\setminus Y|+1
. By the hypothesis that no strictly smaller forest exists, we must have
|V(F_{0})|\geq|V(F)|
for
any
valid choice of targets. This forces
|V(F_{0})|=|V(F^{\prime}_{0})|=|V(F)|
, and
|N\setminus Y|=1
. Let
N\setminus Y=\{z\}
. Since
|N|=|S^{\prime}|+1
, we must have
|Y|=|S^{\prime}|
, meaning each boundary vertex routes to a distinct unique closest neighbor.

Since
|V(F^{\prime}_{0})|=|V(F)|
, the essential paths must cover exactly
V(F)\setminus\{z\}
. Removing
z
from
F
leaves a forest of
d_{F}(z)
components. Because the essential paths cover these components and terminate at exactly
|S^{\prime}|
distinct targets in
Y
, there are at most
|S^{\prime}|
paths, bounding
d_{F}(z)\leq|S^{\prime}|
.

Consider a component
C
of
F\setminus\{z\}
. Because
F
is a tree,
C
connects to
z
via a single unique vertex
v_{C}
. For any
s\in S^{\prime}\cap V(C)
, the path in
F
from
s
to
z
goes through
v_{C}
. Since
y_{s}
is the closest vertex in
N
to
s
, we have
d(s,y_{s})\leq d(s,z)
, which expands to
d(s,v_{C})+d(v_{C},y_{s})\leq d(s,v_{C})+1
, forcing
d(v_{C},y_{s})\leq 1
.

Suppose for contradiction
y_{s}\neq v_{C}
. Then
d(v_{C},y_{s})=1
, making
y_{s}
a branch off
v_{C}
. In this layout,
y_{s}
and
z
are equidistant from
s
, causing a tie for the closest neighbor in
N
.
If we alternatively selected
z
as the target for
s
, the new path
P^{\prime}_{s}
would terminate at
z
instead of
y_{s}
. Since
y_{s}
is a branch off
v_{C}
, the path
P^{\prime}_{s}=s\dots v_{C}
strictly excludes
y_{s}
. This alternative construction removes
y_{s}
from the essential forest. The resulting new forest
F^{*}_{0}
would bypass both
y_{s}
and whatever unique vertex represents
N\setminus Y^{*}
, yielding
|V(F^{*}_{0})|\leq|V(F)|-2+1<|V(F)|
.
This directly violates our hypothesis that no strictly smaller forest exists.

Therefore, we must have
y_{s}=v_{C}
universally to prevent tie-breaking reductions. This dictates that
Y
is precisely the set of immediate neighbors of
z
. Since
Y
covers all
|S^{\prime}|
distinct paths,
d_{F}(z)=|S^{\prime}|
. Finally, since the components
C
are exactly the paths
P_{s}
extending from
s
to
v_{C}
,
F
is structurally identical to a subdivided star centered at
z
.
∎

3.
Dense subgraphs in massed graphs

We introduce a metric to govern our induction. For a set
X\subseteq V(G)
, let
\rho(X)
denote the number of edges with at least one endpoint in
X
.

Definition 3
.

A separation
(A,B)
of
(G,S)
is
rigid
if the pair
(G[B],A\cap B)
is knitted.

Definition 4
.

Let
G
be a graph,
S\subseteq V(G)
, and
\alpha>1
be a real number. The pair
(G,S)
is
\alpha\ell
-massed
if:

(i)

\rho(V(G)-S)\geq\lfloor\alpha\ell|V(G)-S|\rfloor
, and

(ii)

every separation
(A,B)
of
(G,S)
of order at most
|S|-1
satisfies
\rho(B\setminus A)\leq\alpha\ell|B\setminus A|
.

From now on, we will try to show that
(G,S)
is
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{t})
-knitted, where
\sum_{i=1}^{t}s_{i}=\ell
. Since we do not pose any restriction on the partition, this is equivalent to show that
(G,S)
is knitted.

Definition 5
.

The pair
(G,S)
is
(\alpha,\ell)
-minimal
if:

(1)

(G,S)
is
\alpha\ell
-massed,

(2)

|S|\leq\ell
and
(G,S)
is not
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{t})
-knitted,

(3)

subject to the above,
|V(G)|
is minimized,

(4)

subject to the above,
\rho(V(G)-S)
is minimized, and

(5)

subject to the above, the number of edges of
G
with both endpoints in
S
is maximized.

We state the following theorem, which strengthens the core density arguments.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\ell\geq 1
be an integer and
\alpha\geq 2
be a real number. If a pair
(G,S)
is
(\alpha,\ell)
-minimal, then
G
has no rigid separation of order at most
|S|
. Furthermore, there is a partition of
S=S_{1}\cup S_{2}\ldots\cup S_{t}
with
s_{i}=|S_{i}|
, and a subgraph
H=G[N[v]]
in
G
with
|V(H)|\leq\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil
such that

d_{H}(u)\geq\begin{cases}1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-s_{i},&\mbox{if $u\in S_{i}$;}\\
1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor,&\mbox{
if $u\in V(H)-S$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
(G,S)
is
(\alpha,\ell)
-minimal,
(G,S)
is not
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{t})
-knitted. In particular, for a partition
S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}
of
S
with
s_{i}=|S_{i}|
, we cannot find disjoint connected subgraphs respectively containing
S_{i}
’s. By Definition
5
(5), we may assume that vertices between
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
are all adjacent, since adding edges between
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
maintain
(G,S)
to be
(\alpha,\ell)
-minimal.

We structure the proof across three fundamental claims.

Claim 1
.

G
has no rigid separation of order at most
|S|
.

Proof.

Suppose such a rigid separation
(A,B)
exists; select one minimizing
|A|
.

First, assume
|A\cap B|<|S|
. Let
G^{*}[A]
be the graph obtained by adding all missing edges to make
A\cap B
a clique. If
(G^{*}[A],S)
is knitted, we can effortlessly transform its knits into a valid knit for
(G,S)
by leveraging the rigidity of
(A,B)
. Since
(G,S)
is not knitted,
(G^{*}[A],S)
is not knitted.
Because
G
is
\alpha\ell
-massed,
\rho(B\setminus A)\leq\alpha\ell|B\setminus A|
. Consequently,
\rho(A\setminus S)>\alpha\ell|A\setminus S|
. This means condition (i) of being massed is satisfied, forcing condition (ii) to fail in
G^{*}[A]
.
Let
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
be a separation of
G^{*}[A]
containing
S\subseteq A^{\prime}
that violates (ii), minimizing
B^{\prime}
. If
A\cap B\subseteq A^{\prime}
, then
(A^{\prime}\cup B,B^{\prime})
acts as a separation in
G
violating (ii), which is impossible. Thus, since
A\cap B
is a clique,
A\cap B\subseteq B^{\prime}
.
By the minimality of
B^{\prime}
, the subgraph
(G^{*}[B^{\prime}],A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime})
is itself massed and thus knitted. Consequently,
(G^{*}[B\cup B^{\prime}],A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime})
is knitted, making
A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}
a rigid separation of
(G,S)
strictly smaller than
A
, contradicting our minimal choice of
A
.

Next, assume
|A\cap B|=|S|
. If there are
|S|
disjoint paths from
S
to
A\cap B
, the rigidity of
(A,B)
instantly makes
(G,S)
knitted. Otherwise, there is a separation
(A^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime\prime})
of
(G[A],S)
of order less than
|S|
separating
S
from
A\cap B
. Choosing the minimal
|A^{\prime\prime}\cap B^{\prime\prime}|
, we generate disjoint paths from
A^{\prime\prime}\cap B^{\prime\prime}
to
A\cap B
. The rigidity flows back, establishing
(A^{\prime\prime},B\cup B^{\prime\prime})
as a rigid separation of
(G,S)
with
|A^{\prime\prime}|<|A|
, again contradicting minimality.
∎

Claim 2
.

Let
uv
be an edge with
v\notin S
. Then
d(v)\geq 1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor
. Moreover, if
u\notin S
, then
u
and
v
have at least
\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor
common neighbors. If
u\in S
, then
u
and
v
have at least
\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-|N(v)\cap S-N[u]|
common neighbors.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}=G/uv
be the graph resulting from contracting the edge
uv
. If
(G^{\prime},S)
is knitted,
(G,S)
inherits the knit. Thus
(G^{\prime},S)
must violate (i) or (ii).

Suppose
(G^{\prime},S)
violates (i). Then
\rho(V(G^{\prime})-S)\leq\lfloor\alpha\ell|V(G^{\prime})-S|\rfloor-1
. Since
G
satisfies (i),
\rho(V(G)-S)\geq\lfloor\alpha\ell|V(G)-S|\rfloor
. The contraction reduces the vertex count by exactly one.

\rho(V(G)-S)-\rho(V(G^{\prime})-S)\geq 1+\lfloor\alpha\ell|V(G)-S|\rfloor-\lfloor\alpha\ell|V(G^{\prime})-S|\rfloor\geq 1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor.

The edges lost in
\rho(V(G)-S)
during contraction are exactly the edge
uv
, one duplicate edge for every common neighbor of
u
and
v
not in
S
, and (if
u\in S
) any edge from
v
to
S
that becomes redundant. All such edges are incident to
v
, establishing
d(v)\geq\rho(V(G)-S)-\rho(V(G^{\prime})-S)\geq 1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor
.
If
u\notin S
, the exact number of lost edges is
1+|N(u)\cap N(v)|
, yielding
|N(u)\cap N(v)|\geq\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor
. If
u\in S
, the lost edges number
1+|N(u)\cap N(v)|-|N(v)\cap S-N[u]|
, preserving the inequality.

Alternatively, assume
(G^{\prime},S)
violates (ii). Let
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
be the violating separation with minimal
B^{\prime}
. By minimality,
(G^{\prime}[B^{\prime}],A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime})
is knitted, meaning
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
acts as a rigid separation in
G^{\prime}
. In the pre-contraction graph
G
, this induces a separation
(A,B)
. If
\{u,v\}\not\subseteq A\cap B
, then
(A,B)
operates as a rigid separation of
(G,S)
of order at most
|S|-1
, decisively violating Claim
1
. If
u,v\in A\cap B
, the minimality of
B^{\prime}
ensures
(G[B],A\cap B)
is
\alpha\ell
-massed and therefore knitted, creating a rigid separation of size
\leq|S|
, violating Claim
1
once more.
∎

Claim 3
.

Let
\delta^{\prime}
be the minimum degree in
G
among vertices in
V(G)-S
. Then
\alpha\ell<\delta^{\prime}<2\alpha\ell
.

Proof.

Claim
2
guarantees
\delta^{\prime}>\alpha\ell
. We must prove
\delta^{\prime}<2\alpha\ell
. Let
v\in V(G)-S
be an arbitrary vertex. Since
d(v)>\alpha\ell\geq 2\ell>|S|
,
v
possesses at least one neighbor
u\in V(G)-S
. Let
e=uv
, and consider
G_{1}=G-e
. Because
G
is minimal,
G_{1}
must fail (i) or (ii).

If
G_{1}
fails (ii),
(G-e,S)
contains a separation
(A,B)
with
|A\cap B|<|S|
. The edge
e
must cross this separation, forcing
u\in A\setminus B
and
v\in B\setminus A
. Therefore, their common neighbors are restricted to
A\cap B
. This implies
|N(u)\cap N(v)|\leq|A\cap B|<|S|\leq\ell<\alpha\ell
, contradicting Claim
2
.

Thus,
G_{1}
must fail (i), yielding
\rho(V(G_{1})-S)\leq\lfloor\alpha\ell|V(G)-S|\rfloor-1
. Since removing
e
drops the edge count in
V(G)-S
by precisely 1, we find exactly
\rho(V(G)-S)\leq\lfloor\alpha\ell|V(G)-S|\rfloor
.

The sum of the degrees of the vertices in
V(G)-S
satisfies:

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in V(G)-S}d_{G}(w)

\displaystyle=2|E(V(G)-S)|+|E(V(G)-S,S)|=2\rho(V(G)-S)-|E(V(G)-S,S)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\lfloor\alpha\ell|V(G)-S|\rfloor-|E(V(G)-S,S)|<2\lfloor\alpha\ell|V(G)-S|\rfloor\leq 2\alpha\ell|V(G)-S|.

since
|E(V(G)-S,S)|>0
.
By the Pigeonhole Principle, the minimum degree among these vertices,
\delta^{\prime}
, must be strictly less than
2\alpha\ell
.
∎

Finally, select a vertex
v\in V(G)-S
with degree exactly
\delta^{\prime}
. Let
H
be the subgraph induced by
v
and its neighborhood
N(v)
. The number of vertices in
H
is
\delta^{\prime}+1<1+2\alpha\ell
.
By Claim
2
, any
u\in V(H)
with
u\notin S
has
d_{H}(u)=1+|N(u)\cap N(v)|>\alpha\ell
. For any
u\in S_{i}
, its degree within
H
is strictly greater than
\alpha\ell-1-|N(v)\cap S-N[u]|+1\geq\alpha\ell-|S_{i}|+1
, since
u
is adjacent all vertices in
S-S_{i}
.
This constructed subgraph
H
completely fulfills all requirements of the theorem.
∎

4.
Dense graphs contain knitted subgraphs

In this section, we show the dense graph
H
from Section
3
contains a knitted subgraph. This is a critical ingredient for a proof of our main result.

We will make use of the following result:

Theorem 7

(Liu, Rolek, and Yu
[
7
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n\geq 2\ell+3
vertices, where
\ell\geq 5
. If
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n+\ell}{2}-1
, then
G
is
\ell
-knitted.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be an integer, and let
\alpha\geq 4
. Let
H
be a graph with
|V(H)|\leq\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil
, let
T\subseteq V(H)
with
|T|\leq\ell
. For any
S\subseteq V(H)
such that
T\subseteq S
and
|S|=\ell
, and a partition of
S=\cup_{i=1}^{t}S_{i}
with
s_{i}=|S_{i}|
, if
v\in V(H)
,

d(v)\geq\begin{cases}1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor,&\mbox{if $v\in V(H)-T$};\\
1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-s_{i},&\mbox{if $v\in T\cap S_{i}$}.\end{cases}

then
H
contains an
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{t})
-knitted subgraph.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that there is a subset
S\subseteq V(H)
with
T\subseteq S
and
|S|=\ell
, and a partition
S=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}S_{i}
such that
H
does not contain disjoint connected subgraphs corresponding to each
S_{i}
. We analyze a
partial

(\ell,t)
-knit
C=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}C_{i}
, which is a subgraph of
H
wherein
S_{i}\subseteq V(C_{i})
for all
i
, but the components
C_{i}
are not strictly required to be connected.

We define an
optimal

(\ell,t)
-knit
C=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}C_{i}
as a partial
(\ell,t)
-knit that satisfies the following conditions in decreasing order of priority:

(a)

|V(C)|\leq\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)
;

(b)

the total number of connected components of
C
is minimized;

(c)

subject to (a) and (b),
|V(C)|
is minimized.

By the optimality of
C
, any component of
C
containing exactly one vertex of
S
must be an isolated vertex, and any component containing exactly two vertices of
S
must be a path connecting them. We may assume without loss of generality that
S_{1}\subseteq V(C_{1})
, but
C_{1}
is not connected. Thus, there exist
x,y\in S_{1}
belonging to different connected components of
C_{1}
.

Since
|V(C)|\leq\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)\leq d(x)
and
x\in C
,
d(x,H-C)\geq 1
; in particular,
H-C\neq\emptyset
.

Lemma 1
.

\delta^{*}\geq(\alpha-1)\ell-c
, where
c=|\{s_{i}\geq 2:1\leq i\leq t\mbox{ and $C_{i}$ is connected}\}|
.

Proof.

For every
u\in V(H-C)
, we have

d(u,H-C)=d(u,H)-d(u,C)\geq 1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-d(u,C)\geq\alpha\ell-d(u,C).

We now bound
d(u,C)
. Let
C_{i}
be the forest containing
S_{i}
in
C
. Then if
s_{i}=1
, the component
C_{i}
is a single vertex, so
d(u,C_{i})=1=s_{i}
; if
s_{i}\geq 2
, Theorem
4
implies
d(u,C_{i})\leq s_{i}+1
, with equality only if
C_{i}
is a tree and
s_{i}\geq 2
. It follows that

d(u,C)=\sum_{C_{i}}d(u,C_{i})\leq|S|+c=\ell+c.

The lemma follows.
∎

Since
x,y
are not in the same component, so
c\leq\frac{\ell-2}{2}
; furthermore, for each
S_{i}\subseteq S
with
x,y\not\in S_{i}
,

c\leq\frac{\ell-2-s_{i}}{2}+1=\frac{\ell-s_{i}}{2}.

Lemma 2
.

The subgraph
H-C
is connected.

Proof.

Suppose that
H-C
has components
H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}
where
p\geq 2
. Since
H_{i}
does not inherently contain an
\ell
-knit, and
|V(H_{i})|\geq\delta^{*}\geq 2.5\ell
, by Lemma
1
and
c\leq\ell/2
and
\alpha\geq 4
. By Theorem
7
,
\delta^{*}<(|V(H_{i})|+\ell)/2-1
. It follows that

|V(H_{i})|>2\delta^{*}-\ell+2\geq(2\alpha-3)\ell-2c+2\geq(2\alpha-4)\ell+2.

Since
p\geq 2
, we sum the sizes:

|V(H)|\geq|V(C)|+|V(H_{1})|+|V(H_{2})|>\ell+2(2\alpha-4)\ell+4
.
Given
|V(H)|<\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil
, this implies
\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil>(4\alpha-7)\ell+4
, which simplifies to
(2\alpha-7)\ell\leq-3
. This contradicts our hypothesis that
\alpha\geq 3.5
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

|V(C)|\geq\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)-4
.

Proof.

Assume that
|V(C)|\leq\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)-5
.
Let
A=N(x)\cap V(H-C)
and
B=N(y)\cap V(H-C)
. Let
A^{\prime}=N(A)\cap V(H-C)-A
and
B^{\prime}=N(B)\cap V(H-C)-B
. Finally, let
D=V(H-C)-(A\cup A^{\prime}\cup B\cup B^{\prime})
. Since
H-C
is connected, there is a path from
x
to
y
through
A\cup A^{\prime}\cup D\cup B^{\prime}\cup B
. If such a path has length at most
6
, we could append this path to
C
to form a new knit
C^{\prime}
. This new knit
C^{\prime}
would strictly reduce the number of connected components. Furthermore, its size would be bounded by
|V(C^{\prime})|\leq|V(C)|+5\leq\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)-5+5=\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)
. This directly contradicts the assumption that
C
is an optimal knit.

Since no such short path exists, take
u\in D-N(A^{\prime})
;
u
has no neighbors in
A^{\prime}\cup A
. Take
v\in A
; the pair
u,y
share no common neighbors, nor do
v,y
.
Consequently, we can bound the size of
H
:

\displaystyle|V(H)|

\displaystyle\geq 3+d(y)+d(u,H-C)+d(v,H-C)\geq 3+\delta(H)+2\delta^{*}

\displaystyle\geq 3+\alpha\ell-s_{i}+(2\alpha-2)\ell-2c=3+(3\alpha-2)\ell-s_{i}-2c

\displaystyle\geq 3+(3\alpha-2)\ell-s_{i}-2\cdot(\ell-s_{i})/2=3+(3\alpha-3)\ell.

Since
|V(H)|\leq\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil\leq 2\alpha\ell+1
, this forces
2\alpha\ell+1\geq 3+(3\alpha-3)\ell
, yielding
(\alpha-3)\ell\leq-2
. This blatantly contradicts
\alpha\geq 3
.
∎

Therefore,
|V(C)|\geq\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)-4
. Then we have:

|V(H-C)|=|V(H)|-|V(C)|\leq\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil-\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)+4.

Since
|V(H-C)|\geq\delta^{*}+1\geq(\alpha-1)\ell-c+1\geq 2\ell+3
, we try to apply Theorem
7
. Since
H-C
is not
\ell
-knitted, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle>2\delta^{*}-\left(|V(H-C)|+\ell-2\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2+2\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-2d(u,C)-\big(\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil-\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)+4\big)-\ell+2

\displaystyle=\min_{z\in S}d_{H}(z)-2d(u,C)+(2\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil-\ell)

\displaystyle\geq(1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-s_{i})-2d(u,C)-\ell-(2\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil)

\displaystyle\geq(1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor)-s_{i}-2(\ell+c)-\ell-(2\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil)

\displaystyle\geq(1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor)-s_{i}-2\ell-2\cdot(\ell-s_{i})/2-\ell-(2\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil)

\displaystyle\geq(1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor)-4\ell-(2\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-\lceil 2\alpha\ell\rceil)

\displaystyle\geq(\alpha-4)\ell.

where
d(u,H-C)=\delta^{*}
for
u\in H-C
,
\min_{z\in S}d(z)=d(z)\geq 1+\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor-|S_{i}|
for
z\in S_{i}
. We used that
d(u,C)=\sum_{j}d(u,C_{j})\leq\ell+c
, and
c\leq(\ell-|S_{i}|)/2
.
A contradiction to
\alpha\geq 4
.
∎

Combining Theorem
6
and Theorem
8
, we deduce the following result, which encompasses Theorem
2
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\ell
be an integer. Let
G
be a graph and
S\subseteq V(G)
be a subset of size
\ell
. If
(G,S)
is
(4,\ell)
-massed, then
(G,S)
is knitted.

Proof.

Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists a
(4,\ell)
-massed graph that is not
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{t})
-knitted. We may select
G
such that
(G,S)
is
(4,\ell)
-minimal. By Theorems
6
and
8
,
G
contains no rigid separation of order at most
\ell
, but it strictly contains an
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{t})
-knitted subgraph
K
. Let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}
with
s_{i}=|S_{i}|
be a partition of
S
that is not knitted.

By Menger’s Theorem, if there are
\ell
vertex-disjoint paths from
S
to
K
, we can uniquely associate each vertex in
S
to a distinct terminal in
K
(take the first vertices on the paths that are on
K
). Take the corresponding partition of the terminals in
K
which can be connected via disjoint connected subgraphs inside
K
. Tracing back through the paths resolves the knit in
G
.

If no such
\ell
disjoint paths exist, there must be a separation
(A,B)
separating
S
from
K
with
S\subseteq A
and
K\subseteq B
, such that its order
|A\cap B|
is strictly less than
\ell
. We choose
(A,B)
to have the minimum possible order. By Menger’s Theorem again, there are
|A\cap B|
disjoint paths from
A\cap B
to
K
. The identical routing logic demonstrates that for any partition of
A\cap B
, the parts can be connected via
K
. Thus, the induced subgraph
G[B]
securely knits
A\cap B
. This perfectly satisfies Definition
3
, meaning
(A,B)
is a rigid separation of order at most
\ell-1
, squarely contradicting the guarantee of Theorem
6
.
∎
