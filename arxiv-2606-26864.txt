Curvatures of curves in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} endowed with a canonical linear connection
Muhittin Evren Aydin, Rafael López

Abstract
. We investigate the geometry of plane curves in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} endowed with a canonical linear connection. By introducing the notions of the tangential and geodesic curvatures, we prove the existence and uniqueness theorems for curves with prescribed curvatures. We find all curves with constant curvature and we generalize the curves whose curvatures are linear functions of the arc length parameter.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53A04; Secondary 53B05, 53C22

1.
Introduction and main results

The study of linear connections on Riemannian manifolds that deviate from the classical Levi-Civita connection has a rich history in differential geometry. A prominent role is played by semi-symmetric connections, introduced by Friedmann and Schouten
[
2
]
and later systematically developed by Yano
[
11
]
.

Let
(M,\langle,\rangle)
be a Riemannian manifold and let
\nabla
be a linear connection on
M
. The torsion tensor
\mathcal{T}
of
\nabla
is defined by

\mathcal{T}(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-[X,Y],\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M),

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the Lie bracket of vector fields. We say that
\nabla
is
symmetric
if
\mathcal{T}=0
identically. Moreover, the connection
\nabla
is said to
semi-symmetric
if there is a nonzero vector field
\mathsf{W}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
such that

\mathcal{T}(X,Y)=\langle\mathsf{W},Y\rangle X-\langle\mathsf{W},X\rangle Y,

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. If, in addition,
\nabla\langle,\rangle=0
(respectively,
\nabla\langle,\rangle\neq 0
) then
\nabla
is called a
semi-symmetric metric connection
(resp,
semi-symmetric non-metric connection
).

The notion of a semi-symmetric metric connection was introduced by Hayden
[
4
]
whereas that of a non-metric was introduced by Agashe and Chafle
[
1
]
. Over the last decades, these connections have been deeply investigated in relation to hypersurfaces, submanifolds, and curvature inequalities (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
).

We assume that the ambient space
M
is the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, equipped with the Euclidean metric
\langle,\rangle
and let
\mathsf{W}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Let
m,n
be smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, not both identically zero. In this paper, we consider the linear connection

(1)

\nabla_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}^{0}Y+m\langle\mathsf{W},Y\rangle X+n\langle X,Y\rangle\mathsf{W},\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

where
\nabla^{0}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
associated with the metric
\langle,\rangle
. Recall that the torsion tensor
\mathcal{T}^{0}
of
\nabla^{0}
satisfies
\mathcal{T}^{0}=0
and
\nabla^{0}\langle,\rangle=0
. Moreover, if
m=n=0
, then
\nabla=\nabla^{0}
.

The connection given by (
1
) is a special case of the connection considered in
[
10
, Equation (2.4)]
, which corresponds to the choices

\varphi_{1}=-m\mathrm{Id},\quad\varphi=0,\quad U=-U_{2}=-\mathsf{W},\quad f_{1}=0,\qquad f_{2}=-(m+n).

A direct calculation shows that the linear connection
\nabla
given by (
1
) and its torsion tensor
\mathcal{T}
satisfy the following relations:

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=m(\langle\mathsf{W},Y\rangle X-\langle\mathsf{W},X\rangle Y),

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\langle,\rangle)(Y,Z)

\displaystyle=-(m+n)(\langle X,Y\rangle\langle\mathsf{W},Z\rangle+\langle X,Z\rangle\langle\mathsf{W},Y\rangle),

for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Hence, the connection
\nabla
given by (
1
) is

(i)

symmetric (resp. semi-symmetric) if
m
is identically zero (resp. nowhere zero) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
;

(ii)

metric (resp. non-metric) if
m+n
is identically zero (resp. nowhere zero) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

When we state that a function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is nowhere zero, we mean that there is no open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
on which the function vanishes identically. Thus, when dealing with the non-metric the case, the functions
m
and
n
must be chosen appropriately. For example, let
(x,y)
be the canonical coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and suppose that
n=x+y-m.
Then
m+n=x+y,

which vanishes along the straight line
y=-x
. Therefore, the choice
m+n
fails to satisfy our assumption.

While much of the literature focuses on curvature tensors of the ambient space or general submanifold theory, the intrinsic geometry of curves under these non-standard connections remains largely unexplored, especially when the vector field
\mathsf{W}
possesses a canonical nature. In this paper, we bridge this gap by investigating the geometry of curves in the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
endowed with a linear connection
\nabla
, where the vector field
\mathsf{W}
is a constant unit vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The presence of this vector field, together with two functions
m
and
n
, breaks the isotropic nature of the Euclidean plane and induces a non-trivial spatial distortion.

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the only work explicitly considering curves with respect to semi-symmetric connections is
[
3
]
. In that paper, the author studied geodesics with respect to a semi-symmetric metric connection in three-dimensional Euclidean space, as well as in three-dimensional Kenmotsu and Sasakian manifolds.

The choice of the linear connection
\nabla
given by (
1
) depends on the selection of the vector field
\mathsf{W}
. One of the simplest examples of such connections occurs when
\mathsf{W}
is one of the two canonical vector fields
\partial_{x}
or
\partial_{y}
. More generally, in this paper, we will consider that
\mathsf{W}
is a constant unit vector field and thus, there is
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathsf{W}=\cos\theta_{0}\,\partial_{x}+\sin\theta_{0}\,\partial_{y}.

Definition 1.1
.

A linear connection
\nabla
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the form (
1
) is called a canonical linear connection if
\mathsf{W}
is a constant unit vector field. In short, such a connection will be called a cl-connection.

The geometric properties of
\nabla
are therefore determined by the vector field
\mathsf{W}
together with the functions
m
and
n
. In particular, the torsion tensor
\mathcal{T}
and the covariant derivative of the metric depend on these functions. Consequently, the connection
\nabla
is completely determined by the triplet
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
.

Convention 1.2
.

Unless otherwise stated, the expression ”the cl-connection
\nabla
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
” refers to the linear connection
\nabla
defined by (
1
), where
\mathsf{W}
is a constant unit vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Let
\gamma:I\subset\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\gamma=\gamma(s)
, be a smooth curve parametrized by arc length. Denote by
T
and
N
the unit tangent and normal vector fields along
\gamma
, respectively. Along this paper,
\kappa
denotes the Frenet curvature of
\gamma
. The Frenet equations are given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{T}^{0}T

\displaystyle=\kappa N

\displaystyle\nabla_{T}^{0}N

\displaystyle=-\kappa T,

where
\nabla^{0}
is the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Using (
1
), we then obtain:

\displaystyle\nabla_{T}T

\displaystyle=(m+n)\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle T+(\kappa+n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle)N,

\displaystyle\nabla_{T}N

\displaystyle=(m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle-\kappa)T.

To motivate the new curvatures for the connection
\nabla
, recall that in the standard setting, the geodesics of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are straight lines. If
\gamma
is a geodesic, then its Frenet curvature
\kappa
is identically zero. In fact, when
\gamma
is not necessarily parametrized by arc length, straight lines are characterized not only by the vanishing of their Frenet curvature but also by the following two additional properties:

(1)

\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{0}\gamma^{\prime}
is linearly dependent on
\gamma^{\prime}
;

(2)

the unit normal vector field
N
along
\gamma
is parallel, that is,
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{0}N=0
.

Using these facts, we combine the normal acceleration component of
\nabla_{T}T
and the tangential component of
\nabla_{T}N
into a single quadratic scalar. This motivates the following definition of geodesic curvature. Meanwhile, we also define the tangential curvature as the scalar part of the tangential component of
\nabla_{T}T
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
and let
\gamma\colon I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth curve parametrized by arc length. With respect to
\nabla
,

(1)

The tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}
of
\gamma
is defined by

(3)

\kappa_{t}=(m+n)\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle.

(2)

The geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}\geq 0
of
\gamma
is defined by

(4)

\kappa_{g}^{2}=(\kappa+n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle)^{2}+(m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle-\kappa)^{2}.

In addition, if
\kappa_{g}=0
along
\gamma
, then
\gamma
is said to be a geodesic with respect to the connection
\nabla
.

With these definitions, a geodesic curve
\gamma
with respect to a cl-connection
\nabla
satisfies that
\nabla_{T}T
is parallel to
T
and that
\nabla_{T}N=0
, where
T=\gamma^{\prime}
and
N
are the unit tangent and normal vector fields along
\gamma
, respectively.

The main purpose of this paper is to establish the fundamental theorem of curve theory: the existence and uniqueness of
curves with prescribed (tangential or geodesic) curvatures. The first main result solves this problem for a
prescribed tangential curvature via quadratures (see Theorem
3.2
). Our second main result addresses the
prescribed geodesic curvature problem. Because the geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}
involves the Frenet curvature along with the orthogonal projection of the vector field
\mathsf{W}
, prescribing
\kappa_{g}
translates into a more complex problem involving
differential equations. Nevertheless, we prove that given a prescribed function
k(s)>0
, there is a unique
curve whose geodesic curvature is
k(s)
, which is governed by a first-order non-linear system of ordinary
differential equations for the tangent angle (see Theorem
4.3
). More precisely, for any given initial position and direction,
there exists a unique curve realizing this geodesic curvature, provided a strict algebraic discriminant condition holds at the
initial point (see Theorem
4.5
).

The present paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the behavior of the generalized curvatures under geometric transformations, along with the study of their total integrals for simple closed curves.
Section
3
is devoted to the analysis of plane curves with a prescribed tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}
, determining those curves where
\kappa_{t}=0
identically, proving the theorem of existence and uniqueness for a prescribed tangential curvature, and characterizing those curves with constant tangential curvature. In Section
4
, the development is analogous for the geodesic curvature. We characterize the geodesics of the connection, establish the existence and uniqueness theorems for curves with prescribed geodesic curvature and finally, classify those curves with constant geodesic curvature. We also offer a geometric interpretation of these curvatures via the relative angle function (see Theorem
4.8
). Finally, in Section
5
, we construct explicit parametrizations for the modified
\nabla
-clothoids and logarithmic
\nabla
-spirals.

2.
Preliminaries

For the connection given by (
1
), we assume that the connection functions
m
and
n
are not both identically zero, as otherwise it reduces to the Levi-Civita connection. A first question arises as to what happens with the notions of tangential and geodesic curvatures when one of the connection functions vanishes. The following result is immediate from Definition

1.3
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\gamma(s)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth curve parametrized by arc length. Let
\kappa_{t}
and
\kappa_{g}
denote the tangential curvature and the geodesic curvature of
\gamma
with respect to the cl-connection determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, respectively.

(i)

If
m=0
, then

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}

\displaystyle=n\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle,

\displaystyle\kappa_{g}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(\kappa+n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle)^{2}+\kappa^{2}}.

(ii)

If
n=0
, then

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}

\displaystyle=m\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle,

\displaystyle\kappa_{g}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+(m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle-\kappa)^{2}}.

Next, we investigate how the geometry induced by a cl-connection behaves under transformations of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since
\mathsf{W}
is
a constant vector field and the Frenet curvature
\kappa
is translation-invariant, it is straightforward to see that translations of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
do not affect
\kappa_{t}
or
\kappa_{g}
. However, this does not hold for dilations. In
classical Euclidean geometry, scaling a curve by a factor
c>0
simply scales its Frenet curvature
\kappa
by
1/c
. However, the presence of the vector field
\mathsf{W}
and the functions
m
and
n
introduces a scale distortion.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
. Let
\gamma(s)
be a smooth curve parametrized by arc length and let
\tilde{\gamma}(\tilde{s})=c\gamma(s)
be its homothetic transformation by a scale factor
c>0
, where
\tilde{s}=cs
is the arc length of
\tilde{\gamma}
. Then, the tangential and geodesic curvatures of
\tilde{\gamma}
with respect to
\nabla
are given by:

(5)

\begin{split}\tilde{\kappa}_{t}(\tilde{s})&=\left(\tilde{m}+\tilde{n}\right)\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle,\\
\tilde{\kappa}_{g}^{2}(\tilde{s})&=\left(\frac{\kappa(s)}{c}+\tilde{n}\langle\mathsf{W},N(s)\rangle\right)^{2}+\left(\tilde{m}\langle\mathsf{W},N(s)\rangle-\frac{\kappa(s)}{c}\right)^{2},\end{split}

where
\tilde{m}(\tilde{s})=m(c\gamma(s))
and
\tilde{n}(\tilde{s})=n(c\gamma(s))
.

Proof.

The proof is a direct computation using the definitions of the tangential curvature and geodesic curvature of
\tilde{\gamma}
given in (
3
) and (
4
). Let
\tilde{\gamma}(\tilde{s})=c\gamma(\tilde{s}/c)
be the scaled curve. Taking the derivative with respect to the new arc length parameter
\tilde{s}
, the unit tangent vector is

\tilde{T}(\tilde{s})=\frac{d\tilde{\gamma}}{d\tilde{s}}=c\frac{d\gamma}{ds}\frac{ds}{d\tilde{s}}=T(s).

Since the tangent vector remains invariant, so does the normal vector, meaning
\tilde{N}(\tilde{s})=N(s)
. Furthermore, taking the second derivative yields

\frac{d\tilde{T}}{d\tilde{s}}=\frac{dT}{ds}\frac{ds}{d\tilde{s}}=\frac{\kappa(s)}{c}N(s),

which recovers the classical scaling law for Euclidean curves:
\tilde{\kappa}(\tilde{s})=\frac{\kappa(s)}{c}
. Moreover,
\langle\mathsf{W},\widetilde{T}\rangle=\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle
and
\langle\mathsf{W},\widetilde{N}\rangle=\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle
.

However, the connection functions
m
and
n
depend on the spatial position and must be evaluated at the new coordinates
\tilde{\gamma}(\tilde{s})=c\gamma(s)
. Substituting these scaled elements
\tilde{m}
,
\tilde{n}
,
\widetilde{T}
,
\widetilde{N}
, and
\tilde{\kappa}
directly into Definition
1.3
yields the expressions in (
5
).
∎

In the classical theory of planar curves, the integral of the Frenet curvature along a simple closed curve is a topological invariant, evaluating to
\pm 2\pi
by the well-known Hopf’s Umlaufsatz (see
[
9
, Theorem 3.1.4]
). It is a natural question to ask whether the total generalized tangential curvature exhibits a similar behavior.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\gamma(s)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth simple closed curve parametrized by arc length, bounding a compact domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
for some
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the total tangential curvature of
\gamma
with respect to
\nabla
is given by

(6)

\oint_{\gamma}\kappa_{t}(s)\,ds=\iint_{\Omega}\left(\sin\theta_{0}\frac{\partial(m+n)}{\partial x}-\cos\theta_{0}\frac{\partial(m+n)}{\partial y}\right)dxdy.

In particular, if
m+n=\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is constant, then
\oint_{\gamma}\kappa_{t}(s)\,ds=0
.

Proof.

From (
3
), we know that
\kappa_{t}(s)=(m+n)\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle=(m+n)\langle\mathsf{W},\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle
. On
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, define the vector field

V(x,y)=(m(x,y)+n(x,y))\mathsf{W}.

Writing
V
in coordinates, we have
V(x,y)=(P(x,y),Q(x,y))
where

P(x,y)=(m+n)\cos\theta_{0},\quad Q(x,y)=(m+n)\sin\theta_{0}.

Applying Green’s Theorem to
\gamma
, we obtain

\oint_{\gamma}\langle V,T\rangle\,ds=\oint_{\gamma}P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dxdy.

Substituting the partial derivatives of
P
and
Q
yields (
6
). The last statement is immediate.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Unlike the total Frenet curvature, the total tangential curvature is influenced by the functions
m
and
n
, as shown in Proposition
2.3
. Thus, its value is not uniquely determined by the topology of the curve. However, the total tangential curvature vanihes identically whenever
m+n
is constant. In particular, this is the case for metric cl-connections.

In contrast to the tangential curvature, the total geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}
along a closed curve is more difficult due to its non-linear, quadratic definition (see (
4
)). However, we can establish a lower bound for the total geodesic curvature of simple closed curves.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\gamma
be a smooth simple closed curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
parametrized by arc length
s
. Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
. Then, the total geodesic curvature with respect to
\nabla
satisfies the inequality

(7)

\oint_{\gamma}\kappa_{g}(s)\,ds\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|4\pi+\oint_{\gamma}(n-m)\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle\,ds\right|.

If
m=n
, we have

(8)

\oint_{\gamma}\kappa_{g}(s)\,ds\geq 2\sqrt{2}\pi.

Proof.

From (
4
), we write
\kappa_{g}=\sqrt{A^{2}+B^{2}}
, where
A=\kappa+n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle
and
B=m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle-\kappa
. Utilizing the inequality
\sqrt{A^{2}+B^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A-B|
for any real numbers
A
and
B
, we have

A-B=(\kappa+n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle)-(m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle-\kappa)=2\kappa+(n-m)\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle.

This allows us to bound
\kappa_{g}
by

\kappa_{g}(s)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|2\kappa(s)+(n-m)\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle\right|.

Integrating both sides along
\gamma
, we obtain

\oint_{\gamma}\kappa_{g}(s)\,ds\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|2\oint_{\gamma}\kappa(s)\,ds+\oint_{\gamma}(n-m)\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle\,ds\right|.

Since
\gamma
is a simple closed curve, Hopf’s Umlaufsatz states that
\oint_{\gamma}\kappa(s)\,ds=2\pi
(assuming a counterclockwise orientation). Substituting this into the above inequality yields (
7
). If
m=n
, then we arrive at (
8
),
which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The bound given in (
8
) is sharp. In the case
m=n=0
(
\nabla=\nabla^{0}
), we have
A=-B=\kappa
, which means
\kappa_{g}=\sqrt{2}|\kappa|
. For any convex simple closed curve where
\kappa>0
, the integral yields
\oint_{\gamma}\kappa_{g}\,ds=2\sqrt{2}\pi
.

3.
Tangential curvature of plane curves

In this section, we investigate the tangential curvature of plane curves with respect to a cl-connection
\nabla
. Recall that the tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}
vanishes identically whenever
\nabla
is a metric cl-connection. The following result characterizes the curves for which
\kappa_{t}
vanishes identically in the non-metric case.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\nabla
be a non-metric cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
for some
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\gamma
be a smooth curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
parametrized by arc length. The tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}
of
\gamma
with respect to
\nabla
vanishes identically if and only if

\gamma(s)=(-\sin\theta_{0},\cos\theta_{0})s+\vec{w},\quad\vec{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\kappa_{t}
in (
3
), we have
(m+n)\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle=0
, where
T
is the unit tangent vector field of
\gamma
. Since
\nabla
is non-metric, we have
m+n\neq 0
along
\gamma
, which implies
\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle=0
. Since
\mathsf{W}
is constant, it follows that
T
is constant and thus
\gamma
is a straight line of the stated form. The converse follows directly from (
3
).
∎

When
\nabla
is non-metric, not every straight line has vanishing tangential curvature. In particular, straight lines normal to
\mathsf{W}
satisfy
\kappa_{t}=0
.

Let
R_{\theta}
denote a rotation about the origin
(0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
by an angle
\theta
. The following result establishes the existence and uniqueness of plane curves whose tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}
is prescribed.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\nabla
be a non-metric cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
for some
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let also
k:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a nowhere zero smooth function and
|k|\leq|m+n|
. Then, there exists a smooth curve
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
, parametrized by arc length
such that the tangential curvature of
\gamma
with respect to
\nabla
is
k
. In addition, up to translations of

\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the curve
\gamma
is uniquely given by

(9)

\gamma(s)=R_{\theta_{0}}\circ\left(\pm\int^{s}f(t)dt,\pm\int^{s}\sqrt{1-f(t)^{2}}dt\right),

where
f(s):=\frac{k(s)}{(m+n)(\gamma(s))}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\kappa_{t}
in (
3
), we get

(10)

\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle=\frac{k}{m+n}=f.

Note that
|f|\leq 1
along
\gamma
, because
\mathsf{W}
and
T
are unit vector fields and
k
is nowhere zero. Taking the derivative of (
10
) with respect to the Levi-Civita connection and applying the Frenet formulas, we find

(11)

\kappa\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=f^{\prime},

where
\kappa
is the Frenet curvature of
\gamma
.

Assume that
\gamma
is not a straight line. Then,
f
is a non-constant function. Notice that the proof remains true when
\gamma
is a straight line. Since
\mathsf{W}
is a unit vector field, we have

\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle^{2}+\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}=1
which, in combination of (
10
) and (
11
), leads to

\kappa=\pm\frac{f^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1-f^{2}}}.

Integrating, we derive

(12)

\displaystyle\int^{s}\kappa(u)\,du=\begin{cases}\pm\sin^{-1}(f)+\theta,\\
\mp\cos^{-1}(f)+\theta\end{cases}

where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. Recall that
\gamma
is uniquely determined by its Frenet curvature
\kappa
via the expression

(13)

\gamma(s)=\left(\int^{s}\cos\left(\int^{t}\kappa(u)\,du\right)dt,\int^{s}\sin\left(\int^{t}\kappa(u)\,du\right)dt\right).

By taking the inverse cosine in (
12
) and substituting it into (
13
), we obtain the parametrization given by (
9
).
On the other hand, to determine the constant
\theta
, we analyze the alignment of the tangent vector. Let
\alpha(s)=\int^{s}\kappa(u)du
be the angle function from (
13
), so that
T(s)=(\cos\alpha(s),\sin\alpha(s))
. Computing the
inner product yields
\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle=\cos(\alpha(s)-\theta_{0})=f(s)
. Substituting the angular relation from (
12
)
into this identity and expanding it via standard trigonometric formulas for the sum and difference of angles, we obtain

(\cos\theta_{0}\cos\theta+\sin\theta_{0}\sin\theta-1)f(s)\pm(\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta-\cos\theta_{0}\sin\theta)\sqrt{1-f(s)^{2}}=0,

for all
s
, or equivalently,

(\cos(\theta_{0}-\theta)-1)f(s)\pm\sin(\theta_{0}-\theta)\sqrt{1-f(s)^{2}}=0.

Since
f
is non-constant, the functions
f(s)
and
\sqrt{1-f(s)^{2}}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, their coefficients must vanish simultaneously, which implies
\cos(\theta_{0}-\theta)=1
, and consequently
\theta=\theta_{0}
. Substituting this values into the system (
13
) yields the parametrization (
9
).

Conversely, differentiating (
9
) yields the unit tangent vector

T(s)=R_{\theta_{0}}\circ(f(s),\pm\sqrt{1-f(s)^{2}}).

Since
\mathsf{W}=R_{\theta_{0}}\circ(1,0)
, their inner product reduces to
\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle=f(s)
,
which verifies that
\kappa_{t}=(m+n)\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle=k
.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.2
, we classify the curves with constant tangential curvature.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\nabla
be a non-metric cl-connection determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
for any
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
be a prescribed constant. If
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a smooth curve parametrized by arc length with constant tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}=c
, then up to rigid translations,
\gamma
is uniquely given by

(14)

\gamma(s)=R_{\theta_{0}}\circ\left(\pm\int^{s}\frac{c}{m+n}\,dt,\,\pm\int^{s}\sqrt{1-\frac{c^{2}}{(m+n)^{2}}}\,dt\right),

where
|m+n|\geq|c|
along the trace of the curve. Furthermore,
\gamma
is a straight line if and only if
m+n
is constant along
\gamma
.

Proof.

By setting the prescribed curvature function
k(s)=c
in Theorem
3.2
, the function
f
reduces to
f(s)=\frac{c}{m+n}
.
Substituting this into (
9
) yields the parametric form (
14
). Since

|f(s)|\leq 1
, this implies the restriction
|m+n|\geq|c|
.

Finally, from equation (
11
), the classical Frenet curvature satisfies
\kappa\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=f^{\prime}
. If
m+n
is constant
along the curve, then
f^{\prime}=0
, which forces
\kappa=0
(since
\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle\neq 0
), confirming that

\gamma
is a straight line. Conversely, if
\gamma
is a straight line, then
\kappa=0
, which implies
f^{\prime}=0
,
meaning that
m+n
must remain constant along the trace of
\gamma
.
∎

To illustrate this result, we present an example.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the non-metric cl-connection
\nabla
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=\partial_{x}=(1,0)
(hence
\theta_{0}=0
) and the functions
m,n
satisfy
m(x,y)+n(x,y)=e^{x}
. Let us prescribe a constant tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}(s)=1
for
s\in[1,\infty)
. We aim to find the curve
\gamma
satisfying
\kappa_{t}(s)=1
. According to Corollary
3.3
, we set

f(s)=\frac{1}{m+n}=\frac{1}{s}

along the curve, which implies
(m+n)\circ\gamma(s)=s
. Notice that
|(m+n)\circ\gamma|\geq\kappa_{t}
for all
s
. Utilizing (
13
), we find the coordinates of
\gamma
:

\begin{split}x(s)&=\int^{s}f(t)\,dt=\int^{s}\frac{1}{t}\,dt=\log s,\\
y(s)&=\int^{s}\sqrt{1-f(t)^{2}}\,dt=\int^{s}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{t^{2}}}\,dt=\sqrt{s^{2}-1}-\arctan\left(\sqrt{s^{2}-1}\right).\end{split}

Thus, up to an initial translation, the curve is expressed as (see Fig.
1
.)

(15)

\gamma(s)=\left(\log s,\,\sqrt{s^{2}-1}-\arctan\left(\sqrt{s^{2}-1}\right)\right),\quad s\in[1,\infty).

Figure 1.
The curve defined by (
15
) with constant tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}(s)=1
for
s\in[1,3]
.

Remark 3.5
.

Example
3.4
demonstrates the lack of rotational invariance for the tangential curvature in contrast to the Euclidean geometry. If we rotate the curve
\gamma(s)
by an
angle of
\pi/4
around the origin, we obtain
\tilde{\gamma}(s)=R_{\pi/4}(\gamma(s))=(\tilde{x}(s),\tilde{y}(s))
, where its
new
x
-coordinate is
\tilde{x}(s)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(x(s)-y(s))
. Since the connection is
fixed, the tangential curvature of the rotated curve at its new spatial coordinates is

\tilde{\kappa}_{t}(s)=e^{\tilde{x}(s)}\langle\mathsf{W},\tilde{T}(s)\rangle=e^{\tilde{x}(s)}\tilde{x}^{\prime}(s)=\frac{d}{ds}\left(e^{\tilde{x}(s)}\right).

For
\tilde{\kappa}_{t}(s)
to remain identically
1
, we would need
e^{\tilde{x}(s)}=s+c
for some constant
c
, which implies
\tilde{x}(s)=\log(s+c)
. However, we have

\tilde{x}(s)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(\log s-\sqrt{s^{2}-1}+\arctan\left(\sqrt{s^{2}-1}\right)\right),

which shows that
\tilde{x}(s)\not=\log(s+c)
.

It is natural to ask under what conditions the new tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}
coincides with the Frenet curvature
\kappa
of a plane curve.
The following result addresses the question whether coincide along the curve.

Corollary 3.6
.

Under the same hypothesis of Theorem
3.2
, we have
\kappa_{t}=\kappa
if and only if

(16)

(m+n)\circ\gamma(s)=\frac{\kappa(s)}{\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle},

for all
s\in I
where
\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle\neq 0
. Alternatively, if
\theta(s)
denotes the angle between
\mathsf{W}
and the tangent vector
T(s)
, this condition holds if and only if

(17)

(m+n)\circ\gamma(s)=\frac{\theta^{\prime}(s)}{\cos\theta(s)}.

Proof.

Identity (
16
) is a consequence of setting
\kappa_{t}=\kappa
and the definition of
\kappa_{t}
in (
3
). Moreover, if
\cos\theta(s)=\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle
, we know that
\theta^{\prime}=\kappa
, whence (
16
) implies (
17
).
∎

We know that circles are the curves with non-zero constant curvature
\kappa
. In the following result, we ask when these circles have constant tangential curvature.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\nabla
be a non-metric cl-connection determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
for
some
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. A circle
\gamma
of radius
R>0
centered at the origin has a constant tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}=c
(with
c\neq 0
) if and only if the functions
m
and
n
satisfy the following relation along the trace of the circle:

(18)

m(x,y)+n(x,y)=\frac{cR}{\pm\sqrt{R^{2}-(x\cos\theta_{0}+y\sin\theta_{0})^{2}}},

where
|x\cos\theta_{0}+y\sin\theta_{0}|<R
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma(s)
be a circle of radius
R
which, after a translation, can be parametrized by
\gamma(s)=R(\cos(s/R),\sin(s/R))
. Define
u(x,y)=x\cos\theta_{0}+y\sin\theta_{0}
. Evaluating this function along the trace of the curve
\gamma(s)
yields

u(s)=R\cos\left(\frac{s}{R}-\theta_{0}\right).

Moreover,
\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle=-\sin\left(\frac{s}{R}-\theta_{0}\right)
.
Thus,
\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle=u^{\prime}(s)
. This gives

\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle=\pm\sqrt{1-\cos^{2}\left(\frac{s}{R}-\theta_{0}\right)}=\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{u(s)^{2}}{R^{2}}}.

From the definition (
3
) of
\kappa_{t}
, we have
\kappa_{t}(s)=(m+n)\circ\gamma(s)\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle
. Imposing the condition that
\kappa_{t}=c
and substituting, we obtain

c=(m+n)\circ\gamma(s)\left(\pm\frac{1}{R}\sqrt{R^{2}-u(s)^{2}}\right).

Solving for the sum of the functions
m,n
and reverting the curve parameter back to the ambient spatial coordinates
(x,y)
via
u=x\cos\theta_{0}+y\sin\theta_{0}
, we arrive at (
18
). The strict inequality restriction ensures that the denominator is non-zero, completing the proof.
∎

4.
Geodesic curvature of plane curves

In this section, we investigate the geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}
induced by a cl-connection. As in the previous section, this study is divided into three parts:
first, we classify the geodesics of the connection; second, we establish the existence result with a general prescribed geodesic
curvature function
k(s)
; and finally, we find the curves with constant geodesic curvature. We begin by classifying the geodesics of

\nabla
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
. A smooth curve
\gamma
parametrized by arc length is a geodesic with respect to
\nabla
if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\gamma
is a straight line parallel to the vector field
\mathsf{W}
.

(ii)

\nabla
is a metric connection (
m+n=0
) and the Frenet curvature of
\gamma
holds
\kappa=m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle
.

Proof.

From (
4
),
\gamma
is a geodesic if and only if

(19)

\displaystyle\kappa+n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

(20)

\displaystyle m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle-\kappa

\displaystyle=0.

Adding equations (
19
) and (
20
) yields

(21)

(m+n)\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=0.

(1)

Suppose
\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=0
along an interval. Then,
\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle=\pm 1
. Since
\mathsf{W}
is constant, the tangent direction of
\gamma
is fixed, meaning
\gamma
is a straight line parallel to
\mathsf{W}
. Substituting
\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=0
into either (
19
) or (
20
) yields
\kappa=0
. This gives that
\gamma
is a straight line.

(2)

Suppose
\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle\neq 0
along
\gamma
. Then, from (
21
), we must have
m+n=0
, which implies that
\nabla
is a semi-symmetric metric connection. Under this condition, equations (
19
) and (
20
) yield
\kappa=-n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle
.

Conversely, if condition (i) or (ii) holds, then the left-hand sides of (
19
) and (
20
) vanish identically, ensuring that
\kappa_{g}=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

In the case of a metric connection, a direct example is the following.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\nabla
be a metric cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=\partial_{x}=(1,0)
,
m(x,y)=-\frac{1}{x}
,
x\neq 0
, and
n=-m
. Consider the circle
\gamma(s)=2(\cos(\frac{s}{2}),\sin(\frac{s}{2}))
. Since
N(s)=-(\cos(\frac{s}{2}),\sin(\frac{s}{2}))
and
\kappa=1/2
, it follows that
\kappa(s)=(m\circ\gamma)(s)\langle\mathsf{W},N(s)\rangle
. Therefore, by item (ii) of Theorem
4.1
, this circle is a geodesic of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with respect to
\nabla
.

Next, we establish the existence and uniqueness theorem for curves whose geodesic curvature is prescribed by a generic smooth function
k:I\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
k:I\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a smooth function. Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
. If
\gamma
is a smooth curve parametrized by arc length whose geodesic curvature is
k(s)>0
, then its Frenet curvature
\kappa
satisfies

(22)

2\kappa^{2}-2\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle(m-n)\kappa+(m^{2}+n^{2})\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}-k(s)^{2}=0.

In particular,
\kappa
can be expressed as

(23)

\kappa=\frac{\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle(m-n)\pm\sqrt{2k(s)^{2}-\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}(m+n)^{2}}}{2},

provided that

(24)

2k(s)^{2}\geq\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}(m+n)^{2}.

Proof.

The condition
\kappa_{g}(s)=k(s)
is equivalent to

\displaystyle k(s)^{2}

\displaystyle=(\kappa+n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle)^{2}+(m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle-\kappa)^{2}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{2}+2n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle\kappa+n^{2}\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}+m^{2}\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}-2m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle\kappa+\kappa^{2}.

This identity reduces to (
22
). This is a quadratic equation in
\kappa
of the standard form
A\kappa^{2}+B\kappa+C=0
, where the coefficients are given by

A=2,\quad B=-2\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle(m-n),\quad C=(m^{2}+n^{2})\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}-k(s)^{2}.

The discriminant
\Delta=B^{2}-4AC
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{4}

\displaystyle=\left[\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle(m-n)\right]^{2}-2\left[(m^{2}+n^{2})\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}-k(s)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2k(s)^{2}-\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}(m+n)^{2}.

This yields (
23
). The non-negativity of this discriminant is equivalent to (
24
), completing the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Equation (
23
) can be interpreted as a non-linear first-order ordinary differential equation for the angle function
\theta(s)
of the curve. Indeed, let
\theta(s)
denote the angle that the unit tangent vector
T(s)
makes with the positive
x
-axis. Then
T(s)=(\cos\theta(s),\sin\theta(s))
and
N(s)=(-\sin\theta(s),\cos\theta(s))
. If
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
, then

\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle=\cos(\theta(s)-\theta_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\mathsf{W},N(s)\rangle=\sin(\theta(s)-\theta_{0}).

Since
\kappa=\theta^{\prime}(s)
, equation (
23
) is equivalent to

\theta^{\prime}(s)=\frac{(m-n)\sin(\theta(s)-\theta_{0})\pm\sqrt{2k(s)^{2}-(m+n)^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta(s)-\theta_{0})}}{2},

where the connection functions
m
and
n
are evaluated along the trace
\gamma(s)
.

We now establish the existence of curves with a prescribed geodesic curvature. This result is implicitly contained in
Theorem
4.3
because once
\kappa
is determined from (
22
) (or (
23
)), the existence of the
curve
\gamma
with Frenet curvature
\kappa
is guaranteed by the fundamental theorem of curve theory.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
k:I\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a smooth function with
0\in I
,
p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\theta_{0}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
. Assume that at
s=0
, the function
k
satisfies the strict inequality

(25)

2k(0)^{2}>\sin^{2}(\theta_{0}^{*}-\theta_{0})\left(m(p_{0})+n(p_{0})\right)^{2}.

Then, there exists a unique local smooth curve
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
parametrized by arc length such that
\gamma(0)=p_{0}
,
\theta(0)=\theta_{0}^{*}
, and the geodesic curvature of
\gamma
with respect to
\nabla
is identically
k(s)
.

Proof.

Let
\theta(s)
be the angle function that the unit tangent vector
T(s)
makes with the positive
x
-axis. As established in Remark
4.4
, we know that
\langle\mathsf{W},N(s)\rangle=\sin(\theta(s)-\theta_{0})
. Moreover,
\kappa(s)=\theta^{\prime}(s)
.
By substituting this into (
23
), the condition
\kappa_{g}(s)=k(s)
reduces to the following ODE for
\theta(s)
:

(26)

\theta^{\prime}(s)=\frac{(m-n)\sin(\theta-\theta_{0})\pm\sqrt{2k(s)^{2}-(m+n)^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta-\theta_{0})}}{2},

where the connection functions
m=m(x,y)
and
n=n(x,y)
are evaluated along the trace
\gamma(s)=(x(s),y(s))
. Moreover, the coordinate functions satisfy

(27)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\cos\theta(s),

(28)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\sin\theta(s).

Equations (
26
), (
27
), and (
28
) define a first-order autonomous system of non-linear ordinary differential equations for the vector
(x(s),y(s),\theta(s))
with the initial data
x(0)=x_{0}
,
y(0)=y_{0}
, and
\theta(0)=\theta_{0}^{*}
. By hypothesis, the condition (
25
) holds at
s=0
, ensuring that the argument within the radical is positive at the origin. By the standard existence and uniqueness theorems for ODEs, there exists a unique local solution
(x(s),y(s),\theta(s))
defined on a small open interval
I
centered at
s=0
. The curve
\gamma(s)=(x(s),y(s))
is parametrized by arc length and satisfies
\kappa_{g}(s)=k(s)
by construction. This completes the proof.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we can consider the case where the prescribed geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}
is a constant.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
. If a smooth curve
\gamma
parametrized by arc length has a constant geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}=c>0
, satisfying the restriction
2c^{2}\geq\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}(m+n)^{2}
, then its Frenet curvature
\kappa
satisfies

\kappa=\frac{\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle(m-n)\pm\sqrt{2c^{2}-\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}(m+n)^{2}}}{2}.

In the particular case that the cl-connection
\nabla
coincides with the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{0}
of the Euclidean plane (i.e.,
m=n=0
), a curve
\gamma
has constant geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}=c>0
if and only if
\kappa=\pm c
.

We give two examples of curves with constant geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}=1
. The examples depend on the choice of the connection functions
m
and
n
.

Example 4.7
.

Assume
\mathsf{W}=(1,0)
and
\kappa_{g}=1
identically along the curve
\gamma
.

(i)

Let
m=n=\frac{1}{2}
. Under these choices, the restriction
2c^{2}\geq\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}(m+n)^{2}
reduces to
2\geq\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}
, which is trivially satisfied since
\mathsf{W}
and
N
are unit vector fields. Substituting these parameters into Corollary
4.6
, the Frenet curvature
\kappa
must satisfy

\kappa=\pm\frac{\sqrt{2-\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}}}{2},

or equivalently,

\theta^{\prime}(s)=\pm\frac{\sqrt{2-\sin^{2}\theta(s)}}{2}.

By separating variables, this ODE can be rewritten in terms of elliptic integrals as

\int\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}\theta}}=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int\,ds.

The left-hand side is the incomplete elliptic integral of the first kind,
F(\theta,k)
, with modulus
k=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
. Therefore, by inverting this relation via the Jacobi amplitude function
\text{am}(u,k)
, we obtain

\theta(s)=\text{am}\left(\pm\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}+c,\,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right),

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is an integration constant determined by the initial orientation of the curve.

(ii)

Let
m=1
and
n=-1
. Then the restriction
2c^{2}\geq\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle^{2}(m+n)^{2}
reduces to
2\geq 0
, which is always satisfied. Since
\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=-\sin\theta(s)
, and
\kappa=\theta^{\prime}(s)
, then

\theta^{\prime}(s)=-\sin\theta(s)\pm\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.

Separation of variables leads to

\theta(s)=2\arctan\left(\sqrt{2}\pm\tan\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}(s+c)\right)\right),

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is an integration constant. Next, we compute the parametrization of the curve
\gamma
. For simplicity, we assume that
c=0
and choose the positive sign in the expression for
\theta
. Using the identities

\cos(2\arctan v)=\frac{1-v^{2}}{1+v^{2}},\quad\sin(2\arctan v)=\frac{2v}{1+v^{2}},

we have

(29)

\gamma(s)=\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{1-v(u)^{2}}{1+v(u)^{2}}\,du,\int_{0}^{s}\frac{2v(u)}{1+v(u)^{2}}\,du\right),\quad v(u):=\sqrt{2}+\tan(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}u).

The resulting curve is illustrated in Fig.
2
.

Figure 2.
A curve with constant geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}(s)=1
, defined by (
29
) for
s\in[0,10]
.

We finish the section by giving a geometric interpretation of curvatures via relative angles. In the classical Euclidean planar geometry, the Frenet curvature
\kappa
is the derivative of the tangent angle with respect to a fixed coordinate axis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this setting, we analyze how the unit tangent vector
T
varies relative to the reference field
\mathsf{W}
.

Let
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
be the prescribed constant unit vector field defining the cl-connection
\nabla
. Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth curve parametrized by arc length, and let
\theta(s)
denote the angle function that the unit tangent vector
T(s)
makes with the positive
x
-axis. Then we have:

T(s)=(\cos\theta(s),\sin\theta(s)),\quad N(s)=(-\sin\theta(s),\cos\theta(s)).

We introduce the
relative angle function

\psi(s)
, defined as the angle from the constant field
\mathsf{W}
to the unit tangent vector field
T(s)
along
\gamma
, which is given by

(30)

\psi(s)=\theta(s)-\theta_{0}.

The following theorem establishes a relationship between the curvatures and the relative angle function.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
. Let
\gamma
be a smooth curve parametrized by arc length, and let
\psi(s)
be its relative angle function with respect to
\mathsf{W}
. Then:

(31)

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}(s)

\displaystyle=(m+n)\cos\psi(s),

(32)

\displaystyle\kappa_{g}(s)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(\psi^{\prime}(s)-n\sin\psi(s))^{2}+(\psi^{\prime}(s)+m\sin\psi(s))^{2}},

where
m=m\circ\gamma
and
n=n\circ\gamma
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
, we have
\langle\mathsf{W},T(s)\rangle=\cos\psi(s)
and
\langle\mathsf{W},N(s)\rangle=-\sin\psi(s)
. From (
3
), the relation
\kappa_{t}=(m+n)\langle\mathsf{W},T\rangle
yields (
31
).

To prove (
32
), we differentiate the relative angle (
30
) with respect to the arc length parameter
s
to obtain
\psi^{\prime}(s)=\theta^{\prime}(s)=\kappa(s)
. Substituting
\kappa=\psi^{\prime}
and
\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=-\sin\psi(s)
into (
4
), we arrive at

\begin{split}\kappa_{g}^{2}&=(\kappa+n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle)^{2}+(m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle-\kappa)^{2}\\
&=(\psi^{\prime}-n\sin\psi)^{2}+(\psi^{\prime}+m\sin\psi)^{2}.\end{split}

∎

Remark 4.9
.

This result shows that the tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}
acts as a directional projection of the connection due to the factor
\cos\psi
. It vanishes when the curve moves orthogonally to the vector field
\mathsf{W}
(i.e.,
\psi=\pm\pi/2
), regardless of how sharply the curve is turning.

5.
Modified
\nabla
-spirals

In the classical differential geometry of plane curves, spirals are curves characterized by a strict monotonic relation between
their curvature and their arc length parameter. Two of the most important examples are clothoids (also known as the
Euler or Cornu spirals), where the curvature varies linearly as
\kappa(s)=s
, and logarithmic spirals, where

\kappa(s)=1/s
. The parametrization of clothoids involves the
Fresnel integrals, whereas for logarithmics spirals, the parametrizations are given in terms of logarithmic and trigonometric functions.

In our setting, we generalize both types of curves to obtain two new classes of curves: the

\nabla
-clothoids
and the
logarithmic
\nabla
-spirals
. Let
\nabla
be a
non-metric cl-connection determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
. To simplify the discussion, we will assume in this section that the connection functions satisfy the constancy condition

m(x,y)+n(x,y)=\lambda

along the curve, where
\lambda>0
is a constant. Recall that
R_{\theta_{0}}
represents the planar rotation matrix about the origin by the angle
\theta_{0}
.

We first study the analogue of the classical clothoid by investigating curves whose tangential curvature is
\kappa_{t}(s)=s
(see Fig.
3
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection determined by
(\mathsf{W},m,n)
, where
\mathsf{W}=(\cos\theta_{0},\sin\theta_{0})
and
m+n=\lambda>0
along the curve. Let

\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth curve parametrized by arc length with
\kappa_{t}(s)=s
for
s\in(-\lambda,\lambda)
. Then, up to a rigid translation,
\gamma(s)
is given by

(33)

\gamma(s)=R_{\theta_{0}}\circ\left(\pm\frac{s^{2}}{2\lambda},\,\pm\left[\frac{s}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{s^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\arcsin\left(\frac{s}{\lambda}\right)\right]\right).

Proof.

The result follows as a direct application of Theorem
3.2
. Since

\kappa_{t}(s)=s
, and
m+n=\lambda>0
, the auxiliary function is

f(s)=\frac{\kappa_{t}(s)}{m+n}=\frac{s}{\lambda}.

Since
\mathsf{W}
and
T
are unit vector fields, the condition
|f(s)|\leq 1
imposes the domain restriction
|s|\leq\lambda
. Substituting
f(t)=\frac{t}{\lambda}
into (
9
), we evaluate the two corresponding
integrals. The first integral yields

\int^{s}f(t)\,dt=\int^{s}\frac{t}{\lambda}\,dt=\frac{s^{2}}{2\lambda}.

The second integral is a standard trigonometric integral whose value is

\int^{s}\sqrt{1-f(t)^{2}}\,dt=\int^{s}\sqrt{1-\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\,dt=\frac{s}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{s^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\arcsin\left(\frac{s}{\lambda}\right).

Inserting these integrals into (
9
) yields (
33
), completing the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

We compare the geometric behavior of clothoids and
\nabla
-clothoids. A standard clothoid spirals infinitely toward an asymptotic center point as

s\to\infty
, because its curvature grows unbounded (
\kappa\to\infty
). For the modified
\nabla
-clothoid under
a cl-connection, the curve cannot extend infinitely in terms of arc length because its parameter is bounded by
|s|<\lambda
.
As
s\to\pm\lambda
, equation (
33
) shows that
\kappa\to\infty
. This means the curve ends at a point where it turns infinitely fast.

Remark 5.3
.

While Theorem
5.1
determines the trajectory uniquely by prescribing its tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}(s)=s
, its geodesic curvature
\kappa_{g}
is not uniquely constrained. The tangential curvature depends only on the sum
m+n=\lambda
. In contrast, the geodesic curvature evaluates in separate terms, namely
m\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle
and
n\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle
. In the present case,
\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=\pm\sqrt{1-s^{2}/\lambda^{2}}
and
\kappa\langle\mathsf{W},N\rangle=f^{\prime}(s)=1/\lambda
. This yields
\kappa^{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}-s^{2}}
. Thus

\kappa_{g}^{2}(s)=\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}-s^{2}}-\frac{2(m-n)}{\lambda}+(m^{2}+n^{2})\left(1-\frac{s^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right).

This formula indicates that the geodesic curvature depends on how
\lambda
is distributed between
m(x,y)
and
n(x,y)
.

Next, we generalize the logarithmic spirals by prescribing a tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}(s)=1/s
(see Fig.
3
).

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\nabla
be a cl-connection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
m+n=\lambda>0
along the curve. Let
\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth curve parametrized by
arc length with
\kappa_{t}(s)=1/s
for
s>0
. Then, the domain of definition for the curve is restricted to
s\geq 1/\lambda
, and up to rigid translations,
\gamma(s)
is given by

(34)

\gamma(s)=R_{\theta_{0}}\left(\pm\frac{\log(s)}{\lambda},\,\pm\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\sqrt{\lambda^{2}s^{2}-1}-\arccos\left(\frac{1}{\lambda s}\right)\right]\right).

Proof.

The proof relies on a direct application of Theorem
3.2
. Given the prescribed tangential curvature
\kappa_{t}(s)=1/s

and the condition
m+n=\lambda>0
, we obtain

f(s)=\frac{\kappa_{t}(s)}{m+n}=\frac{1}{\lambda s}.

Recall that we require
|f(s)|\leq 1
. Assuming
s>0
, this yields
s\geq 1/\lambda
. To determine the parametrization, we substitute
f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda t}
into (
9
) and evaluate both integrals. The first integral is

\int^{s}f(t)\,dt=\int^{s}\frac{1}{\lambda t}\,dt=\frac{\log(s)}{\lambda}.

For the second integral, a simple integration yields

\int^{s}\sqrt{1-f(t)^{2}}\,dt=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\sqrt{\lambda^{2}s^{2}-1}-\arccos\left(\frac{1}{\lambda s}\right)\right].

Inserting these integrals back into (
9
) produces the parametrization (
34
), thereby concluding the proof.
∎
