Proof of Stake economy under centralized exchanges – a mean field model
Wenpin Tang

Abstract
. We consider the interaction between centralized trading and decentralized Proof of Stake (PoS) blockchain ecosystems. Motivated by the increasing dominance of centralized exchanges and the institutionalization of crypto markets, we study how trading activities on centralized exchanges affect staking behavior, token allocation, and decentralization within a PoS blockchain. We formulate a continuous-time mean field model, where the miners simultaneously act as validators in the PoS protocol and traders in a centralized market with price impact. Under suitable assumptions, we establish the local well-posedness of the mean field system, and derive a semi-explicit characterization of the equilibrium trading strategy. Numerical results suggest that centralized trading activities may enhance staking participation, and promote decentralization of the staking distribution through market incentives. We also study the effects of transaction costs and token supply mechanisms on the equilibrium staking ratio and concentration profile. These results illustrate how market microstructure and centralized liquidity provision can exert significant influence on decentralized blockchain protocols.



Key words
: Blockchain, consumption-investment problem, market impact, mean field games, Proof of Stake, staking profile.

The purpose of this paper is to study the impact of centralized trading activities on decentralized blockchain ecosystem,
which differs from the aforementioned AMM-based literature.
Here we consider the PoS protocol,
where there is a bidding mechanism to select a miner to do the work of validating a new block.
Each miner is required to commit some tokens,
and the winning probability is proportional to the number of tokens committed.
The selected miner will create a new block,
and get some crypto tokens as a reward
1
1

1

The reward includes that issued by the PoS ecosystem, and transaction fees. See e.g.,
https://ethereum.org/staking/
for Ethereum staking rewards.
.
With the presence of trading exchanges,
a miner can play a dual role both as a validator of the PoS blockchain
and as a trader/investor of the PoS tokens.
Here we do not plan to address the security issues of this dual role, which is a substantial problem on its own.
Rather, our focus is on the economic side of the miner’s dual role.
For a miner, there are two revenue resources:
(1) staking reward from the PoS ecosystem;
(2) profit or loss from trading PoS tokens.
The question is how each miner makes decision to maximize her profit by exploiting or trading off the two revenue contributions,
and how the collective behavior of the miners affects the PoS ecosystem in turn.

In this paper, we consider the PoS ecosystem in the presence of a centralized exchange,
following the approach outlined in
[
34
, Section 5]
.
In this setting, we can define a notion of
equilibrium trading strategy
for a representative miner,
taking into account the interactions and competitive behavior among the miners and other investors on the exchange.
This leads to a mean field problem,
which we provide a semi-closed form solution via a fixed-point equation.
As a by-product, we analyze the evolution of the staking profile of the miner population from a mean field perspective.
By solving numerically the fixed-point equation,
we also observe that under (centralized) trading,

•

the staking ratio increases over time;

•

the staking profile decentralizes over time;

•

the staking ratio increases slowly as the transaction costs become aggressive;

•

the staking profile is more decentralized as the token reward grows faster,

demonstrating the market power over blockchain protocols.
We also mention the work
[
4

21

31
]
on the mean field game formulation of the PoW protocol,
which does not involve market microstructure.

2.1.
The PoS model

Time is continuous, indexed by
t\in[0,T]
,
for a fixed
T>0
representing the length of a finite horizon.
Let
\{N(t),\,0\leq t\leq T\}
(with
N(0):=N
) denote the process of the supply of tokens,
which is increasing in time and sufficiently smooth.
So the derivative
N^{\prime}(t)
represents the instantaneous inflation rate,
or rate of “reward” by the PoS protocol.
This rate is dynamically adjusted by blockchain community consensus,
which includes the issuance of new tokens to miners and the burning of transaction fees
(see
[
13
]
for ETH supply mechanism).
Its value depends on the overall network usage and adoption, and is typically stochastic.
Here we decouple the token supply with miner’s (strategic) trading,
so the randomness in token supply is considered to be
external
in our setting.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\{N(t),\,0\leq t\leq T\}
is a
deterministic
process
2
2

2

For instance, the process
N(t)
can take a polynomial form:

N_{\alpha}(t)=(N^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+ct)^{\alpha},\qquad t\geq 0.

This parametric family covers different token supply schemes according to the values of
\alpha
:
for
0<\alpha<1
, the process
N_{\alpha}(t)
corresponds to slowed inflation;
for
\alpha=1
, the process
N_{1}(t)=N+ct
gives a constant inflation rate
c
;
for
\alpha>1
, the process
N_{\alpha}(t)
amounts to accelerated inflation.
See
[
20
]
for discussions on ETH supply, which suggests slowed inflation.
.

Let
K\geq 2
be the number of miners/stakers, who are indexed by
k\in[K]:=\{1,\ldots,K\}
.
For each miner
k
,

\{X_{k}(t),\,0\leq t\leq T\}
(with
X_{k}(0)=x_{k}
) denotes the number of tokens that miner
k
chooses to stake for PoS mining,
and
\{\nu_{k}(t),\,0\leq t\leq T\}
denotes miner
k
’s trading strategy.
Let
\{Z(t),\,t\geq 0\}
(with
Z(0)=z
) be the process of the number of tokens that are not staked for the PoS mining
3
3

3

Z(t)
may have contributions from both the miners and retail investors.
For the miners, they decide to stake a number of tokens for PoS mining, and set aside the remaining for other opportunities such as trading and DeFi lending/yield farming (e.g., Aave and Yearn Finance).
For retail investors, they can also choose to stake some of their tokens.
In this case, they will delegate their tokens to a PoS miner
k
, which is included in
X_{k}(t)
.
,
so

(2.1)

\sum_{k=1}^{K}X_{k}(t)+Z(t)=N(t),\quad\mbox{for }0\leq t\leq T.

That is, only
N(t)-Z(t)
tokens are locked for the PoS mining.

In the (discrete) PoS protocol, in each round of the bidding process,
individual miners commit tokens so as to be elected to validate the block and receive a reward;
and the winning probability is proportional to the number of tokens committed.
For instance, each round in Ethereum takes about
10
seconds, corresponding to the the block-generation time
[
5
]
.
In the continuous-time setting,
time required for each round of voting is “infinitesimal”.
Following
[
35
]
, the dynamics of miner
k
’s tokens under trading is formulated as
4
4

4

We assume that newly issued tokens are automatically staked.
In
[
35
]
, it is assumed that all tokens are staked for PoS mining.
Thus,
Z(t)\equiv 0
and the dynamics (
2.2
) reduces to
X^{\prime}_{k}(t)=\nu_{k}(t)+\frac{N^{\prime}(t)}{N(t)}X_{k}(t)
for
0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{k}
.
For ETH, it is observed that only around
30\%
of total supply is locked in staking
[
20
]
.
This necessities introducing the process of unstaked tokens
Z(t)
.
:

(2.2)

X^{\prime}_{k}(t)=\nu_{k}(t)+\frac{N^{\prime}(t)}{N(t)-Z(t)}X_{k}(t)\quad\mbox{for }0\leq t\leq\tau_{k}\wedge T:=\mathcal{T}_{k},

where
\tau_{k}:=\inf\{t>0:X_{k}(t)=0\}
.
We set
X_{k}(t)=X_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{k})
for
t>\mathcal{T}_{k}
.
The equations (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) together imply that

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nu_{k}(t)+Z^{\prime}(t)=0,

which can be viewed as a clearing house condition.
The instantaneous staking rate is
N^{\prime}(t)-Z^{\prime}(t)
,
where
N^{\prime}(t)
is from staking insurance
and
-Z^{\prime}(t)
is from trading between unstaked and staked tokens.
If
-Z^{\prime}(t)>0
, the miners buy unstaked tokens for staking;
if
-Z^{\prime}(t)<0
, staked tokens are unstaked for trading.
As a result,

(2.3)

Z(t)=Z(0)-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nu_{k}(s)ds.

2.2.
Price formation

Central to each miner’s decision is the price process
\{P(t),\,t\geq 0\}
of each (unit) of token.
As mentioned in the introduction,
crypto trading remains centralized, with CEXs handling most volume via limit order books.
Our goal is to understand the PoS ecosystem under centralized trading mechanism.
Modern market microstructure theory postulates that market impact is the underlying driver of price dynamics,
i.e., trading volume impacts price movement.
For ease of presentation,
we assume linear price impact
5
5

5

Here we neglect market shocks triggered by macroeconomics,
geopolitics, breaking news, etc.
, i.e., the Almgren-Chriss model
[
3
]
:

(2.4)

P(t)=P(0)+\sigma B(t)-\eta(Z(t)-Z(0)),

where
\{B(t),\,t\geq 0\}
is standard Brownian motion,

\sigma>0
is the volatility, and
\eta>0
is a market impact parameter (known as Kyle’s Lambda
[
19
]
).
Refer to
[
16
, Chapter 3]
for background,
and
[
11
,
15
,
37
]
for other market impact models.

2.3.
Consumption-investment problem

We formulate the consumption-investment problem for each miner,
following the idea in
[
32
,
35
]
.

Let
b_{k}(t)
be the (units of) risk-free asset that miner
k
holds at time
t
,
and let
r>0
be the risk-free rate.
Assume that all participants (including the
K
miners and retail investors) are allowed to trade tokens on a CEX,
whereas each miner can only exchange cash with an external source (say, a bank).
Let
\{c_{k}(t),\,0\leq t\leq T\}
be the consumption process of miner
k
,
which evolves as:

(C1)

dc_{k}(t)=rb_{k}(t)dt-db_{k}(t)-P(t)\nu_{k}(t)dt-N^{\prime}(t)\,L\left(\frac{\nu_{k}(t)}{N^{\prime}(t)}\right)dt,\qquad 0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{k},

where

is an even function, increasing on

, strictly convex and asymptotically super-linear.
(C1) is a self-financing condition,
where
rb_{k}(t)dt-db_{k}(t)
is the net change in cash used to
finance new tokens
P(t)\nu_{k}(t)dt
, transaction costs
N^{\prime}(t)\,L\left(\frac{\nu_{k}(t)}{N^{\prime}(t)}\right)dt
,
and consumption
dc_{k}(t)
.
Compared to
[
35
]

-N^{\prime}(t)\,L\left(\frac{\nu_{k}(t)}{N^{\prime}(t)}\right)

but also the total volume

(see
[

The quadratic cost
L(x)=\rho|x|^{2}

We also impose the no-shorting constraint:

(C2)

b_{k}(0)=0,\quad b_{k}(t)\geq 0\mbox{ for }0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{k},\quad 0\leq X_{k}(t)\leq N(t)\mbox{ for }0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{k}.

Set
b_{k}(t)=b_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{k})

\nu_{k}(t)=0

.

Miner’s strategy if

Here, suppose that each miner

The objective of miner

In

, the objective of each miner

\displaystyle\sup_{\{(\nu_{k}(t),b_{k}(t))\}}

for some utility functions

The utilities are expressed as functions of the

In this paper, we take market impact into consideration;

:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{\{(\nu_{k}(t),b_{k}(t))\}}

\displaystyle J(\nu_{k},b_{k}):=\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}e^{-\beta_{k}t}dc_{k}(t)+e^{-\beta_{k}\mathcal{T}_{k}}\left[b_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{k})+P(\mathcal{T}_{k})X_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{k})\right]\right\}

Our goal is to understand the collective behavior of the miners.
Without loss of generality, assume that the miners are

,
so we can drop the the subscript ‘

) to get

(2.6)

\displaystyle U(x):=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{(\nu(t),b(t))\}}J(\nu,b):=\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}e^{-\beta t}dc(t)+e^{-\beta\mathcal{T}}\left[b(\mathcal{T})+P(\mathcal{T})X(\mathcal{T})\right]\right\}

(C0)

\displaystyle\mbox{ subject to }X^{\prime}(t)=\nu(t)+\frac{N^{\prime}(t)}{N(t)-Z(t)}X(t),\,X(0)=x,

(C1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\,dc(t)=rb(t)dt-db(t)-P(t)\nu(t)dt-N^{\prime}(t)\,L\left(\frac{\nu(t)}{N^{\prime}(t)}\right)dt,

(C2)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\,b(0)=0,\,b(t)\geq 0\mbox{ and }0\leq X(t)\leq N(t),

(C3)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\,P(t)=P(0)+\sigma B(t)-\eta(Z(t)-Z(0)),

where (C0) is a repeat of the state dynamics in (

), and (C3) is the price dynamics in (

).
Compared to

]
,
the volume constraint

instead the transaction cost

As a result, the miner’s strategy will no longer be a bang-bang control

Denote

(2.7)

\widetilde{P}_{\beta}(t):=e^{-\beta t}\mathbb{E}P(t)=e^{-\beta t}\left[P(0)-\eta(Z(t)-Z(0))\right]\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{P}(t):=\widetilde{P}_{0}(t).

A simple argument (as in

,

]

U(x):=\sup_{\{(\nu,b)\}}J(\nu,b)=\sup_{b}J_{1}(b)+\sup_{\nu}J_{2}(\nu),

where

and

J_{2}(\nu):=\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\left[-\widetilde{P}_{\beta}(t)\nu(t)-e^{-\beta t}N^{\prime}(t)L\left(\frac{\nu(t)}{N^{\prime}(t)}\right)\right]dt+\widetilde{P}_{\beta}(\mathcal{T})X(\mathcal{T}).

Then

.
So the problem (

.
We argue by dynamic programming, and let

\displaystyle v(t,x):=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\nu(s),s\geq t\}}\int_{t}^{\mathcal{T}}\left[-\widetilde{P}_{\beta}(s)\nu(s)-e^{-\beta s}N^{\prime}(s)L\left(\frac{\nu(s)}{N^{\prime}(s)}\right)\right]dt+\widetilde{P}_{\beta}(\mathcal{T})X(\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\,0\leq X(s)\leq N(s),

Let

.
Under suitable conditions on the model parameters,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\partial_{t}v+\frac{xN^{\prime}(t)}{N(t)-Z(t)}\partial_{x}v+\sup_{\nu}\left\{\nu(\partial_{x}v-\widetilde{P}_{\beta}(t))-e^{-\beta t}N^{\prime}(t)L\left(\frac{\nu}{N^{\prime}(t)}\right)\right\}=0\quad\mbox{in }Q,\\

By optimizing

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\partial_{t}v+\frac{xN^{\prime}(t)}{N(t)-Z(t)}\partial_{x}v+e^{-\beta t}N^{\prime}(t)\bigg\{(e^{\beta t}\partial_{x}v-\widetilde{P}(t))(L^{\prime})^{-1}(e^{\beta t}\partial_{x}v-\widetilde{P}(t))\\

Mean field strategy
.
Assume that the distribution of the miners by their staked coins
are approximated by
m_{0}(x)dx
at time

.
The goal is to find an equilibrium trading strategy

\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t\,|\,m_{0})
, or simply
\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t)
,
which can be viewed as the aggregated trading strategy among all the miners.

Now let’s describe the mean field model.

Mean field problem
(\star)

(1)

Since there are
K
miners, by (
2.3
), the equilibrium of unstaked tokens is:

(2.11)

Z^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t)=Z(0)-K\int_{0}^{t}\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(s)ds,

(2)

Given
Z^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(\cdot)
,
the miner’s optimal strategy is

(2.12)

\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}_{*}(t,x)=N^{\prime}(t)(L^{\prime})^{-1}\left(e^{\beta t}\partial_{x}v(t,x)-\widetilde{P}(t)\right),

where
v
is the solution to (
2.10
) with
Z(t)=Z^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t)
.

(3)

The feedback control of a typical miner is

X^{\prime}(t)=\nu_{*}^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t,X(t))+\frac{N^{\prime}(t)}{N(t)-Z^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t)}X(t)
.
Hence, the density of the miners by their staked coins solves the continuity equation:

(2.13)

\partial_{t}m+\partial_{x}\left(\left(\nu_{*}^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t,x)+\frac{xN^{\prime}(t)}{N(t)-Z^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t)}\right)m\right)=0,\quad m(0,x)=m_{0}(x).

(4)

The equilibrium trading strategy
\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t)
satisfies the fixed point equation:

(2.14)

\int\nu_{*}^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t,x)m(t,x)dx=\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t).

Let’s assume that the mean field problem
(\star)
is well-posed.
We are interested in the following two statistics (or features):

•

Z^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t)
is the number of unstaked tokens at time
t
,
or
\frac{N(t)-Z^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}(t)}{N(t)}
is the staking ratio
that often reflects the adoption of a token,
or activities within the token’s ecosystem.

•

m(t,\cdot)
is the distribution of staked coins of the miner population at time
t
.
A more-concentrated staking profile

implies centralization in blockchain governance;
while a more spread-out
m(t,\cdot)
implies decentralization of the blockchain protocol.

The interesting (and important) problem is to understand the long-time behavior of the above quantities,
i.e., as
t\to\infty
.
It is known that the PoS protocol alone does not lead to decentralization
[
32
,
33
]
.
Our goal is to explore whether allowing trading in the PoS protocol can yield decentralization,
leveraging the market power.

Hence, it is indispensable to first establish the well-posedness of the mean field problem
(\star)
for
all time
.
As pointed out in
[
34
]
, this is equivalent to solving a first-order mean field game with nonlocal and non-separable Hamiltonian,
which is very challenging.
In the next section,
we take the first step to prove the well-posedness of this problem, at least for some (small) finite time.

3.
Well-posedness of the mean field problem

In this section, we prove the well-posedness of the mean field problem
(\star)
for short time
T_{0}
, via a fixed-point approach.

Let’s recast the mean field problem. Fixing
M>0
, we look for an aggregated trading strategy in the set

\mathcal{X}_{M}:=\{\nu\in\mathcal{C}[0,T_{0}]:||\nu||_{\infty}\leq M\}.

Write

(3.1)

Z_{\nu}(t):=Z(0)-K\int_{0}^{t}\nu(s)ds\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(t):=e^{-\beta t}\left(P(0)+\eta K\int_{0}^{t}\nu(s)ds\right).

Choose
\overline{N}>\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}N(t)
.
Formally, let
v_{\nu}(t,x)
solve the HJB equation (
2.9
)
with a fixed boundary
7
7

7

Here is a subtlety: compared to the original equation (
2.9
),
the boundary conditions are removed in (
3.2
).
In fact, we will show in Lemma
3.4
that under suitable condition on
m_{0}
,

m(t,\cdot)
or
X(t)
remains (uniformly) in the interior of
Q_{T}
.
As a result, the boundary conditions and choice of
\overline{N}
become irrelevant.
:

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\partial_{t}v+a_{\nu}(t,x)\partial_{x}v+g(t)f\left(e^{\beta t}(\partial_{x}v-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(t))\right)=0\quad\mbox{in }[0,T_{0})\times(0,\overline{N}),\\
v(T_{0},x)=\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})x,\end{array}\right.

where

(3.3)

a_{\nu}(t,x):=\frac{xN^{\prime}(t)}{N(t)-Z_{\nu}(t)},\quad g(t):=e^{-\beta t}N^{\prime}(t),\quad f(p):=p(L^{\prime})^{-1}(p)-L((L^{\prime})^{-1}(p)).

The corresponding feedback control is

\mu_{\nu}(t,x):=N^{\prime}(t)(L^{\prime})^{-1}\left(e^{\beta t}(\partial_{x}v_{\nu}-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(t))\right)
.
Let
m_{\nu}(t,x)
solve the continuity equation (
2.13
):

(3.4)

\partial_{t}m+\partial_{x}(b_{\nu}(t,x)m)=0,\quad m(0,x)=m_{0}(x),

where

b_{\nu}(t,x):=\mu_{\nu}(t,x)+a_{\nu}(t,x).

Finally, define the fixed-point map

(3.5)

(\mathcal{T}\nu)(t):=\int_{0}^{\overline{N}}\mu_{\nu}(t,x)m_{\nu}(t,x)dx.

A fixed point of
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{X}_{M}
gives a solution to the mean field problem
(\star)
.

The main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that

(1)

N\in\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,T_{0}])
, with
N(0)>0
and
N^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
for all
t
.

(2)

\delta_{0}:=N(0)-Z(0)>0
.

(3)

L\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, with
(L^{\prime})^{-1}
being Lipschitz.

(4)

m_{0}\in\mathcal{C}([0,\overline{N}])
, with
m_{0}\geq 0
,
\int_{0}^{\overline{N}}m_{0}(x)dx=1
and

\operatorname{supp}m_{0}\subset[\varepsilon_{0},N(0)-\varepsilon_{0}]
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.

For
M
sufficiently large and
T_{0}
sufficiently small,
the map
\mathcal{T}
is given by

(3.6)

(\mathcal{T}\nu)(t)=N^{\prime}(t)(L^{\prime})^{-1}\left(e^{\beta t}\left(\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu}(s)ds}-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(t)\right)\right),

and it has a unique fixed point
\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}\in\mathcal{X}_{M}
.
The fixed problem
nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}
is the equilibrium trading strategy of the mean field problem
(\star)
,
and the corresponding
v(t,x)
and
m(t,x)
are given by (
3.8
) and (
3.12
) respectively,
with
\nu=\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}
.

The key takeaway of the theorem is that under the linear terminal condition
v(T_{0},x)=\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})x
,
the HJB equation (
3.2
) is solvable.
In particular, the miner’s optimal strategy (
2.12
) is:

\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}}_{*}(t,x)=N^{\prime}(t)(L^{\prime})^{-1}\left(e^{\beta t}\left(\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu}(s)ds}-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(t)\right)\right),\quad\mbox{with }\nu=\nu^{\tiny\mbox{eq}},

which is independent of the state variable
x
.
That is, the miner’s trading decision is independent of her staked tokens.
So the problem is to find
\nu(\cdot)
such that

(3.7)

N^{\prime}(t)(L^{\prime})^{-1}\left(e^{\beta t}\left(\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu}(s)ds}-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(t)\right)\right)=\nu(t).

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is given in the next subsections.

3.1.
Solving the equations for fixed
\nu

We prove that the fixed-point map (
3.5
) is well-defined
by solving the HJB equation (
3.2
) and the continuity equation (
3.4
).

We start by showing that the denominator
N(t)-Z_{\nu}(t)
in the coefficients
a_{\nu}(t,x)
,
b_{\nu}(t,x)

stays away from zero for some short time
T_{0}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{X}_{M}
and
\delta_{0}:=N(0)-Z(0)>0
.
Assume that
N
is nondecreasing.
For
T_{0}\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2KM}
, we have:

\inf_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\{N(t)-Z_{\nu}(t)\}\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle N(t)-Z_{\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=N(t)-Z(0)+K\int_{0}^{t}\nu(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq\delta_{0}-KMt,

where the inequality follows from the fact that
N
is nondecreasing
and
||\nu||_{\infty}\leq M
.
Thus,
N(t)-Z_{\nu}(t)\geq\delta_{0}/2
for any
t\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2KM}
.
∎

Next we solve explicitly the HJB equation (
3.2
).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let the assumptions in Lemma
3.2
hold,
and assume that
N\in\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,T_{0}])

and
(L^{\prime})^{-1}
is Lipschitz.
We have:

(3.8)

\displaystyle v_{\nu}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu}(s)ds}x+\int_{t}^{T}g(s)f\left(e^{\beta s}\left(\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})e^{\int_{s}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu}(r)dr}-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(s)\right)\right)ds,

where
f
and
g
are defined by (
3.3
).
Consequently, the feedback control is

(3.9)

\mu_{\nu}(t,x)=\mu_{\nu}(t):=N^{\prime}(t)(L^{\prime})^{-1}\left(e^{\beta t}\left(\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu}(s)ds}-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(t)\right)\right).

Proof.

Formally, differentiating (
3.2
) with respect to
x
yields
the quasi-linear equation for
q_{\nu}:=\partial_{x}v_{\nu}
:

(3.10)

\partial_{t}q+c_{\nu}(t,x,q)\partial_{x}q+a_{\nu}(t)q=0,\quad q(T_{0},x)=\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0}),

where
a_{\nu}(t):=\partial_{x}a_{\nu}(t,x)=\frac{N^{\prime}(t)}{N(t)-Z(t)}
and

\displaystyle c_{\nu}(t,x,q):

\displaystyle=a_{\nu}(t,x)+N^{\prime}(t)f^{\prime}\left(e^{\beta t}(q-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(t))\right)

\displaystyle=a_{\nu}(t,x)+N^{\prime}(t)(L^{\prime})^{-1}\left(e^{\beta t}(q-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(t))\right).

By Lemma
3.2
, the coefficients
c_{\nu}(t,x,q)
and
a_{\nu}(t)
are well-defined,
continuous in
(t,x,q)
and Lipschitz in
(x,q)
.
Thus, the characteristic system (see
[
14
, Chapter 3]
)

(3.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} x^{\prime}(t)&=c_{\nu}(t,x(t),q(t)),\\
q^{\prime}(t)&=-\alpha_{\nu}(t)q(t),\end{aligned}\right.\qquad x(0)=x,\quad q(T_{0})=\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0}),

has a unique local solution.
Since
N
is smooth, we get
|a_{\nu}(t)|\leq\frac{2||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}}{\delta}
.
By Grönwall’s inequality,

||q_{\nu}||_{\infty}\leq\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})e^{\frac{2||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}T_{0}}{\delta}}\leq(|P(0)|+\eta KMT_{0})e^{(\frac{2||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}}{\delta}-\beta)T_{0}}.

Further by the fact that
(L^{\prime})^{-1}
is Lipschitz (so it has at most linear growth),
we get:

|c_{\nu}(t,x,q)|\leq\frac{2||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}}{\delta}|x|+C(1+|q|),

for some
C>0
independent of
\nu
.
Again Grönwall’s inequality guarantees that
x_{\nu}(\cdot)
does not blow up.
As a result, the solution is global:

q_{\nu}(t,x)=\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(T_{0})e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu}(s)ds},

and

v_{\nu}(t,x)=\int_{t}^{T}g(s)f\left(e^{\beta s}(q_{\nu}(s,0)-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu}(s))\right)ds+\int_{0}^{x}q_{\nu}(t,y)dy
,
which proves (
3.8
).
∎

Observe a subtlety: compared to the original equation (
2.9
),
the boundary conditions are removed in (
3.2
).
Here we argue that the support of

remains in the interior for short time,
so the choice of
\overline{N}
becomes irrelevant.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let the assumptions in Proposition
3.3
hold, and
assume that
\operatorname{supp}m_{0}\subset[\varepsilon_{0},N(0)-\varepsilon_{0}]
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
There is
T_{0}>0
such that for each
\nu\in\mathcal{X}_{M}
,
the characteristic
x_{\nu}(\cdot)
defined by (
3.11
) starting from

satisfy

x_{\nu}(t)\in\left[\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},N(t)-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\right]\quad\mbox{for all }t\in[0,T_{0}].

Proof.

It is easy to deduce from the proof of Proposition
3.3
that

|c(t,x,q_{\nu}(t,x))|\leq C\quad\mbox{for some }C>0\mbox{ independent of }\nu.

So
|x^{\prime}_{\nu}(t)|\leq C
, and hence
|x_{\nu}(t)-x|\leq Ct
.
Choosing
T_{0}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2C}
yields

x_{\nu}(t)\geq x-Ct\geq\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},

because
x\geq\varepsilon_{0}
.
For the upper bound, we have:

N(t)-x_{\nu}(t)\geq N(0)-x-Ct\geq\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},

because
N(\cdot)
is nondecreasing and
x\leq N(0)-\varepsilon_{0}
.
Thus,
x_{\nu}(t)
remains in the interior of
Q=\{(t,x):0\leq t<T_{0},\,0<x<N(t)\}
.
∎

The following proposition solves the continuity equation (
3.4
).

Proposition 3.5
.

Let the assumptions in Lemma
3.4
hold.
For each
\nu\in\mathcal{X}_{M}
,
the continuity equation (
3.4
) has a unique classical solution
m\in\mathcal{C}([0,T_{0}];L^{1}(0,\overline{N}))

given by the push-forward of
m_{0}
by the flow
b_{\nu}

(3.12)

m(t,x)=m_{0}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{t}a_{\nu}(s)ds}x-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}a_{\nu}(r)dr}\mu_{\nu}(s)ds\right)e^{-\int_{0}^{t}a_{\nu}(s)ds}.

In particular,

m(t,\cdot)\geq 0
and
\int_{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}}^{N(0)-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}}m(t,x)dx=1
.

Proof.

Recall the expression of
\mu_{\nu}(t,x)
from (
3.9
).
Because
N\in\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,T_{0}])
and
(L^{\prime})^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})

we have
\mu_{\nu}(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T_{0}]\times[0,\overline{N}])

and hence,

b_{\nu}(t,x)=a_{\nu}(t)x+\mu_{\nu}(t)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T_{0}]\times[0,\overline{N}])

As a result, the ordinary differential equation

X^{\prime}(t)=b_{\nu}(t,X(t)),\quad X(0)=x,

has a unique solution on

\Phi_{\nu}(t;x)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}a_{\nu}(s)ds}x+e^{\int_{0}^{t}a_{\nu}(s)ds}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}a_{\nu}(r)dr}\mu_{\nu}(s)ds.

The unique distributional solution to (

m(t,x)=m_{0}(\Phi^{-1}_{\nu}(t;x))\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}b_{\nu}(s,\Phi(s;\Phi^{-1}_{\nu}(t;x)))ds\right),

3.2.
Stability estimates

We quantify the stability of the equations (
3.2
) and (

) with respect to the control
\nu

We start with the Lipschitz estimates of the coefficients.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let the assumptions in Proposition
3.3
hold.
For
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{M}
, we have:

(3.13)

\displaystyle||Z_{\nu_{1}}-Z_{\nu_{2}}||_{\infty}\leq KT_{0}||\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}||_{\infty},

(3.14)

\displaystyle||\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu_{1}}-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu_{2}}||_{\infty}\leq\eta KT_{0}||\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}||_{\infty},

(3.15)

\displaystyle||a_{\nu_{1}}-a_{\nu_{2}}||_{\infty}\leq\frac{4\overline{N}||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}KT_{0}}{\delta_{0}^{2}}||\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}||_{\infty}.

Proof.

The bounds (
3.13
)–(
3.14
) follow immediately from (
3.1
).
For (
3.15
), we get:

\displaystyle|a_{\nu_{1}}(t,x)-a_{\nu_{2}}(t,x)|

\displaystyle=xN^{\prime}(t)\frac{|Z_{\nu_{2}}(t)-Z_{\nu_{1}}(t)|}{(N(t)-Z_{\nu_{1}}(t))(N(t)-Z_{\nu_{2}}(t))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\overline{N}||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}KT_{0}}{\delta_{0}^{2}}||\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}||_{\infty},

where we use Lemma
3.2
to lower bound

,
and (
3.13
) to upper bound
|Z_{\nu_{2}}(t)-Z_{\nu_{1}}(t)|
.
∎

Next we study the stability of the feedback control
\mu_{\nu}(t,x)
with respect to
\nu
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let the assumptions in Proposition
3.3
hold.
For
T_{0}
sufficiently small,
we have for
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{M}
,

(3.16)

||\mu_{\nu_{1}}-\mu_{\nu_{2}}||_{\infty}\leq CT_{0}||\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}||_{\infty},

for some
C>0
independent of
T_{0}
.

Proof.

Because
N\in\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,T_{0}])
and
(L^{\prime})^{-1}
is Lipschitz,
we have:

(3.17)

\displaystyle|\mu_{\nu_{1}}(t,x)-\mu_{\nu_{2}}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}\left(\left|\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu_{1}}(T_{0})e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu_{1}}(s)ds}-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu_{2}}(T_{0})e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu_{2}}(s)ds}\right|+|\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu_{1}}(t)-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu_{2}}(t)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}\left((e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu_{1}}(s)ds}+1)|\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu_{1}}(t)-\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu_{2}}(t)|+\widetilde{P}_{\beta,\nu_{2}}(T_{0})\left|e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu_{1}}(s)ds}-e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu_{2}}(s)ds}\right|\right)

3.2
, we get
||a_{\nu}||_{\infty}\leq\frac{2||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}}{\delta_{0}}
,
and hence

(3.18)

e^{\int_{t}^{T_{0}}a_{\nu}(s)ds}\leq e^{\frac{2||N^{\prime}||_{\infty}T_{0}}{\delta_{0}}}.

Combining (

), (
3.15
), (

) and (

) yields

|\mu_{\nu_{1}}(t,x)-\mu_{\nu_{2}}(t,x)|\leq CT_{0}(1+e^{CT_{0}}+e^{CT_{0}}T_{0}+e^{CT_{0}}T_{0}^{2})||\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}||_{\infty},

which proves (

).
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

The proof is split into several steps.

Step 1
. We prove that for
M
sufficiently large and
T_{0}
sufficiently small,
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{X}_{M})\subset\mathcal{X}_{M}
.
Because
N\in\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,T_{0}])
and
(L^{\prime})^{-1}
is Lipschitz,
we have:

||\mu_{\nu}||_{\infty}\leq C(1+e^{CT_{0}})(1+\eta KMT_{0}),

for some
C>0
independent of
T_{0}
.
Thus,

|(\mathcal{T}\nu)(t)|\leq||\mu_{\nu}||_{\infty}\leq C(1+e^{CT_{0}})(1+\eta KMT_{0})
.
Choosing
M\geq 2C(1+e)
,
and then
T_{0}\leq\min(\frac{1}{C},\frac{1}{\eta KM})
yields
||\mu_{\nu}||_{\infty}\leq 2C(1+e)\leq M
.

Step 2
. Note that
|(\mathcal{T}\nu_{1})(t)-(\mathcal{T}\nu_{2})(t)|\leq||\mu_{\nu_{1}}-\mu_{\nu_{2}}||_{\infty}
.
By Proposition
3.7
,
we have for

sufficiently small,

||\mathcal{T}\nu_{1}-\mathcal{T}\nu_{2}||_{\infty}\leq C^{\prime}T_{0}||\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}||_{\infty}\quad\mbox{for all }\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{M}.

Step 3
. By the previous two steps,

is a strict contraction on the complete space

,
provided that

.
By Banach fixed-point theorem, there is a unique

such that

.
The formulas for

and

and

.

5.
Conclusion and discussions

In this paper, we studied the interaction between centralized trading activities and PoS blockchain ecosystems.
By incorporating market impact from centralized exchanges into the PoS framework, we formulated a continuous-time mean field model describing the joint evolution of trading, staking, and token allocation among the miners.
The resulting equilibrium problem couples an HJB equation with a transport equation via a nonlocal fixed-point condition.
We established the local well-posedness of the mean field system, and derived a semi-explicit characterization of the equilibrium trading strategy.
Numerical experiments demonstrate that centralized trading activities may increase staking participation and promote decentralization of the staking distribution through market incentives.

The semi-explicit structure of the solution is largely due to the linear terminal condition imposed in the HJB equation.
For a more general terminal condition, e.g., of the form
e^{-\beta T}h(x)
with
h
being interpreted as a utility function,
the solution to the HJB equation, and consequently the associated continuity equation, is no longer tractable.
Moreover, the feedback control is time- and state-dependent.
Nevertheless, under suitable conditions on
h
,
we can still establish the local well-posedness of the mean field system.
A very interesting question is to determine the maximal time horizon
T_{0}
for which the mean field system remains well-posed.

By a private communication with Paul Y. Zhang
[
40
]
,
it is possible to approximate our mean field problem on bounded domains
by a mean field game on the whole space by introducing a penalty function near the boundary.
Under the Almgren-Chriss model (i.e., quadratic transaction costs),
the argument in
[
25
]
allows us to show that the mean field game is well-posed until
T_{0}
satisfying

\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}e^{\beta t}N^{\prime}(t)\right)^{2}\eta T_{0}^{2}\leq 8\rho^{2}
.

From the numerical perspective, the coupled HJB–continuity system also presents substantial challenges. In particular, numerical approximation of the continuity equation requires careful treatment in order to preserve positivity, mass conservation, and stability of the evolving density profile. Developing robust and efficient numerical schemes for the fully coupled mean field system remains an important direction for future research.
