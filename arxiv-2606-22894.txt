Sparsity-Cone SDP Relaxations and Applications to Variable Fixing for Sparse Quadratic Programs
Di Hou, Thai P.D. Nguyen, Kim-Chuan Toh, Guanyi Wang

Abstract
Quadratic programs (QPs) with sparsity constraint are generally NP-hard, and their efficient global solution depends crucially on tractable tight convex relaxations. In this paper, we propose a sparsity-cone semidefinite programming (SC-SDP) relaxation for sparse (indefinite) QPs. Unlike standard SDP liftings, such as the SDP–RLT relaxation, which involve a ( 2 ​ n + 1 ) (2n+1) -dimensional semidefinite matrix, the proposed SC-SDP formulation uses only a ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -dimensional matrix together with a single sparsity-cone constraint 𝒦 \mathcal{K} to handle the relaxation of the ℓ 0 \ell_{0} -norm constraint. We prove that SC-SDP is equivalent in strength to the SDP–RLT relaxation. We further study the sparsity cone 𝒦 \mathcal{K} , deriving structural characterizations and showing that projection onto 𝒦 \mathcal{K} can be computed efficiently via a one-dimensional subproblem. Building on the dual of SC-SDP, we derive explicit presolving mechanisms, including a dual-fixing rule for individual variables, a screening-cut rule for excluding larger support patterns, and a dual-refinement step for improving presolving certificates. To solve the resulting relaxation SC-SDP efficiently, we develop a two-phase Riemannian-based augmented Lagrangian method and exploits the structured projection subproblems. Numerical experiments on several classes of sparse QPs show that SC-SDP preserves the bound quality of SDP–RLT while offering substantial computational advantages and practically effective presolving capabilities.

Introduction

1.1
Sparsity-constrained quadratic programs

In this paper, we consider the following sparsity-constrained quadratic program (QP):

v^{\mathrm{P}}\;\coloneqq\;\min_{x}\left\{x^{\top}Qx+2c^{\top}x\;\middle|\;Ax=b,\;Bx\geq d,\;\|x\|_{0}\leq k\right\},

(P)

where
Q\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times n}
,
d\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
,
k
is a given integer satisfying
0<k<n
, and
\|x\|_{0}
denotes the number of nonzero entries of
x
. We assume that the matrix
A
has full row rank, the feasible region of (
P
) is nonempty, and the objective function of (
P
) is bounded from below over its feasible set.
Problem (
P
) is more general than many sparse QPs considered in the literature because it includes both equality and inequality constraints in addition to the
\ell_{0}
sparsity constraint.
The objective function contains both quadratic and linear terms, enabling it to model a wide range of nonconvex optimization problems. In particular, the matrix
Q
is allowed to be indefinite.
The
\ell_{0}
-norm sparsity constraint also arises in various applications, such as sparse regression, compressed sensing, portfolio optimization, and feature selection.

(
P
) is generally nonconvex and NP-hard due to the presence of the intractable sparsity constraint and nonconvex quadratic objective.
To handle the sparsity constraint more effectively,
one common approach is to
introduce additional
n
binary variables to represent the support of
x
and
reformulate (
P
) as the following mixed-binary QP:

\min_{x,z}\left\{x^{\top}Qx+2c^{\top}x\;\middle|\;Ax=b,\;Bx\geq d,\;x\circ(1-z)=0,\;e^{\top}z\leq k,\;z\in\{0,1\}^{n}\right\}.

(1)

Problem (
1
) can then be solved globally by branch-and-bound methods
[
20
,
23
,
8
]
. At each node of the branch-and-bound tree, one solves a relaxation problem of (
1
) to obtain a lower bound. The problem is solved when the gap between the lower bound and the best known upper bound is sufficiently small.
Consequently, it is important to develop relaxations of (
1
) that are both tight and computationally efficient. In this paper, we focus on SDP relaxations, which are known to provide tight lower bounds for (
1
)
[
7
]
.

Remark 1
.

The relaxation and algorithm proposed in this paper are not restricted to (
P
). They also extend to sparse quadratically constrained quadratic programs (QCQPs), by directly incorporating the associated lifted constraints into the SDP relaxation (
Q
) and modifying the definition of
\mathcal{B}(Y)
accordingly in the subsequent subsection. In this extension, the quadratic constraint matrices may also be indefinite. For simplicity, we restrict our attention in this paper to linear constraints.

1.2
SC-SDP relaxation

The choice of relaxation is crucial for the efficiency of the branch-and-bound algorithm. A tight relaxation can significantly reduce the size of the search tree and thus improve the computational efficiency.
Therefore, it is important to develop relaxations of (
1
) that are both tight and computationally efficient.

SDP–RLT relaxation.

A commonly used convex relaxation of (
1
) is the SDP relaxation
[
27
]
, which is obtained by replacing the quadratic term with a matrix and introducing a positive semidefinite constraint. The explicit formulation of the SDP relaxation (
19
) of (
1
) is given in Subsection
3.1
.
Although the SDP relaxation can be tight under certain conditions
[
9
,
31
]
, it typically provides weak lower bounds for most instances of (
1
). In some cases, the bound can even be negative infinity.

To strengthen the SDP relaxation and improve the bound quality, we incorporate constraints from the reformulation-linearization technique (RLT)
[
26
,
19
]
and obtain the SDP–RLT relaxation, which typically provides a significantly tighter bound than the standard SDP relaxation alone. However, for large-scale problems, it becomes computationally prohibitive because it must lift both the continuous variables
x
and the support variables
z
, leading to a semidefinite matrix indexed by
[1;x;z]
of dimension
2n+1
, together with a large number of RLT constraints.
For sparse QPs, the
(2n+1)
-dimensional SDP–RLT relaxation can be reduced to an
(n+1)
-dimensional SDP relaxation with
n
additional SOCP constraints. To the best of our knowledge, however, these SOCP constraints do not readily yield explicit presolving rules for variable fixing.

SC-SDP relaxation.

To reduce the computational burden of the SDP–RLT relaxation of (
1
) and eliminate the explicit support variables
z
and the associated SOCP constraints in its reduced form, we propose a sparsity-cone SDP relaxation. Standard SDP formulations for (
1
) typically employ a lifted matrix involving both the
x
- and
z
-variables, thereby effectively doubling the dimension of the lifted space. Our objective is to construct a new SDP relaxation of comparable strength without this dimensional increase. The compact relaxation keeps only the lifted matrix associated with
[1;x]

Y\;\coloneq\;\begin{bmatrix}Y_{11}&Y_{12}\\
Y_{21}&Y_{22}\end{bmatrix}\;=\;\begin{bmatrix}1&x^{\top}\\
x&X\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

(2)

as its semidefinite variable. Using this matrix, the RLT constraints derived from the original linear constraints can be written compactly as

\mathcal{A}(Y)\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}-b&A\end{bmatrix}Y\begin{bmatrix}-b^{\top}\\
A^{\top}\end{bmatrix}=0,\quad\mathcal{B}(Y)\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-d&B\end{bmatrix}Y\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-d&B\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\geq 0.

(3)

These are exactly the RLT constraints obtained from the original linear constraints in the
[1;x]
lifting. To replace the support variables and SOCP constraints in the reduced SDP–RLT form while preserving the sparsity information, we introduce the sparsity-cone

\mathcal{K}\coloneq\left\{Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}:\mathcal{D}(Y)\coloneq\begin{bmatrix}kY_{11}&Y_{12}\\
Y_{21}&\Diag(Y_{22})\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0\right\}.

(4)

The cone
\mathcal{K}
encodes the sparsity structure of the original problem. More precisely, it is exact on normalized rank-one lifts: for
Y=[1;x][1;x]^{\top}
, the membership condition
Y\in\mathcal{K}
is equivalent to the cardinality bound
\|x\|_{0}\leq k
; see Theorem
1
.
Combining the RLT constraints (
3
) with the cone constraint, we obtain the following sparsity-cone semidefinite (SC-SDP) relaxation of (
P
):

\min_{Y}\left\{\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle\;\middle|\;\mathcal{A}(Y)=0,\;\mathcal{B}(Y)\geq 0,\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\in\mathcal{K}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}\right\},

(Q)

where the cost matrix
\overline{Q}\coloneqq[0,c^{\top};c,Q]
.
Compared with the SDP–RLT relaxation of (
1
),
our new relaxation (
Q
) has several key advantages:

1.

(
Q
) preserves the relaxation strength of the SDP–RLT relaxation of (
1
). Indeed, Theorem
2
shows that the two relaxations are equivalent, so (
Q
) yields exactly the same lower bound for (
1
) without any loss in bound quality.

2.

(
Q
) is substantially more compact. It uses a single semidefinite matrix of size
n+1
together with the cone constraint
Y\in\mathcal{K}
, whereas the SDP–RLT relaxation of (
1
) uses a semidefinite matrix of size
2n+1
together with the RLT constraints in (
3
), as well as the
additional constraints that encode the sparsity constraint based on the variable
z
.
This reduction leads to a cleaner formulation and significantly lowers the lifted dimension. Numerically, SC-SDP also tends to yield much lower-rank solutions than the SDP–RLT model, which further supports its computational advantage.

3.

The cone
\mathcal{K}
in (
4
) is not only a modeling device but also computationally tractable. As shown in Section
2
, the projection onto
\mathcal{K}
admits a reduction to a one-dimensional subproblem. Moreover, the Moreau decomposition allows the algorithm to recover the multiplier update associated with
\mathcal{K}^{*}
from this projection. This structure is essential for the first-order method developed in Section
5
to efficiently solve the SC-SDP
relaxation.

4.

Another important advantage of (
Q
)
is that its dual has a simple structure that allows us to derive explicit presolving rules for the original mixed-binary formulation (
1
). In particular, we show that the dual solution of (
Q
) yields a dual-fixing rule for individual variables, a screening-cut rule for excluding larger support patterns. Moreover, based on the dual solution, we can develop
a dual-refinement step for improving the fixing and screening certificates. To the best of our knowledge, this provides the first explicit fixing mechanism derived from an SDP relaxation for general sparsity-constrained quadratic programs with linear constraints.

1.3
Presolving with SC-SDP

For sparsity-constrained problems such as (
P
) and (
1
), the main combinatorial difficulty is to identify the support of the solution. This makes presolving especially valuable in branch-and-bound methods: fixing variables, or more generally ruling out
certain support patterns before branching, can substantially reduce the search tree. Accordingly, a useful relaxation such as (
Q
) should do more than provide a strong lower bound; it should also reveal structural information that can be turned into explicit fixing and screening certificates.

Related work.

Classical presolving methods based on LP relaxations
[
1
,
13
]
provide a rich toolbox of fixing techniques, but their effectiveness is limited by the weakness of the underlying relaxations. Stronger relaxations have therefore been explored in more specialized settings. In sparse ridge regression, perspective-based methods are substantially stronger and have produced effective fixing and screening rules
[
3
,
29
]
, but they remain restrictive.
First, they typically require strong convexity of the objective function with
Q\succ 0
, and their improvement depends on the strength of that convexity. Second, they essentially apply only to problems without additional constraints. In contrast, our SDP-based relaxations such as (
Q
) apply to more general sparsity-constrained quadratic problems
with additional linear equality/inequality constraints without
requiring
Q
to be positive definite, and they
can provide strong lower bounds.
However, effective fixing rules derived from such SDP relaxations are
currently still unexplored. Although related questions have been studied in the mixed-integer SDP literature
[
12
,
22
]
, presolving remains difficult there as well, and the resulting techniques do not directly extend to the explicit fixing rules developed here.

The special structure of the SC-SDP relaxation (
Q
) helps bridge this gap. In Appendix
A
, we explain how SC-SDP can be used under support restrictions, with strong branching as a special case. Subsection
4.1

then develops a dual-fixing rule for individual variables, Subsection
4.2
derives screening cuts for larger support patterns, and Subsection
4.3
introduces a dual-refinement step for strengthening the resulting fixing and screening certificates.
We briefly explain the fixing and screening rules below.

Dual fixing.

Let
z
be the binary vector in (
1
), and let
z_{p}^{*}
be the
p
-th component of an optimal solution of (
1
). Given a dual solution of (
D
), define the scores
w_{i}
as in Theorem
4
, and let
w_{[i]}
denote the
i
-th largest value in
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\}
. If
v^{\mathrm{UB}}
is an upper bound on the optimal value and
v^{\mathrm{LB}}
is the lower bound produced by (
Q
), then Theorem
4
yields the rule

\displaystyle z_{p}^{*}=0

\displaystyle\text{if }w_{[k]}-w_{p}>v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}},

\displaystyle z_{p}^{*}=1

\displaystyle\text{if }w_{p}-w_{[k+1]}>v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}}.

Screening cuts.

When single-variable fixing is not sufficient, the same dual certificate can be used to rule out larger support patterns. For an SCG-tuple
(S,N,C)
defined in Definition
1
, where
S
and
N
are candidate support and non-support sets and
C
is the associated complement support set, Theorem
5
gives

\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{[i]}-\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}-\sum_{i\in C}w_{i}>v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sum_{i\in S}z_{i}+\sum_{i\in N}(1-z_{i})\leq|S|+|N|-1.

Dual refinement.

Because the optimal dual solution of (
Q
) is generally not unique, different optimal solutions may yield the same lower bound but different fixing and screening performance. We therefore introduce a dual-refinement step, which keeps the SC-SDP bound unchanged while selecting a more effective presolving certificate, and derive the compact
(3n+1)
-variable semidefinite–second-order-cone representable formulation (
33
) by using the arrow representation of
\mathcal{D}^{*}(W)
.

The presolving rules derived from the SC-SDP relaxation are explicit and practical, and they can be applied at the root node or throughout the branch-and-bound tree to substantially reduce the search space. To the best of our knowledge, this provides the first explicit fixing mechanism derived from an SDP relaxation for general sparsity-constrained quadratic programs with linear constraints.

1.4
Low-rank augmented Lagrangian method

Although the SC-SDP relaxation (
Q
) is substantially more compact than the SDP–RLT formulation, solving it efficiently at scale remains challenging. The main difficulties are as follows:

1.

A huge number of
\Omega(m^{2})
equality constraints;

2.

A large number of
\Omega(l^{2})
inequality constraints;

3.

A nontrivial conic constraint
Y\in\mathcal{K}
;

4.

Failure of Slater’s condition and strong duality whenever
A\not=0
.

In this paper, we propose RiNNAL-S, a low-rank augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) for solving the SC-SDP relaxation (
Q
). Our method builds on the RiNNAL framework and its variants developed in
[
17
,
19
,
18
]
, but extends them to handle the SC-SDP formulation with the new cone constraint
\mathcal{K}
.
More specifically, we rewrite (
Q
) in the splitting form:

\min_{Y,Z}\left\{\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle+\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(Y)+\delta_{\mathcal{K}}(Z)\;\middle|\;\mathcal{B}(Y)\geq 0,\;Y-Z=0\right\}

(5)

where

\mathcal{F}=\{Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}:\mathcal{A}(Y)=0,\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\succeq 0\}.

Let
\Lambda\geq 0
and
W\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
be the multipliers associated with
\mathcal{B}(Y)\geq 0
and
Y-Z=0
, respectively, and let
\sigma>0
be the penalty parameter. Then each outer ALM iteration solves a subproblem of the form

\min_{Y}\;\left\{L_{\sigma}(Y,\Lambda,W)\coloneq\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|(\sigma^{-1}\Lambda-\mathcal{B}(Y))_{+}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\Pi_{\mathcal{K}^{*}}(\sigma^{-1}W-Y)\|^{2}\;\middle|\;Y\in\mathcal{F}\right\}

(6)

followed by the multiplier updates for
\Lambda
and
W
. This ALM framework guarantees global convergence of the proposed method, so the main remaining issue is how to solve the ALM subproblem (
6
) efficiently by exploiting the structure of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{K}
. As shown in Section
2
and Subsection
5.4
, projections onto
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{K}
can be computed efficiently by solving one-dimensional subproblems. To solve (
6
) efficiently, we alternately use two complementary phases.

Low-rank phase.

Suppose the ALM subproblem (
6
) admits an optimal solution of
rank
r
. To exploit this potential low-rank structure, we apply the following low-rank factorization

Y=\widehat{R}\widehat{R}^{\top},\qquad\widehat{R}=\begin{bmatrix}e_{1}^{\top}\\
R\end{bmatrix},\qquad R\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r},

where the first row can be fixed as
e_{1}^{\top}
so that
Y_{11}=1
holds.
Thus, solving (
6
) is equivalent to solving the following nonconvex problem:

\min_{R}\;\left\{L_{\sigma}(\widehat{R}\widehat{R}^{\top},\Lambda,W)\mid\widehat{R}\widehat{R}^{\top}\in\mathcal{F}\right\}.

(7)

Directly handling the nonconvex quadratic constraint in (
7
) is difficult, but the special structure of
\mathcal{F}
allows us to rewrite this constraint as a simple linear constraint
AR=be_{1}^{\top}
. The detailed low-rank model and algorithm are given in Subsection
5.2
.

Convex lifting phase.

The low-rank phase reduces the objective value of (
7
), but does not guarantee global convergence due to its nonconvexity. To restore global convergence, we lift the low-rank solution back to the original convex space (
6
) and perform a projected gradient step

Y^{+}=\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\bigl(Y-\eta\nabla_{Y}L_{\sigma}(Y,\Lambda,W)\bigr).

On the one hand, this step guarantees global convergence for the ALM subproblem. On the other hand, it automatically identifies a suitable rank and an initial point for the next low-rank phase. The convex lifting procedure is presented in detail in Subsection
5.3
.

1.5
Summary of our contributions

The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows.

1.

We introduce a reduced SDP–RLT relaxation and propose the sparsity-cone semidefinite relaxation (
Q
) for sparsity-constrained quadratic programs (
1
). These formulations retain the tightness of the classical SDP–RLT relaxation while reducing the main lifted matrix dimension from
2n+1
to
n+1
, with SC-SDP further replacing the explicit support variables and perspective LMIs by a single structured cone constraint
\mathcal{K}
.

2.

We establish the geometric and algorithmic foundations of the sparsity cone
\mathcal{K}
and its dual cone
\mathcal{K}^{*}
. In particular, we derive equivalent descriptions and explicit characterizations of these cones and show that projection onto
\mathcal{K}
admits an efficient one-dimensional reduction. We also use the Moreau decomposition to recover the
\mathcal{K}^{*}
multiplier update from the
\mathcal{K}
projection.

3.

We derive explicit presolving rules from the dual solution of SC-SDP for the original mixed-binary formulation (
1
). In particular, we show that SC-SDP can provide more informative support-restriction bounds, with strong branching as a special case, establish a dual-fixing rule for individual variables and a screening-cut rule for excluding larger support patterns. We also introduce a dual-refinement step for improving the resulting fixing and screening certificates, thereby reducing the search space before branching.

4.

We develop RiNNAL-S, a two-phase augmented Lagrangian method for solving the SC-SDP relaxation. In the first phase, RiNNAL-S solves a low-rank ALM subproblem in factorized form under the affine constraint induced by
\mathcal{F}
. In the second phase, it performs a convex lifting step through a projected gradient update in the full matrix space. The method exploits the structured projection subproblems associated with
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{K}
, automatically identifies a suitable rank for the low-rank phase and guarantees global convergence for solving the SC-SDP relaxation.

5.

We illustrate the scope of the SC-SDP framework in the unconstrained sparse ridge regression setting. In particular, we show that SC-SDP dominates the standard perspective relaxation and matches the optimal perspective relaxation.

6.

We evaluate the performance of our (
Q
) framework by
conducting extensive computational experiments on several classes of
\ell_{0}
-constrained quadratic optimization problems. The results show that SC-SDP preserves the bound quality of SDP–RLT while delivering significant computational advantages, and often yields much lower-rank solutions in practice. Moreover,
(
Q
) can offer strong variable fixing and
screening capability for utilization within a branch-and-bound
solver.

2.1
Geometric properties of
\mathcal{K}

For a lifted matrix
Y=[1,x^{\top};x,X]
, the cone
\mathcal{K}
only couples the first row and column with the diagonal of
X
. This observation leads to a simple structural description of
\mathcal{K}
. In particular,

{\operatorname{lin}}(\mathcal{K})=\mathcal{K}\cap(-\mathcal{K})=\{Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}:\;Y_{11}=0,\ Y_{12}=0,\ \Diag(Y_{22})=0\},

so
\mathcal{K}
is not pointed. Nevertheless,
\mathcal{K}
still admits useful characterizations and efficient projection methods.
The following lemma provides several equivalent characterizations of
\mathcal{K}
for matrices with
Y_{11}=1
.

Lemma 1
.

The following equivalence holds for any
Y=[1,x^{\top};x,X]\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}
:

Y\in\mathcal{K}\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\mathcal{D}(Y)\succeq 0\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;k\Diag(X)\succeq xx^{\top}\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{X_{ii}}\leq k.

(10)

Note that for a nonnegative scalar
\alpha
, we define

\alpha/0=0
if
\alpha=0
, and
\alpha/0=+\infty
if
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

The equivalence
Y\in\mathcal{K}\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{D}(Y)\succeq 0
is exactly the definition of
\mathcal{K}
in (
4
). For
Y=[1,x^{\top};x,X]
, we have
\mathcal{D}(Y)=[k,x^{\top};x,\Diag(X)]
. Because
k>0
, applying the Schur complement to this matrix yields

\mathcal{D}(Y)\succeq 0\Longleftrightarrow\Diag(X)-k^{-1}xx^{\top}\succeq 0\Longleftrightarrow k\Diag(X)-xx^{\top}\succeq 0
.
This proves the second equivalence.
It remains to show that
\mathcal{D}(Y)\succeq 0\Longleftrightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}/X_{ii}\leq k
.
Since
Y\succeq 0
, each principal
2\times 2
submatrix
[1,x_{i};x_{i},X_{ii}]
is positive semidefinite. Hence
X_{ii}\geq 0
for every

, and
X_{ii}=0
implies
x_{i}=0
. Therefore
x\in{\operatorname{Range}}(\Diag(X))
. Define
\Diag(X)^{\dagger}=\Diag(d)
by setting
d_{i}=1/X_{ii}
if
X_{ii}>0
and
d_{i}=0
if
X_{ii}=0
. Then

x^{\top}\Diag(X)^{\dagger}x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}/X_{ii}
,
where the terms with
X_{ii}=0
are zero because then
x_{i}=0
.
Now apply the generalized Schur complement to the matrix
[k,x^{\top};x,\Diag(X)]
. This shows that

\mathcal{D}(Y)\succeq 0\Longleftrightarrow x\in{\operatorname{Range}}(\Diag(X))\text{ and }x^{\top}\Diag(X)^{\dagger}x\leq k
.
Since we already know that
x\in{\operatorname{Range}}(\Diag(X))
, this reduces to the third equivalence.

The next result shows that, on rank-one lifted points, membership in
\mathcal{K}
exactly captures the underlying
\ell_{0}
-sparsity property.

Theorem 1
.

Let
Y=yy^{\top}
with
y=(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
Y\in\mathcal{K}
if and only if
t=0
or
\|x\|_{0}\leq k
. In particular, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have
[1;x][1;x]^{\top}\in\mathcal{K}
if and only if
\|x\|_{0}\leq k
.

Proof.

If
t=0
, then
\mathcal{D}(Y)=\Diag(0,x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}^{2})\succeq 0
, so
Y\in\mathcal{K}
. Now assume that
t\neq 0
and set
u=x/t
. Since
\mathcal{K}
is a cone,

Y\in\mathcal{K}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t^{-2}Y=\begin{bmatrix}1&u^{\top}\\
u&uu^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{K}.

Applying Lemma
1
to the normalized matrix
t^{-2}Y
, we obtain

\begin{bmatrix}1&u^{\top}\\
u&uu^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{K}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{u_{i}^{2}}\leq k.

The right-hand side is exactly
\|u\|_{0}=\|x\|_{0}\leq k
. This proves both statements.

We use the following example to illustrate the geometry of
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}
and the relationship between the
\mathcal{K}
-constraint and the semidefinite constraint.

Example 2.1

(A slice of
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathbb{S}_{+}^{3}
)
.

Consider the case
n=2
and
k=1
, and fix

Y=\begin{bmatrix}1&x_{1}&x_{2}\\
x_{1}&1&X_{12}\\
x_{2}&X_{12}&1\end{bmatrix}.

Then Lemma
1
gives

Y\in\mathcal{K}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\leq 1,

so the
\mathcal{K}
-constraint is a cylinder in the
(x_{1},x_{2},X_{12})
-space. On the other hand,

Y\succeq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X_{12}\in\left[x_{1}x_{2}-\sqrt{(1-x_{1}^{2})(1-x_{2}^{2})},\,x_{1}x_{2}+\sqrt{(1-x_{1}^{2})(1-x_{2}^{2})}\right].

Hence this slice of
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathbb{S}_{+}^{3}
is obtained by intersecting the cylinder induced by
\mathcal{K}
with the region between the two semidefinite boundary sheets; see Figure

(
1(a)
) for the cylinder and semidefinite boundary, and Figure

(
1(b)
) for the resulting intersection region.

2.2
Projection onto
\mathcal{K}

Projection onto
\mathcal{K}
is required repeatedly in the algorithm. Since membership in
\mathcal{K}
depends only on the first row and column and on the diagonal of the lower-right block, the off-diagonal part of
\bar{X}
is preserved by the projection. The next result makes this reduction explicit. For

\bar{Y}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{a}&\bar{x}^{\top}\\
\bar{x}&\bar{X}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1},\qquad\bar{d}:=\diag(\bar{X}),

projection onto
\mathcal{K}
reduces to a structured convex problem, as shown in
the next proposition.

Proposition 1
.

The projection of
\bar{Y}
onto
\mathcal{K}
is

\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\bar{Y})=\begin{bmatrix}a^{*}&(x^{*})^{\top}\\
x^{*}&\Diag(d^{*})+\bigl(\bar{X}-\Diag(\bar{d})\bigr)\end{bmatrix},

where

(a^{*},x^{*},d^{*})=\argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(a-\bar{a})^{2}+\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|d-\bar{d}\|^{2}\;\middle|\;\begin{bmatrix}ka&x^{\top}\\
x&\Diag(d)\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0\right\}.

(11)

Proof.

By (
10
), membership in
\mathcal{K}
depends only on
Y_{11}
,
Y_{12}
,
Y_{21}
, and
\diag(Y_{22})
. Since the Frobenius norm is separable entrywise, the off-diagonal entries of
Y_{22}
are unchanged by the projection. Hence any minimizer has the displayed form, which yields (
11
). The feasible set is closed and convex, and the objective is strongly convex, so the solution is unique.

The reduced problem (
11
) already isolates the essential difficulty. By the Schur complement, it is equivalent to a convex problem with the single nonlinear constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}/d_{i}\leq ka
. If the projection onto the orthant
a\geq 0
and
d\geq 0
is feasible, then it is optimal. Otherwise, strong duality introduces a single multiplier
\lambda
, and eliminating
x
reduces the remaining computation to a scalar equation in
\lambda
. The next result gives the resulting one-dimensional reduction.

Proposition 2
.

Let
(a^{*},x^{*},d^{*})
be the unique optimal solution of problem (
11
).
For any
\lambda>0
, consider for each
i\in[n]
,

d_{i}(\lambda):=\argmin_{d_{i}\geq 0}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i})^{2}+\frac{\lambda\bar{x}_{i}^{2}}{d_{i}+\lambda}\right\},\qquad i\in[n].

(12)

Then
d_{i}(\lambda)=0
if

\bar{d}_{i}+{\bar{x}_{i}^{2}}/{\lambda}\leq 0
; otherwise,
d_{i}(\lambda)>0
is the unique solution of

(d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i})(d_{i}+\lambda)^{2}=\lambda\bar{x}_{i}^{2},\qquad i\in[n].

(13)

We have that either of the following two cases holds:

(i)

If
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{x}_{i}^{2}/(\bar{d}_{i})_{+}\leq k\bar{a}_{+}
, then

(a^{*},x^{*},d^{*})=(\bar{a}_{+},\bar{x},\bar{d}_{+}).

(ii)

Otherwise, there exists a multiplier
\lambda^{*}>0
such that

a^{*}=(\bar{a}+k\lambda^{*})_{+},\qquad x_{i}^{*}=\frac{d_{i}^{*}}{d_{i}^{*}+\lambda^{*}}\,\bar{x}_{i},\quad i\in[n],

where
d_{i}^{*}=d_{i}(\lambda^{*})
.
Such a multiplier
\lambda^{*}
satisfies the scalar equation

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\bar{x}_{i}^{2}\,d_{i}(\lambda^{*})}{(d_{i}(\lambda^{*})+\lambda^{*})^{2}}=k(\bar{a}+k\lambda^{*})_{+}.

(14)

Proof.

By the Schur complement, problem (
11
) is equivalent to

\min_{a,x,d}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(a-\bar{a})^{2}+\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|d-\bar{d}\|^{2}\;\middle|\;a\geq 0,\ d\geq 0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{d_{i}}\leq ka\right\}.

(15)

Note that
(\bar{a}_{+},\bar{x},\bar{d}_{+})
is the projection of
(\bar{a},\bar{x},\bar{d})
onto the set defined by
a\geq 0
and
d\geq 0
, which is a superset of the feasible set of (
15
).
If

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\bar{x}_{i})^{2}/(\bar{d}_{i})_{+}\leq k\bar{a}_{+}
,
then
(\bar{a}_{+},\bar{x},\bar{d}_{+})
is feasible for (
15
), and hence it is the projection onto the feasible set of (
15
).
Otherwise, consider the dual problem of (
15
):

\max_{\lambda\geq 0}\min_{a\geq 0,d\geq 0,x}\left\{L(a,x,d;\lambda):=\frac{1}{2}(a-\bar{a})^{2}+\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|d-\bar{d}\|^{2}-\lambda ka+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{d_{i}}\right\}.

(16)

Strong duality holds by Slater’s condition. Let
(a^{*},x^{*},d^{*})
be optimal for
(
15
)
and
\lambda^{*}
be optimal for (
16
) . If
\lambda^{*}=0
, then

(a^{*},x^{*},d^{*})=(\bar{a}_{+},\bar{x},\bar{d}_{+})
, which contradicts the assumption that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\bar{x}_{i})^{2}/(\bar{d}_{i})_{+}>k\bar{a}_{+}
. Hence
\lambda^{*}>0
.

Minimizing the Lagrangian
L(a,x,d;\lambda)
with respect to
x
first gives
x_{i}=d_{i}\bar{x}_{i}/(d_{i}+\lambda)
for all
i\in[n]
. Thus the inner subproblem in (
16
) reduces to

\big(a(\lambda),d(\lambda)\big):=\argmin_{a\geq 0,d\geq 0}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(a-\bar{a})^{2}-\lambda ka+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{1}{2}(d_{i}-\bar{d}_{i})^{2}+\frac{\lambda\bar{x}_{i}^{2}}{d_{i}+\lambda}\right]\right\}.

(17)

Thus
a(\lambda)=(\bar{a}+k\lambda)_{+}
, and each
d_{i}(\lambda)
is the unique minimizer of the stated one-dimensional problem (
12
). If
\bar{d}_{i}+{\bar{x}_{i}^{2}}/{\lambda}\leq 0
, then the function
of
d_{i}
within the square brackets in (
17
) is nondecreasing on
[0,\infty)
, and hence
d_{i}(\lambda)=0
; otherwise, the first-order condition yields (
13
). Since
\lambda^{*}>0
, complementary slackness implies that the constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}/d_{i}\leq ka
is active at the optimum. Substituting the formulas for
x_{i}^{*}={d_{i}(\lambda^{*})\bar{x}_{i}}/(d_{i}(\lambda^{*})+\lambda^{*})
and

a^{*}=a(\lambda^{*})
into this equality gives (
14
).

Any multiplier
\lambda^{*}>0
satisfying (
14
), together with the formulas above, satisfies the KKT conditions and therefore yields the unique projection
(a^{*},x^{*},d^{*})
.

Remark 2
.

To find an optimal multiplier
\lambda^{*}
, we solve the scalar equation (
14
). On the branch where
d_{i}(\lambda)>0
, implicit differentiation of (
13
) gives

\frac{{\rm d}\,d_{i}(\lambda)}{{\rm d}\lambda}=\frac{(d_{i}(\lambda)-\lambda)\bar{x}_{i}^{2}}{(d_{i}(\lambda)+\lambda)^{3}}\bigg/\left(1+\frac{2\lambda\bar{x}_{i}^{2}}{(d_{i}(\lambda)+\lambda)^{3}}\right).

Hence (
14
) can be handled efficiently by a Newton method.

Remark 3
.

In case
(ii)
of Proposition
2
, the multiplier
\lambda^{*}
belongs to the interval
[0,U]
, where
U:=(-\bar{a}+\sqrt{\bar{a}^{2}+\|\bar{x}\|^{2}})/(2k)
.
Indeed, if

\phi(\lambda):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{x}_{i}^{2}d_{i}(\lambda)/(d_{i}(\lambda)+\lambda)^{2}-k(\bar{a}+k\lambda)_{+}
,
then the elementary bound
d/(d+\lambda)^{2}\leq 1/(4\lambda)
for
d\geq 0
implies

\phi(\lambda)\leq\|\bar{x}\|^{2}/(4\lambda)-k(\bar{a}+k\lambda)_{+}
.
Since case
(ii)
implies
\phi(0^{+})>0
and
\phi(\lambda^{*})=0
, the definition of
U
gives
\phi(U)\leq 0
, and hence
\lambda^{*}\in[0,U]
. This bound is convenient for safeguarded Newton or bisection, although it is generally not tight.

Remark 4
.

For problems with additional nonnegativity constraints
x\geq 0
, the RLT system (
3
) naturally induces the lifted constraint
Y\in\mathcal{N}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is defined in (
9
). The projection onto
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{N}
is then obtained from the projection onto
\mathcal{K}
with only two changes: the off-diagonal entries of the lower-right block are projected independently onto the nonnegative orthant, and the vector
\bar{x}
in the structured subproblem is replaced by
(\bar{x})_{+}
. Consequently, the same one-dimensional reduction as in Proposition
2
applies after this replacement.

2.3
Geometric properties of
\mathcal{K}^{*}

The dual cone
\mathcal{K}^{*}
is defined by

\mathcal{K}^{*}=\{W\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}:\langle W,Y\rangle\geq 0\ \forall Y\in\mathcal{K}\}.

The dual cone
\mathcal{K}^{*}
plays a central role in both the multiplier updates of the augmented Lagrangian method and the dual-fixing analysis. We begin by deriving an explicit representation of
\mathcal{K}^{*}
. The following completion lemma provides the key ingredient.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

there exists
U\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
\diag(U)=d
such that

\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&z^{\top}\\
z&U\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0;

(ii)

\alpha\geq 0
,
d\geq 0
, and
z_{i}^{2}\leq\alpha d_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). If (i) holds, then positive semidefiniteness implies
\alpha\geq 0
,
d_{i}=U_{ii}\geq 0
, and every principal
2\times 2
minor gives
z_{i}^{2}\leq\alpha d_{i}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Assume (ii). If
\alpha=0
, then necessarily
z=0
, and choosing
U=\Diag(d)
gives (i). If
\alpha>0
, define
U:=\alpha^{-1}zz^{\top}+\Diag(d-\alpha^{-1}(z\circ z))
, where
z\circ z=(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{n}^{2})^{\top}
. Then
\diag(U)=d
, the diagonal correction is nonnegative by (ii), and
U-\alpha^{-1}zz^{\top}\succeq 0
. Hence the Schur complement implies (i).

Proposition 3
.

Every
W\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
admits a unique representation of the form

W=\begin{bmatrix}k\alpha&z^{\top}\\
z&\Diag(d)\end{bmatrix},

where

\alpha\geq 0,\;d\geq 0,\;z_{i}^{2}\leq\alpha d_{i},\;i\in[n].

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{K}=\{Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}:\mathcal{D}(Y)\succeq 0\}
, standard conic duality gives

\mathcal{K}^{*}=\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+})
. Moreover, for any
T=[\alpha,z^{\top};z,U]\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
, we have

\mathcal{D}^{*}(T)=[k\alpha,z^{\top};z,\Diag(U)]
. Hence
W\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
if and only if

W=[k\alpha,z^{\top};z,\Diag(d)]

for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that there exists

U\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
\diag(U)=d
and
[\alpha,z^{\top};z,U]\succeq 0
. By Lemma
2
, this is equivalent to

\alpha\geq 0
,
d\geq 0
, and
z_{i}^{2}\leq\alpha d_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
, which proves the displayed characterization. Uniqueness follows immediately from the entries of
W=[k\alpha,z^{\top};z,\Diag(d)]
.

The multiplier update associated with
\mathcal{K}^{*}
can be recovered from
\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}
through the Moreau decomposition,

\bar{Y}=\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\bar{Y})-\Pi_{\mathcal{K}^{*}}(-\bar{Y}),

(18)

so the algorithm only needs the projection routine for
\mathcal{K}
.

3
SC-SDP relaxation

In this section, we develop the SC-SDP relaxation and the associated dual objects used later for presolving and algorithm design. Subsection
3.1
presents the relaxation formulation and its dual counterpart, Subsection
3.2
compares its tightness with the standard SDP and SDP–RLT relaxations. Subsection
3.3
studies the unconstrained sparse ridge regression setting and its connection with the optimal perspective relaxation.

3.1
Relaxation formulation

SDP relaxation.

The standard SDP relaxation for (
1
) is constructed by introducing auxiliary variables to represent all quadratic products of
x
and
z
, relaxing the binary variables to continuous ones, and linearizing the nonconvex constraints through positive semidefinite constraints. The resulting SDP relaxation can be written as follows:

\min\left\{\langle Q,X_{xx}\rangle+2c^{\top}x\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &Ax=b,\;Bx\geq d,\;x=\diag(X_{xz}),\;z=\diag(X_{zz})\\
&\begin{bmatrix}1&x^{\top}&z^{\top}\\
x&X_{xx}&X_{xz}\\
z&X_{xz}^{\top}&X_{zz}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\;z\in\mathcal{Z}\end{aligned}\right\},

(19)

where
\mathcal{Z}\coloneqq\{z\in[0,1]^{n}:e^{\top}z\leq k\}
.
The SDP relaxation provides a valid lower bound for (
1
) but is not tight in general.

SDP–RLT relaxation.

To further strengthen (
19
), we add the RLT constraints in (
3
) derived from the original linear constraints. This yields the SDP–RLT relaxation

\min\left\{\langle Q,X_{xx}\rangle+2c^{\top}x\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{A}(Y)=0,\;\mathcal{B}(Y)\geq 0,\;x=\diag(X_{xz}),\;z=\diag(X_{zz})\\
&\begin{bmatrix}1&x^{\top}&z^{\top}\\
x&X_{xx}&X_{xz}\\
z&X_{xz}^{\top}&X_{zz}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\;z\in\mathcal{Z}\end{aligned}\right\}.

(20)

where
\mathcal{A}(Y)
and
\mathcal{B}(Y)
are defined in (
3
).
The SDP–RLT relaxation provides a significantly tighter bound than the standard SDP relaxation alone. However, it is computationally prohibitive for large-scale problems due to the large semidefinite matrix with dimension
2n+1
and the large number of RLT constraints.

Reduced SDP–RLT relaxation.

To avoid the dimensional bottleneck of (
20
), we propose a reduced SDP–RLT relaxation that preserves the SDP–RLT bound while reducing the size of the semidefinite matrix. To construct it, we introduce an auxiliary variable
X=xx^{\top}
to linearize the quadratic term in the objective function and represent the complementarity constraints in (
1
) by the linear matrix inequalities (LMIs)

\begin{bmatrix}z_{i}&x_{i}\\
x_{i}&X_{ii}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\qquad i\in[n].

Then, by relaxing the rank-one constraint
X=xx^{\top}
, we obtain the lifted matrix

Y\;\coloneq\;\begin{bmatrix}Y_{11}&Y_{12}\\
Y_{21}&Y_{22}\end{bmatrix}\;=\;\begin{bmatrix}1&x^{\top}\\
x&X\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0

(21)

and, together with the RLT constraints, the reduced SDP–RLT relaxation of (
1
) becomes

\min_{Y,z}\left\{\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{A}(Y)=0,\;\mathcal{B}(Y)\geq 0,\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\succeq 0\\
&\begin{bmatrix}z_{i}&x_{i}\\
x_{i}&X_{ii}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\ i\in[n],\;z\in\mathcal{Z}\end{aligned}\right\},

(22)

where the cost matrix
\overline{Q}\coloneqq[0,c^{\top};c,Q]
.
As we shall show in Theorem
2
, the reduced SDP–RLT relaxation (
22
) is equivalent to the SDP–RLT relaxation (
20
) and has a much smaller semidefinite matrix with dimension
n+1
instead of
2n+1
.
However, (
22
) still keeps the explicit support vector
z
and
n
separate LMIs, which are not conducive for algorithm design and presolving.

SC-SDP relaxation.

The SC-SDP relaxation is obtained by
eliminating the variable
z
and its associated constraint
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, and
replacing the
n
separate LMIs in (
22
) by a single structured constraint that still captures the sparsity information. The explicit formulation (
Q
) is provided in Subsection
1.2
, where the cone
\mathcal{K}
is defined by (
4
).
The dual problem of (
Q
) can be written as

\max\left\{\alpha\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &\overline{Q}-\mathcal{A}^{*}(\Gamma)-\mathcal{B}^{*}(\Lambda)-\mathcal{D}^{*}(W)-S-\alpha E_{11}=0,\\
&\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\;\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}^{m},\;\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{(l+1)\times(l+1)}_{+},\;W\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+},\;S\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}\end{aligned}\right\},

(D)

Here,
E_{11}
denotes the matrix with 1 in the
(1,1)
-entry and 0 elsewhere. The KKT conditions for (
Q
) and (
D
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(Y)=0,\;\mathcal{B}(Y)\geq 0,\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\in\mathcal{K}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+},

(23)

\displaystyle\overline{Q}-\mathcal{A}^{*}(\Gamma)-\mathcal{B}^{*}(\Lambda)-\mathcal{D}^{*}(W)-S-\alpha E_{11}=0,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{B}(Y),\Lambda\rangle=0,\;\langle\mathcal{D}(Y),W\rangle=0,\;\langle Y,S\rangle=0,\;\Lambda\geq 0,\;W\succeq 0,\;S\succeq 0.

The formulations above provide the basis for the remainder of this section. In particular, Subsection
3.2
shows that (
Q
) has the same optimal value as the reduced SDP–RLT relaxation (
22
) and the SDP–RLT relaxation (
20
). Subsection
3.3
relates SC-SDP to the optimal perspective relaxation in the special case of unconstrained sparse ridge regression setting. The dual problem
(
D
) introduced here will be used again in Section
4
to derive presolving rules and a refined dual certificate.

3.2
Tightness comparison

The next result places the SC-SDP relaxation in the usual hierarchy of relaxations for (
1
). In particular, it has the same optimal value as the reduced SDP–RLT and SDP–RLT relaxations, while remaining tighter than the standard SDP relaxation.

Theorem 2
.

The following inequality chain holds:

\emph{val}\eqref{eq:Shor}\;\leq\;\emph{val}\eqref{eq:Shor-RLT}\;=\;\emph{val}\eqref{eq:Decomposed-SDP-z}\;=\;\emph{val}\eqref{eq:intro-Decomposed-SDP}\;\leq\;\emph{val}\eqref{eq:QP}.

Proof.

We first compare the SDP–RLT relaxation (
20
) with the reduced SDP–RLT relaxation (
22
). Let a feasible solution of (
20
) be given and set
Y=[1,x^{\top};x,X_{xx}]
.
Since
Y
is a principal submatrix of the lifted SDP matrix,
Y\succeq 0
. Moreover, each principal submatrix indexed by
(x_{i},z_{i})
gives

\begin{bmatrix}X_{xx,ii}&X_{xz,ii}\\
X_{xz,ii}&X_{zz,ii}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}X_{xx,ii}&x_{i}\\
x_{i}&z_{i}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

where
X_{xx,ii}
,
X_{xz,ii}
, and
X_{zz,ii}
are the
(i,i)
-entries of
X_{xx}
,
X_{xz}
, and
X_{zz}
, respectively.
After permuting the two rows and columns, this is exactly the perspective LMI in (
22
). The RLT constraints depend only on
Y
, and the objective values agree because
\langle Q,X_{xx}\rangle+2c^{\top}x=\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle
.
Hence every feasible solution of (
20
) projects to a feasible solution of (
22
) with the same objective value.

Conversely, let
(Y,z)
be feasible for (
22
), where
Y=[1,x^{\top};x,X]\succeq 0
. Take a square factorization
Y=PP^{\top}
with
P\in\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)}
, and write the rows of
P
as
p_{0},\ldots,p_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. For each
i\in[n]
, consider

G_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}1&x_{i}&z_{i}\\
x_{i}&X_{ii}&x_{i}\\
z_{i}&x_{i}&z_{i}\end{bmatrix}.

The diagonal entries and the principal
2\times 2
minors of
G_{i}
are nonnegative because
Y\succeq 0
,
z_{i}\in[0,1]
, and
[z_{i},x_{i};x_{i},X_{ii}]\succeq 0
. Its determinant is
\det(G_{i})=(1-z_{i})(z_{i}X_{ii}-x_{i}^{2})\geq 0
. Since a symmetric
3\times 3
matrix is positive semidefinite if and only if all of its principal minors are nonnegative, we have
G_{i}\succeq 0
.
To construct the required new row explicitly, let
P_{i}
be the two-row matrix with rows
p_{0}
and
p_{i}
, and set

S_{i}:=P_{i}P_{i}^{\top}=[1,x_{i};x_{i},X_{ii}],\qquad b_{i}:=(z_{i},x_{i})^{\top}.

Since
G_{i}=[S_{i},b_{i};b_{i}^{\top},z_{i}]\succeq 0
, the generalized Schur complement gives
b_{i}\in{\operatorname{Range}}(S_{i})
and
z_{i}-b_{i}^{\top}S_{i}^{\dagger}b_{i}\geq 0
. Define
u_{i}=P_{i}^{\top}S_{i}^{\dagger}b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then

P_{i}u_{i}=S_{i}S_{i}^{\dagger}b_{i}=b_{i},\qquad\|u_{i}\|^{2}=b_{i}^{\top}S_{i}^{\dagger}b_{i}.

Let
\delta_{i}:=z_{i}-\|u_{i}\|^{2}\geq 0
. Embed the factor rows in
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
by replacing each
p_{j}
with
(p_{j},0)
, and continue to write
P
for the matrix formed by these zero-padded rows. If
e_{i}
is the
i
-th coordinate vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set

q_{i}:=(u_{i},\sqrt{\delta_{i}}\,e_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}.

Then
\langle p_{0},q_{i}\rangle=z_{i}
,
\langle p_{i},q_{i}\rangle=x_{i}
, and
\langle q_{i},q_{i}\rangle=z_{i}
. The remaining inner products involving the vectors
q_{i}
are not prescribed by (
22
); define
(X_{xz})_{ji}:=\langle p_{j},q_{i}\rangle
and
(X_{zz})_{ij}:=\langle q_{i},q_{j}\rangle
.
Let
P_{z}\coloneq[q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}]^{\top}
. Then

\begin{bmatrix}1&x^{\top}&z^{\top}\\
x&X&X_{xz}\\
z&X_{xz}^{\top}&X_{zz}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}P\\
P_{z}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}P\\
P_{z}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\succeq 0.

Moreover,

\diag(X_{xz})=x,\qquad\diag(X_{zz})=z.

The RLT constraints are unchanged because they only involve
Y
, and the objective value is also unchanged. Hence (
20
) and (
22
) have the same optimal value.

Next, we compare (
22
) with the SC-SDP relaxation (
Q
). The two formulations have the same objective function and the same constraints involving
\mathcal{A}(Y)
,
\mathcal{B}(Y)
,
Y_{11}=1
, and
Y\succeq 0
. Let
(Y,z)
be feasible for (
22
). The LMI
[z_{i},x_{i};x_{i},X_{ii}]\succeq 0
gives
z_{i}\geq 0
and
z_{i}X_{ii}\geq x_{i}^{2}
. Hence, with the convention used in Lemma
1
,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{X_{ii}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}=e^{\top}z\leq k.

By Lemma
1
, this is equivalent to
Y\in\mathcal{K}
. Thus
Y
is feasible for (
Q
).

Conversely, let
Y
be feasible for (
Q
). By Lemma
1
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}/X_{ii}\leq k
.
Define

z_{i}:=\begin{cases}x_{i}^{2}/X_{ii},&X_{ii}>0,\\
0,&X_{ii}=0.\end{cases}

Since
Y\succeq 0
, we have
X_{ii}\geq x_{i}^{2}
, and hence
0\leq z_{i}\leq 1
. The preceding inequality gives
e^{\top}z\leq k
, so
z\in\mathcal{Z}
. By construction,
z_{i}X_{ii}=x_{i}^{2}
for every
i
, and therefore

\begin{bmatrix}z_{i}&x_{i}\\
x_{i}&X_{ii}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\qquad i\in[n].

Hence
(Y,z)
is feasible for (
22
). Therefore
\emph{val}\eqref{eq:intro-Decomposed-SDP}=\emph{val}\eqref{eq:Decomposed-SDP-z}
.

Moreover, the SDP–RLT relaxation is obtained by adding RLT constraints to the SDP relaxation, so
\emph{val}\eqref{eq:Shor}\leq\emph{val}\eqref{eq:Shor-RLT}
. Finally, since (
1
) is an exact reformulation of (
P
), both the SDP and SDP–RLT problems are relaxations of the original problem, and hence
\emph{val}\eqref{eq:Shor-RLT}\leq\emph{val}\eqref{eq:QP}
.

Remark 5
.

Han et al.
[
15
]
established an elegant related reduction and equivalence result for a perspective-type SDP relaxation. Their analysis focuses on convex quadratic perspective relaxations; here we allow a nonconvex quadratic objective, incorporate the RLT constraints, and link the reduced formulation to the sparsity-cone SC-SDP model used later for computation and presolving.

3.3
Equivalence to optimal perspective
in the unconstrained sparse ridge regression setting

In this subsection, we use unconstrained sparse ridge regression to illustrate the behavior of SC-SDP in a setting where the optimal perspective relaxation is well defined. We show that SC-SDP dominates the standard perspective relaxation and is equivalent to the optimal perspective relaxation.
The sparse ridge regression problem can be formulated as the following optimization problem:

v^{\mathrm{P}}\;\coloneqq\;\min_{x}\left\{\|Ax-b\|^{2}+\gamma\|x\|^{2}\;\middle|\;\|x\|_{0}\leq k\right\},

(24)

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\gamma>0
, and
k\in[n]
are given.
The perspective relaxation of (
24
) is

v^{\mathrm{PR}}\;\coloneqq\;\min_{x,z}\;\left\{\|Ax-b\|^{2}+\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{z_{i}}\;\middle|\;z\in\mathcal{Z}\right\},

(25)

where
\mathcal{Z}=\{z\in[0,1]^{n}\mid e^{\top}z\leq k\}
.
The SC-SDP relaxation of (
24
) in the form of (
Q
) can be written as

v^{\mathrm{SC}}\;\coloneqq\;\min_{Y}\;\Big\{\langle C_{\gamma},Y\rangle\;\Big|\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\in\mathcal{K}\cap\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}\Big\},

(26)

where

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}\;\coloneqq\;C+\begin{bmatrix}0&0^{\top}\\
0&\gamma I_{n}\end{bmatrix},\;\;C\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}b^{\top}\\
-A^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}b&-A\end{bmatrix}.

We first compare (
26
) with the standard perspective relaxation (
25
) in the next proposition.

Proposition 4
.

The SC-SDP relaxation (
26
) is tighter than the perspective relaxation (
25
), i.e.,
v^{\mathrm{SC}}\geq v^{\mathrm{PR}}
.

Proof.

The perspective relaxation (
25
) admits the lifted reformulation

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{PR}}\;=\;

\displaystyle\min_{x,z,t}\;\left\{\|Ax-b\|^{2}+\gamma e^{\top}t\;\middle|\;\begin{bmatrix}z_{i}&x_{i}\\
x_{i}&t_{i}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\;i\in[n],\;z\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{Y,z,t}\;\Big\{\langle C,Y\rangle+\gamma e^{\top}t\;\Big|\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\succeq 0,\;\begin{bmatrix}z_{i}&x_{i}\\
x_{i}&t_{i}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\ i\in[n],\;z\in\mathcal{Z}\Big\}.

(28)

On the other hand, by the equivalence (
10
),
the SC-SDP relaxation (
26
) can be reformulated as

v^{\mathrm{SC}}\;\coloneqq\;\min_{Y,z,t}\;\Big\{\langle C,Y\rangle+\gamma e^{\top}t\;\Big|\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\succeq 0,\;\begin{bmatrix}z_{i}&x_{i}\\
x_{i}&t_{i}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\ i\in[n],\;z\in\mathcal{Z},\;\diag(X)=t\Big\}.

(29)

The feasible set of (
29
) is contained in that of (
28
), since it imposes the additional constraint
\diag(X)=t
. Because the two problems have the same objective, it follows that
v^{\mathrm{SC}}\geq v^{\mathrm{PR}}
.

The next theorem sharpens this comparison by identifying (
26
) with the strongest relaxation in the perspective family.

Theorem 3
.

The SC-SDP relaxation (
26
) is equivalent to the following optimal perspective relaxation:

\max_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\;\min_{x,z}\left\{x^{\top}Q_{\mu}x+2c^{\top}x+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}\;\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{z_{i}}+b^{\top}b\;\middle|\;z\in\mathcal{Z},\;Q_{\mu}\succeq 0\right\},

(30)

where
Q_{\mu}\coloneqq A^{\top}A+\gamma I-\Diag(\mu)
and
c\coloneqq-A^{\top}b
.

Proof.

Starting from (
29
), eliminate
t
to obtain

\min_{Y,z}\;\Big\{\langle C,Y\rangle+\gamma e^{\top}\diag(X)\;\Big|\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\succeq 0,\;z\in\mathcal{Z},\;X_{ii}\geq\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{z_{i}},\;i\in[n]\Big\}.

(31)

Problem (
31
) admits a strictly feasible point, so strong duality holds. Its dual is exactly the optimal perspective relaxation (
30
) with the constant
b^{\top}b
retained.

4
Presolving with SC-SDP

For sparsity-constrained problems such as (
P
) and (
1
), the main combinatorial difficulty is to identify the support of the solution. A strong relaxation is therefore useful not only as a source of lower bounds, but also as a source of certificates that fix variables or exclude impossible support patterns before extensive branching. This section shows how the SC-SDP dual can be used for this purpose.

Restricted relaxations.

A direct way to test a support decision is to impose the corresponding restriction and solve the restricted relaxation. Although SC-SDP eliminates the explicit support vector
z
, the same sparsity-cone construction and algorithmic framework extend naturally to these restricted subproblems; see Appendix
A
. In sparse ridge regression, the tightness results from Subsection
3.3
imply that the resulting SC-SDP support-restriction bounds dominate the standard perspective bounds and match the optimal perspective bounds. Thus this route provides strong node-restriction bounds, with strong branching as a special case.

Root-dual certificates.

The difficulty is that support-restriction bounds require solving one relaxation for each candidate restriction, and the number of candidates can grow quickly when screening larger support patterns. Our alternative is to extract presolving certificates from a single optimal dual solution of the root SC-SDP relaxation. This is the main contribution of this section: Subsection
4.1
derives dual-fixing rules for individual variables, Subsection
4.2
derives screening cuts for larger support patterns, and Subsection
4.3
introduces a dual-refinement step that strengthens these certificates without changing the SC-SDP lower bound.

Remark 6
.

The same certificate construction can also be applied at a child node of a branch-and-bound tree. In this case, the node restrictions are incorporated into the SC-SDP relaxation, and the resulting node-level dual solution yields fixing and screening certificates with respect to the corresponding node bounds.

4.1
Variable fixing

In this subsection, we show how to derive variable-fixing rules from an optimal dual solution of the SC-SDP relaxation (
Q
). These rules can then be used to reduce the search space of the original problem (
1
).
The details are provided in the next theorem.

Theorem 4

(Dual fixing)
.

Let
v^{\mathrm{UB}}
be any upper bound on the optimal value
v^{\mathrm{P}}
of (
1
).
Let
(\alpha,\Gamma,\Lambda,W,S)
be feasible dual variables of (
D
)
with objective value
v^{\mathrm{LB}}
.
Then, for any
p\in[n]
, any optimal solution of (
1
)
(x^{*},z^{*})
satisfies

z_{p}^{*}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\quad w_{[k]}-w_{p}>v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}},\\
1,&\text{if}\quad w_{p}-w_{[k+1]}>v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}},\end{cases}

where
w_{i}:={(W_{12})_{i}^{2}}/{(W_{22})_{ii}}
if
(W_{22})_{ii}>0
, and
w_{i}:=0
otherwise.

Proof.

Let
(x,z)
be any optimal solution of (
1
). Define
Y=[1,x^{\top};x,xx^{\top}]
as the corresponding rank-one lifted matrix.
By the dual feasibility of
(\alpha,\Gamma,\Lambda,W,S)
and primal feasibility of
Y
, we have

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{UB}}

\displaystyle\geq v^{\mathrm{P}}=x^{\top}Qx+2c^{\top}x=\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle=\alpha Y_{11}+\langle\mathcal{A}^{*}(\Gamma)+\mathcal{B}^{*}(\Lambda)+\mathcal{D}^{*}(W)+S,Y\rangle

\displaystyle\geq v^{\mathrm{LB}}+\langle W,\mathcal{D}(Y)\rangle=v^{\mathrm{LB}}+kW_{11}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left((W_{22})_{ii}x_{i}^{2}+2(W_{12})_{i}x_{i}\right).

We prove the first case by contradiction.
Fix any
p\in[n]
.
Suppose
z_{p}=1
and
w_{[k]}-w_{p}>v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}}\geq 0
.
Let
J:=\{i\in[n]\mid z_{i}=1\}
. Then
|J|\leq k
,
p\in J
, and
i\notin J
implies
x_{i}=0
. Moreover, for every
i
,

(W_{22})_{ii}x_{i}^{2}+2(W_{12})_{i}x_{i}\geq-w_{i},

where the case
(W_{22})_{ii}=0
follows from
W\succeq 0
, which implies
(W_{12})_{i}=0
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}}

\displaystyle\geq kW_{11}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left((W_{22})_{ii}x_{i}^{2}+2(W_{12})_{i}x_{i}\right)\geq kW_{11}-\sum_{i\in J}w_{i}

\displaystyle\geq kW_{11}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{[i]}+(w_{[k]}-w_{p})\geq w_{[k]}-w_{p}>v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}},

where the third inequality uses
|J|\leq k
and
p\in J
, and the fourth inequality uses
W_{11}\geq w_{i}
for all
i
. This leads to a contradiction, and hence
z_{p}=0
. The second case is proved similarly by contradiction.

4.2
Screening cuts

When dual fixing alone does not identify any variable, we can still generate valid cuts from the same dual solution. To state these screening cuts, we first recall the notion of an SCG-tuple from
[
29
]
.

Definition 1

(
SCG-tuple).

Given two disjoint index subsets
S,N\subseteq[n]
with
|S|\leq k
, let

R:=[n]\setminus(S\cup N)

denote the remaining index set. For any index set
C\subseteq R
, we say that

(S,N,C)
is an
SCG-tuple
with respect to
w
if
the index set
C
is constructed as

C:=\{i\in R\mid w_{i}\geq(w_{R})_{[c]}\},

where

c:=\min\{k-|S|,\ |R|\}.

In particular, when
c=0
, we set
C:=\emptyset
.
Throughout this paper, for an SCG-tuple
(S,N,C)
, we refer to
S
as the

candidate support set
,
N
as the
candidate non-support set
, and

C
as the
complement support set
relative to
S
, which is determined
based on
S
,
N
, and
w
.

Using the SCG-tuple, we can obtain the following screening cut from the dual solution of (
Q
).

Theorem 5

(Screening cuts)
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
4
, let
(S,N,C)
be an SCG-tuple with respect to
w
. If

\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{[i]}-\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}-\sum_{i\in C}w_{i}\;>\;v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}},

then the following screening cut is valid for all optimal solutions of (
1
):

\sum_{i\in S}z_{i}+\sum_{i\in N}(1-z_{i})\;\leq\;|S|+|N|-1.

Proof.

Let
(x,z)
be an optimal solution of (
1
), set
J:=\{i\in[n]\mid z_{i}=1\}
, and define
Y=[1,x^{\top};x,xx^{\top}]
. Since
z
is binary and
e^{\top}z\leq k
, we have
|J|\leq k
. Also,
\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle=v^{\mathrm{P}}\leq v^{\mathrm{UB}}
. Since
Y
is feasible for (
Q
), and since
v^{\mathrm{LB}}=\alpha
, the dual stationarity equation gives
\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle-v^{\mathrm{LB}}\geq\langle W,\mathcal{D}(Y)\rangle
, where

\langle W,\mathcal{D}(Y)\rangle=kW_{11}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(W_{22})_{ii}x_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}(W_{12})_{i}x_{i}.

Here the inequality uses
\mathcal{A}(Y)=0
,
\mathcal{B}(Y)\geq 0
,
\Lambda\geq 0
, and
Y,S\succeq 0
.
For each
i
, the principal
2\times 2
submatrix of
W
indexed by
(1,i+1)
is positive semidefinite. Hence
(W_{22})_{ii}\geq 0
and
W_{11}\geq w_{i}
. Moreover,

(W_{22})_{ii}x_{i}^{2}+2(W_{12})_{i}x_{i}\geq-w_{i}.

Indeed, this follows by completing the square when
(W_{22})_{ii}>0
, while if
(W_{22})_{ii}=0
, positive semidefiniteness implies
(W_{12})_{i}=0
and both sides are zero. If
i\notin J
, then
z_{i}=0
and the complementarity constraint in (
1
) gives
x_{i}=0
, so the corresponding term is zero. Therefore

v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}}\geq kW_{11}-\sum_{i\in J}w_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{[i]}-\sum_{i\in J}w_{i}.

Suppose, to obtain a contradiction, that this optimal solution violates the proposed cut. Since each term in the left-hand side of the cut is at most one, violation is possible only if
z_{i}=1
for all
i\in S
and
z_{i}=0
for all
i\in N
. Thus
S\subseteq J
and
J\cap N=\emptyset
. Let
R=[n]\setminus(S\cup N)
and
L:=J\backslash S
. Then
J=S\cup L
and
|L|\leq k-|S|
. By the definition of an SCG-tuple,
C
contains the
c=\min\{k-|S|,|R|\}
largest scores in
R
. Since
L\subseteq R
,
|L|\leq c
, and
w_{i}\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{i\in L}w_{i}\leq\sum_{i\in C}w_{i}.

Consequently,
\sum_{i\in J}w_{i}=\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}+\sum_{i\in L}w_{i}\leq\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}+\sum_{i\in C}w_{i}
, and hence

v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{[i]}-\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}-\sum_{i\in C}w_{i}>v^{\mathrm{UB}}-v^{\mathrm{LB}},

which is impossible. Therefore no optimal solution can violate the cut, so the cut is valid for all optimal solutions of (
1
).

4.3
Dual refinement

The presolving rules in Theorems
4
and
5
both depend on the semidefinite multiplier
W\succeq 0
in the dual problem (
D
) through the scores

w_{i}=\frac{W_{1,i+1}^{2}}{W_{i+1,i+1}},\qquad i\in[n],

with the zero-denominator convention used in Theorem
4
. Since the dual optimal solution is generally not unique, different optimal certificates may give the same lower bound but different presolving strength. We therefore introduce a dual-refinement step to select a certificate that is more useful for presolving.

Fix an optimal triple
(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\Gamma},\bar{\Lambda})
and define
\bar{C}:=\overline{Q}-\mathcal{A}^{*}(\bar{\Gamma})-\mathcal{B}^{*}(\bar{\Lambda})-\bar{\alpha}E_{11}
. A natural refinement is to select the remaining dual certificate by maximizing the score separation

\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{[i]}-\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}w_{[i]}\;\middle|\;\bar{C}-\mathcal{D}^{*}(W)\succeq 0,\ W\succeq 0\right\}.

(32)

This problem is difficult to solve directly because the scores are fractional and the leading index set is unknown. To derive a tractable form, write

\mathcal{D}^{*}(W)=\begin{bmatrix}k\tau&z^{\top}\\
z&\Diag(d)\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{K}^{*},

where
\tau=W_{11}
,
z_{i}=W_{1,i+1}
, and
d_{i}=W_{i+1,i+1}
. Then
w_{i}=z_{i}^{2}/d_{i}\leq\tau
. A leading score with
0<w_{i}<\tau
can be saturated by reducing
d_{i}
to
z_{i}^{2}/\tau
, which only decreases the corresponding diagonal entry of the arrow matrix and therefore preserves feasibility. Hence, after this saturation step, the leading scores are taken at the common upper envelope
\tau
, and we remove the unknown ordering as follows:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{[i]}-\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}w_{[i]}=2\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{[i]}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=2k\tau-\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}.

Introducing variables
t_{i}
with
w_{i}\leq t_{i}\leq\tau
, and we refine the dual certificate by solving

\min\left\{-2k\tau+\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &\bar{C}-\begin{bmatrix}k\tau&z^{\top}\\
z&\Diag(d)\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\\
&\ d\geq 0,\ z_{i}^{2}\leq t_{i}d_{i},\ \tau\geq t_{i}\geq 0,\ i\in[n]\end{aligned}\right\}.

(33)

The refinement keeps the SC-SDP bound unchanged because
(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\Gamma},\bar{\Lambda})
is fixed and only the remaining dual certificate is reselected. Compared with the ordered formulation (
32
), (
33
) is tractable: the ordering is removed, and the constraints
z_{i}^{2}\leq t_{i}d_{i}
are rotated second-order cone representable. We use (
33
) later in the numerical experiments when constructing fixing certificates from an optimal dual solution.

5
Algorithm framework for solving SC-SDP

This section develops the algorithmic framework for solving the SC-SDP relaxation (
Q
). In Subsection
5.1
, we introduce the augmented Lagrangian framework. In Subsections
5.2
and
5.3
, we describe the low-rank and convex lifting phases for solving the ALM subproblems. The projection onto
\mathcal{K}
and the Moreau-based recovery of the
\mathcal{K}^{*}
multiplier update are given in Section
2
; in Subsection
5.4
, we derive the remaining projection onto

5.1
Augmented Lagrangian method

We first equivalently express (
Q
) in the following splitting form:

\min_{Y,Z}\left\{\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle+\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(Y)+\delta_{\mathcal{K}}(Z)\;\middle|\;\mathcal{B}(Y)\geq 0,\;Y-Z=0\right\},

(34)

where
\delta_{\cal S}
denotes the indicator function of the set
{\cal S}
, and the set
\mathcal{F}
is defined as

\mathcal{F}\coloneq\left\{Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}:\mathcal{A}(Y)=0,\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}\right\}.

(35)

The reduced augmented Lagrangian function of (
34
) is

\displaystyle L_{\sigma}(Y,\Lambda,W)=

\displaystyle\langle\overline{Q},Y\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|(\sigma^{-1}\Lambda-\mathcal{B}(Y))_{+}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|\Pi_{\mathcal{K}^{*}}(\sigma^{-1}W-Y)\|^{2}+\delta_{\mathcal{F}}(Y),

(36)

where
\Lambda\geq 0
and
W\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
are the Lagrange multipliers associated with the constraints
\mathcal{B}(Y)\geq 0
and
Y-Z=0
, respectively, and
\sigma>0
is the penalty parameter.
The augmented Lagrangian method for solving (
34
) is summarized in Algorithm 1.

Algorithm 1:

Choose
Y_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
,
\Lambda_{0}\geq 0
,
W_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
, and
\sigma_{0}>0
. Set
k=0
.

Step 1:

Compute
Y_{k+1}=\arg\min\{L_{\sigma_{k}}(Y,\Lambda_{k},W_{k}):Y\in\mathcal{F}\}
. (ALM-sub)

Step 2:

Update
\Lambda_{k+1}=(\Lambda_{k}-\sigma_{k}\mathcal{B}(Y_{k+1}))_{+}
.

Step 3:

Update
W_{k+1}=\Pi_{\mathcal{K}^{*}}(\sigma_{k}^{-1}W_{k}-Y_{k+1})
.

Step 4:

Choose
\sigma_{k+1}
, set
k\leftarrow k+1
, and return to Step 1.

Remark 7
.

Algorithm 1 follows a double-loop augmented Lagrangian framework. Under the standard assumptions used in inexact ALM analysis, if each ALM subproblem is solved to progressively higher accuracy so that the
accumulated inexactness error is summable,
then the outer ALM iterates generate a primal-dual sequence whose accumulation points satisfy the KKT conditions of (
Q
); see
[
17
]
for a detailed analysis of this classical inexact ALM setting.

At the subproblem level, each ALM subproblem is solved by a hybrid strategy consisting of a low-rank phase and a convex lifting phase. Although the low-rank phase is nonconvex, the convex lifting step guarantees a monotonic decrease of the augmented Lagrangian value. As shown in
[
21
,
18
]
, this mechanism ensures global convergence of the subproblem iterates to a minimizer of (ALM-sub), while automatically identifying the true optimal rank.

The next two subsections briefly describe how to implement the low-rank and convex lifting phases for solving the subproblem (ALM-sub) efficiently.

5.2
Low-rank phase

In the low-rank phase, we represent a rank-
r
solution of (ALM-sub) in the factorized form

Y=\begin{bmatrix}1&x^{\top}\\
x&X\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}e_{1}^{\top}\\
R\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}e_{1}&R^{\top}\end{bmatrix}=\widehat{R}\widehat{R}^{\top},

where
R\coloneqq[R_{1},\dots,R_{n}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
and
\widehat{R}\coloneqq[e_{1}^{\top};R]
with
e_{1}
being the first standard unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
. Thus, (ALM-sub) is equivalent to the following nonconvex problem:

\min_{R}\left\{f_{r}(R)\coloneq L_{\sigma}(\widehat{R}\widehat{R}^{\top},\Lambda,W)\;\middle|\;\widehat{R}\widehat{R}^{\top}\in\mathcal{F}\right\}.

(37)

Directly solving (
37
) is difficult because of the nonconvex constraint. However,
[
17
,
19
]
show that it can be reduced to

\min_{R}\left\{f_{r}(R)\;\middle|\;AR=be_{1}^{\top}\right\},

(38)

which is a simpler nonconvex problem with only linear constraints. We then apply a projected gradient method to solve (
38
).

5.3
Convex lifting phase

The low-rank phase serves as a local acceleration step for reducing the objective value of (ALM-sub), but it does not by itself guarantee global convergence of Algorithm 1. To ensure global convergence, we switch to a convex lifting phase after a suitable number of low-rank iterations. In this phase, we solve (ALM-sub) directly in the variable
Y
via the projected gradient step

Y^{+}=\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\left(Y-\eta\nabla L_{\sigma}(Y,\Lambda,W)\right),

(39)

where
\eta_{k}>0
is a suitable step size. As shown in
[
21
,
18
]
, this phase guarantees global convergence of the proposed method and helps identify the optimal rank, which can in turn be used to warm start the low-rank phase
(
37
). The main computational bottleneck in (
39
) is the projection onto
\mathcal{F}
. The next subsection shows that this projection can be computed efficiently through a one-dimensional convex optimization problem.

5.4
Projection onto
\mathcal{F}

The projection onto
\mathcal{K}
was developed in Section
2
, together with the Moreau-based recovery of the
\mathcal{K}^{*}
multiplier update. We next derive the remaining projection formula for the affine-semidefinite set
\mathcal{F}
, which is needed in the convex lifting phase.
Recall that the set
\mathcal{F}
defined in (
35
) can be equivalently expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}:Y_{11}=1\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{\mathcal{A}}_{+},

where
\mathbb{S}^{\mathcal{A}}_{+}\coloneqq\{Y\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}:\mathcal{A}(Y)=0\}
.
Let
P=[b^{\top};-A^{\top}]
and
J=I-P(P^{\top}P)^{-1}P^{\top}
.
By
[
19
, Lemma 2]
, we have

\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{\mathcal{A}}_{+}}(\bar{Y})=\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}}(J\bar{Y}J),\quad\forall\,\bar{Y}\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}.

(40)

Therefore, projecting onto
\mathcal{F}
reduces to a one-dimensional convex optimization problem.

Theorem 6
.

The projection of any given matrix
\bar{Y}\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
onto
\mathcal{F}
is given by

\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{Y})=\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{\mathcal{A}}_{+}}(\bar{Y}+\eta^{*}E_{11}),

where
\eta^{*}
is the unique optimal solution of the following problem:

\min_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\;\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}}(J(\bar{Y}+\eta E_{11})J)\|^{2}-\eta\right\}.

(41)

Proof.

The formula follows from the dual characterization of the projection onto
\mathcal{F}
together with (
40
).

The problem (
41
) is one-dimensional and convex, so it can be solved efficiently by standard methods such as the semismooth Newton method; this dual viewpoint is consistent with the affine-constrained proximal framework in
[
16
]
. The computation can be further accelerated by preprocessing and warm-start strategies from
[
19
]
, using the low-rank solution as an initial point.

Numerical experiments

In this section, we report numerical experiments to evaluate the efficiency and scalability of our algorithm RiNNAL-S for solving the SC-SDP relaxation. We also evaluate
the bound quality of the SC-SDP relaxation, its computational and presolving performance, and the broader applicability of the framework.
All experiments are performed using
Matlab
R2023b
on a workstation equipped with Intel Xeon E5-2680 (v3) processors
and 96GB of RAM.

Baseline Solvers
. For the SC-SDP relaxation, we compare RiNNAL-S with the commercial solver MOSEK 11.0
[
2
]
. For the SDP–RLT relaxation, we report results obtained by RiNNAL+
[
19
]
and SDPNAL+
[
33
,

]
. For the original problem (
1
), we report results from the commercial solver Gurobi 13.0.0
[
14
]
, which solves the problem directly by branch-and-bound methods. These benchmarks are used to assess both the practical performance of SC-SDP and the computational advantage of RiNNAL-S.

Stopping Conditions
. Based on the KKT conditions (
23
) for the SC-SDP relaxation (
34
), we define the following relative KKT residuals to assess the accuracy of the solution:

\operatorname{R_{p}}:=\max\left\{\frac{{\|\Pi_{-}(\mathcal{B}(Y))\|}}{1+\|d\|^{2}},\;\frac{\|Y-Z\|}{1+\|Y\|+\|Z\|}\right\},\;\;\operatorname{R_{d}}:=\frac{\|\Pi_{\mathbb{S}_{-}^{n+1}}(S)\|}{1+\|S\|},\;\;\operatorname{R_{c}}:=\frac{|\langle Y,S\rangle|}{1+\|Y\|+\|S\|}.

We omit residuals associated with the feasibility constraints

Y\in\mathcal{F}
,
Z\in\mathcal{K}
, the dual feasibility constraints
\Lambda\geq 0
,
W\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
, and the complementarity constraints,
as the first is automatically enforced by the projection step in the ALM subproblem, and the others are inherently satisfied by the construction of the ALM iteration.
For a given tolerance
\mathrm{tol}>0
, our algorithm terminates when the maximum residual satisfies
\operatorname{R_{max}}:=\max\{\operatorname{R_{p}},\operatorname{R_{d}},\operatorname{R_{c}}\}<\mathrm{tol}
or the maximum time limit
\mathrm{TimeLimit}
is reached. In our experiments, unless otherwise specified, we set
\mathrm{tol}=10^{-6}
and
\mathrm{TimeLimit}=3600\mathrm{(secs)}
for all solvers.

Implementation
.
In RiNNAL-S, we employ a projected gradient (PG) method with Barzilai-Borwein steps and non-monotone line search to solve the augmented Lagrangian subproblems. Although classical ALM theory often increases the penalty parameter monotonically to obtain fast local convergence, this rule can be overly conservative and may lead to difficult subproblems. We therefore use an adaptive update in the implementation: the penalty parameter
\sigma_{k}
is initialized at
\sigma_{0}=1
, increased by a factor of 1.5 if
\operatorname{R_{p}}/\operatorname{R_{d}}\geq 2
and decreased by a factor of 1.5 if
\operatorname{R_{p}}/\operatorname{R_{d}}\leq 1/5
. The initial rank
r_{0}
is set to be
\min\{200,\lceil n/5\rceil\}
. The stepsize
t_{k}
of the PG step is determined as
1/\sigma_{k}
. The initial point
R_{0}
is randomly selected from the feasible region. In each ALM iteration, the low-rank phase is performed with the maximum number of PG iterations capped at 50. Additionally, a PG step is executed after the low-rank phase at every 5 outer ALM iterations.

The Gurobi relative gap may be much larger than the gaps of the perspective and SC-SDP relaxations, because the MIP gap is computed after adding fixing and screening information rather than using the relaxation lower bound itself. A more specialized branch-and-bound method that incorporates the SC-SDP bound more directly could further improve practical performance, and we leave this direction for future work.

6.3

6.4
Sparse quadratic knapsack problems

We finally consider a more complicated QCQP to test SC-SDP beyond the unconstrained and simplex-constrained settings. Specifically, we study the sparse quadratic knapsack problem with multiple knapsack constraints and pairwise conflict constraints, which takes the form

\min_{x}\left\{x^{\top}Qx+2c^{\top}x\;\middle|\;Gx\leq d,\;x_{i}x_{j}=0\;\;(i,j)\in E,\;\ x\in\{0,1\}^{n},\;\|x\|_{0}\leq k\right\},

(S-QKP)

where
G\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
and
d\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
define multiple knapsack constraints, and
E
encodes pairwise conflicts. The constraint
x_{i}x_{j}=0
prevents two incompatible binary variables from being selected simultaneously.
In the lifted model, the binary constraints and pairwise conflicts are handled as ordinary affine constraints by adding
\diag(X)=x
and
X_{ij}=0
for
(i,j)\in E
, respectively.

We randomly generate the profit and weight data following the classical

[

has been widely used in the literature

[

The multiple knapsack and conflict constraints are added to test the SC-SDP
relaxation on a richer binary QCQP, in the spirit of generalized and

[

,

. Item

7
Conclusions

This paper develops a sparsity-cone semidefinite programming (SC-SDP) framework for sparse quadratic optimization. The proposed relaxation replaces the conventional lifted formulation involving both continuous and binary variables by a compact SDP in the original lifted matrix of size
n+1
, together with a single structured constraint involving the sparsity-cone
\mathcal{K}
. We prove that this compact formulation is equivalent in strength to the SDP–RLT relaxation, while substantially reducing the SDP dimension and simplifying the constraint structure. We also analyze the cones
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{K}^{*}
, derive an efficient projection operation for
\mathcal{K}
, and use the SC-SDP dual structure to obtain support-restriction, variable fixing, screening-cut, and dual-refinement certificates.

For computation, we develop RiNNAL-S, a two-phase augmented Lagrangian method that combines a low-rank phase with a convex lifting phase and exploits the structured projections associated with
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{K}
. Numerical experiments on projection subproblems and several classes of sparse quadratic programs show that SC-SDP preserves the bound quality of SDP–RLT while offering significant computational advantages, supporting effective presolving, and often producing much lower-rank solutions. Future work includes integrating these certificates more fully into branch-and-bound algorithms and further exploiting the sparsity-cone geometry for broader sparse QCQP models.

Appendix A
Support restrictions and strong branching

This section records the restricted-relaxation viewpoint mentioned in Section
4
. Let
S\subseteq[n]
be the indices fixed to
z_{i}=1
,
N\subseteq[n]
the indices fixed to
z_{i}=0
, and
R:=[n]\setminus(S\cup N)
the remaining indices. In the unconstrained case (
P
), the corresponding support-restricted subproblem is

\min_{x}\left\{x^{\top}Qx+2c^{\top}x\;\middle|\;x_{i}=0\ \forall i\in N,\;\|x_{R}\|_{0}\leq k-|S|\right\}.

The SC-SDP relaxation is obtained by applying the sparsity-cone constraint only to the active block. Writing
Y=[1,x^{\top};x,X]
, this gives

\min\left\{\langle C,Y\rangle\;\middle|\;Y_{11}=1,\;Y\succeq 0,\;X_{ii}=0\ \forall i\in N,\;\begin{bmatrix}(k-|S|)Y_{11}&Y_{1R}\\
Y_{R1}&\Diag(Y_{RR})\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0\right\}.

For problems with additional linear constraints, the same construction applies after updating the affine constraints and the active index set. Thus the cone geometry and the projection routines used by RiNNAL-S can be reused at such restricted nodes.

In the unconstrained sparse ridge regression setting, the tightness results in Proposition
4
and Theorem
3
apply directly after the linear restrictions
z_{i}=1
for
i\in S
and
z_{i}=0
for
i\in N
. Thus, with the obvious notation,
v^{\mathrm{PR}}_{S,N}\leq v^{\mathrm{SC}}_{S,N}=v^{\mathrm{OP}}_{S,N}
. If
v^{\mathrm{SC}}_{S,N}
exceeds a known upper bound, then the pattern
(S,N)
cannot occur in an optimal solution, equivalently the no-good cut
\sum_{i\in S}z_{i}+\sum_{i\in N}(1-z_{i})\leq|S|+|N|-1
is valid for all optimal solutions. Strong branching is the special case
S=\emptyset,N=\{p\}
or
S=\{p\},N=\emptyset
.
