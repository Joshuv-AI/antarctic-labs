Compatible Lie conformal bialgebras
Lamei Yuan, Jiansen Zhao

Abstract
We introduce and study compatible Lie conformal bialgebras as conformal counterparts of compatible Lie bialgebras. Such a structure consists of two compatible Lie conformal brackets and two compatible conformal cobrackets on the same ℂ [ ∂ ] \mathbb{C}[\partial] -module, and every simultaneous linear combination of them is again a Lie conformal bialgebra. We develop representations and matched pairs for compatible Lie conformal algebras, introduce compatible Lie conformal coalgebras, and establish their duality through the conformal dual in the finite case. For ℂ [ ∂ ] \mathbb{C}[\partial] -modules that are free of finite rank, we prove the equivalence among compatible Lie conformal bialgebras, standard compatible conformal Manin triples and matched pairs. In the coboundary case, we characterize the tensors r r that determine compatible Lie conformal bialgebras. The characterization requires the symmetric part of r r to be invariant with respect to both brackets and three conformal Yang–Baxter conditions to hold. The first two conditions correspond to the two brackets separately, whereas the third is the compatible conformal Yang–Baxter condition. For comparison, we introduce the compatible conformal classical Yang–Baxter equation (CYBE) and show that each of its solutions satisfies these three conditions, while the converse fails. One explicit example shows that the first two conditions do not imply the third. Another shows that all three conformal Yang-Baxter conditions may hold even though r r is not a solution of compatible conformal CYBE.

Introduction

Lie conformal algebras were introduced by Kac as an algebraic language for the singular part of the operator product expansion of chiral fields in two-dimensional conformal field theory
KAC
. They may be viewed as formal-distribution analogues of Lie algebras: the ordinary Lie bracket is replaced by a
\lambda
-bracket, and the translation operator is encoded in the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module structure. This framework has become a standard setting for the structure theory, representation theory and cohomology of conformal algebras
BKV
;
CK
;
DK
;
DK3
.

For ordinary Lie algebras, one of the basic sources of bialgebra structures is the classical Yang–Baxter equation. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra and
r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, the tensor form is

[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]=0.
Equivalently, if
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}
, this equation reads

\sum_{i,j}\Big([x_{i},x_{j}]\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}-x_{i}\otimes[x_{j},y_{i}]\otimes y_{j}-x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes[y_{j},y_{i}]\Big)=0.

Solutions give rise to triangular Lie bialgebras and are closely related to Poisson–Lie groups, quantum groups and integrable systems
Drinfeld
;
STS
;
BD
. The coboundary formula
\delta(x)=x\cdot r
is the mechanism which connects this equation with Lie bialgebras and Manin triples.

Liberati constructed the conformal counterpart of this picture by introducing Lie conformal coalgebras and Lie conformal bialgebras, together with conformal Manin triples, Drinfeld doubles and a conformal classical Yang–Baxter equation
Li
. In this setting, tensor equations cannot be copied verbatim from Lie algebra theory. The bracket carries a formal parameter, and the position of the translation operator in the tensor factors becomes part of the formula. For example, if
r=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}
in a Lie conformal algebra
\mathcal{A}
, the conformal Yang–Baxter expression is

\displaystyle[\![r,r]\!]=\sum_{i,j}\Big([a_{i}{}_{\mu}a_{j}]\otimes b_{i}\otimes b_{j}\big|_{\mu=1\otimes\partial\otimes 1}-a_{i}\otimes[a_{j}{}_{\mu}b_{i}]\otimes b_{j}\big|_{\mu=1\otimes 1\otimes\partial}-a_{i}\otimes a_{j}\otimes[b_{j}{}_{\mu}b_{i}]\big|_{\mu=1\otimes\partial\otimes 1}\Big),

and the conformal classical Yang–Baxter equation is imposed modulo the total translation operator. Further work relates this equation to conformal
\mathcal{O}
-operators, conformal
S
-equations and bialgebra structures of associative, Gel’fand–Dorfman and Poisson conformal algebras
HB1
;
HB2
;
HBG
;
HB3
;
GR1
;
GR2
;
YL
;
YLZ
.

The question considered here comes from compatibility. In many algebraic problems one studies not a single isolated operation, but a family of linear combinations of two operations. For instance, two Lie brackets are compatible if every linear combination of them is again a Lie bracket. This idea underlies compatible Poisson structures, bi-Hamiltonian systems and several deformation-theoretic constructions
B1
;
B2
;
GS1
;
GS2
;
GS3
;
LSB
. Wu and Bai developed the corresponding bialgebra theory for compatible Lie algebras and obtained a compatible form of the classical Yang–Baxter equation in the coboundary case
WB
.

It is natural to ask whether the same picture survives for Lie conformal algebras. Suppose that a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module carries two Lie conformal brackets

[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1}
,
[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2}

and two conformal cobrackets

\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
.
When does every linear combination
k_{1}[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1}+k_{2}[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2}
,
k_{1}\delta_{1}+k_{2}\delta_{2}

define a Lie conformal bialgebra? The answer is not obtained by a formal translation of the Lie algebra case. Besides the compatible Jacobi and compatible co-Jacobi identities, one has to keep track of conformal sesquilinearity, the conformal dual module and the precise placement of
\partial
in the tensor substitutions. These are exactly the points where the conformal theory differs from the ordinary Lie algebra theory.

This paper studies this compatibility problem for Lie conformal bialgebras. We first introduce compatible Lie conformal algebras, together with their representations and matched pairs, and then define compatible Lie conformal coalgebras and compatible Lie conformal bialgebras. For
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules that are free of finite rank, we prove that compatible Lie conformal bialgebras, standard compatible conformal Manin triples and matched pairs of compatible Lie conformal algebras are equivalent. This is the compatible analogue of Liberati’s correspondence between Lie conformal bialgebras and conformal Manin triples. Along the way we establish a duality between finite compatible Lie conformal algebras and compatible Lie conformal coalgebras through the conformal dual, and we show that the conformal dual of a compatible Lie conformal bialgebra of finite rank is again a compatible Lie conformal bialgebra.

The coboundary case leads to a further question. Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
be a compatible Lie conformal algebra and let
r\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}
. The same tensor
r
gives two coboundary cobrackets, one through each bracket, and we determine exactly when the resulting data form a compatible Lie conformal bialgebra: the symmetric part of
r
must be invariant for both brackets,
r
must satisfy the conformal Yang–Baxter condition for each bracket, and it must satisfy a compatible conformal Yang–Baxter condition involving both brackets (see Theorem
6.6
). We also introduce the compatible conformal CYBE, and show that every solution satisfies these three conformal Yang–Baxter conditions. Two examples delimit the theory: in one, the conformal Yang–Baxter conditions for the two brackets hold while the compatible conformal Yang–Baxter condition fails, so the compatible condition is an independent requirement; in another, built from a Schrödinger–Virasoro type compatible Lie conformal algebra, the three conformal Yang–Baxter conditions all hold although
r
is not a solution of the compatible conformal CYBE.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls Lie conformal algebras, their representations and conformal duals. Section 3 introduces compatible Lie conformal algebras, their representations and matched pairs. Section 4 treats compatible Lie conformal coalgebras and their duality with compatible Lie conformal algebras in the finite free case. Section 5 proves the equivalence among compatible Lie conformal bialgebras, standard compatible conformal Manin triples and matched pairs of compatible Lie conformal algebras. Section 6 is devoted to the coboundary construction and the compatible conformal classical Yang–Baxter equation.

Throughout the paper, all vector spaces, linear maps and tensor products are over
\mathbb{C}
. For a vector space
A
, the space of polynomials in
\lambda
with coefficients in
A
is denoted by
A[\lambda]
.

Definition 2.1

\displaystyle=-\lambda[a_{\lambda}b],\ \ [a_{\lambda}\partial b]=(\partial+\lambda)[a_{\lambda}b],

(2.3)

\displaystyle\ \mbox{skew-symmetry:}~~{[a_{\lambda}b]}

\displaystyle=-[b_{-\lambda-\partial}a],\

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{Jacobi \ identity:}~~{[a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]]}

\displaystyle=[[a_{\lambda}b]_{\lambda+\mu}c]+[b_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]].

(2.5)

Definition 2.3

Let

3
Compatible Lie conformal algebras

In this section, we introduce compatible Lie conformal algebras and their representations, extending the classical compatibility condition to the conformal setting. We then define and study matched pairs of compatible Lie conformal algebras, which serve as a key structure connecting compatible Lie conformal bialgebras and comformal Manin triples.

3.1
Definitions of compatible Lie conformal algebras and representations

By analogy with the case of Lie algebras, the following conclusions hold for Lie conformal algebras:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
be two Lie conformal algebras over the same
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{A}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

[(c)]

(i)

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1}+[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
is a Lie conformal algebra;

(ii)

For all
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,
(\mathcal{A},k_{1}[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1}+k_{2}[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
is a Lie conformal algebra;

(iii)

The compatible Jacobi identity holds for all

:

\displaystyle[x_{\lambda}[y_{\mu}z]^{1}]^{2}+[x_{\lambda}{\left[y_{\mu}z\right]}^{2}]^{1}-[{\left[x_{\lambda}y\right]}^{1}_{\lambda+\mu}z]^{2}-[y_{\mu}{[x_{\lambda}z]}^{1}]^{2}-[{\left[x_{\lambda}y\right]}^{2}_{\lambda+\mu}z]^{1}-[y_{\mu}{\left[x_{\lambda}z\right]}^{2}]^{1}=0.

(3.1)

With this we have the definition of compatible Lie conformal algebras.

Definition 3.2
.

A compatible Lie conformal algebra is a triple
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
, where
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
are Lie conformal algebras satisfying any of the three equivalent conditions in Proposition
3.1
.

If
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
is a compatible LCA, then we say
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
are compatible, and we also say
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
is the compatible LCA of
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
. Sometimes, we will simply denote the compatible LCA of
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})

y=P

The remaining four terms are

\displaystyle-[[L_{\lambda}P]^{1}{}_{\lambda+\mu}Q]^{2}-[P_{\mu}[L_{\lambda}Q]^{1}]^{2}-[[L_{\lambda}P]^{2}{}_{\lambda+\mu}Q]^{1}-[P_{\mu}[L_{\lambda}Q]^{2}]^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\lambda+\mu\right)M_{2}-\left(\partial+\mu+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\right)M_{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\lambda+\mu\right)M_{1}-\left(\partial+\mu+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\right)M_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial M_{1}-\partial M_{2}.

Hence the sum of the six terms in (
3.1
) is zero. Therefore

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
is a compatible Lie conformal algebra.

Example 3.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a Lie conformal algebra, and
T\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\})
is a Lie conformal algebra by the following
\lambda
-bracket (cf. Proposition
2.8
)

\displaystyle[x_{\lambda}y]^{T}=[Tx_{\lambda}y]+[x_{\lambda}Ty]+\alpha[x_{\lambda}y],~~\forall~x,y\in\mathcal{A}.

However,
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
and
(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\})
need not be compatible. For example, let

\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{Cur}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
with basis
\{e,h,f\}
and nonzero brackets

[h_{\lambda}e]=2e,\hskip 20.00003pt[h_{\lambda}f]=-2f,\hskip 20.00003pt[e_{\lambda}f]=h.

Let
T(e)=e
and
T(h)=T(f)=0
, extended
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-linearly. Since

\mathbb{C}e
and
\mathbb{C}h\oplus\mathbb{C}f
are Lie subalgebras of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
,
T
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1
. The descendant bracket is determined by

[h_{\lambda}f]^{T}=2f,\hskip 20.00003pt[h_{\lambda}e]^{T}=[e_{\lambda}f]^{T}=0.

If the two brackets were compatible, then their sum would define a Lie conformal algebra. But for

[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{\prime}=[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]+[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{T}
, one has

[h_{\lambda}[e_{\mu}f]^{\prime}]^{\prime}-[[h_{\lambda}e]^{\prime}_{\lambda+\mu}f]^{\prime}-[e_{\mu}[h_{\lambda}f]^{\prime}]^{\prime}=[h_{\lambda}h]^{\prime}-[2e_{\lambda+\mu}f]^{\prime}-0=-2h\neq 0.

Hence the Jacobi identity fails, and the original bracket and the descendant bracket are not compatible in this example.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
M
be a finite
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module. Let
(M,\rho,\sigma)
be a representation of a compatible LCA
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})

(M^{*c},\sigma^{*})

(M,\rho,\sigma)
. In particular,
(\mathcal{A}^{*c},{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1},{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{2})
is a dual representation of the adjoint representation
(\mathcal{A},{\rm{ad}}_{1},{\rm{ad}}_{2})
of
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
, where the adjoint actions are defined by
{\rm{ad}}_{1}(a)_{\lambda}b=[a_{\lambda}b]^{1}
, and
{\rm{ad}}_{2}(a)_{\lambda}b=[a_{\lambda}b]^{2}
, for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Assume that
(M,\rho,\sigma)
is a representation of the compatible Lie conformal algebra

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
. By Definition
3.7
this means that

(M,\rho)
is a representation of
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1})
,

(M,\sigma)
is a representation of
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
, and

(M,\rho+\sigma)
is a representation of
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1}+[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
.
Dual representation theory for Lie conformal algebras immediately yields that

(M^{*c},\rho^{*})
and
(M^{*c},\sigma^{*})
are representations of

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
respectively.

It remains to verify that
(M^{*c},\rho^{*}+\sigma^{*})
is a representation of

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1}+[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
. We first show that duality is linear:

(\rho+\sigma)^{*}=\rho^{*}+\sigma^{*}

\displaystyle=-\varphi_{\mu-\lambda}\big(\rho(a)_{\lambda}v+\sigma(a)_{\lambda}v\big)

is a representation of the compatible

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})

Finally, taking the adjoint representation

on

(\mathcal{A}^{*c},{\rm{ad}}_{1}^{*},{\rm{ad}}_{2}^{*})

4
Compatible Lie conformal coalgebras

In this section, we define compatible Lie conformal coalgebras and provide constructions of compatible Lie conformal algebras from finite compatible Lie conformal coalgebras and vice versa. These results extend the duality theory of Lie conformal algebras to the compatible case.

Definition 4.1
.

(
Li
)
A Lie conformal coalgebra
\mathcal{A}
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module endowed with a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-homomorphism

\delta:\mathcal{A}\to\wedge^{2}\mathcal{A}

such that

\displaystyle({\rm id}\otimes\delta)\delta-\tau_{12}({\rm id}\otimes\delta)\delta=(\delta\otimes{\rm id})\delta,

(4.1)

where
\tau_{12}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=b\otimes a\otimes c
, for
a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 4.2
.

(
Li
)
A Lie conformal bialgebra is a triple
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\delta)
, where
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a Lie conformal algebra,
(\mathcal{A},\delta)
is a Lie conformal coalgebra and they satisfy the cocycle condition:

\displaystyle\delta([x_{\lambda}y])=\left({\rm{ad}}(x)_{\lambda}\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes{\rm{ad}}(x)_{\lambda}\right)\delta(y)-\left({\rm{ad}}(y)_{-\lambda-\partial}\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes{\rm{ad}}(y)_{-\lambda-\partial}\right)\delta(x),\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{A}.

(4.2)

Proposition 4.3
.

(
Li
)
(a) Let
(\mathcal{A},\delta)
be a finite Lie conformal coalgebra, then
\mathcal{A}^{*c}=\mathrm{Chom}(\mathcal{A},\mathbb{C})
is a Lie conformal algebra with the following
\lambda
-bracket
(f,g\in\mathcal{A}^{*c})
:

\displaystyle([f_{\mu}g])_{\lambda}(r)=\sum f_{\mu}(r_{(1)})g_{\lambda-\mu}(r_{(2)})=(f\otimes g)_{\mu,\lambda-\mu}(\delta(r)),

(4.3)

where
\delta(r)=\sum r_{(1)}\otimes r_{(2)}
.

(b) Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a Lie conformal algebra free of finite rank, that is
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}[\partial]a^{i}
, then
\mathcal{A}^{*c}=\mathrm{Chom}(\mathcal{A},\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}[\partial]a_{i}
, where
\{a_{i}\}
is a dual
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-basis in the sense that
(a_{i})_{\lambda}(a^{j})=\delta_{ij}
, is a Lie conformal coalgebra with the following co-bracket:

\displaystyle\delta(f)=\sum_{i,j}f_{\mu}([a^{i}_{\lambda}a^{j}])(a_{i}\otimes a_{j})|_{\lambda=\partial\otimes 1,\mu=-\partial\otimes 1-1\otimes\partial}.

(4.4)

More precisely, if

[a^{i}_{\lambda}a^{j}]=\sum_{k}P_{k}^{ij}(\lambda,\partial)a^{k},

where
P_{k}^{ij}
are some polynomials in
\lambda
and
\partial
, then the co-bracket is

\delta(a_{k})=\sum_{i,j}Q_{k}^{ij}(\partial\otimes 1,1\otimes\partial)a_{i}\otimes a_{j},

where
Q_{k}^{ij}(x,y)=P_{k}^{ij}(x,-x-y)
.

We give the definition of compatible Lie conformal coalgebras.

Definition 4.4
.

Two Lie conformal coalgebras
(\mathcal{A},\delta_{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},\delta_{2})
over the same
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{A}
are called compatible if for all
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,
k_{1}\delta_{1}+k_{2}\delta_{2}
defines a Lie conformal coalgebra structure on
\mathcal{A}
.

If
(\mathcal{A},\delta_{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},\delta_{2})
are compatible Lie conformal coalgebras, then we denote it by
(\mathcal{A},\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
.

Remark 4.5
.

The condition in the above definition is equivalent to the following compatible co-bracket identity

\displaystyle({\rm id}\otimes\delta_{1})\delta_{2}-\tau_{12}({\rm id}\otimes\delta_{1})\delta_{2}-(\delta_{1}\otimes{\rm id})\delta_{2}+({\rm id}\otimes\delta_{2})\delta_{1}-\tau_{12}({\rm id}\otimes\delta_{2})\delta_{1}-(\delta_{2}\otimes{\rm id})\delta_{1}=0.

(4.5)

Example 4.6
.

\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module in Example
3.4

Proposition 4.7

5
Compatible Lie conformal bialgebras and Manin triples

In this section, we introduce compatible conformal Manin triples and compatible Lie conformal bialgebras. The main result proves the equivalence among compatible Lie conformal bialgebras, compatible conformal Manin triples, and matched pairs of compatible Lie conformal algebras for
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules that are free of finite rank.

Definition 5.1
.

(
BKL
)
Let
V
be a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module. A conformal bilinear form on
V
is a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\lambda}
:
V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
such that

\displaystyle\langle\partial v,w\rangle_{\lambda}=-\lambda\langle v,w\rangle_{\lambda}=-\langle v,\partial w\rangle_{\lambda},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall v,w\in V.

(5.1)

The conformal bilinear form is symmetric if
\langle v,w\rangle_{\lambda}=\langle w,v\rangle_{-\lambda}
for all
v,w\in V
.
The conformal bilinear form in a Lie conformal algebra
R
is called invariant if

\displaystyle\langle[a_{\mu}b],c\rangle_{\lambda}=\langle a,[b_{\lambda-\partial}c]\rangle_{\mu}=-\langle a,[c_{-\lambda}b]\rangle_{\mu},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall a,b,c\in R.

(5.2)

The conformal bilinear form is non-degenerate if
\langle v,w\rangle_{\lambda}=0
for all
w\in V
, implies
v=0
.

Definition 5.2
.

(
Li
)
A finite conformal Manin triple is a triple of finite Lie conformal algebras
(R,R_{1},R_{0})
, where
R

is equipped with a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\lambda}

such that

1.
R_{1}
,
R_{0}
are Lie conformal subalgebras of
R
and
R=R_{0}\oplus R_{1}
as
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module.

2.
R_{0}
and
R_{1}
are isotropic with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\lambda}
, that is
\langle R_{i},R_{i}\rangle_{\lambda}=0
for
i=0,1
.

Definition 5.3
.

A subalgebra of a compatible Lie conformal algebra
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-submodule of
\mathcal{A}
which is closed for both
\lambda
-brackets.

In what follows, we will write
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
to denote a compatible Lie conformal algebra
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
for simplicity.

Definition 5.4
.

A finite compatible conformal Manin triple is a triple of finite compatible Lie conformal algebras
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{0}})
, where
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric conformal bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\lambda}
which is invariant with respect to both compatible
\lambda
-brackets, namely, for
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle\langle[a_{\mu}b]^{i},c\rangle_{\lambda}=\langle a,[b_{\lambda-\partial}c]^{i}\rangle_{\mu}=-\langle a,[c_{-\lambda}b]^{i}\rangle_{\mu},\hskip 10.00002pta,b,c\in\mathcal{A}.

Equivalently, by linearity, the form is invariant for every linear combination
k_{1}[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1}+k_{2}[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2}
. Moreover,

1.
\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{0}}
are compatible Lie conformal subalgebras of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{0}}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
as
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module.

2.
\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{0}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
are isotropic with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\lambda}
, that is
\langle\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{i}},\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{i}}\rangle_{\lambda}=0
for
i=0,1
.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
be a compatible Lie conformal algebra whose underlying
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module is free of finite rank, and suppose that its conformal dual
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
carries a compatible Lie conformal algebra structure
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}
. If there is a compatible Lie conformal algebra structure on the direct sum of

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}
such that
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c})
is a compatible conformal Manin triple with the non-degenerate symmetric conformal bilinear form on
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}
defined by

\displaystyle\langle a+f,b+g\rangle_{\lambda}=f_{\lambda}(b)+g_{-\lambda}(a)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall~a,b\in\mathcal{A},\ f,g\in\mathcal{A}^{*c},

(5.3)

then
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c})
is called a standard compatible conformal Manin triple.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
be a compatible Lie conformal algebra whose underlying
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module is free of finite rank, and suppose that its conformal dual carries a compatible Lie conformal algebra structure
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}=(\mathcal{A}^{*c},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}^{1},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}^{2})
. From now until the end of this section, we will use
{\rm{ad}}
(resp.
\mathfrak{ad}
) to denote the adjoint representations of
\mathcal{A}
(resp.
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
), and use
{\rm{ad}}^{*}
(resp.
\mathfrak{ad}^{*}
) to denote the coadjoint actions of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
(resp.
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
on
\mathcal{A}
).

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
be a compatible Lie conformal algebra whose underlying
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module is free of finite rank, and suppose that its conformal dual carries a compatible Lie conformal algebra structure
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}=(\mathcal{A}^{*c},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}^{1},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}^{2})
.
Then
(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c})
forms a standard compatible conformal Manin triple if and only if the 6-tuple

(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*c};{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1},{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{2},\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1},\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{2})
is a matched pair of compatible Lie conformal algebras, where

{\rm{ad}}_{i}(x)_{\lambda}y=[x_{\lambda}y]^{i},x,y\in\mathcal{A};~~\mathfrak{ad}_{i}(f)_{\lambda}g=\{f_{\lambda}g\}^{i},f,g\in\mathcal{A}^{*c},~~i=1,2.

Proof.

If
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*c};{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1},{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{2},\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1},\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{2})
is a matched pair of compatible Lie conformal algebras, then
there exists a compatible Lie conformal algebra
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\bowtie_{\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1},\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{2}}^{{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1},{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}
given by (cf. Theorem
3.11
)

\displaystyle[\!\![(a+f)_{\lambda}(b+g)]\!\!]^{1}=[a_{\lambda}b]^{1}+\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(f)_{\lambda}b-\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(g)_{-\lambda-\partial}a+\{f_{\lambda}g\}^{1}+{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(a)_{\lambda}g-{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}f,

(5.4)

\displaystyle[\!\![(a+f)_{\lambda}(b+g)]\!\!]^{2}=[a_{\lambda}b]^{2}+\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{2}(f)_{\lambda}b-\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{2}(g)_{-\lambda-\partial}a+\{f_{\lambda}g\}^{2}+{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{2}(a)_{\lambda}g-{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{2}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}f,

(5.5)

where
f,g\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}
and
a,b\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
.
The bilinear form given by Eq.(
5.3
) is symmetric because

\langle a+f,b+g\rangle_{\lambda}=f_{\lambda}(b)+g_{-\lambda}(a)=\langle b+g,a+f\rangle_{-\lambda}.

It is non-degenerate since, if
\langle a+f,b+g\rangle_{\lambda}=0
for all
b+g
, then taking

g=0
gives
f_{\lambda}(b)=0
for all
b\in\mathcal{A}
, hence
f=0
, and taking
b=0
gives

g_{-\lambda}(a)=0
for all
g\in\mathcal{A}^{*c}
, hence
a=0
by conformal duality in the finite free case. It remains to prove (
5.2
).
For all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}
and
f,g,h\in\mathcal{A}^{*c}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle[\!\![(a+f)_{\lambda}(b+g)]\!\!]^{1},c+h\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\langle[a_{\lambda}b]^{1}+\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(f)_{\lambda}b-\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(g)_{-\lambda-\partial}a+\{f_{\lambda}g\}^{1}+{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(a)_{\lambda}g-{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}f,c+h\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\{f_{\lambda}g\}^{1}_{\mu}c+({\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(a)_{\lambda}g)_{\mu}c-({\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}f)_{\mu}c+h_{-\mu}[a_{\lambda}b]^{1}+h_{-\mu}(\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(f)_{\lambda}b)-h_{-\mu}(\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(g)_{-\lambda-\partial}a)

\displaystyle=\{f_{\lambda}g\}^{1}_{\mu}c+g_{\mu-\lambda}[c_{-\lambda-\partial}a]^{1}+f_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]^{1}+h_{-\mu}[a_{\lambda}b]^{1}-\{f_{\lambda}h\}^{1}_{-\mu+\lambda}b+\{g_{\mu-\lambda}h\}^{1}_{-\lambda}a

\displaystyle=-\{g_{-\lambda-\partial}f\}^{1}_{\mu}c+g_{\mu-\lambda}[c_{-\mu}a]^{1}+f_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]^{1}-h_{-\mu}[b_{-\lambda-\partial}a]^{1}+\{h_{-\lambda-\partial}f\}^{1}_{-\mu+\lambda}b+\{g_{\mu-\lambda}h\}^{1}_{-\lambda}a

\displaystyle=-\{g_{\mu-\lambda}f\}^{1}_{\mu}c+g_{\mu-\lambda}[c_{-\mu}a]^{1}+f_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]^{1}-h_{-\mu}[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]^{1}+\{h_{-\mu}f\}^{1}_{-\mu+\lambda}b+\{g_{\mu-\lambda}h\}^{1}_{-\lambda}a,

and

\displaystyle\langle a+f,[\!\![(b+g)_{\mu-\partial}(c+h)]\!\!]^{1}\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\langle a+f,[b_{\mu-\partial}c]^{1}+\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(g)_{\mu-\partial}c-\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(h)_{-\mu}b+\{g_{\mu-\partial}h\}^{1}+{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(b)_{\mu-\partial}h-{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(c)_{-\mu}g\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=f_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\partial}c]^{1}+f_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(g)_{\mu-\partial}c)-f_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1}(h)_{-\mu}b)+\{g_{\mu-\partial}h\}^{1}_{\lambda}a+({\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(b)_{\mu-\partial}h)_{\lambda}a-({\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(c)_{-\mu}g)_{\lambda}a

\displaystyle=-\{g_{\mu-\lambda}f\}^{1}_{\mu}c+g_{\mu-\lambda}[c_{-\mu}a]^{1}+f_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]^{1}-h_{-\mu}[b_{\mu-\lambda}a]^{1}+\{h_{-\mu}f\}^{1}_{-\mu+\lambda}b+\{g_{\mu-\lambda}h\}^{1}_{-\lambda}a.

Hence
\langle[\!\![(a+f)_{\lambda}(b+g)]\!\!]^{1},c+h\rangle_{\mu}=\langle a+f,[\!\![(b+g)_{\mu-\partial}(c+h)]\!\!]^{1}\rangle_{\lambda}
. Similarly, we have
\langle[\!\![(a+f)_{\lambda}(b+g)]\!\!]^{2},c+h\rangle_{\mu}=\langle a+f,[\!\![(b+g)_{\mu-\partial}(c+h)]\!\!]^{2}\rangle_{\lambda}
.

Now we prove the converse part. Suppose that

(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c})

is a standard compatible conformal Manin triple. Denote by

\pi_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\pi_{\mathcal{A}^{*c}}
the projections from

\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{*c}
onto
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
, respectively. Since

\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
are compatible Lie conformal subalgebras and

\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{*c}
is their direct sum as a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module, we define four conformal linear maps by

\rho_{i}^{*}(a)_{\lambda}f=\pi_{\mathcal{A}^{*c}}\big([\!\![a_{\lambda}f]\!\!]^{i}\big),\hskip 20.00003pt\rho_{i}(f)_{\lambda}a=\pi_{\mathcal{A}}\big([\!\![f_{\lambda}a]\!\!]^{i}\big),\hskip 20.00003pti=1,2.

Thus, by skew-symmetry, for
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
f,g\in\mathcal{A}^{*c}
,

\displaystyle[\!\![(a+f)_{\lambda}(b+g)]\!\!]^{i}=[a_{\lambda}b]^{i}+\{f_{\lambda}g\}^{i}+\rho_{i}(f)_{\lambda}b-\rho_{i}(g)_{-\lambda-\partial}a+\rho_{i}^{*}(a)_{\lambda}g-\rho_{i}^{*}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}f,\hskip 20.00003pti=1,2.

Since
\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{*c}
is a compatible Lie conformal algebra, by Theorem

3.11
,

(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*c};\rho_{1}^{*},\rho_{2}^{*},\rho_{1},\rho_{2})

is a matched pair of compatible Lie conformal algebras. It remains to
identify these four actions with the coadjoint actions.

For
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
,
f\in\mathcal{A}^{*c}
and
i=1,2
, using the invariance of the
standard bilinear form and the isotropy of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{i}^{*}(a)_{\lambda}f,b\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\langle[\!\![a_{\lambda}f]\!\!]^{i},b\rangle_{\mu}=\langle a,[\!\![f_{\mu-\partial}b]\!\!]^{i}\rangle_{\lambda}=\langle f,[b_{-\lambda-\partial}a]^{i}\rangle_{\mu-\lambda}=f_{\mu-\lambda}([b_{-\mu}a]^{i})

\displaystyle=-f_{\mu-\lambda}([a_{\mu-\partial}b]^{i})=({\rm{ad}}_{i}^{*}(a)_{\lambda}f)_{\mu}b=\langle{\rm{ad}}_{i}^{*}(a)_{\lambda}f,b\rangle_{\mu}.

Hence, by the non-degeneracy of (
5.3
), it follows that

\rho_{i}^{*}={\rm{ad}}_{i}^{*}
,
i=1,2
.
For the action of
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
on
\mathcal{A}
, the same invariance identity gives a second explicit computation.
For
f,g\in\mathcal{A}^{*c}
,
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{i}(f)_{\lambda}a,g\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\langle[\!\![f_{\lambda}a]\!\!]^{i},g\rangle_{\mu}=\langle f,[\!\![a_{\mu-\partial}g]\!\!]^{i}\rangle_{\lambda}=\langle a,\{g_{-\lambda-\partial}f\}^{i}\rangle_{\mu-\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\langle a,\{f_{\mu-\partial}g\}^{i}\rangle_{\mu-\lambda}=(\mathfrak{ad}_{i}^{*}(f)_{\lambda}a)_{\mu}g=\langle\mathfrak{ad}_{i}^{*}(f)_{\lambda}a,g\rangle_{\mu}.

Again by non-degeneracy,

\rho_{i}=\mathfrak{ad}_{i}^{*}
,
i=1,2
.

Therefore

(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*c};{\rm{ad}}_{1}^{*},{\rm{ad}}_{2}^{*},\mathfrak{ad}_{1}^{*},\mathfrak{ad}_{2}^{*})

is a matched pair of compatible Lie conformal algebras. This proves the
converse implication.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a Lie conformal algebra whose underlying
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module is free of finite rank, and suppose that its conformal dual carries a Lie conformal algebra structure
(\mathcal{A}^{*c},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\})
. Then
(\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{*c},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*c})
forms a standard conformal Manin triple if and only if
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*c};{\rm{ad}}^{*},\mathfrak{ad}^{*})
is a matched pair of LCAs.

Proof.

It is a special case of Theorem
5.6
.
∎

We combine the corollary above with
(
Li
, Theorem 3.9)
into a single theorem, and then proceed to prove it in the case of compatible Lie conformal algebras.

Theorem 5.8
.

(
Li
)
Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
be a Lie conformal algebra whose underlying
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module is free of finite rank. Suppose that its conformal dual carries a Lie conformal algebra structure
(\mathcal{A}^{*c},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\})
. Denote by
\delta
the Lie conformal coalgebra structure induced on
\mathcal{A}
by Proposition
4.3
(cf. Remark
4.9
).
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

[(c)]

(a)

(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*c};{\rm{ad}}^{*},\mathfrak{ad}^{*})
is a matched pair of Lie conformal algebras, where
{\rm{ad}}
and
\mathfrak{ad}
are the adjoint representations of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
, respectively;

(b)

(\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{*c},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*c})
is a standard Manin triple of Lie conformal algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
;

(c)

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\delta)
is a Lie conformal bialgebra.

Now we define compatible Lie conformal bialgebras.

Definition 5.9
.

Two Lie conformal bialgebras
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},\delta_{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},\delta_{2})

are called compatible, if

[(3)]

(i)

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
is a compatible Lie conformal algebra,

(ii)

(\mathcal{A},\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
is a compatible Lie conformal coalgebra,

(iii)

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1}+[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})
is a Lie conformal bialgebra.

If
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},\delta_{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},\delta_{2})
are compatible Lie conformal bialgebras, then we denote it by the five-tuple
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
.

Remark 5.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},\delta_{1})
and
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},\delta_{2})
be two Lie conformal bialgebras. The following statements are equivalent:

[(iii)]

(i)

(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
is a compatible Lie conformal bialgebra.

(ii)

The compatible Jacobi identity (
3.1
), the compatible co-Jacobi identity (
4.5
), and the following compatible cocycle identity hold for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
:

\displaystyle\delta_{1}([x_{\lambda}y]^{2})+\delta_{2}([x_{\lambda}y]^{1})

\displaystyle=({\rm{ad}}_{2}(x)_{\lambda}\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes{\rm{ad}}_{2}(x)_{\lambda})\delta_{1}(y)-({\rm{ad}}_{2}(y)_{-\lambda-\partial}\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes{\rm{ad}}_{2}(y)_{-\lambda-\partial})\delta_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+({\rm{ad}}_{1}(x)_{\lambda}\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes{\rm{ad}}_{1}(x)_{\lambda})\delta_{2}(y)-({\rm{ad}}_{1}(y)_{-\lambda-\partial}\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes{\rm{ad}}_{1}(y)_{-\lambda-\partial})\delta_{2}(x).

(5.6)

(iii)

For all
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,

\big(\mathcal{A},k_{1}[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1}+k_{2}[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},k_{1}\delta_{1}+k_{2}\delta_{2}\big)

is a Lie conformal bialgebra.

Example 5.11
.

Keep the notation in Examples
3.4
and
4.6
. We show that

(R,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},\delta_{1},\delta_{2})

is a compatible Lie conformal bialgebra.

By
(
Li
, Example 2.18)
, for the rank two solvable Lie conformal algebra
R_{p}
with

[a_{\lambda}b]=p(\lambda)b
and the cobracket

\delta_{q}(a)=0
,
\delta_{q}(b)=q(\partial)(a\wedge b)
, the triple

(R_{p},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot],\delta_{q})
is a Lie conformal bialgebra if and only if

p(x)q(x)
is an odd polynomial. In the first structure we have

p_{1}(x)=1,\hskip 20.00003ptq_{1}(x)=x,

and hence
p_{1}(x)q_{1}(x)=x
. In the second structure we have

p_{2}(x)=x^{2},\hskip 20.00003ptq_{2}(x)=x^{3},

and hence
p_{2}(x)q_{2}(x)=x^{5}
. Therefore

(R,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},\delta_{1})
and
(R,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},\delta_{2})
are Lie conformal bialgebras.

We now check the three conditions in Definition
5.9
. First,

(R,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
is a compatible Lie conformal algebra by Example
3.4
. Second,

(R,\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
is a compatible Lie conformal coalgebra by Example
4.6
. Finally, the sum bracket and the sum cobracket correspond to

p(x)=1+x^{2},\hskip 20.00003ptq(x)=x+x^{3}.

The product is

p(x)q(x)=(1+x^{2})(x+x^{3})=x+2x^{3}+x^{5},

which is an odd polynomial. Thus the sum bracket together with the sum cobracket gives a
Lie conformal bialgebra. Hence

(R,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2},\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
is a compatible Lie conformal bialgebra.

Example
5.11
gives a compatible Lie conformal bialgebra obtained from the general bialgebra criterion, rather than from the coboundary construction considered in Section 6.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{1},[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{2})
be a compatible Lie conformal algebra whose underlying
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module is free of finite rank.
Suppose that its conformal dual carries a compatible Lie conformal algebra structure
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}=(\mathcal{A}^{*c},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}^{1},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}^{2})
.
Let
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
be the compatible Lie conformal coalgebra structure induced on
\mathcal{A}\simeq\mathcal{A}^{**c}
by Proposition
4.8
.
Then the compatible Jacobi identity for

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\bowtie_{\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1},\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{2}}^{{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1},{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}

(cf. Theorem
3.11
)
is equivalent to the compatible cocycle identity (
(ii)
).

Proof.

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-basis of
\mathcal{A}
and
\{e_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-basis of
\mathcal{A}^{*c}
. We suppose

\displaystyle[{e_{i}}_{\lambda}e_{j}]^{1}=\sum_{s}A_{ij}^{s}(\lambda,\partial)e_{s},\hskip 10.00002pt\{{e_{i}^{*}}_{\lambda}e_{j}^{*}\}^{1}=\sum_{s}B_{s}^{ij}(\lambda,\partial)e_{s}^{*},

\displaystyle[{e_{i}}_{\lambda}e_{j}]^{2}=\sum_{s}\tilde{A}_{ij}^{s}(\lambda,\partial)e_{s},\hskip 10.00002pt\{{e_{i}^{*}}_{\lambda}e_{j}^{*}\}^{2}=\sum_{s}\tilde{B}_{s}^{ij}(\lambda,\partial)e_{s}^{*}.

Since

\displaystyle({\rm{ad}}_{1}^{*}(e_{j})_{\lambda}{e_{i}^{*}})_{\mu}e_{k}=-{e_{i}^{*}}_{\mu-\lambda}({\rm{ad}}_{1}(e_{j})_{\lambda}e_{k})=-{e_{i}^{*}}_{\mu-\lambda}\Big(\sum_{s}A_{jk}^{s}(\lambda,\partial)e_{s}\Big)=-A_{jk}^{i}(\lambda,\mu-\lambda),

we have
{\rm{ad}}_{1}^{*}(e_{j})_{\lambda}e_{i}^{*}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{jk}^{i}(\lambda,-\lambda-\partial)e_{k}^{*}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{ad}_{1}^{*}(e_{i}^{*})_{\lambda}(e_{j})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}B_{j}^{ik}(\lambda,-\lambda-\partial)e_{k}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{j}^{ik}(\lambda,\partial)e_{k};

\displaystyle{\rm{ad}}_{2}^{*}(e_{j})_{\lambda}e_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{A}_{jk}^{i}(\lambda,-\lambda-\partial)e_{k}^{*};

\displaystyle\mathfrak{ad}_{2}^{*}(e_{i}^{*})_{\lambda}(e_{j})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{B}_{j}^{ik}(\lambda,-\lambda-\partial)e_{k}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{C}_{j}^{ik}(\lambda,\partial)e_{k},

where
C(\lambda,\partial)=B(\lambda,-\lambda-\partial)
and
\tilde{C}(\lambda,\partial)=\tilde{B}(\lambda,-\lambda-\partial)
.

For
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\bowtie_{\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{1},\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{2}}^{{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1},{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{*c}
, the compatible

\lambda
-brackets are

\displaystyle[\!\![{e_{i}^{*}}_{\lambda}e_{j}]\!\!]^{1}=\mathfrak{ad}_{1}^{*}(e_{i}^{*})_{\lambda}e_{j}-{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{1}(e_{j})_{-\lambda-\partial}e_{i}^{*},\hskip 10.00002pt[\!\![{e_{i}^{*}}_{\lambda}e_{j}]\!\!]^{2}=\mathfrak{ad}_{2}^{*}(e_{i}^{*})_{\lambda}e_{j}-{\rm{ad}}^{*}_{2}(e_{j})_{-\lambda-\partial}e_{i}^{*}.

Therefore, we get

Theorem 6.6

\displaystyle\delta_{2}(a)

[(ii)]

(6.18)

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced compatible Lie conformal bialgebras and developed their basic
structural theory. We first studied compatible Lie conformal algebras, their representations and
matched pairs, and then established the corresponding duality between finite compatible Lie
conformal algebras and compatible Lie conformal coalgebras. Based on these preparations, we proved
that compatible Lie conformal bialgebras, standard compatible conformal Manin triples and matched pairs of
compatible Lie conformal algebras are equivalent in the finite-rank case. For the coboundary case, we characterized the tensors
r
that define compatible Lie conformal bialgebras by the invariance of
r+r^{21}
with respect to both brackets and three conformal Yang–Baxter conditions. We introduced the compatible conformal CYBE for comparison and proved that each of its solutions satisfies these three conditions. The converse does not hold. The examples show that the first two conditions do not imply the third and that all three may hold even when
r
is not a solution of the compatible conformal CYBE; in the latter case, the third condition holds by cancellation of nonzero terms.

These results extend the classical bialgebra–Manin triple–Yang–Baxter correspondence to the
compatible conformal setting. They also provide an algebraic framework for constructing new Lie
conformal bialgebra structures from compatible brackets, compatible cobrackets and
r
-matrices.
Further directions include the classification of finite examples, the study of explicit solutions
of the compatible conformal Yang–Baxter equation and the investigation of connections with
deformation theory, vertex algebra theory and integrable systems.
