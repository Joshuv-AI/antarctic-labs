Sharp Nordhaus-Gaddum bounds for throttling
Ryan Blair, Gabriel Elvin, Veronika Furst, Leslie Hogben, Tony W. H. Wong

Abstract
Throttling is a graph optimization problem, where the throttling number of a graph is the minimum sum or minimum product of the number of vertices in an initial set and the time required to complete a certain graph operation. A Nordhaus-Gaddum bound refers to an upper or lower bound of the sum or product of a graph parameter together with that of its complement. In this paper, we study the Nordhaus-Gaddum sum and product bounds of the various throttling numbers (sum throttling and product throttling with or without initial cost). Graph operations considered are standard zero forcing, positive semidefinite forcing, power domination, and Cops and Robbers.



Keywords
throttling, Nordhaus-Gaddum, propagation time, zero forcing, positive semidefinite forcing, power domination, Cops and Robbers, graph searching

AMS subject classification
05C69, 05C50, 68R10

Introduction

A
Nordhaus-Gaddum (NG) bound
is an upper or lower bound on the sum or product of the values of a parameter on a graph and its complement. Aouchiche and Hansen provide an extensive survey of NG bounds in
[
4
]
. By simultaneously limiting the possible values of a graph invariant of
G
and of its complement
\overline{G}
, Nordhaus-Gaddum bounds provide useful structural information about a graph, given such information about its complement. In this paper, we focus on the graph parameters known as throttling numbers.

Throttling in graphs belongs in the intersection of graph theory and combinatorial optimization. It involves the analysis of the efficiency of a task by minimizing the sum or the product of a measure of the resources used and the time needed to accomplish the task. We consider two metrics: in sum throttling, the total we aim to minimize is the sum of the amount of resources used and the time needed, while product throttling aims to minimize their product. Small differences in the initial set of resources or in the propagation time can have a much larger overall impact on product throttling than they would on sum throttling. Product throttling is separated into two variants, one in which initial cost is taken into account and one in which it is not but at least one time step is required; both stipulations have the effect of ruling out the trivial situation in which the product throttling number of a graph equals
0
.

In this paper, we calculate NG sum and product bounds for sum and both types of product throttling for the following four graph parameters: the zero forcing number
\operatorname{Z}(G)
, the positive semindefinite zero forcing number
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)
, the power domination number
\gamma_{P}(G)
, and the cop number
c(G)
of a graph
G
. Tables
1.1
and
1.2
summarize the NG sum and NG product bounds, respectively. Over all graphs, the NG sum bounds for
\operatorname{Z}
were known as was the NG sum upper bound for
\operatorname{Z}_{+}
; otherwise the results are new. We provide all necessary definitions and some background in the remainder of this section.

Section
2
contains our results for standard zero forcing. We construct several novel examples (e.g., Example
2.9
) used to prove that the new NG bounds we present (e.g., Theorems
2.11
and
2.12
) are sharp, almost sharp, or tight, even in the subproblem that restricts to graphs for which both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected. In Section
3
we turn our attention to positive semidefinite zero forcing, improving known bounds and establishing new ones. The key findings in this section are Corollaries
3.4
for arbitrary graphs and
3.8
when both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected, with sharpness/tightness provided by several examples. Finally, since both power domination and Cops and Robbers are closely related to graph domination, we consider both of these processes together in Section

A graph is simple, undirected, finite, and has a nonempty vertex set. For a graph

Given

, the

of

The

.
A vertex

\deg_{G}(\ell)=1
. A vertex

. An

of

A

in a graph

A graph

The

Let

1.2
Parameters

In this section we define the parameters for which we will discuss Nordhaus-Gaddum problems.
Standard zero forcing, positive semidefinite (PSD) zero forcing, and power domination are propagation processes on a graph, where the goal is to fill all the vertices (starting with each vertex filled or unfilled); unfilled vertices are filled by applying a color change rule.
1
1

1

Originally colors were used for the vertices, leading to the name ‘color change rule’, but filled/unfilled has become standard due to its suitability for all media.

Standard and PSD zero forcing originated in combinatorial matrix theory, providing upper bounds on the nullities of certain symmetric matrices whose off-diagonal pattern of nonzero entries is described by the given graph;
standard zero forcing was also introduced in control of quantum systems. Power domination originated from the problem of optimal placement of PMUs, monitoring devices in an electrical network. More information on the origins of these parameters and relevant references can be found in
[
21
]
.

The
standard color change rule
is: If
w
is the unique unfilled neighbor of a filled vertex
v
, then fill
w
.
The
PSD color change rule
is: Let
B
be the set of currently filled vertices and let
W_{1},\dots,W_{k}
be the sets of vertices of the components of
G[V(G)\setminus B]
. If
v\in B
,
w\in W_{i}
, and
w
is the only unfilled neighbor of
v
in
G[W_{i}\cup B]
, then fill
w
.
It is possible that there is only one component of
G[V(G)\setminus B]
, and in that case the effect of the PSD color change rule is the same as that of the standard color change rule.
Forcing using the standard color change rule or the PSD color change rule is also called
standard zero forcing
or
PSD forcing
.
Repeated application of the standard or PSD color change rule until no more vertices can be filled is called the
standard zero forcing propagation process
or the
PSD forcing propagation process
.

Power domination
begins with a
domination step
, in which every neighbor of a filled vertex is filled. After the domination step, the standard color change rule is applied; together these steps are called the
power domination propagation process
.

A set
B
of vertices is called a
standard zero forcing set, PSD forcing set
, or
power dominating set
, respectively, if starting with the vertices in
B
filled and the remaining vertices unfilled, the respective propagation process can fill all the vertices. The
standard zero forcing number

\operatorname{Z}(G)
is the minimum cardinality of a standard zero forcing set, and similarly for the
PSD forcing number

\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)
and
power domination number

\gamma_{P}(G)
.
Power domination has natural connections to domination, so we provide definitions and notation for that also: A set
S\subseteq V(G)
is a
dominating set of
G
if every vertex of
G
is in
S
or is a neighbor of a vertex in
S
; the
domination number

\gamma(G)
is the minimum cardinality of a dominating set.

Cops and Robbers
is a pursuit-evasion game played on a graph by two players. One player places and moves a set of cops and the other places and moves one robber. The goal for the cops is to catch the robber by occupying the same vertex the robber occupies, which is called
capture
.
The goal of the robber is to avoid capture. Initially the cops are placed on a multiset
B
of vertices (more than one cop can occupy a single vertex), and then the robber is placed on a vertex. For the rest of the game, players alternate turns, starting with the cops player; a
round
consists of one turn for the cops player and one for the robber player. A turn consists of repositioning all cops or the robber so that each is at most distance one away from its current vertex. The cops win the game if a cop captures the robber after a finite number of rounds, whereas if the robber has a strategy to evade the cops indefinitely, the robber wins. If choosing the set
B
results in the cops player being able to win for any choice of strategy by the robber player, then
B
is called a
capture set
. The
cop number

c(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum cardinality of a capture set; see
[
10
]
for more information on Cops and Robbers.

We often use
Y
to represent one or more of the processes such as standard zero forcing, PSD forcing, power domination, or Cops and Robbers, or the
Y
-number
Y(G)
of a graph
G
, a
Y
-set, etc.

We define
Y
-propagation time for
Y

Start with
B^{[0]}=B^{(0)}=B\subseteq V(G)
.
Assume
B^{(i)}
and
B^{[i]}
have been constructed. Then

B^{(i+1)}=\{w:\mbox{ $w$ can be filled (given that all vertices in $B^{[i]}$ are filled)}\}.

and
B^{[i+1]}=B^{[i]}\cup B^{(i+1)}
.
For power domination,
B^{[1]}=N[B]
and
B^{(1)}=B^{[1]}\setminus B
, and
B^{(i+1)}
and
B^{[i+1]}
are defined as above for
i\geq 1
.
The
Y
-propagation time
(for standard or PSD forcing or power domination) of
B\subseteq V(G)
, denoted by
\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G;B)
, is the least
t
such that
B^{[t]}=V(G)
; if
B^{[t]}\neq V(G)
for all
t
, then
\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G;B)=\infty
.
The
k
-propagation time of
G
for
Y
is

\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G,k)=\min_{|B|=k}\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G;B).
The
Y
-propagation time of
G
is

\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G)=\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G,Y(G)).

For Cops and Robbers, there is no analog of
\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G;B)
because the cops can be placed anywhere. The
k
-capture time

\operatorname{capt}_{k}(G)
is the minimum number of rounds needed for
k\geq c(G)
cops to capture the robber on
G
over all possible games (when both the cops and the robber play optimally);
\operatorname{capt}_{k}(G)=\infty
for
k<c(G)
.
The
capture time
of
G
is the
c(G)
-capture time and is denoted by
\operatorname{capt}(G)
.

For
Y
one of standard zero forcing, PSD forcing, or power domination, we define the sum and two types of product throttling number of a set, the
k
-throttling number, and the throttling number of the graph. Let
G
be a graph of order

.

Sum throttling
2

2

for
Y
: For a set

and

\operatorname{th}_{Y}(G;B)=|B|+\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G;B),\ \operatorname{th}_{Y}(G,k)=k+\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G,k),\mbox{

No initial cost product throttling for

It is assumed that

B\subsetneq V(G)
and

\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G;B)=|B|\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G;B),\ \operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G,k)=k\cdot\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G,k),\mbox{

Initial cost product throttling for
Y

For a set
B\subseteq V(G)
and
1\leq k\leq n

\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(G;B)=|B|(1+\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G;B)),\ \operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(G,k)=k(1+\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G,k)),\mbox{

For Cops and Robbers we define the

\operatorname{pt}_{Y}(G,k)
by
\operatorname{capt}_{k}(G)

Remark 1.1
.

Let
G
be a graph (and assume each of
G
and
\overline{G}
has an edge for no initial cost product throttling), and let
Y
be one of the parameters
\operatorname{Z},\operatorname{Z}_{+},\gamma_{P}
, or
c
. Then
1\leq Y(G)\leq n
, and if
G
has an edge, then
Y(G)\leq n-1
.
If
G
does not have an edge, then
\operatorname{th}_{Y}(G)=\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(G)=n
(and
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G)
is not defined).

If
G
has an edge, then
\operatorname{th}_{Y}(G),\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(G)\leq n
and
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G)\leq n-1
;
furthermore,
\operatorname{th}_{Y}(G)\geq Y(G)+1\geq 2
,
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G)\geq Y(G)\geq 1
, and
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(G)\geq\min\{2Y(G),n\}\geq 2
.
As noted in
[
2
]
,
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(G)>\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G)\geq\operatorname{th}_{Y}(G)-1
, which also implies
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(G)\geq\operatorname{th}_{Y}(G)
.
Thus

4\leq\operatorname{th}_{Y}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{Y}(\overline{G})\leq 2n,\ 2\leq\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\leq 2n-2,\mbox{ and }4\leq\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(\overline{G})\leq 2n.

4\leq\operatorname{th}_{Y}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{Y}(\overline{G})\leq n^{2},\ 1\leq\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\leq(n-1)^{2},\mbox{ and }4\leq\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\times}(\overline{G})\leq n^{2}.

Given a graph parameter
\zeta
, a
sharp Nordhaus-Gaddum sum bound
is a bound on
\zeta(G)+\zeta(\overline{G})
that is attained by infinitely many graphs. Analogously, a
sharp Nordhaus-Gaddum product bound
is a bound on
\zeta(G)\cdot\zeta(\overline{G})
that is attained by infinitely many graphs. We say a Nordhaus-Gaddum sum (respectively, product) bound
b(n)
on

is

if infinitely many graphs satisfy
\zeta(G)+\zeta(\overline{G})=b(n)\pm k
(respectively,
\zeta(G)\cdot\zeta(\overline{G})=b(n)\pm k
) for some positive integer
k<10
. A Nordhaus-Gaddum sum (respectively, product) bound
b(n)\pm o(b(n))
is
tight
if infinitely many graphs satisfy
\zeta(G)+\zeta(\overline{G})=b(n)\pm o(b(n))
(respectively,
\zeta(G)\cdot\zeta(\overline{G})=b(n)\pm o(b(n))
).

In the proof of Theorem
2.7
we make use of the following definitions. Consider a fixed forcing process for a graph

\operatorname{rd}(v)=0

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 4
. Then
(2-\sqrt{3})n^{\frac{3}{2}}-(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n<\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(\overline{G})
.

Proof.

Choose
A\subseteq V(G)
such that
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G)=\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G;A)
and fix a forcing process for
G
using
A
as the forcing set.
Define
B^{[i]}=\{v\in V(G)|\operatorname{rd}(v)\leq i\}
,
and note that
|B^{[i]}\setminus B^{[i-1]}|\leq|A|
for every
i\geq 1
since at most
|A|
vertices can be forced at the
i
th round of propagation. Additionally, define
F_{i}\subseteq B^{[i-1]}
to be the set of inactive vertices after the
i
th round of propagation (so
B^{[i]}\setminus F_{i}
is the set of active vertices after the
i
th round).

Suppose first that
|A|\geq(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n
. Then
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G)\geq(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n+1>(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n
. Additionally, it is known that
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(H)\geq 2\sqrt{n}-1
for every graph
H
of order
n

[
11
]
,
[
21
, Theorem 11.14]
. Hence,

\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(\overline{G})>(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n\cdot(2\sqrt{n}-1)=(2-\sqrt{3})n^{\frac{3}{2}}-(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n.

Now suppose that
|A|<(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n
. Let
m
be the smallest integer such that
|B^{[m]}|\geq\frac{n}{2}
. Since
|B^{[m-1]}|<\frac{n}{2}
and
|B^{[m]}\setminus B^{[m-1]}|\leq|A|
, we have that
|B^{[m]}|<\frac{n}{2}+(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n=\left(\frac{3-\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)n
and
|\overline{B^{[m]}}|>\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}\right)n
. It is known that the number of active vertices each round is constant and equal to the number of vertices in the initial forcing set
[
21
, p. 188]
. Hence,
|B^{[m]}\setminus F_{m}|=|A|

and
|F_{m}|>\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}\right)n
.
Since each vertex in
F_{m}
applied a force at the
m
th or earlier round of propagation to fill a vertex in
B^{[m]}
, there are no edges in
G
between
F_{m}
and
V(G)\setminus B^{[m]}
. Consequently,
\overline{G}
contains more than
\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}\right)^{2}n^{2}=(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n^{2}
edges. By
[
11
]
,
\operatorname{Z}(H)>\frac{|E(H)|}{n}
for every graph
H
of order
n
. Hence,
\operatorname{Z}(\overline{G})>(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n
and
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(\overline{G})>(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n
. Since we know
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G)\geq 2\sqrt{n}-1
,

\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}(\overline{G})>(2\sqrt{n}-1)(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n=(2-\sqrt{3})n^{\frac{3}{2}}-(1-\sqrt{3}/2)n.\qed

Corollary 2.8
.

The NG product lower bound for
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}
is
\Theta(n^{3/2})
(both for arbitrary graphs and when both

\overline{G}

When

. The result then follows from Theorem
2.7
. ∎

. If
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\geq\frac{5n}{8}
, the result follows. So we may assume that
\frac{5n}{8}>\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)=n-|A_{1}|
. This implies
\frac{3n}{8}<|A_{1}|
, and hence
|A_{2}|<\frac{5n}{8}-|A_{1}|<\frac{n}{4}
. Consequently

implies

. By Lemma

(
2
) and (

.

In each of Cases 1, 2, 3, and 4 we arrive at the conclusion that
\frac{5n}{4}-2\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(\overline{G})
.
Example
2.9
shows this bound is almost sharp because
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(\mathsf{R}_{4m})+\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(\overline{\mathsf{R}_{4m}})\leq 2m+3m=5|V(\mathsf{R}_{4m})|/4
.
∎

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 4
. Then
\frac{3n^{2}}{8}-O(n)\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(\overline{G})
and this bound is tight even when both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected.

Proof.

We begin by partitioning
V(G)
into sets
A_{1},A_{2}
and
B
and partitioning

into sets
A^{\prime}_{1}
,
A^{\prime}_{2}
and
B^{\prime}
as in Lemma
2.10
. For each of the following cases, we assume that we have labeled
G
and
\overline{G}
such that
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(\overline{G})
.

Case 1: Suppose
A^{\prime}_{1}\cap A_{1}\neq\emptyset
and
A^{\prime}_{1}\cap B\neq\emptyset
.
By Lemma
2.10
(
9
),
|A_{1}|+|B|-4\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(\overline{G})
.
If
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\geq\frac{\sqrt{6}n}{4}
, then
\frac{3n^{2}}{8}\leq\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(\overline{G})
. Hence, we can assume that
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)<\frac{\sqrt{6}n}{4}

2.10
(
4

n-\frac{\sqrt{6}n}{4}<|A_{1}|
. Again by Theorem
2.2
, we know
\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}

We conclude that

\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\geq\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)(2|A_{1}|-4)>\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\!\left(2\!\left(n-\frac{\sqrt{6}n}{4}\right)-4\right)=\frac{4-\sqrt{6}}{4}n^{2}-2n>\frac{3}{8}n^{2}-2n.

Case 2: Suppose
A^{\prime}_{1}\cap A_{1}=\emptyset
and
A^{\prime}_{1}\cap B\neq\emptyset
.
By Lemma
2.10
(
8
),
|A_{1}|-2\leq|A^{\prime}_{2}|
.
By Lemma
2.10
(

\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\operatorname{Z}}^{\ast}(\overline{G})=(n-|A_{1}|)\!\left(\frac{n+|A^{\prime}_{2}|}{2}\right)\geq(n-|A_{1}|)\!\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{|A_{1}|}{2}-1\right)=\frac{n^{2}}{2}-n-\frac{|A_{1}|^{2}}{2}+|A_{1}|.

and
A^{\prime}_{1}\cap B=\emptyset
. By switching between

and
B

Case 4: Suppose

and

Theorem 3.1
.

[
21
, Theorem 11.39]
Let

be a graph of order

.
Then
n+o(n)\leq\operatorname{th}_{+}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{G})\leq 2n

and

is required to have an edge, then

We begin with a lemma on NG product lower bounds that will be used in both the general case and the case in which both graphs must be connected. Recall the straightforward fact that

\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}

\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(G)=n

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 6
such that
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)\leq\operatorname{Z}_{+}(\overline{G})
.
Then:

(1)

2n\leq\operatorname{th}_{+}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{G})
, and
3n-9\leq\operatorname{th}_{+}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{G})
if
G\not\cong K_{1,n-1}
.

(2)

n-1\leq\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\overline{G})
, and
2n-8\leq\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\overline{G})
if
G\not\cong K_{1,n-1}
.

(3)

2n\leq\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(\overline{G})
, and
3n\leq\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(\overline{G})
if and
G\not\cong K_{1,n-1}
.

Each of the first lower bounds is sharp, realized by
K_{1,n-1}
.

Proof.

We examine three cases based on
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)
and
\operatorname{pt}_{+}(G)
.
Consider first the case in which
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)=1
and
\operatorname{pt}_{+}(G)=1
. This is equivalent to
G\cong K_{1,n-1}
; note that
\overline{K_{1,n-1}}\cong K_{n-1}\,\dot{\cup}\,K_{1}

\operatorname{Z}_{+}(\overline{K_{1,n-1}})=n-1
and
\operatorname{pt}_{+}(\overline{K_{1,n-1}})=1

\operatorname{th}_{+}(K_{1,n-1})=2,\qquad\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(K_{1,n-1})=1,\qquad\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(K_{1,n-1})=2.

\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{K_{1,n-1}})=n,\qquad\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\overline{K_{1,n-1}})=n-1,\qquad\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(\overline{K_{1,n-1}})=n,

which shows that the first lower bound in each statement (once established) is sharp.
For the remaining two cases, we will use the fact that
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)+\operatorname{Z}_{+}(\overline{G})\geq n-2

[
21
, Theorem 9.60]
.

For the second case, suppose that
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)=1
and
\operatorname{pt}_{+}(G)\geq 2
. Then
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(G)\geq 3
and
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(\overline{G})\geq n-3
.
Since
n\geq 6
,
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(\overline{G})=n
, so
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(\overline{G})\geq 3n
.
Also
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(G)\geq 2
and
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\geq n-3

\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\geq 2n-6\geq n-1
since
n\geq 6
(and
2n-6\geq 2n-8
for all
n
).
Furthermore,
\operatorname{th}_{+}(G)\geq 3
, and
\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{G})\geq n-2
. Thus
\operatorname{th}_{+}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{G})\geq 3n-6\geq 2n
since
n\geq 6
(and
3n-6\geq 3n-9
for all
n
).

For the third case, assume
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)\geq 2
.
First we consider
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}
and note that
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(G)\geq 4
. It has been verified in
[
20
]
that
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(\overline{G})\geq 3n
for
n=6
(since
G\not\cong K_{1,n-1}
). Since
n=7

\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(\overline{G})\geq 4\!\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)(2)=4n-8\geq 3n

\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}
.

Next we consider
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}
with
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)=z\geq 2
.
First assume
2\leq z\leq\frac{n}{2}-1
. To show that
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\geq 2n-8
for

, it suffices to show that
0\leq g(z)=z(n-2-z)-(2n-8)=-z^{2}+(n-2)z-2n+8

[
20

\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\geq n-1
for

for

\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}
.

Finally, we consider
\operatorname{th}_{+}
with
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(G)=z\geq 2

need to be filled before any of the unfilled vertices in

can be filled. The only vertex that can possibly force

,
v^{\prime}
, or

is

(in the case that

), but at most one of
v
,
v^{\prime}
, and

can be forced and no further PSD forces can occur. Thus

is not a PSD forcing set.

Finally suppose that
|W\cap V_{i}|\leq 2
for every

. Since
|W|\geq c+1
, there exists some

such that
|W\cap V_{p}|=2
; let

. Then

is adjacent to every other vertex in

except

. Since

,
|\{i:W\cap V_{i}\neq\emptyset\}|\geq 4
, so
v_{p}
has at least one neighbor in

. Therefore
\overline{\mathsf{F}_{c}}[W]
is connected. Given any
v\in B
, suppose
v\in V_{j}
for some

|W\cap V_{j}|\leq 2
and

has at most two non-neighbors in

, we have
|N(v)\cap W|\geq c+1-2-2\geq 3
. So

is a PSD fort, and
B
is not a PSD forcing set.

We complete the proof by establishing the statements about throttling numbers. Note that
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(\mathsf{F}_{c})=\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\times}(\overline{\mathsf{F}_{c}})=c^{2}
is immediate from
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(\mathsf{F}_{c})=c^{2}-2c+2
and
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(\overline{\mathsf{F}_{c}})=c^{2}-c
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{th}_{+}(\mathsf{F}_{c})\geq c^{2}-2c+3
is immediate from
\operatorname{Z}_{+}(\mathsf{F}_{c})=c^{2}-2c+2
and Remark
1.1
. Since
\operatorname{pt}_{+}(\overline{\mathsf{F}_{c}},c^{2}-c)=1
,
\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{\mathsf{F}_{c}})=c^{2}-c+1
and
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\overline{\mathsf{F}_{c}})=c^{2}-c
.

Lastly we show that
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\mathsf{F}_{c})=c^{2}-c
. Since
\operatorname{pt}_{+}(\mathsf{F}_{c},c^{2}-c)=1
,
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\mathsf{F}_{c})\leq c^{2}-c
is immediate. Suppose
B
is a set such that
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\mathsf{F}_{c};B)=\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\mathsf{F}_{c})<c^{2}-c
. Since
|B|\geq c^{2}-2c+2
and
t(c^{2}-2c+2)>c^{2}-c
for all

, we must have
\operatorname{pt}_{+}(\mathsf{F}_{c};B)=1
and
|B|<c^{2}-c
.
So there are at least

unfilled vertices, which implies

which cannot be forced in the first round, and the contradiction implies
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\mathsf{F}_{c})\geq c^{2}-c
.
∎

For (

For (

Proof.

The upper bounds are all immediate by Corollary

(the cases

Since

The NG sum lower bounds were established in Corollary

Recall the graph

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
G
be a graph of order

such that both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected. Then:

(1)

\operatorname{th}_{+}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{G})\leq 2n-\log_{4}n+2
.

(2)

\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\leq 2n-\log_{4}n
.

Proof.

By
[
13
, Proposition 2.9]
,

\operatorname{th}_{+}(G)\leq n-\alpha(G)+1.

Simply using this bound for both
G
and
\overline{G}
, we get

\operatorname{th}_{+}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{G})\leq 2n-[\alpha(G)+\alpha(\overline{G})]+2.

(3.1)

The next NG bound on

is known
[
4
, Theorem 2.100]
:

\min\{a+b:R(a+1,b+1)>n\}\leq\alpha(G)+\alpha(\overline{G}),

(3.2)

where

is the Ramsey number for
a+1
and
b+1
, i.e., the smallest integer
N
such that every graph on
N
vertices has an
(a+1)
-clique or a
(b+1)
-independent set.

Without loss of generality, suppose

[

]

R(a+1,b+1)\leq R(a+1,a+1)\leq 4^{a}.

Therefore, the left-hand side of (

\min\{a+b:4^{a}>n\}\geq\min\{a:4^{a}>n\}\geq\log_{4}n.

Combining this with (
3.1
) and (

) gives the desired result:

\operatorname{th}_{+}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{+}(\overline{G})\leq 2n-[\alpha(G)+\alpha(\overline{G})]+2\leq 2n-\log_{4}n+2.

The second result follows in a similar manner from the fact that
\operatorname{th}_{+}^{\ast}(G)\leq n-\alpha(G)

[
2
, Proposition 2.77]
.
∎

4
Power domination and Cops and Robbers

In this section we establish Nordhaus-Gaddum bounds on throttling for two forcing parameters, namely power domination and Cops and Robbers. We combine the studies of these two forcing parameters because many bounds for both parameters are determined from domination bounds, including those in the next remark.
For results that apply to both forcing parameters, we use

Remark 4.1

[

\gamma(G)+\gamma(\overline{G})=\frac{n}{2}+2
. Then by Theorem
4.2
(
2
),

\{\gamma(G),\gamma(\overline{G})\}=\{\frac{n}{2},2\}
(since
n\neq 9
because
n
is even), and we obtain the stated upper bound for
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})
by Lemma
4.9
.

A similar approach establishes that the bound
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})\leq 4n
is not sharp except for
K_{3}\,\Box\,K_{3}
. From Remark
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
(
1
), we see that
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})=4n
only if
\gamma(G)\cdot\gamma(\overline{G})=n
. By
[
18
, Theorem 16.7]
, either
\{\gamma(G),\gamma(\overline{G})\}\in\{\{n,1\},\{\frac{n}{2},2\}\}
or
G=K_{3}\Box K_{3}
. However, if
\{\gamma(G),\gamma(\overline{G})\}=\{n,1\}
, then
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})\leq 2n
, and if
\{\gamma(G),\gamma(\overline{G})\}=\{\frac{n}{2},2\}\}
, then
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})\leq 3n
by Lemma
4.9
. Thus
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})<4n
except for
K_{3}\,\Box\,K_{3}
, and
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(K_{3}\,\Box\,K_{3})\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{K_{3}\,\Box\,K_{3}})=6\cdot 6=4\cdot 9

[
20
]
.

Example
4.5
shows both of the NG upper bounds for
\operatorname{th}_{Y}
are sharp and the NG sum upper bound for
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G)
is sharp.
Example
4.6
shows that the NG product upper bound for
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}
is sharp, both NG
upper bounds for
\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\times}
are sharp, and the NG sum upper bound for
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}
is sharp (note the order there is
2r
).
∎

The next result shows that any upper bound on
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})
for graphs
G
of order
n
is at least
\frac{24n}{7}\approx 3.42857n
.

Corollary 4.12
.

For
r\geq 1
there exists a graph
G
of order
n=7r
such that
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})=24r=\frac{24n}{7}
. Thus the NG product upper bound for
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}
is
\Theta(n)
.

Proof.

The NG product upper bound for
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}
is less than
4n
by Theorem
4.10
, except for
G=K_{3}\,\Box\,K_{3}
.

Let
G=rC_{3}\sqcup rC_{4}
be a graph on
n=7r
vertices. Then
\gamma(G)=3r
,
\gamma_{P}(G)=2r
, and
\operatorname{pt}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)=2
. Hence,
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)=\min(3r(1+1),2r(2+1))=6r
. The complement
\overline{G}
is the join of
r
copies of
3K_{1}
and
r
copies of
2K_{2}
, so
\gamma_{P}(\overline{G})=2=\gamma(\overline{G})
.
Thus
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})=6r\cdot 4=\frac{24n}{7}
. ∎

Next we establish Nordhaus-Gaddum bounds for power domination and Cops and Robbers throttling when both graphs are required to be connected.

Example 4.13
.

Consider the graph
G_{0}
of order
n
obtained by adding a new leaf to one leaf of
H\circ 2K_{1}
, where
H
is a connected graph of order at least
4
. It was shown in
[
21
, Example 11.57]
that
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G_{0})=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+2
. Moreover,
\gamma(\overline{G_{0}})=2
and
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G_{0}})=3
, so
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G_{0})+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G_{0}})=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+5
. Also
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G_{0})\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G_{0}})=3\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+2\right)=n+o(n)
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
such that both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected.

If
n\geq 4
and
n\neq 9,10,11
, then

\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+5

and this bound is sharp. Furthermore,
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+6
for
n=9,10
and
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+7
for
n=11
.

For all
n\geq 4
,

\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq n+2(1+\ln n)\sqrt{n}+4.

Proof.

Since
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected, we have
\gamma(G)\geq 2
and
\gamma(\overline{G})\geq 2
. Suppose
\gamma(\overline{G})=2
; this implies
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})=3
.
It was shown in
[
7
]
that
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+2
, so
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+5
and
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq n+6
. Similarly, we obtain the same upper bounds if
\gamma(G)=2
.
Hence, it remains to consider the case where
\gamma(G)\geq 3
and
\gamma(\overline{G})\geq 3
.
Under this assumption, we have
\gamma(G)+\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\min\{\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\}+3

[
4
, Theorem 2.7]
.

If
\min\{\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor
, then
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq\gamma(G)+\gamma(\overline{G})+2\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+5
. Hence, in the remainder of the proof of the NG sum upper bound, we assume that
\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})>\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor
.
Since
\gamma(G)\leq\left(\frac{1+\ln(\delta(G)+1)}{\delta(G)+1}\right)n
for every graph
G
with no isolated vertices
[
19
, Theorem 16]
, we have
\max\{\gamma(G),\gamma(\overline{G})\}\leq\left(\frac{1+\ln(\left\lfloor n/3\right\rfloor+{2})}{\left\lfloor n/3\right\rfloor+{2}}\right)n
, where the inequality follows from the fact that
f(x)=\frac{1+\ln(x)}{x}
is a decreasing function on
x\geq 1
and
\delta(G)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+1
. As a result,

\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq 2\left(\frac{1+\ln(\left\lfloor n/3\right\rfloor+{2})}{\left\lfloor n/3\right\rfloor+2}\right)n+2,

which is numerically verified to be less than
\left\lfloor n/3\right\rfloor+5
for all
n\geq 63
.

Now assume
15\leq n\leq 62
. Since
n\geq 15
,
\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\geq 6
. By
[
4
, Theorem 2.16]
,
\gamma(G)+\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{6n}{17}\right\rfloor+2
, which implies

\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{6n}{17}\right\rfloor+4\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+5

because
15\leq n\leq 62
. The case
n=12,13,14
is similar using
\gamma(G)+\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{5n}{14}\right\rfloor+2
when
\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\geq 5
and
n\neq 16
by
[
4
, Theorem 2.15]
.

Finally we consider
4\leq n\leq 11
. For all pairs
G
and
\overline{G}
where both are connected,
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+5
for
n=4,5,6,7,8
is verified in
[
20
]
. Since
\min\{\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\}+\max\{\Delta(G),\Delta(\overline{G})\}=n-1
,
\min\{\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor
. Thus
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor+5
. Computation shows that
\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor+5\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+5+x
with
x=1
for
n=9,10
and
x=2
for
n=11
.

This completes the proof of the NG sum upper bound for
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}
, and Example
4.13
shows that the bound for
n\geq 12
is sharp.

As for the NG product upper bound,

\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq(\gamma(G)+1)(\gamma(\overline{G})+1)\leq n+\gamma(G)+\gamma(\overline{G})+1\leq n+\min\{\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\}+4

since
\gamma(G)+\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\min\{\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\}+3
.
If
\min\{\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\}\leq\sqrt{n}
(which is always true if
n\leq 6
), then our result is established.
So assume
\min\{\delta(G),\delta(\overline{G})\}>\sqrt{n}
. Then
\max\{\gamma(G),\gamma(\overline{G})\}<\left(\frac{1+\ln(\sqrt{n}+1)}{\sqrt{n}+1}\right)n<(1+\ln n)\sqrt{n}
since
n\geq 7
, which implies that
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})<n+2(1+\ln n)\sqrt{n}+4
.
∎

.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 5
such that both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected.
For power domination number:

(1)

6\leq\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+5

(n\geq 12)

and
9\leq\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}(\overline{G})\leq n+o(n)
. The exact bounds are sharp and the asymptotic bound is tight.

(2)

4\leq\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\ast}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+2
and
4\leq\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\leq n
.
All bounds are sharp.

(3)

6\leq\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})\leq\frac{6n}{7}+3

(n\geq 16)
and
9\leq\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\times}(\overline{G})\leq 4n
. Both lower bounds and the NG sum upper bound are sharp.

For cop number :

(4)

6\leq\operatorname{th}_{c}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{c}(\overline{G})\leq\min\!\left(\frac{2n}{(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}-o(1)}},\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+4\right)

9\leq\operatorname{th}_{c}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{c}(\overline{G})\leq n+\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+3
. Both lower bounds are sharp.

(5)

4\leq\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\ast}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+2
and
4\leq\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\leq n
. Both lower bounds are sharp.

(6)

6\leq\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\times}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\times}(\overline{G})\leq n+4
and
9\leq\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\times}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{c}^{\times}(\overline{G})\leq 4n
. Both lower bounds are sharp.

Proof.

Lower bounds: When both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected, neither has a universal vertex. Thus Remark
4.3
establishes the lower bounds. Example
4.16
shows that all lower bounds are sharp.

For the upper bounds in (
1
) and (
4
): Theorem
4.14
establishes the upper bounds for
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}
and
Example
4.13
shows those upper bounds are tight. Since neither
G
nor
\overline{G}
have isolated vertices, (
4.1
) and Theorem
4.10
establish the upper bounds for
\operatorname{th}_{c}
.

For the upper bounds in (
2
) and (
5
):
Parts (
2
) and (
1
) of Theorem
4.2

show that
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G)+\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\leq\gamma(G)+\gamma(\overline{G})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+2
and
\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(G)\cdot\operatorname{th}_{Y}^{\ast}(\overline{G})\leq\gamma(G)\cdot\gamma(\overline{G})\leq n
,
respectively.
Example
4.8
shows that
the upper bounds for
\operatorname{th}_{\gamma_{P}}^{\ast}

Now we turn our attention to the upper bounds in (
3
) and (
6
).
For
n\geq 10
, Theorem
4.2

3
