A Two-Sided Sketching Algorithm for Low-rank Tensor-Train Approximation Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Gaohang Yu, Yihao Pan, Ailun Jian, xiaohao Cai

Abstract
Tensor train (TT) decomposition is a powerful method to acquire low-rank tensors. However, the computational process is frequently obstructed by the large-scale matrix singular value decomposition (SVD). The sketching algorithm serves as an efficient data compression technique that can quickly derive low-rank matrix approximations. In this paper, we propose a randomized algorithm to obtain the TT approximation of tensors using a one-pass sketching algorithm and subspace iteration, and offer thorough error-bound and robustness analysis. Numerical experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed algorithm.

Introduction

In real-world scenarios, large high-dimensional datasets can often be naturally expressed as tensors, frequently showing a low-rank structure that can be described with significantly fewer parameters. As a result, low-rank tensor approximations have been widely used in various areas, including machine learning, graph analysis, and signal processing, among others
[

[

Traditional tensor decomposition techniques encompass canonical (CP) decomposition

[

[

[

Although Tucker decomposition provides enhanced stability over CP decomposition, it is plagued by the curse of dimensionality. In contrast, TT decomposition overcomes the curse of dimensionality and is more dependable. This paper primarily focuses on TT decomposition, which is gaining popularity due to its robustness and efficiency.

TT decomposition involves breaking down large-scale tensors into several third-order tensors. One straightforward method is TT-SVD
[
25
]
, which utilizes auxiliary unfolding matrices for SVD. Nonetheless, conducting SVD on large-scale matrices is highly time-intensive. There are various methods to make the computation both efficient and accurate. One particular category is randomized methods
[

[

introduced a randomized SVD (R-SVD) technique that effectively computes low-rank approximations of large-scale matrices. When the original data is so vast that it can only be read once by the core memory, so one-pass algorithms become essential. Tropp et al.
[
29
]
proposed a one-pass sketching algorithm for streaming data, which can create low-rank approximations of input matrices from random linear sketches of the matrix and also provide error bounds.

In recent years, many scholars have extended matrix random methods to tensors, yielding promising results

[
26

proposed parallelizable algorithms to compute the decomposition of the TT with theoretical guarantees of precision. Ma et al.
[
22

recovered a low-rank matrix and low-tubal-rank tensor from a noisy sketch with rigorous theoretical guarantees.

Power iteration can improve the accuracy of approximations by reducing tail energy, making it more accurate than simple random projection algorithms. Vogel et al.
[
32

introduced the subspace iteration method and considered the efficient iterative computation of partial SVDs. Gu
[
15

presented a novel error analysis considering randomized algorithms within the subspace iteration framework. Yu et al.
[

[

In this paper, we propose a randomized algorithm named TT-subSKETCH for TT approximations of tensors. This algorithm utilizes two-sided sketching for low-rank matrix approximation to enhance its efficiency. By integrating the sketching method with the power iteration approach, we can obtain higher quality TT approximations and deliver thorough error analysis. Additionally, we address low-rank approximations in noisy conditions and expand upon the findings of

2.2
TT-SVD

The TT low-rank approximation model for solving the tensor
\mathcal{A}
can be expressed as
[
6

\mathop{\min}\limits_{\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\cdots,\mathcal{G}_{d}}\|\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{3}^{1}\mathcal{G}_{2}\times_{3}^{1}\cdots\times_{3}^{1}\mathcal{G}_{d}\|_{F},

(7)

where
\mathcal{G}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{i-1}\times n_{i}\times r_{i}}(r_{0}=r_{d}=1,i\in[1,d])

for

the TT core
\mathcal{G}_{i}
satisfy

G_{i}^{\top}G_{i}=I_{r_{i}},G_{i}=reshape(\mathcal{G}_{i},[r_{i-1}n_{i},r_{i}]).

Assume
{\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2},\cdots,\mathcal{T}_{d}}
form a

7

(i=1,2,\cdots,d-1)

\begin{cases}&\mathcal{A}_{0}=\mathcal{A},\\
&\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\times_{1}^{1}\mathcal{A}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{d}},\\

An approximate solution can be obtained by solving the following d-1 subproblem
for

3
A two-sided sketching algorithm for TT approximation

As depicted in Algorithm
2
, although R-SVD can effectively obtain low-rank approximations of matrices, it requires us to revisit the original data matrix. Assuming
A
represents an enormous matrix, too large to be stored solely within the core memory. The expense associated with data transmission might be so substantial that we are constrained to loading the matrix into the core memory only once. Tropp et al.
[
29
]
introduced a two-sided sketching algorithm for matrices, which can efficiently obtain low-rank approximations of matrices while reading the original data only once.

Randomized algorithms have proven effective in swiftly obtaining low-rank approximations. However, their efficacy diminishes when faced with matrices exhibiting slow singular value decay, yielding suboptimal results. Power iteration can enhance the rate of decay of singular values, thereby reducing the tail energy and improving the accuracy. From
[
29
]
, it can be inferred that the error of the two-sided sketching algorithm depends on the tail energy of the matrix. Dong et al.
[
12
]
proposed an algorithm that combines the subspace power iteration technique with two-sided sketching, i.e., sub-SKETCH, see Algorithm
4
. When
A
is dense, the arithmetic cost of Algorithm

is
\mathcal{O}((q+1)(k+l)mn+kl(m+n))
flops.

We first present the following theorem characterizing the error of Algorithm

Theorem 3
.

(Average spectral error for Algorithm
4
).
For
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, assume that
m>n
and the sketch size parameter satisfies
l>k+1
. Draw random test matrices
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
and
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times m}
independently of the standard normal distribution. The rank-
k
approximation
\hat{A}
obtained from Algorithm
4
satisfies

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\lVert A-\hat{A}\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq&\Big[1+\frac{e^{2}l}{(l-k)^{2}}\big(\sqrt{l}+\sqrt{n-k}\big)^{2}\Big]\\
&\Big[1+\sqrt{\frac{k}{l-k-1}}+\frac{e\sqrt{l}}{l-k}\sqrt{n-k}\Big]^{2/(2q+1)}\sigma_{k+1}^{2}(A)\ .\end{split}

(14)

Proof.

We decompose the approximation error into two distinct components via the triangle inequality, i.e.,

\begin{split}\lVert A-\hat{A}\rVert_{2}^{2}=&\lVert(A-QQ^{\top}A)+(QQ^{\top}A-QX)\rVert_{2}^{2}\\
&\leq\lVert A-QQ^{\top}A\rVert_{2}^{2}+\lVert X-Q^{\top}A\rVert_{2}^{2}\ .\end{split}

(15)

Using
Q
computed from Algorithm
4
, construct a matrix
P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-k)}
with orthonormal columns satisfying

PP^{\top}=I-QQ^{\top}.

(16)

Define matrices

\Psi_{1}:=\Psi P\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times(n-k)},\quad\Psi_{2}:=\Psi Q\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times k}.

(17)

From
lemma

7
, we have

X-Q^{\top}A=\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}\Psi_{1}(P^{\top}A)\ .

(18)

Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\lVert X-Q^{\top}A\rVert_{2}^{2}=&\mathbb{E}\lVert\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}\Psi_{1}(P^{\top}A)\rVert_{2}^{2}\\
\leq&\mathbb{E}\lVert\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}\rVert_{2}^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\lVert\Psi_{1}\rVert_{2}^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\lVert P^{\top}A\rVert_{2}^{2}\ .\end{split}

(19)

Since
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
are Gaussian random matrices, by
[
16
]
(Proposition A.4), we have

\mathbb{E}\lVert\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}\rVert_{2}<\frac{e\sqrt{l}}{l-k}\ .

(20)

Furthermore, since
P
and
Q
have mutually orthogonal column spaces (i.e.,
P^{\top}Q=0
), the Gaussian random matrices
\Psi_{1}=\Psi P
and
\Psi_{2}=\Psi Q
are statistically independent. Following
[
31
]
(Theorem 5.32), the expected spectral norm of
\Psi_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times(n-k)}
is bounded by

\mathbb{E}\lVert\Psi_{1}\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{n-k}+\sqrt{l}\ .

(21)

Combining (
16
), (
19
), (
20
) and (
21
), and utilizing the independence of
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
, it can be shown that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\lVert X-Q^{\top}A\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq&\frac{e^{2}l}{(l-k)^{2}}\big(\sqrt{l}+\sqrt{n-k}\big)^{2}\mathbb{E}\lVert P^{\top}A\rVert_{2}^{2}\\
\leq&\frac{e^{2}l}{(l-k)^{2}}\big(\sqrt{l}+\sqrt{n-k}\big)^{2}\mathbb{E}\lVert A-QQ^{\top}A\rVert_{2}^{2}\\
\leq&\frac{e^{2}l}{(l-k)^{2}}\big(\sqrt{l}+\sqrt{n-k}\big)^{2}\\
&\Big[1+\sqrt{\frac{k}{l-k-1}}+\frac{e\sqrt{l}}{l-k}\cdot\sqrt{n-k}\Big]^{2/(2q+1)}\sigma_{k+1}^{2}(A)\ .\end{split}

(22)

The last inequality holds due to the average spectral error for the power scheme
[
16
]
(Corollary 10.10). Combining this result with Eq. (
15
), we complete the proof.

Remark
. When

Theorem 4
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
be the TT approximation of a tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}
by TT-subSKETCH (i.e., Algorithm
5
) with the target TT-rank
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\cdots,r_{d-1})
,
(r_{0}=r_{d}=1)
, sketch size
\mathbf{l}=(l_{1},\cdots,l_{d-1})
, and the power iteration q satisfied

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\lVert\mathcal{A}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq&\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\Big[1+\frac{e^{2}l_{k}}{(l_{k}-r_{k})^{2}}\big(\sqrt{l_{k}}+\sqrt{\min(r_{k}n_{k+1},n_{k+2}\cdots n_{d})-r_{k}})^{2}\Big]\\
&\Big[1+\sqrt{\frac{r_{k}}{l_{k}-r_{k}-1}}+\frac{e\sqrt{l_{k}}}{l_{k}-r_{k}}\sqrt{\min(r_{k}n_{k+1},n_{k+2}\cdots n_{d})-r_{k}}\Big]^{2/(2q+1)}\\
&\sigma_{r_{k}+1}^{2}(A^{(k)}).\end{split}

(23)

Proof.

Since
X^{(k)}(k\in[1,\cdots,d-1])
is naturally associated with
A^{(k+1)}
, it will continue to decompose further in the Algorithm
5
, which means that
X^{(k)}
is approximated by some matrix
\hat{X}^{(k)}
. Let
\hat{A}^{(k)}=Q^{(k)}\hat{X}^{(k)}
be an approximated matrix of
A^{(k)}
.

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\lVert\mathcal{A}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}\rVert_{2}=&\mathbb{E}\lVert A^{(1)}-\hat{A}^{(1)}\rVert_{2}\\
=&\mathbb{E}\lVert A^{(1)}-Q^{(1)}\hat{X}^{(1)}\rVert_{2}\\
=&\mathbb{E}\lVert A^{(1)}-Q^{(1)}X^{(1)}+Q^{(1)}(X^{(1)}-\hat{X}^{(1)})\rVert_{2}\\
\leq&\mathbb{E}\lVert A^{(1)}-Q^{(1)}X^{(1)}\rVert_{2}+\mathbb{E}\lVert Q^{(1)}(X^{(1)}-\hat{X}^{(1)})\rVert_{2}\\
=&\mathbb{E}\lVert A^{(1)}-Q^{(1)}X^{(1)}\rVert_{2}+\mathbb{E}\lVert X^{(1)}-\hat{X}^{(1)}\rVert_{2}\ .\end{split}

(24)

The last relation holds due to the unitary invariance of the spectral-norm, since
Q^{(k)},k\in[1,\cdots,d-1]
, has orthonormal columns. Moreover,
\forall k\in[1,\cdots,d-1]
, there holds

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\lVert X^{(k)}-\hat{X}^{(k)}\rVert_{2}=&\mathbb{E}\lVert A^{(k+1)}-Q^{(k+1)}\hat{X}^{(k+1)}\rVert_{2}\\
\leq&\mathbb{E}\lVert A^{(k+1)}-Q^{(k+1)}X^{(k+1)}\rVert_{2}+\mathbb{E}\lVert X^{(k+1)}-\hat{X}^{(k+1)}\rVert_{2}\ .\end{split}

(25)

Inserting this iteratively into (
24
) gives

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\lVert\mathcal{A}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}\rVert_{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\mathbb{E}\lVert{A^{(k)}-\hat{A}^{(k)}}\rVert_{2}\ .\end{split}

(26)

Combining with
theorem

3
, we complete the proof.

Furthermore, the computational cost of TT-subSKETCH is dominated by the two-sided sketching and the
q
subspace power iterations at the first unfolding step. Assuming
n_{i}\approx n
and
r_{i}\approx r
, the overall arithmetic cost scales as
\mathcal{O}((q+1)r^{2}n^{d})
. Compared to the
\mathcal{O}(n^{d+1})
complexity of the deterministic TT-SVD, our algorithm provides a substantial theoretical speedup since
(q+1)r^{2}\ll n
strictly holds in typical low-rank scenarios. This theoretically justifies the dramatic reduction in CPU time observed in our numerical experiments.

3.2
Robustness analysis

In practical applications, we may only have access to data that are corrupted with noise. Unlike the noise-free setting in Theorems
3
and
4
, where the randomness stems solely from the Gaussian sketch matrices
\Omega^{(k)}
and
\Psi^{(k)}
with known distributional properties, the noise matrices
Z^{(k)}
and
\widetilde{Z}^{(k)}
here are treated as arbitrary deterministic perturbations with unknown distributions. Consequently, an expected error bound is no longer attainable, and the approximation error must instead be controlled in a high-probability sense with respect to the randomness of the sketch matrices alone. In this scenario, Ma et al.
[
22
]
conducted a robustness analysis for the recovery of low-rank matrices and low-tubal-rank tensors from noisy sketches. We generalize the result from t-product to TT format in Theorem
5
below.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}
be an initial tensor. For each k we have
r_{k}<l_{k}
, and consider the sketches in Algorithm
5
, i.e.,

Y^{(k)}=A^{(k)}\Omega^{(k)}+Z^{(k)},\ \ W^{(k)}=\Psi^{(k)}A^{(k)}+\widetilde{Z}^{(k)},

(27)

where
\Psi^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{k}\times(r_{k-1}n_{k})}
and
\Omega^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{k+1}\cdots n_{d})\times r_{k}}
are independent Gaussian random matrices, and
Z^{(k)}
and
\widetilde{Z}^{(k)}
are noise matrices originated from k sketching.
For any
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3}>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}-\delta_{3}
, the TT approximation
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
obtained by TT-subSKETCH (i.e., Algorithm
5
) satisfies

\begin{split}\lVert\mathcal{A}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}\rVert_{2}\leq&\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\Big[\frac{r_{k}\sqrt{(r_{k-1}n_{k}-r_{k})}\lVert Z^{(k)}\lVert_{2}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{1}\log(1/(1-\delta_{2}))+\delta_{1}}-\sqrt{\delta_{1}}}\\
&+\frac{\lVert(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\lVert_{2}}{\sqrt{l_{k}}-\sqrt{r_{k}}-\sqrt{2\log(2/\delta_{3})}}\Big]\ .\end{split}

(28)

Proof.

From Theorem
4
, we know that the TT approximation error originates from the sketching error of the auxiliary unfolding matrices. Therefore, we first consider the

k-th
sketching of the unfolding matrix, i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{A}^{(k)}&=Y^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}Y^{(k)})\backslash W^{(k)}\\
&=\widetilde{A}^{(k)}+Y^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}Y^{(k)})^{\dagger}(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\ ,\end{split}

(29)

where
\widetilde{A}^{(k)}:=Y^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}Y^{(k)})^{\dagger}(\Psi^{(k)}A^{(k)})
. Denote the SVD of
Y^{(k)}
as

\displaystyle Y^{(k)}=U_{Y}^{(k)}\Sigma_{Y}^{(k)}{V_{Y}^{(k)}}^{\top},

(30)

where
U_{Y}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{(r_{k-1}n_{k})\times r_{k}},\Sigma_{Y}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times r_{k}}
, and
{V_{Y}^{(k)}}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times r_{k}}.
Since
\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)}
has linearly independent columns
[
22
]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}Y^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}Y^{(k)})^{\dagger}&=U_{Y}\Sigma_{Y}V_{Y}^{\top}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}\Sigma_{Y}V_{Y}^{\top})^{\dagger}\\
&=U_{Y}^{(k)}\Sigma_{Y}^{(k)}{V_{Y}^{(k)}}^{\top}V_{Y}^{(k)}{\Sigma_{Y}^{(k)}}^{-1}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}\\
&=U_{Y}^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}\ .\end{split}

(31)

Thus, Eq. (
29
) can be simplified to

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{(k)}=\widetilde{A}^{(k)}+U_{Y}^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\ .

(32)

Our goal is to bound the approximation error, i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert\hat{A}^{(k)}-A^{(k)}\lVert_{2}&=\lVert\widetilde{A}^{(k)}-A+U_{Y}^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\lVert_{2}\\
&\leq\lVert\widetilde{A}^{(k)}-A\lVert_{2}+\lVert U_{Y}^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\lVert_{2}.\end{split}

(33)

For the first term, let
P^{(k)}:=I-U_{Y}^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}\Psi^{(k)}
, and then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{A}^{(k)}-A&=U_{Y}^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}\Psi^{(k)}A^{(k)}-A^{(k)}\\
&=-[I-U_{Y}^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}\Psi^{(k)}]A^{(k)}\\
&=-P^{(k)}A^{(k)}\ .\end{split}

(34)

We also observe that
P^{(k)}
is a projection, i.e.,
{P^{(k)}}^{2}=P^{(k)}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ker P^{(k)}=U_{Y}^{(k)},\ \ {\rm Im}P^{(k)}:=S^{(k)}.\end{split}

(35)

From Lemma
9
in the Appendix, we have

\displaystyle P^{(k)}A^{(k)}=S^{(k)}(T^{(k)}S^{(k)})^{-1}T^{(k)}A^{(k)},

(36)

where
T^{(k)}:={{U_{Y}^{(k)}}_{\perp}^{\top}}
represents the transpose of the orthogonal complement of
U_{Y}^{(k)}
. With the SVD of
A^{(k)}
being
A^{(k)}=U_{A}^{(k)}\Sigma_{A}^{(k)}{V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}
, we then can bound

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert\widetilde{A}^{(k)}-A\lVert&=\lVert P^{(k)}A^{(k)}\lVert_{2}\\
&=\lVert S^{(k)}(T^{(k)S^{(k)}})^{-1}T^{(k)}U_{A}^{(k)}\Sigma_{A}^{(k)}{V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\lVert_{2}\\
&\leq\lVert(T^{(k)}S^{(k)})^{-1}\lVert_{2}\lVert T^{(k)}U_{A}^{(k)}\Sigma_{A}^{(k)}{V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\lVert_{2}\ .\end{split}

(37)

Since
Y^{(k)}=A^{(k)}\Omega^{(k)}+Z^{(k)}=U_{A}^{(k)}\Sigma_{A}^{(k)}{V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)}+Z^{(k)}
, we have

\displaystyle U_{A}^{(k)}\Sigma_{A}^{(k)}{V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)}=Y^{(k)}-Z^{(k)}.

(38)

where
{V_{A}^{(k)}}
has orthonormal columns and
\Omega^{(k)}
is Gaussian matrix. With probability 1,
{V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)}
has linearly independent rows
[
22
]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)})({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)})^{\dagger}=I\ .

(39)

Combining (
38
) and (
39
) yields

\displaystyle U_{A}^{(k)}\Sigma_{A}^{(k)}=(Y^{(k)}-Z^{(k)})({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)})^{\dagger}.

(40)

Simplify the second term of the right hand side of (
37
), Since
T^{(k)}:={{U_{Y}^{(k)}}_{\perp}}^{\top}
and
Y^{(k)}=U_{Y}^{(k)}\Sigma_{Y}^{(k)}{V_{Y}^{(k)}}^{\top}
, we know that
T^{(k)}Y^{(k)}=0
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert T^{(k)}U_{A}^{(k)}\Sigma_{A}^{(k)}{V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\lVert_{2}&=\lVert T^{(k)}U_{A}^{(k)}\Sigma_{A}^{(k)}\lVert_{2}\\
&=\lVert T^{(k)}(Y^{(k)}-Z^{(k)})({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)})^{\dagger}\lVert_{2}\\
&=\lVert T^{(k)}Z^{(k)}({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)})^{\dagger}\lVert_{2}\\
&\leq\lVert T^{(k)}Z^{(k)}\lVert_{2}\lVert({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)})^{\dagger}\lVert_{2}\\
&=\frac{\lVert T^{(k)}Z^{(k)}\lVert_{2}}{\sigma_{\min}({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)})}\\
&=\frac{\lVert Z^{(k)}\lVert_{2}}{\sigma_{\min}({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)})}.\end{split}

(41)

The above first and last equations are derived from the unitary invariance property. Using the fact that
\lVert(T^{(k)}S^{(k)})^{-1}\lVert_{2}=1/\sigma_{\min}(T^{(k)}S^{(k)})
for the first term of (
37
), we obtain the following bound

\displaystyle\lVert\widetilde{A}^{(k)}-A\lVert\leq\frac{\lVert Z^{(k)}\lVert_{2}}{\sigma_{\min}(T^{(k)}S^{(k)})\sigma_{\min}({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Omega^{(k)})}.

(42)

We now derive a probabilistic bound from (
41
) using concentration inequalities from random matrix theory. Since
S^{(k)}
can be seen as the
(r_{k-1}n_{k})\times(r_{k-1}n_{k}-r_{k})
submatrix of a Haar unitary matrix
(S^{(k)},S^{(k)}_{\perp})
, by unitary invariance property, we have

M^{(k)}:=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}T^{(k)}\\
{U^{(k)}_{Y}}^{\top}\end{smallmatrix}\Big](S^{(k)},S^{(k)}_{\perp}),

(43)

where
M^{(k)}
is also a Haar unitary matrix
[
22
]
, and
T^{(k)}S^{(k)}
is exactly the upper left
(r_{k-1}n_{k}-r_{k})\times(r_{k-1}n_{k}-r_{k})
corner of
M^{(k)}
.
Applying Lemma
10
in the Appendix, we have

\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma_{\min}(T^{(k)}S^{(k)})\geq\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{1}}}{\sqrt{r_{k}(r_{k-1}n_{k}-r_{k})}}\big)\geq 1-\delta_{1}\ ,

(44)

for
\forall\delta_{1}>0
. Since
{V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Sigma^{(k)}
is distributed as an
r_{k}\times r_{k}
Gaussian random matrix, by Lemma
11
in the Appendix, for any
0<\delta_{2}<1
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma_{\min}({V_{A}^{(k)}}^{\top}\Sigma^{(k)})\geq\frac{\sqrt{2\log(1/(1-\delta_{2}))+1}-1}{\sqrt{r_{k}}}\big)=1-\delta_{2}\ .

(45)

Combining the two probability estimates, with probability at least
1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}
,

\displaystyle\lVert\widetilde{A}^{(k)}-A\lVert\leq\frac{r_{k}\sqrt{(r_{k-1}n_{k}-r_{k})}\lVert Z^{(k)}\lVert_{2}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{1}\log(1/(1-\delta_{2}))+\delta_{1}}-\sqrt{\delta_{1}}}\ .

(46)

For the second term of (
33
), note that
\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)}
is distributed as an
l_{k}\times r_{k}
Gaussian random matrix. Since
l_{k}>r_{k}
, by Lemma
12
in the Appendix,
\forall\delta_{3}\in(0,1]
, with probability at least
1-\delta_{3}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\min}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})\geq\sqrt{l_{k}}-\sqrt{r_{k}}-\sqrt{2\log(2/\delta_{3})}.

(47)

Thus, with probability at least
1-\delta_{3}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert U_{Y}^{(k)}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\lVert_{2}&=\lVert(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})^{\dagger}(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\lVert_{2}\\
&\leq\frac{\lVert(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\lVert_{2}}{\sigma_{\min}(\Psi^{(k)}U_{Y}^{(k)})}\\
&\leq\frac{\lVert(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\lVert_{2}}{\sqrt{l_{k}}-\sqrt{r_{k}}-\sqrt{2\log(2/\delta_{3})}}\ .\end{split}

(48)

Combining (
46
) and (
48
), we finally get the error of the
k
-th sketching of unfolding matrix with probability at least
1-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}-\delta_{3}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert\hat{A}^{(k)}-A^{(k)}\lVert_{2}\leq&\frac{r_{k}\sqrt{(r_{k-1}n_{k}-r_{k})}\lVert Z^{(k)}\lVert_{2}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{1}\log(1/(1-\delta_{2}))+\delta_{1}}-\sqrt{\delta_{1}}}\\
&+\frac{\lVert(\widetilde{Z}^{(k)})\lVert_{2}}{\sqrt{l_{k}}-\sqrt{r_{k}}-\sqrt{2\log(2/\delta_{3})}}\ .\end{split}

(49)

Finally, by applying the triangle inequality to the tensor unfolding operations

\lVert\mathcal{A}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}\rVert_{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\lVert\hat{A}^{(k)}-A^{(k)}\lVert_{2}\ ,

(50)

we complete the proof.

4

To test the effectiveness of the proposed TT-subSKETCH algorithm, we compare it with the most related state-of-the-art algorithms TT-SVD and TT-rSVD. For ease of comparison, we set the power iteration parameter

(52)

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a two-sided sketching algorithm, named TT-subSKETCH, for TT approximation. Detailed error analysis and robustness analysis for the proposed algorithm TT-subSKETCH were conducted. Thorough experimental results on both synthetic and real-world data demonstrate that the proposed algorithm achieves superior accuracy and robustness compared to the related state-of-the-art random algorithms, while requiring significantly less time compared to deterministic algorithms.
