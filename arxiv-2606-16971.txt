Preliminaries and Organization

All of our notation (except perhaps the
d
-neighborhood notation) is standard. A simple digraph

and a set of arcs

. All digraphs in this paper are simple digraphs. A vertex

of

of

A

.

Let

.
For

For

. For a subset

and

. When

, we omit the subscript

,

, and

Monotonicity of
c_{\delta,q}

In this section, we prove the following.

\restatemono

We begin by introducing an asymptotic variation of this problem. Namely, let
\tilde{c}_{\delta,q}
be the smallest constant for which every
sufficiently large
digraph
D
with minimum in-degree
\delta
contains a
q
-kernel
Q
with
|Q|\leq\tilde{c}_{\delta,q}|V(D)|
. In more detail,

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\delta,q}\coloneqq\inf\bigg\{c>0:\exists N=N(c)\text{ such that all digraphs $D$ on at least $N$ vertices}

\displaystyle\text{with min in-degree $\delta$ have a $q$-kernel of size at most $c|V(D)|$}\bigg\}.

This variation is sometimes easier to work with and, as the next lemma shows, is equivalent.

Lemma 3.1

(asymptotic equivalence)
.

Let
q\geq 2
,
\delta\geq 1
be fixed integers. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a sequence of digraphs
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\{|V(D_{k})|\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing, the minimum in-degree of
D_{k}
is
\delta
, and the size of the smallest
q
-kernel in

is at least
(c_{\delta,q}-\varepsilon)|V(D_{k})|
. Hence
\tilde{c}_{\delta,q}\geq c_{\delta,q}
. Since
\tilde{c}_{\delta,q}\leq c_{\delta,q}
holds by definition, we deduce

c_{\delta,q}=\tilde{c}_{\delta,q}.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. If, for all
n
and all digraphs
D
on
n
vertices with minimum in-degree
\delta
, there exists a
q
-kernel of size less than
(c_{\delta,q}-\varepsilon)n
, then we have a contradiction with the definition of
c_{\delta,q}
. So, there is some

and some digraph
D
on
n
vertices with minimum in-degree
\delta
whose smallest
q
-kernel has size at least
(c_{\delta,q}-\varepsilon)n
. Now, for each

, let

be the digraph formed by taking

H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
. Note that the minimum in-degree of

remains at

. Consider a

-kernel

in

. Every vertex in

can be reached from

by a directed path of length

. But since

are disjoint for all

, it must be that every vertex in
H_{i}
is reached from

by a path contained in

. That is, each of the sets

is a

-kernel of

. Hence,

contains at least
(c_{\delta,q}-\varepsilon)n

. The sequence
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has the desired property.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(sink trick lemma)
.

Let
q\geq 2
and
\delta\geq 1
be positive integers and let
D
be a digraph on
n
vertices with minimum in-degree
\delta+1
. Then
D
contains a
q
-kernel
Q
with

|Q|\leq\left(c_{\delta,q}+\frac{1}{n}\right)n.

Proof.

Let
D
be a digraph on
n
vertices with minimum in-degree
\delta+1
. Add to
D
a sink
u
with in-degree exactly
\delta
, and let
D^{\prime}
denote the resulting digraph (the in-neighbors of

has minimum in-degree

-kernel

with

|Q^{\prime}|\leq c_{\delta,q}(n+1)=c_{\delta,q}n+c_{\delta,q}\leq c_{\delta,q}n+1=\left(c_{\delta,q}+\frac{1}{n}\right)n.

We claim that

is a

-kernel for

. Clearly

is independent in

. Let

. Then there exists a directed path in

from

to

of length at most

is a

-kernel for

.

We now prove Theorem

Proof.

Let

The modified first-phase Chvátal–Lovász algorithm

Instead of constructing
q
-kernels directly, our proofs often focus on constructing the following object which tends to be easier to work with.

Definition 4.1
.

A set
R\subseteq V(D)
is said to be a
q
-
prekernel
if
D[R]
is acyclic and
N^{\leq q}[R]=V(D)
.

This is almost the definition of a
q
-kernel except that we only require the set to be acyclic instead of independent. A
q
-prekernel naturally gives rise to a
(q+1)
-kernel as we show in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

If a digraph
D
contains a
q
-prekernel
R
, then it contains a
(q+1)
-kernel
Q
with
Q\subseteq R
. Furthermore, if
R
induces an arc, then
Q
is a proper subset of
R
and
|Q|<|R|
.

Proof.

Let
R
be a
q
-prekernel of
D
. Since
R
is acyclic, there exists an ordering

of the vertices of
R
such that all arcs
(v_{i},v_{j})
induced by

satisfy

. Initialize
Q=\emptyset
and
A=R
. While
A
is nonempty, let
i=\min\{j:v_{j}\in A\}
. Add
v_{i}
to
Q
and remove
N^{\leq 1}[v_{i}]
from
A
. Iterate this procedure until
A
is empty. We claim that the final set

is independent. Suppose not. Then there is some arc
(v_{i},v_{j})
in

with

. By the construction of

,
v_{i}
was added before
v_{j}
. But when
v_{i}
was added to
Q
,

was removed from

and could not have been chosen in any later step, a contradiction. For

, there exists a (shortest) directed path of length at most

from some

to

or

has an in-neighbor in

. In either case, we obtain a directed path of length at most

from

to

(the in-neighbor cannot lie on the path from

to

is a

-kernel. Notice that if

due to the removal of

for each

.
∎

Question 7.2
.

Let
k\geq 2

\delta

\delta

(k,2k-3)

For example, the answer is no when
k=2
(consider any tournament with minimum in-degree
\delta

(k,\ell)

(k,\ell)
-kernels exist. Since
(k,2k-2)

Question 7.3

k\geq 2
