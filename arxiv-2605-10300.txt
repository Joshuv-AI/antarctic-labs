Proof of a conjecture of Andrews and Bachraoui on a Hecke sum
Koustav Banerjee, Kathrin Bringmann

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a conjecture of Andrews and Bachraoui relating a generating function arising from two-color partitions (with odd smallest part and restrictions on the even parts) to a Hecke-type double sum. Our proof is based on Zwegers’ theory of indefinite theta functions together with modular transformation properties of mock theta functions.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

Andrews and Bachraoui
[
1
]
studied two-color partitions
C(k,n)
(
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
). To be more precise,
C(k,n)
counts the number of two-color partitions of
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with the following conditions:

(1)

the smallest part is odd and occurs at least once in blue,

(2)

every even blue part is at least
2k-1
greater than the smallest part,

(3)

the even parts of the same color are distinct.

Andrews–Bachraoui
[
1
, (1.1)]
showed that

C_{k}(q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}C\left(k,n\right)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(-q^{2n+2k},-q^{2n+2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}q^{2n+1}}{\left(q^{2n+1};q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}}.

Here, for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
,
(a)_{n}=(a;q)_{n}:=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{j})
denotes the usual
q
-Pochhammer symbol
. Andrews and Bachraoui
[
1
, Section 10]
showed that as
k\to\infty
, the generating function
C_{k}(q)
converges to a function

C(q)=\lim_{k\to\infty}C_{k}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(-q^{2n+2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}q^{2n+1}}{\left(q^{2n+1};q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}}.

They then represented
C(q)
as (using different notation)

C(q)=qD(q)S(q),

where

\displaystyle D(q)

\displaystyle:=\!\frac{\left(-q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}},\quad S(q):=\!\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q;q^{2}\right)^{2}_{n}q^{2n}}{\left(-q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n}}.

The function
S
is the main object in this paper.

To isolate the even coefficients of
S
we use a sieving operator. To be more precise, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
sieving operator

S_{N,r}
is defined on a
q
-series
f(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}c(n)q^{n}
by

f(q)\!\bigm|\!S_{N,r}:=\sum_{n\equiv r\ \left(\mathrm{mod}\,N\right)}c(n)q^{n},

Andrews and Bachraoui
[
1
, Conjecture 4]
proposed the following conjecture, relating a sieved generating function to a Hecke-type double sum.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\left(q^{4};q^{4}\right)_{\infty}S(q)\right)\bigm|S_{2,0}=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{6n^{2}+4n}\left(1+q^{4n+2}\right)\sum_{|j|\leq n}(-1)^{j}q^{-2j^{2}}.

In this paper, we prove Conjecture
1.1
. The proof proceeds by “completing” both sides of Conjecture
1.1
to real-analytic modular forms, using Zwegers’ theory of indefinite theta functions
[
8
]
. We then show that these extra terms cancel and finally use Sturm’s theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

The identity in Conjecture
1.1
holds.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the necessary background on modular forms, mock theta functions, and indefinite theta functions. Section
3
relates the right-hand side of Section
1.1
to an indefinite theta function. In Section
4
, we rewrite the left-hand side of Conjecture
1.1
. Section
5
establishes the required modular transformation properties. In Section
6
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
, using Sturm’s theorem. Finally, in Section
7
, we give a few questions for future research.

2.1.
Modular forms

We briefly introduce modular forms, for more background, we refer the reader to
[
6
]
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
is defined by

\Gamma_{0}\left(N\right):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\textnormal{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\ \left(\mathrm{mod}\,N\right)\right\}.

Definition
.

A holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a modular form of weight
k\in\mathbb{Z}
for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
if it satisfies the modular transformation property

(2.1)

f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=\left(c\tau+d\right)^{k}f(\tau)\ \ \text{for all}\ \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N).

Moreover, it is required to have at most polynomial growth at the “cusps” of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. We denote the complex vector space of modular forms of weight
k
over
\Gamma_{0}(N)
by
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
.

Remark
.

Although some of the functions occuring in this paper have natural “completions” to so-called harmonic Maass forms, we do not need this general formulation. We only require their explicit non-holomorphic completions and transformation properties.

We also use classical functions of half-integral weight, but only via their explicit transformations listed below. The
Dedekind eta-function

\eta
is defined by
(q=e^{2\pi i\tau})

\eta\left(\tau\right):=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1-q^{n}\right).

The modular transformations of

are given by (see
[
6
, Theorem 1.61]
)

(2.2)

\eta(\tau+1)=\zeta_{24}\eta(\tau),\qquad\eta\left(-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)=\sqrt{-i\tau}\eta(\tau),

where
\zeta_{N}:=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N}}
. Thus
\eta
transforms like a modular form of weight
\frac{1}{2}
(with a multiplier).

Next, define the
theta function

(2.3)

\Theta(\tau):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{n^{2}}.

We use the following transformation properties of
\Theta

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Theta(\tau+1)=\Theta(\tau),\quad\Theta\left(-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-i\tau}\Theta\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right).

Again

transforms like a modular form of weight of
\frac{1}{2}

We also require the following representation of

\displaystyle\Theta(\tau)=\frac{\eta^{5}(2\tau)}{\eta^{2}(\tau)\eta^{2}(4\tau)},\quad\Theta\left(\tau+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{\eta^{2}(\tau)}{\eta(2\tau)},\quad\frac{\eta^{2}\left(16\tau\right)}{\eta\left(8\tau\right)}=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{(2n+1)^{2}}.

7.
Open questions

We conclude by stating several directions for future research:

(1)

All of our functions are components of vector-valued harmonic Maass forms. Do the holomorphic parts of the other components have a combinatorial interpretation?

(2)

In Conjecture
1.1
even residue classes are studied. It might be worthwhile to study odd residue classes.

(3)

Can some of the results be extended to other mock theta function to obtain objects with combinatorial meaning.

(4)

Is there a direct proof of Conjecture
1.1
, e.g. by using Bailey pairs?
