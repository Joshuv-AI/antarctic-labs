Improved Approximation Guarantees for Groupwise Maximin Share Fairness
Georgios Amanatidis, Anna Korfiati, Evangelos Markakis, Christodoulos Santorinaios

Abstract
We study the problem of fairly allocating a set of indivisible goods to a set of n n agents with additive valuation functions. We focus on the very demanding notion of groupwise maximin share fairness ( gmms ), which requires that each agent i i receives value comparable to her maximin share, where the latter is computed with respect to any subset of agents that contains i i . We show that it is possible to compute ( ϕ − 1 ) (\phi-1) -approximate gmms allocations in polynomial time, where ϕ ≈ 1.618 \phi\approx 1.618 is the golden ratio. This improves on the previously known guarantee of 4 / 7 4/7 of 9 and 4 . We propose a simple algorithm that maintains the same main properties as the Draft-and-Eliminate algorithm of 4 and we improve on the approximation guarantee analysis by carefully bounding the maximin share value within any subinstance induced by the restriction of our allocation to a subset of agents. Our analysis is asymptotically tight for algorithms that share these properties and has the additional benefit of giving improved guarantees for restricted settings; in particular, when the agents agree on the top- n n goods or when the number of agents is small. To illustrate the challenges of going beyond the guarantees of our algorithm, we also present a variant with an improved approximation of ( 10 − 1 ) / 3 ≈ 0.72 (\sqrt{10}-1)/3\approx 0.72 for the case of three agents. To achieve this improvement we partially characterize the maximin share guarantees of short picking sequences for a small number of goods.

Introduction

Fair division is concerned with the problem of partitioning a set of resources among agents with diverse preferences in a manner that is considered
fair
by the agents. The study of this fundamental problem has a rich history, originating in the work of
23

divisible

[
23
]

[

24

In the indivisible setting, strong fairness guarantees are in general unattainable: when a single valuable item must be assigned to one of several agents, no allocation can be fair to all. This has motivated the study of appropriate
relaxations
, most notably
envy-freeness up to one good
(
\textsc{ef}{1}
)
[
19
;
7
]
,
envy-freeness up to any good
(

)
[

]

(

)
[

]

n
bundles (where
n
is the total number of agents) and receiving her least preferred one; an
mms
allocation assigns every agent at least her maximin share. While such allocations need not always exist
[
17
;
12
]
, a flourishing line of work has established that constant-factor approximations are always achievable
[
17
;
15
;
16
]
. A natural strengthening of

is

(

This stronger requirement captures collective fairness on a richer level, yet its approximability is significantly less understood. In this paper, we study the existence and efficient computation of approximate
gmms
allocations for agents with additive valuation functions.

Note that even checking whether an allocation is approximately
gmms
seems nontrivial, and this is not just because computing the maximin shares is hard; even if we had an oracle for the latter, it is still unclear how to efficiently verify that
mms
guarantees hold for every single subset of agents. Given this, it is surprising that there are any positive results on the existence and computation of allocations that approximately satisfy
gmms

1/2
-

4/7

[

;
4

Technical Considerations.
Our main algorithm itself (
Algorithm

1
) is relatively simple, following the general framework
of the state-of-the-art algorithms for computing approximately

Our main contribution here is our novel analysis that tightly bounds the proportional share after performing a series of reductions on an extended set of “problematic” items.
Interestingly, our analysis can be transferred with small adaptations to either one

(\phi-1)
-

In our proofs we often use the

(i.e., the average value) of an agent, restricted to any given subset of agents and the union of their bundles, as a

As an additional step, we use known monotonicity properties of the maximin share to reduce our instances to “nicer” ones. The most standard such reduction is to ignore all agents who have a bundle consisting of a single good
[
6
]

5

To improve our results for

Preliminaries

A discrete fair division instance can be described by the triplet
(N,M,V)
where
N=[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
is a set of
n
agents and
M
a set of
m
indivisible items. The set
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
corresponds to the
additive
valuation functions of the agents, i.e.,
v_{i}(S)=\sum_{g\in S}v_{i}(g)
. We assume that all the items are
goods
, i.e.,
v_{i}(g)\geq 0
for every agent
i\in N
and item
g\in M
.
Hence, the valuation functions are also
monotone
. For the sake of readability, in what follows, we write
v_{i}(g)
instead of
v_{i}(\{g\})
, for
g\in M
.

A complete
allocation
of
M
to the
n
agents is a partition,
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
, where
A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset
and
\cup_{i}A_{i}=M
.
By
\Pi_{n}(M)
we denote the set of all partitions of a set
M
into
n
bundles. If
\mathcal{A}
is a partial allocation we use
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}):=M\setminus\bigcup_{i\in N}A_{i}
to denote the
pool
of unallocated items.
As a first step in our main algorithm, we use a partial allocation
\mathcal{F}
, such that
|F_{i}|\leq 1
, for all
i\in N
, and the product
\prod_{i\in N}v_{i}(F_{i})
is maximized. We refer to
\mathcal{F}
as a
NSW-maximizing matching allocation
, as it corresponds to a matching between agents and items that maximizes the Nash social welfare. Such allocations can be computed efficiently
[
11
]
.

In fair division, most fairness notions can be categorized into two large families:
share-based
and
envy-based
notions. In the former category, agents perceive fairness by comparing their bundles to the complete set of items while in the latter they compare bundles with each other. As our work focuses on a notion that combines both aspects in a very strong sense, we provide below the necessary definitions from both areas, as well as some tools originally developed for envy-based notions.

2.1
Share-based fairness notions

Definition 1
.

Given
n
agents, and a subset
S\subseteq M
of goods, the
n
-maximin share of agent

with respect to

is
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,S)=\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{A}\in\Pi_{n}(S)}\min_{A_{j}\in\mathcal{A}}v_{i}(A_{j})
.

From the definition, it follows that
n\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,S)\leq v_{i}(S)
.
When
S=M
, this quantity is just called the
maximin share
of agent
i
.
An allocation
\mathcal{A}
is an
n
-maximin share defining partition
for agent

\min_{A_{j}\in\mathcal{A}}v_{i}(A_{j})=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M)
.
When it is clear from context what

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}
instead of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M)
.

Maximin share fairness

asks for a partition that gives each agent her (approximate) maximin share.

Definition 2
.

An allocation

\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
is called an
\alpha

mms

\alpha

v_{i}(A_{i})\geq\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}\,
, for every
i\in N
.

Variations of maximin share fairness have also been proposed. Here, we focus on a strengthening of the notion, by demanding an allocation to have an
mms

of agents. That is, for an agent

and any
S

and requesting to receive at least her
|S|
-maximin share of this bundle as if it were reallocated among
S
. This is referred to as

, introduced by
5
.

Definition 3
.

An allocation
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
is called an
\alpha
-
gmms

\alpha

v_{i}(A_{i})\geq\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(|N^{\prime}|,\cup_{j\in N^{\prime}}A_{j})
.

In
Definitions

2
and
3

\alpha=1
, we refer to the corresponding allocations as
mms
and
gmms
allocations respectively.
It is clear that the notion of

mms

Before proceeding to the envy-based notions we present some useful lemmas we will repeatedly invoke in the sequel. The first is a monotonicity lemma that allows removing agents and items from an instance, without reducing the maximin share of an agent. Similar statements are standard in the literature of
mms
allocations and are used to reduce general instances to more structured ones.

Lemma 1

(
3
)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}\in\Pi_{n}(M)
is an
n
-maximin share defining partition for agent
i
.
Then, for any set of goods
S
, such that there exists some
j

S\subseteq A_{j}
, it holds that
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n-1,M\setminus S)\geq\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M)
.

An immediate corollary of this reduction lemma is that singleton bundles do not pose an obstacle to obtaining

6

Lemma

1
.

We conclude this section with another simple monotonicity lemma.
Intuitively, if we start with an
\alpha
-
mms
allocation and only agent
i

\alpha
-
mms
from agent
i

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})
be an allocation of
M^{\prime}=\cup_{j\in N}A_{j}\subsetneq M
such that
v_{i}(A_{i})\geq\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime})
for some agent

and some

Then, for any
g\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})

v_{i}(A_{i}\cup\{g\})\geq\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime}\cup\{g\})
.

Proof.

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that this is not the case, i.e.,

v_{i}(A_{i}\cup\{g\})<\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime}\cup\{g\})
.

By the given lower bound on

and additivity, this implies:

\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime})+v_{i}(g)\leq v_{i}(A_{i})+v_{i}(g)=v_{i}(A_{i}\cup\{g\})<\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime}\cup\{g\}).

(1)

Now, consider an
n
-maximin share defining partition
\mathcal{\bar{A}}
of
M^{\prime}\cup\{g\}
for agent
i
.
Assume, without loss of generality, that
g\in\bar{A}_{1}
.
We construct a partition
\mathcal{B}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})
of

B_{1}=\bar{A}_{1}\setminus\{g\}
and
B_{j}=\bar{A}_{j}
for all

•

v_{i}(B_{1})=v_{i}(\bar{A}_{1})-v_{i}(g)\geq\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime}\cup\{g\})-v_{i}(g)

•

v_{i}(B_{j})=v_{i}(\bar{A}_{j})\geq\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime}\cup\{g\}),\quad\forall j\in N\setminus\{1\}

Thus,
\min_{j\in N}v_{i}(B_{j})\geq\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime}\cup\{g\})-v_{i}(g)
. The existence of

implies

and, therefore,

\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime}\cup\{g\})\leq\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime})+\alpha\,v_{i}(g)\leq\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}_{i}(n,M^{\prime})+v_{i}(g),

which contradicts (

2.2
Envy-based fairness notions

We begin our exposition with the notion of envy.

Definition 4

(
\alpha
-envy)
.

For any
\alpha\geq 1
, we say that agent
i

v_{i}(A_{j})>\alpha\cdot v_{i}(A_{i})
.

When
\alpha=1
, we simply say that agent
i
envies agent
j
. To address the issue of allocating an indivisible item to two (or more) agents, envy-based notions involve the hypothetical removal of item(s) from agents’ bundles. This is reflected in the following definition.

Definition 5
.

An allocation
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
is an

a)

\alpha

\textsc{ef}{1}
allocation (
\alpha
-envy-free up to one good), if for every pair
i,j\in N

A_{j}\neq\emptyset
, there exists a good
g\in A_{j}
, such that

v_{i}(A_{i})\geq\alpha\cdot v_{i}(A_{j}\setminus\{g\})
.

b)

\alpha
-
efx
allocation (
\alpha
-envy-free up to any good), if for every pair
i,j\in N
, with
A_{j}\neq\emptyset
and every good
g\in A_{j}
, it holds that
v_{i}(A_{i})\geq\alpha\cdot v_{i}(A_{j}\setminus\{g\})
.

Of course, for
\alpha=1
we obtain precisely the notions of envy-freeness up to one good (
\textsc{ef}{1}
)
[
7
;
19
]
and envy-freeness up to any good (
efx
)
[
8
]
. In words,
\alpha
-
efx
dictates that agent
i
does not
\alpha
-envy any proper subset of agent’s
j
bundle while
\alpha
-
\textsc{ef}{1}
is satisfied if the envy vanishes for some maximal proper subset.

It is well known that
\textsc{ef}{1}
allocations can be computed in polynomial time, by
19
, with the celebrated Envy-Cycle-Elimination algorithm (
Algorithm

3
), which we also use as a crucial algorithmic component. This is by now a standard algorithm in the literature of fair division but, for the sake of completeness, we provide here a brief presentation and refer to
Appendix

A
for the full details. A central concept of this algorithm is the
envy graph
. Given a partial allocation
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
, the envy graph is a directed graph
G_{\mathcal{A}}=(N,E_{\mathcal{A}})
, where
(i,j)\in E_{\mathcal{A}}
if and only if agent
i
currently envies agent
j
, i.e.,
v_{i}(A_{i})<v_{i}(A_{j})
. The key observation of the algorithm is that a source node in
G_{\mathcal{A}}
corresponds to a non-envied agent in the partial allocation
\mathcal{A}
, and this agent can receive one item without disrupting the
\textsc{ef}{1}
property. If no such agent exists, then there is an envy cycle. By repeatedly reallocating the bundles to the agents backwards along envy cycles, the valuation of each agent (weakly) improves, until a source node eventually arises. The fact that the agents’ valuations do not decrease is crystallized in the following statement.

Theorem 1

(Follows by
19
)
.

Let
A_{i}
be the bundle assigned to an agent
i\in N
at the beginning of some iteration of the Envy-Cycle-Elimination algorithm. If
A_{i}^{\prime}
is assigned to
i
at the end of any future iteration, then
v_{i}(A_{i}^{\prime})\geq v_{i}(A_{i})
.

We will frequently need to reason about an agent’s value compared to some good in the pool of unallocated items. For this matter, the next definition will be helpful.

Definition 6

(
\alpha
-content)
.

For a partial allocation
\mathcal{A}
and any
\alpha\geq 1
, we say that agent

is
\alpha
-content with respect to
\mathcal{A}
if
v_{i}(A_{i})\geq\alpha\cdot v_{i}(g)
for any
g\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})
.

Throughout the execution of any of our algorithms, we assume that whenever the allocation
\mathcal{A}
changes,
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})
and
G_{\mathcal{A}}
are silently updated.

Discussion

In this work we show that

-
gmms
allocations always exist and can be efficiently computed, for any number of agents with additive valuation functions. For small values of
n
or for instances where the agents agree about their top-
n
goods, we obtain significant improvements. This is the first improvement on
gmms
allocations since the works of
9
and
4
, indicating how challenging the problem is. A main reason for this is that the notion of
gmms

gmms

gmms
and
efx

gmms

[

efx

efx
)
[
5
]
, the state-of-the-art approximation algorithms for the two notions are the same and, as it turns out now, with the same exact approximation ratio. Given the significance of
efx
-related questions in the recent development of the fair division literature, we believe that the connection between the two notions should be further explored.
