Local Energy Decay for Non-Stationary Damped Wave Operators
Nicholas Arsenault, Mihai Tohaneanu

Abstract
. The paper establishes integrated local energy decay (ILED) estimates for the damped wave equation on certain non-stationary spacetimes. The main technical result is a high frequency estimate that holds in great generality, provided that null geodesics trapped in a compact region are sufficiently damped.



1.
Introduction

The damped wave equation is used in physics as a model to describe various phenomena involving wave propagation in the presence of friction. A relevant example is the equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta_{g}u+a(x)\partial_{t}u=0.

Here
(M,g)
is a three-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
\Delta_{g}
is the associated Laplace-Beltrami operator, and
a\in C^{\infty}(M)
is the damping function satisfying
a(x)\geq 0
.

Let

E[u](T)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|{\partial}_{t}u(T,x)|^{2}+g^{ij}{\partial}_{i}u{\partial}_{j}udV_{g}\approx\int_{M}|{\partial}_{t,x}u(T,x)|^{2}dV_{g}

denote the associated energy. Multiplying (
1.3
) by
\partial_{t}u
and integrating by parts immediately yields that

E[u](T)=E[u](0)-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}a(x)|\partial_{t}u(t,x)|^{2}dxdt\leq E[u](0)

which establishes uniform boundedness of the energy.

A relevant question to ask is what conditions one needs to impose on
a(x)
so that the energy decays. In the case of compact manifolds
M
, one can prove exponential decay of the energy, under the geometric control condition (GCC), namely there is a fixed
L>0
so that every null bicharacteristic intersects the set
\{a(x)>0\}
in time less than
L
; see, for example,
[
15
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
17
]
. More precisely, one has

(1.2)

E[u](T)\leq Ce^{-ct}E[u](0)

for some constants
C
,
c
. When the assumption fails, assuming extra regularity on the initial data, one can generically prove that the energy decays at most logarithmically, though the rate could be better depending on various geometries; see for example
[
7
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
18
]
.

Things are considerably different if the manifold
M
is not compact. Even in the most basic case

(1.3)

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+a(x)\partial_{t}u=0,

(
1.2
) does not hold even if GCC does (though it does hold for the Klein-Gordon equation
[
6
]
).
More refined arguments yield pointwise decay and Strichartz estimates of the solution to (
1.3
)
[
26
]
,
[
14
]
.

In the present paper we will be interested in establishing integrated local energy estimates (ILED) for damped wave operators (
2.4
). Such estimates originated in the work of Morawetz
[
31
]
and have played a crucial role in understanding the dispersive properties of linear solutions to undamped waves, as well as global well-posedness and asymptotic behavior of solutions to nonlinear equations. There has been an enormous amount of work done in this direction for undamped operators, see the references in Section 2. On the other hand, local energy estimates for the damped wave operators and variable coefficients have also been recently studied, but only in the case of time-independent coefficients, see
[
5
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
16
]
.

Our main result Theorem
2.4
establishes ILED for damped wave operators with coefficients that may be allowed to depend on time. There are two parts to it. The first part, which is the main contribution of the paper, establishes a two point ILED estimate: with minimal assumptions on the coefficients (in particular no smallness assumptions), and assuming that the GCC holds (see Assumption
1
), we can control the local energy on a time interval
[0,T]
by the energy at initial and final time and lower order spacetime terms. The second part is an unconditional ILED result, under the additional assumption that the coefficients are almost stationary (see Assumption
2
). It would be interesting to understand to what degree Assumption
2
can be weakened, and if ILED holds without any smallness assumptions in this context.

2.
Preliminaries and Setup

2.1.
The Damped Wave Operator

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{4},g)
denote a Lorentzian manifold, where
g
is a metric with signature
(-,+,+,+)
. Throughout this work, we will study operators of the form

(2.4)

P=\Box_{g}+iaD_{t},

where
\Box_{g}=D_{\alpha}g^{\alpha\beta}D_{\beta}
and
D_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{i}\partial_{\alpha}
. We adopt the standard convention that Greek indices
\alpha,\beta,\ldots
range from
0
to
3
, while Latin indices
i,j,k,\ldots
range from
1
to
3
. The Einstein summation convention is used throughout.

The function
a\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is non-negative and positive on an open set; we call
a
the
damping function
. The presence of the damping term
iaD_{t}
models energy dissipation in the system and is crucial for obtaining decay estimates.

2.2.
Local Energy Norms

To study the behavior of solutions to the damped wave equation, we introduce several weighted mixed-norm spaces. We work with spaces
L^{p}L^{q}
, where the
L^{p}
norm is taken in the time variable and
L^{q}
in the spatial variables. For
R\geq 1
, let
A_{R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:R\leq\langle x\rangle\leq 2R\}
denote the dyadic annulus at scale
R
, where
\langle x\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
is the Japanese bracket. We also define

A_{\leq R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\langle x\rangle\leq 2R\}

A_{\geq R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:R\leq\langle x\rangle\}

Definition 2.1

(Local energy norms)
.

We define the local energy norm by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{LE}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times A_{2^{j}})},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{LE^{1}}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{LE}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-1}\partial u\|_{LE},

where
\partial=(\partial_{t},\nabla)
denotes the full space-time gradient.

For the inhomogeneous term in our wave equation, we require the dual-type norm

(2.7)

\|F\|_{LE^{*}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|\langle x\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}F\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times A_{2^{j}})},

and the mixed norm

(2.8)

\|F\|_{L^{1}L^{2}+LE^{*}}=\inf_{F=f_{1}+f_{2}}\left(\|f_{1}\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}+\|f_{2}\|_{LE^{*}}\right).

We also define by
LE[t_{0},t_{1}]
the
LE
norm restricted to the time interval
[t_{0},t_{1}]
and similarly for all the other norms.

We will be interested in establishing an integrated local energy estimate of the form

(2.9)

\|\partial u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}+\|u\|_{LE^{1}}\lesssim\|\partial u(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}+L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

and a similar estimate involving the
LE^{1}[t_{0},t_{1}]
and
LE^{*}[t_{0},t_{1}]
norms.

The first estimate of this kind was obtained by Morawetz for the Klein-Gordon equation
[
31
]
. In the self-adjoint case
P=\Box_{g}
, there are many such results for asymptotically flat,
small perturbations of the Minkowski space-time (
[
24
,
8
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
1
,
27
,
29
]
). Even for large perturbations, in the absence of trapping, (
2.9
) still sometimes holds, see for instance
[
4
]
,
[
28
]
. In the presence of trapping, (
2.9
) is known to fail, see
[
35
]
,
[
36
]
. However, a weaker form of (
2.9
) may hold if the trapping is normally hyperbolic, if one allows for a loss of derivatives. This is the case for the Schwarzschild
[
32
,

,

,

,

and Kerr
[

,

,

,

]
black hole backgrounds in General Relativity.

2.3.
Asymptotically Flat Metrics

To quantify how the metric
g
approaches the Minkowski metric
m=\text{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
at spatial infinity, we introduce the asymptotically flat norm.

Definition 2.2

(AF norm)
.

For a metric
g
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 3
, we define

(2.10)

\|g\|_{AF}=\sup_{0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 3}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|\langle x\rangle^{|\gamma|}\partial^{\gamma}g^{\alpha\beta}\|_{\ell_{j}^{1}L^{\infty}([0,T]\times A_{2^{j}})}.

More generally, for a cutoff region, we write
\|g\|_{AF_{>R_{0}}}
to denote the norm restricted to
|x|>R_{0}
, and
\|g\|_{AF(A_{2^{j}})}
for the norm restricted to the dyadic shell
A_{2^{j}}
.

Definition 2.3

(Asymptotically flat operator)
.

We say that
P
is
asymptotically flat
if
\|g-m\|_{AF}<\infty
.

In particular, we have the important bound

(2.11)

|\partial g^{\alpha\beta}(t,x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1}.

2.3.1.
Quantitative Parameters

For technical reasons, we need precise quantitative control on how the metric approaches the Minkowski metric. We introduce the following parameters that will be used throughout.

First, we fix a small constant
\mathbf{c}>0
and choose a radius
R_{0}>0
sufficiently large such that

(2.12)

\|g-m\|_{AF_{>R_{0}}}\leq\mathbf{c}\ll 1.

This ensures that outside a ball of radius
R_{0}
, the metric
g
is uniformly close to the Minkowski metric
m
.

Second, for each dyadic shell with
j>\log_{2}R_{0}
, we define coefficients
\{c_{j}\}
satisfying

(2.13)

\|g-m\|_{AF(A_{2^{j}})}\lesssim c_{j},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j}c_{j}\lesssim\mathbf{c}.

We may additionally assume that the sequence
\{c_{j}\}
satisfies a slow variation condition:

(2.14)

\frac{c_{j}}{c_{k}}\leq 2^{\delta|k-j|},\quad\text{for some}\quad\delta\ll 1.

This slow variation condition ensures that the rate of approach to the Minkowski metric does not oscillate too rapidly between adjacent dyadic scales, which is crucial for the propagation of estimates across different spatial regions.

These quantitative controls provide the foundation for treating
P
as a small perturbation of the flat wave operator in the far field, enabling the use of perturbative techniques in our analysis.

We will take
R_{0}
large enough so that the support of
a
is contained in
\{|x|\leq R_{0}\}
.

Finally, we let
\tilde{P}=\Box_{\tilde{g}}
be a small AF flat perturbation of
\Box_{g}
, in the sense that
\tilde{P}=\Box_{g}
when
|x|\geq R_{0}
, and so that (
2.9
) holds for
\tilde{P}
.

Timelike and Spacelike Foliation Assumptions

Two crucial geometric assumptions in our analysis are the following:

(1)

Uniformly timelike time-translation.
The coordinate vector field

\partial_{t}=\partial/\partial x^{0}
is uniformly timelike, i.e. there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

(2.15)

g(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})=g_{00}(t,x)\leq-c_{0}<0\qquad\text{for all }(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

(2)

Uniformly spacelike time-slices.
The hypersurfaces
\{t=\mathrm{const}\}
are
uniformly spacelike. Equivalently, the covector field
dt
is uniformly timelike with respect
to the dual metric
g^{-1}
, i.e. there exists
c_{1}>0
such that

(2.16)

g^{-1}(dt,dt)=g^{00}(t,x)\leq-c_{1}<0\qquad\text{for all }(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

Under these assumptions, the spatial part of the metric induces a uniformly elliptic operator. Specifically, the operator
D_{i}g^{ij}D_{j}
is uniformly elliptic in the sense that

(2.17)

g^{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\approx|\xi|^{2},\quad\text{for all }\xi\neq 0,

where the implicit constants are uniform in space and time. This ellipticity is essential for energy estimates and provides control on spatial derivatives.

2.4.
Cutoff Functions

To localize our analysis to different spatial regions, we introduce a family of smooth cutoff functions that will be used throughout the subsequent proofs.

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a non-increasing function satisfying

(2.18)

\chi(x)=\begin{cases}1,&|x|\leq 1,\\
0,&|x|>2,\end{cases}

with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
everywhere. For any radius
R>0
, we define the scaled cutoff functions

(2.19)

\displaystyle\chi_{<R}(|x|)

\displaystyle:=\chi\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right),\quad\text{(supported in $|x|\lesssim R$)}

(2.20)

\displaystyle\chi_{>R}(|x|)

\displaystyle:=1-\chi_{<R}(|x|),\quad\text{(supported in $|x|\gtrsim R$)}.

These functions allow us to separate contributions from the interior region
|x|<R
and the exterior region
|x|>R
.

Finally, we let
\chi_{R}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
denote a cutoff function satisfying
0\leq\chi_{R}\leq 1
with
\operatorname{supp}\chi_{R}\subset\{x:|x|\approx R\}
. This localizes to a neighborhood of the sphere of radius
R
and will be particularly useful when we perform dyadic decompositions.

The derivatives of these cutoff functions satisfy the natural bounds

(2.21)

|\partial^{\alpha}\chi_{<R}|+|\partial^{\alpha}\chi_{>R}|\lesssim_{\alpha}R^{-|\alpha|},\quad|\partial^{\alpha}\chi_{R}|\lesssim_{\alpha}R^{-|\alpha|},

which must be tracked carefully in commutator estimates.

2.5.
The Geometric Control Condition

Our analysis rests crucially on the
Geometric Control Condition (GCC)
, which we state precisely as:

Assumption 1

(Geometric Control Condition)
.

Fix
R_{0}
from (
2.12
). Then there exists
L>0
and
\delta>0
such that for
w\in\Sigma
and all
s_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
:
if
x_{[0,L]}(w)\subset\{|x|\leq R_{0}\}
, then

\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}a\bigl(x_{s}(w)\bigr)\,ds\geq\delta.

This is similar to the GCC of
[
16
]
.

This condition requires that

(1)

For any null-bicharacteristic and any time interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
of length
L
, if the trajectory stays in the interior region throughout that interval,

x_{I}(w)\subset\{|x|\leq R_{0}\},

then it must spend accumulate enough damping in the interval.

(2)

Whenever a null-bicharacteristic remains in
\{|x|\leq R_{0}\}
, the time gaps between visits to damping are uniformly bounded by
L
.

The crucial piece is the
uniformity
of the constants
L
and
\delta
over all null-bicharacteristics, in both forward and backward flow directions, and over all time windows. This uniform control prevents non-trapped null-bicharacteristics from staying in the interior region for arbitrarily long times without interacting with damping, and then leaving. This allows us to construct global escape functions with time-independent symbol bounds, leading to estimates with constants independent of
t
.

For the second part of our theorem, we will also need the following, which is the definition of
\epsilon-
slowly varying from
[
28
]
.

Assumption 2

(Slow variation in time)
.

There exists
0<\epsilon\ll 1
such that

(2.22)

\|\partial_{t}g\|_{AF}<\epsilon.

2.6.
The Main Result

We are now ready to state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 2.4
.

(i)

Let
P
be an asymptotically flat damped wave operator satisfying (
2.15
)- (
2.17
) and Assumption
1
. Then the following two point estimate holds:

\|u\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}+\|\partial u\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]L^{2}}\lesssim E[u](0)+E[u](T)+\|u\|_{L^{2}[0,T]L^{2}(A_{\leq R_{0}})}+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]+L^{1}L^{2}[0,T]}.

(ii)

If in addition we assume that Assumption
2
holds, ILED and uniform energy bounds hold. More precisely

(2.23)

\|u\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}+\|\partial u\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]L^{2}}\lesssim E[u](0)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]+L^{1}L^{2}[0,T]}.

All the implicit constants are independent of
T
.

Our theorem extends the result of Kofroth
[
19
]
to the nonstationary case. In the stationary case, an elementary compactness argument ensures that Assumption
1
is equivalent to all trapped null geodesics intersecting the damped set, which was the assumption used in
[
19
]
. In the nonstationary case, the latter assumption is not quite enough, as it is possible for geodesics to linger in a compact region for an arbitrary length of time. Another issue we need to contend with is the lack of a conserved coercive energy, which is why we obtain only a two point estimate in part (i) of Theorem
2.4
.

3.
High Frequency Analysis

The high-frequency regime constitutes the central technical challenge in establishing local energy decay for damped wave operators on non-stationary space-times. This difficulty stems from the phenomenon of trapping. In contrast to the low- and medium-frequency regimes, where energy either disperses to infinity or can be controlled by Carleman-type estimates with large parameters, high-frequency waves concentrate along null-bicharacteristics and can exhibit persistent localized energy when these trajectories avoid damped regions.

The fundamental obstacle is that trapped null-bicharacteristics may fail to interact sufficiently
with the damping. In the stationary setting studied in
[
19
]
, the temporal and spatial variables can be decoupled, allowing one to work on
T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Additionally, a compactness argument provides a uniform escape time for non-trapped null-bicharacteristics. However, in our non-stationary setting, the time-dependence of the phase space structure necessitates working on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{4}
rather than
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and loss of compactness in the temporal direction requires control for non-trapped null-bicharacteristics that linger in a compact set.

3.1.
Rescaling

It will be convenient to have a large constant
\gamma>0
in front of the damping term. Following
[
19
]
and
[
16
]
, we rescale the problem to

(3.24)

\tilde{P}\tilde{u}=\tilde{F}

where

\tilde{P}=D_{\alpha}\tilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}D_{\beta}+i\gamma\tilde{a}D_{t}

and

\begin{split}\tilde{u}(t,x)=\gamma^{-2}u(\gamma t,\gamma x)\\
\tilde{F}(t,x)=F(\gamma t,\gamma x)\\
\tilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}(t,x)=g^{\alpha\beta}(\gamma t,\gamma x)\\
\tilde{a}(x)=a(\gamma x)\\
\tilde{R}_{0}=\gamma R_{0}\end{split}

It is important to notice that Assumption
1
still holds under rescaling, with the same constants
L
and
\delta
. Moreover, we also have, due to (
2.11
) that

(3.25)

|{\partial}_{x}\tilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}(t,x)|=|\gamma({\partial}_{x}g^{\alpha\beta})(\gamma t,\gamma x)|\lesssim\|{\partial}_{x}g^{\alpha\beta}(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}}

with a constant that is independent of
\gamma
.
We will from now on consider (
3.25
), and drop the
\tilde{}
for convenience.

3.2.
The Positive Commutator Method

Our proof of the main high-frequency estimate follows the positive commutator method. The key idea is to construct a pseudodifferential operator
Q=q^{w}(t,x,\tau,\xi)
satisfying

\mathrm{Re}\langle i[P,Q]u,u\rangle+2\mathrm{Re}\langle\gamma aQu,u\rangle+\|\langle x\rangle^{-2}u\|_{LE}^{2}\gtrsim\|u\|_{LE^{1}}^{2}

where
\gamma>0
is large enough, and
a(x)
is the damping. At the symbol level, this translates to the following:

Lemma 3.1
.

There exist symbols
q\in S_{hom}^{1}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{4})
and
m\in S_{hom}^{0}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{4})
so that

H_{p}q+2\gamma\tau a(x)q+pm\gtrsim\langle x\rangle^{-2}(\tau^{2}+|\xi|^{2}),

where the implicit constant is independent of time
t
.

The proof proceeds through several steps:

Section
3.3
:

We establish fundamental properties of the bicharacteristic flow
\varphi_{s}:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}\to T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}
associated to the principal symbol
p(t,x,\tau,\xi)=\tau^{2}-2\tau g^{0j}\xi_{j}-g^{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}
. The time-dependence of
g
requires careful attention to uniformity in
t

Section
3.4
:

This constitutes the core innovation. For
L
-trapped null-bicharacteristics a symbol is constructed that accumulates damping seen either forward or backward in flow time. It is crucial that the constants in this estimate be uniform, and it is here that we use the GCC.

Section
3.5
:

For null-bicharacteristics escaping to spatial infinity, we follow the method in
[
19
]
, but adopt it to the non-stationary setting. In particular, in this region null-bicharacteristics exit
B(0,2R_{0})
within flow time
L
. We construct the symbol that measures how long the null-bicharacteristic will remain inside this ball. Finally, to handle error terms created we add an exterior correction coming from a modified Morawetz multiplier.

Section
3.6
:

After constructing the symbols on the
L
-trapped and interior non-trapped sets, we combine these constructions on each light-cone, providing the desired positivity on the characteristic set. To obtain positivity on the elliptic set we construct a Langrangian correction. Our proof deviates from that of
[
28
,
19
,
16
]
and does not rely on the symbol
q
being affine in
\tau
.

3.3.
Results on the Hamilton Flow

From the assumption that
\partial_{t}
is uniformly time-like, the signature of the metric, and the
co-factor expansion, we have that
g^{00}\lesssim-1
. Using this we divide the operator by

g^{00}
, so that, after doing so,
g^{00}=1
. Such an operation preserves the assumptions placed
on the coefficients of operator. Moving forward, we assume without loss of generality, that
g^{00}=1
and, since
g^{00}
was initially negative, we can denote
g^{ij}/g^{00}
by
-g^{ij}
. This
produces an operator having the symbol

(3.26)

\sigma(P)(t,x,\tau,\xi)=\tau^{2}-2\tau g^{0j}(t,x)\xi_{j}-g^{ij}(t,x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}.

Furthermore, denote the principal symbol of symmetric and skew-symmetric parts of
P
by

\displaystyle p(t,x,\tau,\xi)

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}-2\tau g^{0j}(t,x)\xi_{j}-g^{ij}(t,x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle s_{skew}(x,\tau,\xi)

\displaystyle=i\gamma\tau a(x).

Factoring the principal symbol of the symmetric part gives

p(t,x,\tau,\xi)=p^{+}(t,x,\tau,\xi)p^{-}(t,x,\tau,\xi),

where
p^{\pm}(t,x,\tau,\xi)=\tau-b^{\pm}(t,x,\xi)
. It can be seen that

(3.27)

b^{\pm}(t,x,\xi)=g^{0j}\xi_{j}\pm\sqrt{(g^{0j}(t,x)\xi_{j})^{2}+g^{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}.

Moreover for fixed
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle b^{\pm}(t,x,\eta\xi)=g^{0j}\eta\xi_{j}\pm\sqrt{(g^{0j}(t,x)\eta\xi_{j})^{2}+g^{ij}\eta^{2}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}=\eta b^{\pm}(t,x,\xi).

Thus,
b^{\pm}
is homogeneous of degree 1.

The symbol
p
generates a Hamilton flow,
\varphi_{s}(w)\in{\mathbb{R}}\crossproduct T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
, given by

\begin{cases}\dot{t}_{s}=\partial_{\tau}p(\varphi_{s}(w)),&\dot{\tau}_{s}=-\partial_{t}p(\varphi_{s}(w))\\
(\dot{x}_{s})_{k}=\partial_{\xi_{k}}p(\varphi_{s}(w)),&\dot{\xi}_{s}=\partial_{x_{k}}p(\varphi_{s}(w))\end{cases}

which for our operator, gives

\begin{cases}\dot{t}_{s}=2\tau_{s}-2g^{0j}(t_{s},x_{s})[\xi_{s}]_{j},&\dot{\tau}_{s}=-2\tau_{s}\partial_{t}g^{0j}(t_{s},x_{s})[\xi_{s}]_{j}-\partial_{t}g^{ij}(t_{s},x_{s})[\xi_{s}]_{i}[\xi_{s}]_{j}\\
(\dot{x}_{s})_{k}=-2\tau_{s}g^{0k}(t_{s},x_{s})-2g^{kj}(t_{s},x_{s})[\xi_{s}]_{j},&(\dot{\xi}_{s})_{k}=-2\tau_{s}\partial_{x_{k}}g^{0j}(t_{s},x_{s})[\xi_{s}]_{j}-\partial_{x_{k}}g^{ij}(t_{s},x_{s})[\xi_{s}]_{i}[\xi_{s}]_{j}.\end{cases}

Additionally, we denote the flow generated by
p^{\pm}
by
\varphi_{s}^{\pm}(w)=(t_{s}^{\pm}(w),x_{s}^{\pm}(w),\tau_{s}^{\pm}(w),\xi_{s}^{\pm}(w))
. This is a solution to the system

(3.28)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{t}^{+}_{s}&=1\\
\dot{\tau}^{+}_{s}&=\partial_{t}b^{+}(\varphi_{s}(w))\\
(\dot{x}_{s})^{+}_{k}&=p^{+}_{\xi_{k}}(\varphi_{s}(w))\\
(\dot{\xi}^{+}_{s})_{k}&=-p_{x_{k}}^{+}(\varphi_{s}(w))\\
(t^{+}_{0},\tau^{+}_{0},x^{+}_{0},\xi^{+}_{0})&=w\end{aligned}\right.

We now state a useful proposition that allows us to reduce the construction to the cosphere bundle.

Proposition 3.2
.

The Hamilton vector field
H_{p^{\pm}}
is homogeneous of degree
0
. That is, denoting dilation in the fiber by
M_{\lambda}(t,x,\tau,\xi)=(t,x,\lambda\tau,\lambda\xi)
,
\lambda>0
, then

M_{\lambda}^{*}(H_{p^{\pm}}|_{(t_{0},x_{0},\tau_{0},\xi_{0})})q=H_{p^{\pm}}|_{(t_{0},x_{0},\tau_{0},\xi_{0})}q.

Proof.

\displaystyle M^{*}_{\lambda}(H_{p^{\pm}}|_{(t_{0},x_{0},\tau_{0},\xi_{0})})q

\displaystyle=H_{p^{\pm}}|_{(t_{0},x_{0},\lambda\tau_{0},\lambda\xi_{0})}(q\circ M^{-1}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{\xi}p^{\pm})(t_{0},x_{0},\lambda\tau_{0},\lambda\xi_{0})\cdot(\nabla_{x}(q\circ M^{-1}_{\lambda}))(t_{0},x_{0},\lambda\tau_{0},\lambda\xi_{0})

\displaystyle\quad-(\nabla_{x}p^{\pm})(t_{0},x_{0},\lambda\tau_{0},\lambda\xi_{0})\cdot(\nabla_{\xi}(q\circ M^{-1}_{\lambda}))(t_{0},x_{0},\lambda\tau_{0},\lambda\xi_{0})

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{\xi}p^{\pm})(t_{0},x_{0},\tau_{0},\xi_{0})\cdot(\nabla_{x}q)(t_{0},x_{0},\tau_{0},\xi_{0})

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda(\nabla_{x}p^{\pm})(t_{0},x_{0},\tau_{0},\xi_{0})\cdot\lambda^{-1}(\nabla_{\xi}q)(t_{0},x_{0},\tau_{0},\xi_{0})

\displaystyle=H_{p^{\pm}}|_{(t_{0},x_{0},\xi_{0},\tau_{0})}.

Fix any smooth positive “radius”

Proposition 3.4

Every null-bicharacteristic for the flow generated by

Proof.

Let

Since

\Sigma
lies on exactly one of the two sheets

\Sigma^{\pm}
.
Fix one sign and work on
\Sigma^{\pm}
. For any smooth function

H_{p}f=\{p,f\}=\{p^{+}p^{-},f\}=p^{-}H_{p^{+}}f+p^{+}H_{p^{-}}f.

Restricting to

, where

(H_{p}f)|_{\Sigma^{\pm}}=p^{\mp}H_{p^{\pm}}f.

Finally, we note that GCC holds by a change of variables, since

Remark 3.5
.

In order to simplify notation, we will continue to use

Proposition 3.6
.

If there exist

,

such that

|x^{\pm}_{\pm s^{\prime}}(w)|\geq\max\{2R_{0},\ |x|+\delta\},

then
|x^{\pm}_{\pm s}(w)|\to\infty
as

(in particular, (
3.33
) holds for all

Proof.

We treat the forward flow
x_{s}^{+}(w)
associated to

p^{+}(t,x,\tau,\xi)=\tau-b^{+}(t,x,\xi),

and write
\varphi_{s}^{+}(w)=(t_{s},x_{s},\tau_{s},\xi_{s})
. The remaining sign choices are handled by the
same argument after reversing the flow parameter.

Since
b^{+}
is positively homogeneous of degree
1
in
\xi
, the quantity
\partial_{\xi}b^{+}
is
homogeneous of degree
0
in
\xi
. Consequently the spatial velocity

\dot{x}_{s}=-\partial_{\xi}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s})
depends only on the direction of
\xi_{s}
, and by
rescaling the initial covector we may assume
|\xi_{0}|=1
without loss of generality (the
x
–projection
of the bicharacteristic is unchanged).

Our aim is to prove a uniform convexity estimate for the function

r(s):=|x_{s}|^{2}

once the trajectory is outside a sufficiently large ball. Hamilton’s equations are

(3.34)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{t}_{s}&=\partial_{\tau}p^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\tau_{s},\xi_{s})=1,\\
\dot{x}_{s}&=\partial_{\xi}p^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\tau_{s},\xi_{s})=-\partial_{\xi}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s}),\\
\dot{\tau}_{s}&=-\partial_{t}p^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\tau_{s},\xi_{s})=\partial_{t}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s}),\\
\dot{\xi}_{s}&=-\partial_{x}p^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\tau_{s},\xi_{s})=\partial_{x}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s}).\end{aligned}\right.

Differentiating
r(s)
gives

\dot{r}(s)=2x_{s}\cdot\dot{x}_{s},\qquad\ddot{r}(s)=2|\dot{x}_{s}|^{2}+2x_{s}\cdot\ddot{x}_{s}.

We now invoke the asymptotic flatness hypotheses (uniformly in
t
) in the region
|x|\geq R_{0}
.
In particular, for
|x|\geq R_{0}
and
|\xi|=1
one has the standard symbol asymptotics

(3.35)

\partial_{\xi}b^{+}(t,x,\xi)=\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}+O(|x|^{-1}),\qquad\partial_{t\xi}b^{+}(t,x,\xi)=O(|x|^{-2}),\qquad\partial_{x\xi}b^{+}(t,x,\xi)=O(|x|^{-2}),

together with

(3.36)

\partial_{x}b^{+}(t,x,\xi)=O(|x|^{-2}),\qquad\partial_{\xi\xi}b^{+}(t,x,\xi)=O(1),

where all implicit constants are independent of
t
. The first relation in (
3.35
)
implies that, after enlarging
R_{0}
if necessary, there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

(3.37)

|\dot{x}_{s}|=\bigl|\partial_{\xi}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s})\bigr|\geq c_{0}\qquad\text{whenever }|x_{s}|\geq R_{0}.

Next we estimate
\ddot{x}_{s}
. Differentiating
\dot{x}_{s}=-\partial_{\xi}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s})
along the flow
and using
\dot{t}_{s}=1
gives

\ddot{x}_{s}=-\partial_{t\xi}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s})-\partial_{x\xi}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s})\,\dot{x}_{s}-\partial_{\xi\xi}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s})\,\dot{\xi}_{s}.

Substituting
\dot{\xi}_{s}=\partial_{x}b^{+}(t_{s},x_{s},\xi_{s})
from (
3.34
), and using
(
3.35
)–(
3.36
) together with the bound

from

3.37
), we obtain

|\ddot{x}_{s}|\lesssim|x_{s}|^{-2}\qquad\text{whenever }|x_{s}|\geq R_{0},

hence

(3.38)

|x_{s}\cdot\ddot{x}_{s}|\leq|x_{s}|\,|\ddot{x}_{s}|\lesssim|x_{s}|^{-1}\qquad\text{whenever }|x_{s}|\geq R_{0}.

Combining (
3.37
) and (
3.38
) yields, for
|x_{s}|\geq R_{0}
,

\ddot{r}(s)=2|\dot{x}_{s}|^{2}+2x_{s}\cdot\ddot{x}_{s}\geq 2c_{0}^{2}-C|x_{s}|^{-1}.

Choosing
R_{0}
large enough so that

gives a uniform constant

with

(3.39)

\ddot{r}(s)\geq c\qquad\text{whenever }|x_{s}|\geq R_{0}.

Crucially, all constants here are independent of the time variable because the asymptotic flatness
bounds are uniform in
t
.

With (
3.39
) in hand, we now locate a time at which
r
is already outside

R_{0}^{2}
and has positive derivative. By hypothesis,
|x_{s^{\prime}}|\geq\max\{2R,|x|+\delta\}
and
R\geq R_{0}
,
so
r(s^{\prime})\geq(2R_{0})^{2}>R_{0}^{2}
and
r(s^{\prime})>r(0)
.

If
r(s)>R_{0}^{2}
for every

, then the mean value theorem gives some
s^{\prime\prime}\in(0,s^{\prime})
with

\dot{r}(s^{\prime\prime})=\frac{r(s^{\prime})-r(0)}{s^{\prime}}>0,

and in particular
|x_{s^{\prime\prime}}|>R_{0}
. Otherwise, define

\alpha:=\sup\{\,s\in[0,s^{\prime}):\ r(s)\leq R_{0}^{2}\,\}.

Continuity of
r
implies
\alpha<s^{\prime}
and
r(\alpha)=R_{0}^{2}
, while
r(s)>R_{0}^{2}
for all

Applying the mean value theorem on
(\alpha,s^{\prime})
produces
s^{\prime\prime}\in(\alpha,s^{\prime})
such that

\dot{r}(s^{\prime\prime})=\frac{r(s^{\prime})-r(\alpha)}{s^{\prime}-\alpha}=\frac{r(s^{\prime})-R_{0}^{2}}{s^{\prime}-\alpha}>0,

and again
|x_{s^{\prime\prime}}|>R_{0}
.

Thus in all cases we have found
s^{\prime\prime}\in(0,s^{\prime})
with

(3.40)

|x_{s^{\prime\prime}}|>R_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\dot{r}(s^{\prime\prime})>0.

We now propagate this information forward. Since
|x_{s^{\prime\prime}}|>R_{0}
, the convexity bound
(
3.39
) applies at
s^{\prime\prime}
. Moreover, as long as
|x_{s}|\geq R_{0}
we have

\ddot{r}(s)\geq c>0
, so
\dot{r}
is strictly increasing. In particular, (
3.40
)
implies
\dot{r}(s)\geq\dot{r}(s^{\prime\prime})>0
for all
s\geq s^{\prime\prime}
for which

. This forces
r(s)

to be strictly increasing on such intervals, and therefore

for all

.
Consequently
|x_{s}|>R_{0}
for every
s\geq s^{\prime\prime}
, so the lower bound (
3.39
) holds
globally for all
s\geq s^{\prime\prime}
.

Integrating (
3.39
) twice for
s\geq s^{\prime\prime}
yields

\dot{r}(s)\geq\dot{r}(s^{\prime\prime})+c(s-s^{\prime\prime}),\qquad r(s)\geq r(s^{\prime\prime})+\dot{r}(s^{\prime\prime})(s-s^{\prime\prime})+\frac{c}{2}(s-s^{\prime\prime})^{2}.

The quadratic term forces

as

. Since

,
the same conclusion holds in particular for all

.

3.4.
L
-trapped Symbol Construction

We begin by constructing a symbol on the
L
-trapped set
\mathcal{A}^{\pm}_{L}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Define

\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(w):=a(x_{s}^{\pm}(w)),

and

q^{\pm}(w):=\int_{0}^{L}(L-s)\big(\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(w)-\alpha_{-s}^{\pm}(w)\big)\,ds.

Then there exist neighborhoods
V^{\pm}
of
\mathcal{A}_{L}^{\pm}
in
\Sigma^{\pm}
and constants
C,c>0
, such that

H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}+Ca\geq c{\mathbf{1}}_{V^{\pm}}.

Proof.

Let
U^{\pm}
be an open neighborhood of
\mathcal{A}_{L}^{\pm}
in
\Sigma^{\pm}
. For each fixed
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, set

\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(w):=a(x_{s}^{\pm}(w))=a(\varphi_{s}^{\pm}(w)).

Using the flow property

\varphi_{s}^{\pm}(\varphi_{h}^{\pm}(w))=\varphi_{s+h}^{\pm}(w),

we obtain

\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(\varphi_{h}^{\pm}(w))=\alpha_{s+h}^{\pm}(w),

hence

H_{p^{\pm}}\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(w)=\partial_{s}\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(w),\qquad H_{p^{\pm}}\alpha_{-s}^{\pm}(w)=-\partial_{s}\alpha_{-s}^{\pm}(w).

Therefore

H_{p^{\pm}}\big(\alpha_{s}^{\pm}-\alpha_{-s}^{\pm}\big)=\partial_{s}\big(\alpha_{s}^{\pm}+\alpha_{-s}^{\pm}\big).

Differentiating under the integral and integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}(L-s)\,\partial_{s}\big(\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(w)+\alpha_{-s}^{\pm}(w)\big)\,ds

\displaystyle=\Big[(L-s)\big(\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(w)+\alpha_{-s}^{\pm}(w)\big)\Big]_{s=0}^{s=L}+\int_{0}^{L}\big(\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(w)+\alpha_{-s}^{\pm}(w)\big)\,ds

\displaystyle=-2L\,\alpha_{0}^{\pm}(w)+\int_{0}^{L}\alpha_{s}^{\pm}(w)\,ds+\int_{0}^{L}\alpha_{-s}^{\pm}(w)\,ds

\displaystyle=-2L\,a(w)+\int_{0}^{L}a(x_{s}^{\pm}(w))\,ds+\int_{0}^{L}a(x_{-s}^{\pm}(w))\,ds.

Set

U_{0}^{\pm}:=U^{\pm}\cap\Bigl\{w\in\Sigma^{\pm}:\ a(w)\leq\frac{\delta}{4L}\Bigr\}.

Fix
w_{0}\in\Omega_{R,L}^{\pm}\cap U_{0}^{\pm}
. If
w_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{L}^{f,\pm}
, then by definition

x^{\pm}_{[0,L]}(w_{0})\subset\{|x|\leq R\},

so GCC gives

\int_{0}^{L}a(x_{s}^{\pm}(w_{0}))\,ds\geq\delta.

If instead
w_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{L}^{b,\pm}
, then

x^{\pm}_{[-L,0]}(w_{0})\subset\{|x|\leq R\},

so GCC gives

\int_{0}^{L}a(x_{-s}^{\pm}(w_{0}))\,ds\geq\delta.

In either case,

H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}(w_{0})\geq\delta-2L\cdot\frac{\delta}{4L}=\frac{\delta}{2}.

By continuity, there exists an open neighborhood
U_{w_{0}}\subset U^{\pm}
of
w_{0}

such that

H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}(w)\geq\frac{\delta}{4}\qquad\text{for all }w\in U_{w_{0}}\cap U_{0}^{\pm}.

Define

V_{0}^{\pm}:=\Bigl(\bigcup_{w_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{L}^{\pm}\cap U_{0}^{\pm}}U_{w_{0}}\Bigr)\cap U_{0}^{\pm}.

Then
V_{0}^{\pm}
is an open neighborhood of

\mathcal{A}_{L}^{\pm}\cap\Bigl\{a\leq\frac{\delta}{4L}\Bigr\}

in
U^{\pm}
, and

H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}\geq\frac{\delta}{4}\qquad\text{on }V_{0}^{\pm}.

Now consider the complementary region

V_{1}^{\pm}:=U^{\pm}\cap\Bigl\{w\in\Sigma^{\pm}:\ a(w)\geq\frac{\delta}{4L}\Bigr\}.

Since
a\geq 0
, the identity above implies

H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}(w)\geq-2L\,a(w).

Hence

H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}(w)+Ca(w)\geq(C-2L)a(w).

Using
a(w)\geq\delta/(4L)
on
V_{1}^{\pm}
, we obtain

H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}(w)+Ca(w)\geq(C-2L)\frac{\delta}{4L}.

It suffices to choose
C=3L
, in which case

H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}+Ca\geq\frac{\delta}{4}\qquad\text{on }V_{1}^{\pm}.

Finally set

V^{\pm}:=V_{0}^{\pm}\cup V_{1}^{\pm},\qquad c:=\frac{\delta}{4}.

Then
V^{\pm}
is an open neighborhood of
\mathcal{A}_{L}^{\pm}
in
\Sigma^{\pm}
and

H_{p^{\pm}}q^{\pm}+Ca\geq c{\mathbf{1}}_{V^{\pm}}

This proves the lemma.

The proof proceeds by decomposing solutions into low, medium, and high time-frequency components, applying the previous estimates to each piece, and then controlling the resulting commutator errors.

Fix thresholds

The time-frequency symbols are then defined by

q_{l}(\tau):=\eta(\tau/\tau_{l}),\qquad q_{\leq h}(\tau):=\eta(\tau/\tau_{h}),

|\tau|\lesssim\tau_{l}

q_{m}

q_{h}(\tau)=1\quad\text{for }|\tau|\geq\tau_{h}\qquad\text{and}\qquad q_{h}(\tau)=0\quad\text{for }|\tau|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\tau_{h}.

For clean separation one may assume
\tau_{l}\leq\tau_{h}/4
so that
q_{m}\geq 0

Quantizing via the Weyl calculus gives operators

Q_{l}:=\text{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(q_{l}),\qquad Q_{m}:=\text{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(q_{m}),\qquad Q_{h}:=\text{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(q_{h}),

\|u_{h}\|_{LE^{1}}\lesssim\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(|x|\leq 2R_{0})}+\|Pu_{h}\|_{LE^{*}}\lesssim\tau_{h}^{-1}\|u_{h}\|_{LE^{1}}+\|Pu_{h}\|_{LE^{*}}

\tau_{h}
large enough, we obtain

\|u_{h}\|_{LE^{1}}\lesssim\|Pu_{h}\|_{LE^{*}}

Similarly, Theorem
5.2
and Plancherel’s formula yield

\|u_{l}\|_{LE^{1}}\lesssim\|\partial_{t}u_{l}\|_{LE^{1}_{c}}+\|Pu_{l}\|_{LE^{*}}\lesssim\tau_{l}\|u_{l}\|_{LE^{1}}+\|Pu_{l}\|_{LE^{*}}

and by picking
\tau_{l}
small enough, we obtain

\|u_{l}\|_{LE^{1}}\lesssim\|Pu_{l}\|_{LE^{*}}

Finally, choosing
\delta>0
small enough (depending on
\tau_{h}
and
\tau_{l}
) in Theorem
5.1
, we obtain

\|u_{m}\|_{LE^{1}}\lesssim\|Pu_{m}\|_{LE^{*}}

We now use Proposition 7.2 in
[
28
]
to control the commutators, which states that

\|[P,\eta^{w}]u\|_{LE^{*}}\lesssim\epsilon\left(\|u\|_{LE^{1}}+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}}\right).

We note here that since the damping term
a(x)
is time-independent, the damping term commutes with
\eta^{w}
.

After rescaling, one easily obtains for all
i\in\{l,m,h\}
that

\|[P,Q_{i}]\|_{LE^{*}}\lesssim\epsilon\left(\|u\|_{LE^{1}}+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}}\right)

For
\epsilon
small enough the commutators can thus be absorbed on the left hand side. This finishes the proof.

∎

5.1.
Local Energy Decay with Initial Data

We now establish the full local energy decay estimate, which incorporates initial data. Once again, we will use the ideas from
[
30
]
.

It is enough prove the two-point estimate

(5.68)

\|u\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}\lesssim E[u](0)+E[u](T)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]+L^{1}L^{2}[0,T]}.

Indeed, due to Assumption
2
and energy estimates, we obtain

E[u](T)\lesssim\|\partial u\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]L^{2}}\lesssim E[u](0)+C(\epsilon)\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]+L^{1}L^{2}[0,T]}+\epsilon\left(\|u\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}+\|\partial u\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]L^{2}}\right)

and the last term can be absorbed for small enough
\epsilon
.

Assume first that
Pu\in LE^{*}
; we will show that

(5.69)

\|u\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}\lesssim E[u](0)+E[u](T)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}.

We will construct a parametrix
v
satisfying
u[0]=v[0]
,
u[T]=v[T]
and

(5.70)

\|v\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}+\|P(u-v)\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}\lesssim E[u](0)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}

The conclusion (
5.69
) now follows after applying Theorem
5.3
to
u-v
.

We first construct an approximate solution in the exterior region
|x|\geq R_{0}
.

Let
v_{out}^{0}
be the solution to

\tilde{P}v_{out}^{0}=Pu,\quad v_{out}^{0}[0]=u[0].

Since local energy estimates hold for the operator
\tilde{P}
, we have

\|v_{out}^{0}\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}+\|\partial v_{out}^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]L^{2}}\lesssim E[u](0)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}

Similarly, let
v_{out}^{T}
to be the solution to

\tilde{P}v_{out}^{T}=Pu,\quad v_{out}^{0}[T]=u[T].

which satisfies

\|v_{out}^{T}\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}+\|\partial v_{out}^{T}\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]L^{2}}\lesssim E[u](T)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]+L^{1}L^{2}[0,T]}

We now let
\chi_{T}(t)
be identically
1
for
0\leq t\leq T/2
and supported in
t\leq 3T/4
. Define

v_{out}=\chi_{>R_{0}}(|x|)\left(\chi_{T}(t)v_{out}^{0}+(1-\chi_{T}(t))v_{out}^{T}\right).

Clearly

(5.71)

\|v_{out}\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}+\|\partial v_{out}\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]L^{2}}\lesssim E[u](0)+E[u](T)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}

We now construct
v_{in}
to be an approximate solution in the interior region
|x|\leq R_{0}
. WLOG we assume that
T
is an integer, and write
[0,T]=\cup_{k=0}^{T-1}[k,k+1]
. Let
v_{k}
solve

Pv_{k}=(1-\chi_{>R_{0}}(|x|))Pu

so that

v_{0}[0]=(1-\chi_{>R_{0}}(|x|))u[0]

v_{T-1}[T]=(1-\chi_{>R_{0}}(|x|))u[T]

v_{k}[k]=0,\quad 1\leq k\leq T-1

Let
\chi_{k}
be a partition of unity subordinate to
[k,k+2]
so that
\sum_{k}\chi_{k}(t)=1
, and define

v_{in}=\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\chi_{k}(t)v_{k}

By finite speed of propagation and local-in-time energy estimates we have

(5.72)

\|v_{in}\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}+\|\partial v_{in}\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]L^{2}}\lesssim E[u](0)+E[u](T)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}

Finally, we let

v=v_{in}+v_{out}.

By construction,
v[0]=u[0]
and
v[T]=u[T]
. Moreover, (
5.71
) and (
5.72
) immediately give

\|v\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}+\|\partial v\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]L^{2}}\lesssim E[u](0)+E[u](T)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}

Finally, we see that

P(u-v)=[P,\chi_{>R_{0}}(|x|)\chi_{T}(t)]v_{out}^{0}+[P,\chi_{>R_{0}}(|x|)(1-\chi_{T}(t))]v_{out}^{T}+\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}[P,\chi_{k}(t)]v_{k}

and we estimate

\|[P,\chi_{>R_{0}}(|x|)\chi_{T}(t)]v_{out}^{0}\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}\lesssim\|v_{out}^{0}\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}\lesssim E[u](0)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}

\|[P,\chi_{>R_{0}}(|x|)(1-\chi_{T}(t))]v_{out}^{T}\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}\lesssim\|v_{out}^{T}\|_{LE^{1}[0,T]}\lesssim E[u](T)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}

For the interior term we have when
1\leq k\leq T-2

\|[P,\chi_{k}(t)]v_{k}\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}\lesssim\|v_{k}\|_{LE^{1}[k,k+2]}\lesssim\|Pv_{k}\|_{LE^{*}[k,k+2]}\lesssim\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[k,k+2]}.

Moreover

\|[P,\chi_{0}(t)]v_{0}\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}\lesssim E[u](0)+\|v_{0}\|_{LE^{1}[0,2]}\lesssim E[u](0)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,2]}

\|[P,\chi_{T-1}(t)]v_{T-1}\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}\lesssim E[u](T)+\|v_{T-1}\|_{LE^{1}[T-2,T]}\lesssim E[u](T)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[T-2,T]}

and after summation

\|\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}[P,\chi_{k}(t)]v_{k}\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}\lesssim E[u](0)+E[u](T)+\|Pu\|_{LE^{*}[0,T]}

This finishes the proof of (
5.69
).

If
Pu\in L^{1}L^{2}
we use the parametrix from the previous section and (
5.69
) to obtain the desired estimate.
