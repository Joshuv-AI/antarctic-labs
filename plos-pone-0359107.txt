Design and implementation of a Wireless Power Transfer with double-sided LCC compensation for a farming robot

Aryadharma Sudhartio, Marojahan Tampubolon, Gervasius Geovan, M B Nugraha, Ahmad Syahril Muharom

ABSTRACT
Farming robots are used nowadays to increase farming efficiency and production. In this paper, the Wireless Power Transfer (WPT) is proposed to be used as the enabler technology to support the autonomous characteristics of the robot. A low input voltage supplied from a solar panel is assumed as the source for the WPT system in this work. The mathematical modeling, design, and hardware implementation are discussed in this paper. The experimental results of WPT with double-sided LCC compensation maintained its efficiency to a certain distance with output power 5–76 W under the variation of coupling coefficient from 0.18 to 0.05. The peak efficiency obtained from the experiment was 69.19%. In addition, the study also showed that the output voltage of WPT can be regulated at 24V by changing the switching frequency of the inverter.

Introduction Agriculture has a significant role in a country’s growth [ 1 ] due to the increasing needs of the crops to feed the population over time. This demand pushes the agriculture technology for improvement, especially the farming sector [ 2 , 3 ]. Farming industries have developed along with the Industrial Revolution since the 17th century. In Industrial Revolution 4.0, the farming sector has reached more advanced tools because of the digital technology integration [ 4 ]. One of the technology developments in farming is smart farming. One part of smart farming is using an autonomous robot, or an agriculture robot, to operate in hard-to-access areas [ 1 , 5 ]. The robot used in farming sector have a considerable risk to be contaminated with dust, dirt, mud, or another filth, resulting in disturbance of power charging [ 6 ], to optimize charging in farming robot, a large capacity battery can be used, however this method can increase the robot size, weight, and affect robot performance [ 7 ], so another solution are needed for a farming robot charging system. A practical, autonomous, and efficient charging system for a farming robot can be achieved using Wireless Power Transfer (WPT) [ 8 , 9 ]. With WPT, the charging process can work without problem during rainy days [ 10 ], even when the farming robots are contaminated by dirt [ 11 – 13 ], and does not interfere with the work process. The resonant inductive coupling method is used for the WPT, using coils on the transmitter and receiver sides with a compensation circuit to achieve resonance frequency and higher efficiency [ 14 , 15 ]. Wireless transferred energy using mutual inductance between the transmitter and receiver coils. The leakage inductance is significant due to the coil gap, misalignment, and tilt. Use of a compensation circuit to compensate for the leakage inductance is a must to make power transfer more efficient [ 16 , 17 ]. Leakage inductance compensation is done by adding a capacitor. Basic types of compensation circuits are using a capacitor connected in series (SS), parallel (PP), series-parallel (SP), or parallel-series (PS) [ 18 – 20 ]. An SS compensation circuit is simple and low-cost, but its performance is highly dependent on the mutual inductance and load dynamics, which makes it require a more complex control system [ 21 – 23 ]. The SP-type compensation is more suitable for a current-source application, resulting in a better efficiency for an asymmetrical coil case in which the transmitter side is bigger [ 24 ]. A more advanced compensation circuit is achieved by adding an inductor or an additional capacitor to the basic compensation to achieve different system characteristics. One of the advanced compensation circuits is a double-sided LCC [ 25 , 26 ]. With double-sided LCC, the system can achieve zero-voltage switching (ZVS) by adjusting the compensation component parameter [ 27 , 28 ]. Another advantage of double-sided LCC compensation is its robustness against misalignment, slope, and coil gap, resulting in more stable efficiency. Considering the environment for farming robots that are subject to the presence of dirt, gravel, and uneven surfaces, the double-sided LCC compensation is selected [ 29 ]. Working principle and system modeling The design of WPT utilized here is based on the inductive coupling model, which means the power will be transferred using the changing magnetic field in the transmitter coil that induces a voltage in the receiver coil. The WPT system can be illustrated by a circuit diagram as shown in Fig 1 . The input voltage of the system is a low DC voltage (24 V), which is then converted into an AC voltage using a full bridge inverter by Q1-Q4 MOSFETs. After the inverter circuit passes, then U1 has become a square wave AC voltage. The compensation method to cancel out leakage induction in the designed system coil uses double-sided LCC compensation, consisting of 1 inductor and two capacitors connected in series and parallel, both on the transmitter and receiver sides. After the power is successfully transferred, the voltage is rectified to DC using a full bridge rectifier by D1-D4 before being used by the load (RL). 10.1371/journal.pone.0359107.g001 Fig 1 Double-sided LCC wireless power transfer circuit diagram. MOSFETs in full-bridge inverters operate with a gate signal given by an STM32 microcontroller with a 50% duty cycle. Dead time is used in the gate signal to ensure there is no short circuit between the transitions of each MOSFET pair. A bootstrap circuit and gate resistor are used to generate a good gate signal to turn the MOSFETs on and off effectively. Results and analysis The inverter output voltage is shown in Fig 6 , indicating the circuit has successfully converted a DC voltage source into a square wave AC voltage. The Gate (G) and the Drain (D) voltage waveforms are also observed to show the condition of switching transition, as shown in Fig 7 . It shows that at the turn-on transition, the D-S voltage has reached zero voltage before the G-S voltage rises. In this case, the G-S voltage is the representation of the Drain-Source Current (I DS ) since the current can flow only if the MOSFET is on. Therefore, at the transition zone, the D-S voltage multiplication to I DS is close to zero. This phenomenon indicates that the inverter is operating in zero voltage switching (ZVS) mode, resulting in a reduction of switching loss of the MOSFET. A compensation circuit is used to cancel out the effect of leakage induction to make the load seem as fully resistive, with a near unity power factor of transferred energy, resulting in efficient power transfer. Voltage and current output of the compensation circuit in Fig 8 demonstrate that the designed double-sided LCC compensation can realize a near unity power factor on the receiver side. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359107.g006 Fig 6 Inverter output voltage. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359107.g007 Fig 7 Turn-on transition of the inverter MOSFET. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359107.g008 Fig 8 Double-sided LCC compensation output voltage and current. The largest loss occurs at the transmitter coil, with 49% of the contribution, followed by the receiver-side coil, with 21% of the contribution. The total contribution of coils is 70% of the total loss, while the combined contribution of compensation capacitors is only 3.7%. These values should be a concern when a higher power application is required. It is very important to keep the coil’s total resistance low and the effective current at its optimal value to achieve a more efficient system. As a mitigation, the selection of the coil wire diameter and the reduction of the circulating current in the coils should be considered in the design stage. The remaining power loss can be caused by the IC power consumption, resistance of the PCB tracks, the losses due to the parasitic capacitances of the MOSFET, and the reverse recovery loss of the diode. The analysis shows the biggest loss happens in the transmitter coil with the value of 8.56W. The energy produced due to the losses causes the temperature rise. However, due to the size and the construction of the coil that is placed in the open air, the heat can be transferred directly to the open air. Therefore, no excessive thermal rise was observed during the experiment, which shows the thermal stability of the coils. The results presented here are limited to the testing scenarios that were explained previously. In the practical operation, the battery charging should have other features such as closed loop control, so that the charger is capable of operating with Constant Voltage (CV), or Constant Current (CC) mode to charge the battery. This can also be done by using a post-regulator circuit and treating the presented WPT system here as the open-loop stage. Conclusions The data acquired shows that in 50–100% load conditions, until a certain limit, the designed WPT system can maintain its efficiency under coil gap and misalignment. Maximum efficiency of the system has reached 69,19% in the lowest coil gap and 2 cm misalignment, conversely lowest efficiency 8,58% in the furthest coil gap and misalignment (8 and 6 cm) with coil slope 30˚. The WPT system using double-sided LCC has proven successful in transferring power using a wireless method to the receiver side with regulated voltage output by changing the switching frequency. The experiment shows a designed system capable of maintaining a 24 V voltage output under variation of coil gap, misalignment, and slope by changing switching frequency in the range of 63–76 kHz. In conclusion, the WPT system with double-sided LCC compensation is suitable for a farming robot. The results we obtained are limited to the implemented case. The improvement in the future can be done by implementing the closed loop control system that capable to charge the battery at the required voltage and current level. This could be by adding a post-regulator as the backend of the system. Supporting information S1 Data Double side LCC WPT experimental data. (XLSX)
