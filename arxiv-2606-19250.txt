Local and global well-posedness for the extended Schrödinger-Benjamin-Ono system
Puti Dai and Justin Forlano

Abstract
. We study the well-posedness problem for the extended Schrödinger-Benjamin-Ono system (eSBO) on the real line. This system couples a Schrödinger field u u with a Benjamin-Ono type field v v , including a term of the form ∂ x ( v 2 ) \partial_{x}(v^{2}) . This latter term, just as in the case of the Benjamin-Ono equation, causes the system to become quasilinear and unsolvable via Picard iteration. We prove that eSBO is locally well-posed in H s + 1 2 ​ ( ℝ ) × H s ​ ( ℝ ) H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}) for any s ≥ 0 s\geq 0 . In particular, this result covers the energy space at s = 1 2 s=\frac{1}{2} , yielding global well-posedness in H 1 ​ ( ℝ ) × H 1 2 ​ ( ℝ ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}) with a small L 2 L^{2} -assumption on the Schrödinger part of the initial data.

1.
Introduction

We study the Cauchy problem for the
extended Schrödinger-Benjamin-Ono system (eSBO):

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=uv+\beta|u|^{2}u,\\
&\partial_{t}v-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}v=\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}-\rho v^{2}),\\
&(u,v)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
,
v:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\beta,\rho\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\mathcal{H}
is the Hilbert transform which is defined by the singular integral operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}f(x)=\text{p.v.}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}dy.

Physically, the eSBO system (
1.1
) arises as a particular case of the following general system which
models the interaction of a short wave (the Schrödinger part) and a long wave (the Benjamin-Ono part):

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=\alpha uv+\beta|u|^{2}u,\\
&\partial_{t}v+\nu P(\partial_{x})v=\gamma\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})+\lambda\partial_{x}v^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha,\beta,\nu,\gamma,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
P(\partial_{x})
is a constant coefficient differential operator.
Such systems arise ubiquitously in applications
[
20
,
31
,
24
,
18
,
3
]
, from capillary and gravity water waves, ion-acoustic waves, and sonic-Langmuir waves in plasmas.
In particular, special interest is taken to the following two cases:
First, when
P(\partial_{x})=-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}
,
\beta=0
and
\lambda=0
:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=\alpha uv,\\
&\partial_{t}v-\nu\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}v=\gamma\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}),\end{aligned}\right.

which is known as the Schrödinger-Benjamin-Ono system (SBO), first derived by Funakoshi-Oikawa
[
20
]
. Second, when
P(\partial_{x})=\nu\partial_{x}^{3}
and, in general,
\lambda\neq 0
,

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=\alpha v+\beta|u|^{2}u,\\
&\partial_{t}v-\nu\partial_{x}^{3}v=\gamma\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})+\lambda\partial_{x}v^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

which is the so-called Schrödinger-Korteweg-de Vries system (SKdV). See
[
17
]
for further references on the applicability of SKdV (
1.4
).
Relative to (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), the eSBO system (
1.1
) involves the most difficult parts of both previous systems:
\lambda=-\rho\neq 0
and the weaker nonlocal dispersion
P(\partial_{x})=-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}
.

Our main goal in this article is to advance the well-posedness theory for the eSBO system (
1.1
). To motivate the function spaces, we note that smooth solutions to (
1.1
) enjoy a number of conservation laws:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{0}(v)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t,x)dx\quad\text{and}\quad E_{1}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\\
E_{2}(u,v)&=\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)\overline{\partial_{x}u(t,x)}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t,x)^{2}dx\\
H(u,v)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\partial_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}v)^{2}-\frac{\rho}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{3}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}v|u|^{2}dx+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{4}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}dx,\end{split}

where we will refer to
H
as the energy and
E_{1}
as the mass.
In view of the conserved energy
H
in (
1.5
),
it is natural to study the well-posedness of the system (
1.1
) for the initial data in

\displaystyle(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})

where
s\in\mathbb{R}
, where the case
s=\frac{1}{2}
constitutes the energy space.

To discuss what is known, let us first recall the current well-posedness theory for SBO (
1.3
), essentially corresponding to the case
\rho=0
in (
1.1
).
Within the space
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, local well-posedness for any
s\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and when
|\nu|\neq 1
(the non-resonant case) was first shown in
[
2
]
. Global well-posedness was also established in
[
2
]
for some range of parameters in
\alpha,\gamma
and
\nu<0
in
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
using suitable conservation laws similar to (
1.5
). Pecher
[
43
]
then extended the local well-posedness to the resonant case
|\nu|=1
for
s>-\frac{1}{2}
, and used the I-method to establish global well-posedness, for the same parameter ranges, for
s>-\frac{1}{6}
. This latter global result was improved by Angulo-Matheus-Pilod
[
1
]
to any
s\geq-\frac{1}{2}
using a method due to Colliander-Holmer-Tzirakis
[
16
]
.
Later, Domingues
[
19
]
proved local well-posedness results in the non-resonant case in
H^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for sharp ranges of
(s,s^{\prime})
, not necessarily restricted to
s^{\prime}=s+\frac{1}{2}
. We also point out the results of Oh
[
41
]
in the setting of periodic boundary conditions, in particular the construction of global-in-time solutions on the support of the Gibbs measure.

The local well-posedness arguments mentioned above are based on contraction mapping arguments using the Fourier restriction norm method
[
6
,
7
]
.
Consequently, in these regimes the data to solution map is analytic, and thus (
1.3
) is semilinear.
Moving back to (
1.1
), as soon as
\rho\neq 0
, the Cauchy problem becomes quasilinear, in the sense that for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
the data to solution map cannot be
C^{2}
. This result can be shown following the strategy in
[
40
]
, which showed such a failure for the Benjamin-Ono equation (BO):

(1.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}v=\partial_{x}(v^{2}).

This implies that the previously cited results for (
1.3
) cannot be adapted to handle (
1.1
) when
\rho\neq 0
.
Thus the low-regularity well-posedness analysis of (
1.1
) is more similar to that of BO (
1.6
) rather than SBO (
1.3
).

By now, the well-posedness theory for (
1.6
) is quite well-understood, see
[
30
,
35
,
44
,
29
,
10
,
38
,
28
]
, culminating in the global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for any
s\geq 0
by using a gauge transformation and Fourier analytic/dispersive arguments. Using the complete integrability of (
1.6
), this was pushed to the full scaling sub-critical regime
s>-\frac{1}{2}
in
[
32
]
. See also
[
37
,
22
]
for analogous results in the periodic setting.

However, we point out that (
1.1
) is a nonlinear system with genuine nonlinear interactions between the Schrödinger and BO-parts so it is not a-priori clear that results for (
1.6
) can be adapted to (
1.1
). Moreover, we do not know whether (
1.1
) is completely integrable.
Recently, Linares-Mendez-Pilod
[
36
]
provided the first result on the local well-posedness for eSBO (
1.1
) in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>\frac{5}{4}
. Their method is based on the energy method with refined Strichartz estimates alá
[
35
]
and modified energies.

Our first contribution is to extend the local well-posedness for (
1.1
) to the range
s\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the extended SBO system (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
. More precisely, for every
(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists
T=T(\|(u_{0},v_{0})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{s}})>0
and a unique distributional solution
(u,v)\in C([0,T];H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}))\times C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
to (
1.1
) satsifying
J^{s}v\in\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}
and
w:=\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{+,\textup{hi}}[e^{i\rho F[v]}]\in Z^{s}_{T}
, where
F
is defined in (
2.5
) and the space
Z^{s}_{T}
is defined in (
3.7
). Moreover, the data-to-solution map

(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto(u,v)\in C([0,T];H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}))\times C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
is continuous.

The range
s\geq 0
in Theorem
1.1
appears to be optimal in view of our method, as discussed below.
In particular, our result covers the energy space at
s=\frac{1}{2}
, for which we can exploit the conservation laws (
1.5
) to obtain a priori bounds on solutions. We thus obtain the first global well-posedness results for the eSBO system (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\rho,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and for
r>0
, let
B_{r}=\{u_{0}\,:\,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq r\}
.
There exists
R>0
such that the extended SBO system (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in
(H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap B_{R})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
.

It is unclear to us if the restriction to small
L^{2}-
norm for the Schrödinger part can be relaxed.
Ultimately, this stems from the additional cubic term
\frac{\rho}{3}\int v^{3}dx
in
H(u,v)
, which presents a kind of criticality in the exponents as the
L^{2}
-norm of
v
is not conserved.
Instead, we have to combine the conservation of
E_{1}(u)
and
E_{2}(u,v)
to adequately control this cubic term. See (
5.22
). Notice that no such term arises for the SBO system (
1.3
), and thus no small data restriction is imposed in that setting.

If we take the initial datum for the Schrödinger part
u_{0}\equiv 0
, and assume that
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then Theorem
1.2
and the conservation of
E_{1}
implies that
u(t)\equiv 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, whence (
1.1
) reduces completely to the BO equation (
1.6
) (modulo the factor of
-\rho
). This observation allows us to immediately transport the known ill-posedness result due to Biagoni-Linares
[
9
]
for BO to the system (
1.1
). The ill-posedness here is that when
s<-\frac{1}{2}
, an extension of the solution map
\Phi:H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{s}\to C([-T,T];H^{s+\frac{1}{2}})\times C([-T,T];H^{s})
, sending initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
to solutions
(u,v)=\Phi(u_{0},v_{0})
, must be discontinuous at
(u_{0},v_{0})=(0,-2\pi\delta_{0})
for any
T>0
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s<-\frac{1}{2}
. Then, the eSBO equation (
1.1
) is ill-posed in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
.

It remains an interesting problem to determine the well-posedness for the system (
1.1
) in the remaining range
-\frac{1}{2}\leq s<0
. As mentioned above, the only work that can cover the range
-\frac{1}{2}<s<0
for BO (
1.6
) is
[
32
]
that relies on the complete integrability and thus is not applicable to (
1.1
). On the circle, the situation for BO (
1.6
) is also now completely resolved with well-posedness for
s>-\frac{1}{2}
and ill-posedness for
s\leq-\frac{1}{2}
. See
[
22
]
and the references therein.

We now discuss our method of proof for Theorem
1.1
.
To weaken the bad high
\times
low
\to
high interactions in term
\partial_{x}(v^{2})
, we use a gauge transformation for the Benjamin-Ono part
v
, which is motivated by the theory of the BO equation by Tao
[
44
]
and further analysed in
[
10
,
38
]
.
This gauged variable, which we call
w=w[v]
, satisfies (
3.23
) which is similar to the gauged equation for BO but with the extra term

\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,hi}}[e^{-i\rho F}|u|^{2}].
Whilst
u
has spatial regularity
s+\frac{1}{2}
, the low
\times
low
\times
high
\to
high interactions in this term cannot be controlled without additional smoothing. In this case, we gain the extra
\frac{1}{2}
-degree of smoothing by applying to the product
u\overline{u}
the bilinear Strichartz estimate due to Bourgain
[
8
]
and Ozawa-Tsutsumi
[
42
]
; see (
3.12
). Notice that it is then natural to posit that
u\in X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b}_{T}
, for some
b>\frac{1}{2}
, where
X^{s,b}_{T}
is the Fourier restriction norm space
[
6
]
, which is defined in (
3.1
).

Next, we consider the Schrödinger part for which the main nonlinear term is the product
uv
. This product needs to be estimated in
X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b-1}_{T}
but only assuming that
v\in X^{s-\theta,\theta}_{T}
for
0\leq\theta\leq 1
; namely, with a loss of spatial derivatives. The main issue is the low
\times
high interactions, as we need to observe
\frac{1}{2}
-degree of smoothing to control this product at regularity
s+\frac{1}{2}
.
It turns out that the product
u\mathbf{P}_{+}v
is highly non-resonant and thus a bilinear estimate can be established; see Lemma
4.4
. Here, by highly non-resonant we mean that the phase function is the square of the maximum frequency; see (
4.27
). In comparison, the phase function for the product
u\mathbf{P}_{-}v
is worse behaved being the minimum times the maximum frequency, which is insufficient.
However, since
v
is real-valued,
\mathbf{P}_{-}v=\overline{\mathbf{P}_{+}v}
and we may rewrite this using in (
3.21
) in terms of the gauged variable
w
at the cost of a (smoother) commutator term
R(v,w)
:

\displaystyle u\mathbf{P}_{-}v``="e^{-i\rho F}u\overline{w}+u\overline{R(v,w)},

where we neglected some inessential additional frequency projections and constant factors.
It turns out that, after some careful frequency decompositions, the commutator term enjoys
exactly
an additional
\frac{1}{2}
-degree of regularity as compared to
w
(measured in
\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}
, see Lemma
3.12
) which allows us to control the low
\times
high
\to
high interactions.

It still remains to control the first term
e^{-i\rho F}u\overline{w}
in
X^{s+\frac{1}{2},b-1}_{T}
. As we cannot obtain favourable Fourier restriction norm properties for the exponential term
e^{-i\rho F}
all the way down to
s=0
, we do not know how to exploit mulitlinear dispersion to gain additional smoothing for the low
\times
low
\times
high
\to
high interactions here. Again, we rely on the bilinear Strichartz estimate to absorb
exactly

\frac{1}{2}
of the derivative. Unfortunately, the transference property for Fourier restriction norm spaces would require us to put
w\in X^{s,b}_{T}
for
b>\frac{1}{2}
. When
s>0
and
b>\frac{1}{2}
, we can prove the main bilinear estimate for the BO part

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}[(\partial_{x}^{-1}w)\mathbf{P}_{-}\partial_{x}v].

However, due to a
\log
-loss when
s=0
, we would miss the endpoint space
s=0
. If we instead force
b=\frac{1}{2}
for
w
, then we only gain
(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)
-smoothing, for any
\varepsilon>0
, from the bilinear Strichartz estimate which is insufficient.
We point that this issue does not seem to be resolved by taking the
\ell^{1}
-based Besov variants of
X^{s,b}
, as done in
[
39
]
, or by considering the spaces used in
[
38
]
.

Our desire to reach the endpoint space
s=0
led us to consider the adapted function spaces based on the framework of the atomic function spaces
U^{2}
and their pre-dual
V^{2}
as a replacement for
X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}
. These spaces were first used in
[
33
,
25
,
26
]
and have proven very effective for scaling critical problems. This allows us to obtain a transference for the bilinear Strichartz estimate without paying an
\varepsilon
-derivative loss. The price to pay here is that we need to prove an analog of the bilinear estimate for (
1.7
) in
[
38
, Proposition 3.5]
but in the
U^{2}-V^{2}
-framework; see Lemma
4.1
.

Remark 1.4
.

We point out that our argument still works for the local well-posedness for any
\nu\neq 0
of the system:

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=uv+\beta|u|^{2}u,\\
&\partial_{t}v-\nu\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}v=\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}-\rho v^{2}),\end{aligned}\right.

The point is that we only use the bilinear strichartz estimate in the case of one input much larger than the other and here the bilinear estimate still holds with the two dispersion relations
-i\partial_{x}^{2}
and
\pm i\nu\partial_{x}^{2}
. The only part that fails as written is (
4.27
) when
\nu=-1
as we only gain
N_{\max}N_{\min}
rather than
N_{\max}^{2}
. However, this is easily rectified by replacing
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,HI}}v
in the product
u\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,HI}}v
by (
3.21
) as we already did in (
3.24
).

More generally, Theorem
1.1
continues to hold for suitable dispersive perturbations of eSBO (
1.1
), in the spirit of the more general system (
1.2
). More precisely, as long as the operator
P(\partial_{x})-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}
is
L^{2}\to L^{2}
bounded, viewing (
1.2
) as a perturbation of (
1.1
) entails an additional harmless linear term in the BO part in (
1.1
).
For example, Theorem
1.1
also holds for the system:

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=uv+\beta|u|^{2}u,\\
&\partial_{t}v-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}v=\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}-\rho v^{2}),\end{aligned}\right.

where, for
\delta>0
,
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}=\mathcal{T}_{\delta}-\delta^{-1}\partial_{x}^{-1}
, with
\mathcal{T}_{\delta}
a Fourier multiplier operator with multiplier
-i\coth(\delta\xi)
.
Formally, (
1.9
) represents a coupling of a short wave with a long wave of “finite depth”
\delta
, which is a system version of the intermediate long wave equation (ILW)
\partial_{t}v-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}v=\partial_{x}(v^{2})
. Recently, the ILW equation has attracted a lot of attention as it is a physically relevant model, which interpolates between BO (
1.6
) and the KdV equation in the limits
\delta\to\infty
and
\delta\to 0
, respectively. See
[
27
,
14
,
21
,
13
]
and references therein.

This article is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce notation and prove product estimates. As we need to cover the gap
0\leq s\leq\frac{5}{4}
, the estimates become aesthetically more complicated as
s
increases. In Section
3
, we first recall the Fourier restriction norm spaces and adapted spaces based on the
U^{2}
and
V^{2}
-spaces.
We then apply a gauge transformation to find the equations for the new gauged variable and study some properties of the original solution
v
as well as the commutator term
R(v,w)
. The main multilinear estimates then appear in Section
4
. Finally, in Section
5
we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

In this subsection, we introduce relevant notation, projections, and function spaces, which will be used throughout.

We use
A\lesssim B
to denote
A\leq CB
for some constant
C>0
,

A\ll B
if there is a small
c>0
such that
A\leq cB
, and
A\sim B
if both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold.
The notation
a\pm
refers to
a\pm\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\land b
and
a\lor b
denote the minimum and the maximum between
a
and
b
, respectively. We define
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Given a function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
, we use
\mathcal{F}f
and
\widehat{f}
to denote its Fourier transform

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-i\xi x}dx.

For space-time functions
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, we may use the notation
\mathcal{F}_{t}u
and
\mathcal{F}_{x}u
to indicate the Fourier transform with respect to the time and space variables. We omit this indexing, when clear from context.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We define the
L^{p}
-based Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R})
by the norm:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{s,p}}=\|J^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}=\big\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\big(\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\big)\big\|_{L^{p}},

where
J^{s}
denotes the Bessel potential with Fourier multiplier
\langle\xi\rangle^{s}
, where
\langle x\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}
stands for the inverse Fourier transform.
We also use
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R})
for the homogeneous Sobolev spaces with norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}}=\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}=\big\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\big(|\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\big)\big\|_{L^{p}},

where
D^{s}
is the Riesz potential, with Fourier multiplier
|\xi|^{s}
.
When
p=2
, we write
W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R})=H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces, with norm
\|f\|_{H^{s}}=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}.

We define the inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by
\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{f}gdx,

and dyadic
L^{p}
-spaces, for
1<p<\infty
, via the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\widetilde{L^{p}_{t,x}}}=\big\|\|\mathbf{P}_{N}f\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}\big\|_{\ell^{2}_{N}},

where
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
.
By the Littlewood-Paley square function theorem and Minkowski’s inequality, it holds that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\widetilde{L^{p}_{t,x}}}

for any
2\leq p<\infty
.
When working with space-time functions, given
T>0
, we often use the shorthand notation
L^{p}_{T}W^{s,q}_{x}
for
L^{p}([0,T);W^{s,q}(\mathbb{R}))
and
L^{p}_{T}L^{q}_{x}
for
L^{p}([0,T);L^{q}(\mathbb{R}))
.

We now introduce notation to perform Litlewood-Paley decompositions.
Let
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function supported on
[-2,2]
and equal to
1
on
[-1,1]
.
Given
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, let
\eta_{N}(\xi)=\eta(\frac{\xi}{N})
and
\psi_{N}(\xi)=\eta(\frac{\xi}{N})-\eta(\frac{2\xi}{N})
.
Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{N\geq 1}\psi_{N}(\xi)=1-\eta_{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\quad\text{when }\xi\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Moreover, we use
\mathbf{P}_{\leq N}
and
\mathbf{P}_{N}
to denote
the Littlewood-Paley projectors defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\,(\mathbf{P}_{\leq N}f)

\displaystyle=\eta_{N}\widehat{f},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P}_{1}f)

\displaystyle=\eta_{1}\widehat{f}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{N}f=\mathcal{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{\leq N}f-\mathcal{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{\leq\frac{N}{2}}f=\psi_{N}\widehat{f},\quad\text{when }N\geq 2,

and
\mathbf{P}_{>N}:=1-\mathbf{P}_{\leq N}
. Note that
\sum_{N\geq 1}\mathbf{P}_{N}f=f,

which we will often use in our estimates, where by abuse of notation, we assume to sum over dyadic numbers in
2^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
.
We use
\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
for the wider projector with multiplier
\widetilde{\psi}_{N}(\xi)=\psi_{N}(\frac{\xi}{2})+\psi_{N}(\xi)+\psi_{N}(2\xi)
.
We then set

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\,(\mathbf{P}_{+}f)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi>0\}}\widehat{f}(\xi),

\displaystyle\qquad\mathcal{F}\,(\mathbf{P}_{-}f)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi<0\}}\widehat{f}(\xi),

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N\geq 2}\mathbf{P}_{N},

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N\geq 8}\mathbf{P}_{N},

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}

\displaystyle=\text{Id}-\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}},

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}

\displaystyle=\text{Id}-\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}.

We also define the shorthand
\mathbf{P}_{\pm,\text{hi}}=\mathbf{P}_{\pm}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}
and similarly for
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}},\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}},\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}
.

For space-time functions
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, we define frequency projectors on the space-time Fourier variables
(\tau,\xi)
: given
K\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, we define
\mathbf{Q}_{K}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{\leq K}
via

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t,x}\{\mathbf{Q}_{\leq K}u\}(\tau,\xi)=\eta_{K}(\tau+\xi^{2})\widehat{u}(\tau,\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}_{t,x}\{\mathbf{Q}_{K}u\}(\tau,\xi)=\psi_{K}(\tau+\xi^{2})\widehat{u}(\tau,\xi).

We also define
\mathbf{Q}_{>K}:=\textup{Id}-\mathbf{Q}_{\leq K}
.

2.2.
The primitive

We adapt the gauge transform, originally due to Tao
[
44
]
, and as presented by Burq-Planchon
[
10
]
and Molinet-Pilod
[
38
]

The aim is to first construct a spatial primitive
F=F[u,v]
of the smooth solution
v
to the BO part of the system (
1.1
) so that
\partial_{x}F=v
and
F
satisfies:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F-\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}F+\rho(\partial_{x}F)^{2}=|u|^{2}.

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(x)dx=1
. Define

(2.5)

\displaystyle F(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(y)\int_{y}^{x}v(t,z)dzdy+G(t)

for some function
G
to be chosen later. Then, using (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(y)\int_{y}^{x}\partial_{t}v(t,z)dzdy+G^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(y)\int_{y}^{x}\Big[\mathcal{H}\partial_{z}^{2}v(t,z)-\rho\partial_{z}(v(t,z)^{2})+\partial_{z}(|u(t,z)|^{2})\Big]dzdy+G^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}v(t,x)-\rho v(t,x)^{2}+|u(t,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{XX}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\mathcal{H}\psi^{\prime}(y)v(t,y)-\psi(y)\rho v(t,y)^{2}+\psi(y)|u(t,y)|^{2}]dy+G^{\prime}(t).

Choosing
G
so that

\displaystyle G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\mathcal{H}\psi^{\prime}(y)v(t^{\prime},y)-\psi(y)\rho v(t^{\prime},y)^{2}+\psi(y)|u(t^{\prime},y)|^{2}]dydt^{\prime}

then verifies (
2.4
). Note that
G(0)=0
and is real-valued.

We then consider the function
e^{-i\rho F}
, which satisfies
|e^{-i\rho F}|=1
since
F
is real-valued.
It follows that
e^{-i\rho F}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\setminus L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Consequently,
e^{-i\rho F}
is merely a tempered distribution. Nonetheless, as
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\partial_{x}e^{-i\rho F}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and we have that for almost every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{x}\{e^{-i\rho F}\}(\xi)=\tfrac{1}{i\xi}\mathcal{F}_{x}\{\partial_{x}e^{-i\rho F}\}(\xi).

Whilst
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{i\rho F}
,
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}e^{i\rho F}
, and
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,hi}}e^{i\rho F}
are well-defined and belong to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, due to the non-integrable singularity at the origin, the quantities
\mathbf{P}_{\pm}(e^{i\rho F})
,
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}
and
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}e^{i\rho F}
are ill-defined. Here, we understand the latter two via the differences

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}:=e^{i\rho F}-\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{i\rho F}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}e^{i\rho F}:=e^{i\rho F}-\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}e^{i\rho F}.

It follows that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}(e^{i\rho F})=\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}e^{i\rho F}-\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}e^{i\rho F}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}(e^{i\rho F})=\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}e^{i\rho F},

where the
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}
appearing on the right-hand side of (
2.7
) agrees with a Littlewood-Payley projection to frequencies
\{|\xi|\lesssim 1\}
. Due to the definition (
2.6
), in the following we need to carefully handle estimates for terms including factors of the form
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{-i\rho F}
or
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}e^{-i\rho F}
.

2.3.
Product estimates

We will need a product estimate involving products of functions with the exponential factors
e^{i\rho F}
. First, we recall the fractional Leibniz rule
[
15
,
23
,
9
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Fractional Leibniz rule)
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
1<p_{j},q_{j},r\leq\infty
,
j=1,2
,
such that
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p_{j}}+\frac{1}{q_{j}}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\|J^{s}(fg)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|J^{s}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\|J^{s}g\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}.

We now give a product estimate for products involving an exponential factor
e^{i\rho F}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
2\leq q<\infty
,
0\leq s\leq\frac{3}{2}
, and
\sigma:=\max(0,s-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
. For
j=1,2
, let
F_{j}
denote two-real valued functions such that
\partial_{x}F_{j}=v_{j}
for
v_{j}\in H^{\sigma}
for
j=1,2
. Then,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|J^{s}_{x}[e^{\pm\rho F_{j}}g]\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\|v_{j}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2})\|J^{s}_{x}g\|_{L^{q}},

\displaystyle\|J^{s}_{x}[(e^{\pm\rho F_{1}}-e^{\pm\rho F_{2}})g]\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\|e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}-e^{\pm i\rho F_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}})

(2.9)

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}\times(1+\|v_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\|v_{2}\|_{H^{\sigma}})^{3}\|J^{s}_{x}g\|_{L^{q}}.

Proof.

This lemma was proved in
[
38
, Lemma 2.7]
for
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{q}
and in
[
14
, Lemma 3.3]
when
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
We extend these ranges to cover the range
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq\frac{3}{2}
.
We want to use the fractional Leibniz rule however there is an issue in making sense of
D^{s}_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}
in
L^{\infty}
, especially when
s
is low. See
[
11
, Section 2.2]
.
Namely,
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}
is defined via (
2.6
) and not as a projection to frequencies
\{|\xi|\lesssim 1\}
.

For
N\gg 1
, we first claim that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{N}[\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}\cdot g]=\mathbf{P}_{N}[\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}g]

point-wise. Note that we use
\cdot
to denote a product and to make clear which factors the projection operators are acting on.
To see this, for any test function
h
, we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle h,\mathbf{P}_{N}[\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}\mathbf{P}_{\gg N}g]\rangle&=\langle\mathbf{P}_{\gg N}g\cdot\mathbf{P}_{N}h,\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}\rangle\\
&=\langle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}[\mathbf{P}_{\gg N}g\cdot\mathbf{P}_{N}h],\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}\rangle\\
&=\langle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}[\mathbf{P}_{\gg N}g\cdot\mathbf{P}_{N}h],\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}\rangle=0,\end{split}

where in the last equality we used (
2.7
).
Similarly, we have that
\mathbf{P}_{N}[\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}\mathbf{P}_{\ll N}g]=0.
Thus, (
2.10
) follows. Then, by writing

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{N}[\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}g](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{N}(y)[\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}g](x-y)dy,

and by the Littlewood-Payley square function theorem and Minkowski’s inequality, we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|D^{s}_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}(\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}g)\|_{L^{q}}&\sim\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{N\gg 1}N^{2s}|\mathbf{P}_{N}[\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{i\rho F}g]|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|_{L^{q}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{N}(y)|\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{N\gg 1}N^{2s}|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}g(x-y)|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|_{L^{q}_{x}}dy\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{N}(y)|\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{N\gg 1}N^{2s}|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}g|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|_{L^{q}_{x}}dy\lesssim\|D^{s}_{x}g\|_{L^{q}_{x}},\end{split}

where we used the Littlewood-Payley square function theorem again in the last inequality.

We now consider the contribution from
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}
.
By the fractional Lebiniz rule, we have

\displaystyle\|D^{s}_{x}[\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}\cdot g]\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{s}_{x}g\|_{L^{q}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{s}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{iF}\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\|D^{s}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{iF}\|_{L^{q}})\|J^{s}_{x}g\|_{L^{q}}.

When
s\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}
, Sobolev inequality implies

\displaystyle\|D^{s}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{iF}\|_{L^{q}}\sim\|D^{s+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{iF}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|D^{s-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}[v_{j}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2}}.

If instead
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}<s\leq 1+\frac{1}{q}
, then we additionally use the fractional Leibniz rule and (
2.12
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|D^{s-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}[v_{j}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{s-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}[v_{j}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{s-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}[v_{j}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{j}\|_{H^{\max(0,s-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}}\big(1+\|D^{s-1-\frac{1}{q}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}[v_{j}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{j}\|_{H^{\max(0,s-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}}\big(1+\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2}})

Hence, when
0\leq s\leq 1
, we have shown that

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|D^{s}_{x}[e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}g]\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim(1+\|v_{j}\|_{H^{\max(0,s-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}}^{2})\|J^{s}_{x}g\|_{L^{q}}.

Now we consider when
s>1
, for which we have

\displaystyle\|D^{s}_{x}[e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}g]\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\sim\|D^{s-1}_{x}\partial_{x}[e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}g]\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\sim\|D^{s-1}_{x}[v_{j}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}g]\|_{L^{q}}+\|D^{s-1}_{x}[e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}\partial_{x}g]\|_{L^{q}}

For the second term, we apply (
2.8
) since
s-1\leq\frac{1}{2}
. For the first term similar arguments as above splitting
e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}
using
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}
and
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}
and using (
2.12
) and fractional Leibniz rule, we obtain

\displaystyle\|D^{s-1}_{x}[v_{j}e^{\pm i\rho F_{j}}g]\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{2}})\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

Combining this with (
2.13
) establishes (
2.8
) in the range
0\leq s\leq\frac{3}{2}
.
Finally for the difference estimate (
2.9
), we follow a similar strategy as in (
2.12
). We omit the details.
∎

As we will need to use the dyadic spaces
\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}
, Lemma
2.2
will not alone be sufficient. We need to refine the estimates by using frequency decompositions and gaining derivatives from the smoother terms
e^{\pm i\rho F}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
2\leq q<\infty
and
0\leq s\leq\frac{3}{2}
. For
j=1,2
, let
F_{j}
denote two-real valued functions such that
\partial_{x}F_{j}=v_{j}
for
v_{j}\in L^{2}
for
j=1,2
.
Let
\mathcal{V}:=\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}
,
V:=v_{1}-v_{2}
, and
G(F_{1},F_{2}):=e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}-e^{\pm i\rho F_{2}}
.
Then, for any
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, it hold that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{N}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{q}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-2-\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{V}^{4}+N^{-1-\frac{1}{q}}(1+\mathcal{V})\mathcal{V}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-1}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}v\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{N}G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{q}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}V\|_{L^{q}}+N^{-1}\mathcal{V}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}v_{2}\|_{L^{q}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+N^{-2-\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{V}^{3}(1+\mathcal{V})^{3}\Big\{\|V\|_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{V}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+N^{-1-\frac{1}{q}}(1+\mathcal{V})\Big\{\mathcal{V}(1+\mathcal{V})\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}V\|_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{V}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\hphantom{XXXXXXXXX}+(1+\mathcal{V})(\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}})\|V\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}.

Proof.

We first establish (
2.14
) in the case
q=\infty
. By Bernstein inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{N}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[v_{1}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}]\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big[\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[(\mathbf{P}_{\ll N}v_{1})e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}]\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[(\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{1})e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}]\|_{L^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big[\|\mathbf{P}_{\ll N}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\big\{\|\mathbf{P}_{\ll N}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{2}}\big\}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{2}}

(2.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{3}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{2}})\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{2}}.

Now fix
2\leq q<\infty
. By employing similar arguments as above and using (
2.16
), we have

\displaystyle N\|\mathbf{P}_{N}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{P}_{\ll N}v\|_{L^{q}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}v\|_{L^{q}}+\sum_{N_{1}\gg N}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}v\|_{L^{q}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{1}}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}v\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{N_{1}\gg N}\big\{N_{1}^{-1-\frac{1}{q}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}v\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{3}+N_{1}^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}v\|_{L^{2}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{2}})\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N_{1}}v\|_{L^{2}}\big\}

(2.17)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1-\frac{1}{q}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+N^{-\frac{1}{q}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{2}})\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}v\|_{L^{q}}.

This proves (
2.14
).
For (
2.15
), we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{N}G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[Ve^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}]\|_{L^{q}}+N^{-1}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[v_{2}G(F_{1},F_{2})]\|_{L^{q}},

and we are left to estimate both of the factors on the right-hand side of (
2.18
). First, by a similar argument as in (
2.17
) using (
2.16
), we have

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[Ve^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}]\|_{L^{q}}&\lesssim\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}V\|_{L^{q}}+N^{-1-\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{V}^{3}\|V\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}(1+\mathcal{V})\|V\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
2.18
), we need an auxiliary estimate on
\mathbf{P}_{N}G(F_{1},F_{2})
in
L^{\infty}_{x}
.
To start, we need a slight variant of (
2.16
) which is proved in exactly the same way:

(2.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[ge^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}]\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}\|g\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\qquad+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|v_{1}\|_{L^{2}})\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}g\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Similarly, using
G(F_{1},F_{2}):=e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}-e^{i\pm\rho F_{2}}
and
V:=v_{1}-v_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[v_{2}G(F_{1},F_{2})]\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{V}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{V}\big\{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}[Ve^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}]\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}[v_{2}G(F_{1},F_{2})]\|_{L^{2}}\big\}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\mathcal{V}(1+\mathcal{V})^{3}\|V\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-1}\mathcal{V}^{2}\|G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\qquad+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{V}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}V\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}

Then, by (
2.20
),

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{N}G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[Ve^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}]\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[v_{2}G(F_{1},F_{2})]\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\mathcal{V}(1+\mathcal{V})^{3}\|V\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\mathcal{V})\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}V\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\{\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{V}^{2}\}\|G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Using this, we can obtain further decay in
N
:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{N}G(F_{1},F_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}V\|_{L^{2}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|V\|_{L^{2}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{V}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}G\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\mathcal{V})^{2}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}V\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\mathcal{V})\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}+N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathcal{V}^{3}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+\big\{N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathcal{V}^{2}(1+\mathcal{V})^{3}+N^{-1}(1+\mathcal{V})\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\big\}\|V\|_{L^{2}}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{N}[v_{2}G]\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{P}_{\ll N}v_{2}\|_{L^{q}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}G\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}v_{2}\|_{L^{q}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{N_{1}\gg N}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}v_{2}\|_{L^{q}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{1}}G\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{V}(1+\mathcal{V})\Big\{(1+\mathcal{V})\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}V\|_{L^{2}}+\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+N^{-1-\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{V}^{3}\Big\{\mathcal{V}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}+(1+\mathcal{V})^{3}\|V\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{V}(1+\mathcal{V})\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|V\|_{L^{2}}+\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}v_{2}\|_{L^{q}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Combining this with (
2.19
) in (
2.18
), we then obtain (
2.15
).
∎

Definition 3.1

(i)

(3.3)

so then by Bernstein’s inequality and (
2.14
), this contribution to
A_{3}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\bigg(

\displaystyle\sum_{N\gg 1}N^{2s+1}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}e^{i\rho F}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{4}_{x}}^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}w\|_{L^{4}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{4}_{T,x}}\bigg(\sum_{N\gg 1}N^{2s+1}N^{-4-\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{8}+N^{2s+1}N^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}})^{2}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{4}+(1+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L_{x}^{2}})\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}})

Therefore, we have shown that

(3.40)

\displaystyle A_{3}\lesssim\|w\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{4}+(1+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L_{x}^{2}})\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}).

We move onto
A_{4}
, which is more delicate. In view of the signs of the frequencies, Bernstein’s inequality, and (
2.14
), we have

\displaystyle A_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s+1}\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,hi}}\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}(e^{i\rho F})\cdot\mathbf{P}_{\lesssim N}\mathbf{P}_{-,\text{hi}}[e^{-i\rho F}v]\|_{L^{4}_{T,x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s+1}\Big\|\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}(e^{i\rho F})\|_{L^{4}_{x}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\lesssim N}[e^{-i\rho F}v]\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\Big\|_{L^{4}_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s+1}N^{-\frac{9}{2}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{8}\|\mathbf{P}_{\lesssim N}[e^{-i\rho F}v]\|_{L^{4}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s+2}\Big\{N^{-\frac{5}{2}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}})^{2}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{4}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+N^{-2}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}v\|_{L^{4}_{T,x}}^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{8}\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s-\frac{7}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\lesssim N}[e^{-i\rho F}v]\|_{L^{4}_{T,x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}})^{2}\|v\|^{4}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{\gtrsim N}v\|_{L^{4}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\|J^{s}_{x}v\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}^{2}

(3.41)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{8}\|v\|_{L^{4}_{T,x}}^{2}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}})^{2}\|v\|^{4}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\|J^{s}_{x}v\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}^{2}.

In a similar way, we have

\displaystyle A_{5}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s+1}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N}e^{i\rho F}\|_{L^{4}_{T,x}}^{2}\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}(e^{-i\rho F}v)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}^{2}

(3.42)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\Big(T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}^{8}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}})^{2}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{\max(0,s-\frac{3}{4}+)}_{x}}^{2}+\|J^{s}_{x}v\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}^{2}\Big).

Combining (
3.38
), (
3.39
), (
3.40
), (
3.41
), and (
3.42
) then establishes (
3.36
).

For (
3.37
), we take the difference of
R(v_{1},w_{1})
and
R(v_{2},w_{2})
using (
3.22
). Relative to the estimate for (
3.36
), it remains only to consider the contributions for terms with a factor of

appearing. By the same argument as for (

), we see that

\displaystyle\|J^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}[Gw_{1}]\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}\|w_{1}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}.

Next, similar to (
3.39
) and (
3.40
) but using (
2.15
), we have

\displaystyle\|J^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{-,\text{HI}}[Gw_{1}]\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}+\|J^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}[G\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}w_{1}]\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|w_{1}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\Big(\|V\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}+\mathcal{V}_{12}\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}

\displaystyle+(1+\mathcal{V}_{12})^{6}\{\|V\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}+(1+\|v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}+\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}})(\|V\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+\mathcal{V}_{12}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}})\Big)

\displaystyle+\|w_{1}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}.

Similarly to (
3.41
), using (

) instead of (

\displaystyle\|J^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,HI}}[\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,hi}}G\cdot\mathbf{P}_{-,\text{hi}}(e^{-i\rho F_{2}}v_{2})]\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{V}_{12}^{3}(1+\mathcal{V}_{12})^{3}\|v_{2}\|_{L^{4}_{T,x}}\big(\|V\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+\mathcal{V}_{12}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}})\big)

\displaystyle\quad+(1+\mathcal{V}_{12})^{3}\big(\|V\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}+\mathcal{V}_{12}\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}+(\|v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}+\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}})\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}\big)\Big\}.

A similar bound holds for the term

. This proves (

The estimates in Lemma

.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
w
be such that
\mathbf{P}_{+}w=w
and
F
be a real-valued function such that
\partial_{x}F=v
. Then,

(3.43)

\displaystyle\|R(v,w)\|_{L^{4}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\|v\|_{L^{2}})^{4}(\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}).

Moreover, for
j=1,2,
let
w_{j}\in L^{2}_{x}
such that
\mathbf{P}_{+}w_{j}=w_{j}
,
F_{j}
be two real-valued functions such that
\partial_{x}F_{j}=v_{j}
. Let
G:=e^{i\rho F_{1}}-e^{i\rho F_{2}}
,
V:=v_{1}-v_{2}
, and
W:=w_{1}-w_{2}
. Then,

(3.44)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|R(v_{1},w_{1})-R(v_{2},w_{2})\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim&(1+\mathcal{V}_{12}+\|w_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|w_{2}\|_{L^{2}})^{8}(\|W\|_{L^{2}}+\|V\|_{L^{2}})\\
&+\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}(1+\mathcal{V}_{12})^{8}(\|w_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{V}_{12}).\end{split}

Proof.

We begin with (
3.43
).
From Bernstein’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}[e^{i\rho F}w]\|_{L^{4}}+\|e^{i\rho F}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}w\|_{L^{4}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,HI}}[e^{i\rho F}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}(e^{-i\rho F}v)]\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus, from (
3.22
), it remains to estimate the contributions from the second and fourth terms on the right-hand side of (
3.22
).
We only give details for the second term as the same arguments work to estimate the fourth.
By the signs of the frequencies,
e^{i\rho F}
carries the largest frequency. Thus, by Bernstein’s inequality and (
2.14
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{-,\text{HI}}[e^{i\rho F}w]\|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}e^{i\rho F}\|_{L^{4}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}w\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{2}}\sum_{N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(N_{1}^{-\frac{9}{4}}\mathcal{V}^{4}+N_{1}^{-\frac{5}{4}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{2}_{x}})\|v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+N_{1}^{-1}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}v\|_{L^{4}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{2}}\bigg(\|v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{4}+(1+\|v\|_{L^{2}_{x}})\|v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\sum_{N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}(1+\|v\|_{L^{2}_{x}})^{3}.

This proves (
3.43
).
The estimate (
3.44
) follows in the same way but using (
2.15
).
∎

4.
Multilinear estimates

In this section, we detail the key multilinear estimates needed to analyse the left-hand sides of (
3.23
) (the “BO-part”) and (
3.24
) (the “Schrödinger-part”).

4.1.
The BO-part

The first of these concerns one of the main nonlinear contributions in (
3.23
). The estimate is an adaptation of the key multilinear estimate in
[
38
, Proposition 3.5]
to the framework of the atomic functions spaces. As such, the estimate in
[
38
, Proposition 3.5]
does not imply the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
0<T\leq 1
. Then,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,hi}}[(\partial_{x}^{-1}w)\mathbf{P}_{-}\partial_{x}v]\big\|_{N^{s}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{Z^{s}_{T}}\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}_{T,x}}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}+\|v\|_{X_{BO;T}^{-1,1}}\big).

Proof.

In order to ease the notation, we define the bilinear form:

\displaystyle B(w,v):=\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{+,hi}}[(\partial_{x}^{-1}w)\mathbf{P}_{-}\partial_{x}v].

Note that by the signs of the frequencies, we have
|\xi_{1}|=|\xi|+|\xi_{2}|
. Thus, it suffices to prove (
4.1
) in the case
s=0
.
By Lemma
3.5
and (
3.6
), we reduce to showing that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}B(\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}w,v)\overline{\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}h}dxdt\bigg|\lesssim\|h\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\|w\|_{Z^{0}_{T}}\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}_{T,x}}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}+\|v\|_{X^{-1,1}_{BO;T}}\big),

for any
h\in Y^{0}_{T}
.
We then take extensions
\widetilde{h},\widetilde{w},\widetilde{v}
of
h
,
w
, and
v
, respectively, and define
\widetilde{h}_{T}=\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}\widetilde{h}
and
\widetilde{w}_{T}=\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}w
, and further reduce, using (
3.6
), to showing

(4.3)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}B(\widetilde{w}_{T},\widetilde{v})\overline{\widetilde{h}_{T}}dxdt\bigg|\lesssim\|\widetilde{h}_{T}\|_{Y^{0}}\|\widetilde{w}_{T}\|_{Z^{0}}\big(\|\widetilde{v}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\|\widetilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{t,x}}}+\|\widetilde{v}\|_{X^{-1,1}_{BO}}\big).

In the following, we drop the tildes, writing
h_{T},w_{T},v
in place of
\widetilde{h}_{T},\widetilde{w}_{T},\widetilde{v}
, respectively.

We will use the following bounds:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}+\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}^{-1}\{|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}\{\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}f\}|\}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\lesssim(1+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\|f\|_{V^{2}_{S}L^{2}_{x}}.

We only show the second bound, with the one for
\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}f
following from the same arguments. Using (
3.15
), (
3.3
), (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}^{-1}\{|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}\{\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}f\}|\}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}^{-1}\{|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}\{\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}f\}|\}\|_{X^{0,\frac{3}{8}}}

\displaystyle\sim\|\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}f\|_{X^{0,\frac{3}{8}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}f\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\|\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}f\|_{\dot{X}^{0,\frac{1}{2},\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}+\|\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}f\|_{V^{2}_{S}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|S(-t)f\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}+2\|f\|_{V^{2}_{S}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\|f\|_{V^{2}_{S}L^{2}_{x}}.

This establishes (
4.4
).

By dyadic decomposition, we write

(4.5)

\displaystyle\text{LHS}\eqref{BO11}=\bigg|\sum_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}B(\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}w_{T},\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v)\overline{\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}}\,dxdt\bigg|=\bigg|\sum_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}I_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}\bigg|.

Due to the signs of the frequencies in
B(\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}w,\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v)
, we have
N_{1}\gtrsim\max(N_{2},N_{3})
and from the outer
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}
in the definition of
B
, we have
N_{3}\gtrsim 1
.
We first dispense with the trivial case when
N_{2}\lesssim 1
. Here, we must have that
N_{3}=2^{j}N_{1}
for
|j|\leq 2
. Then, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|I_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\|B(\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}w_{T},\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v)\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}w_{T}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}},

so then by Cauchy-Schwarz and (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}\mathbf{1}_{\{N_{2}\lesssim 1\}}|I_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\sum_{|j|\leq 2}\sum_{N_{1}}\|\mathbf{P}_{2^{j}N_{1}}h_{T}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}w_{T}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\|h_{T}\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{t,x}}}\|w_{T}\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{t,x}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\|h_{T}\|_{Y^{0}}\|w_{T}\|_{Z^{0}}.

We now assume that
N_{2}\gg 1
. We write

(4.6)

\displaystyle I_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}I^{(\ell)}_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}},

(4.7)

\displaystyle I^{(1)}_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B(\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}w_{T},\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v)\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim N_{2}N_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}}\,dxdt,

(4.8)

\displaystyle I^{(2)}_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B(\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim N_{2}N_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}w_{T},\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v)\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{\ll N_{2}N_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}}\,dxdt,

(4.9)

\displaystyle I^{(3)}_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B(\mathbf{Q}_{\ll N_{2}N_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}w_{T},\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim N_{2}N_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v)\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{\ll N_{2}N_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}}\,dxdt.

The decomposition (
4.6
) is exhaustive in view of the resonance relation

(4.10)

\displaystyle(\tau_{1}+\xi_{1}^{2})+(\tau_{2}-\xi_{2}^{2})-(\tau+\xi^{2})=\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi^{2}=-2\xi\xi_{2},

where the second equality holds when
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
.
We consider each of
I^{(\ell)}_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}
separately.

Case
\ell=1
:

Consider first the case when
N_{1}\sim N_{3}
. Using that
\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{1}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{3}}

along with Hölder’s inequality, (
3.4
) and (
3.9
),

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\\
N_{1}\sim N_{3}\end{subarray}}I_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}^{(1)}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{N_{2}\lesssim N_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B(\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{3}}w_{T},\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v)\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim N_{2}N_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}}dxdt\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{N_{3}}\sum_{j=0}^{\log_{2}(N_{3})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B(\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{3}}w_{T},\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{3}}v)\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim 2^{-j}N_{3}^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}}dxdt\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{3}}\sum_{j=0}^{\log_{2}(N_{3})}N_{3}\|\partial_{x}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{3}}w_{T}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\|\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{-}\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{3}}v\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\|\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim 2^{-j}N_{3}^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{3}}\sum_{j=0}^{\log_{2}(N_{3})}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}\|_{V^{2}_{S}L^{2}_{x}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{3}}w_{T}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\|\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{3}}v\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h\|_{Y^{0}}\|w_{T}\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{t,x}}}\|v\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}.

If instead
N_{1}\sim N_{2}
, then we write
N_{3}=2^{-j}N_{2}
and we similarly obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\\
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\end{subarray}}I_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}^{(1)}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{N_{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\log_{2}(N_{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{2}}B(\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{2}}w_{T},\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v)\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim 2^{-j}N_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{2}}h_{T}}dxdt\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\log_{2}(N_{2})}2^{\frac{j}{2}}N_{2}^{-1}\|\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{2}}B(\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{2}}w_{T},\mathbf{P}_{N_{2}}v)\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\|\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{2}}h_{T}\|_{V^{2}_{S}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h_{T}\|_{Y^{0}}\|w_{T}\|_{\widetilde{L^{4}_{t,x}}}\|v\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}.

Case
\ell=2
:

Again, we consider first the case when
N_{1}\sim N_{3}
. By summing over
N_{1}\sim N_{3}
, integrating by parts, using
N_{2}=2^{-j}N_{3}
, (

), (
3.5
), and (
3.9
), and (

), we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\\
N_{1}\sim N_{3}\end{subarray}}I_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}^{(2)}\bigg|=\bigg|\sum_{N_{3}}\sum_{j=0}^{\log_{2}(N_{3})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B(\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim 2^{-j}N^{2}_{3}}\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{3}}w_{T},\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{3}}v)\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{\ll 2^{-j}N^{2}_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}}\,dxdt\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{N_{3}}\sum_{j=0}^{\log_{2}(N_{3})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{x}^{-1}\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim 2^{-j}N^{2}_{3}}\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{3}}w_{T}\cdot\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{-}\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{3}}v\cdot\overline{\mathbf{P}_{-,\text{hi}}\partial_{x}\mathbf{Q}_{\ll 2^{-j}N^{2}_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}}\,dxdt\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{3}}\sum_{j=0}^{\log_{2}(N_{3})}\|\mathbf{Q}_{\gtrsim 2^{-j}N^{2}_{3}}\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}_{N_{3}}w_{T}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\|\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{-}\mathbf{P}_{2^{-j}N_{3}}v\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\|\mathbf{P}_{-,\text{hi}}\partial_{x}\mathbf{Q}_{\ll 2^{-j}N^{2}_{3}}\mathbf{P}_{N_{3}}h_{T}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}

If instead

, then we argue similarly:

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\\
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\end{subarray}}I_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}^{(2)}\bigg|

We recall the following result from

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(0)\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+T^{\delta}\|\mathbf{1}_{[0,T)}\mathcal{N}_{S;\lambda}(u,v)\|_{X^{s+\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}+2\delta}_{S}}\lesssim N^{s}_{T}(u,v).

Next, as
u,v,
and

is continuous. Moreover, this map is non-decreasing in
T
.

Note that by (

), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq 1}\|v\|_{X^{s-\theta,\theta}_{\text{BO};T}}\lesssim N^{s}_{T}(u,v)+N^{s}_{T}(u,v)^{2}\lesssim N_{T}^{s}(u,v).

By the Duhamel formula for
w
, Lemma
3.5
, (
3.25
), (

), (
4.12
), and (

), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to 0^{+}}\|w\|_{Z^{s}_{T}}\lesssim\|w(0)\|_{H^{s}}+\|u(0)\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim(1+\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}})^{4}\|v(0)\|_{H^{s}}+\|u(0)\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Therefore, with Lemma
4.4

4.5
, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to 0^{+}}N_{T}^{s}(u,v)\lesssim(1+\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}})^{4}\|v(0)\|_{H^{s}}+\|u(0)\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}.

By combining Lemma
3.5
, Lemma
3.6
, Lemma
3.10
, (

), Lemma

, Lemma
4.2
, Lemma

, Lemma

, Lemma
4.5
, we obtain

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}N^{\sigma}_{T}(u,v)&\leq\|u(0)\|_{H^{\sigma+\frac{1}{2}}}+C_{1}(1+\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}})^{a}\|v(0)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\\
&+C_{2}(1+N_{T}^{0}(u,v))^{5}N_{T}^{0}(u,v)N_{T}^{\sigma}(u,v)+C_{2}T^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{T}^{0}(u,v),\end{split}

where
\sigma\in\{0,s,1\}
, and
for some constants
a>0
and
C_{1},C_{2}>0
.

1

Whilst the constants depend on the choices
\{0,s,1\}
, we only use these three regularities and can thus take the maximum of the given constants over
\{0,s,1\}
.

We first put
\sigma=0
in (
5.7
).
We then choose
T>0
so that
C_{2}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{3}
, and obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle N_{T}^{0}(u,v)\leq\tfrac{3}{2}\|u(0)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\tfrac{3}{2}C_{1}(1+\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}})^{a}\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{3}{2}C_{2}(1+N_{T}^{0})^{5}(N_{T}^{0})^{2}.

In view of (
5.6
) and (
5.3
), by choosing
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle N_{T}^{0}(u,v)\leq 2\|u(0)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}+2C_{1}(1+\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}})^{a}\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}}

Using this information in (
5.7
) at regularity
\sigma=1
, we obtain

(5.10)

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T^{\prime}}H^{\frac{3}{2}}\times L^{\infty}_{T^{\prime}}H^{1}}\leq N_{T^{\prime}}^{1}(u,v)\leq 2\|u(0)\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}}+2C_{1}(1+\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}})^{a}\|v(0)\|_{H^{1}},

for any

which implies that the maximal time of existence for these solutions is bounded from below by
T_{\ast}=T_{\ast}(\|(u(0),v(0))\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times L^{2}})
.

Step 2: Difference estimate:

As for the uniqueness and continuity of the flow map, we consider differences of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
solutions and derive a difference estimate. Given two such solutions
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})
to (
5.2
) with initial data
(u_{j}(0),v_{j}(0))\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
j=1,2
. We assume that
\max_{j=1,2}\|(u_{j}(0),v_{j}(0))\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,
and that
v_{j}(0)
are equal on low frequencies:

(5.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}v_{1}(0)=\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}v_{2}(0).

This property is crucial; see Lemma
5.2
below. By Step 1, there exists
T_{\ast}>0
such that

\displaystyle\max_{j=1,2}N_{T}^{s}(u_{j},v_{j})\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\max_{j=1,2}\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq 1}\|v_{j}\|_{X^{s-\theta,\theta}_{\text{BO}:T}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}

for all
0<T\leq T_{\ast}
.
We consider the difference
U:=u_{1}-u_{2}
and
V:=v_{1}-v_{2}
.
We also define the primitives
F_{j}=F_{j}[u_{j}]
as in (
2.5
) and the attendant gauged variables
v_{j}=v_{j}[u_{j}]
as in (
3.19
) with corresponding difference
W:=w_{1}-w_{2}
.

We then obtain difference estimates for the norms appearing in (
5.4
) using additionally (
2.9
),
Lemma
3.11
, (
3.37
), (
3.44
), Lemma
4.2
, (
4.18
), Lemma
4.5
. In order to control the differences
e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}-e^{\pm\rho F_{2}}
, we use the mean value theorem to get
\|e^{\pm i\rho F_{1}}-e^{\pm\rho F_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}\lesssim\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}.

To estimate the difference
F_{1}-F_{2}
, we need to use the assumption (
5.11
). For this purpose, we rely on the following result, which will be proved at the end of this step.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})
be two
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
solutions to (
5.2
) with initial data satisfying

(5.12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}v_{1}(0)=\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}v_{2}(0).

For
j=1,2
, let
F_{j}=F_{j}[u_{j},v_{j}]
denote the primitive defined in (
2.5
). Then, it holds that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\|(F_{1}-F_{2})|_{t=0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{1}(0)-v_{2}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

\displaystyle\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{1}(0)-v_{2}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+T(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}})\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}

(5.14)

\displaystyle\hphantom{XXXXXXXXXX}+T(\|v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}})\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}.

At the end of this procedure, by reducing
T_{\ast}=T_{\ast}(\varepsilon_{0})
if necessary, we obtain

(5.15)

\displaystyle\|J^{\sigma}V\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}\cap\widetilde{L^{4}_{T,x}}}+\|W\|_{Z^{s}_{T}}+\|U\|_{X^{\sigma+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\delta}_{T}}\leq C_{3}\|(U(0),V(0))\|_{H^{\sigma+\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{\sigma}},

for any
0<T\leq T_{0}
and
\sigma\in\{0,s\}
.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

We begin by showing (
5.13
). In the following, we apply the single-variable operators
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}
,
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}
,
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}
, and
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}
, as defined in (
2.2
) and (
2.6
), to functions of two-variables. We keep track of which variable these operators act (keeping the remaining variable fixed) using a superscript notation:
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}(h(x,y))
and
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}^{y}(h(x,y))
, etc.
The argument is similar to that in
[
38
, Section 4B]
, tracking the variables these operators act on.

We first derive a useful identity. From (
2.5
), we have
F_{1}(x)-F_{1}(y)=\int_{y}^{x}v_{1}(t,z)dz
. Then

(5.16)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}v_{1}(t,z)dz\bigg)=\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}F_{1}(x)-F_{1}(y),

where we used that
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}^{x}F_{1}(y)=0
. It then follows from the commutativity of
\partial_{x}
and
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}
that
\partial_{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}F_{1}=\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}v_{1}
. Integrating both sides of this then gives

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}v_{1}(t,z)dz=(\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}F_{1})(x)-(\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}F_{1})(y).

Combining this with (
5.16
) then implies

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}v_{1}(t,z)dz-\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}v_{1}(t,z)dz\bigg)=(\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}F_{1})(y).

Finally, we then apply
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}^{y}
to both sides to find the desired result:

(5.17)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}^{y}\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}v_{1}(t,z)dz\bigg)=\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}^{y}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}v_{1}(t,z)dz\bigg).

Clearly (
5.17
) applies to any
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
solution
(u,v)
to (
5.2
).
Now we establish (
5.13
). Using that
G_{j}(0)=0
, the operators
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}^{y}
and
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}
commute, (
5.17
), (
5.12
), and that
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}(h(y))=h(y)-\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}^{x}(h(y))=h(y)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}(F_{1}-F_{2})(0,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(y)\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}(v_{1}-v_{2})(0,z)dz\bigg)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(y)\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}^{y}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}(v_{1}-v_{2})(0,z)dz\bigg)dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(y)\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}^{y}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}(v_{1}-v_{2})(0,z)dz\bigg)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(y)\mathbf{P}_{\textup{lo}}^{y}\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}(v_{1}-v_{2})(0,z)dz\bigg)dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}\psi)(y)\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}\partial_{y}^{-1}\partial_{y}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}^{y}\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}(v_{1}-v_{2})(0,z)dz\bigg)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{y}^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}\psi)(y)\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}^{x}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}^{y}[v_{1}-v_{2}](0,y)dy

(5.18)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{y}^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}\psi)(y)\mathbf{P}_{\textup{hi}}^{y}[v_{1}-v_{2}](0,y)dy.

Then, by Cauchy-Schwarz we obtain

(5.19)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}(F_{1}-F_{2})|_{t=0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\|v_{1}(0)-v_{2}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

To see (
5.13
), we note that
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}(F_{1}-F_{2})=\partial_{x}^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}[v_{1}-v_{2}]
so Bernstein’s inequality implies

(5.20)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}(F_{1}-F_{2})|_{t=0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\|v_{1}(0)-v_{2}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

This completes the proof of (
5.13
).

For (
5.14
), we split
F_{1}-F_{2}=\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}(F_{1}-F_{2})+\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}(F_{1}-F_{2})
. As in (
5.20
), we have
\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{HI}}(F_{1}-F_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}\lesssim\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}
. For the low part
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}(F_{1}-F_{2})
, we use the Duhamel formulation of (
2.4
):

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}(F_{1}-F_{2})(t)=e^{it\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}[F_{1}-F_{2}]|_{t=0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}[|u_{1}|^{2}-|u_{2}|^{2}+\rho(v_{2}^{2}-v_{1}^{2})]dt^{\prime}.

For the linear solution part, we use (
5.18
) and (
5.19
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{it\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}[F_{1}-F_{2}]|_{t=0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}}=\|\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}[F_{1}-F_{2}]|_{t=0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\|v_{1}(0)-v_{2}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

For the Duhamel integral piece, we note that since
u_{j},v_{j}\in L^{2}
, we can interpret
\mathbf{P}_{\textup{LO}}
as a smooth projection to frequencies
\{|\xi|\lesssim 1\}
, and thus by the Bernstein inequality, this term in
L^{\infty}_{T,x}
is bounded by

\displaystyle T(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}})\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+T(\|v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+\|v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}})\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}.

This completes the proof of (
5.14
).

Step 3: Existence, uniqueness, and continuity property:

For the existence of solutions in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, we fix
(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
such that (
5.3
) is satisfied and consider the sequence of approximations

\displaystyle u_{0,j}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{\mathbf{1}_{[-j,j]}\widehat{u}_{0}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad v_{0,j}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{\mathbf{1}_{[-j,j]}\widehat{v}_{0}\}

with corresponding
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
solutions
\{(u_{j},v_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. By the previous results, these all belong to
C([0,T_{\ast}];H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))
, where
T_{\ast}=T_{\ast}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s_{0}}})>0
. For all
j\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, the sequence
\{v_{0,j}\}_{j}
satisfies (
5.11
) by virtue of equalling
v_{0}
.
Thus, by (
5.15
), we see that the sequence
\{(u_{j},v_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is then Cauchy in the norm appearing in
N_{T}^{s}(u,v)
and hence converges to a limit
(u,v)
there, which satisfies (
5.2
) in the distributional sense and has
(u,v)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0})
. The continuous dependence with respect to initial data then follows by a standard argument. See for instance
[
38
, p. 387]
.
This completes the proof of the local well-posedness in Theorem
1.1
.

5.2.
Global well-posedness

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
which relies on the following a priori bound coming from the conservation laws.

Proposition 5.3

(A priori bound)
.

Let
\rho,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. There exists
r>0
such that for every
(u,v)
a global smooth solution to (
1.1
) with parameters
(\beta,\rho)
and initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
satisfying

(5.21)

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq r,

there exists a
C:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
increasing in its arguments such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})},\|v_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}).

Proof.

By the conservation of
E_{2}
and
E_{1}
, Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\text{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)\overline{\partial_{x}u(t,x)}\bigg|+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(5.22)

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+C_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}).

Next by the conservation of
H
, we may write

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\||\partial_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=H(u_{0},v_{0})+\frac{\rho}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t)^{3}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t)|u(t)|^{2}dx-\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t)|^{4}dx,

and we need to estimate the remaining time dependent terms on the right-hand side.
By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality,
E_{1}
conservation, and Young’s inequality,

(5.23)

\displaystyle\frac{|\beta|}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t)|^{4}dx\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{3}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{6}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Next, by Cauchy-Schwarz, (
5.22
) and (
5.23
), we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t)

\displaystyle|u(t)|^{2}dx\bigg|\leq\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{4}}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{3}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

(5.24)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}})+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{6},

for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Lastly, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, (
5.22
) and (
5.21
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\rho|}{3}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t)^{3}dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C|\rho|\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\||\partial_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C|\rho|\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\||\partial_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}+CC_{1}|\rho|\||\partial_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}\||\partial_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+C_{2}.

Combining this with (
5.23
), (
5.24
) and choosing
\varepsilon>0
and
r>0
sufficiently small, we obtain

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\||\partial_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}).

Inserting this into (
5.22
) then gives control on
\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}
. This completes the proof.
∎
