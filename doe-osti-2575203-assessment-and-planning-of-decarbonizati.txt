Final Report (Non-RD&D Projects)
During the one-year project, we identified research areas that align with Fossil Energy and Carbon
Management (FECM) mission goals and assessed our university's current research capability
and resources. In addition, we determined the resources needed to support FECM-related
research and development (R&D) at our minority-serving institution, University of Texas Rio
Grande Valley (UTRGV) in to increase our competitiveness for future funding opportunities in
this area. We also gathered information on the current academic courses, programs and
curriculum at UTRGV that aligns with FECM goals and described additional needs pertaining to
student training.

2. Project Objectives and Goals Accomplished
 The overarching goal of the proposed R&D scoping study and self-assessment is to evaluate
 how the UTRGV’s current capabilities, expertise, personnel, facilities and equipment, aligned
 with FECM objectives, which will lead to identifying gaps in capabilities in order to provide a
 plan to better prepare UTRGV for potential future competitive solicitations focused on FECM
 supported technologies. This overarching goal can be realized through the following specific
 objectives:
 Objective 1: Assessment of Current Capabilities, Resources, Facilities and Equipment
 First objective of this project is to identify and assess existing UTRGV research thrust areas
 and associated resource capabilities relevant to the mission of FECM to meet the
 decarbonization goals set for 2035 and 2050. The focus research areas are hydrogen with
 carbon Management, carbon transport and storage, CO2 removal and conversion, and point-
 source carbon capture. This objective will involve identification and assessment of available
 faculty expertise, laboratory equipment, other research resource capabilities, and relevant
 course offerings and curriculum. The focus will be to identify the currently available faculty
 members and researchers that can serve to address the 4 areas of decarbonization
 mentioned above. The team will evaluate the potential for collaborative interdisciplinary work
 within UTRGV and with our partner Louisiana State University (LSU). The current number of
 personnel and their relevant expertise will be evaluated along with detailed information on
 active and past R&D projects on decarbonization, which will include the solicitation
 requirement of awards received at UTRGV from all federal agencies on decarbonization
 research since January 2017. The team will specifically cover the projects funded by
 DOE/NETL both on decarbonization and other areas of research at UTRGV. The final
 outcome of this activity will be a detailed evaluation of the facilities and equipment available
 to UTRGV for research related to the focus research areas and how they can be used for this

  purpose.
  Objective 2: Assessment of Missing/Required Capabilities, Resources, Facilities and
  Equipment
  Decarbonization is an interdisciplinary topic and should be addressed from different
  perspectives including fundamental science, engineering, technology, economics, public
  policy, regulatory requirements, etc. As part of this project, the team will provide an estimate
  of the number, relevant experience, and cost of personnel necessary to build the required
  interdisciplinary team to address future opportunities on decarbonization. Laboratory space
  is an important element to conduct any research and for training purposes. The team will
  present an assessment of how the existing equipment could be modified, and the associated
  cost, to improve UTRGV’s competitiveness for future FECM research opportunities. The
  outcome of this activity would be a detailed report with an estimated list and description of,
  and cost to acquire equipment necessary for accessing future FECM research opportunities.
  Objective 3: Student Training & Education
  Student education and training is crucial to develop the required workforce to enable
  decarbonization implementation at the scale required. As part of this project, a detailed
  analysis of current academic courses, programs, and/or curriculum at UTRGV that aligns
  with FECM goals related to decarbonization as well as student training will be conducted.
  The team will identify and describe additional needs related to student training. The team will
  present plans to work with LSU, which has already introduced a decarbonization training
  program, to fill the gaps in our curriculum in a timely manner. Finally, the team will also
  suggest ways in which FECM may support the professional development of students.
  All project goals established for the project were accomplished to some degree within the
  proposed timeline.

3. Accomplishments
  We have identified two research thrust areas at UTRGV that align with FECM mission goals,
  which are Carbon Dioxide Removal & Conversion and Carbon Transport and Storage. We
  successfully assessed our university's current capabilities and resources in these areas,
  including personnel, awards, facilities, and equipment. In addition, we have determined the
  resources needed at UTRGV to increase our minority- serving institution's competitiveness in
  these research areas. Furthermore, we described our current academic courses, programs,
  and curriculum that align with FECM goals of decarbonization in the two areas mentioned
  above. We have also identified and described additional needs pertaining to student training.

a. Approach
  To assess the current capabilities and resources at UTRGV, the research team gathered
  data from various entities on campus along with web search and visits to laboratories. The
  team contacted the Division of Research to obtain information on all grant applications since
  2017 regardless of content or agency (including private entities). Records of all completed
  thesis and dissertations since 2017 were requested from the Graduate College. The Office
  for Sustainability was also contacted to gather information on any relevant projects
  conducted through them since 2017. The research team visited laboratories in the College of
  Engineering and Computer Science to gather information on available equipment. UTRGV’s
  undergraduate and graduate course catalog were looked into to find relevant course
  offerings. A web search of student service entities on campus was conducted to find any
  relevant training and activities relevant to FECM. Based on all the data obtained, faculty
  members with experience and expertise within the four areas of interest were identified and
  invited to participate in an initial one-hour Zoom meeting followed by a half-day meeting to

 discuss current capabilities, future collaborations, and gaps that need to be addressed to
 allow successful future research progress. Finally, we compiled and analyzed all the data
 collected.

 Discussion: The above results demonstrated that UTRGV has some talented faculty
 members whose expertise and research experience are relevant to FECM technologies.
 These faculty members have been active in submitting proposals to secure funding and

 Objective 2. Assessment of Missing/Required Capabilities, Resources, Facilities and
 Equipment
The resources needed for UTRGV to be more competitive in FECM related research endeavors
are summarized based on personnel and equipment.
Personnel
We believe the best approach would be to create faculty clusters in the two areas of strength of
UTRGV, which are Carbon Dioxide Removal & Conversion and Carbon Transport and Storage.
Once the faculty clusters are created, then the focus would be to hire faculty to fill in the missing
gap of existing faculty. Based on our current expertise, we would benefit from hiring an
enthusiastic and active early career faculty along with one experienced researcher to
complement our existing faculty cohort with new energy and a new mentor. This would be for
each cluster. This would require an initial investment of about $125,000 per faculty member for
salary and benefits ($100,000 for early career faculty and $150,000 for experienced faculty).
Additionally, each cluster should be provided with support for at least one postdoc and lab
technician with funding of $120,000 for both. In order to enhance the research output, funding for
two years to support about 6 graduate students should be set aside, which would be $120,000
per year. For smooth running of each cluster, one administrative coordinator to keep track of the
research program activities and personnel paperwork should be hired, requiring investment of
about $50,0000.

Equipment
While UTRGV has extensive amount of equipment to support research, they are not focused on
FECM relevant research areas. Additionally, the equipment is distributed throughout the two
different campuses at different locations. Our first suggestion to improve productivity of research
in FECM relevant areas is to designate a specific research space for relevant equipment in order
to ensure there is a central place for all researchers to come together to conduct experiments.
These central research spaces should be located close to personnel that currently conduct the
research activities. This would require the movement of equipment and remodeling of exiting
facilities to best match requirement of the central research center. The cost of this will probably
be around $250,000.
Once these central research centers are created, the following equipment would be helpful to
complement existing equipment and provide a venue for new research activities to support
research proposals:
 1) Inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) for materials research:
 $150,000
 2) High-resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM) for materials
 characterizations: $125,000
 3) A time-resolved steady state Fluorescence Spectrometer for studying photoactive
 materials for carbon capture/conversion: $170,000

 5) Steady-state photolysis apparatus, including transparent Dewar for low- temperature

 6) An environmental simulation system for gas hydrate research (hydrogen generation

 Discussion: UTRGV has several talented researchers whose expertise and research
 experience are relevant to FECM technologies; however, their expertise is not well aligned
 and lacks strong connection. To enhance collaboration, several new faculty members at both
 the entry and advanced level in research areas that can complement the current faculty's
 research should be hired. This would be best achieved through the development of research
 clusters to strengthen research productivity in FECM relevant areas. In addition, supporting
 staff will be needed to maintain the facilities/equipment and train new users along with
 running administrative part of the new research centers. Furthermore, student researchers
 will be essential to conduct research and be trained to be the next-generation workforce in
 decarbonization. As for the facility development, the first step would be to create new
 centralized research spaces for existing and new equipment. In addition to repairs and
 upgrades of current equipment, several new equipment is needed to expand our current
 research capabilities and develop/maintain robust research programs.

Objective 3. Student Training & Education

The programs and resources required to enhance student training to be well prepared next-
generation workforce to lead decarbonization efforts are summarized after discussion of training
activities conducted and planned through this grant.

As part of the UTRGV employment process, the students were provided with training on various
topics including information security and cyber security. The students were also given tours of
laboratories in our engineering buildings at two different campus locations. The students were
exposed to operations of different equipment of interest. Additionally, the team held two training
sessions for both students and faculty with the aid of our collaborator from LSU (Figure 1).
Students were also exposed to organizing and advertising the training sessions with our guest
from LSU. The team developed and executed an introductory seminar for students at UTRGV
on decarbonization (Figure 2). It had 10 students attending from various fields of study and the
program lasted 2 hours. It was well received and provided attendees with a good foundation.
The team gathered data on relevant decarbonization training at other institutions and prepared
an outline of an event for students, which was not able to be held due to lack of time.

While there are several courses of relevance to decarbonization, there is lack of focused courses
on FECM relevant focus areas. There is a need to develop focused courses with mainly a
certificate offered on decarbonization with a focus on preparing future decarbonization leaders.
UTRGV should develop a certificate program based on current successful programs at other
institutions with possible collaboration with those units in delivering the new certificate. There
should be funding set aside to support decarbonization workshops throughout the academic year
on campus to increase interest and develop better future ambassadors. Additionally, UTRGV
should work with industry to provide meaningful and focused internships and coop opportunities
to students.

 c. Conclusion
 During this project, we identified two research thrusts that are strong at UTRGV, which are
 Carbon Dioxide Removal & Conversion and Carbon Transport and Storage, based on
 gathered information on grant applications, publications, thesis work, equipment, and
 courses at UTRGV. Faculty members with diverse expertise in FECM relevant focus areas
 were identified and invited to share their experiences.
 Based on the gathered data, UTRGV needs to create research clusters and attract new
 faculty members and scientists with expertise in the above two research areas to develop
 more robust and diverse research programs. Current facilities and equipment at UTRGV are
 essential baselines for relevant research, but new equipment are needed to further expand
 the research capability and increase the institution's competitiveness in future FECM
 research/funding opportunities.
 There is an urgent need to develop a certificate program with focused courses on
 decarbonization in addition to workshops and industry sponsored internships to prepare the
 next generation of decarbonization leaders. This would be in addition to research
 opportunities that should be provided to students after development of focused research
 centers.
