A Counterexample to Wegner’s Conjecture for Axis-Parallel Rectangles
Deepak Ajwani, Rishikesh Gajjala, Rajiv Raman, Saurabh Ray

Abstract
In 1965, Wegner conjectured that every finite family ℛ \mathcal{R} of axis-parallel rectangles in the plane satisfies τ ⁡ ( ℛ ) ≤ 2 ​ ν ​ ( ℛ ) − 1 \tau(\mathcal{R})\leq 2\nu(\mathcal{R})-1 , where τ ⁡ ( ℛ ) \tau(\mathcal{R}) denotes the minimum number of points needed to pierce all rectangles in ℛ \mathcal{R} , and ν ⁡ ( ℛ ) \nu(\mathcal{R}) denotes the maximum size of a pairwise disjoint subfamily. Over the last six decades, the conjecture has motivated a long line of work: it has been verified for several special classes of rectangle families, and the best known general upper bounds have been progressively improved, but the conjecture itself had remained open. We give an explicit counterexample. More precisely, we construct a triangle-free rectangle-intersection graph on n n vertices whose independence number is at most n / 4 n/4 . Since the graph is triangle-free, no point of the plane can lie in three rectangles; hence every piercing point hits at most two rectangles. Consequently, τ ⁡ ( ℛ ) ≥ n / 2 ≥ 2 ​ ν ​ ( ℛ ) \tau(\mathcal{R})\geq n/2\geq 2\nu(\mathcal{R}) , contradicting Wegner’s conjectured bound. We also give a slightly more general construction for which τ ⁡ ( ℛ ) ≥ 2.21 ​ ν ​ ( ℛ ) \tau(\mathcal{R})\geq 2.21\nu(\mathcal{R}) . This shows that the standard point relaxation, equivalently the clique relaxation, for the Maximum Independent Set of Rectangles problem has integrality gap at least 2.21 2.21 .

1
Introduction

For a finite family
\mathcal{R}
of axis-parallel rectangles in the plane, let

\nu(\mathcal{R})
be the largest size of a pairwise disjoint subfamily, and let

\tau(\mathcal{R})
be the minimum number of points piercing all rectangles.
Throughout the paper, rectangles are closed. The intersection graph

G(\mathcal{R})
has vertex set
\mathcal{R}
, with an edge between two vertices if
and only if the corresponding rectangles have nonempty intersection.
Wegner’s conjecture
[
15
]
asserts the sharp inequality

\tau(\mathcal{R})\leq 2\nu(\mathcal{R})-1.

(1)

By the Helly property for axis-parallel rectangles, every clique in a rectangle
intersection graph has a common point. Thus
\tau(\mathcal{R})
is equivalently
the minimum number of cliques needed to cover the vertices of the intersection
graph
G(\mathcal{R})
, while
\nu(\mathcal{R})=\alpha(G(\mathcal{R}))
. In graph-theoretic
language, Wegner’s conjecture says that every rectangle-intersection graph
satisfies
\theta(G)\leq 2\alpha(G)-1
, where
\theta(G)
is the
clique-cover number. This is one of the classical packing–piercing questions
for geometric set systems initiated by Hadwiger and Debrunner; it is stronger
than the Gyárfás–Lehel question asking whether
\tau(\mathcal{R})/\nu(\mathcal{R})

is bounded by an absolute constant for rectangle families

[
11
,
15
,
10
]
.

Known upper bounds are still far from Wegner’s proposed coefficient. Károlyi proved that every finite family

\mathcal{F}
of axis-parallel boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies

\tau(\mathcal{F})\leq\nu(\mathcal{F})(1+\log\nu(\mathcal{F}))^{d-1}

[
12
]
; for
rectangles this gives

\tau(\mathcal{F})=O(\nu(\mathcal{F})\log\nu(\mathcal{F}))
. For axis-parallel boxes in higher
dimensions, Tomon showed that no constant bound in terms of
\nu
can hold:
for every
d\geq 3
and every
\nu
, there are families
\mathcal{B}
of
axis-parallel boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

\tau(\mathcal{B})\geq\Omega_{d}(\nu)\left(\frac{\log\nu}{\log\log\nu}\right)^{d-2}.

Thus the Gyárfás–Lehel constant-bound question is genuinely a phenomenon for rectangles
[
14
]
. Correa,
Feuilloley, Pérez-Lantero, and Soto proved the sharper bound

\tau(\mathcal{F})=O(\nu(\mathcal{F})(\log\log\nu(\mathcal{F}))^{2})
for rectangles
[
7
]
. Their
proof combines small
\varepsilon
-net bounds for axis-parallel rectangles
due to Aronov, Ezra, and Sharir
[
1
]
with the

O(\log\log n)
-approximation framework for maximum independent set of
rectangles due to Chalermsook and Chuzhoy
[
4
]
. More recently, recursive
partitioning methods led to constant-factor approximations giving a
(2+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm

[
13
,
9
]
.

For rectangle
families in which every rectangle intersects a common diagonal, Correa et al.
proved that the hitting/packing duality gap is between
2
and
4

[
7
]
. For rectangles of
r
-bounded aspect ratio,
Chudnovsky, Spirkl, and Zerbib proved the linear bound

\tau(\mathcal{F})\leq(14+2r^{2})\nu(\mathcal{F})

[
6
]
. They also proved
O(\nu)
and

O(\nu\log\log\nu)
piercing bounds for several families of axis-parallel
boxes satisfying additional cube-type or corner-intersection assumptions

[
6
]
.

A construction of Jelínek, reported by Correa et al.,
gives
\tau(\mathcal{F})=2\nu(\mathcal{F})-4
for every

\nu(\mathcal{F})\geq 4

[
7
]
; thus, if Wegner’s inequality
were true, its leading coefficient would be sharp. Earlier examples of
Fon-Der-Flaass and Kostochka showed that no universal coefficient below

5/3
can hold
[
8
]
. Chen and Dumitrescu gave
small-
\nu
improvements, including a family with

\nu(\mathcal{F})=4
and
\tau(\mathcal{F})=7

[
5
]
.

A useful comparison comes from intersection graphs of axis-parallel segments.
Caoduro, Cslovjecsek, Pilipczuk, and Węgrzycki proved that every
triangle-free intersection graph of
n
axis-parallel segments has
independence number at least
n/4+\Omega(\sqrt{n})
, and they gave a matching
construction up to the constant in the
\sqrt{n}
term

[
3
]
. Our construction is a triangle-free
rectangle-intersection graph with
\alpha<n/4
. It therefore crosses the
coefficient-
2
threshold for rectangles rather than merely approaching it.

1.1
Our results

Theorem 1

(Main theorem)
.

There is a finite family
\mathcal{R}
of axis-parallel rectangles such that

\tau(\mathcal{R})>2\nu(\mathcal{R})-1
.

More explicitly,
\mathcal{R}
may be chosen so that
its intersection graph
G=G(\mathcal{R})
is triangle-free and

|V(G)|=|\mathcal{R}|=2196\cdot 8^{9},\qquad\alpha(G)=\nu(\mathcal{R})\leq 544\cdot 8^{9}.

(2)

Here and below, after defining
G=G(\mathcal{R})
, we identify a vertex of
G

with its corresponding rectangle in
\mathcal{R}
.
Since
G
is the intersection graph of
\mathcal{R}
, pairwise disjoint
subfamilies of
\mathcal{R}
are exactly independent sets of
G
, and hence

\nu(\mathcal{R})=\alpha(G)
. Also, triangle-freeness implies that no point of
the plane lies in three rectangles of
\mathcal{R}
: three rectangles sharing a
point would be pairwise intersecting and would therefore form a triangle in

G
. Conversely, by the Helly property for axis-parallel rectangles, any
three pairwise-intersecting rectangles have a common point. Thus
triangle-freeness is equivalent here to the arrangement having depth at most
two, and every piercing point hits at most two rectangles.
Combining this
with
Eq.

2
gives

\tau(\mathcal{R})\geq\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{2}=1098\cdot 8^{9}>1088\cdot 8^{9}>2\nu(\mathcal{R})-1.

(3)

This proves
Theorem

1
and disproves Wegner’s conjecture.

The same example also gives an integrality-gap lower bound for the standard LP
relaxation for Maximum Independent Set of Rectangles (MISR). For a finite rectangle
family
\mathcal{R}
, this relaxation assigns a nonnegative variable
x_{R}
to
each rectangle and requires

\sum_{R\ni p}x_{R}\leq 1
for every point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Equivalently,
by the Helly property for axis-parallel rectangles, it is the clique relaxation

\alpha^{*}(G)=\max\left\{\sum_{v\in V(G)}x_{v}:\sum_{v\in Q}x_{v}\leq 1\text{ for every clique }Q,\quad x_{v}\geq 0\right\}.

The integral optimum is
\nu(\mathcal{R})=\alpha(G)
.

Corollary 2
.

The standard point relaxation, equivalently the clique relaxation, for MISR has
integrality gap at least
549/272>2.018
.

Proof.

For the family in
Theorem

1
, every point lies in at most two
rectangles. Hence the uniform vector
x_{R}=1/2
is feasible for the point LP,
or equivalently
x_{v}=1/2
is feasible for the clique LP. Therefore

\frac{\alpha^{*}(G)}{\alpha(G)}\geq\frac{|V(G)|/2}{544\cdot 8^{9}}=\frac{1098}{544}=\frac{549}{272}>2.018.

∎

This corollary, together with the
2+\varepsilon
-approximation by Gálvez, Khan, Mari, Mömke, Reddy, and Wiese
[
9
]
, shows that the standard point/clique relaxation cannot by itself yield the best known approximation ratio for MISR.
A larger version of the same construction gives a stronger LP gap
exceeding
2.21
; this is proved in
Section

6
.

It is useful to separate the proof into a weighted template and an unweighted
realization. The weighted template assigns integer weights to the rectangles
in each local package, proves triangle-freeness, and certifies by a dynamic
program that every independent set has weight less than one quarter of the
total weight. The unweighted construction then realizes these integer weights
by replacing each weighted package with separated induced subpackages.

Our construction is inspired by the construction of
Asplund and Grünbaum
[
2
]
, who showed an example of a triangle-free
intersection graph of rectangles with chromatic number at least 6.
In their proof, they use a
twelve-rectangle “filter-bed” (which we call a
package
) with eight
“filters” (which we call
ports
) and use it in a hierarchical,
tree-like construction. We keep this local package architecture, but change
the global layout and use the ports to control independent sets rather than
colorings.

We remark that several choices in our construction are ad-hoc and were obtained by trial and error. We do not currently have good explanations for these. We hope that a simpler and more intuitive construction will eventually be discovered.

The same models were also used to write parts of the text and generate the figures. All mathematical claims, computations, and the final text were reviewed by the authors.

3
Overview of the proof

The proof is built around two targets. First, the rectangle intersection graph
must be triangle-free. This gives a direct lower bound on the piercing number:
if three rectangles shared a point, they would form a triangle, so each
piercing point can hit at most two rectangles. Second, every independent set
must have size less than one quarter of the total number of rectangles.
Together these two facts force

\tau(\mathcal{R})\geq|\mathcal{R}|/2>2\nu(\mathcal{R})-1
, contradicting Wegner’s conjecture.

The geometric layout can be viewed as a controlled version of the
Asplund–Grünbaum filter-bed construction
[
2
]
. In a
filter-bed construction, horizontal structures create windows through which
later vertical structures can interact with them. Here the windows are made
explicit as ports. More concretely, the filter-beds in the
Asplund–Grünbaum gadget become our packages, their filters become the eight
ports
P_{0},\ldots,P_{7}
, and their amplification is replaced by a tree
layout. Each node of the complete
8
-ary tree carries one package. Internal
packages are horizontal, leaf packages are vertical, and rectangles from
different packages meet only through prescribed ancestor-to-leaf port
crossings.

The tree is used to make many long-range interactions without losing local
control. A rectangle chosen in an ancestor package may block certain choices
inside many leaf packages in its subtree, but the effect depends only on which
ports are used, not on the full history of choices above the leaf. This is
why the construction has eight ports: the levels are arranged cyclically, so a
leaf package has a separate port for each congruence class of ancestor levels
modulo
8
.
The geometry thereby records a large recursive combinatorial object using only
a small, repeated local gadget.

Triangle-freeness is protected in two layers. Inside one package the local
intersection graph is explicitly triangle-free. Between packages, every edge
comes from a port crossing. If rectangles from more than one
package formed a triangle, then two of the rectangles would lie in one package
and would have to use the same
port; by construction, rectangles using a fixed port are pairwise disjoint.
Thus the port rule prevents the recursive filter-bed layout from creating an
unwanted triangle.
We first prove a weighted version. The weights of the rectangles were found by a finite auxiliary linear
program, recorded in
Appendix

B
, but the proof does not rely on
optimality of that program. Once the weights are fixed, a dynamic program
bounds the weight of every independent set. In each subtree, the state records
only which leaf ports have already
been blocked by choices made in ancestor packages. The local package chooses a
set
Y
of ports, contributes the best local independent set using only those
ports, and then passes the updated forbidden leaf-port set to each child
subtree according to whether its child port lies in
Y
.
For the chosen weights, this exact computation gives total weight

2196\cdot 8^{9}
and maximum independent-set weight at most

544\cdot 8^{9}
.

The weighted construction is then converted into an unweighted family
by replacing weighted packages with separated copies of suitable induced
subpackages. This step is more delicate than simply creating multiple copies of each rectangle proportional to its weight. The chosen subpackages give exactly the required multiplicities
while keeping the same local independent-set values used by the dynamic
program.

Finally,
Section

6
applies the same filter-bed layout
and dynamic-programming certificate at larger depth to obtain the stated
integrality gap for the standard relaxation.

We note that the constructions presented here are not optimized for size. We
are aware of smaller certified counterexamples, including examples with
comparable integrality gaps. The examples given here are chosen instead for ease of exposition.

How the weights were found

The weights in

only a search tool; the proof uses only the displayed integer weights and the
exact dynamic program in
Appendix

C
. The LP and the
rational solution returned by the solver are recorded in

Appendix

B
.

The LP used tentative nonnegative weights
u_{\ell,r}
for each level

\ell
and rectangle type

as in the construction. It also used variables for the local
values
\lambda(\ell,Y)
and the dynamic-programming values

For every independent local set

whose port set satisfies

Solving this finite LP produced rational weights. After scaling and
