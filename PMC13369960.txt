Evidence-informed prioritization of interventions for integrating hypertension management into cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic care in primary health care: HEARTS 2.0 Phase 3
Authors: Pedro Ordunez, Andres Rosende, Maria Florencia Grande Ratti, Donald J DiPette, Ana Marcela Torres, Esteban Londoño, Sonia Y Angell, Mariana L Rodriguez de la Cerda, Norm R C Campbell, Libardo Rodríguez Martínez, Jeffrey Brettler, Fernando Tortosa, Vilma Irazola, Marc G Jaffe, Ariel Izcovich, Niamh Chapman, Andelys de la Rosa, Ana Claudia de Souza, Miguel Angel Diaz Aguilera, Paula Diaz Valencia, David Flood, Lourdes Jarquin, Taskeen Khan, Javier Mariani, Andrew E Moran, Carolina Neira Ojeda, Emily Ridley, Marcela Rivera, Gonzalo Rodriguez, Cesar A Romero, Santiago Torroba, Yamile Valdes Gonzalez, Natalia Vensentini, Irmgardt A Wellmann, Eric Zuniga, Ludovic Reveiz, Paul K Whelton

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHEARTS in the Americas is the regional adaptation of the WHO Global HEARTS Initiative. This paper presents Phase 3 of HEARTS 2.0, an evidence-informed process to prioritize clinical and health-system interventions to support the operationalization of integrated cardiovascular, kidney, and metabolic (CKM) care in primary health care (PHC) and to inform the update of the HEARTS Clinical Pathway. CKM conditions frequently coexist, sharing common risk factors and pathophysiological mechanisms that exacerbate cardiovascular disease burden. Despite their overlap, clinical practice often remains siloed due to condition-specific guidelines, and its integration remains limited. To prioritize evidence-informed clinical and health-system interventions that support the operationalization of integrated CKM care within PHC and inform the update of the HEARTS Clinical Pathway.MethodsBuilding on previous phases of HEARTS 2.0, a multidisciplinary panel evaluated 45 candidate interventions in different areas. The selection process utilized structured evidence appraisal, Evidence-to-Decision frameworks, and a modified RAND/UCLA appropriateness method involving anonymized rating rounds.ResultsA total of 38 interventions were prioritized for implementation. Although hypertension management remains the primary entry point, the resulting framework integrates early detection of diabetes and chronic kidney disease, lifestyle interventions, risk-based combination pharmacotherapy, task-sharing, and longitudinal follow-up.ConclusionsBy translating dispersed guideline recommendations into implementable interventions, Phase 3 of HEARTS 2.0 provides an evidence-informed basis for advancing integrated CKM care at scale in PHC and guiding the forthcoming update of the HEARTS Clinical Pathway. The resulting framework offers a flexible, scalable approach for countries confronting similar epidemiological and health system challenges.

Introduction

The world is undergoing a rapid demographic transition, marked by population aging, and a rising burden of high blood pressure, overweight and obesity, diabetes mellitus, and chronic kidney disease (CKD).1 These conditions frequently coexist and share common atherogenic pathways that accelerate vascular risk and substantially increase the likelihood of cardiovascular (CVD) morbidity and mortality. Although these trends occur worldwide, their consequences are particularly severe in low- and middle-income countries.2

Cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome has recently been proposed as a framework to understand the biological and clinical interconnections among CVD, CKD, and metabolic disorders. The recognition of CKM syndrome underscores the need for integrated models of care that address multimorbidity rather than isolated diseases.3 However, most clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) continue to focus on individual conditions, and their recommendations are often dispersed across multiple documents. This fragmentation can hinder coordinated clinical decision-making and complicate implementation, particularly in primary health care (PHC).

PHC provides a critical platform for addressing the growing burden of chronic diseases through integrated, team-based approaches. Strengthening PHC systems and simplifying clinical decision-making processes are essential to improving the prevention, detection, and management of hypertension and related CKM conditions.4 Translating evidence from CPGs into practical, implementable tools that can be applied consistently in PHC remains a major challenge for health systems worldwide.5 This approach is aligned with the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) strategic initiative “Better Care for NCDs.”

In response to this challenge, PAHO launched HEARTS in the Americas in 2018 as the regional adaptation of the WHO Global HEARTS Initiative.6 Initially focused on improving hypertension control, HEARTS has evolved into a comprehensive quality-improvement strategy that supports standardized treatment protocols, team-based care, access to essential medicines and technologies, and monitoring systems to strengthen CVD risk management in PHC.7 The initiative, now the largest regional program for improving hypertension control,8 is currently implemented in more than 30 countries across Latin America and the Caribbean.

The HEARTS Clinical Pathway serves as the core operational tool of the initiative, translating guideline recommendations into a structured decision-support instrument for frontline health teams.9 A defining characteristic of the HEARTS Clinical Pathway is its emphasis on simplicity, standardization, and feasibility, enabling consistent implementation across diverse PHC settings. The pathway incorporates a standardized protocol for accurate blood pressure measurement, a pragmatic approach to cardiovascular risk assessment, and a hypertension treatment protocol at its core that is simple, direct, and linear. This protocol is organized into a limited number of sequential steps, specifies recommended medications and doses, and provides clear guidance for treatment intensification when blood pressure remains uncontrolled. It also includes delivery-system guidance that supports the organization and work of PHC teams.9 The pathway is currently implemented in more than 11,000 PHC facilities across the Region, supporting large-scale delivery of standardized cardiovascular care.

HEARTS 2.0 represents the next stage of this development, aiming to expand the hypertension-centered model toward integrated CKM care. Rather than developing a new CPG or generating new clinical recommendations, HEARTS 2.0 seeks to prioritize and operationalize existing evidence-based recommendations supported by high-quality CPGs and consensus documents through a practical clinical pathway designed for PHC implementation. HEARTS 2.0 follows a phased approach to identify, assess, and prioritize interventions that can support the integration of CKM care within routine hypertension management.10

By consolidating these prioritized interventions into a single, actionable clinical pathway tool for PHC, HEARTS 2.0 aims to improve clinical effectiveness and optimize resource utilization, ensuring that the resulting interventions are feasible, acceptable, and scalable. Broad country participation throughout the process, including the assessment of readiness to deliver integrated CKM care in PHC,11 further strengthens transparency, country ownership, and the systematic adoption of best practices.

This paper describes Phase 3 of HEARTS 2.0, the evidence-informed and consensus-based process used to prioritize clinical and health-system interventions to advance integrated CKM care in PHC settings and to inform the update of the HEARTS Clinical Pathway.

Methods

HEARTS 2.0 followed a methodology adapted from PAHO for evidence-informed decision-making and for adapting and implementing evidence-informed guidelines.12,13 HEARTS 2.0 was conducted in three sequential phases: identification of candidate interventions (Phase 1),10 assessment of country readiness for implementation of candidate interventions (Phase 2),11 and evidence-informed prioritization of candidate interventions to support the operationalization of integrated CKM care in PHC and to inform the update of the HEARTS Clinical Pathway (Phase 3).

In Phase 1, an international panel of experts reviewed the existing HEARTS Clinical Pathway (see Figure S1, HEARTS Clinical Pathway in supplementary material) and identified 57 specific opportunities for improvement aimed at incorporating CKM-related interventions. This phase adhered to three key principles: (1) proposed modifications should be built on the existing HEARTS Clinical Pathway; (2) new or modified interventions should be supported by evidence, preferably derived from leading CPGs or international consensus statements; and (3) interventions must be feasible and safe for large-scale implementation across diverse PHC settings. Panel participants were instructed to avoid proposing interventions based on grey literature or unsupported expert opinion. Through a structured consensus process, 45 of these opportunities for improvement were selected as candidate interventions for further evaluation. Importantly, the identification of candidate interventions in Phase 1 did not aim to generate new clinical recommendations but rather to identify opportunities to operationalize recommendations already supported by CPGs and related evidence sources. Detailed methods and results of this phase have been reported previously.10

Phase 2 translated this evidence base into an implementation perspective by assessing the readiness of countries implementing HEARTS in the Americas to deliver the 45 candidate interventions identified in Phase 1. A cross-sectional readiness assessment evaluated availability, feasibility, time to scale, and potential barriers across participating countries. The findings highlighted substantial opportunities for advancing integrated CKM care while identifying implementation challenges across diverse PHC contexts.11

Phase 3 represents the focus of the present report and aims to assess the appropriateness of incorporating each of the 45 candidate interventions into the updated HEARTS Clinical Pathway. Building on the candidate interventions selected in Phase 1 and the country readiness assessment performed in Phase 2, this phase applied a structured, evidence-informed, consensus-based process to prioritize interventions for inclusion in the updated pathway. The process combined structured evidence appraisal, Evidence-to-Decision frameworks, and a modified RAND/UCLA Appropriateness Method to guide the consensus process.

For transparency and reproducibility, a full technical report describing the HEARTS 2.0 Phase 3 process will be published separately by the PAHO.

PAHO convened a Steering Committee, a Methodological Team, and a multidisciplinary Clinical Pathway Development Group (CPDG) to guide Phase 3. The Methodological Team provided expertise in guideline adaptation and evidence appraisal, while the CPDG—comprising clinicians from multiple specialties, pharmacists, nurses, public health professionals, policymakers, and researchers—built on experience from Phases 1 and 2 and was further strengthened by experts in CPGs development and implementation with prior country-level experience. All participants completed conflict-of-interest declarations managed per PAHO procedures,12,13 and neither the Steering Committee nor the Methodological Team participated in scoring the interventions.

The HEARTS 2.0 Phase 3 process was structured into four sequential steps (Figure 1):

Four-step process of Phase 3 of HEARTS 2.0 for updating the Clinical Pathway: (1) Classification of candidate interventions; (2) Evidence identification, appraisal, and synthesis; (3) Appropriateness assessment using a modified RAND/UCLA method; (4) Final consensus and validation for inclusion in the pathway.

Step 1: classification of candidate interventions

The Methodological Team classified the 45 candidate interventions into two categories: (1) evidence-informed interventions, which required structured review, synthesis, and appraisal of the best available evidence guided by specific PICO questions,14 and (2) good practice statements, defined as widely accepted best practices supported by strong indirect evidence or consensus, for which formal evidence review was not required but concise justifications were provided for CPDG consideration.15

Within HEARTS 2.0, the term intervention refers broadly to clinical or health-system-level recommendations, including diagnostic thresholds, lifestyle prescriptions, therapeutic steps, follow-up strategies, and delivery-system actions. This definition extends beyond the conventional PICO intervention concept to encompass the range of actions required to implement integrated CKM care in PHC.

Step 2: evidence identification, appraisal, and GRADE approach

For each candidate intervention, the Methodological Team identified and appraised evidence using the GRADE methodology. High-quality CPGs published in the last five years were prioritized, with searches conducted in BIGG, Medline, and Virtual Health Library, and international guideline repositories. When GRADE-based guidelines were unavailable, systematic reviews or primary sources were considered.

The quality of CPGs, systematic reviews, and trials was evaluated using AGREE II, AMSTAR 2, and RoB 2, respectively, and the overall certainty of evidence was categorized as high, moderate, low, or very low.16

Relevant recommendations were extracted along with the Summary of Findings tables and Evidence-to-Decision frameworks. These were complemented with contextual information from Phase 2 regarding the availability of interventions, feasibility, implementation time, and key barriers for scaling up (see HEARTS 2.0 Phase 3—Summary of evidence in supplementary material). This combined evidence–context package, including concise justifications for good practice statements, provided structured input for Step 3 and guided panel deliberations and scoring on the appropriateness of the 45 candidate interventions.

Step 3: assessment process

Each member of the CPDG evaluated 45 candidate interventions using a modified RAND/UCLA Appropriateness Method. Building on Step 2, participants applied an evidence-informed, consensus-based process to assess the appropriateness of incorporating each intervention into the updated HEARTS Clinical Pathway. The RAND/UCLA Appropriateness Method was selected because it allows integration of evidence summaries with expert judgment in situations where evidence alone is insufficient to guide implementation decisions.17 Descriptive statistics and participant comments guided the discussion.

In the first rating round, 26 participants independently scored interventions on a 9-point Likert scale (1-3 = inappropriate; 4-6 = uncertain; 7-9 = appropriate) using PICO questions, evidence summaries, and contextual inputs. Following this round, 25 interventions were rated as appropriate and 20 as uncertain; the latter were reevaluated in a second round.

Panel members received anonymized participant comments from the first round on the 20 interventions rated as uncertain to inform the second-round scoring using the same 9-point Likert scale. Ultimately, 13 additional interventions were deemed appropriate, while seven remained uncertain.

Step 4: final consensus

The HEARTS 2.0 Steering Committee convened the full CPDG to review the results of Step 3 and conduct final deliberations. The Methodological Team presented median scores and interquartile ranges for all 45 interventions. Clinical and policy implications were then discussed to inform interpretation of the findings. All procedures adhered to PAHO standards, including anonymous individual ratings and detailed documentation of the deliberative process.12,13

Results

Phase 3 of HEARTS 2.0 evaluated 45 candidate interventions across diagnosis, treatment, monitoring, and service delivery. Following structured evidence appraisal, appropriateness rating, and anonymized consensus review, 38 interventions were rated as appropriate (median scores ≥7 and interquartile ranges 7-9) and were therefore prioritized for inclusion in the subsequent updated HEARTS Clinical Pathway. Of these, three reinforced recommendations already present in the current HEARTS Clinical Pathway, seven represented revisions to existing pathway recommendations, and 28 were new additions. The remaining seven interventions received uncertain appropriateness ratings and were not prioritized by the CPDG. The prioritized interventions informed by the proposed update of the HEARTS Clinical Pathway, integrating hypertension, diabetes, CKD, dyslipidemia, and secondary CVD prevention, while maintaining hypertension as the primary entry point for patient management. Key prioritized interventions include the use of validated blood pressure measurement devices, accurate diagnosis and early detection of multiple chronic conditions, pragmatic cardiovascular risk assessments, and targeted screening for subclinical conditions such as CKD, dysglycemia, dyslipidemia, and atrial fibrillation.

Lifestyle interventions are considered for the clinical pathway, including advice on salt reduction and increased intake of potassium-rich foods, as well as the use of low-sodium/potassium-enriched salt substitutes in individuals without CKD or risk of hyperkalemia, prescribed physical activity, and structured smoking cessation programs.

Pharmacologic management includes single-pill antihypertensive combinations, high-intensity statins, and SGLT 2 inhibitors, which are applied according to CVD risk, with treatment intensification guided by blood pressure targets. Integrated pharmacologic strategies are therefore applied for patients with high CVD risk, diabetes, or CKD to address CKM care.

Discussion

Phase 3 of HEARTS 2.0 provides an evidence-informed prioritization of clinical and health-system interventions to support the integration of CKM care within the HEARTS framework. Thus, HEARTS 2.0 represents a systematic programmatic effort to operationalize integrated CKM care at scale within routine PHC settings. Implementation of integrated care interventions in PHC has been associated with improved access, continuity of care, and better outcomes for patients with chronic diseases,18 which are core functions of strong PHC systems that provide the foundation for integrated service delivery.19

This prioritization was achieved through a structured process combining evidence appraisal, appropriateness assessment, and expert consensus, building on the sequenced CKM framework identified in Phase 1 and further contextualized through the readiness assessment conducted in Phase 2.11 This evidence and context-informed approach strengthens the relevance and feasibility of the prioritized interventions for real-world PHC implementation.

The 38 prioritized interventions are supported by high-quality evidence and recommendations widely supported by high-quality CPGs and consensus documents. Seven interventions with uncertain appropriateness ratings were not prioritized due to considerations related to evidence quality, safety, feasibility, health-system applicability, or lack of alignment with the core HEARTS model. Detailed supporting documentation is provided in the Supplementary Material; selected illustrative examples are highlighted below.

For instance, reinforcing the exclusive use of clinically validated blood pressure measuring devices is essential, as accurate blood pressure measurement underpins hypertension diagnosis and management.20 Similarly, identifying and addressing multiple risk factors contributing to CKD in a standardized manner is essential for both prevention and management.21 WHO guidelines also recommend lower-sodium/potassium enriched salt substitutes, prescribed physical activity, and tobacco cessation to reduce CVD risk.22–24 Integrating these measures within the HEARTS framework facilitates the translation of guideline recommendations in routine clinical practice and aligns lifestyle and pharmacological interventions to optimize CVD risk management.

Thirteen of the 38 prioritized interventions relate to pharmacological treatment. These include renewed emphasis on single-pill antihypertensive combination therapy, reflecting calls to expand its use,25,26 and emerging evidence supporting its effectiveness.27 Several recommendations also reinforce the use of statins for both primary and secondary prevention of CVD, particularly by emphasizing maximal statin dosing in secondary prevention to achieve optimal lipid lowering and CVD risk reduction.28 A notable addition is the introduction of SGLT2 inhibitors, with indications for patients with CKD,29 diabetes, and established CVD,30 or heart failure.31 Importantly, all medicines included among the panel’s prioritized interventions are already listed in the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines,32 which may facilitate their potential incorporation into national formularies and pooled procurement mechanisms, and support improvements in availability, affordability, and equitable access.

Building on the established HEARTS hypertension pathway,33 and aligned with the WHO 2021 Guideline for the Pharmacological Treatment of Hypertension in Adults25 and the HEARTS hypertension control drivers,34 this phase extends a hypertension-centered model to a more comprehensive CKM approach while remaining feasible for large-scale implementation in PHC and reaffirming the strategic value and instrumental role of hypertension control as a tracer condition for broader chronic disease prevention and management programs.

The prioritized interventions provide the foundation for informing the update of the HEARTS Clinical Pathway as an operational framework for frontline clinicians and PHC teams. This approach complements existing CPGs rather than replacing them. By organizing and harmonizing existing guideline recommendations into a simple and standardized sequence of actions, the updated pathway is expected to facilitate clinical decision-making across comorbidities and support integrated, high-quality CKM management. Standardized CKM care may also improve risk stratification, promote rational use of medicines, strengthen team-based care, and enhance continuity of care supported by health information systems.35

HEARTS 2.0 also underscores the value of coordination among CPG developers, including health authorities, professional societies, and academic institutions. Coordination across CKM-related CPGs is essential to improve the quality and consistency of clinical practice and to reduce the duplication and proliferation of CPGs.36

Strengths and limitations

Phase 3 of HEARTS 2.0 applied a structured methodology to prioritize interventions and to inform the update of the HEARTS Clinical Pathway. PAHO, in collaboration with countries and partners, convened a multidisciplinary expert panel and ensured adherence to a transparent process, which contributes to the initiative’s credibility and regional relevance. The methodology combined structured evidence appraisal with expert consensus, integrating high-quality CPGs with context-specific professional judgment.

Although Phase 3 was not designed to develop de novo a CPG, the use of GRADE and anonymized RAND/UCLA consensus rounds strengthened rigor, transparency, and reproducibility, while reducing the risk of individual influence and dominance bias during the panel deliberations. The panel’s multidisciplinary composition ensured consideration of feasibility, equity, patient preferences, and implementation factors. Reliance on existing CPGs, rather than de novo systematic reviews, enabled timely appraisal and prioritization of a broad set of relevant interventions.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. Reliance on existing CPGs and systematic reviews constrained the assessment to the evidence available at the time, including any gaps or inconsistencies. Distinguishing good practice statements from evidence-informed interventions required expert judgment, and consensus outcomes may have been influenced by panel composition. Cost-effectiveness was not assessed as economic evaluation was beyond the scope of this phase and will need to be considered during national adaptation and implementation planning. In addition, the HEARTS 2.0 process may not yet reflect developments in areas where the evidence base is rapidly evolving or where new advances have emerged in parallel with its development. For example, the expanding evidence on GLP-1 receptor agonists, particularly following their recent inclusion in the WHO Essential Medicines List,32 may provide additional considerations for future updates, as may evolving recommendations such as the forthcoming 2025 ACC/AHA hypertension guideline.37 These developments highlight the opportunity for HEARTS to continue functioning as a living framework, capable of incorporating new evidence and recommendations as they emerge.

Next step

The next step of the HEARTS 2.0 process will be the development of the updated HEARTS 2.0 Clinical Pathway for CKM care in PHC. This new pathway will retain hypertension management as the primary entry point and backbone, while integrating additional interventions in a coherent manner and preserving the simple, standardized, and user-friendly characteristics of the existing clinical pathway.

In conclusion, Phase 3 of HEARTS 2.0 evaluated and prioritized 38 evidence-informed clinical and health-system interventions through a structured process combining evidence appraisal and expert consensus, providing a transparent and reproducible approach to prioritize interventions supported by the best available evidence that are most suitable for integration within a practical clinical pathway for PHC teams. This process builds on existing CPGs and other high-quality sources of evidence for CKM-related conditions, not to generate new clinical recommendations, but to prioritize those that can be integrated and harmonized into a coherent and implementable instrument for routine PHC practice.

By translating dispersed recommendations from multiple CPGs and practice statements into implementable actions, this prioritization process provides the foundation for the forthcoming revision of the standardized and integrated HEARTS Clinical Pathway. The clinical pathway emerging from this process could further strengthen hypertension prevention and control while reducing fragmentation in the management of CKM-related conditions across PHC and promoting more coordinated, patient-centered care. Although the process was conducted within the context of Latin America and the Caribbean, the methodological approach may be applicable to other settings facing similar epidemiological and health system challenges.
