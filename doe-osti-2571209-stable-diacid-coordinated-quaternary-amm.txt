Public Executive Summary
Current automotive fuel cells that use sulfonated polymer-based proton exchange membranes
are challenging to operate at > 100 °C without humidification. In this project, we developed
polymer electrolyte fuel cells that run at > 100 °C without humidification using a novel class of
proton exchange membranes made from thermos-oxidatively stable acid-coordinated
quaternary ammonium polymers. These fuel cells remove the external humidifiers/demisters
and reduce the size of radiators, thus vastly simplifying fuel cell integration, which increases
tolerance to impurities and improves electrode kinetics of catalysts. Operating fuel cells
without hydration has a significant benefit on fuel cell cost and economic feasibility was
investigated by comparing existing fuel cell systems. This project bridges the scale-up process to
manufacture the fuel cell component through the U.S. DOE L’Innovator project sponsored by
the U.S. DOE Hydrogen and Fuel Cell Technologies Office (HFTO).

Project Activities
The project focuses on developing high-temperature proton exchange membrane fuel cells (HT-PEMFCs)
for transportation applications. To operate a fuel cell for transportation applications, the stability of the
fuel cell under dynamic operating conditions is the most critical requirement. This project achieved fuel
cell stability by introducing ion-pair interaction in the proton exchange membranes. We have started
with a GEN1 membrane based on a quaternized poly(terphenylene) membrane. The GEN1 membrane
was mechanically tough; however, stability with phosphoric acid is low due to the high dissolution rate
of the membrane in phosphoric acid. The GEN1 membrane structure is modified to prepare the GEN2
membrane, which has a crosslinking moiety. The GEN2 membrane showed reasonably high mechanical
properties with phosphoric acid. Later in the project, we developed the GEN3 membranes based on
copolymer structure was developed and down-selected for fuel cell testing. The GEN3 membranes are
commercially available from Orion Polymers Inc. While the fuel cell durability was obtained with the ion-
pair membrane, the performance of the fuel cell was much inferior to the conventional
polybenzimidazole-based HT-PEMFCs. To improve the fuel cell performance, we focused on developing
highly-performing ionomers. We first developed a quaternized polystyrene-based ionomer. However,
the polystyrene ionomer was too hydrophilic, which caused electrode flooding. Therefore, we
developed an ionomer based on fluorophenyl phosphonic acid, which showed improved performance.
Later in the project, we developed a protonated phosphonic acid ionomer with much higher proton
conductivity than the previously developed ionomer. The electrode using the protonated phosphonic
acid ionomer was licensed to Advent Technologies. The performance and durability of the proposed fuel
cells are shown in Figure 1 and Figure 2.
