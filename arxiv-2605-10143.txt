Thompson’s groups and Teichmüller modular groups of generalized Cantor sets
Hiroshige Shiga

Abstract
. Thompson’s groups, which are denoted by F , T F,T and V V , were introduced by R. Thompson. It is known that they are related to various fields in mathematics. In this paper, we establish that Thompson’s groups are regarded as subgroups of Teichmüller modular groups of Teichmüller spaces of generalized Cantor sets. Moreover, Thompson’s groups F F and T T act properly discontinuously on such Teichmüller spaces but Thompson’s group V V does not. In some sense, those results are improvements of the results by E. de Faria, F. P. Gardiner and W. J. Harvey on Thomnpson’s group F F and asymptotic Teichmüller spaces. We also show that Thompson’s groups act infinitely many Teichmüller spaces of generalized Cantor sets.

1.
Introduction

Thompson’s groups, which are denoted by
F,T
and
V
, were introduced by R. Thompson in 1965.
After the introduction, it is noticed that those groups are related to various fields in mathematics.

In terms of the symptotic Teichmüller theory, E. de Faria, F. P. Gardiner and W. J. Harvey
[
4
]
show that Thompson’s group
F
is regarded as a subgroup of the asymptotic conformal mapping class group of a Riemann surface
R
which is the complement of a Cantor set of bounded type.
They also show that the action of the subgroup on the asymptotic Teichmüller space is discrete.

In this paper, we focus on the Teichmüller theory not the aymptotic one, and we consider to realize Thompson’s groups
F,T
and
V
as subgroups of the Teichmüller modular group of a Riemann surface which is the complement of a generalized Cantor set.

Our realization given in §4 is quite quasiconformally geometric.
Because of this, we may consider the action of Thompson’s groups on Teichmüller spaces and discuss the discreteness of the action of those groups on the Teichmüller space.
Actually we show that the action of Thompson group
T
(and
F
) is discrete, but the action of the group
V
is not (Theorem
III
).
Since we consider the action of Thompson’s groups on Teichmüller space not asymptotic one, we may say that our results improve the facts obtained in
[
4
]
in a certain sense.

At the final section, we show that Thompson’s groups act infiinitely many Teichmüller spaces of generalized Cantor sets.

2.1.
Thompson groups

We will give a brief introduction of Thompson’s groups and exhibit some fundamental properties. See

A rational number is called a
dyadic rational number
if it is written by
\frac{k}{2^{n}}
for some
k,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

Thompson’s group
F
is the set of self-homeomorphisms
f
of
[0,1]
with
f(0)=0
which are differentiable except at finitely many dyadic rational numbers and such that the derivatives are power of
2
on the intervals of differentiability.

For each

, we may associate binary trees
D_{f}
for the domain
f
and
R_{f}
for the range as follows.

An interval in
[0,1]
is called a
standard dyadic interval
if it has the form
[\frac{k}{2^{n}},\frac{k+1}{2^{n}}]
for some integers

with
k\leq 2^{n}-1
.
From standard dyadic intervals, we may define a tree
\mathcal{T}
called a
tree of standard dyadic intervals
.

A vertex of
\mathcal{T}
corresponds to a standard dyadic interval.
We denote a vertex by
v_{I}
if it corresponds to a standard dyadic interval
I
.
The vertex corresponding to
[0,1]
is denoted by
v_{0}
and we call it the
root
of
\mathcal{T}
.
Two vertices
v_{I}
and
v_{J}
are joined by an edge
(I,J)
if
J
is either the left half or the right half of
I
.
The edge is called a left edge if
J
is the left half and a right edge otherwise.
Hence,
\mathcal{T}
is a binary tree rooted at
v_{0}
.

A finite subtree

is called an

v_{0}\in S
with two edges and a vertex
v_{I}
of
S

(I,J)
and
(I,J^{\prime})

Vertices with valence

The tree

Let

be the set of leaves of an ordered rooted binary tree

Then,
[0,1]=\cup_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
and they give a partition of
[0,1]
via standard dyadic intervals.
Conversely, a partition of
[0,1]
via standard dyadic intervals gives an ordered rooted binary tree such that the set of the leaves corresponds to the partition.

For
f\in F
, there exist

is differentiable in
(x_{i},x_{i+1})

(i=0,1,\dots n-1)

We denote by

the ordered rooted binary tree for the partition by

R_{f}
for the partition by

We put the number

and for the leaf
f(v_{[x_{n-1},x_{n}]})

(n\in\{1,\dots,n\})
.
We called the pair
(D_{f},R_{f})
a
tree diagram
for
f\in F
.
If

.
On the other hand, for a given

We identify two tree diagrams if they determines the same

Since

is an increasing function, the numbers associated to the leaves of

Such a diagram

Let

Then, there exists

2.2.
Generalized Cantor sets and the tree

For
\omega=(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega:=(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}}
, we may define a generalized Cantor set
E(\omega)
on
[0,1]
.

The construction of
E(\omega)
is similar to that of the standard middle one-third Cantor set.
We start from the unit interval
I_{0}=[0,1]
.
At the first step, we remove an open interval
J_{1}^{1}
of length
q_{1}=q_{1}|I_{0}|
so that the remaining intervals
I_{1}^{1}
,
I_{1}^{2}
have the same length, where the closed interval
I_{1}^{1}
is located at the left of
I_{1}^{2}
.
We put
E_{1}(\omega)=I_{1}^{1}\cup I_{1}^{2}
.
We continue this process inductively to obtain
E_{k}(\omega)=\cup_{j=1}^{2^{k}}I_{k}^{j}
; for each closed interval
I_{k-1}^{j}

(j\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{k-1}\})
, we remove open interval
J_{k}^{2j-1}
of length
q_{k}|I_{k-1}^{j}|
so that
I_{k-1}^{j}\setminus J_{k}^{2j-1}
consists of two closed intervals
I_{k}^{2j-1}
,
I_{k}^{2j}
of the same length, where
I_{k}^{2j-1}
is at the left of
I_{k}^{2j}
.
We put
E_{k}(\omega)=\cup_{j=1}^{2^{k}}I_{k}^{j}
, which is the union of
2^{k}
closed intervals of the same length and

consists of
2^{k}-1

J_{k}^{1},\dots,J_{k}^{2^{k}-1}
.
The interval
J_{k}^{j}
is located between
I_{k}^{j}
and
I_{k}^{j+1}
.
Then,
E(\omega):=\cap_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k}(\omega)
is a Cantor set on
[0,1]
and we call it the generalized Cantor set for
\omega\in\Omega
.

The canonical pants decomposition (cf.
[

]
).

We put

for
\omega=(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega:=(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}}

The surface

First, we take simple closed geodesics

Inductively, we construct a pair of pants

Each

Each geodesic

From the canonical pants decomposition of

The vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{C}

We take the vertex

The vertex corresponding to the pants geodesic

The vertex

2.3.
Teichmüller space and Teichmüller modular group

We will give brief explanations about Teichmüller spaces, quasiconformal mappings and Teichmüller modular groups.
The reader is referred to Ahlfors
[
1
]
, Lehto-Vitanen
[
9
]
and Imayoshi-Taniguchi
[
7
]
for details.

Let
X_{0}
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface.
A pair
(X,f)
of a Riemann surface
X
which is quasiconformally equivalent to
X_{0}
and a quasiconformal mapping
f:X_{0}\to X
is called a
marked Riemann surface
.
Two marked Riemann surfaces
(X_{i},f_{i})

(i=1,2)
is (Teichmüller) equivalent if there exists a conformal mapping
F:X_{1}\to X_{2}
such that
f_{2}\circ f_{1}^{-1}
is homotopic to
F
with respect to the homotopy keeping any point at the ideal boundary fixed.
We denote by
[X,f]
the equivalence class of
(X,f)
and the set of all marked Riemann surfaces is called
Teichmüller space of
X_{0}
and it is denoted by
\textrm{Teich}(X_{0})
.

The Teichmüller space
\textrm{Teich}(X_{0})
has a natural complex structure while it is of infinite dimensional if
X_{0}
is of topologically infinite type.
The Teichmüller space
\textrm{Teich}(X_{0})
has also a natural complete distance called
the Teichmüller distance

d_{T}
defined by

d_{T}([X_{1},f_{1}],[X_{2},f_{2}])=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{f}\log K(f),

where
K(f)=(1+\|\mu_{f}\|_{\infty})(1-\|\mu_{f}\|_{\infty})^{-1}
for
\mu_{f}=f_{\bar{z}}/f_{z}
, the maximal dilatation of
f
, and the infimum is taken over all quasiconformal mappings
f
homotopic to
f_{2}\circ f_{1}^{-1}
.

The
Teichmüller modular group

\textrm{Mod}(X_{0})
is the set of homotopy classes
[\varphi]
of quasiconformal self-mappings
\varphi
of
X_{0}
.
The Teichmüller modular group
\textrm{Mod}(X_{0})
acts on
\textrm{Teich}(X_{0})
by

\chi_{[\varphi]}([X,f])=[X,f\circ\varphi^{-1}].

It is easy to see that the action is isometric with respect to the Teichmüller distance.
If the dimension of the Teichmüller space is finite, then the action of the Teichmüller modular group is properly discontinuous.
However, if the Teichmüller space is infinite dimensional, then the action is not properly discontinuous in general.

Here, we exhibit a fundamental fact on the hyperbolic geometry and quasiconformal mappings which we frequently use as “Wolpert’s lemma” in this paper.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\varphi:R\to R^{\prime}
be a quasiconformal mapping between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces
R,R^{\prime}
.
Then, for any closed hyperbolic geodesic
\gamma\subset R
, we have

\frac{1}{K(\varphi)}\ell_{R}(\gamma)\leq\ell_{R^{\prime}}([\varphi(\gamma)])\leq K(\varphi)\ell_{R}(\gamma).

Therefore, the extension theorem for quasiconformal mappings (cf.

]
,
[

∎

Now, we consider the Teichmüller modular group

From Lemma

A Möbius transformation

We say that
[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))
is of
positive order
if
[\varphi](a),[\varphi](b),[\varphi](c)
are located in positive order whenever
a,b,c\in E(\omega)
are located in positive order.
The set of positive order elements of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))
is denoted by
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{PO}}
, and it is also a subgroup of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))
.

For a pants curve
\gamma
, we put

E(\omega)_{\gamma}=\Delta(\gamma)\cap E(\omega),

where
\Delta(\gamma)
is the Jordan domain bounded by
\gamma
.
A quasiconformal mapping
\varphi:X(\omega)\to X(\omega)
is called
piecewise order preserving
if there exists pants curves
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N}
such that

(1)

E(\omega)=\sqcup_{i=1}^{N}E(\omega)_{\gamma_{i}}
;

(2)

\varphi|_{E(\omega)_{\gamma_{i}}}:E(\omega)_{\gamma_{i}}\to E(\omega)_{\varphi(\gamma_{i})}
is order preserving.

An element
[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))
is called
piecewise order preserving
if a representative
\varphi
of
[\varphi]
is piecewise order preserving.
This definition is well-defined, namely, it does not depend on the choice of representatives.
The set of piecewise order preserving elements in
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))
is denoted by
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{POP}}
. It is also a subgroup of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))
.

Lemma 2.2
.

\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{OP}}\subset\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{PO}}\subset\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{POP}}.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{OP}}\subset\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{PO}}
and
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{OP}}\subset\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{POP}}
.
So, we show that
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{PO}}\subset\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{POP}}
.

Take
[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{PO}}
.
If
\varphi(0)=0
, then
[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{OP}}
and there is nothing to prove.
We may assume that
x_{0}:=\varphi(0)\not=0
.

Since
\varphi
is a mapping of positive order, we see that
E(\omega)\cap(x_{0},x_{0}+\varepsilon)\not=\emptyset
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Hence,
x_{0}\not=1
.
By the same reason, we have
x_{1}:=\varphi(1)\not=0
,
x_{1}<x_{0}
and
E(\omega)\cap(x_{1},x_{0})=\emptyset
.
We also see for
y_{0}:=\varphi^{-1}(0),y_{1}:=\varphi^{-1}(1)

that
0<y_{1}<y_{0}<1
and
E(\omega)\cap(y_{1},y_{0})=\emptyset
.
So, we conclude that

\varphi(E(\omega)\cap[0,y_{1}])=E(\omega)\cap[x_{0},1],\quad\varphi(E(\omega)\cap[y_{0},1])=E(\omega)\cap[0,x_{1}]

and
E(\omega)=(E(\omega)\cap[x_{0},1])\cup(E(\omega)\cap[0,x_{1}])
.
We may find pants curves
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N},\gamma_{N+1},\dots,\gamma_{N+M}
so that

E(\omega)\cap[0,x_{1}]=\cup_{i=1}^{N}E(\omega)_{\gamma_{i}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad E(\omega)\cap[x_{0},1]=\cup_{i=N+1}^{M}E(\omega)_{\gamma_{i}}.

It is not hard to see that
\varphi|_{E(\omega)_{\gamma_{i}}}
are order preserving
(i=1,\dots,N+M)
.
Thus, we verify that
[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{POP}}
.
∎

2.5.
Main results.

Now, we exhibit our main results.

We say that a sequence
\omega=(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega
satisfies the
bounded rate divergence
(BRD) condition if
\lim_{n\to\infty}q_{n}=1
and

\left|\log\frac{1-q_{n}}{1-q_{n+1}}\right|<M\quad(n=1,2,\dots)

hold for some
M<\infty
.

Theorem I
.

Suppose that
\omega\in\Omega
satisfies the BRD condition.
Then, there exists a homomorphism
\Theta
which induces the following short exact sequences;

(2.1)

\displaystyle 0\to\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{0}\xrightarrow{\iota}\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{OP}}\xrightarrow{\Theta}F\to 0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0\to\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{0}\xrightarrow{\iota}\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{PO}}\xrightarrow{\Theta}T\to 0,

and

(2.3)

0\to\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{0}\xrightarrow{\iota}\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{POP}}\xrightarrow{\Theta}V\to 0,

where
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{0}=\{[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))\mid[\varphi]|_{E(\omega)}=id.\}
and
\iota
is the inclusion map.

As we see in the later section, the construction of
\Theta
is quite geometric.
Thus, we may find subgroups of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))
which are isomorphic to Thompson’s groups
F,T
and
V
via
\Theta
, respectively.

Let
J(z)=\overline{z}
be the complex conjugate.
We define subgroups
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{F}
,
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
and
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{V}
as follows;

\displaystyle\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{F}=\{[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{OP}}\mid\mbox{$J\circ\varphi$ is homotopic to $\varphi\circ J$}\},

\displaystyle\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}=\{[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{PO}}\mid\mbox{$J\circ\varphi$ is homotopic to $\varphi\circ J$}\}

and

\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{V}=\{[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))^{\textrm{POP}}\mid\mbox{$J\circ\varphi$ is homotopic to $\varphi\circ J$}\}.

Theorem II
.

Suppose that
\omega\in\Omega
satisfies the BRD condition.
Let
G
be
F,T
or
V
.
The homomorphism
\Theta
gives a group isomorphism between the groups
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{G}
and
G
.

Finally, we consider the actions of those groups on
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))
.

Theorem III
.

Suppose that
\omega\in\Omega
satisfies the BRD condition.
Then,
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{F}
and
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
act properly discontinuously on
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))
.
However, the action of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{V}
on
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))
is not properly discontinuous.

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
4
]
, E. de Faria, F. P. Gardiner and W. J. Harvey show the same statements as in Theorems
I
–
III
for Thompson’s group
F
, the asymptotically conformal Teichmüller modular group and the asymptotic Teichmüller space of the standard middle one-third Cantor set.

be elements given in §4.
Since there exist
\varphi_{i}\in[\Phi_{i}]

(i=1,2)
such that
\varphi_{i}:X(\omega)\to X(\omega)
are symmetric with respect to the real line
\mathbb{R}
, we see that
[\Phi_{0}],[\Phi_{1}]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{F}
,
[\Phi_{2}]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
and
[\Phi_{3}]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{V}
.
Hence, we have

\Theta(\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{F})=F,\quad\Theta(\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T})=T,

and

\Theta(\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{V})=V.

It suffices to show that
\Theta
is injective on those subgroups of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))
.

Let
[\varphi]
be in
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{F}
with
\Theta([\varphi])=id.

Then,
\varphi|_{E(\omega)}=id.
and
\varphi(\gamma_{d}^{j})
are homotopic to
\gamma_{d}^{j}

(j=1,2,\dots,2^{d})
for a sufficiently large
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
We show that
\varphi(\gamma_{d-1}^{j})
is homotopic to
\gamma_{d-1}^{j}

(j\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{d-1}\})
.

We put
\gamma_{\varphi}^{j}:=[\varphi(\gamma_{d-1}^{j})]
.
Since
[J\circ\varphi]=[\varphi\circ J]
and
J(\gamma_{d-1}^{j})=\gamma_{d-1}^{j}
, we have

\gamma_{\varphi}^{j}=[\varphi(\gamma_{d-1}^{j})]=[\varphi\circ J(\gamma_{d-1}^{j})]=[J\circ\varphi(\gamma_{d-1}^{j})].

Hence,
J(\gamma_{\varphi}^{j})
is homotopic to
J\circ J(\varphi(\gamma_{d-1}^{j}))=\varphi(\gamma_{d-1}^{j})
.
However,
J
is a hyperbolic isometry on
X(\omega)
. Therefore,
J(\gamma_{\varphi}^{j})
is still a geodesic and we have
J(\gamma_{\varphi}^{j})=[\varphi(\gamma_{d-1}^{j})]=\gamma_{\varphi}^{j}
.
Namely, a simple closed curve
\gamma_{\varphi}^{j}
is symmetric with respect to the real line
\mathbb{R}
and we have
\gamma_{\varphi}^{j}=(\gamma_{\varphi}^{j}\cap\overline{\mathbb{H}})\cup J(\gamma_{\varphi}^{j}\cap\mathbb{H})
.
Since the simple closed curve surrounds
\gamma_{d}^{2j-1}
and
\gamma_{d}^{2j}
, it has to be homotopic to
\gamma_{d-1}^{j}
and we have
\gamma_{\varphi}^{j}=\gamma_{d-1}^{j}
.
This implies
\varphi(\gamma_{d-1}^{j})
is homotopic to
\gamma_{d-1}^{j}
and
the mapping
\varphi
keeps the homotopy class of a pants curve of depth
k-1
fixed.

Continuing this argument above, we conclude that
\varphi
keeps the homotopy class of every pants curve fixed.
Therefore, if
\varphi
is not homotopic to the identity on
X(\omega)
, the mapping class
[\varphi]
has to contain a Dehn twist with respect to some pants curve.
However, because of
[\varphi\circ J]=[J\circ\varphi]
, it dose not occur.
So, we conclude that
[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{F}\cap\textrm{Ker}(\Theta)
is the identity and
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{F}
is isomorphic to
F
via
\Theta
.

The above argument also works for
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
and
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{V}
.
Thus, we verify that
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
and
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{V}
are isomorphic to
T
and
V
via
\Theta
, respectively.

6.
Proof of Theorem
III

Since
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{F}\subset\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
, it suffices to show that
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
acts properly discontinuously on
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))
.
The following proposition almost shows the discreteness of the action of the group.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that
\omega
satisfies the BRD condition.
For
K\geq 1
, we define a subset
\Delta(K)
of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
by

\Delta(K)=\{[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}\mid K([\varphi])<K\},

where

K([\varphi])=\inf\{K(\psi)\mid\psi\in[\varphi]\}.

Then,
{}^{\#}\Delta(K)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
be in
\Delta(K)
.
Then, there exists a quasiconformal mapping
\varphi:X(\omega)\to X(\omega)
in
[\varphi]
of positive order such that
K(\varphi)<K
.
Let
d(K)\in\mathbb{N}
be the quantity given in Lemma
4.1
.
Then, the map
\varphi
sends any pants curve of depth
d\geq d(K)
to a pants curve, and the positive ordered tree diagram
(D_{[\varphi]}^{d(K)},R_{[\varphi]}^{d(K)})
for
[\varphi]
obtained in §4 determines
\Theta([\varphi])
.
Note that
D_{[\varphi]}^{d(K)}
is determined by
\partial W_{d(K)}
, and
R_{[\varphi]}^{d(K)}
is determined by
[\varphi(\gamma_{d(K)}^{1}],[\varphi(\gamma_{d(K)}^{2})],\dots,[\varphi(\gamma_{d(K)}^{2^{d(K)}})]
.

For each
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

M_{d}=\max_{j=1,2,\dots,2^{d}}\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{d}^{j}),

and

m_{d}=\min_{j=1,2,\dots,2^{d}}\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{d}^{j}).

Since
\lim_{d\to\infty}\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{d}^{j})=0
, we see that

N(K):={}^{\#}\left\{(d,j)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\mid\frac{1}{K}m_{d(K)}\leq\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{d}^{j})\leq KM_{d(K)}\right\}<\infty.

On the other hand, it follows from Wolpert’s lemma that

\frac{1}{K}\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{d(K)}^{j})\leq\ell_{X(\omega)}([\varphi(\gamma_{d(K)}^{j})])\leq K\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{d(K)}^{j}).

Therefore, the number of possibilities of
[\varphi(\gamma_{d(K)}^{j})]
is at most
N(K)

(j\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{d(K)}\})
and the number of possibilities of
R_{[\varphi]}^{d(K)}
is at most
N(K)^{2^{d(K)}}
.
This means that the number of possibilities of
\Theta([\varphi])
for
[\varphi]\in\Delta(K)
is at most
N(K)^{2^{d(K)}}
.
Since
\Theta:\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}\to T
is injective, we have
{}^{\#}\Delta(K)\leq N(K)^{2^{d(K)}}<\infty
.
∎

Now, we show that the action of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
on
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))
is properly discontinuous.
For
\varepsilon>0
and
P=[X,f]\in\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))
, we put

B(P,\varepsilon)=\{Q\in\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))\mid d_{T}(P,Q)<\varepsilon\}.

Suppose that
\chi_{[\varphi]}(P)\in B(P,\varepsilon)
for
[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
.
Then, we see that an inequality

d_{T}(P_{0},\chi_{[\varphi]}(P_{0}))\leq d_{T}(P_{0},P)+d_{T}(P,\chi_{[\varphi]}(P))+d_{T}(\chi_{[\varphi]}(P),\chi_{[\varphi]}(P_{0}))

holds for
P_{0}=[X(\omega),id.]
.

Since
\chi_{[\varphi]}
is an isometry with respect to the Teichmüller distance, we have

d_{T}(P_{0},\chi_{[\varphi]}(P_{0}))\leq\varepsilon+2d_{T}(P_{0},P).

By the definition of the Teichmüller distance,
d_{T}(P_{0},\chi_{[\varphi]}(P_{0}))=\log K([\varphi])
.
Thus, we see
[\varphi]\in\Delta(\exp\{\varepsilon+2d_{T}(P_{0},P)\}
.
Therefore, it follows from Proposition
6.1
that the set

\{[\varphi]\in\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}\mid\chi_{[\varphi]}(P)\in B(P,\varepsilon)\}

is finite.
We conclude that the action of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
is properly discontinuous on
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))
.

Next, we show that the action of
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{V}
is not properly discontinuous.
To show this, it suffices to show that there exists a sequence
\{[\Psi_{n}]\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of distinct elements in

.

We look at closed intervals
I_{n+1}^{1},I_{n+1}^{2}

.

I_{n+1}^{1}=[0,\frac{1-q_{n}}{2}|I_{n}^{1}|]

Hence, annuli

\displaystyle U_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{|I_{n}^{1}|}{2}<\left|z-\frac{|I_{n}^{1}|}{2}\right|<\frac{1+3q_{n}}{2(1-q_{n})}|I_{n}^{1}|\right\},

\displaystyle U_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle=

7.
Examples

We have shown that
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
acts properly discontinuously on the Teichmüller space
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))
if
\omega\in\Omega
satisfies the BRD condition.
Since
\textrm{Mod}(X(\omega))_{T}
is isomorphic to Thompson’s group
T
via
\Theta
, we may say that the action of Thompson’s group
T
on
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))
is properly discontinuous.
In this section, we show that there are infinitely many Teichmüller spaces of generalized Cantor sets where the action of Thompson’s group
T
is properly discontinuous.

From a result of our previous paper
[
11
]
, we obtain the following.

Proposition 7.1
.

Suppose tht
\omega=(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
\omega^{\prime}=(q_{n}^{\prime})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega
are increasing, that is,
q_{n}\leq q_{n+1},q_{n}^{\prime}\leq q_{n+1}^{\prime}

(n\in\mathbb{N})
.
If
\lim_{n\to\infty}q_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}q_{n}^{\prime}=1
and

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\log\frac{\log(1-q_{n})}{\log(1-q_{n}^{\prime})}\right|=\infty,

then
X(\omega)
and
X(\omega^{\prime})
are not quasiconformally equivalent to each other.
In particular,
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega))\not=\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega^{\prime}))
.

We define functions
\log^{(1)}x
on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
by
\log^{(1)}x=\log x
.
Functions
\log^{(k)}x

(k\in\mathbb{N})
are inductively defined by

\log^{(k+1)}x=\begin{cases}1,&0<x\leq e_{k}\\
\log\log^{(k)}x,&e_{k}<x\end{cases}

where
e_{k}
is the positive number with
\log^{(k)}e_{k}=e
.

We take
\omega_{k}=(q_{n}^{(k)})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
as

q_{n}^{(k)}=1-\frac{1}{2\log^{(k)}n},n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then,
\omega_{k}
are increasing
(k=1,2,\dots)
and if
k^{\prime}>k
, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(1-q_{n}^{(k^{\prime})})}{\log(1-q_{n}^{(k)})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log^{(k^{\prime}+1)}n}{\log^{(k+1)}n}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log^{(k^{\prime}-k)}N}{N}=0.

It follows from Proposition
7.1
that
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega_{k}))\not=\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega_{k^{\prime}})
if
k\not=k^{\prime}
.

On the other hand, it is not hard to see that each
\omega_{k}
satisfies the BRD condition.
Thus, we verify that Thompson’s group
T
acts properly discontinuously on all
\textrm{Teich}(X(\omega_{k}))

(k=1,2,\dots)
.
