Pesticide active substances that do not require a review of the existing maximum residue levels under Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRegulation (EC) No 396/2005 establishes the rules governing the setting and the review of pesticide maximum residue levels (MRLs) at European level. According to Article 12(1) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) shall provide within 12 months from the date of the inclusion or non‐inclusion of an active substance in Annex I to Directive 91/414/EEC a reasoned opinion on the review of the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for that active substance. Article 12(2) of that Regulation stipulates that EFSA shall provide by 1 September 2009 a reasoned opinion on the review of the existing MRLs for all active substances included in Annex I to Directive 91/414/EEC before 2 September 2008. Among the active substances subject to review under Article 12(1) or Article 12(2) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA identified three active substances for which a review of MRLs is no longer considered necessary. This includes two substances that were temporarily listed in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 by risk managers pending finalisation of their evaluation under Directive 91/414/EEC or Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 as well as the publication of EFSA's reasoned opinion in accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. EFSA prepared a statement explaining the reasons why a review of MRLs for these substances became obsolete. The relevant question numbers are considered addressed by this statement.

SUMMARY

Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 establishes the rules governing the setting and the review of pesticide maximum residue levels (MRLs) at European level.

According to Article 12(1) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA shall provide within 12 months from the date of the inclusion or non‐inclusion of an active substance in Annex I to Directive 91/414/EEC (repealed by Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009) a reasoned opinion on the review of the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for that active substance. Article 12(2) of that Regulation stipulates that, for all active substances included in Annex I to Directive 91/414/EEC before 2 September 2008, EFSA shall provide (originally by 1 September 2009) a reasoned opinion on the review of the existing MRLs.

According to the legal provisions, EFSA shall base its reasoned opinion on the relevant assessment report prepared under Directive 91/414/EEC or Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The reasoned opinion should cover all pesticide residues data relevant to the risk assessment and MRL setting for a given active substance, including analytical methods and limit of determination (LOD) for enforcement of the proposed MRLs. All proposed MRLs should accommodate uses authorised within the European Union (EU) as well as any uses authorised in third countries that have a significant impact on international trade.

Among the active substances subject to review under Article 12(1) and 12(2) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA identified three active substances for which a review of MRLs is no longer considered necessary, including two substances that were temporarily included in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 by risk managers pending finalisation of their evaluation under Directive 91/414/EEC or Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 as well as submission of EFSA's reasoned opinion in accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

In May 2026, EFSA prepared a statement explaining the reasons why a review of MRLs for these substances became obsolete. The corresponding question numbers are considered addressed by this statement.

The statement was circulated to Member States for consultation via a written procedure before finalisation.

INTRODUCTION

Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 1 establishes the rules governing the setting and the review of pesticide maximum residue levels (MRLs) at European level.

In accordance with Article 12(1) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) shall provide within 12 months from the date of the inclusion or non‐inclusion of an active substance in Annex I to Directive 91/414/EEC a reasoned opinion on the review of the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for that active substance. In addition, article 12(2) of that Regulation stipulates that for all active substances included in Annex I to Directive 91/414/EEC 2 (repealed by Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 3 ) before 2 September 2008 EFSA shall provide (originally by 1 September 2009) a reasoned opinion on the review of the existing MRLs.

In line with the legal provisions, EFSA shall base its reasoned opinion on the relevant assessment report prepared under Directive 91/414/EEC or Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The reasoned opinion should cover all pesticide residues data relevant to the risk assessment and MRL setting for a given active substance, including analytical methods and limit of determination (LOD) for enforcement of the proposed MRLs. All proposed MRLs should accommodate uses authorised within the European Union (EU) as well as any uses authorised in third countries that have a significant impact on international trade.

Furthermore, based on Article 5(1) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, active substances of plant protection products evaluated under Directive 91/414/EEC for which no MRLs are required shall be defined and listed in Annex IV to this Regulation, taking into account the uses of those active substances and the matters referred to in points (a), (c) and (d) of Article 14(2). 4

The general principles for the establishment and update of Annex IV are laid down in Article 5 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which requires that for an active substance which shall be included in Annex IV account should be taken of: the use of the active substance;the scientific and technical knowledge available;the results of an assessment of any potential risks to consumers with a high intake and high vulnerability and, where appropriate, to animals;the results of any evaluations and decisions to modify the use of plant protection products.

Among the active substances that need to be reviewed under Article 12(1) and Article 12(2) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA identified three active substances for which a review of MRLs is no longer considered necessary, including two active substances that were included temporarily in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 by risk managers pending finalisation of their evaluation under Directive 91/414/EEC or Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 as well as publication of EFSA's reasoned opinion in accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. EFSA prepared a statement explaining the reasons why a review of MRLs for these substances became obsolete.

The draft statement was circulated to Member States (MSs) for consultation via a written procedure. Comments received by 18 June 2026 were considered during the finalisation of this statement. The collation of comments received on the draft statement is considered as a background document to this statement and is made publicly available (EFSA, 2026).

ASSESSMENT

The following substances have been assessed by EFSA in this statement.

The active substance gibberellic acid has been temporarily included in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 by Commission Regulation (EU) No 588/2014, 5 pending the submission of EFSA's reasoned opinion in accordance with Article 12(1). Following the EFSA peer review of the pesticide risk assessment (EFSA, 2024a), the approval of gibberellic acid was renewed as a low‐risk active substance by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2313. 6

As part of the peer review for the renewal, EFSA concluded that further data on the natural background levels of gibberellins (including gibberellic acid) and the levels and relevance of gibberellic acid metabolites in plant commodities other than grapes were needed to verify whether the criteria for the inclusion of gibberellic acid in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 are met or whether MRLs are needed (EFSA, 2024a).

The data gaps identified in the EFSA conclusion were discussed by the Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed in view of the renewal of the approval of gibberellic acid as a low‐risk substance in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009.

The Committee concluded that, according to the available information, the risk to consumers from exogenous use of gibberellic acid in plant protection products is not expected to increase compared to the risk associated with naturally occurring gibberellic acid and that there is a large margin of safety that covers intake by consumers of all (exogenous and endogenous) gibberellic acids and their metabolites (European Commission, 2025a).

Therefore, during the meeting of the Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed held in November 2025, it was agreed to maintain gibberellic acid in Annex IV on a temporary basis and to close the Article 12 MRL review by the present statement, noting that it will be possible to assess any additional data that might become available in the future under an ad hoc mandate (European Commission, 2025b).

The active substance gibberellins have been temporarily included in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 by Commission Regulation (EU) No 839/2008, 7 pending finalisation of their evaluation under Directive 91/414/EEC and pending submission of EFSA's reasoned opinion in accordance with Article 12(1). Following the EFSA peer review of the pesticide risk assessment (EFSA, 2024b), the approval of gibberellins was renewed as a low‐risk active substance by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2272. 8 As part of the peer review for the renewal, EFSA concluded that further data on the residues levels of metabolites, on the relevance of epoxidised gibberellins for consumers and on the natural background levels of gibberellins in plant commodities other than apple and pears were needed to verify whether the criteria for the inclusion of gibberellic acid in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 are met or whether MRLs are needed (EFSA, 2024b).

The data gaps identified in the EFSA conclusion were discussed by the Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed in view of the renewal of the approval of gibberellins as a low‐risk substance in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009.

The Committee concluded that based on the available information, the risk to consumers from exogenous use of gibberellins (GA4/GA7) in plant protection products is not expected to increase compared to the risk from naturally occurring gibberellins (GA4/GA7), and there is a large margin of safety that covers intake by consumers of all (exogenous and endogenous) gibberellins (GA4/GA7) and their metabolites (European Commission, 2025c).

Therefore, during the meeting of the Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed held in November 2025, it was agreed to maintain gibberellins in Annex IV on a temporary basis and to close the Article 12 MRL review by the present statement, noting that it will be possible to assess any additional data that might become available in the future under an ad hoc mandate (European Commission, 2025b).

The active substance metribuzin was initially included in Annex I to Council Directive 91/414/EEC by Commission Directive 2007/25/EC. 9 Following the EFSA peer review of the pesticide risk assessment (EFSA, 2023), the approval of metribuzin was not renewed on 31 October 2024 by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2806 10 taking effect on 24 November 2024. No Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) are established by the Codex Alimentarius Commission and no import tolerances are currently in place for metribuzin. Consequently, risk managers decided to lower all MRLs for this active substance and its main metabolite metribuzin‐desamino‐diketo to the product‐specific limits of determination (LODs) by Regulation (EU) 2026/1314. 11 Therefore, the review of MRLs for this substance became obsolete.

CONCLUSIONS

Among the active substances that need to be reviewed under Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA identified three active substances for which a review of MRLs is not needed, including two active substances that were already included temporarily in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 by risk managers pending finalisation of their evaluation under Directive 91/414/EEC or Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 and pending submission of EFSA's reasoned opinion in accordance with Article 12(1) or Article 12(2) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. EFSA therefore prepared a statement explaining the reasons why a review of MRLs is no longer necessary for these active substances. The corresponding question numbers are considered addressed by this statement.
