Optimal Shadow Estimation with Minimal Measurement Settings
Zhiyao Yang, Datong Chen, Huangjun Zhu

Abstract
Shadow estimation is a powerful framework for predicting quantum properties from randomized measurements. While 3 3 -design protocols achieve optimal worst-case performance, the minimal number of measurement bases required for such optimality has remained open. Here we prove that Θ ⁡ ( d 2 ) \Theta(d^{2}) measurement bases are both necessary and sufficient for worst-case optimal shadow estimation and construct an explicit basis family. In stark contrast, any state 2 2 -design already suffices for average-case optimality: the mean squared shadow norm of normalized observables is bounded by a universal constant, and we prove strong concentration for Haar-random states, yielding constant sample complexity for generic pure-state fidelity estimation. Easily implementable 2 2 -designs—from mutually unbiased bases, cyclic measurements, or shallow 𝒪 ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) \mathcal{O}(\log n) -depth circuits—enable optimal average-case protocols with remarkably simple measurement strategies. Our results establish a fundamental complexity separation: worst-case estimation requires Θ ⁡ ( d 2 ) \Theta(d^{2}) bases, whereas average-case performance requires only Θ ⁡ ( d ) \Theta(d) bases, with broad implications for quantum information theory and near-term experiments.

A shadow estimation protocol is specified by a state ensemble

{\sum_{i}w_{i}\phi_{i}=\mathds{1}/d}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}(O)=d\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr(\phi_{i}O)\,\phi_{i}.

(1)

When

The sample complexity of estimating

\|O_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}

is defined as follows. For a traceless observable

(the traceless part of
O

\displaystyle\|O_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E},\rho}^{2}\coloneqq d\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr(\phi_{i}\rho)\bigl[\tr\bigl(O_{0}\,\hat{\rho}_{i}\bigr)\bigr]^{2},

(2)

and the state-independent (worst-case) one is
\|O_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\coloneqq\max_{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})}\|O_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E},\rho}^{2}
. The worst-case norm provides a universal guarantee for all unknown states, while its

A key quantity in our analysis is the

is the dimension of the symmetric

\mathcal{H}^{\otimes t}

\mathcal{E}
forms a
state

-design

Scott 2006

\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})
quantifies how closely a 2-design
approximates a 3-design and governs the performance gap in shadow
estimation. For any state 2-design, its magnitude is controlled by:

.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a state 2-design in dimension

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})\leq\frac{(d+2)(d^{2}+2d-1)}{6d^{2}}.

(4)

In particular,

Minimal optimal protocols in the worst-case setting
—We first establish a fundamental lower bound on the worst-case shadow norm achievable with a given number of measurement outcomes.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
consists of
K
pure states in
\mathcal{H}
and induces an IC-POVM. Then

\displaystyle\max_{\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})}\|\psi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\geq\frac{(d^{2}-1)^{2}}{Kd}.

(5)

This result encodes a fundamental trade-off: fewer measurement outcomes necessarily imply larger worst-case shadow norms. Achieving optimal worst-case performance—meaning
\|O\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq C\|O\|_{2}^{2}
for a universal constant
C
—requires at least
\Omega(d^{3})
outcomes, or equivalently
\Omega(d^{2})
orthonormal bases, given that each basis corresponds to
d
outcomes. Incidentally, when
\mathcal{E}
forms a state 2-design, the worst-case shadow norm satisfies
\|O\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq(d+1)\|O\|_{2}^{2}

Innocenti et al. 2023
;
Zhang et al. 2024
;
Wang and Cui 2024
. For typical 2-designs consisting of
\mathcal{O}(d^{2})
states—such as SICs and complete sets of MUBs—the lower and upper bounds match up to constant factors, demonstrating that standard 2-design measurements are suboptimal for worst-case performance. We now show that the
\Omega(d^{2})
-basis lower bound is tight.

Theorem 1
.

In dimension
d
,
\Theta(d^{2})
measurement bases are both necessary and sufficient for worst-case optimal shadow estimation.

Our explicit construction achieving this scaling rests on two ingredients: phase 3-designs and MUBs. A pure state
\lvert\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
is a phase state with respect to the computational basis
\{\lvert k\rangle\}_{k}
if all its entries have the same magnitude. A
phase
t
-design
is a finite ensemble of phase states whose
t
-th moment matches that of the uniform random-phase (URP) ensemble

\mathcal{T}=\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{k}}\lvert k\rangle\colon\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{k}}\sim\mathrm{U}(1)\right\},

(6)

which geometrically forms a
d
-dimensional torus. Intuitively, a phase
t
-design captures the same statistical correlations as fully random phases up to order
t
, using only finitely many states. Mathematically, it is equivalent to a projective toric
t
-design studied in Ref.
Iosue et al. 2024
.
As shown in the End Matter, a phase 2-design can be constructed from
3p
bases and a phase 3-design from
7p^{2}
bases, where
p
is any prime satisfying
p\geq\max\{d,5\}
and can be chosen such that
p\leq\max\{2d,5\}
. When
d
is not divisible by
3
, the number of bases required for a phase 3-design can be reduced to
3p^{2}
.
Since any phase
t
-design (with
t\geq 1
) is also a state 1-design, it gives rise to a valid POVM.

The construction proceeds as follows. Consider
N\geq 2
MUBs
\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{N}
. For each basis
\mathcal{B}_{j}
, we construct a phase 3-design
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}
with respect to
\mathcal{B}_{j}
. The combined ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{N}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}/N
assigns uniform probability across all sub-ensembles and comprises at most
7Np^{2}
bases in total. MUBs ensure that the combined ensemble probes complementary degrees of freedom: while a single phase 3-design can only access coherence relative to its reference basis, MUBs guarantee that no observable direction is left unexplored.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}_{N}
is the ensemble constructed from
N

with
2\leq N\leq d+1

\displaystyle\|O\|_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{2}\leq\frac{3N^{2}+N}{(N-1)^{2}}\|O\|_{2}^{2}\leq 14\|O\|_{2}^{2}.

(7)

Already for

bases—matching the lower bound from Proposition

N=d+1

3

In practice,
N=2
or
3

\mathcal{E}_{N}
is not a state 2-design unless

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}
and reconstruction map
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{-1}
both admit simple closed forms (see the End Matter), enabling straightforward construction of unbiased estimators.

Minimal optimal protocols for average performance
—We now turn to average performance and show that far fewer measurement bases suffice. We call a protocol average-case optimal if the mean squared shadow norm over a unitary orbit—observables sharing the spectrum of
O
but with arbitrary eigenbases—is bounded by
c\|O\|_{2}^{2}
for a dimension-independent constant

For
O\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
and a unitary ensemble
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\Xi(O,\mathcal{U})\coloneqq\left\{UOU^{\dagger}\mid U\sim\mathcal{U}\right\}.

(8)

Let
\|\Xi(O,\mathcal{U})\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}
denote the mean squared shadow norm averaged over
\mathcal{U}
, and
\|\Xi(O,\mathcal{U})\|_{\mathcal{E},\rho}^{2}
its state-dependent counterpart. Note that
\mathcal{U}
defines only the observable class over which performance is averaged; it plays no role in the measurement protocol. For a unitary 2-design
\mathcal{U}
and a state 2-design
\mathcal{E}

;
Chen and Zhu 2024
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Xi(O,\mathcal{U})\|_{\mathcal{E},\rho}^{2}=\frac{d+1}{d}\|O\|_{2}^{2},

(9)

so any state 2-design is already optimal in the state-dependent setting. However, this is of limited practical interest because
\rho
is usually unknown. The state-independent norm
\|\Xi(O,\mathcal{U})\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}
, providing a universal guarantee regardless of
\rho

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a state
2
-design,
O\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}_{0}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\|\Xi(O,\mathcal{U})\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\sqrt{2}+1\right)\|O\|_{2}^{2}.

The 4-design assumption on

quantifies deviation from a 3-design [

State 2-designs can be constructed from

In dimension

\Theta(d)

Summary and outlook
—We have determined the fundamental measurement complexity of shadow estimation. In the worst case,
\Theta(d^{2})
bases are both necessary and sufficient for optimal performance; our explicit construction from phase 3-designs and MUBs achieves this without requiring exact state 3-design structure. In the average case, only
\Theta(d)
bases suffice: any state 2-design yields a constant mean squared shadow norm governed by
\bar{\Phi}_{3}
. For fidelity estimation, the shadow norm concentrates tightly for Haar-random targets, implying constant sample complexity for generic instances. Together, these results reveal a sharp quadratic gap between worst-case and average-case complexities:
d^{2}
versus
d
bases. For a
10
-qubit system, this reduces the required number of bases from
{\sim}10^{6}
to
{\sim}10^{3}
, demonstrating that the practical cost can be far lower than the worst-case bound suggests.

Looking forward, extending these results to multipartite settings and structured observable classes—such as local Hamiltonians, where problem-specific structure may yield further reductions—remains an important open direction. On the practical side, easily implementable 2-designs—realized via complete sets of MUBs
Zhang et al. 2024
;
Wang and Cui 2024
, cyclic measurements
Gonzalez Avella et al. 2025
, or shallow
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
-depth circuits
Cleve et al. 2016
;
Bertoni et al. 2024
;
Hu et al. 2025
;
Schuster et al. 2025
—already achieve optimal average-case performance. This is especially relevant for decoherence-limited devices, where measurement overhead is a primary bottleneck. Finally, connections with Hamiltonian-driven shadow protocols
Hu and You 2022
;
Liu et al. 2024
offer a promising route: the system’s intrinsic dynamics could naturally generate structured measurements, enabling optimal shadow estimation without external randomization.

Appendix D: Fidelity estimation beyond Haar-random pure states
—The main text establishes concentration for Haar-random target states. Here we show that constant mean squared shadow norms also hold for other physically motivated ensembles.

Proposition 6
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a state 2-design. Then:

(i)
For the uniform random-phase (URP) ensemble with respect to any orthonormal basis,

\displaystyle\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{URP}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\psi_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{48(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)}{d^{3}}}-1.

(22)

(ii)
For
d=2^{n}
and the
n
-qubit Clifford group
\mathrm{Cl}(n)
,

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\mathrm{Cl}(n)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\|U\psi_{0}U^{\dagger}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq 2\sqrt{15}-1\quad\forall\,\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H}).

(23)

URP ensembles
—The URP ensemble models long-time outputs of Hamiltonian evolution with random energy eigenvalues under a no-resonance condition
Mark et al. 2024
, a standard setting in quantum thermalization. The bound Eq. (
22
) holds for the URP ensemble with respect to any orthonormal basis and converges to
\sqrt{48}-1\approx 5.93
as
d\to\infty
, guaranteeing efficient fidelity estimation with any 2-design measurement regardless of system size.

Clifford orbits
—The bound Eq. (
23
) is uniform over all initial states
\psi
and equals
2\sqrt{15}-1\approx 6.75
. It applies to any Clifford orbit, including orbits of stabilizer states, central to quantum error correction, and magic states, relevant to magic state distillation. Since Clifford orbits arise naturally in verification and benchmarking, efficient fidelity estimation for these targets is particularly valuable in practice.

Comparison
—Both bounds are slightly larger than the Haar-random mean of
2\sqrt{2}+1\approx 3.83
, reflecting less randomness in these ensembles. Nevertheless, all three bounds are
\mathcal{O}(1)
, confirming that constant sample complexity for fidelity estimation is a robust structural feature of 2-design measurements, not an artifact of Haar randomness.

S1.5
Auxiliary results on frame potentials

Here we introduce auxiliary results on (normalized) frame potentials and their variants, which are useful for studying shadow norms in shadow estimation.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}=\{\lvert\phi_{i}\rangle,w_{i}\}_{i}
is a pure state ensemble on
\mathcal{H}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
. We define the
t
-th frame potential of
\mathcal{E}
relative to
\psi
and its normalized version as

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\coloneqq\sum_{i}w_{i}\left[\tr(\psi\phi_{i})\right]^{t},\quad\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\coloneqq D_{[t]}\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi).

(S23)

where
D_{[t]}=\binom{d+t-1}{t}
is the dimension of the symmetric subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes t}
. These definitions are also applicable when
t=0
, in which case

\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)=\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)=\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E})=\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E})=D_{[t]}=1
.
By definition,
\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{j}w_{j}\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E},\phi_{j})
, which implies

\min_{\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})}\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\leq\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E})\leq\max_{\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})}\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi).

(S24)

Lemma S3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a state ensemble on
\mathcal{H}
,
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
, and
t
is a positive integer. Then

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{2}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{t-1}(\mathcal{E})\Phi_{t+1}(\mathcal{E}),

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\psi)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{t-1}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\Phi_{t+1}(\mathcal{E},\psi),

(S25)

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\Phi}_{t}^{2}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[t]}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bar{\Phi}_{t-1}(\mathcal{E})\bar{\Phi}_{t+1}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[t-1]}D_{[t+1]}},

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\Phi}_{t}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}{D_{[t]}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bar{\Phi}_{t-1}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\bar{\Phi}_{t+1}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}{D_{[t-1]}D_{[t+1]}}.

(S26)

If
\mathcal{E}
forms a state 2-design, then

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\geq\dfrac{2(d+2)}{3(d+1)}>\dfrac{2}{3},\quad\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\geq\dfrac{(d+2)(d+3)}{3(d+1)^{2}}>\dfrac{1}{3},

(S27)

and the same bounds hold with
\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)
replaced by
\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E})
for
t=3,4
.

Lemma S4
.

Suppose
\psi,\phi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\displaystyle\tr\left[P_{[8]}\left(\psi^{\otimes 4}\otimes\phi^{\otimes 4}\right)\right]=\frac{1+16\tr(\psi\phi)+36\left[\tr(\psi\phi)\right]^{2}+16\left[\tr(\psi\phi)\right]^{3}+\left[\tr(\psi\phi)\right]^{4}}{70}.

(S28)

Lemma S5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a state ensemble on
\mathcal{H}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
is a Haar-random pure state. Then

\displaystyle\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)

\displaystyle=1,

(S29)

\displaystyle\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{1}}{D_{2}}\left(1+\dfrac{16\bar{\Phi}_{1}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[1]}}+\dfrac{36\bar{\Phi}_{2}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[2]}}+\dfrac{16\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[3]}}+\dfrac{\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[4]}}\right),

(S30)

where
D_{1}=d(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)
and
D_{2}=(d+4)(d+5)(d+6)(d+7)
. If
\mathcal{E}
forms a state 2-design, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6(17d+51)[\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})-1]+6(d-1)}{D_{2}}<\frac{[\xi(\mathcal{E})]^{2}}{(d+6)^{3}},

(S31)

where
\xi(\mathcal{E})=\sqrt{102[\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})-1]+6}
, as defined in Eq.
(
13
)
.

Lemma S6
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a state ensemble on
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{B}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Then

\displaystyle\underset{\psi\sim\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathbb{E}}\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\leq\frac{t!D_{[t]}}{d^{t}},

(S32)

where
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}
is the uniform random phase ensemble with respect to
\mathcal{B}
, as defined in Eq.
(
S8
)
.

Proof of Lemma
S3
.

Equation (
S25
) follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to the definitions of frame potentials and relative frame potentials. Equation (
S26
) is a simple corollary of Eq. (
S25
) given that
\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E})=D_{[t]}\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E})
and
\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)=D_{[t]}\Phi_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)
for any positive integer
t
.

Next, suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a 2-design; then
\bar{\Phi}_{1}(\mathcal{E},\psi)=\bar{\Phi}_{1}(\mathcal{E})=1
and
\bar{\Phi}_{2}(\mathcal{E},\psi)=\bar{\Phi}_{2}(\mathcal{E})=1
. By virtue of Eq. (
S26
) with
t=2,3
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E},\psi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{D_{[1]}D_{[3]}\bar{\Phi}_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}{D_{[2]}^{2}\bar{\Phi}_{1}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}=\frac{D_{[1]}D_{[3]}}{D_{[2]}^{2}}=\dfrac{2(d+2)}{3(d+1)}>\dfrac{2}{3},

(S33)

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{D_{[2]}D_{[4]}\bar{\Phi}_{3}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}{D_{[3]}^{2}\bar{\Phi}_{2}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}=\frac{3(d+3)\bar{\Phi}_{3}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}{4(d+2)}\geq\dfrac{(d+2)(d+3)}{3(d+1)^{2}}>\dfrac{1}{3},

(S34)

which confirm Eq. (
S27
). The same reasoning applies with
\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E},\psi)
replaced by
\bar{\Phi}_{t}(\mathcal{E})
for
t=1,2,3,4
. This completes the proof of Lemma
S3
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
S4
.

Note that
P_{[8]}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{8}}R(\sigma)/8!
, where
S_{t}
for a positive integer
t
denotes the symmetric group of
t
numbers, and
R(\sigma)
denotes the unitary representation of the permutation
\sigma
. Accordingly,

\displaystyle\tr\left[P_{[8]}\left(\psi^{\otimes 4}\otimes\phi^{\otimes 4}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{8!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{8}}\tr\left[R(\sigma)\left(\psi^{\otimes 4}\otimes\phi^{\otimes 4}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{8!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{8}}[\tr(\psi\phi)]^{\gamma(\sigma)},

(S35)

where
\gamma(\sigma)=|\sigma(\{1,2,3,4\})\cap\{5,6,7,8\}|
. A straightforward counting argument shows that

\displaystyle|\{\sigma\in S_{8}\mid\gamma(\sigma)=m\}|=\begin{cases}576&m=0,\,4,\\
9216&m=1,\,3,\\
20736&m=2.\end{cases}

(S36)

Combining the above two equations, we obtain

\displaystyle\tr\left[P_{[8]}\left(\psi^{\otimes 4}\otimes\phi^{\otimes 4}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{576+9216\tr(\psi\phi)+20736\left[\tr(\psi\phi)\right]^{2}+9216\left[\tr(\psi\phi)\right]^{3}+576\left[\tr(\psi\phi)\right]^{4}}{8!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+16\tr(\psi\phi)+36\left[\tr(\psi\phi)\right]^{2}+16\left[\tr(\psi\phi)\right]^{3}+\left[\tr(\psi\phi)\right]^{4}}{70},

(S37)

which completes the proof of Lemma
S4
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
S5
.

For any positive integer
t
, we have

\displaystyle\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\psi^{\otimes t}=\dfrac{P_{[t]}}{D_{[t]}},

(S38)

where
P_{[t]}
denotes the projector onto the
t
-partite symmetric subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes t}
and
D_{[t]}=\tr(P_{[t]})=\binom{d+t-1}{t}
. Based on this observation, Eq. (
S29
) can be proved as follows:

\displaystyle\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)=D_{[4]}\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\sum_{i}w_{i}\left[\tr(\psi\phi_{i})\right]^{4}=D_{[4]}\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr\left[\left(\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right)\phi_{i}^{\otimes 4}\right]=\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr\left(P_{[4]}\phi_{i}^{\otimes 4}\right)=1.

(S39)

In conjunction with Lemma
S4
, Eq. (
S30
) can be verified as follows:

\displaystyle\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\right]^{2}=D_{[4]}^{2}\sum_{i,j}w_{i}w_{j}\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left[\tr(\psi\phi_{i})\right]^{4}\left[\tr(\psi\phi_{j})\right]^{4}\right]=\dfrac{D_{[4]}^{2}}{D_{[8]}}\sum_{i,j}w_{i}w_{j}\tr\left[P_{[8]}\left(\phi_{i}^{\otimes 4}\otimes\phi_{j}^{\otimes 4}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{D_{[4]}^{2}}{D_{[8]}}\sum_{i,j}\frac{w_{i}w_{j}\left\{1+16\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})+36\left[\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})\right]^{2}+16\left[\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})\right]^{3}+\left[\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})\right]^{4}\right\}}{70}

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{[4]}^{2}}{70D_{[8]}}\left[1+16\Phi_{1}(\mathcal{E})+36\Phi_{2}(\mathcal{E})+16\Phi_{3}(\mathcal{E})+\Phi_{4}(\mathcal{E})\right]=\frac{D_{1}}{D_{2}}\left(1+\dfrac{16\bar{\Phi}_{1}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[1]}}+\dfrac{36\bar{\Phi}_{2}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[2]}}+\dfrac{16\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[3]}}+\dfrac{\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[4]}}\right),

(S40)

where
D_{1}=d(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)
and
D_{2}=(d+4)(d+5)(d+6)(d+7)
.

Next, suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a state 2-design; then
\bar{\Phi}_{2}(\mathcal{E})=\bar{\Phi}_{1}(\mathcal{E})=1
. The variance of
\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)
can be upper bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi))

\displaystyle=\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\right]^{2}-\left[\underset{\psi\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\right]^{2}=\frac{D_{1}}{D_{2}}\left(1+\dfrac{16\bar{\Phi}_{1}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[1]}}+\dfrac{36\bar{\Phi}_{2}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[2]}}+\dfrac{16\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[3]}}+\dfrac{\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E})}{D_{[4]}}\right)-1

\displaystyle=\frac{24[(4d+12)\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})+\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E})-4d-13]}{D_{2}}\leq\frac{6(17d+51)[\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})-1]+6(d-1)}{D_{2}}

\displaystyle<\frac{(d+3)\{102[\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})-1]+6\}}{D_{2}}<\frac{[\xi(\mathcal{E})]^{2}}{(d+6)^{3}},

(S41)

which confirms Eq. (
S31
). Here the first inequality holds because
\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E})\leq{(d+3)\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})}/{4}
by Eq. (
S5
), the second inequality holds because

\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})\geq 1,\quad\frac{(17d+51)[\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})-1]+d-1}{d+3}<17[\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})-1]+1,

(S42)

and the last inequality holds because
(d+3)/D_{2}<1/(d+6)^{3}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
S5
.

S2
Shadow estimation within the framework of rank-1 IC-POVMs

In this section we briefly introduce shadow estimation within the framework of rank-1 IC POVMs, following the presentation in the main text.
Let
\mathcal{E}=\{\lvert\phi_{i}\rangle,w_{i}\}_{i}
be a state ensemble that forms a
1
-design, which induces the POVM
\{dw_{i}\phi_{i}\}_{i}
. If
\mathcal{E}
is informationally complete (IC), any quantum state
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
can be reconstructed as
\rho=d\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr(\phi_{i}\rho)\,\hat{\rho}_{i}
, where
\hat{\rho}_{i}
is the estimator associated with outcome
i
. When
\mathcal{E}
is informationally overcomplete, the choice of
\hat{\rho}_{i}
is not unique. In the shadow-estimation framework, one usually employs the canonical reconstruction, which is the standard choice in the absence of prior information about
\rho
.

The canonical measurement channel can be represented in two equivalent ways. The first directly defines the channel action:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=d\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr(\phi_{i}\rho)\,\phi_{i}.

(S44)

The second uses the vectorization (double-ket) notation. For any operator
A\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{H})
, we define its vectorization
\lvert A\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
by expanding
\lvert A\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle=\sum_{k,l}A_{kl}\,\lvert k\rangle\otimes\lvert l\rangle
in a fixed orthonormal basis, so that the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product becomes
\langle\mkern-3.5mu\langle A\,|\,B\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle=\tr\left(A^{\dagger}B\right)
. The outer product
\lvert A\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle\mkern-1.5mu\langle\mkern-3.5mu\langle A\rvert=\lvert A\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle\langle\mkern-3.5mu\langle A\rvert
then acts as a linear operator on this space. With this notation the measurement channel becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}=d\sum_{i}w_{i}\lvert\phi_{i}\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle\mkern-1.5mu\langle\mkern-3.5mu\langle\phi_{i}\rvert,

(S45)

where
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}
is viewed as an operator acting on vectorized operators via
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}\lvert A\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle=\lvert\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}(A)\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle
. This representation makes the positive semidefiniteness of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}
manifest and facilitates spectral analysis and inversion of the channel.
By definition,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathds{1})=\mathds{1}
, so
\mathds{1}
is an eigenoperator of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}
with eigenvalue
1
. With a slight abuse of notation, we use
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}
to denote both representations of the measurement channel when no confusion arises.
Let
\bar{\mathbf{I}}
denote the projector onto the space of traceless Hermitian operators. The restricted channel is

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}\coloneqq\bar{\mathbf{I}}\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}\,\bar{\mathbf{I}}=d\sum_{i}w_{i}\lvert\phi_{i,0}\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle\mkern-1.5mu\langle\mkern-3.5mu\langle\phi_{i,0}\rvert,

(S46)

where
\phi_{i,0}=\phi_{i}-\mathds{1}/d
is the traceless part of
\phi_{i}
. Because
\mathcal{E}
forms a
1
-design, this simplifies to

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}-\frac{1}{d}\lvert\mathds{1}\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle\mkern-1.5mu\langle\mkern-3.5mu\langle\mathds{1}\rvert.

(S47)

For any observable
O\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{H})
and an IC-POVM
\mathcal{M}=\{E_{i}\}_{i}
, the expectation value
\tr(\rho O)
can be estimated as

\displaystyle\tr(\rho O)=\sum_{i}\tr(E_{i}\rho)\,\hat{o}_{i},

(S48)

where
\hat{o}_{i}=\tr(\hat{\rho}_{i}O)
and
\hat{\rho}_{i}
is the canonical reconstruction operator for outcome
i
. The single-shot estimation variance reads

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\hat{o})=\sum_{i}\tr(E_{i}\rho)\,\hat{o}_{i}^{2}-[\tr(\rho O)]^{2}=\sum_{i}\tr(E_{i}\rho)\,\hat{o}_{0,i}^{2}-[\tr(\rho O_{0})]^{2},

(S49)

where
O_{0}=O-\tr(O)\mathds{1}/d
is the traceless part of
O
and
\hat{o}_{0,i}=\tr(\hat{\rho}_{i}O_{0})
. The second equality shows that the variance depends solely on
O_{0}
. The dominant term defines the
state-dependent squared shadow norm
:

\displaystyle\|O_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E},\rho}^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{i}\tr(E_{i}\rho)\,\hat{o}_{0,i}^{2}=d\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr(\rho\phi_{i})\left\{\tr\left[\phi_{i}\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(O_{0})\right]\right\}^{2}=d\tr\left\{Q_{3}(\mathcal{E})\left[\rho\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(O_{0})\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(O_{0})\right]\right\},

(S50)

where
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}
denotes the inverse of the measurement channel. The (state-independent) squared shadow norm is obtained by maximizing
\|O\|_{\mathcal{E},\rho}^{2}
over all states:

\displaystyle\|O_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\coloneqq\max_{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})}\|O_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E},\rho}^{2}=\left\|d\sum_{i}w_{i}\phi_{i}\left\{\tr\left[\phi_{i}\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(O_{0})\right]\right\}^{2}\right\|=\left\|d\tr_{2,3}\left\{Q_{3}(\mathcal{E})\left[\mathds{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(O_{0})\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(O_{0})\right]\right\}\right\|,

(S51)

which applies to any traceless operator
O_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}_{0}(\mathcal{H})
.

If
\mathcal{E}
forms a state
2
-design, the measurement channel takes the simple form

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}=\frac{\mathbf{I}+\lvert\mathds{1}\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle\mkern-1.5mu\langle\mkern-3.5mu\langle\mathds{1}\rvert}{d+1},

(S52)

where
\mathbf{I}
is the identity superoperator on
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{H})
. Restricted to traceless operators, its inverse gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(O_{0})=\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(O_{0})=(d+1)\,O_{0}.

(S53)

Consequently, the squared shadow norm reduces to

\displaystyle\|O_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}=\left\|d(d+1)^{2}\sum_{i}w_{i}\phi_{i}\left[\tr(\phi_{i}O_{0})\right]^{2}\right\|=\left\|d(d+1)^{2}\tr_{2,3}\left[Q_{3}(\mathcal{E})\left(\mathds{1}\otimes O_{0}\otimes O_{0}\right)\right]\right\|.

(S54)

If
\mathcal{E}
further forms a state
3
-design, then
Q_{3}(\mathcal{E})=6P_{[3]}/[d(d+1)(d+2)]
, and we obtain the closed-form expression

\displaystyle\|O_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}=\frac{d+1}{d+2}\left(2\|O_{0}\|^{2}+\|O_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\leq 3\|O_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

(S55)

For fidelity estimation, where the observable is a pure-state projector
\psi
with traceless part
\psi_{0}=\psi-\mathds{1}/d
, the shadow norm evaluates to

\displaystyle\|\psi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}=\frac{(3d-2)(d+1)(d-1)}{d^{2}(d+2)}.

(S56)

Proofs of results on worst-case optimal shadow estimation

S3.1
Proof of Proposition
2

Proof.

Suppose the ensemble has the form
\mathcal{E}=\{\lvert\phi_{i}\rangle,w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
. For any collection of states
\{\rho_{j}\}_{j=1}^{K}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, any collection of rank-1 projectors
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{K}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
, and any probability distribution
\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{K}
(i.e.,
v_{j}\geq 0
and
\sum_{j}v_{j}=1
), we have

\displaystyle\max_{\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})}\|\psi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max_{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}),\,\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})}d\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr(\rho\phi_{i})\left\{\tr\left[\phi_{i}\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(\psi_{0})\right]\right\}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\geq d\sum_{i,j}w_{i}v_{j}{\tr(\rho_{j}\phi_{i})\left\{\tr\left[\phi_{i}\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(\psi_{j,0})\right]\right\}^{2}},

(S57)

where the inequality follows from the fact that the maximum is lower bounded by any convex combination. To establish the inequality in Proposition
2
, we now choose

\displaystyle\rho_{j}=\phi_{j},\qquad\psi_{j}=\phi_{j},\qquad v_{j}=\frac{1}{K}.

(S58)

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})}\|\psi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq d\sum_{i,j}w_{i}v_{j}\tr(\rho_{j}\phi_{i})\left\{\tr\left[\phi_{i}\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(\phi_{j,0})\right]\right\}^{2}\geq\frac{d}{K}\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr(\rho_{i}\phi_{i})\left\{\tr\left[\phi_{i}\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(\phi_{i,0})\right]\right\}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{K}\sum_{i}w_{i}\left\{\tr\left[\phi_{i,0}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(\phi_{i,0})\right]\right\}^{2}.

(S59)

The second inequality holds because
\tr(\rho_{j}\phi_{i})
and
\left\{\tr\left[\phi_{i}\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(\phi_{j,0})\right]\right\}^{2}
are nonnegative for all
i,j
; the equality follows from the facts that
\phi_{i,0}
is traceless and that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}
restricted to
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}_{0}(\mathcal{H})
coincides with
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Since
f(x)=x^{t}
is convex for
t>1
, Jensen’s inequality implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{i}w_{i}\left\{\tr\left[\phi_{i,0}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(\phi_{i,0})\right]\right\}^{2}\geq\left\{\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr\left[\phi_{i,0}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(\phi_{i,0})\right]\right\}^{2}.

(S60)

To evaluate the right-hand side, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr\left[\phi_{i,0}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(\phi_{i,0})\right]

\displaystyle=\Tr\left[\sum_{i}w_{i}\lvert\phi_{i,0}\rangle\mkern-3.5mu\rangle\mkern-1.5mu\langle\mkern-3.5mu\langle\phi_{i,0}\rvert\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}\right]=\frac{1}{d}\Tr\left(\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}\right)=\frac{1}{d}\Tr(\bar{\mathbf{I}})=\frac{d^{2}-1}{d},

(S61)

where
\Tr
denotes the trace on
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{H})
(viewing
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}
as a linear operator on the operator space), and
\bar{\mathbf{I}}
denotes the identity on

S3.2
Auxiliary results on the combined phase-design ensemble based on MUBs

In this section, we clarify the basic properties of the combined phase 3-design ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{N}
with
2\leq N\leq d+1
, as introduced in the main text, and provide a proof of Theorem

. This ensemble is a uniform mixture of
N

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}
associated with
N
mutually unbiased bases (MUBs), denoted by
\mathcal{B}_{j}=\{\lvert k\rangle_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}\}_{k=0}^{d-1}
for

. More precisely,

can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}.

(S63)

Proposition S1
.

The second and third moment operators of
\mathcal{E}_{N}
read

\displaystyle Q_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{d^{2}}P_{[2]}-\frac{1}{Nd^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}(\lvert kk\rangle\mkern-2.0mu\langle kk\rvert)_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}

(S64)

\displaystyle Q_{3}(\mathcal{E}_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{d^{3}}P_{[3]}-\frac{5}{Nd^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}(\lvert kkk\rangle\mkern-2.0mu\langle kkk\rvert)_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}-\frac{3}{Nd^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{[\bm{k}]\colon|[\bm{k}]|=3}(\lvert[\bm{k}]\rangle\mkern-2.0mu\langle[\bm{k}]\rvert)_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}.

(S65)

Proposition S2
.

The second frame potential of
\mathcal{E}_{N}
reads

\Phi_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{N})=\frac{2d^{2}-2d+1}{d^{4}}+\frac{d-1}{Nd^{4}}.

(S66)

The ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{N}
is a state
2
-design if and only if
N=d+1
.

Proposition
S1
is a direct corollary of Lemma

, and Proposition

follows
from Proposition

,
given that the bases

Taking the trace against

where the second inequality holds because every

S3.3
Proof of Theorem
2

Proof.

By Eq. (
S50
) and Lemma
S8
, we bound the squared shadow norm as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|O\right\|_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{2}=d\max_{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})}\tr\left\{Q_{3}(\mathcal{E})\left[\rho\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{-1}(O)\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{-1}(O)\right]\right\}\leq\frac{3N+1}{Nd^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{-1}(O)\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(S84)

According to Proposition
S3
,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{-1}(O)
has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{-1}(O)=dO_{\perp}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{Nd}{N-1}\,O_{\mathcal{B}_{j}},

(S85)

where the identity component vanishes because
\tr(O)=0
by assumption. These components are mutually orthogonal with respect to the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, so

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{-1}(O)\right\|_{2}^{2}=d^{2}\left[\left\|O_{\perp}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{N^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\|O_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{N^{2}d^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}}\,\|O\|_{2}^{2},

(S86)

where the inequality holds because

.
Together with Eq. (
S84

\displaystyle\|O\|_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}^{2}\leq\frac{3N+1}{Nd^{2}}\cdot\frac{N^{2}d^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}}\,\|O\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{N(3N+1)}{(N-1)^{2}}\,\|O\|_{2}^{2},

(S87)

which completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

S4.1
Fourth moments of Haar-random observables

In preparation for studying the average shadow norm achieved by 2-design POVMs, we recall some basic results about Schur–Weyl duality for the unitary group.

Let
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
be the unitary group on
\mathcal{H}
and
S_{t}
the symmetric group of
t
numbers.
According to Schur–Weyl duality, the
t
-th tensor power
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes t}
decomposes into a multiplicity-free direct sum of irreducible representations of
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})\times S_{t}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\otimes t}=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{S}_{\lambda},

(S88)

where each
\lambda
labels a nonincreasing partition of
t
into at most
d
parts,
\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}
is the Weyl module carrying an irreducible representation of
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
is the Specht module carrying an irreducible representation of
S_{t}
. The dimensions of
\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
are denoted by
D_{\lambda}
and
d_{\lambda}
, respectively. The projector
P_{\lambda}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}=\frac{d_{\lambda}}{24}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{4}}\chi_{\lambda}(\sigma)R(\sigma),

(S89)

where
\chi_{\lambda}(\sigma)
is the character of
\sigma
in the representation
\lambda
, and
R(\sigma)
is the unitary operator representing the permutation
\sigma
. By definition,
\tr(P_{\lambda})=\rk(P_{\lambda})=d_{\lambda}D_{\lambda}
.

Lemma S9
.

Suppose
\mathcal{U}
is a unitary 4-design and

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\mathcal{U}}{\mathbb{E}}\,U^{\otimes 4}O^{\otimes 4}U^{\dagger\otimes 4}=\sum_{\lambda}\tr\left(P_{\lambda}O^{\otimes 4}\right)\frac{P_{\lambda}}{d_{\lambda}D_{\lambda}},

(S90)

where
\underset{U\sim\mathcal{U}}{\mathbb{E}}
denotes the average over
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

By assumption,
\mathcal{U}
is a unitary 4-design, which implies that

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\mathcal{U}}{\mathbb{E}}\,U^{\otimes 4}O^{\otimes 4}U^{\dagger\otimes 4}=\underset{U\sim\mathrm{Haar}}{\mathbb{E}}\,U^{\otimes 4}O^{\otimes 4}U^{\dagger\otimes 4}.

(S91)

Thus both sides are invariant under permutations and diagonal unitary transformations of the form
U^{\otimes 4}
for
U\in\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
. The lemma then follows directly from Schur–Weyl duality.

S4.2
Fourth moments of Clifford orbits

In this appendix, we focus on an
n
-qubit system, whose Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
has dimension
d=2^{n}
.

Let
\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}
be the set of
n
-qubit Pauli operators. Then
\{W^{\otimes 4}\mid W\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}\}
forms a stabilizer group with stabilizer projector

P_{n}=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{W\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}}W^{\otimes 4}.

(S93)

For a pure state
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
, the stabilizer 2-Rényi entropy
Leone et al. 2022
is defined as

\displaystyle M_{2}(\psi)\coloneqq-\log_{2}\sum_{W\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}}\left[\tr(W\psi)\right]^{4}+\log_{2}d=-\log_{2}\tr\left(P_{n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right)-\log_{2}d.

(S94)

By definition,
\tr\left(P_{n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right)=2^{-M_{2}(\psi)}/d
. As established in Ref.
Zhu et al. 2016
, the stabilizer 2-Rényi entropy satisfies

\displaystyle 0\leq M_{2}(\psi)\leq\log_{2}(d+1)-1\quad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H}),

(S95)

where the lower bound is saturated if and only if
\psi
is a stabilizer state. Under the action of the Clifford group, the symmetric subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 4}
decomposes into two inequivalent irreducible components with projectors

\displaystyle P_{+}=P_{[4]}P_{n}=P_{n}P_{[4]},\quad P_{-}=P_{[4]}(\mathds{1}-P_{n})=(\mathds{1}-P_{n})P_{[4]},

(S96)

and dimensions

\displaystyle D^{+}_{[4]}=\tr(P_{+})=\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}{6},\quad D^{-}_{[4]}=\tr(P_{-})=\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d+2)(d+4)}{24}.

(S97)

By construction, we have

\displaystyle\tr\left(P_{+}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right)=\tr\left(P_{n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right)=\frac{2^{-M_{2}(\psi)}}{d},\quad\tr\left(P_{-}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right)=1-\frac{2^{-M_{2}(\psi)}}{d}\quad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H}).

(S98)

Lemma S11
.

Suppose
\psi,\phi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\mathrm{Cl}(n)}{\mathbb{E}}\left(U\psi U^{\dagger}\right)^{\otimes 4}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{-M_{2}(\psi)}}{d\,D^{+}_{[4]}}\,P_{+}+\frac{1}{D^{-}_{[4]}}\left(1-\frac{2^{-M_{2}(\psi)}}{d}\right)P_{-},

(S99)

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\mathrm{Cl}(n)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\tr\left(\phi\,U\psi U^{\dagger}\right)\right]^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{-M_{2}(\psi)-M_{2}(\phi)}}{d^{2}\,D^{+}_{[4]}}+\frac{1}{D^{-}_{[4]}}\left(1-\frac{2^{-M_{2}(\phi)}}{d}\right)\left(1-\frac{2^{-M_{2}(\psi)}}{d}\right)\leq\frac{5(d+3)}{4(d+4)D_{[4]}}\leq\frac{5}{4D_{[4]}},

(S100)

where
D^{+}_{[4]}
and
D^{-}_{[4]}
are given in Eq.
(
S97
)
, and
D_{[4]}
is given in Table
S1
.

Proof of Lemma
S11
.

Equation (
S99
) follows from Schur’s lemma and Eq. (
S98
); it was essentially proved in Ref.
Zhu et al. 2016
, though without the concept of stabilizer 2-Rényi entropy. The equality in Eq. (
S100
) is a direct corollary of Eqs. (
S98
) and (
S99
). The first inequality in Eq. (
S100
) follows from Eq. (
S95
), and the second is trivial.
∎

Lemma S12
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}=\{\lvert\phi_{i}\rangle,w_{i}\}_{i}
is a state ensemble on
\mathcal{H}
, and
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
is a pure state. Then

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\mathrm{Cl}(n)}{\mathbb{E}}\Phi_{4}\left(\mathcal{E},U\psi U^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i}2^{-M_{2}(\phi_{i})-M_{2}(\psi)}w_{i}}{d^{2}\,D^{+}_{[4]}}+\frac{1}{D^{-}_{[4]}}\left(1-\frac{\sum_{i}2^{-M_{2}(\phi_{i})}w_{i}}{d}\right)\left(1-\frac{2^{-M_{2}(\psi)}}{d}\right)\leq\frac{5(d+3)}{4(d+4)D_{[4]}}\leq\frac{5}{4D_{[4]}}.

(S101)

S5.1
Auxiliary results on shadow norms

Lemma S13
.

Suppose
A
is a positive semidefinite operator acting on
\mathcal{H}
with
\|A\|_{1}=a
and
\|A\|_{2}^{2}=b
, where
a
and
b
are positive numbers satisfying
{a^{2}}/{d}\leq b\leq a^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\|A\|\leq\frac{a+\sqrt{(d-1)(db-a^{2})}}{d},

(S102)

and the inequality is saturated when all eigenvalues of
A
, except for the largest one, are equal.

Proof of Lemma
S13
.

Let
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{d}\geq 0
be the eigenvalues of
A
in nonincreasing order; then
\|A\|=\mu_{1}
. By definition,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mu_{i}=\|A\|_{1}=a,\quad\quad\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mu_{i}^{2}=\|A\|_{2}^{2}=b.

(S103)

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle(a-\mu_{1})^{2}=\left(\sum_{i=2}^{d}\mu_{i}\right)^{2}\leq(d-1)\sum_{i=2}^{d}\mu_{i}^{2}=(d-1)\left(b-\mu_{1}^{2}\right),

(S104)

which simplifies to

\displaystyle d\mu_{1}^{2}-2a\mu_{1}+\left[a^{2}-(d-1)b\right]

\displaystyle\leq 0.

(S105)

This quadratic inequality implies that

\displaystyle\|A\|=\mu_{1}\leq\frac{a+\sqrt{(d-1)(db-a^{2})}}{d},

(S106)

which confirms Eq. (
S102
). If
\mu_{2}=\mu_{3}=\dots=\mu_{d}
, then all three inequalities above are saturated, which completes the proof of Lemma
S13
.
∎

Lemma S14
.

Suppose
O\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}_{0}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\frac{1}{d}\|O\|_{2}^{4}\leq\|O\|_{4}^{4}\leq\frac{d-1}{d}\|O\|_{2}^{4}.

(S107)

Proof.

The first inequality in Eq. (
S107
) follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. To prove the second inequality, denote by
O_{+}
and
O_{-}
the positive and negative parts of
O
, respectively. Then, by assumption, we have

\tr(O_{+})=\tr(O_{-}),\quad\frac{1}{d-1}\|O_{+}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|O_{-}\|_{2}^{2}\leq(d-1)\|O_{+}\|_{2}^{2}.

(S108)

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\|O_{-}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|O_{+}\|_{2}^{2}=1
; then
\|O\|_{2}^{2}=\|O_{+}\|_{2}^{2}+\|O_{-}\|_{2}^{2}\geq d/(d-1)
. In addition, the eigenvalues of
O
are bounded in absolute value by 1, so that

\|O\|_{4}^{4}=\|O_{+}\|_{4}^{4}+\|O_{-}\|_{4}^{4}\leq\|O_{+}\|_{2}^{2}+\|O_{-}\|_{2}^{2}=\|O\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{d-1}{d}\|O\|_{2}^{4},

(S109)

which confirms the second inequality in Eq. (
S107
) and completes the proof of Lemma
S14
.
∎

Proposition S4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a state
2
-design,
O\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}_{0}(\mathcal{H})
, and
\mathcal{U}
is a unitary
4
-design. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\Xi(O,\mathcal{U})\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{d+1}{d}+\sqrt{f(d,r)\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})+g(d,r)}\mkern 2.0mu\right)\|O\|_{2}^{2},

(S110)

where

r=\frac{\|O\|_{4}^{4}}{\|O\|_{2}^{4}},\quad f(d,r)=\frac{12(d+1)^{2}\left[(d^{2}+3d+3)+(d^{2}+d)r\right]}{d^{2}(d+2)^{2}(d+3)},\quad g(d,r)=\frac{4(d+1)^{2}\left[(d^{2}-3d)r-(4d+3)\right]}{d^{2}(d+2)(d+3)}.

(S111)

By definition in Eq. (
S111
) and Lemma
S14
, it is straightforward to verify that

\frac{1}{d}\leq r\leq\frac{d-1}{d},\quad f(d,r)\leq\frac{24}{d}-\frac{26}{d^{2}},\quad g(d,r)\leq 4-\frac{g_{d}}{d},\quad g_{d}\coloneqq\begin{cases}18&d=2,\\
22&d=3,\\
25&d=4,5,\\
29&d\geq 6,\\
30&d\geq 7.\end{cases}

(S112)

Proof of Proposition
S4
.

Suppose the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
has the form
\mathcal{E}=\{\lvert\phi_{i}\rangle,w_{i}\}_{i}
and let
A\coloneqq\sum_{i}w_{i}\left[\tr\left(\phi_{i}O\right)\right]^{2}\phi_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle\|A\|_{1}=\frac{1}{d(d+1)}\|O\|_{2}^{2},\quad\|A\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i,j}w_{i}w_{j}\left[\tr(O\phi_{i})\right]^{2}\left[\tr(O\phi_{j})\right]^{2}\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j}).

(S113)

Using Lemma
S13
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|O\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq(d+1)^{2}\sqrt{d(d-1)\sum_{i,j}w_{i}w_{j}\left[\tr(O\phi_{i})\right]^{2}\left[\tr(O\phi_{j})\right]^{2}\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})-\frac{d-1}{d^{2}(d+1)^{2}}\|O\|_{2}^{4}}+\frac{d+1}{d}\|O\|_{2}^{2}.

(S114)

Applying Jensen’s inequality, we further derive an upper bound for
\underset{U\sim\mathcal{U}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\|UOU^{\dagger}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\mathcal{U}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\|UOU^{\dagger}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)^{2}\sqrt{d(d-1)\underset{U\sim\mathcal{U}}{\mathbb{E}}\sum_{i,j}w_{i}w_{j}\left[\tr(UOU^{\dagger}\phi_{i})\right]^{2}\left[\tr(UOU^{\dagger}\phi_{j})\right]^{2}\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})-\frac{d-1}{d^{2}(d+1)^{2}}\|O\|_{2}^{4}}

\displaystyle\,\hphantom{=}\,+\frac{d+1}{d}\|O\|_{2}^{2}.

(S115)

The expectation under the square root can be evaluated as follows:

\displaystyle\underset{U\sim\mathcal{U}}{\mathbb{E}}\sum_{i,j}w_{i}w_{j}\left[\tr(UOU^{\dagger}\phi_{i})\right]^{2}\left[\tr(UOU^{\dagger}\phi_{j})\right]^{2}\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})=\sum_{i,j}w_{i}w_{j}\tr\left[\underset{U\sim\mathcal{U}}{\mathbb{E}}U^{\otimes 4}O^{\otimes 4}U^{\dagger\otimes 4}\left(\phi_{i}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\phi_{j}^{\otimes 2}\right)\right]\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\sum_{\lambda}\frac{w_{i}w_{j}}{d_{\lambda}D_{\lambda}}\tr\left(P_{\lambda}O^{\otimes 4}\right)\tr\left(P_{\lambda}\phi_{i}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\phi_{j}^{\otimes 2}\right)\tr(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})=h_{1}(d,O)\Phi_{1}+h_{2}(d,O)\Phi_{2}+h_{3}(d,O)\Phi_{3},

(S116)

where
\lambda
represents a nonincreasing partition of 4 into at most
d
parts (see SM Sec.
S4
) and

\displaystyle h_{1}(d,O)

\displaystyle=\frac{(d^{2}+3d+6)\left[\tr(O^{2})\right]^{2}-4d\tr(O^{4})}{d^{2}(d-1)(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)},

(S117)

\displaystyle h_{2}(d,O)

\displaystyle=\frac{-(12d+12)\left[\tr(O^{2})\right]^{2}+(4d^{2}-4d)\tr(O^{4})}{d^{2}(d-1)(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)},

\displaystyle h_{3}(d,O)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2d^{2}+6d+6)\left[\tr(O^{2})\right]^{2}+(2d^{2}+2d)\tr(O^{4})}{d^{2}(d-1)(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)}.

The second equality in Eq. (
S116
) follows from Lemma
S9
, and the third equality is derived using Lemma
S10
.

Combining Eqs. (
S115
) and (
S116
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\Xi(O,\mathcal{U})\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d+1}{d}\|O\|_{2}^{2}+\sqrt{d(d+1)^{4}(d-1)\left[h_{1}(d,O)\Phi_{1}+h_{2}(d,O)\Phi_{2}+h_{3}(d,O)\Phi_{3}\right]-\frac{(d+1)^{2}(d-1)}{d^{2}}\|O\|_{2}^{4}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d+1}{d}+\sqrt{f(d,r)\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E})+g(d,r)}\mkern 2.0mu\right)\|O\|_{2}^{2},

(S118)

where
\Phi_{j}
is shorthand for the frame potential
\Phi_{j}(\mathcal{E})
for
j=1,2,3
, and the equality holds because
\Phi_{1}=1/d
and
\Phi_{2}=2/[d(d+1)]
(since
\mathcal{E}
is a state 2-design) together with the definition
r=\|O\|_{4}^{4}/\|O\|_{2}^{4}
. This completes the proof of Proposition
S4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
3
.

By virtue of Proposition
S4
and the inequalities in Eq. (

\displaystyle\left\|\Xi(O,\mathcal{U})\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

S5.3
Auxiliary results on shadow norms for fidelity estimation

Suppose
\mathcal{E}=\{\lvert\phi_{i}\rangle,w_{i}\}_{i}
is a state 2-design on
\mathcal{H}
,
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
, and
\psi_{0}=\psi-{\mathds{1}}/{d}
. To better understand the properties of the squared shadow norm
\|\psi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}
tied to fidelity estimation, we introduce some auxiliary results. Define

J(\mathcal{E},\psi)\coloneqq d(d+1)^{2}\sum_{i}w_{i}\left[\tr\left(\phi_{i}\psi\right)\right]^{2}\phi_{i},\quad\eta(\mathcal{E},\psi)\coloneqq\left\|J(\mathcal{E},\psi)\right\|.

(S121)

Proposition S5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
forms a state 2-design on
\mathcal{H}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\displaystyle\eta(\mathcal{E},\psi)-3-\frac{2}{d}\leq\left\|\psi_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}=\left\|J(\mathcal{E},\psi)-\frac{2(d+1)}{d}\psi\right\|-1+\frac{1}{d^{2}}\leq\eta(\mathcal{E},\psi)-1+\frac{1}{d^{2}}.

(S122)

Proposition S6
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
forms a state 2-design on
\mathcal{H}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\displaystyle\dfrac{6(d+1)}{d+2}\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E},\psi)\leq\eta(\mathcal{E},\psi)

\displaystyle\leq\mkern 2.0mu\dfrac{d+1}{\sqrt{(d+2)(d+3)}}\sqrt{48\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}\leq\sqrt{48\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)},

(S123)

\displaystyle\dfrac{6(d+1)}{d+2}\bar{\Phi}_{3}(\mathcal{E},\psi)-3-\frac{2}{d}\leq\left\|\psi_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{d+1}{\sqrt{(d+2)(d+3)}}\sqrt{48\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}-1+\frac{1}{d^{2}}\leq\sqrt{48\bar{\Phi}_{4}(\mathcal{E},\psi)}-1.

(S124)

Proof of Proposition
S5
.

By assumption,
\mathcal{E}
is a state 2-design, so
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}(O)=(d+1)O
whenever
O
is traceless. By virtue of (
S51
) with
O=\psi_{0}=\psi-\mathds{1}/d
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|d(d+1)^{2}\sum_{i}w_{i}\left[\tr\left(\phi_{i}\psi_{0}\right)\right]^{2}\phi_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|d(d+1)^{2}\sum_{i}w_{i}\left[\tr\left(\phi_{i}\psi\right)\right]^{2}\phi_{i}-2(d+1)^{2}\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr\left(\phi_{i}\psi\right)\phi_{i}+\dfrac{(d+1)^{2}}{d}\sum_{i}w_{i}\phi_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|J(\mathcal{E},\psi)-\frac{2(d+1)}{d}\psi-\left(1-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\right)\mathds{1}\right\|=\left\|J(\mathcal{E},\psi)-\frac{2(d+1)}{d}\psi\right\|-1+\frac{1}{d^{2}},

(S125)

which confirms the equality in Eq. (
S122
). Here the third equality holds because

and

is a state 2-design. In addition, the operator

is positive semidefinite, which implies that

\eta(\mathcal{E},\psi)-\frac{2(d+1)}{d}\leq\left\|J(\mathcal{E},\psi)-\frac{2(d+1)}{d}\psi\right\|\leq\eta(\mathcal{E},\psi).

(S126)

The above two equations together confirm Eq. (
S122
) and complete the proof of Proposition
S5
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
S6
.

The first inequality in Eq. (
S123
) can be proved as follows:

\displaystyle=\max_{\rho}{d(d+1)^{2}}\sum_{i}w_{i}\tr(\phi_{i}\rho)\left[\tr(\phi_{i}\psi)\right]^{2}\geq{d(d+1)^{2}}\sum_{i}w_{i}\left[\tr(\phi_{i}\psi)\right]^{3}

where the second equality holds because

, given that

forms a state 2-design by assumption, and the third equality holds by definition. The above two equations together confirm Eq. (

Next, the first two inequalities in Eq. (

S5
and Eq. (
S123

(S129)

where the second inequality holds because

.
