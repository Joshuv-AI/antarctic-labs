 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 Instruments which are categorized as shared user instruments at the National Laboratory of the
 Rockies (NLR) will be utilized for ongoing technical work. Samples will be provided by
 University of Colorado Boulder (CU) and thus no need for materials at NLR is anticipated.

 CRADA benefit to DOE, Participant, and US Taxpayer:

 Shared use for ongoing technical work of the transmission electron microscope (TEM), x-ray
 diffractometers, (XRDs), and X-ray flourescence (XFR) spectrometers provide a benefit to DOE,
 the Participant, and the taxpayer in that government resources are being leveraged to further
 research. The use of equipment provides enhanced technical capabilities to both NLR and CU,
 thereby bringing benefit through use of government resources that ultimately enhance the
 technical outcome of the overall CRADA.

 Synthesis and Characterization of (Ga1-xZnx)(N1-xOx) Nanocrystals with a Wide Range of
 Compositions

 The oxynitride gallium zinc oxynitride (Ga₁₋ₓZnₓ)(N₁₋ₓOₓ), a solid solution of gallium nitride
 (GaN) and zinc oxide (ZnO), has drawn considerable interest for solar water splitting since its
 discovery in 2005. This material is intriguing because its band gap can be tuned down to the
 visible region by varying the composition (fraction of ZnO, x) even though the parent
 semiconductors have band gaps in the UV region (3.4 eV for GaN and 3.2 eV for ZnO). The
 origin of the band gap reduction upon mixing between ZnO and GaN is still under debate.
 Moreover, it was demonstrated that (Ga1- xZnx)(N1-xOx) could act as a stable visible light
 driven photocatalyst for overall water splitting into hydrogen (H₂) and oxygen (O₂) when it was
 modified with proper co-catalysts.

 NLR oversaw a graduate student at CU Boulder, to characterize the (Ga1- xZnx)(N1-xOx) that he
 prepared by varying x. As part of the X-ray diffraction (XRD) training, NLR staff provided one-
 on-one demonstration of instrument operation and use, and secondary oversight during the
 worker’s initial use. Management ensured the required training and skills were met, and
 authorized work. Instrument maintenance costs were also incurred from the general user base
 and charged as appropriate based on usage. Select data collected using NLR’s Rigaku Ultima IV
 diffractometer are shown in the figure that appears in the following report:
    https://doi.org/10.1039/c5ta04314j.
