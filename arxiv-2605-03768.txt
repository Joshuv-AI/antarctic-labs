What is the Strouhal number of turbulence driven by supernovae? We use ramses ( 81 ) for all simulations. Data analysis and visualization software used in this study includes C++ ( 79 ) , mpi , hdf5 , fftw , numpy ( 65 ; 36 ) , numba ( 56 ) , matplotlib ( 41 ) , cython ( 6 ) , visit ( 14 ) , scipy ( 86 ) , scikit-image ( 85 ) , cmasher ( 84 ) , yt ( 83 ) , pandas ( 67 ) , joblib ( 44 ) , and pyfftw ( 31 ) .

Abstract
The Strouhal number, St = t cor / t out {\rm{St}}=t_{\rm cor}/t_{\rm out} , measures the temporal coherence of turbulent driving relative to the outer-scale eddy turnover time. In turbulence-box models one commonly sets St = 1 {\rm{St}}=1 , although recent work by 35 and 72 has shown that turbulence statistics, especially the mass-density distribution in compressively driven turbulence, are sensitive to this choice. In this Letter, we compute St {\rm{St}} directly from the measured two-time correlation tensor and outer-scale eddy time in stratified multiphase ISM simulations of Milky Way-like and starburst disks. We find isotropic median values St = 0.26 − 0.16 + 0.30 {\rm{St}}=0.26^{+0.30}_{-0.16} for the Milky Way-like model and St = 0.25 − 0.12 + 0.11 {\rm{St}}=0.25^{+0.11}_{-0.12} for the starburst model. These values are consistent with the picture that supernova remnants (SNRs) drive turbulence locally near R cool R_{\rm cool} , where the unstable contact discontinuity in the expanding SNR sets comparable forcing and eddy times, St ⁡ ( R cool ) ≈ 1 {\rm{St}}(R_{\rm cool})\approx 1 . The reconstructed scale-dependent curves reach St = 1 {\rm{St}}=1 at a nearly universal outer-scale fraction, ℓ ∗ / ℓ out ≈ 0.12 ​ – ​ 0.13 \ell_{\ast}/\ell_{\rm out}\approx 0.12\text{--}0.13 ( ℓ ∗ ≈ 25 ​ – ​ 32 ​ pc \ell_{\ast}\approx 25\text{--}32\,\rm{pc} ), so the standard St = 1 {\rm{St}}=1 prescription is not an outer-scale model of SN-driven ISM turbulence, but a local-scale approximation tied to injection near the cooling radius of the SNR.

Introduction

In standard momentum-forced turbulence models, as commonly employed in idealized box simulations of astrophysical turbulence, the momentum equation is written

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho\bm{u}}{\partial t}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\mathbb{F}_{\rho\bm{u}}=\rho\bm{f}_{\rm turb},

In this Letter, we make the first measurement of
{\rm{St}}
in a supernova-driven, multiphase ISM using two stratified galactic disk cutout models, Milky Way (MW) and starburst (SB), previously analyzed in
16

{\rm{St}}
directly from the measured temporal correlation time and the characteristic outer-scale eddy turnover time of the flow. We find projected median values
{\rm{St}}\approx 0.2\text{--}0.25
in both galaxy models, implying that the measured correlation time is substantially shorter than the outer-scale turnover time. We then interpret these outer-scale values with a cooling-radius/contact-layer model, following the SN-driven turbulence framework developed in
5

{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm inj})\approx 1
, while the larger eddies that populate the scale-free cascade evolve more slowly toward the outer scale. This model predicts
{\rm{St}}(\ell)\propto\ell^{-3/4}

{\rm{St}}=1

\ell_{\ast}/\ell_{\rm out}\approx 0.12\text{--}0.13

\ell_{\ast}\approx 25\text{--}32\,\rm{pc}

{\rm{St}}=1
prescription is therefore not an outer-scale model of SN-driven ISM turbulence, but at most a local-scale approximation.

III.1.2
A cooling-radius/contact-layer model for SN-driven turbulence

35
studied compressively driven turbulence by varying
\tau_{\rm a}/\tau_{\rm e}
, the analogue of
{\rm{St}}
for their imposed stochastic acceleration field, and in their §4 estimated this ratio for SN-driven turbulence by associating the forcing time with the large-scale radial expansion time of the remnant. However, earlier SN-driven ISM simulations already indicate that SN energy injection need not map onto a single effective driving scale
(
17
;
45
;
39
;
27
;
43
)
. Here we follow the interpretation of
3
,
5
, and
16
, in which turbulence injection in SN-driven flows is localized instead to the unstable contact layer at the cooling radius of the remnant,
R_{\rm cool}
, where
\bm{\nabla}P\times\bm{\nabla}\rho/\rho^{2}
generates solenoidal turbulence via the corrugated layer
(
18
;
46
, see also)
. In this model, the natural local condition is not
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm out})\sim 1
, but rather
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm inj})\sim 1
, with
\ell_{\rm inj}\sim R_{\rm cool}
. This can be understood as a consequence of instabilities at the contact discontinuity itself. We intentionally leave the specific mode ambiguous; it could involve Rayleigh–Taylor or Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities at the contact discontinuity, although the smooth morphology of the forward shock in our simulations disfavors thin-shell modes. If the forcing correlation time is set by the inverse instability growth rate,
\gamma_{\rm inst}^{-1}
, on
R_{\rm cool}
, then

\displaystyle t_{\rm cor}\sim\gamma_{\rm inst}^{-1}\sim t_{\rm nl}(\ell_{\rm inj})\sim t_{\rm eddy}(\ell_{\rm inj}),

(13)

where
t_{\rm nl}
is the nonlinear turnover time of the eddy generated at the injection scale. Thus the local
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm inj})\sim 1
condition is not an imposed forcing prescription, but a consequence of turbulence being generated by the unstable contact discontinuity. Since the measured velocity spectrum satisfies
\mathcal{P}_{u}(k)\propto k^{-3/2}
, the corresponding real-space velocity scaling is
u_{\ell}\propto\ell^{1/4}
, and therefore

\displaystyle{\rm{St}}(\ell)=\frac{t_{\rm cor}}{t_{\rm eddy}(\ell)}\propto\frac{t_{\rm cor}u_{\ell}}{\ell}\propto\ell^{-3/4},

(14)

if
t_{\rm cor}
is identified with the forcing correlation time.

The corresponding outer-scale value is then

\displaystyle{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm out})\sim\mathcal{C}_{\rm cool}{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm inj})\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm inj}}{\ell_{\rm out}}\right)^{3/4}\sim\mathcal{C}_{\rm cool}\left(\frac{R_{\rm cool}}{\ell_{\rm out}}\right)^{3/4},

(15)

where
\mathcal{C}_{\rm cool}
is an order-unity coefficient that absorbs how sharply one identifies the efficient injection scale with the onset of the radiative/contact-layer phase. Using the canonical range
R_{\rm cool}\approx 10\text{--}30\,\rm{pc}

(
15
;
8
;
82
;
51
;
57
)
, together with the measured outer scales
\ell_{\rm out}\approx 198\,\rm{pc}
for MW and
\ell_{\rm out}\approx 270\,\rm{pc}
for SB, gives

\displaystyle{\rm{St}}_{\rm MW}(\ell_{\rm out})\approx 0.11\text{--}0.24,\qquad{\rm{St}}_{\rm SB}(\ell_{\rm out})\approx 0.08\text{--}0.19,

(16)

for
\mathcal{C}_{\rm cool}=1
. These estimates overlap the measured
16^{\rm{th}}
–
84^{\rm{th}}
percentile ranges of the projected Strouhal numbers,
{\rm{St}}^{\perp}=0.22^{+0.27}_{-0.13}
and
{\rm{St}}^{\parallel}=0.25^{+0.45}_{-0.17}
for MW, and
{\rm{St}}^{\perp}=0.24^{+0.13}_{-0.12}
and
{\rm{St}}^{\parallel}=0.22^{+0.23}_{-0.12}
for SB. Thus, once the spatial variation in the measured correlation-time field is included, the cooling-radius/contact-layer model has the correct order of normalization. The empirical scale-dependent reconstruction below reaches
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\ast})=1
at
\ell_{\ast,\rm MW}\approx 25\,\rm{pc}
and
\ell_{\ast,\rm SB}\approx 32\,\rm{pc}
. These values place the empirical injection scale near the upper end of the canonical cooling-radius range, and somewhat above it in the SB case. We therefore interpret
R_{\rm cool,eff}\sim\ell_{\ast}
as an effective radiative contact-layer scale, including order-unity uncertainty in
\mathcal{C}_{\rm cool}
and environmental variation, rather than as an exact measurement of a single-remnant cooling radius. The model identifies the relevant scale as the radiative cooling/contact-layer scale, not the outer scale. In this interpretation, the measured outer-scale
{\rm{St}}
values are not set directly by the overall remnant lifetime, but by propagating the local condition
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm inj})\sim 1
from the radiative injection scale up the observed scale-free cascade.

III.2
The Strouhal number as a function of scale

The cooling-radius/contact-layer model above explains the measured outer-scale values by propagating a local injection-scale condition through the turbulent cascade. It therefore makes two predictions: the scale-free branch should follow
{\rm{St}}(\ell)\propto\ell^{-3/4}
, and the empirically inferred
{\rm{St}}=1
crossing should recover an effective radiative contact-layer scale. To test these predictions, we define a scale-dependent Strouhal number by adopting the simplest assumption:
t_{\rm cor}
is set by the driving and therefore does not vary with scale, whereas
t_{\rm eddy}
decreases deeper into the cascade. Hence,

\displaystyle{\rm{St}}(\ell)=\frac{t_{\rm cor}}{t_{\rm eddy}(\ell)}.

(17)

We extract the turbulent velocity as a function of scale using Parseval’s theorem, with
k=1/\ell
,

\displaystyle u(k)=\sqrt{\int_{k}^{\infty}\operatorname{d}\!{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{P}_{u}(k^{\prime})}

(18)

and use it to define the scale-dependent
t_{\rm eddy}
,

\displaystyle t_{\rm eddy}(k)=\frac{1}{ku(k)},

(19)

which gives
Equation 17
. We show the resulting
{\rm{St}}(\ell)
curves in
Figure 3
. Along the scale-free branch, the curves agree with the predicted
\ell^{-3/4}
scaling of
Equation 14
, as expected from the measured velocity cascade. For a pure scale-free branch normalized by an outer-scale value
{\rm{St}}
, defining the crossing scale
\ell_{\ast}
by
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\ast})=1
gives

\displaystyle\ell_{\ast}=\ell_{\rm out}\,{\rm{St}}^{4/3},\qquad\frac{\ell_{\ast}}{\ell_{\rm out}}={\rm{St}}^{4/3},

(20)

so
\ell_{\ast}
is the scale at which the extrapolated scale-free curve reaches the standard
{\rm{St}}=1
regime.

IV
Summary and conclusions

In this Letter we make the first measurement of the Strouhal number,
{\rm{St}}=t_{\rm cor}/t_{\rm out}
, in a supernova (SN)-driven, multiphase ISM. The S
{\rm{St}}=t_{\rm cor}/t_{\rm out}
can be interpreted as the temporal coherence of the effective forcing relative to the nonlinear turnover time of the outer-scale turbulence: it is the ratio between the forcing decorrelation time
t_{\rm cor}
, inferred from the two-time velocity correlation tensor in
Equation 7
and
Equation 9
, and the outer-scale eddy turnover time
t_{\rm out}
defined in
Equation 6
. This is analogous to the acceleration-field correlation time in standard turbulence-box models, where it is introduced as a free parameter and commonly set to unity; in contrast, here it is measured directly from the disk response to SN driving rather than prescribed. We use stratified SN-driven turbulence simulations of Milky Way (MW)- and starburst (SB)-analog galaxy models in a
1\,\rm{kpc}^{3}
domain (see
Section II
; two-dimensional slice visualizations are shown in
Figure 1
, and the setup is detailed in
3
and
16
). We summarize our main results as follows:

•

In
Section III.1.1
we find that
{\rm{St}}
in SN-driven multiphase turbulence is below unity, with projected median values
{\rm{St}}\approx 0.2\text{--}0.25
in both the MW and SB models. These measurements are shown in
Figure 2
and summarized in
Table 1
. This shows that the effective forcing in SN-driven turbulence decorrelates substantially faster than the outer-scale turbulent eddies.

•

In
Section III.1.2
, we present a cooling-radius/contact-layer model based on the SN-driven turbulence framework developed in
3
,
5
, and
16
, in which the contact discontinuity becomes unstable near the cooling radius,
R_{\rm cool}
, and the relevant condition is
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm inj})\sim 1
rather than
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm out})\sim 1
. Our interpretation is that the forcing time is set by the instability growth time,
t_{\rm cor}\sim\gamma_{\rm inst}^{-1}
, while the same process sets the nonlinear time of the eddy formed at that scale,
t_{\rm nl}(\ell_{\rm inj})\sim t_{\rm eddy}(\ell_{\rm inj})
, so that
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm inj})\sim 1
follows directly. This leads to the scale dependence in
Equation 14
and predicts outer-scale values of order
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\rm out})\sim 0.1\text{--}0.3
, in good agreement with the measured
16^{\rm{th}}
–
84^{\rm{th}}
percentile ranges in
Table 1
. SN-driven turbulence should therefore be viewed as locally forced near an effective radiative contact-layer scale, while the larger eddies that populate the cascade out to the outer scale evolve more slowly than the injection by factors of
\sim 3\text{--}10
and extend to scales of
\ell_{\rm out}/\ell_{0}\approx 1.7
in MW and
\approx 6.4
in SB (
Table 1
).

•

In
Section III.2
, we construct a scale-dependent Strouhal number,
{\rm{St}}(\ell)
, using
Equation 17
to test the cooling-radius/contact-layer model. Along the scale-free branch, the resulting curves in
Figure 3
agree well with the predicted scaling
{\rm{St}}(\ell)\propto\ell^{-3/4}
from
Equation 14
, consistent with the measured velocity spectrum in
3
and
16
. When the curves are scaled by
\ell_{\rm out}
, the vertical markers in
Figure 3
show that the reconstructed
{\rm{St}}=1
regime is reached at the nearly universal dimensionless scale
\ell_{\ast}/\ell_{\rm out}\approx 0.12\text{--}0.13
, corresponding to
\ell_{\ast,\rm MW}\approx 25\,\rm{pc}
and
\ell_{\ast,\rm SB}\approx 32\,\rm{pc}
. These physical scales are best interpreted as effective radiative contact-layer scales, not exact single-remnant cooling radii. The pure scale-free conversion between
\ell_{\rm out}
- and
\ell_{0}
-normalized crossing scales is given in
Equation 20
and
Appendix C
.

•

Taken together,
Figure 2
,
Table 1
, and
Figure 3
suggest that idealized turbulence-box models with
{\rm{St}}=1
do not generally represent outer-scale SN-driven ISM turbulence. In the notation of
35
and
72
, our measured outer-scale values lie on the short-correlation side of parameter space, away from the long-lived compressive-forcing cases that produce the strongest low-density voids and broadest mass-density PDFs. PDF-based models of ISM, CGM, and IGM structure, as well as star-formation rates calibrated in the long-correlation regime, may therefore need reassessment
(
73
;
71
;
72
)
.

More generally, the cooling-radius/contact-layer model described here should apply to turbulence driven by radiative cooling blast waves, indicating that its implications likely extend beyond the SN-driven ISM to a broader class of blast-wave-driven media.

IV.1
Limitations

The simulations do not directly resolve the physical scales that contain the inferred
{\rm{St}}(\ell)\sim 1
regime: the numerical cascade reaches
\sim 40\,\rm{pc}
, while
Figure 3
places the empirical crossing at
\ell_{\ast,\rm MW}\approx 25\,\rm{pc}
and
\ell_{\ast,\rm SB}\approx 32\,\rm{pc}
. Interpreting
\ell_{\ast}
as an effective radiative contact-layer scale therefore relies on extending the measured
\mathcal{P}_{u}(k)\propto k^{-3/2}
cascade below the resolved range, which should be reasonable as long as the turbulence remains SN-driven; if another driving mechanism (e.g., large-scale galactic shear or even
B\rightarrow u
fluxes from the Lorentz force;
34
), additional microphysics, or numerical dissipation changes the slope before
\ell_{\ast}
, the inferred crossing scale would shift according to
Equation 20
. We also infer an effective forcing decorrelation time from the velocity response, rather than measuring the driving correlation directly. As discussed in Footnote
1
, a direct forcing signal could be constructed from the baroclinic source term emphasized by
5
,
18
, and
46
. For example, with
\bm{B}\equiv\bm{\nabla}P\times\bm{\nabla}\rho/\rho^{2}
, one could measure
C_{ij}^{\omega B}(\bm{x},t)=\left\langle\omega_{i}(\bm{x},\tau)B_{j}(\bm{x},\tau+t)\right\rangle_{\tau}
, which correlates vorticity with the baroclinic torque generated at the unstable contact layer. We leave this direct forcing-correlation measurement to future work that will focus on both the co-spectrum and the characteristic scales of the forcing.

Appendix A
Correlation-function implementation

This section describes the estimator used for
Equation 7
and
Equation 9
, which produce the correlation functions in
Figure 2
,
Figure 4
, and
Figure 5
. The temporal correlation functions were computed from a time-ordered sequence of
ramses
-derived
hdf5
snapshots containing the simulation time and the three velocity components. All snapshots are ordered by their stored simulation times and restricted to the same spatial domain. For the measurements reported in
Section III.1.1
, the spatial domain is the model-dependent gaseous disk slab with vertical extent set by the scale height,
\ell_{0}
. The selected volume is then decomposed spatially so that the correlation sums can be accumulated in parallel.

Before forming correlations, we subtract a single space-time mean velocity for each component, computed over the selected snapshots and selected spatial domain,

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}\right\rangle_{\bm{x},t}=\frac{1}{N_{\rm cell}N_{t}}\sum_{\bm{x}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{t}-1}u_{i}(\bm{x},t_{n}).

(A1)

Here
N_{t}
is the number of snapshots,
N_{\rm cell}
is the number of selected grid cells,
t_{n}
is the simulation time of snapshot
n
, and the sum over
\bm{x}
runs over the selected cells.

We then compute the temporal correlation at every nonnegative lag supported by the sampled time series. For lag index
m
, the physical lag is the average snapshot-time separation

\displaystyle\tau_{m}=\frac{1}{N_{t}-m}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{t}-m-1}\left(t_{n+m}-t_{n}\right).

(A2)

For this lag, only the
N_{t}-m
valid, non-wrapping snapshot pairs enter the numerator. We use the linear-correlation convention returned by a zero-padded spectral estimator, for which the correlation amplitude is normalized by the total number of snapshots,
N_{t}
, rather than by the number of overlapping pairs,
N_{t}-m
. The corresponding unnormalized tensor is

\displaystyle C_{ij}(\tau_{m})=\frac{1}{N_{\rm cell}N_{t}}\sum_{\bm{x}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{t}-m-1}\left[u_{i}(\bm{x},t_{n})-\left\langle u_{i}\right\rangle_{\bm{x},t}\right]\left[u_{j}(\bm{x},t_{n+m})-\left\langle u_{j}\right\rangle_{\bm{x},t}\right].

(A3)

For the correlation-time measurements used in
Equation 9
, the tensor is normalized locally before spatial averaging. That is, each cell is divided by its own temporal rms, so that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}(\bm{x},\tau_{m})=\frac{1}{N_{t}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{t}-m-1}\frac{\left[u_{i}(\bm{x},t_{n})-\left\langle u_{i}\right\rangle_{\bm{x},t}\right]\left[u_{j}(\bm{x},t_{n+m})-\left\langle u_{j}\right\rangle_{\bm{x},t}\right]}{\sigma_{i}(\bm{x})\sigma_{j}(\bm{x})},

(A4)

with

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{2}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{N_{t}}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{t}-1}\left[u_{i}(\bm{x},t_{n})-\left\langle u_{i}\right\rangle_{\bm{x},t}\right]^{2}.

(A5)

Cells with zero temporal rms in either component contribute zero to the corresponding normalized component. The normalized tensor shown in the figures is the spatial average of these cell-wise normalized correlations,

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{ij}(\tau_{m})=\frac{1}{N_{\rm cell}}\sum_{\bm{x}}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}(\bm{x},\tau_{m}).

(A6)

The sums above are evaluated with zero-padded one-dimensional
fftw
transforms along the time axis. For each cell and velocity component, the time series is padded to at least twice its original length before transformation. Cross products of Fourier coefficients,
\widehat{u_{i}}^{\,*}(\bm{x},\omega)\widehat{u_{j}}(\bm{x},\omega)
, where
\omega
denotes the temporal Fourier frequency, are then inverse transformed to obtain the linear, non-periodic correlation sums for all nonnegative lags. The zero padding prevents wrap-around correlations, while the spatial decomposition allows the cell-wise sums and percentile summaries to be accumulated over the full selected volume.

The spatial distribution of integral correlation times is computed from the same cell-wise correlations. For each sampled cell,
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{ij}(\bm{x},\tau)
is integrated with the trapezoidal rule until the first zero crossing, with linear interpolation over the final interval. The
16^{\rm{th}}
,
50^{\rm{th}}
, and
84^{\rm{th}}
percentiles reported in
Table 1
are then recorded over cells for
t_{\rm cor}^{ij}
, along with the projected quantities
t_{\rm cor}^{\perp}(\bm{x})=[t_{\rm cor}^{xx}(\bm{x})+t_{\rm cor}^{yy}(\bm{x})]/2
and
t_{\rm cor}^{\parallel}(\bm{x})=t_{\rm cor}^{zz}(\bm{x})
, as defined in
Equation 10
. The projection is applied before taking spatial percentiles, so the percentile summaries of
t_{\rm cor}^{\perp}
are not expected to equal the arithmetic averages of the percentile summaries of
t_{\rm cor}^{xx}
and
t_{\rm cor}^{yy}
.

Appendix C
Converting between outer-scale and scale-height Strouhal normalizations

In
Section III.2
and
Equation 17
we define the scale-dependent Strouhal number relative to the outer scale,

\displaystyle{\rm{St}}(\ell)={\rm{St}}\left(\frac{\ell}{\ell_{\rm out}}\right)^{-3/4},

(C1)

where
{\rm{St}}
denotes the outer-scale normalization used for a given scale-dependent curve. The exponent
-3/4
follows from the measured velocity power spectrum and gives the model prediction in
Equation 14
,
\mathcal{P}_{u}(k)\propto k^{-3/2}
, which implies
u_{\ell}\propto\ell^{1/4}
and hence
{\rm{St}}(\ell)\propto\ell^{-3/4}
. If instead one wishes to express the same curve as a function of the scale-height-normalized coordinate
\ell/\ell_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\ell}{\ell_{\rm out}}=\frac{\ell/\ell_{0}}{\ell_{\rm out}/\ell_{0}},

(C2)

so that

\displaystyle{\rm{St}}\left(\frac{\ell}{\ell_{0}}\right)={\rm{St}}\left(\frac{\ell/\ell_{0}}{\ell_{\rm out}/\ell_{0}}\right)^{-3/4}={\rm{St}}\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm out}}{\ell_{0}}\right)^{3/4}\left(\frac{\ell}{\ell_{0}}\right)^{-3/4}.

(C3)

Thus changing from
\ell/\ell_{\rm out}
to
\ell/\ell_{0}
leaves the slope unchanged, but rescales the amplitude by the factor
(\ell_{\rm out}/\ell_{0})^{3/4}
. Using the values in
Table 1
, this factor is
\approx 1.48
for the MW model and
\approx 4.01
for the SB model, corresponding to
\ell_{\rm out}/\ell_{0}\approx 1.68
and
6.37
, respectively. A useful consequence is the scale at which the extrapolated curve reaches unity. Setting
{\rm{St}}(\ell_{\ast})=1
gives the crossing scale used in
Equation 20
,

\displaystyle\ell_{\ast}=\ell_{\rm out}\,{\rm{St}}^{4/3},\qquad\frac{\ell_{\ast}}{\ell_{\rm out}}={\rm{St}}^{4/3},\qquad\frac{\ell_{\ast}}{\ell_{0}}=\frac{\ell_{\rm out}}{\ell_{0}}\,{\rm{St}}^{4/3}.

(C4)

This provides the direct conversion between the crossing scale expressed in units of
\ell_{\rm out}
and the same crossing scale expressed in units of
\ell_{0}
. The empirical crossings shown in
Figure 3
give a nearly model-independent outer-scale fraction,
\ell_{\ast}/\ell_{\rm out}\approx 0.12\text{--}0.13
, with
\ell_{\ast,\rm MW}\approx 25\,\rm{pc}
(
\ell_{\ast}/\ell_{0}\approx 0.21
) and
\ell_{\ast,\rm SB}\approx 32\,\rm{pc}
(
\ell_{\ast}/\ell_{0}\approx 0.75
). These crossing scales are notably comparable to the canonical cooling radii of individual supernova remnants,
R_{\rm cool}\sim 10\text{--}30\,\rm{pc}

(
15
;
8
;
82
;
51
;
57
)
, suggesting that the scale where the reconstructed
{\rm{St}}(\ell)
reaches unity is close to the physical scale where remnants become radiative and baroclinically active
(
5
)
.
