Access Selection for Finite-SNR Modal Recoverability in Sampled-Wave Receivers
Shaojie Zhang, Ozgur B. Akan

Abstract
Large-aperture wave receivers can contain a large number of candidate sensor locations, antenna ports, or measurement blocks, while hardware and processing constraints allow only a subset to be activated. In this paper, receiver selection is formulated as a finite-SNR modal-recoverability problem, where the selected subset is required to support reliable recovery in every direction of a prescribed modal subspace. However, large trace, log-det, or codebook-distance values alone do not ensure that every prescribed modal direction is resolved. Specifically, the proposed framework consists of three nested recoverability criteria for individual modal degrees, a joint target subspace, and target modes in the presence of active non-target modes. The third criterion, the Schur-complement information floor, provides an exact worst-direction posterior-error interpretation. We further show that stricter recoverability criteria require at least as many active receiver accesses and derive tests that identify reliability targets that remain unattainable even when all available accesses are activated. Next, we specialize the framework to finite spherical-wave sampling and compare greedy receiver-selection rules. Numerical results demonstrate that global log-det is generally more access-efficient at moderate reliability floors, whereas Schur-based selection is more effective at stringent floors. While this paper is motivated by holographic and XL-MIMO receivers, the framework can be applied to general sampled wave systems.

In practice, a large-aperture receiver contains a large number of

hardware and processing constraints allow only a subset to be activated. Therefore, receiver selection for preserving reliable recovery in every

certification is that the former selects a candidate subset, whereas
the latter determines its operational adequacy. Given a finite candidate access set, an access budget, a target degree

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.

•

Problem formulation:

We formulate receiver selection as a finite-SNR modal recoverability
problem to preserve every required modal direction using a limited
number of activated measurements. Specifically, the proposed framework consists of three increasingly
stringent tests for individual target degrees, the joint target
subspace, and target modes coupled to active non-target modes.

•

Recoverability guarantees:

We first show that the three tests are nested and that the minimum
required access budget cannot decrease as the test is tightened. Next, we establish an exact worst-direction posterior-error
interpretation for the Schur-complement test and quantify the
information loss caused by target–non-target coupling.

•

Limits of receiver selection:

We do not treat selector failure and full-set infeasibility as the
same event, but instead distinguish a path-dependent failure from a
limitation of the available measurements. For a monotone library, if the full candidate set fails the required
recoverability test, then no selected subset can satisfy it. However, large trace, log-det, or finite-codebook-distance values
alone do not guarantee recovery of every target modal direction.

•

Design insights:

To verify the proposed framework, we conduct controlled
spherical-wave experiments and compare degree-wise, target-subspace,
Schur-based, and aggregate selection rules. Numerical results demonstrate that global log-det generally reaches
moderate reliability floors with fewer activated measurements,
whereas Schur-based selection is more effective under stringent
requirements.

Sampled Modal Receiver and Recoverability Model

II-A

Modal Representation and Receiver Access Library

We consider a finite modal truncation of order
L
. The retained index set and its dimension are given by

\mathcal{I}_{L}=\{(\ell,m):0\leq\ell\leq L,\;-\ell\leq m\leq\ell\},\hskip 20.00003ptQ=(L+1)^{2}.

After truncation, let
x
denote the modal coefficient vector of the transmitted or incident field, and let
C_{x}
denote its covariance:

x\in\mathbb{C}^{Q},\hskip 20.00003ptC_{x}=\mathbb{E}[xx^{\mathsf{H}}]\succeq 0.

Suppose that
C_{x}
is rank deficient. Its compact eigendecomposition is given by

C_{x}=U_{x}P_{x}U_{x}^{\mathsf{H}},\hskip 20.00003ptP_{x}\succ 0,\hskip 20.00003ptr_{x}=\operatorname{rank}(C_{x}),

which gives the active-coordinate representation

x=U_{x}P_{x}^{1/2}s,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathbb{E}[ss^{\mathsf{H}}]=I_{r_{x}}.

We consider a receiver access library that consists of the fixed finite set
\mathcal{V}
of physically available accesses before selection. The available accesses can include sensor locations, antenna ports, frequency samples, radii, time gates, and calibrated measurement blocks. The receiver selector selects a subset of the fixed library:

S\subseteq\mathcal{V}.

For a selected set
S
, let
m_{S}
denote the dimension of the stacked receiver output. The corresponding sampled modal matrix is given by

A_{S}\in\mathbb{C}^{m_{S}\times Q}.

The sampled modal matrix
A_{S}
is determined by receiver geometry, access constraints, and the propagation model, where aperture geometry controls the sampled wavefront structure in field-domain and near-field receivers
[
17
,
3
]
.

II-B

Sampled Gaussian Model and Recoverability Grams

For the Gaussian branch, the selected receiver output is modeled as

y_{S}=A_{S}x+n_{S},\hskip 20.00003ptn_{S}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,R_{S}),\hskip 20.00003ptR_{S}\succ 0,

where
R_{S}
denotes the selected noise covariance. The covariance
R_{S}
accounts for independent sample noise and correlated receiver noise. By applying receiver-noise whitening, we obtain

\bar{y}_{S}=R_{S}^{-1/2}y_{S}=R_{S}^{-1/2}A_{S}x+\bar{n}_{S},\hskip 20.00003pt\bar{n}_{S}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,I_{m_{S}}).

Given the active-coordinate representation of
x
, the whitened model is given by

\bar{y}_{S}=H(S)s+\bar{n}_{S},\hskip 20.00003ptH(S)=R_{S}^{-1/2}A_{S}U_{x}P_{x}^{1/2}.

We next define the active source-whitened recoverability Gram as

K(S)=H(S)^{\mathsf{H}}H(S).

Let
F(S)
and
G(S)
denote the unweighted normal Gram and the prior-weighted full-coordinate recoverability Gram, respectively, where

F(S)=A_{S}^{\mathsf{H}}R_{S}^{-1}A_{S},\hskip 20.00003ptG(S)=C_{x}^{1/2}F(S)C_{x}^{1/2}.

The matrix
F(S)
measures sampled information in physical modal coordinates, while
G(S)
includes the modal covariance. When
C_{x}
is singular,
K(S)
provides the equivalent recoverability Gram in active coordinates.

II-C

Modal Subspaces and the Additive-Gram Convention

For each modal degree
\ell
, let
V_{\ell}
and
d_{\ell}
denote the associated subspace and its dimension, respectively.

V_{\ell}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{\ell,m}:m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell\},\hskip 20.00003ptd_{\ell}=2\ell+1,

Let
\Pi_{\ell}
denote the orthogonal projector onto
V_{\ell}
. We assume that the target degree set

\mathcal{T}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,L\}
is specified before the receiver-selection stage. To evaluate finite-SNR certificates, we restrict
G(S)
, or
F(S)
for an unweighted physical certificate, to the relevant modal subspaces.

For access measurements with independent noise blocks, the prior-weighted Gram is given by the following additive form.

G(S)=\sum_{e\in S}B_{e},\hskip 20.00003ptB_{e}=C_{x}^{1/2}A_{e}^{\mathsf{H}}R_{e}^{-1}A_{e}C_{x}^{1/2}\succeq 0.

Although this additive form applies to independent noise blocks, we do not impose it on the general model. For correlated receiver noise, shared analog front ends, or joint processing, the full selected covariance
R_{S}
is used to construct

. Lemma

III
Finite-SNR Modal Recoverability Criteria

In this section, we first construct a degree-wise information test for one modal degree. Next, we introduce a target-subspace test for the joint activation of the prescribed modal layers. At last, we develop a Schur-complement test for the setting in which the non-target modes remain Gaussian nuisance coefficients.

III-A

Degree-Wise Information Floors

Consider a selected access subset
S\subseteq\mathcal{V}
. Let
G(S)
and
F(S)
denote the prior-weighted recoverability Gram and the unweighted normal Gram introduced in Section II, respectively. For modal degree
\ell
, we define the prior-weighted degree information floor as follows.

\mu_{\ell}(S)=\lambda_{\min}\left(\left.\Pi_{\ell}G(S)\Pi_{\ell}\right|_{V_{\ell}}\right)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in V_{\ell}\\
\|u\|_{2}=1\end{subarray}}u^{\mathsf{H}}G(S)u.

(1)

Here,
\Pi_{\ell}
denotes the orthogonal projector onto
V_{\ell}
. Likewise, the unweighted modal information floor in physical coordinates is given by the following expression.

\nu_{\ell}(S)=\lambda_{\min}\left(\left.\Pi_{\ell}F(S)\Pi_{\ell}\right|_{V_{\ell}}\right)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in V_{\ell}\\
\|u\|_{2}=1\end{subarray}}u^{\mathsf{H}}F(S)u.

(2)

Here,
\mu_{\ell}(S)
represents the prior-weighted information floor, whereas
\nu_{\ell}(S)
represents the unweighted physical-coordinate floor. In this paper, the admission rules below focus on
\mu_{\ell}(S)
, whereas
\nu_{\ell}(S)
is invoked only when an unweighted test is stated explicitly.

Given a fixed threshold
\tau>0
, we use the following degree-
\ell
admission condition.

\mu_{\ell}(S)\geq\tau.

(3)

Consider a prescribed target degree set

T\subseteq\{0,\ldots,L\}
, and define

\mu_{\mathrm{deg}}(S)=\min_{\ell\in T}\mu_{\ell}(S).

(4)

The subset
S
satisfies degree-wise target admission when

\mu_{\mathrm{deg}}(S)\geq\tau
.

Joint target-subspace admission:

We next define the target modal subspace and its associated projector.

V_{T}=\bigoplus_{\ell\in T}V_{\ell},\hskip 20.00003pt\Pi_{T}=\sum_{\ell\in T}\Pi_{\ell}.

Based on these definitions, we use the following target-subspace information floor.

\mu_{T}(S)=\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\Pi_{T}G(S)\Pi_{T}\big|_{V_{T}}\right).

Given
\tau
, the subset
S
jointly admits the target modal stack when
\mu_{T}(S)\geq\tau
. However, this joint condition can fail even if every degree in
T
meets its degree-wise requirement.

Admission with Gaussian nuisance modes:

We further retain the modal coefficients outside the target subspace. Let
V_{c}=V_{T}^{\perp}
denote the non-target subspace within the retained modal coordinates. With respect to
V_{T}\oplus V_{c}
, the prior-weighted Gram is given by the following block form.

G(S)=\begin{bmatrix}G_{TT}(S)&G_{Tc}(S)\\
G_{cT}(S)&G_{cc}(S)\end{bmatrix}.

According to the Schur complement, we use the following nuisance-conditioned target information matrix.

I_{T|c}(S)=G_{TT}(S)-G_{Tc}(S)\big(I+G_{cc}(S)\big)^{-1}G_{cT}(S).

We next define the associated nuisance-conditioned information floor.

\mu_{T|c}(S)=\lambda_{\min}\big(I_{T|c}(S)\big).

Given
\tau
, the subset
S
satisfies nuisance-robust target admission when
\mu_{T|c}(S)\geq\tau
. In this case, the non-target coefficients are modeled as active Gaussian nuisance variables rather than deleted coordinates.

Theorem 1

(Nested modal-admission certificates)
.

Assume that the source-whitened Gaussian model holds. Let the retained coefficient vector be partitioned as
s=(s_{T},s_{c})
, where
s_{T}\in V_{T}
and
s_{c}\in V_{c}
. For a selected access set
S
, marginalizing
s_{c}
gives the following posterior covariance of
s_{T}
.

\Sigma_{T|y}(S)=\big(I+I_{T|c}(S)\big)^{-1}.

As a result, the nuisance-robust information-floor condition and the worst posterior-variance condition are equivalent.

\mu_{T|c}(S)\geq\tau\hskip 10.00002pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{\max}\big(\Sigma_{T|y}(S)\big)\leq(1+\tau)^{-1}.

Moreover, the three information floors obey the following ordering.

0\leq\mu_{T|c}(S)\leq\mu_{T}(S)\leq\min_{\ell\in T}\mu_{\ell}(S).

Therefore, nuisance-robust admission guarantees joint target-subspace admission, and joint target-subspace admission guarantees degree-wise admission. However, the two reverse implications are not guaranteed in general.

(Intermodal coupling penalties and nested access budgets)
.

Let

\mu_{T|c}(S)\geq\mu_{T}(S)-\|G_{Tc}(S)(I+G_{cc}(S))^{-1}G_{cT}(S)\|_{2}.

(5)

\mu_{\ell}(S)=P_{\ell}\nu_{\ell}(S).

III-B

Monotonicity of Fixed Access Libraries

The full-set infeasibility result is based on monotone information growth.

Definition 1

(Monotone finite candidate measurement set)
.

A finite candidate measurement set
\mathcal{V}
is referred to as
G

G(S)\preceq G(S^{\prime}).

(7)

Likewise, the

F(S)\preceq F(S^{\prime})
for every

(8)

Then

where

is independent of

. If the

F(S)\preceq F(S^{\prime}),\hskip 20.00003ptG(S)\preceq G(S^{\prime}).

III-C

Separation Between Aggregate Objectives and Admission Certificates

The multi-tier information floors introduced above are used to determine finite-SNR feasibility for modal admission. Common aggregate objectives can be applied to rank candidate access subsets. However, their objective values alone do not provide a degree-wise modal-admission certificate. Let
G_{\ell}(S)
denote the restriction of the prior-weighted Gram to degree
\ell
.

G_{\ell}(S)=\Pi_{\ell}G(S)\Pi_{\ell}|_{V_{\ell}}.

(11)

For a finite source-whitened codeword-difference set

\mathcal{D}\subset V_{\ell}\setminus\{0\}
, we define the following three aggregate objectives.

\displaystyle\Phi_{\rm tr}(S)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}G_{\ell}(S),

(12)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\log}(S)

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\det(I+G_\ell(S)),

(13)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}(S)

\displaystyle=\min_{\Delta\in\mathcal{D}}\Delta^{H}G_{\ell}(S)\Delta.

(14)

For physical-coordinate codebooks, let
F_{\ell}(S)=\Pi_{\ell}F(S)\Pi_{\ell}|_{V_{\ell}}
. If
G_{\ell}(S)=P_{\ell}F_{\ell}(S)
, then the prior-weighted and physical codebook-distance objectives differ only by the positive scalar
P_{\ell}
.

(Aggregate-objective separation)

(15)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\rm tr}(S_{\rm cl}^{\rm rep})

\displaystyle>\Phi_{\rm tr}(S_{\rm sp})+R,

(16)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\log}(S_{\rm cl}^{\rm rep})

\displaystyle>\Phi_{\log}(S_{\rm sp})+R,

(17)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}(S_{\rm cl}^{\rm rep})

\displaystyle>\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}(S_{\rm sp})+R.

(18)

IV
Recoverability Conditions for Finite Wave-Receiver Layouts

IV-B

Residual-Interval Certificates

For a general finite angular layout, we consider the following residual representation.

\Gamma_{\ell}=\frac{N_{\rm sens}}{4\pi}I_{2\ell+1}+E_{\ell},\hskip 20.00003pt\|E_{\ell}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon_{\ell},

(27)

Here,
N_{\rm sens}

We next define the nominal degree-
\ell

VIII
Conclusion

In conclusion, the key distinction between receiver selection and modal feasibility is that
the two procedures address different design tasks. A selection objective is used to determine the order in which candidate
measurements are activated. However, the selection objective alone does not enable the conclusion that
the prescribed modal subspace is recoverable. To this end, modal operation is gated by a worst-direction finite-SNR
requirement. Aggregate objectives can then be applied only to construct or refine a
candidate subset.

If the full access library fails the required certificate, i.e., its
certificate value is below the prescribed floor, then the failure represents
a system-level limitation and no subset selector over the same monotone
library can satisfy that tier. In contrast, when the full access library is feasible, the remaining problem
focuses on access-efficient selection, where the preferred rule depends on
the required reliability level. Although universal selector optimality and calibrated HMIMO/XL-MIMO
performance are important, we do not focus on them in the normalized
spherical-wave study, instead, we use the study to illustrate these design
principles.
