Cohomological Obstructions for Varieties over p p -adic Function Fields
Yisheng TIAN

Abstract
We introduce various cohomological obstructions for smooth integral varieties over p p -adic function fields. We show that the unramified obstruction is the finest one among obstructions arising from arithmetic dualities. We also construct an explicit example whose unramified obstruction gives a proper obstruction while Manin obstruction does not. Finally, we compare the unramified obstruction with the descent obstruction.

Introduction

In the past decade,
there has been increasing interest in the arithmetic of varieties
defined over higher dimensional semi-global function fields.
For instance, the following works are particularly relevant to this article.
Harari, Scheiderer, Szamuely, Tian, Linh and Zhang
[

,

,

,

,

p
-adic function fields,
and Izquierdo
[

,

investigated the local-global principle for function fields over higher local fields.
Also,
[

,

showed that the degree three unramified cohomology group gives

Another origin of the present article is the comparison of various obstructions over number fields.
The works

,

,

,

,

,

Besides,

[

*p. 174 conjectured that weak approximation with Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only one

This conjecture also motivates us to explore the correct analogue of the usual Brauer–Manin obstruction over

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

-group.

(i)

The unramified obstruction is finer than the Manin obstruction,

.

(ii)

For any

X(\mathbf{A})^{H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}}\subset X(\mathbf{A})^{\alpha}

(iii)

Suppose that

For any

.

The obstructions

and

H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{3}(X,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}(2))\to H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z}(2))

Now we may use either cohomological tools or arithmetic dualities
to conclude

.

For

Then a piece of a Poitou–Tate sequence for groups of multiplicative type yields the desired comparisons.

The descent obstruction is central in obstruction theory (see for instance
[

,

Over

As of today, the lack of a suitable generalization of the Kottwitz exact sequence (

6
Unramified obstructions and descents

This section aims to comparing the unramified obstruction with the descent obstruction.
It is well-known
H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}(K(\SL_{n})/K,\Lambda)\simeq H^{3}(K,\Lambda)
since
\SL_{n}
is rational
(for instance, see
[
Mer02
]
*p. 446).
However, we still have
H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}(\SL_{n},\Lambda)\simeq H^{3}(K,\Lambda)
for distinct reasons.

Lemma 6.1
.

There is an isomorphism
H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}(\SL_{n},\Lambda)\simeq H^{3}(K,\Lambda)
for any
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

Applying
[
EKLV98
]
*Assumption 3.19 and Proposition 3.20(i) to
G=H=\SL_{n}
yields
the desired isomorphism.
∎

(6.2).

Let
G
be a linear
K
-group and embed it into
\SL_{n}
for some
n\geq 2
.
Let
X=\SL_{n}/G
.
Take any
x\in X(K)
and
let
q_{x}:\SL_{n}\to X
,
s\mapsto s.x
.
By the functoriality of cup-product,
we see that

s\in\SL_{n}(\mathbf{A})^{q_{x}^{*}(H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}(X))}\iff q_{x}(s)\in X(\mathbf{A})^{H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}(X)}.

But
q_{x}^{*}(H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}(X))\subset H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}(\SL_{n},\Lambda)\simeq H^{3}(K,\Lambda)
,
so we conclude
q_{x}(s)=s.x\in X(\mathbf{A})^{H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}(X)}
for any

, i.e.,

\SL_{n}(\mathbf{A}).X(K)\subset X(\mathbf{A})^{H_{{\mathrm{nr}}}^{3}(X)}
.
If its converse holds, we prove that the unramified obstruction is finer than the descent obstruction.

Theorem 6.4
