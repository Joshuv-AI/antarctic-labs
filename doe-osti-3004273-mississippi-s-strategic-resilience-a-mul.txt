 ABSTRACT

 Mississippi’s electric grid resilience challenges are linked to an intersection of complex
 socioeconomic, ecological, technological, historical, and political challenges, exacerbated by
 increasing severe weather like flooding and tornado events. The state’s legacy of
 underinvestment in critical energy infrastructure, particularly in rural areas and vulnerable
 floodplains, have stressed an aging grid, creating long-lasting disruptions in electric service
 during weather-related outages.

 Effective emergency management and preparedness is further hampered by a lack of
 coordination across local, county, and regional scales. Using the TASTI-GRID platform and
 partnership with Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), Mississippi is developing a
 comprehensive regional resilience strategy to overcome energy security and reliability
 challenges, mitigating the impacts of natural hazards, and positioning Mississippi as a resilient
 and premier destination for residents, businesses, and economic development.

 An Overview of Mississippi’s Grid Resilience Challenges

Mississippi’s electric grid resilience challenges are linked to an
intersection of complex socioeconomic, ecological, technological,
historical, and political systems, compounded by severe weather.

Underinvestment in energy infrastructure, particularly in rural areas and
vulnerable floodplains, coupled with an aging grid, have contributed to
long-lasting disruptions in electric service during severe weather.

According to the Energy Information Administration (EIA), since 2011,
the number of weather-related outages has increased by
approximately 80% across the US, where customers are without
power for an average of 7 hours per year.

Though Mississippi trails behind Louisiana in terms of outage hours per
year, its rural landscape, low population and population density, and
overall data availability provide unique circumstances in its overall
grid reliability.

Both states endure similar weather to induce major outages, yet
Mississippi’s profile indicates a nuanced approach to recovery and
resilience, particularly in relationship to adaptive capacity and unique
utility and customer contexts. Percentage of customer utility meter data

 Supporting MDA to Enhance Mississippi’s Electric Grid Resilience

 Overall resilience will require both top-down and bottom-up efforts aided by data and resources for
 decision-making support, with hands-on stakeholder engagement & consideration of multidimensional
 drivers behind localized resilience. The Mississippi Development Authority has partnered with Oak Ridge
 National Laboratory’s Critical Infrastructure Resilience Group to evaluate, analyze, and strategize for
 statewide grid resilience that supports economic development, energy security, and human health & safety.
 Leveraging ORNL expertise, MDA is:

 • Partnering with state and local agencies to couple management practices and share resources
 • Using multi-systems analysis to develop innovative solutions the short-and-long-term impacts of
 increased severe weather, temperature extremes, and long duration power outages, and
 • Preparing for large load additions (i.e. data centers), their siting, and necessary resource management to
 ensure utility customer grid reliability.

 Through these resources, grid resilience investments can empower small municipalities, rural
 cooperatives, and other utilities across the state to engage in a regional resilience strategy that enables
 emergency preparedness, energy security, and rapid response to major power outages.
