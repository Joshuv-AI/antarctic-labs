Convergence of the PML method for scattering problems in poroelastic media
Qianyuan Yin, Changkun Wei, Bo Zhang

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the time-harmonic wave scattering problems in three dimensional poroelastic media. By introducing an intermediate variable p p , the original 𝐮 − 𝐰 \mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w} system is equivalently transformed into a 𝐮 − p \mathbf{u}-p system with fewer degrees of freedom, which facilitates the derivation of the fundamental solution, Green’s identity and positivity of the complex wave numbers. A perfectly matched layer (PML) method is then introduced in the spherical coordinates to truncate the unbounded scattering problem. Under certain assumptions on the poroelastic and PML parameters, we prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the PML problems both in the truncated domain and layer. Moreover, the exponential convergence of the PML method is established in terms of the thickness and parameters of the PML layer. The proof is based on the PML extension and the exponential decay properties of the stretched fundamental solution. As far as we know, this is the first convergence result of the PML method for poroelastic scattering problems. Keywords: Biot, poroelastic wave equations, PML, convergence

Introduction

Poroelastic wave scattering problem can be effectively modeled by Biot’s theory, which extends conventional acoustic
or pure elastic models by incorporating factors such as fluid, porosity, pressure, etc
[
3
,
4
]
.
Biot’s theory of poroelasticity is highly relevant to many fields of applications such as geophysical exploration
of oil or gas, bio-engineering and materials science
[
13
,
23
]
. The study on this problem has been
a subject of interest in both mathematical and engineering communities (see
[
22
,
27
,
28
,
30
]
and the reference therein).

The truncation of the unbounded domain remains a challenging topic for wave scattering problems. Absorbing boundary
conditions (ABCs) play a crucial role in truncation for both mathematical analysis and numerical computation
[
15
]
,
among which the perfectly matched layer (PML) method is widely used due to its simplicity, flexibility and effectiveness.
Originally proposed by Bérenger in 1994 for solving time-dependent Maxwell’s equations
[
2
]
, the PML method
surrounds the computational domain with a specially designed medium in a finite thickness layer in which the scattered waves
decay rapidly regardless of the wave incident angle, thereby greatly reducing the computational complexity of the scattering
problem. A fundamental and challenging issue about PML method is the convergence analysis, i.e., the error estimate in the
computational domain between the solutions for the original scattering problem and the truncated PML problem, since the
truncated boundary generates reflected waves which will contaminate the solution in practical computation.

The convergence analysis of the PML method has been widely studied for the acoustic and electromagnetic wave scattering problems;
see, e.g.,
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
10
,
14
,
24
,
25
]
. Several works are also available on the convergence of
the PML method for the elastic wave scattering problems. For the elastic obstacle scattering problem, the exponential convergence
of a spherical PML method was established in
[
8
]
, and the exponential convergence of a Cartesian PML method were
established in
[
11
]
. For the elastic scattering problems in periodic structures, the exponential convergence for the
proposed PML method was established in
[
17
,
18
]
for both one-dimensional and two-dimensional cases. However,
the convergence analysis of the PML methods is quite rare for the relatively complicated interaction scattering problems between
multi-physical fields. We refer to
[
16
,
31
]
for the exponential convergence result of the PML method for the
acoustic-elastic and electromagnetic-elastic interaction scattering problems. Recently, Yin et al.
[
26
]
established the
well-poseness and exponential convergence of the PML method for the three-dimensional thermoelastic wave scattering problems.
To the best of our knowledge, there is no convergence
theory of the PML method for poroelastic scattering problems.

In this paper, we focus on the theoretical analysis for three-dimensional poroelastic obstacle scattering problems.
Biot’s initial formulation
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w}
is confined to the displacements of solid and fluid phases.
The time-harmonic poroelastic wave equations are reformulated by replacing the fluid displacement with pressure
p
,
thereby reducing the number of degrees of freedom. An abstract
\mathbf{u}-p
system is thus obtained that incorporates
thermoelastic and poroelastic wave models into a unified framework, thereby addressing the key issues of the mathematical model,
such as the derivation of the fundamental solution and Green’s identity. We prove that the real and imaginary part of the three
wave numbers for the poroelastic system are positive under some reasonable constraints on these poroelatic parameters. This fact
plays a key role in the PML analysis for the poroelastic scattering problems. Finally, we propose a spherical PML method to truncate
the unbounded poroelastic scattering domain into a bounded computational domain. The complex spherical coordinate stretching to
derive the PML problem is
[
12
]
:

\widetilde{r}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}s(\tau)d\tau,\;s=1+{\bf i}\sigma\;(\sigma\geq 0)

where the continuous function
s(\tau)
is the model medium property. We remark that the PML equation is derived directly from
the original
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w}
formulation to make use of the symmetry of the system. Under some assumptions on the
poroelastic and PML medium parameters, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the solutions to the PML equation in the truncated
domain and layer. We estimate the PML extension of the poroelastic boundary integral representation by the geometric and polynomial
methods. The exponential convergence of the PML method is established in terms of the thickness and parameters of the PML layer.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
3
, we present the mathematical model for the time‑harmonic poroelastic scattering problem, introduce an abstract
\mathbf{u}-p
system, and derive the fundamental solution and Green’s identity, as well as establish the positivity of the complex wave numbers. Section
4
is devoted to propose the PML method for the poroelastic scattering problem based on a complex spherical coordinate stretching. The well-posedness for the poroelastic PML equation in the truncated domain
and layer are then studied and carefully analyzed, and the exponential convergence result
is finally established for the proposed PML method. Some conclusions are given in section
5
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For regular vectors
U=(\mathbf{u}^{\top},p)^{\top}
and

defined in a
bounded domain

with the unit outward normal

to the smooth boundary

second Green’s identity holds

\int_{D}(U^{\top}B^{*}V-V^{\top}BU)dx=\int_{\partial D}(U^{\top}R^{*}V-V^{\top}RU)ds(x),

where

R^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{T}\mathbb{I}_{3}&-T_{3}\boldsymbol{n}\\
0&\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad R=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{T}\mathbb{I}_{3}&T_{2}\boldsymbol{n}\\
0&\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(3.30)

and the surface traction operator
\mathcal{T}
is defined by
\mathcal{T}\mathbf{u}=\mu\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\mathbf{u}+(\lambda+\mu)({\rm div}\mathbf{u})\boldsymbol{n}\;{\rm on}\;\partial D

Proof.

A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle U^{\top}B^{*}V-V^{\top}BU

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\cdot(\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}-T_{3}\nabla p^{\prime}+T_{1}\mathbf{u}^{\prime})+p(\Delta p^{\prime}+T_{4}p^{\prime}-T_{2}{\rm div}\mathbf{u}^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\cdot(\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u}+T_{1}\mathbf{u}+T_{2}\nabla p)-p^{\prime}(\Delta p+T_{3}{\rm div}\mathbf{u}+T_{4}p)

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle(\mathbf{u}\cdot\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\cdot\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u})+(p\Delta p^{\prime}-p^{\prime}\Delta p)-T_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\cdot\nabla p+p{\rm div}\mathbf{u}^{\prime})-T_{3}(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla p^{\prime}+p^{\prime}{\rm div}\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle(\mathbf{u}\cdot\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\cdot\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u})+(p\Delta p^{\prime}-p^{\prime}\Delta p)-T_{2}{\rm div}(p\mathbf{u}^{\prime})-T_{3}{\rm div}(p^{\prime}\mathbf{u}).

Integrating the differential identity (
3.31

and applying the divergence theorem
yields the integral identity

\displaystyle\int_{D}(U^{\top}B^{*}V-V^{\top}BU)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D}(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathcal{T}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\cdot\mathcal{T}\mathbf{u})ds+\int_{\partial D}\Big(p\frac{\partial p^{\prime}}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}-p^{\prime}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}\Big)ds-\int_{\partial D}(T_{2}p\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\prime}ds-\int_{\partial D}(T_{3}p^{\prime}\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\mathbf{u}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D}\Big[\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathcal{T}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+p\frac{\partial p^{\prime}}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}-(T_{3}p^{\prime}\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\mathbf{u}\Big]ds-\int_{\partial D}\Big[\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\cdot\mathcal{T}\mathbf{u}+p^{\prime}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}+(T_{2}p\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\Big]ds.

(3.32)

With the definitions of

and

into the desired Green’s identity (
3.29

(3.48)

Taking the limit

3.42

). The positivity of the imaginary

∎

Remark 3.5

(A sufficient condition for (
3.40
))
.

We now give a sufficient condition for (
3.40
) to hold. Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\rho}{\lambda+2\mu+\alpha^{2}M}\left(\frac{\rho+\alpha^{2}m-2\alpha\rho_{f}}{\lambda+2\mu}-\frac{\rho}{\lambda+2\mu+\alpha^{2}M}+\frac{m}{M}\right)-\frac{\rho m-\rho_{f}^{2}}{M(\lambda+2\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\left[\frac{\rho^{2}+\alpha^{2}m\rho-2\alpha\rho\rho_{f}}{\alpha^{2}(\lambda+2\mu)+\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda+2\mu)^{2}}{M}}-\frac{\alpha^{2}(\rho m-\rho_{f}^{2})}{\alpha^{2}(\lambda+2\mu)}+m-\frac{\rho^{2}M}{(\lambda+2\mu+\alpha^{2}M)^{2}}\right].

Since

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\left[\frac{\rho^{2}+\alpha^{2}m\rho-2\alpha\rho\rho_{f}}{\alpha^{2}(\lambda+2\mu)+\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda+2\mu)^{2}}{M}}-\frac{\alpha^{2}(\rho m-\rho_{f}^{2})}{\alpha^{2}(\lambda+2\mu)}+m-\frac{\rho^{2}M}{(\lambda+2\mu+\alpha^{2}M)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{2}+\alpha^{2}m\rho-2\alpha\rho\rho_{f}}{\alpha^{2}(\lambda+2\mu)}-\frac{\alpha^{2}(\rho m-\rho_{f}^{2})}{\alpha^{2}(\lambda+2\mu)}+m=\frac{(\rho-\alpha\rho_{f})^{2}}{\alpha^{2}(\lambda+2\mu)}+m>0,

we conclude that the condition (
3.40

is sufficiently large.

4
The spherical PML method

In this section, we shall derive the PML formulation for the poroelastic problem
(
3.38a
)-(
3.38d
). The well-posedness and stability of the PML problem
in truncated domain and layer can be established under certain constraints on poroelastic parameters.
Finally, we prove the exponential convergence of the PML method based on the PML extension
and the exponential decay properties of the stretched fundamental solution.

4.1
PML formulation

For given
r_{2}>r_{0}
, let
B_{r_{2}}
be a spherical domain surrounding
B_{r_{0}}
.
The truncated PML domain and the PML layer are denoted by
\Omega_{2}:=B_{r_{2}}\backslash\overline{\Omega}

and
\Omega_{{\rm PML}}:=B_{r_{2}}\backslash\overline{B_{r_{0}}}
, respectively.
For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
consider the spherical coordinates

x_{1}=r\sin\theta\cos\phi,\;x_{2}=r\sin\theta\sin\phi,\;x_{3}=r\cos\theta

with
r=|x|
and Euler angle
(\theta,\phi)
.
Let
s(r)=1+{\bf i}\sigma(r)
be the model medium property which satisfies

\sigma\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{+}),\;\sigma\geq 0,\;{\rm and}\;\sigma=0\;\;{\rm for}\;r\leq r_{0}.

Denote by
\widetilde{r}
the complex radius defined as

\widetilde{r}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}s(\tau)d\tau\triangleq r\beta(r)=r[1+{\bf i}\sigma_{m}(r)],

(4.53)

where
\sigma_{m}(r)=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\sigma(\tau)d\tau
. For the Cartesian coordinates

x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})^{\top}
, the corresponding change of variables is
\widetilde{x}=(\widetilde{x}_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2},\widetilde{x}_{3})^{\top}
with

\widetilde{x}_{1}=\widetilde{r}\sin\theta\cos\phi,\;\widetilde{x}_{2}=\widetilde{r}\sin\theta\sin\phi,\;\widetilde{x}_{3}=\widetilde{r}\cos\theta.

(4.54)

Following
[
8
]
, we impose the constraint conditions on
\sigma_{m}\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(r)=0\quad{\rm for}\;r\leq r_{0},

(4.55)

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(r)=\sigma_{0}\quad{\rm for}\;r\geq r_{1},

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(r)\;\text{is monotone increasing for $r\in(r_{0},r_{1})$},

where
\sigma_{0}>0
and
r_{1}
are given constants satisfying

r_{0}+\frac{3}{4}d<r_{1}<r_{2},\;d=r_{2}-r_{0}\;\text{denotes the thickness of PML layer}.

(4.56)

The lower bound on
r_{1}
guarantees that the cut‑off function used in the convergence analysis
(see the proof of Lemma
4.5
) is well defined.

From (
4.53
)-(
4.55
), one easily verifies that
\sigma_{m}\leq\sigma
for
all
r\geq r_{0}
. In the following text, we take
\sigma_{m}(r)
in the specific form

\sigma_{m}(r)=\begin{cases}0,&r\leq r_{0},\\
\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\frac{h\Big(\frac{r-r_{0}}{r_{1}-r_{0}}\Big)}{h\Big(\frac{r-r_{0}}{r_{1}-r_{0}}\Big)+kh\Big(\frac{r_{1}-r}{r_{1}-r_{0}}\Big)},\quad&r_{0}<r<r_{1},\\
\sigma_{0},&r\geq r_{1},\end{cases}

(4.57)

where
h(\tau)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}},\;&\tau>0\\
&0,&\tau\leq 0\end{aligned}\right.
and
0<k<1
.

Let
\{\boldsymbol{e}_{r},\boldsymbol{e}_{\theta},\boldsymbol{e}_{\phi}\}
be the unit vectors of the spherical coordinates

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{e}_{r}

\displaystyle=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)^{\top},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{e}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=(\cos\theta\cos\phi,\cos\theta\sin\phi,-\sin\theta)^{\top},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{e}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=(-\sin\phi,\cos\phi,0)^{\top}.

For any scalar
f
and vector
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})^{\top}
, the stretched differential
operators in spherical coordinates are defined as follows

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}f

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\widetilde{r}}\boldsymbol{e}_{r}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\boldsymbol{e}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}\sin\theta}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi}\boldsymbol{e}_{\phi},

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\rm div}}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{u}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}^{2}}\frac{\partial(\widetilde{r}^{2}u_{r})}{\partial\widetilde{r}}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}\sin\theta}\frac{\partial(\sin\theta u_{\theta})}{\partial\theta}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}\sin\theta}\frac{\partial u_{\phi}}{\partial\phi},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}\times\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}\sin\theta}\left[\frac{\partial(\sin\theta u_{\phi})}{\partial\theta}-\frac{\partial u_{\theta}}{\partial\phi}\right]\boldsymbol{e}_{r}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}}\left[\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial u_{r}}{\partial\phi}-\frac{\partial(\widetilde{r}u_{\phi})}{\partial\widetilde{r}}\right]\boldsymbol{e}_{\theta}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}}\left[\frac{\partial(\widetilde{r}u_{\theta})}{\partial\widetilde{r}}-\frac{\partial u_{r}}{\partial\theta}\right]\boldsymbol{e}_{\phi},

with
u_{r}=\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{r}
,
u_{\theta}=\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{\theta}
and

u_{\phi}=\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{\phi}
.
It is easy to verify that

\widetilde{\nabla}f=P_{1}^{-1}\nabla f,\;\widetilde{{\rm div}}\mathbf{u}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}s}{\rm div}(P_{2}\mathbf{u}),\;\widetilde{\nabla}\times\mathbf{u}=P_{2}^{-1}\nabla\times P_{1}\mathbf{u},

(4.58)

where
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are defined by

P_{1}=\operatorname{diag}\{s,\beta,\beta\},\quad P_{2}=\operatorname{diag}\{\beta^{2},s\beta,s\beta\}.

(4.59)

Moreover, it follows from a complex variable analyticity argument
[
8
]
that

\widetilde{\Delta}\mathbf{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\widetilde{\Delta}u_{i})\boldsymbol{e}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\widetilde{{\rm div}}(\widetilde{\nabla}u_{i})\boldsymbol{e}_{i}=-\widetilde{\nabla}\times\widetilde{\nabla}\times u+\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{{\rm div}}\mathbf{u}.

(4.60)

Here
u_{i}
denotes the
i

\mathbf{u}
and
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
denotes the unit vector
in the
i

\widetilde{\Delta_{c}^{*}}
defined by

\widetilde{\Delta_{c}^{*}}\mathbf{u}=\mu\widetilde{\Delta}\mathbf{u}+(\lambda_{c}+\mu)\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{u}.

For the complex stretched coordinates
\widetilde{x}=(\widetilde{x}_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2},\widetilde{x}_{3})^{\top}

and
\widetilde{y}=(\widetilde{y}_{1},\widetilde{y}_{2},\widetilde{y}_{3})^{\top}
, we define the complex distance

d(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})=\left[(\widetilde{x}_{1}-\widetilde{y}_{1})^{2}+(\widetilde{x}_{2}-\widetilde{y}_{2})^{2}+(\widetilde{x}_{3}-\widetilde{y}_{3})^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.61)

Given
\boldsymbol{f}=(\boldsymbol{f}_{s}^{\top},f_{4})^{\top}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}
, let the PML
extension of

(see (
3.35

\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})(y)=E(\boldsymbol{f})(\widetilde{y})
,
where the term

is replaced by
\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{y},x)
.
This extension is obviously a solution to the exterior Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\omega^{2}(\rho\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+\rho_{f}\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})+\widetilde{\Delta_{c}^{*}}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+\alpha M\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=0\quad{\rm in}\;\;B_{r_{0}}^{c},\\
\omega^{2}(\rho_{f}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+m\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})+{\bf{i}}\omega b\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}+\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot[\alpha M\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+M\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}]=0\quad{\rm in}\;\;B_{r_{0}}^{c},\\
(\mathbf{u}^{\top},\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\mathbf{w})^{\top}=(\boldsymbol{f}_{s}^{\top},f_{4})^{\top}\quad{\rm on}\;\;S_{r_{0}}.\end{cases}

(4.62)

From the definition of
\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})
and (
3.36
), we know that it has the
form
\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})=[\widetilde{E}_{1}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top},\widetilde{E}_{1}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top}]^{\top}
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{1}(\boldsymbol{f})(y)_{(k)}=

\displaystyle\int_{S_{r_{0}}}[\boldsymbol{D}(x-\widetilde{y})^{(k)}\cdot U(x)-\boldsymbol{S}(x-\widetilde{y})^{(k)}\cdot U^{\prime}(x)]ds(x),

(4.63)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{2}(\boldsymbol{f})(y)_{(k)}=

\displaystyle\int_{S_{r_{0}}}[\boldsymbol{D_{w}}(x-\widetilde{y})^{(k)}\cdot U(x)-\boldsymbol{S_{w}}(x-\widetilde{y})^{(k)}\cdot U^{\prime}(x)]ds(x).

All elements of the matrices
\boldsymbol{D}(x-\widetilde{y})
,
\boldsymbol{S}(x-\widetilde{y})
,
\boldsymbol{D_{w}}(x-\widetilde{y})
and
\boldsymbol{S_{w}}(x-\widetilde{y})
are of the form
\displaystyle\frac{e^{{\bf i}k\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{y},x)}}{\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{y},x)^{m}}
(

) multiplied by a
multivariate polynomial in the variables

\displaystyle\left\{\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{y},x),\widetilde{y}_{1},\widetilde{y}_{2},\widetilde{y}_{3},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\frac{x_{1}}{|x|},\frac{x_{2}}{|x|},\frac{x_{3}}{|x|}\right\}.

By imposing a certain homogeneous boundary condition on
S_{r_{2}}
, we obtain the following truncated PML problem

\begin{cases}\omega^{2}(\rho\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+\rho_{f}\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})+\widetilde{\Delta_{c}^{*}}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+\alpha M\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=\mathbf{Q}\quad{\rm in}\;\;\Omega_{2},\\
\omega^{2}(\rho_{f}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+m\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})+{\bf{i}}\omega b\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}+\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot[\alpha M\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+M\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}]=0\quad{\rm in}\;\;\Omega_{2},\\

(\mathbf{u}^{\top},\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\mathbf{w})^{\top}=0\quad{\rm on}\;\;S_{r_{2}}.\end{cases}

(4.64)

4.2
The PML equation in truncated domain and layer

In this subsection, we shall show that the PML system (
4.64
) in the truncated domain
has a unique weak solution in the space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{2})^{6}
under appropriate constraints
on the poroelastic and PML parameters. We begin with a key lemma concerning the PML Helmholtz decomposition.

Lemma 4.1

(PML Helmholtz decomposition)
.

Let
D=B_{r_{2}}\backslash\overline{\Omega}
or
D=B_{r_{2}}\backslash\overline{B_{r_{0}}}
be the truncated PML domain or the PML layer.
Define
V(D)=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in H^{1}(D)^{3}:\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}=0\;{\rm on}\;\partial D\}
. The following two decompositions hold:

(1)

For any
\boldsymbol{U}\in H_{0}^{1}(D)^{3}
, there exists
\theta_{1}\in H^{2}(D)\cap H_{0}^{1}(D)
and
\Phi_{1}\in H^{1}(D)^{3}

such that
\boldsymbol{U}=\widetilde{\nabla}\theta_{1}+\Phi_{1}
and
\widetilde{{\rm div}}\Phi_{1}=0
.

(2)

For any
\boldsymbol{W}\in V(D)
, there exists
\theta_{2}\in H^{2}(D)/\mathbb{C}
and
\Phi_{2}\in V(D)
such that

\boldsymbol{W}=\widetilde{\nabla}\theta_{2}+\Phi_{2}
,
\widetilde{{\rm div}}\Phi_{2}=0
and
\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\widetilde{\nabla}\theta_{2}=0
on
\partial D
.

Proof.

(1) For any fixed
\boldsymbol{U}\in H_{0}^{1}(D)^{3}
, consider the Dirichlet problem

\widetilde{\Delta}\theta=\widetilde{{\rm div}}\boldsymbol{U}\quad\text{in }D,\qquad\theta=0\quad\text{on }\partial D.

(4.1)

From the relations of the stretched operators (
4.58
)-(
4.60
),
we have the identity

\widetilde{\Delta}\theta=\frac{1}{s\beta^{2}}{\rm div}\bigl(P_{2}P_{1}^{-1}\nabla\theta\bigr).

(4.2)

This leads to the weak formulation of (
4.1
): to find
\theta\in H_{0}^{1}(D)
such that

a(\theta,\psi):=\int_{D}P_{2}P_{1}^{-1}\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\overline{\psi}\,dx=-\int_{D}s\beta^{2}\,(\widetilde{{\rm div}}\boldsymbol{U})\,\overline{\psi}\,dx\quad\forall\psi\;\in H_{0}^{1}(D).

It follows from the definitions of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
(see (
4.59
)) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}a(\psi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\int_{D}P_{2}P_{1}^{-1}\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\overline{\psi}dx

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\int_{D}\operatorname{diag}\{\beta^{2}s^{-1},s,s\}\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\overline{\psi}dx

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\int_{D}\left[\beta^{2}s^{-1}|(\nabla\psi)_{r}|^{2}+s|(\nabla\psi)_{\theta}|^{2}+s|(\nabla\psi)_{\phi}|^{2}\right]dx.

From the definition of
\beta
in (
4.53
) and the fact that
\sigma_{m}\leq\sigma
for
r\geq r_{0}
, we obtain

\operatorname{Re}(\beta^{2}s^{-1})=\operatorname{Re}\frac{(1+{\bf i}\sigma_{m})^{2}}{1+{\bf i}\sigma}=\frac{1-\sigma_{m}^{2}+2\sigma\sigma_{m}}{1+\sigma^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{1+\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{2}})}^{2}}.

(4.4)

This, combined with (
4.3
) yields

\operatorname{Re}a(\theta,\theta)\geq\frac{\lVert\nabla\theta\rVert_{{L^{2}(D)^{3}}}^{2}}{1+\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{2}})}^{2}}.

(4.5)

By the Poincaré inequality and elliptic regularity theory, problem (
4.1
)
admits a unique solution
\theta_{1}\in H^{2}(D)\cap H_{0}^{1}(D)
. Setting
\Phi_{1}=\boldsymbol{U}-\widetilde{\nabla}\theta_{1}
gives the desired decomposition.

(2) For any fixed
\boldsymbol{W}\in V(D)
, consider the Neumann problem

\widetilde{\Delta}\theta=\widetilde{{\rm div}}\boldsymbol{W}\quad\text{in }D,\qquad\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\widetilde{\nabla}\theta=0\quad\text{on }\partial D.

(4.6)

The compatibility conditions are satisfied since

\displaystyle\int_{D}{\rm div}(P_{2}P_{1}^{-1}\nabla\theta)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot P_{2}P_{1}^{-1}\nabla\theta ds(x)=\int_{\partial D}\frac{\beta^{2}}{s}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla\theta ds(x)=0

\displaystyle\int_{D}{\rm div}(P_{2}\boldsymbol{W})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot P_{2}\boldsymbol{W}ds(x)=\int_{\partial D}\beta^{2}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{W}ds(x)=0.

Proceeding as in the first part, the Lax-Milgram theorem and elliptic regularity theory implies the
existence of the unique solution

\theta_{2}\in H^{2}(D)/{\mathbb{C}}
to the problem (
4.6
). Defining
\Phi_{2}=\boldsymbol{W}-\widetilde{\nabla}\theta_{2}

completes the proof.
∎

For
D=B_{r_{2}}\backslash\overline{\Omega}
or
D=B_{r_{2}}\backslash\overline{B_{r_{0}}}
, we introduce the sesquilinear form similar to

\mathcal{B}_{\Omega_{0}}
in (
3.51
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{D}(\Phi,\Psi)=

\displaystyle\int_{D}\Bigg\{\mu P_{2}P_{1}^{-1}\nabla\Phi^{s}:\nabla\overline{\Psi^{s}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}s}\Big[(\lambda_{c}+\mu)(\nabla\cdot P_{2}\Phi^{s})(\nabla\cdot P_{2}\overline{\Psi^{s}})

(4.7)

\displaystyle+\alpha M(\nabla\cdot P_{2}\Phi^{f})(\nabla\cdot P_{2}\overline{\Psi^{s}})+\alpha M(\nabla\cdot P_{2}\Phi^{s})(\nabla\cdot P_{2}\overline{\Psi^{f}})+M(\nabla\cdot P_{2}\Phi^{f})(\nabla\cdot P_{2}\overline{\Psi^{f}})\Big]

\displaystyle-\omega^{2}\beta^{2}s(\rho\Phi^{s}\cdot\overline{\Psi^{s}}+\rho_{f}\Phi^{f}\cdot\overline{\Psi^{s}}+\rho_{f}\Phi^{s}\cdot\overline{\Psi^{f}}+m\Phi^{f}\cdot\overline{\Psi^{f}})-{\bf i}\omega b\beta^{2}s\Phi^{f}\cdot\overline{\Psi^{f}}\Bigg\}dx

where
\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}\Phi^{s}\\
\Phi^{f}\end{bmatrix}
,
\Psi=\begin{bmatrix}\Psi^{s}\\
\Psi^{f}\end{bmatrix}
.
Now we are in the position to present the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.2
.

For given
\boldsymbol{P}=(\boldsymbol{U}^{\top},\boldsymbol{V}^{\top})^{\top}\in H_{0}^{1}(D)^{3}\times V(D)
, the variational problem

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{D}(\Phi,\Psi)=(\beta^{2}s\boldsymbol{P},\Psi)_{D},\;\forall\;\Psi\in H_{0}^{1}(D)^{3}\times V(D)

(4.8)

has a unique weak solution
\Phi\in H_{0}^{1}(D)^{3}\times V(D)
under the smallness assumption on
\omega

and
\alpha
, where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{D}
denotes the inner product of
L^{2}(D)
.

Proof.

(\vec{A}-\vec{C})\cdot(\vec{A}-\vec{B})=(r-r_{0})(r-r_{0}\boldsymbol{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{r}^{0}),

hence

\sum_{j=1}^{3}a_{j}b_{j}=r\sigma_{m}(r)\,\frac{(\vec{A}-\vec{C})\cdot(\vec{A}-\vec{B})}{r-r_{0}}=r\sigma_{m}(r)(r-r_{0}\boldsymbol{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{r}^{0}).

Since
\boldsymbol{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{r}^{0}\leq 1
, we have
r-r_{0}\boldsymbol{e}_{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{r}^{0}\geq r-r_{0}

and therefore

\sum_{j=1}^{3}a_{j}b_{j}\geq r\sigma_{m}(r)(r-r_{0}).

This, combined with Lemma 6.1 yields

\operatorname{Im}\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{y},x)\geq\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{3}a_{j}b_{j}}{\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}}}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{3}a_{j}b_{j}}{|\vec{A}-\vec{C}|}\geq\frac{r\sigma_{m}(r)(r-r_{0})}{r_{0}+r}.

(4.36)

Moreover, an estimate for the modulus of
\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{y},x)
follows the triangle inequality

|\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{y},x)|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{3}(a_{j}^{2}+b_{j}^{2})\leq|\vec{A}-\vec{C}|^{2}+[r\sigma_{m}(r)]^{2}\leq(r+r_{0})^{2}+r^{2}\sigma_{0}^{2}.

Under the assumptions for parameters of the
\mathbf{u}-p
system in Theorem
3.4
,
we have
\operatorname{Re}k_{j},\operatorname{Im}k_{j}>0
for
j=1,2,3
. This, combined the estimate (
4.36
) yields

\displaystyle\lvert e^{{\bf i}k_{j}\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{y},x)}\rvert\leq\lvert e^{-\operatorname{Re}k_{j}\operatorname{Im}\widetilde{d}(\widetilde{y},x)}\rvert\leq e^{-k_{m}\frac{r\sigma_{m}(r)(r-r_{0})}{r_{0}+r}},\quad x\in S_{r_{0}},y\in S_{r},

where
k_{m}=\min\{\operatorname{Re}k_{1},\operatorname{Re}k_{2},\operatorname{Re}k_{3}\}
(see (
3.39
) for the definitions of
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
).
From the definition of PML extensions
\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})
(see (
4.63
)), one then obtains
the pointwise estimate

\displaystyle|\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})|,|\nabla\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})|\leq C\frac{\widetilde{F}(r_{0},r,\sigma_{0})}{\left[\frac{r\sigma_{m}(r)(r-r_{0})}{r_{0}+r}\right]^{m_{0}}}e^{-k_{m}\frac{r\sigma_{m}(r)(r-r_{0})}{r_{0}+r}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}},

(4.37)

where
m_{0}
is a positive integer and

\widetilde{F}(r_{0},r,\sigma_{0})
is defined by a polynomial
\widetilde{P}

\widetilde{F}(r_{0},r,\sigma_{0})=\widetilde{P}\left(\sqrt{(r+r_{0})^{2}+r^{2}\sigma_{0}^{2}},r\sqrt{1+\sigma_{0}^{2}},r_{0},\sqrt{1+[\sigma_{0}+r\sigma_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)]}\right).

The following lemma on the decay property of the PML extension
\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})
will play an
important role in the convergence analysis of the PML method.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\boldsymbol{f}\in H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}
, under the condition (
3.40
) and for
sufficiently small
\omega
, we have the following
estimate for the PML extension
\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})=(\widetilde{E}_{1}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top},\widetilde{E}_{2}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top})^{\top}

\|\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{2}})^{6}}\leq C\frac{F(r_{0},r,\sigma_{0},d)}{(\sigma_{0}d)^{m}}e^{-\frac{k_{m}\sigma_{0}d}{8}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}},

(4.38)

where
m
is a positive integer and
F(r_{0},\sigma_{0},d)
is defined by a polynomial
P

F(r_{0},\sigma_{0},d)=P\left(\sqrt{(2r_{0}+d)^{2}+(r_{0}+d)^{2}\sigma_{0}^{2}},\sqrt{1+\sigma_{0}^{2}},r_{0},d,\sqrt{1+[\sigma_{0}+(r_{0}+d)\max\limits_{r\in B_{r_{2}}}\sigma_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)]}\right).

Proof.

Choose a smooth cut-off function
\chi
satisfying
0\leq\chi(x)\leq 1
,
\chi(x)=0

for
\lvert x\rvert<r_{0}+d/2
and
\chi(x)=1
for
\lvert x\rvert>r_{1}-d/4
.
For example, an explicit construction is

\chi(r)=\begin{cases}0,&\displaystyle r\leq r_{0}+\frac{d}{2},\\
\dfrac{h\Bigl(\frac{r-(r_{0}+d/2)}{d/4}\Bigr)}{h\Bigl(\frac{r-(r_{0}+d/2)}{d/4}\Bigr)+k\,h\Bigl(\frac{(r_{1}-d/4)-r}{d/4}\Bigr)},&\displaystyle r_{0}+\frac{d}{2}<r<r_{1}-\frac{d}{4},\\
1,&r\geq\displaystyle r_{1}-\frac{d}{4},\end{cases}

with
h(\tau)=e^{-1/\tau}
for
\tau>0
and
h(\tau)=0
for
\tau\leq 0
, and
k>0
such that

|\chi^{\prime}(r)|\leq C/d
. Consequently,

\Bigl|\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nabla\chi(x)\Bigr|=|\chi^{\prime}(r)|\leq\frac{C}{r}\quad\text{for }r_{0}+d/2<r=|x|<r_{0}+d.

This property guarantees that all terms involving
\nabla\chi
remain polynomially bounded.
By trace theorem,

\|\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{2}})^{6}}\leq C\|\chi\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{2})^{6}}=C\|\chi\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega_{2}\backslash\overline{B_{r_{0}+d/2}}\right)^{6}}.

(4.39)

For a generic component
v
of
\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})
in
D=\Omega_{2}\backslash\overline{B_{r_{0}+d/2}}
we have

\|\chi v\|_{H^{1}(D)}^{2}=\|\chi v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}+\|\nabla(\chi v)\|_{L^{2}(D)^{3}}^{2}\leq\|\chi v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}+2\bigl(\|v\nabla\chi\|_{L^{2}(D)^{3}}^{2}+\|\chi\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)^{3}}^{2}\bigr).

(4.40)

The rightmost term of the estimate (
4.40
) can be expressed explicitly in Spherical coordinate

\displaystyle\|v\nabla\chi\|_{L^{2}(D)^{3}}^{2}+\|\chi\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}+\frac{d}{2}}^{r_{0}+d}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Bigg[\Big|v\chi^{\prime}(r)\Big|^{2}+\Bigg(\Big\lvert\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}\chi\Big\rvert^{2}+\Big\lvert\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\theta}\chi\Big\rvert^{2}+\Big\lvert\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\phi}\chi\Big\rvert^{2}\Bigg)\Bigg]r^{2}\sin\theta d\phi d\theta dr.

For
r\in(r_{0}+d/2,r_{0}+d)
, from the definition of
\sigma_{m}
(see (
4.57
)), we know

\sigma_{m}
is increasing and
\sigma_{m}(r_{0}+d/2)\geq\sigma_{0}/2
. Hence

\frac{r\sigma_{m}(r)(r-r_{0})}{r_{0}+r}\geq\frac{(r_{0}+d/2)(\sigma_{0}/2)(d/2)}{2r_{0}+d}=\frac{\sigma_{0}d}{8}.

This, combined with (
4.37
) yields

|v(x)|,\;|\nabla v(x)|\leq C\frac{\widetilde{F}(r_{0},r,\sigma_{0})}{\left[\frac{r\sigma_{m}(r)(r-r_{0})}{r_{0}+r}\right]^{m_{0}}}e^{-\frac{k_{m}\sigma_{0}d}{8}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}},

(4.41)

where
\widetilde{F}(r_{0},r,\sigma_{0})
denotes a polynomial with positive coefficients.
Combining (
4.39
)-(
4.41
) yields the desired estimate (
4.38
).
The proof is thus complete.
∎

Given data
\boldsymbol{f}=(\boldsymbol{f}_{s}^{\top},f_{4})^{\top}\in H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}
with

\boldsymbol{f}_{s}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})^{\top}\in H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{3}
, consider the PML layer system

\begin{cases}\omega^{2}(\rho\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+\rho_{f}\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})+\widetilde{\Delta_{c}^{*}}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+\alpha M\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=0\quad{\rm in}\;\;\Omega_{{\rm PML}},\\
\omega^{2}(\rho_{f}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+m\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})+{\bf{i}}\omega b\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}+\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot[\alpha M\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+M\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}]=0\quad{\rm in}\;\;\Omega_{{\rm PML}},\\
(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})^{\top}=(\boldsymbol{f}_{s}^{\top},f_{4})^{\top}\quad{\rm on}\;\;S_{r_{0}},\\
(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})^{\top}=0\quad{\rm on}\;\;S_{r_{2}}.\end{cases}

(4.42)

The well-posedness of the system (
4.42
) can be similarly obtained
following Theorem
4.3
.
We can define the associated Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator

\hat{\mathcal{N}}:H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}\to H^{-1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}
:

\hat{\mathcal{N}}\boldsymbol{f}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{T}_{c}&\alpha M\boldsymbol{n}\nabla^{\top}\\
\alpha M\nabla^{\top}&M\nabla^{\top}\end{bmatrix}(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top}.

Based on Lemma
4.5
, we have the error estimate between DtN operators

\mathcal{N}
and
\hat{\mathcal{N}}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Given
\boldsymbol{f}=(\boldsymbol{f}_{s}^{\top},f_{4})^{\top}\in H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}
with
\boldsymbol{f}_{s}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})^{\top}
, we have

\|(\mathcal{N}-\hat{\mathcal{N}})\boldsymbol{f}\|_{{H^{-1/2}(S_{r_{0}})}^{4}}\leq C\|\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})\|_{{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{2}})}^{6}}.

(4.43)

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})=(\widetilde{E}_{1}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top},\widetilde{E}_{2}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top})^{\top}

be the PML extension potential, and
\widetilde{V}
be the solution of the system
(
4.42
).
Then

(\mathcal{N}-\hat{\mathcal{N}})\boldsymbol{f}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{T}_{c}&\alpha M\boldsymbol{n}\nabla^{\top}\\
\alpha M\nabla^{\top}&M\nabla^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{W}|_{S_{r_{0}}},

where
\widetilde{W}=\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})-\widetilde{V}
satisfies the system

\begin{cases}\omega^{2}(\rho\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+\rho_{f}\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})+\widetilde{\Delta_{c}^{*}}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+\alpha M\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=0\quad{\rm in}\;\;\Omega_{{\rm PML}},\\
\omega^{2}(\rho_{f}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+m\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})+{\bf{i}}\omega b\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}+\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot[\alpha M\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+M\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}]=0\quad{\rm in}\;\;\Omega_{{\rm PML}},\\
(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})^{\top}=0\quad{\rm on}\;\;S_{r_{0}},\\
(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})^{\top}=(\widetilde{E}_{1}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top},{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\widetilde{E}_{2}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top})^{\top}\quad{\rm on}\;\;S_{r_{2}}.\end{cases}

(4.44)

By Theorem
4.3
, the following stability estimate

\|\widetilde{W}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{{\rm PML}})^{6}}\leq C(d)\|(\widetilde{E}_{1}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top},{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\widetilde{E}_{2}(\boldsymbol{f})^{\top})^{\top}\|_{{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{2}})}^{4}}\leq C(d)\|\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})\|_{{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{2}})}^{6}}

(4.45)

therefore holds, where the constant
C(d)
depends on the thickness
d
and grows at most polynomially in
d
.

Let
\Psi\in H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{3}
be extended to a function in
H^{1}(\Omega_{{\rm PML}})^{3}

(still denoted by
\Psi
) such that
\Psi=0
on
S_{r_{2}}
and
\|\Psi\|_{{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})}^{3}}\geq\|\Psi\|_{{H^{1}(\Omega_{{\rm PML}})}^{3}}
. Let
g\in H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})
be extended to a function

\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{{\rm PML}})^{3}
such that
\Phi=0
on the
S_{r_{2}}
,
{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\Phi=g
on the

S_{r_{0}}
and
C\|g\|_{{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})}}\geq\|\Phi\|_{{H^{1}(\Omega_{{\rm PML}})}^{3}}
.
Then it follows from the integration by parts and (
4.45
) that

\displaystyle\lvert\langle(\hat{\mathcal{N}}-\mathcal{N})\boldsymbol{f},(\Psi^{\top},g)^{\top}\rangle_{S_{r_{0}}}\rvert=

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{B}_{{\rm PML}}(\widetilde{W},(\Psi^{\top},\Phi^{\top})^{\top})\rvert

(4.46)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\widetilde{W}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{{\rm PML}})^{6}}\|(\Psi^{\top},\Phi^{\top})^{\top}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{{\rm PML}})^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(d)\|\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})\|_{{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{2}})}^{6}}\|(\Psi^{\top},g)^{\top}\|_{{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})}^{4}},

where the sesquilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{{\rm PML}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined in (
4.7
)
for
D=\Omega_{{\rm PML}}
. The it follows from the definition of
H^{-1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}
that

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{N}-\hat{\mathcal{N}})\boldsymbol{f}\|_{{H^{-1/2}(S_{r_{0}})}^{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{(\Psi^{\top},g)^{\top}\in H^{1/2}(\partial B_{0})^{4}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\lvert\langle(\mathcal{N}-\hat{\mathcal{N}})\boldsymbol{f},(\Psi^{\top},g)^{\top}\rangle_{S_{r_{0}}}\rvert}{\|(\Psi^{\top},g)^{\top}\|_{{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})}^{4}}}\leq C(d)\|\widetilde{E}(\boldsymbol{f})\|_{{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{2}})}^{6}}.

This, combined with Lemma
4.5
arrive at the desired estimate (
4.43
).
∎

Now we can establish the main result on the exponential convergence of the PML method.

Theorem 4.7

(Convergence Theorem)
.

For given

{\bf Q}\in H^{1}(\Omega^{c})^{3}
with compact support inside
B_{r_{0}}
, let
(\mathbf{u}^{\top},\mathbf{w}^{\top})^{\top}\in V_{\partial\Omega}(\Omega_{0})
and
(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{2})^{3}\times V(\Omega_{2})
be the solutions of the
problem (
3.38a
)-(
3.38d
) and (
4.64
), respectively.
Then under the condition (
3.40
) and the smallness assumption on
\alpha

and
\omega
, the following error estimate holds:

\|(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top}-(\mathbf{u}^{\top},\mathbf{w}^{\top})^{\top}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})^{6}}\leq C\frac{F(r_{0},\sigma_{0},d)}{(\sigma_{0}d)^{m}}e^{-\frac{k_{m}\sigma_{0}d}{8}}\|(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})^{\top}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}},

(4.47)

where
m
is a positive integer and
F(r_{0},\sigma_{0},d)
is defined by a polynomial
P

F(r_{0},\sigma_{0},d)=P\left(\sqrt{(2r_{0}+d)^{2}+(r_{0}+d)^{2}\sigma_{0}^{2}},\sqrt{1+\sigma_{0}^{2}},r_{0},d,\sqrt{1+[\sigma_{0}+(r_{0}+d)\max\limits_{r\in B_{r_{2}}}\sigma_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)]}\right).

Proof.

Noting that
{\bf Q}\in H^{1}(\Omega^{c})^{3}
is compactly supported inside
B_{r_{0}}
, then

(\mathbf{u}^{\top},\mathbf{w}^{\top})^{\top}
and

(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top}
satisfy the following variational problems

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}((\mathbf{u}^{\top},\mathbf{w}^{\top})^{\top},\Psi)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{0}}{\bf Q}\cdot\overline{\Psi}dx,\quad\forall\;\Psi\in V_{\partial\Omega}(\Omega_{0}),

(4.48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\Omega_{2}}((\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top},\Psi)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{0}}{\bf Q}\cdot\overline{\Psi}dx,\quad\forall\;\Psi=(\Psi^{s},\Psi^{f})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{2})^{3}\times V(\Omega_{2}).

(4.49)

Simple decomposition of
\mathcal{B}_{\Omega_{2}}
and integration by parts imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\Omega_{2}}((\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top},\Psi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}((\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top},\Psi)+\langle\mathcal{N}\widetilde{U},\Psi_{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{S_{r_{0}}}+\mathcal{B}_{\Omega_{{\rm PML}}}((\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top},\Psi)

(4.50)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}((\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top},\Psi)+\langle\mathcal{N}\widetilde{U},\Psi_{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{S_{r_{0}}}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{N}}\widetilde{U},\Psi_{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{S_{r_{0}}},

where

\widetilde{U}=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}\\
\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\end{bmatrix}\in H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4},\quad\Psi_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\begin{bmatrix}\Psi^{s}\\
\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\Psi^{f}\end{bmatrix}\in H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}.

Subtracting the equations (
4.48
) and (
4.49
) and using (
4.50
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}((\mathbf{u}^{\top},\mathbf{w}^{\top})^{\top}-(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top},\Psi)=\langle(\mathcal{N}-\hat{\mathcal{N}})\widetilde{U},\Psi_{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{S_{r_{0}}}

(4.51)

This, combined with the trace theorem gives

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{B}((\mathbf{u}^{\top},\mathbf{w}^{\top})^{\top}-(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top},\Psi)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\mathcal{N}-\hat{\mathcal{N}})\widetilde{U}\|_{H^{-1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}}\|\Psi_{\boldsymbol{n}}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}}.

(4.52)

which obviously implies

\frac{\lvert\mathcal{B}((\mathbf{u}^{\top},\mathbf{w}^{\top})^{\top}-(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}^{\top})^{\top},\Psi)\rvert}{\|\Psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})^{6}}}\leq C\|(\mathcal{N}-\hat{\mathcal{N}})\widetilde{U}\|_{H^{-1/2}(S_{r_{0}})^{4}}.

(4.53)

The desired error estimate (
4.47
) follows from Lemma
4.43

and the inf-sup condition (
3.52
) for the sesquilinear form
\mathcal{B}
.
∎

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied three-dimensional time-harmonic poroelastic scattering problems.
We first introduced an equivalent
\mathbf{u}-p
system with fewer degrees of freedom, derived
the associated fundamental solution and Green’s identity, and proved that under reasonable parameter
constraints the real and imaginary parts of all three wave numbers are positive. Under certain assumptions,
we proved the well-posedness of the PML problems in the truncated domain and in the layer.
Moreover, the exponential convergence of the spherical PML method was established in terms of the
layer thickness and the absorbing parameters. The proof relies on an error estimate between the
Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DtN) operators of the original scattering problem and the truncated PML problem,
which in turn follows from the exponential decay of the PML extensions of the stretched fundamental solution.

Our approach can be extended to other poroelastic scattering models and to more general boundary conditions
on the truncated PML boundary, such as the mixed boundary conditions. It would also be of interest to study
the uniaxial PML method for time-harmonic poroelastic scattering problems. We hope to report such results in the future.
