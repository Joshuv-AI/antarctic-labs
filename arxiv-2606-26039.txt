In-medium QCD splittings beyond the soft, large- N c N_{c} and harmonic-oscillator approximations all at once
Marco Leitão, José Guilherme Milhano, Alba Soto-Ontoso

Abstract
Nearly thirty years ago, Baier, Dokshitzer, Mueller, Peigné, Schiff, and Zakharov (BDMPS-Z) introduced a formalism to calculate the fully differential probability for a high-energy quark or gluon to radiate inside a finite-volume QCD plasma. We report on the first complete numerical solution of the BDMPS-Z equations for in-medium QCD splittings. Our numerical routines are precise across phase-space, enabling a determination of the in-medium splitting functions that is significantly beyond the state-of-the-art, including finite-energy effects, subleading-color contributions, and a realistic model for parton-medium interactions. We quantify the uncertainties associated with standard approximations in the literature, revealing substantial deviations across phase-space. This work opens a path toward more precise calculations of jet observables and for powerful new constraints of medium parameters from high-energy heavy-ion collider data.

Explicit numerical schemes, such as Runge-Kutta (RK) methods, approximate this exponential operator via a truncated polynomial expansion in powers of
\delta\tilde{t}

Consequently, the accuracy of such methods is strictly tied to the step size, requiring a stringent stability condition to prevent exponential error propagation. For the equations at hand, this condition is neither available in closed form nor easily guaranteed, rendering explicit methods difficult to control over long propagation times, as previously noted in Ref.
57
.

To overcome these limitations, we opt for a spectral method based on a Faber polynomial expansion of the evolution operator, building on techniques from electrodynamics
32
and condensed matter physics
45

\mathcal{H}_{\rm{sc}}\equiv\mathcal{\hat{H}}/\lambda
, ensuring its spectrum lies within a complex ellipse of unit logarithmic capacity. The Faber expansion takes the form

e^{-i\mathcal{H}\delta\tilde{t}}\bm{f}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}(\delta\tau)\,\Phi_{n}(\mathcal{H}_{\rm sc})\bm{f},

(11)

where
\delta\tau\equiv\lambda\delta\tilde{t}
.
Both the scaling factor
\lambda
and the expansion coefficients
c_{n}
are determined using the spectral bounds of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
, which can be inferred either from the structural properties of the operator or via numerical diagonalization techniques. The action
\Phi_{n}(\mathcal{H}_{\rm sc})\bm{f}
is evaluated using the recurrence relation of Faber polynomials tailored to the bounding ellipse. This series converges geometrically, with the truncation error controlled by the magnitude of the coefficients: truncating at the smallest

This approach decouples the expansion accuracy from the step size and, crucially, removes the step-size stability constraint, thereby enabling a robust and stable solution for
\cal{A}
and
\cal{B}

Medium description

A central ingredient in computing the splitting spectrum is the description of the QCD medium through which the splitting parton and its offsprings propagate. Following the standard assumptions of the BDMPS-Z formalism, we adopt a semi-classical treatment of the medium. Specifically, interactions between the hard partons and the medium are modeled via a background color field,
A
, acting over the light-cone time interval
[0,L]
. When computing the squared matrix element, we average over all possible field configurations assuming Gaussian statistics and locality in both color and light-cone time. Consequently, the only non-trivial averaging over
A
reduces to the two-point correlator:

\langle A^{-,a}(\bm{r},t)A^{-,b}(\bm{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=n(t)\delta^{ab}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\gamma(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}),

(13)

where
n(t)
represents the density of scattering centers and
\gamma(\bm{x})
is defined as

\gamma(\bm{x})=\int_{\bm{q}}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{x}}\,\dfrac{d\sigma}{d^{2}\bm{q}}.

(14)

Here,
d\sigma/d^{2}\bm{q}
denotes the elastic
2\to 2
differential cross-section for a single scattering between the hard probe and the medium. By introducing the dipole cross-section
\sigma(\bm{x})=g^{2}(\gamma(0)-\gamma(\bm{x}))
, where
g^{2}\equiv 4\pi\alpha_{s}
, we can readily establish the connection to Eq. (
In-medium QCD splittings beyond the soft, large-
N_{c}
and harmonic-oscillator approximations all at once
) via the process-dependent scattering potential
\tilde{v}_{ba}
. In transverse-coordinate representation, this potential reads

v_{ba}(\bm{x})=n(t)\left[\dfrac{c_{cba}}{2}\sigma(\bm{x})+\dfrac{c_{acb}}{2}\sigma(z\bm{x})+\dfrac{c_{bac}}{2}\sigma((1-z)\bm{x})\right].

(15)

In the preceding expression, we have introduced the color factors
c_{ijk}=C_{i}+C_{j}-C_{k}
, where
C_{i}
denotes the Casimir of the color representation for particle
i
, which splits into the

pair. The results shown in the main text assumed the density of scattering centers to be constant,
n(t)=n
. However, our numerical routines can be adapted to time-dependent densities by considering a Magnus expansion of the time-ordered evolution operator in Eq. (

), which is systematically improvable and does not affect stability. We leave the question of expanding media for future work.

To complete the description of the medium, all that remains to be specified is the elastic scattering cross-section. In this work, we consider the two most widely used models in the literature, although our numerical routines are flexible enough to accommodate any arbitrary potential, such as those discussed in Ref.
68
. First, the Gyulassy-Wang (GW) model
73
describes the medium as a collection of static, screened scattering centers, yielding

\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{GW}}}{d^{2}\bm{q}}=\dfrac{g^{4}}{|\bm{q}^{2}+\mu^{2}|^{2}}

where
\mu
serves as an infrared regulator. Alternatively, assuming the medium is in thermal equilibrium, the cross-section can be computed using Hard Thermal Loop (HTL) perturbation theory. This approach yields
16

\dfrac{d\sigma_{\mathrm{HTL}}}{d^{2}\bm{q}}=\dfrac{g^{2}\mu_{D}^{2}T}{\bm{q}^{2}(\bm{q}^{2}+\mu^{2})},

(17)

where
\mu_{D}^{2}=(1+N_{f}/6)g^{2}T^{2}
is the squared Debye mass. Both models can be recast into a unified form at leading power, given by

n\sigma(\bm{x})=\dfrac{1}{4}\tilde{q}\,\bm{x}^{2}\ln\dfrac{1}{\mu_{\star}\bm{x}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\mu_{\star}^{2}\bm{x}^{4}),

(18)

where
\mu_{\star}
corresponds to an effective screening mass. The explicit expression for
\mu_{\star}
in terms of the parameters of each scattering model was derived in Ref.
27
. The second parameter entering Eq. (
18
) is
\tilde{q}
, which is defined as

\tilde{q}=ng^{2}\int^{Q}\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}q^{2}\frac{d\sigma}{d^{2}\bm{q}}\,,

(19)

where
Q
denotes an ultraviolet cutoff and
\tilde{q}\equiv\hat{q}_{R}/C_{R}
, with
\hat{q}_{R}
being the standard jet quenching parameter for a soft particle in color representation
R
. Finally, a standard approach in the literature, known as harmonic oscillator approximation, consists of neglecting the logarithm in Eq. (
18
), which reduces most subsequent integrals to a solvable Gaussian form.

.2
Explicit formulae for color-mixing matrices

In this section, we provide explicit expressions for the color matrix
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{\sigma}
that enters the evolution equation, Eq. (
8
), of the main text. For completeness, we also present the
\gamma\to q\bar{q}
case, which was omitted in the main text. These results are derived within the framework established in Refs.
56
and
57
.

The starting point is the definition of the splitting kernel
{\cal K}(t_{2},t_{1})
and quadrupole
{\cal Q}(t_{\infty},t_{2})
entering Eq. (
In-medium QCD splittings beyond the soft, large-
N_{c}
and harmonic-oscillator approximations all at once
) in terms of path-integrals over traces of Wilson lines, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\bm{u}_{2},\bm{u}_{1}|t_{2},t_{1})=\int_{\bm{u}_{1}}^{\bm{u}_{2}}\mathcal{D}\bm{u}\,e^{i\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}ds\frac{\omega}{2}\dot{\bm{u}}^{2}}\mathbb{C}^{(3)}(\bm{u})\,,

(20a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\bm{u},\bar{\bm{u}},\bm{u}_{2},\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}|t_{\infty},t_{2})=\int_{\bm{u}_{2}}^{\bm{u}}\mathcal{D}\bm{u}^{\prime}\int_{\bm{\bar{u}}_{2}}^{\bm{\bar{u}}}\mathcal{D}\bm{\bar{u}}^{\prime}\,e^{i\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{\infty}}ds\frac{\omega}{2}(\dot{\bm{u}}^{\prime 2}-\dot{\bm{\bar{u}}}^{\prime 2})}\mathbb{C}^{(4)}(\bm{u}^{\prime},\bar{\bm{u}}^{\prime})\,.

(20b)

While the main text is formulated in momentum space, here we switch to position space to facilitate comparisons with existing literature. The relations expressing the
(\bm{u},\bar{\bm{u}})
-coordinates in terms of the parton positions are provided in App. B and A of Refs.
31
;
57
, respectively.

The correlators
\mathbb{C}^{(n)}
are obtained by averaging traces of
n
Wilson lines over the medium background field configurations. For
n\geq 3
,
\mathbb{C}^{(n)}
depends on the specific splitting process under consideration. For the three-point function (
n=3
), the correlator reads

\mathbb{C}^{(3)}(t_{2},t_{1}|\bm{u})=\exp\left[-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}ds\,v_{ba}(\bm{u}(s))\right],

(21)

where
v_{ba}(\bm{u})
is defined in Eq. (
15
). For a generic dipole potential
v_{ba}
, the path-integral in Eq. (
20a
) cannot be evaluated analytically. Consequently, it is more convenient to directly solve the evolution equation presented in the main text, see Eq. (
In-medium QCD splittings beyond the soft, large-
N_{c}
and harmonic-oscillator approximations all at once
). A notable exception is the harmonic approximation of the scattering potential discussed in the previous section, since then Eq. (
20a
) reduces to Gaussian integrals and can be solved analytically.

For
n=4
, we define the process-dependent correlators
\mathbb{C}^{(4)}
in terms of traces of fundamental Wilson lines as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{\gamma\to q\bar{q}}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\langle\mathrm{Tr}[V_{1}V_{2}^{\dagger}V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger}]\rangle,

(22a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{g\to q\bar{q}}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}-1}\langle\mathrm{Tr}[V_{\bar{1}}V_{1}^{\dagger}]\,\mathrm{Tr}[V_{2}V_{\bar{2}}^{\dagger}]-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}[V_{1}V_{2}^{\dagger}V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger}]\rangle,

(22b)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{q\to gq}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}-1}\langle\mathrm{Tr}[V_{1}V_{2}^{\dagger}V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger}]\,\mathrm{Tr}[V_{2}V_{\bar{2}}^{\dagger}]-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}[V_{1}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger}]\rangle,

(22c)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{g\to gg}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\langle\mathrm{Tr}[V_{\bar{1}}V_{1}^{\dagger}]\,\mathrm{Tr}[V_{2}V_{\bar{2}}^{\dagger}]\,\mathrm{Tr}[V_{1}V_{2}^{\dagger}V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger}]-\mathrm{Tr}[V_{\bar{1}}V_{2}^{\dagger}V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger}V_{1}V_{\bar{2}}^{\dagger}V_{2}V_{1}^{\dagger}]\rangle,

(22d)

where
(V_{i},V_{\bar{i}})\equiv(V(\bm{r}_{i}),V(\bar{\bm{r}}_{i}))
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Here,
i=1
denotes the parton carrying an energy fraction
z
, and the bar notation
\bar{\bm{r}}
indicates positions within the complex conjugate amplitude. Evaluating the path integrals in Eq. (
20b
) with these correlators is highly non-trivial. Alternatively, one can derive a closed system of differential equations governing the evolution of each correlator. In the following, we briefly outline this approach.

Let us consider pairs of fundamental Wilson lines denoted by
W_{a_{i},b_{i}}\equiv V_{a_{i}}V_{b_{i}}^{\dagger}
. In general, a
2n
-point function can be expressed in the form

C_{p}=\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{n}}^{p[j_{1}j_{2}\dots j_{n}]}(W_{a_{1},b_{1}})_{i_{1}j_{1}}(W_{a_{2},b_{2}})_{i_{2}j_{2}}\dots(W_{a_{n},b_{n}})_{i_{n}j_{n}},

(23)

where
\delta_{\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{n}}^{\beta_{1}\dots\beta_{n}}=\delta_{\alpha_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}\dots\delta_{\alpha_{n}}^{\beta_{n}}
and
p[j_{1}j_{2}\dots j_{n}]=j_{p_{1}}\dots j_{p_{n}}
represents a permutation of the index sequence
[j_{1}j_{2}\dots j_{n}]
. We label each correlator by its associated permutation, writing
C_{(p_{1}\dots p_{n})}\equiv C_{p}
. As shown in
56
, correlators of this class satisfy

\frac{d}{dt}C_{p}=-\frac{1}{2}n(t)\left[\bm{M}_{\mathrm{diag},p}\,C_{p}+\sum_{\pi=p\,\mathrm{+\,one\,swap}}(\bm{M}_{\mathrm{non-diag}})_{\pi}^{p}\,C_{\pi}\right],

(24)

where the diagonal matrix elements are given by

\bm{M}_{\mathrm{diag},(p_{1}\dots p_{n})}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[N_{c}\sigma_{b_{i}a_{p_{i}}}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sigma_{a_{i}b_{i}}\right]+\dfrac{1}{N_{c}}\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j>i}^{n}(\sigma_{a_{i}a_{j}}+\sigma_{b_{i}b_{j}}-\sigma_{b_{i}a_{j}}-\sigma_{a_{i}b_{j}})

(25)

with
\sigma_{ab}=\sigma(\bm{r}_{a}-\bm{r}_{b})
defined in Eq. (
18
). The non-diagonal elements, which couple the permutation
p
to configurations
\pi
differing by a single index swap
k\leftrightarrow l
, e.g.
(p_{1},...,p_{k},...p_{l},...p_{n})\to\pi=(p_{1},...,p_{l},...p_{k},...p_{n})
, read

(\bm{M}_{\mathrm{non-diag}})_{\pi}^{(p_{1}\dots p_{n})}=\sigma_{b_{k}a_{p_{l}}}+\sigma_{a_{p_{k}}b_{l}}-\sigma_{a_{p_{k}}a_{p_{l}}}-\sigma_{b_{k}b_{l}}\,.

(26)

Using these ingredients, the evolution equations for the correlators in Eq. (
22
) can be written in a compact matrix form as

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{ijk}}{dt}=\mathbb{M}_{\sigma}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{C}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{ijk},

(27)

where the index
\sigma
runs over all color configurations that couple to the correlator of interest. In what follows, we define
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ijk}\equiv\mathbb{C}^{(4)}_{i\to jk}
, meaning that
\sigma=1
corresponds directly to the physical correlators defined in Eq. (
22
). These correlators are normalized such that the initial value is
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}(0)=1
; for configurations where the initial value is zero, no normalization factor is applied. Furthermore, the evolution matrix
\mathbb{M}_{\sigma}^{\sigma^{\prime}}
depends only on a few linear combinations of the dipole scattering amplitudes:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{12}+\sigma_{\bar{1}\bar{2}}=\sigma(\bm{u})+\sigma(\bar{\bm{u}}),

(28a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{+}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1\bar{1}}=\sigma(z(\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{u}}))

(28b)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{-}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2\bar{2}}=\sigma((1-z)(\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{u}}))

(28c)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{zs}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\bar{1}2}+\sigma_{1\bar{2}}=\sigma((1-z)\bm{u}+z\bar{\bm{u}})+\sigma(z\bm{u}+(1-z)\bar{\bm{u}}).

(28d)

We note that
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{zs}
are invariant under the energy fraction exchange
z\to 1-z
, whereas
\Sigma_{\pm}
transform into each other (
\Sigma_{+}\leftrightarrow\Sigma_{-}
). This transformation property ensures that the combination
\Sigma_{\pm}=\Sigma_{+}+\Sigma_{-}
remains invariant under the exchange of energy fractions. In what follows, we provide explicit expressions for each splitting channel.

•

\gamma\to q\bar{q}
: the evolution equations for this channel were previously derived in Ref.
56
. The correlator associated with this vertex stems from the double-pair structure

\mathbb{C}_{\gamma\to q\bar{q}}^{(4)}=\frac{1}{N_{c}}C_{(21)}\quad{\rm with}\quad C_{p}=\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}}^{p[j_{1}j_{2}]}(V_{1}V_{2}^{\dagger})_{i_{1}j_{1}}(V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger})_{i_{2}j_{2}}\,.

(29)

The resulting coupled system of equations reads

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}_{1}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\left[\Big(2C_{F}\Sigma_{\pm}+\frac{1}{N_{c}}(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{0})\Big)\mathcal{C}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}_{1}-N_{c}(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{0})\mathcal{C}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}_{2}\right],

(30a)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}_{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\left[-\dfrac{1}{N_{c}}(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{\pm})\mathcal{C}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}_{1}+\Big(2C_{F}\Sigma_{0}+\frac{1}{N_{c}}(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{\pm})\Big)\mathcal{C}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}_{2}\right],

(30b)

where the auxiliary state is defined as
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}\equiv\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}C_{(12)}
.

•

g\to q\bar{q}
: the underlying correlator contains two independent color structures. We write

\mathbb{C}_{g\to q\bar{q}}^{(4)}=\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}-1}\,[C_{(12)}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\,D_{(21)}]\quad{\rm with}\quad\begin{cases}C_{p}\equiv\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}}^{p[j_{1}j_{2}]}(V_{\bar{1}}V_{1}^{\dagger})_{i_{1}j_{1}}(V_{2}V_{\bar{2}}^{\dagger})_{i_{2}j_{2}},\\
\displaystyle D_{p}\equiv\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}}^{p[j_{1}j_{2}]}(V_{1}V_{2}^{\dagger})_{i_{1}j_{1}}(V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger})_{i_{2}j_{2}}.\end{cases}

(31)

Although the full evolution initially yields a system of four coupled differential equations, they can be combined into a closed set of two equations that read

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}^{gq\bar{q}}_{1}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\left[\Big(2C_{F}\Sigma_{\pm}+\frac{1}{N_{c}}(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{0})\Big)\mathcal{C}^{gq\bar{q}}_{1}+\dfrac{1}{N_{c}}\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}^{gq\bar{q}}_{2}\right],

(32a)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}^{gq\bar{q}}_{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\left[\left(\Sigma_{\pm}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}^{gq\bar{q}}_{1}+\Big(2C_{F}\Sigma_{0}+\frac{1}{N_{c}}(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{\pm})\Big)\mathcal{C}^{gq\bar{q}}_{2}\right],

(32b)

where
\mathcal{C}^{gq\bar{q}}_{2}\equiv N_{c}C_{(21)}-D_{(12)}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{gq\bar{q}}(t=0)=0
. As a consequence, its associated source term in the evolution equation for
\cal{B}
vanishes, i.e.,
\xi_{\sigma=2}=0
in Eq. (
8
).

•

q\to gq
: the physical correlator is expressed as

\mathbb{C}^{(4)}_{q\to gq}=\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}-1}[C_{(213)}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}C_{(312)}]\quad{\rm with}\quad C_{p}=\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}^{p[j_{1}j_{2}j_{2}]}(V_{1}V_{2}^{\dagger})_{i_{1}j_{1}}(V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger})_{i_{2}j_{2}}(V_{2}V_{\bar{2}}^{\dagger})_{i_{3}j_{3}}\,.

(33)

The resulting reduced system of evolution equations is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{qgq}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\left[2\left(C_{F}\Sigma_{+}+N_{c}\,\Sigma_{-}\right)\mathcal{C}_{1}^{qgq}-N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{0}\right)\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{2}\right]\,,

(34a)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{2}^{qgq}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\left[\left(-\frac{\Sigma_{+}}{N_{c}}+N_{c}(\Sigma_{0}+\Sigma_{-})\right)\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{2}+\dfrac{1}{N_{c}^{2}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\left(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{0}\right)\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{3}\right]\,,

(34b)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{3}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\big[N_{c}^{2}(N_{c}^{2}-1)\big(\left(\Sigma_{zs}+\Sigma_{0}-2\Sigma_{\pm}\right)\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{1}+\left(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{0}\right)\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\left(N_{c}(\Sigma_{zs}+\Sigma_{-})-\frac{\Sigma_{+}}{N_{c}}\right)\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{3}\big],

(34c)

where the new color configurations read

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}[C_{(123)}-C_{(231)}]\,,

(35a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{3}

\displaystyle=C_{(132)}-C_{(213)}-N_{c}C_{(231)}+C_{(321)}.

(35b)

In this case,
\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{3}(t=0)=0
and
\xi_{\sigma=3}=0
. Notably, the potential matrix contains terms proportional to
\Sigma_{+}
and
\Sigma_{-}
. Their origin can be traced back to the lack of symmetry of
q\to gq
splittings under
z\to 1-z
transformations.

•

g\to gg
: the evolution equations for this vertex had not been explicitly reported in previous literature. The color structures associated with this splitting channel are

\mathbb{C}^{(4)}_{g\to gg}=\frac{1}{N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}[C_{(1243)}-D_{(2341)}]\quad{\rm with}\quad\begin{cases}C_{p}\equiv\delta^{p[j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}j_{4}]}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}(V_{\bar{1}}V_{1}^{\dagger})_{i_{1}j_{1}}(V_{2}V_{\bar{2}}^{\dagger})_{i_{2}j_{2}}(V_{1}V_{2}^{\dagger})_{i_{3}j_{3}}(V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger})_{i_{4}j_{4}},\\
\displaystyle D_{p}\equiv\delta^{p[j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}j_{4}]}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}(V_{\bar{1}}V_{2}^{\dagger})_{i_{1}j_{1}}(V_{\bar{2}}V_{\bar{1}}^{\dagger})_{i_{2}j_{2}}(V_{1}V_{\bar{2}}^{\dagger})_{i_{3}j_{3}}(V_{2}V_{1}^{\dagger})_{i_{4}j_{4}}.\end{cases}

(36)

Individually, each structure generates a system of
24
coupled differential equations, leading to a total of
48
equations. Remarkably, this full set can be reduced to only six independent equations, which read:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ggg}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\Big[\,2N_{c}\Sigma_{\pm}\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ggg}+N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}\Big]\,,

(37a)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\Big[\dfrac{2}{N_{c}}\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ggg}+N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}+\Sigma_{0}\right)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}-\dfrac{2}{N_{c}}\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{3}^{ggg}\Big]\,,

(37b)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{3}^{ggg}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\Big[-N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}-\Sigma_{0}\right)\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ggg}-N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}+N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}+\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{3}^{ggg}

(37c)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2}{N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\left(-2\Sigma_{\pm}+\Sigma_{0}+\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{4}^{ggg}-3N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{5}^{ggg}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{4}^{ggg}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\Big[2N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{\pm}\right)\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ggg}+2N_{c}^{3}(N_{c}^{2}-1)\left(\Sigma_{\pm}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}

\displaystyle-2N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{\pm}\right)\mathcal{C}_{3}^{ggg}-N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}-3\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{4}^{ggg}

\displaystyle+2N_{c}^{3}(N_{c}^{2}-1)\left(\Sigma_{\pm}+2\Sigma_{0}-3\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{5}^{ggg}-N_{c}^{3}(N_{c}^{2}-1)\left(\Sigma_{\pm}+4\Sigma_{0}-5\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{6}^{ggg}\Big]\,,

(37d)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{5}^{ggg}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\Big[N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}+\dfrac{1}{N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\left(\Sigma_{zs}-\Sigma_{0}\right)\mathcal{C}_{4}^{ggg}

(37e)

\displaystyle-2N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{zs}-2\Sigma_{0}\right)\mathcal{C}_{5}^{ggg}-3N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{6}^{ggg}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{6}^{ggg}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n(t)}{2}\Big[\dfrac{1}{N_{c}(1-N_{c}^{2})}\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{\pm}\right)\mathcal{C}_{4}^{ggg}-2N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}-\Sigma_{0}\right)\mathcal{C}_{5}^{ggg}+N_{c}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}-\Sigma_{0}+2\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{6}^{ggg}\Big]\,.

(37f)

The auxiliary color configurations are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N_{c}^{2}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\left[C_{(1234)}-C_{(1342)}+C_{(2143)}-C_{(4213)}+D_{(1342)}-D_{(2314)}+D_{(2431)}-D_{(3241)}\right]\,,

(38a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{3}^{ggg}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\Big[-C_{(1432)}+2C_{(2134)}-C_{(2341)}-C_{(2413)}-C_{(4231)}+2C_{(4312)}+D_{(1324)}-2D_{(1432)}+D_{(2134)}

\displaystyle+N_{c}\left(C_{(1342)}+C_{(4213)}-D_{(1342)}-D_{(2431)}+D_{(3142)}-2D_{(3214)}+D_{(3421)}\right)\Big]\,,

(38b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{4}^{ggg}

\displaystyle=-C_{(1342)}+C_{(2314)}+C_{(4132)}-C_{(4213)}-D_{(1234)}+D_{(1342)}+D_{(2431)}-D_{(3412)}

\displaystyle+N_{c}\left[2C_{(2134)}-C_{(4312)}+D_{(1432)}-2D_{(3214)}\right]\,,

(38c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{5}^{ggg}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\Big[C_{(2134)}-C_{(4312)}+D_{(1432)}-D_{(3214)}\Big]\,,

(38d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{6}^{ggg}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N_{c}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\Big[D_{(1432)}-C_{(4312)}\Big]\,.

(38e)

\mathcal{C}_{4}^{ggg}(t=0)=0

.

) and the large-

Large-

•

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{c}\gg 1}}{{\simeq}}\dfrac{N_{c}\,n(t)}{2}\left[\Sigma_{\pm}\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}+\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}_{2}\right]\,,

(39a)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{c}\gg 1}}{{\simeq}}\dfrac{N_{c}\,n(t)}{2}\,\Sigma_{0}\,\mathcal{C}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}_{2}\,.

(39b)

•

g\to q\bar{q}
:

\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{gq\bar{q}}}{dt}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{c}\gg 1}}{{\simeq}}\dfrac{N_{c}}{2}\,n(t)\,\Sigma_{\pm}\mathcal{C}_{1}^{gq\bar{q}}\,.

(40)

•

q\to gq
:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{qgq}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{c}\gg 1}}{{\simeq}}\dfrac{N_{c}\,n(t)}{2}\left[\left(\Sigma_{+}+2\,\Sigma_{-}\right)\mathcal{C}_{1}^{qgq}+\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{2}\right]\,,

(41a)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{2}^{qgq}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{c}\gg 1}}{{\simeq}}\dfrac{N_{c}\,n(t)}{2}(\Sigma_{0}+\Sigma_{-})\mathcal{C}^{qgq}_{2}\,.

(41b)

•

g\to gg
:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ggg}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{c}\gg 1}}{{\simeq}}\,\dfrac{N_{c}\,n(t)}{2}\Big[\,2\Sigma_{\pm}\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ggg}+\left(\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{zs}\right)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}\Big]\,,

(42a)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N_{c}\gg 1}}{{\simeq}}\,\dfrac{N_{c}\,n(t)}{2}\left(\Sigma_{\pm}+\Sigma_{0}\right)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{ggg}.

(42b)

The size of
N_{c}
-corrections for each splitting channel is quantified in Fig.

Soft limit.

When
z\to 0
, Eqs. (
28
) reduce to
\Sigma_{zs}\to\Sigma_{0}
,
\Sigma_{+}\to 0
and
\Sigma_{-}\to\Sigma_{d}
. Therefore, across all vertices,
\mathcal{C}_{1}
decouples from the remaining color states. This yields a single evolution equation for each splitting channel:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle z\to 0}}{{\simeq}}C_{F}\,n(t)\,\Sigma_{d}\,\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\gamma q\bar{q}}\,,

(43a)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{gq\bar{q}}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle z\to 0}}{{\simeq}}C_{F}\,n(t)\,\Sigma_{d}\,\mathcal{C}_{1}^{gq\bar{q}}\,,

(43b)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{qgq}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle z\to 0}}{{\simeq}}N_{c}\,n(t)\,\Sigma_{d}\,\mathcal{C}_{1}^{qgq}\,,

(43c)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ggg}}{dt}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle z\to 0}}{{\simeq}}\,N_{c}\,n(t)\,\Sigma_{d}\,\mathcal{C}_{1}^{ggg}\,.

(43d)

We observe that, in this regime, the pairs of vertices
\{q\to gq,\,g\to gg\}
and
\{\gamma\to q\bar{q},\,g\to q\bar{q}\}
share the same equations, meaning that they share the quadrupole structure. Furthermore, their splitting kernels, governed by the medium potential
v_{ba}
in Eq. (
15
), become identical within each pair. As a result, the medium-induced radiation spectra coincide within each pair, as the modifications are mainly imprinted in the soft particle. The evolution equations for
\mathcal{C}_{1}
are identical when
z\to 1
except in the
q\to gq
channel, where the color factor becomes
C_{F}
, indicating that soft-quark modifications become dominant. Finally, the joint large-
N_{c}
and soft limit is straightforwardly recovered by implementing the standard replacement
C_{F}\to N_{c}/2
in Eq. (
43
).

.3
Considerations regarding the evolution equations of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}

In this section we derive the evolution equations for the objects
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, demonstrating how to reduce their dimensionality by exploiting symmetries.

We begin with the position-space evolution for the splitting kernel
\mathcal{K}

57
,

i\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(\bm{u},\bm{u}_{1}|t,t_{0})+\left(\dfrac{\nabla^{2}_{\bm{u}}}{2\omega}+iv_{ab}(\bm{u})\right)\mathcal{K}(\bm{u},\bm{u}_{1}|t,t_{0})=i\delta^{(2)}_{\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{1}}\delta(t-t_{0}).

(44)

The object
\mathcal{A}
, defined in Eq. (
In-medium QCD splittings beyond the soft, large-
N_{c}
and harmonic-oscillator approximations all at once
), is extracted via the relation

\mathcal{A}(t,\bm{p}^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{t}dt_{0}\,\int_{\bm{u}}e^{-i\bm{u}\cdot\bm{p^{\prime}}}\,\nabla_{\bm{u}_{1}}\,\mathcal{K}(\bm{u},\bm{u}_{1}|t,t_{0})|_{\bm{u}_{1}=0}.

(45)

Combining this definition with Eq. (
44
), yields a momentum-space, Schrödinger-like equation for
\cal A
:

i\partial_{t}{\cal A}(t,\,\bm{{p^{\prime}}})=\dfrac{{\bm{p^{\prime}}}^{2}}{2\omega}{\cal A}(t,\bm{p^{\prime}})-i\int_{\bm{q}}\tilde{v}_{ba}(\bm{q})\mathcal{A}(t,\bm{p}^{\prime}-\bm{q})-\bm{p}^{\prime},

(46)

where
\tilde{v}_{ba}(\bm{q})=\int_{\bm{u}}v_{ba}(\bm{u})e^{-i\bm{u}\cdot\bm{q}}
, and the initial condition is
\mathcal{A}(t=0)=\bm{0}
. This expression matches Eq. (
5
).

By adopting polar coordinates
\bm{p}=(p,\theta)
and decomposing
\mathcal{A}
into radial and angular components,
\mathcal{A}(t,\bm{p})=a_{p}(t,p,\theta)\hat{\bm{p}}+a_{\theta}(t,p,\theta)\hat{\bm{\theta}}
, the rotational invariance of the medium potential, i.e.
\tilde{v}_{ba}(\bm{q})=\tilde{v}_{ba}(|\bm{q}|)
, ensures that the two components decouple. Given that
{\cal A}
vanishes at
t=0
, the angular amplitude
a_{\theta}
remains zero throughout the evolution. Furthermore, the scalar amplitude becomes purely isotropic
a_{p}(t,p,\theta)\equiv a(t,p)
, which reduces Eq. (
46
) to a scalar
(1+1)
-dimensional equation,

i\partial_{t}a(t,\,p)=\dfrac{p^{2}}{2\omega}a(t,\,p)-i\,n(t)\sum_{a}c_{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tilde{p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\tilde{\theta}\,\tilde{p}\,V(\tilde{p})\left[a(t,p)-\frac{a(t,R_{a})}{R_{a}}(p-\tilde{p}g_{a}(z)\cos\tilde{\theta})\right]-p,

(47)

where
c_{a}
and
g_{a}(z)
refer to the color and
z
-dependent factors (taking values
1
,
z
or
1-z
) in Eq. (
15
), and
R_{a}\equiv\sqrt{p^{2}+g_{a}(z)^{2}\tilde{p}^{2}-2p\tilde{p}g_{a}(z)\cos\tilde{\theta}}
. Finally, the isotropic potential is defined via the differential cross-section as
V(\bm{p})=\frac{d\sigma}{d^{2}\bm{p}}\equiv V(p)
, with some examples provided by GW (
16
) and HTL (
17
) models.

Turning to the object
\mathcal{B}
, the starting point is the evolution equation for the quadrupole
\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma}=\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma}(\bm{u},\bar{\bm{u}},\bm{u}_{2},\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}|t,t_{2})
, defined in Eq. (
20b
)
57
:

i\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma}+\left[\dfrac{\nabla_{\bm{u}}^{2}-\nabla^{2}_{\bm{\bar{u}}}}{2\omega}\delta_{\sigma}^{\sigma^{\prime}}-i\mathbb{M}_{\sigma}^{\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{u},\bm{\bar{u}})\right]\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma^{\prime}}=i\delta^{(2)}_{\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{2}}\delta^{(2)}_{\bm{\bar{u}}-\bm{\bar{{u}}}_{2}}\delta(t-t_{2})\,\xi_{\sigma}\,.

(48)

The functions
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}
, defined in Eq. (
7
), are related to the quadrupole
\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma}
via the transformation

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(t,\bm{k},\bm{l})=\int_{\bm{u},\bar{\bm{u}}}e^{-i[\bm{u}\cdot(\bm{k}+\bm{l})+\bar{\bm{u}}\cdot(\bm{l}-\bm{k})]}\int_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t}dt_{2}\int_{\bm{u}_{2}}(\nabla_{\bm{u}_{1}}\cdot\nabla_{\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}})\,\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma}(\bm{u},\bm{\bar{u}},\bm{u}_{2},\bm{\bar{u}}_{2}|t,t_{2})\,\mathcal{K}(\bm{u}_{2},\bm{u}_{1}|t_{2},t_{1})|_{\bm{u}_{1}=\bm{\bar{u}}_{2}=\bm{0}}\,.

(49)

Differentiating this definition with respect to time and inserting Eq. (
48
), leads to the momentum-space evolution for
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}
:

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}{\cal B}_{\sigma}(t,\,\bm{k^{\prime}},\,\bm{l^{\prime}})=

\displaystyle\left[\,\frac{2\bm{k^{\prime}}\!\cdot\!\bm{l^{\prime}}}{\omega}\,\delta_{\sigma}^{\sigma^{\prime}}+i\left(\tilde{\mathbb{M}}_{\sigma}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\ast\cdot\right)\right]\mathcal{B}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\!\left(t,\bm{k^{\prime}},\,\bm{l^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+(\bm{k^{\prime}}-\bm{l^{\prime}})\cdot\int_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\,\int_{\bm{u}_{2}}e^{-i\bm{u}_{2}\cdot(\bm{k^{\prime}+l^{\prime}})}\,\nabla_{\bm{u}_{1}}\,\mathcal{K}(\bm{u}_{2},\bm{u}_{1}|t,t_{1})|_{\bm{u}_{1}=0}\,\xi_{\sigma}\,,

(50)

Noting that the integral in the second line, which we refer to as source term, corresponds to
\mathcal{A}(\bm{k}+\bm{l})
allows us to recover Eq. (
8

50
) to (3+1)-dimensions we proceed as follows. First, by exploiting the previously established isotropy of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.
\mathcal{A}(t,\bm{p})=a(t,p)\hat{\bm{p}}
, the scalar component of the source term simplifies to

S(\bm{k},\bm{l})\equiv(\bm{k}-\bm{l})\cdot\mathcal{A}(\bm{k}+\bm{l})=\dfrac{\bm{k}^{2}-\bm{l}^{2}}{|\bm{k}+\bm{l}|}\,a(|\bm{k}+\bm{l}|)\,.

(51)

Upon transforming to polar coordinates,
\bm{k}=(k,\phi)
and
\bm{l}=(l,\eta)

(52)

where
\psi
denotes the relative angle between the two momentum vectors. This simplification reduces the dependence of

k,l
and

,

\psi

\tilde{\mathbb{M}}_{\sigma}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\ast\mathcal{B}_{\sigma^{\prime}}=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime},i}M^{i}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\int_{\tilde{\bm{p}}}V(|\tilde{\bm{p}}|)\ [\mathcal{B}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k},\bm{l})-\mathcal{B}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-a^{i}(z)\,\bm{\tilde{p}},\,\bm{l}-b^{i}(z)\,\bm{\tilde{p}})],

(53)

where we have omitted the temporal dependence and the index

runs over

28

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Sigma}_{0}\ast f)(\bm{k},\bm{l})

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Sigma}_{+}\ast f)(\bm{k},\bm{l})

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{\bm{p}}}V(\tilde{\bm{p}})\left[\,f(\bm{k},\bm{l})-f(\bm{k}-z\tilde{\bm{p}},\bm{l})\right],

(54b)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Sigma}_{-}\ast f)(\bm{k},\bm{l})

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{\bm{p}}}V(\tilde{\bm{p}})\left[\,f(\bm{k},\bm{l})-f(\bm{k}-(1-z)\tilde{\bm{p}},\bm{l})\right],

(54c)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Sigma}_{zs}\ast f)(\bm{k},\bm{l})

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{\bm{p}}}V(\tilde{\bm{p}})\left[2\,f(\bm{k},\bm{l})-f(\bm{k}-(z-1/2)\tilde{\bm{p}},\bm{l}-\tilde{\bm{p}}/2)-f(\bm{k}+(z-1/2)\tilde{\bm{p}},\bm{l}-\tilde{\bm{p}}/2)\right].

Let

where

.5
Numerical validation

To rigorously validate the correctness of the algorithm presented in this work, it has been instrumental to benchmark our numerical results against semi-analytic expectations. These analytic baselines are only available when considering two specific approximations: the large-
N_{c}
limit and the harmonic-oscillator approximation for the medium scattering potential. The former involves setting
C_{A}=N_{c}=2C_{F}
and using Eqs. (
39
)-(
42
) to determine the color matrices, which is straightforward to do in our code. The latter requires replacing Eq. (
18
) by

n\sigma({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{4}\tilde{q}{\bm{x}}^{2}\,.

(56)

Adapting the algorithm to this concrete form of the potential is less trivial. The main complication arises because the momentum-space representation of Eq. (
56
) involves a Laplacian operator acting on a Dirac delta function, given by

\int_{\bm{x}}\bm{x}^{2}f(\bm{x})e^{-i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}}=\nabla^{2}_{\bm{p}}f(\bm{p}).

(57)

Consequently, the momentum-space convolutions in Eqs. (
5
) and (
8
) reduce to pure partial derivatives. This violates a fundamental assumption of our algorithm: the continuity of the potential.

To resolve this issue, we construct a continuous surrogate of the Laplacian operator using a Gaussian kernel, exploiting the identity

\nabla_{\bm{p}}^{2}f(\bm{p})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int\frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon^{4}}\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}-1\right)e^{-\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}}\,f(\bm{p}-\bm{p}^{\prime})\,.

(58)

In practice, we run the code for a series of values of
\varepsilon
and obtain the
\varepsilon\to 0
limit via numerical extrapolation.
Alternatively, one can evaluate the necessary derivatives analytically to simplify the expressions prior to numerical computation. This approach requires a dedicated algorithm to solve the resulting equations and we have verified that both methods yield identical results.

We present a comparison between the
\varepsilon\to 0
extrapolation and the exact analytic result for
{\cal R}_{\rm med}(z=0.25,\theta=k_{T}/\omega)
in Fig.
6
. The analytic benchmarks are taken from Refs.
15
;
5
. In all channels, the numerical results agree well with the analytic expectation, with deviations consistently bounding below the
1-2\%
level. We have carried out analogous tests for other medium and kinematic parameters finding robust agreement with the corresponding analytic calculations.

Lastly, in Fig.
7
we perform a similar comparison but for a smaller value of
z=0.01
and use the soft limit approximation in the analytic results
19
;
18
;
17
;
78
;
79
;
31
, given its widespread phenomenological use. In this limit, numerical simulations and analytic results also agree at the percent level.
