Mean convex flows with surgery
Gábor Székelyhidi

Abstract
. We construct a mean curvature flow with surgery starting with any compact mean convex hypersurface in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} , extending previous results of Huisken-Sinestrari, Brendle-Huisken, and Haslhofer-Kleiner for 2-convex flows. In contrast with previous constructions of mean curvature flow with surgery, the topological surgeries are performed by the flow itself through nondegenerate cylindrical singularities. At the same time the flow needs to be slightly adjusted at finitely many smooth times.



1.
Introduction

The mean curvature flow deforms a hypersurface
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
in the direction of its mean curvature, and can be
viewed as the negative
L^{2}
-gradient flow of the area functional. Since the
foundational studies by Brakke
[
3
]
and
Huisken
[
23
]
, the flow has become a central object in
geometric analysis and the study of hypersurfaces.
A fundamental feature of the flow is that typically
singularities will form in finite
time. In the setting of convex hypersurfaces Huisken
[
23
]
showed that the
flow eventually becomes graphical over a shrinking sphere that
collapses to a point. More general initial conditions can lead to more
complicated singularities, changing the topology of the
evolving hypersurface. For instance, suitable dumbbell shaped surfaces in

\mathbb{R}^{3}
are decomposed by the flow into two
pieces, which then continue to collapse into round points.
Understanding the behavior of the flow through such
singularities is essential for geometric and topological
applications.

A successful approach under certain assumptions
is to perform surgery at the singular times:
one removes high-curvature regions, caps off the
resulting boundary components, and restarts the flow on the resulting
smooth hypersurface. If this process terminates after finitely many
surgeries, then it yields a complete picture of the topology of the
initial hypersurface. This program was carried out in full for the
class of 2-convex hypersurfaces with
n>2
– i.e. where the principal curvatures

\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}
satisfy
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>0
– by Huisken-Sinestrari in a landmark paper

[
25
]
, and for
n=2
by Brendle-Huisken
[
5
]
,
Haslhofer-Kleiner
[
22
]
. The key geometric consequence of the 2-convexity
assumption is that all singularities are either spherical, or are
modeled on the cylinder
S^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, by
White
[
35
]
. Powerful canonical
neighborhood theorems in
[
25
,
22
,
5
]
give enough
information in neighborhoods of such singularities to successfully
execute the surgery procedure.

While the 2-convexity hypothesis is natural, it is somewhat
restrictive. In this paper we will instead consider the larger class
of mean convex hypersurfaces, i.e. those that satisfy

\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{n}>0
. In this case, by White
[
35
]
, the singularities
can be modelled on more general cylinders of the form
S^{n-k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Compared to the 2-convex case, the higher dimensional Euclidean
factors with
k\geq 2
present serious difficulties in extending the surgery program to this
setting. Instead of attempting to
change the topology of the evolving hypersurface near singularities
by a cutting and pasting procedure, our approach is to show that after suitable
perturbations the flow admits only nondegenerate singularities in the
sense of Angenent-Velázquez
[
1
]
, more recently studied
extensively by Sun-Xue
[
32
]
and Sun-Wang-Xue
[
31
]
. This
approach is suggested by the following conjecture. See
Sun-Wang-Xue
[
31
, Conjecture 1.4]
, as well as
Ilmanen
[
27
, Problem 9]
,
Colding-Minicozzi-Pedersen
[
13
, Conjecture 7.1]
and
Sun-Xue
[
32
, Conjecture 1.1]
for closely related conjectures.

Conjecture 1
.

For a generic compact embedded initial hypersurface
L_{0}
, the mean curvature flow

L_{t}
admits only spherical and nondegenerate cylindrical
singularities.

The importance of nondegenerate singularities is that by Sun-Wang-Xue
[
31
]

they correspond to well understood topological surgeries (see
Theorem
5
), so
a flow through nondegenerate cylindrical singularities can be viewed
as a canonical flow with surgery.
While we are not able to prove this conjecture in such generality, our
main result is the following.

Theorem 2
.

For any compact, embedded, mean convex initial hypersurface

L_{0}
, there exists an
almost mean curvature flow

L_{t}
for all

t\geq 0
that
has only spherical and nondegenerate cylindrical singularities.

An almost mean curvature flow is a mean curvature flow at all but
finitely many smooth times, while at these times it is slightly
adjusted by smooth isotopies. See Definition
43

for the precise definition. This result yields a long sought-after surgery-type procedure
applicable to the class of mean convex hypersurfaces, and thus opens
the way to topological applications to this wider class.

At the heart of the proof of Theorem
2
is
Proposition
42
which shows, roughly speaking, that if a mean
curvature flow
L_{t}
has a degenerate cylindrical singularity at a
point
(x_{0},t_{0})
, then we can find arbitrarily small perturbations
of
L_{t}
, which have no degenerate singularities of the same type in a
neighborhood of
(x_{0},t_{0})
of a definite size. This result is
obtained by a generalization of the technique introduced by the author
in
[
33
]
in order to deal with cylindrical singularities of the
form
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
. The advantage of having a
one-dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-factor in that work is that these
singularities satisfy a dichotomy: either they are nondegenerate, or
the corresponding rescaled mean curvature flow
decays exponentially fast to the cylinder. This is no longer the case for
cylindrical singularities modeled on
S^{n-k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
for

k>1
, because of the possibility that the behavior is nondegenerate
in certain directions in the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
factor, but not
others. This is the substantial new difficulty that is addressed in this
paper.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we
collect some basic definitions and preliminary results. In the next
two sections,
3
and
4
, the main
goal is to identify
the asymptotics of rescaled mean curvature flows converging to
cylinders.
Some of these results are stated in the work of Sun-Xue
[
32
]
, but we
need to obtain more precise quantitative control, and so we reprove most
of the ingredients of their normal form theorem. In Section
4
we
also prove Proposition
26
, which shows that the
derivatives
\partial_{y_{j}}u
of the graphical function in the
degenerate directions decay exponentially. In Section
5
we will prove the
main result, Proposition
39
that allows us to locally perturb away degenerate
cylindrical singularities. Finally in Section
6
we use
this local perturbation result to prove Theorem
2
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we fix the dimension of our hypersurfaces to be

n
, and we assume a bound
for the entropy of the initial hypersurface, introduced by
Colding-Minicozzi
[
10
]
. By Huisken’s monotonicity
formula
[
24
]
, the entropy is non-increasing along the
flow, which implies that all
rescalings of all hypersurfaces that appear in our arguments will have
uniform entropy bounds, or equivalently, uniform bounds for the area
ratios at all scales. We will usually suppress dependence of our constants on this
entropy bound and the dimension. In estimates we will sometimes use the notation

\Psi(\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{k}\,|\,\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{l})

to denote a function which converges to zero if the
\delta_{i}
are
fixed and
\epsilon_{i}\to 0
. This function
\Psi
may change depending
on the context.

Suppose that
L_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a mean curvature flow
of mean convex hypersurfaces. This can be defined through
singularities as a Brakke flow
[
3
]
, or equivalently, the level set
flow
[
16
,
6
]
. Recall that by the mean convexity assumption,
these flows are
non-fattening and unique
[
16
]
.

The singularities of
L_{t}
can be studied using
tangent flows. Suppose that
(x_{0},t_{0})
is a point in spacetime. A
tangent flow of
L_{t}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
is defined as a subsequential
limit of a sequence of rescalings

(1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(L_{t_{0}+\lambda_{k}^{-2}t}-x_{0}).

By Huisken
[
24
]
(see also Ilmanen
[
26
]
), any tangent flow is given by a
self shrinking solution
\sqrt{-t}\Sigma
of the mean curvature
flow.
A convenient way to study tangent flows at
(x_{0},t_{0})
is through the rescaled mean
curvature flow (RMCF) centered at
(x_{0},t_{0})
, which is defined to be the flow

(2)

\displaystyle M_{\tau}=e^{\tau/2}(L_{t_{0}-e^{-\tau}}-x_{0}).

This flow has normal variation given by

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial x}{\partial\tau}=H+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2},

and the self-shrinkers
\Sigma
corresponding to the possible tangent
flows of
L_{t}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
are given by the
subsequential limits of
M_{\tau}
as
\tau\to\infty
.

Let us write

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{n,k}=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times S^{n-k}(\sqrt{2(n-k)})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

for
k=1,\ldots,n-1
. Throughout the paper we will always use
coordinates
y_{1},\ldots y_{k}
on the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
factor, coordinates

z_{1},\ldots,z_{n+1-k}
on the
\mathbb{R}^{n+1-k}
factor, and
x

for a point in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

In general the tangent flows may have
multiplicity, and singularities. However, by White
[
35
]
, all tangent flows of the
mean convex flow
L_{t}
have multiplicity one, and up to rotation are
given by homothetically shrinking
cylinders
\sqrt{-t}\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
, or the sphere
\sqrt{-t}\mathcal{S}_{n}=S^{n}(\sqrt{-2nt})
. We will say that
L_{t}
has a

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
-singularity at a spacetime point
(x_{0},t_{0})
if
the rescaled flow
M_{\tau}
centered at
(x_{0},t_{0})
converges, along a
subsequence, to
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
as
\tau\to\infty
. By the uniqueness result of
Colding-Minicozzi
[
11
]
in this case the whole flow
M_{\tau}

converges to the same cylinder as
\tau\to\infty
. In this case we
will also say that
M_{\tau}
has a
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
-singularity at
infinity. More generally we will say that
L_{t}
has an

(n,k)
-cylindrical singularity at
(x_{0},t_{0})
if the corresponding
rescaled flow converges to a rotation
Q\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
.

If the rescaled flow
M_{\tau}
converges to
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
, then
as
\tau\to\infty
we can write
M_{\tau}
as the graph of a function

u(x,\tau)
over
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
on larger and larger regions. We
will say that
M_{\tau}
is
\epsilon
-graphical over

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
on the region
U
, if
M_{\tau}\cap U
can be
written as the graph of a function
u
with
\|u\|_{C^{4}}\leq\epsilon
.

On several occasions we will consider a rescaled flow
M^{\prime}_{\tau}
that is
the normal graph of a function
u(x,\tau)
over another rescaled flow

M_{\tau}
. In this case
u
satisfies an equation of the form

(5)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}u=\mathcal{L}_{M_{\tau}}u+Q_{M_{\tau}}(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u),

where
\mathcal{L}
is the linearized operator given by

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M_{\tau}}(u)=\Delta u-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla u+\frac{1}{2}u+|A|^{2}u,

and the quantities are computed with respect to
M_{\tau}
. For the
cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
we have

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla+1,

and we will sometimes omit the subscript if from the context it is
clear which linearized operator we are using. We will denote by

\sigma(\mathcal{C}_{n,k})
the spectrum of

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{n,k}}
corresponding to the rates of
homogeneous solutions of the linearized equation. As explained in

[
32
]
we have

\sigma(\mathcal{C}_{n,k})\cap[0,\infty)=\{0,1/2,1\}
. The
eigenfunctions corresponding to 1 are the constants; those
corresponding to
1/2
are the coordinate functions
y_{i},z_{\alpha}
;
the ones corresponding to
0
are the quadratic functions

y_{i}z_{\alpha}
corresponding to rotations, as well as
y_{i}y_{j}-2\delta_{ij}
. The next largest (negative) eigenvalue is

-\min\{\frac{1}{n-k},\frac{1}{2}\}
(see
[
32
, Table 1]
).

Sun-Xue
[
32
]
(see also
[
1
,
19
,
20
]
) further analyzed cylindrical tangent
flows, and proved a normal form theorem for rescaled flows converging to

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
. They showed the following.

Theorem 3

(
[
32
]
)
.

Suppose that the rescaled flow
M_{\tau}

converges to the cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
. Then there is a number

\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
such that up to a rotation in the

\mathbb{R}^{k}
-factor, for sufficiently large
\tau
we have the
following: for some
\alpha>0
, we can write
M_{\tau}
as the graph of a function

u(y,z,\tau)
over the ball
B_{\tau^{\alpha}}
, such that

(8)

\displaystyle u(y,z,\tau)=\frac{\sqrt{2(n-k)}}{4\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(y_{i}^{2}-2)+o(\tau^{-1}),

as
\tau\to\infty
.

Definition 4
.

Following Sun-Xue
[
32
]
, we say that the flow
L_{t}
has a

non-degenerate

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
singularity at
(x_{0},t_{0})
,
if up to rotation, in the normal form above for the rescaled flow
centered at
(x_{0},t_{0})
, we have
\ell=k
.
If
\ell<k
, we will say that the flow has an

\ell
-degenerate

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}

singularity. We will also
say that
M_{\tau}
has a non-degenerate or
\ell
-degenerate

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
singularity at infinity.

More generally, we will say that the flow has a
non-degenerate or degenerate
(n,k)
-cylindrical singularity at a
point, if up to a rotation it has a non-degenerate or degenerate

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
-singularity. When it is
clear from the context, we will drop the
(n,k)
prefix or subscript.

The importance of non-degenerate cylindrical singularities is that, as
shown by Sun-Wang-Xue
[
31
]
, we can understand the topological
change performed by the flow through them.
More precisely, they proved the following (see

[
31
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Theorem 5

(
[
31
]
)
.

Suppose that the flow
L_{t}
has a non-degenerate
(n,k)
-cylindrical
singularity at
(x_{0},t_{0})
. Then in a spacetime neighborhood

(9)

\displaystyle Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}):=\{(x,t)\,:\,|x-x_{0}|+|t-t_{0}|^{1/2}<r\}

the singularity is isolated. In addition if
t_{-}<t_{0}<t_{+}
are sufficiently close, then inside
B_{r}(x_{0})
,

M_{t_{+}}
is obtained topologically by performing
an
(n-k)
-surgery on
M_{t_{-}}
. In other words,
M_{t_{+}}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
is obtained by removing a region diffeomorphic to

S^{n-k}\times B^{k}
from
M_{t_{-}}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
, and gluing in

B^{n-k+1}\times S^{k-1}
along the boundary, where
B^{k}
denotes the

k
-dimensional ball.

An important ingredient in this paper is a more quantitative version
of the normal form result. The main goal is to show the following type
of result. Suppose that the flow

L_{t}
has an
\ell
-degenerate
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
-singularity at a
point
(x_{0},t_{0})
, and the normal form asymptotics holds with good
enough error terms in the sense that the
o(\tau^{-1})
term is
bounded by
\tau^{-19/10}
, say, on a time interval
[T_{0},T_{0}+L_{0}(T_{0})]
. Suppose that a nearby flow
L_{t}^{\prime}
has an

(n,k)
-cylindrical singularity at a nearby point
(x_{0}^{\prime},t_{0}^{\prime})
(with
“nearby” depending on
T_{0}
). We want to show that then this singularity is either

\ell
-degenerate, with good error term estimates in its asymptotics
for
\tau>T_{1}
, where
T_{1}
depends on
T_{0}
, or the singularity is

\ell^{\prime}
-degenerate for
\ell^{\prime}>\ell
, or non-degenerate.
The overall argument roughly follows the
strategy of
[
32
]
, or earlier works such as
Velazquez
[
34
]
and Filippas-Liu
[
17
]
on semilinear
parabolic PDE, however the details are somewhat different. This will
take up most of the next two sections.

An important new result will be Proposition
26
on the exponential decay
of derivatives in the degenerate
y
-directions. This is related to a
result of Choi-Haslhofer
[
9
, Theorem 1.10]
, which studies
ancient flows in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
asymptotic to a cylinder
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and in particular deals with a situation analogous to

1
-degenerate cylindrical singularities.

The starting point is

[
32
, Proposition 2.3]
, which is implicitly contained in the
work of Colding-Minicozzi
[
11
]
.

Proposition 6
.

There is an
\alpha>0
, with the following property. Suppose that

M_{\tau}
is a RMCF converging to
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
, and
M_{0}
is

\delta
-graphical
over
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
on the ball
B_{\delta^{-1}}
, for
sufficiently small

. Then for all

\tau>1

r(\tau)=\sqrt{\alpha\ln\tau}

M_{\tau}
is the graph of a
function
u(x,\tau)
over
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
on
B_{r(\tau)}
,
with

\|u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}(B_{r(\tau)})}\leq\tau^{-\alpha}
.

Recall that we are suppressing the dependence of constants on the
assumed entropy bound and dimension. In general

The next step is to extend the good graphicality
to balls of size
B_{\tau^{\beta}}
for some
\beta>0
. For this we will
use an approach based on Ecker’s log-Sobolev

[

. We also use

First recall the following consequence of the log-Sobolev
inequality from
[
14
]
, where we have an additional forcing
term. We give the proof in the appendix. It is worth noting that while
the flow
M_{\tau}
may have singularities, and might only satisfy the
rescaled mean curvature equation in a weak sense, in the following
results we will always consider functions that are supported on the
smooth part of the flow – in practice they will be supported in a
region where the flow is close to a cylinder.

Proposition 7
.

Suppose that
M_{\tau}
is a rescaled mean curvature flow, and for
some
\epsilon\geq 0
, the nonnegative function

f(x,\tau)
satisfies the differential inequality

(10)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}f\leq\Delta f-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla f+a\cdot\nabla f+bf+\mathcal{E},

where
a(x,\tau)

, and
\mathcal{E}(x,\tau)\geq 0
.
Then we have

(11)

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{p(\tau)}}\leq e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})\tau}\|f(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})(\tau-s)}\|\mathcal{E}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p(s)}}\,ds,

where we define

(so
that
p(0)=2
). The

The following result leading to pointwise points is similar to
[

Proposition 8
.

Suppose that along the rescaled mean curvature flow

, the

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}f\leq\Delta f-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla f+\mathcal{E},

for

.
There is a constant

\displaystyle f(x_{0},1)\leq Ce^{|x_{0}|^{2}/4}\int_{M_{0}}f(x,0)e^{-|x|^{2}/4}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}+C\delta e^{-R^{2}/15}.

Proof.

The function
f
satisfies the differential inequality

(14)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f\leq\Delta f+\frac{1}{|t|}\mathcal{E},

(21)

\displaystyle f(x_{0},\tau)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{4p(\tau-\tau_{0}-1)}}\left(e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})(\tau-\tau_{0})}\|f(\cdot,\tau_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})(\tau-s)}\|\mathcal{E}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p(s-\tau_{0})}}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle+C\delta e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{15p(\tau-\tau_{0}-1)}}.

Proof.

First we apply Proposition
7
to the flow
translated in time by
\tau_{0}
, to estimate the
L^{p}
norm of
f
at
time
\tau-1
. We find that

(22)

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot,\tau-1)\|_{L^{p(\tau-\tau_{0}-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})(\tau-\tau_{0}-1)}\|f(\cdot,\tau_{0})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau-1}e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})(\tau-1-s)}\|\mathcal{E}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p(s-\tau_{0})}}\,ds,

Next we consider the function
F=f^{p}
, where for simplicity we
wrote

. The function
F
satisfies the
differential inequality

(23)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}F\leq\Delta F-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla F-p(p-1)f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}+pf^{p-1}a\cdot\nabla f+pb_{0}f^{p}+pf^{p-1}\mathcal{E}.

Using that

|a|<1

pf^{p-1}a\cdot\nabla f

and the
f^{p}

(24)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}F\leq\Delta F-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla F+Cpb_{0}F+\mathcal{E}^{p},

and so if

(25)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\tilde{F}\leq\Delta\tilde{F}-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla\tilde{F}+e^{-Cb_{0}p\tau}\mathcal{E}^{p}.

We apply Proposition
8
, on the time interval

[\tau-1,\tau]
. The conclusion is that

(26)

\displaystyle e^{-Cb_{0}p}F(x_{0},\tau)\leq Ce^{|x_{0}|^{2}/4}\|F(\cdot,\tau-1)\|_{L^{1}}+C\delta^{p}e^{-R^{2}/15}.

We have
\|F(\cdot,\tau-1)\|_{L^{1}}=\|f(\cdot,\tau-1)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}
. Taking
p^{th}
roots we get the required

∎

We want to apply this result to a function

over
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}

Proposition 10
.

Given
\epsilon,\beta_{0}>0

We will apply Corollary
9
. We let

\displaystyle e^{\tau-\tau_{0}}=K^{2}\tau^{2\beta^{\prime}}.

\displaystyle\tau_{0}=\tau-2\log K-2\beta^{\prime}\log\tau>\frac{2}{3}\tau,

For

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}e^{\tau-\tau_{0}}\leq p(\tau-\tau_{0}-1)=1+\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)e^{\tau-\tau_{0}-1}\leq e^{\tau-\tau_{0}},

\displaystyle e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4p(\tau-\tau_{0}-1)}}\leq e.

9
at time

\tau

|x_{0}|<\frac{7}{6}K\tau^{\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{15p(\tau-\tau_{0}-1)}}\leq e^{-\frac{K^{2}\tau^{2\beta}}{15\cdot 64e^{\tau-\tau_{0}}}}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{10^{4}}\tau^{2(\beta-\beta^{\prime})}}.

From Corollary

(34)

\displaystyle f(x_{0},\tau)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{(b_{0}+\epsilon)(\tau-\tau_{0})}\|f(\cdot,\tau_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+C\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}e^{(b_{0}+\epsilon)(\tau-s)}\|\mathcal{E}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p(s-\tau_{0})}}\,ds

\displaystyle+CK^{-1}\tau^{-\beta}e^{-\frac{1}{10^{4}}\tau^{2(\beta-\beta^{\prime})}},

for

and
\tau\in[\frac{5}{3}T_{0},T_{1}]
.

By our bound for

(35)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p(s-\tau_{0})}}\leq CK^{-1}s^{-\beta}e^{-K^{2}s^{2\beta}/5p(s-\tau_{0})}

We have
p(s-\tau_{0})\leq 2e^{s-\tau_{0}}

(36)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p(s-\tau_{0})}}

\displaystyle\leq CK^{-1}\tau^{-\beta}e^{-\frac{K^{2}\tau^{2\beta}}{40e^{\tau-\tau_{0}}e^{s-\tau}}}

\displaystyle=CK^{-1}\tau^{-\beta}e^{-\frac{1}{40}\tau^{2(\beta-\beta^{\prime})}e^{\tau-s}}

\displaystyle\leq CK^{-1}\tau^{-\beta}e^{-\frac{1}{40}\tau^{2(\beta-\beta^{\prime})}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{80}(\tau-s)^{2}\right),

where
\tau\geq s
and we also used

Using this in the estimate for

above, we get

(37)

\displaystyle f(x,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq C(K^{2}\tau^{2\beta^{\prime}})^{b_{0}+\epsilon}\|f(\cdot,\tau_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+CK^{-1}\tau^{-\beta}e^{-\frac{1}{40}\tau^{2(\beta-\beta^{\prime})}}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}e^{(b_{0}+\epsilon)(\tau-s)-\frac{1}{80}(\tau-s)^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle+CK^{-1}\tau^{-\beta}e^{-\frac{1}{10^{4}}\tau^{2(\beta-\beta^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{2\beta^{\prime}(b_{0}+\epsilon)}\|f(\cdot,\tau_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+CK^{-1}\tau^{-\beta}e^{-\frac{1}{10^{4}}\tau^{2(\beta-\beta^{\prime})}},

for
|x|<\frac{7}{6}K\tau^{\beta^{\prime}}
, and
\tau\in[\frac{5}{3}T_{0},T_{1}]
.
∎

Proposition 11
.

Given
\epsilon>0

Proposition 12
.

Given small

11

3.
The
L^{2}
normal form

In the previous section we saw that if the rescaled flow
M_{\tau}

has a cylindrical singularity at infinity, then the
L^{2}
-norm of the
graphicality function
u
over the cylinder decays at a rate

\tau^{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
, which in turn is related to
good graphicality on balls of size
\tau^{\beta}
for
\beta<\alpha/2
. In this section our goal is to improve the
L^{2}
-asymptotics of the graphicality
function to obtain an error of order
o(\tau^{-1})
, as done in

[
32
, Section 5]
. As can be seen from
the normal form, Theorem
3
, for this we need to
identify the term of order
\tau^{-1}
giving the leading order
behavior. It is worth noting that even if
M_{\tau}
has good
graphicality over
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
on a ball of radius larger than

\tau^{\beta}
, the contribution to the
L^{2}
-norm of
u

from outside of
B_{\tau^{\beta}}
is bounded
by
e^{-\tau^{2\beta}/9}
for large

\tau
, and this is of much lower order than
\tau^{-1}
. So at this
point we can still work on balls of radius
\tau^{\beta}
for small

\beta>0
. Once we have the
L^{2}
-asymptotics identified up to an

o(\tau^{-1})
error, in the next section we will be able to obtain
graphicality on balls of radius
\tau^{1/2}
, over suitable models.

To obtain an effective
o(\tau^{-1})
estimate for the
L^{2}
-asymptotics, our
hypothesis can no longer be simply closeness to the cylinder

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
at time zero. This is because as
\tau\to\infty

the flow could become “less degenerate”, with a larger value of

\ell
, than what it appears to be
initially. In this case additional terms of size
(T_{1}+\tau)^{-1}

could appear, which would not be detectable at times
\tau\ll T_{1}
. In addition we need to allow for rotations in the
y
-directions
to correctly identify the degenerate and nondegenerate directions.

We have the following.

Proposition 14
.

Suppose that
M_{\tau}
is a rescaled flow converging to

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
at infinity, and we can
write
M_{\tau}
as the graph of
u(\cdot,\tau)

over
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
on
B_{\tau^{\beta}}
, for all
\tau\geq 0
.
For any
T_{0}>1
there is an
L_{0}(T_{0})>0
with the
following property. For some
\ell\geq 0
let us define

(46)

\displaystyle E(\tau):=\left\|u(\cdot,\tau)-\frac{\sqrt{2(n-k)}}{4\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(y_{i}^{2}-2)\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\tau^{\beta}})},

and suppose that for all
\tau\in[T_{0},T_{0}+L_{0}]

we have
E(\tau)<\tau^{-3/2}
. Then we have the following.

(i)

If
M_{\tau}
has an
\ell^{\prime}
-degenerate

(n,k)
-cylindrical singularity at infinity, then
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
.

(ii)

If
M_{\tau}
has an
\ell
-degenerate

(n,k)
-cylindrical singularity at infinity, then after a
rotation of size
\Psi(T_{0}^{-1})
in the
y
-coordinates, we
have
E(\tau)<\tau^{-19/10}
for all
\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}
. In
particular the rotated flow
QM_{\tau}
has an
\ell
-degenerate

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
-singularity at infinity.

In other words, if we have
\ell
directions that look sufficiently
nondegenerate on a large range of scales, then actually for all
sufficiently large
\tau
we must have at least
\ell
nondegenerate
directions. And if no further nondegenerate directions appear, then we
have a good estimate in the nondegenerate directions. Note that when

\ell=0
, in case (ii) we actually have that
E(\tau)
decays
exponentially fast (see
[
33
, Proposition 5]
), but we do not
need this here.

The proof of this will require a number of preliminary steps. We first recall the
discrete frequency monotonicity results in
[
31
]
, and in
particular the non-concentration estimate
[
31
, Corollary
3.3]
. Let us define the
L^{2}
-distance from
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
by

(47)

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{\mathcal{C}_{n,k}}(M)^{2}=\int_{M}\overline{\mathrm{dist}}_{n,k}(x)^{2}\,e^{-|x|^{2}/4}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},

where
\overline{\mathrm{dist}}_{n,k}
is a regularized distance
function from
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
. For our purposes we can take

\overline{\mathrm{dist}}_{n,k}(x)=\min\{d(x,\mathcal{C}_{n,k}),1\}
,
which is uniformly equivalent to the definition used in
[
31
]
.
This
L^{2}
-distance satisfies the following
non-concentration estimate.

Proposition 15

(See Corollary 3.3 in
[
31
]
)
.

There is a dimensional constant
C_{n}
with the following
property. If
M_{\tau}
is a rescaled mean curvature flow then for
\tau\in[0,2]
we have

(48)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\tau}}\overline{\mathrm{dist}}_{n,k}(x)^{2}(1+\tau|x|^{2})\,e^{-|x|^{2}/4}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq C_{n}\int_{M_{0}}\overline{\mathrm{dist}}_{n,k}(x)^{2}\,e^{-|x|^{2}/4}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

As a consequence we have the following, which is essentially

[
31
, Remark 3.8]
. Note that in
[
31
, Remark 3.8]
this result is stated for

L=1
, which is a stronger result, but may need a more careful
definition of the distance function.

Proposition 16
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
. There exist
L_{0},\delta>0
, depending on

\epsilon
, such
that if
L>L_{0}
and

\delta
-graphical over

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
on
B_{\delta^{-1}}
for
\tau\in[0,2L]

(49)

Proof.

Let

Let us now define the cut off graphical functions

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\tilde{v}=\mathcal{L}\tilde{v}+Q(\tilde{v},\nabla\tilde{v},\nabla^{2}\tilde{v})+\mathcal{R}_{\xi_{\tau}}(\tilde{v},\nabla\tilde{v})+\chi\xi_{\tau}+\mathcal{E},

Note that by adjusting the

\tau

Then, taking the inner product with
\xi_{\tau}
, and noting that the

\displaystyle|\xi_{\tau}|\leq\|Q(\tilde{v},\nabla\tilde{v},\nabla^{2}\tilde{v})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{E}\|_{L^{2}}+|\xi_{\tau}|^{-1}\langle\xi_{\tau},\partial_{\tau}\tilde{v}\rangle.

We have
\|\mathcal{E}\|_{L^{2}}\leq e^{-\tau^{\gamma}}
. By
construction we also have that the
L^{2}
-product of

\partial_{\tau}\tilde{v}
with
\xi_{\tau}
on
B_{\delta^{-1}}

vanishes, and using (
80

(

(

\delta^{-1}

we can choose
\delta

\lambda

(83)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathcal{C}\setminus B_{\delta^{-1}}}\xi_{\tau}\partial_{\tau}\tilde{v}\,d\mu\right|\leq\lambda|\xi_{\tau}|\|\tilde{v}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+e^{-\tau^{\gamma}}|\xi_{\tau}|.

Since each term in

is at least quadratic in the entries,

implies that

(84)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}Q(\tilde{v},\nabla\tilde{v},\nabla^{2}\tilde{v})^{2}\,e^{-|x|^{2}/4}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq C_{L_{1}}\|\tilde{v}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\delta^{-1}})}^{4}+e^{-\tau^{\gamma}}.

Using these estimates in (

(85)

\displaystyle|\xi_{\tau}|\leq C\|\tilde{v}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\tau^{\delta}})}^{2}+\lambda\|\tilde{v}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\tau^{\gamma}})}+e^{-\tau^{\gamma}},

further decreasing
\gamma
slightly.
Note also that by (

L^{2}
-norm here is
comparable to the
L^{2}
-norm on

B_{\delta^{-1}}
, or also to
\mathbf{d}_{\mathcal{C}_{n,k}}(\tilde{M}_{\tau})
.

Now we consider the evolution of the component of
\tilde{v}
lying in

\mathfrak{h}
. Let us therefore write

(86)

\displaystyle\tilde{v}=\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}+\tilde{v}_{\perp},

where
\tilde{v}_{\perp}
is orthogonal to
\mathfrak{h}
.
Further, following
[
17
, Section 2]
(see also

[

, Section 3]
or
[

(87)

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{ii}(\tau)h_{ii}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\not=j}A_{ij}(\tau)h_{ij}(y),

for a symmetric matrix

. Here
h_{ii}(y)=c_{1}(y_{i}^{2}-2)
and

h_{ij}=c_{2}y_{i}y_{j}
for suitable constants

so that these

norm on
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
.
Note that
\tilde{v}_{\perp}
also satisfies pointwise bounds like in
(

\tilde{v}
or

\tilde{v}_{\perp}
on the

are explicit functions that satisfy such estimates.

Denote by
f\in\mathfrak{h}
an element with unit norm, and consider
the evolution of the corresponding component of
\tilde{v}
. We will write

(88)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\int_{\mathcal{C}}f\tilde{v}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}}f(\mathcal{L}\tilde{v}+Q+\mathcal{R}_{\xi_{\tau}}+\xi_{\tau}+\mathcal{E})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}}f(Q+\mathcal{R}_{\xi_{\tau}}+\mathcal{E})\,d\mu,

where we used that
f
is orthogonal to the image of
\mathcal{L}
,
and also to
\xi_{\tau}
. Let us further decompose

into the quadratic and higher order terms. We have (see

[
32
, Proposition A.1]
)
that

(89)

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2(n-k)}}\left(\tilde{v}^{2}+2|\nabla_{z}\tilde{v}|^{2}+4\tilde{v}\Delta_{z}\tilde{v}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2(n-k)}}\left(\tilde{v}^{2}+2|\nabla_{z}\tilde{v}_{\perp}|^{2}+4\tilde{v}\Delta_{z}\tilde{v}_{\perp}\right),

where the gradient and Laplacian are only taken in the sphere
direction of the cylinder, and these vanish on the

\mathfrak{h}
-component. In order to deal with the norm squared
component, we integrate by parts, and note that the measure
d\mu
,
and
f
, are
invariant in the
z
-directions. So we have

(90)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}f|\nabla_{z}\tilde{v}_{\perp}|^{2}\,d\mu=-\int_{\mathcal{C}}f\tilde{v}_{\perp}\Delta_{z}\tilde{v}_{\perp}\,d\mu.

We can also integrate the Laplacian term by parts twice to get that

\tilde{v}\Delta_{z}\tilde{v}_{\perp}
can be replaced by

\tilde{v}_{\perp}\Delta_{z}\tilde{v}_{\perp}
. Using the pointwise
bounds (
69
) and integrating, we find that

(91)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathcal{C}}f\tilde{v}_{\perp}\Delta_{z}\tilde{v}_{\perp}\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{v}_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}}(\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}+e^{-\tau^{\gamma}})

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+e^{-\tau^{\gamma}}),

for
\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}/2
,
where we can take
\lambda>0
arbitrarily small by taking
L_{0}

large. This deals with the
terms in
Q_{2}
other than
\tilde{v}^{2}
. We also have

(92)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}f\tilde{v}^{2}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathcal{C}}f(\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{2}+2\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}\tilde{v}_{\perp}+\tilde{v}_{\perp}^{2})\,d\mu,

so

(93)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathcal{C}}f\tilde{v}^{2}\,d\mu-\int_{\mathcal{C}}f\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{2}\,d\mu\right|\leq C\lambda\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}e^{-\tau^{\gamma}}.

The terms in
Q_{>2}
contain at least 3 factors of
\tilde{v}
or its
derivatives, which we estimate using the pointwise bounds (
69
), and that
\|\tilde{v}\|_{C^{4}}\leq\tau^{-\gamma}
on

B_{\tau^{\gamma}}
. Similarly the
\mathcal{R}_{\xi_{\tau}}
term can be
estimated using (
85
) and (
69
). We get

(94)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathcal{C}}f(Q_{>2}+\mathcal{R}_{\xi_{\tau}})\,d\mu\right|\leq C\tau^{-\gamma}\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lambda\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+e^{-\tau^{\gamma}}.

Finally, the cutoff term
\mathcal{E}
is bounded, and supported where
|x|\sim\tau^{\gamma}
. Combining everything, we find that for any
\lambda>0
we can arrange that for all
\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}/2
, if

is
chosen large enough, we have

(95)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\tau}\int_{\mathcal{C}}f\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}\,d\mu+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2(n-k)}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}f\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{2}\,d\mu\right|\leq C\lambda\|\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+e^{-\tau^{\gamma}}.

Note that we replaced
\tilde{v}
by
\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}
on the
left since
f\in\mathfrak{h}
, and on the right by the fact that the

L^{2}
-norm of
\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}
dominates the
L^{2}
-norm of

\tilde{v}
.

This is now a
finite dimensional problem. Following the calculation in

[
34
, Section 3]
or
[

, Lemma 5.6]
,
we can write it as the following ODE for the matrix

(96)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{d}{d\tau}A(\tau)+\nu_{n,k}A(\tau)^{2}\right\|\leq C\lambda\|A(\tau)\|^{2}+e^{-\tau^{\gamma}},

where
\nu_{n,k}>0
is a fixed constant.
We can arrange that
\lambda
is small enough, so that

. Moreover, note that on the interval
[T_{0},T_{0}+L_{0}]
we have,
by (

), that

(97)

\displaystyle A_{ii}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu_{n,k}^{-1}}{\tau}+O(\tau^{-3/2}),\text{ for }i\leq\ell,

\displaystyle A_{ii}

\displaystyle=O(\tau^{-3/2}),\text{ for }i>\ell,

\displaystyle A_{ij}

\displaystyle=O(\tau^{-3/2}),\text{ for }i\not=j.

If
T_{0}
is chosen sufficiently large, then we also have

e^{-\tau^{\gamma}}<\tau^{-3/2}
. Using this, together with the ODE
for

, we can use
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
to
find that if
\ell>0
, then for
\tau\geq T_{0}
we have

(98)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\tau^{-1}\leq\|A(\tau)\|\leq C\tau^{-1}

for a uniform constant

In particular this means that
\|\tilde{v}_{\mathfrak{h}}\|\sim\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}\sim\tau^{-1}
for all
\tau\geq T_{0}
. When
\ell=0
, then by (

) we have
exponential decay of
A(\tau)
until eventually (
75
)
begins to hold, and then the argument above still leads to
\|A(\tau)\|\leq C\tau^{-1}
for all
\tau\geq T_{0}
.

To get more precise
asymptotics we need to return to the original flow
M_{\tau}
, not
modified by rotations. From (
85
) we now have

|\xi_{\tau}|\leq C\tau^{-2}
, which implies that
|S_{\tau}-Id|\leq C\tau^{-1}
. Writing
M_{\tau}
as the graph of
u(x,\tau)
on

B_{\tau^{\gamma}}
, we now have the improved

-estimate

. From Proposition
12
we find that for small

\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}
we have

\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{4}(B_{\tau^{\gamma}})}\leq\tau^{2\gamma^{\prime}-1}
,
for
\tau\geq T_{0}+1
, if
T_{0}
is large enough.

Let
\tilde{u}=\chi u
as before, cutting off
on
B_{\tau^{\gamma}}
. This satisfies the equation

(99)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\tilde{u}=\Delta\tilde{u}-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}+a_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}+a_{2}\tilde{u}+\mathcal{E},

as before.
Consider the decomposition

(100)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}=\tilde{u}_{0}+\tilde{u}_{>0}+\tilde{u}_{<0}=\tilde{u}_{\mathfrak{h}}+\tilde{u}_{rot}+\tilde{u}_{>0}+\tilde{u}_{<0},

according to the eigenspaces of
\mathcal{L}
corresponding to

\mathfrak{h}
, rotations, positive, and negative eigenvalues

We first show that
\tilde{u}_{<0},\tilde{u}_{>0}
are of lower
order. We have

(101)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\tilde{u}_{>0}^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}}2\tilde{u}_{>0}(\mathcal{L}\tilde{u}_{>0}+Q(\tilde{u},\nabla\tilde{u},\nabla^{2}\tilde{u})+\mathcal{E})\,d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\tilde{u}_{>0}^{2}\,d\mu-C\tau^{8\gamma^{\prime}-4}-e^{-\tau^{\gamma}},

using that the smallest positive eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}
is

1/2
, and the term

is bounded by

(102)

\displaystyle C|\tilde{u}_{>0}\tau^{4\gamma^{\prime}-2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{u}_{>0}^{2}+C^{2}\tau^{8\gamma^{\prime}-4}.

Letting

(103)

\displaystyle p^{\prime}(\tau)\geq\frac{1}{2}p(\tau)-C\tau^{8\gamma^{\prime}-4},

for large
\tau
. Integrating this inequality from
\tau
to
\infty
,
and using that

, we find that

(104)

\displaystyle p(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{\frac{1}{2}(\tau-s)}s^{8\gamma^{\prime}-4}\,ds\leq C\tau^{8\gamma^{\prime}-4}.

It follows that for any
\gamma^{\prime}>0
we can arrange that

\|\tilde{u}_{>0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\tau^{-2+4\gamma^{\prime}}
for large
\tau
.

Similarly we have

(105)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\tilde{u}_{<0}^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq-c_{1}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\tilde{u}_{<0}^{2}\,d\mu+C\tau^{8\gamma^{\prime}-4}-e^{-\tau^{\gamma}},

where
c_{1}
is the smallest negative eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}
, so
q(\tau)=\|\tilde{u}_{<0}\|_{L^{2}}
satisfies

(106)

\displaystyle q^{\prime}(\tau)\leq-c_{1}q(\tau)+C\tau^{8\gamma^{\prime}-4}.

Now we integrate from
T_{0}
to
\tau
, and use that by
(
72
) we have
\|\tilde{u}_{<0}(T_{0})\|_{L^{2}}<2T_{0}^{-3/2}
. We find that

(107)

\displaystyle q(\tau)\leq 2T_{0}^{-3/2}e^{c_{1}(\tau-T_{0})}+\int_{T_{0}}^{\tau}e^{c_{1}(s-t)}s^{-4+8\gamma^{\prime}}\,ds.

If
L_{0}
is chosen sufficiently large, depending on
T_{0}
, then this
implies that
\|\tilde{u}_{<0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\tau^{-2+4\gamma^{\prime}}
for all
\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}/2
.

Now we consider the component
\tilde{u}_{rot}
in

\mathfrak{h}_{rot}
. Letting
h\in\mathfrak{h}_{rot}
be an element
with norm 1, we have

(108)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\int_{\mathcal{C}}h\tilde{u}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathcal{C}}h(Q_{2}+Q_{>2}+\mathcal{E})\,d\mu,

decomposing the nonlinear terms into quadratic and higher degree

as before, using the bound

\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{4}(B_{r^{\gamma}})}\leq\tau^{2\gamma^{\prime}-1}
:

(109)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathcal{C}}h(Q_{>2}+\mathcal{E})\,d\mu\right|\leq C\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-3}.

Let us substitute the decomposition (
100
) into the formula
(
89
) for
Q_{2}
. The terms arising purely from the

\tilde{u}_{rot}
component are orthogonal to
h
– this follows from
the fact that the Jacobi fields in
\mathfrak{h}_{rot}
are
integrable. One can check explicitly that the other terms arising from

\tilde{u}_{rot}
and
\tilde{u}_{\mathfrak{h}}
are also
orthogonal to

. The remaining terms in (

can all be bounded using
\|\tilde{u}_{>0}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\tilde{u}_{<0}\|_{L^{2}}

\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}/2

of order

, multiplied

\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{4}(B_{\tau^{\gamma}})}\leq\tau^{2\gamma^{\prime}-1}

(110)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\tau}\int_{\mathcal{C}}h\tilde{u}\,d\mu\right|\leq C\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-3}.

Integrating this, using that as

the

\|\tilde{u}_{rot}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-2}
, for
\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}/2
.

Finally we return to the component
\tilde{u}_{\mathfrak{h}}
, and
define the matrix
A(\tau)
as in (
87

. For

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\int_{\mathcal{C}}f\tilde{u}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathcal{C}}f(Q_{2}+Q_{>2}+\mathcal{E})\,d\mu.

We write

,
and use that

The terms arising from

\displaystyle C(\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}+e^{-\tau^{\gamma}})\leq C\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-3}.

As a consequence, instead of (

(113)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{d}{d\tau}A(\tau)+\nu_{n,k}A(\tau)^{2}\right\|\leq\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-3},

once

and taking

large

We can now argue similarly to
[
17
, Proposition 5.1]

to analyze the ODE (

, while in our case it is only

First we can show that for any
\kappa>0
, once
T_{0},L_{0}
are large enough the eigenvalues

\lambda_{i}(\tau)
of

in decreasing order satisfy

(114)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu_{n,k}^{-1}}{\tau}+O(\tau^{\kappa-2}),\text{ for }i\leq\ell,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\tau\lambda_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle>0,\text{ for }\ell<i\leq\ell^{\prime},

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=O(\tau^{\kappa-2}),\text{ for }i>\ell^{\prime},

for some
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
, and
all
\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}
. To see this, note that for
\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}/2
the eigenvalues satisfy the ODEs

(115)

\displaystyle|\lambda^{\prime}(\tau)+\nu_{n,k}\lambda(\tau)^{2}|\leq\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-3},

where

are large). Instead of arguing like

use barriers. The functions

(116)

\displaystyle g_{\pm}(\tau)=\nu_{n,k}^{-1}\tau^{-1}\pm(\tau-L_{0}/2)^{7\gamma^{\prime}-2}

are sufficiently large. At the same time, by (

(117)

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{i}(T_{0}+L_{0}/2)-\frac{\nu_{n,k}^{-1}}{T_{0}+L_{0}/2}\right|<(T_{0}+L_{0}/2)^{-3/2},\text{ for }i\leq\ell,

so we have

(118)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(T_{0}+L_{0}/2)\leq g_{+}(T_{0}+L_{0}/2),

if
L_{0}
is sufficiently large, depending on
T_{0}
and
\gamma^{\prime}
. By the

g_{+}
, this inequality then holds for all

\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}/2
as well. If
\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}
, then

(119)

\displaystyle g_{+}(\tau)\leq\nu_{n,k}^{-1}\tau^{-1}+\tau^{8\gamma^{\prime}-2},

if

is sufficiently large, and in particular the upper bound
in (

if

.

To estimate the
\lambda_{i}
for

satisfies
(

\displaystyle(\tau\lambda)^{\prime}\leq\tau^{-1}(\tau\lambda-\tau^{2}\nu_{n,k}\lambda^{2}+\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-1}).

It follows that if
\tau_{0}\lambda(\tau_{0})\leq-\tau_{0}^{6\gamma^{\prime}-1}

for some
\tau_{0}
, then

|\tau\lambda|
is increasing, and this inequality persists for all

. Eventually this leads to

, which contradicts that our flow

converges to

. It follows that we have

for all
\tau\geq T_{0}+L_{0}/2
.

To bound the
\lambda_{i}
from above for

, consider the

(121)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}(\tau)\geq-\nu\lambda(\tau)^{2}-\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-3}.

Then

(122)

\displaystyle(\tau\lambda)^{\prime}\geq\tau^{-1}(\tau\lambda-\nu(\tau\lambda)^{2}-\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-1}).

If at some time
\tau_{0}
we have the two conditions
\tau_{0}\lambda(\tau_{0})>2\tau_{0}^{6\gamma^{\prime}-1}
and

, then

\tau\lambda(\tau)
is increasing, and it keeps increasing until

, and this inequality then persists for all

. If this happens for

with some

,
then according to the normal form (

of

at infinity is

-degenerate for some

. This is the alternative (i) in

The other possibility is that
\tau\lambda(\tau)\leq 2\tau^{6\gamma^{\prime}-1}
for all

, which applied to the

for

Once the bounds for the eigenvalues are established, one can consider

4.
Geometric normal form

In the previous section we identified the second term in the normal
form for a rescaled flow
M_{\tau}
with a cylindrical singularity at
infinity, in an
L^{2}
sense. Recall that for
any
\beta>0
the
contribution of the region outside of the
\tau^{\beta}
-ball to the

L^{2}
-norm of the graphicality function
u
in
Proposition
14
is negligible compared to

\tau^{-1}
, so the
L^{2}
normal form does not immediately say anything
about the geometry of
M_{\tau}
outside of such balls.
Our goal in this section is to obtain good graphicality of
M_{\tau}

over suitable models over balls of size

B_{K_{0}\sqrt{\tau}}
for large
K_{0}>0
, where in practice we can take
K_{0}=20
. The corresponding asymptotic result was obtained in the

C^{1}
-normal form result of Sun-Xue
[
32
, Theorem 1.3]
, but we
need a more quantitative version. We will also prove the exponential
decay of the derivatives
\partial_{y_{q}}u
in the degenerate

y_{q}
-directions in Proposition
26
, which will play an
important role later.

As explained in
[
1
]
,
[
32
]
,
near the boundary
|x|=K_{0}\tau^{1/2}
of the ball
B_{K_{0}\tau^{1/2}}
the flow can
no longer be expected to have good graphicality over

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
, and we must use a corrected model. For the moment
consider the setting of a nondegenerate

\mathcal{C}_{n,1}
-singularity, i.e. a nondegenerate neck-pinch of
hypersurfaces modeled on the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times S^{n-1}
. It was
observed by Angenent-Velázquez
[
1
]
(see also
Sun-Xue
[
32
, Theorem 1.3]
) that to leading order,
near
|y|=K_{0}\sqrt{\tau}
as
\tau\to\infty
, the correct asymptotic
behavior is to consider the graph of

(124)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2(n-1)}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{y^{2}}{2\tau}}-1\right)

over
\mathcal{C}_{n,1}
. In
[
1
]
it is briefly discussed how
one can construct a rotationally symmetric flow with a nondegenerate
neck-pinch by further refining this approximate solution, in analogy
to their construction of degenerate neck-pinches. As far as we know
this construction has not been carried out in detail,
however. Moreover we also need analogous “model flows” for
nondegenerate
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
-singularities with
k>1
.

In this paper we will use an indirect inductive argument to show that
flows developing non-degenerate
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
-singularities
exist for all
n,k
, see Proposition
45
. For each

n,k
we fix one such flow as our model, and use that asymptotically
as
\tau\to\infty
the geometry of such non-degenerate singularities
is described by the
C^{1}
-normal form of
[
32
]
. Taking a product
with a Euclidean factor we obtain suitable
\ell
-degenerate models as
well. We will then show that arbitrary
\ell
-degenerate flows have
good graphical behavior over these models.

Eventually we will work by induction on the dimension

n
, and for now we assume that for all
n^{\prime}<n
and
0<k^{\prime}<n^{\prime}
we
have fixed a mean curvature flow
\mathcal{N}^{n^{\prime},k^{\prime}}_{t}

which develops a nondegenerate
\mathcal{C}_{n^{\prime},k^{\prime}}
-singularity at

(0,0)
. We write
\mathcal{M}^{n^{\prime},k^{\prime}}_{\tau}
for the corresponding
rescaled flow. Let us recall the
H^{1}
and
C^{1}
normal form theorems
due to Sun-Xue
[
32
, Theorem 1.3]
. These say that for any
K_{0}>0

and
\gamma>0
there exists a
T_{0}(K_{0},\gamma)
, such that
for
\tau\geq T_{0}
we can write
\mathcal{N}^{n^{\prime},k^{\prime}}_{\tau}
as the
graph of
U(x,\tau)
over
\mathcal{C}_{n^{\prime},k^{\prime}}
on the ball

B_{K_{0}\sqrt{\tau}}
, satisfying

(125)

\displaystyle\left\|U(x,\tau)-\frac{\sqrt{2(n^{\prime}-k^{\prime})}}{4\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}(y_{i}^{2}-2)\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq\tau^{\gamma-2},

and

(126)

\displaystyle\left\|U(x,\tau)-\sqrt{2(n^{\prime}-k^{\prime})}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{2\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}(y_{i}^{2}-2)}-1\right)\right\|_{C^{1}}\leq\tau^{\gamma-1}.

Note that
|U(x,\tau)|
is not small, since it is of order
K_{0}

near the boundary of
B_{K_{0}\sqrt{\tau}}
, but
|\nabla_{x}U(x,\tau)|\lesssim K_{0}\tau^{-1/2}
, so for large
\tau
the model is very close
to a cylinder of radius
\sqrt{2(n^{\prime}-k^{\prime})}+U((y_{0},z_{0}),\tau)
on the region
where
|y-y_{0}|\ll\tau^{1/2}
.
The following statements result from this description. Let us define the function

(127)

\displaystyle F(\xi)=\sqrt{1+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}}.

We emphasize that the following result refers to a specific model flow

\mathcal{M}^{n,k,\ell}_{\tau}

25
we will show that any rescaled flow

\ell
-degenerate cylindrical singularity will have similar

-norm of the graphicality function.

\displaystyle\Delta-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla+|A|^{2}=\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4\tau}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}(y_{i}^{2}-2)\right)(2\Delta_{z}+1)+O(\tau^{-19/10}).

To see the more precise formula (

\displaystyle w=\frac{\sqrt{2(n^{\prime}-k^{\prime})}}{4\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}(y_{i}^{2}-2)+O(\tau^{-19/10})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}-\frac{2w}{\sqrt{2(n^{\prime}-k^{\prime})}}\Delta_{z}+\frac{\sqrt{2(n^{\prime}-k^{\prime})}}{2}\nabla w\cdot\nabla+O(\tau^{-19/10})

Proposition 35
.

For sufficiently small

Up to translation and rotation, we can assume that

\ell

5.

\ell

Proposition 37

Similarly to Proposition

Proposition 38

Given

(212)

\displaystyle\|v(\cdot,T+1)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R(T+1)})}\geq e^{\frac{1}{2}-\lambda}\|v(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R(T)})},

(213)

\displaystyle\|v(\cdot,T+2)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R(T+2)})}\geq e^{\frac{1}{2}-\lambda}\|v(\cdot,T+1)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R(T+1)})}.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction, similarly to Proposition
31
.
Suppose that we have a sequence
N^{i}_{\tau}
, and
T_{i}\to\infty

on satisfy

(214)

\displaystyle\|v^{i}(\cdot,T_{i})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda)}\|v^{i}(\cdot,T_{i}+1)\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|v^{i}(\cdot,T_{i}+2)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda)}\|v^{i}(\cdot,T_{i}+1)\|_{L^{2}}.

Each

We define

Similarly to before, Proposition

∎

Although we did not make this explicit in the proof, note that the

\ell

The following is the main ingredient that we use to perturb away
degenerate singularities. Here we will denote by

Proposition 39
.

Let
c_{1}\in(0,1)
. Choose
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{0}/4
for the
\kappa_{0}
in

\epsilon_{0},T_{0}

are chosen according to Proposition

\lambda=\kappa_{1},2\kappa_{1},3\kappa_{1},1/4

T_{1}\geq T_{0}
there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(c_{1},T_{1})
with the following property.
Suppose that the flow
N_{\tau}
satisfies the following conditions
for some
\gamma>0
:

(i)

For
\tau\geq T_{1}
, as long as
c_{1}^{-1}\gamma T_{1}e^{\tau-T_{1}}<\epsilon_{0}
,

N_{\tau}
is the graph of
v
over
M_{\tau}
on the

10\tau^{1/2}
-balls, and
\|v\|_{C^{4}}\leq c_{1}^{-1}T_{1}\gamma e^{\tau-T_{1}}
. Suppose that
\gamma
is small
enough so that this holds in
particular for
\tau\in[T_{1},3T_{1}]
, i.e.
\gamma<\epsilon_{0}c_{1}T_{1}^{-1}e^{-2T_{1}}

(ii)

\|v(\cdot,T_{1})\|_{10T_{1}^{1/2}}\geq c_{1}\gamma
.

(iii)

\|v(\cdot,T_{1}+1)\|_{10(T_{1}+1)^{1/2}}\geq e^{\frac{1}{2}-\kappa_{1}}\|v(\cdot,T_{1})\|_{10T_{1}^{1/2}}

Let

or

(i.e. it is only nonzero in the

(n,k)

Proof.

The proof is somewhat similar to
[
33

, but it

to
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}

(1)

To start,
we will use Proposition
38
on
10\tau^{1/2}
-balls
to propagate the growth of

until we find a time
T_{2}
, where

\|v(\cdot,T_{2}+1)\|>C_{2}e^{-\kappa_{0}T_{2}}
for some large

dominates

\partial_{y_{q}}u
for any degenerate direction

.

is the graph of
u+v
over the model flow

\mathcal{M}^{n,k,\ell}_{\tau}

N_{\tau}
with the translated flow

. In
[

the argument

to

\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
was exponentially small.

(2)

Next we need to show
that the distance from
N^{\prime}_{\tau}
to
M_{\tau}
keeps growing.
We can continue to apply
Proposition
38
to the translated flow
N^{\prime}_{\tau}
, as long as we
maintain sufficient graphicality over
M_{\tau}
on the

9\tau^{1/2}

(3)

At this point the

T_{4}

cannot have a

-cylindrical singularity (possibly a rotation

Step 1.

\epsilon_{0}

M_{\tau}
on the

\tau\in[T_{1},3T_{1}]

(215)

\displaystyle\|v(\cdot,T_{1}+k+1)\|_{R(T_{1}+k+1)}\geq e^{\frac{1}{2}-\kappa_{1}}\|v(\cdot,T_{1}+k)\|_{R(T_{1}+k)},

. For simplicity let us work with a fixed

\displaystyle|x-f(x)X_{1}|^{2}\leq(1+\delta)|x|^{2}+C_{\delta}|X_{1}|^{2},

\displaystyle e^{|x-f(x)X_{1}|^{2}/8p}\leq e^{C|X_{1}|^{2}}e^{|x|^{2}/8p^{\prime}}.

7.
Appendix

In this Appendix we give the proof of
Proposition
7
, following the argument in

[
14
]
. For convenience we restate the proposition here.

Proposition 47
.

Suppose that
M_{\tau}
is a rescaled mean curvature flow of compact
hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and for
some
\epsilon\geq 0
, the nonnegative function

f(x,\tau)
satisfies the differential inequality

(265)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}f\leq\Delta f-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla f+a\cdot\nabla f+bf+\mathcal{E},

where
a(x,\tau)
and
b(x,\tau)
satisfy
|a|\leq\epsilon<1
and

|b|\leq b_{0}
, and
\mathcal{E}(x,\tau)\geq 0
.
Then we have

(266)

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{p(\tau)}}\leq e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})\tau}\|f(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})(\tau-s)}\|\mathcal{E}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p(s)}}\,ds,

where we define
p(\tau)=1+\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)e^{\tau}
(so
that
p(0)=2
). The
L^{p}
norms here are taken using the weight

(4\pi)^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/4}
.

Proof.

Let us recall the
sharp log-Sobolev inequality for submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

proved by Brendle
[
4
]
: for any positive smooth function
on
M_{\tau}
we have

(267)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\tau}}\phi\log\phi\,d\mu

\displaystyle-\int_{M_{\tau}}\phi|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\,d\mu-\int_{M_{\tau}}\phi\left|H+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M_{\tau}}\phi\,d\mu\right)\log\int_{M_{\tau}}\phi\,d\mu.

Let us compute the evolution of the
L^{p}
-norm of
f
, where

p=p(\tau)
also depends on
\tau
. We have

(268)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\left(e^{-|x|^{2}/4}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{x}{2}\cdot\left(H+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right)e^{-|x|^{2}/4}d\mathcal{H}^{n}-e^{-|x|^{2}/4}H\cdot\left(H+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle=-\left|H+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}\,e^{-|x|^{2}/4}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},

and

(269)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}f^{p}

\displaystyle=pf^{p-1}\partial_{\tau}f+\dot{p}\log f\,f^{p}

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{\tau}}\left(pf^{p-1}\partial_{\tau}f+\dot{p}\log f\,f^{p}-f^{p}\left|H+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}\right)d\mu,

so

(270)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\|f\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tau}\left(\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\|f\|_{p}^{1-p}\,\partial_{\tau}\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}d\mu-\frac{\dot{p}}{p^{2}}\left(\log\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}d\mu\right)\|f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\|f\|_{p}^{1-p}\int_{M_{\tau}}\dot{p}\log f\,f^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle+\|f\|_{p}^{1-p}\int_{M_{\tau}}\Big(f^{p-1}(\Delta f-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla f+a\cdot\nabla f+bf+\mathcal{E})-\frac{1}{p}f^{p}|H+x^{\perp}/2|^{2}\Big)\,d\mu

\displaystyle-\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\|f\|_{p}\log\|f\|_{p},

where we used the differential inequality for
\partial_{\tau}f
.
Integrating by parts we have

(271)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p-1}\Delta f\,e^{-|x|^{2}/4}d\mathcal{H}=\int_{M_{\tau}}\Big(-(p-1)f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}+f^{p-1}\nabla f\cdot\frac{x}{2}\Big)e^{-|x|^{2}/4}\,d\mathcal{H},

so

(272)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p-1}(\Delta f-\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla f)d\mu=-(p-1)\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mu.

From the log-Sobolev inequality (
267
) we get

(273)

\displaystyle p\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}\log f\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}\right)\log\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle+\int_{M_{\tau}}p^{2}f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}\left|H+x^{\perp}/2\right|^{2}\,d\mu.

Using this in our formula for
\partial_{\tau}\|f\|_{p}
we get

(274)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\|f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\dot{p}}{p^{2}}\|f\|_{p}^{1-p}\left(p\|f\|_{p}^{p}\log\|f\|_{p}+\int_{M_{\tau}}\left(p^{2}f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}+f^{p}|H+x^{\perp}/2|^{2}\right)d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p}^{1-p}\int_{M_{\tau}}-(p-1)f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}+\epsilon f^{p-1}|\nabla f|+b_{0}f^{p}+f^{p-1}\mathcal{E}\,d\mu

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p}\|f\|_{p}^{1-p}\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}|H+x^{\perp}/2|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle-\frac{\dot{p}}{p}\|f\|_{p}\log\|f\|_{p}.

We estimate

(275)

\displaystyle\epsilon f^{p-1}|\nabla f|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}f^{p},

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p-1}\mathcal{E}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{M_{\tau}}\mathcal{E}^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}=\|f\|_{p}^{p-1}\|\mathcal{E}\|_{p}.

Using this in the calculation above, and simplifying, we get

(276)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\|f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dot{p}-(p-1-\epsilon)\right)\|f\|_{p}^{1-p}\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\dot{p}}{p^{2}}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\|f\|_{p}^{1-p}\int_{M_{\tau}}f^{p}|H+x^{\perp}/2|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle+(b_{0}+\epsilon)\|f\|_{p}+\|\mathcal{E}\|_{p}.

We set
p(t)=1+\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)e^{\tau}
so that
\dot{p}=p-1-\epsilon
and
p(0)=2
. Then we get

(277)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\|f\|_{p}\leq(b_{0}+\epsilon)\|f\|_{p}+\|\mathcal{E}\|_{p}.

Integrating this differential inequality from 0 to
\tau
we get

(278)

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{p(\tau)}}\leq e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})\tau}\|f(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\epsilon+b_{0})(\tau-s)}\|\mathcal{E}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p(s)}}\,ds,

as required.
∎
