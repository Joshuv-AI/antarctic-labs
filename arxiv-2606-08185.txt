Geometry of Holomorphic One-forms on Smooth Projective Varieties
Jiabin Du, Feng Hao, Haoyuan Li, Zichang Wang

Abstract
. In this article, we show that any morphism f f from a smooth projective variety X X to a simple abelian variety A A is smooth, if and only if there exists a holomorphic 1-form ω \omega on A A such that f ∗ ​ ω f^{*}\omega has no zero. As the key ingredient in the proof, we show any ℤ \mathbb{Z} -homology fibre bundle morphism is without blow-up in codimension 0 in the sense of Sabbah. Furthermore, we investigate the structure of the spaces of holomorphic 1-forms with zeros, and show that they are linear for large classes of varieties. Also, we construct a delicate example of a smooth projective subvariety of an abelian variety for which the holomorphic 1-forms with positive dimensional zero loci do not form a linear subset. Finally, we study algebraic surfaces admitting holomorphic 1-forms that have zeros and do not arise from cohomology jump loci.

Holomorphic 1-form is a basic concept in algebraic geometry and occupies a central position in analyzing the geometry and topology of irregular varieties. The space of holomorphic 1-forms governs the morphisms from varieties to abelian varieties, especially the Albanese maps, so the information encoded in holomorphic 1-forms should potentially reveal many structural properties of irregular varieties. be the graph of

3.
Linearity of the space of holomorphic 1-forms with zeros

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. For any holomorphic 1-form
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
,
Z(\omega)
is denoted to be the set-theoretic zero loci of
\omega
in
X
. We recall the following algebraic invariant introduced by Budur-Wang-Yoon
[
2
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
i
-th lower level set
of
X
is defined to be

W^{i}(X):=\{\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})\ |\ \codim Z(\omega)\leq i\}.

For a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
n
,
W^{n}(X)
is the whole space of holomorphic 1-forms with zeros on
X
. Inside of
W^{i}(X)
, one has subsets arising from the tangent cone of the cohomology jump loci, which is very well studied,

V^{i}(X):=\{\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})\ |\ (H^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{C}}),\wedge\omega)\text{ is not exact at }H^{j}(X,{\mathbb{C}})\text{ for }j\leq i\}.

Consider the character variety
\Hom(\pi_{1}(X),{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})
of
X
which is a direct product of
({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{b_{1}(X)}
with a finite group. Let
\mathcal{V}^{i}(X)
be the subset of
\Hom(\pi_{1}(X),{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})
consisting of rank one local systems
\mathcal{L}
on
X
such that
H^{j}(X,\mathcal{L})\neq 0
for
j\leq i
. By the tangent cone theorem
[
8
, Theorem A]
,
V^{i}(X)
is the intersection of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
with the tangent cone of
\mathcal{V}^{i}(X)
at the trivial local system. Therefore,
V^{i}(X)
is
linear
, i.e., a union of finitely many vector subspaces in
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
(See
[
1
]
and
[
25
]
).

One can show that
V^{i}(X)\subseteq W^{i}(X)
. In fact, suppose by contradiction that there is a holomorphic 1-form
\omega\in V^{i}(X)
and
\omega\notin W^{i}(X)
. Then
\codim Z(\omega)>i
. By
[
11
, Proposition 3.4]
,
(H^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{C}}),\wedge\omega)
is exact at any degree
\leq i
, which is a contradiction.

Motivated by the linearity of
V^{n}(X)
, it is natural to ask whether
W^{n}(X)
is linear or not
[
9
, Question 1.10]
. There are some known cases. For example, if
X
is of general type or has nonzero topological/holomorphic Euler characteristic, then
W^{n}(X)
is linear (see
[
20
]
and
[
11
]
). If the Albanese map of
X
is finite, then
W^{n}(X)
is linear by
[
9
]
. Moreover, if
X
is a subvariety of an abelian variety, then
W^{n}(X)
is linear by
[
27
]
. Based on works of
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
, we obtain the linearity property for any smooth good minimal models.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
X
is a smooth projective good minimal model (i.e.,
K_{X}
is semiample) of dimension
n
, then

W^{n}(X)=\{\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})~|~Z(\omega)\neq\emptyset\}

is a vector subspace of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
.

Proof.

If every holomorphic 1-form on
X
has zeros, then
W^{n}(X)=H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
. Now we may assume that
X
admits a nowhere vanishing holomorphic 1-form. Then by
[
4
]
or
[
14
]
, there exists an isotrivial smooth fibration
f\colon X\to A
where
A
is a positive dimensional abelian variety. Moreover, there exists a finite
étale cover
A^{\prime}\to A
such that the base change of
X
along
A^{\prime}\to A
splits, that is,
X^{\prime}:=X\times_{A}A^{\prime}\cong Y\times A^{\prime}
, where
Y
is a smooth projective variety. Since
K_{X^{\prime}}
is semiample, it follows that
K_{Y}
is semiample.
Iterating the above process, we can assume that there exists a finite étale cover
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
such that
\widetilde{X}\cong\widetilde{Y}\times B
, where
\widetilde{Y}
is a smooth projective variety without nowhere vanishing holomorphic 1-forms, and
B
is a positive dimensional abelian variety. Denote by
p_{1}:\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{Y}
the projection to the first factor. Since any nonzero holomorphic 1-form on
B
has no zeros, we have
W^{n}(\widetilde{X})=p_{1}^{*}H^{0}(\widetilde{Y},\Omega_{\widetilde{Y}}^{1})
, which is a linear subspace of
H^{0}(\widetilde{X},\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{1})
. To show that
W^{n}(X)\subset H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
is a linear subspace, it suffices to verify that
W^{n}(X)
is closed under addition. Since
\pi
is étale, for any two holomorphic 1-forms
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in W^{n}(X)
,
\pi^{*}(\omega_{1}),\pi^{*}(\omega_{2})\in W^{n}(\widetilde{X})
. As
W^{n}(\widetilde{X})
is a linear subspace, we have
\pi^{*}(\omega_{1})+\pi^{*}(\omega_{2})=\pi^{*}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})\in W^{n}(\widetilde{X})
. Using again that
\pi
is étale we conclude
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\in W^{n}(X)
.
∎

For varieties
X
with simple Albanese varieties, we obtain the linearity property of
X
by Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\colon X\to A
be a morphism from an
n
-dimensional smooth projective variety
X
to a simple abelian variety
A
, then
W^{n}(X)\cap f^{*}H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A}^{1})=f^{*}H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A}^{1})
or
\{0\}
. In particular, if the Albanese variety of
X
is simple, then
W^{n}(X)=H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
or
\{0\}
.

Proof.

This trivially follows from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

In general, for an
n
-dimensional smooth projective variety
X
,
W^{n}(X)
is not always a vector subspace.

Example 3.4
.

Let
Y=E_{1}\times E_{2}\times Z
, where
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
are two non-isogenous elliptic curves,
Z
is a simply connected smooth projective variety of dimension
n-2
. Let
p,q\in Z
be two distinct closed points, and let
C_{1}=E_{1}\times\{e_{2}\}\times\{p\}
,
C_{2}=\{e_{1}\}\times E_{2}\times\{q\}
. Let
\rho:X\to Y
be the blow-up of
Y
along
C_{1}\cup C_{2}
. Then we have

W^{n}(X)=p_{1}^{*}H^{0}(X,\Omega_{E_{1}}^{1})\cup p_{2}^{*}H^{0}(X,\Omega_{E_{2}}^{1}),

where
p_{i}\colon X\to E_{i}

(i=1,2)
are the natural projections. In particular,
W^{n}(X)
is a linear subset of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
.

Remark 3.5
.

In general, the structure of
W^{n}(X)
is very sensitive to the birational modifications involved in the minimal model program from a smooth projective variety to its minimal models.

For the remaining part of this section, we discuss the linearity of
W^{n}(X)
for
n=\dim X\leq 3
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X
be an n-dimensional smooth projective variety and
C\subset X
be a smooth projective subvariety of dimension
d
. Let
Y=\Bl_{C}X
be the blow-up of
X
along
C
. If
W^{n}(X)
and
W^{d}(C)
are both linear, then so is
W^{n}(Y)
.

Proof.

For the blow-up morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
, we have the following two simple observations. We leave the proof to the reader.

(a)

the set
\{\eta\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})|Z(\eta|_{C})\neq\emptyset\}
is a linear subspace of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
;

(b)

for
\pi^{\ast}\omega\in H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{1})
,
\pi^{\ast}\omega
has zeros along
\pi^{-1}(C)
if and only if
Z(\omega|_{C})\neq\emptyset
.

Then by these observations, we have

\displaystyle W^{n}(Y)=

\displaystyle\{\pi^{\ast}\omega\in\pi^{\ast}H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})\ |\ Z(\pi^{\ast}\omega)\cap(Y-\pi^{-1}(C))\neq\emptyset\}

\displaystyle\cup\{\pi^{\ast}\omega\in\pi^{\ast}H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})\ |\ Z(\pi^{\ast}\omega)\cap\pi^{-1}(C)\neq\emptyset\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{\ast}\{\omega\ |\ Z(\omega)\cap(X-C)\neq\emptyset\}\cup\pi^{\ast}\{\omega\ |\ Z(\omega|_{C})\neq\emptyset\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{\ast}W^{n}(X)\cup\pi^{\ast}\{\omega\ |\ Z(\omega|_{C})\neq\emptyset\}.

Hence
W^{n}(Y)
is linear.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with
\dim X\leq 3
, then
W^{\dim X}(X)
is linear.

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem
3.2
, Lemma
3.6
,
[
24
, Corollary 3.1]
, and
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
.
∎

4.
Non-linearity of lower level sets and Non-formal 1-forms

In this section, we give an example for a smooth projective subvariety of an abelian variety with a nonlinear lower level set. Moreover, we study the related holomorphic 1-forms that have zeros and do not come from cohomology jump loci, and smooth projective surfaces with this kind of holomorphic 1-forms.

D^{2}<0
. It follows that
\dim Z(f^{\ast}\omega)=1
and
f^{\ast}D\subset Z(f^{\ast}\omega)
. Hence there is an irreducible component
\widetilde{D}\subset f^{\ast}D
with
\widetilde{D}^{2}<0
, which is a contradiction since the higher dimensional zero locus of a holomorphic 1-form on
C_{1}\times C_{2}
is either horizontal or vertical with respect to projections to its factors, which has non-negative self-intersection numbers.
∎
