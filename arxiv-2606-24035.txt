Kink Localization in Diffusion-Based Speech Enhancement: A Variational-Flow Analysis of StoRM under Noise-Power Mismatch
Shuubham Ojha

Abstract
Diffusion-based speech enhancement architectures that pair a deterministic predictor with a learned score network, exhibit a sharp non-smooth transition (“kink”) in the SI-SDR degradation curve at the training-time noise amplitude. We give a pathwise variational-flow analysis that localizes this non-smoothness to the predictor stage. The central identity is an exact factorization of the parametric sensitivity, ∂ s ^ ( M ) / ∂ M = K ⁡ ( M ) ⋅ ∂ C M / ∂ M \partial\widehat{s}^{(M)}/\partial M=K(M)\cdot\partial C_{M}/\partial M , where K ⁡ ( M ) K(M) is a continuous matrix-valued functional of the score Jacobian along the reverse trajectory and C M = Π ⁡ ( y ( M ) ) C_{M}=\Pi(y^{(M)}) is the predictor output. Under three hypotheses on the reverse-process flow (score-Jacobian continuity, conditioning-Jacobian continuity, non-degeneracy of K K ), failure of M ↦ s ^ ( M ) M\mapsto\widehat{s}^{(M)} to be C 1 C^{1} at M ∗ M^{\ast} holds if and only if M ↦ Π ⁡ ( y ( M ) ) M\mapsto\Pi(y^{(M)}) fails to be C 1 C^{1} at M ∗ M^{\ast} . We extend the localization to the finite-step Euler–Maruyama sampler actually run at inference. The hypotheses translate into a concrete experimental program; this paper specifies the program and presents the variational structure. The empirical validation is deferred to a companion experimental report.



1
Introduction

Score-based diffusion models for speech enhancement have converged on a hybrid architecture in which a deterministic predictor produces an initial estimate that initializes and conditions a learned reverse diffusion. StoRM
(
Lemercier et al. 2023
)
is the canonical instance: a predictor network
\Pi
produces
C=\Pi(y)
from the noisy observation
y
, and the reverse SDE is run on a learned score
s_{\theta}(x,t,C)
with
C
as conditioning and
X_{T}=C+\sigma_{T}\varepsilon
as initialization. This decomposition has delivered substantial empirical gains over predictor-only and score-only baselines on standard benchmarks
(
Lemercier et al. 2023
;
Richter et al. 2023
)
.

The behavior of such architectures under
distributional shift
, specifically under perturbations of the noise power away from the training condition, is poorly understood theoretically. Empirically, the SI-SDR degradation curve as a function of noise scaling exhibits a striking phenomenon: a sharp non-smooth transition at the highest training-time noise amplitude that we call a
kink
. The transition is consistent across structurally diverse noise types (six in our experimental setup) and is absent from predictor-removed and score-network-removed ablations of the same architecture trained on the same data.

This paper asks:
which component of the StoRM architecture produces the kink, and why does it appear at the training amplitude
? We give a partial answer to the first question: the kink is localized to the predictor stage through a pathwise variational analysis of the reverse SDE.

Approach.

Rather than compare the learned reverse process to an oracle reverse process via path-space KL divergence (the standard tool for diffusion-model generalization bounds;
Chen et al. 2022
), we differentiate the learned reverse process with respect to the noise-scaling parameter
M
and analyze its parametric sensitivity directly. The reverse process depends on
M
only through the predictor output
C_{M}
, and parameter-dependence of ODE flows gives an exact factorization of the output sensitivity into a downstream amplification matrix
K(M)
(determined by the score Jacobian) and the upstream predictor sensitivity
\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
. The multiplicative form of this factorization yields an iff localization of non-smoothness, in contrast to the one-sided bounds produced by additive Girsanov-style decompositions.

Contributions.

1.

We derive an exact pathwise factorization
V_{0}=K(M)\cdot\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
for the parametric sensitivity of a StoRM output to the noise-scaling parameter (Lemma
1
).

2.

Under three hypotheses on the reverse-process flow, we state an iff localization theorem (Theorem
2
) attributing non-smoothness of the enhancement output to non-smoothness of the predictor map. The hypotheses translate into measurable quantities along the reverse trajectory.

3.

We extend the localization to the discrete-time Euler–Maruyama sampler (Corollary
4
), closing the gap between the continuous-time theorem and the discrete-time experimental object.

4.

We specify a complete experimental program (§
6
) that substantiates the hypotheses and rules out discretization as the cause of the kink. Each table and measurement is enumerated; existing data substantiates one of the four required pillars, and three additional measurements are identified.

2
Setup

2.1
Notation and the three-process formulation

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote a clean speech signal and
n\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
an additive noise sample. For a noise-scaling parameter
M>0
define the noisy observation

y^{(M)}\;:=\;s+\sqrt{M}\,n,

(1)

so that
M=1
recovers the training-time noise level. We hold
s
and
n
fixed throughout; all
M
-dependence is parametric.

The predictor map
\Pi
produces an initial estimate

C_{M}\;:=\;\Pi(y^{(M)}),

(2)

which is used both to initialize the reverse SDE and as a conditioning input to the score network. The StoRM reverse SDE is

\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{(M)}\;=\;b_{\theta}\!\left(X_{t}^{(M)},t,C_{M}\right)\mathrm{d}t\;+\;g(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\qquad t\in[T,0],

(3)

where the drift decomposes as
b_{\theta}(x,t,C)=f(x,t)-g(t)^{2}\,s_{\theta}(x,t,C)
,
s_{\theta}
is the learned score,
W_{t}
is a Brownian motion held fixed across
M
(synchronous coupling), and the initial condition is
X_{T}^{(M)}=C_{M}+\sigma_{T}\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
held fixed.

The enhanced output is
\widehat{s}^{(M)}:=X_{0}^{(M)}
.

2.2
Object of interest

The SI-SDR degradation curve is a deterministic functional of the enhancement output:

\Psi(M)\;:=\;\mathrm{SI\text{-}SDR}\!\left(\widehat{s}^{(M)},\,s\right).

(4)

A
kink
in
\Psi
at
M^{\ast}
is a failure of
\Psi
to be
C^{1}
at
M^{\ast}
:
\Psi
is continuous at
M^{\ast}
but
\Psi^{\prime}
has a jump. Since
\mathrm{SI\text{-}SDR}(\cdot,s)
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, kinks in
\Psi
at non-degenerate operating points are inherited from kinks in
M\mapsto\widehat{s}^{(M)}
. We work with the latter and recover statements about
\Psi
by the chain rule.

2.3
Structural assumption

Assumption 1

(Single-channel
M
-dependence)
.

The drift
b_{\theta}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
, the diffusion coefficient
g(\cdot)
, the schedule
\sigma_{\cdot}
, and the Brownian motion
W_{\cdot}
are
M
-independent as functions of their explicit arguments. The map
M\mapsto C_{M}
is the unique channel through which
M
enters the reverse dynamics.

Assumption
1
is the structural fact that powers the factorization in Lemma
1
; an explicit
\partial_{M}b_{\theta}
term would produce additional forcing that does not factor through the predictor map. Verification that the noise schedule and architecture used in our experiments satisfy Assumption
1
is part of the experimental program (§
6
, item E1).

Assumption 2

(Regularity)
.

The drift
b_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is jointly
C^{2}
in
(x,C)
, with
\nabla_{x}b_{\theta}
,
\nabla_{C}b_{\theta}
, and their derivatives bounded on the compact set traced out by the trajectories
\{(X_{t}^{(M)},t,C_{M}):t\in[0,T],\,M\in\mathcal{M}\}
for any compact
\mathcal{M}\subset[1,\infty)
.

3
Variational-flow analysis

Under Assumption
2
, the flow
M\mapsto X_{t}^{(M)}
is
C^{1}
in
M
on any subset where
M\mapsto C_{M}
is
C^{1}
(standard parameter-dependence for ODEs;
Hartman 2002
, Ch. V). Define the sensitivity process

V_{t}\;:=\;\frac{\partial X_{t}^{(M)}}{\partial M}.

(5)

Lemma 1

(Variational identity)
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, the sensitivity process satisfies the linear ODE

\frac{\mathrm{d}V_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}\;=\;\nabla_{x}b_{\theta}\!\left(X_{t}^{(M)},t,C_{M}\right)V_{t}\;+\;\nabla_{C}b_{\theta}\!\left(X_{t}^{(M)},t,C_{M}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{M}}{\mathrm{d}M},

(6)

with terminal condition
V_{T}=\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
. Let
\Phi(t,\tau)
denote the state-transition matrix of the homogeneous part
\dot{Z}=\nabla_{x}b_{\theta}\,Z
along the trajectory, with
\Phi(\tau,\tau)=I
. Then

V_{0}\;=\;\underbrace{\left[\Phi(0,T)\;+\;\int_{T}^{0}\Phi(0,\tau)\,\nabla_{C}b_{\theta}\!\left(X_{\tau}^{(M)},\tau,C_{M}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\right]}_{=:\;K(M)}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{M}}{\mathrm{d}M}.

(7)

Proof.

Differentiate (
3
) with respect to
M
. By Assumption
1
, the Brownian increment is
M
-independent and
b_{\theta}
has no explicit
M
-argument; the chain rule gives (
6
). The terminal condition follows from differentiating
X_{T}^{(M)}=C_{M}+\sigma_{T}\varepsilon
, since
\varepsilon
is
M
-independent. Equation (
7
) is the variation-of-constants formula applied to (
6
) with this terminal condition; the homogeneous part contributes
\Phi(0,T)V_{T}=\Phi(0,T)\,\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
and the particular part contributes
\int_{T}^{0}\Phi(0,\tau)\nabla_{C}b_{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\cdot\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
. Both terms factor a common
\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
on the right.
∎

The factorization

\frac{\partial\widehat{s}^{(M)}}{\partial M}\;=\;K(M)\cdot\frac{\partial C_{M}}{\partial M}

(8)

is exact and pathwise. The matrix
K(M)
is determined entirely by the score Jacobian
\nabla_{x}s_{\theta}
(through
\Phi
, since
\nabla_{x}b_{\theta}=\nabla_{x}f-g^{2}\nabla_{x}s_{\theta}
) and the conditioning Jacobian
\nabla_{C}b_{\theta}
along the trajectory.

Remark 1

(Multiplicative versus additive decomposition)
.

The factorization (
7
) differs structurally from the predictor/score-error decompositions used in oracle-comparison bounds for diffusion models
(
Chen et al. 2022
)
. The latter compare two processes (oracle
X^{\star}
, learned
X_{\theta}
) and split the drift difference as
b^{\star}-b_{\theta}=-g(t)^{2}(s^{\star}-s_{\theta})
, feeding into a path-space KL identity via Girsanov’s theorem.
The output is an additive bound of the form
\mathrm{KL}(P^{\star}\,\|\,P_{\theta})\lesssim
predictor error
+
score error.

Here a single learned process is parametrized by
M
and there is no oracle. The decomposition is by
role
: the score network enters as a coefficient inside the flow (through
\nabla_{x}b_{\theta}
inside
\Phi
), the predictor as boundary data and conditioning. The multiplicative form is what enables iff localization of non-smoothness; an additive bound on
\|X^{(M)}-X^{(1)}\|
would give only one-sided control and would not distinguish between the kink origin and it’s propagation.

4
Kink localization

A kink in
\Psi
at
M^{\ast}
corresponds to a failure of
M\mapsto X_{0}^{(M)}
to be
C^{1}
at
M^{\ast}
, equivalently a discontinuity of
V_{0}
as a function of
M
at
M^{\ast}
. We list three hypotheses on the reverse-process flow.

Hypothesis 1

(Score-Jacobian continuity)
.

The score Jacobian
M\mapsto\nabla_{x}s_{\theta}(X_{t}^{(M)},t,C_{M})
is
C^{0}
in
M
along the reverse trajectory for every
t\in[0,T]
.

Hypothesis 2

(Conditioning-Jacobian continuity)
.

The conditioning Jacobian
M\mapsto\nabla_{C}b_{\theta}(X_{t}^{(M)},t,C_{M})
is
C^{0}
in
M
for every
t\in[0,T]
.

Hypothesis 3

(Non-degeneracy)
.

The matrix
K(M^{\ast})
defined in (
7
) is non-singular along the direction of any one-sided limit of
\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
at
M^{\ast}
.

Hypotheses
1
–
3
are properties of the trained model along the reverse trajectory; their empirical status is the subject of §
6
.

Theorem 2

(Kink localization)
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
2
and Hypotheses
1
–
3
,

M\mapsto\widehat{s}^{(M)}\text{ fails to be $C^{1}$ at $M^{\ast}$}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad M\mapsto\Pi(y^{(M)})\text{ fails to be $C^{1}$ at $M^{\ast}$}.

Proof sketch.

The forward direction follows from (
7
): under Hypotheses
1
and
2
,
K(M)
is continuous at
M^{\ast}
; if
\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
is also continuous, the product
V_{0}
is continuous and no kink appears in the output. The reverse direction uses Hypothesis
3
: a jump in
\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
at
M^{\ast}
is transferred non-trivially through
K(M^{\ast})
to
V_{0}
.

∎

Corollary 3

(Order-
k
localization)
.

Strengthen Assumption
2
to
C^{k+1}
joint smoothness of
b_{\theta}
and strengthen Hypotheses
1
and
2
to
C^{k-1}
regularity in
M
. Then
M\mapsto\widehat{s}^{(M)}
fails to be
C^{k}
at
M^{\ast}
if and only if
M\mapsto\Pi(y^{(M)})
fails to be
C^{k}
at
M^{\ast}
.

The proof of Corollary
3
extends the variational argument by differentiating (
3
)
k
times in
M
; details are deferred along with the proof of Theorem
2
.

5
Discrete-sampler localization

The experiments measure outputs of a discrete Euler–Maruyama sampler with step size
h=T/N
. We extend the localization to this setting. Let
t_{k}=T-kh
and
\widehat{s}^{(M),h}:=X_{0}^{(M),h}
denote the output of
N
Euler–Maruyama steps applied to (
3
) with synchronous Brownian increments
\{\Delta W_{k}\}
held fixed across
M
.

Corollary 4

(Discrete kink localization)
.

Define the discrete state-transition matrix

\Phi^{h}(t_{k},t_{j})\;:=\;\prod_{\ell=j}^{k-1}\left[I-h\,\nabla_{x}b_{\theta}\!\left(X_{t_{\ell}}^{(M),h},t_{\ell},C_{M}\right)\right]\qquad(k>j),

and

K^{h}(M)\;:=\;\Phi^{h}(0,T)\;-\;h\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Phi^{h}(0,t_{k})\,\nabla_{C}b_{\theta}\!\left(X_{t_{k}}^{(M),h},t_{k},C_{M}\right).

The discrete sensitivity
V_{0}^{(M),h}:=\partial\widehat{s}^{(M),h}/\partial M
satisfies
V_{0}^{(M),h}=K^{h}(M)\cdot\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
. Under discrete analogues of Hypotheses
1
–
3
evaluated at the grid points
\{t_{k}\}
, the iff localization of Theorem
2
holds for
M\mapsto\widehat{s}^{(M),h}
.

Proof.

Differentiating the Euler–Maruyama recursion
X_{t_{k+1}}^{(M),h}=X_{t_{k}}^{(M),h}-h\,b_{\theta}(X_{t_{k}}^{(M),h},t_{k},C_{M})+g(t_{k})\Delta W_{k}
with respect to
M
yields the linear recursion

V_{t_{k+1}}^{(M),h}\;=\;\left[I-h\,\nabla_{x}b_{\theta}\right]V_{t_{k}}^{(M),h}\;-\;h\,\nabla_{C}b_{\theta}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{M}}{\mathrm{d}M},

with terminal condition
V_{T}^{(M),h}=\mathrm{d}C_{M}/\mathrm{d}M
. Unrolling produces the stated factorization. The iff argument is then identical to that of Theorem
2
with
K
replaced by
K^{h}
.
∎

Corollary
4
closes the gap between the theorem-object (continuous reverse SDE) and the experimental object (discrete sampler): the factorization survives discretization.
A separate empirical question — whether the kink itself is generated by discretization rather than transported by it — is addressed by experiment E2 in §
6
.

7
Limitations and scope

Localization vs. prediction.

Theorem
2
localizes kinks but does not predict the value of
M^{\ast}
. Locating
M^{\ast}
requires an analysis of the predictor’s input-output map under noise scaling, specifically, why discriminative regression at a fixed training amplitude produces a
C^{1}
failure at that amplitude. We do not address this question here; it is the subject of a companion analysis of the predictor map in isolation.

Hypotheses pending validation.

Two of the three hypotheses underlying Theorem
2
are not yet directly substantiated by measurement: Hypothesis
2
(conditioning-Jacobian continuity) and Hypothesis
3
(non-degeneracy of
K
). The proof of Theorem
2
is provided only in sketch until experiments E3 and E4 establish these hypotheses. Assumption
1
similarly awaits the audit of experiment E1.

Distinction from path-measure analyses.

The variational machinery used here is distinct from the Girsanov-based path-measure machinery commonly used for diffusion-model generalization bounds. The latter compares two path measures and is the right tool for population-level statements (e.g., bounds on the KL divergence between an oracle reverse process and a learned reverse process). The kink phenomenon is a pathwise, sample-conditional statement about how a specific functional of
X_{0}
depends on a perturbation parameter, and the natural machinery is ODE parameter-dependence theory
(
Coddington and Levinson 1955
;
Hartman 2002
)
.

8
Related work

Diffusion-based speech enhancement.

StoRM
(
Lemercier et al. 2023
)
introduced the predictor–score architecture studied here, building on SGMSE+
(
Richter et al. 2023
)
. We are not aware of prior theoretical analysis of the predictor–score split in this architecture under distributional shift in noise power.

Score-based diffusion theory.

Convergence analyses of score-based diffusion models
(
Chen et al. 2022
;
Benton et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2023
)
provide path-space KL bounds between the learned reverse process and an oracle process. As discussed in Remark
1
, these bounds use Girsanov’s theorem and yield additive predictor/score-error decompositions. Our analysis is structurally different: it is pathwise (not population-level), it concerns parametric sensitivity (not divergence from an oracle), and it produces a multiplicative (not additive) decomposition.

9
Conclusion

We have given a variational-flow analysis of noise-power mismatch in StoRM that localizes the SI-SDR kink to the predictor stage, conditional on three hypotheses about the trained model’s reverse-process flow. The central identity is an exact pathwise factorization of the parametric sensitivity into a downstream amplification matrix and an upstream predictor sensitivity. The factorization survives discretization (Corollary
4
). Four experiments substantiate the hypotheses, audit the structural assumption, and rule out discretization as the cause of the kink (§
6
). Two of these experiments are immediately runnable with existing infrastructure (E2, E4) and two require small extensions of an existing measurement script (E3) or a pipeline audit (E1).
