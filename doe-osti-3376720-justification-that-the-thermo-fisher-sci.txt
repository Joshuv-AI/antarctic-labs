 1.0 INTRODUCTION AND PURPOSE
 The Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP) Land Withdrawal Act (LWA) as amended by the
 National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 1997 (1) requires that for Transuranic
 (TRU) waste to be eligible for disposal at WIPP, it must have been generated by atomic energy
 defense activities. The definition of “atomic energy defense activity” is defined in the Nuclear
 Waste Policy Act of 1982 (NWPA) (2).
 This report addresses residues containing americium (Am), (specifically 241Am), that have been
 retrieved from the Thermo-Fisher Scientific (TFS) Facility on Airport Road in Santa Fe, New
 Mexico. These residues were the subject of a de-inventory and disposition effort jointly
 conducted by TFS and Los Alamos National Security (LANS), under the direction of the United
 States Department of Energy (DOE), the National Nuclear Security Agency (NNSA)-Los
 Alamos Field Office and the New Mexico Environment Department (NMED) (3). As part of
 this effort, LANS is seeking to document that the 241Am residues qualify as originating from
 atomic energy defense activities and therefore are eligible for disposal at WIPP.

 2.0 BACKGROUND
 The Atomic Energy Commission (AEC) first offered 241Am for sale in 1962 in the form of
 americium dioxide (4). Throughout the 1960s, the AEC encapsulated and distributed 241Am in
 the form of sealed sources. The 241Am used in sealed sources manufactured in the U.S. was
 derived from AEC-operated defense reactors and distributed through AEC-sponsored
 programs. In 1963 the AEC issued the first commercial license for 241Am to be used in smoke
 detectors (5). In 1975, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) assumed responsibility for
 the regulation of 241Am.
 In the late 1970s, Eberline Instrument Corporation (Eberline) located in Albuquerque, New
 Mexico, manufactured 241Am electroplated sealed sources for use in smoke detectors (5).
 During 1978 and 1979, Eberline produced approximately 1.2 million 241Am sources and
 anticipated this production would continue. However, in 1979, Eberline terminated the 241Am
 source production and placed the remaining inventory of 241Am and associated residues into
 safe and secure storage at their Albuquerque, New Mexico facility (3). In 1979, Eberline was
 purchased by Thermo Electron Corporation. Thermo Electron and Fisher Scientific merged in
 2006 to become Thermo Fisher Scientific (6). In 2000, the Albuquerque facility was being
 prepared for sale and the 241Am residues were retrieved from storage, visually inspected, and
 repackaged as required for transportation (3). In November 2000, the 241Am residues were
 transported to the TFS (formerly Eberline) facility in Santa Fe, New Mexico. The 241Am
 residues were held in safe and secure storage at the TFS Santa Fe facility until February 2016
 (7), when the TFS residues were transported to Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) for
 safe and secure storage, and to evaluate the residues for possible recovery, purification, and
 other programmatic use of the 241Am in support of the Americium Oxide Production Capability
 Program. This evaluation was carried out using the contents of Criteria Considered in Selecting
 Feed Items for Americium-241 Oxide Production Operations (LA-UR-15-20593) (19). Recycle
 and recovery of the TFS material was determined to be “Insufficient SNM Content to Allow
 Process Efficiency.” (Section III.B.). As a result, the material was defined as Transuranic Waste
 ultimately destined for WIPP.

 3.0 DEFENSE DETERMINATION
 The WIPP LWA requirements (and the NWPA definition of atomic energy defense activities)
 are found in Section 3.1.5 of the Transuranic Waste Acceptance Criteria for the Waste Isolation
 Pilot Plant (WIPP WAC), which states (8): Hence, by law, WIPP can accept only radioactive
 waste generated by atomic energy defense activities of the United States. The DOE and its
 predecessor agencies were engaged in a broad range of activities that fall under the heading of
 atomic energy defense activities. A TRU waste is eligible for disposal at WIPP if it has been
 generated in whole or in part by one or more of the following functions:
 • naval reactors development No
 • weapons activities, including defense inertial confinement fusion. Yes
 • verification and control technology No
 • defense nuclear materials production. Yes
 • defense nuclear waste and materials by-products management Yes
 • defense nuclear materials security and safeguards and security investigations Yes
 • defense research and development. No
 see Appendix 3 for the checklist
 Using acceptable knowledge, DOE sites must determine that each waste stream to be disposed
 of at WIPP is “defense” TRU waste. The TFS 241Am waste is eligible for disposal at WIPP
 because it has been generated in whole or in part during the following functions included in the
 WIPP WAC:
 • weapons activities, including defense inertial confinement fusion,
 • defense nuclear materials production,
 • defense nuclear waste and materials by-products management, and
 • defense nuclear materials security and safeguards.
 The following subsections establish that the 241Am originated from atomic energy defense
 activities and provides the justification for approval of the defense determination of the TFS
 241
 Am and hence for disposal at WIPP.

 3.1 Weapons activities including defense inertial confinement fusion
 241
 Am is a man-made isotope. It is a by-product of plutonium creation through defense nuclear
 reactors and is a decay isotope that is created from plutonium aging. Thus, the only source is
 from weapons activities (see below). To expand upon this justification, the U.S. no longer
 produces plutonium. Thus, all weapons manufacturing is based upon the recycle of “old”
 plutonium. Aged plutonium has a given amount of 241Am ingrowth from radioactive decay.
 This material must be separated and removed before it can be used for new/refurbished
 weapons. Thus, it is a key element in the manufacture of nuclear weapons.

 3.2 Defense Nuclear Material Production / Defense Nuclear Waste and
 Materials By-Products Management Criteria
 241
 Am is a man-made isotope and was first discovered in 1944 (9). 241Am is a by-product of
 defense nuclear material production and is formed through neutron capture and decay of
 uranium (U) and plutonium (Pu) atoms in defense reactors (10). The Am accumulates in the
 defense production reactor fuels or in the weapons components and is separated from the Pu
 during weapons remanufacturing. As stated above, the TFS 241Am was purchased by Eberline
 in the 1970s to be used in the production of smoke detectors (3). The manufacturing process
 was terminated in 1979, and the remaining Am inventory was placed in storage. The original
 241
 Am purchase records from the 1970s have been lost or destroyed (3). An interview was
 conducted in March 2016 with Mr. Nels Johnson, a former employee (now retired) of TFS and
 its predecessor company, Eberline, from 1976 to 2002 (11). Mr. Johnson was a staff health
 physicist and was the Radiation Safety Officer for TFS and Eberline. He stated that the smoke
 detector sources were manufactured for Honeywell and the 241Am contained in the TFS residues
 was purchased in 1978 or 1979 (11 and 12). In November 2012, the DOE/NNSA General
 Counsel issued a memorandum that addressed the defense waste determination for certain
 sealed sources, including 241Am used in smoke detectors (14). This memorandum states, Until
 the 2004-to-2006-time frame, sealed sources in the U.S. were generally produced containing
 americium which originated as a byproduct of domestic nuclear weapons production.
 Previously, 241Am materials in sealed sources collected by the NNSA Offsite Source Recovery
 Program were determined to be of defense origin for LANL waste stream LA-OS-NA-01 (10).
 Defense determinations approved by the DOE Carlsbad Field Office (CBFO) for two additional
 LANL waste streams (LA-OS-NA-02, LA-OS-NA-03) further established the defense origin
 for 241Am in sealed sources (15). These approved defense determinations state that 241Am is a
 by-product of defense nuclear materials production and was formed through neutron capture
 and subsequent decay of U and Pu in defense production reactors at the DOE Hanford Site and
 Savannah River Site. These reactors produced Pu for the sole purpose of weapons production.
 The 241Am started to form as soon as the Pu was created in the defense production reactors.
 241
 Am was therefore created as a defense production by-product by accumulation through Pu
 decay in irradiated targets, weapons components, weapons materials, and in fuel reprocessing
 residues (10 and 15). The 241Am was separated from Pu during weapons remanufacturing and
 maintenance activities. Therefore, because the TFS 241Am material was purchased before 2002,
 the 241Am in the TFS residues was created as part of defense nuclear materials production. In
 addition, TFS 241Am material was managed and distributed by the AEC and the NRC as a
 by-product of defense nuclear production for use in the manufacturing of smoke detectors.

 3.3 Defense Nuclear Materials Security and Safeguards Criterion
 The DOE/NNSA General Counsel memorandum also addressed whether or not nuclear material
 used in civilian applications (such as smoke detectors), could be considered of defense origin
 (14). The memorandum concludes that:
 In taking possession of sealed sources (pursuant to proliferation threat reduction
 criteria developed by NNSA and NRC) and making the decision to enhance the
 protection of that material (now waste) through disposal at a secure site, NNSA
 is clearly engaged in “security and safeguards” activities concerning what is
 now “defense waste” and thus is taking actions encompassed within the
 definitional language of the NWPA.
 In April 2012, F. David Martin, Cabinet Secretary of NMED, requested assistance from NNSA
 and the Los Alamos Site Office (LASO) to transport and dispose of the 241Am stored in Santa
 Fe at the TFS Facility (16). The request stated, The continued long-term storage of this material
 within a commercial/residential area of the City of Santa Few with no current or future use or
 pathway for disposition other than LANL, poses an unnecessary long-term risk to public health
 and safety. The Project Execution Plan for the Deinventory and Disposition of 241Am Residues
 from the Thermo Fisher Facility in Santa Fe, NM14, describes the efforts to collect and transfer
 the TFS 241Am residues to LANL for disposition (3). As this material is not considered a sealed
 source, it is not normally considered under the Global Threat Reduction Initiative (7).
 However, because 241Am materials fall under this program, the:
 NNSA and NRC made the decision to enhance the protection of that material (now
 waste) through disposal at a secure site, NNSA is clearly engaged in “security and
 safeguards” activities… (16)
 The TFS 241Am residues were transferred to LANL and received at the Chemistry and
 Metallurgy Building on 02/21/2016 and are currently stored in a limited access room. A project
 execution plan was developed for movement of the 241Am residues within LANL facilities (3).
 The LANL Material Control and Accountability Program required non-destructive assay
 (NDA) on approved gamma assay equipment and the results of the NDA and associated quality
 assurance report are included as Attachment 1 (17).

 4.0 CONCLUSIONS
 Based on the preceding discussions, the TFS 241Am residues are considered to have originated
 from atomic energy defense activities, specifically:
 • defense nuclear materials production,
 • defense nuclear waste and materials by-products management, and
 • defense nuclear materials security and safeguards and security investigations.

 4.0 CONCLUSIONS (continued)
 The process for submitting a request for approval of a TRU waste defense determination is as
            follows (18):
 1. Complete a Defense Determination Approval Form (Attachment 2) by TRU waste site
 personnel with sufficient justification to establish that the waste stream originated from
 an atomic energy defense activity.
 2. Complete a Defense Determination Checklist (Attachment 3) with copies of any
 documents that confirm the defense activity origin.
 3. Review and sign the Defense Determination Approval Form recommendation portions
 by the individual initially making the determination, a technical reviewer, the local DOE
 counsel, and the DOE TRU waste manager at the site.
 4. Forward the signed Defense Determination Approval Form, the checklist, and
 supporting documentation to CBFO for review and approval.
 5. Review and approved by the principal point of contact, a DOE technical representative,
 the chief counsel and finally by the CBFO manager. CBFO notifies DOE headquarters
 (HQ).
 6. In some circumstances, if CBFO is unable to make a determination, the Defense
 Determination Approval Form, the checklist, and supporting documentation will be sent
 to DOE HQ for approval.
