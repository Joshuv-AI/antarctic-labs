The global contributions and emerging trends of extended reality-driven transformation in orthopedic surgical education: a bibliometric study
Authors: Zibo Gao, Yang Liu, Honglin Liu, Meiping Yang, Fangzheng Lin, Dingkun Lin, Yongjin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractExtended reality (XR) technologies have made remarkable progress and been widely applied in orthopedic surgical education, offering a valuable and innovative pathway for traditional surgical training paradigms. Current reviews have not yet systematically synthesized the multimodal XR application landscape and emerging artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled adaptive tutoring technologies, making a comprehensive bibliometric analysis necessary to delineate the global research status and evolutionary trajectory of this field. Based on the Web of Science Core Collection, this study retrieved relevant literature published from 2000 to 2025 and screened 420 eligible publications following the PRISMA 2020 Guidelines. Multiple analytical tools were employed to investigate publication trends, geographical distribution, collaboration networks, research hotspots, and citation bursts. The field has undergone three developmental stages: initial exploration, steady growth, and explosive expansion. The USA, the UK, and China dominate the global research output with noticeable regional disparities, and McGill University ranks first in publication volume. Research foci have shifted from conventional virtual reality and augmented reality simulators toward the integration of AI with XR technologies, with AI demonstrating the strongest citation burst from 2020 to 2025. Technological advancement and post-pandemic demand for remote education have jointly driven the rapid growth of this field, and global research presents distinct developmental patterns between developed and emerging economies. Future research may further deepen the integrated application of AI and XR technologies, and expand cross-border academic collaboration to facilitate the inclusive and high-quality development of technological outcomes in this domain.Keywords: Orthopedic surgical education, Extended reality, Virtual reality, Augmented reality, Mixed reality, Bibliometric analysis

Introduction

Orthopedic surgical education is undergoing a profound technology-driven transformation, in which interdisciplinary research at the intersection of extended reality (XR) technologies and orthopedic training have gradually exhibited exponential growth [1, 2]. The rise of this field is rooted in long-standing structural challenges in orthopedic training: increasing limitations on the anatomical learning resources such as cadavers, steep learning curves for high-risk procedures, significant regional disparities in apprenticeship-based instruction, and the urgent demand for competency-based, quantitative assessment systems in modern medical education [3, 4]. Against this backdrop, XR technology, encompassing virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and mixed reality (MR), provides a novel paradigm for addressing these dilemmas through its repeatable, safe, and data-rich simulation environments [5, 6]. These immersive environments align with modern competency frameworks, thereby holding promise to enhance the equity, standardization, and longitudinal skill tracking capabilities of training programs. Currently, XR technologies has demonstrated transformative potential in areas such as orthopedic surgical training, procedural accuracy, and rehabilitation outcomes [7]; for example, VR simulators have garnered considerable attention due to their advantages in improving surgical skills and learner engagement [8].

Despite this rapid growth, existing reviews have notable limitations in guiding future research and clinical practice. First, some reviews focus exclusively on a single XR modality or isolated educational outcomes, neglecting a comprehensive investigation of multimodal integration and behavioral and clinical translational outcomes [9–11]. Second, since core indicators such as simulation fidelity, learner engagement, and skill retention have not been standardized, results from different studies are difficult to compare and synthesize across studies, undermining the cumulative value of evidence [8, 12]. Third, some reviews fail to cover the disruptive advances that have emerged explosively in recent years, including haptic feedback, artificial intelligence (AI)-driven adaptive tutoring, and distributed collaborative XR platforms—technologies that are redefining the boundaries of what is possible in medical education [2, 6, 13]. These limitations indicate that traditional qualitative inductive methods are no longer adequate to manage the increasingly complex body of knowledge in this field, necessitating the intervention of an objective, macroscopic, and reproducible approach.

Against this demand, bibliometric analysis demonstrates exceptional suitability. Co-citation analysis, keyword co-occurrence analysis, and thematic evolution mapping conducted using tools such as VOSviewer and CiteSpace can systematically and quantitatively reveal the knowledge structure, distribution of research forces, and conceptual evolution trajectory of a field, thereby elevating descriptive reviews to evidence-based strategic insights [14]. Previous scholars have employed bibliometric methods to explore the application landscape and emerging trends of XR technology in the broader orthopedic field [15]. However, a comprehensive bibliometric study specifically targeting the subfield of orthopedic surgical education that maps the global scientific landscape since the turn of the millennium remains lacking. This method enables us not only to identify the dominant research themes and high-impact entities but also to objectively pinpoint emerging subfields and knowledge gaps, thereby integrating fragmented knowledge pieces into a structured panoramic map [16, 17].

Accordingly, this study aims to map, quantify, and interpret the global scientific landscape of XR applications in orthopedic surgical education from 2000 to 2025. Specifically, we focus on the following core research questions: First, what are the dominant thematic clusters, evolutionary trajectories, and key conceptual turning points in this field? Second, which countries, institutions, and journals are driving knowledge production in this field, and are there significant inequities in the global distribution of research resources? Third, what are the common characteristics of highly cited publications, and which low-cited but rapidly growing areas foreshadow future research frontiers and high-potential interdisciplinary intersections? This study seeks to provide a complete, bibliometrically grounded scientific map of XR-driven orthopedic education research, which will serve as an evidence-based decision-making reference for educators, policymakers, and technology developers to optimize curriculum designs, prioritize funding allocation, and develop validated assessment frameworks.

Methods

Data Extraction

All publications in this study were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) developed by Clarivate Analytics. As a comprehensive and authoritative global database of scientific literature, WoSCC is widely recognized as a reliable data source for bibliometric analysis. Given the discrepancies in bibliographic data structures across different databases, adopting a single authoritative database serves as an effective strategy to reduce human errors during data integration [18]. The publication search was conducted in January 2026, with the time range restricted from January 1, 2000, to December 31, 2025, to ensure the comprehensiveness of the collected data. All records were exported from WoSCC in “plain text” format, including “Full Records and Cited References”, which formed the basis for subsequent analyses. The publication retrieval and data were downloaded from the WoSCC database using the following formulas:

#1 TS = (educat* OR curricul* OR school* OR teach* OR student* OR simulat*).

#2 TS = ((orthopedic OR orthopaedic OR skeletal OR skeleton OR musculoskeletal) OR (spine OR spinal OR cervical OR thoracic OR lumbar OR coccyx OR sacrum OR discectomy OR laminectomy OR foraminotomy OR foraminoplasty OR spinal fusion OR arthrodesis OR decompression OR vertebroplasty OR kyphoplasty OR interbody fusion) OR (joint OR shoulder OR elbow OR wrist OR hand OR hip OR knee OR ankle OR foot OR joint replacement OR arthroplasty OR arthroscopy) OR (trauma OR fracture OR osteosynthesis OR intramedullary nail OR fixation) OR (ligament reconstruction OR rotator cuff repair OR meniscectomy OR cartilage repair OR osteotomy OR tendon repair OR nerve repair) OR (limb salvage OR tumor resection OR endoprosthesis OR bone tumor) OR (pediatric orthopedic OR deformity OR epiphysiodesis)).

#3 TS = (surg* OR operation OR procedure).

#4 TS = (artificial intelligence OR AI OR machine learning OR deep learning OR virtual reality OR VR OR augmented reality OR AR OR mixed reality OR MR OR extended reality OR XR OR natural language processing OR NLP OR large language model OR LLM).

#5 = #1 AND #2 AND #3 AND #4.

Data selection

A total of 4421 publications were retrieved in the initial search. To ensure the quality and relevance of the included data, the entire study selection process was conducted in strict accordance with the PRISMA 2020 Guidelines. The document types were restricted to “Article” and “Review Article”, and the language of the publications was limited to English, which yielded 4143 remaining records. The inclusion criteria required studies to focus on the application of XR technologies in orthopedic surgical education. The exclusion criteria were defined as follows: studies that deviated from the core topics of XR technologies or orthopedic surgical education; studies with incomplete data or insufficient information to support subsequent bibliometric analysis.

Subsequently, two independent researchers screened the titles and abstracts of all remaining publications to verify their alignment with the predefined research topic. Inter-rater reliability was quantified using Cohen’s κ coefficient. The overall observed agreement rate was 92.85% (3847/4143), with a Cohen’s κ coefficient of 0.63, indicating substantial inter-rater agreement. All discrepancies regarding inclusion decisions were resolved through in-depth discussion with a third senior researcher. Of the 245 publications with initial screening disagreements, 116 were included after expert adjudication. Finally, a total of 420 publications were included in bibliometric analysis (Fig. 1).

Data processing

Based on the raw data exported from WoSCC, standardization procedures were performed for keywords, institutions, and countries/regions. For keyword normalization, inconsistent expressions were unified by expanding abbreviations, deleting special symbols, and consolidating singular and plural forms, etc. For instance, variants including “virtual-reality”, “vr”, and “virtual reality (vr)” were combined into the standardized term “virtual reality”. For institutional normalization, abbreviated names were replaced with full official names to ensure analytical consistency. Country/region names were standardized in accordance with the built-in nomenclature of Scimago Graphica (version 1.0.51, Spain) to facilitate subsequent visualization and analysis. Accordingly, a complete analytical dataset was established, covering standardized fields such as DOI, title, abstract, keywords, citation count, authors, journal sources, publication year, and references. This refined dataset served as the fundamental matrix for all subsequent bibliometric analyses.

Bibliometric analysis

The annual publication output trend of the included publications from 2000 to 2025 was plotted using Microsoft Excel (version 2019, USA), and piecewise linear regression combined with Bayesian Information Criterion implemented in MATLAB (version R2025a, USA) was applied for breakpoint detection to divide the developmental stages of this research field.

The geographical distribution of publication output across countries/regions was visualized using Scimago Graphica. The weight and size of the circular markers on the geographic map were positively correlated with the total number of publications of the corresponding country/region, which directly reflected the overall academic output scale and contribution of each country/region in this field.

VOSviewer (version 1.6.19, the Netherlands) was used for descriptive statistical analysis of the included publications, and the top 10 most productive journals and top 10 most highly cited documents in this field were summarized and presented in tabular form. Meanwhile, co-occurrence network analyses of institutions, authors, and keywords were constructed and visualized via VOSviewer. In the resulting network maps, the size of each node was proportional to the number of relevant publications, the thickness of the connecting links denoted the strength of collaborative relationships, and the color of nodes indicated the assigned cluster group. The visualization of the above complex networks was further optimized using Pajek (version 6.02, Slovenia) to improve the structural clarity and interpretability of the networks, so as to identify the core collaborative relationships between research institutions and authors, as well as the overall distribution of research hotspots in the field.

Burst detection analysis was conducted using CiteSpace (version 6.3.1, USA) to explore the dynamic changes of research frontiers and emerging topics in the field over the study period. The top 10 keywords and top 10 cited references with the strongest citation burst intensity were identified and analyzed, to reveal the evolutionary trajectory and emerging research trends of this field between 2000 and 2025.

Results

Bibliometric analysis of publication output

The annual distribution of publications from 2000 to 2025, together with the fitted dashed trend line reflecting the overall growth pattern, was presented in Fig. 2. Over the 25-year study period, the annual number of publications in this field showed an overall continuous upward trend. According to the inflection points of publication volume and growth characteristics, the development of this research domain was divided into three distinct phases.

Annual number of publications with the blue dashed line showing the growth trend

The first phase spanned 2000 to 2012, with persistently low publication output. The annual number of publications remained below 10 throughout this period, with a maximum of 7 publications in a single year, and no relevant publications were recorded in multiple years. The second phase covered 2013 to 2019, marked by steady moderate growth in publication volume. The annual number of publications broke the single-digit bottleneck, ranging from 11 to 23, with a gradual increase in research output over this period. The third phase was 2020 to 2025, characterized by explosive exponential growth in annual publications. The publication volume rose rapidly from 36 in 2020 to 71 in 2025, making this the most productive period, and indicating this cross-disciplinary field had become a growing research focus in orthopedic surgical education.

Bibliometric analysis of countries/regions

A total of 49 countries/regions contributed to the research publications in this field. Over the entire study period, the USA was the leading contributor, with 157 publications (37.38%), followed by the UK (59, 14.05%), China (52, 12.38%), Canada (41, 9.76%), and Germany (28, 6.67%). The geographical distribution map of global publications further visualized the unbalanced spatial distribution of research output in this field (Fig. 3). North America (represented by the USA and Canada), Western Europe (represented by the UK, Germany, Italy, and Switzerland), and East Asia (represented by China, Japan, and South Korea) were the most concentrated regions for publication output. Only a small number of publications were distributed in South America, Africa, and Oceania, with the overall output far lower than that of the core research regions.

Global collaboration network of countries/regions

International collaboration network analysis revealed that the USA had the highest total link strength (58), serving as the core hub of global collaboration in this field, with particularly close cooperative relationships with English-speaking countries including the UK, Canada, and Australia. European countries formed a compact collaborative cluster, with Italy (38), Germany (35), the UK (29), and Switzerland (28) as the core contributors, showing the most frequent intra-regional collaboration within Western Europe. As the main contributor in the Asia-Pacific region, China maintained active collaborative relationships with the USA, Australia, and other Asia-Pacific countries, with a total link strength of 6.

The academic influence of high-productivity countries/regions was comprehensively evaluated based on total citations, average citations per document, and normalized citations. The USA ranked first in both total citations (5057) and normalized citations (194.21), maintaining a leading position in the overall academic influence of this field. For average citations per document, the Netherlands achieved the highest value (45.83), followed by the UK (38.86), Canada (35.07), Italy (34.09), and the USA (32.21). China ranked fourth in both total citations (984) and normalized citations (48.34). However, its average citations per document (18.92) still lagged behind those of major developed countries, indicating room for further improvement in the global influence of individual studies.

Discussion

Emerging trends in XR technology for orthopedic surgical education

Our bibliometric analysis revealed a three-stage evolutionary pattern in the annual publication output of research on XR for orthopedic surgical education from 2000 to 2025, which was consistent with the global iterative process of XR technologies and the changing demands in medical education.

The first phase was the initial exploratory period from 2000 to 2012, during which the annual publication volume remained at an extremely low level. This was mainly attributed to the insufficient maturity of XR technologies at this stage: technical bottlenecks including high hardware costs, high latency and visual vertigo risk failed to meet the requirements of orthopedic surgical education for high-precision anatomical restoration and realistic operational force feedback [20, 21]. Meanwhile, this technology was mainly applied in professional simulation fields such as military and aerospace [22], its application value in orthopedic teaching had not been widely recognized, and relevant research was only in a scattered exploratory stage.

The second phase was the steady growth period from 2013 to 2019, with continuously increasing academic attention. This growth was mainly driven by two core factors: first, the commercialization of consumer-grade VR and AR hardware, as well as the iteration of core technologies such as six degrees of freedom spatial positioning, effectively solved the main pain points of early immersive systems and greatly reduced the application threshold of the technology [23, 24]; second, the inherent limitations of traditional orthopedic surgical teaching, including shortage of cadaver specimens, high risk of invasive operation training and difficulty in replicating standardized surgical scenarios, became increasingly prominent, and the application potential of XR technology in constructing a repeatable, non-invasive and standardized surgical training system was gradually verified, promoting the steady growth of relevant research output [25, 26].

The third phase was the explosive growth period from 2020 to 2025, and this field has become an interdisciplinary research hotspot in orthopedics, medical education and biomedical engineering. This explosive growth was driven by the synergy of multiple factors: the global COVID-19 pandemic generated a rigid demand for remote medical education, providing a practical opportunity for the large-scale application and scenario expansion of this technology [27, 28]. The integration of advanced technologies such as large language models and spatial computing with immersive interactive systems significantly improved the simulation accuracy, interactive intelligence and teaching adaptability of the system, further expanding the research boundary [13, 29]; in addition, the increasing emphasis on standardized training of operational skills in global medical education has jointly promoted this field to become a key focus of interdisciplinary research.

Global contributions in XR technology for orthopedic surgical education

Our bibliometric findings delineate a geographically heterogeneous landscape of global contributions to this field over the 2000–2025 study period. A total of 49 countries/regions contributed to the academic development of this domain, yet over 90% of the total included publications were concentrated in three core regions: North America, Western Europe, and East Asia, while research output from South America, Africa, and Oceania accounts for less than 5% of the global total, reflecting regional disparities in medical education investment and XR technology infrastructure. The USA emerges as the global leader, accounting for 157 publications and the highest total citations, normalized citation score, and international collaboration total link strength; North America has led foundational advancements in the field through VR simulator development and commercial platforms, which provide high-fidelity systems for repeatable practice and objective performance assessment and effectively address limitations of the traditional apprenticeship model including patient safety risks and the high cost of cadaveric training [30–32]. Western Europe forms a compact collaborative cluster contributing nearly 30% of global publications, with EU Horizon 2020 projects focusing on evidence-based validation of skill transfer and interoperability with clinical radiographical systems [33, 34]. East Asia, led by China, Japan, and South Korea, is an important growing contributor, with key innovations including haptic-integrated AR systems, and cloud-enabled collaborative simulation to expand access to training resources [14, 35].

Two distinct development trajectories have emerged globally, alongside shared barriers to widespread implementation. Developed countries represented by the Netherlands, the UK and Canada had relatively lower total publication output in this field, but as their research focuses on high-level evidence-based validation of training efficacy and skill transfer, they achieve far higher average citations per publication [36]. On the other hand, China, backed by its large orthopedic surgery volume and growing research investment, has generated substantial research output, reflecting rising academic recognition and development potential, and the development path may offer a valuable reference for other emerging economies. However, despite the recognized potential of XR technologies, formal large-scale implementation in global orthopedic education remains limited by regional gaps in infrastructure, cost, and digital literacy [37], with sparse cross-border collaboration between developed and emerging economies hindering the equitable global popularization of these transformative training tools.

The cluster-based pattern observed in institutional and author collaboration reflects the highly interdisciplinary nature of XR-enhanced orthopedic surgical education, which draws on immersive technology engineering, clinical orthopedics, and educational science. Research output is therefore concentrated in institutions with established multidisciplinary infrastructure and long-term research investment, forming stable core teams that advance the field across hardware development, clinical validation, and pedagogical integration [35]. The considerable overlap between the most productive and most cited authors further indicates that the field remains at a stage where core research groups shaped methodological frameworks and guide dominant research directions. Collaborative relationships are largely contained within individual clusters, with relatively sparse deep collaboration across separate research hubs. While this concentrated model supports efficient technical iteration within established teams, it may also result in relative homogeneity of research perspectives and technical solutions.

Research hotspot evaluation and future trends in XR technology for orthopedic surgical education

Based on the keyword co-occurrence network, burst detection, and high-cited publication evaluation, this study delineates the evolutionary trajectory of research hotspots in XR for orthopedic surgical education from 2000 to 2025. The primary and sustained hotspot is the development of high-fidelity VR and AR simulators integrated with biomechanical modeling, particularly for bone-implant interactions in complex procedures [38]. This is consistent with the high frequency of “virtual reality” and “simulation”, as well as the early burst of arthroscopic simulator validation studies (2009–2020). Haptic feedback represents another focus, as force calculation is essential for delicate tasks such as bone drilling, and has been shown to improve performance in bone drilling tasks among orthopedic residents [39, 40]. However, the sustained emphasis on simulator development also suggests that the field has remained largely at the proof-of-concept stage, with comparatively fewer studies examining whether simulation-acquired skills translate to real-world surgical performance or improved patient outcomes.

The ongoing burst of AI-related keywords, including “artificial intelligence” and “machine learning”, indicates that the research frontier is shifting toward the integration of XR with AI technologies [13, 41, 42]. AI-driven real-time performance analytics and adaptive feedback, utilizing motion tracking and gesture recognition, have propelled recent research efforts forward [42]. Recent work has developed an interpretable mixed reality framework for orthopedic assessment, integrating motion simulation and immersive visualization to enable quantitative, explainable postoperative evaluation [43]. However, the supporting evidence base remains relatively limited, and most AI‑empowered XR systems are still at an early exploratory stage, requiring further large‑scale clinical validation.

Based on the observed trends, several promising avenues for future research may be identified. AI has the potential to enable more personalized and context-aware training by synthesizing patient-specific data, procedural characteristics, and expected outcomes, thereby extending simulation beyond isolated technical skill practice. Multi-user immersive environments and metaverse platforms could facilitate remote mentorship and distributed team training, helping to overcome geographical barriers and promote the equitable distribution of global educational resources [44, 45]. AI-driven analytics, combined with motion tracking and gesture recognition, could also accelerate the iterative refinement of XR systems and enable more objective, fine-grained assessments of trainee competence [42]. Nevertheless, these directions should be viewed as informed perspectives derived from thematic synthesis and author interpretation, and their feasibility and priority warrant further exploration.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the publication search was restricted to WoSCC, which may have excluded relevant studies indexed in other databases. Second, the inclusion of only English-language articles may introduce language bias. Third, bibliometric indicators reflect academic influence rather than methodological quality or educational value, and therefore cannot be used to assess the rigor of individual studies. Fourth, while the analysis identifies research trends and hotspots, it cannot fully explain the underlying drivers, nor can it directly validate the future directions discussed, which are based on the authors’ interpretation. Despite these limitations, this study can still serve as a useful reference for understanding the global contributions and developmental trends in the field.

Conclusion

This study presents a bibliometric analysis of the global contributions and evolutionary trends of XR application in orthopedic surgical education. Visualization mapping reveals that XR technologies are progressively converging with AI and multimodal systems, which may offer powerful tools for enabling more networked, multi‑layered, and cross‑regional orthopedic surgical education to address complex educational challenges. Nevertheless, the technological and geographical divides inherent to these technologies must be recognized. An open and equitable approach should therefore be adopted to ensure that XR can genuinely promote the further development of orthopedic surgical education on a global scale.
