1.
Introduction

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q
, and let
L(s,\chi)
be the associated Dirichlet
L
-function, with non-trivial zeros
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma
. The Generalised Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for
L(s,\chi)
asserts that
\gamma
is real for all such zeros. In this paper we study two discrete second moments of
L(s,\chi)
, taken over its non-trivial zeros, that generalise to the Dirichlet setting two conjectures and theorems known for the Riemann zeta-function. We emphasise that our results are uniform in the conductor
q
.

1.1.
Background: the moments at
q=1

On the assumption of the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) and the simplicity of the zeros, Gonek made the following conjecture in his Mathematical Sciences Research Institute talk
[
10
]
.

Conjecture 1

(Gonek)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis and that the non-trivial zeros
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
are simple. Then, as
T\to\infty
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}}\sim\frac{3}{\pi^{3}}\,T.

Milinovich and Ng
[
17
]
established a lower bound for this sum that is half the conjectured size.

Theorem 2

(Milinovich–Ng)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis and that the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple. Then, for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
and all
T
sufficiently large,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}}\geq\Bigl(\frac{3}{2\pi^{3}}-\varepsilon\Bigr)T.

The companion moment was conjectured by Ng
[
19
,
20
]
and appears as a problem of the Comparative Prime Number Theory symposium
[
7
]
.

Conjecture 3

(Ng)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis and that the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple. Then, as
T\to\infty
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\right|^{2}\sim\frac{T}{2\pi}.

The matching lower bound, again half the conjectured size, was proved by Sinha
[
23
]
; an independent proof, differing in its treatment of the critical contour, was given by the author
[
21
]
.

Theorem 4

(Sinha)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis and that the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple. Then, for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
and all
T
sufficiently large,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\right|^{2}\geq\Bigl(\frac{1}{4\pi}-\varepsilon\Bigr)T.

1.2.
The Dirichlet moments, uniformly in the conductor

The analogous Dirichlet moments are dictated by two Dirichlet-series identities. The Möbius function is defined by

\mu(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n=1,\\
(-1)^{k}&\text{if }n=p_{1}\dotsm p_{k}\text{ for distinct primes }p_{1},\dotsc,p_{k},\\
0&\text{if }n\text{ is not square-free},\end{cases}

and gives, for
\Re s>1
,

(1.1)

\frac{1}{L(s,\chi)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\mu(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}},

so that the residue of
1/L(s,\chi)
at a simple zero
\rho
is
1/L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)
. We refer to the corresponding sum

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2}}

as the reciprocal moment. The Liouville function is defined by

\lambda(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n=1,\\
(-1)^{\Omega(n)}&\text{if }n>1,\end{cases}

where
\Omega(n)
denotes the number of prime factors of
n
counted with multiplicity, and gives, for
\Re s>1
,

(1.2)

\frac{L(2s,\chi^{2})}{L(s,\chi)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}},

where
\chi^{2}
denotes the character
n\mapsto\chi(n)^{2}
, so that the residue of
L(2s,\chi^{2})/L(s,\chi)
at a simple zero
\rho
is
L(2\rho,\chi^{2})/L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)
. We refer to the corresponding sum

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2}

as the ratio moment.

When
q
is allowed to grow with
T
, the strength of the bounds we obtain is governed by the single quantity

(1.3)

\beta=\beta(q,T)=\frac{\log T}{\log qT}=\Bigl(1+\frac{\log q}{\log T}\Bigr)^{-1}\in(0,1).

Theorem 5
.

Fix
A>0
and
\varepsilon>0
. Assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
and that all of its non-trivial zeros are simple. Then, uniformly for all primitive characters
\chi
modulo
q
with
3\leq q\leq T^{A}
, and all
T
sufficiently large in terms of
A
and
\varepsilon
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2}}\;\geq\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{A_{q}}{2\pi}\,\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\,T\;=\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{A_{q}}{2\pi\bigl(2+\frac{\log q}{\log T}\bigr)}\,T,

with
\beta
as in (
1.3
), and where
A_{q}
is the arithmetic factor

(1.4)

A_{q}=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\mid q}\frac{p}{p+1}=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\prod_{p\mid q}\frac{p}{p+1}.

Theorem 6
.

Fix
0<\delta<1
and
\varepsilon>0
. Assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
and that all of its non-trivial zeros are simple. Then, uniformly for all primitive characters
\chi
modulo
q
with
3\leq q\leq T^{1-\delta}
, and all
T
sufficiently large in terms of
\delta
and
\varepsilon
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2}\;\geq\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\,T\;=\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\,\frac{1}{2\pi\bigl(2+\frac{\log q}{\log T}\bigr)}\,T,

where
\varphi
is the Euler totient function, given by

\varphi(q)=\#\{1\leq a\leq q:(a,q)=1\}=q\prod_{p\mid q}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr).

The ranges of the two theorems differ because we do not assume GRH for the second character
\chi^{2}
appearing in the ratio moment: at one point in the proof of Theorem
6
the factor
L(s,\chi^{2})
can only be bounded by convexity, and this is what restricts the range to
q\leq T^{1-\delta}
. If one is willing to assume GRH for
L(s,\chi^{2})
as well, the restriction disappears and the range may be taken to be
q\leq T^{A}
as in Theorem
5
; we make this precise in Section
5
.

In what follows we write

(1.5)

\mathfrak{a}(q)=\begin{cases}A_{q}&\text{for the reciprocal moment},\\[2.0pt]
\varphi(q)/q&\text{for the ratio moment},\end{cases}

for the arithmetic factor of the two problems. Two regimes deserve to be singled out: small conductors, where the bounds take their cleanest form, and power conductors, where the conductor begins to cost.

Remark
.

If
q=q(T)
satisfies
\log q=o(\log T)
(in particular if
q
is fixed, and more generally if
q\leq(\log T)^{B}
for any fixed
B
, or
q=\exp\bigl((\log T)^{1-\delta}\bigr)
), then
\beta\to 1
and, uniformly in this range,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2}}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{A_{q}}{4\pi}\,T.

Remark
.

If
q=q(T)
satisfies
\log q=o(\log T)
, then, uniformly in this range,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\,\frac{1}{4\pi}\,T.

At
q=1
the constants
A_{1}/4\pi=3/2\pi^{3}
and
\tfrac{1}{4\pi}
recover Theorems
2
and
4
; each of these two bounds is half of the expected main term (Conjectures
7
and
8
below).

Remark
.

If
q=T^{A}
with
A>0
fixed, then
\beta=1/(1+A)
, so that

\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}=\frac{1/(1+A)}{(2+A)/(1+A)}=\frac{1}{2+A},

and the proved constant is
\frac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}\,\frac{1}{2+A}
, with
\mathfrak{a}(q)
as in (
1.5
). That is, the factor
\tfrac{1}{2}
of the small-conductor regime degrades to
\tfrac{1}{2+A}
of the expected main term:
\tfrac{2}{5}
of it at
A=\tfrac{1}{2}
,
\tfrac{1}{3}
at
A=1
,
\tfrac{1}{4}
at
A=2
. (For the ratio moment, Theorem
6
requires
A<1
.)

Remark
.

The arithmetic factor itself decays only very slowly: by Mertens’ theorem
\mathfrak{a}(q)\gg 1/\log\log q
, so the bounds remain of the exact order
\mathfrak{a}(q)T
of the expected main term throughout the stated ranges, losing at most a factor
\ll\log\log q
against
T
. Beyond those ranges, the method continues to give a positive constant until the conditional size estimate
\exp\bigl(c\log qT/\log\log qT\bigr)=T^{o(1)}
fails, that is, for
q
up to
T^{o(\log\log T)}
; we do not pursue this outer range.

1.3.
The expected size, and the source of the conductor deficit

We do not import an external prediction for the size of these moments unlike the zeta function case. Rather, the method itself suggests one, and at the same time explains the factor
\beta/(1+\beta)
. As we show in Section
6
, the optimised Cauchy–Schwarz inequality produces the quantity

(1.6)

\frac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}\,\frac{u}{1+u}\,T,\qquad u=\frac{\log\xi}{\log qT},

where
\xi
is the length of the mollifier. The mean value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials caps the length at
\xi\leq T^{1-\varepsilon}
independently of
q
(a single
L
-function observed up to height
T
resolves frequencies only up to
T
), so
u\leq\beta
, and the supremum of (
1.6
) is the bound of Theorems
5
and
6
. In the opposite direction, Farmer’s long-mollifier heuristic
[
8
]
suggests that the value of (
1.6
) as
u\to\infty
, namely
\tfrac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}T
, is the true size of each moment. This leads us to the following two conjectures.

Conjecture 7
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q>1
. Assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
and that all of its non-trivial zeros are simple. Then, as
T\to\infty
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2}}\sim\frac{A_{q}}{2\pi}\,T,

and we expect this asymptotic to hold uniformly for
q\leq T^{A}
, for any fixed
A>0
.

Conjecture 8
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q>1
. Assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
and that all of its non-trivial zeros are simple. Then, as
T\to\infty
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2}\sim\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\,\frac{T}{2\pi},

and we expect this asymptotic to hold uniformly for
q\leq T^{A}
, for any fixed
A>0
.

Thus, conjecturally, our theorems capture exactly the proportion
\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}
of the truth, and in the small-conductor regime exactly half of it: the deficiency is the ratio of the value of
u/(1+u)
at the cap
u=\beta
to its limiting value
1
, a structural feature of the diagonal Cauchy–Schwarz construction rather than a numerical accident. Conjectures
7
and
8
reduce to Conjectures
1
and
3
at
q=1
, which lends them support; we discuss their provenance further in Section
6
. It would be of interest to see whether they can be derived from the recipe of Conrey–Farmer–Keating–Rubinstein–Snaith
[
2
]
or from the
L
-functions Ratios Conjecture
[
3
,
4
]
; we do not pursue this here.

1.4.
Averages over the family

The constants in Conjectures
7
and
8
depend only on the modulus and not on the character, so conjectures for the averages over all primitive characters modulo
q
follow by additivity. We write
\sum{{}^{*}}_{\chi\bmod q}
for a sum over these characters and
\varphi^{*}(q)
for their number, so that

(1.7)

\varphi^{*}(q)=\sum_{d\mid q}\mu(q/d)\varphi(d)=q\prod_{p\,\|\,q}\Bigl(1-\frac{2}{p}\Bigr)\prod_{p^{2}\mid q}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)^{2},

where
p\,\|\,q
means that
p\mid q
but
p^{2}\nmid q
; in particular
\varphi^{*}(q)=0
precisely when
q\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, in which case there are no primitive characters and the statements below are empty.

Conjecture 9
.

Let
q\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, and assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
and the simplicity of its zeros for every primitive character
\chi
modulo
q
. Then, as
T\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{\varphi^{*}(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\bmod q}\;\sum_{0<\gamma_{\chi}\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2}}\sim\frac{A_{q}}{2\pi}\,T,

and we expect this asymptotic to hold uniformly for
q\leq T^{A}
, for any fixed
A>0
.

Conjecture 10
.

Let
q\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, and assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
and the simplicity of its zeros for every primitive character
\chi
modulo
q
. Then, as
T\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{\varphi^{*}(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\bmod q}\;\sum_{0<\gamma_{\chi}\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2}\sim\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\,\frac{T}{2\pi},

and we expect this asymptotic to hold uniformly for
q\leq T^{A}
, for any fixed
A>0
.

We explain the reasoning behind Conjectures
9
and
10
, and comment on the prospects for a uniform treatment of the family, in Section
8
.

1.5.
Higher moments

The lower bounds of Theorems
5
and
6
propagate, by a
single application of Hölder’s inequality, to lower bounds for all higher
moments

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2k}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2k},

where
k\geq 1
, uniformly in the conductor. For
k>1
these are of order
T/(\log qT)^{\,k-1}=o(T)
, far below the conjectured sizes. A slight advantage is that our lower bounds are explicit, but we emphasise that they are not good lower bounds in terms of what we expect the truth to be.

Corollary 11
.

Fix
A>0
, a real number
k\geq 1
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Assume the Generalised
Riemann Hypothesis for
L(s,\chi)
and that all of its non-trivial zeros are
simple. Then, uniformly for all primitive characters
\chi
modulo
q
with

3\leq q\leq T^{A}
and all
T
sufficiently large in terms of
A
,
k
and

\varepsilon
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2k}}\;\geq\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{A_{q}^{\,k}}{2\pi}\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\Bigr)^{k}\frac{T}{(\log qT)^{\,k-1}}\;=\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{A_{q}^{\,k}}{2\pi\bigl(2+\frac{\log q}{\log T}\bigr)^{k}}\,\frac{T}{(\log qT)^{\,k-1}},

with
\beta
as in (
1.3
) and
A_{q}
as in (
1.4
). In particular,
if
\log q=o(\log T)
then
\beta\to 1
and
\log qT\sim\log T
, so that

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2k}}\;\geq\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{A_{q}^{\,k}}{2^{\,k+1}\pi}\,\frac{T}{(\log T)^{\,k-1}}.

Remark
.

In the case of the Riemann zeta-function (
q=1
), the negative discrete moments
\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)|^{-2k}
have been studied directly, and far more precisely than the Hölder bound of Corollary
11
permits. Heap, Li and Zhao
[
13
]
proved lower bounds for all rational
k\geq 0
, of the order predicted by the conjecture of Gonek and Hejhal, and Gao and Zhao
[
9
]
removed the rationality restriction, obtaining the same for all real
k\geq 0
, with bounds expected to be sharp for
k<\tfrac{3}{2}
; in the other direction Bui, Florea and Milinovich
[
1
]
obtained upper bounds, almost optimal for
k\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
and averaged over a subfamily of the zeros of asymptotically full density. The content of Corollary
11
is thus its uniformity in the conductor rather than its strength.

Corollary 12
.

Fix
0<\delta<1
, a real number
k\geq 1
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Assume the
Generalised Riemann Hypothesis for
L(s,\chi)
and that all of its non-trivial
zeros are simple. Then, uniformly for all primitive characters
\chi
modulo
q

with
3\leq q\leq T^{1-\delta}
and all
T
sufficiently large in terms of
\delta
,

k
and
\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2k}\;

\displaystyle\geq\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{\varphi(q)^{k}}{q^{k}}\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\Bigr)^{k}\frac{T}{(\log qT)^{\,k-1}}

\displaystyle=\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{\varphi(q)^{k}}{q^{k}}\,\frac{1}{2\pi\bigl(2+\frac{\log q}{\log T}\bigr)^{k}}\,\frac{T}{(\log qT)^{\,k-1}},

with
\beta
as in (
1.3
) and
\varphi
the Euler totient function. In
particular, if
\log q=o(\log T)
then
\beta\to 1
and
\log qT\sim\log T
, giving

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2k}\;\geq\;(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{\varphi(q)^{k}}{2^{\,k+1}\pi\,q^{k}}\,\frac{T}{(\log T)^{\,k-1}}.

Remark
.

A refinement of these methods, developed in a follow-up paper, narrows the deficit towards the conjectured size in both the reciprocal and ratio moments.

Remark
.

The corresponding family bounds follow in an analogous way. Since the single-character bounds above depend on
\chi
only through the modulus
q
, averaging over the
\varphi^{*}(q)
primitive characters modulo
q
shows that the family averages

\frac{1}{\varphi^{*}(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\bmod q}\;\sum_{0<\gamma_{\chi}\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2k}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{\varphi^{*}(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\bmod q}\;\sum_{0<\gamma_{\chi}\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2k}

obey the same lower bounds as the single-character moments of Corollaries
11
and
12
, in the ranges
3\leq q\leq T^{A}
and
3\leq q\leq T^{1-\delta}
respectively.

2.
Setup of the theorems

We follow the strategy of Milinovich and Ng
[
17
]
, who in turn based their argument on the work of Rudnick and Soundararajan
[
22
]
. Throughout the remainder of the paper
\chi
is a primitive character modulo
q\geq 3
,
\bar{\chi}
is its complex conjugate, and we assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
together with the simplicity of its zeros. The parameters
A>0
(for Theorem
5
),
\delta\in(0,1)
(for Theorem
6
),
\vartheta\in(0,1)
and the polynomial
P
below are fixed. All implied constants are absolute or depend only on these fixed parameters, never on
q
or
\chi
, and all estimates are uniform over
3\leq q\leq T^{A}
(respectively
3\leq q\leq T^{1-\delta}
for the ratio moment, where indicated).

Let
\xi=T^{\vartheta}
, and let
P(x)
be a polynomial satisfying
P(0)=0
and
P(1)=1
. For brevity we write

P_{n}=P\!\left(\frac{\log(\xi/n)}{\log\xi}\right).

The two theorems are attacked with two mollifiers, one for each. For Theorem
5
we use the Möbius-twisted Dirichlet polynomial

\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(s)=\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\mu(n)\psi(n)}{n^{s}}\,P_{n}\qquad(\psi\in\{\chi,\bar{\chi}\}),

which is a smoothed truncation of the Dirichlet series (
1.1
). For Theorem
6
we use the Liouville-twisted polynomial

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}(s)=\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\lambda(n)\psi(n)}{n^{s}}\,P_{n}\qquad(\psi\in\{\chi,\bar{\chi}\}),

which by (
1.2
) is a smoothed truncation of
L(2s,\chi^{2})/L(s,\chi)
. In each case the residue of the underlying Dirichlet series at a simple zero
\rho
of
L(s,\chi)
is precisely the summand of the corresponding moment:
1/L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)
for the reciprocal moment and
L(2\rho,\chi^{2})/L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)
for the ratio moment. This is what motivates the choice of mollifier in each case.

Since
\overline{L(s,\chi)}=L(\bar{s},\bar{\chi})
, and since under GRH every non-trivial zero satisfies
\bar{\rho}=1-\rho
, both mollifiers enjoy the symmetry

(2.1)

\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(\rho)}=\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\chi}}(1-\rho),\qquad\overline{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}(\rho)}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\bar{\chi}}(1-\rho).

Writing the summand of the reciprocal moment as
1/L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)
and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to
\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(\rho)}
, the symmetry (
2.1
) gives

(2.2)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)|^{2}}\geq\frac{|M_{1}|^{2}}{M_{2}},

where

M_{1}=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\chi}}(1-\rho)}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad M_{2}=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(\rho)|^{2}.

Likewise, applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to
\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\overline{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}(\rho)}
and using the second relation in (
2.1
), we obtain for the ratio moment

(2.3)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\right|^{2}\geq\frac{|\widetilde{M}_{1}|^{2}}{\widetilde{M}_{2}},

where

\widetilde{M}_{1}=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{L(2\rho,\chi^{2})}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\bar{\chi}}(1-\rho)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{M}_{2}=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}(\rho)|^{2}.

Our task is therefore to obtain an asymptotic for each of
M_{1},M_{2},\widetilde{M}_{1},\widetilde{M}_{2}
, uniformly in
q
, and then to choose
P
so as to make the resulting lower bounds as large as possible.

One technical convention is needed before we begin. The contour rectangles of Sections
4
and
5
have their bottom edge at a height
t_{0}=t_{0}(\chi)\in[1,2]
, chosen in Lemma
20
so as to avoid the low-lying zeros uniformly in

, and the residue theorem therefore produces sums over the zeros with
t_{0}<\gamma\leq T
rather than
0<\gamma\leq T
. This costs nothing, for two reasons. First, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) are valid over any set of zeros, so we are free to define
M_{1},M_{2},\widetilde{M}_{1},\widetilde{M}_{2}
by sums over
t_{0}<\gamma\leq T
. Secondly, the summands of the two moments are non-negative, so a lower bound for the sum restricted to
t_{0}<\gamma\leq T
is also a lower bound for the full sum over
0<\gamma\leq T
. We adopt this convention without further comment.

Remark
.

The two hypotheses play distinct roles. The simplicity of the zeros guarantees that the moments are well-defined (
L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)\neq 0
) and that the poles of
1/L(s,\chi)
and of
L(2s,\chi^{2})/L(s,\chi)
at the zeros are simple, with the stated residues, in the contour integrals of Sections
4
and
5
. GRH for
L(s,\chi)
places all the zeros on the critical line, which is what yields the symmetry (
2.1
); it is also used through the size estimates of Sections
4
and
5
. No hypothesis on
L(s,\chi^{2})
is required.

The arithmetic of the two problems enters only through one coprimality sum: on the diagonal
m=n
the character contribution is
\chi(n)\bar{\chi}(n)=|\chi(n)|^{2}=\mathbf{1}_{(n,q)=1}
, and the relevant densities, with conductor-uniform secondary terms, are recorded in Lemma
21
below. The conductor itself enters the main terms in exactly one place: the zero-counting density and the additive functional equation carry the analytic-conductor logarithm
\log qT
rather than
\log T
, and this survives precisely in the second-moment term
K
of Sections
4
and
5
. We can now state the four asymptotic estimates. The first pair concerns the reciprocal moment.

Proposition 13
.

Assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
and that its zeros are simple, and let

and

be fixed. Then there is a sequence
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau_{m}\}
of admissible heights, with bounded gaps, such that, uniformly for

and

.

Assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
and that its zeros are simple, and let

and

be fixed. Then, uniformly for
3\leq q\leq T^{A}
and

in the sequence of Lemma

The second pair concerns the ratio moment, and differs in the arithmetic factor and in the admissible range of

Proposition 15
.

Assume GRH for

and that its zeros are simple, and let
0<\vartheta<1
and

be fixed. Then there is a sequence
\mathcal{T}
as above such that, uniformly for

\widetilde{M}_{1}=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\,\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T\;\vartheta\!\int_{0}^{1}P(x)\,dx+O\bigl(T\log\log 3q\bigr).

.

Assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
and that its zeros are simple, and let

and

be fixed. Then, uniformly for

and

Remark
.

For fixed

one has
\log qT=\log T+O(1)
and
\log\log 3qT=O(1)
, and the propositions reduce to their classical shapes, with second moments
\tfrac{T(\log T)^{2}}{2\pi}\mathfrak{a}(q)\bigl[\vartheta\int P^{2}+\vartheta^{2}(\int P)^{2}\bigr]
. Since

by Mertens’ theorem, the error terms above are smaller than the corresponding main terms by the relative factor

and

In Section

) and (

), optimise over

3.
Preliminary lemmas

We collect here the analytic facts used in the proofs. In contrast to the fixed-conductor setting, every lemma below is stated with explicit uniformity in
q
; implied constants are absolute unless a dependence is displayed.

The central tool is the mean value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials of Montgomery and Vaughan
[
18
]
, in the bilinear form due to Tsang
[
25
]
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be sequences of complex numbers. Then for any real
T>0
,

\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}n^{-it}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}n^{it}\Bigr)\,dt=T\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}b_{n}+O\!\left(\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 1}n|a_{n}|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{n\geq 1}n|b_{n}|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\right),

with an absolute implied constant.

We remark at once that Lemma
17
is the source of the conductor-independent cap on the mollifier: its error term is of size
\xi
times logarithms whatever the value of
q
(the coefficients see
\chi
only through
|\chi|\leq 1
), and must be dominated by main terms of size
T
times logarithms; hence
\xi\leq T^{1-\varepsilon}
, with no power of
q
available on either side. Throughout we apply Lemma
17
with the
t
-integration over
[1,T]
in place of
[0,T]
; the omitted
\int_{0}^{1}
contributes
O(\xi\log T)=o(T)
and is absorbed into the error terms.

Next we record the functional equation

(3.1)

L(s,\chi)=X(s,\chi)L(1-s,\bar{\chi})

and the consequences of Stirling’s formula for the factor
X
.

Lemma 18
.

For
s=\sigma+it
with
\sigma
in any fixed bounded interval and
|t|\geq 1
, uniformly in
q
,

|X(s,\chi)|\asymp\Bigl(\frac{q|t|}{2\pi}\Bigr)^{1/2-\sigma},\qquad\arg X(\sigma+it,\chi)=-t\log\frac{q|t|}{2\pi}+t+O(1),

and the latter asymptotic may be differentiated termwise, giving
\frac{d}{dt}\arg X=-\log\frac{q|t|}{2\pi}+O(1/t)
and
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\arg X=-1/t+O(1/t^{2})
; the conductor enters the phase only through its
t
-linear part. Moreover the logarithmic derivative satisfies, uniformly for
1\leq|t|\leq T
and in
q
,

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1-s,\bar{\chi})=-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)-\log\frac{q|t|}{2\pi}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{|t|}\right).

Under GRH we have the following size and reciprocal-size estimates, with the conductor inside the exponent; the second part is the Dirichlet analogue of a classical theorem of Titchmarsh
[
24
, Theorem 14.16]
; in the conductor aspect these bounds are standard (see, e.g.,
[
14
, Ch. 5]
).

Lemma 19
.

Assume GRH for
L(s,\chi)
. There is an absolute constant
c_{0}>0
such that, uniformly for
\sigma\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
,
|t|\geq 1
and all
q
,

|L(\sigma+it,\chi)|\ll\exp\!\left(\frac{c_{0}\log(q|t|)}{\log\log(q|t|)}\right)=(q|t|)^{o(1)},

and the same bound holds for
|L(\sigma+it,\chi)|^{-1}
at points bounded away from the zeros. In particular, for
q\leq T^{A}
and
|t|\leq T
these are
T^{o_{A}(1)}
, and there is a fixed
A_{0}>0
and a sequence
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau_{m}\}_{m}
with
m<\tau_{m}\leq m+1
on which

|L(\sigma+i\tau_{m},\chi)|^{-1}\ll\exp\!\left(\frac{A_{0}\log(q\tau_{m})}{\log\log(q\tau_{m})}\right)

uniformly for
-\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq 2
. For
\sigma<\tfrac{1}{2}
this follows from the case
\sigma\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
by the functional equation, since
|X(s,\chi)|^{-1}\ll 1
there and
\Re(1-s)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
.

For the horizontal segments of the contours we use the standard device of choosing the height to avoid the zeros (cf. Davenport
[
5
, p. 108]
), with the gaps now measured against the conductor density.

Lemma 20
.

There is a sequence of heights
T
, of bounded gaps, with
|\gamma-T|\gg 1/\log(qT)
for all ordinates
\gamma
of
L(s,\chi)
, and

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+iT,\chi)\ll(\log qT)^{2}

uniformly for
1-c\leq\sigma\leq c
, where
c=1+1/\log T
. There is likewise a bottom height
t_{0}=t_{0}(\chi)\in[1,2]
with
|\gamma-t_{0}|\gg 1/\log 3q
for all ordinates, on which
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it_{0},\chi)\ll(\log 3q)^{2}
and, under GRH,
|L(\sigma+it_{0},\chi)|^{-1}\ll q^{o(1)}
, uniformly for
1-c\leq\sigma\leq c
.

Such heights exist by the pigeonhole principle: the number of zeros in any interval of unit length is
O(\log qT)
(respectively
O(\log 3q)
near height
1
), so some sub-interval of length
\gg 1/\log qT
contains no ordinate. The sequence
\mathcal{T}
of Lemma
19
may be taken to satisfy the conditions of Lemma
20
as well.

The arithmetic densities, with conductor-uniform secondary terms, are as follows; they absorb what would otherwise be hidden
q
-dependence in the partial-summation steps, and they are the source of the arithmetic factor
A_{q}
.

Lemma 21
.

Uniformly for
x\geq 3
and all
q\geq 3
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\log x+O\Bigl(\log\log 3q+\frac{2^{\omega(q)}}{x}\Bigr)

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{n}=A_{q}\log x+O\Bigl(\log\log 3q+\frac{2^{\omega(q)}}{\sqrt{x}}\Bigr),

where
\omega(q)
is the number of distinct prime factors of
q
. Moreover

\sum_{p\mid q}\frac{\log p}{p}\ll\log\log 3q.

Proof.

For the prime sum, split at
y=2\log 3q
: Mertens’ theorem gives
\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p}\ll\log y\ll\log\log 3q
, while at most
\log q/\log y
primes dividing
q
exceed
y
, each contributing at most
\frac{\log y}{y}
, for a total
O(1)
.

For the first display, detect the coprimality condition by Möbius inversion over the square-free divisors
d
of
q
:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}=\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\sum_{m\leq x/d}\frac{1}{m}=\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\Bigl(\log\frac{x}{d}+O(1)+O\Bigl(\frac{d}{x}\Bigr)\Bigr).

The main term is
\bigl(\sum_{d\mid q}\mu(d)/d\bigr)\log x=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\log x
; the tail is
O(2^{\omega(q)}/x)
(divisors
d>x
give an empty inner sum, absorbed here since
\log(d/x)<d/x
); and the secondary term is controlled by the identity

\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{\mu(d)\log d}{d}=-\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\sum_{p\mid q}\frac{\log p}{p-1},

which is
\ll\log\log 3q
in absolute value, obtained by logarithmic differentiation of
\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-s})=\sum_{d\mid q}\mu(d)d^{-s}
at
s=1
, together with the prime-sum bound above.

For the second display, write
\mu(n)^{2}=\sum_{d^{2}\mid n}\mu(d)
, so that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq\sqrt{x}\\
(d,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq x/d^{2}\\
(m,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}.

Applying the first display to the inner sum and summing the result over
d
, the constant in front of
\log x
is

\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\sum_{(d,q)=1}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2}}=\prod_{p\mid q}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)\,\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\mid q}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\Bigr)^{-1}=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\mid q}\frac{p}{p+1}=A_{q},

which is (
1.4
), and the secondary terms are again
O(\log\log 3q+2^{\omega(q)}/\sqrt{x})
.
∎

In our applications
x=\xi\geq T^{\vartheta}
while
2^{\omega(q)}\leq q^{o(1)}\leq T^{o(1)}
, so the tails in Lemma
21
are negligible and we will quote the lemma with error
O(\log\log 3q)
only; in the partial-summation steps the lemma is integrated against a weight of derivative
\ll(x\log\xi)^{-1}
, so the tail
2^{\omega(q)}/x
contributes
\ll 2^{\omega(q)}/\log\xi=T^{o(1)}/\log T
, which is likewise negligible.

Finally, the oscillatory integrals in the proof of Proposition
15
are estimated by van der Corput’s second-derivative test
[
24
, Lemma 4.5]
.

Lemma 22
.

Let
F
be real-valued and twice continuously differentiable on
[a,b]
, with
F^{\prime\prime}(t)
of constant sign and
|F^{\prime\prime}(t)|\geq\Lambda>0
throughout. Let

be real-valued of bounded variation on

\left|\int_{a}^{b}g(t)\,e^{iF(t)}\,dt\right|\ll\Lambda^{-1/2}\Bigl(\sup_{[a,b]}|g|+\operatorname{Var}_{[a,b]}g\Bigr),

with an absolute implied constant.

Throughout the proofs we set

, so that

\zeta(c)\asymp\log T,\qquad\zeta(2c-1)\asymp\log T,\qquad\zeta(2c)\asymp 1,\qquad\xi^{c}\asymp\xi;

these are genuinely

\displaystyle A(t)

\displaystyle=t\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\xi\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}P_{n}^{2}}{n}+O\bigl(\xi(\log T)^{1/2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=A_{q}\,t\log\xi\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}+O\bigl(t\log\log 3q+\xi(\log T)^{1/2}\bigr).

Integrating by parts,

K=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigl[\log\tfrac{qt}{2\pi}\,A(t)\Bigr]_{1}^{T}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{A(t)}{t}\,dt.

The first term on the right-hand side contributes
\frac{1}{2\pi}A_{q}\,T\bigl(\log\tfrac{qT}{2\pi}\bigr)(\log\xi)\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}
to main order, while
\int_{1}^{T}\frac{A(t)}{t}dt=A_{q}\,T\log\xi\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}+O(T\log\log 3q)
is smaller by a full factor
\log qT
. Hence

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{q}}{2\pi}\,T(\log\xi)(\log qT)\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}+O\bigl(T\log T+T\log qT\log\log 3q\bigr)

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\,A_{q}\log qT\;\vartheta\log T\!\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}+O\bigl(T\log T\log\log 3qT\bigr),

using
\log qT\ll_{A}\log T
in the error.

We now evaluate
J_{1}
. On the line
\Re s=c
we expand

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\,\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(s)=\Bigl(\sum_{k}\Lambda(k)\chi(k)k^{-s}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{l\leq\xi}\mu(l)\chi(l)P_{l}\,l^{-s}\Bigr)=\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}n^{-s},

where
\Lambda
is the von Mangoldt function and, by complete multiplicativity of
\chi
, the coefficients factor as
\alpha_{n}=\chi(n)\,\widehat{\alpha}_{n}
with

\widehat{\alpha}_{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}kl=n\\
l\leq\xi\end{subarray}}\Lambda(k)\mu(l)P_{l}.

Applying Lemma
17
with this Dirichlet series against
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\chi}}(1-s)
on
\Re s=c
, the diagonal pairing
\chi(n)\overline{\chi(n)}=\mathbf{1}_{(n,q)=1}
forces
(n,q)=1
and leaves

J_{1}=-\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\xi\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\widehat{\alpha}_{n}\,\mu(n)P_{n}}{n}+O\bigl(\xi(\log T)^{3/2}\bigr).

We now evaluate the main sum. Since
\mu(n)\neq 0
requires
n
square-free, in
\sum_{kl=n}\Lambda(k)\mu(l)\,\mu(n)
the factor
\Lambda(k)
restricts
k
to a single prime
p
(a higher prime power would make
n=kl
non-square-free), and then
p\nmid l
and
\mu(pl)=-\mu(l)
, so

\Lambda(p)\,\mu(l)\,\mu(pl)=-\mu(l)^{2}\log p.

Carrying out the sum over
k
first, and writing
a=\frac{\log(\xi/l)}{\log\xi}
so that
P_{l}=P(a)
, only the primes
k=p
contribute. The prime number theorem in the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\nmid lq\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p}=\log y+O(\log\log 3lq),

where the cost of removing the primes dividing
lq
is controlled by Lemma
21
, together with partial summation, then yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq\xi/l\\
p\nmid lq\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p}\,P_{pl}=\log\xi\int_{0}^{a}P(u)\,du+O(\log\log 3qT).

Hence the main sum equals
-\sum_{l\leq\xi,\,(l,q)=1}\frac{\mu(l)^{2}P_{l}}{l}\Bigl(\log\xi\int_{0}^{a}P\Bigr)+O\bigl(\log\xi\log\log 3qT\bigr)
, and summing over
l
by Lemma
21
once more,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\xi\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\widehat{\alpha}_{n}\,\mu(n)P_{n}}{n}=-A_{q}(\log\xi)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}P(a)\Bigl(\int_{0}^{a}P(u)\,du\Bigr)da+O\bigl(\log\xi\log\log 3qT\bigr).

By the substitution
Q(a)=\int_{0}^{a}P
the inner double integral satisfies

\int_{0}^{1}P(a)\Bigl(\int_{0}^{a}P(u)\,du\Bigr)da=\int_{0}^{1}Q^{\prime}(a)Q(a)\,da=\tfrac{1}{2}Q(1)^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}P\Bigr)^{2},

so the main sum is
-\tfrac{A_{q}}{2}(\log\xi)^{2}\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}P\bigr)^{2}+O(\log\xi\log\log 3qT)
. Substituting into the expression for
J_{1}
and recalling
\log\xi=\vartheta\log T
,

(4.4)

2\Re J_{1}=\frac{T}{2\pi}\,A_{q}\log T\;\vartheta^{2}\log T\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}P\Bigr)^{2}+O\bigl(T\log T\log\log 3qT\bigr).

Adding (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) proves Proposition
14
.

Proof of Propositions

and

This section runs parallel to Section

, and the reader will recognise the overall structure at once. We nevertheless give the details in full: the changes, where they occur, are subtle, and one step, the left edge of

5.1.
The first moment

Let

, let

We begin with the right edge

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\bar{\chi}}(1-s)=\sum_{n\leq\xi}\lambda(n)\bar{\chi}(n)P_{n}n^{s-1}

I_{1}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\Bigl(\sum_{m}\frac{\lambda(m)\chi(m)}{m^{c+it}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\lambda(n)\bar{\chi}(n)P_{n}}{n^{1-c-it}}\Bigr)\,dt.

Applying Lemma
17
with
a_{m}=\lambda(m)\chi(m)m^{-c}
and
b_{n}=\lambda(n)\bar{\chi}(n)P_{n}n^{c-1}
,

I_{1}=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{n\leq\xi}\lambda(n)^{2}|\chi(n)|^{2}\frac{P_{n}}{n}+O\!\left(\Bigl(\sum_{m}m|a_{m}|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{n\leq\xi}n|b_{n}|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\right).

Since
\lambda(n)^{2}=1
, the main sum is
\sum_{n\leq\xi,(n,q)=1}P_{n}/n
. For the error,

\sum_{m}m|a_{m}|^{2}=\sum_{m}|\chi(m)|^{2}m^{1-2c}\leq\zeta(2c-1)\ll\log T

\sum_{n\leq\xi}n|b_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{n\leq\xi}|\chi(n)|^{2}P_{n}^{2}\,n^{2c-1}\ll\xi^{2c}\ll\xi^{2},

with absolute constants, so the error is
O\bigl(\xi(\log T)^{1/2}\bigr)
. By the first density of Lemma
21
and partial summation,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\xi\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{P_{n}}{n}=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\log\xi\int_{0}^{1}P+O(\log\log 3q),

hence

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}(\log\xi)\int_{0}^{1}P+O\bigl(\xi(\log T)^{1/2}+T\log\log 3q\bigr)

(5.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\,\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T\;\vartheta\!\int_{0}^{1}P+O\bigl(T\log\log 3q\bigr).

Next we bound the horizontal edges. On
I_{2}
we now have, in addition to the factor
|L(\sigma+iT,\chi)|^{-1}\ll(qT)^{o(1)}
from the
-\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq 2

, the numerator
|L(2\sigma+2iT,\chi^{2})|
. For
\sigma\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
this is
\ll(qT)^{o(1)}
since
2\sigma\geq 1
(the imprimitive Euler factors contributing at most

), while for
1-c\leq\sigma<\tfrac{1}{2}
the convexity bound for the primitive character inducing

(whose conductor divides

|L(2\sigma+2iT,\chi^{2})|\ll(qT)^{1/2-\sigma+o(1)}
. The integrand is therefore
\ll(qT)^{o(1)}\bigl(\xi+(qT)^{1/2}\bigr)
, and

I_{2}\ll\xi\,(qT)^{o(1)}+(qT)^{1/2+o(1)}=o(T)\qquad\text{precisely when }q\leq T^{1-\delta}.

On the bottom edge, at the admissible height
t_{0}
, convexity gives
|L(2\sigma+2it_{0},\chi^{2})|\ll q^{1/2-\sigma+o(1)}
for
\sigma<\tfrac{1}{2}
and
|L(\sigma+it_{0},\chi)|^{-1}\ll q^{o(1)}

I_{4}\ll\bigl(\xi+q^{1/2}\bigr)(qT)^{o(1)}=o(T)

for
q\leq T^{1-\delta}
.

Remark
.

The estimate for
I_{2}
is the only point in the paper at which the range of
q
is restricted; every other estimate in this section is uniform for
q\leq T^{A}
. The restriction enters because we know only the convexity bound for
L(\cdot,\chi^{2})
in the strip
0<\Re w<1
. If one assumes GRH for
L(s,\chi^{2})
, the restriction can be removed. Keeping the left edge at
\Re s=1-c
does not help: the worst horizontal contribution is near
\sigma=1-c
, where
2\sigma\approx 0
and even GRH gives
|L(2\sigma+2iT,\chi^{2})|\asymp(qT)^{1/2}
, the same size as convexity. The efficient route is instead to move the left edge of the rectangle to
\Re s=\tfrac{1}{4}
, so that
2s
stays on or to the right of the critical line of
L(\cdot,\chi^{2})
and the Lindelöf-type bound of Lemma
19
applies to it. The horizontal edges are then
\ll\xi(qT)^{o(1)}
, the new left edge is
\ll q^{-1/4}T^{3/4}\xi^{1/4}(qT)^{o(1)}=o(T)
, and the range extends to
q\leq T^{A}
as in Theorem
5
. This is also the route taken by Sinha
[
23
]
at
q=1
, and it renders the oscillation argument for the left edge below unnecessary. We prefer to keep Theorem
6
unconditional in
\chi^{2}
, which forces the left edge to stay at
\Re s=1-c
and is the reason for the functional-equation reflection that follows.

It remains to bound the left edge
I_{3}
, and here the argument departs from Section
4
. On
\Re s=1-c
we have
2\Re s=2-2c=-2/\log T<0
, so
2s
lies just to the left of the critical strip of
L(\cdot,\chi^{2})
, and by the functional equation
|L(2s,\chi^{2})|\ll(q|t|)^{1/2}T^{o(1)}
, a bound of genuinely growing size. This growth offsets the decay
|L(s,\chi)|^{-1}\ll(q|t|)^{-1/2}\log T
established in Section
4
: the product of the two bounds no longer decays in
t
, and the resulting trivial estimate
I_{3}\ll T(\log T)^{3}
exceeds the main term. We therefore exploit the oscillation of the archimedean factor instead.

Let
\chi^{*}
be the primitive character, of conductor
q^{*}\mid q
, that induces
\chi^{2}
, so that

L(2s,\chi^{2})=E(2s)\,L(2s,\chi^{*}),\qquad E(w)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid q\\
p\nmid q^{*}\end{subarray}}\bigl(1-\chi^{*}(p)p^{-w}\bigr),

the factor
E
being a finite Euler product. Applying the functional equations
L(2s,\chi^{*})=X(2s,\chi^{*})L(1-2s,\bar{\chi}^{*})
and
L(s,\chi)=X(s,\chi)L(1-s,\bar{\chi})
, we may write, for
s=(1-c)+it
with
1\leq t\leq T
,

(5.2)

\frac{L(2s,\chi^{2})}{L(s,\chi)}=\underbrace{\frac{X(2s,\chi^{*})}{X(s,\chi)}}_{G(t)}\;E(2s)\;\frac{L(1-2s,\bar{\chi}^{*})}{L(1-s,\bar{\chi})},

where
G(t)
denotes the quotient of the two archimedean factors. The point of (
5.2
) is that
G(t)
is a ratio of two primitive archimedean factors, while the remaining factors are evaluated in the region of absolute convergence: indeed
\Re(1-2s)=2c-1>1
and
\Re(1-s)=c>1
, while
E(2s)=\sum_{d}c_{d}\,d^{-2s}
is a Dirichlet polynomial supported on square-free
d\mid\prod_{p\mid q}p
with
|c_{d}|\leq 1
. Writing
s=(1-c)+it
, these factors expand as

\begin{gathered}L(1-2s,\bar{\chi}^{*})=\sum_{a\geq 1}\frac{\overline{\chi^{*}}(a)}{a^{2c-1}}\,(a^{2})^{it},\qquad\frac{1}{L(1-s,\bar{\chi})}=\sum_{b\geq 1}\frac{\mu(b)\bar{\chi}(b)}{b^{c}}\,b^{it},\\
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\bar{\chi}}(1-s)=\sum_{m\leq\xi}\frac{\lambda(m)\bar{\chi}(m)P_{m}}{m^{c}}\,m^{it},\end{gathered}

together with
E(2s)=\sum_{d}c_{d}\,d^{2(c-1)}\,(d^{2})^{-it}
, the last carrying negative frequencies. Their product is therefore an absolutely convergent sum of oscillations over the positive rational frequencies
r=a^{2}bm/d^{2}
,

E(2s)\,\frac{L(1-2s,\bar{\chi}^{*})}{L(1-s,\bar{\chi})}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\bar{\chi}}(1-s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,d,b\geq 1\\
m\leq\xi\end{subarray}}\beta_{a,d,b,m}\,r^{it},

with coefficients

\beta_{a,d,b,m}=c_{d}\,d^{2(c-1)}\,\frac{\overline{\chi^{*}}(a)}{a^{2c-1}}\,\frac{\mu(b)\bar{\chi}(b)}{b^{c}}\,\frac{\lambda(m)\bar{\chi}(m)P_{m}}{m^{c}},

so that

I_{3}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{a,d,b,m}\beta_{a,d,b,m}\,J_{r},\qquad J_{r}=\int_{1}^{T}G(t)\,r^{it}\,dt.

We bound each
J_{r}
uniformly in
r
and in
q
by Lemma
22
. By Lemma
18
, the modulus

|G(t)|=\frac{|X(2-2c+2it,\chi^{*})|}{|X(1-c+it,\chi)|}\asymp\Bigl(\frac{q^{*}}{q}\Bigr)^{1/2}\bigl(qq^{*}t\bigr)^{O(1/\log T)}\ll e^{O(A)}\ll 1

for
q\leq T^{A}
and
1\leq t\leq T
, with monotone modulus up to
O(1/t)
, so
\sup|G|+\operatorname{Var}G\ll 1
with constants depending only on
A
. For the phase, write
\psi_{r}(t)=\arg G(t)+t\log r
; the term
t\log r
is linear in
t
whatever the (rational) frequency
r>0
, so Lemma
22
applies just as for integer frequencies. By the differentiated Stirling asymptotic of Lemma
18
, in which the conductors of
\chi
and
\chi^{*}
enter only the
t
-linear part of the phase and hence shift
\psi_{r}^{\prime}
but not
\psi_{r}^{\prime\prime}
,

\psi_{r}^{\prime}(t)=-\log t+\log r+c_{q,q^{*}}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{t}\right),\qquad\psi_{r}^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{t}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right),

where
c_{q,q^{*}}=\log\frac{q}{2\pi}-2\log\frac{q^{*}}{\pi}\ll\log q
is constant in
t
(it shifts the stationary point but not the curvature). The second derivative is independent of
r
and of
q
and, for
t\geq t_{1}
with
t_{1}
an absolute constant, is of constant sign with
|\psi_{r}^{\prime\prime}(t)|\geq T^{-1}(1+o(1))
on
[t_{1},T]
; the initial segment
[1,t_{1}]
contributes
O(1)
to
J_{r}
trivially. Lemma
22
therefore gives, uniformly in
r
and
q
,

|J_{r}|\ll\Bigl(\min_{[t_{1},T]}|\psi_{r}^{\prime\prime}|\Bigr)^{-1/2}\ll\sqrt{T}.

It remains to bound the coefficient sum. The imprimitivity polynomial contributes

\sum_{d}|c_{d}|\,d^{2(c-1)}\leq 2^{\omega(q)}q^{2/\log T}=T^{o(1)}\qquad(q\leq T^{A}),

and hence

\sum_{a,d,b,m}|\beta_{a,d,b,m}|\ll T^{o(1)}\Bigl(\sum_{a}\frac{1}{a^{2c-1}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{b}\frac{\mu(b)^{2}}{b^{c}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\max_{[0,1]}|P|\sum_{m\leq\xi}\frac{1}{m^{c}}\Bigr)\leq T^{o(1)}\,\zeta(2c-1)\,\frac{\zeta(c)^{2}}{\zeta(2c)}\max_{[0,1]}|P|,

which is
\ll T^{o(1)}(\log T)^{3}
since

and

. Combining the last three displays,

(5.3)

I_{3}\ll\Bigl(\sum_{a,d,b,m}|\beta_{a,d,b,m}|\Bigr)\,\sup_{r}|J_{r}|\ll T^{1/2+o(1)}=o(T).

No hypothesis on

has been used: the reflected

-factors in (

) are evaluated only in

, and

is a finite Euler product. Combining (

), the horizontal bounds, and (

) proves Proposition

.

The second moment

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\xi\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\widetilde{\alpha}_{n}\,\lambda(n)P_{n}}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}kl\leq\xi\\
(kl,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(k)\lambda(l)P_{l}\,\lambda(kl)P_{kl}}{kl}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}kl\leq\xi\\
(kl,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(k)\lambda(k)\,P_{l}P_{kl}}{kl}.

The factor
\Lambda(k)\lambda(k)
is supported on prime powers, with
\Lambda(p)\lambda(p)=-\log p
on primes (and
\Lambda(p^{j})\lambda(p^{j})=(-1)^{j}\log p
for
j\geq 2
contributing
O(\log\xi)
in total, absorbed into the error term), and vanishes for
p\mid q
. Writing
a=\frac{\log(\xi/l)}{\log\xi}
and carrying out the sum over
k
first, the prime number theorem in the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p}=\log y+O(\log\log 3q)

and partial summation give

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq\xi/l\\
(k,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(k)\lambda(k)}{k}\,P_{kl}=-\log\xi\int_{0}^{a}P(u)\,du+O(\log\log 3q).

Summing over
l\leq\xi
with
(l,q)=1
by Lemma
21
, and writing
Q(a)=\int_{0}^{a}P
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\xi\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\widetilde{\alpha}_{n}\,\lambda(n)P_{n}}{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}(\log\xi)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}P(a)Q(a)\,da+O\bigl(\log\xi\log\log 3qT\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varphi(q)}{2q}(\log\xi)^{2}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}P\Bigr)^{2}+O\bigl(\log\xi\log\log 3qT\bigr),

since

\int_{0}^{1}P(a)Q(a)\,da=\int_{0}^{1}Q^{\prime}(a)Q(a)\,da=\tfrac{1}{2}Q(1)^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}P\Bigr)^{2}.

Substituting into the expression for
\widetilde{J}_{1}
and recalling
\log\xi=\vartheta\log T
,

(5.5)

2\Re\widetilde{J}_{1}=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\,\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T\;\vartheta^{2}\log T\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}P\Bigr)^{2}+O\bigl(T\log T\log\log 3qT\bigr).

Adding (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) proves Proposition
16
.

6.
Optimisation of the mollifier, the conductor deficit, and the conjectures

6.1.
Proof of Theorems
5
and
6

We treat both theorems at once; write
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}(q)
for the arithmetic factor (
1.5
), and let
(M_{1},M_{2})
stand for the relevant pair from (
2.2
) or (
2.3
), with
q
in the relevant range.

Fix
T\geq 4
and choose a height
\tau\in\mathcal{T}
with
T-2\leq\tau<T
, possible since the gaps in
\mathcal{T}
are bounded. As the summand in (
2.2
) (respectively (
2.3
)) is non-negative, the moment up to
T
dominates the moment up to
\tau
, so it suffices to prove the bound at
\tau
. By Propositions
13
and
14
(respectively
15
and
16
), and since
\mathfrak{a}(q)\gg 1/\log\log q
makes every error term smaller than its main term by the uniform relative factor
O\bigl((\log\log qT)^{2}/\log T\bigr)
,

\frac{|M_{1}|^{2}}{M_{2}}=\frac{\Bigl(\frac{T}{2\pi}\mathfrak{a}\log T\;\vartheta\!\int_{0}^{1}P\Bigr)^{2}}{\frac{T}{2\pi}\mathfrak{a}\log T\Bigl[\log qT\;\vartheta\!\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}+\log T\;\vartheta^{2}\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}P\bigr)^{2}\Bigr]}\;\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr),

with the
o(1)
uniform in the stated ranges of
q
. Cancelling the common factors,

(6.1)

\frac{|M_{1}|^{2}}{M_{2}}=\frac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}\,T\,\frac{ur}{1+ur}\,\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr),\qquad u=\frac{\log\xi}{\log qT}=\vartheta\beta,\quad r=\frac{\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}P\bigr)^{2}}{\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}},

which is increasing in both
u
and
r
. Equation (
6.1
) is the key formula of the paper: every statement in Section
1
is a reading of it.

The two variables are constrained as follows. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality
\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}P\bigr)^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{1}1^{2}\,\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}
, so
r\leq 1
. The constraints
P(0)=0
and
P(1)=1
exclude the constant polynomial, so
r=1
is a supremum rather than a maximum; it is approached by the admissible choice
P(x)=1-(1-x)^{N}
as
N\to\infty
, for which
\int_{0}^{1}P=\tfrac{N}{N+1}
and
\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}=\tfrac{2N^{2}}{(N+1)(2N+1)}
, so that
r\to 1
.

As for
u
, we have
u\leq\beta
: the mean-value error terms of Lemma
17
cap the mollifier at
\xi\leq T^{1-\varepsilon}
independently of
q
(the remark following the lemma), that is,
\vartheta<1
.

Hence, given
\varepsilon>0
, taking
N
large and then
\vartheta
close enough to
1
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|R(\rho)|^{2}\geq\frac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}\,\frac{ur}{1+ur}\,T\,\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}\,\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\,T

for all sufficiently large
T
, uniformly in the stated ranges of
q
, where
R(\rho)
is the relevant summand and we used that
v\mapsto v/(1+v)
is increasing with
\tfrac{\vartheta\beta r}{1+\vartheta\beta r}\to\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}
as
\vartheta,r\to 1
, uniformly in
\beta\in(0,1)
.

With
\mathfrak{a}=A_{q}
this is Theorem
5
, and with
\mathfrak{a}=\varphi(q)/q
it is Theorem
6
. the small-conductor estimates follow since
\log q=o(\log T)
gives
\beta\to 1
and
\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}\to\tfrac{1}{2}
, and the power-conductor estimate follows since
q=T^{A}
gives
\beta=\tfrac{1}{1+A}
and
\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}=\tfrac{1}{2+A}
. ∎

6.2.
The conductor deficit and the conjectures

The key formula (
6.1
) tells the whole story at once. The method produces the quantity
\tfrac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}\tfrac{u}{1+u}T
at
r\to 1
; the mean value theorem caps
u
at
\beta=\log T/\log qT
; and the cap is conductor-independent because the
t
-integration window
[1,T]
, not the conductor, sets the resolution of the mean value theorem: a single
L
-function observed up to height
T
cannot support a mollifier longer than
T
, while the zero density it must match grows with
\log qT
. For small conductors
\beta\to 1
and the proved value is half the limiting one; for
q=T^{A}
the proportion drops to
\tfrac{1}{2+A}
.

In the opposite direction, Farmer’s long-mollifier heuristic
[
8
]
corresponds to letting
u\to\infty
in (
6.1
): the formal limit is
\tfrac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}T
, and this is the content of Conjectures
7
and
8
. In particular our theorems are, conjecturally, exactly the proportion
\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}
of the truth, one half in the small-conductor regime, the deficiency being the ratio of the value of
u\mapsto\tfrac{u}{1+u}
at the cap
u=\beta
to its limiting value
1
, a structural feature of the diagonal Cauchy–Schwarz construction rather than a numerical accident.

This heuristic is, of course, not a proof, and it is applied here to a discrete sum over zeros rather than to the continuous mean values for which it was framed. Its main external support is the case
q=1
, where the limiting values
\tfrac{3}{\pi^{3}}T
and
\tfrac{T}{2\pi}
coincide with the independently-formulated Conjectures
1
and
3
of Gonek and Ng. As noted in Section
1
, it would be of interest to see whether Conjectures
7
and
8
can be derived from the recipe of Conrey–Farmer–Keating–Rubinstein–Snaith
[
2
]
or from the
L
-functions Ratios Conjecture
[
3
,
4
]
.

7.
Proof of Corollaries in Section
1.5

The four corollaries we prove here are elementary consequences of the second-moment lower bounds of Theorems
5
and
6
: each follows from a single application of Hölder’s inequality, fed only by the Riemann–von Mangoldt count of the zeros of
L(s,\chi)
, and uses no further information about the zeros or the
L
-function. We first prove the single-character bounds, Corollaries
11
and
12
; the family forms then follow by summing over the primitive characters modulo
q
.

Proof of Corollaries
11
and
12
.

Write
R(\rho)
for the summand in each corollary and
\mathfrak{a}(q)
for the arithmetic factor, so
\mathfrak{a}(q)=A_{q}
in the reciprocal case and
\mathfrak{a}(q)=\varphi(q)/q
in the ratio case. For
k=1
the statements are Theorems
5
and
6
, so assume
k>1
. By Hölder’s inequality with exponents
k
and
k/(k-1)
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|R(\rho)|^{2}\;\leq\;\Bigl(\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|R(\rho)|^{2k}\Bigr)^{1/k}N(T,\chi)^{1-1/k},

where
N(T,\chi)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{qT}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log qT)
is
the zero-counting function for
L(s,\chi)
(see Davenport
[
5
, Ch. 16]
), uniformly for
q\leq T^{A}
this is
\frac{T}{2\pi}\log qT\,(1+o(1))
. Rearranging and inserting the relevant second-moment bound (Theorem
5
, resp.
6
) for
the numerator,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|R(\rho)|^{2k}\;\geq\;\frac{\bigl(\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|R(\rho)|^{2}\bigr)^{k}}{N(T,\chi)^{\,k-1}}\;\geq\;\frac{\bigl((1-\varepsilon)\tfrac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}T\bigr)^{k}}{\bigl(\tfrac{T}{2\pi}\log qT\bigr)^{k-1}}\,(1+o(1)).

Since
\bigl(\tfrac{\mathfrak{a}(q)}{2\pi}\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}\bigr)^{k}\big/\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\bigr)^{k-1}=\tfrac{\mathfrak{a}(q)^{k}}{2\pi}\bigl(\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}\bigr)^{k}
, this gives the first display of each corollary after absorbing
(1-\varepsilon)^{k}(1+o(1))
into a single
1-\varepsilon
. The explicit form follows from
\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}=\bigl(2+\tfrac{\log q}{\log T}\bigr)^{-1}
. When
\log q=o(\log T)
one has
\tfrac{\beta}{1+\beta}\to\tfrac{1}{2}
and
\log qT\sim\log T
, yielding the small-conductor displays.
∎

At
q=1
these recover the Hölder bounds for the zeta-function:

A_{1}=6/\pi^{2}
gives, at
k=1
, the constant
A_{1}/4\pi=3/2\pi^{3}
for the reciprocal moment, while
\varphi(1)/1=1
gives
\tfrac{1}{2^{k+1}\pi}\,T/(\log T)^{k-1}
for the ratio
moment.

The family forms require no further work: the bounds just proved are uniform in
\chi
and depend on it only through the modulus
q
, so they may be summed over the
\varphi^{*}(q)
primitive characters.

8.
Further directions: family averages

We conclude by recording what the preceding calculation suggests about averages over the family of primitive characters modulo
q
. Conjectures
9
and
10
should be viewed as the uniform family versions of Conjectures
7
and
8
. Indeed, for fixed
q
they follow formally by averaging the individual conjectures over the
\varphi^{*}(q)
primitive characters modulo
q
, since the constants
A_{q}/2\pi
and
(\varphi(q)/q)/2\pi
depend on the modulus but not on the particular character. When
q
is allowed to grow with
T
, the same statements require the corresponding uniformity in the character aspect.

The theorems of this paper already give uniform family lower bounds by summing over primitive characters. Thus, at
k=1
, the family lower bounds capture the proportion

\frac{\beta}{1+\beta},\qquad\beta=\frac{\log T}{\log qT},

of the conjectural family main terms, exactly the proportion of the individual estimates. The reason is that the proof treats each
L(s,\chi)
separately: the mollifier length is restricted by the height interval
[1,T]
, and hence by
\xi\leq T^{1-\varepsilon}
, independently of the conductor.

A natural next question is whether a genuinely family-averaged argument can lessen this conductor loss. One would apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality once over all pairs
(\chi,\rho)
, with a common mollifier length and polynomial but the usual
\chi
-twisted mollifier for each character. The resulting family second moment would involve sums over primitive characters, and hence the orthogonality relation

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\bmod q}\chi(m)\bar{\chi}(n)=\sum_{d\mid(q,\,m-n)}\mu(q/d)\,\varphi(d)\qquad((mn,q)=1).

Its off-diagonal terms are therefore organised by the congruence conditions
m\equiv n\pmod{d}
with
d\mid q
, rather than by equality alone. This extra averaging may allow one to replace the single-character length restriction
\xi\leq T^{1-\varepsilon}
by a family-length restriction of size roughly
\xi\leq(qT)^{1-\varepsilon}
.

In the notation of (
6.1
), such an improvement would let

u=\frac{\log\xi}{\log qT}

approach
1
, rather than only
\beta=\log T/\log qT
, and the Cauchy–Schwarz factor would then satisfy

\frac{u}{1+u}\longrightarrow\tfrac{1}{2}.

Thus a direct family average of this type could plausibly lessen the deficit in the conductor aspect, for instance improving the captured proportion from
1/(2+A)
to
\tfrac{1}{2}
when
q=T^{A}
. It would not, however, remove the deficit within the present mollifier framework: the conjectures correspond to the formal long-mollifier limit
u\to\infty
, not merely to
u\to 1
.

Making this rigorous would require conductor-uniform analogues of the explicit formula of Landau
[
16
]
and Gonek
[
11
,
12
]
(see also
[
6
]
) for the zero-sums

\sum_{0<\gamma_{\chi}\leq T}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{i\gamma_{\chi}},

averaged over primitive characters modulo
q
, uniformly in both the conductor and the frequencies
m/n
. Such formulae must control the prime-power contributions and the primitive-character congruences simultaneously, which lies beyond the single-character estimates used here; we do not pursue it.

Finally, the individual
L(s,\chi)
considered here and the family of primitive characters modulo
q
are both of unitary type in the sense of Katz and Sarnak
[
15
]
. It would be interesting to investigate the corresponding discrete moments in families of other symmetry types, for example the symplectic family of real characters or orthogonal families of modular-form
L
-functions. The results of the present paper apply to each individual member of such a family when the relevant hypotheses are assumed; the genuinely new question is whether averaging over the family permits longer mollifiers, and hence stronger lower bounds, than are available one
L
-function at a time.
