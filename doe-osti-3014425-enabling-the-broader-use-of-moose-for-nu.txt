2 Executive summary
This Final Scientific and Technical Report summarizes work performed under the Phase IIA SBIR project
“Enabling the Broader Use of MOOSE for Nuclear Energy and Other Simulation” (DE-SC0020906) from
August 2023 through August 2025. The objective of the Phase IIA effort was to mature and harden capabilities
developed during Phase II, with the goal of enabling practical interoperability between Coreform’s isogeometric
analysis (IGA) technologies and the Multiphysics Object-Oriented Simulation Environment (MOOSE), while
improving robustness, performance, and scalability for complex, nuclear-relevant geometries.
 Over the course of Phase IIA, the project established and validated an extraction-based interoperability
pathway between Coreform tools and MOOSE. A combined mesh and matrix format was defined collaboratively
with MOOSE developers and integrated into the solver, enabling standard MOOSE workflows to operate on
data exported from Coreform’s IGA and Flex Representation Method (FRM) pipelines. Early demonstrations
validated architectural compatibility using linear solid mechanics problems, while later efforts focused on
benchmark testing and external use. By the end of the project period, engineers at BWXT were able to
independently set up and execute a simulation using the Coreform–MOOSE workflow and provide direct
feedback that informed further refinement.
 In parallel, substantial effort was devoted to improving the robustness of trimmed U-spline construction for
complex CAD geometries. A growing test suite of nuclear-relevant models was compiled through collaboration
with multiple stakeholders and used to drive extensive bug fixing and reliability improvements. These efforts
resulted in improved robustness and performance, including the addition of fallback capabilities that enhance
reliability when the underlying commercial CAD kernel fails.
 Performance-oriented work progressed later in the project, with the development and demonstration of
methods to decompose complex geometries into structured subregions and updated data representations
to support more efficient solver processing. Additionally, extensive enhancements to threadsafe parallel
data structures and trimming operations established a foundation for scalable processing of large assemblies.
Collaboration with Sandia National Laboratories on the SGM geometric modeling kernel advanced to a
functioning interface test case, positioning the workflow for future kernel integration.
 Overall, the Phase IIA effort successfully transitioned the project from architectural proof-of-concept to
externally exercised, solver-integrated capability, while clarifying remaining technical challenges related to
standardization, performance optimization, and kernel integration.

3 Accomplishments
3.1 What are the major goals and objectives of this project?
We include for convenience the project’s proposed Technical Objectives:

 1. Technical Objective 1: IGA simulations in MOOSE
 2. Technical Objective 2: Robust conversion of CAD data to trimmed U-splines
 3. Technical Objective 3: Performance on single nuclear parts
 4. Technical Objective 4: Parallelization to handle complex reactor assemblies

3.2 What has been accomplished under these goals?
We report our Phase IIA project accomplishments below, organized by technical objectives and associated
tasks.

3.2.1 Technical Objective 1: IGA simulations in MOOSE
Technical Objective 1 focused on enabling isogeometric analysis (IGA) simulations in the Multiphysics
Object-Oriented Simulation Environment (MOOSE) through an extraction-based interoperability workflow.
Work under this objective established the necessary data representations, implemented export and import
pathways, and demonstrated execution of simulations using standard MOOSE workflows, culminating in
successful use of the workflow by external engineers.
Task 1.1: Specification for open combined mesh and matrix format
The project defined a combined mesh and matrix representation suitable for transferring IGA-related data
from Coreform tools into MOOSE. An initial approach paired an Exodus-based mesh representation with
a PETSc binary matrix representation to support solver integration and testing. During development,
limitations were identified that made the PETSc binary matrix representation unsuitable as the foundation
for a broadly adopted open standard due to build-dependent discrepancies (for example, differences between
32-bit and 64-bit builds). As a result, the combined representation was treated as an evolving component of
the workflow and adjusted as needed to carry additional information required to improve robustness and
performance on the solver side.
Task 1.2: Develop extraction format based on mesh and matrix outputs
An extraction-data export capability was implemented within Coreform software to generate both a mesh
file and a matrix file consistent with the combined representation. The export pathway preserves CAD sets
within the mesh output so that geometric groupings can be reused for downstream problem definition, such as
the application of boundary conditions and loads within solver workflows. Initial implementations supported
conforming representations, and the export pathway was refined over the course of the project in coordination
with solver-integration requirements.
Task 1.3: Support use of the new extraction format in MOOSE
The extraction-based interoperability workflow was integrated into MOOSE and exercised through a solid
mechanics simulation. As shown in Figure 1, the workflow was used to execute a linear elastic solid mechanics
simulation of a C-clamp geometry using the standard MOOSE pipeline. This example demonstrates that
data exported from Coreform tools can be consumed by MOOSE without the need for special-case solver
workflows, and that analysis can be carried out using MOOSE’s existing infrastructure.
 Collaboration with the MOOSE development team during this integration also resulted in improvements
to MOOSE’s handling of imported data, contributing to increased robustness and performance of the
interoperability pathway.
Task 1.4: Test on customer benchmarks
The integrated Coreform–MOOSE workflow was exercised on benchmark models in collaboration with external
engineers. Working directly with BWXT, appropriate benchmark geometries were identified and used to test
the workflow. BWXT engineers successfully set up and executed a simulation using the extraction-based IGA
workflow in MOOSE and provided feedback that informed further refinements to robustness and usability.

3.2.2 Technical Objective 2: Robust conversion of CAD data to trimmed U-splines
Technical Objective 2 addressed the robustness of converting complex CAD data into trimmed U-spline
representations suitable for IGA and Flex Representation Method (FRM) workflows. Work under this
objective emphasized systematic testing on challenging geometries, identification and correction of failure
modes, and the development of mechanisms to improve reliability when processing complex inputs.
Task 2.1: Demonstrate full Flex IGA spectrum on complex nuclear part
The project exercised Flex IGA capabilities on complex CAD geometries provided through collaboration
with nuclear-industry stakeholders, including geometries subject to information restrictions that limited
the ability to include detailed imagery. In addition to nuclear-relevant examples, the project applied the
same meshing-free workflow to other complex geometries to demonstrate the ability to process and analyze
feature-rich models within a single toolchain. For example, flex cable geometries were simulated to assess
durability-related behavior, and a full-assembly vehicle brake disk was analyzed to extract natural frequencies,
illustrating the application of the workflow to large, detailed assemblies (see Figure 2 and Figure 3).

Figure 2: Example of complex geometries that have been processed using the tools developed in part during
this Phase IIA SBIR effort: Flex cables simulated to determine parameters for bending radius during assembly
needed to prevent cable delamination over decades-long service life.

 These examples demonstrate the ability of the tools developed in this project to handle complex geometries
without reliance on traditional, labor-intensive finite element meshing workflows.
Task 2.2: Compile test suite of nuclear CAD models
A test suite of complex, nuclear-relevant CAD models was identified and used to support ongoing development
and validation. The suite was assembled through collaboration with multiple stakeholders and was used
to repeatedly exercise trimmed U-spline construction workflows. Over the course of the project, the size
and complexity of the test suite increased, enabling systematic exposure of edge cases and supporting
regression-style testing as robustness improvements were implemented.

3.2.3 Technical Objective 3: Performance on single nuclear parts
Technical Objective 3 focused on improving performance for individual parts by identifying and exploiting
structured subregions within complex geometries. This objective produced concrete methods and data
structures to support efficient downstream processing.
Task 3.1: Develop algorithms to decompose large meshes into structured subregions
The project developed functional data structures and a working decomposition algorithm that partitions
suitable geometries into structured cuboidal subregions. A representative example is shown in Figure 4,
where a Boolean-composed volume is decomposed into four cuboidal regions. The resulting decomposition
output contains the information required to compute extraction operators that enable efficient processing of
structured regions within solver workflows.

Task 3.2: Optimize processing of structured subregions
An updated internal representation and serialization format was developed to incorporate structured spline
blocks identified through the decomposition process. This representation supports more efficient downstream

handling of solver data associated with structured regions, contributing to improved performance for geometries

3.2.4 Technical Objective 4: Parallelization to handle complex reactor assemblies
Technical Objective 4 addressed scalability and parallel execution, with emphasis on threadsafe data structures,
parallel trimming operations, and collaboration with Sandia National Laboratories on capabilities needed to
support multithreaded geometric processing.
Task 4.1: Leverage SGM kernel to enable parallel and multithreaded processing
The project collaborated with the SGM development team by providing test geometries and interface
requirements needed to support trimming and related operations. A functioning interface test case satisfying
the specified requirements was produced, and internal testing of this interface was prepared. In addition, new
base mesh configurations aligned with cylindrical and other nuclear-relevant geometries were developed to
support downstream use cases, as illustrated in Figure 5.

Task 4.2: Enable threadsafe parallel data structures for trimmed U-spline construction
Significant improvements were made to data structures supporting trimmed U-spline construction, including
threadsafe parallel implementations that provide consistent indexing for parallel workflows. Methods for
exchanging data between parallel processes were developed and enhanced to support efficient parallel execution
and to prepare the workflow for larger assemblies.
Task 4.3: Parallel construction of trimmed U-splines
Functional capability for parallel construction of trimmed U-splines was achieved and exercised on complex
CAD geometries. Substantial progress was made to improve the robustness and performance of trimming
operations supporting parallel construction workflows, contributing to increased reliability and efficiency
when processing large or complex models under parallel execution.

3.3 What opportunities for training and professional development has the project provided?
Coreform engages in intensive one-on-one mentoring. Nearly every task completed during this period of
performance was accomplished through such mentoring. This is driven by necessity—the senior members
of Coreform each have diverse and complementary skillsets, so we are continually collaborating to create
innovative solutions to problems. Additionally, we have a number of recent hires at Coreform and we are
continually investing in their development to scale the size of our team. Mentored activities include sharing
development best practices, code reviews, and technical mentoring.

3.4 How have the results been disseminated to communities of interest?
The project included activities intended to disseminate technical results and to support broader understanding
and use of the developed capabilities within the engineering and nuclear simulation communities.
 Project team members presented technical work related to this project at the IGA 2024 conference.
This presentation provided an opportunity to share details of the isogeometric analysis and interoperability
approaches developed during the project with an audience of researchers and practitioners working in IGA
and related computational methods.
 In addition, the project team conducted a short course on Coreform software for analysis applications.
The short course was attended by eleven participants drawn from nuclear and non-nuclear industries. This
activity provided hands-on exposure to the workflows and capabilities relevant to the project’s objectives and
supported engagement with potential users of the technology.
 These dissemination activities complemented the project’s technical work by facilitating knowledge transfer
and by engaging members of the broader simulation community who may benefit from or contribute to future
development of the demonstrated workflows.
