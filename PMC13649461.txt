Genomic convergence of mobilome-associated multidrug resistance and host-adaptation determinants in a postoperative Raoultella ornithinolytica isolate carrying blaKPC-2, blaNDM-5, and a truncated mcr-10-like element

Ziheng Wang, Guangping Xu, Ruodan Li, Jiaxu Qin, Ruiming Bao, Zihao Chen, Jie Li, Peng Zhang

Abstract

AbstractObjectiveTo characterize the convergence of resistance determinants, plasmid-mediated dissemination, and host adaptation in the postoperative Raoultella ornithinolytica isolate RO0725 through integrated genomic analyses and phenotypic validation. We further sought to evaluate its clinical and One Health risks.MethodsLong-read sequencing was used to generate a complete genome. Subsequent analyses assessed phylogeny, average nucleotide identity, resistance genes, and plasmid comparisons. Phenotypic validation included conjugation assays, bile salt tolerance, biofilm formation, and a Galleria mellonella infection model.ResultsThe RO0725 genome comprised one chromosome and five circular plasmids. RO0725 carried 14 antimicrobial-resistance genes distributed across the chromosome and three resistance-associated plasmids. Chromosomal determinants included intrinsic resistance-associated genes such as blaORN-1, oqxA10, oqxB9, and fosA9. The plasmid pRO0725–1 carried a truncated mcr - 10-like element, whereas pRO0725–2 harbored blaKPC-2, blaCTX-M-3, blaTEM-1, qnrS1, dfrA14, arr-3, and aac(6′)-Ib-AKT. The IncX3-like plasmid pRO0725–3 carried blaNDM-5 and ble-MBL. Both pRO0725–2 and pRO0725–3 exhibited transferable potential in conjugation assays. RO0725 did not form a near-clonal cluster (defined as ≤10 core-genome SNPs) with any public isolates. RO0725 contained host-adaptation-associated determinants involved in adhesion (ecp, fim, and mrk), iron acquisition (ent, fep, ybt, irp, fyuA, and iutA), stress response (phoP, rcsB, rpoS, acrAB, and ompA), and cellulose biosynthesis (bcs). Phenotypically, RO0725 exhibited enhanced bile salt tolerance, weak biofilm formation, and moderate lethality in the G. mellonella model.ConclusionRO0725 represents a convergence of transferable multidrug-resistance plasmids and host-adaptation determinants. These findings indicate that R. ornithinolytica may act as both an opportunistic pathogen and a potential reservoir for the interspecies dissemination of resistance genes. They support its inclusion in hospital and One Health antimicrobial-resistance surveillance.Keywords:

Introduction

Carbapenemases and plasmid-mediated resistance to polymyxins and tigecycline are progressively limiting treatment options for infections caused by Enterobacterales (). Resistance determinants such as blaKPC, blaNDM, mcr, and tmexCD-toprJ often occur on plasmids, integrons, and transposon-associated regions. These mobile locations facilitate their horizontal dissemination across bacterial species and ecological niches (; ; ). In recent years, blaKPC, blaNDM, mcr-10, tmexCD-toprJ, and tet(X) have been identified in Raoultella ornithinolytica. These findings suggest that this species may serve as an important reservoir and transmission vehicle for multidrug-resistance determinants (; ; ; ; ; ).

The genus Raoultella, formerly classified within the genus Klebsiella, currently includes several recognized species, such as R. ornithinolytica, R. planticola, and R. terrigena (). Among these species, R. ornithinolytica has received particular clinical attention because it is the species most frequently reported in human infections and has demonstrated a greater association with multidrug-resistant clinical isolates (). R. ornithinolytica is an encapsulated, facultatively anaerobic, Gram-negative bacillus widely distributed in aquatic environments, soil, food, animals, and healthcare-associated settings. Because of its phenotypic similarity to Klebsiella oxytoca, conventional methods may misidentify it. Matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) and whole-genome sequencing have improved its clinical recognition (). This organism has been associated with bloodstream, urinary tract, pulmonary, biliary tract, soft-tissue, and postoperative infections. Such infections occur particularly in immunocompromised patients and those undergoing invasive procedures (; ; ; ; ; ; ). However, rapidly progressive invasive infections have also occurred in otherwise healthy individuals, indicating that its pathogenic potential is not restricted to traditionally susceptible populations ().

From a One Health perspective, isolates from clinical cases, companion animals, river sediments, and aquaculture wastewater carry antimicrobial-resistance genes and mobile plasmids. These findings suggest that R. ornithinolytica may contribute to the circulation of resistance determinants among humans, animals, and the environment (; ; ). Here, we performed complete genomic and phenotypic characterization of RO0725, an isolate recovered from postoperative drainage fluid. The strain co-harbored blaKPC-2, blaNDM-5, and a truncated mcr-10-like element. We conducted phylogenetic and plasmid structural analyses, conjugation assays, and host-adaptation analyses to evaluate its dissemination potential and clinical significance.

Materials and methods

Clinical information, bacterial identification, and antimicrobial susceptibility testing

A 59-year-old man with Stanford type A aortic dissection and coronary artery disease underwent ascending aortic resection, prosthetic graft replacement, and coronary artery bypass grafting on January 6, 2026. An R. ornithinolytica isolate was recovered from postoperative drainage fluid. It was identified using MALDI-TOF MS (VITEK MS system, bioMérieux), confirmed by whole-genome sequencing, and designated RO0725. MICs were determined using the VITEK 2 Compact system (bioMérieux). Ceftazidime–avibactam and aztreonam–avibactam were tested by disk diffusion. Results were interpreted according to CLSI M100, 36th edition. Aztreonam–avibactam results were interpreted according to EUCAST v16.1.

Whole-genome sequencing, assembly, and genome annotation

Genomic DNA from RO0725 was extracted using the NucleoBond® HMW DNA Kit (Macherey-Nagel, Germany). DNA quality was assessed using NanoDrop, Qubit 4.0, and agarose gel electrophoresis. Libraries were sequenced on the Oxford Nanopore PromethION platform using R10.4.1 flow cells. After contamination assessment with Kraken2 v2.1.3, Oxford Nanopore reads were assembled de novo using Canu v2.2 with the following parameters: genomeSize=5.5m, correctedErrorRate=0.105, and minReadLength=1000. The resulting assembly was polished using Medaka v2.0.1 with the r1041_e82_400bps_sup_g610 model. Genome circularization was confirmed by terminal-overlap analysis (; ). Sequencing depth was calculated using minimap2 v2.24-r1122 and SAMtools v1.18. The final assembly comprised one complete chromosome and five circular plasmids (; ).

Genome annotation was performed using Bakta v1.10.1 and included CDSs, tRNAs, rRNAs, and ncRNAs. Functional annotation was then performed against the COG and KEGG databases () (; ; ). CRISPR-Cas systems and prophages were predicted using CRISPRCasFinder v4.2.20 and PhiSpy v4.2.30, respectively (; ). Antimicrobial-resistance genes, virulence factors, and plasmid replicons were screened using ABRicate v1.4.0. The CARD v4.0.1, ResFinder v4.7.2, VFDB, and PlasmidFinder v3.0.3 databases were used (; ; ; ). Identity and coverage thresholds were set at ≥80%. Plasmid mobility was assessed using MOB-suite v3.1.9, oriTfinder, and MacSyFinder v2.1.6 with MacSyLib v1.0.4, CONJScan v2.0.2, and HMMER v3.2.1 (; ; ). Sequence comparisons were performed using NCBI BLAST+ v2.17.0+ ().

Global genomic dataset construction and phylogenomic analysis

Public R. ornithinolytica genomes were retrieved using NCBI Datasets CLI v18.33.1. After deduplication and quality control, we selected the 100 genomes most closely related to RO0725 in the preliminary global core-genome SNP analysis. Together with RO0725, these genomes yielded a dataset of 101 genomes. Coding sequences were uniformly predicted using Pyrodigal v3.7.1 (). Core genes were identified using NCBI BLAST+ v2.17.0+, with RO0725 as the reference. Thresholds were ≥95% nucleotide identity, ≥90% alignment coverage, and presence in ≥99% of genomes. Maximum-likelihood phylogenetic analysis was performed using IQ-TREE v3.1.3. ModelFinder was used to evaluate eight candidate nucleotide substitution models, including combinations of GTR/HKY with Gamma and FreeRate heterogeneity models. The best-fit model was selected according to the Bayesian information criterion (BIC), resulting in GTR+F+R4 as the optimal model. Because the phylogenetic reconstruction was based on core-genome SNP alignment, ascertainment bias correction was applied using the ASC option. The final tree was inferred with 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates and 1,000 SH-aLRT replicates (). Whole-genome ANI was calculated using pyfastani v0.6.1, and all results were visualized using ChiPlot (; ).

Comparative analysis of antimicrobial resistance and mobilome features

Presence–absence matrices were generated from the annotation results for antimicrobial-resistance genes, virulence genes, and plasmid replicons. The matrices were visualized using ChiPlot (). Complete circular reference plasmids homologous to pRO0725-1, pRO0725-2, and pRO0725–3 were screened and aligned using NCBI BLAST+ v2.17.0+ (). After deduplication and sequence-orientation normalization, circular plasmid comparisons were generated using gbdraw (). The truncated mcr-10-like element, dfrA14 integron, multidrug-resistance region, and blaNDM-5 region were compared using blastn. The results were visualized as linear synteny maps.

Characterization of host-adaptation determinants and phenotypic validation

Biofilm formation by RO0725 and the reference strains was assessed using a crystal-violet assay in 96-well plates. Six replicate wells were used per strain, and results were classified according to . Bile salt tolerance was evaluated by comparing viable counts before and after exposure to 50 mg/mL bile salts for 4 h. Virulence was assessed using a Galleria mellonella infection model. Larvae were injected with different bacterial inocula through the last left proleg. Each group contained 10 larvae. Hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae NTUH-K2044 and PBS served as positive and negative controls, respectively. Survival was recorded at regular intervals and analyzed using Kaplan–Meier curves.

Because sodium azide is subject to regulatory restrictions in China and was unavailable, plasmid conjugation assays used RO0725 as the donor. A laboratory-preserved K. pneumoniae strain, KP033, served as the recipient. KP033 was resistant to doxycycline but susceptible to polymyxin, aztreonam, and carbapenems. Log-phase donor and recipient cultures were mixed at a 1:2 volume ratio and statically co-incubated overnight at 37 °C. Putative pRO0725–1 transconjugants were selected using 16 μg/mL doxycycline and 16 μg/mL polymyxin. Putative pRO0725–2 and pRO0725–3 transconjugants were selected using doxycycline plus aztreonam or ceftazidime–avibactam, respectively. Aztreonam was used at 64 μg/mL, and ceftazidime–avibactam was used at 16/4 μg/mL. Candidate colonies were purified and identified using the VITEK MS system (bioMérieux, France). Transfer of the corresponding plasmids was confirmed by PCR targeting the truncated mcr-10-like element, blaKPC-2, and blaNDM-5, respectively (). The conjugative transfer frequency was calculated as the number of confirmed transconjugant colony-forming units (CFUs) divided by the number of donor cells (CFUs) used in the mating assay and expressed as transconjugants per donor cell.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation. Differences among groups were evaluated using one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparison test. Survival curves were compared using the log-rank (Mantel–Cox) test.

Results

Clinical course and antimicrobial susceptibility

A 59-year-old man with Stanford type A aortic dissection and coronary artery disease underwent ascending aortic resection, prosthetic graft replacement, and coronary artery bypass grafting on January 6, 2026. He received multiple antimicrobial agents during hospitalization, and R. ornithinolytica RO0725 was isolated from postoperative drainage fluid on January 21 ().

RO0725 was resistant to most β-lactams, carbapenems, fluoroquinolones, tobramycin, and colistin. It was intermediate to amikacin and norfloxacin and susceptible to tetracyclines, tigecycline, and trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole. The resistance phenotype was largely consistent with the presence of blaKPC-2, blaNDM-5, qnrS1, and aac(6′)-Ib. However, detection of dfrA14 was discordant with susceptibility to trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole ().

Genomic features and functional annotation

Nanopore sequencing generated 63,256 high-quality reads totaling 999.42 Mb, with a read N50 of 25,523 bp and a mean quality score of 22.7. De novo assembly yielded six complete circular replicons totaling 5,878,252 bp, with an overall GC content of 55.54%. These replicons comprised one chromosome and five plasmids (). The chromosome was 5,526,141 bp long and had a GC content of 55.77%. pRO0725–1 to pRO0725–5 were 165,530, 85,621, 50,555, 46,434, and 3,971 bp, respectively (). We annotated 5,480 CDSs, 89 tRNAs, 25 rRNAs, 81 ncRNAs, and one tmRNA and predicted five prophage regions. Genome annotation was initially performed using Bakta v1.10.1, covering CDS, tRNA, rRNA, and ncRNA. As the final genome record was processed via the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP), the resulting annotation statistics may differ slightly from those reported in the manuscript. Five candidate CRISPR arrays were detected, but no associated cas genes were identified.

Phylogenomic position and genomic relatedness

A phylogenetic tree of 101 isolates was constructed from 79,563 SNPs across 4,346 core genes and resolved six major clades. RO0725 clustered within Clade IV. Its closest relative was the environmental isolate NUITM-VR1 (GCF_019973635.1), recovered in Vietnam in 2021. The next closest relatives were the human clinical isolate 0 (GCF_905219255.2), recovered in Spain in 2016, and two leafy-vegetable isolates from the United Kingdom. The latter were KB18LG-0270–2 and KB18LG-0270-1, recovered in 2019 ().

Pairwise ANI values among the 101 isolates ranged from 98.79% to 99.99%. Values between RO0725 and the other 100 isolates ranged from 99.15% to 99.88%. RO0725 had the highest ANI with GCF_905219255.2 (99.88%), followed by KB18LG-0270-2 (99.80%), KB18LG-0270-1 (99.77%), and NUITM-VR1 (99.77%) (). Minimum-spanning-tree analysis showed that RO0725 differed from NUITM-VR1 and GCF_905219255.2 by 124 and 139 core-genome SNPs, respectively. RO0725 did not form a near-clonal cluster, defined by ≤10 SNPs, with any other isolate ().

Distribution of resistance genes, virulence genes, and plasmid replicons

The clinical isolate RO0725 carried 14 antimicrobial-resistance genes, including blaCTX-M-3, blaKPC-2, blaNDM-5, blaORN-1, blaTEM-1, aac(6′)-Ib-AKT, dfrA14, oqxA10, oqxB9, qnrS1, fosA9, arr-3, ble-MBL, and a truncated mcr - 10-like element. These acquired ARGs were consistently identified by both CARD and ResFinder (). The detected mcr-10-like sequence had 100% nucleotide identity across the aligned region but covered only 68.52% of the full-length reference sequence. It was therefore classified as truncated. In contrast, NUITM-VR1 (GCF_019973635.1) carried a truncated mcr-10-like element (). The corresponding isolate was collected in Vietnam in 2021. These resistance determinants were distributed across the chromosome and three resistance-associated plasmids. Chromosome-encoded determinants mainly included intrinsic resistance-associated genes such as blaORN-1, oqxA10, oqxB9, and fosA9. The plasmid pRO0725–1 carried the truncated mcr - 10-like element, whereas pRO0725–2 harbored blaKPC-2 together with additional acquired resistance genes, including blaCTX-M-3, blaTEM-1, aac(6′)-Ib-AKT, qnrS1, dfrA14, and arr-3. The plasmid pRO0725–3 carried blaNDM-5 and ble-MBL.

In the comparative genomic analysis of 101 isolates, we identified 85 antimicrobial-resistance genes. oqxA10 and oqxB9 were present in all isolates, whereas fosA- and blaORN-family genes were also widely distributed (). In contrast, acquired β-lactamases and other resistance determinants were detected in a subset of isolates with variable distribution patterns. blaKPC-2 and blaNDM-5 were detected in nine and four isolates, respectively. RO0725 was the only isolate co-harboring blaKPC-2, blaNDM-5, and an mcr - 10-like element.

Using the VFDB-based systematic screening approach, we identified 56 virulence-associated genes among the 101 R. ornithinolytica isolates. Of these, 35 were conserved across all isolates, whereas the remaining genes showed variable distributions among strains. These genes were mainly associated with fimbrial adhesion, iron acquisition, stress regulation, and outer-membrane functions. They indicated a relatively conserved virulence-associated genetic backbone. RO0725 carried 40 virulence-associated genes. These included the fimbrial genes ecpA, fim, and mrk and the iron-acquisition genes ent, fep, ybt, irp, fyuA, and iutA. Several variable type VI secretion system-associated genes and east1 were absent ().

We detected 41 plasmid replicon types. IncFIB(K), IncFII(pKP91), and Col(pHAD28) were the most prevalent and occurred in 31, 24, and 22 isolates, respectively (). RO0725 carried five replicon types: Col(pHAD28), IncFIB(K), IncFII(pKP91), IncN, and IncU. This combination was not observed in any other isolate and further highlighted the complexity of its plasmid repertoire ().

Comparative analysis of resistance plasmids

pRO0725–1 was a 165,530-bp IncFIB(K)-positive F-type large plasmid. Its overall backbone was most similar to CP193782.1, with 93.29% query coverage and 98.78% nucleotide identity. AP025011.1, PQ136552.1, and MN175502.1 retained only partially homologous backbones. Replication, maintenance, and conjugative-transfer regions were relatively conserved. In contrast, regions associated with cellulose biosynthesis, arsenic and tellurite tolerance, and the genomic context of the truncated mcr-10-like element showed marked structural variation (). In addition to resistance-associated elements, pRO0725–1 carried a 13.4-kb cellulose biosynthesis-associated locus containing bcsO, bcsQ, bcsA, bcsB, bcsG, bcsF, and BcsC/BcsD/BcsZ-like genes. The bcsG and bcsF genes showed opposite orientation relative to the bcsOQABCDZ-related region. An IS1N-associated element and an IS6-family transposase were identified upstream and downstream of this locus, respectively, suggesting possible involvement of mobile elements in its acquisition. oriTfinder, MOB-suite, and CONJScan consistently predicted an oriT, a relaxase, a type IV coupling protein, and a complete F-type type IV secretion system. Nevertheless, no pRO0725-1-positive transconjugants were recovered under the experimental conditions used ().

pRO0725–2 was an 85,621-bp IncN/IncU multidrug-resistance plasmid (). It shared approximately 72.5%–81.8% of its backbone with reference plasmids from diverse Enterobacterales, with nucleotide identities exceeding 95%. The hosts included Enterobacter hormaechei, K. pneumoniae, Citrobacter koseri, E. coli, and Morganella morganii. Replication, stable-maintenance, and conjugative-transfer modules were relatively conserved. In contrast, the resistance region contained integrons, insertion sequences, and transposases and showed extensive insertions, deletions, and rearrangements. The plasmid carried blaKPC-2, blaCTX-M-3, blaTEM-1, qnrS1, dfrA14, arr-3, and aac(6′)-Ib-AKT (). In conjugation assays, pRO0725–2 transferred to KP033 at a mean frequency of (6.3 ± 0.43) × 10-3. This result was consistent with its complete oriT–relaxase–T4CP–T4SS architecture.

pRO0725–3 was a 50,555-bp IncX3-like resistance plasmid carrying the blaNDM-5–ble-MBL resistance module (). It showed 100% query coverage and 99.97% nucleotide identity to CP094514.1. It also shared 86.36% of a highly conserved backbone with KX023261.1 and LC521848.1. These findings place it within the widely disseminated IncX3-like blaNDM-5–ble-MBL plasmid family. Structural differences were mainly confined to local insertion sequences and expanded accessory regions. Replication, partitioning, and conjugative-transfer modules remained highly conserved (). pRO0725–3 transferred to KP033 at a mean frequency of (3.5 ± 2.1) × 10-4. This frequency was approximately 18-fold lower than that of pRO0725-2. Although its predicted T4SS completeness was lower than that of the other two plasmids, the conjugation assay confirmed its capacity for horizontal transfer ().

Genetic contexts and transferability of key resistance determinants

The truncated mcr-10-like element on pRO0725–1 was embedded in a mobile genetic region containing integrase, recombinase, tnpA, and ISEc27. A repA–tap–copB replication-control module was located downstream. The surrounding backbone was highly homologous to several reference plasmids carrying full-length mcr-10.1, although the gene was truncated in RO0725 ().

On pRO0725-2, dfrA14 occurred in a class 1 integron-associated structure arranged as intI1–dfrA14–IS6100–IS1. The structure was adjacent to recombinase, mobC, and virB4. This region was highly conserved among Enterobacterales plasmids (). The approximately 42-kb multidrug-resistance region comprised ISEc9–blaCTX-M-3, blaTEM-1, ISKpn27–blaKPC-2, arr-3–aac(6′)-Ib–intI1, and qnrS1 modules. It was enriched in IS15DIV, ISPa38, and other mobile elements. Reference plasmids showed extensive rearrangements and modular recombination ().

On pRO0725-3, blaNDM-5 was adjacent to ble-MBL in a region containing IS3000, ISAba125, IS5, and transposase-associated elements. The core structure was highly conserved among IncX3-like reference plasmids. Variation was mainly confined to downstream regions associated with IS26, ISKox3, and recombinases ().

Host-adaptation and virulence-associated phenotypes

After 4 h of exposure to 50 mg/mL bile salts, RO0725 had a survival rate of 18.80% ± 0.56%. This rate exceeded that of the negative control W3110 (4.10% ± 1.40%, P < 0.0001). It also exceeded that of R. ornithinolytica ATCC 31898 (14.47% ± 0.84%, P = 0.0043). However, it was lower than that of the positive control PBIO1289 (37.67% ± 1.19%, P < 0.0001) (). In the crystal-violet assay, RO0725 had an OD570 of 0.355 ± 0.035. This value was significantly higher than those of ATCC 31898 and the blank control but markedly lower than that of hypervirulent K. pneumoniae NTUH-K2044. According to the Stepanović criteria, RO0725 was classified as a weak biofilm producer ().

In the G. mellonella model, the 72-h survival rates after infection with 1 × 108 and 1 × 107 CFU of RO0725 were 50% and 80%, respectively. At the corresponding inocula, NTUH-K2044 caused 100% mortality within 24 h. No deaths occurred in the PBS group during the 72-h observation period. Survival curves differed significantly among groups at both inocula (log-rank, P < 0.0001) ().

The RO0725 genome contained several adhesion-associated genes, including ecpA, fimA/C/D/E/H, and mrkA/B/C. It also contained the iron-acquisition systems ent, fep, ybt, irp, fyuA, and iutA. Stress-response and outer-membrane genes included phoP, rcsB, rpoS, acrAB, and ompA. In addition, pRO0725–1 carried a bcs gene cluster associated with cellulose biosynthesis. These genetic features were consistent with the observed bile salt tolerance, biofilm formation, and moderate in vivo virulence. However, they do not independently establish functional causality.

Discussion

This study integrated complete genome sequencing, phylogenetic analysis, plasmid structural comparisons, conjugation assays, and host-adaptation phenotyping to characterize R. ornithinolytica RO0725 from postoperative drainage fluid. Its defining feature was not the acquisition of a single resistance determinant but the convergence of multiple resistance plasmids and adaptation-associated factors. Distinct plasmids carried blaKPC-2, blaNDM-5, and a truncated mcr-10-like element. These plasmids coexisted with determinants associated with adhesion, iron acquisition, stress tolerance, and cellulose biosynthesis. Together, these features endowed RO0725 with an extensively drug-resistant phenotype, transferable resistance plasmids, and a measurable capacity for host adaptation. They highlight the dual role of R. ornithinolytica as an opportunistic pathogen and a reservoir of antimicrobial-resistance determinants (; ). Previous reports of blaKPC, blaNDM, mcr-10, tmexCD-toprJ, and tet(X) in this species further illustrate its capacity to acquire mobile resistance elements (; ; ; ). RO0725 was resistant to carbapenems, ceftazidime–avibactam, and aztreonam–avibactam. This finding emphasizes that treatment of isolates co-producing different carbapenemases should follow standardized antimicrobial susceptibility testing rather than rely solely on genotype.

RO0725 was closely related to environmental, food-associated, and clinical isolates from Vietnam, the United Kingdom, and Spain, respectively. However, it did not form a near-clonal cluster defined by ≤10 SNPs, providing no evidence of recent direct transmission. Its closest relatives occupied diverse ecological niches. Previous reports have also identified resistance plasmids in R. ornithinolytica from companion animals, river sediments, and aquaculture wastewater (; ; ). Together, these findings support a possible role for this species in the circulation of resistance determinants among humans, animals, food systems, and the environment. RO0725 carried a unique combination of five plasmids. Acquired resistance determinants were distributed among multiple phylogenetic lineages rather than being restricted to a single clonal background. These patterns suggest that plasmid acquisition and recombination, rather than expansion of a single resistant clone, primarily drove its multidrug-resistance profile. However, given the single-isolate design and lack of regional surveillance data, we cannot infer the prevalence or geographic expansion of R. ornithinolytica in eastern China. Our findings instead suggest that multidrug resistance in this species is primarily shaped by acquisition and recombination of mobile resistance elements, with transferable IncN/IncU and IncX3-like plasmids potentially facilitating horizontal dissemination. This interpretation is consistent with long-term hospital surveillance showing repeated blaKPC spread among species and clonal backgrounds through stable plasmid vehicles ().

Among the plasmids identified, pRO0725–2 showed the clearest dissemination potential. This IncN/IncU plasmid integrated blaKPC-2, blaCTX-M-3, blaTEM-1, qnrS1, and a class 1 integron-associated resistance module. It transferred to K. pneumoniae KP033 at a mean frequency of 6.3 × 10-3. Previous studies demonstrated that blaKPC-carrying IncN plasmids can disseminate across bacterial species and persist in healthcare settings (). This evidence is consistent with the conserved conjugative backbone and structurally variable resistance island observed here. The discordance between dfrA14 and trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole susceptibility further indicates that gene detection alone does not necessarily predict phenotypic resistance. Phenotypic resistance may also depend on gene integrity, expression level, regulatory context, and the recipient background.

Although pRO0725–3 transferred at a lower frequency, it was highly homologous to IncX3-like blaNDM-5 plasmids from diverse sources. It also retained the conserved IS3000–ISAba125–IS5–blaNDM-5–ble-MBL core structure. Recent clinical studies similarly documented interspecies transfer, stable maintenance, and broad dissemination of blaNDM-5 mediated by conserved IncX3 plasmids (; ). Therefore, a relatively low transfer frequency under a single in vitro condition should not be interpreted as evidence of low epidemiological risk.

The mcr-10-like fragment carried by pRO0725–1 covered only 68.52% of the full-length reference gene. It occurred in a region enriched in integrases, recombinases, tnpA, and ISEc27. The plasmid backbone was closely related to those carrying intact mcr-10.1. This context suggests that the fragment may have arisen through mobilization followed by partial deletion or structural disruption. Full-length mcr-10 has previously been reported with blaKPC-2 on IncF-type plasmids in environmentally derived Enterobacterales. Studies of wastewater and clinical isolates in Japan also showed that mcr-10 may disseminate with tellurite-resistance-associated ter loci (; ). Nevertheless, colistin resistance in RO0725 cannot be attributed solely to the truncated element. Alterations in phoPQ, pmrAB, mgrB, or other lipid A modification pathways may also contribute. Although pRO0725–1 contained a complete predicted conjugative apparatus, no corresponding transconjugant was recovered. This result may reflect recipient-range restrictions, insufficient expression of the conjugation system, plasmid-associated fitness costs, or the selection conditions. It does not establish that the plasmid is intrinsically non-transferable. Beyond antimicrobial-resistance determinants, pRO0725–1 also carried a plasmid-associated cellulose biosynthesis locus. Plasmid-associated bcs loci have been reported in several Enterobacterales species, although their distribution and biological significance remain incompletely characterized. Previous examples include bcs-bearing plasmids from Klebsiella variicola, Citrobacter portucalensis, and Escherichia coli (; ; ). In RO0725, the pRO0725-1-associated locus consisted of a bcsOQABCDZ-related region with oppositely oriented bcsFG, while bcsE and bcsR could not be confirmed (). The presence of an IS1N-associated element upstream and an IS6-family transposase downstream suggests a possible history of mobile-element-associated acquisition or rearrangement; however, the mechanism of acquisition remains unresolved. The coexistence of this locus with the truncated mcr-10-like element and other adaptation-associated genes highlights the potential accumulation of resistance-associated and persistence-related traits within a single plasmid background. Nevertheless, functional experiments are required to determine whether this bcs locus contributes to cellulose production, biofilm formation, or host adaptation.

Phenotypic analyses indicated that RO0725 was not a typical hypervirulent strain. It exhibited substantial bile salt tolerance, weak biofilm formation, and intermediate lethality in the Galleria mellonella model. Its acute virulence was markedly lower than that of hypervirulent K. pneumoniae NTUH-K2044. The fim, mrk, and ecp genes provide a potential genetic basis for adhesion. The ybt and iutA genes may support iron acquisition, whereas acrAB, ompA, and bcs may contribute to stress tolerance and environmental persistence. However, the functional contributions of these genes were not experimentally established. RO0725 may therefore possess sufficient host adaptation for persistence in postoperative drainage systems, implanted materials, or intensive-care environments without causing an acutely lethal phenotype. Previous reports have described postoperative, biliary, pulmonary, and other invasive infections caused by R. ornithinolytica (; ; ; ; ; ; ). Accordingly, RO0725 may represent an opportunistic pathotype characterized by moderate virulence, extensive antimicrobial resistance, and transferable plasmids. Its clinical risk may arise primarily from persistent colonization, treatment failure, and transfer of resistance determinants to other Enterobacterales rather than exceptionally high acute virulence.

This study has several limitations. Only one clinical isolate was analyzed, and no contemporaneous patient-screening or environmental isolates were available. Conjugation experiments used KP033 as the sole recipient. The resulting transconjugants were not subjected to whole-genome sequencing. In addition, neither the truncated mcr-10-like element nor the putative host-adaptation genes were validated through expression or genetic studies. Despite these limitations, our findings demonstrate that R. ornithinolytica can serve as a convergence platform for distinct resistance plasmids and host-adaptation determinants. These results support its inclusion in hospital and One Health antimicrobial-resistance surveillance, particularly for the interspecies dissemination of IncN/IncU and IncX3-like plasmids.

Conclusion

This study showed that the postoperative R. ornithinolytica isolate RO0725 harbored an IncN/IncU plasmid carrying blaKPC-2 and an IncX3-like plasmid carrying blaNDM-5. It also carried a truncated mcr-10-like element. RO0725 exhibited experimentally confirmed transferability of resistance plasmids and measurable host-adaptation phenotypes. Its major risks are extensive antimicrobial resistance, persistent colonization, and potential dissemination of resistance genes to other Enterobacterales. These findings support enhanced clinical and One Health surveillance of this emerging opportunistic pathogen.
