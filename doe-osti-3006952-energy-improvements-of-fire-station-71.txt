3. Executive Summary:
Since 2018, the City of Shawnee, Kansas has completed two phases of the State of
Kansas Facility Conservation Improvement Program (FCIP), an initiative that
guarantees operational cost and energy savings through targeted construction
improvements on City facilities and infrastructure. The City is currently in the third phase
of this FCIP, where one of the projects included an investment in energy improvements
for Fire Station 71 (FS 71). The City partnered with Navitas, an Energy Service
Company (ESCO), to implement a Photovoltaic Solar Array on FS71. The purpose of
this project was to invest in sustainable building improvements with Energy
Conservation Measures (ECM) to bring cost savings to the City and to provide
sustainable benefits to the residents of Shawnee.
In the first task of the project, Navitas collaborated with the City of Shawnee and the
Community Development Department to determine the optimal layout and schedule for
the installation of the solar array on FS 71. In the second task of the project, Navitas
installed the 99.8 kW DC Photovoltaic solar array system. This system installation
comprised of racking, inverters, optimizers, load center, and disconnect, which were all
installed at a total ECM price of $247,948. The third task focused on start-up and
commissioning of the array. Navitas installed a real-time data analytics information
management system integrated with utility meters, which evaluates the operations of
the utility system and verifies operation of equipment and ensures optimum operation
for energy efficiency. In the final task of this project, this analytics system was used for
monitoring and verification, which will continue to be used to evaluate the success of the
project for the coming years.
The primary goal of the project was to install the 99.8 kW DC PV solar array at FS 71 to
demonstrate the viability of solar energy systems in essential municipal facilities. Fire
stations are energy demanding structures, as they require a constant intake of power
and have a high baseline energy usage. The success of solar arrays on a fire station

exemplifies their energy efficiency and effectiveness and displays their potential for
application on other city facilities. By installing a solar array at such a facility, the City
sought not only to offset electricity usage but also to serve as a model for ECMs in other
municipal facilities and infrastructure projects.
From an economic standpoint, this project demonstrates the feasibility of renewable
energy at the municipal level. The total project cost of $247,948 was split evenly
between city funds and award funding, minimizing financial risk while ensuring
guaranteed long-term savings. Any excess savings that are beyond the guaranteed
minimums remain with the city, which enables future investment in sustainable energy
initiatives.
This project provides many benefits to the public. In addition to reducing the
environmental footprint of city operations, it lowers taxpayer-funded utility spending and
improves the energy security of a critical facility. The knowledge gained from this
implementation motivates the City to focus on similar efforts across other public facilities
in future FCIP phases and other City projects.

5. Background:
City governments across the United Sates are investing in renewable energy options as
a means to reduce utility costs, enhance energy and community resilience, and meet
climate goals. Among various renewable energy technologies, photovoltaic solar energy
systems have emerged as a cost effective and environmentally sustainable option for
public facilities. As part of phase 3 of the City of Shawnee’s Facility Conservation
Improvement Program project, a 99.8 kW Photovoltaic solar array was installed at FS
71, which represents the viability of renewable energy at an energy intensive facility and
the City’s commitment toward energy efficient solutions.
Current literature supports the addition of photovoltaic solar arrays on municipal
facilities. A recent peer-reviewed study by Farthing et al. (2021) presents their findings

 Page 3 of 9

on the optimal way to design and deploy solar-plus-storage systems in public facilities
by accounting for climate, health, resilience, and energy bill benefits simultaneously. In
their analysis, they examined hospitals, schools, and warehouses in 14 U.S. cities. They
used an optimization model called REopt Lite to determine the complex tradeoffs when
accounting for climate, health, resilience, and cost savings. Using this model, they
quantified the impact of these factors on the three building types, and found that
“monetizing and co-optimizing for climate and health benefits, as compared to only
energy bill savings and resilience, increases the net present value of the solar-plus-
storage systems by $200k to $5.2M and makes larger systems financially attractive”
(Farthing et al., 1). This highlights the importance of accounting for health and climate
benefits when installing solar panels on public facilities instead of committing to the
project for future cost savings alone and underscores the value that solar panels can
bring to public facilities.
This published work is relevant to the solar installation project at FS 71 because it
shows the true value that solar arrays can bring to the fire station if they are optimized
correctly. Within the Statement of Project Objectives (SOPO), it was clarified that the
overall purpose of this project was to invest in sustainable building improvements with
energy conservation measures. SOPO also listed the total emission reductions this
project can expect, which includes 678,560 total pounds of carbon dioxide, 14 pounds
of methane, 9 pounds of nitrous oxide, 1,826 pounds of sulfur dioxide, and 628 pounds
of nitrogen oxide. These greenhouse gases pose a threat to the climate and public
health. The clarification of these expected savings, as well as the commitment to
sustainability that this project addresses in its goals, indicate that climate and health
have been accounted for and are being optimized in the project design. They were
optimized along with resilience cost savings, as energy efficiency and financial benefits
from this project were also addressed in the SOPO.
The primary difference between this project and what was discussed in the peer-
reviewed study was that the FS 71 project did not include plans to install energy storage
systems. Since the station operates on a 24/7 schedule and has such high baseline
energy usage, it is likely to not have any excess energy from the solar array due to the
fact that the solar array only produces energy during the day, so any excess energy
would be used immediately or sent back to the grid. The peer-reviewed study only
analyzed solar-plus-storage systems in hospitals, schools, and warehouses, so fire
stations and their high baseline energy demands were not considered for this study and
optimization patterns for climate, health, resilience, and cost savings may differ.

The insights from current literature directly inform the work being done in this project. It
highlights the true value and opportunities that are presented with the use of solar
energy at public facilities, which Shawnee is taking full advantage of with this project.
This project situated the City to be among national forerunners in renewable energy
deployment for public facilities. This allows the City to be seen as an example for other
municipalities aiming to adopt similar projects and shows the residents of Shawnee that
the City is committed to innovative sustainability efforts.

6. Project Objectives:
Implementing photovoltaic solar technology into city facility energy systems is an
important step for demonstrating effective uses of solar and renewable energy. By
integrating a Photovoltaic solar array into FS 71, the City of Shawnee is contributing to
broader U.S. goals to reduce carbon emissions and create a more resilient and cost-
effective energy grid. Overall, this project demonstrates how local governments can
make meaningful progress through renewable energy investments that provide both
environmental and economic benefits. The installation of photovoltaic solar arrays on
FS 71 further exemplifies this by displaying the effectiveness of solar energy on city
facilities that require constant energy and high baseline consumption. Successful solar
projects enhance public awareness of renewable energy and encourage a sustainable
future for all residents of Shawnee. Expected outcomes of this project included a
significant reduction in electric energy consumption, a reduction in gas consumption,
and a reduction in utility spending, which bring a variety of benefits to residents of
Shawnee and fall in line with broad energy goals.
There are various goals for this project. The primary goals are to significantly reduce
electric energy consumption at FS 71 through the generation of on-site renewable
energy and to reduce utility costs for the City and taxpayers while maintaining or
improving operational performance. The long-term goal for this project is to achieve
guaranteed minimum cost savings over the contract term. The combined annual
savings must be sufficient to cover all project costs, including debt service and
contractor fees, maintenance, monitoring, and other services, for the duration of the
contract term. Annual cost savings beyond the guaranteed minimum savings will be
held by the City and will not be allocated to shortfalls in other years.
The timeline of this project was divided into four tasks, each with their own deliverables
and milestones to measure progress. Task 1 for this project was for the City to work
with an ESCO to complete project design and develop a timeline to install photovoltaic
solar arrays on FS 71. Navitas was selected as the ESCO to complete the project.
Project design and planning were completed by Navitas, with finalized plans submitted
to Shawnee’s Community Development Department. Permitting was completed by the
Community Development Department and the approved permit was used as this task’s
milestone.
Task 2 was installation the photovoltaic solar array on Fire Station 71 by Navitas. The
milestones for this task were photographs of the installed array along with a completed
punch list.
Task 3 had Navitas ensure that the array is functioning correctly through startup testing
of the system. Commissioning and testing were performed by Navitas on each piece of
equipment. Navitas tested and verified the array was functioning properly by measuring
real-time readings against their data platform. Navitas also tested proper DC and AC
connections associated with the solar array, inverters to ensure proper operation, and
overall solar output to the building. Testing documentation was provided as milestone
verification.

 Page 5 of 9

Task 4 was for Navitas to maintain and monitor the measures to ensure optimal
performance. The operating parameter for the solar photovoltaic array is to allow for
generation of electricity during daylight hours to reduce purchased utility consumption.
The system was programmed to provide maximum energy output based upon the final
installation, considering tilt angles of the array. ECM savings were determined within a
measurement boundary that encompasses only the solar panels installed as a part of
this project. For the post-retrofit test, kW of the photovoltaic system was logged over a
minimum two-week period. The milestone for this task is for solar performance
documentation to be provided by Navitas detailing energy operation of the solar array.
The project’s go/no go decision point was approval by the Shawnee City Council,
through the partnership with Navitas, to design, install, and conduct final systems
testing on the 99.8 kW DC PV array, for FS 71. This specific solar project for FS 71 will
require total expenditure in the amount of $247,948. Internal cash funding from the City
in the amount of $123,947 has been secured to cover 50% of the project costs. This
award will cover the remaining amount of $123,974. Upon completion, demonstration of
the project will be a report of solar performance documentation provided by Navitas
detailing energy operation of the solar array sent to the DOE.

7. Project Results and Discussion:
At the time of this report, all tasks and associated milestones for the FS 71 solar
installation project (except for ongoing monitoring and performance documentation
under Task 4) have been successfully completed. The City’s Community Development
Department worked closely with Navitas to develop comprehensive designs and obtain
necessary approvals. A permit for the FS71 solar array was officially approved, marking
the successful conclusion of Task 1.
Following this, Task 2 involved the physical installation of the 99.8 kW DC photovoltaic
solar array at FS71. This installation included solar panels, racking system, inverters,
optimizers, load center, and disconnect. Navitas produced photographic documentation
and completed a punch list to verify the installation, satisfying the milestone for Task 2.
Some of the photographs taken for the milestone are pictured below:

In Task 3, Navitas conducted system startup and commissioning. Each piece of
equipment underwent testing, verifying that the array was functioning properly by
measuring real-time readings against their data platform. Navitas tested proper DC and
AC connections associated with the solar array, inverters to ensure proper operation,

 Page 6 of 9

and overall solar output to the building. The system was confirmed to be functioning
within expected parameters, and all testing documentation was sent to the City.
Currently, under Task 4, Navitas is maintaining and monitoring the solar array to ensure
optimal performance. Once Navitas is monitoring performance and generation data over
a year’s time, that data will be presented to the City in the form of detailed
documentation to verify that the project’s goals were met.
While final results from Navitas regarding the Photovoltaic solar array at Fire Station 71
have yet to be released, Navitas’s previous work and data from the first two phases of
Shawnee’s FCIP and the to-date generation data from FS71 since installation can be
used to forecast what energy and cost savings FS 71 can expect to have. Shawnee’s
Fire Station 72 was involved in an earlier phase of this FCIP project and, similarly, saw
the installation of solar panels. The installation of solar panels at Fire Station 72
dramatically reduced its electric energy consumption from the grid. Between April 1st of
2022 and March 31st of 2023, Fire Station 72 used 26,080 kWh of electricity. This
represents a signification reduction from its baseline consumption of 183,262 kWh,
which amounts to an 85.8% decrease in electric energy from the utility. The target
decrease for this time period was 86%, so the energy savings from the solar array met
expectations. This massive decrease displays how effective solar energy can be at
reducing electricity consumption, especially for city facilities that may require energy
constantly and have a high electricity baseline. The solar array on Fire Station 72 also
saved the City $18,388 annually. Given the similar energy demands between Fire
Station 71 and 72, it can be expected that Fire Station 71 will see similar electricity and
cost savings.

8. Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions:
The implementation of the photovoltaic solar array at Fire Station 71 represents a
significant milestone in the City of Shawnee’s ongoing commitment to energy efficiency
and sustainability through the Facility Conservation Improvement Program. Among the
most important accomplishments under this project was the successful completion of
project design, permitting, installation, and commissioning tasks. These tasks were
completed with collaboration between the City’s Community Development Department
and Navitas. The successful execution of these tasks displays the benefits of deploying
solar energy systems on municipal facilities such as fire stations, which are operational
24/7 and have high baseline energy requirements.
One significant achievement of this project was the integration of an analytics platform
that will allow for ongoing monitoring of the solar array’s performance over time. This
monitoring system ensures that the solar array is successful in operation and allows
Navitas and the City to measure the system’s energy production and its long-term
impact. It also allows Navitas and the City to find shortfalls and errors within the system,
which could save the City from future expenses. Although final data for the first year of
Fire Station 71’s solar array operation is not yet available, this monitoring system
supports the financial and environmental goals of the City.

 Page 7 of 9

While the outcomes so far have been largely positive, there are limitations and
challenges associated with solar energy that must be considered. Based on
performance data from Fire Station 72, the most significant challenge is reduced energy
production during periods of poor weather. Overcast and rainy conditions can
temporarily decrease the solar array’s output, transferring reliance to utility energy
sources. While this does not negate the overall benefit of the system, it must be
considered when weighing the overall benefit of solar energy.
No reduction in scope or pivots in the project were required. The project progressed
according to the original Statement of Project Objectives, and all tasks except for the
ongoing monitoring period were completed as planned. The project stayed within its
expected timeline and budget, totaling $247,948, with 50% of funding secured internally
by the City and the remaining 50% were covered by this award.

9. Path Forward:
The success of this project presents the opportunity to potentially implement renewable
solar energy at other municipal facilities. Although no immediate plans are underway at
this time, the City will continue looking for opportunities to utilize sustainable facility
conservation practices where possible.
