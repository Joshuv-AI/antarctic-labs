Multiparametric ultrasonography, elastography, and the Milan System for reporting salivary gland cytopathology in the preoperative evaluation of major salivary gland tumors
Authors: Ping-Chia Cheng, Yuh-Fan Hsu, Wei-Chen Hung, Wu-Chia Lo, Chih-Ming Chang, Ming-Hsun Wen, Andy Wei-Ge Chen, Li-Jen Liao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionHigh-resolution ultrasonography (US) is widely used to evaluate diseases of the major salivary glands. Previous studies on salivary gland US have focused primarily on qualitative descriptions and have often been limited by small sample sizes. This study aims to identify the most relevant US features for differentiating major salivary gland tumors within a large surgical cohort.MethodsThis retrospective observational study included 271 patients who underwent surgical resection of salivary gland tumors between August 2015 and December 2025. All patients received preoperative US and fine-needle aspiration and had definitive postoperative histopathological diagnoses. Sonographic characteristics, Doppler vascular patterns, elastography findings, and Milan cytological categories were analyzed and compared with histopathological diagnoses using chi-square, Fisher’s exact, and Kruskal-Wallis tests, followed by multivariable logistic regression to identify independent predictors of malignancy.ResultsAmong the 271 tumors, 231 (85.24%) were benign (predominantly pleomorphic adenoma [38.01%] and Warthin tumor [33.21%]) and 40 (14.76%) were malignant. On univariable analysis, malignancy was significantly associated with irregular or speculated shape, ill-defined margins, heterogeneous echotexture, calcifications, solid architecture, spotted or mixed vascularity, and higher real-time elastography (RTE) scores (all p < 0.05). After multivariable adjustment, heterogeneous echotexture (OR 3.78, 95% CI 1.31–10.92) and calcification (OR 14.84, 95% CI 2.67–82.50) remained independent predictors of malignancy, while oval (OR 0.10) and lobulated (OR 0.13) shapes were independently associated with a lower likelihood of malignancy. Pleomorphic adenomas exhibited the highest mean shear wave elastography (SWE) values, whereas Warthin tumors demonstrated lower stiffness and more frequent cystic architecture. Cytologically, the Milan System demonstrated favorable diagnostic performance. Comparing category IVa with categories V and VI yielded a sensitivity of 86.67% (95% CI, 69.5–100.0%), specificity of 98.84% (95% CI, 97.3–100.0%), and accuracy of 97.87% (95% CI, 95.8–99.9%).ConclusionsConventional US features remain highly valuable for differentiating benign from malignant salivary gland tumors. Elastography provides complementary information regarding tissue stiffness and heterogeneity, while the Milan cytology system demonstrates excellent diagnostic reliability.

Background

Salivary gland tumors represent a highly heterogeneous group of diseases with diverse histopathological features and biological behavior (1). Accurate preoperative differentiation between benign and malignant tumors is essential for appropriate treatment planning and determination of surgical extent (2). High-resolution ultrasonography (US) is widely used as a first-line imaging modality for the evaluation of salivary gland tumors, providing real-time information on lesion morphology, internal architecture, and vascularity (3). In recent years, US elastography has been increasingly applied to assess tissue stiffness and offers additional diagnostic information (4, 5). Furthermore, US-guided fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) is commonly used as a minimally invasive diagnostic tool, and the Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology has been established as a standardized reporting system (6–8). Despite these advancements, early salivary gland sonography primarily focused on qualitative descriptions rather than quantitative metrics, and investigations were frequently constrained by small sample sizes (9, 10). While conventional grayscale descriptors have repeatedly demonstrated an ability to discriminate malignant from benign processes, the diagnostic weight of individual imaging and cytological features remains highly variable. Color Doppler vascular evaluation effectively complements grayscale morphology by characterizing tumor perfusion, yet its diagnostic contribution remains modest when interpreted in isolation. Furthermore, although elastographic techniques are increasingly incorporated into major salivary gland protocols, a recent meta-analysis of elastosonography demonstrated only moderate pooled sensitivity and specificity for malignancy when qualitative stiffness scores or raw threshold values were used independently (11). In parallel, the Milan System has achieved widespread global adoption, with pooled meta-analytic data confirming high diagnostic efficacy, particularly in prospective settings, while its second edition has further refined category-specific risk-of-malignancy estimates based on cumulative multi-institutional experience (12, 13). Despite this growing body of evidence, few studies have concurrently evaluated grayscale, Doppler, elastographic, and cytological parameters within a single, adequately powered surgical cohort, which limits direct comparison of their relative diagnostic contributions and motivated the present analysis.

The aim of this study is to systematically evaluate gray-scale sonographic features, Doppler vascular patterns, elastography findings and cytology results of salivary gland tumors, and to correlate these findings with surgical histopathology as the reference standard in order to identify the most clinically useful diagnostic features.

Methods

This retrospective study was performed in a tertiary medical center and approved by the institutional review board (No. 115087-E). We reviewed the medical records of patients who underwent surgical resection of salivary gland tumors between August 2015 and December 2025. We included adult patients aged ≥20 years who underwent preoperative ultrasonography (including gray-scale US, Doppler imaging, and elastography when available), FNAC, and surgical resection with available histopathological confirmation. Patients with non-neoplastic entities, such as sialolithiasis, or those with incomplete clinical data were excluded. All ultrasound examinations were performed utilizing a Toshiba Aplio 500 system (Canon Medical Systems, Tochigi-ken, Japan) equipped with a 5–14 MHz linear-array transducer. Shear wave elastography (SWE) metrics were generated following an acoustic radiation force impulse; propagation velocities (m/s) were recorded and automatically converted into Young’s modulus (kilopascals, kPa) (14, 15). Real-time elastography (RTE) was induced via light manual transducer compression and categorized according to a validated 4-point scoring system, as described originally by Bhatia et al. in 2010 (16), where scores 1 and 2 were classified as benign and scores 3 and 4 were classified as malignant, which is also validated in our previous study (15). To ensure diagnostic objectivity, all archived cine loops and static DICOM images were retrospectively re-evaluated by two board-certified otolaryngologists, each possessing over 10 years of dedicated experience in head and neck ultrasonography. Both examiners were completely blinded to the cytological and final histopathological outcomes, with any inter-observer disagreements resolved by consensus with a third senior examiner.

Out of 274 initially screened entries, 3 were excluded: 2 patients were under the age of 20 and 1 presented with isolated sialolithiasis. The final study sample comprised 271 unique patients. Baseline parameters collected included age, sex, smoking status, tumor multiplicity (single vs. multiple), anatomical subsite, and maximum dimensions. For cases presenting with synchronous or multifocal masses, only the specific lesion targeted for surgical resection or the largest resected mass was included, yielding a final analytical cohort of 271 discrete tumors from 271 unique patients. Sonographic features, Doppler vascular characteristics, elastography parameters, and cytological categories based on the Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology were analyzed and compared with histopathological diagnoses classified according to the World Health Organization (WHO) criteria. Statistical analyses were performed to compare benign and malignant tumors and to evaluate the diagnostic performance of each modality individually. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test, or Fisher’s exact test when the expected cell frequency in any cell was below 5; continuous variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test.

In addition, lesions were further classified into four groups: malignant tumors, pleomorphic adenomas (PA), Warthin tumors, and other benign salivary gland tumors. This other benign group comprised basal cell adenoma, cystadenoma, oncocytoma, myoepithelioma, lymphoepithelial neoplasms, cavernous hemangioma, fibroblastic proliferative lesions, and lipomatous lesions. Significant differences were observed among the four groups across most clinical and sonographic parameters. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test, or Fisher’s exact test when the expected cell frequency in any cell was below 5. Because the Shapiro-Wilk test demonstrated that the continuous variables were not normally distributed, data were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges and compared across tumor groups using the Kruskal–Wallis test. When a significant overall difference was identified, post hoc pairwise comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Bonferroni correction.

Results

This study enrolled 271 patients between August 2015 and December 2025. Table 1 summarizes the demographic, clinical, sonographic, and pathological characteristics of the cohort. Patients had a mean age of 53.19 ± 14.61 years with a slight male predominance, and the parotid gland was the predominant tumor site. Benign tumors accounted for the majority of lesions, led by pleomorphic adenoma and Warthin tumor, whereas mucoepidermoid carcinoma was the most common malignant subtype. On grayscale US, most lesions were hypoechoic with clear boundaries, and heterogeneous echotexture was slightly more prevalent than homogeneous echotexture. Elastographic data, detailed in Table 1, showed a fairly even distribution between RTE 1&2 and 3&4, and a wide range of SWE stiffness values, reflecting the heterogeneous composition of the cohort.

Table 2 compares demographic and US features between benign (n = 231) and malignant (n = 40) tumors. Malignant tumors occurred significantly more often in female patients (p = 0.010) but did not differ significantly by age, smoking status, gland location, or tumor size. Among grayscale features, irregular or speculated shape, unclear boundaries, heterogeneous echotexture, absence of posterior acoustic enhancement, presence of calcification, and solid architecture were all significantly associated with malignancy (all p ≤ 0.001; Figures 1–4). Doppler vascular pattern also differed significantly between groups (p = 0.011), with spotted or mixed vascularity more common in malignant lesions. Higher RTE elasticity scores (3–4) were significantly associated with malignancy (p < 0.001), whereas mean SWE values did not differ significantly between groups (p = 0.430); by contrast, SWE SD was significantly higher in malignant tumors (p = 0.020), suggesting that stiffness heterogeneity may be more discriminative than mean stiffness alone. Cytological classification by the Milan system was also strongly associated with final pathology (p < 0.001), with malignant tumors concentrated in categories III, V, and VI.

Mucoepidermoid carcinoma. A 61-year-old woman presented with a left parotid gland tumor. Gray-scale US demonstrated a superficial hypoechoic solid lesion within the parotid gland. The lesion exhibited an irregular shape, ill-defined margins, and heterogeneous internal echotexture. No obvious cystic component was identified. Posterior acoustic features were inconspicuous, without definite posterior acoustic enhancement. Histopathological examination following surgical resection confirmed the diagnosis of mucoepidermoid carcinoma.

Pleomorphic adenoma. A 46-year-old woman presented with a left parotid gland tumor. Gray-scale US demonstrated a well-defined, oval-to-lobulated hypoechoic solid lesion within the superficial lobe of the parotid gland. The lesion exhibited smooth margins, mild internal heterogeneity, and posterior acoustic enhancement. No calcification, cystic degeneration, or evidence of adjacent tissue invasion was identified. These sonographic features were suggestive of a benign salivary gland neoplasm. Histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of pleomorphic adenoma.

Warthin tumor. A 65-year-old man with a history of cigarette smoking presented with a progressively enlarging left parotid gland tumor. Gray-scale US demonstrated a well-circumscribed, oval hypoechoic lesion within the superficial lobe of the parotid gland. The tumor exhibited heterogeneous internal echotexture with multiple small cystic components and mild posterior acoustic enhancement. No calcification or evidence of adjacent tissue invasion was identified. These sonographic features were suggestive of Warthin tumor. Histopathological examination following surgical resection confirmed the diagnosis of Warthin tumor.

Other benign tumor (cavernous hemangioma). A 54-year-old man presented with left parotid swelling. Gray-scale US demonstrates an ill-defined, oval, predominantly hypoechoic solid mass with heterogeneous internal echotexture. No significant posterior acoustic enhancement is observed. A small internal hyperechoic calcified focus (arrow), consistent with a phlebolith, is identified within the lesion. Histopathological examination confirmed a cavernous hemangioma.

Table 3 presents the multivariable logistic regression analysis of independent predictors of malignancy after adjustment for age, sex, and grayscale features. Heterogeneous echotexture and the presence of calcification remained independent predictors of malignancy, whereas oval and lobulated shapes were independently associated with a lower likelihood of malignancy relative to irregular shape. Age, sex, posterior acoustic enhancement, boundary definition, cystic architecture, and RTE score did not reach independent significance after adjustment (all p > 0.05).

Table 5 compares malignant tumors, pleomorphic adenomas, Warthin tumors, and other benign tumors across demographic and sonographic parameters, with significant intergroup differences observed for nearly all variables examined (p < 0.001 unless otherwise noted). Warthin tumors were characterized by an older, strongly male-predominant, smoking-associated population with near-exclusive parotid involvement, frequent multifocality, and the largest tumor dimensions among the four groups. Pleomorphic adenomas were more common in younger female patients and typically presented with oval-to-round shape, homogeneous echotexture, and prominent posterior acoustic enhancement. Malignant tumors most frequently exhibited irregular shape, unclear boundaries, calcification, and solid architecture, together with a higher frequency of spotted or mixed vascularity (p = 0.007) and higher RTE elasticity scores. On SWE, pleomorphic adenomas showed the highest mean stiffness values while Warthin tumors showed the lowest (p = 0.001), and malignant tumors demonstrated the greatest SWE variability (p < 0.001), consistent with the heterogeneity pattern noted above.

Table 6 summarizes the diagnostic performance of the Milan System under two clinically relevant classification schemes. When category IVa (benign) was compared against categories V and VI (malignant or suspicious for malignancy), the system achieved high specificity and overall accuracy with modestly lower sensitivity, reflecting the small number of malignant cases classified as benign neoplasm. When categories III and IVb (indeterminate) were considered alongside category Iva (benign), sensitivity improved but specificity and overall accuracy were somewhat lower, reflecting a trade-off between capturing more potential malignancies and increasing false-positive calls. These findings support the clinical value of the Milan system for preoperative risk stratification while underscoring the need for careful interpretation of indeterminate cytological categories.

Discussion

Our findings demonstrated that several conventional grayscale US features remain highly valuable in differentiating benign from malignant salivary gland tumors. Malignant lesions were significantly associated with irregular or speculated shapes, unclear margins, heterogeneous internal echotexture, solid architecture, and the presence of calcifications. In contrast, benign tumors more commonly demonstrated oval or round morphology, well-defined boundaries, posterior acoustic enhancement, and cystic components (Figures 1–4). These results support previous studies suggesting that tumor morphology and margin characteristics remain among the most reliable sonographic indicators of malignancy (17, 18). Consistent with these observations, recent radiomics and deep learning models identified morphological irregularity, poorly defined boundaries, and internal heterogeneity as the discriminators of malignancy (19). Multivariable logistic regression revealed that heterogeneous echotexture (OR 3.78, 95% CI 1.31–10.92, p = 0.014) and calcification (OR 14.84, 95% CI 2.67–82.50, p = 0.002) were independent predictors of malignancy after adjusting for age, sex, and concurrent grayscale features. Oval (OR 0.10, 95% CI 0.02–0.46, p = 0.003) and lobulated (OR 0.13, 95% CI 0.03–0.59, p = 0.008) shapes were independently associated with a lower likelihood of malignancy. Conversely, boundary definition, posterior acoustic enhancement, cystic architecture, and RTE score lost independent significance after adjustment, likely due to collinearity within infiltrative lesions. Thus, echotexture and calcification should be given greater diagnostic weight during real-time risk stratification, while oval or lobulated contours serve as reassuring markers.

Anatomical location also modulated features. Compared with parotid tumors, submandibular tumors more frequently demonstrated irregular shapes, poorly defined margins, calcifications, solid architecture, higher RTE scores, and higher mean SWE values (all p < 0.05), despite a similar overall malignancy rate (p = 0.071). Submandibular tumors may demonstrate imaging features that overlap with those of malignant lesions due to site-specific variations in parenchymal composition, stromal fibrosis, or inflammation. These findings suggest that site-specific interpretation may improve diagnostic accuracy.

Distinct sonographic patterns were also observed among different benign tumor subtypes. Pleomorphic adenomas frequently exhibited lobulated contours, homogeneous echotexture, and posterior acoustic enhancement, while Warthin tumors more commonly demonstrated oval morphology, cystic architecture, heterogeneous echotexture, and multifocal presentation. The strong association between Warthin tumors, male sex, and smoking history observed in this study is also consistent with prior epidemiological observations. Recognition of these characteristic imaging patterns may improve diagnostic confidence during routine US examination. The mild male predominance (56.57%) in our overall cohort is driven by Warthin tumors, which comprise 32.85% of all cases and exhibit a pronounced male bias (92.22%) likely linked to local smoking demographics. Excluding Warthin tumors reveals the classic female predominance in our pleomorphic adenoma (64.42%) and malignant tumor (62.5%) groups, aligning with international literature. While pleomorphic adenomas typically constitute 60–70% of benign salivary masses in general epidemiologic series, our lower rate (37.96%) reflects the referral and surgical selection biases inherent to a tertiary medical center. Additionally, an elevated smoking rate likely expanded the absolute volume of Warthin tumors, thereby artificially attenuating the relative percentage of pleomorphic adenomas.

Elastography provided additional information regarding tissue stiffness and tumor heterogeneity. Although the mean SWE values did not significantly differ between benign and malignant tumors overall, malignant tumors demonstrated significantly higher SWE standard deviation values, suggesting increased internal heterogeneity, including variable cellularity, necrosis, and cystic degeneration, which produce uneven stiffness distribution. Therefore, SWE SD may provide additional discriminatory information beyond mean stiffness when differentiating malignant from benign salivary gland tumors. Interestingly, pleomorphic adenomas exhibited the highest mean SWE values among all tumor subtypes, likely reflecting their abundant fibrous and myxochondroid stromal components. In contrast, Warthin tumors showed relatively lower stiffness values. These findings indicate that elastography alone may not reliably distinguish benign from malignant lesions, but may still provide complementary information when interpreted alongside grayscale and Doppler findings. Real-time elastography (RTE) demonstrated significant associations with pathological diagnosis, with higher elasticity scores more frequently observed in malignant tumors. However, overlap between malignant tumors and pleomorphic adenomas was also noted, highlighting the limitations of qualitative stiffness assessment. Therefore, elastography should not be interpreted independently but rather integrated into a multiparametric US approach. These outcomes match and expand upon existing literature. A meta-analysis highlighted only moderate pooled diagnostic accuracy for salivary malignancies when qualitative scoring or raw stiffness magnitudes were used alone (11), a limitation echoed by the cross-modality overlap in our RTE and mean SWE series. Our emphasis on SWE variability rather than absolute magnitude aligns with recent advancements in multiparametric viscoelastic imaging (20), which incorporating shear wave dispersion and tissue viscosity have demonstrated improved diagnostic separation, allowing investigators to distinguish malignant processes and clearly separate PAs from Warthin tumors. This is further reinforced by our prior study, which confirmed that SWE SD consistently outperforms mean stiffness metrics (15).

The Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology showed excellent diagnostic performance in this cohort, achieving high sensitivity, specificity, and overall accuracy. Notably, cytological classifications designated as suspicious for malignancy (Category V) or malignant (Category VI) were strongly predictive of final malignant histology. Nevertheless, Milan Category III (Atypia of Undetermined Significance) accounted for a substantial proportion of eventually proven malignant tumors (48.72%, 19/39). This marked representation emphasizes the critical need for meticulous clinicopathological correlation, advanced imaging evaluation, and close clinical surveillance in patients presenting with indeterminate cytological findings. These results also align with recent meta-analytic data confirming that the Milan framework performs exceptionally well across diverse cohorts, demonstrating pooled sensitivity and specificity rates that mirror those observed in our series (12). This performance is further contextualized by the second edition of the Milan system, which refined category-specific risk-of-malignancy (ROM) estimates based on cumulative multi-institutional experience (13). Our observation of a notable malignant representation within Category III aligns with these updated global benchmarks, which emphasize that indeterminate categories often harbor a heterogeneous mix of low-grade malignancies. These multi-institutional data strongly support the continued utilization of the Milan system as the preferred cytological framework in our institution’s preoperative surgical work-up.

This study has several strengths, including a relatively large sample size, histopathological confirmation of all cases, and a comprehensive evaluation of multimodal ultrasonographic features. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a retrospective single-center study, which may be subject to selection bias and may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, not all patients underwent complete elastography assessment, which may have affected the robustness of subgroup analyses. Third, the considerable histopathological heterogeneity of malignant salivary gland tumors limited the statistical power for evaluating individual malignant subtypes. Fourth, detailed data on symptom duration, symptom presentation, and long-term clinical outcome or follow-up (e.g., recurrence, disease-specific survival) were not consistently available in retrospective form across the full study period and could therefore not be incorporated into the comparative analyses; prospective collection of these variables represents an important goal for future studies. Recent studies have demonstrated that artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted US analysis may improve diagnostic performance in salivary gland tumor characterization (19, 21). However, AI-based image analysis was not included in the present study. Future multicenter prospective studies incorporating standardized imaging protocols, complete elastography evaluation, and AI-assisted image analysis may further enhance the diagnostic performance and clinical utility of salivary gland US.

Overall, the present study demonstrates that conventional US findings, elastography, and cytological classification provide complementary diagnostic information in the evaluation of salivary gland tumors. A multiparametric US approach combined with cytological assessment may substantially improve preoperative differentiation between benign and malignant salivary gland lesions in routine clinical practice.

Conclusions

Conventional grayscale ultrasonographic features remain valuable for differentiating benign from malignant salivary gland tumors, with heterogeneous echotexture and calcification emerging as independent predictors of malignancy on multivariable analysis, and oval or lobulated shape as independent markers of a lower likelihood of malignancy. Elastography provides complementary information regarding tissue stiffness and tumor heterogeneity, while the Milan System demonstrates excellent diagnostic performance across clinically relevant classification scenarios. Because these imaging and cytological features are readily assessable and reproducible, they may serve as practical, multiparametric diagnostic references for clinicians and trainees involved in the preoperative evaluation of salivary gland tumors.
