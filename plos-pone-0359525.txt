Investigating the anticancer potential of phytochemicals from the mangrove Rhizophora mucronata as inhibitors of lung cancer-associated proteins: Insights from multiscale computational and in silico analyses

Zena Abdul-Ameer Mahdi, Batool Shakir Abed AlmjalawI, Mollah Naimuzzaman, Md Mahabub Hasan, Ajoy Kumer, Farah Tareq Al-Alaq, Hussein OM Al-Dahmoshi, Faizur Rahman

ABSTRACT
Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with a five-year survival rate of approximately 20–25% despite advances in targeted therapy and immunotherapy. There is a continuous need for novel, low-toxicity compounds capable of modulating key oncogenic pathways in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Mangrove ecosystems are rich reservoirs of bioactive phytochemicals, yet the inhibitory potential of compounds from Rhizophora mucronata against validated lung cancer-associated proteins has not been systematically explored using multi-scale computational approaches. This study employed an integrated computational pipeline to evaluate 14 phytochemicals from R. mucronata. Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations at the B3LYP/6-31G* level were performed to characterize electronic properties of the ligands. Molecular docking studies were conducted using both AutoDock Vina (PyRx) and Schrödinger Glide against the Keap1 Kelch domain (PDB 1X2J) and the YEATS2 YEATS domain (PDB 5XNV). Top-ranked complexes were subjected to 100-ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulations in Desmond, and pharmacokinetic and toxicity profiles were predicted using SwissADME and pkCSM. Among the screened compounds, ligand L10 (cyclohexane-1,2,3,4,5,6-hexaol) exhibited the smallest HOMO–LUMO energy gap (0.36 eV), favorable binding affinities (Glide score of –7.789 kcal/mol against 1X2J), multiple hydrogen-bonding interactions, and stable complex behavior during MD simulations (Cα RMSD < 3 Å). Several other ligands (L02, L05, and L07) also displayed promising profiles. Most compounds complied with Lipinski’s rule of five and showed acceptable predicted ADMET properties. These preliminary findings suggest that Rhizophora mucronate -derived phytochemicals, particularly L10, are promising candidates for further experimental evaluation as modulators of Keap1 and YEATS2 in lung cancer. This study provides a computational framework for prioritizing natural product leads from mangrove species for subsequent in vitro and in vivo validation.

1. Introduction Lung cancer is one of the top 3 causes of cancer mortality globally, causing nearly 1.8 million deaths a year and representing a significant burden on global health [ 1 ]. Even with significant progress in targeted therapy and immunotherapy, the overall five-year survival is still modest, mainly due to patients being diagnosed at advanced stages [ 2 ]. The five-year relative survival rate is still only ~25% for all stages, highlighting a continued unmet need in finding safe and effective therapeutic agents [ 3 ]. The pathogenesis of lung cancer is affected by diverse modifiable and environmental risk factors [ 4 , 5 ]. Tobacco smoking persists the leading aetiological factor, with passive smoking also playing an important role in disease causation [ 6 – 8 ]. These include environmental exposures such as ambient air pollution, household air pollution, and radon, as well as occupational carcinogens and genetic susceptibility [ 9 – 11 ]. Lung cancer is asymptomatic in most cases during the early stages and hence detected at advanced disease state when curative treatment options become scarce [ 12 ]. Management for current treatment approaches consists of surgical options, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, molecularly-targeted therapy and immunotherapy. While these strategies have improved clinical outcomes, their utility can be limited by treatment-induced toxicity, pan-tumor drug resistance and recurrence, biomarker-restrictive accessibility as well as high costs [ 13 – 16 ]. These drawbacks are driving the identification of less toxic and more efficacious therapeutic candidates. Natural-product-based phytochemicals have been used as the primary source of structurally diverse scaffolds for anticancer compound discovery [ 17 ]. Ecosystems of mangroves are notably significant because they constitute, as yet underexplored, a true reservoir of bioactive secondary metabolites: viz. flavonoids, tannins, terpenoids and alkaloids with reported antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and cytotoxic activity [ 18 ]. Lung cancer is generally divided into small-cell lung cancer and non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Non-small-lung- cell carcinoma accounts for about 85% of these cases [ 19 ]. Traditional medicinal practice uses mangrove species, which are known to contain phytochemicals with a wide range of biological activities including anticancer [ 20 ], antibacterial properties (p-glyoproteins in Rhizophora mucronata and plant extracts based natural product) antioxidant, anti-inflammatory property. It contains many polyphenols, alkaloids, and other secondary metabolites that may have a pharmacological importance [ 21 , 22 ]. Antitumor activity of R. mucronata extracts was found in various cancer-cell models, such as lung carcinoma cells [ 18 , 23 ]. Thus, R. mucronata appears not only as a source of active cysteine proteinase inhibitors, but other biological activities have also been reported from the extracts and from isolated constituents that warrant future investigation of this species for potential anticancer compounds [ 24 ]. Nevertheless, no systematic all-in-one identification of R. mucronata phytochemicals acting as protein-selective modulators against lung cancer-related targets has been illustrated through an integrated method in silico studies till date. Thus, we selected two mechanistically relevant NSCLC-associated proteins as molecular targets: the Kelch domain of Keap1 (PDB ID: 1X2J) and the YEATS domain of YEATS2 (PDB ID: 5XNV). Keap1 is a major negative regulator of Nrf2 and the Keap1–Nrf2 pathway is often abrogated in NSCLC. Mutations of Keap1 induce persistent Nrf2 activation, which can lead to antioxidant protection, metabolic adaptation, cell survival and more importantly anticancer treatment resistance [ 25 , 26 ]. Thus, targeting Keap1–Nrf2 offers a biologically relevant approach for probing redox-dependent mechanisms of NSCLC progression. Histone acetylation is associated with transcriptional activation. In lung cancer, amplification or overexpression of YEATS2 is linked to the progression of NSCLC via oncogenic transcriptional programs associated with tumor-cell proliferation and survival [ 27 ]. Hence, targeting the YEATS2 YEATS domain may allow a strategy to modulate aberrant acetylation-dependent transcriptional signaling. Thus, Keap1 and YEATS2 were chosen as composite molecular targets indicative of redox-regulatory and epigenetic mechanisms associated with NSCLC pathogenesis. Here, we provide a systematic evaluation of phytochemicals from R. mucronata against Keap1 and YEATS2 to test our hypothesis that they could act as modulators of these proteins, representing an innovation in overcoming this identified knowledge gap via an integrated computational workflow. We used Density Functional Theory (DFT), molecular docking through AutoDock Vina/PyRx and Schrödinger Glide, ADMET profiling and 100-ns molecular dynamics simulations to provide the electronic properties, predicted binding interactions, pharmacokinetic behavior and complex stability. This multiscale approach was therefore carried out to rank potential phytochemicals for further in vitro validation instead of deriving conclusive biological activity directly from hundreds of thousands of predicted computationally. 2. Methodology 2.1 Optimization and ligand preparation The 14 phytochemicals from R. mucronata were systematically selected based on a comprehensive literature review of its reported secondary metabolites, prioritizing chemical diversity across multiple classes including nitrogen-containing heteroaromatics (L01, L02, L05, L014), polyethers/siloxanes (L03, L13), long-chain esters/fatty acids (L04, L06, L09), polyhydroxy carbohydrates/sugar derivatives (L07, L08, L10), steroidal alcohols (L011), and aromatic oximes/naphthalene derivatives (L012) to (L014) cover a broad range of polarity, aromaticity, hydrogen-bonding capacity, and lipophilicity that could enable diverse interactions with target proteins. The Universal Force Field (UFF) was used for geometry pre-optimization to fix bond lengths and angles that were too long or too short, get rid of steric clashes, and get reasonable starting conformations. The Build Parameters module of Materials Studio was then used to make parameter files that included all the necessary force-field terms, atom types, and partial charges for the next quantum-mechanical calculations. The DMol 3 module in Materials Studio was employed to perform the density functional theory calculations using the computational level specified for this study. The molecular structures were initially subjected to geometry pre-optimization using the Universal Force Field (UFF) to remove unfavorable geometries and steric clashes. Solvent effects were incorporated using the COSMO model where applicable. Geometry optimization was continued until the specified convergence criteria for total energy and nuclear coordinates were satisfied. The optimized structures were subsequently used to calculate the frontier molecular orbital energies and global chemical-reactivity descriptors, including the HOMO–LUMO energy gap, ionization potential, electron affinity, chemical potential, electronegativity, hardness, softness, and electrophilicity index. E. Shaw Research, New York, NY, USA). Each system was parameterized with the OPLS‑AA force field, placed in an orthorhombic periodic box of TIP3P water with a 10 Å buffer, and electrically neutralized by adding Na⁺ or Cl - counter‑ions. A standard multi-stage equilibration protocol was applied: steepest-descent energy minimization, gradual heating to 300 K, and restrained equilibration (NVT followed by NPT) to relax the solvent while gradually releasing restraints on the solute. Production MD runs were conducted in the NPT ensemble (300 K, 1.01325 bar) employing the Nose-Hoover thermostat and Martyna-Tuckerman-Klein barostat, with a 2-fs integration time step and SHAKE constraints on all bonds involving hydrogen atoms. Trajectory files and energy logs were analyzed in Maestro and the Simulation Event Analysis module of Desmond. Main constancy metrics including root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of the protein Cα atoms, root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) per residue, radius of gyration (R_g), and persistent protein–ligand contacts (hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic interactions, and water bridges) were calculated over the entire 100-ns trajectory [ 35 ]. This inclusive computational investigation followed conventional best practices and parameters, enabling a thorough exploration of the molecular system’s dynamic behavior. 2.5 Lipinski rule, drug-likeness and pharmacokinetics On October 9, 2025, SwissADME, a common online platform and its famous for flexibility and convenience, was attached to predict pharmacokinetics and evaluate drug-like assets [ 36 ]. Compounds with smaller HOMO–LUMO energy gaps are generally considered more electronically polarizable and reactive, whereas larger energy gaps are associated with greater electronic stability. However, these descriptors characterize intrinsic electronic properties and should not be interpreted as direct predictors of anticancer activity or protein-binding affinity. Their principal value in the present study is to complement the molecular docking and molecular dynamics analyses by providing additional physicochemical information on the screened compounds. The calculated energy gaps ranged widely from 0.36 eV (L10) to 8.98 eV (L05). Ligand L10 exhibited the smallest HOMO–LUMO gap, indicating higher electronic reactivity and a potentially greater tendency for charge transfer. In contrast, L05 showed the largest energy gap, consistent with higher electronic stability. Variations were also observed in other descriptors, including chemical potential, hardness, and electrophilicity index. For example, L10 displayed the most negative chemical potential and relatively high electrophilicity, whereas L05 exhibited contrasting behavior. The quantum chemical descriptors revealed notable differences in the electronic properties and reactivity profiles across the ligand series. Ligands L01, L04, L06, L08, and L10 showed the most negative LUMO energies (ranging from −9.05 to −10.51 eV). L10, in particular, exhibited the lowest LUMO energy (−10.51 eV). However, these DFT-derived parameters provide only supportive insights into the intrinsic electronic characteristics of the compounds. They are not validated predictors of biological or anticancer activity, and a smaller HOMO–LUMO gap should not be interpreted as implying superior therapeutic potential. These results are best viewed as complementary chemical characterization that may help rationalize possible molecular interaction mechanisms when considered alongside docking and molecular dynamics data. 3.1.3 Frontier molecular orbital (FMOs): HOMO and LUMO. Frontier molecular orbital analysis was employed to examine the electronic distribution of the screened phytochemicals. The HOMO identifies regions with relatively greater electron-donating character, whereas the LUMO indicates regions capable of accepting electron density. The HOMO–LUMO energy gap was considered an electronic-reactivity descriptor and was not interpreted as a direct measure of protein-binding affinity or anticancer activity [ 45 – 49 ]. A smaller HOMO–LUMO energy gap favors stronger interaction of the ligands with protein targets, and the patterns seen in Fig 2 and S2 Fig reveal multiple HOMO and LUMO‑rich zones that help explain their predicted reactivity and pharmacological potential. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359525.g002 Fig 2 Frontier molecular orbitals diagram for HOMO and LUMO. 3.1.4 Electrostatic potential map for Frontier Molecular Orbital. Understanding the reactivity and stability of molecules in chemical reactions is paramount, often elucidated through electrostatic potential maps of Frontier Molecular Orbitals (FMO). The pivotal roles played by HOMO and the LUMO in dictating a molecule’s reactivity, as proposed by FMO theory, cannot be overstated. Analysis of the HOMO electrostatic potential map offers valuable insights into regions of electron density susceptible to nucleophilic attacks, while the LUMO map identifies sites prone to electrophilic attacks due to electron deficiency. By examining these maps, chemists can forecast reaction mechanisms and outcomes. Moreover, comparative analysis of electrostatic potential maps among different molecules enables the assessment of their relative reactivity and selectivity in diverse reactions, facilitating rationalization and prediction of chemical reactivity and aiding in the design of molecules with tailored properties. Reported Keap1 and YEATS-domain inhibitors were therefore used primarily as qualitative structural and pharmacological references rather than as directly comparable numerical standards. In particular, docking scores expressed in kcal/mol are distinct from the octanol–water partition coefficient (logP), which describes molecular lipophilicity and is dimensionless. Accordingly, no direct relationship between logP and docking energy is assumed in the present analysis. Conclusion In this multi-scale computational study, phytochemicals from the mangrove species R. mucronata was investigated for their ability to modulate two important lung cancer-related proteins: the Keap1 Kelch domain (PDB 1X2J) and the YEATS2 YEATS domain (PDB 5XNV). In this study, a number of compounds with favorable electronic properties, moderate predicted binding affinities and acceptable in-silico physicochemical profiles were screened using Density Functional Theory (DFT), molecular docking (PyRx and Schrödinger Glide), 100-ns molecular dynamics simulations and ADMET profiling. Among the analyses performed, Ligand L10 (cyclohexane-1,2,3,4,5,6-hexaol) was one of the most preferred candidates. Our results propose that naturally derived compounds from R. mucronata can be a promising starting point for the design of new inhibitors of Keap1–Nrf2 and YEATS2-pathways, relevant to NSCLC. Nevertheless, the current results are purely based on calculations. Docking scores, DFT descriptors and MD stability indicators suggest potential activity in a semi-quantitative manner and should not be over-interpreted as indicative of biological activity. Experimental validation will be a critical way to build on these findings. We propose a series of initial validation steps to assess the efficacy in vitro: [ 1 ] cytotoxicity and anti-proliferative effects in NSCLC cell lines such as A549 and H1975 that represent Keap1-mutant models with selectivity assessment in normal lung epithelial cells [ 2 ], examination of target engagement and pathway modulation utilizing Western blotting, qRT-PCR, and H3K27ac-dependent reporter assays for Nrf2 transcriptional activity; [ 3 ] indications of apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, or potential synergy with standard chemotherapeutics; and [ 4 ] further mechanistic pharmacokinetics studies on the most active compounds.
