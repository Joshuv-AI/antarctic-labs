Mean-field theory via dissociated arrays for particle systems interacting through noisy weights
Nicolas Fournier, Datong Zhou

Abstract
. We study a mean-field limit for a N N -particle system in which each particle follows a diffusion and interacts with other particles through a weight on each directed edge. Each weight evolves according to its own nonlinear SDE driven by a Brownian motion, with coefficients involving the states of the two endpoint particles of the edge. The initial vertex and edge variables are assumed to have a dissociated Aldous–Hoover form. We construct the limiting nonlinear SDE by averaging the interaction over an independent neighbor and an edge input, prove its well-posedness, and show that the dissociated vertex-edge structure is propagated by the dynamics. This propagation property is an analogue of propagation of chaos in the case where the weight of each edge may remain correlated with the states of the two endpoint particles. Under either a bounded-observable assumption or a sub-Gaussian edge-input condition, the finite system converges to this limit through quantitative coupling estimates for a typical particle and a typical edge. We also prove the convergence of the empirical measure of particle’s state pairs and their interaction weights.

Description of the model and main result

1.1.
Motivation

In its classical exchangeable form, mean-field theory describes the
large-population limit of an interacting particle system through one typical particle, of which the state solves a nonlinear SDE, see the seminal works of Kac
[
22
]
, McKean
[
27
]
, Sznitman
[
30
]
, Méléard
[
28
]
.

A first way to incorporate heterogeneity is to keep the interaction structure
fixed: the interaction kernel is prescribed in advance.
Graphon and graph-kernel mean-field limits describe such system, see for instance
Chiba and Medvedev
[
9
]
, Kaliuzhnyi-Verbovetskyi and Medvedev
[
23
]
, Bayraktar, Chakraborty and Wu
[
3
]
and Jabin and co-authors
[
18
,
20
,
19
]
.
In all these works, the interaction weights remain fixed along the dynamics.

However, in many adaptive network models, the interaction strength between
two particles is itself a dynamical variable, depending on the states of the two
particles and possibly driven by its own noise. Such feedback is natural in
co-evolutionary network models and in synaptic plasticity, see Gross and Blasius
[
16
]
and Bi and Poo
[
5
]
.

Mathematical works on genuine co-evolutionary dynamics include deterministic
continuum limits for pairwise adaptive networks, see Gkogkas, Kuehn and Xu
[
14
,
15
]
, as well as probabilistic models
with intrinsic stochasticity and dynamically evolving edge variables, see Bayraktar and Wu
[
4
]
and Ganguly, Spiliopoulos and Sussman
[
13
]
. The companion work

[
31
]
develops a structural metric and sampling viewpoint for
linear weight adaptation.

The goal of the present paper is to treat a class of particle systems with
fully noisy and nonlinear real-valued edge weights. We keep track not only of a one-particle law, but of a vertex-edge array whose dissociated structure has to be propagated by the
dynamics.

Our first main result establishes well-posedness for the limiting equation before

1.6.
Propagation of dissociatedness

In the classical setting, propagation of chaos is
represented by independent copies of a single nonlinear process. Here the
limiting object is an array: subarrays supported on disjoint vertex sets are
independent, but an edge remains coupled with its two endpoints. The next remark
makes explicit how this Aldous–Hoover structure is propagated by the limiting
dynamics.

Remark 10
.

After fixing measurable versions of the strong solution maps, we
may write

\bar{X}^{i}=\mathsf{F}(\xi_{i},B^{i}),\qquad\bar{U}^{ij}=\mathsf{G}(\xi_{i},B^{i},\xi_{j},B^{j},\xi^{\dagger}_{ij},W^{ij}),

where
\mathsf{F}:E\times{\mathcal{C}}_{m}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
is the solution map for the
nonlinear SDE and

\mathsf{G}(a,v,b,\tilde{v},c,w)=\Gamma(a,\mathsf{F}(a,v),b,\mathsf{F}(b,\tilde{v}),c,w).

The dissociatedness (Aldous–Hoover representation) of Assumption
1
is therefore propagated by
the limit dynamics: vertices carry
(\xi_{i},B^{i})
and directed edges carry

(\xi^{\dagger}_{ij},W^{ij})
. Hence, for any
t\geq 0
, for any collection

C\subset\{(i,j):i\geq 1,j\geq 1,i\neq j\}
such that for all
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in C
distinct, the four numbers
i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}
are distinct, the variables
(\bar{X}^{i}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t},\bar{U}^{ij}_{t})_{(i,j)\in C}
are
i.i.d. This propagation of dissociatedness is the analogue of the classical propagation of chaos.

1.7.
Comments

One line of non-exchangeable
mean-field theory starts from a fixed graph kernel or graphon and derives an
extended Vlasov or McKean–Vlasov equation, see for
instance the works cited above
[
9
,
23
,
3
,
18
,
20
]
.
This is particularly well-suited to non-adaptive interaction structures,
with fixed interaction weights.
Here the fixed-label picture means the following type of representation: one
embeds the vertices into a predetermined latent space
D
and represents the
edge structure at time
t
by a deterministic kernel

w_{t}:D\times D\to{\mathbb{R}}
, evaluated at the two labels. The label of a vertex is
chosen once and for all, and the random evolution is not allowed to enlarge the
domain by adding new vertex or edge randomness.

A second line studies dynamic random networks using exchangeable arrays and
graph limits, closely connected with the Aldous–Hoover viewpoint; see
Crane
[
10
,
11
]
, Černỳ and Klimovsky
[
8
]
, Athreya, Den Hollander and Röllin
[
2
]

and Braunsteins, den Hollander and Mandjes
[
7
,
6
]
.
These papers actually study some graph-valued Markov processes. There are no underlying particles.

Closer to our setting are works on genuine co-evolutionary dynamics, where the
edge variables and the vertex states influence each other. In the deterministic
case, Gkogkas, Kuehn and Xu
[
14
,
15
]

establish continuum and mean-field limits for adaptive Kuramoto-type networks
through evolving kernels, graph measures, or signed digraph measures. These
models share the pairwise feedback mechanism with ours, but they are fully
deterministic systems.

Probabilistic models with intrinsic stochasticity have also been considered.
Bayraktar and Wu
[
4
]
study a continuous-time model in
which both the vertex and edge states take values in some countable spaces, while
Ganguly, Spiliopoulos and Sussman
[
13
]
analyze a discrete-time
latent-variable model with binary dynamic edges, feedback effects, and
graphon/multiplexon limits.

The companion work
[
31
]
develops a structural metric and sampling
viewpoint for the same system (
1
)-(
2
) when the weight adaptation is
linear and non noisy (that is when
\phi(u)=u
,
\alpha(x,y,u)=a(x,y)+b(x,y)u
and
\beta(x,y,u)=0
). In that setting, weight fluctuations can be absorbed at the level of the empirical
state-weight structure. The main stability estimate in
[
31
]
controls the
evolution in a hybrid Wasserstein and cut distance by the initial structural
error and the graphon sampling error.

Here we allow a more general edge dynamics,
where the averaging reduction is no longer available. The edge variables solve their own noisy dynamics and enter
the particle equation nonlinearly, so the full edge law and its correlation with
the endpoint trajectories have to be retained in the mean-field description. To
our knowledge, this is the first mean-field result for fully
nonlinear and noisy adaptive weights.

1.8.
Technical comments

We finish with a few comments on possible extensions and on the rates obtained
above. First, it would be natural to relax the boundedness assumptions on the
coefficients, while keeping global Lipschitz continuity. This should be possible
under suitable additional moment assumptions, but would require more involved
estimates.

The convergence rate in Theorem
9
-(iii) is not intended to be optimal.
Our proof partitions the directed edges, so that each independent
subsample has only order
N
(independent) elements. Applying the standard empirical

{\mathcal{W}}_{1}
estimate in dimension
2d+1
, see
[
12
, Theorem 1]
, gives the rate

N^{-1/(2d+1)}
. In the idealized situation where the
N(N-1)
edge
triples were independent, the same general empirical
{\mathcal{W}}_{1}
estimate
would give a rate of order
N^{-2/(2d+1)}
. We do not know what the optimal rate should be.

A related extension would be to replace the diffusive particle dynamics by
jump-type dynamics, as in integrate-and-fire models from mathematical
neuroscience. Such models are often driven by Poisson events rather than
Brownian noises, see for instance
[
19
,
20
]
. We expect
that the same dissociated vertex-edge viewpoint should remain useful.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\phi(U^{1j,N}_{t})\gamma(X^{1,N}_{t},X^{j,N}_{t})-\phi(\bar{U}^{1j}_{t})\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t})\Big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[|U^{1j,N}_{t}-\bar{U}^{1j}_{t}|^{2}+|X^{1,N}_{t}-\bar{X}^{1}_{t}|^{2}+|X^{j,N}_{t}-\bar{X}^{j}_{t}|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{T}}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}(|X^{1,N}_{s}-\bar{X}^{1}_{s}|^{2}+|X^{j,N}_{s}-{\bar{X}^{j}_{s}}|^{2})\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{1,N}_{s}-\bar{X}^{1}_{s}|^{2}\Big]

by (
19
) and exchangeability.
Since
\bar{U}^{1j}_{t}=\Gamma_{t}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1},\xi_{j},\bar{X}^{j},\xi^{\dagger}_{1j},W^{1j})
by Lemma
5
,

K_{t}=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\phi(\Gamma_{t}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1},\xi_{j},\bar{X}^{j},\xi^{\dagger}_{1j},W^{1j}))\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t})-\Lambda_{\mu}(t,\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})\Big|^{2}\Big].

By independence of
(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})
and

(\xi_{j},\bar{X}^{j},\xi^{\dagger}_{1j},W^{1j})_{j\geq 2}
, see (
18
),
The family

\big(\phi(\Gamma_{t}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1},\xi_{j},\bar{X}^{j},\xi^{\dagger}_{1j},W^{1j}))\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t})\big)_{j\geq 2}

is i.i.d. conditionally on
(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})
. Moreover, by definition of
\Lambda_{\mu}
,

\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\phi(\Gamma_{t}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1},\xi_{2},\bar{X}^{2},\xi^{\dagger}_{12},W^{12}))\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{2}_{t})\Big]=\Lambda_{\mu}(t,\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1}),

where
\mathbb{E}_{1}=\mathbb{E}[\cdot|(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})]
. Hence, abusively writing
\mathrm{Var}_{1}Z=\mathbb{E}_{1}[|Z-\mathbb{E}_{1}[Z]|^{2}]
for
Z
valued in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

K_{t}=\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{N-1}\mathrm{Var}_{1}\Big(\phi(\Gamma_{t}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1},\xi_{2},\bar{X}^{2},\xi^{\dagger}_{12},W^{12}))\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{2}_{t})\Big)\Big]\leq\frac{||\phi||_{\infty}^{2}||\gamma||_{\infty}^{2}}{N-1}\leq\frac{C}{N}.

We used that
N\geq 2
in the last inequality.

All in all, we have proved that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

I_{t}\leq 2J_{t}+2K_{t}\leq C_{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{1,N}_{s}-\bar{X}^{1}_{s}|^{2}\Big]+\frac{C}{N}.

Recalling (
20
), we get, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{1,N}_{s}-\bar{X}^{1}_{s}|^{2}\Big]\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{u\in[0,s]}|X^{1,N}_{u}-\bar{X}^{1}_{u}|^{2}\Big]{\mathrm{d}}s+\frac{C_{T}}{N}.

Using finally the Gronwall lemma, we find as desired that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{1,N}_{t}-\bar{X}^{1}_{t}|^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{C_{T}}{N},\quad\text{whence also}\quad\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|U^{12,N}_{t}-\bar{U}^{12}_{t}|^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{C_{T}}{N}

by (
19
).

We now prove (ii). We adopt the same notation as above and observe that we still have (
18
).
We fix
T>0
and divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1 : Gaussian moment for the edge.

There are
\theta_{T}>0
and
C_{T}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(2\theta_{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(\bar{U}^{12}_{t})^{2}\Big)\Big]\leq C_{T}.

Recall (
9
): setting
G_{12}=G(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi^{\dagger}_{12})
and

R_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(\bar{X}^{1}_{s},\bar{X}^{2}_{s},\bar{U}^{12}_{s}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}^{12}
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{U}^{12}_{t}|\leq|G_{12}|+T||\alpha||_{\infty}+R_{T}^{*},\quad\hbox{where}\quad R_{T}^{*}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|R_{t}|.

By (
8
),
G_{12}
has a Gaussian moment.
By the Dubins-Schwarz theorem, see e.g. Revuz-Yor
[
29
, Theorem 1.6 p 181]
,
we can find a Brownian motion
M
such that a.s., for all
t\geq 0
,
R_{t}=M_{A_{t}}
,
with
A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\beta^{2}(\bar{X}^{1}_{s},\bar{X}^{2}_{s},\bar{U}^{12}_{s}){\mathrm{d}}s
. Thus
R_{T}^{*}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,||\beta||_{\infty}^{2}T]}|M_{t}|
.
Hence
R_{T}^{*}
has a Gaussian moment. Choosing
\theta_{T}>0
small
enough and using
(x+y+z)^{2}\leq 3(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})
together with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get the conclusion.

Step 2: Empirical fluctuation.
Set

\mu=\mathrm{Law}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})
. There is
C_{T}>0
such that for all

t\in[0,T]
,

\Delta_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\phi(\bar{U}^{1j}_{t})\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t})-\Lambda_{\mu}(t,\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})\Big|\Big]\leq\frac{C_{T}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

As in the proof of (i) (study of
K_{t}
)
we have
\Delta_{t}=\mathbb{E}[E_{t}]
, where, setting
\mathbb{E}_{1}=\mathbb{E}[\cdot|(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})]
,

E_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\phi(\Gamma_{t}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1},\xi_{j},\bar{X}^{j},\xi^{\dagger}_{1j},W^{1j}))\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t})-\Lambda_{\mu}(t,\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})\Big|\Big].

We have

\displaystyle E_{t}\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\phi(\Gamma_{t}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1},\xi_{j},\bar{X}^{j},\xi^{\dagger}_{1j},W^{1j}))\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t})-\Lambda_{\mu}(t,\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})\Big|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{N-1}\mathrm{Var}_{1}\;\Big(\phi(\Gamma_{t}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1},\xi_{2},\bar{X}^{2},\xi^{\dagger}_{12},W^{12}))\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{2}_{t})\Big)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

as in the proof of (i). Thus, recalling that
\Gamma_{t}(\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1},\xi_{2},\bar{X}^{2},\xi^{\dagger}_{12},W^{12})=\bar{U}^{12}_{t}
, for all
N\geq 2
,

\Delta_{t}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}\mathbb{E}[(\phi(\bar{U}^{12}_{t})\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{t},\bar{X}^{2}_{t}))^{2}]\leq\frac{C}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathbb{E}[1+(\bar{U}^{12}_{t})^{2}]\leq\frac{C_{T}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

We used that
\phi
has at most linear growth, that
\gamma
is bounded, and
that
\sup_{[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[(\bar{U}^{12}_{t})^{2}]<\infty
.

Step 3 : Notation.

For
i,j\in I_{N}
with
i\neq j
and
0\leq s\leq t
, set

Y^{i,N}_{s,t}=\sup_{u\in[s,t]}|X^{i,N}_{u}-\bar{X}^{i}_{u}|\quad\text{and}\quad Z^{ij,N}_{s,t}=\sup_{u\in[s,t]}|U^{ij,N}_{u}-\bar{U}^{ij}_{u}|.

Step 4. Edge stability.

In this step we establish a short-time estimate for the edge error
Z^{12,N}
.
Fix
0\leq S\leq S^{\prime}\leq T
and
t\in[S,S^{\prime}]
. Recalling (
2
) and (
9
), we see that

Z^{12,N}_{S,t}\leq Z^{12,N}_{S,S}+I^{1}_{S,t}+I^{2}_{S,t}
, where

\displaystyle I^{1}_{S,t}=

\displaystyle\int_{S}^{t}|\alpha(X^{1,N}_{s},X^{2,N}_{s},U^{12,N}_{s})-\alpha(\bar{X}^{1}_{s},\bar{X}^{2}_{s},\bar{U}^{12}_{s})|{\mathrm{d}}s,

\displaystyle I^{2}_{S,t}=

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in[S,t]}\Big|\int_{S}^{r}(\beta(X^{1,N}_{s},X^{2,N}_{s},U^{12,N}_{s})-\beta(\bar{X}^{1}_{s},\bar{X}^{2}_{s},\bar{U}^{12}_{s})){\mathrm{d}}W^{12}_{s}\Big|.

Using that
\alpha
is Lipschitz continuous, we find

\mathbb{E}[I^{1}_{S,t}]\leq C(t-S)\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Y^{2,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]\leq C(S^{\prime}-S)\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]

by exchangeability. Using the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality and that
\beta
is Lipschitz continuous,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[I^{2}_{S,t}]\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{S}^{t}(\beta(X^{1,N}_{s},X^{2,N}_{s},U^{12,N}_{s})-\beta(\bar{X}^{1}_{s},\bar{X}^{2}_{s},\bar{U}^{12}_{s}))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]\leq C(S^{\prime}-S)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]

by exchangeability again. Combining the bounds on
I^{1}_{S,t}
and
I^{2}_{S,t}
, we get,
for all
0\leq S\leq t\leq S^{\prime}\leq T
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z^{12,N}_{S,S}]+\kappa_{1}[S^{\prime}-S+(S^{\prime}-S)^{\frac{1}{2}}]\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]

for some
\kappa_{1}>0
depending only on
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Step 5: Particle stability.
Here we establish a short-time estimate
for
Y^{1,N}
. Fix
0\leq S\leq S^{\prime}\leq T
and
t\in[S,S^{\prime}]
.
Recalling (
1
) and (
17
), we have

Y^{1,N}_{S,t}\leq Y^{1,N}_{S,S}+J^{1}_{S,t}+J^{2}_{S,t}+J^{3}_{S,t}
, where

\displaystyle J^{1}_{S,t}=\int_{S}^{t}|b(X^{1,N}_{s})-b(\bar{X}^{1}_{s})|{\mathrm{d}}s,\quad J^{2}_{S,t}=\sup_{r\in[S,t]}\Big|\int_{S}^{r}(\sigma(X^{1,N}_{s})-\sigma(\bar{X}^{1}_{s})){\mathrm{d}}B^{1}_{s}\Big|,

\displaystyle J^{3}_{S,t}=\int_{S}^{t}\Big|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\phi(U^{1j,N}_{s})\gamma(X^{1,N}_{s},X^{j,N}_{s})-\Lambda_{\mu}(s,\xi_{1},\bar{X}^{1})\Big|{\mathrm{d}}s.

The terms
J^{1}_{S,t}
and
J^{2}_{S,t}
are the drift and martingale contributions
coming from the single-particle coefficients
b
and
\sigma
, while
J^{3}_{S,t}

is the interaction contribution. Exactly as in Step 4, using the Lipschitz
continuity of
b
and
\sigma
and the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality, we find that

\mathbb{E}[J^{1}_{S,t}+J^{2}_{S,t}]\leq C[S^{\prime}-S+(S^{\prime}-S)^{\frac{1}{2}}]\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}].

Recalling Step 2, we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[J^{3}_{S,t}]\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\Big(\phi(U^{1j,N}_{s})\gamma(X^{1,N}_{s},X^{j,N}_{s})-\phi(\bar{U}^{1j}_{s})\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{s},\bar{X}^{j}_{s})\Big)\Big|\Big]{\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{S}^{t}\Delta_{s}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|\phi(U^{12,N}_{s})\gamma(X^{1,N}_{s},X^{2,N}_{s})-\phi(\bar{U}^{12}_{s})\gamma(\bar{X}^{1}_{s},\bar{X}^{2}_{s})|]{\mathrm{d}}s+C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}

by exchangeability and Step 2. We now set

H_{s}^{N}=(|X^{1,N}_{s}-\bar{X}^{1}_{s}|\wedge 1)+(|X^{2,N}_{s}-\bar{X}^{2}_{s}|\wedge 1)
.
Since
\gamma
and
\phi
are Lipschitz continuous, with
\gamma

bounded, for
t\in[S,S^{\prime}]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[J^{3}_{S,t}]\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{S}^{t}\mathbb{E}[(1+|\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|)H_{s}^{N}+|U^{12,N}_{s}-\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|]{\mathrm{d}}s+C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{S}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|H_{s}^{N}]{\mathrm{d}}s+C(S^{\prime}\!-\!S)\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]+C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining these estimates (and using exchangeability),
we get, for all
0\leq S\leq t\leq S^{\prime}\leq T
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}]\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,S}]+C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\kappa_{2}[S^{\prime}-S+(S^{\prime}-S)^{\frac{1}{2}}]\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]+\kappa_{2}\int_{S}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|H_{s}^{N}]{\mathrm{d}}s

for some
\kappa_{2}>0
depending only on
b
,
\sigma
,
\gamma
and
\phi
.

Step 6: Truncation and recursion.
Gathering Steps 4 and 5, we find
\kappa_{3}>0
such that for all

0\leq S\leq S^{\prime}\leq T
, all
t\in[S,S^{\prime}]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,S}+Z^{12,N}_{S,S}]+C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\kappa_{3}[S^{\prime}-S+(S^{\prime}-S)^{\frac{1}{2}}]\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]

\displaystyle+\kappa_{3}\int_{S}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|H_{s}^{N}]{\mathrm{d}}s.

Let
\delta>0
such that
\kappa_{3}(\delta+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}})=\frac{1}{2}
and

\kappa_{4}=2\kappa_{3}
. Then for all

0\leq S\leq S^{\prime}\leq T\land(S+\delta)
, all
t\in[S,S^{\prime}]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,S}+Z^{12,N}_{S,S}]+C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\kappa_{4}\int_{S}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|H_{s}^{N}]{\mathrm{d}}s.

The only remaining difficulty is the unbounded factor
\bar{U}^{12}
in
the last integral. We handle it by truncating at a suitable level
A
.
For any
A>0
and
s\geq S
, using exchangeability and Step 1,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|H_{s}^{N}]

\displaystyle\leq A\mathbb{E}[|X^{1,N}_{s}-\bar{X}^{1}_{s}|+|X^{2,N}_{s}-\bar{X}^{2}_{s}|]+2\mathbb{E}[|\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|{\bf 1}_{\{|\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|>A\}}]

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2A\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,s}]+C_{T}e^{-\theta_{T}A^{2}}.

In the last line, we used that

u{\bf 1}_{\{u>A\}}\leq C_{T}e^{-\theta_{T}A^{2}}e^{2\theta_{T}u^{2}}
and the Gaussian moment
from Step 1. Plugging this into the preceding inequality, we get that
for all
0\leq S\leq S^{\prime}\leq T\land(S+\delta)
, all
t\in[S,S^{\prime}]
and all
A>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,t}+Z^{12,N}_{S,t}]\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,S}+Z^{12,N}_{S,S}]+C_{T}(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+e^{-\theta_{T}A^{2}})+2A\kappa_{4}\int_{S}^{t}\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,s}+Z^{12,N}_{S,s}]{\mathrm{d}}s.

Using the Gronwall lemma, we find that for all
0\leq S\leq S^{\prime}\leq T\land(S+\delta)
, for all
A>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,S^{\prime}}+Z^{12,N}_{S,S^{\prime}}]\leq

\displaystyle\Big(2\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,S}+Z^{12,N}_{S,S}]+C_{T}(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+e^{-\theta_{T}A^{2}})\Big)e^{2\kappa_{4}A\delta}.

Choosing
A=(\frac{\log N}{2\theta_{T}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
this gives, for all
0\leq S\leq S^{\prime}\leq T\land(S+\delta)
,

(21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,S^{\prime}}+Z^{12,N}_{S,S^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle\leq\Big(2\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{S,S}+Z^{12,N}_{S,S}]+C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)K_{N,T},

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad K_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(2\kappa_{4}\delta\Big(\frac{\log N}{2\theta_{T}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

Step 7: Conclusion.
Set

k=\lceil T/\delta\rceil
and
t_{\ell}=\ell\delta\land T
, for
\ell=0,\dots,k
.
Set
u_{N,0}=0
and, for
\ell=0,\dots,k-1
,

u_{N,\ell+1}=\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,N}_{t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1}}+Z^{12,N}_{t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1}}].

By (
21
), for all
\ell=0,\dots,k-1
,

u_{N,\ell+1}\leq(2u_{N,\ell}+C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2}})K_{N,T}.

Indeed, the error at the left endpoint of the
\ell
-th block is bounded by

u_{N,\ell}
. We classically conclude that, modifying the value of
C_{T}
,

u_{N,\ell}\leq C_{T}K_{N,T}^{k}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}

for all
\ell=1,\dots,k
.
All in all, modifying again the value of
C_{T}
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}(|X^{1,N}_{s}\!-\!\bar{X}^{1}_{s}|\!+\!|U^{12,N}_{s}\!-\!\bar{U}^{12}_{s}|)\Big]\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}u_{N,\ell}\leq C_{T}K_{N,T}^{k}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Since

K_{N,T}^{k}=\exp\Big(2\kappa_{4}k\delta\Big(\frac{\log N}{2\theta_{T}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big),

the conclusion of (ii) follows.

We finally prove (iii). Under the standing alternative in the statement,
either the assumption of Theorem
9
-(i) or that of Theorem
9
-(ii)
holds.
We let
P_{N}:=\{(i,j)\in I_{N},i\neq j\}
. As already seen,
\bar{X}^{i}
is
\sigma(\xi_{i},B^{i})
-measurable and
\bar{U}^{ij}
is
\sigma(\xi_{i},B^{i},\xi_{j},B^{j},\xi^{\dagger}_{ij},W^{ij})
-measurable. Using Theorem
8
and Assumption
1
,
we see that
all the random variables
\mathcal{Z}^{ij}_{t}=(\bar{X}^{i}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t},\bar{U}^{ij}_{t})
are
f_{t}
-distributed
and that for
C\subset P_{N}
, the family
(\mathcal{Z}^{ij}_{t},(i,j)\in C)
is i.i.d. as soon as
for all
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in C
distinct, the four numbers

i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}
are distinct. We set

\mu^{N}_{t}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{(i,j)\in P_{N}}\delta_{(X^{i,N}_{t},X^{j,N}_{t},U^{ij,N}_{t})}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{(i,j)\in P_{N}}\delta_{(\bar{X}^{i}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t},\bar{U}^{ij}_{t})}.

We write
{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu^{N}_{t},f_{t})\leq{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu^{N}_{t},\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t})+{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t},f_{t})
.
We classically have

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu^{N}_{t},\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t})]\leq\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{(i,j)\in P_{N}}\mathbb{E}[|X^{i,N}_{t}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}|+|X^{j,N}_{t}-\bar{X}^{j}_{t}|+|U^{ij,N}_{t}-\bar{U}^{ij}_{t}|]\leq\frac{C_{T}}{N^{\frac{1}{2d+1}}}

by Theorem
9
-(i) (since
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N^{-\frac{1}{2d+1}}
) or Theorem
9
-(ii)
(since
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp(C_{T}\sqrt{\log N})\leq C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2d+1}}
), using
Cauchy–Schwarz in the first case to pass from the
L^{2}
estimate to
L^{1}
.

We next write
P_{N}=\bigsqcup_{k=0}^{2N-1}P_{N}^{k}
,
where, for
k=0,\dots,N-1
,

P_{N}^{k}=\{(i,j)\in I_{N}:i+j=k\!\!\mod N,i<j\}\;\;\text{and}\;\;P_{N}^{N+k}=\{(i,j)\in I_{N}:i+j=k\!\!\mod N,i>j\}.

Easy considerations show that for all
k=0,\dots,2N-1
, all
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in P_{N}^{k}
with
(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
,
the four numbers
i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}
are distinct, so that the family
(\mathcal{Z}^{ij}_{t},(i,j)\in P_{N}^{k})
is i.i.d.
Moreover,

\#(P_{N}^{k})=\begin{cases}\frac{N-1}{2}&\text{if $N$ is odd},\\
\frac{N}{2}&\text{if $N$ is even and $k$ is odd},\\
\frac{N}{2}-1&\text{if $N$ is even and $k$ is even}.\end{cases}

Assuming now that
N\geq 3
is odd, we have

\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t}=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{2N-1}\bar{\mu}^{N,k}_{t},\quad\text{where}\quad\bar{\mu}^{N,k}_{t}=\frac{2}{N-1}\sum_{(i,j)\in P_{N}^{k}}\delta_{(\bar{X}^{i}_{t},\bar{X}^{j}_{t},\bar{U}^{ij}_{t})}.

Using the joint convexity of
{\mathcal{W}}_{1}
in its measure arguments, we have

{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t},f_{t})\leq\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{2N-1}{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\bar{\mu}^{N,k}_{t},f_{t}).

For each
k=0,\dots,2N-1
,
\bar{\mu}^{N,k}_{t}
is the empirical measure of
\frac{N-1}{2}
i.i.d. random variables
with law
f_{t}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}})
. Using the defining equations,
Assumption
2
, the standing alternative in the theorem, and the boundedness
of the coefficients, one easily checks that for all
T>0
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|\bar{X}^{1}_{t}|^{2}+|\bar{X}^{2}_{t}|^{2}+|\bar{U}^{12}_{t}|^{2}]<\infty.

By
[
12
, Theorem 1]
with
(p,d,q)
replaced by
(1,2d+1,2)
, for all
t\in[0,T]
, all
k=0,\dots,2N-1
,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\bar{\mu}^{N,k}_{t},f_{t})]\leq C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2d+1}}.

All this shows that if
N\geq 3
is odd,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t},f_{t})]\leq C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2d+1}},\quad\text{whence}\quad\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu^{N}_{t},f_{t})]\leq C_{T}N^{-\frac{1}{2d+1}}.

The case where
N\geq 4
is even is treated similarly, with light complications.
Taking the supremum over
t\in[0,T]
gives the claim.
∎

3.
Measurability

We first prove Lemma
5
, using arguments found in Karandikar
[
26
]
.

Proof of Lemma
5
.

We set
{\mathcal{X}}=E\times{\mathcal{C}}_{d}\times E\times{\mathcal{C}}_{d}\times E^{\dagger}\times{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
.

Step 1: Euler scheme.

Fix
n\geq 1
.
For
(a,x,b,y,c,w)\in{\mathcal{X}}
, we denote by

(\Gamma^{n}_{t}(a,x,b,y,c,w))_{t\geq 0}
the Euler scheme with step
2^{-n}
for the
equation
u_{t}=G(a,b,c)+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(x_{s},y_{s},u_{s}){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(x_{s},y_{s},u_{s}){\mathrm{d}}w_{s}
:
we set
q_{0}=G(a,b,c)
and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle q_{(k+1)2^{-n}}=q_{k2^{-n}}+2^{-n}\alpha(x_{k2^{-n}},y_{k2^{-n}},q_{k2^{-n}})+(w_{(k+1)2^{-n}}-w_{k2^{-n}})\beta(x_{k2^{-n}},y_{k2^{-n}},q_{k2^{-n}}).

Finally, for all
t\geq 0
, choose
k=\lfloor 2^{n}t\rfloor
and set

\Gamma^{n}_{t}(a,x,b,y,c,w)=q_{k2^{-n}}+\Big(t-k2^{-n}\Big)\alpha(x_{k2^{-n}},y_{k2^{-n}},q_{k2^{-n}})+(w_{t}-w_{k2^{-n}})\beta(x_{k2^{-n}},y_{k2^{-n}},q_{k2^{-n}}).

The map
\Gamma^{n}:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is measurable, because it is a continuous function of
(G(a,b,c),x,y,w)
.
By construction, it holds that for all
(a,x,b,y,c,w)\in{\mathcal{X}}
, all
t\geq 0
,

(22)

\Gamma^{n}_{t}(a,x,b,y,c,w)=\Gamma^{n}_{t}(a,(x_{s\land t})_{s\geq 0},b,y,c,w).

Step 2: Convergence to the edge SDE.

Here we prove that for any space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
, any independent

{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
-measurable triple
(\xi,\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\dagger})
with
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\sim\pi
and
\xi^{\dagger}\sim\pi^{\dagger}
,
any pair of continuous
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-adapted processes
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
valued in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

both satisfying (
3
),
any
1
-dimensional
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-Brownian motion

W=(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, the pathwise unique solution
U=(U_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to (
4
)
satisfies

(23)

\text{for all $T>0$,}\quad\lim_{n}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|U_{t}-\Gamma^{n}_{t}(\xi,X,\xi^{\prime},Y,\xi^{\dagger},W)|^{2}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are bounded, there is a constant
C>0
such that
for all
0\leq s<t<s+1
,

(24)

\mathbb{E}[|U_{t}-U_{s}|^{2}]\leq C(t-s).

We next set
U^{n}_{t}=\Gamma^{n}_{t}(\xi,X,\xi^{\prime},Y,\xi^{\dagger},W)
,
which classically satisfies, introducing
\rho_{n}(t)=2^{-n}\lfloor 2^{n}t\rfloor
,

U_{t}^{n}=G(\xi,\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\dagger})+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(X_{\rho_{n}(s)},Y_{\rho_{n}(s)},U^{n}_{\rho_{n}(s)}){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(X_{\rho_{n}(s)},Y_{\rho_{n}(s)},U^{n}_{\rho_{n}(s)}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}.

Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are bounded and Lipschitz continuous, we have, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|U_{s}-U^{n}_{s}|^{2}\Big]\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[|X_{s}-X_{\rho_{n}(s)}|^{2}+|Y_{s}-Y_{\rho_{n}(s)}|^{2}+|U_{s}-U^{n}_{\rho_{n}(s)}|^{2}\Big]{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}\varepsilon_{T,n}+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{u\in[0,s]}|U_{u}-U^{n}_{u}|^{2}\Big]{\mathrm{d}}s,

where

\varepsilon_{T,n}:=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[|X_{s}-X_{\rho_{n}(s)}|^{2}+|Y_{s}-Y_{\rho_{n}(s)}|^{2}+|U_{s}-U_{\rho_{n}(s)}|^{2}\Big]{\mathrm{d}}s\leq C_{T}2^{-n}

by (
24
), since
X
and
Y
satisfy (
3
) and since

0\leq s-\rho_{n}(s)\leq 2^{-n}
for all
s\geq 0
. By Gronwall’s lemma,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|U_{s}-U^{n}_{s}|^{2}\Big]\leq C_{T}2^{-n}.

Thus the series
\sum_{n\geq 1}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|U_{s}-U^{n}_{s}|^{2}
a.s. converges, whence
\lim_{n}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|U_{s}-U^{n}_{s}|^{2}=0
a.s.
Taking the intersection of the corresponding probability-one events over integer

T\geq 1
, we also have
U^{n}\to U
in
({\mathcal{C}}_{1},\delta)
a.s.
where
\delta(z,z^{\prime})=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}2^{-\ell}[1\land\sup_{t\in[0,\ell]}|z(t)-z^{\prime}(t)|]
classically metrizes the
topology of uniform convergence on compact time intervals.

Step 3: Measurability of the limit map.

Since
({\mathcal{C}}_{1},\delta)
is complete, the set

{\mathcal{A}}=\{(a,x,b,y,c,w)\in{\mathcal{X}}:\lim_{n}\Gamma^{n}(a,x,b,y,c,w)\text{ exists in }({\mathcal{C}}_{1},\delta)\}

is measurable.
We then set

\Gamma(a,x,b,y,c,w)=\lim_{n}{\bf 1}_{\{(a,x,b,y,c,w)\in{\mathcal{A}}\}}\Gamma^{n}(a,x,b,y,c,w)

for all
(a,x,b,y,c,w)\in{\mathcal{X}}
. This map
\Gamma:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is measurable.

Step 4: Identification with the SDE.

Consider a space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
, a pair of independent

\pi
-distributed
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
-measurable random variables
\xi,\xi^{\prime}
valued in

E
, an
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
-measurable
E^{\dagger}
-valued
random variable
\xi^{\dagger}
independent of
(\xi,\xi^{\prime})
and with law

\pi^{\dagger}
, a pair of continuous
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-adapted processes

X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
valued in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
both satisfying (
3
),
a
1
-dimensional
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-Brownian motion

W=(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and the pathwise unique solution
U=(U_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to (
4
).
Consider the event
A=\{(\xi,X,\xi^{\prime},Y,\xi^{\dagger},W)\in{\mathcal{A}}\}
, which has probability
1
by Step 2.
On

U=\Gamma(\xi,X,\xi^{\prime},Y,\xi^{\dagger},W)
(in the sense that for all

.

Step 5: Non-anticipativity.

With the same notation as in Step 4, on

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}(\xi,X,\xi^{\prime},Y,\xi^{\dagger},W)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\Gamma_{t}^{n}(\xi,X,\xi^{\prime},Y,\xi^{\dagger},W)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\Gamma_{t}^{n}(\xi,(X_{s\land t})_{s\geq 0},\xi^{\prime},Y,\xi^{\dagger},W)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{t}(\xi,(X_{s\land t})_{s\geq 0},\xi^{\prime},Y,\xi^{\dagger},W)

). The last equality is legitimate because

This implies (

).
∎
