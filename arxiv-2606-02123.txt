Applications of a formula of Maesaka-Seki-Watanabe type for multiple harmonic q q -sums
Yoshihiro Takeyama, Yuto Tsuruta

Abstract
. Maesaka, Seki and Watanabe proved a formula for multiple harmonic sums. Yamamoto generalized it to Schur-type multiple harmonic sums, and the second author proved a q q -analogue of this generalization. In this paper, we give two applications of the q q -analogue formula. The first is an alternative proof of the duality of a q q -analogue of multiple zeta values. The second is a proof of an identity for a q q -analogue of the Kawashima function.



1.
Introduction

We call a tuple of positive integers an
index
.
An index
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
is said to be
admissible
if

k_{r}\geq 2
.
For an admissible index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
,

multiple zeta value
(MZV for short)

\zeta(\bm{k})
is defined by

(1)

\displaystyle\zeta(\bm{k})=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

In
[
3
]
,
Maesaka, Seki and Watanabe obtained an interesting formula,
which we call the MSW formula in this paper,
for the truncated sum

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}(\bm{k})=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}<N}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
3
, Theorem 1.3]

For an index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and
a positive integer
N
, we set

(2)

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}^{\flat}(\bm{k})=\sum\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{N-n_{j,1}}\prod_{l=2}^{k_{j}}\frac{1}{n_{j,l}}\right),

where the sum is taken over all integers

n_{j,l}\,(1\leq j\leq r,\,1\leq l\leq k_{j})
satisfying

(3)

\displaystyle 0<n_{j,1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{j,k_{j}}<N\quad(1\leq j\leq r),\qquad n_{j,k_{j}}<n_{(j+1),1}\quad(1\leq j<r).

Then, for any index
\bm{k}
and any positive integer
N
,
it holds that

(4)

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}(\bm{k})=\zeta_{<N}^{\flat}(\bm{k}).

For example,
\zeta^{\flat}_{<N}(1,2,3)
is written as

(5)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\flat}_{<N}(1,2,3)=\sum_{0<n_{1}<n_{2}\leq n_{3}<n_{4}\leq n_{5}\leq n_{6}<N}\frac{1}{(N-n_{1})(N-n_{2})n_{3}(N-n_{4})n_{5}n_{6}}.

In the limit as
N\to\infty
,
the sum converges to the multiple integral

\displaystyle\idotsint\limits_{0<t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}<t_{4}<t_{5}<t_{6}<1}\frac{\mathrm{d}t_{1}}{1-t_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t_{2}\mathrm{d}t_{3}}{(1-t_{2})t_{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t_{4}\mathrm{d}t_{5}\mathrm{d}t_{6}}{(1-t_{4})t_{5}t_{6}},

which is the iterated integral representation of
the MZV
\zeta(1,2,3)
.
In this sense, the MSW formula (
4
) gives
a finite discretization of the iterated integral representation of MZVs.

In
[
9
]
,
Yamamoto generalized the MSW formula (
4
) to
Schur-type multiple harmonic sums,
which may be viewed as a finite discretization
of the integral formula for Schur multiple zeta values
established by Hirose, Murahara and Onozuka
[
2
]
.
It contains the star-version of the MSW formula:

Theorem 1.2
.

[
9
, Theorem 2.1]

For an index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})

and a positive integer
N
, we set

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}^{\star}(\bm{k})=\sum_{0<m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{r}<N}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}

and

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}^{\star\flat}(\bm{k})=\sum\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{N-n_{j,1}}\prod_{l=2}^{k_{j}}\frac{1}{n_{j,l}}\right),

where the sum is taken over all integers

n_{j,l}\,(1\leq j\leq r,\,1\leq l\leq k_{j})

satisfying

(6)

\displaystyle 0<n_{j,1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{j,k_{j}}<N\quad(1\leq j\leq r),\qquad n_{j,k_{j}}\geq n_{j-1,1}\quad(1<j\leq r).

Then, for any index
\bm{k}
and any positive integer
N
, it holds that

(7)

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}^{\star}(\bm{k})=\zeta_{<N}^{\star\flat}(\bm{k}).

In
[
8
]
,
the second author obtained
a
q
-analogue of Yamamoto’s generalization.
It contains a
q
-analogue of
(
4
) and (
7
)
given as follows.

We assume throughout this paper that

q
is a constant satisfying

\displaystyle 0<q<1.

For an integer
m
, the
q
-integer
[m]
is defined by

[m]=(1-q^{m})/(1-q)
.

For an index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, we set

(8)

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}<N}\frac{q^{(k_{1}-1)m_{1}+\cdots+(k_{r}-1)m_{r}}}{[m_{1}]^{k_{1}}\cdots[m_{r}]^{k_{r}}}

and define its star-version

\zeta_{<N}^{\star,q}(\bm{k})

as (
8
) with the summation range
replaced by
0<m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{r}<N
.
Note that
\zeta_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})
is a truncated sum of
the Bradley-Zhao model of
a
q
-analogue of MZV (
q
MZV for short) defined by

(9)

\displaystyle\zeta^{q}(\bm{k})=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{q^{(k_{1}-1)m_{1}+\cdots+(k_{r}-1)m_{r}}}{[m_{1}]^{k_{1}}\cdots[m_{r}]^{k_{r}}}

for an admissible index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
8
, Special cases of Theorem 1.2]

For an index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and
a positive integer
N
, we set

(10)

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}^{q\flat}(\bm{k})=\sum\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{[N-n_{j,1}]}\prod_{l=2}^{k_{j}}\frac{q^{n_{j,l}}}{[n_{j,l}]}\right),

where the sum is taken over all integers

n_{j,l}\,(1\leq j\leq r,\,1\leq l\leq k_{j})
satisfying
(
3
).
We also define its star-version
\zeta_{<N}^{\star,q\flat}(\bm{k})

as (
10
) with the summation range replaced by
(
6
).
Then, for any index
\bm{k}
and
any positive integer
N
, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\zeta^{q}_{<N}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\zeta^{q\flat}_{<N}(\bm{k}),

(12)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star,q}_{<N}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\zeta^{\star,q\flat}_{<N}(\bm{k}).

The purpose of this paper is to discuss
two applications of the formulas
(
11
) and (
12
).
The first is an alternative proof for the duality
of
q
MZVs (
9
)
using the formula (
11
).
The second is a proof of an identity
of a
q
-analogue of the Kawashima function
using the formula (
12
).

In Section
2
,
we present a new proof of the duality of
q
MZVs.

Let
\bm{k}
be an admissible index.
There uniquely exist positive integers
s
and

a_{1},\ldots,a_{s},b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}

such that

(13)

\displaystyle\bm{k}=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{a_{1}-1},b_{1}+1,\ldots,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{a_{s}-1},b_{s}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+1).

Then the dual index
\bm{k}^{\dagger}
of
\bm{k}
is defined by

\displaystyle\bm{k}^{\dagger}=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{b_{s}-1},a_{s}+1,\ldots,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{b_{1}-1},a_{1}+1).

Theorem 1.4

(Duality of
q
MZVs,
[
1
]
)
.

For any admissible index
\bm{k}
,
it holds that
\zeta^{q}(\bm{k})=\zeta^{q}(\bm{k}^{\dagger})
.

By taking the limit as
q\to 1
, we recover the duality

\zeta(\bm{k})=\zeta(\bm{k}^{\dagger})
of MZVs.

The paper
[
3
]
provides
a proof of the duality of MZVs by means of the MSW formula.
It is proved that,
for any admissible index
\bm{k}
, there exists
J>0
such that

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}(\bm{k})-\zeta_{<N}(\bm{k}^{\dagger})=N^{-1}O((\log{N})^{J})\qquad(N\to\infty).

By taking the limit as
N\to\infty
,
we obtain the duality

\zeta(\bm{k})=\zeta(\bm{k}^{\dagger})
.

In contrast to the MZV case,
our proof of the duality of
q
MZVs
proceeds as follows.
Using the formula (
11
),
one can obtain,
for any admissible index
\bm{k}
,
a finite sum
\phi_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})

such that

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})=\zeta_{<N}^{q\flat}(\bm{k})=\phi_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})+q^{N}O(N^{J})

with some
J>0
and

(14)

\displaystyle\phi_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})\to\zeta^{q}(\bm{k}^{\dagger})

as
N\to\infty
.
The duality is then derived from this.
To show (
14
),
we employ the same technique as was used to prove
the resummation identity given in
[
7
]
.

In Section
3
,
we prove an identity of a
q
-analogue of the Kawashima function.

In
[
4
]
, Kawashima introduced a function

F_{\bm{k}}(z)
for an index
\bm{k}
, which we call the Kawashima function.
The function
F_{\bm{k}}(z)
interpolates the truncated sum

\zeta_{<N}(\bm{k})
as

F_{\bm{k}}(N-1)=\zeta_{<N}(\bm{k})

for any positive integer
N
.
In
[
5
]
, Kawashima obtained an alternative expression
G_{\bm{k}}(z)

of the Kawashima function such that

(15)

\displaystyle F_{\bm{k}}(z)=G_{\overleftarrow{\bm{k}}}(z)

in the region
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{z}>-1
,
where
\overleftarrow{\bm{k}}:=(k_{r},k_{r-1},\ldots,k_{1})
for

\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
.
The key to the proof of (
15
)
is the identity

(16)

\displaystyle F_{\bm{k}}(N-1)=G_{\overleftarrow{\bm{k}}}(N-1)

for any positive integer
N
.
In
[
9
]
, Yamamoto pointed out that
the identity (
16
) is nothing but the star-version
(
7
) of the MSW formula.

In
[
6
]
, the first author defined a
q
-analogue

F_{\bm{k}}^{q}(z)
of the Kawashima function.
In Section
3
, we introduce a
q
-analogue
G_{\bm{k}}^{q}(z)

of the function
G_{\bm{k}}(z)

and show the identity
F_{\bm{k}}^{q}(z)=G_{\overleftarrow{\bm{k}}}^{q}(z)

on the disk
|z|<q^{-1}
,
which follows from the formula (
12
) and
the identity theorem for analytic functions.

Throughout this paper we set

|\bm{k}|=\sum_{a=1}^{r}k_{a}

for an index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
.

2.
A new proof of duality of
q
MZVs

In this section, we give an alternative proof of
Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For an index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and a positive integer
N
,
we set

\displaystyle\phi_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})=(1-q)^{r}\sum\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\prod_{l=2}^{k_{j}}\frac{q^{n_{j,l}}}{[n_{j,l}]}\right),

where the sum is taken over all integers
n_{j,l}\,(1\leq j\leq r,\,1\leq l\leq k_{j})

satisfying (
3
).
Then, for any admissible index
\bm{k}
, it holds that

(17)

\displaystyle\zeta_{<N}^{q\flat}(\bm{k})=\phi_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})+q^{N}O(N^{J})\qquad(N\to\infty)

for some
J>0
independent of
N
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
is admissible.
Note that
k_{r}\geq 2
.
Since
0<q<1
, we have
1/[m]\leq 1
for any positive integer
m
.
Therefore, if
0<n_{j,1}\leq n_{r,k_{r}}<N
, it holds that

(18)

\displaystyle 0<\frac{1}{[N-n_{j,1}]}\frac{q^{n_{r,k_{r}}}}{[n_{r,k_{r}}]}=\left(1-q+\frac{q^{N-n_{j,1}}}{[N-n_{j,1}]}\right)\frac{q^{n_{r,k_{r}}}}{[n_{r,k_{r}}]}\leq(1-q)\frac{q^{n_{r,k_{r}}}}{[n_{r,k_{r}}]}+q^{N}.

Applying (
18
) to
\zeta^{q\flat}_{<N}(\bm{k})

for
1\leq j\leq r
, we obtain (
17
).
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
a,b\geq 1
and
c,d\geq 0
.
It holds that

(19)

\displaystyle(1-q)^{a}\sum_{d<n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{b}}\binom{n_{1}-d}{a}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{b-1}\frac{q^{n_{j}}}{[n_{j}]}\right)\frac{q^{n_{b}}}{[n_{b}]}q^{cn_{b}}=\sum_{c<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{b}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{b-1}\frac{1}{[m_{j}]}\right)\frac{q^{am_{b}}}{[m_{b}]^{a+1}}q^{dm_{b}}.

Proof.

We denote the left-hand side by
I
.
We have

\displaystyle I=(1-q)^{a+b}\sum_{d\leq n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{b}}\sum_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{b}\geq 1}\binom{n_{1}-d}{a}q^{\sum_{j=1}^{b}n_{j}l_{j}+cn_{b}}

because
\binom{n_{1}-d}{a}=0
if
n_{1}=d
.
We perform the change of summation variables

(n_{1},\ldots,n_{b},l_{1},\ldots,l_{b})\mapsto(s_{1},\ldots,s_{b},m_{1},\ldots,m_{b})

defined by

\displaystyle s_{1}=n_{1}-d,\qquad s_{j}=n_{j}-n_{j-1}\quad(2\leq j\leq b)

and

\displaystyle m_{j}=c+\sum_{i=b-j+1}^{b}l_{i}\quad(1\leq j\leq b).

The sum is then taken over integers
s_{j}
and
m_{j}\,(1\leq j\leq b)

satisfying
s_{1},\ldots,s_{b}\geq 0
and

c<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{b}
.
It holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{b}n_{j}l_{j}+cn_{b}=\sum_{j=1}^{b}s_{j}m_{b-j+1}+dm_{b}.

We take the sum over
s_{1},\ldots,s_{b}\geq 0
.
By using

\displaystyle\sum_{s\geq 0}\binom{s}{a}x^{s}=\frac{x^{a}}{(1-x)^{a+1}}\qquad(|x|<1),

we see that

\displaystyle I=(1-q)^{a+b}\sum_{c<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{b}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{b-1}\frac{1}{1-q^{m_{j}}}\right)\frac{q^{am_{b}}}{(1-q^{m_{b}})^{a+1}}\,q^{dm_{b}},

which is equal to the right-hand side of (
19
).
∎

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\bm{k}
be an admissible index.
We define positive integers
a_{j},b_{j}\,(1\leq j\leq s)

by (

From the definition of
\phi_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})

we see that

(20)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\phi_{<N}^{q}(\bm{k})=(1-q)^{\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}}\sum\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left(\binom{n_{j,1}-n_{j-1,b_{j-1}}}{a_{j}}\prod_{l=1}^{b_{j}}\frac{q^{n_{j,l}}}{[n_{j,l}]}\right),

where

and the sum is taken over
all integers
n_{j,l}\,(1\leq j\leq s,\,1\leq l\leq b_{j})

satisfying

\displaystyle 0<n_{1,1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{1,b_{1}}<n_{2,1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{2,b_{2}}<\cdots<n_{s,1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{s,b_{s}}.

By using (
19

we see that

∎

3.
An identity of a
q
-analogue of Kawashima function

For an index
\bm{k}
, we define its Hoffman dual
\bm{k}^{\vee}
as follows.
We write
\bm{k}
in the form

\bm{k}=(1\square 1\square\cdots\square 1)
,
where
\square
is either
+
(plus symbol) or
,
(comma).
Then
\bm{k}^{\vee}
is the index obtained by changing
+
to
,
and
vice versa.
For example, if
\bm{k}=(2,1,3,2)=(1+1,1,1+1+1,1+1)
,
then
\bm{k}^{\vee}=(1,1+1+1,1,1+1,1)=(1,3,1,2,1)
.

Let
\bm{k}
be an index.
We set
\bm{k}^{\vee}=(k_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,k_{s}^{\prime})

and define the function
F_{\bm{k}}^{q}(z)
, which is a
q
-analogue
of the Kawashima function, by

(21)

\displaystyle F_{\bm{k}}^{q}(z)=-\sum_{0<m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{s}}\left(\prod_{a=1}^{s-1}\frac{q^{(k_{a}^{\prime}-1)m_{a}}}{[m_{a}]^{k_{a}^{\prime}}}\right)\frac{q^{k_{s}^{\prime}m_{s}}}{[m_{s}]^{k_{s}^{\prime}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{s}}\frac{z-q^{j-1}}{1-q^{j}}.

Proposition 3.1
.

The infinite sum in (
21
) converges absolutely and
defines an analytic function in the region
|z|<q^{-1}
.

Proof.

Fix a constant
c
with
0<c<q^{-1}
.
Suppose that
|z|\leq c
.
Set
d=q(1-qc)/(1+q)
.
Note that
0<d<1
.
We take a positive integer
M
such that

q^{M}<d
.
Then, if
j>M
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{z-q^{j-1}}{1-q^{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{z-q^{j-1}}{1-q^{j}}-z\right|+|z|=\frac{q^{j-1}}{1-q^{j}}|1-zq|+|z|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{M}}{1-q^{M}}(1+c)+c\leq\frac{c+d}{1-d}.

For
1\leq j\leq M
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{z-q^{j-1}}{1-q^{j}}\right|\leq\frac{c+1}{1-q}.

For any positive integer
m
, we have
1/[m]\leq 1
.
Therefore, if
0<m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{s}
and
m_{s}>M
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\left(\prod_{a=1}^{s-1}\frac{q^{(k_{a}^{\prime}-1)m_{a}}}{[m_{a}]^{k_{a}^{\prime}}}\right)\frac{q^{k_{s}^{\prime}m_{s}}}{[m_{s}]^{k_{s}^{\prime}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{s}}\frac{z-q^{j-1}}{1-q^{j}}\right|\leq q^{m_{s}}\left(\frac{c+1}{1-q}\right)^{M}\left(\frac{c+d}{1-d}\right)^{m_{s}-M}

because
k_{s}^{\prime}\geq 1
.
From the definition of
d
and
0<c<q^{-1}
, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{c+d}{1-d}=(1+q)\frac{1+c}{1+q^{2}c}-1<q^{-1}.

Hence, we can apply the Weierstrass
M
-test to
the series in (
21
).
∎

The function
F_{\bm{k}}^{q}(z)
has the following property.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
6
]

For any index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and
any positive integer
N
, it holds that

\displaystyle F_{\bm{k}}^{q}(q^{N-1})=\sum_{0<m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{r}<N}\frac{q^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}}}{[m_{1}]^{k_{1}}\cdots[m_{r}]^{k_{r}}}.

For an integer
m
, we set

\displaystyle[m+\log_{q}{z}]=\frac{1-zq^{m}}{1-q}.

Note that, if
z=q^{N}
for an integer
N
,
we have
[m+\log_{q}{z}]=[m+N]
.

For an index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
,
we define the function
G_{\bm{k}}^{q}(z)
by

(22)

\displaystyle G^{q}_{\bm{k}}(z)=\sum

\displaystyle\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{k_{a}-1}\frac{1}{[m_{j,l}+\log_{q}{z}]}\,\right)\frac{1}{[m_{j,k_{j}}]}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\left(\prod_{l=1}^{k_{r}-1}\frac{1}{[m_{r,l}+\log_{q}{z}]}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m_{r,k_{r}}}}{[m_{r,k_{r}}]}-\frac{q^{m_{r,k_{r}}}z}{[m_{r,k_{r}}+\log_{q}{z}]}\right),

where the sum is over the region

\displaystyle 0<m_{1,1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{1,k_{1}}<m_{2,1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{2,k_{2}}<\cdots<m_{r,1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{r,k{r}}.

Proposition 3.3
.

The infinite sum in (

) converges absolutely and

Proof.

Let
c
be a constant satisfying

.
Suppose that

For any positive integer

Therefore, the Weierstrass

-test applies to the series

We prove the following lemma,

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

For any positive integers

the following identities hold.

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[m^{\prime}+N-1]}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=m^{\prime}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{q^{m+N-1-n^{\prime\prime}}}{[m+N-1-n^{\prime\prime}]}-\frac{q^{m+N-n^{\prime}}}{[m+N-n^{\prime}]}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=n^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime\prime}}\left(\frac{q^{m^{\prime}+N-1-n}}{[m^{\prime}+N-1-n]}-\frac{q^{m^{\prime}+N-1}}{[m^{\prime}+N-1]}\right)\frac{1}{[n]},

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[m^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=m^{\prime}+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{q^{m+N-1-n^{\prime\prime}}}{[m+N-1-n^{\prime\prime}]}-\frac{q^{m+N-n^{\prime}}}{[m+N-n^{\prime}]}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=n^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime\prime}}\left(\frac{q^{m^{\prime}}}{[m^{\prime}]}-\frac{q^{m^{\prime}+N-n}}{[m^{\prime}+N-n]}\right)\frac{q^{N-n}}{[N-n]}.

Proof.

Here we prove (
23
).
The proof of (
24
) is similar.
Since the sum
\sum_{m\geq 1}q^{m}/[m]
converges and
n^{\prime}\leq n^{\prime\prime}
,
we see that the left-hand side is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[m^{\prime}+N-1]}\sum_{n=n^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime\prime}}\frac{q^{m^{\prime}+N-n-1}}{[m^{\prime}+N-n-1]}.

Using the partial fraction decomposition

\displaystyle\frac{q^{m^{\prime}+N-n-1}}{[m^{\prime}+N-1][m^{\prime}+N-n-1]}=\left(\frac{q^{m^{\prime}+N-1-n}}{[m^{\prime}+N-1-n]}-\frac{q^{m^{\prime}+N-1}}{[m^{\prime}+N-1]}\right)\frac{1}{[n]},

we obtain (
23
).
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

For any index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and
any positive integer
N
, we have

(25)

\displaystyle G^{q}_{\overset{\leftarrow}{\bm{k}}}(q^{N-1})=\sum\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{q^{N-n_{j,1}}}{[N-n_{j,1}]}\prod_{l=2}^{k_{j}}\frac{1}{[n_{j,l}]}\right),

where the sum is taken over all integers

n_{j,l}\,(1\leq j\leq r,\,1\leq l\leq k_{j})
satisfying
(
6
).

Proof.

We prove the case
\bm{k}=(2,3)
as an illustration.
The general case follows similarly.
We have

(26)

\displaystyle G^{q}_{(3,2)}(q^{N-1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq m_{3}\\
<m_{4}\leq m_{5}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[m_{1}+N-1][m_{2}+N-1][m_{3}][m_{4}+N-1]}

(27)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{q^{m_{5}}}{[m_{5}]}-\frac{q^{m_{5}+N-1}}{[m_{5}+N-1]}\right).

Applying (
23
) to the sum over
m_{5}
, we obtain

(28)

\displaystyle G^{q}_{(3,2)}(q^{N-1})=\sum_{0<n_{5}<N}\frac{1}{[n_{5}]}\sum_{0<m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq m_{3}<m_{4}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[m_{1}+N-1][m_{2}+N-1][m_{3}]}

(29)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{q^{m_{4}+N-1-n_{5}}}{[m_{4}+N-1-n_{5}]}-\frac{q^{m_{4}+N-1}}{[m_{4}+N-1]}\right).

Using (
24
) to the sum over
m_{4}
, we get

(30)

\displaystyle G^{q}_{(3,2)}(q^{N-1})=

\displaystyle\sum_{0<n_{4}\leq n_{5}<N}\frac{q^{N-n_{4}}}{[N-n_{4}][n_{5}]}

(31)

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\sum_{0<m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq m_{3}}\frac{1}{[m_{1}+N-1][m_{2}+N-1]}\left(\frac{q^{m_{3}}}{[m_{3}]}-\frac{q^{m_{3}+N-n_{4}}}{[m_{3}+N-n_{4}]}\right).

Applying (
23
) twice and then (
24
),
we see that the right-hand side is equal to

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<n_{3}<N,\,0<n_{4}\leq n_{5}<N\\
n_{3}\geq n_{4}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[n_{3}]}\frac{q^{N-n_{4}}}{[N-n_{4}][n_{5}]}\sum_{0<m_{1}\leq m_{2}}\frac{1}{[m_{1}+N-1]}\left(\frac{q^{m_{2}+N-1-n_{3}}}{[m_{2}+N-1-n_{3}]}-\frac{q^{m_{2}+N-1}}{[m_{2}+N-1]}\right)

(33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<n_{2}\leq n_{3}<N,\,0<n_{4}\leq n_{5}<N\\
n_{3}\geq n_{4}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[n_{2}][n_{3}]}\frac{q^{N-n_{4}}}{[N-n_{4}][n_{5}]}\sum_{0<m_{1}}\left(\frac{q^{m_{1}+N-1-n_{2}}}{[m_{1}+N-1-n_{2}]}-\frac{q^{m_{1}+N-1}}{[m_{1}+N-1]}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq n_{3},\,0<n_{4}\leq n_{5}<N\\
n_{3}\geq n_{4}\end{subarray}}\frac{q^{N-n_{1}}}{[N-n_{1}][n_{2}][n_{3}]}\frac{q^{N-n_{4}}}{[N-n_{4}][n_{5}]}.

It is equal to the right-hand side of (
25
) with
\bm{k}=(2,3)
.
∎

We now prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.6
.

For any index
\bm{k}
, it holds that

(35)

\displaystyle F_{\bm{k}}(z)=G_{\overleftarrow{\bm{k}}}(z)

in the region
|z|<q^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
N
be a positive integer.
Note that replacing
q
by
q^{-1}
sends
[m]
to

q^{1-m}[m]
for any integer
m
.
From Proposition
3.2
, we see that

\displaystyle q^{-|\bm{k}|}\,F_{\bm{k}}^{q}(q^{N-1})=\zeta_{<N}^{\star,q}(\bm{k})\bigg|_{q\to q^{-1}}.

Similarly, from Proposition
3.5
, we find that

\displaystyle q^{-|\bm{k}|}\,G_{\overleftarrow{\bm{k}}}^{q}(q^{N-1})=\zeta_{<N}^{\star,q\flat}(\bm{k})\bigg|_{q\to q^{-1}}.

Since the formula (
12
) is an identity of
rational functions in
q
, it implies that

F_{\bm{k}}^{q}(q^{N-1})=G_{\overleftarrow{\bm{k}}}^{q}(q^{N-1})

for any positive integer
N
.
Now the desired equality follows from the identity theorem
for analytic functions.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

In the case
\bm{k}=(1)
,
the identity (
35
) can be proved directly
by means of the
q
-Gauss summation formula,
as follows.

We set
(z)_{m}=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-q^{j}z)
for
m\geq 0

and
(z)_{\infty}=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-q^{j}z)
.
It holds that

\displaystyle F_{(1)}^{q}(z)=-(1-q)\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-q^{m}}\frac{(z^{-1})_{m}}{(q)_{m}}(qz)^{m}.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-q^{m}}=\lim_{a\to 1}\frac{1}{1-a}\frac{(a)_{m}}{(aq)_{m}}

for
m\geq 1
,
we find that

\displaystyle F_{(1)}^{q}(z)=(1-q)\lim_{a\to 1}\frac{1}{a-1}\left({}_{2}\phi_{1}(a,z^{-1},aq;qz)-1\right),

where
{}_{2}\phi_{1}(a,b,c;z)
is the

q
-hypergeometric series

\displaystyle{}_{2}\phi_{1}(a,b,c;z)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{(a)_{m}(b)_{m}}{(c)_{m}(q)_{m}}z^{m}.

The
q
-Gauss summation formula

\displaystyle{}_{2}\phi_{1}(a,b,c;c/ab)=\frac{(c/a)_{\infty}(c/b)_{\infty}}{(c)_{\infty}(c/ab)_{\infty}}\qquad(|c/ab|<1)

implies that

\displaystyle F_{(1)}^{q}(z)=(1-q)\lim_{a\to 1}\frac{1}{a-1}\left(g(a;z)-1\right),

where

\displaystyle g(a;z)=\frac{(q)_{\infty}(aqz)_{\infty}}{(aq)_{\infty}(qz)_{\infty}}.

Since
g(1;z)=1
, we have

\displaystyle F_{(1)}^{q}(z)

\displaystyle=(1-q)\frac{\partial}{\partial a}g(a;z)\bigg|_{a=1}=(1-q)\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\left(\log{g(a;z)}\right)\bigg|_{a=1}

\displaystyle=(1-q)\sum_{m\geq 1}\left(\frac{q^{m}}{1-q^{m}}-\frac{q^{m}z}{1-q^{m}z}\right)=G_{(1)}^{q}(z).
