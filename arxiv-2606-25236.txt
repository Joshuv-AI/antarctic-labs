Notes on q-normed Spaces in Constructive Analysis
Douglas S. Bridges

Abstract
What we call q-normed (linear) spaces were introduced into constructive analysis, under the name pseudonormed spaces, in [ 10 ] , as a means of handling spaces, such as L ∞ L_{\infty} , in which not all elements are constructively normable. We prove a number of q-normed-space analogues/generalisations of standard theorems in the constructive analysis of normed linear spaces, and give examples showing that two natural analogues are essentially nonconstructive.

and therefore
there cannot exist
i\in I
with
\left\|x-y\right\|_{i}>\varepsilon/2
. Thus
\left\|x-y\right\|_{i}<\varepsilon
for all

i\in I
.

In the special case of a normed space
X
, the next proposition strengthens
the uniqueness conclusion in
[
4
, 2.3.7]
.

Proposition 8

If
u
is a nonzero normable linear functional on a complete,
uniformly convex q-normed space
\left(X,\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{i}\right)_{i\in I}\right)
, then there exists a normable element

x_{\infty}\in B_{X}
, with norm
1
, such that
u(x_{\infty})=\left\|u\right\|
. Moreover,
x_{\infty}
is
strongly unique
, in the
sense that if
x\in B_{X}
and
x\neq x_{\infty}
, then
u(x)<\left\|u\right\|
.

Proof.
Construct a sequence
\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
of elements of
B_{X}

such that
u(x_{n})\rightarrow\left\|u\right\|
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
\delta<3
be a uc-associate number for

\varepsilon/2
. There exists
N
such that
\left\|u\right\|-u(x_{n})<\frac{\delta}{3}\left\|u\right\|
for all
n\geq N
. By
Proposition
7
,
\left\|x_{m}-x_{n}\right\|_{i}<\varepsilon
for all
m,n\geq N
and all
i\in I
. Since
\varepsilon>0
is
arbitrary, we see that
\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}\
is a Cauchy sequence
in the complete q-normed space
X
and therefore converges to a limit

x_{\infty}\in X
. Then
\left\|x_{\infty}\right\|_{i}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{i}\leq 1
for each
i\in X
,
so
x_{\infty}\in B_{X}
. Moreover, since
u
is continuous,
u(x_{\infty})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u(x_{n})=\left\|u\right\|
. Finally, to
prove the strong uniqueness of
x_{\infty}
, let
x\in B_{X}
and
x\neq x_{\infty}
, choose
j\in I
such that
\left\|x-x_{\infty}\right\|_{j}>0
, and let
\alpha
be a uc-associate number for
\frac{1}{2}\left\|x-x_{\infty}\right\|_{j}
. By Proposition
7
, if
\left\|u\right\|-u(x)<\frac{\alpha}{3}\left\|u\right\|
, then
\left\|x-x_{\infty}\right\|_{i}<\left\|x-x_{\infty}\right\|_{j}
for all

i
, which is plainly absurd. Hence
\left\|u\right\|-u(x)>0
, so

u(x)<\left\|u\right\|
.

We say that the q-normed space
X
is

•

reflexive
if for each normable element
\phi
of
X^{\ast\ast}

there exists a (perforce unique)
x\in X
such that
\phi=\widehat{x}
;

•

pliant
if for each

x\in X
, each
i\in I
, and each

\varepsilon>0
, there exists
u\in B_{X^{\ast}}
such that
u(x)>\left\|x\right\|_{i}-\varepsilon
.

Classically, every normed space is pliant. Constructively, a nontrivial normed
space is pliant if it is either separable
[
4
, 5.3.1]
or else has
Gâteaux differentiable norm (see
[
9
, Lemma 1]
or

[
4
, 5.3.6]
). In particular, every Hilbert space, separable or
otherwise, has Gâteaux differentiable norm and so is reflexive.

Perhaps the most interesting result about uniformly convex q-normed spaces is
the constructive
Milman-Pettis theorem
.

Theorem 9

Every complete, pliant, uniformly convex q-normed space
X
is
reflexive, and the natural embedding into
X^{\ast\ast}
is a norm-preserving
bijection of the set of normable elements of
X
onto the set of normable
elements of
X^{\ast\ast}

[
3
, Theorem 3]
.

Total boundedness

Let
S
be a subset of our q-normed space
\left(X,\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{i}\right)_{i\in I}\right)
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, we call
a set
A\subset S
an
\varepsilon
-approximation
to
S
if for each

x\in S
there exists
x^{\prime}\in A
such that
\left\|x-x^{\prime}\right\|_{i}<\varepsilon
for all
i\in I
. We say that
S
is

•

bounded
if there exists
r>0
such that
\left\|x\right\|_{i}<r
for all
x\in S
and all
i\in I
;

•

totally bounded
if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
finitely enumerable
\varepsilon
-approximation to
S
.

Note that totally bounded implies bounded.

Proposition 10

If
S
is a totally bounded subset of the q-normed space
X
,
and
f
is a uniformly continuous mapping of
S
into the q-normed space
Y
,
then
f(S)
is totally bounded.

Proof.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, let
\delta
be a continuity associate for

\varepsilon/2
and
f
. Let
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\right\}
be a
finitely enumerable
\delta
-approximation to
S
. For each
x\in S
there
exists
k
such that
\left\|x-x_{k}\right\|_{i}<\delta
for all
i\in I
. If
\left\|f(x)-f(x_{k})\right\|_{j}>\varepsilon/2
for some
j\in J
, then
\left\|x-x_{k}\right\|_{i}>\delta
for some
i\in I
, which
is absurd. Hence
\left\|f(x)-f(x_{k})\right\|_{j}\leq\varepsilon/2<\varepsilon
for all
j\in J
. It follows that
\left\{f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{N})\right\}
is a finitely enumerable
\varepsilon
-approximation to
f(S)
.

Corollary 11

If
u
is a bounded linear mapping of the q-normed space
X

into the q-normed space
Y
, and
S\subset X
is totally bounded, then
u(S)

is totally bounded in
Y
.

Proof.
By
[
2
, Chapter 7, (5.6)]
,
u
is uniformly continuous on
X
and hence
on
S
. It remains to apply Proposition
10
.

Corollary 12

If
S
is a totally bounded subset of a q-normed space
X
, and

f
is a uniformly continuous mapping of
S
into
\mathbf{R}
, then

\sup_{x\in S}f(x)
and
\inf_{x\in S}f(x)
exist.

Proof.
This follows from Proposition
10
and
[
4
, Proposition
2.2.5]
.

Proposition 13

The following are equivalent conditions on a q-normed space

X.

(i)

Every element of
X
is normable.

(ii)

Every totally bounded subset of
X
is located.

Proof.
Suppose that (i) holds, and let
K\subset X
be totally bounded. Given

\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{R}
with
\alpha<\beta
, let
S=\left\{x_{1},\ldots.x_{n}\right\}
be a finitely enumerable
\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\alpha)
-approximation to
K
. For each
x\in X
, since
S
is located in
X
(by
Proposition
5
), either for each
k\leq n\
there exists
i_{k}

such that
\left\|x-x_{k}\right\|_{i_{k}}>\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+\beta)

or else there exists
k\leq n
such that
\left\|x-x_{k}\right\|_{i}<\beta
for all
i\in I
. In the former case, for each
y\in K
, choosing

k\leq n
such that
\left\|y-x_{k}\right\|_{i}<\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\alpha)
for all
i\in I
, we have

\left\|x-y\right\|_{i_{k}}\geq\left\|x-x_{k}\right\|_{i_{k}}-\left\|y-x_{k}\right\|_{i_{k}}>\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha+\beta)-\tfrac{1}{2}(\beta-\alpha)=\alpha.

Since
\alpha,\beta
, and
x
are arbitrary, it follows that that
K
is
located in
X
. Thus (i)
\Rightarrow\
(ii). Conversely, if (ii) holds, then
every singleton subset of
X
is located; whence (i) holds, by Proposition

5
.

Proposition 14

Let
K
be a totally bounded subset of the q-normed space
X
,
and let
S\subset K
be located in
K
. Then
S
is totally bounded.

Proof.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, construct a finitely enumerable
\varepsilon/3
-approximation
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\right\}
to
K
. Write

\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}
as a union of two sets
P
and
Q
, where

–

if
k\in P
, then there exists
s_{k}\in S
such that
\left\|x_{k}-s_{k}\right\|_{i}<2\varepsilon/3
for all
i\in I
, and

–

if
k\in Q
, then for each
s\in S
there exists
i\in I
such that

\left\|x_{k}-s\right\|_{i}>\varepsilon/3
.

Given
s\in S
, choose
k
such that
\left\|s-x_{k}\right\|_{i}<\varepsilon/3
for all
i\in I
. Then
k\notin Q
, so
k\in P
and
therefore

\left\|s-s_{k}\right\|_{i}\leq\left\|s-x_{k}\right\|_{i}+\left\|x_{k}-s_{k}\right\|_{i}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}=\varepsilon

for all
i\in I
. Thus
\left\{s_{k}:k\in P\right\}
is a finitely
enumerable
\varepsilon
-approximation to
S
.

Finite-dimensional q-normed spaces

First place on record this theorem from
[
5
, Theorem 20.5]
.

Theorem 15

Every
n
-dimensional locally convex space over
\mathbf{R}
is
homeomorphic to
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

In fact, the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 20.5]
shows that if
\left(X,\left(p_{i}\right)_{i\in I}\right)
is an
n
-dimensional real locally
convex space with basis
\left\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\right\}
, then the

canonical linear bijection

f_{X}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\rightsquigarrow\left(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\right)

(4)

of
X
onto
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is uniformly continuous, and has uniformly
continuous inverse, relative to the locally convex structures on
X
and

\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

Corollary 16

Let
\left(X,\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{i}\right)_{i\in I}\right)
be a
n
-dimensional real q-normed with basis
\left\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\right\}
. Then
f_{X}\
is bi-uniformly continuous
relative to the q-norms on the two spaces.

Proof.
Since
f_{X}
is bi-uniformly continuous in the locally-convex-space sense,
the result follows from Proposition
1
.

Proposition 17

Let
\left(X,\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{i}\right)_{i\in I}\right)
be a
n
-dimensional real q-normed with basis
\left\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\right\}
. Then for each
j\leq n
the
j^{\mathbf{th}}
coordinate functional

u_{j}:\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}b_{k}\rightsquigarrow\lambda_{j}\ \ (\mathbf{\lambda}\in\mathbf{R}^{n})

is q-norm uniformly continuous on
X
.

Proof.
The function
u_{j}
is the composition of the q-norm uniformly continuous
mappings
f_{X}
on
X\
and
\mathbf{\lambda}\rightsquigarrow\lambda_{j}
on

\mathbf{R}^{n})
.

We say that a q-normed space
\left(X,\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{i}\right)_{i\in I}\right)
is

–

locally totally bounded
if every bounded subset of
X
is
contained in some totally bounded set, and

–

locally compact
if every bounded subset of
X
is contained
in some
compact
—that is, totally bounded and q-complete—set.

Proposition 18

Every finite-dimensional q-normed space is locally compact.

Proof.
Let
\left(X,\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{i}\right)_{i\in I}\right)
be an
n
-dimensional q-normed space, with basis
\left\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\right\}
, let
f_{X}
be as at (
4
), and let
B
be a
bounded subset of
X
. There exists
r>0
such that
\left\|x\right\|_{i}<r
for all
x\in B
and all
i\in I
. Also, there exists
c>0
with the
property: for each
x\in X
there exists
i\in I
such that
c\left\|x\right\|_{i}>\left\|f_{X}(x)\right\|-1
. Thus for each
x\in B

there exists
i\in I
such that
\left|f_{X}(x)\right|<c\left\|x\right\|_{i}+1<cr+1
, and therefore
f_{X}(B)
is bounded in

\mathbf{R}^{n}
. Since the latter space is locally compact
[
4
, 4.1.6]
, there exists a compact
K\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that

f_{X}(B)\subset K
. Then
B\subset f^{-1}(K)
. Since
f^{-1}
is uniformly
continuous,
K
is totally bounded in
X
, by Proposition
10
.
Moreover, since
K
is complete and
f_{X}^{-1}
is bi-continuous,
f^{-1}(K)

is complete and hence compact.

It is tempting to suggest that every element of a finite-dimensional q-normed
space is normable. Here is a Brouwerian counterexample. Let
\left(a_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
be a binary sequence with
a_{1}=0
. Define seminorms

\left\|\ \right\|_{n}

(n\geq 1)\
on
\mathbf{R}
as follows: for
each
x\in\mathbf{R}
,

\left\|x\right\|_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}(1-a_{n})\left|x\right|&\text{if }a_{n}=0\text{ or }a_{n-1}=1,\\
&\\
2\left|x\right|&\text{if }a_{n}=1-a_{n-1}\end{array}\right.

Then
\left\|x\right\|_{n}\leq 2\left|x\right|
for all

x\in\mathbf{R}
, so
\left(\mathbf{R},\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}\right)
is a q-normed space. If

\left\|1\right\|_{\infty}\equiv\sup\left\{\left\|1\right\|_{n}:n\geq 1\right\}

exists, then either
1<\left\|1\right\|_{\infty}
or
\left\|1\right\|_{\infty}<2
. In the first case, there exists
N
such that

\left\|1\right\|_{n}>1
, so
a_{N}=1-a_{N-1}
and therefore
a_{N}=1
. In the case
\left\|1\right\|_{\infty}<2
, we must have
a_{n}=0

for all
n
. Thus the statement

Every element of a finite-dimensional q-normed space is normable

implies the essentially nonconstructive omniscience principle LPO:

For each binary sequence
\left(a_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
either
a_{n}=0

for all
n
or else there exists
N
such that
a_{N}=1

Hence, a fortiori, the statement

Every element of a finite-dimensional locally convex space is normable

implies LPO.

Need the unit ball of a finite-dimensional q-normed space be totally bounded?
Consider the same q-normed space
\left(\mathbf{R},\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}\right)
as in the preceding paragraph,
and suppose that its unit ball
B
is totally bounded. Let
\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{\nu}\right\}
be a finite
\frac{1}{4}
-approximation to
B

relative to
\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
, and let

\xi=\max_{1\leq k\leq\nu}x_{k}
. If there exists
N
such that
a_{N}=1-a_{N-1}
, then
2\left|x_{k}\right|=\left\|x_{k}\right\|_{N}\leq 1
for each
k\leq\nu
, so
\left|\xi\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}
. On the other hand, if
a_{n}=0
for all
n
, then
1\in B
and so there
exists
k\leq\nu
such that
\left|1-x_{k}\right|=\left\|1-x_{k}\right\|_{n}<\frac{1}{4}
for all
n
; whence
1-\xi\leq 1-x_{k}<\frac{1}{4}
and therefore
\xi>\frac{3}{4}
. Now, either
\xi>\frac{1}{2}
or
\xi<\frac{3}{4}
. In the first case we cannot have
a_{N}=1

for any
N
, so
a_{n}=0
for all
n
; in the second, we cannot have
a_{n}=0

for all
n
. Thus the statement

The unit ball of a finite-dimensional q-normed space is totally bounded

implies the omniscience principle WLPO:

For each binary sequence
\left(a_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
either
a_{n}=0

for all
n
or it is not the case that
a_{n}=0
for all
n
.

For the future

Perhaps the most obvious examples of q-normed spaces for deeper study are the
dual q-normed space
X^{\ast}\
and its dual
X^{\ast\ast}
, where
\left(X,\left(\left\|\ \right\|_{i}\right)_{i\in I}\right)
is a given
q-normed (but not necessarily either normed or uniformly convex) space. In
order to carry out such deeper study, it seems that we may need a version of
the Hahn-Banach theorem applicable to q-normed spaces (cf.
[
8
]
).
This is something to pursue in future research.
