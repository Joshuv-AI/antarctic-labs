Executive Summary: The objective of the project was to use previous insights
developed through synthesis and quantitative modeling of rigid metal–organic
frameworks (MOFs) in an established synergistic theoretical/experimental team to
create, modify, and evaluate flexible MOFs (FlexMOFs) for natural gas (NG) storage
and release at practically useful pressures and transform the NG storage economy. The
goal was reduced pressure absorbed natural gas (ANG) FlexMOF storage at operating
pressures less than 100 bar with physisorption exploiting the favorable thermodynamics
and kinetics of flexible porous material opening in response to adsorption. Hydrogen
behavior in this context was also considered.
 The specific aim of the project was to design and develop a standard
computational modeling methodology for, first, detailed atomistic retrodiction of FlexMOF
gating behavior and ultimately prediction of the effects of functionalization and/or
substitution on structural transitioning. The project was also geared towards the
establishment of the interaction of methane with the framework and binding sites using
modeling and the FlexMOF will be both internally and external validated as a SMART
metric.
 This has value to the scientific community both from the obvious standpoint of
providing a better understanding of the promising test case systems (CdIF-13 and the
MIL-53(Al) series of MOFs), but also in providing an avenue of obtaining insight into these
FlexMOF systems in general, which is of particular interest given the tendency of
structure-function correlation to lag behind synthesis methodology (making the latter a hit
or miss proposition for applications). This gap remains significant for FlexMOF systems
whose gate opening behaviors complicate computational examination. The
computational methodologies are relatively inexpensive in terms of both money and

computational resources enhancing the general viability of these methodologies. The
ultimate gain to the public will be in the application of these techniques to design systems
for natural gas storage and use to cut down on green-house emissions.

Background:

 The recent phenomena of MOF synthesis has been extremely prolific in the last
few decades, yet the lack of understanding regarding structure function correlation
meant that synthesis of a structure with desirable sorptive behavior involved a
significant degree of serendipity rather than rational design.
 A significant effort has been exerted by the computational community to rectify
this fall short, yet initial efforts in the regard were aimed at rigid MOF structures[1-3]
owing both to their greater tractability as well as the greater preponderance of these
structures.
 FlexMOFs, considered extremely promising for sorptive applications due to the
potential to control the gate-opening conditions to maximize desired sorptive behavior in
the application condition regimes,[4-5] posed a more significant problem for
computational examination as the structures cannot be treated accurately by a single
system snapshot. Even local motions in otherwise rigid frameworks have frequently
proved impossible to accurately model in the rigid structure approximation[6-7], while
the subset of MOFs which undergo long range conformational shifting (i.e. FlexMOFs)
are almost never tractable for these techniques.
 Simulation studies have, however, more recently begun to examine these
systems,[8-11] though these studies are currently largely contained to those FlexMOFs
with simple open to closed form transitions, most notably wine-rack type frameworks
with obvious accordion like collapse trajectories. Notably, even these seemingly simple
systems offer surprising and non-intuitive complexity in behavior, and examination of
these is ongoing.[8, 9]
 Various methodologies have been used to examine these systems, the most
prevalent being simulation of FlexMOFs in the osmotic ensemble,[9] for which chemical
potential of the sorbate, pressure, and temperature are held constant while other
statistical mechanical variables are permitted to fluctuate. As cell parameters and
relative atomic positions are among the latter, the cell itself undergoes conformational
shifting for the given conditions.
 This methodology does, however, come with some not insignificant drawbacks
as it requires a combination of dynamic modeling with insertion/deletion moves more
tractable to Monte Carlo simulation. This is not feasible for a number of systems,
particularly those with greater complexity, and tends to be computationally expensive.
Their viability varies depending on the rigor of their underlying energetic modeling.
These techniques can be complimentary to the methodology developed for this grant,
providing thermodynamic state point insights to compliment the mechanical atomistic
behavioral understanding yielded by our techniques.
 Various other computational treatments, such as analysis of structure preference
based on adsorption forces under various conditions, have also been performed[8] and
add insight into sorbate induced structure transition. Yet a notable gap remains in
regards to atomistic understanding of both sorbate-MOF interaction and MOF priming
for sorbate interaction (i.e. what functionality causes greater receptivity prior to
sorption). It is this missing element that this work seeks to provide using ab initio
methodology to examine specific binding behavior and the contributing elements, both
direct and indirect.

 5

 It should be noted that a number of publications have examined the energetics of
structural transitions, either for the void or sorbate loaded structures. Graphing the
potential of the framework can elucidate the energetics has been used to obtain a state
to state understanding[10, 11]. Mapping of full energetic landscapes, as by using a
collective volume variable to identify various minima and transition states,[11] provides
a superior and more detailed understanding. Notably, the underlying methodology of
these techniques can vary widely using for example the osmotic ensemble, purely
classical simulation, or purely ab initio calculations. This grant work uses an ab initio
variant of the potential graphing to aid in analysis of the opening and closing of the
systems either voided or in response to sorbate stimulation.
 For the experimental side of this grant, the initial object was to identify promising
MOF platforms both for methane sorption applications and/or iterative modification
studies, with the latter taking focus as the grant carried forth. A number of novel MOFs
were designed, synthesized and examined for CH4 sorption, as well as extant MOFs
synthesized to examine the same. Ultimately, focus came on the MIL-53(Al) series as a
promising platform and a number of variants were synthesized in iterative consideration
with simulation results.
 The bulk of this work ultimately identified and focused on two general systems.
Firstly, MIL-53(Al) and its derivatives which is one of the simplest and most studied
wine-rack type FlexMOF platforms. A number of variants have been synthesized[12, 13]
and the variant response to different sorbates reported in literature.[12, 14, 15] This
body of literature informed our choice of FlexMOF, with a number of published structure
as well as some novel variants here examined. The MIL-53(Al)-X series (or metal
substituted variants thereof) has undergone considerable computational examination
using various methodologies discussed above,[8, 9, 16, 17] and to this body we have
added specific atomistic insights to guide synthesis of variants.
 The second system was CdIF-13 and its derivatives. These structures have been
extensively studied with consideration to CH4 and H2 as well as other hydrocarbons,[18-
22] providing an extensive body of experimental data to compare to. This structure was
known to undergo a long range concerted transformational shifting of the sort that was
not generally tractable to extant methodologies. We therefore worked in tandem with the
wet lab experimentalists to not only shed on the unusual effects of functionalization, but
more fundamentally to design and test a methodology capable of treating this type of
system. We have published a paper on this successful methodology.

Project Objectives:

 This project was aimed at the development of modeling techniques capable of
not only retrodicting MOF sorptive behavior in the presence of CH4 and H2, but also
predicting the effects of functionalization in a manner which allows guided synthesis of
FlexMOFs tailored to applications.
 With the ability to rationally design economically viable structures capable of
practical CH4 and/or H2 storage, we will be able to cut down on green house emissions.
In a broader sense, it will forward the field of MOF development along all application
lines as our understanding of these systems in increased and we provide methods to
obtain such understanding in their absence.

Project Results and Discussion: This project consisted of three broad tasks, firstly the
design and synthesis of flexible dia-MOFs and investigation of their performance.
Secondly, characterization of CH4-MOF interactions in those systems and others. The
third task was initially a techno economic analysis of the results produced with an
ultimate goal of providing a FlexMOF with a minimum total CH4 deliverable capacity of
300 v/v (5-65 bar, room temperature).
 As the project commenced, however, findings indicated the desirability of a
change in focus to take advantage of a point of great interest to the scientific
community, especially for those in the field of MOF design and synthesis for specific
applications. As such, and with permission, the final task was reassigned to verifying
modeling insights and synthesis of to-order structures with the final goal of
demonstrating a predictive modeling methodology for the MIL-53(Al) and CdIF-13
FlexMOF platforms, for which interesting and promising preliminary results had been at
that point obtained, and whose elucidation would provide avenues for understanding
and rationally designing modified systems. Those prior tasks, though unchanged as
they were already in execution, were nevertheless somewhat redirected towards this
new objective and included collaboration with Dr. McGuirk at CSM, especially on flexible
MOF hydrogen uptake.
 It is our contention that the ultimate goals of this grant were largely obtained as a
paper has been published on the predictive modeling of the latter structure, while work
on the former work is currently in preparation for publication. What work remains to be
done along these lines is relatively straightforward. A comprehensive breakdown of
each task, subtask, and milestone is discussed below.

TASK 1: Design/synthesis of flexible dia-MOFs and investigation of their
performance in methane storage: Starting with the proposed candidate structures our
iterative design process was initiated. Synthesis/modeling of extant flexible diamondoid
MOFs were chosen as the jumping off point. Literature materials were synthesized, and
parallel computational models established. These were modified based on the empirical
measurements and modeling insights.

  MILESTONE 1.2.1: Carry out high-pressure CH4 storage measurements and

 The third milestone was involved a combined experimental and theoretical approach.
All structures synthesized for the previous milestone were examined for CH4 uptake.
Binding energy is discussed under the next milestone, to which it also applies, for the
sake of clarity. The more interesting results are discussed below.
 We measured CH4 adsorption in CFA-1[31] and observed that although this MOF
surpassed a total uptake of 200 v/v at 298 K and 65 bar, the 5–65 bar working capacity
is only 171 v/v at 298 K (Figure 7(a)). It was deduced that this MOF is not flexible for
methane sorption as indicated in the shape of the isotherm.

Figure 7: Experimental high-pressure total (red) and excess (blue) CH4 adsorption
isotherms at 298 K and up to 100 bar for (a) CFA-1 and (b) MIL-53(Al)-OH.

In addition, a high pressure CH4 isotherm for MIL-53(Al)-OH[33] was collected at 298 K
(Figure 7(b)). The total volumetric working capacity of this material was measured to be

 14

188 cm3/cm3 between 5 to 65 bar, which is slightly lower than our Phase I milestone of
200 cm3/cm3.
 We then synthesized the flexible MOF NH2-MIL-53(Al)[34], also known as MIL-
53(Al)-NH2, and collected the high pressure CH4 adsorption isotherm at 273 and 298 K
(Figure 8(a)). The total volumetric working capacity of this material was measured to be
143 cm3/cm3 between 5 to 65 bar at 298 K, which is lower than our Phase I milestone of
200 cm3/cm3. This volumetric working capacity is also lower than that for the
isostructural MIL-53(Al)-OH (188 cm3/cm3). This indicates that substituting the
functional group on the organic linker from –OH to –NH2 did not improve the CH4
adsorption performance and working capacity.
 We also collected the experimental high-pressure CH4 adsorption isotherm for
NKU-FlexMOF-1[35] at 298 K. This material has a Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET)
surface area of 952 m2/g. Although this MOF exhibits gate-opening behavior toward
hydrocarbons such as C2H6 and C3H6, this phenomenon was not observed in the case
of CH4 adsorption (Figure 8(b)). The total volumetric working capacity for this MOF was
calculated to be 85.3 cm3/cm3 between 5 to 65 bar, which is significantly lower than our
Phase I milestone. Based on this, it can be deduced that a suitable BET surface area is
a prerequisite to obtain a high CH4 volumetric working capacity for FlexMOFs.

Figure 8: Experimental total (red) and excess (blue) high-pressure CH4 adsorption
(filled symbols) and desorption (open symbols) isotherms at 298 K and up to 100 bar for

 To provide an insight into the deliverable capacity performance for methane
storage over the delivery cycles, high-pressure methane measurements were carried
out on MIL-53(Al)-NH2. During the first cycle, the methane was adsorbed up to 110 bar
and then desorbed completely. In the second cycle, the same sample without activation
was subjected to CH4 adsorption to 110 bar and then desorbed to 5 bar. For the final
cycle, the adsorption was again carried out to 110 bar followed by complete desorption
to 1 bar. All the measurements were carried out at 303 K.
 The MIL-53(Al)-NH2 displayed excess and total uptake capacities of 166 cc/g and
222 cc/g, respectively at 100 bar during the first cycle; and upon desorbing to 1 bar the
corresponding values were 2.08 and 2.71 cc/g, respectively (Figure 11a and 11b). In the
second cycle, the excess and total capacities reached 167 cc/g and 223 c/g at 100 bar,
however the uptake reached 0.79 cc/g (excess) and 3.28 cc/g (total) when the material
was desorbed down to 5 bar.
 Quite interestingly, during the third cycle the capacities increased to 178 cc/g
(excess) and 234 cc/g (total) at 100 bar; and the material did not desorb completely during
the desorption step with the excess and total uptake capacities being 15.16 and 15.98
cc/g, respectively. Moreover, the material does not reach saturation even at 110 bar.

Figure 11. The high-pressure methane adsorption-desorption isotherms for MIL-53(Al)-
NH2 at 303K displaying the (a) excess, and (b) total uptake capacities over three cycles.

 The corresponding total volumetric uptake capacities at 100 bar were found out to
be 226 cc/cc (first cycle), 228 cc/cc (second cycle), and 238 cc/cc (third cycle) (Figure
12). The measurements will be reproduced for verification and further studied to
investigate these behaviors.

During the latter part of Budget Period 1 (BP1), the Department of Energy (DOE) recognized
that the computational insights from the NCSU project could significantly enhance other
HyMARC initiatives focused on Metal-Organic Framework (MOF) flexibility for hydrogen
adsorption. Consequently, a formal collaboration was established between this project and the
HyMARC seedling project at the Colorado School of Mines. This strategic alignment is mutually
beneficial, as the experimental materials synthesized at Mines serve as critical inputs for the
high-fidelity computational models developed at NCSU. The Statement of Project Objectives
(SOPO) was updated to formalize this collaboration and reflect our shared research goals. This
allowed us to move beyond the requirements of Budget Period 1 despite the outcome of those
milestones and continue the project in line with the updated SOPO.”

 40

The partnership with Dr.McGuirk’s team at the Colorado School of Mines has created a robust
feedback loop between experimental synthesis and advanced molecular modeling. By integrating
detailed structural data and sorbent characteristics with experimental thermodynamic
measurements, such as adsorption isotherms, we can now characterize flexible MOF systems
with unprecedented precision. This holistic approach allows us to validate our computational
predictions against real-world physical data, accelerating the discovery of materials with
optimized hydrogen storage capacities. Specifically, sorption folding and unfolding pathways
specific to the sorbent were determined using novel methodology combining Grand Canonical
Monte Carlo methods iteratively with periodic DFT calculations. The results were guided and
validated by the detailed experimental data that was available in the McGuirk group.

Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions:

 In addition to the synthesis of a number of MOFs to add to the general body of
known structures, this grant has identified two promising test case FlexMOF systems
and provided an accurate modeling methodology for an understanding of them. It has

 42

shed light on the highly unpredictable behavior of MIL-53(Al)-X variants to CH4
stimulation and provided some design principles for predicting the effects of various
modifications on sorptive behavior. For CdIF-13, we have provided the first, to our
knowledge, clear atomistic description of the collapse of a FlexMOF which undergoes a
complex concerted and non-intuitive gate-closing mechanism.

Path Forward:

Broad application of the computational techniques developed will be applied to more
FlexMOF systems, and as they can be implemented by broadly available simulation
software, their general use is viable for the computational community. The specific
elucidation of the two test case systems, CdIF-13, for which a computational
understanding has been published, application to structural variants including partial
functionalization of linker is called for to determine the specific atomistic effects of these
interactions. For MIL-53(Al)-X, isotherms for the amino variant are being redone to
confirm and elucidate unusually inconsistent isothermal behavior (Figure 9), before
publication of results.
