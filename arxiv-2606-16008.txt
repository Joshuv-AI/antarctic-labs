Equilibria of the Pressureless Euler System in Dimension 1 1
Nicholas Biglin, Columbia University, nb3101@columbia.edu

Abstract
The pressureless Euler equations in dimension 1 1 describe the evolution of a distribution of mass over the real line. We are interested in sticky particles solutions: distributional solutions in which mass that collides sticks together for all time. Given initial data, a projection formula is known to give a sticky particles solution. We obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for this solution to go to equilibrium based only on the initial condition. Furthermore, we give a precise characterization of the equilibrium, as well as a formula for the time of collapse.

x\in[1-\delta,1)
. Physically, this means that, for all time, there is no outward velocity outside of some fixed compact set. In this case, since
N_{t}
is nondecreasing for any
t
, we obtain the following bounds on
N_{t}(x)
.

\displaystyle N_{0}(\delta)\leq N_{t}(\delta)\leq N_{t}(x)\leq N_{t}(1-\delta)\leq N_{0}(1-\delta),\quad

\displaystyle x\in[\delta,1-\delta],

\displaystyle N_{0}(x)\leq N_{t}(x)\leq N_{t}(1-\delta)\leq N_{0}(1-\delta),\quad

\displaystyle x\in(0,\delta],

\displaystyle N_{0}(\delta)\leq N_{t}(\delta)\leq N_{t}(x)\leq N_{0}(x),\quad

\displaystyle x\in[1-\delta,1).

As a consequence,
|N_{t}(x)|\leq H(x)\coloneq\max\{|N_{0}(x)|,|N_{0}(\delta)|,|N_{0}(1-\delta)|\}
on
(0,1)
. Since
N_{0}
is nondecreasing and square-integrable,
H\in L^{2}(0,1)
. Thus, we have
\|N_{t}-N_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
as
t\to\infty
by the
L^{2}
version of the Dominated Convergence Theorem. Of course, more meaningful would be a result depending only on the initial condition
(N_{0},V_{0})
. This is difficult because convergence in
L^{2}(0,1)
depends on the behavior of
N_{t}
near
0
and
1
, and yet, any analysis of the projection formula must consider global properties of
N_{0}+tV_{0}
when taking the convex envelope. Finally, we note that this condition is not necessary for convergence in
W_{2}
. We conclude with an example of a solution not satisfying the above condition, but which converges in
W_{p}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Example 3.14
.

Consider the initial conditions given by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}N_{0}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big[2n\chi_{[1-2^{-n},1-3\cdot 2^{-2-n})}+(2n+1)\chi_{[1-3\cdot 2^{-2-n},1-2^{-1-n})}\big],\\
V_{0}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big[\frac{1}{2}\chi_{[1-2^{-n},1-3\cdot 2^{-2-n})}-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{[1-3\cdot 2^{-2-n},1-2^{-1-n})}\big].\end{cases}

(3.8)

One may verify that
\|N_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-n}\big(2n^{2}+n+1/4\big)<\infty
and
\quad\|V_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=1/2
, and that the solution originating at (
3.8
) to (
PE
) is captured by

\displaystyle N_{t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big[(2n+\frac{t}{2})\chi_{[1-2^{-n},1-3\cdot 2^{-2-n})}+(2n+1-\frac{t}{2})\chi_{[1-3\cdot 2^{-2-n},1-2^{-1-n})}\big]\quad(0<t<1),

V_{t}=V_{0}

(0<t<1)
, and that, for
t>1
,

\displaystyle N_{\infty}=N_{t}=N_{1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2n+\frac{1}{2})\chi_{[1-2^{-n},1-2^{-1-n})},\quad V_{t}=0.

Thus, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\rho_{t}={N_{t}}_{\#}\mathcal{L}
,
\rho_{\infty}={N_{\infty}}_{\#}\mathcal{L}
, and
0\leq t\leq 1
, we compute

\displaystyle W_{p}(\rho_{t},\rho_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\|N_{t}-N_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}=\|\frac{t}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{t}{2}.
