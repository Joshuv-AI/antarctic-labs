A Shape Design Approximation for Degenerate Partial Differential Equations and Its Application This work was carried out with the support of the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grant nos. 12131008 and U23B2033, and National Key R&D Program of China under grant no. 2024YFA1013101.
Bao-Zhu Guo, Dong-Hui Yang, Jie Zhong

Abstract
In this paper, we focus on two types of degenerate partial differential equations: a degenerate elliptic equation and a degenerate parabolic equation. Significantly, both categories are characterized by the same principal operator. To obtain solutions for these equations, we introduce a novel approximation approach, termed the shape design approximation. As a practical application of this method, we derive a Carleman estimate for the backward degenerate parabolic equation. This estimate plays a pivotal role in establishing the null controllability of the degenerate parabolic equation. A notable advantage of employing the shape design approximation in deriving the Carleman estimate is that it enables us to bypass the requirement for second order derivatives in the degenerate equation. Usually, this has been a significant obstacle in the derivation of Carleman estimates for degenerate parabolic equations. Keywords: Degenerate partial differential equations, shape design approximation, Carleman estimate, null controllability. AMS subject classifications (2010): 35J70, 35K65, 49Q10, 93B05.

Introduction

Degenerate partial differential equations have been extensively studied in numerous works, including
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
. The solution spaces for these equations are typically formulated within the framework of weighted Sobolev spaces (as detailed in
[
16
,
21
]
), which are contingent upon the so-called
A_{p}
weight function.
To elaborate, for a given
p\in(1,\infty)
, a locally integrable, non-negative function
w
is said to be an
A_{p}
-weight if there exists a constant
c>0
such that, for all cubes
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the following inequality holds:

\left(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w(x)\,dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w(x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dx\right)^{p-1}\leq c.

(1.1)

The infimum of the set of constants
c=c(w,p,N)>0
satisfying (
1.1
) is referred to as the
A_{p}
-constant of
w
. For
p=1
, a locally integrable, non-negative function
w
is designated an
A_{1}
-weight if there exists a constant
c>0
such that, for all cubes
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, it has

\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}w(x)\,dx\leq c\operatorname*{ess\ \!inf}_{x\in K}w(x).

It is well established that
w=|x|^{\alpha}\ (x\in\mathbb{R}^{N})
constitutes an
A_{p}
-weight if and only if
-N<\alpha<N(p-1)
. In particular,
x^{\alpha}\ (0<x<1)
is an
A_{1+\frac{2}{N}}
-weight for
\alpha\in(0,2)
. In this study, we assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\ (N\geq 1)
is a bounded domain with a
C^{2}
-boundary, and
w\in C^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
is a weight function belonging to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{1+\frac{2}{N}}
(i.e.,
w
is an
A_{1+\frac{2}{N}}
-weight), satisfying

w(x)>0\text{ in }\Omega\hbox{ and }w(x)=0\text{ on }\Gamma,

(1.2)

where
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
is a nonempty open subset. Note that
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
is a possible scenario.
Let
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{N}
denote a measurable matrix function that fulfills the following conditions for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

\Lambda w|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Lambda^{-1}w|\xi|^{2}\text{ a.e.\! in }\Omega,\quad a_{ij}\in C^{1}(\Omega)\text{ for }i,j=1,\cdots,N,

(1.3)

where
\Lambda>0
is a given constant.
In this paper, we focus on two types of degenerate partial differential equations which are characterized by the same principal operator.
The first type is the following degenerate elliptic equation:

\begin{cases}-\Div(A\nabla\varphi)=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\varphi=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
f\in L^{2}(\Omega;w^{-1})
is a given function. Here,
\nabla\varphi=(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{N}})
represents the gradient of
\varphi
,
\Div F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial F_{i}}{\partial x_{i}}
denotes the divergence of
F=(F_{1},\cdots,F_{N})
, and
L^{2}(\Omega;w^{-1})
is a weighted Sobolev space that will be defined subsequently.

The second type is the following degenerate parabolic equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi-\Div(A\nabla\varphi)=f&\text{in }Q,\\
\varphi=0&\text{on }\partial Q,\\
\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0}&\text{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.5)

where
Q=\Omega\times(0,T),\partial Q=\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
, and
f\in L^{2}(Q;w^{-1})
is a given function (to be specified later), and
\varphi_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

1.4
) and the degenerate parabolic equation (

2
Solution spaces

In this section, we shall present the solution spaces for equations (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), which are classical weighted Sobolev spaces. We assume that
p\in(1,\infty)
.

2.1
Degenerate elliptic case

We define the weighted
L^{p}
space as follows:

L^{p}(\Omega;w)=\left\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\colon\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}w\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\},

where
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
denotes the space of real distributions on
\Omega
(refer to
[
13
,
16
,
21
,
28
]
)
and
w
is an
A_{p}
-weight function. For
L^{2}(\Omega;w)
, we can define an inner product:

(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}uvw\,\mathrm{d}x.

It is well established that
(L^{2}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)})
forms a Hilbert space, and

(L^{p}(\Omega;w),\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;w)})
is a Banach space with the norm defined as

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;w)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}w\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Furthermore, we note that

u\in L^{p}(\Omega;w^{-1})\text{ if and only if }\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}w^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Now, we introduce the weighted first order Sobolev space:

\begin{split}W^{1,p}(\Omega;w)=\left\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\colon\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}w\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}w\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\}.\end{split}

For
H^{1}(\Omega;w):=W^{1,2}(\Omega;w)
, we can define an inner product:

(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}uvw\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v)w\,\mathrm{d}x.

It is a well known fact that
(H^{1}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)})
is a Hilbert space (see
[
8
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
28
]
), and
(W^{1,p}(\Omega;w),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega;w)})
is a Banach space, where the norm is given by

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega;w)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}w\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}w\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Additionally, we define the closure of the space of smooth functions with compact support in the
W^{1,p}(\Omega;w)
norm:

W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;w)=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega;w)}},

and
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)=W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega;w)
.

We are now in a position to provide the definition of the solution to the equation (
1.4
).

Definition 2.1

A function
\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
is said to be a weak solution of the degenerate elliptic equation (
1.4
) if it satisfies the following condition:

\int_{\Omega}A\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x

for every
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be an open and bounded domain, and let
w\in A_{p}
with
1<p<\infty
. Then, there exist positive constants
C_{\Omega}
and
\delta
such that for all
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;w)
and all
\theta
satisfying
1\leq\theta\leq\frac{N}{N-1}+\delta
, the following inequality holds:

\|u\|_{L^{\theta p}(\Omega;w)}\leq C_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;w)}.

(2.1)

Proof.

The proof of this lemma can be found in
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
, or alternatively in (1.5) of
[
14
]
, or in
[
9
, Theorem 2]
.

\square

Lemma 2.2

The space defined as

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;A):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\colon\int_{\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\}

is equivalent to the space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.

Proof.

By utilizing condition (
1.3
) and Lemma
2.1
, we can establish the equivalence of these two spaces.

\square

Lemma 2.3

Suppose
f\in L^{2}(\Omega;w^{-1})
. Then, the equation (
1.4
) admits a unique solution.

Proof.

This result follows directly from Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
, in conjunction with the Lax-Milgram Theorem. For a detailed proof, one may refer to
[
13
, Theorem 2.2]
or
[
8
, Theorem 2.10]
.

\square

2.2
Degenerate parabolic case

Let us denote

L^{p}(Q;w)=\left\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(Q)\colon\iint_{Q}|u|^{p}w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\right\},

and

H^{1,p}(Q;w)=\iint_{Q}|u|^{p}w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}|\nabla u|^{p}w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Here,
H_{0}^{1,p}(Q;w)
represents the closure of Lipschitz functions with compact support in
Q
under the norm of
H^{1,p}(Q;w)
.

Definition 2.2

We say that
\varphi\in H_{0}^{1,2}(Q;w)
is a weak solution of the equation in (
1.5
) if

\begin{split}-\iint_{Q}\varphi\partial_{t}\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}A\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}f\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}(x)\psi(x,0)\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

holds for any
\psi\in W=\{\psi\in H_{0}^{1,2}(Q;w)\colon\partial_{t}\psi\in H^{-1,2}(Q;w)\}
such that
\psi(T)=0
. Here,
H^{-1,2}(Q;w)
denotes the dual space of
H_{0}^{1,2}(Q;w)
.

Lemma 2.4

Let
\Lambda>0
be defined in (
1.3
). Let
w
be an
A_{2}
weight function,
f\in L^{2}(Q;w^{-1})
, and
\varphi_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a weak solution of the equation (
1.5
), and it satisfies

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi(x,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\iint_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w^{-1})}^{2}\right),\end{split}

where the positive constant
C
depends only on
\Lambda
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

For every
\tau\in(0,T)
, by Definition
2.2
, we substitute
\varphi
for
\psi
and follow a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem 2.4 in
[
4
]
or the proof of Lemma 3.7 in
[
14
]
. This yields

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\tau)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}(x)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\iint_{\Omega\times(0,\tau)}A\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}f\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Note that

\iint_{Q}f\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\left(\iint_{Q}f^{2}w^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\iint_{Q}\varphi^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, by Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\tau)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\Lambda}{2}\iint_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}(x)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C_{\Omega}}{\Lambda}\iint_{Q}f^{2}w^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

The existence of the solution to (
1.5
) is established in
[
14
, Theorem 3.11]
.

\square

3
Approximation

In this section, we present an approximation method (Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
) for solving the equations (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) through shape design. This method is pivotal for establishing the Carleman estimate (Theorem
4.2
) in Section
4
.
We define

\mathcal{O}(\Gamma;\varepsilon)=\left\{x\in\Omega\colon\dist(x,\Gamma)>\varepsilon\right\},

where

\dist(x,\Gamma)=\inf_{y\in\Gamma}|x-y|.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we select a domain
\Omega_{k}\in C^{2}
such that
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma,\frac{1}{k})\subseteq\Omega_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\Gamma,\frac{1}{k+1})
.

3.1
Degenerate elliptic case

Consider the equation

\begin{cases}-\Div(A_{k}\nabla\varphi_{k})=f_{k}&\text{in }\Omega_{k},\\
\varphi_{k}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{k},\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
A_{k}=A|_{\Omega_{k}}
is defined in (
1.3
), and
f_{k}=f|_{\Omega_{k}}\in L^{2}(\Omega;w^{-1})
is defined in (
1.4
). By (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and the definition of
\Omega_{k}
, equation (
3.1
) is uniformly elliptic.

Definition 3.1

A function
\varphi_{k}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{k};w_{k})=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{k})
with
A_{k}=A|_{\Omega_{k}},w_{k}=w|_{\Omega_{k}}
, and
f_{k}=f|_{\Omega_{k}}
is called a weak solution to the elliptic equation (
3.1
) if

\int_{\Omega_{k}}A_{k}\nabla\varphi_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega_{k}}f_{k}\psi\mathrm{d}x

for all
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{k})
.

Lemma 3.1

Define

\beta_{k}=\inf_{u_{k}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{k};w_{k})\atop\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{k};w_{k})}=1}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x.

Then, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta_{k}\geq\Lambda C_{\Omega}^{-2}
, where
C_{\Omega}
is the constant from Lemma
2.1
. Furthermore,

\int_{\Omega_{k}}u_{k}^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{\Omega}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x

for all
u_{k}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{k};w_{k})
.

Proof.

Since
w_{k}\geq C_{k}>0
on
\Omega_{k}
by (
1.2
), there exists
\varphi_{k}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{k};w_{k})=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{k})
such that

\beta_{k}=\int_{\Omega_{k}}A_{k}\nabla\varphi_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}\mathrm{d}x=\inf_{u_{k}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{k};w_{k})\atop\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{k};w_{k})}=1}\int_{\Omega_{k}}A_{k}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla u_{k}\mathrm{d}x.

Define

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{k}(x),&x\in\Omega_{k},\\
0,&x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}.\end{cases}

Then,
\|\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}=1
and
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
for
w_{k}=w
on
\Omega_{k}
. Thus,

\beta_{k}=\int_{\Omega_{k}}A_{k}\nabla\varphi_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}\mathrm{d}x{{=\int_{\Omega}A\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\mathrm{d}x}\geq\inf_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\atop\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}=1}\int_{\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\mathrm{d}x=\Lambda C_{\Omega}^{-2}}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.

\square

Theorem 3.1

Let
\varphi_{k}\ (k\in\mathbb{N})
be the solution of (
3.1
) with
A_{k}=A|_{\Omega_{k}}
and
f_{k}=f|_{\Omega_{k}}
, and let
\varphi
be the solution of (
1.4
). Then, the sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}
satisfies

\varphi_{k}\rightharpoonup\varphi\text{ weakly in }H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\hbox{ and }\varphi_{k}\rightharpoonup\varphi\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(\Omega;w).

Moreover,
(i) if
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega;w)
is compact, then

\varphi_{k}\rightarrow\varphi\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;w).

(ii) alternatively, if there exists a nonempty open subset
\omega\subseteq\Omega
such that
\overline{\omega}\subseteq\Omega
, then

\varphi_{k}\rightarrow\varphi\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\omega;w).

Proof.

Since
\varphi_{k}
is a solution of (
3.1
), it has

\int_{\Omega_{k}}A_{k}\nabla\varphi_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega_{k}}f_{k}\psi\mathrm{d}x

for every
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{k})
. Substituting
\psi
with
\varphi_{k}
and applying the condition (
1.3
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\Lambda\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x&\leq\int_{\Omega_{k}}A_{k}\nabla\varphi_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega_{k}}f_{k}\varphi_{k}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}f_{k}^{2}w_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

where
w_{k}=w|_{\Omega_{k}}
. This implies, by Lemma
3.1
, that

\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\leq\Lambda^{-2}C_{\Omega}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}f_{k}^{2}w_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{d}x.

Define

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{k}(x),&x\in\Omega_{k},\\
0,&x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k}.\end{cases}

Then,
\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}w\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\mathrm{d}x\leq\Lambda^{-2}C_{\Omega}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}f_{k}^{2}w_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{d}x\leq\Lambda^{-2}C_{\Omega}^{2}\int_{\Omega}f^{2}w^{-1}\mathrm{d}x.

This shows that
\{\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\}
is a bounded sequence in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
. Hence, there exists a subsequence of
\{\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\}
, still denoted by the same notation, and
\widehat{\varphi}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
such that

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\rightharpoonup\widehat{\varphi}\text{ weakly in }H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\hbox{ and }\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\rightharpoonup\widehat{\varphi}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(\Omega;w).

(3.2)

Next, we show that
\widehat{\varphi}
is a solution of (
1.4
).
For every
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by the definition of
\Omega_{k}
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
,
\supp\psi\subseteq\Omega_{k}
.
By (
3.2
) and Lemma
2.2
. This implies

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}A\nabla\widehat{\varphi}\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x&=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}A_{k}\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{k}}f_{k}\psi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\psi\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

This means that
\widehat{\varphi}
is a solution of (
1.4
).
Finally, if we further assume that
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega;w)
is compact, then by (
3.2
),

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\rightarrow\widehat{\varphi}\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;w),

which is the assertion (i).

For the assertion (ii), assume there exists a nonempty open subset
\omega\subseteq\Omega
such that
\overline{\omega}\subseteq\Omega
. Let
\delta=\frac{1}{4}\dist(\omega,\partial\Omega)
, and choose
\zeta\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
such that
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
, and

\zeta=1\text{ on }\mathcal{O}(\omega,\delta)=\{x\in\Omega\colon\dist(x,\omega)<\delta\},\quad\zeta=0\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\mathcal{O}(\omega,2\delta),

with
|\nabla\zeta|\leq\frac{C}{\delta}
and
|\frac{\partial^{2}\zeta}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}|\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}\ (i,j=1,\cdots,N)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, where the constants
C>0
are absolute and do not depend on any other constant. Choose
\widehat{\Omega}\in C^{2}
such that
\mathcal{O}(\omega,\delta)\subseteq\widehat{\Omega}\subseteq\overline{\widehat{\Omega}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\omega,3\delta)\subseteq\Omega
. Then,
\zeta\widehat{\varphi}_{k}
(for sufficiently large
k
) is a solution of the equation

\begin{cases}-\Div(A\nabla(\zeta\widehat{\varphi}_{k}))=\zeta f-2A\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}-\Div(A\nabla\zeta)\widehat{\varphi}_{k}&\text{in }\widehat{\Omega},\\
\zeta\widehat{\varphi}_{k}=0&\text{on }\partial\widehat{\Omega}.\end{cases}

This equation is uniformly elliptic by (
1.3
) and (
1.2
).
By
[
12
, Theorem 1, Chapter 6.3.1, p. 309]
, we have

\begin{split}\|\zeta\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{H^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}&\leq C\left(\|\zeta f-2A\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{k}-\Div(A\nabla\zeta)\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}+\|\zeta\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}+\|\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Omega})}\right)\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)},\end{split}

where the constants depend only on
\Lambda,w,\omega
, and
\Omega
. Moreover,
\|\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\omega,\delta))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;w)}
. By the Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem in Chapter 5.7 of
[
12
]
, we obtain

\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\rightarrow\widetilde{\varphi}\text{ strongly in }\mathcal{O}(\omega,\delta).

Combining this with
\varphi_{k}\rightharpoonup\varphi
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;w)
, we get
\widetilde{\varphi}=\varphi
on
\omega
. This shows that assertion (ii) holds.

\square

3.2
Degenerate parabolic case

We consider the following equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}-\Div(A_{k}\nabla\varphi_{k})=f_{k}&\text{in }Q_{k}=\Omega_{k}\times(0,T),\\
\varphi_{k}=0&\text{on }\partial Q_{k},\\
\varphi_{k}(0)=\varphi_{0}|_{\Omega_{k}}&\text{in }\Omega_{k},\end{cases}

(3.3)

where
f_{k}=f|_{\Omega_{k}}
, and
\varphi_{0}
is defined in (
1.5
).

Theorem 3.2

Let
\varphi
be the solution of (
1.5
), and
\varphi_{k}\ (k\in\mathbb{N})
be the solution of (
3.3
) with
f_{k}=f|_{\Omega_{k}}
and
\varphi_{k}(0)=\varphi_{0}|_{\Omega_{k}}
. Then the sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}
satisfies

\varphi_{k}\rightharpoonup\varphi\text{ weakly in }H_{0}^{1,2}(Q;w)\hbox{ and }\varphi_{k}\rightharpoonup\varphi\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(Q;w).

Furthermore, if there exists a non-empty open subset
\omega\subset\Omega
such that
\overline{\omega}\subset\Omega
, and
\varphi_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, then,

\varphi_{k}\to\varphi\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\omega;w).

Proof.

Since
\varphi_{k}
is a solution of (
3.3
) for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, by Lemma
2.1
, for any
\tau\in(0,T)
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\varphi_{k}(x,\tau)^{2}\,dx+\Lambda\iint_{Q_{k}}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\varphi_{k}(x,\tau)^{2}\,dx+\iint_{Q_{k}}A_{k}\nabla\varphi_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}\,dx\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\varphi_{0}^{2}\,dx+\iint_{Q_{k}}f_{k}\varphi_{k}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\varphi_{0}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\iint_{Q_{k}}f_{k}^{2}w_{k}^{-1}\,dx\,dt+\varepsilon\iint_{Q_{k}}\varphi_{k}^{2}w_{k}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\varphi_{0}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\iint_{Q_{k}}f_{k}^{2}w_{k}^{-1}\,dx\,dt+\varepsilon C_{\Omega}^{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\,dt,\end{split}

where
A_{k}=A|_{\Omega_{k}}
,
f_{k}=f|_{Q_{k}}
, and
w_{k}=w|_{\Omega_{k}}
. Choosing
\varepsilon=\frac{\Lambda C_{\Omega}^{-2}}{2}
, we get

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\varphi_{k}(x,\tau)^{2}\,dx+\frac{\Lambda}{2}\iint_{\Omega_{k}\times(0,\tau)}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\varphi_{0}^{2}\,dx+\Lambda^{-1}C_{\Omega}^{2}\iint_{\Omega_{k}\times(0,\tau)}f_{k}^{2}w_{k}^{-1}\,dx\,dt\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}^{2}\,dx+\Lambda^{-1}C_{\Omega}^{2}\iint_{Q}f^{2}w^{-1}\,dx\,dt\end{split}

(3.4)

for all
\tau\in(0,T)
.
Moreover, we have

\iint_{\Omega_{k}\times(0,T)}\varphi_{k}(x,t)^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq T\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}^{2}\,dx+2\Lambda^{-1}C_{\Omega}^{2}\iint_{Q}f^{2}w^{-1}\,dx\,dt\right).

Define

\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}(x,t)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{k}(x,t),&(x,t)\in Q_{k},\\
0,&(x,t)\in Q\setminus Q_{k},\end{cases}

Then,
\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\in H_{0}^{1,2}(Q;w)
, and hence there exists a subsequence of
\{\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\}
, still denoted by the same notation, and
\widetilde{\varphi}\in H_{0}^{1,2}(Q;w)
such that

\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{\varphi}\text{ weakly in }H_{0}^{1,2}(Q;w)\hbox{ and }\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{\varphi}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(Q;w)\cap L^{2}(Q).

(3.5)

Next, we show that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a solution of (
1.5
), i.e.,
\widetilde{\varphi}=\varphi
on
Q
.

Suppose
\psi\in W
,
\psi(T)=0
. By the density argument (see the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
), we can assume
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q})
and
\psi(\cdot,t)
is compactly supported in
\Omega
for any
t\in[0,T]
. By the definition of
\Omega_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\supp\psi\subset Q_{k}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Note that we have

\begin{split}-\iint_{Q}\widetilde{\varphi}\psi_{t}\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q}A\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\,dt&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(-\iint_{Q}\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\psi_{t}\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q}A_{k}\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\,dt\right)\\
&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\iint_{Q_{k}}f_{k}\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega_{k}}\varphi_{0}(x)\psi(x,0)\,dx\right)\\
&=\iint_{Q}f\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}(x)\psi(x,0)\,dx\end{split}

by (
3.5
).
This implies that
\widetilde{\varphi}\in H_{0}^{1,2}(Q;w)
is a solution of (
1.5
).

Finally, since
\overline{\omega}\subset\Omega
is compact, we denote
\delta=\frac{1}{4}\dist(\overline{\omega},\partial\Omega)
. Choosing
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
satisfying

\zeta=1\text{ on }\mathcal{O}(\omega,\delta)=\{x\in\Omega\colon\dist(x,\omega)<\delta\},\quad\zeta=0\text{ on }\Omega\setminus\mathcal{O}(\omega,2\delta),

and

|\nabla\zeta|\leq\frac{C}{\delta}\text{ and }\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\zeta}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N},

where the constants
C>0
are absolute constants. Denote
\widehat{\Omega}\in C^{2}
satisfying
\supp\zeta\subset\widehat{\Omega}\subset\overline{\widehat{\Omega}}\subset\mathcal{O}(\omega,3\delta)\subset\Omega
.

By the definition of
\Omega_{k}
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
, it has
\widehat{\Omega}\subset\Omega_{k}
. Hence
\zeta\varphi_{k}
is a solution of the following equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}(\zeta\varphi_{k})-\Div(A\nabla(\zeta\varphi_{k}))=\zeta f-2A\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{k}\Div(A\nabla\zeta),&\text{in }\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T),\\
\zeta\varphi_{k}=0,&\text{on }\partial\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T),\\
\zeta\varphi_{k}(0)=\zeta\varphi_{0},&\text{in }\widehat{\Omega}.\end{cases}

(3.6)

Note that
A\in C^{1}(\overline{\widehat{\Omega}})
. Then,
\Div(A\nabla\zeta)\in C(\overline{\widehat{\Omega}})
, and hence (
3.6
) is a uniformly parabolic equation.
Multiplying
\zeta\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}
on both sides of (
3.6
), and integrating on
\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,\tau)
,
\tau\in(0,T)
, we have

\begin{split}&\iint_{\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,\tau)}|\partial_{t}(\zeta\varphi_{k})|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}A\nabla(\zeta\varphi_{k}(\tau))\cdot\nabla(\zeta\varphi_{k}(\tau))\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}A\nabla(\zeta\varphi_{0})\cdot\nabla(\zeta\varphi_{0})\,dx+\iint_{\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,\tau)}\zeta^{2}f\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}\,dx\,dt\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt-2\iint_{\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,\tau)}(A\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k})(\zeta\partial_{t}\varphi_{k})\,dx\,dt-\iint_{\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T)}\varphi_{k}\Div(A\nabla\zeta)(\zeta\partial_{t}\varphi_{k})\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}A\nabla(\zeta\varphi_{0})\cdot\nabla(\zeta\varphi_{0})\,dx+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\iint_{\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T)}\zeta^{2}f^{2}w_{k}^{-1}\,dx\,dt+\varepsilon\iint_{\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T)}\zeta^{2}(\partial_{t}\varphi_{k})^{2}w_{k}\,dx\,dt\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\iint_{\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T)}|A\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\varepsilon\iint_{\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T)}\zeta^{2}(\partial_{t}\varphi_{k})^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\iint_{\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T)}|\Div(A\nabla\zeta)|^{2}\varphi_{k}^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\iint_{Q}f^{2}w^{-1}\,dx\,dt+C\iint_{Q_{k}}|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}|^{2}w_{k}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\left(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\iint_{Q}f^{2}w^{-1}\,dx\,dt\right)\end{split}

by taking
\varepsilon=\min\{\frac{1}{4\gamma},\frac{1}{4}\}
with
\gamma=\inf_{x\in\widehat{\Omega}}w(x)
and Lemma
2.1
, and the constants
C>0
depend only on
\delta,\Lambda,C_{\Omega},\gamma
and
\Omega
.
This implies that
\{\zeta\varphi_{k}\}
is a bounded sequence in

\widehat{W}:=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T))\colon\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T)),u\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\widehat{\Omega})\cap H^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}))\right\}.

Since
\widehat{W}\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T))
is compact, there exists a subsequence of
\{\varphi_{k}\}
, still denoted by the same notation, and
\widetilde{\varphi}\in\widehat{W}
such that

\zeta\varphi_{k}\to\widetilde{\varphi}\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega}\times(0,T)).

Noting that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\rightharpoonup\varphi
weakly in
L^{2}(Q;w)\cap L^{2}(Q)
and
\zeta\varphi_{k}=\varphi_{k}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}
on
\mathcal{O}(\omega,\delta)\times(0,T)\subset Q
, we get
\widetilde{\varphi}=\varphi=\widetilde{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{O}(\omega,\delta)
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.

\square

4
Application to 1-d degenerate parabolic equation

In this section, we aim to derive the Carleman estimate (Theorem
4.2
) for the backward degenerate parabolic equation (
4.1
). The weight function (
4.3
) employed for this estimate differs slightly from the weight (3.2) presented in
[
1
, p.175]
. By utilizing this weight function, we can circumvent the need for boundary estimates. To establish the Carleman estimate, we partition the estimation process into three distinct parts. By integrating these three estimates, we ultimately obtain the desired Carleman estimate. Finally, we employ an approximation method to prove Theorem
4.2
. The advantage of this approximation method lies in its independence from the second partial derivatives of equation (
4.1
). Given the challenges in defining second derivatives for equations (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), particularly in higher-dimensional degenerate partial differential equations, this method proves particularly advantageous.
We focus on the case where
N=1
and the weight function is
w=x^{\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The investigation of the case where
N=1
and
w=x^{\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in(1,2)
(or
N\geq 2
) will be addressed in a separate study.
We consider the following degenerate parabolic equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi+\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}\varphi)=g&\text{in }Q,\\
\varphi=0&\text{on }\partial Q,\\
\varphi(T)=\varphi_{T}&\text{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

(4.1)

where
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
,
\Omega=(0,1)
,
w=x^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
g\in L^{2}(\Omega;w^{-1})
, and
\varphi_{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. We observe that, from

\iint_{Q}g^{2}\,dx\,dt=\iint_{Q}g^{2}w^{-1}w\,dx\,dt\leq\iint_{Q}g^{2}w^{-1}\,dx\,dt,

it follows that
g\in L^{2}(Q)
.
To derive the Carleman estimate (Theorem
4.2
) for equation (
4.1
), we rely on Theorem
3.2
. Consequently, we first need to establish the Carleman estimate (Theorem
4.1
) for the following equation (
4.2
).
Let
\varphi_{k}
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) denote the solution of the equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}+\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}\varphi_{k})=g_{k}&\text{in }Q_{k},\\
\varphi_{k}=0&\text{on }\partial Q_{k},\\
\varphi_{k}(T)=\varphi_{T}|_{\Omega_{k}}&\text{in }\Omega_{k},\end{cases}

(4.2)

where
\Omega_{k}=(\frac{1}{k},1)
,
Q_{k}=\Omega_{k}\times(0,T)
, and
g_{k}=g|_{\Omega_{k}}
. We note that (
4.2
) represents a uniformly parabolic equation for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We assume
\omega=(a,b)\subset\Omega
with
0<a<b<1
. We select
\eta\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{3}(\Omega)
satisfying

\eta(x)=\begin{cases}x^{2-\alpha}&\text{for }x\in(0,\frac{2a+b}{3}),\\
>0&\text{for }x\in(\frac{2a+b}{3},\frac{a+2b}{3}),\\
(1-x)x^{-\alpha}&\text{for }x\in(\frac{a+2b}{3},1).\end{cases}

(4.3)

We define

\Theta=\frac{1}{[t(T-t)]^{4}},\quad\xi=\gamma\Theta[-2|\eta|_{\infty}+\eta],

(4.4)

where
|\eta|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|\eta(x)|
and
\gamma>0
is a constant to be specified later. It is straightforward to verify that

\partial_{x}\xi=\gamma\Theta(\partial_{x}\eta).

Let

v_{k}=e^{s\xi}\varphi_{k}\ \Leftrightarrow{}\ \varphi_{k}=e^{-s\xi}v_{k}.

(4.5)

Then, we have the following properties (noting that
\xi
is independent of
k\in\mathbb{N}
):

i)
v_{k}=\partial_{x}v_{k}=0
at
t=0
and
t=T
;

ii)
v_{k}=0
on
\partial Q_{k}
.

By direct computation, we obtain

e^{s\xi}g=P_{1}v_{k}+P_{2}v_{k},

where

\begin{split}P_{1}v_{k}&=\sum_{i=1}^{3}P_{1i}v_{k}=\partial_{t}v_{k}-2sw(\partial_{x}v_{k})(\partial_{x}\xi)-sv_{k}\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}\xi),\\
P_{2}v_{k}&=\sum_{i=1}^{3}P_{2i}v_{k}=\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}v_{k})-sv_{k}\partial_{t}\xi+s^{2}v_{k}w(\partial_{x}\xi)^{2}.\end{split}

(4.6)

We now proceed to compute
(P_{1}v_{k},P_{2}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}
term-by-term as presented in (
4.6
). It is important to note that (
4.2
) represents a uniform parabolic equation, thereby ensuring the meaningfulness of the second derivatives involved.
This is split into several steps.

Step 1: Computation of
(P_{11}v_{k},P_{21}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}
.

Given i) and the condition
\partial_{t}v_{k}=0
on
\partial Q_{k}
, we derive:

\begin{split}&(P_{11}v_{k},P_{21}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}\\
&=\iint_{Q_{k}}(\partial_{t}v_{k})\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}v_{k})\,dx\,dt=-\iint_{Q_{k}}w(\partial_{x}v_{k})\partial_{x}(\partial_{t}v_{k})\,dx\,dt\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}\partial_{t}\left[w(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}\right]\,dx\,dt=0.\end{split}

(4.7)

Step 2: Computation of
(P_{12}v_{k},P_{21}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}
.

We calculate as follows:

\begin{split}&(P_{12}v_{k},P_{21}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}\\
&=-2s\iint_{Q_{k}}w(\partial_{x}v_{k})(\partial_{x}\xi)\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}v_{k})\,dx\,dt\\
&={{2s\int_{0}^{T}w^{2}(k^{-1})(\partial_{x}\xi)(k^{-1},t)(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}(k^{-1},t)\,dt-2s\int_{0}^{T}w^{2}(1)(\partial_{x}\xi)(1,t)(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}(1,t)\,dt}}\\
&\quad+2s\iint_{Q_{k}}w(\partial_{x}v_{k})\partial_{x}\big[w(\partial_{x}v_{k})(\partial_{x}\xi)\big]\,dx\,dt\\
&={{s\int_{0}^{T}w^{2}(k^{-1})(\partial_{x}\xi)(k^{-1},t)(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}(k^{-1},t)\,dt-s\int_{0}^{T}w^{2}(1)(\partial_{x}\xi)(1,t)(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}(1,t)\,dt}}\\
&\quad+s\iint_{Q_{k}}w^{2}(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}\xi)\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(4.8)

Step 3: Computation of
(P_{13}v_{k},P_{21}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}
.

Using ii), we obtain:

\begin{split}(P_{13}v_{k},P_{21}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}&=-s\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}\big[\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}\xi)\big]\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}v_{k})\,dx\,dt\\
&=s\iint_{Q_{k}}w(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}\xi)\,dx\,dt+s\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}w(\partial_{x}v_{k})\partial_{x}^{2}(w\partial_{x}\xi)\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(4.9)

Step 4: Computation of
(P_{1}v_{k},P_{22}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}
.

Combining i) and ii), we have:

\begin{split}&(P_{1}v_{k},P_{22}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}\\
&=\frac{s}{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}\xi\,dx\,dt-s^{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}^{2}\partial_{x}\big[w(\partial_{t}\xi)(\partial_{x}\xi)\big]\,dx\,dt+s^{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}^{2}(\partial_{t}\xi)\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}\xi)\,dx\,dt\\
&=\frac{s}{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}\xi\,dx\,dt-s^{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}^{2}w(\partial_{x}\xi)\partial_{xt}\xi\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(4.10)

Step 5: Computation of
(P_{11}v_{k},P_{23}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}
.

From i), we derive

\begin{split}(P_{11}v_{k},P_{23}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}&=s^{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}w(\partial_{x}\xi)^{2}\partial_{t}v_{k}\,dx\,dt=\frac{s^{2}}{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}w(\partial_{x}\xi)^{2}\partial_{t}v_{k}^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&=-s^{2}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}^{2}w(\partial_{x}\xi)\partial_{xt}\xi\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(4.11)

Step 6: Computation of
(P_{12}v_{k}+P_{13}v_{k},P_{23}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}
.

We obtain

\begin{split}&(P_{12}v_{k}+P_{13}v_{k},P_{23}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}\\
&=-2s^{3}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}w^{2}(\partial_{x}v_{k})(\partial_{x}\xi)^{3}\,dx\,dt-s^{3}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}^{2}w(\partial_{x}\xi)^{2}\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}\xi)\,dx\,dt\\
&=s^{3}\iint_{Q_{k}}v_{k}^{2}w(\partial_{x}\xi)\partial_{x}\big[w(\partial_{x}\xi)^{2}\big]\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(4.12)

4.1
Estimation

Let us define the following regions:

Q_{k}^{1}=\left(0,\frac{2a+b}{3}\right)\times(0,T),\quad Q_{k}^{3}=\left(\frac{a+2b}{3},1\right)\times(0,T),\quad Q_{k}^{2}=Q_{k}\setminus(Q_{k}^{1}\cup Q_{k}^{3}).

The estimation is split into several steps.

Step 1.
It can be readily verified that the first order partial derivative of
\xi
defined by (
4.4
) with respect to
x
is given by

\partial_{x}\xi=\begin{cases}(2-\alpha)\gamma\Theta x^{1-\alpha},&x\in Q_{k}^{1},\\
\gamma\Theta\left[-x^{-\alpha}+(-\alpha)(1-x)x^{-\alpha-1}\right],&x\in Q_{k}^{3},\end{cases}

the second order partial derivative of
\xi
with respect to
x
is

\partial_{x}^{2}\xi=\begin{cases}(2-\alpha)(1-\alpha)\gamma\Theta x^{-\alpha},&x\in Q_{k}^{1},\\
\gamma\Theta\left[2\alpha x^{-\alpha-1}+\alpha(\alpha+1)(1-x)x^{-\alpha-2}\right],&x\in Q_{k}^{3},\end{cases}

the partial derivative of
w\partial_{x}\xi
with respect to
x
is

\partial_{x}(w\partial_{x}\xi)=\begin{cases}(2-\alpha)\gamma\Theta,&x\in Q_{k}^{1},\\
\gamma\Theta\left[\alpha x^{-1}+\alpha(1-x)x^{-2}\right],&x\in Q_{k}^{3},\end{cases}

and the second order partial derivative of
w\partial_{x}\xi
with respect to
x
is

\partial_{x}^{2}(w\partial_{x}\xi)=\begin{cases}0,&x\in Q_{k}^{1},\\
\gamma\Theta\left[-2\alpha x^{-3}\right],&x\in Q_{k}^{3}.\end{cases}

Combining these results with
\partial_{x}\xi(1,t)=-\gamma\Theta
, we obtain

\begin{split}E_{1}(Q_{k})&=(P_{1}v_{k},P_{21}v_{k})_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}\\
&\geq(2-\alpha)^{2}s\gamma\iint_{Q_{k}^{1}}\Theta x^{\alpha}(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+Cs\gamma\iint_{Q_{k}^{3}}\Theta(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad-Cs\gamma\iint_{Q_{k}^{2}}\Theta(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-Cs\gamma\iint_{Q_{k}^{2}\cup Q_{k}^{3}}\Theta|v_{k}\partial_{x}v_{k}|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\geq(2-\alpha)^{2}s\gamma\iint_{Q_{k}^{1}}\Theta x^{\alpha}(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+Cs\gamma\iint_{Q_{k}^{3}}\Theta(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad-Cs\gamma\iint_{Q_{k}^{2}}\Theta(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-Cs\iint_{Q_{k}^{2}\cup Q_{k}^{3}}\Theta(\partial_{x}v_{k})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-Cs\gamma^{2}\iint_{Q_{k}^{2}\cup Q_{k}^{3}}\Theta v_{k}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

where the positive constants
C
depend solely on
\alpha,a
, and

a(x)=x^{\alpha}
), namely,

\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha-2}\varphi^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha}(\partial_{x}\varphi)^{2}\,dx,

where
C>0
depends on
\alpha
only, we derive:

\begin{split}s\iint_{Q}\Theta\varphi^{2}e^{2s\xi}\,dx\,dt&\leq s\iint_{Q}\Theta x^{\alpha-2}\varphi^{2}e^{2s\xi}\,dx\,dt\leq s\iint_{Q}\Theta x^{\alpha}\left[\partial_{x}(\varphi e^{s\xi})\right]^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&=s\iint_{Q}\Theta x^{\alpha}\left[e^{s\xi}\partial_{x}\varphi+se^{s\xi}\varphi\partial_{x}\xi\right]^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq 2s\iint_{Q}\Theta x^{\alpha}(\partial_{x}\varphi)^{2}e^{2s\xi}\,dx\,dt+Cs^{3}\gamma_{0}^{2}\iint_{Q}\Theta^{3}x^{2-\alpha}\varphi^{2}e^{2s\xi}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq Cs^{3}\gamma_{0}^{2}\iint_{\omega\times(0,T)}\Theta^{3}\varphi^{2}e^{2s\xi}\,dx\,dt,\end{split}

where the third inequality uses
x^{\alpha}(\partial_{x}\xi)^{2}\leq C\gamma_{0}^{2}\Theta^{2}x^{2-\alpha}
for some constant
C>0
depending only on
\alpha
, and the last inequality employs (4.18) in Theorem 4.2 with
g=0
. By choosing
s_{0}\geq 1
, we obtain:

\iint_{Q}\Theta\varphi^{2}e^{2s_{0}\xi}\,dx\,dt\leq C\iint_{Q}\Theta^{3}\varphi^{2}e^{2s_{0}\xi}\,dx\,dt.

Here, the constant
C>0
depends solely on
\alpha
,
\gamma_{0}
, and
T
. Utilizing the inequalities:

e^{2s_{0}\xi}\Theta\geq e^{-C(1+\frac{1}{T})}\frac{1}{T^{8}}\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1)\times\left(\frac{T}{4},\frac{3T}{4}\right),

and

e^{2s_{0}\xi}\Theta^{3}\leq e^{-C(1+\frac{1}{T})}\frac{1}{T^{8}}\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1)\times(0,T),

we deduce:

\iint_{(0,1)\times(\frac{T}{4},\frac{3T}{4})}|\varphi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\iint_{\omega\times(0,T)}|\varphi|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

where the constant
C>0
depends solely on
\alpha
,
a
,
b
,
\gamma_{0}
, and
T
. Finally, since:

\|\varphi(0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{T}\int_{\frac{T}{4}}^{\frac{3T}{4}}\|\varphi(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\,dt,

it follows that:

\|\varphi(0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq C\iint_{\omega\times(0,T)}|\varphi|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

(4.19)

which represents the observability inequality, where the constant
C>0
depends on
\alpha
,
a
,
b
,
\gamma_{0}
, and
T
only. As established in
[
1
, Proposition 4.1, Section 4]
, the following result holds firmly.

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we apply for the first time the shape design method to approximate solutions for both degenerate elliptic and parabolic equations. As a practical demonstration, we employ this method to approximate the Carleman estimate for a one-dimensional degenerate parabolic equation. Through this Carleman estimate, we derive the observability of the degenerate parabolic equation in a novel manner. This novel methodology paves the way for future research aimed at addressing the Carleman estimate or observability of higher-dimensional degenerate parabolic equations.
