1.
Introduction

This paper continues our previous work
[
XYY26+
]
, where we introduced the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow
and established its convergence under various
differential-geometric
conditions, with applications to solving general prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills tensor equations. Here, building on these results, we prove convergence of the flow under
algebraic-geometric
conditions and thereby obtain solutions to the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills tensor equations in this new setting.

As discussed in our previous works
[
WYY26+
,
FWYY26+
,
WYY26b+
,
XYY26+
]
, the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation

(1.1)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)=P

serves as a vector bundle analogue of the Calabi–Yau theorem (
[
Cal57
,
Yau78
]
) for prescribed Ricci curvatures. This equation is closely related to the Hermitian-Einstein equation and extends the classical Hermitian-Einstein existence theory to the setting of fully prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills curvature tensors. We anticipate that this equation will emerge as a crucial bridge between the geometry of vector bundles and the broader landscape of Kähler and Hermitian geometry.

Let’s recall the classical Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem (
[
Don85
,
UY86
,
Don87
]
) for stable vector bundles (see also the Hermitian analogue in
[
LY86
]
):

Theorem A
.

For a stable holomorphic vector bundle
E
over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
, there exists a unique Hermitian-Einstein metric
h
on
E
up to scaling, satisfying

(1.2)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\lambda_{E}\cdot h,

where
R^{h}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
is the Chern curvature tensor of
(E,h)
, and

(1.3)

\lambda_{E}=\frac{2\pi n\int_{M}c_{1}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}}{\mathrm{{\rm rk}}(E)\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}.

Slope stability is a fundamental algebraic condition: for every non-zero proper coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset E
, the following inequality holds:

(1.4)

\frac{\deg_{\omega_{g}}(\mathcal{F})}{\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F})}<\frac{\deg_{\omega_{g}}(E)}{\operatorname{rk}(E)}.

Here the degree is defined by

(1.5)

\deg_{\omega_{g}}(\mathcal{F})=2\pi n\int_{M}c^{\mathrm{BC}}_{1}(\mathcal{F})\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1},

where
c^{\mathrm{BC}}_{1}({\mathcal{F}})
denotes the first Bott–Chern class in
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(M,\mathbb{C})
, coinciding with the usual first Chern class
c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})
when
M
is Kähler. We refer to
[
Kob87
]
for more details.
Stable bundles and Hermitian-Einstein metrics have become a central topic in differential geometry and mathematical physics; see, for instance,
[
NS65
,
Siu87
,
Sim88
,
Sim92
,
BGP96
,
Li00
,
DW04
,

,
JW18
,
Fu19
,
CS20
,
DS21
,
Li21
,
Wu23
,
CW24
,
MPS25
]
.

The first main result of this paper is an analogue of Theorem
A
,
established via the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow

(1.6)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\!\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)+P,\\[10.0pt]
h(0)=h_{0}.\end{cases}

We solve the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation under natural algebraic-geometric conditions, in contrast to earlier differential-geometric assumptions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold with

\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0
,
and let
E\to M
be a holomorphic vector bundle. Suppose that for every proper coherent subsheaf

\mathcal{F}\subset E
, the inequality

(1.7)

\deg_{\omega_{g}}(\mathcal{F})<\deg_{\omega_{g}}(E)

holds. Then, for any initial Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E

and any positive-definite Hermitian tensor

P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
,
the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow (
1.6
)
admits a unique global smooth solution on
[0,\infty)
.
Moreover,
h(t)
converges smoothly as
t\to\infty

to a Hermitian metric
h_{\infty}
on
E
satisfying

(1.8)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=P.

By Serre duality, the corresponding statements of Theorem
1.1
remain valid for negative bundles; see
[
WYY26+
]
. We outline the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The central ingredient is a uniform
C^{0}
-estimate for
h_{t}
. By applying the parabolic comparison principle established in our previous paper
[
XYY26+
]
, we obtain the uniform lower bound

C_{1}h_{0}\leq h_{t}.

Conversely, a uniform upper bound
h_{t}\leq C_{2}h_{0}
would imply convergence of the flow. It therefore remains to rule out the case of unbounded metrics. Assuming no such uniform bound exists, we combine several a priori estimates along the flow (
1.6
) with the method of Uhlenbeck–Yau to extract a convergent subsequence of a normalization
\widetilde{H}_{t}
of Hermitian matrices
H_{t}=h_{t}\cdot h_{0}^{-1}
. This limiting process yields a proper coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset E
satisfying

\deg_{\omega_{g}}(\mathcal{F})\geq\deg_{\omega_{g}}(E),

contradicting condition (
1.7
).

It is well-known that on a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
with

\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0
, if there exists a Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)>0,

then the condition (
1.7
) is satisfied. As an
immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
and

[
XYY26+
, Theorem 1.2]
, we obtain:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let

(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold with

{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0
, and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over

M
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

For every proper
coherent subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}\subset E
, the following inequality holds:

\deg_{\omega_{g}}({\mathcal{F}})<\deg_{\omega_{g}}(E).

(2)

For any positive (resp. quasi-positive) Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a Hermitian metric
h
on
E
satisfying

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)=P.

Moreover, Theorem
1.1
admits the following algebro-geometric formulation. As a consequence, we obtain a criterion for the solvability of the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation with respect to an arbitrary Hermitian metric form
\omega
on
M
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact complex manifold
M
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

For any nonzero coherent quotient sheaf
{\mathcal{Q}}
of
E
, the determinant line bundle
\det{\mathcal{Q}}=:\det({\mathcal{Q}}^{**})
is pseudo-effective but not unitary flat.

(2)

For any Hermitian metric
\omega
on
M
, and any (quasi-)positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=P.

Condition
(1)
in Theorem
1.3
is commonly encountered in algebraic geometry. It is satisfied, for example, if

(1)

E
is ample (
[
Har66
]
);

(2)

E
is strictly nef and
M
is a weak Fano manifold (
[
LOY19
,
LOY21
,
LOY24
]
).

Combining Theorem
1.3
with recent results of W.-H. Ou
[
Ou23
]
, we obtain existence of solutions to the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills tensor equation on Fano manifolds and certain rationally connected manifolds. This may be viewed as a Fano-manifold analogue of the classical Calabi-Yau theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M
be
a rationally connected projective manifold with
-K_{M}
nef.
Then for any Hermitian metric form
\omega
on
M
, and for any (quasi-)positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma\left(M,T^{*1,0}M\otimes T^{*0,1}M\right)
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h
on
T^{1,0}M
such that

(1.9)

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=P.

In particular, the same conclusion holds if
M
is a projective manifold with
-K_{M}
strictly nef, or if
M
is a Fano manifold.

Building on Theorem
1.1
,
[
Yang18
, Corollary 1.5]
,
[
XYY26+
, Theorem 1.2]
, and the recent result of Li-Zhang-Zhang
[
LZZ25
, Theorem 1.7]
, we obtain:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
M
be a compact Kähler manifold. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

M
is a rationally connected projective manifold.

(2)

there exists a Hermitian metric form
\omega
on
M
and a Hermitian metric tensor
h_{0}
on
T^{1,0}M
such that

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)>0.

(3)

there exists a Hermitian metric form
\omega
on
M
such that: for any (quasi-)positive Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma\left(M,T^{*1,0}M\otimes T^{*0,1}M\right)
, there is a unique Hermitian metric tensor
h
on
T^{1,0}M
such that

(1.10)

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=P.

Let
M
be a rationally connected projective manifold,
and let
\widetilde{M}\to M
be a sufficiently high blow-up. Then there exists a Kähler metric
\omega
on
\widetilde{M}
such that for any positive Hermitian tensor

) is not solvable. This highlights a key difference between Theorem

and Corollary
1.5

It is well-known (e.g.,
[
Har66
]
) that ample vector bundles satisfy the condition (
1.7
). Consequently, we obtain:

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
E
be an ample vector bundle over a compact complex manifold
M
. Then for any (quasi-)positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})

\omega
on
M
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h
on
E
satisfying

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)=P.

In particular, every ample vector bundle
E
over a compact Riemann surface
M
admits a Hermitian metric with Griffiths-positive curvature of the prescribed form
\omega\otimes P
, where
\omega
is a Kähler metric on
M
and
P
is a positive-definite Hermitian tensor in

It is also well-known that strictly nef vector bundles arise as extensions of ample bundles. Thanks to
[

, Theorem 1.5]
and
[

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
E
be a strictly nef vector bundle over a Fano manifold
M
. Then for any (quasi-)positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
and for any Hermitian metric
\omega
on
M
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h
on

satisfying

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)=P.

Remark 1.8
.

The Griffiths conjecture asserts that every ample vector bundle over a compact complex manifold admits a Griffiths-positive Hermitian metric. It is the vector bundle analogue of Kodaira’s embedding theorem, which states that any ample line bundle admits a smooth metric with positive curvature. The conjecture was settled for compact Riemann surfaces through several independent approaches:

•

Campana and Flenner
[
CF90
]
proved it using base-change techniques for algebraic curves (see also
[
Ume73
]
).

•

Li, Zhang, and Zhang
[
LZZ25
]
established the result by employing the refined continuity method of Uhlenbeck-Yau
[
UY86
]
. They established that the existence of a Hermitian metric with positive Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor is equivalent to a condition analogous to (
1.7
), which is a special case of Corollary
1.2
.

•

Recently, Murakami
[
Mur26
]
provided an alternative proof utilizing the Demailly system proposed in
[
Dem21
]
. See also the approaches in
[
Pin21
]
.

•

Our proof provides a complete picture of the Griffiths-positive curvature tensor by constructing a Hermitian metric with prescribed curvature.

The Griffiths conjecture has also been investigated in greater generality; see, e.g., Berndtsson
[
Ber09
]
, as well as Liu, Sun, and the second-named author
[
LSY13
]
. More recently, an RC‑positivity analogue of the Griffiths conjecture was proposed in
[
Yang18
]
and
[
Yang20
]
, which also serves as the primary motivation for this series of works.

Remark 1.9
.

The strategy employed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
carries over, with appropriate changes, to the twisted Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow:

(1.11)

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)+\lambda h+P.

This flow approach generalizes the results in
[
WYY26b+
]
.

In this section, we recall some background material and key results from our previous work
[
XYY26+
]
for the reader’s convenience.
Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
.
We say that
(M,\omega_{g})
is a
Gauduchon manifold
if

\partial\overline{\partial}\,\omega_{g}^{\,n-1}=0.

It is well known that, for any Hermitian metric
\omega
on
M
,
there exists a smooth function
f
such that

\omega_{g}=e^{f}\omega

satisfies
\partial\overline{\partial}\,\omega_{g}^{\,n-1}=0
(
[
Gau84
]
). Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
of rank
r
. We use the following notations:

•

\mathrm{Herm}(E)
: the space of Hermitian tensors in

\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
;

•

\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
: the subspace consisting of
positive-definite Hermitian tensors;

•

\mathrm{Herm}^{\geq 0}(E)
: the subspace consisting of
non-negative Hermitian tensors.

Given a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
, the Chern connection of

(E,h)
is denoted by
\nabla^{h}
. We shall use the natural
decomposition
\nabla^{h}={\partial}^{h}+{\overline{\partial}}
, where
{\partial}^{h}
is the

(1,0)
part and
{\overline{\partial}}
is the
(0,1)
part. Let
R^{h}
be the Chern
curvature tensor of
(E,h)
. In local holomorphic coordinates

\{z^{i}\}
of
M
and local holomorphic basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E
,

(2.1)

R^{h}=R_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}^{\beta}\in\Gamma\left(M,\wedge^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}\right).

The
Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor

S^{h}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
of
(E,h)
is defined as

(2.2)

S^{h}:=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\left(g^{i\overline{j}}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right)e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}^{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}).

By using the Hermitian
h
, there
are natural lifts of
R^{h}
and
S^{h}
:

(2.3)

\Theta^{h}=R^{h}\cdot h^{-1}=R_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes{e}_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E),

and

(2.4)

K^{h}=S^{h}\cdot h^{-1}=\left(g^{i\overline{j}}R_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}\right)e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

The Chern scalar curvature
s_{h}
is defined as

(2.5)

s_{h}=\mathrm{tr}_{E}K^{h}=\mathrm{tr}_{h}S^{h}=g^{i\overline{j}}h^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}R_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}^{h}.

The following transformation formula for the

3.
Convergence under the condition
h_{t}\leq Ch_{0}

In this section, we establish several general results concerning the prescribed Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow (
1.6
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(E,h_{0})
be a Hermitian vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold

(M,\omega_{g})
. For any
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{\geq 0}(E)
, the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.6
) exists for all
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
[0,T_{\max})
be the maximal existence interval of the flow (
1.6
). Let

(3.1)

u=|S^{h_{t}}-P|_{h_{t}}^{2}.

Since
P\geq 0
, Corollary
2.3
gives

(3.2)

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)u\leq 0.

By the parabolic maximum principle, for any
t\in[0,T_{\max})
,

(3.3)

\sup_{M}|S^{h_{t}}-P|_{h_{t}}\leq C_{4},

Here
C_{4}=C_{4}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)=\sup_{M}|S^{h_{0}}-P|_{h_{0}}
. Since
{\partial}_{t}h_{t}=-(S^{h_{t}}-P)
, for every
x\in M
and
v\in E_{x}\setminus\{0\}
,

(3.4)

\left|\frac{d}{dt}\log h_{t}(v,\overline{v})\right|\leq C_{4}.

If
T_{\max}<\infty
, then

(3.5)

e^{-C_{4}T_{\max}}h_{0}\leq h_{t}\leq e^{C_{4}T_{\max}}h_{0}\quad\text{on }[0,T_{\max}).

Together with (
3.3
), Theorem
2.5
extends the solution beyond
T_{\max}
, a contradiction. Hence
T_{\max}=\infty
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(E,h_{0})
be a Hermitian vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold

(M,\omega_{g})
and
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
, the following estimates hold along the flow (
1.6
):

(3.6)

Ch_{0}\leq h_{t},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{M}\left|\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{t}}\right)\right|_{h_{t}}\leq C.

Proof.

Choose
C_{1}=C_{1}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)\in(0,1)
so small that

C_{1}S^{h_{0}}<P
. The stationary metric
\ell_{t}\equiv C_{1}h_{0}
satisfies

(3.7)

\frac{{\partial}\ell_{t}}{{\partial}t}+S^{\ell_{t}}-P=C_{1}S^{h_{0}}-P<0,\qquad\ell_{0}<h_{0}.

The parabolic comparison principle in Theorem
2.6
asserts that

(3.8)

C_{1}h_{0}\leq h_{t}

holds for all
t\geq 0
. This is equivalent to the estimate

h_{t}^{-1}\leq C_{1}^{-1}h_{0}^{-1}.

Together with (
3.3
), this gives

(3.9)

\left|\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{t}}\right)\right|_{h_{t}}=|S^{h_{t}}|_{h_{t}}\leq|S^{h_{t}}-P|_{h_{t}}+|P|_{h_{t}}\leq C_{4}+C_{1}^{-1}\sup_{M}|P|_{h_{0}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold

(M,\omega_{g})
. Let
h_{0}
be a Hermitian metric on
E
and
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
.
If there exists a uniform constant
C_{1}>0
such that the following estimate holds along the flow (
1.6
)

(3.10)

h_{t}\leq C_{1}h_{0},

then the flow (
1.6
) converges smoothly to a Hermitian metric
h_{\infty}
on
E
satisfying

(3.11)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=P.

Proof.

The long-time existence follows from Proposition
3.1
. Let

(3.12)

C_{2}:=\min_{x\in M}\lambda_{\min}(Ph_{0}^{-1})(x)>0.

The upper bound (
3.10
) implies

(3.13)

\lambda_{\min}(Ph_{t}^{-1})=\min_{v\neq 0}\frac{P(v,\overline{v})}{h_{t}(v,\overline{v})}\geq C_{1}^{-1}C_{2}.

For
u=|S^{h_{t}}-P|_{h_{t}}^{2}
, Corollary
2.3
gives

(3.14)

\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)u+2C_{1}^{-1}C_{2}u\leq 0.

By comparison with the corresponding ODE, for all
t\geq 0
,

(3.15)

\sup_{M}u(\bullet,t)\leq e^{-2C_{1}^{-1}C_{2}t}\sup_{M}u(\bullet,0).

In particular,

(3.16)

\left|S^{h_{t}}-P\right|_{h_{t}}\leq C_{3}e^{-C_{4}t},

where
C_{3}=C_{3}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)=\sup_{M}\left|S^{h_{0}}-P\right|_{h_{0}}
and
C_{4}=C_{4}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,C_{1})
. Moreover, for every
x\in M
and
v\in E_{x}\setminus\{0\}
,

(3.17)

\left|\frac{d}{dt}\log h_{t}(v,\overline{v})\right|\leq C_{3}e^{-C_{4}t}.

Integrating over
[0,\infty)
, one has the following estimates for all
t\geq 0

(3.18)

C_{5}^{-1}h_{0}\leq h_{t}\leq C_{5}h_{0},

where
C_{5}=C_{5}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,C_{1})>1
. Since
\partial_{t}h_{t}=-(S^{h_{t}}-P)
, estimates (
3.16
) and (
3.18
) imply

(3.19)

\left\|\frac{\partial h_{t}}{\partial t}\right\|_{C^{0}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C_{6}e^{-C_{7}t}.

Thus, for
t\geq s\geq 0
,

(3.20)

\|h_{t}-h_{s}\|_{C^{0};h_{0}}\leq\int_{s}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial h_{\sigma}}{\partial\sigma}\right\|_{C^{0}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}d\sigma\leq 2C_{6}e^{-C_{7}s}.

Hence
h_{t}
converges uniformly to a continuous Hermitian tensor
h_{\infty}
. Taking the limit in (
3.18
), we conclude that
h_{\infty}
is a continuous Hermitian metric on
E
.
Finally, utilizing (
3.16
) and (
3.18
), Theorem
2.4
implies that for every integer
m\geq 0
, there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}_{m}=\widetilde{C}_{m}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,C_{6},C_{7})
such that for all
t\geq 1
,

(3.21)

\|h_{t}\|_{C^{m}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{m}.

The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem implies that every sequence
t_{i}\to\infty
admits a subsequence converging in
C^{\infty}
to a smooth Hermitian metric. Since we have already established
C^{0}
convergence, the limit must be
h_{\infty}
. Thus, the entire flow converges to
h_{\infty}
in
C^{\infty}
. Passing to the limit in (
3.16
) yields

(3.22)

S^{h_{\infty}}=P,

which is equivalent to
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h_{\infty}})=P
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The blow-up analysis along the flow

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold, and
(E,h_{0})
be a Hermitian vector bundle on
M
. Suppose that
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
.
Let
h_{t}
be the solution to the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.6
) defined for
0\leq t<\infty
.
We define

(4.1)

H_{t}=h_{t}\cdot h_{0}^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

Let

(4.2)

\Lambda_{t}:=\sup_{M}\lambda_{\max}(H_{t})

denote the supremum over
M
of the largest eigenvalue of
H_{t}
. It is well known that the eigenvalues of an endomorphism are invariant under a change of basis, and hence are independent of the choice of Hermitian metric. Define

(4.3)

\widetilde{H}_{t}:=\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\cdot H_{t}.

Then it is clear that
\widetilde{H}_{t}\leq\mathrm{Id}_{E}
with respect to both
h_{0}
and
h_{t}
. The following lemma will be useful for later computations.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(E,h_{0})
be a Hermitian holomorphic bundle over a complex manifold
M
.
If
H_{t}\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{0})\subset\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is a smooth family of positive
h_{0}
-Hermitian endomorphisms on
E
, then for any
\sigma\geq 0
,

(4.4)

\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\partial_{t}H_{t}\right)\cdot H_{t}^{-1}\cdot\partial_{t}H_{t}^{\sigma}\right)\geq 0.

Proof.

Fix a point
x\in M
and choose an
h_{0}
-orthonormal basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E_{x}
such that

H_{t}(x)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\lambda_{\alpha}\,e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\alpha}.

In this basis, we write

(\partial_{t}H_{t})(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{r}K_{\alpha\beta}\,e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}.

Since
\partial_{t}H_{t}
is also
h_{0}
-Hermitian,

K_{\beta\alpha}=\overline{K_{\alpha\beta}}.

A straightforward computation shows that

(4.5)

\left(\partial_{t}H_{t}^{\sigma}\right)(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta},

where

(4.6)

\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}:=\begin{cases}\sigma\lambda_{\alpha}^{\sigma-1},&\alpha=\beta,\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{\lambda_{\alpha}^{\sigma}-\lambda_{\beta}^{\sigma}}{\lambda_{\alpha}-\lambda_{\beta}},&\alpha\neq\beta,\lambda_{\alpha}\neq\lambda_{\beta},\\[3.0pt]
\sigma\lambda_{\alpha}^{\sigma-1},&\alpha\neq\beta,\lambda_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\beta}.\end{cases}

It is obvious that
\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}>0
. Hence

(4.7)

\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\partial_{t}H_{t}\right)\cdot H_{t}^{-1}\cdot\partial_{t}H_{t}^{\sigma}\right)(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{\beta}^{-1}\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}|K_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\geq 0.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle on a Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
.
Suppose that
h
and
h_{0}
are two Hermitian metrics on
E
and
H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}
. Then for any
\sigma\in(0,1]
, one has

(4.8)

\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}H^{\sigma}\cdot H^{-\sigma/2}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}H^{\sigma}\right)+\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right)\cdot H^{\sigma}\right),

and

(4.9)

\left|{\overline{\partial}}H^{\sigma}\right|_{g,h}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}H^{2\sigma}\right)+\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right)\cdot H^{2\sigma}\right).

Proof.

For any
\rho>0
, Lemma
2.1
gives

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right)\cdot H^{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left\{({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1},H^{\rho}\right\}_{h_{0}}+\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left\{({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1},{\partial}^{h_{0}}H^{\rho}\right\}_{h_{0}}.

Here
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{h_{0}}
denotes contraction induced by
h_{0}
. Since

(4.11)

\left\{({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1},H^{\rho}\right\}_{h_{0}}=\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{\rho-1}\right)=\frac{1}{\rho}{\partial}\mathrm{tr}_{E}(H^{\rho}),

and
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}{\partial}=-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}
on functions, (
4.10
) is reduced to

(4.12)

\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right)\cdot H^{\rho}\right)=-\frac{1}{\rho}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{tr}_{E}(H^{\rho})+Q_{\rho},

where

(4.13)

Q_{\rho}:=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\left\{({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1},{\partial}^{h_{0}}H^{\rho}\right\}_{h_{0}}.

In particular, (
4.8
) is equivalent to the estimate

(4.14)

Q_{\sigma}\geq\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}H^{\sigma}\cdot H^{-\sigma/2}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}

and
(
4.9
) is equivalent to the estimate

(4.15)

Q_{2\sigma}\geq\left|{\overline{\partial}}H^{\sigma}\right|_{g,h}^{2}.

Indeed, we fix
x\in M
and choose an
h_{0}
-unitary basis

\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E_{x}
which diagonalizes
H(x)
:

(4.16)

H(x)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}\lambda_{\alpha}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\alpha},\qquad\lambda_{\alpha}>0.

We also write

(4.17)

\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\right)(x)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}

where
K_{\alpha\beta}\in T_{x}^{*1,0}M
. By
computations in Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

(4.18)

{\partial}^{h_{0}}H^{\sigma}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta},\qquad{\partial}^{h_{0}}H^{2\sigma}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\varphi^{2\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta},

where
\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}
and
\varphi^{2\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}

are defined in (
4.6
). It is easy to see that

(4.19)

\displaystyle Q_{\sigma}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{\beta}^{-1}\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}|K_{\alpha\beta}|_{g}^{2}\geq\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{\beta}^{-\sigma}\left(\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}\right)^{2}|K_{\alpha\beta}|_{g}^{2}=\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}H^{\sigma}\cdot H^{-\sigma/2}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2},

where the inequality follows from the fact that the inequality

(4.20)

0\leq\frac{x^{\sigma}-y^{\sigma}}{x-y}\leq y^{\sigma-1}

holds for any
0<\sigma\leq 1
,
x,y>0
and
x\neq y
.

On the other hand, note that
{\overline{\partial}}H^{\sigma}
is the
h_{0}
-adjoint of
{\partial}^{h_{0}}H^{\sigma}
, i.e.,

(4.21)

\overline{{\partial}}_{i}\left(H^{\sigma}\right)_{\gamma}^{\alpha}=h_{0}^{\alpha\overline{\lambda}}h_{0,\gamma\overline{\mu}}\left(\overline{{\partial}^{h_{0}}_{i}\left(H^{\sigma}\right)_{\lambda}^{\mu}}\right).

It is easy to compute that

(4.22)

\left|{\overline{\partial}}H^{\sigma}\right|_{g,h}^{2}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}^{-1}\left(\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}\right)^{2}|K_{\alpha\beta}|_{g}^{2}.

Hence, one has

(4.23)

\displaystyle Q_{2\sigma}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{\beta}^{-1}\varphi^{2\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}|K_{\alpha\beta}|_{g}^{2}\geq\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}^{-1}\left(\varphi^{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}\right)^{2}|K_{\alpha\beta}|_{g}^{2}=\left|{\overline{\partial}}H^{\sigma}\right|_{g,h}^{2},

where the inequality follows from the fact that the inequality

(4.24)

\frac{x^{2\sigma}-y^{2\sigma}}{x-y}\geq x\left(\frac{x^{\sigma}-y^{\sigma}}{x-y}\right)^{2}

holds for any
0<\sigma\leq 1
,
x,y>0
and
x\neq y
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(E,h_{0})
be a Hermitian vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold

(M,\omega_{g})
and
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
. Then there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)>0
such that for all
\sigma\in(0,1]
, the following estimates hold along the flow (
1.6
):

(4.25)

\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{t}^{\sigma}\cdot H_{t}^{-\sigma/2}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}H_{t}^{\sigma}\right)+C_{1}\mathrm{tr}_{E}H_{t}^{\sigma},

and

(4.26)

\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\leq\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\cdot\widetilde{H}_{t}^{-\sigma/2}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\right)+C_{1}.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)>0
such that for all
t\in[0,+\infty)
,

(4.27)

\left\|\widetilde{H}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\geq C_{2}.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
4.2
with
h=h_{t}
yields

(4.28)

\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{t}^{\sigma}\cdot H_{t}^{-\sigma/2}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}H_{t}^{\sigma}\right)+\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{t}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right)\cdot H_{t}^{\sigma}\right).

Since the tensor
H_{t}^{\sigma}
is self-adjoint
with respect to both
h_{0}
and
h_{t}
,

(4.29)

|H_{t}^{\sigma}|_{h_{t}}=|H_{t}^{\sigma}|_{h_{0}}=\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}H_{t}^{2\sigma}\right)^{1/2}\leq\mathrm{tr}_{E}H_{t}^{\sigma},

and by Proposition
3.2
, there is a constant

C=C(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{t}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right)\cdot H_{t}^{\sigma}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{t}}\right)\right|_{h_{t}}\cdot\left|H_{t}^{\sigma}\right|_{h_{t}}+\left|\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right|_{h_{0}}\cdot\left|H_{t}^{\sigma}\right|_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathrm{tr}_{E}H_{t}^{\sigma}.

Hence, we get

(4.30)

\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{t}^{\sigma}\cdot H_{t}^{-\sigma/2}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}H_{t}^{\sigma}\right)+C\mathrm{tr}_{E}H_{t}^{\sigma}.

Multiplying by
\Lambda_{t}^{-\sigma}
yields

(4.31)

\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\cdot\widetilde{H}_{t}^{-\sigma/2}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\right)+C\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}.

Since
0<\widetilde{H}_{t}\leq\mathrm{Id}_{E}
, we have
\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\leq{\rm rk}(E)
and

\widetilde{H}_{t}^{-\sigma/2}\geq\mathrm{Id}_{E}
. This proves (
4.26
) with

C_{1}=C{\rm rk}(E)
.

Taking
\sigma=1
in (
4.31
), one has

(4.32)

-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}\right)\leq C_{5}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t},

where
C_{5}=C_{5}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)
. Applying the Moser iteration to this scalar inequality yields

(4.33)

\sup_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}\leq C_{6}\left\|\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})},

where
C_{6}=C_{6}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)
. By construction, we have

(4.34)

\sup_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}\geq 1.

Therefore

(4.35)

1\leq C_{6}\left\|\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}.

This proves (
4.27
)
with
C_{2}=\left({\rm rk}(E)C_{6}\right)^{-1}
.
The proof is completed. ∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(E,h_{0})
be a Hermitian vector bundle over a compact Gauduchon manifold

(M,\omega_{g})
and
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
.
Suppose that
h_{t}
is the solution to the flow (
1.6
) defined for
0\leq t<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)
such that for all
t\in[0,+\infty)
and
\sigma\in(0,1]
,

(4.36)

\left\|\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C,

and

(4.37)

\left\|{\overline{\partial}}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\right\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{t})}\leq C.

Proof.

Since
0<\widetilde{H}_{t}\leq\mathrm{Id}_{E}
,

(4.38)

\left\|\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\right\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}^{2}\leq{\rm rk}(E)\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g}).

Integrating inequality (
4.26
) over
M

and using the Gauduchon condition
\partial\overline{\partial}\,\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0
,
we obtain

(4.39)

\bigl\|\partial^{h_{0}}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}^{2}\leq C_{1}\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g}).

Since
\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}
is Hermitian with respect to
h_{0}
, i.e.,
(\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma})^{*}=\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}
,
we have

\overline{\partial}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}=\bigl(\partial^{h_{0}}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\bigr)^{*},

and therefore

(4.40)

\bigl\|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}=\bigl\|\partial^{h_{0}}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}.

Combining (
4.39
) and (
4.40
)
yields a uniform
W^{1,2}
-bound

\|\widetilde{H}_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{W^{1,2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C,\qquad C=C(\omega_{g},h_{0},P).

Set
h=h_{t}
in inequality (
4.9
) of Lemma
4.2

and multiply by
\Lambda_{t}^{-2\sigma}
. Then

(4.41)

\left|{\overline{\partial}}\widetilde{H}^{\sigma}_{t}\right|_{g,h_{t}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{2\sigma}\right)+\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{t}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right)\cdot\widetilde{H}_{t}^{2\sigma}\right).

By Proposition
3.2
,

(4.42)

\left|\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{t}}\right|_{h_{t}}\leq C_{2},

where

C_{2}=C_{2}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P)>0
. Since
\widetilde{H}_{t}^{2\sigma}
is self‑adjoint with respect to both
h_{0}
and
h_{t}
,
and its eigenvalues lie in
(0,1]
, we have

(4.43)

\bigl|\widetilde{H}_{t}^{2\sigma}\bigr|_{h_{0}}=\bigl|\widetilde{H}_{t}^{2\sigma}\bigr|_{h_{t}}\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{rk}(E)}.

Noting that
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)
is fixed,
there exists a constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(\omega_{g},h_{0},P,\operatorname{rk}(E))>0

such that

(4.44)

\left|{\overline{\partial}}\widetilde{H}^{\sigma}_{t}\right|_{g,h_{t}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma}\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{E}\widetilde{H}_{t}^{2\sigma}\right)+C_{3}.

Integrating over
M
and using the Gauduchon condition

\partial\overline{\partial}\,\omega_{g}^{n-1}=0
,
the Laplacian term drops out. Hence

\bigl\|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{H}^{\sigma}_{t}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{t})}^{2}\leq C_{3}\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g}),

and therefore

\bigl\|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{H}^{\sigma}_{t}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{t})}\leq C_{1},

where
C_{1}:=\bigl(C_{3}\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,\omega_{g})\bigr)^{1/2}
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(E,h_{0})
be a Hermitian vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold

(M,\omega_{g})
. For any
P\in\mathrm{Herm}(E)
, if we set

(4.45)

\theta_{t}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{t}}\right)-P\cdot h_{t}^{-1},\quad\mbox{and}\quad V_{t}=P\cdot h_{t}^{-1},

then for all
t\geq 0

(4.46)

\partial_{t}\theta_{t}=g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\theta_{t}-\theta_{t}V_{t},

and

(4.47)

\left({\partial}_{t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\right)\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\theta_{t}^{2}\right)=-2\left|{\overline{\partial}}\theta_{t}\right|_{g,h_{t}}^{2}-2\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\theta_{t}^{2}V_{t}\right)

where
\nabla
is the Chern connection on
\mathrm{End}(E)
induced by
h_{t}
.

Proof.

We use the following notations:

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{t_{i}}}\right)\cdot\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}^{\sigma_{j}}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\leq 0,

and so

(5.11)

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\left[\int_{M}\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}^{\sigma_{j}}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}+\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\cdot\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}^{\sigma_{j}}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\right]\leq 0.

On the other hand, by Theorem
5.2
, the weak convergence in
W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
implies that

(5.12)

\pi=\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{E}-\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}^{\sigma_{j}}\right)

in
L^{2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. In particular,

(5.13)

\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\cdot\left(\pi-\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\right)\omega_{g}^{n}=-\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\cdot\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}^{\sigma_{j}}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

From the weak convergence in Theorem
5.2
, we have

(5.14)

\displaystyle\left\|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\pi\right\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}\pi,{\partial}^{h_{0}}\pi\right)_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}\pi,{\partial}^{h_{0}}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{E}-\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}^{\sigma_{j}}\right)\right)_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}

(5.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\pi\right\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\cdot\limsup_{j\to\infty}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\left\|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}^{\sigma_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}.

This implies

(5.16)

\int_{M}\left|\partial^{h_{0}}\pi\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\int_{M}\left|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}^{\sigma_{j}}\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}.

From (
5.11
), (
5.13
) and (
5.16
), we establish

(5.17)

\int_{M}\left|\partial^{h_{0}}\pi\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}\leq\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\cdot\left(\pi-\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Gauduchon manifold, and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
.
Suppose that for every coherent subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}\subset E
with
{\rm rk}({\mathcal{F}})<{\rm rk}(E)
, the inequality holds:

(5.18)

\deg_{\omega_{g}}({\mathcal{F}})<\deg_{\omega_{g}}(E).

Then, for any Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
and any positive-definite tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow (
1.6
) admits a global smooth solution on
[0,\infty)
. Moreover, the flow converges smoothly as
t\rightarrow\infty
to a Hermitian metric
h_{\infty}
satisfying

(5.19)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h_{\infty}}\right)=P.

Proof.

Case
1
. If

(5.20)

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Lambda_{t}=\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{M}\lambda_{\max}(H_{t})<+\infty,

then there exists a uniform constant
C_{1}>0
such that the following estimate holds along the flow (
1.6
)

(5.21)

h_{t}\leq C_{1}h_{0}.

By Theorem
3.3
, the flow (
1.6
) converges as desired.

Case
2
. Suppose that

(5.22)

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Lambda_{t}=\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{M}\lambda_{\max}(H_{t})=+\infty.

We choose sequences
\{t_{i}\}
and
\{\sigma_{j}\}
as in Theorem
5.2
.
In particular, by Lemma
5.1
, there exists a weakly holomorphic projection
\pi
which represents a coherent subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
of
E
.
Let
\Sigma\subset M
be the analytic subset as in Lemma
5.1
. Then
\Sigma
has measure
0
and
\pi
is smooth and has constant rank on
M\setminus\Sigma
.
Moreover, Theorem
5.2
implies that
\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}\to\widetilde{H}_{\infty}
and
\widetilde{H}_{\infty}^{\sigma_{j}}\to\widetilde{H}
in
L^{2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
.
By choosing a further subsequence, we may assume

(5.23)

\widetilde{H}_{\infty}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\widetilde{H}_{t_{i}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{H}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\widetilde{H}_{\infty}^{\sigma_{j}}

almost everywhere on
M
.
Since
\widetilde{H}_{\infty}
is non-zero in
W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
, it has a strictly positive eigenvalue on a set of positive measure in
M\setminus\Sigma
. We deduce that
{\rm rk}(\widetilde{H})\geq 1
on
M\setminus\Sigma
. Moreover,
from our construction, eigenvalues of
\widetilde{H}
lie in
\{0,1\}
.
This implies

(5.24)

{\rm rk}({\mathcal{F}})={\rm rk}(\pi)={\rm rk}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{E}-\widetilde{H}|_{M\setminus\Sigma}\right)\leq{\rm rk}(E)-1.

By Lemma
5.1
,
\pi|_{M\setminus\Sigma}
coincides with the
h_{0}
-orthogonal projection from
E|_{M\setminus\Sigma}
to
{\mathcal{F}}|_{M\setminus\Sigma}
.
By the second fundamental form computation, the degree of
{\mathcal{F}}
is given by:

(5.25)

\deg_{\omega_{g}}({\mathcal{F}})=\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\cdot\pi\right)\omega_{g}^{n}-\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}\left|B\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\omega_{g}^{n},

where
B=\left(\mathrm{Id}_{E}-\pi\right)\circ\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}\pi\right)\circ\iota
is the second fundamental form of the inclusion
\iota:{\mathcal{F}}\rightarrow E
. A simple calculation shows

(5.26)

|B|^{2}_{g,h_{0}}=|{\partial}^{h_{0}}\pi|^{2}_{g,h_{0}}.

Hence, we obtain

(5.27)

\deg_{\omega_{g}}({\mathcal{F}})=\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\cdot\pi\right)\omega_{g}^{n}-\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}\left|\partial^{h_{0}}\pi\right|_{g,h_{0}}^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}.

By the estimate (
5.8
) in Proposition
5.3
,

(5.28)

\deg_{\omega_{g}}({\mathcal{F}})\geq\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}=\deg_{\omega_{g}}(E),

and this is a contradiction. The proof is completed.
∎

6.
Applications in algebraic geometry

In this section, we discuss applications of Theorem
1.1
in algebraic geometry and prove Theorem
1.3
, Theorem
1.4
, and Corollary
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We recall the classical characterization of pseudo‑effectivity on compact complex manifolds (
[
Buc99
,
Lam99
]
, see also
[
Yang19
, Theorem 3.4]
):
a holomorphic line bundle
L
is pseudo‑effective if and only if

(6.1)

\int_{M}c_{1}^{\mathrm{BC}}(L)\wedge\omega_{\mathrm{G}}^{\,n-1}\;\geq\;0

for every Gauduchon metric
\omega_{\mathrm{G}}
on
M
.
Moreover,
L
is pseudo‑effective but not unitary flat if and only if
the inequality is strict for some (hence every) Gauduchon metric.

Suppose that
(1)
holds. For any Hermitian metric form
\omega
on
M
, let
\omega_{g}=e^{f}\omega
be a Gauduchon metric (
[
Gau84
]
). By
(1)
, for
every proper coherent subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}\subset E
, the following inequality holds:

(6.2)

\deg_{\omega_{g}}(E)-\deg_{\omega_{g}}({\mathcal{F}})=\deg_{\omega_{g}}({\mathcal{Q}})=2\pi n\int_{M}c^{\mathrm{BC}}_{1}({\mathcal{Q}})\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}>0,

where
{\mathcal{Q}}
is the coherent quotient sheaf
{\mathcal{Q}}=E/{\mathcal{F}}
.
By Theorem
1.1
, for any positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P_{1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
such that

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)=P_{1}.

In particular,

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)=e^{f}P_{1}>0.

Hence, by
[
XYY26+
, Theorem 1.2]
, for any (quasi-)positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

(6.3)

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=P.

Conversely, we assume that
(2)
is valid. Then for any Gauduchon metric
\omega_{g}
and for any (quasi-)positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

(6.4)

\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=P.

Since the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors are non-decreasing along quotients, for any nonzero coherent quotient sheaf
{\mathcal{Q}}
of
E
, one has

(6.5)

\deg_{\omega_{g}}({\mathcal{Q}})=2\pi n\int_{M}c^{\mathrm{BC}}_{1}({\mathcal{Q}})\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}>0.

In particular,
{\mathcal{Q}}
is pseudo-effective but not unitary flat.
∎

The proof of Corollary
1.2
proceeds along the same lines as the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By
[
Ou23
, Theorem 1.7]
, if
M
is a rationally connected manifold and
-K_{M}
is nef, then
TM
is generically ample. In particular, by
[
Ou23
, Theorem 1.4]
for any nonzero coherent quotient sheaf
{\mathcal{Q}}
of
TM
, the determinant line bundle
\det{\mathcal{Q}}
is pseudo-effective. The generic ampleness of
TM
implies that there exists a generic curve
C\subset M
such that
\det({\mathcal{Q}})\cdot C>0
. In particular,
\det({\mathcal{Q}})
is not unitary flat. Now the conclusion follows from Theorem
1.3
. On the other hand, if
M
is a projective manifold with
-K_{M}
strictly nef, it is established in
[
LOY19
, Theorem 1.2]
that
M
is rationally connected and
-K_{M}
is nef. This completes the proof.
∎
