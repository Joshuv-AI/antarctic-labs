From fragmented records to living evidence: health system-governed, artificial intelligence-driven, continuously updated real-world clinical data from Truveta
Authors: Hannah A Burkhardt, Sarah J Blach, Srinivasa R Burugapalli, Brianna M G Cartwright, Ryley Martin, Michael Simonov, Sarah Stewart, Grace Turner, Angela L Winegar, Nicholas Stucky
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesReal-world data (RWD) have historically suffered from fragmentation, delayed availability, variable data quality, and limited analytic utility. Truveta developed an artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled data platform to address these longstanding challenges in using RWD for clinical research. This paper describes Truveta’s partnership model, platform design, data scale, and research applications.Materials and MethodsTruveta de-identifies, aggregates, and harmonizes electronic health record (EHR) data for 130 million patients—1 in 3 Americans—from US health systems. The platform links structured and unstructured EHR content with closed claims, mortality, and social determinants of health. Data undergo daily ingestion, normalization to standard ontologies, and de-identification. Advanced AI, including NLP, extracts key clinical concepts from free-text notes, such as physician notes, imaging reports, and pathology narratives, transforming them into standardized variables suitable for large-scale observational research.ResultsTruveta Data comprise over 130 million de-identified patient records that are updated daily and represent diverse geographic regions, care settings, and patient populations in the United States. The data have supported over 100 scientific publications to date; additionally, they support health system participants’ own research interests and patient care insights. Published studies have addressed treatment effectiveness, post-market device surveillance, COVID-19 vaccine safety, and health equity.DiscussionTruveta addresses critical barriers that have hindered the realization of a learning health system. Unlike prior RWD initiatives limited by scope, latency, and vendor dependence, Truveta enables near-real-time, population-scale research grounded in rich clinical data. Its governance model ensures alignment with ethical, privacy, and regulatory standards. By rethinking the role of health systems from passive suppliers into active, incentivized partners, Truveta creates an unprecedented virtuous cycle of data quality and continuous improvement.ConclusionTruveta represents a paradigm shift in real-world evidence generation. By aligning incentives and AI-driven harmonization, it provides a scalable, sustainable infrastructure for continuously updated clinical data. Providing large-scale, high-fidelity EHR data and daily updates, the platform accelerates clinical discovery, policy and public health decision-making, and improved patient outcomes.

Introduction

Adoption of electronic health records (EHRs) has increased from less than 30% of hospitals in 2011 to over 95% by 2021,1 creating a transformational opportunity to improve both clinical care and research. In 2012, randomized controlled trials supported only 10%-20% of clinical decisions,2,3 and in 2026, the FDA announced that 1 robust pivotal trial, rather than 2, is sufficient for approvals of new drugs and treatments when accompanied by real-world evidence (RWE),4 highlighting the substantial opportunity for real-world data (RWD) and RWE to inform clinical decision-making, quality improvement, public health, and regulatory purposes.

Central to contemporary health care is the vision of the learning health system,5 in which routine care data continuously feed back into evidence generation and decision support. The promise of modern data platforms aligning with this vision lies in their ability to capture the real-time telemetry of US health and thereby accelerate discovery in medicine. Beyond the primary purpose of care delivery, they enable the secondary use of EHR data for research by offering rich clinical details including billing data, but also physiological observations, laboratory values, medication prescriptions and administrations, imaging, and notes.2,6 They also provide timeliness and efficiency: dedicated data collection efforts, including registry submissions, become obsolete, resulting in substantially condensed study timelines compared to slow and costly randomized controlled trials.7 At scale, these platforms draw from large, diverse populations, improving inclusivity of groups historically underrepresented in clinical trials.8 Platforms meeting regulatory-grade standards for data quality, clinical accuracy, provenance, and audit readiness facilitate not only research on health equity, adherence, and precision medicine but also post-market safety and effectiveness studies after drug or treatment approval.9

Historical background

Early efforts to leverage EHR data for research, such as regional Health Information Exchanges and institutional clinical data warehouses, demonstrated the feasibility of multi-source data integration but often struggled with sustainability, governance, and data quality challenges.10

The development of Common Data Models marked a turning point, with projects such as the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership (OMOP),11 later evolving into the international Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics community,12,13 and PCORnet, a national federated research network funded by the Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute for comparative effectiveness research. Federally stewarded networks further expanded the landscape, including the FDA Sentinel System,14 which links EHR and claims data from multiple sources for active safety surveillance, and the National Clinical Cohort Collaborative (N3C),15 which rapidly centralized EHR data from more than 90 institutions for pandemic research in a harmonized enclave, and All of Us,16 which builds a participant-consented national cohort with participant governance. Commercial platforms such as TriNetX17 and Epic Cosmos18 have also played a growing role, offering large-scale access to de-identified data for clinical trial optimization and population health research.

Persistent gaps motivating new platforms

Prior experience underscores the importance of addressing limitations inherent in EHR-based research networks. Electronic health records were not designed for research, resulting in biased documentation, inconsistent coding, and fragmented data that hinder interoperability and require extensive normalization.13,19–21 Important clinical details often reside in unstructured notes, necessitating advanced artificial intelligence (AI) and natural language processing (NLP) tools.22 Privacy and security concerns demand rigorous de-identification, while governance models must balance stakeholder needs without compromising patient trust.

There are additional opportunities for the next generation of RWD platforms. Current networks are often constrained in scope—focusing on specific diseases, institutions, or populations—and rely on vendor-dependent architectures. Most struggle with timeliness, providing data that lag by weeks or even months, limiting utility for near-real-time safety monitoring and surveillance; for example, N3C is updated via time-intensive, project-based data expansion cycles.23 Robust integration across EHRs, claims, social determinants of health, and genomics will enable accurate reconstruction of complete patient journeys and comprehensive outcome assessment. Representativeness remains a challenge, with underrepresentation of minority groups, rural populations, and patients with complex comorbidities.19,24–26 Funding models reliant on federal or state support limit sustainability, leaving networks vulnerable to shifting policy priorities.27 Delivering regulatory-grade data quality and provenance is key, but gaps remain in auditability, provenance documentation, and traceability, preventing FDA submissions for safety and labeling decisions.28,29

In response, Truveta was founded in 2020 by a consortium of US health systems to enable high-quality, timely, scalable, sustainable real-world evidence generation that improves patient care and accelerates public health and regulatory decision-making. Here, we describe the design, governance structure, and technical principles underlying the Truveta platform, focusing on how it addresses historical limitations in RWD systems and meets emerging standards for regulatory-grade evidence generation.

Materials and methods

Truveta transforms daily EHR data feeds from member health systems into a unified, research-oriented dataset. Within each health system’s secure environment, clinical data are normalized to the Truveta Data Model (TDM), indexed, and de-identified in compliance with HIPAA. These processes yield longitudinal patient journeys inclusive of diverse populations and geographies. Data are aggregated for rapid iterative querying in Truveta Studio using the cohort definition language Prose. Using precise cohort definitions, full de-identified datasets are generated for analysis within or outside of Truveta (Figure 1).

Architecture of the Truveta platform.

Truveta Data Model

To enable multi-institutional integration of clinical data, Truveta leverages the TDM, a common data model designed to facilitate harmonization of diverse EHR data. Building on the HL7 FHIR standard and inspired by the OMOP common data model, the TDM is developed in collaboration with health systems and research users to capture the clinical nuance required for the scenarios most relevant to Truveta’s research users.

Truveta continuously develops a set of data models for each stage in the harmonization process. The logical data model is informed by the source data structure, as well as by the expertise of clinical data analysts and informaticists, and defines elements along with their relationships and constraints (Figure 2).

The entities in the Truveta Data Model and their relationships to each other.

The physical models used throughout the data pipeline are optimized for different purposes ranging from lossless transformation of source EHR data into syntactically normalized data, mapping of syntactically normalized data to semantically normalized data, and data models optimized for high-performance querying. Research-ready exports use the TDM, the unified relational data model optimized for research analytics.

Transformation scripts are automatically generated from the logical data model, ensuring consistency and auditability. At every stage, embedded metadata on protected health information (PHI) status, de-identification configurations, primary and foreign keys, and uniqueness constraints support automation and ensure data quality and governance.

Data processing and harmonization pipeline

As described below, the daily data processing proceeds along an extract, load, transform (ELT) pipeline, in which raw health system data are first loaded into a secure environment before being transformed. Transformation includes syntactic normalization to align disparate formats and schemas, followed by semantic normalization and concept extraction from clinical notes. Data are made available in Studio after indexing and de-identification (Figure 3).

Data pipeline overview contextualizing the data acquisition, syntactic normalization, semantic normalization, and de-identification steps.

Data acquisition

Electronic health record-agnostic data contributions conform to a member data specification, expanded bi-annually to address the evolving needs of the research community. Truveta’s unique membership relationship with contributing health systems allows ongoing feedback loops that are used to both collaboratively develop the data specification and provide feedback regarding the quality of each member’s data submissions during a comprehensive data profiling process. The specification defines the exact data elements each member will provide and their required format, balancing clinical utility with technical feasibility. Each health system’s data are stored and normalized in their Embassy: a secure, cloud-based environment within the health system’s own infrastructure. Member health system analysts query Epic Clarity/Caboodle, Oracle Health Data Intelligence, and/or their clinical data warehouse and securely deposit files in a landing zone within the Embassy.

The onboarding process includes iterative review of sample and full files. Schema conformance checks and gap analyses are performed using internal automated tooling. Data completeness, referential integrity, and value distribution are validated in close partnership with members. Deviations from the data specification are captured in a member issue log, and resolution is a prerequisite for production data ingestion. Truveta works directly with health system members to investigate data anomalies and address questions about their data. All ingestion and profiling activities are versioned, auditable, and documented. A member portal is provided to member data teams to transparently measure, communicate, and incentivize high-quality source data submissions.

Syntactic normalization

Syntactic normalization is the process of translating health system-specific EHR data into the canonical TDM format. This includes structural harmonization, logical validation, key alignment, and metadata enrichment.

Transformations that convert each source EHR schema to the TDM (Figure 4) are authored by clinical data experts using declarative configurations stored in the data catalog and deployed through a version-controlled pull request workflow. During onboarding, Truveta works with each health system to identify the source tables corresponding to each Truveta table. Because EHR vendor implementations and institution-specific builds vary, bespoke transformation logic maps these source schemas into the TDM.

Example of syntactic normalization: mapping similar fields in 2 different data models to TDM.30

After onboarding, incoming files are continuously transformed into a staging format and validated against source data using completeness and accuracy metrics. For example, medication orders stored in vendor-specific medication or encounter tables are standardized into the canonical TDM medication tables with consistent keys and encounter relationships. Syntactic normalization ensures consistent inputs for downstream operations, including patient linking and semantic normalization.

Every transformation is associated with a specific schema version, and changes are merged to production only after passing validation criteria, ensuring both traceability and stability of the pipeline.

Semantic normalization

Semantic normalization ensures that clinical data from diverse EHR systems can be pooled and consistently interpreted and analyzed across institutions. Source data are mapped to widely adopted standards (Table 1), enabling cohort definitions using common research ontologies.

At the core of semantic normalization is a suite of machine learning models designed to perform large-scale concept normalization. Its modular architecture supports rapid adaptation to new terminologies and health system conventions across diagnoses, medications, labs, and devices. These models outperform traditional rule-based approaches in assigning standard concepts to ambiguous, partial, and noisy input data.31–33

Models are trained using thousands of expert-curated mappings, refined iteratively with human-in-the-loop quality assurance, and continuously benchmarked against gold-standard references. For example, a diagnosis coded as ICD-10 N64.89 | Other specified disorders of breast | may include a physician-entered synonym such as galactocele. Semantic normalization can supplement the ICD code with a more specific SNOMED CT concept (42385006 |Lactocele|), allowing queries using either representation. Where model confidence is low or clinical nuance is high, manual review processes supplement automation to ensure accuracy. Clinical informatics experts author normalization guidelines that define preferred ontologies, mapping precedence rules, and handling of ambiguous or emerging concepts.

Community requests for new mappings are reviewed by the clinical informatics team and incorporated through version-controlled updates. When emerging medications or concepts appear before inclusion in standards such as RxNorm, provisional Truveta concepts may be created until the external ontology is updated.

To ensure performance at least as good as or better than that of human experts, a rigorous model quality assessment process is employed, involving continuous performance monitoring and evaluation to account for data and concept drift.33

Notes extraction

Extraction of structured data from unstructured clinical notes is powered by the Truveta Language Model (TLM),33 which uses transformer-based NLP models with rule-based postprocessing to identify and disambiguate clinical concepts. Key targets include diagnoses, symptoms, measurements, and disease severity scores, with outputs standardized to terminologies such as SNOMED CT, LOINC, and UCUM, ensuring alignment with the semantic normalization framework.

Truveta Language Model is trained on thousands of expert-annotated note segments from diverse specialties, ensuring adaptability to varied documentation styles. Performance is benchmarked against gold-standard annotations, with precision, recall, and F1-score tracked longitudinally to detect model drift. Low-confidence extractions are routed for human review, enabling correction and feedback to continuously refine model behavior. Extracted data are then normalized to concept codes.

Integrated into the Truveta pipeline, extracted concepts are linked to encounters, time-stamped for temporal analyses, and incorporated into longitudinal patient journeys. This enhances cohort definitions, improves outcome ascertainment, and supports studies requiring clinical detail, such as symptom progression or functional status, not consistently available in structured fields.

Data indexing

After syntactic and semantic normalization are complete, data are collated into individual patient journeys: each patient’s encounters, conditions, procedures, medications, labs, and extracted concepts from unstructured clinical notes are linked to create a single patient document. Patient documents are stored in Elasticsearch, a distributed search engine optimized for fast lookup and retrieval. By leveraging data structures such as inverted indexes and term-based mappings, Elasticsearch enables efficient traversal of longitudinal patient records. Concept identifiers are indexed to support rapid filtering and querying. Cohort definitions are highly scalable: performance decreases minimally with query complexity.

De-identification

Each participating health system enters into a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with Truveta under the HIPAA Privacy Rule. This agreement authorizes Truveta to securely store, process, and de-identify PHI on behalf of the member organization. When a researcher initiates a study within Truveta Studio, their cohort definition is securely transmitted to each health system’s Embassy service. This service identifies matching patients using the prebuilt index and performs de-identification locally before any data leaves the health system’s secure perimeter.

To safeguard privacy, all strong identifiers are stripped from the data at the Embassy level. Truveta applies a multilayered de-identification protocol34 that includes event-level date shifting to maintain internal clinical timelines while obscuring absolute dates. Patient identifiers are hashed deterministically to prevent linkage across privacy zones. Additionally, tokenization methods are used to create 1-way cryptographic hashes from combinations of direct identifiers. These identity hashes allow accurate patient matching without risk of re-identification and have undergone independent statistical disclosure risk analysis confirming compliance with HIPAA’s Expert Determination standards.

Truveta also employs k-anonymity strategies, generalizing quasi-identifiers such as ZIP code to broader geographic units (eg, state) and reducing granularity in birthdates or rare attributes to prevent unique identification. Pseudo-identifiers such as race, gender, and year of birth are retained in generalized form to preserve analytic utility.

Truveta supports multiple configurable de-identification protocols. Each applies different weights to pseudo-identifiers to balance privacy protection with analytic utility, allowing tailoring to study-specific granularity needs. Researchers may also conduct aggregate-level real-time analyses, enabling timely insights without exposing patient-level identifiers.

To avoid potential PHI leakage, full notes content is not routinely shared; instead, only the extracted concept codes are shared. However, if notes samples are shared for any purpose, a gold-standard human redaction process is followed.

Quality control

In alignment with FDA’s 2024 guidance on the use of RWD from EHRs and medical claims for regulatory decision-making,28 Truveta ensures its data are both reliable and relevant for high-stakes use. Reliability is achieved through a comprehensive quality management system governing software/AI model development, data processing, and analytics. Standardized operating procedures, audit trails, data version tracking, and regulatory-grade documentation ensure every stage—from accrual to ingestion, normalization, and final dataset creation—meets FDA and HIPAA requirements. Each step is logged, versioned, and auditable, providing a verifiable chain of custody. Provenance documentation clarifies the origin and transformation of each data element, with validation against gold-standard references where feasible. Continuous quality assurance processes with quantitative bias analyses, and automated monitoring safeguard completeness, timeliness, traceability, and validity—positioning Truveta data for FDA submissions in safety surveillance, effectiveness evaluations, and post-approval studies.

Data quality

Truveta’s approach to data quality builds on established frameworks. The foundation consists of Kahn et al.’s dimensions of conformance, completeness, and plausibility, together with Weiskopf et al.’s correctness, concordance, and timeliness.35,36 Unlike traditional frameworks designed for periodic updates, Truveta ingests and harmonizes EHR data daily from a large network of health systems, requiring additional safeguards to address risks unique to near-real-time refreshes, such as sudden drops in completeness, loss of historical values, or discontinuities from pipeline or ELT disruptions.

To the robust foundation of prior frameworks, Truveta therefore adds a novel dimension, stability, defined as the consistency of the foundational data quality attributes across successive daily updates. This extension fills a methodological gap not explicitly addressed in prior frameworks such as PCORnet’s curation process37 or the 3 × 3 DQA guideline.36

Quality is monitored with each daily update through automated metrics collection and alerting infrastructure across the DQ dimensions shown in Table 2. Issues are triaged and remediated in close collaboration between engineering teams and health systems partners. Following corrections, data are revalidated to ensure resolution, establishing a continuous improvement cycle.

Truveta’s health system partners invest in improving pipelines and documentation because high-quality, timely data directly benefits care and research; the financial incentives for good contributions typically exceed the marginal costs of storing and computing data in an Embassy. That partnership model lets Truveta detect issues early, work transparently with system engineers, and accelerate remediation.

For example, when one system appeared to supply far fewer blood pressure, temperature, and weight measurements than peers, Truveta traced the gap to vitals stored in an EHR flowsheet that had not been included in the integration feed; once the source was added, encounter-level coverage of those observations rose from ∼50% to >80%. In another case, quality monitoring revealed missing foreign-key relationships that corrupted linkage between medication administrations and encounters; the system’s engineers corrected an internal integration bug and restored linkage across millions of encounters, recovering longitudinal continuity for downstream analyses.

Data security

Truveta employs a multi-layered approach to data security38 designed to exceed healthcare industry standards for privacy, confidentiality, and regulatory compliance. Data are encrypted at rest and in transit using current industry standards. Security practices are based on the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework,39 a widely recognized standard for healthcare security. Additional certifications held include HITRUST r2, SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, ISO 27018, and ISO 27701.

Enhanced safeguards are applied for data types with elevated sensitivity, such as clinician notes, imaging, and genomic sequences, including isolating sensitive data from other data, risk-based access controls, and ongoing technical and governance reviews.

Truveta Studio

Truveta Studio provides researchers with a secure, web-based analytics environment for defining and analyzing cohorts. Using Prose, a declarative, domain-specific query language, users can rapidly build cohort definitions across an exabyte-scale dataset with immediate feedback on population size and demographics. Prose supports defining computed attributes and aggregates, enabling fast feasibility assessment and study design. The Truveta Library offers thousands of curated, composable clinical definitions and study templates, including regulatory reference definitions such as FDA-guided GLP-1 Receptor Agonist criteria. An AI-powered assistant allows natural language exploration and surfaces relevant code sets and provenance for transparency and auditability.

Once cohorts are finalized, researchers can generate snapshots, which are fixed datasets containing all relevant patient records, metadata, and samples frozen in time for reproducible analysis. A comprehensive data dictionary is available both within Truveta Studio and at Truveta’s documentation site Learn (https://learn.truveta.com), providing standardized definitions and usage guidance aligned with TDM. The analytics environment is powered by Apache Spark, which enables efficient processing of large datasets and supports Python, R, and Spark SQL. Truveta Studio includes common open-source libraries for statistical modeling and visualization, and researchers may also export data to external environments such as SAS, Excel, or custom analytic pipelines. Truveta Studio supports both collaborative and public-facing research. Notebooks, visualizations, and dashboards can be securely shared with internal and external collaborators within an audited environment, and outputs can be exported in portable formats suitable for dissemination or publication. Together, these capabilities provide an end-to-end platform that combines expressive cohort definition, scalable computation, and transparent sharing to accelerate RWE generation.

Governance structure and partners

Owned and governed by its health system partners, Truveta was founded to enable clinicians to generate and share learnings that will ultimately improve patient care. Each participating health system has entered into a BAA with Truveta under the HIPAA Privacy Rule, establishing a long-term commitment to contribute patient medical records on a daily basis. The health system-led Board of Directors provides ongoing governance of Truveta to ensure the company is operating in pursuit of its vision and mission, while leaders from all of Truveta’s health system partners are invited to participate in health system chaired Board of Governors committees where they provide strategic, scientific, and operational input to ensure their data is held to the highest standards of ethics, privacy, security, and data quality with a commitment to accelerating patient care and advancing health equity (Table 3).

Third-party data integration and linking

Because many aspects of a patient’s health journey cannot be fully understood from EHR data alone, Truveta securely integrates third-party data sources to create a more complete and clinically meaningful view. This process is designed to protect patient privacy and comply with HIPAA and IRB requirements by converting personally identifiable information into privacy preserving tokens before linkage. Patients are then matched to third-party records using these tokens, allowing clinical data to be exchanged without exposing identifiers. Truveta supports both industry tokens and its own open-source tokenization method. Truveta integrates open and closed claims, mortality data, and social determinants of health, with the ability to incorporate additional sources such as wearable device data for specific studies.

Results

Network growth

Truveta is a collective of 30 US health systems that provide 18% of patient care in the United States (as a share of hospital beds). The network includes over 20 000 clinics and 900 hospitals across the full United States. The health system members along with hospital counts and US state representation are listed in Table S1.

Data

As of March 2026, Truveta data contained longitudinal patient journeys for more than 130 million de-identified patients (39.3% of the US population) dating back to 2016. Patient journeys consisted of an average of 5.6 years of historical data, with more than 60% of patients contributing at least 1 year of historical data. More than 8.8 billion procedures, 6.1 billion encounters, 4.5 billion medication dispense records, and 161.9 million device records were available. Additionally, 192 million images (CT, MRI, Radiography, Ultrasound, Nuclear Medicine, PET or Mammography) were available to link with the full patient journey. For a subset of patients, unstructured data were extracted from clinical notes and made available in structured format for research alongside the other structured data elements. This included (but was not limited to) common cardiomyopathy measurements such as NYHA class, LVEF and KCCQ scores extracted for 6.3 million patients. Finally, de-identified data from more than 1 million linked mother-child pairs was available. All major geographical regions of the United States were represented (Figure 5) and unlike other RWD aggregators, which source data from fragmented segments across health care (by EHR, specialty, or facility type), Truveta data represent real-world care across diverse care settings.

US population representation: the number of patient lives contained in Truveta Data for that state, divided by the total patients residing in that state per the US census.

Publication output and metrics

As of March 2026, Truveta Data have enabled over 100 publications, including monitoring reports, preprints, conference proceedings, and peer-review publications in major journals. An additional upward trend in both the volume of Truveta-enabled publication output and citation counts occurred in 2023. Collectively, these works have been cited hundreds of times. A list of all work published using Truveta data (peer-review and conference abstracts) is provided in the supplemental materials.

Key research domains

Analysis of RWD use cases across Truveta publications from 2022 to March 2026 reveals notable trends in the application of EHR data to address diverse clinical and public health questions (Figure 6). Early publications were primarily focused on disease surveillance and time-series analyses, particularly in the context of infectious disease trends such as COVID-19. By 2024 and increasingly in 2025, the scope of RWD studies expanded to include a growing number of comparative effectiveness research and healthcare utilization and adherence investigations—many centered on pharmacologic therapies such as GLP-1 receptor agonists, or in the cardiovascular space. Concurrently, there was an uptick in studies leveraging EHR data for descriptive epidemiology and treatment safety monitoring, reflecting a broader engagement with population-level data to inform both clinical practice and policy. These trends suggest a transition from foundational surveillance applications toward more complex, outcomes-focused analyses.

Discussion

Truveta tackles longstanding challenges in the secondary use of EHR data in cleanliness (standardization and data quality), completeness (comprehensive coverage of patient journeys), representativeness, and timeliness, while maintaining trusted, sustainable models of governance and funding.

To align data collected in traditional EHR systems designed for billing and care delivery with research needs, Truveta restructures clinical data into TDM entities capable of comprehensively characterizing disease progression, treatments, outcomes, and more. Data are semantically normalized to standard ontologies. Automated monitoring of conformance, completeness, plausibility, and stability provides continuous visibility into data quality, with remediation workflows ensuring that daily updates meet regulatory expectations.

Additional data sources—including claims, mortality, social drivers of health, and clinical notes—are integrated and linked to fill the gaps resulting from primary use-driven biases in EHR documentation. Unstructured clinical notes are transformed into structured data using the TLM, which employs advanced natural language processing with human-in-the-loop validation.

Challenges of scope, representativeness, and timeliness are addressed by integrating data for over 130 million patients, updated daily for near-real-time surveillance. This scale enhances breadth, diversity, and inclusivity, and is a significant improvement over comparable networks, such as N3C, which contained 22.8 million patient records in 2025.52 The daily refresh cadence ensures relevancy and is unprecedented amongst comparable data networks.

Reproducibility, a frequent challenge in RWE research, is supported by multiple capabilities built into the Truveta platform. Truveta Studio persistently stores all study artifacts and analytic logic, including Prose cohort definitions, derived variables, stratifications, and summary metrics, and it supports configurable snapshots that freeze the exact dataset, metadata, and provenance so analyses can be re-executed, independently validated, and audited.

Privacy and security concerns are addressed through HIPAA expert determination-compliant de-identification, encryption, and additional security certifications. Truveta’s Embassy architecture further normalizes and de-identifies source data inside each member health system and applies deterministic hashing/tokenization and configurable privacy controls to enable linkage to claims, mortality, and SDOH without PHI egress. Unlike vendor-facilitated commercial networks, this model keeps PHI within member control while permitting health systems to run studies on their own non-deidentified data in PHI Embassies for secure operational and quality-improvement work.

Governance and funding stability is achieved through Truveta’s unique governance model as a real-world intelligence company built with and owned by US health systems. Truveta fosters unique collaboration and deep relationships with its health system partners, in part by incentivizing data completeness and timeliness, as well as responsiveness to new data requests serving emerging RWD interests, unlike other RWD aggregators.

Publicly available whitepapers detailing privacy, data quality, and security practices reinforce transparency and trust. Truveta’s collaborative structure and mixed funding model provide sustainability while aligning incentives across health systems, researchers, and industry partners.

Limitations

Despite its innovations, Truveta faces limitations common to large-scale EHR secondary use platforms. Although Truveta’s processes are designed to minimize the effect of residual variation in documentation practices and coding conventions, it cannot be eliminated completely; harmonization, especially for nuanced concepts or rare conditions, may therefore be incomplete. Truveta’s commitment to quality through human-in-the-loop AI may create bottlenecks due to expert reviewer capacity. While unstructured data are included through NLP, domains such as imaging annotations, device-specific metrics, or social narratives remain incompletely captured. As with all observational data, causal inference is constrained by residual confounding, even with advanced statistical or machine learning methods.

Conclusion

Truveta represents a transformative advance in the secondary use of cross-vendor, multi-institution real-world data to generate trustworthy real-world evidence, by addressing longstanding barriers to scale, quality, timeliness, and regulatory readiness.

The platform delivers multi-modal data comprising comprehensive and clinically meaningful patient journeys. Research-ready data are refreshed daily, made possible by close relationships with health system partners and deep integration with AI for rigor and scalability of automated normalization. Truveta’s governance as a collective of US health systems ensures trust and alignment of incentives, while its collaborative partnership model elevates data quality and drives continuous improvement, in stark contrast to conventional aggregator-supplier relationships.

Despite ongoing challenges inherent to observational research, the platform demonstrates a path toward real-time, regulatory-grade real-world evidence to advance patient care, support policy and regulatory decisions, and accelerate scientific discovery. Looking forward, Truveta is positioned to play a central role in realizing the vision of a national learning health system, in which the ongoing use of clinical data supports improved outcomes and advances in healthcare equity.
