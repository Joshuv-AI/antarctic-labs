 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 NLR will help ACE Green Recycling evaluate and develop its processes for environmentally
 friendly and low-cost recycling of graphite, lithium-iron phosphate (LFP), and other cathode
 materials from spent lithium-ion batteries.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Task 1: Manufacturing Battery Grade Graphite from Scrap Li-Ion Batteries

 Samples of recovered battery graphite (20 g) will be supplied to NLR by ACE Green after going
 through ACE Green’s novel recycling processes for initial testing. NLR will first evaluate the
 chemical and morphological state of the recovered samples using NLR’s advanced analytical
 characterization tools. Next, NLR will cast electrodes and perform electrochemical evaluation of
 the recovered materials. Electrochemical testing will first be conducted in coin cells and
 subsequently in small-format pouch cells using standard United States Advanced Battery
 Consortium, LLC (“USABC”) protocols. NLR will develop and apply electrochemical models
 specific to ACE Green’s recovered/recycled materials. The combination of electrochemical
 testing and modeling will help ACE Green evaluate the performance of its recycled material.
 NLR will help ACE-Green determine the level of contaminants that are required for their
 recycled material to meet the performance and lifetime requirements similar to that of pristine
 materials. If an observed contaminant or morphology is determined to be problematic, then NLR
 will work with ACE Green to reduce contaminant levels and/or improve sample morphology.
 With each iterative modification to ACE Green’s process, ACE Green and NLR will collectively
 determine through physical, chemical, and/or morphological analysis whether significant
 improvements have been made to warrant holistic electrochemical characterization. The goal is
 to determine optimal processing conditions that balance processing costs versus electrochemical
 performance.

 NLR will help ACE Green optimize its hydrometallurgical processes for recycling graphite from
 spent lithium-ion batteries. First, NLR will perform a detailed analysis of graphite samples that
 have been recovered using ACE Green’s novel recycling methods. Characterization – including
 SEM, EDS, Inductively Coupled Plasma (“ICP”), and Differential Scanning Calorimetry
 (“DSC”), and Thermogravimetric Analyzer (“TGA”) – will provide detailed information into
 contaminant concentrations, form, and critical particle morphology parameters (size, shape, D/G
 ratio, etc.).

 NLR will perform electrochemical testing and develop pseudo two-dimensional (“2D”)
 electrochemical models for recycled graphite materials. To evaluate the recovered graphite, NLR
 will cast graphite-anode electrodes at a modest loading of 2-3 mAh/cm2 according to established
 protocols (i.e., preparation of an n-methyl-2-pyrrolidon (“NMP”) based slurry containing
 graphite, carbon black, and Polyvinylidene fluoride (“PVdF”) binder and doctor-blade casting
 onto a Cu current collector). The electrochemical performance of anodes will be evaluated in

 2

 symmetric coin cells (graphite vs. graphite) and full coin cells (graphite vs. nickel manganese
 cobalt (“NMC”) cathode). NLR will measure the rate performance (up to ~5C), impedance
 (Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (“EIS”)), and capacity fade over initial cycling (~50
 cycles) using standard protocols outlined by USABC. If these preliminary electrochemical
 measurements do not match pristine material performance, then NLR and ACE Green will work
 together to iteratively improve ACE Green’s recycling processes to reduce contaminant levels
 and/or improve graphite morphology. Once the coin cell performance meets performance metrics
 similar to pristine materials, ACE-Green will supply NLR with 50 g of recycled graphite within
 2 weeks and NLR will cast larger-area electrodes for assembly into small-format pouch cells
 with NMC cathodes of nominal capacity 50-300 mAh. NLR will measure the rate capability,
 impedance, and cycle life (~200 cycles) of the pouch cells and report results to ACE-Green.

 If the concentration of metal-containing impurities in ACE Green’s recovered graphite is
 unacceptably high, NLR will co-develop a supplemental impurity treatment process with ACE
 Green. This approach will build off NLR’s existing expertise and methods in metallic impurity
 removal and will involve the introduction of tailored chelating agents to solubilize and separate
 contaminants from the target graphite material. Specifics on this effort will be dictated by the
 identity and nature of the contaminants, as identified through initial characterization described
 above.

 Both coin and pouch cell data will be used to build an electrochemical model for recycled
 graphite. Previous research has shown important model parameters for graphite are effective
 particle radius, solidstate diffusion coefficient, exchange current density, and electrode
 tortuosity. NLR will use model to compare recycled graphite performance vs. pristine material
 performance. NLR will report to ACEGreen how anode/full cell performance changes as a
 certain percentage of recycled material is blended with pristine material. Modeling will also help
 with understanding if specific applications (like fast charging) are not suitable for recycled
 graphite materials and how much certain improvements must be made to enable these types of
 demanding applications.

 Task 1 Results:

 After recycling and further treatment, graphite is turned into anodes and electrochemically tested
 in half and full cells. Graphite is first extracted from end-of-life (EOL) lithium-ion batteries
 (LIBs) through a proprietary industrial process, followed by solvent-liquid extraction to
 eliminate residual electrolyte. The graphite obtained after this step is referred to as reclaimed
 graphite (RG). Subsequently, RG undergoes treatment with an acidic and oxidizing mixture of
 sulfutic acid (H2SO4) and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), resulting in acid-treated RG (AT-RG).
 Three distinct treatments are then applied to AT-RG: annealing at 1,000 °C in a hydrogen
 environment, or coating with a petroleum pitch solution at 5% or 10% by weight, followed by
 annealing at 1,000 °C (hereinafter referred to as annealed AT-RG, 5% pitch coated AT-RG and
 10% pitch coated AT-RG, respectively).

 3

 The treated graphite was used to fabricate anodes using a tape-casting method. The treated
 graphite was mixed into a slurry at 92% by mass with 5% carbon black (Timcal C45) and a
 poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF)/1-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP) at 3% by mass. The
 PVDF/NMP solution was 2.5% PVDF and 97.5% NMP by mass.

 The slurry was tape-casted using a doctor blade and a wet gap of 300–400 µm. The tape-casted
 anodes were then dried overnight under vacuum pressure at room temperature and then 100 ◦C
 for 1–2 hours. They were not calendared in this study.

 For graphite anode testing, 2032 coin cells were assembled inside an argon (Ar)-filled glovebox.
 All coin cells were fabricated with a 19 mm diameter Celgard 2325 separator and 40 μL of
 electrolyte composed of 1.2 M lithium hexafluorophosphate (LiPF₆) in ethylene carbonate and
 ethyl methyl carbonate (EC:EMC) (3:7 w/w) with 3 wt% fluoroethylene carbonate added. The
 coin cells were cycled using a Maccor 4300 tower housed in a temperature-controlled oven.

 After fabrication, full cells rested at open circuit voltage (OCV) for four hours. For formation
 and conditioning, the cells underwent three cycles at C/10 from 3-4.2V. For half cells, for
 formation and conditioning, the cells underwent three cycles at C/10 rested at open circuit
 voltage (OCV) for four hours then cycled from 0.05-1.2V. To measure cycling performance, the
 cells were tested using hybrid pulse power characterization (HPPC) with regular reference
 performance tests (RPTs). An RPT step consists of three C/10 cycles followed by a HPPC test
 during charging. The HPPC test involves charging the cell at C/10 for one hour, resting for one
 hour, discharging at 3C for 10 seconds, resting for 40 seconds, charging at 2.25C for 10 seconds,
 and resting for 60 seconds. This sequence is repeated until the cell is fully charged. The
 impedances shown are derived from the maximum values observed during the discharge pulses.

    The results from the lithium nickel manganese cobalt oxide (NMC)|graphite full cells can be
 seen in Figure 1 below. Figure 1b shows the third C/10 cycle in the RPT protocol for their
 corresponding full cells. All cells exhibit consistently decreasing capacity, suggesting a common
    mechanism. The capacities at 303 cycles for the NMC|graphite full cells are 145, 145, 135, 133,
 129, and 85.1 mAh/ganode for pristine graphite, 10% pitch coated AT-RG, 5% pitch coated AT-
 RG, annealed AT-RG, AT-RG and RG, respectively. Thus, the treatment and pitch coating
 significantly improved the performance and capacity retention of cells employing recycled
 graphite.

 Task 2: Recycling LFP and other Cathode Materials

 Samples of recovered battery lithium-iron phosphase (20 g) will be supplied to NLR by ACE
 Green after going through ACE Green’s novel recycling processes. NLR will first evaluate the
 chemical and morphological state of the recovered samples using NLR’s advanced analytical
 characterization tools. Next, NLR will cast electrodes and perform electrochemical evaluation of
 the recovered materials. Electrochemical testing will first be conducted in coin cells and
 subsequently in small-format pouch cells using standard USABC protocols. NLR will develop
 and apply electrochemical models specific to ACE Green’s recovered and recycled materials.
 The combination of electrochemical testing and modeling will help ACE Green validate the
 performance of its recycled material. NLR will help ACEGreen determine what level of
 contaminants are required to meet performance and lifetime requirements similar to the pristine
 materials. If an observed contaminant or morphology is determined to be problematic, then NLR
 will work with ACE Green to reduce contaminant levels and/or improve sample morphology.
 With each iterative modification to ACE Green’s process, ACE Green and NLR will collectively
 determine through physical/chemical/morphological analysis whether significant improvements
 have been made to warrant holistic electrochemical characterization. The goal is to determine
 optimal processing conditions that balance processing costs versus electrochemical performance.

 5

 NLR will help ACE-Green with developing recycling processes for LFP and other cathode
 materials. LFP can be a difficult material to resynthesize and maintain the required performance.
 The particle size must be kept in the nm range ((<100 nm) due to limited conductivity/solid-state
 diffusion and must have a thin carbon coating. NLR will help ACE-Green determine if particle
 size has been kept small enough and if appropriate carbon coating has been formed.

 NLR will perform electrochemical testing and develop pseudo 2D electrochemical models for
 recycled LFP and potentially other cathode materials. To evaluate the recovered cathodes, NLR
 will cast cathode electrodes at a modest loading of 2-3 mAh/cm2 according to established
 protocols. For LFP, NLR will cast electrodes with a loading of ~2 mAh/cm2 using an NMP-
 based slurry (containing LFP, carbon black, and PVdF binder). The recovered LFP material will
 be evaluated in symmetric coin cells (LFP vs LFP), full coin cells (LFP vs. graphite), and
 pending successful coin-cell results, in smallformat full pouch cells (LFP vs graphite). The
 electrochemical performance of cathodes will be evaluated in symmetric coin cells (cathode vs.
 cathode) and full coin cells (graphite vs. cathode). NLR will measure the rate performance (up to
 ~5C), impedance (EIS), and capacity fade over initial cycling (~50 cycles) using standard
 protocols outlined by USABC. If these preliminary electrochemical measurements do not match
 pristine material performance, then NLR and ACE Green will worktogether to iteratively
 improve ACE Green’s recycling processes to reduce contaminant levels and/or improve cathode
 morphology. Once coin cell performance meets performance metrics similar to pristine
 materials, ACE-Green will supply NLR with 50 g of recycled cathode material within 2 weeks
 and NLR will cast larger-area electrodes for assembly into small-format pouch cells with
 graphite anodes of nominal capacity 50-300 mAh. NLR will measure the rate capability,
 impedance, and cycle life (~200 cycles) of the pouch cells and report results to ACE-Green.

 Both coin and pouch cell data will be used to build an electrochemical model for recycled
 cathode materials like LFP. Previous research has shown important model parameters for
 cathode active materials are effective particle radius, solid-state diffusion coefficient, exchange
 current density, and electrode tortuosity. NLR will use model to compare recycled cathode
 performance vs. pristine material performance. NLR will report to ACE-Green how cathode/full
 cell performance changes as a certain percentage of recycled material is blended with pristine
 material. Modeling will also help with understanding if specific applications (like fast charging)
 are not suitable for recycled cathode materials and how much certain improvements must be
 made to enable these types of demanding applications.
