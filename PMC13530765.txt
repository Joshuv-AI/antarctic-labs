Poster 221. The Glenoid Track Width is Smaller Under Dynamic Loading Conditions during Functional Daily Activities: A Finite Element Analysis Study
Authors: Christian Klemt, Tetsuro Sueyoshi, Charles M. Chan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:The glenoid track concept is widely used to evaluate the risk of shoulder instability in the setting of bipolar bone loss. 83% of the glenoid width is often accepted as the threshold to differentiate between on-track and off-track Hill-Sachs lesions. However, this threshold assumes a static state, which may not fully reflect the dynamic nature of shoulder motion. With the reported incidences of shoulder instability as high as 78% in athletes, accurate quantification of the glenoid track width is paramount for clinical decision-making. This study aims to compare the glenoid track width between static and dynamic loading conditions using finite element analysis (FEA) for all essential shoulder motions, including activities of daily living, athletic overhead throwing motions, and maximum ranges of shoulder motion.Methods:This computational modelling study included shoulders from 10 patients (gender: 7 male, 3 female; height: 176±11cm; BMI: 21±5 kg/m2) that underwent high-resolution Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI). The MRI scans were manually segmented to create 3D models for each patient. The 3D anatomical structures were converted to triangular surface meshes and imported into the FEA software. The patient-specific FEA models were used to evaluate the glenoid track width under physiological joint loading during 30 functional daily activities including activities of daily living, athletic overhead throwing motions, and maximum ranges of shoulder motion. A paired t-test was used to compare the glenoid track widths between static and dynamic loading conditions.Results:The glenoid track width was significantly smaller for all dynamic simulations, when compared to static simulations, across all 30 functional daily activities (p<0.01). The smallest dynamic glenoid track width of 70 ± 8% (16.2 ± 1.6mm) was reported for the shoulder dislocation position. A glenoid track width of 72 ± 9% (16.6 ± 1.7mm) of the glenoid width was reported for a quarterback throw. A glenoid track width of 72 ± 9% (16.6 ± 1.7mm) of the glenoid width was reported for a quarterback throw. The glenoid track width was 74 ± 7% (17.1 ± 1.7mm) of the glenoid width for a cleaning the upper back task.Conclusions:Prior research studies have overestimated the glenoid track width by disregarding the dynamic nature of shoulder mobility and instability. A dynamic-based value that incorporates humeral head translations during functional tasks as demonstrated in this study suggests that the threshold for distinguishing on-track and off-track Hill-Sachs lesions may actually be lower. Additionally, this is the first study to our knowledge to demonstrate the variability of the glenoid track based on functional activity.
