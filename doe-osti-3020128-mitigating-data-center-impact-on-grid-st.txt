Background
July 10, 2024: Large Load Loss Incident
On July 10, 2024 (NERC 2025), the fragility of modern load dynamics was exposed when a
lightning strike in Northern Virginia caused a transmission-level fault, lasting less than 100
milliseconds (ms), which triggered the cascading disconnection of approximately 1,500
megawatts (MW) of data center load (see Figure 1). Despite the grid fault clearing in roughly 50
ms, sensitive internal protection schemes across dozens of facilities interpreted the voltage sag as
a valid outage, transitioning information technology (IT) loads to backup power and severing
grid connection. The resulting sudden drop in demand caused a voltage swell to 1.07 p.u.,
requiring manual operator intervention to stabilize the area.

Crucially, although the facilities in Northern Virginia effectively operated within the voltage
immunity bounds of the Information Technology Industry Council (ITIC) curve, the event
exposed a fundamental misalignment: The ITIC standard is designed to protect individual server
hardware, not to maintain macro-grid stability. This resulted in the grid losing several hundred
megawatts of load almost instantaneously, creating overvoltage and oscillation.

This “near miss” has become a primary case study for North American Electric Reliability
Corporation and Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) working groups, driving
new regulatory frameworks that treat data centers not just as passive customers, but as large,
dynamic loads that must possess robust voltage ride-through capabilities, adaptive protection,
and coordinated control strategies. The Electric Reliability Council of Texas recently released its
ride-through requirements (ERCOT 2025) for large electronic loads (which includes data
centers), particularly to address these types of massive load disconnection concerns.

Current State
In practice, many data centers use disturbance-count protection that locks out after three
undervoltage events (below ~0.7–0.8 p.u.) within 60 seconds (s), transferring loads to backup
power until a manual reset is performed. In the July 10 event shown in Figure 1, the third
undervoltage (NERC 2025) triggered widespread lockouts, disconnecting approximately 1.5
gigawatts of data center load and amplifying grid instability as seen in Figure 2.

 Figure 2. Aggregate grid load (NERC 2025)
Recently, some new data centers (Vaidhynathan et al. 2025) have incorporated commercial
battery energy storage systems (BESS) units that typically include fault ride-through and
undervoltage ride-through capabilities, as mandated by IEEE 1547 standards. However, these
responses are typically self-contained and uncoordinated with data center protection logic or
uninterruptible power supply (UPS) operation to provide a “plant-level” ride-through response.
As a result, although the BESS remains connected during short faults, it is not synchronized to
provide grid load recovery/ride-through behavior with UPS load transfer or point of
interconnection (POI) protection timing. This results in delayed power restoration and short-term
grid instability following voltage recovery persisting with conventional BESS.

This observation highlights a key gap: Existing BESS operate effectively at the device level but
lack integrated control with other critical data center assets needed for a grid-supportive response
during and after “successive” undervoltage events.

 3

Innovation
As data centers represent a larger percentage of overall grid load, it is increasingly critical that
they stay connected and assist the grid during short-duration grid contingencies (<~5-s) to avoid
causing wide-area outages. This is accomplished by intelligent control algorithms and/or
parameter settings for controllable assets in a data center such as IT load, cooling load, UPS,
protection settings, and distributed energy sources such as BESS and generators.

The Verrus™ StabiliGrid™ data center architecture involves four steps for data center response
that improve grid resilience:

 1. Grid contingency event detection. Uses high-resolution power quality monitoring to
 detect grid state (during events such as undervoltage, overvoltage, underfrequency, and
 overfrequency).
 2. Grid-resilient protection settings. POI settings that allow extended ride-through/grid-
 connected operation of the data center during the grid contingency, reducing the
 probability of an islanding event.
 3. Control of assets. Depending on detection of grid event, enables grid-state-driven
 autonomous dispatch of the asset to improve grid resiliency (for example, reduce power
 draw from the grid during an undervoltage event or absorb more power from the grid
 during an overvoltage event).
 4. Post-grid event response. Post-grid recovery and following a grid contingency event,
 coordinates data center assets to present firm load to the grid more quickly, similar to the
 levels before the grid contingency event.

Results
Verrus explored the real-life incident shown in Figure 1 in its controller hardware in the loop (C-
HIL) setup at the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) to demonstrate the effectiveness of
our data center design with large-scale BESS and Verrus StabiliGrid (applicable features
described previously) in responding to the cascaded grid undervoltage events. The C-HIL setup
is developed in the Advanced Research on Integrated Energy Systems virtual emulation
environment at NLR and is based on a digital real-time simulation platform (Vaidhynathan et al.
2025).

Note although we present results from undervoltage events, the Verrus StabiliGrid design can
contribute to grid resilience during other types of short-duration grid contingencies such as
overvoltage, overfrequency, and underfrequency. Our work with NLR instantiates the previous
four-step strategy for data centers to ride through short-duration grid contingencies (~ seconds)
and provide grid support. Controllable assets used are BESS and UPS. Results from the
undervoltage grid contingency event of Figure 1 are presented in Table 1.

Conclusion
Through this coordinated strategy implemented in Verrus StabiliGrid, the data center achieves
fault ride-through and rapid recovery for both undervoltage and overvoltage conditions without
triggering protection lockouts. The digital real-time simulations demonstrate by synchronizing
BESS, UPS, and protection logic behavior, the data center transitions from being a disturbance-
sensitive load to an active, grid-supporting participant—a key requirement for future grid-
integrated data center operations.
