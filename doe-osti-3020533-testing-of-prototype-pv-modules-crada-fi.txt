 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 The American-Made Solar Prize is a prize competition from the U.S. Department of Energy
 (DOE) that is designed to revitalize U.S. solar manufacturing. The competition aims to support
 the growth of U.S. solar manufacturing and reenergize American energy innovation by tapping
 into Americans’ competitive spirit and the nation's unparalleled innovation ecosystem,
 leveraging national labs, incubators, facilities, and more. Finalists have been chosen based on the
 work they intend to do with a manufacturing partner to advance their innovation to market. NLR
 will provide technical advisement to assist participants in demonstrating the full potential of their
 product.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Task 1. NLR will perform the thermocycling and damp heat test sequences outlined in
 IEC61215 and the PID test sequence outlined in IEC62804-1 on six, 4-cell modules supplied
 by the Participant.

 Set 2 modules provided by SunFlex for temperature cycling yielded standard test condition
 (STC) results shown in Fig. 1. Fill factor loss of about 2% relative is seen associated with series
 resistance.

 Fig. 1. Set 2 module results through temperature cycling

 2

 STC results of Set 3 modules provided by SunFlex for temperature cycling are shown in Fig. 2.
 There was negligible degradation seen.

 Fig. 2. Set 3 module results through temperature cycling

 Damp heat and potential-induced degradation testing was not performed in favor of additional
 combined accelerated stress testing, covered below.

 Task 2. NLR will perform the thermocycling and damp heat/mechanical load test
 sequences outlined in IEC 61215 and the pelvic inflammation disease (PID) test sequence
 outlined in IEC 62804-1 on six 60-cell modules supplied by the participant. Each of these
 accelerated environmental test sequences will be performed for twice the number of cycles
 stipulated in the IEC standards for photovoltaic (PV) module testing.

 Testing of 60 cell modules was not performed in favor of additional combined accelerated stress
 testing, covered below.

 Task 3: NLR will perform C-AST on two 4-cell modules.

 A total of four sets of two mini modules per set were run through two cycles of the combined-
 accelerated stress testing (CAST) cycle shown in Fig. 3.

 It appears there is more darkening at the cell edges of M2206-0001 (PERC type cell); see Fig 5.
 We can see one metallization wrap-through bond in the middle of the upper right cell having
 some series resistance issue. These factors contribute to the parameter losses seen in Fig. 4.

 Set 2 results

 Fig. 6. Results of set 2 samples through two cycles of CAST

 5

 Set two results using interdigitated back contact (IBC) cells were dominated by lifetime losses,
 leading to depressed Isc and Voc seen in Fig. 6. The transient nature and the +1000 V voltage
 bias point to PID-polarization being the root cause. Series resistance losses can be seen
 developing in the fill factor toward the end of testing based on the 1000 W/m2 flash test
 measurements.

 Set 3 results

 Fig. 7. Results of set 3 samples through two cycles of CAST

 Results of set 3 samples through two cycles of CAST are shown in Fig. 7. Looking closely at the
 EL in M2304-0006 having +1000 V applied (Fig. 8), with darkening appearing in the middle and
 edges of the glass/backsheet-constructed module, it could be grid finger (usually contact)
 resistance developing, contributing to the 1-sun FF loss. We aren’t seeing anywhere this level of
 contrast in the 0.1 Isc image on the right, so I would conclude series resistance as the source.

 6

 Fig. 8. Final electroluminescence images of sample M2304-0006 through two cycles of CAST; left
 image taken with Isc driving current; right, 0.1·Isc.

 The effect, like corrosion, seems accelerated by the +1000 V because the signature is
 different/missing with the sample M2304-0005 having -1000 V applied.

 Other issues seen are glass solarization based on the Isc loss that is incongruent with the
 development of the Voc loss. Voc loss, especially in M2304-0005, which experienced HV -1000
 V bias applied, has PID occurring, but there are additional Voc loss sources considering Voc
 losses in both -1000 V and +1000 V. Light and elevated temperature-induced degradation may
 be the cause because it happened at a higher temperature (Tropical 1a), but that is just
 speculation.

 Set 4 as seen in the results in Fig. 9 did not fare as well as set 3. We saw evidence of PID-

                 Fig 12. Electroluminescence image M2310-0002_2024-04-29T13-52-39-07-

 In the +1000 V (+HV) voltage bias conditions of sample M2310-0002, at cell edges, there are
 some series resistance effects visible in the EL at Isc (Fig. 11); look at the left part of the upper
 right cell in Fig. 11 obtained with Isc driving current (left-side image) and magnified in Fig. 12.
 The area became brighter under further testing. It’s a little hard to imagine the contacts of the
 laser welds improving when going from tropical to desert sequences of CAST, but it is a
 possibility. There is clearly some corrosion at the cell corners, maybe gridfingers. I have been
 thinking there are some PID-corrosion effects in +1000 V CAST.

 Task 4: NLR will measure STC equivalent parameters and take EL images of each of the
