Abstract
It is a well-known theorem of homotopy type theory, originally due to Voevodsky, that function extensionality holds inside any univalent universe. We consider a weaker variant of the univalence axiom, asserting that the wild category formed by the universe is univalent, which we call categorical univalence. We show that categorical univalence does not imply function extensionality by an analysis of Von Glehn’s polynomial model construction, which produces models of Martin-Löf type theory that always refute function extensionality. We find in particular that when the base model has a univalent universe, its polynomial model has a universe that is categorically univalent but lacks function extensionality.

Introduction

In 2010, Voevodsky
[
Voevodsky2010Funext
,
Voevodsky2010NSF
]
discovered that any universe of intensional Martin-Löf type theory (
\mathsf{ITT}
) satisfying his univalence axiom also satisfies function extensionality: (dependent) functions between types in the universe are equal as soon as they are homotopic.
This result became a foundational pillar of Homotopy Type Theory / Univalent Foundations.
For constructivists, it was an additional motivation to justify univalence constructively—noted for example by Bezem, Coquand, and Huber
[
BezemCoquandHuber2013
]
—given the historical difficulty of integrating function extensionality with constructive type theory.

At the same time, the connection between univalence and function extensionality has always seemed contingent.
It is unclear whether univalence implies extensionality principles for other negative type formers, such as coinductive
[
Voevodsky2014Coinductives
]
or modal
[
Gratzer2023
, Conjecture 11.2.2]
types, which suggests functions might be privileged simply because they appear in the statement of univalence.
Furthermore, minor variations on the univalence axiom are not known to imply function extensionality.

In a post on MathOverflow in 2013
[
Dorais2013
]
, François G. Dorais proposed

Part of the appeal of weak foundations is that they allow us to tease apart the components of mathematics.
Each type former of Martin-Löf’s type theory has a distinct, well-defined purpose.
Univalence fits in uneasily in this picture.
While it has beautiful consequences, it also has
many
consequences, and—like impredicativity or the law of the excluded middle—it may be hiding finer structure beneath its surface.

By scratching at that surface, we hope to understand what makes univalence tick.
The polynomial model offers some motivation and a testing ground for weaker forms of the axiom.
We are left with more questions than answers; unlike in the case with
FE

Still, we hope our results can provoke further reflection on the foundations of homotopy type theory.

\end{proof}\par\begin{proposition}${C})\vDash\neg\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\end{proposition}\begin{proof}The functions from \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:evil-functions} are homotopic since the codomain is a proposition by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:types-are-prop-iff-shapes-are}.
Note that $\top\!\langle{A}\rangle$ belongs to the universe of ${C})$ for $A\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathcal{U}$.
\end{proof}\par\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Independence of function extensionality from familial categorical univalence}
\par\begin{proposition}There is a model $\mathbb{C}$ of $\mathsf{ITT}$ with extensive finite coproducts satisfying the strict $\eta$ rule such that $\mathbb{C}\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$ and $\mathbb{C}\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\end{proposition}\begin{proof}Take the model of $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ constructed by Cohen, Coquand, Huber, and M\"{o}rtberg \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{CohenCoquandHuberMortberg2015}{}{}]}, whose category of contexts is the category of presheaves on the De Morgan cube category and whose types are dependent cubical sets equipped with a uniform Kan filling operation.
Orton and Pitts \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{OrtonPitts2018}{}{}, Theorem 5.14]} show that binary coproducts of types can be modeled by coproducts of dependent cubical sets, and it is easy to check the same for nullary coproducts.
Thus these coproducts satisfy the strict $\eta$ law and, since every topos is an extensive category \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{CarboniLackWalters1993}{}{}, Remark 4.10]}, are also extensive.
\end{proof}\par\begin{remark}The particular choice of cubical model is not important in the proof above; any model in the style of Orton and Pitts \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{OrtonPitts2018}{}{}]} or Angiuli et al.\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{AngiuliBrunerieCoquandHarperFavoniaLicata2021}{}{}]} will do, as will Voevodsky's (non-constructive) simplicial model \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{KapulkinLumsdaine2021}{}{}]}.
There are, however, models of $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ that do not support extensive finite coproducts of types; see for example the need for a factorization in Shulman~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Shulman2019}{}{}, Proposition 6.2]}.
\end{remark}\par\begin{theorem}$\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}\nvdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\end{theorem}\begin{proof}Take $\mathbb{C}$ to be a model of $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ with extensive finite coproducts of types, as provided by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:extensive-univalent-base-model}.
The combination $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ implies $\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$, as $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$ tells us that $(A=_{I\to\mathcal{U}}B)\simeq(A\sim B)$.
Thus ${C})\vDash\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$ by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:poly-inherits-famcua}, while ${C})\nvDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:polynomial-model-no-funext-universe}.
\end{proof}\par\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Variations}
\par Once $\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ was proposed by Voevodsky, it was quickly taken up as the canonical axiom for its intended purpose.
Inequivalent variations on $\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ usually turn out to be significantly weaker, as in case of the ``isomorphism reflection'' that holds in Bauer and Winterhalter's cardinal model \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Winterhalter2020}{}{}, \lx@sectionsign 8.3]}, or else inconsistent, as in the case of ``$\mathsf{qinv}$-univalence'' \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{HoTTBook2013}{}{}, Exercise 4.6]}.
\par Unfortunately, we do not see evidence for a canonical form of ``$\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$-free univalence''.
In this section, we show that a few possible candidates are inequivalent; none stands out as the most natural.
In \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:other-univalences:non-familial}, we show that $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ does not imply $\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
In \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:other-univalences:categorical-categorical}, we identify an axiom $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ that also satisfies $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}\nvdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ and $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}\vdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ but is not equivalent to $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ or $\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\par In \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:other-univalences:approximate}, we recall a variant of univalence used by Van den Berg \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{VanDenBerg2020}{}{}, Definition 2.13]} which we call \emph{approximate univalence} or $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}^{\sim}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
It is an open question whether $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}^{\sim}_{\mathcal{U}}$ implies $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$; we do not resolve the question, but we pose a related question that avoids mention of a universe.
\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Non-familial categorical univalence}
\par Our \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:famcua-not-implies-fe} is a priori more than an answer to Dorais' question of whether $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ proves $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$: we prove not only that $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}\nvdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ but that $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}\nvdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
One may then wonder if $\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$ is strictly stronger than $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
This is indeed the case.
\par\begin{theorem}$\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}\nvdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\end{theorem}\begin{proof}Take $\mathbb{C}$ to be a model of $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ with extensive finite coproducts of types, as provided by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:extensive-univalent-base-model}.
Then ${C})\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$ by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:poly-inherits-famcua}, and in particular ${C})\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
We now consider the slice model ${C})/\top\!\langle{1}\rangle$ for $\top\!\langle{-}\rangle$ from \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{defn:topgun}.
That is, we work in the context $t\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\top\!\langle{1}\rangle$.
However, we modify the interpretation of the universe $\mathcal{U}$.
\par Define $(\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathsf{E\kern-0.56905pt\bm{\ell}}^{\prime})$ by $\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{U}\times\top\!\langle{1}\rangle\in\mathrm{Ty}_{{C})}(1)$ with $\mathsf{E\kern-0.56905pt\bm{\ell}}^{\prime}\langle A,t\rangle\coloneqq A\in\mathrm{Ty}_{{C})}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})$.
In the slice model, $\top\!\langle{1}\rangle$ is contractible by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:types-are-prop-iff-shapes-are}, so this universe is closed under the same type formers as $\mathcal{U}$ and the projection $\pi\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{U}$ is an equivalence.
For $A,B\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathcal{U}^{\prime}$ in the slice model, the interpretation of $\mathsf{id}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{ceq}$ for $\mathcal{U}^{\prime}$ is homotopic (by path induction) to the composite of $\mathsf{ap}_{\pi}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}(A=_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}B)\to(\pi A=_{\mathcal{U}}\pi B)$ followed by $\mathsf{id}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{ceq}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}(\pi A=_{\mathcal{U}}\pi B)\to(\pi A\mathbin{\cong}\pi B)$.
Since $\mathsf{ap}_{\pi}$ is an equivalence, $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}$ holds in the slice model.
\par To see that $\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}$ fails in the slice model, take $I\coloneqq\top\!\langle{1+1}\rangle$ and recall from \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:evil-functions} that there exist distinct $f_{0}\neq f_{1}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}I\to\top\!\langle{1}\rangle$.
For $k\in\{0,1\}$, set $A_{k}\coloneqq(\lambda i.\langle 1,f_{k}(i)\rangle)\in(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})^{I}$.
Then $A_{0}\cong_{(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})^{I}}A_{1}$ is by definition $1\cong_{(\mathcal{U})^{I}}1$ and thus inhabited, while $A_{0}=_{(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})^{I}}A_{1}$ would imply $f_{0}=f_{1}$ and is thus empty.
\end{proof}\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Categorical categorical univalence}
\par In our formulation of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$, we could have required that $\mathsf{id}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{ceq}$ be a \emph{categorical} equivalence.
\par\begin{definition}\emph{Categorical categorical univalence} ($\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$) is the principle that the canonical map $\mathsf{id}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{ceq}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}(A=_{\mathcal{U}}B)\to(A\mathbin{\cong}B)$ is a categorical equivalence for all $A,B\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathcal{U}$.
\end{definition}\par A point in favor of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ is that it is a proposition (cf.~\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:being-isomorphism-is-proposition}); the structure of ``being an equivalence'' need not be a proposition without $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$ (cf.~implication \eqref{prop:funext-characterizations:isequiv-is-prop} $\implies$ \eqref{prop:funext-characterizations:funext} of \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:funext-characterizations}).
However, it is unusually strong relative to other identity type characterizations.
For example, the equivalence $(\langle a,b\rangle=_{A\times B}\langle a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\rangle)\simeq(a=_{A}a^{\prime})\times(b=_{B}b^{\prime})$ characterizing identities in $\Sigma$ types cannot be shown to be a categorical equivalence in $\mathsf{ITT}$.
$\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ also seems brittle.
Note that the ``canonical'' map $\mathsf{id}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{ceq}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}A=_{\mathcal{U}}B\to A\mathbin{\cong}B$ is only canonically defined \emph{up to homotopy} by the requirement $\mathsf{id}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{ceq}(\mathsf{refl}_{A})=\mathrm{id}$!
It is not clear to us that different formulations of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ using homotopic definitions of $\mathsf{id}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{ceq}$ are interderivable.
\par In any case, using $)$, we will show that $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ is strictly stronger than $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ and moreover not implied by $\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$, yet still does not imply $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.

By general properties of reflective subcategories, these are exactly those with strictly invertible unit.
In fact, they are also exactly those with categorically invertible unit, but we will not need this.

\par\begin{lemma}If $\mathbb{C}\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$, then equivalences coincide with categorical equivalences between $\bigcirc_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$-modal types.
\end{lemma}\begin{proof}Let $A,B\in\mathrm{Ty}_{{C})}(\Gamma)$ and $f\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}A\to B$ an equivalence.
Since $-_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$ preserves identity types, the map $f_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}\to B_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$ is an equivalence, and by $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$ also a categorical equivalence.
The pseudomorphism $\bigcirc_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$ preserves categorical equivalences by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:inclusion-preserves-categorical-equivalences}.
Hence, so does the dependent right adjoint $\bigcirc_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$ since it is defined by substituting the image of the pseudomorphism along the unit.
Since $\bigcirc_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}f\mathbin{\circ}\eta_{A}\mathrel{\mathring{=}}\eta_{B}\mathbin{\circ}f$ the claim follows by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:modal-types-characterization} and $2$-out-of-$3$ for categorical equivalences (\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:isomorphisms-satisfy-2-out-of-3}).
\end{proof}\par We show that the type family $I\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathcal{U},A,B\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathsf{Fam}(I)\vdash\mathsf{Iso}(I,A,B)\coloneqq(A\mathbin{\cong}_{\mathcal{U}^{I}}B)$ from \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:familial-categorical-univalence} is $\bigcirc_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$-modal, which we can use to improve the equivalence in the definition of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$ to a categorical equivalence over well-behaved base models.
\par\begin{lemma}If $\mathbb{C}\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$, then $\mathsf{Iso}$ in ${C})$ is $\bigcirc_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$-modal.
\end{lemma}\begin{proof}By \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:modal-types-characterization}, it suffices to show that the positions of $\mathsf{Iso}\in\mathrm{Ty}(1.\mathcal{U}.\mathsf{Fam}\times\mathsf{Fam},\mathsf{Iso})$ are empty.
We work internally to $\mathbb{C}$.
We show for all $I\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathcal{U}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$, $A\mathrel{\mathring{=}}(A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}},A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}),B\mathrel{\mathring{=}}(B_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}},B_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}})\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathsf{Fam}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}(I)$, $e\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathsf{Iso}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}(I,A,B)$ that $\mathsf{Iso}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}(I,A,B,e)\to 0$, which suffices by strict initiality of $0$.
By \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:famcua-on-shapes}, we can assume $A\mathrel{\mathring{=}}B$ and $e\mathrel{\mathring{=}}\mathrm{id}$.
In this case, the functions on positions are given by $\mathsf{in}_{1}$ and therefore the type \eqref{prop:characterization-of-Familial-categorical-equivalence-in-polynomial-model:pos} is empty.
\end{proof}\par\begin{proposition}If $\mathbb{C}$ is a model of $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ with extensive finite coproducts of types, then ${C})\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\end{proposition}\begin{proof}By \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:poly-inherits-famcua}, we have ${C})\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
Since the types declared equivalent in the statement of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ are $\bigcirc_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$-modal, by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:type-of-familial-categorical-equivalences-is-modal} and the definition of identity types (\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:polynomial-model:identity-types}), the claim follows from \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:on-fe-model-modal-types-rectify-equivalences}.
\end{proof}\par\begin{corollary}$\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}\nvdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\end{corollary}\begin{proof}\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:extensive-univalent-base-model} provides a model $\mathbb{C}$ of of $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ with extensive finite coproducts of types.
We have ${C})\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:poly+fe-inherits-ccua} and ${C})\vDash\lnot\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:polynomial-model-no-funext-universe}.
\end{proof}\par Finally, we show that while $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ is still strictly weaker than $\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$, it is strictly stronger than $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\par\begin{proposition}$\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}\nvdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\end{proposition}\begin{proof}Take $\mathbb{C}$ to be a model of $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ with extensive finite coproducts of types, as provided by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:extensive-univalent-base-model}.
Then ${C})\vDash\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$ by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:poly-inherits-famcua}.
As in the proof of \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:cua-does-not-imply-famcua}, we now consider the slice model ${C})/\top\!\langle{1}\rangle$ for $\top\!\langle{-}\rangle$ from \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{defn:topgun}.
This time, however, we modify the interpretation of the identity type.
\par We define our new identity types by $(u=^{\prime}_{A}v)\coloneqq(u=_{A}v)\times\top\!\langle{1}\rangle$, where $u=_{A}v$ is the identity type in ${C})$.
Since $\top\!\langle{1}\rangle$ is a proposition by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:types-are-prop-iff-shapes-are}, it is contractible in the slice, so the projection $(u=^{\prime}_{A}v)\to(u=_{A}v)$ is an equivalence and in particular $u=^{\prime}_{A}v$ is an identity type.
If we write $\cong^{\prime}$ for wild-categorical isomorphisms defined with $=^{\prime}$, it follows also that $(a\cong_{{\mathbb{D}}}b)\simeq(a\cong^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{D}}}b)$ for any wild category $\mathbb{D}$.
Thus $\hyperref@@ii[defn:familial-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}$, which holds in ${C})$ by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:poly-inherits-famcua}, transfers to the slice model with the new identity type.
\par However, $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CCUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ cannot hold (in or out of the slice) when formulated with $=^{\prime}$ and $\cong^{\prime}$.
For $A,B\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathcal{U}$, the family of positions for $(A=^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}}B)$ is the constant family $1$.
The family of positions for $A\cong^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}}B$, which is categorically equivalent to $(A\cong_{\mathcal{U}}B)\times\top\!\langle{1}\rangle\times\top\!\langle{1}\rangle$, is the constant family $1+1$ (using \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:type-of-familial-categorical-equivalences-is-modal}).
Thus, by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:good-characterization-of-Familial-categorical-equivalence-in-polynomial-model}, the equivalence $(A=^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}}B)\simeq(A\mathbin{\cong}^{\prime}B)$ is only categorical when both sides are empty.
\end{proof}\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Approximate univalence}
\par Van den Berg \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{VanDenBerg2020}{}{}, Definition 2.13]} defines another weak form of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$, in the language of path categories, which can be rendered in type theory as follows.
\par\begin{definition}\emph{Approximate univalence} ($\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}^{\sim}_{\mathcal{U}}$) is the principle that for all $A,B\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathcal{U}$ and $e\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}A\simeq B$, we have some $p\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}A=_{\mathcal{U}}B$ such that $\mathsf{id}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{eq}(p)\sim e$.
\end{definition}\par Notably, $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}^{\sim}_{\mathcal{U}}$ can be expressed as an inference rule without $\Pi$ types.
In the presence of $\Pi$ types, Swan \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Swan2024}{}{}, Remark 4.6]} comments that it is an open question whether $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}^{\sim}_{\mathcal{U}}$ implies $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
An immediate but subtle consequence of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}^{\sim}_{\mathcal{U}}$ is that there is a composite map $(A\simeq B)\to(A=_{\mathcal{U}}B)\to(A\cong B)$ that improves any homotopy equivalence to a homotopic categorical equivalence.
In light of the decomposition of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$ in \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:decomposing-univalence:funext}, it is natural to consider an analogue of \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{defn:equivalence-improvement}:
\par\begin{definition}\emph{Approximate equivalence improvement} ($\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-equivalence-improvement]{\mathsf{EI}}^{\sim}$) is the principle that for all types $A,B$ and $e\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}A\simeq B$, we have some $e^{\prime}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}A\mathbin{\cong}B$ such that $\mathsf{ceq}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{eq}(e^{\prime})\sim e$.
\end{definition}\par One $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$-like corollary of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-equivalence-improvement]{\mathsf{EI}}^{\sim}$ is that if $P$ is a contractible type, then $A\to P$ is also contractible for every type $A$: by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:cat-isomorphism-characterization}, we have $(A\to P)\simeq(A\to 1)\mathrel{\mathring{\cong}}1$.
This is not provable in $\mathsf{ITT}$, as \lx@cref{creftypepluralcap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:types-are-prop-iff-shapes-are} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{prop:evil-functions} show.
However, the exact relationship between $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-equivalence-improvement]{\mathsf{EI}}^{\sim}$ and $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$ is a mystery to us:
\par\begin{ques}Does $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-equivalence-improvement]{\mathsf{EI}}^{\sim}\vdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$?
\end{ques}\par An answer to \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:pei-implies-fe} might not tell us whether $\mathsf{ITT}+\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-univalence]{\mathsf{UA}}^{\sim}_{\mathcal{U}}\vdash\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}_{\mathcal{U}}$, but it may be a more tractable question.
The polynomial models refute $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-equivalence-improvement]{\mathsf{EI}}^{\sim}$: $\top\!\langle{1}\rangle$ and $\top\!\langle{1+1}\rangle$ are equivalent (\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:equivalences-in-polynomial-model}) but not categorically equivalent (\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:good-characterization-of-Familial-categorical-equivalence-in-polynomial-model}).
Boulier, P\'{e}drot, and Tabareau's \emph{intensional function translation} \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{BoulierPedrotTabareau2017}{}{}, \lx@sectionsign 3]} sends a theory with $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$ to a syntactic model with $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-equivalence-improvement]{\mathsf{EI}}^{\sim}\land\lnot\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$, but its function types do not satisfy any $\eta$ rule, so this does not answer the question for $\mathsf{ITT}$ as we define it.
Shulman \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Shulman2014}{}{}]} has a recipe for expressing universal properties without $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$ that suggests stronger forms of $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-equivalence-improvement]{\mathsf{EI}}^{\sim}$; for example, one can also ask that homotopic categorical equivalences are equal.
It is not clear to us how these strengthenings relate to $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-equivalence-improvement]{\mathsf{EI}}^{\sim}$ or to $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$.
\par\begin{remark}Naturally, we can also consider \emph{approximate categorical univalence} $\hyperref@@ii[defn:approximate-categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}^{\sim}_{\mathcal{U}}$: the principle that for all $A,B\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\mathcal{U}$ and $e\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}A\mathbin{\cong}B$, we have some $p\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}A=_{\mathcal{U}}B$ such that $\mathsf{id}\text{-}\mathsf{to}\text{-}\mathsf{ceq}(p)\sim e$.
This is the weakest of all the univalence principles we have considered, but we do not know if it is strictly weaker than $\hyperref@@ii[defn:categorical-univalence]{\mathsf{CUA}}_{\mathcal{U}}$.
\end{remark}\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Related work}
\par To conclude, we comment on the status of weak forms of univalence in other known models of type theory without function extensionality.
\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Realizability models}
\par Realizability is a standard source of models of $\mathsf{ITT}$ that refute extensionality principles, including $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$; see Streicher \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Streicher1993}{}{}, Theorem 2.9, \lx@sectionsign 3.7]}.
However, most work combining features of realizability and homotopical semantics, such as that of Frumin and Van den Berg \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{FruminVanDenBerg2018}{}{}]} and Uemura \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Uemura2019}{}{}]}, constructs models that \emph{do} satisfy $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$.
An exception is Speight's \emph{groupoidal realizability} \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Speight2024}{}{}]}; his function types have neither $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$ nor the $\eta$ rule.
Speight constructs an impredicative universe of modest fibrations, but we do not know if this or any other universe in the model satisfies some kind of univalence.
\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{P{\'{e}}drot and Tabareau's parametric exceptional translation}
\par The \emph{parametric exceptional translation} \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{PedrotTabareau2018}{}{}]} is another source of models of type theory without $\hyperref@@ii[defn:function-extensionality]{\mathsf{FE}}$.
Presented as a syntactic translation, it induces a construction $)$ on models.
Unlike $)$, however, $)$ does not preserve any form of univalence that we know of.
We sketch here a reason for the simplest form of the translation ($\mathbb{E}=1$ and $\Omega_{i}(\star)=1$).
Kov{\'{a}}cs \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Kovacs2024antifunext}{}{}]} has formalized this case in Agda.
\par Given a model $\mathbb{C}$, the category of contexts in ${C})$ is $\int_{\Gamma\in\mathbb{C}}(\Gamma)$: objects are pairs $\Gamma=(\Gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}},\Gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}})$ as in ${C})$, but a morphism $\sigma\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\Delta\to\Gamma$ is a pair of $\sigma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\Delta_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}\to\Gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$ in $\mathbb{C}$ and $\sigma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mkern-1.0mu\colon\mkern-5.0mu}{\colon}\Delta_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}\to\Gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}\sigma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$ in $(\Delta_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}})$.
We think of $\Gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}$ as selecting ``valid'' elements of $\Gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$.
Types $A\in\mathrm{Ty}(\Gamma)$ have components $A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}\in\mathrm{Ty}(\Gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}})$, $A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}\in\mathrm{Ty}(\Gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}.\Gamma_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}.A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}})$, and $A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}E}}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\Gamma,A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}})$.
Again we think of $A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}$ as selecting valid elements of $A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$, while $A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}E}}$ is a distinguished ``error'' element.
While intuitively $A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}E}}$ should not be valid, this is not enforced.
\par The mismatch between $A\mathbin{\cong}B$ and $A=_{\mathcal{U}}B$ is that for the former, categorical equivalences of the $-_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}$ and $-_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}$ components suffice, while the latter requires also that $A_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}E}}$ corresponds to $B_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}E}}$.
For example, define $X^{0},X^{1}\in\mathrm{Ty}(1)$ by $X^{k}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}S}}\coloneqq 1+1$, $X^{k}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}P}}(b)\coloneqq 1$, and $X^{k}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}E}}=\mathsf{in}_{k}(\star)$.
The identity equivalence on $1+1$ defines a strict isomorphism $X^{0}\mathrel{\mathring{\cong}}X^{1}$ that cannot induce a path $X^{0}=_{\mathcal{U}}X^{1}$ because it does not send $X^{0}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}E}}$ to $X^{1}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}E}}$.
