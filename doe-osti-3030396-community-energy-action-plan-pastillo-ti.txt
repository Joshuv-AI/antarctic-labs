 ABSTRACT

 Community Energy, Creating a Plan

You are welcome to use this outline as a starting point for your own plan. Delete
anything in red and complete it with your own words.

1.1 INTRODUCTION
The Energy Community Action Plan for Pastillo Tibes is a comprehensive strategy aimed at
strengthening the community's resilience against energy challenges. This draft document
emphasizes the importance of community ownership, inclusivity, and adaptability in the planning
process, and is specifically tailored for this community.

1.2 PRINCIPLES OF THE PLAN
The plan must originate from and belong to the community. While external support is beneficial,
the community should take full ownership of the plan. It is crucial to ensure that all community
voices are heard, including dissenting opinions, during decision-making processes. The planning
process is iterative rather than linear, meaning that plans should evolve as values become clearer,
funding sources are identified, and new issues emerge. Documentation should be specific,
descriptive, and quantitative where appropriate. Dates and deadlines should be included, and the
plan should be accessible to all stakeholders.

1.2.1 Vision and Values
The community's vision should reflect genuine and true values. These values should be articulated
in a narrative vision statement, guiding the desired outcomes and supporting outputs.

1.2.2 Desired Outcomes and Supporting Outputs
Values inform desired outcomes, outcomes are achieved through outputs, and outputs are the
results of actions. It can be tempting to start with actions (let’s install solar home systems), but
you’re more likely to get what you want if you start from the highest level of what you want to
achieve. No one wants a solar system; they want to not be left in the dark whenever there is a
storm.

leading to the need for action, such as frequent and lengthy power outages. It is important to gather
specific statistics on power outages, assess household energy consumption (typically around
10kWh/day in Puerto Rico), and understand the special requirements of medically vulnerable
community members.

1.2.4 Community Engagement and Accessibility
Community engagement is vital, with a focus on improving energy literacy and ensuring all voices
are heard, especially the unpopular ones. Multiple ways for expression should be provided, like
town meetings, Facebook groups, or one-on-one interactions.

Access to the conversation merits particular attention in a community with a large number of
elderly or disabled residents. They may be less likely to be connected to social media and may also
have more trouble getting to in-person meetings. Deliberate outreach to the most vulnerable is
necessary, as they are most in need of resilience solutions.

1.2.5 Strategic Areas of Focus

 1. Vegetation Management: Influence with LUNA, politicians, and FEMA to maintain
 vegetation management commitments. Community cooperation is needed to maintain
 vegetation close to home. Vegetation management gives high impact at low cost.
 2. Community Resilience Hub: A long-term project focusing on community building and
 resilience, requiring funding and engineering design. Challenges include the potential lack
 of mobility to a central location.
 3. Water Resilience: Addressing the issue of water interruption during energy outages by
 developing alternative water sources, providing backup power for pumping, and
 considering water storage options at the community or household level.
 4. Household-level Resilience: A phased approach to providing sufficient power for critical
 loads (medical equipment, portable communications, fans, critical refrigeration) and
 eventually meeting all normal energy demands.

1.2.6 Implementation Plan
The implementation plan includes assigning roles and responsibilities, identifying actions, setting
timelines and schedules, developing metrics, securing funding, and continuously monitoring the
project and adjusting the plan. It is essential to engage the community in capacity building,
assessing household needs, selecting technologies for a pilot phase, and scaling up based on pilot
results.

This implementation plan looks specifically at Household-level Resilience, Phase 1 Critical Loads.

1.2.6.1 Identify actions:

 Local capacity building. Socialize the idea of various solar home system options. Identify
 key community members who are or can be trained in system installation and maintenance.
 What other local capacity building is needed?
  Town halls for socializing ideas.
  Have vendors train system maintainers.
  What else do you need to do to build capacity?
 Assess household needs. Identify specific loads for each household. Identify what loads
 will be considered critical and which must be excluded. Really think seriously about the
 old refrigerators!
 Identify households for a pilot.
  Choose a number of households for a pilot phase based on total number of
 households, interested early adopters, and number of technologies to be piloted.
  Would choosing vacant houses be worth considering, and letting them act as de
 facto resilience hubs for surrounding neighbors?
  Do you choose the most in need, such as those with medical equipment? Or do you
 choose those most technically savvy and likely to be successful while working out
 the bugs?
  Who is included in the decision? How do you reach out to them?
 Identify technologies for a pilot.
  Turnkey solar generators.
  Installed solar home systems.
  Other technologies you’re considering?
  Include low electricity use appliances like absorption refrigerators or portable DC
 refrigerators?
  Considerations: Solar generators are less expensive, but require physical labor to
 set up each time they are used and may be subject to theft. They have shorter

 warrantees and less of a proven track record. Do funders prefer one over the other?
 Should a pilot include both?

  Potential installers/maintainers within the community: identify person and company.

1.2.9 Summary
This sample action plan can be used as a template to create one specific to Pastillo Tibes.
Key points for an action plan include these:
  Make your plans in this order, not the other way around
 o Values  Outcomes  Outputs  Activities
  Plans are an important starting point, but expect to revise them as you go. It’s all
 iterative.
  Put everything into writing, including and especially assumptions that often go unspoken.
  Reflect everyone’s voice, even those expressing unpopular or infeasible ideas.
  Monitoring, evaluation, and reporting are done at every step; they are not an afterthought.

By prioritizing values, desired outcomes, and supporting outputs, the community can develop a
resilient and sustainable strategy for addressing energy challenges, with a special focus on the
unique needs and characteristics of this amazing community.
