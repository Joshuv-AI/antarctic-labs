Association between sinus rhythm restoration and sleep-disordered breathing at three months after pulmonary vein isolation for persistent atrial fibrillation
Authors: Taro Narumi, Yoshihisa Naruse, Yuki Tokonami, Kazuki Ito, Yutaro Kaneko, Makoto Sano, Yuichiro Maekawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Sinus restoration for persistent AF is associated with improved preoperative AHI.•Clinically, AHI improved in 39% of patients, and 51% had reduced severity of sleep-disordered breathing.•Subsequent recurrence of AF was significantly reduced in the patients with improved AHI.Keywords: Sleep-disordered breathing, Atrial fibrillation, Apnea-hypopnea index, Pulmonary vein isolation

Introduction

The Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Sleep-Disordered Breathing in Cardiology 2023 recommend a Class I, Level of Evidence A recommendation to interview and screen patients with Atrial Fibrillation (AF) for clinical symptoms of Sleep-Disordered Breathing (SDB)1. For patients with AF, the recommendation is Class IIa, Level of Evidence B, to consider treatment of SDB to prevent recurrence and improve treatment efficacy. The rationale for this recommendation is that a meta-analysis of previous studies has shown that patients with concomitant SDB have an increased incidence of AF recurrence after Pulmonary Vein Isolation (PVI) compared with patients without such a disorder,2 moreover, appropriate treatment of SDB with continuous positive pressure breathing therapy can reduce AF recurrence3, 4, 5 In this way, the relationship between SDB and AF has been extensively studied, and a close association has been suggested.

SDB generates strong negative intrathoracic pressure due to inspiratory effort against upper airway obstruction. This pressure imposes hemodynamic stress on the atria, left ventricle, and pulmonary circulation, which can induce left atrial enlargement and stretching6 In addition, intermittent hypoxia and subsequent reoxygenation lead to oxidative and metabolic stress on atrial myocytes and interstitial tissue7 Furthermore, during apneic or obstructive respiratory events, sympathetic nerve activity is enhanced, leading to an imbalance between the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems, which may alter the electrical excitability of the atria7,8 Thus, several studies have reported the potential mechanisms linking SDB with the onset of AF. However, research examining the impact of sinus rhythm restoration through PVI on SDB is limited to the study by Naruse et al., which examined the acute effects at 1-week post-procedure. To the best of our knowledge, no studies have investigated this effect > 1-week after the ablation.

In the present study, the authors examined the effect of sinus rhythmicity by PVI on SDB in patients with persistent AF 3-months after the ablation.

Methods

Study population

Consecutive patients with persistent AF who underwent an Apnea-Hypopnea Index (AHI) measurement by a simple sleep apnea test (WatchPAT, ZOLL Itamar, MA, USA) before and 3-months after PVI in the studied hospital between October 2021 and October 2024 were retrospectively evaluated. Patients with a prior history of AF ablation, or who underwent CPAP therapy before PVI were excluded from this study. The indications for catheter ablation of AF followed the guidelines of the Japanese Circulation Society/Japanese Heart Rhythm Society9,10 This was a single‐center study and was approved by the institutional review board (approval number #20‐347). The procedures were performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration and followed the STROBE Statement. The requirement for written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of this study.

Pulmonary vein isolation

The AF procedure was performed with deep sedation or general anesthesia. Oral anticoagulants were continued throughout the procedure. A 6Fr 20-polar steerable catheter (BeeAT, Japan Lifeline Co, Tokyo, Japan) was inserted into the coronary sinus via the femoral vein. A 10Fr View Flex Xtra ICE catheter (Abbott, St Paul, MN, USA) was inserted into the right atrium via the right femoral vein. After introducing sheaths and a trans-septal puncture, 3000 and 5000 units of heparin were injected, respectively, followed by continuous heparin administration to maintain an activated clotting time of greater than 300–400 s during the procedure. The SL0 (Abbott) and a steerable sheath (Agilis, Abbott) were inserted into the left atrium. The intraluminal esophageal temperature was continuously measured via a trans-orally inserted probe (SensiTherm, Abbott). Circumferential pulmonary vein isolation was done using a Tacti Flex ablation catheter (Abbott), which was advanced into the left atrium via the Agilis with EnSiteX EP system (Abbott). Radiofrequency (RF) energy was delivered at 50W. After RF energy was delivered, the catheter tip was dragged by 4–6 mm every 10–12 s. Pulmonary vein isolation was confirmed by the elimination of all pulmonary vein potentials recorded by Advisor HD Grid Mapping catheter (Abbott) placed at the ostium of the pulmonary vein and exit block during pacing at 10-V output with 1-ms pulse width. In all cases, only PVI was performed, and no additional procedures such as posterior left atrial wall isolation were added.

Endpoints and follow-up

The primary endpoint was the change in AHI value measured by a simple sleep apnea test (WatchPAT, ZOLL Itamar, MA, USA) performed before and more than 3 months after PVI. Severity of SDB was defined as follows: normal (AHI of < 5), mild (5–15), moderate (15–30), and severe (> 30). Improvement in clinical AHI was evaluated as a composite endpoint; that was defined as either a 50% reduction in the preoperative AHI or an improvement of AHI to 15 events per hour or less. The study patients were divided into two groups based on whether they showed improvement or not in AHI 3-months after the ablation. No patient-initiated CPAP therapy before the second sleep apnea test. Blood collection data and cardiac computed tomography were also reevaluated at 3-months after PVI. Atrial tachycardia and AF recurrence after the blanking period of 3 months were used as a secondary endpoint. AF recurrence was defined as AF or atrial tachycardia lasting > 30 s, irrespective of the presence of symptoms.11 After discharge, all patients were seen in our hospital, at the outpatient arrhythmia clinic, or by their referring physician for 1-month, and then every 3-months thereafter. At each hospital visit, the patients underwent 12-lead electrocardiography and intensive questioning regarding any arrhythmia-related symptoms. Holter electrocardiography was routinely performed at 3-, 6-, 12-, 24-, and 36-months after PVI and was temporarily performed for any patient reporting symptoms suggesting recurrent arrhythmia. Repeat ablation was not performed in any of the patients during the study period.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as means ± Standard Deviation (SD). If the data were not distributed normally, the data are presented as medians and interquartile ranges. Unpaired Student’s t-test and the Chi-Square test were used for comparison between 2 groups of continuous and categorical variables, respectively. The Mann–Whitney U-test was used when the data were not normally distributed. Univariate and multivariate analyses with logistic regression were used to determine significant predictors of improving AHI 3-months after pulmonary vein isolation. Multivariate analysis was adjusted for factors found to be significant in univariate analysis, as no previous studies have reported predictors of improvement in AHI > 3-months after ablation in patients with persistent AF. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed with a standard statistical program package (JMP version 10; SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina).

Results

The baseline characteristics

During the study period, 41 cases were enrolled. Baseline clinical characteristics are shown in Table 1. The mean age of the entire study was 68 ± 9 years, and 36 (88%) were male. Underlying comorbidity was hypertension in 20 patients (49%), dyslipidemia in 16 (39%), and diabetes mellitus in 10 (24%). The left ventricular ejection fraction and the left atrial diameter measured by echocardiography were 58% ± 10% and 43 ± 6 mm, respectively. Median plasma NT-proBNP was 891 pg/mL.

Clinical AHI improvement, defined as either a 50% reduction in the preoperative AHI or an improvement of AHI to 15 events per hour or less, was observed in 16 patients (39%).

Improvement of AHI and recurrence of atrial fibrillation after pulmonary vein isolation

During the median follow-up period of 12.9 months (interquartile range: 7.4–22.9 months), AF recurrence was observed in 12 patients (29%). Kaplan–Meier analysis showed significantly fewer AF recurrences in the AHI-improved group compared with the AHI non-improved group (Log-rank p = 0.048, Fig. 2). On the other hand, this analysis suggests that the group showing improvement in SDB at 3-months after PVI had significantly lower recurrence of atrial fibrillation, but it does not demonstrate a causal relationship that restoration of sinus rhythm improves SDB. Among the 29 patients without AF recurrence at 3-months post-PVI, the change in NT-proBNP level (−731 vs. −146 pg/mL, p < 0.001) and the left atrial volume assessed by cardiac computed tomography (−70.3 vs. −21.5 mL, p < 0.001) were significantly greater in the AHI-improved group.

Discussion

Main findings

The present study showed that 1) Sinus restoration by PVI for persistent AF is associated with improved preoperative AHI at 3-months postoperatively, 2) Subsequent recurrence of AF was significantly reduced in the patients with improved AHI.

Proposed mechanisms of SDB triggering AF

Several studies have suggested a potential link between SDB and the onset of AF12,13 Hypoxemia induced by SDB has been demonstrated to activate the sympathetic nervous system14 and cause structural remodeling of the left atrium from increased blood pressure15 Additionally, negative pressure within the thoracic cavity16 and increased venous return have been observed in individuals with SDB17 It has been postulated that structural remodeling of the left atrium may be induced. The prevalence of AF is reported to be four times higher in patients with SDB than in those without18 Comitant SDB could be associated with an increased risk of AF recurrence after the ablation, and the usage of CPAP can attenuate the risk of AF recurrence3, 4, 5

Proposed mechanisms of sinus restoration improving the severity of SDB

The observed association between sinus rhythm maintenance and lower AHI at three months is compatible with two non-mutually exclusive interpretations: 1) That sinus rhythm restoration leads to hemodynamic improvements that reduce SDB severity, or 2) That patients who experienced a reduction in SDB severity (due to unmeasured factors or natural variation) were more likely to maintain sinus rhythm. The present study design cannot distinguish between these pathways. While several reports described the efficacy of CPAP therapy for suppressing AF recurrence after the ablation,3, 4, 5 there have been few reports about the effectiveness of sinus restoration by PVI for the severity of SDB. A previous study by our group compared pre- and post-operative AHI changes at one week after PVI19 This report demonstrated that the AHI significantly improves one week after pulmonary vein isolation; however, this effect was not observed in cases where AF recurred immediately after the procedure. On the other hand, it was unclear whether this effect would persist over a mid-term period of three months postoperatively, which led us to conduct this study. The present study demonstrated that sinus restoration by PVI in patients with persistent AF was associated with a reduction in AHI 3-months after PVI. As observed in this study, sinus rhythm restoration in AF significantly reduces NT-proBNP levels and left atrial volume, suggesting a decrease in circulating plasma volume. A high circulating plasma volume is known to cause SDB through volume central shift20,21 The previous study evaluated the AHI before and one week after PVI, and in that study, the AHI improved from 22 to 17, corresponding to a 23% improvement rate, which appears smaller than that observed in the present study19 Considering both previous and current studies, the improvement in AHI appears to be sustained from one week to three months after PVI, and the improvement rate may increase over time as fluid retention progressively resolves with sustained sinus rhythm. On the other hand, this study did not measure body fluid volume using bioimpedance analysis, so this remains merely a speculation. It is a well-documented fact that patients suffering from AF frequently have comorbid SDB, and a significant number of cases utilize CPAP therapy before PVI12,13 The findings of this study demonstrated that the improvement in AHI resulting from sinus rhythm restoration in AF endures in the mid-term following PVI. The present findings suggest it may be reasonable to re-evaluate SDB severity after PVI once sinus rhythm is established, as this could inform the ongoing need for and intensity of SDB therapy.

The association between the improvement in AHI and ejection fraction

In a large cohort study of patients with heart failure and an ejection fraction below 45%, a lower ejection fraction was identified as an independent predictor of sleep-disordered breathing (Odds Ratio = 1.10)22 It has been proposed that reduced ejection fraction limits cardiac output, resulting in pulmonary congestion, increased pulmonary interstitial fluid volume, and a rostral fluid shift, thereby destabilizing respiratory control during sleep. Accordingly, in the present study, the improvement in ejection fraction observed after restoration of sinus rhythm in patients with AF may have contributed to the observed reduction in the AHI. Furthermore, in the present study, preoperative ejection fraction was identified as a predictor of improvement in the AHI. This finding also suggests that early rhythm control for atrial fibrillation may be beneficial from the perspective of SBD.

The association between the improvement in AHI and the decreased recurrence of AF after the ablation

The present study demonstrated that the patients with an improved AHI after PVI exhibited a reduced incidence of subsequent AF recurrences. As demonstrated in this study, the improvement in AHI is associated with a reduction in left atrial volume and NT-proBNP levels. In cases where AHI improved, a decrease in circulating plasma volume could occur, leading to reduced left atrial load and suppression of AF recurrence. A recent study reported that 6-month CPAP therapy improved the atrial conduction time and low-voltage areas within the left atrium compared with SDB patients without CPAP therapy23 Consequently, the observed enhancement in AHI, attributable to sinus rhythm restoration, may have exerted an effect analogous to that of sustained CPAP therapy.

Limitations

This study has limitations. It was retrospectively conducted at a single center and included a relatively small number of cases. Moreover, the present cohort represents a highly selected group of patients who were both eligible for and agreed to repeat sleep testing three months post-PVI. This likely selects for a more compliant, health-conscious, and possibly healthier subset of the AF population, limiting the generalizability of these findings. However, despite the limited sample size, the effect of PVI in improving AHI through sinus rhythm restoration was demonstrated, suggesting strong potential for future large-scale studies. Further clarification of the relationship between SDB and AF treatment will require prospective multicenter studies. The polysomnography used in this study was a simplified type (WatchPAT, ZOLL Itamar) and therefore could not distinguish between obstructive sleep apnea and central sleep apnea. In previous investigation using full polysomnography, the authors reported that most cases of SDB in patients with AF were obstructive sleep apnea19 Furthermore, other studies have also demonstrated that the prevalence of obstructive sleep apnea is remarkably high24 Thus, it may be reasonable to assume that the findings of the present study primarily reflect obstructive sleep apnea. In addition, this study measured AHI only at two time points: preoperatively and three months postoperatively. The measurement of AHI at only two time points (pre-PVI and 3-months post-PVI) is a critical limitation. The authors cannot determine when the AHI reduction occurred relative to sinus rhythm restoration during the intervening “blanking period.” Therefore, the authors cannot establish the temporal sequence required to infer causality. To further evaluate the benefits of sinus rhythm restoration for atrial fibrillation, it would be necessary to measure AHI immediately after surgery and/or at more frequent intervals.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the findings of this study indicate that the sinus restoration achieved by PVI for persistent AF is associated with a reduction in the AHI compared to the preoperative levels. Furthermore, the patients who exhibited an improvement in their AHI experienced a significantly lower incidence of recurrences of AF.
