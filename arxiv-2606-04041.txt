Abstract
. Inspired by the problem of constructing ( ω \omega -)pre-Lie algebra structures on the dual space of a pre-Lie algebra, we introduce the A A -generalized Yang–Baxter equation as a generalization of the Yang–Baxter equation of pre-Lie algebras. We study its symmetric solutions through A A -generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras and split these solutions into two types. We further consider factorizable solutions of this equation and establish a one-to-one correspondence between them and generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebras of nonzero weight. By studying the structure of these algebras, we find all factorizable solutions. Finally, we study the structure of A A -generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras. In particular, we obtain a structural description via central and double extensions and classify low-dimensional non-trivial A A -generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras. Key words: Yang-Baxter equation, Hessian pre-Lie algebra, double extension, ω \omega -pre-Lie algebra

Definition 2.1

[

A vector space

(x\cdot y)\cdot z-x\cdot(y\cdot z)=(y\cdot x)\cdot z-y\cdot(x\cdot z)

(x,y,z):=(x\cdot y)\cdot z-x\cdot(y\cdot z)

Let

.

3.
Solutions of the
A
-generalized Yang-Baxter equations

In this section, we study symmetric and factorizable solutions of the
A
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation. We start with the operator form of symmetric solutions on arbitrary pre-Lie algebras.

3.1.
Symmetric Solutions on Arbitrary pre-Lie algebras

For arbitrary pre-Lie algebras, symmetric solutions of the classical

S
-equation can be characterized by relative Rota–Baxter operators
associated with the coregular representation.

Definition 3.1
.

[
1
]

A representation of a pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot)
is a triple
(\rho,\mu,V)
, where
V
is a vector space,
\rho:A\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
A_{\mathrm{c}}
, and
\mu:A\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a linear map satisfying

\rho(x)\mu(y)-\mu(y)\rho(x)=\mu(x\cdot y)-\mu(y)\mu(x)

for all
x,y\in A
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra. The regular representation of
A
is
(L,R,A)
, where
L,R:A\to\mathfrak{gl}(A)
are the left and right multiplication operators, namely
L_{x}y=x\cdot y
and
R_{x}y=y\cdot x
for all
x,y\in A
.

Definition 3.3
.

[
1
]

Let
(\rho,\mu,V)
be a representation of a pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot)
. The dual representation on
V^{*}
is
(\rho^{*}-\mu^{*},-\mu^{*},V^{*})
, where
\rho^{*},\mu^{*}:A\to\mathfrak{gl}(V^{*})
are defined by

\displaystyle\langle\rho^{*}(x)\xi,v\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\xi,\rho(x)v\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\mu^{*}(x)\xi,v\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\xi,\mu(x)v\rangle

for all
x\in A
,
\xi\in V^{*}
, and
v\in V
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra. The coregular representation of
A
is
(\operatorname{ad}^{*},-R^{*},A^{*})
, where
\operatorname{ad}_{x}^{*}=L_{x}^{*}-R_{x}^{*}
is the coadjoint representation of
A_{\mathrm{c}}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

[
27
]

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra, and let
T:A^{*}\to A
be a linear map satisfying
\langle\xi,T(\eta)\rangle=\langle\eta,T(\xi)\rangle

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
. Let
\{e_{i}\}
be a basis of
A
, and let

\{e^{i}\}
be its dual basis. Then

r=\sum_{i}T(e^{i})\otimes e_{i}

is a symmetric solution of the classical
S
-equation if and only if
T

is a relative Rota–Baxter operator associated to the coregular representation, namely

T(\xi)\cdot T(\eta)=T\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{T(\xi)}^{*}\eta-R_{T(\eta)}^{*}\xi\bigr)

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
.

We generalize the above result to obtain symmetric solutions of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra, let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, and let
T:A^{*}\to A
be a linear map satisfying
\langle\xi,T(\eta)\rangle=\langle\eta,T(\xi)\rangle
for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
. Let
\{e_{i}\}
be a basis of
A
, and let
\{e^{i}\}
be the dual basis of
A^{*}
. Then

r=\sum_{i}T(e^{i})\otimes e_{i}

is a symmetric solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation if and
only if

\displaystyle T(\xi)\cdot T(\eta)={}

\displaystyle T\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{T(\xi)}^{*}\eta-R_{T(\eta)}^{*}\xi\bigr)+\langle\xi,u\rangle T(\eta)-\langle\xi,T(\eta)\rangle u

(2)

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
.

Proof.

The symmetry of
T
is equivalent to the symmetry of
r=\sum_{i}T(e^{i})\otimes e_{i}
. Pairing

-r_{12}\cdot r_{13}+r_{12}\cdot r_{23}+[r_{13},r_{23}]-(u\otimes 1\otimes 1)r_{23}+(1\otimes u\otimes 1)r_{13}

with
\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta
, we obtain

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Big\langle\xi,\,T(\eta)\cdot T(\zeta)-T\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{T(\eta)}^{*}\zeta-R_{T(\zeta)}^{*}\eta\bigr)+\langle\zeta,T(\eta)\rangle u-\langle\eta,u\rangle T(\zeta)\Big\rangle.

Since the pairing between
A^{*}
and
A
is nondegenerate, the
u
-generalized
S
-equation is equivalent to

\displaystyle T(\eta)\cdot T(\zeta)={}

\displaystyle T\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{T(\eta)}^{*}\zeta-R_{T(\zeta)}^{*}\eta\bigr)+\langle\eta,u\rangle T(\zeta)-\langle\zeta,T(\eta)\rangle u.

Renaming
\eta,\zeta
as
\xi,\eta
, and using
\langle\eta,T(\xi)\rangle=\langle\xi,T(\eta)\rangle
gives (
2
).
∎

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra. A linear map
T:A^{*}\to A
satisfying (
2
) for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
is called a
u
-generalized relative Rota–Baxter operators associated with the coregular representation
.

3.2.
u
-Generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra and Symmetric Solutions

In the same way that Hessian pre-Lie algebras give rise to relative Rota–Baxter operators,
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras give rise to
u
-generalized relative Rota–Baxter operators.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra and let
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
. A symmetric bilinear form
\gamma
on
A
is called a
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle
if

\displaystyle\gamma(x\cdot y,z)-\gamma(x,y\cdot z)-\gamma(y\cdot x,z)+\gamma(y,x\cdot z)

\displaystyle-\gamma(x,u)\gamma(y,z)+\gamma(y,u)\gamma(x,z)=0

for all
x,y,z\in A
. A
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra
is a quadruple
(A,\cdot,\gamma,u)
, where
(A,\cdot)
is a pre-Lie algebra and
\gamma
is a nondegenerate
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle
.

Remark 3.9
.

The notion of a
u
-generalize Hessian pre-Lie algebra
has the following geometric interpretation. Let
(A,\cdot)
be realized as the algebra of left-invariant vector fields on the simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
A_{\mathrm{c}}
, whose left-invariant flat torsion-free connection is determined by
\nabla_{x}y=x\cdot y
. A nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\gamma
corresponds to a left-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
. For left-invariant vector fields, we have

(\nabla_{x}\gamma)(y,z)=-\gamma(x\cdot y,z)-\gamma(y,x\cdot z).

Define
\theta\in A^{*}
by
\theta(x)=\gamma(x,u)
. Then the
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle condition is equivalent to the total symmetry of

\nabla g+\theta\otimes g.

Indeed, the difference between the values of this tensor on
(x,y,z)
and

(y,x,z)
is the negative of the left-hand side of the
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle identity. Moreover, taking
z=u
in that identity and using
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, we get

\gamma(x\cdot y,u)-\gamma(y\cdot x,u)=0,

equivalently,
\theta([x,y])=0
. Hence
\theta
is closed as a left-invariant
1
-form. If
\vartheta=-\theta
, then

\nabla g-\vartheta\otimes g

is totally symmetric and
\vartheta
is closed. Thus, in the positive
definite case,
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras correspond to the infinitesimal form of locally conformally Hessian manifolds in the sense of Osipov
[
29
]
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\gamma,u)
be a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra. Define
\gamma^{\sharp}:A\to A^{*}
by

\langle\gamma^{\sharp}(x),y\rangle=\gamma(x,y)

for all
x,y\in A
. Then
T_{\gamma}=(\gamma^{\sharp})^{-1}:A^{*}\to A
is a

u
-generalized relative Rota–Baxter operators associated with the coregular representation.

Proof.

The nondegeneracy of
\gamma
implies that
\gamma^{\sharp}
is an
isomorphism. Let
a=T_{\gamma}(\xi)
and
b=T_{\gamma}(\eta)
. Then

\langle\xi,y\rangle=\gamma(a,y)
and
\langle\eta,y\rangle=\gamma(b,y)
for all
y\in A
. Since
\gamma
is symmetric, we have

\langle\xi,T_{\gamma}(\eta)\rangle=\gamma(a,b)=\gamma(b,a)=\langle\eta,T_{\gamma}(\xi)\rangle.

For any
z\in A
, using the definitions of
\operatorname{ad}^{*}
and

R^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle\gamma\Big(T_{\gamma}\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{a}^{*}\eta-R_{b}^{*}\xi\bigr)+\langle\xi,u\rangle b-\langle\xi,b\rangle u,\ z\Big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\gamma(b,[a,z])+\gamma(a,z\cdot b)+\gamma(a,u)\gamma(b,z)-\gamma(a,b)\gamma(u,z).

By the
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle identity applied to
(a,z,b)
, the right-hand side is equal to
\gamma(a\cdot b,z)
. Hence, by the nondegeneracy of
\gamma
,

\displaystyle T_{\gamma}(\xi)\cdot T_{\gamma}(\eta)={}

\displaystyle T_{\gamma}\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{T_{\gamma}(\xi)}^{*}\eta-R_{T_{\gamma}(\eta)}^{*}\xi\bigr)+\langle\xi,u\rangle T_{\gamma}(\eta)-\langle\xi,T_{\gamma}(\eta)\rangle u.

Thus
T_{\gamma}
satisfies (
2
).
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\gamma,u)
be a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra with
u\neq 0
. Let
\{e_{i}\}
be a basis of
A
, and let
\{e^{i}\}
be its dual basis. Then

r_{\gamma}=\sum_{i}T_{\gamma}(e^{i})\otimes e_{i}

is a symmetric solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation.

Conversely, a symmetric solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation also gives rise to a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra, let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, and let
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes A
be a symmetric solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation. Define
r^{\sharp}:A^{*}\to A
by

\langle\xi,r^{\sharp}(\eta)\rangle=\langle\xi\otimes\eta,r\rangle

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
. If
r^{\sharp}
is a linear isomorphism, then

\gamma(x,y)=\bigl\langle(r^{\sharp})^{-1}(x),y\bigr\rangle

for all
x,y\in A
defines a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot,\gamma,u)
.

Proof.

The nondegeneracy of
\gamma
follows from the invertibility of

r^{\sharp}
. Since
r
is symmetric,
r^{\sharp}
is symmetric with respect to the natural pairing. Hence, for
x=r^{\sharp}(\xi)
and

y=r^{\sharp}(\eta)
, we have

\gamma(x,y)=\langle\xi,r^{\sharp}(\eta)\rangle=\langle\eta,r^{\sharp}(\xi)\rangle=\gamma(y,x).

Thus
\gamma
is symmetric.

It remains to prove that
\gamma
is a
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle. Set
T=r^{\sharp}
. Then
r=\sum_{i}T(e^{i})\otimes e_{i}
, and by Theorem
3.6
,
T
satisfies

\displaystyle T(\xi)\cdot T(\eta)={}

\displaystyle T\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{T(\xi)}^{*}\eta-R_{T(\eta)}^{*}\xi\bigr)+\langle\xi,u\rangle T(\eta)-\langle\xi,T(\eta)\rangle u

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
. Let
a=T(\xi)
,
b=T(\eta)
, and

c=T(\zeta)
. Using the definitions of
\operatorname{ad}^{*}
and
R^{*}
,
we get

\displaystyle\gamma(a\cdot b,c)={}

\displaystyle-\gamma(b,[a,c])+\gamma(a,c\cdot b)+\gamma(a,u)\gamma(b,c)-\gamma(a,b)\gamma(u,c).

By interchanging
a
and
b
in this identity and subtracting, we get

\displaystyle\gamma(a\cdot b,c)-\gamma(a,b\cdot c)-\gamma(b\cdot a,c)+\gamma(b,a\cdot c)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\gamma(a,u)\gamma(b,c)-\gamma(b,u)\gamma(a,c).

Therefore

\displaystyle\gamma(a\cdot b,c)-\gamma(a,b\cdot c)-\gamma(b\cdot a,c)+\gamma(b,a\cdot c)

\displaystyle-\gamma(a,u)\gamma(b,c)+\gamma(b,u)\gamma(a,c)=0.

Since
T=r^{\sharp}
is surjective, this identity holds for all
a,b,c\in A
. Hence
\gamma
is a nondegenerate
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle, and
(A,\cdot,\gamma,u)
is a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra.
∎

3.3.
Two Types of Symmetric Solutions

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra, let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, and let

r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in S^{2}(A)

be a symmetric solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation. Equivalently, this equation can be written in the following expanded tensor form:

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i,j}\Big(

\displaystyle-x_{i}\cdot x_{j}\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}+x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\cdot x_{j}\otimes y_{j}+x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes[y_{i},y_{j}]\Big)

(3)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i}u\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i}+\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes u\otimes y_{i},

where the bracket is taken in
A_{\mathrm{c}}
.

Define the linear map
r^{\sharp}:A^{*}\to A
from Proposition
3.12
by

r^{\sharp}(\xi)=\sum_{i}\langle\xi,y_{i}\rangle x_{i}=\sum_{i}\langle\xi,x_{i}\rangle y_{i}

(4)

for all
\xi\in A^{*}
, and let
H=\operatorname{Im}(r^{\sharp})
. Then

H
is a subspace of
A
and
r\in H\otimes H
. Indeed, let
\dim H=k
, and let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
be a basis of
H
extended to a
basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
of
A
. Then
r
can be written in the form

r=\sum_{p,q=1}^{k}\alpha_{pq}e_{p}\otimes e_{q}=\sum_{q=1}^{k}a_{q}\otimes e_{q},\qquad a_{q}=r^{\sharp}(e^{q})\in H.

And
H=\operatorname{span}\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
.

Let
e_{1}^{*},\ldots,e_{n}^{*}
be the dual basis of A. Applying
\operatorname{id}\otimes e^{p}\otimes e^{q}
,
1\leq p,q\leq k
, to (
3
), we obtain

r^{\sharp}(e_{p}^{*})\cdot r^{\sharp}(e_{q}^{*})\in H+\langle u\rangle

Therefore

H\cdot H\subseteq H+\langle u\rangle.

Since
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, it follows that

H_{1}:=H+\langle u\rangle

is a pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
.
Thus two cases naturally arise:

(1)

If
u\in H
, then
H
itself is a pre-Lie subalgebra of

A
. We call such symmetric solutions
solutions of type
1
.

(2)

If
u\notin H
, then
H_{1}\neq H
, and
H_{1}=H\oplus\langle u\rangle
is a
pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
. We call such symmetric solutions

solutions of type
2
.

Example 3.13
.

Let
A=\operatorname{span}\{x,y,u\}
be the
3
-dimensional pre-Lie algebra with nonzero products
x\cdot y=y\cdot x=-u
. Then
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, and
r=x\otimes y+y\otimes x
is a solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation. Indeed, the classical
S
-equation part is cancelled exactly by the
u
-correction terms.

Moreover, the map
r^{\sharp}:A^{*}\to A
associated with
r
satisfies

\operatorname{Im}(r^{\sharp})=\operatorname{span}\{x,y\}.

Since
x\cdot y=-u\notin\operatorname{span}\{x,y\}
, this subspace is not a pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
. Hence
r
is a symmetric solution of
type
2
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra and let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
. Then symmetric solutions of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation of type
1
are in one-to-one correspondence with
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie subalgebras
(H,\cdot,\gamma,u)
of
A
.

Proof.

Let
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in S^{2}(A)
be a symmetric solution of the

u
-generalized
S
-equation of type
1
. Define
r^{\sharp}:A^{*}\to A
by (
4
) and set
H=\operatorname{Im}(r^{\sharp})
. Since
r
is of type
1
, we have
u\in H
. Hence
H
is a pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
. Moreover,
r\in S^{2}(H)
.

Let
H^{\circ}=\{\xi\in A^{*}\mid\xi(H)=0\}.
Since
r
is symmetric,
\ker r^{\sharp}=H^{\circ}
. Indeed, for any
\xi,\alpha\in A^{*}
, we have

\langle\alpha,r^{\sharp}(\xi)\rangle=\langle\alpha\otimes\xi,r\rangle=\langle\xi\otimes\alpha,r\rangle=\langle\xi,r^{\sharp}(\alpha)\rangle.

Thus
\xi
annihilates
\operatorname{Im}(r^{\sharp})
if and only if

r^{\sharp}(\xi)=0
. Therefore
r^{\sharp}
induces an isomorphism

\overline{r^{\sharp}}:H^{*}\cong A^{*}/H^{\circ}\to H.

Now regard
r
as an element of
S^{2}(H)
. Since
\overline{r^{\sharp}}
is an isomorphism,
r
is nondegenerate on
H
. As
H
is a pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
and
u\in H
, the
u
-generalized
S
-equation for
r
in
A
is exactly the same equation in
H
.

Define

\gamma(a,b)=\left\langle(\overline{r^{\sharp}})^{-1}(a),b\right\rangle

for all
a,b\in H
. By Proposition
3.12
, applied to the pre-Lie algebra
H
, the quadruple
(H,\cdot,\gamma,u)
is a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra. Thus every type
1
solution gives a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie subalgebra
of
A
.

Conversely, let
(H,\cdot,\gamma,u)
be a
u
-generalized Hessian
pre-Lie subalgebra
of
A
. By Proposition
3.10
,
T_{\gamma}=(\gamma^{\sharp})^{-1}:H^{*}\to H
is a symmetric
u
-generalized relative Rota–Baxter operator associated with the coregular representation
of
H
. Hence, by Theorem
3.6
, for any basis
\{e_{i}\}
of
H
with dual basis
\{e_{i}^{*}\}
,

r_{\gamma}=\sum_{i}T_{\gamma}(e_{i}^{*})\otimes e_{i}\in S^{2}(H)

is a symmetric solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation on

H
. Since
H
is a pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
and
u\in H
, the
same tensor is also a solution on
A
. Moreover, the associated map
r_{\gamma}^{\sharp}:A^{*}\to A
is given by

r_{\gamma}^{\sharp}(\xi)=T_{\gamma}(\xi|_{H})

for all
\xi\in A^{*}
. Since the restriction map
A^{*}\to H^{*}
is surjective, we get

\operatorname{Im}(r_{\gamma}^{\sharp})=H.

Since
u\in H
, the resulting solution is of type
1
. This proves the correspondence.
∎

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra and let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
. Then symmetric solutions of the

-generalized
S
-equation of type

are in one-to-one correspondence with pairs
(H,\gamma)
, where

is a subspace and

(1)

;

(2)

for all

;

(3)

(H,*,\gamma)
is a Hessian pre-Lie algebra.

Proof.

Let
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in S^{2}(A)
be a type
2
symmetric solution,
define
r^{\sharp}:A^{*}\to A
by (
4
) and set
H=\operatorname{Im}(r^{\sharp})
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.14
,
r^{\sharp}
induces an isomorphism
\overline{r^{\sharp}}:H^{*}\cong A^{*}/H^{\circ}\to H
, where
H^{\circ}=\{\xi\in A^{*}\mid\xi(H)=0\}
. Define

\gamma(a,b)=\bigl\langle(\overline{r^{\sharp}})^{-1}(a),b\bigr\rangle

(5)

for all
a,b\in H
. Then
\gamma
is symmetric and nondegenerate.

Let
f,g\in A^{*}
, and put
a=r^{\sharp}(f)
,
b=r^{\sharp}(g)
. By the
u
-generalized relative Rota–Baxter operators identity associated with the

u
-generalized
S
-equation, we have

\displaystyle a\cdot b={}

\displaystyle r^{\sharp}\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{a}^{*}g-R_{b}^{*}f\bigr)+\langle f,u\rangle b-\langle f,b\rangle u.

Since
\langle f,b\rangle=\gamma(a,b)
, it follows that
a\cdot b+\gamma(a,b)u\in H
. Thus the product
a*b:=a\cdot b+\gamma(a,b)u
is well defined on
H
. Since
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, the space
H\oplus\langle u\rangle
is a pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
, and the original product on this subalgebra is given by

a\cdot b=a*b-\gamma(a,b)u,\qquad u\cdot(H\oplus\langle u\rangle)=(H\oplus\langle u\rangle)\cdot u=0.

In particular,
(H,*)
is the quotient pre-Lie algebra
(H\oplus\langle u\rangle)/\langle u\rangle
.

Substituting
a\cdot b=a*b-\gamma(a,b)u
into the
u
-generalized
S
-equation, the
u
-terms cancel by the inverse-tensor identities for
r
and
\gamma
. Hence
r
viewed as an element of
S^{2}(H)
, satisfies the ordinary
S
-equation in the pre-Lie algebra
(H,*)
. Since
r
is nondegenerate on
H
, the standard correspondence between nondegenerate symmetric solutions of the classical
S
-equation and Hessian pre-Lie algebras implies that
(H,*,\gamma)
is a Hessian pre-Lie algebra.

Conversely, suppose that a pair
(H,\gamma)
satisfies the three stated
conditions. Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
be a basis of
H
, and let
C=(c_{ij})
be the inverse of the matrix
(\gamma(e_{i},e_{j}))
. Define

r=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}c_{ij}e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\in S^{2}(H)\subseteq S^{2}(A).

Thus
r
is the inverse tensor of
\gamma
. In particular, for every

a\in H
,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}c_{ij}\gamma(e_{j},a)e_{i}=a.

(6)

Since
(H,*,\gamma)
is a Hessian pre-Lie algebra, the tensor
r
is a symmetric solution of the classical
S
-equation of
(H,*)
. Now use
x\cdot y=x*y-\gamma(x,y)u
for all
x,y\in H
. Because
\gamma
is symmetric, the sub-adjacent Lie brackets determined by
\cdot
and by
*
agree on
H
. Hence the classical
S
-equation part with respect to
*
vanishes, and the additional terms produced by
-\gamma(x,y)u
are exactly cancelled by the correction terms

-(u\otimes 1\otimes 1)r_{23}+(1\otimes u\otimes 1)r_{13}

in the
u
-generalized
S
-equation. Indeed, (
6
) implies the tensor identities

\sum_{p,q}\gamma(x_{p},x_{q})y_{p}\otimes y_{q}=r,\qquad\sum_{p,q}\gamma(y_{p},x_{q})x_{p}\otimes y_{q}=r,

where
r=\sum_{p}x_{p}\otimes y_{p}
. Thus

-r_{12}\cdot r_{13}+r_{12}\cdot r_{23}+[r_{13},r_{23}]-(u\otimes 1\otimes 1)r_{23}+(1\otimes u\otimes 1)r_{13}=0.

Since
u\notin H
, the solution is of type
2
.
∎

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra, let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, and let
r\in S^{2}(A)
be a symmetric solution of the classical
S
-equation. Let
(H,\cdot,\gamma)
be the Hessian pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
corresponding to
r
, and assume that
u\notin H
. Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
be a basis of
H
, with
e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\kappa_{ij}^{s}e_{s}
. Suppose that the linear system

\sum_{s=1}^{k}\kappa_{ij}^{s}\lambda_{s}=\gamma(e_{i},e_{j})

(7)

has a solution
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\in F^{k}
for all

1\leq i,j\leq k
. If
r=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\alpha_{ij}e_{i}\otimes e_{j},
then

r_{\lambda}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\alpha_{ij}(e_{i}+\lambda_{i}u)\otimes(e_{j}+\lambda_{j}u)

is a symmetric solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation of type
2
.

Proof.

Define
\Phi:H\to A
by
\Phi(e_{i})=e_{i}+\lambda_{i}u
. Since
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(e_{i})\cdot\Phi(e_{j})

\displaystyle=e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\kappa_{ij}^{s}e_{s}

(8)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\kappa_{ij}^{s}\Phi(e_{s})-\left(\sum_{s=1}^{k}\kappa_{ij}^{s}\lambda_{s}\right)u

\displaystyle=\Phi(e_{i}\cdot e_{j})-\gamma(e_{i},e_{j})u.

Define a product on
\Phi(H)
by
\Phi(x)*\Phi(y)=\Phi(x\cdot y)
for all
x,y\in H
, and define a symmetric bilinear form
\gamma_{\Phi}
on
\Phi(H)
by
\gamma_{\Phi}(\Phi(a),\Phi(b))=\gamma(a,b)
for all
a,b\in H
. Then
\Phi:(H,\cdot,\gamma)\to(\Phi(H),*,\gamma_{\Phi})
is an isomorphism of Hessian pre-Lie algebras.

Moreover, (
8
) gives

\Phi(x)\cdot\Phi(y)=\Phi(x)*\Phi(y)-\gamma_{\Phi}(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))u.

Also
u\notin\Phi(H)
, because
\Phi(H)
is the graph of a linear map

H\to\langle u\rangle
inside
H\oplus\langle u\rangle
, while
u\notin H
. Therefore Theorem
3.15
applies, and

r_{\lambda}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\alpha_{ij}\Phi(e_{i})\otimes\Phi(e_{j})

is a symmetric solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation of type
2
.
∎

Example 3.17
.

Let
A=\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},u\}
be the
4
-dimensional pre-Lie algebra with nonzero products

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},\qquad e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=e_{2},\qquad e_{1}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{3},\qquad e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{3}.

Then
A
is an associative left-symmetric algebra, and
\operatorname{Ann}(A)=\langle u\rangle
. Let

H=\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}.

Then
H
is a pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
. Define a symmetric bilinear form

\gamma
on
H
by

\gamma(e_{1},e_{3})=1,\qquad\gamma(e_{2},e_{2})=1,

and all other values to be zero. Then
(H,\cdot,\gamma)
is a Hessian pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
. The inverse tensor of
\gamma
is

r=e_{1}\otimes e_{3}+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+e_{3}\otimes e_{1},

which is a symmetric solution of the classical
S
-equation of
A
.

With respect to the basis
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
of

\sum_{s=1}^{3}\kappa_{ij}^{s}\lambda_{s}=\gamma(e_{i},e_{j})
has the solution
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})=(0,0,1)
. Thus, setting

\Phi(e_{1})=e_{1},\qquad\Phi(e_{2})=e_{2},\qquad\Phi(e_{3})=e_{3}+u,

Proposition
3.16
gives that

r_{\lambda}=e_{1}\otimes(e_{3}+u)+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+(e_{3}+u)\otimes e_{1}

is a symmetric solution of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation of type
2
of the pre-Lie algebra
A
.

3.4.
Factorizable Solutions

We study factorizable solutions of the nontrivial
A
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation. For
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes A
, define linear maps
r_{+},r_{-}:A^{*}\to A
by

r_{+}(\xi)=\sum_{i}\langle\xi,x_{i}\rangle y_{i},\qquad r_{-}(\xi)=r^{\sharp}(\xi)=\sum_{i}\langle\xi,y_{i}\rangle x_{i}

for all
\xi\in A^{*}
, and set
I=r_{+}-r_{-}
. A key tool is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.18
.

[
37
]

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra and let
r\in A\otimes A
. Assume that
I=r_{+}-r_{-}
is a linear isomorphism and that
r-\sigma(r)
is
(L,\operatorname{ad})
-invariant. Let
\diamond
be the product on
A^{*}
defined by
\langle\xi\diamond\eta,x\rangle=\langle\xi\otimes\eta,\alpha_{r}(x)\rangle
for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
and
x\in A
. Then
r
is a factorizable solution of the classical Yang–Baxter equation of
A
if and only if

r_{-}(\xi\diamond\eta)=r_{-}(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
.

The preceding lemma leads to a characterization of factorizable solutions of
the classical Yang–Baxter equation of a pre-Lie algebra in terms of quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebras of nonzero weight. We first recall the following definitions.

Definition 3.19
.

[
26
]

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra and let
\lambda\in F
. A linear map
\mathcal{R}:A\to A
is called a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
if

\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)=\mathcal{R}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot y\bigr)

for all
x,y\in A
.

Definition 3.20
.

[
37
]

A Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
is a triple
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{R})
, where
(A,\cdot)
is a pre-Lie algebra and

\mathcal{R}:A\to A
is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
.

Definition 3.21
.

[
37
]

Let
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{R})
be a Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
, and let
S
be a nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form on
A
. The quadruple
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{R},S)
is called a quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
if
(A,\cdot,S)
is a quadratic pre-Lie algebra and

S(x,\mathcal{R}(y))+S(\mathcal{R}(x),y)+\lambda S(x,y)=0

for all
x,y\in A
.

Theorem 3.22
.

[
37
]

Let
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{R},S)
be a quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebra of nonzero weight
\lambda
. Let
I_{S}:A^{*}\to A
be the linear isomorphism determined by
S(I_{S}(\xi),y)=\langle\xi,y\rangle
for all
\xi\in A^{*}
and
y\in A
.
Define

r_{-}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{R}\circ I_{S}:A^{*}\to A,

and let
r\in A\otimes A
be the unique tensor satisfying
\langle\operatorname{id}\otimes\xi,r\rangle=r_{-}(\xi)
for all
\xi\in A^{*}
. Then

r_{-}(\xi\diamond\eta)=r_{-}(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
. Consequently,
r
is a factorizable solution
of the classical Yang–Baxter equation of
A
.

Theorem 3.23
.

[
37
]

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra, and let
r\in A\otimes A
be a factorizable solution of the classical Yang–Baxter equation of
A
. Let

0\neq\lambda\in F
. Set
I=r_{+}-r_{-}:A^{*}\to A
, and define

\mathcal{R}=\lambda r_{-}\circ I^{-1}:A\to A,\qquad S_{I}(x,y)=\langle I^{-1}(x),y\rangle

for all
x,y\in A
. Then
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{R},S_{I})
is a quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
.

We now generalize the above result to obtain factorizable solutions of the
nontrivial
A
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation. Let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
and
r\in A\otimes A
. Define the
product
\diamond_{u}
on
A^{*}
by

\langle\xi\diamond_{u}\eta,x\rangle=\langle\xi\otimes\eta,\alpha_{r}(x)\rangle+\langle\xi,u\rangle\langle\eta,x\rangle

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
and
x\in A
. A key tool for studying factorizable solutions of the
A
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation
is the following
u
-analogue of Lemma
3.18
.

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra, let
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, and let
r\in A\otimes A
. Assume that
r_{+}-r_{-}:A^{*}\to A
is a linear isomorphism and that
r-\sigma(r)
is
(L,\operatorname{ad})
-invariant. Then
r
is a factorizable solution of the
u
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation if and only if

r_{-}(\xi\diamond_{u}\eta)=r_{-}(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)+\langle\xi\otimes\eta,r\rangle u

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\diamond
be the product on
A^{*}
obtained from
\diamond_{u}
by
setting
u=0
. The computation underlying Lemma
3.18
gives

\langle\xi\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes\eta,[[r,r]]\rangle=r_{-}(\xi\diamond\eta)-r_{-}(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)

for all
\xi,\eta\in A^{*}
. Using

[[r,r]]_{u}=[[r,r]]+\sum_{i}\bigl(u\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i}-x_{i}\otimes u\otimes y_{i}\bigr),

we get

\displaystyle\langle\xi\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes\eta,[[r,r]]_{u}\rangle={}

\displaystyle r_{-}(\xi\diamond\eta)-r_{-}(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)+\langle\xi,u\rangle r_{-}(\eta)-\langle\xi\otimes\eta,r\rangle u.

Since

\xi\diamond_{u}\eta=\xi\diamond\eta+\langle\xi,u\rangle\eta
, it follows that

\langle\xi\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes\eta,[[r,r]]_{u}\rangle=r_{-}(\xi\diamond_{u}\eta)-r_{-}(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)-\langle\xi\otimes\eta,r\rangle u.

By the nondegeneracy of the natural pairing,
[[r,r]]_{u}=0
is equivalent to
the stated identity. Together with the assumed invertibility of
r_{+}-r_{-}

and the
(L,\operatorname{ad})
-invariance of
r-\sigma(r)
, this is precisely the factorizable solution condition.
∎

We now introduce
u
-generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebras of weight
\lambda
.

Definition 3.25
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra, let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
,
\lambda\in F
, and let
S
be a bilinear form on
A
. A linear map
\mathcal{R}:A\to A
is called a
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
if

\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)=\mathcal{R}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot y+\lambda S(x,u)y\bigr)-\lambda S(x,\mathcal{R}(y))u

for all
x,y\in A
.

Definition 3.26
.

Let
(A,\cdot,S)
be a quadratic pre-Lie algebra, let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, and
\lambda\in F
. A quintuple
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{R},S,u)
is called a
u
-generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
if
\mathcal{R}:A\to A
is a
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
and

S(x,\mathcal{R}(y))+S(\mathcal{R}(x),y)+\lambda S(x,y)=0

for all
x,y\in A
.

The following result constructs factorizable solutions of the
u
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation from
u
-generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebras of nonzero weight.

Theorem 3.27
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{R},S,u)
be a
u
-generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebra of nonzero weight
\lambda
. Let
I_{S}:A^{*}\to A
be the linear isomorphism determined by
S(I_{S}(\xi),y)=\langle\xi,y\rangle
for all
\xi\in A^{*}
and
y\in A
. Define

r_{-}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{R}\circ I_{S}:A^{*}\to A,

and let
r\in A\otimes A
be the unique tensor satisfying
\langle\operatorname{id}\otimes\xi,r\rangle=r_{-}(\xi)
for all
\xi\in A^{*}
. Then
r
is a factorizable solution of the
u
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation.

Proof.

Let
x=I_{S}(\xi)
and
y=I_{S}(\eta)
. The identity

S(x,\mathcal{R}(y))+S(\mathcal{R}(x),y)+\lambda S(x,y)=0

implies
r_{+}-r_{-}=I_{S}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\langle\eta,(r_{+}-r_{-})(\xi)\rangle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigl(S(x,\mathcal{R}(y))-S(y,\mathcal{R}(x))\bigr)=S(y,x)=\langle\eta,I_{S}(\xi)\rangle.

Hence
r_{+}-r_{-}
is a linear isomorphism. Moreover, the invariance of
S

implies that
r-\sigma(r)
is
(L,\operatorname{ad})
-invariant.

Let
\diamond
denote the classical product on
A^{*}
corresponding to

u=0
. By the classical relation between quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebras and factorizable tensors, we have

I_{S}(\xi\diamond\eta)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigl(x\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot y+\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot y\bigr).

Since

\xi\diamond_{u}\eta=\xi\diamond\eta+\langle\xi,u\rangle\eta
, we get

I_{S}(\xi\diamond_{u}\eta)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigl(x\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot y+\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot y+\lambda S(x,u)y\bigr).

Thus

r_{-}(\xi\diamond_{u}\eta)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{R}\bigl(x\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot y+\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot y+\lambda S(x,u)y\bigr).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle r_{-}(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)+\langle\xi\otimes\eta,r\rangle u={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\frac{1}{\lambda}S(x,\mathcal{R}(y))u

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda S(x,\mathcal{R}(y))u\bigr).

By the defining identity of the
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter operator, the last two displayed expressions are equal. Hence

r_{-}(\xi\diamond_{u}\eta)=r_{-}(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)+\langle\xi\otimes\eta,r\rangle u.

By Lemma
3.24
,
r
is a factorizable solution
of the
u
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation.
∎

Conversely, factorizable solutions of the
u
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation induce
u
-generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebras of
nonzero weight.

Theorem 3.28
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra, let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, and
r\in A\otimes A
be a factorizable solution of the
u
-generalized Yang–Baxter equation.
Let
0\neq\lambda\in F
. Set
I=r_{+}-r_{-}:A^{*}\to A
, and define

\mathcal{R}=\lambda r_{-}\circ I^{-1}:A\to A,\qquad S_{I}(x,y)=\langle I^{-1}(x),y\rangle

for all
x,y\in A
. Then
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{R},S_{I},u)
is a
u
-generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
.

Proof.

Since
r
is factorizable,
I=r_{+}-r_{-}
is a linear isomorphism and
r-\sigma(r)
is
(L,\operatorname{ad})
-invariant. Hence
S_{I}
is nondegenerate and skew-symmetric, and the invariance of
r-\sigma(r)
is
equivalent to the invariance of
S_{I}
. Thus
(A,\cdot,S_{I})
is a quadratic pre-Lie algebra.

The compatibility condition follows as in the classical case. Indeed, for

x=I(\xi)
and
y=I(\eta)
, we have

\displaystyle S_{I}(x,\mathcal{R}(y))+S_{I}(\mathcal{R}(x),y)+\lambda S_{I}(x,y)={}\lambda\bigl(\langle\xi,r_{-}(\eta)\rangle-\langle\eta,r_{-}(\xi)\rangle+\langle\xi,I(\eta)\rangle\bigr)=0.

Therefore it remains to prove that
\mathcal{R}
is a
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
.
For
x=I(\xi)
and
y=I(\eta)
, the classical relation gives

I(\xi\diamond\eta)=r_{-}(\xi)\cdot I(\eta)+I(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)+I(\xi)\cdot I(\eta).

Since
\xi\diamond_{u}\eta=\xi\diamond\eta+\langle\xi,u\rangle\eta
, we get

\lambda I(\xi\diamond_{u}\eta)=\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot y+\lambda S_{I}(x,u)y.

Applying
\mathcal{R}
to both sides gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot y+\lambda S_{I}(x,u)y\bigr)-\lambda S_{I}(x,\mathcal{R}(y))u

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\bigl(r_{-}(\xi\diamond_{u}\eta)-\langle\xi\otimes\eta,r\rangle u\bigr).

By Lemma
3.24
, the right-hand side equals
\lambda^{2}r_{-}(\xi)\cdot r_{-}(\eta)=\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)
. Hence

\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)=\mathcal{R}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot y+\lambda S_{I}(x,u)y\bigr)-\lambda S_{I}(x,\mathcal{R}(y))u.

Thus
\mathcal{R}
is a
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
, and the proof is complete.
∎

To construct
u
-generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter left-symmetric algebras of nonzero weight, we relate them to
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter symplectic Lie algebras of nonzero weight.

Definition 3.29
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,])
be a Lie algebra, let
0\neq u\in C(\mathfrak{g})
, where
C(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the center of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
0\neq\lambda\in F
. A quintuple
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,],\mathcal{R},S,u)
is called a
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter symplectic Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

(\mathfrak{g},S)
is a symplectic Lie algebra, namely
S
is a nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form satisfying

S([x,y],z)+S([y,z],x)+S([z,x],y)=0

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(2)

The linear map
\mathcal{R}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfies

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl([\mathcal{R}(x),y]+[x,\mathcal{R}(y)]+\lambda[x,y]\bigr)

\displaystyle+\lambda S(x,u)\bigl(\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda y\bigr)+\lambda S(u,y)\bigl(\mathcal{R}(x)+\lambda x\bigr)

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(3)

The compatibility condition

S(\mathcal{R}(x),y)+S(x,\mathcal{R}(y))+\lambda S(x,y)=0

holds for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 3.30
.

Let
(A,\cdot,S)
be a quadratic pre-Lie algebra, let
0\neq u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
, and
0\neq\lambda\in F
. Then
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{R},S,u)
is a
u
-generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
if and only if
(A_{\mathrm{c}},[\,,\,],\mathcal{R},S,u)
is a
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter symplectic Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
.

Proof.

Since
(A,\cdot,S)
is a quadratic pre-Lie algebra,
S
is a symplectic form on
A_{\mathrm{c}}
. Moreover,
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
implies
u\in C(A_{\mathrm{c}})
. The compatibility condition between
S
and
\mathcal{R}
is identical in the two definitions. It remains to compare the operator identities.

For
x,y,z\in A
, the quadratic identity of
S
gives

\displaystyle S\Big(\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)-\mathcal{R}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot y\bigr),z\Big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle S\Big(y,\,\mathcal{R}\bigl([\mathcal{R}(x),z]+[x,\mathcal{R}(z)]+\lambda[x,z]\bigr)-[\mathcal{R}(x),\mathcal{R}(z)]\Big).

Furthermore, the compatibility condition gives

\displaystyle S\Big(-\lambda S(x,u)\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda S(x,\mathcal{R}(y))u,\ z\Big)={}S\Big(y,\,\lambda S(x,u)\bigl(\mathcal{R}(z)+\lambda z\bigr)+\lambda S(u,z)\bigl(\mathcal{R}(x)+\lambda x\bigr)\Big).

Adding these two identities and using the nondegeneracy of
S
, we obtain
that the
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter identity on
(A,\cdot)
is equivalent to

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl([\mathcal{R}(x),z]+[x,\mathcal{R}(z)]+\lambda[x,z]\bigr)

\displaystyle+\lambda S(x,u)\bigl(\mathcal{R}(z)+\lambda z\bigr)+\lambda S(u,z)\bigl(\mathcal{R}(x)+\lambda x\bigr).

Renaming
z
as
y
, this is precisely the operator identity in the definition of a
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter symplectic Lie algebra. Hence the two structures are equivalent.
∎

By the correspondence above, the study of
u
-generalized quadratic Rota–Baxter pre-Lie algebras of nonzero weight reduces to the study of
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter symplectic Lie algebras of nonzero weight. The latter class is highly restricted, as shown by the following result.

Theorem 3.31
.

Let
0\neq\lambda\in F
,
0\neq u\in C(\mathfrak{g})
, and let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,],\mathcal{R},S,u)
be a
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter symplectic Lie algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is a two-dimensional abelian Lie algebra.

Proof.

Set
P=\mathcal{R}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and

Q=P+\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. The compatibility
condition gives

S(Px,y)+S(x,Py)=0

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus
P
is skew-symmetric with respect to

S
.

In terms of
P
and
Q
, the
u
-generalized Rota–Baxter
identity is equivalent to

\displaystyle T(x,y):={}

\displaystyle[Px,Py]+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}[x,y]-P\bigl([Px,y]+[x,Py]\bigr)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lambda\bigl(S(x,u)Qy-S(y,u)Qx\bigr).

We claim that
\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}S(T(x,y),z)=0
. Since
P
is skew-symmetric with respect to
S
, we have

\displaystyle S(T(x,y),z)={}

\displaystyle S([Px,Py],z)+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}S([x,y],z)+S([Px,y],Pz)+S([x,Py],Pz).

The cyclic sum of the second term vanishes because
S
is a Lie algebra

2
-cocycle. The remaining cyclic sum is the sum of the cocycle identities
for the triples
(Px,Py,z)
,
(Py,Pz,x)
, and
(Pz,Px,y)
. Hence the
claim follows.

Let
\varphi(x)=S(x,u)
. Using the expression for
T(x,y)
, the above
claim gives

\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\bigl(\varphi(x)S(Qy,z)-\varphi(y)S(Qx,z)\bigr)=0.

Since
Q=P+\frac{\lambda}{2}\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
P
is
skew-symmetric with respect to
S
, we have

S(Qa,b)-S(Qb,a)=\lambda S(a,b)

for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore

\varphi(x)S(y,z)+\varphi(y)S(z,x)+\varphi(z)S(x,y)=0

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, equivalently,
\varphi\wedge S=0
.

Since
u\neq 0
and
S
is nondegenerate,
\varphi=S(\,\cdot\,,u)
is
nonzero. If
\dim\mathfrak{g}\geq 4
, choose
e\in\mathfrak{g}
with
\varphi(e)\neq 0
. Then
\langle u,e\rangle
is a nondegenerate symplectic plane, and its symplectic orthogonal has positive even dimension.
Hence there exist
w,z\in\langle u,e\rangle^{\perp}
such that

S(w,z)\neq 0
. But then

(\varphi\wedge S)(e,w,z)=\varphi(e)S(w,z)\neq 0,

a contradiction. Thus
\dim\mathfrak{g}<4
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is symplectic and
u\neq 0
, we get
\dim\mathfrak{g}=2
.

Finally, a two-dimensional Lie algebra with a nonzero central element is
abelian. Therefore
\mathfrak{g}
is a two-dimensional abelian Lie algebra.
∎

4.
u
-Generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras and Their Low-Dimensional Classification

In Section
3
, we introduced
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras and established their connection with symmetric solutions of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation. In this section, we further study their structure.

4.1.
A Structure Theorem for
u
-Generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras

A natural question is how to construct
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras. In the nontrivial case
0\neq u
, we give a complete construction from Hessian pre-Lie algebras. The construction splits naturally into two cases according to whether
u
is isotropic with respect to
\gamma
. If
\gamma(u,u)\neq 0
, the algebra is obtained by a
one-dimensional annihilator extension. If
\gamma(u,u)=0
, it is obtained by a double extension. These two constructions exhaust all
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebras.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(V,*,f)
be a Hessian pre-Lie algebra, and let
0\neq c\in F
.
Put
\bar{A}=V\oplus\langle u\rangle
. Define a bilinear product
\cdot

on
\bar{A}
by

\displaystyle x\cdot y

\displaystyle=x*y-f(x,y)u,\quad\forall x,y\in V,

\displaystyle u\cdot z

\displaystyle=z\cdot u=0,\quad\forall z\in\bar{A}.

Define a symmetric bilinear form
\gamma
on
\bar{A}
by

\displaystyle\gamma(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in V,

\displaystyle\gamma(x,u)

\displaystyle=\gamma(u,x)=0,\quad\forall x\in V,

\displaystyle\gamma(u,u)

\displaystyle=c.

Then
(\bar{A},\cdot,\gamma,u)
is a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra.

Proof.

The form
\gamma
is nondegenerate, and
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(\bar{A})

by construction. Since
u
is an annihilator, the pre-Lie identity only needs to be checked on
V
. For
x,y,z\in V
, the
V
-component is
the pre-Lie identity of
(V,*)
, while the
u
-component is

-f(x*y,z)+f(x,y*z)+f(y*x,z)-f(y,x*z),

which is zero because
f
is a Hessian
2
-cocycle.

It remains to check the
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle identity. For triples in
V
, it reduces to the Hessian
2
-cocycle identity for
f
.
If one of the first two variables is
u
, the only nontrivial cancellation
is

-\gamma(u,x\cdot z)-\gamma(u,u)\gamma(x,z)=-\gamma(u,x*z-f(x,z)u)-cf(x,z)=0.

for any
x,z\in V
. The case where the second variable is
u
is similar. If the third variable is
u
, the identity follows from the symmetry of
f
. The remaining cases are immediate. Hence
\gamma
is a nondegenerate
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\gamma,u)
be a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra such that
\gamma(u,u)\neq 0
. Then
(A,\cdot,\gamma,u)
is obtained from the construction in Theorem
4.1
.

Proof.

Set
V=u^{\perp}=\{x\in A\mid\gamma(x,u)=0\}
. Since
\gamma(u,u)\neq 0
, we have
A=V\oplus\langle u\rangle
, and
f:=\gamma|_{V\times V}
is nondegenerate. For
x,y\in V
, write

x\cdot y=x*y+\tau(x,y)u

with
x*y\in V
. Since
\langle u\rangle
is an annihilator ideal, the
quotient by
\langle u\rangle
shows that
(V,*)
is a left-symmetric
algebra.

Taking the
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle identity on
(u,x,z)
,
where
x,z\in V
, gives

-\gamma(u,x\cdot z)-\gamma(u,u)\gamma(x,z)=0.

Since
x\cdot z=x*z+\tau(x,z)u
, we get
\tau(x,z)=-f(x,z)
. Hence

x\cdot y=x*y-f(x,y)u

for all
x,y\in V
.

Finally, applying the
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle identity to triples in
V
gives the Hessian
2
-cocycle identity for
f
on
(V,*)
. Therefore
(V,*,f)
is a Hessian pre-Lie algebra, and the given algebra is obtained from Theorem
4.1
with
c=\gamma(u,u)
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(A,*,\gamma)
be a Hessian pre-Lie algebra. Fix a bilinear form
\beta:A\times A\to F
, endomorphisms
D,E\in\operatorname{End}(A)
, linear functionals
\partial,\rho\in A^{*}
, an element
b_{0}\in A
, and a
scalar
\mu\in F
. Put
\bar{A}=\langle u\rangle\oplus A\oplus\langle v\rangle
. Define a bilinear product
\cdot
on
\bar{A}
by

\displaystyle u\cdot a

\displaystyle=a\cdot u=0,\quad\forall a\in\bar{A},

\displaystyle x\cdot y

\displaystyle=x*y+\beta(x,y)u,\quad\forall x,y\in A,

\displaystyle v\cdot x

\displaystyle=D(x)+\partial(x)u,\quad\forall x\in A,

\displaystyle x\cdot v

\displaystyle=E(x)+\rho(x)u,\quad\forall x\in A,

\displaystyle v\cdot v

\displaystyle=b_{0}+\mu u+v.

Define a symmetric bilinear form
\bar{\gamma}
on
\bar{A}
by

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\gamma(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in A,

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\bar{\gamma}(v,u)=1,

and by requiring all other pairings involving
u
or
v
to be zero.
Assume that the following identities hold for all
x,y,z\in A
:

\displaystyle\gamma(D(x),z)-\beta(x,z)-\gamma(E(x),z)+\gamma(x,D(z))-\gamma(x,z)=0,

(G1)

\displaystyle\partial(x)-2\rho(x)+\gamma(x,b_{0})=0,

(G2)

\displaystyle\beta(x,y)-\beta(y,x)-\gamma(x,E(y))+\gamma(y,E(x))=0,

(G3)

\displaystyle\beta(x*y,z)-\beta(x,y*z)-\beta(y*x,z)+\beta(y,x*z)=0,

(L1)

\displaystyle E(x*y)-x*E(y)-E(y*x)+y*E(x)=0,

(L2)

\displaystyle\rho(x*y)-\beta(x,E(y))-\rho(y*x)+\beta(y,E(x))=0,

(L3)

\displaystyle D(x)*z-D(x*z)-E(x)*z+x*D(z)=0,

(L4)

\displaystyle\beta(D(x),z)-\partial(x*z)-\beta(E(x),z)+\beta(x,D(z))=0,

(L5)

\displaystyle E(D(x))-D(E(x))-E^{2}(x)+x*b_{0}+E(x)=0,

(L6)

\displaystyle\rho(D(x))-\partial(E(x))-\rho(E(x))+\beta(x,b_{0})+\rho(x)=0.

(L7)

Then
(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\gamma},u)
is a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra.

Proof.

Clearly, the form
\bar{\gamma}
is nondegenerate and
u\in\operatorname{Ann}(\bar{A})
. We first check the
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle identity. For triples in
A
, it reduces to the Hessian
2
-cocycle identity for
\gamma
. The cases involving
u
are automatic, except that the case
(u,v,v)
uses precisely the fact that the
v
-component of
v\cdot v
is
1
. The remaining nontrivial cases
(v,x,z)
,
(v,x,v)
, and
(x,y,v)
are respectively equivalent to
(\mathrm{G1})
,
(\mathrm{G2})
, and
(\mathrm{G3})
.

It remains to verify the pre-Lie identity. Since
u
is an annihilator, it suffices to consider variables in
A\cup\{v\}
. The cases
(x,y,z)
,
(x,y,v)
,
(v,x,z)
, and
(v,x,v)
are respectively equivalent to
(\mathrm{L1})
, to
(\mathrm{L2})
and
(\mathrm{L3})
, to
(\mathrm{L4})
and
(\mathrm{L5})
, and to
(\mathrm{L6})
and
(\mathrm{L7})
. The cases with the first two variables both equal to
v
are automatic. Hence
(\bar{A},\cdot)
is a pre-Lie algebra, and
\bar{\gamma}
is a nondegenerate
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle.
∎

The conditions (
G1
)–(
L7
) in Theorem
4.3
are rather involved. In fact, under suitable assumptions, all these conditions are automatically
satisfied.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
(A,*,\gamma)
be a Hessian pre-Lie algebra, and let
D\in\operatorname{Der}(A,*)
satisfy
\gamma(D(x),y)+\gamma(x,D(y))=0

for all
x,y\in A
. Put
\bar{A}=\langle u\rangle\oplus A\oplus\langle v\rangle
. Define a bilinear product
\cdot
on
\bar{A}
by

\displaystyle u\cdot a

\displaystyle=a\cdot u=0,\quad\forall a\in\bar{A},

\displaystyle x\cdot y

\displaystyle=x*y-\gamma(x,y)u,\quad\forall x,y\in A,

\displaystyle v\cdot x

\displaystyle=D(x),\quad\forall x\in A,

\displaystyle x\cdot v

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall x\in A,

\displaystyle v\cdot v

\displaystyle=v.

Define a symmetric bilinear form
\bar{\gamma}
on
\bar{A}
by

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\gamma(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in A,

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\bar{\gamma}(v,u)=1,

and by requiring all other pairings involving
u
or
v
to be zero. Then

(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\gamma},u)
is a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\gamma},u)
be a
u
-generalized Hessian pre-Lie algebra with
0\neq u
and
\bar{\gamma}(u,u)=0
. Then
(\bar{A},\cdot,\bar{\gamma},u)
is obtained from the construction in Theorem
4.3
.

Proof.

Since
\bar{\gamma}
is nondegenerate and
\bar{\gamma}(u,u)=0
, choose
v\in\bar{A}
such that

\bar{\gamma}(u,v)=1,\qquad\bar{\gamma}(v,v)=0.

Set
A=(\langle u\rangle\oplus\langle v\rangle)^{\perp}.
Then
\bar{A}=\langle u\rangle\oplus A\oplus\langle v\rangle,
and
\gamma:=\bar{\gamma}|_{A\times A}
is nondegenerate.

The
u
-generalized
2
-cocycle identity with one variable equal to

u
implies that, for
x,y\in A
, the products
x\cdot y
,
v\cdot x
, and
x\cdot v
have no
v
-component, while
v\cdot v
has

v
-component equal to
1
. Hence the multiplication has the form

\displaystyle x\cdot y

\displaystyle=x*y+\beta(x,y)u,\quad\forall x,y\in A,

\displaystyle v\cdot x

\displaystyle=D(x)+\partial(x)u,\quad\forall x\in A,

\displaystyle x\cdot v

\displaystyle=E(x)+\rho(x)u,\quad\forall x\in A,

\displaystyle v\cdot v

\displaystyle=b_{0}+\mu u+v.

Here
\beta:A\times A\to F
,
D,E\in\operatorname{End}(A)
,

\partial,\rho\in A^{*}
,
b_{0}\in A
, and
\mu\in F
.

Taking all three variables in
A
, the pre-Lie identity gives that
(A,*)
is a pre-Lie algebra, and the

-generalized
2
-cocycle identity gives the Hessian
2
-cocycle identity for
\gamma
. Hence
(A,*,\gamma)
is a Hessian pre-Lie algebra. Expanding the remaining

-generalized

-cocycle identities and the left-symmetric identity gives exactly
(\mathrm{G1})
–
(\mathrm{G3})
and
(\mathrm{L1})

(\mathrm{L7})
. Thus the given algebra is obtained from Theorem

.
∎

We start from Bai’s classification

Fix a representative pair

-cocycle identity, and the listed restrictions on the parameters are exactly those ensuring nondegeneracy.

It remains to determine isomorphisms. By Definition
4.6
, an isomorphism must be a pre-Lie algebra isomorphism preserving
u
and pulling back one bilinear form to the other. Thus, for each fixed pair
(A,u)
, the relevant group is

\operatorname{Aut}(A,u)=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)\mid\varphi(u)=u\}.

The action of this group on the symmetric matrix
(\gamma_{ij})
gives the
parameter identifications stated in the list. The only nontrivial permutation
appearing in the nonzero
u
classification is the exchange of the two idempotent factors in
\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus 0
, which gives the
unordered equivalence in
(6)
.

All remaining three-dimensional pre-Lie algebras in Bai’s list either have
\operatorname{Ann}(A)=0
, or the finite system above has no nondegenerate solution with
u\neq 0
, or the resulting structure is isomorphic to one of the displayed families. Hence the list is complete and irredundant.
∎

Example 4.8

Let

-dimensional pre-Lie algebra with nonzero products

e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},\qquad e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=2e_{3}.

Then
A
is non-abelian and
\operatorname{Ann}(A)=\mathbb{C}u
. This is
the
N1_{2}
-type algebra in Bai’s classification of three-dimensional
complex pre-Lie algebras. In the notation of Theorem
4.7
, it corresponds to the family
N1_{2,\kappa}
after identifying
e_{1}=\frac{1}{2}u
, equivalently, the distinguished element in that family is
u=2e_{1}
.

We first determine the symmetric solutions of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation on
A
. Let

\displaystyle r={}

\displaystyle a_{11}u\otimes u+a_{12}(u\otimes e_{2}+e_{2}\otimes u)+a_{13}(u\otimes e_{3}+e_{3}\otimes u)

\displaystyle+a_{22}e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+a_{23}(e_{2}\otimes e_{3}+e_{3}\otimes e_{2})+a_{33}e_{3}\otimes e_{3}\in S^{2}(A).

Substitution into the
u
-generalized

-equation gives

a_{12}=0,\qquad a_{23}=0,\qquad a_{13}(2a_{13}-1)=0,

and

(2a_{13}-1)a_{33}=0,\qquad(1-2a_{13})a_{22}=0,\qquad a_{22}a_{33}=0.

Hence every nonzero symmetric solution is of one of the following forms:

r_{\rho}=\rho\,u\otimes u,\qquad\rho\in\mathbb{C}^{*},

or

r(A,B,C)=A\,u\otimes u+B\,e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+C\,e_{3}\otimes e_{3}+\frac{1}{2}(u\otimes e_{3}+e_{3}\otimes u),\qquad BC=0.

(9)

The automorphisms of
A
fixing
u
are precisely

\psi_{\lambda}(u)=u,\qquad\psi_{\lambda}(e_{2})=\lambda e_{2},\qquad\psi_{\lambda}(e_{3})=e_{3},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}.

Indeed, if
\psi(u)=u
,
\psi(e_{2})=au+be_{2}+ce_{3}
, and
\psi(e_{3})=du+ee_{2}+fe_{3}
, then comparing the identities

e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=0,\qquad e_{2}\cdot e_{3}=0,\qquad e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},\qquad e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=2e_{3}

after applying
\psi
gives
a=c=d=e=0
,
f=1
, and
b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Therefore, up to automorphisms fixing
u
, the family (
9
) is represented by the following two normal forms:

If
B\neq 0
, then
C=0
, and after choosing
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
such that
\lambda^{2}B=1
, we get

r_{\mu}^{\mathrm{nd}}=\mu\,u\otimes u+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+\frac{1}{2}(u\otimes e_{3}+e_{3}\otimes u),\qquad\mu\in\mathbb{C}.

If
B=0
, then we get

r_{\mu,\nu}^{0}=\mu\,u\otimes u+\nu\,e_{3}\otimes e_{3}+\frac{1}{2}(u\otimes e_{3}+e_{3}\otimes u),\qquad\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{C}.

Together with the tensors
r_{\rho}
, these give all nonzero symmetric solutions up to automorphisms fixing
u
.

Type
1
solutions.

The rank-one solutions

r_{\rho}=\rho\,u\otimes u,\qquad\rho\in\mathbb{C}^{*},

are of type
1
, since

\operatorname{Im}(r_{\rho}^{\sharp})=\langle u\rangle.

The solutions
r_{\mu}^{\mathrm{nd}}
are also of type
1
. Indeed, the
matrix of
(r_{\mu}^{\mathrm{nd}})^{\sharp}
with respect to the basis
\{u,e_{2},e_{3}\}
is

\begin{pmatrix}\mu&0&\frac{1}{2}\\
0&1&0\\
\frac{1}{2}&0&0\end{pmatrix},

whose determinant is
-\frac{1}{4}
. Hence
r_{\mu}^{\mathrm{nd}}
is nondegenerate and

\operatorname{Im}\bigl((r_{\mu}^{\mathrm{nd}})^{\sharp}\bigr)=A.

By Proposition
3.10
and Theorem
3.6
, the generalized Hessian
forms in Theorem
4.7
give nondegenerate symmetric solutions of the
u
-generalized
S
-equation. Conversely, by
Proposition
3.12
, every
nondegenerate symmetric solution arises from a generalized Hessian form.
For the present algebra, the relevant generalized Hessian forms are

\gamma_{\kappa}(u,e_{3})=2,\qquad\gamma_{\kappa}(e_{2},e_{2})=1,\qquad\gamma_{\kappa}(e_{3},e_{3})=\kappa,\qquad\kappa\in\mathbb{C},

with all other values zero. The inverse tensor of
\gamma_{\kappa}
is

-\frac{\kappa}{4}u\otimes u+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+\frac{1}{2}(u\otimes e_{3}+e_{3}\otimes u),

which is
r_{\mu}^{\mathrm{nd}}
with
\mu=-\kappa/4
. Thus the nondegenerate type
1
solutions are exactly those obtained from the
generalized Hessian classification.

We now consider the remaining degenerate family
r_{\mu,\nu}^{0}
among

1

u
if and only if

\mu\nu-\frac{1}{4}\neq 0.

Thus
r_{\mu,\nu}^{0}
is of type
1
if and only if
\mu\nu\neq\frac{1}{4}
. In this case

\operatorname{Im}\bigl((r_{\mu,\nu}^{0})^{\sharp}\bigr)=\operatorname{span}\{u,e_{3}\},

which is a pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
.

Type
2
solutions.

The remaining solutions in the family
r_{\mu,\nu}^{0}
are precisely those
satisfying

\mu\nu=\frac{1}{4}.

In particular,
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. For such a solution, we have

\operatorname{Im}\bigl((r_{\mu,\nu}^{0})^{\sharp}\bigr)=\mathbb{C}\left(\mu u+\frac{1}{2}e_{3}\right),\qquad u\notin\operatorname{Im}\bigl((r_{\mu,\nu}^{0})^{\sharp}\bigr).

Therefore these are type
2
solutions in the sense of the preceding
definition.

Set
v_{\mu}=\mu u+\frac{1}{2}e_{3}
. Then

v_{\mu}\cdot v_{\mu}=\frac{1}{4}(e_{3}\cdot e_{3})=\frac{1}{2}e_{3}.

Since
\mu\neq 0
, we have
\frac{1}{2}e_{3}\notin\mathbb{C}v_{\mu}
. Thus,
\operatorname{Im}\bigl((r_{\mu,\nu}^{0})^{\sharp}\bigr)
is not a pre-Lie subalgebra of
A
. However,

\operatorname{Im}\bigl((r_{\mu,\nu}^{0})^{\sharp}\bigr)+\langle u\rangle=\operatorname{span}\{u,e_{3}\},

and
\operatorname{span}\{u,e_{3}\}
is a pre-Lie subalgebra, since

u\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
and
e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=2e_{3}
. This is exactly the
phenomenon described in Proposition
3.15
.

and
