1 Introduction
 In 2025, the Korea Atomic Energy Research Institute (KAERI) and Argonne National
Laboratory initiated a collaboration based on software validation using data from tests performed
at KAERI’s STELLA-2 facility. STELLA-2 is a large-scale thermal-hydraulic test facility that was
originally developed to support KAERI’s development of the PGSFR design concept. KAERI has
performed a large number of tests at STELLA-2, providing valuable data to support sodium fast
reactor code validation.
 STELLA-2 was designed as scaled version (one-fifth by length) of the PGSFR design concept.
The primary heat transport system (PHTS) includes an electrically heated core simulator, two
pump simulation loops (PSLS), one electromagnetic (EM) pump in each PSLS loop, four
intermediate heat exchangers (IHX), and four decay heat exchangers (DHX). STELLA-2 has two
intermediate heat transport systems (IHTS), each connected to two of the four IHXs. Each IHTS
loop has its own EM pump and a sodium-to-air ultimate heat exchanger (UHX). The UHX is the
standard heat rejection pathway rejecting most of the heat generated by the electrical core. To
replicate the PGSFR’s decay heat removal systems, STELLA-2 also includes four decay heat
removal systems (DHRS) and a reactor vessel cooling system (RVCS). One or both of these
systems become the heat rejection pathway during tests where the UHXs are disabled.
 Within this collaboration, KAERI will be performing validation with their GAMMA+ [1] code
and Argonne with their SAS4A/SASSYS-1 [2] code. In addition to direct validation against data
from STELLA-2 experiments, KAERI and Argonne will compare their modeling approaches and
simulation results to identify potential areas of improvement for their respective methodologies.
 KAERI plans to conduct new tests in 2026, targeting system conditions that were not achieved
during previous test campaigns. KAERI has requested from Argonne proposed tests that could be
performed during the upcoming test campaign. This report documents Argonne’s proposed tests
in fulfillment of KAERI’s request. Most of the STELLA-2 tests that were previously performed
simulated conditions that would be observed during protected transients, i.e. transients where the
reactor protection system successfully detects off-normal plant conditions and takes actions to
terminate the transient. During these transients, a reactor scram quickly terminates fission power,
leading to temperature reductions.
 Argonne has proposed tests that are more representative of unprotected transient conditions
where the reactor protection system either fails to detect the off-normal plant conditions or fails to
take actions in response to the transient. During these types of transients, the reactor core is
undercooled or overpowered, leading to temperature increases not experienced in previous tests.
Argonne’s proposed tests are designed to represent the conditions a system would experience
during unprotected loss of heat sink (ULOHS), transient overpower (UTOP), and loss of flow
(ULOF) type transients, which are transients often simulated to assess the inherent safety
characteristics of SFR designs and design concepts. Validation based on data from these types of
tests is important as they represent the three key ways to perturb a reactor core: through changes
to the core inlet temperature (ULOHS), reactivity (UTOP), and mass flow rate (ULOF).

2 Proposed STELLA-2 Tests
 Because each test can take as long as a week to run with KAERI’s testing procedures, KAERI
requested that Argonne propose approximately 10-15 higher priority tests. To encourage as many
new conditions for validation as possible, Argonne prioritized proposing variety of test types rather
than proposing a single test type with initial power and/or flow varied. Sections 2.1-2.3 below
summarize the ULOHS, UTOP, and ULOF type tests, respectively, that Argonne proposed.
Section 2.4 summarizes additional miscellaneous tests that do not fit into the categories of the first
three sections.
 For each system state from which tests are initiated, KAERI develops a specific procedure to
ensure the targeted initial temperatures, power level, and flow rates are all achieved accurately in
a repeatable manner. Many of the previously run protected-like tests were run with a core power
level close to its rated value and flow rates that ensured the core outlet temperature remained below
a 600°C limit. Specific core power levels, mass flow rates, and temperatures are not defined for
the proposed tests and instead of are suggested to be “half” or “full” with respect to previously run
tests. Defining precise values for those parameters is left to KAERI to fit within their testing
procedures and system capabilities.
 KAERI specifically requested minimizing the number of new initial system states they would
need to develop procedures to achieve. For this reason, the initial conditions for Argonne’s new
proposed tests are either the same “full power and full flow” conditions as the previously run
protected-like transients, or “half power and full flow” conditions. By starting at half power and
full flow and maintaining the same core inlet temperature as previously run tests, the core outlet
temperature would be lower initially, allowing for temperature increases during the proposed
undercooling or overpower transients to ensure the 600°C core outlet temperature limit is not
exceeded. Several values in the tables defining the tests below are to be determined (TBD) and
will be considered at a future time if KAERI decides to pursue those tests.
 For each proposed test defined in the sections below, the transient behavior of each key system
must be described. The following are the general target power levels specified for the electrical
core:
 • Increase/Decrease to X%: Core power increases or decreases to a percentage of the initial
 power level, replicated power changes via reactivity feedbacks,
 • Decay Heat Level: Gradual reduction to approximately a few percent of the initial power
 level, replicating power reduction via reactivity feedbacks, and
 • RVCS Level: Reduction to approximately half a percent of the initial power level after
 several hours, replicating total heat production stabilizing with total decay heat rejection in
 the long-term of a transient.
For most of the proposed transients, pre-test analysis is necessary to define the transient evolution
of power that best represents how reactivity feedbacks develop during such transients, subject to
facility operational restrictions.
 For the two primary pumps, PSLS1 and PSLS2, and the two intermediate pumps, IHTS1 and
IHTS2, each test was defined with one of the following transient conditions:

 2

 • Stay On: Constant power supplied to pumps,
 • Trip with Coastdown: Power supplied to pumps gradually reduced, and
 • Trip with Seizure: Power supplied to pumps abruptly terminated.
As the transient power evolution for each test is not defined in this report, the coastdown profile
following pump trip is also not defined. This will depend on a number of factors, including system
capabilities and limits, and can be discussed as part of pre-transient analysis for those tests with
coastdown that KAERI elects to pursue.
 For the UHXs, the following transient conditions are used for the proposed tests:
 • Stay On: Continue rejecting the nominal amount of heat,
 • Increase/Reduce to X%: UHX heat rejection increases or decreases to a percentage of its
 initial level, and
 • Trip: UHX heat rejection reduces as low as possible.
 The RVCS has two air inlet pipes and two air outlet pipes, which were installed on opposite
sides of the primary vessel. Most tests are defined with the RVCS On, though for two tests the
RVCS could be disabled (Off) without affecting the objectives of those tests. For three tests, RVCS
transient behavior is defined as “One Side Closed”, which is intended to induce asymmetries in
the cold pool temperature distribution.
 KAERI informed us that because they have already run many tests using the decay heat
removal system (DHRS), that system will be disabled for the upcoming tests. Therefore, the
proposed tests do not include any specification for DHRS transient behavior.

 2.1 Loss of Heat Sink Tests
 During an unprotected loss of heat sink transient, one or more initiating events result in heat
rejection to the intermediate system or balance of plant being significantly reduced or lost while
the primary pumps continue operating as normal. This leads to the cold pool and core inlet
temperatures increasing. In many pool-type SFRs such as the PGSFR [3,4], the increasing core
inlet temperature induces negative reactivity feedbacks that eventually terminate fission power in
the core. With flow through the core remaining approximately constant, the coolant temperature
rise across the core decreases proportionally with the decreasing power level. Often, for metallic
fueled pool-type SFR designs, the increasing core inlet temperature combines with the decreasing
coolant temperature rise to maintain a core outlet temperature that remains relatively close to its
pre-transient value. The system also gradually approaches isothermal conditions due to the primary
pumps remaining on.
 Argonne has proposed four ULOHS tests. The conditions of these tests are summarized in
Table 2.1. ULOHS #1 is the standard definition of a ULOHS transient that Argonne evaluates in
its safety analyses, such as those in Reference 5 for example. ULOHS #2 is an asymmetric version
with the loss of heat rejection through one IHTS train while the other IHTS train remains operating
as usual.

 ULOHS #3 is a modified version of ULOHS #1 where the primary pumps are tripped at some
point after the primary system has reached nearly isothermal conditions. This case is designed to
demonstrate how primary flow must be reduced gradually after achieving nearly isothermal
conditions due to the challenge of transitioning to natural circulation if the pumps were simply

margins are maintained to all temperature limits. ULOHS #4 is also a modified version of ULOHS
#1 to observe how asymmetric RVCS behavior impacts temperatures in the cold pool, and how
the differing conditions from ULOHS #1 further impact the rest of the primary system.

 2.2 Transient Overpower Tests
 During an unprotected transient overpower scenario, positive reactivity is introduced to the
core causing power to increase. This is often postulated to be due to an unintentional withdrawal
of a control rod. Core flow remains unchanged, so the overpowered core becomes undercooled.
During UTOP scenarios, fission power increases from the positive reactivity insertion.
Temperatures in the core increase, generating negative reactivity feedbacks that first bring the core
back to critical, then subcritical until an equilibrium between total power and total heat rejection
is reestablished. When the system finally stabilizes at its new condition, system temperatures are
higher than before the transient.
 Argonne has proposed two UTOP tests. UTOP #1 is the standard definition of a UTOP
transient that Argonne generally evaluates in its safety analyses. [5] The power level that the core
increases to depends on the magnitude of the positive reactivity insertion and the reactivity
feedbacks that are generated. With the core initially at half power, the 175% target power level is
intended to maintain temperatures below the nominal “full power and full flow” temperatures from
previous tests. Having core power return to 100% aligns with the assumption that heat rejection to
the balance of plant, or in STELLA’s case to the environment, remains at 100%. UTOP #2 is
identical to UTOP #1 except the UHX heat rejection rate is assumed to increase to 130% due to
the elevated sodium temperatures in the IHTS loops. During such a test, reactivity feedbacks would
adjust core power until it is 130% and the equilibrium between total power and total heat rejection
is reestablished.

 2.3 Loss of Flow Tests
 During an unprotected loss of flow transient, coolant flow through the reactor core is reduced
due to a pump fault, for example a loss of power to one or more pumps or a pump seizure. ULOF
transients are often simulated with other assumed component failures depending on what initiating
faults are assumed. During ULOF transients, temperatures increase in the undercooled core,
resulting in negative reactivity feedbacks. The resulting fission power reduction lags behind the
reducing core flow and the power-to-flow ratio increases. For transients and designs where the
PHTS transitions to natural circulation, core flow will level off within a few minutes while power
continues to reduce, and the power-to-flow ratio reduces, though it can reduce below its nominal
level depending on the design. In the longer term, core flow stabilizes at the level where the
buoyancy head from decay heat plus any remaining fission power balance the hydraulics losses
through the PHTS.
 Argonne has proposed five ULOF tests. ULOF #1 represents the conditions that would occur
during an unprotected station blackout (USBO), a specific type of loss of flow transient. During
this test, a loss of electrical power to all plant systems results in the primary and intermediate
pumps tripping, and a loss of heat rejection via the UHXs. Heat rejection duties transfer to the
RVCS. This is the standard unprotected loss of flow type transient that Argonne usually evaluates
in its safety analyses [5]. A safety grade power supply provides power to the primary pump
coastdown mechanism, allowing primary flow to transition gradually to natural circulation. ULOF
#2 is identical to ULOF #1 except the intermediate pumps do not trip.
 ULOFs #3 and #4 are asymmetric variations on ULOF #2 where only one of the two primary
pumps trips. In ULOF #3, coastdown is available for the tripping pump. In ULOF #4, the pump is
assumed to trip without coastdown. Similar to the final proposed ULOHS test, ULOF #5 is a
variation on the first ULOF test designed to observe how asymmetric RVCS behavior impacts
temperatures in the cold pool, and how the differing conditions from ULOF #1 further impact the
rest of the primary system.

 2.4 Miscellaneous Steady-State Tests
 Argonne has proposed two additional tests that are not ULOHS, UTOP, or ULOF transients.
In Misc. Test #1, the system is initialized to full power and full flow conditions with one side of
the RVCS closed. This test is intended to be used for characterization of the RVCS, particularly
for assessing how the resulting asymmetry affects steady-state conditions prior to efforts to model
RVCS asymmetries during transient conditions.
 In Misc. Test #2, the assemblies in the core are heated with a non-uniform power distribution
to induce temperature gradients across core assemblies. Such conditions would be useful for
evaluating the accuracy of a 1D systems code’s capability of accurately predicting those
temperature gradients, as well as for validation of subchannel and CFD codes. In this proposed
test, the core is initialized at some power level less than full power to enable power in some
assemblies to be increased. Once steady-state is achieved, the power supplied to one assembly is
reduced, for example to 50%, while power in all other assemblies is increased slightly to keep the
total core power level constant. Because temperatures outside the core would remain constant in
such a test, a number of these tests could be run back-to-back by changing the power distribution,
so the core outlet temperature remains constant.

3 Preliminary Test Prioritization
 This report formally presents Argonne’s proposed tests to be run in the STELLA-2
experimental test facility. Argonne has previously presented these tests to KAERI who have
indicated preliminarily which tests they consider high, medium, and low priority to perform in the
next STELLA-2 test campaign. Argonne agrees with their justification for test prioritization.
 Because experimental test campaigns for SFRs generally include a number of loss of flow type
transients, the proposed ULOF tests are considered low priority for KAERI. EBR-II’s Shutdown
Heat Removal Test (SHRT) program, for example, included dozens of loss of flow tests while
FFTF’s Passive Safety Test (PST) program included thirteen unprotected loss of flow tests.
 ULOHS transients are less common in test campaigns. The SHRT program included only three
such tests. Therefore, KAERI has marked ULOHS #1, ULOHS #2, and ULOHS #4 as high priority
and indicated their interest in performing these tests during the next test campaign. Because Misc.
Test #1 represents the steady-state condition for ULOHS #4, KAERI has also marked that test as
high priority.
 UTOP-type tests are rarely performed in SFR test campaigns and therefore KAERI believes
they have a higher priority than other tests. Because these tests require trial runs for KAERI to
gain confidence that they can be performed properly and within system limits, KAERI has labeled
the UTOP cases as mid-priority. The proposed UTOP-types will be considered during upcoming
efforts to define the test matrix but are more likely to be performed at a later date once KAERI
understands how best to perform them.
