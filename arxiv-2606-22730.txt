Optimal Estimating Equations for Compact-Memory Hawkes Processes
Louis Davis ??, Conor Kresin ??

Abstract
Likelihood is standard for Hawkes-process inference, while less computationally demanding methods have largely developed separately. We show that least squares, Takács–Fiksel, and related moment-based estimators form a single class of compensator-based estimating equations, with the likelihood score as the efficient benchmark. For fixed-dimensional multivariate Hawkes processes with compact memory, nonlinear positive links, and signed kernels allowing inhibition, every suitably regular predictable functional of a fixed lag window yields an unbiased estimating equation when integrated against d ​ N − λ ​ d ​ t \mathrm{d}N-\lambda\,\mathrm{d}t . Under common regularity, identification, and rank conditions, estimators based on every admissible finite library achieve uniform high-probability and pointwise almost-sure 𝒪 ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( T ) / T ) \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log(T)/T}) rates, asymptotic normality with Godambe covariance, and admit feasible two-step optimal weighting. A projection identity quantifies each library’s exact efficiency loss as the score information outside its predictable span; a two-point bound shows the root- T T scale cannot be improved uniformly. Although compact memory localizes the intensity rather than the stationary law, exponential forgetting yields Bernstein-type concentration and transfers the theory to nonstationary starts after a logarithmic burn-in. Within this scope, the compensator class is exhaustive for finite-library comparisons: it contains the score, gives admissible libraries common guarantees, and quantifies their efficiency gaps exactly.

Introduction

The martingale compensator identity

\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\{\mathrm{d}N(t)-\lambda(t;\theta^{\star})\,\mathrm{d}t\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-}\}=0

(1.1)

is the natural moment identity for a point process
N
characterised by conditional intensity
\lambda(t;\theta^{\star})
at the true parameter
\theta^{\star}

(
Daley and Vere-Jones 2008
)
. When integrated against a predictable weight
H(t;\theta)
, Equation (
1.1
) defines a broad family of unbiased martingale estimating equations of the form

\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta)=\int_{0}^{T}H(t;\theta)\{\mathrm{d}N(t)-\lambda(t;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t\}.

(1.2)

Members of this family of estimators include least squares, maximum likelihood (MLE), Takács–Fiksel and generalized method of moments (GMM). The family (
1.2
) is the point process analogue to Godambe’s estimating-function theory
(
Godambe 1991
)
.

Such estimating equations have long been employed for spatial point processes: the spatial analogue to Equation (
1.1
) is referred to as the Campbell–Mecke identity in the Poisson case
(
Daley and Vere-Jones 2008
)
, and the Georgii–Nguyen–Zessin (GNZ) formula more generally
(
Georgii 1976
;
Xanh and Zessin 1979
)
. In the case of spatial Gibbs processes, the likelihood has an intractable normalising constant, and Takács–Fiksel estimation
(
Fiksel 1984
)
(of which the Stoyan–Grabarnik statistic is most well known
(
Stoyan and Grabarnik 1991
;
Kresin and Schoenberg 2023
)
) is often applied alongside Godambe-optimal quasi-likelihood and various GMM approaches
(
Coeurjolly et al. 2016
;
Guan et al. 2015
)
. For Hawkes processes
(
Hawkes 1971
;
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
)
, the situation is inverted: the likelihood is available in closed form, and has dominated both theory and practical application
(
Ogata 1978
;
Clinet and Yoshida 2017
;
Kwan et al. 2025
)
. Perhaps for this reason, a theory of predictable compensator moments, containing the score as one member and pricing every other member against it, has remained undeveloped.

There are good reasons to develop one. Likelihood is asymptotically efficient but computationally expensive: each evaluation is quadratic in the event count for non-Markovian Hawkes processes, and a
D
-variate model has
\mathcal{O}(D^{2})
parameters corresponding to the full interaction kernel. Because of this, dense multivariate settings are often assumed away, or sparsity-inducing estimation strategies are used to make estimation tractable
(
Hansen et al. 2015
)
. Recent work has accordingly turned to penalized least squares
(
Reynaud-Bouret and Schbath 2010
;
Hansen et al. 2015
;
Bacry et al. 2020
)
, spectral estimation
(
Cheysson and Lang 2022
;
Tang et al. 2026
)
, and nonparametric Bayesian modelling
(
Sulem et al. 2024
;
Donnet et al. 2020
)
. Likelihood-based approaches have made recent headway for nonlinear Hawkes processes
(
Bonnet et al. 2023
)
. But each of these approaches provides a remedy for a relatively narrow gap.

At the cost of assuming compact memory (
à la

Hansen et al. 2015
and
Donnet et al. 2020
), we develop a unifying fixed-dimensional theory for a large class of Hawkes processes and the complete family of compensator moment-based estimators. Our theory has another dividend: its standardized moments are precisely the summary statistics that make simulation-based inference
(
McFadden 1989
;
Cranmer et al. 2020
)
work (see Remark
2.9
). In the compact-memory setting the conditional intensity is a predictable functional of a realisation on a fixed lag window
[t-A,t)
. We assume the parameter dimension
p=\dim(\Theta)
and
D
the number of components are fixed, i.e. do not grow with
T
. We further assume that
q
, the number of scalar moment conditions generated by the weight
H
(equivalently the number of rows of
H
) is fixed (although
q>p
is permitted without necessitating regularisation).

A row of the estimating weight
H
can be any smooth predictable feature of the realisation viewed on
[t-A,t)
, controlled by a polynomial local-count envelope. This means that binned counts, lagged summaries, inter-arrival times, filtered convolutions, and generally any smooth transformation are all admissible members of the same finite feature library. In particular, as noted above, this includes the likelihood score and least squares (see Example
2.1
). The model class our results apply to allows for nonlinear positive link functions
(
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
)
as well as signed interaction kernels (allowing for inhibition, cf.
(
Bonnet et al. 2023
)
). Further, our theory accommodates nonstationary initial conditions, allowing inference without assuming equilibrium at the observation origin.

Under one single set of mild, checkable assumptions, we prove uniform high probability and almost sure
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log(T)/T})
rates for compact window estimators of the form of (
1.2
). Further, we prove asymptotic normality with sandwich covariance, a feasible two-step optimal weighting, and a Godambe projection theorem that says that no admissible finite moment library carries more information than the likelihood score, and that the cost for not using it is exactly the variance of the score component orthogonal to the library’s predictable span. As an immediate corollary, the same results apply to the stationary MLE, and a two-point Le Cam bound shows that the root-
T
scale cannot be uniformly improved.

The conclusion is clean: even for dependent data, with appropriate concentration, i.i.d. behavior reappears. Rates, sandwich covariance and feasible optimal weighting, and most importantly optimal likelihood efficiency present just as they do in the i.i.d. case, where the Cramér Rao lower bound is only attained by the optimally weighted GMM estimator when the moment library spans the score
(
Chamberlain 1987
;
Carrasco and Florens 2014
)
. The technical inputs to achieve this i.i.d.-like behavior rely on the Poisson embedding and exponential forgetting arguments of
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
(which provide a way around the core challenge of our setting, namely that compact support localizes the intensity but not the stationary law) and the compact window functional coupling argument of
Hansen et al. 2015
. We note the behavior of the MLE for Hawkes processes is well understood in the existing literature (for instance in the univariate case
(
Ogata 1978
)
, the multivariate Markovian case
(
Clinet and Yoshida 2017
)
). What we provide is a comprehensive and unified treatment, in the compact-memory setting, of a larger estimator class containing the MLE.

2
details the model, estimator class, standing assumptions and main results. Section
3
then contextualizes the scope of our results, and the costs of the assumptions stated in the previous section, verifying them on two model families. Section
4
discusses the probabilistic tools needed to obtain concentration, and a simulation study and discussion are provided in Sections
5
and
6
. All proofs are contained in the supplement.

2
Model, estimators, and main results

In this section we describe the model class, estimator family, and then standing assumptions and main statistical results. The implicit scope, limitations and verification that such assumptions are reasonable is deferred to Section
3
, and the necessary technical probabilistic tools are detailed in Section
4
.

2.1
Compact-memory Hawkes processes

Let
N=(N_{1},\ldots,N_{D})
be a simple stationary point process on
\mathbb{R}
with natural filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. For
\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the
i^{\text{th}}
component intensity is

\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)=\phi_{\theta,i}\left(\nu_{\theta,i}+\sum_{j=1}^{D}\int_{[t-A,t)}h_{\theta,ij}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s)\right),\qquad i=1,\ldots,D,

(2.1)

where
\nu_{\theta,i}
is the baseline rate, and the interaction kernels
h_{\theta,ij}(u)=\tilde{h}_{\theta,ij}(u)\mathbf{1}\{0\leq u\leq A\}
are compactly supported and can take either sign (positive values represent excitation, negative values inhibition). Note that
A
is fixed and known. The smooth positive link function
\phi_{\theta,i}
ensures that the intensity is positive; in the canonical linear case
(
Hawkes 1971
)
,
\phi_{\theta,i}(x)=x
. Under the spectral radius condition Assumption
2.3(c)
, a multivariate Hawkes process characterized by (
2.1
) exists and is stationary
(
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
)
. Compact support means only that
\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)
is a finite-memory functional of
N|_{[t-A,t)}
. It does not imply stationarity of the law, nor Poisson-type independence of disjoint time windows.

Next, we introduce some notation. The true parameter
\theta^{\star}
lies in a compact set
\Theta_{0}\Subset\Theta^{\circ}
. The conditional log-likelihood on
[0,T]
is

\ell_{T}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\log\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)\mathrm{d}N_{i}(t)-\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)\mathrm{d}t\right).

(2.2)

For a configuration
N
, let
G_{t}N
denote
N|_{[t-A,t)}
recentred on
[-A,0)
, and write
C(x)=x([-A,0))
for a local configuration on
x
; the map
G_{t}
standardizes the time-
t
window to the reference window
[-A,0)
, so that intensities, weights, and their stationary expectations can all be written as fixed functionals applied to
G_{t}N
. We denote the local count

\mathfrak{C}_{t}=N([t-A,t))=\sum_{j=1}^{D}N_{j}([t-A,t))

noting that the half-open convention is used for predictability.

2.2
Compensator moments

Let
H(t;\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times D}
be predictable with fixed
q\geq p
. The estimating map (Equation (
1.2
)) is restated here:

\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{T}H_{\cdot i}(t;\theta)\{\mathrm{d}N_{i}(t)-\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t\}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}.

Write
M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}(t)=N_{i}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{i}(s;\theta^{\star})\,\mathrm{d}s
for the compensated components under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}
. The estimating map splits into a martingale term and a drift,

\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{T}H_{\cdot i}(t;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}H(t;\theta)\{\lambda(t;\theta^{\star})-\lambda(t;\theta)\}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.3)

Under stationarity the population drift is

g_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star})=\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[H(0;\theta)\{\lambda(0;\theta^{\star})-\lambda(0;\theta)\}\right].

(2.4)

At the truth,
\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})
is a martingale. For a fixed deterministic positive-definite matrix
W
, the GMM criterion and estimator are

Q_{T,W}^{H}(\theta)=\left\|T^{-1}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta)\right\|_{W}^{2},\qquad\|x\|_{W}^{2}=x^{\top}Wx,

(2.5)

with
\hat{\theta}_{H}=\hat{\theta}_{H,W}
any measurable minimizer over
\Theta
(Supplement Proposition
S3.1
). The likelihood score is the specific weight

H_{\rm score}(t;\theta)=D_{\theta}(t;\theta)^{\top}\Lambda(t;\theta)^{-1},

(2.6)

where here and throughout

D_{\theta}(t;\theta)=\partial_{\theta}\lambda(t;\theta),\qquad\Lambda(t;\theta)=\diag\{\lambda_{1}(t;\theta),\ldots,\lambda_{D}(t;\theta)\},

(2.7)

so that
\Psi_{T}^{H_{\rm score}}(\theta)=\nabla\ell_{T}(\theta)
: the score is not outside the moment theory but rather it is the efficient member (Proposition
2.8
).

Example 2.1
(Four canonical weight families).

The class contains the standard constructions.
Score weights

H=D_{\theta}^{\top}\Lambda^{-1}
give maximum likelihood
(
Ogata 1978
)
.
Derivative weights

H=D_{\theta}^{\top}
give the first-order equation for least squares: the point-process least-squares contrast

\gamma_{T}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\left\{\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t-\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)\,\mathrm{d}N_{i}(t)\right\}

(2.8)

of
Reynaud-Bouret and Schbath 2010
(see also
Hansen et al. 2015
;
Bacry et al. 2020
) satisfies
\nabla_{\theta}\gamma_{T}(\theta)=-(2/T)\,\Psi_{T}^{D_{\theta}^{\top}}(\theta)
, so its first-order equation is the derivative-weight member of the class. The estimator defined by global minimization of
\gamma_{T}
is treated in Supplement Proposition
S3.5
; this avoids imposing a global no-spurious-critical-point condition on the derivative moment.
Inverse-intensity weights

H=Z\Lambda^{-1}
, for compact-window features
Z
, give temporal Stoyan–Grabarnik identities
(
Stoyan and Grabarnik 1991
;
Cronie and Van Lieshout 2018
;
Kresin and Schoenberg 2023
)
, matching
\sum_{i}\int Z_{\cdot i}\lambda_{i}^{-1}\mathrm{d}N_{i}
to
\int\sum_{i}Z_{\cdot i}\,\mathrm{d}t
.
Direct weights

H=Z
give Campbell–Mecke/Takács–Fiksel equations that match event sums to compensator integrals
(
Takacs 1986
;
Coeurjolly et al. 2016
;
Last and Penrose 2017
)
. Each moment family is admissible under the standing assumptions when its weight satisfies Assumption
2.3(e)
and the corresponding identification, rank, and covariance conditions hold; the envelopes are automatic for the score and derivative weights, where they follow from
2.3(a)
–
2.3(b)
, and otherwise reduce to a polynomial-envelope condition on the feature
Z
.

The four families above are illustrative rather than exhaustive. The admissible-weight formulation also accommodates smooth contrast criteria through their first-order equations.

Remark 2.2
(Separable Bregman contrasts).

The score and least-squares weights are also special cases of a separable
Bregman-contrast construction
(
Bregman 1967
;
Gneiting and Raftery 2007
)
. Let

b:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be
C^{3}
and strictly convex, set

g_{b}(x)=xb^{\prime}(x)-b(x)
, and define

\Gamma_{b,T}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\left\{T^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}g_{b}(\lambda_{i}(t;\theta))\,\mathrm{d}t-T^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}b^{\prime}(\lambda_{i}(t;\theta))\,\mathrm{d}N_{i}(t)\right\}.

Writing
\Phi_{b}(t;\theta)=\diag\{b^{\prime\prime}(\lambda_{1}(t;\theta)),\ldots,b^{\prime\prime}(\lambda_{D}(t;\theta))\}
and
H_{b}(t;\theta)=D_{\theta}(t;\theta)^{\top}\Phi_{b}(t;\theta)
, suppose that
H_{b}
is admissible in the sense of Assumption
2.3(e)
. Then

\nabla_{\theta}\Gamma_{b,T}(\theta)=-T^{-1}\Psi_{T}^{H_{b}}(\theta).

Thus the first-order condition for
\Gamma_{b,T}
is exactly the compensator estimating equation with weight
H_{b}
. The choices
b(x)=x^{2}
and
b(x)=x\log x-x
make
\Gamma_{b,T}
, respectively, the least-squares contrast (
2.8
) and exactly
-T^{-1}\ell_{T}(\theta)
. In the latter case,
H_{b}=H_{\mathrm{score}}
, so
\Psi_{T}^{H_{b}}(\theta)=\nabla_{\theta}\ell_{T}(\theta)
.

We now proceed to define various functionals necessary for the analysis of the asymptotic geometry of our setting.

A_{H}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\{H(0;\theta)D_{\theta}(0;\theta)\},

(2.9)

\Omega_{H}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\{H(0;\theta)\Lambda(0;\theta)H(0;\theta)^{\top}\},

(2.10)

their plug-in version

\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\theta)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}H(t;\theta)\Lambda(t;\theta)H(t;\theta)^{\top}\,\mathrm{d}t,

(2.11)

and, for local expansions, the two-parameter drift Jacobian

B_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star})=-\partial_{\theta}g_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star}),\qquad B_{H}(\theta,\theta)=A_{H}(\theta).

(2.12)

Here
A_{H}
records how the moments respond, on average, to local parameter changes, and
\Omega_{H}
records their intrinsic martingale variability per unit time. Both
A_{H}
and
\Omega_{H}
are time-free by stationarity,
\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}
is the natural plug-in, and
B_{H}
controls expansions of the drift away from the diagonal.

2.3
Standing assumptions

All results below are stated under the following block of standing assumptions. Parts (a)-(d) concern the model; parts (e)-(f) are relative to a given weight
H
. Section
3
discusses what each part means and how it is verified for specific examples. The GMM results of Section
2.4
run on (a)-(c) and (e)-(f); part (d) enters through the likelihood benchmark, the least-squares contrast result in the supplement, and the efficiency comparison.

Assumption 2.3
(Standing assumptions).

Throughout the remainder of the paper, the dimensions
p
,
D
, and
q
are fixed and do not depend on
T
, and we assume

(a)

(Smooth positive links.)
The parameter set
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
is compact, with
\Theta_{0}\Subset\Theta^{\circ}
, and is contained in a compact convex set
K_{\Theta}\Subset\Theta^{+}
, where
\Theta^{+}
is the natural parameter domain. For each
i
, let
\mathcal{X}_{i}(K_{\Theta})
denote the set of linear-predictor values in (
2.1
) attainable with
\theta\in K_{\Theta}
. The maps
\theta\mapsto\nu_{\theta,i}
and
(\theta,x)\mapsto\phi_{\theta,i}(x)
are
C^{3}
on neighborhoods of
K_{\Theta}
and
K_{\Theta}\times\mathcal{X}_{i}(K_{\Theta})
, respectively. Uniformly in
i
,
\phi_{\theta,i}
is bounded away from zero on this set,
\partial_{x}\phi_{\theta,i}
is bounded, and all mixed
(\theta,x)
-derivatives of
\phi_{\theta,i}
up to order three have at most polynomial growth in
x
. The derivatives of
\nu_{\theta,i}
up to order three are uniformly bounded on
K_{\Theta}
. Structural-zero coordinates are removed before applying the theory.

(b)

(Smooth compact kernels.)
Each
\tilde{h}_{\theta,ij}
and its active parameter derivatives up to order three are uniformly bounded and continuous on
[0,A]\times K_{\Theta}
, with second derivatives uniformly Lipschitz in
\theta
. Explicitly,
A
is assumed to be fixed and known.

(c)

(Uniform absolute-kernel stability.)
With
L_{\theta,i}
a Lipschitz constant for the
i^{\text{th}}
link and
G^{\mathrm{abs}}(\theta)_{ij}=L_{\theta,i}\int_{0}^{A}|h_{\theta,ij}(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u
, there is
\varepsilon>0
such that
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\rho\{G^{\mathrm{abs}}(\theta)\}\leq 1-\varepsilon
, where
\rho(\cdot)
denotes the spectral radius.

(d)

(Identifiability and Fisher information.)
For all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
, if
\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)=\lambda_{i}(t;\theta^{\prime})
for every
i
and Lebesgue-a.e.
t
,
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
-a.s., then
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
—equivalently, under
2.3(c)
,
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
is injective. The per-time Fisher information

I(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{D}\frac{\nabla_{\theta}\lambda_{i}(0;\theta)\nabla_{\theta}\lambda_{i}(0;\theta)^{\top}}{\lambda_{i}(0;\theta)}\right]

(2.13)

is well defined and uniformly nonsingular on
\Theta_{0}
.

(e)

(Admissible compact-window weights.)

H
is shift-covariant and compact-window: for
r=0,1,2
there are measurable maps
\mathsf{H}_{\theta}^{(r)}
on local configurations on
[-A,0)
, continuous in
\theta
, with

\nabla_{\theta}^{r}H(t;\theta)=\mathsf{H}_{\theta}^{(r)}(G_{t}N),\qquad r=0,1,2,

(2.14)

satisfying the polynomial local-count and Lipschitz envelopes

\|\nabla_{\theta}^{r}H(t;\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq B_{r}\{1+\mathfrak{C}_{t}^{m_{r}}\},\qquad r=0,1,2,

(2.15)

\|\nabla_{\theta}^{r}H(t;\theta)-\nabla_{\theta}^{r}H(t;\theta^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq B^{\prime}_{r}\{1+\mathfrak{C}_{t}^{m^{\prime}_{r}}\}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{2},\qquad r=0,1,2.

(2.16)

(f)

(Moment identification and rank.)
For every
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
,
g_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star})=0
if and only if
\theta=\theta^{\star}
; moreover, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\inf_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\inf_{\|\theta-\theta^{\star}\|_{2}\geq\epsilon}\|g_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star})\|_{2}>0,

(2.17)

\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\lambda_{\min}\{A_{H}(\theta)^{\top}A_{H}(\theta)\}>0,

(2.18)

and
\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\lambda_{\min}\{\Omega_{H}(\theta)\}=\omega_{0}>0
.

Throughout Section
2.4
, the constants
C,K
may be taken to depend only on the constants named in (a)-(f), the dimensions
D,p,q
, the memory length
A
, and the exponent
c
. Of primary importance, they carry no hidden dependence on
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
.

2.4
Rates, limits, and efficiency

We now state the main results of the paper. These results are the compact-window martingale counterparts of finite-dimensional GMM consistency, sandwich normality, and two-step optimal weighting
(
Hansen 1982
;
Pakes and Pollard 1989
;
van der Vaart 1998
)
.

Theorem 2.4
(GMM high-probability rate).

Let
\hat{\theta}_{H}
be a measurable global minimizer of (
2.5
) with fixed deterministic positive-definite
W
. Under Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
and
2.3(e)
–
2.3(f)
, for every
c>0
there are
C,K<\infty
such that, for all sufficiently large
T
,

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\lVert\hat{\theta}_{H}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}>K\sqrt{\frac{\log T}{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c}.

(2.19)

Theorem 2.5
(GMM asymptotic normality).

Fix
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.4
,

\sqrt{T}(\hat{\theta}_{H}-\theta^{\star})\Rightarrow N_{p}\{0,V_{H}(W;\theta^{\star})\},

(2.20)

with

V_{H}(W;\theta^{\star})=(A_{H}^{\top}WA_{H})^{-1}A_{H}^{\top}W\Omega_{H}WA_{H}(A_{H}^{\top}WA_{H})^{-1},

which for
W=\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})^{-1}
reduces to

V_{H}^{\rm opt}(\theta^{\star})=\{A_{H}(\theta^{\star})^{\top}\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})^{-1}A_{H}(\theta^{\star})\}^{-1}.

Efficient weighting balances sensitivity against variability, exactly as in classical two-step GMM
(
Hansen 1982
)
; the Gaussian limit is a martingale central limit theorem for stochastic integrals
(
Rebolledo 1980
;
Jacod and Shiryaev 2003
)
.

Corollary 2.6
(Feasible optimal GMM).

Let
\tilde{\theta}_{H}
satisfy (
2.19
), set
\hat{W}_{T}=\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{H})^{-1}
, as per equation (
2.11
), on the event that this inverse exists and
\hat{W}_{T}=I_{q}
otherwise, and let
\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)}
be a measurable minimizer of
\vartheta\mapsto m_{T}(\vartheta)^{\top}\hat{W}_{T}m_{T}(\vartheta)
with
m_{T}(\vartheta)=T^{-1}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.4
, for every
c>0
there are
C,K<\infty
such that, for all sufficiently large
T
,

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\lVert\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}>K\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c},

(2.21)

and, for each fixed
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
,

\sqrt{T}\left(\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)}-\theta^{\star}\right)\Rightarrow N_{p}\!\left(0,\{A_{H}(\theta^{\star})^{\top}\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})^{-1}A_{H}(\theta^{\star})\}^{-1}\right).

(2.22)

Corollary 2.7
(Almost-sure logarithmic rates).

Fix
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
and write
\hat{\theta}_{H,T}
,
\hat{\theta}_{H,T}^{(2)}
when the horizon must be explicit. Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.4

3
Assumptions, examples, and scope

The assumptions are intentionally stronger than the weakest possible: they isolate the dependence and efficiency questions without boundary, sparsity, or high-dimensional pathologies, which require different tools. This section explains what each part asks of a model and of a moment library, verifies the conditions on two kernel families and on a genuinely non-score library, and records that the theory requires neither sign restrictions on the kernels nor stationary initialization.

Assumption
2.3
serves three purposes. Parts
2.3(a)
and
2.3(b)
are regularity conditions. The link
\phi_{\theta,i}
acts on the inner linear predictor, and
2.3(a)
asks for smoothness and positivity only on the predictor values the model can actually reach (its input domain). With excitation alone these values never fall below the baseline, so a positive set suffices and the identity link
\phi(x)=x
is admissible. Inhibition removes that floor: negative kernels can drive the predictor below zero and, as recent events accumulate, across an unbounded part of
\mathbb{R}
. A signed model whose predictor ranges this widely therefore needs a link that stays positive and smooth on all of
\mathbb{R}
, while a positive linear subfamily is already covered on its positive domain.

The stability assumption
2.3(c)
is the same subcriticality assumption used by Brémaud–Massoulié for their Poisson-embedding theory
(
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
)
, and its absolute values are not cosmetic: a disagreement coupling must dominate propagation without relying on cancellation, so it is the absolute kernels, not the signed ones, that must be uniformly subcritical. Under
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
the embedding admits a stationary solution for every
\theta\in\Theta
, stochastically dominated, under a common Poisson embedding, by the linear Hawkes process with baselines
\phi_{\theta,i}(\nu_{\theta,i})
and nonnegative kernels
L_{\theta,i}|h_{\theta,ij}|
. That dominating process is subcritical uniformly in
\theta
, so its fixed-window counts have uniformly bounded exponential moments
(
Leblanc 2024
)
; the domination passes the bound to
N
, giving, for every fixed
L<\infty
and some
\xi_{L},C_{L}>0
,

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\bigl[\exp\{\xi_{L}N([s,s+L])\}\bigr]\leq C_{L}.

This uniform control of fixed-window counts (Supplement Lemma
S1.2
) is the moment input the concentration and envelope arguments rely on throughout.

The remaining conditions concern identification, at two levels: the model, and the finite moment library an estimator actually uses. Part
2.3(d)
is the model-level condition. It ensures that distinct parameters produce distinct intensity paths, and the Fisher information
I(\theta)
must be nonsingular. When this condition is met,
\theta
is recoverable. The other two parts govern an estimator that replaces the whole path with a handful of summaries. Part
2.3(e)
keeps that library well-behaved (finitely many compact-window, shift-covariant features with polynomial count envelopes) so the limit functionals
A_{H}
and
\Omega_{H}
exist and are time-free. Part
2.3(f)
then asks that the summaries still determine
\theta
: the population moment
g_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star})
vanishes only at the truth, with rank and covariance margins. This does not follow from
2.3(d)
. A model identified from its full path can be underdetermined by a small library, exactly as a well-identified GMM model can still carry irrelevant or rank-deficient instruments
(
Hansen 1982
;
Newey 1990
)
.

The derivative library
H=D_{\theta}^{\top}
of Example
2.1
is instructive. Its drift is a gradient,

g_{D_{\theta}^{\top}}(\theta,\theta^{\star})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_{\theta}\,\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\bigl\|\lambda(0;\theta)-\lambda(0;\theta^{\star})\bigr\|_{2}^{2},

so zeros of the drift are critical points of the population least-squares contrast. Model identifiability implies that this contrast has the unique global minimizer
\theta^{\star}
, but it does not by itself exclude other critical points of the derivative drift. Thus the derivative-moment GMM theorem applies to
H=D_{\theta}^{\top}
once (
2.17
) is verified, while the globally minimized least-squares contrast is covered separately in Supplement Proposition
S3.5
. The local rank and covariance margins for the derivative expansion are automatic, since pointwise
D_{\theta}^{\top}D_{\theta}\succeq\underline{\lambda}\,D_{\theta}^{\top}\Lambda^{-1}D_{\theta}
and
D_{\theta}^{\top}\Lambda D_{\theta}\succeq\underline{\lambda}\,D_{\theta}^{\top}D_{\theta}
, so that
A_{D_{\theta}^{\top}}(\theta)\succeq\underline{\lambda}\,I(\theta)
and
\Omega_{D_{\theta}^{\top}}(\theta)\succeq\underline{\lambda}\,A_{D_{\theta}^{\top}}(\theta)
are uniformly nonsingular on
\Theta_{0}
.

Generally, the pattern for verifying a GMM library is to compute the drift
g_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star})
, show its zero set is
\{\theta^{\star}\}
with a separation margin, check that
A_{H}(\theta)
has full column rank on the active set, and remove redundant rows so that
\Omega_{H}(\theta)
is nonsingular. For least squares, the global step is instead contrast separation, supplied by model identifiability; the derivative moment is used locally through the first-order condition. Worked verifications for the truncated-exponential, smooth-rectifier, and one-point-age libraries are collected in Supplement Section
S6
.

Example 3.1

Example 3.2
(Smooth-ReLU nonlinear Hawkes with truncated-exponential filters).

3.2
Inhibition and nonstationary starts

Two modelling idealisations are ubiquitous in the current literature: the first is that interactions are excitatory, and the second is that the process is observed in equilibrium (i.e. that it is observed with a stationary start). The results in Section
2.4
assume neither of these (often artificial) modelling conveniences.

Remark 3.4
(Signed and inhibitory compact-memory interactions).

Under Assumptions

–

Proposition 3.5
(Logarithmic burn-in from nonstationary initial histories).

Suppose Assumptions

–

Consequently every stationary high-probability bound for a compact-window statistic computed over an interval of length

Stationary initialization is the standard idealization of applied Hawkes inference: consistency and asymptotic normality are established for the equilibrium process, as though the data had been generated by a system running since the infinite past, whereas a real record begins at a finite and generally out-of-equilibrium time. The proposition shows the idealization is inessential: any start with finite expected local count is forgotten after a logarithmic burn-in, and the stationary rates and limit laws then govern the estimator computed from the finite, nonstationary record actually observed. The transfer device itself is elementary: the variational characterization of total variation, applied after mapping the path to the statistic (Supplement Lemma

4
The probabilistic mechanism

Compact support in (
2.1
) makes
\lambda(t;\theta)
depend only on the window
[t-A,t)
. But crucially, this locality is pathwise, and does not extend to the entire stationary law because two faraway windows can contain points that share an ancestor. For nonnegative linear models, this dependence is managed via the Poisson-cluster characterisation
(
Hawkes and Oakes 1974
;
Reynaud-Bouret and Roy 2007
;
Hansen et al. 2015
)
, with convergence-to-equilibrium rates going back to
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
and
Brémaud et al. 2002
. In the complementary bounded-intensity, bounded-memory setting,
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
proved exponentially fast convergence in total variation from arbitrary initial conditions. Our contribution in this section is the extraction of the finite-window consequences that facilitate inference: a total-variation replacement bound for local windows with an explicit initial-window count, and concentration for sliding compact-window functionals with polynomial envelopes. Compared with quoting a generic mixing coefficient, this formulation keeps track of exactly the window an estimating equation sees and feeds directly into the stochastic-integral brackets used in the proofs; it is close in spirit to the nonasymptotic arguments of
Hansen et al. 2015
, with the Poisson embedding replacing the cluster genealogy.

In the remainder of this section, we outline the proof strategy and probabilistic mechanism underpinning the main results presented in Section
2.4
. The full proof splits into two halves. The first demonstrates a uniform analogue of Hansen et al.’s (2015) Proposition 3, which gives a Bernstein type inequality for functionals of the shifted counting process. Our theorem extends Hansen et al.’s so that the same result holds uniformly over a compact (finite-dimensional) parameter set, for linear
and
non-linear Hawkes processes, with the latter (more general) case not being covered by their proposition.

Theorem 4.1
(Local-window concentration).

Suppose Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
hold. Let
\Xi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact with fixed
d
, and let
\{Z_{\xi}:\xi\in\Xi\}
be real-valued functionals of local configurations on
[-A,0)
. Assume that, for constants
B<\infty
and
m\geq 0
,

\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}|Z_{\xi}(x)|\leq B\{1+C(x)^{m}\},

(4.1)

and

|Z_{\xi}(x)-Z_{\xi^{\prime}}(x)|\leq B\{1+C(x)^{m}\}\left\lVert\xi-\xi^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}.

(4.2)

Then, for every
c>0
, there exist
C,K<\infty
and an integer
q_{0}\geq 1
such that, for all sufficiently large
T
,

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\left|\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\{Z_{\xi}\circ G_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}Z_{\xi}\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|>K\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c}.

We note that Theorem
4.1
may be of independent interest since it is a general Bernstein-type inequality for a large class of functionals of the underlying non-linear multivariate Hawkes process. The proof of Theorem
4.1
mostly follows that of
Hansen et al. 2015
, which uses a standard blocking, coupling, and truncation argument to apply Bernstein’s inequality for i.i.d. random variables. The key insight is to use the Poisson embedding techniques of
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
to construct the coupling law for non-linear Hawkes processes when no cluster representation exists. In doing so, we prove that conditional on a finite pre-history in
[s-A,s)
, the law of the non-stationary process converges exponentially fast in total variation distance, uniformly over
\Theta
, to its stationary distribution; see the Supplement section
S1
for the formal statement.

The remainder of the proof for the main results follows a more standard
M
-estimation route. We prove that the empirical risk, and its Jacobian, concentrate uniformly around their expectation and then apply standard Taylor expansion arguments. In more detail, the concentration arguments we obtain for our class of estimators, follows from repeated applications of Theorem
4.1
and a continuous time Freedman type inequality from
Dzhaparidze and van Zanten 2001
. We are able to exploit such martingale concentration inequalities via the Doob-Meyer decomposition
(e.g.
Daley and Vere-Jones 2008
, Corollary 14.I.V)
which ensures that the estimation equations admit a martingale and drift decomposition. Pointwise in
\theta
, the drift terms, by assumption, satisfy the necessary conditions of Theorem
4.1
. Moreover, pointwise in
\theta
, the martingale terms concentrate around
0
as their predictable variations concentrate around their expectation (which is linear in
T
); also by Theorem
4.1
. Thus, we may apply the Freedman type inequality to obtain fluctuations around the mean that are
\mathcal{O}\left(\log^{q_{0}}(T)/\sqrt{T}\right)
with high-probability. A standard stochastic equicontinuity, and
\epsilon
-net, argument ensures that the empirical risk and its Hessian uniformly concentrate around their expectations at these aforementioned radii. We then conclude the proof of Theorem
2.4
via a standard pathwise Taylor expansion argument. Using the preceding techniques, the two step estimation results and central limit theorems easily follow from Taylor expansions and the martingale central limit theorem.

As one may expect, the almost sure rate results directly extend from the previous nonasymptotic results. Such rates are obtained from the same high probability bounds by applying the first Borel–Cantelli lemma on integer horizons and then extending from integer times to all positive real
T
by a pathwise interpolation lemma. On the event
\sup_{0\leq t\leq n+1}\mathfrak{C}_{t}\leq L\log n
, the normalized estimating equations, their local Jacobians, and the plug-in covariance change by at most
\mathcal{O}\left(\log^{q_{0}}(n)/n\right)
as
T
ranges over
[n,n+1]
. Since this event holds eventually almost surely, the integer-horizon pathwise Taylor expansion arguments extend to all real horizons. A similar argument, after a suitable burn-in period and an application of Proposition
3.5
, yields the same almost sure convergence rates under a non-stationary start.

6
Discussion

This paper unifies a broad family of estimators used for Hawkes models inside a single inferential geometry and equips admissible finite moment libraries with common guarantees. An admissible moment is any smooth predictable feature of the recent configuration controlled by a polynomial local-count envelope, so the likelihood score, the derivative equation behind the least-squares contrast, and non-score Takács–Fiksel and Stoyan–Grabarnik-type moments all belong to one family rather than to separate methods. For each admissible GMM member we establish uniform high-probability and almost-sure
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log(T)/T})
rates, asymptotic normality with explicit sandwich covariance, and feasible two-step optimal weighting; the least-squares contrast minimizer has the corresponding high-probability rate and local derivative-weight sandwich limit by Supplement Proposition
S3.5
. A projection geometry then prices efficiency: the score is the efficient member, and any other library loses exactly the part of the score lying outside its predictable span. The scope is wide on purpose. Signed and inhibitory interactions are handled by the same dependence theory through absolute-kernel stability; nonstationary starts are absorbed by a logarithmic burn-in; and a two-point bound shows the root-
T
scale cannot be improved uniformly. For the modeller, then, the choice between likelihood, least squares, and richer libraries is not a jump between unrelated methods but a single, quantified trade-off of computational cost against efficiency.

The limitations made implicit by our assumptions indicate where new tools are required. Allowing the library to grow,
q=q_{T}\to\infty
, affects two fixed-dimensional steps: the fixed-dimensional nets behind Theorem
4.1
and the unregularized inversion of
\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}
. A growing library would therefore require dimension-explicit martingale concentration, entropy control for the library, and regularized weighting in the spirit of efficient-instrument GMM
(
Newey 1990
;
Donald et al. 2009
;
Newey and Windmeijer 2009
)
. This is also the regime in which the penalized least-squares literature for Hawkes already operates
(
Reynaud-Bouret and Schbath 2010
;
Hansen et al. 2015
;
Bacry et al. 2020
)
.

Noncompact memory removes the deterministic window on which the coupling bookkeeping of Section
4
rests, so the local memory count would have to be replaced by a tail-weighted residual-influence functional. Growing dimension,
D=D_{T}\to\infty
, creates a different obstruction. The stability condition
\rho\{G^{\mathrm{abs}}(\theta)\}\leq 1-\varepsilon
is formally dimension-free, but the present constants in the envelopes, count moments, nets, union bounds, and matrix inversions are not tracked as functions of
D
. A high-dimensional theory would have to make these dependences explicit, and would likely require sparsity or other structural restrictions, as in
Hansen et al. 2015
. Boundary and sparse regimes degenerate the rank condition (
2.18
) rather than the dependence theory. Within the fixed-dimensional compact-window setting studied here, however, the
compensator class is exhaustive for finite-library comparisons: it contains the
efficient score-based choice, accommodates any other admissible finite library, and quantifies the resulting efficiency gap.

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{\theta,i}(\nu_{\theta,i})+\sum_{j=1}^{D}\int_{[t-A,t)}K_{\theta,ij}(t-u)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{N}_{j,\theta}(u)=\vcentcolon\bar{\lambda}_{i}(t;\theta).

(S1.14)

Thus, the histories of
\bar{N}_{\theta}
and
N^{[-m]}_{\theta}
are ordered, prior to time
-m
, so that the acceptance region
(0,\lambda_{i}^{\rm nl}(t;\theta)]\subset(0,\bar{\lambda}_{i}(t;\theta)]
for every
t>-m
. Hence, the common-thinning construction preserves order over all future times,
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
and
Morariu-Patrichi and Pakkanen 2018
. Therefore,
N_{\theta}^{[-m]}([-m,\infty))\leq\bar{N}_{\theta}([-m,\infty))
almost surely.

Next, let
N_{\theta}^{\rm stat}|_{(s+r,\infty)}
be a stationary nonlinear multivariate Hawkes process, with the same parametric conditional intensity function as
N_{\theta}^{[-m]}
. Pick
s=-m
and
r=m-1
so that for any fixed window
[0,L]
equation (
S1.3
) gives

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\theta}\{N_{\theta}^{[-m]}|_{[0,L]}\},\mathcal{L}_{\theta}\{N_{\theta}^{\rm stat}|_{[0,L]}\}\right)\leq Ce^{-c(m-1)}.

(S1.15)

Such an inequality follows since the restriction from
(-1,\infty)
to
[0,L]
is measurable so that equation (
S1.15
) holds via a data-processing inequality.

The stochastic ordering under the embedding implies that

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\exp\{\xi_{L}N_{\theta}^{[-m]}([0,L])\}\right]\leq\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\exp\{\xi_{L}\bar{N}_{\theta}([0,L])\}\right]

(S1.16)

To ensure the right hand side of equation (
S1.16
) is finite, consider the following argument. The productivity matrix of
\bar{N}_{\theta}
is
G_{\theta}=\left(\int_{0}^{A}K_{\theta,ij}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\right)_{i,j=1}^{D}
, and by Assumption
2.3(c)
, there exists some fixed
\delta>0
such that
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\rho(G_{\theta})=1-\delta
. Set
r=1-\delta/2
. Since
G_{\theta}
is nonnegative with
\rho(r^{-1}G_{\theta})\leq(1-\delta)/(1-\delta/2)<1
, every power is dominated entrywise by the Neumann series,

\left(\frac{G_{\theta}}{r}\right)^{n}\leq\sum_{m\geq 0}\left(\frac{G_{\theta}}{r}\right)^{m}=(I-r^{-1}G_{\theta})^{-1}\implies\left\lVert G_{\theta}^{n}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq Kr^{n}

for all
n\geq 1
with
K:=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\left\lVert(I-r^{-1}G_{\theta})^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty}
, which is finite by the continuity of
\theta\mapsto G_{\theta}
. Such a result should be clear by the stated assumptions, and compactness of
\Theta
, exactly as in (
S1.1
). Therefore, we may apply Theorem 1 of
Leblanc 2024
with this pair
(r,K)
. Together with the uniform upper bound of the baseline intensities the resulting upper bound of the exponential-moment is uniform over
\Theta
. Thus, the right hand side of equation (
S1.16
) is finite for sufficiently small
\xi_{L}
, additionally, call this upper bound
C_{L}
.

Therefore, for every finite
m

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\exp\{\xi_{L}N_{\theta}^{[-m]}([0,L])\}\right]\leq C_{L}<\infty.

We now pass to the stationary limit and to do so, define
g_{R}(k)=\min\left\{e^{\xi_{L}k},R\right\}
. Then by equation (
S1.15
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[g_{R}\left(N_{\theta}^{\rm stat}([0,L])\right)\right]=\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[g_{R}\left(N_{\theta}^{[-m]}([0,L])\right)\right]\leq C_{L}.

The monotone convergence theorem, taking
R\to\infty
, yields

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\exp\left\{\xi_{L}N_{\theta}^{\rm stat}([0,L])\right\}\right]\leq C_{L}.

Since
C_{L}
is independent of
\theta
, stationarity, and taking a supremum over
\theta
yields equation (
S1.12
).

Polynomial moments follow by the existence of the exponential moment in a neighbourhood of
0
.
∎

Under Assumptions

–
2.3(c)
, for every

on

Now using Lemma

independent stationary nonlinear multivariate Hawkes processes on disjoint separated blocks that are close in total variation distance to any indivual stationary nonlinear multivariate Hawkes process restricted to the same blocks.

Lemma S1.4
(Block coupling for nonlinear compact-memory Hawkes processes).

Suppose Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
hold. Let

N_{\theta}\sim\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
be the stationary nonlinear Hawkes process with intensity

\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)=\phi_{\theta,i}\!\left(\nu_{\theta,i}+\sum_{j=1}^{D}\int_{[t-A,t)}h_{\theta,ij}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}N_{j,\theta}(s)\right),\qquad i=1,\ldots,D.

from left to right and separated by gaps at least
g>0

a_{q+1}-b_{q}\geq g
. Then there exist constants
C,c>0
, independent of

\theta
,
n
,
g
, and the intervals, such that

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\theta}\{N|_{I_{1}},\ldots,N|_{I_{n}}\},\bigotimes_{q=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{\theta}\{N|_{I_{q}}\}\right)\leq Cne^{-cg}.

(S1.19)

The proof of this lemma exploits the result of Lemma
S1.1
by considering Hawkes processes with two independent stationary histories. In particular, the coupling constructed in Lemma
S1.1
implies that if

and

\mathbb{Q}_{\theta}^{s}\left(N_{\theta}|_{I}\neq N_{\theta}^{\prime}|_{I}\right)\leq C_{0}e^{-cr}

(S1.20)

where
C_{0}
absorbs the integrable finite local stationary history
Z_{\mathcal{H}}(s)
.

We now construct the independent block copies. Consider the deterministic intervals
I_{q}=[a_{q},b_{q}]
for
q=1,\dots,n
with gaps
g
so that
a_{q+1}-b_{q}\geq g
, let
r=g/3
and define
s_{q}=a_{q}-r
. Consider the enlarged windows
J_{q}=(s_{q},b_{q}]
, each of which is disjoint since
s_{q+1}=a_{q+1}-r\geq b_{q}+g-r>b_{q}
.

We construct the coupling law
\mathbb{Q}_{\theta}
as follows. Sample one stationary nonlinear Hawkes process
N_{\theta}\sim\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\text{nl}}
, and represent it through the Poisson embedding with
\Pi
, as was done in the proof of Lemma
S1.1
. For each block
q
, construct an auxiliary process,
\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)}
in the following manner.

1.

Prior to time
s_{q}
, give
\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)}
an independent stationary nonlinear history with law
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\text{nl}}
restricted to
(-\infty,s_{q}]
.

2.

On the window
J_{q}=(s_{q},b_{q}]
drive
\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)}
using the same Poisson embedding
\Pi
as for
N_{\theta}
.

Specifically, for
t\in J_{q}
,

\tilde{N}_{\theta,i}^{(q)}(dt)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}\left\{z\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{\theta,i}^{\text{nl},(q)}(t;\theta)\right\}\Pi_{i}(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)

where

\tilde{\lambda}_{\theta,i}^{\text{nl},(q)}(t;\theta)=\phi_{\theta,i}\left(\nu_{\theta,i}+\sum_{j=1}^{D}\int_{[t-A,t)}h_{\theta,ij}(t-u)d\tilde{N}_{\theta,j}^{(q)}(u)\right)

Since the history of each
\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)}
is independent, and the Poisson embedding noises are independent across
q
as the windows

are disjoint (Poisson random measures have independent increments) the family

are independent under

so that

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\theta}}\left(\{\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)}|_{I_{q}}\}_{q=1}^{n}\right)=\bigotimes_{q=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\theta}}\left(\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)}|_{I_{q}}\right).

Now for a fixed pair
(N_{\theta},\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)})
on the interval
(s_{q},b_{q}]
has exactly the same distribution as the two-process coupling under
\mathbb{Q}_{\theta}^{s_{q}}

S1.20
) gives

\mathbb{Q}_{\theta}\left(N_{\theta}|_{I_{q}}\neq\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)}|_{I_{q}}\right)\leq C_{0}e^{-\alpha g/3}.

Via the coupling characterisation of the total variation distance, and a union bound,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\text{nl}}}(N_{\theta}|_{I_{1}},\ldots,N_{\theta}|_{I_{n}}),\bigotimes_{q=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\text{nl}}}(\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)}|_{I_{q}})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{Q}_{\theta}\left(\exists q\ \mid\ N_{\theta}|_{I_{q}}\neq\tilde{N}_{\theta}^{(q)}|_{I_{q}}\right)\leq C_{0}ne^{-c_{0}g}.

As
C_{0},c_{0}
are independent of
\theta
taking the supremum yields equation (
S1.19
) so that the proof concludes.

Just prior to proving Theorem
4.1
we consider a practical scenario which allows for the local initial history to be integrable, where the history is non-empty but stochastic and finite. In particular, this is the case in which the history starts off empty at some prior finite time, and then propagates forward via the non-explosive Hawkes process. As a concrete example suppose that the process truly started at time
T_{\rm int}
with an empty history. Then if data collection starts at some future time,
T_{\rm data}>T_{\rm int}
, even though the process itself is not stationary for
t>T_{\rm data}
the local history in
[T_{\rm data}-A,T_{\rm data}]
has a first moment, so that the law generating the data converges exponentially fast in total variation distance to the stationary law.

Lemma S1.5
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
hold. Fix a
finite starting time
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
N^{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta}
be the
Hawkes process with parameter
\theta
, constructed from an initial history

\mathcal{H}
on
(-\infty,\tau]
. Assume that

Z_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau):=\sum_{j=1}^{D}\mathcal{H}_{j}([\tau-A,\tau])

has finite expectation uniformly over the parameter set:

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[Z_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)\right]<\infty.

Then, for every finite
B<\infty
,

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{\tau\leq s\leq\tau+B}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{D}N_{j,\theta}^{\mathcal{H}}([s-A,s])\right]<\infty.

In particular, if the process is started from the empty history at time

\tau
, then the conclusion holds for every finite
B
.

Following the notation of Lemma
S1.2
, and by equation (
S1.13
), under the common Poisson embedding
[
Brémaud and Massoulié 1996
, e.g.]
,
N^{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta}
is dominated by
the nonnegative linear Hawkes envelope
\bar{N}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta}
with baseline

\phi_{\theta,i}(\nu_{\theta,i})
, kernels
K_{\theta,ij}
, and the same initial history

\mathcal{H}
. It is therefore enough to prove the claim for

\bar{N}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta}
.

Let

\bar{m}_{i}(t)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\bar{\lambda}_{i}(t;\theta)\right],\qquad t\geq\tau,

where
\bar{\lambda}_{i}
is the intensity of the linear envelope. Since the
kernels are compactly supported,

\bar{m}_{i}(t)\leq\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\phi_{\theta,i}(\nu_{\theta,i})+\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{D}\int_{[\tau-A,\tau]}K_{\theta,ij}(t-u)\,d\mathcal{H}_{j}(u)\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{D}\int_{\tau}^{t}K_{\theta,ij}(t-u)\bar{m}_{j}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u

The first term is uniformly bounded by compactness. For the second summand, compact support and boundedness of the kernels ensures that

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{D}\int_{[\tau-A,\tau]}K_{\theta,ij}(t-u)\,d\mathcal{H}_{j}(u)\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[Z_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)\right],

uniformly in
t\geq\tau
,
i
, and
\theta
, in addition to the fact that
t\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{D}\bar{m}_{i}(t)
is locally finite on compact intervals due to the finite initial history. Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{D}\bar{m}_{i}(t)\leq C\{1+\mathbb{E}_{\theta}Z_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)\}+C\int_{\tau}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\bar{m}_{i}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u,\qquad t\geq\tau,

where
C<\infty
is deterministic and uniform over
\theta\in\Theta
. By
Gronwall’s inequality for locally finite measures
[
Horváth 1996
, e.g.]
, for every finite
B<\infty
,

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+B}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\bar{m}_{i}(t)\leq C_{B}\left\{1+\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[Z_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)\right]\right\}<\infty.

Therefore,

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[N^{\mathcal{H},\theta}([\tau,\tau+B])\right]\leq\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\bar{N}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta}([\tau,\tau+B])\right]=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+B}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\bar{m}_{i}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Finally, if
s\in[\tau,\tau+B]
, then clearly
[s-A,s]\subset[\tau-A,\tau+B]
so that

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{\tau\leq s\leq\tau+B}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{D}N_{j,\theta}^{\mathcal{H}}([s-A,s])\right]<\infty

and the proof concludes.
∎

S1.2

|I_{b}|\leq x_{T}+A\leq C\log T
.

Next, we place a deterministic grid of mesh
A/2
on
I_{b}
; it contains at most
C\log T
grid intervals and every sliding window
[t-A,t)
, with
t\in B_{b}
, intersects at most three of these grid intervals. Therefore,
R_{b}>r
implies that some grid interval has more than
r/3
points. The exponential moment (
S1.12
), with
L=A/2
, therefore gives constants
C_{R},a_{R}>0
such that

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(R_{b}>r\right)\leq C_{R}\log(T)\,e^{-a_{R}r},\qquad r\geq 0.

(S1.29)

Choose
R_{0}
so large that
a_{R}R_{0}\geq M+4
. Then, for all large
T
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left((\Omega_{\rm blk})^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leq n_{T}C_{R}\frac{\log(T)}{T^{a_{R}R_{0}}}\leq CT^{1-a_{R}R_{0}}\leq CT^{-M-3}.

(S1.30)

On the event
\left\{R_{b}\leq R_{0}\log(T)\right\}
, the envelope (
4.1
) and the fixed-window moment (
S1.12
) imply, uniformly in
\xi
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert F_{b,\xi}\right\rvert\mathbf{1}\left\{R_{b}\leq R_{0}\log(T)\right\}\leq Bx_{T}\{1+\left(R_{0}\log(T)\right)^{m}\}+x_{T}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left\lvert Z_{\xi}\right\rvert\leq B_{1}\log^{m+1}(T)=:b_{T},

(S1.31)

for some universal constant
B_{1}<\infty
. Moreover,

\Var_{\theta}\left(F_{b,\xi}\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}\leq R_{0}\log(T)\}\right)\leq B_{2}\log^{2m+2}(T).

(S1.32)

Next, we consider the tail term. Since
\mathbb{E}\left[F_{b,\xi}\right]=0
, truncation creates only the centering error

\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[F_{b,\xi}\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}\leq R_{0}\log(T)\}\right]\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[F_{b,\xi}\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}>R_{0}\log(T)\}\right]\right\rvert.

Then, by the tail-integral identity, for any
m>0
,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[R_{b}^{m}\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}>R_{0}\log(T)\}\right]=\left(R_{0}\log(T)\right)^{m}\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\left(R_{b}>R_{0}\log(T)\right)+m\int_{R_{0}\log(T)}^{\infty}u^{m-1}\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(R_{b}>u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Hence, by (
S1.29
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[R_{b}^{m}\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}>R_{0}\log(T)\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\log(T)\left[\left(R_{0}\log(T)\right)^{m}e^{-a_{R}R_{0}\log(T)}+m\int_{R_{0}\log(T)}^{\infty}u^{m-1}e^{-a_{R}u}\,\mathrm{d}u\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\log(T)\,(1+\left(R_{0}\log(T)\right)^{m})e^{-a_{R}R_{0}\log(T)}.

(S1.33)

The same bound is trivial for
m=0
. Therefore, uniformly over
\xi\in\Xi
, from equation (
4.1
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert F_{b,\xi}\right\rvert\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}>R_{0}\log(T)\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Cx_{T}\mathbb{E}\big[\{1+R_{b}^{m}\}\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}>R_{0}\log(T)\}\big]

\displaystyle\leq C\log^{m+2}(T)\,e^{-a_{R}R_{0}\log T}\leq CT^{-M-3},

(S1.34)

where the last inequality follows from the choice of
R_{0}
, after potentially increasing
C
. Hence,

\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[\left\lvert F_{b,\xi}\right\rvert\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}>R_{0}\log(T)\}\right]\leq CT^{-M-2}.

We now apply this truncation to the sum of independent Hawkes processes, and define

\bar{F}_{b,\xi}=F_{b,\xi}\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}\leq R_{0}\log(T)\}-\mathbb{E}\left[F_{b,\xi}\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}\leq R_{0}\log(T)\}\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}F_{b,\xi}>\frac{u}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}F_{b,\xi}\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}\leq R_{0}\log(T)\}>\frac{u}{3}\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)+CT^{-M-3}

(S1.35)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\bar{F}_{b,\xi}>\frac{u}{6}\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)+CT^{-M-3}+

(S1.36)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[\left\lvert F_{b,\xi}\right\rvert\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}>R_{0}\log(T)\}\right]>\frac{u}{6}\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)

(S1.37)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\bar{F}_{b,\xi}>\frac{u}{6}\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)+CT^{-M-3}+\mathbf{1}\left\{CT^{-M-2}>\frac{u}{6}\right\}

(S1.38)

The first term of equation (
S1.36
) can be bounded via Bernstein’s inequality
[e.g.
Wainwright 2019
, Proposition 2.14]
for
independent centred bounded block variables, as in
Hansen et al. 2015
, we also extend it to the supremum via a standard
\epsilon
-net argument.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{T}
be a
T^{-1/2}
-net of
\Xi
with
\left\lvert\mathcal{N}_{T}\right\rvert\leq C_{\Xi}T^{d/2}
. For a fixed
\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{T}
, we apply Bernstein’s inequality to the independent centred variables
\bar{F}_{\eta,b}
on the event
\Omega_{\rm blk}
. Doing so yields,

\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\left|\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\bar{F}_{b,\eta}\right|>\frac{u}{6}\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)\leq 2\exp\left\{-\frac{u^{2}/36}{2\left(n_{T}B_{2}\log^{2m+2}(T)+2b_{T}u/18\right)}\right\}.

(S1.39)

as
\left\lvert\bar{F}_{b,\eta}\right\rvert\leq 2b_{T}
. Since
n_{T}\leq T/(2L_{0}\log(T))
,

n_{T}B_{2}\log^{2m+2}(T)\leq V_{0}T\log^{2m+1}(T)

(S1.40)

for some constant
V_{0}<\infty
. Next, choose an integer
q_{0}
such that

2q_{0}-(2m+1)\geq 1;

(S1.41)

for instance,
q_{0}=\lceil m+1\rceil
suffices. Pick
u=K\sqrt{T}\log^{q_{0}}(T)
. Thus, for all sufficiently large
T
,

2\left(n_{T}B_{2}\log^{2m+2}(T)+2b_{T}u/18\right)\leq 4V_{0}T\log^{2m+1}(T).

Combining this bound with (
S1.41
) yields

\frac{u^{2}}{2\left(n_{T}B_{2}\log^{2m+2}(T)+2b_{T}u/3\right)}\geq\frac{K^{2}}{4V_{0}}\log^{2q_{0}-(2m+1)}T\geq\frac{K^{2}}{4V_{0}}\log(T).

Choose
K
so large that

\frac{K^{2}}{4V_{0}}\geq M+d/2+4.

Then (
S1.39
) gives

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\left|\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\bar{F}_{b,\eta}\right|>K\sqrt{T}\log^{q_{0}}(T)\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)\leq 2T^{-M-d/2-4}.

A union bound over
\mathcal{N}_{T}
yields

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\max_{\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}\left|\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\bar{F}_{b,\eta}\right|>K\sqrt{T}\log^{q_{0}}(T)\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)\leq CT^{-M-3},

On the event
\Omega_{\rm blk}
, if
\pi(\xi)\in\mathcal{N}_{T}
is nearest to
\xi
, then the pathwise Lipschitz envelope, equation (
4.2
) gives

\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\frac{1}{T}\int_{2bx_{T}}^{(2b+1)x_{T}}\left\lvert\tilde{Z}_{\xi}\circ G_{t}-\tilde{Z}_{\pi(\xi)}\circ G_{t}\right\rvert\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}\leq R_{0}\log(T)\}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
\leq\frac{B\{1+(R_{0}\log(T))^{m}\}}{\sqrt{T}}.

(S1.42)

With the expectation similarly satisfying

\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[(Z_{\xi}-Z_{\pi(\xi)})\mathbf{1}\{R_{b}\leq R_{0}\log(T)\}\right]\right\rvert\leq\frac{B\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[(1+N[-A,0))^{m}\right]}{\sqrt{T}}\leq CT^{-1/2},

(S1.43)

where the last bound follows the fact that
N([-A,0))
has moments of all order. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\left|\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\bar{F}_{b,\xi}\right|>K^{\prime}\sqrt{T}\log^{q_{0}^{\prime}}(T)\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)

(S1.44)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}\frac{1}{T}\left|\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\bar{F}_{b,\eta}\right|>K^{\prime}\frac{\log^{q_{0}^{\prime}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}-\Delta_{T}\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)

(S1.45)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\max_{\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{T}}\frac{1}{T}\left|\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\bar{F}_{b,\eta}\right|>K\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)\leq CT^{-M-3}

(S1.46)

where
K^{\prime}
and
q_{0}^{\prime}
are selected so that
\Delta_{T}
, which is the pathwise Lipschitz envelope from adding the right hand sides of equations (
S1.42
) and (
S1.43
), ensures that

K^{\prime}\frac{\log^{q_{0}^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{T}}-\Delta_{T}\geq K\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}.

We now combine all of the estimates to conclude the final bound. In particular,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\{Z_{\xi}\circ G_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}Z_{\xi}\}\,\mathrm{d}t>K^{\prime}\frac{\log^{q_{0}^{\prime}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\int_{2bx_{T}}^{(2b+1)x_{T}}\{\tilde{Z}_{\xi}\circ G_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}Z_{\xi}\}\,\mathrm{d}t>K^{\prime}\frac{\log^{q_{0}^{\prime}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right)+CT^{-M-3}

(S1.47)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbf{1}\left\{\frac{C\log^{m+1}(T)}{T}>K^{\prime}\frac{\log^{q_{0}^{\prime}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\{\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{b=0}^{n_{T}-1}\bar{F}_{b,\xi}>K^{\prime}\frac{\log^{q_{0}^{\prime}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right\}\cap\Omega_{\rm blk}\right)+CT^{-M-3}+\mathbf{1}\left\{CT^{-M-2}>\frac{K}{6}\sqrt{T}\log^{q_{0}}(T)\right\}

(S1.48)

\displaystyle\leq CT^{-M-3}\leq CT^{-c}

(S1.49)

Inequality (
S1.47
) follows from equations (
S1.23
), (
S1.26
) and (
S1.27
). Equation (
S1.48
) follows from the fact that the indicator is eventually always 0 and the definition of
F_{b,\xi}
, and equations (
S1.35
) to (
S1.38
). Finally, equation (
S1.49
) follows from equations (
S1.44
) to (
S1.46
).

The constants
C,M
are uniform over
\Theta
so that a supremum can be taken. Furthermore, the lower tail follows by applying the same argument to

-Z_{\xi}
, and the proof concludes by union bounding over the two events.
∎

We conclude this section by proving a brief proposition about the population finite-window continuity input used in the generic GMM and likelihood-localisation arguments. The argument is standard and follows easily from the previously demonstrated probabilistic tools.

Proposition S1.7
(Finite-window population continuity).

Let
K\subset\Theta
be compact, let
L<\infty
, and let
\Xi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact. Suppose
Z_{\xi}
depends only on the multivariate restriction
N_{\theta}|_{[-L,0]}
, satisfies a polynomial finite-window count envelope

|Z_{\xi}|\leq C\{1+N_{\theta}([-L,0])^{m}\},\qquad\xi\in\Xi,

and is Lipschitz in
\xi
with a polynomial count envelope: for some
C^{\prime},m^{\prime}<\infty
,

|Z_{\xi}-Z_{\eta}|\leq C^{\prime}\{1+N([-L,0])^{m^{\prime}}\}\left\lVert\xi-\eta\right\rVert_{2},\qquad\xi,\eta\in\Xi.

Then
(\xi,\theta)\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[Z_{\xi}\right]
is continuous on
\Xi\times K
.

Write
\mathfrak{C}_{0}=N_{\theta}([-L,0])
. Fix
(\xi_{n},\theta_{n})\to(\xi,\theta)
in
\Xi\times K
. By the triangle and Jensen’s inequalities,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}[Z_{\xi_{n}}]-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[Z_{\xi}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}\!\left[\left|Z_{\xi_{n}}-Z_{\xi_{n},M}\right|\right]+\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[|Z_{\xi_{n},M}-Z_{\xi,M}|\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[|Z_{\xi,M}-Z_{\xi}|\right],

where
Z_{\xi,M}=Z_{\xi}\mathbf{1}\{\mathfrak{C}_{0}\leq M\}
.

We first bound
\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]\right|
. To do so fix
B>L+A
and let
\mathbb{P}_{\zeta,B}^{0,[-B,0]}
be the law on
[-B,0]
of the process obtained by starting at time
-B
with empty pre-
-B
history. Let
\mathbb{P}_{\zeta,B}^{0,[-L,0]}
denote its restriction to
[-L,0]
, and write
\mathbb{E}_{\zeta,B}
for expectation with respect to this finite-start law whenever the integrand depends only on
N_{\zeta}|_{[-L,0]}
.

By Lemmata
S1.1
and
S1.5
, uniformly over
\zeta\in K
,

\Delta_{B}:=\sup_{\zeta\in K}d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\mathbb{P}_{\zeta}^{[-L,0]},\mathbb{P}_{\zeta,B}^{0,[-L,0]}\right)\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }B\to\infty.

Hence, since
Z_{\xi_{n},M}
is bounded by
C(1+M^{m})
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]-\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n},B}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n},B}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]-\mathbb{E}_{\theta,B}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta,B}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[Z_{\xi_{n},M}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4C(1+M^{m})\Delta_{B}+2C(1+M^{m})d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{n},B}^{0,[-L,0]},\mathbb{P}_{\theta,B}^{0,[-L,0]}\right).

For fixed
B
, the finite-start laws are continuous in total variation as a function of the parameter. In short, this is due to Scheffé’s lemma. In more detail, take
\mathbb{P}_{0}^{B}
, the law of
D
independent unit-rate Poisson processes on
[-B,0]
, as the reference measure. For each
\zeta\in K
, the finite-start law on
[-B,0]
has likelihood density

L_{B}(\zeta;N)=\prod_{i=1}^{D}\exp\left\{\int_{-B}^{0}\log\lambda_{i}^{B,0}(t;\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}N_{i}(t)-\int_{-B}^{0}\{\lambda_{i}^{B,0}(t;\zeta)-1\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right\}

with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{0}^{B}
, where
\lambda_{i}^{B,0}(t;\zeta)
is the predictable intensity functional computed from the empty pre-
(-B)
history and the realized path
N|_{[-B,t)}
. Such a formula is standard
[e.g.
Daley and Vere-Jones 2003
, Section 7]
or
Leskelä 2024
and references cited therein.

For
\mathbb{P}_{0}^{B}
-almost every path there are finitely many points on
[-B,0]
; by Assumptions
2.3(a)
and
2.3(b)
, the displayed likelihood is then continuous in
\zeta
. Since
\int L_{B}(\zeta;N)\,d\mathbb{P}_{0}^{B}(N)=1
for every
\zeta
, Scheffé’s lemma yields convergence in total variation on
[-B,0]
, and therefore also after restriction to
[-L,0]
:

d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{n},B}^{0,[-L,0]},\mathbb{P}_{\theta,B}^{0,[-L,0]}\right)\to 0.

(S1.50)

We next bound the remaining terms. By the fact that
Z_{\xi}
is Lipschitz in
\xi
with a polynomial count envelope, so too is
Z_{\xi,M}
. Thus,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[|Z_{\xi_{n},M}-Z_{\xi,M}|]\leq C^{\prime}\left\lVert\xi_{n}-\xi\right\rVert_{2}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{m^{\prime}}]=O(\left\lVert\xi_{n}-\xi\right\rVert_{2})

by the exponential fixed-window count moment (
S1.12
).

For the two truncation terms, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}\!\left[\left|Z_{\xi_{n}}-Z_{\xi_{n},M}\right|\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[\left|Z_{\xi}-Z_{\xi,M}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}[(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{m})^{2}]\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{n}}\left(\mathfrak{C}_{0}>M\right)}+C\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{m})^{2}]\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\left(\mathfrak{C}_{0}>M\right)}.

By (
S1.12
), the moments of
\mathfrak{C}_{0}
are uniformly bounded over
K
, and the tails
\mathbb{P}_{\zeta}(\mathfrak{C}_{0}>M)
converge to zero uniformly over
\zeta\in K
. Hence, for every
\epsilon>0
, we can choose
M
large enough such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}\!\left[\left|Z_{\xi_{n}}-Z_{\xi_{n},M}\right|\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\!\left[\left|Z_{\xi}-Z_{\xi,M}\right|\right]\right\}<\epsilon.

For this fixed
M
, choose
B
sufficiently large that

4C(1+M^{m})\Delta_{B}<\epsilon.

Finally, with
M
and
B
fixed, (
S1.50
) and
\xi_{n}\to\xi
imply that, for all sufficiently large
n
,

2C(1+M^{m})d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{n},B}^{0,[-L,0]},\mathbb{P}_{\theta,B}^{0,[-L,0]}\right)+\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[|Z_{\xi_{n},M}-Z_{\xi,M}|]<\epsilon.

Combining the preceding displays gives

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}[Z_{\xi_{n}}]-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[Z_{\xi}]\right|\leq 3\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, the claim follows.
∎

S2
Common compact-window calculus and martingale tools

The estimates in the generic estimating-map arguments and in the likelihood appendix use the same two elementary inputs: polynomial envelopes for local intensity derivatives, and a predictable-truncation version of the Dzhaparidze–van Zanten Bernstein inequality for local martingales
[
Dzhaparidze and van Zanten 2001
]
. Under the assumptions given in Subsection
2.3
we demonstrate further polynomial count envelopes of the intensity, and its log derivatives. Throughout Section
S3
we can refer back to the generic compact-window machinery rather than reproving the same martingale-net arguments.

For a multi-index
a
in the coordinates of
\theta
with
|a|\leq 3
, define the generic local kernel derivative convolution

Y_{ij}^{(a)}(t;\theta)=\int_{[t-A,t)}\partial_{\theta}^{a}h_{\theta,ij}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s),

(S2.1)

with the convention that inactive coordinates have derivative zero. By compact support and Assumption
2.3(b)
,

\sup_{\theta\in K_{\Theta}}\left\lvert Y_{ij}^{(a)}(t;\theta)\right\rvert\leq C\mathfrak{C}_{t},\qquad|a|\leq 3.

(S2.2)

Lemma S2.1
(Polynomial derivative envelopes).

Under Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(b)
, for each
r=0,1,2,3
there are constants
C_{r}<\infty
and
m_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
i
, all
t
, and all
\theta\in K_{\Theta}
,

\left\lVert\nabla_{\theta}^{r}\log\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)\right\rVert_{2}\leq C_{r}\{1+\mathfrak{C}_{t}^{m_{r}}\},

(S2.3)

and

\left\lVert\nabla_{\theta}^{r}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)\right\rVert_{2}\leq C_{r}\{1+\mathfrak{C}_{t}^{m_{r}}\}.

(S2.4)

Write

X_{i}(t;\theta)=\nu_{\theta,i}+\sum_{j=1}^{D}\int_{[t-A,t)}h_{\theta,ij}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s).

For each multi-index
a
with
|a|\leq 3
, Assumptions
2.3(a)
and
2.3(b)
give

\left\lvert\partial_{\theta}^{a}X_{i}(t;\theta)\right\rvert\leq C(1+\mathfrak{C}_{t}),\qquad\theta\in K_{\Theta},

by the boundedness of the baseline derivatives and (
S2.2
). The multivariate Faà di Bruno formula
[
Hardy 2006
, e.g.]
applied to

\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)=\phi_{\theta,i}\{X_{i}(t;\theta)\}
expresses each
\partial_{\theta}^{a}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)
,
|a|\leq 3
, as a finite sum of products of link derivatives evaluated at
X_{i}(t;\theta)
, baseline derivatives, and the local convolutions
Y_{ij}^{(b)}(t;\theta)
. The link derivatives have at most polynomial growth in the input coordinate and
|X_{i}(t;\theta)|\leq C(1+\mathfrak{C}_{t})
, so (
S2.4
) follows.

The positive-link assumption gives a uniform lower bound
\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)\geq\underline{\lambda}>0
on
K_{\Theta}
, and the case
r=0
above gives
\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)\leq C(1+\mathfrak{C}_{t}^{m_{0}})
. Derivatives of
\log\lambda_{i}
up to order three are finite sums of products of derivatives of
\lambda_{i}
divided by powers of
\lambda_{i}
; the denominator is controlled by
\underline{\lambda}
. This proves (
S2.3
).

∎

Lemma S2.2
(Predictability, endpoints, and bracket bookkeeping).

Under Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
, the following standard point-process facts hold.

(i)

The local count process
\mathfrak{C}_{t}=N([t-A,t))
is predictable. More generally, for every deterministic continuous
f
on
[0,A]
and every component
j
,

Y_{f}(t)=\int_{[t-A,t)}f(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s)

is predictable, as a left-continuous adapted local-window functional under the half-open predictable convention
[e.g.
Björk 2011
, Def. 2.4.1, Prop. 2.4.1 and Lem. 2.4.1]
.

(ii)

Endpoint and no-coincidence convention. Assume that each component counting process
N_{k}
has a continuous predictable compensator
\Lambda_{k}
, and that the marked point process has a simple ground process (equivalently, is completely simple). Then each
N_{k}
is quasi-left continuous, so
\Delta N_{k}(\sigma)=0
almost surely for every finite predictable stopping time
\sigma

[
Jacod and Shiryaev 2003
, Cor. II.1.19]
; see also
Bandini et al. 2024
. In particular, deterministic times carry no points. If
T
is an event time and
A>0
is fixed, then
T+A
is predictable, being announced by
T+A(1-1/n)
; hence no component jumps at
T+A
. By local finiteness, this excludes exact
A
-lag pairs on compact intervals. Complete simplicity of the ground process excludes simultaneous jumps of distinct components
[
Schoenberg 2006
, e.g.]
. Consequently, in stochastic-integral estimating equations, the history window
[t-A,t)
may be replaced by
(t-A,t)
almost surely.

(iii)

By the standard jump formula for stochastic integrals with predictable integrands
[e.g.
van der Vaart n.d.
, Lem. 5.93]
, if
H_{i}
is a predictable scalar integrand and
M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}=N_{i}-\int_{0}^{\cdot}\lambda_{i}(s;\theta^{\star})\,\mathrm{d}s
, then

\Delta\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}H_{i}(t)\,\mathrm{d}M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}(t)\right)_{s}=H_{i}(s)\Delta M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}(s)=H_{i}(s)\Delta N_{i}(s).

Consequently, for a finite sum
\sum_{i}\int H_{i}\,\mathrm{d}M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}
, each jump is
\sum_{i}H_{i}(s)\Delta N_{i}(s)
.

(iv)

For predictable scalar integrands
H_{i}
, the predictable variation is of the form

\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{\cdot}H_{i}(t)\,\mathrm{d}M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}(t)\right\rangle_{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{T}H_{i}(t)^{2}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta^{\star})\,\mathrm{d}t.

[pg. 233
Wood 1999
, and references cited]
.

Lemma S2.3
(Dzhaparidze–van Zanten Bernstein inequality;

Dzhaparidze and van Zanten 2001
, Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.4(iii)
).

Let
M
be a cadlag locally square-integrable martingale with
M_{0}=0
and
predictable quadratic variation
\langle M\rangle_{T}
. Suppose that all jumps
of
M
on
[0,T]
are bounded in absolute value by
b
. Then, for all
x,v>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(M_{T}\geq x,\ \langle M\rangle_{T}\leq v\right)\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{x^{2}}{2(v+bx/3)}\right\}.

The same bound applies to
-M_{T}
, and hence

\mathbb{P}\left(|M_{T}|\geq x,\ \langle M\rangle_{T}\leq v\right)\leq 2\exp\left\{-\frac{x^{2}}{2(v+bx/3)}\right\}.

Lemma S2.4
(Predictable truncation Dzhaparidze–van Zanten device).

Let
M_{t}=\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{t}H_{i,s}\,\mathrm{d}M_{i,s}^{0}
be a scalar martingale integral with respect to finitely many point-process martingales, where the
H_{i}
are predictable. Let
H_{i}^{(R)}
be predictable truncated integrands such that the martingale integral
M_{t}^{(R)}=\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{t}H_{i,s}^{(R)}\,\mathrm{d}M_{i,s}^{0}
has jumps bounded by
b
almost surely. Furthermore, suppose that on an event
\mathcal{A}_{R}
one has
H_{i,s}^{(R)}=H_{i,s}
for all
i
and all
s\in[0,T]
. Then, for all
x,v>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(|M_{T}|>x\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{R}^{c}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\langle M\rangle_{T}>v\right)+2\exp\left\{-\frac{x^{2}}{2(v+bx/3)}\right\}.

On
\mathcal{A}_{R}
,
M_{T}=M_{T}^{(R)}
. Hence

\{|M_{T}|>x\}\subset\mathcal{A}_{R}^{c}\cup\{\langle M\rangle_{T}>v\}\cup\{|M_{T}^{(R)}|>x,\ \langle M^{(R)}\rangle_{T}\leq v\}.

Apply Lemma
S2.3
to
M^{(R)}
and to
-M^{(R)}
.
∎

Proposition S3.1
(Existence and measurable selection of GMM minimizers).

Under Assumptions

,
2.3(b)
, and

On the event

On

gives pathwise continuity of

The map from the observed configuration to

∎

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)-g_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{W}>K\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right)

(S3.2)

Moreover, for every fixed

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\vartheta\in B(\theta^{\star},r)}\left\lVert\partial_{\vartheta}\left\{\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)\right\}+B_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}>K\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c}.

(S3.3)

Write

is the sum of a martingale average and a centred drift average. Specifically,

m_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)-g_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})=\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{T}H_{\cdot i}(t;\vartheta)\mathrm{d}M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}(t)\right)+\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}H(t;\vartheta)\{\lambda(t;\theta^{\star})-\lambda(t;\vartheta)\}\mathrm{d}t-g_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right).

Throughout the proof, all vector and matrix statements are obtained coordinatewise and then converted to Euclidean or operator norms using the fixed dimensions
q
,
p
, and
D
. Moreover, the envelope assumptions are stable under the finite sums, products, and matrix multiplications that appear below. Thus, by Assumption
2.3(e)
and Lemma
S2.1
, every indexed local-window functional that appears in drift terms, predictable variations, or as a derivative of such a term has both a polynomial local-count envelope and a polynomial local-count Lipschitz envelope in the sense of Theorem
4.1
. In particular, multiplication by
\lambda_{i}(\cdot;\theta_{0})
is harmless because
\lambda_{i}(t;\theta_{0})\leq C(1+\mathfrak{C}_{t})
uniformly on
K_{\Theta}

Moreover, pathwise differentiability is justifiable as the
\mathrm{d}N
terms on
\{N([-A,T])<\infty\}
, are finite sums at event times; differentiation of the compensator terms is justified by the same polynomial envelopes and the uniform exponential moment (
S1.12

First consider the drift term, where the shift-covariant local-window functional is

Z_{a,\vartheta,\theta_{0}}(N)=e_{a}^{\top}H(0;\vartheta)\{\lambda(0;\theta_{0})-\lambda(0;\vartheta)\},\qquad a=1,\ldots,q,

where

is the
q

has the required polynomial envelope and Lipschitz envelope, uniformly over

and
(\vartheta,\theta_{0})\in\Theta\times\Theta_{0}
. To see this, use (
2.15
) and the uniform bound
\lambda_{i}(t;\theta_{0})\leq C(1+\mathfrak{C}_{t})
:

\displaystyle\left|e_{a}^{\top}H(0;\vartheta)\{\lambda(0;\theta_{0})-\lambda(0;\vartheta)\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert H(0;\vartheta)\lambda(0;\theta_{0})\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert H(0;\vartheta)\lambda(0;\vartheta)\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{m_{0}})(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0})\leq B_{0}(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{m_{0}+1})

where

\displaystyle\left|e_{a}^{\top}\left(H(0;\vartheta)\{\lambda(0;\theta_{0})-\lambda(0;\vartheta)\}-H(0;\eta)\{\lambda(0;\theta_{0})-\lambda(0;\eta)\}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert H(0;\vartheta)\{\lambda(0;\theta_{0})-\lambda(0;\vartheta)\}-H(0;\eta)\{\lambda(0;\theta_{0})-\lambda(0;\eta)\}\right\rVert_{2}

CT^{p/2}

\max_{\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{T}(\theta^{\star})}\left\lvert\frac{1}{T}M_{ab,\eta}^{J}(T)\right\rvert\leq K\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}

(S3.10)

with probability at least
1-CT^{-c}
, uniformly in
\theta^{\star}
.

The net bound extends to the full local ball by the second-derivative envelope. If
\left\lVert\vartheta-\eta\right\rVert_{2}\leq T^{-1/2}
, then on
\mathcal{A}_{T}
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\max_{i=1}^{D}\left\lvert\partial_{\vartheta_{b}}H_{ai}(t;\vartheta)-\partial_{\vartheta_{b}}H_{ai}(t;\eta)\right\rvert\leq K\frac{\log^{m}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}.

Using
\mathrm{d}M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}=\mathrm{d}N_{i}-\lambda_{i}(t;\theta^{\star})\mathrm{d}t
and
\lambda_{i}(t;\theta^{\star})\leq C(1+\mathfrak{C}_{t})
, the difference between the martingale averages at
\vartheta
and
\eta
is at most
K\log^{q_{0}}(T)/\sqrt{T}
. To be precise,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\left|M_{ab,\eta}^{J}-M_{ab,\vartheta}^{J}\right|\leq K\frac{\log^{m}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\left\{\frac{1}{T}N([0,T])+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta^{\star})\,\mathrm{d}t\right\}\leq K\frac{\log^{m^{\prime}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}

(S3.11)

Hence, (
S3.10
) holds with the maximum replaced by the supremum over
B(\theta^{\star},r)
, see e.g. equation (
S3.5
), for
q_{0}
sufficiently large. We conclude the proof through a union bound over the fixed entries
(a,b)
and (
S3.8
) concludes the proof of (
S3.3
).
∎

Under Assumptions

2.3(e)
and

with
\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{2}\leq r_{H}
and
\theta^{\star}+h\in\Theta

Assumption

S3.1
Proof of Theorem
2.4
(GMM high-probability rate)

We now have the necessary tools to prove the first main result, Theorem
2.4
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. By (
2.17
) and the positive
definiteness of the fixed matrix
W
, the corresponding
W
-norm
separation at distance
\epsilon
is positive:

\delta_{H,W,\epsilon}:=\inf_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vartheta\in\Theta:\\
\left\lVert\vartheta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\epsilon\end{subarray}}\left\lVert g_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{W}>0.

Indeed,

\left\lVert g_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{W}\geq\lambda_{\min}(W)^{1/2}\left\lVert g_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{2},

and the last Euclidean norm is uniformly separated from zero by
(
2.17
).

The uniform law of large numbers (
S3.2
) implies that, for all
sufficiently large
T
,

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)-g_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{W}>\delta_{H,W,\epsilon}/4\right)\leq CT^{-c}.

On the complementary event, since

g_{H}(\theta^{\star},\theta^{\star})=0
, we have

\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{W}\leq\delta_{H,W,\epsilon}/4.

Moreover, for every

\vartheta\in\Theta
with

\left\lVert\vartheta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\epsilon
,

\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)\right\rVert_{W}\geq\left\lVert g_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{W}-\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)-g_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{W}\geq 3\delta_{H,W,\epsilon}/4.

Thus no minimizer of

Q_{T,W}^{H}(\vartheta)=\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)\right\rVert_{W}^{2}

can lie outside
B(\theta^{\star},\epsilon)
on this event. Consequently,

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\lVert\hat{\theta}_{H}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}>\epsilon\right)\leq CT^{-c}

(S3.12)

for all sufficiently large
T
.

For the following let
r_{H}
and
b_{H}
be as in Lemma
S3.3
. We next intersect the high-probability events from equations (
S3.1
), (
S3.3
) and (
S3.12
). Explicitly, with
\epsilon=r_{H}/2
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{W}\leq K_{0}\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}\right)\geq 1-C_{0}T^{-c_{0}}

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\vartheta\in B(\theta^{\star},r_{H})}\left\lVert\partial_{\vartheta}\left(\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)\right)+B_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq K_{1}\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right)\geq 1-C_{1}T^{-c_{1}}

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\lVert\hat{\theta}_{H}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\epsilon\right)\geq 1-C_{2}T^{-c_{2}}.

We next compare any
\vartheta=\theta^{\star}+h
with
\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{2}\leq r_{H}/2
to the truth. By the fundamental theorem of calculus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+h)-\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\theta}\left(\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh)\right)h\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=g_{H}(\theta^{\star}+h,\theta^{\star})+\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\partial_{\theta}\left(\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh)\right)+B_{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh,\theta^{\star})\right\}h\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=g_{H}(\theta^{\star}+h,\theta^{\star})+R_{T}(h)

To control the remaining integral

\displaystyle\left\lVert R_{T}(h)\right\rVert_{W}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\left\{\partial_{\theta}\left(\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh)\right)+B_{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh,\theta^{\star})\right\}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq K_{1}\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{2}.

Thus,

\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+h)=\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})+g_{H}(\theta^{\star}+h,\theta^{\star})+R_{T}(h),\qquad\left\lVert R_{T}(h)\right\rVert_{W}\leq K_{1}\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{2}.

For all large
T
,
K_{1}\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\leq b_{H}/4
. Therefore, by Lemma
S3.3
,

\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+h)\right\rVert_{W}\geq\frac{3b_{H}}{4}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{2}-K_{0}\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}.

If
\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{2}>\frac{4K_{0}}{b_{H}}\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}
, the right-hand side is strictly greater than
2K_{0}\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}
, for all sufficiently large
T
, whereas

\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{W}\leq K_{0}\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}.

Thus no minimizer inside
B(\theta^{\star},r_{H}/2)
can lie outside a ball of radius
K\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}
.

This concludes the proof under a stationary start. Under a non-stationary start, that satisfies the finite pre-history condition, the result can easily be transferred to the non-stationary start via Lemma
S1.6
.

In particular, let
\psi_{T}:N_{\theta}^{\eta}|_{(b_{T},\infty)}\mapsto\left\lVert\hat{\theta}_{H}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}
and
E_{T}=\left\{\omega\ :\ \psi_{T}(\omega)>K\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}\right\}
. Thus the proof concludes.
∎

In order to demonstrate the central limit theorem, we first demonstrate that the estimating equation
\mathcal{L}_{\theta^{\star}}\left\{T^{-1/2}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})\right\}
is asymptotically normal, which we can leverage in a classical Taylor expansion argument.

Proposition S3.4
(Martingale CLT for the estimating map).

Suppose Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
and Assumption
2.3(e)
hold. Then

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}d_{\mathrm{BL}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\theta^{\star}}\{T^{-1/2}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})\},N_{q}(0,\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star}))\right)\to 0.

(S3.13)

Here
d_{\mathrm{BL}}
is the bounded-Lipschitz metric on probability laws on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
.

Suppose that (
S3.13
) fails. Then there are
\epsilon>0
,
T_{n}\to\infty
, and
\theta_{n}\in\Theta_{0}
such that

d_{\mathrm{BL}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{n}}\{T_{n}^{-1/2}\Psi_{T_{n}}^{H}(\theta_{n})\},N_{q}(0,\Omega_{H}(\theta_{n}))\right)>\epsilon.

By compactness, pass to a subsequence, still denoted
\theta_{n}
, such that
\theta_{n}\to\theta_{\infty}\in\Theta_{0}
.

Fix
a\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
. Under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{n}}
,

X_{n}^{a}=a^{\top}T_{n}^{-1/2}\Psi_{T_{n}}^{H}(\theta_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}T_{n}^{-1/2}a^{\top}H_{\cdot i}(t;\theta_{n})\,\mathrm{d}M_{i}^{\theta_{n}}(t)

is a scalar martingale endpoint, the following argument follows Lemma 3.13 of
Clinet and Yoshida 2017
. The predictable variation of the scalar martingale is

V_{n}^{a}=\frac{1}{T_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\{a^{\top}H_{\cdot i}(t;\theta_{n})\}^{2}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta_{n})\,\mathrm{d}t.

The integrand is a shift-covariant compact-window functional with a polynomial local-count envelope and a polynomial Lipschitz envelope, by Assumption
2.3(e)
and Lemma
S2.1
. Theorem
4.1
, applied with the parameter index fixed at
\theta_{n}
, gives

V_{n}^{a}-a^{\top}\Omega_{H}(\theta_{n})a\to 0\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{n}}\text{-probability}.

Proposition
S1.7
gives
\Omega_{H}(\theta_{n})\to\Omega_{H}(\theta_{\infty})
, and hence

V_{n}^{a}\to a^{\top}\Omega_{H}(\theta_{\infty})a\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{n}}\text{-probability}.

The Lindeberg condition follows from the jump formula in Lemma
S2.2
. For every
\delta>0
, using
x^{2}\mathbf{1}\{|x|>\delta\}\leq|x|^{3}/\delta
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}\left[\sum_{0<t\leq T_{n}}(\Delta X_{n}^{a}(t))^{2}\mathbf{1}\{|\Delta X_{n}^{a}(t)|>\delta\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta T_{n}^{3/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{n}}\left[|a^{\top}H_{\cdot i}(t;\theta_{n})|^{3}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta_{n})\right]\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{C_{a}}{\sqrt{T_{n}}},

where the last bound uses stationarity, the polynomial envelopes, and the fixed-window exponential moment bound. The martingale central limit theorem of Rebolledo, in the form of
Jacod and Shiryaev 2003
, yields

a^{\top}T_{n}^{-1/2}\Psi_{T_{n}}^{H}(\theta_{n})\Rightarrow N\{0,a^{\top}\Omega_{H}(\theta_{\infty})a\}.

By Cramér–Wold,

T_{n}^{-1/2}\Psi_{T_{n}}^{H}(\theta_{n})\Rightarrow N_{q}(0,\Omega_{H}(\theta_{\infty})).

Also
N_{q}(0,\Omega_{H}(\theta_{n}))\Rightarrow N_{q}(0,\Omega_{H}(\theta_{\infty}))
. Since
d_{\mathrm{BL}}
metrizes weak convergence on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
, the displayed subsequential convergence contradicts the choice of
(T_{n},\theta_{n})
. Therefore (
S3.13
) holds.
∎

S3.2
Proof of Theorem
2.5
(GMM asymptotic normality)

We now prove Theorem
2.5
via a standard Taylor expansion argument, after the estimate is contained within the interior of
\Theta_{0}
.

By Theorem
2.4
,

\hat{\theta}_{H}-\theta^{\star}=O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}\right).

Since
\Theta_{0}\Subset\Theta^{\circ}
, there is
\rho>0
such that
B(\theta,\rho)\subset\Theta^{\circ}
for every
\theta\in\Theta_{0}
. The preceding rate implies

\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\{\hat{\theta}_{H}\in B(\theta^{\star},\rho)\}\to 1,

so
\hat{\theta}_{H}
is an interior minimizer with probability tending to one. On this event the first-order condition is

\partial_{\vartheta}\left(\frac{1}{T^{2}}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H})^{\top}W\Psi_{T}^{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H})\right)=0.

Moreover, optimality of the estimator
\hat{\theta}_{H}
yields that

\left\|\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H})\right\|_{W}\leq\left\|\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})\right\|_{W}.

By Proposition
S3.4
,
\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})=O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2})
, and the fixed positive-definite matrix
W
has deterministic eigenvalue bounds. Hence

\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H})=O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2}).

(S3.14)

Lemma
S3.7
, with
h_{T}=\hat{\theta}_{H}-\theta^{\star}
, gives

\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H})=\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})-A_{H}(\theta^{\star})(\hat{\theta}_{H}-\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2})

and

\partial_{\vartheta}\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H})=-A_{H}(\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1).

Substituting these two expansions into the first-order condition and using (
S3.14
) yields

A_{H}^{\top}WA_{H}\sqrt{T}(\hat{\theta}_{H}-\theta^{\star})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}A_{H}^{\top}W\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1),

where
A_{H}
is evaluated at
\theta^{\star}
. Since
A_{H}^{\top}WA_{H}
is nonsingular by Assumption
2.3(f)
, Proposition
S3.4
and Slutsky’s theorem give (
2.20
); the oracle optimally weighted formula follows by setting
W=\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})^{-1}
.

Transfer of the central limit theorem to non-stationary start follows from equation (
S1.21
), and using that
\sqrt{T-b_{T}}\sim\sqrt{T}.

S3.3
Least-squares contrast minimizer

In this section of the supplemental material we apply our theoretical results to the least squares estimator introduced in example
2.1
. We do so since, like the maximum likelihood estimate, it is a member of our derivative weight class via its first order condition, i.e. that it is the zero of a derivative estimator. To ensure the maximum of the equation (
2.8
) lies inside
\Theta_{0}
we must first localize
\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS}
to be contained within
\Theta_{0}^{\circ}
with high probability.

Proposition S3.5
(Least-squares contrast minimizer).

Let
\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS}
be a measurable global minimizer of the least-squares contrast
\gamma_{T}
in (
2.8
) over
\Theta
, and define

A_{\rm LS}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\{D_{\theta}(0;\theta)^{\top}D_{\theta}(0;\theta)\},\qquad\Omega_{\rm LS}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\{D_{\theta}(0;\theta)^{\top}\Lambda(0;\theta)D_{\theta}(0;\theta)\}.

Under Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(d)
, for every
c>0
there are
C,K<\infty
such that, for all sufficiently large
T
,

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\lVert\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}>K\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c}.

For each fixed
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
,

\sqrt{T}(\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS}-\theta^{\star})\Rightarrow N_{p}\{0,A_{\rm LS}(\theta^{\star})^{-1}\Omega_{\rm LS}(\theta^{\star})A_{\rm LS}(\theta^{\star})^{-1}\}.

Set
H_{D}(t;\theta)=D_{\theta}(t;\theta)^{\top}
and
m_{T}^{D}(\theta)=T^{-1}\Psi_{T}^{H_{D}}(\theta)
. By Lemma
S2.1
,
H_{D}
satisfies the envelope requirements of Assumption
2.3(e)
. Let
\underline{\lambda}>0
be the uniform lower bound on the intensities over
K_{\Theta}
. Then

A_{\rm LS}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\{D_{\theta}(0;\theta)^{\top}D_{\theta}(0;\theta)\}\succeq\underline{\lambda}\,I(\theta),

so
A_{\rm LS}
is uniformly nonsingular on
\Theta_{0}
by Assumption
2.3(d)
. Similarly,

\Omega_{\rm LS}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\{D_{\theta}(0;\theta)^{\top}\Lambda(0;\theta)D_{\theta}(0;\theta)\}\succeq\underline{\lambda}\,A_{\rm LS}(\theta)

is uniformly nonsingular on
\Theta_{0}
. On
N([-A,T])<\infty
, the map
\theta\mapsto\gamma_{T}(\theta)
is continuous on compact
\Theta
, by the same finite-event and dominated-convergence argument as Proposition
S3.1
; hence a measurable global minimizer exists.

Define the population contrast

\Gamma(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})=\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\{\lambda_{i}(0;\vartheta)^{2}-2\lambda_{i}(0;\vartheta)\lambda_{i}(0;\theta^{\star})\}.

The same compact-window concentration, martingale-bracket, and net argument used in Proposition
S3.2
gives, for every
c>0
, constants
C,K<\infty
and
q_{0}\geq 1
such that

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\left|\gamma_{T}(\vartheta)-\Gamma(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right|>K\frac{\log^{q_{0}}T}{\sqrt{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c}.

Indeed, writing
\mathrm{d}N_{i}(t)=\lambda_{i}(t;\theta^{\star})\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}M_{i}^{\theta^{\star}}(t)
, the deterministic average has integrand
\lambda_{i}(0;\vartheta)^{2}-2\lambda_{i}(0;\vartheta)\lambda_{i}(0;\theta^{\star})
, while the martingale average has integrand
\lambda_{i}(0;\vartheta)
. Both are compact-window functionals with polynomial local-count and Lipschitz envelopes uniformly over
(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\in\Theta\times\Theta_{0}
.

Moreover,

\Gamma(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})-\Gamma(\theta^{\star},\theta^{\star})=\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\bigl\|\lambda(0;\vartheta)-\lambda(0;\theta^{\star})\bigr\|_{2}^{2}.

If this quantity is zero, stationarity gives equality of the two intensity paths for Lebesgue-a.e. time under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}
, and Assumption
2.3(d)
gives
\vartheta=\theta^{\star}
. Proposition
S1.7
gives continuity of
(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\mapsto\Gamma(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})
, so compactness yields, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\delta_{\epsilon}:=\inf_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\inf_{\left\lVert\vartheta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\epsilon}\{\Gamma(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})-\Gamma(\theta^{\star},\theta^{\star})\}>0.

Combining this separation with the preceding uniform law gives

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\lVert\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}>\epsilon\right)\leq CT^{-c}

for all sufficiently large
T
.

It remains to sharpen consistency to the stated logarithmic rate. Since
\Theta_{0}\Subset\Theta^{\circ}
, the preceding consistency places
\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS}
in an interior neighborhood of
\theta^{\star}
with probability at least
1-CT^{-c}
. On this event the first-order condition holds, and
m_{T}^{D}(\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS})=0
, because
\nabla_{\theta}\gamma_{T}(\theta)=-(2/T)\Psi_{T}^{H_{D}}(\theta)
. The local-curvature argument used in Lemma
S3.3
, applied here to
H_{D}
, gives constants
r_{D},b_{D}>0
such that

\|g_{H_{D}}(\theta^{\star}+h,\theta^{\star})\|_{2}\geq b_{D}\|h\|_{2}

whenever
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
,
\|h\|_{2}\leq r_{D}
, and
\theta^{\star}+h\in\Theta
. This local statement uses only the rank bound just verified, not global derivative-moment separation.

On the high-probability event from Proposition
S3.2
, the Taylor expansion

m_{T}^{D}(\theta^{\star}+h)=m_{T}^{D}(\theta^{\star})+g_{H_{D}}(\theta^{\star}+h,\theta^{\star})+R_{T}(h)

has
\|R_{T}(h)\|_{2}\leq K\log^{q_{0}}(T)T^{-1/2}\|h\|_{2}
, and for large
T
this last coefficient is at most
b_{D}/2
. Since
m_{T}^{D}(\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS})=0
and
\|m_{T}^{D}(\theta^{\star})\|_{2}\leq K\sqrt{\log(T)/T}
, we obtain

\|\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS}-\theta^{\star}\|_{2}\leq K\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}},

uniformly with polynomially high probability.

For the limit distribution, fix
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
. The rate just proved and Lemma
S3.7
, again with
H=H_{D}
, yield

0=m_{T}^{D}(\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS})=m_{T}^{D}(\theta^{\star})-A_{\rm LS}(\theta^{\star})(\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS}-\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2}).

Hence

\sqrt{T}(\hat{\theta}_{\rm LS}-\theta^{\star})=A_{\rm LS}(\theta^{\star})^{-1}T^{-1/2}\Psi_{T}^{H_{D}}(\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1).

Proposition
S3.4
gives
T^{-1/2}\Psi_{T}^{H_{D}}(\theta^{\star})\Rightarrow N_{p}\{0,\Omega_{\rm LS}(\theta^{\star})\}
, which proves the asserted sandwich limit.
∎

The remainder of this section of the supplement aims to transfer the main results to the two-step estimator. It does so by demonstrating that the plug-in optimal weight matrix concentrates uniformly around the population optimal weight matrix, and then applying standard M-estimation arguments. For later use, define the two-parameter population covariance

\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})=\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\{H(0;\vartheta)\Lambda(0;\vartheta)H(0;\vartheta)^{\top}\}.

Thus
\Omega_{H}(\theta)=\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\theta,\theta)
.

Lemma S3.6
(Polynomial-tail well-conditioning of the plug-in covariance).

Suppose Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
and
Assumptions
2.3(e)
–
2.3(f)
hold.

Let
\tilde{\theta}_{T}
be a preliminary estimator satisfying the same
polynomial-tail logarithmic rate as in Theorem
2.4
; that is,
for every
c>0
there are
C,K<\infty
such that, for all
large
T
,

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\lVert\tilde{\theta}_{T}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}>K\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c}.

Then, for every
c>0
, there are
C<\infty
and deterministic constants

0<\omega_{-}<\omega_{+}<\infty
such that, for all sufficiently large
T
,

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\lambda_{\min}\{\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})\}<\omega_{-}\ \vee\lambda_{\max}\{\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})\}>\omega_{+}\right)\leq CT^{-c}.

(S3.15)

Equivalently, on an event of probability at least
1-CT^{-c}
,

\omega_{-}I_{q}\preceq\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})\preceq\omega_{+}I_{q}

and hence

\omega_{+}^{-1}I_{q}\preceq\hat{W}_{T}\preceq\omega_{-}^{-1}I_{q},\qquad\hat{W}_{T}:=\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})^{-1}.

First, for each entry of the matrix

H(0;\vartheta)\Lambda(0;\vartheta)H(0;\vartheta)^{\top}
, Assumption
2.3(e)

and Lemma
S2.1
give a polynomial local-count envelope, uniformly over

\vartheta\in\Theta
, in particular, there exist uniform
B,m>0
such that

\left|\left(H(0;\vartheta)\Lambda(0;\vartheta)H(0;\vartheta)^{\top}\right)_{ij}\right|\leq C(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{m})

for each
ij
pair. This follows from the fact that
p,q
and
D
are fixed so the operator norm bounds for
H
imply entrywise bounds. This holds true also for the uniform polynomial local-count Lipschitz envelope. Therefore, entrywise concentration from Theorem
4.1
, which reads for all
c>0
there exists a
K>0
,
C>0
and
q_{0}>0
so that

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\left\lvert\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\vartheta)_{ij}-\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})_{ij}\right\rvert>K\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c}

and the fixed dimension converts the
entrywise bound to the operator norm, for some new constant
K
proving

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\left\lVert\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\vartheta)-\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}>K\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c}

(S3.16)

Next, note that the population plug-in covariance is locally Lipschitz in its
first argument, uniformly over the true parameter. More precisely, there are

r_{\Omega}>0
and
L_{\Omega}<\infty
such that, whenever

\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
and

\|\vartheta-\theta^{\star}\|_{2}\leq r_{\Omega}
,

\|\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})-\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L_{\Omega}\|\vartheta-\theta^{\star}\|_{2}.

This can be seen by considering

F(\vartheta,N)=H(0;\vartheta)\Lambda(0;\vartheta)H(0;\vartheta)^{\top},

the derivative
\partial_{\vartheta}F(\vartheta,N)
is a finite sum of terms
of the form

\partial_{\vartheta}H(0;\vartheta)\Lambda(0;\vartheta)H(0;\vartheta)^{\top},\qquad H(0;\vartheta)\partial_{\vartheta}\Lambda(0;\vartheta)H(0;\vartheta)^{\top},

and their transposes. By Assumption
2.3(e)
and
Lemma
S2.1
, these derivatives have a polynomial
local-count envelope uniformly on a fixed neighbourhood of
\Theta_{0}
.
The fixed-window moment bound then gives an integrable uniform envelope under

\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}
, uniformly in
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
. The mean-value
theorem therefore gives the displayed Lipschitz bound.

Let

a_{T}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}},\qquad b_{T}=\frac{\log^{q_{0}}(T)}{\sqrt{T}},

where
q_{0}
is the exponent in equation (
S3.16
). Next, define the events

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,T}=\left\{\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\|\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\vartheta)-\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq K_{\Omega}b_{T}\right\}

and

\mathcal{E}_{\theta,T}=\left\{\|\tilde{\theta}_{T}-\theta^{\star}\|_{2}\leq K_{\theta}a_{T}\right\}.

By equation (
S3.16
) and by the assumed polynomial-tail
rate of
\tilde{\theta}_{T}
, after increasing constants,

\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\{(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,T}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\theta,T})^{c}\}\leq CT^{-c}.

For all sufficiently large
T
,
K_{\theta}a_{T}\leq r_{\Omega}
. On

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,T}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\theta,T}
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})-\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})-\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\tilde{\theta}_{T},\theta^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\tilde{\theta}_{T},\theta^{\star})-\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\Omega}b_{T}+L_{\Omega}K_{\theta}a_{T}.

Since
a_{T}\to 0
and
b_{T}\to 0
, the last display is at most

\omega_{0}/2
for all sufficiently large
T
. Weyl’s eigenvalue
inequality gives

\lambda_{\min}\{\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})\}\geq\lambda_{\min}\{\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})\}-\|\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})-\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq\omega_{0}/2.

Thus
\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})
is invertible on this event.

It remains only to obtain the upper eigenvalue bound. By continuity of

\Omega_{H}(\theta)
on the compact set
\Theta_{0}
,

\bar{\omega}:=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\lambda_{\max}\{\Omega_{H}(\theta)\}<\infty.

Again by Weyl’s inequality, on the same event,

\lambda_{\max}\{\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})\}\leq\bar{\omega}+\omega_{0}/2=\vcentcolon\omega_{+}<\infty.

Therefore

\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}I_{q}\preceq\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})\preceq\omega_{+}I_{q}.

Inverting reverses the Loewner order and gives

\omega_{+}^{-1}I_{q}\preceq\hat{W}_{T}\preceq 2\omega_{0}^{-1}I_{q}.

With
\omega_{-}=\omega_{0}/2
, the bounds
\lambda_{\min}\{\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})\}\geq\omega_{-}
and
\lambda_{\max}\{\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})\}\leq\omega_{+}
are exactly the complement of the event in (
S3.15
), which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma S3.7
(Local stochastic differentiability of compact-window GMM).

Fix
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
. Under Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
and Assumption
2.3(e)
, if
h_{T}=O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(\sqrt{\log(T)/T})
and
\theta^{\star}+h_{T}\in\Theta
with probability tending to one, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+h_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})-A_{H}(\theta^{\star})h_{T}+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2}),

(S3.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{\vartheta}\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+h_{T})

\displaystyle=-A_{H}(\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1).

(S3.18)

Choose
r>0
such that
B(\theta^{\star},r)\subset K_{\Theta}
. Since

h_{T}=o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1)
, the whole segment

\{\theta^{\star}+sh_{T}:0\leq s\leq 1\}
lies in
B(\theta^{\star},r)
with
probability tending to one. By Proposition
S3.2
, for
some integer
q_{0}
,

\sup_{\vartheta\in B(\theta^{\star},r)}\left\lVert\partial_{\vartheta}\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\vartheta)+B_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}\left(\frac{\log^{q_{0}}T}{\sqrt{T}}\right).

Continuity of
B_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})
at
\vartheta=\theta^{\star}
, together with
B_{H}(\theta^{\star},\theta^{\star})=A_{H}(\theta^{\star})
, gives

B_{H}(\theta^{\star}+h_{T},\theta^{\star})=A_{H}(\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1),

which proves (
S3.18
).

For (
S3.17
), use the fundamental theorem of calculus:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+h_{T})-\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}B_{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh_{T},\theta^{\star})h_{T}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\partial_{\vartheta}\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh_{T})+B_{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh_{T},\theta^{\star})\right\}h_{T}\,\mathrm{d}s.

The second integral is

O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}\left(\frac{\log^{q_{0}}T}{\sqrt{T}}\right)O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}\right)=o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2}).

It remains to replace
B_{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh_{T},\theta^{\star})
by
A_{H}(\theta^{\star})
. The exact two-parameter derivative is

\displaystyle B_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\{H(0;\vartheta)D_{\theta}(0;\vartheta)\}

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[\partial_{\vartheta}H(0;\vartheta)\{\lambda(0;\theta^{\star})-\lambda(0;\vartheta)\}\right],

where the second line is interpreted as the corresponding contraction of the derivative tensor of
H
with the intensity difference. Hence, for
\vartheta=\theta^{\star}+sh_{T}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert B_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})-A_{H}(\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\{H(0;\vartheta)D_{\theta}(0;\vartheta)-H(0;\theta^{\star})D_{\theta}(0;\theta^{\star})\}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[\partial_{\vartheta}H(0;\vartheta)\{\lambda(0;\theta^{\star})-\lambda(0;\vartheta)\}\right]\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

The first term is
\mathcal{O}(\left\lVert\vartheta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2})
by the Lipschitz envelopes for
H
, Lemma
S2.1
for the envelope of
D_{\theta}
, the mean-value theorem, and the fixed-window moment bound. The second term is also
\mathcal{O}(\left\lVert\vartheta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2})
, because
\nabla_{\vartheta}H
has a polynomial envelope by assumption
2.3(e)
, and

\left\lVert\lambda(0;\vartheta)-\lambda(0;\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{2}\leq C(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{m})\left\lVert\vartheta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}

by the polynomial local-count envelope of
\nabla\lambda(0;\theta)
and the mean-value theorem. Therefore,

\sup_{0\leq s\leq 1}\left\lVert B_{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh_{T},\theta^{\star})-A_{H}(\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C\left\lVert h_{T}\right\rVert_{2}

with the displayed deterministic bound holding on the event
\theta^{\star}+h_{T}\in\Theta
. Consequently,

\left\lVert\int_{0}^{1}\{B_{H}(\theta^{\star}+sh_{T},\theta^{\star})-A_{H}(\theta^{\star})\}h_{T}\,\mathrm{d}s\right\rVert_{2}\leq C\left\lVert h_{T}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}\left(\frac{\log(T)}{T}\right)=o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2}).

Combining the preceding displays proves (
S3.17
).
∎

S3.4
Proof of Corollary
2.6
(feasible optimal GMM)

The necessary regularity conditions have now been obtained to prove the asymptotic results for the two-step estimator. We first use Lemma
S3.6
for local invertibility which immediately implies the proof of Theorem
2.4
holds. The central limit theorem follows from the same argument as Theorem

By Lemma

On this event, the consistency and local-rate proof of

applies with

in place of the
fixed weighting matrix. The lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue follows from

and the full-column-rank condition on

. Moreover,

on this event, so the global separation in Assumption
2.3(f)
transfers to the random weighted norm. In particular, this implies

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}\left(\left\lVert\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}>K\sqrt{\frac{\log(T)}{T}}\right)\leq CT^{-c}

by equation (
S3.15
). The proof of Theorem
2.4
can be applied exactly to the first summand above, using the lower bounding constant
\omega_{+}^{-1}
in place of

. Therefore, equation (
2.21
) holds under a stationary or non-stationary start (after burning in the data).

To prove equation (
2.22
), first note that
\Theta_{0}\Subset\Theta^{\circ}
so that the second-step estimator is an interior minimizer with probability tending to one with first-order condition

\partial_{\vartheta}\left(\frac{1}{T^{2}}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)})^{\top}\hat{W}_{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)})\right)=0

Lemma
S3.7

\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)})=\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})-A_{H}(\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)}-\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2}),

Moreover, on the same event, optimality gives

\frac{1}{T^{2}}\Psi_{T}^{H}\left(\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)}\right)^{\top}\hat{W}_{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}\left(\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)}\right)\leq\frac{1}{T^{2}}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})^{\top}\hat{W}_{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star}).

Since
\omega_{+}^{-1}I_{q}\preceq\hat{W}_{T}\preceq 2\omega_{0}^{-1}I_{q}

S3.4

\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})=O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2})
, it follows that

\frac{1}{T}\Psi_{T}^{H}\left(\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)}\right)=O_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(T^{-1/2}).

Substituting the previous displays into the first-order condition yields

A_{H}^{\top}\hat{W}_{T}A_{H}\sqrt{T}(\hat{\theta}_{H}^{(2)}-\theta^{\star})=A_{H}^{\top}\hat{W}_{T}\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\Psi_{T}^{H}(\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1).

If
\hat{W}_{T}\to\Omega_{H}^{-1}(\theta^{\star})
, Slutsky’s theorem, and non-singularity of

gives the displayed asymptotic linear expansion. The limiting normal distribution follows from Proposition
S3.4
. Moreover, in the case of a non-stationary start that satisfies the local integrability assumptions, the justification at the end of the proof of Theorem
2.5
via the same total variation bound implies the same limit after a suitable burn-in period and rescaling.

It remains to show

By (
S3.16
),

\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\left\lVert\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\vartheta)-\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1).

Since

and

is continuous at

\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\tilde{\theta}_{T},\theta^{\star})\xrightarrow{p}\bar{\Omega}_{H}(\theta^{\star},\theta^{\star})=\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star}).

Therefore,

\hat{\Omega}_{H,T}(\tilde{\theta}_{T})\xrightarrow{p}\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star}).

By the positive-definiteness of
\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})
and the continuous

\hat{W}_{T}\xrightarrow{p}\Omega_{H}(\theta^{\star})^{-1}.

∎

S4

This appendix proves Theorem
2.10
and Corollary
2.11
. The local score, local curvature, and score CLT inputs are consequences of the generic compact-window estimating-map theory applied to the score weight

In particular, this section of the supplement ensures that the maximum likelihood estimate agrees with the zero of the score in the constrained problem where
\Theta
is compact.

First, write

\sup_{\vartheta\in B(\theta^{\star},\rho_{T})}\left\|\frac{1}{T}\nabla^{2}\ell_{T}(\vartheta)+J(\vartheta,\theta^{\star})\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1).

The continuity of
(\vartheta,\theta)\mapsto J(\vartheta,\theta)
, proved through Proposition
S1.7
, and
J(\theta^{\star},\theta^{\star})=I(\theta^{\star})
, imply

\sup_{\vartheta\in B(\theta^{\star},\rho_{T})}\left\|\frac{1}{T}\nabla^{2}\ell_{T}(\vartheta)+I(\theta^{\star})\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1).

(S4.5)

A Taylor expansion of the score around
\theta^{\star}
gives, on the event that
\hat{\theta}_{T}
is interior,

0=T^{-1/2}\nabla\ell_{T}(\theta^{\star})+\left[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{T}\nabla^{2}\ell_{T}\{\theta^{\star}+s(\hat{\theta}_{T}-\theta^{\star})\}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]\sqrt{T}(\hat{\theta}_{T}-\theta^{\star}).

The whole segment lies in
B(\theta^{\star},\rho_{T})
with probability tending
to one, and (
S4.5
) gives

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{T}\nabla^{2}\ell_{T}\{\theta^{\star}+s(\hat{\theta}_{T}-\theta^{\star})\}\,\mathrm{d}s=-I(\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1).

Since
I(\theta^{\star})
is nonsingular, inversion and (
S4.4
) yields

\sqrt{T}(\hat{\theta}_{T}-\theta^{\star})=I(\theta^{\star})^{-1}T^{-1/2}\nabla\ell_{T}(\theta^{\star})+o_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}}(1),

and the displayed normal limit follows by Slutsky’s theorem and the same burn-in argument as given at the end of the proof of Theorem
2.5
.
∎

S5
Likelihood lower bound

We conclude the general theoretical discussion of the supplement by demonstrating the
T^{-1/2}
rate cannot be uniformly improved by any (measurable) estimator. To do so, we first prove a technical lemma about the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between two “well-behaved” conditional intensity functions, conditional on a locally finite history. Using this result, we are able to apply Le Cam’s two-point lemma
[e.g.
Wainwright 2019
, Chapter 15]
and thus prove Theorem
2.12
.

Lemma S5.1
(Conditional KL on the likelihood interval).

Let
K\Subset\Theta^{\circ}
. For a locally finite history
\mathcal{H}
on
[-A,0)
, let
\mathbb{P}_{\theta,\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}
be the law on
[0,T]
of the multivariate point process whose predictable intensity, given
\mathcal{H}
and its own past
\omega|_{[0,t)}
, is the general compact-memory intensity

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{\mathcal{H},\omega}(t;\theta)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\theta,i}\left(\nu_{\theta,i}+\sum_{j=1}^{D}\int_{[t-A,t)}h_{\theta,ij}(t-s)\,d(\mathcal{H}_{j}+\omega_{j})(s)\right),\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

Here
\mathcal{H}_{j}
is understood as a measure on
[-A,0)
and
\omega_{j}
as a measure on
[0,T]
. This family is a regular conditional version of the stationary future law: if
\mathcal{H}=N|_{[-A,0)}
under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
, then

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(N|_{[0,T]}\in\cdot\mid\mathcal{H}\big)=\mathbb{P}_{\theta,\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}(\cdot)\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\text{-a.s.}

There is a constant
C_{K}<\infty
such that, for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in K
and all
T\geq 1
, if
\mathcal{H}=N|_{[-A,0)}
has law
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{[-A,0)}
, then

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\mathbb{P}_{\theta,\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}\,\middle\|\,\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\prime},\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}\right)\right]\leq C_{K}T\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

S5.1
Proof of Lemma
S5.1
(Conditional KL on the likelihood interval)

The existence of a regular conditional version follows from the compactly supported finite-memory property and the existence of regular conditional distributions on the standard Borel configuration spaces; see
Kallenberg 2017
.

For a fixed history
\mathcal{H}
, the standard likelihood and KL formula for point processes with strictly positive predictable intensities gives, conditional on
\mathcal{H}
,

D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\mathbb{P}_{\theta,\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}\middle\|\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\prime},\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta,\mathcal{H}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{D}\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\lambda_{i}^{\mathcal{H},N}(t;\theta)\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}^{\mathcal{H},N}(t;\theta)}{\lambda_{i}^{\mathcal{H},N}(t;\theta^{\prime})}\right)-\lambda_{i}^{\mathcal{H},N}(t;\theta)+\lambda_{i}^{\mathcal{H},N}(t;\theta^{\prime})\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right],

see
Daley and Vere-Jones 2003
. Let
\underline{\lambda}>0
be the uniform lower bound on the intensities over
K_{\Theta}
. Then

x\log(x/y)-x+y\leq\underline{\lambda}^{-1}(x-y)^{2},\qquad x,y\geq\underline{\lambda}.

By the mean-value theorem on
K_{\Theta}
and Lemma
S2.1
, for the concatenated path under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta,\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}
,

\big|\lambda_{i}^{\mathcal{H},N}(t;\theta)-\lambda_{i}^{\mathcal{H},N}(t;\theta^{\prime})\big|\leq C_{K}\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}\{1+N([t-A,t))^{m}\}.

Averaging first over
\mathcal{H}\sim\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{[-A,0)}
and then over
\mathbb{P}_{\theta,\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}
is the same as averaging the concatenated path under the stationary law
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
on
[-A,T]
by the tower property. Stationarity and the fixed-window exponential count moment (
S1.12
) therefore give

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\mathbb{P}_{\theta,\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}\middle\|\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\prime},\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}\right)\right]\leq C_{K}\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\{1+N([t-A,t))^{2m}\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq C_{K}T\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

which is as required.
∎

S5.2
Proof of Theorem
2.12

We are now ready to prove the rate-level minimax lower bound.

Since
B(\theta_{0},r)\subset\Theta_{0}
, choose

K:=\overline{B(\theta_{0},r/2)}\subset\Theta_{0},\qquad K\Subset\Theta^{\circ},

and define the empty pre-sample event

E_{0}:=\{N|_{[-A,0)}=\emptyset\}.

By the finite-memory property, and the uniform fixed-window moment bounds, there exists
p_{0}>0
such that

\inf_{\theta\in K}\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(E_{0})\geq p_{0}.

Indeed, conditional on the past before
-A
, the probability of no accepted point in
[-A,0)
is
e^{-\Lambda^{0}_{\theta}}
, where
\Lambda^{0}_{\theta}=\int_{-A}^{0}\lambda(t;\theta)dt
is the conditional total hazard with no points accepted in
[-A,0)
. Polynomial local-count envelopes and compact memory give

\Lambda^{0}_{\theta}\leq C_{E}\{1+N([-2A,-A))^{m_{0}}\}

for some
m_{0}<\infty
, uniformly over
\theta\in K
. Hence, using Jensen’s inequality and the fixed-window moment bound,

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(E_{0})=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}e^{-\Lambda^{0}_{\theta}}\geq e^{-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\Lambda^{0}_{\theta}}\geq p_{0}>0

uniformly over
\theta\in K
.

Let
C_{K}
be the constant in Lemma
S5.1
for this compact set
K
, and set
C_{\varnothing}=C_{K}/p_{0}
. Choose
a_{0}>0
so small that

a_{0}\leq r/4,\qquad 4C_{\varnothing}a_{0}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Fix
0<a\leq a_{0}
and a unit vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and define

\theta_{1}=\theta_{0}+\frac{2a}{\sqrt{T}}v.

Then
\theta_{1}\in K
for every
T\geq 1
, since
\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{0}\|_{2}\leq 2a\leq r/2
.

Define the conditional future laws from the empty history by

Q_{0}:=\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{0},\varnothing}^{[0,T]},\qquad Q_{1}:=\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{1},\varnothing}^{[0,T]}.

Since
E_{0}
is the atom
\{\mathcal{H}=\varnothing\}
of the history distribution and has probability at least
p_{0}
under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{0}}
, Lemma
S5.1
gives

p_{0}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_{0}\|Q_{1})\leq\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{0},\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}\middle\|\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{1},\mathcal{H}}^{[0,T]}\right)\right]\leq C_{K}T\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

Therefore

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_{0}\|Q_{1})\leq 4C_{\varnothing}a^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Let
\tilde{\theta}_{T}
be any estimator measurable with respect to
N|_{[-A,T]}
. On
E_{0}
, the history is fixed and empty, so the estimator is a measurable function of the future path only. Set
s_{T}=a/\sqrt{T}
. Since
\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{0}\|_{2}=2s_{T}
, the two-point testing reduction
[e.g.
Wainwright 2019
, Chapter 15]
gives

\max_{\ell=0,1}Q_{\ell}\left(\|\tilde{\theta}_{T}(\varnothing,N)-\theta_{\ell}\|_{2}\geq s_{T}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\{1-d_{\rm TV}(Q_{0},Q_{1})\}.

By Pinsker’s inequality,

d_{\rm TV}(Q_{0},Q_{1})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_{0}\|Q_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{2},

and hence the preceding maximum is at least
1/4
.

For
\ell=0,1
, conditioning on the empty pre-sample event gives

\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{\ell}}\left(\|\tilde{\theta}_{T}-\theta_{\ell}\|_{2}\geq s_{T}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{\ell}}(E_{0})Q_{\ell}\left(\|\tilde{\theta}_{T}(\varnothing,N)-\theta_{\ell}\|_{2}\geq s_{T}\right).

Since
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in K
,
\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{\ell}}(E_{0})\geq p_{0}
. Thus

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\left(\|\tilde{\theta}_{T}-\theta\|_{2}\geq\frac{a}{\sqrt{T}}\right)\geq\frac{p_{0}}{4}.

Taking the infimum over all estimators proves (
2.27
) with
\epsilon_{0}=p_{0}/4
and, for instance,
T_{0}=1
.
∎

S6
Identifiability and moment-identification examples

In this section of the supplement we verify that the examples given in Section
3.1
satisfy our standing assumptions.

Proposition S6.1
(Identifiability and information for positive-active truncated exponentials).

Consider the active scalar truncated-exponential family of Example
3.1
, with each active amplitude restricted to a compact subinterval of
(0,\infty)
. Then the model is identifiable in the sense of
Assumption
2.3(d)
. Moreover, for every

\theta
in this active compact interior model, the Fisher information matrix

I(\theta)
is positive definite. Consequently, for every compact

\Theta_{0}\Subset\Theta^{\circ}
contained in this model,

\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\lambda_{\min}\{I(\theta)\}>0.

The proof identifies parameters by matching the jump sizes of the intensities
\lambda_{i}(\cdot;\theta)
, so we begin by recording why those jumps are well defined and why an intensity difference that vanishes for Lebesgue-almost every
t
must in fact vanish at every time, jumps included.

Each intensity, and each of its parameter derivatives used below, is an affine function of finite-memory convolutions of the form
Y_{f}(t)=\int_{[t-A,t)}f(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s)
with
f
a smooth kernel, as in Lemma
S2.2
(i). As
t
increases, the events contributing to
Y_{f}(t)
change only when an event of
N_{j}
at some time
s
enters the window
[t-A,t)
, at
t=s
, or leaves it, at
t=s+A
; call these the entry and exit times. Between two consecutive entry/exit times
Y_{f}
is a fixed finite sum of smooth terms
f(t-s_{\ell})
, hence
C^{1}
, and its only discontinuities are the one-sided jumps at those times.

We work on the full-probability event of Lemma
S2.2
(ii): on every compact interval, distinct components never jump simultaneously, no event falls on a prescribed deterministic time, and no two events are separated by exactly
A
. On this event all entry and exit times are distinct and isolated, so any finite linear combination of such convolutions is
C^{1}
on each open interval between consecutive entry/exit times. Being continuous there, it is identically zero on each such interval as soon as it is zero for Lebesgue-almost every
t
; and since its one-sided limits at every entry or exit time are then limits of zero, each entry and exit jump vanishes as well. These vanishing jumps are the identities exploited below.

First we prove identifiability. Suppose that, under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
,

\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)=\lambda_{i}(t;\theta^{\prime})\quad\text{for all }i\text{ and for Lebesgue-a.e. }t,\quad\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\text{-a.s.}

We also intersect the preceding path-regularity event with the event on which
this equality of intensities holds. Fix a receiving component
i
and a
source component
j
. Since the stationary intensity of component
j
is
positive,
N_{j}
has infinitely many jumps on the real line almost surely.
Let
s
be a jump time of
N_{j}
.

By the no-simultaneous-jump and no-exact-
A
-separation properties, no other
event enters a predictable window at time
s
, and no event exits a
predictable window at time
s
. Therefore the only right discontinuity of

\lambda_{i}(\cdot;\theta)-\lambda_{i}(\cdot;\theta^{\prime})
at
s
caused by this
source event is its entry into the
j
-convolution. Since the intensity
difference is zero on the continuity intervals immediately before and after

s
, this right jump must vanish. Thus

\alpha_{ij}c_{ij}(\beta_{ij})-\alpha^{\prime}_{ij}c_{ij}(\beta^{\prime}_{ij})=0.

Similarly, at time
s+A
, the same source event is the only event exiting
the
j
-convolution, and no other event enters or exits at that time. Hence
the right jump at
s+A
must also vanish, which gives

\alpha_{ij}c_{ij}(\beta_{ij})e^{-\beta_{ij}A}-\alpha^{\prime}_{ij}c_{ij}(\beta^{\prime}_{ij})e^{-\beta^{\prime}_{ij}A}=0.

Both factors
\alpha_{ij}c_{ij}(\beta_{ij})
and

\alpha^{\prime}_{ij}c_{ij}(\beta^{\prime}_{ij})
are nonzero by the active-amplitude restriction. Dividing the second
display by the first yields

e^{-\beta_{ij}A}=e^{-\beta^{\prime}_{ij}A},

and hence
\beta_{ij}=\beta^{\prime}_{ij}
. Substituting back into the first display
gives
\alpha_{ij}=\alpha^{\prime}_{ij}
. Since
i
and
j
were arbitrary, all
active amplitude and decay coordinates agree. The equality of intensities then
reduces to
\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}^{\prime}
for every
i
. Thus
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
, proving
identifiability.

We now prove positive definiteness of
I(\theta)
. Fix

\theta
in the active compact interior model. It is enough to show that,
for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

v^{\top}I(\theta)v=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v=0.

For each receiving component
i
, define the directional derivative of the
intensity

D_{i}^{v}(t)=v_{\mu_{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{D}v_{\alpha_{ij}}\int_{[t-A,t)}k_{ij}(t-s;\beta_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s)+\sum_{j=1}^{D}\alpha_{ij}v_{\beta_{ij}}\int_{[t-A,t)}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(t-s;\beta_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s).

Then
D_{i}^{v}(t)=v^{\top}\nabla_{\theta}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)
. Hence, if

v^{\top}I(\theta)v=0
, then

0=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{D}\frac{\{D_{i}^{v}(0)\}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}(0;\theta)}\right].

Since
\lambda_{i}(0;\theta)>0
, it follows that

D_{i}^{v}(0)=0\qquad\text{almost surely for every }i.

By stationarity,
D_{i}^{v}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}D_{i}^{v}(0)
for
every fixed
t
. Hence, for every integer
m
,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\int_{m}^{m+1}\mathbf{1}\{D_{i}^{v}(t)\neq 0\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]=\int_{m}^{m+1}\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\{D_{i}^{v}(t)\neq 0\}\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Thus, after taking a countable intersection over
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

D_{i}^{v}(t)=0\quad\text{for Lebesgue-a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\text{-a.s.}

We now use the path regularity of the compactly supported convolutions. On every
compact interval, the discontinuity times of
D_{i}^{v}
are contained in the
finite set of event times and event times plus
A
. Between these times

D_{i}^{v}
is continuously differentiable. Therefore, since
D_{i}^{v}
vanishes
for Lebesgue-a.e. time, it must vanish identically on each open continuity
interval. At any entry or exit time
r
, left-continuity gives

D_{i}^{v}(r)=0
, while the right-hand continuity interval gives

D_{i}^{v}(r+)=0
. Hence every right jump

\Delta_{+}D_{i}^{v}(r):=D_{i}^{v}(r+)-D_{i}^{v}(r)

vanishes.

Fix
i,j
, and let
s
be a jump time of
N_{j}
. By the same
path-regularity properties used in the identifiability argument, the only
right jump of
D_{i}^{v}
at
s
caused by this source event is its entry into
the
j
-convolution. Therefore

v_{\alpha_{ij}}k_{ij}(0+;\beta_{ij})+\alpha_{ij}v_{\beta_{ij}}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(0+;\beta_{ij})=0.

At
s+A
, the only right jump caused by the same source event is its exit
from the
j
-convolution. The exit discontinuity is determined by the
left limit of the convolution contribution as the lag approaches
A
, not by
the chosen pointwise value
k(A;\beta)
. This right jump is the negative of

v_{\alpha_{ij}}k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij})+\alpha_{ij}v_{\beta_{ij}}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij}),

and it must vanish. Hence

v_{\alpha_{ij}}k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij})+\alpha_{ij}v_{\beta_{ij}}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij})=0.

The two equations may be written as

\begin{pmatrix}k_{ij}(0+;\beta_{ij})&\alpha_{ij}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(0+;\beta_{ij})\\
k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij})&\alpha_{ij}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_{\alpha_{ij}}\\
v_{\beta_{ij}}\end{pmatrix}=0.

It remains only to check that this
2\times 2
matrix is nonsingular. Since

k_{ij}(0+;\beta)=c_{ij}(\beta),\qquad k_{ij}(A-;\beta)=c_{ij}(\beta)e^{-\beta A},

and

\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(0+;\beta)=c^{\prime}_{ij}(\beta),\qquad\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(A-;\beta)=\{c^{\prime}_{ij}(\beta)-Ac_{ij}(\beta)\}e^{-\beta A},

its determinant is

\alpha_{ij}\left[k_{ij}(0+;\beta_{ij})\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij})-k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij})\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(0+;\beta_{ij})\right]=-\alpha_{ij}Ac_{ij}(\beta_{ij})^{2}e^{-\beta_{ij}A}.

This is nonzero because the active amplitude
\alpha_{ij}
is bounded away from zero,
A>0
, and

c_{ij}(\beta_{ij})>0
. Hence

v_{\alpha_{ij}}=v_{\beta_{ij}}=0.

Since
i,j
were arbitrary, all amplitude and decay coordinates of
v

vanish. The identity
D_{i}^{v}(t)=0
then reduces to

D_{i}^{v}(t)=v_{\mu_{i}}=0

for every
i
. Therefore
v=0
, proving that
I(\theta)
is positive
definite.

Finally,
\theta\mapsto I(\theta)
is continuous on compact interior
parameter sets by Proposition
S1.7
, applied to
the local-window integrand in (
2.13
), together with the derivative
envelope Lemma
S2.1
. Since

(\theta,u)\mapsto u^{\top}I(\theta)u

is continuous and strictly positive on the compact set

\Theta_{0}\times S^{p-1}
, it has a positive minimum there. Equivalently,

\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\lambda_{\min}\{I(\theta)\}>0.

∎

Proposition S6.2
(Verification for the positive-active smooth-ReLU truncated-exponential example).

The model in Example
3.2
, formulated on compact
positive active-amplitude coordinate sets satisfying the displayed spectral-radius
condition, satisfies Assumptions
2.3(a)
–
2.3(c)
.
Moreover, it is identifiable in the sense of Assumption
2.3(d)
,
and its Fisher information matrix is positive definite at every active
interior parameter value. Consequently, for every compact

\Theta_{0}\Subset\Theta^{\circ}
contained in this active model,

\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\lambda_{\min}\{I(\theta)\}>0.

For active edges, write

d_{ij}(\beta)=\frac{\beta}{1-e^{-\beta A}},\qquad k_{ij}(u;\beta)=d_{ij}(\beta)e^{-\beta u}\mathbf{1}\{0\leq u\leq A\}.

On every compact interval
B_{ij}\Subset(0,\infty)
, the normalising map

\beta\mapsto d_{ij}(\beta)
is
C^{\infty}
, strictly positive, and has
uniformly bounded derivatives of all orders. Hence

h_{\theta,ij}(u)=\gamma_{ij}k_{ij}(u;\beta_{ij})

is compactly supported, nonnegative, strictly positive on
[0,A]
for active
edges, and has uniformly bounded parameter derivatives up to order four on
the active compact parameter set. Inactive edges are fixed at

h_{\theta,ij}\equiv 0
and are not part of the active coordinate system.

The link

\Phi_{i}(x)=\epsilon_{i}+\frac{a_{i}}{b_{i}}\log\{1+\exp(b_{i}(x-c_{i}))\}

is
C^{\infty}
, positive, non-affine, and globally Lipschitz. Its first
derivative is

\Phi_{i}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{a_{i}}{1+\exp\{-b_{i}(x-c_{i})\}},

so

0<\frac{a_{i}}{1+\exp\{-b_{i}(\underline{\nu}_{i}-c_{i})\}}\leq\Phi_{i}^{\prime}(x)\leq a_{i},\qquad x\geq\underline{\nu}_{i}.

All higher derivatives of
\Phi_{i}
are bounded globally. Since

\nu_{i}\in[\underline{\nu}_{i},\bar{\nu}_{i}]\Subset(0,\infty)
, the baseline link
values
\Phi_{i}(\nu_{i})
are uniformly bounded above and below. Thus the
positivity, smoothness, and link-regularity requirements in
Assumption
2.3(a)
hold, with
\phi_{\theta,i}=\Phi_{i}
.

Let

Y_{i}(t;\theta)=\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{[t-A,t)}h_{\theta,ij}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s).

Because
Y_{i}(t;\theta)\geq 0
and
\Phi_{i}
is
a_{i}
-Lipschitz,

\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)=\Phi_{i}\{\nu_{i}+Y_{i}(t;\theta)\}\leq\Phi_{i}(\nu_{i})+a_{i}Y_{i}(t;\theta).

Thus the nonlinear intensity is dominated by the linear Hawkes
intensity with baseline
\eta_{\theta,i}=\Phi_{i}(\nu_{i})
and excitation
kernels
a_{i}h_{\theta,ij}
. Since the truncated-exponential filters are
normalized,

\int_{0}^{A}h_{\theta,ij}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=\gamma_{ij}\mathbf{1}\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}\}.

The corresponding Lipschitz stability matrix is therefore

G(\theta)_{ij}=a_{i}\gamma_{ij}\mathbf{1}\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}\}.

The assumed uniform bound

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\rho\{G(\theta)\}\leq 1-\varepsilon

gives the required nonlinear Lipschitz stability, stationary Poisson
embedding, and fixed-window exponential moment bounds.

The compact-window derivative structure is explicit. For the latent inputs, with
X_{i}(t;\theta)=\nu_{i}+Y_{i}(t;\theta)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu_{r}}X_{i}(t;\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}\{r=i\},

\displaystyle\partial_{\gamma_{ij}}X_{i}(t;\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{[t-A,t)}k_{ij}(t-s;\beta_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s),\qquad(i,j)\in\mathcal{E},

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta_{ij}}X_{i}(t;\theta)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{ij}\int_{[t-A,t)}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(t-s;\beta_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s),\qquad(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}.

Higher parameter derivatives are finite sums of the same form with

\partial_{\beta}^{r}k_{ij}
,
r\leq 4
. Since these kernel derivatives are
uniformly bounded on
B_{ij}\times[0,A]
, all derivatives of
X_{i}
up to
order four are bounded by a constant times
1+\mathfrak{C}_{t}
. Combining this with
the bounded derivatives of
\Phi_{i}
and the chain rule shows that the
intensity, score, Hessian, and higher derivatives are compact-window
functionals with polynomial local-count envelopes. This verifies the
regularity requirements used by the likelihood and estimating-equation
arguments.

It remains to prove identifiability and positive definiteness of Fisher
information. First suppose that, under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
,

\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)=\lambda_{i}(t;\theta^{\prime})\quad\text{for all }i\text{ and Lebesgue-a.e. }t,\quad\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\text{-a.s.}

Because each
\Phi_{i}
is known and strictly increasing, this implies

X_{i}(t;\theta)=X_{i}(t;\theta^{\prime})\quad\text{for all }i\text{ and Lebesgue-a.e. }t,\quad\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\text{-a.s.}

Work on the full-probability path-regularity event from
Lemma
S2.2
: there are no simultaneous jumps, no jumps
at deterministic times, and no two event times are separated by exactly

A
. On this event, finite linear combinations of the truncated-exponential
convolutions are continuously differentiable between event times and event
times shifted by
A
. Hence, if such a combination is zero for
Lebesgue-a.e. time, then its one-sided entry and exit jumps must vanish.

Fix an active edge
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
. Since component
j
has
intensity bounded below by a positive constant,
N_{j}
has infinitely many jumps almost
surely on the real line and let
s
be such a jump time of
N_{j}
. At time
s
,
the event enters the predictable memory window. No other event enters or
exits at the same time on the regularity event. Therefore, the right jump of

X_{i}(\cdot;\theta)-X_{i}(\cdot;\theta^{\prime})
at
s
gives

\gamma_{ij}d_{ij}(\beta_{ij})-\gamma^{\prime}_{ij}d_{ij}(\beta^{\prime}_{ij})=0.

At time
s+A
, the same event exits the memory window. Again, no other
event enters or exits at that time. Hence,

\gamma_{ij}d_{ij}(\beta_{ij})e^{-\beta_{ij}A}-\gamma^{\prime}_{ij}d_{ij}(\beta^{\prime}_{ij})e^{-\beta^{\prime}_{ij}A}=0.

The first display has a strictly positive common value. Dividing the second
display by the first gives

e^{-\beta_{ij}A}=e^{-\beta^{\prime}_{ij}A},

and hence
\beta_{ij}=\beta^{\prime}_{ij}
. Substitution into the first display
then gives
\gamma_{ij}=\gamma^{\prime}_{ij}
. Since the active edge was arbitrary,
all active excitation parameters agree. The equality

X_{i}(t;\theta)=X_{i}(t;\theta^{\prime})
then reduces to

\nu_{i}=\nu_{i}^{\prime},\qquad i=1,\ldots,D.

Thus
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
, proving identifiability on the active coordinate
space.

We now prove positive definiteness of Fisher information. Fix an active
interior parameter
\theta
, and suppose
v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
satisfies

v^{\top}I(\theta)v=0.

For each component
i
, define the directional derivative of the latent
input by

\dot{X}_{i}^{v}(t)=v_{\nu_{i}}+\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}v_{\gamma_{ij}}\int_{[t-A,t)}k_{ij}(t-s;\beta_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s)+\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\gamma_{ij}v_{\beta_{ij}}\int_{[t-A,t)}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(t-s;\beta_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}N_{j}(s).

Then

v^{\top}\nabla_{\theta}\lambda_{i}(t;\theta)=\Phi_{i}^{\prime}\{X_{i}(t;\theta)\}\dot{X}_{i}^{v}(t).

Therefore,

0=v^{\top}I(\theta)v=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{D}\frac{\Phi_{i}^{\prime}\{X_{i}(0;\theta)\}^{2}\{\dot{X}_{i}^{v}(0)\}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}(0;\theta)}\right].

Since
\lambda_{i}(0;\theta)>0
and
\Phi_{i}^{\prime}\{X_{i}(0;\theta)\}
is bounded away from zero on the relevant state space, it follows that

\dot{X}_{i}^{v}(0)=0\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\text{-a.s. for every }i.

By stationarity and Fubini’s theorem,

\dot{X}_{i}^{v}(t)=0\quad\text{for Lebesgue-a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\text{-a.s. for every }i.

On the same path-regularity event used above, this implies that every
one-sided entry and exit jump of
\dot{X}_{i}^{v}
vanishes.

Fix an active edge
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
, and let
s
be a jump time of

N_{j}
. The vanishing entry jump at
s
gives

v_{\gamma_{ij}}k_{ij}(0+;\beta_{ij})+\gamma_{ij}v_{\beta_{ij}}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(0+;\beta_{ij})=0.

The vanishing exit jump at
s+A
gives

v_{\gamma_{ij}}k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij})+\gamma_{ij}v_{\beta_{ij}}\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(A-;\beta_{ij})=0.

Because

k_{ij}(0+;\beta)=d_{ij}(\beta),\qquad k_{ij}(A-;\beta)=d_{ij}(\beta)e^{-\beta A},

and

\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(0+;\beta)=d_{ij}^{\prime}(\beta),\qquad\partial_{\beta}k_{ij}(A-;\beta)=\{d_{ij}^{\prime}(\beta)-Ad_{ij}(\beta)\}e^{-\beta A},

the two equations can be written as

\begin{pmatrix}d_{ij}(\beta_{ij})&\gamma_{ij}d_{ij}^{\prime}(\beta_{ij})\\
d_{ij}(\beta_{ij})e^{-\beta_{ij}A}&\gamma_{ij}\{d_{ij}^{\prime}(\beta_{ij})-Ad_{ij}(\beta_{ij})\}e^{-\beta_{ij}A}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_{\gamma_{ij}}\\
v_{\beta_{ij}}\end{pmatrix}=0.

The determinant of this matrix is

-\gamma_{ij}Ad_{ij}(\beta_{ij})^{2}e^{-\beta_{ij}A},

which is nonzero because
\gamma_{ij}>0
,
A>0
, and

d_{ij}(\beta_{ij})>0
. Hence,
v_{\gamma_{ij}}=v_{\beta_{ij}}=0
.
Since
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
was arbitrary, all active excitation-coordinate
components of
v
vanish. The identity
\dot{X}_{i}^{v}(t)=0
then reduces to

v_{\nu_{i}}=0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,D.

Thus
v=0
. Hence
I(\theta)
is positive definite at every active
interior parameter value.

Finally,
\theta\mapsto I(\theta)
is continuous on compact active interior
parameter sets. Indeed, the Fisher integrand is a compact-window functional
with a polynomial local-count envelope, because the kernels and the
smooth-rectifier derivatives have the bounds verified above. Proposition

S1.7
therefore gives continuity. Since

(\theta,u)\mapsto u^{\top}I(\theta)u

is continuous and strictly positive on the compact set

\Theta_{0}\times S^{p-1}
, it has a positive minimum there. Equivalently,

\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\lambda_{\min}\{I(\theta)\}>0.

∎

S6.1
A fully identified non-score GMM example

We finally demonstrate a non-score GMM example in this subsection of the appendix.

Proposition S6.3
(One-point age moments identify the univariate truncated-exponential model).

Consider the univariate Hawkes model

\lambda(t;\theta)=\mu+\alpha X_{\beta}(t),\qquad X_{\beta}(t)=\int_{[t-A,t)}k_{\beta}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}N(s),

where

k_{\beta}(u)=c(\beta)e^{-\beta u}\mathbf{1}\{0\leq u\leq A\},\qquad\theta=(\mu,\alpha,\beta),

where
c\in C^{4}([\underline{\beta},\bar{\beta}])
is strictly positive.
Let
\Theta_{0}
be a compact interior parameter set with

\mu>0
,
\alpha>0
,
\beta\in[\underline{\beta},\bar{\beta}]\Subset(0,\infty)
,
and uniform subcriticality. Let

\mathfrak{C}_{t}=N([t-A,t)),\qquad U_{t}=\int_{[t-A,t)}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}N(s),

and define

H(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}\{\mathfrak{C}_{t}=0\}\\
\mathbf{1}\{\mathfrak{C}_{t}=1\}\\
U_{t}\mathbf{1}\{\mathfrak{C}_{t}=1\}\end{pmatrix}.

Then
H
is an admissible compact-window non-score weight. Moreover, for
every
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{0}
, the population drift

g_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star})=\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\star}}\left[H(0)\{\lambda(0;\theta^{\star})-\lambda(0;\theta)\}\right]

has the unique zero
\theta=\theta^{\star}
. The uniform separation and local
rank conditions in Assumption
2.3(f)
hold.
Finally,

\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\lambda_{\min}\{\Omega_{H}(\theta)\}>0.

The weight is predictable, shift-covariant, bounded by
1+A
, and depends
only on
N|_{[t-A,t)}
. Since it is independent of
\theta
, the
smoothness and envelope requirements in Assumption
2.3(e)
are
immediate.

Fix
\theta^{\star}=(\mu^{\star},\alpha^{\star},\beta^{\star})
. Write

p_{0}=\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}(\mathfrak{C}_{0}=0),\qquad\nu(B)=\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\star}}(\mathfrak{C}_{0}=1,\ U_{0}\in B),\qquad B\subset(0,A).

By the Poisson embedding, positivity of the baseline intensity, and compact
memory,
p_{0}>0
, and
\nu
gives positive mass to every nonempty open
subinterval of
(0,A)
. Indeed, conditional on the history before
-A
,
the probability of no accepted Poisson marks in
[-A,0)
is positive on
large-count truncation events, and those events have positive stationary
probability. Similarly, for any nonempty open age interval
I\subset(0,A)
,
the event that exactly one accepted mark occurs in the corresponding time
interval
-I
and no other accepted mark occurs in
[-A,0)
has positive
probability.

Suppose
g_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star})=0
. On
\{\mathfrak{C}_{0}=0\}
, both convolution
terms vanish, so the first coordinate gives

0=p_{0}(\mu^{\star}-\mu),

and hence
\mu=\mu^{\star}
. On
\{\mathfrak{C}_{0}=1\}
, the unique point has age

U_{0}
, so

\lambda(0;\theta)=\mu+\alpha k_{\beta}(U_{0}).

The second and third coordinates of
g_{H}=0
therefore give

\int_{0}^{A}\{\alpha^{\star}k_{\beta^{\star}}(u)-\alpha k_{\beta}(u)\}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}u)=0,

and

\int_{0}^{A}u\{\alpha^{\star}k_{\beta^{\star}}(u)-\alpha k_{\beta}(u)\}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}u)=0.

Define

L_{r}(\beta)=\int_{0}^{A}u^{r}e^{-\beta u}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}u),\qquad r=0,1.

Since
k_{\beta}(u)=c(\beta)e^{-\beta u}
, the two equations imply

\alpha c(\beta)L_{0}(\beta)=\alpha^{\star}c(\beta^{\star})L_{0}(\beta^{\star}),\qquad\alpha c(\beta)L_{1}(\beta)=\alpha^{\star}c(\beta^{\star})L_{1}(\beta^{\star}).

Dividing yields

m(\beta)=m(\beta^{\star}),\qquad m(\beta)=\frac{L_{1}(\beta)}{L_{0}(\beta)}.

But

m^{\prime}(\beta)=-\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\beta}}(U)<0,\qquad\nu_{\beta}(\mathrm{d}u)=\frac{e^{-\beta u}\nu(\mathrm{d}u)}{L_{0}(\beta)},

because
\nu
is not concentrated at one point. Hence

\beta=\beta^{\star}
, and then the first equation gives

\alpha=\alpha^{\star}
. Thus
g_{H}(\theta,\theta^{\star})=0
implies

\theta=\theta^{\star}
. Continuity of
g_{H}
and compactness give the
uniform separation condition.

It remains to check local rank. Under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
, let

\nu_{\theta}(B)=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(\mathfrak{C}_{0}=1,\ U_{0}\in B),\qquad p_{0}(\theta)=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(\mathfrak{C}_{0}=0).

The derivative of the intensity is

D_{\theta}(0;\theta)=\partial_{\theta}\lambda(0;\theta)=\bigl(1,\ X_{\beta}(0),\ \alpha\,\partial_{\beta}X_{\beta}(0)\bigr).

Thus

A_{H}(\theta)=E_{\theta}\{H(0)D_{\theta}(0;\theta)\}.

The first row is
(p_{0}(\theta),0,0)
. The lower
2\times 2
block in the

(\alpha,\beta)
-columns is

\begin{pmatrix}M_{0}&\alpha\dot{M}_{0}\\
M_{1}&\alpha\dot{M}_{1}\end{pmatrix},

where

M_{r}=\int_{0}^{A}u^{r}k_{\beta}(u)\,\nu_{\theta}(\mathrm{d}u),\qquad\dot{M}_{r}=\int_{0}^{A}u^{r}\partial_{\beta}k_{\beta}(u)\,\nu_{\theta}(\mathrm{d}u).

Since

\partial_{\beta}k_{\beta}(u)=k_{\beta}(u)\left\{\frac{c^{\prime}(\beta)}{c(\beta)}-u\right\},

we have

\dot{M}_{r}=\frac{c^{\prime}(\beta)}{c(\beta)}M_{r}-M_{r+1}.

Therefore

\det A_{H}(\theta)=p_{0}(\theta)\alpha\{M_{1}^{2}-M_{0}M_{2}\}.

The bracketed term is strictly negative by the Cauchy Schwarz inequality, because the
probability measure proportional to
k_{\beta}(u)\nu_{\theta}(\mathrm{d}u)
is not
concentrated at a single point. Hence
A_{H}(\theta)
is nonsingular.
Continuity and compactness give the uniform local rank condition.

Next,
\Omega_{H}(\theta)=E_{\theta}\{H(0)\lambda(0;\theta)H(0)^{\top}\}
is
nonsingular. If
a^{\top}H(0)=0

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
-a.s. on the support weighted by

\lambda(0;\theta)>0
, then
\{\mathfrak{C}_{0}=0\}
gives
a_{1}=0
, while

\{\mathfrak{C}_{0}=1\}
gives
a_{2}+a_{3}U_{0}=0

\nu_{\theta}
-a.e. Since

\nu_{\theta}
is not concentrated at one point,
a_{2}=a_{3}=0
.

To conclude, the map
\theta\mapsto\Omega_{H}(\theta)
is continuous by
Proposition
S1.7
, since

H(0)\lambda(0;\theta)H(0)^{\top}
has a polynomial finite-window envelope.
Since
\Theta_{0}
is compact and
\Omega_{H}(\theta)
is positive definite
for every
\theta\in\Theta_{0}
, compactness gives

\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\lambda_{\min}\{\Omega_{H}(\theta)\}>0.

∎

S7
Simulation implementation details

This appendix records the implementation details omitted from the main simulation section. Paths are generated using the linear Hawkes cluster representation
[
Hawkes and Oakes 1974
]
. Immigrants are generated from time
-B_{T}-A
to
T
, offspring are generated recursively using the truncated-exponential offspring density, and the retained observation is
N|_{[-A,T]}
. In the reported design
B_{T}=450
, so the pre-sample interval
[-A,0)
is available for likelihood and moment evaluation while the effect of truncating clusters born before the burn-in time is negligible relative to the Monte Carlo scale.

All criteria are optimized over the same compact parameter set, with positive baselines, positive excitation amplitudes,
\beta\in[0.25,4.50]
, and
\rho(\alpha)<0.98
. The likelihood is fit by bounded L-BFGS-B. The least-squares contrast (
2.8
), reported as method
J
, and the overidentified GMM criterion are optimized after a smooth transformation of the compact box using BFGS with a Nelder–Mead fallback. The overidentified moment integrals are evaluated by adaptive Gauss–Legendre quadrature, split at event times and at event times plus
A
. The plug-in covariance matrix is symmetrized and row-scaled before inversion, with a relative ridge
10^{-8}
used only if the scaled matrix is numerically singular or too ill-conditioned.

Population Godambe matrices are estimated independently from
128
long simulations with
T_{\rm pop}=250000
and
250000
random time-evaluation points per run. These population matrices are used only for the horizontal target lines in Figure
2
and the oracle diagnostic intervals in Appendix
S8
.

S8
Additional simulation diagnostics

This appendix reports two supplementary diagnostics for the bivariate simulation study in Section
5
. For each method
m\in\{{\rm M},{\rm J},{\rm O}\}
and coordinate
k
, we form the population-target asymptotic normal interval

\hat{\theta}_{m,k}\pm 1.96\sqrt{\frac{(V_{m})_{kk}}{T}},

where
V_{\rm M}=I(\theta_{0})^{-1}
,
V_{\rm J}=A_{\rm LS}(\theta_{0})^{-1}\Omega_{\rm LS}(\theta_{0})A_{\rm LS}(\theta_{0})^{-1}
is the local least-squares sandwich covariance, and
V_{\rm O}
is the optimally weighted overidentified GMM covariance. These intervals use the population covariance targets, not the sample plug-in covariance estimates; their purpose is to diagnose the asymptotic normal approximation and the Godambe width ordering. With
2000
replications, the Monte Carlo standard error of a single-coordinate
0.95
coverage estimate is about
0.005
, and the plotted average over coordinates is correspondingly more stable.

Figure
3
reports empirical marginal coverage and mean CI width for
T\geq 1000
. Coverage is computed as the fraction of replication–coordinate pairs for which the marginal interval contains the true coordinate. The coverage panel shows that all three methods remain close to the nominal
0.95
target. The width panel shows the expected
T^{-1/2}
shrinkage, with the least-squares intervals visibly wider than the MLE intervals, while the overidentified GMM intervals are almost indistinguishable from MLE.

Fig 3:
Asymptotic normal confidence-interval diagnostics. Left: empirical marginal coverage of nominal
95\%
intervals, averaged over coordinates and replications. Right: mean asymptotic CI width.

Figure
4
compares the full covariance matrices through the ordered eigenvalues of

I(\theta_{0})^{1/2}V_{m}I(\theta_{0})^{1/2},\qquad m\in\{{\rm J},{\rm O}\}.

The MLE benchmark is one in every eigendirection. The least-squares sandwich covariance is inflated in several directions, with the largest eigenvalue around
1.55
, whereas the overidentified covariance remains close to one throughout the spectrum. This confirms that the efficiency ordering is not an artifact of marginal standard errors alone.

Together, these diagnostics support the same conclusion as the main simulation figures: all three procedures exhibit the expected asymptotic calibration, while the efficiency ordering is governed by the Godambe covariance. The overidentified moment library nearly recovers the MLE benchmark in this design, whereas the least-squares derivative target retains a non-negligible efficiency loss.
