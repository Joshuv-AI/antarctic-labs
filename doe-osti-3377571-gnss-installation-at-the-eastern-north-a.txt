1.0 Summary
 GRUAN, the Global Climate Observing System (GCOS) Reference Upper Air Network, is an
 international reference observing network of sites measuring essential climate variables above Earth's
 surface, designed to fill an important gap in the current global observing system. GRUAN’s objectives are
 to (1) provide long-term, high-quality climate records, (2) constrain and calibrate data from more spatially
 comprehensive global observing systems (including satellites and current radiosonde networks), and
 (3) fully characterize the properties of the atmospheric column.

 In support of these goals, Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) precipitable water (GNSS-PW)
 measurement has been included as a high-priority measurement of the essential climate variable, water
 vapor. Since 2013, GFZ has hosted a GRUAN central processing facility for the GNSS-PW. GFZ is
 responsible for the installation of GNSS hardware, for raw data transfer, for processing and archiving, for
 derivation of GNSS-PW products according to GRUAN requirements, including PW uncertainty
 estimation, as well as quality checks and archiving of the GNSS-PW products. To provide GNSS-PW for
 the ENA observatory, the GNSS station ENAO was installed in July 2019 (see Figure 1).

 Station ENAO is officially registered under DOMES 31902M005 and is part of the IGS tracking network.
 GFZ provides official coordinate solutions for ENAO annually. The variations in the North component
 correspond with the transient motion signal associated with the São Jorge's Volcano-Tectonic Unrest in
 2022 (Suarez et al. 2025).

 The GNSS-PW data products are compared with PW derived from ERA5, the atmospheric reanalysis of
 the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF). These comparisons are performed
 on a routine basis. Daily updates (sliding window of 30 days) are available through:

 ftp://ftp.gfz-potsdam.de/GNSS/products/nrttrop/MONITORING_IFS/

 2

 As an example, Figure 3 shows the comparison of GNSS-derived PW with ERA-5 PW for 30 days in
 March 2026. The small bias of -0.5 kg/m2 and the standard deviation of 1.6 kg/m2 indicates a high
 accuracy of the GNSS-PW data product.

 Figure 3. Example of PW validation with the atmospheric reanalysis ERA-5 of the ECMWF for March

 GNSS-PW data availability in the considered period.
