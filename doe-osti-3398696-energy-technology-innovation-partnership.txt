Energy Technology Innovation Partnership
 Project (ETIPP) Overview

 The U.S. Department of Energy’s (DOE) Energy Technology
 Innovation Partnership Project (ETIPP) helps coastal, remote, and
 island U.S. communities develop more affordable, reliable, and
  secure energy systems through strategic energy planning and deep-
 dive technical assistance.

Technical Assistance for St.
Vincent’s House

 Supporting St. Vincent’s House

 St. Vincent's House provides essential services that empower Galveston
 residents to become healthy, self-sustaining community members. They are
planning a new facility and want it to be a model — one that stays operational
 when the grid goes down and the community needs it most.

 The goal: a resilience hub that maintains services
 during coastal weather events, reduces long-term
 utility costs, and demonstrates what energy
 resilience looks like for mission-driven organizations
 across the Gulf Coast.

 How You Can Help
This work is only as good as what we learn from you this week.
• How do the City, County, nonprofits, and community organizations coordinate during
  outages and extreme weather events — and where do the gaps show up?
• What is the current state of the Galveston grid, and what is the transmission utility
  planning for future upgrades or modernization?
• What are the policies and pathways around on-site energy generation and microgrid
  interconnection — and what would utilities need to see from a facility like this?
• Which St. Vincent's House services are most critical to the community during a disaster,
  and which populations are most affected when those services go down?
• What other resilience projects or initiatives are already underway in Galveston — through
  the City, UTMB, Port of Galveston, or others — that this facility should connect with or
  build on?
• What would it mean for this community to have a facility that stays operational no matter
  what hits the island?
