Development and validation of a PD-L1 and tumor mutational burden-based predictive model for pathological complete response after neoadjuvant immunotherapy in resectable lung cancer
Authors: Xiao Wu, Duo Jiang, Bin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for > 80% of all cases. A considerable proportion of patients present with locally advanced disease, for whom surgical resection alone is associated with a high risk of recurrence and a 5-year survival rate of less than 50% [1]. Therefore, optimizing perioperative treatment strategies is critical for improving long-term outcomes.

Neoadjuvant therapy is administered before surgery to reduce tumor burden and eradicate micrometastatic disease. Previous studies have demonstrated that neoadjuvant chemotherapy, either alone or in combination, can improve pathological complete response (pCR) rates in patients with locally advanced resectable NSCLC [2–4]. Recently, immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting the programmed death-1 (PD-1)/ programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) pathway have been incorporated into neoadjuvant treatment regimens with promising efficacy. However, the treatment response remains highly heterogeneous. A substantial proportion of patients derive limited or no benefit while being exposed to immune-related adverse events, disease progression, or delays in definitive surgery [5]. Therefore, identifying patients who are most likely to benefit from neoadjuvant immunotherapy is essential for optimizing individualized treatment strategies and minimizing unnecessary treatment exposure.

Predictive modeling has emerged as an important approach to support treatment decision-making in oncology. Nomograms based on multivariable regression models provide user-friendly tools for estimating the probability of clinical outcomes and are widely used because of their interpretability [6]. In contrast, machine learning approaches, such as random forest (RF) algorithms, can capture complex nonlinear relationships and interactions among variables, offering potential advantages for multifactorial predictions [7]. The integration of these complementary approaches may improve the accuracy and robustness of predictive models.

PD-L1 expression and tumor mutational burden (TMB) are among the most extensively studied biomarkers of immunotherapy response in NSCLC. PD-L1 mediates immune evasion by binding to PD-1 receptors on T cells, whereas TMB reflects the number of somatic mutations within the tumor genome and is associated with increased neoantigen generation and tumor immunogenicity [8, 9]. Higher PD-L1 expression and elevated TMB have been associated with improved responses to immunotherapy in lung cancer [10, 11]. Nevertheless, the predictive value of either biomarker alone remains limited, and the integration of both biomarkers may better characterize the tumor immune microenvironment and improve predictive accuracy.

Although several predictive models for immunotherapy response in lung cancer have been reported, most rely on a single biomarker or a limited number of clinical variables. Moreover, few studies have simultaneously incorporated PD-L1 expression and TMB while directly comparing traditional regression-based models with machine learning approaches in the neoadjuvant setting for treating resectable NSCLC. Therefore, there is a need for clinically applicable prediction models that integrate molecular biomarkers with clinical characteristics to support biomarker-informed treatment strategies.

Aim

This study aimed to develop and validate predictive models integrating PD-L1 expression and TMB for estimating pCR following neoadjuvant immunotherapy in patients with resectable lung cancer and to compare the performance of a nomogram and RF model.

Method

Study design and population

This retrospective cohort study included patients with resectable lung cancer who underwent surgical resection following neoadjuvant immunotherapy at the Henan Provincial Chest Hospital. A total of 232 consecutive patients treated between January 2023, and December 2024 were enrolled in the model development cohort. The cohort was randomly assigned to training and internal validation sets in a 7:3 ratio using a random-number table. The training set was used for model development, and the internal validation set was used to evaluate the model performance. A temporally independent validation cohort consisting of 115 consecutive patients who met the same eligibility criteria and were treated between January and December 2025 was used to assess model generalizability. The sample collection process is shown in Supplemental Fig. 1.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathologically confirmed lung cancer; (2) clinically resectable disease (stage II–IIIB according to the 8th edition of the TNM staging system [12]); (3) receipt of neoadjuvant immunotherapy followed by surgical resection; (4) availability of tumor tissue for PD-L1 testing and next-generation sequencing (NGS); and (5) complete clinical and pathological data were available. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) concurrent malignancies; (2) incomplete clinical or follow-up data; (3) incomplete neoadjuvant therapy or treatment modification; (4) autoimmune disease or severe hepatic or renal dysfunction; and (5) prior exposure to immunotherapy or targeted therapies.

Data collection and variable definitions

Clinical data were extracted from the electronic medical records. Baseline variables included age, sex, smoking history (≥ 100 cigarettes/year), histological type (adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, or other), clinical stage (II, IIIA, or IIIB), tumor size, lymph node metastasis status, tumor location, history of pulmonary disease, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, neoadjuvant treatment regimen, number of treatment cycles, and type of chemotherapy agent.

Performance status was assessed using the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status scale [13]. The laboratory parameters included PD-L1 expression, TMB, and carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA). Tumor samples were obtained before the initiation of neoadjuvant therapy. PD-L1 expression was assessed by immunohistochemistry using the SP263 antibody kit (Roche Diagnostics). Two experienced pathologists independently evaluated the staining results in a blinded manner, and discrepancies were resolved by consensus agreement. PD-L1 expression was quantified using the tumor proportion score (TPS) and categorized as negative (TPS < 1%), low (TPS 1% to < 50%), or high (TPS ≥ 50%) [14]. TMB was measured using next-generation sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform with a targeted panel of 422 genes. After excluding germline and synonymous mutations, TMB was calculated as the number of somatic non-synonymous mutations per megabase (mut/Mb). Patients were categorized as having low (< 10 mut/Mb) or high (≥ 10 mut/Mb) TMB [15]. CEA levels were measured using a chemiluminescent immunoassay (UniCel DxI 800; Beckman Coulter), with an upper reference limit of 5.0 ng/mL.

Outcome definition

The primary outcome was pCR, assessed approximately 4 weeks after the completion of neoadjuvant immunotherapy. All surgical specimens were independently evaluated by two senior pathologists according to the criteria of the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer [16], with discrepancies resolved by consensus agreement. pCR was defined as the complete absence of viable tumor cells in both the primary tumor and regional lymph nodes (0% residual tumor cellularity).

Treatment regimen

Neoadjuvant treatment consisted of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor monotherapy or PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor combined with platinum-based chemotherapy. Treatment regimens were selected by a multidisciplinary team according to the tumor histology, clinical stage, and patient condition. For immunotherapy monotherapy, patients received atezolizumab (1200 mg every 3 weeks), durvalumab (1500 mg every 4 weeks), nivolumab (240 mg every 2 weeks or 360 mg every 3 weeks), or pembrolizumab (200 mg every 3 weeks) administered intravenously for 2–4 cycles. For combination therapy, patients with adenocarcinoma received pemetrexed plus cisplatin or carboplatin, whereas those with squamous cell carcinoma received paclitaxel plus platinum-based chemotherapy. Immunotherapy was administered concurrently with chemotherapy for two to four cycles. Detailed information on treatment regimens and treatment cycle distribution is presented in Table 1.

Model development and statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 26.0) and R software (version 4.2.0). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation and were compared using independent-samples t tests. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies (percentages) and were compared using the chi-square test. Only patients with complete clinical, pathological, and molecular data were included in the final analysis. Cases with missing key variables were excluded; therefore, no imputation methods were applied.

Univariate analyses were first performed to identify variables associated with pCR. Variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analyses were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model to identify independent predictors. These independent predictors were used to develop prediction models.

A nomogram was constructed using the rms package in R based on a multivariable logistic regression model. The RF model was developed using the randomForest package. The number of trees was set to 500 (ntree = 500), and the number of variables randomly selected at each split was fixed at five (mtry = 5). Bootstrap resampling with replacement (replace = TRUE) was used to generate training subsets, and five-fold cross-validation was performed during model development to reduce the risk of overfitting the model.

Model performance was evaluated in training, internal validation, and temporally independent validation cohorts. Discrimination was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the curve (AUC). The calibration was evaluated using calibration plots. Classification performance was assessed using accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, recall, precision, and the F1 score. The variable importance in the RF model was assessed using the mean decrease in Gini impurity.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Because this was a retrospective study using consecutively available patients, no formal a priori sample size calculation was performed. To reduce the risk of overfitting, the number of predictors included in the model development was limited, and both internal and temporally independent validations were performed.

Ethics approval

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Henan Provincial Chest Hospital, Approval No. 2025 (12–27). The requirement for informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Results

Baseline characteristics

The model development cohort comprised 232 patients with resectable lung cancer, with a median age of 63 years (range, 40–81 years), including 160 men (68.97%) and 72 women (31.03%). The cohort was randomly assigned to a training set (n = 162) and an internal validation set (n = 70). The temporally independent validation cohort included 115 patients with a median age of 61 years (range, 39–85 years), of whom 68 (59.13%) were men and 47 (40.87%) were female.

Adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma accounted for most cases in both cohorts, with only a small proportion of other histological subtypes present. Most patients received neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy, whereas a minority received immunotherapy. Most patients underwent two or three cycles of neoadjuvant therapy, and the distribution of treatment cycles was comparable between the two cohorts.

Compared with the model development cohort, the temporally independent validation cohort included a higher proportion of patients with lymph node metastasis. No statistically significant differences were observed in the remaining baseline characteristics (all P > 0.05), indicating that the two cohorts were generally comparable (Table 1).

Independent predictors of pathological complete response

Univariate analyses identified clinical stage, lymph node metastasis status, neoadjuvant treatment regimen, PD-L1 expression, TMB, and ECOG performance status as variables significantly associated with pCR following neoadjuvant immunotherapy (all P < 0.05) (Table 2). Subsequently, these variables were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model. Clinical stage II (odds ratio [OR] 0.54, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.31–0.94), absence of lymph node metastasis (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.16–0.77), receipt of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy (OR 4.00, 95% CI 1.54–10.39), higher PD-L1 expression (OR 1.62, 95% CI 1.03–2.54), and higher TMB (OR 3.07, 95% CI 1.42–6.65) were independently associated with an increased likelihood of achieving pCR (all P < 0.05) (Supplemental Table 1).

Model development

A nomogram incorporating clinical stage, lymph node metastasis status, neoadjuvant treatment regimen, PD-L1 expression, and TMB was developed to estimate the probability of achieving pCR (Fig. 1A). RF model was developed using these variables. The variable importance analysis identified PD-L1 expression and TMB as the most influential predictors, followed by the neoadjuvant treatment regimen, lymph node metastasis status, and clinical stage (Fig. 1B and Supplemental Table 2).

Model performance

Discrimination

In the training cohort, the random forest model demonstrated superior discrimination compared with the nomogram (AUC 0.866 vs. 0.782; P < 0.001). Similar findings were observed in the internal validation cohort (AUC 0.769 vs. 0.669; P < 0.001) (Fig. 2).

ROC curve analysis of predictions by the two models. A and C show predictions by the nomogram model in the training and validation cohorts, respectively; B and D show predictions by the random forest model in the training and validation cohorts, respectively. AUC: area under the curve, CI: confidence interval

Calibration

Both models demonstrated good calibration in the training cohort. In the internal validation cohort, the nomogram showed a modest reduction in calibration performance (mean absolute error [MAE] 0.067), whereas the random forest model maintained acceptable calibration with only minor deviations at higher predicted probabilities (Fig. 3).

Calibration curve analysis of predictions by the two models. A and C show predictions by the nomogram model in the training and validation cohorts, respectively; B and D show predictions by the random forest model in the training and validation cohorts, respectively. AUC: area under the curve, CI: confidence interval

Classification performance

The random forest model achieved higher accuracy, specificity, and precision than the nomogram in both the training and internal validation cohorts. Although performance declined in the validation cohort, the random forest model maintained more consistent classification performance, suggesting better generalizability (Supplemental Table 3).

Temporal validation

In the temporally independent validation cohort, the random forest model demonstrated acceptable discrimination, with an AUC of 0.757 (95% CI 0.682–0.902), a sensitivity of 82.6%, and a specificity of 76.2% (Fig. 4A). Calibration analysis yielded a Brier score of 0.139, an intercept of − 0.467, and a slope of 0.183, indicating suboptimal calibration in the temporally independent validation cohort (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

Neoadjuvant immunotherapy has demonstrated promising efficacy in resectable lung cancer; however, the treatment response remains highly heterogeneous, and a substantial proportion of patients derive limited clinical benefits. Therefore, identifying patients who are most likely to benefit from immunotherapy before treatment initiation is essential for optimizing individualized treatment strategies and improving clinical outcomes.

In this study, clinical stage II, absence of lymph node metastasis, receipt of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy, higher PD-L1 expression, and higher TMB were independently associated with an increased likelihood of achieving pCR after neoadjuvant immunotherapy. These findings are consistent with the current understanding of tumor biology and treatment response. Patients with earlier-stage disease generally have a lower tumor burden and a less immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, which may facilitate immune cell infiltration and enhance responsiveness to immunotherapy [17]. Similarly, the absence of lymph node metastasis may reflect a more localized disease and preserved antitumor immune function, whereas nodal involvement is associated with systemic dissemination and immune suppression, potentially reducing treatment efficacy [18].

Our findings are broadly consistent with those of previous studies evaluating biomarkers and predictive models in the neoadjuvant immunotherapy setting for lung cancer. PD-L1 expression and TMB have been widely recognized as important predictors of response to immune checkpoint inhibitors; however, their predictive performance when used individually has been inconsistent across studies [19–21]. Consistent with these observations, both biomarkers were independently associated with pCR in our study, supporting their continued clinical relevance and highlighting the limitations of relying on a single biomarker. More recently, multivariable prediction models integrating clinical, molecular, and immunological characteristics have demonstrated improved predictive performance compared with individual biomarkers, although differences in study populations, variable selection, and modeling approaches have limited their generalizability [22, 23]. By integrating established clinical factors with key immunotherapy-related biomarkers and directly comparing conventional regression and machine learning approaches, our study further supports the value of multidimensional prediction models in the neoadjuvant setting.

The observed benefit of combining immunotherapy and chemotherapy may be explained by the synergistic effects of these treatment modalities. Cytotoxic chemotherapy reduces tumor burden and modulates the tumor microenvironment, thereby enhancing tumor antigen release and immune recognition, whereas immune checkpoint inhibition restores T cell-mediated antitumor activity [24]. Consequently, combined therapy may increase the likelihood of achieving pCR compared with immunotherapy alone.

PD-L1 expression and TMB were also identified as the two most influential variables in the random forest model. PD-L1 expression reflects tumor immune evasion through activation of the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway, whereas TMB serves as a surrogate marker of tumor immunogenicity by reflecting the neoantigen burden [25, 26]. Integrating these complementary biomarkers may provide a more comprehensive assessment of the tumor immune microenvironment and improve the prediction of treatment response. Therefore, our findings support the potential value of combining PD-L1 expression and TMB in biomarker-guided treatment strategies for patients undergoing neoadjuvant immunotherapy [27].

The RF model demonstrated superior discrimination, specificity, and overall classification performance compared with the nomogram, while maintaining acceptable performance in both the internal and temporally independent validation cohorts. In contrast, the nomogram demonstrated higher sensitivity, suggesting that it may be more suitable for identifying potential responders, whereas the random forest model may better reduce false-positive predictions. These findings highlight the complementary strengths of traditional regression-based models and machine learning approaches for clinical predictions.

From a clinical perspective, the proposed model may support biomarker-informed risk stratification by identifying patients with a higher probability of achieving pCR, thereby facilitating individualized neoadjuvant treatment strategies. Such predictive tools may also assist multidisciplinary teams in integrating molecular biomarker information into therapeutic decision-making.

The potential clinical utility of the proposed model warrants consideration. By providing individualized estimates of the probability of achieving pCR, these models may facilitate risk stratification and support personalized treatment planning for patients undergoing neoadjuvant immunotherapy. Patients predicted to have a high probability of achieving pCR may be appropriate candidates for standard immunotherapy-based regimens, whereas those predicted to have a lower probability may benefit from alternative treatment strategies or enrollment in clinical trials. Consequently, these models may help reduce unnecessary exposure to ineffective treatments while supporting more individualized care. However, their clinical utility should be confirmed in prospective studies with larger sample sizes. Future investigations incorporating decision curve analysis and prospective multicenter validation are warranted to evaluate the net clinical benefits and real-world applicability of these models.

This study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective single-center study, and selection bias and unmeasured confounding factors cannot be excluded. Second, although temporal validation was performed, calibration in the temporally independent validation cohort was suboptimal. This may reflect differences in patient characteristics, treatment regimens, and biomarker distributions between cohorts. In particular, treatment heterogeneity, including differences in chemotherapy regimens, immune checkpoint inhibitors, and treatment schedules, may have contributed to variations in model performance. Consequently, although the model demonstrated acceptable discrimination, caution is warranted when interpreting the absolute risk estimates. Future studies should evaluate model updating and recalibration strategies, including the recalibration of the intercept and slope, using larger independent cohorts. Third, the relatively limited sample size may have affected model stability despite internal and temporal validation. Because of the retrospective design, no formal sample size calculations were performed. Fourth, PD-L1 assessment was based on a single assay platform, and TMB measurement methods and cut-off values may differ across institutions, potentially limiting generalizability. Fifth, additional biomarkers, including tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, microsatellite instability status, and driver gene mutations, were not incorporated and may further improve predictive performance. Finally, this study focused on pCR as a short-term endpoint, and long-term outcomes, including disease-free survival and overall survival, were not evaluated. Prospective multicenter studies with longer follow-up periods are required to further validate and refine these prediction models.

Conclusion

Increased PD-L1 expression and tumor mutational burden were independently associated with pathological response to neoadjuvant immunotherapy in patients with resectable lung cancer, and their combined use may improve predictive performance. A prediction model integrating these biomarkers with clinical characteristics demonstrated acceptable discrimination and generalizability, with the random forest model showing superior performance compared to the nomogram. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Further prospective multicenter studies with large-scale external validation are required before clinical application of the model.
