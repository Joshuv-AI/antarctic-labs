Executive Summary
This document is a Record of Decision regarding changes or modifications to the LAMP Conceptual
Design. Specifically, the following changes have been reviewed and are approved:
  Change of the LAMP LEBT design energy from 100 keV to 65 keV; and
  Replacing the Conceptual Design H- chopper and prebuncher for WNR bunch generation with a
 two-chopper system.
This change impacts:
  The design of certain components within the LAMP LEBT, such as beam chopper structures;
  The design of the LAMP ion source high voltage systems; and
  The design of the LAMP radiofrequency quadrupole.

These changes, in total, are assessed as being beneficial to the project for the following reasons: improved
technical maturity, reliability and maintainability, with reduced risk and cost.

1 Introduction
The LAMP Conceptual Design (LCD), as described in the LAMP Conceptual Design Report [1]
incorporates multiple 100-keV low-energy beam transport (LEBT) lines between the ion sources and the
radiofrequency quadrupole (RFQ). The LEBT lines transport H+ and H- beams from their respective ion
sources to the RFQ entrance, and provide the required manipulations (focusing, steering, chopping, etc.) to
the ion beams to prepare them for injection into the RFQ and further acceleration.
An RFQ input energy of 100 keV was initially selected for expected performance benefits in transporting
the ion beams from the sources to the RFQ entrance. Specifically, higher beam energies generally lead to
easier beam propagation, as the impact of space-charge forces reduces as beam energy increases.
General consequences of higher beam energies also include, for otherwise similar systems, higher-strength
magnetic and electric fields for beam focusing, guiding, chopping, bunching, etc. In the specific cases of
the LEBT lines, a higher design energy mandates operating the ion sources at higher voltages, increasing
requirements on high-voltage standoffs and isolation, and increasing stored energy.
The chopper and prebuncher system used in the LCD to form beams for the WNR facility is replaced by a
two-chopper system, with one chopper being placed directly upstream of the RFQ. This change eliminates
the introduction of beam energy spread from the buncher system, providing two notable benefits. First, the
lower energy spread should result in improved beam quality, even for the high charge WNR bunches.
Second, the merge angle between the H+ and H- LEBT lines can be increased, improving component
clearance.

2 Potential Impacts
2.1 Summary of proposed change

The design energy of the LAMP LEBT would be lowered from 100 keV to 65 keV.

2.2 Impact on Key Performance Parameters

The proposed change is not anticipated to impact the ability of the project to meet its Objective Key
Performance Parameters.

2.3 Impact on Accelerator Performance

WNR beam emittance is expected to improve, with notably higher bunch charge and lower emittance
compared to the conceptual design. The lower emittance results from the removal of the low-frequency
buncher.

The LEBT may be more difficult to tune, due to the lower beam energy and increased impact from space-
charge forces.

2.4 Impact on Technology Maturation

The proposed changes will not introduce any new Critical Technology Elements (CTEs) to the Project.

2.5 Impact on Project Risks

The proposed change will directly impact risk #148 – H- ion source. Lowering the source voltage should
reduce the risk of the ion source meeting its reliability requirements, by allowing direct adoption of existing
designs from the Spallation Neutron Source, which operates at 65 kV.

The proposed change will indirectly impact risk #65 – Kicker Requirement. Lowering the LEBT voltage
should relax magnetic field strength requirements on the H- kicker (merger) magnet.

2.6 Impact on Commissioning, Operations and Maintenance

Tuneup of the LEBT may be more difficult. The WNR beam formation requires transport of higher beam
currents compared to the conceptual design, and in general the lower beam energy makes space-charge
forces more significant.

Impact on maintenance is expected to be minimal to none, beyond what would be anticipated for the
conceptual design. The LAMP H- source will be operated at 120 Hz, vs SNS’s 60 Hz. Even though this
change will result in operating the H- source at a greater beam current than the conceptual design, it will
still be lower than the SNS source current. SNS performs routine source maintenance once per run cycle,
primarily replacement of the antenna as a preventive measure. Assuming antenna wear is proportional to
total operating time, running at 120 Hz would suggest one replacement in the middle of a run cycle for
either the LAMP conceptual design or the proposed configuration.

2.7 Impact on Major Accelerator Subsystems

2.7.1 Ion Sources

The ion sources would be operated at 65 kV rather than 100 kV. This results, for otherwise identical
designs, in a reduction by approximately ½ of the capacitively stored energy in the sources. Operating at
65 kV would therefore significantly reduce the energy released in arcdown events, and operating at a lower
voltage is also likely to reduce the rate of arcdowns.

For the H- sources, in particular, reducing the operating voltage to 65 kV matches the operating voltage of
the SNS sources upon which the LAMP source designs are based. This allows direct adaptation of SNS’s
solutions for high voltage isolation, rather than requiring additional development.

2.7.2 Low-Energy Beam Transport

Generally, to achieve the same focusing and steering effects, beam optics elements (e.g. quadrupoles,
steering magnets, solenoids) can be made shorter, can be operated at lower current (e.g. lower fields), or a
combination thereof. Magnetic elements in the LAMP LEBT are not expected to represent significant
design challenges arising from the reduction in beam energy.

Some elements will require redesign due to the change in the beam’s velocity. These include travelling-
wave beam choppers and buncher cavities. Operating voltages of such devices are generally expected to
be lower than they would for higher-voltage beams.

No changes are anticipated to other beamline components such as diagnostics, vacuum pumping systems,
etc.

2.7.3 Radiofrequency Quadrupole

The input energy of the LAMP radiofrequency quadrupole would be reduced from 100 to 65 keV. This
will result in a slightly shorter RFQ physically.

2.7.4 Medium-Energy Beam Transport

No changes are anticipated to the medium-energy beam transport lattice due to these changes. MEBT
chopper flattop durations may need to be extended based on satellite bunch formation around the WNR
pulse.

2.7.5 Drift Tube Linac

The proposed change is anticipated to have no impact on the drift tube linac.

2.7.6 Controls and Diagnostics

No significant impact on accelerator controls and diagnostics is expected; no new beam characterization
techniques are required.

2.7.7 DC and Pulsed Power

No significant impact is expected on DC power systems; required currents for quadrupole and steering
corrector magnets may change, but not enough to warrant a reconsideration of the DC power supply
selection.

Pulsed power requirements will likely change due to the switch to the two-chopper approach for WNR
bunch formation, but initial analysis and testing suggest several feasible approaches.

2.7.8 RF Systems

Removal of the low-frequency buncher removes one complete RF system from LAMP, and associated
systems needed to synchronizing the LFB (at a subharmonic) to the other LANSCE RF systems.

2.7.9 Vacuum Systems

No significant changes to the vacuum system requirements are anticipated.

2.7.10 Cooling Systems

No significant changes to the cooling systems are anticipated.

2.7.11 Protective Systems

The changes will reduce the level of high voltage required for the ion sources, but would not alter the
general hazard category. An additional chopper system is added in the LEBT.

2.7.12 Infrastructure

The reduction in operating voltage may reduce the required standoff distances around the ion sources (e.g.
“source cage” dimensions, making the source region easier to design in general terms.

3 Assessment of the Proposed Changes
The proposed changes have been evaluated by a panel consisting of Greg Dale, John Lewellen, Michael
Holloway, Kip Bishofberger, Anna Alexander, Tyler Thornton, and Henry Gaus, with further discussion
and advice from the contributors listed in the Acknowledgments.

The evaluation was conducted by assessing the potential impact of the proposed changes across a variety
of factors, using the LAMP Conceptual Design as a baseline for comparison. Comparisons are made
between the conceptual design and the proposed change for each factor, and quantified using a 5-level scale
ranging from -2 (notably worse than the LAMP Conceptual Design) to +2 (notably improved from the
LAMP Conceptual Design). Each factor is assigned a relative weight, or importance; the product of the
scores and weights for each factor are then summed to provide an aggregate score assessing the proposed
change. A positive aggregate score indicates the proposed change is favored over the conceptual design; a
negative score indicates the conceptual design is preferred. A list of the factors, along with description and
weights used in this evaluation, is given below in Table 1.

Scores are averaged over all evaluators to provide a final score for the proposed change.
