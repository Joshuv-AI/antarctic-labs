 EXTENDED ABSTRACT

Vacuum-insulated panel (VIP)-integrated retrofit systems have the potential to significantly improve the
thermal performance of existing buildings while minimizing increases in wall thickness. However,
because VIPs are highly vapor resistant, concerns remain regarding their long-term effects on heat and
moisture transport within building envelopes. This study presents the first year of post-retrofit field
monitoring and hygrothermal evaluation of a next-generation VIP-integrated exterior retrofit cladding
system installed on two occupied multiunit residential buildings in Albany, New York. The work builds
upon a 22-month pre-retrofit baseline monitoring campaign and evaluates the thermal resistance, moisture
behavior, and long-term durability of the retrofit system under real operating conditions.

The retrofit panels were installed on two Buildings between September 2024 and January 2025, followed
by sensor commissioning completed in March 2025. The monitoring system replicated the baseline
instrumentation layout to enable direct pre- and post-retrofit comparisons. Duplicate temperature, relative
humidity (RH), and heat flux sensors were installed at multiple wall layers, including the original
sheathing, the VIP-integrated retrofit panel, and the exterior cladding, providing continuous hourly
measurements while ensuring data reliability through sensor redundancy. Post-retrofit performance was
evaluated using measurements collected between April 1, 2025, and March 31, 2026.

To complement the field measurements, hygrothermal performance simulations were conducted to
evaluate the long-term hygrothermal performance of the retrofit system. Two complementary analyses
were performed. The first developed a project-specific model of the Albany wall assembly to investigate
the influence of north- and south-facing orientations on moisture accumulation and drying. The second
evaluated the retrofit concept under representative environmental conditions across four U.S. weather
regions using different wall assemblies, cavity insulation configurations, and vapor control strategies. The
simulations focused on moisture accumulation and mold risk to determine whether the addition of
continuous VIP insulation adversely affects long-term wall durability.

The monitored wall assemblies demonstrated stable thermal and moisture performance throughout the
first year following retrofit. Across all monitored locations, moisture contents generally remained below
commonly accepted durability thresholds for wood-based materials. The interior sheathing layers
consistently exhibited low moisture contents, while the OSB facing exterior cladding (vinyl siding)
experienced greater seasonal variability because of its proximity to exterior environmental exposure.
Although isolated short-duration moisture excursions were observed, particularly during winter wind-
driven rain events, they were followed by rapid drying and did not result in sustained moisture
accumulation. Monthly and seasonal moisture distributions further confirmed that the majority of hourly
observations remained concentrated within acceptable moisture ranges, indicating good hygrothermal
performance of the retrofit system.

Field measurements also demonstrated substantial improvements in the thermal performance of the wall
assemblies. Whole-wall thermal resistance measured in accordance with ASTM C1155 showed
significant increases following installation of the VIP-integrated retrofit panels, with measured post-
retrofit values reaching approximately R-33.0 to R-48.5, representing a major improvement over the
baseline wall assemblies (R-10.2 to R-13.0). These results demonstrate the capability of the retrofit
system to provide high levels of thermal insulation while preserving acceptable moisture performance
under occupied residential operating conditions.

The WUFI simulations, both the Albany-specific analysis and the broader simulations under
representative environmental conditions predicted acceptable moisture performance, limited moisture
accumulation, and low mold risk across the evaluated wall assemblies.

 1. PROJECT OBJECTIVE

The objective of this post-retrofit monitoring phase is to evaluate the thermal and hygrothermal
performance of the newly installed next-generation vacuum-insulated panel (VIP)–integrated retrofit wall
system. Building on the interim post-retrofit study (Iffa and Desjarlais 2025a), this phase assesses
whether the retrofit panels enhance thermal resistance, reduce heat flux (HF) through the building
envelope, and maintain moisture durability in ASHRAE weather Zone 5A.

Specific goals of this post-retrofit assessment include the following:

• Measuring changes in temperature, relative humidity (RH), and HF across key wall layers following
 the retrofit

• Comparing post-retrofit indicators of thermal performance, including interior-to-exterior temperature
 gradients and HF

• Evaluating moisture performance by analyzing moisture content (MC) profiles and estimating MC in
 the oriented strand board (OSB) sheathing immediately adjacent to the VIP and an OSB used as the
 front cover of the VIP retrofit

• Identifying any unexpected hygrothermal behavior that may require long-term monitoring or
 additional moisture management strategies

• Using hygrothermal simulations to predict the long-term moisture durability of the VIP-integrated
 retrofit system

• Evaluating the VIP retrofit panel’s performance in different weather zones and wall configurations on
 moisture accumulation and mold risk using hygrothermal modeling

This report focuses on measurements from April 1, 2025, to March 31, 2026. A comprehensive
performance evaluation was conducted using long-term field monitoring and hygrothermal simulations to
assess the thermal performance, moisture durability, and overall effectiveness of the VIP-integrated
retrofit system.

 2. INSTRUMENTATION AND SENSOR INSTALLATION

The post-retrofit performance evaluation was carried out in two multiunit residential buildings in Albany,
New York. Both buildings were constructed in 1973 and together house 30 apartment units, each with
occupants independently controlling their thermostat set points.

To establish pre- and post-retrofit hygrothermal conditions, identical monitoring systems were installed in
the two buildings, which share similar orientations. The sensors’ layout was designed to capture
temperature, RH, and HF.

As shown in Figure 1, the sensors were installed on the first-floor exterior-facing walls, not on interior
partitions. The sensors were located in units A13 and B13 and the maintenance rooms of Buildings A and
B, positioned at the front and rear sides of the buildings. This configuration enabled consistent data
collection across both buildings and allowed for a comparative assessment of how orientation affects
moisture dynamics and thermal performance within the building envelope.

2.2.3 VIP Retrofit Panel Configuration, Sensor Placement

The VIP retrofit system used in this study incorporated VIPs approximately 1.18–1.2 in. thick, with a
nominal thermal resistance of approximately R-42 h·ft²·°F/Btu. The VIPs were separated by
approximately 1-in. EPS ribs, which also served as locations for mechanical fastening of the retrofit
panels as shown in Figure 2 (d). Prior to the field evaluation, the VIP panel configuration and area
coverage were systematically evaluated in the laboratory through material characterization and guarded
hot box testing (Iffa et al. 2025). Eight configurations with VIP area coverage ranging from 45.2% to
90.8% were tested, demonstrating a strong relationship between VIP coverage and overall assembly
thermal resistance. Temperature, relative-humidity, and heat-flux sensors were installed at representative
locations within the VIP-retrofitted wall assembly to characterize its thermal and moisture performance.
Therefore, the field results presented in this study should be interpreted as representative of the specific
VIP dimensions, panel configuration, and sensor arrangement evaluated, while the preceding laboratory
study provides additional evidence of how changes in VIP coverage and panel configuration can influence
overall thermal performance.

 3. HYGROTHERMAL MODELING OF WALL SYSTEMS WITH VIP-INTEGRATED
 RETROFIT PANELS

Hygrothermal modeling was conducted using WUFI Pro coupled heat and moisture transfer simulations
(Fraunhofer Institute for Building Physics 2020) to evaluate the moisture durability of wall systems
incorporating VIP-integrated retrofit panels. Because the addition of highly vapor-resistant insulation can
alter heat and moisture transport through the wall assembly, the simulations focused on assessing the
long-term moisture response and potential risk of condensation and mold development following retrofit.

Two complementary simulation studies were performed. The first study focused on the Albany, New
York, demonstration project, where the post-retrofit wall assembly was modeled using the actual wall
construction to evaluate the influence of wall orientation (i.e., north and south) on moisture performance.
The second study assessed the hygrothermal performance of VIP-integrated retrofit panels across four
representative US zones using a baseline wall configuration comparison. Together, these simulations
provide evaluations of the moisture durability of VIP retrofitted wall systems both for project-specific
geographic area and wall components.

3.1 ALBANY, NEW YORK–BASED HYGROTHERMAL MODELING OF NORTH- AND
 SOUTH-FACING WALLS

A project-specific WUFI model was developed to evaluate the moisture durability performance of the
VIP-integrated retrofit wall system installed in Albany, New York. The objective was to investigate the
influence of wall orientation on the long-term moisture response of the retrofitted wall assembly under
geographic area and operating conditions.

3.2 HYGROTHERMAL EVALUATION ACROSS MULTIPLE WEATHER ZONES AND
 WALL CONFIGURATIONS

To assess the broader applicability of the VIP-integrated retrofit system, additional WUFI simulations
were conducted for representative outdoor weather across the United States (Table 3). The analysis
included four ASHRAE zones represented by Charleston, South Carolina (Zone 3), Baltimore, Maryland
(Zone 4), Chicago, Illinois (Zone 5), and Minneapolis, Minnesota (Zone 6). Simulations were performed
over a 3-year period using cold-year weather files, whereas indoor boundary conditions were defined in
accordance with ASHRAE Standard 160 (ANSI/ASHRAE Standard 160-2021).

The baseline wall assemblies represented common residential construction and included vinyl siding,
spun-bonded polyolefin, plywood sheathing, a 2 × 4 wood-frame wall, optional cellulose cavity insulation
or an uninsulated air cavity, optional kraft paper–faced vapor retarder, and interior painted gypsum board.
The VIP retrofit panels were installed on the exterior side of the existing sheathing system (i.e., spun-
bonded polyolefin and plywood), maintaining the original wall construction while adding continuous
exterior insulation.

To evaluate the influence of existing wall characteristics on retrofit performance, four baseline wall
configurations were considered by combining two cavity conditions (cellulose-insulated and uninsulated
air cavity) with two vapor control strategies (kraft paper–faced vapor retarder and no vapor barrier).

The primary objective of this study was to determine whether the VIP retrofit panels affect the long-term

mold growth across a range of outdoor environment and wall assemblies.

 4. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

This section presents the thermal and hygrothermal performance of the retrofitted VIP wall assemblies.
The report analyzes the moisture behavior of the retrofitted wall parts at different layers using hourly
averaged RH data from April 1 to March 31, 2026. MC is calculated using interpolation equations derived
from the WUFI material property database of OSB sorption isotherm values. The primary goal is to
understand how OSBs respond to seasonal moisture loading, daily RH variations, and environmental
driving forces. Visualizations, including violin distributions and dual-axis temperature–moisture plots, are
used to interpret trends and evaluate potential hygrothermal risks.

 9

The results are organized by building, with each building discussed in its own subsection. Within each
building, the wall orientations (the maintenance and front sides) are evaluated separately. For both
buildings and orientations, sensor data are analyzed layer by layer to assess performance across the full
wall assembly.

In addition, the thermal performance of the VIP-integrated retrofit panels was evaluated using in situ
measurements in accordance with ASTM C1155 (ASTM 2021). The measured whole-wall R-values were
used to quantify the improvement in thermal resistance achieved by the retrofit under actual field
conditions.

WUFI simulations were performed and are presented in this section to evaluate the long-term
hygrothermal performance of the VIP-integrated retrofit system.

4.1 RH–MC RELATIONSHIP

The RH-to-MC relationship for OSB is nonlinear, particularly near saturation, and the WUFI sorption
curve provided a set of discrete RH–MC data points. Table 4 shows the sorption isotherm values used to
generate the MC value.

These points were used to construct two interpolation regions:

• Region 1: RH = 0–0.895 → (primarily vapor diffusion) gradual moisture uptake
• Region 2: RH = 0.895–1.0 → (dominated by liquid transport) rapid moisture accumulation

4.2 BUILDING A: THERMAL AND HYGROTHERMAL MEASUREMENT RESULTS

4.2.1 Building A—Maintenance Room Side

The MC of Layer 2 (situated behind the existing OSB as in Figure 2(a) and Table 1) remained
consistently low throughout the monitoring period, with values staying below 13%. Similarly, the outer
surface of the existing OSB (Layer 3) maintained moisture levels well within the safe limits, never
exceeding 10%.

In contrast, Layer 4—corresponding to the OSB within the VIP located behind the vinyl siding—
displayed greater variability over time. During the whole monitoring period, Layer 4 remained below
18% except for one incident in December 2025. The lowest moisture levels were observed in the summer
when higher OSB temperatures likely enhanced drying toward the exterior. Overall, despite these
occasional peaks, the layer performed satisfactorily.

4.4 MODELING RESULTS OF THE RETROFITTED WALL

The results presented in this section evaluate both the thermal and hygrothermal performance of the
VIP-integrated retrofit panels using field measurements and numerical simulations. Field monitoring
provides insight into the in-service thermal performance of the retrofit system, whereas WUFI simulations
complement the measurements by assessing long-term moisture behavior under conditions that cannot be
directly observed in the field.

The hygrothermal analyses are presented at two levels. First, a project-specific simulation of the Albany
demonstration building examines the influence of wall orientation on moisture response after retrofit.
Second, a broader parametric study investigates the moisture durability of VIP-integrated retrofit panels
across multiple outdoor environmental locations and baseline wall configurations. Together, these results
demonstrate the effectiveness of the retrofit system in improving thermal performance while maintaining
acceptable moisture durability under a range of climatic and construction conditions.

4.4.1 Site-Specific Modeling Results

The retrofitted wall assembly includes a moisture control strategy with a weather-resistive barrier placed
in front of the VIP OSB and kraft paper applied to the exterior side of the gypsum board and vapor-
impermeable vinyl siding. Simulation results indicate that the MC and RH of the OSB on the VIP exterior
side are lower on south-facing walls. Despite most of the wind-driven rain affecting the south orientation,
higher solar radiation accelerates drying, thus reducing moisture accumulation.

The VIP OSB MC for both north- and south-facing walls remains predominantly below 12% and 9%,
respectively, throughout the simulation period, as shown in Figure 16(a–b). The gypsum board shows
similar RH and MC values for both orientations, reflecting the uniform indoor conditions used in the
simulations. The pre-retrofit existing plywood maintains stable MC and RH across both orientations, with
MC remaining below 4% once initially built-in moisture dissipates (Figure 16(a–b)).

The mold growth sensitivity class is determined using VTT’s Mold Growth Model (Ojanen et al. 2010).
MI calculations for all VIP OSB, existing plywood, and gypsum board surfaces remain negligible
throughout the simulation period, indicating a low risk of microbial growth under the current retrofit
design and indoor control conditions.

4.5 POST-RETROFIT THERMAL RESISTANCE (R-VALUE)

The post-retrofit thermal resistance (R-value) measurements show a substantial improvement in thermal
performance for Building A. Both the main and front wall orientations of Building A demonstrate
significant increases in R-value after the retrofit. The maintenance side wall of Building A improved from
R-12.5 to R-45.3, and the Building A front wall improved from R-13.0 to R-48.5. These results indicate
that the retrofit produced a strong enhancement in thermal resistance across both orientations.

For Building B, the results are less complete. The Building B main wall did not yield a valid post-retrofit
R-value measurement, preventing a direct comparison. This missing value is due to fire hazards for a
potential cause of sensor failure; in contrast, the Building B front wall shows a measurable improvement,
increasing from R-10.2 to R-33.0, which is below the other retrofit wall systems. This post-retrofit R-
value is modest and may be due to an air path behind the VIP. Figure 18 presents a comparison of pre-
and post-retrofit R-values for each building orientation, illustrating substantial improvement in thermal

 5. CONCLUSION

The first year of post-retrofit monitoring demonstrates that the vacuum-insulated panel (VIP)-integrated
retrofit system provides substantial improvements in the thermal performance of the building envelope
while maintaining good hygrothermal performance. Field measurements from the four monitored wall
assemblies indicate that moisture conditions remained well within acceptable limits in Layers 2 (behind
the existing OSB) and Layer 3 (the outer surface of the existing OSB) throughout the monitoring period.
Although Layer 4 ( corresponding to the OSB facing the retrofit panel located behind the exterior
cladding), experienced occasional short-duration moisture excursions, these events were infrequent,
primarily associated with seasonal exterior wetting conditions such as wind-driven rain, and were

 33

consistently followed by rapid drying. No evidence of sustained moisture accumulation or long-term
durability concerns was observed during the monitoring period.

Thermally, the retrofit achieved a significant improvement in whole-wall thermal resistance. Using the
ASTM C1155-95 (2021) in-situ measurement procedure, the retrofitted walls achieved R-values as high
as R-48.5, confirming the high insulating capability of the VIP-integrated wall system under actual
operating conditions. Although one wall (Building B—Front side) exhibited a lower measured R-value of
approximately R-33 than anticipated. This is potentially due to air movement through a gap behind the
VIP. However, its thermal performance still represented a substantial improvement over the pre-retrofit
condition. Because of the exceptionally high thermal resistance of VIPs, accurately determining in-situ R-
values remains challenging, emphasizing the importance of careful measurement procedures and
continued field evaluation.

The accompanying WUFI hygrothermal simulations further support the field observations. Both the
project-specific Albany simulations and the broader evaluation under representative environmental
conditions demonstrated that the VIP-integrated retrofit system maintains acceptable moisture
performance while minimizing the potential for mold growth across different wall configurations and
exposure conditions. The close agreement between the field measurements and simulation results
provides increased confidence in the long-term durability and moisture resilience of the retrofit system.
Overall, the findings indicate that the VIP-integrated retrofit panels offer a highly effective solution for
deep energy retrofits by substantially improving thermal resistance without compromising moisture
durability. Continued monitoring over additional annual cycles will further validate the long-term thermal
and hygrothermal performance of the system, confirm the repeatability of the isolated moisture events,
and provide additional insight into the lower-performing wall assembly, thereby supporting broader
implementation of this technology in future high-performance building retrofit applications.
