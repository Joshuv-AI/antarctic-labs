Spectral Dynamics in Deep Networks: Feature Learning, Outlier Escape, and Learning Rate Transfer
Clarissa Lauditi, Cengiz Pehlevan, Blake Bordelon

Abstract
We study the evolution of hidden-weight spectra in wide neural networks trained by (stochastic) gradient descent. We develop a two-level dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) that jointly tracks bulk and outlier spectral dynamics for spiked ensembles whose spike directions remain statistically dependent on the random bulk. We apply this framework to two settings: (1) infinite-width nonlinear networks in mean-field/ μ \mu P scaling and (2) deep linear networks in the proportional high-dimensional limit, where width, input dimension, and sample size diverge with fixed ratios. Our theory predicts how outliers evolve with training time, width, output scale, and initialization variance. In deep linear networks, μ \mu P yields width-consistent outlier dynamics and hyperparameter transfer, including width-stable growth of the leading NTK mode toward the edge of stability (EoS). In contrast, NTK parameterization exhibits strongly width-dependent outlier dynamics, despite converging to a stable large-width limit. We show that this bulk+outlier picture is descriptive of simple tasks with small output channels, but that tasks involving large numbers of outputs (ImageNet classification or GPT language modeling) are better described by a restructuring of the spectral bulk. We develop a toy model with extensive output channels that recapitulates this phenomenon and show that edge of the spectrum still converges for sufficiently wide networks.

In this work, we develop a theoretical framework for tracking the dynamics of singular values of hidden weight matrices in wide, randomly initialized deep networks. We show that learning induces spike-like directions in the weights, which are in general

Our main contributions are:

•

We develop a two-level DMFT framework for random matrices with finite-rank spikes statistically coupled to the random bulk. This yields a finite-dimensional condition for BBP-style outlier escape in trained weight spectra.

•

For infinite-width nonlinear networks in mean-field/
\mu

•

We characterize width-dependent spectral dynamics in deep linear networks and show that

•

For tasks with extensive output dimension, including compute-optimal language-model pretraining and large-class vision classification, training can restructure the entire spectral bulk rather than produce only finitely many isolated outliers. 6
Discussion

We developed a method to compute the dynamics of spectral outliers for spiked matrix ensembles where the spikes have a causal dependence on the random component of the matrix. This method enabled us to characterize the outlier dynamics of weights in (1) super wide nonlinear networks and (2) proportional width linear networks. We showed that this theory describes some regimes of network training, such as CNNs on CIFAR-10 but fails to capture others such as language model pretraining, for which we propose a simple toy model to account for an extensive number of classes. Several recent works have proposed alternative solvable models of extensive rank adaptations in fully trained quadratic networks
51
;
33
and student-teacher attention networks
9
. Further, similar DMFT treatment in these solvable models has been fruitfully applied
55
. Our two-level DMFT framework could potentially be useful to examine the evolution of the spectrum and outliers in these and related extensive rank models.

Limitations

Currently the theory only applies to either (1) nonlinear networks in the super-wide regime where the constant bulk + spikes always holds or (2) a proportionally wide regime for linear networks where the outliers are also finite when learning a finite set of output channels. Further, the outlier computation scales as
\mathcal{O}(S^{3})
in the number of spike directions since the zero determinant condition. Lastly, our analysis of the extensive class linear network is currently restricted to a single step of GD. More detailed analysis of the dynamics of the spectrum is left for future work.

Appendix A
Classical BBP Transition: Wigner + Rank-One Spike

Figure 9
:
Classical BBP transition in the rank-one spiked Wigner model. The outlier location at
\lambda=\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{0}^{-1}
detaches from the Wigner bulk edge
\lambda=2
for
\gamma_{0}>1
.

As a warm-up, we consider the standard rank-one spiked Wigner model

\bm{M}=\bm{M}_{0}+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{D}\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\top},\qquad\|\bm{v}\|^{2}=D

(17)

where
\bm{M}_{0}
is a GOE matrix. The goal is to recover the classical BBP outlier condition using the same response-function method that we later apply to training-induced spikes. This example illustrates the main idea in the simplest setting: the normalized trace response gives the random-matrix bulk, while the projection of the linear probe onto the spike direction detects isolated eigenvalues.

We introduce an auxiliary spectral-time dynamics

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\bm{h}(\tau)

\displaystyle=-\bm{M}\bm{h}(\tau)

\displaystyle=-\bm{M}_{0}\bm{h}(\tau)-\gamma_{0}C_{vh}(\tau)\bm{v}

(18)

where

C_{vh}(\tau)=\frac{1}{D}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{h}(\tau).

(19)

The trace response of this linear system is

R_{h}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{h}(\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{h}}(\tau^{\prime})

(20)

and its Fourier transform gives the resolvent of the Wigner bulk.

Averaging over the GOE disorder gives the usual single-site DMFT equation

\partial_{\tau}h(\tau)=u_{h}(\tau)+\int d\tau^{\prime}\,R_{h}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})h(\tau^{\prime})-\gamma_{0}C_{vh}(\tau)v

(21)

with Gaussian noise

u_{h}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,C_{h}).

(22)

In Fourier space

h(\omega)=\left[i\omega-R_{h}(\omega)\right]^{-1}\left(u_{h}(\omega)-\gamma_{0}C_{vh}(\omega)v\right).

(23)

The bulk response is therefore the GOE resolvent

R_{h}(\omega)=\frac{i\omega-\sqrt{(i\omega)^{2}-4}}{2}

(24)

which captures the continuous Wigner bulk.

To detect outliers, we project the probe dynamics onto the spike direction. The projected correlation
C_{vh}(\omega)
satisfies

C_{vh}(\omega)=-\gamma_{0}\left[i\omega-R_{h}(\omega)\right]^{-1}C_{vh}(\omega).

(25)

Equivalently, allowing for an initial overlap
C_{0}
,

C_{vh}(\omega)=\frac{C_{0}}{i\omega+\gamma_{0}-R_{h}(\omega)}.

(26)

Thus isolated eigenvalues correspond to poles of the projected resolvent, i.e. to zeros of

i\omega+\gamma_{0}-R_{h}(\omega)=0.

(27)

Analytically continuing to the real spectral axis with
i\omega=-\lambda-i0^{+}
, the outlier condition becomes

-\lambda+\gamma_{0}=\frac{-\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-4}}{2}.

(28)

Solving gives the classical spiked-Wigner outlier location

\lambda_{\rm out}=\gamma_{0}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}.

(29)

This solution lies outside the Wigner bulk only when
\gamma_{0}>1
, giving the usual BBP threshold. Thus the toy model demonstrates the two ingredients used throughout the paper: the trace response determines the bulk, while the spike-probe correlation reveals outliers through their poles.

Appendix E
Two-Level DMFT for Spectral Dynamics in Deep Linear Networks

In this section, we extend the proportional-limit DMFT for deep linear networks to compute the spectrum of trained weight matrices. As usual, the first level of the theory describes the training dynamics of the fields generated by gradient descent, while the second level introduces a linear spectral probe whose response gives the resolvent of the trained weight covariance. This two-level construction is still accurate because the directions generated by the gradient updates are both low-rank and statistically coupled to the random initial weights.

E.1
Model definition and high dimensional limit

We consider a depth-
L
linear network with input dimension
D
, hidden width
N
, and scalar output

f(\bm{x})=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{N\gamma_{0}}(\bm{w}^{L})^{\top}\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}\right)\right]\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{0}\right)\bm{x}.

(118)

The first-layer weight matrix is
\bm{W}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}
, the hidden-layer weights are
\bm{W}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
for
\ell=1,\ldots,L-1
, and the readout is
\bm{w}^{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The parameter
\gamma_{0}
controls the feature-learning scale.

The training data are
\mathcal{D}=\{(\bm{x}_{\mu},y_{\mu})\}_{\mu=1}^{P}
, with isotropic random covariates and a linear teacher

y_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{w}_{\star}\cdot\bm{x}_{\mu}.

(119)

We study the proportional high-dimensional asymptotic limit

P,N,D\to\infty,\qquad\alpha=\frac{P}{D},\qquad\nu=\frac{N}{D}

(120)

and given a fixed number of training steps
T=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Our goal is to characterize the spectrum of the trained weight covariances

\bm{M}^{\ell}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}\qquad\forall\ell=1,\ldots,L-1

(121)

and similarly for the first layer

\bm{M}^{0}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{W}^{0}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{0}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}.

(122)

E.2
Training fields and finite-rank weight updates

Let
\Delta_{\mu}(t)=y_{\mu}-f(\bm{x}_{\mu},t)
denote the training error at step
t
. We define the training-set averaged forward field

\bm{h}^{0}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{P}\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}\Delta_{\mu}(t)\bm{x}_{\mu},

(123)

and the hidden forward and backward fields recursively by

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\qquad\ell=0,\ldots,L-1

(124)

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{L}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{w}^{L}(t)

(125)

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t),\qquad\ell=1,\ldots,L-1.

(126)

The gradient updates can be written as finite-rank deformations of the initial random Gaussian weights
W_{ij}^{\ell}(0)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{W}^{0}(0)+\frac{\eta\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{D}}\sum_{s<t}\bm{g}^{1}(s)\bm{h}^{0}(s)^{\top}

(127)

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)+\frac{\eta\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{s<t}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(s)\bm{h}^{\ell}(s)^{\top}\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq L-1

(128)

\displaystyle\bm{w}^{L}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{w}^{L}(0)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\bm{h}^{L}(s).

(129)

which at fixed training time
t
is spanned by the training fields. To isolate the dependence on the initialized weights, we define

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{N\gamma_{0}}\bm{W}^{0}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle\bm{\chi}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{0}(0)\bm{h}^{0}(t)

(130)

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t),

\displaystyle\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq L-1.

(131)

Substituting the finite-rank weight updates into the forward and backward recursions gives

\displaystyle\bm{v}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{w}_{\star}-\bm{\xi}^{0}(t)-\eta\sum_{s<t}C_{g}^{1}(t,s)\bm{h}^{0}(s)

(132)

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}(t)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}C_{h}^{\ell}(t,s)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(s)

(133)

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\xi}^{\ell}(t)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}C_{g}^{\ell+1}(t,s)\bm{h}^{\ell}(s).

(134)

Here

C_{h}^{\ell}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{h}^{\ell}(s),\qquad C_{g}^{\ell}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}(s)

(135)

with the convention that
C_{h}^{0}
is normalized by
D
when
\bm{h}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
.

The first-level DMFT determines the joint law of the fields

\bm{v}(t),\quad\Delta(t),\quad\bm{h}^{\ell}(t),\quad\bm{g}^{\ell}(t),

(136)

through their correlation and response functions. These are the same training-level order parameters used to characterize the loss dynamics in the proportional limit (see
[
16
]
for more details).

E.3
Spectral probe for the trained weight covariance

We now introduce a second-level probe to compute the spectrum of
\bm{M}^{\ell}(t)
. For a fixed layer
\ell
and fixed training time
t
, consider the linear response dynamics

\partial_{\tau}\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)=\bm{M}^{\ell}(t)\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)+\bm{j}(\tau).

(137)

The response of
\bm{\psi}_{0}
to the source
\bm{j}
gives the resolvent of
\bm{M}^{\ell}(t)

\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(\omega)=\frac{1}{d_{\ell}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(i\omega\bm{I}-\bm{M}^{\ell}(t)\right)^{-1}

(138)

where
d_{0}=D
for the first layer and
d_{\ell}=N
for the hidden layers.

The key step is to express the action of
\bm{M}^{\ell}(t)
on the probe using the same initialized weights and training fields that appear in the first-level DMFT.

For the first layer,
\ell=0
, define

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{0}(0)\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)}_{\zeta_{1}(\tau)}+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\bm{g}^{1}(s)C^{h^{0}\psi_{0}}(s,\tau)

(139)

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{\sqrt{D}}{N}\bm{W}^{0}(0)^{\top}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)}_{\zeta_{2}(\tau)}+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\bm{h}^{0}(s)C^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(s,\tau)

(140)

where

C^{h^{0}\psi_{0}}(s,\tau)=\frac{1}{D}\bm{h}^{0}(s)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau),\qquad C^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(s,\tau)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{1}(s)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau).

(141)

By construction

\bm{\psi}_{2}(\tau)=\bm{M}^{0}(t)\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau).

(142)

For hidden layers
1\leq\ell\leq L-1
, define instead

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)}_{\zeta_{1}(\tau)}+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(s)C^{h^{\ell}\psi_{0}}(s,\tau)

(143)

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)}_{\zeta_{2}(\tau)}+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\bm{h}^{\ell}(s)C^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(s,\tau)

(144)

with

C^{h^{\ell}\psi_{0}}(s,\tau)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{h}^{\ell}(s)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau),\qquad C^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(s,\tau)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(s)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau).

(145)

Again

\bm{\psi}_{2}(\tau)=\bm{M}^{\ell}(t)\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau).

(146)

The spectral problem therefore introduces new probe fields
\bm{\psi}_{0},\bm{\psi}_{1},\bm{\psi}_{2}
and new mixed order parameters between the probe and the training fields. These mixed order parameters play the role of projected resolvents onto the finite-dimensional subspace generated by the training dynamics.

E.4
Path-Integral (MGF)

We now aim to characterize the joint distribution of our training fields and spectral probe
\{\bm{v}(t),\bm{\Delta}(t),\bm{h}^{0}(t),\{\bm{h}^{\ell}(t),\bm{g}^{\ell}(t)\}_{\ell=1}^{L},\bm{\psi}_{0}\}
over draws of the quenched disorder
\mathcal{D}\equiv\{\bm{X},\{\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\}_{\ell=0}^{L}\}
represented by data and initialization. To do so, we will start by calculating the moment generating function of these fields with a Martin-Siggia-Rose integral over the trajectories

\begin{split}&Z\left[\bm{j}_{v},\bm{j}_{\Delta},\{\bm{j}_{h^{\ell}},\bm{j}_{\bm{g}^{\ell}}\}_{\ell\in\left[L\right]},\bm{j}_{\psi}\right]\\
&=\left<\exp\left(\sum_{t}(\bm{j}_{v}(t)\cdot\bm{v}(t)+\bm{j}_{\Delta}(t)\cdot\Delta(t)+\sum_{\ell}\bm{j}_{h^{\ell}}(t)\cdot\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)+\sum_{\ell}\bm{j}_{g^{\ell}}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}(t)+\sum_{\tau}\bm{j}_{\psi}(\tau)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)\right)\right>.\end{split}

(147)

As in the previous section, imposing all the fields definitions that appear in (
147
) leads to isolate each disorder contribution to then perform easily the averages.

E.5
Data average

The relevant terms involving
\bm{X}
are

\left\langle\exp\left[-i\operatorname{Tr}\bm{X}^{\top}\sum_{t}\left(\frac{\sqrt{D}}{P}\bm{\Delta}(t)\hat{\bm{h}}^{0}(t)^{\top}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\hat{\bm{\Delta}}(t)\bm{v}(t)^{\top}\right)\right]\right\rangle_{\bm{X}}.

(148)

Averaging over the Gaussian data gives

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}C_{v}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{\bm{\Delta}}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\Delta}}(t^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}C_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{\bm{h}}^{0}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{h}}^{0}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle\times\exp\left[D\sum_{tt^{\prime}}R_{\Delta}(t^{\prime},t)R_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})\right]

(149)

where

\displaystyle C_{v}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}\bm{v}(t)\cdot\bm{v}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle C_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{P}\bm{\Delta}(t)\cdot\bm{\Delta}(t^{\prime})

(150)

\displaystyle R_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{P}\bm{\Delta}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\Delta}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle R_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{v}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{h}}^{0}(t^{\prime}).

(151)

To enforce these definitions, we use Fourier representation of Dirac delta function, for instance

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\equiv\int\frac{dR_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})d\hat{R}_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})}{2\pi}\exp\Bigg(-P\sum_{tt^{\prime}}R_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{R}_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})-i\sum_{tt^{\prime}}\hat{R}_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})\bm{\Delta}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\Delta}}(t^{\prime})\Bigg)

(152)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\equiv\int\frac{dR_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})d\hat{R}_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})}{2\pi}\exp\Bigg(-D\sum_{tt^{\prime}}R_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{R}_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})-i\sum_{tt^{\prime}}\hat{R}_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})\bm{v}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{h}}^{0}(t^{\prime})\Bigg)

(153)

At the saddle point, differentiating with respect to
R_{\Delta}
and
R^{0}_{vu}
gives

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\Delta}(t^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}R_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})

(154)

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=R_{\Delta}(t^{\prime},t).

(155)

These terms encode the memory induced by reusing the same random dataset in full-batch gradient descent.

E.6
First-layer weight average

We now average over the first-layer initialization
\bm{W}^{0}(0)

\begin{split}&\Bigg<\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\sqrt{D}}\text{Tr}\bm{W}^{0}(0)^{\top}\left(\sum_{t}\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{1}(t)\bm{h}^{0}(t)^{\top}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}\sum_{t}\bm{g}^{1}(t)\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{0}(t)^{\top}+\sum_{\tau}\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau)\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)^{\top}+\frac{1}{\nu}\sum_{\tau}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau)^{\top}\right)\right)\Bigg>\\
&=\exp\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}C_{h}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{1}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{1}(t^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2\gamma_{0}^{2}\nu}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}C_{g}^{1}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{0}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{0}(t^{\prime})\Bigg)\\
&\times\exp\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}C^{\psi_{0}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2\nu}\sum_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}C^{\psi_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau^{\prime})\Bigg)\\
&\times\exp\Bigg(+\frac{D}{\gamma_{0}}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}}R_{gu}^{1}(t^{\prime},t)R_{hr}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})-\sum_{t\tau}C^{h^{0}\psi_{0}}(t,\tau)\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{1}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau)\Bigg)\\
&\times\exp\Bigg(+D\sum_{t\tau}R^{\psi_{1}u^{1}}(\tau,t)R^{h_{0}\zeta_{2}}(t,\tau)+\frac{D}{\gamma_{0}}\sum_{t\tau}R^{\psi_{0}r^{0}}(\tau,t)R^{g^{1}\zeta_{1}}(t,\tau)\Bigg)\\
&\times\exp\Bigg(-\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}\sum_{t\tau}C^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(t,\tau)\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{0}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau)+D\sum_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}R^{\psi_{1}\zeta_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})R^{\psi_{0}\zeta_{2}}(\tau^{\prime},\tau)\Bigg).\end{split}

(156)

This Gaussian average over
\bm{W}^{0}(0)
generates covariance terms for each pair of fields, together with mixed training–probe response terms.
Define

\displaystyle C_{h}^{0}(s,s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}\bm{h}^{0}(s)\cdot\bm{h}^{0}(s^{\prime}),

\displaystyle C_{g}^{1}(s,s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{1}(s)\cdot\bm{g}^{1}(s^{\prime}),

(157)

\displaystyle C^{\psi_{0}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau^{\prime}),

\displaystyle C^{\psi_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau^{\prime}),

(158)

\displaystyle C^{h^{0}\psi_{0}}(s,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}\bm{h}^{0}(s)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau),

\displaystyle C^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(s,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{1}(s)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau).

(159)

The response-type order parameters generated by the same average are

\displaystyle R_{hr}^{0}(s,s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{h}^{0}(s)\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{0}(s^{\prime}),

\displaystyle R_{gu}^{1}(s,s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{g}^{1}(s)\cdot\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{1}(s^{\prime}),

(160)

\displaystyle R^{h^{0}\zeta_{2}}(s,\tau)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{h}^{0}(s)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau),

\displaystyle R^{g^{1}\zeta_{1}}(s,\tau)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{g}^{1}(s)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau),

(161)

\displaystyle R^{\psi_{0}r^{0}}(\tau,s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{0}(s),

\displaystyle R^{\psi_{1}u^{1}}(\tau,s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{1}(s),

(162)

\displaystyle R^{\psi_{0}\zeta_{2}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau^{\prime}),

\displaystyle R^{\psi_{1}\zeta_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau^{\prime}).

(163)

E.7
Hidden layers weight average

For
1\leq\ell\leq L-1
the weight matrix
\bm{W}^{\ell}
is square. The Gaussian average is

\displaystyle\Bigg<\exp\Bigg(-\frac{i}{\sqrt{N}}\text{Tr}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\Bigg(\sum_{t}\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}(t)\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)^{\top}+\sum_{t}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t)\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}(t)^{\top}+\sum_{\tau}\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau)\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)^{\top}+\sum_{\tau}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau)^{\top}\Bigg)\Bigg)\Bigg>

(164)

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}C_{h}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}(t^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}C_{g}^{\ell+1}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\Bigg)

(165)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}C^{\psi_{0}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}C^{\psi_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau^{\prime})\Bigg)

(166)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigg(N\sum_{tt^{\prime}}R_{gu}^{\ell+1}(t^{\prime},t)R_{hr}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})-\sum_{t\tau}C^{h^{\ell}\psi_{0}}(t,\tau)\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau)\Bigg)

(167)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigg(N\sum_{t\tau}R^{\psi_{1}u^{\ell+1}}(\tau,t)R^{h^{\ell}\zeta_{2}}(t,\tau)+N\sum_{t\tau}R^{\psi_{0}r^{\ell}}(\tau,t)R^{g^{\ell+1}\zeta_{1}}(t,\tau)\Bigg)

(168)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigg(-\sum_{t\tau}C^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(t,\tau)\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau)+N\sum_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}R^{\psi_{1}\zeta_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})R^{\psi_{0}\zeta_{2}}(\tau^{\prime},\tau)\Bigg)

(169)

which yields the same structure as for the first layer, but without the aspect-ratio
\nu
which comes from having a rectangular first layer
\bm{W}^{0}
. The hidden-layer weight average generates the following
correlation functions

C_{h}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{h}^{\ell}(t^{\prime}),\qquad C_{g}^{\ell+1}(t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t^{\prime}),

C^{\psi_{0}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau^{\prime}),\qquad C^{\psi_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau^{\prime}),

and the mixed training-probe correlations

C^{h^{\ell}\psi_{0}}(t,\tau)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau),\qquad C^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(t,\tau)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau).

The corresponding response functions are defined by contractions with
the MSR conjugate fields. The training response functions are

R_{hr}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime}),\qquad R_{gu}^{\ell+1}(t,t^{\prime})=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}(t^{\prime}).

The mixed probe-training response functions are

R^{\psi_{0}r^{\ell}}(\tau,t)=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}(t),\qquad R^{\psi_{1}u^{\ell+1}}(\tau,t)=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}(t),

R^{h^{\ell}\zeta_{2}}(t,\tau)=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau),\qquad R^{g^{\ell+1}\zeta_{1}}(t,\tau)=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau).

Finally, the pure probe response functions are

R^{\psi_{1}\zeta_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\zeta}}_{1}(\tau^{\prime}),\qquad R^{\psi_{0}\zeta_{2}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{\psi}_{0}(\tau)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}(\tau^{\prime}).

E.8
Last-layer readout average

The readout initialization only appears in

\exp\Big[-i\bm{w}^{L}(0)\cdot\sum_{t}\hat{\bm{g}}^{L}(t)\Big].

(170)

Thus

\left<\exp\left(-i\bm{w}^{L}(0)\cdot\sum_{t}\hat{\bm{g}}^{L}(t)\right)\right>_{\bm{w}^{L}(0)}=\exp\Big[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}\hat{\bm{g}}^{L}(t)\cdot\hat{g}^{L}(t^{\prime})\Big].

(171)

Equivalently, after Hubbard linearization, the readout contributes as a Gaussian process
r^{L}(t)
with covariance

\langle r^{L}(t)r^{L}(t^{\prime})\rangle=1.

(172)

The covariance is one for all pairs of times because the same readout initialization is reused at every training step.

E.9
From the averaged action to single-site processes

All correlations and responses after the averages can be now enforced by delta-function identities. After these insertions, the joint generating functional has the schematic form

Z[\mathcal{J}]=\int\mathrm{d}\mathcal{Q}\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}\,\exp\left[-D\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{Q},\widehat{\mathcal{Q}};\mathcal{J})\right]

(173)

where
\mathcal{Q}
collects all training and probe order parameters.

The key factorization is as follows. Once the disorder has been averaged, distinct data samples
\mu
, input coordinates
k
, and hidden neurons
i
no longer interact directly. They interact only through the macroscopic order parameters
\mathcal{Q}
. Therefore the microscopic path integral factorizes as

\begin{split}Z[\mathcal{J}]=\int\mathrm{d}\mathcal{Q}\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}\,e^{-D\bm{\Phi}(\mathcal{Q},\widehat{\mathcal{Q}})}\prod_{\mu=1}^{P}Z_{\Delta}^{(\mu)}[\mathcal{Q}]\prod_{k=1}^{D}Z_{0}^{(k)}[\mathcal{Q}]\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}\prod_{i=1}^{N}Z_{\ell}^{(i)}[\mathcal{Q}]\times Z_{\rm pr}^{(a)}[\mathcal{Q}].\end{split}

(174)

Here
Z_{\Delta}
is the scalar process for one training sample,
Z_{0}
is the scalar process for one input coordinate, and
Z_{\ell}
is the scalar process for one hidden neuron at layer
\ell
. The factor
Z_{\rm pr}^{(a)}
denotes the same single-site factors augmented by probe variables on the probed layer. At zero source the sites are exchangeable, so

\frac{1}{D}\sum_{k=1}^{D}F_{k}\to\langle F\rangle_{Z_{0}},\qquad\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}\to\langle F\rangle_{Z_{\ell}},\qquad\frac{1}{P}\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}F_{\mu}\to\langle F\rangle_{Z_{\Delta}}.

(175)

At the saddle point the conjugates of the correlation order parameters vanish in the zero-source limit, while the response conjugates produce the retarded memory kernels. The remaining quadratic forms in the Fourier fields are linearized by Hubbard transformations, e.g.

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}\widehat{\Delta}(t)C_{v}(t,t^{\prime})\widehat{\Delta}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle e^{-i\sum_{t}\widehat{\Delta}(t)u_{\Delta}(t)}\right\rangle_{u_{\Delta}\sim GP(0,C_{v})},

(176)

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}\widehat{h}^{0}(t)C_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})\widehat{h}^{0}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle e^{-i\sum_{t}\widehat{h}^{0}(t)u^{0}(t)}\right\rangle_{u^{0}\sim GP(0,\alpha^{-1}C_{\Delta})},

(177)

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2\gamma_{0}^{2}\nu}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}\widehat{\xi}^{0}(t)C_{g}^{1}(t,t^{\prime})\widehat{\xi}^{0}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle e^{-i\sum_{t}\widehat{\xi}^{0}(t)r^{0}(t)}\right\rangle_{r^{0}\sim GP(0,(\gamma_{0}^{2}\nu)^{-1}C_{g}^{1})},

(178)

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}\widehat{\chi}^{\ell}(t)C_{h}^{\ell-1}(t,t^{\prime})\widehat{\chi}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle e^{-i\sum_{t}\widehat{\chi}^{\ell}(t)u^{\ell}(t)}\right\rangle_{u^{\ell}\sim GP(0,C_{h}^{\ell-1})},

(179)

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{tt^{\prime}}\widehat{\xi}^{\ell}(t)C_{g}^{\ell+1}(t,t^{\prime})\widehat{\xi}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle e^{-i\sum_{t}\widehat{\xi}^{\ell}(t)r^{\ell}(t)}\right\rangle_{r^{\ell}\sim GP(0,C_{g}^{\ell+1})}.

(180)

Integrating over the Fourier variables then gives stochastic single-site equations. The response functions can equivalently be read as functional derivatives with respect to the Gaussian noises. For example,

R_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial\Delta(t)}{\partial u_{\Delta}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,\qquad R_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial v(t)}{\partial u^{0}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle.

(181)

E.10
Deterministic limiting order parameters

For fixed
L
and fixed training horizon, the action is proportional to
D
. Hence the macroscopic order parameters concentrate at the saddle point. In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D}\bm{v}(t)\cdot\bm{v}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[D\to\infty]{}C_{v}(t,t^{\prime})=\langle v(t)v(t^{\prime})\rangle,

(182)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{P}\bm{\Delta}(t)\cdot\bm{\Delta}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[D\to\infty]{}C_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})=\langle\Delta(t)\Delta(t^{\prime})\rangle,

(183)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D}\bm{h}^{0}(t)\cdot\bm{h}^{0}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[D\to\infty]{}C_{h}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})=\langle h^{0}(t)h^{0}(t^{\prime})\rangle,

(184)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{h}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[D\to\infty]{}C_{h}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\langle h^{\ell}(t)h^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\rangle,

(185)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[D\to\infty]{}C_{g}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\langle g^{\ell}(t)g^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\rangle.

(186)

Similarly,

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{P}\bm{\Delta}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\Delta}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to R_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial\Delta(t)}{\partial u_{\Delta}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

(187)

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{D}\bm{v}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{h}}^{0}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to R_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial v(t)}{\partial u^{0}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

(188)

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{D}\bm{h}^{0}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\xi}}^{0}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to R_{hr}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial h^{0}(t)}{\partial r^{0}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

(189)

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{N}\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to R_{hr}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial h^{\ell}(t)}{\partial r^{\ell}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

(190)

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to R_{gu}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial g^{\ell}(t)}{\partial u^{\ell}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle.

(191)

The mixed probe order parameters also converge to deterministic single-site averages. For a hidden probed layer
a
,

\displaystyle C^{h^{a}\psi_{0}}(t,\tau)

\displaystyle\to\langle h^{a}(t)\psi_{0}(\tau)\rangle,

\displaystyle C^{g^{a+1}\psi_{1}}(t,\tau)

\displaystyle\to\langle g^{a+1}(t)\psi_{1}(\tau)\rangle,

(192)

\displaystyle R^{\psi_{0}r^{a}}(\tau,t)

\displaystyle\to\left\langle\frac{\delta\psi_{0}(\tau)}{\delta r^{a}(t)}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle R^{\psi_{1}u^{a+1}}(\tau,t)

\displaystyle\to\left\langle\frac{\delta\psi_{1}(\tau)}{\delta u^{a+1}(t)}\right\rangle.

(193)

The first-layer version is identical except for the normalizations by
D,N
and the factors of
\nu,\gamma_{0}
already displayed in the first-layer average.

E.11
Training-level DMFT

The zero-source training DMFT is obtained from the joint saddle by setting the probe sources to zero after the disorder average has been performed. This gives a closed scalar stochastic process for one data point, one input coordinate, and one representative hidden neuron in each layer. The probe does not alter this training process; it only probes the solution obtained from it.

The data sector is

\displaystyle\Delta(t)

\displaystyle=u_{\Delta}(t)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{s<t}R_{vu}^{0}(t,s)\Delta(s)+\sigma\epsilon,

\displaystyle u_{\Delta}

\displaystyle\sim GP(0,C_{v}),\quad\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(194)

\displaystyle h^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=u^{0}(t)+\sum_{s<t}R_{\Delta}(t,s)v(s),

\displaystyle u^{0}

\displaystyle\sim GP(0,\frac{1}{\alpha}C_{\Delta}).

(195)

The first-layer boundary is

\displaystyle h^{1}(t)=u^{1}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\left[\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}R_{hr}^{0}(t,s)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{h}^{0}(t,s)\right]g^{1}(s),\quad u^{1}(t)\sim GP(0,C_{h}^{0}),

(196)

\displaystyle v(t)=w_{\star}-r^{0}(t)-\sum_{s<t}\left[\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}R_{gu}^{1}(t,s)+\eta C_{g}^{1}(t,s)\right]h^{0}(s),\quad r^{0}(t)\sim GP(0,\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}^{2}\nu}C_{g}^{1}).

(197)

For the remaining forward fields,
2\leq\ell\leq L
,

\displaystyle h^{\ell}(t)=u^{\ell}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\left[R_{hr}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{h}^{\ell-1}(t,s)\right]g^{\ell}(s),\quad u^{\ell}\sim GP(0,C_{h}^{\ell-1}).

(198)

For the backward fields below the readout,
1\leq\ell\leq L-1
,

\displaystyle g^{\ell}(t)=r^{\ell}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\left[R_{gu}^{\ell+1}(t,s)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{g}^{\ell+1}(t,s)\right]h^{\ell}(s),\quad r^{\ell}

\displaystyle\sim GP(0,C_{g}^{\ell+1}).

(199)

The readout boundary is

g^{L}(t)=r^{L}+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\left[u^{L}(s)+\sum_{r<s}\left(R_{hr}^{L-1}(s,r)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{h}^{L-1}(s,r)\right)g^{L}(r)\right],\quad\langle r^{L}(t)r^{L}(t^{\prime})\rangle=1.

(200)

The correlation fixed points are

\displaystyle C_{v}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\langle v(t)v(t^{\prime})\rangle,

\displaystyle C_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta(t)\Delta(t^{\prime})\rangle,

(201)

\displaystyle C_{h}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\langle h^{\ell}(t)h^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\rangle,

\displaystyle C_{g}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\langle g^{\ell}(t)g^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\rangle.

(202)

The response fixed points are

\displaystyle R_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial\Delta(t)}{\partial u_{\Delta}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle R_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial v(t)}{\partial u^{0}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

(203)

\displaystyle R_{hr}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial h^{0}(t)}{\partial r^{0}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle R_{hr}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial h^{\ell}(t)}{\partial r^{\ell}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

(204)

\displaystyle R_{gu}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial g^{\ell}(t)}{\partial u^{\ell}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle 1\leq\ell

\displaystyle\leq L.

(205)

Finally,

\mathcal{L}_{\rm test}(t)=C_{v}(t,t)+\sigma^{2},\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm train}(t)=C_{\Delta}(t,t).

(206)

E.12
Probe-level DMFT fixed points

The probe equations are solved after the training-level DMFT has produced
C_{h}^{\ell},C_{g}^{\ell},R_{hr}^{\ell},R_{gu}^{\ell}
. Thus the training process and the spectral probe are separated at the final stage: the training order parameters are inputs to the probe fixed point.

E.12.1
First-layer probe

For
\ell=0
, the effective first-layer probe process is

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=u_{\zeta_{1}}(\tau)+\frac{1}{\nu}\int d\tau^{\prime}\,R^{\psi_{0}\zeta_{2}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\psi_{1}(\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{s<t}\left[\eta\gamma_{0}C^{h^{0}\psi_{0}}(s,\tau)+\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}R^{\psi_{0}r^{0}}(\tau,s)\right]g^{1}(s)

(207)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=u_{\zeta_{2}}(\tau)+\int d\tau^{\prime}\,R^{\psi_{1}\zeta_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\psi_{0}(\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{s<t}\left[\eta\gamma_{0}C^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(s,\tau)+R^{\psi_{1}u^{1}}(\tau,s)\right]h^{0}(s).

(208)

The Gaussian noises have covariances

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{\zeta_{1}}(\tau)u_{\zeta_{1}}(\tau^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=C^{\psi_{0}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{\zeta_{2}}(\tau)u_{\zeta_{2}}(\tau^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu}C^{\psi_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime}),

(209)

and mixed covariances

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{\zeta_{1}}(\tau)u^{1}(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=C^{h^{0}\psi_{0}}(t,\tau),

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{\zeta_{2}}(\tau)r^{0}(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}C^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(t,\tau).

(210)

These equations are the spectral-probe analogue of the first-layer single-site training equations.

To obtain the outlier condition, Fourier transform in the spectral time
\tau
and set

z=i\omega,\qquad\mathcal{H}(z)=\left[z-R^{\psi_{1}\zeta_{1}}(z)\right]^{-1}.

The
\psi_{2}
equation gives

\left[i\omega-R^{\psi_{1}\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\right]\psi_{0}(\omega)=u_{\zeta_{2}}(\omega)+\sum_{s<t}\left[\eta\gamma_{0}C^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(s,\omega)+R^{\psi_{1}u^{1}}(\omega,s)\right]h^{0}(s).

(211)

Projecting onto
h^{0}
once you stack the training time
T
in
T
-dimensional vectors, and differentiating with respect to
r^{0}
gives

\displaystyle\Big(i\omega-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\Big)\bm{C}^{h^{0}\psi_{0}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\langle\zeta_{2}(\omega)\bm{h}^{0}\rangle+\bm{C}_{h}^{0}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(\omega)+\bm{R}^{\psi_{1},u^{1}}(\omega)\Big)

(212)

\displaystyle\Big(i\omega-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\Big)\bm{R}^{\psi_{0}r^{0}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=(\bm{R}_{hr}^{0})^{\top}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(\omega)+\bm{R}^{\psi_{1},u^{1}}(\omega)\Big)

(213)

where we use Stein’s lemma to write

\left\langle u_{\zeta_{2}}(z)\bm{h}^{0}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}\bm{R}^{0}_{hr}\bm{C}^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(z).

(214)

Similarly, the
\psi_{1}
equation gives

\Big(1+\nu^{-1}\mathcal{H}(\omega)\Big)\psi_{1}(\omega)=u_{\zeta_{1}}(\omega)+\sum_{s<t}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}C^{h^{0},\psi_{0}}(s,\tau)+\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}R^{\psi_{0},r^{0}}(\tau,s)\Big)g^{1}(s)

(215)

where

\mathcal{H}(\omega)=-R_{0,2}(\omega)=\Big(i\omega-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\Big)^{-1}.

(216)

Projecting onto
g^{1}
and differentiating with respect to
u^{1}
gives

\displaystyle\Big(1+\nu^{-1}\mathcal{H}(\omega)\Big)\bm{C}^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\langle u_{\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\bm{g}^{1}\rangle+\bm{C}_{g}^{1}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{h^{0},\psi_{0}}(\omega)+\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}\bm{R}^{\psi_{0},r^{0}}(\omega)\Big)

(217)

\displaystyle\Big(1+\nu^{-1}\mathcal{H}(\omega)\Big)\bm{R}^{\psi_{1}u^{1}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=(\bm{R}_{gu}^{1})^{\top}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{h^{0},\psi_{0}}(\omega)+\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}\bm{R}^{\psi_{0},r^{0}}(\omega)\Big).

(218)

Here, again, we used Stein’s lemma to express

\left\langle u_{\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\bm{g}^{1}\right\rangle=\bm{R}^{1}_{gu}\bm{C}^{h^{0}\psi_{0}}(\omega).

(219)

Collecting the four projected quantities and with the analytic continuation from
z=i\omega
to the real spectral axis one gets

\bm{q}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{C}^{h^{0}\psi_{0}}(z)\\
\bm{R}^{\psi_{0}r^{0}}(z)\\
\bm{C}^{g^{1}\psi_{1}}(z)\\
\bm{R}^{\psi_{1}u^{1}}(z)\end{pmatrix}

(220)

from which we obtain the finite-dimensional linear system

\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(z)\bm{q}(z)=0

(221)

where

\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}(z-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(z))\bm{I}&\bm{0}&-\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}\bm{R}_{hr}^{0}-\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}_{h}^{0}&-\bm{C}_{h}^{0}\\
\bm{0}&(z-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(z))\bm{I}&-\eta\gamma_{0}(\bm{R}_{hr}^{0})^{\top}&-(\bm{R}_{hr}^{0})^{\top}\\
-\bm{R}_{gu}^{1}-\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}_{g}^{1}&-\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}\bm{C}_{g}^{1}&(R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(z))^{-1}\bm{I}&\bm{0}\\
-\eta\gamma_{0}(\bm{R}_{gu}^{1})^{\top}&-\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}\nu}(\bm{R}_{gu}^{1})^{\top}&\bm{0}&(R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(z))^{-1}\bm{I}\end{array}\right)

(222)

where we remember

R^{\psi_{1}\zeta_{1}}(z)=\left[1+\nu^{-1}\mathcal{H}(z)\right]^{-1}.

(223)

The first-layer outliers are therefore obtained from the pole condition

\det\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(z)=0.

(224)

E.12.2
Hidden-layer probe

As before, for
\ell=1,\ldots,L-1
the probe process generates

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=u_{\zeta_{1}}(\tau)+\int R^{\psi_{0},\zeta_{2}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\psi_{1}(\tau^{\prime})+\sum_{s<t}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}C^{h^{\ell}\psi_{0}}(s,\tau)+R^{\psi_{0},r^{\ell}}(\tau,s)\Big)g^{\ell+1}(s)

(225)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=u_{\zeta_{2}}(\tau)+\int R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\psi_{0}(\tau^{\prime})+\sum_{s<t}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}C^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(s,\tau)+R^{\psi_{1},u^{\ell+1}}(\tau,s)\Big)h^{\ell}(s)

(226)

with

\displaystyle\langle u_{\zeta_{1}}(\tau)u_{\zeta_{1}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=C^{\psi_{0}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime}),\quad\langle\zeta_{1}(\tau)u^{\ell+1}(t)\rangle=C^{h^{\ell}\psi_{0}}(t,\tau)

(227)

\displaystyle\langle u_{\zeta_{2}}(\tau)u_{\zeta_{2}}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=C^{\psi_{1}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime}),\quad\langle\zeta_{2}(\tau)r^{\ell}(t)\rangle=C^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(t,\tau).

(228)

Thus, the variable of interest has the form

\Big(i\omega-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\Big)\psi_{0}(\omega)=u_{\zeta_{2}}(\omega)+\sum_{s<t}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}C^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(s,\omega)+R^{\psi_{1},u^{\ell+1}}(\omega,s)\Big)h^{\ell}(s)

(229)

from which by projecting onto
h^{\ell}
and differentiating with respect to
r^{\ell}
one gets

\displaystyle\Big(i\omega-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\Big)\bm{C}^{h^{\ell}\psi_{0}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\langle\zeta_{2}(\omega)\bm{h}^{\ell}\rangle+\bm{C}_{h}^{\ell}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(\omega)+\bm{R}^{\psi_{1},u^{\ell+1}}(\omega)\Big)

(230)

\displaystyle\Big(i\omega-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\Big)\bm{R}^{\psi_{0}r^{\ell}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=(\bm{R}_{hr}^{\ell})^{\top}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(\omega)+\bm{R}^{\psi_{1},u^{\ell+1}}(\omega)\Big).

(231)

We can again apply Stein’s Lemma for

\langle\zeta_{2}(\omega)\bm{h}^{\ell}\rangle=\bm{R}_{hr}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(\omega).

(232)

Similarly for
\psi_{1}(\omega)

\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(\omega)\Big)\psi_{1}(\omega)=u_{\zeta_{1}}(\omega)+\sum_{s<t}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}C^{h^{\ell},\psi_{0}}(s,\omega)+R^{\psi_{0},r^{\ell}}(\omega,s)\Big)g^{\ell+1}(s)

(233)

with
\mathcal{H}(\omega)=-R_{0,2}(\omega)=\Big(i\omega-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\Big)^{-1}
. From this,

\displaystyle\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(\omega)\Big)\bm{C}^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\langle u_{\zeta_{1}}(\omega)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\rangle+\bm{C}_{g}^{\ell+1}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{h^{\ell},\psi_{0}}(\omega)+\bm{R}^{\psi_{0},r^{\ell}}(\omega)\Big)

(234)

\displaystyle\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(\omega)\Big)\bm{R}^{\psi_{1}u^{\ell+1}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=(\bm{R}_{gu}^{\ell+1})^{\top}\Big(\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{h^{\ell},\psi_{0}}(\omega)+\bm{R}^{\psi_{0},r^{\ell}}(\omega)\Big).

(235)

By collecting all the four projected quantities as for the first layer and setting
z=i\omega
, then

\bm{q}^{\ell}(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{C}^{h^{\ell}\psi_{0}}(z)\\
\bm{R}^{\psi_{0}r^{\ell}}(z)\\
\bm{C}^{g^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(z)\\
\bm{R}^{\psi_{1}u^{\ell+1}}(z)\end{array}\right)

(236)

by giving the poles condition of

\det\bm{A}^{\ell}(z)=0

(237)

with

\bm{A}^{\ell}(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}(z-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(z))\bm{I}&\bm{0}&-\bm{R}_{hr}^{\ell}-\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}_{h}^{\ell}&-\bm{C}_{h}^{\ell}\\
\bm{0}&(z-R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(z))\bm{I}&-\eta\gamma_{0}(\bm{R}_{hr}^{\ell})^{\top}&-(\bm{R}_{hr}^{\ell})^{\top}\\
-\bm{R}_{gu}^{\ell+1}-\eta\gamma_{0}\bm{C}_{g}^{\ell+1}&-\bm{C}_{g}^{\ell+1}&(R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(z))^{-1}\bm{I}&\bm{0}\\
-\eta\gamma_{0}(\bm{R}_{gu}^{\ell+1})^{\top}&-(\bm{R}_{gu}^{\ell+1})^{\top}&\bm{0}&(R^{\psi_{1},\zeta_{1}}(z))^{-1}\bm{I}\end{array}\right).

(238)

E.13
Multi-Class setting

The extension to multiple output classes is straightforward when the number
of classes
C
remains
\mathcal{O}(1)
as

D,N,P\to\infty,\qquad\alpha=\frac{P}{D},\qquad\nu=\frac{N}{D}.

The important point is that the class index is not averaged away in the
proportional limit. Instead, every training-time order parameter becomes a
block kernel indexed by both time and class. Thus the scalar-output DMFT is
lifted to a class-time DMFT by replacing

t\quad\longrightarrow\quad(c,t),\qquad c\in\{1,\ldots,C\}.

Equivalently, every
T\times T
correlation or response kernel becomes an

(CT)\times(CT)
block kernel.

For each class
c\in\{1,\ldots,C\}
, introduce a teacher vector

\bm{w}_{\star,c}
, a readout vector
\bm{w}_{c}^{L}
, an error vector

\bm{\Delta}_{c}(t)
, and class-resolved training fields

\bm{v}_{c}(t),\qquad\bm{h}_{c}^{\ell}(t),\qquad\bm{g}_{c}^{\ell}(t).

The hidden weights are shared across classes, while the readout vectors are
class-specific. Keeping the same normalization as in the scalar-output case,
the finite-rank weight updates become

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{W}^{0}(0)+\frac{\eta\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{D}}\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\bm{g}_{c}^{1}(s)\bm{h}_{c}^{0}(s)^{\top},

(239)

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)+\frac{\eta\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\bm{g}_{c}^{\ell+1}(s)\bm{h}_{c}^{\ell}(s)^{\top},\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq L-1,

(240)

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{c}^{L}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{w}_{c}^{L}(0)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\bm{h}_{c}^{L}(s).

(241)

The initialized-weight fields are now class-dependent as well

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}_{c}^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{N\gamma_{0}}\bm{W}^{0}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}_{c}^{1}(t),

\displaystyle\bm{\chi}_{c}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{0}(0)\bm{h}_{c}^{0}(t),

(242)

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}_{c}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}_{c}^{\ell+1}(t),

\displaystyle\bm{\chi}_{c}^{\ell+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\bm{h}_{c}^{\ell}(t),\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq L-1.

(243)

Expanding the finite-rank updates gives

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{c}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{w}_{\star,c}-\bm{\xi}_{c}^{0}(t)-\eta\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}C_{g,cc^{\prime}}^{1}(t,s)\bm{h}_{c^{\prime}}^{0}(s),

(244)

\displaystyle\bm{h}_{c}^{\ell+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\chi}_{c}^{\ell+1}(t)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}C_{h,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t,s)\bm{g}_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}(s),

(245)

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{c}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\xi}_{c}^{\ell}(t)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}C_{g,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}(t,s)\bm{h}_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(s).

(246)

The only structural change in the full-batch DMFT is that every correlation
and response function acquires two class indices. The class-resolved
correlations are

\displaystyle C_{v,cc^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}\bm{v}_{c}(t)\cdot\bm{v}_{c^{\prime}}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle C_{\Delta,cc^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{P}\bm{\Delta}_{c}(t)\cdot\bm{\Delta}_{c^{\prime}}(t^{\prime}),

(247)

\displaystyle C_{h,cc^{\prime}}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}\bm{h}_{c}^{0}(t)\cdot\bm{h}_{c^{\prime}}^{0}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle C_{h,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\bm{h}_{c}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{h}_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime}),

(248)

\displaystyle C_{g,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}_{c}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{g}_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime}).

(249)

The corresponding response functions are

\displaystyle R_{\Delta,cc^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{P}\bm{\Delta}_{c}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\Delta}}_{c^{\prime}}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle R_{vu,cc^{\prime}}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{v}_{c}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{h}}_{c^{\prime}}^{0}(t^{\prime}),

(250)

\displaystyle R_{hr,cc^{\prime}}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{h}_{c}^{0}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\xi}}_{c^{\prime}}^{0}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle R_{gu,cc^{\prime}}^{1}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{g}_{c}^{1}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\chi}}_{c^{\prime}}^{1}(t^{\prime}),

(251)

\displaystyle R_{hr,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{h}_{c}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\xi}}_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle R_{gu,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{g}_{c}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\chi}}_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}(t^{\prime}).

(252)

Equivalently, in the single-site description, the scalar Gaussian noises
become
C
-dimensional Gaussian processes over class index. For instance

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{\Delta,c}(t)u_{\Delta,c^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{v,cc^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{c}^{0}(t)u_{c^{\prime}}^{0}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}C_{\Delta,cc^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime}),

(253)

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{c}^{\ell}(t)u_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{h,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell-1}(t,t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\left\langle r_{c}^{\ell}(t)r_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{g,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}(t,t^{\prime}).

(254)

The first-layer backward noise has covariance

\left\langle r_{c}^{0}(t)r_{c^{\prime}}^{0}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\nu\gamma_{0}^{2}}C_{g,cc^{\prime}}^{1}(t,t^{\prime}).

The readout boundary becomes

g_{c}^{L}(t)=r_{c}^{L}+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}h_{c}^{L}(s),\qquad\left\langle r_{c}^{L}r_{c^{\prime}}^{L}\right\rangle=\delta_{cc^{\prime}}.

Thus the training DMFT equations are the scalar-output equations with the
replacement
t\mapsto(c,t)
, together with sums over repeated class
indices. Explicitly

\displaystyle\Delta_{c}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{\Delta,c}(t)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}R_{vu,cc^{\prime}}^{0}(t,s)\Delta_{c^{\prime}}(s)+\sigma\epsilon_{c}(t),

(255)

\displaystyle h_{c}^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{c}^{0}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}R_{\Delta,cc^{\prime}}(t,s)v_{c^{\prime}}(s),

(256)

\displaystyle v_{c}(t)

\displaystyle=w_{\star,c}-r_{c}^{0}(t)-\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}\left[\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}R_{gu,cc^{\prime}}^{1}(t,s)+\eta C_{g,cc^{\prime}}^{1}(t,s)\right]h_{c^{\prime}}^{0}(s),

(257)

\displaystyle h_{c}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{c}^{1}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}\left[\frac{1}{\nu\gamma_{0}}R_{hr,cc^{\prime}}^{0}(t,s)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{h,cc^{\prime}}^{0}(t,s)\right]g_{c^{\prime}}^{1}(s),

(258)

\displaystyle h_{c}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{c}^{\ell}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}\left[R_{hr,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{h,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell-1}(t,s)\right]g_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(s),\qquad 2\leq\ell\leq L,

(259)

\displaystyle g_{c}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=r_{c}^{\ell}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}\left[R_{gu,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}(t,s)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{g,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}(t,s)\right]h_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(s),\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq L-1.

(260)

The responses are again functional derivatives of the single-site process

R_{\Delta,cc^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial\Delta_{c}(t)}{\partial u_{\Delta,c^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,\qquad R_{vu,cc^{\prime}}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial v_{c}(t)}{\partial u_{c^{\prime}}^{0}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

R_{hr,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial h_{c}^{\ell}(t)}{\partial r_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,\qquad R_{gu,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\frac{\partial g_{c}^{\ell}(t)}{\partial u_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle.

The deterministic self-consistency conditions are

\displaystyle C_{v,cc^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle v_{c}(t)v_{c^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle,

\displaystyle C_{\Delta,cc^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta_{c}(t)\Delta_{c^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle,

(261)

\displaystyle C_{h,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle h_{c}^{\ell}(t)h_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle,

\displaystyle C_{g,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle g_{c}^{\ell}(t)g_{c^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle.

(262)

The spectral calculation changes in the same way. The probe vector itself has
no class index because it probes the shared weight matrix. However, the
finite-rank update directions are now the collection

\left\{\bm{h}_{c}^{\ell}(s),\bm{g}_{c}^{\ell+1}(s)\right\}_{c=1}^{C,\,s<t}.

For example, for a hidden layer
\ell

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\bm{\zeta}_{1}(\tau)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\bm{g}_{c}^{\ell+1}(s)C^{h_{c}^{\ell}\psi_{0}}(s,\tau),

(263)

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\bm{\zeta}_{2}(\tau)+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\bm{h}_{c}^{\ell}(s)C^{g_{c}^{\ell+1}\psi_{1}}(s,\tau).

(264)

Therefore the pole matrix derived above is unchanged in structure, but its
blocks are enlarged from
T\times T
matrices to

(CT)\times(CT)
block matrices. If

I=(c,t),\qquad J=(c^{\prime},t^{\prime}),

we define

[\mathsf{C}_{h}^{\ell}]_{I,J}=C_{h,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime}),\qquad[\mathsf{C}_{g}^{\ell+1}]_{I,J}=C_{g,cc^{\prime}}^{\ell+1}(t,t^{\prime}),

and similarly for

\mathsf{R}_{hr}^{\ell},\qquad\mathsf{R}_{gu}^{\ell+1}.

Then the hidden-layer spectral condition is obtained by the substitutions

\bm{C}_{h}^{\ell}\to\mathsf{C}_{h}^{\ell},\qquad\bm{C}_{g}^{\ell+1}\to\mathsf{C}_{g}^{\ell+1},\qquad\bm{R}_{hr}^{\ell}\to\mathsf{R}_{hr}^{\ell},\qquad\bm{R}_{gu}^{\ell+1}\to\mathsf{R}_{gu}^{\ell+1},\qquad\bm{I}_{T}\to\bm{I}_{CT}.

Thus

\det\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}^{\rm multi-class}(z)=0

(265)

has exactly the same form as in the scalar-output case.

If the teacher/readout initialization is class-symmetric and the targets are
orthogonal or independent across classes, the saddle can often be restricted
to a class-diagonal or permutation-symmetric ansatz. In the most general case,
however, one should keep all cross-class order parameters.

E.14
Online Dynamics

The full-batch calculation above assumes that the same dataset

\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times D}
is reused at every training step. In online
SGD, this data block is replaced by a fresh mini-batch

\bm{X}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times D}
at each step
t
, with

B=\alpha_{B}D,\qquad D,N,B\to\infty,\qquad\nu=\frac{N}{D},\qquad\alpha_{B}=\frac{B}{D}.

The input-layer data fields are now

\bm{h}^{0}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{B}\bm{X}(t)^{\top}\bm{\Delta}(t),\qquad\bm{\Delta}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{X}(t)\bm{v}(t)+\sigma\bm{\epsilon}(t),

(266)

where
\bm{X}(t)
and
\bm{\epsilon}(t)
are resampled independently at
each training step. All weight-dependent fields and all finite-rank weight
updates are unchanged.

The only modification to the DMFT is therefore in the data average. Since
the data are fresh at each time step, the average over
\bm{X}(t)
factorizes
over
t
. Thus the nonlocal data-response sector of the full-batch theory is
removed. Equivalently, in the full-batch data block one makes the replacement

R_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})\longrightarrow\delta_{tt^{\prime}},

(267)

and the error covariance becomes

C_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})=C_{v}(t,t^{\prime})+\sigma^{2}\delta_{tt^{\prime}}.

(268)

The stochastic input-layer process is therefore

\Delta(t)=u_{\Delta}(t)+\sigma\epsilon(t),\qquad u_{\Delta}\sim\mathrm{GP}(0,C_{v}),\qquad\langle\epsilon(t)\epsilon(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{tt^{\prime}},

(269)

and

h^{0}(t)=v(t)+u^{0}(t),\qquad u^{0}\sim\mathrm{GP}\left(0,\frac{1}{\alpha_{B}}C_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})\delta_{tt^{\prime}}\right).

(270)

In particular, the full-batch memory term

\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}R_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})\Delta(t^{\prime})

in the
\Delta
-equation is absent in online SGD. The finite-batch effect
instead appears as a time-local stochastic-gradient noise in
h^{0}
. On the
diagonal,

\langle h^{0}(t)^{2}\rangle=C_{v}(t,t)+\frac{1}{\alpha_{B}}C_{\Delta}(t,t),

(271)

so smaller batches increase the effective input noise.

The remaining single-site training equations are unchanged, except that they
are driven by the online-SGD input process above. Thus

v(t)=w_{\star}-r^{0}(t)-\sum_{s<t}\left[\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}R_{gu}^{1}(t,s)+\eta C_{g}^{1}(t,s)\right]h^{0}(s),\qquad r^{0}\sim\mathrm{GP}\left(0,\frac{1}{\nu\gamma_{0}^{2}}C_{g}^{1}\right),

(272)

h^{1}(t)=u^{1}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\left[\frac{1}{\nu\gamma_{0}}R_{hr}^{0}(t,s)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{h}^{0}(t,s)\right]g^{1}(s),\qquad u^{1}\sim\mathrm{GP}(0,C_{h}^{0}),

(273)

and, for
1<\ell\leq L
,

h^{\ell}(t)=u^{\ell}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\left[R_{hr}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{h}^{\ell-1}(t,s)\right]g^{\ell}(s),\qquad u^{\ell}\sim\mathrm{GP}(0,C_{h}^{\ell-1}).

(274)

Similarly, for
1\leq\ell\leq L-1
,

g^{\ell}(t)=r^{\ell}(t)+\sum_{s<t}\left[R_{gu}^{\ell+1}(t,s)+\eta\gamma_{0}C_{g}^{\ell+1}(t,s)\right]h^{\ell}(s),\qquad r^{\ell}\sim\mathrm{GP}(0,C_{g}^{\ell+1}),

(275)

with the same readout boundary condition

g^{L}(t)=r^{L}+\eta\gamma_{0}\sum_{s<t}h^{L}(s),\qquad r^{L}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1).

(276)

The self-consistency equations for the correlations and responses are also
unchanged in form:

\displaystyle C_{v}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\langle v(t)v(t^{\prime})\rangle,

\displaystyle C_{\Delta}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta(t)\Delta(t^{\prime})\rangle,

(277)

\displaystyle C_{h}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\langle h^{\ell}(t)h^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\rangle,

\displaystyle C_{g}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\langle g^{\ell}(t)g^{\ell}(t^{\prime})\rangle,

(278)

and

\displaystyle R_{vu}^{0}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial v(t)}{\partial u^{0}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle R_{hr}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial h^{\ell}(t)}{\partial r^{\ell}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle,

(279)

\displaystyle R_{gu}^{\ell}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial g^{\ell}(t)}{\partial u^{\ell}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle.

(280)

Finally, the spectral-probe DMFT derived above is not modified at the level of
the weight averages. The probe equations and the pole conditions

\det\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(z)=0,\qquad\det\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}(z)=0

retain the same form. The only change is that the training order parameters

C_{h}^{\ell},\quad C_{g}^{\ell},\quad R_{hr}^{\ell},\quad R_{gu}^{\ell}

that enter the probe fixed-point equations must now be computed from the
online-SGD training DMFT rather than from the full-batch DMFT.

For a two-layer linear network, i.e.
L=1
, the model is

f_{t}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{N\gamma_{0}}\bm{w}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}(t)\bm{x},

(281)

where
\bm{W}(t)\equiv\bm{W}^{0}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}
and

\bm{w}(t)\equiv\bm{w}^{1}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In this case the backward
field is simply

\bm{g}^{1}(t)=\bm{w}(t).

(282)

The population NTK can be written as a kernel on input space. Indeed,

\frac{\partial f_{t}(\bm{x})}{\partial\bm{w}}=\frac{1}{N\gamma_{0}}\bm{W}(t)\bm{x},\qquad\frac{\partial f_{t}(\bm{x})}{\partial W_{ij}}=\frac{1}{N\gamma_{0}}w_{i}(t)x_{j}.

(283)

Therefore, up to the overall convention-dependent prefactor coming from the
learning-rate normalization, the NTK has the

\Theta_{t}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{D}\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{B}_{t}\bm{x}^{\prime},

(284)

where the
D\times D
population NTK core is

\bm{B}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\bm{W}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}(t)+q_{g}(t)\bm{I}_{D}

(285)

with

q_{g}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\|\bm{w}(t)\|^{2}=C_{g}^{1}(t,t)

(286)

in the usual notation. Thus, in the two-layer case, the NTK core is exactly the first-layer weight covariance plus an isotropic readout-gradient contribution. The scalar
q_{g}(t)
is already one of the training DMFT order
parameters, since
C_{g}^{1}(t,t)=N^{-1}\bm{g}^{1}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{1}(t)
.

Consequently, the spectrum of the population NTK core is just a shift of the
spectrum of the first-layer weight covariance

\bm{M}^{0}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{W}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}(t).

(287)

If

\lambda_{1}^{M}(t),\ldots,\lambda_{D}^{M}(t)

(288)

are the eigenvalues of
\bm{M}^{0}(t)
, then the eigenvalues of
\bm{B}_{t}
are

\lambda_{a}^{\mathrm{NTK}}(t)=\lambda_{a}^{M}(t)+q_{g}(t),\qquad a=1,\ldots,D.

(289)

Equivalently, if
m_{M}(z;t)
is the Stieltjes transform of the spectral
measure of
\bm{M}^{0}(t)
, then the NTK-core resolvent is

m_{\mathrm{NTK}}(z;t)=m_{M}(z-q_{g}(t);t).

(290)

Thus the NTK spectral density satisfies

\rho_{\mathrm{NTK}}(\lambda;t)=\rho_{M}(\lambda-q_{g}(t);t).

(291)

Notice that computing the spectrum of
\bm{K}_{t}
would require in principle
an additional probe involving the data matrix
\bm{X}
. The simple shift
relation above applies to the population limit only.

Appendix F
Large Output Channels

Consider a linear network with input dimension
D
, width
N
, and
C
output channels. Let

\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times P}
be the data matrix,
\bm{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times P}
the labels,

\bm{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}
the hidden weight matrix, and
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times C}
the readout. We study the spectrum of the weight covariance

\bm{M}=\frac{1}{N}\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}

(292)

in a proportional limit where
D,N,C\to\infty
with fixed ratios
\nu=\frac{N}{D}
and
\chi=\frac{C}{D}
. The network output and teacher are respectively

\bm{F}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}N}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{W}\bm{X},\qquad\bm{Y}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{B}^{\star}\bm{X},

(293)

being
\bm{B}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times D}
the target matrix. We are interested in the quadratic loss
\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2CP}\|\bm{F}-\bm{Y}\|_{F}^{2}
in the population limit
P\to\infty
where
\frac{1}{P}\bm{X}\bm{X}^{\top}\to\bm{I}_{D}
. One gradient step from random initialization produces an update of the form

\bm{W}(1)=\bm{W}(0)+\frac{\eta\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{C}}\bm{A}\bm{B}^{\star}.

(294)

Thus

\bm{M}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\bm{W}(0)+\frac{\eta\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{C}}\bm{A}\bm{B}^{\star}\right)^{\top}\left(\bm{W}(0)+\frac{\eta\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{C}}\bm{A}\bm{B}^{\star}\right).

(295)

At initialization,
\bm{W}(0)
,
\bm{A}
, and
\bm{B}^{\star}
are independent random matrices, which makes the model tractable for the extensive output regime.

To study the spectrum of
\bm{M}
, we introduce an auxiliary linear response problem driven by a source
\bm{j}(t)

\frac{d\bm{v}(t)}{dt}=-\bm{M}\bm{v}(t)+\bm{j}(t).

(296)

The reason for introducing this dynamics is that its response function is exactly the resolvent of
\bm{M}
. Indeed, the causal solution is
\bm{v}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}ds\,e^{-\bm{M}(t-s)}\bm{j}(s)
, so that
\frac{\delta\bm{v}(t+\tau)}{\delta\bm{j}(t)}=\Theta(\tau)e^{-\bm{M}\tau}.

Taking the Fourier transform of the normalized trace of this response gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}\text{Tr}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\,e^{-(i\omega\bm{I}_{D}+\bm{M})\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}\text{Tr}\left(i\omega\bm{I}_{D}+\bm{M}\right)^{-1}.

(297)

Thus the spectral density of
\bm{M}
can be recovered from the analytic continuation of
\mathcal{H}(\omega)
.

We implement this response calculation using a Martin–Siggia–Rose generating functional (MGF)

\mathcal{Z}[\bm{j}]=\int D\bm{v}^{0}D\hat{\bm{v}}^{0}\exp\left[i\int dt\,\hat{\bm{v}}^{0}(t)^{\top}\left(\partial_{t}\bm{v}^{0}(t)+\bm{M}\bm{v}^{0}(t)-\bm{j}(t)\right)\right].

(298)

The remaining task is to average this generating functional over the random matrices entering
\bm{M}
. To expose the random-matrix structure, we represent the action of
\bm{M}
on
\bm{v}^{0}(t)
through the auxiliary fields

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{B}\bm{v}^{0}(t),

(299)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}(0)\bm{v}^{0}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{C}}\bm{A}\bm{v}^{1}(t),

(300)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{C}}{N}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{v}^{2}(t),

(301)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{4}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{N}\bm{W}(0)^{\top}\bm{v}^{2}(t)+\gamma\frac{\sqrt{D}}{C}\bm{B}^{\top}\bm{v}^{3}(t).

(302)

With these definitions,

\bm{v}^{4}(t)=\bm{M}\bm{v}^{0}(t)

(303)

and resolvent is obtained from the response of the linear system

\partial_{t}\bm{v}^{0}(t)=-\bm{v}^{4}(t)+\bm{j}(t).

(304)

We enforce the above using the response fields
\hat{\bm{v}}^{i}
through the integral representation of the Dirac delta function, e.g.

1\equiv\int D\bm{v}^{1}D\hat{\bm{v}}^{1}\exp\left[i\int dt\,\hat{\bm{v}}^{1}(t)^{\top}\left(\bm{v}^{1}(t)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{B}\bm{v}^{0}(t)\right)\right].

(305)

Since
\bm{W}(0)
,
\bm{A}
, and
\bm{B}
are independent Gaussian matrices, their averages generate only quadratic contractions between the dynamical fields. The resulting effective theory depends on the correlation order parameters

\displaystyle C_{00}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D}\bm{v}^{0}(t)\cdot\bm{v}^{0}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle C_{11}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}\bm{v}^{1}(t)\cdot\bm{v}^{1}(t^{\prime}),

(306)

\displaystyle C_{22}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\bm{v}^{2}(t)\cdot\bm{v}^{2}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle C_{33}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}\bm{v}^{3}(t)\cdot\bm{v}^{3}(t^{\prime}),

(307)

and response order parameters

\displaystyle R_{04}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{v}^{0}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}^{4}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle R_{13}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{C}\bm{v}^{1}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}^{3}(t^{\prime}),

(308)

\displaystyle R_{22}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{N}\bm{v}^{2}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}^{2}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle R_{31}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{C}\bm{v}^{3}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}^{1}(t^{\prime})

(309)

which converge to deterministic quantities in the high-dimensional limit.

F.1
DMFT action

At large
D,N,C
, with
\nu=N/D
and
\chi=C/D
fixed, the disorder-averaged generating functional factorizes into independent single-site problems for the
D
-dimensional sector
(\bm{v}^{0},\bm{v}^{4})
, the
N
-dimensional sector
\bm{v}^{2}
, and the
C
-dimensional sector
(\bm{v}^{1},\bm{v}^{3})

\mathcal{Z}[\bm{j}]=\int d\mathcal{Q}\,\exp\left\{DS_{\rm eff}[\mathcal{Q};\bm{j}]\right\},

(310)

where

\mathcal{Q}=\{C_{00},C_{11},C_{22},C_{33},R_{04},R_{13},R_{22},R_{31}\},

(311)

while the DMFT action can be compressed as

S_{\rm eff}=S_{\rm op}[\mathcal{Q}]+\log Z_{04}[\bm{j}]+\nu\log Z_{2}+\chi\log Z_{13}.

(312)

Here,
S_{\rm op}
collects the order-parameter terms generated by the Gaussian averages, while
Z_{04}
,
Z_{2}
, and
Z_{13}
are the corresponding single-site path integrals. The response-sector saddle point equations from
\frac{\partial S_{\rm eff}}{\partial R}=0
are

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{04}

\displaystyle=R_{22}+\gamma R_{31}

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{13}

\displaystyle=\gamma R_{22}

(313)

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{22}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu}R_{04}+\gamma\frac{\chi}{\nu}R_{13}

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{31}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{\chi}R_{04}.

(314)

Substituting these back into the effective action gives three coupled single-site theories. Specifically, the
D
-sector, which contains the resolvent response, is

Z_{04}[\bm{j}]=\int Dv^{0}D\hat{v}^{0}Dv^{4}D\hat{v}^{4}\exp\left\{S_{04}[\bm{j}]\right\},

(315)

with

\displaystyle S_{04}[\bm{j}]

\displaystyle=i\int dt\,\hat{v}^{0}(t)\left(\partial_{t}v^{0}(t)+v^{4}(t)-j(t)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int dtdt^{\prime}\,\hat{C}_{00}(t,t^{\prime})v^{0}(t)v^{0}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int dtdt^{\prime}\,\left[\frac{1}{\nu}C_{22}(t,t^{\prime})+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}C_{33}(t,t^{\prime})\right]\hat{v}^{4}(t)\hat{v}^{4}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad-i\int dtdt^{\prime}\,\left[R_{22}(t,t^{\prime})+\gamma R_{31}(t,t^{\prime})\right]v^{0}(t)\hat{v}^{4}(t^{\prime})+i\int dtv_{4}(t)\hat{v}_{4}(t).

(316)

The
N
-sector is

Z_{2}=\int Dv^{2}D\hat{v}^{2}\exp\left\{S_{2}\right\},

(317)

with

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int dtdt^{\prime}\,\hat{C}_{22}(t,t^{\prime})v^{2}(t)v^{2}(t^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\int dtdt^{\prime}\,\left[C_{00}(t,t^{\prime})+\gamma^{2}C_{11}(t,t^{\prime})\right]\hat{v}^{2}(t)\hat{v}^{2}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad-i\int dtdt^{\prime}\,\left[\frac{1}{\nu}R_{04}(t,t^{\prime})+\gamma\frac{\chi}{\nu}R_{13}(t,t^{\prime})\right]v^{2}(t)\hat{v}^{2}(t^{\prime})+i\int dt\,v^{2}(t)\hat{v}^{2}(t).

(318)

Finally, the
C
-sector is

Z_{13}=\int Dv^{1}D\hat{v}^{1}Dv^{3}D\hat{v}^{3}\exp\left\{S_{13}\right\},

(319)

with

\displaystyle S_{13}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int dtdt^{\prime}\,\hat{C}_{11}(t,t^{\prime})v^{1}(t)v^{1}(t^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\int dtdt^{\prime}\,\hat{C}_{33}(t,t^{\prime})v^{3}(t)v^{3}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\chi}{\nu}\int dtdt^{\prime}\,C_{22}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{v}^{3}(t)\hat{v}^{3}(t^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\int dtdt^{\prime}\,C_{00}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{v}^{1}(t)\hat{v}^{1}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad-i\gamma\int dtdt^{\prime}\,R_{22}(t,t^{\prime})v^{1}(t)\hat{v}^{3}(t^{\prime})-i\frac{\gamma}{\chi}\int dtdt^{\prime}\,R_{04}(t,t^{\prime})v^{3}(t)\hat{v}^{1}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad+i\int dt\,v^{1}(t)\hat{v}^{1}(t)+i\int dt\,v^{3}(t)\hat{v}^{3}(t).

(320)

F.1.1
Saddle point equations

The saddle point of the effective action identifies the order parameters with the corresponding single-site correlations and responses. We start by differentiating with respect to the conjugate order parameters for the correlation functions which give

\displaystyle C_{00}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle v^{0}(t)v^{0}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle_{04}

\displaystyle C_{11}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle v^{1}(t)v^{1}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle_{13}

(321)

\displaystyle C_{22}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle v^{2}(t)v^{2}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle C_{33}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle v^{3}(t)v^{3}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle_{13}.

(322)

while the response functions are

\displaystyle R_{04}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\delta v^{0}(t)}{\delta u_{4}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle_{04}

\displaystyle R_{22}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\delta v^{2}(t)}{\delta u_{2}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle_{2}

(323)

\displaystyle R_{13}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\delta v^{1}(t)}{\delta u_{3}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle_{13}

\displaystyle R_{31}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\delta v^{3}(t)}{\delta u_{1}(t^{\prime})}\right\rangle_{13}.

(324)

For causality conditions, the conjugate correlation fields vanish at the saddle

\hat{C}_{00}=\hat{C}_{11}=\hat{C}_{22}=\hat{C}_{33}=0.

(325)

The final DMFT equations are then

v^{2}(t)=u_{2}(t)+\sum_{t^{\prime}}\left[\frac{1}{\nu}R_{04}(t,t^{\prime})+\gamma\frac{\chi}{\nu}R_{13}(t,t^{\prime})\right]v^{2}(t^{\prime})

(326)

where

u_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\,C_{00}+\gamma^{2}C_{11}\right).

(327)

The
C
-sector gives the coupled equations

\displaystyle v^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{1}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{\chi}\sum_{t^{\prime}}R_{04}(t,t^{\prime})v^{3}(t^{\prime})

(328)

\displaystyle v^{3}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{3}(t)+\gamma\sum_{t^{\prime}}R_{22}(t,t^{\prime})v^{1}(t^{\prime})

(329)

where

u_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,C_{00})\qquad u_{3}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\,\frac{\chi}{\nu}C_{22}\right)

(330)

and the
D
-sector gives the resolvent probe

\displaystyle v^{4}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{4}(t)+\sum_{t^{\prime}}\left[R_{22}(t,t^{\prime})+\gamma R_{31}(t,t^{\prime})\right]v^{0}(t^{\prime})

(331)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{0}(t)

\displaystyle=-u_{4}(t)-\sum_{t^{\prime}}\left[R_{22}(t,t^{\prime})+\gamma R_{31}(t,t^{\prime})\right]v^{0}(t^{\prime})+j(t)

(332)

where

u_{4}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\,\frac{1}{\nu}C_{22}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}C_{33}\right).

(333)

These equations form the DMFT closure: the correlation functions are computed from the stochastic processes above, while the response functions are obtained by differentiating the same processes with respect to their Gaussian noises.

Since the linear system is time-translation-invariant (T.T.I.), in Fourier space

\displaystyle\int dt\,e^{-i\omega t}\Bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}}v^{0}(t^{\prime})+u_{4}(t)+\sum_{t^{\prime}}\Big(R_{2,2}(t,t^{\prime})+\gamma R_{3,1}(t,t^{\prime})\Big)v^{0}(t^{\prime})-j(t)\Bigg)

(334)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\Big(i\omega+R_{2,2}(\omega)+\gamma R_{3,1}(\omega)\Big)v_{0}(\omega)+u_{4}(\omega)-j(\omega)=0

(335)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mathcal{H}(\omega)=\frac{1}{i\omega+R_{2,2}(\omega)+\gamma R_{3,1}(\omega)}

(336)

and since
R_{04}
is the response of
v^{0}
to
u_{4}
, one has

R_{04}(\omega)=-\mathcal{H}(\omega).

(337)

The remaining response functions can be solved algebraically from the single-site equations. Defining

\Delta(\omega)=1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H}(\omega)R_{22}(\omega)

(338)

we obtain

\displaystyle R_{31}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma R_{22}(\omega)}{\Delta(\omega)}

(339)

\displaystyle R_{13}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma}{\chi}\frac{\mathcal{H}(\omega)}{\Delta(\omega)}

(340)

\displaystyle R_{22}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\left[1+\frac{1}{\nu}\mathcal{H}(\omega)+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\nu}\frac{\mathcal{H}(\omega)}{\Delta(\omega)}\right]^{-1}.

(341)

Together with

\mathcal{H}(\omega)=\left[i\omega+R_{22}(\omega)+\frac{\gamma^{2}R_{22}(\omega)}{\Delta(\omega)}\right]^{-1}

(342)

these equations give a closed self-consistency problem for the resolvent.

The spectral density of
\bm{M}
is obtained by analytic continuation,

\rho(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{H}(\omega=i\lambda-\epsilon).

(343)

This set of equations shows explicitly why the extensive-output regime differs from the finite-rank spike setting: when
\chi=\mathcal{O}(1)
, the readout-induced update contributes at the level of the full single-site, modifying the resolvent self-consistently and restructuring the bulk spectrum rather than only producing isolated outliers.

F.2
Edge of the Bulk

Spectral edges correspond to poles of the Stieltjes transform. Suppose we have
G(z)=\text{tr}\left[z-\bm{M}\right]^{-1}=\frac{1}{z-R(G)}
. We can re-express this as

z=\frac{1}{G}+R(G).

(344)

The derivative
\frac{dz}{dG}=\Big(\frac{dG}{dz}\Big)^{-1}
should be zero at the spectral edge since
\frac{dG}{dz}
diverges there.
In our notation, the resolvent is

\mathcal{H}(z)=\left[z+R_{22}(z)+\frac{\gamma^{2}R_{22}(z)}{1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H}(z)R_{22}(z)}\right]^{-1}

(345)

together with

R_{22}(z)=\left[1+\frac{1}{\nu}\mathcal{H}(z)+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\nu}\frac{\mathcal{H}(z)}{1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H}(z)R_{22}(z)}\right]^{-1}.

(346)

In general, the bulk edge is obtained by solving these equations together with the condition that the Jacobian of the map from
(\mathcal{H},R_{22})
to
z
becomes singular. A simple closed form is obtained in the limit
\nu\to\infty
. In this case
R_{22}(z)\to 1
,
and the resolvent equation reduces to

\mathcal{H}(z)=\left[z+1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H}(z)}\right]^{-1}.

(347)

Equivalently, using the physical spectral variable
\lambda=-z
,

\lambda(\mathcal{H})=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}+1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H}}.

(348)

The bulk edges are therefore determined by

\frac{d\lambda}{d\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{4}/\chi}{\left(1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H}\right)^{2}}=0.

(349)

Solving this equation and substituting back into
\lambda(\mathcal{H})
gives a shifted Marchenko–Pastur bulk with upper edge

\lambda_{\rm edge}=1+\gamma^{2}\left(1+\chi^{-1/2}\right)^{2}.

(350)

This is consistent with the intuition that, when
N/D\to\infty
, the initialization covariance concentrates as

\frac{1}{N}\bm{W}(0)^{\top}\bm{W}(0)\to\bm{I}_{D},

(351)

while the learned extensive-output term contributes a Wishart-like component proportional to
\bm{B}^{\top}\bm{B}/C
.

F.2.1
Expansion in the
\nu\to\infty
limit

In the large
\nu
limit, we expect the corrections term to the response function to be

R_{22}=1+\frac{1}{\nu}R^{(1)}+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{-2})

(352)

with

R^{(1)}=-\mathcal{H}\Big[1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H}}\Big].

(353)

By expanding the edge to first order in
1/\nu
as well, one gets

\lambda(\mathcal{H})=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}+1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H}}+\frac{1}{\nu}R^{(1)}\Big[1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{(1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H})^{2}}\Big]+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{-2})

(354)

and therefore
\lambda(\mathcal{H})=\lambda_{0}(\mathcal{H})+\frac{1}{\nu}\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H})+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{-2})
with

\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H})=-\mathcal{H}\Big[1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H}}\Big]\Big[1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{(1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\mathcal{H})^{2}}\Big].

(355)

The edge is stable across
\nu
when the relative correction to the bulk edge is small

\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H})}{\lambda_{\rm edge}}\ll 1

(356)

therefore the condition for the upper edge to have stabilized is

\nu\gg\nu_{\star}=\sqrt{\chi}+\frac{\chi}{\gamma^{2}(1+\sqrt{\chi})}.

(357)

Figure
19
verifies this scaling collapse: when the relative edge shift is plotted against
\nu/\nu_{\star}(\chi,\gamma)
, curves at different
\chi
collapse onto the predicted
\nu_{\star}/\nu
behavior at large rescaled width.

F.3
Multi-Layer setting

It is easy to extend the calculation to a multi-layer setting. Let the forward pass be

\bm{H}^{\ell}=\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell-1}(0)\Big)\ldots\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{0}(0)\Big)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}

(358)

and backwards

\bm{G}^{\ell+1}=\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell+1}(0)^{\top}\Big)\ldots\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{L-1}(0)^{\top}\Big)\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times C}.

(359)

After one gradient step, the weight update for a generic hidden layer
\ell
is

\bm{W}^{\ell}(1)=\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{CD}}\bm{G}^{\ell+1}\bm{B}^{\star}(\bm{H}^{\ell})^{\top}.

(360)

We wish to study the spectrum of

\bm{M}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{N}\bm{W}^{\ell}(1)^{\top}\bm{W}^{\ell}(1).

(361)

In order to do that, we can define the following fields

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{H}^{\ell\top}\bm{v}^{0}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{D}

(362)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{B}^{\star}\bm{v}^{1}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}

(363)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{C}}\bm{G}^{\ell+1}\bm{v}^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\bm{v}^{0}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

(364)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{4}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{C}}{N}\bm{G}^{\ell+1\,\top}\bm{v}_{3}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}

(365)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{5}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{C}\bm{B}^{\star\top}\bm{v}^{4}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{D}

(366)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{6}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{0}\sqrt{N}}{D}\bm{H}^{\ell}\bm{v}^{5}(t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\bm{v}^{3}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(367)

Then

\bm{v}^{6}(t)=\bm{M}^{\ell}\bm{v}^{0}(t)

(368)

and the probe dynamics is simply

\partial_{t}\bm{v}^{0}(t)=-\bm{v}^{6}(t).

(369)

And the calculation proceeds by just computing averages over the random independent Gaussian weights. For instance, for three hidden layers linear network, if we to study the spectrum of the hidden layer
\ell=1
, the moment generating function can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[j]

\displaystyle=\int\prod_{a=0}^{6}D\bm{v}^{a}(t)D\hat{\bm{v}}^{a}(t)\>\exp\Bigg[i\int dt\hat{\bm{v}}^{0}(t)\cdot\Big(\partial_{t}\bm{v}^{0}(t)+\bm{v}^{6}(t)-\bm{j}(t)\Big)+i\sum_{a=0}^{6}\int dt\bm{v}^{a}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}^{a}\Bigg]

(370)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigg[-i\int dt\Bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{1}(t)\cdot\bm{W}^{0\top}\bm{v}^{0}(t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{2}(t)\cdot\bm{B}\bm{v}^{1}(t)\Bigg)\Bigg]

(371)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigg[-i\int dt\Bigg(\frac{\sqrt{C}}{N}\hat{\bm{v}}^{4}(t)\cdot\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{v}_{3}(t)+\frac{\sqrt{D}}{C}\hat{\bm{v}}^{5}(t)\cdot\bm{B}^{\top}\bm{v}^{4}(t)+\frac{\gamma_{0}\sqrt{N}}{D}\hat{\bm{v}}^{6}(t)\cdot\bm{W}^{0}\bm{v}^{5}(t)\Bigg]

(372)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigg[-i\int dt\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{6}(t)\cdot\bm{W}^{1}(0)^{\top}\bm{v}^{3}(t)+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{C}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{3}(t)\cdot\bm{A}\bm{v}^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\hat{\bm{v}}^{3}(t)\cdot\bm{W}^{1}(0)\bm{v}^{0}(t)\Bigg)\Bigg]

(373)

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg[-i\text{Tr}\bm{W}^{0}(0)^{\top}\int dt\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{v}^{0}(t)\hat{\bm{v}}^{1}(t)^{\top}+\frac{\gamma_{0}\sqrt{N}}{D}\hat{\bm{v}}^{6}(t)\bm{v}^{5}(t)^{\top}\Big)\Bigg]

(383)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{C}\bm{v}^{2}(t^{\prime})\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}^{4}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{D}\bm{v}^{1}(t^{\prime})\cdot\hat{\bm{v}}^{5}(t)

giving the DMFT equations

\displaystyle v_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{2}(t)+\frac{1}{\chi}\int dsR_{15}(t,s)v_{4}(s),\quad\mathcal{N}(0,C_{v}^{1})

(399)

\displaystyle=u_{3}(t)+\int ds\Big(R_{06}+\gamma_{0}\frac{\chi}{\nu}R_{24}\Big)v_{3}(s),\quad u_{3}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,C_{v}^{0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}C_{v}^{2})

\displaystyle v_{4}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{4}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int dsR_{33}(t,s)v_{2}(s),\quad u_{4}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{\chi}{\nu}C_{v}^{3})

\displaystyle=u_{5}(t)+\int dsR_{42}(t,s)v_{1}(s)\quad u_{5}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{\chi}C_{v}^{4})

\displaystyle v_{6}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{6}(t)+\int ds\Big(\gamma_{0}R_{51}(t,s)+R_{33}(t,s)\Big)v_{0}(s),\quad u_{6}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,C_{v}^{3}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\nu C_{v}^{5})

(403)

and finally

\partial_{t}v_{0}(t)=-u_{6}(t)-\int ds\Big(\gamma_{0}R_{51}(t,s)+R_{33}(t,s)\Big)v_{0}(s)+j(t).

(404)

In Fourier space

\displaystyle R_{06}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{i\omega+R_{33}(\omega)+\gamma_{0}R_{51}(\omega)}=-\mathcal{H}(\omega)

(405)

\displaystyle R_{15}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{0}\nu\mathcal{H}(\omega)}{1+\gamma_{0}\nu\mathcal{H}(\omega)R_{42}(\omega)}

(406)

\displaystyle R_{51}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{R_{42}(\omega)}{1+\gamma_{0}\nu R_{42}(\omega)\mathcal{H}(\omega)}

(407)

\displaystyle R_{24}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{\chi}R_{15}(\omega)}{1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\chi}R_{15}(\omega)R_{33}(\omega)}

(408)

\displaystyle R_{42}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{0}R_{33}(\omega)}{1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\chi}R_{33}(\omega)R_{15}(\omega)}

(409)

\displaystyle R_{33}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\mathcal{H}(\omega)-\gamma_{0}\frac{\chi}{\nu}R_{24}(\omega)}.

(410)

The equations above give the closed response-sector DMFT for the spectrum, which can then be obtained from

\rho(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\text{Im}\mathcal{H}(i\lambda-\epsilon).

(411)
