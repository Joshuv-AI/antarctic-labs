Cobordism-equivalence for codimension-one submanifolds
Stefan Friedl, Tobias Hirsch, Clayton McDonald, José Pedro Quintanilha, Daniel Zach

Abstract
We show that two hypersurfaces in a manifold are related by a sequence of embedded cobordisms if and only if they represent the same homology class. By applying handle decompositions we turn these cobordisms into a sequence of embedded surgeries. Specializing to Seifert surfaces we obtain a conceptual proof that two Seifert surfaces of a fixed link are related by tube attachments and tube removals.

Introduction

It is a well-known result in knot theory that two Seifert surfaces of a given link are related by a sequence of tube attachments and tube removals. This is a key input for showing that two Seifert matrices of the same link are S-equivalent. There are many different types of proofs in the literature,

As a consequence, our proof also applies to Seifert surfaces in rational homology spheres.

This proposition only proves cobordism-equivalence for hypersurfaces which are already disjoint. The final ingredient we need for the proof of our main result is a theorem of Herrmann and the fourth author

allowing us to make hypersurfaces disjoint:

Theorem 3.4

(Sequentially disjoint hypersurfaces)
.

Let
A,B
be hypersurfaces in
M
representing the same homology class
\phi\in\operatorname{H}_{n-1}(M,\partial M)
. Then there exists a sequence of hypersurfaces in
M

A=S_{0},S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}=B,

all representing
\phi
, such that each two consecutive
S_{i-1},S_{i}
are disjoint.

If
A
,
B
only allow product cobordisms in the boundary, we may assume that
S_{i-1}
,
S_{i}
also only allow product cobordisms in the boundary.

Proof.

We first reduce the problem to the case where the intersection of
A
and
B
is transverse.
To that end, let
A_{+}
be a parallel copy of
A
(in particular,
A_{+}\cap A=\emptyset
), and then by a general position argument perturb
A_{+}
by a small isotopy to produce a hypersurface
A^{\prime}
that is still disjoint from
A
and intersects
B
transversely.
A^{\prime}
is isotopic, and hence homologous, to
A
, so taking
S_{1}=A^{\prime}
, we are left to find sequentially disjoint homologous hypersurfaces from
A^{\prime}
to
B
.

Working under the assumption that
A
and
B
are transverse, we prove the result by induction on the number of components of
A\cap B
. Of course if this number is
0
there is nothing to prove. If
|\pi_{0}(A\cap B)|\geq 1
, we will show that there is a hypersurface
S
representing
\phi
that intersects both
A
and
B
transversely, and for which

|\pi_{0}(S\cap A)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|\pi_{0}(A\cap B)|\quad\text{and}\quad|\pi_{0}(S\cap B)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|\pi_{0}(A\cap B)|.

Once such
S
is constructed, the induction hypothesis yields sequences of hypersurfaces from
A
to
S
and from
S
to
B
, which can be concatenated to produce a sequence from
A
to
B
.

The first step to produce

is to construct a so-called “oriented surgery” of
A
and
B
. This is a hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
obtained from

by replacing a small neighborhood of

with a “ramp” that pairs each sheet of

with a sheet of

in a manner consistent with orientations;

(center) gives a visual idea of the construction.
\Sigma_{0}
inherits a canonical orientation from
A
and
B
. We then push
\Sigma_{0}
in the positive direction of its normal bundle, as in

4
(right), to obtain an isotopic hypersurface
\Sigma
transverse to
A
and
B
.

\Gamma

The graph

may be used to produce a loop

, and define

Theorem 3.5

(Homology and cobordism-equivalence)
.

Two hypersurfaces
A
,
B
in an
n
-dimensional manifold
M
are cobordism-equivalent if and only if they represent the same homology class in
\operatorname{H}_{n-1}(M,\partial M)
.

If
A
,
B
only allow product cobordisms in the boundary, the above conditions are also equivalent to
A
and
B
being cobordism-equivalent with vertical boundary.

Proof.

One implication is
3.2
, the other follows from
3.4
and
3.3
.
∎

4
Cobordisms and surgery

We now apply the theory of handle decompositions to the cobordisms from the main theorem. A handle decomposition of a cobordism from
A
to
B
corresponds to a sequence of surgeries from
A
to
B
. In light of this, a hypersurface
B
arises from a hypersurface
A
in
M
by
(embedded) surgery of index
k
if there exists a smooth embedding
\tau\colon\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k}\to M\setminus\partial M
such that

–

\tau(\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k})\cap A=\tau(\partial\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k})\subseteq A\setminus\partial A
, and

–

\tau(\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-k})\cap B=\tau(\mathbb{D}^{k}\times\partial\mathbb{D}^{n-k})\subseteq B\setminus\partial B
.

The vertical part of the cobordisms constructed in the second part of
3.3
need not be a product cobordism because it could have closed components. We therefore cannot directly construct handle decompositions, but need to allow an additional operation: A hypersurface
B
arises from a hypersurface
A
in
M
by
removing a boundary-parallel component
if there exist a boundary component
C
of
M
and a smooth embedding
f\colon C\times[0,1]\to M\setminus B
that restricts to the identity on
C\times\{0\}
and an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism onto a component of
A
on
C\times\{1\}
. Here we equip
C
with the boundary orientation.

We obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.1

(Equivalence through surgery)
.

Let
A
,
B
be hypersurfaces in an
n
-dimensional manifold
M
that only allow product cobordisms in the boundary and represent the same homology class in
\operatorname{H}_{n-1}(M,\partial M)
. There exists a sequence of smooth isotopies, surgeries and removing boundary-parallel components from
A
to
B
.

Proof.

By
3.5

A
and
B
are cobordism-equivalent through cobordisms with vertical boundary. Hence, it suffices to consider the case when there exists a cobordism
W
with vertical boundary from
A
to
B
. Then
\partial_{v}W
is given by a product cobordism and a union
C
of boundary components of
M
. Slightly push
C
into
M
. Since the orientation of
W
is induced by the orientation of
M
,
W
now gives a cobordism from
A
to
B\cup C
.

By
2.3

W
has a handle decomposition as a cobordism from
A
to
B\cup C
. As surgery is the effect of a handle attachment in the boundary, we obtain a sequence of surgeries from a hypersurface isotopic to
A
to
B\cup C
. By construction,
B
arises from
B\cup C
by removing boundary-parallel components.
∎

Just as with handle decompositions we may reorder these surgeries into a standard form. Note however that this is not a formal consequence of the corresponding statement about handle decompositions since the cobordisms in the last proof may interact with each other.

Lemma 4.2

(Rearranging surgeries)
.

Let
A
,
B
be hypersurfaces in an
n
-dimensional manifold
M
. Then every sequence of surgeries and smooth isotopies from
A
to
B
may be rearranged into a sequence with the same number of surgeries, and in which the surgery indices occur in increasing order.

Proof.

This is essentially proven in the same manner as the analogous statements about handle decompositions (see
[
13
, Lemma 5.2.1]
). Note that the surgeries being embedded does not present an obstruction as the hypersurfaces allow bicollars. ∎

The property of only allowing product cobordisms in the boundary is somewhat contrived. One setting where it arises naturally are 3-manifolds

with

\partial M

This leads to our motivating example when

is the complement of an open tubular neighborhood of a link

and

. To align terminology, we call a surgery of index 1 on a hypersurface a

The last step is to bridge the gap between Seifert surfaces with boundary of a link in the interior of a manifold and properly embedded hypersurfaces by removing small enough tubular neighborhood of the link. To ensure that the resulting hypersurfaces represent the same homology class, we use that the surrounding manifold is a rational homology sphere.

Corollary 4.4

(Tube equivalence for Seifert surfaces)
.

Let
A
,
B
be Seifert surfaces for a link
L
in a rational homology sphere
X
. Then
B
arises from
A
by a sequence of smooth isotopies, tube attachments and tube removals with the tube attachments occurring before the tube removals. In particular, all intermediate surfaces are also Seifert surfaces.

Proof.

After a smooth isotopy of
A
and
B
we may assume that there exists a tubular neighborhood
\tau\colon L\times\mathbb{D}^{2}\to X
such that

\displaystyle A\cap\tau(L\times\mathbb{D}^{2})

\displaystyle=\tau(L\times\{t\cdot a\mid 0\leq t\leq 1\})

\displaystyle B\cap\tau(L\times\mathbb{D}^{2})

\displaystyle=\tau(L\times\{t\cdot b\mid 0\leq t\leq 1\})

for distinct
a,b\in\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}
. Then
M\coloneqq X\setminus\tau(L\times\mathring{\mathbb{D}}^{2})
is a connected 3-dimensional manifold with toroidal boundary and
\hat{A}\coloneqq M\cap A
,
\hat{B}\coloneqq M\cap B
are connected spanning hypersurfaces in
M
.

To apply
4.3
it remains to show that
[\hat{A}]=[\hat{B}]
in
\operatorname{H}_{2}(M,\partial M)
. As
\partial\hat{A}
and
\partial\hat{B}
are smoothly isotopic in
\partial M
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{H}_{2}(M,\partial M)\to\operatorname{H}_{1}(\partial M)
is injective. Since both are free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules, we may consider rational coefficients where we have an exact sequence

0\!\to\!\operatorname{H}_{3}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Q})\!\to\!\operatorname{H}_{2}(\partial M;\mathbb{Q})\!\to\!\operatorname{H}_{2}(M;\mathbb{Q})\!\to\!\operatorname{H}_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Q})\!\to\!\operatorname{H}_{1}(\partial M;\mathbb{Q}).

Note that

–

\dim\operatorname{H}_{3}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Q})=1
,

–

\dim\operatorname{H}_{2}(\partial M;\mathbb{Q})=k
, where
k
is the number of components of
L
, and

–

\dim\operatorname{H}_{2}(M;\mathbb{Q})=k-1
by Alexander duality in the rational homology sphere
X
.

Hence,
\operatorname{H}_{2}(\partial M;\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{H}_{2}(M;\mathbb{Q})
is surjective and
\operatorname{H}_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{H}_{1}(\partial M;\mathbb{Q})
is injective as desired.
∎
