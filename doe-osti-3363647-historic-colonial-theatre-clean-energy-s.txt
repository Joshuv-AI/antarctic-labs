 Executive Summary
Motivation
The Colonial Theatre—an historic cultural facility in Bethlehem, New Hampshire operated by
the nonprofit Bethlehem Redevelopment Association (BRA) d/b/a Friends of The Colonial—
undertook this project to strengthen building resilience and long-term operational sustainability
through energy infrastructure modernization. The Theatre’s aging roof created operational risk
(water intrusion, structural uncertainty, and potential interruption to public programming), while
rising electricity costs created ongoing budget pressure. Together, these factors threatened the
reliability and affordability of maintaining a year-round community-serving venue.
Importance
This project demonstrates a replicable model for rural nonprofits and historic community
facilities: coordinating roof rehabilitation with rooftop solar PV deployment to address
foundational building needs while enabling long-term clean energy generation. While solar
technology is commercially mature, project significance lies in execution within a historic
structure and rural context—where contractor availability, structural conditions, and upfront
capital barriers frequently limit renewable adoption. By integrating roof work, structural
reinforcement, interconnection planning, and community engagement, the project provides a
practical roadmap for similar institutions.

Goals
BRA pursued four primary goals:
Replace the aging roof with durable, energy-efficient materials suitable for solar installation and
long-term performance.
Install a solar PV system sized to offset a meaningful portion of the facility’s electricity use.
Evaluate the technical effectiveness and financial feasibility of integrated roof + solar upgrades
in a historic nonprofit performance venue.

Deliver public benefit through reduced operating costs, reduced emissions, and expanded

Major Accomplishments
The project achieved the end-of-project goal: a fully installed, interconnected, and operational
solar array serving The Colonial Theatre. Key accomplishments include:
 • Roof replacement and building-envelope readiness: Completion of major roof work in
 July 2024, including replacement of roofing surfaces and flashing work required to
 support PV installation and protect the building long-term.
 • Structural engineering review and reinforcement: An unanticipated structural evaluation
 identified the need for additional roof reinforcement. BRA completed substantial beam
 sistering and related structural upgrades, which served as a critical go/no-go checkpoint
 and ensured safe long-term load-bearing capacity for the PV system.
 • Solar PV system installed as designed: Installation of a 37.2 kW roof-mounted solar
 photovoltaic array (93 × 400W modules) using Enphase microinverters and a rooftop
 mounting system, completed with commissioning and verified operational status.
 • Grid interconnection and monitoring: Utility interconnection and net metering approvals
 were completed, with the system confirmed operational on October 3, 2025. BRA
 established monitoring access and workflows to support system oversight and public-
 facing reporting.

Contribution to Understanding of the Area
This project adds to the practical understanding of clean energy deployment in historic,
community-serving facilities by documenting the sequencing, technical dependencies, and
decision points that determine success—particularly the importance of early structural evaluation
and roof readiness to protect the longevity of solar investments. The project reinforces sector
best practices that rooftop PV performance and lifespan are tightly linked to underlying roof
condition and structural capacity, and it provides an applied example of how nonprofit
governance, permitting, and contractor coordination can be integrated to execute a technically
sound installation in a legacy building.

Technical Effectiveness and Economic Feasibility
Technically, the project demonstrated high effectiveness by delivering the full planned PV
capacity and achieving verified commissioning, inspection approvals, and interconnection.
System design and installed equipment matched planned specifications, and monitoring
capability was established to track production and performance over time. Economically, the
project demonstrates that pairing roof replacement with PV installation can be feasible for rural
nonprofits when approached as an integrated capital investment: the roof work protects the solar
asset’s useful life, the PV system offsets electricity purchases (with projected production of
approximately 93 kWh/year), and net metering improves value capture. While precise post-
install savings will be confirmed through monitoring data over successive seasons, contractor
modeling and utility rate context indicate the system is expected to offset a meaningful portion of
annual electricity costs—supporting improved operational stability and reducing long-term
exposure to rising energy prices.

 Background
Latest Developments in Similar Technology Fields
Current state-of-the-art for PV deployment is characterized by continued cost normalization and
improved component performance, with the DOE Solar Energy Technologies Office and NREL
publishing annual PV cost benchmarks and market updates that quantify installed-cost drivers,
“soft costs,” and typical system configurations. These benchmarks are widely used to evaluate
project economic feasibility, inform design assumptions, and support credible savings
projections for commercial-scale rooftop PV.

Recent roofing-industry technical proceedings and federal guidance emphasize “solar-ready”
roof strategies that reduce lifecycle risk: coordinating roof replacement timing with PV
installation, limiting penetrations, designing safe access/maintenance pathways, and ensuring
long-term waterproofing and serviceability. This body of guidance aligns with best practice for
low-slope and hybrid roof conditions typical of older commercial and assembly-use buildings.

For rooftop PV in snow climates, modern practice increasingly centers on rigorous load-path
evaluation and roof capacity verification using nationally adopted structural loading standards
(snow/wind load provisions and load combinations). The current ASCE loading standard
(ASCE/SEI 7-22) is a key structural reference for snow and wind loads, and training/industry
guidance underscores the importance of up-front structural assessment to avoid late-stage
redesign or reinforcement.

Recent peer-reviewed work and applied frameworks address the integration of photovoltaics into
historic contexts, including criteria for visual/architectural compatibility, reversibility, and risk
management—often concluding that PV can be consistent with preservation goals when sited
and detailed appropriately. The National Park Service provides practical guidance on
sustainability/resilience measures for historic properties and solar panel placement
considerations under the Secretary of the Interior’s Standards.

Grid-interactive inverter controls and “grid-forming” research is an active area, particularly as
distributed PV grows and resilience expectations increase. Current literature summarizes
emerging grid-forming paradigms for inverter-based resources and discusses commercial devices
capable of advanced modes under certain system configurations. While many community-scale
rooftop systems remain standard grid-tied, the direction of R&D supports future add-ons such as
islanding controls or storage integration where appropriate.

How This Project Builds on Prior Work

How the published work is similar to this project
Integrated roof + PV sequencing is a widely recommended best practice: replacing or
rehabilitating the roof before PV installation protects the PV asset’s lifespan and reduces
lifecycle costs by avoiding future removal/reinstallation. This mirrors the project’s approach of
completing major roof work prior to installing the 37.2 kW array.

Structural readiness as a gating item is repeatedly emphasized in literature and standards,
particularly for older buildings and snow climates. The project’s engineering review and

subsequent reinforcement aligns with state-of-practice expectations that PV design must be
matched to verified structural capacity and code-based load assumptions.

Historic-context integration is consistent with recent heritage-focused frameworks that
prioritize minimizing visual impact, limiting harm to historic fabric, and ensuring solutions
remain maintainable and reversible where possible.

How the published work is different from this project
Much of the PV literature focuses on technology development (modules, controls, power
electronics) or large portfolios of buildings, whereas this project is a full-scale implementation
in a single historic, rural, community-serving performance venue, where practical constraints
(roof condition, limited contractors, permitting cadence, seasonal access, nonprofit capital
planning) dominate schedule and risk. (This is a key differentiator versus lab- or portfolio-scale
studies.)

Many heritage PV studies emphasize BIPV (photovoltaics integrated into building materials) as
an aesthetic solution; this project used building-applied PV on a rehabilitated roof, prioritizing
constructability, cost control, and proven performance rather than experimental envelope
products.

Grid-forming inverter research is rapidly evolving, but this project’s core requirement was
reliable grid-tied operation with monitoring and net metering, not experimental microgrid
operation. The project nonetheless positioned itself for future resilience enhancements (e.g.,
storage) as suggested by broader inverter-control R&D trajectories.

How this project leveraged published work to accelerate timeline and improve quality
Economic feasibility and design assumptions were strengthened by using authoritative cost
and market benchmark references to validate vendor pricing, set realistic expectations about
savings drivers, and ensure the financial narrative aligns with national datasets and SETO/NREL
modeling practices.

Risk reduction through “solar-ready roof” practices: the project’s coordinated roof
replacement, attention to roof service life, and planning for safe PV access/maintenance reflect
the best-practice guidance intended to reduce leakage risk, minimize rework, and improve long-
run O&M outcomes.

Higher confidence commissioning and long-term performance: aligning installation and
verification with current best practices for monitoring/inspection and emphasizing
maintainability supports the same reliability objectives highlighted in roofing + PV durability
guidance and federal training materials.

Historic-property compliance and public-facing acceptability: the project’s approach is
consistent with current preservation guidance that encourages sustainability improvements when
they avoid undue impact to character-defining features—helping streamline stakeholder
acceptance and reduce the risk of late-stage design changes.

 Project Objectives
National Goals Alignment
This project directly advances national clean energy and economic development goals by
installing a 37.2 kW roof-mounted solar photovoltaic array on a historic community facility,
reducing reliance on grid electricity and supporting long-term adoption of renewable energy at
the local level. By transitioning The Colonial Theatre to onsite renewable power generation, the
project supports U.S. priorities around decarbonization, energy independence, and expanded
clean energy infrastructure—particularly in rural communities where renewable adoption can be
limited by cost and contractor availability. The project also supports economic benefit by
strengthening the financial sustainability of a nonprofit cultural institution that draws visitors to
Bethlehem and the surrounding region, supporting local businesses and tourism-driven economic
activity. The installation required skilled labor, permitting, electrical work, and procurement of
U.S. energy-related services and equipment, contributing to the clean energy economy and
regional workforce activity

Project Goals & Expected Outcomes
The goals of the project were to complete necessary roof repair and preparation and implement
full installation, interconnection, and testing of a solar PV system that would reduce overhead
electricity costs, promote sustainable energy generation, and enable The Colonial to responsibly
extend its presenting season. Expected outcomes include reduced electricity expenses
(potentially offsetting up to six months of in-season electricity costs), increased operational
resilience through energy independence, and the ability to sustain expanded year-round
programming. The significance of the work is that it applies proven clean energy technology to a
historic building in a rural setting, demonstrating that renewable infrastructure can be integrated
into legacy community facilities while supporting both environmental and economic
sustainability. While the technology itself is not experimental, the project represents a
meaningful and replicable model for rural nonprofit institutions seeking to modernize
infrastructure, reduce carbon footprint, and stabilize operating costs in order to preserve
community-serving services.

The project scope was organized into five sequential tasks. Task 1.0 (Roof Repair, Months 1–
14) included interim patching and full replacement/repair of the upper rubber roof and flashing to
ensure the roof could support the array. Task 2.0 (Identify Solar Contractor, Months 1–10)
included evaluating bids and selecting a qualified solar contractor. Task 3.0 (Solar Array
Design, Months 10–13) included system design, electrical upgrades, permitting, and
interconnection/net metering negotiations, with a milestone confirming full permissions and
board sign-off. Task 4.0 (Solar Installation, Months 14–15) included scheduling installation,
ordering materials, and installing the solar panels and mounting system. Task 5.0 (Inspection,
Interconnection, and System Testing, Months 15–17) included inspections, grid
interconnection, final testing, and delivery of a maintenance plan, culminating in the end-of-
project goal of a fully operational solar array integrated into the local power grid. Key milestones
included completion of roof repair (Milestone 1.1.1), selection of a roofing contractor (Milestone
1.2.1), completion of roof readiness for solar (Milestone 1.3.1), selection of a solar contractor
(Milestone 2.1.1), board finalization of system feasibility and scope (Milestone 3.1.1), receipt of
permits and interconnection approval (Milestone 3.3.1), completion of array installation
(Milestone 4.3.1), and final system operational verification as the end-of-project goal. No

Significance & Innovation
The significance of this project is that it demonstrates how clean energy infrastructure can be
successfully implemented in a historic, community-serving facility in a rural region—an
environment where high upfront costs, aging infrastructure, and limited contractor availability
often create barriers to renewable energy adoption. By integrating a 37.2 kW solar PV array into
The Colonial Theatre’s operations, this project advances national clean energy goals by reducing
reliance on grid electricity and supporting a measurable transition toward distributed renewable
generation. It also strengthens the long-term resilience of a nonprofit institution that serves as a
cultural and economic anchor for the North Country, helping ensure that community arts and
public gathering spaces can remain viable as energy costs rise.

While the technology itself is commercially available, the innovation of this project lies in its
application and replicability: it combines roof rehabilitation, solar installation, and grid
interconnection planning into a single coordinated approach tailored to a historic building with
unique structural and permitting considerations. The project demonstrates an effective model for
other rural nonprofits and legacy community facilities seeking to modernize infrastructure while
preserving historic character. Additionally, the project supports fundamental advances in rural
clean energy progression by showing how energy investments can unlock broader community
outcomes—such as reduced operational overhead, expanded year-round public programming,
and local economic activity—thereby linking clean energy implementation to sustainable
community development.

 Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
Under this award, the most significant accomplishment was the successful completion of a fully
installed and operational 37.2 kW roof-mounted solar photovoltaic system on The Colonial
Theatre, a historic community facility in rural New Hampshire. This accomplishment is
significant because it represents a major infrastructure investment that will reduce long-term
operating costs, improve organizational sustainability, and directly support the organization’s
ability to expand year-round programming and community access. The project also demonstrates
the feasibility of integrating modern clean energy technology into an older building with unique
structural requirements, creating a replicable model for similar rural nonprofit and historic
facilities. The project achieved its final milestone with full utility interconnection on October 3,
2025, confirming successful completion of installation, inspection, and commissioning activities.

A second major accomplishment was the completion of substantial roof repair work in July
2024, ensuring the building envelope was stable and fully prepared to support solar installation.
Completing roof improvements prior to installation was essential to protect the longevity of the
solar investment and prevent future damage or rework. This work also strengthened the overall
resilience of the building itself, which is a critical asset to the community and the organization’s
mission. The sequencing of roof repair followed by solar installation ensured the system could be
installed safely and in accordance with best practices.

                                           Page 20 of 23

The most significant challenge encountered during the award period was a required structural
engineering review of the interior roofing structure, which functioned as an unanticipated go/no-
go technical checkpoint. The review determined that the roof structure required significant
reinforcement through additional sistering of beams in order to safely support the solar array.
This additional structural work increased complexity and required additional time and
coordination before installation could proceed. While this challenge delayed progress relative to
the original anticipated timeline, it resulted in a positive outcome by ensuring the installation met
structural safety standards and protecting both the building and the solar investment over the
long term. This challenge also reinforced the importance of early structural evaluation when
planning renewable energy installations on older or historic buildings.

No reduction in scope occurred as a result of this engineering challenge. The project was
ultimately completed at the full planned capacity and configuration, with the system installed “as
planned” and fully interconnected. The key lesson learned from this process is that historic
structures can present hidden structural limitations that may not be fully apparent until formal
engineering review occurs, and that project timelines should include contingencies for structural
reinforcement or design modifications when working with legacy infrastructure. This experience
strengthened project management practices for future capital work by underscoring the value of
early technical due diligence, clear sequencing of dependencies, and strong contractor
coordination.

 Path Forward
Into the future, BRA will continue to monitor the PV system for performance and energy savings
in order to plan for programmatic growth commensurate with the building’s technical ability to
sustain those programs affordably. BRA is also implementing a number of other energy
efficiency upgrades, including updating sound, lighting, and projection equipment, converting to
LED lighting power wherever possible, replacing external doors and windows, assessing the
need for further insulation, and optimizing the building’s HVAC system. BRA may also explore
battery storage options to further enhance the resilience of the PV system. BRA also plans to
share project learnings with other community nonprofits and stakeholders; we have already
shared a public version of our solar monitoring system so the public can track and learn about
our efficiency, and we have been open and communicated with at least two similar nonprofit
organizations looking to complete similar projects to offer advice.

 Products
