TPRG-01 TRIO-MBM: A MULTICENTER, PHASE II TRIAL OF RELATLIMAB (RELA), NIVOLUMAB (NIVO), AND IPILIMUMAB (IPI) IN PATIENTS WITH ASYMPTOMATIC AND SYMPTOMATIC MELANOMA BRAIN METASTASES
Authors: Allison Betof, Haley Hedlin, Phuong Pham, Elizabeth Park, Divya Pathak, Pauline Funchain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMelanoma brain metastases (MBM) are a leading cause of morbidity and mortality for patients (pts) with advanced melanoma. Modern systemic therapies (tx) including immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) are insufficient at controlling MBM, particularly in pts with who are symptomatic and/or require corticosteroids. CheckMate 204 established nivolumab (NIVO) 1 mg/kg + ipilimumab (IPI) 3mg/kg followed by maintenance NIVO as the standard tx for pts with MBM based on intracranial (IC) ORR of 55% and 6-month PFS of 64% in asymptomatic pts. In symptomatic pts, intracranial benefit rate (iCBR) was only 22% and median PFS was 1.2 mos. Adding anti-LAG-3 with relatlimab (RELA) is being explored to improve efficacy. Arm 2B of RELATIVITY-048 (NIVO 480 mg Q4W + RELA 160 mg Q4W + IPI 1 mg/kg Q8W) reported promising efficacy (ORR 59%, 3y PFS 52%) and 2/3 (67%) pts with MBM had confirmed responses. We hypothesize that triplet ICB will be more effective than NIVO+IPI for the tx of symptomatic and asymptomatic MBM. In this multicenter, investigator-initiated, phase II study (NCT06712927) pts with MBM will be assigned to one of two cohorts: Cohort A (asymptomatic, n = 40) and Cohort B (symptomatic, n = 20) defined as having symptoms attributable to MBM and/or requiring steroids >10 mg prednisone/day or equivalent. All pts will receive NIVO 480mg + RELA 160mg IV Q4W plus IPI 1mg/kg IV Q8W. Pts must have ≥1 untreated intracranial lesion (5-40mm) and no prior ICB for unresectable stage III/IV melanoma (>6 mo since neoadjuvant/adjuvant ICB). Primary endpoint is iCBR (CR+PR+SD ≥6 mo) determined by modified RECIST 1.1; secondary endpoints include safety, ORR, duration of response, and PFS. Extensive correlative analyses of peripheral blood mononuclear cells, plasma, tumors, and cerebrospinal fluid are planned. The study is currently enrolling at Stanford Cancer Institute with multisite expansion underway.
