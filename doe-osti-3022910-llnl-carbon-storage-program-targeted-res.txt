 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The project is organized in two tasks which are advanced simultaneously. Below, technical progress are presented for each
task.

Task 1: Development of high-resolution induced seismicity hazard modeling tools
The main goal of this task is to develop a coupled poromechanical-earthquake simulator, representing a unique and novel
capability to perform high-resolution, high-fidelity, fully coupled simulations of induced seismicity by coupling the most
advanced numerical models for reservoir physics with an earthquake model. This capability has been implemented within
the open-source GEOS framework (www.geos.dev) to model induced seismicity at geologic carbon sequestration (GCS)
sites. GEOS is a Multiphysics simulator developed at LLNL in collaboration with industrial and academic partners, with a
focus on modeling subsurface processes.

To date, substantial progress has been achieved in implementing a novel strategy for coupled poromechanical and quasi-
dynamic earthquake simulations in GEOS. In particular, we have:

 - Developed, implemented, and validated a strategy for explicitly modeling faults in poromechanical simulations.
 This approach employs a low-order finite element discretization for mechanics, coupled with a finite volume
 discretization for flow equations. Faults are treated as lower-dimensional manifolds, with surface elements
 enforcing contact constraints via face-based, piecewise-constant Lagrange multipliers (i.e., tractions). To stabilize
 the formulation, face bubble functions are used to enrich the solution space. (Milestone 1.1.1).
 - Implemented a rate- and state-dependent friction law, incorporating both the slip and aging formulations.
        (Milestone 1.2.1).
 - Developed a quasi-dynamic earthquake solver based on the assumption that fault friction follows a rate- and
 state-dependent law. The solver has been validated using the classical spring-slider benchmark. Time integration
 is supported via both implicit Euler discretization and an embedded explicit Runge-Kutta method with adaptive
 error control. (Milestone 1.2.2)
 - Integrated a new physics module that couples GEOS’s quasi-static mechanical and poromechanical solvers with
 the quasi-dynamic earthquake model. The module supports both sequential implicit coupling and the embedded
 explicit Runge-Kutta integrator.

 The progress made so far is aligned with the scope of work and the timeline outlined in the SOPO.

Task 2: Development of hollow-core fiber optic gas sensors for CO2 containment assurance
In Y3Q2, we focused on the optimization of laser hole-drilling on Hollow Core Fibers (HoF), investigation of Hof integrity
under elevated temperature/pressure and brine environmental conditions, testing of fiber splicing repeatability and
optical throughput measurement, and implementation of optical test setup in preparation for incorporating FBG (or
retroreflector) with HoFs. We have:

 - Optimized drill hole parameter through systematic simulation study of optical throughput and experimental
 investigation of laser hole-drilling parameters. (Milestone 2.1.2a).
 - Investigated optical transmission of HoFs under relevant temperature, pressure and brine conditions in
 collaboration with Dr. Ruishu Wright’s group at NETL. Specifically, a reflectometer is used to monitor the back
 reflection of light from the HoF fiber end fact while the HoF is immersed in a chamber set to various relevant
 environmental conditions. The results show that the optical transmission characteristics of the HoF is largely
 maintained. (Milestone 2.1.2b and Milestone 2.4).

- Validated repeatability of HoF-solid core splicing and measured optical throughput of splices, showing high
 throughput of ~60% is achieved. (Milestone 2.2.1).
- Implemented and validated a reflection mode operation setup for testing spliced HoF-FBG. (Milestone 2.2.2).
- Met with relevant parties at Colorado School of Mines and Carbon Farm at LLNL in preparation for field testing.

Task 2: Development of hollow-core fiber optic gas sensors for CO2 containment assurance
Our overall goal is to demonstrate the value of Raman interrogation within gas-filled holey fibers (HoFs), so that the
location and concentration of the gases would be provided simultaneously via backscattering. Infrared (IR) Spectroscopy
will also be used with Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG) to enable reflections to the I/O controls and timestamp the leakage
location. We have reached the proposed milestones by their due date and expect to complete the rest as expected by
end of FY25.

The main activities and achievements associated with the listed milestones are as follows
 - Main initial critical milestones 2.1.1.a and 2.1.2a have been reached by demonstrating Raman uptake rate and
 concentration limit studies with HoFs with drilled slots optimization. We have reached 400ppm LOD with uptake
 time reduced from hours to minutes on a 1m drilled HoF, but we have continued any relevant optimization. We
 have developed a Lumerical based model to predict and optimize the optical performances of drilled fibers, as a
 function of size, depth, and distribution of the drilled holes along the HoF; we also developed a COMSOL model

 5

 for the gas diffusion in the experimental setup box and the HoF. Both models greatly helped build confidence of
 our experimental setup and expectations, from design optimization to data validation.
 - The characterization of HoF in harsh environments and brine conditions has been successful, validating the
 mechanical and optical stability of the HoFs. We are continuing to study these aspects to collect more statistics
 on data, even if we have already reached both milestones 2.1.2b and 2.4.
 - We were able detect other molecules such as O2 and N2 within CO2 measurements in air, and separately also
 measured gaseous H2 <100pm within 10 secs, with a slightly different fiber configuration, reaching milestone
 2.1.1.b. We have been unable so far to measure CH4 due to lab safety constraints.
 - Splicing recipes to merge solid core and HoF for fielding the system have been successfully developed and used
 to repeatably fabricated splices with <3dB loss. Even though we reached milestone 2.2.1, there is room for
 optimization which we are undertaking.
 - To improve the signal, we have been investigating FBGs. At this stage we believe that we can achieve better
 reflection with an integrated retroflector on the sliced solid core fiber that will be tested in the new setup arranged
 to measure in reflection mode rather transmission (with the advantage of increasing our SNR and decrease the
 LOD and uptake time). We believe that milestone 2.2.2 will be reached by the end of this year, replacing the
 spliced FBG with a spliced fiber customized with an integrated retroreflector.
 - The field testing (milestone 2.3) is being addressed, and we are actively interacting with internal and external
 collaborators to partner in the deployment of a simple system consisting of a stretch of a drilled ~1m HoF
 sandwiched between spliced solid core fiber in more realistic environments with controlled leakages (i.e. shallow
 wells, pipelines at Colorado Mines and carbon farm in LLNL).

Plans for next quarter:

 - Optimize splicing to reduce loss by half
 - Optimize back reflection setups (we have already shown an improvement of 5x in the Raman signal and we expect
 to be at least 10x)
 - Test a drilled 1m HoF at either the LLNL Carbon Farm or Colorado Mines Edgar Mine (complete milestone 2.3)
