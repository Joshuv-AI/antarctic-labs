Counting and Sampling Anti-Ferromagnetic Potts Models on Random Regular Bipartite Graphs in the Non-uniqueness Regime
Zhidan Li, Siyu Liu, Kuan Yang

Abstract
. The anti-ferromagnetic multi-state Potts model, a generalization of the Ising model, is one of the most fundamental models in statistical physics. It was conjectured by Kotecký (Phys. Rev. B, 1985) that the model undergoes a phase transition from a disordered phase at infinite temperature to an ordered phase at sufficiently low temperature on lattices. Such phase transitions are believed to play an important role in computational complexity theory and remain closely connected to the problem of approximating the partition function of the system. For proper three-coloring models (corresponding to the zero-temperature), torpid mixing of a family of local-update Markov chains on lattices was established by Galvin, Kahn, Randall and Sorkin (SIDMA, 2015), coinciding with the presence of phase coexistence following shown by Feldheim and Spinka (J. Eur. Math. Soc., 2019). In this work, we study approximating the partition function of the anti-ferromagnetic multi-state Potts model at low temperature on random regular bipartite graphs, which are with high probability good bipartite expanders. On the negative side, we generalize the result by Geisler, Kang, Sarantis and Wdowinski (arXiv, 2026) for anti-ferromagnetic Ising models to show that when the temperature is sufficiently low relative to the degree of the underlying graph, the celebrated single-site Glauber dynamics has exponentially slow mixing time. On the positive side, we design a deterministic algorithm that yields an approximation to the partition function of the model via the framework of abstract polymer models as Jenssen, Keevash and Perkins (SICOMP, 2020), Liao, Lin, Lu and Mao (Theor. Comput. Sci., 2022), Galanis, Goldberg and Stewart (TOCT, 2021) and Geisler, Kang, Sarantis and Wdowinski (arXiv, 2026).

The
q
-state Potts model (

Formally, given a ground state

A

(1)

\displaystyle w(\sigma):=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta H(\sigma)}.

Let the state space or configuration space

When

-Potts model

We study the

Namely, for a graph

by the number of monochromatic edges in

(2)

\displaystyle\forall\sigma\in\Omega,\quad H(\sigma)=m_{G}(\sigma):=\ab|\left\{(u,v)\in E\;\colon\;\sigma|_{u}=\sigma|_{v}\right\}|.

The Gibbs distribution induced by the anti-ferromagnetic

-Potts model on

\displaystyle\forall\sigma\in\Omega,\quad\mu(\sigma)=\frac{w(\sigma)}{Z_{G,q}(\beta)}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta m_{G}(\sigma)}}{Z_{G,q}(\beta)}

For example, when the temperature is zero (

Thus we can view the proper vertex-coloring model as a special case of anti-ferromagnetic

A central problem to study the anti-ferromagnetic Potts model is the computation of the partition function

Unfortunately, the exact estimation to

Therefore, a natural problem is arising for computing Potts models:

Is there an efficient algorithm giving an approximation to the partition function of the Potts model, either a deterministic one or a randomized one?

with probability at least
3/4

An important topic closely related to the design of approximate algorithms is the

Consider the parameter

\displaystyle\beta_{c}(q,\Delta):=\ln{\max\set{1, \frac{\Delta}{\Delta- q}}}.

It is a folklore conjecture that the Gibbs distribution on

Such characterization of the uniqueness threshold on the infinite

To design an approximate algorithm in the tree-uniqueness threshold, Lu and Yin

Afterwards in the study of the zero-free region of the proper vertex-coloring model, Liu, Sinclair and Srivastava

, leading to an

The result of the zero-freeness region is recently improved by

For an efficient sampler, Blanca et al.

In the non-uniqueness threshold, Galanis, Štefankovič and Vigoda

However, despite the hardness result, there might be different cases when the anti-ferromagnetic Potts model is defined on a bipartite graph.
When the model is defined on lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{\Delta}

for anti-ferromagnetic Potts models.
Briefly speaking, when the temperature goes from

It is following answered by Feldheim and Spinka

The existence of long-range order might give some inspiration to the design of algorithms.
On one hand, Galvin et al.

On the other hand, Jenssen, Keevash and Perkins

shows that for

Similar techniques are applied to general spin systems (including both ferromagnetic and anti-ferromagnetic Potts models) by Galanis, Goldberg and Stewart

\Delta
-regular bipartite graphs, with a harder constraint
\Delta/\ln^{4}{\Delta}=\Omega((q\ln{q})^{4})
on
\Delta

.
It still needs a deep understanding on whether the existence of such multiple phases indicates an efficient (randomized) approximation scheme.

1.1.
Main results

We focus on anti-ferromagnetic Potts models on
random regular bipartite graphs
at sufficiently low temperature.
The probabilistic model of random regular bipartite graphs exhibits a special combinatorial structure.
It can be shown that wiht high probability, the random regular bipartite graph is of good expansion (
15
;
7
;
20
).
We will discuss details in
Section

Firstly we show the slow mixing of single-site Glauber dynamics.
The Glauber dynamics
P^{\mathsf{GD}}
is one of the most celebrated Markov chains with local updates.
To sample from a target distribution
\pi
on
\Omega

X_{0}\in\Omega

X_{t}
as following:

•

•

X_{t+1}\sim\pi(\cdot\;|\;X_{t+1}(V\setminus\left\{v\right\})=X_{t}(V\setminus\left\{v\right\}))
.

We show that it has an exponentially slow mixing time on the anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on random bipartite regular graphs at sufficiently low temperature.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist absolute constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that, for every positive integers
q,\Delta\geq 3
and a real number
\beta>0
satisfying
\Delta>C_{1}q\ln{q}
and
\beta>C_{2}q^{2}/\Delta
, the following holds with high probability over
G
drawn from all random
\Delta
-regular bipartite
2n
-vertex graphs for all sufficiently large positive integers

.
The Glauber dynamics

for the anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on
G
at inverse temperature

has a mixing time at least
\mathrm{e}^{Tn}
for some factor
T=T(q,\Delta,\beta)>0
.

Despite the slow mixing time (
Theorem

1.1
), our second result shows that there exists an efficient deterministic algorithm to output an approximation to the partition function of the anti-ferromagnetic Potts models on random bipartite regular graphs at low temperature.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist a positive integer

and a real number

such that for positive integers
q\geq q_{0}
,
\Delta\geq 4
and a real number

satisfying that
\Delta(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})\geq C(q\ln{(q\Delta/(1 - \e^{-\beta}))})^{2}
and
\Delta\beta(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})^{2}\geq C(q\ln{(q\Delta/(1 - \e^{-\beta}))})^{2}

\Delta

There exists a deterministic algorithm to output

Existence of ordered phases

To show

A key observation is that, at sufficiently low temperature, most of the configurations follow one of typical ‘coloring patterns’, similar to the anti-ferromagnetic
3
-states case on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{\Delta}
in

6

Based on this kind of observation, we apply the concept of patterns in

3

3

We note here that the concept of patterns in

.
to give a description of ordered phases and provide some properties which play crucial roles in our proof.

Conductance

The conductance of Markov chains, introduced by Sinclair and Jerrum
25
, has been widely used to show both rapid and torpid mixing of a Markov chain.
By a type of the Cheeger’s inequality, there exists a bridge between the conductance of a given Markov chain and the second largest eigenvalue of the Laplacian matrix of it, leading to both upper and lower bound to its mixing time.
For anti-ferromagnetic Ising models (
q=2
) on a family of regular bipartite expander graphs, a recent work by Geisler et al.
13
has shown that the conductance of Glauber dynamics is exponentially small, and thus the mixing time is exponential in the size of the instance.

Like the torpid mixing of
P^{\mathsf{GD}}
on the anti-ferromagnetic Ising case in
13
, to show
Theorem

1.1
, we apply the method of conductance.
We show how to find a subset of the state space with small conductance and apply a simpler lower bound for the mixing time by Levin and Peres
18
than the one induced by the Cheeger’s inequality.
To find the very subset, we also prove some useful results to describe the geometry of the anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on random regular bipartite graphs.

Abstract polymer models

The
abstract polymer model
together with
the cluster expansion
is a useful tool to study statistical mechanics models.
In the field of theoretic computer science, the abstract polymer model has been successfully used in the design of
FPTAS
or
FPRAS
to the hard-core models (
15
;
5
;
20
), ferromagnetic Potts models (

;
5
;
8
) on (bipartite) expander graphs, general spin systems (

We follow the methodology in

Based on our description of ordered phases, we specify each configuration in

Therefore, for each pattern, we design a suitable abstract polymer model and follow a routine way to obtain an efficient approximation scheme to the partition function by verifying the Kotecký-Preiss condition.

1.3.
Comparison with related works

The torpid mixing of Glauber dynamics has been recently shown on the anti-ferromagnetic Ising models with uniform external fields by
13
on bipartite expander graphs.
Their result for the slow mixing time relies on an observation that the set of configurations which is ‘balanced’ in some way owns a exponentially small weight.
In this work, we aim to show a similar property for the anti-ferromagnetic Potts models on random regular bipartite graphs which are ‘good expanders’ with high probability.
The challenge arises from the difficulty to define an appropriate collection of ‘balanced configurations’ and upper bound their total weights.
To overcome this, we investigate the geometry of the configuration space and show that the configurations are highly concentrated around some specific ‘ground states’.
Therefore, we are able to define a proper ‘boundary’ and show the small conductance of the Glauber dynamics.

For approximately counting the anti-ferromagnetic Potts models on random regular bipartite graphs, it has been shown in
15
that the state space can be viewed as a union of configurations specified by different coloring ‘patterns’ representing assigning almostly disjoint colors to each side for proper vertex-colorings.
For the soft-constrained models, Galanis et al.
7
apply maximal bi-cliques which is a generalization of patterns and has been successfully used to design an efficient sampler on the ferromagnetic case in
5
.
As a result, they design an
FPRAS
for counting the anti-ferromagnetic Potts models on random regular bipartite graphs, with a strong constraint on the relation between the degree
\Delta
and the number of colors
q
.
To loose the constraint on
\Delta
with respect to
q
, we consider the concept of patterns in
15
to adapt anti-ferromagnetic Potts models and provide a
deterministic
algorithm to approximate the partition function.

We use the following lemma in
15
that a random regular bipartite graph is a bipartite expander with high probability.

Lemma 2.3

(Lemma 23 in
15
)
.

For sufficiently large
\Delta

G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\rho,\alpha}

and

We also need the following expansion derived by the spectral method in

Lemma 2.4

(
7
)
.

For a
\Delta
-regular bipartite graph

such that the second largest eigenvalue of

satisfies that

and
S\subseteq{\mathcal{R}}

\displaystyle\ab|N_{G}(S)|\geq\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+(\Delta^{2}-\lambda^{2})\ab|S|/n}\ab|S|.

The following vertex expansion is a direct corollary of

Corollary 2.5

For positive integers
\Delta\geq 4

.
For every
\theta\in(0,1)
and a vertex subset

such that

\displaystyle\ab|N_{G}(S)|\geq\ab(\frac{\Delta}{4+(\Delta-4)\theta}-1)\ab|S|.

Proof.

We use the result in
4
to show that with high probability over
G=(V={\mathcal{L}}\sqcup{\mathcal{R}},E)\sim{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\mathsf{bip}}

.
Then by
Lemma

2.4
, for every subset
A\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}
and

\displaystyle\ab|N_{G}(A)|\geq\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4\Delta+(\Delta^{2}-4\Delta)\ab|A|/n}\ab|A|=\frac{\Delta}{4+(\Delta-4)\ab|A|/n}\,\ab|A|.

Then for every

\ab|S|\leq\theta n

\displaystyle\ab|N_{G}(S\cap{\mathcal{L}})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\Delta}{4+(\Delta-4)\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{L}}|/n}\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{L}}|\geq\frac{\Delta}{4+(\Delta-4)\theta}\,\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{L}}|,

2.3.
Markov chains

We state here some basic definitions and lemmas for Markov chains and for more details, please see
18
.

For a state space
\Omega
, a Markov chain
M
consists of a transition kernel
P
on
\Omega
with its stationary distribution
\pi
.
For brevity, we sometimes use the transition matrix
P
to denote the Markov chain
M
.
In this work, we only consider
ergodic
Markov chains (see details in
18
), and for an ergodic Markov chain
M
, we say it is reversible with respect to
\pi
if the detailed balanced equation holds:

(4)

\displaystyle\forall\sigma,\tau\in\Omega,\quad\pi(\sigma)P(\sigma,\tau)=\pi(\tau)P(\tau,\sigma).

Recall the Glauber dynamics
P^{\mathsf{GD}}
with its stationary distribution
\pi
.
It is direct to verify the following fact.

Fact 2.6
.

The single-site Glauber dynamics
P^{\mathsf{GD}}
is reversible w.r.t. its stationary distribution
\pi
.

Proof.

We verify (
4
) for
P^{\mathsf{GD}}
and
\pi
and it is direct to show the fact.
∎

We put our attention on the
mixing time
of Markov chains.
For two probability distributions
\mu,\nu
on
\Omega
, the
total variation distance
is defined as

\displaystyle\ab\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\Omega}\ab|\mu(x)-\nu(x)|=\max_{\Lambda\subseteq\Omega}\ab|\mu(\Lambda)-\nu(\Lambda)|.

For a positive real
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, define the
mixing time
of
M
with error
\varepsilon
as

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(M,\varepsilon):=\inf\left\{t>0\;\colon\;\forall x\in\Omega,\ab\|P^{t}(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

We refer to the value of
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(M,\cdot)
when
\varepsilon=1/4
as the mixing time of
M
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(M):=\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(M,1/4).

For
\sigma,\tau\in\Omega
, we define
Q(\sigma\to\tau):=\pi(\sigma)P(\sigma,\tau)
and for two subsets
\Lambda,\Pi\subseteq\Omega
, we define

\displaystyle Q(\Lambda\to\Pi):=\sum_{\sigma\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T}Q(\sigma\to\tau).

The
conductance
of a subset
\Lambda\subseteq\Omega
is defined as:

\displaystyle\Phi_{M}(\Lambda):=\frac{Q(\Lambda\to(\Omega\setminus\Lambda))}{\pi(\Lambda)}

and the conductance of
M
is defined by

\displaystyle\Phi_{M}:=\inf_{\Lambda\subseteq\Omega:0<\pi(\Lambda)\leq 1/2}\Phi_{M}(\Lambda).

The following lemma shows that the mixing time can be lower bounded by the conductance.

Lemma 2.7

(
(
18
, Theorem 7.4)
)
.

For an ergodic Markov chain
M
, it holds that

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(M)\geq\frac{1}{4\Phi_{M}}.

Abstract polymer model

In this part, we introduce the
abstract polymer model
.
The abstract polymer model
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{C}},\sim)
consists of the collection
{\mathcal{C}}
of
abstract polymers
and a binary relation
\sim\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{C}}
.
A polymer
\gamma=(V_{\gamma},w_{\gamma})
is a subset
V_{\gamma}\subseteq V
equipped with a weight function
w_{\gamma}
.
With abuse of notation, we sometimes use
\gamma
to denote
V_{\gamma}
and set the size
\ab|\gamma|:=\ab|V_{\gamma}|
.
For two polymers
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
, we say
\gamma_{1}
is
compatible
with
\gamma_{2}
if
\gamma_{1}\not\sim\gamma_{2}
and otherwise they are
incompatible
.
We denote by
{\mathcal{K}}
the collection of finite subsets
\Gamma
of
{\mathcal{C}}
such that polymers in
\Gamma
are mutually compatible.
Then we define the partition function of the abstract polymer model
{\mathcal{M}}
as

\displaystyle\Xi({\mathcal{M}}):=\sum_{\Gamma\in{\mathcal{K}}}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}w_{\gamma}.

Example: abstract polymer models induced by a graph

Mostly we consider the abstract polymer model induced by a graph
G
.
Then we would define a polymer
\gamma=(V_{\gamma},w_{\gamma})
via a connected subgraph
V_{\gamma}
.
We say two polymers
\gamma_{1}\sim\gamma_{2}
if
V_{\gamma_{1}}\cup V_{\gamma_{2}}
forms a connected subgraph in
G
.

The famous Kotecký-Preiss condition in
16
is of great significance to compute
\Xi
.

Condition 2.8

(Kotecký-Preiss condition)
.

We say
({\mathcal{C}},\sim)
satisfies Kotecký-Preiss condition if there exists a function
g:{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\sim\gamma}\ab|w_{\gamma^{\prime}}|\mathrm{e}^{\ab|\gamma^{\prime}|+g(\gamma^{\prime})}\leq g(\gamma).

We use the following theorem to give an
FPTAS
to
\Xi
together with
2.8
.

Theorem 2.9

(Theorem 8 in
15
)
.

Fix
\Delta>0
and a graph family
{\mathcal{G}}
of maximum degree at most
\Delta
.
Suppose that the followings hold for the polymer models induced by
{\mathcal{G}}
and a function
g(\cdot)
:

(1)

There exist two constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for every connected subgraph
\gamma
, it takes at most
\ab|\gamma|^{C_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{C_{2}\ab|\gamma|}
time to determine whether
\gamma
is in the model, and then compute
w_{\gamma}
and
g(\gamma)
.

(2)

There exists some constants
C=C(\Delta)>0
such that for every
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
and each polymer
\gamma
induced by
G
,
g(\gamma)\geq C\ab|\gamma|
.

(3)

2.8
holds with choice
g
.

Then there exists an
FPTAS
to approximate the partition function
\Xi
of the abstract polymer model induced by every graph
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
with running time
O\left((n/\varepsilon)^{\tau(\Delta)}\right)
for
\tau(\Delta)=(\ln{\Delta}+C_{2})/C(\Delta)
.

3.
Anti-ferromagnetic Potts Models on Random Regular Bipartite Graphs

In this section, we investigate the geometry of the configuration space of the anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts model.
As mentioned above, we aim to show that, at sufficiently low temperature, the anti-ferromagnetic
q
-state Potts models on
{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\mathsf{bip}}
follows some ordered phases with high probability.
Briefly, for each configuration in
\Omega
, there exists a coloring pattern such that it assigns almost disjoint colors to each side, and most of vertices agree with the pattern under the configuration.
To describe ordered phases, we formalize the definition of
patterns
as
15
.

Definition 3.1

(Patterns)
.

For a color set
[q]=\left\{1,\ldots,q\right\}
and two subsets
A,B\subseteq[q]
, we say
(A,B)
is a
pattern
if
A
and
B
form a partition of
[q]
,
i.e.
,
[q]=A\sqcup B
and
\ab|A|\neq 0
,
\ab|B|\neq 0
.
We denote by
{\mathscr{P}}
the collection of all patterns.

Recall that in
15
, we say a configuration
\sigma\in\Omega

agrees
with
(A,B)
at
v\in V
if
\sigma(v)\in A
when
v\in{\mathcal{L}}
or
\sigma(v)\in B
when
v\in{\mathcal{R}}
; otherwise we say
\sigma

disagrees
with it at
v
.
And given a pattern
(A,B)
and a vertex subset
S\subseteq V
, define
\chi_{A,B}(S)
as the collection of configurations
\sigma\in\Omega
such that
\sigma
agrees with
(A,B)
at for every
v\in V\setminus S
and disagrees with the pattern at
v\in V
.
We remark here that unlike the case in
15
, we allow that the configuration is not necessarily a proper coloring.

To capture the geometric properties of the configuration space
\Omega
, define the following rank function
\mathsf{rank}:\Omega\to\mathbb{N}
by

\displaystyle\forall\sigma\in\Omega,\quad\mathsf{rank}(\sigma)=\inf\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}:\exists(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}},S\subseteq V,\ab|S|=\ell\land\sigma\in\chi_{A,B}(S)\right\}.

Furthermore, we separate
\Omega
according to the ranks
\mathsf{rank}
as:

\displaystyle\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N},\quad\Omega^{\ell}:=\left\{\sigma\in\Omega\;\colon\;\mathsf{rank}(\sigma)=\ell\right\}

and define
\Omega^{\leq\ell}:=\cup_{t\leq\ell}\Omega^{t},\Omega^{>\ell}:=\cup_{t>\ell}\Omega^{t}
and the partition functions
Z^{\leq\ell}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega^{\leq\ell}}w(\sigma),Z^{>\ell}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega^{>\ell}}w(\sigma)
restricted on them respectively.
For brevity, given a parameter
\theta\in(0,1)
, a parameter
\theta\in(0,1)
, we say a configuration
\sigma
is of a low rank if
\mathsf{rank}(\sigma)\leq\theta n
and otherwise it has a high rank.
Moreover, for a configuration
\sigma\in\chi_{A,B}(S)
, we say
\sigma
is
specified by
(A,B)
at rank
\ab|S|
.

We define the total weight of
\chi_{A,B}(S)
by

\displaystyle W_{A,B}(S):=\sum_{\sigma\in\chi_{A,B}(S)}w(\sigma).

The following lemma gives an upper bound to
W_{A,B}(S)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every pattern
(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}
and a vertex subset
S\subseteq V
, it holds that

\displaystyle W_{A,B}(S)\leq\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}\ab(\frac{\ab|A|}{\ab|B|})^{\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{R}}|-\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}\ab(1-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{\ab|A|})^{\ab|N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}\ab(1-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{\ab|B|})^{\ab|N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{R}}|}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
(
15
, Lemma 27)
with some modifications to adapt the Potts model.
We calculate
W_{A,B}(S)
by the following steps using the multiplication rule.

•

At first we consider all partial colorings on
S
(there are at most
\ab|A|^{\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{R}}|}\ab|B|^{\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}
partial colorings of weight
1
);

•

secondly we enumerate the vertices
T\subseteq N_{G}(S)
with the same colors as their neighbors in
S
(they contribute
\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\ab|E_{G}(S,T)|}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\ab|T|}
);

•

then we consider each vertex
v\in N_{G}(S)\setminus T
(there are at most
(\ab|A|-1)^{\ab|(N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{L}})\setminus T|}(\ab|B|-1)^{\ab|(N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{R}})\setminus T|}
partial colorings of weight
1
);

•

lastly we calculate the number of colorings on
V\setminus(S\cup N_{G}(S))
; there are

\displaystyle\ab|A|^{n-\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{L}}|-\ab|N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}\ab|B|^{n-\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{R}}|-\ab|N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{R}}|}

partial colorings contributing
1
.

We combine all steps and arrange it to obtain that

\displaystyle W_{A,B}(S)

\displaystyle\leq\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}\ab(\frac{\ab|A|}{\ab|B|})^{\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{R}}|-\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}\ab(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}+\ab|A|-1}{\ab|A|})^{\ab|N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}\ab(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}+\ab|B|-1}{\ab|B|})^{\ab|N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{R}}|}

\displaystyle=\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}\ab(\frac{\ab|A|}{\ab|B|})^{\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{R}}|-\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}\ab(1-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{\ab|A|})^{\ab|N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}\ab(1-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{\ab|B|})^{\ab|N_{G}(S)\cap{\mathcal{R}}|}.

Thus we conclude the lemma.
∎

In this part, we present some properties of configurations at low ranks.

Proposition 3.4

For two different patterns

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\chi_{A,B}(S)\cap\chi_{C,D}(T)}w(\sigma)\leq\ab(\frac{q-1}{2})^{2n}q^{\ab|S|+\ab|T|}.

Proof.

The proof is implicitly stated in

7

Assume that
A

We enumerate

•

and

•

By (

Then we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\chi_{A,B}(S)\cap\chi_{C,D}(T)}w(\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq(\ab|A|-1)^{n}\ab|B|^{n}q^{\ab|S|+\ab|T|}

\displaystyle\leq\ab(\frac{\ab|A|-1+\ab|B|}{2})^{2n}q^{\ab|S|+\ab|T|}

as

\displaystyle\mu\ab(\Omega^{>\theta n})

\displaystyle\leq\mu\ab(\cup_{i\in[q]}\Omega_{i}^{\mathsf{big}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[q]}\mu\ab(\Omega_{i}^{\mathsf{big}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{i\in[q]}\ab|\Omega_{i}^{\mathsf{big}}|\exp\ab(-\beta(1-\delta)\Delta(\theta/q)^{2}n)}{Z_{G,q}(\beta)}

\displaystyle\leq q\binom{n}{(\theta/q)n}^{2}q^{2n-2(\theta/q)n}\frac{\exp\ab(-\beta(1-\delta)\Delta(\theta/q)^{2}n)}{(q/2)^{2n}}

\displaystyle\leq q\exp\ab(2nH(\theta/q)-2(\theta/q)n\ln{q}-\beta(1-\delta)\Delta(\theta/q)^{2}n+2n\ln{2})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-n/q}

where we use the inequality that
\binom{n}{k}\leq\exp(nH(k/n))
and a crude lower bound for the partition function
Z_{G,q}(\beta)\geq(q/2)^{2n}
(by considering all proper colorings).
Thus we conclude the lemma.
∎

4.
Slow Mixing of Glauber Dynamics

In this section, we show how to prove the torpid mixing of the single-site Glauber dynamics
P^{\mathsf{GD}}
for anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on random regular bipartite graphs
{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\mathsf{bip}}
(
i.e.
,
Theorem

1.1
).
Based on properties in
Section

3
, the key ingredient is to pick an appropriate subset of
\Omega
and to show that is has an exponentially small conductance.

Pick a pattern
(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}
such that
A=\left\{1,\ldots,\lceil q/2\rceil\right\}
and
B=\left\{\lceil q/2\rceil+1,\ldots,q\right\}
.
Recall the definition of
\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}
, the collection of configurations specified by
(A,B)
at low ranks:

\displaystyle\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}=\bigcup_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}\chi_{A,B}(S).

We show that with high probability over random
\Delta
-regular bipartite graphs,
\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}
has a small conductance.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given positive integers
q,\Delta\geq 3
, there exists some constant
C>0
such that for real numbers
\delta,\theta\in(0,1)
,
\beta>0
satisfying
\delta^{2}\Delta(\theta/q)>C(1-\ln{(\theta/q)})
and

\displaystyle\beta(1-\delta)\Delta(\theta/q)^{2}-2H(\theta/q)-2(1-\theta/q)\ln{q}+\ln{(\ceil{q/2}\floor{q/2})}>2/q,

the following holds with probability
1-o_{n}(1)
over
G\sim{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\mathsf{bip}}
for a sufficiently large positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
There exists a factor
T=T(q,\theta,\beta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{P^{\mathsf{GD}}}\ab(\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})\leq\mathrm{e}^{-Tn}.

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.2
, there exists some constant
C>0
such that when
\delta^{2}\Delta(\theta/q)>C(1-\ln{(\theta/q)})
and the term
n
is sufficiently large, with probability
1-o_{n}(1)
over
G\sim{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\mathsf{bip}}
, the lower bound (
3
) holds for every
S\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}
and
T\subseteq{\mathcal{R}}
with
\ab|S|,\ab|T|\geq(\theta/q)n
.
Then we assume that such statement holds.

Since
P^{\mathsf{GD}}
is reversible with respect to
\mu_{G,q;\beta}
by
2.6
, by definition of
Q(\cdot\to\cdot)
and the detailed balanced equation (
4
), it holds that

\displaystyle Q\ab(\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}\to\ab(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}}\sum_{\tau\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}}\mu(\sigma)P^{\mathsf{GD}}(\sigma,\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\mathrm{out}}{\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}}}\mu(\sigma)P^{\mathsf{GD}}(\sigma,\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\mathrm{out}}{\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}}}\mu(\tau)P^{\mathsf{GD}}(\tau,\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\tau\in\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\mathrm{out}}{\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}}}\mu(\tau)

\displaystyle=\mu\ab(\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\mathrm{out}}\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}).

Then we upper bound the conductance

\displaystyle\Phi_{P^{\mathsf{GD}}}\ab(\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})

\displaystyle=\frac{Q\ab(\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}\to\ab(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n}))}{\mu\ab(\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})}\leq\frac{\mu\ab(\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\mathrm{out}}\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})}{\mu(\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})}=\frac{w\ab(\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\mathrm{out}}\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})}{w\ab(\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})}.

By
Lemma

3.3
and a similar calculation in
Lemma

3.6
, it holds that

\displaystyle w\ab(\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\mathrm{out}}\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})

\displaystyle\leq Z^{>\theta n}+2^{q}\binom{2n}{\theta n}\binom{2n}{\theta n+1}\ab(\frac{q-1}{2})^{2n}q^{2\theta n+1}

\displaystyle\leq q\binom{n}{(\theta/q)n}^{2}q^{2n-2(\theta/q)n}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta(1-\delta)\Delta(\theta/q)^{2}n}+2^{q}\binom{2n}{\theta n}\binom{2n}{\theta n+1}\ab(\frac{q-1}{2})^{2n}q^{2\theta n+1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-T(q,\theta,\beta)n}\ab(\lceil q/2\rceil\lfloor q/2\rfloor)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-T(q,\theta,\beta)n}w\ab(\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})

for some factor
T=T(q,\theta,\beta)
and sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
by using the crude bound
w\ab(\Omega_{A,B}^{\leq\theta n})\geq\ab(\lceil q/2\rceil\lfloor q/2\rfloor)^{n}
.
Thus we conclude the lemma.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

We pick parameters

and

and thus by

,

Pick

.
Then there exist absolute constants

5.
Approximately Counting Anti-ferromagnetic Potts Models

In this section, we design an
FPTAS
for counting the partition function of anti-ferromagnetic Potts models on random bipartite regular graphs (
Theorem

1.2
).
We apply the following ideas to approximate
Z_{G,q}(\beta)
when
G
is a bipartite expander, which is an extension of the algorithm to count the number of proper colorings in
15
:

(1)

firstly, we know that the major weight of
Z_{G,q}(\beta)
lies in the configurations of rank at most
\theta n
by the tail bound
Lemma

3.6
;

(2)

then, we show that
Z^{\leq\theta n}
can be approximately estimated by summing up configurations specified by each pattern respectively;

(3)

finally, for each pattern, we design a proper abstract polymer model to estimate the weight of configurations specified by it.

To formalize the proof idea, we introduce the sparse subset used in
15
.

Definition 5.1

(Sparse subset
15
)
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, for a vertex subset
S\subseteq V
, we say
S
is
sparse
if each
3
-connect component in
G[S]
is of size at most
\theta n
.
We denote by
\Gamma^{\mathsf{sp}}=\Gamma^{\mathsf{sp}}(G,\theta)
be the collection of all sparse subsets of
G
.
Moreover, for a pattern
(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}
, we define
Z_{A,B}^{\mathsf{sp}}=\sum_{S\in\Gamma^{\mathsf{sp}}}W_{A,B}(S)
as the partial partition function projected to sparse subsets with respect to
(A,B)
.

The following lemmas are key ingredients to show
Theorem

1.2
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Given positive integers
q,\Delta\geq 3
and a real number
\beta>0
satisfying that
4H(\theta/2)+2\theta\ln{q}\leq 1/q
, the following holds for every bipartite
2n
-vertex graph
G=(V={\mathcal{L}}\cup{\mathcal{R}},E)
for sufficiently large integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}W_{A,B}(S)}{Z^{\leq\theta n}}\leq 1+\mathrm{e}^{-n/(100q)}.

Lemma 5.3
.

There exist absolute constants
C_{0}\in(0,1)
and
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that, for positive integers
q\geq 3
and
\Delta\geq 4
sufficiently large, and real numbers
\beta>0,\theta\in(0,1)
satisfying that
\theta\geq 8\ln{\Delta}/\Delta
,
\theta<C_{0}
and

\displaystyle 2H(3\theta/2)+3\theta\ln{q}\leq\frac{C_{1}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})}{q},

the following holds for every bipartite
2n
-vertex graph
G=(V={\mathcal{L}}\cup{\mathcal{R}},E)
for sufficiently large integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(5)

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}Z_{A,B}^{\mathsf{sp}}}{\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}W_{A,B}(S)}\leq 1+\exp\ab(-\frac{C_{2}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})}{q}n).

Lemma 5.4
.

Given positive integers
q,\Delta\geq 4
and real numbers
\varepsilon,\theta\in(0,1)
,
\beta>0
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q}\ab(\frac{\Delta}{4+(\Delta-4)\theta}-1)-\ln{q}\geq 5+9\ln{\Delta},

the following holds with high probability over
G\sim{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\mathsf{bip}}
.
For each pattern
(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}
, there exists a deterministic algorithm to output an
\varepsilon
-approximation to the quantity
Z_{A,B}^{\mathsf{sp}}
running in time
(n/\varepsilon)^{\tau(\Delta)}
for a computable function
\tau:\mathbb{R}\to(0,+\infty)
depending only on
\Delta
.

We defer the proof of
Lemma

5.2
in
Section

5.1
, the proof of
Lemma

5.3
in
Section

5.2
and the proof of
Lemma

5.4
in
Section

5.3
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Pick parameters
\delta=\frac{1}{100}
and
\theta=\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{10q\ln{\frac{q\Delta}{1 - \e^{-\beta}}}}
.
Then there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that when
\Delta(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})\geq Cq^{2}\ln^{2}{\frac{q\Delta}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}}
and
\beta(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})^{2}\geq\frac{Cq^{4}\ln^{2}{\frac{q\Delta}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}}}{\Delta}
, conditions in
Lemmas

3.6
,
5.2
,
5.3
and
5.4
hold for sufficiently large positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the bound (
5
) holds for
C_{2}=C
.

When
\varepsilon\leq 5\mathrm{e}^{-C\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q}n}
, we compute
Z_{G,q}(\beta)
by a brute force enumeration of all configurations and it is direct to see that the algorithm terminates in time
\mathrm{poly}\left(n,\varepsilon^{-1}\right)
.

When
\varepsilon>5\mathrm{e}^{-C\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q}n}
, we have the following holds:

(1)

Z^{\leq\theta n}
is an
(\varepsilon/5)
-approximation to
Z_{G,q}(\beta)
by
Lemma

3.6
;

(2)

the quantity

\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}W_{A,B}(S)

is an
(\varepsilon/5)
-approximation to
Z^{\leq\theta n}
by
Lemma

5.2
;

(3)

\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}Z_{A,B}^{\mathsf{sp}}
is an
(\varepsilon/5)
-approximation to the quantity
\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}W_{A,B}
by

5.3
;

(4)

for each pattern
(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}
, there exists a deterministic algorithm outputting an

-approximation
\widehat{Z}_{A,B}
to

in time
(n/\varepsilon)^{\tau(\Delta)}
for a factor
\tau=\tau(\Delta)
by
Lemma

5.4
.

Combining all above together, we obtain that there exists a deterministic algorithm such that it outputs a quantity
\widehat{Z}:=\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\widehat{Z}_{A,B}
in time
2^{q}(n/\varepsilon)^{\tau(\Delta)}
and
\widehat{Z}
is an
\varepsilon
-approximation to

, thus concluding the theorem.
∎

5.1.
Estimation by patterns

The proof of
Lemma

5.2
is similar to
(
15
, Lemma 29)
for proper colorings, and we show how it adapts the anti-ferromagnetic case.

Proof of
Lemma

5.2
.

It is trivial to see
Z^{\leq\theta n}\leq\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}W_{A,B}(S)
.
For the other side, consider a configuration in both
\chi_{A,B}(S)
and
\chi_{C,D}(T)
for
(A,B,S)\neq(C,D,T)
with patterns
(A,B),(C,D)\in{\mathscr{P}}
and
\ab|S|,\ab|T|\leq\theta n
.
It must hold that
(A,B)\neq(C,D)
(otherwise
S=T
).
Then by
Proposition

3.4
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}W_{A,B}(S)-Z^{\leq\theta n}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(A,B)\neq(C,D)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S,T\subseteq V:\ab|S|,\ab|T|\leq\theta n}\sum_{\sigma\in\chi_{A,B}(S)\cap\chi_{C,D}(T)}w(\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2q}\ab(\sum_{\ell\leq\theta n}\binom{2n}{\ell})^{2}\ab(\frac{q-1}{2})^{2n}q^{2\theta n}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2q}\mathrm{e}^{(4H(\theta/2)+2\theta\ln{q}-2/q)n}\ab(q/2)^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-n/(100q)}Z^{\leq\theta n}

where we use the inequality
\sum_{\ell\leq k}\binom{n}{\ell}\leq\mathrm{e}^{nH(k/n)}
for
k\leq n/2
and a crude bound
Z^{\leq\theta n}\geq(q/2)^{2n}
by considering the number of proper colorings.
Thus we conclude the lemma.
∎

5.2.
From sparse subsets to low-rank subsets

The following claim about the size of a sparse set plays a crucial role in the proof of
Lemma

5.3
.

Claim 5.5

(a general form of
(
15
, Claim 30)
)
.

Consider a bipartite
(\rho,\alpha)
-expander
G=(V={\mathcal{L}}\cup{\mathcal{R}},E)
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
\rho\leq\theta/2
and
\rho\alpha+\theta/2>1
.
For every
S\in\Gamma^{\mathsf{sp}}(G,\theta)
, it holds that
\ab|S|\leq 3\theta n
.

Proof.

Assume that
\ab|S|>3\theta n
.
Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
be its
3
-connected components.
Since
S
is sparse, we know that
\ab|\gamma_{i}|\leq\theta n
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.
Then we partition
\left\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}\right\}
into
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
such that
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\ab|\gamma|\geq\theta n
and
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{2}}\ab|\gamma|\geq\theta n
.
Let
S_{1}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}\gamma
and
S_{2}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{2}}\gamma
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
\ab|S_{1}\cap{\mathcal{L}}|\geq\theta n/2
.
By the assumption that
G
is a bipartite
(\rho,\alpha)
-expander, it holds that

\displaystyle\ab|S_{1}\cup N_{G}(S_{1})|\geq\ab|(S_{1}\cap{\mathcal{L}})\cup N_{G}(S_{1}\cap{\mathcal{L}})|\geq(\theta/2)n+\rho\alpha n>n.

A similar argument shows that
\ab|S_{2}\cup N_{G}(S_{2})|>n
, leading to a contradiction that
d_{G}(S_{1},S_{2})>3
.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

5.3
.

By
Lemmas

2.3
and
2.5
, we assume that statements in
Lemmas

2.3
and
2.5
hold (this event occurs with high probability).

Observe that for every
\ab|S|\leq\theta n
,
S\in\Gamma^{\mathsf{sp}}(G,\theta)
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}W_{A,B}(S)\leq\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}Z_{A,B}^{\mathsf{sp}}.

For the other side, consider a sparse set
S
of size
\ab|S|>\theta n
.
By
5.5
, it holds that
\ab|S|\leq 3\theta n
for
\theta\geq\frac{8\ln{\Delta}}{\Delta}>\max\left\{2\rho,2(1-\rho\alpha)\right\}
.
Then by
Corollary

2.5
, it holds that
\ab|N_{G}(S)|\geq\ab(\frac{\Delta}{4+(\Delta-4)\ab|S|/n}-1)\ab|S|
.
Using the crude bounds
\ab|A|,\ab|B|\leq q
,
\ab|A|/\ab|B|\in[1/q,q]
and
\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{R}}|-\ab|S\cap{\mathcal{L}}|\leq\ab|S|
, by
Lemma

3.2
,

\displaystyle W_{A,B}(S)

\displaystyle\leq\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}\cdot q^{\ab|S|}\ab(1-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q})^{\ab|N_{G}(S)|}.

Thus we calculate that

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}Z_{A,B}^{\mathsf{sp}}-\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}W_{A,B}(S)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\sum_{S\in\Gamma^{\mathsf{sp}}:\ab|S|\geq\theta n}\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}\exp\ab(\ab|S|\ln{q}-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q}\ab|N_{G}(S)|)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}\sum_{\ell=\theta n}^{3\theta n}\binom{2n}{\ell}\exp\ab(\ell\ab(\ln{q}-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q}\ab(\frac{\Delta}{4+(\Delta-4)\ell/n}-1)))

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{C(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})}{q}n}\sum_{(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}}\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}

for some absolute constant
C>0
, where we use the inequality
\sum_{\ell\leq k}\binom{n}{\ell}\leq\mathrm{e}^{nH(k/n)}
for
k\leq n/2
and the fact that there exists some
C^{\prime}>0
for every
x\in[\theta,3\theta]
,
x(\Delta/(4+(\Delta-4)x)-1)\geq C\min\left\{\Delta x,1\right\}
for some
C>0
and the last inequality holds by a crude bound
\sum_{S\subseteq V:\ab|S|\leq\theta n}W_{A,B}(S)\geq\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}

Thus we conclude the lemma.
∎

5.3.
Abstract polymer models

In this part, we provide our algorithm in
Lemma

5.4
to approximately compute
Z_{A,B}^{\mathsf{sp}}
for each pattern
(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}
.
The main tool is the
abstract polymer model
introduced in
Section

2.4
.
Fix a pattern
(A,B)
.
We construct the abstract polymer model
{\mathcal{M}}_{A,B}^{\theta}=({\mathcal{C}},\sim)
as following.
For every connected subgraph
S\subseteq V
in
G^{3}
of size
\ab|S|\leq\theta n
, it induces a polymer
\gamma=(V_{\gamma},w_{\gamma})
with
V_{\gamma}=S
and

\displaystyle w_{\gamma}:=\frac{W_{A,B}(V_{\gamma})}{\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}}.

For two polymers
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
, we say
\gamma_{1}\sim\gamma_{2}
if the induced subgraph
G^{3}[\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}]
is connected.

For the model of proper colorings,
15
shows that the partition function of the polymer model is somehow a ‘normalized’ number of proper colorings at low ranks.
For the anti-ferromagnetic Potts model, we put the following proposition showing the relation between
\Xi({\mathcal{M}}_{A,B}^{\theta})
and
Z_{A,B}^{\mathsf{sp}}
stated in
7
and for completeness we provide the proof.

Proposition 5.6
.

For every pattern
(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle Z_{A,B}^{\mathsf{sp}}=\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}\cdot\Xi({\mathcal{M}}_{A,B}^{\theta}).

Proof.

For a subset
S\subseteq V
, observe that (for simplicity, let
\Lambda=S\cup N_{G}(S)
)

\displaystyle\frac{W_{A,B}(S)}{\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}}\sum_{\sigma\in\chi_{A,B}(S)}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\ab|m_{G}(\sigma)|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}}\sum_{\sigma_{\Lambda}\in[q]^{\Lambda}:\exists\sigma\in\chi_{A,B}(S),\sigma(\Lambda)=\sigma_{\Lambda}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\ab|m_{G}(\sigma_{\Lambda})|}\ab|A|^{n-\ab|\Lambda\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}\ab|B|^{n-\ab|\Lambda\cap{\mathcal{R}}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\sigma_{\Lambda}\in[q]^{\Lambda}:\exists\sigma\in\chi_{A,B}(S),\sigma(\Lambda)=\sigma_{\Lambda}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\ab|m_{G}(\sigma_{\Lambda})|}}{\ab|A|^{\ab|\Lambda\cap{\mathcal{L}}|}\ab|B|^{\ab|\Lambda\cap{\mathcal{R}}|}}.

Assume that
3
-connected components of
S
are
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{W_{A,B}(S)}{\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{W_{A,B}(S_{i})}{\ab|A|^{n}\ab|B|^{n}}.

Note that there is a one-to-one correspondence between sparse subsets and compatible polymers.
Thus we conclude the proposition.
∎

5.3.1.
Verify Kotecký-Preiss condition

Now we verify the Kotecký-Preiss condition (
2.8
) and thus we obtain an
FPTAS
for computing
\Xi({\mathcal{M}}_{A,B}^{\theta})
by
Theorem

2.9
.
The following upper bound for the weight of a polymer is the key ingredient.

Proposition 5.7
.

For positive integers
q,\Delta\geq 4
and real numbers
\beta>0,\theta\in(0,1)
, the following holds with high probability over
G\sim{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\mathsf{bip}}
in the abstract polymer model
{\mathcal{M}}_{A,B}^{\theta}=({\mathcal{C}},\sim)
induced by every pattern
(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}
for the anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts model on
G
at inverse temperature
\beta
together with
\theta
:

\displaystyle\forall\gamma\in{\mathcal{C}},\quad\ab|w_{\gamma}|\leq\exp\ab(\ab(\ln{q}-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q}\ab(\frac{\Delta}{4+(\Delta-4)\theta}-1))\ab|\gamma|).

Proof.

We assume that the statement in
Corollary

2.5
holds (this event occurs with high probability).
For a polymer
\gamma=(V_{\gamma},w_{\gamma})
, by the crude bound
\ab|A|,\ab|B|\leq q
,
\ab|A|/\ab|B|\in[1/q,q]
and
\ab|V_{\gamma}\cap{\mathcal{R}}|-\ab|V_{\gamma}\cap{\mathcal{L}}|\leq\ab|\gamma|
, it holds that

\displaystyle\ab|w_{\gamma}|

\displaystyle\leq q^{\ab|\gamma|}\ab(1-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q})^{\ab|N_{G}(V_{\gamma})|}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\ab(\ab|\gamma|\ln{q}-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q}\ab|N_{G}(V_{\gamma})|)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\ab(\ab(\ln{q}-\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{q}\ab(\frac{\Delta}{4+(\Delta-4)\theta}-1))\ab|\gamma|)

By
Corollary

2.5
.
Thus we obtain the upper bound.
∎

Now we are ready to prove

Proof of

5.4
.

Fix a pattern
(A,B)\in{\mathscr{P}}
and we construct the abstract polymer model
{\mathcal{M}}_{A,B}^{\theta}=({\mathcal{C}},\sim)
as before.
To apply
Theorem

2.9
, we pick
g(\gamma)=\ab|\gamma|
and verify
Items

1
,
2
and
3
in

2.9
.
Obviously

1
and

Then we verify the Kotecký-Preiss condition (

For a polymer
\gamma\in{\mathcal{C}}

\gamma^{\prime}\sim\gamma

(1)

(2)

6.
Conclusion

In this work, we study the anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on random regular bipartite graphs
{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\mathsf{bip}}
.
Though the celebrated single-site Glauber dynamics exhibits a slow mixing time, there still might be an
FPTAS
for approximating the partition function
Z_{G,q}(\beta)
in the non-uniqueness regime.
Both negative and positive results rely on the geometric properties of the model.
We state some discussion and conjectures here.

6.1.
Geometric properties of anti-ferromagnetic Potts models

In
Section

3
, we demonstrate the configuration space according to ranks when
\beta
is far away from the uniqueness regime for sufficiently large
q
.
The key observation is that, for anti-ferromagnetic Potts models on bipartite expanders, when the inverse temperature
\beta
is sufficiently large, most of configurations are concentrated around some ground states characterized by different patterns.
It has been mentioned above that such properties are inspired by the existence of long-range order of anti-ferromagnetic
3
-state Potts models on lattices
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
conjectured by
17
and answered by
6
for sufficiently large
d
.
However, it still remains an understanding when such phase transition occurs for anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on random regular bipartite graphs and we ask the following question.

Question 6.1
.

For anti-ferromagnetic
q
-state Potts models on random
\Delta
-regular bipartite graphs, when does the model undergo a phase transition?

We remark here that
6.1
is important since our results in this work actually rely on the property that the model is somehow in an ordered phase.

6.2.
Torpid and rapid mixing of Markov chains

Theorem

1.1
shows the torpid mixing of Glauber dynamics for anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models when
\beta
is far away from the uniqueness.
It has been shown in
12
that the local-update Markov chain (including the Glauber dynamics) exhibits torpid mixing for
3
-colorings on lattices by the existence of phase transition.
We conjecture that the coexistence of different phases implies the torpid mixing of this family of Markov chains.

Conjecture 6.2
.

For anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models where the phase transition occurs, the local-update Markov chains (including the single-site Glauber dynamics
P^{\mathsf{GD}}
) exhibit exponential mixing time at sufficiently low temperature.

On the other side, in spite of the torpid mixing of
P^{\mathsf{GD}}
, it remains open whether there exists a fast Markov chain to design an
FPAUS
for
\mu_{G,q;\beta}
(for example, polymer dynamics in
7
).

Conjecture 6.3
.

For anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on random
\Delta
-regular
2n
-vertex bipartite graphs at
\beta>0
, with high probability there exists an
FPAUS
for
\mu_{G,q;\beta}
running in time
\mathrm{poly}\left(n,\Delta\right)
.

6.3.
Approximate counting

To estimate the partition function
Z_{G,q}(\beta)
of anti-ferromagnetic
q
-state Potts model on
{\mathcal{G}}_{n,\Delta}^{\mathsf{bip}}
, we apply the method of abstract polymer models at low temperature far away from the uniqueness threshold.
We put the following conjecture on counting anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on random regular bipartite graphs.

Conjecture 6.4
.

For anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on random
\Delta
-regular bipartite graphs at
\beta>0
, with high probability there exists an
FPTAS
for
Z_{G,q}(\beta)
.

Meanwhile, the Erdős-Rényi random bipartite graph
{\mathcal{G}}(n,n,p)
is another important random models in combinatorics and theoretic computer science.
We generate
G=({\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{R}},E)\sim{\mathcal{G}}(n,n,p)
of size
\ab|{\mathcal{L}}|=\ab|{\mathcal{R}}|=n
by picking each edge
(u,v)\in{\mathcal{L}}\times{\mathcal{R}}
in
E
with probability
p
independently at random.
We put the following conjecture on counting anti-ferromagnetic Potts models on sparse Erdős-Rényi random bipartite graphs.

Conjecture 6.5
.

For anti-ferromagnetic
q
-Potts models on Erdős-Rényi random bipartite graph
{\mathcal{G}}(n,n,d/n)
at
\beta>0
, with high probability there exists an
FPTAS
for
Z_{G,q}(\beta)
.

Remark 6.6
.

The technique we apply for random regular bipartite graphs seems to fail on the Erdős-Rényi random bipartite graphs, since it is seemingly impossible to verify the convergent criterion when the underlying graph is not a bipartite expander.
Also, it remains a barrier to design the approximating algorithm on unbounded-degree graphs (
Theorem

2.9
has a strict restriction on the assumption that the degree
\Delta
is a constant).
