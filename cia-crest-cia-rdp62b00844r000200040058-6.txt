fully in your plan for bringing this capability into being, I feet I must call

the steps outlined in your letter are taken as and when presently planned.

\ ®° not for the po*« of proposing that you now modify or delay the

The problem is that of maintaining the plausibility of the various
explanations that have been given out or have been allowed to circulate to
explain the nature and activities of our Detachments and the reason for
their presence at various locations overseas. I am sure you share my
profound hope that we will be granted permission to have this Joint Project
resume overflights both in Europe and in the Far East beginning this spring
or in the early summer. Though such overflights might be limited in number
and closely controlled in timing by higher authority, most certainly a great
deal of vital intelligence could be obtained, even with a low sortie rate. If
such operations were about to begin or were underway when the USAF units
openly established themselves at Laughlin Air Force Base as part of a SAC
Wing, previously known as a Strategic Reconnaissance organisation but
recently redesignated a Weather Reconnaissance Wing, I fear the conse-
quences could be serious. The danger is that the association of the U-2

I realise, of course, that events may render this fear irrelevant
or may lead us to conclude that the risk is not sufficiently serious to
Justify any modification of your program. I do believe, however, that it
will be desirable for us to review the situation together about 1 May 1957
