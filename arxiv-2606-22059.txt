Abstract
. We show that spatial zero extension across the boundary may fail in parabolic Sobolev spaces ℋ ̊ p 1 ​ ( ( 0 , T ) × Ω ) \mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega) , which can also be characterized as L p ​ ( 0 , T , W ̊ p 1 ​ ( Ω ) ) ∩ W p 1 ​ ( 0 , T , W p − 1 ​ ( Ω ) ) . L_{p}(0,T;\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\Omega))\cap W^{1}_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega)). More precisely, for any p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ) p\in[1,\infty) , we construct a function u ∈ ℋ ̊ p 1 ​ ( ( 0 , T ) × ℝ + d ) u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}) whose zero extension does not belong to ℋ p 1 ​ ( ( 0 , T ) × ℝ d ) \mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}) . The obstruction occurs even for a flat boundary and is caused by a self-similar boundary layer concentrated at the initial-boundary corner, which produces a boundary supported normal flux defect after zero extension. We also discuss the suitability of various Sobolev-type spaces as solution spaces for parabolic equations in divergence form.

1.
Introduction and main results

It is well known that the zero extension of a function
u\in\mathring{W}_{p}^{1}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open set and
\mathring{W}_{p}^{1}(\Omega)
denotes the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W_{p}^{1}(\Omega)
, belongs to
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
This property arises naturally in various fields of analysis and is highly useful in the theory of PDEs.
Specifically, it allows one to reduce problems posed on the domain
\Omega
to corresponding problems on the whole space, thereby enabling the use of global tools—for example, the Sobolev–Gagliardo–Nirenberg embedding theorem and Fourier analysis techniques on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Such reductions are widely used in the study of Dirichlet problems for divergence-form elliptic equations, particularly in establishing solvability and regularity results.
A natural question then arises:

Does the extension property hold for functions
u(t,x)
arising in parabolic equations and having zero trace on the spatial boundary?

(Q)

In this paper, we study this problem in standard parabolic Sobolev spaces for divergence form equations.
More precisely, we investigate whether functions vanishing on the lateral boundary remain in the same Sobolev class after extension by zero to the whole space.
From this perspective, we also discuss the
suitability
of various Sobolev-type spaces for the solvability of parabolic equations.

The parabolic spaces mainly considered in this paper are

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega),\quad\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega),

where
p\in[1,\infty)
,
T\in(0,\infty]
, and
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The space
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
consists of functions
u
with
u,Du\in L_{p}
, while the time derivative admits the distributional form

u_{t}=D_{i}g_{i}+f

(1.1)

for some
g_{i},\,f\in L_{p}
.
The space
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}
corresponds to the zero-lateral-boundary subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
.
These spaces can be characterized in terms of Bochner–Sobolev spaces.
For instance, for
p\in(1,\infty)
, the following equivalence holds:

\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)\sim L_{p}(0,T;\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\Omega))\cap W^{1}_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega)).

See §
1.1
for precise definitions of
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
and
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}
, as well as a brief discussion of this equivalence.

These spaces provide a natural functional framework for second-order parabolic equations in divergence form, such as linear equations of the form

u_{t}-D_{i}(a^{ij}D_{j}u+a^{i}u)+b^{i}D_{i}u+cu=D_{i}g_{i}+f.

Indeed, when all coefficients are bounded, the time derivative
u_{t}
can be interpreted in the sense of (
1.1
).
In particular,
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}
is well suited for Dirichlet problems with zero lateral boundary conditions, since the boundary condition is built into the definition of the space.
Motivated by advances in parabolic theory, this Sobolev framework has been widely used in the study of
L_{p}
-estimates and solvability for divergence-form equations; see, for instance,
[
16
,
3
,
4
,
9
,
10
,
20
]
for results on both linear and nonlinear equations, and
[
5
,
8
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
21
]
for further results in more general functional settings involving mixed and/or weighted norms.
Some of these works address higher-order, kinetic, and nonstationary Stokes equations.
We also refer to
[
12
,
17
]
for related results in the spaces with fractional time derivatives.
For further discussions on Bochner–Sobolev type function spaces such as

L_{p}(0,T;X_{1})\cap W^{1}_{p}(0,T;X_{0}),

see, for instance,
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
15
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
and the references therein.

The main purpose of this paper is to show that, in general, the answer to (
Q
) is negative.
More precisely, we prove that the zero extension may fail in
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}
for every
p\in[1,\infty)
, even when the underlying domain is the half-space

\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}=\{x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{1}>0,\,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\},

which has a
flat
boundary.
The main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty]
.
For any
p\in[1,\infty)
, there exists a function
u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
such that

\bar{u}\not\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\bar{u}
is the zero extension of
u
with respect to the spatial variable
x
.
More precisely, although
\bar{u},D\bar{u}\in L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there do not exist

G_{i},\,F\in L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad i=1,\ldots,d

such that

\bar{u}_{t}=D_{i}G_{i}+F\quad\text{ in }\,\,(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Remark 1.2
.

A few remarks regarding Theorem
1.1
are in order.

(i)

The proof of Theorem
1.1
actually yields a slightly stronger statement.
Namely, for every
p\in[1,\infty)
, the representation

\bar{u}_{t}=D_{i}G_{i}+F

considered above is impossible even under the weaker requirement
G_{i},F\in L_{p,\operatorname{loc}}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that is,

G_{i},F\in L_{p}((0,\tau)\times K),\quad i=1,\ldots,d

for every
\tau\in(0,T)
and every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(ii)

Unlike the case
p\in[1,\infty)
, the zero extension
\bar{u}
of any
u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
This property relies on a duality argument and extends to Lipschitz domains via local flattening.
See §
2.1
for the detailed proof and further discussion.

It has been previously pointed out that spatial zero extension need not preserve the parabolic Sobolev class
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}
in the above sense; see, for example,
[
19
]
.
However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, an explicit construction or detailed justification cannot be found in the existing literature.
In this paper, we provide an explicit construction and a rigorous proof of this failure.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
The rest of §
1
is devoted to preparatory material, generalizations, and implications of the main result.
In particular, §
1.2
explains the main mechanism behind the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We also discuss implications of Theorem
1.1
in §
1.4
, and summarize the comparison among the relevant function spaces in §
1.5
.
In §
2
, we provide the detailed proof of the main theorem and treat the case
p=\infty
.

1.1.
Function spaces

Let
T\in(0,\infty]
and
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d\geq 1
.
For
p\in[1,\infty]
, we define

W^{1,0}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)=\{u:D^{\ell}_{x}u\in L_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega),\,|\ell|\leq 1\}

with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,0}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|Du\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}.

By
u_{t}\in\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, we mean that there exist
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})\in L_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)^{d}
and
f\in L_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
such that

u_{t}=D_{i}g_{i}+f\quad\text{in }\,(0,T)\times\Omega

in a distribution sense, and that

\|u_{t}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}=\inf\big\{\|g\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}:u_{t}=D_{i}g_{i}+f\big\}

is finite.

We define
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
V_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
as the spaces of all functions in
W^{1,0}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
equipped with the norms

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}=\|u_{t}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|u\|_{W^{1,0}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

and

\|u\|_{V_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in(0,T)}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{W^{1,0}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)},

respectively.

Let
C^{\infty}_{0}([0,T]\times\Omega)
be the set of all infinitely differentiable functions in
[0,T]\times\Omega
with compact support in
[0,T]\times\Omega
,
where if
T=\infty
, the interval
[0,T]
is understood as
[0,\infty)
.
We denote by
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
\mathring{V}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
the closures of
C^{\infty}_{0}([0,T]\times\Omega)
in
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
V_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, respectively.

The corresponding mixed-norm spaces
W^{1,0}_{p,q}
,
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p,q}
,
V_{p,q}
, and so on, are defined analogously, replacing all
L_{p}
-norms in the preceding definitions by the following
L_{p,q}
-norm:

\|u\|_{L_{p,q}((0,T)\times\Omega)}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}|u(t,x)|^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{q/p}\,dt\bigg)^{1/q}.

As usual, the definition of the norm is modified in the standard way when
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
.

Remark 1.3
.

The parabolic Sobolev spaces introduced above can be described in terms of Bochner–Sobolev spaces.
More precisely, for
p\in(1,\infty)
, the following identifications hold with equivalent norms:

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)\sim L_{p}(0,T;W^{1}_{p}(\Omega))\cap W^{1}_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega))

(1.2)

and

V_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)\sim L_{p}(0,T;W^{1}_{p}(\Omega))\cap L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{p}(\Omega)).

Here,
W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega)
denotes the dual space of
\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, where
\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in
W^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
.
For the corresponding spaces with zero lateral boundary conditions, the spatial Sobolev space
W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
is replaced by
\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\Omega)

yielding

\mathring{\mathcal{H}}_{p}^{1}\left((0,T)\times\Omega\right)\sim L_{p}(0,T;\mathring{W}_{p}^{1}(\Omega))\cap W_{p}^{1}(0,T;W_{p}^{-1}(\Omega)).

For the reader’s convenience, we give a brief justification of (
1.2
) by verifying, in particular, the norm equivalence

\|u_{t}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}\sim\|u_{t}\|_{L_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega))},

where
\mathcal{Q}=(0,T)\times\Omega
.
Let

u\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}(\mathcal{Q}),

and suppose that

u_{t}=D_{i}g_{i}+f\quad\text{in }\,\mathcal{Q}

for some
g_{i},\,f\in L_{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
.
Then, it is easy to see that
u\in L_{p}(0,T;W_{p}^{1}(\Omega))
.
Set

\langle F(t),\varphi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\left(f(t,x)\varphi(x)-g_{i}(t,x)D_{i}\varphi(x)\right)\,dx\quad\text{for}\,\,\varphi\in\mathring{W}_{p^{\prime}}^{1}(\Omega).

This is well defined for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,
and
F
belongs to
L_{p}(0,T;W_{p}^{-1}(\Omega))
with

\|F\|_{L_{p}(0,T;W_{p}^{-1}(\Omega))}\leq\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}+\|g_{i}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}.

One can also verify that
u_{t}=F
in the sense of the Bochner weak derivative.
Hence, we see that
u\in L_{p}(0,T;W^{1}_{p}(\Omega))\cap W^{1}_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega))
and

\|u_{t}\|_{L_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega))}\lesssim\|u_{t}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}.

Conversely, suppose that

u\in L_{p}(0,T;W^{1}_{p}(\Omega))\cap W^{1}_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega)).

By standard properties of Bochner spaces,
u
admits a jointly measurable representative on
\mathcal{Q}
, still denoted by
u
, with
u,Du\in L_{p}(\mathcal{Q})
.
Consider a linear functional
\ell:X\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\ell(-D_{1}\phi,\ldots,-D_{d}\phi,\phi)=\int_{0}^{T}\langle u_{t}(t),\phi(t,\cdot)\rangle\,dt,

(1.3)

where

X=\{(-D_{1}\phi,\ldots,-D_{d}\phi,\phi)\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})^{d+1}:\phi\in L_{p^{\prime}}(0,T;\mathring{W}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega))\}.

Since
X
is a subspace of
L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})^{d+1}
and
\ell
is bounded with

\|\ell\|_{X^{*}}\lesssim\|u_{t}\|_{L_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega))},

the Hahn-Banach theorem allows us to extend
\ell
to a bounded linear functional on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})^{d+1}
.
Moreover, by the duality of
L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})^{d+1}
, there exists

(G_{1},\ldots,G_{d},F)\in L_{p}(\mathcal{Q})^{d+1}

such that for all
(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{d},\zeta)\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})^{d+1}
,

\ell(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{d},\zeta)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(G_{i}\eta_{i}+F\zeta)\,dx\,dt,

(1.4)

and that

\|G_{i}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}+\|F\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}\lesssim\|u_{t}\|_{L_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega))}.

Combining (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), we have

\int_{0}^{T}\langle u_{t}(t),\phi(t,\cdot)\rangle\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(-G_{i}D_{i}\phi+F\phi)\,dx\,dt

for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{Q})
.
Consequently,

u_{t}=D_{i}G_{i}+F\quad\text{in }\,\mathcal{Q}

and

\|u_{t}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}\lesssim\|u_{t}\|_{L_{p}(0,T;W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega))}.

This proves the converse inequality.

1.2.
Idea of the proof

We illustrate the mechanism behind Theorem
1.1
in the special case
p=d=1
.
The argument for
p>1
requires a different implementation, but the basic idea is the same, and it extends straightforwardly to higher dimensions
d\geq 2
.

Consider a function on
(t,x)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
of the form

u(t,x)=\partial_{x}v(t,x),\quad\textrm{ where }\,\,v(t,x)=t^{-1/4}e^{-x^{2}/t}.

Indeed,

u(t,x)=t^{-3/4}U(x/\sqrt{t}),\quad v(t,x)=t^{-1/4}V(x/\sqrt{t}),

where

V(y)=e^{-y^{2}},\quad U(y)=V^{\prime}(y)=-2ye^{-y^{2}}.

Thus, for small
t>0
, the profile is concentrated in the region
x\sim\sqrt{t}
near
x=0
.
A direct computation shows that

u,\;\partial_{x}u\in L_{1}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}),\quad u_{t}\notin L_{1}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}),

and that the time derivative admits the special structure

u_{t}=\partial_{x}g,\quad g=v_{t}\in L_{1}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

The relevant point is how the spatial norms of these functions scale as
t\searrow 0
.
Indeed, one finds

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\sim t^{-1/4},\quad\|u_{x}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})},\,\|g(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\sim t^{-3/4},

while

\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\sim t^{-5/4}.

That is, the time derivative
u_{t}
exhibits a non-integrable temporal singularity, while
u,u_{x}
, and
g
remain relatively mild.
On the half-line, however, this wild behavior is completely absorbed by the divergence structure
u_{t}=\partial_{x}g
.
Thus, the singularity is harmless at the distributional level, and since
u(t,0)=0
we have

u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{1}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

The obstruction appears after extending
u
by zero across the boundary
x=0
.
Formally, we have

\bar{u}_{t}=\partial_{x}(\mathbf{1}_{\{x>0\}}g)-g(t,0)\delta_{0}\quad\textrm{ in }\,\,(0,1)\times\mathbb{R},

where
g(t,0)=-t^{-5/4}/4
which is not integrable near
t=0
.
Thus spatial zero extension creates a boundary supported flux defect.
More precisely, assume for contradiction that the zero extension
\bar{u}
still belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{1}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
and suppose, for simplicity, that
\bar{u}_{t}=\partial_{x}G
(i.e.
F\equiv 0
) for some
G\in L_{1}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
.
Then
\bar{u}_{t}
would satisfy the global identity

\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u\varphi_{t}\,dx\,dt=\bigg)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{u}\,\varphi_{t}\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\,\varphi_{x}\,dx\,dt

(1.5)

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
.

Although the extension is performed only with respect to the spatial variable, (
1.5
) forces a compatibility between the divergence structure
and the temporal behavior of
u
.
To test this compatibility, one considers functions of the form

\varphi(t,x)=\eta_{m}(t)\phi(x),

where
\phi
is supported near the boundary
x=0
and
\eta_{m}
is a temporal cutoff concentrating on an interval
[\tfrac{1}{2m},\tfrac{1}{m}]
.
Plugging this choice into the left-hand side of
(
1.5
) yields the contribution

\int_{0}^{1}u(t,x)\,\eta_{m}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\sim m\int_{1/(2m)}^{1/m}u(t,x)\,dt.

(1.6)

Since
u(t,\cdot)
is localized near the support of
\phi(\cdot)
, one finds that

\|u(t,\cdot)\phi(\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\sim\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\sim t^{-1/4},

(1.7)

and then, (
1.6
) produces a divergent sequence

m\int_{1/(2m)}^{1/m}t^{-1/4}\,dt\,\to\,\infty\quad\textrm{ as }\,\,m\to\infty

on the left-hand side of (
1.5
).
(Note that if
\phi
vanishes near
x=0
, the first relation in (
1.7
) no longer holds.)

On the other hand, the assumed representation of
\bar{u}_{t}
by a
L_{1}
function
G
would force the right-hand side of (
1.5
) to remain bounded as
m\to\infty
.
This contradiction shows that the divergence structure of
u_{t}
, which is admissible on the half-line, cannot survive the zero extension.
In this sense, the failure of the extension is not just caused by a lack of spatial regularity, but also by the interaction between the spatial boundary and the temporal singularity.

Remark 1.4
.

Although
u(t,0)\equiv 0
, the
L_{1}
-mass of
u(t,\cdot)
is concentrated in the shrinking boundary layer
x\sim\sqrt{t}
as
t\searrow 0
.
Thus the singular behavior is located at the initial-boundary corner
(t,x)=(0,0)
.

1.3.
Generalizations

We describe a generalization of Theorem
1.1
in two directions.

(1)

Theorem
1.1
extends to the mixed-norm Sobolev spaces
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p,q}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, where
p\in[1,\infty]
and
q\in[1,\infty)
with
(p,q)\neq(\infty,1)
.
This follows by adapting the proof of the theorem (see §
2
) with the condition

\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{3}{2}\leq a<\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1}{q}-1

in place of (
2.1
).

(2)

The result extends from the half-space to arbitrary Lipschitz domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This relies on a standard boundary flattening argument and the observation that a suitable spatial localization of the counterexample from Theorem
1.1
, when restricted near the boundary, still serves as a counterexample (see Remark
1.4
).
Pulling this localized function back to the original domain
\Omega
yields a function
v\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p,q}((0,T)\times\Omega)
whose zero extension does not belong to
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p,q}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover, there do not exist

G_{i},\,F\in L_{p,q}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad i=1,\ldots,d

such that

\bar{v}_{t}=D_{i}G_{i}+F\quad\textrm{in }\,(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\bar{v}
denotes the spatial zero extension of
v
.

1.4.
Some comments

In this subsection, we discuss some implications of Theorem
1.1
concerning the suitability of Sobolev-type spaces as solution spaces for parabolic equations.

1.4.1.
Realization as weak solutions

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
T\in(0,\infty]
.
We first recall that
every function in
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be regarded as a weak solution to a parabolic equation in divergence form.
More precisely,
if
u\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

u_{t}=D_{i}G_{i}+F

for some
G_{i},\,F\in L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then we can write

u_{t}-\Delta u=D_{i}(G_{i}-D_{i}u)+F

to see that
u
satisfies an equation with the heat operator and
L_{p}
-data.
This observation remains valid when
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is replaced by an arbitrary domain.

We now contrast this with the space
V_{p}
.
Let
u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
be the counterexample from Theorem
1.1
, and let
\bar{u}
denote its spatial zero extension.
From the estimate (
2.3
) with
a=1/p-1<0
, it follows that

u\in\mathring{V}_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}).

Since the zero extension preserves the class
V_{p}
, we have

\bar{u}\in V_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

However, Theorem
1.1
shows that
\bar{u}
cannot be realized as a weak solution to
any
parabolic equation in divergence form.
Thus, unlike
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
the space
V_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
contains elements that do not arise as weak solutions to divergence-form equations.
The same phenomenon occurs when extending functions from an arbitrary Lipschitz domain
\Omega
.
This sharply contrasts with the elliptic setting:
for any domain
\Omega
, the zero extension of a function in
\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
trivially belongs to
W^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and remains a weak solution to a divergence-form elliptic equation on the whole space.

1.4.2.
Sobolev framework for parabolic equations

The
L_{p}
-theory of parabolic equations is concerned with solvability and a priori estimates for solutions with data in Lebesgue spaces, under some regularity assumptions on the coefficients.
In this theory, various types of Sobolev spaces have been employed as solution spaces, depending on the form of the equation, the singularity of the lower-order terms, the boundary conditions under consideration, and the underlying norm structure.

Here, we focus on linear parabolic equations in divergence form with lower-order terms

u_{t}-D_{i}(a^{ij}D_{j}u+a^{i}u)+b^{i}D_{i}u+cu=D_{i}g_{i}+f

(1.8)

posed in a cylindrical domain
(0,T)\times\Omega
, and discuss the corresponding Sobolev spaces for the
L_{p}
-theory.

For equations with
bounded lower-order coefficients
, as noted earlier, the spaces
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
and
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}
provide a natural setting for investigating unique solvability and estimates of the form

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}}\lesssim\|g_{i}\|_{L_{p}}+\|f\|_{L_{p}},

under zero initial conditions.
We again refer the reader to
[
3
,
4
,
9
,
10
,
20
]
and the references therein for results in these Sobolev spaces under suitable assumptions on the leading coefficients
a^{ij}
and on the boundary of the underlying domain.
Conversely, as mentioned in §
1.4.1
, every function in
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
can be regarded as a weak solution to a parabolic equation of the form (
1.8
).
In this sense, the Sobolev spaces
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
and
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}
serve as natural and essentially optimal solution spaces for the
L_{p}
-solvability of parabolic equations with bounded lower-order coefficients.
In particular,
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}
is well suited for Dirichlet problems with zero lateral boundary conditions.
Similar observations remain valid in the mixed-norm spaces
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p,q}
for equations with
L_{p,q}
-data.

By contrast, for parabolic equations with
(sub)critical lower-order coefficients
, namely

a^{i}\in L_{\ell_{1},r_{1}},\quad b^{i}\in L_{\ell_{2},r_{2}},\quad c\in L_{\ell_{3},r_{3}},

(1.9)

the spaces
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
as well as
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p,q}
are, in general, no longer large enough to accommodate solutions; see
[
19
]
.
In that work, the authors addressed this issue by establishing solvability results in a parabolic Sobolev framework based essentially on
W^{1,0}_{p,q}
for
p,q\in(1,\infty)
, with the estimate

\|u\|_{W^{1,0}_{p,q}}\lesssim\|g_{i}\|_{L_{p,q}}+\|f\|_{L_{p_{1},q_{1}}},

where the pairs
(\ell_{k},r_{k})
and
(p_{1},q_{1})
are determined by appropriate lower summability conditions.
When
p,q\in[2,\infty)
, they also employed the space
V_{p,q}
in order to capture additional regularity of solutions with respect to the time variable.
We remark, however, that
V_{p,q}
is not suitable for the solvability theory in the range
p,q\in(1,2)
; see
[
1
]
for a counterexample.
Moreover, as mentioned in §
1.4.1
,
V_{p,q}
contains functions that cannot be realized as weak solutions to any parabolic equation.

Motivated by these observations, we finally discuss a related Sobolev space for divergence-form equations with an additional half-order time derivative term in the data.
More precisely, we consider parabolic equations of the form

u_{t}-D_{i}(a^{ij}D_{j}u+a^{i}u)+b^{i}D_{i}u+cu=D_{t}^{1/2}h+D_{i}g_{i}+f

(1.10)

in an
infinite cylinder

\mathbb{R}\times\Omega
, where the half-time derivative is defined as

D_{t}^{1/2}h(t,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{h(t+\ell,x)-h(t,x)}{|\ell|^{3/2}}\,d\ell.

This class of equations was treated in
[
17
,
18
]
, mainly in the case
a^{i}=b^{i}=0
with
c\geq 0
constant, while the corresponding equations with bounded lower-order terms were briefly addressed.
There, the authors employed the Sobolev space
H^{1/2,1}_{p}
, equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{1/2,1}_{p}}:=\|D_{t}^{1/2}u\|_{L_{p}}+\|u\|_{W^{1,0}_{p}},

to prove unique solvability
in the whole domain
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, under suitable regularity assumptions on
a^{ij}
.
Building on this approach, and investigating anisotropic embeddings on
H^{1/2,1}_{p}
(cf.
[
19
, Appendix]
),
one may further consider the equations (
1.10
) with unbounded lower-order terms as in (
1.9
) in the spaces
H^{1/2,1}_{p}
and
H^{1/2,1}_{p,q}
.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we prove our main result Theorem
1.1
.
It suffices to consider the theorem on a finite-height cylinder, and hence, by scaling, we may assume that
(0,T)=(0,1)
.

We first treat the case
d=1
.

Choice of the function
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{1}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Let
a
be a constant to be chosen later, such that

\frac{3}{2p}-\frac{3}{2}<a<\frac{3}{2p}-1.

(2.1)

Define

u(t,x)=v_{x}(t,x)

on
(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where

v(t,x)=t^{-a}e^{-x^{2}/t}.

We claim that

u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

To see this, note that

u(t,x)=-2t^{-a-1}xe^{-x^{2}/t},

u_{x}(t,x)=-2t^{-a-1}e^{-x^{2}/t}+4t^{-a-2}x^{2}e^{-x^{2}/t},

and

u_{t}(t,x)=2(a+1)t^{-a-2}xe^{-x^{2}/t}-2t^{-a-3}x^{3}e^{-x^{2}/t}=g_{x}(t,x),

(2.2)

where

g(t,x):=v_{t}(t,x)=-at^{-a-1}e^{-x^{2}/t}+t^{-a-2}x^{2}e^{-x^{2}/t}.

For
t\in(0,1)
, we calculate the
L_{p}
-norms by applying the change of variables
y=x/\sqrt{t}
.
For
u(t,\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|-2t^{-a-1}\sqrt{t}ye^{-y^{2}}\big|^{p}\sqrt{t}\,dy

\displaystyle=2^{p}t^{-p(a+1/2)+1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{p}e^{-py^{2}}\,dy.

This yields

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq N_{1}t^{-a-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}},

(2.3)

where
N_{1}=N_{1}(a,p)
.
Similarly, we obtain

\|u_{x}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|g(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq N_{2}t^{-a-1+\frac{1}{2p}},

and for the time derivative
u_{t}
,

\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}=t^{-p(a+3/2)+1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|2(a+1)y-2y^{3}\big|^{p}e^{-py^{2}}\,dy,

which implies

\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\geq N_{3}t^{-a-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}},

for some positive constant
N_{2}
and
N_{3}
depending only on
a
and
p
.
Then by (
2.1
),

u,u_{x},g\in L_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})

(2.4)

and

u_{t}\notin L_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

On the other hand, since
u_{t}=g_{x}\in L_{p}((\varepsilon,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u\varphi_{t}\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g\varphi_{x}\,dx\,dt

(2.5)

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
, which shows that

u_{t}\in\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Together with (
2.4
), this implies that
u\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Zero lateral boundary condition:
u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}_{p}^{1}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Since
u(t,x)=O(x)
as
x\to 0^{+}
, one can apply a standard spatial cut-off argument.
Let
\{\xi_{n}\}
be a sequence of functions in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\xi_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ for }\,x\leq\frac{1}{n},\\[4.0pt]
1&\text{ for }\,x\geq\frac{2}{n},\end{cases}

and
|\xi_{n}^{\prime}|\leq 2n
.
Defining
u^{(n)}(t,x)=u(t,x)\xi_{n}(x)
, we show that

\|u^{(n)}-u\|_{W^{1,0}_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})}\to 0.

We have
u^{(n)}_{x}-u_{x}=u_{x}(\xi_{n}-1)+u\xi_{n}^{\prime}
.
Since
\operatorname{supp}\xi_{n}^{\prime}\subset[1/n,2/n]
, we observe that
|\xi_{n}^{\prime}(x)|\leq 2n\leq 4/x
.
Thus, the error term is bounded by

|u(t,x)\xi_{n}^{\prime}(x)|\leq\frac{4}{x}|u(t,x)|=8t^{-a-1}e^{-x^{2}/t}\in L_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}),

where the inclusion is due to the condition
a<3/(2p)-1
in (
2.1
).
By the dominated convergence theorem, this implies
\|u\xi_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})}\to 0
and thus
\|u^{(n)}-u\|_{W^{1,0}_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})}\to 0
.

For the time derivative, we estimate the
\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
norm of
u^{(n)}_{t}-u_{t}=u_{t}(\xi_{n}-1)
.
We represent this distribution as
\mathcal{G}^{(n)}_{x}(t,x)
, where

\mathcal{G}^{(n)}(t,x)=-\int_{x}^{\infty}u_{t}(t,y)\big(\xi_{n}(y)-1\big)\,dy.

Indeed, for any test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
, applying Fubini’s theorem gives

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathcal{G}^{(n)}\varphi_{x}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{t}(t,y)\big(\xi_{n}(y)-1\big)\bigg(\int_{0}^{y}\varphi_{x}(t,x)\,dx\bigg)dy\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u_{t}(\xi_{n}-1)\varphi\,dy\,dt,

which confirms
\mathcal{G}^{(n)}_{x}=u_{t}(\xi_{n}-1)
in the distribution sense.
Since
\xi_{n}(y)-1\equiv 0
on
[2/n,\infty)
by definition,
\mathcal{G}^{(n)}(t,x)\equiv 0
for
x\geq 2/n
.
For
x<2/n
, substituting
u_{t}(t,y)=g_{y}(t,y)
and integrating by parts yields

\mathcal{G}^{(n)}(t,x)=g(t,x)\big(\xi_{n}(x)-1\big)+\int_{x}^{2/n}g(t,y)\xi_{n}^{\prime}(y)\,dy.

Since
g\in L_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
, the
L_{p}
-norm of the 1st term converges to zero as
n\to\infty
.
For the 2nd term, applying Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{x}^{2/n}g(t,y)\xi_{n}^{\prime}(y)\,dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2n\int_{1/n}^{2/n}|g(t,y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq 2n\bigg(\frac{1}{n}\bigg)^{1-1/p}\bigg(\int_{1/n}^{2/n}|g(t,y)|^{p}\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}.

Then, integrating the
p
-th power over
x\in(0,2/n)
yields

\int_{0}^{2/n}\bigg|\int_{x}^{2/n}g(t,y)\xi_{n}^{\prime}(y)\,dy\bigg|^{p}dx\leq\frac{2}{n}\cdot 2^{p}n\int_{1/n}^{2/n}|g(t,y)|^{p}\,dy=2^{p+1}\int_{1/n}^{2/n}|g(t,y)|^{p}\,dy.

Integrating over
t\in(0,1)
, the last term tends to zero as
n\to\infty
by the absolute continuity of the Lebesgue integral, since
g\in L_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Consequently,
\|\mathcal{G}^{(n)}\|_{L_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})}\to 0
, which implies
\|u^{(n)}_{t}-u_{t}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}((0,1\times\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\to 0
.
A standard mollification then yields a sequence in
C_{0}^{\infty}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
, concluding
u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Zero extension is impossible for
u
.

Now we prove

\bar{u}\notin\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})

by showing that there are no functions
G
and
F
in
L_{p}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\bar{u}_{t}=G_{x}+F\quad\text{on }\,(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}.

Here,
\bar{u}
denotes the zero extension of
u
with respect to
x
.
Suppose, to the contrary, that such functions
G
and
F
exist.
Then, for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{u}\varphi_{t}\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G\varphi_{x}\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F\varphi\,dx\,dt.

(2.6)

We consider the following two cases:
p=1
and
p\in(1,\infty)
.

1.

p=1
.
In this case, we fix
a\in(0,1/2)
.
Let
\eta
and
\eta_{m}
for
m\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
be piecewise linear continuous functions on
[0,1]
defined as follows.
First, we set
\eta
such that

\eta(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ for }\,t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right],\\[5.0pt]
-4t+3&\text{ for }\,t\in\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4}\right),\\[5.0pt]
0&\text{ for }\,t\in\left[\frac{3}{4},1\right].\end{cases}

Then, for each
m\geq 2
, we define
\eta_{m}
by

\eta_{m}(t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ for }\,t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2m}\right],\\[5.0pt]
2mt-1&\text{ for }\,t\in\left(\frac{1}{2m},\frac{1}{m}\right],\\[5.0pt]
\eta(t)&\text{ for }\,t\in\left(\frac{1}{m},1\right].\end{cases}

We also take a sequence
\{\zeta_{k}\}
in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\zeta_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ for }\,x\leq 0,\\[5.0pt]
1&\text{ for }\,x\geq\frac{1}{k}.\end{cases}

For a given
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, by setting
\varphi(t,x)=\eta_{m}(t)\zeta_{k}(x)\phi(x)
in (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(\eta_{m}\zeta_{k}\phi)_{t}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g(\eta_{m}\zeta_{k}\phi)_{x}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g_{x}(\eta_{m}\zeta_{k}\phi)\,dx\,dt,

where the last integral is well-defined because
\eta_{m}
has compact support in
(0,1)
and
g_{x}\in L_{1}((\varepsilon,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Letting
k\to\infty
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(\eta_{m}\phi)_{t}\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g_{x}(\eta_{m}\phi)\,dx\,dt.

Together with (
2.6
), this yields

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g_{x}(\eta_{m}\phi)\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(\eta_{m}\phi)_{x}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(\eta_{m}\phi)\,dx\,dt,

(2.7)

where, as
m\to\infty
, the right-hand side converges to a finite number.
Thus,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g_{x}(\eta_{m}\phi)\,dx\,dt\quad\text{exists}.

(2.8)

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g_{x}(\cdot,x)\eta_{m}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}u_{t}(\cdot,x)\eta_{m}\,dt=-\int_{0}^{1}u(\cdot,x)\eta_{m}^{\prime}\,dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{1/(2m)}^{1/m}t^{-a-1}xe^{-x^{2}/t}\eta^{\prime}_{m}\,dt+2\int_{1/2}^{1}t^{-a-1}xe^{-x^{2}/t}\eta^{\prime}\,dt.

Hence,

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g_{x}(\eta_{m}\phi)\,dx\,dt=:I_{m}+J,

where

I_{m}=2\int_{1/(2m)}^{1/m}t^{-a-1}\eta^{\prime}_{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}xe^{-x^{2}/t}\phi\,dx\,dt,

J=2\int_{1/2}^{1}t^{-a-1}\eta^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}xe^{-x^{2}/t}\phi\,dx\,dt.

Using the fact that
\eta_{m}^{\prime}(t)=2m
on
[1/(2m),1/m]
and applying the change of variables
x\mapsto\sqrt{t}x
, we obtain

I_{m}=4m\int^{1/m}_{1/(2m)}t^{-a}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}xe^{-x^{2}}\phi(\sqrt{t}x)\,dxdt.

By choosing
\phi
to be non-increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(which implies
\phi(x)\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
) and positive near
x=0
, for
x>0
and
t\in[1/(2m),1/m]
, we have

\phi(\sqrt{t}x)\geq\phi(x/\sqrt{m})\geq\phi(x).

Hence,

I_{m}\geq 4m\int_{1/(2m)}^{1/m}t^{-a}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}xe^{-x^{2}}\phi\,dx\,dt=4mM\int_{1/(2m)}^{1/m}t^{-a}\,dt,

where

0<M=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}xe^{-x^{2}}\phi\,dx<\infty.

Since
a>0
, we see that
I_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
.
This contradicts (
2.8
) because
J
is finite.

2.

p\in(1,\infty)
.
In this case, we set

a=\frac{1}{p}-1<0.

(2.9)

Clearly,
a
satisfies (
2.1
).
Let
\eta\in L_{p^{\prime}}((0,\tau))
, and let
\{\eta_{m}\}
be a sequence in
C^{\infty}_{0}((0,\tau))
such that

\eta_{m}\to\eta\quad\text{in }\,L_{p^{\prime}}((0,\tau))

and

\eta_{m}(t)\to\eta(t)\quad\text{for a.e. }\,t\in(0,\tau),

where
p^{\prime}
denotes the Hölder conjugate of
p
, and
\tau\in(0,1]
is a constant to be chosen later.
As seen in (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), for a given
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g_{x}(\eta_{m}\phi)\,dx\,dt=\mathcal{K},

where

\mathcal{K}:=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(\eta\phi)_{x}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(\eta\phi)\,dx\,dt.

By the explicit formula for
g_{x}
in (
2.2
), we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g_{x}(t,\cdot)\phi\,dx=2t^{-a-1}H(t),

where

H(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\big((a+1)x-x^{3}\big)e^{-x^{2}}\phi(\sqrt{t}x)\,dx.

Hence,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{-a-1}H\eta_{m}\,dt=\frac{\mathcal{K}}{2}.

We fix
\phi
such that
\phi(0)=1
.
Since
H
is continuous on
[0,1]
and

H(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\big((a+1)x-x^{3}\big)e^{-x^{2}}\,dx=a\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}xe^{-x^{2}}\,dx<0,

one can find constants
\nu>0
and
\tau\in(0,1]
such that

H(t)\leq-\nu\quad\text{for all }\,t\in[0,\tau].

If
\eta\geq 0
, we may choose
\{\eta_{m}\}
so that
\eta_{m}\geq 0
for all
m
.
Since

t^{-a-1}(-H(t))\eta_{m}(t)\geq 0\quad\textrm{ on }\,\,(0,\tau),

by using Fatou’s lemma and the fact that
|\mathcal{K}|\leq N\|\eta\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}((0,\tau))}
, we obtain

0\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{-a-1}(-H)\eta\,dt\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{-a-1}(-H)\eta_{m}\,dt\leq N\|\eta\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}((0,\tau))}.

Therefore, for a general
\eta\in L_{p^{\prime}}((0,\tau))
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{-a-1}H\eta\,dt\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{-a-1}H(\eta_{+}-\eta_{-})\,dt\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{-a-1}(-H)\eta_{+}\,dt+\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{-a-1}(-H)\eta_{-}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq N\|\eta\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}((0,\tau))},

where
\eta_{+}
and
\eta_{-}
are the positive and negative parts of
\eta
, respectively.
This estimate, together with duality, implies that
t^{-a-1}H\in L_{p}((0,\tau))
.
Since
|H|
is bounded away from zero on
[0,\tau]
, we conclude
t^{-a-1}\in L_{p}((0,\tau))
, which contradicts the condition (
2.9
).

This completes the proof for the case
d=1
.

For
d\geq 2
, we take

u(t,x)=v_{x_{1}}(t,x_{1})w(x^{\prime}),

where
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
,
w\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
, and
v
is
defined as before.
Then by direct calculation, we see that
u
provides the desired example for the case
d\geq 2
.
∎

2.1.
The case
p=\infty

To see why the counterexample for
p\in(1,\infty)
in Theorem
1.1
fails in the limit
p=\infty
, we formally set the exponent (
2.9
) to
a=-1
.
The functions
u
and
g=v_{t}
then become

u(t,x)=-2xe^{-x^{2}/t},\quad g(t,x)=\Big(1+\frac{x^{2}}{t}\Big)e^{-x^{2}/t}.

Unlike the finite
p
case where
g(t,0)
has a non-integrable singularity as
t\to 0^{+}
, here
g(t,0)
is bounded.
Thus, the singularity preventing the zero extension does not occur.
In fact, we have the following:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty]
. For any
u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, its zero extension
\bar{u}
with respect to
x
satisfies

\bar{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

Since
u\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, we have
u,Du\in L_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, and there exist
g_{1},\ldots,g_{d},f\in L_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
such that

u_{t}=D_{i}g_{i}+f\quad\text{in }\,(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}.

(2.10)

For the zero extension
\bar{u}
, it is clear that
\bar{u},D\bar{u}\in L_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
To show that
\bar{u}_{t}
can be represented in divergence form, let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
be an arbitrary test function.
We consider the functional

I=I(\varphi):=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{u}\varphi_{t}\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}u\varphi_{t}\,dx\,dt.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we introduce a cut-off function
\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x_{1})
defined by

\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x_{1})=\begin{cases}0&\text{for }x_{1}\leq 0,\\[4.0pt]
x_{1}/\varepsilon&\text{for }0<x_{1}<\varepsilon,\\[4.0pt]
1&\text{for }x_{1}\geq\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Then by testing (
2.10
) with
\zeta_{\varepsilon}\varphi
, we have

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}u(\zeta_{\varepsilon}\varphi)_{t}\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}-g_{i}D_{i}(\zeta_{\varepsilon}\varphi)+f\zeta_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,dx\,dt.

Taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the left-hand side converges to
I
.
On the right-hand side, since
D_{1}\zeta_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}I_{(0,\varepsilon)}(x_{1})
, we obtain

I=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}-g_{i}D_{i}\varphi+f\varphi\,dx\,dt+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}g_{1}\varphi\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{1}\,dt.

(2.11)

Note that

|\varphi(t,x_{1},x^{\prime})|=\bigg|\int_{-\infty}^{x_{1}}D_{1}\varphi(t,s,x^{\prime})\,ds\bigg|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|D_{1}\varphi(t,s,x^{\prime})|\,ds.

Using this, we estimate the last term in (
2.11
) as follows:

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}g_{1}\varphi\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{1}\,dt\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{1}\|_{L_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|D_{1}\varphi(t,s,x^{\prime})|\,ds\,dx^{\prime}\,dt\bigg)dx_{1}

\displaystyle=\|g_{1}\|_{L_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D_{1}\varphi(t,x)|\,dx\,dt.

Therefore,

|I|\leq N\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(|\varphi|+\sum_{i=1}^{d}|D_{i}\varphi|\bigg)\,dx\,dt,

where

N=N\left(\|g_{i}\|_{L_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})},\|f\|_{L_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\right)>0.

This inequality implies that the linear mapping

(\varphi,D_{1}\varphi,\dots,D_{d}\varphi)\mapsto I

is bounded with respect to the
L_{1}
-norm.
By the Hahn-Banach theorem and the duality relation
(L_{1})^{*}=L_{\infty}
, there exist functions
\bar{F},\bar{G}_{1},\dots,\bar{G}_{d}\in L_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

I=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}-\bar{G}_{i}D_{i}\varphi+\bar{F}\varphi\,dx\,dt.

This concludes that

\bar{u}_{t}=D_{i}\bar{G}_{i}+\bar{F}\quad\textrm{ in }\,\,(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Hence,
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎
