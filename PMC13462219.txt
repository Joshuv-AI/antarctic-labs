Transcriptomic analysis of expression profiles of key genes in the immune pathway of Apostichopus japonicus under intermittent chitooligosaccharides supplementation
Authors: Huidong Zhang, Zhiyin Chen, Xiaofan Li, Rongyue Wang, Xiaoyu Nie, Juan Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Chitooligosaccharides can regulate the synergistic upregulation of HSP70 and HSP26 genes in intestinal tissue, thereby enhancing the immune function of Apostichopus japonicus. Additionally, they reduce immune stress by downregulating the expression levels of HSP70 and FADD genes in coelomocytes.•Chitooligosaccharides may balance the inflammatory response and inhibit excessive apoptosis by upregulating the expression of NOC4L and RSL1D1 genes in coelomocytes.•Chitooligosaccharides are involved in regulating the MAPK, TLR, and NLR pathway networks, and modulate the intestinal immune function of Apostichopus japonicus by mediating the core hub gene TRAF6.•Chitooligosaccharides may reduce the efficiency of fatty acid β-oxidation by inhibiting the PPAR pathway.Keywords: Apostichopus japonicus, Chitooligosaccharides, Coelomocytes, Intestine, Respiratory tree, Transcriptome

Introduction

Apostichopus japonicus, the primary farmed echinoderm in North China, lacks specialized immune organs, with coelomocytes, intestines, and respiratory trees forming its innate immune defense system [1]. Previous transcriptomic studies have shown that external stimuli alter gene expression via TLR(Hongjuan [2]; Yao [3]), NLR [4], MAPK [5], and PPAR(Jinqi [6]) pathways, and the three tissues exhibit tissue-specific immune responses. Most existing studies only focus on single tissues, lacking molecular evidence for coordinated immune regulation across multiple tissues.

Intensive aquaculture frequently suffers from disease outbreaks, and chitooligosaccharides (COS) serve as promising antibiotic-free immunostimulants [7]. Zhang [8] found that dietary COS reduced intestinal pathogenic bacteria and enhanced non-specific immunity in red-claw crayfish (Cherax quadricarinatus). Most published feeding trials adopt continuous COS supplementation, and persistent stimulation easily triggers immune fatigue and disrupts tissue homeostasis via sustained inflammatory activation. Li(Li [9]; Rongyue [10]) conducted preliminary intermittent feeding trials on A. japonicus and verified that 0.5% COS supplementation every three days simultaneously improved growth, antioxidant capacity, intestinal morphology, and basal immunity. Given the limitations of continuous COS administration, we hereby clearly put forward the core research hypothesis in the introduction. We speculate that intermittent COS supplementation can effectively avoid excessive immune activation. At the transcriptional level, it is expected to trigger differential activation of core pathways including the MAPK signaling pathway and NF-κB immune pathway, and further mediate tissue-specific immune and metabolic reprogramming across different tissues.This feeding mode enables immune cells to obtain sufficient recovery time, thereby balancing energy allocation between immune response and basal metabolism. In addition, the intermittent administration pattern is consistent with the natural discontinuous feeding habit of sea cucumbers, which is more applicable to their long-term aquaculture compared with continuous supplementation strategy.

Although COS effects are generally described, it remains unclear what is fundamentally unknown about immune regulation in A. japonicus, particularly at the transcriptomic and pathway integration level. Current COS-related research is mostly limited to phenotypic observations, including growth performance, immune enzyme activity(Liuyang [11]), and single-tissue gene quantification(Shu [12]), and few transcriptomic analyses cover only one tissue. No studies have comprehensively characterized transcriptional profiles of coelomocytes, intestines, and respiratory trees to clarify the holistic immune regulatory network. Different from previous single-tissue trials with continuous COS feeding, this study performs integrated transcriptome profiling, pathway enrichment, and protein-protein interaction network analysis on three key immune tissues to dissect tissue-specific immunomodulatory mechanisms of COS from a multi-tissue coordination perspective. Core immune regulatory genes are screened to characterize divergent pathway activation under intermittent COS treatment. The results elaborate on the molecular basis of COS-mediated innate immunity in sea cucumbers and provide theoretical references for optimizing segmented COS feeding strategies in aquaculture.

Materials and methods

Aquaculture trial and biological sample collection

Sea cucumbers (Apostichopus japonicus) used in this experiment were sourced from a large-scale aquaculture facility in Dalian City, Liaoning Province, China. After a 1-week acclimation period in filtered seawater (temperature: 17 ± 1 °C; salinity: 29 ± 1.2 ‰), 204 healthy individuals (initial weight: 18.51 ± 0.28 g) were randomly allocated to either the control group or the experimental group. A COS-supplemented diet was prepared by incorporating 0.5% (w/w) COS into the basal diet. Both the basal diet and COS diet were pelleted, air-dried, and stored at −20 °C until use. The control group received daily satiation feeding with the basal diet, while the experimental group was subjected to intermittent feeding: satiation feeding with the COS diet every third day and the basal diet on the remaining days. The feeding trial lasted for 8 weeks.

All sea cucumbers were randomly assigned into two groups, each containing three biological replicates with 34 individuals per replicate. Three sea cucumbers were randomly chosen from each replicate and pooled together for subsequent RNA extraction and transcriptome library construction. This pooling strategy was adopted primarily because the RNA yield of coelomocytes and respiratory tree tissues isolated from a single individual was insufficient to meet the minimum RNA input requirement for transcriptome library construction. Meanwhile, sample pooling reduces random transcriptional noise induced by individual variation in stress status and feeding intake, which enables more objective characterization of population-level immune responses. The dual-replicate design eliminates extreme abnormal expression signals from single individuals and minimizes the masking effect on inter-individual differential transcripts. Pearson correlation and principal component analysis further validated the stability and reliability of sequencing data generated by this sampling scheme.

For sampling, sea cucumbers were randomly selected from each group and rested for 5 min to expel seawater from respiratory trees. Coelomic fluid was extracted with sterile 1 mL syringes, centrifuged at 6000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was discarded. Cell pellets were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Intestinal and respiratory tree tissues were rapidly dissected, transferred into sterile centrifuge tubes and frozen in liquid nitrogen for subsequent RNA extraction and cDNA library construction.

Total RNA extraction, library construction, and high-throughput sequencing

Total RNA was extracted using the Trizol Reagent kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Tissue samples from three individual sea cucumbers (Apostichopus japonicus) were pooled per biological replicate under RNase-free conditions. RNA integrity and potential genomic DNA contamination were assessed using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA, USA). RNA purity was quantified via spectrophotometry (NanoDrop™), and samples were normalized to 3 μg total RNA input. mRNA was enriched from total RNA using oligo(dT) magnetic beads. Fragmentation was performed using a fragmentation buffer to generate short RNA fragments (200∽500 bp). Double-stranded cDNA synthesis was conducted using the NEBNext Ultra™ RNA Library Prep Kit (NEB #E7530, New England Biolabs, Ipswich, MA, USA). End repair and A-tailing were applied to cDNA fragments, followed by adapter ligation. Size selection of adapter-ligated fragments (target range: 300∽500 bp) was performed using agarose gel electrophoresis, and libraries were amplified by PCR (15 cycles).

Prior to sequencing, all 12 cDNA libraries were randomly rearranged and evenly distributed across two sequencing lanes. Control and treatment samples from identical tissues were alternately loaded to avoid systematic bias caused by centralized loading of one experimental group. Uniform protocols were applied to all samples during RNA extraction, library amplification, and sequencing. The Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform was used for 2 × 150 bp paired-end sequencing.TPM normalization was adopted to eliminate sequencing depth bias during bioinformatic analysis. Pearson correlation and principal component analysis revealed no obvious batch clustering, confirming negligible batch effects in this dataset. Library quality verification and sequencing were performed by Gene Denovo Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China).

Quality control and sequence alignment of sequencing data

To obtain high-quality clean reads, raw sequencing data underwent stringent quality filtering prior to bioinformatic analysis to minimize analytical interference from artifacts. Reads containing adapter sequences, ambiguous nucleotides (i.e., "N" bases), or bases with low-quality scores (Phred Q ≤ 20) were systematically removed to generate clean reads. The resulting clean reads were subsequently mapped to the Apostichopus japonicus reference genome (NCBI accession number: GCA_002754855.1).Random library loading and unified sequencing parameters eliminated obvious batch bias, and high reproducibility was observed within biological replicates.

Identification of DEGs and functional enrichment analysis

To ensure analytical accuracy for downstream analyses, TPM values (Transcripts Per Million) were calculated for all genes to normalize for sequencing depth, gene length, and transcript abundance. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using DESeq2 (v1.36.0) with stringent thresholds of |log₂(fold change)| ≥ 1 and adjusted p-value (padj) < 0.05. Functional annotation of DEGs was performed through the Gene Ontology (GO) database (available at www.geneontology.org), and pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database (available at www.kegg.jp), yielding comprehensive functional annotations and pathway associations for the identified DEGs.

qRT-PCR validation of DEGs

To validate the reliability of sequencing results, qRT-PCR was performed using primers designed through NCBI Primer-BLAST (Table S1). A subset of differentially expressed genes identified in transcriptomic analysis was experimentally validated by qRT-PCR. The endogenous β-actin gene of Apostichopus japonicus was used as the reference gene for normalization. Melting curve analysis and amplification were conducted using a real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR detection system (Bio-Rad CFX96) to confirm PCR product specificity. Primer efficiency was verified by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis, and relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2-ΔΔCt method.

Protein-protein interaction network analysis

DEGs were mapped to the STRING database (v11.5) to construct protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks with a minimum interaction confidence score of 0.7. Auxiliary network parameters were set as k-core ≥ 2, degree cutoff = 2, maximum additional interactors = 100, and node confidence score cutoff = 0.2. The confidence threshold of 0.7 was used to filter numerous low-confidence random protein connections and reduce the background noise of the network. The MCC algorithm was selected for topological analysis to identify hub genes due to its higher sensitivity in capturing core regulatory nodes compared with other topological algorithms. Combined with k-core and degree cutoffs, this strategy eliminates marginal weakly connected nodes and accurately screens the top 10 core genes with the highest connectivity in the interaction network. The network was visualized via Cytoscape (v3.9.1), and topologically central hub genes were identified using the MCC algorithm embedded in the cytoHubba plugin.

Bioinformatics analysis

All samples for qPCR were performed in three biological replicates. Data from these experiments were collected and analyzed, and results were expressed as mean±standard deviation (SD) with a significance level of P < 0.05. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s test was used to determine significant differences.

Transcriptomic responses exhibited profound tissue specificity, with over 80% of DEGs being tissue-specific, reflecting fundamental functional differentiation across tissues. Coelomocyte-specific DEGs, which constitute the majority of its total DEGs, were primarily associated with phagosome formation and lysosomal degradation. Intestinal-specific DEGs, also accounting for a high proportion, were concentrated in mucosal barrier formation and inflammatory signal transduction, identifying the intestine as the core target tissue for COS-mediated immunostimulation. Respiratory tree-specific DEGs, representing the largest proportion, were almost exclusively involved in lipid metabolism and energy homeostasis with no significant immune-related enrichment.

Within the small fraction of shared DEGs, a core set of genes common to all three tissues accounted for only a minor percentage of total DEGs. These genes, involved in cellular antioxidant defense, protein folding, and basal energy metabolism, represent a conserved cytoprotective program activated in response to external stimuli. Meanwhile, a notable pool of immune-related DEGs was shared between the intestine and coelomocytes, enriched in antigen processing and presentation pathways, forming a key functional module for cooperative host defense. Collectively, intermittent COS exposure triggered coordinated immune transcriptional programs in the intestine and coelomocytes, while the respiratory tree independently regulated lipid metabolism to support systemic immune activation, together forming an integrated adaptive regulatory network balancing immune activation and metabolic support.

Differentially expressed genes analysis results

DESeq2 (v1.36.0)-based differential expression analysis was performed with the thresholds of | log₂(Fold Change)|>1 and BH-adjusted P < 0.05, which fully confirmed distinct tissue-specific transcriptional responses(Fig. 4). A total of 1099 DEGs were identified in coelomocytes with more upregulated transcripts. 1655 DEGs were detected in intestines with balanced expression tendency. The respiratory tree exhibited the most extensive transcriptomic remodeling with 2100 DEGs dominated by downregulated genes. Notably, 41.2% of respiratory tree DEGs showed strong expression variation with | log₂(Fold Change)|>2, and these downregulated genes were significantly enriched in diverse metabolic pathways. As shown in volcano plots, the three tissues exhibited markedly different gene expression patterns under identical COS treatment. Upregulated genes in coelomocytes possessed higher expression levels, and most immune-related genes were markedly induced to participate in host defense by activating positive immune pathways.The intestine presented stable transcriptional regulation with evenly distributed upregulated and downregulated genes, which could simultaneously mediate immune signal transduction and maintain internal physiological balance.

Significant differences in gene distribution Volcano.

The respiratory tree was obviously dominated by extensive transcriptional repression compared with the other two tissues. Such widespread gene downregulation was not caused by external stimulation-induced tissue damage, but belonged to adaptive metabolic reprogramming triggered by systemic immune activation. Intermittent COS supplementation efficiently activated multiple immune response pathways in intestines and coelomocytes, and massive synthesis of immune effector molecules greatly increased the overall energy consumption of sea cucumbers. As an essential tissue responsible for gas exchange and metabolic processes, the respiratory tree actively inhibited the expression of genes involved in energy-intensive fatty acid metabolism and lipid transport, thereby reducing energy consumption in unnecessary metabolic processes. It further redistributed internal nutrients and energy resources to prioritize supporting immune defense activities, steadily maintained its own basic physiological functions, and provided a reliable metabolic guarantee for systemic immune responses. This regulatory mode plays a crucial role in balancing immune activation and energy metabolism homeostasis of marine invertebrates.

Gene ontology enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes

GO enrichment analysis was performed for DEGs from three tissues. In total, 6325, 10,201, and 9892 DEGs were successfully annotated in coelomocyte, intestinal, and respiratory tree groups, respectively, which were further classified into 65 GO functional terms, including 26 terms in biological process, 20 terms in cellular component, and 19 terms in molecular function (Fig. 5). Distinct functional preferences were observed across different tissues based on enrichment significance and gene distribution. This study mainly focused on terms closely associated with host immune defense to clarify functional regulation differences induced by COS treatment, and the representative significantly enriched functional terms of each tissue were displayed in Fig. 6.

Immunity-related terms were significantly enriched in coelomocyte DEGs, and genes involved in phagocytosis, hydrolase activity, and extracellular antigen recognition occupied a relatively high proportion, which was consistent with their physiological role in cellular immunity and foreign substance elimination. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed to clarify the regulatory pathways involved by DEGs, and distinct differences were observed in pathway quantity, significant enrichment items and expression trends among different tissues (Fig. 7). Intestinal DEGs were annotated into a total of 236 biological pathways, among which 12 pathways were significantly enriched (P < 0.05) (Fig. 8). Ribosome pathway and MAPK signaling pathway showed the highest enrichment degree. These significantly enriched pathways were divided into four categories: basic cellular life activities, immune defense, metabolic regulation, and signal and developmental regulation. The upregulated DEGs were prominently enriched in immune-related pathways such as antigen processing and presentation, Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, and Nod-like receptor signaling pathway, confirming the core status of the intestine in immune signal perception and response activation.

The divergent immune and metabolic responses of different tissues under identical COS treatment were determined by their inherent physiological division, signal perception patterns, and overall in vivo regulatory mechanism. As the primary tissue contacting exogenous substances, the intestine could directly sense COS stimulation and preferentially activate multiple immune signaling pathways. Coelomocytes, as typical immune effector cells, mainly received internal immune signals and focused on immune recognition and pathogen elimination. The respiratory tree, mainly responsible for gas exchange and internal energy allocation, could not directly recognize exogenous immune stimuli. With increased energy consumption caused by systemic immune activation, it actively downregulated energy-intensive metabolic pathways and adjusted its metabolic rhythm to meet the demands of systemic immunity, forming a coordinated response network with clear physiological division of labor in sea cucumbers.

Enrichment analysis of immune-related DEGs

Combined with previous KEGG pathway enrichment results, referring to pathway enrichment and gene expression data in Supplementary Tables 1, 2, and 3, this study analyzed the expression variation of functional genes in typical immune pathways and illustrated the immune response characteristics of different tissues after COS treatment at the molecular level. According to the table data, the intestine showed prominent enrichment in Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, Nod-like receptor signaling pathway, and antigen processing and presentation pathway, and most core genes within these pathways were upregulated, which participated in exogenous stimulus recognition, inflammatory signal transmission, and immune effect regulation to mediate a moderate immune response. Genes related to phagosome and lysosome pathways were generally upregulated in coelomocytes, which participated in foreign substance recognition and intracellular clearance and assisted in maintaining immune homeostasis.

It can be seen from supplementary tables that the most significantly enriched pathways in the respiratory tree belong to metabolic pathways such as fatty acid degradation and glutathione metabolism, while a few immune-related pathways were enriched. Immune functional genes presented stable expression without obvious changes. This tissue mainly adapts to systemic physiological changes by regulating metabolic processes and is rarely involved in specific immune regulation. In general, COS treatment could induce the expression changes of immune pathways and related genes in intestines and coelomocytes to help establish basic immune defense, while the respiratory tree mainly maintained normal metabolic activities with relatively low participation in immune regulation.

qRT-PCR validation of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

To validate the reliability of transcriptome sequencing data, ten DEGs were randomly selected from DEG sets for qRT-PCR validation across three Apostichopus japonicus tissues: coelomocytes, intestinal tissue, and respiratory tree. The results showed that in coelomocytes, MMP16, CYP1A1, MGST1, EML5, and NAPEPLD exhibited significant upregulation, while Orpin, DMBT1, SCNN1B, FCN2, and FGL showed downregulation. In intestinal tissue, HINT1, RCBTB1, PNN, EPS8, and TPK demonstrated significant upregulation, whereas SELP, CTSL, MFI2, DMBT1, and fiberopellin-3 showed significant downregulation. In respiratory tree tissue, MNSFB, FIBCD1, SSR4, RPS24, and RPS13 displayed upregulation trends, while sushi von Willebrand factor type A, AprA, PLB1, MALRD1, and AChE exhibited downregulation trends.

Collectively, the expression patterns of all 30 DEGs validated by qRT-PCR were completely consistent with the RNA-seq results, confirming the accuracy and reliability of the transcriptome sequencing data for subsequent in-depth analysis (Fig. 11).

Protein-protein interaction network of DEGs

In this study, the STRING database combined with Cytoscape software was applied to construct PPI networks based on 1099 DEGs in coelomocytes, 1655 DEGs in intestines, and 2011 DEGs in respiratory trees, respectively. A total of ten hub genes, including RSL1D1, NOC4L, GNL2, DDX21, DDX31, PYGB, PKM, SLC3A1, RPA1, and SAS10, were screened out from coelomocytes (Fig. 12A). These genes were mainly annotated to basic biological processes such as RNA metabolism, energy metabolism, and ribosome formation, and some were involved in substance transport and nucleolus-related functions. Among them, RSL1D1 and NOC4L exhibited higher connectivity and occupied core positions in the interaction network. The major hub genes in intestinal tissues were RPL17, RPL23A, RPL38, and RPS7 (Fig. 12B), while hub genes in respiratory trees were represented by RPS16, RPS8, RPS6, and RPS26 (Fig. 12C). Functional characteristics of hub genes in the intestines and respiratory trees were highly similar. Most core members belonged to RPL and RPS ribosomal protein families, which were enriched in ribosome-related pathways and participated in ribosome assembly and basic protein translation regulation.

Combined with COS induction and immune response characteristics reflected by PPI network results, these core hub genes exerted practical regulatory effects during physiological changes. Under COS treatment, the expression alteration of ribosomal protein hub genes in intestines and respiratory trees could regulate intracellular protein translation efficiency, and further support the normal expression of immune-related functional proteins. The hub genes in coelomocytes regulated cellular metabolic level via mediating RNA metabolism and energy metabolism, to maintain stable physiological activities in the process of immune response. Consistent with the topological characteristics of the PPI network, these hub genes mainly functioned by regulating basic biosynthesis and metabolic processes, and provided necessary internal conditions for COS-induced immune regulation and physiological adaptation.

Disscusion

In aquaculture, chitooligosaccharides (COS) are widely recognized as safe immunostimulants capable of reshaping transcriptional profiles and activating innate immune cascades to improve pathogen resistance of aquatic organisms(Han [13]). Apostichopus japonicus lacks specialized immune organs, and coelomocytes, intestines, and respiratory trees jointly constitute its innate immune and metabolic regulatory system(Ye [14]). Previous feeding trials have proven that COS can enhance immune enzyme activities of sea cucumbers, yet most research only focuses on physiological phenotypes, and the multi-tissue coordinated molecular response network remains unclear(Dang [15]). In this study, intermittent COS feeding treatment was carried out, and integrated transcriptomic analysis was performed on coelomocytes, intestines, and respiratory trees. Combined with DEG Venn analysis, GO/KEGG enrichment, and PPI network data, we systematically decipher tissue-specific coordinated immune-metabolic responses triggered by COS.

Heat shock proteins are evolutionarily conserved molecular chaperones, among which HSP70, HSP90, and small HSP26 undertake core functions including antigen processing and cellular stress homeostasis maintenance. According to the immune pathway gene expression data in Supplementary Table 2, HSP70 and HSP26 were synchronously upregulated in the intestinal antigen processing and presentation pathway after COS treatment. Existing in vitro protein interaction studies have verified that HSP70 can bind antigen peptides cleaved by CTSL and stabilize antigen peptide loading ([16], p. 2019; Hongjuan [17,18]). HSP26 stabilizes the cytoskeleton and eliminates reactive oxygen species, creating a mild intracellular environment to support the activation of intestinal mucosal immune cells [19]. In contrast, HSP70 was significantly downregulated in coelomocytes, accompanied by decreased expression of FADD, a key adaptor mediating extrinsic apoptotic signals. Downregulation of FADD attenuates apoptotic signals and stabilizes the population of immune cells(Sun [20]). Based on mRNA expression differences observed in this transcriptome dataset, intestinal mucosal barrier activation by COS reduces the invasion of exogenous antigens into coelomocytes and alleviates persistent stress burden, thereby lowering cellular demand for HSP70-mediated stress protection. Such divergent expression patterns imply tissue-specific stress response tendencies in sea cucumbers. Nevertheless, this study only provides transcript-level evidence, without direct functional verification such as protein quantification, cellular stress detection, and gene interference. We cannot draw definitive conclusions that this bidirectional pattern is controlled by systemic immune resource redistribution. Such inference is merely a transcriptional trend observed in this trial, and further in vitro cellular stimulation tests are required for validation.

For TLR, NLR, and MAPK innate immune pathways enriched in the intestines, this paper mainly focuses on the shared core regulatory molecules identified in our research. KEGG analysis of intestines revealed synchronous significant enrichment of TLR, NLR, and MAPK pathways, with TRAF6, TBK1, and MAP3K7IP2 serving as shared signal connectors. TBK1 mediates interferon-related immune signaling(Yu [21]), while MAP3K7IP2 receives ubiquitinated signals from TRAF6 and initiates downstream MAPK kinase cascades ([22]; Sun [23]). As the central hub of the three pathways, TRAF6 integrates pattern recognition signals and amplifies transcription programs related to inflammatory responses(Huang [24]; Li [[25], [26], [27]]). Transcript data showed synchronous upregulation of TRAF6 and TBK1 in COS-treated intestines. Combined with existing studies on sea cucumber antibacterial responses, it is suggested that COS can synergistically activate intestinal mucosal innate immunity by upregulating these core molecules and strengthen recognition and clearance of exogenous microorganisms(Li [28]). Different from the robust immune activation in the intestines, no typical immune pathways were enriched in the respiratory trees. Differentially expressed genes of respiratory trees were mainly enriched in the PPAR lipid metabolism pathway, with key lipid oxidation genes such as ACOX1 synchronously downregulated. The PPAR pathway exerts anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting NF-κB nuclear translocation and DNA binding activity [29].ACOX1 serves as a key target gene within the PPAR signaling pathway, promoting fatty acid oxidation and energy metabolism [30].The PPARα agonist MHY553 enhances fatty acid β-oxidation by upregulating ACOX1 expression [31,32].The classical NF-κB immune axis downstream of PPAR was not enriched in DEGs of respiratory trees, indicating that COS mainly regulates lipid remodeling of respiratory trees under current experimental conditions, without synchronously inducing immune transcriptional changes via the PPAR cascade. Immune transcriptional fluctuation in the respiratory trees remained mild. It can be inferred that after intestinal and coelomic immune activation, energy resources are preferentially allocated to immune tissues, and respiratory trees reduce energy consumption by inhibiting non-essential fatty acid oxidation. However, this coordinated energy allocation trend is only deduced from pathway enrichment results, which still need to be supplemented by physiological and biochemical indicators for verification.

The biological roles of ribosomal hub genes in immune regulation can be rigorously evaluated according to topological characteristics of PPI networks. In this study, core hub genes of PPI networks in intestines and respiratory trees were mostly RPL/RPS ribosomal proteins (RPL17, RPL23A, RPS6, RPS16), while central nodes of coelomocytes tended to RNA and nucleolus regulatory genes (NOC4L, RSL1D1). As core components of translation machinery, ribosomal proteins fundamentally mediate whole-cell protein synthesis [33]. Based on the immune and transcriptional changes observed in this study, their auxiliary immune functions can be reasonably inferred. Massive upregulation of immune-related genes, including TRAF6, TBK1, HSP70, and CTSL in intestines requires elevated translational efficiency to synthesize immune receptors, inflammatory mediators, and antigen-processing proteases. This explains the dominant connectivity of RPL/RPS hub genes in intestinal networks, providing material support for intestinal immune activation. Although no obvious immune pathway activation was observed in respiratory trees, the ribosome pathway was independently upregulated to sustain continuous translation of gas exchange-related basal proteins and compensate for limited synthesis caused by suppressed lipid metabolism. Accordingly, ribosomal proteins with high connectivity in the PPI network do not function as direct regulators of immune signal transduction or inflammatory responses. They act as universal translational guarantors providing basal metabolic support for tissue-specific responses induced by COS, serving as auxiliary prerequisites rather than core immune regulators. NOC4L and RSL1D1 exhibited the highest connectivity in coelomic networks(Ding [34]; Wang [35,36]). The two genes play distinct roles. One balances excessive inflammatory signals while the other suppresses immune cell apoptosis. They work alongside phagocytosis-related genes to sustain coelomic immune homeostasis, offsetting the limitation that ribosomal proteins cannot directly regulate inflammatory signals.

Comprehensive analysis of DEG Venn results, pathway enrichment, and PPI networks shows that different tissues of Apostichopus japonicus exhibit divergent transcriptional responses under COS stimulation. The intestine forms mucosal immune defense relying on TRAF6, TBK1, and HSP family molecules. Coelomocytes downregulate apoptotic and stress-related genes to reduce the overall immune burden of the organism. Respiratory trees adjust lipid metabolism to realize adaptive energy allocation. Obvious differences exist in the upregulation amplitude of immune-related genes among different tissues, and divergent response intensity can be observed at the transcriptional level. All analytical conclusions of this study are derived from transcriptomic mRNA data and can only reflect transcriptional variations of genes. To fully clarify the complete molecular mechanisms of the HSP family, TRAF6 and ribosomal hub genes mediating immune-metabolic crosstalk, follow-up functional experiments, including gene interference, western blot, and ex vivo tissue incubation, can be carried out for further validation.

Conclusions

In summary, this work performed integrated transcriptomic profiling of coelomocytes, intestinal tissue and respiratory tree from Apostichopus japonicus under intermittent COS feeding. Reliable transcriptome data were validated via qRT-PCR in this study, while most prior COS-related transcriptomic researches only adopted single-tissue sampling or continuous feeding schemes. Our three-organ joint dataset provides robust supplementary omic evidence for the tissue-divergent immune and metabolic responses triggered by COS.

Comprehensive cross-tissue comparison uncovered a coordinated partitioned regulatory mode shaped by COS stimulation. As the main mucosal defensive tissue, the intestine exhibited coordinated elevated expression of HSP70 and HSP26 alongside activated MAPK, TLR and NLR innate immune signaling modules. TRAF6 was identified as a potential core signaling candidate that bridges these interconnected immune pathways. Coelomocytes serve as regulators maintaining systemic homeostasis. Their transcriptional abundance of HSP70 and pro-apoptotic FADD decreased noticeably. Two candidate hub genes NOC4L and RSL1D with high network connectivity may work together to relieve overactive inflammation and preserve the survival of immune cells. Unlike the intestine and coelomocytes that focus on immune responses, the respiratory tree mainly undergoes lipid metabolic remodeling. Its PPAR signaling pathway was broadly inhibited, while ribosome-related transcripts were selectively upregulated to sustain fundamental gas exchange functions amid reduced fatty acid breakdown. Ribosomal protein genes dominated hub nodes in both intestinal and respiratory PPI networks, which universally supply fundamental translational machinery to match tissue-specific physiological requirements.

Collectively, the multi-tissue transcriptional profiles systematically illustrate compartmentalized immune activation and metabolic adaptation triggered by COS across sea cucumber tissues. These findings provide reliable molecular evidence to facilitate the application of intermittent COS feeding in aquaculture. It should be noted that all regulatory inferences in this study are drawn solely from mRNA transcriptomic data, and dedicated functional assays are needed in follow-up work to validate the authentic biological functions of these candidate hub genes and associated physiological hypotheses.
