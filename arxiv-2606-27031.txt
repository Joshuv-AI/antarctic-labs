Local stability for a class of Saint-Venant type inequalities
Luca Barbato, Francesco Salerno

Abstract
We establish a local stability result for a class of Saint–Venant type inequalities. Given the solution u u of the Dirichlet torsion problem in a domain Ω \Omega , we consider shape functionals 𝒥 ⁡ ( Ω ) \mathcal{J}(\Omega) involving the integral of j ⁡ ( u ) j(u) , where j j is convex and satisfies suitable structural assumptions. By Talenti’s comparison principle, balls maximize 𝒥 \mathcal{J} among sets of prescribed measure. We prove that this extremal property is stable in the class of nearly spherical sets: the deficit from the optimal value controls the square of the H 1 / 2 H^{1/2} -norm of the boundary perturbation. The argument relies on shape derivative techniques, including the computation of the second variation and the introduction of an adjoint state. As applications, the result covers several relevant examples, including the torsional rigidity, L p L^{p} -norms of the torsion function for p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , and Moser–Trudinger functional in dimension two. Keywords: Shape Derivative, Adjoint state, Torsional rigidity. MSC 2020: 35J05, 35B35, 49K40.

Introduction

In their seminal paper
[
9
]
, Brasco, De Philippis and Velichkov proved a sharp quantitative version of the Faber–Krahn inequality for the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet Laplacian. More precisely, for an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, with finite measure, the first eigenvalue is defined by

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)=\min_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\left\{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx:\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1\right\}.

It is well known, by the Faber–Krahn inequality, that
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is minimized among sets of fixed measure by the ball
\Omega^{\sharp}
having the same Lebesgue measure as
\Omega
. Brasco, De Philippis and Velichkov strengthened this result by proving a quantitative stability estimate of the form

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(\Omega^{\sharp})\geq c(n)\,d(\Omega,\Omega^{\sharp})^{2},

where
d(\Omega,\Omega^{\sharp})
denotes a suitable measure of the deviation of
\Omega
from spherical symmetry. Actually this result was the first with sharp exponent on the asymmetry, while results with different exponent were already available in literature (see for instance
[
7
,
14
,
21
]
).

A key step in their analysis consists in reducing the problem to a quantitative version of the Saint–Venant inequality. The central object in this setting is the torsional rigidity, defined as the
L^{1}
-norm of the solution to the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=1&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1)

Namely,

T(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}u\,dx.

In the planar case, this quantity represents the resistance to torsion of an elastic beam whose cross-section is
\Omega
. The classical Saint–Venant inequality asserts that, among sets of fixed measure,
T(\Omega)
is maximized by the ball
\Omega^{\sharp}
. Brasco, De Philippis and Velichkov proved the corresponding quantitative stability estimate

T(\Omega^{\sharp})-T(\Omega)\geq c(n)\,d(\Omega,\Omega^{\sharp})^{2}.

This result belongs to a broad literature on quantitative inequalities for spectral and geometric functionals
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
15
]
, see also
[
18
, Chapter 7]
. Related stability questions have also been investigated in the more general context of solutions to PDEs
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
20
]
.

The classical Saint–Venant inequality can be derived from Talenti’s celebrated comparison principle
[
23
]
, which compares the solution of (
1
) with the solution of a suitable symmetrized problem posed on
\Omega^{\sharp}
. This comparison also yields the so-called mass comparison principle; see
[
2
]
. As a consequence, the ball is extremal, among sets of prescribed measure, for a broad class of shape functionals of the form

\mathcal{J}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}j(u)\,dx,

where
j:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex, nonnegative, Lipschitz continuous function satisfying
j(0)=0
.

The aim of the present paper is to extend the quantitative stability result discussed above to this more general class of functionals, at least within the class of nearly spherical sets. More precisely, we consider bounded sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose boundary is a small normal perturbation of the unit sphere. Namely, there exists a function
\varphi\in C^{2,\gamma}(\partial B_{1})
, with
\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{2},
such that

\partial\Omega=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x=(1+\varphi(y))y,\quad y\in\partial B_{1}\right\}.

Our approach is based on the method of shape derivatives; we refer to
[
17
]
for a comprehensive introduction to the subject. In this framework, one considers a volume-preserving deformation
\{\Omega(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
of the unit ball, with
\Omega(0)=B_{1}
, and studies the corresponding Gâteaux derivative of the functional. Formally, this derivative is given by

d\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[V]=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{J}(\Omega(t))-\mathcal{J}(B_{1})}{t},

where
V
denotes the velocity field associated with the deformation.

In particular, we shall use a family of sets
\Omega(t)
, starting from
\Omega(0)=B_{1}
, whose evolution is described by the following lemma. This result is taken from
[
9
, Lemma A.1]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Given
\gamma\in(0,1]
there exists
\delta_{e}=\delta_{e}(n,\gamma)>0
and a modulus of continuity
\omega_{e}
such that, for every nearly spherical set
\Omega
parametrized by
\varphi
with
\|\varphi\|_{C^{2,\gamma}(\partial B_{1})}\leq\delta_{e}
and
{\left|\Omega\right|}={\left|B_{1}\right|}
, we can find an autonomous vector field
V_{\varphi}
for which the following holds true:

(i)

\mathrm{div}\,V_{\varphi}=0
in a
\delta_{e}
-neighborhood of
\partial B_{1}
,

(ii)

if
\Phi_{t}:=\Phi(t,x)
is the flow of
V_{\varphi}
, i.e.

\partial_{t}\Phi_{t}=V_{\varphi}(\Phi_{t}),\quad\Phi_{0}(x)=x,

then
\Phi_{1}(\partial B_{1})=\partial\Omega
and
{\left|\Phi_{t}(B_{1})\right|}={\left|B_{1}\right|}
for all
t\in[0,1]
,

(iii)

We have

\|\Phi_{t}-Id\|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\leq\omega_{e}(\|\varphi\|_{C^{2,\gamma}(\partial B_{1})})\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,1],

\|\varphi-(V_{\varphi}\cdot\nu_{0})\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}\leq\omega_{e}(\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})})\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})},

and

(V_{\varphi}\cdot\theta)\circ\Phi_{t}-V_{\varphi}\cdot\nu_{0}=(V_{\varphi}\cdot\nu_{0})\psi_{t}\quad\text{on }\partial B_{1},

with
\|\psi_{t}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}(\partial B_{1})}\leq\omega_{e}(\|\varphi\|_{C^{2,\gamma}(\partial B_{1})})
.

For notational simplicity, throughout the paper we shall omit the subscript and write
V
instead of
V_{\varphi}
. Therefore, by additionally assuming that

•

j
is of class
C^{2}
;

•

j^{\prime}
is positive in
(0,\delta)
for some
\delta\geq\frac{1}{2n}
;

•

j^{\prime\prime}
is bounded.

Our main result states as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(Stability for nearly spherical sets)
.

Let
0<\gamma\leq 1
. Then there exist
\delta_{c}=\delta_{c}(n,\gamma,j)
and
C(n,j)>0
such that if
\Omega
is a nearly spherical set of class
C^{2,\gamma}
parametrized by
\varphi
with

\|\varphi\|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\leq\delta_{c},\quad{\left|\Omega\right|}={\left|B_{1}\right|},\quad\text{and}\quad x_{\Omega}=0,

then

\mathcal{J}(B_{1})-\mathcal{J}(\Omega)\geq C(n,j)\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}.

We emphasize that, although our result is restricted to the class of nearly spherical sets, the stability deficit obtained in terms of the
H^{1/2}
-norm captures both volumetric and oscillatory information, since it controls not only the size of the perturbation but also the oscillations of the boundary.

The main difference with respect to
[
9
]
, which corresponds to the special case
j(s)=s
, lies in the structure of the second variation. Since the functional considered here is no longer simply the torsional rigidity, its second shape derivative contains additional terms depending on the second material derivative of the state function. These terms have to be either controlled or eliminated from the expression of the second variation. For this purpose, we introduce an adjoint state, namely the solution of a suitable auxiliary boundary value problem, which allows us to remove precisely this dependence and to obtain a tractable second-order expansion.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

3

4
we prove improved continuity and coercivity which are the main tools in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, in the Appendix

2
Notations and Preliminaries

2.1
Rearrangements and comparison principles

Throughout this section,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes a measurable set with finite measure. We write
{\left|E\right|}
for the Lebesgue measure of a measurable set
E
and
\omega_{n}
for the measure of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is measurable, we denote by
E^{\sharp}
the open ball centered at the origin such that

{\left|E^{\sharp}\right|}={\left|E\right|}.

We recall the basic notation concerning rearrangements.

Definition 2.1

(Decreasing rearrangement)
.

Let
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function. Its distribution function is defined by

\mu_{u}(t):={\left|\{x\in\Omega:{\left|u(x)\right|}>t\}\right|},\qquad t\geq 0.

The decreasing rearrangement of
u
is the function
u^{\ast}:(0,{\left|\Omega\right|})\to[0,+\infty]
given by

u^{\ast}(s):=\inf\{t\geq 0:\mu_{u}(t)\leq s\}.

Equivalently,
u^{\ast}
is the unique nonincreasing, right-continuous function which is equimeasurable with
{\left|u\right|}
.

For a comprehensive account of the main properties of rearrangements and their applications to variational problems, we refer to
[
19
]
.

Rearrangement techniques are a classical tool in the proof of sharp geometric inequalities. In particular, they were used by Pólya to prove Saint-Venant’s inequality for the torsional rigidity
[
22
]
, which states that, among all domains with prescribed measure, the ball maximizes the torsional rigidity. A different and very influential approach was later introduced by Talenti
[
23
]
. More precisely, let
u
be the solution of the torsion problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=1&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

and let
v
be the solution of the corresponding problem in the ball
\Omega^{\sharp}
. Talenti’s comparison principle gives the sharp pointwise estimate

u^{\ast}(s)\leq v^{\ast}(s),\qquad s\in(0,{\left|\Omega\right|}).

In particular, this implies the integral comparison

\int_{\Omega}u\,dx=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}u^{\ast}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}v^{\ast}(s)\,ds=\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}v\,dx,

which is precisely Saint-Venant’s inequality.

We shall use the following standard characterization of comparison by mass concentration; see, for instance,
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(\Omega)
be nonnegative functions. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

for every
t\in[0,{\left|\Omega\right|}]
,

\int_{0}^{t}f^{\ast}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}g^{\ast}(s)\,ds;

2.

for every nonnegative
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}g^{\ast}(s)\varphi^{\ast}(s)\,ds;

3.

for every nonnegative
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{\ast}(s)\varphi^{\ast}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}g^{\ast}(s)\varphi^{\ast}(s)\,ds;

4.

for every nonnegative Lipschitz convex function
F
such that
F(0)=0
,

\int_{\Omega}F(f)\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}F(g)\,dx.

In this terminology, Talenti’s theorem asserts that the solution of the torsion problem in a general domain is less concentrated than the corresponding radially symmetric solution in the ball of the same measure. In the applications below, this will provide the maximality of the ball for the functional
\mathcal{J}(\Omega)
.

2.2
Harmonic extension and spherical harmonics

Definition 2.2
.

Given a function
\varphi:\partial B_{1}\to\R
we define

\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}:=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\varphi^{2}\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla H(\varphi)|^{2}\,dx,

where
H(\varphi)
is the
W^{1,2}
harmonic extension of
\varphi
, i.e.

\begin{cases}-\Delta H(\varphi)=0&\text{ in }B_{1}\\
H(\varphi)=\varphi&\text{ on }\partial B_{1}.\end{cases}

In particular
H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})
, equipped with this norm, is a Hilbert space. We also recall that the trace Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality.

\int_{\partial B_{1}}{\left|w-\int_{\partial B_{1}}w\right|}^{2}\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{B_{1}}{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{2}\,dx,\qquad w\in W^{1,2}(B_{1}),

implies

{\left\|\nabla H(\varphi)\right\|}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\leq{\left\|\varphi\right\|}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}\leq\sqrt{2}{\left\|\nabla H(\varphi)\right\|}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\quad\text{for every }\varphi\text{ s.t. }\int_{\partial B_{1}}\varphi\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0.

We now recall some basic facts about spherical harmonics; these functions form a Hilbert basis of
L^{2}(\partial B_{1})
, and hence also provides the standard spectral decomposition in
H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})
(see, for instance,
[
8
, Remark 4.1]
).
In our setting, assuming that
\xi
is orthogonal to spherical harmonics of degree
0
and
1
, we may write

\xi(\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}Y_{k}(\theta)\quad\text{where }\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Its harmonic extension is then given by

H(\xi)(x)=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}{\left|x\right|}^{k}Y_{k}\left(\frac{x}{{\left|x\right|}}\right)\quad\text{where }x\in B_{1}.

By orthonormality, it follows that

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi^{2}\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}^{2}.

Moreover, using polar coordinates, we obtain

\int_{B_{1}}H(\xi)^{2}\,dx=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2k+n-1}\,dr=\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{c_{k}^{2}}{2k+n}\leq\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi^{2}\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Finally, since
H(\xi)
is harmonic, its Dirichlet energy can be expressed in terms of the Steklov eigenvalues:

\int_{B_{1}}{\left|\nabla H(\xi)\right|}^{2}\,dx=\sum_{k\geq 2}\sigma_{k}(B_{1})c_{k}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 2}kc_{k}^{2},

where the last equality follows from the fact that the Steklov eigenvalue associated with spherical harmonics of degree
k
on the unit ball is precisely
k
.

2.3
Adjoint state

In this subsection, we show how the introduction of an auxiliary problem, namely the
adjoint problem
, can sometimes simplify the expression of the derivatives of shape functionals. We explain this idea for functions that vanish on the boundary of
\Omega
, although the technique is not specific to Dirichlet boundary conditions (see
[
17
, §5.8]
for a more comprehensive discussion of the topic).

The main idea is to eliminate the derivative
\dot{u}_{t}
from the expression of the derivative of the functional. More precisely, when dealing with a function
u_{t}
that vanishes on
\partial\Omega_{t}
, we obtain the following information on
\dot{u}_{t}|_{\partial\Omega_{t}}
:

\dot{u}_{t}=-\nabla u_{t}\cdot V\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{t},

where
V
is the velocity vector field associated with the deformation under consideration. It then remains to handle the occurrences of
\dot{u}_{t}
inside the integrals over
\Omega_{t}
. Having explained the idea and motivation behind the adjoint problem, let us now define it.

Consider the following shape functional:

g(t)=\mathcal{G}(\Omega_{t})=\int_{\Omega_{t}}G(x,u_{t}(x),\nabla u_{t}(x))\,dx,

where
G=G(x,u,q)
is a sufficiently regular function. Then, using Hadamard’s formula, we obtain

g^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega_{t}}[\dot{u}_{t}\partial_{u}G+\nabla_{q}G\cdot\nabla\dot{u}_{t}]\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}G\,V\cdot\nu_{t}\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(2)

From a heuristic point of view, we now want to replace
\dot{u}_{t}
with a test function
v
in the expression of
g^{\prime}(t)
in order to derive a variational formulation for the equation satisfied by
p_{t}
. This leads to the following adjoint problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta p_{t}+p_{t}=\partial_{u}G-\mathrm{div}_{x}(\nabla_{q}G)&\text{ in }\Omega_{t},\\
p_{t}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{t}.\end{cases}

Multiplying the equation by
\dot{u}_{t}
and integrating by parts, we obtain

-\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\dot{u}_{t}\frac{\partial p_{t}}{\partial\nu_{t}}\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega_{t}}[\dot{u}_{t}\partial_{u}G+\nabla\dot{u}_{t}\cdot\nabla_{q}G]\,dx-\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\dot{u}_{t}\nabla_{q}G\cdot\nu_{t}\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Using (
2
), we then deduce

g^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}(V\cdot\nu_{t})\left[\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu_{t}}\frac{\partial p_{t}}{\partial\nu_{t}}+G-\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu_{t}}\nabla_{q}G\cdot\nu_{t}\right]\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(3)

Comparing (
2
) and (
3
), we see that all contributions involving
\dot{u}_{t}
in
\Omega_{t}
have been eliminated. Furthermore, this will allow us to exclude a priori any dependence on
\ddot{u}_{t}
in the second derivative of the functional for terms defined on
\Omega
.

3
First and Second variation

Defining
f(t):=\mathcal{J}(\Omega(t))
, in the next results we compute the first and second variation of the shape functional.

Moreover, we emphasize that, here and in what follows, since all the functions under consideration are radial in
t=0
, whenever
{\left|\nabla\cdot\right|}
appears outside an integral, it is to be understood as the constant value it attains on
\partial B_{1}
.

Lemma 3.1

(First Variation)
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
1.1
, we have

f^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Proof.

Recalling the well-known
Hadamard’s formula

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}f(x,t)\,dx=\int_{\Omega(t)}[f_{t}(x,t)+\mathrm{div}(fV)]\,dx,

we can compute the first derivative as follows

f^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega(t)}j^{\prime}(u_{t})\dot{u}_{t}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}j(u_{t})(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

From the boundary data in (
1
), since the vector field
V
is divergence-free in a neighborhood of
\partial B_{1}
(where
\partial\Omega(t)
is contained), we have that the boundary integral is zero. Looking at (
1
) we remark that
\dot{u}
solves the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta\dot{u}_{t}=0&\text{ in }\Omega(t)\\
\dot{u}_{t}=-\nabla u_{t}\cdot V&\text{ on }\partial\Omega(t).\end{cases}

(4)

We want now to introduce an auxiliary problem, known in literature as
adjoint problem
(see §
2
for more details), which will allow us to remove the dependence on
\dot{u}
in
f^{\prime}(t)
, and therefore remove the dependence on
\ddot{u}
in
f^{\prime\prime}(t)
. More precisely we consider the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta p_{t}=j^{\prime}(u_{t})&\text{ in }\Omega(t)\\
p_{t}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega(t).\end{cases}

(5)

With this notation, we can rewrite
f^{\prime}(t)
as follows

f^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega(t)}-\Delta p_{t}\dot{u}_{t}\,dx=-\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\dot{u}_{t}\nabla p_{t}\cdot\nu_{t}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where we have applied
Green’s formula
and (
4
)-(
5
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Recalling that
\Omega(0)=B_{1}
and
u_{0}
,
p_{0}
are radial functions, we have that

f^{\prime}(0)={\left|\nabla u_{0}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}\int_{\partial B_{1}}V\cdot\nu_{0}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0,

which is not surprising since, from Talenti’s inequality, we already know that the ball is a maximizer for
\mathcal{J}(\cdot)
.

Lemma 3.2

(Second Variation)
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
1.1
, we have

\begin{split}f^{\prime\prime}(t)=&-2\int_{\Omega(t)}\nabla\dot{u}_{t}\cdot\nabla\dot{p}_{t}\,dx+\int_{\Omega(t)}j^{\prime\prime}(u_{t})\dot{u}_{t}^{2}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\frac{{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}}{{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}}\langle D^{2}u_{t}.\nabla u_{t},V^{\tau}\rangle(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\frac{{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}}{{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}}\langle D^{2}p_{t}.\nabla p_{t},V^{\tau}\rangle(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}(V\cdot\nu_{t})^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}j^{\prime}(u_{t})(V\cdot\nu_{t})^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-2\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}\mathscr{H}_{\partial\Omega(t)}(V\cdot\nu_{t})^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

(6)

Proof.

Since we want to apply the Hadamard’s formula to
f^{\prime}(t)
, we rewrite the boundary integral, using the divergence Theorem, as follows

f^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega(t)}\mathrm{div}({\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}V)\,dx.

Then we can compute the second derivative

f^{\prime\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega(t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathrm{div}({\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}V)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\mathrm{div}({\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}V)(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(7)

Regarding the first integral, since
V
is an autonomous vector field, applying again the divergence Theorem, we have that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega(t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathrm{div}({\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}V)\,dx&=\int_{\Omega(t)}\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}({\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|})V\right)\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}({\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|})(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\left[{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}\frac{\nabla u_{t}}{{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}}\cdot\nabla\dot{u}_{t}+{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}\frac{\nabla p_{t}}{{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}}\cdot\nabla\dot{p}_{t}\right](V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=-\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\left[{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}\frac{\partial\dot{u}_{t}}{\partial\nu_{t}}+{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}\frac{\partial\dot{p}_{t}}{\partial\nu_{t}}\right](V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

(8)

Now we want to rewrite the second term in (
7
). For simplicity we recall that

\nabla({\left|\nabla g\right|})\cdot V=\frac{\langle D^{2}g.\nabla g,V\rangle}{{\left|\nabla g\right|}}.

Then, recalling again that
\mathrm{div}V=0
in a neighborhood of
\partial B_{1}
, the boundary integral in (
7
) can be rewritten as follows

\begin{split}\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\mathrm{div}({\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}V)(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}&=\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}\frac{\langle D^{2}u_{t}.\nabla u_{t},V\rangle}{{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}}(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|}\frac{\langle D^{2}p_{t}.\nabla p_{t},V\rangle}{{\left|\nabla p_{t}\right|}}(V\cdot\nu_{t})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

(9)

Finally, combining (
8
)-(
9

4
Stability for nearly spherical sets

In order to study the stability of critical shapes, one cannot rely on a positivity condition for the full second shape derivative with respect to arbitrary deformation fields. Indeed, shape functionals are invariant under reparametrizations of the domain: tangential deformations, or more generally diffeomorphisms leaving the set unchanged, do not modify the shape and therefore necessarily generate degenerate directions.

As in the first-order Hadamard structure theorem (see
[
12
]
), the relevant information is carried by the normal component of the perturbation. At a critical shape, the second variation reduces to a quadratic form acting on normal boundary deformations. Hence, in the class of nearly spherical sets, stability is obtained by proving coercivity of this quadratic form on the admissible perturbations, after removing the neutral directions associated with the invariances of the problem.

This coercivity estimate must be combined with an improved continuity property of the second variation. The latter ensures that the quadratic expansion remains uniform along nearly spherical perturbations and that the nonlinear remainder does not destroy the positivity given by the second variation. These two ingredients, improved continuity and coercivity, are therefore the key tools for deriving local stability results.

Hence, our first step is to evaluate
f^{\prime\prime}
at

. In order to do this, we recall that
\dot{u}_{0}
is exactly the harmonic extension

. Then our aim is to write

, that is not harmonic, in terms of an harmonic extension. To this aim, we introduce the following Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta Q=j^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})H(V\cdot\nu_{0})&\text{ in }B_{1}\\
Q=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{1}.\end{cases}

(13)

Then, since the harmonic extension satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta H(V\cdot\nu_{0})=0&\text{ in }B_{1}\\
H(V\cdot\nu_{0})={\left|\nabla u_{0}\right|}V\cdot\nu_{0}&\text{ on }\partial B_{1},\end{cases}

(14)

from (
13
)-(
14

\dot{p}_{0}={\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}H(V\cdot\nu_{0})+{\left|\nabla u_{0}\right|}Q.

The last gives us

4.2
Coercivity

Let us now define the bilinear form induced by the second order shape derivative:

\begin{split}\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]=&2{\left|\nabla u_{0}\right|}{\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}\left\{\int_{B_{1}}{\left|\nabla H(\xi)\right|}^{2}\,dx+\left[n-1-\frac{1}{2{\left|\nabla u_{0}\right|}}-\frac{j^{\prime}(0)}{2{\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}}\right]\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right\}\\
&-{\left|\nabla u_{0}\right|}^{2}\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})H(\xi)^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

(25)

Remark 4.1
.

It holds

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[V\cdot\nu_{0},V\cdot\nu_{0}]=-f^{\prime\prime}(0)>0,

since
B_{1}
is a maximum for
\mathcal{J}(\cdot)

In the next result we prove the continuity for
\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\cdot,\cdot]
, which will be useful in the proof of the coercivity.

Lemma 4.2

(Continuity of
\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})
)
.

There exist a constant
c_{1}(n,j)
such that

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]\leq c_{1}(n,j)\|\xi_{1}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}\|\xi_{2}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}.

Proof.

By a triangle inequality we get

\begin{split}{\left|\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]\right|}\leq&\frac{2}{n}{\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}\int_{B_{1}}{\left|\nabla H(\xi_{1})\cdot\nabla H(\xi_{2})\right|}\,dx\\
+&\frac{2}{n}{\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}{\left|\frac{n}{2}-1-\frac{j^{\prime}(0)}{2{\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}}\right|}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
+&\frac{1}{n^{2}}{\left|\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})H(\xi_{1})H(\xi_{2})\,dx\right|}.\end{split}

On the first two term the continuity follows just after a Hölder inequality and the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}
. Let us now focus on the last term, by boundedness of
j^{\prime\prime}
and Hölder inequality we find

{\left|\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})H(\xi_{1})H(\xi_{2})\,dx\right|}\leq{\left\|j^{\prime\prime}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}{\left\|H(\xi_{1})\right\|}_{2}{\left\|H(\xi_{2})\right\|}_{2}\leq\frac{{\left\|j^{\prime\prime}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{n}\|\xi_{1}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}\|\xi_{2}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}.

∎

Lemma 4.3

(Coercivity)
.

Let
\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})
be the symmetric and continuous bilinear form on
H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})
defined in (
25
). Then there exists
\delta>0
depending on
j,n
and a constant
c_{2}(n,j,\delta)
such that

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]\geq c_{2}(n,j,\delta){\left\|\xi\right\|}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}\quad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta},

where

\mathcal{M}_{\delta}=\left\{\xi\in H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})\,:\,{\left|\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right|}+{\left|\int_{\partial B_{1}}x\xi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right|}\leq\delta{\left\|\xi\right\|}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}\right\}.

Proof.

Let us focus first on proving the estimate in the space
\mathcal{M}_{0}
that is defined as

\mathcal{M}_{0}=\left\{\xi\in H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})\,:\,\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}x_{i}\xi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0,\,i=1,\dots,n\right\}.

First notice by Green’s identity we have that:

\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime}(u_{0})\,dx=\int_{B_{1}}-\Delta p_{0}\,dx=\int_{\partial B_{1}}-\frac{\partial p_{0}}{\partial\nu}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}{\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}={\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}n\omega_{n},

therefore, to simplify the notation, let us set

{\left|\nabla p_{0}\right|}=\frac{\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime}(u_{0})\,dx}{n\omega_{n}}=\gamma_{j}.

Moreover, by introducing the basis of spherical harmonics in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, as defined before, then each term in (
25
) becomes

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{1}}&\xi^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}^{2},\\
\int_{B_{1}}&{\left|\nabla H(\xi)\right|}^{2}\,dx=\sum_{k\geq 2}kc_{k}^{2},\\
\int_{B_{1}}&j^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})H(\xi)^{2}\,dx=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}j^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1-r^{2}}{2n}\right)r^{2k+n-1}\,dr=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}^{2}I_{k}.\end{split}

Now, let us notice that if
r\in[0,1]
we have that
r^{2(k+1)+n-1}\leq r^{2k+n-1}
which implies that
I_{k}
is non increasing and it holds

I_{k}\leq{\left\|j^{\prime\prime}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2k+n-1}\,dr=\frac{{\left\|j^{\prime\prime}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}}{2k+n}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{for }k\rightarrow\infty,

where the norm of
j^{\prime\prime}
doesn’t blow up due to the assumption.

Hence, (
25
) becomes

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}^{2}\left[\frac{2\gamma_{j}k}{n}+\frac{\gamma_{j}(n-2)-j^{\prime}(0)}{n}-\frac{I_{k}}{n^{2}}\right]=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}^{2}A_{k},

thus the proof concludes if we find a positive constant
C
such that
A_{k}\geq Ck
for every
k\geq 2
. Now, we have that

A_{k+1}-A_{k}=\frac{2\gamma_{j}}{n}-\frac{I_{k+1}-I_{k}}{n^{2}}>\frac{2\gamma_{j}}{n}>0

which means that
A_{k}
is increasing and
A_{k}>A_{2}
. Moreover,
A_{2}
can be rewritten using an integration by parts as

A_{2}=\frac{n+2}{n}\left[\gamma_{j}-\int_{0}^{1}j^{\prime}\left(\frac{1-r^{2}}{2n}\right)r^{n+1}\,dr\right].

Also, using coarea formula we have

\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime}(u_{0}){\left|x\right|}^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{0}^{1}j^{\prime}\left(\frac{1-r^{2}}{2n}\right)r^{2}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dr=\int_{0}^{1}j^{\prime}\left(\frac{1-r^{2}}{2n}\right)r^{n+1}\,dr,

hence, using definition of
\gamma_{j}
, the bracket in
A_{2}
is given by

\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime}(u_{0})-j^{\prime}(u_{0}){\left|x\right|}^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime}(u_{0})(1-{\left|x\right|}^{2})\,dx,

thus
A_{2}
is positive if
j^{\prime}(u_{0})
is positive.

Therefore, if we consider the sequence
\frac{A_{k}}{k}
, it is positive and converges to
\frac{2\gamma_{j}}{n}
, so it admits a positive minimum
\overline{C}(n,j)
. Thus, using the equivalence gives

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]=\sum_{k\geq 2}c_{k}^{2}A_{k}\geq\overline{C}\sum_{k\geq 2}kc_{k}^{2}\geq\frac{\overline{C}}{2}{\left\|\xi\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}.

For every
\xi\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}
let us consider its
L^{2}
projection on
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\perp}
, given by

\Pi(\xi)=a_{0}W_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{1,i}W_{1,i},

where

W_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{nw_{n}}}\qquad W_{1,i}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{w_{n}}}x_{i},\quad x\in\partial B_{1},\quad i=1,\dots,n

and

a_{0}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi W_{0}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\qquad a_{1,i}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi W_{1,i}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\quad i=1,\dots,n

then
\xi-\Pi(\xi)\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Moreover, by Green formula and definition of
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}

{\left\|\xi-\Pi(\xi)\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}={\left\|\xi\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}-{\left\|\Pi(\xi)\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}\geq{\left\|\xi\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}-C\delta^{2}{\left\|\xi\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}.

(26)

Hence using bilinearity, the identity (
26
), the previous computation in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and the continuity
4.2
gives

\begin{split}\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]&=\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi-\Pi(\xi),\xi-\Pi(\xi)]+2\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\Pi(\xi)]-\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\Pi(\xi),\Pi(\xi)]\geq\\
&\geq\frac{\overline{C}}{2}{\left\|\xi-\Pi(\xi)\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}-2C{\left\|\xi\right\|}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}{\left\|\Pi(\xi)\right\|}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}-C{\left\|\Pi(\xi)\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}\geq\\
&\geq\frac{\overline{C}}{2}{\left\|\xi\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}-C\delta{\left\|\xi\right\|}^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}\end{split}

which gives the thesis for
\delta
sufficiently small.
∎

We conclude by adding that, furthermore, in Appendix
B
we provide a set of different assumptions regarding
j
for which the result remains valid.

4.3
Proof of the stability for nearly spherical set

The last result that we need to conclude the local stability, is the following lemma due to Dambrine, see
[
13
, Theorem 1]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
0<\gamma\leq 1
, there exist a modulus of continuity
\omega
and a constant
\delta_{b}=\delta_{b}(n,\gamma)
, such that, for every
C^{2,\gamma}
nearly spherical set
\Omega
parametrized by
\varphi
with
\|\varphi\|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\leq\delta_{b}
and
{\left|\Omega\right|}={\left|B_{1}\right|}
, we have

\mathcal{J}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{J}(B_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\varphi,\varphi]+\omega(\|\varphi\|_{C^{2,\gamma}})\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}.

Proof.

By Taylor formula we have

\mathcal{J}(\Omega)=\mathcal{J}(B_{1})+f^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(f^{\prime\prime}(s)-f^{\prime\prime}(0))\,ds.

Then from Talenti we know that
f^{\prime}(0)=0
and from Remark
4.1
we have
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=-\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[V\cdot\nu_{0},V\cdot\nu_{0}]
. Then the previous identity becomes

\mathcal{J}(\Omega)=\mathcal{J}(B_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[V\cdot\nu_{0},V\cdot\nu_{0}]+\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(f^{\prime\prime}(s)-f^{\prime\prime}(0))\,ds.

(27)

Since
\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\cdot,\cdot]
is symmetric and continuous, from Lemma
1.1
, we find

{\left|\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[V\cdot\nu_{0},V\cdot\nu_{0}]-\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\varphi,\varphi]\right|}\leq\omega(\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})})\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}.

(28)

Then, combining (
27
)-(
28
), we get the thesis.
∎

We now have all the ingredients to prove stability for nearly spherical sets.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By assumption on
\varphi
we get

{\left|\Omega\right|}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{(1+\varphi)^{n}}{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\quad x_{\Omega}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}y\frac{(1+\varphi)^{n+1}}{n+1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0.

Thanks to the smallness assumption on
\varphi
we have

{\left|\int_{\partial B_{1}}\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right|}={\left|\sum_{k=2}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\varphi^{k}}{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right|}\leq c\int_{\partial B_{1}}\varphi^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq c\delta_{c}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})},

and

{\left|\int_{\partial B_{1}}y_{i}\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right|}\leq\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{k}\int_{\partial B_{1}}{\left|\frac{\varphi^{k}}{n+1}\right|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq c\delta_{c}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})},

where
c=c(n)
is a dimensional constant. In the same notation of Lemma
4.3
, we have that
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{c\delta_{c}}
and, by Lemmas
4.3
-
4.4
, if
\delta_{c}<<\min\{\delta_{b},\delta_{d}\}
, we can infer:

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})-\mathcal{J}(\Omega)&\geq\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\varphi,\varphi]-\omega(\|\varphi\|_{C^{2,\gamma}})\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}\\
&\geq C(n,j)\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}.\end{split}

∎

Appendix A
Some functionals that satisfy the assumptions

In the following with
u_{\Omega}
we will denote the solution to (
1
) in
\Omega
, while with

u_{B_{1}}(x)=\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{2n},

the solution to (
1
) in
B_{1}
.

•

The
Moser-Trudinger functional
in dimension
n=2
:

j(s)=e^{s^{2}}-1.

For this function, the stability result reads as follow

\int_{B_{1}}e^{u_{B_{1}}(x)^{2}}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}e^{u_{\Omega}(x)^{2}}\,dx\geq c(n)\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}.

We notice that for
n\geq 3
, the function
j(s)=e^{s^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}
no longer has a bounded second derivative.

•

The
p
-th power of the Lebesgue norm
for
2\leq p<\infty
:

j(s)=s^{p}.

For this function, the stability result reads as follow

\|u_{B_{1}}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}^{p}-\|u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\geq c(n)\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}

We notice that for
1<p<2
, the function
j(s)=s^{p}
no longer has a bounded second derivative.

•

The
Torsional rigidity
(or
L^{1}
-norm):

j(s)=s.

In this case our result recovers, in the class of nearly spherical sets, the result proved in
[
9
]
, and it reads as follows

T(B_{1})-T(\Omega)\geq c(n)\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}.

Appendix B
Alternative proof

We can also establish the coercivity of the functional
\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}
under a structural assumption on
j
, different from the regularity assumption stated in the introduction. First, we provide a different proof of continuity using a weaker summability assumption

j^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\frac{n}{2}}\!\left(0,\frac{1}{2n}\right),

(29)

Lemma B.1

(Continuity of
\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}
)
.

There exists a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(n,j)>0
such that

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]\leq c_{1}(n,j)\|\xi_{1}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{8}1)}\|\xi_{2}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}

for every
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})
.

Proof.

The proof is unchanged, except for the estimate of the last term arising in the triangle inequality, where we exploit the integrability assumption (
29
). Set

q=\frac{n}{2},\qquad r=\frac{n}{n-2}.

Then
1/q+1/r=1
and
2r=2^{\ast}
. By Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})\,H(\xi_{1})\,H(\xi_{2})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|j^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}\|H(\xi_{1})H(\xi_{2})\|_{L^{r}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|j^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}\|H(\xi_{1})\|_{L^{2^{*}}(B_{1})}\|H(\xi_{2})\|_{L^{2^{*}}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq c(n)\|j^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}\|H(\xi_{1})\|_{H^{1}(B_{1})}\|H(\xi_{2})\|_{H^{1}(B_{1})},

where in the last step we used the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(B_{1})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(B_{1})
.
∎

While, the proof of coercivity only differs in the treatment of the class
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, and yields an explicit coercivity constant assuming that
j^{\prime\prime}
is bounded (at least in the interval
\left(0,\frac{1}{2n}\right)
) and satisfies:

\dfrac{j^{\prime}(0)}{\int_{0}^{1}j^{\prime}(s)(1-2ns)^{(n-2)/2}\,ds}\geq n(n-2),

(30)

\frac{j^{\prime}(0)\beta_{n/2,1}^{4}+4\|j^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1/2n)}}{\beta_{n/2,1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}j^{\prime}(s)(1-2ns)^{(n-2)/2}\,ds}\leq n^{2}(n+2),

(31)

here
\beta_{n/2,1}
denotes the first positive zero of a Bessel function of the first kind.

Lemma B.2

(Coercivity on
\mathcal{M}_{0}
)
.

Let
\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}
be the symmetric continuous bilinear form on
H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})
defined in (
25
). Then
\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})
is coercive on
\mathcal{M}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
, that is, there exists a constant
c_{2}=c_{2}(n,j)>0
such that

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]\geq c_{2}(n,j)\|\xi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}\qquad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathcal{M}_{0}.

More precisely,

c_{2}(n,j)=\frac{|\nabla p_{0}|}{n}\left[\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j^{\prime}(0)}{4|\nabla p_{0}|}-\frac{\|j^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{n|\nabla p_{0}|\mu_{1}(B_{1})^{2}}\right],

and
c_{2}(n,j)>0
in view of (
31
).

Proof.

We first recall that, by Green’s formula,

|\nabla p_{0}|=\frac{\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime}(u_{0})\,dx}{n\omega_{n}}.

Factoring out the common term, the bilinear form can be written as

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n}|\nabla p_{0}|\,\|\nabla H(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\left[1+\left(\frac{n}{2}-1-\frac{j^{\prime}(0)}{2|\nabla p_{0}|}\right)\frac{\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla H(\xi)|^{2}\,dx}\right]

\displaystyle-|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})\,H(\xi)^{2}\,dx.

By (
30
), the coefficient of the Rayleigh quotient in brackets is non-positive. Therefore, using

\min\left\{\frac{\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla H(\xi)|^{2}\,dx}{\int_{\partial B_{1}}\xi^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}:\;\xi\in\mathcal{M}_{0}\setminus\{0\}\right\}=2,

we infer that

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]\geq\frac{2}{n}|\nabla p_{0}|\,\|\nabla H(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\left[\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j^{\prime}(0)}{4|\nabla p_{0}|}\right]-|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})\,H(\xi)^{2}\,dx.

We now estimate the last term by Poincaré’s inequality:

-|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\int_{B_{1}}j^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})\,H(\xi)^{2}\,dx\geq-\frac{\|j^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{n^{2}\mu_{1}(B_{1})^{2}}\|\nabla H(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}.

Combining the previous inequalities, we obtain

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]\geq\frac{2}{n}|\nabla p_{0}|\left[\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j^{\prime}(0)}{4|\nabla p_{0}|}-\frac{\|j^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{2n|\nabla p_{0}|\mu_{1}(B_{1})^{2}}\right]\|\nabla H(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}.

Finally, since

\|\nabla H(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}\leq\|\xi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}\leq 2\|\nabla H(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2},

it follows that

\partial^{2}\mathcal{J}(B_{1})[\xi,\xi]\geq\frac{|\nabla p_{0}|}{n}\left[\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j^{\prime}(0)}{4|\nabla p_{0}|}-\frac{\|j^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{n|\nabla p_{0}|\mu_{1}(B_{1})^{2}}\right]\|\xi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎
