 PROJECT SCOPE AND OBJECTIVE
The aim of this project was to commercialize an instrument that will accurately and precisely
measure carbon in soil, providing a breakthrough in scalable soil core analysis and enabling
a robust measurement of the total amount of carbon per acre. The instrument will measure
the whole soil core, rather than just a few sub-samples, and provide data for carbon depth
profiling, as well as other key soil elements such as Al, Si, O, Fe. This instrument will
provide faster turnaround times and more precise results than current methods (such as loss
on ignition), facilitating the analysis of many soil cores per acre. Ultimately, this will enable
farmers to improve their carbon-in-soil content, thus contributing to an improved soil health
and product yield. To achieve this goal, we divided the project into three main tasks: 1)
commercialization research, 2) technical development, and 3) instrument development,
demonstration, and benchmarking.

 BENEFIT TO DOE MISSION
The development of the real-time soil core scanner offers significant benefits to the
Department of Energy by advancing measurement technologies that enhance agricultural
productivity and resource management. This innovative instrument provides rapid, accurate
assessments of soil composition, crucial for optimizing land use and improving crop yields.
By enabling precise measurements of essential elements in soil, the technology supports
informed decision-making for farmers, thereby promoting efficient nutrient management and
reducing resource waste. Additionally, the project fosters collaboration between national
laboratories and private sector partners, stimulating job creation and technological growth
within the domestic economy. Through this initiative, the DOE strengthened its role in
promoting cutting-edge scientific advancements and supported the development of practical
solutions that enhance agricultural cost efficiency and productivity.
Furthermore, the technology can be used for other research sectors and is now, for
example, helping with nuclear physics measurements and planetary research through a new
NASA grant.

 COMMERCIALIZATION ADVANCEMENTS

 MARKET ADVANCEMENTS
From the beginning of the project, we always received very positive feedback on our
technology. The main issue was that our technology is complex and not ready for field tests,
which many potential customers invited us for. The main advancements we made were the
completion of the I-Corps program and the completion of a techno-economical analysis,
which were both well received when talking to potential customers.
Furthermore, the funding allowed us to de-risk several of the main technical risks. Especially
in respect to sensitivity and to the measurement time which were both main focus areas for
this project and where we made great progress.

CONCLUSION

 PATH FORWARD
  We recently pivoted to be able to attract new funding. We identified two areas of
  potential growth: a) The search for rare-earth-elements (REE) where neutron
  scattering techniques can help identifying REE. We currently have submitted one
  proposal to ARPA-E on this topic and are writing a second one for FE. For both we
  were able to attract multi-billion dollar companies as potential industrial partners. b)
  Planetary science is another application space where elemental identification is
  very beneficial. We already attracted follow-up funding from NASA on this topic and
  see a potential growth area for this instrument with space mission relevance. These
  would not have been possible without this Technology Commercialization Fund
  Project, supported by OTC and funded by FE.

  Furthermore, we will continue to look for funding opportunities in the carbon
  measurement regime and soil health sector where we continue to see a great
  potential for this technology. We are in the process of reaching out to several
  non-profit organizations and companies that are active in this sector and hope to
  attract follow up funding from the private sector.

 LESSONS LEARNED
  Going through the full techno-economical analysis was a very helpful step to really
  see where the costs for the technology come from. Also going through the full
  I-Corps program was a very useful exercise that helped us understand potential
  markets in more detail. Finally, being able to de-risk some major technical
  challenges was essential.
