Some Results about the Expressivity of Preference-Incomplete Structured Argumentation Frameworks
Antonio Yuste-Ginel

Abstract
This paper studies the expressive power of ASPIC + argumentation frameworks with uncertain preference profiles by comparing them with several abstract formalisms with uncertain defeats. Most of our results are negative (and some of them are theoretically unexpected). We also conjecture a positive, non-trivial threshold for the expressivity of uncertain preferences, and prove some essential preliminary steps toward the confirmation of this conjecture.

Introduction

Context

Arguing and uncertainty appear intertwined in many real contexts. On one hand, assessing conflicting arguments is a canonical way to deal with uncertain and incompatible sources of information. On the other hand, uncertain argumentative information (e.g, which rules or premises are available) is key to knowing which arguments are relevant and, eventually, which propositions shall get accepted. This second sense of influence is especially relevant in multi-agent scenarios (including adversarial ones), where the lack of information of one agent about another’s argumentative background determines which arguments are considered as potentially good moves in dialogue. Consequently, the formal argumentation community has devoted considerable effort to modelling uncertainty in abstract argumentation frameworks, both in a probabilistic fashion
[
10
]
and in a qualitative (i.e., non-numerical) one
[
11
]
. Regarding the latter, the standard abstract tool is Incomplete Argumentation Frameworks (IAFs), these are Dung’s frameworks
[
7
]
, where the sets of arguments and defeats are split into two disjoint components representing, respectively, certain and uncertain arguments and defeats. Reasoning about argument acceptability in IAFs is done via the notion of completion (hypothetical removals of uncertainty that can be seen as epistemically possible worlds).

Motivation and previous work

However, critical voices have emerged about the conceptual plausibility of remaining at an abstract formal level of argumentation, ignoring the internal structure of argument and the nature of conflict among them. In
[
15
]
, the authors show how some popular abstract formalisms make strong assumptions that are unjustifiable once the structure of arguments is taken into account. This motivated a new line of research on the viability of instantiating abstract formalisms within the standard tools (e.g., ASPIC
+

[
12
]
or ABA
[
4
]
, among others). Such a trend has also permeated the analysis of arguing with qualitative uncertainty. In this line, structured argumentation with uncertain rules
[
18
]
and uncertain premises
[
14
]
have been studied (and recently compared
[
16
]
). Furthermore, structured frameworks with uncertain preferences were first mentioned in
[
2
]
and later defined in
[
19
]
, but a more comprehensive analysis remains missing.

Focus and methodology

3.2
Comparing sets of completions

Recall our research question: What kind of defeat-uncertainty do we get from preference-uncertainty? To provide a clear, formal answer, we need a way to compare sets of completions. The following definition does so.

\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}_{1},....,\mathsf{D}_{n}\rangle

\langle\mathsf{A}^{\prime},\mathsf{D}^{\prime}_{1},....,\mathsf{D}^{\prime}_{n}\rangle

\mathsf{Y}\preccurlyeq\mathsf{X}
) iff for all
Y\in\mathsf{Y}
there is a
X\in\mathsf{X}
such that
\mathsf{com}(X)\approxeq\mathsf{com}(Y)
.

Fact 1
.

The relation
\preccurlyeq
is reflexive and transitive.

Remark 2
.

The previous notion of expressivity significantly simplifies the one used in
[
16
]
for comparing sets of completions. The reason for this simplification is that we focus here on defeat-uncertainty (vs. the argument-uncertainty of
[
16
]
), and hence we do not need to keep track of the identity of arguments across different completions to get an intuitive notion of equivalence.

The following proposition, proved by
[
8
, p. 304]
, shows that dep-IAFs provide an upper bound for expressing uncertainty about defeats. In other words, dep-IAF are maximally expressive wrt defeat incompleteness.

Proposition 2

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
T=\{\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}_{1}\rangle,...,\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}_{n}\rangle\}
be any finite set of AFs with the same domain (i.e., any finite set of defeat-completions). There is a dep-IAF
\mathsf{dIA}=\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}^{F},\mathsf{D}^{?},\Delta\rangle
such that
\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{dIA})=T
.

Hence, as a corollary, dep-IAFs are expressive enough to subsume pref-ISAFs.

Corollary 1
.

pref-ISAFs
\preccurlyeq
dep-IAFs.

3.3
Some negative results

The next natural question is if we can get rid of dependencies and simulate pref-ISAFs at the abstract level with simple def-IAFs (as originally suggested by
[
2
]
). The answer was shown to be negative by
[
19
, Proposition 2]
.

Proposition 3

(
[
19
]
)
.

\text{pref-ISAFs }\not\preccurlyeq\text{ def-IAFs.}

Proof.

As a witness, consider the pref-ISAF of Example
2
. Reasoning towards a contradiction, suppose that there is a def-IAF with an equivalent set of completions. Note that the defeats among
p\Rightarrow r
and
q\Rightarrow\lnot r
must be uncertain in the abstract set of completions (because they appear in some but not in some others of
\mathsf{IS}
), but then there must be an abstract completion containing no defeats, and therefore an equivalent one in the pref-ISAF, and this is not the case.
∎

It can be shown, however, that the pref-ISAF of Example
2
is equivalent to a disjunctive def-IAFs. So our next question is whether this can be generalised to all finite pref-ISAFs. The answer, again, is negative.

Proposition 4
.

\text{pref-ISAFs }\not\preccurlyeq\text{ dis-IAFs.}

Proof.

As a witness, consider the set of completions of the ISAF of Example
3
.
It is easy to show that these AFs do not correspond to the set of completions of any dis-IAF.

∎

Note that the pref-ISAF used in the previous proof encodes an implicative defeat dependency
\mathsf{IMPLY}(\{\langle\lnot p,p\rangle\},\{\langle\lnot p,p\Rightarrow q\rangle\})
. However, it also encodes a disjunctive dependency, namely
\mathsf{OR}(\{\langle p,\lnot p\rangle,\langle\lnot p,p\rangle\})
. Hence, we can show that implicative dependencies alone are not enough to capture pref-ISAFs. This contrasts with recent work on the expressivity of uncertain rules and premises, where they are shown to be captured by implicative argument-incomplete AFs
[
16
]
.

Proposition 5
.

\text{pref-ISAFs }\not\preccurlyeq\text{ imp-IAFs}

Proof.

The pref-ISAF of Example
2
serves again as a witness. Details are omitted for brevity.
∎

We can ask the reverse question: Are pref-ISAFs expressive enough to simulate the kind of defeat-uncertainty encoded in def-IAFs? A negative answer is provided in the next proposition. Again, this can be compared with recent work on the expressivity of uncertain rules and premises, where they are shown to be more expressive than abstract argument-incomplete AFs
[
16
]
.

Proposition 6
.

\text{def-IAFs }\not\preccurlyeq\text{ pref-ISAFs}
.

Proof.

Take
\mathsf{IA}=\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}^{F},\mathsf{D}^{?}\rangle
where
\mathsf{A}=\{a\}
,
\mathsf{D}^{F}=\varnothing
and
\mathsf{D}^{?}=\{\langle a,a\rangle\}
. We have two completions, namely,
\mathsf{AF}_{1}=\langle\{a\},\varnothing\rangle
and
\mathsf{AF}_{2}=\langle\{a\},\{\langle a,a\rangle\}\rangle
. Reasoning toward contradiction, suppose there is a pref-ISAF
\mathsf{IS}=\langle\mathsf{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathsf{K},\leq\rangle
with an equivalent set of completions. Let
i:\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{A}(\langle\mathsf{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathsf{K},\leq\rangle)
be an isomorphism that witnesses this equivalence. Since we only have one argument, then either
i(a)
is the only element of
\mathsf{K}_{p}
, call it
\varphi
, or
i(a)
is of the form
\Rightarrow\varphi
(a premise-less argument with a defeasible rule). Otherwise, we would have more than one argument or an undefeatable one, and
\mathsf{AF}_{1}
could not be generated (the reader is invited to check these details). We can also deduce that
\varphi=-\varphi
(
\varphi
is a contradictory formula). But then, the value of
\leq
does not matter, since in both cases we have that
i(a)
defeats
i(a)
; and hence no structured completion is equivalent to
\langle\{a\},\varnothing\rangle
.

∎

The following corollaries follow from the previous proposition, the definition of
\preccurlyeq
and the fact that every def-IAF can be seen as a (subtype of) dep-IAFs with an empty set of dependencies.

Corollary 2
.

\text{dis-IAFs }\not\preccurlyeq\text{ pref-ISAFs}
.

Corollary 3
.

\text{dis-imp-IAFs }\not\preccurlyeq\text{ pref-ISAFs}
.

Corollary 4
.

dep-IAFs
\not\preccurlyeq
pref-ISAFs.

3.4
Towards a non-trivial positive expressivity threshold

Finally, we take the first step towards providing a non-trivial, upper threshold for the expressivity of pref-ISAFs. Let us provide some definitions.

Definition 13

(Candidate relations and dependencies)
.

Let
\mathsf{IS}=\langle\mathsf{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathsf{K},\leq\rangle
be a pref-ISAF with
\leq=\leq^{F}\cup\leq^{?}
. Define the following relations and sets of dependencies over
\mathsf{A}(\mathsf{IS})
:

•

\leq^{N}=(\mathsf{A}(\mathsf{IS})\times\mathsf{A}(\mathsf{IS}))\setminus\leq
.

•

\mathsf{D}^{F}_{\mathsf{IS}}=\{\langle X,Y\rangle\mid\exists Y^{\prime}\mathsf{PIA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime})\lor\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,X)\lor

\qquad\qquad\qquad\exists Y^{\prime}(\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime})\land(X\leq^{N}Y^{\prime}\lor Y^{\prime}\leq^{F}X))\}
.

•

\mathsf{D}^{?}_{\mathsf{IS}}=\{\langle X,Y\rangle\mid\langle X,Y\rangle\notin\mathsf{D}^{F}_{\mathsf{IS}}\land\exists Y^{\prime}\big(\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime})\land(X\leq^{?}Y^{\prime}\lor Y^{\prime}\leq^{?}X)\big)\}
.

•

\Delta^{\mathsf{OR}}_{\mathsf{IS}}=\{\mathsf{OR}(\{\langle X,Y\rangle,\langle Y,X\rangle\})\mid\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y)\land\mathsf{PDA}(Y,X,X)\land X\leq^{?}Y\land Y\leq^{?}X\}
.

•

\Delta^{\mathsf{IMPLY}}_{\mathsf{IS}}=\{\mathsf{IMPLY}(\{\langle X,Y\rangle\},\{\langle X,Y^{\prime}\rangle\})\mid Y\in\mathsf{Sub}(Y^{\prime})\}
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\mathsf{IS}=\langle\mathsf{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathsf{K},\leq\rangle
be a pref-ISAF. Then for all
X,Y,W\in\mathsf{A}(\mathsf{IS})
, we have that:

1.

\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{F}
iff
\forall\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}^{\ast}\rangle\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{IS})
,
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{\ast}
.

2.

(\exists\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}^{\ast}\rangle\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{IS})
such that
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{\ast}
) iff
\big(\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{F}
OR
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{?}_{\mathsf{IS}}\big)
.

3.

\mathsf{OR}({\langle X,Y\rangle,\langle Y,X\rangle})\in\Delta^{\mathsf{OR}}_{\mathsf{IS}}
implies
\forall\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}^{\ast}\rangle\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{IS})
either
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{\ast}
or
\langle Y,X\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{\ast}
.

4.

\mathsf{IMPLY}(\langle X,Y\rangle,\langle X,W\rangle)\in\Delta^{\mathsf{IMPLY}}_{\mathsf{IS}}
implies that
\forall\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}^{\ast}\rangle\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{IS})
, if
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{\ast}
, then
\langle X,W\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

(
1.
) (
\Longrightarrow
) Suppose
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{IS}}^{F}
. Let
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{\ast}
and
\mathsf{D}^{\ast}=\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
(
\leq^{\ast}
is the preference relation of the preference-completion that generates
\mathsf{D}^{*})
. We have three cases (on the membership to
\mathsf{D}^{F}_{\mathsf{IS}}
).

(
Case
:
\exists Y^{\prime}(\mathsf{PIA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime}))
) Then it does not matter the preference relation, we will have
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
.

(
Case
:
\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,X)
) Suppose, reasoning towards contradiction, that
\langle X,Y\rangle\notin\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
, but then by the case hypothesis and the definition of defeat, we have
X\leq^{\ast}X
and
X\nleq^{\ast}X
, which is absurd.

(
Case
:
\exists Y^{\prime}(\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime})\land(X\leq^{N}Y^{\prime}\lor Y^{\prime}\leq^{F}X))
) Let
Y_{1}
be such an
Y^{\prime}
. We have two cases, if
X\leq^{N}Y_{1}
, then
X\nleq^{\ast}Y
(and hence
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
, because
\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y_{1}))
. If, on the other hand,
Y_{1}\leq^{F}X
, then
Y_{1}\leq^{\ast}X
and we arrive to
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
again.

(
1.
) (
\Longleftarrow
)
Suppose that:

(1)
\forall\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}^{\ast}\rangle\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{IS})
,
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{\ast}
,

(2)
\forall Y^{\prime}(\lnot\mathsf{PIA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime}))
,

(3)
\lnot\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,X)
,

and, reasoning towards contradiction, suppose that:

(4)
\forall Y^{\prime}(\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime})\to(X\nleq^{N}Y^{\prime}\land Y^{\prime}\nleq^{F}X)
.

Moreover, define
\leq_{1}=\leq^{F}\cup\{\langle X,Y^{\prime}\rangle\mid\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime})\}
. We will show that
\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}(\leq_{1})\rangle\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{IS})
and
\langle X,Y\rangle\notin\mathsf{D}(\leq_{1})
(which contradicts (1)). For the former claim, note that
\leq^{F}\subseteq\leq_{1}\subseteq\leq^{F}\cup\leq^{?}
follows from the definition of
\leq_{1}
((4) is needed to deduce this, details are omitted for brevity). For the latter claim, by (2), we know that if
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
, then there must be an argument
Z
such that
\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Z)
and
X\not<_{1}Z
. But this can be shown to be absurd. For suppose there is such a
Z
, then it follows from the definition of
\leq_{1}
that
X\leq_{1}Z
. Then it must be the case that
Z\leq_{1}X
and, from (4) and
\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Z)
, we can derive
Z\leq^{?}X
, which by definition of
\leq_{1}
leads to
\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,X)
, which contradicts (3).

(
2.
) (
\Longrightarrow
) Suppose
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{\ast}
for some
\mathsf{D}^{\ast}\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{IS})
. Let
\mathsf{D}^{\ast}=\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
. This implies by definition of defeat that
\exists Y^{\prime}(\mathsf{PIA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime}))\lor\exists Y^{\prime}(\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime})\land X\not<^{\ast}Y^{\prime})
. We continue by cases.

(
Case
:
\exists Y^{\prime}(\mathsf{PIA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime}))
) Then
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{F}_{\mathsf{IS}}
by definition and we are done.

(
Case
:
\exists Y^{\prime}\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime})\land X\not<^{\ast}Y^{\prime})
) Let
Z=Y^{\prime}
, we get
\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Z)
and (
X\not\leq^{\ast}Z
or
Z\leq^{\ast}X
). The second claim is equivalent to a four-element disjunction: either
X\leq^{N}Z
or
X\leq^{?}Z
or
Z\leq^{F}X
or
Z\leq^{?}X
(by definition of preference-completions). Now it can be shown, on the one hand, that
X\leq^{N}Z
or
X\leq^{F}Z
leads to
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{F}_{\mathsf{IS}}
. On the other hand,
X\leq^{?}Z
or
Z\leq^{?}X
leads to
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{F}_{\mathsf{IS}}
or
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{?}_{\mathsf{IS}}
and, retrieving the existential quantifier, we are done. Details are omitted for brevity.

(
2.
) (
\Longleftarrow
) Suppose that
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{F}_{\mathsf{IS}}
or
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{?}_{\mathsf{IS}}
.

(
Case
:
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{F}_{\mathsf{IS}}
) It follows from item 1 of this proposition.

(
Case
:
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}^{?}_{\mathsf{IS}}
). Then
\exists Y^{\prime}(\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y^{\prime})\land(Y^{\prime}\leq^{?}X\lor X\leq^{?}Y^{\prime}))
. Let
Y_{1}
be such an
Y^{\prime}
, then either
Y_{1}\leq^{?}X
or
X\leq^{?}Y_{1}
. For the first case (
Y_{1}\leq^{?}X
), define
\leq_{1}=\leq^{F}\cup\{(Y_{1},X)\}
. It is then easy to show that
\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}(\leq_{1})\rangle\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{IS})
, and that
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}(\leq_{1})
. For the second case (
X\leq^{?}Y_{1}
), we can arrive that
\langle X,Y\rangle\in\mathsf{D}(\leq^{F})
.

(
3.
) Let
\mathsf{OR}(\{\langle X,Y\rangle,\langle Y,X\rangle\})\in\Delta^{\mathsf{OR}}_{\mathsf{IS}}
, which implies
\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y)
,
\mathsf{PDA}(Y,X,X)
,
X\leq^{?}Y
and
Y\leq^{?}X
. Let
\langle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{D}^{\ast}\rangle\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{IS})
, with
\mathsf{D}^{\ast}=\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
. Suppose that
\langle X,Y\rangle\notin\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
. The latter implies together with
\mathsf{PDA}(X,Y,Y)
and the definition of defeat that
X<^{\ast}Y
, which together with
\mathsf{PDA}(Y,X,X)
implies
\langle Y,X\rangle\in\mathsf{D}(\leq^{\ast})
.

(
4.
) This is a well known fact of ASPIC
+
(it follows directly from the definition of defeat).
∎

Discussion

If we could strengthen points 3 and 4 of the previous proposition to show that any other possible dependency that
\mathsf{IS}
satisfies is already implied by
\Delta^{\mathsf{OR}}_{\mathsf{IS}}
and
\Delta^{\mathsf{IMPLY}}_{\mathsf{IS}}
, then a non-trivial abstract expressivity threshold for pref-ISAFs would have been reached. We have not arrived there yet. Nonetheless, after examining a substantial number of potential counterexamples without success, we formulate the following conjecture, which delineates our main venue for future work.

Conjecture 1
.

pref-ISAFs
\preccurlyeq
dis-imp-IAFs.

Future work.

We close this paper by outlining three clear open paths for future work. First, and foremost, we aim at proving Conjecture
1
. This, besides informing us with what kind of expressivity is needed for modelling uncertain preferences at the abstract level, would also give us some hints about computational complexity, since dis-imp-IAFs are a well-studied fragment of dep-IAFs (see
[
9
, Table 1]
). Second, as mentioned in the Background section, preferences among arguments in ASPIC
+
are sometimes rooted on more basic relations among defeasible rules and/or ordinary premises, and these are in turn assumed to have some properties. Hence, a natural way to continue our work is to study uncertainty at this more concrete level, and to compare it with the current results on uncertain preferences at the level of arguments. Finally, we could compare the expressivity of pre-ISAFs with other forms of uncertainty that generate uncertain defeats (namely, uncertain contrary functions and uncertain naming conventions).
