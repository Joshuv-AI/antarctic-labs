DATA SERVICES

ARM provides tools and interactive computing environments to
help users work with its data. One of those environments is the
ARM Data Workbench, which gives users the tools they need to
access ARM data and perform tasks such as data visualization
and analysis.

In response to user feedback, the ARM Data Center rolled out
the next phase of the data workbench in FY2025. The latest phase
includes a new application for visualizing data, a dashboard that
gathers workbench-related tools in one location, and a smaller
“widget” version of the dashboard that is available in certain
applications.

ARM also released a new browser to make it easier to navigate
data from intensive operational periods. Such data can vary The new ARM Data Workbench dashboard provides links
 to different ARM tools that users can access for specific

Behind the scenes, ARM’s data services team worked to
modernize the user facility’s cyberinfrastructure to ensure smooth
operations, strengthen network resiliency, and provide capabilities
to support artificial intelligence use cases.

INSTRUMENTS

In FY2025, ARM focused on advancing instrument capabilities
through radar and aerosol data and engineering activities,
instrument upgrades to ensure consistent, high-quality data
sets, and laboratory experiments to stay at the cutting edge of
measurements.

Following ARM’s FY2025 radar plan, staff finished installing a
C-band scanning precipitation radar and a Ka/X-band scanning
cloud radar in Alabama while maintaining radars at other sites.
Also, ARM released calibrated and quality-controlled radar data
from past mobile deployments in California and Colorado.

Meanwhile, ARM continued to replace aging instruments, such
as broadband radiometers and surface energy balance systems,
with newer technology.

SITES AND FACILITIES

In addition to supporting major ARM Mobile Facility deployments in
Alabama, Maryland, and Tasmania in FY2025, ARM conducted 23
small campaigns (costing ARM less than $300,000 apiece) at its fixed
observatories in Alaska, Oklahoma, and the eastern Atlantic Ocean.

FY2025 marked ARM’s first proposal call for ArcticShark uncrewed
aerial system flights in Alabama. Flights for the selected campaign
happened in June.

ARM’s tethered balloon system (TBS) team conducted FY2025
flights in Alabama, Maryland, and Oklahoma. During a July mission
in Maryland, the TBS team flew two balloons simultaneously to get a
broader set of measurements.

With the ongoing nationwide helium shortage, the TBS team
prepared to use a new helium reclamation system to extend the life of
this finite resource and store the gas for additional flights. This will
also help reduce deployment costs.

Maintenance activities at ARM sites included shelter roof repairs at
the North Slope of Alaska atmospheric observatory to protect ARM
instruments.

 ARM tethered balloon system flights include lighting
 at night to maintain visibility.

TECHNICAL COORDINATION

ARM launched its refreshed external news and information website,
ARM.gov, in February 2025 after more than eight years with the
previous look.

The aim of the refreshed ARM.gov is to meet user needs with an
audience-focused design instead of the previous news-focused design.
The new design incorporates feedback from an FY2020 usability
study and FY2023 website satisfaction survey.

Website redesign goals included implementing the latest best user
experience practices and web standards, aligning with U.S. web design
standards, creating straightforward navigation for new and potential
ARM users, and integrating consistent searching capabilities.
