Abstract
This project will evaluate the benefits of greater coordination between Middle Columbia (Mid-C)
hydropower (hydro) facility owners and operators, with and without the proposed Banks Lake
Pump-Hydro (BLPH) Project. 1 The project objectives will be achieved through three distinct
areas of focus: data collection under three (3) “hydro year” (October through September)
conditions, modeling and analysis of operations in the identified hydro years, and modeling and
analysis of operations during simulated extreme weather events.

The anticipated outcomes of this project are a quantification of benefits possible from returning
Mid-C facilities to coordinated operations; the identification of possible methods for allocating
the benefits of coordination to the Mid-C facilities, including using software solutions like Avista
Decision Support System (ADSS); and an assessment of how similar outcomes might be
possible when coordination is applied more broadly to the Pacific Northwest and other regions
of the country.

1 The BLPH Project is proposed by the Columbia Basin Hydro Project to generate as much 1,000 MW of capacity

from pumping water between Lake Roosevelt and Banks Lake. Besides studying its potential for increasing the value
of the Mid-C projects under coordination, the BLPH Project is not connected to this project.
