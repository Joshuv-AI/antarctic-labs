Invariant measures of the stochastic theta method for stochastic differential equations with super-linearly growing coefficients
Xiaotong Li, Wei Liu, Wenjie Xiao

Abstract
The stochastic theta method is proposed to approximate invariant measures of stochastic differential equations (SDEs), both of whose drift and diffusion coefficients may grow super-linearly. For the numerical solution generated by the stochastic theta method, we show the existence and uniqueness of the numerical invariant measure first. Then, we prove that the numerical invariant measure is convergent to the exact invariant measure of the underlying SDE. We also provide some numerical simulations to illustrate our theoretical results. This work could be regarded as an extension of the results in [Y. Jiang et al, Numer. Algorithms 83(4)(2020), pp. 1531–1553] to the case of super-linearly growing diffusion coefficient. As the backward Euler-Maruyama (EM) method is a special case of the stochastic theta method, the results derived in this work could also be regarded as a generalization of the results for the backward EM method in [W. Liu et al. Appl. Numer. Math. 184(2023), pp. 137–150] to the stochastic theta method.

1
Introduction

Invariant measures of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) have many applications. For classical population models, invariant measures is related the persistency of species
1
;
2
. For generative diffusion model, the invariant measure serves as the stable terminal distribution for the forward noising process and provides a well
-
defined target distribution for the reverse generative process, ensuring theoretical stability and sampling consistency
4
;
21
. Although invariant measures are of importance in many areas, their explicit forms are hardly obtained. Therefore, it is essential to select appropriate numerical methods to approximate the invariant measure and conduct rigorous numerical analysis. In this paper, we employ the stochastic theta method and explore its capability to approximate the invariant measure of SDEs with super-linearly growing coefficients.

The stochastic theta method is a set of numerical methods for SDEs, since the classical Euler-Maruyama (EM) method can be derived by setting the theta to be zero and the backward EM method can be obtained by fixing the theta to be one
8
.

As one of the most popular numerical methods, the stochastic theta method has been broadly applied and studied. In the following, we first try to review existing literature about the stochastic theta method from different aspects. Then, the purpose of this paper and our main contributions are introduced.

In the past several decades, the stochastic theta method has been applied to different types of SDEs. Higham et al. explored it for SDEs with Markovian switching
7
. Huang applied it to stochastic delay differential equations
9
. Li and Gan employed it for stochastic delay integro-differential equations
12
. Liu et al. studied it for delayed stochastic Hopfield neural networks
15
. Chen et al. investigated it for a class of SDEs with random periodic solutions
5
. Niu et al. discussed it for free stochastic differential equations
19
. Yuan and Zhu used it for stochastic McKean-Vlasov equations
25
.

When noises other than the standard Brownian motion are used to drive SDEs, the stochastic theta method still has its place in numerical approximations. Li et al. applied it to a class of SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion
11
. Wang et al. employed it for some SDEs driven by Poisson jump process
24
. Chen et al. studied it for SDEs driven by time-changed Lévy noise
6
.

The stochastic theta method is an implicit method, if the theta is not taken to be zero. One of the advantages that implicit methods naturally possess is that they are capable to handle SDEs with super-linear coefficients. For the topic of the finite time strong convergence, Wang et al. proved the mean-square convergence rate of the stochastic theta method for SDEs under a coupled monotonicity condition
23
. For the topic of the stability of the trivial solution, Zong and Wu studied the mean-square exponential stability of the stochastic theta method and revealed effects of different values of the theta on handling the super-linear coefficients. For the topic of the approximations to the exact invariant measures of SDEs, Jiang et al. used the stochastic theta method to numerically approximate the invariant measure of some SDE whose drift coefficient grows super-linearly but diffusion coefficient still satisfies the linear growth condition
10
.

In this paper, we work a bit harder and manage to show that the numerical invariant measure generated by the stochastic theta method is still convergent to the exact one even when the diffusion coefficient grows super-linearly. So, the results obtained in this work could be regarded as non-trivial extension of those results in
10
to SDEs with both the drift and diffusion growing super-linearly. As mentioned above that the backward EM method is a special case of the stochastic theta method with the theta being one, the results obtained in this work could also be regarded as a generalization of the results of the backward EM method in
16
. It should be mentioned that techniques neither in
10
or
16
could be straightly adapted in this work to obtain our results. We need to carefully deal with the remained explicit part in the stochastic theta method when the theta taking values less than one.

Before we close Section
1
, it should be noted that various other numerical methods, apart from the stochastic theta method, were also employed to approximate the exact invariant measures of SDEs. We just mention some of them here
3
;
14
;
17
;
20
and refer the readers to the references therein.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, the mathematical preparations are presented and the stochastic theta method is revisited. The main results are stated and proved in Section
3
. The numerical examples are provided in Section
4
. Conclusions, discussions and future research are put in Section
5
.

2
Mathematical preliminary

In this section, we give some basic settings, notations and useful lemma for the rest of the paper.

Throughout this paper, we work with the complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Let
|a|
denote the Euclidean norm if
a
is a vector and the Hilbert-Schmidt norm if
a
is a matrix. The inner product of vectors
a
and
b
in the Euclidean space is denoted by
\langle a,b\rangle
. The lager one between
a
and
b
is denoted by
a\vee b
, and the smaller one is denoted by
a\wedge b
. For a vector or matrix
a
,
a^{\mathrm{T}}
denotes its transpose. Let
\mathbb{I}_{A}
be the indicator function for set
A
. The family of all probability measures on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the family of all Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
p
-Wasserstein distance between
u,v\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
p\in(0,1)
is defined by

{W}_{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|X-Y|^{p}\nu(dX,dY),

where
\mathcal{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
denotes the set of all couplings of
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.

Given a stochastic process
X(t)
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, denote the transition probability kernel of
X(t)
by
\mathbb{P}_{t}(x,B)
for any
t>0
and any
B\in\mathcal{B}
. We use
\delta_{x}
to emphasise the initial value
x
and denote
\mathbb{P}_{t}(x,\cdot)
by
\delta_{x}\mathbb{P}_{t}
. A probability measure
\pi(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called an invariant measure of
X(t)
, if

\pi(B)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbb{P}_{t}(x,B)\pi(dx)

holds for any
t>0
and any
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
W(t)
be a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. We consider the
n
-dimensional SDE of the Itô type

dX(t)=b(X(t))dt+g(X(t))dW(t)

(1)

with initial value
X(0)=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. We impose several assumptions on
b
and
g
as follows.

Assumption 2.1
.

There exists a pair of constant
L>0
and
q>1
such that

|b(x_{1})-b(x_{2})|\vee|g(x_{1})-g(x_{2})|\leq L(1+|x_{1}|^{q-1}+|x_{2}|^{q-1})|x_{1}-x_{2}|

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Assumption 2.2
.

There exist constants
l_{1}\geq 1
and
\beta>0
such that

2\langle x_{1}-x_{2},b(x_{1})-b(x_{2})\rangle+l_{1}|g(x_{1})-g(x_{2})|^{2}\leq-\beta|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Assumption 2.3
.

There exist constants
l_{2}\geq 1
,
c_{1}>0
and
\alpha>0
such that

2\langle x,b(x)\rangle+l_{2}|g(x)|^{2}\leq c_{1}-\alpha|x|^{2}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Assumption
2.1
together with Assumption
2.2
guarantees the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
1
), see, e.g.,
(
18
, Theorem 3.4)
.

For a given time step
h\in(0,1)
, the stochastic theta method to SDE (
1
) is defined by

X_{k+1}=X_{k}+\theta b(X_{k+1})h+(1-\theta)b(X_{k})h+g(X_{k})\Delta W_{k},\quad X_{0}=X(0)=x_{0},

(2)

where
\theta\in(1/2,1]
and
\Delta W_{k}=W(t_{k+1})-W(t_{k})
is the Brownian motion increment with
t_{k}=kh
, for
k=1,2,\cdots
.

To investigate the invariant measure of the numerical solution, we introduce the following notation. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, let
\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h}(x,B)
be the transition probability kernel of
X_{k}
. A probability measure
\hat{\pi}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called an invariant measure of
X_{k}
, if

\hat{\pi}(B)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h}(x,B)\hat{\pi}(dx)

holds for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The implementation of (
2
) requires solving a nonlinear equation at each iteration. The well-posedness of the difference equation (
2
) is proved in the next lemma. The proof is analogous to that of
10
and is therefore omitted.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let Assumption
2.2
holds. For any
h\in(0,1)
and
\theta\in(1/2,1]
, then the stochastic theta method (
2
) is well defined.

3
Main results

In this section, the existence and uniqueness of the invariant measure of the numerical solution generated by the stochastic theta method is proved. Furthermore, the convergence of the numerical invariant measure to the underlying counterpart is discussed.

To obtain the existence and uniqueness of the invariant measure, we need the following two auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let Assumption
2.3
holds. Define

\rho_{0}=\left\{\dfrac{\alpha}{2}\wedge\dfrac{2\theta-1}{2\theta^{2}}\right\}.

For any
h\in(0,1)
satisfying
0<h<1/\rho_{0}
, the numerical solution (
2
) obeys

\mathbb{E}|X_{k}|^{2}\leq C_{1},

where
C_{1}>0
is a constant.

Proof.

Define
M_{k}=X_{k}-\theta hb(X_{k})
,

\displaystyle M_{k+1}

\displaystyle=X_{k+1}-\theta hb(X_{k+1})

\displaystyle=X_{k}+(1+\theta)hb(X_{k})+g(X_{k})\Delta W_{k}-\theta hb(X_{k+1})

\displaystyle=X_{k}+hb(X_{k})-\theta hb(X_{k})+\theta hb(X_{k})+g(X_{k})\Delta W_{k}-\theta hb(X_{k+1})

\displaystyle=M_{k}+hb(X_{k})+g(X_{k})\Delta W_{k}.

Squaring both sides yields

\displaystyle|M_{k+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|M_{k}+hb(X_{k})+g(X_{k})\Delta W_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|M_{k}|^{2}+h^{2}|b(X_{k})|^{2}+|g(X_{k})\Delta W_{k}|^{2}+2\langle M_{k},hb(X_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle+2\langle M_{k},g(X_{k})\Delta W_{k}\rangle+2\langle hb(X_{k}),g(X_{k})\Delta W_{k}\rangle.

Taking expectation,

\mathbb{E}|M_{k+1}|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})|^{2}+h\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})|^{2}+2h\mathbb{E}\langle M_{k},b(X_{k})\rangle.

Using
M_{k}=X_{k}-\theta hb(X_{k})
, we have

\displaystyle 2h\mathbb{E}\langle M_{k},b(X_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle=2h\mathbb{E}\langle X_{k}-\theta hb(X_{k}),b(X_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle=2h\mathbb{E}\langle X_{k},b(X_{k})\rangle-2\theta h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})|^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|M_{k+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})|^{2}+h\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})|^{2}+2h\mathbb{E}\langle X_{k},b(X_{k})\rangle-2\theta h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+2h\mathbb{E}\langle X_{k},b(X_{k})\rangle+h\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})|^{2}-(2\theta-1)h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})|^{2}.

By Assumption
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle 2\langle X_{k},b(X_{k})\rangle+|g(X_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\langle X_{k},b(X_{k})\rangle+l_{2}|g(X_{k})|^{2}-(l_{2}-1)|g(X_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}-\alpha|X_{k}|^{2}-(l_{2}-1)|g(X_{k})|^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|M_{k+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+h\left(c_{1}-\alpha|X_{k}|^{2}-(l_{2}-1)|g(X_{k})|^{2}\right)-(2\theta-1)h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+c_{1}h-\alpha h\mathbb{E}|X_{k}|^{2}-h(l_{2}-1)\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})|^{2}-(2\theta-1)h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})|^{2}.

Since
l_{2}\geq 1
, we have
-h(l_{2}-1)\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})|^{2}\leq 0
, so

\mathbb{E}|M_{k+1}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+c_{1}h-\alpha h\mathbb{E}|X_{k}|^{2}-(2\theta-1)h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})|^{2}.

Let
\theta>\frac{1}{2}
and define

\rho_{0}=\left\{\dfrac{\alpha}{2}\wedge\dfrac{2\theta-1}{2\theta^{2}}\right\}.

Since
\theta>\frac{1}{2}
, we have
\frac{2\theta-1}{2\theta^{2}}>0
, and
\alpha>0
by Assumption 2.2, so
\rho_{0}>0
. This choice of
\rho_{0}
ensures the inequalities
2\rho_{0}\leq\alpha
and
2\rho_{0}\theta^{2}\leq 2\theta-1
hold.

We assume the stepsize
h
satisfies

0<\rho_{0}h<1,

which is feasible because
\rho_{0}>0
and
h
can be chosen sufficiently small. This guarantees
0<1-\rho_{0}h<1
, a critical condition for convergence of the geometric series in the stability analysis.

Now, from
M_{k}=X_{k}-\theta hb(X_{k})
, we derive

|M_{k}|^{2}=|X_{k}-\theta hb(X_{k})|^{2}\leq 2|X_{k}|^{2}+2\theta^{2}h^{2}|b(X_{k})|^{2}

Multiplying both sides by
\rho_{0}h
, we get

\rho_{0}h|M_{k}|^{2}\leq 2\rho_{0}h|X_{k}|^{2}+2\rho_{0}\theta^{2}h^{3}|b(X_{k})|^{2}.

By the definition of
\rho_{0}
and
h\in(0,1)
:

•

2\rho_{0}\leq\alpha\implies 2\rho_{0}h|X_{k}|^{2}\leq\alpha h|X_{k}|^{2}
,

•

2\rho_{0}\theta^{2}h^{2}\leq(2\theta-1)h^{2}\implies 2\rho_{0}\theta^{2}h^{3}|b(X_{k})|^{2}\leq(2\theta-1)h^{2}|b(X_{k})|^{2}
,

so

\rho_{0}h|M_{k}|^{2}\leq\alpha h|X_{k}|^{2}+(2\theta-1)h^{2}|b(X_{k})|^{2}.

Substituting back into the expression for
\mathbb{E}|M_{k+1}|^{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|M_{k+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+c_{1}h-\alpha h|X_{k}|^{2}-(2\theta-1)h^{2}|b(X_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\rho_{0}h)\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+\rho_{0}h\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+c_{1}h-\alpha h|X_{k}|^{2}-(2\theta-1)h^{2}|b(X_{k})|^{2}\

\displaystyle\leq(1-\rho_{0}h)\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+c_{1}h.

Iterating this inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|M_{k+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\rho_{0}h)\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}+c_{1}h

\displaystyle\leq(1-\rho_{0}h)^{2}\mathbb{E}|M_{k-1}|^{2}+c_{1}h(1-\rho_{0}h)+c_{1}h

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq(1-\rho_{0}h)^{k+1}\mathbb{E}|M_{0}|^{2}+c_{1}h\sum_{j=0}^{k}(1-\rho_{0}h)^{j}.

Since
0<1-\rho_{0}h<1
(by the stepsize condition
0<\rho_{0}h<1
), the geometric series converges:

\sum_{j=0}^{k}(1-\rho_{0}h)^{j}=\frac{1-(1-\rho_{0}h)^{k+1}}{\rho_{0}h}\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{0}h}.

Thus, for any
k\geq 0
, we have

\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}\leq(1-\rho_{0}h)^{k}\mathbb{E}|M_{0}|^{2}+\frac{c_{1}}{\rho_{0}}.

Noting
M_{0}=X_{0}-\theta hb(X_{0})
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}\leq(1-\rho_{0}h)^{k}\mathbb{E}|X_{0}-\theta hb(X_{0})|^{2}+\frac{c_{1}}{\rho_{0}}.

From the definition of
M_{k}
,

|M_{k}|^{2}=|X_{k}-\theta hb(X_{k})|^{2}=|X_{k}|^{2}-2\theta h\langle X_{k},b(X_{k})\rangle+\theta^{2}h^{2}|b(X_{k})|^{2}.

Using Assumption
2.3
again,
2\langle X_{k},b(X_{k})\rangle\leq c_{1}-\alpha|X_{k}|^{2}-l_{2}|g(X_{k})|^{2}\leq c_{1}-\alpha|X_{k}|^{2}
, so

\displaystyle|M_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|X_{k}|^{2}-\theta h(c_{1}-\alpha|X_{k}|^{2})+\theta^{2}h^{2}|b(X_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\theta\alpha h)|X_{k}|^{2}-c_{1}\theta h.

Taking expectations and rearranging,

(1+\theta\alpha h)\mathbb{E}|X_{k}|^{2}-c_{1}\theta h\leq\mathbb{E}|M_{k}|^{2}\leq(1-\rho_{0}h)^{k}\mathbb{E}|X_{0}-\theta hb(X_{0})|^{2}+\frac{c_{1}}{\rho_{0}}.

Therefore,

\mathbb{E}|X_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{(1-\rho_{0}h)^{k}}{1+\theta\alpha h}\mathbb{E}|X_{0}-\theta hb(X_{0})|^{2}+\frac{c_{1}}{\rho_{0}(1+\theta\alpha h)}+\frac{c_{1}\theta h}{1+\theta\alpha h}.

So under Assumption
2.3
and stepsize condition
0<\rho_{0}h<1
, then the solution obeys

\mathbb{E}|X_{k}|^{2}\leq C_{1},

where
C_{1}>0
is a constant .
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
hold. Let
\{X_{k}\}
and
\{Y_{k}\}
be two numerical solutions of the stochastic theta method (
2
) with different initial values
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
. For any
h\in(0,1)
satisfying
0<h<1/\tilde{\rho}_{0}
, then

\mathbb{E}|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\leq e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{0}hk}(1+C_{2})|X_{0}-Y_{0}|^{2},

where
\tilde{\rho}_{0}=\left\{\dfrac{\beta}{2}\wedge\dfrac{2\theta-1}{2\theta^{2}}\right\}
and
C_{2}>0
is a constant.

Proof.

Define
D_{k}=X_{k}-Y_{k}
and
R_{k}=D_{k}-\theta h[b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})]
. The sequences
\{X_{k}\}
and
\{Y_{k}\}
satisfy

\displaystyle X_{k+1}

\displaystyle=X_{k}+\theta h\,b(X_{k+1})+(1-\theta)h\,b(X_{k})+g(X_{k})\,\Delta W_{k},

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=Y_{k}+\theta h\,b(Y_{k+1})+(1-\theta)h\,b(Y_{k})+g(Y_{k})\,\Delta W_{k},

where
\Delta W_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,h)
is the Brownian increment. Subtracting the two equations gives

D_{k+1}=D_{k}+\theta h\big[b(X_{k+1})-b(Y_{k+1})\big]+(1-\theta)h\big[b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})\big]+\big[g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})\big]\Delta W_{k}.

Hence, by the definition of
R_{k+1}
, we have

R_{k+1}=D_{k+1}-\theta h\big[b(X_{k+1})-b(Y_{k+1})\big]=D_{k}+(1-\theta)h\big[b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})\big]+\big[g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})\big]\Delta W_{k},

which can be rewritten as

R_{k+1}=R_{k}+h\big[b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})\big]+\big[g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})\big]\Delta W_{k}.

Squaring both sides yields and taking expectation , we obtain

\mathbb{E}|R_{k+1}|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}+2h\,\mathbb{E}\langle R_{k},\,b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})\rangle+h^{2}\,\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}+h\,\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})|^{2}.

Substituting
R_{k}=D_{k}-\theta h[b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})]
into the inner product term yields:

\langle R_{k},\,b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})\rangle=\langle D_{k},\,b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})\rangle-\theta h\,|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}.

By Assumption
2.2
,

\displaystyle 2\langle D_{k},b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})\rangle+l_{1}|g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})|^{2}\leq-\beta|D_{k}|^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle 2\langle D_{k},b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})\rangle\leq-\beta|D_{k}|^{2}-l_{1}|g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})|^{2}.

Substitute this inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|R_{k+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}+h\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})|^{2}+h\left(-\beta\mathbb{E}|D_{k}|^{2}-l_{1}\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+(1-2\theta)h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}-\beta h\mathbb{E}|D_{k}|^{2}-(l_{1}-1)h\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-2\theta)h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}.

Since
l_{1}\geq 1
, we get
-(l_{1}-1)h\mathbb{E}|g(X_{k})-g(Y_{k})|^{2}\leq 0
.
Therefore,

\mathbb{E}|R_{k+1}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}-\beta h\mathbb{E}|D_{k}|^{2}+(1-2\theta)h^{2}\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}.

Now define

\tilde{\rho}_{0}=\left\{\frac{\beta}{2}\wedge\frac{2\theta-1}{2\theta^{2}}\right\}.

Since
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
and
\beta>0
, we have
\tilde{\rho}_{0}>0
and
0<\tilde{\rho}_{0}h<1
for sufficiently small
h
. From the definition of
\tilde{\rho}_{0}
, we have
2\tilde{\rho}_{0}\leq\beta
and
2\tilde{\rho}_{0}\theta^{2}\leq 2\theta-1
. Using the inequality
|R_{k}|^{2}\leq 2|D_{k}|^{2}+2\theta^{2}h^{2}|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}
, we get

\tilde{\rho}_{0}h\,|R_{k}|^{2}\leq 2\tilde{\rho}_{0}h\,|D_{k}|^{2}+2\tilde{\rho}_{0}\theta^{2}h^{3}\,|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}\leq\beta h\,|D_{k}|^{2}+(2\theta-1)h^{2}\,|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}.

Rearranging this yields

\beta h\,|D_{k}|^{2}\geq\tilde{\rho}_{0}h\,|R_{k}|^{2}-(2\theta-1)h^{2}\,|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}.

Substituting this lower bound into the inequality for
\mathbb{E}|R_{k+1}|^{2}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|R_{k+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{0}h\,\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}+(2\theta-1)h^{2}\,\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}+(1-2\theta)h^{2}\,\mathbb{E}|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\tilde{\rho}_{0}h)\,\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}.

Iterating this inequality from step
0
to
k
:

\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}\leq(1-\tilde{\rho}_{0}h)^{k}\,\mathbb{E}|R_{0}|^{2}.

Using the elementary inequality
(1-x)^{k}\leq e^{-xk}
for
x\in(0,1)
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}\leq e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{0}hk}\,\mathbb{E}|R_{0}|^{2}.

Noting
R_{0}=D_{0}-\theta h[b(x_{0})-b(y_{0})]=(x_{0}-y_{0})-\theta h[b(x_{0})-b(y_{0})]
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}\leq e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{0}hk}\mathbb{E}\left|(x_{0}-y_{0})-\theta h[b(x_{0})-b(y_{0})]\right|^{2}.

Retracing
D_{k}
, by Assumption
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle|R_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|D_{k}-\theta h[b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})]|^{2}

\displaystyle=|D_{k}|^{2}-2\theta h\langle D_{k},b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})\rangle+\theta^{2}h^{2}|b(X_{k})-b(Y_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|D_{k}|^{2}+\beta\theta h|D_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+\beta\theta h)|D_{k}|^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(1+\beta\theta h)\mathbb{E}|D_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|R_{k}|^{2}\leq e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{0}hk}\mathbb{E}\left[(x_{0}-y_{0})-\theta h(b(x_{0})-b(y_{0}))\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\implies\mathbb{E}|D_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{0}hk}}{(1+\beta\theta h)}\mathbb{E}\left[(x_{0}-y_{0})-\theta h(b(x_{0})-b(y_{0}))\right]^{2}.

Noting
D_{k}=X_{k}-Y_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}=e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{0}hk}\mathbb{E}\left[(x_{0}-y_{0})-\theta h(b(x_{0})-b(y_{0}))\right]^{2}\leq e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{0}hk}(1+C_{2})|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{2}.

So under Assumption
2.2
and stepsize condition
0<\tilde{\rho}_{0}h<1
, then the solution obeys

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\leq e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{0}hk}(1+C_{2})|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{2},

where
\tilde{\rho}_{0}=\left\{\dfrac{\beta}{2}\wedge\dfrac{2\theta-1}{2\theta^{2}}\right\}
and
C_{2}>0
is a constant.
∎

Now, we are ready to present the main result on the existence and uniqueness of the numerical invariant measure.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
hold, for any
h\in(0,1)
satisfying

h<h^{*}:=\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\wedge\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}_{0}},

where
\tilde{\rho}_{0}=\left\{\dfrac{\beta}{2}\wedge\dfrac{2\theta-1}{2\theta^{2}}\right\}
and

\rho_{0}=\left\{\dfrac{\alpha}{2}\wedge\dfrac{2\theta-1}{2\theta^{2}}\right\}
,
the stochastic theta method (
2
) converges in the
p
-Wasserstein distance to a unique invariant measure
\widehat{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with some exponential rate
\xi_{1}>0
for any
p\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Due to the Chebyshev inequality and Lemma
3.1
, for any initial value
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain that the sequence
\{\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h}\}_{k\geq 1}
is tight, where
\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h}=\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h}(x_{0},\cdot)
denotes the probability measure of
X_{k}
starting from
x_{0}
. Then, a subsequence that converges weakly to an invariant measure
\hat{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be extracted. By Lemma
3.2
, under the stepsize condition
0<h<h^{*}
, we have

\mathbb{E}|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\leq(1+C_{2})|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{2}e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{0}hk}.

By the Hölder inequality and the definition of the
p
-Wasserstein distance, for any
p\in(0,1)
,

W_{p}(\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h},\delta_{y_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h})\leq\mathbb{E}|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{p}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\leq(1+C_{2})^{p/2}|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{p}e^{-p\tilde{\xi}_{1}kh},

(3)

where
\tilde{\xi}_{1}=\tilde{\rho}_{0}/2
.

Thanks to Lemma
3.1
and the Kolmogorov-Chapman equation, for any
k,l>0
and
p\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle W_{p}(\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h},\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{(k+l)}^{h})

\displaystyle=W_{p}(\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h},\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{l}^{h})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W_{p}(\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h},\delta_{y_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h})\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{l}^{h}(x_{0},dy_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+C_{2})^{p/2}|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{p}e^{-p\tilde{\xi}_{1}kh}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{l}^{h}(x_{0},dy_{0})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}(1+C_{2})^{p/2}\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+\mathbb{E}|X_{l}|^{p}\right)e^{-p\tilde{\xi}_{1}kh}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}(1+C_{2})^{p/2}\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+(\mathbb{E}|X_{l}|^{2})^{p/2}\right)e^{-p\tilde{\xi}_{1}kh}.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\mathbb{E}|X_{l}|^{2}\leq C_{1}
for all
l\geq 0
. Therefore,

W_{p}(\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h},\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{(k+l)}^{h})\leq K(p)e^{-p\tilde{\xi}_{1}kh},

(4)

where
K(p):=2^{p/2}(1+C_{2})^{p/2}\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+C_{1}^{p/2}\right)
. Now, letting
l\to\infty
in (
4
), since
\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{(k+l)}^{h}
converges weakly to
\hat{\pi}
, we have

W_{p}(\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h},\hat{\pi})\leq K(p)e^{-p\tilde{\xi}_{1}kh}.

(5)

Moreover, we have
W_{p}(\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h},\hat{\pi})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, which guarantees that
\hat{\pi}
is an invariant measure of
\{\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h}\}
.

Now, assume that
\hat{\pi}_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an invariant measure of
X_{k}
with initial value
x_{0}
and
\hat{\pi}_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an invariant measure of
X_{k}
with initial value
y_{0}
. We can see that

W_{p}(\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}W_{p}(\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h},\delta_{y_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h})\nu(dx_{0},dy_{0}),

for any
x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x_{0}\neq y_{0}
, and
\nu
is a coupling of
\hat{\pi}_{1}
and
\hat{\pi}_{2}
. Consequently, it follows from (
3
) that the stochastic theta method admits a unique invariant measure.
∎

Motivated by the argument in
3
, we derive the following theorem concerning the invariant measure of the underlying equation. Since the proof follows the same line of reasoning as that in
3
, we omit the proof.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
hold. Then the solution of (
1
) converges in the
p
-Wasserstein distance to a unique invariant measure
\pi
with some exponential rate
\xi_{2}>0
.

We now state the convergence result of the numerical invariant measure to its underlying counterpart.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that Assumptions 2.1, 2.2 and 2.3 hold. Then the numerical invariant measure
\hat{\pi}
converges to the underlying invariant measure
\pi
in the
p
-Wasserstein distance, that is, for any
h\in(0,h^{*})
, we have

W_{p}(\hat{\pi},\pi)\leq Ch^{p/2}.

Proof.

Thanks to the triangle inequality, we have

W_{p}(\hat{\pi},\pi)\leq W_{p}\bigl(\hat{\pi},\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h})+W_{p}\bigl(\delta_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{k},\pi\bigr)+W_{p}\bigl(\delta_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{k},\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h}\bigr).

It follows from Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
that
\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h}
and
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{h}
converge to
\hat{\pi}
and
\pi
, respectively. Therefore,

W_{p}(\hat{\pi},\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h})\leq Ce^{-p\tilde{\xi}_{1}kh}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{p}\bigl(\delta_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{k},\pi\bigr)\leq Ce^{-r\xi_{2}kh},

where
C
is some constant.
For the last term, by the strong convergence result of the stochastic theta method in
23
, we have

\displaystyle W_{p}(\delta_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{k},\delta_{x_{0}}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{h})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|X(t_{k})-{X}_{k}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}|X(t_{k})-{X}_{k}|^{2}\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{p/2}.

Letting
k\to\infty
, we obtain

W_{p}(\hat{\pi},\pi)\leq Ch^{p/2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

dX(t)=(1-2X(t)-5X^{3}(t))dt+X^{2}(t)dW(t),\quad X(0)=5.

b(x)=1-2x-5x^{3}
and
g(x)=x^{2}

2.1
-
2.3
hold. Indeed, Assumption
2.1
holds with
q=3
and
L=8
. Assumption
2.2
holds with
l_{1}=4
and
\beta=4
. Moreover, Assumption
2.3
holds with
l_{2}=9
,
c_{1}=1
and
\alpha=3
. Therefore, according
to our theorems in Section
3
there exists a unique invariant measure for the stochastic theta method.

One thousand sample paths are simulated with
X_{0}=5
,
h=0.01
and
T=100
, which are then used to construct empirical density functions at different time points. It is clear to see from the left plot in Figure

that the shapes of empirical density functions at

,

and

are quite

t=4
and

-5

Example 4.2
.

Consider the following two dimensional SDE

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dX_{1}(t)=(1-2X_{1}(t)+0.5X_{2}(t)-X^{3}_{1}(t))dt+0.3X^{2}_{1}(t)dW_{1}(t),\\
dX_{2}(t)=(1+0.5X_{1}(t)-2X_{2}(t)-X^{3}_{2}(t))dt+0.3X^{2}_{2}(t)dW_{2}(t),\end{array}\right.

where
X_{1}(0)=X_{2}(0)=5
.

We now verify that Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
are satisfied for this example.

Set
x=(x_{1},x_{2})^{\mathrm{T}}
, we have

b(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1-2x_{1}+0.5x_{2}-x_{1}^{3}\\
1+0.5x_{1}-2x_{2}-x_{2}^{3}\end{pmatrix},\qquad g(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0.3x_{1}^{2}&0\\
0&0.3x_{2}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

First, we verify Assumption
2.1
. For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y)=-2(x_{1}-y_{1})+0.5(x_{2}-y_{2})-(x_{1}^{3}-y_{1}^{3}),

\displaystyle b_{2}(x)-b_{2}(y)=0.5(x_{1}-y_{1})-2(x_{2}-y_{2})-(x_{2}^{3}-y_{2}^{3}).

Moreover,

g(x)-g(y)=\begin{pmatrix}0.3(x_{1}^{2}-y_{1}^{2})&0\\
0&0.3(x_{2}^{2}-y_{2}^{2})\end{pmatrix},

Since
x_{i}^{3}-y_{i}^{3}=(x_{i}-y_{i})(x_{i}^{2}+x_{i}y_{i}+y_{i}^{2})
and
x_{i}^{2}-y_{i}^{2}=(x_{i}-y_{i})(x_{i}+y_{i})
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|b(x)-b(y)|\vee|g(x)-g(y)|\leq C(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})|x-y|.

Therefore, Assumption
2.1
holds with
q=3
.

Next, we verify Assumption
2.2
. Let

\delta=x-y=(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})^{T}
, where
\delta_{i}=x_{i}-y_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle 2\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle=

\displaystyle-4\delta_{1}^{2}-4\delta_{2}^{2}+2\delta_{1}\delta_{2}

\displaystyle-2\delta_{1}^{2}(x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}y_{1}+y_{1}^{2})-2\delta_{2}^{2}(x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}y_{2}+y_{2}^{2}).

Moreover,

|g(x)-g(y)|^{2}=0.09\delta_{1}^{2}(x_{1}+y_{1})^{2}+0.09\delta_{2}^{2}(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2}.

Taking
l_{1}=4
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle+4|g(x)-g(y)|^{2}=

\displaystyle-4\delta_{1}^{2}-4\delta_{2}^{2}+2\delta_{1}\delta_{2}

\displaystyle+\delta_{1}^{2}\left[-2(x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}y_{1}+y_{1}^{2})+0.36(x_{1}+y_{1})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\delta_{2}^{2}\left[-2(x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}y_{2}+y_{2}^{2})+0.36(x_{2}+y_{2})^{2}\right].

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

-2(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})+0.36(a+b)^{2}=-1.64a^{2}-1.28ab-1.64b^{2}\leq 0.

Therefore,

2\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle+4|g(x)-g(y)|^{2}\leq-4\delta_{1}^{2}-4\delta_{2}^{2}+2\delta_{1}\delta_{2}.

Using
2\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\leq\delta_{1}^{2}+\delta_{2}^{2}
, we further get

2\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle+4|g(x)-g(y)|^{2}\leq-3(\delta_{1}^{2}+\delta_{2}^{2})=-3|x-y|^{2}.

Thus, Assumption
2.2
holds with
l_{1}=4
and
\beta=3
.

Finally, we verify Assumption
2.3
. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle 2\langle x,b(x)\rangle=

\displaystyle 2x_{1}(1-2x_{1}+0.5x_{2}-x_{1}^{3})+2x_{2}(1+0.5x_{1}-2x_{2}-x_{2}^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2x_{1}+2x_{2}-4x_{1}^{2}-4x_{2}^{2}+2x_{1}x_{2}-2x_{1}^{4}-2x_{2}^{4}.

Moreover,

|g(x)|^{2}=0.09(x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{4}).

Taking
l_{2}=9
, we have

\displaystyle 2\langle x,b(x)\rangle+9|g(x)|^{2}=

\displaystyle 2x_{1}+2x_{2}-4x_{1}^{2}-4x_{2}^{2}+2x_{1}x_{2}

\displaystyle-1.19(x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{4}).

Since

2x_{1}+2x_{2}\leq x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+2

and

2x_{1}x_{2}\leq x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2},

it follows that

\displaystyle 2\langle x,b(x)\rangle+9|g(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2-2x_{1}^{2}-2x_{2}^{2}-1.19(x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{4})

\displaystyle\leq 2-2|x|^{2}.

Thus, Assumption
2.3
holds with
l_{2}=9
,
c_{1}=2
and
\alpha=2
.

5
Conclusions, discussions and future research

In this paper, the stochastic theta method is used to approximate the invariant measure of some SDEs, whose drift and diffusion coefficients are allowed to contain super-linear terms. The existence and uniqueness of the numerical invariant measure of the numerical solution generated by the stochastic theta method is proved. And the convergence of the numerical invariant measure to the exact invariant measure of the underlying SDEs is also proved.

As the second moments are used, the coefficient of the linear term in the drift part has to be negative (the
-\alpha
in Assumption
2.3
). Meanwhile, it is well known that if some
p
th moments are used for some small
p\in(0,1)
, one may still get the similar results
13
. Therefore, one of the potential future research is to release the requirement in Assumption
2.3
.

Another very promising future research is to investigate the stochastic theta method for some SDEs with state-dependent switching, as numerical invariant measures of this type of SDEs could help with sampling from finite mixture distributions, which then are quite useful in various machine learning topics
22
.
