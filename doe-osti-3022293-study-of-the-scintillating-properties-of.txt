 Abstract of CRADA work:

 The goal of the project is to investigate scintillating properties of materials based on self-
 assembled InAs QDs embedded into GaAs bulk. According to theoretical predictions, such
 materials can have scintillating properties and low self-absorption. Compared to scintillators
 based on inorganic scintillating crystals, QD-based scintillators could have significantly higher
 light yield and shorter emission times. We intend to check the theoretical predictions
 experimentally, measure scintillation parameters and their dependence on the QD-based
 materials and learn how the properties could be optimized for practical applications.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Within the period covered by this CRADA
 • the technology of manufacturing QD/GaAs-based, thin, fast, and radiation hard
 scintillation detectors integrated with the bump-bonded readout electronics has been
 developed

 • with the introduction of an AlGaAs barrier, the scintillator radiation hardness has been
 improved up to 10^15 1MeV-neq/cm2
 • the signal rise time of ~ 150 ps and the scintillation time of ~0.3 ns has been measured
 • the energy resolution of the QD/GaAs-based scintillators of σ(E)/E=1.9% @ 4.4 MeV has
 been measured
 • detection of 122 keV and 60 keV photons has been demonstrated
 • the light collection of up to ~40,000 photons/MeV has been measured, corresponding to
 overall light collection efficiency of 15%. This marks an improvement by a factor of x3
 within this research effort
