Nature of Work
Describe the research (summary of Scope of Work and principal objectives of the CRADA):

ANL-1060 (02/13/2023)

Oklo Inc. and Deep Isolation US LLC partnered with Argonne National Laboratory to develop and demonstrate a
highly efficient electrochemical oxide reduction (OR) process that meets the cost and waste metrics required for a
commercial pyroprocessing facility.

The work aimed to advance OR technology to sufficient commercial readiness to support the goal of 1¢/kWh
uranium/transuranic (U/TRU) fuel by (1) developing new multimodal process monitoring sensors that provide several
unique data streams to accurately determine the extent of metal conversion, (2) developing next-generation mixed-
metal oxide anodes that avoid costs and operational constraints associated with traditional platinum anodes, and
(3) designing a new fuel basket to eliminate inhomogeneities in the metal product and maximize oxide to metal
conversion. These enabling advances in OR technologies were combined and demonstrated in a pilot-scale OR
module to convert uranium dioxide feedstock to a metal suitable for electrorefining.

In parallel, commercial analyses aimed to optimize the selection and procurement of existing used light water reactor
(LWR) fuel for recycling into advanced reactor (AR) fuel and develop an integrated waste disposal plan using cost-
effective borehole disposal for OR process wastes. Performing the technical and economic analyses of the waste
streams alongside OR process development supports that a disposal pathway exists before the technology is fully
commercialized. A survey of existing LWR fuel inventories examining the range of burnup and cooling times was
completed to ensure that suitable material is available to support startup and long-term operation of a commercial
pyroprocessing facility.

Electroreduction of uranium oxide was first demonstrated and patented by Argonne in the early 2000s as a
technology to convert used oxide nuclear fuel from light water reactors to metal for further fuel reprocessing. More
recently, executive orders issued by President Trump highlighted the need for a source of fuel for new advanced
reactors and ability for commercialization of pyroprocessing to support AR deployment. This project’s efforts to
address feedstock, processing technology, and waste disposal were combined to increase the OR process
technology readiness level (TRL) and provide confidence in the economics of pyrochemical reprocessing of LWR
fuel in support of pyroprocessing commercialization and advanced reactor deployment.

Conclusions drawn from this CRADA; include any major accomplishments:
This document serves as a summary report for the work conducted under ARPA-E CURIE CRADA A00072 between
Argonne National Laboratory, Oklo Inc., and Deep Isolation US LLC to advance a key step in recycling used nuclear
fuel via pyroprocessing by improving the electrochemical oxide reduction of uranium oxide fuel into metal. The work
adds to OR technology in three main ways. First, real time monitoring to assess the extent of oxide reduction was
developed and demonstrated using a combination of in situ sensors to improve process efficiency and decrease
operating time. The suite of monitoring tools successfully indicated oxide-to-metal conversion to within 5% of that
determined by direct analytical measurements of the product. Second, novel mixed metal oxide (MMO) anodes
were formulated and demonstrated to provide a stable alternative to conventional platinum anodes, significantly
increasing the anode lifetime and decreasing cost. High electrochemical stability of the MMO anodes was
demonstrated in laboratory, engineering, and pilot-scale tests over extended operational times. Third, the fuel basket
was redesigned, which improved coulombic efficiency while maintaining high conversion. Finally, these advances
were integrated into the design and installation of a pilot-scale oxide reduction module capable of processing up to
10 kg of heavy metal in a single batch. The technical results from the engineering-scale tests and pilot-scale cell
demonstration ultimately showed that these advancements are effective and promising for commercial applications.
ANL-1060 (02/13/2023)

The project included performing an economic analysis matching potential light water reactor fuel vectors with optimal
and preferred pyroprocessing feedstocks and identified viable strategies for securing the initial material needed to
support a future recycling facility. The project further evaluated management of process waste streams from OR
and pyroprocessing flowsheets and determined that utilizing deep borehole disposal could meet safety requirements
while remaining below the target disposal cost metric. Disposal pathways were identified for all waste streams
generated by OR.

Summary of technology transfer benefits to industry and, if applicable, path forward/anticipated next steps
towards commercialization:
This work addressed two long-standing nuclear energy challenges: improving fuel utilization by recovering the
energy still contained in used nuclear fuel and managing the resulting waste safely and affordably. Recovering
uranium and transuranic materials from existing used fuel supplies could decrease reliance on non-domestic
uranium supplies, support advanced reactor fuel supply, and extract more value from resources already produced
by the current nuclear fleet. Implementation of improved process monitoring and advanced electrode materials
decreases technical risk for oxide reduction technology and will support future licensing and deployment of recycling
technology. In addition, the results of the waste management analysis support economical deep borehole disposal
of pyroprocessing waste streams. Overall, the project provides technical knowledge, engineering tools, and
commercial planning that move nuclear fuel recycling closer to practical implementation in the United States.
