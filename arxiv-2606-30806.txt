Thermal Double-Twist Data in Holography
V. Niarchos, C. Papageorgakis, A. Stratoudakis

Abstract
We explain how to extract thermal OPE coefficients of double-twist operators in scalar two-point functions at infinite spatial volume from suitably regularized integrals of thermal response functions in momentum space. As a specific application, we implement the proposed approach to four-dimensional holographic CFTs with an Einstein bulk action, where the finite-temperature state is captured by an AdS 5 black-brane geometry. In that case, the thermal response function can be computed on the gravitational side by solving numerically a radial ODE reduction of the Klein–Gordon equation. We obtain accurate values of double-twist data from this solution, completing previous holographic and bootstrap studies of thermal two-point functions. Some of the reported spin-resolved data that we compute are new.

Introduction and Summary

We consider
d
-dimensional conformal field theories (CFTs) on
S^{1}_{\beta}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
. The
S^{1}_{\beta}
is a thermal circle with inverse temperature
\beta
. We are interested in the structure of the Operator Product Expansion (OPE) of scalar two-point functions
\langle{\cal O}(x){\cal O}(0)\rangle_{\beta}
on this geometry for generic scalar operators
{\cal O}
with scaling dimension
\Delta_{\cal O}
. When the OPE is inserted inside the two-point function, it allows us to re-express the latter as a series of terms that are proportional to the thermal one-point functions of primary operators. These thermal one-point functions encode useful information about the thermal properties of the CFT and are the main target of this paper.

When the CFT admits a holographic limit at large central charge
c
, the OPE of a thermal scalar two-point function is dominated at leading order in
1/c
by the contributions of the identity operator, double-twist operators
[{\cal O}{\cal O}]_{s,J}
(schematically of the form

), the energy-momentum tensor
T^{\mu\nu}
and multi-trace composites thereof,
[T^{m}]_{J}
, without derivatives
[
25
]
. The holographic importance of the energy-momentum sector is immediately obvious: it maps directly to graviton physics in the dual AdS black-hole geometry. Nevertheless, a fully consistent Kubo–Martin–Schwinger (KMS)-invariant two-point function is impossible without the double-twist sector and its presence is crucial in subtle geometric effects, like the resolution of the bouncing singularity in
d\geq 3
CFTs

The thermal one-point functions of the energy-momentum sector can be computed cleanly from gravity

The present paper aims to fill the current gap in the holographic computation of thermal double-twist data by providing a new computational route. The main input (the thermal response function) still comes from a numerical solution of the bulk Klein–Gordon equation, but we do not attempt to determine directly the two-point function in physical space (by solving a PDE) and read off the OPE coefficients as in previous approaches. Instead, we compute the thermal response function in momentum space by solving a radial ODE reduction of the bulk Klein–Gordon equation and the solution becomes the input to a formula that relates each double-twist OPE datum individually to a regulated momentum-space integral of the thermal response function. The basis of this formula is a point-splitting definition of the double-twist operators together with the OPE that allows us to understand how to regulate the divergences in the coincident-point limit.

Our analysis is general and applies to holographic CFTs in spacetime dimensions
d\geq 3
. As a specific illustration, we focus on the case of a four-dimensional holographic CFT with an AdS
5
black-brane dual and consider the thermal two-point function of a scalar primary operator with generic scaling dimension
\Delta_{\cal O}

\Delta_{\cal O}
is an integer (see e.g.
[

]

\Delta_{\cal O}
is a non-integer number above the unitarity bound. For example, at
\Delta_{\cal O}=\frac{3}{2}

a_{0,0}=1.113079(1)\;,\qquad a_{1,0}=0.489(1)\;,\qquad a_{0,2}=2.399(1)\;.

The lightest scalar coefficient
a_{0,0}

[

Nothing in our construction is special to the lightest operators reported above. The same approach
expresses every double-twist coefficient as a regulated momentum-space
integral of the thermal response function. Reaching the heavier double-twists is
essentially a matter of numerical control. As the operator dimension grows, the corresponding thermal data probe the thermal response at
larger momenta, demanding a correspondingly more accurate solution of the radial
Klein–Gordon ODE and a more delicate regularization of the coincident-point

Thermal response function from the
AdS_{5}
black brane

We are working in five-dimensional Einstein gravity with a negative cosmological constant. The relevant Euclidean background is the planar AdS
5
black brane solution with metric

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\frac{\ell^{2}}{z^{2}}\Bigl(f(z)\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}z^{2}}{f(z)}\Bigr)\;,\qquad f(z)=1-\frac{z^{4}}{z_{h}^{4}}\;,\qquad T=\frac{1}{\pi z_{h}}\;,

(2.1)

where
z=0
is the conformal boundary,
z=z_{h}
the horizon,
\ell
the AdS radius,
and the Euclidean time is periodic,
\tau\sim\tau+\beta
with
\beta=1/T=\pi z_{h}
.
The zero-temperature, pure AdS
5
geometry is recovered in the limit
z_{h}\to\infty

(
f\to 1
). This theory is assumed to be the gravitational dual of a four-dimensional CFT on
S^{1}_{\beta}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
at large central charge
c\sim N^{2}
and strong coupling.

The bulk theory also contains a free scalar field
\phi
of mass
m
, which is dual to a scalar primary operator
{\cal O}
on the boundary with scaling dimension
\Delta_{\cal O}
. The relation between bulk mass and the boundary scaling dimension follows from the standard identification

m^{2}\ell^{2}=\Delta_{\cal O}(\Delta_{\cal O}-4)\;.

(2.2)

We are interested in the thermal two-point function of the boundary operator

g(\tau,\boldsymbol{x})\coloneqq\langle{\cal O}(\tau,\boldsymbol{x})\,{\cal O}(0,\boldsymbol{0})\rangle_{\beta}\;.

(2.3)

2.1
Zero-temperature warm-up

To begin with, we consider the zero-temperature case, which allows us to fix the notation and the corresponding operator normalization. At quadratic order, the bulk scalar equation of motion is the Klein–Gordon equation on the AdS
5
background. Separating the bulk radial coordinate
z
from the boundary coordinates
x^{\mu}=(\tau,\boldsymbol{x})
, we set

\phi(x,z)=e^{ip\cdot x}\psi_{p}(z)
, with Euclidean momentum
p
such that
p^{2}\geq 0
. This reduces the Klein–Gordon equation to the radial ODE

\psi^{\prime\prime}_{p}-\frac{3}{z}\psi^{\prime}_{p}-\Bigl(p^{2}+\frac{m^{2}\ell^{2}}{z^{2}}\Bigr)\psi_{p}=0\;.

(2.4)

The substitution
\psi_{p}=z^{2}\chi_{p}
maps this equation further to the modified Bessel equation of order
\nu=|\Delta_{\cal O}-2|
, and the requirement of regularity in the interior (
z\to\infty
) selects the solution

\psi_{p}(z)=z^{2}\,K_{\nu}(pz)\;,

(2.5)

with
K_{\nu}
the modified Bessel function of the second kind. Expanding near the boundary exposes the two
characteristic powers,

\psi(z)=A(p)\,z^{4-\Delta_{\cal O}}+\cdots+B(p)\,z^{\Delta_{\cal O}}+\cdots\;,

(2.6)

which identify the source
A(p)
and the response
B(p)
in the holographic dictionary. For simplicity, we have assumed
\Delta_{\cal O}\notin\mathbb{N}
.
4
4

4

For integer
\Delta_{\cal O}
one must consider carefully the presence of logarithmic terms in the near-boundary expansion (
2.6
).
In standard quantization (
\Delta_{\cal O}>2
) the source is
A
and the response
B
, giving the zero-temperature response function
{\cal G}_{0}=B/A
. The case where
\Delta_{\cal O}<2
corresponds to the alternative quantization, in which the roles of
A
and
B
are exchanged and the response function is

{\cal G}_{0}(p)=\frac{A(p)}{B(p)}=\frac{\Gamma(\nu)}{\Gamma(-\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{p}{2}\Bigr)^{-2\nu}\;,

(2.7)

which decays as
p\to\infty
. The subscript 0 emphasizes that this is a zero-temperature quantity. Holographic renormalization of the
on-shell action (see
Appendix A. Holographic renormalization
) and the holographic dictionary yield the standard CFT two-point function, the position-space Fourier transform of the response (
2.7
). We adopt the unit normalization

\langle{\cal O}(x){\cal O}(0)\rangle_{T=0}=\frac{1}{|x|^{2\Delta_{\cal O}}}={\cal N}_{\Delta_{\cal O}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\,{\cal G}_{0}(p)\;,

(2.8)

where carrying out the transform of (
2.7
) fixes the constant

{\cal N}_{\Delta_{\cal O}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{(\Delta_{\cal O}-1)(\Delta_{\cal O}-2)}\;.

(2.9)

2.2
The finite-temperature radial problem

At finite temperature, the separation of variables

\phi(\tau,\boldsymbol{x},z)=e^{i\omega_{n}\tau+i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\,\psi_{n,k}(z)\;,\qquad\omega_{n}=\frac{2\pi n}{\beta}=\frac{2n}{z_{h}}\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z}),\quad k\coloneqq|\boldsymbol{k}|\;,

(2.10)

takes into account the periodicity in
\tau
with
\omega_{n}
the bosonic Matsubara frequencies. Upon substitution, the Klein–Gordon equation yields the following radial ODE

f\,\psi_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}+\Bigl(f^{\prime}-\frac{3}{z}f\Bigr)\psi_{n,k}^{\prime}+\Bigl(-\frac{\omega_{n}^{2}}{f}-k^{2}+\frac{4-\nu^{2}}{z^{2}}\Bigr)\psi_{n,k}=0\;,

(2.11)

with
f
the blackening factor of
Eq.

2.1
. Near the boundary
z=0
, this equation has a regular singular point with indices
\rho=2\pm\nu=\{4-\Delta_{\cal O},\,\Delta_{\cal O}\}
, reproducing the source/response structure of
Eq.

2.6
. The
associated Frobenius solution is

\psi_{n,k}(z)=A(n,k)\,z^{2-\nu}\sum_{m\geq 0}a_{m}z^{m}+B(n,k)\,z^{2+\nu}\sum_{m\geq 0}b_{m}z^{m},\qquad a_{0}=b_{0}=1\;,

(2.12)

with the relevant recursion relations between the coefficients collected in Appendix
B.1
. It is straightforward to check that the odd-
m
terms vanish and that the leading thermal corrections first appear at
m=4
.

To analyze the behavior near the horizon
z=z_{h}
, it is convenient to change variables to the dimensionless coordinate
u=z/z_{h}
and rewrite the radial
Eq.

2.11
as

f\,\ddot{\psi}_{n,k}+\Bigl(\dot{f}-\frac{3}{u}f\Bigr)\dot{\psi}_{n,k}+\Bigl(-\frac{4n^{2}}{f}-(kz_{h})^{2}+\frac{4-\nu^{2}}{u^{2}}\Bigr)\psi_{n,k}=0\;,

(2.13)

where the dots denote
u
-derivatives. At the horizon,
u=1
, the indices are
\rho=\pm|n|/2
, and regularity selects

\psi_{n,k}(u)=(1-u)^{|n|/2}\sum_{m\geq 0}h_{m}(1-u)^{m}\;.

(2.14)

The explicit form of the recursion relations between the
h_{m}
coefficients is also relegated to Appendix
B.1
. Horizon regularity fixes the bulk solution uniquely up to an overall normalization.

Connecting this horizon-regular solution to the boundary fixes the Frobenius
coefficients
A(n,k)
and
B(n,k)
in (
2.12
). Their overall
normalization cancels in the ratio, which defines the thermal response function (in standard quantization)

{\cal G}_{T}(n,k)=\frac{B(n,k)}{A(n,k)}\;,

(2.15)

the finite-temperature generalization of
{\cal G}_{0}
.

Holographic renormalization proceeds exactly as in the zero-temperature case (see
Appendix A. Holographic renormalization
) and gives the non-local renormalized action

S_{\rm ren}^{T}=-\nu\,\ell^{3}\,\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,A_{n}(k)\,{\cal G}_{T}(n,k)\,A_{n}(-k)\;,

(2.16)

from which the thermal momentum-space two-point function follows,

\langle{\cal O}{\cal O}\rangle\propto 2\nu\,\ell^{3}\,{\cal G}_{T}
.
We emphasize that
Eq.

2.16
is written for standard quantization. For the alternative quantization the Legendre transform exchanges source and response, as already incorporated in the response function (
2.7
). Inheriting the unit normalization from zero temperature,
Eq.

2.8
, once again trades the dimensional
2\nu\,\ell^{3}

for the
\ell
-independent
{\cal N}_{\Delta_{\cal O}}
of (
2.9
), so that

\langle{\cal O}(\omega_{n^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\,{\cal O}(\omega_{n},\boldsymbol{k})\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\,\beta\,{\cal N}_{\Delta_{\cal O}}\,{\cal G}_{T}(n,k)\,\delta_{n+n^{\prime},0}\,\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\;.

(2.17)

In position space, the thermal two-point function (
2.3
) is correspondingly the Fourier transform of the response,

g(\tau,\boldsymbol{x})={\cal N}_{\Delta_{\cal O}}\,\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{ip_{n}\cdot x}\,{\cal G}_{T}(n,k)\;,\qquad p_{n}=(\omega_{n},\boldsymbol{k})\;.

(2.18)

This is the finite-temperature analog of (
2.8
).

Double-twist data from double-trace deformations

[{\cal O}{\cal O}]_{s,J}

{\cal G}_{T}

The thermal two-point function

[

\beta
,

g(\tau,\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\Delta,J}\frac{a_{\Delta,J}}{\beta^{\Delta}}\,C_{J}^{(\hat{\nu})}(\eta)\,|x|^{-2\Delta_{\cal O}+\Delta}\;,\quad|x|\coloneqq\sqrt{\tau^{2}+\boldsymbol{x}^{2}}\;,\quad\eta\coloneqq\frac{\tau}{|x|}\;.

(3.1)

The sum runs over primary operators of dimension
\Delta
and even spins
J
in the
{\cal O}\times{\cal O}
OPE, while
C_{J}^{(\hat{\nu})}(\eta)
denote the Gegenbauer polynomials with
\hat{\nu}=(d-2)/2=1

\beta^{-\Delta}C_{J}^{(\hat{\nu})}(\eta)|x|^{-2\Delta_{\cal O}+\Delta}
is the position-space thermal block, while the coefficients

are the so-called thermal OPE data. For an exchanged operator
{\cal O}^{\prime}
of scaling dimension
\Delta
and spin

the coefficients
a_{\Delta,J}
combine the zero-temperature OPE coefficient

, the thermal one-point coefficient

, and the two-point normalization

as follows
[
25
]

a_{\Delta,J}=\frac{f_{{\cal O}{\cal O}{\cal O}^{\prime}}\,b_{{\cal O}^{\prime}}}{c_{{\cal O}^{\prime}}}\,\frac{J!}{2^{J}(\hat{\nu})_{J}}\;,

(3.2)

with

the Pochhammer symbol.

For a holographic CFT at large

the spectrum splits into: i) the identity, ii) the double-twist tower

[{\cal O}{\cal O}]_{s,J}\sim{\cal O}\,\Box^{s}\mathscr{D}_{J}{\cal O}

\Delta_{s,J}=2\Delta_{\cal O}+2s+J\;,\qquad s,J\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\ (J\ \text{even})\;,

(3.3)

and iii) the multi-stress-tensor tower
[T^{m}]_{J}
(without derivatives)
[

, with scaling dimension
\Delta=4m
and even spin

. The OPE decomposition of the thermal two-point function then reduces to

g(\tau,\boldsymbol{x})=|x|^{-2\Delta_{\cal O}}+\sum_{s,J}\frac{a_{s,J}}{\beta^{2\Delta_{\cal O}+2s+J}}\,C_{J}^{(1)}(\eta)\,|x|^{2s+J}+\sum_{m,J}\frac{a^{(m)}_{J}}{\beta^{4m}}\,C_{J}^{(1)}(\eta)\,|x|^{-2\Delta_{\cal O}+4m}\;.

(3.4)

The lowest-twist multi-stress-tensor data of the second tower have been obtained from holography

In this paper we focus on the computation of the double-twist contributions and the corresponding coefficients

3.1
Multi-trace deformations

Consider the generating functional
W[{\cal J}]
of connected single-trace correlation functions in a large-
N
(boundary) CFT defined formally as the path integral over some collection of fundamental fields denoted by
\varphi
, with action
S[\varphi]

Z[{\cal J}]=e^{-W[{\cal J}]}=\int\mathcal{D}\varphi\,e^{-S[\varphi]-\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,{\cal J}(x)\,{\cal O}(x)}\;.

(3.5)

{\cal O}
is an arbitrary single-trace operator of our choice,
{\cal J}(x)
the corresponding source and we assume the path integral is performed in the thermal vacuum. We can extend this standard setup to include generic multi-trace operators
F({\cal O})
(where
F
is, e.g., some monomial) by introducing additional sources
\lambda(x)
for the multi-trace operators as
6
6

6

We assume a suitable implicit large-
N
scaling of the multi-trace source that scales all terms in the action as
N^{2}
. In this sense, a single-trace source
{\cal J}(x)
scales like
N
, a double-trace like
N^{0}
, and so on and so forth.

Z[{\cal J},\lambda]=e^{-\widetilde{W}[{\cal J},\lambda]}=\int\mathcal{D}\varphi\,e^{-S[\varphi]-\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,({\cal J}(x){\cal O}(x)+\lambda(x)F({\cal O})(x))}\;.

(3.6)

We introduced the notation
\widetilde{W}
to distinguish the case with multi-trace sources from the one involving only single-trace sources. A functional derivative with respect to
\lambda(x)
and setting
\lambda=0
gives the thermal one-point function of the multi-trace operator
F({\cal O})
in the presence of the single-trace source
{\cal J}
,

\frac{\delta\widetilde{W}}{\delta\lambda(x)}\Big|_{\lambda\to 0}=\langle F({\cal O})(x)\rangle_{\beta,{\cal J}}\;.

(3.7)

Setting also
{\cal J}=0
gives the thermal one-point function of the multi-trace operator in the original undeformed theory.

In this paper, we are interested in the double-twist operators
[{\cal O}{\cal O}]_{s,J}\sim{\cal O}\,\Box^{s}\mathscr{D}_{J}{\cal O}

4
The OPE perspective

The previous section demonstrates a direct link between the thermal one-point functions of the double-twist operators and the thermal response function, and suggests that a proper derivation of this relation involves a point-splitting definition of the double-twist operators
[{\cal O}{\cal O}]_{s,J}\sim{\cal O}\,\Box^{s}\mathscr{D}_{J}{\cal O}

5
Regularization and evaluation

The explicit coefficients (
4.15
)-(
4.3
) (and their higher
(s,J)
generalizations) are written as differences between individually divergent quantities. In this section, we implement a concrete evaluation procedure for such expressions and use it to compute numerically the values of the thermal OPE coefficients

a_{0,0}
,
a_{1,0}
and
a_{0,2}
, comparing where possible with available results in the literature.

5.1
A window-subtraction scheme

The computation of the thermal coefficients
a_{s,J}
in (
4.10
)

\displaystyle a_{s,J}\,\mathcal{Q}_{J}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{J}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2\Delta_{\cal O}+2s+J}}{\mathcal{A}(s,J)}\times

(5.1)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[{\cal N}_{\Delta_{\cal O}}\,\frac{(-1)^{s+J/2}}{(J+1)}\,\frac{{\cal Q}_{J}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{J}}}{2\pi^{2}\beta}\sum_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\,{p_{n}}^{2s+J}\,C_{J}^{(1)}\!\Bigl(\frac{\omega_{n}}{{p_{n}}}\Bigr){\cal G}_{T}(n,k)-\mathcal{X}_{T}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{J}}\bigg]

involves the careful cancellation of ultraviolet divergences between the two terms on the RHS. To make the computation of these expressions more amenable to explicit numerical computation, we introduce the following scheme.

First, we need to identify the precise source of the ultraviolet divergence in the radial integrals

\mathcal{W}^{(s,J)}=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}\beta}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\,{p_{n}}^{2s+J}\,C_{J}^{(1)}\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\omega_{n}}{{p_{n}}}\Bigr)\,{\cal G}_{T}(n,k)\;,

(5.2)

where, as always,
{p_{n}}^{2}\coloneqq\omega_{n}^{2}+k^{2}
.
At large
k
, where
{\cal G}_{T}\to{\cal G}_{0}\sim k^{2\Delta_{\cal O}-4}
, the integrand grows as
k^{2\Delta_{\cal O}+2s+J-2}
capturing the leading source of the divergence. In general, there are additional subleading sources of divergence. Since the large-momentum expansion of
{\cal G}_{T}
is governed by the OPE, the corresponding asymptotics are fixed by the operators with power-law momentum tails. Crucially, every double-twist block has an exponentially small momentum tail, and the power-law growth comes entirely from the identity and the multi-stress tower (see Appendix
B.2
). For example, the large-momentum asymptotics of
{\cal G}_{T}
corresponding to the identity and the energy-momentum contributions are

{\cal G}_{T}(n,k)={\cal G}_{0}(p_{n})\left[1+16\,a^{(1)}_{2}\,\frac{\Gamma(\Delta_{\cal O})}{\Gamma(\Delta_{\cal O}-4)}\,\frac{3\omega_{n}^{2}-k^{2}}{\beta^{4}{p_{n}}^{6}}+\mathcal{O}({p_{n}}^{-8})\right]\;.

(5.3)

The constant
a^{(1)}_{2}
is the energy-momentum tensor thermal one-point coefficient. Since each multi-stress-tensor term lowers the large-
k
growth by an additional inverse power
k^{-4}
, the number of terms in the large-
p
expansion that contribute to the full divergence of
{\cal W}^{(s,J)}
(including the Matsubara sum, which leads effectively to a four-dimensional momentum integral) is captured by the first
M_{s,J}\coloneqq\lceil(2\Delta_{\cal O}+2s+J)/4\rceil
terms in the large-
p
expansion of
{\cal G}_{T}
. To be concrete, let us express the large-
p
divergence-generating part of
{\cal G}_{T}
as a finite sum of the form

{\cal G}^{(\rm div)}_{T}(n,k)=\sum_{m=0}^{M_{s,J}-1}{\cal G}_{T}^{(m)}(n,k)~.

(5.4)

The individual contributions
{\cal G}_{T}^{(m)}(n,k)
capture well the ultraviolet behavior of
{\cal G}_{T}
but are badly inappropriate in the infrared. Since they involve inverse powers of the momentum, inserting them in the momentum integrals
\sum_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk
can lead to infrared divergences. This problem can be mitigated by localizing each
{\cal G}_{T}^{(m)}(n,k)
within a window around the UV with a dressing function
w_{m}(\mu,p)
that approaches unity in the ultraviolet and an appropriate positive power in the infrared,

w_{m}(\mu,p)\xrightarrow{\;p\to\infty\;}1\;,\qquad w_{m}(\mu,p)\xrightarrow{\;p\to 0\;}(p/\mu)^{q}+\mathcal{O}((p/\mu)^{>q})\;.

(5.5)

The scale
\mu
is arbitrary and drops out at the end of the computation. An example of the type of dressing we will choose later is

w(\mu,p)=1-e^{-p^{2}/\mu^{2}}\;.

(5.6)

The corresponding UV-localized version of Eq. (
5.4
) is therefore

{\cal G}^{(\rm div,win)}_{T}(n,k)=\sum_{m=0}^{M_{s,J}-1}w_{m}(\mu,{p_{n}}){\cal G}_{T}^{(m)}(n,k)~.

(5.7)

Subtracting and adding back
{\cal G}^{(\rm div,win)}_{T}(n,k)
inside
{\cal W}^{(s,J)}
yields an equivalent expression for the double-twist coefficients in (
5.1
) of the form

\displaystyle a_{s,J}=\frac{\beta^{2\Delta_{\cal O}+2s+J}}{\mathcal{A}(s,J)}\times

\displaystyle\times\bigg\{{\cal N}_{\Delta_{\cal O}}\,\frac{(-1)^{s+J/2}}{(J+1)}\,\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}\beta}\sum_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\,{p_{n}}^{2s+J}\,C_{J}^{(1)}\!\Bigl(\frac{\omega_{n}}{{p_{n}}}\Bigr)\bigg({\cal G}_{T}(n,k)-\sum_{m=0}^{M_{s,J}-1}w_{m}{\cal G}_{T}^{(m)}(n,k)\bigg)

\displaystyle+{\cal N}_{\Delta_{\cal O}}\,\frac{(-1)^{s+J/2}}{(J+1)}\,\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}\beta}\sum_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}k\,k^{2}\,{p_{n}}^{2s+J}\,C_{J}^{(1)}\!\Bigl(\frac{\omega_{n}}{{p_{n}}}\Bigr)\sum_{m=0}^{M_{s,J}-1}w_{m}{\cal G}_{T}^{(m)}(n,k)-\mathcal{X}_{T}\bigg\}~,

(5.8)

where we defined
{\cal X}_{T}
by the
|x|\to 0
limit
{\cal X}_{T}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{J}}\to{\cal X}_{T}\mathcal{Q}_{J}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{J}}
.

Now the second line is a finite residual that can be computed efficiently numerically. The regulated cancellation of UV infinities is relegated to the third line with one important advantage: the first term involving the add-back functions
{\cal G}_{T}^{(m)}
can now be computed analytically, facilitating a more practical evaluation of the finite leftover.

We note that in this subtract-and-add-back scheme,
M_{s,J}
is the minimum number of terms that should be incorporated from the large-
{p_{n}}
asymptotics of
{\cal G}_{T}
. Higher-order terms can also be incorporated freely. They change the finite residual on the second line of (
5.1
) by a finite amount and typically improve the numerical accuracy of the computation.

We proceed to demonstrate explicitly how this scheme is implemented on the first few double-twist coefficients in the following subsections.

6
Outlook

In this work we have presented a method for extracting the thermal one-point
coefficients
a_{s,J}
of the double-twist operators
[{\cal O}{\cal O}]_{s,J}
that
appear in the scalar two-point function
\langle{\cal O}(x){\cal O}(0)\rangle_{\beta}

of a holographic CFT on
S^{1}_{\beta}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
at large central charge.
Our main result is a formula that expresses each
a_{s,J}
individually as a
regulated momentum-space integral of the thermal response function

{\cal G}_{T}(\omega_{n},k)
. We derived this in two independent ways. In the first, we deformed the boundary theory by the corresponding double-trace operator
and read off the thermal one-point coefficient by differentiating the resulting
one-loop generating functional in the deformation coupling. In the second, we defined the same operator by
point-splitting. We acted on the thermal two-point function with the corresponding
differential operator, isolated the target block in the coincidence limit and
matched the result to the momentum-space integral of the thermal response
function. The only theory-dependent input
was
{\cal G}_{T}
itself, which follows from a radial reduction of the bulk Klein–Gordon
equation. For the case of a probe scalar in the planar AdS
5

black brane (
d=4
, leading order in
1/N
), it was obtained by numerically solving the resulting radial
ODE.

By choosing a representative non-integer dimension
\Delta_{\cal O}=\tfrac{3}{2}
, we used this formula to
determine the three lightest thermal coefficients
a_{0,0}
,
a_{1,0}
and
a_{0,2}
.
The scalar datum
a_{0,0}
was given to 7 significant figures and was compatible with the previous holographic calculation of
[
32
]
, while

a_{1,0}
and
a_{0,2}
are, to our knowledge, the first determinations of these
individual coefficients. The significance of these numbers is
that they are spin-resolved data, inaccessible to the current bootstrap analysis. The KMS combination
a_{1,0}+3a_{0,2}
agrees with the
zero-spatial-separation thermal bootstrap result of
[
4
]
.

It would be useful to streamline the computation of higher double-twist thermal data. Reaching a heavier operator of dimension
2\Delta_{\cal O}+2s+J
requires subtracting the finite number of lighter multi-stress-tensor contributions below it (which can be obtained through
[
18
,
26
,
27
,
5
]
). At the same time, the regularization of the momentum integral
Eq.

5.2
becomes increasingly difficult: its UV divergence worsens with the dimension and
progressively more terms of the high-momentum asymptotic expansion of

{\cal G}_{T}
must be subtracted to render it finite, leaving a small residual of an increasingly delicate cancellation. Since
Eq.

B.19
involves factorially growing coefficients, the subtracted terms are individually large and the bulk response must be resolved to correspondingly higher accuracy. Thus, while the method is not limited in principle, the numerical cost grows steeply and the double precision used in our integration would need to be increased in practice to gain access beyond the leading double-twists.

More generally, one can explore various other related aspects of this work in holography. For example, our derivation of the master formula (
4.10
) was essentially field-theoretic, with the bulk entering only through the input of the thermal response function
{\cal G}_{T}
. It would be interesting to reformulate the derivation of the double-twist thermal one-point functions as a genuinely bulk computation. The one-loop generating
functional (
3.10
) already contains a functional
determinant that one could evaluate directly from the bulk. This is what the
double-trace and mixed-boundary-condition framework of

[
34
,
21
,
20
,
29
,
31
]
,
together with the functional-determinant technology developed for this purpose
in
[
22
,
12
]
, is designed to do. Applied to the AdS
5

black brane, such an approach would provide an alternative starting point for computing thermal double-twist data.

Another natural further direction would be to place the theory at finite spatial volume (on

S_{\beta}^{1}\times S^{d-1}
), replacing the planar black brane by the global
AdS
5
–Schwarzschild black hole. Our construction should carry over once the
continuous spatial-momentum integrals are traded for discrete sums over the
hyperspherical harmonics on
S^{d-1}
(the machinery of
Appendix
B.3
), now coupled to the radial problem in the global
geometry. This would give access to genuinely finite-volume thermal data, making direct contact with recent studies of thermal CFT on
S_{\beta}^{1}\times S^{d-1}

[
