Poster 126. An Investigation of Response Shift Bias and Coping Styles Following Hip Arthroscopy
Authors: Nicole Bryant, Dianne Bryant, Hana Marmura, Kevin Willits, Ryan Degen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) are commonly used to assess success following hip arthroscopy for femoroacetabular impingement (FAI). However, response shift, defined as changes in patients' internal standards or perceptions over time, may bias longitudinal evaluations of PROMs. This study aimed to investigate the presence of response shift one and two years following hip arthroscopy for FAI and to explore whether coping strategies or life events predict response shift or postoperative PROMs.Methods:A prospective cohort of 56 patients undergoing hip arthroscopy completed the International Hip Outcome Tool-12 (iHOT-12), Hip Outcome Score (HOS-ADL and HOS-Sport), and Brief-COPE questionnaires pre-operatively and at one- and two-years follow-up. At one- and two-year visits, patients were also asked to recall their health state prior to surgery and complete the iHOT-12, HOS-ADL, and HOS-Sport according to their recall, or how they remember feeling at that time. Response shift was assessed using the then-test method. We classified participants as either having a meaningful positive response shift, if the patient's recalled preoperative health was worse than initially reported, or having a meaningful negative response shift, if the patient's recalled preoperative health was better than initially reported. We used regression analyses to examine whether coping strategies, life events, adverse events, age, and lifestyle changes could predictor post-operative outcomes and the magnitude of response shift.Results:Statistically significant improvements were observed across all PROMs between pre-operative and one- and two-years (p < 0.001). Meaningful response shift was observed for the HOS-ADL (62%), HOS-Sport (51%) and iHOT (55%), with most participants demonstrating negative shifts, remembering their health as better than initially reported, for HOS-ADL (62%) and iHOT (70%), but positive shifts, remembering their health as worse than initially reported, for the HOS-Sport (53%). At one year, effect sizes were large when calculated with actual preoperative scores (Cohen's d = 1.49-1.55) but were reduced when calculated with recalled preoperative scores (d = 1.28-1.45), showing that response shift meaningfully diminished the perceived magnitude of improvement. At two years, the same pattern was observed: very large conventional effect sizes (d = 1.33-2.33) were attenuated when accounting for response shift (d = 1.28-1.76). Negative coping strategies and adverse events were significantly associated with worse post-operative PROM scores, though no consistent predictors of response shift magnitude were identified.Conclusions:A clinically important response shift was observed in most participants one and two years after hip arthroscopy. Psychological factors such as coping strategies shaped patient-reported outcomes, emphasizing the need to interpret PROMs with caution over time. Notably, effect sizes were consistently reduced when response shift was considered, showing that it meaningfully reduces the perceived magnitude of surgical benefit. These findings highlight the importance of accounting for response shift when interpreting PROMs in orthopaedic research.
