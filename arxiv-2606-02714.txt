Projected Energy Correlators: Two-Loop Jet Functions and NNLL Resummation Preprint: MITP-26-021 DESY-26-069 MIT-CTP 6028
Kyle Lee, Yibei Li, Zhen Xu, and Xiaoyuan Zhang

Abstract
We present the next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic (NNLL) collinear resummation of projected N N -point energy correlators (ENCs) up to N = 6 N=6 , matched to fixed-order predictions at NLO, in both electron-positron annihilation and Higgs decay to gluons. The key new ingredient is the two-loop jet function for N = 4 , 5 , 6 N=4,5,6 , which we compute semi-analytically using Integration-by-Parts and differential equations. We further include the leading non-perturbative corrections for ENCs, described by two universal soft matrix elements Ω ¯ 1 ​ q , Ω ¯ 1 ​ g {\overline{\Omega}_{1q},\overline{\Omega}_{1g}} of order Λ QCD \Lambda_{\rm QCD} , whose evolution is governed by anomalous dimensions for ( N − 1 ) (N-1) -point correlators. The matched distributions are compared with parton-shower simulations from Pythia8 and Herwig7 , and we study the sensitivity of both the absolute spectra and their ratios to the two-point energy correlator under variations of α s \alpha_{s} and Ω ¯ 1 ​ q , 1 ​ g \overline{\Omega}_{1q,1g} . Our results show that higher-point projected energy correlators are now under quantitative control at NNLL accuracy, opening the door to future α s \alpha_{s} extractions with complementary systematics.

Introduction

An important goal of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is to identify observables that are simultaneously sensitive to the underlying quark–gluon dynamics and under sufficient theoretical control to enable precision studies. Energy correlators are particularly attractive in this respect. As correlation functions of energy flow operators

More recently, the subject has entered a qualitatively new stage through the re-analysis of archival LEP data by the Electron-Positron Alliance. A particularly interesting regime of energy correlators is their collinear limit, in which all the energy flow operators have small relative angles. This is the natural domain of the light-ray operator product expansion (OPE)

2.1
Definitions

The projected
N
-point energy correlator is the correlation function of
N
energy flow operators, weighted by the largest pairwise angular separation between them. In the language of detectors placed on the celestial sphere at directions
\vec{n}_{i}
, with
x_{ij}\equiv(1-\vec{n}_{i}\cdot\vec{n}_{j})/2
, this reads

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}}{dx_{L}}\;=\;\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}d\Omega_{\vec{n}_{i}}\,\delta\!\left(x_{L}-\max_{1\leq i<j\leq N}x_{ij}\right)\frac{1}{Q^{N}}\langle 0|\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}\,\mathcal{E}(\vec{n}_{1})\cdots\mathcal{E}(\vec{n}_{N})\,\mathcal{O}|0\rangle\,,

(1)

where
\mathcal{E}(\vec{n})=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{2}n^{i}\,T_{0i}(t,r\vec{n})
is the energy flow operator
Sveshnikov:1995vi
;
Tkachov:1995kk
;
Hofman:2008ar
,
\mathcal{O}
is the source operator for the process under consideration (e.g. the electromagnetic current for
e^{+}e^{-}\to q\bar{q}
or the effective
Hgg
vertex for
H\to gg
), and
Q
is the total hard scale. For
e^{+}e^{-}
this would be the center-of-mass energy scale, whereas for Higgs decay this would be the mass of the decaying Higgs
m_{H}
. The
1/Q^{N}
normalization makes the distribution dimensionless and ensures the sum rule
\int_{0}^{1}dx_{L}\,d\sigma^{[N]}/dx_{L}=\sigma_{\rm tot}
, the total cross-section (or the Higgs to gluon pair decay width).

For an
m
-particle final state
|X_{m}\rangle
, the energy flow operator acts as
\mathcal{E}(\vec{n})|X_{m}\rangle=\sum_{i\in X_{m}}E_{i}\,\delta^{(2)}(\vec{n}-\vec{n}_{i})|X_{m}\rangle
. Inserting this into Eq. (
1
) gives the equivalent particle-level definition in perturbation theory,

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}}{dx_{L}}\;=\;\sum_{m}\int d\sigma_{X_{m}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{N}\leq m}\frac{\prod_{a=1}^{N}E_{i_{a}}}{Q^{N}}\,\delta\!\left(x_{L}-\max\{x_{i_{1}i_{2}},\,x_{i_{1}i_{3}},\,\dots,\,x_{i_{N-1}i_{N}}\}\right)\,,

(2)

where
x_{ij}=(1-\vec{n}_{i}\cdot\vec{n}_{j})/2
is the angle between two particles
i
and
j
. Here,
d\sigma_{X_{m}}
is the differential cross-section of producing
m
final-state particles, in both
e^{+}e^{-}
collision and
H
decays. The indices
i_{a}
run independently over all final-state particles and need not be distinct. For convenience in computation, we can decompose different terms in
\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{N}\leq m}\prod_{a=1}^{N}E_{i_{a}}
by different configurations.

The configuration in which all
N
indices land on the same particle (
i_{1}=\cdots=i_{N}
) has every pairwise angle equal to zero and contributes to a contact term at
x_{L}=0
. In general, we write the weight associated with the
N
indices landing on an
r
-particles as
\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{r}
. Such term will be accompanied by the delta function that measures the largest angle amongst
\binom{r}{2}
pairwise angles. This allows us to rewrite the projected
N
-point correlator as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}}{dx_{L}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\int d\sigma_{X_{m}}\,\Bigg[\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{1}(i)\,\delta(x_{L})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{2}(i,j)\,\delta(x_{L}-x_{ij})

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\sum_{1\leq i<j<k\leq m}\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{3}(i,j,k)\,\delta\!\left(x_{L}-\max\{x_{ij},x_{ik},x_{jk}\}\right)+\,\cdots\Bigg]\,,

(3)

where the ellipsis denotes terms with four or more distinct measured particles. The weights
\mathcal{W}_{r}^{[N]}
are precisely the integer-
N
specialization of the recursive weights introduced in Ref.
Chen:2020vvp
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{1}(i)=

\displaystyle\frac{E_{i}^{N}}{Q^{N}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{2}(i,j)=

\displaystyle\frac{(E_{i}+E_{j})^{N}-E_{i}^{N}-E_{j}^{N}}{Q^{N}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{3}(i,j,k)=

\displaystyle\frac{(E_{i}+E_{j}+E_{k})^{N}-(E_{i}+E_{j})^{N}-(E_{i}+E_{k})^{N}-(E_{j}+E_{k})^{N}+E_{i}^{N}+E_{j}^{N}+E_{k}^{N}}{Q^{N}}\,,

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{M}(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{M})=\frac{\left(\sum_{a=1}^{M}E_{i_{a}}\right)^{N}}{Q^{N}}-\sum_{1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\ldots<a_{M-1}\leq M}\mathcal{W}_{M-1}^{[N]}\left(i_{a_{1}},i_{a_{2}},\ldots,i_{a_{M-1}}\right)

\displaystyle-\ldots-\sum_{1\leq a\leq M}^{M}\mathcal{W}_{1}^{[N]}\left(i_{a}\right)\,.

(4)

Note that
\mathcal{W}_{r}^{[N]}=0
when
r>N
. As pointed out in Refs.
Chen:2020vvp
;
Budhraja:2024tev

\text{Re}(\nu)>0
as well.
For a final state with
m

r=1,2,\dots
at fixed
m
reconstructs the binomial expansion
(\sum_{i}E_{i})^{N}/Q^{N}=1
, which is the origin of the sum rule mentioned above.

Fixed-order results

\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\frac{d\sigma^{[N],\rm LO}}{dx_{L}}\;\xrightarrow{x_{L}\to 0}\;\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\,\frac{c^{[N]}_{1}}{x_{L}}\,+\,(\text{less singular})\,,

(20)

i.e. a single
1/x_{L}
enhancement times a constant coefficient
c^{[N]}_{1}
. Careful regulation would also promote this into a plus distribution
[1/x_{L}]_{+}
. Once integrated up to a cumulant, this gives a single logarithm of
x_{L}
, consistent with the
\mathcal{L}^{j}(x_{L})=[\ln^{j}(x_{L})/x_{L}]_{+}
plus-distribution structure of the singular expansion discussed in Sec.
3
that requires resummation.

For the EEC (
N=2
), the NLO QCD calculation of
d\sigma/dx_{L}
has been carried out analytically in both
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation
Dixon:2018qgp
and hadronic Higgs decay
Luo:2019nig
;
Gao:2020vyx
, and the NNLO numerical prediction for
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation is computed in
Colorfulnnlo

Somogyi:2006da
;
Somogyi:2006db
;
Aglietti:2008fe
. For the higher-point projected correlators
N\geq 3
, no analytic NLO calculation is currently available. Instead, we obtain numerical NLO results for
e^{+}e^{-}\to q\bar{q}
from the dipole-subtraction program
Event2

Catani:1996jh
;
Catani:1996vz
, which provides the highest fixed order to which our resummed predictions can be matched in this channel. For
H\to gg
, we use
Eerad3

Gehrmann-DeRidder:2014hxk
;
Aveleira:2025svg
to calculate the NLO distributions numerically.

3
Collinear factorization of projected energy correlators

The fixed-order results, both analytic (LO) and numerical (NLO from
Event2
and
Eerad3
), are singular in the collinear limit as already pointed out in Eq. (
20
).
At
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{L})
, this singularity in the projected ENC is captured by a tower of plus distributions
[\ln^{j}(x_{L})/x_{L}]_{+}
with
0\leq j\leq L-1
, corresponding to a single-logarithmic series in the differential distribution. Explicitly, we observe

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}}{dx_{L}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-1}^{L-1}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\right)^{L}e_{L,j}\mathcal{L}^{j}(x_{L})+\ldots\,,\,

\displaystyle\text{where}\qquad\mathcal{L}^{-1}(x_{L})=\delta(x_{L}),\,\qquad\mathcal{L}^{j}(x_{L})=\left[\ln^{j}(x_{L})/x_{L}\right]_{+}\,,

(21)

where the ellipsis denotes terms that are less singular as
x_{L}\to 0
.
These collinear logarithms become large when
\alpha_{s}\ln(1/x_{L})\sim 1
,
and must be resummed to all orders in
\alpha_{s}
.
The natural framework for this resummation is provided by collinear factorization, to which we now turn.

It is convenient to formulate the resummation at the level of the cumulant,

\displaystyle\Sigma^{[N]}\left(x_{L},\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\int_{0}^{x_{L}}dx_{L}^{\prime}\,\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}}{dx_{L}^{\prime}}\left(x_{L}^{\prime},\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\,,

(22)

since the cumulant maps the plus distributions into ordinary logarithms and makes the logarithmic counting transparent. In particular,
[\ln^{j}(x_{L})/x_{L}]_{+}
integrates to
\ln^{j+1}x_{L}
and
\mathrm{N}^{k}\mathrm{LL}
resummation accuracy corresponds to resumming the logarithmic tower
\alpha_{s}^{n}\ln^{m}x_{L}
up to
n\geq m\geq n-k
.

At leading power, the cumulant factorizes into a convolution of a process-dependent hard function and a universal jet function,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{ee,H}^{[N]}\left(x_{L},\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{N}\vec{J}^{[N]}\left(\ln\frac{x_{L}x^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\cdot\vec{H}_{ee,H}\left(x,\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\,,

(23)

where the subscript
ee
refers to
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation and
H
refers to the Higgs decay process
H\to gg
. The factor
x^{N}
reflects the energy weighting carried by the projected
N
-point correlator.
The hard function
\vec{H}_{ee,H}
describes the production of a parent parton with energy fraction
x
and can be identified with that of a
semi-inclusive fragmentation function
Rijken:1996vr
;
Rijken:1996ns
;
Rijken:1996npa
;
Mitov:2006wy
;
Mitov:2006ic
;
Moch:2007tx
;
Almasy:2011eq
, while the jet function
\vec{J}^{[N]}
captures the subsequent collinear dynamics probed by the measurement. Both quantities are two-component vectors in quark–gluon flavor space. The important point for the present work is that the process dependence is entirely encoded in the hard function, whereas the dependence on the projected
N
-point measurement resides in the jet function.

The renormalization group (RG) equation of the hard function is timelike DGLAP evolution,

\frac{d\vec{H}(x,\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}})}{d\ln\mu^{2}}=-\int_{x}^{1}\!\frac{dy}{y}\widehat{P}(y)\cdot\vec{H}\left(\frac{x}{y},\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\,,

(24)

where
\widehat{P}(y)
is the singlet timelike splitting matrix,
now known in full through three loops
Chen:2020uvt
;
Almasy:2011eq

and partially at four-loop
Gehrmann:2023cqm
;
Falcioni:2023luc
;
Falcioni:2023vqq
;
Falcioni:2024xyt
;
Falcioni:2024qpd
;
Falcioni:2025hfz
.
Requiring the factorization formula in Eq. (
23
) to be RG invariant then fixes the evolution of the jet function. One finds a modified timelike DGLAP equation,

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{J}^{[N]}(\ln\frac{x_{L}Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}})}{d\ln\mu^{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}dy\,y^{N}\vec{J}^{[N]}\left(\ln\frac{x_{L}y^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\cdot\widehat{P}(y)\,.

(25)

Compared with the evolution of the hard function, the jet equation carries an additional factor of
y^{N}
and a shifted logarithmic argument. These terms encode how the projected observable transforms under a collinear splitting. The factorization theorem above holds for arbitrary
N
at leading power, and thus we only need to compute the jet boundary constants for
N
-point correlators.

The solutions to these RGEs will evolve the hard (jet) function from its typical scale
\mu_{h}
(
\mu_{j}
) to the common scale
\mu
. We can formally write the solutions as

\displaystyle\vec{H}\left(x,\mu,\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=U^{H}(\mu,\mu_{h},x)\otimes\vec{H}\left(x,\mu_{h},\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu_{h}^{2}}\right)\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\vec{J}^{[N]}\left(\mu,\ln\frac{x_{L}x^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=U^{J}(\mu,\mu_{j},x)\otimes\vec{J}^{[N]}\left(\mu_{j},\ln\frac{x_{L}x^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}}\right)\,,

(27)

with
U^{H}(\mu,\mu_{h},x)
and
U^{J}(\mu,\mu_{j},x)
the RG evolution kernels.
Since the physical observable, i.e. the LHS of Eq. (
23
), should not depend on the factorization scale
\mu
, we can choose its value arbitrarily. We set
\mu=\mu_{h}
for convenience, and the resummed cumulant becomes

\Sigma^{[N]}(x_{L})=\int_{0}^{1}dxx^{N}\left[U^{J}(\mu_{h},\mu_{j},x)\otimes\vec{J}^{[N]}\left(\mu_{j},\ln\frac{x^{2}x_{L}Q^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}}\right)\right]\vec{H}\left(x,\mu_{h},\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu_{h}^{2}}\right)\,.

(28)

Note that when convolving
\vec{H}\left(x,\mu_{h},\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu_{h}^{2}}\right)
with
\vec{J}^{[N]}\left(\mu_{j},\ln\frac{x_{L}x^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}}\right)
, we should truncate the product to some order in
\alpha_{s}(\mu_{h})\sim\alpha_{s}(\mu_{j})\sim\lambda
, such that equal scale
\mu_{h}=\mu_{j}
will give precisely the singular expansion in the collinear limit of the fixed-order calculations we match to.

We use the expanded solution to the jet function RGE in this work. Similarly to Refs.
Dixon:2019uzg
;
Chen:2023zlx
, we write an ansatz

\vec{J}^{[N]}=\underbrace{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{s}^{i}(\mu)L^{i}\vec{c}^{\,[N]}_{i,i}}_{\text{LL}}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{s}^{i}(\mu)L^{i-1}\vec{c}^{\,[N]}_{i,i-1}}_{\text{NLL}}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{s}^{i}(\mu)L^{i-2}\vec{c}^{\,[N]}_{i,i-2}}_{\text{NNLL}}+\cdots\,,

(29)

with
L\equiv\ln(x_{L}Q^{2}/\mu^{2})
and
c^{[N]}_{i,j}
unknown constants. Note that we always normalize the jet function to have
\vec{c}^{[N]}_{0,0}=\{2^{-N},2^{-N}\}
, which is natural due to
1/Q^{N}
normalization in Eq. (
2
). Combining with the
\beta
-RGE,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{\mathrm{d}\ln\mu}=\beta(\alpha_{s}(\mu)),\quad\beta(\alpha)=-2\alpha\,\left[\left(\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\right)\beta_{0}+\left(\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\beta_{1}+\left(\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\right)^{3}\beta_{2}+\cdots\right]\,,

(30)

we can form linear equations at each order in
\alpha_{s}
and
L
, and determine the
c^{[N]}_{i,j}
coefficients. In practice, we need to truncate the sum to some order
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{k})
in Eq. (
29
).
We find that
k=10\sim 15
gives more than sufficient convergence in the perturbative regime at
Q\sim 100\,\mathrm{GeV}
, which is the scale we consider.
The expressions for
c_{i,j}
depend on the jet constants and anomalous dimensions, defined as

\gamma_{T}(\nu)\equiv-\int_{0}^{1}dy\,y^{\nu}\hat{P}(y)=\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)\gamma_{T}^{(0)}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\gamma_{T}^{(1)}+\cdots\,,

(31)

using the timelike splitting kernel
\hat{P}(y)
. We have summarized the values of these anomalous dimensions in App.
B
.

\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{N}\,\ln^{M}(x^{2})\,\vec{H}\left(x,\mu_{h},\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu_{h}^{2}}\right)=\sum_{L}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{h})}{4\pi}\right)^{L}\vec{h}_{L}(N,M)\,,

4
Calculation of the two-loop jet functions

Given the factorization in Eq. (
23
) and the jet ansatz in
Eq. (
29
), the only missing ingredient for NNLL resummation is the
two-loop boundary constant
c_{2,0}^{[N]}
of the jet function. The
N=2,3
cases were
obtained in Refs.
Dixon:2019uzg
;
Chen:2023zlx
; here we extend the calculation
to
N=4,5,6
. Following Ref.
Chen:2023zlx
, we extract
c_{2,0}^{[N]}
by matching
the singular
x_{L}\to 0
limit of the fixed-order projected correlator at

\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
onto Eq. (
23
), using
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation
for quark jets and the corresponding
H\to gg
channels in the Higgs effective field theory for
gluon jets. We decompose the calculation into different measurement sectors
(Sec.
4.1
), and then organize the calculations into contact terms (Sec.
4.2
), and
three-particle terms (Sec.
4.3
). Finally we collect the results in
Sec.
4.4
.

4.1
Measurement decomposition

Recall from Eq. (
2.1
) and Eq. (
2.1
), we write the definition of projected
N
-point correlator in perturbation theory in terms of different measurement sectors
\mathcal{W}_{r}^{[N]}
. To NLO
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
, we can have at most four final-state particles. For
N\leq 3
, we will at most measure three particles; starting from
N=4
, we can have detectors on all four particles. However, momentum conservation forbids all final-state particles from sitting inside an arbitrarily small cone, which places a lower bound on
x_{L}
whenever an energy flow operator is placed on every produced particle. Such configurations therefore do not contribute in the
x_{L}\to 0
limit.
Following this spirit, since NLO has up to four particles in the final state, we only need to consider
\mathcal{W}_{r}^{[N]}
with
r=1,2,3
at NLO for the collinear limit.

Following the definition, each term in
\mathcal{W}_{r}^{[N]}
takes the form

\displaystyle\sim\frac{E_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}E_{i_{2}}^{a_{2}}\cdots E_{i_{r}}^{a_{r}}}{Q^{N}}\,,\qquad a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{r}=N\,,

(33)

i.e. a monomial of total degree
N
in the parton energies. For the calculation it is
useful to organize
\mathcal{W}_{r}^{[N]}
further by grouping together monomials that share
the same multiset of exponents. Writing

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}^{[N]}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}^{(2)}}\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}^{[N]}(i,j)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{3}^{[N]}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}^{(3)}}\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}^{[N]}(i,j,k)\,,

(34)

where
\Lambda_{N}^{(r)}
denotes the set of unordered partitions of
N
into
r

positive parts. For each partition
\lambda=\{a,b\}\in\Lambda_{N}^{(2)}
or

\lambda=\{a,b,c\}\in\Lambda_{N}^{(3)}
, the symmetrized monomial is

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{\{a,b,c\}}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{N!}{a!\,b!\,c!}\sum_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Perm}(a,b,c)}\frac{E_{i}^{a^{\prime}}E_{j}^{b^{\prime}}E_{k}^{c^{\prime}}}{Q^{N}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{\{a,b\}}(i,j)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{N!}{a!\,b!}\sum_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Perm}(a,b)}\frac{E_{i}^{a^{\prime}}E_{j}^{b^{\prime}}}{Q^{N}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{\{N\}}(i)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{E_{i}^{N}}{Q^{N}}\,,

(35)

where
\mathrm{Perm}(\cdots)
denotes the distinct permutations of the multiset of
exponents, and the multinomial prefactor counts the number of ways to distribute
the
N
detector insertions over the chosen particles. For the observables considered
here, the relevant partitions with
N\leq 6
are

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(3)}_{3}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{1,1,1\}\bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(2)}_{3}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{2,1\}\bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(3)}_{4}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{2,1,1\}\bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(2)}_{4}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{3,1\},\{2,2\}\bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(3)}_{5}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{3,1,1\},\{2,2,1\}\bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(2)}_{5}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{4,1\},\{3,2\}\bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(3)}_{6}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{4,1,1\},\{3,2,1\},\{2,2,2\}\bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(2)}_{6}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\{5,1\},\{4,2\},\{3,3\}\bigr\}\,.

(36)

Specializing Eq. (
2.1
) to the two-loop singular cross section, the decomposition is most transparent in the
N=4
case:

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{[4]}}{dx_{L}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j<k\leq 4}\int\mathrm{d}\mathrm{LIPS}_{4}\,|\mathcal{M}_{4}|^{2}\,\mathcal{W}_{3}^{[4]}(i,j,k)\,\delta\left(x_{L}-\max\{x_{ij},x_{ik},x_{jk}\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m\in\{3,4\}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\int\mathrm{d}\mathrm{LIPS}_{m}\,|\mathcal{M}_{m}|^{2}\,\mathcal{W}_{2}^{[4]}(i,j)\,\delta\left(x_{L}-x_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m\in\{2,3,4\}}\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\int\mathrm{d}\mathrm{LIPS}_{m}\,|\mathcal{M}_{m}|^{2}\,\mathcal{W}_{1}^{[4]}(i)\,\delta(x_{L})\,,

(37)

with
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{LIPS}_{m}
the Lorentz-invariant phase space for
m
final-state particles and
|\mathcal{M}_{m}|^{2}
the corresponding squared matrix elements.
The first line involves only the double-real
m=4
contribution. Notice that we dropped configurations in which detectors are placed on every final-state particle, e.g.
we do not consider
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{[N]}
on three-particle final states or
\mathcal{W}_{2}^{[N]}
on two-particle final states, as they do not contribute to the
x_{L}\to 0
limit.
The measurement weights are explicitly given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{3}^{[4]}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{[4]}_{\{2,1,1\}}(i,j,k)=\frac{12}{Q^{4}}\left(E_{i}^{2}E_{j}E_{k}+E_{i}E_{j}^{2}E_{k}+E_{i}E_{j}E_{k}^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}^{[4]}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{[4]}_{\{3,1\}}(i,j)+\mathcal{W}^{[4]}_{\{2,2\}}(i,j)=\frac{4}{Q^{4}}\left(E_{i}^{3}E_{j}+E_{i}E_{j}^{3}\right)+\frac{6}{Q^{4}}E_{i}^{2}E_{j}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}^{[4]}(i)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{\{4\}}^{[4]}(i)=\frac{E_{i}^{4}}{Q^{4}}\,.

(38)

The same notation keeps the higher-point cases compact. For
N=5
one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{3}^{[5]}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{[5]}_{\{3,1,1\}}(i,j,k)+\mathcal{W}^{[5]}_{\{2,2,1\}}(i,j,k)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[5]}_{\{3,1,1\}}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{20}{Q^{5}}\left(E_{i}^{3}E_{j}E_{k}+E_{i}E_{j}^{3}E_{k}+E_{i}E_{j}E_{k}^{3}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[5]}_{\{2,2,1\}}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{30}{Q^{5}}\left(E_{i}^{2}E_{j}^{2}E_{k}+E_{i}^{2}E_{j}E_{k}^{2}+E_{i}E_{j}^{2}E_{k}^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}^{[5]}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{[5]}_{\{4,1\}}(i,j)+\mathcal{W}^{[5]}_{\{3,2\}}(i,j)\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{Q^{5}}\left(E_{i}^{4}E_{j}+E_{i}E_{j}^{4}\right)+\frac{10}{Q^{5}}\left(E_{i}^{3}E_{j}^{2}+E_{i}^{2}E_{j}^{3}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}^{[5]}(i)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{\{5\}}^{[5]}(i)=\frac{E_{i}^{5}}{Q^{5}}\,,

(39)

and for
N=6
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{3}^{[6]}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{[6]}_{\{4,1,1\}}(i,j,k)+\mathcal{W}^{[6]}_{\{3,2,1\}}(i,j,k)+\mathcal{W}^{[6]}_{\{2,2,2\}}(i,j,k)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[6]}_{\{4,1,1\}}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{30}{Q^{6}}\left(E_{i}^{4}E_{j}E_{k}+E_{i}E_{j}^{4}E_{k}+E_{i}E_{j}E_{k}^{4}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[6]}_{\{3,2,1\}}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{60}{Q^{6}}\sum_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Perm}(3,2,1)}E_{i}^{a^{\prime}}E_{j}^{b^{\prime}}E_{k}^{c^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{[6]}_{\{2,2,2\}}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{90}{Q^{6}}E_{i}^{2}E_{j}^{2}E_{k}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}^{[6]}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{[6]}_{\{5,1\}}(i,j)+\mathcal{W}^{[6]}_{\{4,2\}}(i,j)+\mathcal{W}^{[6]}_{\{3,3\}}(i,j)\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{Q^{6}}\left(E_{i}^{5}E_{j}+E_{i}E_{j}^{5}\right)+\frac{15}{Q^{6}}\left(E_{i}^{4}E_{j}^{2}+E_{i}^{2}E_{j}^{4}\right)+\frac{20}{Q^{6}}E_{i}^{3}E_{j}^{3}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}^{[6]}(i)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{\{6\}}^{[6]}(i)=\frac{E_{i}^{6}}{Q^{6}}\,.

(40)

This decomposition is the direct two-loop truncation of the exact observable definition in Ref.
Chen:2020vvp
. The three-particle terms are the only pieces with support on the projected three-angle measurement involving multiple nontrivial angles,
\delta[x_{L}-\max\{x_{ij},x_{ik},x_{jk}\}]
. All other remaining pieces collapse either to a single pairwise angle
\delta(x_{L}-x_{ij})
or to
\delta(x_{L})
itself. Below we will discuss how to compute these two parts separately.

4.2
Contact terms

In this subsection, we describe the analytic calculations of the contact terms. As described above, the contact terms contain both one-particle partition and two-particle partition
\Lambda_{N}^{(2)}
. The one-particle partition gives the self-correlation contribution, which has support only at
x_{L}=0
. In the two-particle partition, all
N
detector insertions are carried by only two resolved final-state particles, so only depends on a single pairwise angle. Therefore, the contact term share the same type of integrals as ordinary EEC in Refs.
Dixon:2018qgp
;
Luo:2019nig
, with the EEC weight
2E_{i}E_{j}/Q^{2}
replaced by the polynomial weight
\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{\lambda}(i,j)\propto E_{i}^{a}E_{j}^{b}+E_{i}^{b}E_{j}^{a}
. Explicitly, we will consider the following integrals

\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{2}}{dx_{L}}=\sum_{m\in\{3,4\}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\int\mathrm{d}\mathrm{LIPS}_{m}\,|\mathcal{M}_{m}|^{2}\,\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{2}(i,j)\,\delta(x_{L}-x_{ij})\,,

(41)

and

\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{1}}{dx_{L}}=\sum_{m\in\{2,3,4\}}\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\int\mathrm{d}\mathrm{LIPS}_{m}\,|\mathcal{M}_{m}|^{2}\,\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{\{N\}}(i)\,\delta(x_{L})\,.

(42)

The pairwise terms in Eq. (
41
) receive contributions from
m=3
and
m=4
final states, corresponding to real-virtual and double-real corrections. The self-correlation term in Eq. (
42
) also contains the
m=2
double-virtual contribution, but is otherwise simpler because it never involves a nontrivial angular measurement.
We will denote the sum of these two integral as

\frac{d\sigma^{[N],\mathrm{2\mbox{-}loop}}_{C}}{dx_{L}}=\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{2}}{dx_{L}}+\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{1}}{dx_{L}}\,.

(43)

In order to extract both quark and gluon jet functions, we consider the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation and Higgs decay into gluons up to NLO, which involves the following subprocesses:

\displaystyle{\bf e^{+}e^{-}\textbf{annihilation }}\qquad\qquad

Higgs decays

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}\rightarrow q\bar{q}+VV\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle H\rightarrow gg+VV

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}\rightarrow q\bar{q}g+V\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle H\rightarrow ggg+V

\displaystyle H\rightarrow q\bar{q}g+V

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}\rightarrow q\bar{q}gg\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle H\rightarrow gggg

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}\rightarrow q\bar{q}q\bar{q}\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle H\rightarrow q\bar{q}gg

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}\rightarrow q\bar{q}q^{\prime}\bar{q}^{\prime}\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle H\rightarrow q\bar{q}q\bar{q}

\displaystyle H\rightarrow q\bar{q}q^{\prime}\bar{q}^{\prime}

(44)

where
V
and
VV

The phase-space integrals in Eq. (

\displaystyle\delta\left(x_{L}-\frac{1-\cos\theta_{ij}}{2}\right)=\frac{p_{i}\!\cdot p_{j}}{x_{L}}\,\delta\!\left[2x_{L}(p_{i}\!\cdot Q)(p_{j}\!\cdot Q)-p_{i}\!\cdot p_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{p_{i}\!\cdot p_{j}}{x_{L}}\left\{\frac{1}{\left[2x_{L}(p_{i}\!\cdot Q)(p_{j}\!\cdot Q)-p_{i}\!\cdot p_{j}\right]-i0}\,-\,\frac{1}{\left[2x_{L}(p_{i}\!\cdot Q)(p_{j}\!\cdot Q)-p_{i}\!\cdot p_{j}\right]+i0}\right\}\,.

(46)

We then perform the so-called topology classification procedure: classify these integrals into families by their denominator structure after loop-momentum relabeling and shifts, and external momenta exchanging. In this representation the dependence on the observable is isolated in the numerator weight
\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}^{[N]}(i,j)
and in the single nonlinear propagator above.

We use
LiteRed

;

to generate the standard Integration-by-Part (IBP) equations. Since the additional measurement propagator is nonlinear in the loop momenta, the family is first initialized without it; the nonlinear denominator is then inserted directly into the propagator list and scalar-product replacement rules before generating the IBP system. In addition to the standard IBP relations, one needs the identity

\left(2x_{L}\,p_{i}\!\cdot Q\,p_{j}\!\cdot Q-p_{i}\!\cdot p_{j}\right)\left[\delta\!\left(\mathcal{K}_{ij}(x_{L})\right)\right]^{k}=\left[\delta\!\left(\mathcal{K}_{ij}(x_{L})\right)\right]^{k-1}\,,

(47)

\delta\!\left(\mathcal{K}_{ij}(x_{L})\right)\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\frac{1}{2x_{L}\,p_{i}\!\cdot Q\,p_{j}\!\cdot Q-p_{i}\!\cdot p_{j}-i0}\,-\,\frac{1}{2x_{L}\,p_{i}\!\cdot Q\,p_{j}\!\cdot Q-p_{i}\!\cdot p_{j}+i0}\right)\,,

(48)

which lowers the power of the measurement propagator and closes the reduction. The resulting system is exported to
FIRE6

Smirnov:2019qkx
, which performs the Laporta reduction after the cut propagators are specified. The self-correlation sector
\lambda=\{N\}

and therefore involves no nonlinear propagator; it reduces to inclusive cut integrals with polynomial energy numerators.
After reduction, the differential cross-section can be written as

\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{2}}{dx_{L}}=\sum_{k}\mathcal{C}_{k}^{[N]}(x_{L},\epsilon)\,\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{k}(x_{L},\epsilon)\,,

(49)

where the coefficient functions
\mathcal{C}_{k}^{[N]}
are rational in
x_{L}
and
\epsilon
, and the nontrivial analytic dependence is contained in the master integrals
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{k}
. We derive differential equations for the master integrals by differentiating with respect to
x_{L}
and reducing the resulting integrals back to the master basis. The differential systems are transformed to canonical form with
Canonica

Meyer:2017joq
or
Libra

;

\epsilon

Lastly, we also need to calculate the
\delta(x_{L})
part for the jet function. In this case, we make use the

singular limit. By performing the asymptotic expansion at the level of the differential equation, we find that the leading power takes the following form

x_{L}^{-1-\epsilon}A_{1}(\epsilon)+x_{L}^{-1-2\epsilon}A_{2}(\epsilon)\,,

(50)

with unknown
\epsilon

and
A_{2}(\epsilon)
. Then we can match this formula to the analytic expression in the bulk

x_{L}
. This amounts to giving the
\epsilon

. Then we can use the definition of plus distribution to obtain the

endpoint contribution at the level of differential cross-section,

x_{L}^{-1-a\epsilon}=-\frac{\delta(x_{L})}{a\epsilon}+\left[\frac{1}{x_{L}}\right]_{+}-a\epsilon\left[\frac{\ln x_{L}}{x_{L}}\right]_{+}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\,.

(51)

This avoids explicitly calculating the asymptotic expansion of individual master integral. Putting everything together, we obtain the analytic solution for the contact terms.

4.3
Three-particle terms

We now turn to the genuinely new part of the higher-point calculation, namely the three-particle sector. For
N=4,5,6
this corresponds to the partitions in
\Lambda_{N}^{(3)}
, and at two loops it is generated entirely by the double-real contribution.
Since we are only interested in extracting the jet functions, we can take the triple-collinear limit. Under this limit, the tree-level four-parton matrix element factorizes into the Born hard process times the
1\to 3
timelike splitting kernel, so this sector can be computed starting from the fully differential triple-collinear distribution.

For a given partition
\lambda=\{a,b,c\}\in\Lambda_{N}^{(3)}
we define the measurement operator

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{[N]}_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\equiv\sum_{i,j,k}\mathcal{W}^{[N]}_{\lambda}(i,j,k)\,\delta(x_{1}-x_{jk})\,\delta(x_{2}-x_{ik})\,\delta(x_{3}-x_{ij})\,,

(52)

where the sum runs over all ordered triples of distinct final-state particles. The corresponding three-particle term that contributes to the jet function is

J^{[N]}_{\mathrm{3p},\lambda}=\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{c}^{(3)}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}e^{\gamma_{E}}}{4\pi}\right)^{2\epsilon}\frac{4g^{4}}{s_{123}^{2}}\sum_{i,j,k}P_{ijk}\,\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{[N]}_{\lambda}\,,

(53)

with
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{c}^{(3)}
the standard triple-collinear phase space and
P_{ijk}
the
1\to 3
splitting function
Campbell:1997hg
;
Catani:1998nv
;
Ritzmann:2014mka
. The full three-particle term is obtained by summing Eq. (
53
) over
\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}^{(3)}
:
J^{[N],\mathrm{2\mbox{-}loop}}_{\mathrm{3p}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}^{(3)}}J^{[N],\mathrm{2\mbox{-}loop}}_{\mathrm{3p},\lambda}
.

As in the E3C case, it is convenient to parametrize the three measured angles by

x_{1}=x_{L}z\bar{z}\,,\qquad x_{2}=x_{L}(1-z)(1-\bar{z})\,,\qquad x_{3}=x_{L}\,,

(54)

so that the projected measurement is implemented by fixing the largest angle to be
x_{3}=x_{L}
. For each partition
\lambda
, the differential integrand takes the form of

\frac{dJ^{[N],\mathrm{2\mbox{-}loop}}_{\mathrm{3p},\lambda}}{dx_{L}\,d\text{Re}(z)\,d\text{Im}(z)}=\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q^{2}}\right)^{2\epsilon}\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}}{\pi^{3}}\frac{e^{2\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\frac{1}{x_{L}^{1+2\epsilon}}\frac{1}{(2\text{Im}z)^{2\epsilon}}\\
\times\Bigl[G_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})+\epsilon F_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})+\epsilon^{2}H_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\Bigr]\,.

(55)

The dependence on
x_{L}
is universal due to the triple collinear limit, so after integrating over
z
we only need the coefficient of Eq. (
55
) through
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
to obtain the finite
\delta(x_{L})
term via Eq. (
51
).

The integration region is identical to the E3C case. Using the
S_{3}
symmetry of the three-parton configuration space, the full
z
plane can be reduced to the fundamental domain

\mathcal{S}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,\bigg|\,0\leq\text{Im}(z)\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{2}\leq\text{Re}(z)\leq\sqrt{1-\text{Im}^{2}(z)}\right\},

(56)

and the total three-particle contribution becomes

\frac{dJ^{[N]}_{\mathrm{3p}}}{dx_{L}}=\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q^{2}}\right)^{2\epsilon}\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}}{\pi^{3}}\frac{e^{2\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\frac{6}{x_{L}^{1+2\epsilon}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}^{(3)}}A_{\lambda}(\epsilon)\,,

(57)

with

A_{\lambda}(\epsilon)\equiv\int_{\mathcal{S}}\frac{d\text{Re}(z)\,d\text{Im}(z)}{(2\text{Im}z)^{2\epsilon}}\Bigl[G_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})+\epsilon F_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})+\epsilon^{2}H_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\Bigr]\,.

(58)

The difficulty is that the integrand develops squeezed singularities when two partons become much closer to each other than to the third one. In the
z
parametrization, these singular regions sit at
z\to 1
and at its
S_{3}
images. To avoid the divergence, we construct the local subtraction term by expanding the
G_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z}),F_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z}),H_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})
in the squeeze
z\to 1
limit. Note that in the complex plane, there is path dependence when approaching the squeeze limit. To capture that, we introduce
1-z=r\exp(i\theta),1-\bar{z}=r\exp(-i\theta)
, take
r\to 0
for generic
\theta
, and then convert it back to
z,\bar{z}
.
For
G_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})
, the
N=3
result is computed in Ref.
Chen:2019bpb
and the higher points are bootstrapped recently in Ref.
Gong:2025jqi
. We can perform the

expansion directly. For
F_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})
and
H_{\lambda}(z,\bar{z})
, in principle, we can also compute them analytically; however, it is more convenient to perform the expansion in the middle of phase space integrations. Notice that a naive expansion in the splitting function will lead to the wrong result since it does not commute with the integration, so instead, we perform the first two energy integrals in
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{c}^{(3)}
in Eq. (
53
).
Eventually, for each partition
\lambda
we denote them as

G_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}(z,\bar{z})+\epsilon F_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}(z,\bar{z})+\epsilon^{2}H_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}(z,\bar{z})\,,

(59)

and rewrite Eq. (
58
)

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\frac{d\text{Re}(z)\,d\text{Im}(z)}{(2\text{Im}z)^{2\epsilon}}\Bigl[G_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}+\epsilon F_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}+\epsilon^{2}H_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathcal{S}}\frac{d\text{Re}(z)\,d\text{Im}(z)}{(2\text{Im}z)^{2\epsilon}}\Bigl[\bigl(G_{\lambda}-G_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}\bigr)+\epsilon\bigl(F_{\lambda}-F_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sq}}\bigr)\Bigr]\,+\,\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\,.

(60)

The first integral is evaluated analytically in
d=4-2\epsilon
dimensions. The second one is finite and can therefore be expanded in
\epsilon
before integration. As mentioned above, we do not have the analytic form for
G_{\lambda}
and
F_{\lambda}
for higher points, and thus we also need to evaluate the phase space integrals numerically. In practice, we implement the second term in

5
NNLL collinear resummation

With the two-loop jet constants determined in Sec.
4
, all ingredients required for NNLL resummation up to six-point projected energy correlators are now available. In this section we present the resummation for both
e^{+}e^{-}\to q\bar{q}
and
H\to gg
with matching to available fixed-order results. In addition, we also include the leading non-perturbative corrections proportional to
\overline{\Omega}_{1q,1g}
.

5.1
Matching procedure and nonperturbative power corrections

To obtain precise predictions from NNLL resummation, we need to determine the resummation scales and estimate uncertainties from missing higher orders. To eliminate the large logarithms associated with the hard and jet function boundaries, we choose the canonical scales

\mu_{h}=e_{h}Q\,,\qquad\mu_{j}=e_{h}e_{j}Q\sqrt{x_{L}}\,,

(64)

with
(e_{h},e_{j})=(1,1)
for the central values. Here,
e_{h}\in[1/2,2]
and
e_{j}\in[1/2,2]
control the hard scale and the jet scale variations, respectively. In this work, we choose the envelope of the following seven-point scale variations:

(e_{h},e_{j})\in\left\{(1,1),\,(2,1),\,\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right),\,(1,2),\,\left(1,\frac{1}{2}\right),\,(2,2),\,\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\}\,.

(65)

In principle, we will also need to consider a profile function to transition
from non-perturbative scales
\gtrsim\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
in very small
x_{L}

to canonical scales in Eq. (
64
).
This in particular requires a prescription in the canonical scale
to avoid
\mu_{j}
being too small and going out of the perturbative regime. Here, we will not implement such transition and our perturbative curves should not be trusted in the non-perturbative region. The onset of where the transition occurs will be discussed below and will depend actually on the process and choice of
N
-point projected energy correlators.

We also need to include the non-singular contribution from the fixed-order calculation,

\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm tot}}\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm match}}{dx_{L}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm tot}}\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm resum}}{dx_{L}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm tot}}\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm ns}}{dx_{L}}\,,

(66)

where
\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm ns}}{dx_{L}}
becomes increasingly important as one approaches the bulk angular region of the distribution. It is defined as

d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm ns}\equiv d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm FO}-d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm sing}\,,

(67)

with
d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm sing}
the singular expansion of the resummed distribution through the order in
\alpha_{s}
to which we match. Standard logarithmic counting will require matching N
k
LL to N
k-1
LO,
i.e.
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{k})
.
In principle, the transition from the resummation region to the fixed-order region should also include turning off the resummation via profile functions
Abbate:2010xh
. In practice, we find the naive matching in Eq. (
67
) sufficient, since the resummation effects already taper off in the region where the non-singular contribution becomes important. Note that we have been using the hat symbol in
\hat{\sigma}
to denote purely perturbative predictions. The full prediction requires including nonperturbative power corrections:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm tot}}\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}}{dx_{L}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm tot}}\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm match}}{dx_{L}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm tot}}\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{\rm match,NP}}{dx_{L}}\,,

(68)

where
d\sigma^{[N]}_{\rm match,NP}
is the additive leading-power NP corrections which we now discuss.

The leading non-perturbative power corrections
Lee:2024esz
;
Chen:2024nyc
for projected energy correlators are given by the representation-dependent soft matrix element

\Omega_{1\kappa}\equiv\frac{1}{N_{\kappa}}\langle 0|\mathrm{tr}\!\left[\overline{Y}_{\bar{n}}^{\dagger\kappa}Y_{n}^{\dagger\kappa}\mathcal{E}_{T}(0)Y_{n}^{\kappa}\overline{Y}_{\bar{n}}^{\kappa}\right]|0\rangle\,,\qquad\kappa=q,g\,,

(69)

with
N_{q}=N_{c}
and
N_{g}=N_{c}^{2}-1
.
In the fixed-order region, the distribution with leading non-perturbative power correction
in the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme takes the form

\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{\rm FO}}{dx_{L}}=\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{[N]}_{\rm FO}}{dx_{L}}+\sigma_{0}\,\frac{N}{2^{N}}\frac{\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}}{Q\,[x_{L}(1-x_{L})]^{3/2}}\,,

(70)

which exhibits enhancement as we approach the kinematic endpoints
x_{L}\to 0,1
. For
e^{+}e^{-}
and
H\to gg
processes, the soft matrix elements are in
\kappa=q,g
representation at LO, respectively.
For the collinear resummation, the leading non-perturbative power correction is realized
in the jet boundary

2^{N}J^{\kappa[N]}\!\left(\ln\frac{x_{L}x^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}},\mu_{j}\right)=2^{N}\hat{J}^{\kappa[N]}\!\left(\ln\frac{x_{L}x^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}},\mu_{j}\right)-\frac{N\,\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}}{xQ\sqrt{x_{L}}}\,2^{N-1}\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{\kappa[N-1]}\!\left(\ln\frac{x_{L}x^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}},\mu_{j}\right)\,,

(71)

where the prefactor
2^{N}
follows from our jet-function normalization convention. The important point is that the power-correction term is proportional to
the perturbative coefficient
\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{\kappa[N-1]}
, whose logarithmic evolution
is the same as that of the
(N-1)
-point jet function, up to different boundary constants. Therefore, the RG of the jet function associated with nonperturbative power corrections can also be solved using the ansatz given in Eq. (
29
). At LL accuracy, only the leading matching coefficient
\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}
is required. Although Eq. (
71
) appears as a product of
\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}
and

\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{\kappa[N-1]}
,
\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}
should be treated as the zeroth-order
jet constant in the RG evolution, i.e.
\vec{c}_{0,0}^{[N-1]}
in Eq. (
29
).
Throughout this paper, we keep the
\overline{\rm MS}
form in Eq. (
71
)
and do not perform renormalon subtraction
Schindler:2023cww
;
Lee:2024esz
.

The leading singular expansion of the resummed non-perturbative corrections is simply given by

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2^{N}}\frac{\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}}{Q\,x_{L}^{3/2}}\,.

(72)

Therefore, the corresponding non-singular contribution of the nonperturbative corrections is given as

\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{\rm ns,NP}}{dx_{L}}\equiv\sigma_{0}\,\frac{N}{2^{N}}\frac{\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}}{Q}\left[\frac{1}{[x_{L}(1-x_{L})]^{3/2}}-\frac{1}{x_{L}^{3/2}}\right]\,.

(73)

Combining with the collinear resummation for the nonperturbative corrections, the matched contribution is given as

\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm tot}}\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{\rm match,NP}}{dx_{L}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm tot}}\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{\rm resum,NP}}{dx_{L}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm tot}}\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}_{\rm ns,NP}}{dx_{L}}\,.

(74)

Finally, as an observable, projected ENC obeys the sum rule

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx_{L}\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}}{dx_{L}}=\sigma_{\rm tot}\,.

(75)

This sum rule makes it natural to normalize projected energy correlators by its total cross-section or total decay width.

5.3
Ratio of projected energy correlators

Ratios of projected energy correlators provide a useful way to isolate the anomalous scaling. As discussed in the introduction, the anomalous scaling of the
N
-point projected correlator is governed by twist-two spin-
(N+1)
operators.
Ignoring the mixing of different partonic channels and the running of QCD coupling, the ENCs in the perturbative collinear limit obey

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}}{dx_{L}}\sim x_{L}^{-1+\gamma(N+1)}\,,

(80)

where
\gamma(N+1)
denotes the anomalous dimension of the twist-two spin-
(N+1)
operator. Forming the ratio of the
N
-point correlator to the EEC thus cancels the classical
x_{L}^{-1}
scaling and leaves the anomalous scaling

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{[N]}/dx_{L}}{d\sigma^{[2]}/dx_{L}}\sim x_{L}^{\gamma(N+1)-\gamma(3)}\,.

(81)

The slope of the log-log plot of the ratio is therefore set by a difference of anomalous dimensions, and is consequently sensitive to
\alpha_{s}
. This naive expectation is of course modified by the running coupling and by non-perturbative corrections, but the schematic picture illustrates why ratios are useful observables. Note also that
\gamma(N+1)
grows monotonically with
N

Nachtmann:1973mr
, so the slope of the ratio is expected to steepen as
N
increases.

In Fig.
6
and Fig.
7
, we show the perturbative convergence of the ratios for both
e^{+}e^{-}
and
H\to gg
up to
N=6
, without and with the leading non-perturbative power corrections respectively. We use the canonical seven-point scale variations of Eq. (
65
) for both numerator and denominator, treating the two as fully correlated. Since both inherit their anomalous scaling from twist-two operators, differing only in spin and accessed through different moments of the same QCD splitting functions, this correlated variation is a natural starting point. We emphasize, however, that for ratio observables a quantitative treatment of the correlation between numerator and denominator uncertainties is particularly important and deserves careful study
Tackmann:2024kci
. We find good perturbative convergence in both figures, especially once non-perturbative corrections are included and most clearly in the
e^{+}e^{-}
channel. Ref.
Lee:2024esz
showed that non-perturbative corrections reduce the slope of the ratio, consistent with the
N
-scaling of Eq. (
70
); we observe the same trend here in both
e^{+}e^{-}
and
H\to gg
. The same reference also showed that omitting these corrections at
Q=1000
GeV can shift the extracted
\alpha_{s}
by about
10\%
. In Sec.
6
we examine in detail the sensitivity of the ratio to
\alpha_{s}
and
\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}

In Fig.

6
Sensitivity to the strong coupling and non-perturbative corrections

In this section, we discuss the potential phenomenological applications of projected
N
-point energy correlators to carry out precision studies. In particular, we want to study their sensitivity to extract the strong coupling constant
\alpha_{s}
and the non-perturbative soft matrix elements
\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}
. As closely related observables, the family of projected energy correlators for different
N
provides an ideal set to validate the extractions among different
N
-point observables and to study theory correlations and uncertainties. Being precise about the correlations between different
N
-point projected correlators is beyond the scope of this work and deserves further investigation; here we will carry out a more naive sensitivity study of the observables to
\alpha_{s}
and
\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}
with uncertainties and matching prescriptions described in Sec.
5
. We study the sensitivity to
\alpha_{s}
in Sec.
6.1
, to
\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}
as a whole in Sec.
6.2
, and to the less-constrained gluon parameter
\overline{\Omega}_{1g}
in particular in Sec.
6.3
.

\alpha_{s}
extraction

To illustrate the sensitivity of energy correlators to different
\alpha_{s}(m_{Z})
values, we define the logarithmic slope of both the absolute spectrum and the ratio of higher

-point projected energy correlators to

(82)

where both of them are functions of

10

11

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we extend the NNLL collinear resummation of projected energy correlators to four-, five-, and six-point correlators. To accomplish this precision, we have computed the two-loop jet function for projected
N
-point correlators with
N=4,5,6
, for both quark and gluon jets. We extracted these boundary constants by matching the singular
x_{L}\to 0
limit of the fixed-order distributions onto the collinear factorization theorem in Eq. (
23
). The calculation was organized using the measurement decomposition of Eqs. (
2.1
)–(
2.1
), which separates the two-loop problem into contact terms and resolved three-particle terms. The contact terms were calculated using IBP reductions and differential equations, while the remaining three-particle contributions were obtained via Monte Carlo integration. We verified our calculation against the known
N=2,3
results in the literature and the
x_{L}\to 0
behavior from numerical programs.

With these ingredients, we obtained NNLL+NLO predictions for
N
-point projected correlators up to
N=6
in both
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation and
H\to gg
decay. In the perturbative collinear region, the matched distributions show relatively stable order-by-order behavior from NLL to NNLL. At sufficiently small
x_{L}
, the perturbative expansion becomes sensitive to the leading hadronization correction, whose parametric size is governed by
N\,\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}/(Q\sqrt{x_{L}})
.
This explains why the onset of non-perturbative effects moves to larger
x_{L}
as
N
increases. We incorporated these leading non-perturbative power corrections in the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme through the
(N-1)
-point evolution formula of Eq. (
71
), controlled by the two hadronic parameters
\overline{\Omega}_{1q}
and
\overline{\Omega}_{1g}
.
We also compared the matched predictions with parton-shower simulations from
Pythia8
and
Herwig7
. In
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation, including the leading non-perturbative correction improves the qualitative agreement with the shower results in the small-angle region. In
H\to gg
decay, and especially for higher-point correlators, we observe sizeable differences both between our predictions and the shower results and between the two showers themselves. These likely reflect the absence of precision
H\to gg
data against which
Pythia8
and
Herwig7
could be tuned.

Beyond the absolute spectra, we studied ratios of projected correlators to the EEC. These ratios largely cancel the common classical
1/x_{L}
behavior and isolate the anomalous scaling associated with twist-two operators of different spin. Their logarithmic slopes are therefore directly sensitive to the timelike anomalous dimensions and to the value of
\alpha_{s}
. We find that the sensitivity to
\alpha_{s}
increases with
N
, consistent with the growth of the relevant anomalous dimensions, and is generally larger in the gluon channel than in the quark channel. The ratios are also less sensitive to the leading non-perturbative power correction than the absolute spectra, since the
N
-dependent prefactor of
\overline{\Omega}_{1\kappa}
in Eq. (
70
) partially cancels between numerator and denominator. This partial cancellation sharpens the
\alpha_{s}
sensitivity of the ratio relative to the absolute spectrum, and motivates the ratio as a particularly promising observable for future
\alpha_{s}
determination.

The framework developed here extends beyond
e^{+}e^{-}
processes and color-singlet decays, and can be applied to projected energy correlators measured inside small-
R
jets
Kang:2016mcy
;
Lee:2024icn
;
Generet:2025vth
at hadron colliders or in events with a high-
p_{T}
recoil against a color-singlet boson such as a
Z
or a photon. Indeed, the three-point projected energy correlator has already been measured inside jets by the CMS collaboration
CMS:2024mlf
to extract
\alpha_{s}
. A re-analysis of archival LEP data along the lines of Refs.
Bossi:2025xsi
;
Electron-PositronAlliance:2025fhk
;
Zhang:2025nlf
would also be especially valuable, enabling precision
\alpha_{s}
extractions from a closely related family of observables and helping to disentangle the systematics that drive the longstanding tension between event-shape and lattice determinations of
\alpha_{s}
. Looking even further ahead, future Higgs factories such as FCC-ee and CEPC
FCC:2018evy
;
CEPCStudyGroup:2018ghi
will provide the first sample in which the
H\to gg
predictions presented here can be confronted with precision data, and in which
\overline{\Omega}_{1g}
can plausibly be extracted directly.

On the theoretical side, several extensions are also possible. Promoting the NNLL predictions for higher-point projected correlators to track-based observables using the track-function formalism
Chang:2013rca
;
Li:2021zcf
;
Chen:2022muj
;
Jaarsma:2023ell
;
Lee:2023npz
;
Lee:2023tkr
would make them directly comparable to high-precision measurements on tracks. It would also be interesting to extend the analytic continuation of the projected
\nu
-point correlator to two loops; the one-loop continuation in
\nu
was carried out in Ref.
Chen:2020vvp
, and the two-loop jet constants computed here at integer
N=4,5,6
may provide enough data to constrain or even bootstrap an analytic solution at general
\nu
.
