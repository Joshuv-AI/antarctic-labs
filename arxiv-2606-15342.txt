Mixed Local-Nonlocal Parabolic Equations in Heisenberg Group : Harnack Inequality with an Optimal Tail
Debraj Kar

Abstract
. We prove Harnack inequality with an optimal tail for linear mixed local-nonlocal parabolic equation on the Heisenberg group. For this purpose, we make use of the expansion of positivity method and a clustering lemma, thereby bypassing Moser iteration and logarithmic estimates. Keywords: Mixed local nonlocal, Harnack inequality, Heisenberg group, Parabolic equation. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35D30, 35B65, 35M30, 35R03, 35R11.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the regularity theory, specifically the Harnack estimate, for the following mixed local-nonlocal parabolic equation on the Heisenberg group

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u+\mbox{ p.v. }\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{{u}({\xi},t)-{u}({\eta},t)}{|{\eta}^{-1}\circ{\xi}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+2s}}d\eta dt=0,

on
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T]
where
\Omega
is a bounded open subset in the Heisenberg Group
\mathbb{H}^{N}
,
T>0
and
p.v. denotes the Cauchy principal value.

History

Context

The parabolic mixed local nonlocal equation arises in the stochastic process that involves superposition of classical random walk and a Lev́y flight. This problem has gained attention due to its extensive applications in phenomena, such as, in plasma physics
[
BdCN13
]
and in biology
[
DLV22
]
.

Harnack inequality is a cornerstone in the field of regularity theory. In 1964, Moser
[
Mos64
]
proved the Harnack inequality for the linear parabolic equation and Hölder continuity, as its consequence. Later, this result was extended to the quasilinear counterpart by Trudinger
[
Tru68
]
. For the
p
-Laplace parabolic problem, DiBenedetto
[
DB88
]
proved intrinsic Harnack inequality for non-negative solutions. Still later, DiBenedetto, Gianazza, and Vespri
[
DBGV08
]
gave a new approach using the
expansion of positivity
and an exponential change of variable to establish Harnack Inequality. In the setting of measure metric spaces, Kinnunen and co-authors
[
KMMJP12
]
have shown Harnack inequality for Parabolic De Giorgi-class of functions.

Next, we provide a brief overview of the nonlocal case. For the elliptic nonlocal p-Laplace type equations, one can see the work of Di Castro, Kuusi, and Palatucci
[
DCKP14
]
. In parabolic contexts, we start with the paper of Kassmann and Schwab
[
KS13
]
where authors prove the Harnack result for
p=2
. This is followed by a series of papers such as those of Felsinger, Kassmann
[
FK13
]
, Prasad
[
Pra23
]
, Nakamura
[
Nak23b
]
, Ciani and Nakamura
[
CN25
]
, Adimuthi, Prasad, and Tewary
[
APT25
]
, Kar
[
Kar26
]
where various regularity estimates for nonlocal parabolic equations are established. The regularity theory for the nonlocal heat equation culminates in the paper of Kassmann and Weidner
[
KW24
]
where Harnack inequality with an optimal tail is proved.

Due to their interesting structure and applications, mixed local and non-local type of problems have attained importance and purely analytic methods have been developed as opposed to their probabilistic origin. In their paper
[
GK22
]
, Garain and Kinnunen studied the Harnack inequality using
tail
estimate and expansion of positivity. Foondum
[
Foo09
]
proves the Harnack inequality using probabilistic method for the mixed local nonlocal elliptic equations. Moreover, for boundary Harnack result, see
[
CKSV12
]
. Also, of note is the paper of Nakamura
[
Nak23a
]
fox mixed local-nonlocal doubly nonlinear equations. Also one can look into papers of Shang and Zhang
[
SZ23
]
for the Harnack result when
p>2
and Bhowmick et al
[
BGKL26
]
for the Harnack estimate of nonlinear mixed fractional superposition operator with nonlocal superposition tail.

Novelty

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first ever result where the Harnack inequality for the mixed local nonlocal parabolic equation on the Heisenberg Group has been studied taking into account an optimal tail. This type of Harnack result with optimal tail condition is inspired from the result of Kassmann and Weidner
[
KW24
]
where the nonlocal heat equation was considered in a Euclidean setup. Unlike their paper, we rely on the De Giorgi method and expansion of positivity to deduce our result. Also, as compared to the paper of Ciani and Nakamura
[
CN25
]
, where authors prove Harnack type result for nonlocal parabolic De Giorgi class of functions under
L^{p-1+\varepsilon}
- tail condition, where
\varepsilon>0
and
p>1
, our result is optimal as far as tail is concerned. Additionally, Nakamura
[
Nak23b
]
proves their results with additional restriction over
s
whereas our result is free from such restriction. It becomes possible due to the structure of the equation (
1.1
). Moreover, one can find a clustering lemma in the Heisenberg group in this paper (
Lemma

6.1
).

\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega):=\Bigg\{v\in L^{p}(\Omega):\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|{y}^{-1}\circ{x}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+sp}}\,dx\,dy<\infty\Bigg\}.

•

We introduce the parabolic solution space

•

The

, denoted by

L^{m}(I;L^{m}_{\gamma}(\Omega)):=\Bigg\{v\in L^{m}(I;L^{m}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega)):\iint_{I\times\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|v(x,t)|^{m}}{1+|x|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+\gamma}}\,dx\,dt<\infty\Bigg\},

for some

•

Nonlocal parabolic tail :
For
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{H}^{N}\times I

\textup{ Tail }(v;z_{0},r,\tau):=\Bigg(\fint_{t_{0}-\tau}^{t_{0}}r^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(y,t)|}{|{y}^{-1}\circ{x_{0}}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+2s}}\,dy\,dt\Bigg).

Note that if
v\in L^{1}(I;L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))
then it holds that
\textup{Tail }(v;z_{0},r,\tau)<\infty
. Throughout the paper, we mainly use the following time dependent tail on a parabolic cylinder as follows,

\textup{Tail}(v(t);r,x_{0}):=r^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(y,t)|}{|{y}^{-1}\circ{x_{0}}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+2s}}\,dy\,dt.

•

Weak Solution:
A function
u\in\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T]\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\displaystyle u\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(0,T;L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))\cap L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))\cap C_{\textup{loc}}([0,T];L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))

and for every test function

\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{\textup{loc}}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathcal{K}))\cap L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathcal{K})),

following holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{K}}u\,\varphi\,d\eta\Biggr|_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}-\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\int_{\mathcal{K}}u\partial_{t}\varphi\,d\eta\,dt+\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\varphi\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{H}^{N}\times\mathbb{H}^{N}}(u(\xi,t)-u(\eta,t))(\varphi(\xi,t)-\varphi(\eta,t))\,K(\xi,\eta,t)d\xi\,d\eta\,dt\leq(\geq)\,0.

Main Result

Our first result is local boundedness result proved via De Giorgi method. This is the refined version of its counterpart in

Theorem 1.1
.

(Local Boundedness Result)
Suppose
z_{0}=(\xi_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega_{T}
and
R>0
. Let
u\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(0,T;L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))\cap L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))\cap C_{\textup{loc}}([0,T];L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))
be a weak subsolution of the problem (
1.1
). Then there exist some constant
C=C(\textup{{data}}),\,C_{1}=C_{1}(Q)
and
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}(\textup{{data}},\sigma)
for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{z_{0}+\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma R}^{\ominus}}u(\xi,t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1-\sigma)^{C_{1}}}\left[\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{z_{0}+\mathcal{Q}_{R}^{\ominus}}u_{+}^{2}\,d\xi dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\fint_{t_{0}-R^{2}}^{t_{0}}\left(\fint_{B_{R}(\xi_{0})}u_{+}(\eta,t)\,d\eta\right)^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+1\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\mathfrak{C}R^{-2}\int_{t_{0}-R^{2}}^{t_{0}}\textup{Tail}(u_{+}(t);R,\xi_{0})dt.

As far as the Harnack estimate is concerned, the basic difference between elliptic and parabolic problem is that in the elliptic Harnack inequality, the supremum of the positive solution is controlled by the infimum of the same solution over the same ball whereas in the parabolic case, a
waiting time
or
delay time
or
time lag
is needed. In a counterexample, Moser
[
Mos64
]
also proves the necessity of this time gap for caloric functions. There is also a time lag in our result. In contrast, recently Liao and Weidner
[
LW25
]
proves time insensitive Harnack for parabolic nonlocal equation where sup and inf are on overlapping cylinders.

Heisenberg Group

The results in this subsection is based on the book
[
BLU07
]
. The Heisenberg group is a nilpotent Lie Group. This is the set
\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
equipped with the following group operation

\xi\circ\xi^{\prime}=(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},\Upsilon+\Upsilon^{\prime}+2\left(\big<x^{\prime},y\big>-\big<x,y^{\prime}\big>\right),

where

\xi=(z,\Upsilon)=(x,y,\Upsilon)=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{N},y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{N},\Upsilon)

and

\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},\Upsilon^{\prime})=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},\Upsilon^{\prime})=(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},...,x^{\prime}_{N},y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2},...,y^{\prime}_{N},\Upsilon^{\prime}).

A one parameter group of automorphism on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
is defined as

\Phi_{\lambda}(x,y,\Upsilon)=(\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda^{2}\Upsilon),

so that it has a
homogeneous dimension
of
Q=2N+2
. Also a
homogeneous norm

d_{0}
such that
d_{0}:\mathbb{H}^{N}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is a function satisfying

•

d_{0}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\xi))=\lambda d_{0}(\xi)
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
and
\lambda>0
.

•

d_{0}(\xi)=0
if and only if
\xi=0
.

Define standard
homogeneous norm
on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
by

|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}=(|z|^{4}+|\Upsilon|^{2})^{1/4}.

It is well known that all homogeneous norm on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
are equivalent (see
[
BLU07
, Corollary 5.1.5]
). Additionally, Heisenberg group with any homogeneous norm
d_{0}
satisfies a pseudo-triangle inequality as in the following lemma.

Lemma 1.5
.

[
BLU07
, Proposition 5.1.7]

Consider Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{N}
with homogeneous norm
d_{0}
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
, following holds

•

d_{0}(\xi\circ\eta)\geq\frac{1}{c}d_{0}(\xi)-d_{0}(\eta^{-1}),

•

d_{0}(\xi\circ\eta)\leq c(d_{0}(\xi)+d_{0}(\eta)),

•

d_{0}(\xi\circ\eta)\geq\frac{1}{c}d_{0}(\xi)-cd_{0}(\eta).

Remark 1.6
.

In the case of standard homogeneous norm, the constant
c
above is replaced by
1
. In that case we may assume that
\mathbb{H}^{N}
is a metric space equipped with the metric
|\cdot^{-1}\circ\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}
(for details see
[
BFS18
, Example 5.1]
). Moreover, this metric is symmetric i.e.
|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}=|\eta^{-1}\circ\xi|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}
for any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{N}.

Also note that the
Haar measure
on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
is the same as the
Lebesgue measure
on
\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
and satisfies the following the
doubling measure
property, that is for any
R>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
,

|B_{2R}(x_{0})|\leq C(Q)|B_{R}(x_{0})|

This properties make
(\mathbb{H}^{N},|\cdot^{-1}\circ\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}},\mathfrak{L}^{2N+1})
into a
Q
-regular measure metric space with a doubling measure (for more, see
[
HaK00
, Section 11.3]
and
[
HKST15
, Proposition 14.2.9]
).

Some Technical Results

Regularization in time is one of the important idea in the regularity theory of parabolic PDE. This idea is taken from the paper
[
BLS21
]
. Let us consider nonnegative, smooth even function
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with compact support in
(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma=1
. Then for
\phi\in L^{1}((a,b))
and
0<\varepsilon<\min\{b-t,t-a\}
, set the convolution as follows

(2.1)

\displaystyle\phi^{\varepsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{t-\varepsilon/2}^{t+\varepsilon/2}\gamma\Big(\frac{t-\tau}{\varepsilon}\Big)\phi(\tau)d\tau=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-\varepsilon/2}^{\varepsilon/2}\gamma\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon}\Big)\phi(t-\alpha)d\alpha,\;\;\;\;\;t\in(a,b).

We now state some technical lemmas regarding this regularization in time.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
KT26
, Lemma 2.2]

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{N}
be a open set. Consider
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{T_{2}-T_{2}}{2}
and
q\geq 1
. If
f\in C([T_{1},T_{2}];L^{q}(\Omega))
, then for every
t\in(T_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},T_{2}-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2})
, the sequence
\phi^{\epsilon}(\cdot,t)
converges to
\phi(0,t)
in
L^{q}(\Omega)
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
KT26
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{T_{1}-T_{2}}{2}
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. Consider
\phi\in L^{q}\left(T_{1},T_{2};L^{p}(\Omega)\right)
. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(p,q)
such that for any
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\|\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}\left(T_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},T_{2}-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2};L^{p}(\Omega)\right)}\leq C.

Next, we are going to state the lemma concerning the geometric convergence of sequence of numbers.

Lemma 2.4
.

[

Let consider

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}\leq Mb^{j}\left(Y_{j}^{1+\delta}+Z_{j}^{1+\kappa}Y_{j}^{\delta}\right),\,\,\textup{ and }\,\,Z_{j+1}\leq Mb^{j}\left(Y_{j}+Z_{j}^{1+\kappa}\right).

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}Y_{j}=0=\lim_{j\rightarrow 0}Z_{j}.

The following lemma gives a relation between the measure of the three level sets of function. This prevents a function from jumping abruptly between two levels without having a significant intermediate region. This lemma is known as
De Giorgi Isoperimetric Lemma
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k<l
be real numbers and
u\in W^{1,1}(B_{\rho}(\xi))
. Then there exists a constant
\gamma=\gamma(Q,p)
, independent of

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{Q+2s}\frac{\lambda\omega}{\sigma^{Q+2s}}|B_{\rho}|\left\{\int_{0}^{\delta\rho^{2}}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{\rho}}\frac{w_{+}(\eta,t)}{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+2s}}\,d\eta dt+\int_{0}^{\delta\rho^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{w_{+}(\eta,t)}{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+2s}}\,d\eta dt\right\}

Weak Harnack Inequality I

We start this subsection with a lemma, known as
Clustering Lemma
, is a quantitative version of the Severini-Egorov Theorem
[
Sev10
,
Ego11
]
. For Sobolev case, this theorem is discussed in
[
DGV06
]
and for fractional case, see
[
DIV25
]
. The interesting part of this lemma is that unlike to the Euclidean space, we can not have the covering of cubes due to the geometrical structure of Heisenberg group. For this purpose, we relies on the covering result
[
Lu94
, Lemma 3.2]
and steps in
[
KMMJP12
, Lemma 2.5]
,
[
Nak23b
, Lemma 5.1]
. This lemma is used to refine the result in Lemma
5.4
to the power like dependence result in Proposition
6.2
. The important feature of this lemma is that it helps to prove
\delta
independent Weak Harnack Inequality (Weak Harnack Inequality I).

Note that, we will consider
W^{1,2}(B_{\rho}(\xi_{0}))
instead of
W^{s,2}(B_{\rho}(\xi_{0}))
as the former one can be embedded into the later one (see Lemma
2.8
).

Lemma 6.1
.

(Clustering Lemma)
Let,
\rho_{0}>\rho>0
with
s\in(0,1)
. Consider
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
and
u\in W^{1,2}(B_{\rho}(\xi_{0}))
satisfies

(C-I)

\displaystyle\left|\left\{u(.)>c\right\}\cap B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})\right|>\alpha|B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})|,

and

(C-II)

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{1,2}(B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})}\leq B,

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
c,B>0
. Then, for every
\delta,\lambda\in(0,1)
there exists
\xi_{*}\in B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})
and a parameter
k\equiv k(Q,s,\alpha,B,\delta,\lambda)\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\left|\left\{u(.)>\lambda c\right\}\cap B_{k\rho}(\xi_{*})\right|>(1-\delta)|B_{k\rho}(\xi_{*})|.

Proof.

Let,
0<k<\frac{\rho_{0}-\rho}{2m\rho}
, for some constant
m>1
. Let
\left\{B_{k\rho}(\xi)\right\}_{x\in B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})}
be a covering of
B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})
, then there exists disjoints sequence
\left\{B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})\right\}_{\xi_{i}\in B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})}
(see
[
MS79
]
), for all
i\in I
, such that

(6.1)

\displaystyle B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})\subset\cup_{i\in I}B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{0})\subset B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0}).

It is obvious that
B_{2mk\rho}(\xi_{i})\subset B_{R}(\xi_{0})
. Moreover, the ball
B_{2mk\rho}(\xi_{i})
have bounded overlap with bound independent of
k
, due to the measure doubling property. Next, let us define

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D^{+}:=\left\{i\in I:\left|\{u>c\}\cap B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{0})\right|>\frac{\alpha}{2\mathcal{C}}|B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{0})|\right\},\\
D^{-}:=\left\{i\in I:\left|\{u>c\}\cap B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{0})\right|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2\mathcal{C}}|B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{0})|\right\}.\end{cases}

By assumption, we get

\displaystyle\alpha|B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\{u(.)>c\right\}\cap B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in D^{+}}\left|\left\{u(.)>c\right\}\cap B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{0})\right|+\sum_{i\in D^{-}}\left|\left\{u(.)>c\right\}\cap B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\sum_{i\in D^{+}}\left|\left\{u(.)>c\right\}\cap B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{0})\right|}_{A}+\frac{\alpha}{2\mathcal{C}}\sum_{i\in D^{-}}\left|B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq A+\frac{\alpha}{2}\sum_{i\in D^{-}}\left|B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq A+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left|B_{(1+k)\rho}(\xi_{0})\right|.

The last inequality follows by the fact that
\cup_{i\in I}B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})\subset B_{(1+k)\rho}(\xi_{0})
. Consequently, we have

(6.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\{|B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})|-|B_{(1+k)\rho}(\xi_{0})\setminus B_{k\rho}(\xi_{0})|\right\}\leq A.

Whenever
C-I
and
C-II
are true, there might be two outcomes namely either

(I)

\displaystyle\left|\left\{u>\lambda c\right\}\cap B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})\right|>(1-\delta)|B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})|,

or

(II)

\displaystyle\left|\left\{u>\lambda c\right\}\cap B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})\right|\leq(1-\delta)|B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})|.

Now, if (I) holds, then let
\xi_{*}\in B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})
be the center of
B_{k\rho}
and the result follows. If (II) is true for
i\in D^{+}
, then

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left|\left\{u\leq\lambda c\right\}\cap B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})\right|>\delta|B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})|.

For every
\xi\in\left\{u(.)>c\right\}\cap B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{i})
and
\eta\in\left\{u(.)\leq\lambda c\right\}\cap B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})
, we have

\displaystyle u(\xi)-u(\eta)>c(1-\lambda)>0,

and

\displaystyle|{\eta}^{-1}\circ{\xi}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq|{\xi_{i}}^{-1}\circ{\xi}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+|{\eta}^{-1}\circ{\xi_{i}}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq 3k\rho.

Therefore, summing up over
D^{+}
and using the bounded overlapping property, assumptions
C-II
, (
6.2
) and (
6.3
), we have by Lemma
2.8

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\delta}{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}

\displaystyle\left[|B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})|-|B_{(1+k)\rho}(\xi_{0})\setminus B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i\in D^{+}}\int_{\{u(.)<\lambda c\}\cap B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})}\int_{\{u(.)>c\}\cap B_{2k\rho}(\xi_{i})}\frac{(3k\rho)^{Q+2s}}{|B_{k\rho}(\xi_{i})|}\frac{|u(\xi-u(\eta)|^{2}}{|{\eta}^{-1}\circ{\xi}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+2s}}\,d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\leq 3^{Q+2s}C(k\rho)^{2s}\iint_{B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})\times B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})}\frac{|u(\xi-u(\eta)|^{2}}{|{\eta}^{-1}\circ{\xi}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+2s}}\,d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\leq 3^{Q+2s}CB(k\rho_{0})^{2s},

where
C=C(Q,s)
is a constant. Consequently, sending
k\rightarrow 0
in the last inequality renders that

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\delta}{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}|B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})|=0,

which contradicts that
|B_{\rho}(\xi_{0})|>0
.
∎

\displaystyle u\geq\frac{\lambda}{8}\,\,\,\,\,\textup{a.e. in }\,B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(\xi_{0})\times\left(t_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\delta\rho^{2},t_{0}+\delta\rho^{2}\right].

Proof.

This lemma mainly follow the steps of the De Giorgi Lemma
5.1
. For simplicity
(\xi_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
. Let us introduce
\rho_{n},\tilde{\rho}_{n},B_{n},\tilde{B}_{n},\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\oplus},\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}^{\oplus},\lambda_{n}
as follows

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{n}=\rho+\frac{\rho}{2^{n+1}},\,\tilde{\rho}_{n}=\frac{\rho_{n}+\rho_{n+1}}{2},\\
B_{n}=B_{\rho_{n}}(0),\,\tilde{B}_{n}=B_{\tilde{\rho}_{n}}(0),\\
\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\oplus}=B_{n}\times(0,\delta\rho_{n}^{2}],\,\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}^{\oplus}=\tilde{B}_{n}\times(0,\delta\tilde{\rho}_{n}^{2}],\\
\lambda_{n}=\lambda+\frac{\lambda}{2^{n+1}}.\end{cases}

Unlike Lemma
5.1
, we consider time independent cut-off function
\psi
as follows

\displaystyle\psi\equiv\begin{cases}1\,\,\,\,\textup{ on }B_{n+1},\\
0\,\,\,\,\textup{ on }B^{c}_{n},\end{cases}\,\,\,\,\textup{ such that }|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\psi|\leq\gamma\frac{2^{n+4}}{\rho}.

Set truncated function

\displaystyle w_{-}:=\left(u+g(t)-\lambda_{n}\right)_{-}\,\,\,\,\textup{ where }\,g(t)=\mathfrak{C}\int_{R^{2}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|u(\eta,t)|}{|\eta|^{Q+2s}_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}}\,d\eta dt,

where positive constant
\mathfrak{C}
to be chosen later. Moreover, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}:=\left\{u(\xi,t)+g(t)<\lambda_{n}\right\}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\oplus},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}(t):=\left\{u(\xi,t)+g(t)<\lambda_{n}\right\}\cap B_{n}\,\,\,\,\textup{ for }\,t\in(0,\delta\rho^{2}].

Considering all these entities in (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\esssup_{0<t\leq\delta\rho_{n+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n+1}}w^{2}_{-}(\xi,t)\,d\xi+\iint_{\mathcal{Q}_{n+1}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}w_{-}|^{2}\,d\xi dt

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\int_{0}^{\delta\rho^{2}}\iint_{B_{n}\times B_{n}}\max\{w^{2}_{-}(\xi,t),w^{2}_{-}(\eta,t)\}\frac{|\psi(\xi)-\psi(\eta)|}{|{\eta}^{-1}\circ{\xi}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+2s}}\,d\xi d\eta dt

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\iint_{\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\oplus}}(w_{-}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\psi\nu)^{2}\,d\xi dt+\iint_{\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\oplus}}(w_{-}\psi^{2}\nu^{2})\,d\xi\left\{\frac{w_{-}(\eta,t)}{|{\eta}^{-1}\circ{\xi}|_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{Q+2s}}\,d\eta\right\}dt

(6.17)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\mathfrak{C}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\oplus}}g^{\prime}(t)(w_{-}\psi^{2}\nu^{2})(\xi,t)\,d\xi dt.

Next, considering the respective calculations in Lemma
5.2
with suitable choice of
\mathfrak{C}
and
\gamma
, we conclude that the left hand side of the previous inequality is dominated by

\displaystyle\gamma\frac{2^{(Q+2s+2)n}}{\delta\rho^{2}}\lambda^{2}|\mathcal{A}_{n}|.

After that by similar calculations as in Lemma
5.1
with the forward in time interval, we have the recursive inequality

(6.18)

\displaystyle Y_{n+1}\leq\gamma(2b)^{n}\delta^{-\left[\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{2}{q\kappa}\right]}Y_{n},

where
b=b(Q),\,\gamma=\gamma(\texttt{data}),\,\kappa=\frac{Q}{Q-2}
and
Y_{n}=\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{n}|}{|\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\oplus}|}.

At this stage, we consider two possibilities.

Case I:
For some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{n_{0}}|}{|\mathcal{Q}_{n_{0}}^{\oplus}|}\leq\iota_{*}.

This yields the following

\displaystyle\left|\{u+g(t)\leq\lambda_{n_{0}}\}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{n_{0}}^{\oplus}\right|\leq\iota_{*}|\mathcal{Q}_{n_{0}}^{\oplus}|

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\left|\{u+g(t)\leq\lambda\}\cap(0,\delta\rho^{2})+\mathcal{Q}_{\rho}^{\ominus}(\delta)\right|\leq\iota_{*}2^{Q}|(0,\delta\rho^{2})+\mathcal{Q}_{\rho}^{\ominus}(\delta)|.

We get the last line because
\rho<\rho_{n_{0}}\leq 2
and
\lambda\leq\lambda_{n_{0}}
. We choose
\iota_{*}
sufficiently small such that

(6.19)

\displaystyle\iota_{*}\leq 2^{-Q}\iota,

where
\iota
is chosen as in Lemma
5.1
. Then we have

(6.20)

\displaystyle\left|\{u+g(t)\leq\lambda\}\cap(0,\delta\rho^{2})+\mathcal{Q}_{\rho}^{\ominus}(\delta)\right|\leq\iota|(0,\delta\rho^{2})+\mathcal{Q}_{\rho}^{\ominus}(\delta)|.

At this point, by
tail
condition and (
6.20
), we are allowed to apply Lemma
5.1
with the center
(0,\delta\rho^{2})
such that

\displaystyle u+g(t)\geq\frac{\lambda}{4}\,\,\,\,\textup{a.e. in }B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}\times\left(\frac{3}{4}\delta\rho^{2},\delta\rho^{2}\right].

Considering the
tail
condition with
g(T_{2})\leq\frac{1}{8}\lambda
, we have

(6.21)

\displaystyle u\geq\frac{\lambda}{8}\,\,\,\,\textup{a.e. in }B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}\times\left(\frac{3}{4}\delta\rho^{2},\delta\rho^{2}\right].

Case II:
If for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{n}|}{|\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\oplus}|}>\iota_{*}.

Then applying convergence result (Lemma
2.4
), we have
\iota\in(0,1)
as in Lemma
5.1
such that

\displaystyle Y_{0}\leq\iota,

and
Y_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow 0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle u+g(t)\geq\lambda\,\,\,\,\textup{a.e. in }B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}\times\left(0,\delta\rho^{2}\right].

Considering the
tail
condition with
g(T_{2})\leq\frac{1}{8}\lambda
, we have

(6.22)

\displaystyle u\geq\frac{7\lambda}{8}\,\,\,\,\textup{a.e. in }B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}\times\left(0,\delta\rho^{2}\right]

Finally, combining both (
6.21
) and (
6.22
), we have our result.
∎

Proof of the Weak Harnack Inequality II

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let us consider
(\xi_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)

6.4
with redefining
