Active-Line Dynamics and Residual Work in a Reduced Oldroyd–B Mechanism
Sai Peng School of Mathematics and Computational Science, Xiangtan University pscfd@xtu.edu.cn

Abstract
. We study a one-dimensional active-line equation arising as a thin-sheet reduced mechanism for high-Weissenberg Oldroyd–B dynamics. The unknown is a positive periodic line density ρ = m + η \rho=m+\eta , and ρ t + c ​ ρ ​ Λ ​ ρ − c ⁡ ( ℋ ​ ρ ) ​ ρ s + γ ⁡ ( ρ − m ) = 0 , m > 0 , \rho_{t}+c\rho\Lambda\rho-c(\mathcal{H}\rho)\rho_{s}+\gamma(\rho-m)=0,\qquad m>0, where Λ = ℋ ∂ s \Lambda=\mathcal{H}\partial_{s} . We prove positive-density local well-posedness, a no-rupture principle, small-oscillation global stability, finite-band relaxation, and a one-tail alternative for any remaining positive-density obstruction. We also separate the reduced scalar mechanism from the two-dimensional positive-cone Oldroyd–B admissibility problem. 1.
Introduction

The Oldroyd–B system is a basic continuum model for dilute polymeric
fluids
[
14
,
3
,
19
]
. Its
mathematical difficulty is most acute in the stress-diffusion-free regime,
where the conformation tensor is transported and stretched by the flow while
remaining constrained to the positive cone. Classical existence and
continuation theories identify several stabilizing mechanisms, including
viscosity, smallness, stress diffusion or regularization, and logarithmic
control of the conformation tensor

[
11
,
13
,
4
,
1
,
5
,
8
,
18
]
.
At the same time, the inviscid or non-diffusive stress channel leaves open
which concentrated stress configurations are compatible with continuation.

This paper studies one such configuration, namely a thin, strongly aligned
stress sheet whose dominant trace evolves along a material curve. The
analysis is guided by two complementary viewpoints. First, BKM-type
continuation criteria and their viscoelastic analogues suggest that any
breakdown mechanism should be visible through a small number of geometric
clocks rather than through arbitrary Sobolev growth

[
2
,
12
,
15
,
17
]
.
Second, the log-conformation representation provides a natural coordinate
system for the positive cone and for high-Weissenberg stress alignment

[
9
,
10
]
. A formal flattening of the
aligned sheet leads to the active-line equation

(1.1)

\rho_{t}+c\rho\Lambda\rho-c(\mathcal{H}\rho)\rho_{s}+\gamma(\rho-m)=0,\qquad\left\langle\rho\right\rangle=m>0,

with
\Lambda=\mathcal{H}\partial_{s}
. In perturbation form,
\rho=m+\eta
,

(1.2)

\eta_{t}+cm\Lambda\eta+\gamma\eta=-c\bigl(\eta\Lambda\eta-(\mathcal{H}\eta)\eta_{s}\bigr)^{\circ}.

The positive mean creates half-Laplacian damping, while the quadratic term has a strong null structure: interactions of two equal-sign Fourier modes vanish. Thus the model is neither a generic Burgers-type cascade nor a passive damping equation.

The reduced equation is in the tradition of one-dimensional nonlocal models
for fluid singularity mechanisms

[
6
,
7
]
. It isolates the
part of a thin-sheet scenario that is carried by the active line itself. We
prove positive-density local well-posedness, a no-rupture principle,
small-oscillation global control, finite-band relaxation, and a one-tail
alternative: after finite-band modes and projected forcing errors are charged,
any remaining positive-density obstruction must be an infinite-tail Sobolev
event. This gives a scalar set of diagnostics for numerical or asymptotic
active-line transfer.

A finite-thickness Oldroyd–B lift must also satisfy the positive-cone
energy–entropy balance. In log-conformation variables
A=e^{B}
, this
balance contains a signed residual channel: the conformation residual must pay
pressure-free work against the entropy lever
G=I-A^{-1}
. Building on
the residual-level admissibility criterion of
[
16
]
, we
prove

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}.

Thus a proposed active-line concentration has two independent requirements:
the scalar one-tail condition and the positive-cone residual-work inequality.

We do not prove that every two-dimensional Oldroyd–B blow-up or
noncompactness sequence converges to an active-line sheet. The geometric
criteria of

[
15
,
17
]
identify continuation clocks
for genuine flows, while
[
16
]
identifies which relaxed
residual directions are admissible; the compactness bridge from those clocks
to the active-line ansatz is a separate rigidity problem. The result proved
below is the implication available once such an extraction is given: the
reduced scalar clocks and the positive-cone residual-work budget must be
simultaneously satisfied.

2.
Three-Level Structure of the Paper

We separate three levels of assertion. The first is an intrinsic statement about the reduced active-line equation. The second is a scalar finite-thickness bookkeeping statement: if a two-dimensional sheet projects to the active-line equation up to a residual, then any loss of lower barrier or high-frequency compactness is measured by explicit residual clocks. The third is a positive-cone admissibility statement for Oldroyd–B lifts: pressure-free residual work must be paired with a signed conformation residual. The paper proves all three statements, and uses them only in this order.

The resulting conclusion is a necessary-condition theorem for active-line
noncompactness, not a regularity theorem for the full two-dimensional
Oldroyd–B system. Any blow-up extraction into a finite-thickness active line
must satisfy both the reduced one-tail condition and the residual-work budget.
In particular, a profile with vanishing scalar clocks and a positive
residual-work gap is ruled out.

(Reduced active-line package)
.

Let
r>3/2
and let
\rho_{0}=m+\eta_{0}\geq\kappa_{0}>0
. The reduced active-line equation (
1.1
) is locally well posed in
H^{r}
, preserves strict positivity while smooth, and is globally controlled for sufficiently small
H^{r}
oscillation. Positive finite-band data relax globally. Finally, if a positive-density solution fails to continue in
H^{r}
, then the failure must be visible in the one-tail alternative of Theorem
4.2
: no uniformly controlled finite-band approximation can produce such an obstruction.

Proof.

This is the combination of Theorem
3.2
, Corollary
3.3
, Theorem
3.4
, Theorem
4.1
, and Theorem
4.2
. The common mechanism is the same-sign Fourier cancellation in Lemma
3.1
, together with the positive mean half-Laplacian damping.
∎

Theorem 2.2

(Conditional finite-thickness exclusion)
.

Let
(u^{\varepsilon},A^{\varepsilon})
,
A^{\varepsilon}=e^{B^{\varepsilon}}
, be a family of smooth positive-cone finite-thickness Oldroyd–B profiles whose dominant trace projects to a density
\rho^{\varepsilon}=m+\eta^{\varepsilon}
satisfying the forced active-line equation (
5.1
) on a time window
E
, with forcing clocks
\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{R}_{E,K,r}^{\varepsilon}
. Suppose, along a sequence
\varepsilon\to 0
, that the scalar clocks and initial high tails vanish at the scale required by Proposition
5.2
, but the positive-cone residual-work data satisfy

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}>\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon}+o(1).

Then the family cannot be an energy–entropy admissible positive-cone Oldroyd–B lift of the active-line trace on
E
.

Proof.

The scalar part follows from Proposition
5.2
: after the forcing clocks and initial high tails vanish, the reduced trace cannot hide density loss or high-frequency growth outside the active one-tail channel. Independently, Theorem
6.1
gives the necessary positive-cone admissibility inequality

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon}

for every energy–entropy admissible residual-matching Oldroyd–B Reynolds state. The assumed strict violation contradicts this necessary condition. Thus the proposed finite-thickness lift is excluded before any claim about full Oldroyd–B realization or blow-up compactness is needed.
∎

Remark 2.3

(Scope of the implication)
.

Theorem
2.2
is not a full two-dimensional regularity theorem. It assumes the finite-thickness active-line reduction as input. A statement for arbitrary noncompactness sequences would require an additional rigidity theorem showing that, after rescaling and extraction, such sequences produce residual-work admissible finite-thickness active-line concentrations.

Theorem 2.4

(Conditional active-line rigidity consequence)
.

Let
(u^{\varepsilon},A^{\varepsilon})
,
A^{\varepsilon}=e^{B^{\varepsilon}}
, be a sequence of smooth positive-cone Oldroyd–B profiles on a time window
E
with uniformly controlled energy–entropy and with a noncompactness signal in the trace channel. Assume that, after rescaling and extraction, the sequence admits an active-line concentration in the following precise sense:

(1)

the dominant trace converges to a positive active-line density
\rho=m+\eta
solving (
1.1
) up to a forcing
g^{\varepsilon}
;

(2)

the scalar residual clocks
\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\varepsilon}
,
\mathcal{R}_{E,K,r}^{\varepsilon}
, and the initial high tails vanish along the extraction scale;

(3)

the positive-cone residual-work tuple
(W_{E}^{\varepsilon},L_{E}^{\varepsilon},S_{E}^{\varepsilon},\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon})
is the tuple induced by the same finite-thickness profiles.

Then one of the following alternatives must occur:

(2.1)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{A}_{E,K,r}^{\varepsilon}>0\quad\hbox{for every admissible cutoff sequence }K\to\infty,

or

(2.2)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(W_{E}^{\varepsilon}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\right)\leq 0.

Consequently, a finite-thickness active-line concentration with vanishing scalar clocks and a positive residual-work gap cannot be the blow-up profile of an energy–entropy admissible positive-cone Oldroyd–B sequence.

Proof.

If the active one-tail alternative (
2.1
) fails, then the scalar trace has no uncharged high-frequency channel after the extraction: the projected residual clocks and initial tails vanish by assumption, and the active one-tail clock is negligible. Proposition
5.2
and Theorem
4.2
therefore leave no scalar mechanism outside the active-line limit. Since the profiles are assumed energy–entropy admissible in the positive cone, Theorem
6.1
applies to the induced residual-work tuple for each
\varepsilon
, giving

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon}.

Taking
\limsup
gives (
2.2
). Thus a positive residual-work gap is incompatible with the stated active-line compactness extraction.
∎

Remark 2.5

(Use in a future compactness theorem)
.

A compactness theorem would have to verify the three extraction hypotheses above for every admissible noncompactness sequence. Under that additional input, the active-line mechanism and the residual-work law give an exclusion principle.

3.
The Reduced Equation

Let
P_{0}f=f-\left\langle f\right\rangle
. The Hilbert transform is normalized by
\widehat{\mathcal{H}f}(k)=-\mathrm{i}\operatorname{sgn}(k)\hat{f}(k)
, so
\widehat{\Lambda f}(k)=|k|\hat{f}(k)
. Define

B(f,g)=P_{0}\{f\Lambda g-(\mathcal{H}f)g_{s}\}.

Then (
1.2
) is
\eta_{t}+cm\Lambda\eta+\gamma\eta=-cB(\eta,\eta)
.

Lemma 3.1

(Fourier null structure)
.

For nonzero integers
k,\ell
,

\widehat{B(f,g)}(k+\ell)=\bigl(|\ell|-\operatorname{sgn}(k)\ell\bigr)\hat{f}(k)\hat{g}(\ell).

In particular this symbol is zero when
k
and
\ell
have the same sign.

Proof.

The identity follows from the Fourier symbols of
\Lambda
,
\mathcal{H}
, and
\partial_{s}
. If
k\ell>0
, then
|\ell|=\operatorname{sgn}(k)\ell
, so the multiplier vanishes.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Positive-density local theory)
.

Let
r>3/2
and let
\rho_{0}=m+\eta_{0}\in H^{r}(\mathbb{T})
satisfy
\rho_{0}\geq\kappa_{0}>0
. Then (
1.1
) has a unique solution on a time interval
[0,T]
,

\eta\in C([0,T];H^{r})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{r+1/2}),

with
\rho(t)\geq\kappa_{0}e^{-\gamma t}
in the classical approximation sense. The solution depends continuously on the data while the positive lower barrier and
H^{r}
norm remain controlled.

Proof.

A Friedrichs approximation gives smooth solutions. The principal linear part contributes

cm\left\|\Lambda^{r+1/2}\eta\right\|_{2}^{2}+\gamma\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}.

Kato–Ponce commutator estimates and
H^{r}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow C^{1}
for
r>3/2
bound the quadratic term by

C\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r}}\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r+1/2}}^{2}+C\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r}}^{3}.

After lowering
T
, the dissipative term closes the estimate. At a spatial minimum of a smooth positive solution,
\rho_{s}=0
and
\Lambda\rho\leq 0
, so
\dot{\rho}_{\min}+\gamma(\rho_{\min}-m)\geq 0
. Passing through the approximation gives the stated lower barrier and uniqueness follows from the same estimate applied to differences.
∎

Corollary 3.3

(No rupture)
.

A smooth positive solution of (
1.1
) cannot lose positivity at a finite time. More precisely,

\min_{s}\rho(t,s)\geq m+e^{-\gamma t}\bigl(\min_{s}\rho_{0}(s)-m\bigr)

whenever the solution remains smooth.

Theorem 3.4

(Small oscillation global stability)
.

There exists
\delta>0
, depending only on
m,c,\gamma,r
, such that if
\left\|\eta_{0}\right\|_{H^{r}}\leq\delta
, then the solution of (
1.2
) is global and

\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\|\eta(t)\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}+cm\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|\eta(t)\right\|_{H^{r+1/2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\left\|\eta_{0}\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}.

Proof.

The energy inequality in the proof of Theorem
3.2
becomes

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}+cm\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r+1/2}}^{2}+2\gamma\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}\leq C\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r}}\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r+1/2}}^{2}+C\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r}}^{3}.

Choosing
\delta
small absorbs the first right-hand term and the usual continuity argument gives global control.
∎

4.
Finite Bands and the One-Tail Alternative

The null structure prevents same-sign self-cascade. In a finite Fourier band, every output channel has only finitely many possible ancestors; damping eventually dominates.

Theorem 4.1

(Finite-band relaxation)
.

Let
\eta_{0}
be a trigonometric polynomial and suppose
m+heta\eta_{0}\geq 0
for all
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. Then the solution of (
1.2
) exists globally. If it remains strictly positive, then
\eta(t)\to 0
in every Sobolev norm as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

The positive cone is preserved by Corollary
3.3
. A Fejer regularization gives positive finite-band approximants whose top modes cannot regenerate through equal-sign top-top interactions by Lemma
3.1
. Downward induction on the largest active frequency, together with half-Laplacian damping, controls each shell. Compactness gives the stated limit for the original finite-band datum.
∎

For general data, define the high tail
P_{>K}\eta
and the one-tail clock

\mathcal{A}_{T,K,r}=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|P_{>K}B(P_{\leq K}\eta,P_{>K}\eta)+P_{>K}B(P_{>K}\eta,P_{\leq K}\eta)\right\|_{H^{r-1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Theorem 4.2

(One-tail alternative)
.

Let
r>3/2
. If a positive-density solution of (
1.2
) loses its
H^{r}
continuation at time
T_{*}<\infty
, then for every sequence
K_{j}\to\infty
, at least one of the following quantities fails to remain small on some interval approaching
T_{*}
: the initial high tail
\left\|P_{>K_{j}}\eta(0)\right\|_{H^{r}}
, the solution high tail
\sup_{t<T_{*}}\left\|P_{>K_{j}}\eta(t)\right\|_{H^{r}}
, or the one-tail clock
\mathcal{A}_{T_{*},K_{j},r}
.

Proof.

Split
\eta=u+v
into low and high frequencies. The low part is finite dimensional and controlled on bounded time intervals once
\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r}}
is bounded. The high part solves a damped equation forced by low-high, high-low, and high-high interactions. The high-high equal-sign channel is absent at the top by Lemma
3.1
; the remaining terms are bounded by the one-tail clock and the high-tail norm. If all three listed quantities were uniformly small for large
K
, the local continuation estimate from Theorem
3.2
would extend the solution beyond
T_{*}
, contradiction.
∎

5.
Finite-Thickness Forcing

A finite-thickness active-line calculation produces a forced scalar equation

(5.1)

\eta_{t}+cm\Lambda\eta+\gamma\eta=-cB(\eta,\eta)+g^{\varepsilon},

where
g^{\varepsilon}
is the projected trace defect and thickness residual. The scalar theory sees this forcing only through clocks.

Definition 5.1

(Scalar residual clocks)
.

For
T>0
and cutoff
K
, set

\mathcal{M}_{T}^{\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|g^{\varepsilon}(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\mathcal{R}_{T,K,r}^{\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|P_{>K}g^{\varepsilon}(t)\right\|_{H^{r-1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Proposition 5.2

(Forced bookkeeping)
.

The conclusions of Theorems
3.2
–
4.2
remain valid for (
5.1
), with the minimum barrier and tail alternative enlarged by
\mathcal{M}_{T}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{R}_{T,K,r}^{\varepsilon}
. In particular, a reported finite-thickness active-line cascade must charge any density loss or high-tail growth either to the active one-tail clock or to the projected residual clocks.

Proof.

The lower-barrier argument gains the additive forcing term
g^{\varepsilon}
, yielding a Gronwall bound with
\mathcal{M}_{T}^{\varepsilon}
. The high-frequency estimate gains
P_{>K}g^{\varepsilon}
, which is exactly measured by
\mathcal{R}_{T,K,r}^{\varepsilon}
. No other part of the null-structure argument changes.
∎

6.
Positive-Cone Residual Work

The terminology used in the statements below is made precise in
the definitions below. In particular, a
residual-matching profile is a smooth triple
(u,p,B)
,
A=e^{B}
, whose
momentum and conformation residuals are matched by symmetric Reynolds defects,
and
P(t)
denotes the associated pressure-free work.

Anatomy of the theorem.

The proof has four independent pieces. The defect-work identity converts the
energy–entropy inequality into a signed work balance. The pressure-free
reduction removes isotropic and mean modes, leaving a gauge-invariant number

P(t)
. The sharp pointwise cost is then a Hilbert-space minimization
against the lever
G=I-A^{-1}
. Finally, the windowed estimate follows by
integrating the pointwise inequality and taking the worst negative-alignment
coefficient on the time window. The structured-family and power-law tests are
consequences of this chain, not additional assumptions.

Theorem 6.1

(Residual-work obstruction)
.

Let
(u,p,B)
be a residual-matching profile on a time interval
I
, and
let
P(t)
be its pressure-free work in
Definition
6.10
. Define

A=e^{B},\qquad G=I-A^{-1}.

For a.e.
t
, set

\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\left[-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\right]_{+}}{\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\|_{L^{2}}},&\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\|_{L^{2}}>0,\\[11.99998pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If the associated residual-matching state is energy–entropy admissible, then

[P(t)]_{+}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Consequently, on every measurable
E\subset I
,

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E},

where

\displaystyle W_{E}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}[P(t)]_{+}\,dt,

\displaystyle L_{E}

\displaystyle=\left\|G\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},

\displaystyle S_{E}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B]\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},

\displaystyle\eta_{E}

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in E}\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t).

For each fixed time, among all conformation residuals with the same lever

G(t)
, the smallest possible
L^{2}
-size needed to balance positive work is

\frac{2[P(t)]_{+}}{\alpha\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}},

with the convention that no finite residual can balance positive work when

G(t)\equiv 0
. If
P(t)>0
and
G(t)\not\equiv 0
, the unique minimizer is

\Sigma_{\min}=-\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}G(t).

Definition 6.2

(Windowed residual-work data)
.

For a residual-matching profile and a measurable time window
E\subset I
,
we call

(W_{E},L_{E},S_{E},\eta_{E})

the residual-work data on
E
, where

W_{E}=\int_{E}[P(t)]_{+}\,dt,\quad L_{E}=\left\|G\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},\quad S_{E}=\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B]\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},

and
\eta_{E}=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in E}\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)
.
The degenerate convention is the one in Theorem
6.1
:
if
\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\|_{L^{2}}=0
, then

\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)=0
. Thus positive work cannot be hidden where either
the lever or the residual vanishes.

Theorem 6.3

(Closed structured-family test)
.

Let
(u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon})
be smooth residual-matching states on a
time window
E
, and let

W_{E}^{\varepsilon},\quad L_{E}^{\varepsilon},\quad S_{E}^{\varepsilon},\quad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}

be their residual-work data. If there exist constants

W_{*}>0,L_{*},S_{*},\eta_{*}
such that

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\geq W_{*},\qquad L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq L_{*},\qquad S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq S_{*},\qquad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\eta_{*},

and

W_{*}>\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{*}L_{*}S_{*},

then no member of the family is energy–entropy admissible on
E
.
In the algebraic converse direction, if the exact residual matching equations
hold,
P^{\varepsilon}\leq 0
whenever
G^{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
, and

\mathcal{S}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}]=\Sigma_{\rm pay}^{\varepsilon}+Z^{\varepsilon},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G^{\varepsilon}:Z^{\varepsilon}\,dx\leq 0,

for a.e. time, where

\Sigma_{\rm pay}^{\varepsilon}=-\frac{2[P^{\varepsilon}]_{+}}{\alpha\left\|G^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}G^{\varepsilon}

on the set
\{G^{\varepsilon}\not\equiv 0\}
and

\Sigma_{\rm pay}^{\varepsilon}=0
on
\{G^{\varepsilon}\equiv 0\}
,
then the pressure-free energy–entropy work criterion is satisfied on
E
.

Proof.

The obstruction direction is immediate from
Theorem
6.1
. If an admissible member existed, then

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{*}L_{*}S_{*},

contradicting
W_{*}>\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{*}L_{*}S_{*}
.

For the algebraic converse, insert

\mathcal{S}=\Sigma_{\rm pay}+Z

into the pressure-free work identity. On
\{G\not\equiv 0\}
, the aligned
term gives

\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\Sigma_{\rm pay}\,dx=-[P]_{+}.

The additional condition on
Z
makes the remaining contribution
non-positive. Therefore

P+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\mathcal{S}\,dx\leq P-[P]_{+}\leq 0.

On
\{G\equiv 0\}
, the stated hypothesis
P\leq 0
gives the same
conclusion.
∎

6.1.
Notation and preliminaries

We work on
\mathbb{T}^{2}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. Matrix contractions are Frobenius
products,
A:C=\operatorname{tr}(A^{T}C)
, and spatial averages are denoted by

\left\langle f\right\rangle=|\mathbb{T}^{2}|^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}f(x)\,dx.

All function spaces are over
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. We write
H^{s}=H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and

L^{2}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, with componentwise norms for vector and matrix fields.
Space-time norms over a time window
E\subset[0,T]
are written, for
instance, as
L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
. The symmetric gradient is

D(u)=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+\nabla u^{T}).

The conformation tensor is parametrized by

A=e^{B},\qquad B(t,x)\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

so
A(t,x)\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{2}
automatically. The entropy-dual lever is

G(A)=I-A^{-1}.

It is the derivative of the elastic entropy density

\operatorname{tr}A-\log\det A-2
with respect to
A
.

For a smooth profile
(u,p,B)
, the momentum and conformation residuals are
denoted by
\mathcal{N}[u,p,B]
and
\mathcal{S}[u,B]
. The momentum residual is always
understood modulo spatial means and pressure tensors. A pressure-free
symmetric antidivergence
R_{\mathcal{N}}
is paired with
D(u)
through

P(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}-R_{\mathcal{N}}(t):D(u(t))\,dx.

This number is independent of the pressure-free representative; see
Lemma
6.13
. On a time window
E
, the residual-work diagnostics
are

W_{E}=\int_{E}[P(t)]_{+}\,dt,\qquad L_{E}=\left\|G\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},\qquad S_{E}=\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B]\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},

together with the alignment coefficient
\eta_{E}\in[0,1]
from
Definition
6.2
. These four quantities are the complete
input for the windowed admissibility test.

\begin{array}[]{c|l|l}\hbox{symbol}&\hbox{definition}&\hbox{role}\\
\hline\cr P(t)&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}-R_{\mathcal{N}}:D(u)\,dx&\hbox{gauge-invariant pressure-free work}\\[5.0pt]
W_{E}&\displaystyle\int_{E}[P(t)]_{+}\,dt&\hbox{positive work to be paid}\\[5.0pt]
L_{E}&\left\|G\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}&\hbox{entropy-lever size}\\[5.0pt]
S_{E}&\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B]\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}&\hbox{conformation-residual size}\\[5.0pt]
\eta_{E}&\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in E}\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)&\hbox{negative-alignment efficiency}\end{array}

6.2.
Positive-cone Reynolds states

Definition 6.4

(Positive-cone Reynolds state)
.

Let
B(t,x)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and set
A=e^{B}
. A positive-cone
Oldroyd–B–Reynolds state is a tuple

(u,p,B,R,\Sigma)

with
\operatorname{div}u=0
,
R,\Sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, satisfying

(6.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\alpha\operatorname{div}(A-I)+\operatorname{div}R,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A+u\cdot\nabla A-\nabla u\,A-A(\nabla u)^{T}+\lambda^{-1}(A-I)

\displaystyle=\Sigma.

Definition 6.5

(Defect admissibility)
.

A smooth positive-cone Reynolds state is energy–entropy admissible if

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left[-R:D(u)+\frac{\alpha}{2}(I-A^{-1}):\Sigma\right]\mathrm{d}\leq 0\quad\hbox{for a.e. }t.

Definition 6.6

(Free energy and entropy lever)
.

For
A\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{2}
, define

\Phi(A)=\operatorname{tr}A-\log\det A-2,\qquad G(A)=D_{A}\Phi(A)=I-A^{-1}.

The free energy of a positive-cone state is

\mathcal{E}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Phi(A(t))\,dx.

Lemma 6.7

(Lever bounds on spectral windows)
.

If the spectrum of
A
lies in
[m,M]
,
0<m<M<\infty
, then

|G(A)|\leq C_{m,M}|A-I|,\qquad|A-I|\leq C_{m,M}|G(A)|.

Near equilibrium,
G(A)=A-I+O(|A-I|^{2})
.

Proof.

The map
A\mapsto I-A^{-1}
is smooth on the compact spectral set

\{mI\leq A\leq MI\}
. Its derivative at
I
is the identity map on
symmetric matrices, which gives the local expansion.
∎

Proposition 6.8

(Defect-work identity)
.

Every smooth positive-cone Reynolds state satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle+\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}+\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\operatorname{tr}(A+A^{-1}-2I)\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R:D(u)\mathrm{d}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(I-A^{-1}):\Sigma\mathrm{d}.

Proof.

Testing (
6.1
) by
u
, integrating over the torus, and
using
\operatorname{div}u=0
, gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u|^{2}\,dx+\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx=-\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(A-I):\nabla u\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R:D(u)\,dx.

The pressure and transport terms vanish, and
R:D(u)=R:\nabla u
because

R
is symmetric.

For the elastic part, since
D_{A}\Phi(A)=G=I-A^{-1}
,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Phi(A)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\partial_{t}A\,dx.

The transport term satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:(u\cdot\nabla A)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u\cdot\nabla\Phi(A)\,dx=0.

Moreover
G
commutes with
A
, and

G:(\nabla u\,A+A(\nabla u)^{T})=2(A-I):\nabla u.

Finally,

G:(A-I)=\operatorname{tr}(A+A^{-1}-2I).

Pairing (
6.2
) with
\alpha G/2
therefore yields

\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Phi(A)\,dx=\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(A-I):\nabla u\,dx-\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\operatorname{tr}(A+A^{-1}-2I)\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\Sigma\,dx.

Adding the kinetic and elastic balances cancels the principal stress work and
gives the stated identity.
∎

6.3.
Residual matching and sharp cost

For smooth
u,p,B
, define

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[u,p,B]

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}u+\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p-\alpha\operatorname{div}(A-I),

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[u,B]

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}A+u\cdot\nabla A-\nabla u\,A-A(\nabla u)^{T}+\lambda^{-1}(A-I).

The residual matching equations are

\operatorname{div}R=\mathcal{M}[u,p,B],\qquad\Sigma=\mathcal{S}[u,B].

Definition 6.9

(Residual-matching profile)
.

A smooth triple
(u,p,B)
, with
\operatorname{div}u=0
and
A=e^{B}
, is called a
residual-matching profile if the mean of
\mathcal{M}[u,p,B]
vanishes for a.e.
time and if
\Sigma=\mathcal{S}[u,B]
is used as the conformation residual. A symmetric
tensor
R
is an admissible momentum representative for the profile if

\operatorname{div}R=\mathcal{M}[u,p,B].

Two representatives are identified if they differ by a divergence-free
symmetric tensor plus an isotropic pressure tensor.

Definition 6.10

(Pressure-free work of a profile)
.

For a residual-matching profile, set

\mathcal{N}[u,B]=\partial_{t}u+\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)-\nu\Delta u-\alpha\operatorname{div}(A-I).

Let

\mathcal{N}_{0}(t,x)=\mathcal{N}(t,x)-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mathcal{N}(t,y)\,dy

be the zero-mean part. A pressure-free representative is any symmetric tensor

R_{\mathcal{N}}
satisfying

\operatorname{div}R_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathcal{N}_{0}

after quotienting out isotropic tensors and divergence-free symmetric tensors.
The pressure-free work is

P(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}-R_{\mathcal{N}}(t):D(u(t))\,dx.

By Lemma
6.13
, this number is independent of the representative.
The removed mean is the harmless mean-velocity forcing mode and does no work
against periodic gradients.

Remark 6.11

(Mean mode)
.

On the torus, a constant vector field cannot be represented as the divergence
of a periodic tensor. This is why the spatial mean of
\mathcal{N}
is removed in
Definition
6.10
. The residual-work obstruction is
concerned with deformation work, and
D(u)
only sees the gradient part of
the velocity. Fixing the mean velocity therefore removes no admissibility
information relevant to the pressure-free work.

Proposition 6.12

(Symmetric pressure-free antidivergence)
.

If a vector field
m
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
has zero mean, then there is a symmetric
trace-free tensor
R_{m}
with

\operatorname{div}R_{m}=m,\qquad\left\|R_{m}\right\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq C_{s}\left\|m\right\|_{H^{s}}.

Proof.

Let
\Delta\phi=m
with zero mean and set

(R_{m})_{ij}=\partial_{i}\phi_{j}+\partial_{j}\phi_{i}-\delta_{ij}\operatorname{div}\phi.

Then
R_{m}
is symmetric, trace-free in two dimensions, and

\operatorname{div}R_{m}=m
.
∎

Lemma 6.13

(Gauge invariance of pressure-free work)
.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfy

\operatorname{div}R_{1}=\operatorname{div}R_{2}

and assume
R_{1}-R_{2}
differs from a divergence-free symmetric tensor by an
isotropic pressure tensor. Then, for every divergence-free
u
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R_{1}:D(u)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R_{2}:D(u)\,dx.

Proof.

Write
R_{1}-R_{2}=Q+pI
, with
\operatorname{div}Q=0
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}Q:D(u)\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\operatorname{div}Q)\cdot u\,dx=0,

and

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}pI:D(u)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}p\,\operatorname{div}u\,dx=0.

∎

Proposition 6.14

(Canonical pressure-free reduction)
.

For every residual-matching profile, the value of
P(t)
in
Definition
6.10
is independent of the chosen
pressure-free representative. Moreover, the energy–entropy admissibility of
the profile depends only on
P(t)
,
G(A)
, and
\mathcal{S}[u,B]
.

Proof.

The independence of
P(t)
is Lemma
6.13
. Substituting

\Sigma=\mathcal{S}[u,B]
into the defect-work identity shows that all representatives
with the same pressure-free work produce the same right-hand side in the
energy–entropy balance. Therefore admissibility is determined by

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\leq 0.

∎

Proposition 6.15

(Pressure-free residual work)
.

For a residual-matching profile, the admissibility condition is

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\leq 0,

for a.e.
t
.

Proof.

Adding an isotropic pressure tensor
pI
changes the divergence constraint
but not the work pairing, since
pI:D(u)=p\operatorname{div}u=0
. The remaining
gauge freedom is removed by Lemma
6.13
. Equivalently, this is the
canonical pressure-free reduction in
Proposition
6.14
.
∎

Proposition 6.16

(Sharp residual-work cost)
.

Assume
G(t)\not\equiv 0
. Among all conformation residuals
\Sigma
satisfying

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\Sigma(t)\,dx\leq 0,

the minimal
L^{2}
norm is

\frac{2[P(t)]_{+}}{\alpha\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}},

and, for
P(t)>0
, the unique minimizer is

-\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}G(t).

Proof.

The constraint requires

-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\Sigma\,dx\geq\frac{2P_{+}}{\alpha}.

Cauchy’s inequality gives

\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|G\right\|_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{2P_{+}}{\alpha},

with equality exactly when
\Sigma
is a negative multiple of
G
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

The pressure-free identity (
6.15
) implies

[P(t)]_{+}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\right]_{+}.

Indeed, if
P(t)\leq 0
there is nothing to prove, while for
P(t)>0
the
admissibility inequality forces the negative signed projection of

\mathcal{S}[u,B]
onto
G
to pay
P(t)
. The positive part of this projection
is bounded by its absolute value, and the definition of
\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)

therefore gives the pointwise bound in Theorem
6.1
, with

\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)=0
on the zero-lever or zero-residual set.

Integrating the pointwise bound on
E
and using

\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)\leq\eta_{E}=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in E}\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)
, we get

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}\int_{E}\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\,dt.

Cauchy’s inequality in time gives

\int_{E}\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\,dt\leq L_{E}S_{E},

which is the windowed estimate in Theorem
6.1
. The sharp cost is
Proposition
6.16
.
∎

Corollary 6.17

(Three-channel alternative)
.

Fix a time window
E
and assume
W_{E}>0
. If a residual-matching state is
energy–entropy admissible, then at least one of the following must hold:

L_{E}\geq\left(\frac{2W_{E}}{\alpha\eta_{E}}\right)^{1/2},\qquad S_{E}\geq\left(\frac{2W_{E}}{\alpha\eta_{E}}\right)^{1/2},\qquad\eta_{E}\geq\frac{2W_{E}}{\alpha L_{E}S_{E}}.

Equivalently, a family with positive work can remain admissible only by
developing a large entropy lever, a large conformation residual, or a strong
negative alignment between them.

Proof.

The statement is a direct rewriting of

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}.

If all three alternatives fail, then

\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}<W_{E},

because
\eta_{E}\leq 1
,
L_{E},S_{E}\geq 0
, and at least one of the three
displayed inequalities is then strict. This contradicts the windowed
residual-work estimate.
∎

Remark 6.18

(Cone-tip degeneracy)
.

When
A\approx I
, the lever
G=I-A^{-1}
is small. Thus a profile that
tries to keep the conformation close to equilibrium while generating positive
pressure-free Reynolds work must compensate by either producing a large
conformation residual or arranging nearly perfect negative alignment. This is
the algebraic form of the positive-cone obstruction near equilibrium.

6.4.
Structured-family tests and a concrete obstruction

The next statements translate the residual-work obstruction into scale
criteria. They are useful because a localized ansatz usually comes with
separate estimates for work, lever, residual size, and alignment. The theorem
below says that no compensation outside these four channels is available at the
level of energy–entropy admissibility.

Corollary 6.19

(Bulk power-law exclusion test)
.

Let
(u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon})
,
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, be
smooth residual-matching states on the same time window
E
. Suppose

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\geq c_{W}\varepsilon^{-w},\quad L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}\varepsilon^{-\ell},\quad S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{S}\varepsilon^{-r},\quad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{-q}.

If
w>\ell+r+q
, then the family is not energy–entropy admissible on
E

for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. The same conclusion holds when

w=\ell+r+q
if

c_{W}>\frac{\alpha}{2}C_{\eta}C_{L}C_{S}.

Proof.

For small
\varepsilon
, (
6.19
) gives

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}>\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon},

which contradicts Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Remark 6.20

(Exponent bookkeeping)
.

The bulk test is intentionally one-sided. The exponent
w
measures how fast
positive pressure-free work grows. The exponents
\ell,r,q
measure how much
budget is available through the lever, conformation residual, and alignment.
The inequality

w>\ell+r+q

means that the work grows faster than the entire admissible budget. In the
borderline case, the constants matter. This is useful for finite-thickness
ansatzes because the powers are usually robust under small changes of cutoff,
whereas the constants record the exact shape.

Definition 6.21

(Line-to-bulk data package)
.

Let
(u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon})
be a finite-thickness lift of a line state.
A data package on
E
consists of numbers

W_{\rm line},L_{\rm line},S_{\rm line},\eta_{\rm line},\delta_{W},\delta_{L},\delta_{S},\delta_{\eta}

such that

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\geq W_{\rm line}-\delta_{W},\qquad L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq L_{\rm line}+\delta_{L},

and

S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\rm lift}S_{\rm line}+\delta_{S},\qquad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\eta_{\rm line}+\delta_{\eta}.

Theorem 6.22

(Power-law active-line test)
.

Assume a finite-thickness lift admits a line-to-bulk package. If

W_{\rm line}^{\varepsilon}\geq c_{W}\varepsilon^{-w},\quad\delta_{W}^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{c_{W}}{2}\varepsilon^{-w},

L_{\rm line}^{\varepsilon}+\delta_{L}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}\varepsilon^{-\ell},\quad C_{\rm lift}S_{\rm line}^{\varepsilon}+\delta_{S}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{R}\varepsilon^{-r},

and

\eta_{\rm line}^{\varepsilon}+\delta_{\eta}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{-q},

then
w>\ell+r+q
excludes energy–entropy admissibility for small

\varepsilon
. In the borderline case
w=\ell+r+q
, the same conclusion
holds if
c_{W}>\alpha C_{\eta}C_{L}C_{R}
.

Proof.

The data package converts the active-line bounds into the bulk bounds of
Corollary
6.19
, with a factor
1/2
in the work lower
bound.
∎

Remark 6.23

(What the active-line test does not assume)
.

Theorem
6.22
does not assume that the line equation is
strictly equivalent to the full Oldroyd–B system. It assumes only that a
finite-thickness lift has computable bulk residual-work data. This is why the
test remains meaningful even when the reduced model is used as a mechanism
detector rather than as an exact reduction.

6.5.
Model examples and non-examples

We record two elementary uses of the criterion before the concrete
finite-thickness construction. The point is to distinguish failure of
admissibility from mere largeness of a residual.

Proposition 6.24

(Orthogonal residuals cannot pay positive work)
.

Let
(u,p,B)
be a residual-matching profile on
E
. Suppose

P(t)>0\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx=0

on a subset of
E
of positive measure. Then the profile is not
energy–entropy admissible on that subset.

Proof.

The pressure-free work identity gives

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx=P(t)>0,

which directly violates

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\leq 0.

∎

Proposition 6.25

(Aligned residuals are exactly efficient)
.

Let
G(t)\not\equiv 0
and
P(t)>0
. If

\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)=-\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\|G(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}G(t),

then the pressure-free admissibility inequality is saturated at time
t
.
If an additional residual
Z(t)
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):Z(t)\,dx\leq 0,

then replacing
\mathcal{S}
by
\mathcal{S}+Z
preserves admissibility.

Proof.

Substitution gives

\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\mathcal{S}\,dx=-P.

The additional term contributes a non-positive quantity to the left-hand side
of the admissibility inequality.
∎

6.6.
A concrete finite-thickness obstruction

We now give a fully bulk example, not a formal one-dimensional reduction. The
profile is supported in a layer of thickness
\varepsilon
, remains inside the
positive cone by construction, and has an explicitly matched momentum and
conformation residual. The point of the example is that all four quantities
in the residual-work criterion can be computed on the same scale. The positive
pressure-free work grows faster than the entire lever–residual budget, so the
family fails admissibility for a structural reason rather than because of a
choice of gauge or a missing pressure mode.

Lemma 6.26

(Finite-thickness scaling)
.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-1/4,1/4))
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and

f_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})=\varepsilon^{-a}f(x_{2}/\varepsilon)

on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, by viewing the expression as a periodic
function of the coordinate
x_{2}
. Then

\left\|f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}=\varepsilon^{1/2-a}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\qquad\left\|\partial_{2}f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}=\varepsilon^{-1/2-a}\left\|f^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

If
F
is a smooth bounded function with
F(0)=0
, then

\left\|F(f(x_{2}/\varepsilon))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq C_{F}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

Proof.

The first two identities follow from the change of variables

y=x_{2}/\varepsilon
. Since
f
is supported in
(-1/4,1/4)
, the layer is
contained in an interval of length
O(\varepsilon)
; for small
\varepsilon
, its
periodic copies do not overlap on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. The last estimate follows from
boundedness and the same change of variables.
∎

Proposition 6.27

(Finite-thickness shear-layer obstruction)
.

Assume
\nu>0
. Fix a time window
E\subset[0,T]
of positive measure and

\beta>0
. Let
\theta,\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-1/4,1/4))
, with

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta(y)\,dy=0,\qquad\theta^{\prime}\not\equiv 0,\qquad\chi\not\equiv 0.

Define

\theta_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})=\theta(x_{2}/\varepsilon),\qquad\chi_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})=\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon),

and set

H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad u^{\varepsilon}(x)=\left(\varepsilon^{-\beta}\theta_{\varepsilon}(x_{2}),0\right),

B^{\varepsilon}(x)=b\chi_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})H,\qquad A^{\varepsilon}=e^{B^{\varepsilon}}.

Let

R^{\varepsilon}=-2\nu D(u^{\varepsilon})-\alpha(A^{\varepsilon}-I),\qquad\Sigma^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{S}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}].

Then

(u^{\varepsilon},0,B^{\varepsilon},R^{\varepsilon},\Sigma^{\varepsilon})
is a smooth
positive-cone Reynolds state with fixed mean velocity. Its residual-work data
obey

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\geq c_{W}\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)},\qquad L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}\varepsilon^{1/2},

S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{S}\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)},\qquad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq 1.

Thus the bulk exponents may be taken as

w=2\beta+1,\qquad\ell=0,\qquad r=\beta+\frac{1}{2},\qquad q=0,

and the family is not energy–entropy admissible for all sufficiently small

\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The support and zero-mean assumptions on
\theta
make

u^{\varepsilon}
a smooth periodic divergence-free velocity with zero mean.
Since
B^{\varepsilon}
is symmetric,
A^{\varepsilon}=e^{B^{\varepsilon}}
is
positive definite. More explicitly,

A^{\varepsilon}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}&0\\
0&e^{-b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}\end{pmatrix},

so
A^{\varepsilon}
,
(A^{\varepsilon})^{-1}
, and all smooth functions of
them are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}
, independently of
\varepsilon
.
Writing
u^{\varepsilon}=(U^{\varepsilon}(x_{2}),0)
, one has

\operatorname{div}(u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon})=0,\qquad\operatorname{div}(2D(u^{\varepsilon}))=\Delta u^{\varepsilon}.

Therefore

\operatorname{div}R^{\varepsilon}=-\nu\Delta u^{\varepsilon}-\alpha\operatorname{div}(A^{\varepsilon}-I)=\mathcal{N}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}],

and the conformation residual is matched by the definition of

\Sigma^{\varepsilon}
. Moreover
D(u^{\varepsilon})
and

A^{\varepsilon}-I
are symmetric, hence
R^{\varepsilon}
is a symmetric
representative of the zero-mean momentum residual.

The deformation tensor is purely off-diagonal:

\nabla u^{\varepsilon}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad D(u^{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}\\
\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Since
A^{\varepsilon}-I
is diagonal, its Frobenius product with

D(u^{\varepsilon})
vanishes:

(A^{\varepsilon}-I):D(u^{\varepsilon})=0.

Thus

P^{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}-R^{\varepsilon}:D(u^{\varepsilon})\,dx=2\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|D(u^{\varepsilon})|^{2}\,dx.

Since

\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-\beta-1}\theta^{\prime}(x_{2}/\varepsilon),

and
|D(u^{\varepsilon})|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}|^{2}
, Lemma
6.26

gives

P^{\varepsilon}(t)=\nu\left\|\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}=\nu\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)}\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

Thus

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}=|E|\nu\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)}.

The lever is

G^{\varepsilon}=I-(A^{\varepsilon})^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1-e^{-b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}&0\\
0&1-e^{b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}\end{pmatrix}.

It is a bounded smooth function of
b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)
, vanishing outside a
layer of thickness
O(\varepsilon)
. Lemma
6.26
gives

L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

Finally,

\mathcal{S}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}]=-\nabla u^{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}-A^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u^{\varepsilon})^{T}+\lambda^{-1}(A^{\varepsilon}-I),

because the state is steady and
u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla A^{\varepsilon}=0
.
The matrices
-\nabla u^{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}
and

-A^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u^{\varepsilon})^{T}
are off-diagonal and contain the
factor
\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}
multiplied by the uniformly bounded
entries
e^{\pm b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}
. Hence their
L^{2}
norm is bounded by

C\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}.

The relaxation term is a bounded smooth function of
b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)
, so its

L^{2}
norm is
O(\varepsilon^{1/2})
. Therefore

S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}.

Using the weaker lever bound
L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}
corresponds to

\ell=0
. Since
2\beta+1>\beta+1/2
, the bulk power-law exclusion test
applies.
∎

Corollary 6.28

(Scale table for the shear layer)
.

For the family in Proposition
6.27
, the leading powers are

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\hbox{quantity}&\hbox{definition}&\hbox{scale}\\
\hline\cr W_{E}^{\varepsilon}&\displaystyle\int_{E}[P^{\varepsilon}(t)]_{+}\,dt&\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)}\\[3.99994pt]
L_{E}^{\varepsilon}&\left\|I-(A^{\varepsilon})^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}&\varepsilon^{1/2}\\[3.99994pt]
S_{E}^{\varepsilon}&\left\|\mathcal{S}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}]\right\|_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}&\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}\\[3.99994pt]
\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}&\hbox{alignment coefficient}&\leq 1\end{array}

Consequently,

\frac{W_{E}^{\varepsilon}}{L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon}}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1)}\to\infty.

If one uses only the rougher bound
L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C
, the ratio still diverges
like
\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}
.

Proof.

The table collects the estimates proved in
Proposition
6.27
. Dividing the work scale by the
lever–residual scale gives

\frac{\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)}}{\varepsilon^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}}=\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1)}.

The rough lever estimate replaces
\varepsilon^{1/2}
by
1
.
∎

Remark 6.29

(Why viscosity makes the obstruction explicit)
.

The explicit layer uses the viscous part of the momentum equation to generate
large positive pressure-free work:

P^{\varepsilon}=2\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|D(u^{\varepsilon})|^{2}\,dx.

Thus the obstruction is clearest when
\nu>0
. In the inviscid case, a
different source of positive work would be needed, typically coming from
transport or from an imposed Reynolds stress. The residual-work criterion
itself is independent of this choice; only the concrete ansatz changes.

Corollary 6.30

(Gauge-invariant failure of the shear layer)
.

The obstruction is not produced by choosing an arbitrary unfavorable defect.
The fields
(u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon})
determine the bulk momentum and
conformation residuals. The tensor
R^{\varepsilon}
in
(
6.27
) is only an explicit symmetric antidivergence of the
momentum residual. Any other symmetric representative gives the same
pressure-free work. Hence failure of admissibility is a property of the bulk
residual-work tuple of the layer, not a gauge artifact.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{R}^{\varepsilon}
be another symmetric representative of the
same momentum residual, modulo an isotropic pressure tensor and a
divergence-free symmetric tensor. Lemma
6.13
gives

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\widetilde{R}^{\varepsilon}:D(u^{\varepsilon})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R^{\varepsilon}:D(u^{\varepsilon})\,dx.

Thus the pressure-free work
P^{\varepsilon}
, and hence

(W_{E}^{\varepsilon},L_{E}^{\varepsilon},S_{E}^{\varepsilon},\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon})
, is
unchanged by the choice of representative. Corollary
6.28

therefore gives the same violation of the residual-work inequality for every
representative in the gauge class.
∎

6.7.
Admissibility versus realization

6.8.
Positive-cone correctors

The residual-work law also gives a precise necessary condition for any proposed
repair of an inadmissible profile. Changing the pressure or the
pressure-free representative cannot help; positive work can be paid only by a
negative signed component in the conformation residual.

Proposition 6.31

(Necessary signed component of any corrector)
.

Let
P(t)>0
and
G(t)\not\equiv 0
. If a correction

\widetilde{\Sigma}
makes the pressure-free work admissible, then its projection
onto
-G
must satisfy

\left\langle\widetilde{\Sigma},-\frac{G}{\left\|G\right\|_{L^{2}}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\|G\right\|_{L^{2}}}.

In particular, a correction that is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to
G
cannot pay any
positive work.

Proof.

The admissibility inequality requires

-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\widetilde{\Sigma}\,dx\geq\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha}.

Dividing by
\left\|G\right\|_{L^{2}}
gives the claimed projection lower bound.
∎

Remark 6.32

(Constructive meaning)
.

The proposition is only a necessary direction. It identifies the signed
component a correction must contain, but it does not construct a localized
positive-cone iteration realizing such a component.

6.9.
Residual-level exactness and the realization problem

The results above are exact at the residual level. Given a smooth
positive-cone profile and its residuals, the theorem decides whether the
pressure-free work can be paid by the conformation residual in the
energy–entropy balance. This does not imply realization by genuine solutions.
Realization would require producing the residual through
A=e^{B}
, localizing
the correction without losing the work balance, and passing to a limit while
preserving the energy–entropy inequality.

Remark 6.33

(Open problem)
.

Does there exist a smooth positive-cone Reynolds state satisfying the
residual-work admissibility inequality, but which cannot be approximated by
any sequence of energy–entropy admissible Oldroyd–B weak solutions? An
affirmative answer would show that the present criterion is sharp as a
necessary residual-level condition but not sufficient for realization. A
negative answer would amount to a positive-cone realization theorem.

Remark 6.34

(Connection with large-data closure)
.

For the large-data regularity problem, the theorem says that a closure
mechanism must do more than control the entropy. It must prevent positive
pressure-free work from outrunning the available lever–residual–alignment
budget. For a blow-up or non-compactness construction, the same theorem says
where to look: one needs either a growing lever, a growing conformation
residual, or increasingly efficient negative alignment.

6.10.
Conclusion of the residual-work criterion

The positive-cone parametrization
A=e^{B}
removes the positivity constraint
from the relaxed Oldroyd–B problem. The remaining admissibility condition is
a signed residual-work law. The main theorem says that positive pressure-free
Reynolds production can be admissible only if the conformation residual has
sufficient size and negative alignment against
G=I-A^{-1}
:

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}.

This gives an exact residual-level answer to the admissibility problem for
smooth positive-cone Reynolds profiles. It yields sharp pointwise costs,
closed structured-family tests, bulk and active-line power-law exclusions, and
a concrete finite-thickness shear-layer obstruction.

The finite-thickness layer shows that the law has genuine content in bulk
variables: positive work can outrun all available
lever–residual–alignment budget even when the conformation tensor remains
positive by construction. The discussion section separates this necessary
admissibility test from the harder realization problem. A profile may satisfy
the residual-work inequality and still fail to arise from a genuine weak
solution, convex-integration scheme, or localized positive-cone correction.

Two questions remain open. First, the realization problem in
Remark
6.33
asks whether residual-work admissibility can be
upgraded to approximation by true admissible Oldroyd–B states. Second, can
one construct localized logarithmic correctors whose leading conformation
residual realizes the optimal direction

-G/\left\|G\right\|_{L^{2}}
while keeping the full energy–entropy inequality stable
under passage to the limit? These questions mark the boundary between the
exact residual-level criterion proved here and a future realization theory for
positive-cone Oldroyd–B Reynolds states.

7.
A Hidden-Positivity Open Problem

The arguments above do not use the following derivative-energy question, but it is a useful guide for the stronger active-line regularity program. For smooth positive solutions define

X(t)=\left\|\rho_{s}(t)\right\|_{2}^{2},\quad I_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(\Lambda\rho_{s})\rho_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad I_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Lambda\rho)\rho_{s}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Then

(7.1)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}X}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\gamma X-cI_{1}-\frac{c}{2}I_{2}.

The Córdoba–Córdoba identity gives the one-sided positivity

2I_{1}-I_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigl(2\rho\Lambda\rho-\Lambda(\rho^{2})\bigr)\rho_{s}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\geq 0.

It remains open whether the stronger hidden positivity
I_{1}\geq 0
, or even
2I_{1}+I_{2}\geq 0
, holds for every positive trigonometric density. We have verified this in the one-mode case, in the two-mode even case, and in the three-mode even case by boundary reduction and Bernstein certificates. A degree-independent proof would require showing that the boundary reductions

p_{N}=(x-x_{0})^{2}q_{N-2},\qquad p_{N}=(1\pm x)q_{N-1}

preserve the corresponding reduced forms. We leave this as an open problem rather than as an input to the present paper.

For reference we record the finite-dimensional evidence used in this
reduction. If

\rho(s)=m+a\cos s+b\cos 2s+d\cos 3s\geq 0,

then a direct Fourier computation gives

I_{1}[\rho]=\pi\left(ma^{2}+8mb^{2}+27md^{2}+\frac{5}{2}a^{2}b+18abd\right).

Writing
x=\cos s
, the positivity constraint is

p(x)=m-b+(a-3d)x+2bx^{2}+4dx^{3}\geq 0,\qquad-1\leq x\leq 1.

Since the displayed cubic form is increasing in the mean direction,
possible negativity would have to occur on the boundary of this polynomial
cone. Interior boundary points have the form

p(x)=(x-x_{0})^{2}(R+Lx),\qquad R\geq|L|,

while endpoint boundary points have the form

p(x)=(1\mp x)q_{2}(x),\qquad q_{2}\geq 0.

Both reduced two-parameter problems admit positive Bernstein certificates,
which proves the three-mode even case.

The same boundary picture identifies the next, genuinely inductive,
degree-four tests. The endpoint stratum can be written as

p_{4}(x)=(1-x^{2})(Ax+B)^{2}+(1-x)^{2}(Cx+D)^{2},

and the interior stratum as

p_{4}(x)=(x-x_{0})^{2}\{\alpha(1-x^{2})+(\beta x+\gamma)^{2}\},\qquad|x_{0}|<1,\quad\alpha\geq 0.

Thus a degree-independent proof of
I_{1}\geq 0
would have to show that the
active reduced forms generated by these endpoint and interior factorizations
remain nonnegative on the lower-degree positive cones. This is the precise
induction step, not merely a continuation of low-mode checking.

8.
Conclusion

The active-line equation preserves positive density, has small-data global control, and relaxes finite-band positive data. Any remaining obstruction must concentrate in an infinite-tail channel visible to the one-tail clock. A finite-thickness interpretation also has to satisfy the positive-cone residual-work inequality of Theorem
6.1
.

The remaining step toward a full two-dimensional Oldroyd–B statement is a
blow-up compactness or active-line rigidity theorem showing that every
admissible noncompactness sequence has a residual-work admissible
finite-thickness active-line concentration. Until such a theorem is proved,
the result here should be read as a reduced mechanism together with an
admissibility obstruction. Any compactness theorem of this type would have to
verify, in addition to geometric concentration, the budget

W_{E}\leq(\alpha/2)\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}
for the resulting finite-thickness lift.

9.
Reduced-Energy Details

This section records the estimates behind the compressed active-line statements. They are included to make the shortened version self-contained while keeping the long frequency-ledger arguments out of the main text.

Lemma 9.1

(Basic commutator estimate)
.

Let
r>3/2
. For every mean-zero
f\in H^{r+1/2}(\mathbb{T})
,

\left|\left\langle\Lambda^{r}B(f,f),\Lambda^{r}f\right\rangle\right|\leq C_{r}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r}}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r+1/2}}^{2}.

The same estimate holds for differences:

\left\|B(f,f)-B(g,g)\right\|_{H^{r-1/2}}\leq C_{r}(\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r}}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{r}})\left\|f-g\right\|_{H^{r}}.

Proof.

Write
B(f,f)=P_{0}(f\Lambda f-(\mathcal{H}f)f_{s})
. The principal terms are handled by the one-dimensional Kato–Ponce estimate

\left\|\Lambda^{r}(fh)-f\Lambda^{r}h\right\|_{2}\leq C_{r}\bigl(\left\|f_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|h\right\|_{H^{r-1}}+ormf_{H^{r}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigr),

with
h=\Lambda f
and
h=f_{s}
. Since
r>3/2
,
H^{r}\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}
in the low-order factors after the standard interpolation between
H^{r}
and
H^{r+1/2}
. The skew part generated by the Hilbert transform cancels the apparent derivative loss in the top term; equivalently, one may use the Fourier symbol from Lemma
3.1
, whose size is bounded by
C\min\{|k|,|\ell|\}
on the nonzero interaction set. Summing the resulting Coifman–Meyer multiplier gives the stated energy bound. The difference estimate follows by polarizing
B(f,f)-B(g,g)=B(f-g,f)+B(g,f-g)
.
∎

Lemma 9.2

(Lower-barrier identity)
.

Let
\rho
be a smooth positive solution of (
1.1
). If
s_{t}
is a point where
\rho(t,\cdot)
attains its minimum, then

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\rho(t,s_{t})+\gamma(\rho(t,s_{t})-m)=-c\rho(t,s_{t})\Lambda\rho(t,s_{t})\geq 0.

Consequently Corollary
3.3
holds.

Proof.

At a minimum,
\rho_{s}(t,s_{t})=0
, so the transport contribution
-c(\mathcal{H}\rho)\rho_{s}
vanishes. The periodic representation

\Lambda\rho(s)=c_{\mathbb{T}}\,\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\rho(s)-\rho(y)}{\sin^{2}((s-y)/2)}\,\mathrm{d}y

shows
\Lambda\rho(s_{t})\leq 0
at a minimum. This gives the differential inequality and hence the explicit lower bound by comparison with
y^{\prime}+\gamma(y-m)=0
.
∎

Lemma 9.3

(High-tail inequality)
.

Let
v=P_{>K}\eta
. On any interval where
\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{r}}\leq M
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}+cm\left\|v\right\|_{H^{r+1/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\left\|P_{>K}B(P_{\leq K}\eta,P_{>K}\eta)+P_{>K}B(P_{>K}\eta,P_{\leq K}\eta)\right\|_{H^{r-1/2}}^{2}.

The analogous forced estimate has the additional term
C\left\|P_{>K}g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{H^{r-1/2}}^{2}
.

Proof.

Project (
1.2
) to frequencies
|k|>K
, pair with
\Lambda^{2r}v
, and use the damping from
cm\Lambda
. Low-low interactions have no output above
K
except through a finite leakage band, which vanishes when the low cutoff is fixed below the output scale. The high-high term is controlled by Lemma
9.1
and absorbed after the usual small-window continuation. The remaining low-high and high-low pieces are precisely the one-tail clock. The forced equation contributes
P_{>K}g^{\varepsilon}
by Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequality.
∎

Proposition 9.4

(Continuation criterion)
.

Let
r>3/2
. A positive solution can be continued past
T
provided

\inf_{t<T}\min_{s}\rho(t,s)>0,\qquad\sup_{t<T}\left\|\eta(t)\right\|_{H^{r}}<\infty.

Moreover, if the high-tail quantities in Theorem
4.2
are small uniformly for some large cutoff, then the second condition follows from the finite-dimensional low-mode bound and Lemma
9.3
.

Proof.

The first statement is the standard continuation part of Theorem
3.2
: the local existence time depends only on the positive lower barrier and the
H^{r}
norm. For the second statement, the low modes solve a finite-dimensional ODE with coefficients controlled by the positive-density bound and the already controlled Sobolev norm below the cutoff. Lemma
9.3
bounds the high tail by Gronwall in terms of its initial size and the one-tail clock. Hence the full
H^{r}
norm stays bounded, giving continuation.
∎
