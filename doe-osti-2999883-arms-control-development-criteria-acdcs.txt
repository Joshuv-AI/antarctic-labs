B.3. ProacAve Task EvaluaAons using the ACDCs
All tasks in the proac>ve venture were asked to ﬁll out an ACDC form that was structured with the
categories, levels, checkboxes at each level, and with an open comment box that could be used in
addi>on, or instead of, the check boxes. Tasks were allowed to assess their technology at its current
state, or at the expected state at the end of the original 5-year venture. These forms are aCached in
sec>on B.5, with summary tables at the start of the sec>on.

Overall, most tasks used the ACDC chart as designed and provided informa>on that was not in the
storyboards or as easily accessible in other venture repor>ng. Many provided simple comments that
helped to understand why scores were given and helped to clarify the tasks development work planned
for the Proac>ve venture. As expected, most tasks in Proac0ve are early in development, with ACDC
level 1 and 2 being most common in all categories. Higher scores were from tasks that were using an
established tool or technique but adap>ng and applying it to arms control, or tasks that chose to
evaluate their technology as they planned for it to be at the end of the Proac>ve venture. (Higher ACDC
levels require extensive integrated tes>ng of a developed technology, which was beyond the scope of
most Proac>ve tasks, which were s>ll in technology development and ini>al tes>ng stages.)

Observed Beneﬁts of Proac6ve ACDC form
The ACDC had several benefits for R&D technology development:
• Both ‘Current’ and “Planned at end of venture’ assessments are useful for evaluations. From the
 few people that assessed “Planned at end of venture”, the ACDC form and entered comments made
 it easy to see what aspects of development were planned for the venture and could be used to help
 guide future testing and assessment needs.
§ Although the equipment and data authentication categories were skipped or scored lower than
 others, they did spur people to start thinking about the issues early in technology design. Even in
 the short duration of Proactive, many tasks entered descriptions into the ACDC chart that indicated
 that doing the ACDC evaluation spurred them to talk to experts in authentication so that they could
 implement design changes that would improve authentication early. This is an incredible benefit to
 efficiency and ultimate functionality--It is easier to design for arms control requirements from the
 start.
§ Having a ‘system’ category, although challenging for some tasks to use, helped others recognize that
 their technology will be part of a larger verification system and would need to be evaluated in
 combination with other technologies. This helped task leads think about integration issues earlier in
 design.
§ The combina>on of Storyboard, providing a technical overview of the technology, and the ACDC
 providing a ‘where is the development at, and what needs to be done” aspect to the diﬀerent tasks
 was an eﬀec>ve task status update.

Improvements needed in the ACDCs:
  § What was meant by ‘Use cases’ and technology descrip>ons in ACDC needs to be beCer
 described and beCer aligned with the Proac>ve Storyboard. Many were not wriCen for a non-
 SMEs to understand the current state of the technology. Examples of what was meant by ‘use
 case’ would have helped.
  § Clarification in the Instructions for how to score were needed--instructions should indicate that
 a level should be checked if the task was working at that level in the category, rather than only
 checking if they were ‘complete’ in the level/category. This would better reflect that the ACDCs
 are not clear benchmarks/levels, but a process. (Note: in summary tables, scores were changes
 to reflect this—for example, if any task entered a score of 0, it was changed to 1 for comparison
 of the set.)
  § Instructions and form fields need to help tasks better define the aspects of the technology that
 they are accessing. For example, if a task were using existing technologies combined with new
 data processing, the scores were a combination of the technology and the new algorithms.
 Ideally, a task would provide an assessment for the technology, and one for the new data
 processing algorithm used with the technologies. In the second evaluation, category E for the
 combined system would be used extensively. (These are the cases that led to majority of scores
 of 3 or above.) This type of component/system assessment is extremely useful to knowing
 actual status of technologies and the growing system-level combinations.
  § Additions would be needed to make the ACDCs more applicable for tools/methods used to help
 develop arms control technologies--ACDCs did not work well for tools used for the development
 of other technologies.
  § Additions would be needed to better address software/algorithm tasks. While the checkboxes
 worked well for FA1 & FA2, they did not work as well for FA3 & FA4. Descriptions and
 checkboxes more applicable to software would improve the utility of the ACDCs. Improved
 checkboxes would be needed for non-cooperative arms control technology assessments, but
 this would not be a large effort.
