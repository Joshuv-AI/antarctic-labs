Explicit bounds for Dickman’s function
Andreas Weingartner

Abstract
. We establish numerically explicit upper and lower bounds for Dickman’s function ρ ⁡ ( u ) \rho(u) , resulting in estimates with a relative error of less than 0.005 / u 2 0.005/u^{2} for all u ≥ 5 u\geq 5 . This allows for an approximate evaluation of ρ ⁡ ( u ) \rho(u) without the need to solve the delay differential equation numerically.

Introduction

Dickman’s function
\rho(u)
is defined as the continuous solution to the delay differential equation

u\rho^{\prime}(u)+\rho(u-1)=0
with initial condition
\rho(u)=1
for
0\leq u\leq 1
.
It represents the natural density of integers
n
whose largest prime factor does not exceed
n^{1/u}

[
3
]
.

For
u>1
, let
\xi=\xi(u)
be the unique positive solution of
e^{\xi}=1+u\xi
and put
\xi(1)=0
.
Define

I(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{e^{z}-1}{z}dz.

De Bruijn
[
2
]
showed that
\rho(u)\sim\tilde{\rho}(u)
as
u\to\infty
, where

\tilde{\rho}(u):=\frac{\exp\{\gamma-u\xi+I(\xi)\}}{\sqrt{2\pi I^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}}

and
\gamma
is Euler’s constant.
Alladi
[
1
]
sharpened this to
\rho(u)=\tilde{\rho}(u)(1+O(1/u))
.
Our goal is to make the estimate entirely explicit,
providing a convenient way to obtain approximate values of
\rho(u)

without the need to solve the delay differential equation numerically.

.

For
u>1
we have

\tilde{\rho}(u)\left(1-\frac{1}{12u}\right)<\rho(u)<\tilde{\rho}(u)\left(1-\frac{1}{12u(1+\frac{1}{\log u})}\right).

Dickman

]
showed that
\rho(u)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Psi(x,x^{1/u})/x

\Psi(x,y)

[

\Psi(x,y)\geq x\rho(u)

, where

Gorodetsky
[

confirmed this for

[

Together with Theorem

[

[

To compute
\tilde{\rho}(u)
(or
\log\tilde{\rho}(u)
when
u
is large) and
\alpha(u)
, note that

I(\xi)=\mathrm{Ei}(\xi)-\gamma-\log\xi,

where
\mathrm{Ei}(z)
is the exponential integral (
ExpIntegralEi[z]
in Mathematica),
and

\xi=\xi(u)=-W_{-1}\left(-\frac{e^{-1/u}}{u}\right)-\frac{1}{u},

where
W_{-1}(z)
is the lower of the two real branches, for
-1/e\leq z<0
,
of the Lambert W function (
ProductLog[-1,z]
in Mathematica).

Buchstab’s function
\omega(u)
is the unique continuous solution, for
u\geq 1
, of the delay differential equation

(u\omega(u))^{\prime}=\omega(u-1)
, with initial condition
\omega(u)=1/u
for
1\leq u\leq 2
.
It arises in connection with the distribution of integers free of small prime factors
[
10
, Ch. III.6]
.
Corollary
1
shows that
\tilde{\rho}(u)

can serve as a convenient and numerically explicit upper bound for
|\omega(u)-e^{-\gamma}|
,
although it is not of the smallest possible order of magnitude, according to Tenenbaum
[
9
, Lem. 4]
.

Corollary 1
.

For
u\geq 1
, we have

|\omega(u)-e^{-\gamma}|<\rho(u)<\tilde{\rho}(u),\qquad|\omega^{\prime}(u)|\leq\rho(u)<\tilde{\rho}(u).

Proof.

The inequality
\rho(u)<\tilde{\rho}(u)
follows from Theorem
1
.
Tenenbaum
[
10
, Thm. III.6.6]
shows that
|\omega^{\prime}(u)|\leq\rho(u)
,
which implies

|\omega(u)-e^{-\gamma}|=|\int_{u}^{\infty}\omega^{\prime}(t)dt|\leq\int_{u}^{\infty}\rho(t)dt<\int_{u}^{\infty}-\rho^{\prime}(t)dt=\rho(u),

where the last inequality follows from

\rho(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{t-1}^{t}\rho(v)dv<\frac{1}{t}\rho(t-1)=-\rho^{\prime}(t)\qquad(t>1).

∎
