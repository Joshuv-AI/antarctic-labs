Localized Centered Second-Chaos Operators
Guangqian Zhao

Abstract
We prove a localized continuous-frequency operator estimate for centered Gaussian chaoses of order two. The result applies to operator-valued centered second chaoses, including Wick-centered same-family variants, between Hilbert spaces. In the model, two Gaussian frequency legs at scale N N , an input leg at scale Q Q , and an output leg at scale M M are coupled through a soft incidence kernel; non-orthogonal Gaussian profiles are represented by covariance synthesis maps. The proof combines four oriented flattenings, rectangular non-commutative Khintchine inequalities, soft-incidence Schatten bounds, and Sobolev–Besov dyadic summation. The time lift gives L p L^{p} operator convergence, while a Galerkin stabilization hypothesis gives pathwise full-cutoff convergence by the first Borel–Cantelli lemma. Under 𝒢 ⁡ ( N ) ≲ N − Γ \mathcal{G}(N)\lesssim N^{-\Gamma} one obtains the window Γ > d 2 , s < λ + Γ − d , max ⁡ { 0 , d − Γ } < σ < λ + Γ − d . \Gamma>\frac{d}{2},\qquad s<\lambda+\Gamma-d,\qquad\max\{0,d-\Gamma\}<\sigma<\lambda+\Gamma-d. The theorem applies to the near-output Wick-centered branch of localized paracontrolled resonant products on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} .



Keywords.
Centered Gaussian chaos; non-commutative Khintchine inequality; Schatten estimates; continuous frequency analysis; soft incidence kernels; paracontrolled operators; pathwise cutoff convergence; power-law envelopes.

The continuous replacement in the table is local in physical space: it uses the soft incidence kernel generated by a physical cutoff, not an unweighted translation-invariant global kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The estimate isolates the centered second-chaos component that remains after the deterministic covariance contraction has been separated. In applications, the Gaussian frequency legs are encoded by covariance synthesis maps, the four Schatten flattening bounds are verified for the localized kernel, and the dyadic Sobolev–Besov summation gives the operator bound; Corollary

The argument is in the spirit of the row/column and flattening estimates of Deng–Nahmod–Yue
[
4

. The closest periodic/discrete mixed paracontrolled framework is the author’s preprint
[
11

. The present paper keeps only the centered second-chaos branch, replaces lattice incidence by a localized continuous-frequency soft incidence, and uses covariance synthesis maps for non-orthogonal smooth frequency profiles.

The proof proceeds through Wick centering, covariance pullback, four flattenings, soft-incidence Schatten bounds, dyadic assembly, and Borel–Cantelli convergence.

Smooth frequency projections are not assumed independent. The two stochastic legs are represented by bounded maps from frequency
L^{2}
spaces into covariance Hilbert spaces, and complete Wick centering leaves a second homogeneous Wiener-chaos object. The abstract estimate is not tied to the later Volterra form: it starts from a finite-rank operator-valued centered second chaos

Z_{\mathbf{H}}=\sum_{i,j}g_{i}h_{j}H_{ij}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E},

or, in the same-family case, from

\sum_{i,j}H^{\rm sym}_{ij}\bigl(G_{i}G_{j}-\mathbb{E}[G_{i}G_{j}]\bigr).

The deterministic input is Schatten control of the four Hilbert-space flattenings; the soft-incidence kernel and Volterra integration provide the continuous-frequency model and its paracontrolled realization.

The four flattenings are the leaves of the two-step row/column Khintchine tree:

(a,b,c)\to e,\qquad(a,b,e)\to c,\qquad(a,c)\to(b,e),\qquad(b,c)\to(a,e).

After the continuous frequency variables are inserted, these become

(\ell,\eta,q)\to n,\qquad(\ell,\eta,n)\to q,\qquad(\ell,q)\to(\eta,n),\qquad(\eta,q)\to(\ell,n).

The first two directions retain one high-frequency degree and one external shell; the mixed directions see only the input/output phase-space volumes. After the admissible domination
Q,M\lesssim N
, this gives the final
M^{d/2}+Q^{d/2}
profile rather than a product profile.

Only the completely centered branch is treated. The final application concerns the near-output sector
M\leq C_{\rm out}N
of

[I_{\lambda}(w\prec_{\rm loc}\Psi_{1})\circ_{\rm loc}\Psi_{2}]^{\circ},

where
∘
denotes complete Wick centering. Deterministic contractions and localization tails are left to separate estimates. The pathwise step uses only dyadic summability and the first Borel–Cantelli lemma.

Main theorem

Theorem 1.1

(Main theorem, condensed form)
.

Assume the localized continuous Gaussian model described in Section

, together with the time-regularity and cutoff convergence hypotheses used in Section
3
. Suppose that the centered dyadic amplitude obeys the power-law envelope

\mathcal{G}(N)\leq C_{G}N^{-\Gamma},\qquad\Gamma>\frac{d}{2}.

Let

and choose exponents

in the strict window

s<\lambda+\Gamma-d,\qquad\max\{0,d-\Gamma\}<\sigma<\lambda+\Gamma-d.

Then the localized centered Galerkin operators are uniformly bounded in every finite
L^{p}(\Omega)
as maps

\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma}\longrightarrow C_{T}H^{s-\lambda}\cap L_{T}^{1}B^{\sigma-\lambda}_{2,\infty},

where
\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma}
is the dyadic model of
C_{T}L^{2}\cap L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma}_{2,\infty}
defined in (

L^{p}(\Omega)
in the corresponding operator norm. Under the finite-state Galerkin stabilization hypothesis, the full cutoff sequence also converges pathwise on one probability-one event. The same operator estimate applies to same-family Wick squares after complete Wick centering and order-two decoupling.

The

1.3
The localized model and centered blocks

1.3.1
Dyadic geometry and physical localization

Let

\mathbb{D}:=\{2^{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

with the usual inhomogeneous convention at scale one. Fix constants

0<c_{\rm ap}<1,\qquad C_{\rm out}\geq 1,

and define the admissible dyadic set

\mathfrak{D}:=\{(N,Q,M)\in\mathbb{D}^{3}:Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N,\ M\leq C_{\rm out}N\}.

(1.1)

Finite low-frequency exceptions may be added to
\mathfrak{D}
without changing any statement below, since they only modify the constants. Thus the inhomogeneous scale one causes no separate endpoint issue.

Choose fixed real-valued smooth cutoffs

\rho,\chi_{\rm in},\chi_{\rm out}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\chi_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\chi_{\rm out}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 3

with

\operatorname{supp}\rho\subset\{\xi:r_{0}\leq|\xi|\leq R_{0}\}

for fixed
0<r_{0}<R_{0}<\infty
. Put

\rho_{N}(\xi)=\rho(\xi/N),\qquad\chi_{Q}(q)=\chi_{\rm in}(q/Q),\qquad\chi_{M}(n)=\chi_{\rm out}(n/M).

Thus

\left\lVert\rho_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{d/2},\qquad\left\lVert\chi_{Q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim Q^{d/2},\qquad\left\lVert\chi_{M}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim M^{d/2}.

Throughout the deterministic incidence estimates, expressions such as
\rho_{N}^{2}
and
\chi_{Q}^{2}
may equivalently be read as absolute squares. All estimates are invariant under replacing the cutoffs by their absolute values inside the Schur and Hilbert–Schmidt majorants.

Let
k\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In applications
k
is typically the Fourier transform of a compactly supported physical localizer, hence is Schwartz and in particular belongs to
L^{1}\cap L^{2}
. The factor

k(n-q-\ell-\eta)

is the soft form of the incidence
n=q+\ell+\eta
.

All frequency variables are integrated with respect to Lebesgue measure. The only properties of the incidence kernel used below are the
L^{1}
Schur control and the
L^{2}
Hilbert–Schmidt control. The changes of variables

z=n-q-\ell-\eta,\qquad\eta=n-q-\ell-z,\qquad\ell=n-q-\eta-z,

and their variants are affine maps with determinant
\pm 1
. Thus the continuous estimates below contain no hidden Jacobian loss relative to the soft-incidence profile. We work with real Hilbert spaces to keep the notation aligned with real isonormal processes; the complex Fourier-space case follows by applying the estimates to the underlying real Hilbert spaces, with at most universal changes in constants.

Normalized order-zero amplitudes

Fix an even integer
K>4d
. For a smooth amplitude

m=m_{N,Q,M}(\ell,\eta,q,n)
on a fixed enlargement of the dyadic support, set

\left\lVert m\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{M}^{K}_{N,Q,M}}:=\max_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+|\gamma|+|\delta|\leq K}N^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}Q^{|\gamma|}M^{|\delta|}\left\lVert\partial_{\ell}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}\partial_{q}^{\gamma}\partial_{n}^{\delta}m\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

(1.2)

This is a uniform
C^{K}
norm after the normalization

(u,v,x,y)=(\ell/N,\eta/N,q/Q,n/M).

For each cutoff
\Lambda\in\mathbb{D}\cup\{\infty\}
, let

c_{\Lambda}^{(1)},c_{\Lambda}^{(2)}
be smooth Galerkin multipliers satisfying

|c_{\Lambda}^{(j)}|\leq 1,\qquad c_{\Lambda}^{(j)}(\xi)=1\text{ for }|\xi|\leq\Lambda,\qquad c_{\Lambda}^{(j)}(\xi)=0\text{ for }|\xi|\geq 2\Lambda,

and
c_{\infty}^{(j)}\equiv 1
. Define

\rho_{\Lambda,N}^{(j)}=\rho_{N}c_{\Lambda}^{(j)}.

For the pathwise full-sequence results we use the standard rescaled dyadic Galerkin family: there are fixed smooth profiles
c^{(j)}
such that
c_{\Lambda}^{(j)}(\xi)=c^{(j)}(\xi/\Lambda)
for finite
\Lambda
. In that fixed-profile situation, for each fixed
N
, the family
\{\rho_{\Lambda,N}^{(j)}\}_{\Lambda\in\mathbb{D}\cup\{\infty\}}
has only finitely many distinct restrictions to the shell
|\xi|\sim N
and is eventually equal to
\rho_{N}
. The
L^{p}
convergence statements below only need convergence of the multipliers and synthesis maps; the finite-state property is used only for the pathwise full-cutoff upgrade.

Let
K_{\mathrm{det}}(N)>0
be a deterministic multiplier envelope. For

u=(t,s)\in\Delta_{T}:=\{0\leq s\leq t\leq T\}
, define the frequency four-linear kernel

\displaystyle H^{0}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u}(\ell,\eta,q,n):={}

\displaystyle K_{\mathrm{det}}(N)\rho_{\Lambda,N}^{(1)}(\ell)\rho_{\Lambda,N}^{(2)}(\eta)\chi_{Q}(q)\chi_{M}(n)

(1.3)

\displaystyle\times m_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u}(\ell,\eta,q,n)k(n-q-\ell-\eta).

We impose the normalized size bound

\sup_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u}\left\lVert m_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{M}^{K}_{N,Q,M}}\leq C_{m}.

(1.4)

1.3.3
Covariance synthesis maps and Wick centering

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}
be real separable Hilbert spaces with independent isonormal Gaussian processes

W_{j}:\mathcal{H}_{j}\to L^{2}(\Omega),\qquad j=1,2.

We use the standard Gaussian Hilbert space and multiple Wiener integral conventions; see, for example,
[
6
,
8
]
.
For each
N
and time, let

S_{1,\Lambda,N,s}:L^{2}_{\ell}\to\mathcal{H}_{1},\qquad S_{2,\Lambda,N,t}:L^{2}_{\eta}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}

be bounded covariance synthesis maps. Assume envelopes
\mathcal{A}_{1}(N),\mathcal{A}_{2}(N)>0
such that

\sup_{\Lambda,s}\left\lVert S_{1,\Lambda,N,s}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\leq C_{S}\mathcal{A}_{1}(N),\qquad\sup_{\Lambda,t}\left\lVert S_{2,\Lambda,N,t}\right\rVert_{\rm op}\leq C_{S}\mathcal{A}_{2}(N).

(1.5)

The total centered amplitude is

\mathcal{G}(N):=K_{\mathrm{det}}(N)\mathcal{A}_{1}(N)\mathcal{A}_{2}(N).

(1.6)

Let
\mathbf{H}^{0}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u}
be the four-linear form associated with the kernel (
1.3
). Its covariance pullback is

\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u}(a,b,f,g):=\mathbf{H}^{0}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u}(S_{1,\Lambda,N,s}^{*}a,S_{2,\Lambda,N,t}^{*}b,f,g).

(1.7)

For independent Gaussian legs, the centered block is defined by

\left\langle B^{\circ}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}(u)f,g\right\rangle:=I_{1,1}\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u;f,g}\bigr),

(1.8)

where
I_{1,1}
is the decoupled first-by-first Wiener integral on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{H}_{2}
.

If the two stochastic legs come from the same isonormal process
W
on a covariance Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we instead define

\left\langle B^{\circ}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}(u)f,g\right\rangle:=I_{2}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u;f,g}\bigr).

(1.9)

Here
\operatorname{Sym}
denotes orthogonal symmetrization in the two covariance legs. We fix the following finite-coordinate convention. If
(e_{i})
is an orthonormal basis of the covariance Hilbert space,
G_{i}=W(e_{i})
, and
a=(a_{ij})
is a symmetric finite matrix, then

I_{2}\left(\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\right)=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\bigl(G_{i}G_{j}-\mathbb{E}[G_{i}G_{j}]\bigr)=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(G_{i}G_{j}-\delta_{ij}).

Equivalently, for a non-symmetric finite matrix one first replaces
a
by
a^{\rm sym}_{ij}=(a_{ij}+a_{ji})/2
and then applies the preceding formula. Other common Wiener–Itô normalizations differ only by fixed
2!
-type constants; throughout this paper those constants are absorbed into the universal constants in the estimates.

Thus, if
H^{\rm sym}_{ij}(f,g)
are the coordinates of
\operatorname{Sym}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u;f,g}
, then the same-family block is

\sum_{i,j}H^{\rm sym}_{ij}(f,g)\,\bigl(G_{i}G_{j}-\mathbb{E}[G_{i}G_{j}]\bigr).

The subtraction is the full scalar covariance pairing in the covariance Hilbert space, not merely a diagonal subtraction in the original frequency variables. Non-diagonal covariances in physical Fourier coordinates are first pulled back to an orthonormal basis of the covariance Hilbert space; the covariance matrix is encoded in the synthesis maps
S_{j,\Lambda,N,t}
. No orthogonality of smooth frequency projectors is imposed. Complete Wick centering is performed before the operator estimate is applied, and the same-family estimate follows from the Banach-valued order-two decoupling estimate in Lemma
2.3
. Since symmetrization only exchanges the two high covariance legs, the deterministic soft-incidence estimates below control the symmetrized same-family kernel by the same profiles.

Four flattenings and continuous incidence bounds

2.1
The abstract four-flattening inequality

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}
be real separable Hilbert spaces. For a finite-rank four-linear form

\mathbf{H}:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{R}
, define the four oriented flattenings by Riesz representation:

\displaystyle\left\langle F_{1}(a\otimes b\otimes c),e\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}(a,b,c,e),

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{A}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle F_{2}(a\otimes b\otimes e),c\right\rangle_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}(a,b,c,e),

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{A}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{C},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\langle F_{3}(a\otimes c),b\otimes e\right\rangle_{\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}(a,b,c,e),

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{A}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{E},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle F_{4}(b\otimes c),a\otimes e\right\rangle_{\mathcal{A}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}(a,b,c,e),

\displaystyle F_{4}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{E}.

(2.4)

We use the following standard Schatten ideal facts; see, for example,
[
10
,
9
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Schatten ideal, interpolation, and tensor regrouping)
.

Let
U,V,U^{\prime},V^{\prime}
be Hilbert spaces.

(i)

If
T\in\mathcal{L}(U,V)\cap\mathfrak{S}_{2}(U,V)
and
2\leq r<\infty
, then

\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\rm op}^{1-2/r}\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{2}}^{2/r}.

(2.5)

(ii)

If
A\in\mathcal{L}(V,V^{\prime})
,
B\in\mathcal{L}(U^{\prime},U)
, and
T\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V)
, then

\left\lVert ATB\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})}\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\rm op}\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V)}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\rm op}.

(2.6)

(iii)

The canonical regroupings of Hilbert tensor products, for instance

((\mathcal{A}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{B})\otimes_{2}\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathcal{A}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{C}
, are unitary. Consequently Hilbert–Schmidt norms are unchanged by a mere reorientation of tensor legs. Multiplication by unimodular factors on any frequency leg is also unitary and hence preserves every Schatten norm.

Proof.

For (i), let
(s_{j}(T))
be the singular values. Since

\sum_{j}s_{j}(T)^{r}\leq\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\rm op}^{r-2}\sum_{j}s_{j}(T)^{2}
, taking the
r
-th root gives (
2.5
). Statement (ii) is the standard two-sided ideal property of Schatten classes, first for finite-rank operators and then by completion. Statement (iii) follows from the defining universal property of Hilbert tensor products and from Fubini’s theorem for the concrete
L^{2}
models used in the frequency variables.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(Finite compression of Schatten flattenings)
.

Let
2\leq r<\infty
and let
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4}
be the four flattenings associated with a four-linear form
\mathbf{H}
, with
F_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
. Let
P_{\mathcal{A}}^{(m)},P_{\mathcal{B}}^{(m)},P_{\mathcal{C}}^{(m)},P_{\mathcal{E}}^{(m)}
be finite-rank orthogonal projections increasing strongly to the identities. Define the compressed form

\mathbf{H}_{m}(a,b,c,e):=\mathbf{H}(P_{\mathcal{A}}^{(m)}a,P_{\mathcal{B}}^{(m)}b,P_{\mathcal{C}}^{(m)}c,P_{\mathcal{E}}^{(m)}e).

If
F_{j,m}
are the four flattenings of
\mathbf{H}_{m}
, then

\left\lVert F_{j,m}-F_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad j=1,2,3,4.

Proof.

For example,

F_{1,m}=P_{\mathcal{E}}^{(m)}F_{1}(P_{\mathcal{A}}^{(m)}\otimes P_{\mathcal{B}}^{(m)}\otimes P_{\mathcal{C}}^{(m)}),

and the other orientations have the analogous left and right compressions. If
T\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V)
and
P_{m},Q_{m}
are finite-rank orthogonal projections converging strongly to the identities, then
P_{m}TQ_{m}\to T
in
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
. This follows first for finite-rank
T
and then by approximation in
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
. Applying this observation to each flattening proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(Banach-valued decoupling for Wick squares)
.

Let
B
be a separable Banach space. Let
(x_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq m}\subset B
, let
(G_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
be a standard real Gaussian family, and let
(g_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
and
(h_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
be two independent standard Gaussian copies. Then, for every
1\leq p<\infty
,

\left\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}x_{ij}\bigl(G_{i}G_{j}-\mathbb{E}[G_{i}G_{j}]\bigr)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;B)}\leq C_{\rm dec}\left\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}x_{ij}g_{i}h_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;B)},

(2.7)

where
C_{\rm dec}
depends only on the order-two decoupling theorem, and not on
m
, on the Banach space
B
, or on
p
. In particular, after symmetrizing the same-family kernel, a completely Wick-centered square may be estimated through its decoupled version.

Proof.

This is the standard order-two Banach-valued decoupling inequality for Gaussian homogeneous chaoses; see, for instance,
[
3
, Chapter 3]
. The subtraction of
\mathbb{E}[G_{i}G_{j}]
removes the zeroth-chaos component and leaves the second homogeneous Wiener-chaos projection. The estimate is finite-dimensional here and is applied below with
B=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
, or with the corresponding separable finite-shell operator space before passage to the limit.
∎

Theorem 2.4

(Four-flattening second-chaos inequality)
.

Let
2\leq p\leq r<\infty
, and suppose
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4}\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
. Let
Z_{\mathbf{H}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E}
be the decoupled second-chaos operator associated with
\mathbf{H}
. Then

\left\|\,\left\lVert Z_{\mathbf{H}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E}}\,\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq Cr\sum_{j=1}^{4}\left\lVert F_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}},

(2.8)

where
C
is universal; the displayed linear factor
r
is the only dependence on the chosen Schatten exponent in this estimate. The map
\mathbf{H}\mapsto Z_{\mathbf{H}}
extends continuously from finite-rank forms to all forms with
F_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
. The same conclusion holds for the completely Wick-centered same-family chaos, up to the universal order-two decoupling constant.

Proof.

Choose finite orthonormal systems
(a_{i})
and
(b_{j})
supporting a finite-rank approximation and write

Z=\sum_{i,j}g_{i}h_{j}H_{ij},\qquad H_{ij}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}),

with independent standard real Gaussian families. Condition on
(g_{i})
and set

S_{j}(g)=\sum_{i}g_{i}H_{ij}.

For each fixed realization of
(g_{i})
, the rectangular non-commutative Khintchine inequality in the
h
-variables, in the Schatten-valued form of Lust-Piquard–Pisier
[
7
]
and Pisier
[
9
]
, gives

\left\lVert Z(g,\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{r}_{h}\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq C\sqrt{r}\bigl(\left\lVert\mathscr{S}(g)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}+\left\lVert\mathscr{T}(g)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\bigr),

Here this is only used in the finite-dimensional rectangular form

\left\|\sum_{j}h_{j}A_{j}\right\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r})}\leq C\sqrt{r}\left(\left\|\left(\sum_{j}A_{j}A_{j}^{*}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}+\left\|\left(\sum_{j}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\right),

with rectangular operators
A_{j}
between Hilbert spaces.
The block row and block column are

\mathscr{S}(g):\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E},\qquad\mathscr{S}(g)(b_{j}\otimes c)=S_{j}(g)c,

and

\mathscr{T}(g):\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{E},\qquad\mathscr{T}(g)c=\sum_{j}b_{j}\otimes S_{j}(g)c.

Taking
L^{r}
in the
g
-variables, we estimate these two random Schatten norms once more by non-commutative Khintchine. Write
\mathscr{S}(g)=\sum_{i}g_{i}T_{i}
, with

T_{i}:\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E},\qquad T_{i}(b_{j}\otimes c)=H_{ij}c.

The two square functions generated by this block row are

\left(\sum_{i}T_{i}T_{i}^{*}\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\sum_{i}T_{i}^{*}T_{i}\right)^{1/2},

and the flattening identities are

F_{1}F_{1}^{*}=\sum_{i}T_{i}T_{i}^{*},\qquad F_{4}^{*}F_{4}=\sum_{i}T_{i}^{*}T_{i}.

Similarly, write
\mathscr{T}(g)=\sum_{i}g_{i}U_{i}
, where

U_{i}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{B}\otimes_{2}\mathcal{E},\qquad U_{i}c=\sum_{j}b_{j}\otimes H_{ij}c.

The second pair of square functions is

\left(\sum_{i}U_{i}U_{i}^{*}\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\sum_{i}U_{i}^{*}U_{i}\right)^{1/2},

and the corresponding flattening identities are

F_{3}F_{3}^{*}=\sum_{i}U_{i}U_{i}^{*},\qquad F_{2}F_{2}^{*}=\sum_{i}U_{i}^{*}U_{i}.

Thus the two-step row/column conditioning produces exactly the four oriented flattenings. Each Khintchine application costs
C\sqrt{r}
, hence

\left\lVert Z\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r})}\lesssim r\sum_{j=1}^{4}\left\lVert F_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

Since
p\leq r
and
\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\rm op}\leq\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}
, this proves (
2.8
).

For general Schatten flattenings, choose finite-rank compressions of the four Hilbert legs. Lemma
2.2
gives convergence of the compressed flattenings in
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
, and the finite-rank estimates above therefore make the compressed random operators Cauchy in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}))
. Because
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{E}
are separable Hilbert spaces,
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
is separable in the operator norm. The limit is independent of the chosen compressions. This defines the extension of
\mathbf{H}\mapsto Z_{\mathbf{H}}
and preserves (
2.8
). The same-family statement follows by applying Lemma
2.3
, with values in the separable compact-operator space, before the decoupled estimate.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Covariance/Bessel transfer)
.

Let
U_{1}:\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{A}
and
U_{2}:\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathcal{B}
be bounded, and define

Then, for

(2.9)

Proof.

For

\widetilde{F}_{j}=F_{j}(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\operatorname{Id}).

For the mixed orientations,

\widetilde{F}_{3}=(U_{2}^{*}\otimes\operatorname{Id})F_{3}(U_{1}\otimes\operatorname{Id}),\qquad\widetilde{F}_{4}=(U_{1}^{*}\otimes\operatorname{Id})F_{4}(U_{2}\otimes\operatorname{Id}).

Apply Lemma
2.1

Lemma 2.6

(Soft-incidence Schur fibers)
.

Let the cutoffs be as in Section

3
Time lift, dyadic assembly, and pathwise convergence

3.1
Time-dependent centered block estimate

We assume time regularity only through normalized coefficient and covariance-map increments.

Assumption 3.1

(Time increments and cutoff stability)
.

For every sufficiently small
0<\theta\leq\theta_{0}
, there are constants
C_{\theta}
and nonnegative scales

\Theta_{0}(N),\Theta_{1}(N),\Theta_{2}(N)
such that, uniformly in all cutoffs,

\displaystyle\left\lVert m_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,u}-m_{\Lambda,N,Q,M,v}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{M}^{K}_{N,Q,M}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\theta}\Theta_{0}(N)^{\theta}d_{\Delta}(u,v)^{\theta},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{1,\Lambda,N,s}-S_{1,\Lambda,N,s^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{\rm op}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\theta}\mathcal{A}_{1}(N)\Theta_{1}(N)^{\theta}|s-s^{\prime}|^{\theta},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{2,\Lambda,N,t}-S_{2,\Lambda,N,t^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{\rm op}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\theta}\mathcal{A}_{2}(N)\Theta_{2}(N)^{\theta}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\theta}.

(3.3)

Here
d_{\Delta}((t,s),(t^{\prime},s^{\prime}))=|t-t^{\prime}|+|s-s^{\prime}|
. We also assume the absorption condition: for every
\varepsilon>0
one can choose
\theta>0
so small that

\bigl(1+\Theta_{0}(N)+\Theta_{1}(N)+\Theta_{2}(N)\bigr)^{\theta}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}.

(3.4)

Finally, for every fixed
(N,Q,M)
the normalized amplitudes and synthesis maps converge in the corresponding
C^{\theta}
norms as
\Lambda\to\infty
. For the pathwise full-sequence theorem we impose the standard Galerkin-state condition: for every fixed dyadic triple, the family

(m_{\Lambda,N,Q,M},S_{1,\Lambda,N,\cdot},S_{2,\Lambda,N,\cdot})

assumes at most
L_{\rm cut}
distinct states, with
L_{\rm cut}
independent of the scales, and is eventually equal to the limiting state. For the standard rescaled dyadic Galerkin multipliers introduced above, this is a finite relative-position statement: for fixed
N
, the support of
\rho_{N}
lies in
\{r_{0}N\leq|\xi|\leq R_{0}N\}
, so the ratio
\Lambda/N
has only
O_{r_{0},R_{0}}(1)
relevant dyadic positions on this shell and the cutoff becomes identically one once
\Lambda\geq R_{0}N
. If one only assumes convergence rather than eventual stabilization, the
L^{p}
convergence statement remains valid, while the full-sequence pathwise theorem below should be read with this finite-state hypothesis included.

Polynomial time regularity of the normalized amplitudes and synthesis maps is a standard sufficient condition for (
3.1
)–(
3.4
): small Holder exponents absorb polynomial losses into
N^{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 3.2

(Two-parameter Banach-valued chaining)
.

Let
X(u)
,
u\in\Delta_{T}
, take values in a separable Banach space
B
. Suppose that for some
p_{0}\geq 2
,
0<\theta\leq 1
, and constants
R_{0},R_{1}
,

\sup_{u}\left\lVert X(u)\right\rVert_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega;B)}\leq R_{0},\qquad\left\lVert X(u)-X(v)\right\rVert_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega;B)}\leq R_{1}d_{\Delta}(u,v)^{\theta}.

If
p_{0}>4/\theta
, then
X
has a separable continuous modification and, for every
2\leq p\leq p_{0}
,

\left\|\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{C(\Delta_{T};B)}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p,p_{0},\theta,T}R_{0}+R_{1}.

(3.5)

Proof.

Use dyadic rational grids in the two-dimensional simplex. At level
j
there are
O_{T}(2^{2j})
grid points. Choose a parent in the coarser grid at distance
O(2^{-j})
, remaining inside the simplex. The
L^{p_{0}}
norm of the maximal increment at level
j
is bounded by

CR_{1}2^{-j\theta}2^{2j/p_{0}}.

The series is summable because
p_{0}>4/\theta
. Chaining on the rational skeleton and completion give a continuous modification. Minkowski’s inequality yields (
3.5
).
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Uniform centered dyadic block)
.

Assume (
1.4
), (

), and Assumption
3.1
. For every finite

Under the Galerkin-state condition in Assumption
3.1
, the same estimate holds with the supremum over
\Lambda
inside the

norm, with a constant depending additionally on the uniform finite-state bound
L_{\rm cut}
. For each fixed dyadic triple,

Proof.

At fixed

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{u}-\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{v}={}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{H}^{0}_{u}-\mathbf{H}^{0}_{v})(S_{1,u}^{*}\cdot,S_{2,u}^{*}\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)

∎

For

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{H}^{(\varepsilon)}(\beta,\sigma)

with the sums restricted by (

Lemma 3.4

Let
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda,\mathcal{E}}
denote the operator formed by summing only over a finite or cofinite set of triples
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathfrak{D}
. Repeating the proof of Theorem
3.5
with the sums restricted to
\mathcal{E}
gives, for every finite
p\geq 2
,

\left\|\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda,\mathcal{E}}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma}\to\mathbb{Y}_{T}^{\beta,\zeta}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p,T,\varepsilon}\mathfrak{S}_{H,\mathcal{E}}^{(\varepsilon)}(\beta,\sigma)+\mathfrak{S}_{B,\mathcal{E}}^{(\varepsilon)}(\zeta,\sigma),

where the two majorants are defined as in (
3.11
)–(
3.12
), but with the dyadic sums restricted to
\mathcal{E}
. Since the full majorants are finite, their tails tend to zero as
\mathcal{F}\nearrow\mathfrak{D}
. Therefore the finite-shell operators form a Cauchy sequence in the displayed
L^{p}
space. The limit is the full operator by definition of the dyadic series in operator norm in
L^{p}
. The operator norm of the limit is the
L^{p}
limit, along a subsequence if necessary, of measurable finite-shell norm variables; hence it is measurable. This lemma only supplies the
L^{p}
construction and measurability. The pathwise construction is proved later under the additional blockwise Borel–Cantelli bound of Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Theorem 3.5

(
L^{p}
centered operator)
.

Fix
p\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. If

\mathfrak{S}_{H}^{(\varepsilon)}(\beta,\sigma)+\mathfrak{S}_{B}^{(\varepsilon)}(\zeta,\sigma)<\infty,

(3.14)

then
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}
is a well-defined random bounded operator

\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}:\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma}\to\mathbb{Y}_{T}^{\beta,\zeta},

uniformly in
\Lambda
, and

\left\|\left\lVert\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma}\to\mathbb{Y}_{T}^{\beta,\zeta}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p,T,\varepsilon}\mathfrak{S}_{H}^{(\varepsilon)}(\beta,\sigma)+\mathfrak{S}_{B}^{(\varepsilon)}(\zeta,\sigma).

(3.15)

Moreover, as
\Lambda\to\infty
,

\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}\to\mathcal{B}^{\circ}\quad\text{in }L^{p}\bigl(\Omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Y}_{T}^{\beta,\zeta})\bigr).

(3.16)

Proof.

For fixed
M,N,Q
, Theorem
3.3
and Minkowski’s inequality in the Volterra variable give

\displaystyle\left\|\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}B^{\circ}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}(t,s)w_{Q}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\rVert_{C_{T}L^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{T}a^{(\varepsilon)}_{N,Q,M}\left\lVert w_{Q}\right\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}.

The same bound holds in
L_{T}^{1}L^{2}
. Since

\left\lVert w_{Q}\right\rVert_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq Q^{-\sigma}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma}},

absolute summation in
(N,Q)
, followed by the
\ell^{2}_{M}
norm of the shellwise
C_{T}L^{2}
bounds and the
\ell^{\infty}_{M}
Besov norm, gives (
3.15
). This is legitimate because

\sup_{t}\left(\sum_{M}M^{2\beta}\left\lVert u_{M}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\sum_{M}M^{2\beta}\left\lVert u_{M}\right\rVert_{C_{T}L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The
\ell^{1}_{M}
majorant (
3.12
) supplies the high-output tail required for convergence in
B^{\zeta}_{2,\infty}
; explicitly,
\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{M}M^{\zeta}\left\lVert u_{M}(t)\right\rVert_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\sum_{M}M^{\zeta}\left\lVert u_{M}\right\rVert_{L_{T}^{1}L^{2}}
.

For cutoff convergence, fix a finite dyadic set
\mathcal{F}
. On
\mathcal{F}
, the block convergence (
3.7
) and the finite sum give convergence in
L^{p}
operator norm. On the complement
\mathfrak{D}\setminus\mathcal{F}
, the block estimate (
3.6
) applied to the difference of two cutoff families gives

\left\|\left\lVert(\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}-\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda^{\prime}})_{\mathfrak{D}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma}\to\mathbb{Y}_{T}^{\beta,\zeta}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\mathfrak{S}^{(\varepsilon)}_{H,\mathfrak{D}\setminus\mathcal{F}}+\mathfrak{S}^{(\varepsilon)}_{B,\mathfrak{D}\setminus\mathcal{F}}.

The right-hand side tends to zero as
\mathcal{F}\nearrow\mathfrak{D}
. This finite-set/tail argument proves that
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}
is Cauchy in
L^{p}
operator norm and identifies its limit with the operator built from the limiting blocks. Hence (
3.16
) follows.

Measurability follows from Lemma
3.4
.
∎

3.3
Borel–Cantelli and full-sequence pathwise convergence

The almost-sure statement requires more than
L^{p}
convergence: it requires one probability-one event on which all dyadic blocks are simultaneously controlled.

Lemma 3.6

(Dyadic first-Borel–Cantelli device)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{D}^{3}
be countable. Let
X_{i}\geq 0
be random variables and
A_{i}>0
deterministic weights. Suppose that for every finite
p\geq 2
,

\left\lVert X_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}A_{i},\qquad i\in\mathcal{I}.

Let
L_{i}\geq 1
be deterministic losses such that
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}L_{i}^{-p}<\infty
for some
p
. Then there is a full-probability event
\Omega_{*}
and a finite random variable
C_{*}(\omega)
such that

X_{i}(\omega)\leq C_{*}(\omega)L_{i}A_{i},\qquad i\in\mathcal{I},\quad\omega\in\Omega_{*}.

No independence assumption on the family
(X_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
is required.

Proof.

For
R>0
define
E_{i}(R)=\{X_{i}>RL_{i}A_{i}\}
. Chebyshev’s inequality gives

\mathbb{P}(E_{i}(R))\leq C_{p}^{p}R^{-p}L_{i}^{-p}
, hence
\sum_{i}\mathbb{P}(E_{i}(R))<\infty
. By the first Borel–Cantelli lemma, for each fixed
R
only finitely many
E_{i}(R)
occur almost surely. Taking
R=1
and absorbing the finitely many exceptional ratios
X_{i}/(L_{i}A_{i})
into
C_{*}(\omega)
proves the claim. The proof used only summability of probabilities.
∎

Lemma 3.7

(Simultaneous pathwise block bound)
.

Assume, in addition to Assumption
3.1
, the Galerkin-state finite-state condition stated there. Fix a final loss
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\varepsilon
and
\rho>0
so that

\varepsilon_{0}+3\rho<\varepsilon.

(3.17)

Then there is a full-probability event
\Omega_{*}
and a finite random variable
C_{*}(\omega)
such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{*}
, every dyadic triple in
\mathfrak{D}
, and every cutoff,

\left\lVert B^{\circ}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}(\omega)\right\rVert_{C(\Delta_{T};\mathcal{L}(L^{2}_{q},L^{2}_{n}))}\leq C_{*}(\omega)a^{(\varepsilon)}_{N,Q,M}.

(3.18)

No independence between distinct dyadic blocks is required.

Proof.

Let

X_{N,Q,M}:=\sup_{\Lambda\in\mathbb{D}\cup\{\infty\}}\left\lVert B^{\circ}_{\Lambda,N,Q,M}\right\rVert_{C(\Delta_{T};\mathcal{L})}.

The maximal estimate in Theorem
3.3
, run with loss
\varepsilon_{0}
, gives for every finite
p
,

\left\lVert X_{N,Q,M}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{p}a^{(\varepsilon_{0})}_{N,Q,M}.

Apply Lemma
3.6
with the deterministic loss

L_{N,Q,M}=N^{\rho}Q^{\rho}M^{\rho}
. Choosing
p
so that
p\rho>1
makes

\sum_{(N,Q,M)\in\mathfrak{D}}N^{-p\rho}Q^{-p\rho}M^{-p\rho}<\infty,

because the variables are dyadic and start at scale one. Hence, on one full-probability event,

X_{N,Q,M}(\omega)\leq C_{*}(\omega)N^{\rho}Q^{\rho}M^{\rho}a^{(\varepsilon_{0})}_{N,Q,M}

for all admissible triples. Since
Q,M\lesssim N
,

N^{\rho}Q^{\rho}M^{\rho}\lesssim N^{3\rho},

and (
3.17
) absorbs this dyadic loss into the final
\varepsilon
-majorant. This proves (
3.18
).
∎

Theorem 3.8

(Pathwise centered operator and full cutoff convergence)
.

Assume the Galerkin-state finite-state condition in Assumption
3.1
and

\mathfrak{S}_{H}^{(\varepsilon)}(\beta,\sigma)+\mathfrak{S}_{B}^{(\varepsilon)}(\zeta,\sigma)<\infty.

Then on the full-probability event
\Omega_{*}
of Lemma
3.7
, for every cutoff
\Lambda
the series (
3.13
) defines a bounded operator

\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}(\omega):\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma}\to\mathbb{Y}_{T}^{\beta,\zeta},

and

\sup_{\Lambda\in\mathbb{D}\cup\{\infty\}}\left\lVert\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}(\omega)\right\rVert_{\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma}\to\mathbb{Y}_{T}^{\beta,\zeta}}\leq C_{*}(\omega)\left[\mathfrak{S}_{H}^{(\varepsilon)}+\mathfrak{S}_{B}^{(\varepsilon)}\right].

(3.19)

Moreover the entire Galerkin sequence, not merely a subsequence, satisfies

\mathcal{B}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}(\omega)\to\mathcal{B}^{\circ}(\omega)\quad\text{in }\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X}_{T}^{\sigma},\mathbb{Y}_{T}^{\beta,\zeta}).

(3.20)

For every fixed input and output test vector, the limiting scalar pairing belongs to the centered second Wiener chaos.

Proof.

Fix
\omega\in\Omega_{*}
. The probability argument is now finished. Replace the random block norm by the deterministic pathwise majorant (
3.18
) and repeat the assembly proof of Theorem
3.5
. This gives (
3.19
) directly and defines the operator on the full nonseparable source space without a density argument.

For convergence, choose a finite set of dyadic triples so that the pathwise Sobolev and Besov tails are arbitrarily small. Every block in the finite set is stable for all sufficiently large Galerkin cutoffs. Hence two large cutoffs agree on the finite set and differ only by the uniformly small tail. This proves the Cauchy property of the full sequence and hence (
3.20
).

Finite partial pairings are centered second chaoses. Their
L^{2}(\Omega)
limit remains in the closed second-chaos subspace, proving the final assertion.
∎

3.4
Power-law window and final theorem

We now impose the power-law envelope relevant for dispersive and paracontrolled applications. The dyadic decay of the deterministic multiplier and the covariance envelopes is encoded in a single exponent
\Gamma
, and the Sobolev–Besov summability conditions are verified explicitly from this exponent.

Proposition 3.9

(Power-law summability)
.

Assume that, for some
C_{G}>0
and
\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{G}(N)\leq C_{G}N^{-\Gamma}\qquad(N\in\mathbb{D}).

(3.21)

If

\Gamma>\frac{d}{2},\qquad\sigma>\max\{0,d-\Gamma\},\qquad\beta<\Gamma-d,\qquad\zeta<\Gamma-d,

(3.22)

then one can choose
\varepsilon>0
so small that

\mathfrak{S}_{H}^{(\varepsilon)}(\beta,\sigma)+\mathfrak{S}_{B}^{(\varepsilon)}(\zeta,\sigma)<\infty.

(3.23)

Proof.

We suppress an arbitrarily small positive loss, which is restored at the end by decreasing the strict margins in (
3.22
). The
M^{d/2}
branch of the block profile is

N^{d/2-\Gamma}M^{d/2}Q^{-\sigma}.

Since
\sigma>0
, the dyadic
Q
-sum is bounded. Summing over
N\gtrsim M
gives
M^{d/2-\Gamma}
because
\Gamma>d/2
. After multiplication by the output weight, the resulting powers are

M^{\beta+d-\Gamma}\qquad\text{and}\qquad M^{\zeta+d-\Gamma},

which are summable under
\beta<\Gamma-d
and
\zeta<\Gamma-d
.

For the
Q^{d/2}
branch, the relevant summand is

N^{d/2-\Gamma}Q^{d/2-\sigma}.

If
\sigma<d/2
, the
Q
-sum contributes
N^{d/2-\sigma}
, and the subsequent
N
-tail is controlled by
M^{d-\Gamma-\sigma}
because
d-\Gamma-\sigma<0
. The output exponents are then
\beta+d-\Gamma-\sigma
and
\zeta+d-\Gamma-\sigma
, which are negative under
\beta,\zeta<\Gamma-d
and
\sigma>0
. If
\sigma\geq d/2
, the
Q
-sum is bounded up to a harmless endpoint logarithm, absorbed by the small
\varepsilon
, and the
N
-tail is
M^{d/2-\Gamma}
; this is again summable after multiplication by
M^{\beta}
or
M^{\zeta}
, since
\beta,\zeta<\Gamma-d
is stronger than
\beta,\zeta<\Gamma-d/2
. Finally choose
\varepsilon>0
so small that all strict inequalities in (
3.22
) remain strict after the harmless replacements forced by the factors
N^{C\varepsilon}Q^{C\varepsilon}M^{C\varepsilon}
. This proves (
3.23
).
∎

Corollary 3.10

(Centered paracontrolled window)
.

Let

be a deterministic smoothing gain and write

Under the power-law envelope (

follow from

\Gamma>\frac{d}{2},\qquad s<\lambda+\Gamma-d,\qquad\max\{0,d-\Gamma\}<\sigma<\lambda+\Gamma-d.

and hence in
\Gamma
, or in the output exponents
\beta=s-\lambda
and

. The input interval is nonempty exactly when

Assume the continuous Gaussian setup of Section

3.5
Localized Bony notation and paracontrolled interpretation

We finally record the near-output paracontrolled form of the estimate.

Fix a physical cutoff
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and set
k=\widehat{\varphi}
. We use the localized Bony operations

f\prec_{\rm loc}g:=\varphi\,(f\prec g),\qquad f\circ_{\rm loc}g:=\varphi\,(f\circ g).

When the same physical multiplier is inserted at the dyadic block level, the Fourier side of each block contains the soft incidence factor

\widehat{\varphi}(n-q-\ell-\eta)=k(n-q-\ell-\eta).

Thus
\prec_{\rm loc}
and
\circ_{\rm loc}
are the usual Bony low–high and resonant operations followed by a fixed physical localization
[
2
,
1
,
5
]
. The corollary records only the near-output sector
Q\leq c_{\rm ap}N
,
M\leq C_{\rm out}N
.

Corollary 3.12

(Application to a near-output Wick-centered paracontrolled block)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
3.11
. Let
\Delta_{Q}
and
\Delta_{M}
be the Fourier multipliers associated with the fixed cutoffs
\chi_{Q}
and
\chi_{M}

with the centered kernel of Section

\Gamma>\frac{d}{2},\qquad\sigma\in I_{\rm in}:=\bigl(\max\{0,d-\Gamma\},\,\lambda+\Gamma-d\bigr),\qquad s<\lambda+\Gamma-d,

\mathcal{T}^{\circ,{\rm near}}_{\Lambda}:C_{T}L^{2}\cap L_{T}^{\infty}B^{\sigma}_{2,\infty}\longrightarrow C_{T}H^{s-\lambda}\cap L_{T}^{1}B^{\sigma-\lambda}_{2,\infty}
