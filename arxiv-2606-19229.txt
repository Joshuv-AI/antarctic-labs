Distinct Near-Horizon Trend of Synchrotron Polarization in Kerr Spacetime
Yehui Hou ⋆, Jiewei Huang ⋆, Bin Chen

Abstract
We show that the near-horizon expansion of the linear polarization vector for synchrotron emission in a Kerr background admits a distinct analytic form. For emission from a stationary, axisymmetric, degenerate electromagnetic field, the leading-order polarization pattern depends only on the Kerr spin and the source polar angle, while the next-to-leading-order correction further encodes the geometric and rotational structure of the electromagnetic field. Our result extends the equatorial analysis of Hou:2024qqo and the off-equatorial leading-order result of Chael:2026fhf . Near-horizon polarization thus offers a potential probe of the fundamental properties of rotating black holes and of gravito-electromagnetic interactions.

Introduction

As a fundamental astrophysical object, a spinning black hole is characterized by the rotation of its spacetime
kerr1963gravitational
;
misner1973gravitation
. This rotation gives rise to a variety of phenomena, most notably frame dragging

;
Ciufolini:2004rq
, whereby particles and fields are compelled to co-rotate and develop characteristic spiral structures
Blandford:1977ds
;
Komissarov:2004ms
;
Gelles:2021kti
;
Ricarte:2022sxg
;
Ricarte:2022wpd
;
Chael:2023pwp
;
Wang:2025btn
. In the immediate vicinity of the horizon, strong frame dragging significantly twists infalling matter and electromagnetic fields, driving a wide range of accretion flows toward predominantly toroidal configurations
Ricarte:2022wpd
;
Chen:2024jkm
;
Ruales:2026xjb
.

Polarimetric observations provide a particularly sensitive probe of the near-horizon environment
EventHorizonTelescope:2021srq
;
Wielgus:2022heh
;
EventHorizonTelescope:2024rju
. Synchrotron radiation from an optically thin medium is linearly polarized perpendicular to the local magnetic field in the emitter frame, and is thus shaped by the near-horizon toroidal field structure. As a result, it can imprint a universal
Near-Horizon Polarization
(NHP) pattern on the image plane, largely independent of the detailed properties of the flow. This behavior was first identified in
Hou:2024qqo
, where a controlled near-horizon expansion of the polarization vector for equatorial emitters was carried out up to next-to-leading order in the Kerr redshift factor
\Delta
. That work also demonstrated that the same NHP structure persists in Kerr–Newman–Taub–NUT spacetime. This program was subsequently extended to Kerr–Horndeski geometry
Chen:2025ysv
. More recently, the leading-order NHP was established away from the equatorial plane
Chael:2026fhf
, and was shown to closely trace the time-averaged near-equatorial general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics (GRMHD) emission, underscoring its observational relevance.

Despite this progress, it remains premature to claim full universality of the NHP throughout the near-horizon region, as the global subleading corrections across the entire region are not yet understood. Although the exact polarization on the horizon itself is unobservable due to extreme redshift, the near-horizon behavior remains accessible and encodes valuable physical information. This naturally raises the question of whether the NHP persists at subleading order away from the equatorial plane, where jet-associated emission may also contribute.
In this work, we carry out a more detailed near-horizon expansion of the Kerr black hole. We show that, assuming stationarity, axisymmetry and degenerate electromagnetic field, the next-to-leading-order correction to the NHP encodes the derivative of the stream function and the field-line angular velocity in a distinct manner, while remaining independent of the emitter four-velocity. In the equatorial limit, our result reduces to that of
Hou:2024qqo
. These findings point to a remarkable near-horizon structure in Kerr spacetime underlying this elegant polarization behavior.
Throughout, we adopt natural units with
G=c=1
.

II
General scheme

For synchrotron emission from an optically thin source, the polarization vector is determined by the local electromagnetic field and the photon wavevector
1979Lightman
. The electromagnetic tensor satisfies

4F^{\alpha\mu}(*F)_{\mu\beta}=F^{\rho\lambda}(*F)_{\rho\lambda}\,\delta^{\alpha}_{\,\,\beta}
,
where
*
denotes the Hodge dual. Accordingly, the linear polarization vector associated with a photon of wavevector
p^{\mu}
can be expanded on the following covariant basis:

\displaystyle f^{\mu}\propto\xi_{1}\,F^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}+\xi_{2}\,(*F)^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}\,,

(1)

where
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
are real coefficients determined by the local emission process.
For an emitter with four-velocity
u^{\mu}
, the electromagnetic tensor admits the decomposition

F^{\mu\nu}=u^{\mu}e^{\nu}-u^{\nu}e^{\mu}+\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}b_{\beta}
,
where
e^{\mu}
and
b^{\mu}
are the electric and magnetic fields measured in the emitter frame, respectively. In the magnetically dominated regime with vanishing electric field, this reduces to
F^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}b_{\beta}
, and the synchrotron polarization becomes
f^{\mu}\propto\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}b_{\beta}p_{\nu}

Chen:2024jkm
. In this case, Eq. (
1
) simplifies to
Chael:2026fhf

\displaystyle f^{\mu}\propto F^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}\,.

(2)

Although Eq. (
1
) does not explicitly depend on
u^{\mu}
, in magnetized plasma systems it is generally coupled to the electromagnetic field, so that any velocity dependence is implicitly encoded in
F^{\mu\nu}

Komissarov:2004ms
.

To analyze polarized radiation in curved spacetimes, we adopt the Newman–Penrose (NP) tetrad
\{l,n,m,\bar{m}\}

Newman:1961qr
, satisfying
l\cdot n=-1
,
m\cdot\bar{m}=1
, and
l^{2}=n^{2}=m^{2}=\bar{m}^{2}=0
, together with
l\cdot m=n\cdot m=l\cdot\bar{m}=n\cdot\bar{m}=0
. The spacetime metric then takes the form
g_{\mu\nu}=-2l_{(\mu}n_{\nu)}+2m_{(\mu}\bar{m}_{\nu)}
.
A Maxwell field is described by the following three complex scalars:

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}=F_{\ell m},\quad\Phi_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(F_{\ell n}+F_{\bar{m}m}),\quad\Phi_{2}=F_{\bar{m}n}.

(3)

After emission, the polarization vector is parallel transported along null geodesics,
p^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}f^{\nu}=0
.
In spacetimes admitting a nontrivial valence-
(2,0)
Killing spinor, and a hidden symmetry encoded by a Killing–Yano
2
-form

, there exists a conserved Walker–Penrose constant along null geodesics

, namely

\displaystyle\kappa=\left[(*J)_{\mu\nu}+i\,J_{\mu\nu}\right]p^{\mu}f^{\nu},\quad p^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\kappa=0.

(4)

For
f^{\mu}=F^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}
in a vacuum Petrov type D spacetime, the Walker–Penrose constant, as defined in the NP tetrad, takes the form (up to an overall constant)

\displaystyle\kappa\,\propto\,\Psi_{2}^{-\frac{1}{3}}(A-\mathrm{i}B)\,,\quad A-\mathrm{i}B\,=\,2\left(\overline{\Phi_{0}}\,p^{\bar{m}}p^{\ell}+\overline{\Phi_{2}}\,p^{m}p^{n}+2\overline{\Phi_{1}}\,|p^{m}|^{2}\right)\,,

(5)

where
\Psi_{2}
is the only nonvanishing background Weyl scalar, and
A,B
are real functions. For subsequent polarization calculations, we consider the complex combination
A-\mathrm{i}B
and the ratio

Z=-A/B
, which constitutes the sole degree of freedom of the local polarization vector
Himwich:2020msm

\displaystyle z=\frac{\operatorname{Re}\{\kappa\}}{\operatorname{Im}\{\kappa\}}=\frac{\operatorname{Re}\{\Psi_{2}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\}Z-\operatorname{Im}\{\Psi_{2}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\}}{\operatorname{Im}\{\Psi_{2}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\}Z+\operatorname{Re}\{\Psi_{2}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\}}\,.

(6)

It suffices to compute the “source-side” variable; the remaining prefactor in the Walker–Penrose constant is incorporated via the map Eq. (
6
) when evaluating the observed polarization angle.

To probe the near-horizon regime, we expand the relevant quantities in terms of a radial

For a Killing horizon, a natural choice is

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &A-\mathrm{i}B=\Delta^{-1}C_{-1}\left[1+\Delta\mathcal{G}+O(\Delta^{2})\right],\\
&\mathcal{G}=\frac{C_{0}}{C_{-1}}=\frac{p^{\ell}_{0}}{p^{\ell}_{-1}}+\frac{\overline{p^{m}_{1}}}{\overline{p^{m}_{0}}}+\frac{\overline{\Phi_{0}^{(1)}}}{\overline{\Phi_{0}^{(0)}}}+\frac{2\overline{\Phi_{1}^{(0)}}|p^{m}_{0}|^{2}}{p^{\ell}_{-1}\overline{p^{m}_{0}}\,\overline{\Phi_{0}^{(0)}}}.\end{aligned}

(9)

The source-side variable is expanded as

(10)

By explicitly evaluating the coefficients in this expansion for a given spacetime geometry, one can, in principle, extract all information relevant to near-horizon polarized emission, although the procedure may be algebraically cumbersome.

III
Expansions in Kerr

We now specialize to a Kerr black hole, characterized by its mass
M
and angular momentum
J

a=J/M

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\ell^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2H}\left(H,\Delta,0,a\right),\quad n^{\mu}=\frac{H}{\Delta\Sigma}\left(H,-\Delta,0,a\right),\\

(11)

Here the redshift factor is
\Delta=r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2}

The horizon radii and the angular velocity of the horizon are

\displaystyle r_{\pm}=M\pm\sqrt{M^{2}-a^{2}}\,,\quad\Omega_{\rm H}=\frac{a}{r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}}=\frac{a}{2Mr_{+}}\,.

(12)

For the non-extremal case with

In the extremal Kerr limit,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\{\mathcal{G}\}=-\frac{q_{\psi}D_{+}}{2}+\frac{\nu_{s}q_{\psi}-\beta_{s}D_{+}}{2\mathcal{P}_{+}}=\frac{q_{\psi}(\nu_{s}-\mathcal{P}_{+}D_{+})-\beta_{s}D_{+}}{2\mathcal{P}_{+}}.

(39)

Then, using the identity

\displaystyle\nu_{s}-\mathcal{P}_{+}D_{+}=\frac{(\lambda-a\sin^{2}{\theta_{s}})W_{+}-(H_{+}-a\lambda)(1-a\Omega_{+}\sin^{2}{\theta_{s}})}{\sin{\theta_{s}}W_{+}}=-\frac{\Sigma_{+}(1-\lambda\Omega_{+})}{\sin{\theta_{s}}W_{+}},

(40)

we arrive at an explicit expression for
\operatorname{Im}\{\mathcal{G}\}
, which is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\{\mathcal{G}\}=\frac{-\beta_{s}(1-a\Omega_{+}\sin^{2}{\theta_{s}})-q_{\psi}\Sigma_{+}(1-\lambda\Omega_{+})}{2\sin{\theta_{s}}(H_{+}-a\lambda)(H_{+}\Omega_{+}-a)}.

(41)

Substituting this into Eq. (
10
), together with Eq. (
37
), yields

\displaystyle Z_{1}=\frac{\beta_{s}^{2}+\nu_{s}^{2}}{\nu_{s}^{2}}\,\frac{\beta_{s}(1-a\Omega_{+}\sin^{2}{\theta_{s}})+q_{\psi}\Sigma_{+}(1-\lambda\Omega_{+})}{2\sin{\theta_{s}}(H_{+}-a\lambda)(H_{+}\Omega_{+}-a)}.

(42)

The EVPA admits the same expansion structure as in
Hou:2024qqo
. Namely, away from the arctangent branch cut,

\displaystyle\chi=\tan^{-1}z-\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\beta_{o}}{\nu_{o}}\right)=\chi_{+}+\Delta\chi_{1}+O(\Delta^{2})\,,

(43)

with
\chi_{+}
given in Eq. (
38
). Using
Eq. (
35
), the next-to-leading coefficient is

\displaystyle\chi_{1}=\frac{z_{1}}{1+z_{0}^{2}}=\frac{(r_{+}+a\cos\theta_{s}\,z_{0})^{2}}{\Sigma_{+}(1+z_{0}^{2})}\,Z_{1}-\frac{a\cos\theta_{s}}{(r_{+}-r_{-})\Sigma_{+}}\,.

(44)

Using
Z_{1}=-(1+Z_{0}^{2})\operatorname{Im}\{\mathcal{G}\}
together with the relation between
Z_{0}
and
z_{0}
, this is equivalently

\displaystyle\chi_{1}=-\operatorname{Im}\{\mathcal{G}\}-\frac{a\cos\theta_{s}}{(r_{+}-r_{-})\Sigma_{+}}\,.

(45)

Substituting Eq. (
41
), this becomes

\displaystyle\chi_{1}=\frac{\beta_{s}(1-a\Omega_{+}\sin^{2}{\theta_{s}})+q_{\psi}\Sigma_{+}(1-\lambda\Omega_{+})}{2\sin{\theta_{s}}(H_{+}-a\lambda)(H_{+}\Omega_{+}-a)}-\frac{a\cos\theta_{s}}{(r_{+}-r_{-})\Sigma_{+}}.

(46)

We see that the next-to-leading-order correction depends explicitly on
q_{\psi}
and
\Omega_{F}
, which characterize the near-horizon structure of the poloidal magnetic field and the field-line angular velocity induced by both black hole rotation and accretion flows

V
Discussion

We have derived a near-horizon expansion of the linear polarization vector for synchrotron emission in Kerr spacetime under the assumptions of stationarity, axisymmetry, and electromagnetic degeneracy (i.e., the ideal GRMHD limit). At leading order, the polarization depends only on the black hole spin and the emitter’s polar angle, consistent with
Chael:2026fhf
. At next-to-leading-order order, the correction encodes the stream function and the field-line angular velocity in a specific combination, while remaining independent of the emitter’s four-velocity. This structure highlights that the leading-order polarization is purely geometric, whereas the first correction probes intrinsic properties of the electromagnetic field without direct sensitivity to plasma kinematics.

These results suggest a degree of robustness that may extend beyond the idealized setup considered here. The GRMHD simulation has indicated that the leading-order near-horizon polarization (NHP) at the equator persists in time-averaged, nearly equatorial accretion flows
Wong:2025zuh
;
Chael:2026fhf
.
It would therefore be interesting to assess whether the NHP, including subleading corrections, remains stable in fully dynamical simulations across a broader range of spins. Time-dependent or turbulent electromagnetic fields, nonthermal emission
Zhou:2025moa
;
Long:2026yfa
, as well as non-axisymmetric structures, could introduce departures from the analytic structure identified here or reduce its apparent universality. Clarifying the extent to which these effects modify the NHP is important for evaluating its observational utility.

Throughout the above, we fixed the polar angle to
\theta=\theta_{s}
, yielding an expansion on cones of constant
\theta_{s}
; for non-conical emission surfaces, additional chain-rule contributions from
\theta
-drift are required.
For an emitting surface
\theta=F(r)
, a point at
r=r_{+}+d_{H}^{-1}\Delta
satisfies

\displaystyle Z(r,\theta)=Z_{0}(\theta_{+})+\Delta\left[Z_{1}(\theta_{+})+d_{H}^{-1}F^{\prime}(r_{+})\,\partial_{\theta}Z_{0}(\theta_{+})\right]+O(\Delta^{2})\,,

(49)

with
\theta_{+}=F(r_{+})
, indicating that the NHP formula applies to emitters of arbitrary shape, such as parabolic-like jet boundary
Song:2025mhj
;
Gelles:2026mxg
.

Our derivation has relied on several non-degeneracy conditions to ensure the validity of the expansion. In particular, requiring a nonzero and regular leading-order coefficient
leads to

\displaystyle\Omega_{H}\lambda\neq 1\,,\quad\Omega_{+}\neq\Omega_{H}\,,\quad(\psi_{,\theta})_{+}\neq 0\,,\quad\lambda\csc\theta_{s}\neq a\sin\theta_{s}\,.

The first condition excludes the superradiant bound, corresponding to co-rotation with the horizon and vanishing energy flux, in which case the leading contribution to the wave vector vanishes. The second condition avoids a corresponding degeneracy in the electromagnetic sector, while the third ensures that magnetic field lines thread the horizon. If
\Omega_{+}=\Omega_{\rm H}
or
(\psi_{,\theta})_{+}=0
, then
\Phi_{0}^{(0)}=0
, and ratios involving
\Phi_{0}^{(0)}
are not well defined. The fourth condition ensures that
Z_{0}=\beta_{s}/\nu_{s}
remains finite. When any of these conditions fails, the near-horizon expansion must be reorganized starting from the next nonvanishing order.
The extremal Kerr case discussed in Appendix
A
further indicates that the near-horizon expansion and the extremal limit
a\to M
do not strictly commute, although the leading and next-to-leading-order terms coincide with those in the non-extremal case.

Finally, our results point to a possible avenue for probing departures from Kerr. If the analytic structure identified here persists more generally, then deviations from it are expected to appear in subleading polarization signatures in alternative theories of gravity or non-Kerr rotating spacetimes
Zhang:2022klr
;
Guo:2024bzq
;
Chen:2024cxi
;
Shi:2024bpm
;
Rosa:2025pqp
;
Angelov:2025rut
;
Wang:2025qpv
;
Yang:2025byw
;
Chen:2026kys
. In this sense, near-horizon polarization may offer a sensitive diagnostic of beyond-Kerr physics.

Appendix A
Near-horizon expansion for extremal Kerr

For the extremal Kerr geometry, we set
M=a=1
for simplicity, yielding
r_{+}=r_{-}=1
and
\Delta=(r-1)^{2}
. It is convenient to introduce the variable

\epsilon=r-1,\quad\Delta=\epsilon^{2}\,,

and the near-horizon expansions is formulated as power series in
\epsilon
. The horizon parameters reduce to
H_{+}=2
,
\Sigma_{+}=1+\cos^{2}{\theta_{s}}
,
\Omega_{\rm H}=1/2
,
\mathcal{P}_{+}=2-\lambda
, and
W_{+}=2\Omega_{+}-1
. As in the non-extremal case, we must assume
\mathcal{P}_{+},W_{+},(\psi_{,\theta})_{+}\neq 0
to ensure regularity in the near-horizon limit.

A.1
Factorization of leading-order terms

Given that
R(r)=\mathcal{P}(r)^{2}-\epsilon^{2}\mathcal{K}
and that
\sqrt{R}
is related to the outgoing photon branch, it follows that for
\mathcal{P}_{+}\neq 0
,
\sqrt{R}
is real-analytic in
\epsilon
. The components of the photon momentum are given by

\displaystyle p^{\ell}=\epsilon^{-2}L_{\ell}(\epsilon)\,,\quad p^{n}=\epsilon^{2}L_{n}(\epsilon)\,,\quad p^{m}=\frac{E(\beta_{s}-\mathrm{i}\nu_{s})}{\sqrt{2}(1+\epsilon-\mathrm{i}\cos{\theta_{s}})}\,,

where
L_{\ell}
and
L_{n}
are real-analytic functions with
L_{\ell}(0)\neq 0
.

For stationary, axisymmetric, and degenerate magnetospheres,
\Phi_{0}
takes the form

\Phi_{0}=\frac{\sin{\theta_{s}}(1+\epsilon-\mathrm{i}\cos{\theta_{s}})}{2\sqrt{2}\,H(r)}\left[X(\epsilon)+\mathrm{i}Y(\epsilon)\right].

The Znajek regularity condition in Eq. (
28
) further implies

X(0)=2W_{+}B^{r}_{+}=\frac{2W_{+}(\psi_{,\theta})_{+}}{\Sigma_{+}\sin{\theta_{s}}}\neq 0.

Furthermore, the physical requirements that
E^{\phi}=0
and that
E^{r}
and
B^{\theta}
remain regular on the future horizon dictate that

Y(\epsilon)=\epsilon^{2}\widetilde{Y}(\epsilon),

with
\widetilde{Y}
being real-analytic. Therefore, the combination pertinent to Eq. (
5
) evaluates to

\displaystyle\overline{p^{m}}\,\overline{\Phi_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{E(\beta_{s}+\mathrm{i}\nu_{s})}{\sqrt{2}(1+\epsilon+\mathrm{i}\cos{\theta_{s}})}\frac{\sin{\theta_{s}}(1+\epsilon+\mathrm{i}\cos{\theta_{s}})}{2\sqrt{2}\,H(r)}\left[X(\epsilon)-\mathrm{i}Y(\epsilon)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{E\sin{\theta_{s}}(\beta_{s}+\mathrm{i}\nu_{s})}{4H(r)}X(\epsilon)\left[1-\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{2}\frac{\widetilde{Y}(\epsilon)}{X(\epsilon)}\right],

where the geometric factor
1+\epsilon+\mathrm{i}\cos{\theta_{s}}
cancels exactly.

Given that
2\overline{\Phi_{1}}|p^{m}|^{2}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
, this term is parametrically suppressed by
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
relative to the leading-order term in Eq. (
5
), which scales as
2\overline{\Phi_{0}}\,\overline{p^{m}}p^{\ell}\sim\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
. Additionally, we have
\overline{\Phi_{2}}p^{m}p^{n}\sim\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
, which is
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})
relative to the leading contribution. Therefore, we can factorize this expression using a complex-analytic function
\mathcal{H}(\epsilon)
and a real-analytic function
\rho(\epsilon)
such that

A-\mathrm{i}B=\epsilon^{-2}(\beta_{s}+\mathrm{i}\nu_{s})\rho(\epsilon)\left[1+\epsilon^{2}\mathcal{H}(\epsilon)\right],\quad\rho(0)\neq 0,\quad\rho(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{R}.

A.2
Absence of the
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\Delta})
term and expansion structure

The real-valued function
\rho(\epsilon)
strictly modulates the amplitude of
A-\mathrm{i}B
, leaving its phase invariant. Let us expand
\mathcal{H}(\epsilon)
as

\mathcal{H}(\epsilon)=\mathcal{H}_{0}+\mathcal{H}_{1}\epsilon+\mathcal{H}_{2}\epsilon^{2}+\cdots.

Substituting this into Eq. (
A.1
) yields the bracketed term
1+\epsilon^{2}\mathcal{H}(\epsilon)=1+\epsilon^{2}\mathcal{H}_{0}+\epsilon^{3}\mathcal{H}_{1}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})
. Because
\epsilon^{2}=\Delta
and
\epsilon^{3}=\Delta^{3/2}
, this explicitly demonstrates the absence of an
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\Delta})
correction.

Recalling that a small complex variation
\delta=u+\mathrm{i}v
to a baseline amplitude
C_{0}(1+\delta)
induces a phase shift
\delta Z=-(1+Z_{0}^{2})v+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})
, in the extremal Kerr background, the source-side variable becomes

Z=Z_{0}+\Delta Z_{1}^{\rm ext}+\Delta^{3/2}Z_{3/2}^{\rm ext}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta^{2}),

where we use “ext” to denote functions specific to the extremal limit, with the coefficients given by

Z_{0}=\frac{\beta_{s}}{\nu_{s}},\quad Z_{1}^{\rm ext}=-(1+Z_{0}^{2})\operatorname{Im}\{\mathcal{H}_{0}\},\quad Z_{3/2}^{\rm ext}=-(1+Z_{0}^{2})\operatorname{Im}\{\mathcal{H}_{1}\}.

While a generic, regular field is mathematically analytic in the radial parameter
\epsilon=r-1
, this property does not physically necessitate that
\mathcal{H}(\epsilon)
be an even function. If the field smoothly extended across the horizon satisfies the parity condition
\mathcal{H}(\epsilon)=\mathcal{H}(-\epsilon)
, then
\mathcal{H}
should be a function of
\epsilon^{2}
(i.e.,
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)
), restricting
Z
to integer powers
\Delta^{n}
. However, such a symmetry is not a priori guaranteed by the stationarity, axisymmetry, the degeneracy condition, or Znajek regularity; rather, it constitutes an independent, supplementary assumption regarding the radial structure of the magnetosphere.

A.3
Expansion coefficients at
\mathcal{O}(\Delta)

The imaginary part of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
receives two distinct contributions. From Eq. (
A.1
), the first contribution is

\left(\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{H}_{0}\right)_{\Phi_{0}}=-\frac{q_{\psi}}{2}D_{+}^{\rm ext},\quad D_{+}^{\rm ext}=\frac{1-\Omega_{+}\sin^{2}{\theta_{s}}}{\sin{\theta_{s}}(2\Omega_{+}-1)}.

The mixed term involving
\overline{\Phi_{1}}
yields a contribution identical to that in the non-extremal case,

\left(\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{H}_{0}\right)_{\Phi_{1}}=\frac{\nu_{s}q_{\psi}-\beta_{s}D_{+}^{\rm ext}}{2(2-\lambda)}.

Summing these gives

\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{H}_{0}=\frac{q_{\psi}\left(\nu_{s}-(2-\lambda)D_{+}^{\rm ext}\right)-\beta_{s}D_{+}^{\rm ext}}{2(2-\lambda)}.

Using the identity
\nu_{s}-(2-\lambda)D_{+}^{\rm ext}=-\csc{\theta_{s}}(1+\cos^{2}{\theta_{s}})(1-\lambda\Omega_{+})(2\Omega_{+}-1)^{-1}
established in Eq. (
40
), we finally obtain

\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{H}_{0}=\frac{-\beta_{s}(1-\Omega_{+}\sin^{2}{\theta_{s}})-q_{\psi}(1+\cos^{2}{\theta_{s}})(1-\lambda\Omega_{+})}{2\sin{\theta_{s}}(2-\lambda)(2\Omega_{+}-1)}.

Accordingly,
Z_{1}^{\rm ext}=-(1+Z_{0}^{2})\operatorname{Im}\{\mathcal{H}_{0}\}
exactly matches the non-extremal result in Eq. (
42
) when evaluated using extremal horizon data. However, this cannot be recovered simply by taking the limit
d_{H}\to 0
. Instead, treating the expansion in
\epsilon=\sqrt{\Delta}
independently is physically essential due to the non-commutativity of the near-horizon expansion and the extremal limit.
