 ABSTRACT

This letter report formally documents completion of the Oak Ridge National Laboratory Advanced
Reactor Technologies Program Level 3 Milestone M3AT-26OR0605056, “Provide status on studies on
ASTM D8289 optimization studies,” due August 1, 2026. The status and progress made on the ASTM
D8289 optimization studies are detailed in the following sections.

 1. BACKGROUND

This report provides an update on the status of ASTM D8289 optimization studies. Compared to the
traditional graphite tensile test standard ASTM C749, Standard Test Method for Tensile Stress-Strain of
Carbon and Graphite, the ASTM D8289 Standard Test Method for Tensile Strength Estimate by Disc
Compression of Manufactured Graphite offers another method to obtain graphite’s equivalent tensile
strength in an easier, but still effective, experimental setup. Particularly, ASTM D8289 uses small disc
specimens (typically 6 to 12.7 mm in diameter), making it the ideal standard for testing materials
subjected to severe environmental constraints, such as nuclear irradiation or oxidation. However, as the
alternative of ASTM C749, ASTM D8289 retains some disadvantages of disc splitting tests, such as the
measurement of only one output (the splitting tensile strength), the ambiguity in the definition of
specimen thickness, the specimen size effect and the grain size effect on the strength result.

The primary objective of the ASTM D8289 optimization studies is to improve this widely adopted
standard, advancing it with an emphasis on extending its single splitting tensile strength output to
multiple meaningful outputs if possible, clarifying the ambiguities in specimen descriptions, and
providing deep understanding of the mechanical differences in graphite grades.

During FY 2026, focus was on applying ASTM D8289 to various graphite discs, which were grouped in
different grades and specimen thicknesses. Digital image correlation (DIC) was integrated into ASTM
D8289 to extract more useful information from this standard disc splitting test.

 2. TECHNICAL OVERVIEW AND KEY OUTCOMES

Following the ASTM D8289 standard, an Instron 4465 universal tensile compression load frame was used
to implement the disc splitting test. With a fixed crosshead speed (displacement controlled, 0.009
in./min), the top platen moved down slowly to apply the compression load on the fixture, which was
manufactured according to the ASTM D8289 fixture specifications. The fixture delivered the load on disc
without creating local chips around two loading ends on the disc, guaranteeing the specimen cracked at
the disc center until specimen failure. Figure 1 shows the test facility. Sitting nearby the load frame, the
DIC device captured the disc surface throughout the loading procedure until specimen failure. Two
cameras were used in this test configuration to enable the observation of out-of-plane displacement on the
specimen surface. The spatial resolution of the recorded images reached roughly 203 pixels per
millimeter, and each subset consists of 85 × 85 pixels.

Another key outcome of this work is the full-field displacement and strain distribution on the disc surface
extracted throughout the entire loading procedure. Tensile strain fields plotted in Figure 3 illustrate the
strain field distribution difference between fine-grained graphite (such as Mersen 2114) and coarse-
grained graphite (such as PCEA) immediately before the disc cracks and load drops. As shown in Figure
3a, the Mersen 2114 sample exhibited a more uniform tensile strain field, whereas Figure 3b shows the
representative tensile strain localization around the disc center for the PCEA sample. This distinction
indicates that the different graphite grades might have different failure mechanisms under such a complex
loading.

Figure 3. Tensile strain distribution at disc center. (a) Mersen 2114 6.35 mm thick sample S4, (b) PCEA against-
 grain (AG) 6.35 mm sample S1.

The standard ASTM D8289 test normally terminates with a large open crack around the disc center; this
crack propagates along the vertical direction while a sudden load drop is detected. If the graphite disc
sample does not fall apart when the load drops and holds there, then releasing the compressive load
consistently closes the center crack. Although the graphite samples had been cracked during the disc
compression test, recompressing them in the orthogonal direction yields surprising findings in this ASTM
D8289 study. Figure 4 illustrates the recompression test procedure and the recompression peak loads of
PCEA specimens AG-2 and WG-2. After 90° rotation from the compression direction, the recompressed
PCEA AG-2 and WG-2 specimens showed similar peak loads compared to their first compression values.
In another words, the failed specimens could still sustain equivalent stress like the undamaged specimens.
The strain field analysis on the recompressed specimens, such as the PCEA AG-2 in Figure 5, exposed
the first crack at the low stress state and the two crossover cracks at the peak load. These findings could
be used to further study graphite’s structural integrity as well as crack propagation and failure detection
under a complex stress state.

 3. PRIMARY TECHNICAL WORK PRODUCTS AND MILESTONE ACHIEVEMENTS

After conducting the ASTM D8289 standard test for various graphite grades (Mersen 2114, IG-110, ETU-
10, PCEA, NBG-17, NBG-18), the comprehensive analysis on the test results has achieved the following
milestones:

• Identified the ambiguity of specimen thickness definition in ASTM D8289 and suggested the revision
 on the specimen thickness requirement to ASTM.
• Extracted additional useful information from tested specimens (in-plane displacements, strains, out-
 of-plane displacements).
• Evaluated the equivalence between splitting tensile strength and uniaxial tensile strength.
• Assessed graphite specimen’s remaining strength after its first standard ASTM D8289 test.
• Submitted one paper, “Investigation of Nonlinear Mechanical Behavior of Two Superfine-Grained
 Graphites with DIC Assisted Disc Splitting Test,” to Journal of Nuclear Materials.
• Drafted one paper, “Effect of Sample Thickness on the Tensile Strength of Small Superfine-Grained
 Graphite Discs.” Plan to submit it to journal Carbon in this calendar year.

 4. LABORATORY COLLABORATION AND TECHNICAL SIGNIFICANCE

The key technical achievements in this fiscal year were the study and development of this ASTM D8289
test standard. These test improvements were shared with laboratories and industry on consistent
applications. The latest results were presented at the joint Electric Power Research Institute Nuclear
Graphite Workshop and US Department of Energy Office of Nuclear Energy Advanced Reactor
Technologies Gas-Cooled Reactor program review on July 8, 2026, with the title “ASTM D8289
Standard Development in the Study of Graphite’s Structure-Mechanical Behaviors.” Further results were
presented at the ASME 2026 Pressure Vessels & Piping Conference section CS-15-01, Mechanical

Properties of Nuclear Graphite and their implementation in Codes and Standards–1 on July 22, 2026, with
the title “Specimen Thickness Effect in Standard Disc Compression Test on Nuclear Graphites.” The
planned presentation at the International Nuclear Graphite Specialists Meeting in October 2026, with the
title “Effect of Grain Size and Sample Thickness on Graphite Mechanical Behavior,” will introduce these
standard optimization studies to extensive international nuclear graphite manufacturers and researchers.

The ASTM D8289 optimization studies enhanced the collaborations between Oak Ridge National
Laboratory and Idaho National Laboratory nuclear graphite researchers through identification of the
appropriate graphite grades for testing, proposal of revisions to the ASTM D8289, and use of the DIC
displacement/strain fields for graphite material modeling.

 5. NEAR-TERM TECHNICAL FOCUS (NEXT STEPS)

Next steps toward further studies on the ASTM D8289 include the following:

• Apply the same test on more graphite grades to have comprehensive evaluation of the effects of the
 specimen thickness on graphite’s splitting tensile strength and mechanical behavior.
• Utilize the displacement/strain field to study graphite’s material behavior and build a corresponding
 material model.
• Conduct recompression tests to investigate graphite’s remaining strength after cracking.
• Combine the standard test with another nondestructive test, such as acoustic emission technique, to
 extract more information from the standard ASTM D8289 test.
