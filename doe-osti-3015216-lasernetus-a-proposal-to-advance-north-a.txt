1. Executive summary

LaserNetUS Collaborative Network established in 2018 is a network of high-power laser facilities
supported by the Department of Energy (DOE) Office of Fusion Energy Sciences (FES) and operating
effectively as a user facility. Its mission is to advance and promote intense laser science and applications
by providing scientists and students with broad access to unique facilities and enabling technologies,
advancing the frontiers of laser-science research, and fostering collaboration among researchers and
networks from around the world. Users who submit proposals through an annual call are selected by an
external and independent proposal review panel (PRP) not involving personnel from any of the facilities.

Besides the Omega Laser Facility at the University of Rochester’s Laboratory for Laser Energetics
(UR/LLE), the network during this project period includes high-intensity laser facilities from six other
universities and three national laboratories, namely, the Colorado State University, the University of
Nebraska at Lincoln, The Ohio State University, Universite du Quebec–Institut National de la Recherche
Scientifique, the University of Texas at Austin, the University of Central Florida, Lawrence Berkeley
National Laboratory, SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, and Lawrence Livermore National
Laboratory, respectively. The network facilities span a wide range in laser pulse energy, pulse duration,
repetition rate, and experimental diagnostic equipment enabling innovative research in a variety of exciting
areas. The network facilities are extremely complementary. Even though several of the lasers are based on
similar technologies, every single facility has unique capabilities in its laser, optical setup, and target areas.
LaserNetUS has made the full set of diverse capabilities available to US and international researchers for
the first time. The network is advised by its Scientific Advisor Board (SAB). During this project period,
LaserNetUS appointed its first Coordinator to fully support the network operation and formed several
committees including the intense-light Users Engagement Committee (I-USE), Diagnostics Committee and
Simulations Committee. Details of the LaserNetUS facilities, organization and committees, events, and
accomplishments can be found at the network website (https://lasernetus.org/).

A very important role that the LaserNetUS fulfills is the training of students and young scientists who will
be key for the future development of high-energy-density physics (HEDP), inertial fusion energy (IFE),
and high-power laser technology itself. The network provides these students not only with access to the
most advanced instrumentation and laser facilities, but also the opportunity to interact and collaborate with
students from other institutions and with a large group of experienced scientists. Leveraging a broad
expertise of the network, LaserNetUS also started partnering with three other institutions, namely, the
Florida A&M University, the Morehouse College, and the University of California Merced, to attract and
train undergraduate students on key aspects of LaserNetUS-related science and technology at the network’s
facilities.

As the largest university-based high-power laser users’ facility in the world, the Omega Laser Facility at
the UR/LLE, primarily funded by the DOE National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA), has served
the HEDP and inertial confinement fusion (ICF) community for more than 40 years. The multi-beam multi-
kJ OMEGA EP Laser System brings unique capabilities to the LaserNetUS network. The combination of
high intensity and high energy in short- and long-pulse operation together with solid or gas-jet targets and
externally applied magnetic fields provides users a wide domain of experimental conditions. This award
provided a total of eleven (11) shot days on OMEGA EP to ten (10) LaserNetUS user experiments selected
from the Cycle 3 and Cycle 4 proposals. During the award period of performance (August 2020–January
2024), nine teams fully utilized the eleven shot days for their unique science experiments and conducted a
total of 89 target shots. These experiments have involved 87 unique users from 25 institutions including 13
graduate students and 10 postdoctoral researchers. To date, LaserNetUS-awarded OMEGA EP experiments
have contributed to 9 journal publications, 2 PhD theses, and over 50 presentations including 13 invited
talks at various international conferences.

2. Summary of the project activities and accomplishments

2.1 LaserNetUS network activities

User Access to the Network Facilities. LaserNetUS provides a broadly open access to all these advanced
systems in the network. Users obtain LaserNetUS facility time for experiments via a competitive proposal
process managed by the DOE FES through the dedicated Coordinator, which is independent of the facilities,
except for assessment of technical feasibility performed at the end of the process. The network solicits
proposals for experimental time on the network facilities annually. Proposals are reviewed, ranked, and
allocated to facilities by an independent proposal review panel (PRP) of national and international subject‐
matter experts who are anonymous to facility personnel except for the PRP Chair. Users that request a run
at a primary facility that is fully booked are switched to a different (secondary) facility when possible.
Additional user support including travel, targets and equipment is also provided to U.S. users for conducting
their experiments. During the project period, LaserNetUS completed three annual solicitations for beam
time in 2021–2022 (Cycle 3), 2022–2023 (Cycle 4), and 2023–2024 (Cycle 5), respectively. A total of 185
user proposals were submitted in response to the three solicitations. LaserNetUS selected 93 projects for
experiments at the network facilities including 12 on the OMEGA EP laser system. This award funds all
Cycle 3 and Cycle 4 user experiments at the OMEGA EP laser.

LaserNetUS as a Network. It should be emphasized that the LaserNetUS is a network and not simply a
collection of facilities. The LaserNetUS Leadership Team consists of Chair, Vice Chair and the Coordinator,
who are guided by the DOE FES Program Manager. Chair and Vice Chair are members of the network
facility PIs or point of contacts (POCs) and are elected by the POCs. The Network Facilities Committee,
which includes all facility POCs and other facility personnel, and the Coordinator meet almost every week
to discuss status and plans for the network including designing the proposal calls, executing the Townhalls
and Annual LaserNetUS Meetings, expanding the LaserNetUS community, writing white papers, etc.
Increased collaboration was a primary motivation for the formation of LaserNetUS and the network
facilities support each other to a degree not seen previously. In addition to the PRP, LaserNetUS is advised
by its SAB with whom it meets in-person every year and has regular interaction with the Chair. The network
has also formed several other committees including the I-USE Committee representing users’ interest
within the network, the Diagnostics Committee advising on user diagnostic needs, and the Simulation
Committee facilitating connections between investigators and the teams that build simulation codes and
capabilities.

UR/LLE PI/POC, Dr. Wei, has fully participated in all LaserNetUS network activities. She has played the
leadership role serving as LaserNetUS Vice Chair (August 2022 – July 2024). In this role, she also chaired
the Program Committee for the 2023 LaserNetUS Annual Meeting. Dr. Wei also served as founding Vice
Chair on the Diagnostic Committee (from 2021 to March 2023). The Diagnostics Committee met monthly
discussing common diagnostics and data management needs of LaserNetUS users. A set of findings and
recommendations on diagnostics were developed and presented at the annual LaserNetUS meetings (2021
and 2022), and the committee submitted a report to the LaserNetUS.

Response of HEDP and Optical Science Community. LaserNetUS, in a short time, has achieved very
high visibility in the DOE and the national and international science communities, being viewed already as
a major success and a model network which has also inspired several newly formed networks such as
ZNetUS and MagNetUS. The broad range of capabilities of the LaserNetUS network facilities, open science
mission, and demonstrated success in user research puts it in an excellent position to play an even stronger
role in advancing science, technology, and the workforce for frontier HEDP research and IFE. This is well
recognized by the HEDP and optical science communities. LaserNetUS has figured prominently in multiple
reports in the last few years: the 2019 Brightest Light Initiative (BLI) Workshop, the American Physical

3. Summary of training and professional development provided

An important role that LaserNetUS fulfills is the training of students and postdocs who will be key to the
future development of HED and fusion science as well as high power laser technology itself. Through
LaserNetUS, students and postdocs have the opportunity to lead experiments and to collaborate and learn
from scientists and engineers from different institutions. This provides a well-rounded education and
leadership development for students, first giving them experience at a high-power laser laboratory and
teamwork, ultimately providing expertise that they will bring to the broader workforce and future facilities.
The ten LaserNetUS experiments completed on OMEGA EP listed above have involved 87 unique
researchers from 25 institutions including 13 PhD students, 10 postdocs, 11 professors, and 53 scientists
and research engineers. Four graduate students and postdocs have played a leading role as project PI in
planning and execution of experiments as well as data analysis and modeling. Simulations and data from
these experiments are significant part of two graduate students’ theses, namely, Abraham Chien (PhD 2021,
Princeton University) and Gaia Righi (PhD 2022), University of California at San Diego).

The LaserNetUS experiments have also provided opportunities to cross training students and professionals
on different platforms, e.g., OMEGA EP versus the National Ignition Facility or the Jupiter Laser Facility
at LLNL; OMEGA EP versus the High-Pressure Collaborative Access Team (HPCAT) facility at the
Advanced Photon Source at Argonne National Laboratory. This directly benefits the development of a talent
pool for the national labs, academia and high technology industry.

Several OMEGA EP LaserNetUS lead students received awards and advanced their career, including:
 • Graduate student Ian Ocampo from Princeton University presented their team’s Cycle-2
 LaserNetUS OMEGA EP experiment at the 2021 LaserNetUS User Meeting and won the Best
 Student Poster award.
 • Gaia Righi was awarded the LLNL's Harold Brown Postdoctoral Fellowship (2022-2025), and she
 also won the Best Postdoctoral Poster award at the 2022 LaserNetUS User Meeting.
 • Michael Wadas was awarded the Cecil and Sally Drinkward and the Caltech Presidential
 Postdoctoral Fellowships (2023–present).

During this project period, LaserNetUS has partnered with the Morehouse College, the Florida Agricultural
and Mechanical University (FAMU), and the University of California, Merced, on a pilot internship
program for undergraduates at the network facility institutions. Two students from the Morehouse
conducted their summer research in 2023 mentored by LLE scientists and graduate students. Both students
attended the 2023 LaserNetUS Meeting and participated in a multi-week LLE-led LaserNetUS experiment
at the Texas Petawatt laser.
