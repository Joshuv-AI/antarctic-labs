Standpoint Logics with Defeasible Beliefs
Nicholas Leisegang, Thomas Meyer, Sebastian Rudolph

Abstract
In this paper, we integrate the defeasible logic of Kraus, Lehmann and Magidor (KLM) with the standpoint logic framework of Gómez Álvarez and Rudolph. This is done with the goal of formally expressing knowledge taking into account multiple (possibly contradicting) viewpoints, which in turn may hold defeasible beliefs. In doing so, we utilise Defeasible Restricted Standpoint Logics (DRSL), introduced by Leisegang et al. Our work expands on previous work by providing a foundational representation result for DRSL semantics and systematically lifting several well-known entailment relations from the propositional case to the standpoint-enhanced setting. In particular, we characterise the semantics for DRSL through a set of KLM-style postulates adapted for the standpoints case. We furthermore provide a means to lift preferential entailment, and the class of entailment relations based on single ranking functions from the purely propositional to the standpoint-enhanced context, including rational and lexicographic closure. We show this can be done equivalently through semantic and algorithmic means. Furthermore, we show that, for each considered form of entailment, the complexity class of entailment checking does not change when moving from propositional KLM to DRSL.

Introduction

Standpoint logics
denote a modal logic framework introduced by Rudolph and Gómez Álvarez
10
with the purpose of expressing multiple standpoints or perspectives, which may hold conflicting beliefs about some topic, while still maintaining consistency in the logic. Much of the work in standpoint logics concerns the addition of
standpoint modalities
to base logics such as description logics
8
;
9
, while showing that the added expressivity does not increase the complexity of reasoning. Recent work has integrated standpoint modalities in non-monotonic formalisms, modelling situations where standpoints may encompass beliefs that resemble default rules or defeasible implications
17
;
12
. As a motivating case, consider the following example.

Example 1

(
17
)
.

In the 19th century, crops imported into the USA were divided into fruits and vegetables, where fruits were exempt from import tax. This led to a court case on whether a tomato should be legally classified as a fruit or a vegetable. From a botanical standpoint, tomatoes are fruits and all fruits are also vegetables. This can be expressed using standpoint logics with the formulas:

\Box_{B}(tomato\rightarrow fruit)
and
\Box_{B}(fruit\rightarrow veg.)
,
where
B
represents the botanical standpoint. However, the court considered a different standpoint based on the culinary use of tomatoes, according to which vegetables are those crops which are savoury and fruits are those which are sweet, giving us the beliefs:
\Box_{C}(savoury\leftrightarrow veg.)
,
\Box_{C}(sweet\leftrightarrow fruit)
,
\Box_{C}(tomato\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim savoury)
, and
\Box_{C}((fruit\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\neg veg.)\wedge(veg.\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\neg fruit))
,
where
C
represents the “culinary” standpoint. Here, the third statement tells us that tomatoes are usually used in savoury dishes, and the last proposition states that fruits and vegetables are usually considered distinct from each other. Ultimately, courts agreed with the culinary standpoint that tomatoes were usually considered vegetables. We can represent this by
L\preceq C
,
\Box_{L}(veg.\rightarrow\neg fruit)
, where
L\preceq C
tells us that the legal standpoint
L
holds true each conclusion of
C
’s standpoint, and the second proposition states that fruits and vegetables are strictly distinct, legally speaking. This system allows for both internal exceptions, and strict disagreements between standpoints:
B
believes that every fruit is a vegetable, while
L
believes that no fruit is a vegetable. We also see that it is possible that a certain kind of exceptional tomato is not savoury from a culinary standpoint. In fact, from a culinary perspective it is possible to consider a tomato both a fruit and vegetable, due to its exceptional nature (i.e.
\Diamond_{C}(tomato\rightarrow(fruit\wedge veg.)
).

This shows us a scenario in which there are both different standpoints with conflicting points of view, as well as standpoints that hold beliefs which are defeasible in their nature. For example, the rule
fruit\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\neg vegetable
allows for non-prototypical fruits to also be considered vegetables. The addition of standpoint modalities therefore adds a new layer of expressivity to the propositional defeasible reasoning of Kraus, Lehmann and Magidor (KLM)
14
, in which multiple perspectives can be considered simultaneously. On the other hand, adding defeasibility to the beliefs held by standpoints allows for beliefs to be weakened, and therefore for more agreements in beliefs to be held across standpoints, ultimately increasing the inferences that can be drawn from the classical case. In our paper, we extend the work of Leisegang et al.
17
, who introduce the language of Defeasible Restricted Standpoint Logic (DRSL), and the corresponding semantics of
preferential standpoint structures
. Our contribution characterises preferential standpoint structures by defining a set of KLM-style postulates, and providing a representation result that each set of DRSL statements closed under these postulates can be represented by a preferential standpoint structure. We then consider entailment for DRSL, where we first lift monotonic preferential entailment from the propositional case to the standpoints case, as well as providing a method for lifting the class of non-monotonic entailment relations which are based on single ranking functions to the standpoint logic case. We further show that entailment-checking for DRSL remains in the same complexity class as in the propositional case, in both the monotonic and non-monotonic case. Our paper is structured as follows: Section
2
introduces preliminary material from propositional KLM-style defeasible reasoning. Section
3
introduces the syntax and semantics for DRSL, and provides the representation result which shows soundness and completeness for our adapted set of postulates. Section
4
establishes results for both monotonic and non-monotonic entailments for DRSL. Section
5
considers related work and provides concluding remarks.

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some basic results and definitions from propositional defeasible reasoning. The framework for defeasible reasoning we use was proposed by Kraus, Lehmann and Magidor (KLM)

\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim

\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta

Definition 1
.

The language of KLM propositional logic

\phi::=\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta\mid\phi\wedge\phi\ ,

This definition is a slight extension of the original language of KLM, which does not allow conjunction in the language. This distinction is important in our case when we apply modal operators across conjunctions of KLM implications, rather than single implications. It is also worth noting that the
\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim

\alpha

\neg\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\bot

3
, and so our language can express classical Boolean statements, as well as defeasible implications. Abusing notation, we use the Boolean formula
\alpha

\neg\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\bot
. The semantics for
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
is defined below.

.

\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}

such that
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\phi

and

.

If a set

3
Defeasible Beliefs in Standpoint Logics

Standpoint logics are a family of modal logics used to describe scenarios involving different viewpoints which may hold conflicting beliefs. For each such standpoint
s
, one introduces modal operators
\Box_{s}
and
\Diamond_{s}
. Then,
\Box_{s}\phi
and
\Diamond_{s}\phi
read as
“it is unequivocal to
s
that
\phi
”
and
“it is possible to
s
that
\phi
”
, respectively. In this section, we consider an extension of propositional standpoint logic, as defined by Gòmez Álvarez and Rudolph
10
. In particular, we apply standpoint modalities to KLM-style defeasible implications by introducing a logic with formulas of the form
\Box_{s}(\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta)
or
\Diamond_{s}(\alpha\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\beta)
. This represents the case where a standpoint holds a defeasible belief, and these sentences should be read as
“it is unequivocal to
s
that
\alpha
usually implies
\beta
”
and
“it is possible to
s
that
\alpha
usually implies
\beta
”
. In this section, we describe the syntax and semantics for DRSL, and show that this can be characterised through an extended system of postulates inspired by the original KLM postulates. We restrict the syntax to only allow for conjunction on the outermost level. This restriction is made with the spirit of propositional KLM in mind, in which disjunctions and negations of defeasible statements are not expressible. Such a restriction is also needed to facilitate the representation and complexity results found in the remainder of the paper.

Definition 3
.

17

A vocabulary is a pair
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{S})
where
\mathcal{P}
is a finite set of propositional atoms and
\mathcal{S}
is a finite set of standpoint symbols. The language of Defeasible Restricted Standpoint Logic (DRSL)
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}
is defined as follows,

\psi::=\phi\mid\Box_{s}\psi\mid\Diamond_{s}\psi\mid\psi\wedge\psi\text{ or }\psi::=s\preceq t

where
s,t\in\mathcal{S}\cup\{*\}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
with atoms in
\mathcal{P}
.

Statements of the form
s\preceq t
are referred to as
standpoint sharpening
statements, and intuitively, they say that
s
is a more specific version of
t
’s viewpoint. The
*
symbol is referred to as the
universal standpoint
and represents the beliefs which all standpoints agree upon. The semantics for this are given by
preferential standpoint structures
, which again are built on top of preferential interpretations given in propositional defeasible reasoning.

Definition 4
.

17

A preferential standpoint structure is a triple
M=(\Pi,\sigma,\tau)
, where:

1.

\Pi
is a non-empty set of
precisifications
(possible worlds).

2.

\sigma:\mathcal{S}\cup\{*\}\rightarrow 2^{\Pi}
is a map which assigns a non-empty set of precisifications to each standpoint, such that
\sigma(*)=\Pi
.

3.

\tau:\Pi\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
is function where
\mathbb{I}
is the set of preferential interpretations over the set
\mathcal{P}
. That is,
\tau
is a map which assigns to each precisification a preferential interpretation.

Definition 5
.

17

Given a preferential standpoint structure
M
and a precisification
\pi\in\Pi
, the satisfaction relation
\Vdash
is defined as followed (where
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
,

•

,

•

•

Intuitively,

Definition 6
.

A set of DRSL formulas
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}

\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}

We now describe a set of proof-theoretic postulates which characterise preferential semantics for DRSL. As with KLM and propositional standpoint logic, we operate under the assumption that our logic is built on top of Boolean propositional logic. We therefore accept that any axioms or rules of inference which hold in Boolean propositional logic hold in our logic. Besides this, the postulates we give here can be broadly split into two groups. The first of these is an adapted set of the modal axioms which characterise standpoint modalities. In the classical case standpoint logic acts as a multimodal variant of
KD45
with additional axioms tailored for standpoint sharpenings.
\Box_{*}
acts as an
S5
modality, and therefore has additional properties
10
. In our setting, we include an adapted version of the original axioms for standpoint modalities, which are sufficient to cover the restrictions in our syntax. These axioms rephrased in terms of Gentzen-style rules in order to be expressible in our restricted version of standpoint logic, which does not allow for unrestricted material implication or disjunction. Furthermore, the restriction of negation means
\Box_{s}
and
\Diamond_{s}
must be treated separately, and not simply as duals of each other; although semantically they behave as duals. The derived modality postulate for DRSL are given in Figure
2
. They are named directly after the original classical standpoint logic axioms from which they are derived
10
, with some of the original modal axioms requiring two or more rules in our setting of restricted expressivity (for example,
P.a
and
P.b
). Additional postulates describe how modalities distribute over conjuncts, and govern the behaviour of standpoint sharpenings. Besides these modal rules which govern the external structure between precisifications in the semantics, we also require rules which describe how the preferential interpretations underlying the precisifications behave. In order to describe this, we adapt the original KLM postulates, as given by Kraus et al.
14
, into the standpoint logic modal case. These are given in Figure
3
. From each original KLM postulate, we derive a pair of postulates which correspond to preferential reasoning which is both global or local to a given standpoint. Each “a.” postulate describes how the KLM postulates apply when defeasible beliefs hold throughout an entire standpoint, while each “b.” postulate describes conclusions reached in a specific possible precisification for a standpoint. That is, in each rule we cumulatively add a new conjunct to the premise, ultimately obtaining a large
\Diamond
-bound conjunction of defeasible implications. This is done in order to collect every conclusion valid at a possible precisification, and make sure that we do not lose any conclusions which may be derived relative to the same possible world.

4
Entailment

In this section, we turn our attention to defining and computing entailment from DRSL knowledge bases. In the propositional setting, KLM-style defeasible reasoning has several non-equivalent notions of entailment defined for a given knowledge base
3
. In this paper, we do not focus on a single form of defeasible entailment, but rather we attempt to show principled means to lift classes of entailments from the propositional setting to the standpoints setting. The next sections characterise the lifting of several well-known defeasible entailment operators from the propositional case. We assume that each knowledge base is given in
conjunction free normal form
. That is, for each formula in
\mathcal{K}
in normal form that has the shape of a conjunction
\bigwedge^{n}_{i=1}\phi_{i}
, we replace it by its set of conjuncts
\{\phi_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
. It is clear to see that this is logically equivalent, and this assumption allows us simpler definitions for characterizing derived propositional knowledge bases within the following sections.

4.1
Preferential Entailment

The first form of defeasible entailment we consider is preferential entailment, which is the monotonic core of KLM-style reasoning. This is defined via the usual Tarskian methods. We provide both the propositional and DRSL definition here.

Definition 13
.

Given a (finite) knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
, we say that
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}

\mathcal{K}
, denoted
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P,prop}\phi
if for any preferential interpretation
\mathcal{I}
, we have that
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\mathcal{K}
implies
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\phi
.

Definition 14
.

Given a (finite) knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}

\phi\in\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
is preferentially entailed by
\mathcal{K}
, denoted
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P}\phi
if for any preferential standpoint structure
M
, we have that
M\Vdash\mathcal{K}
implies
M\Vdash\phi
.

From Theorem
2
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

\phi\in\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
is preferentially entailed by
\mathcal{K}
iff
\phi\in C(\mathcal{K})
, where
C(\mathcal{K})
is the set obtained by exhaustively applying the rules in Figures
2
and
3
, as well as the rules of classical propositional logic to
\mathcal{K}
.

The above corollary shows that we are able to compute preferential entailment through applications of the rules given in Figures
2
and
3
, in order to determine whether
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P}\phi
for some
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
and
\phi\in\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
. Next we show that we can reduce entailment-checking in the standpoint case to entailment checking in the propositional case. We first note following preliminary lemma.

Lemma 7
.

For any
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
, we have that a standpoint sharpening statement
s\preceq t\in C(\mathcal{K})
iff
t=*
or
t=s
or

\preceq_{\mathcal{K}}=\{(s_{1},s_{2})\mid s_{1}\preceq s_{2}\in\mathcal{K}\}

The above holds since it is clear from the rules in Figure
2
that the only way to obtain standpoint sharpenings as conclusions are through one of the cases above. We are therefore able to compute the set of standpoint sharpening statements in
PTime
, since this amounts to computing transitive closure. With this in mind, we derive a set of KLM propositional statements from
\mathcal{K}
for each standpoint. Intuitively, this set represents the “base case” of beliefs for this standpoint.

Definition 15
.

For a DRSL knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
, and a standpoint

we define the derived set

\mathcal{K}_{s}:=\{\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}\mid\phi\in\mathcal{K}\text{ or }\Box_{t}\phi\in\mathcal{K},s\preceq t\in C(\mathcal{K})\}.

We then define another set of propositional KLM statements for each diamond-bound statement occurring in
\mathcal{K}
. Intuitively, this represents constructing the precise “version” of a standpoint
s

Definition 16
.

For a DRSL knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}

\mathcal{K}^{\phi}_{s}:=\mathcal{K}_{s}\cup\{\phi\}.

Moreover, from this we can find a collection of sets in

Definition 17

For a knowledge base
\mathcal{K}

\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s):=\{\mathcal{K}_{t}\mid t\preceq s\in C(\mathcal{K})\}\cup\{\mathcal{K}^{\phi}_{t}\mid t\preceq s\in C(\mathcal{K}),\Diamond_{s}\phi\in\mathcal{K}\}.

These derived sets are inspired by the sets constructed in the algorithm

\mathcal{K}^{\phi}_{s}
and

is in

. We then show how the sets defined above allow us to check for preferential entailment in the DRSL case.

Proposition 1
.

Consider a DRSL knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}

\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P}\Box_{s}\psi

\mathcal{K}_{s}\vDash_{P,\textit{prop}}\psi

Note that this also allows us to check whether
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P}\psi
for
\psi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
, since we can equivalently check
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P}\Box_{*}\psi
. For diamond-bound statements, we cannot reduce DRSL preferential entailment-checking to a single propositional entailment check, but rather to a set of propositional entailment checks.

Proposition 2
.

Consider a DRSL knowledge base
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
, and a statement
\Diamond_{s}\psi\in\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}

We can then, in general, reduce DRSL preferential entailment-checking to propositional preferential entailment-checking. Each DRSL formula can be rewritten in normal form. Then, in order to check such a conjunction in normal form, we just check each of the conjuncts using the corresponding propositional entailment checks from Propositions
1
and
2
. Using this, we are able to analyse the complexity of preferential reasoning in DRSL, and show that it is within the same complexity class as preferential entailment for KLM propositional logic.

Theorem 3
.

Preferential entailment-checking in DRSL is
coNP
-complete.

The

only contains those statements which we know are universally true, and therefore, emulates a standpoint where nothing except universal beliefs are known. We make this design choice since when we are working with defeasible entailment, we are reasoning non-monotonically about the beliefs of known standpoints, and so we believe this assumption is a valid one. In fact, the alternative may lead us to disregard conclusions which seem reasonable in a setting of prototypical reasoning. Consider the knowledge base
\mathcal{K}=\{\Box_{s}(p\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim q),\Box_{t}(p\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim q)\}
. It seems reasonable here to say that all considered standpoints believe that
p\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim q
holds. That is
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\Box_{*}(p\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim q)
. However, if we were to require that
\mathcal{K}_{*}
is considered, then we must consider whether the empty set entails
p\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim q
, which it usually does not for well-known definitions of
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
. Hence,
\mathcal{K}\not\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\Box_{*}(p\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim q)
, simply because
\mathcal{K}_{*}
is empty. This seems undesirable, since our logic ultimately looks to increase possible agreements between standpoints, if possible, and withdraw upon learning new information which contradicts this (for example, by adding
v
to
\mathcal{S}
and
\Box_{v}(p\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim\neg q)
to
\mathcal{K}
). The same assumption is not made when
\Diamond_{*}\psi
is introduced to the knowledge base. In this case, we still consider
\mathcal{K}^{\psi}_{*}
, since our knowledge informs us that some standpoint holds
\psi
possible, but we cannot be more specific about which standpoint that is. A result of this is that it is possible to emulate the open world setting using our framework by simply adding
\Diamond_{*}\top
to a given DRSL knowledge base. This necessitates the consideration of the knowledge base
\mathcal{K}^{\top}_{*}
in the semantics, which is deductively equivalent to
\mathcal{K}_{*}
. That is, it introduces a “place-holder” precisification, whose only beliefs are those we know to be universal across our whole standpoint domain, and therefore includes the possibility of unnamed standpoints where no details of their beliefs are known.

One corollary of this, is that the entailment for DRSL is not well-defined when our knowledge base is propositional. If
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
, then for any defeasible entailment
\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}=\emptyset
and so
M^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}_{\mathcal{K}}
is no longer a well-defined standpoint structure. However, we note that in cases where the knowledge base is entirely propositional, we can refer to well-known propositional approaches to KLM, or we can avoid this by simulating the open world scenario by adding
\Diamond_{*}\top
to our knowledge base. With this addition, we get the expected result that
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{DRSL}
collapses into
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{prop}
when considering propositional statements.

Related Work and Conclusions

DRSL and its semantics were originally considered by Leisegang et al.
17
. Similar notions of non-monotonic standpoint logics with default-style beliefs are considered by Gorczyca and Straß
12
;
11
who consider standpoint modalities in the non-monotonic modal logic S4F. Another method of integrating standpoint logics with KLM defeasibility is given by Leisegang et al.
18
, who introduce Propositional Defeasible Standpoint Logic (PDSL) in which a defeasible notion of standpoint modalities and standpoint sharpenings are introduced. However, while defeasible implications occur within PDSL, they act as outer-level implications between modal statements, rather than defeasible beliefs held by standpoints. Hence, the semantics for PDSL and DRSL are non-equivalent. KLM-style defeasible reasoning has been employed in other modal logics such as
K

2
and linear temporal logic (LTL)
4
. The addition of standpoint modalities to monotonic modal logics, such as LTL, has also been considered
1
;
5
;
7
.

In this paper, we provided a study of integrating standpoint modalities and KLM-style defeasible beliefs, extending the work by Leisegang et al.
17
on DRSL. In particular, this paper contributes a KLM-style representation result for a set of proof-theoretic modal and KLM-style postulates which are sound and complete with respect to the semantics of preferential standpoint structures. Moreover, any set of DRSL statements closed under our postulates can be represented by a unique preferential standpoint structure. We then characterised preferential entailment in DRSL, as well as providing a systematic means for lifting the class of single ranking function based defeasible entailment relations from propositional to standpoint logics. This includes well-known relations such as rational and lexicographic closure. Lastly, for all defeasible entailments considered, we showed that entailment-checking in the DRSL case falls within the same complexity class as the propositional case.

is not a standpoint sharpening we can assume it is in normal form (and if it is not in normal form we can reduce it to normal form in polynomial time). Then
\xi
is a conjunction of formulas of the form
\phi
, where either
\phi=\psi
,
\phi=\Box_{s}\psi
or
\phi=\Diamond_{s}\psi
for
\psi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
.

If
\phi=\psi
then we can check entailment for this conjunct by checking whether
\mathcal{K}_{*}\vDash_{P,prop}\psi
by Proposition
1
.

Similarly, if
\phi=\Box_{s}\psi
then we can check entailment for this conjunct by checking whether
\mathcal{K}_{s}\vDash_{P,prop}\psi
.

If
\phi=\Diamond_{s}\psi
then we can check entailment by checking whether
X\vDash_{P,prop}\psi
for some
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)
.

We here note that
\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)
, and furthermore we note that the size
\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
is in the worst case the size of
\mathcal{K}
, since at most each element of
\mathcal{K}
initiates the need for a new member of
\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
. Hence for any
s
,
|\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)|\leq|\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)|\leq|\mathcal{K}|
. Moreover, each element of any
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
is a subformula of an element of
\mathcal{K}
, and so the size of
X
is no larger than
\mathcal{K}
.

Therefore, we can reduce checking whether
\mathcal{K}\vDash\xi
to polynomially many propositional preferential entailment checks (in the size of
\mathcal{K}
and
\xi
), where each check is polynomial in the size of
\mathcal{K}
and
\xi
.

Now note the following. Since none of the preferential entailment checks are dependent on each other, we can replace the atoms occurring in all the formulas involved in the preferential entailment checks. That is, for each conjunct
c
in
\xi
and each
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
for which a propositional preferential entailment check is required, we can replace each
p\in\mathcal{P}
with the indexed atom
p_{c}^{X}
which results in a logically equivalent entailment check, but where each check has a distinct vocabulary.

Let
C
be the set of conjuncts in
\xi
and let
\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(c)
denote the subset of
\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
used when querying preferential entailment for
c
. Then, since each propositional preferential entailment check is
CoNP
-complete we can polynomially reduce it to an
UNSAT
query of a Boolean formula
q_{c}^{X}
for each
c\in C
and
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(c)
. This Boolean formula is polynomial in the size of
\mathcal{K}
and
\xi
. That is, checking if
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P}\xi
is equivalent to checking whether
q
is unsatisfiable for each
q\in\{q_{c}^{X}\mid c\in C,X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(c)\}
.

Since we are able to query each preferential entailment with a distinct vocabulary, we can assume w.l.o.g. that the atoms occurring in each
q_{c}^{X}
are distinct. Since our vocabulary is partitioned for each conjunct, we have that each
q\in\{q_{c}^{X}\mid c\in C,X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(c)\}
is unsatisfiable if and only if the conjunction

\bigwedge_{c\in C,X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(c)}q_{c}^{X}

is unsatisfiable. Then, since the number of such conjunctions are polynomial the size of
\mathcal{K}
and
\xi
, and each
q_{c}^{X}
is also of polynomial size, we can reduce checking is
\mathcal{K}\vDash\xi
holds to a single polynomially large
UNSAT
check, and hence the complexity is dominated by the
CoNP
complexity of
UNSAT
. Therefore checking preferential entailment in DRSL is in
CoNP
.
∎

Proposition
3
.
Consider
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
, a defeasible entailment
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
and the associated ranking strategy
r
:

1.

If
\mathcal{K}
is satisfiable, then there is no
\pi_{X}\in\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}
such that
\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{r}(\pi_{X})=\emptyset
.

2.

If
\phi\in\mathcal{K}
then
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}\Vdash\phi
.

Proof.

1.

We show this by contrapositive. For
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
suppose there exists an defeasible entailment
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
with a ranking strategy
r
where
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}=(\Pi_{\mathcal{K}},\sigma_{\mathcal{K}},\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{r})
and
\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{r}(\pi_{X})=\emptyset
for some
\pi_{X}\in\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}
. We show that in this case
\mathcal{K}
must be unsatisfiable.

Then
\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{r}(\pi_{X})=r_{X}=\emptyset
for some
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
. Moreover, since
r_{X}
is empty, we have that
r_{X}\Vdash\phi
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
. In particular,
r_{X}\Vdash\bot
. But then, by construction, we have that
X\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\bot
. Furthermore, since we assume that
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
satisfies classical preservation,
X\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\bot
iff
X\vDash_{P,prop}\bot
. However, then since
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
, we have by Proposition
2
that
\mathcal{K}\vDash_{P}\Diamond_{*}\bot
. But this is true iff in every model
M=(\Pi,\sigma,\tau)
of
\mathcal{K}
we have that
M,\pi\Vdash\bot
for some
\pi\in\Pi
. That is,
\tau(\pi)\Vdash\bot
. Since the only preferential interpretation that satisfies
\bot
is the empty interpretation, we then have that
\tau(\pi)=\emptyset
. And therefore,
\mathcal{K}
is not satisfiable.

2.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{K}
we show by cases on the structure of
\phi
that
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi
.

•

If
\phi=s\preceq t
then by definition
\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)\subseteq\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
and so if
\pi_{X}\in\sigma_{\mathcal{K}}(s)
, then
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)
. Thus
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
and so by definition
\pi_{X}\in\sigma(t)
. Hence
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}\Vdash s\preceq t
.

•

If
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
then
\phi\in X
for all
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
and therefore, for each
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}
we have
\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{r}(\pi)=r_{X}
for some
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
. By our assumption that
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
satisfies
Inclusion
we have that for each
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)
that
X\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi
and therefore
r_{X}\Vdash\phi
. That is
M^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}_{\mathcal{K}},\pi\Vdash\phi
for all
\pi\in\Pi
and so
M^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}_{\mathcal{K}}\Vdash\phi
.

•

If
\phi=\Box_{s}\psi
for
\psi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
then using similar reasoning as in the previous case we have
\psi\in X
for all
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)
and so by a similar argument
M,\pi\Vdash\psi
for all
\pi\in\sigma_{\mathcal{K}}(s)
.

Since
\mathcal{K}
is in conjunction-free normal form, this is sufficient to cover all cases.

Proposition
4
.
Suppose
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\cup\{\Diamond_{*}\top\}
where
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
. Then for
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
, we have
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{DRSL}\phi
iff
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{prop}\phi
.

In the case specified above, it is clear that
\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(*)=\{\mathcal{K}_{*},\mathcal{K}_{*}^{\top}\}
and so in this case if
M^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}_{\mathcal{K}}=(\Pi_{\mathcal{K}},\sigma_{\mathcal{K}},\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{r})
we have that
\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}=\{\pi_{\mathcal{K}_{*}^{\top}}\}
and
\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{r}(\pi_{\mathcal{K}_{*}^{\top}})=r_{\mathcal{K}_{*}^{\top}}
. Therefore
M^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}_{\mathcal{K}}\Vdash\phi
for
\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
iff
r_{\mathcal{K}_{*}^{\top}}\Vdash\phi
. Furthermore, by definition
r_{\mathcal{K}_{*}^{\top}}\Vdash\phi

\mathcal{K}^{\top}_{*}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi
. Then note that since
\mathcal{K}^{\top}_{*}=\mathcal{K}_{*}\cup\{\top\}
and the addition of
\top
is tautologous we have
\mathcal{K}^{\top}_{*}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi

\mathcal{K}_{*}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi
. Finally, since
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\mathcal{K}_{*}
and so
\mathcal{K}_{*}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{prop}\phi

Theorem
4
.
For a given DRSL knowledge base
\mathcal{K}
, a formula
\phi\in\smash{\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}_{\mathbb{S}}}
and a defeasible entailment
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx
with a selection strategy
r
, we have that
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\psi
iff
StdptRankEntail(\mathcal{K},r,\phi)=\text{True}
.

Proof.

We once again consider the base case for single box and diamond quantified statements, and then prove by induction over conjunctions:

•

If
\phi=\Box_{s}\psi
for
\psi\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrel{|}\joinrel\sim}
then
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi
iff
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx},\pi_{X}\Vdash\psi
for all
\pi_{X}\in\sigma(s)
iff

\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{r}(\pi_{X})=r_{X}\Vdash\phi
for all
X\in\textbf{PropKB}(s)\setminus\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
. By construction this is equivalent to
X\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\psi
for all
X\in\textbf{PropKB}(s)\setminus\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
. Lastly, by the result given by Casini, Meyer and Varzinczak
3
, this is true iff
\text{DefeasibleEntail}(X,r_{X},\psi)=True
for all
X\in\textbf{PropKB}(s)\setminus\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{K}\not\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi
iff
\text{DefeasibleEntail}(X,r_{X},\psi)=False
for some
X\in\textbf{PropKB}(s)\setminus\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
. Then lastly note that
\text{StdptRankEntail}(K,r,\Box_{s}\psi)=False
iff
\text{DefeasibleEntail}(X,r_{X},\psi)=False
for some
X\in\textbf{PropKB}(s)\setminus\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
(this is clear from lines 7-13 of the algorithm). Thus
\mathcal{K}\not\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\Box_{s}\psi
iff
\text{StdptRankEntail}(K,r,\Box_{s}\psi)=False
.

•

If
\phi=\Diamond_{s}\psi
we repeat a similar dual argument:
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi
iff
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx},\pi_{X}\Vdash\psi
for some
\pi_{X}\in\sigma(s)
iff
\tau_{\mathcal{K}}^{r}(\pi_{X})=r_{X}\Vdash\phi
for some
X\in\textbf{PropKB}(s)\setminus\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
. Again, this is equivalent to
X\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\psi
for some
X\in\textbf{PropKB}(s)\setminus\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
, which by Casini, Meyer and Varzinczak
3
is equivalent to
\text{DefeasibleEntail}(X,r_{X},\psi)=True
for some
X\in\textbf{PropKB}(s)\setminus\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
. Then note that
\text{StdptRankEntail}(K,r,\Diamond_{s}\psi)=True
iff
\text{DefeasibleEntail}(X,r_{X},\psi)=True
for some
X\in\textbf{PropKB}(s)\setminus\{\mathcal{K}_{*}\}
(note lines 14-21 of the algorithm). Thus
\text{StdptRankEntail}(K,r,\Diamond_{s}\psi)=True
iff
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\Diamond_{s}\psi
.

•

For our inductive step on conjunctions we assume the result holds for each conjunct
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
and then we assume
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi_{1}\wedge\phi_{2}
. In this case, this is equivalent to
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}\Vdash\phi_{1}\wedge\phi_{2}
which is equivalent to
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}\Vdash\phi_{1}
and
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mid\hskip-1.20552pt\approx}\Vdash\phi_{2}
. Then we note by inductive hypothesis that this is equivalent to
\text{StdptRankEntail}(K,r,\phi_{1})=True
and
\text{StdptRankEntail}(K,r,\phi_{2})=True
which, by lines 1-6 of the
StdptRankEntail
algorithm, is equivalent to
\text{StdptRankEntail}(K,r,\phi_{1}\wedge\phi_{2})=True
.

∎

Theorem
5
.
For any propositional defeasible entailment
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{prop}
, we have that if
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{prop}
is computable in a complexity class
C
such that
\textsc{P}_{\parallel}^{\textsc{NP}}\subseteq C
, then entailment-checking for
\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx_{DRSL}
remains in
C
. In particular:

•

Entailment checking for rational closure in DRSL is
\textsc{P}_{\parallel}^{\textsc{NP}}
-complete.

•

Entailment checking for lexicographic closure in DRSL is
\textsc{P}^{\textsc{NP}}
-complete.

Proof.

Presume we wish to compute whether
\mathcal{K}\mid\hskip-1.72218pt\approx\phi
, where we assume the size of the input is the size of
\mathcal{K}
and
\phi
(we consider the ranking strategy
r
as being determinable from
\mathcal{K}
and
\phi
). We show this by showing that each query of
StdptRankEntail
involves at most polynomially many checks to
DefeasibleEntail
, in the size of
\mathcal{K}
and
\phi
.

Let
n
be the size of
\mathcal{K}
and let
k
be the length of the query
\phi
. For each conjunct in of the form
\Box_{s}\psi
occurring in
\phi
we complete the if loop at line 7 of
StdptRankEntail
at most once for each
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)\setminus\{K_{*}\}
. Each repeat of the loop calls
DefeasibleEntail
once. Note that as mentioned in previous proofs we have that each set
X\in\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)\setminus\{K_{*}\}
is no larger than the original knowledge base
\mathcal{K}
. Similarly
\Box_{s}\psi
is no larger than
\phi
. Therefore the input to each call of
DefeasibleEntail
is no larger than
n+k
.

Furthermore, we once again recall that for any standpoint symbol
s
the size of
\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)\setminus\{K_{*}\}
is no larger than the size of
\mathcal{K}
. Therefore we only repeat the loop at line 7 at most
n
times.

For similar reasons, for each conjunct of the form
\Diamond_{s}\psi
we call
DefeasibleEntail
at most
n
times with an input no larger than
n+k
.

Then note there at most
k
conjuncts of each form occurring is
\phi
, and so we call
DefeasibleEntail
at most
n\cdot k
times with an input no greater than the input of
StdptRankEntail
. Furthermore, no input of a call to
DefeasibleEntail
depends on the outcome of any previous call, and all of the given calls can be made in parallel.
Lastly note that
\textbf{PropKB}_{\mathcal{K}}(s)
can be computed in polynomial time in the size of
\mathcal{K}

17
and can be computed without relying on the output of any call to
DefeasibleEntail
. That is,
StdptRankEntail
involves at most polynomially many parallel calls to
DefeasibleEntail
. Therefore, if the complexity class of this algorithm is in
C
such that
\textsc{P}_{\parallel}^{\textsc{NP}}\subseteq C
, computing
StdptRankEntail
remains within
C
.

In particular since
RationalClosure
in the propositional case is
\textsc{P}_{\parallel}^{\textsc{NP}}
-complete, extending it to DRSL is
\textsc{P}_{\parallel}^{\textsc{NP}}
-complete. Similarly, since
LexicographicClosure
in the propositional case is
\textsc{P}^{\textsc{NP}}
-complete, extending it to DRSL is
\textsc{P}^{\textsc{NP}}
-complete.
