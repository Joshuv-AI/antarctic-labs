The Task Force agreed, however, that this matter, like certain other
topics added to the list of eighteen, was too important to be neglected
and properly came within the more general charge to the Task Force in
paragraph 12 of the Minutes of the Career Service Board's 8th Meeting.

b. the problem of this study seems to resolve itself most appropri-

4 CONCLUSIONS

a. The Task Force therefore concludes:

|1) The proposed legislation should neither authorise nor mention
any entity such as an "Intelligence Service*©

(2) The objectives of the law, if General Counsel thinks such a
section indicated, should refer to the Central Intelligence Agency
rather than to an "Intelligence Service" and should be stated in terms
of strengthening CIA by creating conditions conducive to retention

of experienced employees over the long term; and, if appropriate in
the light of later sections, conditions favoring the prompt but equitable
elimination of the ineffective;

(3) Since no "Service" is set up, no provisions for the government
of such a service will be necessary;

(4) la sections dealing with benefits, benefits should he made
available under prescribed conditions to employees of the Agency
rather than to members of an "Intelligence Service. '
