13-16 June 2023

1. Overview: scientific focus, participation, and outcomes
The 35th Annual Workshop on Recent Developments in Electronic Structure Theory was
held June 13 – 16, 2023 at the University of California, Merced, a minority-serving institution
with R2 status. This workshop series travels to a different institution within the US each year,
and this was the first time it was held in the underserved Central Valley of California. The Local
Organizers collected suggestions from the international steering committee to put together a list
of topics representing recent major developments in the field and speakers.

Details of the event can be found in the official conference website, housed on the UC Merced
Physics Department website, where we list a general overview of the conference, have a full
program available, describe in detail the tutorials and other additional activities, and list the
volunteers and award winners from ES23.

The scientific program consisted of 20 invited talks, 48 posters. Optional activities preceding the
opening of the scientific program included a hands-on tutorial on the Real-space MultiGrid
(RMG) code, a hands-on workshop on the MIT Computational Nanoscience Toolkit on NSF-
funded nanoHUB.org, and a session of Wikipedia editing to improve electronic structure—
related articles. Evening activities during the workshop included a “Meet the Program Manager”
session, a venue to practice presentations and discuss preliminary results with peers, a banquet
and awards session, as well as an informal gathering at the lake next to campus. This collection
of professional development and networking activities was supplemented by the invitation to all
participants to present poster presentations and to submit manuscripts and/or “technical notes” to
a special issue of Electronic Structure.

This year’s Workshop complemented the in-person meeting by enabling some limited virtual
participation in technical sessions, online discussion groups, and the ability to do virtual poster
presentations. This led to 99 virtual registrants in addition to the 98 in-person attendees. The
participants came from across the globe, with their home institutions including those in the US,
Italy, India, Iran, Pakistan, Brazil, The Netherlands, Sweden, Ethiopia, Taiwan, and South Korea.
More than half (28 of 48) our poster presenters came from Minority Serving Institutions (MSIs),
and our 20 invited speakers included 3 from MSIs, 8 speakers considered “early career” (within
10 years of their PhD), 2 from underrepresented minority (URM) groups, and 4 women. The
workshop participants included advanced undergraduate students, graduate students, postdoctoral
researchers, and senior scientists from academia, industry, and national laboratories. Participants
noted the high quality of the scientific content, especially amongst the poster presenters, and the
collegial environment and usefulness of the tutorials and activities was noted by both organizers
and participants. Participants who stayed on campus were provided meals, with those staying
off-campus joining for breakfast and lunch, which encouraged vibrant scientific discussions and
enhanced networking between junior and senior researchers.

This year, particular effort was made to reach out to regional institutions who might not usually
be able to support their students or early career faculty attending a national conference, which in
practice meant reaching out to specific physics, chemistry, and materials researchers at
California State Universities across California, along with major research institutions
(universities, industrial researchers, and national laboratories) within driving distance of Merced.
With the support of DOE funds, as well as those from other sponsors, we were able to keep the
registration fee relatively low ($125) and subsidize the already-low on-campus housing fees and
travel expenses for early career and underserved participants. This also included meals, which
further eased the travel costs borne by student participants. We also provided online mechanisms
to arrange carpooling and supported new presenters in gaining speaking and analysis confidence
by providing a space to discuss their results with other junior researchers before the poster
session. We also used steering and organizing committee talks at national conferences and
academic seminars to promote the Workshop, as well as emailed promotion to the large ES
mailing list, in hopes that could draw a broad and enthusiastic group.

In order to showcase work related to the topics of the 2023 Workshop, a special focus issue of
the IOP journal Electronic Structure was guest edited by local organizers David Strubbe and
Aurora Pribram-Jones, which currently has 7 published articles and 2 technical notes, covering
topics as varied as ensemble density functional theory, stochastic electronic structure,
superconductivity, and more.

The 2024 workshop was held at Boston University, and the 2025 workshop will be held at the
University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP).

Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry.
