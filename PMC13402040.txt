Beyond a single diagnosis: coexisting PAPSS2 brachyolmia and growth hormone deficiency in a child with short stature
Authors: Antony Fu
Published: 2026

Image legend

A 6-year-old girl presented with short stature. Her height was 98.5 cm (<third percentile), with an upper-to-lower segment ratio 1.1 (51:46) and arm span 3 cm greater than height. She was prepubertal with no dysmorphic features (Fig. 1A). Her bone age matches chronological age. Growth hormone deficiency (GHD) was confirmed by 2 stimulation tests (peak GH was 5.3 μg/L [5.3 ng/mL] and 5.5 μg/L [5.5 ng/mL], respectively), while pituitary magnetic resonance imaging was normal. Spine x-ray demonstrated generalized vertebral end plate irregularities and thoracolumbar disc-space narrowing (Fig. 1B). Whole-exome sequencing identified a frameshift PAPSS2 variant, confirming PAPSS2-associated brachyolmia type 4.

Clinical picture showing A, short-trunk short stature and B, vertebral end plate irregularities on thoracolumbar spine x-ray.

PAPSS2-associated brachyolmia is an autosomal recessive skeletal dysplasia characterized by short-trunk short stature, spinal deformity, and platyspondyly with abnormal vertebral end plates. Patients typically showing normal intelligence and facial features but may develop back pain and progressive short-spine phenotype during childhood. Radiological features include flattened vertebral bodies, irregular end plates, and narrowed disc spaces [1]. Although generally not associated with GHD, the coexistence of both conditions may obscure the clinical picture, as each impairs growth through distinct mechanisms. This highlights the importance of comprehensive evaluation in short stature, as identifying a treatable endocrine disorder such as GHD may allow for targeted therapy despite underlying skeletal dysplasia.
