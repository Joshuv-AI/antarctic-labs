Quantitative Estimates for Mean-Field Limits and Correlation Functions through a Duality Framework
Nadia Khoury, P.-E. Jabin

Abstract
We investigate the mean-field limit for interacting particle systems through a duality-based framework and obtain quantitative estimates on the convergence of marginals as well as on correlation functions. In particular, for merely square–integrable interaction forces, we derive the natural fluctuation–scale rate 𝒪 ( N − 1 / 2 ) \mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2}) . By introducing an iterative argument on the hierarchy of dual cumulants, we leverage this bound to recover the optimal mean-field rate 𝒪 ⁡ ( N − 1 ) \mathcal{O}(N^{-1}) and to obtain robust estimates on the dual cumulants, at the expense of corresponding regularity assumptions on the interaction kernel. Finally, using the relation between dual and direct correlations, we transfer these bounds to direct cumulants, yielding refined information on correlations and deviations from chaos.



1.  Introduction

We investigate the collective behavior of a large system of
N
identical
particles interacting pairwise through forces under a mean-field scaling. We start from Newtonian dynamics, allowing for a possible velocity diffusion. Thus, for
i=1,\dots,N
, let
(X_{i}(t),V_{i}(t))\in\mathcal{D}
denote the position and velocity of the
i

\begin{cases}dX_{i}=V_{i}\,dt,\\[5.69054pt]
dV_{i}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}^{N}K(X_{i}-X_{j})\,dt+\sqrt{2\sigma}\,dW_{i},\end{cases}

(1)

where
K:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an interaction force kernel,

,
and
X_{i}\in\Omega

\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
or

Although the formulation above allows for any

\sigma=0

\sigma=\sigma_{N}\to 0

\sigma\geq 0

\sigma=0

Our goal is to derive a precise statistical description of the particles’ dynamics. In the deterministic case
\sigma=0
, the particle system (
1

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F_{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(v_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x_{i}}F_{N}+\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}K(x_{i}-x_{j})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}F_{N}\right)=0,

(2)

where
F_{N}
is the full joint law of the system and is correspondingly a probability density on the
N

\mathcal{D}^{N}=\left(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)^{N}

f^{0}
-chaotic in the sense of

F_{N}|_{t=0}=\left(f^{0}\right)^{\otimes N},

(3)

for some bounded probability density
f^{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})

The full joint law

is however posed in a space that grows with

F_{N,m}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m}):=\int_{\mathcal{D}^{\,N-m}}F_{N}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})\,dz_{m+1}\cdots dz_{N},

We expect that the dynamics of the system follows the so-called mean-field limit

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+(K*f)\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\qquad f|_{t=0}=f^{0},

(4)

where we denote
K*f(x):=\int_{\mathcal{D}}K(x-\bar{x})f(\bar{x},\bar{v})d\bar{x}d\bar{v}
. In particular, we expect propagation of chaos to hold, namely

F_{N,m}-f^{\otimes m}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty,

since initially at time
t=0
, we have exactly
F_{N,m}|_{t=0}=(f^{0})^{\otimes m}
.

The marginals are not expected to converge at a faster rate than
O(N^{-1})
. A convenient way to quantify further deviations from the mean-field limit is through the analysis of higher-order correlation structures, referred to in the present paper as direct cumulants. In the classical setting, these are defined as combinations of the marginals through a cluster expansion over partitions.

There exist various notions of direct cumulants. One of the most classical ones is probably the following, denoting by
F_{N}
the joint density,

F_{N}(z_{1},...,z_{N})=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,N\})}\;\prod_{\sigma\in\pi}G_{N,|\sigma|}(z_{\sigma}),

where the sum runs over all partitions of
\{1,\dots,N\}
, and the product is over blocks of the partition
\sigma\in\pi
, and
z_{\sigma}:=(z_{i})_{i\in\sigma}
. To guarantee uniqueness of this expansion, we impose the following cancellation property

\int_{\mathcal{D}}G_{N,m}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\,dz_{j}=0,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq m.

In addition,
\{G_{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
can also be defined by Möbius inversion as

G_{N,m}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,m\})}(|\pi|-1)!(-1)^{|\pi|-1}\prod_{\sigma\in\pi}F_{N}^{(|\sigma|)}.

The
G_{N,m}
correspond to some well-known physical quantities:
G_{N,1}
is just the 1-particle distribution for example while
G_{N,2}
measures the correlations in the system.

However, we are specifically interested here in the deviation from the mean-field limit, for which it is more convenient to express the
N
-particle distribution as a perturbation of the factorized mean–field density
f^{\otimes N}
. The marginals include redundant information: if the first marginal
F_{N,1}
is already close to
f
, then we are more interested in how far
F_{N,2}
is from
f^{\otimes 2}
for example. Concretely, if
F_{N}
denotes the joint density, one can consider the cluster expansion

F_{N}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})=f^{\otimes N}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{N}}\kappa_{N,n}(z_{\sigma}),

with the convention
\kappa_{N,0}=1
where
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{N}
denotes the set of all subsets of
\{1,\dots,N\}
with
n
elements and
z_{\sigma}=(z_{i})_{i\in\sigma}\in\mathcal{D}^{n}
.
In order for the functions
\kappa_{N,n}
to be uniquely defined, we impose the cancellation property

\int_{\mathcal{D}}\kappa_{N,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\,f(z_{i})\,dz_{i}=0\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq n.

From a physical and probabilistic perspective, both of these definitions are designed to capture correlations beyond factorization and provide a natural framework for mean–field analysis. Indeed, while the marginals
F_{N,n}
contain both independent and correlated contributions, the direct cumulants
\kappa_{N,n}
remove lower-order factorizations and provide a structured way to quantify correlations at each order. In particular, propagation of chaos can be characterized by the decay of
\kappa_{N,n}
for all
n\geq 2
as
N\to\infty
.

We now state our main result, which provides quantitative bounds on the direct cumulants
\kappa_{N,n}
introduced above.

Theorem 2
.

Let
k\geq 1
,
K\in\mathscr{R}_{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mathscr{R}_{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\begin{cases}L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})+L^{4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})&\text{if }k=1,\\

f\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}))

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Big(|\nabla_{v}\log f|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big|\tfrac{1}{f}\nabla^{j+1}_{z\cdots zv}f\Big|^{2}\Big)f\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,\qquad K*f\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;W^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\right).

Let
\{\kappa_{N,n}\}_{0\leq n\leq N}

F_{N}

For

\Lambda_{N,n,k}:=\max\!\left\{\|\bar{\kappa}_{N,n}^{0}\|_{L^{2}_{f^{0}}},\,\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{j}(f^{0}\bar{\kappa}_{N,n}^{0})}{f^{0}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{f^{0}}}\right\},

(5)

Cumulant methods also play an important role in other settings. In the derivation of the Lenard–Balescu equation,

We emphasize that the mean-field limit is much better understood for so-called first-order systems, with several recent breakthroughs.

For the 2d Navier–Stokes system, early propagation-of-chaos results go back to

. The relative entropy method was later combined with the modulated-energy method in

As already mentioned, our work is closest to
[
4
]
. Instead of directly studying the propagation of the tensorized structure of the
N
-particle density
F_{N}
solving the Liouville equation, that work reformulates the problem through a dual Liouville equation evolving backward in time. The dual equation is initialized with observables having a linear structure, and the mean-field limit problem is then reformulated as the study of how this linear structure propagates under the dual structure.

The advantage of this reformulation is that deviations from linearity can be naturally measured through linear correlation functions associated with the dual equation. These quantities behave more favorably than the usual nonlinear correlations appearing in classical propagation-of-chaos formulations. In particular, they allow the use of Hilbertian techniques and energy-type estimates to obtain a priori bounds. The resulting quantities satisfy a BBGKY-type hierarchy of equations, and the analysis of this hierarchy becomes tractable because the potentially singular derivative losses only appear in perturbative terms that vanish in the mean-field limit.

Compared with
[
4
]
, the present work differs in three main respects. First, while
[
4
]
establishes mean-field convergence at rate
N^{-1/2}
under
H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
-type assumptions on the interaction kernel, we only require
K\in H^{1}\cap L^{4}
at that level, and under the stronger assumption
K\in H^{2}\cap W^{1,\infty}
our approach recovers the optimal rate
N^{-1}
for the convergence of the marginals to the mean-field limit. Second, under the more general assumption
K\in H^{k}\cap W^{k-1,\infty}
, we show that the duality estimates can be iterated beyond the first levels: the remainder term arising at order
k
is analyzed by using the decomposition already obtained at order
k-1
. In this way, each new step of the argument gains an additional factor
N^{-1/2}
, at the expense of one extra derivative assumption on
K
, leading to bounds of order
N^{-k/2}
for the dual cumulants. This iteration process is one of the main improvements and novelties in the paper as it shows how to capture higher-order cumulant structures of the
N
-particle law, and not only the leading mean-field behavior itself. Finally, these estimates are then transferred through the weak duality framework of
[
4
]
, where the backward hierarchy is constructed in a suitable weighted Hilbert space and the forward
N
-particle evolution is encoded by weak duality solutions; the resulting pairing identity is precisely what relates the dual cumulants to the marginals. For
k=1
and
k=2
, this relation provides quantitative bounds on the convergence of the marginals to the mean-field limit. For higher

, the bounds on the dual cumulants can still be converted into corresponding bounds on the direct cumulants through the relation between the two notions.

We also note that our results are valid only for some fixed time interval of order
1
. This is not unusual with methods loosely based on Cauchy-Kovalevski arguments, such as is the case here to control a hierarchy of differential equations. A natural question is whether these local-in-time optimal estimates can be extended to arbitrary times. Unfortunately, this does not appear to be straightforward in the present case, and we do not have for example the equivalent of Lemma 2.1 in
[
11
]
as the structure of the bounds that we can propagate is different.

We now define the dual cumulants associated with an

Assume that

h_{N}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\ \sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{N}}C_{N,n}(z_{\sigma}),

where
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{N}
denotes the collection of all subsets of
\{1,\dots,N\}
with
n
elements, and
z_{\sigma}=(z_{i})_{i\in\sigma}\in\mathcal{D}^{n}
. By symmetry, the ordering of the variables in
z_{\sigma}
is irrelevant.

The decomposition (
11
) is not unique in general. To fix this ambiguity, we impose a cancellation condition with respect to the density
f
. Specifically, for every
n\geq 1
, the dual cumulant
C_{N,n}
is required to satisfy

\int_{\mathcal{D}}C_{N,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\,f(z_{j})\,dz_{j}=0,\qquad\forall\,1\leq j\leq n.

(12)

By definitions (
11
) and (
12
) above and the cancellation property (
10
) of
V_{f}
, only the second–order dual cumulant contributes to the relation with
h_{N}
, and we obtain the following identity

N\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}^{N}}V_{f}(z_{1},z_{2})\,h_{N}\,f^{\otimes N}\right)dt=N\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}^{2}}V_{f}(z_{1},z_{2})\,C_{N,2}\,f^{\otimes 2}\right)dt.

(13)

Combining (
8
) and (
13
) yields the following relation between propagation of chaos and the second–order dual cumulant

\int_{\mathcal{D}^{m}}\psi^{\otimes m}\left(F_{N,m}(T)-f(T)^{\otimes m}\right)=-N\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}^{2}}V_{f}(z_{1},z_{2})\,C_{N,2}\,f^{\otimes 2}\right)dt.

(14)

2.1.  Truncated Rescaled Hierarchy

Following the previous construction, we consider a bounded weak solution
h_{N}\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{D}^{N})
to the backward Liouville equation (
52
), taken in duality with
F_{N}
. Let
\{C_{N,n}\}_{0\leq n\leq N}
denote the associated dual cumulants introduced above.

In order to derive quantitative estimates, we introduce a rescaled version of these cumulants, denoted by
\bar{C}_{N,n}
, defined by

\bar{C}_{N,n}:={N\choose n}^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{N,n},

following the normalization introduced in
[
4
]
.
These rescaled quantities satisfy a closed hierarchy.

We now recall the rescaled hierarchy satisfied by
\bar{C}_{N,n}
in truncated form. Here the truncation consists in isolating the leading interaction terms, while grouping the remaining contributions into a remainder term
R_{N,n}
. We use the notation
z_{[n]}:=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{C}_{N,n}-L_{n}\bar{C}_{N,n}=R_{N,n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}}V_{f}(z_{\star},z_{j})\,\bar{C}_{N,n}(z_{[n]\setminus\{j\}},z_{\star})\,f(z_{\star})\,dz_{\star}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{(n+1)(n+2)}\int_{\mathcal{D}^{2}}V_{f}(z_{n+1},z_{n+2})\,\bar{C}_{N,n+2}(z_{[n+2]})\,f(z_{n+1})f(z_{n+2})\,dz_{n+1}dz_{n+2},

(15)

where the linear operator
L_{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle L_{n}g:=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(v_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x_{i}}+(K\ast f)(x_{i})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\right)g,

(16)

and the remainder term
R_{N,n}
is given by

\displaystyle R_{N,n}:=\;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-1}\left(\frac{N-n+1}{n}\right)^{1/2}S_{N}^{n,+}\bar{C}_{N,n-1}+\tilde{S}_{N}^{n,\circ}\bar{C}_{N,n}+\left(\frac{n+1}{N-n}\right)^{1/2}S_{N}^{n,-}\bar{C}_{N,n+1}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{(n+1)(n+2)}\left(\left(\frac{(N-n)(N-n-1)}{(N-1)^{2}}\right)^{1/2}-1\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{\mathcal{D}^{2}}V_{f}(z_{n+1},z_{n+2})\,\bar{C}_{N,n+2}(z_{[n+2]})\,f(z_{n+1})\,f(z_{n+2})\,dz_{n+1}dz_{n+2},

(17)

where we denote

\displaystyle S_{N}^{n,+}\bar{C}_{N,n-1}:={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}(K\ast f)(x_{i})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\bar{C}_{N,n-1}(z_{[n]\setminus\{j\}})-\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}K(x_{i}-x_{j})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\bar{C}_{N,n-1}(z_{[n]\setminus\{j\}}),

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}}V_{f}(z_{\star},z_{j})\,\bar{C}_{N,n-1}(z_{[n]\setminus\{i,j\}},z_{\star})\,f(z_{\star})\,dz_{\star}

while

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{N}^{n,\circ}\bar{C}_{N,n}:={}

\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(K\ast f)(x_{i})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\bar{C}_{N,n}-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}K(x_{i}-x_{j})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\bar{C}_{N,n}

\displaystyle-\frac{n-1}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}}V_{f}(z_{\star},z_{j})\,\bar{C}_{N,n}(z_{[n]\setminus\{j\}},z_{\star})\,f(z_{\star})\,dz_{\star}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}}K(x_{i}-x_{\star})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\bar{C}_{N,n}(z_{[n]\setminus\{j\}},z_{\star})\,f(z_{\star})\,dz_{\star}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}}V_{f}(z_{\star},z_{j})\,\bar{C}_{N,n}(z_{[n]\setminus\{i\}},z_{\star})\,f(z_{\star})\,dz_{\star}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i\neq j}^{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}^{2}}V_{f}(z_{\star},z_{\star}^{\prime})\,\bar{C}_{N,n}(z_{[n]\setminus\{i,j\}},z_{\star},z_{\star}^{\prime})\,f(z_{\star})f(z_{\star}^{\prime})\,dz_{\star}dz_{\star}^{\prime},

and finally

\displaystyle S_{N}^{n,-}\bar{C}_{N,n+1}:={}

\displaystyle\frac{N-n}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}}V_{f}(z_{\star},z_{j})\,\bar{C}_{N,n+1}(z_{[n]},z_{\star})\,f(z_{\star})\,dz_{\star}

\displaystyle-\frac{N-n}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}}K(x_{i}-x_{\star})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\bar{C}_{N,n+1}(z_{[n]},z_{\star})\,f(z_{\star})\,dz_{\star}

\displaystyle-2\frac{N-n}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}^{2}}V_{f}(z_{\star},z_{\star}^{\prime})\,\bar{C}_{N,n+1}(z_{[n]\setminus\{i\}},z_{\star},z_{\star}^{\prime})\,f(z_{\star})f(z_{\star}^{\prime})\,dz_{\star}dz_{\star}^{\prime}.

3.  Quantitative Estimates for Dual Cumulants

This section is devoted to deriving estimates on the components of the rescaled dual cumulants introduced in the previous section, as stated in the following theorem

Theorem 4
.

Let
k\geq 1
,
K\in\mathscr{R}_{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
where
k-1<s\leq k
and

\mathscr{R}_{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\begin{cases}L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})+L^{4}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})&\text{if }k=1,\\
W^{k-1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})&\text{if }k\geq 2.\end{cases}

Assume further that
f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathcal{D})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}))
satisfies

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Big(|\nabla_{v}\log f|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big|\tfrac{1}{f}\nabla^{j+1}_{z\cdots zv}f\Big|^{2}\Big)f\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,\qquad K*f\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;W^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\right).

Assume that the rescaled dual cumulants satisfy at the final time
T
that for some
0\leq k_{0}\leq k

\|\bar{C}_{N,n}(T)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}\leq C^{n}\,N^{-k_{0}/2}\quad\mbox{for}\ n\leq k_{0},\quad\|\bar{C}_{N,n}(T)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}\leq C^{n}\,N^{-n/2}\quad\mbox{for}\ n\geq k_{0}.

(18)

Then, there exists a decomposition of the dual cumulants
\bar{C}_{N,n}
as

\bar{C}_{N,n}:=h^{(k)}_{N,n}+\sum_{q=1}^{k}\sum_{i_{1},...,i_{q}=1}^{n}div_{z_{i_{1}}...z_{i_{q}}}w^{(k),i_{1},...,i_{q}}_{N,n},

and some
\Delta t_{k}>0
independent of
N
, such that for all
1\leq q\leq k-1
,
k\leq n\leq N
and for all
t\in[T-\Delta t_{k},T]
, we obtain the bounds

\|w_{N,n}^{(k),i_{1},...,i_{k}}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}\lesssim C_{k}C^{nk}N^{-\frac{k}{2}}

(19)

\|w_{N,n}^{(k),i_{1},...,i_{q}}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}\lesssim C_{k}C^{nk}N^{-\frac{k}{2}}

(20)

\|h_{N,n}^{(k)}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}\lesssim C_{k}C^{nk}N^{-\frac{s}{2}},

(21)

for some
C
independent of
k
and
N
and some
{C}_{k}
behaving at least exponentially. For
n<k
, we have the simpler bounds

\|w_{N,n}^{(k),i_{1},...,i_{k}}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}+\|w_{N,n}^{(k),i_{1},...,i_{q}}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}+\|h_{N,n}^{(k)}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}\lesssim C_{k}C^{nk}\,N^{-\max(k_{0},n)/2}.

(22)

In general we expect the rescaled dual cumulant
\bar{C}_{N,n}
to behave like
C^{n}\,N^{-n/2}
at the final time, or the non-rescaled dual cumulant
C_{N,n}
to behave like
C^{n}\,N^{-n}
. This corresponds to (
18
) with
k_{0}=0
. However in some special cases, the first few dual cumulants may be much smaller, which is why we can allow for
k_{0}>0
.

3.1.  Decomposing the Mean-Field Flow

We need to decompose the
\bar{C}_{N,n}
in terms of various negative derivatives and derive separate equations on each such part.

Throughout this section, we make use of the following decomposition of the interaction operator
V_{f}
previously defined in (
9
)

V_{f}=V_{f}^{\delta}+W_{f}^{\delta},

(23)

with the components given explicitly by

V_{f}^{\delta}(z,z^{\prime})=\big(K_{\delta}(x-x^{\prime})-K\ast f(x)\big)\cdot\nabla_{v}\log f(z),

and

W_{f}^{\delta}(z,z^{\prime})=\big(K(x-x^{\prime})-K_{\delta}(x-x^{\prime})\big)\cdot\nabla_{v}\log f(z).

Here the regularized kernel
K_{\delta}
is defined by

K_{\delta}:=K\ast\rho_{\delta},\quad\rho_{\delta}(x):=\delta^{-d}\,\rho\!\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right),

where
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a smooth mollifier with compact support.

We also introduce the
n
–particle linear operator associated with the transport–drift operator
L_{n}
.
For each
n\geq 1
, let
G^{(n)}_{t,T}
denote the operator acting on functions of

z_{[n]}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathcal{D}^{n}
, generated by the operator
L_{n}
defined in (
16
). Namely the solution
F_{n,i}
to

\partial_{t}F_{n,i}-L_{n}\,F_{n,i}=g_{n,i},\quad F_{n,i}(t=T)=\bar{F}_{n,i}

is given by

F_{n,i}(t,z_{[n]})=G^{(n)}_{t,T}\,\bar{F}_{n,i}+\int_{t}^{T}G^{(n)}_{t,\tau}\,g_{n,i}(\tau)\,d\tau.

Note that
G^{(n)}_{t,T}
is trivially bounded on every
L^{p}
space with an operator norm that is uniform in
t
and
T
.

We now observe that, for each fixed
n
, the truncated hierarchy for
\bar{C}_{N,n}
can be viewed as a linear transport equation of the form

\partial_{t}\bar{C}_{N,n}-L_{n}\bar{C}_{N,n}=g_{n},

where the source term
g_{n}
collects the coupling terms and the remainder
R_{N,n}
. In particular,
g_{n}
admits a decomposition in terms of derivatives of lower- and higher-order cumulants. Because our analysis involves right-hand sides with derivatives, we also need to explain how to commute derivatives with
G^{(n)}
which is provided by the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
k\geq 1
.
Let

A_{n}^{(i)}:=v_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x_{i}}+(K*f)(x_{i})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}},

with
K\ast f\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;W^{k+1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
, and denote by
G^{(n,i)}_{t,T}
the corresponding operator solving the PDE:
(\partial_{t}+A_{n}^{(i)})u=0
.
Then, there exist a family of operators
G^{(n,i)}
and
\{G^{(n,i),\ell,k}\}_{\ell=0}^{k}

bounded over
L^{2}
with an operator norm that depends only on
k
and
T-t

such that

G_{t,T}^{(n,i)}\nabla^{k}_{z_{i}}g_{n,i}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\nabla_{z_{i}}^{\,\ell}G^{(n,i),\ell,k}_{t,T}g_{n,i}.

Proof.

Note that all variables
x_{j}
and
v_{j}
for
j\neq i
are just parameters so that we will not indicate them in the various formulas. Using the method of characteristics, we define the characteristic curves

\dot{X}_{i}(t,s,x,v)=V_{i}(t,s,x,v),\qquad X_{i}(t=s,s,x,v)=x_{i},

and

\dot{V}_{i}(t,s,x,v)=K*f\bigl(X_{i}(t,s,x,v)\bigr),\qquad V_{i}(t=s,s,x,v)=v_{i}.

Define

F_{n,i}(t):=f_{n,i}\bigl(t,X_{i}(t,s,x,v),V_{i}(t,s,x,v)\bigr).

By the chain rule, we compute

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F_{n,i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}f_{n,i}(t,X_{i},V_{i})+\bigl(\dot{X}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x_{i}}+\dot{V}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\bigr)f_{n,i}(t,X_{i},V_{i})

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}f_{n,i}(t,X_{i},V_{i})+A_{n}^{(i)}f_{n,i}(t,X_{i},V_{i})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{z_{i}}^{k}g_{n,i}(t,X_{i},V_{i}).

Integrating from
t
to
T
yields

F_{n,i}(t)=F_{n,i}(T)+\int_{t}^{T}\nabla_{z_{i}}^{k}g_{n,i}\bigl(\tau,X_{i}(\tau,s,x,v),V_{i}(\tau,s,x,v)\bigr)\,d\tau.

Taking
s=t
, we obtain the representation of the operator
G^{(n,i)}_{t,T}
as

G^{(n,i)}_{t,T}\bar{f}_{n,i}=\bar{f}_{n,i}(t,X_{i}(T,t,x,v),V_{i}(T,t,x,v)),

so that
G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}
is naturally bounded on
L^{2}
.

We now need to rewrite
G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}\nabla_{z_{i}}^{k}
as a finite sum of
derivatives acting outside the operator, and we proceed by induction on
k
.

Step 1: Case
k=1
.

Assume that
g_{n,i}
is sufficiently smooth and denote by

Z_{i}(\tau,t,x,v):=(X_{i}(\tau,t,x,v),V_{i}(\tau,t,x,v))

the characteristic flow associated with the one–particle operator
A_{n}^{(i)}
.
By the definition of the backward flow operator, for any test function
h
we have

\big(G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}h\big)(x,v)=h\big(\tau,Z_{i}(\tau,t,x,v)\big).

In particular,

G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}\nabla_{z_{i}}g_{n,i}=\big(\nabla_{z_{i}}g_{n,i}\big)\big(\tau,Z_{i}(\tau,t,x,v)\big).

The key observation is that, since the right–hand side of the equation already
contains one derivative, we do not aim to eliminate derivatives altogether, but
rather to rewrite this expression so that exactly one derivative appears

outside
an operator acting on
g_{n,i}
. By the chain rule,

\nabla_{z_{i}}\!\left(g_{n,i}\big(\tau,Z_{i}\big)\right)=\big(\nabla_{z_{i}}g_{n,i}\big)\big(\tau,Z_{i}\big)\,\nabla Z_{i},

where
\nabla Z_{i}
denotes the Jacobian matrix of the flow with respect to
z_{i}
.
Since the characteristic flow is a diffeomorphism, the matrix
\nabla Z_{i}
is
invertible, and we may therefore write

\big(\nabla_{z_{i}}g_{n,i}\big)\big(\tau,Z_{i}\big)=(\nabla Z_{i})^{-1}\nabla_{z_{i}}\!\left(g_{n,i}\big(\tau,Z_{i}\big)\right).

Applying the product rule to the right–hand side yields the decomposition

\big(\nabla_{z_{i}}g_{n,i}\big)\big(\tau,Z_{i}\big)=\nabla_{z_{i}}\!\left((\nabla Z_{i})^{-1}g_{n,i}\big(\tau,Z_{i}\big)\right)-\nabla_{z_{i}}(\nabla Z_{i})^{-1}\,g_{n,i}\big(\tau,Z_{i}\big).

This naturally leads us to define the operators

G^{(n,i),1,1}_{t,\tau}g_{n,i}:=(\nabla Z_{i})^{-1}\,g_{n,i}\big(\tau,Z_{i}\big),\qquad G^{(n,i),0,1}_{t,\tau}g_{n,i}:=-\nabla_{z_{i}}(\nabla Z_{i})^{-1}\,g_{n,i}\big(\tau,Z_{i}\big),

so that

G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}\nabla_{z_{i}}g_{n,i}=\nabla_{z_{i}}\big(G^{(n,i),1,1}_{t,\tau}g_{n,i}\big)+G^{(n,i),0,1}_{t,\tau}g_{n,i}.

(24)

The matrices
(\nabla Z_{i})^{-1}
and
\nabla_{z_{i}}(\nabla Z_{i})^{-1}
depend only on
the characteristic flow. Under the assumption that
K*f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{2,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, standard Grönwall estimates for the
Jacobian of the flow imply that the coefficients
(\nabla Z_{i})^{-1}
and

\nabla_{z_{i}}(\nabla Z_{i})^{-1}
are bounded uniformly in space on any finite time
interval. Consequently, for
\ell=0,1
, all
0\leq t\leq\tau\leq T
, and all
h\in L^{2}
, the operators

G^{(n,i),\ell,1}_{t,\tau}
are bounded on
L^{2}
, with

\|G^{(n,i),\ell,1}_{t,\tau}h\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1}\,e^{C_{1}\,(T-t)}\|h\|_{L^{2}}.

Inserting identity (
24
) into the solution representation with
k=1

yields the desired result.

Step 2: General case.

Let
G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}\nabla_{z_{i}}^{k}g_{n,i}
for a sufficiently smooth
g_{n,i}
. We prove the corresponding statement at order
k
.
Applying the
k=1
decomposition of Step 1 to the function

h:=\nabla_{z_{i}}^{k-1}g_{n,i}
gives

G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}\nabla_{z_{i}}h=\nabla_{z_{i}}\big(G^{(n,i),1,1}_{t,\tau}h\big)+G^{(n,i),0,1}_{t,\tau}h,

(25)

hence

G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}\nabla_{z_{i}}^{k}g_{n,i}=\nabla_{z_{i}}\Big(G^{(n,i),1,1}_{t,\tau}\nabla_{z_{i}}^{k-1}g_{n,i}\Big)+G^{(n,i),0,1}_{t,\tau}\nabla_{z_{i}}^{k-1}g_{n,i}.

We now repeatedly apply the first-order decomposition from Step 1 to move
derivatives outside the flow operators. Since each application produces only
bounded coefficient operators coming from the characteristic flow and lowers the
number of derivatives falling on
g_{n,i}
by one, after finitely many iterations
we may expand and reindex the resulting finite sum to obtain

G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}\nabla_{z_{i}}^{k}g_{n,i}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\nabla_{z_{i}}^{\,\ell}\big(G^{(n,i),\ell,k}_{t,\tau}g_{n,i}\big),

for a suitable family of bounded operators

\{G^{(n,i),\ell,k}_{t,\tau}\}_{\ell=0}^{k}
.

Finally, by the same reasoning as in Step 1, for
0\leq\ell\leq k
, each
G^{(n,i),\ell,k}_{t,\tau}
is bounded
on
L^{2}
, and we may write

\|G^{(n,i),\ell,k}_{t,\tau}h\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{k}(T-t)\,\|h\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\forall\,h\in L^{2}.

This completes the induction step.
∎

We now write the PDE and the solution representation for the fully tensorized
n
–particle flow with derivatives of order exactly
k
acting across all particle variables. This then yields the corresponding representation with derivatives of order up to
k
.

Lemma 2
.

Let

A_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{n}^{(i)},\qquad L_{n}:=-A_{n},\qquad z_{[n]}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}).

Assume that the source term admits the decomposition

g_{n}(t,z_{[n]})=\sum_{r=0}^{k}\ \sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}=1}^{n}\nabla_{z_{i_{1}}\cdots z_{i_{r}}}\,g_{n}^{(k),i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(t,z_{[n]}).

Consider the terminal value problem

\partial_{t}f_{n}+A_{n}f_{n}=g_{n},\qquad f_{n}(T)=\bar{f}_{n}.

For
i_{[r]}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})\in\{1,\dots,n\}^{r}
, define

S(i_{1},\dots,i_{r}):=\Big\{s\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:\operatorname{supp}(s)\subset\{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\},\ |s|\leq r\Big\}.

Then there exist operators

G^{(n)}_{t,T}\quad\text{and}\quad\big\{G^{(n),s,i_{[r]}}_{t,\tau}\big\}_{r=|s|,\dots,k},

bounded on
L^{2}(\mathcal{D}^{n})
, such that the solution admits the representation

f_{n}(t)=G^{(n)}_{t,T}\bar{f}_{n}+\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}=1}^{n}\sum_{s\in S(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})}\nabla^{\,s}\Big(G^{(n),s,i_{[r]}}_{t,\tau}\,g_{n}^{(k),i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(\tau)\Big)\,d\tau.

Moreover, there exists a constant

C_{k}
such that for all
(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})
, all

s\in S(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})
, and all
h\in L^{2}(\mathcal{D}^{n})

\|G^{(n),s,i_{[r]}}_{t,\tau}h\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D}^{n})}\leq C_{k}(T-t)\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D}^{n})}.

(26)

Proof.

Each one–particle operator
A_{n}^{(i)}
generates a backward flow

G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}
on the variables
(x_{i},v_{i})
. Since the full operator

A_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{n}^{(i)}
is tensorized, the associated
n
–particle backward
flow is given by

G^{(n)}_{t,\tau}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}G^{(n,i)}_{t,\tau}.

A direct computation shows that
G^{(n)}_{t,\tau}
solves

\partial_{t}u+A_{n}u=0
on
\mathcal{D}^{n}
, and therefore Duhamel’s formula yields

f_{n}(t)=G^{(n)}_{t,T}\bar{f}_{n}+\int_{t}^{T}G^{(n)}_{t,\tau}g_{n}(\tau)\,d\tau.

Substituting the decomposition of
g_{n}
and using linearity, we obtain

f_{n}(t)=G^{(n)}_{t,T}\bar{f}_{n}+\int_{t}^{T}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}=1}^{n}G^{(n)}_{t,\tau}\big(\nabla_{z_{i_{1}}\cdots z_{i_{r}}}\,g_{n}^{(k),i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(\tau)\big)\,d\tau.

Fix
r
and a tuple
(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})
. Since
G^{(n)}_{t,\tau}
is tensorized, we
may apply Lemma
1
independently in each of the variables

z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{r}}
, while the remaining factors act only by the (derivative–free)
one–particle flow. Iterating Lemma
1
yields

L^{2}
bounded operators
G^{(n),s,i_{[r]}}_{t,\tau}
such that

G^{(n)}_{t,\tau}\big(\nabla_{z_{i_{1}}\cdots z_{i_{r}}}\,h\big)=\sum_{s\in S(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})}\nabla^{\,s}\Big(G^{(n),s,i_{[r]}}_{t,\tau}\,h\Big).

Applying this identity with

h=g_{n}^{(k),i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(\tau)
and summing over
r
and the indices

(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})
yields the claimed representation formula.

Finally, by construction each operator
G^{(n),s,i_{[r]}}_{t,\tau}
acts nontrivially
only on the particle variables indexed by
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\}
, and coincides with
the derivative–free one–particle flow on all remaining variables. Consequently,

G^{(n),s,i_{[r]}}_{t,\tau}
is obtained by composing and taking finite linear
combinations of the one–particle operators provided by
Lemma
1
in at most

Proposition 5

(28)

\int_{t}^{T}\|w^{(1),i_{1}}_{N,n}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}d\tau\lesssim C^{n}\,N^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(29)

From the assumption on

\displaystyle\|\nabla(f\,V_{f}^{\delta})\|_{L^{2}_{f}}

\displaystyle\|W_{f}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}_{f}}

By Lemma

\displaystyle\|w^{(1),i_{1}}_{N,n}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}\;\lesssim

\displaystyle\|h^{(1)}_{N,n}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}\;\lesssim\;\|\bar{C}_{N,n}(T)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}+C_{1}\Bigg(n\,\Lambda\!\left(\frac{n+1}{N}\right)^{1/2}\;+\;n\!\int_{t}^{T}\|h^{(1)}_{N,n}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}\,d\tau+n\delta^{s}(T-t)\;

We start by hierarchy (
30

Inequality (

\partial_{t}b_{N,n}(t)\;\gtrsim\;-C\Lambda(n+1)\left(b_{N,n}(t)+b_{N,n+2}(t)+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Z_{N}(t,\rho):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho^{n}\left(\,b_{N,n}(t)+C(n+1)N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z_{N}(t,\rho)

\displaystyle\geq-C\Lambda\,\rho\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)\rho^{n-1}b_{N,n}

\displaystyle\quad-C\Lambda\,\frac{1}{\rho}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)\rho^{n+1}\,b_{N,n+2}

(34)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}a_{N,n}(t)\;\gtrsim\;-\|\bar{C}_{N,n}(T)\|_{L^{2}_{f}}-n\Lambda\!\left(\frac{n+1}{N}\right)^{\!1/2}-n\delta^{s}(T-t)-n\delta^{s-1}\,C^{n}\,N^{-\frac{1}{2}}

3.3.  Higher Order Decomposition of the Leading Terms and the Remainder

To prove Theorem
4
, we first establish the following lemmas, which yield an explicit decomposition of both the leading–order and remainder terms in the hierarchy (
2.1
), according to the regularity level of the kernel.

Lemma 4
.

Let
k\geq 1
, and suppose
k-1<s\leq k
. Let
G_{K}\in W^{k-1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Assume that the dual cumulants are decomposed up to their
k-1
derivatives, as follows

\bar{C}_{N,n}:=h^{(k-1)}_{N,n}+\sum_{q=1}^{k-1}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}=1}^{n}div_{z_{i_{1}}\ldots z_{i_{q}}}w^{(k-1),i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}}_{N,n}.

Then one can further derive for any particle index
i\neq 2

\displaystyle G_{K}(x_{i},x_{2})\,\bar{C}_{N,n}=G_{K}(x_{i},x_{2})\,h_{N,n}^{(k-1)}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{q=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=\max(q,1)}^{k-1}\sum_{l=0}^{m-q}\alpha_{q,l,m}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{q+1},\ldots,i_{q+l}=i\\
i_{q+l+1},\ldots,i_{m}=2\end{subarray}}\nabla_{z_{i_{1}}\dots z_{i_{q}}}\Big[(\nabla^{m-q}G_{K})\,w_{N,n}^{(k-1),i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\Big],

where for each
1\leq q\leq k-1
,
q\leq m\leq k-1
, and
0\leq l\leq m-q
,
\alpha_{q,l,m}
is given by

\alpha_{q,l,m}:=\binom{m}{q}\,\binom{m-q}{l}.

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward repeated application of the product rule and integration by parts.
More precisely, fix a term with
m
derivatives in the decomposition of
\bar{C}_{N,n}
, where
1\leq m\leq k-1
. Each term on the right–hand side corresponds to the case where exactly
1\leq q\leq m
derivatives act outside the brackets, while the remaining
m-q
derivatives fall on
G_{K}
. The identity naturally holds up to permutations of the indices of

w_{N,n}^{(k-1),i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}}
, so we can freely assume by symmetry that the first
q
indices correspond to the derivatives outside the brackets. Therefore, for
1\leq q\leq m
, the terms with
q
derivatives
z_{i_{1}}\cdots z_{i_{q}}
outside will be of the form

\bigl(\nabla_{x_{i_{q+1}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}}G_{K}\bigr)w_{N,n}^{(k-1),i_{1},\dots,i_{m}},

where the

5.  Proof of Theorem
2

Given the previously established estimates on the dual cumulants

\bar{C}_{N,n}
, we now aim to derive corresponding bounds for the direct cumulants. We begin by denoting
C_{N,n}^{0}=C_{N,n}|_{t=0}
and recalling the following identity, which relates the unrescaled direct cumulants

\kappa_{N,n}
to the unrescaled dual cumulants
C_{N,n}

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}^{n}}\kappa_{N,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})(t)\,C_{N,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})(t)\,f(t)^{\otimes n}\\
&\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}\int_{\mathcal{D}^{n}}\kappa_{N,n}^{0}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\,C_{N,n}^{0}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\,(f^{0})^{\otimes n}.\end{split}

(48)

This relation follows from the weak duality formulation of the solution,
together with the definitions of the direct and dual cumulants and their
associated cancellation properties. This relation directly implies the corresponding relation on the rescaled cumulants,

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=0}^{N}\int_{\mathcal{D}^{n}}\bar{\kappa}_{N,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})(t)\,\bar{C}_{N,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})(t)\,f(t)^{\otimes n}\\
&\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\int_{\mathcal{D}^{n}}\bar{\kappa}_{N,n}^{0}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\,\bar{C}_{N,n}^{0}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\,(f^{0})^{\otimes n}.\end{split}

(49)

Our objective is to derive a propagation estimate on the direct cumulants

\kappa_{N,n}
using suitable norms.

Proof.

Let
\{\kappa_{N,n}\}_{0\leq n\leq N}
and
\{C_{N,n}\}_{0\leq n\leq N}
denote the
direct and dual cumulants, related by the duality identity (
48
). For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\Pi_{i}
denote the averaging operator in the

i
-th variable with respect to
f
, namely

\Pi_{i}\phi(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}):=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\phi(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i-1},z_{i}^{\prime},z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{n})f(z_{i}^{\prime})\,dz_{i}^{\prime}.

We define

P_{n}:=(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{1})\cdots(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{n}),

and choose the terminal data at level
n
as

\bar{C}_{N,n}(T):=\mathbbm{1}_{n\geq k}N^{-n/2}P_{n}\operatorname{sgn}(\bar{\kappa}_{N,n}(T)).

By construction,
\bar{C}_{N,n}(T)
satisfies the cancellation condition (
12

each variable. Indeed, since
(\Pi_{i})^{2}=\Pi_{i}

\Pi_{i}(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{i})=0,

and therefore

By the definition of
\Pi_{i}
, this is precisely the cancellation condition. The final data above also satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
4

is bounded in

is a contraction on

\|P_{n}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2^{n}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Therefore,

\|P_{n}\operatorname{sgn}(\bar{\kappa}_{N,n}(T))\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2^{n}.

Using the relation

C_{N,n}(T)=\binom{N}{n}^{-1/2}\bar{C}_{N,n}(T),

we obtain

\displaystyle\|h_{N}(T)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=k}^{N}\sum_{\sigma\in P_{n}^{N}}\|C_{N,n}(T,z_{\sigma})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=k}^{N}\binom{N}{n}\binom{N}{n}^{-1/2}N^{-n/2}\|P_{n}\operatorname{sgn}(\bar{\kappa}_{N,n}(T))\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=k}^{N}2^{n}N^{-n/2}\binom{N}{n}^{1/2}\leq\sum_{n=k}^{N}\frac{2^{n}}{(n!)^{1/2}}\lesssim 1.

This is actually the most stringent requirement as it is the one that forces us to take the

Since

We begin by introducing the
backward Liouville equation
associated with the
N
–particle dynamics. Given a final time
T>0
, we consider test functions

h_{N}:[0,T]\times\mathcal{D}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\partial_{t}h_{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(v_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x_{i}}h_{N}+\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}K(x_{i}-x_{j})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}h_{N}\right)=0,

(52)

with prescribed terminal condition
h_{N}(T)=h_{N}^{T}
.

Equation (
52
) is the adjoint, in the sense of distributions, of the forward Liouville equation (
2
) satisfied by the
N
–particle density.

The backward formulation plays a central role in our analysis, as it allows us to define solutions to the forward Liouville equation (
2
) through a duality principle, without requiring strong regularity or uniqueness properties of the underlying particle flow. In particular, this approach is well suited to interaction kernels
K
of low regularity, for which classical notions of solutions may fail to be available.

Definition 6
.

We say that a family of probability densities
F_{N}
is a
weak duality solution
to the Liouville equation (
2
) with initial data
F_{N}^{0}
if, for every final time
T>0
and every admissible bounded terminal test function
h_{N}^{T}
, there exists a bounded weak solution
h_{N}
of the backward Liouville equation (
52
) with terminal data
h_{N}^{T}
such that

\int_{\mathcal{D}^{N}}h_{N}^{T}(z)\,F_{N}^{T}(z)\,dz=\int_{\mathcal{D}^{N}}h_{N}^{0}(z)\,F_{N}^{0}(z)\,dz,

(53)

where
F_{N}^{T}:=F_{N}(T)
and
h_{N}^{0}:=h_{N}(0)
.

Identity (
53
) expresses the conservation of the pairing between the forward density
F_{N}
and backward observables
h_{N}
along the dynamics.

This notion of solution is particularly adapted to the duality-based approach developed in this work. By choosing appropriate families of backward solutions
h_{N}^{T}
, one can extract quantitative information on particle correlations and propagate bounds backward in time, which in turn yields control of the forward
N
–particle density.
