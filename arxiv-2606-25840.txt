Search for long-lived particles decaying into muons in proton-proton collisions at s = 13.6 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=13.6\TeV using data scouting


Abstract
A search for long-lived particles decaying into muons is performed using proton-proton collisions at s = 13.6 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=13.6\TeV , collected by the CMS experiment at the LHC in 2022 and 2023, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 62.4 \fbinv . The data were collected using dedicated dimuon triggers with low transverse momentum thresholds, recorded with a high-rate data scouting trigger stream. This data stream retains a reduced amount of information at the high-level trigger, to explore otherwise inaccessible phase space at low multimuon invariant mass and nonzero displacement from the primary interaction vertex. No significant excess of events above the standard model prediction is found. Upper limits on branching fractions at 95% confidence level are set for a wide range of mass and lifetime hypotheses in several beyond the standard model frameworks, where the Higgs boson decays into long-lived dark photons or into dark partons that produce showers containing long-lived particles, or where a long-lived scalar resonance is produced from the decay of a \PQb hadron. The resulting constraints improve and extend existing ones in large regions of the parameter space.

This paper presents a search for narrow, long-lived multimuon resonances, based on data collected with the CMS experiment
in proton-proton (
\Pp\Pp
) collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13.6\TeV
in 2022 and 2023.
A dedicated dimuon trigger stream (
scouting

[
33
]
) with low transverse momentum (
\pt
) thresholds,
recorded at high rate by retaining a reduced amount of information at the high-level trigger (HLT) is used.
The rate of scouting triggers is higher than that
of the standard triggers owing to less stringent requirements
that enable multimuon resonance searches across mass and lifetime ranges
that are otherwise inaccessible.
The selected data correspond to a total integrated luminosity of 62.4
\fbinv
(
34.6
and 27.8
\fbinv
collected in 2022 and 2023, respectively).

The search targets narrow, low-mass, long-lived resonances decaying into pairs of oppositely charged muons,
where the lifetime of the long-lived particle is such that the transverse displacement (
l_{\mathrm{xy}}
) of its decay vertex is within roughly
70\cm

of the primary vertex (PV) of the hard scattering.
The signal is expected to appear as a narrow peak on top of a smooth multimuon invariant mass distribution,
with a resonance width that is assumed to be smaller than the experimental mass resolution.
The search is performed by looking for localized excesses in the dimuon invariant mass spectrum
across different ranges of
l_{\mathrm{xy}}
.
We probe, for the first time in CMS, multimuon final states with large displacements and masses below 10
\GeV
,
which have not been explored so far owing to the limitations of the standard trigger streams and of the dimuon scouting triggers used by CMS before 2022.
In particular, the improvements in the dimuon scouting triggers allow for the reconstruction of displaced muon pairs beyond 11
\cm
in
l_{\mathrm{xy}}
,
which was a limitation of the analysis presented in Ref.
[
24
]
,
where data collected with the scouting triggers available in 2017 and 2018 were used.
Moreover, the extended event content available in the scouting data collected since 2022 is exploited
to further improve the sensitivity of the search.

and

The event selection and categorization strategies are discussed in Section

,
while Section

Finally, results are presented in Section

0.2
The CMS detector

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6
\unit
m internal diameter
providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T. Within the solenoid volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a
lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter, each composed of
a barrel and two endcap sections. Forward calorimeters extend the pseudorapidity (
\eta
) coverage provided by the
barrel and endcap detectors. Muons are reconstructed using gas-ionization detectors interleaved with the layers of the steel flux-return yoke outside
the solenoid.
A global “particle-flow” (PF) algorithm
[
18
]
aims to reconstruct all individual particles in an event,
combining information provided by the all-silicon tracker and by the calorimeters with data from the gas-ionization muon detectors.
The pixel tracker consists of four concentric barrel layers at radii of 2.9, 6.8, 10.9, and 16.0
\cm
,
and three disks on each end at distances of 29.1, 39.6, and 51.6
\cm
from the center of the detector
[
3
]
.
The region between 20 and 116
\cm
is occupied by the silicon strip tracker.
Four barrel layers, supplemented by three disk in the endcaps,
compose the tracker inner barrel and disks,
which extends the radius up to 55
\cm
. Beyond this range,
the tracker outer barrel and the tracker endcaps are situated,
adding six layers and nine additional disks at each end.
A more detailed description of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the coordinate system used and the relevant
kinematic variables, can be found in Refs.
[
17
,
26
]
.

A two-level trigger system is used to select events of potential physics interest.
The first level of the CMS trigger system (L1)
[
21
]
, composed of custom hardware processors, uses information from the
calorimeters and muon detectors to select events of interest in a fixed time interval of about 4
\unit
\mus
.
The HLT processor farm further decreases the event rate from around 100
\unit
kHz to a few
\unit
kHz, before data storage.
A more detailed description of the CMS trigger system can be found in Refs.
[
19
,
29
]
.

In addition to the standard HLT selection streams,
a dedicated set of triggers exists that allows the exploration of additional phase space.
This approach is referred to as
data scouting

[
33
]
.
The scouting trigger algorithms used in this search
select events containing muon pairs with a dimuon invariant mass greater than the muon mass (
m_{\PGm\PGm}\gtrsim 2m_{\PGm}
) at a rate of a few
\unit
kHz.
In order to allow the large rate within an acceptable bandwidth,
the event size is reduced by up to a factor of 1000
by only retaining limited information, as reconstructed at the HLT.

0.3
Data sets and triggers

The standard trigger streams typically select events containing at least one particle (
\eg
, a lepton) or jet with large
\pt
.
Thus, such triggers are not necessarily ideal in a generic search for light, narrow resonances decaying into two muons with
\pt\lesssim 10\GeV
.
Muons are reconstructed at the HLT
by combining the information from the tracker and the muon detectors.
The retained limited information includes their four-momenta,
the number of hits left in the tracker and in the muon detectors, and detailed information on the muon tracks.
These muon tracks are used in pairs to form dimuon vertices, considering all possible pairs.
In the following, these vertices are called secondary vertices (SVs),
and they may be either displaced from the PV or not.
Candidate
\Pp\Pp
interaction vertices are formed
using tracks reconstructed from hits only in the pixel detector,
and the candidate vertex with the largest value of summed track
\pt^{2}
is taken to be the PV of the
\Pp\Pp
interaction.
Events in the scouting data sets contain information on the normalized
\chi^{2}
of all reconstructed vertices,
together with their position with respect to the nominal interaction point.

Between 2018 and 2022, the CMS muon scouting triggers
[
35
]
were updated,
broadening the accessible phase space and increasing the amount of information retained for each event.
The main improvement arises from the removal of the pixel hit multiplicity requirement,
which previously prevented any muon with
<
2 pixel hits from being accepted by the scouting triggers.
As a result, the muons with
l_{\mathrm{xy}}>11\cm
are now accessible for the first time in a search using scouting data.
Additionally, more detailed information on tracks and PF candidates is retained
and used to improve the background rejection, as described in Section
0.5.1
.

Events are preselected at the L1 trigger
with at least two muons passing requirements on the
\pt
,
\eta
and charge of each muon
and on their angular separation,
defined as
\Delta R(\PGm_{1},\PGm_{2})=\sqrt{\smash[b]{[\Delta\phi(\PGm_{1},\PGm_{2})]^{2}+[\Delta\eta(\PGm_{1},\PGm_{2})]^{2}}}
,
where
\phi
is the azimuthal angle and
\PGm_{1}
(
\PGm_{2}
) is the leading (subleading) muon.

In 2022, events were required to pass at least one of three criteria:

•

the presence of at least two opposite-sign (OS) L1 muons with

\pt^{\PGm}>4\GeV
and
\Delta R(\PGm_{1},\PGm_{2})<1.2
;

•

the presence of at least two OS L1 muons with
\pt^{\PGm}>4.5\GeV
and
\abs{\eta^{\PGm}}<2.0
; or

•

the presence of at least two L1 muons with
\pt^{\PGm_{1}}>15\GeV
and
\pt^{\PGm_{2}}>7\GeV
.

In 2023, two additional L1 triggers were added, requiring:

•

the presence of at least two OS L1 muons with
\abs{\eta^{\PGm}}<1.4
and
\Delta R(\PGm_{1},\PGm_{2})<1.4
; or

•

the presence of at least two L1 muons with
\pt^{\PGm}>8\GeV
.

The measurement of the muon momentum at the L1 trigger relies only on the information from the muon detectors.
After the L1 trigger preselections, only events containing two OS muons
with
\pt^{\PGm}>3\GeV
and
\abs{\eta^{\PGm}}<2.4
are retained at the HLT,
where the inner tracker information improves the muon
\pt
measurement and spatial resolution.

The efficiency of the scouting triggers is measured in data,
as a function of the dimuon
l_{\mathrm{xy}}
and of the minimum
\pt^{\PGm}
of the pair,
using events with at least two muons selected with independent, orthogonal triggers.
The measured efficiency and its uncertainty are accounted for in the normalization of simulated events, as discussed in Section
0.4
.

Because only a reduced amount of information is available in the scouting data,
no additional calibration is performed for the muons used in this search.
The resulting dimuon mass resolution was measured to differ by less than 1% from that obtained
using muons that undergo the full offline reconstruction
[
33
]
,
which has a negligible effect on the results presented in this paper.

0.4
Monte Carlo simulation

The SM background is estimated directly from data in the dimuon (
m_{\PGm\PGm}
) or four-muon (
m_{4\PGm}
) invariant mass spectrum,
parameterized by smooth analytical functions.
Thus, the search does not rely on MC simulation in order to estimate the background.

On the other hand, MC simulations of BSM signals are used to interpret the results of the search.
In particular, a model of Higgs boson production via the dominant mode of gluon-gluon fusion is simulated, with
\PH\to\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}
,
where
\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}
is a long-lived resonance and at least one
\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}
decays into a pair of OS muons.
The phenomenology of this model is defined by four parameters:

•

the kinetic mixing
\epsilon
;

•

the mass of the dark photon,
m_{\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}}
;

•

the Higgs portal coupling,
\kappa
; and

•

the mass of the dark Higgs boson,
m_{\PH_{\mathrm{D}}}
.

In the simulation used in this search,
m_{\PH_{\mathrm{D}}}
and
\kappa
are fixed to 400
\GeV
and 0.01, respectively,
in a regime where
\PH\to\PH_{\mathrm{D}}\PH_{\mathrm{D}}
decays are kinematically forbidden.
Instead, the Higgs boson is set to always decay into a pair of
\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}
bosons,
which in turn decay into SM fermions with branching fractions that depend on their mass
[
36
]
.
The branching fraction for
\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}
decays into muon pairs varies from about 50% at a mass of 0.5
\GeV
to about 9% at a mass of 50
\GeV
.
The lifetime of the
\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}
boson for a given mass hypothesis is determined by
\epsilon
.
In this paper, dark photons are simulated with
c\tau_{0}^{\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}}=0.1
, 1, 10, and
100\cm
, except for masses below 1
\GeV
,
where only
c\tau_{0}^{\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}}=0.1
and
1\cm
scenarios are simulated to account for the variation in acceptance
due to the large Lorentz boost of the particles, which results in longer decay lengths for low-mass resonances.
A lifetime-based reweighting procedure is additionally employed to probe intermediate lifetime values.
The production of dark photons is modeled at leading order (LO) with
\MGvATNLO
2.9.9
[
6
]
.
While only the dominant gluon-gluon fusion production mechanism is considered for the generation,
the Higgs boson production cross section is normalized to the most recent theoretical prediction for the sum of
all production modes for the mass of Higgs boson
m_{\PH}=125\GeV
at
\sqrt{s}=13.6\TeV
,
which is 59.8
\unit
pb
[
16
,
45
]
.
The decays of dark photons are simulated using
\PYTHIA
8.306
[
14
]
.
These samples are used to set upper limits on the branching fraction

\mathcal{B}(\PH\to\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}\PZ_{\mathrm{D}})
,
using values of
\mathcal{B}(\PZ_{\mathrm{D}}\to\PGm\PGm)
from the model of Ref.
[
36
]
.

Dark-shower signal models are simulated with
\PYTHIA
8.309 for selected mass combinations of Scenarios A and B1,
considering two dark flavors and three dark colors.
In these models, the SM Higgs boson is set to decay into a pair of dark partons
\HepParticle{\Uppsi}{}{}\HepAntiParticle{\Uppsi}{}{}
,
which then undergo showering and hadronization within the dark sector.
The Higgs boson production cross section is normalized to
59.8\unit{pb}

[
16
,
45
]
.
The mass scale is set by assuming
\tilde{\Lambda}=m_{\HepSusyParticle{\PGh}{}{}}=4m_{\HepSusyParticle{\PGp}{}{}}
,
where
\tilde{\Lambda}
is the dark-sector confinement scale.
The coupling of the dark photon to dark-sector quarks controls the isospin violation through the mixing angle
\theta
.
We consider
\sin\theta=0.1
to ensure that isospin breaking remains relatively small,
resulting in the dark pions having nearly degenerate masses.
The branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(\HepSusyParticle{\PGp}{}{}_{3}\to\HepParticle{A}{}{\prime}\HepParticle{A}{}{\prime})
is set to unity.
Samples are generated with different mass hypotheses for the
\HepSusyParticle{\PGp}{}{}_{3}
and the
\HepParticle{A}{}{\prime}
particles,
and with mean proper decay lengths ranging from
0.01
to
100\cm
for
\HepParticle{A}{}{\prime}
(
\HepSusyParticle{\PGp}{}{}_{3}
) in Scenario A (B1).
These samples are used to set upper limits on the branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(\PH\to\HepParticle{\Uppsi}{}{}\HepAntiParticle{\Uppsi}{}{})
.

For models where a long-lived scalar resonance
\PGf
arises from the decay of a
\PQb
hadron,
the production of
\PQb
hadrons is initially generated at LO using
\PYTHIA
8.306,
where the
\PGf
boson decays into a pair of OS muons with 100% probability.
Subsequently, a fixed-order calculation at next-to-leading logarithmic (FONLL) precision in the strong coupling constant
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}

is used to normalize the signal and to reweight the
\pt
spectrum of
\PQb
hadrons.
These samples are used to set upper limits on the branching fraction product
\mathcal{B}(\Ph_{\PQb}\to\PGf X)\mathcal{B}(\PGf\to\PGm\PGm)
.

The generators use the CP5 tune
[
20
]
and
the NNPDF3.1 sets of parton distribution functions (PDFs)
[
10
]
at next-to-next-to-LO.
The interactions of particles with the CMS detector are simulated using
\GEANTfour

[
5
]
.
Simulated minimum bias events are superimposed on the hard
\Pp\Pp
interaction to describe
the effect of additional inelastic interactions within the same or neighboring bunch crossings (pileup).
The simulation is reweighted to match the distribution of the number of interactions per bunch crossing observed in data.
All simulated events are reconstructed with the same algorithms used for data.

0.5
Event selection and categorization

0.5.1
Event selection

The events collected using the scouting triggers (Section
0.3
)
are further selected to contain at least one pair of OS muons associated with an SV
that satisfies a number of quality criteria:

•

l_{\mathrm{xy}}<70\cm
;

•

x
coordinate uncertainty
<
0.05
\cm
;

•

y
coordinate uncertainty
<
0.05
\cm
;

•

z
coordinate uncertainty
<
0.10
\cm
;

•

The SV fit
\chi^{2}
normalized to the number of degrees of freedom (dof)
\left(\chi^{2}/\mathrm{dof}\right)_{\mathrm{SV}}<3
.

The
\left(\chi^{2}/\mathrm{dof}\right)_{\mathrm{track}}
of each muon track is also required to be less than 3.
Furthermore, any SV located within a tracker module is not considered in order to suppress the contribution from material interaction vertices,

\ie
, vertices from particles produced in the interactions with the detector material.

A large fraction of events contain only one OS muon pair and an associated SV.
Among the events with more than one pair of OS muons,
the pairs are ordered according to increasing
(\chi^{2}/\mathrm{dof})_{\mathrm{SV}}<3

and only the first or the first two pairs are used in this search.
If two muon pairs are reconstructed, the positions of the corresponding SVs may be spatially resolved or may overlap within uncertainties,
thus appearing as a single four-muon (
4\PGm
) vertex.
In this case, the selections are adapted accordingly, as detailed in the following.

For decays of BSM particles into a single muon pair, we expect the transverse momentum of the dimuon system,
\vec{p}_{\,\mathrm{T}}^{\PGm\PGm}
,
to be collinear with the SV vector, which is defined as the vector
\overrightarrow{\mathrm{SV}}
connecting the PV to the SV.
This does not apply to a muon pair where the two muons originate from different vertices
and erroneously form a common SV because their trajectories accidentally cross..
In order to suppress this background, we require both the azimuthal angle (
\abs{\Delta\phi(\vec{p}_{\,\mathrm{T}}^{\PGm\PGm},\overrightarrow{\mathrm{SV}})}
)
and the three-dimensional angle between the two vectors to be less than
\pi/2
.
This requirement is used in events with one or two resolved SVs.
In events where four muons are selected from two SVs that overlap in space, hereafter referred to as
overlapping
SVs,
we require the four-muon transverse momentum,
\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{4\PGm}
, to be collinear with
the vector connecting the PV with the overlapping SVs.
The same requirements on the azimuthal and three-dimensional angles are applied.

Similarly, the opening angle between two muons erroneously forming a common SV tends to be very large, independently of the boost of the dimuon system.
Hence, in order to further reduce the background from events with an erroneous muon SV association, we also require both the azimuthal and three-dimensional angles between the muons to be
<
2.8.
The main goal of such selections is to suppress background where SVs are formed by cosmic ray muons, muons from pileup interactions,
and muons from back-to-back quantum chromodynamics dijet events or Drell–Yan events.

Muons from pileup vertices can overlap in the
r
-
\phi
plane, despite being separated in
r
-
z
at their origin, and,
as a consequence, they can be wrongly associated with a common SV.
These muon pairs are characterized by a large
\Delta\eta(\PGm_{1},\PGm_{2})
and a small
\Delta\phi(\PGm_{1},\PGm_{2})
.
We exploit this feature to suppress this background contribution with the
requirement
\log_{10}(\abs{\Delta\eta_{\PGm\PGm}}/\abs{\Delta\phi_{\PGm\PGm}})<1.25
.
This requirement is not applied in cases with two selected muon pairs.

Additional background may arise from “prompt” (
\ie
, nondisplaced) muons.
Muons from truly displaced vertices do not leave hits in the tracker layers between the interaction point and the SV.
Therefore, this background can be suppressed by applying a selection on the number of hits associated with each muon between the PV and the SV,
referred to as
N_{\text{excess}}^{\PGm\PGm}
:

•

N_{\text{excess}}^{\PGm\PGm}=0
for
l_{\mathrm{xy}}<11\cm
;

•

N_{\text{excess}}^{\PGm\PGm}\leq 1
for
l_{\mathrm{xy}}\in[11,16]\cm
;

•

N_{\text{excess}}^{\PGm\PGm}\leq 2
for
l_{\mathrm{xy}}\in[16,70]\cm
.

The requirement is loosened for higher displacements, because at large values of
l_{\mathrm{xy}}
it is more likely that random hits are wrongly associated with displaced muon tracks,
and is fully relaxed in events where four muons from two overlapping SVs are selected.

In order to identify SVs of interest,
the muon track transverse impact parameter,
d_{\mathrm{xy}}
, is also used.
Specifically, we select events based on the
d_{\mathrm{xy}}
significance (
\abs{d_{\mathrm{xy}}}/\sigma_{d_{\mathrm{xy}}}
) and the lifetime-scaled
d_{\mathrm{xy}}
(
\abs{d_{\mathrm{xy}}}/(l_{\mathrm{xy}}m_{\PGm\PGm}/\pt^{\PGm\PGm})
).
The first expression normalizes
d_{\mathrm{xy}}
to its measurement uncertainty (
\sigma_{d_{\mathrm{xy}}}
),
while the second normalizes
d_{\mathrm{xy}}
to the lifetime corresponding to the observed SV transverse displacement.
This allows the same selection requirements to be used for all lifetime hypotheses.
We require each muon in dimuon events to satisfy:

•

\abs{d_{\mathrm{xy}}}/\sigma_{d_{\mathrm{xy}}}>2
;

•

\abs{d_{\mathrm{xy}}}/(l_{\mathrm{xy}}m_{\PGm\PGm}/\pt^{\PGm\PGm})>0.1
.

In events with two muon pairs, where there are few background events, the selected muons are only required to have
\abs{d_{\mathrm{xy}}}/\sigma_{d_{\mathrm{xy}}}>2
.

Finally, in events with two muon pairs from spatially resolved SVs,
the two dimuon invariant masses are further required to differ by less than
5\%
of their average.

The muon isolation is measured as the scalar
\pt
sum of the additional PF candidates
within a cone of
\Delta R=\sqrt{\smash[b]{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}}=0.4
around its direction,
including corrections for the contributions from pileup.
A muon is considered isolated if its isolation is less than 8
\GeV
and less than 20% of its
\pt
.
These isolation criteria are only used to categorize events with only one selected muon pair, as described in Section
0.5.2
.

0.5.2
Event categorization

Once the objects have been selected, three types of events are considered in the search:

•

events with one muon pair;

•

events with two muon pairs from two SVs whose positions are spatially resolved; and

•

events with two muon pairs from two SVs whose positions overlap within uncertainties,
consistent with a single
4\PGm
vertex.

Because
4\PGm
events are expected to provide sensitivity to a range of BSM scenarios and are depleted in background, we first select events with two muon pairs.
If an event contains four muons from two resolved SVs,
we then assign the event to the so-called
multivertex
category.
If instead we identify four muons from two overlapping SVs, we assign the event to the
overlapping vertex
category.
In both cases, the signal would appear as a narrow peak in the
m_{4\PGm}
spectrum.

If only one muon pair is found, the event is categorized as a dimuon event.
For dimuon events, we first define two orthogonal regions in
\abs{\Delta\phi(\vec{p}_{\,\mathrm{T}}^{\PGm\PGm},\overrightarrow{\mathrm{SV}})}
,
distinguishing
pointing
and
nonpointing
topologies,
with
\abs{\Delta\phi(\vec{p}_{\,\mathrm{T}}^{\PGm\PGm},\overrightarrow{\mathrm{SV}})}<0.02
and
0.02<\abs{\Delta\phi(\vec{p}_{\,\mathrm{T}}^{\PGm\PGm},\overrightarrow{\mathrm{SV}})}<\pi/2
, respectively.

Dimuon events in both the pointing and nonpointing categories are then further split according to
l_{\mathrm{xy}}
,
to maximize the sensitivity to different lifetime hypotheses.
We define eight bins in
l_{\mathrm{xy}}
, following the detector geometry:

•

within the beam pipe: [0, 0.2]
\cm
; [0.2, 1]
\cm
; [1, 2.4]
\cm
;

•

between the beam pipe and the first pixel tracker layer: [2.4, 3.1]
\cm
;

•

between the first and second pixel tracker layers: [3.1, 7]
\cm
;

•

between the second and third pixel tracker layers: [7, 11]
\cm
;

•

between the third and fourth pixel tracker layers: [11, 16]
\cm
; and

•

outside the pixel tracker: [16, 70]
\cm
.

The last two
l_{\mathrm{xy}}
intervals probe phase space that was inaccessible to previous results using scouting data
[
24
]
.

l_{\mathrm{xy}}

\PQb

Two categories are defined in
\pt^{\PGm\PGm}

•

\pt^{\PGm\PGm}<25\GeV

•

\pt^{\PGm\PGm}>25\GeV

\pt^{\PGm\PGm}

0.6
Parameterization of the invariant mass distribution

The search is performed by fitting the sum of signal and the SM background models to mass distributions in the various categories.
We target low-mass multimuon resonances whose intrinsic width is assumed to be much narrower than the detector resolution.
The signal multimuon invariant mass resolution
(
\sigma_{\text{mass}}^{\PGm\PGm}/m_{\PGm\PGm}\sim\sigma_{\text{mass}}^{4\PGm}/m_{4\PGm}
) is estimated from simulated events to be about 1.8% of the mass,
with an uncertainty of about 50%, and both are independent of the signal mass and lifetime hypotheses.
We use the sum of a Gaussian function and a double-sided Crystal Ball function
[
54
,
42
]
,
with common mean and width (
\sigma_{\text{mass}}
) parameters,
to model signal invariant mass distributions in dimuon and
4\PGm
events.
The parameters are determined by fitting the corresponding signal MC simulation.
Additionally, an interpolation of the signal model parameters is performed between different mass hypotheses
to provide a continuous description of the signal shape across the probed mass range.

In each event category,
the SM background is modeled in sliding windows of
\pm 5\sigma_{\text{mass}}
around the considered signal mass hypothesis.
These windows are required not to overlap with the masked mass regions corresponding to known SM resonances
(as discussed in Section
0.5.2
).
A number of functional forms are considered in order to model the background mass distribution.
These include Bernstein polynomials, power law, and exponential functions.
For each family of functional forms,
the best order in each mass window and event category is selected
by means of a Fisher test
[
41
]
.
First, the lowest order (
N
) function is used to fit the data.
Then, the next-order (
N+1
) is used, and the difference

2\Delta\mathrm{NLL}_{N+1}=2(\mathrm{NLL}_{N}-\mathrm{NLL}_{N+1})

(with NLL denoting the negative logarithm of the likelihood of the fit)
is evaluated to determine whether the data support the need for a higher-order function.
This decision is based on the fact that

2\Delta\mathrm{NLL}_{N+1}
is asymptotically described by a
\chi^{2}
distribution with
M
degrees of freedom,
where
M
is the difference in the number of free parameters in the
(N+1)
th and
N
th order functions (in this case
M=1
).
A
p
-value
[
43
]
is calculated as
P_{M}(\chi^{2}>2\Delta\mathrm{NLL}_{N+1})
where
P_{M}(\chi_{\mathrm{min}}^{2})

is the
\chi^{2}
tail probability for
M
degrees of freedom.
If the
p
-value is less than 0.05,
the higher-order function is retained,
since it is determined to significantly improve the description of the data.
Once the best order
N
for each family of functions has been determined,
the corresponding functional forms are included in an envelope, which is then used
in the discrete profiling method
[
37
]
,
where the choice of the background function is treated as a discrete nuisance parameter in the fit
to account for the uncertainty associated with the arbitrary choice of the function.
In addition, we use a goodness-of-fit test to remove background models that do not describe the data appropriately.
The test is based on a
\chi^{2}
test statistic, which is converted into a
p
-value
through a set of pseudo-experiments. Models with
p<0.01
are not considered.

The potential presence of a bias in the measurement of a signal due to the choice of the functional forms used to model the SM background is also assessed
by means of pseudo-experiments.
First, a varying amount of signal is injected on top of the background generated according to the selected functional form.
Then, a background+signal fit is performed allowing the signal yield to float freely.
The bias, quantified as the difference between the measured and injected signal yields relative to the statistical uncertainty in the measured signal yield,
is found to be negligible.

The background-only fit results for the selected functional forms in one of the dimuon search bins using 2022 data
are shown in Fig.

together with the dimuon invariant mass distribution expected for a representative signal hypothesis.

0.7
Results and interpretation

Unbinned maximum likelihood fits to the data
are performed simultaneously in all search bins,
under either background-only or background+signal hypotheses, using background and signal
models and uncertainties described in Section
0.6
.
Additional log-normal constraint terms are used to account
for the uncertainties in the signal yields, when considered (Section
0.7.1
).

No significant peak-like structures are observed in data.
The background+signal fits are used to set 95% confidence level (
\CL
) upper limits
on the branching fractions for the signal models under consideration.
Limits are set using a modified frequentist approach, employing the
\CLs
criterion
[
44
,
56
,
7
]

within the CMS statistical analysis tool
Combine

[
31
]
,
which is based on the
RooFit

[
57
]
and
RooStats

[
53
]
frameworks.
These limits are then used, in conjunction
with the theoretical cross section calculations,
to exclude ranges of masses and/or lifetimes
for the BSM particles of the signal models.

0.7.1
Systematic uncertainties in the signal yields

A systematic uncertainty in the trigger efficiency
(discussed in Section
0.3
) is assessed
to be in the range between 1 and 15%,
depending on the
\pt
and displacement of the muons.
A separate systematic uncertainty is considered, ranging from 1 to 30%, to account for the potential mismodeling of the signal simulation
that may introduce a difference in the event selection acceptance for the selection described in Section
0.5.1
.
Both uncertainties are derived from studies of
\PJGy\to\PGmp\PGmm
data
and account for the differences between the measurements in data and simulation.
Additionally, we assess a systematic uncertainty in the expected signal yields
arising from the uncertainty in the integrated luminosity measurement,
of 1.4 (1.3)% for 2022 (2023) data
[
23
,
27
,
28
]
.
The overall uncertainty in the luminosity measurement for the 2022–2023 period is 1.01%.
Finally, we account for uncertainties resulting from the limited size of the simulated signal samples.
Uncertainties arising from the choice of the PDFs,
and of the renormalization and factorization scales used in the event generators are negligible compared to others.
Uncertainties in the trigger efficiency and integrated luminosity are treated as correlated across the search bins.
Other uncertainties are taken as uncorrelated.
Since we find no significant difference between 2022 and 2023 data and simulations,
all uncertainties are treated as correlated across the two data-taking periods.

Summary

A search for displaced multimuon resonances has been performed using proton-proton collisions
at a center-of-mass energy of 13.6
\TeV
,
collected by the CMS experiment at the LHC in 2022 and 2023, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 62.4
\fbinv
.
The data were collected using a dedicated dimuon trigger stream with low transverse momentum thresholds,
recorded at high rate by retaining a reduced amount of information at the high-level trigger,
to explore otherwise inaccessible phase space at low multimuon invariant mass and nonzero displacement from the primary interaction vertex.
No significant excess above the standard model prediction is found.
The data are used to set constraints on a wide range of mass and lifetime hypotheses for various scenarios of physics beyond the standard model.
Three sets of models are considered:
the hidden Abelian Higgs model, where the Higgs boson decays into a pair of long-lived dark photons;
dark-shower models, where dark showers originate from the Higgs boson decays into dark quarks;
and models where a long-lived scalar resonance is produced from the decay of a
\PQb
hadron.
In models with a
\PQb
hadron, the improved trigger and analysis strategy compensate for the smaller data set,
leading to constraints comparable with the previous results.
For other models, the constraints obtained are instead the most stringent to date in a large fraction of the explored parameter space.
In the hidden Abelian Higgs model,
the most stringent limits to date are obtained for dark-photon proper decay lengths greater than 1
\cm
and
dark-photon masses less than 5
\GeV
.
In the dark-shower models, the most stringent limits to date are set for proper decay lengths larger than 10
\cm
.
