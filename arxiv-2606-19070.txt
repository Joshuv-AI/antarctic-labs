A Measure-Valued Obstacle Problem for an Obliquely Reflected Diffusion with a Max-Type Payoff
Louis Shuo Wang, Jiguang Yu, Ye Liang

Abstract
We study an obliquely reflected optimal stopping problem in the nonnegative quadrant with nonsmooth max-type payoff G ⁡ ( x ) = x 1 ∨ α ​ x 2 G(x)=x_{1}\vee\alpha x_{2} , and we develop a measure-valued potential-theoretic formulation of the associated obstacle problem. The kink of G G on the diagonal x 1 = α ​ x 2 x_{1}=\alpha x_{2} produces a singular surface measure in the distributional generator, while the oblique reflection directions generate boundary local-time contributions on the coordinate faces. Together with the absolutely continuous stopping gain, these terms define a total signed stopping measure Γ tot \Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}} . We derive the corresponding reflected Itô–Tanaka identity, prove a killed-resolvent representation of the value function in the continuation region, and show that the unrestricted reflected resolvent is generally incorrect because the process is not absorbed on the stopping set. The free boundary is formulated through a continuation-side trace condition for the killed potential. Under a vertical monotonicity hypothesis on V − G V-G , the stopping set is shown to have an epigraph form. We finally prove a verification theorem: any admissible epigraph candidate satisfying contact, strict continuation, reflected Neumann compatibility, growth, the trace condition, and measure-superharmonicity coincides with the value function, and its first entry time is optimal.

Contributions

The main contribution of this paper is a measure-valued killed-resolvent
formulation for an obliquely reflected obstacle problem with the nonsmooth
payoff

2
Problem formulation and reflected variational inequality

2.1
Reflected diffusion in the quadrant

Write
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}=[0,\infty)^{2}
and
\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}=(0,\infty)^{2}
. Let
W=(W^{1},W^{2})
be a
two-dimensional Brownian motion on a filtered probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
,
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, satisfying the usual conditions.
We consider the reflected diffusion (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, where

\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},\qquad a:=\sigma\sigma^{\top}.

The cumulative reflection term is

K_{t}:=\rho_{1}L_{t}^{1}+\rho_{2}L_{t}^{2},

so that the reflected SDE may equivalently be written as

dX_{t}=\beta(X_{t})\,dt+\sigma(X_{t})\,dW_{t}+dK_{t}.

Each
L^{i}
is continuous, adapted, nondecreasing, satisfies
L_{0}^{i}=0
, and
obeys the support condition

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{s}^{i}>0\}}\,dL_{s}^{i}=0,\qquad i=1,2,

(10)

so that
L^{i}
increases only on the face
F_{i}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:x_{i}=0\}
. We fix the
constant inward reflection vectors

\rho_{1}=(1,\theta_{1}),\qquad\rho_{2}=(\theta_{2},1),\qquad\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathbb{R},

(11)

each normalized so that its
e_{i}
-component equals
1
; the inward condition

\rho_{i}\cdot e_{i}>0
holds automatically. The case

\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}=0
, i.e.
\rho_{i}=e_{i}
, is normal reflection; the
general case is oblique reflection. For the present payoff and the
reflection vectors
\rho_{1}=(1,\theta_{1})
,
\rho_{2}=(\theta_{2},1)
, the boundary
contribution to the stopping measure vanishes exactly when
\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}=0
.

For
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
the interior generator is

\mathcal{L}f(x)=\beta(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)+\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}\!\big(a(x)D^{2}f(x)\big),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2},

(12)

and the reflected Itô formula reads

f(X_{t})=f(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}f(X_{s})\,ds+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla f)(X_{s})\,dL_{s}^{i}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla f(X_{s})\sigma(X_{s})\,dW_{s}.

(13)

The finite-variation boundary term vanishes for every
t
if and only if
f

satisfies the reflected Neumann condition

\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla f=0\qquad\text{on }F_{i},\quad i=1,2,

(14)

which for normal reflection is the classical condition
\partial_{i}f=0
on

F_{i}
. This point is essential below, since the payoff
G=x_{1}\vee\alpha x_{2}

does not satisfy (
14
) when the reflection is oblique.

Assumption 2.1

(Coefficients and well-posedness)
.

The coefficients
\beta,\sigma
are continuous and locally Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}

with linear growth
|\beta(x)|+\|\sigma(x)\|\leq K(1+|x|)
, and

a=\sigma\sigma^{\top}
is locally uniformly elliptic in
\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
. We assume

1-\theta_{1}\theta_{2}>0,

a standard sufficient completely-
\mathcal{S}
condition

;
46
for the orthant
reflection matrix

R=\begin{pmatrix}1&\theta_{2}\\
\theta_{1}&1\end{pmatrix},

so that the associated orthant Skorokhod problem is well posed; for every

x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
the reflected SDE (
1
) admits a unique strong
solution in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, and the associated transition semigroup is Feller.
Moreover, the boundary local times
L^{1},L^{2}
do not charge the corner
\{0\}
.

2.2
The stopping problem

Fix
r>0
,
\alpha>0
, and a continuous running cost
c:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
. The
obstacle is
G(x)=x_{1}\vee\alpha x_{2}
, and the value function is
(
2
), the supremum being taken over the set
\mathcal{T}
of all

\mathbb{F}
-stopping times. Since
\tau=0
is admissible,

V\geq G\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

(15)

We define the stopping advantage, continuation set, and stopping set by

H:=V-G,\qquad\mathcal{C}:=\{H>0\},\qquad\mathcal{D}:=\{H=0\},

(16)

and the first entry time
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\in\mathcal{D}\}
.

Assumption 2.2

(Growth and integrability)
.

There exist
\Psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
, constants
K_{\Psi},\lambda_{\Psi}>0
, and
m\geq 1

such that

1+|x|^{m}\leq K_{\Psi}\Psi(x),\qquad G(x)+c(x)\leq K_{\Psi}\Psi(x),

\mathcal{L}\Psi(x)\leq\lambda_{\Psi}\Psi(x)+K_{\Psi},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2},

(17)

together with the boundary compatibility
\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla\Psi\leq 0
on

F_{i}
,
i=1,2
, the discount condition
r>\lambda_{\Psi}
, and the integrability

\mathbb{E}_{x}\Big[\sup_{t\geq 0}e^{-rt}\Psi(X_{t})\Big]<\infty,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Applying (
13
) to
\Psi
and using

\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla\Psi\leq 0
to discard the boundary term, followed by
Gronwall’s inequality, gives

\mathbb{E}_{x}[\Psi(X_{t})]\leq C(1+\Psi(x))e^{\lambda_{\Psi}t},

(18)

hence

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}\mathbb{E}_{x}[\Psi(X_{t})]\,dt\leq C(1+\Psi(x))
. In particular the
cost integral is finite and
V
has at most
\Psi
-growth.

Proposition 2.3

(Finiteness and regularity)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
,
V
is finite and

G(x)\leq V(x)\leq C(1+\Psi(x))
. Moreover
V
is lower semicontinuous; if the
reflected semigroup is locally strong Feller, then
V
is continuous, and

\mathcal{D}=\{V=G\}
is closed.

Proof.

The lower bound is (
15
). For the upper bound, by the
reflected Itô formula and the boundary compatibility

\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla\Psi\leq 0
,

e^{-rt}\Psi(X_{t})\leq\Psi(x)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rs}\big[-(r-\lambda_{\Psi})\Psi(X_{s})+K_{\Psi}\big]\,ds+M_{t}.

After localization, the process

e^{-rt}\Psi(X_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rs}K_{\Psi}\,ds

is bounded above by a local martingale plus the initial value
\Psi(x)
.
Taking expectations and then letting the localization parameter tend to
infinity gives

\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-r\tau}\Psi(X_{\tau})]\leq\Psi(x)+\frac{K_{\Psi}}{r}

for every bounded stopping time
\tau
, and then for all stopping times by
monotone convergence.
Since
G+c\leq K_{\Psi}\Psi
and
c\geq 0
,

V(x)\leq\sup_{\tau}\mathbb{E}_{x}\big[e^{-r\tau}G(X_{\tau})\big]\leq K_{\Psi}\Big(\Psi(x)+\frac{K_{\Psi}}{r}\Big)\leq C(1+\Psi(x))<\infty.

The integrability hypothesis of Assumption
2.2
ensures, in
addition, the uniform integrability used in the limiting arguments below. Lower
semicontinuity follows by fixing
a stopping time, using the Feller property and continuity of
G,c
, and taking
the supremum. Under the local strong Feller hypothesis, stability of
finite-horizon approximations and monotone convergence give continuity, whence

\mathcal{D}=\{V-G=0\}
is closed.
∎

2.3
Dynamic programming and the reflected variational inequality

For
T>0
let
V_{T}(x)=\sup_{\tau\leq T}\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-r\tau}G(X_{\tau})-\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-rs}c(X_{s})\,ds]
; then
V_{T}\uparrow V
. The strong Markov
property gives, for every stopping time
\rho
,

V(x)=\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[e^{-r(\tau\wedge\rho)}V(X_{\tau\wedge\rho})-\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge\rho}e^{-rs}c(X_{s})\,ds\right].

(19)

Proposition 2.4

(Dynamic programming)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
–
2.2
,
V
satisfies
(
19
); consequently
V\geq G
and
\mathcal{L}V-rV-c\leq 0
in the viscosity
sense in
\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
, with
\mathcal{L}V-rV-c=0
in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

The finite-horizon values satisfy the dynamic programming principle by the
strong Markov property, and the estimates following
Assumption
2.2
justify passing to the infinite-horizon limit.
Testing (
19
) against small exit times from balls in
\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
gives the
two viscosity inequalities; on the open set
\mathcal{C}=\{V>G\}
the obstacle is
inactive and they combine to the continuation equation.
∎

Thus
V
is a constrained viscosity solution of

\max\{\mathcal{L}V-rV-c,\;G-V\}=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2},

(20)

with the reflection condition imposed on the coordinate faces. We record the
boundary condition only in the following minimal form, which is all that the
present paper requires.

Remark 2.5

(Boundary condition; no comparison principle used)
.

On the faces
F_{i}
the reflection condition is understood in the constrained
viscosity sense associated with reflection along
\rho_{i}
, in the relaxed
(disjunctive) Barles–Lions form. Whenever
V
is differentiable at a boundary
point, this condition reduces to the reflected Neumann condition

\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla V=0
,
i=1,2
. We shall not invoke a comparison
principle for the reflected viscosity problem: the identification of
V
is
obtained entirely from the reflected Itô–Tanaka formula and the
verification theorem of Section
6
.

Theorem 2.6

(Reflected obstacle characterization)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
–
2.2
,
V
is a constrained
viscosity solution of (
20
) with reflection condition

\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla V=0
on
F_{i}
in the sense of
Remark
2.5
, and

V=G\ \text{on }\mathcal{D},\qquad\mathcal{L}V-rV-c=0\ \text{in }\mathcal{C}

in the viscosity sense.

Proof.

The interior statement is Proposition
2.4
. At boundary points the
reflected Itô formula (
13
) applied to a test function
contains the term
\sum_{i}\int(\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla\varphi)\,dL^{i}
, supported on

F_{i}
; the dynamic programming argument over small stopped intervals yields the
relaxed reflection alternatives, which at differentiability points give

\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla V=0
. The contact identity is the definition of
\mathcal{D}
,
and the continuation equation holds because the obstacle is inactive on
\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Distributional generator and reflected Itô–Tanaka formula

3.1
Distributional generator of the max payoff

Set
\Delta=\{x_{1}=\alpha x_{2}\}
,
E_{1}=\{x_{1}>\alpha x_{2}\}
,

E_{2}=\{x_{1}<\alpha x_{2}\}
. On
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
respectively
G=x_{1}
and

G=\alpha x_{2}
, so
\nabla G=e_{1}
on
E_{1}
and
\nabla G=\alpha e_{2}
on
E_{2}
.
Writing
Y(x)=x_{1}-\alpha x_{2}
we have
G(x)=\alpha x_{2}+Y(x)^{+}
, and since

d^{2}(y^{+})/dy^{2}=\delta_{0}
,

D^{2}G=\frac{(1,-\alpha)^{\top}(1,-\alpha)}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}\,\sigma_{\Delta},

(21)

where we used

\delta_{\{x_{1}-\alpha x_{2}=0\}}=|\nabla(x_{1}-\alpha x_{2})|^{-1}\sigma_{\Delta}=(1+\alpha^{2})^{-1/2}\sigma_{\Delta}
. Therefore, in the distributional sense on
\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
,

\mathcal{L}G(dx)=\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ac}}G(x)\,dx+\frac{q(x)}{2\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}\,\sigma_{\Delta}(dx),\qquad\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ac}}G:=\beta_{1}\mathbf{1}_{E_{1}}+\alpha\beta_{2}\mathbf{1}_{E_{2}},

(22)

with

q(x):=(1,-\alpha)\,a(x)\,(1,-\alpha)^{\top}>0\quad\text{on compacts of }\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}.

(23)

Define the preliminary interior stopping gain
\Gamma:=c+rG-\mathcal{L}G
. Then

\Gamma=\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}\,dx+\Gamma^{\Delta}
, where

\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}(x)=c(x)+rG(x)-\beta_{1}(x)\mathbf{1}_{E_{1}}(x)-\alpha\beta_{2}(x)\mathbf{1}_{E_{2}}(x),

(24)

\Gamma^{\Delta}(dx)=-\frac{q(x)}{2\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}\,\sigma_{\Delta}(dx).

(25)

The sign is negative because the singular part of
\mathcal{L}G
is positive and

\Gamma=c+rG-\mathcal{L}G
.

(Distributional stopping gain)
.

Under Assumption

,

is a signed Radon measure on

\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
, with absolutely continuous and diagonal singular

) and (

).

G
is affine on

and

, so the second-order part of
\mathcal{L}G
vanishes

\mathcal{L}G=\beta_{1}
on

and
\mathcal{L}G=\alpha\beta_{2}
on

. The singular part follows from (

\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(a\,D^{2}G)=\tfrac{q}{2\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}\sigma_{\Delta}

Subtracting

).

Lemma 3.2

(Reflected Itô–Tanaka formula for

)

For every
t\geq 0
, after localization,

\displaystyle e^{-rt}G(X_{t})

\displaystyle=G(x)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rs}\big(\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ac}}G(X_{s})-rG(X_{s})\big)\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rs}\,d\ell_{s}^{0}(Y)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha\theta_{1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rs}\,dL_{s}^{1}+\theta_{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rs}\,dL_{s}^{2}+M_{t}^{G},

(26)

where
M^{G}
is a local martingale and

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ac}}G=\beta_{1}\mathbf{1}_{E_{1}}+\alpha\beta_{2}\mathbf{1}_{E_{2}}
.

Proof.

Write
G(X_{t})=\alpha X_{t}^{2}+Y_{t}^{+}
, apply the semimartingale Tanaka formula to

Y^{+}
, insert the reflected SDE for
X
, and multiply by
e^{-rt}
. The

dt
-terms give
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ac}}G-rG
; the kink gives
\tfrac{1}{2}d\ell^{0}(Y)
; the
reflection terms are
(\rho_{1}\!\cdot\!\nabla G)\,dL^{1}+(\rho_{2}\!\cdot\!\nabla G)\,dL^{2}=\alpha\theta_{1}\,dL^{1}+\theta_{2}\,dL^{2}
on the open faces; the remaining
stochastic integral is
M^{G}
.
∎

Remark 3.3

(Two equivalent singular descriptions)
.

The diagonal singularity may be represented either by the surface measure

-\tfrac{q}{2\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}\sigma_{\Delta}
or by the local-time functional

-\tfrac{1}{2}\,d\ell^{0}(Y)
. These describe the same mechanism: the coefficient
q

is already encoded in
\ell^{0}(Y)
through
d\langle Y\rangle_{t}=q(X_{t})\,dt
, and
one must not multiply the local-time term again by
q
.

Remark 3.4

(When the boundary terms vanish)
.

The boundary contributions in (
3.2
) are

\alpha\theta_{1}\,dL^{1}
and
\theta_{2}\,dL^{2}
. For normal reflection
(
\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}=0
) both vanish, since on each face the gradient of
G
is
parallel to that face and the inward normal derivative is zero. Thus, for the
pair
(G,\,\text{reflection})
, the boundary part of the stopping measure is
identically zero under normal reflection and is nonzero precisely when at least
one of
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
is nonzero (equivalently, whenever a reflection
vector has a nonzero component tangent to its face). The remainder of the paper
is stated for general
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
and specializes to normal reflection
by setting them to zero.

4
Total stopping measure and killed resolvents

4.1
The total reflected stopping measure

Let
A^{\Delta}_{t}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\ell_{t}^{0}(Y)
be the diagonal local-time functional,
and let

A_{t}^{\partial}:=\alpha\theta_{1}L_{t}^{1}+\theta_{2}L_{t}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla G)(X_{s})\,dL_{s}^{i}

(27)

be the signed boundary functional. Define the total signed additive functional

A_{t}^{\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}}:=\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}(X_{s})\,ds-A_{t}^{\Delta}-A_{t}^{\partial},

(28)

and let
\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}
be the signed smooth measure whose additive functional is
(
28
). In measure notation

\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}=\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}\,dx+\Gamma^{\Delta}+\Gamma^{\partial},

(29)

with
\Gamma^{\Delta}
as in (
25
) and
\Gamma^{\partial}
the signed boundary Revuz
measure of
-A^{\partial}
, i.e. the measure
-\alpha\theta_{1}
times boundary
occupation measure of
F_{1}
plus
-\theta_{2}
times that of
F_{2}
.

Combining (
3.2
) with
\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}=c+rG-\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ac}}G
(so that

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ac}}G-rG=c-\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}
) and (
28
) yields the bridge identity in the
clean form

e^{-rt}G(X_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rs}c(X_{s})\,ds=G(x)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rs}\,dA_{s}^{\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}}+M_{t}^{G},

(30)

where the running cost
c
is already contained in
\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}
. This is the form
used throughout.

Assumption 4.1

(Smooth-measure admissibility)
.

The positive and negative variations of
\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}
are smooth measures for the
reflected diffusion, and for every relatively compact open
O\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\int_{0}^{\tau_{O}}e^{-rs}\,d|A_{s}^{\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}}|\right]<\infty,\qquad x\in O,\ \ \tau_{O}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\notin O\}.

4.2
Killed resolvents

For open
O\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
put
\tau_{O^{c}}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\notin O\}
. For a
signed smooth measure
\mu=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}
with functional

A^{\mu}=A^{\mu^{+}}-A^{\mu^{-}}
define the killed
r
-resolvent on
O
,

R_{r}^{O}\mu(x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\int_{0}^{\tau_{O^{c}}}e^{-rs}\,dA_{s}^{\mu}\right],

(31)

whenever the expectation is defined. When
O=\mathcal{C}
we write
R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\mu
and,
by Proposition
2.3
,
\tau_{\mathcal{C}^{c}}=\tau_{\mathcal{D}}
up to polar
exceptional sets. The killed resolvent solves

(r-\mathcal{L})R_{r}^{O}\mu=\mu\ \text{in }O,\qquad R_{r}^{O}\mu=0\ \text{on }\partial O,

(32)

in the weak potential-theoretic sense

3
;
15
, with the reflection
condition retained
on the parts of
\partial O
lying in
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
.

Lemma 4.2

(Resolvent identity in killed domains)
.

Let
O\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
be open and
\mu
a signed smooth measure satisfying the
integrability in (
31
). Then
u=R_{r}^{O}\mu
satisfies

u(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\big[e^{-r(t\wedge\tau_{O^{c}})}u(X_{t\wedge\tau_{O^{c}}})\big]+\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{O^{c}}}e^{-rs}\,dA_{s}^{\mu}\right]

whenever both terms are finite.

Proof.

Split the integral defining
R_{r}^{O}\mu
at
t\wedge\tau_{O^{c}}
, use additivity
of
A^{\mu}
, and apply the strong Markov property to the shifted process.
∎

4.3
Killed-resolvent representation of the value

Theorem 4.3

(Killed-resolvent representation)
.

Assume Assumptions
2.1
–
4.1
, that
V
is
continuous, and that
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}
is optimal for (
2
).
Suppose

\mathbb{E}_{x}[\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathcal{D}}}e^{-rs}\,d|A_{s}^{\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}}|]<\infty
for
x\in\mathcal{C}
and
that the martingale in (
30
) stopped at
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}
is uniformly
integrable. Then, for every
x\in\mathcal{C}
,

V(x)=G(x)-R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}(x),\qquad H(x)=-R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}(x).

(33)

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathcal{C}
,
\tau=\tau_{\mathcal{D}}
. By optimality

V(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-r\tau}G(X_{\tau})-\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-rs}c\,ds]
. Apply
(
30
) at
t\wedge\tau
, localize, and let the localization parameter
tend to infinity; the integrability hypotheses give

\mathbb{E}_{x}[M_{t\wedge\tau}^{G}]\to 0
, whence

\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[e^{-r\tau}G(X_{\tau})-\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-rs}c\,ds\Big]=G(x)-\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-rs}\,dA_{s}^{\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}}\Big]=G(x)-R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}(x).

Subtracting
G(x)
gives the second identity.
∎

Remark 4.4

(Sign and existence of an optimal time)
.

Since
H=V-G\geq 0
, (
33
) shows the killed potential

R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}
is nonpositive on
\mathcal{C}
, consistent with

\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}=c+rG-\mathcal{L}G
corrected by the singular terms. Optimality of
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}

is not automatic; it requires
\mathcal{D}
closed (guaranteed by continuity of
V
,
Proposition
2.3
) together with the standard regularity of the
stopping problem. We therefore state Theorem
4.3

conditionally and use it only as a representational counterpart to the
verification result of Section
6
.

4.4
Failure of the unrestricted reflected resolvent

Let
R_{r}^{\mathrm{R}}\mu(x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}\,dA_{s}^{\mu}]
be the
unrestricted
r
-resolvent. A tempting replacement for (
33
) is

V=G-R_{r}^{\mathrm{R}}(\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}})
. Splitting at
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}
,

R_{r}^{\mathrm{R}}\big(\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\big)(x)=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathcal{D}}}e^{-rs}\,dA_{s}^{\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}}\Big]}_{=\,R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}(x)}+\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[\int_{\tau_{\mathcal{D}}}^{\infty}e^{-rs}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{s})\,dA_{s}^{\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}}\Big].

(34)

Proposition 4.5

(Post-stopping occupation bias)
.

Assume the integrability of Assumption
4.1
and suppose that
both terms in (
34
) are well defined. Then the
unrestricted representation
V=G-R_{r}^{\mathrm{R}}(\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}})
agrees with
the killed representation only when

\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[\int_{\tau_{\mathcal{D}}}^{\infty}e^{-rs}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{s})\,dA_{s}^{\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}}\Big]=0,\qquad x\in\mathcal{C}.

(35)

In general this condition is not implied by reflection or by the stopping
structure, because the process is not absorbed on
\mathcal{D}
: a conservative
diffusion that enters
\mathcal{D}
may subsequently return to
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

The decomposition (
34
) is the splitting of the
unrestricted functional at
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}
; the first term is
R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}
by
Theorem
4.3
. Equality of the two representations is
thus equivalent to the vanishing of the second term. The phenomenon is one of
non-absorption rather than of reflection per se: without an absorption
or invariance hypothesis on
\mathcal{D}
, the post-stopping occupation of
\mathcal{C}
does
not vanish.
∎

5
Free-boundary trace formulation

The representation (
33
), together with
V=G
on
\mathcal{D}
, forces the
continuation-side trace of the killed potential to vanish:

\operatorname{Tr}_{\partial\mathcal{C}}R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}=0,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\operatorname{Tr}_{\partial\mathcal{C}}\big(G-R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}\big)=G.

(36)

This is the nonlocal free-boundary condition for the reflected measure-valued
obstacle problem.

Remark 5.1

(Meaning of the trace)
.

The trace in (
36
) is the continuation-side quasi-continuous trace of
the killed potential. When the killed potential has a continuous extension to a
regular boundary point, it reduces to the ordinary one-sided limit from
\mathcal{C}
.
For locally Lipschitz epigraph boundaries the classical expression

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}\big(x_{1},\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})-\varepsilon\big)=0

(37)

is used only at points where this one-sided trace exists; in general the trace
is taken in the quasi-continuous or fine-potential-theoretic sense, according
to the regularity available for
\mathfrak{b}
and for the killed potential.

Remark 5.2

(Trace versus smooth fit)
.

At regular boundary points away from
\Delta
, additional regularity may turn
the trace condition into a more classical matching condition; we do not rely on
such a reduction.
The trace formulation is the appropriate global substitute precisely because of
the two singular mechanisms: the diagonal kink of
G
, where smooth fit
genuinely breaks down, and the boundary local-time terms generated by oblique
reflection. The condition (
36
) is a contact condition for the
killed potential, not a pointwise
C^{1}
- or
C^{2}
-matching condition.

6
Verification of epigraph candidates

6.1
Conditional epigraph geometry

In two-dimensional stopping problems the stopping set need not be a graph. We
impose graph structure through monotonicity of the stopping advantage.

Assumption 6.1

(Vertical monotonicity)
.

For every
x_{1}\geq 0
the map
x_{2}\mapsto H(x_{1},x_{2})
is nonincreasing on

[0,\infty)
.

Theorem 6.2

(Conditional epigraph representation)
.

Assume
V
continuous and Assumption
6.1
. Define

\mathfrak{b}(x_{1}):=\inf\{x_{2}\geq 0:H(x_{1},x_{2})=0\}\in[0,\infty],\qquad\inf\varnothing:=+\infty.

(38)

Then
\mathfrak{b}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is lower semicontinuous and

\mathcal{D}=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{2}\geq\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})\},\qquad\mathcal{C}=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):0\leq x_{2}<\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})\}.

(39)

Proof.

Fix
x_{1}
. Since
H\geq 0
and
x_{2}\mapsto H(x_{1},x_{2})
is nonincreasing,

H(x_{1},x_{2})=0
implies
H(x_{1},y_{2})=0
for all
y_{2}\geq x_{2}
; hence each vertical
stopping section is an interval
[\mathfrak{b}(x_{1}),\infty)
(empty if

\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})=+\infty
). Continuity of
V,G
gives continuity of
H
, so
\mathcal{D}
is
closed and
\mathfrak{b}
is lower semicontinuous, and
H(x_{1},\mathfrak{b}(x_{1}))=0
when

\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})<\infty
. This yields (
39
).
∎

Remark 6.3

(Conditional nature)
.

Theorem
6.2
is not a consequence of convexity of
G
or of
reflection: it follows from monotonicity of
H=V-G
, which in applications must
be established from the model primitives or imposed as part of a candidate
class. Allowing
\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})=+\infty
avoids an artificial nonempty-section
hypothesis.

6.2
Candidate epigraphs and the structural constraint

Let
\mathfrak{b}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
be locally Lipschitz on
\{\mathfrak{b}<\infty\}
, and
set

\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}:=\{x_{2}\geq\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})\},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}:=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}},\qquad\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}\}.

(40)

Define the killed potential and the candidate value

P_{\mathfrak{b}}(x):=R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}}e^{-rs}\,dA_{s}^{\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}}\Big],\qquad U_{\mathfrak{b}}:=\begin{cases}G-P_{\mathfrak{b}}&\text{on }\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}},\\[2.0pt]
G&\text{on }\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}.\end{cases}

(41)

The following proposition makes explicit a constraint that the
superharmonicity requirement of Definition
6.6
below imposes on
the geometry; it should be read as part of the structural content of
admissibility rather than as an independently verifiable condition.

Proposition 6.4

(Diagonal avoidance by the stopping interior)
.

Suppose
U_{\mathfrak{b}}=G
on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}
and the measure-superharmonicity condition

(r-\mathcal{L})U_{\mathfrak{b}}+c\geq 0
holds on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
. Then

\Delta\cap\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}})=\varnothing.

Consequently, for every
x_{1}
with
\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})<\infty
,

\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})\geq\frac{x_{1}}{\alpha}.

(42)

Proof.

On the interior of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}
one has
U_{\mathfrak{b}}=G
, hence

(r-\mathcal{L})U_{\mathfrak{b}}+c=c+rG-\mathcal{L}G=\Gamma=\Gamma^{\mathrm{ac}}\,dx+\Gamma^{\Delta},

whose singular part is
\Gamma^{\Delta}=-\tfrac{q}{2\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}\sigma_{\Delta}
, a strictly
negative measure on
\Delta
by (
23
). If
\Delta
met the interior
of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, the measure
(r-\mathcal{L})U_{\mathfrak{b}}+c
would carry strictly negative
singular mass there, contradicting
(r-\mathcal{L})U_{\mathfrak{b}}+c\geq 0
. Hence

\Delta\cap\operatorname{int}\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\varnothing
. For the epigraph

\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\{x_{2}\geq\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})\}
the point
(x_{1},x_{1}/\alpha)\in\Delta
lies in the
interior of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}
iff
x_{1}/\alpha>\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})
; excluding this gives
(
42
).
∎

Remark 6.5

(Interpretation)
.

Constraint (
42
) says the free boundary lies on or above
the diagonal. The sign of the local-time term
+\tfrac{1}{2}\,d\ell^{0}(Y)
in
(
3.2
) is consistent with the fact that, under
measure-superharmonicity, the diagonal cannot lie in the stopping interior. The
constraint is not an independent hypothesis on
\mathfrak{b}
that can be checked before
fixing the geometry; it is forced by superharmonicity, which is why we record
it explicitly.

6.3
Admissible candidates and the verification theorem

Definition 6.6

(Admissible epigraph candidate)
.

A locally Lipschitz epigraph boundary
\mathfrak{b}
is admissible if, with

P_{\mathfrak{b}},U_{\mathfrak{b}}
as in (
41
):

[label=(A0)]

1.

P_{\mathfrak{b}}
is finite q.e. in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
and admits a
quasi-continuous modification;

2.

\operatorname{Tr}_{\partial\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}}P_{\mathfrak{b}}=0

in the sense of Remark
5.1
;

3.

U_{\mathfrak{b}}
extends continuously to
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
;

4.

U_{\mathfrak{b}}=G
on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}
and
U_{\mathfrak{b}}>G
on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}

(contact and strict continuation);

5.

U_{\mathfrak{b}}\geq G
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
(majorization);

6.

\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla U_{\mathfrak{b}}=0
on
F_{i}
,
i=1,2
,
in the trace sense (reflected Neumann compatibility);

7.

|U_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)|\leq C(1+\Psi(x))
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, with
\Psi
from
Assumption
2.2
;

8.

U_{\mathfrak{b}}
belongs locally to the generalized Itô class,
its distributional second derivatives having signed smooth-measure parts
with locally integrable functionals;

9.

(r-\mathcal{L})U_{\mathfrak{b}}+c\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
as a signed smooth
measure (measure-superharmonicity).

By Proposition
6.4
, any admissible
\mathfrak{b}
satisfies

\mathfrak{b}(x_{1})\geq x_{1}/\alpha
.

Inside
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
the killed potential satisfies
(r-\mathcal{L})P_{\mathfrak{b}}=\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}
weakly, and
since
U_{\mathfrak{b}}=G-P_{\mathfrak{b}}
with
\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}=c+rG-\mathcal{L}G
(corrected by the singular and
boundary terms), the candidate value satisfies the continuation equation

\mathcal{L}U_{\mathfrak{b}}-rU_{\mathfrak{b}}-c=0\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}

(43)

in the weak measure sense.

Lemma 6.7

(Martingale property up to the candidate boundary)
.

If
\mathfrak{b}
is admissible, then

M_{t}^{\mathfrak{b}}:=e^{-r(t\wedge\tau_{\mathfrak{b}})}U_{\mathfrak{b}}(X_{t\wedge\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}})-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}}e^{-rs}c(X_{s})\,ds

is a local martingale, and a true martingale after localization and passage to
the limit under
7
and Assumption
2.2
.

Proof.

Apply the generalized reflected Itô formula (
13
) to

U_{\mathfrak{b}}(X_{t\wedge\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}})
. By
6
the boundary terms

\sum_{i}\int(\rho_{i}\!\cdot\!\nabla U_{\mathfrak{b}})\,dL^{i}
vanish, and by
(
43
) the interior drift and all smooth-measure
finite-variation terms before
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
cancel against

-\int e^{-rs}c\,ds
. The remaining term is a local martingale; the Lyapunov
estimate gives uniform integrability after the standard localization.
∎

Theorem 6.8

(Verification of an epigraph candidate)
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}
be an admissible epigraph candidate in the sense of
Definition
6.6
, and
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
as in (
40
). Then

U_{\mathfrak{b}}=V\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
is optimal for (
2
), and

\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\{V=G\},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\{V>G\}.

Proof.

U_{\mathfrak{b}}\geq V
.
Let
\tau
be arbitrary. By (
13
) and
the superharmonicity
9
, the process

e^{-r(t\wedge\tau)}U_{\mathfrak{b}}(X_{t\wedge\tau})-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}e^{-rs}c\,ds
is a
supermartingale after localization; the boundary terms vanish by

6
. Using
7
and letting the localization
parameter tend to infinity,

U_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)\geq\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[e^{-r\tau}U_{\mathfrak{b}}(X_{\tau})-\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-rs}c\,ds\Big]\geq\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[e^{-r\tau}G(X_{\tau})-\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-rs}c\,ds\Big],

where the last inequality is
5
. Taking the supremum over
\tau

gives
U_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)\geq V(x)
.

U_{\mathfrak{b}}\leq V
.
Take
\tau=\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
. By
Lemma
6.7
,

U_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-r\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}}U_{\mathfrak{b}}(X_{\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}})-\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}}e^{-rs}c\,ds]
.
Since
X_{\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}
and
U_{\mathfrak{b}}=G
there by
4
,

U_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[e^{-r\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}}G(X_{\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}})-\int_{0}^{\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}}e^{-rs}c\,ds\Big]\leq V(x).

Hence
U_{\mathfrak{b}}=V
and
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
is optimal. Finally
4
gives

\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\{U_{\mathfrak{b}}=G\}=\{V=G\}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\{U_{\mathfrak{b}}>G\}=\{V>G\}
.
∎

Remark 6.9

(Verification, not formal boundary solving)
.

Theorem
6.8
does not assert that every solution of the trace
condition is optimal. The trace condition
2
must be accompanied by
majorization, contact, strict continuation, reflected Neumann compatibility,
growth, generalized Itô admissibility, and measure-superharmonicity. These
conditions are the analytical substitute for a formal smooth-fit calculation.

Corollary 6.10

(Uniqueness within the admissible class)
.

If
\mathfrak{b}_{1},\mathfrak{b}_{2}
are admissible epigraph candidates, then

U_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}}=U_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}=V
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}
, and

\mathfrak{b}_{1}=\mathfrak{b}_{2}
at every common point of continuity of the boundary.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.8
both candidate values equal
V
, and

\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{b}_{j}}=\{U_{\mathfrak{b}_{j}}=G\}=\{V=G\}
, so the stopping sets coincide; being
epigraphs, their boundary functions agree at common continuity points.
∎

7
Conclusion

We have formulated a reflected optimal stopping problem with the max-type payoff

G=x_{1}\vee\alpha x_{2}
as a measure-valued obstacle problem. The nonsmooth
obstacle produces a diagonal singular measure
\Gamma^{\Delta}
on
\Delta
, while oblique
reflection produces boundary local-time contributions; combining these with the
absolutely continuous interior gain yields the total stopping measure
\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}
.
For normal reflection the boundary component
\Gamma^{\partial}
vanishes, but the
measure-valued formulation remains essential because the diagonal component

\Gamma^{\Delta}
persists.

The correct potential representation of the value in the continuation region is
the killed resolvent
R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}
, not the unrestricted reflected resolvent,
because the process is not absorbed on the stopping set. Under a vertical
monotonicity hypothesis on
V-G
the stopping set admits an epigraph
representation, and the killed-potential trace condition

\operatorname{Tr}_{\partial\mathcal{C}}R_{r}^{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma^{\mathrm{tot}}=0
becomes the natural free-boundary condition.
For admissible epigraph candidates, measure-superharmonicity prevents the
diagonal from entering the stopping interior; equivalently, at finite boundary
points one has

The verification theorem shows that any admissible
epigraph candidate satisfying contact, strict continuation, reflected Neumann
compatibility, growth, the trace condition, and measure-superharmonicity
coincides with the value function, and that first entry into the candidate
stopping set is optimal.

The remaining analytical questions are the derivation of monotonicity of
V-G

from primitive assumptions on
(\beta,\sigma,c,r,\alpha)
, finer regularity of
the killed-potential trace at the free boundary, and uniqueness of admissible
epigraph boundaries under minimal hypotheses.
