On the Spectral Structure and Objective Equivalence of Orthogonal Multilabel Fisher Discriminants
Brian Keith-Norambuena brian.keith@ucn.cl, Juan Bekios-Calfa juan.bekios@ucn.cl

Abstract
We provide a unified theoretical analysis of Linear Discriminant Analysis with simultaneous multilabel scatter matrix formulations and Stiefel orthogonality constraints. Our contributions span both algebraic structure and statistical guarantees. On the algebraic side, we characterize the rank of the multilabel between-class scatter matrix, showing that the effective discriminant dimensionality can strictly exceed the classical single-label bound of C − 1 C-1 ; we establish a multilabel partition of variance and prove that all four Fisher objectives are equivalent under the 𝐖 ⊤ ​ S t ML ​ 𝐖 = 𝐈 r \mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r} constraint while characterizing their divergence under the Stiefel constraint; and we prove a two-sided label-distance preservation bound relating projected distances to Hamming distances in label space. On the statistical side, we establish a finite-sample O ⁡ ( k max ​ d ​ log ⁡ d / n / gap r ) O(k_{\max}\sqrt{d\log d/n}/\mathrm{gap}_{r}) bound on the subspace estimation error under sub-Gaussian noise with a matching Ω ⁡ ( σ 2 ​ d / ( n ​ gap r ) ) \Omega(\sigma^{2}d/(n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r})) minimax lower bound, establishing a near-minimax-optimal rate (matching up to logarithmic and k max k_{\max} factors) for multilabel discriminant subspace estimation. We further provide high-probability distance concentration, robustness guarantees under label interactions, and a regularization analysis preserving the spectral structure when d ≫ n d\gg n . All results are verified numerically on synthetic data generated from the linear label-effect model, covering both the algebraic identities and the multilabel-specific quantities ( k max k_{\max} , κ ⁡ ( S t ML ) \kappa({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}) , ‖ 𝚪 / n ‖ 2 \|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2} , Δ r \Delta_{r} ) that govern the statistical bounds. The numerical experiments are designed as a sanity check for the theorems rather than as an empirical benchmark; evaluation on real multilabel datasets is left to future work targeting application-oriented venues.

Introduction

Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)
(
10
;
26
)
is among the most fundamental techniques for supervised dimensionality reduction. Given
n
samples in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with class labels, LDA seeks a linear projection that maximizes between-class separation relative to within-class spread, as measured by scatter matrices. The classical formulation solves a generalized eigenvalue problem
S_{b}\mathbf{w}=\lambda S_{w}\mathbf{w}
, yielding at most
C-1
discriminant directions for
C
classes.

Two important extensions of classical LDA have been developed largely
independently.

Orthogonality-constrained LDA.

Standard LDA eigenvectors are
S_{w}
-conjugate and orthogonal (
\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\top}S_{w}\mathbf{w}_{j}=0
for
i\neq j
) but not Euclidean-orthogonal. Several methods enforce explicit orthogonality: the Foley–Sammon method
(
11
)
extracts directions sequentially with
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}
constraints via Lagrange multipliers; Orthogonal LDA (OLDA)
(
42
;
41
)
provides a batch formulation via SVD and QR decomposition, with
6
providing a fast variant for undersampled problems; Uncorrelated LDA (ULDA)
(
18
)
enforces
\mathbf{W}^{\top}S_{t}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}
for statistical uncorrelatedness (see
16
for a comparison of ULDA and OLDA); and trace ratio solvers
(
23
;
37
)
address the true trace ratio problem on the Stiefel manifold with globally convergent algorithms.
21
proved that under orthonormality constraints, the four standard Fisher objectives—trace ratio, ratio trace, determinant ratio, and trace difference—yield equivalent optimal solutions in the single-label setting.

Multilabel LDA.

In multilabel classification
(
33
)
, each sample may belong to multiple classes simultaneously, requiring scatter matrix redefinition.
25
gave an early treatment of LDA for multilabel problems.
36
introduced multilabel scatter matrices
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
and
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
with weighted per-label class means.
17
proposed a regression-based framework subsuming LDA, PLS, and CCA.
30
explored hypergraph spectral learning for multilabel dimensionality reduction.
40
unified five weighting schemes for multilabel scatter matrices, later extended with saliency-based weights by
39
.
45
and
46
explored dependence maximization (using HSIC
(
13
)
) and kernel alignment approaches, respectively.
2
proposed a Gram–Schmidt-based multilabel LDA extension. See
28
for a comprehensive survey.

Why this combination matters.

Orthogonality and multilabel scatter matrices address two complementary weaknesses of classical LDA when applied to modern multilabel problems. First, the single-label rank bound
\mathrm{rank}(S_{b})\leq C-1
is an artefact of the scatter construction: in a multilabel problem with
L
labels, the natural analogue is governed by the label co-occurrence structure, and—as we show—can be as large as
L
rather than
L-1
, yielding genuinely more discriminant directions than the single-label setting. Second, the eigenvectors of the generalized eigenvalue problem
S_{b}\mathbf{w}=\lambda S_{w}\mathbf{w}
are
S_{w}
-conjugate but not Euclidean-orthogonal, which makes the resulting projection sensitive to noise scaling and complicates downstream tasks that rely on Euclidean geometry (nearest-neighbor classification, clustering, visualization). Imposing a Stiefel constraint gives an explicit control of projected noise (Corollary
19
)—a property that matters precisely when the projected features are used with a distance-based downstream classifier. The combination of the two—multilabel scatter matrices with a Stiefel (or multilabel total-scatter) orthogonality constraint—is therefore the natural target for a theoretical analysis of multilabel discriminant projections, even though it has so far been studied only in restricted forms.

The gap.

Despite the maturity of both families, no published work directly analyzes the
theoretical properties
of LDA with simultaneous multilabel scatter matrices and Stiefel orthogonality constraints:

\max_{\mathbf{W}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)}\;\frac{\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})}{\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})},

(1)

where
\mathrm{St}(d,r)=\{\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}:\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}\}
is the Stiefel manifold. The closest existing methods are Stiefel-constrained and multilabel-aware, yet each of them optimizes an objective that is
not
an explicit Fisher ratio over multilabel scatter matrices, and none provides the algebraic structure or statistical guarantees established here:

•

kaLDA

(
46
)
maximizes the kernel-alignment objective
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W})
, which is related to but not identical with the multilabel between-class trace and, critically, ignores the within-class scatter entirely—so there is no Fisher ratio, no analysis of the effective rank of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
, and no finite-sample subspace estimation rate.

•

MDDM

(
44
;
45
)
maximizes the HSIC dependence between features and labels under a Stiefel constraint. The resulting spectral structure, objective-equivalence behavior, and finite-sample rates analyzed here are orthogonal to the dependence-maximization viewpoint and have not been derived for the Fisher formulation.

•

The
regression framework
of
17
unifies LDA, PLS, and CCA as least-squares problems, but it does not analyze the multilabel rank of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
, the equivalence of the four Fisher objectives, or the effect of Stiefel constraints on the solution.

•

Sparse multilabel bilinear embedding on Stiefel manifolds

(
20
)
and the
orthogonal multi-view framework
of
38
combine Stiefel constraints with multilabel-aware objectives, but the objectives are sparsity-regularized bilinear or multi-view-specific rather than the classical Fisher ratio with multilabel scatter matrices.

In short, the closest prior methods either drop the Fisher-ratio form, replace it by a surrogate of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
alone, or target a sparse/multi-view variant; none of them characterizes the rank of the multilabel between-class scatter, the equivalence (and divergence) of the four Fisher objectives under Stiefel and multilabel total-scatter constraints, the label-distance preservation property, or the near-minimax-optimal subspace estimation rate for the formulation (
1
). The present paper fills exactly this theoretical gap.

Contributions.

We provide a unified analysis of the formulation (
1
) across four groups of results:

1.

Rank characterization
(Section
3
):
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y})\leq\min(d,n-1,\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y}))
, recovering the classical
C-1
bound as a special case and showing that the multilabel setting can provide strictly more discriminant dimensions than the single-label case.

2.

Objective equivalence
(Section
4
): a multilabel partition of variance identity and the equivalence of all four Fisher objectives under
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
; under the Stiefel constraint, we identify the commutativity conditions for equivalence and characterize the divergence under variable cardinalities.

3.

Label-distance preservation
(Section
5
): a two-sided bound relating expected squared projected distances to Hamming distances, with the noise contribution bounded by the orthogonality constraint.

4.

Statistical guarantees
(Section
6
): an
O(k_{\max}\sqrt{d\log d/n}/\mathrm{gap}_{r})
finite-sample upper bound (Theorem
24
) with a matching
\Omega(\sigma^{2}d/(n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}))
minimax lower bound (Theorem
29
), plus high-probability distance concentration (Theorem
35
), robustness to label interactions (Theorem
39
), and a regularization analysis preserving the spectral structure when
d\gg n
(Theorem
43
).

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

Let
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be a data matrix with
n
samples in
d
dimensions, and
\mathbf{Y}\in\{0,1\}^{n\times L}
be a binary label matrix where
y_{i\ell}=1
indicates that sample
i
has label
\ell
. In the multilabel setting, each row of
\mathbf{Y}
may contain multiple ones. We denote:

•

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}
—the global mean;

•

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{n_{\ell}}\sum_{i:y_{i\ell}=1}\mathbf{x}_{i}
—the mean of samples with label
\ell
, where
n_{\ell}=\sum_{i}y_{i\ell}
;

•

k_{i}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}y_{i\ell}
—the label cardinality of sample
i
;

•

K=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}n_{\ell}
—the total label count;

•

\boldsymbol{\Gamma}=\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times L}
—the label co-occurrence (Gram) matrix;

•

\mathbf{D}_{n}=\mathrm{diag}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{L})
—diagonal matrix of label frequencies;

•

\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{I}_{n}-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}
—the centering matrix (the orthogonal projector onto
\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\mathbf{v}=0\}
, with
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H})=n-1
);

•

\tilde{\mathbf{X}}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{X}
—the centered data matrix.

When a distributional model is assumed (Section
5
), we write
\pi_{\ell}=\mathbb{E}[y_{i\ell}]
for the marginal probability of label
\ell
and
\mathbf{D}_{\pi}=\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{L})
.

2.2
Multilabel Scatter Matrices

Following
36
, the multilabel scatter matrices are defined as:

\displaystyle{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}n_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top},

(2)

\displaystyle{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{i:\,y_{i\ell}=1}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell})^{\top}.

(3)

The standard (single-label) total scatter is
S_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
.

Remark 1
.

In the multilabel setting, a sample
\mathbf{x}_{i}
with labels
\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{k_{i}}\}
contributes to
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
once for
each
of its
k_{i}
labels. This sample-reweighting is the fundamental distinction from the single-label case and is the source of the phenomena we analyze.

2.3
The Stiefel Manifold and Fisher Objectives

The Stiefel manifold
\mathrm{St}(d,r)=\{\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}:\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}\}
is a smooth compact manifold
(
8
;
1
)
. On it, we consider four Fisher-type objectives with multilabel scatter matrices:

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{TR}}(\mathbf{W})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})}{\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})},

(trace ratio)

(4)

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{RT}}(\mathbf{W})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\!\left((\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})^{-1}\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}\right),

(ratio trace)

(5)

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{DR}}(\mathbf{W})

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}|}{|\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}|},

(determinant ratio)

(6)

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{TD}}(\mathbf{W})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})-\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}).

(trace difference)

(7)

2.4
Distributional Assumptions and Population Quantities

For the statistical guarantees in Section
6
, we require
distributional assumptions on the data-generating process.

Definition 2

(Linear label-effect model)
.

A sample
(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{i})
is generated as:

\mathbf{x}_{i}=\boldsymbol{\mu}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}y_{i\ell}\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\ell}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i},

(8)

where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the additive effect of label
\ell
on the feature vector,
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the baseline mean, and
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\sim(0,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
is zero-mean noise with covariance
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\succ 0
, independent of
\mathbf{y}_{i}
.

Let
\mathbf{A}=[\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{L}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times L}
denote the matrix of label effects.

Definition 3

(Sub-Gaussian noise)
.

The noise vector
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}
in the model (
8
) is called
\sigma
-sub-Gaussian if for every unit vector
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp(t\,\mathbf{u}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i})\right]\leq\exp\!\left(\frac{t^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular, Gaussian noise
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
is
\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})}
-sub-Gaussian.

Definition 4

(Population scatter matrices)
.

Under the linear label-effect model (
8
) with label distribution
\pi_{\ell}=\mathbb{E}[y_{i\ell}]
, define the population scatter matrices:

\displaystyle{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{A}^{\top},\quad\mathbf{D}_{\pi}=\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{L}),

(9)

\displaystyle{S_{w}^{\mathrm{pop}}}

\displaystyle=K^{\mathrm{pop}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w},\quad K^{\mathrm{pop}}=\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\pi_{\ell},

(10)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}^{*}}

\displaystyle=2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}-{S_{w}^{\mathrm{pop}}},

(11)

where
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{pop}}}
is the population multilabel total scatter.
The eigenvalue gap of
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}
at rank
r
is

\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})=\lambda_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})-\lambda_{r+1}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})
,
where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots
are the eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}
.

Remark 5

(Label co-occurrence structure)
.

The multilabel
label co-occurrence matrix

\mathbf{C}=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{y}_{i}\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}]
has entries
C_{\ell\ell}=\pi_{\ell}
and
C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\pi_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}:=\Pr(y_{i\ell}=y_{i\ell^{\prime}}=1)

for
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
. The
label covariance
is
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{y}}=\mathbf{C}-\boldsymbol{\pi}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\top}
. The finite-sample discriminant
\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n=({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})/n
converges (in the noiseless limit) to the
centered population reference

{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}=\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}\,\mathbf{A}^{\top}-K^{\mathrm{pop}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w},

(12)

where
\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}=\mathbf{B}_{\pi}-\mathbf{W}_{\pi}
is the
net label discriminant matrix
with
\mathbf{B}_{\pi}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{y}}\,\mathbf{D}_{\pi}^{-1}\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{y}}
(population centered between-class label scatter) and
\mathbf{W}_{\pi}=\sum_{\ell}\pi_{\ell}\,\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{y}\mid y_{\ell}=1)
(population within-class label scatter). In the single-label case,
\mathbf{W}_{\pi}=\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}=\mathbf{D}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{\pi}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\top}
, so the top-
r
eigenspace of
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}
coincides with that of
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{A}^{\top}-K^{\mathrm{pop}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}
.

3
Rank Characterization of the Multilabel Between-Class Scatter

The rank of the between-class scatter matrix determines the dimensionality of the useful discriminant subspace and dictates whether the orthogonality constraint is binding. In single-label LDA with
C
classes, the classical result is
\mathrm{rank}(S_{b})\leq C-1
. The
-1
in this bound comes from the single-label identity
\sum_{\ell}n_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu})=0
, which holds
because every sample is counted exactly once across the classes (
\sum_{\ell}n_{\ell}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}=\sum_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}=n\boldsymbol{\mu}
) and which forces the
C
vectors
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu})_{\ell=1}^{C}
to span an at-most
(C-1)
-dimensional subspace. In the multilabel case a sample with
k_{i}
labels contributes to
k_{i}
class-means, so
\sum_{\ell}n_{\ell}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}=\sum_{i}k_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}
, and this is no longer proportional to
n\boldsymbol{\mu}
as soon as the label cardinalities
k_{i}
vary across samples. The one rank reduction that produced the
-1
in the classical bound therefore disappears, and—as we show below—the effective multilabel discriminant dimensionality can reach
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})
rather than
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})-1
.

3.1
Matrix Factorization of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}

We begin by expressing
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
in factored form.

Lemma 6

(Factorization of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
)
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=[\sqrt{n_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\mu}),\ldots,\sqrt{n_{L}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{L}-\boldsymbol{\mu})]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times L}
. Then
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top}
. Moreover,

\mathbf{M}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2},

(13)

where
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{X}
is the centered data matrix.

Proof.

The identity
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top}
is immediate from the definition. For the
factorization, note that:

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu}=\frac{1}{n_{\ell}}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{\ell}-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{1}_{n}=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{y}_{\ell}}{n_{\ell}}-\frac{\mathbf{1}_{n}}{n}\right),

where
\mathbf{y}_{\ell}
is the
\ell
-th column of
\mathbf{Y}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sqrt{n_{\ell}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{y}_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}-\frac{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}\right).

Define
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_{\ell}=\frac{\mathbf{y}_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}-\frac{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}
. We verify directly that

\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_{\ell}=\mathbf{H}\frac{\mathbf{y}_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}
:

\mathbf{H}\frac{\mathbf{y}_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}=\left(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\right)\frac{\mathbf{y}_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}=\frac{\mathbf{y}_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}-\frac{\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{\ell}}{n\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}\,\mathbf{1}_{n}=\frac{\mathbf{y}_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}-\frac{\sqrt{n_{\ell}}}{n}\,\mathbf{1}_{n}=\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_{\ell},

where we used
\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{\ell}=n_{\ell}
(the number of samples with label
\ell
). As a consistency check,
\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_{\ell}=\sqrt{n_{\ell}}-\sqrt{n_{\ell}}=0
, confirming
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_{\ell}\in\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\perp}=\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H})
(recall that
\mathbf{H}
is the orthogonal projector onto
\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\perp}
, so its column space is exactly the set of vectors orthogonal to
\mathbf{1}_{n}
). Therefore, the matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}=[\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_{L}]=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}
and
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}
, since
\mathbf{H}
is symmetric and
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{X}
.
∎

3.2
Rank Theorem

Theorem 7

(Rank of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
)
.

Assume all labels are present (
n_{\ell}\geq 1
for all
\ell
). Then:

(i)

\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y})
.

(ii)

\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\min(d,\;n-1,\;\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y}))
.

(iii)

If
\mathbf{1}_{n}\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
, then
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\min(d,\;n-1,\;\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})-1)
.

(iv)

If
\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})\geq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})

and
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
is in general position relative to
\mathrm{col}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})
(i.e.,
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})\cap\ker(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top})=\{\mathbf{0}\}
; see Appendix
A
for discussion), then
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
.

Proof.

(i)
From Lemma
6
,
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{M})=\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2})
. Since
\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}
is an invertible diagonal matrix (all
n_{\ell}\geq 1
),
\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2})=\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y})
.

(ii)
By the rank inequality for matrix products,

\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y})\leq\min(\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}),\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y}))
. Since
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{H}
has rank
n-1
, we have
\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})\leq n-1
. Also
\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})\leq d
. Thus
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\min(d,n-1,\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y}))
.

(iii)
The matrix
\mathbf{M}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}
, so
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{M})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
(since
\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}
is invertible and right-multiplication by an invertible matrix preserves rank). Now assume
\mathbf{1}_{n}\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
. By the definition of the centering matrix,
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{1}_{n}=(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top})\mathbf{1}_{n}=\mathbf{1}_{n}-\mathbf{1}_{n}=\mathbf{0}
; that is,
\mathbf{1}_{n}\in\ker(\mathbf{H})
. Since
\mathbf{1}_{n}
is a nonzero vector in both
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
and
\ker(\mathbf{H})
, applying
\mathbf{H}
to
\mathbf{Y}
annihilates this direction: formally,
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})-\dim(\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})\cap\ker(\mathbf{H}))\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})-1
, because
\ker(\mathbf{H})=\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1}_{n})
and
\mathbf{1}_{n}\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
gives
\dim(\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})\cap\ker(\mathbf{H}))\geq 1
. Thus
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})-1
, and combining with part (ii) yields
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\min(d,n-1,\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})-1)
.

(iv)
We have
\mathbf{M}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}
. Since
\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}
is invertible,
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2})=\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
. Moreover,
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})\subseteq\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H})=\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\perp}
(because
\mathbf{H}
is a projector: every column of
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y}
is of the form
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}
, which lies in
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H})
).

The rank of
\mathbf{M}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2})
is the dimension of the image of
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
under the linear map
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By the rank–nullity theorem applied to
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}
restricted to
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
:

\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{M})=\dim\!\bigl(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}(\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y}))\bigr)=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})-\dim\!\bigl(\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})\cap\ker(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top})\bigr).

The “general-position” condition (see Appendix
A
for a detailed discussion) states precisely that
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})\cap\ker(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top})=\{\mathbf{0}\}
: no nonzero vector in
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
is annihilated by
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}
. Under this condition the second term vanishes and
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{M})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
.
∎

3.3
Consequences and Comparison with the Single-Label Case

Corollary 8

(Recovery of the classical bound)
.

In the single-label setting where each sample has exactly one label (each row of
\mathbf{Y}
has exactly one nonzero entry),
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq C-1
, where
C=L
is the number of classes, assuming all classes are represented. Equality
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=C-1
holds when additionally
\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})\geq C-1
and the general position condition of Theorem
7
(iv) is satisfied.

Proof.

In the single-label case,
\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{1}_{L}=\mathbf{1}_{n}
(each sample has exactly one label), so
\mathbf{1}_{n}\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
. Moreover,
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})=C
when all classes are represented. By Theorem
7
(iii),
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq C-1
.
∎

Corollary 9

(Excess discriminant dimensions in multilabel)
.

In the multilabel setting with
L
labels:

(i)

If
\mathbf{1}_{n}\notin\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
,
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})=L
,
\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})\geq L
, and
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})\cap\ker(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top})=\{\mathbf{0}\}
(the general position condition of Theorem
7
(iv), which holds almost surely for continuous data distributions), then
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=L
, exceeding the single-label bound of
L-1
.

(ii)

More generally,
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})
, and the bound
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})
depends on the label co-occurrence structure: it equals
\min(n,L)
when the label vectors are linearly independent and may be strictly less when labels exhibit linear dependence.

Proof.

(i)
Suppose
\mathbf{1}_{n}\notin\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
and
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})=L
. Because
\mathbf{1}_{n}\notin\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
, the only vector in
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})\cap\ker(\mathbf{H})
is
\mathbf{0}
(recall
\ker(\mathbf{H})=\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1}_{n})
), so centering does not reduce the rank:
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})=L
. Theorem
7
(iv) then applies: provided the data is sufficiently rich (
\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})\geq L
) and
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
is in general position relative to
\mathrm{col}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})
(which holds for continuous data distributions almost surely; see Appendix
A
), we conclude
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})=L
, exceeding the single-label bound of
L-1
.

(ii)
Follows directly from Theorem
7
(ii) and the observation that
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})\leq\min(n,L)
.
∎

Remark 10

(Connection to the label co-occurrence graph)
.

The label co-occurrence matrix
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}=\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
encodes pairwise label co-occurrence counts. Since
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})=\mathrm{rank}(\boldsymbol{\Gamma})
(by the standard identity
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{A})
), the rank of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
is ultimately governed by the spectral structure of
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}
. Specifically,
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\boldsymbol{\Gamma})
when
\mathbf{1}_{n}\notin\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
, and
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\boldsymbol{\Gamma})-1
otherwise.

4
Objective Equivalence Under Multilabel Scatter

We now study when the four Fisher objectives (
4
)–(
7
) yield the same optimal projection under orthogonality constraints with multilabel scatter matrices. In the single-label setting,
21
established equivalence under orthonormality constraints. We extend this analysis to the multilabel case, identifying both where the equivalence is preserved and where it breaks down.

4.1
Multilabel Partition of Variance

The starting point is the relationship between
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
,
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
, and the total scatter.

Theorem 11

(Multilabel partition of variance)
.

Assume every sample has at least one label (
k_{i}\geq 1
for all
i
). Define the
multilabel total scatter
as

{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}.

(14)

Then:

(i)

{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}
, where
k_{i}=\sum_{\ell}y_{i\ell}
is the label cardinality of sample
i
.

(ii)

The residual
\mathbf{R}={S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(k_{i}-1)(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}
is positive semidefinite.

(iii)

{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=S_{t}
if and only if
(k_{i}-1)(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})=\mathbf{0}
for all
i
; equivalently,
k_{i}=1
for every sample not at the global mean (the single-label case, up to degenerate exceptions).

(iv)

{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\preceq{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
(i.e.,
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succeq 0
).

Proof.

(i)
For each label
\ell
, the standard single-class decomposition
gives:

\sum_{i:\,y_{i\ell}=1}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}=n_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}+\sum_{i:\,y_{i\ell}=1}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell})^{\top}.

Summing the left-hand side over all
\ell=1,\ldots,L
gives
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
by the definitions (
2
)–(
3
). For the right-hand side we exchange the order of summation: sample
i
appears in the inner sum for label
\ell
if and only if
y_{i\ell}=1
, so each outer product
(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}
is counted once for every label that sample
i
possesses:

\displaystyle{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{i:\,y_{i\ell}=1}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}y_{i\ell}\right)(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}.

(ii)
Since
k_{i}\geq 1
for all samples (every sample has at least one label), we have
k_{i}-1\geq 0
, and
\mathbf{R}
is a sum of positive semidefinite (PSD) rank-one matrices with non-negative coefficients. Any conic (non-negative) combination of PSD matrices is itself PSD, so
\mathbf{R}\succeq 0
.

(iii)

\mathbf{R}=0
iff
k_{i}=1
for all
i
(assuming
\mathbf{x}_{i}\neq\boldsymbol{\mu}
for at least one
i
with
k_{i}>1
; more precisely,
\mathbf{R}=0
iff
(k_{i}-1)(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})=\mathbf{0}
for all
i
, which holds iff
k_{i}=1
for all
i
not at the mean).

(iv)

{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
and
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
is a sum of positive semidefinite matrices, hence
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succeq 0
.
∎

Remark 12
.

The identity
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
is formally the same as the classical partition of variance
S_{t}=S_{b}+S_{w}
, but with a crucial difference: the “total scatter”
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
is a
cardinality-weighted
version of the standard total scatter
S_{t}
. Each sample is counted
k_{i}
times rather than once. This reweighting is the root cause of the phenomena we identify in the remainder of this section.

4.2
Equivalence Under
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-Orthogonality

We first consider the constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
, which generalizes the ULDA constraint
(
18
;
42
)
to the multilabel setting, and establish the multilabel analogue of the single-label equivalence result of
21
.

Theorem 13

(Objective equivalence under
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality)
.

Assume
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succ 0
(positive definite). Under the constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
with
r\leq\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
, the four objectives
J_{\mathrm{TR}}
,
J_{\mathrm{RT}}
,
J_{\mathrm{DR}}
,
J_{\mathrm{TD}}
attain their respective optima at the same
\mathbf{W}
.

Proof.

Since
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succ 0
and
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succeq 0
, we have
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succeq{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succ 0
, so
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
is positive definite and the constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
is well-defined. Under this constraint, let
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}
. Since
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
(Theorem
11
), we have
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}
.

We first show that the eigenvalues
\theta_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\theta_{r}
of
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
lie in
[0,1)
. Since
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succeq 0
(it is a sum of PSD rank-one matrices by definition (
2
)), we have
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}\succeq 0
, giving
\theta_{i}\geq 0
. Since
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succ 0
by assumption and
\mathbf{W}
has full column rank (any
\mathbf{W}
satisfying
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
with
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succ 0
must have rank
r
), we get
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}\succ 0
, which means every eigenvalue of
\mathbf{I}_{r}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}
is strictly positive, i.e.,
\theta_{i}<1
for all
i
. Together:
0\leq\theta_{i}<1
.

We now express each objective in terms of
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{r}
. Under the constraint,
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})=\sum_{i}\theta_{i}
and
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})=r-\sum_{i}\theta_{i}
.
The four objectives become:

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{TR}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i}\theta_{i}}{r-\sum_{i}\theta_{i}},

\displaystyle\text{monotone increasing in }\textstyle\sum_{i}\theta_{i};

(15)

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{RT}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\theta_{i}}{1-\theta_{i}},

\displaystyle\text{Schur-convex on }[0,1)^{r};

(16)

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{DR}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\theta_{i}}{1-\theta_{i}};

(17)

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{TD}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i}\theta_{i}-r,

\displaystyle\text{monotone increasing in }\textstyle\sum_{i}\theta_{i}.

(18)

For
J_{\mathrm{RT}}
, the expression follows from
(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})^{-1}=(\mathbf{I}_{r}-\boldsymbol{\Theta})^{-1}
, which is diagonal in the eigenbasis of
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
with entries
1/(1-\theta_{i})
, so
\mathrm{tr}((\mathbf{I}_{r}-\boldsymbol{\Theta})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Theta})=\sum_{i}\theta_{i}/(1-\theta_{i})
. For
J_{\mathrm{DR}}
,
|\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}|/|\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}|=|\boldsymbol{\Theta}|/|\mathbf{I}_{r}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}|=\prod_{i}\theta_{i}/(1-\theta_{i})
.

Common optimizer via a change of variable.

We now show that all four objectives share the same optimizer by reducing to a Stiefel problem. Set
\mathbf{W}=({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})^{-1/2}\mathbf{V}
. The constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
becomes
\mathbf{V}^{\top}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})^{-1/2}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})^{-1/2}\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
, so
\mathbf{V}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
.

Let
\mathbf{P}={{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{-1/2}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\,{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{-1/2}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}
. Then

\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{V}^{\top}{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{-1/2}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\,{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{-1/2}\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}.

Since
\mathbf{P}
is PSD with eigenvalues in
[0,1)
(by the same argument as for
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
, applied to the full
d
-dimensional setting), the Poincaré separation theorem
(
15
)
states that the eigenvalues
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{r})
of
\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}
satisfy the componentwise interlacing bounds
\theta_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
, with equality when
\mathbf{V}
spans the top
r
eigenvectors of
\mathbf{P}
.

For
J_{\mathrm{TR}}
and
J_{\mathrm{TD}}
, which depend only on
\sum_{i}\theta_{i}
, the Ky Fan inequality
(
9
)
(which states
\max_{\mathbf{V}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}
, attained at the top
r
eigenvectors) gives
\sum_{i}\theta_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}
, so both are maximized by the top eigenvectors. For
J_{\mathrm{RT}}=\sum_{i}\theta_{i}/(1-\theta_{i})
, the Poincaré separation theorem gives the componentwise bound
\theta_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for each
i
. Since
g(\theta)=\theta/(1-\theta)
is increasing on
[0,1)
, we obtain
g(\theta_{i})\leq g(\lambda_{i})
for each
i
, and summing gives
J_{\mathrm{RT}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}g(\lambda_{i})
, with equality at the top eigenvectors. (The same conclusion follows from the Schur-convexity of
J_{\mathrm{RT}}

(
22
)
; see Appendix
B
for details.) For
J_{\mathrm{DR}}
, the argument is more delicate because the determinant ratio is a product rather than a sum of eigenvalue functions; the full details are given in Lemma
45
(Appendix
B
), and we summarize the key steps here. Note that
\mathbf{V}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P})\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{I}_{r}-\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}
(since
\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
), so its eigenvalues are
1-\theta_{i}
. Thus
J_{\mathrm{DR}}=\prod_{i}\theta_{i}/(1-\theta_{i})
. Observe that
J_{\mathrm{DR}}=0
whenever any
\theta_{i}=0
: the product vanishes if even one eigenvalue of
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}
is zero. Conversely, since the assumption
r\leq\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
ensures the top
r
eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}
of
\mathbf{P}
are strictly positive, the choice
\mathbf{V}=[\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{r}]
(top eigenvectors) achieves
\theta_{i}=\lambda_{i}>0
for all
i
, giving
J_{\mathrm{DR}}=\prod_{i}\lambda_{i}/(1-\lambda_{i})>0
. Any other
\mathbf{V}
yielding some
\theta_{i}=0
is strictly suboptimal. For
\mathbf{V}
with all
\theta_{i}>0
, the Poincaré separation theorem gives
\theta_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
componentwise, and since
t/(1-t)
is increasing on
[0,1)
, each factor satisfies
\theta_{i}/(1-\theta_{i})\leq\lambda_{i}/(1-\lambda_{i})
, so the product is maximized when
\theta_{i}=\lambda_{i}
for all
i
, i.e., when
\mathbf{V}
spans the top
r
eigenvectors of
\mathbf{P}
.

All four objectives are thus simultaneously maximized when the vector
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{r})
equals the
r
largest eigenvalues of
\mathbf{P}
. The corresponding optimal
\mathbf{W}
is:

\mathbf{W}^{*}=({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})^{-1/2}[\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{r}],

(19)

where
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{r}
are the top
r
eigenvectors of
({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})^{-1/2}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})^{-1/2}
, which is equivalent to the top
r
generalized eigenvectors of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{w}=\lambda{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{w}
.
∎

4.3
Equivalence and Inequivalence Under the Stiefel Constraint

We now turn to the Stiefel constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
. Here the situation is fundamentally different because
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}
is no longer fixed—it varies over the Stiefel manifold.

Theorem 14

(Structure under uniform label cardinality)
.

If all label cardinalities are equal, i.e.,
k_{i}=k
for all
i
and some constant
k\geq 1
, then
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=k\cdot S_{t}
and:

(i)

Under the
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
(equivalently,
\mathbf{W}^{\top}S_{t}\mathbf{W}=\frac{1}{k}\mathbf{I}_{r}
), all four objectives share the same optimizer (by Theorem
13
).

(ii)

Under
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
, the trace difference optimizer is the top
r
eigenspace of
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-kS_{t}
, and the trace ratio optimizer is the top
r
eigenspace of
(1+\lambda^{*}){S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\lambda^{*}kS_{t}
where
\lambda^{*}
is the optimal trace ratio value. Two logically distinct conditions govern when the four objectives share a common optimizer:

(a)

Stiefel feasibility of the unconstrained optimizer:
When
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
and
S_{t}
commute (
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}S_{t}=S_{t}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
), all four objectives have optimizers drawn from the common eigenbasis. In particular, the unconstrained RT and DR maximizer (the top
r
generalized eigenvectors of
({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}},{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
) is already orthonormal and hence Stiefel-feasible, so the Stiefel-constrained and unconstrained optima coincide for RT and DR.

(b)

Eigenvalue ordering consistency:
Even under commutativity, the eigenvalue
orderings
of
\alpha{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\beta S_{t}
can depend on
\alpha/\beta
(because the eigenvalues of
S_{t}
vary across eigenvectors). The TD and TR subspaces coincide when, additionally, no eigenvalue crossing occurs at position
r
between the two scalings—i.e., the top
r
eigenvectors of
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-kS_{t}
are the same as those of
(1+\lambda^{*}){S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\lambda^{*}kS_{t}
. A clean sufficient condition for both (a) and (b) simultaneously is
S_{t}=c\mathbf{I}_{d}
for some
c>0
(isotropic data after centering), which makes all eigenvalue orderings of
\alpha{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\beta S_{t}
consistent regardless of
\alpha/\beta
. If, additionally,
r\leq\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
and the generalized eigenvalue ordering of
({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}},{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
agrees with that of
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-kS_{t}
at position
r
, the ratio trace and determinant ratio also share this common optimal subspace.

Proof.

(i)
When
k_{i}=k
for all
i
, Theorem
11
(i) gives
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=kS_{t}
, so
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality (
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
) is equivalent to
\mathbf{W}^{\top}S_{t}\mathbf{W}=\frac{1}{k}\mathbf{I}_{r}
. Theorem
13
applies directly.

(ii)
Under the uniform-cardinality assumption,
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=kS_{t}-{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
(using part (i)). The trace difference therefore becomes:

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{TD}}(\mathbf{W})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})-\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})=\mathrm{tr}\!\bigl(\mathbf{W}^{\top}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-(kS_{t}-{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}))\mathbf{W}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\!\bigl(\mathbf{W}^{\top}(2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-kS_{t})\mathbf{W}\bigr).

The
Ky Fan maximum principle

(
9
)
states that for any symmetric matrix
\mathbf{M}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}
, the maximum of
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{V})
over
\mathbf{V}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
equals
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}
and is attained when
\mathbf{V}
spans the top
r
eigenvectors of
\mathbf{M}
. Applying this with
\mathbf{M}=2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-kS_{t}
, the TD objective under
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
is maximized by the top
r
eigenvectors of
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-kS_{t}
.

For the trace ratio, the equivalent formulation
(
23
)
gives the optimal subspace as the top
r
eigenspace of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\lambda^{*}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=(1+\lambda^{*}){S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\lambda^{*}kS_{t}
, where
\lambda^{*}
is the optimal trace ratio value. Both TD and TR optimizers are top eigenspaces of matrices of the form
\alpha{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\beta S_{t}
with
\alpha,\beta>0
.

When
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
and
S_{t}
commute (
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}S_{t}=S_{t}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
), the spectral theorem guarantees that they share a common orthonormal eigenbasis
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d}\}
with
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{v}_{i}=s_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
and
S_{t}\mathbf{v}_{i}=\mu_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
. Any linear combination
\alpha{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\beta S_{t}
has the
same
eigenvectors with eigenvalues
\alpha s_{i}-\beta\mu_{i}
. However, because
\mu_{i}
varies across eigenvectors, the ordering of
\alpha s_{i}-\beta\mu_{i}
can change with
\alpha/\beta
: two eigenvectors
\mathbf{v}_{i}
and
\mathbf{v}_{j}
swap their relative ranking whenever
\alpha/\beta
crosses the threshold
({\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}})/({s_{i}-s_{j}})
(provided
s_{i}\neq s_{j}
). Consequently, the top
r
eigenspace of
\alpha{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\beta S_{t}
is preserved across all positive
\alpha/\beta
if and only if no such crossing occurs at position
r
. When
S_{t}=c\mathbf{I}_{d}
for some
c>0
, all
\mu_{i}
are equal and no crossing is possible, so the top
r
subspace is independent of
\alpha/\beta
.

For the ratio trace and determinant ratio under
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
: the matrix
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=kS_{t}-{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
also commutes with both (since
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
is a linear combination of commuting matrices), so
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
,
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
, and
S_{t}
share the common eigenbasis, and the generalized eigenvectors of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{w}=\lambda{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{w}
are precisely these common (orthonormal) eigenvectors with generalized eigenvalues
s_{i}/(k\mu_{i}-s_{i})
. The
unconstrained
maximum of
J_{\mathrm{RT}}
over all full-rank
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}
equals
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}},{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
(the sum of the top
r
generalized eigenvalues), attained at the top
r
generalized eigenvectors. Since these eigenvectors are orthonormal, the unconstrained maximizer already satisfies
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
—it lies on the Stiefel manifold. Consequently the Stiefel-constrained maximum of
J_{\mathrm{RT}}
equals the unconstrained maximum and is attained at the same
\mathbf{W}
(because the constrained maximum is bounded above by the unconstrained one, and the unconstrained maximizer is feasible). The same argument applies to
J_{\mathrm{DR}}
.

The RT/DR optimizer (the top
r
generalized eigenspace) coincides with the TD optimizer (the top
r
eigenspace of
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-kS_{t}
) when the ordering of the generalized eigenvalues
s_{i}/(k\mu_{i}-s_{i})
agrees with the ordering of
2s_{i}-k\mu_{i}
at position
r
. Note that for
fixed

\mu_{i}
, the map
t\mapsto t/(k\mu_{i}-t)
is increasing for
t<k\mu_{i}
, so both orderings are consistent with that of
s_{i}
. However, when
\mu_{i}
varies across eigenvectors, the orderings of
s_{i}/(k\mu_{i}-s_{i})
and
2s_{i}-k\mu_{i}
can disagree. When
S_{t}=c\mathbf{I}_{d}
, all
\mu_{i}=c
and the orderings are automatically consistent, ensuring all four objectives share the same optimal subspace.
∎

Remark 15
.

The commutativity condition
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}S_{t}=S_{t}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
holds whenever: (a)
S_{t}=c\mathbf{I}
for some
c>0
(isotropic data); (b) all within-class covariances are equal and proportional to
S_{t}
; or (c) the generalized eigenvectors of
({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}},S_{t})
happen to be orthonormal. Without commutativity, the eigenvectors of
\alpha{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-\beta S_{t}
vary with
\alpha/\beta
, causing TR and TD to have different optimal subspaces. With commutativity, the eigenvectors are shared but the eigenvalue ordering can still depend on
\alpha/\beta
when
\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}(S_{t})
are non-uniform, so commutativity alone does not guarantee a common optimizer; the ordering-consistency condition in Theorem
14
(ii) is needed. The divergence between objectives is controlled by the spectral gap, as quantified in Theorem
16
.

Theorem 16

(Inequivalence under variable label cardinality)
.

When label cardinalities
k_{i}
vary across samples, the trace difference objective under
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
may yield a strictly different optimal subspace from the ratio trace objective. Specifically:

(i)

The trace difference optimizer is the top
r
eigenspace of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
.

(ii)

The ratio trace optimizer is related to the top
r
generalized eigenspace of
({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}},{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
.

(iii)

Assume that
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t}
has a positive spectral gap
\gamma=\lambda_{r}(2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t})-\lambda_{r+1}(2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t})>0
and that
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}<\gamma
(the perturbation is smaller than the gap). Then the trace difference optimal subspace is perturbed from its single-label counterpart by:

\sin\angle(\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{TD}},\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{TD}}^{(0)})\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}}{\gamma},

(20)

where
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{TD}}^{(0)}
is the TD optimizer for the single-label-like matrix
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t}
(i.e., without cardinality reweighting) and
\mathbf{R}={S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t}
is the residual from Theorem
11
(ii).

Proof.

(i)
Under
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
,
J_{\mathrm{TD}}(\mathbf{W})=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\mathbf{W})
. Using the partition
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
(Theorem
11
), we substitute:

{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}},

so
J_{\mathrm{TD}}(\mathbf{W})=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}(2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\mathbf{W})
. (The factor of
2
in front of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
arises because the between-class scatter contributes positively both directly and via the subtraction of
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}={S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
.) By the Ky Fan maximum principle
(
9
)
(see the statement in the proof
of Theorem
14
), this is maximized by the top
r
eigenvectors of
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
.

(ii)
The ratio trace
J_{\mathrm{RT}}(\mathbf{W})=\mathrm{tr}((\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})^{-1}\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})
under
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
does not simplify to a linear eigenvalue problem. Its stationary points satisfy a nonlinear matrix equation involving both
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
and
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}

(
1
;
8
)
. The optimal subspace is related to (but generally different from) the span of the top
r
generalized eigenvectors of
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{w}=\lambda{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{w}
.

(iii)
The multilabel trace difference matrix is
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}=2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t}-\mathbf{R}
, a perturbation of the single-label-like matrix
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t}
by
-\mathbf{R}
. By the Davis–Kahan
\sin\Theta
theorem
(
7
;
43
)
, the perturbation in the top
r
eigenspace satisfies
\sin\angle(\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{TD}},\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{TD}}^{(0)})\leq\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}/\gamma
, where
\gamma
is the spectral gap of
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{TD}}^{(0)}
is the TD optimizer when
\mathbf{R}=0
. In the single-label case,
\mathbf{R}=0
and the bound vanishes, confirming that cardinality reweighting is the sole source of perturbation to the TD eigenspace.
∎

Corollary 17

(Spectral characterization of divergence)
.

The spectral norm of the residual satisfies

\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}\leq\max_{i}(k_{i}-1)\cdot\lambda_{\max}(S_{t}^{(K)}),

where
S_{t}^{(K)}=\sum_{i:\,k_{i}>1}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}
is the unweighted total scatter restricted to multi-labeled samples. In particular,
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}=0
in the single-label case, and the divergence (
20
) vanishes.

Proof.

From Theorem
11
(ii),
\mathbf{R}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(k_{i}-1)(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}
. Each term
(k_{i}-1)(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}
is a non-negative scalar (
k_{i}-1\geq 0
) times a rank-one positive semidefinite (PSD) matrix
(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}
, so
\mathbf{R}\succeq 0
.

To bound
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}=\max_{\|\mathbf{u}\|=1}\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{u}
, we compute the Rayleigh quotient for an arbitrary unit vector
\mathbf{u}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(k_{i}-1)\bigl(\mathbf{u}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\bigr)^{2}.

Since only terms with
k_{i}>1
contribute (the others have coefficient
0
), and each coefficient satisfies
k_{i}-1\leq\max_{j}(k_{j}-1)
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{j}(k_{j}-1)\cdot\sum_{i:\,k_{i}>1}\bigl(\mathbf{u}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\bigr)^{2}=\max_{j}(k_{j}-1)\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\top}S_{t}^{(K)}\mathbf{u},

where
S_{t}^{(K)}=\sum_{i:\,k_{i}>1}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}
is the unweighted scatter restricted to multi-labeled samples. Maximizing over unit
\mathbf{u}
gives
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}\leq\max_{i}(k_{i}-1)\cdot\lambda_{\max}(S_{t}^{(K)})
. In the single-label case,
k_{i}=1
for all
i
, so every coefficient is zero and
\mathbf{R}=0
.
∎

5
Label-Distance Preservation

We now establish that orthogonal multilabel projections preserve the relationship between feature-space distances and label-space distances, in a spirit related to—but distinct from—the Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma
(
19
)
. Unlike JL, which gives probabilistic guarantees for random projections, our bounds are deterministic and relate projected distances to
label-space
distances. This result provides a theoretical foundation for using orthogonally projected features in multilabel nearest-neighbor classification.

5.1
Setup

We work with the linear label-effect model (Definition
2
), under which the label-conditional mean of sample
i
is
\boldsymbol{\mu}+\mathbf{A}\mathbf{y}_{i}
.

5.2
Distance Preservation Bound

Theorem 18

(Label-distance preservation)
.

Under the linear label-effect model (Definition
2
), let
\mathbf{W}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
be an orthogonal projection. For any two samples
i,j
, the projected squared distance decomposes as:

\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}+2\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})+\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})\|^{2},

(21)

where
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}=\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{y}_{j}
. Taking expectations over the noise yields the two-sided bound:

\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})+C_{w}\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})+C_{w},

(22)

where
d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})=\|\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{y}_{j}\|_{1}
is the Hamming distance between label vectors,
\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})
is the largest squared singular value of the full matrix
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times L}
,
C_{w}=2\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W})
, and
\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})
in the lower bound denotes the smallest squared singular value of
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}
. More precisely, the lower bound follows from relaxing
\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S})\geq\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})
where
\mathbf{A}_{S}
is the submatrix of
\mathbf{A}
restricted to the columns indexed by the support
S=\{\ell:\delta_{ij,\ell}\neq 0\}
of
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}
(see the proof below). When
r\geq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{A})
and
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{A})\subseteq\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{W})
(see Proposition
22
),
\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})=\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{A})>0
, yielding a nontrivial lower bound for all label pairs.

Proof.

Under the model (
8
),
\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j}=\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}+(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})
, where
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}=\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{y}_{j}
. Projecting with
\mathbf{W}
gives
\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}+\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})
. Writing
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}
(deterministic signal) and
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})
(random noise), the squared norm expands as

\|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{a}\|^{2}+2\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{b}+\|\mathbf{b}\|^{2},

which is exactly (
21
).

Taking expectations over
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j}
: the signal term
\|\mathbf{a}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}
is deterministic. The cross-term
\mathbb{E}[2\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{b}]=2\,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}\,\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})]
vanishes because
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}
and
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j}
are independent with zero mean (so
\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j}]=\mathbf{0}
), while the vectors
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}
,
\mathbf{A}
, and
\mathbf{W}
are all deterministic. For the noise term:

\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{b}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})^{\top}\mathbf{W})]=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\,\mathbb{E}[(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})^{\top}]\,\mathbf{W})=2\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W}),

where the last step uses independence (
\mathrm{Cov}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})=0
) and identical covariance (
\mathrm{Cov}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i})=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}
). Hence
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}]=\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}+C_{w}
with
C_{w}=2\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W})
.

For the signal term,
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}=\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{y}_{j}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{L}
with
\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}=d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})
(each nonzero entry is
\pm 1
, so
|\delta_{ij,\ell}|^{2}=|\delta_{ij,\ell}|
for every component, giving
\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|_{1}=d_{H}
). Let
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,L\}
denote the
support
of
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}
(the set of labels on which samples
i
and
j
differ), and let
\mathbf{A}_{S}
be the submatrix of
\mathbf{A}
consisting of the columns indexed by
S
. Then
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij,S}
, where
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij,S}\in\{-1,1\}^{|S|}
. By the Rayleigh quotient characterization of singular values:

\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S})\,\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij,S}\|^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij,S}\|^{2}\leq\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S})\,\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij,S}\|^{2},

and since

\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij,S}\|^{2}=|S|=d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})
,

\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S})\geq\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})
, and

\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S})\leq\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})

(for the upper bound,
\mathbf{A}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S}
is a principal submatrix of
\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}
, so Cauchy interlacing gives
\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\geq\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S})
, yielding
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S})\leq\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})
; for the lower bound, any unit vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{|S|}
embeds as a unit vector
\bar{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
(by zero-padding on
S^{c}
) with
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S}\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\bar{\mathbf{v}}
, so

\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S})=\min_{\|\mathbf{v}\|=1}\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{S}\mathbf{v}\|\geq\min_{\|\mathbf{u}\|=1}\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}\|=\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A});

see also
15
), we obtain the two-sided bound (
22
).
∎

5.3
The Role of Orthogonality

The orthogonality constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
provides a direct benefit for label-distance preservation by controlling the noise term.

Corollary 19

(Noise control under orthogonality)
.

For
\mathbf{W}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
:

2r\,\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})\leq C_{w}=2\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W})\leq 2r\,\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}).

(23)

In contrast, for unconstrained
\mathbf{W}
(not on the Stiefel manifold), the noise term
C_{w}
is unbounded above.

Proof.

For
\mathbf{W}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
, the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W}
are bounded between
\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
and
\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
by the Poincaré separation theorem
(
15
)
, since
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W}
is a compression of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}
to the
r
-dimensional subspace
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{W})
. Thus
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W})\in[r\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}),r\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})]
. For unconstrained
\mathbf{W}
, scaling
\mathbf{W}\to c\mathbf{W}
scales
C_{w}
by
c^{2}
.
∎

The decomposition in Theorem
18
naturally suggests a signal-to-noise ratio for pairwise discriminability. Recall that in classical (single-label) LDA, Fisher’s discriminant ratio
J_{F}(\mathbf{W})=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}S_{b}\mathbf{W})/\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}S_{w}\mathbf{W})
quantifies the
global
discriminability of the projection: the ratio of between-class to within-class variance in the projected space
(
10
;
12
)
. In the multilabel setting, there is no single between-class distance—discriminability depends on
which pair
of label vectors is being compared. Theorem
18
provides exactly this pairwise decomposition:
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}]=\underbrace{\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}}_{\text{signal}}+\underbrace{C_{w}}_{\text{noise}}
, where the signal term depends on the specific label difference
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}=\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{y}_{j}
and the noise term is
pair-independent. Dividing signal by noise gives the pairwise analogue
of
J_{F}
.

Definition 20

(Pairwise discriminant SNR)
.

Under the linear label-effect model (Definition
2
), for a projection
\mathbf{W}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
and samples
i,j
, the
pairwise discriminant signal-to-noise ratio
is:

\mathrm{SNR}_{ij}(\mathbf{W})=\frac{\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}}{C_{w}}=\frac{\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}}{2\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W})},

(24)

the ratio of the expected squared projected signal (the label-dependent component of the expected projected distance) to the expected projected noise contribution
C_{w}
(the label-independent component). When specialized to the single-label case with two classes (
L=2
,
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}=\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{2}
), this reduces to
\mathrm{SNR}_{ij}(\mathbf{W})=\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2})\|^{2}/(2\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W}))
, which is (up to a constant factor) the classical Fisher criterion for the two-class projected distance.

Corollary 21

(Pairwise discriminant SNR bounds)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
18
, the pairwise discriminant SNR (Definition
20
) satisfies:

\frac{\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})}{2r\,\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})}\leq\mathrm{SNR}_{ij}(\mathbf{W})\leq\frac{\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})}{2r\,\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})}.

(25)

In particular,
\mathrm{SNR}_{ij}(\mathbf{W})
grows linearly with the Hamming distance
d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})
, confirming that samples with more dissimilar labels are projected further apart relative to noise. Equivalently, these bounds characterize the ratio

\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}]-C_{w}\bigr)/C_{w}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
18
, the expected projected distance satisfies
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}]=\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}+C_{w}
, with signal bounds
\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}\leq\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}\leq\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}
. Dividing by
C_{w}
gives bounds on
\mathrm{SNR}_{ij}(\mathbf{W})
. For the lower bound, we use
C_{w}\leq 2r\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
(Corollary
19
). For the upper bound, we use
C_{w}\geq 2r\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
.
∎

5.4
Connection to the Rank Characterization

The label-distance preservation bound depends critically on the singular values of
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}
. When
\mathbf{W}
captures the column space of
\mathbf{A}
(which requires
r\geq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{A})
), we then have
\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})>0
, yielding a nontrivial lower bound. By Theorem
7
,
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\min(d,n-1,\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y}))
characterizes the effective dimensionality. Under the linear model,
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{A})
relates to
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
: the between-class scatter captures the structure of
\mathbf{A}
modulated by the label distribution.

Proposition 22

(Sufficient projection dimension)
.

Under the linear label-effect model, if
r\geq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{A})
and
\mathbf{W}
spans a subspace containing
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{A})
, then
\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})=\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{A})>0
(assuming
\mathbf{A}
has full column rank). In particular, it suffices to take
r\geq L
to guarantee
\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})>0
when label effects are linearly independent.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{A})\subseteq\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{W})
, then
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}
(since
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\top}
is the orthogonal projector onto
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{W})
), and
\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\|=\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\|
for all
\mathbf{v}
. Thus
\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})=\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{A})
.
∎

5.5
Extension to Jaccard Distance

The Hamming distance treats all label disagreements equally. For multilabel data with varying label cardinalities, the Jaccard distance provides a normalized alternative.

Corollary 23

(Jaccard distance bound)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
18
, if
k_{i},k_{j}\geq 1
:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}\right]-C_{w}\geq\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\cdot(k_{i}+k_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j})\cdot d_{J}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j}),

(26)

where
d_{J}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})=1-\frac{|\mathbf{y}_{i}\cap\mathbf{y}_{j}|}{|\mathbf{y}_{i}\cup\mathbf{y}_{j}|}
is the Jaccard distance, and we use
|\mathbf{y}_{i}\cap\mathbf{y}_{j}|=\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j}
and
|\mathbf{y}_{i}\cup\mathbf{y}_{j}|=k_{i}+k_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j}
.
In particular, since
\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j}\leq\min(k_{i},k_{j})
:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}\right]-C_{w}\geq\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\cdot\max(k_{i},k_{j})\cdot d_{J}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j}).

(27)

Proof.

The Hamming distance satisfies
d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})=k_{i}+k_{j}-2\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j}
. The Jaccard distance is
d_{J}=1-\frac{\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j}}{k_{i}+k_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j}}=\frac{d_{H}}{k_{i}+k_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j}}
. Rearranging,
d_{H}=(k_{i}+k_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j})\,d_{J}
. Substituting into the lower bound of Theorem
18
yields (
26
). For the weakened bound, note that
\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j}\leq\min(k_{i},k_{j})
(since each entry of
\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j}
is in
\{0,1\}
), so
k_{i}+k_{j}-\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{j}\geq k_{i}+k_{j}-\min(k_{i},k_{j})=\max(k_{i},k_{j})
, giving (
27
).
∎

6
Statistical Guarantees

The results of Sections
3
–
5
are algebraic: they characterize the scatter matrices and the projection for a
fixed
data set. We now establish statistical guarantees—finite-sample estimation bounds, concentration inequalities, and robustness results—that hold when the data are generated from the linear label-effect model (Definition
2
).

Throughout, the sample discriminant
\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n
converges to the centered population reference
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}
rather than to the naïve
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}
; see Remark
5
and (
12
) for the definition of
\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}
and the single-label reduction.

Throughout this section, we present each result at three levels of generality that mirror the algebraic structure of Sections
4
–
5
:

(i)

General
(unconstrained or arbitrary
\mathbf{W}
): the baseline bound, expressed in terms of
\mathbf{\Psi}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W}
.

(ii)

Stiefel constraint
(
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
): the Poincaré separation theorem bounds the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{\Psi}
between
\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
and
\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
, yielding tighter variance and tail parameters.

(iii)

{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality
(
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
): the constraint couples signal and noise through
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}-\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}
(Theorem
13
), so that the projected noise
\mathbf{\Psi}

decreases as the signal content increases
. This gives the tightest bounds and provides a direct statistical payoff of the
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality constraint established in Section
4
.

The subspace estimation and minimax bounds (Sections
6.1
–
6.2
) are presented at the same three levels: the general trace-difference bound is complemented by an
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality version that analyzes the generalized eigenspace estimator (the common optimizer of all four objectives under Theorem
13
) and benefits from the self-normalizing structure of the generalized eigenvalue problem.

6.1
Finite-Sample Subspace Estimation

Let
\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{n}
denote the top-
r
eigenspace of the sample trace-difference matrix
\hat{\mathbf{M}}=2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
(the optimizer from Theorem
16
(i)). Recall from Remark
5
that the finite-sample discriminant
\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n
converges to the centered population reference
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}\mathbf{A}^{\top}-K^{\mathrm{pop}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}
; let
{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{c}
denote its top-
r
eigenspace. The subspace estimation error is measured by the
\sin\angle
distance: for subspaces
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
of equal dimension,
\sin\angle(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})=\|\sin\boldsymbol{\Theta}\|_{2}
where
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
is the diagonal matrix of principal angles
(
7
)
.

The result below is a Davis–Kahan-style bound with the operator-norm perturbation
\|\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n-{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}\|_{2}
splitting into a signal–noise cross-term
\sigma\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}\sqrt{d/n}
and a pure within-scatter term
\sigma^{2}k_{\max}\sqrt{d/n}
; the multilabel-specific ingredient is the explicit
k_{\max}
factor.

Theorem 24

(Finite-sample subspace estimation)
.

Under the linear label-effect model (Definition
2
) with
\sigma
-sub-Gaussian noise, assume:

(A1)

The label matrix
\mathbf{Y}
is fixed with all labels present (
n_{\ell}\geq 1
).

(A2)

The centered population reference
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}
has a positive eigenvalue gap
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})>0
at rank
r
.

(A3)

n\geq C_{0}\,k_{\max}^{2}\,d\,\log(d/\delta)
for a constant
C_{0}
depending on
(\sigma\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+\sigma^{2}k_{\max})/\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})
(signal-to-noise ratio) and
\delta\in(0,1)
, ensuring the Davis–Kahan perturbation condition
\|\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n-{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}\|_{2}<\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})/2
holds with high probability.

Then with probability at least
1-\delta
:

\sin\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{n},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{c})\leq\frac{\bigl(C_{1}\,\sigma\,\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+C_{2}\,\sigma^{2}\,k_{\max}\bigr)\,\sqrt{d\,\log(d/\delta)/n}}{\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})},

(28)

for universal constants
C_{1},C_{2}
.

The proof (Appendix
D.1
) combines a matrix Bernstein bound
(
32
)
on
\|\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n-{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}\|_{2}
with the Davis–Kahan
\sin\boldsymbol{\Theta}
theorem
(
7
;
43
)
.

Remark 25

(The role of
k_{\max}
and
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}
)
.

The factor
k_{\max}
quantifies the additional estimation difficulty from multilabel cardinality reweighting: each sample contributes to
k_{i}
label-conditional scatters, inflating the effective within-scatter noise. In the strong-signal regime (
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}\gg\sigma k_{\max}
), the signal-noise cross-term dominates; when
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}\ll\sigma k_{\max}
, the within-scatter noise term dominates instead. In the single-label case (
k_{\max}=1
), the bound reduces to
O\bigl((\sigma\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+\sigma^{2})\sqrt{d\log(d/\delta)/n}/\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})\bigr)
, recovering the standard rate for PCA/LDA subspace estimation
(
4
)
.

An immediate consequence is consistency:

Corollary 26

(Consistency)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
24
with fixed
d
,
L
,
k_{\max}
,
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}
, and
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})>0
:

(i)

\sin\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{n},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{c})\xrightarrow{P}0
as
n\to\infty
.

(ii)

\mathbb{E}[\sin^{2}\!\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{n},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{c})]=O\!\left(\frac{(\sigma\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+\sigma^{2}k_{\max})^{2}\,d\,\log d}{n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})}\right)
.

Proof.

(i)
Set
\delta=1/n
in Theorem
24
. The bound becomes

O\bigl((\sigma\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+\sigma^{2}k_{\max})\sqrt{d\log(dn)/n}\,/\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})\bigr)\to 0,

and
P(\text{bound fails})\leq 1/n\to 0
.

(ii)
We use the layer-cake formula for non-negative random variables:

\mathbb{E}[Z]=\int_{0}^{\infty}P(Z>t)\,dt
, applied to
Z=\sin^{2}\!\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{n},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{c})\in[0,1]
. Theorem
24
provides a high-probability bound of the form
\sin\!\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{n},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{c})\leq B\sqrt{\log(d/\delta)/n}/\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})
with failure probability
\delta
, where
B=O((\sigma\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+\sigma^{2}k_{\max})\sqrt{d})
. Inverting the relationship between
\delta
and
t
: the high-probability bound states
\sin^{2}\!\angle\leq B^{2}\log(d/\delta)/(n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2})
with failure probability
\delta
, so
P(\sin^{2}\!\angle>t)\leq\delta(t)
where
\delta(t)
is found by setting
t=B^{2}\log(d/\delta)/(n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2})
and solving for
\delta
:
\delta(t)=d\,\exp(-c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}\,t/B^{2})
. Note that
\delta(t)=d\,\exp(-c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}\,t/B^{2})\leq 1
precisely when
t\geq t_{0}:=B^{2}\log d/(c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2})
; for
t<t_{0}
the tail probability is trivially at most
1
. For
t\geq t_{0}
we use
P(\sin^{2}\!\angle>t)\leq d\,\exp(-c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}\,t/B^{2})
. Splitting the integral at
t_{0}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\sin^{2}\!\angle]

\displaystyle\leq t_{0}\cdot 1+\int_{t_{0}}^{1}d\,e^{-c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}\,t/B^{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq t_{0}+\frac{d\,B^{2}}{c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}}\,e^{-c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}\,t_{0}/B^{2}}=t_{0}+\frac{d\,B^{2}}{c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{d}=t_{0}+\frac{B^{2}}{c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}}=O(t_{0})

since
e^{-c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}\,t_{0}/B^{2}}=1/d
by definition of
t_{0}
, and
B^{2}/(c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2})=t_{0}/\log d\leq t_{0}
. Substituting
t_{0}=B^{2}\log d/(c\,n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2})
and
B^{2}=O((\sigma\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+\sigma^{2}k_{\max})^{2}d)
:
\mathbb{E}[\sin^{2}\!\angle]=O\bigl((\sigma\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+\sigma^{2}k_{\max})^{2}d\log d/(n\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2})\bigr)
.
∎

6.1.1
Subspace Estimation Under
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-Orthogonality

Theorem
24
analyzes the top-
r
eigenspace of
\hat{\mathbf{M}}
(the trace-difference estimator). Under
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality, all four Fisher objectives share the same optimizer (Theorem

): the top-

generalized eigenspace of
({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}},{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})

Theorem 27

(Subspace estimation under
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality)
.

Under the linear label-effect model (Definition

) with
\sigma
-sub-Gaussian noise and assumption (A1), let

denote the top-

({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}},{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})

\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
(Theorem

). Let
{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{S}
denote the top-

generalized eigenspace of the population limits
({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty},{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})
where
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}_{\pi}\mathbf{A}^{\top}
(the centered population between-class scatter, distinct from
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{A}^{\top}

{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty}+{S_{w,c}^{\mathrm{pop}}}

). Let
\theta_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\theta_{d}
be the generalized eigenvalues of
({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty},{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})
and define the

\Delta_{r}=\theta_{r}-\theta_{r+1}>0

Assume:

(A2
′
)

\Delta_{r}>0
(positive generalized eigenvalue gap at rank

(A3
′
)

n\geq C_{0}^{\prime}\,k_{\max}^{2}\,d\,\log(d/\delta)
for a constant

Then with probability at least

\sin\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{S},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{S})\leq\frac{C_{3}\,\kappa({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})\,\bigl(\sigma\,\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+\sigma^{2}\,k_{\max}\bigr)\,\sqrt{d\,\log(d/\delta)/n}}{\Delta_{r}\cdot\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})}.

(29)

The proof (Appendix

Remark 28

(Comparison with the trace-difference bound)
.

The bound (
29
) differs from the trace-difference bound (
28
) in three respects. (i)
Gap quantity.
The denominator uses the scale-invariant generalized gap
\Delta_{r}=\theta_{r}-\theta_{r+1}\in(0,1)
rather than the absolute gap
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})
(which scales with
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}^{2}
); replacing
\mathbf{A}
by

leaves
\Delta_{r}
unchanged. (ii)
Self-normalization.
The factor
\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})
arises from whitening by
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
; in the isotropic-noise case
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}=\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}

\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})=K^{\mathrm{pop}}\sigma^{2}
, absorbing the noise scale. (iii)
Condition number.
The factor
\kappa({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})
is the price of a data-dependent normalization; it is

in the moderate-signal regime

and grows when the total scatter is ill-conditioned.

6.2
Minimax Lower Bound

We now establish that the rate in Theorem
24
is minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors and the
k_{\max}
term. The lower bound applies to
any
estimator, not just the trace-difference method.

Intuition.

No estimator can drive
\mathbb{E}[\sin^{2}\!\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}^{*}})]
below the order
\sigma^{2}(d-r)/(ng)
, where
g
is the spectral gap: the resulting rate matches the upper bound of Theorem
24
in
n
,
d
, and
g
, up to logarithmic factors and the
k_{\max}
term whose tightness remains open (Remark
31
).

Theorem 29

(Minimax lower bound)
.

Fix
d\geq 2r
,
\sigma>0
, and a gap lower bound
g>0
. Let
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
be the class of parameters
(\mathbf{A},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
in the linear label-effect model such that
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}=\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}
and
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})\geq g
. Then for any
n\geq 1
:

\inf_{\hat{\mathbf{W}}}\sup_{(\mathbf{A},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})\in\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sin^{2}\!\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}^{*}})\right]\geq\frac{c\,\sigma^{2}\,(d-r)}{n\,g},

(30)

where the infimum is over all measurable estimators
\hat{\mathbf{W}}
and
c>0
is a positive constant that does not depend on
d
,
n
,
\sigma
,
r
, or
g
. The constant
c=c(\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2},\,\min_{\ell}\pi_{\ell})
depends on the fixed label matrix (through
\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2}
with
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}=\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
, which bounds the KL divergence in the packing argument) and the population label probabilities (through
\min_{\ell}\pi_{\ell}>0
, which calibrates the worst-case signal construction).

Remark 30

(Implicit assumption
L\geq r
)
.

The condition
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})\geq g>0
forces
L\geq r
: since
\mathrm{rank}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{A})\leq L
, a positive
r
-th eigenvalue gap requires at least
r
strictly positive eigenvalues, hence
L\geq r
.

The proof (Appendix
D.3
) uses a standard Fano–Grassmannian packing argument
(
34
)
: the Grassmannian packing number of order
\exp(c\,r(d-r)/\varepsilon^{2})

(
31
;
24
)
cancels against the
r
factor in the KL divergence to yield the
(d-r)
dependence, consistent with the known
r
-independence of the minimax rate under operator-norm
\sin^{2}\!\angle
loss
(
3
)
.

Remark 31

(The
k_{\max}
gap)
.

Comparing Corollary
26
(ii) with Theorem
29
, the upper bound is
O\bigl((\sigma\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+\sigma^{2}k_{\max})^{2}\,d\log d/(n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2})\bigr)
while the lower bound is

\Omega(\sigma^{2}\,(d-r)/(n\,\mathrm{gap}_{r}))
. Both rates agree in the dependence on
n
and
d
. In the single-label case (
k_{\max}=1
) with
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}=\Theta(\sigma)
, the upper bound becomes
O(\sigma^{4}d\log d/(n\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}))
and the lower bound is
\Omega(\sigma^{2}d/(n\mathrm{gap}_{r}))
, which match up to logarithmic factors when
\mathrm{gap}_{r}=\Theta(\sigma^{2})
. Whether the
k_{\max}^{2}
factor in the upper bound is tight (multilabel estimation is genuinely harder) or an artifact (removable by a sharper analysis) is an open question; see Section
7
.

Remark 32

(Co-occurrence structure in the lower bound)
.

The lower bound is stated in terms of
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})
while the upper bound (Theorem
24
) targets the centered reference
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})
(Remark
5
). For the hypotheses constructed in the proof (Appendix
D.3
), with
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}=\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}
, both matrices share the same top-
r
eigenspace
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{A}_{j})
(since the shift by
\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}
does not change eigenspaces). The gaps are related by a label-dependent constant:

\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})=\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})\cdot\lambda_{\min}\!\bigl((\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})^{-1/2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})^{-1/2}\bigr),

where
\mathbf{Q}
is the partial isometry in the construction. When
\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}
is positive definite on
\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{Q}^{\top})
(which holds whenever the label co-occurrence structure provides at least
r
discriminative directions), the ratio
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})/\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})
is a positive constant depending only on the label distribution, and the lower bound (
30
) holds equally with
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})
in place of
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}})
, up to a modified label-dependent constant
c
. In the single-label case,
\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}=\mathbf{D}_{\pi}-\boldsymbol{\pi}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\top}
has rank

(since

by Remark

when

, or

with the centering perturbation acting within the signal subspace), and the lower bound applies with
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})
at the cost of adjusting

by a factor depending on
\min_{\ell}\pi_{\ell}
.

Corollary 33

(Minimax lower bound for the generalized gap)

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}=\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}

\inf_{\hat{\mathbf{W}}}\sup_{(\mathbf{A},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})\in\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{S}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sin^{2}\!\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{S})\right]\geq\frac{c_{S}\,\sigma^{2}\,(d-r)}{n\,g},

(31)

where
\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{S}
is the class of parameters with
\Delta_{r}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty},{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})\geq g_{\Delta}

32
. Equivalently, restating in terms of the generalized gap:

\inf_{\hat{\mathbf{W}}}\sup_{\Delta_{r}\geq g_{\Delta}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sin^{2}\!\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{S})\right]\geq\frac{c_{S}^{\prime}\,\sigma^{2}\,(d-r)}{n\,g_{\Delta}\cdot\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})},

(32)

where
c_{S}^{\prime}>0
depends on the label structure.

Proof.

By Remark
32
, the hypotheses in the Fano construction satisfy
{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{j}={\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{S,j}=\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{W}_{j})
and
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{j})=g
implies
\Delta_{r}=g\cdot\rho/\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})
with
\rho>0
. Thus
g_{\Delta}=g\cdot\rho/\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})
, giving
g=g_{\Delta}\cdot\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})/\rho
. Substituting into (
30
) yields (
32
) with
c_{S}^{\prime}=c\cdot\rho
.
∎

Remark 34

(Rate comparison)
.

The upper bound (
29
) (squared) and the lower bound (
32
) are both
O(d/n)
and share the generalized gap in the denominator. The upper bound carries additional factors of
\kappa^{2}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})
and
\log d
; whether these can be removed is an open question that parallels the
k_{\max}
gap of Remark
31
. In the moderate-signal regime (
\kappa({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\infty})=O(1)
) with
k_{\max}=O(1)
, the bounds match up to logarithmic factors.

6.3
Concentration of Projected Distances

Theorem
18
provides bounds on the
expected
projected distance. We now show that the projected distance concentrates around its expectation with sub-exponential tails.

Theorem 35

(High-probability distance preservation)
.

Under the linear label-effect model with Gaussian noise
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
, let
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}
be a fixed full-rank matrix. For any two samples
i,j
with label vectors
\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j}
, define
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}=\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{y}_{j}
and
\mathbf{\Psi}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W}
. Then for all
t>0
:

P\!\left(\left|\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}\right]\right|>t\right)\leq 2\exp\!\left(-c\,\min\!\left(\frac{t^{2}}{V_{ij}^{2}},\;\frac{t}{B}\right)\right),

(33)

where
B=4\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}
and

V_{ij}^{2}=16\,\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}\,\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}+32\,\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}^{2},

and
c>0
is a universal constant. Equivalently, with probability at least

1-\delta
:

\left|\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}-\left(\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}+C_{w}\right)\right|\leq V_{ij}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{c}}+B\,\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{c}.

(34)

The proof (Appendix
D.4
) decomposes the centered projected distance into a sub-Gaussian linear term and a Hanson–Wright quadratic form
(
14
;
27
)
; their sum is sub-exponential. The next two corollaries tighten the bound under the Stiefel and
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality constraints.

Corollary 36

(Concentration under the Stiefel constraint)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
35
, if
\mathbf{W}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
(i.e.,
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
), then by Corollary
19
the tail parameters satisfy:

B\leq 4\,\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}),\qquad V_{ij}^{2}\leq 16\,\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})\,\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})+32\,r\,\lambda_{\max}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}).

(35)

Proof.

The Poincaré separation theorem gives
\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}\leq\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
and
\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}^{2}\leq r\,\lambda_{\max}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
. For the signal term,
\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}\leq\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}=\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})
. Substituting into the expressions for
B
and
V_{ij}^{2}
in Theorem
35
yields (
35
).
∎

Corollary 37

(Concentration under
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
35
, suppose
\mathbf{W}
satisfies
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
where
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{pop}}}
is the population multilabel total scatter. Let
\theta_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\theta_{r}\geq 0
be the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\mathbf{W}
. Then:

B\leq\frac{4\,(1-\theta_{r})}{K^{\mathrm{pop}}},\qquad V_{ij}^{2}\leq\frac{16\,\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})\,(1-\theta_{r})}{K^{\mathrm{pop}}}+\frac{32\,\sum_{i=1}^{r}(1-\theta_{i})^{2}}{(K^{\mathrm{pop}})^{2}}.

(36)

In particular,
B<4/K^{\mathrm{pop}}
and
V_{ij}^{2}<16\,\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})/K^{\mathrm{pop}}+32\,r/(K^{\mathrm{pop}})^{2}
. When the between-class signal is strong (
\theta_{r}
close to
1
), the tail parameters shrink toward zero—the
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality constraint automatically concentrates the projection away from noisy directions.

Proof.

Under
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
and the partition
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{pop}}}
, we have
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{w}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}-\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\mathbf{W}
. Since
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{pop}}}=K^{\mathrm{pop}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}
(Definition
4
),

\mathbf{\Psi}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W}=\frac{1}{K^{\mathrm{pop}}}(\mathbf{I}_{r}-\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\mathbf{W}).

The eigenvalues of
\mathbf{\Psi}
are
(1-\theta_{i})/K^{\mathrm{pop}}\in(0,1/K^{\mathrm{pop}}]
, where
\theta_{i}\in[0,1)
are the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\mathbf{W}
(following the analysis in the proof of Theorem
13
). Thus
\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}=(1-\theta_{r})/K^{\mathrm{pop}}
and
\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i}(1-\theta_{i})^{2}/(K^{\mathrm{pop}})^{2}
. Substituting into Theorem
35
and bounding
\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|^{2}
as in Corollary
36
yields (
36
).
∎

Remark 38

(Comparison of concentration bounds)
.

The three bounds form a hierarchy: the general bound leaves
\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}
free; the Stiefel bound (Corollary
36
) caps it at
\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
; the
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality bound (Corollary
37
) reduces it to
(1-\theta_{r})/K^{\mathrm{pop}}
, which can be much smaller in the high-noise regime or when the between-class signal occupies most of the projected variance.

6.4
Robustness Under Label Interactions

The linear label-effect model (Definition
2
) assumes additive, non-interacting label effects. We now show that the distance preservation framework is robust to the inclusion of pairwise label interactions.

Consider the extended model:

\mathbf{x}_{i}=\boldsymbol{\mu}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}y_{i\ell}\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\ell}+\sum_{\ell<\ell^{\prime}}y_{i\ell}\,y_{i\ell^{\prime}}\,\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i},

(37)

where
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the interaction effect of labels
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
. Let
\mathbf{B}=[\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times\binom{L}{2}}
be the matrix of interaction effects, and let
\mathbf{z}_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{\binom{L}{2}}
be the vector of pairwise label products for sample
i
.

Theorem 39

(Robustness to label interactions)
.

Under the extended model (
37
) with zero-mean noise
independent of labels:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}\right]=\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}+\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2}+C_{w}.

(38)

The interaction contribution to the expected distance relative to the linear model satisfies:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{ext}}\!\left[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{lin}}\!\left[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\,\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\,\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|\,\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{B})\,\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}\|+\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{B})\,\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}.

(39)

Moreover,
\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}\|\leq\sqrt{d_{H}(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})\cdot\min(k_{\max}-1,L-1)}
, so the interaction perturbation is controlled by the Hamming distance, the interaction strength
\|\mathbf{B}\|
, and the label cardinality.

Proof.

Under the extended model,
\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j}=\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}+\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j})+(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})
. Projecting with
\mathbf{W}
and taking expectations (the cross-term with noise
vanishes):

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}\right]=\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}+\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j})\|^{2}+C_{w}.

Writing
\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}
and
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j})
:

\|\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{q}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{s}\|^{2}=2\mathbf{s}^{\top}\mathbf{q}+\|\mathbf{q}\|^{2}.

By Cauchy–Schwarz,
|2\mathbf{s}^{\top}\mathbf{q}|\leq 2\|\mathbf{s}\|\,\|\mathbf{q}\|
, and bounding
\|\mathbf{s}\|\leq\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|
,
\|\mathbf{q}\|\leq\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{B})\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}\|
yields (
39
).

For the norm bound on
\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}
: the entry
(z_{i}-z_{j})_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=y_{i\ell}y_{i\ell^{\prime}}-y_{j\ell}y_{j\ell^{\prime}}
can be nonzero only if the label change
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}
affects at least one of
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
. Fix a changed label
\ell
(i.e.,
y_{i\ell}\neq y_{j\ell}
). The number of pairs
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
that can be affected is at most
L-1
(trivially), and also at most
k_{\max}-1
, since
y_{i\ell}y_{i\ell^{\prime}}
is nonzero only when both labels
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
are present in sample
i
, and the interaction partner
\ell^{\prime}
must differ from the changed label
\ell
, limiting the active interactions to at most
k_{i}-1\leq k_{\max}-1
per changed label. Summing over the
d_{H}
changed labels gives at most
d_{H}\cdot\min(k_{\max}-1,L-1)
nonzero entries in
\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}
(this may over-count pairs
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
where both labels changed, but since we seek an upper bound this is valid). Each entry is bounded by
1
in absolute value (
|y_{i\ell}y_{i\ell^{\prime}}-y_{j\ell}y_{j\ell^{\prime}}|\leq 1
for binary labels), so
\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}\|\leq\sqrt{d_{H}\cdot\min(k_{\max}-1,L-1)}
.
∎

Corollary 40

(Robustness under the Stiefel constraint)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
39
, if
\mathbf{W}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
, then the interaction perturbation bound (
39
) satisfies:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{lin}}\right|\leq 2\,\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A})\,\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|\,\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{B})\,\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}\|+\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{B})\,\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}.

(40)

Proof.

For
\mathbf{W}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
, the matrix
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\top}
is an orthogonal projector, so
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\leq\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A})
and
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{B})\leq\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{B})
by the interlacing inequality for singular values. Substituting into (
39
) yields (
40
).
∎

Corollary 41

(Robustness under
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
39
, suppose
\mathbf{W}
satisfies
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
. Let
\theta_{r}
be the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\mathbf{W}
. Then the interaction perturbation bound (
39
) satisfies:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{lin}}\right|\leq\frac{2\,\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A})\,\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}\|\,\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{B})\,\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}\|}{\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}})}+\frac{\sigma_{\max}^{2}(\mathbf{B})\,\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}})}.

(41)

Moreover, the
noise contribution

C_{w}
in (
38
) satisfies the tighter bound
C_{w}=2\sum_{i=1}^{r}(1-\theta_{i})/K^{\mathrm{pop}}\leq 2r(1-\theta_{r})/K^{\mathrm{pop}}
, so the noise floor is reduced relative to the Stiefel case where
C_{w}\leq 2r\,\lambda_{\max}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
.

Proof.

The
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality constraint implies
\mathbf{W}={{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}}^{-1/2}\mathbf{V}
with
\mathbf{V}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
(see the proof of Theorem
13
). By the interlacing inequality for singular values applied to
\mathbf{V}
:

\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})=\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{V}^{\top}{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}}^{-1/2}\mathbf{A})\leq\|{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}}}^{-1/2}\mathbf{A}\|_{2}\leq\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{A})/\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}})},

and similarly
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{B})\leq\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{B})/\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}})}
. Substituting into (
39
) yields (
41
). For
C_{w}
, the identity
\mathbf{\Psi}=(1/K^{\mathrm{pop}})(\mathbf{I}_{r}-\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\mathbf{W})
from Corollary
37
gives
C_{w}=2\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{\Psi})=2\sum_{i}(1-\theta_{i})/K^{\mathrm{pop}}
.
∎

Remark 42

(Role of orthogonality in robustness)
.

The cross-term and pure interaction components of bound (
39
) are both tightened under orthogonality via singular value interlacing; the
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality constraint additionally reduces the noise floor
C_{w}
. Since
\lambda_{\min}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML,pop}}})\geq K^{\mathrm{pop}}\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})
with
K^{\mathrm{pop}}>1
in the multilabel regime, the
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality bound (
41
) is at least as tight as the Stiefel bound (
40
) whenever
K^{\mathrm{pop}}\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w})\geq 1
.

6.5
Regularization

When
d\gg n
(a regime that arises in multilabel applications with high-dimensional features such as text bag-of-words, genomics, or image descriptors), the within-class scatter
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
may be singular, requiring regularization
(
5
)
. We show that Tikhonov regularization preserves the algebraic structure established in Sections
3
–
4
.

Theorem 43

(Regularization preserves structure)
.

For
\gamma>0
, define
{{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}={{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{d}
and
{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+{{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}
. Then:

(i)

\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
is unchanged by regularization.

(ii)

The partition
{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+{{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}
holds, and
{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}
is positive definite for all
\gamma>0
.

(iii)

Under the
{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}
-orthogonality constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
, all four Fisher objectives share the same optimizer (Theorem
13
applies with
{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}
replacing
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
).

(iv)

The trace-difference matrix becomes
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}=(2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})-\gamma\mathbf{I}_{d}
. The eigenvalue gap
\mathrm{gap}_{r}(2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma})=\mathrm{gap}_{r}(2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
is unchanged (the shift
-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{d}
is uniform).

(v)

The condition number of the regularized within-class scatter satisfies
\kappa({{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma})=\frac{\lambda_{\max}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})+\gamma}{\lambda_{\min}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})+\gamma}<\kappa({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
whenever
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
is not a scalar multiple of
\mathbf{I}_{d}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{rank}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\min(n-1,\,K-L)
with
K=\sum_{\ell}n_{\ell}
, so
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
is singular whenever
d>\min(n-1,\,K-L)
, forcing
\kappa({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=\infty
while
\kappa({{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma})<\infty
.

Proof.

(i)

{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
does not depend on
\gamma
: it involves only label means and the global mean.

(ii)

{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}={S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+\gamma\mathbf{I}={S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+\gamma\mathbf{I}
. Since
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\succeq 0
(Theorem
11
) and
\gamma>0
,
{{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}\succeq\gamma\mathbf{I}\succ 0
.

(iii)
The proof of Theorem
13
requires only that the “within-class” part
{{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}
be positive definite (so that
\theta_{i}\in[0,1)
strictly). Since
{{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma}\succeq\gamma\mathbf{I}\succ 0
, the proof carries through verbatim.

(iv)
The eigenvalues of
(2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})-\gamma\mathbf{I}
are exactly
\lambda_{i}(2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})-\gamma
for all
i
. Since the gap
\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}
is invariant under uniform shifts,
\mathrm{gap}_{r}
is preserved.

(v)

\kappa({{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma})=(\lambda_{\max}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})+\gamma)/(\lambda_{\min}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})+\gamma)
. Differentiating with respect to
\gamma
:
\frac{d}{d\gamma}\kappa({{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma})=(\lambda_{\min}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})-\lambda_{\max}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}))/(\lambda_{\min}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})+\gamma)^{2}<0
whenever
\lambda_{\min}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})<\lambda_{\max}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
. Thus
\kappa({{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}}^{\gamma})
is strictly decreasing in
\gamma
. For the rank bound: since
\sum_{i:\,y_{i\ell}=1}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell})=\mathbf{0}
, each per-label scatter has rank
\leq n_{\ell}-1
; subadditivity gives
\mathrm{rank}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\sum_{\ell}(n_{\ell}-1)=K-L
. Separately, all residuals
\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}
lie in the
n
-dimensional affine span of the samples, so
\mathrm{rank}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq n-1
. Combining yields
\mathrm{rank}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq\min(n-1,\,K-L)
, the multilabel analogue of the classical
\mathrm{rank}({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})\leq n-C
bound
(
12
)
.
∎

7
Discussion

7.1
Connections to Existing Methods

Our theoretical framework unifies several existing approaches:

OLDA and ULDA
(
42
)
.

The OLDA constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}
and ULDA constraint
\mathbf{W}^{\top}S_{t}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}
are special cases of our Stiefel and
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality constraints when
k_{i}=1
for all
i
(the single-label case). Theorem
13
generalizes the equivalence result of
21
to multilabel scatter matrices.

Multilabel LDA
(
36
;
40
)
.

The scatter definitions in (
2
)–(
3
) follow
36
; the weighted variants of
40
can be incorporated by replacing
n_{\ell}
with weighted counts in
\mathbf{D}_{n}
.

kaLDA
(
46
)
.

The kernel alignment approach
\max_{\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W})
connects to our framework via Lemma
6
: since
\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{1/2}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
, kaLDA implicitly optimizes a weighted version of
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W})
.

Trace ratio solvers
(
23
;
37
)
.

Our formulation (
1
) is a direct instance of the trace ratio problem on the Stiefel manifold, so the convergence guarantees from
23
apply directly.

7.2
Practical Implications

Choosing the projection dimension.

Theorem
7
guides dimensionality selection: the effective discriminant dimensionality is
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})=\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y})
, so
r
can be selected from the singular-value spectrum of
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
analogously to choosing the number of principal components in PCA.

Choosing between constraints.

Theorem
13
shows that
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality yields unconditional equivalence of the four Fisher objectives, whereas the Stiefel constraint requires an ordering-consistency condition on
S_{t}
(Theorem
14
(ii)) that is difficult to verify a priori. We therefore recommend
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality as the default multilabel constraint; the Stiefel constraint is preferable when explicit control of projected noise (Corollary
19
) matters for distance-based downstream tasks.

Diagnosing objective divergence.

When using the Stiefel constraint, compute
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}
with
\mathbf{R}={S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-S_{t}
: if small relative to the spectral gap
\gamma
, the choice of objective is immaterial; otherwise, the trace ratio (
4
) is the recommended generalization.

Regularization in high dimensions.

Theorem
43
shows that
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}+\gamma\mathbf{I}
preserves
\mathrm{rank}({S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
and the trace-difference gap while improving
\kappa({S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}})
, giving a principled approach for the
d\gg n
regime.

Sample complexity.

Theorem
24
gives a quantitative guide: reliable subspace
estimation requires
n=\Omega(k_{\max}^{2}\,d\,\log d/(\mathrm{gap}_{r}^{2}\,\epsilon^{2}))
for estimation error
\epsilon
, so multilabel problems with large
k_{\max}
need correspondingly more samples than their single-label counterparts.

Computational cost.

All estimators reduce to standard eigendecompositions, so the computational footprint matches classical single-label LDA. Forming
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
costs
O(nd^{2})
and
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
costs
O(Kd+Ld^{2})
with
K=\sum_{i}k_{i}\leq nk_{\max}
; the top-
r
eigenspace of
2{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}-{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
(trace-difference) or the generalized eigenvalue problem
{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{w}=\theta{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{w}
(
{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}
-orthogonality) costs
O(d^{3})
with a dense eigensolver or
O(d^{2}r)
with iterative methods. The factorization
\mathbf{M}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{D}_{n}^{-1/2}
(Lemma
6
) enables efficient
O(dL)
matrix–vector products in the
d\gg n
regime.

7.3
Limitations and Open Questions

Section
6
resolves previously open questions on consistency, regularization, and label interactions, but raises new ones. The
k_{\max}
factor that appears in the upper bound but not in the minimax lower bound (Remark
31
) leaves open whether multilabel estimation is fundamentally harder or whether the Davis–Kahan step admits a sharper analysis. Extending our theorems to the weighted scatter variants of
40
, to heavy-tailed noise via median-of-means or Catoni-type scatter estimators, and to adaptive rank selection with rate guarantees are natural directions for future work. Empirical benchmarking against kaLDA, MDDM, wMLDA, and modern neural embeddings on standard multilabel datasets is deferred to future work aimed at application-oriented venues.

9
Conclusion

We have provided a unified theoretical analysis of orthogonality-constrained multilabel Linear Discriminant Analysis, establishing both algebraic foundations and statistical guarantees for the formulation (
1
).

A central insight is that the multilabel setting introduces a
cardinality reweighting
of the total scatter (Theorem
11
), which preserves the algebraic structure needed for objective equivalence under the
\mathbf{W}^{\top}{S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
constraint but introduces quantifiable divergence under the geometric Stiefel constraint. On the statistical side, the maximum label cardinality
k_{\max}
acts as a complexity parameter: it appears as a squared factor in the finite-sample bound but not in the minimax lower bound, leaving open whether this gap reflects a fundamental cost of multilabel estimation or an artifact of the current analysis.

All results are numerically verified on synthetic data from the linear label-effect model (Section
8
); validation on real multilabel benchmarks is deferred to future work aimed at application-oriented venues.

Appendix A
Proof Details for the Rank Theorem

We provide additional details for the proof of Theorem
7
.

A.1
Centering and Rank Reduction

The centering matrix
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{I}_{n}-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}
is an orthogonal projector onto
\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}\mathbf{v}=0\}
with
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H})=n-1
. For any matrix
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
:

\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Z})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Z})-\dim(\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Z})\cap\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1}_{n})).

(42)

This follows because
\mathbf{H}
annihilates exactly the component of
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Z})
in the direction of
\mathbf{1}_{n}
, and preserves the orthogonal complement.

In the single-label case,
\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{1}_{L}=\mathbf{1}_{n}
, so
\mathbf{1}_{n}\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
, giving
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})-1=C-1
.

In the multilabel case,
\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{1}_{L}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})^{\top}=\mathbf{k}
. We have
\mathbf{1}_{n}\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})
if and only if there exists
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
such that
\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{1}_{n}
, i.e.,
\sum_{\ell}c_{\ell}y_{i\ell}=1
for all
i
. A sufficient condition is that some label
\ell^{*}
satisfies
y_{i\ell^{*}}=1
for all
i
(a “universal” label), in which case
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{e}_{\ell^{*}}
works.

A.2
The General Position Condition

The condition in Theorem
7
(iv) that
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
is in “general position” relative to
\mathrm{col}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})
means:

\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})\cap\ker(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top})=\{\mathbf{0}\}.

Since
\ker(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top})=\mathrm{col}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})^{\perp}
has dimension
n-\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})
, this condition holds generically when
rank(\tilde{\mathbf{X}})\geq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y})
, which is the “sufficient features” condition. For continuous data distributions, this condition holds almost surely.

Appendix B
Schur-Convexity Argument for Theorem
13

We verify the Schur-convexity claims used in the proof of Theorem
13
. Recall that a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
is
Schur-convex
if
\mathbf{x}\prec\mathbf{y}
(majorization) implies
f(\mathbf{x})\leq f(\mathbf{y})
.

Lemma 44

(Schur-convexity of
J_{\mathrm{RT}}
)
.

The function
f_{1}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\theta_{i}/(1-\theta_{i})
is Schur-convex on
[0,1)^{r}
.

Proof.

By the Schur–Ostrowski criterion
(
22
)
, a symmetric function
f:\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
is Schur-convex if and only if
(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})\!\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{i}}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{j}}\right)\geq 0
for all
i,j
. For
f_{1}
,
\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial\theta_{i}}=1/(1-\theta_{i})^{2}
, which is increasing in
\theta_{i}
on
[0,1)
. Thus the Schur–Ostrowski condition holds.
∎

Lemma 45

(Determinant ratio maximization)
.

Let
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be symmetric with eigenvalues
1>\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}\geq 0
and let
\mathbf{V}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
. Then:

\frac{|\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{V}|}{|\mathbf{V}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}_{d}-\mathbf{P})\mathbf{V}|}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}},

with equality when
\mathbf{V}
spans the top
r
eigenvectors of
\mathbf{P}
.

Proof.

By the Poincaré separation theorem (see, e.g.,
15
), the eigenvalues
\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{r}
of
\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{V}
satisfy
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
, with equality when
\mathbf{V}
spans the top
r
eigenvectors of
\mathbf{P}
. Since
\mathbf{P}
has eigenvalues in
[0,1)
and
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}<1
, the function
t\mapsto t/(1-t)
is increasing on
[0,1)
, so
\mu_{i}/(1-\mu_{i})\leq\lambda_{i}/(1-\lambda_{i})
for each
i
. Taking the product:

\frac{|\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{V}|}{|\mathbf{V}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}_{d}-\mathbf{P})\mathbf{V}|}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\mu_{i}}{1-\mu_{i}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}},

where we used
|\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{V}|=\prod_{i}\mu_{i}
and
|\mathbf{V}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}_{d}-\mathbf{P})\mathbf{V}|=\prod_{i}(1-\mu_{i})
, the latter following from
\mathbf{V}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}_{d}-\mathbf{P})\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{I}_{r}-\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{V}
(using
\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
since
\mathbf{V}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)

. Equality holds when

for all

spans the top

\mathbf{P}

∎

These two lemmas, combined with the Ky Fan inequality for

and

, establish that the optimal
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{r})
simultaneously maximizing all four objectives is the vector of
r
largest eigenvalues of
({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})^{-1/2}{S_{b}^{\mathrm{ML}}}({S_{t}^{\mathrm{ML}}})^{-1/2}
.

C.1
Singular Value Bounds for the Projected Label-Effect Matrix

For
\mathbf{W}\in\mathrm{St}(d,r)
and

, the singular values of

\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{A})\geq\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A})\geq\sigma_{i+d-r}(\mathbf{A}),\quad i=1,\ldots,\min(r,L).

For the inverse diagonal: provided

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{y}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Pi}}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{y}}-\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{y}}

(46)

Step 3: Bounding

Under the linear model, write

Oracle decomposition.

The sample means
\bar{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{\ell}
create statistical dependence
between the per-sample summands

\mathbf{Z}_{i}=\sum_{\ell:y_{i\ell}=1}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell})(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell})^{\top}

We resolve this via an oracle decomposition. Define the

\mathbf{Y}

\ell

Expectations.

is the total label count. Also

We now bound

is the empirical within-class label scatter, with

the sample covariance of labels within class
\ell

\mathbf{B}_{\pi}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{y}}
is a product of pairwise label quantities, but

\pi_{jk\ell}
. Hence the telescoping argument used for
\mathbf{B}_{\pi}
does not directly apply; instead we bound the perturbation entry by entry.

Decompose:

\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{n}-\mathbf{W}_{\pi}=\sum_{\ell}\bigl[(n_{\ell}/n-\pi_{\ell})\,\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell}+\pi_{\ell}\,(\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell}-\mathrm{Cov}_{\ell})\bigr]
.
For the first sum,
\|\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell}\|_{2}\leq k_{\max}
(since each
\mathbf{y}_{i}\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\top}
has spectral norm
\|\mathbf{y}_{i}\|^{2}=k_{i}\leq k_{\max}
), so
\bigl\|\sum_{\ell}(n_{\ell}/n-\pi_{\ell})\,\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell}\bigr\|_{2}\leq L\,k_{\max}\,\rho_{n}
.
For the second sum, the entries of
\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell}-\mathrm{Cov}_{\ell}
are:

(\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell})_{jk}-(\mathrm{Cov}_{\ell})_{jk}=\Bigl[\frac{n_{jk\ell}}{n_{\ell}}-\frac{\pi_{jk\ell}}{\pi_{\ell}}\Bigr]-\Bigl[\frac{n_{j\ell}}{n_{\ell}}\,\frac{n_{k\ell}}{n_{\ell}}-\frac{\pi_{j\ell}}{\pi_{\ell}}\,\frac{\pi_{k\ell}}{\pi_{\ell}}\Bigr].

For the first bracket, writing
n_{jk\ell}/n_{\ell}=(n_{jk\ell}/n)/(n_{\ell}/n)
and using
\pi_{jk\ell}\leq\pi_{\ell}
:

\Bigl|\frac{n_{jk\ell}}{n_{\ell}}-\frac{\pi_{jk\ell}}{\pi_{\ell}}\Bigr|=\frac{|\pi_{\ell}(n_{jk\ell}/n-\pi_{jk\ell})+\pi_{jk\ell}(\pi_{\ell}-n_{\ell}/n)|}{(n_{\ell}/n)\,\pi_{\ell}}\leq\frac{2\pi_{\ell}\,\rho_{n}}{(\pi_{\ell}-\rho_{n})\,\pi_{\ell}}\leq\frac{4\rho_{n}}{\pi_{\min}},

where the triple condition in (
45
) supplies
|n_{jk\ell}/n-\pi_{jk\ell}|\leq\rho_{n}
(when
j,k,\ell
are distinct; coinciding indices reduce to the pairwise or marginal conditions). The same ratio bound gives
|n_{j\ell}/n_{\ell}-\pi_{j\ell}/\pi_{\ell}|\leq 4\rho_{n}/\pi_{\min}
using only the pairwise condition. For the second bracket, since all conditional frequencies
n_{j\ell}/n_{\ell}\leq 1
and
\pi_{j\ell}/\pi_{\ell}\leq 1
:

\Bigl|\frac{n_{j\ell}}{n_{\ell}}\frac{n_{k\ell}}{n_{\ell}}-\frac{\pi_{j\ell}}{\pi_{\ell}}\frac{\pi_{k\ell}}{\pi_{\ell}}\Bigr|\leq\frac{8\rho_{n}}{\pi_{\min}}.

Combining:

|(\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell})_{jk}-(\mathrm{Cov}_{\ell})_{jk}|\leq 12\rho_{n}/\pi_{\min}
, so

\|\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell}-\mathrm{Cov}_{\ell}\|_{2}\leq\|\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell}-\mathrm{Cov}_{\ell}\|_{F}\leq 12L\rho_{n}/\pi_{\min}

and

\bigl\|\sum_{\ell}\pi_{\ell}\,(\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\ell}-\mathrm{Cov}_{\ell})\bigr\|_{2}\leq 12L\,K^{\mathrm{pop}}\rho_{n}/\pi_{\min}

where
K^{\mathrm{pop}}=\sum_{\ell}\pi_{\ell}
.
Altogether:

\|\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{n}-\mathbf{W}_{\pi}\|_{2}\leq\bigl(Lk_{\max}+12LK^{\mathrm{pop}}/\pi_{\min}\bigr)\,\rho_{n}=:\eta_{W}(\rho_{n}),

(49)

so
\|{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\mathrm{sig}}/n-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}_{\pi}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}^{2}\,\eta_{W}(\rho_{n})
. Hence
\|\mathbb{E}[{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}]/n-{S_{w,c}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}^{2}\,\eta_{W}(\rho_{n})+L\sigma^{2}\rho_{n}+L\sigma^{2}/n
, where
|K/n-K^{\mathrm{pop}}|\leq L\rho_{n}
provides the
L\sigma^{2}\rho_{n}
term and the
L\sigma^{2}/n
term arises from the correction summand.

Concentration of
{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\mathrm{ora}}
via
truncation.

Since the
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}
are
\sigma
-sub-Gaussian, the oracle summands
\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}]
have sub-exponential operator norms. The classical matrix Bernstein inequality requires almost-sure boundedness, which we achieve via truncation.

Define the high-probability event
\mathcal{E}=\bigl\{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\|\leq\tau\bigr\}
with truncation radius
\tau=C_{\tau}\,\sigma\sqrt{d+\log(n/\delta)}
. Standard sub-Gaussian norm bounds
(
35
, Theorem 3.1.1)
give
\Pr(\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\|>\tau)\leq 2\exp\bigl(-(\tau^{2}/(C^{\prime}\sigma^{2})-d)\bigr)
; choosing
C_{\tau}
large enough and applying a union bound over
i=1,\ldots,n
yields
\Pr(\mathcal{E}^{c})\leq\delta/4
. On
\mathcal{E}
, each oracle summand is deterministically bounded:

\|\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}]\|_{2}\leq 2\,k_{i}\bigl(\tau+\max_{\ell}\|\mathbf{d}_{i\ell}\|\bigr)^{2}=O\!\bigl(k_{i}\,\sigma^{2}(d+\log(n/\delta))\bigr),

since
\|\mathbf{d}_{i\ell}\|\leq 2\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}k_{\max}=O(\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2})
under the linear model. Taking the maximum over
i
gives
B=O(k_{\max}\,\sigma^{2}\,(d+\log(n/\delta)))
.

Conditioned on
\mathcal{E}
, the oracle summands
\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}

remain independent
: since
\mathcal{E}=\bigcap_{i}\{\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\|\leq\tau\}
is a product event and each
\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}
depends only on
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}
, the conditional distribution factors as a product of the truncated marginals
\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\mid\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\|\leq\tau)
. The conditional variance parameter satisfies
v=\bigl\|\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}])^{2}\mid\mathcal{E}]\bigr\|_{2}=O(K\sigma^{4})
(the conditioning inflates variance by at most
(1-\delta/4)^{-1}
, which is
O(1)
).

We apply the matrix Bernstein inequality
(
32
, Theorem 6.1)
to the bounded, independent oracle summands
\{\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{ora}}]\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
\mathcal{E}
, then combine with
\Pr(\mathcal{E}^{c})\leq\delta/4
via a union bound:

\bigl\|{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\mathrm{ora}}/n-\mathbb{E}[{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\mathrm{ora}}]/n\bigr\|_{2}\leq c_{w}^{\prime}\,\sigma^{2}\,k_{\max}\,\sqrt{d\,\log(d/\delta)/n}

(50)

with probability
\geq 1-3\delta/8
.

Bounding the correction term.

Each
\bar{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{\ell}
is a mean of
n_{\ell}
independent
\sigma
-sub-Gaussian vectors, so
\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{\ell}\|^{2}\leq C\sigma^{2}(d+\log(L/\delta))/n_{\ell}
with probability
\geq 1-\delta/(4L)

(
35
, Theorem 3.1.1)
. By a union bound over
\ell=1,\ldots,L
:

\biggl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}n_{\ell}\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{\ell}\bar{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{\ell}^{\top}\biggr\|_{2}\leq\frac{\sum_{\ell}n_{\ell}}{n}\,\max_{\ell}\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{\ell}\|^{2}\leq\frac{C\,k_{\max}\,\sigma^{2}(d+\log(L/\delta))}{n_{\min}}

(51)

with probability
\geq 1-\delta/4
, where
n_{\min}=\min_{\ell}n_{\ell}
and
\sum_{\ell}n_{\ell}/n\leq k_{\max}
. Under assumption (A3), this is
O(k_{\max}\,\sigma^{2}d/n_{\min})
, lower-order relative to (
50
).

Combining.

By (
48
) and the triangle inequality:

\bigl\|{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}/n-{S_{w,c}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\bigr\|_{2}\leq\underbrace{\bigl\|{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\mathrm{ora}}/n-\mathbb{E}[{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\mathrm{ora}}]/n\bigr\|_{2}}_{\text{\eqref{eq:Swora-conc}}}\\
+\underbrace{\biggl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell}n_{\ell}\bar{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{\ell}\bar{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{\ell}^{\top}\biggr\|_{2}}_{\text{\eqref{eq:correction-bound}}}+\underbrace{\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\mathrm{ora}}]/n-{S_{w,c}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\bigr\|_{2}}_{\text{label-freq.\ bias}}.

The label-frequency bias was bounded in the Expectations paragraph above:

\|\mathbb{E}[{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}^{\mathrm{ora}}]/n-{S_{w,c}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}^{2}\,\eta_{W}(\rho_{n})+L\sigma^{2}\rho_{n}+L\sigma^{2}/n

under condition (
45
), using the conditional covariance perturbation bound (
49
). A union bound gives:
\|{S_{w}^{\mathrm{ML}}}/n-{S_{w,c}^{\mathrm{pop}}}\|_{2}\leq c_{w}\,\sigma^{2}\,k_{\max}\,\sqrt{d\,\log(d/\delta)/n}
with probability
\geq 1-\delta/2
(correction and bias terms are lower-order). The
k_{\max}
factor arises from the per-sample operator-norm bound: each sample contributes to
k_{i}\leq k_{\max}
label-conditional scatters.

Step 4: Davis–Kahan application.

Combining Steps 2–3 by a union bound, and using the decomposition from Step 1:

\bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n-{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}\bigr\|_{2}\leq\underbrace{c_{b}\,\sigma\,\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}\,\sqrt{(d+\log(1/\delta))/n}}_{\text{Step~2 noise}}\\
+\;\underbrace{\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}^{2}\,[\eta_{B}(\rho_{n})+\eta_{W}(\rho_{n})]+L\sigma^{2}\,\rho_{n}}_{\text{bias~\eqref{eq:bias-bound},\eqref{eq:within-bias}}}\;+\;\underbrace{c_{w}\,\sigma^{2}\,k_{\max}\,\sqrt{d\,\log(d/\delta)/n}}_{\text{Step~3}}

with probability
\geq 1-\delta
. Since
\sqrt{d+\log(1/\delta)}\leq\sqrt{d\,\log(d/\delta)}
for
d\geq 3
and
\delta\leq 1
, and the combined bias terms are
\Theta((\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}^{2}+\sigma^{2})\rho_{n})
(lower-order under assumption (A3) for the typical rate
\rho_{n}=O(1/\sqrt{n})
), both stochastic terms share the
\sqrt{d\,\log(d/\delta)/n}
rate:

\bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n-{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}\bigr\|_{2}\leq(c_{b}\,\sigma\,\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+c_{w}\,\sigma^{2}\,k_{\max})\,\sqrt{d\,\log(d/\delta)/n}.

By the Davis–Kahan
\sin\boldsymbol{\Theta}
theorem
(
7
;
43
)
, applied to
\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n
and
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}
(whose top-
r
eigenspace is
{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{c}
; see Step 1), provided

(ensured by assumption (A3)):

\sin\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{n},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{c})\leq\frac{2\|\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n-{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}\|_{2}}{\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})}\leq\frac{(C_{1}\,\sigma\,\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}+C_{2}\,\sigma^{2}\,k_{\max})\,\sqrt{d\,\log(d/\delta)/n}}{\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})},

where

are the universal constants in (

and

as noted in Remark

)
, there exist
M\geq\exp(c_{1}\,r(d-r)/\varepsilon^{2})
subspaces
\mathcal{W}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{W}_{M}\in\mathrm{Gr}(d,r)
such that
\sin\angle(\mathcal{W}_{j},\mathcal{W}_{k})\geq\varepsilon
for all
j\neq k
.

For each
\mathcal{W}_{j}
, construct the parameter
\mathbf{A}_{j}
so that the top-
r
eigenspace of
{{\mathbf{M}^{*}}}_{j}={{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}}_{j}-{S_{w}^{\mathrm{pop}}}
equals
\mathcal{W}_{j}
and
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{j})=g
. Concretely, choose
\mathbf{A}_{j}=\mathbf{W}_{j}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{1/2}\mathbf{Q}
where
\mathbf{W}_{j}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{W}_{j}
,
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times L}
is a fixed partial isometry (
\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
; such a
\mathbf{Q}
exists when
L\geq r
), and
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}=g\,(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})^{-1}
so that
{{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}}_{j}=\mathbf{A}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\top}=g\,\mathbf{W}_{j}\mathbf{W}_{j}^{\top}
. Then
{{\mathbf{M}^{*}}}_{j}={{S_{b}^{\mathrm{pop}}}}_{j}-K^{\mathrm{pop}}\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}=g\,\mathbf{W}_{j}\mathbf{W}_{j}^{\top}-K^{\mathrm{pop}}\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}
, whose top
r
eigenvalues equal
g-K^{\mathrm{pop}}\sigma^{2}
and the remaining
d-r
eigenvalues are
-K^{\mathrm{pop}}\sigma^{2}
, giving
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{j})=g
.

Relationship to the centered reference
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}
.

In the multilabel setting, the sample discriminant
\hat{\mathbf{M}}/n
converges to
{{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}}_{j}=\mathbf{A}_{j}\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\top}-K^{\mathrm{pop}}\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}
(Remark
5
), not to
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{j}
. For the construction above,
\mathbf{A}_{j}\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\top}=\mathbf{W}_{j}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{1/2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{1/2}\mathbf{W}_{j}^{\top}
, which has column space
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{W}_{j})
whenever
\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}
is positive definite. Since
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}=\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}
, both
{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{j}
and
{{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}}_{j}
share the same top-
r
eigenspace
\mathcal{W}_{j}=\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{W}_{j})
. The partial isometry
\mathbf{Q}
can be chosen so that
\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{Q}^{\top})
lies in the positive-eigenvalue subspace of
\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}
(which has dimension at least
r
whenever
\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}
has
r
positive eigenvalues—a necessary condition for the method to have discriminative power in
r
directions). Under this choice,
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({{\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c}}_{j})=g\cdot\rho
where
\rho=\lambda_{\min}\bigl((\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})^{-1/2}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})^{-1/2}\bigr)>0
is a label-dependent constant (see Remark
32
). Thus the lower bound holds equally with
\mathrm{gap}_{r}({\mathbf{M}^{*}}_{c})
at the cost of replacing
c
by
c/\rho
.

Step 2: KL divergence computation.

Under the linear model with
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}=\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{d}
, the data
\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n}
are jointly Gaussian with distribution depending on
\mathbf{A}
. The KL divergence between hypotheses
j
and
k
satisfies:

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j}\|P_{k})=\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}\|(\mathbf{A}_{j}-\mathbf{A}_{k})\mathbf{y}_{i}\|^{2}\leq\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}\|\mathbf{A}_{j}-\mathbf{A}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\cdot\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2},

where
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}=\mathbf{Y}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}
is the label co-occurrence matrix (in the single-label case,
\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2}=\max_{\ell}(n_{\ell}/n)
). The bound uses

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\|(\mathbf{A}_{j}-\mathbf{A}_{k})\mathbf{y}_{i}\|^{2}=\mathrm{tr}\!\bigl((\mathbf{A}_{j}-\mathbf{A}_{k})^{\top}(\mathbf{A}_{j}-\mathbf{A}_{k})\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\bigr)\leq\|\mathbf{A}_{j}-\mathbf{A}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\,\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2}
.
By construction,
\mathbf{A}_{j}-\mathbf{A}_{k}=(\mathbf{W}_{j}-\mathbf{W}_{k})\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{1/2}\mathbf{Q}
. For the Frobenius norm, since
\mathbf{Q}
is a partial isometry (
\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{2}=1
):

\|\mathbf{A}_{j}-\mathbf{A}_{k}\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{W}_{j}-\mathbf{W}_{k}\|_{F}\cdot\|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{1/2}\|_{2}\cdot\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{2}=\|\mathbf{W}_{j}-\mathbf{W}_{k}\|_{F}\cdot\|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{1/2}\|_{2}.

The standard identity

\|\mathbf{W}_{j}-\mathbf{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=2\|\sin\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\mathcal{W}_{j},\mathcal{W}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\leq 2r\,\sin^{2}\!\angle(\mathcal{W}_{j},\mathcal{W}_{k})

relates the Frobenius norm to the subspace angle. With the calibration above,
\|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{1/2}\|_{2}=\sqrt{g/\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})}
and
\|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\|_{2}=g/\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})
. Since
\mathbf{Q}
is a partial isometry (
\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}=\mathbf{I}_{r}
) and
\mathbf{D}_{\pi}=\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{L})
with
\pi_{\ell}>0
, Cauchy interlacing gives
\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})\geq\min_{\ell}\pi_{\ell}>0
. Denoting the label-dependent constant

c_{\Gamma}=\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2}/\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})\leq\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2}/\min_{\ell}\pi_{\ell},

we obtain:

\|\mathbf{A}_{j}-\mathbf{A}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\leq 2r\,\|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\|_{2}\cdot\sin^{2}\!\angle(\mathcal{W}_{j},\mathcal{W}_{k}).

Combining:

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j}\|P_{k})\leq r\,c_{\Gamma}\,\frac{n\,g}{\sigma^{2}}\cdot\sin^{2}\!\angle(\mathcal{W}_{j},\mathcal{W}_{k}),

where the factor
r
arises from
\|\mathbf{W}_{j}-\mathbf{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\leq 2r\sin^{2}\!\angle
and
c_{\Gamma}=\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2}/\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})
collects the label-dependent constants. Let
\varepsilon_{0}=\max_{j,k}\sin\angle(\mathcal{W}_{j},\mathcal{W}_{k})
;
using a
2\varepsilon
-separated packing with
\varepsilon_{0}\leq 4\varepsilon
, the pairwise bound becomes
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j}\|P_{k})\leq 16\,r\,c_{\Gamma}\,ng\,\varepsilon^{2}/\sigma^{2}
.

Step 3: Fano’s inequality.

By the generalized Fano inequality
(
34
)
, for any estimator
\hat{\mathbf{W}}
:

\sup_{j}\mathbb{E}_{j}\!\left[\sin^{2}\!\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{j})\right]\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\left(1-\frac{\bar{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}+\log 2}{\log M}\right),

where
\bar{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}=\frac{1}{M(M-1)}\sum_{j\neq k}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j}\|P_{k})
.

Substituting the bounds:
\log M\geq c_{1}\,r(d-r)/\varepsilon^{2}
and
\bar{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\leq 16\,r\,c_{\Gamma}\,ng\varepsilon^{2}/\sigma^{2}
. The Fano condition
\bar{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\log M
becomes

16\,r\,c_{\Gamma}\,\frac{ng\,\varepsilon^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\leq\frac{c_{1}\,r(d-r)}{4\varepsilon^{2}}.

The factor
r
cancels, leaving
64\,c_{\Gamma}\,ng\,\varepsilon^{4}/\sigma^{2}\leq c_{1}(d-r)
. The maximal resolution satisfying this condition is
\varepsilon^{4}\leq c_{1}\sigma^{2}(d-r)/(64\,c_{\Gamma}\,ng)
. We choose
\varepsilon^{2}=c_{4}\,\sigma^{2}(d-r)/(ng)
with
c_{4}=\sqrt{c_{1}/(64\,c_{\Gamma})}
. To verify the Fano condition:
\varepsilon^{4}=c_{4}^{2}\,\sigma^{4}(d-r)^{2}/(ng)^{2}
, and
64\,c_{\Gamma}\,ng\,\varepsilon^{4}/\sigma^{2}=64\,c_{\Gamma}\,c_{4}^{2}\,\sigma^{2}(d-r)^{2}/(ng)=c_{1}\,\sigma^{2}(d-r)^{2}/(ng)\leq c_{1}(d-r)
, where the last step uses
\sigma^{2}(d-r)/(ng)\leq 1
, which holds in the regime of interest (otherwise
\sin^{2}\!\angle=\Omega(1)
trivially). This also ensures
\varepsilon\leq 1
(a valid packing resolution), holding when
n\geq c_{4}\,\sigma^{2}(d-r)/g
. Thus:

\sup_{j}\mathbb{E}_{j}\!\left[\sin^{2}\!\angle(\hat{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}^{*}}_{j})\right]\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2}=\frac{c\,\sigma^{2}\,(d-r)}{n\,g},

where
c=c_{4}/8
depends on the label structure only through
c_{\Gamma}=\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2}/\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})
. Since
\mathbf{Q}
is a partial isometry, Cauchy interlacing gives
\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}_{\pi}\mathbf{Q}^{\top})\geq\min_{\ell}\pi_{\ell}>0
, so
c_{\Gamma}\leq\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2}/\min_{\ell}\pi_{\ell}
is finite. Thus
c=c(\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2},\,\min_{\ell}\pi_{\ell})
is a positive constant that depends on the fixed label matrix
\mathbf{Y}
(through
\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/n\|_{2}
) and the population label probabilities (through
\min_{\ell}\pi_{\ell}
), but not on
d
,
n
,
\sigma
,
r
, or
g
.
∎

D.4
Proof of Theorem
35
(Distance Concentration)

Step 1: Decomposition into linear and quadratic parts.

From (
21
),

\|\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{g}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{s}\|^{2}+2\mathbf{s}^{\top}\mathbf{g}+\|\mathbf{g}\|^{2},

where
\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}
is the deterministic signal and
\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{j})\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},2\mathbf{\Psi})
with
\mathbf{\Psi}=\mathbf{W}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{W}
.

The expectation is
\|\mathbf{s}\|^{2}+2\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{\Psi})=\|\mathbf{s}\|^{2}+C_{w}
. The centered quantity is:

Z=2\mathbf{s}^{\top}\mathbf{g}+(\|\mathbf{g}\|^{2}-2\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{\Psi})).

Step 2: Bound the linear part.

The linear term
L=2\mathbf{s}^{\top}\mathbf{g}
is Gaussian with
\mathrm{Var}(L)=8\,\mathbf{s}^{\top}\mathbf{\Psi}\,\mathbf{s}\leq 8\|\mathbf{s}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}
, so
L
is
(2\sqrt{2\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}}\,\|\mathbf{s}\|)
-sub-Gaussian.

Step 3: Bound the quadratic part.

Write
\mathbf{g}=\sqrt{2}\mathbf{\Psi}^{1/2}\mathbf{h}
where
\mathbf{h}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{r})
. Then
\|\mathbf{g}\|^{2}=2\mathbf{h}^{\top}\mathbf{\Psi}\mathbf{h}
, and the centered quadratic form is
Q=2\mathbf{h}^{\top}\mathbf{\Psi}\mathbf{h}-2\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{\Psi})
.

By the Hanson–Wright inequality
(
27
)
, for
\mathbf{h}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{r})
and the symmetric matrix
2\mathbf{\Psi}
:

P(|Q|>t)\leq 2\exp\!\left(-c_{\mathrm{HW}}\,\min\!\left(\frac{t^{2}}{4\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}^{2}},\;\frac{t}{2\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}}\right)\right),

where
c_{\mathrm{HW}}>0
is the universal constant from the Hanson–Wright inequality, and we used
\|2\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}=2\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}
,
\|2\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}=2\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}
.

Step 4: Combine.

The sum
Z=L+Q
of a sub-Gaussian and a sub-exponential random variable is sub-exponential. Using
P(|Z|>t)\leq P(|L|>t/2)+P(|Q|>t/2)

(
35
)
: the sub-Gaussian tail of
L
gives
P(|L|>t/2)\leq 2\exp(-t^{2}/(64\|\mathbf{s}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}))
, and the Hanson–Wright bound on
Q
gives
P(|Q|>t/2)\leq 2\exp(-c_{\mathrm{HW}}\min(t^{2}/(16\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}^{2}),t/(4\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2})))
. The
t\to t/2
splitting introduces additional constant factors in the denominators (
4\times
in the
\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}^{2}
term and
2\times
in the
\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}
term relative to the direct Hanson–Wright bound). Combining both tails into a single bound of the form
2\exp(-c^{\prime}\min(t^{2}/V_{ij}^{2},\;t/B))
(where
c^{\prime}
is a universal constant absorbing all numerical factors from the union bound and splitting) yields the stated bound (
33
) with
V_{ij}^{2}=16\|\mathbf{s}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}+32\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}^{2}
and
B=4\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}
. (Tighter constants
V_{ij}^{2}=8\|\mathbf{s}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}+4\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{F}^{2}
and
B=2\|\mathbf{\Psi}\|_{2}
are achievable by tracking the Hanson–Wright constant
c_{\mathrm{HW}}
explicitly rather than absorbing it into
c
.)
∎
