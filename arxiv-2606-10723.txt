Simplicity of q q -Gaussian C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras
Tattwamasi Amrutam, David Jekel, Mateusz Wasilewski

Abstract
. We show that q q -Gaussian C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras for q ∈ ( − 1 , 1 ) q\in(-1,1) have the Dixmier averaging property, and hence are simple with a unique trace. We argue by combining rapid decay and spectral gap estimates for the commutators with the generators, which are obtained from free probability.



1.
Introduction

The
q
-commutation relations
a_{i}a_{j}^{\ast}-qa_{j}^{\ast}a_{i}=\delta_{ij}\mathds{1}
for
q\in[-1,1]
, first considered in
[
11
]
, are a natural generalization of the relations satisfied by bosonic (
q=1
) and fermionic (
q=-1
) creation and annihilation operators. Bożejko and Speicher
[
5
]
constructed operators on a Hilbert space satisfying these relations, and introduced the
q
-Gaussian algebras generated by the operators
a_{i}+a_{i}^{\ast}
. Since there is a natural trace on the
q
-Gaussian algebra, it can be completed in two different ways: to a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and to a tracial von Neumann algebra. These
q
-Gaussian operator algebras were first seriously investigated in
[
4
]
, where the authors showed simplicity of the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra and factoriality of the von Neumann algebra in the case of infinitely many generators. However, most of the recent results on
q
-Gaussians have been about the von Neumann algebras, including important structural properties such as factoriality
[
28
]
, non-injectivity
[
25
]
, complete metric approximation property and strong solidity
[
2
]
, and maximal amenability of the generator MASA for
|q|<1/9

[
27
]
. Several of these results have also been generalized to the setting of
q
-Araki Woods factors, a natural non-tracial analog of
q
-Gaussian algebras
[
15
,
31
,
20
]
.

Many of these results were first established for
q=0
, in which the
q
-Gaussian operators are a free semicircular family, and hence the von Neumann algebra is isomorphic to a free group factor. For this reason, the
q
-Gaussian algebras are often viewed as deformations of free group factors. In fact, in the case of finitely many generators, the
q
-Gaussian algebras are isomorphic to free group von Neumann algebras for sufficiently small
|q|
(depending on the number of generators) by
[
12
]
; this was also generalized to the
q
-Araki–Woods and twisted Araki–Woods settings by Nelson
[
24
]
and Yang
[
31
]
. On the other hand, for infinitely many generators the
q
-Gaussian von Neumann algebra is not isomorphic to a free group factor as soon as
q\neq 0

[
7
]
; the non-isomorphism of the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras was obtained earlier in
[
3
]
. The isomorphism problem for finitely many generators and large
q
remains open.

Overall, compared with the litany of results for
q
-Gaussian von Neumann algebras, surprisingly little attention has been given to the
q
-Gaussian
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, with only a few results known in the case of infinitely many generators. This paper will establish several basic results about
q
-Gaussian
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras for finitely many generators, namely, simplicity, uniqueness of the trace, and
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic Dixmier averaging. We use free probability techniques to establish the von Neumann algebraic spectral gap property, and then upgrade this to Dixmier averaging for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras using the rapid decay property. To present the main results, we need to recall some useful definitions. We assume familiarity with the basic theory of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and tracial von Neumann algebras.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
A
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with a faithful trace
\tau
, and let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\tau}
denote the GNS norm. Let
x=(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
in
A
. We say that
(A,\tau,x)
has
spectral gap
if there exists a constant
C
such that for all
y\in A
,

\left\|y-\tau(y)1\right\|_{\tau}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\left\|[y,x_{j}]\right\|_{\tau}+\left\|[y^{*},x_{j}]\right\|_{\tau}).

While the rapid decay property has mostly been studied in the group context (as well as for quantum groups), the definition naturally generalizes to tracial
*
-algebras (see
[
13
]
).

Definition 1.2

(See
[
23
, Proposition 3.4]
and
[
13
, Definition 3.2]
)
.

Let
A
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with a faithful trace
\tau
and let
x=(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
generate
A
. Let

\rho(n)=\sup\left\{\frac{\left\|f(x)\right\|}{\left\|f(x)\right\|_{\tau}}:f\text{ $*$-polynomial of degree }\leq n,f(x)\neq 0\right\}.

Then
(A,\tau,x)
is said to have
rapid decay
if
\rho(n)
is bounded above by a polynomial in
n
. It is said to have
semirapid decay
if
\rho(n)
grows subexponentially, that is,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(n)^{1/n}=1
.

While rapid decay provides the necessary control over polynomial bounds, our primary goal relies on the Dixmier averaging property, which provides a direct route to proving simplicity and the uniqueness of the tracial state.

Definition 1.3

(See e.g.
[
1
]
)
.

A unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
is said to have the
Dixmier averaging property
if for every
y\in A
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
, unitaries
u_{1}
, …,
u_{n}\in A
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}yu_{j}^{*}-\lambda\right\|<\varepsilon.

We recall that if
A
has the Dixmier averaging property, then
A
can have at most one tracial state, since any tracial state
\tau
must satisfy
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau(u_{j}yu_{j}^{*})=\tau(y)
but the constant
\lambda
only depends on
\varepsilon
and not on
\tau
. Moreover, if
A
has a faithful trace
\tau
, then the Dixmier averaging property implies that
A
is simple, because if
I
is a closed ideal containing a nonzero element
x
, then by Dixmier averaging
\tau(x^{*}x)\in I
. We will obtain Dixmier averaging for the
q
-Gaussian
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras using the following general result that spectral gap and rapid decay imply Dixmier averaging.

Theorem A
.

Let
A
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with a faithful trace
\tau
. Suppose that
x=(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
generates
A
and that
(A,\tau,x)
has a spectral gap and that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(n^{2})^{1/n}=0
(which includes, in particular, the case of rapid decay). Then
A
has the Dixmier averaging property, and so in particular it is simple and has a unique trace.

In the case of unitary generators
(u_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
, the proof of the theorem is much easier; one simply defines the averaging operator
Ty=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}(u_{j}yu_{j}^{*}+u_{j}^{*}yu_{j})
and then applies the rapid decay estimate to
\left\|T^{m}y-\tau(y)\right\|
for
y
a polynomial in the
u_{j}
’s (and semi-rapid decay would be sufficient as well). This was already exploited for group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras in
[
26
, proof of Theorem 4.4.5]
; here spectral gap for the group generators is equivalent to
G
not being inner amenable (see e.g.
[
29
, §4]
).

For non-unitary generators, we can first assume without loss of generality that the
x_{j}
’s are self-adjoint with
\left\|x_{j}\right\|\leq 1
and then use the unitaries
u_{j}=\exp(ix_{j})
to define an associated averaging operator
T
. The challenge is that we cannot immediately apply the rapid decay estimate to
T^{m}y
since
u_{j}
is not a polynomial in the given generators
x_{j}
. Hence, we must truncate the power series of the exponential
e^{ix_{n}}
and estimate the errors carefully (see §
2
).

In order to apply Theorem
A
to the
q
-Gaussian family, we need both rapid decay and spectral gap. The rapid decay property was established by
[
6
]
. We obtain spectral gap from a general free probabilistic criterion (for definitions and precise constants, see §
3
below).

Theorem B

(See Theorem
3.8
)
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra generated by a self-adjoint tuple
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
with
d\geq 2
. If
x
has Lipschitz conjugate variables, then
(M,\tau,x)
has spectral gap.

Although this has not been explicitly noted in the literature, it is quite close to the results of Dabrowski. Indeed, in the process of showing that the existence of conjugate variables implies the von Neumann algebra is non-Gamma, Dabrowski notes in
[
9
, Remark 11]
that the spectral gap would follow from having bounded second-order conjugate variables. In turn, the methods of
[
9
]
imply that bounded second-order conjugate variables exist provided that
x
has Lipschitz conjugate variables, which Diez has also shown
[
10
, Proposition 2.14]
. In the interest of clarity, we give in §
3
a self-contained argument for Theorem
B
combining parts of
[
8
, proof of Lemma 9]
with an identity of Mai
[
19
, Corollary V.5.6]
(which was also generalized to the operator-valued setting by Lee
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
).

Finally, to apply Theorem
B
to the
q
-Gaussians, we invoke the recent result of Miyagawa and Speicher
[
22
]
that a finite
q
-Gaussian family has Lipschitz conjugate variables for all
q\in(-1,1)
(this was shown earlier for small
q
by Dabrowski
[
9
, §4.3]
); see also
[
15
,
16
,
31
]
for generalizations to the deformed Araki–Woods setting. We thus conclude the following results about the
q
-Gaussian
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Theorem C
.

Let
q\in(-1,1)
. Let
d\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Let
A
be the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by a
q
-Gaussian family
x=(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
. Then
A
has the Dixmier averaging property, has a unique trace, and is simple.

Assuming Theorems
A
and
B
, the proof for Theorem
C
is short, and therefore, we include it here.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

As mentioned above, unique trace and
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-simplicity follow from Dixmier averaging. It is straightforward to check that the Dixmier averaging property is preserved by inductive limits. Hence, it suffices to prove the claim for
d<\infty
.

The work of Bożejko
[
6
, p. 210, Main Theorem, item (a)]
shows that
A=\mathrm{C}^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
has rapid decay. Moreover, the work of Miyagawa and Speicher
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
shows that
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
has Lipschitz conjugate variables, which implies spectral gap by Theorem
B
. Hence,
A
has Dixmier averaging by Theorem
A
.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

Theorem
C
also generalizes to mixed
q
-Gaussian algebras. The existence of Lipschitz conjugate variables follows from
[
22
, Remark 5.4]
or
[
16
]
. The rapid decay estimate of Bożejko also generalizes to the mixed
q
-Gaussian setting with a similar proof, as shown by Krȯlak
[
14
, Theorem 29]
.

The rest of the paper is organized into two sections: §
2
, which proves Theorem
A
, and §
3
, which proves Theorem
B
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
A
on Dixmier averaging

To leverage the spectral gap property in the context of Dixmier averaging, it is simpler to work with unitary elements. The following preliminary lemma allows us to pass the spectral gap property freely between self-adjoint generators and their unitary exponentials.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with a faithful trace
\tau
. Let
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
be self-adjoint elements of
A
with
\left\|x_{j}\right\|\leq 1
. Let
u_{j}=\exp(ix_{j})
and
u=(u_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
. Then
(A,\tau,x)
has spectral gap if and only if
(A,\tau,u)
has spectral gap.

Proof.

We need to relate the
L^{2}
-derivations associated with
x
and
u
. Since
u_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{i^{k}}{k!}x_{j}^{k}
, the commutator with any element
y\in A
is given by

[u_{j},y]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{i^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}x_{j}^{l}[x_{j},y]x_{j}^{k-1-l}.

Taking the
L^{2}
-norm and using the fact that
\left\|x_{j}\right\|\leq 1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|[u_{j},y]\right\|_{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}k\left\|x_{j}\right\|^{k-1}\left\|[x_{j},y]\right\|_{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\right)\left\|[x_{j},y]\right\|_{\tau}

\displaystyle=e\left\|[x_{j},y]\right\|_{\tau}.

A similar bound holds for
\left\|[u_{j}^{*},y]\right\|_{\tau}
. Therefore, if
x
has a spectral gap,
u
also has a spectral gap.

Conversely, we can recover
x_{j}
via the principal logarithm so that

x_{j}=-i\log(u_{j})=-i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}(u_{j}-1)^{k}.

Note that for
t\in[-1,1]
,
|1-e^{it}|^{2}=2-2\cos t\leq 2-2\cos 1<1
. Letting
r=\sqrt{2-2\cos 1}
, we have by functional calculus that
\left\|u_{j}-1\right\|\leq r<1
, ensuring the series converges absolutely in the operator norm. By expanding the commutators of
(u_{j}-1)^{k}
similarly, we can bound
\left\|[x_{j},y]\right\|_{\tau}
by a constant multiple of
\left\|[u_{j},y]\right\|_{\tau}
plus the bound on the adjoint. Hence, the spectral gaps are equivalent.
∎

With this equivalence in hand, we proceed to prove our first main result. The core strategy is to approximate the averaging operator via a truncated Taylor series while controlling the resulting uniform and
L^{2}
errors.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

First, let
\re(x_{j})=(x_{j}+x_{j}^{*})/2
and
\im(x_{j})=(x_{j}-x_{j}^{*})/2i
. Note that

\left\|[x_{j},y]\right\|_{\tau}+\left\|[x_{j}^{*},y]\right\|_{\tau}\leq 2\left[\left\|[\re(x_{j}),y]\right\|_{\tau}+\left\|[\im(x_{j}),y]\right\|_{\tau}\right].

Thus, by replacing
(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
by
(\re(x_{1}),\im(x_{1}),\dots,\re(x_{d}),\im(x_{d}))
, we can assume that our spectral gap tuple consists of self-adjoint elements. Note that rapid decay is also preserved because
*
-polynomials up to degree
n
in
(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
are the same as
*
-polynomials up to degree
n
in
(\re(x_{1}),\im(x_{1}),\dots,\re(x_{d}),\im(x_{d}))
. Thus, for the rest of the proof, we assume, without loss of generality, that
x_{1}
, …,
x_{d}
are self-adjoint and, by rescaling, we also assume that
\left\|x_{j}\right\|\leq 1
.

Let
u_{j}=\exp(ix_{j})
for
j=1,\dots,d
. By the preceding lemma, the tuple of unitaries
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})
has spectral gap. Let
T:A\to A
be the averaging operator defined by

T(y)=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}(u_{j}yu_{j}^{*}+u_{j}^{*}yu_{j}).

The spectral gap property implies the existence of a constant
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that for all
y\in A
with
\tau(y)=0
we have

\left\|T(y)\right\|_{\tau}\leq(1-\gamma)\left\|y\right\|_{\tau}
. By iterating
T
, we obtain
\left\|T^{n}(y)\right\|_{\tau}\leq(1-\gamma)^{n}\left\|y\right\|_{\tau}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We now approximate the unitaries
u_{j}
by their Taylor polynomials of degree
m

u_{j,m}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{i^{k}}{k!}x_{j}^{k}.

Since the derivative of order
m+1
of
e^{it}
is bounded by
1
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have the Taylor remainder estimate

\sup_{t\in[-1,1]}\left|e^{it}-\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{(it)^{k}}{k!}\right|\leq\frac{1}{(m+1)!}.

Since
\left\|x_{j}\right\|\leq 1
, the properties of functional calculus imply that

\left\|u_{j}-u_{j,m}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{(m+1)!}.

Define the corresponding truncated averaging operator
T_{m}:A\to A
by

T_{m}(y)=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}(u_{j,m}yu_{j,m}^{*}+u_{j,m}^{*}yu_{j,m}).

If
y
is a
*
-polynomial in
x
of degree
\deg(y)
, then
T_{m}(y)
is a
*
-polynomial of degree at most
\deg(y)+2m
. Consequently,
\deg(T_{m}^{n}(y))\leq\deg(y)+2mn
. By the definition of the growth function
\rho
, we have

\left\|T_{m}^{n}(y)\right\|\leq\rho(\deg(y)+2mn)\left\|T_{m}^{n}(y)\right\|_{\tau}.

Next, we estimate the uniform distance between
T^{n}
and
T_{m}^{n}
. Since
u_{j}
is unitary (
\left\|u_{j}\right\|=1
), the triangle inequality yields

\left\|u_{j,m}\right\|\leq\left\|u_{j}\right\|+\left\|u_{j,m}-u_{j}\right\|\leq 1+\frac{1}{(m+1)!}.

Applying
T_{m}
therefore scales the uniform norm by a factor of at most
\left(1+\frac{1}{(m+1)!}\right)^{2}
. Using a standard telescoping sum over
n
iterations, the cumulative truncation error is bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|T^{n}(y)-T_{m}^{n}(y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left\|T_{m}^{k}(T-T_{m})T^{n-1-k}(y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\frac{1}{(m+1)!}\right)^{2n}\frac{n}{(m+1)!}\left\|y\right\|

for some absolute constant
C>0
. Setting
m=n
to balance the bounds, we see that

\displaystyle\left\|T^{n}(y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{n}^{n}(y)\right\|+\left\|T^{n}(y)-T_{n}^{n}(y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\rho(\deg(y)+2n^{2})\left\|T_{n}^{n}(y)\right\|_{\tau}+C\left(1+\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\right)^{2n}\frac{n}{(n+1)!}\left\|y\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\rho(\deg(y)+2n^{2})\left(\left\|T^{n}(y)\right\|_{\tau}+\left\|T_{n}^{n}(y)-T^{n}(y)\right\|_{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle+C\left(1+\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\right)^{2n}\frac{n}{(n+1)!}\left\|y\right\|.

Since the
L^{2}
-norm is dominated by the uniform norm, we substitute
\left\|T^{n}(y)\right\|_{\tau}\leq(1-\gamma)^{n}\left\|y\right\|_{\tau}
to find

\left\|T^{n}(y)\right\|\leq\rho(\deg(y)+2n^{2})\left[(1-\gamma)^{n}\left\|y\right\|_{\tau}+\text{Error}\right]+\text{Error},

where
\text{Error}=C\left(1+\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\right)^{2n}\frac{n}{(n+1)!}\left\|y\right\|
.

By hypothesis,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(n^{2})^{1/n}=1
, meaning
\rho
grows subexponentially. The term
(1-\gamma)^{n}
decays exponentially, and the factorial term in the denominator ensures that the Error term decays super-exponentially. Therefore, the right-hand side converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
, demonstrating that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|T^{n}(y)\right\|=0
for any trace-zero polynomial
y
.

Since polynomials are dense in
A
, for any
y\in A
and
\varepsilon>0
, we can choose a trace-zero polynomial
y_{0}
such that
\left\|(y-\tau(y)1)-y_{0}\right\|<\varepsilon/2
. Because
T
is contractive,
\left\|T^{n}(y)-\tau(y)1\right\|\leq\left\|T^{n}(y_{0})\right\|+\varepsilon/2
. Choosing
n
sufficiently large yields
\left\|T^{n}(y)-\tau(y)1\right\|<\varepsilon
. This proves that
A
possesses the Dixmier averaging property.
∎

While the simple choice of
m=n
suffices to yield the required subexponential decay, a more delicate reworking of the error terms allows for a slightly sharper growth condition. We remark this below.

Remark 2.2
.

In the proof of Theorem
A
, we set
m=n
for simplicity, which requires the subexponential growth condition
\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(n^{2})^{1/n}=1
, or equivalently
\log\rho(k)=o(\sqrt{k})
. However, this can be slightly optimized by choosing
m
to carefully balance the exponential decay from the spectral gap with the factorial decay from the Taylor remainder.

Let
1-\gamma=e^{-c}
for some
c>0
. The proof requires both the spectral gap term and the error term to decay when multiplied by the polynomial growth factor, meaning we need
\rho(2mn)e^{-cn}\to 0
and
\rho(2mn)\frac{n}{(m+1)!}\to 0
.

Taking the logarithm of the error term and applying Stirling’s approximation, the decay is dominated by
-\log((m+1)!)\sim-m\log m
. To balance the two decay rates, we equate the exponents so that
m\log m\approx cn
, which implies
m\approx\frac{cn}{\log n}
.

Substituting this choice into the degree
k=2mn
, we get
k\approx\frac{2cn^{2}}{\log n}
, which inverts to
n\approx\sqrt{\frac{k\log k}{4c}}
. The necessary condition
\log\rho(k)\ll cn
then translates to the optimal growth condition

\log\rho(k)=o(\sqrt{k\log k}),

which is a slightly weaker requirement than
o(\sqrt{k})
. Equivalently, this can be restated as
\lim_{m\to\infty}\rho(m^{2}\log m)^{\frac{1}{m\log m}}=1
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
B
on spectral gap

In this section, we prove Theorem
B
. This result is essentially a combination of
[
8
, Lemma 9, Remark 11]
and
[
19
, Corollary V.5.6]
or
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
. Still, for the reader’s convenience, we give a streamlined, self-contained argument for the spectral gap inequality after recalling the relevant definitions.

Definition 3.1

(Free difference quotients
[
30
, §3]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle
be the
*
-algebra of formal noncommutative polynomials in self-adjoint indeterminates
t_{1}
, …,
t_{d}
. Note that we view
\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle\otimes\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle
as a bimodule over
\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle\otimes\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle
by left multiplication on the left tensorand and right multiplication on the right tensorand. Voiculescu’s
free difference quotient

\partial_{j}:\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle\to\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle\otimes\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle
is the unique linear map satisfying

(3.1)

\partial_{j}t_{k}=\delta_{j,k}1\otimes 1

and the Leibniz rule (i.e., it is a derivation),

(3.2)

\partial_{j}(fg)=f\cdot\partial_{j}g+\partial_{j}f\cdot g.

With the free difference quotients established, we can formalize a noncommutative integration-by-parts formula with respect to the trace. The vectors in
L^{2}(M,\tau)
that implement this formula act as the adjoints to our derivations and are known as conjugate variables.

Definition 3.2

(Conjugate variables
[
30
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra generated by self-adjoints
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
. For
f,g\in\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle
, let
f(x)
denote the evaluation on
x
and
(f\otimes g)(x)=f(x)\otimes g(x)
. We say that
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d})\in L^{2}(M,\tau)_{\sa}^{d}
is family of
conjugate variables
for
x
if for all
f\in\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle
,

(3.3)

\langle\xi_{j},f(x)\rangle_{\tau}=\tau\otimes\tau(\partial f(x)).

If
(M,\tau,x)
admits conjugate variables
\xi
, then
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
are algebraically free
[
18
]
, and therefore the evaluation map
\mathbb{C}\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}\rangle\to M
,
f\mapsto f(x)
is an isomorphism onto its image which we denote by
\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\rangle
. We can therefore view
\partial_{j}
as a densely defined operator

L^{2}(M,\tau)\supseteq\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\rangle\to L^{2}(M,\tau)\otimes L^{2}(M,\tau),

and the definition of conjugate variables means that

1\otimes 1\in\dom(\partial_{j}^{*}),\qquad\xi_{j}=\partial_{j}^{*}(1\otimes 1).

Algebraic computations from (
3.2
) and the relation

\partial_{j}(f^{*})=\operatorname{flip}((\partial_{j}f)^{*}),

where
\operatorname{flip}
is the tensor flip, imply that

\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\rangle\otimes\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\rangle\subseteq\dom(\partial_{j}^{*}),

and

(3.4)

\partial_{j}^{*}(f(x)\otimes g(x))=f(x)\xi_{j}g(x)-({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}f(x))g(x)-f(x)(\tau\otimes{\rm id})(\partial_{j}g(x)).

See
[
30
, Propositions 4.1, 4.3]
. Moreover, since the domain of
\partial_{j}^{*}
is dense, this means that
\partial_{j}
is closable; we denote the closure by
\overline{\partial}_{j}
. Furthermore, we note that if
M^{\op}
denotes the opposite von Neumann algebra, then
(M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op},\tau\otimes\tau^{\op})
is a tracial von Neumann algebra and

L^{2}(M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op},\tau\otimes\tau^{\op})\cong L^{2}(M,\tau)\otimes L^{2}(M,\tau).

For our purposes, the mere existence of conjugate variables is not enough. In addition, we require analytic regularity. Specifically, when these variables fall within the domain of the closures
\overline{\partial}_{j}
and yield bounded derivations, they provide the exact structural rigidity needed to force a spectral gap.

Definition 3.3

(Lipschitz conjugate variables
[
9
, Definition 1]
)
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra generated by self-adjoints
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
. Then
x
is said to have
Lipschitz conjugate variables
if it has conjugate variables
\xi
such that
\xi_{j}
is in the domain of
\overline{\partial}_{k}
and
\overline{\partial}_{k}\xi_{j}
is a element of
M\otimes M^{\operatorname{op}}\subseteq L^{2}(M\otimes M^{\operatorname{op}})\cong L^{2}(M,\tau)\otimes L^{2}(M,\tau)
, for every
j,k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.

We next recall the following result from Dabrowski. In the statement, we use the notation
[x,h]
whenever
x\in M
and
h
is in a bimodule over
M
such as
L^{2}(M,\tau)\otimes L^{2}(M,\tau)
. We also use the bilinear map
\#:M\otimes M\times H\to H
given by the linear extension of
(x\otimes y)\#h=xhy
.

Proposition 3.4

(See
[
8
, Proof of Lemma 9]
)
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra generated by
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
. Then

2(d-1)\left\|f-\tau(f)\right\|_{\tau}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle[f,1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}([f,x_{j}])\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle[[f,x_{j}],1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}f\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}

Proof.

First, we note that
\left\|[f,1\otimes 1]\right\|_{\tau\otimes\tau}^{2}=\left\|f\otimes 1-1\otimes f\right\|_{\tau\otimes\tau}^{2}=2\left\|f-\tau(f)\right\|_{\tau}^{2}
, and so the left-hand side is
(d-1)\left\|[f,1\otimes 1]\right\|_{\tau\otimes\tau}^{2}
. From the Leibniz rule,

\partial_{j}[f,x_{j}]=[\partial_{j}f,x_{j}]+[f,1\otimes 1],

so that

(3.5)

\left\|[f,1\otimes 1]\right\|_{\tau\otimes\tau}^{2}=\langle[f,1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}[f,x_{j}]\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}-\langle[f,1\otimes 1],[\partial_{j}f,x_{j}]\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}.

Focusing on the last term, we use the Jacobi identity to write

(3.6)

\displaystyle\langle[f,1\otimes 1],[\partial_{j}f,x_{j}]\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau([f^{*},[\partial_{j}f,x_{j}]])

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau([[x_{j},f^{*}],\partial_{j}f])+\tau\otimes\tau([[f^{*},\partial_{j}f],x_{j}]).

Focusing on the last term again, we use the traciality of
\tau
to show that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau

\displaystyle([[f^{*},\partial_{j}f],x_{j}])

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau\Bigl(f^{*}(\partial_{j}f)x_{j}-x_{j}f^{*}(\partial_{j}f)-(\partial_{j}f)f^{*}x_{j}+x_{j}(\partial_{j}f)f^{*}\Bigr)

(3.10)

\displaystyle=-\tau\otimes\tau([f^{*},\partial_{j}f\#[x_{j},1\otimes 1]).

More explicitly, for instance, the first term
\tau\otimes\tau(f^{*}(\partial_{j}f)x_{j})
in (
3.8
) is equal to
\tau\otimes\tau(f^{*}\partial_{j}f\#(1\otimes x_{j}))
, using the identity

\tau\otimes\tau(f^{*}(a\otimes b)x_{j})=\tau\otimes\tau(f^{*}a\otimes x_{j}b)=\tau\otimes\tau(f^{*}(a\otimes b)\#(1\otimes x_{j})),

which is then extended linearly from the simple tensors
a\otimes b
, and the other terms in (
3.6
) are handled similarly. This allows us to apply the well-known identity
[
21
, eq. (8.3)]

(3.11)

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}f\#[x_{j},1\otimes 1]=[f,1\otimes 1],

which can be verified by checking it when
f(x)=x_{k}
and noting that both sides are derivations in
f
. We thus have from (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tau\otimes\tau([[f^{*},\partial_{j}f],x_{j}])

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tau\otimes\tau([f^{*},\partial_{j}f\#[x_{j},1\otimes 1]))

\displaystyle=-\tau\otimes\tau([f^{*},[f,1\otimes 1]])

\displaystyle=-\left\|[f,1\otimes 1]\right\|_{\tau\otimes\tau}^{2}.

Combining this with (
3.6
),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle[f,1\otimes 1],[\partial_{j}f,x_{j}]\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tau\otimes\tau\Bigl([[x_{j},f^{*}],\partial_{j}f]+[[f^{*},\partial_{j}f],x_{j}]\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle[[f,x_{j}],1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}f\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}-\left\|[f,1\otimes 1]\right\|_{\tau\otimes\tau}^{2}.

We then substitute this into (
3.5
) and obtain

d\left\|[f,1\otimes 1]\right\|_{\tau\otimes\tau}^{2}\\
=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle[f,1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}([f,x_{j}])\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle[[f,x_{j}],1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}f\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}+\left\|[f,1\otimes 1]\right\|_{\tau\otimes\tau}^{2},

and subtracting
\left\|[f,1\otimes 1]\right\|_{\tau\otimes\tau}^{2}
yields the desired formula.
∎

Both terms on the right-hand side of Proposition
3.4
are pairings
of the form
\langle[g,1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}h\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}
, with
g
and
h
taken from

\{f,[f,x_{j}]\}
. To bound such a pairing, it suffices to control the adjoint
\partial_{j}^{*}
on
elements of the form
g\otimes 1
, since
\langle g\otimes 1,\partial_{j}h\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}=\langle\partial_{j}^{*}(g\otimes 1),h\rangle_{\tau}
. The following identity of Mai supplies exactly this
control once the conjugate variables are Lipschitz.

Proposition 3.5

(See
[
19
, Corollary V.5.6]
or
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra generated by self-adjoints
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
, and assume that
x
has conjugate variables
\xi
. Then for
f,g\in\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\rangle
, we have

(3.12)

\left\|\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1)\right\|_{\tau}^{2}=\langle\partial_{j}^{*}(f^{*}f\otimes 1),\partial_{j}^{*}(1\otimes 1)\rangle_{\tau}.

In particular, if the conjugate variables are Lipschitz, then

(3.13)

\left\|\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1)\right\|_{\tau}\leq\left\|\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op}}^{1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{\tau}.

Proof.

We first note the following consequence of (
3.4
):

\partial_{j}^{*}(f^{*}f\otimes 1)=f^{*}\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1)-({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}(f^{*})f).

We therefore have

(3.14)

\langle\partial_{j}^{*}(f^{*}f\otimes 1),\partial_{j}^{*}(1\otimes 1)\rangle_{\tau}=\langle f^{*}\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1),\partial_{j}^{*}(1\otimes 1)\rangle_{\tau}-\langle({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}(f^{*})f),\partial_{j}^{*}(1\otimes 1)\rangle_{\tau},

and meanwhile

(3.15)

\langle\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1),\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1)\rangle_{\tau}=\langle\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1),f\partial_{j}^{*}(1\otimes 1)\rangle_{\tau}-\langle\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1),({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}f)\rangle_{\tau}.

We note that the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.14
) is equal to the corresponding term in (
3.15
). Therefore, to prove (
3.12
), it suffices to show that

\langle({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}(f^{*})f),\partial_{j}^{*}(1\otimes 1)\rangle_{\tau}=\langle\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1),({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}f)\rangle_{\tau},

which is equivalent to

\langle\partial_{j}({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}(f^{*})f),1\otimes 1\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}=\langle f\otimes 1,\partial_{j}({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}f)\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau},

and in turn

\tau\otimes\tau(\partial_{j}({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}(f^{*})f)^{*})=\tau\otimes\tau(f^{*}\partial_{j}({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}f)).

To prove this, one simply uses the identity
\partial_{j}(f^{*})=\operatorname{flip}((\partial_{j}f)^{*})
and the coassociativity relation
({\rm id}\otimes\partial_{j})\circ\partial_{j}=(\partial_{j}\otimes{\rm id})\circ\partial_{j}
(see e.g.
[
21
, eq. (8.2)]
). Indeed,

\displaystyle\tau\otimes\tau((\partial_{j}({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}(f^{*})f))^{*})

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau((\partial_{j}({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}(f^{*})f)^{*}))

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau(\partial_{j}(\tau\otimes{\rm id})(f^{*}\partial_{j}f))

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau\otimes\tau(f^{*}({\rm id}\otimes\partial_{j})\circ\partial_{j}f)

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau\otimes\tau(f^{*}(\partial_{j}\otimes{\rm id})\circ\partial_{j}f)

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau(f^{*}\partial_{j}({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}f)).

This completes the proof of (
3.12
).

As a consequence of (
3.12
), we have

\left\|\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1)\right\|_{\tau}^{2}=\langle f^{*}f\otimes 1,\partial_{j}\partial_{j}^{*}(1\otimes 1)\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}=\langle f^{*}f\otimes 1,\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\langle f^{*}f\otimes 1,\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}

\displaystyle=\tau\otimes\tau^{\op}((f^{*}f\otimes 1)(\partial_{j}\xi_{j}))

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op}}(\tau\otimes\tau^{\op})(f^{*}f\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\left\|\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op}}\left\|f\right\|_{\tau}^{2},

which proves (
3.13
). ∎

Remark 3.6
.

In (
3.13
), one can also replace
\left\|\partial_{j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op}}
with
\left\|({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\partial_{j}\xi_{j})\right\|_{M}
since

\langle f^{*}f\otimes 1,\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}=\langle f^{*}f,({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j})\rangle_{\tau}.

Hence, Corollary
3.7
and Theorem
3.8
also hold with
\left\|({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j})\right\|_{M}
in place of
\left\|\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op}}
.

With Proposition
3.5
in hand, we now have the required tools to bound the cross-terms arising in Dabrowski’s identity. The following corollary translates the
L^{2}
-norm estimates of the Lipschitz conjugate variables into bounds for these pairings.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra generated by self-adjoints
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
, and assume that
x
has conjugate variables
\xi
. Then for
f,g\in\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\rangle
, we have

|\langle[f,1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}g\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}|\leq 2\left\|\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op}}^{1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{\tau}\left\|g\right\|_{\tau}.

Proof.

Write
[f,1\otimes 1]=f\otimes 1-1\otimes f
. Then

\langle f\otimes 1,\partial_{j}g\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}=\langle\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1),g\rangle_{\tau},

and then we apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the bound (
3.13
) for
\partial_{j}^{*}(f\otimes 1)
. For the other inequality, we apply the tensor flip identity to write

\langle 1\otimes f,\partial_{j}g\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}=\langle\partial_{j}(g^{*}),f^{*}\otimes 1\rangle,

and then apply the previous bound together with
\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{\tau}=\left\|f\right\|_{\tau}
and
\left\|g^{*}\right\|_{\tau}=\left\|g\right\|_{\tau}
.
∎

We now conclude the proof of the following more precise version of Theorem
B
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra generated by self-adjoints
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
with
d\geq 2
, and assume that
x
has Lipschitz conjugate variables
\xi
. Then for
z\in M
,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left\|z-\tau(z)\right\|_{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{d-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op}}^{1/2}\left\|[z,x_{j}]\right\|_{\tau}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{d-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|[z,x_{j}]\right\|_{\tau}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

Proof.

It suffices to prove the claim for
z=f\in\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\rangle
and we can assume without loss of generality that
\tau(f)=0
. From Proposition
3.4
, we obtain

2(d-1)\left\|f\right\|_{\tau}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\langle[f,1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}([f,x_{j}])\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}|+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\langle[[f,x_{j}],1\otimes 1],\partial_{j}f\rangle_{\tau\otimes\tau}|.

Bounding each of the terms with Corollary
3.7
, we see that

2(d-1)\left\|f\right\|_{\tau}^{2}\leq 4\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\overline{\partial}_{j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{M\overline{\otimes}M^{\op}}^{1/2}\left\|[f,x_{j}]\right\|_{\tau}\left\|f\right\|_{\tau},

which yields (
3.16
), and then (
3.17
) follows by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
∎
