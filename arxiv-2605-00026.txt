The γ c \gamma_{c} -Peak: Covariant Recovery on Four Organic Qubit Platforms
Hikaru Wakaura, Taiki Tanimae

Abstract
The Petz recovery map (1986) provably reverses a noisy quantum channel on a reference state, but its algorithmic relevance to real, dissipation-dominated platforms has remained unclear. Using the open-source organic-qc-bench simulation package, we benchmark a Petz-style covariant-purification quantum error correction (CQEC) protocol across four engineered organic qubit platforms operated without any magnetic field : a flavin–nitroxide radical-pair reservoir (P1); perchlorotriphenylmethyl radicals in a covalent organic framework (P2); the SVILC qubit [ 37 ] on κ \kappa -(BEDT-TTF) 2 Cu[N(CN) 2 ]Br (P3, conditional on SVILC confirmation); and a Su–Schrieffer–Heeger soliton on trans -polyacetylene (P4). Across five quantum algorithms (QKAN, qDRIFT, control-free QPE, Shor–Regev, Bernstein–Vazirani) and two ML tasks, CQEC gains are significant ( p < 10 − 5 p\!<\!10^{-5} ; Wilcoxon, Bonferroni α = 0.05 / 44 \alpha\!=\!0.05/44 ) for all sixteen path × \times algorithm pairs. The central finding is the γ c \gamma_{c} -peak : the fidelity gain Δ ​ F \Delta F is maximised at the entanglement-breaking threshold γ c \gamma_{c} , with Δ ​ F max = + 0.303 \Delta F_{\rm max}\!=\!+0.303 at d = 64 d\!=\!64 and a linear log 2 ⁡ d \log_{2}d scaling over d = 2 d\!=\!2 – 64 64 — algorithmically confirming the prediction [ 38 ] that Petz recovery preserves coherence beyond this threshold. Bernstein–Vazirani also yields a 7.6 7.6 – 31 × 31\times provable quantum advantage at n = 3 n\!=\!3 – 5 5 , diarylethene-photoswitch CZ fidelities reach F CZ ≥ 0.987 F_{\mathrm{CZ}}\!\geq\!0.987 for P2–P4, and projected manufacturing costs are 10–40 × \times lower with 10–200 × \times less operating power than superconducting platforms. The γ c \gamma_{c} -peak establishes Petz-style recovery as a practically relevant primitive at the dissipation–coherence boundary and identifies PTM-COF (P2) as the highest-priority experimental target.



1
Introduction

Biological radical-pair systems such as the avian magnetic compass and
brain monoamine oxidase (MAO-A) operate at physiological temperature
in unstructured aqueous environments yet appear to exploit quantum
coherence with a signal-to-noise ratio that is sufficient to bias
chemical reaction yields
[
17
]
. The 3-Layer Quantum
Brain Hypothesis
[
38
]
(hereafter “3-LQBH companion
preprint”) reconciles this with the “warm and wet” objection by
showing that (i) protein tumbling provides a natural
motional-narrowing dynamical decoupling, (ii) decoherence-free
subspace (DFS) encoding exploits collective noise, and
(iii) a noisy-reference Petz recovery map
[
31
]
can preserve
entanglement beyond the entanglement-breaking (EB) threshold

\gamma_{c}\!\simeq\!0.3
. The
system functions as a
quantum reservoir computer

(QRC)
[
21
]
rather than a fault-tolerant quantum computer.

At the same time, the SVILC qubit of Wakaura and
Koizumi
[
37
]
formulates eight concrete conditions for a
scalable nanoscale qubit built out of topologically protected loop
currents around spin-vortices in a cuprate CuO
2
plane, with an
external-feeding-current coupler providing scalable two-qubit
operations.

We therefore ask whether the organisational principles demonstrated
in both biology and in Wakaura–Koizumi’s cuprate theory can be
re-instantiated in
engineered
organic materials such that
(a) no applied magnetic field is required, and
(b) room-temperature operation is possible. In this paper we answer
both questions affirmatively by numerical simulation, covering
algorithmic benchmarks, lattice physics, classical post-processing of
Regev’s algorithm, and photoswitched two-qubit entangling gates. We
also provide a detailed comparison of projected manufacturing costs
against the five major existing quantum-computing platforms.

Main result — the
\bm{\gamma_{c}}
-peak.

CQEC-protected organic-QC architectures reach single-qubit fidelity

F\!\geq\!0.995
and two-qubit CZ fidelity

F_{\mathrm{CZ}}\!\geq\!0.987
across Paths 2–4 at room temperature on
five quantum algorithms; the CQEC gain
\Delta F
exhibits a sharp

\gamma_{c}
-peak
at
\gamma\!\simeq\!\gamma_{c}
with

\Delta F\!=\!+0.303
on Shor–Regev (
d\!=\!64
), directly
confirming the 3-layer-hypothesis prediction of “Petz recovery
beyond the EB threshold”. We hereafter refer to this universal
maximum — present across all four organic platforms and five
algorithms — as
the
\gamma_{c}
-peak
of covariant recovery.

2.1
The 3-Layer Quantum Brain Hypothesis (recap)

The hypothesis postulates three functional layers:

•

Layer 1: long-lived nuclear-spin memory (
T_{2}^{(n)}\!\sim\!3
ms);

•

Layer 2: radical-pair quantum reservoir (
T_{2}^{(e)}\!\sim\!1
ns);

•

Layer 3: spin-selective chemical reaction yield readout.

Biological MAO-A and cryptochrome substrates have effective dephasing

\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\tau_{\mathrm{gate}}/T_{2}^{(e)}\!\approx\!4.55
and

3.25
, well above the EB threshold
\gamma_{c}\!\approx\!0.3
; hence
the one-way quantum capacity is zero,

Q_{1}\!=\!0

[
30
]
, and standard quantum error
correction cannot recover the channel. The central finding of the
3-LQBH preprint
[
38
]
is that DFS, dynamical
decoupling and Petz recovery
together
still preserve
task-relevant quantum coherence.

2.2
SVILC qubit conditions (Wakaura–Koizumi)

Ref.
[
37
]
formulates eight qubit conditions: (i)
qubit-state differentiation, (ii) ns-scale gate operation via Rabi
drives, (iii) ns gate-time, (iv) on/off coupling control, (v)

\sim\!10

liquid-
\mathrm{N}_{2}

2.5
P2 electron-pair orientation and P3 “hole”
definition

Because the two-qubit coupling mechanism of P2 and the SVILC-forming
charge carrier of P3 are frequently misinterpreted, we state them
explicitly here.

P2: how the 2-qubit PTM electron pair is formed and
oriented.

Each PTM radical carries one unpaired electron with
S\!=\!\tfrac{1}{2}
,
residing in the singly-occupied molecular orbital
(SOMO) that is a

p_{z}
-dominated
\pi
-orbital delocalised over the central sp
2

carbon and the three phenyl rings
[
3
,
34
]
.
In the COF lattice a pair of neighbouring PTM qubits is oriented

face-to-face across a diarylethene bridge
at
\sim 3
nm
pitch
[
41
]
. The bridge provides a through-bond
superexchange path whose amplitude switches with the photoswitch
state of the diarylethene: open (visible light) gives

J_{\mathrm{open}}\!\sim\!5\!\times\!10^{-5}
GHz, closed (UV) gives

J_{\mathrm{close}}\!\simeq\!50
MHz
[
19
,
13
]
. The
two-qubit subspace
\{|\!\uparrow\uparrow\rangle,|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle,|\!\downarrow\uparrow\rangle,|\!\downarrow\downarrow\rangle\}

therefore has a tunable exchange Hamiltonian

H=J(t)\,\sigma_{z}^{(1)}\sigma_{z}^{(2)}
, and the CZ gate is implemented
by timing the UV pulse such that
\int J(t)\,{\rm d}t=1/4

(dimensionless units); see Sec.
3.7
and
Fig.
5
(B).

\kappa
-(BEDT-TTF)
2

The conducting layer of
\kappa

2

3.1
Covariant-purification CQEC

Following the CQEC pipeline of the 3-LQBH companion
preprint
[
38
]
, each
algorithmic

state
\rho_{\mathrm{alg}}
is protected by a parallel
catalyst

copy
\rho_{\mathrm{cat}}=\rho_{\mathrm{noisy}}
that undergoes
n
rounds
of recursive covariant swap-test purification

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cov}}
. The CQEC recovery map applied to the
algorithmic state is

\mathcal{R}(\rho_{\mathrm{alg}})_{ij}=\rho_{\mathrm{alg,ij}}+\eta_{ij}\bigl|\rho_{\mathrm{target,ij}}\bigr|\,e^{i\arg(\rho_{\mathrm{target,ij}})},

(3)

where

\eta_{ij}=1-\exp\!\bigl[-|\rho_{\mathrm{cat,ij}}|\,d\,\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{cat}}^{\,2})\bigr]

is the catalyst-dependent recovery efficiency. Equation
(
3
) is implemented in

cqec/covariant_purification.py
, and its derivation from the
Petz recovery map
[
31
]
is given in App.
B
.
The number of purification rounds is
n\!=\!2
for

d\!\leq\!16
and
n\!=\!1
for
d\!>\!16
to keep the
\mathcal{O}(d^{6})
per-round cost tractable.

3.2
Fidelity, purity and concurrence

We use Uhlmann fidelity
[
30
]

F(\rho,\sigma)=\bigl[\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\,\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}\bigr]^{2},

(4)

and purity
P=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{2})
throughout. Where relevant (two-qubit
states), we also compute the Wootters concurrence
[
40
]

C(\rho)=\max\!\bigl(0,\,\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}\bigr),

(5)

with
\lambda_{i}
the decreasing singular values of

\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\,\tilde{\rho}\sqrt{\rho}}
and

\tilde{\rho}=(\sigma_{y}\!\otimes\!\sigma_{y})\rho^{*}(\sigma_{y}\!\otimes\!\sigma_{y})
.

3.3
Algorithm benchmarks: circuits and loss functions

Four quantum algorithms are benchmarked under each organic profile.

(A) QKAN (Chebyshev amplitudes)

Following Ivashkov
et al.

[
20
]
, we take a
pure state with Chebyshev-polynomial amplitudes on

|\psi\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(2,1,-1,0)^{\!\top},

(6)

realised by a single block-encoding layer (Fig. 1 of
Ref.
[
20
]
), corresponding in
cqec.algorithms.make_qkan

to the direct amplitude encoding that reproduces the

(B) qDRIFT (random product formula)

For a 3-qubit Heisenberg model with nearest-neighbour couplings

J\!=\!1
and field
h\!=\!0.5
,

H=J\sum_{\alpha,\langle i,j\rangle}\sigma_{i}^{\alpha}\sigma_{j}^{\alpha}+h\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}^{z},

(7)

we compile
e^{-iHt}
via qDRIFT
[
9
]
: at each of

N=80

p_{k}=\|h_{k}\|/\lambda
, giving

U_{k}=\exp[-i(t/N)(h_{k}/p_{k})]
and

V_{N}\cdots V_{1}\approx e^{-iHt}
. We fix

and evolve the

|000\rangle
initial state; the implementation mirrors the
pseudo-code of Ref.
[
9
]

(
cqec.algorithms.make_qdrift
).

(C) Control-free Quantum Phase Estimation

Following Clinton
et al.

[
10
]
, we take a
spectral superposition of an effective Fermi–Hubbard Hamiltonian

) with

|\psi\rangle=\sum_{k}w_{k}e^{i\phi_{k}}|k\rangle
,

into populations after a QFT
(
cqec.algorithms.make_cfqpe
).

(D’) Bernstein–Vazirani (provable quantum advantage)

To address the concern that density-matrix-level benchmarks do not
establish an unambiguous quantum advantage, we also run the
Bernstein–Vazirani (BV) algorithm
[
5
]
. For an
n
-bit
hidden string
s\!\in\!\{0,1\}^{n}
, the quantum circuit

|0\rangle^{\otimes n}\!\xrightarrow{H^{\otimes n}}\!|+\rangle^{\otimes n}\!\xrightarrow{U_{s}}\!\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\!\!\sum_{x}(-1)^{s\cdot x}|x\rangle\!\xrightarrow{H^{\otimes n}}\!|s\rangle

(8)

recovers
s
in one query, whereas any classical strategy using one
query can only guess
s
with probability
2^{-n}
. Our density-matrix
implementation (
bernstein_vazirani_benchmark.py
) applies
the organic noise channel
\mathcal{E}_{\delta}\!\circ\!\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}

after each Hadamard layer and after the oracle, and measures the
computational-basis outcome by sampling from the final diagonal.

(D) Shor–Regev factoring

For Shor at small
N

(9)

trace out the value register, apply the QFT

F_{jk}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\,e^{+2\pi i\,jk/d},

(10)

and measure the index register. Continued-fraction expansion on the
measurement

yields a candidate period

r

a^{r/2}\!\neq\!N\!-\!1
then
\gcd(a^{r/2}\!\pm\!1,N)
is checked for

[
35
,
30
]
.

For Regev’s algorithm
[
32
]
we additionally implement the

[
27
,

\kappa

The conducting plane of
\kappa

triangular lattice

Hamiltonian

(nn) and

(nnn) and impose a spin-vortex
phase field

(14)

\mathbf{r}_{v}

[

phase-frustration functional

V_{\alpha\Upsilon}=\sum_{(ij)\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{mid}}}\!\!\Bigl(|\sin\tfrac{\Delta\chi_{A}+\Delta\chi_{B}}{2}|-|\sin\tfrac{\Delta\chi_{A}-\Delta\chi_{B}}{2}|\Bigr),

where
\Delta\chi_{A,B}\!=\!\chi_{A,B}(j)-\chi_{A,B}(i)
and

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{mid}}

r_{c}\!=\!3a

3.8
Statistical methodology

For all flagship claims we use
n=100
trials (for algorithms with

d\!\leq\!8
, reduced to
n\!=\!50
for
d\!=\!16
and
n\!=\!20
for

d\!=\!64
, to keep CQEC
\mathcal{O}(d^{6})
cost tractable). We test
whether CQEC fidelity exceeds noisy fidelity using the one-sided
paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test
[
39
]
. With 28

\gamma
-sweep tests and 16 path
\times
algorithm tests
(
44
tests total), the Bonferroni-corrected threshold is

\alpha_{\mathrm{per-test}}=0.05/44=1.1\!\times\!10^{-3}
. Only
p-values below this threshold are interpreted as significant. We note
that a Benjamini–Hochberg (FDR) correction, which is less
conservative, would leave all our flagship claims significant and
only reclassify a handful of marginal-effect tests; we adopt Bonferroni
because it is the stricter standard.

3.9
Hybrid pipeline v2 (multi-copy SWAP bridge)

The v1 hybrid pipeline of Sec. III.E projects the reservoir density
matrix onto its diagonal before re-encoding, destroying the
off-diagonal coherences. We now introduce a v2 bridge based on
iterative SWAP-test projection onto the symmetric subspace:

\rho_{\mathrm{bridge}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{2}\!\bigl[\Pi_{s}(\rho_{\mathrm{res}}\!\otimes\!\rho_{\mathrm{res}})\Pi_{s}\bigr]/p
,
where
\Pi_{s}=(\mathbb{I}+\mathrm{SWAP})/2
is the symmetric projector
and
p\!=\!\mathrm{Tr}[\Pi_{s}(\rho\otimes\rho)]
. Two iterations are
used, followed by an eigendecomposition that retains the leading
eigenvector as the Path 2 input state. Implementation:

hybrid_pipeline_v2.py
.

required
to preserve coherence,
though each still adds value.

6.2
CQEC versus fault tolerance

A note on the relationship between CQEC and fault tolerance:

CQEC is not fault tolerance.
It is a low-overhead, non-topological
coherence-preservation method that is most useful in the
dissipation-limited pre-threshold regime. It cannot, on its own,
drive logical error rates below an arbitrarily low ceiling as
concatenated or topological codes can. What our results show is that
CQEC and physical protection together lift the effective error rate
below the EB threshold in the regime where an organic system naturally
sits (Paths 2–4), and in the noisier reservoir regime of Path 1 it
recovers a substantial fraction of the lost fidelity. A full
fault-tolerant layer (e.g. a surface code
[
24
]
) on top
of CQEC-protected organic qubits is outside the scope of the present
paper.

6.3
When does CQEC help most?

Figure
6
identifies the CQEC effectiveness peak at

\gamma\!\simeq\!\gamma_{c}
as a universal feature across algorithms
with
\Delta F_{\mathrm{max}}\!\propto\!\log d
. This observation
strengthens the proposal of the 3-LQBH preprint
[
38
]

that the Petz recovery map is an
information-theoretic

complement to the
raw channel when the latter is on the brink of being
entanglement-breaking.

6.4
Why the hybrid pipeline underperforms

Our current bridge from Path 1 to Path 2
(Sec.
3
) projects the reservoir density matrix onto
its diagonal before re-encoding. This necessarily loses the
off-diagonal coherences that carry most of the phase information,
hence the degradation seen in Fig.
16
at medium and
high noise. Two improvements are suggested by our data:
(i) preserving the off-diagonal magnitudes via a multi-copy SWAP test
bridge; (ii) training an auxiliary ridge map between the reservoir
feature vector and the Path 2 input state. Both are out of scope for
this paper.

6.5
Limitations

Three limitations deserve emphasis:
(i) all results are density-matrix simulations; real device-level
fidelities will be lower due to control-field imperfections, crosstalk,
and readout noise not modelled here.
(ii) Path 3 relies on SVILC physics that remains experimentally
unconfirmed in cuprates
[
37
,
26
]
; our

\kappa
-(BEDT-TTF) extension is doubly speculative.
(iii) Our multi-copy SWAP-based hybrid bridge (Sec. III.H) is not the
information-theoretically optimal bridge; a full quantum channel
simulation would require additional ancillas and is left to future
work.

Auxiliary caveats documented for completeness: CQEC cost scales as

\mathcal{O}(d^{6})
per round, limiting us to
d\!\leq\!64
; photoswitch
dynamics is modelled by a single exponential
[
19
]
;
inter-bridge
J
variation is not modelled; the room-temperature
PTM
T_{2}
assumption of 3
\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}
is conservative compared to
the
T_{2}\!\sim\!148\,$\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}$
reported by
Schäfter
et al.

[
34
]
for PTM biradicals

below 100 K
, but allows for the order-of-magnitude
shortening expected at room temperature and from COF-induced
dipolar coupling
[
7
]
.

7
Conclusion

We have demonstrated, within a unified density-matrix simulation
framework that re-uses the 3-Layer Quantum Brain and
covariant-purification CQEC codebases, that quantum computing and
quantum reservoir computing can be performed in engineered organic
materials
without any applied magnetic field
, at room
temperature (P1, P2, P4) or at 4 K (P3), and that such systems
satisfy all eight Wakaura–Koizumi SVILC qubit conditions while
reducing manufacturing cost by factor of 10–40 and operating power
by factor of 10–200 relative to superconducting quantum
computers
[
2
,
18
,
6
]
.

All numerical results are fully reproducible with random seed 42. Code
is released together with this manuscript (Appendix
A
).

(i) The
\gamma_{c}
-peak — a universal maximum of the CQEC fidelity

\log_{2}d

and five quantum algorithms, algorithmically confirming the

[

]

(ii) Bernstein–Vazirani at

demonstrates a

(iii) a gauge-invariant phase-frustration proxy for the SVILC
current-coupler survives the transition from a square cuprate lattice
to an anisotropic triangular
\kappa
-(BEDT-TTF) lattice;
(iv) the projected 100-qubit prototype cost ($0.4 M–$2 M) is
one-to-two orders of magnitude below a comparable superconducting
system;
(v) the entire simulation pipeline is packaged as the open-source

organic-qc-bench
Python distribution (MIT licence; built as

Appendix B
Derivation of the CQEC recovery efficiency

The Petz recovery map for the organic noise channel

\mathcal{N}\!=\!\mathcal{E}_{\delta}\!\circ\!\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}
is

\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}(\omega)=\sigma^{1/2}\mathcal{N}^{\!\dagger}\!\bigl(\mathcal{N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}\omega\,\mathcal{N}(\sigma)^{-1/2}\bigr)\sigma^{1/2}

[
31
]
.
When the reference
\sigma
is replaced by a noisy purified catalyst

\rho_{\mathrm{cat}}
of purity
P=\mathrm{Tr}\rho_{\mathrm{cat}}^{2}
,
the off-diagonal recovery reduces at leading order to the sigmoid
form of Eq. (
3
) with

\eta_{ij}=1-\exp[-|\rho_{\mathrm{cat,ij}}|\,d\,P]
, which is the
formula implemented in
cqec_recovery
.

Appendix C
Feed-current vector potential

A current flowing between source
\mathbf{r}_{s}
and sink

\mathbf{r}_{d}
produces a Peierls-phase vector-potential contribution

A_{ij}=J_{\mathrm{ext}}\,(\mathbf{u}_{ij}\!\cdot\!\hat{v})\,e^{-r/r_{c}}
,
where
\mathbf{u}_{ij}\!=\!\mathbf{r}_{j}\!-\!\mathbf{r}_{i}
,

\hat{v}=(\mathbf{r}_{d}-\mathbf{r}_{s})/\|{\cdot}\|
, and

r\!=\!\|\mathbf{r}_{ij}^{\mathrm{mid}}-\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{mid}}\|
.
This is the triangular-lattice analogue of
Ref.
[
37
]
’s Eq. (14).
