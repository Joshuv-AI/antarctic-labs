Squeezed Phonon Lasing via Floquet-Controlled Solid-State Defects
Hugo Molinares, Gianluca Rastelli, Victor Montenegro, Vitalie Eremeev

Abstract
We propose a general Floquet-engineered scheme for phonon lasing that enables a continuous transition from conventional lasing to phase-locked squeezed phonon lasing. Focusing on a solid-state platform based on color centers embedded in a circular hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) membrane, we demonstrate that a mechanical oscillator coupled to principal and ancilla spins, and controlled via effective Floquet driving simultaneously exhibits squeezed-state amplification and cooling dynamics, leading to the emergence of a stable squeezed phonon laser. We analyse the steady-state properties of the system, including the lasing threshold, mechanical occupation, emission spectrum and second-order correlations. Furthermore, we show that Floquet engineering can realize phase-locked lasing while enabling controlled quadrature squeezing, thereby providing a simple yet effective route toward squeezed lasing in quantum mechanical systems. Our results offer new insights into the generation of squeezed phonon lasers in solid-state platforms, with potential applications in quantum metrology.

Crucially, the interplay between lasing and squeezing requires sophisticated control architectures. Periodic driving has been identified as a powerful mechanism to induce nonclassical lasing, enabling direct emission into squeezed states in circuit quantum electrodynamics platforms
47
, while recent optical experiments achieved squeezed lasing using a reservoir-engineered parametric oscillator

;
7

Here, solid-state spin-defect systems offer a distinct architectural advantage: the intrinsic spin-phonon coupling provides a native nonlinearity that can be harnessed via Floquet engineering without requiring external cavities. Furthermore, hBN’s layered nature allows seamless integration into van der Waals heterostructures, and its spin defects have already been successfully deployed as versatile quantum sensors
28
;
58
;
69
. These characteristics make hBN membranes an ideal testbed for realizing squeezed phonon lasing and, simultaneously, for translating this state into a practical sensing resource.

In this work, we address the challenge of generating a squeezed phonon lasing by introducing a general Floquet-engineered scheme that enables a continuous transition from phase-diffused phonon lasing to phase-locked squeezed phonon lasing. We combine periodic driving techniques
13
with engineered effective spin-mechanical interactions to realize both amplification and squeezing within a unified framework. As a concrete implementation, we consider a solid-state platform based on color centers embedded in a circular hBN membrane

;
65

Under suitable resonance conditions, the system exhibits strong squeezed amplification of the mechanical motion induced by the primary spins, while the ancilla spins act as a versatile control knob, stabilizing the lasing regime and tuning key observables. Furthermore, we demonstrate that our specific Floquet-control protocol intrinsically induces phase locking

, a mechanism that stabilizes the oscillator phase without external feedback and thereby enables robust, steady-state squeezed phonon lasing. Taken together, our results establish a unified and highly tunable route for the preparation of nonclassical mechanical states through Floquet engineering, advancing the broader agenda of nonlinear phononics and opening new avenues for quantum sensing, magnetometry, and metrology in 2D materials

This paper is organized as follows: In Sect.
II
, we introduce a generic theoretical model consisting of two interacting spins, only one of which is coupled to a mechanical mode. We show how suitably chosen drives applied to the spin not coupled to the mechanical mode, together with a controlled modulation of the spin-spin interaction, enable a transition from conventional amplification to squeezed phonon amplification. In Sect.
III

With this architecture, we demonstrate that the mechanical motion can reach a regime of coherent squeezed phonon lasing. We further analyze the properties of this squeezed phonon laser, including threshold behavior, phonon statistics, and linewidth narrowing. In Sect.

IV
Spin-Controlled Phase-Locked Squeezed Phonon Lasing

In a conventional laser, the system may select a phase that does not remain stable over long times, due to phase diffusion (Schawlow-Townes linewidth)
60

Introducing an external perturbation, such as a weak coherent field, explicitly breaks the symmetry
21
(see details in the SM) and leads to phase locking, thereby reducing the effective phase diffusion.

In this section, we propose a scheme in which phase-locked squeezed phonon lasing can be achieved without the need to introduce an additional drive into the original system. Instead, we consider a controlled drive between spins that induces a correlated coupling, which in turn stabilizes the phase and enables phase-locked phonon lasing.

IV.2.1
Squeezing witness

The degree of squeezing of the phonon mode is quantified by the variance of the generalized quadrature operator

\hat{p}=\hat{X}_{\pi/2}

In Fig.

In the standard mechanical basis, the coherent state turns into a squeezed coherent state as shown in inset Fig.
4
(b)

The green dashed contour highlights the squeezed profile, while the white dashed contour corresponds to a coherent state with the same amplitude, therefore emphasizing the non-classical character of the lasing steady state.

This result demonstrates that the squeezed lasing behavior emerging from the proposed model exhibits a strong resilience to thermal noise, suggesting its practical viability even under realistic experimental conditions where complete ground-state cooling of the mechanical mode may not be achievable.

V
Conclusions

In this work, we present a general Floquet-engineered framework for phonon lasing that enables a continuous transition from conventional lasing to phase-locked squeezed phonon lasing. By considering a solid-state platform based on color centers embedded in a circular hBN membrane, we have shown that the interplay between local spin driving, periodic spin-spin interactions and spin-mechanical coupling gives rise to a rich dynamical regime where squeezed amplification and squeezed cooling processes coexist.

Our analysis of the steady-state properties demonstrates that the system exhibits clear signatures of phonon lasing, including a well-defined threshold, enhanced mechanical occupation, a narrowed power spectrum, and nontrivial second-order correlations. Importantly, we have shown that Floquet engineering provides a versatile mechanism to control both the amplitude and phase of the emitted phonon field, enabling the stabilization of phase-locked lasing together with controllable quadrature squeezing. These results establish a unified route toward the realization and control of squeezed phonon lasers in solid-state platforms, bridging the gap between conventional phonon lasing and nonclassical mechanical states.

Furthermore, the sensitivity of the lasing dynamics to externally controlled fields suggests potential future applications in quantum sensing. More broadly, our findings may open new avenues for nonlinear phononics, quantum acoustics, and integrated solid-state quantum technologies.

S2.1
Engineering squeezing amplification

To induce the creation of a squeezed state in the mechanical oscillator via the dynamical spin-spin coupling, we drive both the red- and blue-sideband transitions. Without loss of generality, we introduce the dimensionless parameter

\chi\equiv 2\varepsilon/\nu
. Using the frequencies

\omega_{m}=1
,
\nu=2\omega_{m}
, and

\Omega=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\Delta_{j}+\omega_{m}
,
we fix the Bessel coefficients to
\beta^{\prime}=0
,
\delta^{\prime}=1
and
\alpha^{\prime}\notin\mathbb{Z}
. The remaining Bessel coefficients become significantly larger because
\Delta_{1}\gg\{\Delta_{2},\omega_{m},\nu\}
and
\Delta_{2}\gg\nu
. Under these conditions, Eq. (

) yields the effective Hamiltonian

(S29)

with the definition of the normalized parameters:

\displaystyle\mathrm{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{J}_{0}(\chi)}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{J}^{2}_{0}(\chi)-\mathcal{J}^{2}_{1}(\chi)|}},

(S30)

\displaystyle\mathrm{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{J}_{1}(\chi)}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{J}^{2}_{0}(\chi)-\mathcal{J}^{2}_{1}(\chi)|}},

(S31)

\displaystyle g_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}_{\text{eff}}\sqrt{|\mathcal{J}^{2}_{0}(\chi)-\mathcal{J}^{2}_{1}(\chi)|}.

and

In this section, we evaluate how the spin-motion and spin-spin coupling parameters used in the main text can be experimentally realized:

Spin-motion coupling
. Based on Ref.
1
, the spin-motion coupling is defined as:

are the Bohr magneton and the electron gyromagnetic ratio, respectively. The quantity

, where
\Psi(r_{j},\theta_{j})
is the normalized mode profile. Here,

is the effective mass of the membrane, with
R
the membrane radius,
\rho
the mass density, and
h
the membrane thickness.

is the mechanical mode profile function. For the fundamental mode, it takes the form
\Psi(r)=C_{0}J_{0}(\alpha_{0}\frac{r}{R})

J_{0}
is the Bessel function of the first kind and
\alpha_{0}=2.4048

C_{0}
is a normalization constant. Without loss of generality, we consider a magnetic field gradient with a typical decaying spatial profile,

(used in Ref.
1
), where
d_{0}\sim 20\,\mu m
is the tip distance below the center of membrane. On the other hand, the mechanical frequency is given by:

\omega_{m}=2.4\,\sqrt{\frac{T_{0}}{\rho hR^{2}}},

T_{0}
is the tension membrane. For hBN membranes, we consider the parameters
\rho=2100\,\mathrm{kg/m^{3}}

T_{0}=0.00583\,\mathrm{N/m}
