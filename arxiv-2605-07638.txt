Relative 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractibility of smooth schemes
Adrien Dubouloz, Krishna Kumar Madhavan Vijayalakshmi, Paul Arne Østvær

Abstract
. We study smooth morphisms f : X → S f\colon X\to S that are 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractible in the unstable 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -homotopy category ℋ ⁡ ( S ) \mathcal{H}(S) . For base schemes S S of finite Krull dimension, we show that 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractibility is a fiberwise property: such a morphism is 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractible if and only if all its geometric fibers are 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractible. We apply this criterion to 𝔸 n \mathbb{A}^{n} -fiber spaces, obtaining a geometric description of their 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractibility in terms of local factorizations as towers of torsors under vector bundles, building on results of Asanuma. In low relative dimensions, we establish rigidity results. In relative dimension 1 1 , 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractible morphisms over normal bases are precisely Zariski locally trivial 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -bundles. In relative dimension 2 2 , we show that over bases with characteristic zero residue fields, 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractible morphisms are 𝔸 2 \mathbb{A}^{2} -fiber spaces, and we obtain Zariski local triviality under additional hypotheses on the base. We also exhibit counterexamples in positive and mixed characteristic, and formulate open problems concerning the existence of exotic 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -contractible surfaces.

Introduction

A central theme in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory is to understand how motivic homotopy types reflect the geometry of algebraic varieties. Although
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible varieties are indistinguishable from a point in the unstable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category, they can exhibit rich and subtle geometry, especially in families. This leads to natural questions: is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility detected fiberwise for smooth morphisms? What geometric constraints does it impose? And how does it interact with classical structures such as affine fibrations and vector bundle torsors?

While these questions have been extensively studied over fields, their relative counterparts remain largely unexplored. In particular,
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility over higher-dimensional bases—such as Dedekind schemes—has not been systematically investigated. One aim of this paper is to develop a structural theory of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible morphisms over a general base.

A guiding analogy comes from topology: fiber bundles with contractible fibers are typically homotopy equivalent to the base, and often trivial under mild hypotheses. Thus contractibility tends to eliminate obstructions and impose strong local structure. We show that a similar principle holds in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory: for smooth morphisms,
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility is a fiberwise property.

Algebraic varieties that are weakly contractible in the sense of Morel–Voevodsky
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory have been intensively studied in recent years. In dimensions
d\geq 3
, this has led to the construction of many smooth
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible varieties not isomorphic to affine space, including families with positive-dimensional moduli (see
[
10
]
for a survey).
In low dimensions, however,
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility exhibits strong rigidity. The affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is the only
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible quasi-projective curve. In dimension
2
, it was only recently shown that, over fields of characteristic zero, the affine plane
\mathbb{A}^{2}
is the unique smooth
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible affine surface
[
17
]
. In positive characteristic, the existence of exotic
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible surfaces remains open.

In this work, we study the
relative
form of these questions. Given a base scheme
S
, we investigate smooth morphisms
f\colon X\to S
that are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible over
S
, i.e. isomorphisms in the unstable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category
\mathcal{H}(S)
. Our starting point is the observation that, under mild hypotheses,
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility is entirely controlled by the fibers.

Main Results.

(1)

Fiberwise characterization.

Let
S
be a qcqs scheme of finite Krull dimension and let
f\colon X\to S
be a smooth morphism. Then
f
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible if and only if, for every point
s\in S
, the fiber
X_{s}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible over
\kappa(s)
.

(2)

Structure of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber spaces.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be an
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber space over a locally noetherian scheme. Under mild hypotheses (e.g.
f
affine or
S
regular),
X
admits Zariski locally a factorization as a tower of torsors under vector bundles.

(3)

Low-dimensional rigidity.

»

In relative dimension
1
,
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible morphisms over normal bases are precisely Zariski locally trivial
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-bundles.

»

In relative dimension
2
, and over base schemes with characteristic zero residue fields,
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility forces the structure of an
\mathbb{A}^{2}
-fiber space. Over Dedekind schemes, such morphisms are Zariski locally trivial provided they are affine.

(4)

Pathologies in positive and mixed characteristic.

In positive and mixed characteristic, the picture changes markedly. We exhibit
\mathbb{A}^{2}
-fiber spaces that are not Zariski locally trivial, showing that natural triviality results for affine fibrations fail in this setting. In addition, we exhibit failures of cancellation: there exist smooth
S
-schemes
X
with
X\times_{S}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}\cong\mathbb{A}^{3}_{S}
that are nevertheless not isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}
. These pathologies contrast sharply with the rigidity observed in characteristic zero and lead us to formulate open questions concerning the existence of exotic
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible surfaces.

The first result refines the analogous criterion in stable motivic homotopy theory and relies on a combination of base change techniques and a gluing argument originating in
[
41
]
. It shows that
\mathcal{H}(S)
does not detect variation in the isomorphism types of the fibers of a smooth family of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible varieties. Consequently, all known smooth non-isotrivial families of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible varieties of relative dimension
d\geq 3
over positive-dimensional bases
S
(see, for example,
[
22
,
23
,
37
]
) are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible over
S
.
The second result builds on Asanuma’s work on quasi-polynomial algebras
[
3
,
4
]
and provides a geometric explanation for the ubiquity of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible morphisms arising from affine fibrations.
The remaining problems concern, on the one hand, the classification of relatively
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible morphisms of relative dimension at most
2
, and, on the other hand, the local structure of such morphisms in the Zariski and Nisnevich topologies.

A central theme of the paper is the contrast between low and high dimensions. While dimension
\geq 3
admits abundant nontrivial families of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible varieties, dimensions
\leq 2
exhibit strong rigidity. This dichotomy parallels classical phenomena in topology and affine algebraic geometry.

Strategy of proof.

The fiberwise characterization of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible morphisms is obtained by combining base change properties in the unstable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category with a gluing argument along a stratification of the base, ultimately reducing to the case of local schemes. This argument may be viewed as an unstable refinement of analogous results in stable motivic homotopy theory.

The structural results for
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber spaces rely on Asanuma’s description of affine fibrations in terms of quasi-polynomial algebras, together with geometric input from vector bundle torsors and the Jouanolou–Thomason’s trick. This allows us to make the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility of such morphisms more explicit.

S
The low-dimensional classification results combine the fiberwise criterion with classical results in affine algebraic geometry, including the work of Kambayashi, Miyanishi–Sugie, and Russell in dimension
2
, and Sathaye’s theorem on affine fibrations over Dedekind schemes. The analysis of positive and mixed characteristic examples highlights the limitations of these methods and motivates the open problems we formulate.

An ingredient in our approach is the use of techniques introduced in
[
21
]
, which make it possible to study
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy types through the geometry at infinity. These methods relate
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility to the structure of compactifications via the stable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy type of punctured tubular neighborhoods of the boundary. This viewpoint yields strong geometric constraints, and in particular provides a new mechanism for proving affineness and rigidity results for
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible surfaces.

Structure of the paper.

Section 1 recalls the unstable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category and develops the functorial tools needed for our arguments, particularly concerning base change and localization. In Section 2, we study
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber spaces and show that their
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility admits a concrete geometric description in terms of iterated vector bundle torsors. Section 3 focuses on low-dimensional phenomena: we obtain a complete picture in relative dimension
1
, and in dimension
2
we establish rigidity results in characteristic zero while highlighting pathologies in positive and mixed characteristic, leading to several open questions.

\pi_{n}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(f_{U_{\bullet}}):\pi_{n}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(Y_{U_{\bullet}})\to\pi_{n}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X_{U_{\bullet}}).

Combining this with hyperdescent yields an isomorphism

-weak equivalence in

2.
Relative
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber spaces

Flat morphisms of finite presentation with fibers isomorphic to affine spaces have long occupied a central place in affine algebraic geometry, notably in connection with the Zariski Cancellation Problem and the Dolgachev–Weisfeiler Problem; see, for instance,
[
36
]
for an overview. From the motivic viewpoint, such morphisms furnish a particularly rich source of examples of relatively
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible schemes. We begin by recalling the relevant terminology.

Definition 2.1
.

An
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber space
over a scheme
S
is a smooth morphism of finite presentation
f:X\to S
such that, for every point
s\in S
, the fiber
X_{s}=X\times_{S}\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(s))
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\kappa(s)}
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber space
f:X\to S
is called a
locally trivial
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-bundle
in the Zariski (resp. Nisnevich, resp. étale) topology if every point
s\in S
admits a Zariski (resp. Nisnevich, resp. étale) neighborhood
U
such that

U\times_{S}X\cong U\times_{S}\mathbb{A}^{n}_{S}

as schemes over
U
.

Remark 2.3
.

This notion coincides with what is often called an "
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fibration" or "affine fibration" in the classical literature; see, e.g.,
[
46
,
4
,
15
,
20
]
. Other authors, such as
[
33
,
34
]
, adopt weaker hypotheses, requiring only that the general closed fibers be isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{n}
and that all fibers be geometrically integral. Our terminology is chosen to avoid confusion with topological or model-categorical notions of fibration.

The following statement is an immediate consequence of
Section

1.2
.

Theorem 2.4
.

An
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber space
f:X\to S
over a qcqs scheme
S
of finite Krull dimension is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence in
\mathrm{Spc}_{S}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
.

The next result, based on Asanuma’s description of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber spaces between noetherian affine schemes
[
3
, Theorem 4.5]
and
[
4
, Theorem 3.4]
, provides a more precise structural picture, making the relative
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility of such fiber spaces geometrically explicit.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
f:X\to S
be an
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber space over a locally noetherian scheme
S
. Assume that either
f
is affine, or that
f
is separated and
S
is regular. Then for every noetherian Zariski affine open subset
U\subset S
there exist an integer
m
and a commutative diagram

\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\textstyle{\mathbb{A}^{m}_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{pr}_{U}}
\textstyle{X_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{U}}
\textstyle{U}

in which
\rho:V\to X_{U}
is a Zariski locally trivial torsor under a vector bundle
F\to X_{U}
with affine total space, and
\pi:\mathbb{A}^{m}_{U}\to V
is a vector bundle.

Proof.

First assume that
f
is affine. For every noetherian affine open subset
U\subset S
, the scheme
X_{U}=f^{-1}(U)
is affine and
f_{U}:X_{U}\to U
is an
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-fiber space. Since
f_{U}
is smooth, the sheaf of relative Kähler differentials
\Omega_{X_{U}/U}
is locally free. By
[
3
, Theorem 4.5]
, there exists a vector bundle
\pi:W\to X_{U}
whose total space
W
is isomorphic over
U
to
\mathbb{A}^{m}_{U}
for some
m\geq 0
. The assertion follows by taking
\rho:V\to X_{U}
to be the identity.

Now assume that
f
is separated and that
S
is regular, and let
U=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
be a Zariski affine open subset of
S
. Since
A
is regular and
f_{U}:X_{U}\to U
is a smooth separated morphism of finite presentation, the Jouanolou–Thomason homotopy lemma (see, e.g.,
[
10
, Lemma 3.1.4]
) yields a Zariski locally trivial torsor

\rho:V\to X_{U}

under a vector bundle
p:E\to X_{U}
of rank
r
with affine total space
V
. The composite
h:=f_{U}\circ\rho:V\to U
is a smooth affine morphism of finite presentation.

For each point
s\in U
, the induced torsor

\rho_{s}:V_{s}\to X_{s}

is trivial since
X_{s}
is affine. Moreover, by the Quillen–Suslin theorem

[
42
,
49
]
, the vector bundle
E_{s}
on
X_{s}\cong\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\kappa(s)}
is trivial.
It follows that
\rho_{s}
is isomorphic to the trivial
\mathbb{A}^{r}
-bundle

\mathrm{pr}_{2}:\mathbb{A}^{r}_{\kappa(s)}\times_{\kappa(s)}\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\kappa(s)}\to\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\kappa(s)},

and hence that
h:V\to U
is an affine
\mathbb{A}^{n+r}
-fiber space. The conclusion now follows from the affine case.
∎

3.1.
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility and
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-rigidity of étale schemes

We begin with the zero-dimensional case.

Proposition 3.1
.

An étale
S
-scheme
f:X\to S
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible if and only if
f
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Every isomorphism is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence. Conversely, suppose that
f:X\to S
is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence in
\mathrm{Spc}_{S}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
. By
Section

1.2
(1), for every point
s\in S
with residue field
\kappa(s)
, the base change

f_{s}:X_{s}=X\times_{S}\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(s))\to\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(s))

is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence in
\mathrm{Spc}_{\kappa(s)}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
. Since
f_{s}
is étale of finite presentation,
X_{s}
is a finite disjoint union of spectra of finite separable extensions of
\kappa(s)
.

Because
X_{s}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible, it is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected, hence connected as a topological space, and by

3.3.
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility of smooth schemes of relative dimension 2

Relative dimension
2
is the first setting in which
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility exhibits genuinely new phenomena. Unlike the case of curves, where a complete classification is available, the interaction between
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory and the birational geometry of surfaces leads to a much richer and only partially understood picture.

Over fields of characteristic zero, recent advances show that
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility is rigid: smooth
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible surfaces are isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{2}
. This result combines techniques from
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory with deep structure theorems for affine surfaces.
In positive characteristic, however, several key ingredients break down. In particular, separability issues obstruct the use of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-curves, and existing methods do not rule out the existence of exotic
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible surfaces.

In the relative setting, new phenomena already appear over one-dimensional bases. While
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility forces the structure of an
\mathbb{A}^{2}
-fiber space under the assumption that the residue fields have characteristic zero, the problem of Zariski local triviality remains widely open beyond special cases (e.g. Sathaye’s theorem for Dedekind schemes in characteristic zero). Moreover, counterexamples in mixed characteristic show that naive generalizations fail. These considerations lead to the following guiding questions:

»

Are there smooth affine
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible surfaces in positive characteristic that are not isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{2}
?

»

When is an
\mathbb{A}^{2}
-fiber space locally trivial in the Zariski topology?

»

To what extent do cancellation and rigidity phenomena persist in relative dimension
2
?

Smooth
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible surfaces over a field

All known results concerning the characterization of the affine plane
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
among surfaces over a perfect field
k
(with fixed algebraic closure
\bar{k}
) ultimately rely on the following theorem, which synthesizes classical work of Kambayashi
[
35
]
, Miyanishi–Sugie
[

]
, and Russell
[
45
]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

A smooth separated surface

of finite type over a perfect field

is isomorphic to

if and only if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

is connected and connected at infinity, i.e., for every proper completion

\bar{k}
-scheme;

(2)

and

, the latter is a trivial

. Combining this result with

3.3
yields the following characterization, which slightly strengthens
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
:

Theorem 3.12
.

A smooth separated surface
X
of finite type over a field
k
of characteristic zero is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible if and only if it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
.

Proof.

The statement is proved in
[
17
]
under the additional assumption that
X
is affine; this hypothesis implies, via the Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem, that
X_{\bar{k}}
is connected at infinity. Our only contribution is the observation that
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility of
X
already implies that
X
is affine (see
Section

3.3
below).

We briefly recall the argument of
[
17
]
under the affineness assumption. Since
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible over
k
, its base change
X_{\bar{k}}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible over
\bar{k}
by
Section

1.2
(a). In particular,
X_{\bar{k}}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected, hence connected as a scheme, and therefore admits, by the aforementioned result of Choudhury–Roy, a dominant generically finite morphism
\varphi:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{C}\to X_{\bar{k}}
. As
k
has characteristic zero, this morphism is automatically separable. Moreover,
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility of
X_{\bar{k}}
implies that
\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\bar{k}})
is trivial and that
H^{0}(X_{\bar{k}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{\bar{k}}}^{*})=\bar{k}^{*}
. The conclusion now follows from
Section

3.3
.
∎

In the proof of
Section

3.3
, we used the following result, which may be viewed as an algebro-geometric counterpart of a classical theorem of Fujita
[
27
, p. 512, Theorem, item 3]
, asserting that a smooth complex algebraic surface whose analytification is topologically contractible is connected at infinity and, in fact, affine.

Proposition 3.13
.

A smooth separated
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible surface of finite type over a field
k
is affine.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the base change
X_{\bar{k}}
to an algebraic closure
\bar{k}
of
k
is affine. Since
X_{\bar{k}}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible by
Section

1.2
(a), we may assume from the outset that
k
is algebraically closed. We use the notion of the stable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy type at infinity introduced in
[
21
]
. Because
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible in the unstable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category
\mathcal{H}(k)
, its
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-suspension spectrum
\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}(X,x)
(for any
k
-rational point
x\in X
) is contractible in the pointed stable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category
\mathrm{SH}_{\bullet}(k)
.

Let
X\hookrightarrow\bar{X}
be a completion into a proper
k
-scheme. Using resolution of singularities for surfaces, we may assume that
\bar{X}
is smooth and that the boundary
\partial X=\bar{X}\smallsetminus X
is a divisor with simple normal crossings. By
[
21
, Example 4.3.8]
, the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractibility of
\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}(X,x)
implies that the stable punctured tubular neighborhood
\mathrm{Tub}^{\times}(\bar{X},\partial X)
has the same stable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy type as the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-suspension spectrum of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
. Using the description of
\mathrm{Tub}^{\times}(\bar{X},\partial X)
in terms of the irreducible components of
\partial X
(see
[
21
, Section 4.2]
), it follows that
\partial X
is connected. The same argument as in
[
27
, p. 512, Theorem, item 3]
then shows that
\partial X
supports an effective ample divisor on
\bar{X}
, and hence
X
is affine.
∎

It follows from the proof of
Section

3.3
that any smooth separated
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible surface
X
of finite type over a perfect field
k
(with fixed algebraic closure
\bar{k}
) is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
, provided that
X_{\bar{k}}
admits a dominant
separable
morphism
\varphi:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{C}\to X_{\bar{k}}
for some
\bar{k}
-curve
C
.

As explained above, a principal result of
[
17
,
18
]
asserts that the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness of
X_{\bar{k}}
guarantees the existence of a dominant generically finite morphism
\varphi:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{C}\to X_{\bar{k}}
. However, in positive characteristic, the methods of
loc. cit.
do not ensure that such a morphism can be chosen to be separable. The following example shows that this issue is genuine: in positive characteristic, there exist smooth
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected affine surfaces which do not admit any dominant generically finite
separable
morphism
\varphi:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{C}\to X
.

Example 3.14
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, and consider
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x,y])
. Let
\alpha_{p}=\operatorname{Spec}(k[t]/(t^{p}))
act freely on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
via

(x,y)\longmapsto\bigl(x+t(p+1)y^{p},\;y+t((xy)^{p}+1)\bigr),

corresponding to the
p
-derivation
\partial=(p+1)y^{p}\partial_{x}-((xy)^{p}+1)\partial_{y}
of
k[x,y]
. The kernel
R
of
\partial
is generated by

u=x^{p},\quad v=y^{p},\quad w=(xy+1)^{p}x+y^{p+1},

and the quotient morphism

\pi:\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}\to X:=\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}/\alpha_{p}=\operatorname{Spec}(R)

is an
\alpha_{p}
-torsor. The surface
X
is smooth over
k
, and can be identified with the hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{k}=\operatorname{Spec}(k[u,v,w])
defined by

w^{p}=(uv+1)^{p}u+v^{p+1}.

Moreover, the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{u,v}:X\to\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
exhibits
X
as a purely inseparable finite cover of degree
p
of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
, and the composition
\mathrm{pr}_{u,v}\circ\pi:\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}\to\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
coincides with the Frobenius morphism
(x,y)\mapsto(x^{p},y^{p})
.

The morphism
\pi:\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}\to X
is a purely inseparable universal homeomorphism. In particular,
X
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-chain connected, and hence
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected. Furthermore,
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})=k^{*}
, and since the only
\alpha_{p}
-linearized invertible sheaf on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
is the trivial one with trivial linearization, it follows that
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
is trivial.

On the other hand, by
[
39
, Example 1.8.3.2]
, a minimal smooth proper completion
\bar{X}
of
X
with boundary a simple normal crossing divisor is a projective surface of general type. In particular, the logarithmic Kodaira dimension of
X
satisfies
\bar{\kappa}(X)=2
. Consequently,
X
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
, and does not admit any dominant generically finite
separable
morphism
\varphi:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{C}\to X
.

We are currently unable to determine whether the surfaces
X
constructed in

3.3
are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible over
k
. In particular, the following question remains open:

Question 3.15
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of positive characteristic. Do there exist smooth affine surfaces over
k
which are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible but not isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
?

Remark 3.16
.

The affine plane
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
is known to admit nontrivial forms over any imperfect field
k
. For instance, for every such field
k
, the product
C\times_{k}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
, where
C
is a nontrivial purely inseparable form of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
, defines a purely inseparable
k
-form of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
. By
Section

1.2
, any such form either has no
k
-rational point or has a nontrivial Picard group, and is therefore not
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-contractible over
k
.

In contrast with the case of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
, very little is known about the classification of purely inseparable forms of
\mathbb{A}^{2}

\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
that admit a
k
-rational point and have trivial Picard group.

.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth affine morphism of finite presentation and relative dimension
2
over a Dedekind scheme
S
with residue fields of characteristic zero. The following are equivalent:

(1)

f
is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence in

(2)

\mathbb{A}^{2}

Corollary 3.19
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth affine morphism of finite presentation and relative dimension
2
over an
affine
Dedekind scheme
S
with residue fields of characteristic zero. Then
f
is isomorphic to the trivial
\mathbb{A}^{2}
-bundle
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}\to S
if and only if
f
is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence in
\mathrm{Spc}_{S}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
and the relative canonical sheaf
\omega_{f}
is trivial.

Proof.

One implication is immediate. Conversely, by
Section

3.3
, the morphism
f:X\to S
is a Zariski locally trivial
\mathbb{A}^{2}
-bundle. Since
S
is affine, it follows from
[
14
, Theorem 4.9]
that
f
is in fact isomorphic over
S
to a rank-
2
vector bundle

p:E=\operatorname{Spec}_{S}(\Sym^{\bullet}\mathcal{E})\to S

for some locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of rank
2
on
S
. As
S
is one-dimensional,
[
47
, Theorem 1]
implies that
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{O}_{S}\oplus\mathcal{L}
for some invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}
on
S
.

Since

\omega_{f}=\det\Omega_{E/S}\cong p^{*}\det(\mathcal{E})\cong p^{*}\mathcal{L},

and the pullback homomorphism
p^{*}:\operatorname{Pic}(S)\to\operatorname{Pic}(E)
is an isomorphism, the triviality of
\omega_{f}
is equivalent to the triviality of
\mathcal{L}
, and hence to the triviality of
\mathcal{E}
. This shows that
E\cong\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.20
.

Let
S
be an affine Dedekind scheme with residue fields of characteristic zero, and let
f:X\to S
be a smooth affine
S
-scheme of finite presentation and relative dimension
2
. If there exists
n\geq 0
such that

X\times_{S}\mathbb{A}^{n}_{S}\cong\mathbb{A}^{n+2}_{S}

as
S
-schemes, then
X\cong\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}
.

Proof.

Since
\omega_{\mathbb{A}^{m}_{S}/S}
is trivial for every

, the existence of an isomorphism

X\times_{S}\mathbb{A}^{n}_{S}\cong\mathbb{A}^{n+2}_{S}

implies that

\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}\omega_{f}\cong\omega_{X\times_{S}\mathbb{A}^{n}_{S}/S}\cong\omega_{\mathbb{A}^{n+2}_{S}/S}

is trivial. As
\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}:\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to\operatorname{Pic}(X\times_{S}\mathbb{A}^{n}_{S})
is an isomorphism, it follows that
\omega_{f}
itself is trivial.

Moreover, the same isomorphism shows that
f:X\to S
is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence in
\mathrm{Spc}_{S}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
. The conclusion now follows from
Section

3.3
.
∎
