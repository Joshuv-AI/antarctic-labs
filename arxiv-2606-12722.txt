Finite-sheeted Cauchy operator at rational corners
Louis Shuo Wang

Abstract
We study Cauchy singular integral operators on planar wedges whose opening angle is a rational multiple of π \pi . For θ = p ​ π / q \theta=p\pi/q , the covering w = ζ q w=\zeta^{q} yields an exact finite-sheeted factorization of the wedge Cauchy transform into 2 ​ q 2q interval Cauchy transforms with explicit algebraic recombination coefficients. The factorization is formulated on weighted conormal Hölder spaces. We prove that the lifting operator preserves conormal order, lowers the Hölder exponent from β \beta to β / q \beta/q , and has sharp ℓ 1 \ell^{1} sheet norm q q . Combining this operator factorization with a Mellin model for interval Cauchy transforms, we derive a mode-by-mode propagation rule for polyhomogeneous endpoint expansions. Nonresonant powers preserve their logarithmic order, while integer exponents raise it by one. The results also give a local singular decomposition for Cauchy operators on piecewise analytic curves with rational corner angles.

2.1
Elementary reduction

The factorization is based on a two-term reduction that is valid for any opening angle.

Proposition 1

(Rotation reduction)
.

Let
\theta\in(0,2\pi)
,
c>0
, and let
f
have side restrictions
(
3

f_{1}\in L^{1}(0,1)

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}f(z)=\mathcal{C}_{(0,1)}[f_{1}](z)+\mathcal{C}_{(0,c)}[f_{2}]\!\bigl(e^{-i\theta}z\bigr).

(10)

Proof.

The first side,
\zeta=r
,

, gives
\mathcal{C}_{(0,1)}[f_{1}](z)
. On the

\zeta=re^{i\theta}

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{c}\frac{f_{2}(r)\,e^{i\theta}}{re^{i\theta}-z}\,dr=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{c}\frac{f_{2}(r)}{r-ze^{-i\theta}}\,dr=\mathcal{C}_{(0,c)}[f_{2}]\!\bigl(e^{-i\theta}z\bigr).\qed

a>0
, let

C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\alpha}(0,a)=\bigl\{f(r)=r^{\alpha}h(r):\ (r\partial_{r})^{k}h\in C^{\beta}[0,a],\ 0\leq k\leq m_{0}\bigr\},\quad\|f\|_{C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\alpha}}=\sum_{k=0}^{m_{0}}\bigl\|(r\partial_{r})^{k}(r^{-\alpha}f)\bigr\|_{C^{\beta}[0,a]},

where
\|\phi\|_{C^{\beta}[0,a]}=\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+[\phi]_{\beta}
and

[\phi]_{\beta}=\sup_{x\neq y}|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|/|x-y|^{\beta}
. The wedge space is

C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{\theta,c})=C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\alpha_{1}}(0,1)\oplus C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\alpha_{2}}(0,c)
, with norm

\|F\|=\|f_{1}\|_{C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\alpha_{1}}}+\|f_{2}\|_{C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\alpha_{2}}}
,
acting on the side restrictions (
3
).

Assumption 6

(Vertex integrability)
.

\operatorname{Re}\alpha_{j}>-1
for
j=1,2
.

This is exactly the condition that (
2
) converge absolutely at
the vertex, and it is the standing hypothesis below.

3.2
The conormal mapping theorem

We first record the elementary Hölder behaviour of the root substitution.

Lemma 7

(Root composition)
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\beta}[0,a]
and
\psi(s)=s^{1/q}
on
[0,a^{q}]
. Then

\phi\circ\psi\in C^{\beta/q}[0,a^{q}]
and

\|\phi\circ\psi\|_{C^{\beta/q}[0,a^{q}]}\leq\|\phi\|_{C^{\beta}[0,a]}.

Proof.

The supremum is unchanged:
\|\phi\circ\psi\|_{L^{\infty}[0,a^{q}]}=\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}[0,a]}
. For the seminorm,
t\mapsto t^{1/q}
is

\tfrac{1}{q}
-Hölder with constant
1
on
[0,\infty)
, i.e.

|s_{1}^{1/q}-s_{2}^{1/q}|\leq|s_{1}-s_{2}|^{1/q}
; hence

|\phi(s_{1}^{1/q})-\phi(s_{2}^{1/q})|\leq[\phi]_{\beta}|s_{1}^{1/q}-s_{2}^{1/q}|^{\beta}\leq[\phi]_{\beta}|s_{1}-s_{2}|^{\beta/q}
, so

[\phi\circ\psi]_{\beta/q}\leq[\phi]_{\beta}
. Summing the two bounds gives the
claim.
∎

Lemma 8

(Conormal exponent transport)
.

Let
f_{j}(r)=r^{\alpha_{j}}h_{j}(r)\in C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\alpha_{j}}(0,a_{j})
,
a_{1}=1
,

a_{2}=c
, under Assumption
6
. Then for
m=0,\dots,q-1
,

g_{j,m}(s)=s^{\beta_{j,m}}H_{j}(s),\qquad H_{j}(s)=h_{j}\!\bigl(s^{1/q}\bigr),\qquad\beta_{j,m}:=\frac{\alpha_{j}+m+1}{q}-1,

(14)

with the exact conormal transformation rule

(s\partial_{s})^{k}H_{j}(s)=q^{-k}\,\bigl[(r\partial_{r})^{k}h_{j}\bigr]\!\bigl(s^{1/q}\bigr),\qquad 0\leq k\leq m_{0}.

(15)

Consequently
g_{j,m}\in C^{m_{0},\beta/q}_{\beta_{j,m}}(0,a_{j}^{q})
and

\operatorname{Re}\beta_{j,m}>-1
for all
m\geq 0
.

Proof.

Factorization (
14
) is immediate from (
7
). For
(
15
), the case
k=1
is the chain rule,

s\partial_{s}H_{j}=\tfrac{1}{q}s^{1/q}h_{j}^{\prime}(s^{1/q})=\tfrac{1}{q}[(r\partial_{r})h_{j}](s^{1/q})
,
and induction applies the
k=1
rule to
(r\partial_{r})^{k}h_{j}
. Since

(r\partial_{r})^{k}h_{j}\in C^{\beta}[0,a_{j}]
, Lemma
7
gives

(s\partial_{s})^{k}H_{j}\in C^{\beta/q}[0,a_{j}^{q}]
, whence the membership. Finally

\operatorname{Re}\beta_{j,m}=(\operatorname{Re}\alpha_{j}+m+1)/q-1>-1
iff
\operatorname{Re}\alpha_{j}+m+1>0
, which
holds for all
m\geq 0
under Assumption
6
.
∎

Theorem 9

(Conormal mapping theorem)
.

Under Assumption
6
, the lifting operator

\mathcal{L}_{q}:\ C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{\theta,c})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{2}\bigoplus_{m=0}^{q-1}C^{m_{0},\beta/q}_{\beta_{j,m}}(0,a_{j}^{q})

is bounded, with the explicit estimate, independent of
\alpha,\beta,a,m_{0}
,

\|\mathcal{L}_{q}F\|=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{q-1}\|g_{j,m}\|_{C^{m_{0},\beta/q}_{\beta_{j,m}}}\leq q\,\|F\|.

(16)

The sheet-count bound
q
is sharp:
\|\mathcal{L}_{q}\|=q
.
More precisely, for each fixed
j
all
q
components have equal norm,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|g_{j,m}\|_{C^{m_{0},\beta/q}_{\beta_{j,m}}}=\sum_{k=0}^{m_{0}}\bigl\|(s\partial_{s})^{k}H_{j}\bigr\|_{C^{\beta/q}}=&\sum_{k=0}^{m_{0}}q^{-k}\bigl\|[(r\partial_{r})^{k}h_{j}]\circ(\cdot)^{1/q}\bigr\|_{C^{\beta/q}}\\
\leq&\sum_{k=0}^{m_{0}}q^{-k}\bigl\|(r\partial_{r})^{k}h_{j}\bigr\|_{C^{\beta}}\leq\|f_{j}\|_{C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\alpha_{j}}}.\end{aligned}

(17)

Proof.

By (
14
) the conormal-reduced function of
g_{j,m}
is

s^{-\beta_{j,m}}g_{j,m}=H_{j}
, independent of
m
; hence the first equality in
(
17
). The second uses (
15
), and the third
is Lemma
7
applied to
\phi=(r\partial_{r})^{k}h_{j}
. Since

q^{-k}\leq 1
, the sum is at most
\sum_{k}\|(r\partial_{r})^{k}h_{j}\|_{C^{\beta}}=\|f_{j}\|_{C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\alpha_{j}}}
. Summing (
17
) over the

q
values of
m
and over
j=1,2
gives

\|\mathcal{L}_{q}F\|\leq q(\|f_{1}\|+\|f_{2}\|)=q\|F\|
. Sharpness: take
F=(f_{1},0)
with

f_{1}(r)=r^{\alpha_{1}}
, so
h_{1}\equiv 1
and
\|F\|=\|h_{1}\|_{C^{\beta}}=1
. Then

H_{1}\equiv 1
, every conormal derivative
(s\partial_{s})^{k}H_{1}
vanishes for

k\geq 1
, and
\|g_{1,m}\|=\|H_{1}\|_{C^{\beta/q}}=1
for each
m
; hence

\|\mathcal{L}_{q}F\|=q=q\|F\|
, so
\|\mathcal{L}_{q}\|=q
.
∎

Remark 2
.

The operator norm is exactly
q
and is independent of
\beta
,
a
, and

\alpha
: the only cost of resolving the corner is the
q
-fold multiplicity of
sheets. The Hölder exponent degrades from
\beta
to
\beta/q
, the
unavoidable price of the root substitution, while the conormal order
m_{0}
is
preserved exactly.

3.3
Interval Cauchy mapping and boundary values

After the lift one needs the mapping properties of the interval Cauchy operator.
We record a clean local version sufficient for the calculus; the strongest
singular-integral statements are not needed.

Proposition 10

(Interval Cauchy mapping)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
with
\operatorname{Re}\mu>-1
,
a>0
, and
g\in C^{0,\beta}_{\mu}(0,a)
.

1.

\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}[g]\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,a])
.

2.

(Separated bound.)
For compact
K\subset\mathbb{C}
with

d=\operatorname{dist}(K,[0,a])>0
,

\bigl\|\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}[g]\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi d}\,\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,a)}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi d}\,\frac{a^{\operatorname{Re}\mu+1}}{\operatorname{Re}\mu+1}\,\|g\|_{C^{0,\beta}_{\mu}}.

3.

(Endpoint asymptotics.)
The behaviour of
\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}[g](\xi)

as
\xi\to 0
off
[0,a]
is given by the polyhomogeneous model expansion
developed in §
4
, with leading term governed by the endpoint
exponent
\mu
; this is recorded here only as a pointer and is not used in the
proof of the present proposition.

4.

(Plemelj boundary values.)
On every compact subinterval

K\Subset(0,a)
the non-tangential limits
\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}^{\pm}[g]
exist, are
Hölder-
\beta
, and satisfy

\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}^{+}[g]-\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}^{-}[g]=g
on
K

Muskhelishvili and Radok 2008
;
Gakhov 2014
.

Proof.

(1) is standard differentiation under the integral for
\xi
off
[0,a]
. For
(2),
|r-\xi|\geq d
on
[0,a]
gives

|\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}[g](\xi)|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{a}|g|/|r-\xi|\,dr\leq\frac{1}{2\pi d}\|g\|_{L^{1}}
; writing
g=r^{\mu}\tilde{h}
with

\|\tilde{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|g\|_{C^{0,\beta}_{\mu}}
,

\|g\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|\tilde{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{a}r^{\operatorname{Re}\mu}dr=\|\tilde{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}a^{\operatorname{Re}\mu+1}/(\operatorname{Re}\mu+1)
, finite under

\operatorname{Re}\mu>-1
. Item (3) is a pointer to §
4

(Lemma
14
) and requires nothing here. (4) is the classical
Plemelj–Privalov theorem applied on a compact subinterval, where

g\in C^{\beta}
.
∎

Corollary 11

(Mapping of the wedge transform)
.

Under Assumption
6
, for
f\in C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{\theta,c})

the transform
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}f=\mathcal{E}_{p,q,\theta,c}\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}\mathcal{L}_{q}f
lies in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma_{\theta,c})
,
satisfies the separated bound on any compact
K
with
\operatorname{dist}(K,\Gamma_{\theta,c})>0
(with
constant controlled by
\|F\|
through (
16
) and the polynomial
factors of
\mathcal{E}_{p,q,\theta,c}
), and has the boundary values and jump described in
Theorem
12
.

Proof.

Each factor in (
13
) is bounded on the relevant space:

\mathcal{L}_{q}
by Theorem
9
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{int}}
by
Proposition
10
, and
\mathcal{E}_{p,q,\theta,c}
by composition with the
polynomial weights
z^{q-1-m}
,
(e^{-i\theta}z)^{q-1-m}
and the maps

z\mapsto z^{q}
,

.
∎

Plemelj jump relations

(Boundary values and jump relations)
.

Let

(18)

The vertex asymptotics of

Use the rotation reduction (

4
Mellin asymptotics and polyhomogeneous corner-mode propagation

4.1
Sectors and branch convention

The polyhomogeneous expansions established here are the transform-side
counterpart of the classical corner asymptotics for elliptic problems

Grisvard 2011
;
Kozlov et al. 1997
;
Dauge 2006
;
Costabel et al. 2003
;
Schulze 1990
: the densities carry expansions in
powers and logarithms of the distance to the vertex, and we determine how the
Cauchy operator propagates them. The decomposition (
6
) is
branch-free, but the vertex expansion below involves non-integer powers and
logarithms, which require a branch. The two rays of
\Gamma_{\theta,c}
divide a punctured
neighbourhood of
0
into the open sectors

\Sigma_{\mathrm{I}}=\{\,0<\arg z<\theta\,\},\qquad\Sigma_{\mathrm{II}}=\{\,\theta<\arg z<2\pi\,\}.

On each sector we use the determination of
\arg z
from these ranges and set

z^{\alpha}=|z|^{\alpha}e^{i\alpha\arg z}
,
\log z=\ln|z|+i\arg z
. For the
model interval transform we use the principal cut:
(-\xi)^{\alpha}=\exp(\alpha\operatorname{Log}(-\xi))
, where
\operatorname{Log}
is the principal logarithm on

\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
, so
(-\xi)^{\alpha}
is cut along
\xi\in(0,\infty)

and positive for
\xi<0
. All expansions are understood separately on

\Sigma_{\mathrm{I}}
and
\Sigma_{\mathrm{II}}
, with sector-dependent
coefficients; we write a generic sector label
\bullet\in\{\mathrm{I},\mathrm{II}\}
.

4.2
The Mellin model: pure powers

Lemma 13

(Model interval expansion)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
\operatorname{Re}\alpha>-1
,
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
a>0
. Then, as

\xi\to 0
off
[0,a]
,

\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}[s^{\alpha}](\xi)=m(\alpha)\,(-\xi)^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{a^{\alpha-n}}{\alpha-n}\,\xi^{n},\qquad m(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2i\sin\pi(\alpha+1)},

(19)

as an asymptotic expansion. For fixed
\xi\notin[0,a]
the left side is
holomorphic in
\alpha
on
\{\operatorname{Re}\alpha>-1\}
; the two singular contributions
at any
\alpha\to n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
have canceling simple poles, with combined
residue zero, and the value at
\alpha=n_{0}
contains the term

-\tfrac{1}{2\pi i}\xi^{n_{0}}\operatorname{Log}(-\xi)
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}[s^{\alpha}](\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}(I_{\infty}-I_{a})
with

I_{\infty}=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\alpha}(s-\xi)^{-1}ds
,

I_{a}=\int_{a}^{\infty}s^{\alpha}(s-\xi)^{-1}ds
. For
-1<\operatorname{Re}\alpha<0
, scaling

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\sigma-1}(x+1)^{-1}dx=\pi/\sin\pi\sigma
(
0<\operatorname{Re}\sigma<1
)
by
x=s/(-\xi)
,
\sigma=\alpha+1
, gives

I_{\infty}=\pi(-\xi)^{\alpha}/\sin\pi(\alpha+1)
. The uniform expansion

(s-\xi)^{-1}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\xi^{n}s^{-n-1}
on
s\geq a
integrates termwise to

I_{a}=-\sum_{n}\xi^{n}a^{\alpha-n}/(\alpha-n)
. This is (
19
) on

-1<\operatorname{Re}\alpha<0
; both sides are holomorphic in
\alpha
on

\{\operatorname{Re}\alpha>-1\}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, so the identity continues in
\alpha
.
For the residue claim, the residue of
\frac{1}{2i\sin\pi(\alpha+1)}
at

\alpha=n_{0}
is
\frac{(-1)^{n_{0}+1}}{2\pi i}
, so

\mathrm{Res}_{\alpha=n_{0}}[m(\alpha)(-\xi)^{\alpha}]=\frac{(-1)^{n_{0}+1}}{2\pi i}(-\xi)^{n_{0}}=-\tfrac{1}{2\pi i}\xi^{n_{0}}
, exactly
cancelling the residue
\tfrac{1}{2\pi i}\xi^{n_{0}}
of the
n=n_{0}
term. The
finite part is computed in Lemma
14
.
∎

4.3
The Mellin model: powers times logarithms

The key device is differentiation in the exponent,

s^{\alpha}(\log s)^{h}=\partial_{\alpha}^{h}s^{\alpha},\qquad(-\xi)^{\alpha}\bigl(\operatorname{Log}(-\xi)\bigr)^{h}=\partial_{\alpha}^{h}(-\xi)^{\alpha}.

(20)

Lemma 14

(Polyhomogeneous model expansion)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
\operatorname{Re}\alpha>-1
,
h\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
a>0
. For fixed

\xi\notin[0,a]
,

\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}\bigl[s^{\alpha}(\log s)^{h}\bigr](\xi)=\partial_{\alpha}^{h}\,\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}[s^{\alpha}](\xi),

(21)

and the asymptotic expansion of (
19
) may be differentiated termwise
in
\alpha
. Consequently:

1.

(Nonresonant,
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.)
As
\xi\to 0
,

\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}\bigl[s^{\alpha}(\log s)^{h}\bigr](\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{h}\binom{h}{i}m^{(i)}(\alpha)\,(-\xi)^{\alpha}\bigl(\operatorname{Log}(-\xi)\bigr)^{h-i}+\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n,h}\,\xi^{n},

(22)

with constant coefficients
c_{n,h}
depending on
a,\alpha,n
(no logarithm
occurs in the regular part when
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
). The top logarithmic
power of the singular part is
h
, with coefficient
m(\alpha)
.

2.

(Resonant,
\alpha=n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.)
As
\xi\to 0
,

\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}\bigl[s^{n_{0}}(\log s)^{h}\bigr](\xi)=\frac{-1}{2\pi i\,(h+1)}\,\xi^{n_{0}}\bigl(\operatorname{Log}(-\xi)\bigr)^{h+1}+\sum_{0\leq h^{\prime}\leq h}c_{h^{\prime}}\,\xi^{n_{0}}\bigl(\operatorname{Log}(-\xi)\bigr)^{h^{\prime}}+\sum_{n\geq 0}\widetilde{P}_{n,h}(\log\xi)\,\xi^{n},

(23)

with explicit constants
c_{h^{\prime}}
and polynomials
\widetilde{P}_{n,h}
of degree

\leq h+1
. The top logarithmic power is
h+1
, raised by one relative to the
density.

Proof.

For fixed
\xi\notin[0,a]
the integrand of

\mathcal{C}_{(0,a)}[s^{\alpha}](\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{a}s^{\alpha}(s-\xi)^{-1}ds

is, by (
20
), differentiated in
\alpha
to give

s^{\alpha}(\log s)^{h}(s-\xi)^{-1}
, which is absolutely integrable on
(0,a)

for
\operatorname{Re}\alpha>-1
and locally uniformly in
\alpha
; hence
(
21
). The expansion (
19
) is the sum over poles
of the Mellin transform of
(s-\xi)^{-1}
and is holomorphic in
\alpha
on

\{\operatorname{Re}\alpha>-1\}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, so its terms may be differentiated in

\alpha
, yielding the asymptotic expansion of the left side of
(
21
).

Nonresonant.
Apply
\partial_{\alpha}^{h}
to (
19
). By the
Leibniz rule and the second identity of (
20
),

\partial_{\alpha}^{h}[m(\alpha)(-\xi)^{\alpha}]=\sum_{i=0}^{h}\binom{h}{i}m^{(i)}(\alpha)(-\xi)^{\alpha}(\operatorname{Log}(-\xi))^{h-i}
,
the singular part of (
22
).
For the regular part,

\partial_{\alpha}^{h}[a^{\alpha-n}(\alpha-n)^{-1}\xi^{n}]
is, for

\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, a constant (a polynomial of degree
\leq h
in
\ln a
)
times
\xi^{n}
, giving
c_{n,h}\xi^{n}
; no
\log\xi
occurs in the regular
part for nonresonant
\alpha
.

Resonant.
Fix
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and set
\eta=\alpha-n_{0}
. Near
\eta=0
,

m(\alpha)=\frac{\mu_{-1}}{\eta}+\mu_{1}\eta+\cdots
(odd Laurent expansion, since

1/\sin
is odd about its pole) with
\mu_{-1}=\frac{(-1)^{n_{0}+1}}{2\pi i}
, and

(-\xi)^{\alpha}=(-1)^{n_{0}}\xi^{n_{0}}e^{\eta L}
,
L:=\operatorname{Log}(-\xi)
. The
n=n_{0}

regular term is
\frac{\xi^{n_{0}}}{2\pi i}\,\eta^{-1}e^{\eta\ln a}
. Their sum,
the only part singular as
\eta\to 0
, is

G(\alpha,\xi)=\mu_{-1}(-1)^{n_{0}}\xi^{n_{0}}\,\frac{e^{\eta L}-e^{\eta\ln a}}{\eta}+\bigl(\text{terms regular in }\eta\bigr),

using
\frac{\xi^{n_{0}}}{2\pi i}=-\mu_{-1}(-1)^{n_{0}}\xi^{n_{0}}
to combine the two

\eta^{-1}
residues (which therefore cancel, confirming
Lemma
13
).
Now

\frac{e^{\eta L}-e^{\eta\ln a}}{\eta}=\sum_{q\geq 0}\frac{\eta^{q}}{(q+1)!}\bigl(L^{q+1}-(\ln a)^{q+1}\bigr)
, so

\partial_{\alpha}^{h}G|_{\alpha=n_{0}}=h!\,[\eta^{h}]G
contributes

\mu_{-1}(-1)^{n_{0}}\xi^{n_{0}}\frac{1}{h+1}\bigl(L^{h+1}-(\ln a)^{h+1}\bigr)
. The
non-analytic leading term is

\mu_{-1}(-1)^{n_{0}}\frac{1}{h+1}\xi^{n_{0}}L^{h+1}=\frac{-1}{2\pi i(h+1)}\xi^{n_{0}}L^{h+1}
, since

\mu_{-1}(-1)^{n_{0}}=\frac{(-1)^{2n_{0}+1}}{2\pi i}=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}
. The
regular-in-
\eta
remainder of
G
together with the other
n\neq n_{0}
regular
terms supplies the lower powers
L^{h^{\prime}}
,
h^{\prime}\leq h
, and the analytic series with
log-coefficients of degree
\leq h+1
; this is (
23
).
∎

Remark 3
.

The mechanism is transparent: away from integers
m(\alpha)
is regular and the
density’s log power passes through unchanged; at an integer exponent the simple
pole of
m(\alpha)
collides with the integer mode of the regular series, and
each differentiation in
\alpha
that produces the resonant log adds one power,
giving
h\mapsto h+1
.

4.4
Polyhomogeneous propagation

Theorem 15

(Polyhomogeneous corner-mode propagation)
.

Let
f\in C^{m_{0},\beta}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{\theta,c})
satisfy
Assumption
6
, and suppose the side densities have classical
conormal (polyhomogeneous) expansions

f_{j}(r)\sim\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\sum_{h=0}^{N_{\ell}}a_{j,\ell,h}\,r^{\alpha_{j,\ell}}(\log r)^{h}\qquad(r\downarrow 0),\qquad\operatorname{Re}\alpha_{j,\ell}\uparrow\infty,\ j=1,2.

Then on each sector
\bullet\in\{\mathrm{I},\mathrm{II}\}
, as
z\to 0
,

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}f(z)\sim\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\sum_{h^{\prime}=0}^{N_{\ell}+\delta_{j,\ell}}A^{\bullet}_{j,\ell,h^{\prime}}\,z^{\alpha_{j,\ell}}(\log z)^{h^{\prime}}+\sum_{\nu\geq 0}\sum_{h^{\prime}}B^{\bullet}_{\nu,h^{\prime}}\,z^{\nu}(\log z)^{h^{\prime}},

(24)

where
\delta_{j,\ell}=1
if
\alpha_{j,\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
(resonant) and
0

otherwise. The coefficients are explicit: writing
\varphi_{1}(z)=z
,

\varphi_{2}(z)=e^{-i\theta}z
, each density mode
a_{j,\ell,h}r^{\alpha}(\log r)^{h}

contributes, via
\mathcal{C}_{(0,a_{j})}[\cdot](\varphi_{j}(z))
and
Lemma
14
, the singular family

\sum_{i}\binom{h}{i}m^{(i)}(\alpha)(-\varphi_{j}(z))^{\alpha}(\operatorname{Log}(-\varphi_{j}(z)))^{h-i}

in the nonresonant case and the family (
23
) with

\xi=\varphi_{j}(z)
in the resonant case. Re-expanding

Proof.

4.5
Relation to the Kerzman–Stein pathology

For continuously differentiable curves, the Kerzman–Stein operator

is compact

on a unit interval and, after a change of variable, realizes the operator as a

calculus is the constructive counterpart of that spectral diagnosis: the

exhibit the algebraic modes

Logarithmic transforms and real-potential corrections

5.1
The antiderivative relation

Fix a branch of

5.2
Finite-sheeted logarithmic decomposition

Theorem 16

(Logarithmic decomposition)

5.3
Real logarithmic transform

The relation between
\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}
and
\mathfrak{L}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}
is
not
a global real
part:
\log|\zeta-z|=\operatorname{Re}\log(\zeta-z)
holds pointwise, but the orientation
factor
d\zeta=e^{i\theta_{s}}|d\zeta|
on a tilted side rotates the integrand
before the real part is taken.

Proposition 18

(Corrected real-part relation)
.

Let side
s\in\{1,2\}
have tangent phase
\theta_{1}=0
,
\theta_{2}=\theta
, and
write
\mathfrak{L}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{s}
for the single-side transforms.

1.

If
f
is real on side
s
, then

\mathcal{L}_{s}f(z)=\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(e^{-i\theta_{s}}\mathfrak{L}_{s}f(z)\bigr)
, and

\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}f=\sum_{s}\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl(e^{-i\theta_{s}}\mathfrak{L}_{s}f\bigr)
.

2.

For complex
f
,

\mathcal{L}_{s}f(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-i\theta_{s}}\mathfrak{L}_{s}f(z)+\tfrac{1}{2}e^{\,i\theta_{s}}\overline{\mathfrak{L}_{s}\bar{f}(z)}.

Here the bar is the pointwise conjugation of the resulting scalar function at the
same
z
(not
\bar{z}
), using the same logarithm branch before conjugating. In
particular
\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}f\neq\operatorname{Re}(\mathfrak{L}_{1}f+\mathfrak{L}_{2}f)
in general.

Proof.

On side
s
,
|d\zeta|=e^{-i\theta_{s}}d\zeta
, so

\mathcal{L}_{s}f=e^{-i\theta_{s}}\int_{s}\log|\zeta-z|f\,d\zeta
. With

\log|\zeta-z|=\tfrac{1}{2}(\log(\zeta-z)+\overline{\log(\zeta-z)})

is
\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-i\theta_{s}}\mathfrak{L}_{s}f
. For the second, using

\overline{d\zeta}=e^{-2i\theta_{s}}d\zeta

\int_{s}\overline{\log(\zeta-z)}f\,d\zeta=\overline{\int_{s}\log(\zeta-z)\bar{f}\,\overline{d\zeta}}=e^{2i\theta_{s}}\overline{\mathfrak{L}_{s}\bar{f}(z)}

\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-i\theta_{s}}

to obtain (2). For real

the two terms are conjugate and sum to

\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta_{s}}\mathfrak{L}_{s}f)

∎

Rational-corner singular structure of Helmholtz layer potentials

6.1
The local Helmholtz kernel

For
\Delta u+k^{2}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
the outgoing fundamental solution is

\Phi_{k}(x,y)=\tfrac{i}{4}H_{0}^{(1)}(k|x-y|)
. Near the diagonal it has the local
structure

\Phi_{k}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x-y|\,A_{k}\!\bigl(|x-y|^{2}\bigr)+B_{k}\!\bigl(|x-y|^{2}\bigr),\qquad A_{k}(0)=1,

(29)

with
A_{k},B_{k}
analytic near

; indeed

A_{k}(r^{2})=J_{0}(kr)
and
B_{k}
collects the remaining even analytic part. Thus
the leading singularity of
\Phi_{k}
is the logarithmic kernel of
§
5
; the term
(A_{k}-1)\log r
is
O(r^{2}\log r)
, hence two orders
smoother at the diagonal than the leading singularity, while
B_{k}
is analytic
at
r=0
.

6.2
Model potential operators and the singular-operator algebra

On the straight wedge
\Gamma_{\theta,c}
, define the single-, double-, and adjoint
double-layer model operators

\displaystyle S_{0}f(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}\log|\zeta-z|\,f(\zeta)\,|d\zeta|=\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}f(z),

(30)

\displaystyle K_{0}f(z)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}\partial_{n(\zeta)}\log|\zeta-z|\,f(\zeta)\,|d\zeta|,

(31)

\displaystyle K_{0}^{\prime}f(z)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}\partial_{n(z)}\log|\zeta-z|\,f(\zeta)\,|d\zeta|,

(32)

with

a choice of unit normal, constant on each side. Identifying
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with

\mathbb{C}
, write
\nu(\zeta)=n_{1}(\zeta)+in_{2}(\zeta)
for the complex form of the

\nu(\zeta)=\nu_{s}
is constant on side

.

Proposition 19

(Potential operators as Cauchy boundary operators)
.

With
\partial_{\zeta}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}-i\partial_{y})
one has, for

\zeta\neq z
,

\partial_{n(\zeta)}\log|\zeta-z|=\operatorname{Re}\frac{\nu(\zeta)}{\zeta-z},\qquad\partial_{n(z)}\log|\zeta-z|=-\operatorname{Re}\frac{\nu(z)}{\zeta-z}.

(33)

Consequently
S_{0}=\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}
, and
K_{0},K_{0}^{\prime}
are real-linear combinations, with
side-constant coefficients
\nu_{s}
, of boundary values of the finite-sheeted
wedge Cauchy transform
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}
and its conjugate. Explicitly, for the
boundary value from a fixed side,

K_{0}f=\operatorname{Re}\Bigl[\,2\pi i\sum_{s}\nu_{s}e^{-i\theta_{s}}\,\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{bv}}_{s}f\Bigr],\qquad K_{0}^{\prime}f(z)=-\operatorname{Re}\Bigl[\,2\pi i\,\nu(z)\sum_{s}e^{-i\theta_{s}}\,\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{bv}}_{s}f(z)\Bigr]\ \ (\text{for real }f),

(34)

where
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{bv}}_{s}
denotes the boundary value of the side-
s
Cauchy
transform; the factor
2\pi i
is
+2\pi i
because
\int_{s}f(\zeta)(\zeta-z)^{-1}d\zeta=2\pi i\,\mathcal{C}_{s}f
for the convention (
2
). For complex

f
the conjugate term
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}\bar{f}}
appears, via the real-part
splitting of (
33
).

Proof.

For a real function
u
and unit direction
\nu=n_{1}+in_{2}
,

\partial_{n}u=2\operatorname{Re}(\nu\,\partial_{\zeta}u)
(write

\partial_{x}=\partial_{\zeta}+\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}
,

\partial_{y}=i(\partial_{\zeta}-\partial_{\bar{\zeta}})
and use

\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}u=\overline{\partial_{\zeta}u}
). With

u(\zeta)=\log|\zeta-z|=\tfrac{1}{2}\log((\zeta-z)\overline{(\zeta-z)})
,

\partial_{\zeta}u=\tfrac{1}{2}(\zeta-z)^{-1}
, giving the first identity in
(
33

\partial_{z}\log|\zeta-z|=-\tfrac{1}{2}(\zeta-z)^{-1}
and the same formula in

.
For real
f
,
K_{0}f=\operatorname{Re}\int_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}\frac{\nu(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}f\,|d\zeta|
;
on side

\nu(\zeta)=\nu_{s}

\int_{s}\frac{\nu_{s}}{\zeta-z}f\,|d\zeta|=\nu_{s}e^{-i\theta_{s}}\int_{s}\frac{f}{\zeta-z}d\zeta=\nu_{s}e^{-i\theta_{s}}(2\pi i)\mathcal{C}_{s}f
, whose boundary value gives the first
formula in (
34

\nu(z)
is independent of

\zeta

\sum_{s}e^{-i\theta_{s}}(2\pi i)\mathcal{C}_{s}f

complex

\operatorname{Re}\frac{\nu}{\zeta-z}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\frac{\nu}{\zeta-z}+\frac{\bar{\nu}}{\overline{\zeta-z}})

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta,c}}

∎

Let

(Local singular decomposition, kernel level)

Under the above hypotheses there is a neighbourhood of

Proof.

Insert (

6.4
Comparison with classical corner treatments

Classical boundary integral treatments of nonsmooth domains handle corners
numerically, through mesh grading near vertices, specialized singular
quadrature, or local asymptotic enrichment; Kress’s surveys of time-harmonic
acoustic scattering and linear integral equations are standard references for
the formulations and the graded-mesh handling of corners

Kress 1991
. The present results are of a different character. They
do not propose a quadrature rule or a discretization. Instead they give an exact
local symbolic calculus: at a rational corner the singular part of each layer
operator is identified, modulo a strictly smoother remainder, with a
finite-sheeted rational-wedge model operator whose branch structure and corner
asymptotics are explicit (Theorems
15
,
20
).
This is a local operator factorization, complementary to—and insertable
into—existing smooth-panel or Nyström frameworks, rather than a substitute
for them. It is also complementary to the functional-analytic theory of layer
potentials on Lipschitz and nonsmooth domains

Buffa and Sauter 2006
;
Ammari et al. 2009
;
Gao 1991
;
Kenig and Shen 2011
, which provides the mapping
and invertibility framework on global spaces but not the explicit local
symbolic form of the singular part that the wedge calculus supplies. The
closest precedent is the Mellin-transform treatment of the double-layer
potential on polygons
Costabel and Stephan 1983
: there the corner enters
through a Mellin symbol, whereas here the finite covering
w=\zeta^{q}

linearizes the angular monodromy and the singular part is read off the
finite-sheeted model directly.

z\to 0
,
\log(1-z)
is analytic, so

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\pi/2,c}}f(z)=\mathcal{C}_{(0,1)}[1](z)=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\,\operatorname{Log}(-z)+\bigl(\text{analytic}\bigr).

This matches Lemma
14
(2) with
n_{0}=0
,
h=0
, whose leading
coefficient is
\frac{-1}{2\pi i\,(h+1)}=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}
. A perfectly smooth
density on the wedge thus produces a logarithmic corner singularity: the
resonance is geometric, generated by the integer exponent
\alpha=0
meeting the
vertex, not by any singularity of the data.

7.3
Logarithmic data raises the power

Take
f_{1}(r)=\log r
, so
\alpha_{1,0}=0
and
h=1
. By
Lemma
14
(2) the top logarithmic power rises to
h+1=2
,
with leading coefficient
\frac{-1}{2\pi i\,(h+1)}=\frac{-1}{4\pi i}
:

\mathcal{C}_{(0,1)}[\log r](z)\sim\frac{-1}{4\pi i}\,\bigl(\operatorname{Log}(-z)\bigr)^{2}+O\bigl(\operatorname{Log}(-z)\bigr).

One verifies this from
\mathcal{C}_{(0,1)}[\log r]=\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{C}_{(0,1)}[r^{\alpha}]\big|_{\alpha=0}
: in the notation of the proof of
Lemma
14
, with
n_{0}=0
,
a=1
(so
\ln a=0
), the resonant
pair is
G=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\frac{e^{\eta L}-1}{\eta}+\cdots=\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\bigl(L+\tfrac{\eta}{2}L^{2}+\cdots\bigr)
, and

\partial_{\alpha}G|_{\alpha=0}=\frac{-1}{4\pi i}L^{2}+\cdots
with

L=\operatorname{Log}(-z)
. This is the log-power increase
h\mapsto h+1
in its simplest instance.

7.4
A non-resonant power gives a clean algebraic mode

Take
f_{1}(r)=r^{-1/2}
(so
\operatorname{Re}\alpha>-1
),
\alpha_{1,0}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
h=0
. By Lemma
13
,

\mathcal{C}_{(0,1)}[r^{-1/2}](z)\sim m(-\tfrac{1}{2})(-z)^{-1/2}+(\text{analytic}),\qquad m(-\tfrac{1}{2})=\frac{1}{2i\sin(\pi/2)}=\frac{1}{2i},

a purely algebraic corner mode with no logarithm, in contrast to the resonant
cases above. The next term, from the regular series of (
19
), is the
constant
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{i}{\pi}
at
n=0
.

7.5
Higher coverings

For
q=3
and
\theta=2\pi/3
(
p=2
, even,
(-1)^{p}=+1
) the transform is a
sum of three blocks per side, on the intervals
(0,1)
and
(0,c^{3})
, all
evaluated at
z^{3}
, with prefactors
z^{2},z,1
on side
1
and

(e^{-2\pi i/3}z)^{2},(e^{-2\pi i/3}z),1
on side
2
; the lifted exponents are

\beta_{j,m}=(\alpha_{j}+m+1)/3-1
,
m=0,1,2
. A density mode
r^{\alpha_{j}}
splits
into three sheet modes of exponents
(\alpha_{j}+1)/3-1
,
(\alpha_{j}+2)/3-1
,

\alpha_{j}/3
, which recombine to
z^{\alpha_{j}}
by the identity in the remark
following Theorem
15
. The mechanism is identical to the
right-angle case, with
q=3
sheets in place of
2
.

8
Localization on rational polygons

The calculus of §§
2
–
6
is local to one
vertex. We now show that it assembles, with no new singular analysis, into a
global decomposition of the Cauchy operator on a piecewise analytic curve whose
corners are rational. Two structural facts drive the assembly: the Cauchy kernel
is conjugated
exactly
by affine maps, and it is conjugated up to a

smoothing
(analytic-kernel) remainder by the analytic flattening of a
curved analytic side to its tangent ray. Separated vertex supports then make all
inter-vertex coupling analytic.

8.1
Analytic corner charts and cutoffs

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}
be a piecewise analytic Jordan curve with finite vertex
set
V(\Gamma)
, each interior opening angle a rational multiple of
\pi
,

\theta_{v}=p_{v}\pi/q_{v}
,
(p_{v},q_{v})=1
. Near
v
,
\Gamma
is the union of two
analytic arcs
\gamma_{v,1},\gamma_{v,2}
issuing from
v
with distinct unit
tangents. Fix a partition of unity

1=\chi_{0}+\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\chi_{v},

(38)

where
\chi_{v}\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)
is supported in a vertex neighbourhood
U_{v}

containing no other vertex, the
U_{v}
are pairwise disjoint, and

\chi_{0}=1-\sum_{v}\chi_{v}
is supported away from all vertices on the analytic part
of
\Gamma
. For each
v
let

A_{v}(z)=\lambda_{v}e^{-i\phi_{v}}(z-v)

(39)

be the affine normalization sending
v\mapsto 0
and the first incident tangent
to the positive real axis, so that the two tangent rays of
A_{v}\gamma_{v,1},A_{v}\gamma_{v,2}
are the sides of the model wedge
\Gamma_{\theta_{v},c_{v}}
for a
suitable
c_{v}>0
. Let
\sigma_{v}
denote the side-wise analytic flattening map:
on each side it is the biholomorphism, defined near
0
with
\sigma_{v}(0)=0
,

\sigma_{v}^{\prime}(0)=1
, carrying the straight model side onto the (affinely
normalized) analytic arc
A_{v}\gamma_{v,j}
. Figure
6
illustrates the localization scheme for a curvilinear polygon, where cutoff regions reduce the global boundary analysis to local vertex models.

Figure 6:
Localization scheme for a curvilinear polygon. Dashed circles indicate the cutoff regions near the vertices
v_{j}
, isolating the local wedge singularities from the smooth boundary arcs.

8.2
Transport of the wedge model

The next two lemmas are the engine of the localization.

Lemma 21

(Exact affine conjugation)
.

For any integrable density
h
on
\Gamma
and any affine

A(z)=\lambda e^{-i\phi}(z-v)
,

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[h]=\bigl(\mathcal{C}_{A\Gamma}[h\circ A^{-1}]\bigr)\circ A\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma.

The conjugation is exact: no remainder arises.

Proof.

With
w=A(z)
,
\eta=A(\zeta)
, one has
A^{-1}(\eta)=v+\lambda^{-1}e^{i\phi}\eta
,
so
\zeta-z=\lambda^{-1}e^{i\phi}(\eta-w)
and
d\zeta=\lambda^{-1}e^{i\phi}d\eta
.
The factors cancel in
\frac{h(\zeta)\,d\zeta}{\zeta-z}=\frac{(h\circ A^{-1})(\eta)}{\eta-w}\,d\eta
, giving the claim.
∎

Lemma 22

(Analytic flattening with smoothing remainder)
.

Let
\ell
be a ray from
0
,
\sigma
a biholomorphism near
0
with

\sigma(0)=0
,
\sigma^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
, and
\tilde{\gamma}=\sigma(\ell)
the
corresponding analytic arc. For
g
integrable on
\tilde{\gamma}
and
w_{0}
near

0
,

\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{\gamma}}[g]\bigl(\sigma(w_{0})\bigr)=\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\bigl[(g\circ\sigma)\,\sigma^{\prime}\bigr](w_{0})+\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}[g](w_{0}),

where
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}[g](w_{0})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\ell}(g\circ\sigma)(\tau)\sigma^{\prime}(\tau)K_{\sigma}(\tau,w_{0})\,d\tau
has kernel

K_{\sigma}
analytic in a neighbourhood of
(0,0)
, with diagonal value

K_{\sigma}(w_{0},w_{0})=\sigma^{\prime\prime}(w_{0})/(2\sigma^{\prime}(w_{0}))
. Hence
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}
maps

g
to a function analytic at
0
: it is smoothing across the vertex.

Proof.

Parametrize
\eta=\sigma(\tau)
,
\tau\in\ell
, and set the evaluation point

\sigma(w_{0})
. Then

\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{\gamma}}[g](\sigma(w_{0}))=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\ell}\frac{g(\sigma(\tau))\sigma^{\prime}(\tau)}{\sigma(\tau)-\sigma(w_{0})}\,d\tau
. Writing

\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(\tau)}{\sigma(\tau)-\sigma(w_{0})}=\frac{1}{\tau-w_{0}}+K_{\sigma}(\tau,w_{0})
and using

\sigma(\tau)-\sigma(w_{0})=\sigma^{\prime}(w_{0})(\tau-w_{0})\bigl(1+\tfrac{\sigma^{\prime\prime}(w_{0})}{2\sigma^{\prime}(w_{0})}(\tau-w_{0})+\cdots\bigr)
shows the simple pole at
\tau=w_{0}
has
residue
1
, so
K_{\sigma}
extends analytically across the diagonal with

K_{\sigma}(w_{0},w_{0})=\sigma^{\prime\prime}(w_{0})/(2\sigma^{\prime}(w_{0}))
;
K_{\sigma}
is analytic wherever

\sigma
is analytic and
\sigma^{\prime}\neq 0
, i.e. near
(0,0)
. The first term is the
flat-ray Cauchy transform of the transported density

(g\circ\sigma)\sigma^{\prime}
. The remainder
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}[g]
has an analytic
kernel, so it is a holomorphic function of
w_{0}
near
0
.
∎

Proposition 23

(Vertex model with analytic remainder)
.

Define the vertex model operator

\mathcal{C}_{v}[f]:=\bigl(\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta_{v},c_{v}}}\bigl[\Psi_{v}(\chi_{v}f)\bigr]\bigr)\circ\bigl(\sigma_{v}^{-1}\!\circ A_{v}\bigr),\qquad\Psi_{v}(\chi_{v}f)=\bigl((\chi_{v}f)\circ A_{v}^{-1}\circ\sigma_{v}\bigr)\,\sigma_{v}^{\prime},

where
\Psi_{v}
transports the localized density onto the straight model sides.
Then near
v
,

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[\chi_{v}f]=\mathcal{C}_{v}[f]+(\text{analytic at }v).

Proof.

By Lemma
21
,
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[\chi_{v}f]=\mathcal{C}_{A_{v}\Gamma}[(\chi_{v}f)\circ A_{v}^{-1}]\circ A_{v}
, and
A_{v}\Gamma
near
0
is the union of the
analytic arcs
\tilde{\gamma}_{v,j}=\sigma_{v}(\text{model side }j)
. Applying
Lemma
22
on each side to
g=(\chi_{v}f)\circ A_{v}^{-1}
replaces

\mathcal{C}_{A_{v}\Gamma}[g]
by
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta_{v},c_{v}}}[(g\circ\sigma_{v})\sigma_{v}^{\prime}]=\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta_{v},c_{v}}}[\Psi_{v}(\chi_{v}f)]
, evaluated at

\sigma_{v}^{-1}(A_{v}\,\cdot\,)
, plus the analytic remainders
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma_{v}}
.
This is the assertion.
∎

8.3
Smoothness of off-vertex interactions

Lemma 24

(Separated supports)
.

For
v\neq w
in
V(\Gamma)
,
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[\chi_{w}f]
is analytic in a
neighbourhood of
v
; likewise
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[\chi_{0}f]
is analytic near every
vertex.

Proof.

The kernel
\frac{1}{\zeta-z}
with
\zeta\in\operatorname{supp}\chi_{w}
(or

\operatorname{supp}\chi_{0}
) and
z
in a neighbourhood of
v
disjoint from that
support is bounded with
|\zeta-z|\geq d>0
and depends holomorphically on
z
;
differentiation under the integral gives holomorphy of
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[\chi_{w}f]

in
z
near
v
.
∎

8.4
The global decomposition and its asymptotic consequence

Theorem 25

(Local-to-global singular decomposition on rational polygons)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a piecewise analytic Jordan curve with rational corner angles

\theta_{v}=p_{v}\pi/q_{v}
,
v\in V(\Gamma)
, and cutoffs (
38
). Then

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\mathcal{C}_{v}+\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{sm}}+\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma},

(40)

where each
\mathcal{C}_{v}
is the transported finite-sheeted rational-wedge operator of
Proposition
23
for the model angle
\theta_{v}
,

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{sm}}=\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}\chi_{0}
is the smooth-arc Cauchy operator
(singular only along the diagonal away from the vertices), and the remainder

\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}
has a kernel analytic in a neighbourhood of every vertex. In
particular, near each vertex
v
,

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}f-\mathcal{C}_{v}[f]\quad\text{is analytic},

so the singular structure of
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
at
v
is exactly that of the
finite-sheeted wedge model
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{\theta_{v},c_{v}}}
.

Proof.

Decompose
f=\chi_{0}f+\sum_{v}\chi_{v}f
by (
38
), so

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}f=\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[\chi_{0}f]+\sum_{v}\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[\chi_{v}f]=\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{sm}}f+\sum_{v}\mathcal{C}_{v}[f]+\sum_{v}R_{v}[f],

where
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{sm}}:=\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[\chi_{0}\,\cdot\,]
and

R_{v}[f]:=\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}[\chi_{v}f]-\mathcal{C}_{v}[f].

Set
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}:=\sum_{v}R_{v}
; no term is counted twice. Each
R_{v}
is
analytic near
v
by Proposition
23
(the flattening
remainder), and analytic near every other vertex
w\neq v
by
Lemma
24
(separated supports, since
\operatorname{supp}\chi_{v}

is bounded away from
w
). Hence
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}=\sum_{v}R_{v}
is analytic in a
neighbourhood of every vertex. The smooth-arc term
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{sm}}
is
likewise analytic near each vertex (its source
\chi_{0}f
is supported away from
the vertices, Lemma
24
), while retaining the ordinary Plemelj
singularity along the diagonal on the analytic part of
\Gamma
, where the
vertex models are not singular. Thus
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}
(and

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{sm}}
) is smooth precisely across the vertex-localized
singular supports, which is (
40
).
∎

Corollary 26

(Vertexwise polyhomogeneous expansion)
.

Suppose
f
has classical conormal polyhomogeneous expansions at every rational
corner of
\Gamma
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}f
has, at each vertex
v
, the sectorwise
expansion of Theorem
15
for the model angle
\theta_{v}
,
computed from the transported density
\Psi_{v}(\chi_{v}f)
. No new singular
exponents are generated by the curvature of the sides or by inter-vertex
interaction: the analytic flattening
\sigma_{v}
and the analytic remainder

\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}
contribute only integer powers, which are absorbed into the
analytic family of the expansion.

Proof.

By Theorem
25
, near
v
the singular part of
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}f

equals that of
\mathcal{C}_{v}[f]
, a transported wedge model. The transport maps

\Psi_{v}
and
\sigma_{v}^{-1}\!\circ A_{v}
are analytic with nonvanishing
derivative at
0
, so they preserve conormal polyhomogeneity: a mode

r^{\alpha}(\log r)^{h}
has
(\sigma_{v}(r))^{\alpha}=\sigma_{v}^{\prime}(0)^{\alpha}r^{\alpha}(1+O(r))
and
\log\sigma_{v}(r)=\log r+O(1)
, leaving the exponents

\alpha
and the top log power unchanged while shifting only the coefficients.
Theorem
15
then gives the sectorwise expansion of

\mathcal{C}_{v}[f]
; the remainder
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}
is analytic at
v
and adds only
to the integer-power analytic family.
∎

Remark 7
.

Theorem
25
promotes the wedge calculus to a rational-polygon
calculus: the singular part of the Cauchy operator on
\Gamma
is a finite sum
of finite-sheeted wedge models, one per corner, the corners not interacting at
the level of singular structure. The same localization applies verbatim to the
logarithmic and Helmholtz layer operators of §§
5
–
6

through Lemmas
21
–
24
, since those operators were
already reduced there to the wedge Cauchy and logarithmic transforms. We do not
develop a global Fredholm theory, which would require mapping properties of

\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}
and of the corner blocks on a global space; the present
statement is at the level of kernel regularity and conormal asymptotics,
matching the caution of Theorem
20
.

9
Outlook: irrational angles

The construction is genuinely arithmetic in the angle. The factorization
(
13
) and the propagation Theorem
15
use that

\theta=p\pi/q
with
q
finite, so that
w=\zeta^{q}
is a finite covering and
the angular monodromy of
z^{\alpha}
closes after
q
sheets; the polygon
localization of §
8
then assembles the finite-sheeted
models across all corners. For irrational
\theta/\pi
no finite covering
linearizes the corner: the natural object is an infinite-sheeted covering, or a
limit of rational models
p_{n}/q_{n}\to\theta/\pi
with
q_{n}\to\infty
. The conormal
mapping bound of Theorem
9
has operator norm
q_{n}\to\infty

and the Hölder exponent
\beta/q_{n}\to 0
, so the rational models do not pass
uniformly to the limit; an irrational-angle theory would require a genuinely
different, non-finite-sheeted mechanism. We therefore state the present results
only for rational angles, where the finite-sheeted models are exact, and leave
the irrational case open.
