Executive Summary
This DOE Early Career Award supported a coordinated ATLAS research program
focused on advancing measurements and searches involving charm quarks, with the
long-term goal of enabling Standard Model sensitivity to the Higgs-charm Yukawa
coupling at the HL-LHC. The project pursued three tightly connected objectives: (1)
develop and calibrate improved charm-jet identification algorithms, (2) perform precision
Standard Model measurements of electroweak production with charm that also
constrain key backgrounds, and (3) apply these tools and measurements to transform
the ATLAS search for Higgs boson decays to charm quarks (H→cc̄).

A central technical outcome of the award was major contributions to ATLAS’s transition
to graph neural network (GNN) and transformer-based jet flavor tagging for Run 3. The
ATLAS GN2 tagger delivers substantially improved discrimination performance; for
example, at 70% b-tagging efficiency it achieves charm-jet rejection of ~45 and light-jet
rejection of ~2200, improving by more than a factor of four relative to early Run-3
approaches. Within this effort, we developed new algorithmic components and enabling
infrastructure, including optimized secondary-vertex reconstruction inputs for charm, a
soft-electron tagging module that improved charm- and light-jet rejection by ~15% at
fixed b-tagging efficiency, and prototype GNN-based charm vertex reconstruction. We
also made significant contributions to ATLAS taggers for boosted topologies (GN3X)
and to the frameworks used for training and performance evaluation. These
achievements are documented in a paper accepted by Nature Communications
(expected early 2026). In parallel, the award initiated R&D toward heavy-flavor jet
charge tagging, with a prototype GNN algorithm currently under evaluation.

A second major outcome was the development of calibration strategies needed to
correct Monte Carlo predictions to the flavor-tagging performance in collision data. The
project evaluated established tt̄-based methods and developed new charm-focused
approaches, including a calibration method based on gluon splitting to charm pairs
using explicit D-meson reconstruction. The team also developed procedures to
constrain extrapolation uncertainties from D-tagged samples to inclusive charm jets and

 1

incorporated tracking-material uncertainties in light-jet studies, and initiated calibration
work for GN3X using Z→cc̄ events.

The award delivered an important Standard Model result: a precision measurement of
W boson production in association with charm, with charm identified through
reconstructed D mesons and the W reconstructed in leptonic decays. The analysis
introduced a novel implementation of the opposite-sign minus same-sign charge
correlation subtraction directly within a profile-likelihood fit, improving robustness and
reducing analysis bias. Absolute fiducial cross sections were measured with ~5%
precision, while ratios and normalized differential cross sections achieved percent-level
precision. The measurement motivated ongoing follow-on analyses including Z+D,
charm fragmentation, charm production fraction studies, and a developing program to
measure gluon splitting to charm pairs. We recently initiated an electroweak precision
effort to measure the forward–backward asymmetry of charm quarks in Z events.

The project produced major advances in the ATLAS search for H→cc̄ in VH production,
thorough extensive work on background modeling, systematics development, and the
statistical fit. The ATLAS Run-2 VH(cc) analysis was published in JHEP in 2025, which
achieved an expected limit of ~10× the Standard Model rate, a factor of 3 improvement
over the previous expected limit using the same dataset, driven by advanced flavor
tagging and machine-learning discriminants. We also combined direct and indirect
Higgs to charm measurements in a publication in JHEP in 2023, to obtain the strongest
constraints on Higgs coupling to charm, and contribued to the flagship ATLAS paper in
Nature in 2022. We extrapolated these results to the HL-LHC and made initial
projections of the sensitivity of the potential FCC-hh to bottom and charm couplings.
The award also launched a boosted VH(cc) program built on boosted charm tagging
and advanced machine learning techniques, positioning ATLAS for further gains in Run
3 working towards HL-LHC sensitivity.

This award also delivered strong workforce development outcomes, supporting multiple
PhD theses, undergraduate research participation, and hands-on ATLAS operational
training at CERN. Overall, the project advanced key DOE HEP priorities by enabling
improved charm identification, precision Standard Model measurements, and
substantially enhanced sensitivity to Higgs couplings to quarks.

Project Objectives
The project had three inter-related components:
(1) development and calibration of a new algorithm to identify jets containing charm
quarks;

 2

(2) measurements of W boson production in association with charm quarks, which, in
addition to being important SM probes, are key backgrounds to measurements of the
decays of the Higgs boson to quarks;
(3) apply this tagger and the knowledge obtained about these backgrounds to transform
the search for Higgs boson decays to charm quarks to reach SM sensitivity with the
HL-LHC dataset.

Accomplishments and Results

Charm Tagging

Over the course of the award, Graph Neural Network (GNN) and transformer-based
flavor tagging became the baseline approach for Run-3 in the ATLAS experiment. The
ATLAS tagger, GN2, achieves strong discrimination performance; for example, at a 70%
b-tagging efficiency it provides a rejection of approximately 45 for charm jets and ~2200
for light-flavor jets, representing an improvement of more than a factor of four relative to
early Run-3 taggers.

(1a) Algorithmic Development

During the first phase of the award, our efforts focused on prototype charm-tagging
algorithms and the optimization of inputs used for secondary-vertex reconstruction,
which had historically been tuned primarily for bottom quarks. We optimized track and
vertex selections using an initial cut-based approach followed by a DNN-based strategy,
and we developed a soft-electron tagging module integrated into the existing
flavor-tagging workflow. This soft-electron tagger improved charm- and light-jet rejection
by ~15% at fixed b-tagging efficiency (77%) and was completed in FY23 and
incorporated into the ATLAS flavor-tagging framework. We also developed a prototype
GNN-based charm-vertex reconstruction algorithm in FY23, and subsequently merged
this activity into the broader ATLAS GNN effort. As part of that effort, we implemented
and optimized new auxiliary tasks for the boosted X→bb/cc tagger training, added
support for edge-level features (beyond node-level features), and began incorporating
heavy-flavor decay-chain reconstruction into the GNN workflow. In parallel, we
implemented vertex grouping within the SALT framework, which is used as a common
training framework for ATLAS flavor-tagging GNNs. In January 2023, we refocused our
charm-tagging development to align with the newly launched ATLAS boosted
charm-tagging task force, which provides a high-impact physics application for our GNN
studies. We made major contributions to the transformer-based GN2 (single-jet
topologies) and GN3X (boosted decay topologies) taggers, including
vertex-reconstruction algorithm development for GN2 and edge-feature integration for
GN3X. We also contributed substantially to the SALT and PUMA software frameworks

 3

that support training, validation, and performance evaluation of these taggers across
ATLAS. More recently, we have begun exploring the possibility of tagging the charge of
heavy-flavor jets. We have developed a prototype algorithm using a GNN and are
currently evaluating the performance. Our work in flavor tagging is documented in a
publication accepted by Nature and expected to appear in early 2026.

(1b) Calibration

Calibration is required to align Monte Carlo predictions of tagging performance with that
observed in collision data. Our work combined feasibility studies using established
approaches and the development of new methods targeted to charm jets. We
performed an initial study adapting a standard tt̄-based b-tagging calibration technique
for c-tagging with the goal of simultaneously calibrating charm-, bottom-, and light-jes.
While this method provided adequate statistics for charm jets, the selection efficiency
for bottom and light jets was too low for a robust simultaneous calibration, and we
prioritized more promising approaches. Building on our earlier W+D analysis work, we
developed a proof-of-concept charm-jet calibration strategy in FY22 and expanded this
effort in FY23 through a new calibration method based on gluon splitting to charm-quark
pairs. In this approach, explicit D-meson reconstruction is used to identify jets
containing multiple charm hadrons without relying on the tagging algorithm itself,
enabling an independent calibration handle. Initial studies demonstrated that the
charm–charm jet fraction is sufficiently large to calibrate the tagger response to these
jets. We additionally developed a new procedure to constrain extrapolation uncertainties
when translating measurements in D-meson–tagged jets to inclusive charm-jet
selections using tt̄ events, and we incorporated tracking-material systematic variations
into light-jet calibration studies to quantify associated uncertainties. We have also been
developing calibration strategies for our new jet charge tagging algorithm using W+D
events. We are currently developing a dedicated Z→cc̄ calibration program to constrain
data/MC scale factors for boosted charm tagging, including working-point definitions
and evaluation of QCD background efficiencies as a function of jet mass, which are
needed for our ongoing boosted analysis.

(2) Standard Model Measurement with Charm

We performed and published a precision measurement of W boson production in
association with a charm quark. Charm was identified through the reconstruction of
charmed hadrons using secondary-vertex fits, while the W boson was reconstructed in
the electron and muon decay channels using the charged lepton and missing transverse
momentum. The analysis reconstructed two complementary charm-meson signatures,
D⁰ and D±* using the Kππ decay topology. Integrated and normalized differential fiducial
cross sections were extracted with a profile-likelihood fit, using the reconstructed
secondary-vertex mass for the D sample and the D–D⁰ mass difference for the D*
sample. A key feature of the methodology is the use of charge correlations: at leading
order the W boson and charm quark have opposite-sign electric charges, while many
important backgrounds are charge symmetric. We therefore extracted the signal from
the opposite-sign minus same-sign event difference, and a central novelty of this
measurement was implementing this subtraction directly within the profile-likelihood fit
framework, improving robustness and reducing analysis bias. The absolute fiducial
cross sections were measured with approximately 5% precision, dominated by

 5

systematic uncertainties, while cross-section ratios and normalized differential cross
sections achieved percent-level precision with comparable statistical and systematic
contributions. The experimental precision reached the level of modern PDF
uncertainties and was smaller than the total theory uncertainty, enabling the
measurement to provide meaningful constraints on Standard Model predictions. In
particular, the measured lepton pseudorapidity distributions were observed to be
broader than central theoretical predictions, but remained consistent once PDF
uncertainties were included, highlighting the potential of these data to constrain global
PDF fits. The measured charm-sensitive cross-section ratio observable (Rc) was also
found to be consistent with a range of PDF sets, including those favoring a symmetric
strange-antistrange sea.

Building on the success of the W+c measurement, we initiated a broader program of
Standard Model charm measurements that leverage the same reconstruction, fitting,

 6

and background-subtraction techniques. These include analyses of Z+D production and
the charm fragmentation function. The associated Z+D measurement formed the basis
of a completed PhD thesis (Renteria) and will be the subject of a second thesis (Foster),
with continued development toward a differential cross-section measurement that
separates PDF-driven charm production from gluon splitting to charm using shape
information such as the azimuthal separation Δϕ(Z,D). For the charm fragmentation
function, we explored two approaches: (i) a conventional method using track jets to
reduce uncertainties and extracting the fragmentation variable using z=pT(D)/pT(jet)z =
p_T(D) / p_T(jet)z=pT​(D)/pT​(jet), and (ii) a novel proposal using tt̄ events and lepton
kinematics as a proxy for charm momentum; a detailed feasibility studyshowed that the
kinematic correlation is insufficient for an accurate measurement, and we therefore plan
to pursue the track-jet–based approach.

We also performed studies aimed at measuring charm production fractions by
reconstructing additional charmed hadron species, including the D_s meson and the
Λ_c baryon in the context of a completed PhD thesis (Ottino). While Λ_c reconstruction
is particularly challenging due to its short lifetime, progress on D_s reconstruction is
encouraging, but more work would be required before publication. In addition to physics
deliverables, a substantial effort in FY24 was devoted to migrating analysis software to
support the ATLAS transition to release 25, ensuring long-term sustainability of the tools
and workflows developed under this award.

Finally, we launched a focused effort to measure gluon splitting to charm-quark pairs, a
critical and poorly modeled background process for Higgs-to-charm searches and other
heavy-flavor analyses. The core analysis strategy is nearing completion, and we
convened a dedicated kickoff meeting to formally launch this activity within the ATLAS
Standard Model group. The measurement targets events containing a W boson and a
reconstructed D meson with the same sign, providing sensitivity to gluon splitting into
both charm and bottom pairs, and enabling studies of higher-order EW and QCD
effects, four- versus five-flavor schemes, and heavy-quark mass effects. Because the
analysis is particularly sensitive to the low-pT regime—where generator modeling is
least reliable—it is expected to deliver valuable constraints for Monte Carlo tuning and
reduce dominant systematic uncertainties for multiple ATLAS measurements, including
boosted VH→bb/cc. We have developed an initial prototype of the analysis, and we are
evaluating whether it will be included in Foster’s thesis or left for future work.

We have been exploring a new electroweak precision program centered on measuring
the forward–backward asymmetry of charm quarks in Z-boson events at the LHC. This
measurement is motivated by the strong sensitivity of forward–backward asymmetries
to the parity-violating structure of Z–fermion couplings and to the effective weak mixing
angle, and it provides a charm-sector analogue to long-standing precision tests

 7

performed in the bottom sector. The proposed approach uses leptonic Z decays to
reconstruct the Z kinematics and define the relevant angular observables in the Z rest
frame, while using charged D-meson reconstruction to tag the sign of the underlying
charm quark. A principal challenge is the dilution from charm produced via gluon
splitting, rather than from the initial-state proton PDFs, and we therefore developed a
strategy to constrain this contribution using the complementary W+D and Z+D
measurements described above. First-pass studies using reconstructed Monte Carlo
indicate that this measurement could reach statistically competitive precision with Run 2
and Run 3 data, and ongoing work is focused on controlling gluon-splitting and Z+jets
backgrounds and defining a robust strategy for systematic uncertainties.

(3) Higgs to charm
A central physics goal of this award was to enable a transformative improvement in the
sensitivity of ATLAS searches for Higgs boson decays to charm quarks, which remain
one of the most challenging Standard Model Higgs measurements at the LHC. Our
group made major contributions to the legacy Run-2 VH(cc) analysis, including
substantial work on vector-boson + jets background modeling, development and
validation of associated systematic uncertainties, and exploration of a prototype strategy
performing a simultaneous fit of H→bb̄ and H→cc̄ to improve overall constraints.
Graduate students in the group took key leadership roles in the analysis: Xiong served
as editor of the internal note for background modeling and fit contact for the 2-lepton
(ZH) channel, while Ene served as fit contact for the 1-lepton (WH) channel and led
code review efforts for background modeling. We also validated and deployed the CARL
approach, a machine-learning technique designed to reduce the impact of statistical
fluctuations when estimating systematic uncertainties, and used it to evaluate modeling
uncertainties in a more stable and robust way.

This work culminated in a flagship Higgs result that was included in a Nature
publication, featuring—for the first time—a combined interpretation that unifies direct
constraints on κc from the VH(cc) search with indirect sensitivity to κc from the
differential Higgs transverse momentum measurement, thereby enabling a joint
constraint on the Higgs–charm Yukawa coupling that leverages complementary
information. Several members of the group made major contributions to the statistical
analysis and combination framework. In particular, we developed a fit to the diboson
peak as an important validation and cross-check of the analysis strategy, and we
developed the physics interpretation machinery needed to extract key coupling
information, including the measurement of the ratio of charm to bottom Yukawa
couplings. We carried out extensive fit validation studies, including detailed evaluations
of nuisance-parameter correlations, pruning strategies, and the performance of
truth-tagging, to ensure fit stability and robustness. The resulting combined VH(cc)

 8

analysis achieved an expected limit of approximately 10× the Standard Model rate, with
the three individual channels contributing comparably (expected limits of ~17× SM in the
0-lepton channel and ~18× SM in both the 1- and 2-lepton channels). This represents a
factor of 3 improvement relative to the previous iteration of the analysis using the same
dataset (expected limit ~31× SM), driven primarily by the integration of advanced
flavor-tagging algorithms and expanded use of machine-learning discriminants. In
addition, the analysis provides significantly improved sensitivity to VH(bb̄), with an
expected Higgs-to-bottom significance of approximately 8σ, and expected significances
above 5σ for the WH(bb̄) and ZH(bb̄) channels individually, enabling observation-level
sensitivity.

Motivated by the strong impact of boosted-object techniques and our leadership in
boosted charm tagging development, we also initiated a forward-looking program to
extend H→cc̄ sensitivity in the boosted regime. Beginning in spring 2024, we performed
a feasibility study of a boosted VH(cc) search strategy in which the Higgs boson is
produced at high transverse momentum, causing the charm-quark pair to merge into a
single large-R Higgs jet, thereby improving background rejection and enabling powerful

 9

substructure-based charm-tagging. This boosted analysis is currently led by Gagnon as
analysis contact, with the group making leading contributions to all aspects of the
analysis: developing the multivariate discriminant, developing the statistical fit strategy
and validating the suitability of the ATLAS FastSim3 (AF3) framework for sensitivity
studies. The boosted VH(cc) analysis is built around improved charm-tagging using
GN2X/GN3X-based discriminants, an event classification workflow based on
DNN/transformer-style MVAs, and a profile-likelihood fit implemented in the ATLAS
statistical framework. Early AF3-to-full-simulation comparisons in tt̄ events show
encouraging agreement, with remaining differences consistent with statistical limitations
or coverable through additional systematic uncertainties, demonstrating that this
boosted framework is well positioned to deliver further gains in Higgs-to-charm
sensitivity in Run 3 and beyond.

We have also been contributing to the development of the physics case for future
colliders. We performed a projection of our existing H->bb/H->cc results to the HL-LHC.
We obtained an expected upper limit of 2.2 times the predicted cross-section times
branching ratio is estimated at the 95% confidence level, with an additional 30%
increase expected from existing improved jet flavor identification. We also performed an
initial study into the sensitivity of the FCC-hh to constrain Higgs to bottom/charm decays
and found that a 70 (50) TeV collider could constrain κb to 0.27 (0.28) and κc to 2.2 (2.3).

Team members presented results from this award regularly within ATLAS, including in
the Flavor Tagging meetings, Standard Model working group meetings (including SM
plenaries), the Boosted Object Task Force, Higgs subgroup meetings, and relevant
Editorial Board meetings. We also communicated progress through dedicated meetings
of smaller analysis and performance teams to ensure timely feedback and integration of
developments into ATLAS-wide workflows.
