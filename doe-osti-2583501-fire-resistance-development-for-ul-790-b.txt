Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

Class A burning brand (UL 790) tests are used to determine the roof covering’s effectiveness
against severe exposure to external fire. Unfortunately, these tests can be costly to repeatedly
perform when iteratively developing new materials/designs. The NREL team will work with the
R&D Lab to develop and deploy computational tools to facilitate rapid development of roofing
materials for the UL 790 standard test.

Summary of Research Results:

Task 1:

 • NREL shall: modeling thermodynamic heat transfer of a Class A burning brand (UL 790)
 • The Participant shall: collect data from instrumented test decks to run Class A burning
 brand tests in order to validate and/or calibrate the model

NREL developed a Python code to model the heat transfer from a typical Class A burning brand
through a series of resistive layers (i.e., shingle, underlayment, etc) to the final wood layer.
Participant performed a series of tests where the typical roof assembly structure was exposed to a
Class A burning brand and data were collected using several thermocouples.

 Figure 1. Thermocouple data from the Class A burning brand test where the roof assembly passed.

Figure 2. Thermocouple data from the Class A burning brand test where the roof assembly failed.

Figures 1 and 2 show the thermocouple data collected by the Participant during two different
days of testing roof assemblies using the Class A burning brand test. This data was then used by
NREL to calibrate and validate the Python code to model the heat transfer as well as to help infer
properties of the various materials.

Task 2:

 • NREL shall: model the heat transfer to the sheathing of a simulated roof assembly and
 initiation of pyrolysis.
 • The Participant shall: provide thermal properties and geometries for materials used in the
 UL 790 burning brand experiments.

The Participant provided guidance on thermal properties and geometries for the UL 790 burning
brand tests. Figure 3 shows the provided geometry of the roof assembly that was used in the UL
790 burning brandd tests as well as the locations of the thermocouples that collected the data
shown in Figures 1 and 2.

 Figure 3. Class A burning brand test deck diagram showing the various layers of the roof
 assembly and the approximate locations of the thermocouples.

NREL used the information contained in Figures 1 – 3 to calibrate the Python codebase to the
specifications of the Participant provided designs. NREL used the data provided to validate the
model and ensure that the simulated results were consistent with the observations in the Class A
burning brand data.

 Figure 4. Simulated results for a roof assembly with a carbon foam underlayment with thickness
 of 4 mm that is subject to the Class A burning brand test. Results are consistent with observed
 thermocouple data.
Task 3:

 • NREL shall: model the difference in initiation of pyrolysis of the sheathing when
 different materials are used in the roof assembly.

After NREL concluded that the Python-based heat transfer model was consistent with observed
data, the team performed a series of simulations to determine which underlayment material
properties would be required to prevent the pyrolysis process from resulting in a run-away
ignition and subsequent failing of the test. NREL performed a sweep of simulations across a
range underlayment design conditions.

 4

  Figure 5. Summary of simulation results from parameter sweep across different underlayment
 design conditions. Results indicated as "Fail" are likely to ignite and faill, "Pass" are unlikely to
 ignite, and "Close" are dependent upon the conditions of the test.

NREL provided Figure 5 to the Participant as guidance for developing new underlayments. The
results indicate the minimum thickness required for an underlayment of a given thermal
conductivity to be able to prevent the ignition process and therefore pass the test. The means of
achieving these thermal properties are left up to the Participant as there were several candidate
solutions of varying complexities.

Task 4:

 • NREL shall: use Participant-provided test data to validate and calibrate the model.

Task 4 was completed during the development of the Python code during the other tasks as data was
made available. NREL calibrated the heat transfer code to make sure that the results were consistent with
the observed values during the laboratory tests. The data was used to calibrate the thermal properties of
composite materials or those that the manufacturer did not publish and were unquantifiable by either
NREL or the Participant. The data was also used to validate the Pass/Fail criterion used in generating the
recommendations to the Participant contained in Task 3 (Figure 5).
