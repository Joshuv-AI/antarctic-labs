Twist-2 relations for the twist-3 tensor-polarized distribution function f L ​ T f_{LT} of a spin-1 hadron by the operator-product-expansion method Journal: Physics Letters B
S. Kumano, Kenshi Kuroki

Abstract
In a spin-1 hadron, tensor-polarized parton distribution functions (PDFs) exist. The twist-2 function is f 1 ​ L ​ L f_{1LL} and a twist-3 one is f L ​ T f_{LT} . Because an experiment is under preparation at the Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility (JLab) to measure the cross section of electron-deuteron deep inelastic scattering with the tensor-polarized deuteron target, these PDFs need to be understood theoretically. Especially, measurements will be done in a relatively low- Q 2 Q^{2} region at JLab, so that twist-3 contributions could become sizable in the cross section. In a previous work, a twist-2 relation was derived for f L ​ T f_{LT} in terms of f 1 ​ L ​ L f_{1LL} by using a nonlocal operator, and it corresponds to the Wandzura-Wilczek (WW) relation between g 1 g_{1} and g 2 g_{2} . In addition, another relation similar to the Burkhardt-Cottingham (BC) sum rule was obtained. It is known that a formal way to derive the WW relation and the BC sum rule is to use the operator product expansion (OPE) with local operators. In this work, the WW-like relation and the BC-like sum rule for f L ​ T f_{LT} are derived by using the local OPE method as a reliable independent way to establish these relations.

Introduction

Spin structure of the spin-1/2 nucleon has been investigated in details
at high energies for finding the origin of nucleon spin.
Hadrons with higher spin
\geq 1

have much richer spin structure because of the existence
of tensor polarizations. There are recent theoretical developments on
polarized parton distribution functions (PDFs) of
spin-1
[
1
]
and spin-3/2
[
2
]
hadrons.
This additional spin structure has not been investigated extensively
in comparison with the one of the spin-1/2 nucleon
partly because of a lack of an experimental project for a long time.
However, time has come to investigate this topic seriously
because an electron deep inelastic scattering (DIS) experiment
is under preparation now at the Thomas Jefferson National
Accelerator Facility (JLab) with the tensor-polarized deuteron

[
3
,
4
]
.
There are other experimental facilities where tensor-polarized
deuteron experiments will be possible in several years,
such as the Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab)

[
5
,
6
]
,
the Nuclotron-based Ion Collider fAcility (NICA)
[
7
]
,
the Large Hadron Collider (LHC)
[
8
]
,
and Electron-Ion Colliders (EICs)
[
9
,
10
]
.

In order to understand such upcoming experimental measurements
on the spin-1 deuteron, it is necessary to investigate
the tensor-polarized structure functions and PDFs theoretically.
In particular, JLab data are expected to contain significant
higher-twist effects because they are obtained typically
in a few GeV
2

Q^{2}
region.
In addition to the twist-2 functions
[
11
]
,
possible twist-3 and 4 PDFs and transverse-momentum-dependent parton

in Ref.
[
12
]
.
Among them, there are the twist-2 PDF
f_{1LL}
and the twist-3 one
f_{LT}
.
It was shown in Ref.

In addition, a sum rule which is similar to

was also obtained
[

]

Based on these twist-2 relations, the function

In the derivation of the BC-like sum rule and WW-like relation

2
Tensor-polarized parton distribution functions in a spin-1 hadron

The PDFs of a hadron are generally defined in the correlation function
with a nonlocal operator as

\displaystyle\Phi_{ij}^{[c]}(k,P,T)=\!\!\int\!\frac{d^{4}\xi}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,e^{ik\cdot\xi}\langle P,T\left|\,\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)W^{[c]}(0,\xi)\psi_{i}(\xi)\,\right|P,T\rangle,

where
\psi
is the quark field,
P
and
T
indicate the hadron
momentum and tensor polarization,
i
and
j
are components of
\psi
,

k
is the quark momentum, and
W^{[c]}
is the gauge link
for the color gauge invariance with the integral path
c
.
The correlation function is the amplitude to extract a parton
from the hadron and to insert it at a different space-time point
\xi
.
We do not write the spin vector polarization
S
explicitly
in Eq. (
) because only the tensor polarization
T
,
which is specific to hadrons with spin
\geq 1
, is investigated in this paper.
The vector polarization part is essentially the same as the one
for the spin-1/2 nucleons.
The collinear correlation function is obtained by integrating
Eq. (
) over the parton momentum as

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\Phi_{ij}(x,P,T)=\int d^{2}k_{T}\,dk^{+}dk^{-}\,\Phi^{[c]}_{ij}(k,P,T)\,\delta(k^{+}-xP^{+})

\displaystyle\hskip-14.51074pt=\!\int\frac{d\xi^{\,-}}{2\pi}\,e^{ixP^{+}\xi^{\,-}}\langle\,P,T\left|\,\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)\,W(0,\xi\,|\,n)\psi_{i}(\xi)\,\right|P,\,T\,\rangle_{\xi^{+}=0,\,\vec{\xi}_{T}=0},

(2)

where the lightcone
\pm
momenta are defined by

a^{\pm}=(a^{0}\pm a^{3})/\sqrt{2}
, and

x
is the lightcone momentum fraction given by
k^{+}=xP^{+}
.
The gauge link
W(0,\xi\,|\,n)
indicates the line connecting

0
to
\xi=(\xi^{\,+},\xi^{\,-},\vec{\xi}_{T})

along the straight lightcone direction of
\xi^{\,-}
.
The lightcone vectors
n^{\,\mu}
and
\bar{n}^{\,\mu}
are
defined by

\displaystyle n^{\,\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\,1,\,0,\,0,\,-1\,),\ \ \ \bar{n}^{\,\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\,1,\,0,\,0,\,1\,).

(3)

The tensor polarization

in terms of the tensor-polarization parameters

3
Operator-product-expansion method for deriving

the twist-2 relations

The OPE is used for deriving the twist-2 relations in this section.
The OPE method for deriving the WW relation
and BC sum rule is summarized in
Refs.
[
17
,
18
,
20
]
.
The polarized structure function
g_{2}
of the spin-1/2 nucleon
is separated into WW and dynamical twist-3 parts as

\displaystyle g_{2}(x)=g_{2}^{\text{WW}}(x)

\displaystyle+g_{2}^{\text{(HT)}}(x),

(7)

where HT indicates higher twist,
and then the WW part is expressed by the twist-2
structure function
g_{1}
as

\displaystyle g_{2}^{\text{WW}}(x)

\displaystyle=-g_{1}(x)+\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}g_{1}(y).

(8)

This is the WW relation.
If this WW relation is integrated over
x
,
it becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx\,g_{2}^{WW}(x)=0,

(9)

which is the BC sum rule.

In the following derivations, we follow the explanations
in Ref.
[
20
]
on the OPE method
for deriving the WW relation and the BC sum rule
to obtain the corresponding twist-2 relations for
f_{LT}
.
The tensor-polarization part of the matrix element
of the nonlocal vector operator

\bar{\psi}(0)\,\gamma^{\,\sigma}\,\psi(\xi)
is given by

[
13
]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\left.\int\frac{d(P\cdot\xi)}{4\pi P\cdot n}e^{\,ixP\cdot\xi}\langle\,P,T\left|\,\bar{\psi}(0)\,\gamma^{\,\sigma}\,\psi(\xi)\,\right|\!P,T\,\rangle\,\right|_{\,\xi^{+}=0,\,\vec{\xi}_{T}=0}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=S_{LL}\bar{n}^{\,\sigma}f_{1LL}(x)+\frac{M}{P\cdot n}S_{LT}^{\,\sigma}f_{LT}(x),

(10)

in terms of the tensor-polarized PDFs

f_{1LL}
and
f_{LT}
.
The twist-4 distribution
f_{3LL}
is neglected
in this work up to twist 3.
Taking
n

we obtain

\displaystyle\left.\int_{-1}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}\!\!\int\frac{d(P\cdot\xi)}{4\pi P\cdot n}e^{\,ixP\cdot\xi}\langle\,P,T\left|\,\bar{\psi}(0)\,\gamma^{\,\sigma}\,\psi(\xi)\,\right|\!P,T\,\rangle\,\right|_{\,\xi^{+}=0,\,\vec{\xi}_{T}=0}

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt=S_{LL}\bar{n}^{\,\sigma}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}f_{1LL}(x)+\frac{M}{P\cdot n}S_{LT}^{\,\sigma}\int_{-1}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}f_{LT}(x).

The quark field
\psi(\xi)
is expanded in
a Taylor series as

\displaystyle\psi(\xi)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}(\xi\cdot\partial)^{m}\psi(\xi)_{\xi=0}.

(12)

The factor

on the left-hand side of
Eq. (
3

\displaystyle\!\!\!\left.\int_{-1}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}\int\frac{d(P\cdot\xi)}{4\pi P\cdot n}e^{\,ixP\cdot\xi}\langle\,P,T\left|\,\bar{\psi}(0)\,\gamma^{\,\sigma}\,\psi(\xi)\,\right|\!P,T\,\rangle\,\right|_{\,\xi^{+}=0,\,\vec{\xi}_{T}=0}

\displaystyle\!\!\!=\int_{-1}^{1}dx\int\frac{d(P\cdot\xi)}{4\pi P\cdot n}\frac{1}{i^{\,n-1}}\left[\frac{\partial^{\,n-1}}{\partial(P\cdot\xi)^{\,n-1}}e^{\,ixP\cdot\xi}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-3.98337pt\left.\times\left\langle\,P,T\left|\,\bar{\psi}(0)\,\gamma^{\,\sigma}\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}(\xi\cdot\partial)^{m}\psi(\xi)_{\xi=0}\,\right|\!P,T\,\right\rangle\,\right|_{\,\xi^{+}=0,\,\vec{\xi}_{T}=0}.

(13)

Shifting the derivative operation to the matrix-element part
by the partial integration
and noting the relation

\displaystyle\partial^{\,\mu_{1}}\cdots\partial^{\,\mu_{n-1}}\!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}(\xi\cdot\partial)^{m}=\partial^{\,\{\,\mu_{1}}\cdots\partial^{\,\mu_{n-1}\}}\left(1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}(\xi\cdot\partial)^{m}\right),

(14)

we obtain

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\left.\int_{-1}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}\!\!\int\frac{d(P\cdot\xi)}{4\pi P\cdot n}e^{\,ixP\cdot\xi}\langle\,P,T\left|\,\bar{\psi}(0)\,\gamma^{\,\sigma}\,\psi(\xi)\,\right|\!P,\,T\,\rangle\,\right|_{\,\xi^{+}=0,\,\vec{\xi}_{T}=0}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.66573pt=\frac{n_{\mu_{1}}\cdots n_{\mu_{n-1}}}{2\,(P\cdot n)^{\,n}}\!\left\langle\,P,T\left|\,i^{\,n-1}\bar{\psi}(0)\,\gamma^{\,\sigma}\,\partial^{\,\{\,\mu_{1}}\cdots\partial^{\,\mu_{n-1}\}}\psi(0)\,\right|\!P,T\,\right\rangle,

for

The curly bracket
\{\cdots\}

\partial^{\,\{\,\mu_{1}}\cdots\partial^{\,\mu_{n-1}\}}=(\,\partial^{\,\mu_{1}}\cdots\partial^{\,\mu_{n-1}}+\text{permutations}\,)\,/\,(n-1)!
.
The derivatives are replaced by the covariant ones by
the minimal substitution, then the local operator becomes

\displaystyle R^{\,\sigma\{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n-1}\}}=i^{\,n-1}\bar{\psi}(0)\gamma^{\,\sigma}D^{\,\{\mu_{1}}\cdots D^{\,\mu_{n-1}\}}\psi(0)-\text{traces}.

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\frac{n_{\,\mu_{1}}\cdots n_{\,\mu_{n-1}}}{2\,(P\cdot n)^{\,n}}\left\langle\,P,T\left|\,R^{\,\sigma\{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n-1}\}}\,\right|\!P,T\,\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt=S_{LL}\bar{n}^{\,\sigma}\!\int_{-1}^{1}\!dx\,x^{\,n-1}f_{1LL}(x)+\frac{M}{P\cdot n}S_{LT}^{\,\sigma}\!\int_{-1}^{1}\!dx\,x^{\,n-1}f_{LT}(x).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}f(x)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}f(x).

\displaystyle\hskip-7.11317pt\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}f_{LT}^{\,\pm}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}\left[\frac{3}{2}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}f_{1LL}^{\,\pm}(y)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2n}d_{n}.

(27)

On the right-hand side, the first term comes from
the twist-2 term and the second one is from
the dynamical twist-3 term as given by the coefficient
d_{n}
.
This equation indicates that
the PDF
f_{LT}(x)
is written in terms of
the WW-like term
f_{LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{twist-2}}(x)

and the remaining dynamical twist-3 one
f_{LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{(HT)}}(x)

\displaystyle f_{LT}^{\,\pm}(x)=f_{LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{twist-2}}(x)+f_{LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{(HT)}}(x),

(28)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptf_{LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{twist-2}}(x)=\frac{3}{2}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}f_{1LL}^{\,\pm}(y),

(29)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{\,n-1}f_{LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{(HT)}}(x)=-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2n}d_{n}.

(30)

Here,
f_{LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{twist-2}}
is
the twist-3 function part determined by the twist-2 function
f_{1LL}
,
and it contains kinematical and intrinsic terms

[
21
,
22
]
.
Equation (
29
) is the WW-like relation
obtained in Ref.
[
13
]
.
If we define the distribution
f_{2LT}^{\,\pm}(x)
by

\displaystyle f_{2LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{twist-2}}(x)=\frac{2}{3}f_{LT}^{\,\pm}(x)-f_{1LL}^{\,\pm}(x),

(31)

Eq. (
29
) becomes

\displaystyle f_{2LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{twist-2}}(x)=-f_{1LL}^{\pm}(x)+\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}f_{1LL}^{\pm}(y),

(32)

which is the same form as the original WW relation
in Eq. (
8
). Integrating this equation over
x
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx\,f_{2LT}^{\,\pm\,\text{twist-2}}(x)=0,

(33)

which is similar to the BC sum rule for
g_{2}

in Eq. (
9
).
We note that the local operators in Eq. (
16
)
are defined for
n\geq 2
, so that

]
;

In this way, we confirmed the WW- and BC-like relations derived in

[

]
by the independent formal method.
Therefore, these relations can be used as reliable first estimate
of the twist-3 function
f_{LT}(x)
in future theoretical and
experimental studies.
The JLab experimental results will be reported in the near future
on the tensor-polarized deuteron. The JLab data could contain
significant higher-twist contributions in a few GeV
2

so that this work should be valuable.
There are recent theoretical estimates of
f_{LT}
for the deuteron

[

]
and the
\rho

[

]
.
The
f_{LT}
of the deuteron could be measured

[

\phi_{LT}
)

The tensor-polarized PDFs contain different aspects of high-energy
spin physics from the ones of the spin-1/2 nucleon.
Especially, they are sensitive to dynamical aspects beyond
the simple addition of proton and neutron structure functions.
A new spin-physics field could be created by investigating
spin-1 PDFs and structure functions along the experimental projects
at the aforementioned lepton and hadron accelerator facilities
starting from the JLab experiment.

4
Summary

Using the OPE method with the local operators,
we derived the WW-like relation and the BC-like sum rule
for the twist-3 tensor-polarized PDF
f_{LT}
.
The results confirm the relations obtained in Ref.
[
13
]
,
where the matrix element of the non-local operator was expanded
in terms of possible Lorentz vectors,
by an independent formal method. Now, these relations can be used
as reliable ones in future theoretical and experimental studies.
In particular, the JLab data will be reported
for the tensor-polarized deuteron and they could contain
higher-twist effects, so that our studies should be valuable
in analyzing the experimental data and in making theoretical models.
