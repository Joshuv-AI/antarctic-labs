Premacular hemorrhage after backcountry snowboarding
Authors: Chase Paulson, George Sanchez, Paul Micevych, Paul S. Bernstein
Published: 2026

Case report

A 33-year-old African-American man presented for evaluation of a new scotoma in his vision. Two days prior to presentation, he was backcountry snowboarding at high altitude over 10,000 feet, and described extreme exertion and dehydration during the hike. He was otherwise healthy and denied any history of hypertension or diabetes mellitus. Blood pressure taken in the clinic was 137/95 mm Hg. He reported no pertinent family history. His visual acuity was 20/15 in the right eye and 20/150 in the left eye. Anterior segment examination of the left eye was normal. Posterior segment examination revealed a large premacular hemorrhage spanning from arcade to arcade, with mild 360-degree venous tortuosity and scattered peripheral dot hemorrhages (Fig. 1). Hemoglobin electrophoresis demonstrated hemoglobin A at 63.3%, hemoglobin A2 at 3.3%, and hemoglobin C (HbC) at 33.0%, consistent with HbC trait. Given the patient's age, a hypercoagulability lab workup was also obtained and found to be unrevealing. Given the peripheral hemorrhages and tortuous vessels, the overall presentation was most suggestive of retinopathy in the setting of HbC trait exacerbated by acute dehydration and altitude changes. Valsalva, central retinal vein occlusion, and retinal arterial microaneurysm were other etiologies on the differential.

Following completion of the workup, our patient underwent Nd:YAG laser hyaloidotomy with migration of hemorrhage into the vitreous cavity (Fig. 3) after a single 5 mJ shot aimed at 1/3rd the distance from the inferior border of the hemorrhage using the Singh mid-vitreous focusing lens. Despite anatomically successful hyaloidotomy, the re-distributed vitreous hemorrhage did not spontaneously clear, with persistently poor visual acuity at 20/250 in the left eye one month following the procedure. Given the limited visual improvement, the patient elected to proceed with pars plana vitrectomy. Discussion

In a prior study of 143 various hemoglobin allele states, 9 were heterozygous for HbC, 2 with vascular changes and one with obliteration of peripheral vessels.1 HbC trait is usually benign, not leading to proliferative retinopathy.2 HbC can impair oxygen exchange by increasing blood viscosity and decreasing erythrocyte plasticity, and in the setting of high altitude with reduced partial pressure of oxygen and acidotic state from exercise and dehydration, these factors likely led to the patient's macular hemorrhage. Previous work has shown a moderately high rate of success for Nd:YAG laser hyaloidotomy, with vitrectomy for non-clearing vitreous hemorrhage (NCVH) ultimately required in 5 of 21 eyes in one study.3

Conclusions

Hemoglobin C trait in the setting of high altitude and dehydration can induce a transient retinopathy resulting in a visually significant premacular hemorrhage. Nd:YAG laser hyaloidotomy and pars plana vitrectomy are viable treatment options for premacular hemorrhage.
