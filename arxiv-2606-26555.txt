1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u\;=\;|x|^{-\gamma}\,|u|^{p},&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
u(0,x)\;=\;\varepsilon\,u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}

under the standing assumptions

(1.2)

0<s<1,\qquad 0\leqslant\gamma<\min(2s,N),\qquad p>1,\qquad u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter. The fractional Laplacian

(-\Delta)^{s}
is the Fourier multiplier with symbol
|\xi|^{2s}
,
equivalently

(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)=c_{N,s}\,\mathrm{P.V.}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy,\qquad c_{N,s}=\frac{2^{2s}\,\Gamma(\tfrac{N+2s}{2})}{\pi^{N/2}\,|\Gamma(-s)|},

see
[
14
]
. The semigroup
\{e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}\}_{t\geqslant 0}

is given by convolution with a smooth positive kernel
G_{s}(t,\cdot)
,
recalled in Section
2
.

When
\gamma=0
, problem (
1.1
) reduces to the fractional Fujita
equation. In the classical local case
s=1
,
\gamma=0
, Fujita

[
16
]
discovered that the exponent

p_{F}=1+\tfrac{2}{N}

separates global existence (for
p>p_{F}
) from finite-time blow-up of
positive solutions (for
p\leqslant p_{F}
); the critical exponent itself was
shown to belong to the blow-up regime by Hayakawa
[
23
]
and
Kobayashi–Sirao–Tanaka
[
27
]
. Sharp lifespan
estimates were obtained by Lee–Ni
[
29
]
. For higher-order
counterparts (
s=m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma=0
) we refer to

[
38
,
39
,
31
,
33
,
41
]
,
and to
[
42
]
for an exterior-domain treatment.

The nonlocal case (
0<s<1
,
\gamma=0
) goes back to Sugitani

[
37
]
and was further developed by Guedda–Kirane

[
21
,
22
]
, Fino–Karch
[
15
]
,
Alsaedi–Ahmad–Kirane–Nabti
[
2
]
, and many others.
More recently, Biagi–Punzo–Vecchi
[
3
]
and Del Pezzo–Ferreira

[
13
]
obtained Fujita-type results for mixed local–nonlocal
operators, identifying the critical exponent
p_{F}=1+\tfrac{2s}{N}

when
\gamma=0
; their methods rest on the Kaplan eigenfunction technique

[
26
]
and on the construction of explicit global supersolutions,
respectively. These results were subsequently extended in a recent work by Kumar and the second author
[
28
]
. For the weighted (
\gamma>0
) Hardy–Hénon parabolic
problem we mention
[
10
,
12
,
30
,
1
]
and, for the associated
stationary classification,
[
43
,
11
]
.

The singular weight
|x|^{-\gamma}
with
\gamma>0
strengthens the
nonlinear interaction near the origin and modifies the natural scaling
structure of the equation. In fact, equation (
1.1
) (without the
initial condition) is invariant under

(1.3)

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{\frac{2s-\gamma}{p-1}}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2s}t),\qquad\lambda>0,

and a straightforward computation gives

\|u_{\lambda}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{q}}\sim\lambda^{\frac{2s-\gamma}{p-1}-\frac{N}{q}}.

In particular, the
L^{1}
-norm is scale-invariant precisely when

(1.4)

p=p_{F}:=1+\frac{2s-\gamma}{N},

so that
p_{F}
arises naturally as the Fujita critical exponent
of (
1.1
).

We are now in a position to state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume (
1.2
) and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)\,dx>0
.
Then (
1.1
) admits a unique mild solution

u\in C\big([0,T_{\varepsilon});\,L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big),

where the lifespan
T_{\varepsilon}
satisfies, for all
\varepsilon>0

sufficiently small,

(1.5)

T_{\varepsilon}\;\approx\;\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-\,{1\over\beta}},&1<p<p_{F},\\[4.2679pt]
\exp\!\big(C\,\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\big),&p=p_{F},\\[4.2679pt]
+\infty,&p>p_{F},\end{cases}

with

(1.6)

\beta\;=\;\frac{1}{p-1}-\frac{N}{2s}-\frac{\gamma}{2s(p-1)}\;=\;\frac{(2s-\gamma)-N(p-1)}{2s(p-1)}\;>\;0\qquad(1<p<p_{F}),

equivalently

T_{\varepsilon}\approx\varepsilon^{-\frac{2s(p-1)}{(2s-\gamma)-N(p-1)}}

in the subcritical range. The two-sided estimate in the subcritical and
critical regimes is established under the additional restriction

\gamma<2s(p-1)

(
see Section
5
and
Remark
5.1
)
; for
\gamma=0
this restriction is
vacuous.

Remark 1.1
.

For
\gamma=0
formula (
1.6
) reduces to

\beta=\tfrac{1}{p-1}-\tfrac{N}{2s}
, recovering the fractional
Lee–Ni exponent, and for
s=1,\gamma=0
the classical Lee–Ni estimate

[
29
]
. The exponent (
1.6
) vanishes precisely at

p=p_{F}
, consistently with the exponential lifespan in the critical case.

Remark 1.2
.

The natural “Lee–Ni-like” guess

\bigl(\tfrac{1}{p-1}-\tfrac{N}{2s-\gamma}\bigr)^{-1}
— obtained by
formally replacing
2s
with
2s-\gamma
in the Lee–Ni formula — is

not
the correct sharp rate when
\gamma>0
. The two expressions
agree only at
p=p_{F}
(both vanish) and at
\gamma=0
. The discrepancy
stems from the fact that the Hardy weight enters the integrability of the
nonlinearity
additively
(through the exponent
\alpha

in (
2.8
)), while the underlying linear semigroup retains the
unweighted decay
\|e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}\leqslant Ct^{-N/(2s)}
.

Strategy and structure

The lower bound on
T_{\varepsilon}
is obtained by a bootstrap based on the
auxiliary functional

M(t)\;=\;\sup_{0\leqslant\tau<t}\Bigl\{(1+\tau)^{N/(2s)}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}\Bigr\},

the sharp
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
smoothing of the fractional heat semigroup, and the
weighted interpolation

\||x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1+\gamma/N}\|u\|_{L^{1}}^{1-\gamma/N}

(Lemma
2.2
). The upper bound is established by testing the
equation against the
backward fractional heat kernel

G_{s}(b-t,\cdot)
: self-adjointness of
(-\Delta)^{s}
makes the linear terms cancel
identically, a Hölder rearrangement neutralizes the weight, and the
problem reduces to a scalar differential inequality. Crucially, this uses a
globally defined, polynomially decaying test function, thereby avoiding
compactly supported cutoffs, which are incompatible with the nonlocal
operator (Section
5
).

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
presents the preliminary tools needed throughout the
paper, including heat-kernel estimates and a weighted interpolation
inequality. In Section
3
, we establish local well-posedness
and the corresponding blow-up alternative. Sections
4

and
5
are devoted to the proof of the lower and upper lifespan
bounds stated in (
1.5
). Section
6
addresses
the large-data case and derives the corresponding non-decaying lifespan law.
In Section
7
, we characterize the blow-up rate for
nonnegative solutions, while Section
8
is dedicated to the
analysis of the self-similar blow-up profile. Finally,
Section
9
concludes the paper with several remarks and
perspectives.

We write
A\lesssim B
if
A\leqslant CB

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The fractional heat kernel

The semigroup
\{e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
generated by
(-\Delta)^{s}
,
s\in(0,1)
,
acts on Schwartz functions
f
as

e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}f(x)=(G_{s}(t,\cdot)\ast f)(x),

where the kernel
G_{s}
is positive, radial, smooth in

(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and obeys the parabolic scaling

(2.1)

G_{s}(t,x)\;=\;t^{-N/(2s)}\,\mathscr{K}_{s}\!\bigl(t^{-1/(2s)}x\bigr),

with profile

(2.2)

\mathscr{K}_{s}(x)\;=\;(2\pi)^{-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,e^{-|\xi|^{2s}}\,d\xi.

For
s=1/2
,
\mathscr{K}_{s}
is the Poisson kernel

(2.3)

\mathscr{K}_{1/2}(x)\;=\;\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{N+1}{2})}{\pi^{(N+1)/2}}\,(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{N+1}{2}},

so that

G_{1/2}(t,x)=\frac{\Gamma((N+1)/2)\,t}{\pi^{(N+1)/2}(t^{2}+|x|^{2})^{(N+1)/2}}.

For general
s\in(0,1)
the profile has no closed form, but admits the
sharp two-sided bound below.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
s\in(0,1)
there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

(2.4)

c_{1}\,\langle x\rangle^{-N-2s}\;\leqslant\;\mathscr{K}_{s}(x)\;\leqslant\;c_{2}\,\langle x\rangle^{-N-2s},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

In particular
\mathscr{K}_{s}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
r\in[1,\infty]
.

Proof.

The result is classical; see, e.g.,
Blumenthal–Getoor
[
4
, Thm. 2.1]
,
Miao–Yuan–Zhang
[
32
, Lemma 2.1]
, or
Peral Alonso–Soria de Diego
[
34
, p. 395]
. The main
ingredient is the subordination formula

(2.5)

\mathscr{K}_{s}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(4\pi t)^{-N/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/(4t)}\,\chi_{s}(t)\,dt,

where
\chi_{s}\geqslant 0
is the inverse Laplace transform of

\lambda\mapsto e^{-\lambda^{s}}
. Since the Gaussian kernel is positive and

\chi_{s}\geqslant 0
, the positivity of
\mathscr{K}_{s}
follows immediately.
∎

The next two facts are workhorses of the paper.

Proposition 2.1

(
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
smoothing)
.

For all
1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
there exists
C>0
such that

(2.6)

\|e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\;\leqslant\;C\,t^{-\frac{N}{2s}\!\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\qquad t>0,\ f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Proof.

By Young’s convolution inequality with
1/r=1+1/q-1/p\in[1/q,1]
, we have

\|e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}f\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant\|G_{s}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{r}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

From (
2.1
),

\|G_{s}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{r}}=t^{-\frac{N}{2s}(1-\frac{1}{r})}\,\|\mathscr{K}_{s}\|_{L^{r}},

and
\mathscr{K}_{s}\in L^{r}
by Lemma
2.1
. Since

1-1/r=1/p-1/q
, this is the claim.
∎

Proposition 2.2

(Positivity, contraction, mass)
.

For every
t\geqslant 0
,
e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}
maps nonnegative functions to nonnegative
functions,
\|e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}f\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{q}}
for
1\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{s}(t,x)\,dx=1
.

Proof.

Positivity follows from
G_{s}\geqslant 0
(Lemma
2.1
); the mass
identity from
\widehat{G_{s}}(t,0)=e^{-t|0|^{2s}}=1
; and the contraction
from Young’s inequality with
r=1
in (
2.6
).
∎

2.2.
A weighted interpolation inequality

For
0\leqslant\gamma<N
the weight
|x|^{-\gamma}
is locally integrable, and the
nonlinearity in (
1.1
) is controlled by a one-line interpolation
between
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Weighted interpolation)
.

Let
0\leqslant\gamma<N
and
p>1
. Then for every

u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(2.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\gamma}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx\;\leqslant\;C\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\,p-1+\gamma/N}\,\|u\|_{L^{1}}^{\,1-\gamma/N},\qquad C=C(N,\gamma,p)>0.

Proof.

Set
L:=\|u\|_{L^{1}}
,
M:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
and split, for
R>0
,

\displaystyle I:

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|<R}+\int_{|x|\geqslant R}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Using
|u|^{p}\leqslant M^{p}
and
\gamma<N
,

I_{1}\leqslant M^{p}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-\gamma}=\tfrac{\omega_{N-1}}{N-\gamma}M^{p}R^{N-\gamma};

using
|u|^{p}\leqslant M^{p-1}|u|
,

I_{2}\leqslant R^{-\gamma}M^{p-1}L.

Balancing with
R^{N}=L/M
gives

R^{N-\gamma}=L^{1-\gamma/N}M^{-1+\gamma/N}
,

R^{-\gamma}=L^{-\gamma/N}M^{\gamma/N}
, whence

I\leqslant C\,M^{p-1+\gamma/N}L^{1-\gamma/N}
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Inequality (
2.7
) also follows from Hölder’s inequality in
Lorentz spaces, using
|x|^{-\gamma}\in L^{N/\gamma,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;
see
[
20
, Sect. 1.4]
. Under (
1.2
) we have

\gamma<\min(2s,N)\leqslant N
, so the lemma applies throughout.

The scaling exponent appearing repeatedly below is

(2.8)

\alpha\;:=\;\frac{N(p-1)+\gamma}{2s}.

Observe that
\alpha=1\iff p=p_{F}
, and subcritical
\iff\alpha<1
.

3.
Local well-posedness

Definition 3.1

(Mild solution)
.

Given
T>0
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
, a function

u\in C\bigl([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigr)

is a
mild solution
of (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
if

(3.1)

u(t)\;=\;\varepsilon\,e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}u_{0}\;+\;\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)(-\Delta)^{s}}\bigl(|x|^{-\gamma}|u(\tau)|^{p}\bigr)\,d\tau,\qquad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

The
lifespan

T_{\varepsilon}
is the supremum of
T>0
for which a
unique mild solution exists on
[0,T]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Local existence and blow-up alternative)
.

For every
u_{0}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exist

T=T(\varepsilon,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}})>0
and a unique mild solution

u\in C([0,T];L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})
of (
1.1
). Moreover, there is a
maximal existence time
T_{\max}=T_{\max}(\varepsilon u_{0})\leqslant\infty
such
that
u\in C([0,T_{\max});L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})
, and either
T_{\max}=\infty

or

(3.2)

\lim_{t\to T_{\max}^{-}}\bigl(\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\bigr)=+\infty\,\,(T_{\max}^{-}<\infty).

The only delicate point is that
|x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}\notin L^{\infty}
, so the

L^{\infty}
-norm of
\Phi(u)(t)
is controlled through an
L^{r}
-to-
L^{\infty}

smoothing for some
r\in(N/(2s),N/\gamma)
, not through an

L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
contraction.

Proof.

Fix

(3.3)

\frac{N}{2s}\;<\;r\;<\;\frac{N}{\gamma}\qquad\text{(non-empty since $\gamma<2s$).}

For
T,K>0
set

X_{T,K}=\bigl\{u\in C([0,T];L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}):\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leqslant K\bigr\},\quad\|u\|_{X_{T}}=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\!\bigl(\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigr),

with the natural metric, and define

\Phi(u)(t)=\varepsilon e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)(-\Delta)^{s}}(|x|^{-\gamma}|u(\tau)|^{p})\,d\tau.

Step 1 (nonlinearity in
L^{1}
and
L^{r}
).

By Lemma
2.2
,

(3.4)

\begin{split}\bigl\||x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}\bigr\|_{L^{1}}&\leqslant C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1+\gamma/N}\|u\|_{L^{1}}^{1-\gamma/N}\\
&\leqslant CK^{p}.\end{split}

For the
L^{r}
-norm, split
|x|^{-\gamma r}
over
|x|<1
(integrable, since

\gamma r<N
by (
3.3
)) and
|x|\geqslant 1
(bounded by
1
):

\displaystyle\bigl\||x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}\bigr\|_{L^{r}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{pr}\!\!\int_{|x|<1}\!|x|^{-\gamma r}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{pr-1}\!\int_{|x|\geqslant 1}\!|u|

\displaystyle\leqslant CK^{pr},

hence

(3.5)

\bigl\||x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}\bigr\|_{L^{r}}\leqslant CK^{p}.

Step 2 (
L^{1}
estimate).

By (
3.1
), Proposition
2.2

and (
3.4
),

\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leqslant\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C\,T\,K^{p}.

Step 3 (
L^{\infty}
estimate).

Since
\|e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and, by
Proposition
2.1
from
L^{r}
to
L^{\infty}

with (
3.5
),

\Bigl\|e^{-(t-\tau)(-\Delta)^{s}}\bigl(|x|^{-\gamma}|u(\tau)|^{p}\bigr)\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C(t-\tau)^{-\frac{N}{2sr}}K^{p},\qquad\tfrac{N}{2sr}<1,

the time integral converges and

\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\,T^{1-\frac{N}{2sr}}K^{p}.

Step 4 (choice of
K,T
).

Combining,

\|\Phi(u)\|_{X_{T}}\leqslant 2\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}+C(T+T^{1-N/(2sr)})K^{p}.

Take
K=4\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}
and
T
small enough that

C(T+T^{1-N/(2sr)})K^{p-1}\leqslant 1/2
; then
\Phi:X_{T,K}\to X_{T,K}
. With

|a|^{p}-|b|^{p}\leqslant p(|a|^{p-1}+|b|^{p-1})|a-b|
and Hölder,

\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{X_{T}}\leqslant C(T+T^{1-N/(2sr)})K^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{X_{T}},

a contraction by the same choice. Banach’s fixed-point theorem yields a
unique mild solution; continuity in
t
follows from (
3.1
).

Step 5 (maximal solution and blow-up alternative).

By uniqueness, the solution extends to a maximal interval
[0,T_{\max})
,

T_{\max}:=\sup\bigl\{T>0:\ \eqref{eq:main}\text{ has a mild solution on }[0,T]\bigr\}\leqslant\infty.

Suppose
T_{\max}<\infty
but

\liminf_{t\to T_{\max}^{-}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}=:L<\infty.

Pick
t_{m}\to T_{\max}
with
\sup_{m}\|u(t_{m})\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\leqslant L+1
.
The local theory, applied with datum
u(t_{m})
, produces a uniform existence
time
T(L+1)>0
depending only on
L+1
, so the solution continues on

[t_{m},t_{m}+T(L+1)]
for every
m
; hence
T_{\max}\geqslant t_{m}+T(L+1)
. Letting

m\to\infty
gives
T_{\max}\geqslant T_{\max}+T(L+1)
, impossible. Thus

\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\to\infty
as
t\to T_{\max}^{-}
, which
is (
3.2
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

The interval (

) is non-empty thanks to

. For

and

4.1.
The auxiliary functional and the key a priori bound

For a mild solution

By Theorem

[0,T)
, with

M(0^{+})\leqslant C\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}
.

Lemma 4.1

(A priori estimate)
.

There exist
C_{0},C_{1}>0
depending only on
N,s,\gamma,p,u_{0}
such that, for
every
0\leqslant t<T
,

(4.2)

M(t)\;\leqslant\;C_{0}\,\varepsilon\;+\;C_{1}\,M(t)^{p}\,\int_{0}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-\alpha}\,d\tau.

Proof.

We control
\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}}
and
(1+t)^{N/(2s)}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

separately, repeatedly using (via Lemma
2.2
and (
4.1
))

\displaystyle\||x|^{-\gamma}|u(\tau)|^{p}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1+\gamma/N}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}^{1-\gamma/N}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,M(t)^{p}(1+\tau)^{-\alpha}

Step 1 (
L^{1}
).

By (
3.1
) and Proposition
2.2
,

(4.3)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leqslant\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C\,M(t)^{p}\!\int_{0}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-\alpha}\,d\tau.

Step 2 (
L^{\infty}
for
t\geqslant 1
).

Split the Duhamel integral at
\tau=t/2
and use Proposition
2.1
:

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\varepsilon\,t^{-N/(2s)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C\!\int_{0}^{t/2}\!(t-\tau)^{-N/(2s)}\bigl\||x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}\bigr\|_{L^{1}}d\tau+(\mathrm{II}),

where
(\mathrm{II})
is the integral over
[t/2,t]
. On
[0,t/2]
one has

(t-\tau)\geqslant t/2
, so

(4.4)

\int_{0}^{t/2}\!(t-\tau)^{-N/(2s)}(1+\tau)^{-\alpha}\,d\tau\leqslant C\,t^{-N/(2s)}\!\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+\tau)^{-\alpha}\,d\tau.

For
(\mathrm{II})
use the intermediate exponent
r
of (
3.3
)
and Proposition
2.1
from
L^{r}
to
L^{\infty}
, together with the
interpolation

\|f\|_{L^{r}}\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{1}}^{1/r}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-1/r}
applied to

f=|x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}
:

\bigl\||x|^{-\gamma}|u(\tau)|^{p}\bigr\|_{L^{r}}\leqslant C\,M(t)^{p}\,(1+\tau)^{-\alpha+\frac{N}{2s}(1-\frac{1}{r})}.

Since
(1+\tau)\approx(1+t)
on
[t/2,t]
,

(4.5)

\begin{split}(\mathrm{II})&\leqslant C\,M(t)^{p}\,(1+t)^{-\alpha+\frac{N}{2s}(1-\frac{1}{r})}\,t^{1-N/(2sr)}\\
&\leqslant C\,M(t)^{p}\,(1+t)^{1-\alpha-N/(2s)}.\end{split}

Multiplying by
(1+t)^{N/(2s)}
and combining,

(4.6)

(1+t)^{N/(2s)}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}+C\,M(t)^{p}\!\int_{0}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-\alpha}\,d\tau;

for
0<t\leqslant 1
the bound is immediate from Step 3 of
Theorem
3.1
.

Step 3.
Adding (
4.3
) and (
4.6
) and taking the
supremum over
0\leqslant\tau<t
yields (
4.2
) with

C_{0}=C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})
.
∎

4.2.
The bootstrap

Subcritical case
1<p<p_{F}
.

Since
\alpha<1
, we have that

\int_{0}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-\alpha}d\tau\leqslant(1-\alpha)^{-1}(1+t)^{1-\alpha},

so (
4.2
) becomes

(4.7)

M(t)\leqslant C_{0}\varepsilon+C_{2}\,M(t)^{p}\,(1+t)^{1-\alpha},\qquad 1-\alpha=\tfrac{(2s-\gamma)-N(p-1)}{2s}>0.

Let
T_{1}:=\sup\{t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}):M(t)\leqslant 2C_{0}\varepsilon\}
, which is
positive by continuity and
M(0^{+})\leqslant C_{0}\varepsilon
. If
T_{1}<\infty
then

M(T_{1})=2C_{0}\varepsilon
, and (
4.7
) at
t=T_{1}
gives

1\leqslant C\varepsilon^{p-1}(1+T_{1})^{1-\alpha}
, whence

(4.8)

1+T_{1}\geqslant C\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{p-1}{1-\alpha}}=C\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2s(p-1)}{(2s-\gamma)-N(p-1)}}=C\,\varepsilon^{-1/\beta}.

Therefore

(4.9)

T_{\varepsilon}\geqslant T_{1}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-1/\beta}.

Critical case
p=p_{F}
.

Now
\alpha=1
and

\int_{0}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-1}d\tau=\log(1+t),

so

M(t)\leqslant C_{0}\varepsilon+C_{3}M(t)^{p}\log(1+t)
. The same bootstrap yields

\log(1+T_{1})\geqslant C\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}
, i.e.

(4.10)

T_{\varepsilon}\gtrsim\exp\!\bigl(C\,\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\bigr).

Supercritical case
p>p_{F}
.

Here
\alpha>1
,
\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+\tau)^{-\alpha}d\tau<\infty
, and
(
4.2
) reads
M(t)\leqslant C_{0}\varepsilon+C_{4}M(t)^{p}
uniformly in

t
. A standard fixed-point argument
(cf.
[
7
,
36
]
) shows that for
\varepsilon

small
M
is bounded on
[0,\infty)
and
T_{\varepsilon}=+\infty
.

5.
Upper lifespan estimate

Throughout,
c_{0}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}\,dx>0
, and we assume

(5.1)

p>1+\frac{\gamma}{2s}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\gamma<2s(p-1),

a restriction intrinsic to the method (Remark
5.1
).

Because
(-\Delta)^{s}
is nonlocal, the test-function technique
cannot
be
implemented with a compactly supported cutoff: there is no chain rule for

(-\Delta)^{s}(\phi\circ\sigma)
, no Faà di Bruno formula for it
[
25
]
,
and, decisively,
(-\Delta)^{s}\phi
is never compactly supported when
\phi
is.
Indeed, for
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
|x|
large,

(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(x)=-c_{N,s}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\;\sim\;-\,c_{N,s}\Big(\textstyle\int\phi\Big)\,|x|^{-(N+2s)},

so the pointwise bound
|(-\Delta)^{s}\psi_{R}|\leqslant CR^{-1}\psi_{R}^{1/p}

on

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{R}
is false. We use instead a globally defined test
function for which
(-\Delta)^{s}
is controlled
everywhere
, the backward
fractional heat kernel; the linear contribution then cancels identically,
and no composition is differentiated.

Recall
G_{s}(t,x)=t^{-N/(2s)}\mathscr{K}_{s}(t^{-1/(2s)}x)
and the two-sided
bound of Lemma
2.1
. Put
\mu:=\gamma/(2s)\in[0,1)
and

(5.2)

C_{*}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|y|^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}\,\mathscr{K}_{s}(y)\,dy.

Lemma 5.1
.

C_{*}<\infty
if and only if (
5.1
) holds.

Proof.

Near the origin
|y|^{\gamma/(p-1)}
is locally integrable since

\gamma/(p-1)\geqslant 0>-N
. At infinity, by Lemma
2.1
,

|y|^{\gamma/(p-1)}\mathscr{K}_{s}(y)\asymp|y|^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}-(N+2s)}
,
which is integrable iff
\frac{\gamma}{p-1}<2s
.
∎

For
b>0
define the
backward-kernel functional

(5.3)

g_{b}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x,t)\,G_{s}(b-t,x)\,dx,\qquad 0\leqslant t<b\wedge T_{\varepsilon}.

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
b>0
and
0\leqslant t<b\wedge T_{\varepsilon}
,

(5.4)

g_{b}^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\gamma}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,G_{s}(b-t,x)\,dx\;\geqslant\;0.

Proof.

The function
\phi(x,t):=G_{s}(b-t,x)
is smooth and bounded, together with

\partial_{t}\phi
and
(-\Delta)^{s}\phi
, on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,t_{2}]
for every
t_{2}<b
,
and solves the backward equation

\partial_{t}\phi-(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=-\partial_{b}G_{s}(b-t,\cdot)-(-\Delta)^{s}G_{s}(b-t,\cdot)=0,

since
\partial_{b}G_{s}=-(-\Delta)^{s}G_{s}
. Testing the (mild, hence weak) formulation
of (
1.1
) against
\phi
and using self-adjointness of
(-\Delta)^{s}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\int u\,\phi

\displaystyle=\int u\,(\partial_{t}\phi-(-\Delta)^{s}\phi)+\int|x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}\,\phi

\displaystyle=\int|x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}\,\phi,

which is (
5.4
). Nonnegativity follows from
G_{s}>0
.
∎

By (
5.4
)
g_{b}
is nondecreasing, and
g_{b}(0)>0
for
b
large
(see (
5.6
) below), so
g_{b}(t)\geqslant g_{b}(0)>0
. Hölder with
exponents
p,p^{\prime}
applied to

u\,G_{s}=(|x|^{-\gamma/p}u\,G_{s}^{1/p})(|x|^{\gamma/p}G_{s}^{1/p^{\prime}})

gives

0<g_{b}(t)\leqslant\Big(\int|x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}G_{s}(b-t,\cdot)\Big)^{1/p}A(b-t)^{1/p^{\prime}},\quad A(b):=\int|x|^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}G_{s}(b,\cdot)\,dx.

The scaling of
G_{s}
yields
A(b)=b^{\mu/(p-1)}C_{*}
, finite by
Lemma
5.1
. Combining with (
5.4
),

(5.5)

g_{b}^{\prime}(t)\;\geqslant\;c_{1}\,(b-t)^{-\mu}\,g_{b}(t)^{p},\qquad c_{1}:=C_{*}^{-(p-1)},

on
[0,b\wedge T_{\varepsilon})
.

Data.
Since
u_{0}\in L^{1}
and
\mathscr{K}_{s}(b^{-1/(2s)}x)\to\mathscr{K}_{s}(0)
,
dominated convergence gives

b^{N/(2s)}g_{b}(0)=\varepsilon\int u_{0}\,\mathscr{K}_{s}(b^{-1/(2s)}x)\,dx\to\varepsilon\,\mathscr{K}_{s}(0)\,c_{0},

so there are
b_{*},\kappa>0
with

(5.6)

g_{b}(0)\;\geqslant\;\kappa\,\varepsilon\,b^{-N/(2s)},\qquad b\geqslant b_{*}.

(No sign condition on
u_{0}
is used, only
c_{0}>0
.)

Subcritical case
1<p<p_{F}

Here
\beta>0
, equivalently

(5.7)

1-\mu-\frac{N(p-1)}{2s}=1-\alpha=(p-1)\beta>0.

Suppose, for contradiction, that
T_{\varepsilon}>b
for some
b\geqslant b_{*}
. Then

g_{b}
is finite on
[0,b)
; dividing (
5.5
) by
g_{b}^{p}
and
integrating over
[0,t]
, we have

g_{b}(t)^{-(p-1)}\leqslant g_{b}(0)^{-(p-1)}-(p-1)c_{1}\,\Theta(t),

with

\Theta(t):=\int_{0}^{t}(b-\tau)^{-\mu}d\tau=\frac{b^{1-\mu}-(b-t)^{1-\mu}}{1-\mu}.

The right-hand side vanishes at some
t^{*}<b
— forcing
g_{b}(t^{*})=+\infty

and hence
T_{\varepsilon}\leqslant t^{*}<b
, a contradiction — provided

\Theta(b^{-})=\frac{b^{1-\mu}}{1-\mu}>\frac{g_{b}(0)^{-(p-1)}}{(p-1)c_{1}}.

By
(
5.6
) it suffices that

\frac{b^{1-\mu}}{1-\mu}>\frac{\kappa^{-(p-1)}}{(p-1)c_{1}}\,\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\,b^{N(p-1)/(2s)},

i.e., using (
5.7
),
b^{(p-1)\beta}>C\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}
, i.e.

b>C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-1/\beta}
. Hence
T_{\varepsilon}\leqslant b
for every

b>\max\{b_{*},C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-1/\beta}\}
, whence

(5.8)

T_{\varepsilon}\;\leqslant\;C^{\prime}\,\varepsilon^{-1/\beta}\;=\;C^{\prime}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2s(p-1)}{(2s-\gamma)-N(p-1)}}

for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.

Critical case
p=p_{F}

Now
\beta=0
, so (
5.7
) reads
1-\mu=N(p-1)/(2s)
and the
threshold becomes
b
-independent: a single backward kernel no longer forces
blow-up. The mechanism is transparent from the subcritical estimate: the
blow-up threshold and the initial datum bound now scale with the
same

power of
b
, so the two powers cancel identically and no individual scale

b
can force blow-up. The remedy is to superpose the inequality
(
5.5
) over a dyadic band of scales: one replaces the single
profile
\phi(x,t)=G_{s}(b-t,x)
by the scale-superposition

\int_{b_{*}}^{b}G_{s}(r-t,x)\,\frac{dr}{r}
, under which the band
[b_{*},b]

carries logarithmic mass
\log(b/b_{*})
. Each dyadic scale contributes a
critically balanced amount, and summing the
\asymp\log(b/b_{*})
contributions
produces an extra factor
\log(b/b_{*})
in the integrated inequality, which
upgrades the (scale-invariant) marginal condition to

\log(b/b_{*})\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}
; letting
b\uparrow T_{\varepsilon}

yields
\log(T_{\varepsilon}/b_{*})\leqslant C\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}
, i.e.

(5.9)

T_{\varepsilon}\;\leqslant\;\exp\!\big(C\,\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\big).

This is exactly the computation of
[
24
]
(see
also
[
17
,
41
]
); the only change is that the
global, polynomially decaying profile
\mathscr{K}_{s}
replaces the cutoff,
while Lemma
5.2
supplies the same exact linear cancellation, so
the argument transfers verbatim to the nonlocal setting.

Supercritical case
p>p_{F}

Then
\beta<0
, the threshold
b>C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-1/\beta}
is never met for
large
b
, and the method gives no upper bound, consistent with the global
existence of Section
4
.

Together with Section
4
, (
5.8
)
and (
5.9
) complete the proof of the upper bound in
Theorem
1.1
on the range (
5.1
). ∎

Remark 5.1
.

The restriction (
5.1
) is exactly the finiteness of
C_{*}

(Lemma
5.1
), forced by the polynomial decay

\mathscr{K}_{s}(y)\asymp\langle y\rangle^{-(N+2s)}
: no admissible profile
decays faster, so
\int|y|^{\gamma/(p-1)}\mathscr{K}_{s}
diverges once

\gamma/(p-1)\geqslant 2s
. For
\gamma=0
the condition is vacuous and the whole
range
1<p\leqslant p_{F}
is covered, recovering the fractional Lee–Ni law. For

\gamma>0
the sharp two-sided law on
1<p\leqslant 1+\gamma/(2s)
remains open.

6.
Large-data lifespan estimate

We now consider
non-decaying
data. We assume

(6.1)

u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad u_{0}(x)\geqslant c_{0}>0\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

together with the range (
5.1
),
\gamma<2s(p-1)
. Note that

u_{0}\geqslant c_{0}
entails
u_{0}\notin L^{1}
, so the relevant well-posedness is the

L^{\infty}
theory established in the lower bound below, rather than the

L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
theory of Section
3
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume (
6.1
) and
\gamma<2s(p-1)
. Then for all sufficiently
small
\varepsilon>0
the lifespan of the nonnegative solution
of (
1.1
) satisfies

T_{\varepsilon}\;\approx\;\varepsilon^{-\frac{2s(p-1)}{2s-\gamma}}.

6.1.
Lower bound

We use the following weighted smoothing estimate.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
0\leqslant\gamma<\min(2s,N)
there is
C>0
such that, for all
t>0
,

(6.2)

\bigl\|e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}\bigl(|x|^{-\gamma}f\bigr)\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\;\leqslant\;C\,t^{-\gamma/(2s)}\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Proof.

We have

|e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}(|x|^{-\gamma}f)(x)|\leqslant\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{s}(t,x-y)|y|^{-\gamma}\,dy.

With
G_{s}(t,z)=t^{-N/(2s)}\mathscr{K}_{s}(t^{-1/(2s)}z)
and
y=t^{1/(2s)}w
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{s}(t,x-y)|y|^{-\gamma}\,dy=t^{-\gamma/(2s)}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathscr{K}_{s}(\xi-w)\,|w|^{-\gamma}\,dw,\qquad\xi:=t^{-1/(2s)}x.

The last integral is bounded uniformly in
\xi
: near
w=0
,
|w|^{-\gamma}

is integrable (
\gamma<N
) and
\mathscr{K}_{s}
is bounded; near infinity,

\mathscr{K}_{s}(\xi-w)\lesssim\langle\xi-w\rangle^{-(N+2s)}
is integrable
against
|w|^{-\gamma}
uniformly in
\xi
by Lemma
2.1
.
This gives (
6.2
).
∎

Work in
X_{T}:=L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
with

\|u\|_{X_{T}}=\sup_{0<t<T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and let

B_{T}:=\{u\in X_{T}:\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leqslant M\varepsilon\}
with

M>2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. For
u\in B_{T}
, by (
6.2
) and

\gamma/(2s)<1
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\!\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\gamma/(2s)}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C(M\varepsilon)^{p}\,t^{1-\gamma/(2s)}.

Choosing
T
so that
CM^{p}\varepsilon^{p-1}T^{1-\gamma/(2s)}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}
,
i.e.

T=c\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{p-1}{1-\gamma/(2s)}}=c\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2s(p-1)}{2s-\gamma}},

and shrinking
c
if necessary so that
\Phi
is a contraction on
B_{T}
(via

|u^{p}-v^{p}|\leqslant C(|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1})|u-v|
and (
6.2
)),
the solution exists at least up to
T
. Hence

(6.3)

T_{\varepsilon}\;\geqslant\;c\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2s(p-1)}{2s-\gamma}}.

This step uses only
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}
,
u_{0}\geqslant 0
.

6.2.
Upper bound

We reuse the backward-kernel functional and differential inequality of
Section
5
. For
b>0
put
g_{b}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(t)\,G_{s}(b-t,\cdot)
.
Since
u(t)\in L^{\infty}
and
G_{s}(b-t,\cdot)\in L^{1}
,
g_{b}
is finite on

[0,b\wedge T_{\varepsilon})
, and Lemma
5.2
yields

g_{b}^{\prime}(t)=\int|x|^{-\gamma}|u(t)|^{p}G_{s}(b-t,\cdot)\geqslant 0.

Because

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{s}(b,\cdot)\,dx=1
(Proposition
2.2
) and

u_{0}\geqslant c_{0}
a.e.,

(6.4)

g_{b}(0)=\varepsilon\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}\,G_{s}(b,\cdot)\,dx\;\geqslant\;\varepsilon\,c_{0}>0,

so
g_{b}(t)\geqslant\varepsilon c_{0}>0
. The Hölder step of Section
5

(with
A(b)=b^{\mu/(p-1)}C_{*}
, finite by Lemma
5.1
) gives,
exactly as in (
5.5
),

g_{b}^{\prime}(t)\geqslant c_{1}(b-t)^{-\mu}g_{b}(t)^{p},\qquad\mu=\tfrac{\gamma}{2s}\in[0,1),\ c_{1}=C_{*}^{-(p-1)}.

Suppose
T_{\varepsilon}>b
. Dividing by
g_{b}^{p}
and integrating over
[0,t]
,

g_{b}(t)^{-(p-1)}\leqslant(\varepsilon c_{0})^{-(p-1)}-(p-1)c_{1}\,\frac{b^{1-\mu}-(b-t)^{1-\mu}}{1-\mu},

whose right-hand side vanishes before
t=b
— a contradiction — as soon
as

\frac{b^{1-\mu}}{1-\mu}>\frac{(\varepsilon c_{0})^{-(p-1)}}{(p-1)c_{1}},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad b^{1-\mu}>C\,\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}.

Since
1-\mu=\dfrac{2s-\gamma}{2s}
, this reads

b>C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-2s(p-1)/(2s-\gamma)}
. Hence

(6.5)

T_{\varepsilon}\;\leqslant\;C^{\prime}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2s(p-1)}{2s-\gamma}}.

Combining (
6.3
) and (
6.5
) proves
Theorem
6.1
. ∎

Remark 6.1

(On the hypothesis)
.

A lower bound on
u_{0}
at a
single, fixed
scale is insufficient: if

u_{0}\geqslant c_{0}
only on a fixed ball
B_{r_{0}}
and decays outside, then

\int u_{0}\,G_{s}(b,\cdot)\lesssim b^{-N/(2s)}
, the datum reverts to the
small-data form (
5.6
), and one recovers the longer Fujita
lifespan of Theorem
1.1
rather than

\varepsilon^{-2s(p-1)/(2s-\gamma)}
. The mechanism here operates at the
scale
b^{1/(2s)}\sim\varepsilon^{-(p-1)/(2s-\gamma)}\to\infty
, so the datum
must be bounded below at that growing scale. Condition (
6.1
) may
be relaxed to
\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}u_{0}(x)>0
, or, sharply, to

\inf_{b\geqslant b_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}\,G_{s}(b,\cdot)\,dx>0
.

Remark 6.2

(Removing the range restriction)
.

The constant
C_{*}
confines the argument above to
\gamma<2s(p-1)
. For the
residual range
1<p\leqslant 1+\gamma/(2s)
the same rate follows for
every

p>1
from the principal Dirichlet eigenfunction
\phi_{\rho}(x)=\phi_{1}(x/\rho)

of the restricted fractional Laplacian on the ball
B_{\rho}
(its existence
and positivity are classical,
[
35
]
), with eigenvalue

\lambda_{\rho}=\lambda_{1}\rho^{-2s}
. With
F(t)=\int u\,\phi_{\rho}
, the
favorable sign of
(-\Delta)^{s}\phi_{\rho}
outside
B_{\rho}
(where
\phi_{\rho}=0
, so

(-\Delta)^{s}\phi_{\rho}<0
) and Hölder give

F^{\prime}(t)\;\geqslant\;-\lambda_{\rho}\,F(t)+A_{\rho}^{-(p-1)}F(t)^{p},\qquad A_{\rho}=\!\int_{B_{\rho}}\!|x|^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}\phi_{\rho}\,dx<\infty,

where
A_{\rho}<\infty
for
every

\rho
since
B_{\rho}
is bounded (no
constraint on
\gamma/(p-1)
). With
F(0)\gtrsim\varepsilon\rho^{N}

(legitimate because
u_{0}\geqslant c_{0}
on
B_{\rho}
by (
6.1
)),

A_{\rho}\asymp\rho^{N+\gamma/(p-1)}
and
\lambda_{\rho}=\lambda_{1}\rho^{-2s}
,
the choice
\rho\asymp\varepsilon^{-(p-1)/(2s-\gamma)}
makes the nonlinear
term dominate and yields blow-up by time

\asymp A_{\rho}^{p-1}F(0)^{-(p-1)}\asymp\rho^{\gamma}\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}=\varepsilon^{-2s(p-1)/(2s-\gamma)}
.

Remark 6.3
.

For
\gamma=0
one has
C_{*}=\int\mathscr{K}_{s}=1
(no restriction) and

T_{\varepsilon}\approx\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}
, the classical ODE-driven
large-data lifespan for
u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u=u^{p}
; here
g_{b}^{\prime}\geqslant c_{1}g_{b}^{p}
reduces to
comparison with the spatially homogeneous solution. For
\gamma>0
the
exponent
\tfrac{2s(p-1)}{2s-\gamma}>p-1
, so the singular weight shortens
the lifespan.

7.
Blow-up rate: Type-I bounds

We turn to the blow-up rate, assuming in addition
u_{0}\geqslant 0
; by
Proposition
2.2
and iteration of (
3.1
) the
mild solution stays nonnegative on
[0,T_{\varepsilon})
. Throughout this
section we write, to avoid clashing with the dimension
N
,

\mathcal{N}(t):=\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}},\qquad\mathcal{L}(t):=\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}},\qquad T:=T_{\varepsilon},

and we assume
L^{\infty}
blow-up at
T<\infty
, i.e.
\mathcal{N}(t)\to\infty

as
t\uparrow T
. Two rates are relevant: the
ODE rate

1/(p-1)
and the
self-similar rate

(7.1)

\vartheta\;:=\;\frac{2s-\gamma}{2s(p-1)}\;=\;\frac{1}{p-1}\Bigl(1-\frac{\gamma}{2s}\Bigr)\;\leqslant\;\frac{1}{p-1},

with equality iff
\gamma=0
(cf. (
8.1
) below). The weight

|x|^{-\gamma}
separates two scenarios: where blow-up concentrates at the
origin, the active scaling is (
1.3
) and the rate is the slower

\vartheta
; at an interior point
x_{0}\neq 0
the weight is comparable to a
constant and the local dynamics is governed by the ODE rate
1/(p-1)
.

Definition 7.1
.

A solution blowing up at
T<\infty
exhibits
Type-I

blow-up if
\mathcal{N}(t)\leqslant C\,(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)}
as

t\uparrow T
, and
Type-II
otherwise.

For the unweighted nonlocal Fujita equation, Type-I blow-up of nonnegative
subcritical solutions is established
in
[
37
,
15
,
24
]
. The next results extend the

lower
-rate side of this picture to the Hardy–Hénon case in full
rigour, and clarify the precise (conditional) status of the matching upper
bound.

Theorem 7.1

(Blow-up rate)
.

Let
1<p\leqslant p_{F}
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
with
u_{0}\geqslant 0
and

\int u_{0}>0
, and suppose the mild solution of (
1.1
) undergoes

L^{\infty}
blow-up at
T<\infty
. Then:

(a)

(Universal self-similar lower bound.)

Unconditionally,

\mathcal{N}(t)\;\geqslant\;\tau_{0}^{\,\vartheta}\,(T-t)^{-\vartheta},\qquad 0<t<T,

for a constant
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(N,s,\gamma,p)>0
; in particular blow-up is
never slower than self-similar.

(b)

(Interior ODE lower bound.)

If, in addition, the blow-up is realised away from the origin in the sense
of (
7.3
), then

\mathcal{N}(t)\;\geqslant\;\bigl[(p-1)\delta^{-\gamma}\bigr]^{-1/(p-1)}(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)},\qquad t\in[t_{0},T).

(c)

(Conditional Type-I upper bound.)

If the solution satisfies the self-similar Type-I a priori bound
(
8.2
), then
\mathcal{N}(t)\leqslant C(T-t)^{-\vartheta}\leqslant C(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)}
; that is, Type-I blow-up holds.

Consequently, in the interior regime (
7.3
)
(
with the ODE
Type-I bound of Definition
7.1
)
the matching two-sided
rate holds,

(7.2)

\mathcal{N}(t)\;\sim\;(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)}\qquad\text{as }t\uparrow T,

while in the origin regime
(
under (
8.2
)
)

\mathcal{N}(t)\sim(T-t)^{-\vartheta}
. For
\gamma=0
one has

\vartheta=1/(p-1)
, parts
(a)
–
(b)
coincide and are
unconditional, the qualification (
7.3
) is automatic, and the only
conditional input is the exclusion of Type-II blow-up in
(c)
.

The proof occupies the rest of this section: (
7.3
) and the
maximum-principle Lemma
7.1
below give (b); the scaling
Lemma
7.2
gives (a); and (c) is immediate
from (
8.2
). We then assemble the statement and comment on the
direction of the comparison and on the relation with the profile analysis of
Section
8
.

7.1.
Regularity and the maximum-principle inequality

For
t>0
the mild solution satisfies

u(\cdot,t)\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

u(\cdot,t)\in C^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
, and (
1.1
)
holds pointwise on
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})\times(0,T)
with

\partial_{t}u
and
(-\Delta)^{s}u
continuous there. Indeed,
e^{-t(-\Delta)^{s}}u_{0}
is
smooth and vanishes at infinity (Lemma
2.1
), while the
Duhamel term has source
|x|^{-\gamma}u^{p}\in L^{r}
(cf. (
3.3
)),
smooth away from the origin; interior fractional parabolic
regularity
[
8
,
9
]
promotes the mild
solution to a classical one on
\{|x|\geqslant\delta\}\times(0,T)
for every

\delta>0
.

The blow-up qualification “
x_{0}\neq 0
” is used in the following precise
form: there exist
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and
\delta\in(0,1]
with

(7.3)

\mathcal{N}(t)=\max_{|x|\geqslant\delta}u(x,t)\qquad\text{for all }t\in[t_{0},T).

Condition (
7.3
) holds whenever the blow-up set is contained in

\{|x|\geqslant\delta\}
, so that the global maximum is eventually realised away
from the origin — in particular, when the solution blows up at a single
point
x_{0}\neq 0
while remaining locally bounded near
x=0
.

Lemma 7.1

(Nonlocal maximum-principle inequality)
.

Under (
7.3
), the function
t\mapsto\mathcal{N}(t)
is locally
Lipschitz on
[t_{0},T)
and

(7.4)

\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\,\mathcal{N}(t)\;\leqslant\;\delta^{-\gamma}\,\mathcal{N}(t)^{p},\qquad t\in[t_{0},T),

where
\frac{d^{+}}{dt}
denotes the upper right Dini derivative.

Proof.

Fix
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[t_{0},T)
. The map
t\mapsto u(\cdot,t)
is continuous
from
[t_{1},t_{2}]
into
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, so its image is a compact subset of

C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and is therefore uniformly tight: there is
R\geqslant\delta
with

\sup\limits_{|x|\geqslant R}\sup_{t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]}u(x,t)<\min_{t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]}\mathcal{N}(t),

the latter minimum being positive by continuity. Combined
with (
7.3
), every maximiser of
u(\cdot,t)
,
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
,
lies in the fixed compact annulus
A:=\{\delta\leqslant|x|\leqslant R\}
. On
A
the
maps
u,\partial_{t}u
are continuous, so by Danskin’s theorem

\mathcal{N}(t)=\max_{x\in A}u(x,t)
is locally Lipschitz with

\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\,\mathcal{N}(t)\;=\;\max\bigl\{\partial_{t}u(x_{t},t):x_{t}\in M(t)\bigr\},\qquad M(t):=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\in A}u(\cdot,t).

Let
x_{t}\in M(t)
, so
|x_{t}|\geqslant\delta
and
u(x_{t},t)=\mathcal{N}(t)
. As

x_{t}
is a global maximiser of
u(\cdot,t)
,

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x_{t},t)=c_{N,s}\,\mathrm{P.V.}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x_{t},t)-u(y,t)}{|x_{t}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\;\geqslant\;0,

the integrand being nonnegative. Evaluating (
1.1
) at
(x_{t},t)
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x_{t},t)

\displaystyle=-(-\Delta)^{s}u(x_{t},t)+|x_{t}|^{-\gamma}u(x_{t},t)^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant|x_{t}|^{-\gamma}\,\mathcal{N}(t)^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta^{-\gamma}\,\mathcal{N}(t)^{p}.

Taking the maximum over
M(t)
gives (
7.4
).
∎

7.2.
Lower bounds on the blow-up rate

Proposition 7.1

(Interior ODE lower rate)
.

Under (
7.3
),

(7.5)

\mathcal{N}(t)\;\geqslant\;\bigl[(p-1)\,\delta^{-\gamma}\bigr]^{-1/(p-1)}(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)},\qquad t\in[t_{0},T).

In particular
\mathcal{N}(t)\gtrsim(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)}
as
t\uparrow T
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.1
,
\mathcal{N}
is locally Lipschitz and positive on

[t_{0},T)
and satisfies (
7.4
) for a.e.
t
; hence

\mathcal{N}^{-(p-1)}
is locally Lipschitz and

\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathcal{N}(t)^{-(p-1)}=-(p-1)\,\mathcal{N}(t)^{-p}\,\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(t)\;\geqslant\;-(p-1)\,\delta^{-\gamma}\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }t\in[t_{0},T).

Integrating over
[t,t^{\prime}]\subset[t_{0},T)
,

\mathcal{N}(t^{\prime})^{-(p-1)}-\mathcal{N}(t)^{-(p-1)}\;\geqslant\;-(p-1)\,\delta^{-\gamma}\,(t^{\prime}-t).

Letting
t^{\prime}\uparrow T
and using
\mathcal{N}(t^{\prime})\to\infty
(so

\mathcal{N}(t^{\prime})^{-(p-1)}\to 0
) yields

\mathcal{N}(t)^{-(p-1)}\leqslant(p-1)\,\delta^{-\gamma}\,(T-t),

which
is (
7.5
).
∎

Corollary 7.1

(Unweighted case)
.

If
\gamma=0
, then

\mathcal{N}(t)\geqslant[(p-1)(T-t)]^{-1/(p-1)}

for

every
blowing-up solution, with no restriction on the location of the
maximum: the proof of Lemma
7.1
gives

\partial_{t}u(x_{t},t)\leqslant\mathcal{N}(t)^{p}
at any maximiser because the weight is
identically
1
, and (
7.3
) is vacuous. This is the
“qualification is automatic” statement of Theorem
7.1
.

The interior bound (
7.5
) degrades as the maximisers approach
the origin, since
|x_{t}|^{-\gamma}
is then unbounded. The next scale-covariant
estimate replaces it everywhere and is fully unconditional.

Lemma 7.2

(Scale-covariant existence time)
.

There is a constant
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(N,s,\gamma,p)>0
such that, for every

v\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the mild solution of (
1.1
) with datum
v

(i.e.
\varepsilon u_{0}
replaced by
v
) exists on
[0,\tau^{*}(v))
with

(7.6)

\tau^{*}(v)\;\geqslant\;\tau_{0}\,\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-1/\vartheta}\;=\;\tau_{0}\,\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-\frac{2s(p-1)}{2s-\gamma}}.

No sign or integrability condition on
v
is required.

Proof.

Unit scale.
Working in
X_{\tau}=L^{\infty}((0,\tau)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
and using
the weighted smoothing (
6.2
) of
Lemma
6.1
(valid since
\gamma/(2s)<1
), the Duhamel
map
\Phi
obeys, on the ball
\{\|u\|_{X_{\tau}}\leqslant 2\}
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\!\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau^{\prime})^{-\gamma/(2s)}\|u(\tau^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\,2^{p}\,t^{\,1-\gamma/(2s)}.

The Lipschitz estimate is identical, via

|a|^{p}-|b|^{p}\leqslant p(|a|^{p-1}+|b|^{p-1})|a-b|
and (
6.2
).
Hence there is a universal
\tau_{0}>0
such that every
v
with

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant 1
produces a mild solution on
[0,\tau_{0}]
(choose

\tau_{0}
so small that
C2^{p}\tau_{0}^{1-\gamma/(2s)}\leqslant 1
and
\Phi
is a
contraction).

General scale.
Let
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}=A>0
and put

\lambda:=A^{-(p-1)/(2s-\gamma)}
, so that

\lambda^{(2s-\gamma)/(p-1)}A=1
. If
u
is the solution with datum
v
, then
by the invariance (
1.3
) the rescaled function

\tilde{u}(x,t)=\lambda^{(2s-\gamma)/(p-1)}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2s}t)

solves
(
1.1
) with datum

\tilde{v}(x)=\lambda^{(2s-\gamma)/(p-1)}v(\lambda x)
, and

\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{\infty}}=1
. By the unit-scale step
\tilde{u}
exists on

[0,\tau_{0}]
, hence
u
exists on
[0,\lambda^{2s}\tau_{0}]
. Therefore

\tau^{*}(v)\;\geqslant\;\lambda^{2s}\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}\,A^{-2s(p-1)/(2s-\gamma)}=\tau_{0}\,A^{-1/\vartheta},

which is (
7.6
).
∎

Proposition 7.2

(Universal self-similar lower bound)
.

With
\tau_{0}
as in Lemma
7.2
,

(7.7)

\mathcal{N}(t)\;\geqslant\;\tau_{0}^{\,\vartheta}\,(T-t)^{-\vartheta},\qquad 0<t<T.

No assumption on the location of the blow-up set is needed; in
particular (
7.7
) holds for origin-concentrated blow-up, for
sign-changing solutions, and on the full parameter range

0\leqslant\gamma<\min(2s,N)
,
p>1
.

Proof.

Fix
t\in(0,T)
. The solution issued from the datum

u(\cdot,t)\in L^{\infty}
exists on
[t,T)
and cannot be continued as an

L^{\infty}
-valued solution past
T
, since
\mathcal{N}\to\infty
there; thus
its maximal existence time, measured from initial time
t
, equals
T-t
.
Applying Lemma
7.2
with
v=u(\cdot,t)
,

T-t\;=\;\tau^{*}\!\bigl(u(\cdot,t)\bigr)\;\geqslant\;\tau_{0}\,\mathcal{N}(t)^{-1/\vartheta},

which rearranges to (
7.7
).
∎

7.3.
The Type-I upper bound is conditional

The reverse inequality
\mathcal{N}(t)\leqslant C(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)}
— the exclusion
of Type-II (faster) blow-up — does
not
follow from the
maximum-principle inequality (
7.4
), which is one-sided in the
wrong direction (Remark
7.1
). The natural sufficient
condition is the self-similar Type-I a priori bound (
8.2
)
introduced in Section
8
.

Proposition 7.3

(Conditional Type-I upper rate)
.

Let
1<p\leqslant p_{F}
and
\vartheta
as in (
7.1
). If the
solution satisfies the self-similar Type-I bound (
8.2
), namely

(T-t)^{\vartheta}\mathcal{N}(t)\leqslant C_{0}
on
[t_{0},T)
, then

(7.8)

\mathcal{N}(t)\;\leqslant\;C_{0}\,(T-t)^{-\vartheta}\;\leqslant\;C_{0}\,T^{\,\frac{1}{p-1}-\vartheta}\,(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)},\qquad t\in[t_{0},T),

since
\frac{1}{p-1}-\vartheta=\frac{\gamma}{2s(p-1)}\geqslant 0
. In particular the
Type-I bound of Definition
7.1
holds. When
\gamma=0
one has

\vartheta=\frac{1}{p-1}
, and (
8.2
)
is
the Type-I bound.

Proof.

Immediate from
(T-t)^{\vartheta}\mathcal{N}(t)\leqslant C_{0}
and

(T-t)^{\,\frac{1}{p-1}-\vartheta}\leqslant T^{\,\frac{1}{p-1}-\vartheta}
for

0\leqslant T-t\leqslant T
, the exponent being nonnegative.
∎

Status of the hypothesis (
8.2
).

Validating (
8.2
) unconditionally is the substantive point. In
the local case
s=1
it is known for
p
below the Sobolev exponent through
the Giga–Kohn energy method and the Liouville classification
of (
8.5
)
[
18
,
19
]
. In the nonlocal
setting
0<s<1
a complete Liouville theorem for the rescaled stationary
problem (
8.5
) is, to our knowledge, available only partially
(cf. the discussion in Theorem
8.1
); consequently the Type-I

upper
bound is, in the present generality, conditional
on (
8.2
).

Proof of Theorem
7.1

Part (a) is Proposition
7.2
; part (b) is
Proposition
7.1
; part (c) is
Proposition
7.3
. In the interior regime (
7.3
),
combining the lower bound (b) with the ODE Type-I bound of
Definition
7.1
gives (
7.2
); in the origin regime,
combining the floor (a) with (c) gives

\mathcal{N}(t)\sim(T-t)^{-\vartheta}
. For
\gamma=0
,
Corollary
7.1
makes (a)–(b) coincide and unconditional, and

\vartheta=1/(p-1)
. ∎

Remark 7.1

(On the direction of the comparison)
.

Three points deserve emphasis.

(a) Direction.
Inequality (
7.4
),

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\leqslant\delta^{-\gamma}\mathcal{N}^{p}
, controls the derivative

from above
and therefore yields the
lower

bound (
7.5
): a solution whose growth rate is capped by

C\mathcal{N}^{p}
must already be large in order to blow up at
T
. An

upper
rate bound would require the opposite inequality

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\gtrsim\mathcal{N}^{p}
, which the diffusion
-(-\Delta)^{s}u\leqslant 0
at a
maximum does not provide.

(b) The
L^{1}
norm grows.
In the subcritical regime
\mathcal{L}(t)
is

not
bounded as
t\uparrow T
. Integrating (
1.1
) gives

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\gamma}u^{p}\,dx\geqslant 0
, and the self-similar
scaling (
1.3
) predicts
\mathcal{L}(t)\asymp(T-t)^{-\beta}
with

\beta
as in (
1.6
) for origin-type blow-up, so

\mathcal{L}(t)\to\infty
. The lower bounds above avoid
\mathcal{L}
entirely.

(c) Role of
|x|^{-\gamma}
.
The factor

|x_{t}|^{-\gamma}\leqslant\delta^{-\gamma}
in (
7.4
) is exactly what
forces the qualification
x_{0}\neq 0
: if the maximisers approach the origin,

|x_{t}|^{-\gamma}
is unbounded and the comparison degrades to the slower
self-similar rate
\vartheta
of Proposition
7.2
,
consistently with Remark
7.2
.

7.4.
Relation with Theorem
8.1

Remark 7.2

(Two blow-up regimes)
.

For
\gamma>0
the qualification “
x_{0}\neq 0
” is genuine, and
Theorem
7.1
resolves the two scenarios as follows.

•

Interior blow-up
(condition (
7.3
)).
Proposition
7.1
gives

\mathcal{N}(t)\geqslant c(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)}
. For
\gamma>0
this
violates

(
8.2
), since
(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)}\gg(T-t)^{-\vartheta}
as

t\uparrow T
. Hence the hypothesis (
8.2
) of
Theorem
8.1
is incompatible with interior blow-up: a solution
that blows up away from the origin lies
outside
the scope of the
profile theorem and obeys the ODE rate
1/(p-1)
governed by the locally
bounded weight.

•

Origin-concentrated blow-up.
Conversely, (
8.2
)
selects precisely the slower, self-similar regime in which
|x|^{-\gamma}
is
active at the blow-up scale; there the rescaling (
8.3
) is the
correct normalisation and Theorem
8.1
extracts a stationary
profile solving (
8.5
).

The self-similar scaling (
1.3
) assigns to blow-up at
the origin the rate
(T-t)^{-\vartheta}
with

\vartheta=\frac{2s-\gamma}{2s(p-1)}<\frac{1}{p-1}
, while at an interior point
the weight is comparable to a constant and the rate is
1/(p-1)
. A solution
whose blow-up set reduces to

therefore obeys the slower self-similar
rate; this is why the matching ODE lower bound requires blow-up away from the
origin.

Remark 7.3

(Two floors, and the common Liouville gate)
.

Since
\vartheta\leqslant\frac{1}{p-1}
(equality iff
\gamma=0
),
Proposition
7.2
is in general
weaker
than the interior
bound (
7.5
): it is the
universal floor
, valid
everywhere, while Proposition
7.1
raises that floor to the
ODE rate
1/(p-1)
precisely where the weight is harmless. The gain is
exactly the gain from
|x_{t}|^{-\gamma}\leqslant\delta^{-\gamma}
(a fixed constant,
away from the origin) over the scale-covariant weight that drives
Lemma
7.2
.

The exponent in (
7.7
) is the self-similar rate
\vartheta
, so
Proposition
7.2
states that
blow-up is never slower
than self-similar
; it is the unconditional counterpart of the self-similar
Type-I
upper
bound (
8.2
),

\mathcal{N}(t)\leqslant C(T-t)^{-\vartheta}
. Together they pin the origin rate to

\mathcal{N}(t)\asymp(T-t)^{-\vartheta}
, which is precisely the normalisation
under which (
8.3
) is bounded and Theorem
8.1

extracts a stationary profile. Thus the lower bound is free, and the only
conditional input remaining — in
both
the origin regime here and in
Theorem
8.1
— is a fractional Liouville classification of
nonnegative bounded solutions of (
8.5
) (equivalently, the
exclusion of Type-II blow-up). Such a result would simultaneously
(i) upgrade the upper bound in Proposition
7.3
—
hence Theorem
7.1
— to an unconditional statement, and
(ii) supply the uniqueness of the limit profile left conditional in
Theorem
8.1
. For
\gamma=0
one has
\vartheta=1/(p-1)
,
Propositions
7.1
and
7.2
coincide, and the
floor is the genuine Type-I rate of Definition
7.1
,
unconditionally.

Remark 7.4
.

The sign restriction
u_{0}\geqslant 0
is convenient but not essential: it suffices
that the mild solution become nonnegative for

, with positivity then
preserved. The universal lower bound of Proposition
7.2
needs
no sign condition at all.

8.
Asymptotic blow-up profile

The scaling invariance (
1.3
) singles out a self-similar rate at
the blow-up time. Under
u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{(2s-\gamma)/(p-1)}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2s}t)
, the choice
\lambda=(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{-1/(2s)}

gives the rate
(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{-\vartheta}
with

(8.1)

\vartheta:=\frac{2s-\gamma}{2s(p-1)}.

For
\gamma>0
this is strictly slower than the ODE-type rate
1/(p-1)
of
Definition
7.1
; the two coincide only when
\gamma=0
. We
therefore work with the
self-similar Type-I
condition

(8.2)

(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{\vartheta}\,\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\;\leqslant\;C,\qquad t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}),

which is compatible with the rescaling below and reduces to
Definition
7.1
when
\gamma=0
.

In the self-similar variables

(8.3)

y=\frac{x}{(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{1/(2s)}},\qquad\tau=-\log(T_{\varepsilon}-t),\qquad u(x,t)=(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{-\vartheta}\,v(y,\tau),

the chain rule gives

\partial_{t}u=(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{-\vartheta-1}\bigl[v_{\tau}+\tfrac{1}{2s}\,y\cdot\nabla_{y}v+\vartheta\,v\bigr],

while
(-\Delta)^{s}_{x}u=(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{-\vartheta-1}(-\Delta)^{s}_{y}v
and

|x|^{-\gamma}|u|^{p}=(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{-\vartheta-1}|y|^{-\gamma}|v|^{p}

(using
\vartheta(p-1)=(2s-\gamma)/(2s)
). Substituting
in (
1.1
) and dividing by
(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{-\vartheta-1}
,

(8.4)

v_{\tau}\;=\;-(-\Delta)^{s}v\;-\;\frac{1}{2s}\,y\cdot\nabla v\;+\;|y|^{-\gamma}|v|^{p-1}v\;-\;\vartheta\,v.

The drift term
-\tfrac{1}{2s}y\cdot\nabla v
is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck
term of the Giga–Kohn rescaling
[
18
,
19
]
; the
associated stationary problem is

(8.5)

(-\Delta)^{s}V\;+\;\frac{1}{2s}\,y\cdot\nabla V\;+\;\vartheta\,V\;=\;|y|^{-\gamma}\,V^{p},\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Under (
8.2
),
\|v(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
is bounded
uniformly in
\tau
.

Theorem 8.1

(Asymptotic profile, conditional)
.

Let
1<p<p_{F}
and
u_{0}\geqslant 0
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}>0
. Suppose the mild
solution
u
blows up at

and
satisfies (

). Then there is a sequence
\tau_{n}\to\infty
with

By (

,
so the right-hand side of (

) is bounded in

(the weight

is locally integrable

Concluding remarks

•

Theorem
1.1
gives sharp two-sided lifespan estimates for
the fractional Hardy–Hénon equation (
1.1
) in the subcritical and
critical regimes (under
\gamma<2s(p-1)
), and global existence in the
supercritical regime. The sharp exponent (
1.6
) interpolates between
the classical Lee–Ni formula
[
29
]
(
s=1
,
\gamma=0
) and the
higher-order results of
[
41
]
, and incorporates the weight
in the clean additive form

\beta=\tfrac{1}{p-1}-\tfrac{N}{2s}-\tfrac{\gamma}{2s(p-1)}
.

•

The weight
|x|^{-\gamma}
enters the analysis only through the
interpolation Lemma
2.2
(lower bound) and the constant
C_{*}

in (
5.2
) (upper bound); this is the mechanism producing the
additive correction
-\gamma/(2s(p-1))
, as opposed to the multiplicative
guess
2s\rightsquigarrow 2s-\gamma
.

•

The backward fractional heat kernel provides a single, uniformly valid
test function for the upper bound in both the small-data
(Section
5
) and large-data (Section
6
)
regimes; the only difference is the datum bound,
g_{b}(0)\gtrsim\varepsilon b^{-N/(2s)}
versus
g_{b}(0)\geqslant\varepsilon c_{0}
.

•

For the blow-up rate (Section
7
) the
lower
bounds
are unconditional: every blowing-up nonnegative solution obeys the universal
self-similar floor
\mathcal{N}(t)\gtrsim(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{-\vartheta}

(Proposition
7.2
), raised to the ODE rate

\mathcal{N}(t)\gtrsim(T_{\varepsilon}-t)^{-1/(p-1)}
for interior blow-up
(Proposition
7.1
, via a nonlocal maximum principle). The
matching Type-I
upper
bound, and the uniqueness of the self-similar
profile (Theorem
8.1
), are conditional on one and the same
input: a fractional Liouville classification for (
8.5
), which
remains partially open.

•

Several natural extensions are of independent interest:

–

the Hénon case
\gamma<0
(weight
|x|^{|\gamma|}
, weakening the
nonlinearity near the origin), with a different critical exponent;

–

the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
with Dirichlet or Neumann conditions;

–

general weights
V(x)\asymp|x|^{-\gamma}
near the origin and bounded
at infinity;

–

the time-fractional analogue

the residual range
