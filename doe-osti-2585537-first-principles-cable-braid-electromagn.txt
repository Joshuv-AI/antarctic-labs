 Abstract
 In this report the lack of assumed connectivity between braid carriers imposes the need to use the
 original magnetic vector potential formulas for transfer and self impedances rather than the final simplified
 results in the magnetic coupling and propagation formulas. Each of the magnetic sections in the previous
 journal article [1] and Sandia report [2] are addressed with references to the appropriate formulas.

1 INTRODUCTION
The clarification discussed in this report applies to the previous paper [1] and to the previous report [2] when
the magnetic vector potential is used to formulate the braid magnetic penetration, or transfer impedance
per unit length, and propagation, or self impedance per unit length, in the case where there is an assumed
lack of connectivity between the braid carriers in a typical braided cable shield. In this previous paper
[1] and previous report [2] we assumed all braid wires had suﬃcient incidental electrical contact to give
equal electrostatic potential between wires in the electric coupling problem, which is consistent with past
treatments of the braid. However, some of the final formulation results in this paper [1] and report [2] for
the magnetic coupling also implicitly assumed this connectivity, which is not consistent with the assumption
that the net braid wire carrier currents remain intact despite the meander of the shield wires into and out
of the mean shield location. This lack of perfect contact allows magnetic flux to exist between braid layers
as illustrated in Figure 1 and leads to the porpoising contribution to the transfer impedance. In this short
report we refer to these final magnetic coupling and propagation results which assume connectivity, and to
the preceding results which remain correct when such connectivity is not present [1], [2].
 A brief discussion of this lack of connectivity in the magnetic braid penetration can be found in [3], where
it is illustrated with a two layer parallel wire shield. When it was assumed the current has equal splitting
between wire layers, the transfer inductance does not decrease rapidly as the wires in each layer approach
one another, whereas, if the layers were taken to have equal potentials the currents would decrease on the top
layer versus the bottom layer and the sheet transfer inductance would rapidly decrease. This equal current
splitting mimics the porpoising contribution to the transfer inductance in the cable braid.
 This report first discusses the inductance corrections for the perfectly conducting cable. This includes the
self inductance per unit length and the transfer inductance per unit length. It also includes the corrections
for the planar approximations where the cable braid is treated as locally flat in a single cell. Next the
corrections for the finitely conducting impedances are discussed. This again includes the self impedance per
unit length and transfer impedance per unit length. The corrections for the planar approximations of the
braid are also discussed.

4 CONCLUSIONS
This brief report discusses a wire braid connectivity issue with some of the final formulas in the paper [1]
and report [2] which was first discussed in [3] using two layer wire arrays. Some of the final formulas in [1],
[2] which assume wire connectivity, as well as preceding formulas which are applicable without connectivity,
and hence include porpoising contributions in transfer impedance, are listed here in this report.
