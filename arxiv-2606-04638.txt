Abstract
In two seminal articles published in 1964, Brayton and Moser introduced the concept of a mixed potential as a fundamental theoretic tool to describe and analyze a class RLC of nonlinear circuits containing resistors, capacitors and inductors. In this paper, it is shown for the first time that a mixed potential can be introduced for a class RLCM of RLC circuits containing also memristors. This is possible provided a memristor circuit is analyzed not in the traditional voltage-current domain but rather in the flux-charge domain. The flux-charge analysis method (FCAM) plays a crucial role in the extension, in particular, a key step is an equivalence principle established via FCAM between an RLCM circuit in the flux-charge domain and a nonlinear RLC circuit in the voltage-current domain. Several examples are discussed where the mixed potential is explicitly found. These include basic circuits with memristors, such as Chua’s circuit with a memristor and also large-scale memristor arrays with a neural architecture. This paper is mainly devoted to the introduction of a mixed potential for memristor circuits and the study of its main theoretic properties, as the possibility to write the circuit state equations in the flux-charge domain in an effective and compact form via the mixed potential. In a companion paper [ 1 ] , the mixed potential is used to obtain in a systematic way Lyapunov-like results on convergence of RLCM circuits. Those results will extend existing results on convergence that do not cover the important case where there is the simultaneous presence of capacitors and inductors in a memristor circuit.

Memristor has been devised by L. Chua as the fourth basic passive circuit element
in addition to the resistor, capacitor and inductor in a seminal paper published

Furthermore, since memristors are able to keep in memory their final state
also when power is disconnected, they are suitable to implement effective

in-memory
computing schemes where the processing and memorization are at
the same physical location
[
10
,

,

. Such a property appears to be crucial to
circumvent the so-called von Neumann bottleneck of traditional
computer architectures originating from data that
are continuously exchanged between the central processing unit (CPU)
and the memory, e.g., a random access memory (RAM). From a broader
perspective, memristor circuits are believed to excel at mimicking the
parallel and efficient processing capabilities of brain-like nervous
systems
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

A systematic technique has been developed for the study of nonlinear circuits
containing memristors in
[
22
,
23
]
. This is based on the analysis in
the integral domain given by the flux and charge rather than the
traditional domain given by voltage and current. Thus, the name Flux-Charge
analysis method (or, FCAM, for short). The method proved effective to
highlight relevant peculiar dynamical features of memristor circuits, such as the
existence for structural reasons of invariants of motion for the dynamic
equations in the voltage-current domain (VCD). As a consequence, the
state space in the VCD can be foliated in invariant manifolds where a
memristor circuit satisfies a manifold-dependent reduced-order dynamics
that can be completely characterized in the flux-charge domain (FCD).
In turn, this implies that a memristor circuits can
display infinitely many different coexisting dynamics and attractors for
a fixed set of circuit parameters and nonlinearities, a
property termed in the literature extreme multistability
[
24
]
.
Peculiar to this type of dynamics is also the presence of bifurcations due
to changing the initial conditions for fixed circuit parameters and
nonlinearities (bifurcations without parameters).

FCAM has been applied
for a detailed analysis of the bifurcations and oscillatory phenomena of several basic circuits
as Murali-Lakshmanan-Chua circuit and Chua’s circuit with a memristor

three basic

to note that, to our knowledge, no contributions in the literature address
in a systematic way convergence when capacitors and inductors are simultaneously present.
We refer the reader to
[

The dynamic analysis of nonlinear RLC circuits (without memristors) has been one
of the main topics in circuit theory since the sixties

Here, we are mainly interested in the theory developed by Brayton and Moser

to describe and analyze a large class of
nonlinear RLC circuits containing resistors, inductors and capacitors. One main
result is that, when the capacitor voltages and inductor currents are
a complete set of variables, then the state equations can be described via a suitable
function termed

. This result, in addition

theoretic significance, paves the way to obtain nice Lyapunov-like results on
convergence towards equilibrium points for nonlinear

[

[

, passivity-based control of power electronic systems
[

, distributed and large-scale system modeling

, stability and control in modern power grids
[

[
47

48

. However, to our knowledge, no attempt has been done so far to extend the mixed potential to classes of nonlinear circuits containing
memristors.

Here, we consider a class of nonlinear circuits, termed RLCM, containing

all four basic circuit elements
, i.e., resistors, inductors, capacitors
and memristors. On one hand, this class generalizes the class RLC considered by Brayton
and Moser
[

by allowing for the presence of memristors. On the other hand, it also
extends the class RCM studied in
[
31
]
, where only one type of reactive elements is taken into account.
Considering inductors in a circuit is of course of great theoretic interest, since,
as it is well-known, the simultaneous presence of both electric and magnetic field
greatly enriches the
dynamical behavior. Furthermore, from a more practical viewpoint,
small parasitic inductors need to be
necessarily accounted for in any electronic
implementation, especially when the circuits operate at
high frequencies.

The main contributions in the paper can be summarized as follows:

(a) we extend the
fundamental notion of mixed potential, originally introduced by Brayton and
Moser for RLC circuits without memristors, to the considered class RLCM
of memristor circuits.
This extension is possible provided we represent the memristor circuits in the FCD
and it holds under the assumption that capacitor charges and memristor
fluxes are a complete set of state variables in the FCD;

(b) we show that, via the extended mixed potential, the SEs in the FCD assume
a particularly compact and elegant form. Moreover, for some special yet
relevant subclasses of RLCM circuits, we highlight how
the mixed potential is related to the invariants of motion and invariant
manifolds that characterize the dynamics in the VCD;

(c) we discuss the relation between the mixed potential and
a fundamental concept in circuit theory, i.e., the
reciprocity properties
of the
multi-port networks used in the analysis of the RLCM circuits;

(d) we illustrate the obtained results by explicitly finding the mixed
potential of basic nonlinear RLCM circuits and also high-dimensional RLCM
circuits with a neural-like architecture.

It is worth to stress that the use of FCAM plays a fundamental role
to show the existence of a mixed potential.
Indeed, a crucial step in the analysis is an equivalent principle established
in the paper via FCAM between the considered class of nonlinear RLCM circuits
in the FCD and nonlinear RLC circuits in the VCD.

This paper is mainly devoted to the theoretical aspects and the basic properties
of the mixed potential for the considered class of RLCM circuits.
In a companion paper
[
1
]
, we investigate on more practical
yet relevant aspects implied by the existence of a mixed potential. In particular,
we will address how to systematically derive convergence results for subclasses of
RLCM circuits, with the aim to extend the convergence results obtained in

[
31
]

and other contributions in the literature
[
49
,
50
]
to memristor circuits
containing both capacitors and inductors.

Class RLCM of Memristor Circuits

2.1
Representation in the VCD

In the paper, we consider a class of nonlinear circuits
\mathfrak{N}
, denoted RLCM, containing the
following elements:

\bullet

ideal resistors
v_{Ri}=R_{i}i_{Ri}

\bullet

n_{a}
independent current sources

\bullet

(1)

n_{L}

v_{Li}=L_{i}\dot{i}_{Li};

(2)

\bullet

ideal flux-controlled memristors, denoted

, which are defined according to L. Chua

by the constitutive relation (CR)

where

\Phi_{Mi}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}v_{Mi}(\sigma)d\sigma
is the

(or voltage momentum) and

Q_{Mi}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}i_{Mi}(\sigma)d\sigma
is the
charge
(or current momentum). Moreover,

is the nonlinear memristor characteristic. We suppose henceforth that

\hat{Q}_{Mi}(0)=0
and

obtain that the memristor satisfies in the traditional VCD the following CR given by a differential algebraic equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i_{i}=\hat{Q}_{Mi}^{\prime}(\Phi_{Mi})v_{i}\\
\dot{\Phi}_{Mi}=v_{i}\end{array}\right.

(3)

where
\hat{Q}_{Mi}^{\prime}(\Phi_{Mi})
, whose dimension is Ohm
-1
, is termed state-dependent memductance;

\bullet

n_{Q}
ideal charge-controlled memristors, denoted
M_{Qi}
, with CRs
\Phi_{Mi}=\hat{\Phi}_{Mi}(Q_{Mi})
, where

\hat{\Phi}_{Mi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the nonlinear memristor characteristic such
that
\hat{\Phi}_{Mi}(0)=0
and
\hat{\Phi}_{Mi}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Differentiating
in time we
obtain that the memristor satisfies in the VCD the following CR

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{i}=\hat{\Phi}_{Mi}^{\prime}(Q_{Mi})i_{i}\\
\dot{Q}_{Mi}=i_{i}\end{array}\right.

(4)

where
\hat{Q}_{Mi}^{\prime}(\Phi_{Mi})
, whose dimension is Ohm, is termed state-dependent memristance.

2.3
Fundamental Equivalence Principle

Let us consider the analogies
\varphi\sim v
and
q\sim i
.
By comparing the CRs (
1
) and (
5
), it is seen that a capacitor in the
VCD is the analogous of a capacitor in the FCD. Note, however, that its equivalent circuit
in the FCD (Fig.
1
) has an independent charge source accounting for the initial condition
v_{C0i}
at
t=0
. Similar analogies hold for an inductor, a resistor and
for the two types of independent sources.

A flux-controlled memristor is described in the FCD by an algebraic
relationship
\hat{\mathfrak{Q}}_{Mi}(\cdot;\Phi_{M0i})
between flux and charge (cf. (
7
)).
This means that via the considered analogies a flux-controlled memristor in the FCD is the equivalent
of a nonlinear voltage-controlled resistor in the VCD. Clearly, the memristor is not equivalent to a nonlinear resistor in the VCD, since it satisfies the differential algebraic equation
(
3
).
Another observation is that the nonlinear characteristic
\hat{\mathfrak{Q}}_{Mi}(\cdot;\Phi_{M0i})

depends upon the initial memristor state
\Phi_{M0i}
. In conclusion, a flux-controlled memristor
in the FCD is the equivalent of an
initial-condition-dependent
nonlinear voltage-controlled resistor
with characteristic
\hat{\mathfrak{Q}}_{Mi}(\cdot;\Phi_{M0i})
in the VCD.
Analogous considerations hold for a charge-controlled memristor. See Fig.
1
.

On the basis of the previous analogies, a circuit
\mathfrak{N}
in the class RLCM is the analogous in the FCD of a nonlinear RLC circuit in the VCD.
This simple yet important observation implies that we can use existing results in the literature for nonlinear RLC circuits to study the dynamical behavior of memristor circuits in the class RLCM.
When relying on this analogy, we should take into account that the CRs of the capacitors,
inductors and memristors in the FCD bring with them the information on the corresponding initial
conditions of the state variable in the VCD.

Complete Set of Variables

Assumption 1
.

Given a memristor circuit

a) There is no loop made exclusively of capacitors and/or independent voltage sources and no cut-set made exclusively of inductors and/or independent current sources.

Condition a) is a standard topological assumption made in circuit theory to ensure that we can use capacitor voltages and inductor currents as independent variables to write SEs in the VCD. They also ensure we can use capacitor fluxes and inductor charges as independent variables to write SEs in the FCD. If needed, the unwanted loops and cut-sets can be removed via the techniques discussed in
[

(details are omitted). Condition b) is once more a typical condition used to write the SEs (cf.

5
Brayton-Moser mixed potential

In the seminal articles
[
40
,
41
]
, Brayton and Moser proposed a theory of nonlinear RLC circuits (without memristors) based on the concept of a
mixed potential
. In this section, we show that we can extend the mixed-potential to the class RLCM of circuits containing also memristors in addition to RLC components, provided we consider their description via FCAM in the FCD domain.

Consider a circuit
\mathfrak{N}
in the class RLCM.
As seen in Sect.
2.3
, there is an analogy between
\mathfrak{N}
in the FCD and an RLC circuit in the VCD.
Via this analogy, the dynamical behavior of
\mathfrak{N}
can be analyzed through the mixed potential introduced by Brayton and Moser
[
40
]
for nonlinear RLC circuits.

The following result holds.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
,
2

are satisfied. Introduce for
\mathfrak{N}
the mixed potential
{\cal P}(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0}):\mathbb{R}^{(n_{C}+n_{L})}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal P}&(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{C}^{\top}H_{\alpha\alpha}\varphi_{C}-\frac{1}{2}q_{L}^{\top}H_{\beta\beta}q_{L}+\varphi_{C}^{\top}H_{\alpha\beta}q_{L}\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\Phi}}\int_{0}^{\varphi_{\alpha i}}(\hat{Q}_{Mi}(\rho+\Phi_{M0i})-\hat{Q}_{Mi}(\Phi_{M0i}))d\rho\\
&-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{Q}}\int_{0}^{q_{\beta j}}(\hat{\Phi}_{Mj}(\rho+Q_{M0j})-\hat{\Phi}_{Mj}(Q_{M0j}))d\rho\\
&-\varphi_{C}^{\top}Cv_{C0}+q_{L}^{\top}Li_{L0}\end{split}

(30)

which depends upon the initial conditions
w_{0}
for the state variables in the
VCD (cf. (
26
)).
Then, the SEs
(
27
) of
\mathfrak{N}
in the FCD can be written in the form

\begin{pmatrix}C\dot{\varphi}_{C}\\
L\dot{q}_{L}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi_{C}}{\cal P}(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0})\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{L}}{\cal P}(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0})\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(31)

Figure 4
:
Decomposition of a circuit
\mathfrak{N}
in the class RLCM used in the proof of
Theorem
1
.

Proof.

The mixed potential is a purely dc concept related to the resistive multi-port network
N^{\prime}
to which capacitors and inductors are connected (see Fig.
4
). Next, we show that,
under Assumption
2
, a mixed potential of
N^{\prime}
can be explicitly found via the topological matrix describing the K
q
Ls at the fundamental cut-sets (cf. (
11
)) and the CRs of circuit elements.
The multi-port
N^{\prime}

is constituted by the charge and flux sources related to capacitor and inductor initial conditions, respectively, the memristor nonlinearities and the resistive multi-port
N
.
Due to an additivity property
[
40
]
, the mixed potential of
N^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal P}(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0})=&{\cal P}_{0}(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0})+{\cal P}_{M}(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0})\\
&+{\cal P}_{N}(\varphi_{C},q_{L},w_{0})\end{split}

where the first term is the mixed potential of the charge sources
Cv_{C0}
and flux sources
Li_{L0}
, the second term is that of the memristors and the third is the mixed potential of the multi-port
N
.

We have

{\cal P}_{0}(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0})=-\varphi_{C}^{\top}Cv_{C0}+q_{L}^{\top}Li_{L0}.

The mixed potential of flux-controlled memristors in parallel to capacitors is

{\cal P}_{\Phi}(\varphi_{C};w_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\Phi}}\int_{0}^{\varphi_{Ci}}(\hat{Q}_{Mi}(\rho+\Phi_{M0i})-\hat{Q}_{Mi}(\Phi_{M0i}))d\rho

while that of the charge-controlled memristors in series with inductors is

{\cal P}_{Q}(q_{L};w_{0})\!=\!\!-\!\!\sum_{i=1}^{n_{Q}}\int_{0}^{q_{Li}}(\hat{\Phi}_{Mi}(\varrho+Q_{M0i})\!-\!\hat{\Phi}_{Mi}(Q_{M0i}))d\varrho.

Therefore,
{\cal P}_{M}(\varphi_{C},q_{L})={\cal P}_{\Phi}(\varphi_{C})+{\cal P}_{Q}(q_{L})
.
The mixed potential of
N
, given in terms of its
H
-representation, can be expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal P}_{N}(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0})=&\int_{0}^{\varphi_{C}}H_{\alpha\alpha}\sigma\cdot d\sigma\\
&-\int_{0}^{q_{L}}H_{\beta\beta}\varsigma\cdot d\varsigma+\varphi_{C}^{\top}H_{\alpha\beta}q_{L}\end{split}

where the dot denotes the scalar product.
On the other hand, considering that
H_{\alpha\alpha}
is symmetric, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varphi_{C}}H_{\alpha\alpha}\sigma\cdot d\sigma=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varphi_{C}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}(\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{\top}H_{\alpha\alpha}\sigma)\cdot d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{C}^{\top}H_{\alpha\alpha}\varphi_{C}.

Similarly, since
H_{\beta\beta}
is symmetric

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{q_{L}}H_{\beta\beta}\varsigma\cdot d\varsigma=\frac{1}{2}q_{L}^{\top}H_{\beta\beta}q_{L}

and then

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{N}(\varphi_{C},q_{L};w_{0})\!\!=\!\!\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{C}^{\top}H_{\alpha\alpha}\varphi_{C}\!-\!\frac{1}{2}q_{L}^{\top}H_{\beta\beta}q_{L}\!+\!\varphi_{C}^{\top}H_{\alpha\beta}q_{L}.

Summing up these terms, we obtain the expression of the mixed potential
given in (
30
).

The second part of the theorem follows by verifying (
31
)
via direct computation of the partial derivatives of the mixed potential.

6.1
Maximum Number of Memristors

Consider the special yet relevant case where each capacitor has in parallel a flux-controlled
memristor and each inductor has in series a charge-controlled memristor.
Now, we have
n_{\Phi}=n_{C}
,
n_{Q}=n_{L}
, while
\Phi_{MC}=\varphi_{CM}+\Phi_{MC0}

and
Q_{ML}=q_{LM}+Q_{ML0}
.

Consider functions
{\cal Q}:\mathbb{R}^{2(n_{C}+n_{L})}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{C}}
and
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{2(n_{C}+n_{L})}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{L}}

defined as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal Q}=&{\cal Q}(v_{C},i_{L},\Phi_{MC},Q_{ML})=Cv_{C}+H_{\alpha\alpha}\Phi_{MC}\\
&+H_{\alpha\beta}Q_{ML}+\hat{Q}_{MC}(\Phi_{MC})\end{split}

(32)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Psi=&\Psi(v_{C},i_{L},\Phi_{MC},Q_{ML})=Li_{L}-H_{\alpha\beta}^{\top}\Phi_{MC}\\
&+H_{\beta\beta}Q_{ML}+\hat{\Phi}_{ML}(Q_{ML}).\end{split}

(33)

Then, the SEs (
27
) in the FCD can be put into the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}C\dot{\Phi}_{MC}\\
L\dot{Q}_{ML}\\
\end{pmatrix}=&-H\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{MC}\\
Q_{ML}\\
\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\hat{Q}_{MC}(\Phi_{MC})\\
\hat{\Phi}_{ML}(Q_{ML})\\
\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}Q_{0}\\
\Psi_{0}\\
\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(34)

where
Q_{0}={\cal Q}(0)
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{0}\!=\!Cv_{C0}+H_{\alpha\alpha}\Phi_{MC0}\!+\!H_{\alpha\beta}Q_{ML0}\!+\!\hat{Q}_{MC}(\Phi_{MC0})\end{split}

(35)

and
\Psi_{0}=\Psi(0)
amounts to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Psi_{0}\!=Li_{L0}-H_{\alpha\beta}^{\top}\Phi_{MC0}+H_{\beta\beta}Q_{ML0}+\hat{\Phi}_{ML}(Q_{ML0}).\end{split}

(36)

These are constant terms depending upon the initial conditions for the state variables in the VCD.

The SEs in the VCD become

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}Cv_{C}\\
Li_{L}\\
\end{pmatrix}=&-H\begin{pmatrix}v_{C}\\
i_{L}\\
\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\hat{Q}^{\prime}_{MC}(\Phi_{MC})v_{C}\\
\hat{\Phi}^{\prime}_{ML}(Q_{ML})i_{L}\\
\end{pmatrix}\\
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{MC}=&v_{C}\\
\frac{d}{dt}Q_{ML}=&i_{L}.\end{split}

(37)

It is immediate to check that we have

\frac{d}{dt}{\cal Q}=0;\ \ \frac{d}{dt}\Psi=0

along the solutions of the SEs (
37
) in the VCD.
This means that these SEs admit
n_{C}+n_{L}

invariants of motion

(or conserved quantities) given by the components of functions
{\cal Q}
and
\Psi
.
As a consequence, the dynamics evolves on invariant manifolds

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal M}(Q_{0},\Psi_{0})=&\{(v_{C},q_{L},\Phi_{MC},Q_{ML})\in\mathbb{R}^{2(n_{C}+n_{L})}:\\
&{\cal Q}=Q_{0},\Psi=\Psi_{0}\}\end{split}

(38)

where
(Q_{0},\Psi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{C}+n_{L}}
is termed
manifold index
.

Let us now consider the modified mixed potential
\tilde{P}(\Phi_{M},Q_{M};Q_{0},\Psi_{0}):\mathbb{R}^{2(n_{C}+n_{L})}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\begin{split}\tilde{\cal P}&(\Phi_{M},Q_{M};\Psi_{0},Q_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{M}^{\top}H_{\alpha\alpha}\Phi_{M}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{M}^{\top}H_{\beta\beta}Q_{M}\\
&+\Phi_{M}^{\top}H_{\alpha\beta}Q_{M}+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{C}}\int_{0}^{\varphi_{Mi}}\hat{Q}_{Mi}(\sigma)d\sigma\\
&-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{L}}\int_{0}^{q_{Mj}}\hat{\Phi}_{Mj}(\sigma)d\sigma+(\Phi_{M}^{\top}\,Q_{M}^{\top})\begin{pmatrix}-Q_{0}\\
\Psi_{0}\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

(39)

Clearly,
\tilde{\cal P}
is manifold dependent and it is function of the manifold index
(Q_{0},\Psi_{0})
.
It can be verified that the SEs (
34
) in the FCD can be written
as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}C\dot{\Phi}_{MC}\\
L\dot{Q}_{ML}\\
\end{pmatrix}=&\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\Phi_{M}}\tilde{\cal P}(\Phi_{M},Q_{M};Q_{0},\Psi_{0})\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_{M}}\tilde{\cal P}(\Phi_{M},Q_{M};Q_{0},\Psi_{0})\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

(40)

By means of
\tilde{\cal P}
we can also give a compact expression to the invariants of motion.
We have

{\cal Q}=Cv_{C}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\Phi_{M}}\tilde{\cal P}(\Phi_{M},Q_{L};Q_{0},\Psi_{0})+Q_{0}

(41)

while

\Psi=Li_{L}-\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_{M}}\tilde{\cal P}(\Phi_{M},Q_{L};Q_{0},\Psi_{0})+\Psi_{0}.

(42)

6.2
RCM and RLM Networks

Consider the special case where a circuit in
\mathfrak{N}

has no inductors, i.e.,
n_{L}=0
(class RCM).
Under Assumptions
1
,
2
, the tree
{\cal T}
is made by all the elements
{\cal B}_{CM}
and
{\cal B}_{C}
, while all the resistors are on the co-tree
{\cal L}
. In this case, the SEs
in the FCD boil down to

\displaystyle\begin{split}C\dot{\varphi}_{C}=&-G\varphi_{C}-\begin{pmatrix}\hat{Q}_{MC}(\varphi_{CM}+\Phi_{M0})-\hat{Q}_{MC}(\Phi_{M0})\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix}\\
&+Cv_{C0}\end{split}

(43)

where
G
is a symmetric matrix of conductances given by

G=A_{CR}(R_{{\cal L}})^{-1}A_{CR}^{\top}.

Moreover, on the basis of (
30
), the mixed potential
{\cal P}_{\mathrm{RCM}}(v_{C};w_{0}):\mathbb{R}^{n_{C}}\to\mathbb{R}
becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal P}_{\mathrm{RCM}}(v_{C};w_{0})=&\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{C}^{\top}G\varphi_{C}+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\Phi}}\int_{0}^{\varphi_{\alpha i}}(\hat{Q}_{Mi}(\rho+\Phi_{M0i})\\
&-\hat{Q}_{Mi}(\Phi_{M0i}))d\rho-\varphi_{C}^{\top}Cv_{C0}\end{split}

(44)

where
w_{0}=(v_{C0},\Phi_{M0})
.

Due to Theorem
1
, the SEs
(
27
) of
\mathfrak{N}
in the FCD can be written in the

gradient form

C\dot{\varphi}_{C}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi_{C}}{\cal P}_{\mathrm{RCM}}(\varphi_{C};w_{0}).

(45)

This implies that the mixed potential is actually a Lyapunov function that
decreases along the solutions of the SEs (
27
). An analogous
treatment holds when the memristor circuit contains inductors but
there are no capacitors (class RLM).

7
Discussion

Here, we collect some remarks where we discuss the significance of the previous results.

Remark 4
.

The mixed potential (
30
) obtained in Theorem
1
generalizes to
RLCM circuits the mixed potential originally obtained by
Brayton and Moser
[
40
,
41
]
for memristor-less
RLC circuits. The extension has been possible by analyzing memristor circuits in
the FCD, rather than in the VCD, as it was done in the quoted papers by Brayton and Moser.
The use of FCAM and the equivalence principle obtained in Sect.
2.3
between
an RLCM circuit in the FCD and an RLC circuit in the VCD are the key elements enabling
the extension to circuits containing memristors.

Remark 5
.

The mixed potential (
30
) depends upon the initial conditions
for the state variables
w_{0}
in the VCD (cf. (
26
)). It is worth to note that, for special classes
of circuits, as the class considered in Sect.
6.1
, it is directly related to the
invariants of motion and the invariant manifold index
(Q_{0},\Psi_{0})
(cf. (
40
), (
41
) and (
42
)).

Remark 6
.

The expression of the mixed potential (
30
)
will play a crucial role for the analysis of convergence of circuits in the class RLCM,
a topic which we address in a systematic way in a companion paper
[
1
]
.

Remark 7
.

In Sect.
6.2
, we have seen that in the special case of class RCM (or RLM), a memristor circuit
in the FCD is described by a gradient system with respect to the mixed
potential, i.e., the mixed potential is actually a Lyapunov function.
This result is analogous to that obtained in
[
31
, Th. 1]
.
However, while the Lyapunov function used in that paper has been been found via an
abstract mathematical procedure, here we have shown that the Lyapunov
function has a clear circuit theoretic meaning related to the mixed
potential of the memristor circuit.

9
Conclusion

The paper has introduced a mixed potential for a large class of nonlinear
circuits, named RLCM, containing resistors, capacitors, inductors and memristors,
under a completeness assumption for the circuit variables.
This extends to nonlinear circuits with memristors the mixed potential
originally introduced by Brayton and Moser for nonlinear RLC circuits without
memristors
[
40
,
41
]
. The extension
has been obtained by representing a memristor circuit
in the FCD and is based on a fundamental equivalence principle between an
RLCM circuit in the FCD and an RLC circuit in the VCD. Basic properties
of the mixed potential have been established. These include a compact and
useful expression of the SEs in the FCD via the mixed potential, links between
the invariants of motion in the VCD and the mixed potential as well as the
relationships between the existence of the mixed potential and the
reciprocity of multiports used in the analysis.
The results are illustrated and discussed via the application to a number
of basic memristor circuits and large-size memristor circuits
with a neural architecture. In a companion paper
[
1
]
,
currently in preparation,
the mixed potential is used to systematically establish Lyapunov-like
results on convergence for RLCM circuits. Another
topic deserving future research concerns
is the possibility to further extend the
mixed potential to memristor circuits that do not satisfy the assumption of
completeness enforced in this manuscript.
