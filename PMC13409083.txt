Diagnostic accuracy of magnetic resonance enterography and intestinal ultrasound for treatment response assessment in Crohn’s disease (MANTRA): protocol for a multicentre, non-randomised, multiple-arm, prospective cohort comparison study
Authors: Maira Hameed, Busola Adebusoye, Sue Mallett, Katerina Soteriou, Isabelle De Kock, Hameed Rafiee, Steve Halligan, Stuart A Taylor, MANTRA Study Collaborators
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMR enterography (MRE) and intestinal ultrasound (IUS) are used to assess therapeutic response in Crohn’s disease (CD) follow-up. There is, however, no clear guidance regarding which modality should be used for individual patients or if they are interchangeable. Therefore, clinical practice varies. We will compare the accuracy of MRE and IUS to classify treatment response in CD.Methods and analysisMulticentre, non-randomised, multiple arm, prospective cohort study of luminal CD patients aged 16 years or older being treated with steroids, immunomodulators, biologics/biosimilars or small molecules with baseline MRE or IUS. The study will be based in three tertiary referral centres, UK (n=2) and Belgium (n=1), with a target sample size of 155 patients. Patients will undergo both MRE and IUS for response assessment within 20 months of baseline scanning, performed within 6 weeks of each other. One scan will be standard care, the other a research intervention. Interpreting radiologists will be masked to the alternative response assessment test. The primary outcome is the comparative accuracy of MRE and IUS for European Crohn’s and Colitis Organisation-European Society of Gastrointestinal and Abdominal Radiology treatment response group categorisation compared with multidisciplinary consensus panel reference standard, using all available clinical information. Secondary outcomes include the influence of baseline scanning (MRE or IUS) on response characterisation accuracy, response accuracy of MRE and IUS disease activity scoring systems, management impact for each follow-up imaging modality and patient reported preferences.Ethics and disseminationThis protocol has been approved by the National Health Service Research Ethics Committee and West of Scotland Research Ethics Committee 1 (24/WS/0121). Participant informed written consent will be obtained. Findings will be disseminated by peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and patient and public involvement collaborators.Trial registration numberNCT06800326.Keywords: Magnetic Resonance Imaging, Ultrasound, Inflammatory bowel disease, Treatment Outcome, Patient Reported Outcome Measures

Introduction

Background and rationale

The METRIC study demonstrated that both MR enterography (MRE) and intestinal ultrasoundIntestinal Ultrasound (IUS) have high sensitivity and specificity for diagnosing disease extent and staging Crohn’s disease (96% and 83% for MRE, and 90% and 77% for IUS for active disease presence, respectively), and that patients generally find IUS more tolerable than MRE.1 Both modalities are therefore used widely to follow-up Crohn’s disease patients to assess therapeutic response.2 3 However, there is currently no formal guidance regarding which modality should be selected in this setting. We do not know if MRE and IUS can be used interchangeably for treatment response assessment and practice varies widely regarding which imaging modality is selected and/or available, and whether they are used in isolation or interchangeably.4

The imaging signs of disease activity used to assess treatment response have been validated against endoscopic, biochemical and pathological reference standards, with cardinal signs including bowel wall thickening, mural and perimural oedema, ulceration and hypervascularity/neoangiogenesis.5–7 These signs have more recently been incorporated into multiple validated MRE and IUS disease activity scores.5 8 However, even simple measurements, such as bowel wall thickness, are not necessarily equivalent between MRE and IUS given that the physics underlying these modalities differ fundamentally, potentially impacting on image resolution, and differing oral contrast media may influence bowel distension differently. This uncertainty can impede informed management decisions and such considerations assume even greater importance given transmural healing (only assessable using cross-sectional imaging) is an emerging therapeutic target, associated with improved long-term outcomes.5 9

Radiological practice is currently heavily biased towards MRE and IUS provision is relatively sparse in many countries, including the UK.4 10 IUS is generally cheaper than MRE, better tolerated by patients and more environmentally sustainable. For these reasons, there is a need to derive high-quality evidence to support greater use of IUS, if appropriate. Indeed, the European Society of Gastrointestinal and Abdominal Radiology (ESGAR), the James Lind Alliance and Crohn’s & Colitis UK have all identified this topic as a key research priority.11

However, existing literature is limited and comprised largely of small, underpowered (most comprising fewer than 50 patients), single-centre studies, using inconsistent imaging definitions of treatment response and remission, highly variable reference standards (usually endoscopic alone) and differing follow-up intervals.12–17 Furthermore, MRE and IUS scores for objective disease assessment are not fully validated in the setting of response assessment with unclear definitions and thresholds for response, and agreement between intermodality scores is not fully understood.5 14 18 19

This multicentre, prospective study will investigate MRE and IUS treatment response classification accuracy against an expert consensus panel and determine if they can be used interchangeably and, if so, in which patients. Study findings could improve imaging modality selection for individual patients and better define care pathways, which in turn may facilitate appropriate management decisions, for example, timely therapeutic de-escalation or escalation. This is critical to avoid irreversible bowel damage from poorly controlled disease and as some therapeutics, such as biologics, have a significant side-effect profile and cost, exacerbated by prolonged administration. Our study may inform optimal Crohn’s disease follow-up imaging pathways for both clinicians and patients. There are also potential implications for service development, resource planning, and training.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The Diagnostic accuracy of MR Enterography and Ultrasound for Treatment Response Assessment in Crohn’s disease (MANTRA) study is a multicentre, non-randomised, multiple-arm, prospective cohort comparison study. The study protocol has undergone external peer review during ESGAR and Radiological Research Trust grant applications. The study has been registered; Clinicaltrials.gov: NCT06800326.

Study setting

The study will be undertaken in two UK National Health Service (NHS) centres and one academic centre in Belgium. All have established Crohn’s disease multidisciplinary practice, including robust MRE and IUS services, and established referral pathways for response assessment.

Research intervention

An additional, opposing follow-up imaging test, MRE or IUS, was performed depending on which follow-up test has been requested as part of standard clinical care, so that all participants undergo both follow-up MRE and IUS within 6 weeks of each other (figure 1, online supplemental file 1). Based on our prior trial experience, the 6-week interval was chosen to balance the need for contemporaneous imaging with the pragmatics of patients undergoing both imaging tests, for example, MRI appointment availability, patient schedules, etc. We envisage that most recruited patients will undergo both imaging tests at the lower end of the permitted 6-week window and will present the median duration between imaging tests. Follow-up imaging will be compared with either a baseline/pretreatment MRE or IUS performed within the previous 20 months as part of usual clinical care. Although the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence recommendations are for yearly patient treatment monitoring, audit data at recruitment sites revealed variability in practice and this interval accounts for these variations in local practice.20 Audit data suggest most patients are imaged more frequently, and we will present the median interval between baseline/pretreatment and follow-up.

Ultrasound

IU at baseline can be performed as per local protocol, which at all sites includes no oral preparation and only fasting for 4–6 hours prior to the scan. Machines from any ultrasound vendor used as part of standard clinical practice will be eligible.

For the follow-up response assessment scan, a standardised scanning protocol will be implemented to facilitate disease activity scoring.19 All radiologists performing ultrasound will be trained in this standardised scanning protocol during site set up, and an imaging manual will include examples of different IUS parameters and respective degree of severity (online supplemental file 2).

We anticipate that different parameters may be measured at baseline and follow-up IUS because the former is performed pre-enrolment and some aspects cannot be assessed reliably retrospectively. However, when assessable from archived IUS images and/or report, disease activity score parameters will be collected. In contrast, follow-up IUS scan will be performed using a standardised protocol meaning that parameters are consistent between studies.

Imaging interpretation and masking

All study radiologists will have a declared subspecialty interest in gastrointestinal imaging and will be consultant level, with reporting experience of >100 MRE and >100 IUS studies. Both baseline and follow-up imaging tests will be performed and interpreted at each recruitment (figure 1). A minimum of two radiologists will interpret imaging independently at each study site to assess response, one for each of the two follow-up modalities (MRE and IUS), and to enable compliance with the masking protocol.

Specifically, interpreting radiologists will be masked to the opposing follow-up test and clinical details, except for those provided on the request form. They will be unmasked to the baseline MRE or IUS images and original clinical report so they can assess treatment response and finally to all data to assess for any adverse events or uncharacterised incidental findings.

Assessment of ECCO-ESGAR response group

Radiologists will categorise patients into one of the response groups described in the ECCO-ESGAR multidisciplinary expert consensus statement and score their confidence in this decision via a five-point Likert scale.6 Response categorisation will be based on predominantly subjective assessment of standard criteria (mirroring clinical practice) such as change in wall thickness, signal/stratification and mesenteric change (table 1). If a mixed response, the decision will be based on the segment least responding. Prior to patient recruitment, all radiologists will attend a training session to agree regarding how categories will be implemented and will receive an imaging manual (table 1, online supplemental file 2). Any pre-specified serious adverse events and previously uncharacterised incidental findings on all imaging will be reported and flagged using standard local procedures.

Statistical analysis plan

Sample size

The target sample size is 155 patients. The sample size calculation is based on the primary outcome and on an 80% power to detect a 15% difference in paired proportions of accuracy (based on a literature search that estimated accuracy of 85% for MRE vs 70% for IUS).12 13 15–17 25 26 We assume that 55% of patients are positive on both MRE and IUS (from a retrospective review of 120 consecutive MRE and IUS referrals at the central study site). Recruitment will occur until the target sample size is reached with any reasons for non-trial completion or drop out documented.

Primary outcome

Paired comparison within participants for the difference in (a) sensitivity and (b) specificity between MRE and IUS, with 95% CIs to determine statistical significance. We will present tables for: (1) patients with treatment response by reference standard and (2) patients with no treatment response by reference standard. Each table will be a 2×2 table of MRE and IUS for participants.

Reference standard for therapeutic treatment response consists of ECCO-ESGAR response categories.6 Response is defined by transmural remission, stable inactive or significant transmural response. Non-response is defined as stable active or progressive disease.

Patient and public involvement

We are collaborating with IBDrelief, a digital healthcare agency for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) patients. IBDRelief have helped develop the study outcomes, design and acceptability, so that they are focused on patient concerns. IBDrelief representatives will sit on the trial management group and will be involved in interpretation of findings, write-up and dissemination. PPI feedback on the MANTRA study design includes using a questionnaire regarding patient experience of MRE and IUS, and their priorities when considering an imaging test, for example, duration, report availability, comfort and sustainability. This online questionnaire has been reviewed and edited by members of the PPI group.

Their comprehensive patient network will help disseminate study findings to the general public.

The PPI group contributed to our dissemination plans, including to consented participants via direct correspondence, and the wider public via the IBDrelief online platform and social media with a growing number of other IBD patient groups and charities.

Recruitment began in October 2024. We anticipate study closure to recruitment and other local site tasks by the first quarter of 2027.
