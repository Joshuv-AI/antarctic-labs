dFlowGRPO: Rate-Aware Policy Optimization for Discrete Flow Models
Zhengyan Wan, Yidong Ouyang, Panwen Hu, Qiang Sun

Abstract
Discrete flow models (DFMs) are a class of flexible generative models for generating discrete data, and diffusion large language models (dLLMs) can be viewed as a special case with a specific choice of mixture path and a masked source distribution. While several recent works have explored reinforcement learning into dLLMs, its application to more general discrete flow models remains underexplored. In this work, we present discrete Flow-GRPO (dFlowGRPO), a unified reinforcement learning framework for discrete flow models that supports a broad family of probability paths and non-masked source distributions. We derive the full trajectory probability for DFMs and formulate denoising as a Markov decision process, enabling dFlowGRPO to incorporate information from both the associated conditional transition rates and the posterior model during reinforcement learning. We apply dFlowGRPO to FUDOKI, a recent multimodal discrete flow model, and evaluate it on both image generation and multimodal understanding tasks. Empirical results show that dFlowGRPO outperforms existing GRPO-type methods for dLLMs on text-to-image generation tasks and achieves performance competitive with continuous flow-based models trained using FlowGRPO, while also demonstrating strong capabilities on understanding tasks.

To improve the performance of dLLMs, several works on reinforcement learning for dLLMs have recently emerged. Contribution

We summarize our main contributions as follows.

1.

We propose dFlowGRPO, a unified rate-aware policy optimization framework for DFMs with general probability path and conditional rate by utilizing the information of both conditional rate and posterior model.

2.

We derive the transition probability ratio for the denoising trajectory, which can be written as a product of token-wise expected posterior ratio reweighted by a rate-dependent term. This term can be estimated using MC samples without additional forward pass.

3.

We empirically show the effectiveness of dFlowGRPO by applying it to a multimodal DFM. dFlowGRPO improves the reward of base model significantly and consistently on text-to-image generation and multimodal understanding tasks.

Preliminary

In this section, we briefly introduce discrete flow-based models and formulate the denoising process as a Markov decision process.

3.1
Discrete flow models

Consider a data distribution
q_{1}
in a discrete space
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{D}}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the vocabulary and
\mathcal{D}
is the sequence length. We call a stochastic process
({\mathbf{x}}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
a continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) on
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{D}}
if it satisfies the Markov property and for any
x,z\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({\mathbf{x}}_{t+h}=z|{\mathbf{x}}_{t}=x)=\delta_{x}(z)+Q_{t}(x,z)h+o(h),

(1)

where
(Q_{t}(x,z))_{x,z\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{D}}}
is the transition rate matrix satisfying the rate properties:
\sum_{z\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{D}}}Q_{t}(x,z)=0
and
Q_{t}(x,z)\mathbbm{1}(x\neq z)\geq 0
for any
x,z\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{D}}
. Here,
\mathbbm{1}(\cdot)
is the indicator function. Denote
(q_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
as the marginal probability distribution (path) corresponding to
({\mathbf{x}}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
. We say
Q_{t}
can generate
q_{t}
if it satisfies the Kolmogorov forward equation
q_{t}(z)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{D}}}q_{t}(x)Q_{t}(x,z)
for
t\in[0,1]
.

Discrete flow models aim to learn a transition rate
Q_{t}
that can transport a source distribution
q_{0}
(e.g., a uniform distribution or a point mass at the mask token) to the data distribution
q_{1}
by marginalizing a conditional transition rate
Q_{t}(x,z|x_{1})
, parallel to continuous flow matching
35
;
1
. Specifically, given a pre-defined conditional probability path
q_{t|1}(x|x_{1})
and a conditional transition rate
Q_{t}(x,z|x_{1})
that can generate
q_{t|1}
conditional on
x_{1}
, the marginal transition rate
Q_{t}(x,z)=\sum_{x_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{D}}}Q_{t}(x,z|x_{1})p_{1|t}(x_{1}|x)
can generate the marginal probability path
q_{t}

(
8
, see Proposition 3.1 in)
, where
q_{1|t}
is the posterior corresponding to
q_{t|1}
. For computational tractability, the conditional probability path and the associated transition rate are usually restricted to be independent corresponding to each dimension conditional on the terminal state
{\mathbf{x}}_{1}
(that is,
q_{1|t}(x|x_{1})=\prod_{d=1}^{\mathcal{D}}q^{d}_{1|t}(x^{d}|x_{1}^{d})
and
Q_{t}(x,z|x_{1})=\sum_{d=1}^{\mathcal{D}}\delta_{x^{\backslash d}}(z^{\backslash d})Q_{t}^{d}(x^{d},z^{d}|x_{1}^{d})
), which implies that the marginal transition rate matrix is sparse:

\displaystyle Q_{t}(x,z)=\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\delta_{x^{\backslash d}}(z^{\backslash d})Q_{t}^{d}(x,z^{d}),

where
Q_{t}^{d}(x,z^{d})=\sum_{x_{1}^{d}\in\mathcal{S}}Q_{t}^{d}(x^{d},z^{d}|x_{1}^{d})q^{d}_{1|t}(x_{1}^{d}|x)
. In particular, choosing the mixture path
q^{d}_{t|1}(x^{d}|x_{1}^{d})=\kappa_{t}\delta_{x_{1}^{d}}(x^{d})+(1-\kappa_{t})\delta_{\mathbbm{m}}(x^{d})
with the conditional rate
Q_{t}^{d}(x^{d},z^{d}|x_{1}^{d})=\frac{\dot{\kappa}_{t}}{1-\kappa_{t}}(\delta_{x_{1}^{d}}(z^{d})-\delta_{x^{d}}(z^{d}))
, we can recover the masked diffusion models
(
54
;
44
;
49
;
42
)
, where
\kappa_{t}
is the time scheduler.

3.2
Denoising as a Markov decision process

Consider a time discretization scheme
0=t_{0}<t_{1}\cdots<t_{K}=1
. Let
p({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{c}})
be the transition probability at the
k
-th timestep given a prompt (or context)
{\mathbf{c}}
. Following DDPO
(
6
)
and Flow-GRPO
(
33
)
, we can formulate the denoising process as a Markov Decision Process defined by the tuple
(\mathbf{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\rho_{0})
, where
\bf{S}
is a set of states,
\mathcal{A}
is a set of actions,
P:\mathbf{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathbf{S}\to\mathbb{R}
is the transition probability,
r:\mathbf{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
is the reward function and
\rho_{0}:\bf{S}\to\mathbb{R}
is the distribution of the initial state
s_{0}
(considering a constant discount factor for simplicity). At the
k
-th step with a state
{\mathbf{s}}_{k}=({\mathbf{c}},k,{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})
, an agent takes a stochastic action
{\mathbf{a}}_{k}={\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}
with probability
\pi({\mathbf{a}}_{{k}}|{\mathbf{s}}_{k})=p({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{c}})
and receives a reward
R({\mathbf{s}}_{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{k})
, moving to the next state
{\mathbf{s}}_{k+1}
with deterministic transition
P({\mathbf{s}}_{k+1}|{\mathbf{s}}_{{k}},{\mathbf{a}}_{{k}})=(\delta_{{\mathbf{c}}},\delta_{k+1},\delta_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}})
. In continuous diffusion or flow-based models, it is common to take
\rho_{0}({\mathbf{s}}_{0})=(p_{c}({\mathbf{c}}),\delta_{0},\mathcal{N}(\bf{0},\bf{I}))
and assign the reward of the entire denoising trajectory to the terminal state; that is,
R({\mathbf{s}}_{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{k})=\mathcal{R}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{c}})
if
k=K-1
, and
0
otherwise. The goal of RL is to maximize the expected return
\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c},\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|{\mathbf{c}})}{[\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}R({\mathbf{s}}_{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{k})]}
, which has the gradient
\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c},\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|{\mathbf{c}})}{[\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\nabla_{\theta}\log p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{c}})\mathcal{R}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{c}})]}
.

4.1
GRPO for discrete flow models

Given a prompt
{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c}
and the number of denoising steps
K
, we can generate
G
final samples
{\{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i)}\}}_{i\in{[G]}}
using the Euler solver discussed in the previous section. Let
{\{({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)})\}}_{i\in{[G]}}\overset{\triangle}{=}{\{({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{t_{0}},{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{t_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{t_{K}})\}}_{i\in{[G]}}
be the corresponding denoising trajectory. Since the denoising process can be formulated as a Markov decision process and the full trajectory probability can be decoupled
(p({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)})=p({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(i)})\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}p({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i)}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)},{\mathbf{c}}))
, following Flow-GRPO
(
33
)
, we can use the following training objective to optimize the policy model:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{dFlowGRPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c},{\{{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\}}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{c}})}

(2)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}{\Big(\min({[r_{k}^{(i)}(\theta)]}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}}\hat{A}_{k}^{(i)},\text{clip}({[r_{k}^{(i)}(\theta)]}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}},1-{\epsilon},1+{\epsilon})\hat{A}_{k}^{(i)})-\beta D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{ref})\Big)}\Big\}},

where
{\epsilon}
is the clipping parameter,
r_{t}^{(i)}(\theta)
is the step-level transition probability ratio between the current policy and the old policy and the estimated advantage
\hat{A}_{k}^{(i)}
can be computed using the normalized reward in the group, which is independent of
k
:

\displaystyle r_{k}^{(i)}(\theta)=

\displaystyle~\frac{p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i)}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)},{\mathbf{c}})}{p_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i)}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)},{\mathbf{c}})};\quad\hat{A}_{k}^{(i)}=\frac{\mathcal{R}({\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i)},{\mathbf{c}})-\text{mean}({\{\mathcal{R}({\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i)},{\mathbf{c}})\}}_{i\in{[G]}})}{\text{std}({\{\mathcal{R}({\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i)},{\mathbf{c}})\}}_{i\in{[G]}})}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}\sum_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}{\Big\{\frac{p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{d}_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}-\log\frac{p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{d}_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}-1\Big\}},

4.2
Rate-aware transition probability ratio

To compute the GRPO objective, we have to calculate the transition probability ratio for the Euler solver (
algorithm

1
). Following the discussion in
60

30

[t_{k},t_{k+1}]

Q^{\text{Euler}}_{t}(x,z)=\sum_{d=1}^{\mathcal{D}}\delta_{x^{\backslash d}}(z^{\backslash d})\delta_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}}(x^{d})\hat{Q}_{t_{k-1}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},z^{d})

\hat{Q}_{t_{k-1}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},z^{d})=Q^{d}_{t_{k-1}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},z^{d}|X_{1}^{d})
is the estimator of the oracle rate using a single MC sample

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{Q,k}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})=\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}={\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}){\Big(\exp{\Big(-(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})\Big)}\Big)}

(3)

\displaystyle+\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}\neq{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}){\Big(\frac{Q_{t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|X_{1}^{d})}{\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})}{\Big\{1-\exp{\Big(-(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})\Big)}\Big\}}\Big)},

\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{d},X_{1}^{d})=\sum_{z^{d}\neq{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}}Q^{d}_{t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},z^{d}|X_{1}^{d})

.

\displaystyle r_{k}(\theta)=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\frac{p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{d}_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}^{d}\sim p^{\theta_{old}}_{1|t_{k}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{\Big\{\underbrace{w_{Q,k}^{\theta_{old},d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})}_{\text{rate-dependent weight}}\underbrace{\frac{p^{\theta}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{\theta_{old}}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}}_{\text{posterior ratio}}\Big\}},

(4)

where the rate-dependent weight (satisfying

\displaystyle~w_{Q,k}^{\theta,d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})=\frac{\tilde{w}_{Q,k}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})}{p_{\theta}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}.

, we can use the empirical version of the transition probability ratio to estimate
r_{k}(\theta)
at time
t_{k}
without additional forward passes; that is,

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{k}(\theta)=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\frac{\hat{p}^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{\hat{p}^{d}_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}{\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\Big\{\hat{w}_{Q,k}^{\theta_{old},d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{j,1}^{d})\frac{p^{\theta}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{j,1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{\theta_{old}}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{j,1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}\Big\}}\Big)},

(6)

where
{\{X_{j,1}^{d}\}}_{j\in{[n]}}
are i.i.d. samples from
p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})
and

\displaystyle\hat{w}_{Q,k}^{\theta_{old},d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{j,1}^{d})=\frac{\tilde{w}_{Q,k}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{j,1}^{d})}{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\tilde{w}_{Q,k}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{l,1}^{d})}.

(7)

When we only use one single Monte Carlo sample (i.e.,
n=1
), the weight estimator is equal to one, which implies that the transition probability ratio estimator reduces to the product of the token-wise posterior. In this scenario, the estimation of the transition probability ratio is not only independent of the conditional rate but also unrelated to the next state
{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}
, which has a nonignorable bias. Additionally, the ratio estimation with a single MC sample also leads to slow convergence during training; we will discuss this in the ablation study.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce dFlowGRPO, a unified RL training framework for DFMs. By deriving the transition probability ratio of the denoising process, dFlowGRPO leverages information from both the conditional rate and the posterior model. We evaluate the proposed methods on both image-to-text generation and multimodal understanding tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness and strong performance across various benchmarks.

A limitation of this paper is that we evaluate our framework on only a single DFM. In fact, building on our framework, one can also apply different rate-aware transition probability ratio to dLLMs by considering alternative conditional rates constructed according to the detailed balance condition
(
8
, see Appendix F in)
. We leave this interesting direction for future work.

Proof of
theorem

1

Proof.

Note that for each denoising step, the tokens of
{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}
are independent conditioning on the last state
{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}
; that is,
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}p_{\theta}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})
. According to the sampling algorithm (
algorithm

1
), the transition probability for the
d
-th token is:

\displaystyle~p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}^{d}\sim p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}\Big\{\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}={\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}){\Big(\exp{\Big(-(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})\Big)}\Big)}

\displaystyle~\quad+\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}\neq{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}){\Big(\frac{Q_{t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|X_{1}^{d})}{\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})}{\Big\{1-\exp{\Big(-(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})\Big)}\Big\}}\Big)}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}^{d}\sim p^{\theta_{old},d}_{1|t_{k}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}\Big\{\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}={\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d})\exp{\Big(-(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})\Big)}\frac{p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{\theta_{old},d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}

\displaystyle~\quad+\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}\neq{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d})\frac{Q_{t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|X_{1}^{d})}{\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})}\times{\Big\{1-\exp{\Big(-(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})\Big)}\Big\}}\frac{p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{\theta_{old},d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}\Big\},

where
\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})=\sum_{z^{d}\neq{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}}Q^{d}_{t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},z^{d}|X_{1}^{d})
.

Thus, the transition probability ratio between the current policy and the old policy is

\displaystyle r_{k}(\theta)=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\frac{p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{d}_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}^{d}\sim p^{\theta_{old}}_{1|t_{k}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{\Big\{w_{Q,k}^{\theta_{old},d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})\frac{p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{\theta_{old},d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}\Big\}},

where the rate-dependent weight
w_{Q,k}^{\theta_{old},d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})
is defined by

\displaystyle~w_{Q}^{\theta_{old},d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})=\frac{1}{p_{\theta_{old}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}\Big\{\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}={\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}){\Big(\exp{\Big(-(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})\Big)}\Big)}

\displaystyle~\qquad\qquad+\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}\neq{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}){\Big(\frac{Q_{t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|X_{1}^{d})}{\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})}{\Big\{1-\exp{\Big(-(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})\Big)}\Big\}}\Big)}\Big\}.

The transition probability ratio derived above can be estimated by the empirical mean for a general probability path and the associated conditional rate. In the following, we give an example for the specific choice of the mixture path with conditional rate
Q_{t}^{d}(x^{d},z^{d}|x_{1}^{d})=\frac{\dot{\kappa}_{t}}{1-\kappa_{t}}(\delta_{x_{1}^{d}}(z^{d})-\delta_{x^{d}}(z^{d}))
.

Example (mixture path)

For the mixture path with conditional rate
Q_{t}^{d}(x^{d},z^{d}|x_{1}^{d})=\frac{\dot{\kappa}_{t}}{1-\kappa_{t}}(\delta_{x_{1}^{d}}(z^{d})-\delta_{x^{d}}(z^{d}))
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{t_{k}}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d},X_{1}^{d})=\begin{cases}0,&~\text{ if }{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}=X_{1}^{d}\\
\frac{\dot{\kappa}_{t}}{1-\kappa_{t}},&~\text{ if }{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}\neq X_{1}^{d}\end{cases},

which implies that

\displaystyle r_{k}(\theta)=

\displaystyle~\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\frac{p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{d}_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\Big\{\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}\neq{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d})\frac{p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{\theta_{old},d}_{1|t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}

\displaystyle~\quad+\mathbbm{1}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}={\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d})\frac{p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})+(1-p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}))g_{Q}(k)}{p^{\theta_{old},d}_{1|t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})+(1-p^{\theta_{old},d}_{1|t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}))g_{Q}(k)}\Big\},

where

g_{Q}(k)=\exp(-(t_{k+1}-t_{k})\frac{\dot{\kappa}_{t}}{1-\kappa_{t}}).

In particular, if the source distribution is
\delta_{\mathbbm{m}}
, then we have

\displaystyle r_{k}(\theta)=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\frac{p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{d}_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}=\prod_{d:{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{d}=\mathbbm{m}\text{ and }{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}\neq\mathbbm{m}}\frac{p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{p^{\theta_{old},d}_{1|t_{k}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})},

which is independent of the time scheduler.

1
, we have

\displaystyle r_{k}^{(i)}(\theta)=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\frac{p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i),d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)})}{p^{d}_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i),d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)})}=\prod_{d\in{[\mathcal{D}]}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}^{d}\sim p^{\theta_{old}}_{1|t_{k}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{\Big\{w_{Q,k}^{\theta_{old},d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i),d},X_{1}^{d})\frac{p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)})}{p^{\theta_{old},d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)})}\Big\}}.

Thus, we can obtain that

\displaystyle~\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{J}_{\text{dFlowGRPO}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}A_{k}^{(i)}\nabla_{\theta}{[r_{k}^{(i)}(\theta)]}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}A_{k}^{(i)}{[r_{k}^{(i)}(\theta)]}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}}{\Big(\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}\sum_{d=1}^{\mathcal{D}}\nabla_{\theta}\log p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i),d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)})\Big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}A_{k}^{(i)}{[r_{k}^{(i)}(\theta)]}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}}{\Big(\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}\sum_{d=1}^{\mathcal{D}}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}^{d}\sim p^{\theta}_{1|t_{k}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{\Big\{\tilde{w}_{Q,k}^{d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i),d},X_{1}^{d})\nabla_{\theta}\log p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)})\Big\}}}{p^{d}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i),d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)})}\Big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}A_{k}^{(i)}{[r_{k}^{(i)}(\theta)]}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}}{\Big(\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}\sum_{d=1}^{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}^{d}\sim p^{\theta}_{1|t_{k}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}})}{\Big\{w_{Q,k}^{\theta,d}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k+1}}^{(i),d},X_{1}^{d})\nabla_{\theta}\log p^{\theta,d}_{1|t_{k}}(X_{1}^{d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t_{k}}^{(i)})\Big\}}\Big)},

where we use the definition of the rate-dependent weight (see, equation
3
and equation
5
) and the expectation is taken over
{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c},{\{{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\}}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{c}})
. Consequently, the gradient of the dFlowGRPO objective has a form similar to the gradient of standard Flow-GRPO (
{[r_{k}^{(i)}(\theta)]}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}}\approx 1
), with the policy score replaced by the posterior score weighted by rate-dependent weight.

Appendix D
Some discussions and additional experiments

In this section, we discuss other RL methods for DFM such as mean-field approximation methods
[
73
]
and online DPO
[
17
]
, and conduct additional experiments for these methods. We also evaluate our dFlowGRPO on PickScore and GenEval with different NFEs.

D.1
diffu-GRPO and diffu-GSPO

73
proposed to use mean-field approximation to estimate the log-likelihood for dLLMs in RL training. The corresponding diffu-GRPO is

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{diffu-GRPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c},{\{{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\}}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{c}})}

(8)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}\sum_{d=1}^{\mathcal{D}}{\Big(\min(r^{(i)}_{d}(\theta)\hat{A}^{(i)},\text{clip}(r^{(i)}_{d}(\theta),1-{\epsilon},1+{\epsilon})\hat{A}^{(i)})-\beta D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{ref})\Big)}\Big\}},

where
r^{(i)}_{d}(\theta)=\frac{p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i),d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(i)},{\mathbf{c}})}{p_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i),d}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(i)},{\mathbf{c}})}
is the token-level posterior ratio at
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
. If the source distribution is a masked point mass, then the ratio only depends on
{\mathbf{c}}
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(i)}
.

Following GSPO
[
75
]
, we can propose diffu-GSPO to improve the performance of diffu-GRPO by simply replacing the arithmetic mean with geometric mean of token-level posterior ratios (see
fig.

5
for comparison):

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{diffu-GSPO}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c},{\{{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\}}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{c}})}

(9)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}{\Big(\min{\Big({\Big[\prod_{d=1}^{\mathcal{D}}r^{(i)}_{d}(\theta)\Big]}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}}\hat{A}^{(i)},\text{clip}({\Big[\prod_{d=1}^{\mathcal{D}}r^{(i)}_{d}(\theta)\Big]}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}}},1-{\epsilon},1+{\epsilon})\hat{A}^{(i)}\Big)}-\beta D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{ref})\Big)}\Big\}}.

Compared with our proposed dFLowGRPO, the mean-field approximation in diffu-GRPO and diffu-GSPO only use the information of the samples at the terminal time, leading to inaccurate log-likelihood or trajectory probability estimation. Additionally, it can make RL training unstable or even collapse for a general probability path (see
fig.

5
).

D.2
dFlowDPO: Generalization to online DPO

In this section, we generalize our approach to online DPO
[
17
]
.
Consider the following regularized RLHF problem:

\displaystyle\pi^{*}=\argmax_{\pi_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c},{\mathbf{x}}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|{\mathbf{c}})}{\Big\{{[r({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{c}})]}-\beta D_{KL}{\Big(\pi_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})\Big\|\pi_{\text{old}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})\Big)}\Big\}}.

The optimal policy
\pi^{*}
has the closed-form
\pi^{*}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})\propto\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{c}})/\beta)\pi_{old}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})
. Plugging
r({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{c}})=\beta\log\frac{\pi^{*}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})}{\pi_{old}({\mathbf{x}}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})}+\log C({\mathbf{c}})
into the log-likelihood of BT model, then we have the following online DPO training objective for discrete flow models:

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c},{\{{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\}}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{c}})}{\Big\{-\log\sigma{\Big(\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{+}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})}{\pi_{old}({\mathbf{x}}^{+}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})}{\pi_{old}({\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}|{\mathbf{c}})}\Big)}\Big\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}}\sim p_{c},{\{{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\}}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\theta_{old}}}(\cdot|{\mathbf{c}})}{\Big\{-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\log\sigma{\Big(\beta K{\Big\{\log\frac{p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{+}_{t_{k}}|{\mathbf{x}}^{+}_{t_{k-1}},{\mathbf{c}})}{p_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}^{+}_{t_{k}}|{\mathbf{x}}^{+}_{t_{k-1}},{\mathbf{c}})}-\log\frac{p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_{k}}|{\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_{k-1}},{\mathbf{c}})}{p_{\theta_{old}}({\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_{k}}|{\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_{k-1}},{\mathbf{c}})}\Big\}}\Big)}\Big\}}

\displaystyle\overset{\triangle}{=}

\displaystyle~\mathcal{J}_{\text{dFlowDPO}}(\theta),

where we use Jensen’s inequality (for training efficiency, similar to
59
), and
{\mathbf{x}}^{+}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}
and
{\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_{0}:t_{K}}
are the trajectories with highest reward and lowest reward in the group, respectively.

Appendix E
Implementation detail

We use 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs for all experiments. We fix group size
G=24
and the number of MC samples
n=24
across tasks. We train the model using batches of 16 prompts per gradient update, with gradient clipping at
1.0
and the AdamW optimizer with
\beta_{1}=0.9
and
\beta_{2}=0.999
. We set the learning rate
1\times 10^{-5}
for text-to-image generation and
1\times 10^{-6}
for multimodal understanding. We use asymmetric clipping parameters
{\epsilon}_{\text{high}}=1.5\cdot{\epsilon}_{\text{low}}=1.5\times 10^{-3}
for GRPO objective by searching from
{\{1.5\times 10^{-2},1.5\times 10^{-3},1.5\times 10^{-4}\}}
, following the clip-higher strategy proposed in DAPO
[
32
]
. For training stability, we update the old policy once per 48 gradient update. We train dFlowGRPO for 3300 steps on GenEval, 6300 steps on PickScore, and 1500 steps on ScienceQA. We report the computational cost of our experiments in
