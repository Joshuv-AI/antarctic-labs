1 Background

Digital Image Correlation (DIC) is “a non-contact means of measuring motion and deformation
using digital images of the object of interest” [1], capable of full-field measurements over large
areas.

 1

Getting started in DIC can be diﬀicult, since there are many different techniques, and the
body of literature on DIC is extensive. IEEE Xplore1 alone lists nearly 11,000 references,
including magazines, conference proceedings, journal articles, books, etc. A Google search2
returns around 145 million results.
The intent of this annotated bibliography is to provide an entry point for beginners and to
collect references that may be useful to more advanced practitioners.

2 Introduction

Excellent resources for all levels of experience are the International Digital Image Correlation
Society (iDICs) website [1] and its comprehensive DIC guide [2]. Section 10 lists an article
series that is cited by and serves as a good companion to [2].
Other resources that I found useful in getting started are [3], [4], [5].
If you are interested in how DIC performs relative to other full-field optical techniques, [6]
provides a comparison.

3 Processing methods

A good entry point for understanding DIC processing methods, in addition to the references
in Section 2, is [7]. References that present other types of processing methods are [8], [9], [10],
[11].

4 Calibration target and speckle generation software

References [12], [13] are Python and Java codes, respectively, that can generate several different
types of calibration targets. Reference [14] is a compiled Python package that can generate dot
grid calibration targets. Online target generators are also available. There is even an online
version of [13].
The speckle pattern generator [15] is excellent. It is free but not open source.

6 Simulation methods

Simulating the entire DIC measurement process reduces the risk in designing and fielding a
system. Here are a couple of articles that I found useful on the topic of DIC simulation: [29],
[30]. Both use Blender3 , an excellent open-source 3D computer graphics software package.

7 Photography

I found the photography concepts of Depth of Field (DoF) and exposure to be very important
in designing a DIC system with good focus and adequate lighting. Reference [31] is a great
DoF calculator, especially because it lets you enter custom sensor dimensions. Lighting and
exposure became clearer after reading [32].
