 Radioactivity-in-materials lead for nEXO

 R. MacLellan, University of Kentucky (Principle Investigator)

 The nEXO collaboration aims to demonstrate the Majorana nature of the neutrino by ob-
serving the neutrinoless double-beta decay of 136 Xe with a next-generation experiment. The
University of Kentucky nEXO group (UKY) operates a world-leading ultra-low-background
γ-ray spectrometer as part of the nEXO radioactive background control R&D effort. The
nEXO project is a proposed ton-scale neutrinoless double-beta decay experiment. The UKY
PI is leading the radioactivity content assessment for all materials required by the nEXO
project. As a subset of this task, UKY is also responsible for project-wide management
of all low-background γ-ray spectrometry measurements; material assays with these types
of instruments, together with ICP-MS, neutron-activation-analysis, and α-spectrometry, are
critical to ensuring that the nEXO project can achieve its design sensitivity.

AT THE TIME, A DOE NUCLEAR PHYSICS PRIORITY

 In the 2015 Long Range Plan for Nuclear Science [1], NSAC recommended DOE and NSF
pursue “the timely development and deployment of a U.S.-led ton-scale neutrinoless double
beta decay experiment”—second only to the recommendation that they “capitalize on the
investments made.” This award requested DOE NP invest in the operation of Ge-IV, an
ultra-low-background γ-ray spectrometer, the result of a prior investment of DOE NP via
the University of Alabama, as an integral part of the nEXO project. The ultimate goal of
the research activities proposed was to facilitate the observation of the neutrinoless double-
beta decay channel of 136 Xe, and thereby discover the Majorana nature of the neutrino. The
nEXO Collaboration, which includes the University of Kentucky as a member institution and
the PI as a member of the collaboration board, was progressing rapidly on R&D towards
an anticipated DOE Critical Decision 1 (CD-1) process for neutrinoless double-beta decay.
Unfortunately, DOE canceled the nEXO CD-1 review and the nEXO project in late 2024,
early in this award period.
 The PI has been a member of the nEXO Collaboration since its inception in 2014, first as
a research associate at SLAC, then as a faculty member at the University of South Dakota,
and now with the University of Kentucky. The PI was also a key member of numerous
analyses and of the radioactive background control group of EXO-200, the predecessor to
nEXO. nEXO published an early sensitivity study, to the 0νββ-decay of 136 Xe, as early
as 2017 [2], and a recent update including numerous R&D and analysis advancements [3].
The studies include simulating a fully functioning detector and analyzing the data it will
produce. The measured radioactivity content of all candidate[4] materials out of which to
construct this detector were included in these studies. They demonstrate that even this
snapshot of our detector, that is being continuously improved, and with materials at hand,
could establish a limit on the half-life of the 0νββ-decay of 136 Xe greater than 1.35×1028 yr
at 90% CL in 10 yr; or a limit on the effective Majorana neutrino mass that would exclude
the possibility of an inverted hierarchy, should the neutrino be Majorana in nature.

with the Ge-IV. We request funding for travel to SURF bimonthly to perform these assays

NEUTRON ACTIVATION ANALYSIS WITH ENHANCED SENSITIVITY TO
238 U

 The activation product of 238 U, 239 Np, has a half-life of only 2.9 d. Significant side activ-
ities, typically also with relatively short half-lives, can easily spoil the sensitivity of NAA to
238
 U. One prime example of this type of material is sapphire. The nEXO project wanted

to use sapphire (Al2 O3 ) as electrically isolating spacers and support rods within the liquid
xenon TPC, which makes any residual natural radioactivity in the sapphire particularly con-
cerning. Sapphire cannot be dissolved in acids, making it unsuitable for ICP-MS analysis.
Side activation of 27 Al to 24 Na, and of chemical impurities that produce significant 72 Ga
and 99 Mo, results in an overwhelming background. The NAA sensitivity to 238 U is limited
to what can only possibly be observed after a significant fraction of it has decayed away.
Tsang et al. [6] recently published a study that demonstrates that requiring the coincidence
of two 239 Np γ-rays, detected in a coincident germanium detector setup, would result in an
8× improvement in the sensitivity to 238 U.
 The PI identified an existing setup at the Triangle Universities Nuclear Laboratory
(TUNL) that could fill this role in the nEXO radioactive background control program almost
immediately. TUNL originally deployed this setup at KURF before it shut down. It was,
coincidentally, used to search for the two-neutrino double-beta decay of 150 Nd and 96 Zr (Kidd
et al. [7], Finch and Tornow [8]), both to excited daughter states. The TUNL coincident
germanium detector crystals are slightly smaller than those used in the study by Tsang et al.
[6] and employ additional, almost 4π, NaI anticoincidence counters for Compton suppression
(see figure 3). The UA group, in particular Dr. Tsang, repeated their sapphire sensitivity

study with the smaller TUNL detectors (but without the additional Compton suppression,

The TUNL setup was found to be comparable in sensitivity to the one envisaged by Tsang

Research Scientist Dr. Finch to proceed with coincident NAA. After a long delay in the
availability of the only reactor in the country suitable for activating our samples, a sapphire
sample was activated in May 2024. The PI performed the sample recovery at TUNL and
oversaw the initial counting on the TUNL coincident HPGe detectors. After a month of
data collection and a year of unexpectedly complex analysis, the trial NAA confirmed the
sensitivity improvement predicted by Tsang et al. [6] in our paper [9].

RADIOASSAY SUMMARY FOR NEXO

 The publication most relevant to the work of the PI and to the PI as nEXO L-3 for
Radioactivity in Materials is a comprehensive tabulation of nEXO radioassays, including
some radon counting measurements made by the EXO-200 radioassay effort. This publication
reports on almost 300 measurements, including some of the most sensitive ever produced for
many materials. The publication is now with the nEXO collaboration for final review before
we submit it to Physical Review C sometime late in February 2026. The PI, as the nEXO
L-3 for this material, is the lead author of this paper.

WORKFORCE DEVELOPMENT AND UNDERGRADUATE RESEARCH
EXPERIENCES

 This award supported the PI’s supervision of two PhD students; one at the University of
Kentucky, and one at the University of South Dakota, whom the PI has been supervising
since he was a Professor there in 2019. Both students successfully defended their PhD theses
in 2025. The UK student, who graduated in May 2025, was directly supported by this award
as a research assistant. He is now an instructor at Cleveland Community College in North
Carolina.
 This award also supported the PI’s supervision of four undergraduate research projects.
Two of these students are now in graduate physics programs at Temple and Purdue, while
the others are still completing their undergraduate degrees. Their research efforts earned
two of them authorship credit on our sapphire NAA paper [9].

CONCLUSION

 This award led to contributions to six peer-reviewed publications, one of which would
not have been possible without it. The most significant publication, also directly supported
by this award, will soon be submitted to Physical Review C. This award contributed to the
completion of two PhD theses and four undergraduate research projects, all supervised by
the PI. The award funds were exhausted on travel to SURF and TUNL to operate Ge-IV
and perform a coincident NAA of sapphire, to support one of the PhD students as a research
assistant at the University of Kentucky, and the effort of the PI.
