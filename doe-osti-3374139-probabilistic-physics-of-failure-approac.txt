 Task: Probabilistic Physics of Failure

 What is Probabilistic Physics of Failure?
 • Ordinarily take a statistical approach to estimating failure probability when
 sufficient operating experience data is available.
 • Some significantly novel components/functions do not possess sufficient
 operating data to take a statistical evidence-based approach.
 • Probabilistic Physics of Failure Models:
 − Stress-strength model
 − Damage-endurance model
 − Performance-requirement model

 Tokomak Fusion & The Divertor Component

 • The Divertor, and specifically the plasma facing units and their cooling systems,
 lack operating experience in their unique environment and requirements.
 • A probabilistic physics of failure model would seek to:
 − Determine impact of irradiation
 − Determine impact of thermal cycling
 − Determine impact of hydrogen embrittlement
 • To support evaluating reliability with respect to failure pathways including thermal,
 mechanical, radiation shielding, and radioactivity containment.
 • Evaluate failure type and its associated consequences (e.g. melting will have
 greater consequences than loss of tube strength).

 Sensitivity Studies: Nominal or “Steady” Case
 • To reduce computation time, the original pulsed operation was replaced with a single
 steady plasma pulse (Hodille et al., 2021.) to approximate the total permeation rate and retention
 for equivalent plasma fluences.
 • Time step interval was increased, allowed by removing the variation in boundary conditions.
 • Other methods of reducing runtime (convergence limit, spatial mesh fidelity, converting to steady-
 state) were not used. Computation time was reduced from ~115 min to 3-10 min.
 • Important: impacts of cycling temperatures are lost using this simplification

 Co-generation
 Diverting high temperature steam to alternate
 processes:

 Benefits:
 • Greater plant efficiency
 • Flexible operation
 • Greater economic opportunity

 Risks:
 • Addition of diversion components add to plant
 risk
 • Thermal heat diversion changes the operating
 conditions of other plant components
 Sources: https://www.iaea.org/topics/non-electric-applications/industrial-applications-and-nuclear-cogeneration
