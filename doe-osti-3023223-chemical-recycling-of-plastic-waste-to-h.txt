Nature of Work
Describe the research (summary of Scope of Work and principal objectives of the CRADA):
The aim of this project was to develop scalable methods to convert plastic waste to petrochemical-equivalent
feedstocks and demonstrate their utility in lubricant base oil and wax applications. These objectives were partially
accomplished, by scaling up the synthesis of platinum-on-strontium titanate catalysts to twenty gram batches,
scaling up the conversion of plastic waste to twenty-five grams of plastic, producing high-melt waxes and
characterizing their properties, and demonstrating the purification of high-melt waxes. High-melt waxes are one
petrochemical-equivalent feedstock that can be made from plastic waste, consisting of a hard wax with a melt point
above 65 C. They are used as ingredients in hot melt adhesives, coatings, and cosmetics. Producing a
petrochemical-equivalent feedstock from plastic waste provides an advantage in lower carbon footprint and
manufacturing cost, as the waste feedstock costs less than crude oil.

Conclusions drawn from this CRADA; include any major accomplishments:
Summary of Major Accomplishments:
1. The synthesis of platinum-on-strontium titanate, using both hydrothermal and commercial strontium titanate,
was scaled to 20 grams/batch. The process consisted of treating the strontium titanate support to activate the
surface and increase hydroxyl density, followed by two cycles of deposition of platinum precursors by a surface
organometallic chemistry method, and then reduction under hydrogen to form platinum nanoparticles.
2. The synthesis of high-melt waxes was scaled from 3 grams to 25 grams through batch hydrogenolysis with
platinum catalysts. It was discovered that the conversion is mass-transfer limited, and the reaction time was
increased from 48 hours to 144 hours to account for the increased mass of polymer.
3. The high-melt wax was solvent-washed, melt-filtered and distilled to remove catalyst and volatiles, and
exhibited comparable physical properties to a high-grade polyethylene or Fischer-Tropsch wax, with negligible color
or odor. This wax was formulated externally into test candles and lipstick, which exhibited comparable performance
to equivalent products.
4. Two new reactors are in the process of being set up – a 100 mL semi-batch reactor with continuous gas
flow and heavy-duty mixing, and a 4 L semi-batch reactor with equivalent components. These are designed to
explore the mass transfer limitations identified in the scale-up of the high-melt waxes to 25 grams, and should permit
scaling to 1 kg and beyond.

Summary of technology transfer benefits to industry and, if applicable, path forward/anticipated next steps
towards commercialization:
The benefit to industry of transferring the technology for the scalable conversion of plastic waste to petrochemical-
equivalent feedstocks is the ability to inexpensively produce high-grade waxes and base oils, decreasing the cost
of formulations for products like lubricants and adhesives that use those ingredients. Additionally, the technology
ANL-1060 (02/13/2023)

creates an application for grades of plastic waste that are not suitable for mechanical recycling, creating a market
application for plastic waste that is currently landfilled.
Aeternal Upcycling (Participant) has secured $175k in investment from mHUB through the Climate and Energy Tech
Accelerator and $40k in sample purchases from potential customers. Additionally, Argonne National Laboratory
(Contractor) and Participant have been awarded a $2M BETO Lab Call. Together, these funds will be used to
continue development of the technology described here.
