Even harder pseudovariety membership problem
Marcel Jackson

Abstract
. We give a finite semigroup for which the membership problem for the generated pseudovariety is hard for Difference P.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 20M07

For a finite monoid (or semigroup)
{\bf M}
, let
\textsf{varmem}({\bf M})
denote the problem of deciding, when given a finite monoid
{\bf S}
(or semigroup), whether
{\bf S}
lies in the pseudovariety
\mathbb{V}({\bf M})
generated by
{\bf M}
.
In this note, we derive a new level of hardness for the problem
\textsf{varmem}({\bf M})
for monoids, with all arguments holding in either the monoid or semigroup signature. Throughout, hardness reductions will be logspace many-one reductions.

Let
{\bf J}_{k}
denote the monoid of all order-preserving transformations
f
of the
k+1
-element chain
0\leq 1\leq\dots\leq k
satisfying
f(i)\geq i
for all
i
.
Let
{\bf B}_{2}^{1}
denote the six-element Brandt monoid.
The first finite monoids with intractable
varmem
problem were given in Jackson and McKenzie
[
2
]
(
NP
-hard).
More recently Jackson
[
1
]
showed that
\textsf{varmem}({\bf B}_{2}^{1})
is
NP
-hard
while Klíma, Kunc and Polák showed that
\textsf{varmem}({\bf J}_{k})
is co-
NP
-complete, for each
k\geq 4
.

Theorem 1
.

For
k\geq 4
the problem
\textsf{varmem}({\bf B}_{2}^{1}\times{\bf J}_{k})
is
DP
-hard.

The complexity class
DP
(“difference
P
”) is the second level of the Boolean hierarchy (BH), consisting of problems that are the intersection of a problem in
NP
and a problem in co-
NP
. It contains both
NP
and co-
NP
.

Let
I
be an instance of 3SAT. In Jackson
[
1
]
, a monoid
B_{I}
is constructed that lies in
\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1})
if and only if
I
is satisfiable; the monoids
B_{I}
are extensions of
{\bf B}_{2}^{1}
.
(Formally, the reduction in
[
1
]
is from positive NAE3SAT, but 3SAT reduces to positive NAE3SAT so it is simpler in the present case to start at 3SAT.)
Klíma, Kunc, Polák
[
3
]
construct a nilpotent monoid
C_{I}
that lies in
\mathbb{V}({\bf J}_{k})
if and only if
I
is
not
satisfiable.
In both of these results, slightly more can be deduced.

Lemma 2
.

(
Jackson
[
1
, Theorem 7.3]
.
)

If
\mathcal{V}
is a pseudovariety not containing
{\bf B}_{2}^{1}
, then
B_{I}\in\mathcal{V}\vee\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1})
if and only if
I
is a YES instance of 3SAT.

The following is a strengthening of Proposition 6.1 of Klíma, Kunc, Polák
[
3
]
.

Lemma 3
.

For
k\geq 4
, if
\mathcal{V}
is a periodic pseudovariety with index
i\leq k-1
and period
p
, then
C_{I}\in\mathcal{V}\vee\mathbb{V}({\bf J}_{k})
if and only if
I
is a NO instance of 3SAT.

Proof.

Recall that a
scattered subword
of a word
{\bf v}
is any subsequence of
{\bf v}
.
We write
{\bf v}\sim_{k}{\bf w}
if the words
{\bf v}
and
{\bf w}
have the same length-
k
scattered subwords.
It is known that
{\bf J}_{k}\models{\bf v}\approx{\bf w}
if and only if
{\bf v}\sim_{k}{\bf w}
(combining Volkov’s
[
6
, Theorem 2]
with well known results of Simon
[
5
]
; see
[
6
, Proposition 3]
for example).

Let
I
be an instance of 3SAT.
The monoid
C_{I}
is defined as the transition monoid of a constructed finite state automata
\mathcal{A}_{I}
for a language built from
I
.
When
I
is a NO instance, then
C_{I}\in\mathbb{V}({\bf J}_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{V}\vee\mathbb{V}({\bf J}_{k})
by
[
3
, Proposition 6.1]
.
When
I
is a YES instance, the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 6.1]
shows that there is a word
{\bf v}_{I}
with prefix
x^{k-2}
such that
{\bf v}_{I}\sim_{k}x{\bf v}_{I}
but where
{\bf v}_{I}
is accepted by
\mathcal{A}_{I}
and
x{\bf v}_{I}
is not (
x
is the letter
c
in
[
3
]
). It follows that
{\bf J}_{k}\models{\bf v}_{I}\approx x{\bf v}_{I}
but
C_{I}\not\models{\bf v}_{I}\approx x{\bf v}_{I}
, which is why in this situation we have
C_{I}\notin\mathbb{V}({\bf J}_{k})
.
It is easily verified that the same is true if we replace
x{\bf v}_{I}
with
x^{p}{\bf v}_{I}
: non-acceptance by
\mathcal{A}_{I}
follows trivially as the only
x^{k-1}
paths lead to an absorbing non-accepting state (and
x^{p}{\bf v}_{I}
has a prefix
x^{k-2+p}
).
The property
{\bf v}_{I}\sim_{k}x^{p}{\bf v}_{I}
follows for identical reason to the case of
p=1
: there are no new
k
-letter scattered subwords obtained by adding further copies of
x
to the front.
So both
\mathcal{V}
(by virtue of
x^{k-2}\approx x^{k-2+p}
) and
\mathbb{V}({\bf J}_{k})
(by virtue of
{\bf v}_{I}\sim_{k}x^{p}{\bf v}_{I}
) satisfy
{\bf v}_{I}\approx x^{p}{\bf v}_{I}
, which fails on
C_{I}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We have a reduction from the
DP
-complete problem SAT-UNSAT, where instances consist of a pair of instances
(I,J)
of SAT (we use 3SAT, which is equivalent), and we wish to know if
I
is a YES instance and
J
a NO instance of 3SAT. We argue that
B_{I}\times C_{J}\in\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1}\times{\bf J}_{k})
if and only if
(I,J)
is a YES instance of SAT-UNSAT.

For the backward implication, if
I
is a YES instance and
J
a NO instance, then
B_{I}\in\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1})\subseteq\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1}\times{\bf J}_{k})
by Lemma
2
and
C_{J}\in\mathbb{V}({\bf J}_{k})\subseteq\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1}\times{\bf J}_{k})
by Lemma
3
.

For the forward implication, assume either
I
is a NO instance or
I
is a YES instance of 3SAT.
We use that fact that both
B_{I}
and
C_{J}
are contained in the variety of
B_{I}\times C_{J}
.
If
I
is a NO instance, then
B_{I}\notin\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1}\times{\bf J}_{k})
by Lemma
2
because
{\bf J}_{k}
is
\mathscr{J}
-trivial; so
B_{I}\times C_{J}\notin\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1}\times{\bf J}_{k})
.
If
J
is a YES instance, then
C_{J}\notin\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1}\times{\bf J}_{k})
by Lemma
3
as
{\bf B}_{2}^{1}
has index
2
; so
B_{I}\times C_{J}\notin\mathbb{V}({\bf B}_{2}^{1}\times{\bf J}_{k})
.
∎

Remark 4
.

Volkov
[
6
]
has shown that
{\bf J}_{k}
generates the pseudovariety corresponding to
k
-piecewise testable languages (via syntactic monoids), and that this can also be generated by the monoid of reflexive binary relations on a
(k+1)
-element set (equivalently,
(k+1)\times(k+1)
Boolean matrices with
1
on the diagonal), and also by submonoid of upper triangular Boolean matrices with
1
on the diagonal. So each of these can replace
{\bf J}_{k}
in Theorem
1
.

The proof of Theorem
1
does not preclude the possibility that
\textsf{varmem}({\bf B}_{2}^{1}\times{\bf J}_{k})
lies outside
DP
, in some even “harder” class.
Amongst general algebras, it is known that
\textsf{varmem}({\bf A})
can be as hard as 2-
exptime
-complete
[
4
]
, and it is tempting to speculate that hardness beyond
DP
is possible for monoids.
