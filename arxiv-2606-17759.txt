Time integration as filtering: a space–time discretization-aware LES formulation
Syver Døving Agdestein

Abstract
Discretization-aware LES yields an exact expression for the discrete target flux in finite-volume LES by recognizing that a coarse finite difference is a top-hat-filtered exact derivative (the “filter-swap” property). That argument is purely spatial; here we observe that the forward-Euler time difference is itself a (one-sided) top-hat-filtered exact time derivative, and repeat the construction in space–time. The resulting exact discrete flux decomposition extends the spatial one with a single temporal term: a flux-quadrature error that shrinks with the quadrature order of the time integrator. In a Burgers experiment this term grows with the CFL number while the spatial terms do not, and a Smagorinsky closure augmented with its leading order—a Lax–Wendroff-type diffusion—stays accurate at coarse time steps where space-only closures degrade.

Introduction

The turbulence closure problem is usually posed for the
spatially

filtered equations. In the implicit-LES setting the only spatial filter is the
grid (top-hat) filter itself, of width
h>0
, and the residual is dominated
by discretization effects—the
numerical flux and the discrete divergence—rather than by a sub-filter stress
alone
chow-moin
. Discretization-aware LES

agdestein-jcp
addresses this by deriving an
exact
residual
stress, hinging on the

filter-swap
property: the coarse central finite difference

\partial^{h}_{x}u(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(u(x+h/2)-u(x-h/2))/h

equals a top-hat-filtered exact derivative,

\partial^{h}_{x}=\overline{(\cdot)}^{h}\circ\partial_{x},

(1)

where
\overline{(\cdot)}^{h}
is the volume-averaging (top-hat) grid filter of width
h

schumann
;
agdestein-jcp
. This argument is entirely spatial: the time
derivative
\partial_{t}
is kept
exact, and the time integrator is applied only afterwards; the
time-discretization error therefore lives
outside
the exact
residual-stress guarantee.

Filtering in
time
is not in itself new: temporal LES models the
residual stress of an explicitly chosen, smooth causal time filter

pruett
, and the view of the LES equations as the result of two
operators—a smooth filter
and
a discretization operator—goes back to
carati-winckelmans
.
Our observation is different: the forward-Euler time integrator
is
the
(one-sided top-hat) time filter, so the filter-swap is an
exact
identity
and the only new contribution is a single quadrature term—no separate
temporal closure or commutation term arises.

2
From spatial to space–time filter-swap

We consider the scalar conservation law

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}f(u)=0,

(2)

with flux
f
; the experiments use the viscous Burgers flux

f(u)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=u^{2}/2-\nu\,\partial_{x}u
.

\runinhead

Spatial filter-swap.
Let
f^{h}
be a grid-compatible numerical flux. Filtering
(
2
) and absorbing the grid filter into the spatial derivative
through (
1
) gives the spatially discrete, time-continuous
law of
agdestein-jcp
,

\partial_{t}\bar{u}^{h}+\partial^{h}_{x}f(u)=0.

(3)

The grid difference
\partial^{h}_{x}
acts on the
unfiltered
flux
f(u)
:
this is the content of the filter-swap, and it is exact. Replacing the
inaccessible
f(u)
by the numerical flux
f^{h}(\bar{u}^{h})
recovers the usual
semi-discrete finite-volume scheme; the time derivative is still exact.

\runinhead

Temporal filter-swap.
Let
\tau
be the time step. The forward top-hat time filter and the forward
time difference,

\overline{u}^{\tau}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}u(t+s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\partial^{\tau}_{t}u(t)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{u(t+\tau)-u(t)}{\tau}=\overline{\partial_{t}u}^{\,\tau}(t),

(4)

satisfy the temporal filter-swap

\partial^{\tau}_{t}=\overline{(\cdot)}^{\tau}\circ\partial_{t}
exactly—the
temporal analogue of (
1
).
Forward Euler is this one-sided, first-order filter.

\runinhead

Space–time filter-swap.
Applying the space–time filter

\overline{(\cdot)}^{h,\tau}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\overline{(\cdot)}^{\tau}\circ\overline{(\cdot)}^{h}

to (
2
) and absorbing each filter into its own derivative through
(
1
) and (
4
) yields a law that is
discrete in space
and
time,

\partial^{\tau}_{t}\,\bar{u}^{h}+\partial^{h}_{x}\,\overline{f(u)}^{\tau}=0.

(5)

This law is
exact: injecting
\overline{f(u)}^{\tau}
as the flux makes the forward-Euler
scheme reproduce the grid-filtered DNS
\bar{u}^{h}
at the time nodes with zero
residual. The only departure from the spatial law (
3
) is
that the flux is
time-averaged over the step
; the resolved field stays

\bar{u}^{h}
.
In practice
\overline{f(u)}^{\tau}
is replaced by an instantaneous,
step-aware numerical flux

f^{h,\tau}(\bar{u}^{h})\approx\overline{f(u)}^{\tau}
that uses only the
current field but may depend on
\tau
, giving the fully discrete scheme

v^{n+1}_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=v^{n}_{i}-(\tau/h)[f^{h,\tau}_{i+1/2}(v^{n})-f^{h,\tau}_{i-1/2}(v^{n})]

with
v^{n}_{i}\approx\bar{u}^{h}(x_{i},t_{n})
.
The
\tau
-awareness comes through a closure
m^{h,\tau}
as
f^{h,\tau}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f^{h}+m^{h,\tau}
.

3
Decomposition and modeling of the space–time residual flux

The closure target is the
residual flux
—the gap between the exact
space–time flux of (
5
) and the numerical one,

r^{h,\tau}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\overline{f(u)}^{\tau}-f^{h}(\bar{u}^{h}).

(6)

Inserting the intermediate fluxes
f(u)
,
\overline{f(u)}^{h}
, and
f(\bar{u}^{h})

splits it into four errors,

\displaystyle r^{h,\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{h}_{\mathrm{LES}}+r^{h}_{\mathrm{num}}+r^{h}_{\mathrm{div}}+r^{\tau}_{\mathrm{time}},

(7)

\displaystyle r^{h}_{\mathrm{LES}}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=

\displaystyle\overline{f(u)}^{h}-f(\bar{u}^{h}),\qquad r^{h}_{\mathrm{num}}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f(\bar{u}^{h})-f^{h}(\bar{u}^{h}),

\displaystyle r^{h}_{\mathrm{div}}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=

\displaystyle f(u)-\overline{f(u)}^{h},\qquad\;\;\,r^{\tau}_{\mathrm{time}}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\overline{f(u)}^{\tau}-f(u).

The first three are the spatial terms of
agdestein-jcp
:

r^{h}_{\mathrm{LES}}
is the classical LES commutator between grid filtering and
the nonlinear flux;
r^{h}_{\mathrm{num}}
is the numerical-flux error; and

r^{h}_{\mathrm{div}}
corrects

r^{h}_{\mathrm{LES}}
for the
discrete
divergence. The fourth term

r^{\tau}_{\mathrm{time}}
is new: a pure
flux
time-quadrature
error—the step-averaged flux minus its instantaneous
value—that shrinks with the order of the time integrator (forward Euler is
the left-rectangle rule).

\runinhead

A space–time discretization-aware eddy-viscosity model.
In
agdestein-jcp
the three spatial terms are modeled together by a
discretization-aware Smagorinsky
smagorinsky
term
m^{h}
embedded in
the numerical flux, with

f(u)\approx f^{h}(\bar{u}^{h})+m^{h}(\bar{u}^{h})
,

m^{h}(\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=-\,\theta^{2}h^{2}\,|\partial^{h}_{x}\cdot|\,\partial^{h}_{x}\cdot,

(8)

whose single constant
\theta
is tuned by dissipation matching against the
exact flux
f(u)
.
Expanding the
step average and using the conservation law (
2
) gives, for the
convective part of the flux,

r^{\tau}_{\mathrm{time}}(u)=\frac{\tau}{2}\,\partial_{t}f(u)+O(\tau^{2})=-\,\frac{\tau}{2}\,(f^{\prime}(u))^{2}\,\partial_{x}u+O(\tau^{2}),

(9)

a diffusive flux whose effective viscosity
\frac{\tau}{2}(f^{\prime}(u))^{2}
is
non-negative
by construction
—precisely the Lax–Wendroff diffusion
that stabilizes forward-Euler convection
lax-wendroff
. For the
space–time target
\overline{f(u)}^{\tau}

we therefore augment the spatial closure with this term,

m^{h,\tau}(\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=-\,\theta_{h}^{2}h^{2}\,|\partial^{h}_{x}\cdot|\,\partial^{h}_{x}\cdot\,-\,\theta_{\tau}\,\tau\,(f^{\prime}(\cdot))^{2}\,\partial^{h}_{x}\cdot,

(10)

with the theoretical value
\theta_{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}
from
(
9
).

5
Conclusions and outlook

The forward-Euler time difference is a (one-sided) top-hat-filtered exact
time derivative, the temporal analogue of the spatial filter-swap.
Repeating the construction of

agdestein-jcp
in space
and
time turns the fully discrete
forward-Euler finite-volume scheme into an exactly filtered conservation
law, whose residual flux

r^{h,\tau}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\overline{f(u)}^{\tau}-f^{h}(\bar{u}^{h})
is a zero-error
a-posteriori target even at coarse time steps. The time integrator
contributes exactly one new term—a
flux time-quadrature error shrinking with the quadrature order—and no
temporal commutation term. A priori, this term
dominates the residual flux at practical CFL numbers; a posteriori,
a discretization-aware Smagorinsky model augmented with its leading
order—the Lax–Wendroff diffusion, dissipative by construction and linear in

\tau
—keeps the error of a coarse forward-Euler simulation essentially
independent of the CFL number, where step-agnostic closures degrade. Much of
the time-quadrature error is non-dissipative and beyond the reach
of an eddy-viscosity form; machine-learned closures trained on the exact
target
r^{h,\tau}
, implicit and multi-stage integrators, and
three-dimensional Navier–Stokes are natural extensions.
