Galaxy Power Spectrum at Two-Loop Order: Implications for Weak Lensing Surveys and New Physics Preprint: MIT-CTP/6054
Mikhail M. Ivanov

Abstract
We compute the galaxy power spectrum at two-loop order in cosmological perturbation theory (effective field theory, EFT). We derive galaxy bias operators through the fifth order and obtain two-loop renormalization conditions for the their bias coefficients. We compute the two-loop integrals using a renormalization scheme consistent with the CLASS-PT code, allowing for an easy interface of our new computations with standard tools used in the one-loop galaxy power spectrum and bispectrum analyses. We also derive the relevant higher-derivative and stochastic contributions, and implement IR resummation using time-sliced perturbation theory . Having identified the redundant operators, we find that the two-loop galaxy power spectrum requires 21 additional EFT parameters per galaxy sample. We compare our computation with the galaxy-galaxy and galaxy-matter power spectra from the PT Challenge N-body simulation at z = 0.61 z=0.61 and find a per mille-level agreement up to k = 0.85 ​ h k=0.85~h Mpc -1 . We show that even with conservative priors on all EFT parameters, the two-loop model produces an unbiased measurement of the mass fluctuation amplitude σ 8 \sigma_{8} with three times narrower error-bars than the linear theory model. The improvement over the one-loop model is ≃ 40 % \simeq 40\% . This suggests significant gains in the two-loop EFT analyses of galaxy clustering and galaxy–lensing two-point functions (“ 2 × 2 2\times 2 pt”) from CMB lensing maps and imaging surveys like Euclid, LSST, and Roman. In addition, our two-loop computation offers a probe of new physics scenarios that modify the shape of the matter power spectrum on wavenumbers ( 0.4 − 0.8 ) ​ h (0.4-0.8)~h Mpc -1 such as the presence of ultra-light axion dark-matter sub-components with masses m a ∼ 10 − 24 m_{a}\sim 10^{-24} eV.

2
Power counting

An important aspect of EFT is power counting, i.e. rules to estimate the size of various corrections.
These rules allow one to identify all terms relevant at a given
specified precision of the calculation.
The EFT power counting is the simplest for a power-law Universe
Pajer and Zaldarriaga 2013
;
Carrasco et al. 2014b
;
Ivanov 2022
, whose
linear
matter power spectrum is

P_{\rm lin}(k)=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k_{\rm NL}^{3}}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{n}\,.

(1)

In our Universe
n\approx-1.5
around
k\sim 0.2~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
,
and
k_{\rm NL}\simeq 0.45[D(z)]^{-2/(n+3)}~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
is the non-linear scale of cosmological perturbation theory,
where
D(z)
is the growth factor.
In what follows we will always assume that the linear
power spectrum in all expressions
are evaluated at a given fiducial redshift
z
,
and suppress the explicit redshift dependence in
P_{\rm lin}(k)
.

The position space
matter density variance produced by modes in a logarithmic
interval around
k
is

\Delta^{2}_{\rm lin}=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}P_{\rm lin}(k)=\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{n}\,.

(2)

Non-linear terms in matter cosmological
perturbation theory generate loop
corrections to the above linear density variance
Scoccimarro 1997
;
Scoccimarro et al. 1999
;
Bernardeau et al. 2002
.
The non-linear relationship between
the number density of galaxies and the underlying dark matter field
(galaxy bias
Desjacques et al. 2018
) produces additional
loop corrections to the galaxy correlaton functions.
For these deterministic

L
-loop corrections due to both bias or dark matter non-linearities, assuming all bias parameters are

\mathcal{O}(1)
numbers, we can estimate:

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}_{L-\text{loop}}(k)\equiv\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}P_{L-\text{loop}}(k)\sim\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{(1+L)(3+n)}\,.\end{split}

(3)

EFT requires the presence of higher-derivative terms
both in the dark matter non-linearities and bias expansion.
These higher derivative terms (also referred to as counterterme) acting on
L
-loop fields scale as

\begin{split}k^{2m}P_{L-\text{loop}}(k)\to\Delta^{2}_{\mathrm{HD},~mL}\sim\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{(1+L)(n+3)+2m}\,.\end{split}

(4)

Next, the part of the
galaxy density uncorrelated with cosmological
initial conditions
(galaxy stochasticity) produces corrections that
scale as a power-law in
(k/\,k_{\rm NL}\,)^{2}
:

\Delta^{2}_{\rm stoch}=\frac{k^{3}}{\bar{n}_{g}}+\frac{k^{3}}{\bar{n}_{g}}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\rm NL}^{2}}+...

(5)

where
\bar{n}_{g}
is the galaxy number density.
Combining all the above effects we get the following estimate
for the perturbative non-linear density variance of galaxies:

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}\sim&\underbrace{\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{3+n}}_{\rm tree}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{2(3+n)}}_{\rm 1-loop}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{3(3+n)}}_{\rm 2-loop}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{4(3+n)}}_{\rm 3-loop}+\\
&+\underbrace{\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{5+n}}_{k^{2}P_{\rm lin}-\mathrm{ctr}}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{7+n}}_{k^{4}P_{\rm lin}-\mathrm{ctr}}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{2n+8}}_{k^{2}P_{\rm 1-loop}-\mathrm{ctr}}\\
&+\underbrace{\frac{k^{3}}{\bar{n}_{g}}}_{\rm LO~stoch.}+\underbrace{\frac{k^{3}}{\bar{n}_{g}}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\rm NL}^{2}}}_{\rm NLO~stoch.}+\underbrace{\frac{k^{3}}{\bar{n}_{g}}\frac{k^{4}}{k_{\rm NL}^{4}}}_{\rm NNLO~stoch.}+...\,.\end{split}

(6)

For spectroscopic surveys
{\bar{n}}^{-1}_{g}\sim b_{1}^{2}P_{\rm lin}({k_{\text{max}}}=0.2~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1})
, and
b_{1}\sim 1
is the linear galaxy bias.
For imaging surveys the number density is typically much higher
than
[b_{1}^{2}P_{\rm lin}({k_{\text{max}}}=0.2~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1})]^{-1}
,
but we will use
{\bar{n}}^{-1}_{g}\sim b_{1}^{2}P_{\rm lin}({k_{\text{max}}}=0.2~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1})

as a conservative estimate.
This produces the following scaling:

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}({k_{\text{max}}})\sim&\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{1.5}}_{\rm tree}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{3}}_{\rm 1-loop}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{4.5}}_{\rm 2-loop}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{6}}_{\rm 3-loop}\\
&+\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{3.5}}_{k^{2}P_{\rm lin}-\mathrm{ctr}}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{5.5}}_{k^{4}P_{\rm lin}-\mathrm{ctr}}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{5}}_{k^{2}P_{\rm 1-loop}-\mathrm{ctr}}\\
&+\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{1.5}}_{\rm LO~stoch.}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{3.5}}_{\rm NLO~stoch.}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{{k_{\text{max}}}}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{5.5}}_{\rm NNLO~stoch.}+...\,,\end{split}

(7)

The two-loop contributions, defined as all terms
starting with

O(k^{4.5})
and more relevant than
O(k^{6})
-terms, should include:
mode-coupling two-loop contributions
O(k^{4.5})

k^{4}P_{\rm lin}
-counterterms

O(k^{5.5})
,
k^{2}P_{\rm 1-loop}
-like counterterms
O(k^{5})
,
and the NNLO stochastic terms
O(k^{5.5})
.
In what follows we will compute the relevant contributions one by one.

3
Two-loop galaxy bias expansion

In this Section we handle the bias expansion
for the galaxy power spectrum at the two-loop order.
In EFT the galaxy overdensity is expanded as

\delta_{g}(\bm{k})=\sum_{a}b_{a}\,\mathcal{O}_{a}(\bm{k})~\,,

(8)

where each operator
\mathcal{O}_{a}

is build from the underlying degrees of freedom.
At lowest orders these are
the density and velocity scalar potentials.
A standard practice is to use the Eulerian framework for these terms,
i.e. to formulate the bias expansion in terms of
Eulerian operators that depend on the non-linear density
and velocity fields, and then expand them perturbatively
over the linear matter density field using the
Standard Perturbation Theory expansion. At lowest orders this produces
results equivalent to Lagrangian perturbation theory
when the displacement is perturbatively expanded. However, starting from
the fifth order Lagrangian
perturbation theory generates additional unique terms that cannot be
captured by Eulerian fields. These are associated with the
non-locality in time of the bias expansion
Mirbabayi et al. 2015
.
In what follows,
following
Eggemeier et al. 2018
we will
refer to
the Eulerian bias operators as “local evolution” (LE) operators,
and to Lagrangian bias operators are “non-local evolution” operators.

While there are efficient ways to generate the complete sets of operators
both in Eulerian and Lagrangian frameworks
Desjacques et al. 2018
,
in what follows we will adopt a hybrid approach which uses the Eulerian
fields for the LE operators and the Lagrangian fields for the NLE
operators. While this approach creates some asymmetry
in the treatment of bias operators, it has a great advantage of
allowing us to connect our two-loop power
spectrum results with the commonly used Eulerian
one-loop power spectrum and bispectrum
computations. In particular, this way our basis of bias operators
will easily map onto the one used in standard Eulerian
codes such as
CLASS-PT

Chudaykin et al. 2020
.

In Lagrangian perturbation theory (LPT), the displacement field

\bm{\Psi}
is expanded order by order in linear density field
\delta_{L}
:

\bm{\Psi}(\bm{q})=\bm{\Psi}^{(1)}(\bm{q})+\bm{\Psi}^{(2)}(\bm{q})+\bm{\Psi}^{(3)}(\bm{q})+\bm{\Psi}^{(4)}(\bm{q})+\cdots

(9)

and each
\bm{\Psi}^{(n)}
can be obtained by iterating the equation
of motion for this field. Using the scalar-vector decomposition,
the displacement field at a given perturbative order
(n)

can be written as

\Psi_{i}^{(n)}=-\partial_{i}\varphi^{(n)}+(\nabla\times\vec{A}^{(n)})_{i}\,.

(10)

In what follows we adopt a convention that all derivatives
acting on LPT potentials are the w.r.t. to a Lagrangian
coordinate
\bm{q}
, i.e.
\partial_{i}\varphi^{(n)}\equiv\partial\varphi^{(n)}/\partial q_{i}
.
An economic way to summarize the statement about the time
evolution of the bias expansion is to
use the deformation tensor
\partial_{i}\Psi_{j}
to build the bias expansion
Mirbabayi et al. 2015
;
Desjacques et al. 2018
, which we adopt in our treatment of NLE terms.

The operators in the bias expansion
should satisfy symmetries of the problem: the equivalence
principle and the rotational symmetry. The first one dictates
that the galaxy bias
expansion should be built from operators that have at least two
spatial
derivatives acting on the relevant potentials, which is naturally
accounted for when using the deformation tensor.
Rotational invariance
implies that such operators should be Euclidean scalars.

b_{1}^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)+2b_{1}P_{\rm lin}(k)\left(\frac{b_{3}}{2}+\frac{34b_{2}}{21}-\frac{4\gamma_{2}^{\times}}{3}\right)\sigma^{2}\equiv(b_{1}+\delta b_{1}\sigma^{2})^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)\equiv[b^{(R)}_{1}]^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)+O(\sigma^{4})\,,

(24)

where
b_{1}^{(R)}
is the finite renormalized bias,
which should be contrasted with the infinite bare bias
b_{1}
which absorbs
the divergent loop contribution
\delta b_{1}
:

\delta b_{1}\equiv\frac{b_{3}}{2}+\frac{34b_{2}}{21}-\frac{4\gamma_{2}^{\times}}{3}\,.

(25)

The above equation is a
b_{1}
renormaliation condition to order

\sigma^{2}
, assuming in what follows
that
\delta b_{\mathcal{O}}\sigma^{2}
is a loop correction that has to be
exactly cancelled by the bare bias parameter
\delta b_{\mathcal{O}}
to yield
the renormalized bias parameter
b^{(R)}_{\mathcal{O}}

b_{\mathcal{O}}+\delta b_{\mathcal{O}}\sigma^{2}=b^{(R)}_{\mathcal{O}}\Big|_{\sigma^{2}}\,.

(26)

Importantly, the LE operators and the shifted
\delta^{2}

do not have finite, calculable loop corrections. This is a consequence
of using the Galileon bias. In contrast, the NLE operator
\gamma_{21}
has a
convergent finite contribution. The reason this contribution does not vanish
is that its complicated momentum structure implies the terms like
1/|\bm{k}-\bm{q}|^{2}

in the integrand, which cannot be eliminated by angular integration.
Similar terms stem from the shifted
\gamma_{2}
kernel.

3.2
Fourth order bias

Moving to the fourth order we have to take into account that the LPT
displacement ceases to be purely potential at the cubic order, see eq. (
10
).
Explicitly, one has
Eggemeier et al. 2018
;
Matsubara 2008
;
Matsubara 2015

\bm{\Psi}^{(3)}(\bm{q},\tau)=D_{3}^{(a)}\bm{\Psi}^{(3a)}+D_{3}^{(a)}\bm{\Psi}^{(3b)}+D_{3}^{(b)}\bm{\Psi}^{(3b)}+D_{3}^{(c)}\bm{\Psi}^{(3c)}\,,

(27)

where (a,b) are scalar components,
whilst (c) is the vector component
\bm{\Psi}^{(3,c)}=\nabla\times\vec{A}_{3}

the LPT solution for the growth factors in the EdS approximation reads

D_{3}^{(a)}/D^{3}=1/18
,
D_{3}^{(b)}/D^{3}=5/42
,
D_{3}^{(c)}/D^{3}=1/14

The scalar and vector components above are sourced by the low order scalar potentials:

\begin{split}&\nabla\cdot\bm{\Psi}^{(3a)}=\nabla^{2}\varphi_{3}^{(a)}=-\mathcal{G}_{3}(\varphi_{1})\,,\\
&\nabla\cdot\bm{\Psi}^{(3b)}=\nabla^{2}\varphi_{3}^{(b)}=-\mathcal{G}_{2}(\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1})\,,\\
&\nabla\times\bm{\Psi}^{(3,c)}=-\nabla^{2}\vec{A}_{3}=\hat{\vec{e}}_{i}\epsilon_{ijk}(\nabla_{jl}\varphi_{1})(\nabla_{kl}\varphi_{2})\,.\end{split}

We define now the Lagrangian kernel in Fourier space:

\bm{\Psi}^{(n)}=iD^{n}\int_{\bm{q}_{1}...\bm{q}_{n}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-\bm{q}_{1...n})\vec{L}_{n}(\bm{q}_{1},...,\bm{q}_{n})\delta_{0}(\bm{q}_{1})...\delta_{0}(\bm{q}_{L})\,,

and curl parts
S_{n}\equiv{\bm{k}}\cdot\vec{L}_{n}

\vec{T}_{n}=-{\bm{k}}\times\vec{L}_{n}

n=3

S_{3}(\bm{q}_{j},\bm{q}_{k},\bm{q}_{l})\equiv\frac{1}{18}\left(-\mathcal{L}(\bm{q}_{j},\bm{q}_{k},\bm{q}_{l})+\frac{15}{7}K_{\gamma_{21}}^{(3)}(\bm{q}_{j},\bm{q}_{k},\bm{q}_{l})\right)\,,

(31)

While the vector potential does not directly contribute
to the cubic LPT solution for
\delta

Matsubara 2008

Matsubara 2015

We introduce now Galileon operators built

(
M^{(n)}_{ij}\equiv\partial_{j}\Psi_{i}^{(n)}

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{\Psi}^{(n)},\bm{\Psi}^{(m)})&\equiv\text{Tr}(M^{(n)}M^{(m)})-\text{Tr}(M^{(m)})\text{Tr}(M^{(n)})=(\partial_{i}\Psi_{j}^{(n)})(\partial_{j}\Psi_{i}^{(m)})-(\partial_{i}\Psi_{i}^{(n)})(\partial_{j}\Psi_{j}^{(m)})\,,\,\\

Since
\vec{A}^{(1)}=\vec{A}^{(2)}=0

At the 4th order we get 8 new contributions from four LE and four new NLE operators:

Fourier space kernel:

3.4
Evolution and advection of operators

The LPT operators we have used are subject to advection,
which produces higher order contributions. Let us remind
the reader that
in our approach all LE operators built from non-linear
Eulerian velocity or density
potentials encode the evolution of cosmological fields
from the SPT expansion for the density and velocity
fields from the non-linear perfect fluid equations.
This is implemented by plugging the SPT formulas

\delta=\delta_{L}+\int F_{2}\delta_{L}^{2}+...

and
\theta=\delta_{L}+\int G_{2}\delta_{L}^{2}+...

in the relevant LE operators.

For the NLE Lagrangian operators, we implement an explicit LPT advection.

3.6
Bias Renormalization and Redundant Operators

The two-loop power spectrum contains
divergent contributions, which have to be
renormalized by the low order bias parameters.
In this Section we will show how this happens
and explicitly derive new renormalization conditions
relevant at the two-loop order.
In addition, the systematic renormalization
of bias allows us to remove some of the
contributions which appear trivial, or
redundant at the two-loop order.

The two-loop redundancies and renormalization
conditions can be seen as a natural
generalization of the one-loop renormalization
conditions. Recall that at one-loop order
the LE cubic operators with coefficients

b_{3},\gamma_{3}
, and
\gamma_{2}
(from the SPT evolution) are redundant,
i.e. their
P_{13}
diagrams produce a pure renormalization
of
b_{1}
, c.f. Eq. (
25
).
These operators do not have any finite contributions.
The situation is somewhat more interesting at the two-loop order. There we also have some purely redundant operators, but in addition to them
we have non-redundant operators that generate
divergences in the UV. The renormalization of these
contributions is more subtle as it has to preserve
the finite parts. The second important
moment is the presence of two UV domains corresponding
to UV limits
p\gg k
and
q\gg k
, where
p,q

are loop momenta and
k
is the “on-shell” external momentum. This gives rise
to two distinct limits: double-hard
p\sim q\gg k
and
single-hard, where only one of the loop momentum
is hard, while the other is allowed to take
arbitrary values. We will develop a renormalization
procedure that removes UV singularities present
both in single-hard and double-hard limits.

If we want to be consistent
with the one-loop results coded up in standard software,
like the
CLASS-PT
code, we have to use a scheme that
produces convergent contributions in the limit
\Lambda\to\infty
.

CLASS-PT
is based on FFTLog, which implements
dimensional regularization. In this scheme all divergent
integrals are set to zero, and all convergent integrals
are evaluated up to
\Lambda=\infty
. If the two-loop
integrals are evaluated up to a finite cutoff
\Lambda_{\rm 2-loop}
, the renormalization
conditions for the low order bias parameters at one and two-loop orders
will be different, in which case one cannot easily relate the one-loop
and two-loop results. One approach is to derive the appropriate
renormalization group equations for bias parameters
Bakx et al. 2025a
,
and evolve the two-loop bias parameters to
\Lambda=\infty
, or equivalently evolve the one-loop bias parameters to
\Lambda_{\rm 2-loop}
.
Alternatively, one can remove all the divergent contributions
at the level of the two-loop integrands and then evaluate the remanining
finite pieces over the entire loop momentum range. This way the bias
parameters at one-loop and two-loop order will be the same by construction.
We will adopt this approach in our work. Specifically, we shall use
the Bogoliubov-Parasiuk-Hepp-Zimmermann (BPHZ) renormalization scheme
Bogoliubov and Parasiuk 1957
;
Hepp 1966

and systematically subtract divergences directly in momentum space integrals.
Let us discuss now the renormalization of relevant loop contributions one-by-one.

P_{33,\mathrm{II}}
renormalization.

We start by considering the renormalization
of
P_{33,\mathrm{II}}
. This diagram has a very simple topology:
a product of two one-loop diagrams. As a result, the UV-divergences are trivially factorized: the
double-hard domain is simply a product of two
single-hard loops domains from the relevant
loop momenta. Each individual one-loop diagram
is renormalized by the one-loop renormalization
condition (
25
). Let us demonstrate
that this condition holds for
P_{33,\mathrm{II}}
as well.
Focusing on the UV limits of the relevant diagrams we get:

\begin{split}P_{33,\mathrm{II}}(k)&=9P_{\rm lin}(k)\left[\frac{P_{13}}{6b_{1}P_{\rm lin}(k)}\right]^{2}=9P_{\rm lin}(k)\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{b_{3}}{2}+\frac{34b_{2}}{21}-\frac{4\gamma_{2}^{\times}}{3}\right)\sigma^{2}+\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}\right]^{2}\\
&=9\,P_{\rm lin}(k)\;\Big[\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}+\frac{\delta b_{1}}{3}\sigma^{2}\,\Big]^{2}=9\,P_{\rm lin}(k)\;\Big[\big(\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}\big)^{2}+\frac{2\,\delta b_{1}}{3}\sigma^{2}\,\;\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}+\frac{(\delta b_{1})^{2}\sigma^{4}}{9}\,\Big]\,.\end{split}

(63)

In the above formula
O(\delta b_{1}\sigma^{2})
,
O(\delta b_{1}^{2}\sigma^{4})

are the single-hard and double-hard
contributions, respectively.
The above loop is renormalized by the
b_{1}
counterterm contribution with the one-loop renormalization condition. To see this we add all the relevant pieces together:

\begin{split}&P_{33,\mathrm{I}}+P_{13}+P_{11}\\
&=9P_{\rm lin}(k)(\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}\big)^{2}+6\delta b_{1}\sigma^{2}\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}(k)P_{\rm lin}(k)+(\delta b_{1}\sigma^{2})^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)+6b_{1}\left(\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}(k)+\frac{\delta b_{1}}{3}\sigma^{2}\right)P_{\rm lin}(k)+b_{1}^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)\\
&=(b_{1}+\delta b_{1}\sigma^{2})^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)+2(b_{1}+\delta b_{1}\sigma^{2})P_{\rm lin}(k)\sigma^{\rm ren}_{13}+9P_{\rm lin}(k)(\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}\big)^{2}\\
&=(b_{1}^{[R]})^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)+2b_{1}^{[R]}\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}(k)+9P_{\rm lin}(k)(\sigma_{13}^{\mathrm{ren}}\big)^{2}\,,\end{split}

(64)

where we emphasize that the above equation
is exact, i.e.
b_{1}

absorbs the all divergences through
O(\sigma^{4})

from the
P_{33,\mathrm{II}}
diagram. Hence,
the
b_{1}
renormalization to order
O(\sigma^{2})

is systematic in the Bogoliubov sense:
a complex 1-particle-reducible diagram
P_{33,\mathrm{I}}

with nested divergences are renormalized
by the same counterterm as the corresponding
1-particle-irreducible sub-diagrams.

Let us note that the other loop integral

P_{33,\mathrm{I}}
does not produce any non-trivial
renormalization conditions for bias operators.
In contrast to
P_{33,\mathrm{II}}
,
P_{33,\mathrm{I}}

produces a Taylor expansion in
k^{2}
in the UV limit.
These UV-sensitive contributions
are
renormalized by the stochastic counterterms.

To study the renormalization

One can find that the hard limit projects onto the 2nd-order basis:

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{3}}{2}+\frac{34b_{2}}{21}-\frac{4\gamma_{2}^{\times}}{3}\,,

(69a)

\displaystyle\delta b_{2}

P_{15}
renormalization.

The analysis of

3.7
IR-resummation

The perturbative in
\delta_{L}
expansion that we have used
so far does not converge well for the baryon acoustic
oscillations (BAO) in the
linear matter power spectrum. The convergence can be significantly
improved by resumming the displacement contributions enhanced
in the IR domains. This procedure of improving the modeling of the
non-linear evolution of the BAO is known as IR resummation
Crocce and Scoccimarro 2006
;
Crocce and Scoccimarro 2008
;
Senatore and Zaldarriaga 2015
;
Baldauf et al. 2015a
;
Vlah et al. 2016
;
Blas et al. 2016a
.
In this work we account for it using the formalism of time-sliced
perturbation theory (TSPT)
Blas et al. 2016a
;
Blas et al. 2016b
;
Ivanov and Sibiryakov 2018
;
Vasudevan et al. 2019
. Specifically, we apply the “wiggly-smooth”
decomposition to the linear power spectrum
P_{\rm lin}=P_{s}+P_{w}
and resumm the IR-enhanced
contributions in the perturbative series.
2
2

2

As discussed in
Ivanov et al. 2024a
,
IR-resummmation within TSPT, in principle, can be carried out even without
explicitly assuming a “wiggly-smooth” decomposition.
Previous works
Blas et al. 2016b
;
Ivanov and Sibiryakov 2018
have carried out this program in very general settings that apply to the two-loop galaxy power spectrum as well.
The formulas derived in
Blas et al. 2016b
;
Ivanov and Sibiryakov 2018
are applicable to the two-loop galaxy power
spectrum provided that the Eulerian bias expansion
\delta_{g}[\delta]

does not feature any IR-enhancements, which is the case by construction
because these enhancements would violate the equivalence principle.
The only caveat here is that the expansion we are using is hybrid
Eulerian-Lagrangian, so it included Lagrangian operators as well.
However, the Lagrangian bias
is manifestly Galilean-invariant since it is formulated in terms of Lagrangian
coordinates, and hence it does not violate the assumptions of the general
derivations of
Blas et al. 2016b
;
Ivanov and Sibiryakov 2018
. Alternatively, one can explicitly invert the SPT expansion
\delta=\delta_{L}+\int F_{2}\delta_{L}^{2}+...

in perturbation theory
as
\delta_{L}=\delta+\int F^{\prime}_{2}\delta^{2}
and plug them into our Lagrangian
bias operators and confirm that their Eulerian analogs are manifestly
IR safe. After that, it is straightforward to
repeat the computations of
Blas et al. 2016b
;
Ivanov and Sibiryakov 2018
and arrive
at the following answer for the IR-resummed total galaxy power spectrum at
the two-loop order:

\begin{split}&P^{\mathrm{2-loop,~tot}}_{gg}\Big|_{\rm IR-res}=b_{1}^{2}\left[P_{s}+e^{-\mathcal{S}}P_{w}\left(1+\mathcal{S}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{S}^{2}\right)\right]+\Delta P^{\mathrm{1-loop}}_{gg}[P_{s}+e^{-\mathcal{S}}P_{w}(1+\mathcal{S})]+\Delta P^{\mathrm{2-loop}}_{gg}[P_{s}+e^{-\mathcal{S}}P_{w}]\,,\end{split}

(79)

where

S\equiv\frac{k^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\Lambda_{\rm IR}}dq~P_{\rm lin}(q)\left[1-j_{0}(qr_{s})+2j_{2}(qr_{s})\right]\,,

(80)

and
r_{s}
is the comoving sound horizon at decoupling
and
j_{\ell}(x)

is the spherical Bessel functions of order
\ell
,
and the IR cutoff
\Lambda_{\rm IR}=0.2~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
.

,
\gamma_{221}
,
\gamma_{311}
,

\gamma_{41}
, and for the rest of relevant P15 terms in post-processing.
This guarantees the
k\to 0
asymptotic of all P15 terms starts at

k^{4}P_{\rm lin}(k)
. For the matter-matter (
b_{1}^{2}
) contribution we implement IR-safe integrands
following
Carrasco et al. 2014a
. For the remaining terms the
spurious IR enhancements are much milder,
so their computation with high precision is enough
to resolve the physical remainders left after the cancellation
of the IR-enhanced terms.

We implement numerical integration using the Cuba library in Julia, employing
the VEGAS adaptive Monte Carlo algorithm for all two-loop integrals.
All operator kernels are evaluated using exact
symmetrized SPT recursion relations. The loop momenta are parameterized following
Blas et al. 2014
.
Each
loop momenta in five-dimensional integrals are sampled logarithmically over the range
q\in[10^{-4},100]\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}
.
The computation is parallelized across
k
-points using Julia’s distributed
framework. We target a relative tolerance of
10^{-3}
with up to
5\times 10^{7}
integrand evaluations per
k
-point, with increased sampling at low
k

where constant subtraction leads to large cancellations.
The final result comprises 46 shape functions
P^{ij}(k)
associated
with 17 bias parameters, evaluated on a grid of 96 wavenumbers spanning
k\in[10^{-3},1]\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}
.

Our numerical results are shown in Fig. (
3
).
As one can see, the two-loop
shapes are fairly degenerate on large scales,
but in general have distinctive scale-dependence
around
k\sim 0.5~h/\mathrm{Mpc}

where
they start to contribute significantly
to the total power spectrum.

4
Counterterms and Stochasticity

In this Section we work out the new higher-derivative and stochastic contributions.

4.1
Higher-derivative bias

We start with the higher-derivative
operators. The two-loop order power counting
suggests that we need to
consider terms of the order
O(k^{4}\delta)
,

O(k^{2}\delta^{2})
and
O(k^{2}\delta^{3})
.
Let us consider them one-by-one.
In what follows,
we use the one-loop integral basis of Ref.
Chudaykin et al. 2020
.
These include the following
P_{22}
-type integrals:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}F_{2}(\bm{q},{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(82a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}\mathcal{K}(\bm{q},{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q})\,F_{2}(\bm{q},{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(82b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}\delta^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(82c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}\mathcal{K}(\bm{q},{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(82d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}\mathcal{K}^{2}(\bm{q},{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(82e)

and a single
P_{13}
-type NLE integral:

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\equiv 4\,P_{\rm lin}(k)\!\int_{\bm{q}}\mathcal{K}(\bm{q},{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q})\,F_{2}({\bm{k}},-\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,.

(83)

First, we have a singe new operator at
O(k^{4}\delta)
in addition to the usual higher-derivative bias:

\delta_{g}\big|_{\nabla^{2}\delta}+\delta_{g}\big|_{\nabla^{4}\delta}=\beta_{1}\nabla^{2}\delta+\tilde{\beta}_{1}\nabla^{4}\delta\,,

(84)

where
\beta_{1}\equiv b_{1}c_{s}+b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}
(
c_{s}
is the dark matter sound speed and
b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}
is the pure higher-derivative bias parameter).
At two loop order, the above leading
higher derivative bias term

\delta_{g}\big|_{\nabla^{2}\delta}

has to be expanded to second and third orders
in SPT, which gives rise to the following contribution:

P_{gg}\Big|_{\nabla^{n}\delta-\mathrm{like}}=-2b_{1}\beta_{1}k^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)-2\,b_{1}\,\beta_{1}\,k^{2}\,P_{\rm 1\text{-}loop}^{\rm SPT}(k)+2b_{1}\tilde{\beta}_{1}k^{4}P_{\rm lin}(k)-\beta_{1}\,k^{2}\Big[b_{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}}+2\,b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\Big]\,.

(85)

The quadratic higher-derivative operators

O(k^{2}\delta^{2})
have already been
studied in detail in context of the one-loop
galaxy bispectrum
Eggemeier et al. 2018
;
Philcox et al. 2022
;
Bakx et al. 2025c
.
The four non-redundant
operators at this order are:

\delta_{g}\big|_{\nabla^{2}\delta^{2}}=\beta_{2,1}\,\nabla^{2}\delta^{2}+\beta_{2,2}\,\nabla_{i}\delta\nabla^{i}\delta+\beta_{2,3}\,\nabla^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}(\Phi_{v})+\beta_{2,4}\,\mathcal{G}_{2}(\nabla_{i}\Phi_{v},\nabla_{i}\Phi_{v})\,.

(86)

In Fourier space this produces the kernel:

K_{\rm h.d.}^{(2)}=-\beta_{2,1}\,k^{2}-\beta_{2,2}\,(\bm{q}_{1}\!\cdot\!\bm{q}_{2})-\beta_{2,3}\,k^{2}\mathcal{K}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2})-\beta_{2,4}\,(\bm{q}_{1}\!\cdot\!\bm{q}_{2})\,\mathcal{K}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2})\,,

(87)

These corrections generate P22-like integrals when contracted with the bias 2nd order
kernel,

P_{gg}\Big|_{k^{2}P_{22}-\mathrm{like}}=4\int_{\bm{q}}K_{g}^{(2)}({\bm{k}}-\bm{q},\bm{q})K_{\rm h.d.}^{(2)}(-{\bm{k}}+\bm{q},-\bm{q})P_{\rm lin}(q)P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}-\bm{q}|)~\,.

(88)

P_{gg}\Big|_{k^{2}P_{22}-\mathrm{like}}

consists of 12 terms, but many of the loop
integrals can be reduced to the standard bias

P_{22}
integrals (
82
).
To see this, it is convenient
to switch to a new basis of
O(k^{2}\delta^{2})
counterterms
Chen et al. 2026
:

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=k^{2}\,,

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=k^{2}\,\mathcal{K}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2})\,,

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(\bm{q}_{1}\cdot\bm{q}_{2})}{2}\Big(\tfrac{{\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{q}_{2}}{q_{2}^{2}}+\tfrac{{\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{q}_{1}}{q_{1}^{2}}\Big),

\displaystyle E_{4}

\displaystyle=\bm{q}_{1}\cdot\bm{q}_{2}\,.

(89)

The
counterterms in the
\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},E_{4}\}
basis read

\delta_{g}^{(2)}\big|_{\rm HD}=-c_{1}\,E_{1}-c_{2}\,E_{2}+c_{4}\,E_{3}-c_{3}\,E_{4}\,,

(90)

with

c_{1}=\beta_{2,1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{2,4}\,,\quad c_{2}=\beta_{2,3}+\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{2,4}\,,\quad c_{3}=\beta_{2,2}\,,\quad c_{4}=\beta_{2,4}\,.

(91)

The kernels
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
map
onto precomputed
\mathcal{I}
integrals (
82
) multiplied by

k^{2}
. However,

E_{3}
and
E_{4}
have
non-trivial loop-momentum dependence that cannot be reduced to that of the one-loop bias basis.
These produce new
integrals defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{F_{2}}^{E_{3}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}\!E_{3}({\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q},\bm{q})\,F_{2}({\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q},\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(92a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\delta^{2}}^{E_{3}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}\!E_{3}({\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q},\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(92b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}^{E_{3}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}\!E_{3}({\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q},\bm{q})\,\mathcal{K}({\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q},\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(92c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{E_{4}}_{F_{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}\!({\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{q}-q^{2})\,F_{2}({\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q},\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(92d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{E_{4}}_{\delta^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}\!({\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{q}-q^{2})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,,

(92e)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{E_{4}}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\!\int_{\bm{q}}\!({\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{q}-q^{2})\,\mathcal{K}({\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q},\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(|{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q}|)\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,.

(92f)

With some abuse of language, we will call these
terms
“two-loop counterterm,”
even though their complexity is actually that
of the one-loop integrals.

At the cubic order we find eleven non-trivial
new operators:

\begin{split}\delta_{g}\big|_{\nabla^{2}\delta^{3}}=\;&\beta_{3,1}\;\delta^{2}\nabla^{2}\delta+\beta_{3,2}\;\delta\,(\bm{\nabla}\delta)^{2}\\[4.0pt]
+\;&\beta_{3,3}\;(\nabla^{2}\delta)\,\mathcal{G}_{2}+\beta_{3,4}\;\delta\,\nabla^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}+\beta_{3,5}\;\delta\,\mathcal{G}_{2}(\nabla_{i}\Phi_{v},\nabla_{i}\Phi_{v})+\beta_{3,6}\;(\nabla_{i}\delta)(\nabla_{i}\mathcal{G}_{2})\\[4.0pt]
+\;&\beta_{3,7}\;\nabla^{2}\mathcal{G}_{3}(\Phi_{v})+\beta_{3,8}\;\mathcal{G}_{3}(\nabla_{i}\Phi_{v},\nabla_{i}\Phi_{v},\Phi_{v})\\[4.0pt]
+\;&\beta_{3,9}\;\nabla^{2}\!\big[\mathcal{G}_{2}(\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1})\big]+\beta_{3,10}\;\mathcal{G}_{2}(\nabla_{i}\varphi_{2},\nabla_{i}\varphi_{1})+\beta_{3,11}\;\mathcal{G}_{2}(\nabla^{2}\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1})\\[4.0pt]
+\;&2\beta_{2,3}\;\nabla^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}(\Phi_{v}^{(2)},\Phi_{v}^{(1)})+2\beta_{2,4}\;\mathcal{G}_{2}(\nabla_{i}\Phi_{v}^{(2)},\nabla_{i}\Phi_{v}^{(1)})\,,\end{split}

(93)

where the last two terms are the non-trivially contributing SPT-evolved quadratic operators.
When contracted with linear bias term

b_{1}\delta
, the above terms will generate the
P13-type integrals,

P_{gg}\Big|_{k^{2}P_{13}-\mathrm{like}}=6b_{1}P_{\rm lin}(k)\int_{\bm{q}}K_{\rm h.d.}^{(3)}({\bm{k}},\bm{q},-\bm{q})P_{\rm lin}(q)+6K^{(1)}_{\rm h.d.}P_{\rm lin}(k)\int_{\bm{q}}K_{g}^{(3)}({\bm{k}},\bm{q},-\bm{q})P_{\rm lin}(q)~\,,

(94)

where
K^{(1)}_{\rm h.d.}\equiv-\beta_{1}k^{2}
.
Due to the specific momentum configuration
in this P13-like integral
many of the above terms turn out to be redundant.
This holds true for all the first eight LE-type
higher derivative contributions above,
and also for the
\mathcal{G}_{2}(\nabla^{2}\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1})
operator.
For LE operators, the mechanism is the same as in the
case of the cubic LE bias contributions: they
produce purely divergent integrals which are
absorbed through the renormalization mechanism
either into
b_{1}
or the leading order higher derivative
bias
b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}

depending on whether the relevant integral scales as
k^{0}
or
k^{2}
.
The situation is a bit more interesting
for
\mathcal{G}_{2}(\nabla^{2}\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1})
.
It is a genuinely non-local operator
but its non-locality is canceled
by the Laplacian in
\nabla^{2}\varphi_{2}
,
which yields a redundant LE integral.
All in all, this leaves us with only two
non-trivial
P_{13}
-like integrals, one of which
is simply
k^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}
, and one is a new
integral:

\mathcal{F}^{E_{4}}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\equiv 4\,P_{\rm lin}(k)\!\int_{\bm{q}}\!(q^{2}-{\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{q})\,\mathcal{K}(\bm{q},{\bm{k}}\!-\!\bm{q})\,\mathcal{K}({\bm{k}},\bm{q})\,P_{\rm lin}(q)\,.

(95)

Since the complexity of the
new seven two-loop counterterm
integrals is the same as of the one-loop
EFT bias contributions,
and hence they can be easily computed with
the
FFTLog technique
Simonovic et al. 2018
.

Using the known one-loop bias templates and the seven genuinely new two-loop counterterm shapes, the
total higher-derivative contribution can be written as

\Delta P_{gg}^{\rm HD}(k)=\sum_{i=0}^{7}c_{i}\;P^{\rm HD}_{i}(k)

(96)

where
c_{0}=\beta_{1}=b_{1}c_{s}+b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}
and
c_{1,...,7}
are 7 new two-loop counterterm parameters, together multiplying the basis shapes

\displaystyle P^{\rm HD}_{0}(k)

\displaystyle=-2b_{1}\,k^{2}\,P_{\rm 1\text{-}loop}^{\rm SPT}(k)-k^{2}\Big[b_{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}}+2b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}+\tfrac{1}{5}\big(6b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}-7\gamma_{21}\big)\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\Big]\,,

(97a)

\displaystyle P^{\rm HD}_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=-k^{2}\Big[2b_{1}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}}+b_{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}\delta^{2}}+2b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}}\Big]\,,

(97b)

\displaystyle P^{\rm HD}_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=-k^{2}\Big[2b_{1}\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}+b_{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}}+2b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}}+2b_{1}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\Big]\,,

(97c)

\displaystyle P^{\rm HD}_{3}(k)

\displaystyle=-2b_{1}\,\mathcal{J}^{E_{4}}_{F_{2}}-b_{2}\,\mathcal{J}^{E_{4}}_{\delta^{2}}-2b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{J}^{E_{4}}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,,

(97d)

\displaystyle P^{\rm HD}_{4}(k)

\displaystyle=2b_{1}\,\mathcal{J}_{F_{2}}^{E_{3}}+b_{2}\,\mathcal{J}_{\delta^{2}}^{E_{3}}+2b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}^{E_{3}}+b_{1}\,k^{2}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}+\tfrac{10}{7}\,b_{1}\,\mathcal{F}^{E_{4}}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,,

(97e)

\displaystyle P^{\rm HD}_{5}(k)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{7}{5}\,b_{1}\,k^{2}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,,

(97f)

\displaystyle P^{\rm HD}_{6}(k)

\displaystyle=b_{1}\,\mathcal{F}^{E_{4}}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,.

(97g)

\displaystyle P^{\rm HD}_{7}(k)

\displaystyle=2b_{1}\,k^{4}P_{\rm lin}(k)\,,

(97h)

Note that all contributions above, including
P_{0}^{\rm HD}
,
are IR-safe.
The counterterms above
computed for our fiducial PT Challenge cosmology

in these contributions as in (

following

.

4.2
Galaxy-matter higher-derivative contributions

To obtain the galaxy-matter cross-spectrum
counterterms we use the minimal set
of two-loop dark matter counterterms
Chen et al. 2026
(see also
Baldauf et al. 2015b
):

\delta_{m}^{\rm ctr}=-c_{s}k^{2}\delta+\tilde{c}_{s}k^{4}\delta+\sum_{n=1}^{3}e_{n}\int_{\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-\bm{q}_{12})\,E_{n}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2})\,\delta_{L}(\bm{q}_{1})\delta_{L}(\bm{q}_{2})\,,

(98)

where
\delta
in the “sound speed” operator
has to be expanded to higher order in SPT. Contracting the above
expression with the galaxy density we get:

\displaystyle\Delta P_{gm}^{\rm HD}

\displaystyle=\tilde{c}_{s}k^{4}P_{\rm lin}(k)-(\beta_{\nabla^{2}\delta}+2b_{1}c_{s})\,k^{2}\,P_{\rm 1\text{-}loop}^{\rm SPT}-\;c_{s}k^{2}\Big[\tfrac{b_{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}}+b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\Big]

(99a)

\displaystyle-\;(c_{1}-e_{1}b_{1})\,k^{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}}-(c_{2}-e_{2}b_{1})\,k^{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}

(99b)

\displaystyle+\;e_{1}k^{2}\Big[\tfrac{b_{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}\delta^{2}}+b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}}\Big]+e_{2}k^{2}\Big[\tfrac{b_{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{I}_{\delta^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}}+b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}}\Big]

(99c)

\displaystyle+\;(c_{4}+e_{3}b_{1})\,\mathcal{J}_{F_{2}}^{E_{3}}+e_{3}\Big[\tfrac{b_{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{J}_{\delta^{2}}^{E_{3}}+b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}^{E_{3}}\Big]-\;c_{3}\,\mathcal{J}_{F_{2}}^{E_{4}}

(99d)

\displaystyle+\;\Big(\!-c_{2}+\tfrac{c_{4}}{2}-\tfrac{7c_{5}}{10}-\tfrac{c_{s}}{10}(6b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\!-\!7\gamma_{21})\Big)k^{2}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}+\Big(\tfrac{5c_{4}}{7}+\tfrac{c_{6}}{2}\Big)\,\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}^{E_{4}}\,.

(99e)

Importantly, the cross-spectrum depends
only on the galaxy and matter
counterterms that we have encountered above,
i.e. it does not require
any free parameters.

4.3
Stochastic terms

The stochastic terms come from the auto-spectrum
of the stochastic density component
\epsilon_{g}
.
In EFT the spectrum is a power-law series:

\Delta P_{gg}^{\rm stoch}=\langle\epsilon_{g}({\bm{k}})\epsilon_{g}({\bm{k}})\rangle^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\bar{n}_{g}}\left(1+P_{\rm shot}+a_{0}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm stoch}}\right)^{2}+a_{4}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm stoch}}\right)^{4}+\cdots\right)\,,

(100)

where we inserted the galaxy number density

\bar{n}
and non-local stochasticity scale

k_{\rm stoch}=1~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
to make stochastic counterterms dimension-less,
and retained only the contributions
relevant at the two-loop order. Likewise,
for the galaxy-matter cross-spectrum the stochastic term
reads

\Delta P_{gm}^{\rm stoch}=\langle\epsilon_{g}({\bm{k}})\epsilon_{m}({\bm{k}})\rangle^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\bar{n}_{g}}\left(a^{\prime}_{0}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm stoch}}\right)^{2}+a^{\prime}_{4}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm stoch}}\right)^{4}+\cdots\right)\,,

(101)

which does not have the constant term due to the mass
and momentum conservation of the matter
density.

Finally, we find that the mixed stochastic-deterministic
operators
O(\epsilon_{g}\delta)
, studied systemically
in the context of the bispectrum in
Ivanov et al. 2022
;
Bakx et al. 2025c
,
produce only purely redundant
contributions at the level of the galaxy
power spectrum. This result is valid to all orders in EFT, see
Appendix
A
for more detail.

4.4
Summary

All in all, our complete two-loop calculation yields
the following prediction:

\begin{split}&P_{gg}(k)=P_{gg}^{\rm 1-loop,~tot}+\Delta P_{gg}^{\rm 2-loop}+\Delta P^{\rm HD}_{gg}+\Delta P_{gg}^{\rm stoch}\,,\\
&P_{gm}(k)=P_{gm}^{\rm 1-loop,~tot}+\Delta P_{gm}^{\rm 2-loop}+\Delta P^{\rm HD}_{gm}+\Delta P_{gm}^{\rm stoch}\,,\end{split}

(102)

where
P_{gg}^{\rm 1-loop,~tot}
,
P_{gm}^{\rm 1-loop,~tot}

are the total one-loop
galaxy-galaxy and galaxy-matter power-spectra
Chudaykin et al. 2020
;
Ivanov et al. 2026
,
\Delta P^{\rm stoch}_{gg,gm}
are the stochastic
power spectra, which depend on
(P_{\rm shot},a_{0},a_{4})
and

(a^{\prime}_{0},a^{\prime}_{4})
. parameters, respectively.
The two-loop bias contributions read:

\begin{split}&\Delta P_{gg}^{\rm 2-loop}=\sum_{i\leq j}b_{i}\,b_{j}\;P^{ij}(k)\,,\\
&\Delta P_{gm}^{\rm 2-loop}=\sum_{i\leq j}\frac{1}{2}(b^{m}_{i}\,b_{j}+b_{i}\,b^{m}_{j})\;P^{ij}(k)\,,\end{split}

(103)

where
P^{ij}(k)
are two-loop bias shapes and
b^{i}
is the vector of 17 galaxy bias parameters,

b^{i}=\bigl(b_{1},\,b_{2},\,\gamma_{2},\,\gamma_{21},\,b_{3},\,\gamma_{2}^{\times},\,\gamma_{3},\,\gamma_{211},\,\gamma_{22},\,\gamma_{31},\,\gamma_{21}^{\times},\,\gamma_{23},\,\gamma_{221},\,\gamma_{311},\,\gamma_{41},\,\gamma_{22}^{\times},\,\gamma_{31}^{\times}\bigr),

(104)

and
b_{i}^{m}=(1,\vec{0})
is the formal vector of bias
parameters for dark matter.

\Delta P^{\rm HD}_{gg,gm}

are the two-loop higher derivative contributions
from eqs. (
99a
,
96
,
which depend on the higher derivative bias
b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}
,
the
dark matter sound-speed counterterm

c_{s}
, 7 two-loop galaxy higher-derivative bias parameters
c_{1,...,7}
,
and five two-loop dark matter counterterms
\tilde{c}_{s},e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
.
Eqs. (
102
) are subject to two-loop
IR resummation from eq. (
79
).

The full two-loop galaxy power spectrum computation
depends on 17 bias parameters, 8 higher-derivative parameters,
and 3 stochastic parameters. The galaxy-matter cross-spectrum introduces
additional 5 higher-derivative counterterms and 2 stochastic parameters,
totaling to 35 free parameters.

5
Comparison with Simulation Data

In this section we compare our two-loop computation
with the galaxy-galaxy power spectrum and galaxy-matter cross-spectrum
of the PT Challenge simulation data
Nishimichi et al. 2020
at a fixed redshift
z=0.61
.
We focus on the
P_{gg}-P_{gm}

combination in order to understand the implications of the two-loop
galaxy power spectrum model for the
2\times 2
pt analysis of the
galaxy clustering and galaxy-lensing data from imaging surveys or CMB lensing maps.
We do not include
P_{mm}
in this combination for two reasons:
first, if we add
P_{mm}
to
P_{gg}-P_{gm}
data-vector,
it will dominate the constraints on the matter clustering amplitude on all scales,
making it difficult to access the validity of the
galaxy power spectrum model.
Second, the
P_{gg}-P_{gm}-P_{mm}

data-vector does not accurately
approximate the full
3\times 2
pt
data because it does not
capture the finite extend of the
lensing kernel and the lensing noise, making the outcome of such
analysis hard to interpret in the context of the realistic gravitational weak
lensing data. In contrast,
P_{gg}-P_{gm}
data at a fixed redshift is a good proxy to
the galaxy clustering and galaxy-galaxy lensing spectra because
these observables
are mainly sourced by
P_{gg}-P_{gm}

at an effective redshift of the sample if the number
density is narrow enough, which is the case, e.g. for the
DESI photometric sample
Maus et al. 2025
;
Ivanov et al. 2026
.

The PT Challenge simulation is a large-volume N-body simulation,
which consists of 10 boxes with the cumulative volume
566

[
h^{-1}
Gpc]
3
. The galaxy distribution at
z=0.61
is
generated using a Halo Occupation Distribution model
for the BOSS-like luminous red galaxies (LRG)
described
in Ref.
Nishimichi et al. 2020
.
We use the mean real space galaxy auto spectrum
and galaxy-matter cross spectrum from the entire suite.
The power spectra are binned linearly with a bin width
\Delta k=0.01~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
.
We use the data up to
k=0.85~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
in this analysis. We subtract the Poisson
shot noise contribution
1/\bar{n}_{g}
from the
P_{gg}
data.
We use a Gaussian likelihood with the Gaussian covariance matrix for the
P_{gg}-P_{gm}

data vector built from the measurements themselves,

\mathrm{Cov}\!\left[\begin{pmatrix}P_{gm}(k)\\
P_{gg}(k)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}P_{gm}(k)\\
P_{gg}(k)\end{pmatrix}\right]=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\begin{pmatrix}P_{gg}^{\rm tot}(k)\,P_{mm}(k)+P_{gm}^{2}(k)&2\,P_{gg}^{\rm tot}(k)\,P_{gm}(k)\\[6.0pt]
2\,P_{gg}^{\rm tot}(k)\,P_{gm}(k)&2\left(P_{gg}^{\rm tot}(k)\right)^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(105)

where
N=4\pi k^{2}\Delta kV/(2\pi)^{3}
is the
number of modes in a
k
-bin,

P_{gg}^{\rm tot}(k)=P_{gg}(k)+\frac{1}{\bar{n}_{g}}

and
P_{mm}
is the matter power spectrum of the simulation.
Note that we use
P_{mm}
measurements
only for the covariance
matrix estimation.
To make the analysis more relevant to the actual imaging surveys,
we use the fiducial volume
V=1
[
h^{-1}
Gpc]
3
, which roughly reproduces
typical error bars from surveys like DES
Abbott et al. 2026
.
While the Gaussian approximation
to the covariance is fairly accurate on large-scales, one might be
worried about its validity on much smaller scales relevant for the
two-loop fits. In this regime, however, the parameter constraints
are driven by the marginalizion over the EFT nuisance parameters,
producing a large “theoretical error” covariance
Chudaykin et al. 2021b
;
Baldauf et al. 2016
, which is more
significant than any connected contributions.
This was explicitly tested on the BOSS data in Ref.
Wadekar et al. 2020
.

In this work, we carry out a simplified analysis
that (a) varies only on the mass fluctuation amplitude

\sigma_{8}
and the EFT parameters,
and (b) uses a Fisher matrix
approximation to the posterior distribution.
Our fitting parameters are the scaling parameter
A\equiv\sigma_{8}/\sigma_{8,\mathrm{true}}
plus the relevant EFT parameters for each model.
We assume Gaussian priors on all nuisance parameters
\mathcal{N}(\bar{p}_{\rm EFT},\sigma^{2}_{p_{\rm EFT}})
with
\bar{p}_{\rm EFT}=2
for
b_{1}

and
\bar{p}_{\rm EFT}=0
for all other parameters. We use

\sigma_{p_{\rm EFT}}=2
, consistent with requirements of perturbativity
of EFT and the notion of naturalness. For each model and scale
cut
{k_{\text{max}}}
choice,
we minimize the joint Gaussian likelihood
of the data and the priors, compute the covariance matrix
for the relevant parameters around the bets-fit.
The
C^{-1}_{AA}
element
of that covariance is the 1D marginalized
variance of
A
.
While this Gaussian approach
is approximate, it has the great advantage of being extremely computationally efficient,
allowing us to scan over the vast range of
k_{\rm max}
in order
to determine the optimal scale cuts. We leave a more careful
MCMC analysis for future work.

Let us start off with a linear theory analysis.
This analysis is similar to the fiducial analysis of the DES-Y6
collaboration based on
the linear bias model
Legnani et al. 2026
.
In this case the models are simply:

P_{gg}(k)=A^{2}\,b_{1}^{2}\,P_{\rm lin}(k)+P_{\rm shot}\,\bar{n}^{-1},\quad P_{gm}(k)=A^{2}\,b_{1}\,P_{\rm lin}(k)\,,

(106)

where we use the pre-computed
P_{\rm lin}
for true PTC cosmology.
Note that we vary the leading order stochasticity parameter

P_{\rm shot}
in order to be consistent with the EFT power counting. Indeed, the magnitude shot noise of the PT Challenge galaxies is of the same order of magnitude as the linear theory model at
k\sim 0.1~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
, consistent with out power counting.

The results of the linear theory data analysis are shown in Fig.
5
, which demonstrates the drift plot
A({k_{\text{max}}})

and shows the best-fit theory model for the maximal value of

{k_{\text{max}}}
where the fit is unbiased, which is
0.05~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
.
We choose this
k_{\rm max}
by requiring the bias on
A
not to exceed

0.33\sigma_{A}
.
At
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.05~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
our fitting procedure
gives the 1D marginalyzed limit

A=1.0045\pm 0.0192
.

We see that the linear theory model becomes biased for
k>0.05~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
very quickly,
and the bias grows with
{k_{\text{max}}}
quite steeply. For instance, at
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.3~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
the recovery of
\sigma_{8}
is biased already by
10\%
, which
significantly exceeds the precision of the current
2\times 2
pt analyses
Legnani et al. 2026
.

We move on now to the one-loop model,

\begin{split}&P_{gg}(k)=A^{2}\,b_{1}^{2}\,P_{\rm lin}(k)+A^{4}P^{\rm 1-loop}_{gg}(k)-2(b_{1}^{2}c_{s}+b_{1}b_{\nabla^{2}\delta})A^{2}k^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)+\bar{n}^{-1}_{g}\left[P_{\rm shot}+a_{0}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm stoch}}\right)^{2}\right]\,,\\
&P_{gm}(k)=A^{2}\,b_{1}\,P_{\rm lin}(k)+A^{4}P^{\rm 1-loop}_{gm}(k)-(2b_{1}c_{s}+b_{\nabla^{2}\delta})A^{2}k^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)+\bar{n}^{-1}_{g}a^{\prime}_{0}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm stoch}}\right)^{2}\,,\end{split}

(107)

where
P^{\rm 1-loop}_{gg,gm}
are the SPT contributions
computed for the PTC cosmology
with
CLASS-PT
.
We also implement the appropriate
one-loop
IR resummation as in
Chudaykin et al. 2020
.
The one-loop results are shown in Fig.
6
.
In the left panel we see that the one-loop
model becomes biased around
{k_{\text{max}}}\simeq 0.3~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
.
A more precise answer is
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.29~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
,
where we get
A=1.0028\pm 0.0101
.
We observe an excellent agreement
between the best-fit
theory and the data up to
{k_{\text{max}}}\simeq 0.29~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}

in the right panel.
We also observe now that the biasing
of the
A
inference is much more smooth than
in the case of the linear bias model, implying
that the errors due to the omitted two-loop
contributions are now effectively absorbed by
the one-loop bias parameters.
The
A({k_{\text{max}}})

drift curve show a
non-monotonic behavior for
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.6~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
,
which is
a consequence of the significant biasing of the one-loop theory model
on small scales. In particular, this leads to a jump
around
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.8~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
.
This behavior is simply an artifact
of the one-loop model used much beyond its validity regime.

Finally,
Fig.
7
displays the results for the two-loop model summarized in Eq. (
102
),
where we re-scale the new corrections
with appropriate powers of
A
.
We see now that the recovery of
A
is unbiased
on all scales used in the analysis. We stop at
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.85~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
where
we get
A=0.9989\pm 0.0063
.
This constraint is a factor of three better than the linear theory
measurement, and
\approx 40\%
better than the 1-loop result.

Compared to the linear and one-loop results,
we see a much gradual narrowing
of the error-bars as a function of
{k_{\text{max}}}
,
which is a result of having many nuisance parameters in the fit.
A somewhat fast
narrowing of the
A
error at
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.85

happens due to breaking of degeneracies between the fitting parameters
around this scale.
The right panel of Fig.
7

shows an excellent
\lesssim 0.5\%
fit to the data.
While in principle the two-loop model can fit the data even on smaller
scales, we stop the analysis at
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.85
because smaller scale data
is not available to us, and because the resolution

precision level.
It will be interesting to re-do our analysis
for other simulation data, especially galaxy
samples matching the ones used in actual imaging
surveys.

6
Implications for Weak Lensing Data and New Physics

Having determined the reach of the galaxy power spectrum
models in real space (at
z=0.61
for the simulated LRG),
let us now discuss the implications for the weak lensing data.
To keep our discussion more robust w.r.t. the numerical
artifacts due to the mis-modeling of the data,
we will use a Fisher matrix forecast
for
\sigma_{8}
measurements
for the
\{P_{gm},P_{gg}\}

model vector, whose results are shown in Fig.
8
.
This forecast is produced for the best-fit two-loop
galaxy power spectrum model at
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.85~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
.
Our Fisher forecast results are in perfect agreement
with analysis of the actual data. Dots in Fig.
8

correspond to values at optimal
{k_{\text{max}}}
where the
corresponding models are unbiased.

Figure 8:
Comparison of the Fisher forecast constraints on the
mass fluctuation amplitude
\sigma_{8}
as a function of scale cut for
the linear, one-loop EFT, and two-loop EFT models. Vertical dashed lines
show the limiting
{k_{\text{max}}}
values where each model is reliable.

Many current
2\times 2
pt imaging data
analyses are
based on the
linear bias model, see e.g.
Legnani et al. 2026
.
This model has an advantage
of having very few fitting
parameters (
b_{1}
and optionally
P_{\rm shot}
).
In addition, the error on
\sigma_{8}
shrinks very quickly with

{k_{\text{max}}}
, which gains the constraining power quite significantly if one
were to naively push the analysis to small scales.
However, we have seen that the model becomes
biased very quickly as one increases
{k_{\text{max}}}
. Requiring the
unbiased recovery of
\sigma_{8}
selects the scale cut
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.05~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
, which leads to a relatively poor
\approx 2\%
determination of
\sigma_{8}
.

In contrast, the one-loop EFT model leads to a much slower
improvement of
\sigma_{8}
constraints as a function of
{k_{\text{max}}}

due to a large-number of EFT parameters. Nevertheless, we get
a
\approx 1\%
measurement at
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.29~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
, which is a factor of 2 better than the linear theory result. This illustrates that adding more fitting parameters (the one-loop model has 9) and
pushing the analysis to small scales is actually more beneficial
for the cosmological constraints than
using a more minimal
model restricted to linear scales only.

Finally, the two-loop model produces
an relatively slow

\sigma(\sigma_{8})({k_{\text{max}}})
curve.
This model has 35 nuisance parameters,
but it leads to a more significant
eventual gains than the linear theory
or the one-loop EFT models.
Note that our Fisher forecast produces a jump of the

\sigma(\sigma_{8})({k_{\text{max}}})
curve at
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.85~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}

identical to the jump we have seen before in the actual data. This confirms that
it is not an artifact of the mis-modeling,
but a genuine effect of
degeneracy breaking.

Finally, we get a
0.63\%
final error on
\sigma_{8}

at
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.85~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
.
This is a factor
of three and
\approx 40\%
more precise than the linear theory and one-loop results, respectively.
Note that our simulated galaxies are more biased
than the actual imaging galaxy lens samples.
Less biased tracers are typically better modeled in perturbation
theory (see e.g.
Ivanov 2021
;
Ivanov et al. 2024b
;
Sullivan et al. 2025
;
de Belsunce et al. 2026a
), which suggests an even larger gain for realistic samples.

An even more important point than a better measurement of
\sigma_{8}

in
\Lambda
CDM is that the two-loop EFT calculation provides a robust
theoretical control over a much larger range of scales than the linear theory does.
This is especially
important for the new physics models, which in principle,
can be analytically implemented in the two-loop EFT computations.
For instance, the previous BOSS results on the ultralight axions showed
that the masses
m_{a}=10^{-25}
eV can be probed with the one-loop
EFT model thanks to an extension of
{k_{\text{max}}}
to
0.4~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}

Rogers et al. 2023
.
Our two-loop EFT calculations can be pushed to

{k_{\text{max}}}=0.85~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
, which
allows us to probe larger masses
m_{a}=(10^{-24})
eV, which are currently only weakly constrained with
the DES weak lensing data
Dentler et al. 2022
;
Preston et al. 2025
(see also
the recent limits from CMB lensing
Laguë et al. 2026
). We leave the analysis
of this and other new physics models for future work.

7
Conclusions

We have computed the real
space galaxy power spectrum
in cosmological perturbation theory
(effective field theory)
at two-loop order. We have derived
fifth order bias operators using a hybrid scheme
which classifies all operators into Eulerian
local evolution operators and the Lagrangian non-local evolution operators. We have shows that there are 29 distinct bias operators
relevant at the fifth loop order, in agreement
with other 5th order bias studies
Bakx et al. 2025a
.
We have derived
the 5th order bias renormalization
conditions, which are mathematically equivalent to the
renormalization conditions for the one-loop galaxy trispectrum.
In passing, we
recovered the one-loop power spectrum and one-loop bispectrum
renormalization conditions, which is an important consistency check of our new two-loop computation.

We have shown that only 17 operators produce non-redundant contributions
to the galaxy power spectrum at the two-loop order.
We have computed the relevant two-loop shapes numerically using the
Bogoliubov-Parasiuk-Hepp-Zimmermann renormalization scheme,
which allows us to connect our two-loop results with the common one-loop
computations such as the ones used in the
CLASS-PT
code.
We have then derived the relevant two-loop counterterms and stochastic
contributions.

We have shown that the two-loop galaxy power spectrum
allows one to model the
z=0.61

galaxy clustering data from the PT Challenge
N-body simulation to
{k_{\text{max}}}=0.85~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
.
Assuming conservative physically
motivated priors for EFT
parameters, this leads to a
significantly more
precise inference of the mass fluctuation amplitude
\sigma_{8}
,
which if a factor of three better than the linear theory result.
We stress that this improvement happens when all 35 relevant EFT parameters are varied within conservative physically-motivated
priors.
Extrapolating this to the projected galaxy clustering and galaxy-clustering and galaxy-lensing data suggests a factor of three improvement
of limits on the lensing parameter
S_{8}
, and also novel opportunities to
test new physics that modifies the shape of the matter power
spectrum on scales
k\lesssim(0.5-0.9)~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
, such as the
ultralight axion dark matter sub-component with masses
m_{a}\sim 10^{-24}
eV. At face value, this implies exciting new opportunities
for galaxy clustering and weak lensing data from DES, LSST and
Roman surveys.
It will be important to study the implications of the two-loop
computation both for
\Lambda
CDM and beyond it in more
realistic analysis of
the projected clustering data.

Our analysis can be extended in multiple other ways. First, one can
implement a fast evaluation of the two-loop integrals, e.g. by means
of the COBRA technique
Bakx et al. 2025d
;
Bakx et al. 2025a
;
Bakx et al. 2025b
, which will allow us to carry out inference of cosmological
parameters beyond
\sigma_{8}
.
Second, our computation can be extended to redshift space,
though the field-level results of Refs.
Schmittfull et al. 2021
;
Ivanov et al. 2024a
;
Sullivan et al. 2025
;
Sullivan et al. 2025

suggest that the
actual bottleneck there is the modeling of the stochastic
(one-halo) contribution, which becomes non-perturbative
for wavenumbers
\sim 0.3~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
.
Since the EFT is really
useful only to model the deterministic part, it remains
unclear if the two-loop computation in redshift space will be useful
for the actual data analysis.
The two-loop computations, however, may improve the modeling
of the Lyman-
\alpha
forest, where the EFT stochasticity
is smaller than that of galaxies
Ivanov 2024
;
Ivanov et al. 2024c
;
de Belsunce et al. 2025b
;
de Belsunce et al. 2026b
;
de Belsunce et al. 2026a
.
Third, the fifth order EFT computation we carried out here
can be used to compute the one-loop galaxy trispectrum in EFT,
extending the previous matter clustering results
Bertolini et al. 2016
.

We leave these and other research directions for future investigation.

Appendix A
Redundancy of the mixed stochastic-deterministic
power spectrum contributions to all orders

Let us consider the following non-perturbative expression
for the galaxy density field in real space:

\delta_{g}=\delta_{\rm det}+\epsilon+\delta_{\rm mix}\epsilon\,,

(108)

where
\delta_{\rm det},\delta_{\rm mix}
are most general
composite operators built from the deterministic fields,
such as the linear matter density, gravitational potential,
LPT vector potential, etc. The only approximation we have made
so far is to assume a local coupling between stochastic
and deterministic fields in
\delta_{\rm mix}\epsilon
.
By definition,
\langle\delta_{\rm mix}\epsilon\rangle=0
,

\langle\delta_{\rm det}\epsilon\rangle=0
. The fully non-linear power spectrum reads:

\begin{split}\langle|\delta^{2}_{g}|\rangle^{\prime}=\langle|\delta_{\rm det}^{2}|\rangle^{\prime}+P_{\epsilon}(k)+P_{\rm mixed,~tot}\,,\quad P_{\rm mixed,~tot}\equiv\int_{\bm{q}}P_{\rm NL,mix}(q)P_{\epsilon}(|{\bm{k}}-\bm{q}|)\,,\end{split}

(109)

where
P_{\rm NL,mix}
and
P_{\epsilon}(k)
are the non-perturbative power spectra
of
\delta_{\rm mix}
and
\epsilon
, respectively.
In EFT
P_{\epsilon}(k)
assumes a series expansion
in
k^{2}
in the
k\ll k_{\rm stoch}
limit:

P_{\epsilon}(k)=\sum_{n=0}c_{n}k^{2n}\,.

(110)

Plugging this into Eq. (
109
) we find
that the
P_{\rm mixed,~tot}
integral produces
a similar power series

P_{\rm mixed,~tot}=\sum_{n=0}\int_{\bm{q}}P_{\rm NL,mix}(q)c_{n}(q^{2}-2({\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{q})+k^{2})^{2n}=\sum_{n^{\prime}=0}c^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}k^{2n^{\prime}}\,,

(111)

which implies that all
c^{\prime}_{n}
can be absorbed by a re-definition
of the stochastic counterterms
c_{n}
. The key property that we used
is that
P_{\rm NL,mix}(q)
does not depend on the on-shell momentum
k
;
the
k
-dependence is entirely determined by the stochastic power
spectrum (
110
).
The contributions from modes with
q\gtrsim k_{\rm stoch}

are also renormalized by the stochastic counterterms
c_{n}
.
Therefore, the mixed stochastic-deterministic
couplings are completely redundant at the level of the galaxy power spectrum. We stress, however, that they are not redundant
at the level of higher-order
n
-point functions, see
e.g.
Ivanov et al. 2022
;
Bakx et al. 2025c
for detailed
discussions in the context of galaxy bispectrum.

It is trivial now to generalize this argument to the case of
non-local mixed couplings e.g.
\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}F_{\rm mix}[\delta,\Phi,\varphi_{2},\vec{A_{3}},...]\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\epsilon
, where
F_{\rm mix}[\delta,\Phi,\varphi_{2},\vec{A_{3}},...]
is a general function of the deterministic fields and their potentials. The non-trivial derivative structure will collapse into extra integral kernels,
such as
(({\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{q})-q^{2})^{2}
, which will simply shift

c^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}
coefficients in Eq. (
111
), without
affecting the conclusions.
This completes the proof that the mixed
stochastic deterministic contributions
are completely redundant in EFT at the level of the
galaxy power spectrum to all orders.
