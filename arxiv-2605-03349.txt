Sign changes of the Liouville function in arithmetic progressions
Kevin Ford, Maksym Radziwiłł

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Dirichlet established that for any reduced residue class
(a,q)=1
there exists a prime
p
with
p\equiv a\pmod{q}
. It is conjectured, based on probabilistic heuristics, that the least such prime
p
satisfies
p\ll_{\varepsilon}q^{1+\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
;
see
[
4
]
for a more precise conjecture.
Towards this conjecture,
Chowla
[
1
]
showed in 1934 that the generalized Riemann
hypothesis implies
p\ll_{\varepsilon}q^{2+\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. This corresponds to the natural
square root barrier for equidistribution problems in arithmetic
progressions. A decade later, Linnik
[
5
,
6
]
proved
unconditionally that
p\ll q^{L}
for some absolute constant
L
.
Considerable effort has since gone into reducing the admissible value
of
L
. A major advance was made by Heath-Brown
[
2
]
, who
proved
L=5.5
. Building on Heath-Brown’s work,
Xylouris
[
10
]
refined the argument to show that
L=5

is admissible. This remains the best unconditional result to date.

In this short note we consider a multiplicative function analogue. For a prime
q
and
(a,q)=1
, we ask: how large must
N
be to guarantee the existence of integers
n,m\leq N
with
n\equiv m\equiv a\pmod{q}
and
\lambda(n)=-1
,
\lambda(m)=+1
, where
\lambda
denotes the Liouville function?

Theorem 1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then, for any prime
q
, sufficiently large with respect to
1/\varepsilon
and any residue class
(a,q)=1
, there exist integers
n,m\leq q^{5/2+\varepsilon}
with
n\equiv m\equiv a\pmod{q}
such that
\lambda(n)=-1
and
\lambda(m)=1
.

The present result was obtained by the authors in 2018, and a complete proof was shared with Matomäki in July 2023, at the conference honoring Heath-Brown’s 70th birthday, in response to her work with Teräväinen
[
8
]
establishing the existence of products of three primes
p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}\ll q^{3}
in every progression
a\pmod{q}
with
(a,q)=1
. In upcoming work
[
7
]
, using methods different from ours, Matomäki and Teräväinen have obtained the stronger bound
q^{2+\varepsilon}
for general real-valued multiplicative functions unless the sign of the function strongly pretends to be a real Dirichlet character modulo
q
. For the Liouville function, their result gives
q^{2+\varepsilon}
unconditionally, matching what was previously known only under the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis.

Our argument is short and proceeds along different lines from
[
7
]
. We show that if
\lambda(n)
takes a constant sign for all
n\equiv a\pmod{q}
with
n\leq Xq^{2}
then
\lambda(n)
is
q
-periodic for all
n\leq Xq
with
(n,q)=1
and the sum
\lambda(1)+\lambda(2)+\ldots+\lambda(q-1)
is exactly zero. We then show, using the large sieve and Poisson summation, that for
X>q^{1/2+3\varepsilon}
this implies that
\lambda(n)=\kappa^{\prime}\chi_{q}(n)
with
\kappa^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}
and
\chi_{q}
the Legendre symbol with modulus
q
, for all
n\leq q
with at most
\ll q^{1-\varepsilon/4}
exceptions. We rule out the case
\kappa^{\prime}=-1
using mere multiplicativity. We then rule out the remaining case
\kappa^{\prime}=1
using Siegel’s lower bound for
L(1,\chi)
.

We remark that, unlike Linnik’s theorem (where both the exponent and the implied constant
are effectively computable), our argument
uses Siegel’s theorem and therefore the threshold “sufficiently large in terms of
1/\varepsilon
” appearing in Theorem
1
is uncomputable.

3.
Auxiliary lemmas

Lemma 2

(Poisson summation for characters
[
3
, Exercise 5 & Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
q
be a positive integer and let
\chi
be a primitive character modulo
q
.
Let
W\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function with compact support.
Then, for any
N>0
,

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(n)\,W\!\biggl(\frac{n}{N}\biggr)\;=\;\frac{N\tau(\chi)}{q}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\chi}(m)\,\widehat{W}\!\biggl(\frac{mN}{q}\biggr),

where
\tau(\chi)=\sum_{a=1}^{q}\chi(a)\,e(a/q)
is the Gauss sum.
In particular,
|\tau(\chi)|=\sqrt{q}
for
\chi
primitive.

Lemma 3

(Siegel’s theorem
[
9
, Theorem 11.14]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and every real primitive character
\chi
modulo
q
, we have

L(1,\chi)\gg_{\varepsilon}q^{-\varepsilon}.

Lemma 4

(Large sieve inequality
[
3
, Section 7.5]
)
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{N}
and
N\geq 1
. For any complex numbers
(a_{n})_{n\leq N}
,

\sum_{\chi\pmod{q}}\biggl|\sum_{n\leq N}a_{n}\chi(n)\biggr|^{2}\leq(N+q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}|a_{n}|^{2}.

Lemma 5
.

Let
W:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
be smooth and compactly supported in
[1/2,1]
,
and let
\chi
be a primitive character of modulus
q>1
. Then, for
N\geq q
,

\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big(\sum_{d\mid n}\chi(d)\Big)W\!\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)=L(1,\chi)N\widehat{W}(0)+O(q^{1/2}).

Proof.

By Mellin inversion,

\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big(\sum_{d\mid n}\chi(d)\Big)W\!\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)N^{s}\,\widetilde{W}(s)\,ds,

where
\widetilde{W}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}W(x)x^{s-1}dx
is entire and satisfies

\widetilde{W}(s)\ll_{A}(1+|s|)^{-A}
in any vertical strip. We shift the contour to
\Re s=-\delta
with
\delta=1/100
. Since
\chi
is non-principal, there is a simple pole at
s=1
accounting for the main term. We bound the integral on the line
\Re s=-\delta
using the functional equation and this yields the error term
\ll N^{-\delta}q^{1/2+\delta}\ll\sqrt{q}
for
N\geq q
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
A
and
B
be two subsets of
(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

with
|A|+|B|>\varphi(q)
.
Then for every
(n,q)=1
we can find
a\in A
and
b\in B
such that

n\equiv ab\pmod{q}
.

Proof.

Since
|A|+|B|>\varphi(q)
, for any
n
with
(n,q)=1

the sets
A
and
nB^{-1}:=\{nb^{-1}\bmod q:b\in B\}

satisfy
|A|+|nB^{-1}|>\varphi(q)=|(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|
,
so by the pigeonhole principle they must intersect.
Any element in
A\cap nB^{-1}
yields the desired representation.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof hinges on the following lemma.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
q
be
sufficiently large with respect to
1/\varepsilon
.
Let
a
be an integer coprime to
q
,
let
\kappa\in\{-1,+1\}
, and let
N\geq q^{2+\varepsilon}
.
If for every
n\equiv a\pmod{q}
with
n\leq N
we have

\lambda(n)=\kappa
, then

\lambda(n+q)=\lambda(n)

for all
n\leq N/q-q
with
(n,q)=1
, and

\#\Big\{1\leq n<q:(n,q)=1,\lambda(n)=-1\Big\}=\frac{\varphi(q)}{2}=\#\Big\{1\leq n<q:(n,q)=1,\lambda(n)=1\Big\}.

Proof.

Set
\eta:=\varepsilon/3
.
For an interval
I=[M,M+q)
write

A_{I}=\{n\in I:(n,q)=1,\;\lambda(n)=-1\},\qquad B_{I}=\{n\in I:(n,q)=1,\;\lambda(n)=+1\}.

Notice that
|A_{I}|+|B_{I}|=\varphi(q)
.

Step 1.

For every interval
I=[M,M+q)\subset[1,q^{1+\eta})

we have
|A_{I}|=|B_{I}|=\varphi(q)/2
.

Suppose
|B_{I}|>\varphi(q)/2
for some

I\subset[1,q^{1+\eta})
. Since
q
is sufficiently large
with respect to
1/\varepsilon
and
\eta=\varepsilon/3
,
we can choose a prime
p\leq q^{\eta}
coprime to
q
.
Since multiplication by
p
permutes

(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
,
the set
pB_{I}:=\{pb\bmod q:b\in B_{I}\}

satisfies
|pB_{I}|=|B_{I}|>\varphi(q)/2
.

By Lemma
6
applied to
(B_{I},B_{I})
:
for any
(c,q)=1
there exist

b_{1},b_{2}\in B_{I}
with
b_{1}b_{2}\equiv c\pmod{q}
,
giving
\lambda(b_{1}b_{2})=+1
and

b_{1}b_{2}\leq q^{2+2\eta}
.

By Lemma
6
applied to
(B_{I},pB_{I})
:
there exist
b,b^{\prime}\in B_{I}
with

pbb^{\prime}\equiv c\pmod{q}
, giving

\lambda(pbb^{\prime})=(-1)(+1)(+1)=-1
and

pbb^{\prime}\leq q^{2+3\eta}=q^{2+\varepsilon}\leq N
.

Taking
c=a
, we obtain integers of both signs in the
class
a
up to
N
, contradicting the hypothesis.
The case
|A_{I}|>\varphi(q)/2
is identical
(swap the roles of
A
and
B
).
Since
|A_{I}|\leq\varphi(q)/2
and
|B_{I}|\leq\varphi(q)/2

while
|A_{I}|+|B_{I}|=\varphi(q)
,
we conclude
|A_{I}|=|B_{I}|=\varphi(q)/2
.

Step 2.

For every interval
I=[M,M+q)\subset[1,N/q]

we have
|A_{I}|=|B_{I}|=\varphi(q)/2
.

Suppose
|B_{I}|>\varphi(q)/2
for some

I\subset[1,N/q]
.
Let
J=[1,q)
.
Since
J\subset[1,q^{1+\eta})
,
Step 1 gives
|A_{J}|=|B_{J}|=\varphi(q)/2
.

By Lemma
6
applied to
(B_{I},A_{J})

(with
|B_{I}|>\varphi(q)/2
and
|A_{J}|=\varphi(q)/2
):
there exist
b\in B_{I}
,
d\in A_{J}

with
bd\equiv a\pmod{q}
.
Then
\lambda(bd)=(+1)(-1)=-1
and

bd\leq(N/q)\cdot q=N
.

By Lemma
6
applied to
(B_{I},B_{J})
:
there exist
b_{1}\in B_{I}
,
b_{2}\in B_{J}
with

b_{1}b_{2}\equiv a\pmod{q}
.
Then
\lambda(b_{1}b_{2})=(+1)(+1)=+1
and

b_{1}b_{2}\leq N
.

Again both signs appear in class
a
up to
N
,
contradicting the hypothesis.
The case
|A_{I}|>\varphi(q)/2
is symmetric.
Since
|A_{I}|\leq\varphi(q)/2
and
|B_{I}|\leq\varphi(q)/2

while
|A_{I}|+|B_{I}|=\varphi(q)
,
we conclude
|A_{I}|=|B_{I}|=\varphi(q)/2
.

Step 3.

For every
n\leq N/q-q
with
(n,q)=1

we have
\lambda(n+q)=\lambda(n)
.

Let
n\leq N/q-q
with
(n,q)=1
.
The intervals

I=[n,n+q)
and
I^{\prime}=[n+1,n+q+1)

both lie in
[1,N/q]
, so Step 2 gives

|A_{I}|=|A_{I^{\prime}}|=\varphi(q)/2
.

Passing from
I
to
I^{\prime}
removes
n
and adds
n+q
.
Since
q
is prime and
(n,q)=1
, we have

(n+q,q)=1
, so both
n
and
n+q
are counted
by
A
or
B
.
The cardinality changes as

|A_{I^{\prime}}|=|A_{I}|-\mathbf{1}[\lambda(n)=-1]+\mathbf{1}[\lambda(n+q)=-1].

Since
|A_{I^{\prime}}|=|A_{I}|
, we obtain

\mathbf{1}[\lambda(n)=-1]=\mathbf{1}[\lambda(n+q)=-1]
,
which gives
\lambda(n)=\lambda(n+q)
.
∎

We will also need the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
q
be a
large prime. Let
L\geq q^{3/2+3\varepsilon}
. Suppose that

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq n<q}\lambda(n)=0

and that for all
(n,q)=1
with
n\leq L
we have
\lambda(n+q)=\lambda(n).

Then there exists a
\kappa^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}
such that

\lambda(n)=\kappa^{\prime}\cdot\Big(\frac{n}{q}\Big)

for all
n\leq q
with at most
\ll_{\varepsilon}q^{1-\varepsilon/4}
exceptions.

Proof.

Define the Fourier coefficients

\widehat{\lambda}(\chi):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
(x,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}\lambda(x)\,\overline{\chi}(x)

for each Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
q
.
By condition (
1
),
\widehat{\lambda}(\chi_{0})=0
.
Since
\lambda
is
q
-periodic on integers coprime to
q
up to
L

we may write

(2)

\lambda(n)=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\widehat{\lambda}(\chi)\,\chi(n)

for all
(n,q)=1
with
n\leq L
. Let
W
be a smooth function, compactly supported in
[1/2,1]
, satisfying
0\leq W\leq 1
and

\int W(x)\,dx>0
.
Set
P=q^{1/2-\varepsilon}
and
N=q^{1/2+2\varepsilon}
.
Since
\lambda
is completely multiplicative,

\lambda(n^{2}p)=\lambda(n)^{2}\lambda(p)=-1
for every prime
p
.
Therefore

(3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
P/2\leq p\leq P\\
p\text{ prime},\,(np,q)=1\end{subarray}}W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)\;=\;\biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
P/2\leq p\leq P\\
p\text{ prime},\,(np,q)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda(n^{2}p)W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)\biggr|.

Note that
N^{2}P=q^{3/2+3\varepsilon}\leq L
,
so (
2
) is valid for
n^{2}p
whenever

N/2\leq n\leq N
and
P/2\leq p\leq P
. Since
W
is compactly supported in
[1/2,1]
the sum over
n\in\mathbb{Z}
in (
3
) is constrained to
N/2\leq n\leq N
.
Substituting (
2
) and using

\chi(n^{2}p)=\chi^{2}(n)\chi(p)
, the right-hand side becomes

(4)

\biggl|\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\widehat{\lambda}(\chi)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\chi^{2}(n)W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P/2\leq p\leq P\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}\chi(p)\biggr|.

Let
\chi_{q}
denote the quadratic character modulo
q
.
Since
\chi_{q}^{2}=\chi_{0}
, the character
\chi_{q}
contributes

\leq\frac{|\widehat{\lambda}(\chi_{q})|}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)\cdot\biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P/2\leq p\leq P\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}\chi_{q}(p)\biggr|\;\leq\;\frac{|\widehat{\lambda}(\chi_{q})|}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
P/2\leq p\leq P\\
(np,q)=1\end{subarray}}W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big).

We apply Hölder’s inequality with exponents
(2,4,4)
to the sum
over
\chi\neq\chi_{0},\chi_{q}
in (
4
). Thus it remains to
bound

\Big(\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi}|\widehat{\lambda}(\chi)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0},\chi_{q}}\Big|\sum_{n}\chi^{2}(n)W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)\Big|^{4}\Big)^{1/4}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi}\Big|\sum_{P/2\leq p\leq P}\chi(p)\Big|^{4}\Big)^{1/4}.

We now estimate each of these terms.
By Parseval’s identity,

\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi}|\widehat{\lambda}(\chi)|^{2}=\varphi(q)\leq q.

For
\chi\neq\chi_{0},\chi_{q}
, the character
\chi^{2}
is non-principal
(since
\chi_{q}
is the only non-trivial character of order
2

when
q
is prime) and primitive (since
q
is prime).
Therefore by Poisson summation (Lemma
2
),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\chi^{2}(n)W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{N\tau(\chi^{2})}{q}\sum_{m}\overline{\chi^{2}}(m)\,\widehat{W}\!\Bigl(\frac{mN}{q}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{N\tau(\chi^{2})}{q}\sum_{|m|\leq q^{1+\sigma}/N}\overline{\chi^{2}}(m)\widehat{W}\Big(\frac{mN}{q}\Big)+O_{\sigma,A}(q^{-A})

for any
A>0
and any
0<\sigma<\varepsilon
, owing to the rapid decay
\widehat{W}(x)\ll_{A}(1+|x|)^{-A}
of
\widehat{W}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0},\chi_{q}}\Big|\sum_{n}\chi^{2}(n)W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)\Big|^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{4}}{q^{2}\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0},\chi_{q}}\Big|\sum_{|m|\leq q^{1+\sigma}/N}\overline{\chi^{2}}(m)\widehat{W}\Big(\frac{mN}{q}\Big)\Big|^{4}+O_{\sigma}(1)

Since
q
is prime, for each character
\psi
modulo
q
there are at most two characters
\chi
such that
\psi=\chi^{2}
. We can therefore bound the above sum by a sum over all the characters
\psi
modulo
q
, i.e,

\leq\frac{2N^{4}}{q^{2}\varphi(q)}\sum_{\psi}\Big|\sum_{|m|\leq q^{1+\sigma}/N}\psi(m)\widehat{W}\Big(\frac{mN}{q}\Big)\Big|^{4}+O_{\sigma}(1).

By the large sieve, this is

\ll_{\sigma}\frac{N^{4}}{q^{3}}\cdot\Big(q+\Big(\frac{q^{1+\sigma}}{N}\Big)^{2}\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{q^{1+\sigma}}{N}\Big)^{2}\cdot q^{\sigma}\ll N^{2}q^{3\sigma}

since
\sigma\leq\varepsilon
.
Finally, by the large sieve,

\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi}\Big|\sum_{P/2\leq p\leq P}\chi(p)\Big|^{4}\ll\frac{1}{q}\cdot(P^{2}+q)P^{2}\ll P^{2}.

Combining these together it follows that the contribution of all characters
\chi\neq\chi_{0},\chi_{q}
to (
4
) is

\ll_{\sigma}q^{1/2}\cdot\sqrt{N}q^{3\sigma/4}\cdot\sqrt{P}=\sqrt{qNP}\cdot q^{3\sigma/4},

for any
\sigma\in(0,\varepsilon)
.
Combining everything together, we conclude that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
P/2\leq p\leq P\\
(np,q)=1\end{subarray}}W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)\leq\frac{|\widehat{\lambda}(\chi_{q})|}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
P/2\leq p\leq P\\
(np,q)=1\end{subarray}}W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)+O_{\sigma}(\sqrt{qNP}q^{3\sigma/4})

Since

\frac{NP}{\log P}\asymp\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
P/2\leq p\leq P\\
(np,q)=1\end{subarray}}W\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)

and
NP=q^{1+\varepsilon}

we conclude, upon taking
\sigma=\varepsilon/6<\varepsilon
, that

1\leq\frac{|\widehat{\lambda}(\chi_{q})|}{\varphi(q)}+O_{\varepsilon}(q^{-\varepsilon/4}).

Since

\widehat{\lambda}(\chi_{q})=\sum_{1\leq n<q}\lambda(n)\chi_{q}(n)

and
\lambda(n)\chi_{q}(n)\in\{-1,1\}
for all
1\leq n<q
,
it follows that

\lambda(n)=\kappa^{\prime}\chi_{q}(n)

for all
n\leq q
with at most
\ll_{\varepsilon}q^{1-\varepsilon/4}
exceptions.
∎

In the case
\kappa^{\prime}=1
we can obtain a stronger conclusion.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\delta>0
and let
q
be a prime, sufficiently large
with respect to
1/\delta
.
Let
L\geq q^{3/2}
.
Suppose that

(i)

\lambda(n)=\chi_{q}(n)
for all but at most

O_{\delta}(q^{1-\delta})
integers
n\leq q
with
(n,q)=1
;

(ii)

\lambda(n+q)=\lambda(n)
for all

(n,q)=1
with
n\leq L
.

Then for all
N\leq q
,

E(N)\;\ll_{\delta}\;Nq^{-3\delta/4},

where
E(N)
denotes the number of primes
N/2\leq p\leq N

with
\chi_{q}(p)=1
.
In particular,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq q\\
\chi_{q}(p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\;\ll_{\delta}\;q^{-\delta/2}.

Proof.

Suppose that
q\leq L^{\prime}\leq L
.
An integer
n\leq L
with
(n,q)=1
has

\lambda(n)\neq\chi_{q}(n)
if and only if its residue
class modulo
q
is one of the
O(q^{1-\delta})

exceptional classes from (i)
(by the periodicity of both
\lambda
and
\chi_{q}
stemming from (ii)),
and each class contributes at most
L^{\prime}/q+1\ll L^{\prime}/q

representatives up to
L^{\prime}
. Thus for any
L^{\prime}
with
q\leq L^{\prime}\leq L
,

(5)

\displaystyle\#\{n\leq L^{\prime}:\lambda(n)\neq\chi_{q}(n)\}\ll L^{\prime}q^{-\delta}.

Define

\displaystyle Q^{+}_{N}

\displaystyle=\{N/2<p\leq N:p\text{ prime},\chi_{q}(p)=1\},

\displaystyle Q^{-}_{R}

\displaystyle=\{R/2<r\leq R:r\text{ prime},\chi_{q}(r)=-1\}.

Then
E(N)=|Q^{+}_{N}|
.
For each
p\in Q^{+}_{N}
and
r\in Q^{-}_{R}
,
1=\lambda(pr)\neq\chi_{q}(pr)=-1
,
so that
pr
is counted on the left side of (
5
) with
L^{\prime}=RN
.
As the products
pr
are distinct,
it follows from (
5
) that

(6)

E(N)|Q^{-}_{R}|\ll_{\delta}RNq^{-\delta}\qquad(q\leq NR\leq L).

Firstly, take
R=q
. By (i),
Q^{-}_{q}
contains all but
O_{\delta}(q^{1-\delta})
primes in
(q/2,q]
, so
|Q^{-}_{q}|\gg\pi(q)\gg q/\log q
and we conclude that

(7)

E(N)\ll_{\delta}Nq^{-\delta}\log q\ll_{\delta}Nq^{-3\delta/4}\qquad(1\leq N\leq L/q).

Next, take
R=L/q
. By (
7
),
Q_{R}^{-}
contains all but
O_{\delta}(Rq^{-3\delta/4})

primes in
(R/2,R]
, and hence
|Q_{R}^{-}|\gg\pi(R)\gg R/\log R\gg R/\log q
.
Hence, by (
6
),

(8)

E(N)\ll_{\delta}Nq^{-\delta}\log q\ll_{\delta}Nq^{-3\delta/4}\qquad(q^{2}/L\leq N\leq q).

as
L\geq q^{3/2}
, the ranges in (
7
) and (
8
) include all
N\in[1,q]
.
Finally, let

E^{\star}(N):=\#\{p\leq N:\chi_{q}(p)=1\}\leq\sum_{2^{k}\leq 2N}E(2^{k})\ll_{\delta}Nq^{-3\delta/4}.

Partial summation gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq q\\
\chi_{q}(p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=\frac{E^{\star}(q)}{q}+\int_{1}^{q}\frac{E^{\star}(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt\ll_{\delta}\frac{1}{q^{3\delta/4}}+\frac{\log q}{q^{3\delta/4}}\ll_{\delta}q^{-\delta/2}.\qed

We are finally ready to prove the theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
q
be a prime, sufficiently large
with respect to
1/\varepsilon
.
Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary it suffices to prove the theorem
with
q^{5/2+4\varepsilon}
in place of
q^{5/2+\varepsilon}
.
Moreover we can also assume without loss of generality that
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

Suppose for contradiction that there exists a residue class
a

coprime to
q
and a sign
\kappa\in\{-1,+1\}
such that

\lambda(n)=\kappa
for every
n\equiv a\pmod{q}
with

n\leq q^{5/2+4\varepsilon}
.
By Lemma
7
(with
N=q^{5/2+4\varepsilon}
),

(9)

\lambda(n+q)=\lambda(n)\quad\text{for all }(n,q)=1\text{ with }n\leq q^{3/2+3\varepsilon}.

By Lemma
8
(with
L=q^{3/2+3\varepsilon}
),
there exists
\kappa^{\prime}\in\{-1,+1\}
such that

(10)

\lambda(n)=\kappa^{\prime}\,\chi_{q}(n)\quad\text{for all but at most }O_{\varepsilon}(q^{1-\varepsilon/4})\text{ integers }n\leq q\text{ with }(n,q)=1,

where
\chi_{q}
is the Legendre symbol with modulus
q
.
The remainder of the proof shows that neither sign of
\kappa^{\prime}

is compatible with (
10
).

Case
\kappa^{\prime}=-1
.

In this case
\lambda(n)=-\chi_{q}(n)
for almost all

n\leq q
with
(n,q)=1
.

We observe that for any two primes
p_{1},p_{2}
with

\chi_{q}(p_{1})=\chi_{q}(p_{2})
, the product
p_{1}p_{2}

satisfies
1=\lambda(p_{1}p_{2})\neq-\chi_{q}(p_{1}p_{2})
.
Let
F
denote the number of primes
p\leq q^{1/2}

with
\chi_{q}(p)=+1
and let
G
denote the number
of primes
p\leq q^{1/2}
with
\chi_{q}(p)=-1
.
Since
p\neq q
for all such primes,
\chi_{q}(p)\in\{-1,+1\}
,
so
F+G=\pi(q^{1/2})\gg q^{1/2}/\log q
.
The products
p_{1}p_{2}
with
p_{1}<p_{2}\leq q^{1/2}

and
\chi_{q}(p_{1})=\chi_{q}(p_{2})=+1
are distinct
(by unique factorization), satisfy
p_{1}p_{2}\leq q
,
and all have
\lambda(p_{1}p_{2})\neq-\chi_{q}(p_{1}p_{2})

by the observation above.
By (
10
), the number
of integers
n\leq q
with
(n,q)=1
and

\lambda(n)\neq-\chi_{q}(n)
is
O(q^{1-\varepsilon/4})
,
so
\binom{F}{2}\ll q^{1-\varepsilon/4}
.
The same argument with
\chi_{q}(p_{1})=\chi_{q}(p_{2})=-1

gives
\binom{G}{2}\ll q^{1-\varepsilon/4}
.
Hence
F,G\ll q^{1/2-\varepsilon/8}
, and therefore

\pi(q^{1/2})=F+G\ll q^{1/2-\varepsilon/8}
,
contradicting
\pi(q^{1/2})\gg q^{1/2}/\log q
.

Case
\kappa^{\prime}=+1
.

In this case
\lambda(n)=\chi_{q}(n)
for almost all

n\leq q
with
(n,q)=1
.

Since
\lambda(p)=-1
the condition
\lambda(p)=\chi_{q}(p)
forces

\chi_{q}(p)=-1
for
almost all primes
p\leq q
.
To exploit this, consider the multiplicative function

g(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\chi_{q}(d)=(1\ast\chi_{q})(n)
.
Since
g(p^{k})=1+\chi_{q}(p)+\cdots+\chi_{q}(p)^{k}
,
we have
g(p^{k})=0
whenever
\chi_{q}(p)=-1
and
k
is
odd.
Hence
g(n)=0
for every non-square
n
whose prime
factors all satisfy
\chi_{q}(p)=-1
. In addition
g(n)\geq 0
for all
n
.

Every
n<q
with
g(n)\neq 0
is therefore either
a perfect square or has a prime factor in

Q:=\{p\leq q:p\text{ prime},\;\chi_{q}(p)=1\}
.
The squares contribute

\sum_{m^{2}\leq q}|g(m^{2})|\leq\sum_{m\leq\sqrt{q}}\tau(m^{2})\ll\sqrt{q}\,(\log q)^{O(1)}
.
For the remaining terms, since

|g(n)|\leq\tau(n)
and
\tau(pm)\leq 2\tau(m)
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<q\\
\exists\,p\in Q:\,p\mid n\end{subarray}}|g(n)|\;\leq\;\sum_{p\in Q}\sum_{m<q/p}\tau(pm)\;\ll\;q\log q\sum_{p\in Q}\frac{1}{p}\;\ll_{\varepsilon}\;q^{1-\varepsilon/9},

where the final bound follows from
Lemma
9

(applied with
\delta=\varepsilon/4
,
whose hypotheses are
verified by (
10
)
and (
9
)),
which gives
\sum_{p\in Q}1/p\ll_{\varepsilon}q^{-\varepsilon/8}
,
and the fact that
q\log q\cdot q^{-\varepsilon/8}\ll q^{1-\varepsilon/9}

for
q
sufficiently large.
Hence, for any smooth function
0\leq W\leq 1
, compactly supported in
[1/2,1]
and with
\widehat{W}(0)>0
,

(11)

\sum_{n}g(n)W\Big(\frac{n}{q}\Big)\leq\sum_{n<q}g(n)\;\ll_{\varepsilon}\;q^{1-\varepsilon/9}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
5
,

\sum_{n}g(n)W\Big(\frac{n}{q}\Big)=\widehat{W}(0)q\,L(1,\chi_{q})+O(\sqrt{q}).

By Siegel’s theorem (Lemma
3
),

L(1,\chi_{q})\gg_{\varepsilon}q^{-\varepsilon/10}
, so

(12)

\sum_{n}g(n)W\Big(\frac{n}{q}\Big)\;\gg_{\varepsilon}\;q^{1-\varepsilon/10}.

The bounds (
11
) and (
12
) are
contradictory for
q
large enough in terms of

1/\varepsilon
, since
\varepsilon/9>\varepsilon/10
.

Since both cases lead to contradictions, the initial
assumption is false, and Theorem
1
follows.
∎
