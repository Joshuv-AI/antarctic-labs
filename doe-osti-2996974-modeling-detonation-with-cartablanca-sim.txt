Modeling detonation with CartaBlanca
 simulations
 David Torres

 Abstract
 The accurate modeling of high explosive (HE) detonation and the con-
sequent large solid deformation, failure, plastic flow, porosity growth, and
shock wave propagation is important because simulations can capture spatial
and temporal features that experimental diagnostics cannot capture.
 However, the simulation of the explosive event poses challenges to a
computational scientist. These include the accurate modeling of ductile
damage, crack formation, plastic deformation, as well as physical and nu-
merical instabilities. The material response can be history-dependent and
subject to large material deformation.
 Our research simulates the impact of a high explosive (Detasheet) onto
a tantulum metal plate. We performed the simulations using CartaBlanca
at different mesh resolutions. We decided the study would be impactful if
we perform the simulations with the Material Point Method. Differences
were observed at the different mesh resolutions in velocity and nodal stress
magnitude, so increased mesh resolutions may be required. In addition, the
Discontinuous Galerkin method would be needed to account for the large
gas expansion.

 1 Introduction
 CartaBlanca is a code designed to solve nonlinear multi-material interac-
 tion problems on unstructured grids and is capable of simulating shocks in
 materials. The code uses the material point method (MPM) [1] to model
 fluid/solid as well as solid/solid interactions which can produce deforma-
 tions, damage, and failure [2, 3].
 Due to potential history dependent large deformation of the solid, MPM
 uses Lagrangian particles that move within a fixed Eulerian mesh. These
 history-dependent quantities, such as the stress, and the deformation gradi-
 ent, are stored at the particles, while the Eulerian mesh is used to perform
 numerical differentiations. The dual domain material point (DDMP) method
 [4] improves upon the traditional material point method by smoothing the
 gradient of the shape function to reduce numerical noise as the particles
 move across cell boundaries and has been shown to produce more accurate
 and stable numerical results than the traditional material point methods in
 the case where a tantulum slab is impacted by a flyer plate [5].

 3 Future work
 Future work will involve using the Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method to
 simulate the impact of the HE on the tantalum which will exploit the shock-
 capturing capabilities of the code. In addition, we plan to use TEPLA [11] as
 the plasticity model for tantalum. The model has already been implemented
 in Cartablanca as part of the project but will require testing. In addition,
 the HE will be detonated using a burn model in which a detonation wave
 propagates through the HE.

 4 Impact on Laboratory
 Our simulations demonstrate the capabilities of Cartablanca. The availabil-
 ity of the TEPLA plasticity model (once tested) will enhance the capabilities
 of Cartablanca and its use in modeling material fracture induced by high ex-
 plosives detonation.

 5 Conclusions
 The simulations were useful in demonstrating the capabilities of the Material
 Point Method in Cartablanca and the need to use the Discontinuous Galerkin
 method to accurately model the large gas expansion Unfortunately, we were
 not able to compare with experimental results since the JWL model and
 the TEPLA model needed to be implemented in Cartablanca. In addition,
 we noted differences in the velocity and nodal stress magnitude at different
 mesh resolutions. Simulations at different mesh resolutions are ongoing and
 planned.
