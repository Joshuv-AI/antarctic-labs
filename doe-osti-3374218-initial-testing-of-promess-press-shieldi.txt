Memorandum
Q-5

Subject: Initial Testing of Promess Press Shielding

Testing of the initial design for the shielding to be added to the Promess servo press installed in TA9-
0032 was completed on April 28, 2026. This shielding design did not provide satisfactory protection to
either workers or equipment within the bay when tested at an explosive quantity of 82.5g. This memo
details the design, testing method, and results of the test.

Shielding Design

The design of the shielding was based on the design of the existing Carver hydraulic press shielding,
consisting of a welded steel rectangular tube construction with impact resistant panels on 4 sides. That
initial shielding testing was completed by Alan Novak in 2019. That design required adaptations to be
able to be installed and secured to the new press.

The design of the new shielding is in the attachments. The tubing construction consisted of 1.5 x 0.75 x
0.125” steel tubing, welded continuously on the outer sections and skip 1.5 - 6” on interior sections. The
welds were visually inspected and at least 0.125” deep. This steel frame held four UL level 3
polycarbonate plates (McMaster 84805K54) machined to nest inside the frame. These were added to all
faces, replacing the fiberglass laminate on the original design to provide greater visibility into operations.
The door on the press was attached using a welded piano hinge (McMaster 15665A304). The door was
latched using two DESTACO 374 latch clamps reaching to corresponding hooks on the right side of the
shielding. The hooks were screwed into plates welded to the steel frame. The overall frame was centered
in the press superstructure using two-piece clamps in the corner, bracing on each of the four corner posts.
The top front clamps were replaced by a central bar, allowing the shielding to be placed in the press
without press disassembly.

Test Method

The shielding testing was planned to exceed the requirement for shielding testing laid out in DOE-STD-
1212-2025 § 17.2.2 stating that blast shields may be approved after passing a test of 125% of the rated
explosive charge in the experimental setup. For this operation, a test at 150% the maximum event was
conducted. The maximum quantity used for this calculation was the planned mini-cupcake experiment,
which has a maximum mass of 55 g. This led to a charge of 82.5 g being selected.

A sacrificial 1” diameter die body taken from service was used for confinement of the explosive charge.
The charge was assembled from two PBX 9501 1” diameter cores, each measuring approximately 2” long
originally but cut down each to ~1.875” long to add to a total mass of 81.875 g. The charge was initiated
by a RISI RP-1 detonator, bringing the total mass to 82.5 g. The charge was also confined on the top and
bottom by 1” thick 6 x 6” square plates to replicate the pressing conditions. The charge assembly is
shown in Figure 1 both in preparation and staged in the test.

The primary diagnostic for this test was a Phantom TMX 7510 color high-speed camera. This was
mounted in an Armag outside the firing point and directed at the test using a turning mirror. The camera
was recording at 76000 frames per second with an exposure of 2 µs per frame at a resolution of 1200 x
800 pixels. A secondary camcorder was placed on the perimeter of the firing site to record the late-time
effects of the blast.

The front door of the shielding was traveling approximately 27.25 ft/s at 10 ft. from the front shielding, as
estimated by the distance of the pencil probe and the timing of the site camera. The shielding tended to

fail from the bottom on the right side, peeling up and breaking welds. This is likely due to the difference
in shot placement within the assembly, as the testing in the Carver press shielding was done with the shot
mostly centered in the assembly. Further reinforcement of the bottom of the assembly must be done for
this test to pass.

Additionally, the lack of an adequate vent path for product gasses increased the trapped pressure within
the shielding, causing the sides to be exposed to greater force than the Carver press shielding. Potentially
allowing the rear or left sides of the shielding to be open (those sides that are facing the wall) would help
significantly with the dynamic pressure build-up.

Pressure Data
Pressure data from the 4 pressure transducers was much lower than expected for this test, shown in Table
2. The pressure onset for the probes within the shielding occurred at the expected time correlated with the
timing in the video. The peak pressure within the case was well over 370 psi, but it is difficult to extract
pressure data due to the potential for fragment interference with transducer 1. The best estimate for this
probe is shown in the table. The plots for the probes are shown in Figure 12.

Conclusion

The over-test for the shielding designed for the Promess servo press was successful but did not yield the
desired outcome. A few design aspects need to be changed for the shielding to survive the test, but this
test did provide valuable information about die fragmentation and overpressure that operators could be
exposed to in an event. Future testing should incorporate design changes and encompass more tightly the
actual limit for class 2 operations as well.
