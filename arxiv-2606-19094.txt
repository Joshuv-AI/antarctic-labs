Magnetic Field Alignment of Young Stellar Object Motions in Nearby Star-Forming Regions
S.G. Ansari

Abstract
Magnetic fields are widely believed to play an important role in molecular-cloud evolution and star formation. However, the extent to which newly formed stars retain a dynamical relationship with the magnetic environments in which they formed remains poorly understood. In this study, we investigate the relationship between the proper motions of young stellar objects (YSOs) and local magnetic-field orientations in seven nearby molecular-cloud regions. Proper motions were obtained from Gaia Data Release 3, while magnetic-field orientations were derived from Planck 353 GHz dust-polarization measurements. For each YSO, we computed the angular separation Δ ​ θ \Delta\theta between the projected proper-motion vector and the local magnetic-field orientation in Galactic coordinates. A total of 2,037 YSOs were analyzed across Chamaeleon I, Perseus, Ophiuchus, Orion South, Orion North, Taurus, and Lupus. Significant departures from random orientation distributions are detected in every cloud. However, the preferred alignment varies strongly among regions. Chamaeleon I, Perseus, Ophiuchus, and Orion South exhibit preferential alignment between stellar motions and magnetic fields, whereas Orion North, Taurus, and Lupus exhibit preferentially perpendicular orientations. The median angular separation spans a continuous range from 15.0 ∘ 15.0^{\circ} in Chamaeleon I to 67.6 ∘ 67.6^{\circ} in Lupus. These results demonstrate that the relationship between YSO motions and magnetic fields is not universal but depends strongly on the local star-forming environment. The findings suggest that young stellar populations retain measurable kinematic signatures of their natal magnetic environments and provide a new observational framework for investigating the role of magnetic fields in star formation.

Introduction

Magnetic fields constitute one of the fundamental physical components governing the evolution of molecular clouds and the formation of stars

Observations obtained by the Planck satellite have revealed that molecular clouds are permeated by highly structured and often remarkably ordered magnetic fields

If magnetic fields significantly influence the motions of protostars and young stars, one might expect stellar proper motions to exhibit preferred orientations relative to local magnetic-field directions. Conversely, if stellar motions rapidly decouple from their birth environments, no such preferred alignment should remain. The combination of Gaia astrometry and Planck polarization measurements now permits a direct observational test of this hypothesis.

The principal goal of this study is therefore to determine whether YSO motions exhibit systematic relationships with local magnetic fields and whether such relationships are universal among molecular clouds or depend upon local environmental conditions. As we show below, the surveyed clouds occupy a continuous sequence of alignment behaviours extending from strong alignment to strong perpendicularity, indicating that the relationship between stellar motions and magnetic fields is strongly cloud dependent.

The principal objective of this investigation is to determine whether the motions of young stellar objects retain measurable signatures of the magnetic environments in which they formed. Addressing this question requires a sample of molecular clouds spanning a broad range of physical conditions, evolutionary stages, and star-formation characteristics.

Seven nearby molecular-cloud regions were selected for analysis: Chamaeleon I, Perseus, Ophiuchus, Orion South, Orion North, Taurus, and Lupus. These regions represent some of the best-studied sites of nearby star formation and collectively encompass a wide range of cloud masses, stellar populations, gas densities, and magnetic-field morphologies.

The selection of these clouds was guided by three primary criteria. First, reliable and well-characterized YSO catalogues had to be available. Second, a substantial fraction of the YSO population needed to possess Gaia DR3 astrometric measurements of sufficient quality for proper-motion analysis. Third, the cloud had to be covered by Planck polarization observations capable of providing magnetic-field orientation information.

The chosen sample includes both relatively quiescent low-mass star-forming regions and the much more active Orion complex. Chamaeleon I, Taurus, Lupus, Perseus, and Ophiuchus are generally regarded as nearby low-mass star-forming clouds characterized by filamentary structures, modest stellar populations, and limited feedback from massive stars. These regions provide an opportunity to investigate magnetic-field alignment under conditions where gravitational collapse and filamentary evolution dominate the dynamics of the cloud.

The Orion molecular-cloud complex represents a markedly different environment. Orion contains large stellar populations, active massive-star formation, strong radiative feedback, expanding bubbles, and a complex network of molecular structures. Previous studies have shown that the northern and southern portions of Orion exhibit distinct structural and dynamical characteristics
(
6
)
. For this reason, Orion North and Orion South were treated as independent subsamples throughout the analysis.

The resulting cloud sample is therefore not intended to be statistically complete, but rather physically representative. By including clouds with substantially different properties, the survey is designed to determine whether any observed alignment between stellar motions and magnetic fields constitutes a universal phenomenon or whether the relationship depends upon local environmental conditions.

Data

3.1
Young Stellar Object Catalogues

The seven molecular clouds analyzed in this study span a broad range of star-forming environments, from relatively quiescent low-mass regions such as Chamaeleon I and Taurus to the more active Orion complex. This diversity of environments provides an opportunity to investigate whether the relationship between stellar motions and magnetic-field orientations is universal or dependent upon local cloud conditions.

The YSO samples were assembled from published catalogues associated with each molecular cloud. These catalogues provide positions and source classifications for young stellar populations identified through infrared excesses, spectroscopic signatures of youth, X-ray emission, or combinations thereof. The final sample comprises 2,037 YSOs distributed among the seven cloud regions.

The YSO samples used in this investigation

The use of previously published YSO catalogues ensures that the source selection is based upon well-established membership criteria developed specifically for each cloud. Although the precise identification methods differ somewhat among catalogues, all selected samples are widely accepted representations of the young stellar populations associated with their respective molecular clouds.

For each cloud, the YSO catalogue was cross-matched with Gaia Data Release 3

3.3
Gaia Proper Motions

Stellar proper motions were obtained from Gaia Data Release 3. Gaia provides the most precise and homogeneous astrometric dataset currently available for nearby star-forming regions, making it uniquely suited for investigating large-scale stellar kinematics.

The proper-motion measurements consist of the two sky-plane components
\mu_{\alpha}
and
\mu_{\delta}
. These quantities were transformed into Galactic coordinates in order to facilitate direct comparison with magnetic-field orientations derived from Planck polarization maps.

For each YSO, the transformed Galactic proper-motion components were used to calculate the projected direction of motion on the sky. Since the present investigation focuses on orientation rather than velocity magnitude, only the direction of the proper-motion vector enters the alignment analysis.

The use of proper-motion directions rather than full space velocities introduces an unavoidable projection effect; however, the large sample sizes available through Gaia provide substantial statistical power for detecting systematic alignment trends.

3.4
Planck Polarization Data

Magnetic-field orientations were derived from Planck 353 GHz polarization maps. At this frequency, thermal emission from interstellar dust grains dominates the observed signal. Because elongated dust grains tend to align with local magnetic fields, the polarization of the emitted radiation provides a powerful tracer of magnetic-field geometry.

The Planck mission has produced the first all-sky polarization maps of sufficient sensitivity and uniformity to enable systematic magnetic-field studies over entire molecular-cloud complexes. These observations therefore provide an ideal dataset for investigating cloud-scale magnetic structures.

For each YSO position, the local Stokes parameters Q and U were extracted from the Planck maps. The polarization angle
\psi
was calculated according to standard convention:

\psi=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{atan2}(U,Q)

(1)

The corresponding magnetic-field orientation was then obtained by rotating the polarization angle by 90 degrees.

The resulting magnetic-field directions represent the large-scale projected magnetic environment surrounding each source rather than the immediate circumstellar field.

4
Methodology

The fundamental quantity investigated in this study is the angular separation between the projected stellar motion and the projected magnetic-field orientation. This quantity provides a direct measure of the degree to which stellar motions are aligned with, or perpendicular to, the local magnetic field.

For each YSO, the proper-motion vector was transformed into Galactic coordinates, yielding the components
\mu_{l}
,
\cos b
and
\mu_{b}
. These components were used to compute the projected direction of stellar motion.

The local magnetic-field orientation was obtained from the Planck polarization data at the position of the source. Both quantities were therefore expressed within the same Galactic coordinate system, ensuring that the resulting angle measurements are directly comparable.

The angular separation
\Delta\theta
between the stellar motion vector and the magnetic-field orientation was then calculated. Because magnetic fields are axial quantities rather than directional vectors, orientations separated by
180^{\circ}
are physically equivalent. Consequently, all angular separations were folded into the interval
0^{\circ}\leq\Delta\theta\leq 90^{\circ}
.

Within this framework:

•

\Delta\theta
=
0^{\circ}
corresponds to perfect alignment between the stellar motion and magnetic field.

•

\Delta\theta
=
90^{\circ}
corresponds to perfect perpendicularity.

•

\Delta\theta
=
45^{\circ}
represents the expectation for a random distribution of orientations.

The resulting
\Delta\theta
values were computed individually for every source in the survey.

The distributions of
\Delta\theta
were then analyzed separately for each molecular cloud. Summary statistics including the mean, median, and standard deviation were calculated in order to characterize the dominant alignment behaviour of each region.

To assess statistical significance, each cloud distribution was compared with a uniform distribution representing random orientations. This comparison was performed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) test. The KS statistic D measures the maximum difference between the observed cumulative distribution and the expected cumulative distribution under the null hypothesis of randomness. Corresponding p-values quantify the probability that the observed distribution could arise by chance.

Finally, pairwise comparisons between molecular clouds were performed using two-sample KS tests. These comparisons allow the identification of statistically distinct alignment regimes and provide a quantitative measure of the differences between clouds.

The methodology adopted here intentionally avoids assumptions regarding the physical mechanisms responsible for the observed alignments. The analysis is therefore purely observational and focuses exclusively on establishing whether statistically significant relationships exist between stellar motions and magnetic-field orientations.

The results reveal two important conclusions. First, every cloud investigated exhibits a statistically significant departure from a random orientation distribution. This indicates that stellar motions are not randomly oriented with respect to magnetic fields. Second, the preferred orientation varies substantially from cloud to cloud. Rather than converging toward a single alignment state, the clouds occupy a broad continuum extending from strong alignment to strong perpendicularity.

5.2
Individual Cloud Results

•

Chamaeleon I

Chamaeleon I exhibits the strongest alignment observed in the survey. The median angular separation of
15.0^{\circ}
indicates that the majority of YSO proper-motion vectors are oriented approximately parallel to the local magnetic field.

The corresponding Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic (
D=0.532
) and extremely small p-value (
3.0\times 10^{-23}
) demonstrate that the observed distribution differs dramatically from a random orientation distribution.

The strong alignment observed in Chamaeleon I may indicate that stellar motions remain closely coupled to the large-scale magnetic-field structure. As a relatively low-mass and dynamically quiet cloud, Chamaeleon I experiences limited disruption from stellar feedback, potentially allowing magnetic-field-guided gas flows to remain imprinted on the motions of newly formed stars.

•

Perseus

Although more active than Chamaeleon I, Perseus retains a clear alignment signature. This behaviour may indicate that magnetic fields continue to influence gas flows on cloud scales despite the presence of multiple star-forming subregions. The weaker alignment relative to Chamaeleon I could reflect a greater contribution from local turbulence or dynamical interactions.

•

Ophiuchus

Ophiuchus occupies an intermediate position within the alignment sequence. The cloud may represent a regime in which magnetic-field-guided motions and gravitationally-driven motions contribute with comparable importance. The resulting distribution is therefore less strongly aligned than in Chamaeleon I or Perseus. The cloud therefore appears to represent a transitional state between strong alignment and more weakly organized behaviour.

•

Orion South

The presence of alignment within Orion South demonstrates that strong magnetic coupling is not restricted to low-mass clouds. Despite the complex environment of the Orion molecular cloud, the observed alignment suggests that large-scale gas flows remain preferentially organized with respect to the magnetic field. This result indicates that magnetic influence can persist even in regions experiencing substantial star formation activity.

Given the large sample size of 502 YSOs, the statistical significance of the result remains extremely high.

•

Orion North

Orion North marks the point at which the alignment sequence crosses from preferential alignment into preferential perpendicularity. The contrast with Orion South is particularly striking because both regions belong to the same giant molecular-cloud complex. This transition suggests that local environmental conditions, rather than global cloud properties alone, may determine the relationship between stellar motions and magnetic fields.

•

Taurus

Taurus exhibits one of the strongest perpendicular signatures in the survey. Previous analysis within Taurus suggests that the tendency toward perpendicular orientations becomes stronger in regions of higher extinction. This behaviour may indicate that gravitational flows across magnetic field lines become increasingly important within denser portions of the cloud.

•

Lupus

Lupus occupies the opposite end of the alignment sequence from Chamaeleon I. The predominance of large angular separations suggests that stellar motions are preferentially oriented across the local magnetic field. Such behaviour may arise if gravitational contraction, filamentary accretion flows, or cloud geometry dominate the stellar kinematics more strongly than magnetic-field-guided motions.

Taken together, the cloud results suggest that no single alignment regime characterizes all star-forming environments. Instead, molecular clouds appear to occupy different positions along a continuous sequence extending from strong alignment to strong perpendicularity. The physical origin of this sequence remains uncertain, but likely reflects varying balances between magnetic forces, gravitational collapse, turbulence, and stellar feedback.

The systematic displacement of the full distributions reinforces the conclusion that the observed cloud-to-cloud differences reflect genuine differences in the underlying stellar populations rather than statistical fluctuations or a small number of outlying sources.

6
Discussion

6.1
A New Observational Perspective on Magnetic Fields and Stellar Kinematics

Magnetic fields have long been recognized as important agents in molecular-cloud evolution and star formation. Numerous observational and theoretical studies have demonstrated that magnetic fields influence gas dynamics, filament formation, core collapse, and the redistribution of angular momentum.

However, most previous investigations have focused on the relationship between magnetic fields and the interstellar medium itself. Comparatively little attention has been devoted to the motions of the young stars that emerge from these environments.

The present study approaches the problem from a different perspective. Rather than examining the orientation of gas structures relative to magnetic fields, we investigate the orientation of stellar motions relative to magnetic fields.

The distinction is important. Gas structures provide information about the physical conditions under which stars form. Stellar motions, by contrast, provide information about how newly formed stars subsequently move through their natal environments.

The results indicate that YSO proper motions retain measurable information about the magnetic-field geometry of their parent clouds. In every cloud examined, the observed orientation distribution differs significantly from the expectation for random motion.

This finding alone suggests that the influence of magnetic fields does not disappear immediately after star formation but remains encoded in the kinematics of young stellar populations.

6.2
The Existence of Multiple Alignment Regimes

Perhaps the most surprising result of this survey is the existence of both alignment and perpendicularity regimes.

Prior to the analysis, one might reasonably have expected a single preferred behaviour. If magnetic fields systematically guide stellar motions, then all clouds might exhibit preferential alignment. Alternatively, if gravitational collapse and turbulent motions dominate stellar dynamics, then perpendicular orientations might be expected to prevail.

Instead, the observations reveal a much more complex picture.

Chamaeleon I, Perseus, Ophiuchus, and Orion South exhibit preferential alignment between stellar motions and magnetic fields. Orion North, Taurus, and Lupus exhibit preferentially perpendicular orientations.

The coexistence of both regimes demonstrates that the relationship between stellar motions and magnetic fields is not universal.

Instead, the observed behaviour appears to depend on the physical conditions present within each cloud.

This conclusion is particularly important because it suggests that magnetic-field alignment is not a simple binary phenomenon but rather part of a broader continuum of dynamical behaviours.

The nearly continuous progression observed from Chamaeleon I to Lupus supports this interpretation.

6.3
The Alignment Sequence

One of the most striking outcomes of the survey is the ordered sequence formed by the molecular clouds when ranked according to their median angular separation.

The progression extends from approximately
15^{\circ}
in Chamaeleon I to nearly
68^{\circ}
in Lupus and includes intermediate values in Perseus, Ophiuchus, Orion South, Orion North, and Taurus.

The existence of this sequence suggests that the clouds do not fall naturally into two distinct populations. Instead, they appear to occupy different positions along a continuum.

Such behaviour may indicate that a common set of physical processes operates in all clouds, with the observed alignment reflecting different balances among those processes.

Potential controlling factors include magnetic-field strength, cloud density, turbulence, feedback from young stars, cloud geometry, and evolutionary state.

The present data do not permit these possibilities to be disentangled. Nevertheless, the existence of a continuous alignment sequence strongly suggests that cloud properties influence the degree of coupling between stellar motions and magnetic fields.

Understanding the origin of this sequence represents an important challenge for future theoretical work.

These comparisons demonstrate that the alignment sequence is not driven solely by differences in median values. Instead, the entire distributions shift progressively from cloud to cloud. The systematic nature of these shifts reinforces the interpretation that the observed cloud-to-cloud differences reflect genuine variations in the coupling between stellar motions and magnetic fields rather than statistical fluctuations or a small number of outlying objects.

6.5
The Orion Paradox

The Orion complex provides one of the most intriguing results of the survey.

Although Orion North and Orion South belong to the same giant molecular-cloud complex, they occupy opposite sides of the alignment sequence. Orion South exhibits preferential alignment, whereas Orion North exhibits preferential perpendicularity. This result is difficult to reconcile with explanations based solely on global cloud properties. If the alignment behaviour were determined exclusively by large-scale environmental conditions, one might expect both regions to exhibit similar characteristics. Instead, the observations suggest that local conditions within the cloud complex exert a dominant influence.

The contrast between Orion North and Orion South therefore provides strong evidence that alignment behaviour is sensitive to local physical processes. This conclusion reinforces the idea that magnetic-field coupling varies not only between clouds but potentially within individual cloud complexes. The Orion result is particularly important because it demonstrates that the alignment sequence cannot be explained simply as a consequence of cloud-to-cloud differences.

6.6
Magnetic Fields and Filamentary Structure

A possible explanation for the observed diversity of alignment behaviours may lie in the relationship between magnetic fields and molecular-cloud filaments.

Observations from Herschel and Planck have shown that the relative orientation between filaments and magnetic fields varies systematically with gas density. In diffuse environments, filaments often tend to align with magnetic fields, whereas dense filaments frequently appear perpendicular to them.

If YSOs inherit part of their motion from the gas flows within which they form, then the orientation of those flows relative to the magnetic field may influence the resulting stellar kinematics.

In such a scenario, clouds exhibiting aligned stellar motions may be characterized by gas flows that remain coupled to the magnetic field, whereas clouds exhibiting perpendicular motions may be dominated by filamentary or gravitational motions occurring across field lines.

Although the present analysis cannot directly test this hypothesis, the existence of both alignment regimes is qualitatively consistent with the complex relationships observed between filaments and magnetic fields in molecular clouds.

Future comparisons with filament catalogues and column-density maps may help clarify the role of cloud morphology in shaping stellar motions.

6.7
Implications for Star Formation

The results of this survey suggest that magnetic fields influence not only the gas from which stars form but also the subsequent motions of the stars themselves.

This conclusion extends the role of magnetic fields beyond traditional studies of cloud structure and collapse.

If confirmed by future investigations, the observed alignment sequence may provide a new observational diagnostic of star-forming environments. Stellar kinematics could potentially serve as an indirect tracer of magnetic-field coupling, complementing existing techniques based on polarization measurements and Zeeman observations.

More broadly, the results imply that young stellar populations preserve measurable information about the physical conditions present during their formation.

The ability to recover such information from stellar motions opens a new avenue for investigating the interplay between gravity, turbulence, and magnetic fields during the star-formation process.

7
Future Directions

The results presented in this study represent an initial step toward a systematic investigation of the relationship between stellar motions and magnetic fields in star-forming regions.

Although the observed alignment sequence is statistically robust, many questions remain regarding the physical mechanisms responsible for the observed behaviour.

The most immediate extension of this work involves expanding the cloud sample. While the seven molecular clouds analyzed here already reveal a remarkably broad range of alignment regimes, additional clouds would allow the investigation of a wider range of environmental conditions and improve the statistical characterization of the observed sequence.

Particularly valuable would be the inclusion of regions exhibiting different star-formation efficiencies, cloud masses, magnetic-field strengths, and evolutionary states.

A second priority involves incorporating radial velocity measurements. The present analysis is necessarily restricted to projected motions on the plane of the sky. Although the large sample size permits statistically significant conclusions, the true three-dimensional relationship between stellar motions and magnetic fields remains inaccessible for many sources.

Future Gaia releases, together with spectroscopic surveys, may provide sufficient radial velocity information to reconstruct full three-dimensional stellar velocity vectors for substantial YSO populations. Such analyses would provide a more direct test of magnetic-field coupling.

A third avenue involves the investigation of age-dependent effects. The YSO catalogues employed in the present work contain sources spanning a range of evolutionary stages. If magnetic fields influence stellar motions primarily during the earliest phases of star formation, one might expect the alignment signal to weaken as stars age and dynamically decouple from their natal environments.

Conversely, if the observed alignments arise from larger-scale cloud dynamics, similar signatures may persist across a broad range of ages. Separating the samples according to evolutionary class may therefore provide important clues regarding the origin of the observed trends.

The role of cloud structure also deserves further investigation. Molecular clouds exhibit complex networks of filaments, hubs, ridges, and shells. Since both magnetic fields and stellar motions are likely influenced by such structures, future studies should compare YSO kinematics not only with magnetic-field orientations but also with filament geometries derived from Herschel observations.

The extinction dependence observed in Taurus provides an example of the insights that may emerge from such analyses. The tendency toward increasingly perpendicular orientations in regions of higher extinction suggests that cloud density and local environment may influence the degree of magnetic coupling.

Extending this analysis to the remaining molecular clouds represents a natural next step.

Finally, comparisons with magnetohydrodynamic simulations may provide the most powerful route toward physical interpretation. Numerical simulations can simultaneously track gas dynamics, magnetic-field evolution, and stellar motions under controlled conditions. The observational alignment sequence identified in the present work therefore provides a valuable benchmark against which theoretical models may be tested.

Ultimately, a combination of expanded observational surveys and numerical simulations will be required to determine the physical origin of the alignment sequence identified here.

8
Conclusions

The principal result of this investigation is the discovery that young stellar objects in nearby molecular clouds exhibit statistically significant but cloud-dependent alignment patterns with respect to local magnetic fields, spanning a continuous sequence from strong alignment to strong perpendicularity. The existence of this sequence demonstrates that the relationship between stellar motions and magnetic fields is not universal but depends strongly on the physical environment of the parent molecular cloud.

We have conducted a survey of the relationship between young stellar object proper motions and local magnetic-field orientations across seven nearby molecular-cloud regions using Gaia DR3 astrometry and Planck 353 GHz polarization measurements.

The survey includes a total of 2,037 YSOs distributed among Chamaeleon I, Perseus, Ophiuchus, Orion South, Orion North, Taurus, and Lupus. For each source, the angular separation between the projected proper-motion vector and the local magnetic-field orientation was calculated in Galactic coordinates.

The analysis reveals statistically significant departures from random orientation distributions in every molecular cloud examined. This result demonstrates that stellar motions are not randomly oriented relative to local magnetic fields and indicates that young stellar populations retain measurable signatures of their magnetic environments.

The most important result of the survey is the discovery of a systematic progression in alignment behaviour among molecular clouds.

At one end of the sequence, Chamaeleon I exhibits strong alignment between stellar motions and magnetic fields, with a median angular separation of only
15.0^{\circ}
. At the opposite extreme, Lupus exhibits strong perpendicularity, with a median angular separation of
67.6^{\circ}
. Intermediate clouds occupy positions between these two limits, forming a nearly continuous sequence of alignment states.

The existence of both alignment and perpendicularity regimes demonstrates that the relationship between stellar motions and magnetic fields is not universal. Instead, the observed behaviour appears to depend strongly on the local star-forming environment.

Particularly noteworthy is the contrast observed within the Orion complex itself. Orion South exhibits preferential alignment, whereas Orion North exhibits preferential perpendicularity. This result suggests that local physical conditions may be more important than global cloud properties in determining the coupling between stellar motions and magnetic fields.

The present study therefore introduces a new observational perspective on the role of magnetic fields in star formation. Rather than focusing solely on the relationship between magnetic fields and molecular gas, the analysis demonstrates that stellar kinematics can also be used to investigate magnetic-field influence within molecular clouds.

The observed alignment sequence provides a new empirical constraint for theories of star formation and cloud evolution. Explaining the physical origin of this sequence represents an important challenge for future observational and theoretical investigations.

More broadly, the results suggest that young stellar populations preserve measurable dynamical memories of the environments in which they formed. The combination of Gaia astrometry and Planck polarization data therefore offers a powerful new framework for exploring the interplay between stellar motions, molecular-cloud structure, and magnetic fields throughout the star-formation process.
