On a class of Reflected Mean-Field Stochastic Differential Equations with jumps
Mohammed Elhachemy

Abstract
This paper investigates a class of Reflected Mean-Field Stochastic Differential Equations when the noise is driven by a Brownian motion and an independent Poisson measure. We prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions and provide moments estimates for the state processes. We apply our result to derive a Feynman-Kac formula for the solution of an Integral-Partial Differential Equation with Neumann boundary conditions. Key words and phrases. Mean-field, Reflected Stochastic Differential Equation, Jumps, Integral-PDE, Feynman-Kac formula, Neumann boundary condition. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35Q99,60H10, 60H05,60H30,60J60.

In this paper, we are going to study a class of reflected MF-SDEs with jumps defined as follows

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle(i)\penalty\ X_{s}^{t,\zeta}=\zeta+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[b(r,(X_{r}^{t,\zeta})^{\prime},X_{r}^{t,\zeta})]dr+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma(r,(X_{r}^{t,\zeta})^{\prime},X_{r}^{t,\zeta})]dW_{r}\\

(1.2)

where

(A_{s}^{t,\zeta})_{t\leq s\leq T}

(X_{s}^{t,\zeta})_{t\leq s\leq T}

increases only when

This study tackles a gap in the literature by examining particle states subject to mean-field interactions and domain constraints. The incorporation of jumps and reflections into this framework provides a mathematical structure that significantly transcends a mere extension of existing results. This framework enables us to address diverse issues, specifically those related to Integral-PDEs with Neumann boundary conditions, exemplified by the case in
5
.

Our primary contributions in this paper are threefold: we demonstrate the existence and uniqueness of the solution, provide moment estimates, and establish a link between this class of SDEs and the corresponding Integral-PDE with Neumann boundary conditions. Note that the inclusion of the integral with respect to the increasing process
A
, the jumps component, and the mean-field operator complicates the computations relative to classical SDEs. Utilizing a characteristic of the domain
G
(see (
2.3
)) and an appropriate function (see (
3.5
)) to address the Stieltjes integral within the Itô formula, we establish the existence and uniqueness of the solution through a fixed-point argument. We also give some bounds and error estimates for the solutions to (
1.2
), which provide a means to control both the solution itself and the distance between two solutions based on the difference in their associated initial conditions. These estimates will play a crucial role in further researches. Finally, to establish the link with a Neumann boundary value problem for an Integral-PDE, we derive a Markov property for the solution to this kind of SDEs.

To the best of our knowledge, this specific issue has not been addressed in the existing literature, making our conclusion particularly innovative.

More precisely, the rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we introduce basic notations and assumptions. The existence and uniqueness of the solution are proved in Section
3

4
we prove moment bounds and stability estimations. Finally, in Section
5
we provide a Feynman–Kac formula for the solution of an Integral-PDE with Neumann boundary condition.

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}H_{r}\left\{2(X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r})(\overline{\sigma}_{r}-\underline{\sigma}_{r})-\frac{1}{c_{0}}|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r}|^{2}\left(D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})\cdot\overline{\sigma}_{r}+D\varphi(Y^{t,\zeta}_{r})\cdot\underline{\sigma}_{r}\right)\right\}dW_{r}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}H_{r}\left\{2(X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r})D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})-\frac{1}{c_{0}}|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r}|^{2}\right\}dA^{t,\zeta}_{r}

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{s}H_{r}\left\{2(X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r})D\varphi(Y^{t,\zeta}_{r})+\frac{1}{c_{0}}|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r}|^{2}\right\}dK_{r}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{E}H_{r-}\left\{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{c_{0}}\Psi(r)\right)|X^{t,\zeta}_{r-}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r-}+\overline{\gamma}_{r}(e)-\underline{\gamma}_{r}(e)|^{2}-|X^{t,\zeta}_{r-}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r-}|^{2}\right\}\tilde{N}(dr,de)

with
\Psi(r)=\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-}+\overline{\gamma}_{r}(e))-\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})+\varphi(Y^{t,\zeta}_{r-}+\underline{\gamma}_{r}(e))-\varphi(Y^{t,\zeta}_{r-})
.

Remark that, by (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}H_{r}\left\{2(X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r})D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})-\frac{1}{c_{0}}|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r}|^{2}\right\}dA^{t,\zeta}_{r}\leq 0,

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}H_{r}\left\{2(Y^{t,\zeta}_{r}-X^{t,\zeta}_{r})D\varphi(Y^{t,\zeta}_{r})-\frac{1}{c_{0}}|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r}|^{2}\right\}dK^{t,\zeta}_{r}\leq 0.

By Doob’s inequality and considering the boundedness of the exponential term,
\varphi
, as well as its first and second derivatives,
D\varphi
and
D^{2}\varphi
, and the assumptions regarding the coefficients, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq r\leq s}|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r}|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{s}\left(|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r}|^{4}+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}|(U_{r})^{\prime}-(V_{r})^{\prime}|^{4}\right)dr\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{s}\sup_{t\leq u\leq r}|X^{t,\zeta}_{u}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{u}|^{4}du\right]+C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{s}\sup_{t\leq u\leq r}|U_{u}-V_{u}|^{4}du\right].

By Gronwall’s lemma, yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq r\leq s}|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-Y^{t,\zeta}_{r}|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq u\leq r}|U_{u}-V_{u}|^{4}\right]du,

and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq r\leq s}|F(U_{r})-F(V_{r})|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq u\leq r}|U_{u}-V_{u}|^{4}\right]du.

Finally, by applying a standard Picard iteration argument, we establish the existence of a unique fixed point for the operator
F
in
\mathbb{H}
. Since the set
G
is uniformly bounded, any solution to (
1.2
) necessarily belongs to
\mathbb{H}
. Consequently, this ensures the well-posedness and pathwise uniqueness of the solution to (
1.2
). The proof is complete.
∎

4
Moments Estimates

In this part of our paper, we state some moments estimates of the solution
(X^{t,\zeta}_{s},A^{t,\zeta}_{s})_{t\leq s\leq T}
of (
1.2
).

Theorem 4.1
.

For all
t\in[0,T]
, and
\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};\bar{G})
, There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(i)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\underset{s\in[t,T]}{\sup}\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-X^{t,\zeta^{\prime}}_{s}\right|^{4}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C\left|\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}\right|^{4},

(ii)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\underset{s\in[t,T]}{\sup}\left|A^{t,\zeta}_{s}-A^{t^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}}_{s}\right|^{4}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C\left|\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}\right|^{4}.

If moreover we assume that
G
is convex, we have for a constant
C>0

(iii)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\underset{s\in[t,t+\theta]}{\sup}\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-\zeta\right|^{2}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C\theta^{2},\quad a.s.
, for any
0\leq\theta\leq T-t
.

(iv)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\left|A^{t,\zeta}_{t+\theta}\right|^{2}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C\theta^{2},\quad a.s.
, for any
0\leq\theta\leq T-t
,

(v)

\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\mu A^{t,\zeta}_{s}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C(\mu,s),\quad\forall s\in[t,T]
,
\quad\forall\mu>0
.

Proof.

(i). For any
t\leq s\leq T
, applying Itô formula to

\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{c_{0}}(\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{s})+\varphi(X^{t,\zeta^{\prime}}_{s}))\right\}\times|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-X^{t,\zeta^{\prime}}_{s}|^{2},

we derive as in the proof of theorem
3.1

\mathbb{E}\Big[\underset{t\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-X^{t,\zeta^{\prime}}_{s}\right|^{4}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C\left|\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}\right|^{4}+C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-X^{t,\zeta^{\prime}}_{s}\right|^{4}ds\right]

Consequently, by virtue of Gronwall’s lemma, we have the desired outcome.

(ii). From (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle A^{t,\zeta}_{s}=

\displaystyle\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{s})-\varphi(\zeta)

(4.7)

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{s}\Big[D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\mu(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})]\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma^{\top}(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})]D^{2}\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})\Big]dr

\displaystyle\left.-\int_{t}^{s}\int_{E}\Big[\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-}+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\gamma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r-},e)])-\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 42.67912pt-D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\gamma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r-},e)]\Big]\lambda(de)dr\right.

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{s}D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})\cdot\sigma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})dW_{r}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{E}\big[\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-}+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\gamma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r-},e)])-\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})\big]\tilde{N}(dr,de)

From Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality (see
20
, Theorem 48, pp.195) and the properties of
\varphi,\mu,\sigma
and
\gamma
, we have, for
t\leq s\leq T
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\underset{t\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\left|A^{t,\zeta}_{s}-A^{t,\zeta^{\prime}}_{s}\right|^{4}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C\left|\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}\right|^{4}+C\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-X^{t,\zeta^{\prime}}_{s}\right|^{4}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\\
+C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-X^{t,\zeta^{\prime}}_{s}\right|^{4}ds\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

From (i) and Gronwall’s inequality we get the desired result.

(iii). For any
\zeta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};\bar{G})
, by Itô Formula, we get

\displaystyle|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-\zeta^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}|^{2}+2\int_{t}^{s}\left(X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-\zeta^{\prime}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\mu(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})]dr

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})]\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma^{\top}(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})]dr

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{E}|\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\gamma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r-},e)]|^{2}\,\lambda(de)dr

\displaystyle+2\int_{t}^{s}\left(X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-\zeta^{\prime}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})]dW_{r}

\displaystyle+2\int_{t}^{s}\left(X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-\zeta^{\prime}\right)\cdot D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})dA^{t,\zeta}_{r}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{E}\left[|X^{t,\zeta}_{r-}-\zeta^{\prime}+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\gamma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r-},e)]|^{2}-|X^{t,\zeta}_{r-}-\zeta^{\prime}|^{2}\right]\tilde{N}(dr,de).

is supposed convex (see Remark

), we have

\int_{t}^{s}\left(X^{t,\zeta}_{r}-\zeta^{\prime}\right)\cdot D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})dA^{t,\zeta}_{r}\leq 0.

Then, for

, we get

\mathbb{E}\Big[\underset{s\in[t,t+\theta]}{\sup}\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-\zeta^{\prime}\right|^{2}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C\left(\left|\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\theta\right).

Moreover, due to the properties of

, we obtain namely,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\underset{s\in[t,t+\theta]}{\sup}\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-\zeta\right|^{2}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C\theta.

(iv). By (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle A^{t,\zeta}_{s}\leq

\displaystyle\left|\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{s})-\varphi(\zeta)\right|+C\int_{t}^{s}\left(1+\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}\right|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{r}\right|^{2}\right)dr

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{t}^{s}D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})\cdot\sigma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})dW_{r}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{t}^{s}\int_{E}\big[\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-}+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\gamma(r,(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r-},e)])-\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r-})\big]\tilde{N}(dr,de)\right|

and furthermore, from Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and (iii), we get

\mathbb{E}\Big[\left|A^{t,\zeta}_{s}\right|^{2}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\underset{s\in[t,t+\theta]}{\sup}\left|X^{t,\zeta}_{s}-\zeta\right|^{2}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]+C\theta\leq C\theta.

(v). Is a direct consequence of (iv).

5
Associated Integral-PDE with Neumann boundary condition

In this section, we establish the connection between our equation (
1.2
) and a problem involving Integral-PDE with Neumann boundary conditions. Initially, note that due to the uniqueness of the solution of (
1.2
), we obtain the subsequent flow property.

X^{s,X^{t,\zeta}_{s}}_{r}=X^{t,\zeta}_{r},\qquad r\in[s,T],\ \ \forall\ 0\leq t\leq s\leq T,\ \zeta\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};\bar{G}).

(5.8)

Consequently, via (
5.8
) and the analogous reasoning employed in the proof of Theorem 7.1.2 in
19
, we establish the Markov property.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded Borel measurable function. Then, for the solution
(X^{t,\zeta}_{s},A^{t,\zeta}_{s})_{t\leq s\leq T}
of (
1.2
), it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left(g(X^{t,\zeta}_{s})\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(g(X^{s,X^{t,\zeta}_{s}}_{s})\right),\qquad 0\leq t\leq s\leq T.

Therefore, we can construct a semi-group corresponding to this Markov process.

Now, for any data
(t,\zeta)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};\bar{G})
and an arbitrary
x_{0}\in\bar{G}
, we consider the following reflected MF-SDE with jumps,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle(i)\penalty\ X^{t,\zeta}_{s}=\zeta+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\mu(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})]dr+\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r})]dW_{r}\\
\displaystyle\hskip 31.2982pt+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{E}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\gamma(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,\zeta}_{r},e)]\tilde{N}(dr,de)+\int_{t}^{s}D\varphi(X^{t,\zeta}_{r})dA^{t,\zeta}_{r},\\
\displaystyle(ii)\penalty\ A^{t,\zeta}_{s}=\int_{t}^{s}\mathds{1}_{\left\{X^{t,\zeta}_{r}\in\partial G\right\}}dA^{t,\zeta}_{r}.\end{array}\right.

(5.9)

Observe that Theorem
3.1
guarantees the existence and uniqueness of the process
X^{0,x_{0}}
in (
5.9
). With
X^{0,x_{0}}
at hand, equation (
5.9
) reduces to a reflected SDE with jumps. Consequently,
(X^{t,\zeta}_{s},A^{t,\zeta}_{s})_{t\leq s\leq T}
is well-defined, where
A^{t,\zeta}_{\cdot}
is a continuous increasing process.

Now, without loss of generality, we put
\zeta=x
where
x\in\bar{G}
. Subsequently, we obtain the following representation.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(X^{t,x}_{s},A^{t,x}_{s})_{t\leq s\leq T}
be the solution of (
5.9
) and
v\in C_{b}^{1,2}\left([0,T]\times\bar{G}\right)
be a solution to the following integral-PDE with Neumann boundary condition

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}(t,x)+\mathcal{L}v(t,x)=0,&t\in[0,T[,x\in G\\
\\
\displaystyle v(T,x)=f(x),&x\in\bar{G}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}(t,x)=0,&t\in[0,T[,x\in\partial G.\end{array}\right.

(5.10)

where
f
is a bounded function and
\mathcal{L}
is the associated integro-differential operator such that

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{S},\\
&\mathcal{R}v(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\sigma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)].\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{\top}(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)].D^{2}v(t,x)+\mathbb{E}[\mu(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x)].Dv(t,x),\\
&\mathcal{S}v(t,x)=\int_{E}\big[v(t,x+\mathbb{E}[\gamma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x,e)])-v(t,x)-Dv(t,x).\mathbb{E}[\gamma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},x,e)]\big]\lambda(de).\end{split}

and

\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}},\quad\forall x\in\partial G.

Then, we have the following representation

v(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\left[f(X^{t,x}_{T})\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

(5.11)

Proof.

By Itô’s formula, we get

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle f(X^{t,x}_{T})=v(T,X^{t,x}_{T})&\displaystyle=v(t,x)+\int_{t}^{T}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}(r,X^{t,x}_{r})+\mathcal{L}v(r,X^{t,x}_{r})\right)dr\\
&\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}Dv(t,X^{t,x}_{r}).D\varphi(X^{t,x}_{r})dA^{t,\zeta}\\
&\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},X^{t,x}_{r})].Dv(X^{t,x}_{r})dW_{r}\\
&\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{E}\big[v\left(r,X^{t,x}_{r-}+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\gamma(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,x}_{r},e)]\right)-v(r,X^{t,x}_{r-})\big]\tilde{N}(dr,de)\end{array}

(5.12)

The second and the third terms in the right hand side of (
5.12
) are equal to zero by (
5.10
). Next we take the conditional expectation on both sides of (
5.12
). Given the conditions on
v
,
\sigma
and
\gamma
we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\sigma(t,X_{t}^{0,x_{0}},X^{t,x}_{r})].Dv(X^{t,x}_{r})dW_{r}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=0,

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\int_{E}\big[v\left(r,X^{t,x}_{r-}+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}[\gamma(r,(X^{0,x_{0}}_{r})^{\prime},X^{t,x}_{r},e)]\right)-v(r,X^{t,x}_{r-})\big]\tilde{N}(dr,de)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=0.

Hence, we have the representation (
5.11
).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

For further research directions, other extensions would consider the reflected MF-SDE with jumps by considering Stieltjes integration with respect to increasing processes that are not necessarily continuous. Another extension is to combine our result in this paper with the Mean-Field Generalized BSDE with jumps discussed in
