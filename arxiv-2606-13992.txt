Group Quantization and Mellin Representations of the Heston Model
Santiago García

Abstract
We construct a lifted local Lie groupoid formulation of the Heston stochastic-volatility model and use it to give a geometric interpretation of its affine-transform structure. The construction extends the Group Quantization framework of [ 14 ] from quadratic financial diffusion models to the variance-dependent Heston setting. The lifted groupoid carries an ℝ + \mathbb{R}^{+} -central extension whose nontrivial symplectic part is supplemented by an affine-quadratic coboundary encoding the Heston diffusion symbol. The coordinate representation gives the Heston pricing operator, while the momentum representation, together with its higher-polarization operator, gives the Riccati transport underlying the affine transform formula. In the momentum representation, Mellin modes are propagated along Riccati characteristics to produce the affine kernels used in European option pricing. The construction also identifies the projective linear action underlying the Heston Riccati flow. It therefore gives a single geometric framework for the pricing operator, Riccati transport, and Mellin pricing representation.



1
Introduction

The Heston stochastic-volatility model
[
1
]
is central in
mathematical finance. This model is analytically tractable while allowing
stochastic variance and nonconstant volatility surfaces. Its tractability comes
from its affine structure: transform methods reduce pricing to
exponential-affine kernels whose coefficients satisfy Riccati equations. In the
standard affine-transform derivation
[
5
,
7
]
, these
Riccati equations arise from the exponential-affine ansatz and the affine form
of the generator. What is less explicit in that analytic derivation is the
geometric mechanism behind the Riccati flow.

This paper addresses that point and gives a geometric explanation of the
existing affine-transform structure. We show that the Heston pricing operator
and the Heston Riccati equations arise from two complementary representations
of the same geometric construction: the coordinate polarization gives the
pricing operator, while the momentum polarization gives the Riccati transport
underlying the affine transform formula.

The construction extends the Group Quantization approach developed
in
[
14
]
for quadratic Hamiltonian systems and financial diffusion
models. In the Black–Scholes case, the construction reduces essentially to a
Lie-group quantization with flat diffusion. In the present construction, the
Heston model leads naturally to a local Lie groupoid rather than to a single
global Lie group, because its diffusion coefficients depend on the
instantaneous variance
v
.

The lifted groupoid is centrally extended in order to encode the scalar
multipliers that appear in pricing representations. A key feature is the use of
a multiplicative
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-central extension. In ordinary geometric
quantization the central variable is usually
U(1)
-valued and acts by phase
factors. In pricing, the relevant scalar factors are positive and
multiplicative. The
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-extension is therefore natural for
discounting and multiplicative pricing factors; after complexification it also
supports the Mellin representation.

The central extension has two distinct pieces: a nontrivial symplectic orbit
cocycle and an affine-quadratic Heston coboundary. The latter changes the
Cartan connection, the polarization equations, and the induced pricing
operator, but it does not change the curvature class.

The Riccati equations and Mellin transform modes appear in the momentum
representation. After imposing the momentum higher-polarization equation, these
modes are transported into the Heston affine pricing kernels. The Riccati
equations have a projective form. For real parameters this is an

SL(2,\mathbb{R})
flow; along the Mellin inversion contour one uses its
complexified form. Thus the nonlinear Heston Riccati equation appears as a
projectivization of a two-dimensional linear flow.

The Mellin transform enters through the payoff decomposition. The momentum
representation then transports the resulting payoff modes along Riccati
characteristics. Transporting these modes by the higher-polarization operator gives
the affine Heston kernels, and European option prices are recovered by
integrating these kernels against the Mellin transform of the payoff.

The purpose of this paper is not to propose a new Heston pricing formula.
The contribution is geometric: the Heston pricing operator in coordinate space and
the Riccati equations in momentum space arise from two representations of the
same lifted local groupoid construction. The usual characteristic-function and Riccati formulas are recovered.

3.1
Group Approach to Quantization

In the Group Approach to Quantization (GAQ) of Aldaya, de Azcárraga and collaborators,
the model is represented by a local composition law
together with its central extension, invariant fields, Cartan form, and
polarization conditions.

Informally, a polarization is a choice of variables in which the lifted
groupoid is represented. It tells us which directions are eliminated and which
variables remain in the reduced representation. In the present paper, the
coordinate polarization keeps the state variables and gives the Heston pricing
equation, while the momentum polarization keeps the conjugate variables and
gives the transform-space Riccati equations.

The construction uses a central extension by the multiplicative group

\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The central generator is

\Xi=\zeta\partial_{\zeta},\qquad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(3.1)

The left- and right-invariant vector fields associated with the local
composition law give the differential operators acting on polarized functions.

The Cartan one-form
\Theta
defines the connection used in the polarization
conditions. Its exterior derivative

\omega=d\Theta

(3.2)

is the curvature of this connection. The characteristic module is

\mathcal{C}=\ker\Theta\cap\ker d\Theta.

(3.3)

The module
\mathcal{C}
consists of the directions that are horizontal and have zero curvature.

More formally, a polarization is a maximal involutive algebra of invariant
vector fields used to reduce the representation space. If

\mathcal{P}=\mathrm{span}\{X_{i}\}

(3.4)

is a polarization algebra, then polarized functions satisfy

X_{i}\Psi=0,\qquad X_{i}\in\mathcal{P}.

(3.5)

Thus polarized functions are constant along the directions selected by

\mathcal{P}
. Different choices of
\mathcal{P}
give different
representations of the same groupoid.

In the standard first-order GAQ construction, the characteristic module is
included in the polarization algebra. In the present Heston construction we
use a slightly different but standard higher-polarization formulation: now the
first-order polarization selects the representation variables, while
the dynamical pricing equation is imposed by a higher-order operator in the
left enveloping algebra.

4
The Lifted Heston Quantization Groupoid

4.1
Local Groupoid Adaptation of the Group Quantization Framework

Although the Heston construction is naturally formulated as a local Lie
groupoid rather than as a global Lie group, the Group Approach to Quantization
still applies in the form needed here. The essential ingredients are local:
a composition law, a central extension, invariant vector fields, a Cartan form,
a characteristic module, and polarization conditions. These objects are
constructed in a local groupoid chart adapted to the variance state
v
.

The reason for using a groupoid is the state dependence of the Heston diffusion
matrix. The diffusion coefficients depend on
v
, and the quadratic
momentum block degenerates at the boundary
v=0
. Therefore the natural
geometric object is not a single global constant-coefficient Lie group, but a
local groupoid over the variance-state base.

Let

B\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

denote the variance-state base. The local Heston groupoid is written
schematically as

\widetilde{H}\rightrightarrows B,

with source and target maps

s,t:\widetilde{H}\longrightarrow B.

An arrow

g:v_{-}\longrightarrow v_{+}

is a local transport from the fibre over an initial variance state

v_{-}\in B
to the fibre over a terminal variance state
v_{+}\in B
. In the
local chart used below, such an arrow is represented by

g=(t,M_{H}(t,k),\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u},\zeta).

Here
t
represents time,
M_{H}(t,k)
is the
four-dimensional semidirect transport matrix associated with the Riccati
block,
\mathbf{a}
denotes the lifted phase-space variables,

\mathbf{u}
denotes the auxiliary fibre variables, and

\zeta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the central scale coordinate.

The source and target
maps return the initial and terminal variance states:

s(g)=v_{-},\qquad t(g)=v_{+}.

The variance coordinate
v
appears in two related roles. It is the
base coordinate over which the local groupoid is charted, and it is also the
variance component of the lifted phase-space variable

\mathbf{a}=(x,p_{x},v,p_{v})^{T}.

This notation reflects the local chart used in the computations: the groupoid
is organized over the variance state, while the invariant fields and
polarization equations are written in the lifted phase-space coordinates.

The product of two arrows is defined only when they are composable. If

g:v_{-}\longrightarrow v_{+},\qquad g^{\prime}:v_{+}\longrightarrow v_{++},

then

g^{\prime}\star g:v_{-}\longrightarrow v_{++}.

Equivalently, the product
g^{\prime}\star g
is defined only when

s(g^{\prime})=t(g).

In the local chart used in this paper, this composability condition is
suppressed in the notation, and the product is written in the semidirect form
used below. The unit arrow at
v\in B
is denoted by

1_{v}:v\longrightarrow v,

and the inverse of an arrow is defined locally wherever the chart is valid.

Thus the variance dependence of the Heston diffusion changes the global
geometric object from a Lie group to a local groupoid, but it does not prevent
the use of the same quantization mechanism. The coordinate polarization
produces the Heston pricing operator, while the momentum polarization produces
the Riccati transport.

In the present paper we do not need the global integration theory of Lie
groupoids. All computations are local: we use coordinates on the arrows and
compute the composition law, Cartan form, invariant vector fields,
characteristic module, polarization equations, and pricing operators.

4.2
Composition Law and Central Cocycle

The Heston quantization groupoid
\widetilde{H}
is the lifted local
groupoid associated with the Heston stochastic-volatility model. The word

lifted
refers to the passage from the physical state variables

(x,v)
to an enlarged space containing

\mathbf{a}=(x,p_{x},v,p_{v})^{T},\qquad\mathbf{u}=(u_{x},u_{p_{x}},u_{v},u_{p_{v}})^{T},\qquad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

The variables
\mathbf{a}
are phase-space variables. They contain the
log-price coordinate
x
, the variance coordinate
v
, and their conjugate
momentum variables
p_{x}
and
p_{v}
. These variables form
the physical Heisenberg-type phase-space sector of the lifted groupoid.

The variables

\mathbf{u}
are auxiliary fiber variables, and
\zeta
is the central
coordinate. These additional variables do not represent new financial state
variables. They are introduced so that the pricing operator, the Riccati
equations, and the scalar factors in the pricing kernel can be represented
within one lifted construction.
After reduction to polarized spaces, the
\mathbf{u}
variables disappear and the remaining coordinate and momentum representations recover
the usual Heston pricing operator and the corresponding Riccati transport
equations.

The four-dimensional Heston semidirect generator
A_{H}(k)\in\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb{R})
may be chosen as the
symplectic lift of the two-dimensional Riccati generator
A_{R}(k)
associated with the conserved log-price momentum orbit
k
.

A_{H}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}0_{2\times 2}&0\\
0&A_{R}(k)\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_{R}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\beta(k)&\gamma(k)\\
-\alpha&-\frac{1}{2}\beta(k)\end{pmatrix}.

where

\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2},\qquad\beta(k)=\rho\sigma_{\nu}k-\kappa,\qquad\gamma(k)=\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}-k).

Thus

M_{H}(t,k)=\exp\!\bigl(tA_{H}(k)\bigr)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{2}&0\\
0&M_{R}(t,k)\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_{R}(t,k)=\exp\!\bigl(tA_{R}(k)\bigr).

The matrix
M_{H}(t,k)
acts on the four-dimensional lifted phase-space
variables

\mathbf{a}(t)=M_{H}(t,k)\,\mathbf{a}(0),\qquad\mathbf{u}(t)=M_{H}(t,k)\,\mathbf{u}(0).

while
M_{R}(t,k)
is the two-dimensional projective Riccati block
that controls the variance-momentum dynamics on the conserved orbit
p_{x}=k
.

Only the symplectic and semidirect transport properties of
A_{H}(k)

are needed in the lifted groupoid construction.

This semidirect structure is centrally extended by the multiplicative group

\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The central coordinate
\zeta
represents the positive
value scale of the pricing representation. The central cocycle determines how
this scale changes under groupoid composition, producing the scalar factors
that appear in the Heston pricing kernel.

We use the word
transport
to mean propagation by the local groupoid
composition. Before reduction, this propagation is described by the
semidirect matrix
M_{H}(t,k)
on the lifted variables. After imposing
equivariance and a polarization, the same lifted construction reduces either
to the Heston pricing evolution in the coordinate representation or to the
Riccati characteristic flow in the momentum representation.

Before reduction
p_{x}
is kept as a momentum coordinate. The intrinsic conserved orbit parameter is
k=p_{x}
; in the Mellin representation one later restricts to the spectral orbit
k=p_{x}=-q
.

An element
g\in\widetilde{H}
is written locally as

g=(t,M,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u},\zeta),\qquad\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\qquad\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\qquad M\in Sp(4,\mathbb{R}),\qquad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(4.1)

We now define the local groupoid composition over a fixed conserved momentum
orbit
k
. Let

g^{\prime}=(t^{\prime},M_{H}(t^{\prime},k),\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}),\qquad g=(t,M_{H}(t,k),\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u},\zeta)

(4.2)

be two composable elements of
\widetilde{H}
. Their product

g^{\prime\prime}=g^{\prime}\star g

(4.3)

is defined by

\displaystyle t^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=t^{\prime}+t,

(4.4)

\displaystyle M_{H}(t^{\prime\prime},k)

\displaystyle=M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)M_{H}(t,k),

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\mathbf{a},

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\mathbf{u},

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\zeta^{\prime}\zeta\exp\!\left[B_{Q}(g^{\prime},g;k)\right].

The noncentral part is the semidirect product determined by the matrix
M_{H}(t,k)
. The central factor is determined by the exponent

B_{Q}(g^{\prime},g;k)=B_{\mathrm{orb}}(g^{\prime},g;k)+\delta F_{Q}(g^{\prime},g;k).

(4.5)

Thus the central extension contains two contributions: a symplectic orbit
cocycle and an affine-quadratic coboundary. The symplectic orbit cocycle is

B_{\mathrm{orb}}(g^{\prime},g;k)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}J_{4}M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\mathbf{a}+\lambda\mathbf{u}^{\prime T}J_{4}M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\mathbf{a},

(4.6)

with
J_{4}
the canonical four-dimensional symplectic matrix.

J_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}J_{2}&0_{2\times 2}\\
0_{2\times 2}&J_{2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad J_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(4.7)

The nontrivial central
extension is the four-dimensional lift of the transported symplectic cocycle
used in
[
14
]
, with the
\lambda
-term adding the auxiliary-fiber
contribution. The Heston affine symbol enters separately through the coboundary
\delta F_{Q}
.

The coboundary contribution is

\delta F_{Q}(g^{\prime},g;k)=F_{Q}(g^{\prime}\star g;k)-F_{Q}(g^{\prime};k)-F_{Q}(g;k).

It is generated by the affine-quadratic coboundary symbol

F_{Q}(g;k)=t\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v),

where

\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v)=\mathbf{b}^{T}\mathbf{a}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{T}D_{H}(v)\mathbf{a},\qquad\mathbf{b}=(0,r,0,\kappa\theta)^{T},

(4.8)

and

D_{H}(v)=vD_{0},\qquad D_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
0&1&0&\rho\sigma_{\nu}\\
0&0&0&0\\
0&\rho\sigma_{\nu}&0&\sigma_{\nu}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.9)

Equivalently,

F_{Q}(g;k)=t\left[rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}+\frac{1}{2}v\left(p_{x}^{2}+2\rho\sigma_{\nu}p_{x}p_{v}+\sigma_{\nu}^{2}p_{v}^{2}\right)\right].

(4.10)

The symbol
\vartheta_{H}
in the central extension denotes the coboundary cocycle
symbol. It is the affine-quadratic term used in the central factor

F_{Q}=t\vartheta_{H}
. The full Heston operator symbol, which also contains the drift
corrections
-\frac{1}{2}vp_{x}-\kappa vp_{v},
will be denoted by
C_{\mathrm{op}}
.

The role of
F_{Q}
is to encode the affine-quadratic coboundary symbol in the
central extension. Since it enters through the coboundary
\delta F_{Q}
, its
contribution to the Cartan form is exact. Consequently, the Heston parameters modify the Cartan connection and the
polarization equations, but not the underlying symplectic curvature.

The full
4\times 4
quadratic matrix
D_{H}(v)
is not nondegenerate as a
matrix on all orbit variables. What is nondegenerate for
v>0
and

|\rho|<1
is the two-dimensional momentum block associated with

(p_{x},p_{v})
:

v\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\sigma_{\nu}\\
\rho\sigma_{\nu}&\sigma_{\nu}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

At
v=0
, this momentum covariance block degenerates. This state dependence
is the geometric reason for treating the Heston construction as local over the
variance state. The underlying symplectic curvature of the lifted central
extension, however, is independent of
v
.

law in Section
4.2
. The left-invariant fields are
used to define the polarization constraints and the higher-polarization

infinitesimal structure of the lifted local groupoid.

For a direction
\eta

\mathbf{a}
-sector, the left-invariant field is
obtained by differentiating right multiplication by an infinitesimal element:

L_{\eta}f(g)=\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}f\bigl(g\star(0,I,\epsilon\eta,0,1)\bigr).

Similarly, the right-invariant field is obtained from

R_{\eta}f(g)=\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}f\bigl((0,I,\epsilon\eta,0,1)\star g\bigr).

The same convention is used for the
\mathbf{u}

t

\zeta

composition law. In the left-invariant fields, infinitesimal

\mathbf{a}
- and
\mathbf{u}
-directions are transported by

M_{H}(t,k)

and analogously for
\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{u}}

4.4
Cartan Form

The Cartan one-form
\Theta
defines the connection used in the polarization
conditions. This terminology is related to the standard geometric-quantization
language of connections and polarizations

[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

Specifically, the Cartan form is the vertical component of the Maurer–Cartan form associated
with the multiplicative central extension. It is normalized on the central
generator and annihilates the noncentral left-invariant fields. Thus

\Theta(\Xi)=1,\qquad\Theta(L_{\mathbf{a}})=0,\qquad\Theta(L_{\mathbf{u}})=0,\qquad\Theta(L_{t})=0.

(4.11)

Equivalently, the noncentral left-invariant directions are horizontal for the
Cartan connection. In the local coordinates used here, this horizontality
defines a splitting of the lifted Heston groupoid into
\ker\Theta
and the
central
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-fiber.

The affine-quadratic Heston contribution is encoded by the coboundary generator

F_{Q}(g;k)=t\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v),\qquad\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v)=\mathbf{b}^{T}\mathbf{a}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{T}D_{H}(v)\mathbf{a}.

(4.12)

The Cartan form then takes the compact form

\Theta=\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}+\lambda\mathbf{u}\right)^{T}J_{4}\,d\mathbf{a}-dF_{Q}.

(4.13)

It is useful to separate the symplectic orbit contribution from the exact
affine-quadratic contribution:

\Theta=\Theta_{\mathrm{orb}}+\Theta_{\mathrm{aff}},\qquad\Theta_{\mathrm{orb}}=\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}+\lambda\mathbf{u}\right)^{T}J_{4}\,d\mathbf{a},\qquad\Theta_{\mathrm{aff}}=-dF_{Q}.

(4.14)

The first term is determined by the transported Heisenberg orbit cocycle,
whereas the second term is the exact contribution coming from the Heston
affine-quadratic coboundary.

Since
F_{Q}=t\vartheta_{H}
, its differential is

dF_{Q}=\vartheta_{H}\,dt+t\,d\vartheta_{H}.

(4.15)

Writing the dependence of
\vartheta_{H}
on the variance coordinate explicitly gives

d\vartheta_{H}=\left(\mathbf{b}^{T}+\mathbf{a}^{T}D_{H}(v)\right)d\mathbf{a}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{T}\left(\partial_{v}D_{H}(v)\right)\mathbf{a}\,dv.

(4.16)

In particular, for
D_{H}(v)=vD_{0}
,

\partial_{v}D_{H}(v)=D_{0},\qquad d\vartheta_{H}=\left(\mathbf{b}^{T}+\mathbf{a}^{T}D_{H}(v)\right)d\mathbf{a}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{T}D_{0}\mathbf{a}\,dv.

(4.17)

Thus the Heston parameters enter the Cartan connection only through the exact
one-form
-dF_{Q}
.

4.5
Curvature Form

The curvature form is the exterior derivative of the Cartan form:

\omega=d\Theta.

(4.18)

Using

\Theta=\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}+\lambda\mathbf{u}\right)^{T}J_{4}\,d\mathbf{a}-dF_{Q},

(4.19)

we obtain

\omega=-\frac{1}{2}\,d\mathbf{a}^{T}J_{4}\wedge d\mathbf{a}-\lambda\,d\mathbf{u}^{T}J_{4}\wedge d\mathbf{a}.

(4.20)

The affine-quadratic Heston contribution does not appear in the curvature,
because it enters the Cartan form through the exact one-form
-dF_{Q}
:

d(-dF_{Q})=-d^{2}F_{Q}=0.

(4.21)

Thus the curvature is determined entirely by the symplectic part of the
central extension.
1
1

1

In coordinates, the curvature form is

\omega=dx\wedge dp_{x}+dv\wedge dp_{v}+\lambda\left(dx\wedge du_{p_{x}}-dp_{x}\wedge du_{x}+dv\wedge du_{p_{v}}-dp_{v}\wedge du_{v}\right).

The affine and quadratic Heston terms modify the Cartan
connection, but not the curvature
d\Theta
.

4.6
Characteristic Module

The characteristic module is the horizontal kernel of the Cartan curvature. It
consists of the left-invariant fields that are horizontal for the Cartan form
and preserve the Cartan connection:

C_{\Theta}=\ker\Theta\cap\ker d\Theta.

(4.22)

Equivalently, for
X\in C_{\Theta}
,

i_{X}\Theta=0,\qquad i_{X}(d\Theta)=0,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{X}\Theta=d(i_{X}\Theta)+i_{X}(d\Theta)=0.

(4.23)

For the lifted Heston groupoid, the curvature form is

\omega=d\Theta=-\frac{1}{2}\,d\mathbf{a}^{T}J_{4}\wedge d\mathbf{a}-\lambda\,d\mathbf{u}^{T}J_{4}\wedge d\mathbf{a}.

(4.24)

Since
\omega
has no
dt
-component, the time direction lies in its
kernel. The non-time horizontal directions have nontrivial contraction with

\omega
, so the only left-invariant horizontal direction in the curvature
kernel is the time direction.

Therefore the characteristic module is one-dimensional:

C_{\Theta}=\mathrm{span}\{L_{t}\},\qquad L_{t}=\partial_{t}+\vartheta_{H}\,\Xi,

(4.25)

where
\vartheta_{H}
is the affine-quadratic Heston cocycle term defined in (
4.8
).

Thus the intrinsic transport direction of the Cartan connection is generated
by
L_{t}
. The detailed verification is given in
Appendix A, “Derivation of the Characteristic Module.”

Note that this characteristic module calculation has been made using the full lifted construction with

\lambda\neq 0
. If
\lambda=0
, the auxiliary
\mathbf{u}
-sector
decouples and should be discarded or quotiented out before computing the
reduced characteristic module.

Remark on a possible holonomy formulation.

The curvature calculation is specific to the finite coboundary
formulation used in this paper. A different
path-groupoid formulation could instead use the Poincaré–Cartan connection, where
the corresponding curvature form will contain Heston terms.
This suggests a possible extension of the present Heston finite coboundary
construction to an action- or holonomy-type central extension of a path
groupoid. We will address this approach in a future work and we will not pursue this formulation here.

5
Polarization Algebra

The role of polarization is to reduce the number of dynamical variables by selecting a preferred representation of the lifted Heston groupoid.

Although in the standard GAQ construction the characteristic module is
included in the polarization algebra, in the present Heston construction we leave the
characteristic module out of the polarization since
the dynamical pricing equation is imposed separately by a higher-order operator.

Therefore, in this paper we define a first-order polarization
{\mathcal{P}}
as a maximal horizontal involutive algebra of
left-invariant fields.

The Group Approach to Quantization formalism constructs an irreducible representation of

\widetilde{H}
starting from a space of functions on the lifted groupoid.
Since the Mellin representation requires complex spectral parameters, we use
complex-valued smooth polarized functions:

\mathcal{F}=C^{\infty}(\widetilde{H},\mathbb{C}).

(5.1)

The central extension is represented by imposing the equivariance condition

\Xi\Psi=\Psi,\qquad\Xi=\zeta\partial_{\zeta}.

(5.2)

Thus the central variable
\zeta
acts by homogeneous scaling rather than as
an independent physical coordinate.

The corresponding polarized function space is

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}}=\left\{\Psi\in\mathcal{F}\;:\;X\Psi=0\ \text{for all }X\in\mathcal{P},\quad\Xi\Psi=\Psi\right\}.

(5.3)

5.1
Dynamical higher polarization

We separate the first-order polarization constraints from the dynamical pricing constraint.
Using the component notation

\mathbf{a}=(x,p_{x},v,p_{v})^{T},\qquad\mathbf{u}=(u_{x},u_{p_{x}},u_{v},u_{p_{v}})^{T},

(5.4)

the lifted Heston groupoid admits two natural first-order polarizations:

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{mom}}=\mathrm{span}\left\{L_{x},\,L_{v},\,L_{u_{x}},\,L_{u_{v}}\right\}

(5.5)

and

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}}=\mathrm{span}\left\{L_{p_{x}},\,L_{p_{v}},\,L_{u_{p_{x}}},\,L_{u_{p_{v}}}\right\},

(5.6)

The coordinate polarization
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}}
leaves coordinate variables in the reduced
representation and leads to the Heston diffusion operator, while the momentum
polarization
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{mom}}
leaves momentum variables in the reduced representation and leads
to the Riccati transport equations.

The pricing dynamics is imposed separately by higher-polarization operators
compatible with a polarization
\mathcal{P}
, as described in Section
6
.

5.2
Momentum-polarized functions

The momentum polarization gives the transform representation used in the
financial applications. After the momentum higher-polarization equation is
imposed, this representation produces the Riccati transport and the Heston
pricing kernels.

The momentum polarization is

P_{\mathrm{mom}}=\operatorname{span}\{L_{x},L_{v},L_{u_{x}},L_{u_{v}}\}.

Thus momentum-polarized functions satisfy

L_{x}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{v}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{u_{x}}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{u_{v}}\Psi=0,\qquad\Xi\Psi=\Psi.

Introduce the transported variables

\widehat{\mathbf{a}}=M_{H}(t,k)^{-1}\mathbf{a},\qquad\widehat{\mathbf{u}}=M_{H}(t,k)^{-1}\mathbf{u}.

In the polarization equations below we work in these transported variables and
suppress hats on the phase-space components when no confusion can arise.

In the momentum representation the polarization directions are the coordinate directions. Therefore

L_{u_{x}}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{u_{v}}\Psi=0

imply that
\psi
is independent of the transported auxiliary coordinate variables

\widehat{u}_{x}
and
\widehat{u}_{v}
. Hence

\psi=\psi(t,x,v,p_{x},p_{v},\widehat{u}_{p_{x}},\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}).

The remaining two first-order polarization equations are

L_{x}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{v}\Psi=0.

Using the left-invariant fields, these become

\left[\partial_{x}+\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{x}+\lambda\widehat{u}_{p_{x}}\right)\right]\psi=0,

and

\left[\partial_{v}+\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{v}+\lambda\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}+t\frac{\partial\vartheta_{H}}{\partial v}\right)\right]\psi=0.

The
t\,\partial_{v}\vartheta_{H}
term comes from the coboundary contribution

t\nabla_{\mathbf{a}}\vartheta_{H}
in the left-invariant fields. Therefore the Heston coboundary
factor is still present in the momentum-polarized functions.

Since
\vartheta_{H}
has no
x
-dependence, the first equation integrates to the factor

\exp\left[-x\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{x}+\lambda\widehat{u}_{p_{x}}\right)\right].

The second equation integrates to

\exp\left[-v\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{v}+\lambda\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}\right)-t\vartheta_{H}\right].

Thus the general momentum-polarized function is

\Psi_{\mathrm{mom}}^{0}=\zeta\exp\left[-x\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{x}+\lambda\widehat{u}_{p_{x}}\right)-v\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{v}+\lambda\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}\right)-t\vartheta_{H}\right]\chi(t,p_{x},p_{v},\widehat{u}_{p_{x}},\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}).

Here

\vartheta_{H}=rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}+\frac{1}{2}v\left(p_{x}^{2}+2\rho\sigma_{\nu}p_{x}p_{v}+\sigma_{\nu}^{2}p_{v}^{2}\right).

The auxiliary variables
\mathbf{u}
are bookkeeping variables of the lifted central extension. They are needed
before polarization in order to write the central cocycle and the invariant fields uniformly. After equivariance and polarization, they affect only the central scale and do not survive
as financial state variables. Define

\zeta_{\mathrm{red}}=\zeta\exp\left[-\lambda\left(x\widehat{u}_{p_{x}}+v\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}\right)\right].

Choosing the auxiliary representative

\widehat{u}_{p_{x}}=0,\qquad\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}=0,

we obtain the reduced momentum-polarized functions

\Psi_{\mathrm{mom,red}}=\zeta_{\mathrm{red}}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}(xp_{x}+vp_{v})-t\vartheta_{H}\right]\chi(t,p_{x},p_{v}).

The momentum-polarized functions provide the Mellin representation space.
After imposing the momentum higher-polarization equation, their transport
along Riccati characteristics produces the Heston affine pricing kernels.

5.3
Coordinate-polarized functions

The coordinate polarization is

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}}=\operatorname{span}\{L_{p_{x}},L_{p_{v}},L_{u_{p_{x}}},L_{u_{p_{v}}}\}.

Thus coordinate-polarized functions satisfy

L_{p_{x}}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{p_{v}}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{u_{p_{x}}}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{u_{p_{v}}}\Psi=0,\qquad\Xi\Psi=\Psi.

The auxiliary polarization equations

L_{u_{p_{x}}}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{u_{p_{v}}}\Psi=0

imply that
\psi
is independent of
u_{p_{x}}
and
u_{p_{v}}
. Hence

\psi=\psi(t,x,v,p_{x},p_{v},u_{x},u_{v}).

The remaining first-order coordinate polarization equations are

L_{p_{x}}\Psi=0,\qquad L_{p_{v}}\Psi=0.

Using the left-invariant fields, these become

\left[\partial_{p_{x}}-\left(\frac{1}{2}x+\lambda u_{x}-t\,\frac{\partial\vartheta_{H}}{\partial p_{x}}\right)\right]\psi=0,

and

\left[\partial_{p_{v}}-\left(\frac{1}{2}v+\lambda u_{v}-t\,\frac{\partial\vartheta_{H}}{\partial p_{v}}\right)\right]\psi=0.

The derivatives of the Heston coboundary symbol are

\frac{\partial\vartheta_{H}}{\partial p_{x}}=r+v(p_{x}+\rho\sigma_{\nu}p_{v}),

and

\frac{\partial\vartheta_{H}}{\partial p_{v}}=\kappa\theta+v(\rho\sigma_{\nu}p_{x}+\sigma_{\nu}^{2}p_{v}).

The two equations are integrable because the
p_{x}
- and
p_{v}
-coefficients
come from the exact differential of
t\vartheta_{H}
. Solving them gives

\psi=\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}(xp_{x}+vp_{v})+\lambda(u_{x}p_{x}+u_{v}p_{v})-t\vartheta_{H}\right]\chi(t,x,v,u_{x},u_{v}).

Hence the general coordinate-polarized function is

\Psi_{\mathrm{coord}}^{0}=\zeta\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}(xp_{x}+vp_{v})+\lambda(u_{x}p_{x}+u_{v}p_{v})-t\vartheta_{H}\right]\chi(t,x,v,u_{x},u_{v}).

Here

\vartheta_{H}=rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}+\frac{1}{2}v\left(p_{x}^{2}+2\rho\sigma_{\nu}p_{x}p_{v}+\sigma_{\nu}^{2}p_{v}^{2}\right).

As in the momentum polarization, the remaining lifted variables are auxiliary
fiber variables and may be absorbed into the central factor. Define

\zeta_{\mathrm{red}}=\zeta\exp\left[\lambda(u_{x}p_{x}+u_{v}p_{v})\right].

Choosing the constant auxiliary representative

u_{x}=0,\qquad u_{v}=0,

we obtain the reduced coordinate-polarized functions

\Psi_{\mathrm{coord,red}}^{0}=\zeta_{\mathrm{red}}\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}(xp_{x}+vp_{v})-t\vartheta_{H}\right]\chi(t,x,v).

Thus the coordinate representation leaves the variables
(t,x,v)
in the
reduced function
\chi
.

6
Higher-Order Polarization

The first-order polarizations constructed above reduce the lifted Heston
groupoid to coordinate- and momentum-type representation spaces. However, the
Heston pricing dynamics is second order, and therefore cannot be represented by
first-order polarization constraints alone.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be one of the first-order polarization algebras. A
higher-polarization operator
\mathcal{H}
is an element of the universal enveloping algebra
generated by the left-invariant fields of the lifted Heston groupoid. We denote
this algebra by
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h}^{L}),

where
\mathfrak{h}^{L}
is the left-invariant field algebra.

Thus a higher-polarization operator
\mathcal{H}
must satisfy

\mathcal{H}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h}^{L}),\qquad[\mathcal{H},\mathcal{P}]\subset\mathcal{P},\qquad[\mathcal{H},\Xi]=0.

(6.1)

The middle condition means that
\mathcal{H}
maps polarized functions to
polarized functions. Indeed, if
X\in\mathcal{P}
and
X\Psi=0
, then

X(\mathcal{H}\Psi)=[X,\mathcal{H}]\Psi+\mathcal{H}(X\Psi)=0,

(6.2)

because
[X,\mathcal{H}]
lies in the polarization algebra.

Thus higher-order polarization does not introduce a new independent
representation space. Rather, it selects distinguished higher-order
left-invariant operators acting consistently on an already polarized space.
For the Heston groupoid there are two natural choices: the coordinate
higher-polarization operator, which reduces to the Heston pricing operator,
and the momentum higher-polarization operator, which reduces to the Riccati
transport generator.

6.1
Momentum Higher-Polarization and Riccati Transport

A higher-polarization operator
H_{\mathrm{mom}}
in the momentum representation is an operator in the left-invariant enveloping algebra that preserves the momentum
polarization algebra
P_{\mathrm{mom}}

[H_{\mathrm{mom}},P_{\mathrm{mom}}]\subset P_{\mathrm{mom}}.

(6.3)

The momentum higher-polarization operator is

H_{\mathrm{mom}}=L_{t}-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})L_{p_{v}}+\left[rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}-\left(v+\lambda\widehat{u}_{v}\right)R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})\right]\Xi.

where
R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})
is the Heston Riccati polynomial

R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})=\frac{1}{2}(p_{x}^{2}-p_{x})+(\rho\sigma_{\nu}p_{x}-\kappa)p_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}p_{v}^{2}.

(6.4)

and the full Heston operator symbol is

C_{\mathrm{op}}(p_{x},p_{v};v)=rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}+vR_{H}(p_{x},p_{v}).

This is the momentum-space form of the second-order Heston operator. It is first order in
p_{v}
,
but its coefficient
R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})
is quadratic in the momenta. This is the usual transformation of a
second-order affine operator into a first-order Riccati transport equation in momentum space.

Let the momentum-polarized functions be written as

\Psi_{\mathrm{mom}}^{0}=\zeta\exp(S_{\mathrm{mom}})\chi(t,p_{x},p_{v},\widehat{u}_{p_{x}},\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}),

where

S_{\mathrm{mom}}=-x\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{x}+\lambda\widehat{u}_{p_{x}}\right)-v\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{v}+\lambda\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}\right)-t\vartheta_{H}.

With the sign conventions used in the momentum polarization equations, the conjugated actions
of the relevant left-invariant fields are

e^{-S_{\mathrm{mom}}}\zeta^{-1}L_{t}\left(\zeta e^{S_{\mathrm{mom}}}\chi\right)=\partial_{t}\chi,

and

e^{-S_{\mathrm{mom}}}\zeta^{-1}L_{p_{v}}\left(\zeta e^{S_{\mathrm{mom}}}\chi\right)=\left(\partial_{p_{v}}-v-\lambda\widehat{u}_{v}\right)\chi.

Therefore

\displaystyle e^{-S_{\mathrm{mom}}}\zeta^{-1}H_{\mathrm{mom}}\left(\zeta e^{S_{\mathrm{mom}}}\chi\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\chi-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})\left(\partial_{p_{v}}-v-\lambda\widehat{u}_{v}\right)\chi

\displaystyle+\left[rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}-\left(v+\lambda\widehat{u}_{v}\right)R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})\right]\chi.

The terms proportional to
v+\lambda\widehat{u}_{v}
cancel. Hence the reduced
momentum-space operator is

H_{\mathrm{mom}}^{\mathrm{red}}=\partial_{t}-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})\partial_{p_{v}}+rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}.

(6.5)

After choosing the auxiliary representative

\widehat{u}_{p_{x}}=0,\qquad\widehat{u}_{p_{v}}=0,\qquad\widehat{u}_{v}=0,

the reduced momentum-polarized functions are

\Psi_{\mathrm{mom,red}}=\zeta_{\mathrm{red}}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}(xp_{x}+vp_{v})-t\vartheta_{H}\right]\chi(t,p_{x},p_{v}).

(6.6)

6.2
Coordinate higher-polarization operator

The coordinate higher-polarization operator
{H}_{H}
is an element of the
left-invariant enveloping algebra that preserves the coordinate polarization
algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}}
. Thus it must satisfy

[{H}_{H},\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}}]\subset\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}}.

(6.7)

Let

\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{a}}=(L_{x},L_{p_{x}},L_{v},L_{p_{v}})^{T}

and introduce the coordinate-representation momentum substitution

\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}=E_{p_{x}}L_{x}+E_{p_{v}}L_{v},

where

E_{p_{x}}=(0,1,0,0)^{T},\qquad E_{p_{v}}=(0,0,0,1)^{T}.

Recall that (see equation (
4.9
))

D_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
0&1&0&\rho\sigma_{\nu}\\
0&0&0&0\\
0&\rho\sigma_{\nu}&0&\sigma_{\nu}^{2}\end{pmatrix},

and

\mathbf{b}=(0,r,0,\kappa\theta)^{T}.

The Riccati polynomial (
6.4
), written as an element of the left-invariant enveloping
algebra, is

R_{H}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}})=\frac{1}{2}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}^{T}D_{0}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}-\frac{1}{2}E_{p_{x}}^{T}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}-\kappa E_{p_{v}}^{T}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}.

The full Heston operator symbol in coordinate representation is then

C_{\mathrm{op}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}};v)=\mathbf{b}^{T}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}+v\,R_{H}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}),

where
v=E_{v}^{T}\mathbf{a}
, with

E_{v}=(0,0,1,0)^{T}.

Thus the coordinate higher-polarization operator is

H_{H}=L_{t}+C_{\mathrm{op}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}};v).

Equivalently,

H_{H}=L_{t}+\mathbf{b}^{T}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}+v\left[\frac{1}{2}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}^{T}D_{0}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}-\frac{1}{2}E_{p_{x}}^{T}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}-\kappa E_{p_{v}}^{T}\Pi_{\mathrm{coord}}\right].

Expanding this four-dimensional expression gives

H_{H}=L_{t}+rL_{x}+\kappa\theta L_{v}+v\left[\frac{1}{2}(L_{x}^{2}-L_{x})+(\rho\sigma_{\nu}L_{x}-\kappa)L_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}L_{v}^{2}\right].

This is the coordinate-space form of the Heston higher-polarization operator. The terms

-\frac{1}{2}vL_{x}-\kappa vL_{v}

are the drift-correction terms that turn the affine-quadratic symbol into the
standard Heston drift.

If one wants the discounted pricing operator rather than the diffusion
generator, one subtracts the risk-free discount term:

H_{H}^{\mathrm{price}}=H_{H}-r\Xi.

(6.8)

Under central equivariance
\Xi\Psi=\Psi
, this becomes the usual
risk-neutral pricing operator. Equivalently, the discount term in (
6.8
) may be viewed as a shift in the central
fiber weight.

After imposing the coordinate polarization and reducing the auxiliary
variables, the operator
H_{H}
acts on the reduced coordinate function

\chi(t,x,v)
as

H_{H}^{\mathrm{red}}=\partial_{t}+\left(r-\frac{1}{2}v\right)\partial_{x}+\kappa(\theta-v)\partial_{v}+\frac{1}{2}v\partial_{x}^{2}+\rho\sigma_{\nu}v\,\partial_{x}\partial_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}v\,\partial_{v}^{2}.

(6.9)

Thus the coordinate higher-polarization operator gives the standard Heston
pricing PDE after central equivariance, coordinate polarization, auxiliary
reduction, and discounting. The first two terms after
L_{t}
are the affine drift contribution, while the terms proportional to
v
give the second-order Heston diffusion terms.

7
Riccati Geometry as a Property of Higher Polarization

The Riccati polynomial
R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})
in equation (
6.4
) is
not tied to a single choice of representation. In the coordinate polarization,
R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})
appears as the variance-sector coefficient of the
second-order Heston operator. In the momentum polarization, the same polynomial
generates the characteristic flow that gives the Riccati equation.

7.1
Momentum Polarization Riccati Flows

In the momentum representation, the higher-polarization operator
reduces, after equivariance and polarization, to equation (
6.5
)

H_{\mathrm{mom}}^{\mathrm{red}}=\partial_{t}-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})\partial_{p_{v}}+rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}.

(7.1)

The first-order part determines the characteristic equations

\frac{dp_{x}}{dt}=0,\qquad\frac{dp_{v}}{dt}=-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v}).

Therefore
p_{x}
is conserved along the characteristic flow, while
p_{v}

evolves according to the Riccati polynomial.

The Riccati transport is indexed by
the conserved log-price momentum

k=p_{x}.

For each fixed value of
k
, the variance momentum
p_{v}
evolves by a
projective
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
flow. Thus one may regard the lifted Heston
groupoid as a bundle over conserved log-price momentum orbits. The Mellin
representation is obtained later by restricting each spectral mode to the
orbit

k=p_{x}=-q.

The full pricing formula then integrates over the Mellin contour, and hence
over the corresponding family of conserved momentum orbits.

In backward time
\tau=T-t,

the variance-momentum equation becomes

\frac{dp_{v}}{d\tau}=R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v}).

(7.2)

For a Mellin mode,

p_{x}=-q,\qquad D(\tau,q)=p_{v}(\tau),

this gives the Heston Riccati equation

\frac{dD}{d\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}D^{2}-(\rho\sigma_{\nu}q+\kappa)D+\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}+q).

(7.3)

Thus the momentum polarization does not create a new Riccati structure; it
realizes the Riccati polynomial of the higher polarization as a characteristic
transport equation.

7.2
Higher Coordinate Polarization Variance Sector

The Riccati polynomial is also present in the coordinate higher-polarization
operator before reduction:

H_{H}=L_{t}+rL_{x}+\kappa\theta L_{v}+v\left[\frac{1}{2}(L_{x}^{2}-L_{x})+(\rho\sigma_{\nu}L_{x}-\kappa)L_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}L_{v}^{2}\right].

The coefficient multiplying the variance coordinate
v
is the operator
version of the Riccati polynomial.

Passing formally to the momentum symbol by replacing

L_{x}\longrightarrow p_{x},\qquad L_{v}\longrightarrow p_{v},

one obtains

H_{H}\longrightarrow L_{t}+C_{\mathrm{op}}(p_{x},p_{v};v)=L_{t}+rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}+vR_{H}(p_{x},p_{v}).

(7.4)

Thus the coordinate higher-polarization operator contains the same polynomial

R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})
that governs the momentum characteristic flow.

8
Higher-Polarization Diffusion Geometry

This section translates the coordinate higher-polarization operator into the
standard probabilistic language of diffusion generators. The purpose is not to
postulate the Heston stochastic equations, but to recover them from the
second-order symbol of the higher-polarization operator.

After central equivariance and coordinate polarization are imposed, the
higher-polarization operator
H_{H}
in equation (
7.4
) induces a diffusion generator on the variables

\mathbf{y}=(x,v)^{T}.

We write this generator in the form

\mathcal{L}_{H}=\bm{\mu}_{H}(v)^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\{x,v\}}\mathcal{D}_{H,ij}(v)\,\partial_{i}\partial_{j},

(8.1)

where

\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}=(\partial_{x},\partial_{v})^{T}.

The drift vector and the two-dimensional covariance-tensor
\mathcal{D}_{H}(v)
are:

\bm{\mu}_{H}(v)=\left(r-\frac{1}{2}v,\,\kappa(\theta-v)\right)^{T}\qquad\qquad\mathcal{D}_{H}(v)=v\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\sigma_{\nu}\\
\rho\sigma_{\nu}&\sigma_{\nu}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(8.2)

On the regular region

v>0,\qquad|\rho|<1,\qquad\sigma_{\nu}>0,

(8.3)

the tensor
\mathcal{D}_{H}(v)
is positive definite. At the boundary

v=0

the quadratic diffusion part degenerates and the second-order diffusion terms vanish.

8.1
Heston Model Stochastic Equations

A convenient square-root factorization of the covariance tensor is

\operatorname{Cov}_{H}(v)=\Sigma(v)\Sigma(v)^{T},\qquad\Sigma(v)=\sqrt{v}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\rho\sigma_{\nu}&\sigma_{\nu}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\end{pmatrix}

(8.4)

Let

\mathbf{Y}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{t}\\
v_{t}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{W}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}W_{t}^{(1)}\\
W_{t}^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}.

(8.5)

Then the diffusion generated by
\mathcal{L}_{H}
is

d\mathbf{Y}_{t}=\bm{\mu}(\mathbf{Y}_{t})\,dt+\bm{\Sigma}(v_{t})\,d\mathbf{W}_{t},

(8.6)

where

\bm{\mu}(x,v)=\begin{pmatrix}r-\frac{1}{2}v\\
\kappa(\theta-v)\end{pmatrix}.

(8.7)

Equivalently,

dx_{t}=\left(r-\frac{1}{2}v_{t}\right)dt+\sqrt{v_{t}}\,dW_{t}^{(1)},

(8.8)

and

dv_{t}=\kappa(\theta-v_{t})dt+\sigma_{\nu}\sqrt{v_{t}}\left(\rho\,dW_{t}^{(1)}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\,dW_{t}^{(2)}\right).

(8.9)

Introducing correlated Brownian motions

dW_{t}^{(x)}=dW_{t}^{(1)},\qquad dW_{t}^{(v)}=\rho\,dW_{t}^{(1)}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\,dW_{t}^{(2)},

(8.10)

we have

dW_{t}^{(x)}dW_{t}^{(v)}=\rho\,dt.

(8.11)

The stochastic system becomes

dx_{t}=\left(r-\frac{1}{2}v_{t}\right)dt+\sqrt{v_{t}}\,dW_{t}^{(x)},

(8.12)

dv_{t}=\kappa(\theta-v_{t})dt+\sigma_{\nu}\sqrt{v_{t}}\,dW_{t}^{(v)}.

(8.13)

Thus the Heston stochastic equations are recovered from the coordinate
higher-polarization operator by reading its second-order symbol as the
covariance tensor of the diffusion. The drift comes from the first-order part
of the reduced operator, while the covariance structure comes from the
quadratic part of the higher polarization.

Financial Applications: Mellin Pricing, Projective Propagation, and Numerical Validation

The preceding sections constructed two representations of the lifted Heston
groupoid. We now use the momentum representation to reconstruct European
option prices.

The momentum-polarized representation naturally produces the Mellin transform
modes. The full Heston pricing kernels are obtained by transporting these
modes with the momentum higher-polarization operator. This Mellin construction is equivalent to the usual Laplace–Fourier affine
representation; see, for example,

[
23
,
5
,

,

,

9.1
Momentum-Polarized Pricing Kernel

In momentum variables the full Heston operator symbol separates as

C_{\mathrm{op}}(p_{x},p_{v};v)=rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}+vR_{H}(p_{x},p_{v}),

(9.1)

where

R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})=\frac{1}{2}(p_{x}^{2}-p_{x})+(\rho\sigma_{\nu}p_{x}-\kappa)p_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}p_{v}^{2}.

(9.2)

The symbol
C_{\mathrm{op}}
is the full Heston operator symbol. It differs
from the coboundary cocycle symbol
\vartheta_{H}
by the drift-correction terms

-\frac{1}{2}vp_{x}-\kappa vp_{v}.

9.1.1
Momentum transport and conserved log-price momentum

The reduced momentum higher-polarization operator is, from equation (
6.5
),

H_{\mathrm{mom}}^{\mathrm{red}}=\partial_{t}-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})\partial_{p_{v}}+rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}.

(9.3)

Its first-order transport part

\partial_{t}-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})\partial_{p_{v}}

has the following characteristic flows

\frac{dp_{x}}{dt}=0,\qquad\frac{dp_{v}}{dt}=-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v}).

(9.4)

Thus the log-price momentum is conserved along the momentum transport. We
denote its conserved value by

k=p_{x}.

(9.5)

In backward time
\tau=T-t
, the variance-momentum equation becomes

\frac{dp_{v}}{d\tau}=R_{H}(k,p_{v}).

(9.6)

Writing

D(\tau,k)=p_{v}(\tau,k),

(9.7)

we obtain the Riccati equation

\frac{dD}{d\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}D^{2}+(\rho\sigma_{\nu}k-\kappa)D+\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}-k).

(9.8)

If the terminal payoff depends only on
S
, and not on the variance
coordinate
v
, then the terminal variance momentum is

D(0,k)=0.

(9.9)

The scalar part of the same momentum transport gives the geometric affine
amplitude

\frac{dA_{\mathrm{geom}}}{d\tau}=rk+\kappa\theta D(\tau,k),\qquad A_{\mathrm{geom}}(0,k)=0.

(9.10)

Thus the momentum transport produces, for each conserved momentum orbit

k
, the transported affine mode

K_{k}(\tau,x,v)=\exp\!\left[kx+D(\tau,k)v+A_{\mathrm{geom}}(\tau,k)-r\tau\right].

(9.11)

9.1.2
Mellin decomposition of the payoff

The Mellin transform enters through the terminal payoff, not through the
Heston dynamics itself. A European payoff
\Phi(S)
is reconstructed from
its Mellin transform by

\Phi(S)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\widehat{\Phi}(q)S^{-q}\,dq,\qquad\widehat{\Phi}(q)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(S)S^{q-1}\,dS,

(9.12)

where
\Gamma
is a vertical contour in the convergence strip.

Thus the elementary terminal payoff components are powers of the asset price,
S^{-q}.

Since
S=e^{x}
, these components become exponentials in the log-price coordinate,
S^{-q}=e^{-qx}.

On the other hand, the intrinsic momentum transport constructed above is
indexed by the conserved log-price momentum
k
. At terminal time

\tau=0
, the transported mode satisfies

D(0,k)=0,\qquad A_{\mathrm{geom}}(0,k)=0,\qquad A_{\mathrm{pr}}(0,k)=0,

where

A_{\mathrm{pr}}(\tau,k)=A_{\mathrm{geom}}(\tau,k)-r\tau.

(9.13)

Therefore the priced transported mode reduces at maturity to

K_{k}(0,x,v)=e^{kx}.

To match the terminal payoff component
e^{-qx}
, its terminal value must
satisfy

e^{kx}=e^{-qx}.

Hence the payoff component
S^{-q}
selects the conserved momentum orbit

k=p_{x}=-q.

(9.14)

Thus the Mellin parameter does not define the geometry. The geometry first
produces conserved momentum orbits labelled by
k
. The payoff transform
then selects the orbit
k=-q
for each multiplicative payoff component.

We now introduce the
q
-notation by restriction to this orbit:

D(\tau,q):=D(\tau,k)\big|_{k=-q},\qquad A_{\mathrm{geom}}(\tau,q):=A_{\mathrm{geom}}(\tau,k)\big|_{k=-q}.

Substituting
k=-q
into (
9.8
) gives

\frac{dD}{d\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}D^{2}-(\rho\sigma_{\nu}q+\kappa)D+\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}+q),\qquad D(0,q)=0.

(9.15)

Similarly, from (
9.10
),

\frac{dA_{\mathrm{geom}}}{d\tau}=-rq+\kappa\theta D(\tau,q),\qquad A_{\mathrm{geom}}(0,q)=0.

(9.16)

The priced affine amplitude is

A_{\mathrm{pr}}(\tau,q)=A_{\mathrm{geom}}(\tau,q)-r\tau.

(9.17)

Therefore the priced transported payoff component is

K_{q}(\tau,x,v)=\exp\!\left[-qx+D(\tau,q)v+A_{\mathrm{pr}}(\tau,q)\right],

(9.18)

or equivalently

K_{q}(\tau,x,v)=\exp\!\left[-qx+D(\tau,q)v+A_{\mathrm{geom}}(\tau,q)-r\tau\right].

(9.19)

The Mellin character belongs to the payoff decomposition, while
the Heston pricing kernel is the priced transported form of each Mellin payoff
component.

9.1.3
European option prices

Combining the payoff decomposition with the transported Mellin modes gives

\displaystyle C(t,S,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\widehat{\Phi}(q)

(9.20)

\displaystyle\times\exp\!\left[-q\log S+D(T-t,q)v+A_{\mathrm{geom}}(T-t,q)-r(T-t)\right]dq.

For a European call payoff,

\Phi_{\mathrm{call}}(S)=(S-K)^{+},

(9.21)

one has

\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{call}}(q)=\frac{K^{q+1}}{q(q+1)},\qquad\real(q)<-1.

(9.22)

Thus

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{call}}(t,S,v;K,T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{K^{q+1}}{q(q+1)}

(9.23)

\displaystyle\times\exp\!\left[-q\log S+D(T-t,q)v+A_{\mathrm{geom}}(T-t,q)-r(T-t)\right]dq.

Equivalently, if the contour is written as
q=c+\mathrm{i}\eta
, with

\eta\in\mathbb{R}
and
c<-1
, then

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{call}}(t,S,v;K,T)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-r(T-t)}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{K^{q+1}}{q(q+1)}

(9.24)

\displaystyle\times\exp\!\left[-q\log S+D(T-t,q)v+A_{\mathrm{geom}}(T-t,q)\right]d\eta,\qquad q=c+\mathrm{i}\eta.

where
\eta
is the real parameter along the vertical Mellin contour.

9.2
Projective
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
Riccati Flow

The Riccati equation (
9.15
) can be written as the
projectivization of a linear two-dimensional flow. Write

\frac{dD}{d\tau}=\alpha D^{2}+\beta D+\gamma,

(9.25)

Define

A_{R}(q)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\beta&\gamma\\
-\alpha&-\frac{1}{2}\beta\end{pmatrix}.

(9.26)

where

\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2},\qquad\beta=-(\rho\sigma_{\nu}q+\kappa),\qquad\gamma=\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}+q).

For real
q
, this matrix lies in
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Along the
Mellin pricing contour
q=c+\mathrm{i}\eta
, the same formula is understood through its
complexification.
Let

M_{R}(\tau,q)=\exp\!\left(\tau A_{R}(q)\right)=\begin{pmatrix}m_{11}(\tau,q)&m_{12}(\tau,q)\\
m_{21}(\tau,q)&m_{22}(\tau,q)\end{pmatrix}.

(9.27)

With the terminal condition
D(0,q)=0
, the Riccati solution is

D(\tau,q)=\frac{m_{12}(\tau,q)}{m_{22}(\tau,q)}.

(9.28)

Thus the nonlinear Heston Riccati equation is recovered from the fractional
linear action of the projective propagator.

Since

A_{R}(q)^{2}=\Delta_{q}^{2}I,\qquad\Delta_{q}^{2}=\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}-\alpha\gamma,

one may write

\exp(\tau A_R(q))=\cosh(\Delta_q\tau)I+\frac{\sinh(\Delta_q\tau)}{\Delta_{q}}A_{R}(q).

(9.29)

Therefore, if

B_{\tau}=m_{12}(\tau,q),\qquad E_{\tau}=m_{22}(\tau,q),

then

D(q,\tau)=\frac{B_{\tau}}{E_{\tau}},

(9.30)

with

B_{\tau}=\frac{\gamma}{\Delta_{q}}\sinh(\Delta_q\tau),\qquad E_{\tau}=\cosh(\Delta_q\tau)-\frac{\beta}{2\Delta_{q}}\sinh(\Delta_q\tau).

The geometric affine exponent can also be computed from the projective
denominator. Since

A_{\mathrm{geom}}(q,T)=-rqT+\kappa\theta\int_{0}^{T}D(q,\tau)\,d\tau,

and

\int_{0}^{T}D(q,\tau)\,d\tau=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\left[\log E_{T}+\frac{\beta}{2}T\right],

we obtain

A_{\mathrm{geom}}(q,T)=-rqT-\frac{\kappa\theta}{\alpha}\left[\log E_{T}+\frac{\beta}{2}T\right].

Equivalently, because
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}
,

A_{\mathrm{geom}}(q,T)=-rqT-\frac{2\kappa\theta}{\sigma_{\nu}^{2}}\left[\log\!\left(\cosh(\Delta_qT)-\frac{\beta}{2\Delta_{q}}\sinh(\Delta_qT)\right)+\frac{\beta}{2}T\right].

(9.31)

Along a complex inversion contour, the logarithm in
(
9.2
) is evaluated using a continuous branch of

\log E_{T}
. This avoids branch-jump errors and is related to the
log-continuity issues in Heston characteristic-function computations discussed
in
[
28
,
29
]
.

Using this notation, the Mellin price of a European call can be written as

C_{\mathrm{Mellin}}=\frac{e^{-rT}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{K^{q+1}}{q(q+1)}\exp\!\left(-q\log S+D(q,T)\,v+A_{\mathrm{geom}}(q,T)\right)\,d\eta,

Here
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
parametrizes the vertical Mellin contour, with
q=c+\mathrm{i}\eta
and
c<-1.

Thus the numerical pricing formula depends on the projective

SL(2,\mathbb{R})
propagator only through two quantities:

D(q,T)\qquad\text{and}\qquad A_{\mathrm{geom}}(q,T).

D(q,T)
is obtained from the projective ratio
B_{T}/E_{T}
, while
A_{\mathrm{geom}}(q,T)

is obtained from the logarithm of the projective denominator
E_{T}
.
This avoids numerically integrating a separate Riccati ODE at each contour node.

10
Conclusion

This paper gives a geometric interpretation of the affine structure of the
Heston model by formulating it as a lifted local groupoid within the Group
Approach to Quantization framework. The construction separates three geometric
roles: the symplectic orbit cocycle provides the nontrivial central extension,
the affine-quadratic Heston term enters as a coboundary, and the Heston pricing
dynamics is imposed through higher-polarization operators.

The main result is that the pricing PDE and the Riccati transport are two
reduced realizations of the same lifted structure. In the coordinate
polarization, the higher-polarization operator reduces to the standard Heston
diffusion generator and, after subtracting the discount term, to the usual
risk-neutral pricing operator. In the momentum polarization, the corresponding
higher-polarization operator gives a first-order transport equation whose
characteristics are the Heston Riccati equations.

The Mellin representation then appears naturally from the conserved log-price
momentum. Each Mellin mode corresponds to a conserved momentum orbit, while
the variance momentum evolves by a projective
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
Riccati flow.
European option prices are obtained by integrating the transported modes
against the Mellin transform of the payoff.

Thus the construction does not replace the standard affine-transform pricing
formula. Rather, it explains geometrically why the coordinate pricing operator,
the Riccati equations, and the affine pricing kernels are connected. The
numerical tests confirm that the Mellin–projective representation reproduces
the standard semi-analytic Heston benchmark prices. The projective formula
also gives a direct way to compute the Riccati coefficient without numerically
integrating a separate Riccati ordinary differential equation at each contour
point.

Finally, Appendix B shows that the Black–Scholes model is recovered as the
constant-variance reduction of the lifted Heston construction. This connects
the present framework with the earlier group-quantization treatment of
Black–Scholes and other quadratic Hamiltonian models.

Appendix A Invariant Fields and Cartan Geometry

A.1
Associativity of the central extension

We verify that the central factor used in the lifted Heston groupoid defines an
associative product. Write

g=(t,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u}),\qquad g^{\prime}=(t^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}),\qquad g^{\prime\prime}=(t^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}),

and set

M=M_{H}(t,k),\qquad M^{\prime}=M_{H}(t^{\prime},k),\qquad M^{\prime\prime}=M_{H}(t^{\prime\prime},k).

The semidirect composition gives

g^{\prime}g=\bigl(t^{\prime}+t,\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+M^{\prime}\mathbf{a},\ \mathbf{u}^{\prime}+M^{\prime}\mathbf{u}\bigr),

and similarly

g^{\prime\prime}g^{\prime}=\bigl(t^{\prime\prime}+t^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime}+M^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\ \mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}+M^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\bigr).

The multiplicative central extension has the form

(g^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})(g,\zeta)=\bigl(g^{\prime}g,\ \zeta^{\prime}\zeta\exp B_{Q}(g^{\prime},g)\bigr).

Therefore associativity,

\bigl((g^{\prime\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime})(g^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})\bigr)(g,\zeta)=(g^{\prime\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime})\bigl((g^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})(g,\zeta)\bigr),

is equivalent to the cocycle identity

B_{Q}(g^{\prime},g)+B_{Q}(g^{\prime\prime},g^{\prime}g)=B_{Q}(g^{\prime\prime},g^{\prime})+B_{Q}(g^{\prime\prime}g^{\prime},g).

The central exponent is the sum of the orbit cocycle and the exact Heston
coboundary,

B_{Q}=B_{\mathrm{orb}}+\delta F_{Q}.

We first check the orbit part. For the
\mathbf{a}
-sector,

B_{\mathrm{orb}}^{a}(g^{\prime},g)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}.

The left-hand side of the cocycle identity gives

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{orb}}^{a}(g^{\prime},g)+B_{\mathrm{orb}}^{a}(g^{\prime\prime},g^{\prime}g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime\prime}\left(\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime\prime}M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}.

The right-hand side gives

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{orb}}^{a}(g^{\prime\prime},g^{\prime})+B_{\mathrm{orb}}^{a}(g^{\prime\prime}g^{\prime},g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime}+M^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\right)^{T}J_{4}M^{\prime\prime}M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime\prime}M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}(M^{\prime\prime})^{T}J_{4}M^{\prime\prime}M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}.

Since
M^{\prime\prime}
is symplectic,

(M^{\prime\prime})^{T}J_{4}M^{\prime\prime}=J_{4},

the last term becomes

\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}J_{4}M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}.

Thus both sides agree. The same calculation applies to the

\mathbf{u}
-sector, because it uses the same semidirect composition and the
same symplectic identity.

It remains to check the Heston coboundary. Since

\delta F_{Q}(g^{\prime},g)=F_{Q}(g^{\prime}g)-F_{Q}(g^{\prime})-F_{Q}(g),

we have

\displaystyle\delta F_{Q}(g^{\prime},g)+\delta F_{Q}(g^{\prime\prime},g^{\prime}g)

\displaystyle=F_{Q}(g^{\prime\prime}g^{\prime}g)-F_{Q}(g^{\prime\prime})-F_{Q}(g^{\prime})-F_{Q}(g).

The same expression is obtained from

\delta F_{Q}(g^{\prime\prime},g^{\prime})+\delta F_{Q}(g^{\prime\prime}g^{\prime},g).

Therefore
\delta F_{Q}
satisfies the cocycle identity automatically.

Thus both
B_{\mathrm{orb}}
and
\delta F_{Q}
satisfy the cocycle identity,
and hence so does

B_{Q}=B_{\mathrm{orb}}+\delta F_{Q}.

The lifted Heston central extension is therefore associative.

A.2
Derivation of the Left-Invariant Fields for the Quadratic Heston Groupoid

We derive the left-invariant vector fields directly from the local composition
law of the Heston groupoid
\widetilde{H}
given in
Section
4.2
.

The left-invariant vector fields are obtained by differentiating the product

g^{\prime}\star g_{\epsilon}
, where
g_{\epsilon}
is an infinitesimal element in
the relevant direction.

A.2.1
Left-Invariant Fields in the
\mathbf{a}
-Directions

Consider an infinitesimal displacement in the
\mathbf{a}
-sector:

g_{\epsilon}=\left(0,I,\epsilon\bm{\eta},\mathbf{0},1\right),\qquad\bm{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.

(A.1)

Then

g^{\prime\prime}=g^{\prime}\star g_{\epsilon}=\left(t^{\prime},M_{H}(t^{\prime},k),\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\epsilon\bm{\eta},\mathbf{u}^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}\exp\left[B_{Q}(g^{\prime},g_{\epsilon};k)\right]\right).

(A.2)

Differentiating with respect to
\epsilon
at
\epsilon=0
gives

\delta\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime}=M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\bm{\eta},\qquad\delta\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}=0.

(A.3)

Now compute the central variation. From the orbit cocycle,

B_{\mathrm{orb}}(g^{\prime},g_{\epsilon};k)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}J_{4}M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\epsilon\bm{\eta}+\lambda\mathbf{u}^{\prime T}J_{4}M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\epsilon\bm{\eta}.

(A.4)

Hence

\delta B_{\mathrm{orb}}=\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}J_{4}+\lambda\mathbf{u}^{\prime T}J_{4}\right]M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\bm{\eta}.

(A.5)

Next consider the coboundary contribution:

\delta F_{Q}=F_{Q}(g^{\prime}\star g_{\epsilon};k)-F_{Q}(g^{\prime};k)-F_{Q}(g_{\epsilon};k).

(A.6)

Since

F_{Q}(g^{\prime};k)=t^{\prime}\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a}^{\prime};v^{\prime})=t^{\prime}\left[\mathbf{b}^{T}\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}D_{H}(v^{\prime})\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\right],

(A.7)

its first variation is

\displaystyle\delta F_{Q}

\displaystyle=t^{\prime}\,(\nabla_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}\vartheta_{H})^{T}M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\bm{\eta},

(A.8)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}\vartheta_{H}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{b}+D_{H}(v^{\prime})\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}D_{0}\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\,E_{v}.

Therefore

\delta\log\zeta^{\prime\prime}=\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}J_{4}+\lambda\mathbf{u}^{\prime T}J_{4}+t^{\prime}(\nabla_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}\vartheta_{H})^{T}\right]M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\bm{\eta}.

(A.9)

Introducing the central generator

\Xi=\zeta^{\prime}\partial_{\zeta^{\prime}},

(A.10)

the left-invariant field in the
\mathbf{a}
-direction is

L_{\bm{\eta}}=\left(M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\bm{\eta}\right)^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}+\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}J_{4}+\lambda\mathbf{u}^{\prime T}J_{4}+t^{\prime}(\nabla_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}\vartheta_{H})^{T}\right)M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\bm{\eta}\right]\Xi.

(A.11)

Equivalently, in vector notation,

\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{a}}=M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}+M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)^{T}\Gamma_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}\,\Xi,

(A.12)

where

\Gamma_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}J_{4}^{T}\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+\lambda J_{4}^{T}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\nabla_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}\vartheta_{H}.

A.2.2
Left-Invariant Fields in the
\mathbf{u}
-Directions

Now consider an infinitesimal displacement in the
\mathbf{u}
-sector:

g_{\epsilon}=\left(0,I,\mathbf{0},\epsilon\bm{\eta},1\right),\qquad\bm{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.

(A.13)

Then

g^{\prime\prime}=g^{\prime}\star g_{\epsilon}=\left(t^{\prime},M_{H}(t^{\prime},k),\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\epsilon\bm{\eta},\zeta^{\prime}\right).

(A.14)

Therefore

\delta\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}=M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)\bm{\eta},\qquad\delta\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime}=0,\qquad\delta\log\zeta^{\prime\prime}=0.

(A.15)

Hence

\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{u}}=M_{H}(t^{\prime},k)^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}^{\prime}}.

(A.16)

A.2.3
Left-Invariant Time Generator

Finally consider an infinitesimal time displacement:

g_{\epsilon}=\left(\epsilon,M_{H}(\epsilon,k),\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0},1\right).

(A.17)

Then

g^{\prime\prime}=g^{\prime}\star g_{\epsilon}

(A.18)

satisfies

\delta t^{\prime\prime}=1,\qquad\delta\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime}=0,\qquad\delta\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}=0.

(A.19)

The orbit cocycle does not contribute because the infinitesimal element has
zero
\mathbf{a}
-component. Only the coboundary contributes:

\delta\log\zeta^{\prime\prime}=\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a}^{\prime};v^{\prime})=\mathbf{b}^{T}\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{\prime T}D_{H}(v^{\prime})\mathbf{a}^{\prime}.

(A.20)

Therefore

L_{t}=\partial_{t^{\prime}}+\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a}^{\prime};v^{\prime})\,\Xi.

(A.21)

Finally, the central generator is

L_{\zeta}=\Xi=\zeta^{\prime}\partial_{\zeta^{\prime}}.

(A.22)

Suppressing primes in the final expressions gives the left-invariant fields
used in the main text.

A.3
Derivation of the Cartan Form

The Cartan form is the left-invariant one-form dual to the central generator

\Xi=\zeta\partial_{\zeta}.

(A.23)

It is normalized by

\Theta(\Xi)=1,

(A.24)

and it annihilates the noncentral left-invariant fields:

\Theta(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{a}})=0,\qquad\Theta(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{u}})=0,\qquad\Theta(L_{t})=0.

(A.25)

For the Heston law, using the same notation as in
Table
3
, the left-invariant fields are

\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{a}}=M_{H}(t,k)^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{a}}+M_{H}(t,k)^{T}\Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}\,\Xi,\qquad\Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}=\frac{1}{2}J_{4}^{T}\mathbf{a}+\lambda J_{4}^{T}\mathbf{u}+t\nabla_{\mathbf{a}}\vartheta_{H},

(A.26)

\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{u}}=M_{H}(t,k)^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}},

(A.27)

and

L_{t}=\partial_{t}+\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v)\Xi.

(A.28)

We seek a one-form of the form

\Theta=\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}+\bm{\alpha}_{\mathbf{a}}^{T}d\mathbf{a}+\bm{\alpha}_{\mathbf{u}}^{T}d\mathbf{u}+\alpha_{t}\,dt.

(A.29)

Since
\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{u}}
has no central component, the condition

\Theta(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{u}})=0

(A.30)

implies

\bm{\alpha}_{\mathbf{u}}=0.

(A.31)

Next, imposing

\Theta(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{a}})=0

(A.32)

gives

\left[\Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}^{T}+\bm{\alpha}_{\mathbf{a}}^{T}\right]M_{H}(t,k)=0.

(A.33)

Since
M_{H}(t,k)
is invertible, this gives

\bm{\alpha}_{\mathbf{a}}=-\Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}.

(A.34)

Thus

\Theta=\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}-\Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}^{T}d\mathbf{a}+\alpha_{t}\,dt.

(A.35)

Now impose

\Theta(L_{t})=0.

(A.36)

Since

d\mathbf{a}(L_{t})=0,\qquad dt(L_{t})=1,\qquad\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}(L_{t})=\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v),

(A.37)

we get

0=\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v)+\alpha_{t}.

(A.38)

Hence

\alpha_{t}=-\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v).

(A.39)

Therefore

\Theta=\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}-\Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}^{T}d\mathbf{a}-\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v)\,dt.

(A.40)

Recognizing the exact differential

F_{Q}(g)=t\,\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v),

(A.41)

we have

dF_{Q}=\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v)\,dt+t\,(\nabla_{\mathbf{a}}\vartheta_{H})^{T}d\mathbf{a}.

(A.42)

Thus the Cartan form can be written compactly as

\Theta=\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}+\lambda\mathbf{u}\right)^{T}J_{4}\,d\mathbf{a}-dF_{Q}.

(A.43)

A.4
Derivation of the Characteristic Module

The characteristic module is defined by

\mathcal{C}=\ker\Theta\cap\ker\omega,\qquad\omega=d\Theta.

(A.44)

For the quadratic Heston law, using the same notation as in
Table
3
, the Cartan form is

\Theta=\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}+\lambda\mathbf{u}\right)^{T}J_{4}\,d\mathbf{a}-dF_{Q}.

(A.45)

Since the affine–quadratic contribution is exact, it does not contribute to
the curvature:

d^{2}F_{Q}=0.

(A.46)

Therefore

\omega=d\Theta=-\frac{1}{2}\,d\mathbf{a}^{T}J_{4}\wedge d\mathbf{a}-\lambda\,d\mathbf{u}^{T}J_{4}\wedge d\mathbf{a}.

(A.47)

Let a general horizontal left-invariant vector field be

X=\alpha L_{t}+\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{u}},

(A.48)

where

\mathbf{A},\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.

(A.49)

Because the Cartan form annihilates the noncentral left-invariant fields,

\Theta(L_{t})=0,\qquad\Theta(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{a}})=0,\qquad\Theta(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{u}})=0,

(A.50)

we immediately have

\Theta(X)=0.

(A.51)

Thus every horizontal left-invariant vector field lies in
\ker\Theta
.
It remains to impose

i_{X}\omega=0.

(A.52)

The curvature form only involves the
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u})
-directions. Since
L_{t}

has no
\mathbf{a}
- or
\mathbf{u}
-component in this lifted law, one has

i_{L_{t}}\omega=0.

(A.53)

Now evaluate the curvature on the
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u})
-part:

X_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{u}}.

(A.54)

The symplectic part gives a nondegenerate pairing in the
\mathbf{a}
-sector:

-\frac{1}{2}\,d\mathbf{a}^{T}J_{4}\wedge d\mathbf{a}.

(A.55)

The lifted coupling term gives

-\lambda\,d\mathbf{u}^{T}J_{4}\wedge d\mathbf{a}.

(A.56)

For
\lambda\neq 0
, the combined curvature is nondegenerate on the

(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u})
-sector. Hence

i_{X_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u}}}\omega=0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\mathbf{A}=0,\qquad\mathbf{U}=0.

(A.57)

Therefore the only horizontal left-invariant direction lying in both kernels
is the time direction:

\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{span}\{L_{t}\}.

(A.58)

Using the left-invariant time field

L_{t}=\partial_{t}+\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v)\Xi,

(A.59)

we obtain

\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{span}\left\{\partial_{t}+\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v)\Xi\right\}.

(A.60)

Equivalently, defining

h_{H}(\mathbf{a},v)=\vartheta_{H}(\mathbf{a};v)=\mathbf{b}^{T}\mathbf{a}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}^{T}D_{H}(v)\mathbf{a},

(A.61)

the characteristic generator is

X_{\mathcal{C}}=L_{t}=\partial_{t}+h_{H}(\mathbf{a},v)\Xi.

(A.62)

In coordinates, with

\mathbf{a}=(x,p_{x},v,p_{v})^{T},

(A.63)

this becomes

X_{C}=\partial_{t}+\left[rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}+\frac{1}{2}v\left(p_{x}^{2}+2\rho\sigma_{\nu}p_{x}p_{v}+\sigma_{\nu}^{2}p_{v}^{2}\right)\right]\Xi.

(A.64)

In this paper, the pricing dynamics is not imposed by including
X_{C}
as a
first-order polarization constraint. Instead, the Heston time evolution is
imposed by higher-polarization operators in the left enveloping algebra.
The relevant compatibility condition is not

[X_{C},\mathcal{P}]\subset\mathcal{P},

but

[H,\mathcal{P}]\subset\mathcal{P},

where
H
is the corresponding higher-polarization operator.

A.5
Compatibility Calculation for the Higher-Polarization Operators

A.5.1
Momentum polarization

The momentum polarization is

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{mom}}=\operatorname{span}\{L_{x},L_{v},L_{u_{x}},L_{u_{v}}\}.

We look for a first-order higher-polarization operator of the form

H_{\mathrm{mom}}=L_{t}+A(p_{x},p_{v})L_{p_{v}}+B(v,p_{x},p_{v})\Xi.

Since
p_{x}
and
p_{v}
are the surviving momentum variables, the coefficient

A
may depend on
p_{x},p_{v}
, while the compensating central coefficient

B
may also depend on the eliminated coordinate
v
.

The condition with
L_{x}
gives

[L_{x},H_{\mathrm{mom}}]\equiv[L_{x},B]\Xi.

Thus
B
must be independent of
x
. The essential condition is the
commutator with
L_{v}
. Using

[L_{v},L_{p_{v}}]=-\Xi,\qquad[L_{v},\Xi]=0,

we get

[L_{v},H_{\mathrm{mom}}]\equiv A[L_{v},L_{p_{v}}]+[L_{v},B]\Xi.

Hence

[L_{v},H_{\mathrm{mom}}]\equiv\bigl(-A+L_{v}B\bigr)\Xi.

Compatibility requires

-A+L_{v}B=0.

Now take

B(v,p_{x},p_{v})=B_{0}(p_{x},p_{v})+vB_{1}(p_{x},p_{v}).

Then

L_{v}B=B_{1}(p_{x},p_{v}),

and the compatibility condition gives

B_{1}(p_{x},p_{v})=A(p_{x},p_{v}).

The Riccati transport coefficient is chosen to be

A(p_{x},p_{v})=-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v}),

where

R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})=\frac{1}{2}(p_{x}^{2}-p_{x})+(\rho\sigma_{\nu}p_{x}-\kappa)p_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}p_{v}^{2}.

Therefore

B(v,p_{x},p_{v})=B_{0}(p_{x},p_{v})-vR_{H}(p_{x},p_{v}).

The remaining
v
-independent scalar term is fixed by the affine part of the
Heston symbol:

B_{0}(p_{x},p_{v})=rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}.

Thus

H_{\mathrm{mom}}=L_{t}-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})L_{p_{v}}+\left[rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}-vR_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})\right]\Xi.

Before choosing the auxiliary representative, the same commutator calculation
gives the shifted eliminated coordinate

v\longrightarrow v+\lambda\widehat{u}_{v},

and hence

H_{\mathrm{mom}}=L_{t}-R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})L_{p_{v}}+\left[rp_{x}+\kappa\theta p_{v}-(v+\lambda\widehat{u}_{v})R_{H}(p_{x},p_{v})\right]\Xi.

A.5.2
Coordinate polarization

The coordinate polarization is

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}}=\operatorname{span}\{L_{p_{x}},L_{p_{v}},L_{u_{p_{x}}},L_{u_{p_{v}}}\}.

We look for a second-order higher-polarization operator of the form

H_{H}=L_{t}+\mathcal{C},

where
\mathcal{C}
is a quadratic polynomial in the non-polarized fields

L_{x}
and
L_{v}
:

\mathcal{C}=a_{x}L_{x}+a_{v}L_{v}+b_{xx}L_{x}^{2}+b_{xv}L_{x}L_{v}+b_{vv}L_{v}^{2}.

The coefficients may depend on
v
, but not on
p_{x}
or
p_{v}
.

The relevant canonical commutators are

[L_{p_{x}},L_{x}]=\Xi,\qquad[L_{p_{v}},L_{v}]=\Xi,

with all other cross-commutators between
L_{p_{x}},L_{p_{v}}
and

L_{x},L_{v}
equal to zero. Since
\Xi
is central, we have

[L_{p_{x}},L_{x}^{2}]=[L_{p_{x}},L_{x}]L_{x}+L_{x}[L_{p_{x}},L_{x}]=2\,\Xi L_{x},

and

[L_{p_{x}},L_{x}L_{v}]=[L_{p_{x}},L_{x}]L_{v}+L_{x}[L_{p_{x}},L_{v}]=\Xi L_{v}.

Therefore

[L_{p_{x}},\mathcal{C}]=\left(a_{x}+2b_{xx}L_{x}+b_{xv}L_{v}\right)\Xi.

Similarly,

[L_{p_{v}},L_{v}^{2}]=2\Xi L_{v},\qquad[L_{p_{v}},L_{x}L_{v}]=\Xi L_{x},

so that

[L_{p_{v}},\mathcal{C}]=\left(a_{v}+b_{xv}L_{x}+2b_{vv}L_{v}\right)\Xi.

The time generator contributes the corresponding negative central terms modulo
the coordinate polarization:

[L_{p_{x}},L_{t}]\equiv-\left[r-\frac{1}{2}v+vL_{x}+\rho\sigma_{\nu}vL_{v}\right]\Xi\mod\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}},

and

[L_{p_{v}},L_{t}]\equiv-\left[\kappa(\theta-v)+\rho\sigma_{\nu}vL_{x}+\sigma_{\nu}^{2}vL_{v}\right]\Xi\mod\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}}.

Thus the compatibility equations

[L_{p_{x}},H_{H}]\equiv 0\mod\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}},\qquad[L_{p_{v}},H_{H}]\equiv 0\mod\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{coord}}

become

a_{x}+2b_{xx}L_{x}+b_{xv}L_{v}=r-\frac{1}{2}v+vL_{x}+\rho\sigma_{\nu}vL_{v},

and

a_{v}+b_{xv}L_{x}+2b_{vv}L_{v}=\kappa(\theta-v)+\rho\sigma_{\nu}vL_{x}+\sigma_{\nu}^{2}vL_{v}.

Since these identities must hold as operator identities in the non-polarized
fields
L_{x},L_{v}
, we compare coefficients. This gives

a_{x}=r-\frac{1}{2}v,\qquad 2b_{xx}=v,\qquad b_{xv}=\rho\sigma_{\nu}v,

and

a_{v}=\kappa(\theta-v),\qquad b_{xv}=\rho\sigma_{\nu}v,\qquad 2b_{vv}=\sigma_{\nu}^{2}v.

Therefore

a_{x}=r-\frac{1}{2}v,\qquad a_{v}=\kappa(\theta-v),

and

b_{xx}=\frac{1}{2}v,\qquad b_{xv}=\rho\sigma_{\nu}v,\qquad b_{vv}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}v.

Hence

\mathcal{C}=\left(r-\frac{1}{2}v\right)L_{x}+\kappa(\theta-v)L_{v}+\frac{1}{2}vL_{x}^{2}+\rho\sigma_{\nu}vL_{x}L_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}vL_{v}^{2}.

Equivalently,

\mathcal{C}=rL_{x}+\kappa\theta L_{v}+v\left[\frac{1}{2}(L_{x}^{2}-L_{x})+(\rho\sigma_{\nu}L_{x}-\kappa)L_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}L_{v}^{2}\right].

Thus the coordinate higher-polarization operator is

H_{H}=L_{t}+rL_{x}+\kappa\theta L_{v}+v\left[\frac{1}{2}(L_{x}^{2}-L_{x})+(\rho\sigma_{\nu}L_{x}-\kappa)L_{v}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\nu}^{2}L_{v}^{2}\right].

Note that the drift-correction terms

-\frac{1}{2}vL_{x}-\kappa vL_{v}

occur because of compatibility with the coordinate polarization.

Appendix B The Black–Scholes Reduction

The constant-variance reduction consists in setting

\sigma_{\nu}=0,\qquad v=\theta=\bar{v},\qquad p_{v}=0,

(B.1)

in the lifted Heston composition law
(
4.4
) in
Section
4.2
, together with the projection

\pi_{BS}:\mathbf{a}=(x,p_{x},v,p_{v})^{T}\longmapsto\mathbf{a}_{BS}=(x,p_{x})^{T}.

(B.2)

Under this projection the four-dimensional symplectic matrix
J_{4}
descends to
J_{2}
. Setting
\lambda=0

decouples the auxiliary
\mathbf{u}
-sector, and the symplectic cocycle
reduces to

B_{BS}(g^{\prime},g)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}_{BS}^{\prime T}J_{2}M^{\prime}\mathbf{a}_{BS}.

(B.3)

Thus the projected law is a two-dimensional symplectic central extension.
The Black–Scholes group law is obtained by restricting this projected law to
the one-parameter upper-triangular subgroup generated by the constant-variance
quadratic symbol. In the ordering

\mathbf{a}_{BS}=(x,p_{x})^{T},

this subgroup is

M_{BS}(t)=\exp(t Q_{BS}),\qquad Q_{BS}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\bar{v}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_{BS}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\bar{v}t\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(B.4)

Restricting the Heston composition law (
4.4
) to this subgroup,
and separating off the exact reduced coboundary, gives the Bargmann-type
Black–Scholes orbit law

\displaystyle t^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=t+t^{\prime}

(B.5)

\displaystyle x^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}+x+t^{\prime}p

\displaystyle p^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}+p

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\zeta^{\prime}\zeta\exp\!({B_{BS}(g^{\prime},g)})

where we have rescaled the momentum variable by the variance
p=p_{x}\bar{v}
.
In the rescaled momenta
p
, the Black–Scholes cocycle adopts the form

B_{BS}(g^{\prime},g)=\frac{1}{2\bar{v}}\left[-x^{\prime}p+p^{\prime}x+t^{\prime}p^{\prime}p\right]

(B.6)

The constant-variance projection of the Heston orbit cocycle gives the
Bargmann–Galilean cocycle
[
30
,
31
]
. This is the cocycle
that Bargmann used to describe the projective quantum-mechanical
representation of a nonrelativistic free particle with mass
m
. Thus, after the Black–Scholes
reduction, the variance parameter plays the same structural role as the
inverse of the mass in the Galilean free-particle representation.

The comparison between Black–Scholes volatility
\sigma=\sqrt{\bar{v}}
and quantum mass is standard
in the path-integral and quantum-finance literature. In Euclidean-time formulations,
as in
[
11
,
33
]
,
1/\sigma^{2}
plays the
role of a mass parameter. In real-time formulations, such
as
[
32
]
, the same correspondence is
expressed by saying that the inverse variance plays the role of an imaginary mass,

1/\sigma^{2}\mapsto\mathrm{i}m
.

The Heston model has also been studied
using Hamiltonian and path-integral methods. What is emphasized here is a
different geometric reading of the quantum mechanical analogy: the Heston covariance tensor acts as a
variance-dependent inverse mass tensor on the
(x,v)
-space, while the drift
terms play the role of gauge and potential terms.
