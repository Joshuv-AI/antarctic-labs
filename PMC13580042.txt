A survey dataset on digital transformation, public service motivation, and performance outcomes of local civil servants post-amalgamation in Vietnam
Authors: Hien Truong, Thien-Vu Tran
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDigital transformation and the streamlining of government structures are instrumental strategies for boosting the operational efficiency of administrative units at both the national and local levels. This article describes a dataset investigating the links between digital literacy, digital competence, public service motivation, and performance outcomes among local civil servants in the post-amalgamation (July 2025) Vietnamese public sector. The dataset comprises 380 responses from an online survey, spanning Vietnam’s six socio-economic regions, with all scales demonstrating acceptable reliability and construct validity. This dataset is beneficial for practitioners and scholars to gain insights into personnel relevant to digital skills, motivation and performance outcomes within Vietnam’s public sector digitalization and administrative streamlining.Keywords: Digital literacy, Digital competence, Public service motivation, Job satisfaction, Turnover intention, Vietnam public administration

Background

Since July 2025, the Vietnamese government has implemented comprehensive administrative reforms, focusing on the four pillars of national development in the new era [1]. Among these reforms, streamlining administrative apparatus and national digital transformation have become significantly important for local governments [1,2]. First, the national government has expedited the radical overhaul of its municipal units. Key outcomes include reducing the number of ministries and ministerial-level agencies from 22 to 17, abolishing district-level governments, and shifting local governance from a three-tier (provincial/city, district, and ward/commune/special economic zone) to a two-tier model without districts. This consolidation merges provincial-level units from 63 to 34 (28 provinces and six centrally-governed cities) and commune-level units from 10,035 to 3321 [2,3]. These changes aim to cut operational costs, advance comprehensive digital transformation, and enhance decentralization of administration and public finance [4]. For local governments, the two-tier model eliminates intermediate layers and accelerates digitalization. In this specific context, examining civil servants’ digital capabilities and public service motivation relevant to performance outcomes within this evolving governance framework become significantly important.

Although digital transformation is not a ‘silver bullet’ for public service management, these technological innovations can enhance public operational efficiency, dynamic capacity, and continuous improvement of public service delivery to beneficiaries [5]. Local government officials, who directly interface with citizens in the provision of service, therefore require digital literacy and competency to enhance the quality of public service. Instead of being a great potential, digital transformation should be associated with cultural change, leadership, information sharing, and collaboration [5]. Accordingly, public service motivation – serving as an individual psychological factor tied to cultural and leadership shifts – complements digital transformation in this study.

This study focuses exclusively on civil servants (công chức), distinct from public employees (viên chức) in Vietnam’s public sector. Civil servants operate in administrative agencies across all levels, wielding state power and executing state management functions. In contrast, public employees serve in public service units (e.g., healthcare, education, insurance) to deliver services and support state management. Civil servants rely solely on national budgets for pure public service provisions. In contrast, public employees operate in quasi-market settings with hybrid funding from government budgets and other sources of revenues. The research targets local-level civil servants across Vietnam’s six socio-economic regions following the 2025 administrative amalgamation.

Digital literacy and competence

Digital literacy refers to “the ability to access, manage, understand, integrate, communicate, evaluate and create information safely and appropriately through digital technologies for employment, decent jobs and entrepreneurship” [6]. In Europe, organizational assessments of employees’ digital literacy often employ the Digital Competence Framework for Citizens (DigComp), which emphasizes computer skills [7]. For enterprise contexts, [8] framed digital literacy through five indicators: digital reading comprehension, information gathering, creative idea sharing, safe, and efficient technology control. Beyond digital literacy, digital competence is a multifaceted concept. Jansen and his colleagues defined digital competence as integrating knowledge, abilities, skills, and digital etiquette for public authorities’ professional use of technologies across internal and external environments, encompassing 12 distinct areas [9].

Public service motivation (PSM)

PSM refers to “an individual’s predisposition to respond to motives grounded primarily or uniquely in public institutions” [10]. In the context of U.S. public administration, PSM encompasses attraction to public policy making, commitment to the public interest, civic duty, social justice, self-sacrifice, and compassion [10]. Research has expanded the definition of PSM beyond the U.S. context, particularly non-Western countries, with the extension of democratic governance in PSM measurement scale [11]. In the case of Vietnam, Trinh and her colleagues identified four specific dimensions of PSM, including activistic patriotism, egoistic patriotism, realistic humanitarianism, and detached communication [12]. These dimensions reflect the unique cultural and social landscape of Vietnam, underscoring the complexity of PSM in different context.

Performance outcomes: job satisfaction and turnover intention

First, job satisfaction is defined as ‘‘a pleasurable or positive emotional state, resulting from the appraisal of one’s job or job experiences’’ [13,14] applied socialization, structural theory, and social role theory to examine gender differences in job satisfaction in North American business employees, while [15] operationalized it using Mason’s ten-item index in government settings. According to [15], job satisfaction is an emotional response to various facets of one’s job.

Second, turnover intention reflects an individuals’ desire to leave their current job and pursue other employment opportunities due to dissatisfaction [16]. It presents individual’s desire or willingness to leave an organization. Turnover intention was measured using the scale developed by [17] in this study.

Data Description

We distributed the questionnaire survey to local government civil servants across all six socio-economic zones in Vietnam and then collected data using the non-probabilistic snowball sampling method. Initial outreach targeted 43 former colleagues in 14 of the 34 provincial-level units. These initial contacts subsequently shared the survey invitation with eligible colleagues through their professional networks. The sample was significantly expanded through successive referrals during November and December of 2025. Participation was entirely voluntary, and respondents received no financial, material, or other incentives.

Of 458 responses collected, 380 remained eligible after excluding extreme (all one or five answers) and invalid (failed attention check items) cases, yielding an 82.9% valid response rate. Responses was excluded if they exhibited extreme or straight-lining response patterns, failed the attention-check items correctly, or contained other indications of invalid responding. Specially, responses showing a uniform response pattern across the questionnaire, responses that failed the trap questions, and responses that displayed predefined logical inconsistencies were removed from the dataset. First, digital literacy (DL) follows [8] with five dimensions across 10 items: Data literacy (DL1), information literacy (DL2), communication (DL3), collaboration (DL4), digital content (DL5), digital copyright and license (DL6), advanced setting (DL7), security programs (DL8), technical problem (DL9) and media use (DL10).

Second, digital competence (DC) draws from [9], specifically tailored for the Vietnamese public sector, including 12 items: general knowledge and functional skill (DC1), use in everyday (DC2), specialized and advanced competence for work and creative expression (DC3), technology mediated communication and collaboration (DC4), information processing and management (DC5), privacy and security (DC6), legal and ethical aspects (DC7), balanced attitude towards technology (DC8), understanding and awareness of role of information and communication in society (DC9), learning about and with digital technologies (DC10), informed decisions on appropriate digital technologies (DC11), and seamless use demonstrate self-efficacy (DC12) [9].

Third, relevant to PSM measurement scale, we adopted the original work of [10] with eight items (PSM1–PSM8), including inverted items (PSM1, PSM5) to control response bias; dimensions cover attraction to policy making (PSM1), commitment to the public interest (PSM2–PSM4), compassion (PSM5), and self-sacrifice (PSM6–PSM8).

Finally, two critical employee outcomes are job satisfaction (JS) and turnover intention (TI). Job satisfaction is measured using a six-item scale (JS1–JS6) adapted from [15]’s study. Additionally, turnover intention is assessed using a four-item scale (TI1–TI4) from [17], selected for its fit with the profiles of Vietnamese civil servants. All constructs in this study were measured using a five-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).

To ensure the effectiveness of the research instrument, content validity, reliability, and construct validity were assessed (see Table 2). For content validity, the questionnaire was pilot-tested by a panel of civil servants at the Da Nang city government. Then, we revised wording based on their feedback. Internal consistency was examined using Cronbach's alpha and Fornell’s composite reliability [18]. All constructs reported Cronbach’s alpha above the minimum cut-off of 0.7 [18,19]. All composite reliabilities of constructs had a value higher than 0.7, demonstrating adequate internal consistency [18].

Convergent validity was supported as all Average Variance Extracted (AVE) values are greater than 0.5 [18]. Construct validity was further evaluated via the Fornell-Larcker criterion for discriminant validity and factor loadings for convergent validity. Table 3 shows the square root of the AVE value for each construct exceeds its correlations with all other constructs (the off-diagonal elements), thereby satisfying the Fornell-Larcker criterion - with the exception of the DC-DL pair. Accordingly, discriminant validity is not supported between DC and DL, pointing to conceptual overlap between the two constructs. The factor loadings are higher than 0.7 except for PSM1 and PSM5 (reversed questions). These two items should be excluded in further structural equation modelling applications to improve measurement quality.

Limitations

This dataset has several limitations. First, non-probability convenience sampling via snowball method introduces potential bias. Future studies should employ probability sampling by randomly distributing questionnaire surveys to avoid bias. Second, the cross-sectional design limits causal insights; longitudinal data collection before and after administrative reforms would enable comparative analyses. Third, because DC and DL lack discriminant validity, we recommend that researchers use either DC or DL in future models to avoid conceptual redundancy. Fourth, scales adapted from Western contexts require contextualization for Vietnam, potentially affecting respondent comprehension; developing Vietnam-specific instruments for digital literacy, digital competence, PSM, and outcomes would strengthen future research.
