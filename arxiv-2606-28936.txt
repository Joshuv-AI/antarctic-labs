Abstract
. The hydrodynamic limit to the barotropic Euler equations, including power-law pressure P ⁡ ( ρ ) = ρ γ P(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma} , for a kinetic nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation with degenerate diffusion is established. This extends the well-known result of the derivation of isothermal Euler equations via Fokker–Planck equation with linear diffusion. We establish the asymptotic analysis using the relative entropy method by quantifying error estimates for pressures and employing the generalized Log-Sobolev inequality for degenerate diffusion.

1.
Introduction

We consider a kinetic nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation of the following form:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\nabla_{v}\cdot\left(\nabla_{v}L_{\psi}(f)+(v-u_{f})f\right)

where
L_{\psi}
is a non-linear diffusion law (see Definition
2.1
). The distribution
f
stands for the statistical density of a rarefied gas subject to the collisional operator on the right-hand side of (
1.1
) and the corresponding physical quantities, namely the macroscopic density and momentum are defined respectively as

\rho_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f\,\textnormal{d}v,\qquad m_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}vf\,dv.

The bulk velocity
u_{f}
is defined as

u_{f}:=\begin{cases}\frac{m_{f}}{\rho_{f}}\quad&\rho_{f}>0,\\
0\quad&\rho_{f}=0.\end{cases}

The main objective of this work is to establish that the macroscopic quantities
\rho_{f},u_{f}
satisfy the compressible Euler equations with a certain pressure law as
\varepsilon\to 0
; hence, a broad class of barotropic Euler equations can arise as the hydrodynamic limit of (
1.1
).

One of the most typical barotropic Euler equations is the compressible fluid equations with a power-law pressure:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\theta\nabla_{x}\rho^{\gamma}=0,\end{cases}

where
\gamma\geq 1
and
\theta\geq 0
are constants. Here,
\rho
and
u
denote the density and velocity of the gas. In particular, the
\theta=0
case corresponds to the pressureless gas dynamics. As long as
\theta>0
, the system corresponds to the isothermal Euler for
\gamma=1
and isentropic Euler for
\gamma>1
.

For the isothermal case, Berthelin and Vasseur
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
established via the relative entropy method that the isothermal Euler equations can be derived as the hydrodynamic limit of the following kinetic equations:

(1.2)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\nabla_{v}\cdot(\theta\nabla_{v}f+(v-u_{f})f).

We remark that the linear diffusion in (
1.2
) corresponds to the isothermal pressure law
P(\rho)=\theta\rho
.

For the isentropic case, Bouchut
[
4
]
introduced a variant of the BGK model to derive isentropic Euler equations for the range
\gamma\in(1,\frac{N+2}{N}]
, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the dimension of the spatial domain. A mathematical justification of the hydrodynamic limit for this BGK model was established in
[
3
,
17
]
based on the relative entropy method. This BGK operator has been recently employed in deriving the Euler alignment system with isentropic pressure
[
11
]
. In the monodimensional case, the hydrodynamic limit can also be achieved even when shocks appear
[
1
,
2
]
.

Recently, the kinetic Fokker–Planck equation (
1.1
) with
L_{\psi}(s)=s^{m}
and without the alignment term
\nabla_{v}\cdot((v-u_{f})f)
was considered by Brigatti, Carlier, and Dolbeault
[
5
]
. The fundamental solutions are obtained in the range
m\in(1-\frac{1}{N},1)\cup(1,1+\frac{1}{N})
. The asymptotic behaviour, and the construction of weak solutions is studied under initial data being trapped between two self-similar profiles with different masses.

While the hydrodynamic limit related to the isothermal pressure or pressureless case via (
1.2
) has been extensively studied
[
19
,
18
,
7
,
12
,
9
,
16
]
, there has been no study on the derivation of isentropic or more general pressure via Fokker–Planck type equation, to the best of the authors’ knowledge. This motivates the current study, and we fill this gap by considering (
1.1
). The existence of the system (
1.1
) is not treated in this work; thus, the hydrodynamic limit result is a priori. However, more recently, the gradient flow structure and the construction of solutions using the JKO scheme for the Fokker–Planck equation (
1.1
) with
L_{\psi}(s)=s^{m}
with
u_{f}=0
are studied in
[
6
]
.

The main technical contribution of this paper is Section
5
, where the errors of pressure tensors are controlled by (a lower bound of) dissipation of the kinetic models (
1.1
). This type of estimate first arises in the work of Berthelin and Vasseur
[
3
]
in the case of BGK-type model with
\gamma=\frac{N+2}{N}
case. Later, it was extended to
\gamma\in(1,\frac{N+2}{N})
in
[
17
]
by introducing
extension map
. This extension map was recently employed to study nonlinear diffusion limit in
[
10
]
. In the current work, we carefully design the extension map to study general nonlinear diffusion. Then, combined with the generalized Log-Sobolev inequality obtained in
[
8
]
with relative entropy method, we establish the hydrodynamic limit result, which is addressed in Theorem
6.1
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the axiomatic assumptions of diffusion law in kinetic model (
1.1
) with related properties. The relationship between diffusion law in kinetic model and pressure law in compressible fluid equation is addressed. In particular, a class of admissible pressure laws – including power law pressures and even power with different exponents at end points – are discussed in Section
3
. Then, Section
4
studies the properties of the associated local Maxwellian to (
1.1
) and obtain a lower bound of kinetic dissipation by the generalized Log-Sobolev inequality. Section
5
records the key technical lemmas to establish the hydrodynamic limit. Lastly, in Section
6
, we state our main results and provide their proof based on the relative entropy method. In Appendix
A
, we check our diffusion laws satisfy the conditions for the generalized Sobolev inequality to hold.

Notations.
Throughout this paper,
C
may denote a generic constant, which may differ from line to line. We sometimes put subscripts to stress the dependence on certain parameters.

Domain.
We consider
\mathbb{R}^{N}
as a spatial domain. The parallel results should also be satisfied for the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{N}
.

2.
Kinetic diffusion – macroscopic pressure correspondence

In this section, we describe how the macroscopic pressure law at fluid level corresponds to the microscopic nonlinear diffusion law at the kinetic level. We begin by defining the non-linear diffusion law
L_{\psi}
in (
1.1
) as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a
\mathcal{C}^{3}
function and define

\Psi(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\psi(u)\,du,\qquad L_{\psi}(s):=s\psi(s)-\Psi(s)\qquad(s\geq 0).

We call
L_{\psi}
the
diffusion law
and
\Psi
the associated entropy. We assume:

(A1)

\psi^{\prime}(s)>0
for all
s>0
(hence
\psi
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
).

(A2)

\psi(0)=0
.

(A3)

There exists
\Lambda\geq 1
such that for all
0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}<2s_{1}
,

(2.1)

\frac{1}{\Lambda}(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(s_{1})\leq(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(s_{2})\leq\Lambda(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(s_{1}).

(A4)

\psi^{\prime}(s)+s\psi^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0
for all
s>0
.

Example 2.2
.

A typical class of degenerate diffusion satisfying (A1) and (A2) is porous medium diffusion, namely,

L_{\psi}(s)=s^{m},\quad\Psi(s):=\frac{s^{m}}{m-1}

with
m>1
. Then we can easily notice that
\psi(s)=\frac{ms^{m-1}}{m-1}
satisfies

\psi(0)=0,\quad\psi^{\prime}(s)=ms^{m-2}>0

for any
s>0
. Moreover, it also satisfies (A3). Since
\psi^{-1}(s)=\left(\frac{m-1}{m}s\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}
, we have

(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{m-1}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}s^{\frac{2-m}{m-1}}.

For
m\geq 2
,
(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}
is nonincreasing, hence (
2.1
) holds with
\Lambda_{m}=2^{\frac{m-2}{m-1}}\geq 1
. For
1<m<2
,
(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}
is increasing and (
2.1
) is satisfied with
\Lambda_{m}=2^{\frac{2-m}{m-1}}>1
. Notice that
\Lambda_{m}\nearrow\infty
as
m\to 1^{+}
. Lastly,
\psi^{\prime}+s\psi^{\prime\prime}=m(m-1)s^{m-2}>0
for all
s>0
confirms (A4).

Remark 2.3
.

Assumption (A1) implies
\psi
is invertible on
(0,\infty)
and
\Psi
is convex. Assumption (A2) ensures degeneracy of diffusion. Thus, it excludes the linear diffusion
L_{\psi}(s)=s
with the Boltzmann entropy
\Psi(s)=s\log s
, which is in line with the previous observation
\Lambda_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to 1^{+}
. Assumption (A3) is a local doubling condition on
(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}
, which will be crucially employed in proving Proposition
5.1
and Lemma
5.4
, which are to come up later. (A4) will be imployed to satisfy the assumption on nonlinear diffusion
L_{\psi}
for the generalized Log-Sobolev inequality in Section
A
.

We then introduce an important quantity
h_{\psi}
, which plays the role of enthalpy at the macroscopic level.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
\psi
satisfies (A1)–(A2). There exists a strictly increasing
h_{\psi}\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
with
h_{\psi}(0)=0
such that for every
\rho\geq 0

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi^{-1}\!\Big(\big(h_{\psi}(\rho)-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\big)_{+}\Big)\,dv=\rho.

Moreover, for every
\rho>0
,
c>1
,

(2.3)

h_{\psi}(c\rho)\leq c^{\frac{2}{N}}h_{\psi}(\rho).

As a consequence,

(2.4)

\displaystyle h_{\psi}(\rho)\leq h_{\psi}(1)\rho^{\frac{2}{N}}\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\rho>1,

\displaystyle h_{\psi}(\rho)\geq h_{\psi}(1)\rho^{\frac{2}{N}}\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\rho<1.

If we further assume
\psi
satisfies

(2.5)

\psi^{-1}(2s)\leq\overline{\Lambda}\psi^{-1}(s)\quad\text{for all}\,\,\,s\geq 0,

for some
\overline{\Lambda}>1
, then we have

(2.6)

h_{\psi}(\rho)\geq 2h_{\psi}\left(\tfrac{\rho}{L}\right)

where
L:=2^{\frac{N}{2}}\overline{\Lambda}^{2}>1
.

Remark 2.5
.

The estimate (
2.6
) cannot be obtained from (
2.3
), thus we impose the growth condition (
2.5
). Moreover, the (A3) condition, together with (A1), (A2), implies that
\psi
verifies (
2.5
) with
\overline{\Lambda}=2\Lambda
. Indeed,

\psi^{-1}(2s)=\int_{0}^{2s}(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(u)\,\textnormal{d}u=2\int_{0}^{s}(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(2u)\,\textnormal{d}u\leq 2\Lambda\int_{0}^{s}(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(u)\,\textnormal{d}u=2\Lambda\psi^{-1}(s).

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

We first investigate the well-definedness of
h_{\psi}
. To this end, for each
R\geq 0
, we set

\mathcal{I}(R):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi^{-1}\left(\left(R-\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\right)_{+}\right)\,\textnormal{d}v.

By polar coordinate, we can rewrite

(2.7)

\mathcal{I}(R)=\omega_{N}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2R}}\psi^{-1}\left(R-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\right)r^{N-1}\,\textnormal{d}r,

where
\omega_{N}=|\mathbb{S}_{N-1}|
.
Note that
\mathcal{I}(0)=0
and
\mathcal{I}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is strictly increasing and continuously differentiable since

\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(R)=\omega_{N}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2R}}(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}\left(R-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\right)r^{N-1}\,\textnormal{d}r>0,

for any
R>0
. This provides that
\mathcal{I}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is injective and
\mathcal{I}\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
. For
R\geq 1
, as
\psi
is increasing due to (A1), we have

\mathcal{I}(R)\geq\int_{B_{\sqrt{R}}(0)}\psi^{-1}\left(\tfrac{R}{2}\right)\,\textnormal{d}v\geq\omega_{N}\psi^{-1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)R^{\frac{N}{2}}\to+\infty

as
R
tends to infinity. This suggests that
\mathcal{I}
is surjective, hence, for each
\rho\geq 0
, we can uniquely define
h_{\psi}(\rho)
by
h_{\psi}(\rho):=\mathcal{I}^{-1}(\rho)
, this proves the first statement.

To obtain a doubling type estimate (
2.3
), we define for each
c>1
,
R>0
:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{i}(c,R):=

\displaystyle\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\tfrac{R}{c^{i+1}}\leq R-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\leq\tfrac{R}{c^{i}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:R\left(1-\tfrac{1}{c^{i}}\right)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\leq R\left(1-\tfrac{1}{c^{i+1}}\right)\right\},

where
i=0,1,2,\cdots
.
We notice that
v\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(c,cR)
if and only if
v\in c^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{C}_{1}(c,R)
by self similarity. We thus deduce that
|\mathcal{C}_{i}(c,cR)|=|c^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathcal{C}_{i}(c,R)|=c^{\frac{N}{2}}|\mathcal{C}_{i}(c,R)|
. We then estimate

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(cR)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{i}(c,cR)}\psi^{-1}((cR-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2})_{+})\,\textnormal{d}v\geq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\psi^{-1}\left(\tfrac{R}{c^{i}}\right)|\mathcal{C}_{i}(c,cR)|

\displaystyle=c^{\frac{N}{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\psi^{-1}\left(\tfrac{R}{c^{i}}\right)|\mathcal{C}_{i}(c,R)|=c^{\frac{N}{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{i}(c,R)}\psi^{-1}\left(\tfrac{R}{c^{i}}\right)\,\textnormal{d}v\geq c^{\frac{N}{2}}\mathcal{I}(R).

Since
\mathcal{I}
is an inverse of
h_{\psi}
and
h_{\psi}
is increasing as well, we have
cR\geq h_{\psi}(c^{\frac{N}{2}}R)
. Putting
R=h_{\psi}(\rho)
yields
ch_{\psi}(\rho)\geq h_{\psi}(c^{\frac{N}{2}}\rho)
. Then, switching
c:=c_{*}^{\frac{2}{N}}>1
gives
h_{\psi}(c_{*}\rho)\leq c_{*}^{\frac{2}{N}}h_{\psi}(\rho)
, which is equivalent to (
2.3
).

To investigate the asymptotic bounds of
h_{\psi}
, we first consider
\tilde{\rho}>1
. Inserting
(c,\rho)=(\tilde{\rho},1)
into (
2.3
) gives
h_{\psi}(\tilde{\rho})\leq h_{\psi}(1){\tilde{\rho}}^{\frac{2}{N}}
. On the other hand, if
\tilde{\rho}<1
, we take
(c,\rho)=(\tilde{\rho}^{-1},\tilde{\rho})
into (
2.3
) to deduce
h_{\psi}(1)\leq(\tilde{\rho})^{-\frac{2}{N}}h_{\psi}(\tilde{\rho})
. This establishes (
2.4
).

Finally, under (
2.5
), we estimate
\mathcal{I}
similarly as (
2.9
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(2R)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{i}(2,2R)}\psi^{-1}((2R-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2})_{+})\,\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\psi^{-1}\left(\tfrac{2R}{2^{i}}\right)|\mathcal{C}_{i}(2,2R)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{N}{2}}\Lambda^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\psi^{-1}\left(\tfrac{R}{2^{i+1}}\right)|\mathcal{C}_{i}(2,R)|\leq 2^{\frac{N}{2}}\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{I}(R).

Similarly as above, we set
R=h_{\psi}(\rho)
after taking
h_{\psi}
on both sides to find that

2h_{\psi}(\rho)\leq h_{\psi}(2^{\frac{N}{2}}\Lambda^{2}\rho).

We then normalize
\rho=\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2^{\frac{N}{2}}\Lambda^{2}}
to obtain (
2.6
). This concludes the proof.
∎

Based upon the macroscopic enthalpy
h_{\psi}
, we define the macroscopic pressure and entropy as:

(2.10)

P_{\psi}(\rho):=\rho h_{\psi}(\rho)-\Phi_{\psi}(\rho),\qquad\Phi_{\psi}(\rho):=\int_{0}^{\rho}h_{\psi}(s)\,ds.

Remark 2.6
.

Indeed, the relation between
\psi^{-1}
and
h_{\psi}
is equivalent to the equilibrium profile at kinetic level and the diffusion law at macrscopic law in
[
15
]
for the diffusion limit.

We then deduce the elementary behavior of macroscopic pressure and entropy.

Corollary 2.7
.

Under (A1)–(A2), we have
\Phi_{\psi}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a strictly increasing and convex
\mathcal{C}^{2}
function satisfying
\Phi_{\psi}(0)=0
.
P_{\psi}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
strictly increasing function satifying
P_{\psi}(0)=0
. Moreover, it holds that
\rho h_{\psi}(\rho)\geq\Phi_{\psi}(\rho)
and

P_{\psi}(\rho)\leq\rho h_{\psi}(\rho)\leq(2^{\frac{2}{N}+1})\Phi_{\psi}(\rho).

If
\psi
additionally verifies (
2.5
), then

P_{\psi}(\rho)\geq\tfrac{\rho}{L}h_{\psi}\left(\tfrac{\rho}{L}\right),

with
L=2^{\frac{N}{2}}\overline{\Lambda}^{2}>1
.

Proof.

The qualitative properties of
\Phi_{\psi}
and
P_{\psi}
are immediate from the formula (
2.10
) and Lemma
2.4
. It is also immediate that
0\leq\Phi_{\psi}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\rho}h_{\psi}(s)\,\textnormal{d}s\leq\rho h_{\psi}(\rho)
,
P_{\psi}(\rho)\leq\rho h_{\psi}(\rho)
.

To investigate an upper bound of
\rho h_{\psi}(\rho)
, we split
\Phi_{\psi}(\rho)
in a dyadic manner and apply Lemma
2.4
to see that

\int_{0}^{\rho}h_{\psi}(s)\,ds=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\rho/2^{k}}^{\rho/2^{k-1}}h_{\psi}(s)\,ds\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho}{2^{k}}\,h_{\psi}\!\big(\tfrac{\rho}{2^{k}}\big).

By recursively applying (
2.3
), we have
h_{\psi}(\rho/2^{k})\geq 2^{-\frac{2k}{N}}h_{\psi}(\rho)
, which yields

\Phi_{\psi}(\rho)\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho}{2^{k}}\cdot\frac{h_{\psi}(\rho)}{2^{\frac{2k}{N}}}=\frac{\rho h_{\psi}(\rho)}{2^{\frac{2}{N}+1}-1}.

Lastly, under (
2.5
), we recall (
2.6
) to find that

P_{\psi}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\rho}(h_{\psi}(\rho)-h_{\psi}(s))\,\textnormal{d}s\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{\rho}{L}}(h_{\psi}(\rho)-h_{\psi}(s))\,\textnormal{d}s\geq\frac{\rho}{L}(h_{\psi}(\rho)-h_{\psi}(\tfrac{\rho}{L}))\geq\tfrac{\rho}{L}h_{\psi}\left(\tfrac{\rho}{L}\right)

since
h_{\psi}
is increasing as desired.
∎

3.
Admissible Pressures

This section concerns concrete examples of macroscopic pressures
P_{\psi}(\rho)
that arise from
\psi
satisfying (A1)–(A3). From Lemma
2.4
and Corollary
2.7
, we have

P_{\psi}(\rho)\lesssim\rho^{1+\frac{2}{N}}\quad\text{for large}\,\,\,\rho,\quad P_{\psi}(\rho)\gtrsim\rho^{1+\frac{2}{N}}\quad\text{for small}\,\,\,\rho.

We establish that a broad class of pressure laws are indeed admissible in the class of
P_{\psi}
. We first investigate the case of power law pressures.

Lemma 3.1

(Power law pressure)
.

Let
m>1
and
\theta_{m}>0
be given with
L_{\psi_{m}}(s):=\theta_{m}s^{m}
. Then we have

P_{\psi_{m}}(\rho)=\theta\rho^{\gamma}

where

(3.1)

\gamma=\gamma(m):=1+\left(\frac{1}{m-1}+\frac{N}{2}\right)^{-1},

with
\theta>0
satisfying (
3.2
). Conversely, for any given
\gamma\in(1,1+\frac{2}{N})
and
\theta>0
, there exist unique
m
and
\theta_{m}
, explicitly given as (
3.3
)–(
3.5
), such that
P_{\psi_{m}}(\rho)=\theta\rho^{\gamma}
.

Proof.

Notice that
L_{\psi_{m}}(s)=\theta_{m}s^{m}
is equivalent to
\psi_{m}(s)=\frac{m\theta_{m}}{(m-1)}s^{m-1}
, which gives

\psi_{m}^{-1}(s)=\left(\frac{m-1}{m\theta_{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}s^{\frac{1}{m-1}}.

We first characterize
h_{m}=h_{\psi_{m}}
and determine the values of
m
and
\theta_{m}
to obtain
P_{\psi_{m}}(\rho)=\theta\rho^{\gamma}
.

We recall (
2.2
) and use polar coordinate as (
2.7
) to find

\rho=\omega_{N}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2h_{m}(\rho)}}\left(\frac{m-1}{m\theta_{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\left(h_{m}(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}r^{N-1}\,dr.

By changing coordinate
r=\sqrt{2h_{m}(\rho)s}
, we have
r^{N-1}dr=\tfrac{1}{2}(2h_{m}(\rho))^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}-1}\textnormal{d}s
, so that

\rho=\frac{\omega_{N}}{2}\left(\frac{m-1}{m\theta_{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}(2h_{m}(\rho))^{\frac{N}{2}+\frac{1}{m-1}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}s^{\frac{N}{2}-1}\,\textnormal{d}s.

We thus define
\gamma>1
as in (
3.1
) so that
\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}=\frac{N}{2}+\frac{m}{m-1}
. By noticing
\omega_{N}=\frac{2\pi^{{N}/{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}
and

\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}s^{\frac{N}{2}-1}\,\textnormal{d}s=B\left(\frac{m}{m-1},\frac{N}{2}\right)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{m}{m-1})\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1})},

we find

h_{m}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\gamma-1}\left[\frac{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{m}{m-1})}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1})}\right]^{-(\gamma-1)}\left(\frac{m\theta_{m}}{m-1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{m-1}}.

This shows that
P_{\psi_{m}}(\rho)=\theta\rho^{\gamma}
for some
\theta
, where
\theta=\theta(m,\theta_{m},N)
is defined as

(3.2)

\frac{2\theta\gamma}{\gamma-1}=\left[\frac{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{m}{m-1})}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1})}\right]^{-(\gamma-1)}\left(\frac{m\theta_{m}}{m-1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{m-1}}.

To conclude, we find
m>1
and
\theta_{m}>0
to satisfy
P_{\psi_{m}}=\theta\rho^{\gamma}
for any given
\theta>0
,
\gamma\in(1,1+\frac{2}{N})
. We uniquely choose
m>1
satisfying (
3.1
), in other words,

(3.3)

m:=1+\left(\frac{1}{\gamma-1}-\frac{N}{2}\right)^{-1}.

To determine
\theta_{m}>0
, we recall the so-called “degree of freedom” to simplify the notation:

(3.4)

\frac{d}{2}:=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}-\frac{N}{2}.

Thus, (
3.2
) can be reformulted as

(3.5)

\theta_{m}=\frac{2}{d+2}\left[\left(\frac{2\theta\gamma}{\gamma-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\frac{\pi^{N/2}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1})}\right]^{\frac{2}{d}}.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Throughout the paper, we always denote

\psi_{m}(s):=\frac{m\theta_{m}}{m-1}s^{m-1}.

If
\theta_{m}
is given as (
3.5
), then we can formally characterize its asymptotic limit as
m\to\infty
. By writing

(3.6)

c_{1,m}:=\frac{2\gamma\theta}{\gamma-1},\quad c_{2,m}:=\left[\left(c_{1,m}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\left(\frac{\pi^{N/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1})}\right)\right]^{-1},

with
\gamma
,
d
defined as (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) respectively,

\theta_{m}
in (
3.5
) can be rewritten as

\theta_{m}=\frac{m-1}{m}(c_{2,m})^{-\frac{1}{m-1}}.

As
m\to\infty
, one can notice that
P_{\psi_{m}}(\rho)=\theta\rho^{\gamma(m)}\to\theta\rho^{1+\frac{2}{N}}
and

c_{2,m}\to c_{2,\infty}
where
c_{2,\infty}=[(N+2)\theta]^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+1)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}
. In particular, since
\psi_{m}(s)=(\frac{s}{c_{2,m}})^{m-1}
, we have

\psi_{m}(s)\to\psi_{\infty}(s):=\begin{cases}+\infty\quad&\text{if}\quad s>c_{2,\infty}\\
\frac{1}{2}\quad&\text{if}\quad s=c_{2,\infty}\\
0\quad&\text{if}\quad s<c_{2,\infty}.\end{cases}

In fact, not only the power law pressure but pressure with different exponents depending on the density near the origin or at infinity are admissible in the class of
P_{\psi}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1<\alpha<\beta<+\infty
. We define a class of functions
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\beta}
as

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\beta}:=\left\{\psi\text{ verifies (A1)--(A2), \eqref{eq:d:ov} satisfying }(\text{S-}\alpha),\,(\text{S-}\beta)\right\}

where

\displaystyle(\text{S-}\alpha)\quad\underline{c}s^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\leq\psi^{-1}(s)\leq\overline{c}s^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\quad\text{for all}\,\,\,s\leq M_{\alpha}

\displaystyle(\text{S-}\beta)\quad\underline{c}s^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\leq\psi^{-1}(s)\leq\overline{c}s^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\quad\text{for all}\,\,\,s\geq M_{\beta},

for some
\overline{c}\geq\underline{c}>0
, with
0<M_{\alpha}<M_{\beta}
. Then, for any
\psi\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\beta}
, we have the asymptotic behaviour of
P_{\psi}(\rho)
characterized as follows: if
P_{\psi}(\rho)
is small (resp. large),

P_{\psi}(\rho)\sim\rho^{\gamma(\alpha)},\quad\text{resp.}\quad P_{\psi}(\rho)\sim\rho^{\gamma(\beta)},

where
\gamma(\cdot)
is defined as (
3.1
).

Proof.

First, we investigate a lower bound of
h_{\psi}(\rho)
. Since
h_{\psi}(\rho)
is defined as an inverse function of
\mathcal{I}
in (
2.7
) and
\psi^{-1}
is increasing thanks to (A1), we immediately have

\rho=\omega_{N}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2h_{\psi}(\rho)}}\psi^{-1}(h_{\psi}(\rho)-\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2})r^{N-1}\,\textnormal{d}r\leq\frac{\omega_{N}}{N}\psi^{-1}(h_{\psi}(\rho))(2h_{\psi}(\rho))^{\frac{N}{2}}.

We apply upper bounds of
\psi^{-1}
to obtain

h_{\psi}(\rho)\gtrsim\rho^{\gamma(\alpha)-1}\quad\text{resp.}\quad h_{\psi}(\rho)\gtrsim\rho^{\gamma(\beta)-1}

if
h_{\psi}(\rho)
is small (resp. large).

On the other hand, to obtain a upper bound of
h_{\psi}(\rho)
, we recall (
2.8
) and proceed similarly as in the proof of Lemma
2.4
. We observe that
h_{\psi}(\rho)
satisfies

\rho=\int\psi^{-1}((h_{\psi}(\rho)-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2})_{+})\,\textnormal{d}v\geq\psi^{-1}(\tfrac{h_{\psi}(\rho)}{2})\left|\mathcal{C}_{0}\left(2,h_{\psi}(\rho)\right)\right|,

with

|\mathcal{C}_{0}(2,R)|=\frac{\omega_{N}}{N}R^{\frac{N}{2}}
. This provides

\rho\geq\frac{\omega_{N}}{N\overline{\Lambda}}\psi^{-1}(h_{\psi}(\rho))(h_{\psi}(\rho))^{\frac{N}{2}}.

When the
h_{\psi}(\rho)
is large (resp. small), we have

h_{\psi}(\rho)\lesssim\rho^{\gamma(\beta)-1}\quad(\text{resp.}\,\,\,h_{\psi}(\rho)\lesssim\rho^{\gamma(\alpha)-1}).

Since
P_{\psi}(\rho)\sim\rho h_{\psi}(\rho)
by Corollary
2.7
, smallness (resp. largeness) of
h_{\psi}(\rho)
is equivalent to that of
P_{\psi}(\rho)
, and we deduce the asymptotic behaviour of pressures in these two extreme regimes, thus we conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Obviously, the class of diffusion laws
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\beta}
defined in Lemma
3.3
is non empty as one can easily construct
\psi\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\beta}
by connecting two smooth curves which behave as
s^{\alpha}
near the origin and
s^{\beta}
in the far field.

Lastly, in order to close the estimate for the relative entropy below in Lemma
6.5
, we further impose
h_{\psi}
satisfy the following

\text{(A5)}\quad\rho|h_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)|\leq Ch_{\psi}^{\prime}(\rho)\qquad\text{for all}\,\,\,\rho>0

for some
C>0
. It is evident to see that this inequality holds for
\psi(s)=\theta s^{m}
with
m>1
. It also holds if
h_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}
behaves as a power law of
\rho
near the origin and the far field.

4.
Properties of the associated Maxwellians

In this section, we study the properties of the local equilibria of the collision operator in (
1.1
):

(4.1)

\mathcal{Q}_{\psi}(f):=\nabla_{v}\cdot\big(\nabla_{v}L_{\psi}(f)+(v-u_{f})f\big).

Set the admissible class

(4.2)

\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\big\{f\in L^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\ |v|^{2}f+\Psi(f)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big\}.

For fixed
f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we consider the Cauchy problem

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}g=\nabla_{v}\cdot\big(\nabla_{v}L_{\psi}(g)+(v-u_{f})g\big),\\
g(0,\cdot)=f(\cdot).\end{cases}

Denoting
V(v):=\tfrac{1}{2}|v-u_{f}|^{2}
, the equation (
4.3
) can be rewritten as

(4.4)

\partial_{t}g=\nabla_{v}\cdot\big(g\nabla_{v}(\psi(g)+V)\big).

Define the free energy and information (dissipation) term as:

(4.5)

\mathcal{F}(g):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Psi(g)+Vg\,dv,\qquad I(g):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\big|\nabla_{v}(\psi(g)+V)\big|^{2}\,dv.

Then, one can formally obtain the dissipation identity

(4.6)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}(g)=-I(g)\leq 0.

Classical theory (see e.g.
[
8
]
) provides existence and convergence toward its long-time asymptotic (hence, steady state)
g_{\infty}
; we state several standard results below.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g_{\infty}
be the long-time limit of (
4.3
). Then, we have

(1)

(Mass and momentum)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
v\end{pmatrix}g_{\infty}\,dv=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{f}\\
\rho_{f}u_{f}\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

(Second moment)

(4.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v-u_{f})\otimes(v-u_{f})\,g_{\infty}\,dv=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}L_{\psi}(g_{\infty})\,dv\Big)\,I_{N}.

(3)

(Explicit profile)

g_{\infty}(v)=\psi^{-1}\Big(\big(h_{\psi}(\rho_{f})-\tfrac{1}{2}|v-u_{f}|^{2}\big)_{+}\Big).

(4)

(Constitutive relation)

(4.8)

\rho_{f}h_{\psi}(\rho_{f})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}L_{\psi}(g_{\infty})\,dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v-u_{f}|^{2}g_{\infty}+\Psi(g_{\infty})\Big)\,dv.

(5)

(Enthalpy relation)

(4.9)

h_{\psi}(\rho_{f})=\frac{d}{d\rho_{f}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v-u_{f}|^{2}g_{\infty}+\Psi(g_{\infty})\Big)\,dv.

Proof.

The items are standard given (
4.4
) and (
2.2
); we sketch the main steps. Mass and momentum conservation follow from integrating (
4.4
) against
1
and
v
. Stationarity
\nabla_{v}(\psi(g_{\infty})+V)=0
on the support yields (3). Identity (4) follows from
L_{\psi}^{\prime}(s)=s\psi^{\prime}(s)
and integrating by parts; differentiating with respect to
\rho_{f}
gives (5).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

From the constitutive relation (
4.8
) and enthalpy relation (
4.9
), by recalling the definition of macroscopic pressure (
2.10
), we obtain

P_{\psi}(\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}L_{\psi}(g_{\infty})\,\textnormal{d}v.

We now associate the long-time asymptotic
g_{\infty}
with the local Maxwellian of
f
for the collisional operator (
4.1
).

Definition 4.3
.

For
f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we define

M_{\psi}[f]:=M_{\psi}^{(\rho_{f},u_{f})},\qquad M_{\psi}^{(\rho,u)}(v):=\psi^{-1}\Big(\big(h_{\psi}(\rho)-\tfrac{1}{2}|v-u|^{2}\big)_{+}\Big).

For
\psi_{m}
as in Lemma
3.1
, the local Maxwellian is given as

M_{\psi_{m}}(v)=c_{2}(c_{1}\rho_{f}^{\gamma-1}-|v-u_{f}|^{2})_{+}^{\frac{d}{2}},

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are defined as in (
3.6
). This Maxwellian arises in the BGK model introduced by Bouchut
[
4
]
, see also
[
11
,
17
]
for more details.

From Lemma
4.1
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

For
f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as defined in (
4.2
), the Maxwellian
M_{\psi}[f]
satisfies:

(1)

(Minimization)

(4.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}f+\Psi(f)\Big)\,dv\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}M_{\psi}[f]+\Psi(M_{\psi}[f])\Big)\,dv.

(2)

(Compatibility)

(4.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}M_{\psi}[f]+\Psi(M_{\psi}[f])\Big)\,dv=\tfrac{1}{2}\rho_{f}|u_{f}|^{2}+\Phi_{\psi}(\rho_{f}),

where
\Phi_{\psi}
is defined in (
2.10
).

(3)

(Generalized Log-Sobolev inequality)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}f+\Psi(f)\Big)\,dv\,-

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}M_{\psi}[f]+\Psi(M_{\psi}[f])\Big)\,dv

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f\big|\nabla_{v}(\psi(f)+V)\big|^{2}\,dv.

Proof.

(1) follows from dissipation structure (
4.6
). (2) is a direct consequence of (
4.8
) and (
2.10
). (3) is the generalized Log Sobolev inequality, see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 17, Section 3.4]
. See also Appendix
A
. Concerning the generalized Log Sobolev inequality, we also refer to
[
14
]
and the references therein.
∎

Now, multiplying
\begin{pmatrix}1\\
v\end{pmatrix}
to (
1.1
) and integrating against
\textnormal{d}v
, we obtain

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho_{f}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho_{f}u_{f})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho_{f}u_{f})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho_{f}u_{f}\otimes u_{f})+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v-u_{f})\otimes(v-u_{f})\,f\textnormal{d}v\right)=0.\end{aligned}

From Remark
4.2
and (
4.7
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v-u_{f})\otimes(v-u_{f})\,f\,dv-P_{\psi}(\rho_{f})I_{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v-u_{f})\otimes(v-u_{f})\,(f-M_{\psi}[f])\,dv

The rest of the analysis mainly concerns the control of error between the pressure tensor

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v-u_{f})\otimes(v-u_{f})\,f\,dv

and barotropic pressure law
P_{\psi}(\rho_{f})
. From the trivial inequality

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v-u_{f})\otimes(v-u_{f})\,(f-M_{\psi}[f])\,dv\Big|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v-u_{f}|^{2}\,|f-M_{\psi}[f]|\,dv,

we aim to bound the right-hand side to obtain the passage from (
1.1
) to the barotropic Euler equations with pressure
P_{\psi}
.

5.
Control of pressure error

In this section, we prove the following quantitative estimate.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume (A1)–(A3) hold. For any admissible
f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists
C=C_{\Lambda,N}>0
such that

(5.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v-u_{f}|^{2}\,|f-M_{\psi}[f]|+\big|\Psi(f)-\Psi(M_{\psi}[f])\big|\Big)\,dv\leq C\Big(\sqrt{\Phi_{\psi}(\rho_{f})}\sqrt{R\mathcal{F}(f)}+R\mathcal{F}(f)\Big),

where

(5.2)

R\mathcal{F}(f):=\mathcal{F}(f)-\mathcal{F}(M_{\psi}[f])=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}f+\Psi(f)\Big)\,dv-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}M_{\psi}[f]+\Psi(M_{\psi}[f])\Big)\,dv.

Remark 5.2
.

Proposition
5.1
is known in special cases for
\psi_{m}
in
[
17
, Lemma 1.4]
for
1<m<\infty
and for
\psi_{\infty}
in
[
3
]
(see Remark
3.2
for definitions of
\psi_{m}
). To treat the general degenerate diffusion case, we use the extension map technique inspired by
[
17
]
and rearrangement ideas in
[
3
]
.

5.1.
Extension of
f
to dimension
N+1

Since
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an invertible
\mathcal{C}^{1}
function, we have

s=\int_{\psi(0)}^{\psi(s)}(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(u)\,du.

Moreover, it also holds that

\int_{0}^{s}\psi(u)\,du=\int_{\psi(0)}^{\psi(s)}u(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(u)\,du.

By changing variables
u=\tfrac{1}{2}I^{2}
, we obtain the following useful representation

\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}f+\Psi(f)=\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2\psi(f(v))}}\tfrac{1}{2}\big(|v|^{2}+I^{2}\big)\,(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}\!\big(\tfrac{1}{2}I^{2}\big)\,I\,dI.

Inspired by
[
17
]
, we define the
extension map

\widehat{f}(v,I):=\mathbf{1}_{\{0\leq I\leq\sqrt{2\psi(f(v))}\}},

and the weight

(5.3)

\mu(I):=(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}\!\big(\tfrac{1}{2}I^{2}\big)\,I.

Then, we have

(5.4)

f(v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(v,I)\,\mu(I)\,dI,\qquad\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}f+\Psi(f)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{2}\big(|v|^{2}+I^{2}\big)\widehat{f}(v,I)\,\mu(I)\,dI.

For the Maxwellian
M_{\psi}[f]
, its extension map is the characteristic function on a ball:

(5.5)

\widehat{M_{\psi}[f]}(v,I)=\mathbf{1}_{\{(v,I):\,|v-u_{f}|^{2}+I^{2}\leq 2h_{\psi}(\rho_{f})\}}.

Translating
v\mapsto v+u_{f}
, we may assume
u_{f}=0
. It is crucial to notice that
\widehat{M_{\psi}[f]}
is radial in
(v,I)
in the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

5.2.
Projection to one-dimensional space by radialization

In this section, we radialize the extended map
\widehat{f}
. This type of projection idea appears in
[
3
]
. Denoting the radius
r=\sqrt{|v|^{2}+I^{2}}
for coordinates in
(v,I)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
, we let

\overline{f}(r):=\frac{1}{s(r)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{+}_{N}}\widehat{f}(r\sigma)\,\mu(r\cos\theta)\,d\sigma,

where
\theta
denotes the angle between
(v,I)
and
I
-axis in
(v,I)
-space, and
s(r)
is defined as normalizing constant

(5.6)

s(r):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{+}_{N}}\mu(r\cos\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta).

Set

(5.7)

a(r):=r^{N}s(r),\qquad dA(r):=a(r)\,dr.

Notice that
\overline{f}(r)
is valued in
[0,1]
. Moreover, recalling (
5.5
), the projected Maxwellian is given by
\overline{M}(r)=\mathbf{1}_{[0,r_{1}]}(r)
with
r_{1}:=\sqrt{2h_{\psi}(\rho_{f})}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

\rho_{f}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{f}(r)\,dA(r),\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}f+\Psi(f)\Big)\,dv=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}\overline{f}(r)\,dA(r).

Proof.

Notice that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \rho_{f}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v)\,\textnormal{d}v=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(v,I)\,\mu(I)\,dIdv\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}\widehat{f}(r\sigma)\mu(r\cos\theta)r^{N}d\sigma(\theta)dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{f}(r)r^{N}s(r)\,dr.\end{aligned}

The second equality can be proved similarly by (
5.4
).
∎

As a direct application of Lemma
5.3
, we can rewrite
R\mathcal{F}(f)
in (
5.2
) as

(5.8)

R\mathcal{F}(f)=D=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}(\overline{f}(r)-\overline{M}(r))\,dA(r).

5.3.
Decomposition of Dissipation

The monotonicity of
\Psi
and (
5.4
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}|f-M|+|\Psi(f)-\Psi(M)|\Big)\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big|\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}(f-M)+(\Psi(f)-\Psi(M))\Big|\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{2}\big(|v|^{2}+I^{2}\big)(\widehat{f}(v,I)-\widehat{M_{\psi}[f]})\,\mu(I)\,dI\right|\,dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{2}\big(|v|^{2}+I^{2}\big)\left|\widehat{f}(v,I)-\widehat{M_{\psi}[f]}\right|\,\mu(I)\,dI\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}\left|\widehat{f}(r\sigma)-\widehat{M_{\psi}[f]}(r\sigma)\right|\,\mu(r\cos\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta)r^{N}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}(1-\widehat{f}(r\sigma))\,\mu(r\cos\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta)r^{N}dr+\int_{r_{1}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}\widehat{f}(r\sigma)\,\mu(r\cos\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta)r^{N}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r_{1}}\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}\,(1-\overline{f}(r))\,dA(r)+\int_{r_{1}}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}\,\overline{f}(r)\,dA(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}\,|\overline{f}(r)-\overline{M}(r)|\,dA(r)=:F.

Note that the fifth identity holds by separating the integral into
r\leq r_{1}
and
r>r_{1}
parts since
\widehat{M_{\psi}[f]}(r\sigma)=1_{r\leq r_{1}}
and
\widehat{f}(r\sigma)\in\{0,1\}
. Similarly, the last identity holds because
\overline{f}(r)\in[0,1]
. It is now easy to see that
F\geqslant D
by (
5.8
). Estimating
F
in terms of
D
is challenging due to cancellations in the integrand of
D
.
Following
[
3
]
, we decompose
F
and
D
into local and global parts and treat each case in a suitable way.

We first notice that Lemma
5.3
implies

\int_{0}^{r_{1}}(1-\overline{f})dA=\rho_{f}-\int_{0}^{r_{1}}\overline{f}dA=\int_{r_{1}}^{\infty}\overline{f}dA.

In other words, the mass fluctuations are the same for the
r\leq r_{1}
and
r>r_{1}
parts. Now, we may assume
\overline{f}\neq\overline{M}
, otherwise
F=D=0
. We decompose the fluctuations near
r_{1}
and away from
r_{1}
. Namely, we
fix
r_{0}\in(0,r_{1})
and uniquely choose
r_{2}>r_{1}
in the following way:

r_{2}:=\min\left\{\tilde{r}>r_{1}:\int_{r_{1}}^{\tilde{r}}\overline{f}(r)\,dA(r)=\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}(1-\overline{f}(r))\,dA(r)\right\}.

We then obtain

(5.9)

M_{\rm loc}:=\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}(1-\overline{f})\,dA=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\overline{f}\,dA,\qquad M_{\rm glo}:=\int_{0}^{r_{0}}(1-\overline{f})\,dA=\int_{r_{2}}^{\infty}\overline{f}\,dA.

Notice that

M_{\rm loc}+M_{\rm glo}=\int_{0}^{r_{1}}(1-\overline{f})dA=\int_{r_{1}}^{\infty}\overline{f}dA.

We similarly define
F_{\rm loc},F_{\rm glo},D_{\rm loc},D_{\rm glo}
in the following way.

\displaystyle F_{\rm loc}:=\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}(1-\overline{f}(r))\,dA(r)+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\overline{f}(r)\,dA(r)

\displaystyle F_{\rm glo}:=\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}(1-\overline{f}(r))\,dA(r)+\int_{r_{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\overline{f}(r)\,dA(r)

\displaystyle D_{\rm loc}:=\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}(\overline{f}(r)-1)\,dA(r)+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\overline{f}(r)\,dA(r)

\displaystyle D_{\rm glo}:=\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}(\overline{f}(r)-1)\,dA(r)+\int_{r_{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\overline{f}(r)\,dA(r)

Using monotonicity of
r\mapsto r^{2}
one obtains the elementary bound

F_{\rm glo}-D_{\rm glo}=\int_{0}^{r_{0}}r^{2}(1-\overline{f}(r))\,dA(r)\leq r_{0}^{2}M_{\rm glo}.

On the other hand, from the definition of
D_{\rm glo}
we deduce that

\displaystyle D_{\rm glo}

\displaystyle\geq r_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\frac{1}{2}(\overline{f}(r)-1)\,dA(r)+r_{2}^{2}\int_{r_{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\overline{f}(r)\,dA(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(r_{2}^{2}-r_{0}^{2})M_{\rm glo}.

Combining these two, we get

(5.10)

F_{\rm glo}\leq\Big(\frac{r_{2}^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}{r_{2}^{2}-r_{0}^{2}}\Big)D_{\rm glo}.

5.4.
Control of the local (critical) part

To control the
F_{\rm loc}
term, we make use of the following observation: namely, we can choose
\alpha>r_{0}
and
r_{2}>\beta>0
such that

(5.11)

\displaystyle M_{\rm loc}=\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}(1-\overline{f}(r))\,\textnormal{d}A(r)=\int_{\alpha}^{r_{1}}\,\textnormal{d}A(r)=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\overline{f}(r)\,\textnormal{d}A(r)=\int_{r_{1}}^{\beta}\,\textnormal{d}A(r)\,,

since
0\leqslant 1-\overline{f}(r)\leqslant 1
and
0\leqslant\overline{f}(r)\leqslant 1
.
We then note that

(5.12)

D_{\rm loc}\geq-\int_{\alpha}^{r_{1}}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\,\textnormal{d}A(r)+\int_{r_{1}}^{\beta}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\,\textnormal{d}A(r)=:D_{\rm cri}.

Note that the right-hand side
D_{\rm cri}
can be written as

(5.13)

D_{\rm cri}=\frac{1}{2}\left(A_{2}(\beta)-2A_{2}(r_{1})+A_{2}(\alpha)\right)

where
A_{2}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}r^{2}dA(r)
.
This motivates the following auxiliary result, which is the main technical lemma of the paper.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
b:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfy the doubling condition

(5.14)

\frac{b(r_{1})}{\Lambda_{*}}\leq b(r_{2})\leq\Lambda_{*}b(r_{1})

for some
\Lambda_{*}\geq 1
whenever
0\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq 2r_{1}
. We define

B_{0}(R):=\int_{0}^{R}b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r,\quad B_{2}(R):=\int_{0}^{R}r^{2}b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r,

for any
R\geq 0
. Suppose that

(5.15)

B_{0}(x)-B_{0}(y)=B_{0}(y)-B_{0}(z)

for some
0<\frac{1}{2}y\leq z<y<x\leq 2y
. Then we have

\left(B_{2}(x)-B_{2}(z)\right)^{2}\leq 128\Lambda_{*}^{4}y^{2}B_{0}(y)\left(B_{2}(x)-2B_{2}(y)+B_{2}(z)\right).

Proof.

We first decompose

\displaystyle B_{2}(x)-B_{2}(y)=\int_{y}^{x}r^{2}b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r=y^{2}\left[B_{0}(x)-B_{0}(y)\right]+\int_{y}^{x}(r^{2}-y^{2})b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r,

and obtain a lower bound via doubling condition

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{x}(r^{2}-y^{2})b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r

\displaystyle\geq\frac{b(y)}{\Lambda_{*}}\int_{y}^{x}(r^{2}-y^{2})\,\textnormal{d}r.

Similarly, we decompose

\displaystyle B_{2}(z)-B_{2}(y)

\displaystyle=-\int_{z}^{y}r^{2}b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r=-y^{2}[B_{0}(y)-B_{0}(z)]+\int_{z}^{y}(y^{2}-r^{2})b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r

and derive a lower bound estimate

\displaystyle\int_{z}^{y}(y^{2}-r^{2})b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r\geq\frac{b(y)}{\Lambda_{*}}\int_{z}^{y}(y^{2}-r^{2})\,\textnormal{d}r

where the last inequality follows from the doubling condition.
Summing these two and applying (
5.15
), we obtain

(5.16)

\displaystyle B_{2}(x)-2B_{2}(y)+B_{2}(z)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{b(y)}{\Lambda_{*}}\int_{z}^{x}|y^{2}-r^{2}|\,\textnormal{d}r\geq\frac{yb(y)}{\Lambda_{*}}\int_{z}^{x}|y-r|\,\textnormal{d}r\geq\frac{yb(y)}{4\Lambda_{*}}(x-z)^{2},

where we have used
z<y<x
for the last inequality and minimizing on
y
.
On the other hand,

B_{2}(x)-B_{2}(y)\leq\Lambda_{*}b(y)\int_{y}^{x}r^{2}\,\textnormal{d}r

where we have employed
x<2y
and the doubling condition. Similarly, we also get

B_{2}(y)-B_{2}(z)\leq\Lambda_{*}b(y)\int_{z}^{y}r^{2}\,\textnormal{d}r.

Combining these two,

(5.17)

B_{2}(x)-B_{2}(z)\leq\Lambda_{*}b(y)\int_{z}^{x}r^{2}\,\textnormal{d}r\leq 4\Lambda_{*}y^{2}b(y)(x-z),

where we have applied
x\leq 2y
for the last inequality.
Hence, by combining (
5.16
) and (
5.17
), we obtain

(5.18)

\displaystyle[B_{2}(x)-B_{2}(z)]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 64\Lambda_{*}^{3}y^{3}b(y)\left(B_{2}(x)-2B_{2}(y)+B_{2}(z)\right).

Finally, we notice that

B_{0}(y)=\int_{0}^{y}b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r\geq\int_{y/2}^{y}b(r)\,\textnormal{d}r\geq\int_{y/2}^{y}\frac{b(y)}{\Lambda_{*}}\,\textnormal{d}r=\frac{yb(y)}{2\Lambda_{*}}.

Inserting this inequality into (
5.18
), we conclude the proof.
∎

To apply Lemma
5.4
to
a(r)
defined in (
5.7
), we need to confirm the following:

Lemma 5.5
.

The function
a(\cdot)
defined in (
5.7
) satisfies the doubling condition (
5.14
).

Proof.

We recall (
5.7
) that
a(r)=r^{N}s(r)
. Thus, it suffices to study the doubling property of
s(r)
. Indeed, from (
5.3
) and (
5.6
), we have

s(r)=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}\mu(r\cos\theta)\textnormal{d}\sigma,\quad\mu(I)=(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}I^{2}\right)I.

For
r_{2}\leq 2r_{1}
, by (A3) (
2.1
), we notice that

s(r_{2})\leq 2\Lambda^{2}s(r_{1}).

Similarly,
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
implies

s(r_{2})\geq\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}s(r_{1}).

Hence, we obtain

\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}a(r_{1})\leq a(r_{2})\leq 2^{N+1}\Lambda^{2}a(r_{1}),

satisfying (
5.14
) with
\Lambda_{*}:=2^{N+1}\Lambda^{2}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

The delicate part of the proof lies in the estimate of
F_{\rm loc}
term, where we apply Lemma
5.4
to
a(r)
as defined in (
5.7
). To this end, we first choose
r_{0}:=\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2\Lambda_{*}^{2}}\right)r_{1}
where
\Lambda_{*}\geq 1
as in the proof of Lemma
5.5
. Recall the definitions of
r_{2}
in (
5.9
). Since
r_{2}\geq r_{1}
, we have

\frac{r_{2}^{2}+r_{0}^{2}}{r_{2}^{2}-r_{0}^{2}}=1+\frac{2}{\frac{r_{2}^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}-1}\leq 1+\frac{2}{\left(1-\frac{1}{2\Lambda_{*}^{2}}\right)^{-2}-1}=:C^{(1)},

so that

(5.19)

F_{\rm glo}\leq C^{(1)}D_{\rm glo}

by (
5.10
), where

depends on
\Lambda
and

.

We then recall the definition of
\alpha
,
\beta
in (

\frac{1}{2}r_{1}\leq r_{0}\leq\alpha\leq r_{1}
. Next, we claim

5.11
) and Lemma
5.5
implies

M_{\rm loc}=\int_{r_{1}}^{\beta}dA>\int_{r_{1}}^{2r_{1}}dA\geq\frac{a(r_{1})}{\Lambda_{*}}r_{1}.

On the other hand, using again the doubling condition on

due to Lemma

M_{\rm loc}=\int_{\alpha}^{r_{1}}dA\leq\Lambda_{*}a(r_{1})(r_{1}-\alpha)\leq\Lambda_{*}a(r_{1})(r_{1}-r_{0})=\frac{a(r_{1})}{2\Lambda_{*}}r_{1},

By applying Lemma

6.
Main Results: Hydrodynamic limit

6.1.
Statement of main results

Our main results concern the convergence of macroscopic quantities associated to (
1.1
) toward the following barotropic Euler equations as
\varepsilon\to 0
:

(6.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla_{x}P_{\psi}(\rho)=0.\end{cases}

The aim is to approximate the solution of (
6.1
) from the local conservation law (
4.13
). To this end, we briefly introduce the notion of relative entropy. This idea is based on the weak-strong uniqueness principle
[
13
]
. The entropy of (
6.1
) is defined as

\eta(U):=\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+\Phi_{\psi}(\rho)

where
U:=(\rho,\rho u)^{T}
and
\Phi_{\psi}
is defined in (
2.10
). Recall that
\Phi_{\psi}
is convex (Lemma
2.7
). For classical solutions of (
6.1
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+\Phi_{\psi}(\rho)\,\textnormal{d}x=0.

The relative entropy
\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)
is defined as

\begin{split}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U):=&\,\eta(U^{\varepsilon})-\eta(U)-d\eta(U)\cdot(U^{\varepsilon}-U)\cr=&\,\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}}{2}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|^{2}+\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)\end{split}

where

d\eta(U)=(h_{\psi}(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2},u)^{T}

and

\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)=\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon})-\Phi_{\psi}(\rho)-\Phi_{\psi}^{\prime}(\rho)(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho).

We now state our main result on the hydrodynamic limit to barotropic Euler equations (
6.1
) from the kinetic Fokker–Planck equations (
1.1
).

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume (A1)–(A5) hold. Let
(\rho,u)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
be given a classical solution of (
6.1
) having finite initial entropy
\eta(U_{0})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, let
f^{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) satisfying the kinetic entropy inequality

(6.2)

\iint H(f^{\varepsilon}(t),v)\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}I(f^{\varepsilon}(s))\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s\leq\iint H(f^{\varepsilon}_{0},v)\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v

for all
0\leq t\leq T^{*}
, where

H(f,v):=\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}f+\Psi(f)

with the dissipation term
I(\cdot)
as defined in (
4.5
).

We further assume that initial datum
f^{\varepsilon}_{0}
is well-prepared in the sense that
\|f^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}}=1
,

\textnormal{(H0)}\quad\int\eta(U_{0}^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}x\leq C_{A},\qquad\textnormal{(H1)}\quad\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{0}|U_{0})\,\textnormal{d}x\leq C_{A}\varepsilon^{1/2},

together with compatibility condition

\textnormal{(H2)}\quad\iint H(f^{\varepsilon}_{0},v)\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{0})\textnormal{d}x+C_{A}\varepsilon^{1/2},

then there exists
C_{*}>0
depending only on
C_{A},N,T^{*},\psi
,
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T^{*})\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, and
\|\eta(U_{0})\|_{L^{1}}
such that the following quantitative estimates hold for the relative entropy:

(6.3)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{t}|U_{t})\,\textnormal{d}x\leq C_{*}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

As a result, we have strong convergence of
\rho^{\varepsilon}\to\rho
,
\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\to\rho u
and
\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon}\to\rho u\otimes u
in
L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}];L^{1}_{x})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v-u^{\varepsilon})\otimes(v-u^{\varepsilon})f^{\varepsilon}\,\textnormal{d}v\to P_{\psi}(\rho)
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Remark 6.2
.

We remark that the condition (H2) is related to the following inequality

\int H(f_{0}^{\varepsilon},v)\,\textnormal{d}v\geq\eta(U_{0}^{\varepsilon})

by Corollary
4.4
. The (H2) condition ensures that the kinetic entropy behaves as its macroscopic part.

Corollary 6.3
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
6.1
and in the particular case of
\psi=\psi_{m}
as in Lemma
3.1
so that
P_{\psi_{m}}(\rho)=\theta\rho^{\gamma}
, we derive quantified convergence rate of macroscopic quantities as:

•

the density

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\gamma})}\leq C_{*}\varepsilon^{\beta},

•

the momentum

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}})}\leq C_{*}\varepsilon^{\beta},

•

the flux

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\otimes u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{1})}\leq C_{*}\varepsilon^{\beta},

•

the pressure tensor

\left\|\int(v-u^{\varepsilon})\otimes(v-u^{\varepsilon})f^{\varepsilon}\textnormal{d}v-\theta\rho^{\gamma}\mathbb{I}_{N}\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T^{*};L^{1})}\leq C_{*}\varepsilon^{\beta},

where
\beta=\frac{1}{2\max\{2,\gamma\}}
.

6.2.
Estimates for relative entropy

In this section, we prove (
6.3
) in Theorem
6.1
. We denote

U:=(\rho,\rho u)^{T},\quad U^{\varepsilon}:=(\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}},m_{f^{\varepsilon}})^{T}

where
(\rho,u)
is a local strong solution of (
6.1
) and
f
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) satisfying the assumptions stated in Theorem
6.1
. The proof is rather standard; we refer to
[
3
]
for the proof.

Lemma 6.4
.

It holds that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{t}|U_{t})\textnormal{d}x=\frac{d}{dt}\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{t})\textnormal{d}x\,-\int\nabla u:\left(G(U^{\varepsilon}_{t}|U_{t})+\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}\right)\textnormal{d}x\end{split}

where

(6.4)

G(U^{\varepsilon}_{t}|U_{t}):=\rho^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon}-u)\otimes(u^{\varepsilon}-u)+P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)\mathbb{I}_{N}

and

(6.5)

\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v-u^{\varepsilon})\otimes(v-u^{\varepsilon})\,f^{\varepsilon}\,dv-P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\mathbb{I}_{N}

Lemma 6.5
.

Under (A5), we have the following pointwise estimate

|P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)|\leq C\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)

for any
\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho\geq 0
.

Proof.

Notice that

P_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)=(\rho h_{\psi}^{\prime}(\rho))^{\prime}=h_{\psi}^{\prime}(\rho)+\rho h_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}(\rho),\quad\Phi_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)=h_{\psi}^{\prime}(\rho).

In particular, (A5) implies that

(6.6)

\rho|P_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)|\leq Ch_{\psi}^{\prime}(\rho).

By Taylor’s expansion (as (
6.8
) in the sequel), we find that

\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)=\left[\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)h_{\psi}^{\prime}((1-s)\rho+s\rho^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}s\right](\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho)^{2}.

Similarly,

P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)=\left[\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)P_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}((1-s)\rho+s\rho^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}s\right](\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho)^{2}.

Since
h_{\psi}^{\prime}(\rho)\geq 0
and
|P_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)|\leq h_{\psi}^{\prime}(\rho)+\rho|h_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)|\leq Ch_{\psi}^{\prime}(\rho)
by appealing to (
6.6
), we obtain
|P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)|\leq C\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)
by triangle’s inequality.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Under the hypothesis (H0)–(H2) in Theorem
6.1
, we have

\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{t}|U_{t})\,\textnormal{d}x\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall t\in[0,T^{*}]

where
C>0
is independent of
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.4
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{t}|U_{t})\,\textnormal{d}x&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{0}|U_{0})\,\textnormal{d}x+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,\textnormal{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{0})\,\textnormal{d}x\right)\cr&\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int\nabla u:G(U^{\varepsilon}_{s}|U_{s})\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\int\nabla u:\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s\cr&=:\sum_{i=1}^{4}J_{i}\end{split}

for any
t\in[0,T^{*}]
. First, it is clear from (H1) that

J_{1}\leq C_{A}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

To estimate
J_{2}
, we proceed as

\begin{split}\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,\textnormal{d}x&=\iint H(M_{\psi}(f^{\varepsilon})(t),v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\quad(\because\eqref{eq:comp})\\
&\leq\iint H(f^{\varepsilon}(t),v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\quad(\because\eqref{eq:minpr})\cr&\leq\iint H(f^{\varepsilon}_{0},v)\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\quad(\because\eqref{eq:kep})\\
&\leq\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{0})\,\textnormal{d}x+C_{A}\varepsilon^{1/2}\quad(\because(H2)),\end{split}

hence,

J_{2}\leq C_{A}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

Since
|G(U^{\varepsilon}|U)|_{1}\leq C\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)
where

|A|_{1}:=\sum_{i,j}|a_{ij}|

for
A\in M(\mathbb{R}^{N\times N})
by recalling (
6.4
) and Lemma
6.5
, we obtain

J_{3}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int_{0}^{t}\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{s}|U_{s})\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s.

Lastly, for the estimate of
J_{4}
, we apply Proposition
5.1
to find that

\begin{split}J_{4}\leq&C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\sqrt{\Phi_{\psi}(\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}})}\sqrt{R\mathcal{F}(f^{\varepsilon})}+R\mathcal{F}(f^{\varepsilon})\Big)\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s\\
\leq&C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\Phi_{\psi}(\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}})}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{R\mathcal{F}(f^{\varepsilon})}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}R\mathcal{F}(f^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}x\right)\textnormal{d}s\\
\leq&C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left[\sqrt{t}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\Phi_{\psi}(\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}})}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{R\mathcal{F}(f^{\varepsilon})}\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}R\mathcal{F}(f^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s\right)\right]\end{split}

Notice that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{\psi}(\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}})\,\textnormal{d}x
is uniformly bounded as

(6.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{\psi}(\rho_{f^{\varepsilon}})\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\iint H(f^{\varepsilon},v)\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\quad(\because\eqref{eq:minpr},\eqref{eq:comp})

\displaystyle\leq\iint H(f^{\varepsilon}_{0},v)\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\quad(\because\eqref{eq:kep})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{0})\textnormal{d}x+C_{A}\varepsilon^{1/2}\quad(\because(H2))

\displaystyle\leq C\quad(\because(H0)).

On the other hand, by the generalized Log-Sobolev inequality (
4.12
) and the kinetic entropy inequality (
6.2
), we obtain

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}R\mathcal{F}(f^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}I(f^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s\leq\varepsilon\iint H(f^{\varepsilon}_{0},v)\,\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}v\leq C\varepsilon.

This proves
J_{4}\leq C(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon)
. Combining all, we obtain

\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{t}|U_{t})\textnormal{d}x\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\int\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{s}|U_{s})\textnormal{d}x\textnormal{d}s,\quad\forall t\in[0,T^{*}]

for all small
\varepsilon>0
. By Grönwall’s inequality, the conclusion of the lemma follows.
∎

6.3.
Convergence of macroscopic quantities: general case

Based on the estimates on the relative entropy (
6.3
), we aim deduce the convergence of macroscopic variables associated to
f^{\varepsilon}
.

Fix
\delta>0
small enough (such that
3\delta<1/{\delta}
) and a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Since
h_{\psi}
is strictly increasing
\mathcal{C}^{1}
function on
(0,\infty)
thanks to Lemma
2.4
, we have
\inf_{s\in[\delta,1/{\delta}]}h_{\psi}^{\prime}(s)=:c_{\delta}>0
. This yields, by means of mean value theorem,

\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)1_{\{\delta\leq\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho\leq 1/{\delta}\}}\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{\delta}(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho)^{2}1_{\{\delta\leq\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho\leq 1/{\delta}\}}.

This provides

\||\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho|1_{\{\delta\leq\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho\leq 1/{\delta}\}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\left(\frac{2|\Omega|}{c_{\delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left(\frac{2|\Omega|C_{*}}{c_{\delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}

by Hölder’s inequality and (
6.3
). On the other hand, we recall (
6.7
) that
\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon})
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, and

\Phi_{\psi}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}h_{\psi}(u)\,\textnormal{d}u\geq\frac{s}{2}h_{\psi}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)

so that
\frac{\Phi_{\psi}(s)}{s}\to\infty
as
s\to\infty
. This implies that
\rho^{\varepsilon}
are uniformly integrable; there exists
\mu(\delta)\to 0
as
\delta\to 0

such that

\int_{\omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}+\rho\,\textnormal{d}x\leq\mu(\delta)

whenever a measurable set
\omega\subset\Omega
satisfies
|\omega|=O(\delta)
. On the other hand, we also have

\left|\{\rho^{\varepsilon}\geq 1/{\delta}\}\right|\leq\delta\int\rho^{\varepsilon}\,\textnormal{d}x\leq C\delta.

Analogously, we also have
\left|\{\rho\geq 1/{\delta}\}\right|\leq C\delta.

Notice that these estimates hold uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
t\in[0,T^{*}]
. We then decompose
\Omega
into four subsets:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle I}:=\{x\in\Omega:\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho\leq\delta\},\quad II:=\{x\in\Omega:\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq\delta<\rho\leq{1}/{\delta}\}\cup\{x\in\Omega:\rho\leq\delta<\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq 1/{\delta}\}

\displaystyle III:=\{x\in\Omega:\delta<\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho\leq 1/{\delta}\},\quad IV:=\{x\in\Omega:\rho^{\varepsilon}>{1}/{\delta}\}\cup\{x\in\Omega:\rho>1/{\delta}\}.

It is obvious that

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}(I)}\leq\delta|I|,\quad\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}(III)}\leq\left(\frac{2|\Omega|C_{*}}{c_{\delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}},\quad\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}(IV)}\leq 2\mu(\delta).

To bound the integral of
\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho
on
II
, we further decompose this set into two parts:

\displaystyle II_{\rm good}:=\{x\in\Omega:\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq\delta\leq\rho\leq 5\delta\}\cup\{x\in\Omega:\rho\leq\delta<\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq 5\delta\},

\displaystyle II_{\rm bad}:=II_{\rm bad}^{(1)}\cup II_{\rm bad}^{(2)}=\{x\in\Omega:\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq\delta\leq 5\delta<\rho\leq 1/{\delta}\}\cup\{x\in\Omega:\rho\leq\delta\leq 5\delta<\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq 1/{\delta}\}.

The estimate on
II_{\rm good}
is immediate:

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}(II_{\rm good})}\leq 5\delta|II_{\rm good}|.

We then estimate the integral on
II_{\rm bad}^{(2)}
. Let us define
l_{x}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by setting

l_{x}(s):=\rho(x)+s(\rho^{\varepsilon}(x)-\rho(x))

whenever
x\in II_{\rm bad}^{(2)}
. We set
g(s):=(\Phi_{\psi}\circ l_{x})(s)=\Phi_{\psi}(l_{x}(s))
for
s\in[0,1]
. Notice that

(6.8)

g(1)-g(0)-g^{\prime}(0)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)g^{\prime\prime}(s)\,\textnormal{d}s

with
g^{\prime}(s)=h_{\psi}(l_{x}(s))(\rho^{\varepsilon}(x)-\rho(x))
,
g^{\prime\prime}(s)=h_{\psi}^{\prime}(l_{x}(s))(\rho^{\varepsilon}(x)-\rho(x))^{2}
. Since

1/{\delta}\geq\rho^{\varepsilon}(x)\geq l_{x}(s)\geq\frac{1}{4}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(x)-\rho(x))\geq{\delta}\quad\text{for all}\,\,\,s\in\left[\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}\right],

we obtain a pointwise estimate: for all
x\in II_{\rm bad}^{(2)}
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(x)|\rho(x))=\left[\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)h_{\psi}^{\prime}(l_{x}(s))\,\textnormal{d}s\right](\rho^{\varepsilon}(x)-\rho(x))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{\psi}^{\prime}(l_{x}(s))\,\textnormal{d}s\right](\rho^{\varepsilon}(x)-\rho(x))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{\delta}}{8}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(x)-\rho(x))^{2}.

The
II_{\rm bad}^{(1)}
case can be estimated similarly. Combining all, we find that

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}];L^{1}(\Omega))}\leq 5\delta|\Omega|+\left(\frac{8|\Omega|C_{*}}{c_{\delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}+2\mu(\delta).

By taking limsup
\varepsilon\to 0
first and choosing
\delta>0
arbitrarily small, we deduce that

(6.9)

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}];L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

The strong convergence of
\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}
and
\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon}
follows from the following standard estimates:

[
7
, Lemma 2.2]

\displaystyle\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}},

\displaystyle\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\otimes u\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}x+2\|\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}.

Lastly, we proceed to establish the convergence of the pressure tensor as

(6.10)

\int(v-u^{\varepsilon})\otimes(v-u^{\varepsilon})f^{\varepsilon}\,\textnormal{d}v-P_{\psi}(\rho)\mathbb{I}_{N}=\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}+(P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon})-P_{\psi}(\rho))\mathbb{I}_{N},

where
\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}
is defined as in (
6.5
). We have

(6.11)

\|\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}([0,T^{*}];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\leq C_{*}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}

following the
J_{4}
estimate in the proof of Lemma
6.6
. We then notice that

P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon})-P_{\psi}(\rho)=P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)+h_{\psi}(\rho)(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho).

Since
|P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)|\leq C\Phi_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)\leq C\eta(U^{\varepsilon}_{t}|U_{t})
and
h_{\psi}(\rho)\in L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
with (
6.9
), we have

\|P_{\psi}(\rho^{\varepsilon})-P_{\psi}(\rho)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}];L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\to 0.

Combining these two, we obtain

\left\|\int(v-u^{\varepsilon})\otimes(v-u^{\varepsilon})f^{\varepsilon}\,\textnormal{d}v-P_{\psi}(\rho)\mathbb{I}_{N}\right\|_{L^{1}_{\rm loc}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0.

This establishes Theorem
6.1
; the estimate of relative entropy (
6.3
) is established in Lemma
6.6
, the strong convergece of macroscopic quantities are addressed in Section
6.3
.

6.4.
Convergence of macroscopic quantities: power law case

In this section, we prove Corollary
6.3
to obtain a quantitative convergence rate of the case of
P_{\psi_{m}}(\rho)=\theta\rho^{\gamma(m)}
. We recall some useful inequalities. For
p>1
, we denote

\varphi_{p}(f|g):=f^{p}-g^{p}-pg^{p-1}(f-g).

for
f,g\geq 0
. Let us denote
\Omega
be any subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We recall the following classical results.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
f,g\in L^{p}_{+}(\Omega)
be given. If
p\in(1,2]
, then we have

\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p}+g^{p}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{p}(f|g)\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
C_{p}
is independent of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Note that by Taylor’s theorem, we have

f^{p}-g^{p}-pg^{p-1}(f-g)\geq\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\min\left\{\frac{1}{f},\frac{1}{g}\right\}^{2-p}(f-g)^{2}.

On the other hand, we observe that

|f-g|^{p}=\max\{f,g\}^{\frac{p(2-p)}{2}}\min\left\{\frac{1}{f},\frac{1}{g}\right\}^{\frac{p(2-p)}{2}}|f-g|^{p}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality with exponent
\left(\frac{2}{p},\frac{2}{2-p}\right)
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|f-g|^{p}\textnormal{d}x&\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\max\{f,g\}^{p}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\min\left\{\frac{1}{f},\frac{1}{g}\right\}^{2-p}|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho|^{2}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{2}{p(p-1)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p}+g^{p}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p}-g^{p}-pg^{p-1}(f-g)\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 6.8
.

For
p\geq 2
, then the following pointwise estimate holds:

|b-a|^{p}\leq c(p)\left(|b|^{p}-|a|^{p}-p|a|^{p-2}a\cdot(b-a)\right)\quad\forall a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for some
c(p)>0
. As a consequence, for any
f,g\in L^{p}_{+}(\Omega)
, we have

\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c(p)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{p}(f|g)\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By applying Lemma
6.7
,
6.8
with
p=\gamma
and Lemma
6.6
, we can easily obtain that

(6.12)

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}];L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2\max\{2,\gamma\}}}.

Lemma 6.9
.

It holds that

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}\leq\|\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}}\left(\int\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\gamma}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}.

Proof.

The proof is based on following observation and Hölder’s inequality.

\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u=(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}(u^{\varepsilon}-u)+(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho)u

∎

This Lemma, combined with previous estimate for
\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\gamma}}
in (
6.12
), yields

(6.13)

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}];L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2\max\{2,\gamma\}}}.

Lemma 6.10
.

We have

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\otimes u\|_{L^{1}}\leq\int\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|^{2}\textnormal{d}x+2\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}+\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\gamma}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is based on the following observation,

\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\otimes u=\rho^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon}-u)\otimes(u^{\varepsilon}-u)+(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u)\otimes u+u\otimes(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u)-(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho)u\otimes u.

∎

Similarly, applying this lemma with (
6.13
), we obtain

(6.14)

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\otimes u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2\max\{2,\gamma\}}}

To obtain the error estimate for pressure tensor, we notice the following:

Lemma 6.11
.

For any
f,g\in L^{\gamma}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\gamma>1
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f^{\gamma}-g^{\gamma}|\,\textnormal{d}x\leq\gamma\|f-g\|_{L^{\gamma}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{\gamma}+g^{\gamma}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}.

Proof.

For any
a,b\geq 0
, we observe that

|a^{\gamma}-b^{\gamma}|\leq\gamma|a-b|\max\{a,b\}^{\gamma-1}

Hence, by Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f^{\gamma}-g^{\gamma}|\,\textnormal{d}v\leq\gamma\|f-g\|_{L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\max\{f,g\}^{\gamma}\,\textnormal{d}v\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}.

∎

Applying this lemma with (

\|(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}-(\rho)^{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{*}];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2\max\{2,\gamma\}}}.

We then recall (

\left\|\int(v-u^{\varepsilon})\otimes(v-u^{\varepsilon})f^{\varepsilon}\,\textnormal{d}v-P_{\psi}(\rho)\mathbb{I}_{N}\right\|_{L^{1}([0,T^{*}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2\max\{2,\gamma\}}}.

Appendix A
Assumptions on
L_{\psi}
for generalized Log Sobolev inequality

In
[
8
]
, the assumptions on the nonlinear diffusion law
L_{\psi}
were imposed to obtain the generalized Log Sobolev inequality. They read:

(HF1)

L_{\psi}:\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly increasing continuous function satisfying
L_{\psi}(0)=0
.

(HF2)

L_{\psi}|_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\in\mathcal{C}^{3}
.

(HF3)

\psi\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}([0,\infty))
,
\Phi^{\prime}(s)=\psi(s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

(HF4)

L_{\psi}(s)\leq\frac{d}{d-1}sL_{\psi}^{\prime}(s)
for all
s>0
.

(HF5)

There exists
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\psi^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\in(0,s_{0})
.

Indeed, (HF1)–(HF5) can be easily deduced from (A1) and (A4). (HF1), (HF3) and (HF5) are immediate from (A1). To see (HF4), we notice that

L_{\psi}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}L_{\psi}^{\prime}(u)\,\textnormal{d}u\leq\int_{0}^{s}L_{\psi}^{\prime}(s)\,\textnormal{d}u=sL_{\psi}(s)

since
L_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}(s)=\psi^{\prime}(s)+s\psi^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0
by (A4). Lastly, as
L_{\psi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)=2\psi^{\prime\prime}(s)+s\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)
,
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{3}((0,\infty))
implies (HF2).
