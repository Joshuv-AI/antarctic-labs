On { 2 , 3 , 5 } \{2,3,5\} -groups with conjugacy classes of distinct sizes
Wei Zhou, Ilya Gorshkov

Abstract




Abstract.

A finite group
G
is called an
ah
-group
if any two distinct conjugacy classes of
G
have distinct sizes.
In this paper, we show that if
G
is an
ah
-group and
\pi(G)\subseteq\{2,3,5\}
,
where
\pi(G)
denotes the set of prime divisors of
|G|
, then
G\cong S_{3}
.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
: 20E45, 20D60.

Keywords:
finite group,
ah
-group, rational group, conjugacy class.

Introduction

In this paper, all groups considered are finite.
The sizes of conjugacy classes carry essential information about the algebraic structure of a finite group.
For example, Burnside
[
1
]
proved that a simple group has no conjugacy class of prime power size,
and Itô
[
2
]
showed that any group with only one possible conjugacy class size other than
1
is nilpotent.

A group in which distinct conjugacy classes have distinct sizes is called an anti-homogeneous group, or simply an
ah
-group.
The unique known non-trivial
ah
-group is the symmetric group
S_{3}
, whose conjugacy class sizes are exactly
1
,
2
, and
3
.
In 1973, Markel
[
3
]
proposed the famous
S_{3}
-conjecture: any non-trivial finite
ah
-group is isomorphic to
S_{3}
.

Many researchers have been interested in this conjecture, and it has been completely resolved for solvable groups.
Markel
[
3
]
first verified the
S_{3}
-conjecture for all supersolvable groups.
Gilotti
[
4
]
verified it for
\{2,3\}
-groups and
\{2,5\}
-groups,
and Ward
[
5
]
verified it for
\{2,3\}
-groups
or solvable groups containing a self-centralizing element of order
p
for some prime
p
.
The conjecture for all solvable groups was independently verified
by Zhang
[
6
]

and by Knörr, Lempken, and Thielcke
[
7
]
.

However, the general non-solvable cases remain open.
Partial progress was made by Arad, Muzychuk, and Oliver
[
8
]
,
who proved that the non-abelian socle of an
ah
-group must be isomorphic to specific groups.
They also showed that if
G
is an
ah
-group with
\pi(G)\not\subseteq\{2,3,5,7\}
,
then the Fitting subgroup
{\mathrm{F}}(G)>1
.
Luo and Liu
[
9
]
showed that every finite non-abelian simple group is not an
ah
-group.

In this paper, we extend the investigation of the
S_{3}
-conjecture to some non-solvable groups.
Our research focuses on
\{2,3,5\}
-groups.
We prove the following main result,
which contributes towards a full proof of the
S_{3}
-conjecture.

Main Theorem
.

If
G
is an
ah
-group and
\pi(G)\subseteq\{2,3,5\}
, then
G\cong S_{3}
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
g\in G
.
If
|{\mathrm{C}}_{G}(g)|=p
where

is a prime, then
|G|_{p}=p
.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
be such that
|{\mathrm{C}}_{G}(g)|=p
.
We have
g\in{\mathrm{C}}_{G}(g)
.
Therefore
|g|=p
.
Let
P
be the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
which contains
g
.
We know that
{\mathrm{Z}}(P)\leq{\mathrm{C}}_{G}(g)
.
Therefore
{\mathrm{Z}}(P)=\langle g\rangle
, which implies that
P={\mathrm{C}}_{P}(g)=\langle g\rangle
.
Hence
|G|_{p}=p
.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
G
be a finite group,

, and

{\overline{G}}=G/K
.
Suppose

Proof.

Suppose that

Since

Let

be a Sylow

-subgroup of

Since

Lemma 13
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
.
Write
\alpha
for the natural homomorphism from
G
onto
G/N
.
For an element
x\in G
, let
\overline{x}=\alpha(x)
and let
\overline{X}
be the conjugacy class of
\overline{x}
in
G/N
.
Suppose that the preimage
\alpha^{-1}(\overline{X})
partitions into
k

-conjugacy classes
with representatives

. We have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dfrac{1}{|{\mathrm{C}}_{G}(x_{i})|}=\dfrac{1}{|{\mathrm{C}}_{G/N}(\overline{x})|}.

Proof.

We have
|{\overline{X}}|=\dfrac{|G/N|}{|{\mathrm{C}}_{G/N}({\overline{x}})|}
.
Since
\alpha
is a homomorphism with kernel
N
,

|\alpha^{-1}({\overline{X}})|=|N||{\overline{X}}|=|N|\dfrac{|G/N|}{|{\mathrm{C}}_{G/N}({\overline{x}})|}=\dfrac{|G|}{|{\mathrm{C}}_{G/N}({\overline{x}})|}
.
On the other hand,
|\alpha^{-1}({\overline{X}})|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|x_{i}^{G}|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dfrac{|G|}{|{\mathrm{C}}_{G}(x_{i})|}
.
Hence
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dfrac{1}{|{\mathrm{C}}_{G}(x_{i})|}=\dfrac{1}{|{\mathrm{C}}_{G/N}(\overline{x})|}
.
∎

Lemma 14
