1 Introduction
Programming systems are the first point of contact between humans and computers. Even in the era of AI
and state-of-the-art large language models (LLMs) that lowered the cost of code generation dramatically,
programming HPC systems requires careful understanding of the hardware, algorithms, software dependen-
cies (e.g. compilers, libraries), facility requirements, and domain science [84].
The report provides an overview of the funded activities, accomplishments and future directions of the
S4PST software portfolio for the 2024-2025 period. Section 1 provides a description of the project, its prin-
ciples, current portfolio, budget structures, and project level highlights summary. Section 2 highlights the
stewardship activities across the 10 core software products (8 funded, 2 unfunded) impacting scientific ap-
plication users, collaborations, sustainability activities, and outreach. Incubation projects accomplishments
are outlined in Section 3 exploring AI-assisted software development, and high-productivity languages for
HPC. Finally, project’s future goals and new directions aimed at to maximize the impact to DOE is presented
in Section 4.
1.1 S4PST Overview, Products, and Budgets
S4PST is part of the DOE’s Office of Science ASCR-funded NGSST projects. Established as a phase-
I seed project in calendar year (CY) 2023 and funded for a period of five years (CY 2024-2028) during
phase-II; S4PST gathers a portfolio of investments for the programmability of HPC systems in which the
high-specialized role of the stewards is critical. We highlight that S4PST funding is complementary to
larger investments in the broader landscape of computing programming. As such, our vision and mission,
and principles are stated as follows:

 S4PST Vision
 “For the US Department of Energy and the scientific community to have access to an impactful
 portfolio of vendor-neutral programming systems and tools properly advanced for science.”

 S4PST Mission
 “To deliver an impactful portfolio of programming systems and tools stewarded by a highly-
 specialized community for advanced scientific computing driven by AI, quantum computing, and
 energy advantage to fulfill the US Department of Energy mission.”

 S4PST Principles

 • Stewardship in day-to-day activities to enable scientific users via bug fixes, consultancy, best
 practices, etc. preserving the legacy of the Exascale Computing Project (2016-2023) [49].

 • Community: participation representing DOE best interests in the evolution of programming sys-
 tems via tutorials, standard committees, conferences (e.g. SC), etc.

 • Advancement to impact new science needs, e.g. AI, Quantum Computing, and upcoming ASCR
 facilities, (e.g. Discovery, Doudna).

 • Partnership co-funded activities with relevant software stakeholders, e.g. ASCR research and
 facilties, NNSA, vendors, foundations, CASS, and the broader community.

 • Workforce: training and elevating the next generation of HPC programming experts (internships,
 mentoring, recognition).

2.1 Kokkos
Kokkos™ [90] is a production-level solution for writing modern C++ applications in a hardware-agnostic
way. Kokkos originated at SNL in 2011 and is now a Linux HPSF foundation product.
Budget and Personnel S4PST provides important funding for Kokkos to engage with the CASS commu-
nity and support Aurora ($150k/year at Sandia, $100k/year at ORNL). Primary funding comes from OLCF
($750k/year), NNSA CSSE ($2,000k/year), and French CEA (about 4 FTEs), with approximately 75% used
for software sustainability.
Addressing DOE mission Kokkos is a critical within DOE’s HPC efforts as demonstrated by:
• User support to relevant applications and libraries relying on Kokkos including LAMMPS [85], deal.II [3],
  Trilinos [42, 53], PETSc [7], NALU-Wind [81], HACC [39, 41], and E3SM [37].
• Citations: primary Kokkos publications [25, 91] have 1,300 and 540 citations, respectively.
• Other metrics include 2,500 Slack members, 2,400 GitHub stars, and over 100 public repositories tagged
  with ”Kokkos”.
Highlights and Awards Key achievements include:
• Gordon Bell applications: SC25 finalist [29] co-authors, while continued support for SC23 winner [83].
• The Software earned the OpenSSF Best Practices Badge Recognition.
• HPSF efforts won the Best HPC Collaboration Award by HPCwire at SC25.

• Best practices: test-driven development, multi-review code changes, CI across diverse hardware/toolchains
  including the Frank system provided by PESO, automated checks, and cryptographically signed releases.
• Sustainment: 5 FTEs covers toolchain adaptation, performance tuning, and user support.
• Kokkos members are part of the C++ standards committee and facilitated the inclusion of BLAS linear
  algebra subroutines in C++26.
• Ethan Puyaubreau, an undergraduate ORNL summer 2025 intern from Paris-Saclay University, France,
  worked on Kokkos’ performance tool capabilities to analyze energy usage of HPC applications. The
  results were presented at the Smoky Mountains Computational Sciences and Engineering Conference [63].

2.2 OpenMP and OpenACC
The OpenMP [21] and OpenACC [98] standards define pragmas providing a portable and scalable model
with a simple interface for developing parallel applications.
Budget and Personnel S4PST provides funding to support two 100% Graduate Students in the University
of Delaware’s Department of Computer & Information Sciences in years 1-5, 5 Undergrad Students for 15
hours per week for 8 weeks, and 1 month PI effort in years 1-5. In addition, it partially covers two ORNL
staff members at a 0.4 FTE level.
Addressing DOE mission OpenMP and OpenACC are widely adopted in science, include DOE applica-
tions [27], and supported on multiple platforms. S4PST funding ensures OpenMP and OpenACC compilers
are tested and validated as implementations evolve. Efforts include:

• Regularly polling application users for novel features in OpenMP’s implementation in LLVM
• Extensive testsuite execution across major compilers and large DOE systems (Frontier, Perlmutter) with
  weekly SPEChpc2021 benchmarking for performance evaluation, regression detection.
• Serving as backends to frameworks like Kokkos, while being in deep partnership with LLVM develop-
  ment, both also funded by S4PST
• Advancing the software partnering with research projects on LLM-driven Validation and Verification

• Infrastructure advances include operational OpenMP offloading on AMD MI300A and full OpenMP/Ope-
  nACC validation suites running on Google Cloud Platform.
• Poster presented on NVIDIA Blackwell Architecture microbenchmarks at IEEE HiPC, with deeper archi-
  tectural analysis accepted to IPDPS 2026 [47] and LLM4VV [80] accepted to IEEE HiPC 2025.
• Three publications highlight progress in compiler V&V: LLM4VV: Exploring LLM-as-a-Judge for Val-
  idation and Verification Testsuites [79], CI/CD Efforts for Validation, Verification and Benchmarking
  OpenMP Implementations [46], and LLM4VV: LLM-driven testsuite for compiler validation [59]
Best practices, sustainability and workforce
• LLVM OpenMP offloading CI/CD pipelines continue to mature with regular updates to OpenMP and
  OpenACC testsuites, which enabled OpenMP 6.0 tests and infrastructure enhancements
• Sustained performance test environment has been built through CI/CD execution and benchmarking on
  GH200, MI300A, and RTX6000 GPUs demonstrate portability and reproducibility
2.3 LLVM
LLVM [50] is a highly modular compiler framework widely adopted by vendors and stakeholders (including
DOE) for backend implementations of vendor-neutral programming systems.
Budget and Personnel LLVM receives S4PST funding at less than 0.08 FTE per year at LLNL, supporting
Johannes Doerfert (currently on extended leave) and Ignacio Laguna (PI, project coordination). Kevin Sala
and Konstantinos Parasyris have assumed Doerfert’s responsibilities during his absence. Additionally, Jan

at NERSC collaborates extensively with the S4PST team through NERSC’s funding, and LLNL and ORNL
receive NNSA funding for extensive LLVM compiler R&D support for HPC.
Addressing DOE mission DOE has been successful in LLVM development and stewardship during ECP.
S4PST continues funding stewardship activities in Flang, OpenMP, parallel program optimization, tooling,
and ML enhancements through code development, meetings organization, and academic research. This
stewardship steers vendor development and collaboration for HPC community benefit while connecting
DOE efforts including Flang/Fortran, Kokkos/C++, and OpenMP with upstream LLVM development.

2.4 Fortran
Fortran [28] supports critical DOE science in numerous fields including those application users reported
by ASCR facilities NERSC and OLCF. LBNL’s S4PST stewardship efforts have focused on Fortran stan-
dardization and ensuring language support for advanced parallel and GPU programming for HPC and AI.
Our sustainment work supports standard language features for Single-Program Multiple-Data (SPMD) par-
allelism with a Partitioned Global Address Space (PGAS). LBNL has also contributed to robust compiler
support for Fortran’s do concurrent parallel loop construct by co-developing advanced use cases with
LLVM compiler developers.
Budget and Personnel The LBNL S4PST Fortran budget funds approximately 0.3 FTE annually spread
primarily across three personnel.
Addressing DOE mission LBNL’s participation in Fortran standardization committee work contributes
to DOE’s mission by ensuring that developments to the language align with the needs of the DOE HPC
community. The LBNL team’s committee work is guided by the needs and experiences of users at NERSC
(ASCR facility). LBNL hosted four in-person Fortran standards meetings in the past two years, providing
a venue for the committee to make progress on future Fortran standards that will help support the needs of
the DOE scientific community.

• We co-founded an international committee to write and maintain the Parallel Runtime Interface for Fortran
  (PRIF) [14], a compiler- and runtime-library-agnostic interface supporting compiler implementations of
  SPMD/PGAS features [15–17, 61, 62].
• We presented papers on PRIF at the LLVM-HPC’24 [14] and LLVM-HPC’25 [19] workshops.

2.5 Legion
Legion [8,86] is a data-centric asynchronous task-based runtime for distributed and heterogeneous computa-
tion. Through logical regions, Legion decouples parallel algorithm specification from architecture mapping
in a machine-independent manner. The runtime automatically extracts parallelism from task dependencies,
manages data movement across memory hierarchies, and provides a mapping interface for controlling task
and data placement without compromising correctness. Built on the Realm [87] event-based runtime, Legion
has demonstrated strong scalability on leadership-class supercomputers.
Budget and Personnel The Legion team at SLAC and LANL split $100K funding equally to Alex Aiken
and Pat McCormick at each institution, or about 1/8 of an FTE each. In late CY25, SLAC received a 3-
year US Department of Defense (DoD) grant for AI-based compilation work using Legion ($2.1M), which
will support about 1.3 FTE. Additionally, SLAC and LANL secured funding from the Air Force Research
Laboratory (AFRL) for cuPyNumeric, NVIDIA’s Legion-based Python library.
Addressing DOE mission Collaboration continues with Jackie Chen’s team at SNL on Legion versions of
S3D, a combustion simulation code [88]. Current work focuses on a new algorithm utilizing reduced-order
models expected to enable simulations orders of magnitude larger than previously possible. Implementation
and validation testing are mostly complete, with remaining work focused on full machine scaling.

• Paper published at the PAW-ATM workshop at SC25 presented a Legion-based Krylov solver library [99]
  demonstrating performance comparable to or exceeding Trilinos and PETSc. The Legion library provides
  significant performance advantages for in-situ data operations, avoiding required copies and supporting
  matrices distributed across locations and formats.
• The team organized “The Legion Programming Model” tutorial at SC24.
Best practices, Sustainability, and Workforce To enhance modularity, the team extracted Legion’s low-
level tasking runtime into the standalone Realm package [87, 89], now maintained separately [82]. This
supports applications requiring event-based tasking without the full Legion model and has attracted active
industry contributors.
The team resolved persistent non-deterministic failures at scale on Slingshot networking systems including
Frontier and El Capitan. Through collaboration with HPE and the GASNet project (§2.8), multiple root
causes were diagnosed and addressed. Full resolution is anticipated within the coming year.

2.6 MPICH
MPICH is a widely used implementation of the Message Passing Interface (MPI) [57], the de-facto standard
for writing portable message-passing communication programs in C, C++, and Fortran targeting parallel
computing architectures.
Budget and Personnel MPICH is funded by S4PST for less than 0.5 FTE per year, covering small frac-

2.8 GASNet and Caffeine
This project was proposed as a “Scale-up” activity titled “UPC++ and GASNet”. Due to limited funding and
to better meet the needs of scientific-computing communities contributing to DOE’s mission, the focus has
shifted to sustainment of GASNet-EX in support of the Legion project (§2.5) and development of Caffeine,
a GASNet-EX client that complements standardization efforts in the Fortran project (§2.4).
GASNet-EX [10, 11, 32] is a portable, high-performance communications middleware library leveraging
hardware support to implement APIs that provide a tight semantic match to client requirements, often yield-
ing better performance than competing libraries. It provides communication services to a broad ecosystem of
alternative HPC programming models, including UPC++, Legion, Chapel, UPC and multi-process Fortran.
Caffeine [18, 51, 74] is a parallel runtime library implementing PRIF [14] to provide compiler-agnostic
runtime support for the multi-process parallel features of Fortran, using GASNet-EX for communication.
Budget and Personnel Total funding for GASNet-EX is roughly 0.6 FTE, of which S4PST provides just
0.05 FTE. This is the only funding for general GASNet-EX sustainment activities. The DoD funds the
remainder of GASNet-EX work, specifically to support the Chapel programming language.
Caffeine is included here due to its technical relationship with GASNet-EX, though it is funded via the
Fortran project (§2.4). This investment has produced a production-quality runtime library that enables sig-
nificant expansion of Fortran 2023 parallel feature compliance in the forthcoming LLVM Flang v.22.
Addressing DOE mission Example uses of GASNet-EX in support of DOE mission goals include ap-
plications (primarily at the NNSA) written using Legion or its Realm library when run on HPE Cray EX
systems (e.g., Aurora, Frontier and Perlmutter) or InfiniBand clusters, and use at DOE’s Joint Genome
Institute [43] of the MetaHipMer [34, 58] (MHM) de novo metagenome short-read assembler, written in
UPC++ [5, 6, 13]. Caffeine supports DOE mission goals by improving compiler support for HPC features
of the Fortran standard. The DOE Fortran community is coalescing around use of the LLVM Flang Fortran
compiler, which appears in multiple HPC vendor road-maps. The latest release of Flang (v.21) supports most
features in the Fortran standard [28] but notably lacks support for Fortran’s SPMD parallelism. Our team

2.9 HIP
HIP is a widely-used programming model developed by AMD, initially targeting NVIDIA and AMD GPUs.
By design, HIP closely resembles NVIDIA CUDA, aiming to be a more portable version. To enable HIP
applications on Intel GPUs without extensive rewriting, DOE collaborated with AMD and Intel on a HIP
implementation for Intel accelerators. This implementation, called chipStar, shows performance comparable
to other programming models like SYCL on Intel GPUs.
Budget and Personnel Annual funding for chipStar is $320k and 0.5 FTE from ALCF. The $320k sub-
contracts Pagan LC Consulting for development, user support, quality assurance, and CI capabilities. The
0.5 FTE from ALCF’s Performance Engineering Team supports INCITE and ALCC users and provides
project leadership.
Addressing DOE mission chipStar enables users with existing HIP or CUDA applications to use Aurora
with minimal porting effort, being the only project allowing such applications to run on Aurora’s Intel GPUs.
This year, the INCITE project OpenFanWing and its HIP GENESIS application were ported to Aurora
using chipStar with only small kernel adjustments. The OpenFanWing project is meeting its milestones and
expected to continue next year. The chipStar team is currently enabling the ALCC project ZPinch’s WarpM
and WarpX on Aurora. The project is gaining popularity, with additional ALCC and INCITE projects
planning to use chipStar for Aurora. The European HPC DARE project plans to use chipStar for RISC-
V architecture, including flagship application GROMACS, demonstrating that chipStar fulfills a real HPC
community need and helps DOE provide computing resources to the scientific community.

2.10 SYCL
SYCL is a royalty-free, cross-platform C++ programming model for heterogeneous computing that enables
developers to write standard, modern C++ code targeting a wide range of heterogeneous devices while
maintaining a single-source programming model. Device kernels are expressed using standard C++ con-
structs—including templates, inheritance, and lambda expressions—and can coexist with host code in the
same source files, improving portability, productivity, and long-term maintainability. Kokkos provides a
SYCL backend, and SYCL implementations are available atop CUDA, HIP, OpenCL, and OpenMP, en-
abling performance portability across diverse DOE-relevant architectures.
Budget and Personnel Thomas Applencourt has been engaging the SYCL activities through the support
by ALCF (0.20 FTE).
Addressing DOE Mission SYCL directly supports DOE mission needs by enabling portable HPC appli-
cations across current and emerging heterogeneous platforms. Its single-source C++ model lowers barriers
for scientific application developers while preserving access to accelerator performance, benefiting HPC,
data analytics, and AI workloads. SYCL bridges the gap between higher-level programming frameworks
(e.g., Kokkos and AI frameworks) and lower-level accelerator APIs (e.g., OpenCL and CUDA), reducing
redundant development efforts and improving efficiency and coherence across the DOE scientific software
ecosystem.

• Recent activities include a BoF session at SC, tutorials delivered at SC and IWOCL (International Work-
  shop on OpenCL and SYCL), and over 15 SYCL-related papers at IWOCL on HPC and AI.
• A new SYCL specification version was released with extensions to narrow the feature gap with CUDA
• Contracted collaboration with CMake maintainers improved build-system support
• One SYCL implementation initiated an upstreaming effort into LLVM

• The project maintains a comprehensive, openly governed language specification (Argonne has submitted
  a proposal to chair the Working Group)
• An official conformance test suite ensures correctness and portability across implementations
• Active community support channels include Slack, Reddit, and Discord

3.3 Automatic Differentiation
Automatic Differentiation (AD) [9] is an algorithmic approach to generate derivatives based on function
implementations that significantly speeds up code development, allows faster exploration of new models,
and enables end-to-end training when combining ML with conventional models. Accurate derivatives are
fundamental to numerical simulations, optimization, uncertainty quantification, and AI. Manual derivative
implementation is error-prone and costly. The Enzyme project provides a high-performance, LLVM-based
automatic differentiation tool that generates derivatives directly from function implementations, significantly
accelerating model exploration. Enzyme differentiates at the LLVM Intermediate Representation (IR) level,
ensuring portability across architectures including CPUs and GPUs.
Budget and Personnel For FY25, the subproject budget at Argonne National Laboratory supports senior
personnel Michel Schanen. The current S4PST allocation for AD activities is approximately 0.20 FTE.
While this funding supports coordination and specific integration tasks, core Enzyme development remains
largely community-driven and is leveraged through other funding sources such as NSF CSSI (DJ4Earth) and
DOE SciDAC (ImPACTS).
Addressing DOE mission Enzyme addresses a critical gap in the DOE software stack by providing unified
infrastructure for differentiating source code targeting diverse accelerator architectures. It enables simulation
of large-scale systems, such as the Texas ERCOT power grid with millions of buses, requiring high-fidelity
derivatives for numerical integration. By facilitating convergence of AI and scientific simulation, Enzyme
ensures that DOE mission-critical codes in climate modeling (e.g., MPAS, CliMA) and power systems run
efficiently on leadership-class facilities like Aurora and Frontier.
