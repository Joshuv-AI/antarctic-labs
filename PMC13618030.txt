How 3‐O‐Glucosylation
Modulates Resveratrol Radical Scavenging: A QM-ORSA Study of Polydatin/Piceid,
a Stilbenoid Glucoside

Maciej Spiegel, Nino Russo

Abstract

The antioxidant properties of polydatin/piceid (3-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl-resveratrol),
a stilbenoid
polyphenol widely found in Vitaceae, Liliaceae, and Leguminosae plants and used in traditional
medicine, have been investigated against the •OOH
radical by means of a density functional theory-based QM-ORSA protocol.
The central mechanistic question addressed is how 3-O-glucosylation modulates the primary antiradical activity. To provide
a biologically meaningful answer, its mechanisms of action were studied
in both aqueous solution at physiological pH and in a lipid-like environment.
Conformational analysis performed at the outset confirmed that the trans isomer is preferred over the cis form
by 4.9 kcal·mol–1, and species distribution
calculations show that the neutral form dominates at pH 7.4 (98.6%).
The para-hydroxyl of ring B emerges as the dominant
contributor to anti-•OOH activity regardless of
the milieu; however, the activity trends show that while polydatin
reacts faster than Trolox in the modeled lipid-like phase (9.78 ×
103 vs. 3.40 × 103 M–1·s–1), in water its apparent rate constant
(4.12 × 104 M–1·s–1) falls below both trans-resveratrol (1.42 ×
105 M–1·s–1) and
Trolox (8.96 × 104 M–1·s–1).

Introduction

Polydatin, also known as piceid or 3-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl-resveratrol
(), is a stilbenoid
polyphenol first isolated
from the rhizome and root of Polygonum cuspidatum. Additional natural sources include
plants of the Vitaceae, Liliaceae, and Leguminosae families. In traditional Chinese medicine, polydatin has long been used to
treat inflammation, infections, and cardiovascular disorders. The
substances has been also associated with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory,
neuroprotective, hepatoprotective, immunostimulatory, and anticancer
effects.
,−

 Nonetheless, the scavenging power of polydatin
relative to its aglycone is disputed: it has been found to exceed
that of resveratrol in some experimental assays,
−

 whereas resveratrol
shows greater activity in other assay systems, even within a single
multi-assay study
,
. Such discrepancies invite a
mechanistic clarification especially given that polydatin, not resveratrol,
is the major circulating stilbenoid following oral ingestion of plant-derived
sources.
,

The chemistry of resveratrol and its derivatives
has been extensively
characterized by computational methods.
−

 From a structural perspective, polydatin differs from resveratrol
solely by a β-d-glucopyranose unit attached at the
C3 position of the stilbene backbone. One might assume, therefore,
that polydatin’s antioxidant mechanism can be inferred by direct
analogy to the parent compound; however, in our previous work on the
related flavonoid glucosides vitexin and isovitexin, we demonstrated
that glucosylation at an apparently unconnected motif relative to
the main reactive part of the molecule may alter the overall radical-scavenging
activity. Here the structure is smaller,
and glycosylation directly masks one critical phenolic hydroxyl group,
raising the question of whether and to what degree the polyhydroxylated
sugar ring indirectly impacts the activity. Polydatin itself has not
previously been characterized at this level of detail and the present
work addresses this gap by comparing the calculated radical-scavenging
behavior of polydatin with that of trans-resveratrol,
providing for the first time systematic kinetic data for this stilbenoid
glucoside.

We used density functional theory within a dedicated
thermochemistry-kinetic
framework, previously applied to a series of natural antioxidants,
−

 to examine the structural and electronic properties of polydatin
and its Hydrogen Atom Transfer (HAT), Single Electron Transfer (SET),
and Radical Adduct Formation (RAF) reactions with •OOH in aqueous and lipid-like environments. The computed rate constants
were then compared with those of Trolox, trans-resveratrol,
and selected natural and non-natural antioxidants to contextualize
the effect of 3-O-glucosylation on the radical-scavenging
profile of the stilbene scaffold.

Computational Details

Since unambiguous structural
parameters for polydatin in its ground-state conformation are not
established in the literature, initial geometries were obtained via metadynamics conformational sampling as implemented
in the CREST software. The resulting
structures were subjected to full geometry optimization using the
M05–2X exchange-correlation meta-GGA functional combined with the 6–311+G­(d,p) basis
set
,
 and the SMD implicit solvation model. Frequency calculations were performed at the
same level of theory to confirm that the optimized structures correspond
to true minima (ground states) or first-order saddle points (transition
states). Open-shell species were treated using the unrestricted formalism.
Intrinsic reaction coordinate calculations were carried out for each
transition state to confirm proper connectivity between reactants
and products. All calculations were carried out using Gaussian 16
(Rev. C.01).

Pentyl ethanoate (ε
= 4.7) and water (ε = 78.4) environments were used to simulate
lipid-like environments and aqueous conditions, respectively. The
pK
a values and molar fractions in aqueous
phase at physiological pH (7.4) were obtained using an empirically
fitted parameters method to account
for the different protonation states of the same molecule.

Global
reactivity indicesbond dissociation energies (BDE)
and ionization potentials (IP)were estimated within the adiabatic
approximation using the solvation enthalpies of H+ (ΔH(H+) = 252.3 kcal·mol–1) and the electron (ΔH(e–) = 18.5 kcal·mol–1).

Results and Discussion

Conformational Preference and Structural Overview

Polydatin
can adopt two distinct conformations depending on the orientation
of the stilbene double bond. As shown in , the trans form is more
stable than the cis isomer by 4.9 kcal·mol–1 (in aqueous solution), which, according to a Boltzmann
distribution at 298 K, corresponds to a population of the cis form of approximately 0.025%. All subsequent calculations
were therefore conducted exclusively on the trans isomer. This conformational preference mirrors that observed for
resveratrol.

Acid–Base Behavior and Species Distribution

Given that polydatin contains two free phenolic hydroxyl groups capable
of deprotonation, it can exist as neutral, monoanionic, and dianionic
species in aqueous solution, with each protonation state contributing
differently to overall antioxidant activity. The order of deprotonation
of these groups is not a priori evident. Therefore,
we first investigated its acid–base behavior to determine the
distribution of protonation states at physiological pHexperimental
studies report only the first dissociation constant (9.40 and 9.22), whereas
our calculations provide both pK
a values.

Global Reactivity Descriptors

The reactivity indices
in reveal a
mechanistically important consequence of 3-O-glucosylation.
With the C3-hydroxyl of ring A blocked by the glycosidic bond, the
molecule retains two free phenolic groups whose relative lability
governs which site should dominate radical scavenging. Consistent
with prior computational studies on trans-resveratrol
that have established the role of para-hydroxyl of
the ring B11,12 (here denoted as C4”), this group
also bears the weakest O–H bond in both environments (BDE =
89.2 kcal·mol–1 in lipid, 90.2 kcal·mol–1 in water), implying it as the thermodynamically preferred
HAT site. The C5 group is a weaker H-donor (BDE = 95.5 kcal·mol–1 in lipid, 97.5 kcal·mol–1 in
water), though its reactivity increases substantially in the anionic
species HPld
– (BDE = 89.4 kcal·mol–1). This activating effect can be associated with the
increased electron density propagated through the conjugated π-system
from the deprotonated C4” group. The remaining possible sites
of H atom abstraction are vinyl C–H positions (C1’,
C2’), which exhibit BDE values exceeding 110 kcal·mol–1 in all environments, confirming they are not competitive
HAT donors. The adiabatic ionization potential decreases from the
lipid-like phase (122.2 kcal·mol–1) to aqueous
solution (104.1 kcal·mol–1), reflecting enhanced
charge stabilization by the polar environment, and decreases further
for HPld
– (82.8 kcal·mol–1), consistent with increased electron-donating character upon deprotonation.

Thermodynamics and Kinetics of Radical Scavenging

Intrinsic
reactivity indices alone cannot discriminate among competing mechanisms
but only guide the analysis. The antioxidant properties of polydatin
were therefore determined by means of the QM-ORSA protocol.

The Gibbs free energies for the reaction between the •OOH radical and the polydatin species are collected in . The HAT mechanism at C4”
is thermodynamically most favored in both lipid (ΔG = −2.4
kcal·mol–1) and aqueous (ΔG = −5.3
kcal·mol–1) media, reflecting the low BDE of
this hydroxyl group. The C5 site is also thermodynamically accessible
to HAT in aqueous solution (ΔG = 2.3 kcal·mol–1) and becomes exergonic for the monoanionic species (ΔG = −5.9
kcal·mol–1), rationalizing the high reactivity
of HPld–
 as discussed below.

Among the RAF pathways, adduct formation at C1’
and C2’
yields slightly negative ΔG values (−2.5 and −0.1
kcal·mol–1 in lipid, respectively), indicating
thermodynamic feasibility. Of note, these pathways become more exergonic
upon conversion of H2Pld to HPld
– and upon changing the solvent from lipid to water.
RAF on the non-glycosylated ring carbons (C1”–C6”)
is generally endergonic, but several positions show modest positive
ΔG values (6–15 kcal·mol–1) that
do not preclude kinetic participation, particularly for the monoanionic
species. This confirms that the stilbenoid π-system remains
chemically accessible to RAF pathways beyond the phenolic HAT sites.

SET is strongly endergonic for neutral polydatin in aqueous solution
(23.3 kcal·mol–1), and it becomes near-thermoneutral
for the monoanionic species (0.5 kcal·mol–1). Such behavior is consistent with the high aIP of H

2

Pld

aq
 and with the general observation that SET processes are significant
mainly for deprotonated polyphenol species, where electron-donating
ability is substantially enhanced. In this regard, although the SET
pathway appears relevant primarily for the monoanionic species, a
strongly endergonic free energy change does not in itself rule out
electron transfer, since within the framework of Marcus theory the
reaction rate depends on both ΔG and the reorganization energy.

For the exergonic and mildly endergonic reactions (ΔG ≤
10.0 kcal·mol–1), the activation free energies

In lipid, glycosylation
does not suppress antioxidant activity
relative to Trolox, and the C4” hydroxyl remains the most labile
H-donor, producing a rate constant 2.9-fold higher than Trolox and
approximately 60-fold higher than ascorbic acid. Hence, in this environment,
polydatin is a competent lipid-phase antioxidant.

In water at
physiological pH, the picture reverses relative to
resveratrol, and polydatin’s apparent rate constant falls approximately
3.4-fold below trans-resveratrol and 2.2-fold below
Trolox. The mechanistic origin is unambiguous and can be associated
with the C3-involving pathway that drives resveratrol’s aqueous
reactivity is blocked in polydatin. While the monoanionic HPld
– is intrinsically highly reactive, its low molar
fraction at pH 7.4 limits its contribution to the apparent rate constant.

As a final remark, we emphasize that experimental observations
in which polydatin appears comparable or superior to resveratrol should
therefore not be interpreted as a direct consequence of higher intrinsic •OOH- scavenging reactivity. The present calculations
instead suggest that such differences may arise from biological or
physicochemical factors, including solubility, membrane partitioning,
metabolic stability, or assay-specific radical chemistry.
,,
 In this regard, further studies
on the antioxidant metabolites predominating in the living organism
are clearly warranted.

Conclusions

The primary antioxidant properties of polydatin
against the •OOH radical have been studied using
the QM-ORSA density
functional theory protocol. Overall, the calculations indicate that
3-O-glucosylation changes but does not suppress the
primary radical-scavenging chemistry of the resveratrol scaffold,
yet visibly alters it. The key findings are summarized as follows:

- (i)The trans isomer
is more stable than the cis form by 4.9 kcal·mol–1. At physiological pH the neutral H

2

Pld species dominates (98.6%).
- (ii)Stepwise deprotonation
is predicted
to occur at C4” (pK
a = 9.24), followed
by C5 (pK
a = 9.48), both well above physiological
pH. The first pK
a is in good agreement
with experimental values, lending confidence to the approach.
- (iii)In both lipid-like and
aqueous environments
the HAT mechanism is the preferred antioxidant pathway, with the C4”
hydroxyl as the dominant reactive site. RAF pathways at C1’
and C2’ contribute to radical scavenging in aqueous solution,
for the neutral species, while SET becomes significant only for HPld
–.
- (iv)Relative to the reference compounds
considered here, polydatin performs more favorably in the lipid-like
medium than in water. In pentyl ethanoate, its rate constant is approximately
2.9-fold higher than Trolox and about 60-fold higher than ascorbic
acid. In water, its apparent rate constant is approximately 2.2-fold
lower than Trolox, 3.4-fold lower than trans-resveratrol,
and about 62-fold lower than ascorbic acid.
- (v)The antioxidant behavior of polydatin
relative to resveratrol is medium-dependent. In lipid, the persistence
of the reactive C4” hydroxyl ensures competitive HAT despite
glycosylation at C3. In water, the attenuation relative to resveratrol
is explained by the loss of the C3-phenolate HAT pathway, which is
permanently blocked by the glycosidic bond.
