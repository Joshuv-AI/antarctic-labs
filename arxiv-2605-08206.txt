Construction and Analysis of the Effective Model for the Bulk Steady State under Current in Boundary-Driven Open Systems
Yoshihiro Michishita

Abstract
Current-induced phenomena are often obscured by Joule heating, and their steady states are difficult to analyze in large open systems. We introduce a translationally invariant asymmetric-hopping model as an effective bulk description of boundary-driven systems under current. In a minimal case, it corresponds to an open-system Hatano–Nelson model. We find that the effective temperature rises linearly with current density, as observed experimentally. The model provides a useful tool for separating intrinsic current-induced effects from heating.

Brief Introduction to Boundary-Driven System
–
First, we briefly analyze the particle distribution, the effective temperature, dissipation, and current in the bulk region in BDS as an introduction to the next section. In this section, we examine the following properties: (i) the jump terms only appear at the edges, (ii) the particle distribution is uniform in the bulk, and (iii) the local distribution has higher effective temperature than the edge bath, and these properties are essential for deriving the effective model for the bulk region. For simplicity, we consider a spinless free-fermion model coupled to two different baths. In order to calculate the steady state, we use the universal Lindblad equation because it guarantees the positivity without the secular approximation
Nathan and Rudner 2020
;
Davidović 2022
;
Nathan and Rudner 2024
;

Wichterich et al. 2007
;
Landi et al. 2022

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\rho(t)

\displaystyle=-i[\mathcal{H},\rho(t)]+\mathcal{D}^{(l)}[\rho]+\mathcal{D}^{(r)}[\rho].

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=

\displaystyle-t\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j}),

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{(r/l)}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\Bigl[\gamma^{(r/l)}_{l;ij}\Bigl(c_{i}\rho c_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{i},\rho\}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ +\gamma^{(r/l)}_{g;ij}\Bigl(c_{i}^{\dagger}\rho c_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\{c_{j}c_{i}^{\dagger},\rho\}\Bigr)\Bigr],

We note that the local effective temperature is in general different from that of the whole system. The current and the effective temperature increase as

Although the effective temperature shows a nonlinear dependence on the current over a wide range of the driving strength, its small-current behavior is approximately linear. This weak-driving regime is the regime addressed by the perturbative analysis in the following sections.

The above observations suggest that the local bulk steady state can be characterized without explicitly keeping the edge reservoirs.
In constructing such a bulk effective Liouvillian, we require it to reproduce the following local properties of the boundary-driven steady state:
(i) translationally uniform density in the bulk, and
(ii) a finite and spatially uniform local current.
These local signatures do not determine the effective Liouvillian uniquely.
Nevertheless, as shown below, they impose a strong constraint on local jump operators: a dissipative term containing an asymmetric hopping component is necessary to sustain a local steady state with finite current under uniform density.
In addition, we have shown that the locally defined effective temperature in the bulk is much higher than the bath temperature. This observation will be used below to motivate the simplest representative of the asymmetric jump operators.

Construction of the Effective Model
–
Here we introduce an effective model for the bulk region of the BDS whose steady state has finite local current and uniform particle distribution.
We start from the steady state of the BDS which we have analyzed in the previous section as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}=-i[\mathcal{H},\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}]+\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{BD}}[\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}]=0,

(4)

where
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{BD}}
is the dissipator for the BDS and it corresponds to the sum of the boundary localized dissipators
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}+\mathcal{D}^{(l)}
in Eq. (
3
). We can define the bulk steady state and construct the effective Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}
which has the same bulk steady state as

\displaystyle\rho^{(\mathrm{bulk})}_{\mathrm{ss}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr_{edge}}\Bigl[\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}\Bigr],

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}\rho^{(\mathrm{bulk})}_{\mathrm{ss}}

\displaystyle=-i[\mathcal{H}^{(\mathrm{bulk})},\rho^{(\mathrm{bulk})}_{\mathrm{ss}}]+\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho^{(\mathrm{bulk})}_{\mathrm{ss}}]=0,

(6)

where
H^{(\mathrm{bulk})}
denotes the local Hamiltonian terms supported inside the bulk region.
We note that, in general, we cannot uniquely determine
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{eff}}
(
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}
) only from Eq. (
6
) and additional restrictions are required to determine
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}
.

In this letter, in order to construct the effective model with the translational symmetry, we assume locality of the jump operators on effective model and consider the local two-site effective dissipator
\mathcal{D}^{(j)}_{\mathrm{eff}}
for the sites
j
and
j+1
in the bulk region, which satisfies the local condition in Eq. (
7
), and then construct the effective Liouvillian for the whole bulk region as the sum of the local two-site effective dissipators.
The local condition reads

\displaystyle-i[-t(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j}),\rho^{j,j+1}_{\mathrm{ss}}]+\mathcal{D}^{(j)}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho^{j,j+1}_{\mathrm{ss}}]=0,

(7)

where
\rho^{j,j+1}_{\mathrm{ss}}=\mathrm{Tr_{rest}}\Bigl[\rho^{(\mathrm{bulk})}_{\mathrm{ss}}\Bigr]
is the local two-site steady state for the sites
j
and
j+1
, which can be obtained by tracing out the rest of the bulk system.
We then construct the effective Liouvillian with the translational symmetry for the whole bulk region as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{(j,\alpha)}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho]

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{(j,\alpha)}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{(l)}_{\alpha}\Bigl(L^{(l)}_{(j,\alpha)}\rho L^{(l)\dagger}_{(j,\alpha)}-\frac{1}{2}\{L^{(l)\dagger}_{(j,\alpha)}L^{(l)}_{(j,\alpha)},\rho\}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma^{(g)}_{\alpha}\Bigl(L^{(g)}_{(j,\alpha)}\rho L^{(g)\dagger}_{(j,\alpha)}-\frac{1}{2}\{L^{(g)\dagger}_{(j,\alpha)}L^{(g)}_{(j,\alpha)},\rho\}\Bigr),

(8)

\displaystyle L^{(l)}_{(j,\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}c_{j}+\alpha_{2}c_{j+1},\quad L^{(g)}_{(j,\alpha)}=\alpha_{3}c_{j}^{\dagger}+\alpha_{4}c_{j+1}^{\dagger},

(9)

where
\mathcal{D}^{(j)}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum_{\alpha}\mathcal{D}^{(j,\alpha)}_{\mathrm{eff}}
. We note that
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{eff}}
, in general, does not satisfy the steady state condition in Eq. (
6
) for the whole bulk region, but it is constructed to satisfy the local consistency condition in Eq. (
7
) and preserves the translational symmetry.

Interestingly, when we suppose that the local steady state holds the uniform site-resolved particle distribution and the finite local current expectation value

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\m{J}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{j,j+1}[-i(c^{\dagger}_{j+1}c_{j}-c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j+1})\rho^{j,j+1}_{\mathrm{ss}}]\neq 0,

(10)

as we have shown in the previous section, the condition in Eq. (
7
) requires a finite imaginary part of
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}^{*}
and/or
\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}^{*}
. (see the Supplemental Materials for the detail derivation.

This term can be interpreted as an asymmetric hopping term because the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian of this model can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(j,j+1)}_{\mathrm{eff}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{(j,j+1)}-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\Bigl[\gamma^{(l)}_{\alpha}L^{(l)\dagger}_{(j,\alpha)}L^{(l)}_{(j,\alpha)}+\gamma^{(g)}_{\alpha}L^{(g)\dagger}_{(j,\alpha)}L^{(g)}_{(j,\alpha)}\Bigr]

and the imaginary part of
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}^{*}
and/or
\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}^{*}
emerges as the coefficient of the asymmetric hopping term
(c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j+1}-\mathrm{h.c.})
in the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian.
This construction of the effective model should be understood as a minimal local representation of the bulk steady state rather than a unique derivation of the full reduced Liouvillian. We use the results of the boundary-driven system, which are the uniform density and finite current at the bulk regime, as the constraints to determine the form of the effective model, resulting in the asymmetric hopping term. We then choose the simplest representative jump operators satisfying this condition and analyze their steady state after adding a thermal relaxation channel.

Below, we consider a single
\alpha
channel and set
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4})=(1,-i,1,i)
as the simplest choice to satisfy the above conditions for the finite local current. We note that we can partially justify this choice of
\alpha

Sup

Under the periodic boundary condition where we set
c_{l+1}=c_{1}
, we can summarize our model in wave number space as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho(t)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\Biggl[\gamma^{(l)}(\epsilon_{k})(2-2\sin(k))\Bigl(c_{k}\rho(t)c_{k}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{c_{k}^{\dagger}c_{k},\rho(t)\}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\gamma^{(g)}(\epsilon_{k})(2+2\sin(k))\Bigl(c_{k}^{\dagger}\rho(t)c_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\{c_{k}c_{k}^{\dagger},\rho(t)\}\Bigr)\Biggr].

(11)

We set the lattice constant to be unity. We note that, while the effective Hamiltonian of this model by setting
\gamma^{(l)}(\epsilon_{k})=\gamma^{(g)}(\epsilon_{k})=h
is equivalent to the Hatano–Nelson model

;
Hatano and Nelson 1996
which is a non-Hermitian model with asymmetric hopping, the bulk steady state of this model does not show a boundary-condition sensitivity in the bulk observables, and thus the analysis of the periodic-boundary should give the same result as the analysis of the open-boundary condition
Sup
.
Below we analyze the steady state of this model for
\gamma^{(l)}=\gamma^{(g)}=h
and its effective temperature.
Finally, by considering the
\tilde{D}_{\mathrm{eff}}
as the current source, we add the thermal dissipator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{th}}
to this model so that the steady state with
h=0
is the thermal equilibrium state and our model reflects the experimental setup of the current-induced phenomena, which reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{th}}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\xi_{\epsilon_{k}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}}\Bigl[L^{(\mathrm{th})}_{kk^{\prime}}\rho(t)L^{(\mathrm{th})\dagger}_{kk^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\{L^{(\mathrm{th})\dagger}_{kk^{\prime}}L^{(\mathrm{th})}_{kk^{\prime}},\rho(t)\}\Bigr].

(12)

\displaystyle L^{(\mathrm{th})}_{kk^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=c_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{k}

(13)

where
\xi_{\epsilon_{k}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}}
is the transition rate from the state with energy
\epsilon_{k}
to the state with energy
\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}

\xi_{\epsilon_{k}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}}=\xi_{\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k}}\exp[-\beta(\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{k})]
where
\beta

Our model is described by the following Lindblad equation as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\rho(t)=

\displaystyle-i[\mathcal{H},\rho(t)]+\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\rho(t)]+\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{th}}[\rho(t)].

(14)

In this setup, while
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{th}}
represents the thermal bath,
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{eff}}
represents the current source inherited from the boundary driving and
h
is determined from the strength of the local current in the steady state of the original BDS. Thus, different from the analysis with electric field

, this model is directly described by the parameter

related to the current density.

Steady-State Analysis
–
In this letter, we focus on the expectation value of current and the effective temperature, and it is sufficient to consider the approximated steady state as

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{k}\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}(k),

(15)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}(k)

\displaystyle=p_{k}\ket{k}\bra{k}+(1-p_{k})\ket{0}_{k}\bra{0}_{k}.

(16)

As a first step to analyze the steady state, we consider terms up to first order in
h
; the parameter should be determined from the strength of the local current in the steady state of the original model. We write the deviation from the Fermi distribution as

\displaystyle\delta p_{k}=p_{k}-f_{k},

(17)

where
f_{k}
is the Fermi distribution function as
f_{k}=1/(1+\exp[\beta(\tilde{\epsilon_{k}})])
where
\tilde{\epsilon_{k}}=\epsilon_{k}-\mu
and
\beta
equals to the inverse temperature of the thermal bath. Below we consider the small
h
regime and analyze the steady state by perturbation theory with respect to
h
.
By inserting Eq. (
17
) into Eq. (
15
) and Eq. (
16
), we obtain the following equation for
\delta p_{k}
:

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-2h(1-\sin(k))f_{k}+2h(1+\sin(k))(1-f_{k})

\displaystyle\ +h\sum_{k^{\prime}}\Bigl[\xi_{\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k}}\bigl\{\delta p_{k^{\prime}}(1-f_{k})-f_{k^{\prime}}\delta p_{k}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\xi_{\epsilon_{k}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}}\bigl\{\delta p_{k}(1-f_{k^{\prime}})-f_{k}\delta p_{k^{\prime}}\bigr\}\Bigr],

(18)

and then we get

\displaystyle\delta p_{k}

\displaystyle=\delta\rho^{(\mathrm{c})}_{k}+\delta\rho^{(\mathrm{th})}_{k}+\delta\rho^{(\mu)}_{k},

(19)

\displaystyle\delta\rho^{(\mathrm{c})}_{k}

\displaystyle=h\tau_{k}\beta\sin(k)\sech(\beta\tilde{\epk}/2)/\pi,

(20)

\displaystyle\delta\rho^{(\mathrm{th})}_{k}

\displaystyle=h\tau_{k}\beta\sech(\beta\tilde{\epk}/2)\tanh(\beta\tilde{\epk}/2)/\pi,

(21)

\displaystyle\delta\rho^{(\mu)}_{k}

\displaystyle=h\tau_{k}\beta\sech^{2}(\beta\tilde{\epsilon_{k}}/2)A_{k}/\pi,

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\beta\tau_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}}\sqrt{\xi_{\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k}}\xi_{\epsilon_{k}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}}}\sech(\beta\tilde{\epkp}/2),

(23)

where
A_{k}=\sum_{k^{\prime}}\sqrt{\xi_{\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k}}\xi_{\epsilon_{k}\rightarrow\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}}}\delta\rho_{k^{\prime}}\cosh(\beta\tilde{\epkp}/2)
.
We note that
\delta\rho_{k}^{(\mathrm{c})}
is the current driving term of the steady state as
\expectationvalue{J}=\tr[J\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}]=\tr[J\rho^{(\mathrm{c})}_{\mathrm{ss}}]
,
\delta\rho_{k}^{(\mathrm{th})}
represents heat emission into the bath in the steady state as
\expectationvalue{\dt Q_{s\rightarrow b}}=\tr[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Q_{s\rightarrow b}\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}]=\tr[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Q_{s\rightarrow b}\rho^{(\mathrm{th})}_{\mathrm{ss}}]
, and
\delta\rho_{k}^{(\mu)}
represents the correction to chemical potential due to the coupling to the bath.

First, in the zero-temperature limit, we can approximate as
\beta\sech(\beta\tilde{\epk}/2)\simeq 2\pi\delta(\tilde{\epsilon_{k}})
, and the expectation value of current reads as

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{J}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{k}\tau_{k}v_{k}^{2}\delta(\tilde{\epsilon_{k}})\left(\frac{h}{t}\right)\simeq\tau D(\epsilon_{F})v^{2}_{F}\left(\frac{h}{t}\right),

(24)

where
v_{k}=\partial\epsilon_{k}/\partial k
is the velocity,
v_{F}=|\partial\epsilon_{k}/\partial k|_{\epsilon_{k}=0}
is the Fermi velocity,
\tau=\tau_{k}|_{\epsilon_{k}=0}
, and
D(\epsilon_{F})
is the density of states at the Fermi level, and we set the electron charge
e
as unity.
Eq. (
24
) corresponds to the result from the linear response theory when
h/t
equals to the electric field
E
.
Moreover, approximating
\sinh(\beta\epk/2)\simeq\beta\epsilon_{k}/2
and using
\beta\sech^{2}(\beta\epsilon_{k}/2)/4=-(\partial f/\partial\omega)_{\epsilon_{k}}
, we can derive

\displaystyle\delta\rho^{(\mathrm{th})}_{k}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2h\tau_{k}\tilde{\epsilon_{k}}}{\pi}\left(-\frac{\partial f}{\partial\omega}\right)

(25)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\beta h\tau_{k}}{\pi}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\beta}\right)=\delta\beta\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\beta}\right),

(26)

where
\delta\beta=-2\beta h\tau_{k}/\pi
, which reads
\delta T/T=2h\tau_{k}/\pi
. Thus,
\delta\rho_{k}^{(\mathrm{th})}
can be considered as the increase of the effective temperature and it causes heat emission to the thermal bath. By using Eq. (
24
) and Eq. (
26
), we can derive the relation between the increase of the effective temperature and the current expectation value as

\displaystyle\frac{\delta T}{T}\simeq\frac{4\expectationvalue{J}}{\pi WD(\epsilon_{F})},

(27)

where
W=2t
is the bandwidth of the system.
Thus, we obtain the linear dependence of effective temperature increase on the current. We note that, although Eq.(
24
) corresponds to the results of the linear response theory at
h/t=E
, it does not mean that
h
is always linearly proportional to
E
and, in general,
h
is the control parameter determined by the current in the steady state.

Finally, at low temperature, we can approximate
\delta\rho_{k}^{(\mu)}
as

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{k}^{(\mu)}

\displaystyle\simeq h\tau_{k}\sech^{2}(\beta\tilde{\epsilon_{k}}/2)A_{k}/\pi

(28)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2h\tau A}{\pi}\left(-\frac{\partial f}{\partial\omega}\right)=\delta\mu\left(-\frac{\partial f}{\partial\omega}\right).

(29)

Because we can approximate
\sech(\beta\epk/2)\simeq 2\pi\delta(\tilde{\epsilon_{k}})/\beta
at low temperature, and
\rho^{(\mathrm{th})}_{k}
has the linear dependence of
\beta
,
A_{k}
can be approximated as
A_{k}\simeq A\beta
where the factor
A
is determined by the self-consistent equation. Thus,
\delta\rho_{k}^{(\mu)}
can be considered as the correction to the chemical potential due to the coupling to the bath.

Summary and Discussion
–
In this letter, we have introduced a translationally invariant asymmetric-hopping model as an effective model for the bulk steady state of the BDS under current, and analyze the effective temperature and the current density in its steady state. We found that the effective temperature increase is linearly proportional to the current density, similar to the experimental observation in Ref.
Ootsuki et al. 2025
.

First, we briefly analyzed the boundary-driven system and checked that the jump terms only appear at the edges and the local distribution has higher effective temperature than the edge bath. Then, we have shown that, by focusing on the local bulk state under current, a one-particle local effective Liouvillian which has the steady state with finite local current expectation value and uniform particle distribution must contain an asymmetric hopping term in the jump operator.

We have constructed the effective model with the translational symmetry by summing up the local two-site effective Liouvillian. In this construction, the control parameter
h
of the asymmetric hopping term is determined from the strength of the local current in the steady state of the original BDS, and thus we can get the direct relation between the current density and the effective temperature increase by analyzing the steady state of this model.
We have analyzed the steady state of this model in the simplest setup where the effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian corresponds to Hatano–Nelson model, and revealed that the effective temperature increase is linearly proportional to the current density.

Our results imply that Hatano–Nelson type asymmetric hopping models, which have been eagerly studied in the context of non-Hermitian physics, especially in optics, can emerge in the steady state of the solid-state system under current and be useful to analyze the effective temperature and the current in the steady state under current as some previous works have pointed out
Hatano and Nelson 1996
;
Fukui and Kawakami 1998
;
Takasan et al. 2025
;
Cao et al. 2023
, while the connection between the Hatano-Nelson model and the boundary-driven system has not been clarified before.

Although we have analyzed the simplest setup of the asymmetric-hopping model of one-dimensional spinless free fermions, it is interesting to analyze spinful and multi-orbital models, and the effect of the interaction on the effective temperature and the current in the steady state under current. Multi-orbital and spinful models may show us the relationship between the effective temperature increase and the quantum geometry, and the interaction may cause the nontrivial behavior of the effective temperature increase especially near the phase transition.

We hope that our work will be a step toward understanding the effective temperature and the current in the steady state under current, and thus the intrinsic current-induced phenomena in condensed matter physics.

S1
Rough Analysis of the temperature increase due to the Joule heating

We provide a rough analysis of the temperature increase due to the Joule heating.
To calculate the temperature at the steady state, we add the thermal bath to the driven system, and denote thermal conductance between the system and the bath by
\kappa
.

At the steady state, the heat-generation rate due to the Joule heating, and the heat-emission rate to the bath must be balanced as

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\dt Q_{Joule}}

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\dt Q_{s\rightarrow b}},

(S1)

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\dt Q_{Joule}}

\displaystyle=\rho\expectationvalue{J}^{2},\quad\expectationvalue{\dt Q_{s\rightarrow b}}=\kappa(T_{\mathrm{eff}}-T_{\mathrm{bath}}),

(S2)

where
\rho
is the resistivity,
T_{\mathrm{eff}}
is the effective temperature of the systems at the steady state, and
T_{\mathrm{bath}}
is the bath temperature. Thus, we can get

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{eff}}=T_{\mathrm{bath}}+\frac{\rho\expectationvalue{J}^{2}}{\kappa}.

(S3)

Although the resistivity
\rho
can depend on the effective temperature, its dependence is expected to be
\propto T_{\mathrm{eff}}
at phonon-scattering dominant regime,
\propto T^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}
at the Fermi-liquid regime,
\propto\exp[\Delta/T_{\mathrm{eff}}]
at the insulating phase with the gap
\Delta
, or independent of
T
at the impurity-scattering dominant regime, for all these cases, Eq. (
S3
) does not result in the linear dependence of the effective temperature increase to the current, which is observed in the experiment
Ootsuki et al. 2025
. Thus, the simple linear response analysis in the isolated system and its Joule heating picture does not explain the experimental observation.

S2
Bath setup for the boundary-driven system in the main text

We set the bath parameters for the boundary-driven system as follows. The left and right baths are set to be the thermal baths with the same temperature
T_{\mathrm{B}}=0.05
and chemical potential
\mu_{\mathrm{l}}=-\mu_{\mathrm{r}}=2.4
, and the coupling strength between the system and the baths is set to be
\gamma
. The bath spectral function is chosen as
J(\omega)=1/(1+\exp[(\omega-\mu)/T])\times 1/(1+i\tau\omega)
where
\tau
is the relaxation time, which is set to be
\tau=0.002
in our calculation. We note that, while we have analyzed the boundary-driven system with this specific bath setup, our main results do not depend on this specific bath setup, as long as it produces a finite local current expectation value and boundary-localized dissipation in the steady state.

S3
Proof of the Finite Asymmetric Hopping Term in the effective local Liouvillian

We use the three results, which are (i) the jump terms only appear at the edges, (ii) the site-resolved particle density is uniform in the bulk, and (iii) the local current is finite and constant in the bulk to prove that the effective local Liouvillian which has the steady state with finite local current expectation value must have the asymmetric hopping term in the jump operator. Owing to these three results the local two-site steady state in the bulk is written as

\displaystyle\rho^{i,i+1}_{\mathrm{ss}}=\begin{pmatrix}\ket{0}&\ket{i}&\ket{i+1}&\ket{i, i+1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p_{0}&0&0&0\\
0&n&r+ic&0\\
0&r-ic&n&0\\
0&0&0&p_{d}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bra{0}\\
\bra{i}\\
\bra{i+1}\\
\bra{i, i+1}\end{pmatrix},

(S4)

where
\ket{0}
is the vacuum state,
\ket{i}
and
\ket{i+1}
are the single-particle states at site
i
and
i+1
, and
\ket{i, i+1}
is the two-particle state at site
i
and
i+1
.
p_{0}
,
n
,
r
,
c
, and
p_{d}
are real numbers. The local current expectation value is given by
\expectationvalue{J}=2c
. Thus, if the local current expectation value is finite,
c
must be finite. We then consider the local jump terms of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i,i+1}[\rho]=

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q=i,i+1}\Biggl[\gamma^{(l)}_{pq}\Bigl(c_{p}\rho c_{q}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{c_{q}^{\dagger}c_{p},\rho\}\Bigr)+\gamma^{(g)}_{pq}\Bigl(c_{p}^{\dagger}\rho c_{q}-\frac{1}{2}\{c_{q}c_{p}^{\dagger},\rho\}\Bigr)\Biggr].

(S5)

Because we consider the Lindblad form of the local jump term, the coefficients satisfy

\displaystyle\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i+1}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{(l)*}_{i+1,i},\quad\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i+1}=\gamma^{(g)*}_{i+1,i},\quad\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i}=\gamma^{(l)*}_{i,i},\quad\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i+1}=\gamma^{(g)*}_{i+1,i+1},

(S6)

\displaystyle\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i}\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i+1}

\displaystyle\geq|\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i+1}|^{2},\quad\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i}\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i+1}\geq|\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i+1}|^{2}.

(S7)

When these jump terms and the local Hamiltonian have the local steady state in Eq. (
S4
), it satisfies

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\rho^{i,i+1}_{\mathrm{ss}}

\displaystyle=-i[\mathcal{H}^{i,i+1},\rho^{i,i+1}_{\mathrm{ss}}]+\mathcal{D}_{i,i+1}[\rho^{i,i+1}_{\mathrm{ss}}],

(S8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{i,i+1}

\displaystyle=-t(c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i+1}+c_{i+1}^{\dagger}c_{i}).

(S9)

Focusing on the off-diagonal element
\ket{i}\bra{i+1}
, we can get

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i}+\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i+1}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i+1})(r+ic)-(\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i+1}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i})n+(\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i+1}p_{0}+\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i}p_{d}),

(S10)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i}+\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i+1}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i+1})c-\mathrm{Im}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i+1}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i}]n+(\mathrm{Im}[\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i+1}]p_{0}+\mathrm{Im}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i}]p_{d})=0.

(S11)

If
c
is finite,
\mathrm{Im}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i+1}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i}]
or
\mathrm{Im}[\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i+1}]p_{0}+\mathrm{Im}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i}]p_{d}
must be finite. Thus, the asymmetric hopping terms in the jump operator must be finite to have the local steady state with finite local current expectation value when site-resolved particle density is uniform.

S4
Validity of choice of the jump operator in Section

Assuming that the coefficients
\alpha_{i}
in the effective local Liouvillian operator have the same norm, the Liouvillian operator which cause the asymmetric hopping term can be written as

\displaystyle L_{j}^{(l)}=

\displaystyle a^{(l)}(c_{j}+e^{i\alpha}c_{j+1}),\quad L_{j}^{(g)}=a^{(g)}(c_{j}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\tilde{\alpha}}c_{j+1}^{\dagger}).

(S12)

When we consider the real part in Eq. (
S10
), we can get

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i}+\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i+1}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i+1})r-\mathrm{Re}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i+1}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i}]n+(\mathrm{Re}[\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i+1}]p_{0}+\mathrm{Re}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i}]p_{d}).

(S13)

By inserting Eq. (
S12
) into Eq. (
S11
) and Eq. (
S13
), we can get

\displaystyle\frac{c}{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathrm{Im}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i+1}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i}]n+(\mathrm{Im}[\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i+1}]p_{0}+\mathrm{Im}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i}]p_{d})}{-\mathrm{Re}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i,i+1}+\gamma^{(g)}_{i+1,i}]n+(\mathrm{Re}[\gamma^{(g)}_{i,i+1}]p_{0}+\mathrm{Re}[\gamma^{(l)}_{i+1,i}]p_{d})}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{(l)}\sin(\alpha)(p_{d}-n)+a^{(g)}\sin(\tilde{\alpha})(p_{0}-n)}{a^{(l)}\cos(\alpha)(p_{d}-n)+a^{(g)}\cos(\tilde{\alpha})(p_{0}-n)}.

(S14)

Because, in our boundary-driven model,
r
satisfies
r=\tanh(\beta t/2)
and
\beta t\ll 1
,
c/r\gg 1
for the finite current expectation value, and thus
\tan(\alpha)
and
\tan(\tilde{\alpha})
must be large. Therefore, we can approximate
\alpha\simeq\pi/2
and
\tilde{\alpha}\simeq\pi/2
, which corresponds to the choice of the jump operator in the main text.

S5
Boundary-Condition Dependence of the Steady State of the Asymmetric-Hopping Model

Here we numerically show that the bulk steady state of the asymmetric-hopping model is insensitive to the boundary condition. We analyze the steady state of the asymmetric-hopping model under the open-boundary condition, and compare it with the steady state under the periodic-boundary condition. Fig.
S1
shows that the local distribution and the current expectation value in the bulk region are almost the same between these two boundary conditions, which implies that the bulk steady state of this model is insensitive to the boundary condition. This result supports our expectation that the effective asymmetric hopping model with periodic boundary conditions yields the same bulk steady state as the model with open boundary conditions, and thus can well describe the bulk steady state under current of the boundary-driven system with open boundary conditions.

Figure S1:
The boundary-condition dependence of the steady state of the asymmetric-hopping model. The left and right panels show the current and site-resolved particle density at each site in the steady state under the periodic-boundary condition and the open-boundary condition, which shows that both expectation values are almost same in the bulk region between these two boundary conditions.
