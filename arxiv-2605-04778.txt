Steady Incremental Viscosity Splitting Method for solving the stationary Navier-Stokes equation
Aziz Takhirov, Driss Yakoubi

Abstract
We develop a novel and efficient iterative scheme for solving incompressible steady Navier-Stokes equations. The method is an adaptation of the Incremental Viscosity Splitting approximation for unsteady flows [ 29 ] to steady equations. At each nonlinear iteration, the scheme requires solving an elliptic PDE for the velocity variable and a system with an SPD matrix for the pressure variable, which remains the same across all nonlinear iterations. The method can also be interpreted as an algebraic splitting approach. We prove boundedness and geometric convergence. Numerical tests illustrate the efficiency of the proposed algorithm.



1
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to present a novel splitting method for solving the stationary incompressible Navier-Stokes equation

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla p-\gamma\nabla\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

(3)

where
\mathbf{u}
is the velocity of the fluid,
p
is the pressure,
\mathbf{f}
is the body forcing,
\nu
is the kinematic viscosity. For simplicity, we consider homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for
\mathbf{u}
. The grad-div stabilization term
-\gamma\nabla\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{u}
,
\gamma\geq 0
, in (
1
) is zero for the exact solution, but it will be an important ingredient for constructing our approximation. Numerous advantages of the grad-div stabilization are well-known, cf.
[
17
,
20
,
15
,
19
]
.

Solving the system (
1
)-(
3
) is necessary for moderate Reynolds number flows or time-averaged unsteady Navier-Stokes equations. To fix the ideas, let’s consider the standard Picard linearization
[
11
]
of (
1
)-(
3
):

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}_{k}+\mathbf{u}_{k-1}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k}+\nabla p_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}\quad\text{in }\;\Omega,

(4)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{k}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\;\Omega,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{on }\;\partial\Omega.

(6)

Denoting by
\mathbf{U}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbf{P}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
the coefficient vectors corresponding to the spatial discretizations of
\mathbf{u}_{k}
and
p_{k}
, (
4
)-(
6
), requires the solution of the following non-symmetric saddle-point linear system

\begin{bmatrix}A_{k-1}&B^{T}\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{U}_{k}\\
\mathbf{P}_{k}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{F}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix},

(7)

at each iteration
k
, where
A_{k-1}
is the
n\times n
matrix corresponding to velocity terms in (
4
), and
B
is
m\times n
matrix corresponding to the pressure term. Direct solvers usually run out of memory for 3D problems, and solving this saddle-point system iteratively is also notoriously difficult.

Since the classical work of Chorin
[
7
]
, the numerical approximation of the unsteady counterpart of system (
1
)-(
3
) that gives easier linear systems to solve has received much attention in the literature
[
12
,
13
]
. Many of these fractional-type schemes heavily rely on the presence of the discretized time derivative of
\mathbf{u}
. On the other hand, such splitting schemes for genuinely steady problems are less studied, cf.
[
6
,
23
,
9
]
.

One can formally obtain a decoupling solution approach for (
7
) by performing the following (incremental) block LU decomposition of (
7
) as

\begin{bmatrix}A_{k-1}&0\\
B&-BA_{k-1}^{-1}B^{T}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&A^{-1}_{k-1}B^{T}\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{U}_{k}\\
\mathbf{P}_{k}-\mathbf{P}_{k-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{F}-B^{T}\mathbf{P}_{k-1}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}.

(8)

The system (
8
) formally splits the velocity, and the pressure solves. However, the pressure solve needs the inversion of the
m\times m
Schur complement matrix
S_{k-1}=-BA_{k-1}^{-1}B^{T}
at each iteration
k
, rendering the scheme inefficient for large 3D problems.

More effective algebraic splitting methods for steady incompressible Navier–Stokes equations can be derived by observing that
[
28
]
, for any two
n\times n
matrices
H_{1},H_{2}
, an ILU decomposition of the form

\begin{bmatrix}A_{k-1}&0\\
B&-BH_{1}B^{T}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&H_{2}B^{T}\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{U}_{k}\\
\mathbf{P}_{k}-\mathbf{P}_{k-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{F}-B^{T}\mathbf{P}_{k-1}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}

(9)

is also strongly consistent approximations of (
1
)-(
3
), provided they generate stable schemes. Using this observation, the authors of
[
21
]
considered an iterative Incremental Picard-Yosida scheme based on the following ILU factorization:

\begin{bmatrix}A_{k-1}&B^{T}\\
B&B\,\left(A^{-1}_{k-1}-\widetilde{A}^{-1}\right)\,B^{T}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{k-1}&0\\
B&-B\widetilde{A}^{-1}B^{T}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&A^{-1}_{k-1}B^{T}\\
0&I\end{bmatrix},

(10)

where the
n\times n
matrix
\widetilde{A}
corresponds to linear velocity terms in (
1
).
A related algebraic splitting of
[
28
]
is based on separating the nonlinear term into implicit and explicit parts using a weight parameter
\alpha\in[0,1]
:

\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{A}+\alpha C_{k-1}&B^{T}\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{A}+\alpha C_{k-1}&0\\
B&-B\widetilde{A}^{-1}B^{T}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&\widetilde{A}^{-1}B^{T}\\
0&I\end{bmatrix},

(11)

where the right-hand-side vector is

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{F}-B^{T}\mathbf{P}_{k-1}-(1-\alpha)C_{k-1}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix},

(12)

and
C_{k-1}
is of size
n\times n
corresponds to convection term.

In this work, inspired by
[
29
]
, we propose a novel Steady Incremental Viscosity Splitting (SIVS) method with grad-div stabilization. Unlike projection schemes, the unsteady Viscosity Splitting scheme
[
29
]
yields an end-of-step velocity field that is both divergence-free and satisfies the correct boundary conditions. The scheme that we propose in this paper also gives (discretely) div-free velocity subject to full Dirichlet boundary conditions. Moreover, the scheme can be interpreted as an algebraic splitting method akin to (
9
), which requires much simpler linear system solvers than the standard Picard system (
8
). We also mention the related work
[
16
]
, which studied a non-incremental viscosity splitting method for a stationary second-grade fluid model without any grad-div stabilizations.

This paper is arranged as follows. In the next Section, we introduce the notations and some preliminary results. In Section
3
, we present our Algorithm, establish uniform boundedness and convergence results, and also present the algebraic splitting viewpoint of our scheme. In Section
4
, we present numerical tests, and Section
5
includes the concluding remark.

2
Notations and preliminaries

Throughout this work, vector fields and spaces of the form
\mathcal{S}^{d}
are denoted using boldface notation.
Standard notations for Sobolev spaces and corresponding norms will be used throughout the paper, see e.g.,
[
1
]
. In particular,
(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\|\cdot\|
denote
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product and the corresponding norm, respectively.
\mathbf{H}^{k}
, where
k
is an integer greater than zero, will denote the space of vector-valued functions each of whose
n
components belong to
H^{k}
, the Sobolev space of real-valued functions with square integrable derivatives of order up to
k
equipped with the usual norm
\|\cdot\|_{k}
. The dual space of
\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
will be denoted by
\mathbf{H}^{-1}

and the duality pairing between these two spaces is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
The norm in

\mathbf{H}^{-1}
is given by
\|\mathbf{f}\|_{-1}=\langle\mathbf{f},(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathbf{f}\rangle^{1/2}.

The equivalent weak formulation of (
1
)-(
3
) reads as follows:
\forall(\mathbf{v},q)\in(\mathbf{X},Q)
, find
(\mathbf{u},p)\in(\mathbf{X},Q)
satisfying

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+c^{*}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+b(p,\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\left<\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}\right>,

(13)

\displaystyle b(q,\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=0,

(14)

where
\mathbf{X}:=\mathbf{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\;Q:=L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\nu(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v})+\gamma(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}),

\displaystyle b(p,\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=-(p,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}),

\displaystyle c(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}),

\displaystyle c^{*}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=c(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})+\frac{1}{2}\left({(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u})}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\right).

The following bound holds for all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{X}
, see
for instance

[
11
,
26
]
:

\displaystyle c^{*}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\leq\mathcal{M}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|,

(15)

for some
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.
The assumption on
\Omega
is sufficient to ensure that the inf–sup (or Ladyzhenskaya–Babuška–Brezzi, LBB) condition holds (see
[
4
,
5
,
11
]
). More precisely, there exists a constant
\beta>0
, depending only on
\Omega
, such that

\inf_{q\in Q}\;\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X}}\frac{b(q,\mathbf{v})}{\|q\|\,\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|}\geq\beta.

We also define the div-free subspace of
\mathbf{X}
as usual:

\mathbf{V}:=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X}:\;b(q,\mathbf{v})=0\;\forall q\in Q\},

which is obviously characterized by

\mathbf{V}=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X}:\;\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\;\text{in}\;\Omega\}.

For the operator
{\rm L}:=-\nu\Delta-\gamma\nabla\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}:\;\mathbf{X}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}^{-1}
that is associated with the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
, we define:

\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\mathrm{L}}:=\sqrt{\langle\mathrm{L}\mathbf{g},\mathbf{g}\rangle}\text{ and }\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}:=\sqrt{\langle\mathrm{L}^{-1}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle}.

(16)

The following norm equivalences can be easily verified:

Lemma 1

The following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\forall\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X},\,\sqrt{\nu}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|\leq\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}}\leq\sqrt{\nu+\gamma}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|,

(17)

\displaystyle\text{ and }\forall\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{H}^{-1},\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu+\gamma}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{-1}\leq\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{-1}.

Proof 1

The first equivalence easily follows by using the inequality
\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{v}\|\leq\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|
that holds for
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X}
. The second equivalence follows from the first equivalence, and using the dual norm formulation of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}
:

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}=\sup\limits_{\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X}}\frac{\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}}}.

(18)

Next, we state two preliminary lemmas on non-negative sequences that will be used in the sequel, taken from
[
24
]
.

Lemma 2
(Sequences converging to
0
)

Assume that
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{b_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are non-negative sequences of real numbers and
\exists\,\omega_{i}
,
\varepsilon_{i}
,
i=\overline{1,2}
, such that
0<\varepsilon_{i}\leq\omega_{i}
and

\omega_{1}a_{k+1}+\omega_{2}b_{k+1}+c_{k+1}\leq\left(\omega_{1}-\varepsilon_{1}\right)a_{k}+\left(\omega_{2}-\varepsilon_{2}\right)b_{k}+c_{k}.

Then
\exists\,C\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(a_{k},b_{k},c_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=(0,0,C).

Lemma 3
(Contractivity of sequences converging to
0
)

Assume that
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{b_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are non-negative sequences of real numbers and
\exists\,\omega_{i}
,
i=\overline{1,3}
,
\varepsilon_{i}
,
i=\overline{1,2}
, such that
0<\varepsilon_{i}\leq\omega_{i}
,
i=1,2
,

\omega_{1}a_{k+1}+\omega_{2}b_{k+1}+\omega_{3}c_{k+1}\leq\left(\omega_{1}-\varepsilon_{1}\right)a_{k}+\left(\omega_{2}-\varepsilon_{2}\right)b_{k}+\omega_{3}c_{k}

(19)

and

c_{k}\leq\tau_{1}a_{k+1}+\tau_{2}a_{k}+\tau_{3}b_{k+1}\text{ for some positive }\tau_{i},i=\overline{1,3}.

(20)

Then there exists a sequence, which is a linear combination of
a_{k},b_{k},c_{k}
that is contracting towards
0
.

We also recall the well-posedness result for (
1
)-(
3
) from
[
27
]
:

Lemma 4

The system (
1
)-(
3
) always has a solution which satisfies

\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{f}\|_{-1}}{\nu}\;\text{ and }\;\|p\|\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\left(2\|\mathbf{f}\|_{-1}+\mathcal{M}_{0}\frac{\|\mathbf{f}\|_{-1}^{2}}{\nu^{2}}\right),

(21)

where
\beta>0
is the inf-sup constant. Additionally, the solution is unique under a small data condition:

\Lambda_{0}:=\nu^{-2}\mathcal{M}_{0}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{-1}<1,

(22)

where

\mathcal{M}_{0}:=\sup\limits_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{X}}\dfrac{(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})}{\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|}.

(23)

3
Steady Incremental Viscosity Splitting method

We present our new SIVS method for solving the system (
1
)-(
3
). In the unsteady IVS scheme
[
29
]
, the splitting is performed with respect to the time derivative and the viscosity terms. Since
\mathbf{u}_{t}
is absent in our system, the splitting is performed with respect to the viscous terms enhanced by grad-div stabilization:

Algorithm 3.1

For an initial guess
(\mathbf{u}_{0},p_{0})=(\mathbf{0},0)
,
some positive parameter
\gamma>0
,
and for
k=1,2,\ldots
, we compute the following until convergence:

Step 1:

Given
\mathbf{u}_{k-1}\in\mathbf{V}
,
p_{k-1}\in Q
, find
\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k}\in\mathbf{X}
solving

a(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k},\mathbf{v})+c^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{k-1},\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k},\mathbf{v})+b(p_{k-1},\mathbf{v})=<\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}>,\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X}.

(24)

Step 2:

Find
(\mathbf{u}_{k},p_{k})\in\mathbf{X}\times Q

solving

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k},\mathbf{v})+b(p_{k}-p_{k-1},\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X},

(25)

\displaystyle b(q,\mathbf{u}_{k})

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\,q\in Q.

Next, we establish the uniform boundedness and convergence results for our Algorithm
3.1
.

Theorem 5
(Uniform boundedness)

Assume that
(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k},\mathbf{u}_{k},p_{k})\in(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{V},Q)
is a solution of Algorithm
3.1
. If

\Lambda:=\nu^{-2}\mathcal{M}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{-1}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},

(26)

then
\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k}\|,\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k}\|,\|\nabla p_{k}\|_{-1}
are uniformly bounded, and

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{k}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{u},\text{ and }\,\nabla p_{k}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}\nabla p\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty,

where
(\mathbf{u},p)
is the unique solution of (
1
)–(
3
).

Proof 2

First, let us note that the uniqueness of the solution of (
1
)–(
3
) follows from condition (
26
).
Defining the errors as
\mathbf{e}_{k}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{k}
,
\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k}
,
\delta_{k}=p-p_{k}
, and subtracting (
24
)-(
25
) from (
13
)-(
14
) we get the error equations:

a(\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k},\mathbf{v})+c^{*}(\mathbf{e}_{k-1},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+c^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{k-1},\mathbf{\tilde{e}_{k}},\mathbf{v})+b(\delta_{k-1},\mathbf{v})=0,\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X},

(27)

and

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k},\mathbf{v})+b(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k-1},\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X},

(28)

\displaystyle b(q,\mathbf{e}_{k})

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\,q\in Q.

Letting
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}
in (
27
) and using the skew-symmetry of the trilinear form
c^{*}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+b(\delta_{k-1},\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})

\displaystyle=-c^{*}(\mathbf{e}_{k-1},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})\implies

(29)

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+b(\delta_{k-1},\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}-\mathbf{e}_{k})

\displaystyle=-c^{*}(\mathbf{e}_{k-1},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k-1}\|\,\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|\,\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\nu\Lambda\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k-1}\|\,\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{4}\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\nu\Lambda^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k-1}\|^{2}.

To deal with
b(\delta_{k-1},\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}-\mathbf{e}_{k})
term, we first test (
28
) with
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{e}_{k}\in\mathbf{V}
to obtain that

\nu\left(\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2}-\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\nabla(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})\|^{2}\right)+\gamma\left(\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2}-\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})\|^{2}\right)=0.

(30)

Moreover, the first equation of (
28
) implies that
\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}-\mathbf{e}_{k}=\mathrm{L^{-1}}\nabla(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k-1})
in
\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}
, and that

\|\nabla(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k-1})\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}=\|\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}}=\nu\|\nabla(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})\|^{2}+\gamma\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})\|^{2}.

(31)

Thus,

\displaystyle b(\delta_{k-1},\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}-\mathbf{e}_{k})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla\delta_{k-1},\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}-\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\rangle

(32)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla\delta_{k-1},\mathrm{L^{-1}}\nabla(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k-1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\|\nabla\delta_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}-\|\nabla\delta_{k-1}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}-\|\nabla(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k-1})\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\|\nabla\delta_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}-\|\nabla\delta_{k-1}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}\right]-\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})\|^{2}.

Combine (
29
) with the last identity to get

\displaystyle\frac{3\nu}{4}\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\nabla\delta_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}-\|\nabla\delta_{k-1}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}\right)

(33)

\displaystyle-\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k})\|^{2}\leq\nu\Lambda^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k-1}\|^{2}.

Now divide (
30
) by
2
and add to (
33
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\nu}{4}\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\delta_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\delta_{k-1}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}+\nu\Lambda^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k-1}\|^{2}.

(34)

Now, thanks to (
26
), we can invoke the induction argument, to conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\nu}{4}\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\delta_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\delta_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}+\nu\Lambda^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{0}\|^{2}

(35)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla p\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}+\nu\Lambda^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|^{2}.

The triangle inequality then implies the uniform boundedness of the solution:

\exists K_{i}>0,\,i=\overline{1,3},\text{ with }\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k}\|\leq K_{1},\,\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k}\|\leq K_{2},\text{ and }\|\nabla p_{k}\|_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}\leq K_{3}.

(36)

Moreover, Lemma
2
implies that

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k}\|=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{e}_{k}\|=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|=0,

(37)

where we took

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2},\,b_{k}=\frac{\nu}{4}\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2}\right),\,c_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\delta_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}},

(38)

\displaystyle\omega_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\nu}{2},\omega_{2}=1,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\nu}{2}-\nu\Lambda^{2}>0,\,\varepsilon_{2}=0.

Then passing to the limit as
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
27
) implies the convergence of
\|\nabla\delta_{k}\|_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}
to zero as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Theorem 6
(Contractivity)

Let
(\mathbf{e}_{k},\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k},\delta_{k})
be as in the Theorem
5
. If
\Lambda<\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
as in (
26
), then there exists
\omega_{j}>0
,
j=\overline{1,3}
, and
\tau\in(0,1)
such that

\psi_{k}:=\omega_{1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\|\nabla\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2}}}+\omega_{2}\left(\frac{\nu}{4}\|\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{e}_{k}\|^{2}\right)\right)+\omega_{3}\|\nabla\delta_{k}\|_{\mathrm{L^{-1}}}^{2}

(39)

is a contractive sequence satisfying

\psi_{k+1}<\tau\psi_{k}.

5
Conclusions

We presented a new efficient scheme for solving the steady Navier-Stokes equations with grad-div stabilization. We proved boundedness and convergence under a reasonably smallness assumption on the data.
The algebraic splitting interpretation of the SIVS scheme shows that our scheme results in significantly simpler and cheaper linear systems compared to standard Picard linearizations, making it suitable for large 3D problems.

Numerically, the SIVS scheme demonstrated expected convergence rates and reproduced standard benchmarks accurately, including the 2D lid-driven cavity (up to
{\rm Re}=5000
) and a 3D flow past a circular cylinder, while maintaining low iteration counts. Future work will focus on extending this approach to steady MHD and other multiphysics problems.
