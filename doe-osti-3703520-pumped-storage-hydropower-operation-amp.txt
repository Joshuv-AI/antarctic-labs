1. Overview
The five-component operations and
maintenance cost framework

 1. Introduction

• The National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) develops a bottom-up cost
  model for pumped storage hydropower (PSH), available online as a
  downloadable spreadsheet and an interactive web tool.
• The cost model was initially created to characterize capital costs only.
• This slide deck describes methodology to expand the NLR PSH cost model to
  include itemized operations and maintenance (O&M) costs, using a variety of
  literature sources and approaches.

                                                                    National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   4

 1. Overview

This approach separates O&M into five components
• Direct Costs
 ◦ Routine direct O&M
 ◦ Major overhauls.

• Indirect Costs
 ◦ Administrative and general (A&G) expenses
 ◦ Operating insurance
 ◦ Property taxes and regulatory fees.

• Each component is addressed separately because source literature treats them differently, and data quality
  varies across them.
• No single publication provides a complete estimate, which includes all five components for newly developed
  U.S. PSH.
• Figures herein are assembled from a mix of empirical operating data, parametric equations, and standard
  regulatory percentages.

2. Routine Direct
O&M
Recurring plant-site operating costs and
screening-level basis

 2. Routine Direct O&M

Scope and fixed vs. variable structure
• Covers all recurring day-to-day operating costs at the plant site: operations labor, maintenance labor,
  consumable materials and spare parts, and routine contractor support for inspections, minor repairs,
  and environmental monitoring.
• Costs are typically expensed (not capitalized) under the FERC Uniform System of Accounts. These
  are incurred every year of the plant’s life.
• Major overhauls are explicitly excluded and treated as a separate cost component.
• Fixed component costs (staff wages and benefits, scheduled inspections, periodic servicing, routine
  spares) dominate; staffing is roughly half of direct O&M in plant-survey data from EPRI GS-7325.
• Screening-level analyses reports routine direct O&M as a single $/kW-yr figure rather than a fixed-
  plus-variable cost, which is assumed appropriate at a screening level but obscures operational
  considerations such as pump-generation cycles and capacity factor.

 2. Routine Direct O&M (continued)
Screening-level equation for Routine Direct O&M (from EPRI GS-6669 in 1987$s):

EPRI/HDR equation (primary basis)
• EPRI GS-6669 [1] developed a power-law equation regressed against 1980–1984 FERC Form 1 data for eight operating U.S. PSH plants.
 EPRI 1023140 [2] updated escalation factors (a 2.5X factor from 1987 to 2010 dollars) and confirmed that the structure remained
 appropriate for screening use.

FERC Form 1 empirical cross-check
• Oladosu and Sasthav [4] / 2023 U.S. Hydropower Market Report [5]: “Very Large” plant category (>500 MW) averaged ~$16/kW-yr in
 2020$. Six of seven plants in that category are PSH.

• Cross-check tests temporal stability rather than independent agreement—EPRI/HDR was itself regressed using 1980–1984 Form 1 data.

                                  O&M Costs (1987$/yr) = 34,730 × C0.32 × E0.33
 Where: C = Plant Capacity (MW); E = Annual Gross Energy Output (GWh).

 Then multiply by an escalation factor for the necessary dollar year, e.g., 2.5 for 2010

Periodic refurbishment and replacement of

 3. Major Overhauls

Scope
 Covers periodic refurbishment and replacement of major rotating and hydraulic equipment at 10- to 60-year intervals: pump-turbine runners,
 generator rewinds and rotor-pole refurbishment, generator circuit breakers, spherical (inlet) valves, governors, powerhouse cranes, and end-of-
 life runner and generator core restack.

 Project Element Typical Frequency
 generator circuit breaker and phase reversal switch rebuild 1,000 starts
 Spherical valve seal maintenance 10 years
 Pump-turbine overhaul 20 years
 Powerhouse crane major refurbishment 20 years
 Governor replacement 20 years
 Generator-motor rewind/rotor pole refurbishment 30 years
 Generator circuit breaker replacement 30 years
 Spherical valve refurbishment 30 years
 Runner impeller replacement 60 years
 Generator-motor core restack/rotor pole refurbishment 60 years

 These costs are typically capitalized or tracked as funded reserves in project finance structures rather than expensed annually. They must
 be annualized and included in the O&M cost stack for complete life cycle cost analysis.

 3. Major Overhauls

Source Basis
Mongird et al. [6]—primary screening-level default

• 2020 Grid Energy Storage Technology Cost and Performance Assessment (PNNL) provides an annualized refurbishment cost for
    PSH of $9.0/kW-yr in 2018$, ~$12.0/kW-yr in 2025$.

• This estimate is intended for technology cost comparison rather than project-level life cycle planning. The cost basis is not fully
 documented and should be reviewed before being used as a final value for a specific project.

EPRI GS-7325 [8]—contextual support

• Field visits to over 30 U.S. PSH plants during 1985–1988. Routine annual maintenance: 800–1,200 man-hours/unit/yr (up to
 4,000 for large units). Major overhaul labor: 6,700–36,000 man-hours; planning benchmark 15,000 for 10-year overhaul.

 Major Overhauls ~$12/kW per year

4. Indirect Costs
A&G expenses, operating insurance,
property taxes and FERC fees

4.1 Administrative and General Expenses

 Allocated corporate overhead beyond plant-site operations
 • Covers corporate overhead allocated to the plant from utility or corporate headquarters: executive and general management,
 accounting and finance, human resources, information technology, legal and regulatory affairs, and other shared services.

 • In the FERC Uniform System of Accounts for investor-owned utilities, A&G is captured in Accounts 920–935 and reported at the
 utility level rather than allocated to individual plants in public filings. That is why it is not captured in the FERC Form 1 plant-level
 cross-check.

 FERC Hydropower Evaluation Manual [7], as cited in EPRI GS-6669 [1]

 • Establishes an A&G surcharge of 35% of direct O&M cost as a default for hydropower cost evaluation. This implementation of the
 factor is used throughout the PSH O&M literature.

 EPRI GS-6669 [1] and EPRI 1023140 [2]

 • 1990 EPRI Guide and 2011 HDR update both carry the 35% A&G factor forward without structural change or independent re-
 derivation, endorsing it as an appropriate screening-level assumption for PSH. No more recent published sources were identified
 that update or replace this factor for large PSH applications.

 A&G ~ 35% of direct O&M

                                                                                                                    National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   14

 4.2 Operating Insurance

Property, liability, and business-interruption coverage
• Covers the annual premium for property damage coverage on the physical plant, public liability coverage, and
 business-interruption coverage for lost revenue during an unplanned outage.
• For a facility with a dam, property insurance typically includes dam-failure liability coverage, subject to underwriting
 requirements that may vary by insurer.
• Excludes construction-phase insurance (builder’s risk, contractor’s liability)—this is considered a capital cost rather
 than O&M.
• Excludes worker’s compensation and employer liability—this is included in direct O&M labor costs.

FERC Hydropower Evaluation Manual [7], as cited in EPRI GS-6669 [1]
• Default annual rate of 0.1% of plant investment cost is attributed to the August 1978 FERC manual via EPRI GS-6669
 [1].

 Operating Insurance ~ 0.1% of Plant Investment (assumed total direct+indirect)

4.3 Property Taxes and Annual FERC
 Charges
Two distinct sub-items: local property taxes and FERC fees
• Property taxes are typically levied by local jurisdictions on the physical plant, equipment, and land—the amount depends on state law, local mill
 rates, and assessment methodology.
• FERC annual charges are fees assessed against licensed hydropower capacity under Part I of the Federal Power Act, distinct from one-time
 licensing fees. Income taxes are excluded (these are included in project finance models, not operating cost estimates).
Property taxes—no universal default source
• Inherently site-specific; cannot be derived from a single published default. No published PSH-specific property tax benchmark was identified.
FERC annual charges—18 CFR Part 11, FY2025 schedule [9]
Set under three subsections: § 11.1 (administrative charges), § 11.2 (use of federal lands), § 11.4 (use of federal dams for PSH). For a typical
greenfield closed-loop PSH project on private land, only § 11.1 applies.
• Under § 11.1(c)(3)(ii), administrative annual charge = installed capacity (kW) × Unit Charge Factor (UCF). FY2025 UCF for private licensees:
 $2.775556 per charge-factor unit = ~$ 2.78/kW-yr for a 1,000 MW plant. Well below the 2% maximum cap of FERC’s total adjusted
 hydropower program administration costs for the year per § 11.1(f).
• Under §11.2 annual charges for the use of federal lands are assessed at per-acre rates set by county and updated each fiscal year by FERC;
 for six currently proposed projects, FY2026 rates range from approximately $7/acre (Carbon County, WY) to $47/acre (Knox County, KY),
 reflecting the low agricultural land values typical of rural western and Appalachian counties where PSH development is most likely to occur.
• Under §11.4 Annual charges for the use of federal dams by pumped storage projects are determined by FERC on a case-by-case basis under
 Section 10(e) of the Federal Power Act and cannot be estimated at the screening level; they must be established through the licensing process
 for projects that propose to use a federal dam or other federal structure.

 5. Conclusions

A five-component framework covers the full O&M cost stack for new PSH
• No single published source provides a complete O&M estimate for newly developed U.S. PSH. The five-component
 method assembles direct costs (routine O&M and major overhauls) and indirect costs (A&G, operating insurance,
 property taxes and FERC fees) from separate source literature, each with its own data basis and uncertainty.

The EPRI/HDR equation is appropriate for screening-level routine O&M estimation
• The EPRI GS-6669 power-law equation, escalated using the USBR CCT Composite O&M index, produces estimates
 of ~$19/kW-yr (1,000 MW) in 2025 dollars. This is consistent with FERC Form 1 empirical averages for the Very Large
 plant category, confirming temporal stability of the equation structure over four decades.

The method is scalable and transparent
• Each component can be calculated from a small number of site-specific inputs (capacity, energy throughput, plant
 investment cost) and applied to any prospective PSH facility, including closed-loop sites in the NLR PSH supply curves.
 Estimates are appropriate for AACE Class 5 screening use; project-level analysis requires site-specific cost studies.

Key data gaps remain
• No publicly available plant-level indirect O&M data exists for merchant PSH operators. Property taxes and federal land
 and dam use charges are inherently site-specific with no published PSH default.
