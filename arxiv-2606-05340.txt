Hasse-Weil Zeta Functions Modulo a Prime
Chris Hall

Abstract
. Let 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} be a finite field of characteristic p p and π : Y → X \pi\colon Y\to X be a finite 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -morphism of separated 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -schemes of finite type. Suppose π \pi is generically Galois with group G G of prime order r ≠ p r\neq p . We determine the mod- r r reduction of the zeta function of Y Y in terms of the zeta function of X X and the branch locus Z ⊂ X Z\subset X of π \pi . We give applications to curves and to numerators of hyperelliptic/superelliptic curves.

1.
Theorem Statement

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
be a prime and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
For each separated
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-scheme
X
of finite type, let
|X|
be the set of closed points, that is, the
\Gal(\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
-orbits of
X(\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and

\zeta(X,T):=\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|X(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})|\frac{T^{n}}{n}\right)=\prod_{x\in|X|}\det(1-T^{\deg(x)})^{-1}\in 1+T\cdot\mathbb{Z}[[T]]

be the (Hasse-Weil) zeta function of
X
. Recall
\zeta(X,T)\in\mathbb{Q}(T)
(see
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
and
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a finite
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-morphism of separated
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-schemes of finite type. Let
Z\subset X
be the branch locus of
\pi
. Suppose
\pi
is generically Galois with group
G
of prime order
r\neq p
.
Then

(1.1)

\zeta(Y,T)\equiv\zeta(X,T)^{r}\zeta(Z,T)^{1-r}\bmod r.

A striking aspect of (
1.1
) is that the analogous identity over
\mathbb{Z}
is not necessarily true (because of weight mismatches).

Theorem
1.1
is inspired by
[
7
, Theorem 4]
. We give a naive proof in Section
2
and a cohomological proof in Section
4
. The latter uses a cohomological formula given in Section
3
for the reduction of
\zeta(X,T)
modulo a prime
\ell\neq p
. We also give two corollaries (for curves) in Section
5
.

An impetus for this paper is question of Richard Griffon: when is the numerator of the zeta function of a curve over a finite field a trinomial? A necessary condition is that the numerator is congruent to a trinomial modulo an integer
N>1
.
In Section
6
, we apply the results of Section
5
(where
N=r
) to numerators of hyperelliptic (
r=2
) and superelliptic (
r>2
) curves.

2.
Naive Proof of Theorem

Consider the Euler-product expansion

(2.1)

\zeta(Y,T)=\prod_{y\in|Y|}(1-T^{\deg(y)})^{-1}=\prod_{x\in|X|}\prod_{y\in|Y|:\pi(y)=x}(1-T^{\deg(y)})^{-1}.

Suppose
x\in|X|
, and let

(2.2)

|Y_{x}|:=\{y\in|Y|:\pi(y)=x\}\text{ and }L(Y_{x},T):=\prod_{y\in|Y_{x}|}(1-T^{\deg(y)}).

Suppose
y\in|Y_{x}|
, and let
I_{y}\subseteq D_{y}\subseteq G
be the inertia and decomposition groups of
y
.

Recall that
\pi
is étale over
X\smallsetminus Z
and totally ramified of prime degree
r
over
Z
and that
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/r
acts transitively on
|Y_{x}|
. Deduce

(2.3)

(|I_{y}|,[D_{y}:I_{y}],|Y_{x}|)=\begin{cases}(1,1,r)\text{ or }(1,r,1)&x\in|X|\smallsetminus|Z|\\
(r,1,1)&x\in|Z|\end{cases}.

Observe that

(1-T^{\deg(x)})^{r}\equiv(1-T^{r\deg(x)})\bmod r,

and (
2.3
) imply

(2.4)

L(Y_{x},T)\equiv\begin{cases}(1-T^{\deg(x)})^{r}&x\in|X|\smallsetminus|Z|\\
1-T^{\deg(x)}&x\in|Z|\end{cases}.

Combining (
2.1
), (
2.2
), and (
2.4
) gives

\zeta(Y,T)\equiv\prod_{x\in|X|}(1-T^{\deg(x)})^{-r}\prod_{z\in|Z|}(1-T^{\deg(z)})^{r-1}\equiv\zeta(X,T)^{r}\zeta(Z,T)^{1-r}\bmod r

as desired.

3.
Cohomological Reduction of Zeta Modulo a Prime

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a separated
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-scheme of finite type and
\bar{X}
be its base change to
\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
\phi\in\Gal(\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the geometric Frobenius and
\ell\neq p
be a rational prime. Then

(3.1)

\zeta(X,T)\equiv\prod_{i}\det(1-\phi\,T\mid H^{i}_{c}(\bar{X},\mathbb{F}_{\ell}))^{(-1)^{i+1}}\bmod\ell.

Proof.

A theorem of Grothendieck (see
[
5
, Théorème 5.1]
or
[
3
, Theorem 10.5.1]
) implies that

\zeta(X,T)=\prod_{i}\det(1-\phi\,T\mid H^{i}_{c}(\bar{X},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))^{(-1)^{i+1}}

(since
X
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-compactifiable). We must analyze a product with
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
in lieu of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. This requires taking into account that
M_{i}:=H^{i}_{c}(\bar{X},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is a finitely generated (see
[
6
, Exposée VI, Lemme 2.2.1]
) and not necessarily free (compare
[
4
]
).

Let
T_{i}\subseteq M_{i}
be the torsion submodule and
F_{i}:=M_{i}/T_{i}
be the maximal free quotient so that

\det(1-\phi\,T\mid H^{i}_{c}(\bar{X},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))=\det(1-\phi\,T\mid F_{i})\in 1+T\cdot\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[T]

and thus

(3.2)

\zeta(X,T)=\prod_{i}\det(1-\phi\,T\mid F_{i})^{(-1)^{i+1}}.

Consider the exact sequence of étale
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-sheaves on
X
given by

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\overset{\times\ell}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\longrightarrow 0.

Its long exact cohomology sequence breaks into short exact sequences of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[\phi]
-modules

0\longrightarrow H^{i}_{c}(\bar{X},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\longrightarrow H^{i}_{c}(\bar{X},\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\longrightarrow H^{i+1}_{c}(\bar{X},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})[\ell]\longrightarrow 0

or equivalently

(3.3)

0\longrightarrow(T_{i}\oplus F_{i})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\longrightarrow H^{i}_{c}(\bar{X},\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\longrightarrow T_{i+1}[\ell]\longrightarrow 0.

For each finite
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[\phi]
-module
M
, let

\Lambda(M):=\det(1-\phi\,T\mid M)\in 1+T\cdot\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[T].

Observe that the exactness of (
3.3
) implies that

\Lambda(H^{i}_{c}(\bar{X},\mathbb{F}_{\ell}))=\Lambda((T_{i}\oplus F_{i})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\cdot\Lambda(T_{i+1}[\ell])=\Lambda(F_{i}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\cdot\Lambda(T_{i}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\cdot\Lambda(T_{i+1}[\ell]).

Moreover, Proposition
3.2
(in Appendix
3.1
) implies that

\Lambda(T_{i}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell})=\Lambda(T_{i}[\ell]).

Deduce that

\prod_{i}(\Lambda(T_{i}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\cdot\Lambda(T_{i+1}[\ell]))^{(-1)^{i+1}}=\prod_{i}\Lambda(T_{i}[\ell])^{(-1)^{i+1}}\cdot\prod_{i}\Lambda(T_{i+1}[\ell])^{(-1)^{i+1}}=1

(since the product telescopes) and

(3.4)

\prod_{i}\Lambda(H^{i}_{c}(\bar{U},\mathbb{F}_{\ell}))^{(-1)^{i+1}}=\prod_{i}\Lambda(F_{i}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell})^{(-1)^{i+1}}\equiv\prod_{i}\det(1-\phi\,T\mid F_{i})^{(-1)^{i+1}}\bmod\ell.

The desired identity (
3.1
) now follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.4
).
∎

4.
Cohomological Proof of Theorem

Let
j\colon U\to X
be the inclusion of
U:=X\smallsetminus Z
and
V:=\pi^{-1}(U)
. Observe that

(4.1)

\zeta(X,T)=\zeta(U,T)\zeta(Z,T)

since
X=U\sqcup Z
and that

(4.2)

\zeta(Y,T)=\zeta(V,T)\zeta(Z,T)

since
\pi
induces an isomorphism
\pi^{-1}(Z)\to Z
.

Let
\phi\in\Gal(\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the geometric Frobenius. Recall that
r:=|G|
is prime and
r\neq p
. Observe that
\mathcal{P}_{r}:=\pi_{*}\mathbb{F}_{r}
is a constructible sheaf of
\mathbb{F}_{r}[G]
-modules and that

H^{i}_{c}(\bar{V},\mathbb{F}_{r})\cong H^{i}_{c}(\bar{U},\mathcal{P}_{r})

as
\mathbb{F}_{r}[\phi]
-modules since
\pi
is finite (see
[
3
, Prop. 5.7.4]
), hence

(4.3)

\zeta(V,T)\equiv\prod_{i}\det(1-\phi\,T\mid H^{i}_{c}(\bar{U},\mathcal{P}_{r}))\bmod r

by Theorem
3.1
.

Observe that
j^{*}\mathcal{P}_{r}
is a lisse
\mathbb{F}_{r}[G]
-sheaf on
U
of rank
r
and that its
G
-semisimplification
\mathcal{S}_{r}
satisfies

(4.4)

\det(1-\phi\,T\mid H^{i}_{c}(\bar{U},\mathcal{P}_{r}))=\det(1-\phi\,T\mid H^{i}_{c}(\bar{U},\mathcal{S}_{r}))

for every
i
. Also,
\mathbb{F}_{r}
is the only simple
\mathbb{F}_{r}[G]
-module, hence
\mathcal{S}_{r}\cong\mathbb{F}_{r}^{r}
as
\mathbb{F}_{r}[G]
-sheaves and

(4.5)

\det(1-\phi\,T\mid H^{i}_{c}(\bar{U},\mathcal{S}_{r}))=\det(1-\phi\,T\mid H^{i}_{c}(\bar{U},\mathbb{F}_{r}))^{r}

for every
i
. In particular, combining (
4.3
), (
4.4
), and (
4.5
) yields

\zeta(V,T)\equiv\prod_{i}\det(1-\phi\,T\mid H^{i}_{c}(\bar{U},\mathbb{F}_{r}))^{r(-1)^{i+1}}\equiv\zeta(U,T)^{r}\bmod r

which combines with (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) to yield

(4.6)

\zeta(Y,T)\equiv\zeta(U,T)^{r}\zeta(Z,T)\equiv\zeta(X,T)^{r}\zeta(Z,T)^{1-r}\bmod r

as desired.

5.
Corollaries for Curves

Suppose
X,Y
are proper, smooth, and geometrically connected curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let

L(C,T):=\zeta(X,T)(1-T)(1-qT)\in 1+T\mathbb{Z}[T]

be the numerator of
\zeta(C,T)
for each
C\in\{X,Y\}
, and recall

\deg(L(C,T))=2\cdot\genus(C).

Observe that (
1.1
) (restated in (
4.6
)) is equivalent to

(5.1)

L(Y,T)\equiv L(X,T)^{r}(1-T)^{1-r}(1-qT)^{1-r}\prod_{z\in|Z|}(1-T^{\deg(z)})^{r-1}\bmod r.

Moreover, taking degrees of both sides of (
1.1
) yields the Riemann-Hurwitz formula:

(5.2)

2\cdot\genus(Y)-2=r\cdot(2\cdot\genus(X)-2)+(r-1)\cdot\deg(Z).

6.
Application to Hyperelliptic/Superelliptic Curves

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field,
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be distinct monic irreducibles,
e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
be positive, and

f:=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{i}^{e_{i}},\ \rad(f):=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x].

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{A}^{1}
be the zero locus of
f
, and observe
\deg(Z)=\deg(\rad(f))
.

Let
r
be a prime divisor of
q-1
and
A/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the affine curve
y^{r}=f(x)
. The group
G:={\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{r}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
acts faithfully on
A
via

Proposition 6.5
.

For the base case, observe

Let
