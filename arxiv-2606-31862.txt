Modal Measurable Logics via a Modal Loomis-Sikorski Representation Theorem
Nick Bezhanishvili, Jim de Groot, Lawrence S. Moss

Abstract
We investigate a modal extension of the infinitary classical logic with countable meets and joins, formulated with an eye toward measure-theoretic work in dynamical systems and in point-free ergodic theory. We define a modal formalism in this language, which we call modal measurable logics. We also introduce a Kripke-like semantics for these logics in measurable spaces taking a designated modal σ \sigma -ideal into consideration. Using a restriction of Jónsson-Tarski duality and a modal extension of the Loomis-Sikorski theorem, we prove completeness of modal measurable logics with respect to this new semantics.



1
Introduction

In (modal) logic, representation theorems are often used to bridge the gap between algebraic and geometric (frame-based) semantics. This allows one to transfer algebraic completeness of a logic to a completeness theorem for its frames.
For instance, the celebrated Stone representation theorem for Boolean algebras yields completeness of classical propositional logic
[
4
]
.
Similarly, Priestley’s representation theorem of distributive lattices yields completeness of positive logic with respect to its poset semantics
[
5
]
,
and Esakia’s representation of Heyting algebras
[
6
,
7
]
yields completeness of intuitionistic logic with respect to intuitionistic Kripke frames
[
4
]
.
Representation theorems for modal logics are often based on an existing representation for the underlying propositional logic,
such as the Jónsson–Tarski representation of modal algebras which gives rise to completeness of modal logic with respect to Kripke semantics
[
3
,
4
]
.
In this paper we carry on with this tradition.

The aim of this paper is to develop a modal formalism for reasoning about relational structures based on measurable spaces. Alternatively, such structures can be viewed as Kripke frames equipped with a measurable space structure.
Specifically, we work with a modal language with countable meets and joins. Ideally, these should be interpreted as intersections and unions; we investigate to what extent this is feasible when aiming for a completeness result.

As a starting point, we consider the countably infinite analogue
\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}
of the classical modal logic
\mathsf{K}
, obtained by extending the classical propositional logic with countable joins and meets with a unary modal operator
\Diamond
that distributes over countable joins. We call the algebras corresponding to
\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}

\sigma
-modal algebras
.
These arise from extending
\sigma
-Boolean algebras with a countable-join-preserving operator.
Our first contribution is to prove a Jónsson–Tarski representation theorem for
\sigma
-modal algebras, which yields order-topological completeness for
\sigma
-modal logics.

However, in the order-topological semantics of
\sigma
-modal logics, countable meets and joins are interpreted as the
interiors
of intersections and the

closures
of unions, respectively. While interesting, these structures do not form concrete
\sigma
-algebras in which countable meets and joins are interpreted as set-theoretic intersections and unions themselves.

To remedy this, we prove a modal version of the celebrated Loomis-Sikorski theorem, which was proven (independently) by Loomis
[
18
]
and Sikorski
[
23
]
in the 1940s.
Recall that this theorem states that every “abstract”
\sigma
-Boolean algebra (that is, every Boolean algebra in which countable joins and meets exist) is a quotient of a “concrete”
\sigma
-algebra of sets
(where the meets and joins are intersections and unions)
modulo a
\sigma
-ideal. This provides a concrete representation of
\sigma
-Boolean algebras.
Our modal Loomis-Sikorski theorem is one of the main technical contributions of the paper.

It turns out that to prove the modal Loomis-Sikorski theorem,
we need to extend the logic with a special infinitary rule, which we call the
Infinite Descending Chain
rule (IDC).
Similar rules are not uncommon in the realm of infinitary modal logics, and one
can for instance be found in
[
9
]
.
We call our logic
Modal Loomis-Sikorski Logic

\mathsf{MLS}
, and refer to the corresponding algebras as

measurable
\sigma
-modal algebras
. We prove:

Modal Loomis-Sikorski theorem:

Every measurable
\sigma
-modal algebra is the quotient of a “concrete”
\sigma
-modal algebra by a modal
\sigma
-ideal.

Based on this result, we develop a “measurable” semantics for Modal Loomis-Sikorski Logic based on
modal measurable spaces
with a modal
\sigma
-ideal
N
of marked sets that we think of as “designated null-sets”.
A modal measurable space consists of a measurable space
(X,\Sigma)
and a relation
R
on
X
such that
\Diamonddot
(the modal operator corresponding to
R
) maps measurable sets to measurable sets.
Modal formulas are then interpreted in the modal algebra
(\Sigma,\Diamonddot)
quotiented by
N
.

Quotienting by
N
seems unavoidable, both for technical reasons (in view of the Loomis–Sikorski theorem) and for conceptual ones. The ideal
N
will typically consist of the null-sets of some probability measure, which is also the perspective adopted by Scott
[
22
]
. We note that a similar move was advocated by Lando
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
, Fernández-Duque
[
8
]
and
Pavlov
[
20
]
. Indeed,
[
20
]
states
that
“One can argue that an object of the right category of spaces in measure theory is not a set equipped with a
\sigma
-algebra of measurable sets, but rather a set
S

equipped with a
\sigma
-algebra
M

of measurable sets and a
\sigma
-ideal
N

of negligible sets, i.e., sets of measure 0.”
Moreover, Jamneshan and Tao write in
[
13
]

that the quotient algebra should be viewed
“as a ‘point-free’ or ‘pointless’ abstraction of [a measurable space]
X
in which the null-sets have been ‘deleted’.”
They further observe:
“This ‘point-free’ approach to ergodic theory seems particularly well suited for studying actions of uncountable (discrete) groups, as by deleting the null-sets in advance, one can avoid to a large extent the standard difficulty that an uncountable union of null-sets is null.”
A similar approach appears in G. Bezhanishvili and Fernández-Duque
[
2
]
.

Using our modal version of the Loomis–Sikorski theorem, we prove the main completeness result of this paper:

Completeness theorem:

Every axiomatic extension of Modal Loomis-Sikorski Logic is sound and complete with respect to a class of marked modal measurable spaces.

With our work, we hope to lay the foundation for a modal logic approach to measure-based dynamical systems and (point-free) ergodic theory.
As a first attempt, we give an example of a modal measurable space used in the analysis of measure-preserving transformations in dynamical systems.

Related work

One line of work connects modal logic in its probabilistic variant to reasoning about uncertainty in games. This includes early work on the formalisation of Harsanyi type spaces due to Heifetz and Samet
[
12
]
(where measure theory first appears
in a prominent way), and later papers which use
a probabilistic modal logic, such as Moss and Viglizzo
[
19
]
.
Neither of these papers take up questions of completeness (as we do).
Some papers which do obtain completeness
include Goldblatt
[
11
]

and Zhou
[
24
]
. What all of the above papers have in common is that their modalities are quantitative: the sentences are of the form
B_{p}\varphi
, where
0\leq p\leq 1
, which can be read as saying that the probability of (or “belief” in) the sentence
\varphi
is at least
p
.
In the current paper, we investigate the use of modal operators to study a more dynamic intuition, rather than a quantitative one.

Another line of loosely related work concerns the combination of modal logic and topology, such as
[
2
,
17
,
22
]
. This type of work differs from ours because it often uses a topological interpretation of the modalities, following a long line of work on topological interpretations of modal logic, while we do not use the measurable space structure in this way.

Yet another set of papers
of potential relevance here are ones which call on dualities related to measure theory, such as
[
9
,
14
]
.
The main difference with our work is the semantic formalism:
in
[
9
,
14
]
models are given by

Markov processes
. These are coalgebras of the probability measure functor, and they might be thought of
as probabilistic analogues of Kripke frames. That is, they are structures where the accessibility relation comes with
probabilistic information
and the state set is “mainly just a set.”
Our setting, on the other hand, considers a measurable space with an accessibility relation
that is “mainly just a relation.”
(What we are saying here is not strictly correct: there are connections
between the set-theoretic and measure-theoretic aspects, both our paper and other work.)
Now in moving closer to our ultimate goal, one would like both a measure-theoretic space and a probabilistic
transition relation. We view the work in this paper as a necessary step towards this goal.

Stone duality and Jónsson-Tarski duality

For the results in this section we refer to e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
10
]
. A
Stone space
is a compact Hausdorff space
(X,\tau)

whose clopen subsets form a basis for the topology
\tau
. Write
\mathtt{Stone}
for
the category of Stone spaces and continuous functions,
and
\mathtt{BA}
for the category of Boolean algebras and (Boolean) homomorphisms.

The collection
\clp(X,\tau)
of clopen subsets of a Stone space forms
a Boolean algebra. Moreover, if
f:(X,\tau)\to(Y,\sigma)
is a
continuous function between Stone spaces, then

f^{-1}:\clp(Y,\sigma)\to\clp(X,\tau)
is a Boolean homomorphism.
This gives rise to a contravariant functor

\clp:\mathtt{Stone}\to\mathtt{BA}.

Conversely, every Boolean algebra
B
gives rise to a Stone space

\uf(B):=(X_{B},\tau_{B})
where
X_{B}
is the set of ultrafilters of

B
and
\tau_{B}
is the topology on
X_{B}
generated by the sets of the form
\theta(b):=\{u\in X_{B}\mid b\in u\}
, where
b
ranges over
B
.
Furthermore, if
h:A\to B
is a Boolean homomorphism,
then
h^{-1}:(X_{B},\tau_{B})\to(X_{A},\tau_{A})
is a continuous
function. This yields a contravariant functor

\uf:\mathtt{BA}\to\mathtt{Stone}.

Theorem 2.1

(Stone duality)
.

\clp
and
\uf
establish a dual equivalence

\mathtt{Stone}\equiv^{\op}\mathtt{BA}
.

A
\sigma
-Boolean algebra
is a Boolean algebra with countable meets
and countable joins, and a
\sigma
-homomorphism is a homomorphism that
preserves them. Write
\mathtt{\sigma BA}
for the category of
\sigma
-Boolean algebras
and
\sigma
-homomorphisms.
A Boolean algebra
B
is a
\sigma
-Boolean algebra if and only if its
dual Stone space
\uf(B)
satisfies:

\text{if }\{a_{n}\mid n\in\omega\}\text{ is a countable set of clopen sets, then }\overline{{\textstyle\bigcup}_{n\in\omega}a_{n}}\text{ is clopen.}

(1)

(Here, if
Y
is a subset of a topological space,
then
\overline{Y}
denotes its closure.)
In Stone spaces where (
1
) holds, we can endow the set
of clopens with a
\sigma
-BA structure: for a clopen
a
,
we take
-a
to be the set-theoretic complement of
a
, and
for
each countable sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of clopens we define

\bigvee_{n\in\omega}a_{n}=\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}}\qquad\bigwedge_{n\in\omega}a_{n}=\interior\Big(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\Big)

(2)

Define a
\sigma
-continuous function between
\sigma
-Stone spaces to
be a function whose dual is a
\sigma
-homomorphism, and write

\mathtt{\sigma Stone}
for the category of
\sigma
-Stone spaces and
\sigma
-continuous functions.
Then
[
10
, Section 39]

Theorem 2.2
.

Stone duality restricts to a dual equivalence
\mathtt{\sigma Stone}\equiv^{\op}\mathtt{\sigma BA}
.

Stone duality for Boolean algebras can be extended to a duality for
modal algebras. By a
modal algebra
we mean a pair
(B,\Diamond)

consisting of a Boolean algebra
B
and a unary operator
\Diamond

on
B
that preserves finite joins. (Here and always we include the empty join,
with
\bigvee\emptyset=\bot
.) With the expected notion of homomorphism,
these form the category
\mathtt{MA}
of modal algebras.

Dually, we work with
modal spaces
.
These are structures of the form
(X,\tau,R)
such that
(X,\tau)

is a Stone space,
R
is a point-closed relation (i.e.
R[x]
is closed in
(X,\tau)
for each
x\in X
), and

\Diamonddot a:=\{x\in X\mid R[x]\cap a\neq\emptyset\}

is clopen whenever
a
is clopen.
When the topological structure is viewed as a collection of
admissible sets, modal spaces are often referred to as

descriptive (general) frames

[
3
, Section 5.5]
.

With bounded continuous morphisms, modal spaces comprise the category
\mathtt{MSpace}
.
The functor
\clp
can be extended to a functor

\clp:\mathtt{MSpace}\to\mathtt{MA}

by sending a modal space
(X,\tau,R)
to the modal algebra
(\clp(X,\tau),\Diamonddot)
.
Conversely, if
(B,\Diamond)
is a modal algebra then

(X_{B},\tau_{B},R_{\Diamond})
is a modal space, where

uR_{\Diamond}v
iff (
b\in v
implies
\Diamond b\in u
for all
b\in B
).
With the same action on morphisms, the functor
\uf
extends to

\uf:\mathtt{MA}\to\mathtt{MSpace}.

Theorem 2.3

(Jónsson-Tarski duality)
.

\clp
and
\uf
establish a dual equivalence
\mathtt{MSpace}\equiv^{\op}\mathtt{MA}
.

In particular, we note that the map
\theta_{B}:(B,\Diamond)\to(\clp(X_{B},\tau_{B}),\Diamonddot)
, given by
\theta_{B}(b):=\{u\in X_{B}\mid b\in u\}
, is an isomorphism for every modal algebra
(B,\Diamond)
.

3
The
\sigma
-modal logic and the modal Loomis-Sikorski logic

The obvious “
\sigma
-analogue” of normal modal logic is the extension
with countable meets and joins and a diamond that distributes over
countable joins. This yields what we call
\sigma
-modal logic
.
However, in order to prove a modal analogue of the Loomis-Sikorski theorem,
and obtain completeness with respect to modal measurable spaces modulo
an ideal of null-sets, we need to stipulate an additional rule.
Adding this to
\sigma
-modal logic yields what we call

Modal Loomis-Sikorski logic
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
modal measurable language
is the collection

\mathcal{L_{\sigma}}
of formulas defined by the grammar

\varphi::=p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\bigvee_{i\in\omega}\varphi_{i}\mid\Diamond\varphi

where
p
ranges over a countably infinite set
\Prop
of proposition letters.
We abbreviate
\varphi\vee\psi:=\bigvee(\varphi,\psi,\psi,\psi,\ldots)

and
\bot:=p\vee\neg p
, and define
\top
,
\to
,
\bigwedge
,
\wedge

and
\Box
as expected.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathrm{Ax}\subseteq\mathcal{L_{\sigma}}
be a set of axioms.
The
\sigma
-modal logic

\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}\oplus\mathrm{Ax}
is the least set of
\mathcal{L_{\sigma}}
-formulas derivable from the following axioms and rules:

•

All substitution instances of classical tautologies (allowing for countable conjunction and disjunction) and all substitution instances of formulas in
\mathrm{Ax}

(this includes the infinite De Morgan laws);

•

The join rule: for any countable sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\omega}
and formula
\psi
:

\dfrac{\varphi_{n}\to\psi\qquad n\in\omega}{({\textstyle\bigvee}_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n})\to\psi}

•

The modal axiom and rule:

\Diamond({\textstyle\bigvee}_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n})\leftrightarrow{\textstyle\bigvee}_{n\in\omega}\Diamond\varphi_{n}\qquad\dfrac{\neg\varphi}{\neg\Diamond\varphi}

•

Modus ponens:

\dfrac{\varphi\qquad\varphi\to\psi}{\psi}

Modal Loomis-Sikorski logic

\mathsf{MLS}\oplus\mathrm{Ax}
is the set of
\mathcal{L_{\sigma}}
-formulas
derivable from the axioms and rules above together with the

infinite descending chain rule (IDC)
:

\dfrac{\neg{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n}\qquad\varphi_{n+1}\to\varphi_{n}\text{ for all }n\in\omega}{\neg{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{n\in\omega}\Diamond\varphi_{n}}.

(IDC)

A formula
\varphi
is called
derivable
in
\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}
or
\mathsf{MLS}
if it can be obtained from the axioms and rules of the respective logics.
If
\varphi
is derivable in
\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}
or
\mathsf{MLS}
then we write

\vdash_{\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}}\varphi
or
\vdash_{\mathsf{MLS}}\varphi
, respectively.

In either logic, we get
\varphi_{k}\to\bigvee_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n}

as a substitution instance of a classical tautology, as well as the following dual axioms and rules:

({\textstyle\bigwedge}_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n})\to\varphi_{k}\quad(k\in\omega),\qquad\Box({\textstyle\bigwedge}_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n})\leftrightarrow{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{n\in\omega}\Box\varphi_{n},\qquad\dfrac{\psi\to\varphi_{n}\quad n\in\omega}{\psi\to({\textstyle\bigwedge}_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n})},\qquad\dfrac{\varphi}{\Box\varphi}.

Although the use of diamond as a primitive connective provided intuition for some of the proofs,
using boxes as primitives may yield more natural axioms and rules.

Recall that

\sigma

Definition 4.1
.

A
\sigma
-modal algebra

\sigma

\Diamond\bot=\bot\quad\text{and}\quad\bigvee_{n\in\omega}\Diamond b_{n}=\Diamond\bigvee_{n\in\omega}b_{n}

(4)

Remark 4.2

The condition in (

For all sequences

\bigwedge_{\mbox{\scriptsize finite }F\subseteq\omega}\Diamond\bigwedge_{n\in F}b_{n}>\bot\quad\text{implies}\quad\bigwedge_{n\in\omega}b_{n}>\bot

.

Let
(B,\Diamond)

\sigma

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(B,\Diamond)
be a
\sigma
-modal algebra and
I\subseteq B

\Diamond a\equiv_{I}\Diamond b

Proof.

First, we note

\Diamond a\leq\Diamond(a\vee b)=\Diamond((a\vee b)\wedge(\neg b\vee b))=\Diamond((a\wedge\neg b)\vee b)=\Diamond(a\wedge\neg b)\vee\Diamond b.

This implies

Similarly, we can derive

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathrm{Ax}\subseteq\mathcal{L_{\sigma}}

1.

\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}\oplus\mathrm{Ax}

Proof.

Soundness of all these logics follows from the fact that all the axioms and rules are valid

\sigma

For the propositional axioms and rules this follows from the fact

For example, it is easy to check that the Infinite Descending Chain rule (IDC) corresponds to

For completeness, we use a standard Lindenbaum-Tarski construction; this is

Finally, for

, Theorems 1.3.21 and 1.4.5]
. Thus, the Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of
\mathsf{MLS}\oplus\mathrm{Ax}
validates (IDC) and, as above, it also validates
\mathrm{Ax}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

5
Modal measurable spaces

We turn to the semantic structures in which we will interpret
\mathcal{L_{\sigma}}
.
We use measurable spaces with an
additional relation
R
, where propositional connectives are interpreted in
the measurable space as usual and the modal operator
\Diamond
is
interpreted via
R
. On the one hand, such a semantic structure may be viewed as a modal
analogue of a measurable space. On the other hand it is a Kripke frame
with a measurable space structure.

We prove that such structures provide a sound semantics for
\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}
,
and characterise precisely for which such spaces the infinite descending
chain rule (
IDC
) is valid.
In Section
6
we take the key step of this paper
by extending this semantics to incorporate an ideal of
designated null-sets.

Definition 5.1
.

A
modal measurable space
is a tuple
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,\Sigma)

consisting of a measurable space
(X,\Sigma)
and a relation
R
such that
for every
a\in\Sigma
we have

\Diamonddot a:=\{x\in X\mid R[x]\cap a\neq\emptyset\}\in\Sigma
.

A
modal measurable model
is a pair
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)

consisting of a modal measurable space
\mathfrak{F}
and a valuation

V:\Prop\to\Sigma
that assigns to each proposition letter
p

a measurable subset of
X
.
The interpretation of an
\mathcal{L_{\sigma}}
-formula
\varphi
in
\mathfrak{M}
is defined
recursively via

\llbracket p\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}=V(p),\qquad\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}=X\setminus\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}},\qquad\llbracket{\textstyle\bigvee}_{n\in\omega}\varphi_{n}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\llbracket\varphi_{n}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}},\qquad\llbracket\Diamond\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}=\Diamonddot\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}.

A formula
\varphi
is
valid
in a modal measurable space

\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,\Sigma)
if
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}=X
for every modal measurable
model of the form
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)
.
We write
\mathsf{MMS}\Vdash\varphi
if
\varphi
is valid on all modal measurable spaces.

We clearly have:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a measurable Kripke model.
Then
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
is measurable for each formula
\varphi
.

The complex algebra of a modal measurable space is defined as expected:

Definition 5.3
.

The
complex algebra
of a modal measurable space
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,\Sigma)

is
\mathfrak{F}^{+}:=(\Sigma,\Diamonddot)
.

The semantic interpretation of
\varphi
corresponds to the
algebraic interpretation in
\mathfrak{F}^{+}
, viewing an assignment
V
as an assignment
for
\mathfrak{F}^{+}
. The following proposition entails that

\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}
is sound with respect to modal measurable spaces
by showing that the
complex algebra of every modal measurable space is a
\sigma
-modal algebra.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,\Sigma)
be a modal measurable space.
Then
\mathfrak{F}^{+}
is a
\sigma
-modal algebra.

Proof.

We know that
\Sigma
is a
\sigma
-Boolean algebra, so we only
verify the equations from (
3
).
To this end, compute

\Diamonddot\emptyset=\{x\in X\mid R[x]\cap\emptyset\neq\emptyset\}=\emptyset
and

\Diamonddot{\textstyle\bigcup}_{n\in\omega}b_{n}=\{x\in X\mid R[x]\cap({\textstyle\bigcup}_{n\in\omega}b_{n})\neq\emptyset\}=\{x\in X\mid R[x]\cap b_{n}\neq\emptyset\text{ for some }n\in\omega\}={\textstyle\bigcup}_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot b_{n}.

It follows that
\mathfrak{F}^{+}
is indeed a
\sigma
-modal algebra.
∎

The following result characterises the modal measurable spaces which validate
the rule (
IDC
). It is not needed in what follows.

Definition 5.5
.

1.

Let
(X,\Sigma)
be a measurable space.
We call a set
D\subseteq X

measurably compact

if for every countable measurable cover
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{n}\mid n\in\omega\}

of
X
there exists a finite subset
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}

that covers
D
.

2.

A modal measurable space
(X,R,\Sigma)
is called

pointwise measurably compact
if the set
R[x]
is measurably compact
for each
x\in X
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,\Sigma)
be a modal measurable space.
Then
\mathfrak{F}^{+}
is a measurable
\sigma
-modal algebra if and only
if
\mathfrak{F}
is pointwise measurably compact.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{F}
is pointwise measurably compact
and let
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
be a countable sequence of
elements of
\mathfrak{F}^{+}
such that
a_{n}\geq a_{n+1}
for all
n\in\omega
,
and
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}=\emptyset
.
We need to show that
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}=\emptyset
.

By assumption
\{X\setminus a_{n}\mid n\in\omega\}
is a measurable cover
of
X
, so for any
x\in X
we can find a finite number

a_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{n_{k}}
of
a_{n}
’s such that

R[x]\subseteq(X\setminus a_{n_{1}})\cup\cdots\cup(X\setminus a_{n_{k}})
.
It follows that
R[x]\cap a_{n_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap a_{n_{k}}=\emptyset
.
Since the
a_{n}
form a descending chain, we can take

m:=\min\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\}
to get
R[x]\cap a_{m}=\emptyset
.
This proves
x\notin\Diamonddot a_{m}
, hence
x\notin\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}
.
Since
x\in X
was arbitrary, we conclude
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}=\emptyset
, as desired.

For the converse, suppose there exists some countable measurable cover

\mathcal{C}
of
X
and a world
x\in X
such that
R[x]
cannot be
covered by any finite subset of
\mathcal{C}
.
Write
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{n}\mid n\in\omega\}
.
Define
a_{n}:=X\setminus(c_{0}\cup\cdots\cup c_{n-1})
.
Then
a_{n}\supseteq a_{n+1}
for all
n\in\omega
and

\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}=X\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\omega}c_{n}=X\setminus X=\emptyset
, so the premisses of (
IDC
) are valid.
But for every
n\in\omega
we have
R[x]\not\subseteq c_{0}\cup\cdots\cup c_{n-1}
,
so
R[x]\cap a_{n}\neq\emptyset
, hence
x\in\Diamonddot a_{n}
.
Therefore
x\in\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}\neq\emptyset
,
showing that (
IDC
) is not valid on
\mathfrak{F}^{+}
.
∎

Example 5.7
.

Here is an example of a
modal measurable space that is not pointwise measurably compact.
Consider
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,\Sigma)
with

X=\omega
, i.e. the set of natural numbers,

R[x]=\omega
for all
x\in X
and
\Sigma=\mathcal{P}(\omega)
.
Then
\mathfrak{F}
is a modal measurable space, but it is not
pointwise measurably compact.
Indeed,
\mathcal{C}=\{\{n\}\mid n\in\omega\}

is a measurable cover of
X
but for any
x\in X
,
since
R[x]=\omega
, there is no finite subcover of
\mathcal{C}

covering
R[x]
.

Theorem 5.8
.

The logic
\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}
is sound with respect to the class of all
modal measurable spaces.
The logic
\mathsf{MLS}
is sound with respect to the class of
pointwise measurably compact modal measurable spaces.

Proof.

This follows from Lemmas
5.4

and
5.6

and the fact that the algebraic semantics is sound with respect to the
two logics.
∎

We end this section with the following open problem:

Problem 5.9
.

Are
\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}
and
\mathsf{MLS}
complete with respect to suitable classes of modal measurable spaces? If not, can we axiomatise the logics of (pointwise measurably compact) modal measurable spaces?

6
Marked modal measurable spaces

We ended the previous section with the open problem of axiomatising the logic of modal measurable spaces.
In this section we introduce
marked
modal measurable spaces, which can be considered as “point-free analogues” of modal measurable spaces.
Marked modal measurable spaces extend modal measurable spaces with a designated
\sigma
-ideal of (marked) null-sets.
Their use is closely connected to a point-free approach to ergodic theory, see e.g.
[
13
,
20
]
. We introduce a new semantics on these spaces and
prove in Section
8
that
\mathsf{MLS}
is sound and complete with respect to this semantics.

Definition 6.1
.

A
marked modal measurable space
or
mmm-space
is a tuple

(X,R,\Sigma,N)
where

•

(X,R,\Sigma)
is a modal measurable space; and

•

N
is a modal
\sigma
-ideal on
\mathfrak{F}^{+}=(\Sigma,\Diamonddot)

such that for all countable collections of measurable
sets
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
,

\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\in N\quad\text{implies}\quad\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}\in N.

The
complex algebra
of an mmm-model is the
\sigma
-modal algebra

\mathfrak{F}^{\dagger}:=(\Sigma,\Diamonddot)/N
.

It follows from Lemma
5.4
and
Proposition
4.5

that
\mathfrak{F}^{\dagger}
is a
\sigma
-modal algebra.

Example 6.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{O})
be a topological space,
let
\Sigma
be the induced Borel
\sigma
-algebra,
and let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\Sigma
.
Let
f:X\to X
be a
measure-preserving
transformation, as in
dynamical systems and ergodic theory, i.e.
f^{-1}(b)\in\Sigma
and
\mu f^{-1}(b)=\mu(b)
for all
b\in\Sigma
.
Let
R
be the graph of
f
, i.e.
xRy
iff
f(x)=y
,
and let
N
be the colletion of null-sets.
Then
\Diamonddot a=f^{-1}(a)
for any measurable set
a
.
Furthermore, for any sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of measurable sets whose intersection is in
N
,
we have

\bigcap\Diamonddot a_{n}=\bigcap f^{-1}(a_{n})=f^{-1}(\bigcap a_{n})\in N

because
f
is measure-preserving.
Therefore
(X,R,\Sigma,N)
is an mmm-space.

As a concrete example, we may consider
(X,\mathcal{O})
to be the unit interval with the Euclidean topology,
\mu
the Lebesgue measure and
f(x)
the Bernoulli map
f(x)=2x\mathrel{\mathrm{mod}}1
.

Definition 6.3
.

An
mmm-model
is a pair
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)
consisting of an
mmm-space
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,\Sigma,N)
and a valuation
V:\Prop\to\Sigma
.
The
interpretation
of an
\mathcal{L_{\sigma}}
-formula
\varphi
in
\mathfrak{M}

is the equivalence class of
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{}}
in the complex algebra
\mathfrak{F}^{\dagger}
,
which we denote by
\llparenthesis\varphi\rrparenthesis^{\mathfrak{M}}

for the class of mmm-spaces.

A formula
\varphi
is said to be
valid
on an mmm-space
\mathfrak{F}

if
\llparenthesis\varphi\rrparenthesis^{\mathfrak{M}}
is the top element of
\mathfrak{F}^{\dagger}
for every mmm-model

\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)
based on
\mathfrak{F}
.
In other words,
\varphi
is valid on
\mathfrak{F}
if
X\setminus\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}\in N

for every mmm-model
\mathfrak{M}
based on
\mathfrak{F}
.
For a set
\mathrm{Ax}
, we write
\mathtt{MMM}(\mathrm{Ax})
for class of mmm-spaces validating all formulas in
\mathrm{Ax}
.

Theorem 6.4
.

For any
\mathrm{Ax}\subseteq\mathcal{L_{\sigma}}
, the logic
\mathsf{MLS}\oplus\mathrm{Ax}
is sound with
respect to
\mathtt{MMM}(\mathrm{Ax})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,\Sigma,N)
be an mmm-space that validates
\mathrm{Ax}
.
Then its complex algebra
\mathfrak{F}^{\dagger}
is a
\sigma
-modal algebra
by Proposition
4.5
and it validates
\mathrm{Ax}
by
assumption. Moreover, since
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\in N

implies
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}\in N
, the complex
algebra also satisfies (
IDC
).
Therefore
\mathfrak{F}^{\dagger}
is a measurable
\sigma
-modal algebra
that validates
\mathrm{Ax}
. Soundness now follows from the algebraic soundness given in Theorem
4.6
(
2
).
∎

7
Jónsson-Tarski duality for (measurable)
\sigma
-modal algebras

We restrict Jónsson-Tarski duality (see Section
2
) to
a duality for
\sigma
-modal algebras and measurable
\sigma
-modal algebras.
In order to prove completeness of
\mathsf{MLS}
in Section
8
,
we only need this restriction on objects, so we focus on restricting the
object part of Jónsson-Tarski duality.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a Stone space.
A
basic null-set
is a set of the form

\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}
, where
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is a
sequence of clopen sets of
(X,\tau)
such that

\interior(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n})=\emptyset
.
A
Baire null-set
is a subset of
X
that can be covered by
countably many basic null-sets.

Definition 7.2
.

A
\sigma
-modal space
is a modal space
\mathbb{X}=(X,\tau,R)
such that

(X,\tau)
is a
\sigma
-Stone space and such that for any countable sequence

(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of clopen sets,

\Diamonddot\Big(\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}}\Big)=\overline{\Diamonddot\Big(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\Big)}.

(5)

A
measurable
\sigma
-modal space
is a
\sigma
-modal space that additionally
satisfies that for every Baire null-set
S
, the set

\Diamonddot S:=\{x\in X\mid R[x]\cap S\neq\emptyset\}

is a Baire null-set too.

We will show that these properties correspond to the
Jónsson-Tarski duals being (measurable)
\sigma
-modal algebras.
In the next result, we recall that the
\sigma
-Boolean algebra structure in
the dual space
\mathbb{X}
of a Boolean (or modal) algebra
B
is given by (
2
).

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
(B,\Diamond)
be a modal algebra and
\mathbb{X}=(X,\tau,R)
its
dual modal space. Then

1.

(B,\Diamond)
is a
\sigma
-modal algebra if and only if

\mathbb{X}
is a
\sigma
-modal space;

2.

(B,\Diamond)
is a measurable
\sigma
-modal algebra if and only if

\mathbb{X}
is a measurable
\sigma
-modal space.

Proof.

(1)
Suppose
(B,\Diamond)
is a
\sigma
-modal algebra.
Then we know from Theorem
2.2
that
(X,\tau)

is a
\sigma
-Stone space.
Furthermore, for any countable sequence
(b_{n})_{n\in\omega}

of elements in
B
we have

\bigvee_{n\in\omega}\Diamond b_{n}=\Diamond\bigvee_{n\in\omega}b_{n}

so it follows from Jónsson-Tarski duality that for any sequence

(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of elements in
\clp(\mathbb{X})
we have

\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}}=\Diamonddot\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}}

By definition of
\Diamonddot
we have

\Diamonddot\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}
,
which entails the desired equality

\Diamonddot(\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}})=\overline{\Diamonddot(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n})}
.

For the converse, assume that
(X,\tau,R)
satisfies (
5
).
By assumption
(X,\tau)
is a
\sigma
-Stone space and
(B,\Diamond)

is a modal algebra, so we know that
B
is a
\sigma
-Boolean algebra
and that
\Diamond\bot=\bot
. It remains to show that diamonds distribute
over countable joins.
Since
(B,\Diamond)\cong(\clp(\mathbb{X}),\Diamonddot)
is a modal algebra isomorphism and every such isomorphism is automatically a
\sigma
-modal algebra isomorphism, it suffices to show that the
dual Boolean algebra of
(X,\tau,R)
satisfies

\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}}=\Diamonddot\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}}

for every sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of elements in
\clp(\mathbb{X})
.
In light of (
5
), it suffices to show

\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}}=\overline{\Diamonddot\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}}
. But this follows from
the fact that

\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}=\Diamonddot\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}
,
which is an immediate consequence of the definition of
\Diamonddot
.

(2)
Suppose that
(B,\Diamond)
is a measurable
\sigma
-modal algebra.
Then using the isomorphism
(B,\Diamond)\cong(\clp(\mathbb{X}),\Diamonddot)
,
we know that for any countable descending chain

a_{0}\supseteq a_{1}\supseteq a_{2}\supseteq\cdots

of clopen subsets of
\mathbb{X}
,

\interior\Big(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\Big)=\emptyset\quad\text{implies}\quad\interior\Big(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}\Big)=\emptyset.

Let
S
be a Baire null-set.
Then
S\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{n}
for some collection
(S_{n})_{n\in\omega}

of basic null-sets.
Since
\Diamonddot S\subseteq\Diamonddot(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{n})=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot S_{n}

and the countable union of Baire null-sets sets is a Baire null-set (by definition),
it suffices to show that
\Diamonddot S_{n}
is a Baire null-set for any basic null-set
S_{n}
.

So let
S=\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}
be a basic null-set, where
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}

is a sequence of clopen subsets of
\mathbb{X}
such that
\interior(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n})=\emptyset
.
Defining
a^{\prime}_{n}:=a_{0}\cap\cdots\cap a_{n}
yields a countable descending chain
such that
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a^{\prime}_{n}=\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}
,
hence
\interior(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a^{\prime}_{n})=\emptyset
.
This implies

\interior\Big(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a^{\prime}_{n}\Big)=\emptyset
.
If we can prove that

\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a^{\prime}_{n}=\Diamonddot S

(6)

then we are done.
To this end, let
x\in\Diamonddot S
. Then there is some
y
such that
xRy
and
y\in S
.
This implies
y\in a^{\prime}_{n}
, hence
x\in\Diamonddot a^{\prime}_{n}
, for all
n\in\omega
.
Therefore
x\in\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a^{\prime}_{n}
.
For the converse, suppose
x\in\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a^{\prime}_{n}
.
Then using the fact that the
a^{\prime}_{n}
are decreasing, one can show that

\{R[x]\}\cup\{a^{\prime}_{n}\mid n\in\omega\}
has the finite
intersection property.
So by compactness there exists some
y\in R[x]\cap\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a^{\prime}_{n}=R[x]\cap S
,
so
x\in\Diamonddot S
. This proves (
6
).

Conversely, suppose
\Diamonddot S
is a Baire null-set for any Baire null-set
S
.
We show that
(B,\Diamond)
is a measurable
\sigma
-modal algebra by checking (
4
).
Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
be a countable descending chain of clopen sets such
that
\interior(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n})=\emptyset
.
Then
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n}
is a basic null-set by definition.
The assumption now gives that
\Diamonddot(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}a_{n})

is a Baire null-set.
Using (
6
) we then conclude that
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\Diamonddot a_{n}

is a Baire null-set, as desired.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

It follows immediately from the definitions that every Baire null-set
in a Stone space is a meager set, but it is unclear whether the converse
holds. We leave this as an open question.

8
Completeness via a modal Loomis-Sikorski representation

While Theorem
7.3
provides a duality for the algebraic
semantics of both
\mathsf{K_{\sigma}}
and
\mathsf{MLS}
, it does not yield completeness for
either with respect to (marked) modal measurable spaces.
This is because countable meets and joins in (measurable)
\sigma
-modal spaces
need not be given by intersections and unions.
In order to obtain a completeness result for
\mathsf{MLS}
with respect to
mmm-spaces, we prove a modal analogue of the Loomis-Sikorski theorem
which states that every measurable modal algebra can be obtained as the
quotient of a modal measurable space by a modal
\sigma
-ideal.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
(X,R)
be a Kripke frame.
A
modal
\sigma
-field
on
(X,R)
is a collection

\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}X
that contains
X
and is closed under
countable unions, complements and
\Diamonddot

(hence also contains
\emptyset
and is closed under countable intersections).

We note that a modal measurable space (Definition
5.1
) can be viewed as a triple
(X,R,\Sigma)
consisting of a Kripke frame
(X,R)
together with a modal
\sigma
-field
\Sigma
on
(X,R)
.

The arbitrary intersection of modal
\sigma
-fields on a Kripke frame
is again a modal
\sigma
-field. Therefore we can define the modal

\sigma
-field on
(X,R)
generated by some collection
C
of subsets of

X
as the intersection of all modal
\sigma
-fields containing
C
.

Definition 8.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X,\tau,R)
be a
\sigma
-modal space.
Then we write
\Baire(\mathbb{X})
for the modal
\sigma
-field on
(X,R)

generated by the clopen subsets of
(X,\tau)
.
We call sets in
\Baire(\mathbb{X})

modal Baire sets
.

In analogy with the non-modal case (see e.g.
[
10
, Chapter 40]
)
we find that the modal Baire null-sets, i.e. sets of a
Stone space that are both modal Baire sets and Baire null-sets,
form a modal
\sigma
-ideal.
This only works for measurable
\sigma
-modal spaces, because we need
the fact that
\Diamonddot S
is a Baire null-set whenever
S
is a Baire null-set.
As a consequence, the modal analogue of the Loomis-Sikorski theorem
(Theorem
8.5
) only applies to measurable
\sigma
-modal algebras.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X,\tau,R)
be a measurable
\sigma
-modal space.
Then the collection
\mathcal{M}
of modal Baire null-sets of
(X,\tau)
forms a
modal
\sigma
-ideal of
\Baire(\mathbb{X})
.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of a Baire null-set
that
\mathcal{M}
is a
\sigma
-ideal,
and it is closed under
\Diamonddot
because by the definition of a
measurable
\sigma
-modal space,
\Diamonddot S
is a Baire null-set for any Baire null-set
S
.
∎

Lemma 8.4
.

Every modal Baire set of a measurable
\sigma
-modal space
is equivalent to a unique clopen set modulo
the modal
\sigma
-ideal
\mathcal{M}
of modal Baire null-sets.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X,\tau,R)
be a measurable
\sigma
-modal space and

\mathcal{M}
the modal
\sigma
-ideal of modal Baire null-sets.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be the class of subsets of
X
that are equivalent to a
clopen subset modulo
\mathcal{M}
. We claim that
\mathcal{F}
is a modal
\sigma
-field on
(X,R)
that
includes
\Baire(\mathbb{X})
.

To this end, first we note that every clopen subset
a
of
\mathbb{X}

is in
\mathcal{F}
because
a\mathbin{\vartriangle}a=\emptyset\in\mathcal{M}
.
To see that
\mathcal{F}
is closed under complementation, let
S\in\mathcal{F}

and suppose
a
is a clopen set such that
S\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}a
,
i.e.
S\mathbin{\vartriangle}a\in\mathcal{M}
.
Then

(X\setminus S)\mathbin{\vartriangle}(X\setminus a)=S\mathbin{\vartriangle}a\in\mathcal{M}

and since
X\setminus a
is clopen, we get
X\setminus S\in\mathcal{F}
.

Next, let
(S_{n})_{n\in\omega}
be a sequence of sets in
\mathcal{F}
,
and let
(a_{n})_{n\in\omega}
be a sequence of clopen sets such that

S_{n}\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}a_{n}
for each
n\in\omega
.
Then we have

\Big(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{n}\Big)\mathbin{\vartriangle}\Big(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\Big)\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}(S_{n}\mathbin{\vartriangle}a_{n})\in\mathcal{M},

so
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{n}\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}
.
Now let
a:=\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}}
. Then
a
is clopen because
\mathbb{X}
is a
\sigma
-modal space. Moreover, we have

a\mathbin{\vartriangle}\Big(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\Big)=a\setminus\Big(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\Big)=a\cap\bigcap_{n\in\omega}(X\setminus a_{n}),

which is in
\mathcal{M}
because

\interior{\Big(a\cap\bigcap_{n\in\omega}(X\setminus a_{n})\Big)}=\interior{(a)}\cap\interior{\Big(\bigcap_{n\in\omega}X\setminus a_{n}\Big)}=\interior{(a)}\cap X\setminus\Big(\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}}\Big)=\interior{(a)}\cap(X\setminus a)=\emptyset.

So we have
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{n}\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}a
.
Hence
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
.

Finally, if
S\in\mathcal{F}
and
S\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}a
, where
a

is clopen, then Lemma
4.4
entails
\Diamonddot S\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\Diamonddot a
.
Since
\Diamonddot a
is clopen, it follows that
\Diamonddot S\in\mathcal{F}
.
Therefore
\mathcal{F}
is a modal
\sigma
-field on
(X,R)

that contains the clopen subsets, hence
\Baire(\mathbb{X})\subseteq\mathcal{F}
.
This shows that every modal Baire set is equivalent modulo
\mathcal{M}

to a clopen subset.

For uniqueness, suppose that the modal Baire set
S
is equivalent
modulo
\mathcal{M}
to each of the clopen sets
a
and
b
.
Then
a\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}b
, so
a\mathbin{\vartriangle}b\in\mathcal{M}
.
Then
a\mathbin{\vartriangle}b
is open because
a
and
b
are clopen,
and since every Baire null-set is in particular meager
we can use
[
10
, Theorem 28]

(which is a corollary of the Baire Category Theorem)
to conclude
a\mathbin{\vartriangle}b=\emptyset
.
Therefore
a=b
.
∎

We can now prove a modal analogue of the Loomis-Sikorski
theorem
[
10
, Chapter 40, Corollary 1]
.

Theorem 8.5
.

Every measurable
\sigma
-modal algebra is isomorphic to a modal

\sigma
-field modulo a modal
\sigma
-ideal.

Proof.

Let
(B,\Diamond)
be a
\sigma
-modal algebra,

\mathbb{X}=(X,\tau,R)
its dual
\sigma
-modal space,
and
\Baire(\mathbb{X})
the modal
\sigma
-field of modal Baire sets of
\mathbb{X}
.
Let
f
be the function that takes each
S\in\Baire(\mathbb{X})
to the unique
clopen set
a
to which
S
is equivalent.
Then
f
is a surjective function.

We claim that
f
is a
\sigma
-homomorphism. Clearly
f(X)=X
and
f(\emptyset)=\emptyset
.
If
S\in\Baire(\mathbb{X})
and
S\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}a

then we know
(X\setminus S)\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}(X\setminus a)
.
This implies
f(X\setminus S)=X\setminus a=X\setminus f(S)
,
so
f
preserves negations. Similarly, if
(S_{n})_{n\in\omega}

is a countable sequence of modal Baire sets and
f(S_{n})=a_{n}
for
each
n\in\omega
, then we know that

f\Big(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{n}\Big)=\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\omega}a_{n}}=\bigvee_{n\in\omega}a_{n}=\bigvee_{n\in\omega}f(S_{n}).

Finally, we check that
f
commutes with
\Diamonddot
.
Let
\Diamonddot S\in\Baire(\mathbb{X})
, and let
a
be the unique clopen set such that
S\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}a
.
Then
f(S)=a
.
Moreover, by Lemma
8.3
,
we have
f(\Diamonddot S)=\Diamonddot a
as well:
S\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}a
implies that
\Diamonddot S\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\Diamonddot a
, and this just means that
f(\Diamonddot S)=\Diamonddot a
.
So

f(\Diamonddot S)=\Diamonddot a=\Diamonddot f(S)
.
All in all,
f
is a surjective modal
\sigma
-homomorphism. So

(B,\Diamond)\cong\Baire(\mathbb{X})/\mathcal{M}
.
∎

This sets us up to prove completeness of
\mathsf{MLS}\oplus\mathrm{Ax}
with respect to
the class of mmm-spaces validating all axioms in
\mathrm{Ax}
.

Theorem 8.6
.

The logic
\mathsf{MLS}\oplus\mathrm{Ax}
is sound and complete with
respect to the class of mmm-spaces validating
\mathrm{Ax}
.
That is, is for every formula
\varphi
we have

\mathsf{MLS}\oplus\mathrm{Ax}\vdash\varphi\quad\text{iff}\quad\mathtt{MMM}(\mathrm{Ax})\Vdash\varphi.

Proof.

We proved soundness in Theorem
6.4
.
For completeness, suppose
\varphi
is a formula such that

\mathsf{MLS}\oplus\mathrm{Ax}\not\vdash\varphi
. Then by Theorem
4.6

there exists a measurable
\sigma
-modal algebra
(B,\Diamond)
such that

(B,\Diamond)\Vdash\mathrm{Ax}
and
(B,\Diamond)\not\Vdash\varphi
.
By the proof of the Modal Loomis-Sikorski Theorem
8.5

there exists a modal measurable space
(X,R,\Sigma)

and a modal
\sigma
-ideal
\mathcal{M}
that is closed under
\Diamonddot

such that
(\Sigma,\Diamonddot)/\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to
(B,\Diamond)
.
This implies that
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,\Sigma,\mathcal{M})
is an mmm-space
such that
\mathfrak{F}\not\Vdash\varphi
, as desired.
∎

9
Conclusion

In this paper we introduced a new semantics and logic for reasoning about relational structures based on measurable spaces.
Our main method is a modal version of the Loomis–Sikorski theorem, which yields a completeness result for these infinitary logics with respect to the new semantics.

We view this work as a first step in developing a modal logical framework for reasoning about measure-based dynamical systems and (point-free) ergodic theory. This naturally gives rise to many avenues for future research, the first of which was already mentioned in Problem
5.9
above.

Second, as mentioned in the introduction, our framework considers the standard Kripke semantics but based on measurable spaces. A natural next step would be to make the relations probabilistic and, more generally, to incorporate probability explicitly into the framework.
Orthogonal to this, it would be interesting to investigate whether the logic and semantics benefit a coalgebraic perspective.

Finally, the framework used in this paper could be generalised to other algebraic structures arising in classical logic, such as distributive lattices and (modal) Heyting algebras. This leads to the question of whether there are Loomis–Sikorski type theorems for distributive lattices, (modal) Heyting algebras, and what the corresponding logics, semantics, and completeness results would be.
Ultimately, we would like to develop constructive measure theory based on
\sigma
-Heyting algebras, with an appropriate analogue of the Loomis-Sikorski theorem.
