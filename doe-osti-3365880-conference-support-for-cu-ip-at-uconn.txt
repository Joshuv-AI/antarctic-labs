This award supported six students to attend the national Conference for Undergraduate Women and
Gender Minorities in Physics (CU*IP) held at the University of Connecticut on January 24-26, 2025. The
CU*IP conference series provides opportunities for under-represented undergraduate students to
obtain real conference experience, hear from role models in the field of physics, and to network with
their peers. The funds will support travel, lodging, and meal expenses for students otherwise not able to
attend the meeting.

The University of Connecticut Department of Physics was selected through a competitive process by the
American Physical Society (APS) to host a national Conference for Undergraduate Women and Gender
Minorities in Physics (CU*IP) on January 24-26, 2025. The conference series started at the University of
Southern California in 2006 and has been sponsored by the APS since 2014. The aim of the annual
conferences is to help female and gender-minority physics undergraduate students continue and stay in
the field by providing a forum for sharing experiences and receiving advice, mentoring, guidance and
information from leading role models in physics. Details on the conference organization and program,
including confirmed speakers, can be found at the conference website
https://physics.uconn.edu/cuwip/.

$6,000 was used to support student participants with travel and accommodations to attend the CU*IP
2025 Conference to be hosted by the UConn Department of Physics. The American Physical Society gave
a budget of $1,000 per student. This conference supplement covered costs for six of the 104 student’s
attendance at the conference who would not otherwise be able to attend. The costs included
registration fee, travel, lodging, transport between UConn and the housing accommodation, and meals
for the whole event. The travel costs averaged $500 per person (mileage and/or flight, plus shuttles
to/from the airport), lodging (2 nights, $120/night per person), and food expenses of $260 per person.

The conference included visits to laboratories of a variety of research groups, a reception and opening
remarks from the Provost of the University, and the conference chair, and a plenary lecture from a
representative of the APS, followed by a panel discussion, concluding with social activities. The second
included a plenary lecture, followed by parallel sessions on topics such as applying to graduate schools,
exploring different career paths, balancing work and personal lives, and dealing with harassment and
discrimination.

The final day included an informal networking session, with other lectures and parallel sessions covering
topics including research involvement, confronting imposter syndrome, depression and anxiety, and
investigating career paths in industry.
