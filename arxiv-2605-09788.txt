Symplectic log Kodaira dimension − ∞ -\infty , Hirzebruch–Jung strings and weighted projective planes
Tian-Jun Li, Shengzhen Ning

Abstract
We study symplectic minimal resolutions of weighted projective planes ℂ ​ ℙ ​ ( a , b , c ) \mathbb{CP}(a,b,c) from the perspective of disconnected symplectic divisors with symplectic log Kodaira dimension − ∞ -\infty . Building on the techniques developed in our previous work [ 30 ] for connected divisors, we introduce the notion of exceptional gaps between distinct connected components of the divisor and use it to establish a Torelli-type theorem for certain configurations of three Hirzebruch–Jung strings. Motivated by Daigle–Russell’s study of affine rulings on complete normal rational surfaces in algebraic context ( [ 7 , 8 ] ), we also establish a weighted version of Gromov–McDuff’s characterization of symplectic ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} by showing the existence of symplectic affine rulings implies certain divisor configuration to arise from the minimal resolution of ℂ ​ ℙ ​ ( a , b , c ) \mathbb{CP}(a,b,c) .

Fix pairwise coprime positive integers

The (complex) weighted projective plane is the projective orbifold

Remark 1.1
.

Throughout the paper, we assume that the weights
(a,b,c)
are pairwise coprime, so that the complement of three coordinate points is a smooth symplectic manifold. If
\gcd(a,b,c)=d>1
, then one may simply replace
(a,b,c)
by
(\frac{a}{d},\frac{b}{d},\frac{c}{d})
. When
\gcd(a,b,c)=1
but the weights are not pairwise coprime, then
\mathbb{CP}(a,b,c)
is still well defined as an orbifold, but its orbifold locus is no longer isolated: along each coordinate line
\{z_{i}=0\}
, the generic stabilizer has order equal to the gcd of the other two weights. In this case, the
2

[
10

a^{\prime}:=\frac{a}{\gcd(a,b)\gcd(a,c)},\quad b^{\prime}:=\frac{b}{\gcd(b,a)\gcd(b,c)},\quad c^{\prime}:=\frac{c}{\gcd(c,a)\gcd(c,b)},

then

is pairwise coprime, and
\mathbb{CP}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}(a,b,c)
as projective varieties, though not as orbifolds, induced by the branched covering map

\mathbb{C}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{3}\setminus\{0\},\qquad(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(z_{0}^{\gcd(b,c)},z_{1}^{\gcd(a,c)},z_{2}^{\gcd(a,b)}).

In particular, even without the pairwise coprime assumption, the complement of the three coordinate points in
\mathbb{CP}(a,b,c)
always carries a canonical complex structure.

Given

1.3
Main Results

In this paper, we focus on symplectic divisors that are disjoint unions of three strings.

Definition 1.3
.

A
symplectic string
is a connected symplectic divisor

\mathcal{S}

whose irreducible components are symplectic spheres arranged in a linear chain. An

\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}

[S_{i}]^{2}\leq-2\text{ for all }i.

Given a string

and

, and write

for its homology class. More generally, we will also use

to denote the number of components and total homology class for any divisor
D
. Once an orientation
\mathfrak{o}

r(\mathcal{S},\mathfrak{o}):=[b_{1},\dots,b_{k}]=b_{1}-\frac{1}{b_{2}-\frac{1}{\ddots-\frac{1}{b_{k}}}}>1,

It turns out that these symplectic divisors satisfy a Torelli-type statement: the associated pairs are determined up to symplectomorphism by their (co)homological data. The following can be viewed as a generalization of Proposition

Corollary 1.7

(Torelli Theorem)
.

Let
D=\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}\cup\mathcal{S}_{3}\subseteq(X,\omega)
and
D^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{2}\cup\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{3}\subseteq(X^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
be symplectic divisors, each consisting of three disjoint HJ strings that are both full and of
(a,b,c)
-type. Suppose that
[\omega^{\prime}]\cdot K_{\omega^{\prime}}<0
and
D
is sub-toric. Then

•

if there is an integral isometry
\gamma
between the second (co)homologies of
X

X^{\prime}
that sends
[\omega]
to
[\omega^{\prime}]
and
[\mathcal{S}_{i}]
to
[\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}]
for each

D^{\prime}
is also sub-toric;

•

if, in addition,
\gamma
preserves the class of each component of
D
and
D^{\prime}
, then
(X^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is symplectomorphic to
(X,\omega,D)
.

This corollary will be deduced from the Torelli theorem (Theorem
2.6

Other than imposing the area conditions on
D

contains a Zariski-open dense subset isomorphic to the product of a curve and the affine line. On the symplectic side, we defined and verified the symplectic version of affine-ruledness in our previous work

for divisor complement of any connected symplectic divisor with

Remark 1.9

From the symplectic point of view, the theorem above may also be regarded as a “weighted” version of the classical Gromov–McDuff’s theorem characterizing the symplectic projective plane by the existence of an embedded symplectic sphere of self-intersection

2.1
Degeneration of fiber class

Given a closed symplectic
4
-manifold
(X,\omega)
, if
C
is an embedded symplectic sphere of self-intersection
0
, we will call
[C]
a
fiber class
. When such a fiber class exists, McDuff’s theorem (
[
43
]
) implies that
(X,\omega)
must be either rational or ruled which in particular implies
b_{2}^{+}(X)=1
. We will need the following elementary observation about the complement of fiber classes later.

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume
[C]
is a fiber class of
(X,\omega)
represented by an embedded symplectic sphere
C
. Then there cannot exist a negative definite divisor configuration with
b_{2}^{-}(X)
components in the complement
X\setminus C
.

Proof.

Otherwise, the homology classes of the divisor components span a negative definite sublattice in
\langle[C]\rangle^{\perp}\subseteq H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. Because
b_{2}^{+}(X)=1
,
\langle[C]\rangle^{\perp}
has rank
b_{2}^{-}(X)
. Since
[C]\in\langle[C]\rangle^{\perp}
,
\langle[C]\rangle^{\perp}
cannot contain a negative definite sublattice of rank
b_{2}^{-}(X)
.
∎

For any
\omega
-tame almost complex structure
J
, a
J
-holomorphic subvariety
\Theta
, in the sense of Taubes (
[
52
]
), is a finite collection
\{(C_{i},m_{i})\}
where each
m_{i}
is a positive integer and
C_{i}
is a closed subset of
X
such

•

its
2
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is finite and non-zero;

•

it has no isolated points;

•

away from finitely many singular points,
C_{i}
is a connected smooth submanifold with
J
-invariant tangent space.

Each
C_{i}
is called an irreducible component of
\Theta
, and the homology class of
\Theta
is defined to be
\sum m_{i}[C_{i}]
. For a fiber class
[C]
, the Seiberg–Witten invariant is nontrivial, with index

I_{SW}([C])=[C]^{2}-K_{\omega}\cdot[C]=2[C]^{2}+2=2.

It follows that, for any point
x\in X
, there exists a
J
-holomorphic subvariety in class
[C]
passing through
x
. Now, we investigate the possibly reducible
J
-holomorphic representatives of a fiber class when
[C]
is
J
-nef. Related results, formulated in terms of “broken rulings”, also appear in
[
3
, Section 4]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose
F
is a fiber class of
(X,\omega)
and
J
is an
\omega
-tame almost complex structure such that
F
has an embedded
J
-holomorphic representative. Let
\Theta=\{(C_{i},m_{i})\}
be a reducible
J
-holomorphic subvariety in class
F
. Then we have the following.

1.

The configuration
\Gamma=\cup C_{i}
is a conntected tree of embedded spheres.

2.

The intersection matrix of any proper subtree
\Gamma^{\prime}\subsetneq\Gamma
is negative definite.

3.

In the configuration
\Gamma
, there must be either at least two
(-1)
-spheres, or one
(-1)
-sphere with multiplicity
m_{i}\geq 2
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from
[
33
, Theorem 1.5]
since the assumption guarantees the
J
-nefness of the fiber class
F
.

To prove the second statement, suppose there was a nonzero class

A=\sum_{C_{i}\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}}a_{i}[C_{i}],

supported on a proper subtree
\Gamma^{\prime}
, such that
A^{2}\geq 0
, where all
a_{i}\neq 0
. Since
F
admits an embedded representative, we have
F\cdot[C_{i}]=0
for all
i
, and hence
F\cdot A=0
. As
b_{2}^{+}(X)=1
, the light cone lemma (
[
44
, Lemma 3.7]
) implies that
A
is proportional to
F
.

Now suppose
\Gamma^{\prime}
has
m
connected components, and write

A=A_{1}+\cdots+A_{m},

where each
A_{j}
is supported on a single connected component of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since the components are disjoint, we have

A^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}^{2}=0.

Moreover, each
A_{j}
satisfies
F\cdot A_{j}=0
so that again by light cone lemma each
A_{j}
is proportional to
F
, and hence
A_{j}^{2}=0
for all
j
.

Now let
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
be any connected component of
\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}
, and define

B:=\sum_{C_{i}\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}[C_{i}].

Since
\Gamma
is connected and contains no loops by the first statement, there exists some component of
\Gamma^{\prime}
that meets
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
in exactly one point. In particular, there is some
A_{j}
whose support intersects
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
at a unique component
C_{\ell}\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}
. It follows that

B\cdot A_{j}=a_{\ell}\neq 0.

On the other hand, since
A_{j}
is proportional to
F
and
F\cdot B=0
, we must have

B\cdot A_{j}=0,

which is a contradiction. This shows that no such class
A
can exist, and hence every subtree
\Gamma^{\prime}
must be negative definite.

For the third statement, applying the adjunction formula to the fiber class
F=\sum m_{i}[C_{i}]
, we obtain

-2=K_{\omega}\cdot F=\sum m_{i}(-2-[C_{i}]^{2}).

We first claim that
F
is primitive. Indeed, if
F=2F^{\prime}
, then

(F^{\prime})^{2}+K_{\omega}\cdot F^{\prime}=-1,

which contradicts the Wu formula (
[
20
, Proposition 1.4.18]
), since
K_{\omega}
is an integral lifting of
w_{2}
.

Next, we observe that no component
C_{i}
can satisfy
[C_{i}]^{2}\geq 0
. Otherwise, since
F\cdot[C_{i}]=0
, the light cone lemma would force
[C_{i}]
to be proportional to
F
, contradicting the fact that
F
is primitive and supported on multiple components.

Therefore, all components satisfy
[C_{i}]^{2}\leq-1
. Returning to the identity

-2=\sum m_{i}(-2-[C_{i}]^{2}),

we note that each term
-2-[C_{i}]^{2}\geq-1
, with equality if and only if
[C_{i}]^{2}=-1
. It follows that in order for the sum to equal
-2
, there must be either at least two components with
[C_{i}]^{2}=-1
, or one component with
[C_{i}]^{2}=-1
appearing with multiplicity
m_{i}\geq 2
.
∎

2.2
Building fiber classes from non-negative definite strings

In this section, we explain how to extract a fiber class from a non-negative definite symplectic string, possibly after a sequence of toric blowups. Given an oriented string
\mathcal{S}=\cup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}\subseteq(X,\omega)
with negative self-intersection sequence
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
, one can consider its
negative intersection matrix

M(\mathcal{S}):=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&-1&0&\cdots&0\\
-1&b_{2}&-1&\cdots&0\\
0&-1&b_{3}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&-1\\
0&0&0&-1&b_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, let
M_{k}(\mathcal{S})
denote the
k\times k
leading principal submatrix of
M(\mathcal{S})
, and define integers

\Delta_{1}:=1,\quad\Delta_{l}:=\det M_{l-1}(\mathcal{S}),\quad 2\leq l\leq n+1,

which will satisfy the recurrence relation

\Delta_{l}=b_{l-1}\Delta_{l-1}-\Delta_{l-2},\quad 3\leq l\leq n+1.

One can see that
\gcd(\Delta_{l},\Delta_{l+1})=1
and the continued fraction
[b_{l},b_{l-1},\cdots,b_{1}]=\frac{\Delta_{l+1}}{\Delta_{l}}
by induction. Moreover, one checks directly that a toric blowdown or half-toric blowdown leaves the determinant
\det M(\mathcal{S})
of the negative intersection matrix of the entire string unchanged. If
\mathcal{S}
is in addition assumed to be Hirzebruch–Jung, it then follows from the above recurrence relation that
0<\Delta_{1}<\Delta_{2}<\cdots<\Delta_{n+1}
since all
b_{i}\geq 2
. In particular, this implies that
M(\mathcal{S})
is positive definite for HJ string
\mathcal{S}
by Sylvester’s criterion. Equivalently, the intersection matrix
-M(\mathcal{S})
(and by abuse of notation, the string
\mathcal{S}
itself) is negative definite. Conversely, if
\mathcal{S}
is known to be not negative definite, then there must be some
\Delta_{l}\leq 0
. The following facts will also be needed later.

Lemma 2.9
.

If
b_{1}=1
and
b_{i}\geq 2
for any
2\leq i\leq n
, then
\mathcal{S}
is negative definite.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be the string obtained by performing a half-toric blowdown along the
S_{1}
-sphere. Then
\mathcal{S}
is negative definite if and only if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is. The claim therefore follows by induction.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Assume that for some
1\leq k\leq n
, we have
b_{k}=1
and
b_{i}\neq 1
for
i\neq k
. Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be the string obtained from
\mathcal{S}
by successive toric or half-toric blowdowns, with negative self-intersection sequence
(b_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,b_{m}^{\prime})
where
m<n
. Then at most two of the
b_{i}^{\prime}
are equal to
1
, and if there are two, they must be adjacent.

Proof.

After blowing down the sphere
S_{k}
, the resulting string has negative self-intersection sequence
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{k-1}-1,b_{k+1}-1,\cdots,b_{n})
, so the only entries that can become
1
are
b_{k-1}-1
and
b_{k+1}-1
. This proves the claim when
m=n-1
. If we continue by blowing down either the
(b_{k-1}-1)
-component or the
(b_{k+1}-1)
-component, the resulting string still has at most one exceptional component, or else two adjacent exceptional components. The general case
m<n
therefore follows by induction.
∎

One can further define the homology class

F_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta_{i}[S_{i}]

and easily check that it enjoys the intersection property

F_{k}^{2}=-\Delta_{k}\Delta_{k+1},\qquad K_{\omega}\cdot F_{k}=-\Delta_{k}+\Delta_{k+1}-1,\qquad F_{k}\cdot[S_{i}]=\begin{cases}-\Delta_{k+1},\qquad&i=k\\
\Delta_{k},\qquad&i=k+1\\
0,\qquad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Assume now that
\Delta_{k}>0
and
\Delta_{k+1}\leq 0
; then we obtain a pair of coprime non-negative integers

(p,q):=(-\Delta_{k+1},\Delta_{k}).
To the pair
(p,q)
, one can associate a
weight sequence

\mathcal{W}(p,q)=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{L})
of positive integers, defined as follows. If
(p,q)=(0,1)
, set
\mathcal{W}(p,q)=\varnothing
. Otherwise, set
(p_{1},q_{1}):=(p,q)
, and whenever
(p_{i},q_{i})
is defined with
p_{i}\neq q_{i}
, define

(p_{i+1},q_{i+1}):=(|p_{i}-q_{i}|,\min\{p_{i},q_{i}\})
. Since
p
and
q
are coprime, there exists
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
(p_{L},q_{L})=(1,1)
. For each
i
, define

m_{i}:=\min\{p_{i},q_{i}\}
. If we take a rectangular box of size
p\times q
, the weight sequence corresponds to a decomposition of this rectangle into squares of side lengths
m_{1},\dots,m_{L}
(see
[
42
, Section 4.1]
). By comparing the area and half-perimeter contributions of these squares (the last square of side length
m_{L}=1
contributes twice to the half-perimeter), we have

pq=\sum_{i=1}^{L}m_{i}^{2},\qquad p+q=\sum_{i=1}^{L}m_{i}+1.

There is also a pattern of successive toric blowups associated to the pair
(p,q)\neq(0,1)
. First perform toric blowup at the node
S_{k}\cap S_{k+1}
and take the total transform
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{S}
. The new string
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
can be denoted by
S_{1}^{\prime}\cup\cdots\cup S_{k}^{\prime}\cup C_{1}\cup S_{k+1}^{\prime}\cup\cdots\cup S_{n}^{\prime}
, where
S_{i}^{\prime}
is the proper transform of
S_{i}
and
C_{1}
is the exceptional component. Next, depending on whether
p_{1}>q_{1}
or
p_{1}<q_{1}
, we perform toric blowup at the node
S_{k}^{\prime}\cap C_{1}
or
C_{1}\cap S_{k+1}^{\prime}
to obtain the string
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
containing a new exceptional component
C_{2}
. Depending on whether
p_{2}>q_{2}
or
p_{2}<q_{2}
, one can further choose the ‘left’ or ‘right’ node to perform toric blowup. Repeat this procedure until we reach at
(p_{L},q_{L})=(1,1)
after
L
blowups. The result will be a symplectic string
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq(X\#L\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2},\tilde{\omega})
satisfying
l(\tilde{\mathcal{S}})=l(\mathcal{S})+L
. Denote by
\tilde{C}_{i}
the component in
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}
that comes from the proper transform of the exceptional sphere
C_{i}
created by the
i
-th blowup. Let
e_{i}
be the exceptional class of the
i
-th blowup. Then we see that except for
\tilde{C}_{L}=C_{L}
, all other
\tilde{C}_{i}
components must have class
e_{i}-\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{i}}e_{j}
for some non-empty index set
\Lambda_{i}\subseteq\{i+1,\cdots,L\}
. In particular,
[\tilde{C}_{i}]^{2}\leq-2
for all
i\leq L-1
.

Motivated by the process of resolving a
(p,q)
-cusp curve singularity, one can introduce the

2.3
Symplectic log Kodaira dimension
-\infty

In this section, we review several key notions and results from
[
30
]
concerning connected divisors
D
in a symplectic rational manifold
(X,\omega)
satisfying
[\omega]\cdot(K_{\omega}+[D])<0
. Recall first that if
(X,\omega)
is non-minimal, then Gromov’s compactness theorem implies that

\inf\{\omega(E)\mid E\in\mathcal{E}_{\omega}\}>0,

and this infimum is attained by classes in a nonempty subset
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{min}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\omega}
. To analyze connected divisors with symplectic log Kodaira dimension
-\infty
,
[
30
]
introduced the following notion, which helps reduce the complexity of the divisor configuration.

Definition 2.13
.

A connected pair
(X,\omega,D)
is said to be
quasi-minimal
if
b_{2}(X)\geq 3
and there exists some
E_{\text{min}}\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{min}}
so that
[D]\cdot E_{\text{min}}\geq 2
.

Proposition 2.14

(
[
30
]
)
.

Any pair
(X,\omega,D)
where
D
is connected and
[\omega]\cdot(K_{\omega}+[D])<0
can be reduced to a pair
(\check{X},\check{\omega},\check{D})
, which is either quasi-minimal or satisfies
b_{2}(\check{X})\leq 2
, by a sequence of toric/non-toric/half-toric/exterior blowdowns.

The following structural result for quasi-minimal pairs was proved in
[
30
, Section 5]
using the curve cone theorem for almost complex
4
-manifolds developed by Zhang in
[
53
]
.

Proposition 2.15

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let
(X,\omega,D)
be a connected pair with
[\omega]\cdot(K_{\omega}+[D])<0
where
X
is a rational manifold. If it is quasi-minimal with
[D]\cdot E_{\text{min}}\geq 2
for some
E_{\text{min}}\in\mathcal{E}_{\text{min}}
, then
E_{\text{min}}=-[D]-K_{\omega}
and
E_{\text{min}}\cdot[D]=2
.

A useful fact, first appearing in
[
48
, Lemma 1.2]
, states that when
b_{2}(X)\geq 3
, the class
E_{\text{min}}
always admits an embedded
J
-holomorphic representative
C_{E_{\text{min}}}
for
any

\omega
-tame almost complex structure
J
. Therefore, for a quasi-minimal pair
(X,\omega,D)
with
[\omega]\cdot(K_{\omega}+[D])<0
, after choosing a divisor-adapted almost complex structure
J\in\mathcal{J}(D)
, one of the following two cases occurs.

1.

C_{E_{\text{min}}}
is not a component of
D
:
(X,\omega,D)
is called
quasi-minimal of first kind
.

2.

C_{E_{\text{min}}}
is a component of
D
:
(X,\omega,D)
is called
quasi-minimal of second kind
.

Based on this dichotomy,
[
30
, Section 5]
further established the following refined structural results for the two kinds.

Proposition 2.16

(
[
30
]
)
.

For connected pair
(X,\omega,D)
with
[\omega]\cdot(K_{\omega}+[D])<0
,

•

if it is quasi-minimal of first kind, then
D
must be a string and
D\cup C_{E_{\text{min}}}
will be a cycle of spheres (i.e. log Calabi-Yau divisor);

•

if it is quasi-minimal of second kind and
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
, then it can be reduced to a pair with
b_{2}=2
by successive toric/non-toric/half-toric/exterior blowdowns.

Remark 2.17
.

In
[
30
]
, the notion
‘partially minimal’
was introduced to further reduce the complexity for a quasi-minimal pair. The partially minimal condition forbids the existence of non-toric or toric exceptional spheres. It was shown that quasi-minimal pairs of second kind with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
can never be partially minimal.

3
Symplectic minimal resolutions of weighted projective planes

Our aim in this section is to prove the ‘only if’ part of Theorem
1.6
. We start by reviewing some standard facts about resolution of cyclic quotient singularity and refer to Evans’ book
[
16
, Section 4.5]
and his blog
[
15
]
for an introduction from the symplectic toric perspective.

Let
(r,p,q)
be positive integers with
1\leq p,q\leq r-1
. Recall that a cyclic quotient singularity
Y
is said to be of type

\frac{1}{r}(p,q)

if it is locally isomorphic to the standard model

\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma_{r,p,q},

where
\Gamma_{r,p,q}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{r}
is generated by

(u,v)\longmapsto(\zeta^{p}u,\zeta^{q}v),\qquad\zeta=e^{2\pi i/r},\quad\gcd(r,p)=\gcd(r,q)=1.

Two parameters
q,q^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,r-1\}
determine the same isomorphism type
\frac{1}{r}(p,q)\cong\frac{1}{r}(1,q^{\prime})
if and only if

qq^{\prime}\equiv p\pmod{r}.

Indeed, if
\Gamma_{r,p,q}=\langle g\rangle
with

g(u,v)=(\zeta^{p}u,\zeta^{q}v),

then the coordinate swap

\sigma(u,v)=(v,u)

conjugates this action to

\sigma g\sigma^{-1}(u,v)=(\zeta^{q}u,\zeta^{p}v).

Setting
\eta:=\zeta^{q}
(still a primitive
r
-th root) and using
qq^{\prime}\equiv p\pmod{r}
, we get

\zeta=\eta^{q^{\prime}},

so the conjugated action is generated by

(u,v)\mapsto(\eta u,\eta^{q^{\prime}}v),

which is exactly the local model of
\frac{1}{r}(1,q^{\prime})
.

Assume
p=1
. Let

\frac{r}{q}=[b_{1},\dots,b_{k}]

be the negative continued fraction expansion with
b_{i}\geq 2
. Then the
\frac{1}{r}(1,q)
-type singularity
Y
admits a minimal resolution
\tilde{Y}\rightarrow Y
whose exceptional locus consists of a Hirzebruch–Jung string

\mathcal{S}=S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{k},

with

[S_{i}]^{2}=-b_{i},\qquad[S_{i}]\cdot[S_{i+1}]=1,

and no other intersections. For the singularity type
\frac{1}{r}(1,q^{\prime})
with
qq^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{r}
, the minimal resolution is the same Hirzebruch–Jung string with the orientation reversed by the standard duality phenomenon of continued fractions.

We equip
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with the standard symplectic form and the Poisson-commuting Hamiltonians

H_{1}(u,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|u|^{2},\qquad H_{2}(u,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}.

Since both are
\Gamma_{r,1,q}
-invariant, they descend to the quotient orbifold
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma_{r,1,q}
(still denoted by
H_{1},H_{2}
). To obtain an effective Hamiltonian
T^{2}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma_{r,1,q}
, we choose the moment map

\mu:\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma_{r,1,q}\to\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad\mu(u,v)=\bigl(H_{2},\tfrac{1}{r}(H_{1}+qH_{2})\bigr).

Its image is the non-Delzant corner

\mathcal{O}_{r,q}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid x\geq 0,\ y\geq\tfrac{q}{r}x\}.

In this toric picture, the HJ string
\mathcal{S}
arises from the corner-chopping procedure below, which resolves the non-Delzant corner
\mathcal{O}_{r,q}
into a Delzant one.

•

First truncate the corner
\mathcal{O}_{r,q}
by a horizontal line
y=\varepsilon
, producing two new vertices

V_{1}=(0,\varepsilon),V_{1}^{\prime}=(\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon,\varepsilon).

The vertex
V_{1}
is Delzant. If
V_{1}^{\prime}
is also Delzant, then the process stops.

•

Otherwise, apply an
\text{AGL}(2;\mathbb{Z})
-transformation sending a neighborhood of
V_{1}^{\prime}
to the corner
\mathcal{O}_{q,b_{1}q-r}
. Truncate again by a horizontal line to obtain vertices
V_{2}
and
V_{2}^{\prime}
, where
V_{2}
is Delzant. If
V_{2}^{\prime}
is Delzant, stop; otherwise, repeat the same procedure at
V_{2}^{\prime}
.

•

After finitely many steps, we obtain vertices
V_{k}
and
V_{k}^{\prime}
that are both Delzant. The original non-Delzant corner is then replaced by an open Delzant polygon with
k
compact edges, corresponding to the spheres
S_{1},\cdots,S_{k}
.

Now, let
(a,b,c)
be pairwise coprime positive integers such that
a<b<c
. Consider the symplectic orbifold
\mathbb{CP}(a,b,c)
which has three isolated cyclic quotient singularities of types

\frac{1}{a}(b,c)\cong\frac{1}{a}(1,a_{b})\cong\frac{1}{a}(1,a_{c}),\quad\frac{1}{b}(a,c)\cong\frac{1}{b}(1,b_{a})\cong\frac{1}{b}(1,b_{c}),\quad\frac{1}{c}(a,b)\cong\frac{1}{c}(1,c_{b})\cong\frac{1}{c}(1,c_{a}),

where

\displaystyle 9-n

\displaystyle=-\big(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k_{a}}a_{\alpha}+\sum_{\beta=1}^{k_{b}}b_{\beta}+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{k_{c}}c_{\gamma}\big)+[N_{a}]^{2}+[N_{b}]^{2}+[N_{c}]^{2}+2(n+3)

\displaystyle\geq-\big(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k_{a}}a_{\alpha}+\sum_{\beta=1}^{k_{b}}b_{\beta}+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{k_{c}}c_{\gamma}\big)-3+2(n+3).

Now, we can prove one direction in our main Theorem

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

be a pair coming from the symplectic minimal resolution of

for pairwise coprime

\varnothing,\text{if }[N_{c}]^{2}\geq 0.\end{cases}

In particular,

is full, of
(a,b,c)
-type and gap-admissible.

Proof.

On the one hand, Lemma
3.3
immediately gives

On the other hand, for any

,
the adjunction formula implies

-1=-2-E^{2}=E\cdot K_{\omega}=-E\cdot([\mathcal{S}_{a}]+[\mathcal{S}_{b}]+[\mathcal{S}_{c}]+[N_{a}]+[N_{b}]+[N_{c}]).

By

When

has nontrivial SW invariant.
By definition, if

Proof of ‘only if’ part of Theorem

If a string only contains

(-2)

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
a,b,c>1
be pairwise coprime integers where
a>b
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{a},\mathcal{S}_{b},\mathcal{S}_{c}
be three HJ strings coming from the minimal resolution of
\mathbb{CP}(a,b,c)
at
a,b,c
-point respectively. Then
\mathcal{S}_{a},\mathcal{S}_{b}
are
(-2)
-strings if and only if
c=kab-a-b
for some integer
k\geq 1
.
Moreover, in this situation, the third string
\mathcal{S}_{c}
has self-intersection sequence

•

(-a,-k,-b)
if
k\geq 2
;

•

(1-a,1-b)
if
k=1
and
b\neq 2
;

•

(2-a)
if
k=1
and
b=2
.

Proof.

The cyclic quotient singularity at the
a
-point has type

\frac{1}{a}(b,c)\cong\frac{1}{a}(1,q),\qquad q\equiv b^{-1}c\pmod{a}.

Hence,
\mathcal{S}_{a}
is a
(-2)
-string if and only if
q\equiv-1\pmod{a}
, i.e.,
c\equiv-b\pmod{a}
.
Similarly,
\mathcal{S}_{b}
is a
(-2)
-string if and only if

c\equiv-a\pmod{b}
.
Since
\gcd(a,b)=1
, Chinese remainder theorem implies that these two congruences are equivalent to

c\equiv-(a+b)\pmod{ab},

and

c=kab-a-b

for some integer
k\geq 1
. This proves the equivalence.

Assume now
c=kab-a-b
. Then
a(kb-1)\equiv b\pmod{c},

since

a(kb-1)=akb-a=b+(kab-a-b)=b+c.

So, the cyclic quotient singularity at the
c
-point has type

\frac{1}{c}(a,b)\cong\frac{1}{c}(1,kb-1).

A direct computation gives

[a,k,b]=a-\frac{1}{k-\frac{1}{b}}=\frac{kab-a-b}{kb-1}=\frac{c}{kb-1},

so the third string
\mathcal{S}_{c}
has self-intersection sequence
(-a,-k,-b)
when
k\geq 2
. If
k=1
and
b\neq 2
, in order for the items in the continued fraction to be at least
2
, one should instead write

[a-1,b-1]=(a-1)-\frac{1}{b-1}=\frac{ab-a-b}{b-1}=\frac{c}{b-1}.

Hence,
\mathcal{S}_{c}
has self-intersection sequence
(1-a,1-b)
in this case. Similarly, when
k=1
and
b=2
, the self-intersection sequence should be further reduced to
(2-a)
.
∎

Lemma 4.1

For

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that the disjoint union of three HJ strings
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{3}

E_{\min}\cdot\bigl([\mathcal{S}_{1}]+[\mathcal{S}_{2}]+[\mathcal{S}_{3}]\bigr)=2,

Proof.

Choose any divisor-adapted almost complex structure

E_{\text{min}}
has an embedded

(-2)

3.6

(X^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},D^{\prime})

and

, or exterior, contradicting the full condition. If instead

, then it is toric or half-toric, so we can blow it down and obtain a new pair

still holds after this blowdown. Repeating the same argument, we conclude that either one connected component becomes quasi-minimal at some stage, or we can continue performing toric or half-toric blowdowns until

With the claim understood, we may now assume

with

Claim 4.6
.

Given any

-holomorphic representative

.

Proof.

Otherwise, by Lemma
2.5
, the class

would be an exceptional class in either
\mathcal{E}_{\omega}(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{3};D)
or
\mathcal{E}_{\omega}(\mathcal{S}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{3};D)
. By Proposition
2.15
,

[\omega^{\prime\prime}]\cdot(K_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}+[D^{\prime\prime}])=[\omega^{\prime\prime}]\cdot(K_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}+[D^{\prime\prime}]+e^{\prime\prime}-e^{\prime\prime})=[\omega^{\prime\prime}]\cdot[D_{2}^{\prime\prime}]-[\omega^{\prime\prime}]\cdot e^{\prime\prime}>-[\omega^{\prime\prime}]\cdot e^{\prime\prime}.

However, by Lemma

, we then have

\displaystyle[\omega]\cdot(K_{\omega}+[D])

\displaystyle=[\omega^{\prime}]\cdot(K_{\omega^{\prime}}+[D^{\prime}])-[\omega]\cdot E_{\text{min}}

\displaystyle\geq[\omega^{\prime\prime}]\cdot(K_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}+[D^{\prime\prime}])-[\omega]\cdot E_{\text{min}}

\displaystyle>-[\omega^{\prime\prime}]\cdot e^{\prime\prime}-[\omega]\cdot E_{\text{min}}=-[\omega]\cdot e^{\prime\prime}-[\omega]\cdot E_{\text{min}},

Hence, by adjunction formula, components in

can only be
(-2)
-spheres since

. To see the contradiction, let us introduce the quantity

-K_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}=e^{\prime\prime}+[D_{1}^{\prime\prime}]

K_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}^{2}=(e^{\prime\prime}+[D_{1}^{\prime\prime}])^{2}=-1+[D_{1}^{\prime\prime}]^{2}+2e^{\prime\prime}\cdot[D_{1}^{\prime\prime}]=3+[D_{1}^{\prime\prime}]^{2}=3+\sum_{i=1}^{l(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})}[C_{i}^{\prime\prime}]^{2}+2l(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})-2,

where

denotes the component in

and

denotes the number of components. Note that the last equality holds true since the configuration of

\Xi(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})=-3l(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})-\sum_{i=1}^{l(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})}[C_{i}^{\prime\prime}]^{2}=-3l(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})-\big(K_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}^{2}-2l(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})-1\big)=-l(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})-K_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}^{2}+1.

For

(-2)

Observe that

since only toric or half-toric blowdowns were performed. Therefore, we compute that

\displaystyle\Xi(D^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\Xi(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})+\Xi(D_{2}^{\prime\prime})=-l(D_{1}^{\prime\prime})-l(D_{2}^{\prime\prime})-K_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}^{2}+1

\displaystyle=-b_{2}^{-}(X^{\prime\prime})-K_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}^{2}=-b_{2}^{-}(X^{\prime\prime})-(9-b_{2}^{-}(X^{\prime\prime}))=-9,

∎

In terms of the notion of exceptional gaps, the above proposition particularly implies that
\mathfrak{e}(\mathcal{S}_{12},\mathcal{S}_{3};\overline{D})>0
. As a result, we obtain a connected symplectic divisor

\overline{\overline{D}}:=\overline{D}\cup C_{e},

where, after possibly exchanging the index of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, we may assume that
C_{e}
is an exceptional sphere connecting
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{3}
. For any symplectic divisor

, we call

a

if

[C_{i}]\cdot([\mathcal{C}]-[C_{i}])\geq 3.

Our aim is to show that
\overline{\overline{D}}
cannot have branching component.

Lemma 4.7
.

If
\overline{\overline{D}}
has a branching component
C
with
[C]\cdot([\overline{\overline{D}}]-[C])=3
(resp.
4
), then one (resp. two) of the connected components of
\overline{\overline{D}}\setminus C
can be reduced to a
(-1)
-sphere by a sequence of toric or half-toric blowdowns.

Proof.

By the gap-admissible assumption,
\overline{\overline{D}}
is a connected divisor such that
[\omega]\cdot(K_{\omega}+[\overline{\overline{D}}])<0
. By Proposition
2.14
, it can be reduced to a pair
(\check{X},\check{\omega},\check{D})
which is either quasi-minimal or
b_{2}(\check{X})\leq 2
by successive blowdowns. By Lemma
2.5
, if a non-toric or exterior blowdown was performed at some step, then

will admit some non-toric or exterior exceptional sphere (if it is non-toric to

which intersects

or

4.2
or the full condition. Hence, the reduction process only involves toric or half-toric blowdowns. Since

and each toric or half-toric blowdowns decreases the number of divisor components by

l(\check{D})=1+b_{2}(\check{X})
.

Note that if
\check{D}
has no branching component, then in the process of successive blowdowns starting from

has no branching component. This implies that one (if
\overline{\overline{D}}\setminus C
has three connected components) or two (if
\overline{\overline{D}}\setminus C
has four connected components) of the connected component of
\overline{\overline{D}}\setminus C
can be contracted to a
(-1)
-sphere. Therefore, it remains to discuss the following cases to show that

cannot have a branching component.

•

If

has no branching component since it can only have three components.

•

If

is quasi-minimal of the first kind, then it must be a string by Proposition

, so no branching component can appear.

•

If

is quasi-minimal of the second kind, then we can further reduce it to a divisor in a manifold with

by Proposition

∎

Remark 4.8
.

The argument above yields the following more general statement: if

\overline{\overline{D}}

4.3
Completing the string into a cycle

To finish the proof of Theorem

, it remains to construct a symplectic sphere in class

which completes the string

into the boundary divisor of some toric moment map.

Proposition 4.12
.

-K_{\omega}-[\overline{\overline{D}}]
can be represented by an embedded symplectic sphere

such that

is a toric symplectic log Calabi–Yau divisor.

Proof.

By the gap-admissible condition,
\overline{\overline{D}}\subseteq(X,\omega)
is a connected symplectic divisor satisfying
[\omega]\cdot(K_{\omega}+[\overline{\overline{D}}])<0
. As in the proof of Lemma
4.7
, we can perform successive toric or half-toric blowdowns to obtain a pair
(\check{X},\check{\omega},\check{D})
that is either quasi-minimal or satisfies

, by Proposition

. Let us first show the following.

Claim 4.13
.

-K_{\check{\omega}}-[\check{D}]
can be represented by an embedded
\check{\omega}
-symplectic sphere
\check{C}
.

Proof.

If
(\check{X},\check{\omega},\check{D})

implies that

\check{\omega}

Observe that

\displaystyle(-K_{\omega}-[\overline{\overline{D}}])^{2}

\displaystyle=K_{\omega}^{2}+2K_{\omega}\cdot[\overline{\overline{D}}]+[\overline{\overline{D}}]^{2}=K_{\omega}^{2}+K_{\omega}\cdot[\overline{\overline{D}}]-2

\displaystyle=9-b_{2}^{-}(X)+\sum_{C_{i}\subseteq\overline{\overline{D}}}(-2-[C_{i}]^{2})-2

to represent

Proof of ‘if’ part of Theorem
1.6
.

By

, every toric symplectic log Calabi–Yau divisor can be realized as the boundary divisor of the moment map for a toric structure. Proposition

therefore implies that

is sub-toric in the sense of Definition

∎

With our main Theorem
1.6
established, we can now extend the Torelli theorem for symplectic log Calabi–Yau divisors to those arising from minimal resolutions of weighted projective planes.

Proof of Corollary
1.7
.

First, by
[
25
, Theorem 2.8]
, the integral isometry
\gamma
on second (co)homology is realized by a diffeomorphism between
X
and
X^{\prime}
, since we assume
[\omega^{\prime}]\cdot K_{\omega^{\prime}}<0
and the sub-toric assumption on
D
also implies
[\omega]\cdot K_{\omega}<0
. Moreover, cohomologous symplectic forms on a
4
-manifold have the same first Chern class by
[
49
, Corollary A]
, so
\gamma
must also identify
K_{\omega}
with
K_{\omega^{\prime}}
. Then
[
26
, Theorem A]
implies that
\gamma
identifies the sets of symplectic exceptional classes
\mathcal{E}_{\omega}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\omega^{\prime}}
. By definition, it therefore also identifies the sets of connecting log exceptional classes
\mathcal{E}_{\omega}^{\text{log}}(\mathcal{S}_{i},\mathcal{S}_{j};D)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\text{log}}(\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\prime};D^{\prime})
. Since Theorem
1.6
shows that the sub-toric divisor
D
is gap-admissible, the same holds for
D^{\prime}
by these identifications on the (co)homological level. Applying Theorem
1.6
once more, we conclude that
D^{\prime}
is also sub-toric, proving the first item of Corollary
1.7
.

Now assume further that
\gamma
preserves the homology classes of all components of
D
and
D^{\prime}
. By Theorem
1.6
, both
D
and
D^{\prime}
can be completed to toric symplectic log Calabi–Yau divisors by adding three components. Proposition
3.5
shows that two of these added components belong to the set of connecting log exceptional classes, and these classes are uniquely determined by the total homology classes of the strings. Hence
\gamma
preserves the classes of

components of the toric log Calabi–Yau divisors. Since
\gamma
also sends
K_{\omega}
to

, it preserves the class of the remaining component as well. We may therefore apply Theorem
2.6
to obtain a symplectomorphism between the corresponding log Calabi–Yau pairs. Restricting this symplectomorphism to

and

, we conclude that

and
(X^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},D^{\prime})
are symplectomorphic. This proves the second item.

∎

Remark 4.14
.

The intuition behind this argument is that certain symplectic divisors that arise as part of symplectic log Calabi–Yau divisors always exhibit this rigidity property. We expect that such a Torelli-type result also holds for more general divisors with symplectic log Kodaira dimension
-\infty
.

5
Symplectic affine rulings on weighted projective planes

In this section, we prove Theorem

5.1
Constructing affine rulings

We first consider the sub-toric configuration

\mathcal{S}_{a}
,
\mathcal{S}_{b}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{c}
denote the resolution strings at the orbifold points of orders
a
,
b
, and
c
, respectively. By Lemma
3.3
, there exist exceptional spheres
N_{a}
and
N_{b}
connecting
\mathcal{S}_{b}
to
\mathcal{S}_{c}
and
\mathcal{S}_{a}
to
\mathcal{S}_{c}
, respectively, as well as an embedded symplectic sphere
N_{c}
connecting
\mathcal{S}_{a}
to
\mathcal{S}_{b}
with
[N_{c}]^{2}\geq-1
, such that
D\cup N_{a}\cup N_{b}\cup N_{c}
is the toric boundary divisor of a moment map.
Consider the strings (with orientations indicated by labelings)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ac}:=\mathcal{S}_{a}\cup N_{b}\cup\mathcal{S}_{c}=A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{k_{a}}\cup N_{b}\cup C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k_{c}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{bc}:=\mathcal{S}_{b}\cup N_{a}\cup\mathcal{S}_{c}=B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{k_{b}}\cup N_{a}\cup C_{k_{c}}\cup\cdots\cup C_{1}

which are not negative definite by the full condition. Given any
\nu\in\{1,2,\cdots,k_{c}\}
, define their truncations by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ac}^{\leq\nu}:=\mathcal{S}_{a}\cup N_{b}\cup\mathcal{S}_{c}=A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{k_{a}}\cup N_{b}\cup C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{\nu},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{bc}^{\geq\nu}:=\mathcal{S}_{b}\cup N_{a}\cup\mathcal{S}_{c}=B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{k_{b}}\cup N_{a}\cup C_{k_{c}}\cup\cdots\cup C_{\nu},

and further define
\nu_{a},\nu_{b}\in\{1,2,\cdots,k_{c}\}
by

\displaystyle\nu_{a}:=\inf\{\nu\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{ac}^{\leq\nu}\text{ is not negative definite}\},

\displaystyle\nu_{b}:=\sup\{\nu\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{bc}^{\geq\nu}\text{ is not negative definite}\}.

By the discussion in Section
2.2
, there exist two non-zero classes
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
, obtained as positive integral linear combinations of the classes in
\mathcal{S}_{ac}^{\leq\nu_{a}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{bc}^{\geq\nu_{b}}
, respectively, such that for any component
S
in
D\cup N_{a}\cup N_{b}\cup N_{c}

F_{a}\cdot[S]=\begin{cases}p_{a}\geq 0,\qquad&S=C_{\nu_{a}}\\
q_{a}>0,\qquad&S=C_{\nu_{a}+1}\\
1,\qquad&S=N_{c}\\
0,\qquad&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad F_{b}\cdot[S]=\begin{cases}p_{b}\geq 0,\qquad&S=C_{\nu_{b}}\\
q_{b}>0,\qquad&S=C_{\nu_{b}-1}\\
1,\qquad&S=N_{c}\\
0,\qquad&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
(p_{a},q_{a})
and
(p_{b},q_{b})
are two pairs of coprime integers. Also, we have

F_{a}^{2}=p_{a}q_{a}\geq 0,\quad F_{b}^{2}=p_{b}q_{b}\geq 0,\quad K_{\omega}\cdot F_{a}=-p_{a}-q_{a}-1,\quad K_{\omega}\cdot F_{b}=-p_{b}-q_{b}-1.

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the above assumptions, the classes
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
coincide and one of the following holds.

1.

If
[N_{c}]^{2}\geq 0
, then
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=2
, and the class
F_{a}=F_{b}
is represented by an embedded symplectic sphere of self-intersection
0
that meets the unique component
C_{\nu_{a}+1}=C_{\nu_{b}-1}
of
\mathcal{S}_{c}
transversely in a single point and is disjoint from
\mathcal{S}_{a}\cup\mathcal{S}_{b}
.

2.

If
[N_{c}]^{2}=-1
, then
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=1
, and the class
F_{a}=F_{b}
is represented by a symplectic unicuspidal rational curve of self-intersection
p_{a}q_{a}=p_{b}q_{b}
with a unique cusp singularity of type
(p_{a},q_{a})=(q_{b},p_{b})
at the point
C_{\nu_{a}}\cap C_{\nu_{b}}
; away from this cusp, the curve is disjoint from
D
.

Proof.

Step 1:

\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}\in\{1,2\}
.

When
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}\geq 2
, the supports of the linear combinations defining
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
are disjoint, and hence
F_{a}\cdot F_{b}=0
. By the light cone lemma, it follows that
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
are proportional. However, if
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}\geq 3
, this is impossible since
F_{a}\cdot[C_{\nu_{b}-2}]=0
while
F_{b}\cdot[C_{\nu_{b}-2}]>0
. Thus,
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}\leq 2
.

If
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}\leq 0
, then the configuration
(D\cup N_{a}\cup N_{b})\setminus C_{\nu_{a}}
splits as the disjoint union of two negative definite strings, namely
\mathcal{S}_{ac}^{\leq\nu_{a}-1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{bc}^{\geq\nu_{a}+1}
. Together they contain
b_{2}^{-}(X)+1
components, which is impossible. Therefore,
1\leq\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}
.

Step 2:
If
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=2
, then
F_{a}=F_{b}
and
(p_{a},q_{a})=(p_{b},q_{b})=(0,1)
.

We have seen in Step 1 that
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=2
implies
F_{a}\cdot F_{b}=0
. Since also
F_{a}\cdot[N_{c}]=F_{b}\cdot[N_{c}]=1
, the light cone lemma implies that
F_{a}=F_{b}
.
It follows that

p_{a}=F_{a}\cdot[C_{\nu_{a}}]=F_{b}\cdot[C_{\nu_{a}}]=0,\qquad p_{b}=F_{b}\cdot[C_{\nu_{b}}]=F_{a}\cdot[C_{\nu_{b}}]=0,

and hence
F_{a}^{2}=F_{b}^{2}=0
. Since
(p_{a},q_{a})
and
(p_{b},q_{b})
are coprime pairs, we must have
q_{a}=q_{b}=1
.

Step 3:
If
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=1
, then
F_{a}=F_{b}
and
(p_{a},q_{a})=(q_{b},p_{b})
.

We perform successive toric blowups at the node
C_{\nu_{a}}\cap C_{\nu_{b}}
, using the weight sequence
(m_{1},\dots,m_{l})
associated to the pair
(p_{a},q_{a})
as in Section
2.2
. This produces a new string
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}
in the blowup manifold
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\omega})
with components

A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{k_{a}}\cup N_{b}\cup C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\tilde{C}_{\nu_{a}}\cup\mathcal{L}\cup\tilde{C}_{\nu_{b}}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k_{c}}\cup N_{a}\cup B_{k_{b}}\cup\cdots\cup B_{1},

where
\tilde{C}_{\nu_{a}}
and
\tilde{C}_{\nu_{b}}
are the proper transforms of
C_{\nu_{a}}
and
C_{\nu_{b}}
, and
\mathcal{L}
is the string created by the toric blowup process. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{l}
denote the exceptional classes of these blowups. Then
\mathcal{L}
has the form

\tilde{C}_{e_{\alpha}}\cup\cdots\cup\tilde{C}_{e_{\beta}}\cup C_{e_{l}}\cup\tilde{C}_{e_{\gamma}}\cup\cdots\cup\tilde{C}_{e_{\theta}},

where each
\tilde{C}_{e_{i}}
is the proper transform of the exceptional sphere in class
e_{i}
, and
C_{e_{l}}
is the exceptional sphere arising from the final blowup. The resolution class

\tilde{F}_{a}:=F_{a}-\sum_{i=1}^{l}m_{i}e_{i}

has intersection number
1
with
C_{e_{l}}
and intersection number
0
with every other component of
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}
. It also satisfies
\tilde{F}_{a}\cdot[N_{c}]=1
. Now consider the truncated string
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq\gamma}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{S}}
with components

B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{k_{b}}\cup N_{a}\cup C_{k_{c}}\cup\cdots\cup\tilde{C}_{\nu_{b}}\cup\tilde{C}_{e_{\theta}}\cup\cdots\cup\tilde{C}_{e_{\gamma}}.

This string cannot be negative definite. Indeed, otherwise
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}\setminus(\tilde{C}_{e_{\beta}}\cup C_{e_{l}})
would be a negative definite configuration spanning a lattice of rank
b_{2}^{-}(X)+l
inside
\langle\tilde{F}_{a}\rangle^{\perp}\subseteq H_{2}(\tilde{X};\mathbb{Z})
, which is impossible by Lemma
2.7
. By the same argument as in Step 1, the truncated string
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq\gamma}\setminus\tilde{C}_{e_{\gamma}}
must nevertheless be negative definite. Therefore, as in Section
2.2
, if
\Delta_{i}
denotes the determinant of the negative intersection matrix of the first
(i-1)
components of
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq\gamma}
, then the class

\tilde{F}_{b}:=\Delta_{1}[B_{1}]+\cdots+\Delta_{k_{b}}[B_{k_{b}}]+\Delta_{k_{b}+1}[N_{a}]+\Delta_{k_{b}+2}[C_{k_{c}}]+\cdots+\Delta_{l(\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq\gamma})}[\tilde{C}_{e_{\gamma}}]

satisfies

\tilde{F}_{b}^{2}=-\Delta_{l(\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq\gamma})}\Delta_{l(\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq\gamma})+1}\geq 0.

By the same argument as in Step 2, we conclude that
\tilde{F}_{b}=\tilde{F}_{a}
.
Since the self-intersection sequence of
\mathcal{S}_{bc}^{\geq\nu_{b}}\setminus C_{\nu_{b}}
is unchanged by the toric blowups, we have

F_{b}=\Delta_{1}[B_{1}]+\cdots+\Delta_{k_{b}}[B_{k_{b}}]+\Delta_{k_{b}+1}[N_{a}]+\Delta_{k_{b}+2}[C_{k_{c}}]+\cdots+\Delta_{k_{b}+k_{c}-\nu_{b}+2}[C_{\nu_{b}}].

Hence

\tilde{F}_{b}-F_{b}=\Delta_{k_{b}+k_{c}-\nu_{b}+2}([\tilde{C}_{\nu_{b}}]-[C_{\nu_{b}}])+\Delta_{k_{b}+k_{c}-\nu_{b}+3}[\tilde{C}_{e_{\theta}}]+\cdots+\Delta_{l(\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq\gamma})}[\tilde{C}_{e_{\gamma}}]\in\mathbb{Z}\langle e_{1},\dots,e_{l}\rangle.

Therefore, the equality
\tilde{F}_{a}=\tilde{F}_{b}
implies
F_{a}=F_{b}
, and the equality
(p_{a},q_{a})=(q_{b},p_{b})
follows immediately.

Step 4:
If
[N_{c}]^{2}\geq 0
, then
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=2
.

Suppose
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=1
. By Step 3, we have
F_{b}\cdot[C_{\nu_{b}}]=p_{b}=q_{a}>0
. Since the string
\mathcal{S}_{ac}^{\leq\nu_{a}}
is non-negative definite, so is any of its toric or half-toric blowdowns. After blowing down the
N_{c}
-sphere, there must be at least one
(-1)
-component. Moreover, because
\mathcal{S}_{ac}^{\leq\nu_{a}}
has only one
(-1)
-component, namely
N_{c}
, Lemma
2.10
shows that one of the following cases occurs.

•

If there is only one
(-1)
-component after blowing down
N_{c}
, then by Lemma
2.9
it cannot appear as an endpoint of the string. Hence we can perform a further toric blowdown.

•

If there are two adjacent
(-1)
-components after blowing down
N_{c}
, let
U
be the sum of their homology classes, so that
U^{2}=0
. Since
[N_{c}]^{2}\geq 0
, if
U\cdot[N_{c}]=0
, then the light cone lemma implies that the nonzero classes
U
and
[N_{c}]
are proportional. This is impossible because
U\cdot[B_{1}]=0
while
[N_{c}]\cdot[B_{1}]=1
. Thus one of the
(-1)
-components must intersect
N_{c}
. Moreover, if
U\cdot[C_{\nu_{b}}]=0
, then
U\cdot F_{b}=0
, so the nonzero classes
U
and
F_{b}
are again proportional by the light cone lemma. This contradicts
F_{b}\cdot[C_{\nu_{b}}]>0
. Therefore one of the
(-1)
-components must intersect
C_{\nu_{b}}
, which in turn implies that the entire string consists only of these two
(-1)
-components.

From the above analysis, we see that the string
\mathcal{S}_{ac}^{\leq\nu_{a}}
can be contracted to two
(-1)
-components by a sequence of toric blowdowns. The same argument applies to
\mathcal{S}_{bc}^{\geq\nu_{b}}
. Together with the
N_{c}
-component, this would produce a toric divisor with self-intersection sequence
(-1,-1,[N_{c}]^{2},-1,-1)
. However, such a toric configuration cannot exist: after further blowing down the two
(-1)
-components adjacent to
N_{c}
, we would obtain the toric boundary self-intersection sequence
(0,[N_{c}]^{2}+2,0)
on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, which is impossible. Therefore
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=2
whenever
[N_{c}]^{2}\geq 0
.

Step 5:
If
[N_{c}]^{2}=-1
, then
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=1
.

Suppose
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=2
. As in Step 4, we may perform toric blowdowns contracting
\mathcal{S}_{ac}^{\leq\nu_{a}}
to a string with two adjacent
(-1)
-components, and we denote the sum of their homology classes by
U_{a}
. Since
U_{a}\cdot F_{b}=0
, the light cone lemma implies that the nonzero classes
U_{a}
and
F_{b}
are proportional. By Step 2, we have
F_{b}\cdot[C_{\nu_{a}+1}]=1
, so one of these
(-1)
-components must intersect
C_{\nu_{a}+1}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{S}_{bc}^{\geq\nu_{b}}
can be contracted to a string containing two adjacent
(-1)
-components; denote the sum of their homology classes by
U_{b}
. Again, one of these components must meet
C_{\nu_{a}+1}
. Applying the light cone lemma once more, we obtain
U_{a}=U_{b}
. Hence both contracted strings must also intersect
N_{c}
. This produces a toric configuration with self-intersection sequence
(-1,-1,[N_{c}]^{2},-1,-1,[C_{\nu_{a}+1}]^{2})
.
This is impossible, because in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#3\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}
with symplectic canonical class
K
,

6=K^{2}=(-1-1+[N_{c}]^{2}-1-1+[C_{\nu_{a}+1}]^{2})+12,

while
[N_{c}]^{2}=-1
and
[C_{\nu_{a}+1}]^{2}\leq-2
. Therefore,
\nu_{b}-\nu_{a}=1
whenever
[N_{c}]^{2}=-1
.

Step 6:
Existence of symplectic affine rulings.

It follows from the previous steps together with Lemma
2.11
that, when
[N_{c}]^{2}\geq 0
, the common class
F_{a}=F_{b}
is a fiber class. For generic
J\in\mathcal{J}(D)
, this class is represented by an embedded
J
-holomorphic sphere of self-intersection
0
. By its intersection pattern, this sphere meets the unique component
C_{\nu_{a}+1}=C_{\nu_{b}-1}
transversely at a single point and is disjoint from
\mathcal{S}_{a}\cup\mathcal{S}_{b}
. This is precisely the desired embedded symplectic sphere. When
[N_{c}]^{2}=-1
, after performing successive toric blowups using the common weight sequence of
(p_{a},q_{a})=(q_{b},p_{b})
, the resolved class
\tilde{F}_{a}=\tilde{F}_{b}
is represented by an embedded symplectic sphere of self-intersection
0
. By
[
42
, Proposition 4.3.1]
, this sphere corresponds to a symplectic
(p_{a},q_{a})
-unicuspidal rational curve whose cusp is located at
C_{\nu_{a}}\cap C_{\nu_{b}}
and which is disjoint from the other components of
D
.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

\mathcal{S}_{ac}

\mathcal{S}_{b}

5.2
Recovering sub-toric configurations from affine rulings

We now prove the converse direction of Theorem
1.8
. We begin by adapting the argument of Section
4.2
to show that three strings can still be joined into a connected configuration by exceptional spheres when the symplectic area assumption is replaced by the existence of affine ruling.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
D=\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}\cup\mathcal{S}_{3}
be disjoint symplectic strings in
(X,\omega)
with
l(\mathcal{S}_{1})+l(\mathcal{S}_{2})+l(\mathcal{S}_{3})=b_{2}^{-}(X)
. Assume that
A
is a component of
\mathcal{S}_{3}
such that every component of
D\setminus A
has self-intersection at most
-2
. If there exists an embedded symplectic sphere
C
of self-intersection
0
that intersects
A
transversely at a single point and is disjoint from all other components of
D
, then there exist symplectic exceptional spheres
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

•

D\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2}
is a symplectic divisor without non-toric exceptional spheres;

•

[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{1}]=[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}]=[C_{2}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{2}]=[C_{2}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}]=1
,
[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{2}]=[C_{2}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{1}]=[C_{1}]\cdot[C_{2}]=0
.

Proof.

After a small perturbation, we may assume that
D\cup C
is a symplectic divisor. Choose any
J\in\mathcal{J}(D\cup C)
such that the fiber class
[C]
is
J
-nef. Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
be the
J
-holomorphic subvarieties in class
[C]
passing through points on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, respectively. We claim that
\Theta_{1}\neq\Theta_{2}
. Indeed, otherwise there would exist a component
B\in\Theta_{1}=\Theta_{2}
connecting
\mathcal{S}_{1}
to
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. By Proposition
2.8
(2) and our assumption on the self-intersection numbers of the components of
D\setminus A
, the configuration
(D\setminus A)\cup B
would then be negative definite. However, it has
b_{2}^{-}(X)
components, contradicting Lemma
2.7
.

Therefore, by Proposition
2.8
(3), we can choose two disjoint symplectic exceptional spheres
C_{1},C_{2}
contained in
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
, respectively. Since the configurations of
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
are trees of embedded spheres by Proposition
2.8
(1), after another small perturbation we may assume that
D\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2}
is a symplectic divisor. We now verify the stated intersection relations below.

First, since
\Theta_{1}\neq\Theta_{2}
, we naturally have

[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{2}]=[C_{2}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{1}]=[C_{1}]\cdot[C_{2}]=0.

Next, we claim that
C_{1}
(respectively,
C_{2}
) must intersect both
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{3}
(respectively,
\mathcal{S}_{2}
and
\mathcal{S}_{3}
). Indeed, if
C_{1}
did not intersect both
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, then
\Theta_{1}
would contain components other than
C_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and hence
(D\setminus A)\cup C_{1}
would be negative definite by Proposition
2.8
(2), again contradicting Lemma
2.7
. The same argument applies to
C_{2}
. Since the configurations of
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
are trees and hence contain no loops, it follows that

[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{1}]=[C_{2}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{2}]=1.

Finally, we show that
[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}]\leq 1
; the same proof gives
[C_{2}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}]\leq 1
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime\prime}
denote the two (possibly empty) strings of connected components of
\mathcal{S}_{3}\setminus A
, and let
\Theta^{\prime}_{3},\Theta^{\prime\prime}_{3}
be the
J
-holomorphic subvarieties passing through
\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. There are two cases.

•

If
\Theta_{1}\neq\Theta_{3}^{\prime}
and
\Theta_{1}\neq\Theta_{3}^{\prime\prime}
, then the configuration of
\Theta_{1}
cannot contain more components than
\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup C_{1}
by Lemma
2.7
. This will imply that
C_{1}
must intersect the
A
-component. However, since
C_{1}
is the only
(-1)
-component in
\Theta_{1}
, Proposition
2.8
(3) indicates that the multiplicity of
C_{1}
is at least
2
. This contradicts
[C]\cdot A=1
.

•

If
\Theta_{1}=\Theta_{3}^{\prime}
(similarly, if
\Theta_{1}=\Theta_{3}^{\prime\prime}
), then Proposition
2.8
(1) gives
[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime}]=1
since there is no loop, while
[C_{1}]\cdot[A]=0
because
[C]\cdot[A]=1
, and
[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime\prime}]=0
because
\Theta_{3}^{\prime}\neq\Theta_{3}^{\prime\prime}
.

Therefore, we must have
[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}]=[C_{1}]\cdot([\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime}]+[\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime\prime}]+[A])=1
. By symmetry, also
[C_{2}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}]=1
, and hence

[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{1}]=[C_{1}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}]=[C_{2}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{2}]=[C_{2}]\cdot[\mathcal{S}_{3}]=1.

It remains to show that
D\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2}
admits no non-toric exceptional spheres. By the preceding analysis, after possibly relabeling we may assume that
\Theta_{1}=\Theta_{3}^{\prime}
and
\Theta_{2}=\Theta_{3}^{\prime\prime}
. Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a non-toric exceptional sphere
C_{e}
. Then the configuration
C_{e}\cup(D\setminus A)
must be negative definite. This is immediate if
C_{e}
intersects the
A
-component. If instead
C_{e}
intersects a component of
\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime}
(and similarly for
\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime\prime}
), then
\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup C_{e}\cup\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime}
is a proper sub-configuration of
\Theta_{1}
, so negative definiteness again follows from Proposition
2.8
(2). Since
C_{e}\cup(D\setminus A)
is disjoint from
C
and has
b_{2}^{-}(X)
components, this contradicts Lemma
2.7
.

∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
D=\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}\cup\mathcal{S}_{3}
be three disjoint symplectic HJ strings in
(X,\omega)
where
[\omega]\cdot K_{\omega}<0
. Suppose
D
is full and of
(a,b,c)
-type. If there exists
C\subseteq(X,\omega)
which is

•

either a symplectic
(p,q)
-unicuspidal rational curve with
[C]^{2}=pq
, whose
(p,q)
-cusp is at the intersection of two components of one string
\mathcal{S}_{i}
and disjoint from other components in
D
;

•

or an embedded symplectic sphere of self-intersection
0
intersecting exactly one component in
D
transversely,

then
D
is sub-toric.

Proof.

We may assume that
C
intersects
\mathcal{S}_{3}
after relabeling. First, observe that the hypotheses of Lemma
5.4
can always be recovered. If
C
is embedded, this follows directly from the HJ assumption on
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{3}
. If
C
is
(p,q)
-unicuspidal, then we proceed as in Section
2.2
by performing successive toric blowups determined by the weight sequence
\mathcal{W}(p,q)=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{L})
. Let
e_{1},\cdots,e_{L}
denote the corresponding exceptional classes. Then the total transform of
D
is again a disjoint union of three strings, now with total length
b_{2}^{-}(X)+L
, and all components other than the
e_{L}
-component have self-intersection at most
-2
. Moreover, the resolution class
[C]-\sum_{i=1}^{L}m_{i}e_{i}
is a fiber class in
(X\#L\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2},\tilde{\omega})
, represented by an embedded symplectic sphere
\tilde{C}
that intersects only the
e_{L}
-component of the total transform of
D
. Therefore, by Lemma
5.4
, there exist exceptional spheres
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
in
(X,\omega)
or in its blowup
(X\#L\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2},\tilde{\omega})
such that, by denoting the total transform of
D
still by
\tilde{D}
(
\tilde{D}=D
if
L=0
),
\tilde{D}\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2}
forms a connected symplectic divisor.

Next, consider the class

U:=-K_{\tilde{\omega}}-[\tilde{D}]-[C_{1}]-[C_{2}].

We claim that
[\tilde{\omega}]\cdot U>0
. By the intersection pattern in Lemma
5.4
,

U^{2}=(-K_{\tilde{\omega}}-[\tilde{D}])^{2}+(-[C_{1}]-[C_{2}])^{2}+2(-K_{\tilde{\omega}}-[\tilde{D}])\cdot(-[C_{1}]-[C_{2}])=(K_{\tilde{\omega}}+[\tilde{D}])^{2}+2.

Since
D
is full and of
(a,b,c)
-type, Corollary
3.4
implies that
(K_{\omega}+[D])^{2}\geq-2
. Because toric blowup preserves the adjoint class, it follows that
(K_{\tilde{\omega}}+[\tilde{D}])^{2}\geq-2
as well. Hence
U^{2}\geq-1
.

If
U^{2}\geq 0
, then, because
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
lie in the complement of
\tilde{C}
,

U\cdot[\tilde{C}]=(-K_{\tilde{\omega}}-[\tilde{D}])\cdot[\tilde{C}]=2-1=1.

Therefore
U
and
[\tilde{C}]
lie in the same connected component of the positive cone in
H_{2}(X\#L\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2};\mathbb{Z})
. Since
[\tilde{\omega}]\cdot[\tilde{C}]>0
, we conclude that
[\tilde{\omega}]\cdot U>0
.

If
U^{2}=-1
, then, because the configuration
\tilde{D}\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2}
is connected and loop-free, adjunction gives

\displaystyle K_{\tilde{\omega}}\cdot U

\displaystyle=(-U-[\tilde{D}]-[C_{1}]-[C_{2}])\cdot U=-U^{2}-([\tilde{D}]+[C_{1}]+[C_{2}])\cdot U

\displaystyle=1-([\tilde{D}]+[C_{1}]+[C_{2}])\cdot(-K_{\tilde{\omega}}-[\tilde{D}]-[C_{1}]-[C_{2}])

\displaystyle=1-2=-1.

It then follows from
[
26
, Theorem A]
that
U\in\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{\omega}}
, and hence again
[\tilde{\omega}]\cdot U>0
.

Therefore,
\overline{\overline{D}}:=\tilde{D}\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2}
is a connected symplectic divisor satisfying
[\tilde{\omega}]\cdot(K_{\tilde{\omega}}+[\overline{\overline{D}}])<0
. Hence we may carry out the quasi-minimal reduction for
\overline{\overline{D}}
as in Section
2.3
. By Lemmas
5.4
and
2.5
, this reduction uses only toric and half-toric blowdowns. We claim that
\overline{\overline{D}}
has no branching component.

Suppose on the contrary that
\overline{\overline{D}}
has a branching component
B
. By Remark
4.8
, the argument of Lemma
4.7
still shows that one connected component of
\overline{\overline{D}}\setminus B
can be contracted to a
(-1)
-sphere through a sequence of toric and half-toric blowdowns. Then, exactly as in the proof of Proposition
4.9
, every branching component of
\overline{\overline{D}}
must lie on
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
must be
(-2)
-strings. After permuting
(a,b,c)
, we may therefore assume that
l(\mathcal{S}_{1})=a-1
and
l(\mathcal{S}_{2})=b-1
. By Lemma
3.6
, the string
\mathcal{S}_{3}
has self-intersection sequence
(-a,-k,-b)
for some
k\geq 2
,
(1-a,1-b)
when
k=1
and
b\neq 2
, or
(2-a)
when
k=1
and
b=2
. Performing
a-1
toric blowdowns followed by one half-toric blowdown contracts
\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup C_{1}
, and similarly
b-1
toric blowdowns followed by one half-toric blowdown contract
\mathcal{S}_{2}\cup C_{2}
. Thus
\overline{\overline{D}}
can be reduced to a single string
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
.

If
\tilde{D}=D
, so that
C
is an embedded symplectic sphere, the rest of the argument is identical to that of Proposition
4.9
. In this case the branching component can only be the
(-k)
-component of
\mathcal{S}_{3}
, and
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
is a
3
-component string in a Hirzebruch surface. We can use Claim
4.11
to obtain contradiction.

Assume now that
\tilde{D}\neq D
, so that
C
is cuspidal. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{3}\subseteq\tilde{D}
be the total transform of
\mathcal{S}_{3}
under the successive toric blowups. Then
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
can also be viewed as being obtained from
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
by performing
a+b
non-toric blowdowns at the two distinct components to which
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are attached. After deleting the
e_{L}
-component, the string
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
contains two non-negative definite strings
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}^{\prime}
and
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}^{\prime\prime}
. Aside from the two components where non-toric blowdowns occur, every component of
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}^{\prime}
and
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}^{\prime\prime}
has self-intersection at most
-2
. Lemma
2.10
therefore implies that each of
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}^{\prime}
and
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}^{\prime\prime}
can be reduced, using only toric blowdowns, to a length-
2
string of two
(-1)
-components. Consequently,
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
itself can be reduced by toric blowdowns to a string
\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime\prime\prime}
with self-intersection sequence
(-1,-1,-1,-1,-1)
, where the middle
(-1)
corresponds to the
e_{L}
-component.

We now consider the
\Xi
-invariant introduced in the proof of Proposition
4.4
. In the case where
\mathcal{S}_{3}
has self-intersection sequence
(-a,-k,-b)
, we have
\Xi(\mathcal{S}_{3})=a+b+k-9
, and hence also
\Xi(\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{3})=a+b+k-9
because toric blowups preserve
\Xi
. Contracting
\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup C_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}\cup C_{2}
increases the self-intersection numbers of two components of
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
by
a
and
b
, respectively, so
\Xi(\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3})=k-9
. On the other hand, the previous paragraph shows that
\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
reduces to
\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime\prime\prime}
, so
\Xi(\check{\mathcal{S}}_{3})=\Xi(\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime\prime\prime})=-10
. This is impossible because
k\geq 2
. The remaining cases lead to contradictions in the same way, by considering
k=1
and
k=1
with
b=2
. Therefore
\overline{\overline{D}}
has no branching components.

Finally, once we know
\overline{\overline{D}}
is a string, we can employ the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
4.12
to represent the class
U
by an embedded symplectic sphere
C_{U}
, so that
\overline{\overline{D}}
becomes a toric symplectic log Calabi–Yau divisor. This proves that
D
is sub-toric.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The proof of the above proposition contains two new features in addition to the overlaps with the argument in Section
4
. First, without the gap-admissible condition, one must use the existence of symplectic affine rulings to show that the class
U
has positive symplectic area. Second, because we allow affine rulings given by unicuspidal curves, one must pass to a resolution in order to obtain a fiber class and work with the total transform
\tilde{D}
. The connecting exceptional spheres
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
provided by Lemma
5.4
may intersect components of
\tilde{D}
that do not belong to the proper transform of
D
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

The theorem follows immediately from Propositions
5.1
and
5.5
.
