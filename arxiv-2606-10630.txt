Abstract
. In this paper, we mainly consider positive solution to the D 1 , p ​ ( ℝ N ) D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) -critical quasi-linear Schrödinger system with p p -Laplacian: { − Δ p ​ u = u α ​ v β in ℝ N , − Δ p ​ v = u β ​ v α in ℝ N , \begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\,\ \ \ \ \ \text{in}\,\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\ -\Delta_{p}v=u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}\,\ \ \ \ \ \text{in}\,\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases} where 1 < p < N 1<p<N , N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , 0 ≤ α ≤ β , 0\leq\alpha\leq\beta, and u , v ∈ D 1 , p ​ ( ℝ N ) u,v\in D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) . We establish regularity and the sharp estimates on asymptotic behaviors for any positive solution ( u , v ) (u,v) . Then, we prove that all positive solutions are radially symmetric and strictly decreasing about some point. Furthermore, we obtain the uniqueness and complete classification of positive solutions. Our results extend the uniqueness results in [ 48 , 58 ] for p = 2 p=2 to general cases 1 < p < N 1<p<N .

Introduction

In this paper, we are mainly concerned with positive
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-weak solutions to the following
p
-Laplacian Schrödinger system with critical exponent:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u=u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\,\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\,\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\\
-\Delta_{p}v=u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}\,\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\,\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\\
u,v\in D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,\,\,u,v>0\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}\right.\hskip 28.45274pt

where the full range of
p
is
1<p<N
,
N\geq 2
, and
\Delta_{p}u:=div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
is the usual
p
-Laplace operator,
\alpha,\beta
are real parameters satisfying

\alpha+\beta=p^{\star}-1,\quad 0\leq\alpha\leq\beta,

p^{\star}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
is called Sobolev critical exponent. The function space

D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,\bigg|\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\right\}

is the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}
with respect to the norm
\|u\|:=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. The quasi-linear Schrödinger system (
1
) is
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-critical in the sense that both (
1
) and the
D^{1,p}
-norms
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
,
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
are invariant under the scalings
u\mapsto u_{\lambda}(\cdot):=\lambda^{\frac{N-p}{p}}u(\lambda\cdot)
and
v\mapsto v_{\lambda}(\cdot):=\lambda^{\frac{N-p}{p}}v(\lambda\cdot)
. System (
1
) is also invariant under arbitrary rotations and translations.

Definition 1.1

(
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-weak solution)
.

A
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-weak solution of system (
1
) is a pair of functions
(u,v)\in D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that, for any
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\psi\rangle\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x,\\
\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\langle\nabla v,\nabla\psi\rangle\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x.\end{array}\right.\hskip 28.45274pt

In the special case
p=2
, system (
1
) reduces to the second order semi-linear Schrödinger systems with critical exponents

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\,\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\,\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\\
-\Delta v=u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}\,\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\,\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.\hskip 28.45274pt

Li and Ma
[
48
]
proved the following uniqueness result for system (
1
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
n\geq 3
,
p=2
,
1\leq\alpha<\beta,
and
\alpha+\beta=p^{\star}-1.
Then any positive classical solution
(u,v)\in L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\times L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}
of (

) satisfies
u=v
.

extend the uniqueness result in
[

. For more classification results and Liouville type results on semi-linear systems, c.f. e.g.

In this paper, we aim to prove the classification of solutions to the

-critical quasi-linear Schrödinger system (

•

Nonlinear feature of the
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}
, linear operations can not be exchanged with
\Delta_{p}
, comparison principles can not be deduced from maximum principles and are much more difficult to be proved.

•

Absence of Kelvin type transforms, we need to derive the sharp asymptotic estimates on both
(u,v)
and
(|\nabla u|,|\nabla v|)
first.

•

Lack of the Green integral representation formula, indirect methods via the equivalent integral equation do not work anymore.

•

The

or
p>2

p
-Laplacian. Hence, we need to discuss the different two cases

Thus it would be an quite interesting and challenging problem to study the sharp asymptotic estimates, radial symmetry, strictly radial monotonicity and classification of solutions to (

3.1.
Local boundedness

Inspired by

,

Lemma 3.1

(
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity)
.

Let
(u,v)
be a pair of solutions to the equations (
1
) with
p>1
. Then
(u,v)\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
0<\alpha<\min\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\}
.

Proof.

Lemma
3.1
can be proved via the De Giorgi-Morse-Nash iteration method. Let
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be any bounded domain. For any
q\geq 1
and
k>0
, let

F(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t^{q},&\mbox{if}\,\,0\leq t\leq k,\\
qk^{q-1}t-(q-1)k^{q},&\mbox{if}\,\,t\geq k,\\
\end{array}\right.

and

G(t)=F(t)[F^{\prime}(t)]^{p-1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{p-1}t^{(q-1)p+1},&\mbox{if}\,\,0\leq t\leq k,\\
q^{p-1}k^{(q-1)p}[qt-(q-1)k],&\mbox{if}\,\,t\geq k.\\
\end{array}\right.

It is easy to see that
G
is a piecewise
C^{1}
–function with only one corner at
t=k
and

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[(q-1)p+1]q^{-1}[F^{\prime}(t)]^{p},&\mbox{if}\,\,0\leq t\leq k,\\
{[F^{\prime}(t)]}^{p},&\mbox{if}\,\,t\geq k,\\
\end{array}\right.

and

(3.8)

\displaystyle t^{p-1}G(t)\leq q^{p-1}{[F(t)]}^{p},

where
[(q-1)p+1]q^{-1}\geq 1
and
C
is independent of
k
.

Now we choose
q>1
, and let

\phi=\xi^{p}G(u)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi=\xi^{p}G(v),

where
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
to be determined later. Then the equation (
3
) yields

\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p}G^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+p\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{p-2}G(u)\nabla u\cdot\nabla\xi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\xi^{p}G(u)\mathrm{d}x.

\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p}G^{\prime}(v)|\nabla v|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+p\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p-1}|\nabla v|^{p-2}G(v)\nabla v\cdot\nabla\xi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}\xi^{p}G(v)\mathrm{d}x.

Noting that
G(x)
is increasing, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, by direct computations and Young’s inequality with
\varepsilon
, the previous identity becomes

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p}[F^{\prime}(u)]^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p}G^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{p-1}[F^{\prime}(u)]^{p-1}F(u)|\nabla\xi|\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\xi^{p}G(u)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p}[F^{\prime}(u)]^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}[F(u)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\Omega\cap\{u>v\}}u^{p^{\star}-1}\xi^{p}G(u)\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{\Omega\cap\{u\leq v\}}v^{p^{\star}-1}\xi^{p}G(v)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p}[F^{\prime}(u)]^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}[F(u)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\int_{\Omega\cap\{u>v\}}u^{p^{\star}-p}[\xi F(u)]^{p}\mathrm{d}x+Cq^{p-1}\int_{\Omega\cap\{u\leq v\}}v^{p^{\star}-p}[\xi F(v)]^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p}[F^{\prime}(u)]^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}[F(u)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(u)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}+Cq^{p-1}\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(v)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}},

where we also have used (
3.1
) and (
3.8
), as well as the definition of
G(u)
. Then, taking
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p}[F^{\prime}(u)]^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}[F(u)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(u)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(v)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}.

Using the Sobolev inequality, we can derive

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|\xi F(u)|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\xi\nabla F(u)+F(u)\nabla\xi|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p}[F^{\prime}(u)]^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+C_{1}\int_{\Omega}[F(u)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}[F(u)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(u)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(v)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}.

Similarly, we can also derive

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|\xi F(v)|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}[F(v)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(v)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(u)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}.

By (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\Omega}|\xi F(u)|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}|\xi F(v)|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}[F(u)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{\Omega}[F(v)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(u)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\quad+Cq^{p-1}\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega\cap supp(\xi))}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\Omega}[\xi F(v)]^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}.

Since
u,v\in L^{p^{\star}}
, for any
x_{0}\in\Omega
, we can choose
B(x_{0},2R)\subsetneq\Omega
such that

C\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(B(x_{0},2R))}^{p^{\star}-p}<\frac{1}{2q^{p-1}},\,\,\,\,\,C\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(B(x_{0},2R))}^{p^{\star}-p}<\frac{1}{2q^{p-1}}

and

. From (
3.11
), we get

(3.12)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\Omega}|\xi F(u)|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}|\xi F(v)|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \leq C\int_{\Omega}\left([F(u)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}+[F(v)|\nabla\xi|]^{p}\right)\mathrm{d}x.

Letting
k\to\infty
and taking the limit, we conclude

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p^{\star}}u^{qp^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p^{\star}}v^{qp^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \leq C\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla\xi|^{p}u^{qp}+|\nabla\xi|^{p}v^{qp})\mathrm{d}x

for any
q>1
. Noting that
\frac{p}{p^{\star}}<1
, we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p^{\star}}(u^{qp^{\star}}+v^{qp^{\star}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p^{\star}}u^{qp^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}\xi^{p^{\star}}v^{qp^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\xi|^{p}(u^{qp}+v^{qp})\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, for any
R\leq h<h^{\prime}<2R
, we can choose
\xi=1
in
B(x_{0},h)
, and
\xi=0
in
\Omega\setminus B(x_{0},h^{\prime})
,
0\leq\xi\leq 1
and
|\nabla\xi|<\frac{2}{h^{\prime}-h}
, and hence deduce from (

) that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(x_{0},h)}(u^{qp^{\star}}+v^{qp^{\star}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}\leq\frac{C}{(h^{\prime}-h)^{p}}\int_{B(x_{0},h^{\prime})}(u^{qp}+v^{qp})\mathrm{d}x,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,\,q>1,

which implies

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(x_{0},h)}(u^{qp^{\star}}+v^{qp^{\star}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{qp^{\star}}}\leq\left(\frac{C}{h^{\prime}-h}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B(x_{0},h^{\prime})}(u^{qp}+v^{qp})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{qp}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,\,q>1.

Let
\chi:=\frac{p^{\star}}{p}=\frac{N}{N-p}
, and define
r_{i}:=R+\frac{2R}{2^{i}}
for

. Then
r_{i}-r_{i+1}=\frac{R}{2^{i}}
. By taking

,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r_{i+1})}(u^{p\chi^{i+1}}+v^{p\chi^{i+1}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi^{i+1}}}\leq\left(\frac{C2^{i}}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{\chi^{i}}}\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r_{i})}(u^{p\chi^{i}}+v^{p\chi^{i}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi^{i}}}.

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r_{i+1})}(u^{p\chi^{i+1}}+v^{p\chi^{i+1}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi^{i+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{i}{\chi^{i}}}\left(\frac{C}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{\chi^{i}}}\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r_{i})}(u^{p\chi^{i}}+v^{p\chi^{i}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi^{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{k}{\chi^{k}}}\left(\frac{C}{R}\right)^{\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{1}{\chi^{k}}}\left(\int_{B(x_{0},2R)}(u^{p^{\star}}+v^{p^{\star}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\star}}}.

\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r_{i+1})}u^{p\chi^{i+1}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi^{i+1}}}\leq\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r_{i+1})}(u^{p\chi^{i+1}}+v^{p\chi^{i+1}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi^{i+1}}},

\left(\int_{B(x_{0},2R)}(u^{p^{\star}}+v^{p^{\star}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\star}}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(B(x_{0},2R))}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(B(x_{0},2R))}.

So we get

(3.16)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r_{i+1})}u^{p\chi^{i+1}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi^{i+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{k}{\chi^{k}}}\left(\frac{C}{R}\right)^{\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{1}{\chi^{k}}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(B(x_{0},2R))}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(B(x_{0},2R))}\right)

Noting that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{\chi^{k}}<\infty,

and

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\chi^{k}}=\frac{1}{\chi}\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{\chi}}=\frac{N-p}{p},

we can let

sufficiently large (depending on

) in (

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{r}(B(x_{0},R))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{\frac{N-p}{p}}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(B(x_{0},2R))}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(B(x_{0},2R))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{\frac{N-p}{p}}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right),

where

as
r\rightarrow+\infty
, and the constant
C>0
is independent of
r
and
R
. So we obtain from finite coving theorem that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C(|\Omega|)\,\left(\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,\,\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Thus
u\in L^{r}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
0<r<+\infty
. Arguing in the same way for

, we can also obtain

for any

.

By using the local boundedness and regularity estimates (c.f. e.g. Chapter 4 in
[

]

. Furthermore, by using the standard
C_{loc}^{1,\alpha}
-regularity estimates (c.f. e.g. Chapter 4 in
[
45
]
, see also
[
36
,
46
,
68
,
73
]
), we can deduce that
u,v\in C_{loc}^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
0<\alpha<\min\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\}
. This completes our proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

3.2.
A better preliminary estimate on upper bound of asymptotic behavior

The following result provides a better preliminary decay estimate for solution of the generalized equation (
3
), which is not sharp, but this estimate plays a key role in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(u,v)
be a pair of positive solutions to the equation (
3
) with
p>1
. Then, there is a
\sigma>0
and a constant
C>0
such that

u(x),v(x)\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p}+\sigma}}\quad\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Proof.

Let
R_{0}>0
large (to be determined later) and
q>1
. For any
R>r>R_{0}
, take
\xi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with
\xi=0
in
B_{r}(0)
,
\xi=1
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)
,
0\leq\xi\leq 1
, and
|\nabla\xi|\leq\frac{2}{(R-r)}
. Let
\phi=\xi^{p}u^{1+p(q-1)},\psi=\xi^{p}v^{1+p(q-1)}
. The equations (
3
) imply that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\phi\mathrm{d}x,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}\psi\mathrm{d}x.

Considering the first equation by Hölder’s inequality, one has

(3.19)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\cap\{u\leq v\}}v^{p^{\star}+p(q-1)}\xi^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\cap\{u>v\}}u^{p^{\star}+p(q-1)}\xi^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}v^{p^{\star}-p}v^{pq}\xi^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}u^{p^{\star}-p}u^{pq}\xi^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}})}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi u^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}})}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi v^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

On the other hand, noticing that

\displaystyle|\nabla(\xi u^{q})|^{p}

\displaystyle=|\nabla\xi u^{q}+qu^{q-1}\nabla u\xi|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C|\nabla\xi|^{p}u^{pq}+Cq^{p}u^{p(q-1)}|\nabla u|^{p}\xi^{p}.

Then we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=(1+p(q-1))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}u^{p(q-1)}|\nabla u|^{p}\xi^{p}\mathrm{d}x+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\xi^{p-1}u^{1+p(q-1)}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\xi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq(1+p(q-1))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}u^{p(q-1)}|\nabla u|^{p}\xi^{p}\mathrm{d}x-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\xi^{p-1}u^{(p-1)(q-1)+q}|\nabla u|^{p-1}|\nabla\xi|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq(1+p(q-1))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}u^{p(q-1)}|\nabla u|^{p}\xi^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-p\left(\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}u^{p(q-1)}|\nabla u|^{p}\xi^{p}\mathrm{d}x+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{p}u^{pq}\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1}}{q^{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla(\xi u^{q})|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{p}u^{pq}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{2}}{q^{p-1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi u^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\nabla\xi|^{p}u^{pq}\mathrm{d}x.

It follows from (
3.19
) with (
3.20
) that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{C_{2}}{q^{p-1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi u^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\nabla\xi|^{p}u^{pq}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}})}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi u^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}})}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi v^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}.

Similarly, we get

(3.22)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{C^{\prime}_{2}}{q^{p-1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi v^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}-C^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\nabla\xi|^{p}v^{pq}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}})}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi v^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}+\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}})}^{p^{\star}-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi u^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}.

Take
R_{0}
sufficiently large such that

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}})}^{p^{\star}-p}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{4q^{p-1}},\,\,\,\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}})}^{p^{\star}-p}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{2}}{4q^{p-1}}.

Rescaling the constants and combining (
3.21
) with (
3.22
), we can obtain that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi u^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\xi v^{q}|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq Cq^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}|\nabla\xi|^{p}(u^{pq}+v^{pq})\mathrm{d}x,

which implies

(3.24)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\left(u^{qp^{\star}}+v^{qp^{\star}}\right)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{qp^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq Cq^{\frac{p-1}{pq}}\left(\frac{2}{R-r}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}(u^{pq}+v^{pq})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{pq}},

for any
R>r>R_{0}
.

Let
\chi=\frac{p^{\star}}{p}=\frac{N}{N-p}>1
. For any
R>R_{0}
, define
r_{i}=2R-\frac{2R}{2^{i}}
for
i=1,2,\cdots
. Then
r_{i+1}-r_{i}=\frac{2R}{2^{i}}-\frac{2R}{2^{i+1}}=\frac{R}{2^{i}}
. Taking
R=r_{i+1}
,
r=r_{i}
and
q=\chi^{i}
in (
3.24
), we derive

(3.25)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{i+1}}}(u^{\chi^{i+1}p}+v^{\chi^{i+1}p})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{\chi^{i+1}p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\chi^{i})^{\frac{p-1}{p\chi^{i}}}\left(\frac{2}{r_{i+1}-r_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\chi^{i}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{i}}}(u^{p\chi^{i}}+v^{p\chi^{i}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi^{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\chi^{\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{i}{\chi^{i}}}\left(\frac{2^{i}}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{\chi^{i}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{i}}}(u^{p\chi^{i}}+v^{p\chi^{i}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi^{i}}}

for any
R>R_{0}
. By iteration, we can obtain from (
3.25
) that

(3.26)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{i+1}}}|u|^{\chi^{i+1}p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{\chi^{i+1}p}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{i+1}}}|v|^{\chi^{i+1}p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{\chi^{i+1}p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{i+1}}}(|u|^{\chi^{i+1}p}+|v|^{\chi^{i+1}p})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{\chi^{i+1}p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod^{i}_{k=1}\left[\chi^{\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{k}{\chi^{k}}}\left(\frac{2^{k}}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{\chi^{k}}}\right]\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}(u^{p\chi}+v^{p\chi})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p\chi}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(2\chi^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right)^{\sum\limits^{i}_{k=1}\frac{k}{\chi^{k}}}\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\sum\limits^{i}_{k=1}\frac{1}{\chi^{k}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}(u^{p^{\star}}+v^{p^{\star}})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(2\chi^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right)^{\sum\limits^{i}_{k=1}\frac{k}{\chi^{k}}}\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\sum\limits^{i}_{k=1}\frac{1}{\chi^{k}}}(\left\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}\right)

for any
R>R_{0}
. Since

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{\chi^{k}}<+\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{1}{\chi^{k}}<\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\chi^{k}}=\frac{1}{\chi}\frac{1}{1-\chi}=\frac{N-p}{p},

we have proved that for any
\overline{p}>p^{\star}
, there is a
R_{0}>0
large, depending on
\overline{p}
, such that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\quad\|u\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R})}+\|v\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{\frac{N-p}{p}-o_{\overline{p}}(1)}}(\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}),\,\quad\,\,\,\,\forall\,\,R>R_{0},

where
o_{\overline{p}}(1)\to 0
as
\overline{p}\to\infty
.

Next we estimate
\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}
and
\|v\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}
.

Take
\eta=\xi^{p}u
, where
\xi
satisfies
\xi=0
in
B_{R}
,
\xi=1
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R}
,
0\leq\xi\leq 1
, and
|\nabla\xi|\leq 2R^{-1}
. Then, similar to (
3.23
), we can derive that

(3.28)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi u|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\star}}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi v|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{p}u^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+C\left(\int|\nabla\xi|^{p}v^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=C\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}|\nabla\xi|^{p}u^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+C\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}|\nabla\xi|^{p}v^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{R^{p}}\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+C\left(\frac{1}{R^{p}}\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}v^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\frac{1}{R^{p}}\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}1\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p\frac{N}{N-p}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{N-p}{N}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left[\frac{1}{R^{p}}\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}1\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}v^{p\frac{N}{N-p}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{N-p}{N}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=C\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\star}}}+C\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}v^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\star}}}.

Thus we have

(3.29)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R}}|u|^{p^{\star}}+|v|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi u|^{p^{\star}}+|\xi v|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}|u|^{p^{\star}}+|v|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|u|^{p^{\star}}+|v|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R}}|u|^{p^{\star}}+|v|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x,

which implies

(3.30)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R}}|u|^{p^{\star}}+|v|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{C+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|u|^{p^{\star}}+|v|^{p^{\star}}\mathrm{d}x.

Set
\tau:=\frac{C}{C+1}<1
, and let
\Psi(R)=\|u\|^{p^{\star}}_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}+\|v\|^{p^{\star}}_{L^{p^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}
. So by (
3.30
),

(3.31)

\displaystyle\Psi(2R)\leq\tau\Psi(R),\,\,\quad\forall\,\,R\geq R_{0},

from which we get

(3.32)

\displaystyle\Psi(2^{k}R_{0})\leq\tau^{k}\Psi(R_{0}).

For any
|x|\geq R_{0}
, there is a
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, such that

2^{k}R_{0}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}R_{0},

which combined with the definition of
\Psi(R)
and (
3.32
) yield that

\Psi(|x|)\leq\Psi(2^{k}R_{0})\leq\tau^{k}\Psi(R_{0})\leq\tau^{\log_{2}|x|-\log_{2}(2R_{0})}\Psi(R_{0}).

Since

\tau^{\log_{2}|x|}=2^{\log_{2}\tau\cdot\log_{2}|x|}=|x|^{\log_{2}\tau},

we obtain

\Psi(|x|)\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{\log_{2}\frac{1}{\tau}}},

where
\log_{2}\frac{1}{\tau}>0
.
Thus, we have shown that there exists a
\sigma>0
independent of
\overline{p}>p^{\star}
such that

\Psi(|x|)\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{\sigma}}.

By rescaling the constants and fixing
\overline{p}>p^{\star}
sufficiently large such that
o_{\overline{p}}(1)<\frac{\sigma}{2p^{\star}}
, we obtain from (
3.27
) that

(3.33)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R})}+\|v\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R})}\leq\frac{C}{R^{\frac{N-p}{p}+\frac{\sigma}{2p^{\star}}}},\,\quad\,\forall\,\,R>R_{0}.

Consequently, there exists
q>\frac{N}{p}
and
\widetilde{R}>0
large enough, such that

\|u\|^{p^{\star}-p}_{L^{q(p^{\star}-p)}(B_{1}(x))}+\|v\|^{p^{\star}-p}_{L^{q(p^{\star}-p)}(B_{1}(x))}\leq\overline{C},\qquad\forall\,\,|x|>2\widetilde{R},

where the constant
\overline{C}>0
is independent of
x
and
\widetilde{R}
. Therefore, for any
x
such that
|x|=4R
with
R>\max\{R_{0},\widetilde{R}\}>1
, by the local boundedness estimates (c.f. e.g. Chapter 4 in
[
45
]
, see also
[
36
,
46
,
68
,
73
]
) and the decay property in (
3.33
), we have, for any fixed
\bar{p}\geq\hat{p}
large enough,

|u(x)|\leq\max_{y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)}|u(y)|\leq\widetilde{C}\|u\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(B_{1}(x))}\leq\widetilde{C}\|u\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R}(0))}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}C}{R^{\frac{N-p}{p}+\frac{\sigma}{2p^{\star}}}},

|v(x)|\leq\max_{y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)}|v(y)|\leq\widetilde{C}\|v\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(B_{1}(x))}\leq\widetilde{C}\|v\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R}(0))}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}C}{R^{\frac{N-p}{p}+\frac{\sigma}{2p^{\star}}}},

where the constants
C
and
\widetilde{C}=\widetilde{C}(N,p,\overline{C})
are independent of
x
and
R
. Combining this with Lemma
3.1
finish our proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We have proved in Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
that
u,v\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
(u,v)
satisfy a better preliminary asymptotic upper bound estimate.

Now, we are ready to prove the sharp asymptotic estimates on any positive weak solutions
(u,v)
to the system (
1
) and
|\nabla u|,|\nabla v|
in Theorem
1.3
. The proof of Theorem
1.3
will be divided into the following three steps.

Step 1.
We will show that there exists some constants
c_{0},C_{0}>0
such that

(3.34)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{1+|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\leq u(x),v(x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{1+|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We will show (
3.34
) by applying the following two key Lemmas
3.3
-
3.4
.

Lemma 3.3

(Theorem 1.5 in
[
73
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be an exterior domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\Omega^{c}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
is bounded and
function
V(x)\in L^{\frac{N}{p}}(\Omega)

satisfying
V(x)\leq K|x|^{-\gamma}
in
\Omega
for some positive constants
K
and
\gamma>p
. If
u\in D^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a weak subsolution to

-\Delta_{p}u=V(x)u^{p-1}\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\ \Omega

with
1<p<N
, then there exists a positive constant
C=C(N,p,\gamma,K)>0
such that

u(x)\leq CM|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{1}}(0),

where
M=\sup\limits_{\partial B_{R_{1}}(0)}u^{+}
and
R_{1}>1
is a constant depending on
N,p,K,\gamma
.

Lemma 3.4

(Theorem 2.3 in
[
64
]
)
.

Let
u
be a weak solution of

-\Delta_{p}u\geq 0\qquad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

with
\Omega
compact and
1<p<N
. Then there exist positive constants
m=\inf\limits_{\partial(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}u
and
C=\inf\limits_{\partial(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}
such that

u(x)\geq Cm|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\qquad\mbox{in}\,\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

Obviously, we can deduce the sharp lower bound in (
3.34
) for positive weak solutions
u,v
of (
1
) from Lemma
3.4
.

Defining that

f(u,v):=u^{\alpha-p+1}v^{\beta},\ \ g(u,v):=u^{\beta}v^{\alpha-p+1}.

We will show that
f,g
satisfy the two conditions in Lemma
3.3
, i.e.
f(u,v),g(u,v)\in L^{\frac{N}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

(3.35)

f(u,v),g(u,v)\leq K|x|^{-\gamma}

for some
K>0
,
\gamma>p
and
|x|
large. For the sake of simplicity, we only carry out the proof for
f
,
g
can be handled similarly. One can carry out the proof into two cases.

Case (i)
.
\alpha-p+1\geq 0.
It is obviously that

f(u,v)=u^{\alpha-p+1}v^{\beta}\leq u^{p^{\star}-p}+v^{p^{\star}-p}.

Since the positive weak solutions
u,v
of (
1
) belong to
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
it follows that
u^{p^{\star}-p},v^{p^{\star}-p}\in L^{\frac{N}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

which implies
f(u,v)\in L^{\frac{N}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Recalling that
\frac{N-p}{p}(p^{\star}-p)=p
and the better preliminary estimate in Lemma
3.2
,
one has
f(u,v)\leq K|x|^{-\gamma}
for some
K>0
,
\gamma>p
and
|x|
large.

Case (ii)
.
\alpha-p+1<0.
We will show that for any small
\theta>0
,

(3.36)

u(x),v(x)\leq C|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}+\theta}\,\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,\,|x|>1,

where some positive constant
C
depends on
\theta
.
According to direct calculations, for any
\hat{\delta}\in(0,\frac{N-p}{p-1}),
there exists a constant
c>0
such that

-\Delta_{p}\frac{1}{|x|^{\hat{\delta}}}=\frac{c}{|x|^{\hat{\delta}+2+(p-2)(\hat{\delta}+1)}}.

On the other hand, it is easy to check that if
\delta>\frac{N-p}{p},
then

\delta+2+(p-2)(\delta+1)<(p^{\star}-1)\delta.

So, we can find a small
\overline{\theta}>0
, such that for
|x|>1,

-\Delta_{p}\left[\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p}+\sigma+\overline{\theta}}}\right]=\frac{c}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p}+\sigma+\overline{\theta}+2+(p-2)(\frac{N-p}{p}+\sigma+\overline{\theta}+1)}}\geq\frac{c}{|x|^{(p^{\star}-1)(\frac{N-p}{p}+\sigma)}}.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
,

-\Delta_{p}u=u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{(p^{\star}-1)(\frac{N-p}{p}+\sigma)}},\qquad|x|\geq R_{0}.

By the comparison principle, we have

u(x)\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p}+\sigma+\overline{\theta}}}.

We continue this procedure up to
i,
with
\frac{N-p}{p}+\sigma+i\overline{\theta}<\frac{N-p}{p-1}.
So, for any
\theta>0
small,
(
3.36
) follows.
By Lemma
3.4
and (
3.36
), for
\theta>0
sufficiently small and
\alpha-p+1<0
, we have

\displaystyle f(u,v)=u^{\alpha-p+1}v^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}(\alpha-p+1)}}\cdot\frac{1}{|x|^{(\frac{N-p}{p-1}-\theta)\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}(p^{\star}-p)-\theta\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p}(p^{\star}-p)+\frac{N-p}{p(p-1)}(p^{\star}-p)-\theta\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p}(p^{\star}-p)+\sigma^{\prime}}}=\frac{C}{|x|^{p+\sigma^{\prime}}},

where
\sigma^{\prime}>0
for suitable
\theta
. Recalling
N\geq 2,
it is easy to know that
f(u,v)\in L^{\frac{N}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),f(u,v)\leq K|x|^{-\gamma}
for some
K>0
,
\gamma>p
and
|x|
large.

Consequently, we deduce the desired sharp upper bound in (
3.34
) from Lemma
3.3
and the sharp lower bound in (
3.34
) from Lemma
3.4
, respectively. This proves the sharp asymptotic estimates (
1.14
) for
u,v
in Theorem
1.3
.

Next, in Steps 2 and 3, we will prove the sharp asymptotic estimates (
1.15
) for
|\nabla u|,|\nabla v|
in Theorem
1.3
by using the arguments in
[
59
,
71
]
.

Step 2.

We are to show that, for some constant
C^{\prime}_{0}>0
,

|\nabla u(x)|,|\nabla v(x)|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{0}}{|x|^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,|x|>R_{0}\geq 1.

For any
R>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define

u_{R}(y):=R^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u(Ry).

Firstly, considering the first equation of system (
1
), we deduce that

(3.37)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{R}(y)=-R^{N}\Delta_{p}u(x)=R^{p}f(u(Ry),v(Ry))u^{p-1}_{R}(y)\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}.

It follows from
f(u(Ry),v(Ry))\leq K|x|^{-\gamma}
with
\gamma>p
that

(3.38)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{R}(y)

\displaystyle=R^{p}f(u(Ry),v(Ry))u^{p-1}_{R}(y)\leq\frac{K}{R^{\gamma-p}|y|^{\gamma}}u^{p-1}_{R}(y)\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,R>R_{0}\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,|y|>1,

which implies that

(3.39)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{R}(y)\leq Ku^{p-1}_{R}(y)\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}.

On the other hand, according to (
3.34
), we have, for any
R\geq R_{0}
,

(3.40)

\displaystyle u_{R}(y)=R^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u(Ry)\leq C_{0}R^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}|Ry|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}}=C_{0}|y|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\leq C_{0}\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}.

By (
3.39
)–(
3.40
) and the regularity estimates in DiBenedetto
[
36
]
and Tolksdorf
[
68
]
, we have

(3.41)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,4)\setminus B(0,2))}\leq C_{1}

for some constant
C_{1}>0
depending on
C_{0}
. Finally, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,3R)
, by applying (
3.41
) with
R=\frac{|x|}{3}
and
y\in B(0,4)\setminus B(0,2)
, we obtain

(3.42)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}u(x)|=R^{\frac{1-N}{p-1}}|\nabla_{y}u_{R}(y)|\leq C_{1}|x|^{\frac{1-N}{p-1}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,|x|\geq 3R_{0}.

Arguing in the same way, we can derive the same conclusion for
v
.

Step 3.

We will show that, for some constant
c^{\prime}_{0}
,

|\nabla u(x)|,|\nabla v(x)|\geq\frac{c^{\prime}_{0}}{|x|^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}}\quad\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,|x|>R_{0}.

The proof will be carried out by contradiction arguments. We assume on the contrary that there exist sequences of radii
R_{n}
and points
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
R_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
and
|x_{n}|=R_{n}
, such that

(3.43)

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x_{n})|\leq\frac{\theta_{n}}{R_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}}

with
\theta_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. For
0<a<A
fixed, we set

u_{R_{n}}(x):=R_{n}^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u(R_{n}x),\qquad\forall\,\,x\in\overline{B_{A}\setminus B_{a}}.

It follows from (
3.34
) that for any
n
large enough such that
|R_{n}x|\geq R_{n}a>R_{0}
, we have

(3.44)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\leq u_{R_{n}}(x)=R_{n}^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u(R_{n}x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}},

which implies that

(3.45)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{A^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\leq u_{R_{n}}(x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{a^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\overline{B_{A}\setminus B_{a}},

and in particular,

(3.46)

\displaystyle u_{R_{n}}(x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{A^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\,\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle\mbox{on}\,\,\partial B_{A},

\displaystyle u_{R_{n}}(x)\geq\frac{c_{0}}{a^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\,\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle\mbox{on}\,\,\partial B_{a}.

On the other hand, similar to (
3.38
), we obtain

(3.47)

\displaystyle 0\leq-\Delta_{p}u_{R_{n}}(x)\leq\frac{K}{a^{\gamma}{R_{n}}^{\gamma-p}}u^{p-1}_{R_{n}}(x)\leq Ku^{p-1}_{R_{n}}(x)\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\overline{B_{A}\setminus B_{a}}

for any
n
large enough such that
|R_{n}x|\geq R_{n}a>R_{0}
and
a^{\gamma}{R_{n}}^{\gamma-p}\geq 1
. Therefore, from (
3.47
) and (
3.45
), by the regularity results in
[
68
]
, we get that

\|u_{R_{n}}\|_{C^{1,\theta}(K)}<+\infty\,\,\,\,\quad\mbox{for}\,\,0<\theta<1

for any compact set
K\subset B_{A}\setminus B_{a}
. Without loss of generality, we may redefine
a
and
A
and assume that the
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates hold in
\overline{B_{A}\setminus B_{a}}
. Hence, up to subsequences, we have

(3.48)

\displaystyle u_{R_{n}}(x)\to u_{a,A}(x),\,\quad\mbox{as}\,\,n\to\infty\,\,\,\,\,\quad\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}(B_{A}\setminus B_{a})

for
0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
. From (
3.47
), we deduce that

(3.49)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{a,A}(x)=0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,B_{A}\setminus\overline{B_{a}}.

Now, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, let
a_{j}=\frac{1}{j}
and
A_{j}=j
and we constructs
u_{a_{j},A_{j}}
by reasoning as above. Then, as
j\to\infty
, a diagonal argument implies that there exist a limiting profile
u_{\infty}
such that

u_{\infty}\equiv u_{a_{j},A_{j}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,B_{A_{j}}\setminus B_{a_{j}},\quad\forall\,\,j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}

In particular,

(3.50)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{\infty}(x)=0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

From (
3.46
), which holds with
a=a_{j}
and
A=A_{j}
, it follows that

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u_{\infty}(x)=0\,\,\,\,\quad\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\quad\lim_{|x|\to 0}u_{\infty}(x)=\infty.

Thus it follows from the uniqueness up to multipliers of
p
-harmonic maps in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
under suitable conditions at zero and at infinity in
[
59
, Theorem 2.1]
that

u_{\infty}(x)=\frac{C}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}

for some constants
C
.
Let the sequence
x_{n}
be the same as in (
3.43
) and set
y_{n}=\frac{x_{n}}{R_{n}}
. Then, by (
3.43
), it follows that

|\nabla u_{R_{n}}(y_{n})|={R_{n}}^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}|\nabla u(x_{n})|\leq\theta_{n}\to 0,\quad\,\,\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,n\to\infty.

Since
|y_{n}|=1
, up to subsequences, we have that
y_{n}\to\overline{y}\in\partial B_{1}
.
Consequently, by the uniform convergence of the gradient indicated by (
3.48
), one has

\nabla u_{\infty}(\overline{y})=0,

which is a contradiction since the fundamental solution
u_{\infty}
has no critical points. This establishes the sharp asymptotic lower bound estimates for
|\nabla u|
. The sharp lower bound estimates for
|\nabla v|
can be proved similarly.

Therefore, we have derived the sharp asymptotic estimates (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) in Theorem
1.3
, and hence finished our proof of Theorem
1.3
.
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\square

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
: radial symmetry and monotonicity of solutions

Thanks to the sharp asymptotic estimates (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) in Theorem
1.3
, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
. In this section, using the method of moving planes, we will show the radial symmetry and strictly radial monotonicity of positive
D^{1,p}
-weak solutions to the
D^{1,p}
-critical equations (
1
) by discussing the singular elliptic case
1<p<2
and the degenerate elliptic case
p>2
separately, and we complete the proof by discussing the uniqueness in the end. So we will divide this section into three sub-sections.

Before carrying out the moving planes technique to prove Theorem
1.4
, we need some standard notations.

Let
\nu
be any direction in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
|\nu|=1
, for arbitrary
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid x\cdot\nu<\lambda\},

T_{\lambda}^{\nu}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid x\cdot\nu=\lambda\},

u^{\nu}_{\lambda}(x):=u(x^{\nu}_{\lambda}),\quad\forall\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

and define the reflection
R^{\nu}_{\lambda}
w.r.t. the hyperplane
T^{\nu}_{\lambda}
by

x_{\lambda}^{\nu}:=R^{\nu}_{\lambda}(x)=x+2(\lambda-x\cdot\nu)\nu,\quad\,\,\,\,\forall\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We denote the critical sets that
Z_{u}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|\nabla u(x)|=0\},Z_{v}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|\nabla v(x)|=0\}
and define

(4.1)

Z_{\lambda}^{\nu}(u):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|\nabla u(x)|=|\nabla u_{\lambda}^{\nu}(x)|=0\}\subseteq Z_{u},

(4.2)

Z_{\lambda}^{\nu}(v):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|\nabla v(x)|=|\nabla v_{\lambda}^{\nu}(x)|=0\}\subseteq Z_{v}.

Besides, we shall exploit the following lemma from
[
38
]

Lemma 4.1
.

Let us consider
h(t)=t^{s}
with
s>0
and
b\in[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]
with
\theta_{i}>0
. Then, for
0<a\leq b
, we have

\frac{h(b)-h(a)}{b-a}\leq\overline{C}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}).

4.1.
The degenerate elliptic case
2<p<N

Since equation (
1
) is invariant under any rotations and translations, we may fix
\nu=e_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)
and use the notations
\Sigma_{\lambda}:=\Sigma_{\lambda}^{e_{1}}
,
T_{\lambda}:=T_{\lambda}^{e_{1}}
and
x_{\lambda}:=x_{\lambda}^{e_{1}}
for the sake of simplicity.

Next, we define

\Lambda:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid u\leq u_{\mu}\,\,and\,\,v\leq v_{\mu}\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Sigma_{\mu},\,\,\,\forall\,\,\mu\leq\lambda\},

and, if
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
, we set

\lambda_{0}:=\sup\Lambda.

Now we will prove the radial symmetry and strictly radial monotonicity in Theorem
1.4
for
2<p<N
.

Proof.

We start moving planes from the left-hand side of the
e_{1}
-direction until its limiting position. The moving planes procedure consists of three main steps.

Step 1.
We will prove that
u\leq u_{\lambda}
and
v\leq v_{\lambda}
for
\lambda<0
with
|\lambda|
large, and hence
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
.

We may choose
(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\chi_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}
and
(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\chi_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}
as the test functions in the definition of
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-weak solution of equation (
1
) (see Definition
1.1
) and get

\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x,

\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\langle\nabla v,\nabla(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x.

Since
(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})
satisfies the equations (
1
) as well, analogously we have

\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla u_{\lambda},\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}u^{\alpha}_{\lambda}v^{\beta}_{\lambda}(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x,

\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|\nabla v_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla v_{\lambda},\nabla(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}u^{\beta}_{\lambda}v^{\alpha}_{\lambda}(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x.

By subtracting between the above two formulae, we obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\lambda},\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}-u^{\alpha}_{\lambda}v^{\beta}_{\lambda})(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x,

and

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\langle|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v-|\nabla v_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{\lambda},\nabla(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}-u^{\beta}_{\lambda}v^{\alpha}_{\lambda})(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x.

Exploiting (
2.1
), we arrive at

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}-u^{\alpha}_{\lambda}v^{\beta}_{\lambda})(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(|\nabla v|+|\nabla v_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}-u^{\beta}_{\lambda}v^{\alpha}_{\lambda})(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x.

Let us consider (
4.5
) first. Using the fact that
u\geq u_{\lambda}
in the support of
(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}
, we get

(4.7)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}-u^{\alpha}_{\lambda}v^{\beta}_{\lambda})(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(u^{\alpha}-u^{\alpha}_{\lambda})v^{\beta}(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}u^{\alpha}_{\lambda}(v^{\beta}-v^{\beta}_{\lambda})(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(u^{\alpha}-u^{\alpha}_{\lambda})v^{\beta}(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}u^{\alpha}\frac{v^{\beta}-v^{\beta}_{\lambda}}{v-v_{\lambda}}(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Similarly, we obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}-u^{\beta}_{\lambda}v^{\alpha}_{\lambda})(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(v^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}_{\lambda})u^{\beta}(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}v^{\alpha}_{\lambda}(u^{\beta}-u^{\beta}_{\lambda})(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(v^{\alpha}-v^{\alpha}_{\lambda})u^{\beta}(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\frac{u^{\beta}-u^{\beta}_{\lambda}}{u-u_{\lambda}}v^{\alpha}(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

First, we estimate
I_{1}
. From Theorem
1.3
, we obtain
c_{0}\leq|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u,|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}}v\leq C_{0}
in
\Sigma_{\lambda}
for large
|\lambda|
. By Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\frac{(|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u)^{\alpha}-(|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u_{\lambda})^{\alpha}}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u-|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u_{\lambda}}\leq\overline{C}_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{(|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}v)^{\beta}-(|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}v_{\lambda})^{\beta}}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}v-|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}v_{\lambda}}\leq\overline{C}_{2},

where
\overline{C}_{1}:=\overline{C}(c_{0},C_{0},\alpha),\overline{C}_{2}:=\overline{C}(c_{0},C_{0},\beta).
Then we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}\beta}|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}\alpha}\frac{(|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u)^{\alpha}-(|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u_{\lambda})^{\alpha}}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u-|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u_{\lambda}}|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}[(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}]^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}(p^{\star}-2)}\frac{(|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u)^{\alpha}-(|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u_{\lambda})^{\alpha}}{|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u-|x|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}u_{\lambda}}[(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}]^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\overline{C}_{1}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}(p^{\star}-2)}[(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}]^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Now, in order to apply the weighted Hardy–Sobolev inequality (Lemma
2.2
), we set

s:=-\frac{N-1}{p-1}(p-2)\quad\,\,\mbox{and}\quad\,\,\beta_{1}:=(p^{\star}-2)\frac{N-p}{p-1}+s-2.

Notice that

s:=-\frac{N-1}{p-1}(p-2)=-\left(1-\frac{1}{p-1}\right)(N-1)>2-N

and

\beta_{1}>0.

(4.10)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\overline{C}_{1}\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|x|^{s-2}[(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}]^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\overline{C}_{1}\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|x|^{s}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}\overline{C}_{1}}{(c^{\prime}_{0})^{p-2}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}\overline{C}_{1}}{(c^{\prime}_{0})^{p-2}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\left(|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{\lambda}|\right)^{p-2}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

where
c^{\prime}_{0}
is given by Theorem
1.3
. In the same way, we obtain

(4.11)

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq\frac{C_{0}\overline{C}_{1}}{(c^{\prime}_{0})^{p-2}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\left(|\nabla v|+|\nabla v_{\lambda}|\right)^{p-2}|\nabla(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Now we estimate
I_{2}
. By the same way as estimates of
I_{1}
, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\overline{C}_{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}(p^{\star}-2)}(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}\overline{C}_{2}}{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}(p^{\star}-2)}[(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}]^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}|x|^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}(p^{\star}-2)}[(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}]^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}\overline{C}_{2}}{2(c^{\prime}_{0})^{p-2}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\cdot\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C_{0}\overline{C}_{2}}{2(c^{\prime}_{0})^{p-2}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\cdot\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(|\nabla v|+|\nabla v_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

At the end of the above inequality, we use the conclusion from
I_{1}
. Similarly, we have the estimates

(4.13)

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}\overline{C}_{2}}{2(c^{\prime}_{0})^{p-2}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\cdot\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C_{0}\overline{C}_{2}}{2(c^{\prime}_{0})^{p-2}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\cdot\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(|\nabla v|+|\nabla v_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Combining with (
4.5
)- (
4.8
), (
4.10
)- (
4.13
), we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(|\nabla v|+|\nabla v_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}(\overline{C}_{1}+\overline{C}_{2})}{C^{\prime}(c^{\prime}_{0})^{p-2}|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\left(|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{\lambda}|\right)^{p-2}|\nabla(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C_{0}(\overline{C}_{1}+\overline{C}_{2})}{C^{\prime}(c^{\prime}_{0})^{p-2}|\lambda|^{\beta_{1}}}\left(\frac{2}{N+s-2}\right)^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(|\nabla v|+|\nabla v_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla(v-v_{\lambda})^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

where the positive constants
C_{0}
and
c_{0}
come from the sharp asymptotic estimates (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) in Theorem
1.3
, and
C^{\prime}
comes from the basic point-wise inequalities in Lemma
2.1
. For

implies that, there exists
\theta_{1}=\theta_{1}(\lambda_{0},\nu_{0},K)<\theta_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle u-u^{\nu}_{\lambda}>\frac{m}{2}>0,\ v-v^{\nu}_{\lambda}>\frac{m}{2}>0\qquad\,\mbox{in}\,K_{0},

\displaystyle M_{O\cap\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu}}^{\lambda,\nu}<M,

\displaystyle|B\cap B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R)\cap\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu}|<\frac{\delta}{2},

\displaystyle|O\cap B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R)\cap\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu}|<\frac{\delta}{2},

\displaystyle(B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R)\cap\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu})\subset(B(P_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu_{0}},\overline{R})\cap\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu_{0}})\cup B

for all
(\lambda,\nu)\in(\lambda_{0}-\theta_{1},\lambda_{0}+\theta_{1})\times I_{\theta_{1}}(\nu_{0})
, where
R=R(\lambda_{0},\nu_{0})
is given by Lemma
4.3

(ii)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\quad(supp(u^{\nu}_{\lambda}-u)^{+}\cup supp(v^{\nu}_{\lambda}-v)^{+})\cap B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R)

\displaystyle\subset(B\cup O)\cap B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R)

\displaystyle=(B\cap B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R))\cup(O\cap B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R))

for all
(\lambda,\nu)\in(\lambda_{0}-\theta_{1},\lambda_{0}+\theta_{1})\times I_{\theta_{1}}(\nu_{0})
, which implies that

\displaystyle\quad(supp(u^{\nu}_{\lambda}-u)^{+}\cup supp(v^{\nu}_{\lambda}-v)^{+})\cap B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R)\cap\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu}

\displaystyle\subset(B\cap B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R)\cap\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu})\cup(O\cap B(P_{\lambda}^{\nu},R)\cap\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu})

satisfies the conditions of Lemma
4.3

(ii)
. Therefore, we derive

u\geq u^{\nu}_{\lambda},\ v\geq v^{\nu}_{\lambda}\quad\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu}

for all
(\lambda,\nu)\in(\lambda_{0}-\theta_{1},\lambda_{0}+\theta_{1})\times I_{\theta_{1}}(\nu_{0})
. This completes our proof of Lemma
4.5
.

4.2.2.
Proof of the radial symmetry and strictly radial monotonicity

In this subsection, we are to complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
in the singular elliptic case
1<p<2
.

Proof.

Along any direction
\nu
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we start moving the planes
T^{\nu}_{\lambda}
from
\lambda
near
+\infty
until its limiting position
T^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
.

Step 1.
We will show that
\overline{\Lambda}(\nu)\neq\emptyset
and
\lambda_{0}(\nu)
is well-defined and finite.

We claim that
\overline{\Lambda}(\nu)\supset(R_{1},+\infty)
, where
R_{1}=R_{1}(N,p,R_{0})>0
is given by Lemma
4.3

(i)
. If
\lambda>R_{1}
,

\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu}\cap R_{\lambda}^{\nu}(B_{R_{1}})=\emptyset.

Hence using Lemma
4.3

(i)
, we have

u\geq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,v\geq v^{\nu}_{\lambda}\,\quad\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu}

for any
\lambda\in\overline{\Lambda}(\nu)
and
\lambda>R_{1}
.

Step 2.
We are to prove
u\equiv u^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
and
v\equiv v^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
in
\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
.

At the limiting position
T^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
, by continuity, we have
u\geq u^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
in
\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
. Next, by the strong comparison principle in Lemma
2.4
and the fact that

(4.39)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u=u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\geq(u^{\nu}_{\lambda})^{\alpha}(v^{\nu}_{\lambda})^{\beta}=-\Delta_{p}u^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\quad\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)},

we deduce that, either
u>u^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
or
u=u^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
in all
\mathcal{C}^{\nu}
, where
\mathcal{C}^{\nu}
is a connected component of
\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\setminus Z^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}(u)
.

Noting that the sharp lower bound of
|\nabla u|
in Theorems
1.3
indicates that the critical set
Z_{u}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|\nabla u(x)|=0\}
of the solution
u
is a closed set belonging to
B_{R_{0}}(0)
, i.e.,

Z^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}(u)\subset Z_{u}\subset B_{R_{0}}.

Meanwhile, it follows from Corollary
2.7
that
|Z_{u}|=0
(see Remark
2.8
), and from Lemma
2.9
that
\Omega\setminus Z_{u}
is connected for any smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with connected boundary such that
B_{R_{0}}(0)\subseteq\Omega
. As a consequence, one has
\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\setminus Z_{u}
is connected. Due to the closeness of
Z_{u}
and the fact that
|Z_{u}|=0
, we can deduce that
\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\setminus Z^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}(u)
is connected.

If
u\equiv u^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}}
in
\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\setminus Z^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}(u)
, then Step 2 is finished. Otherwise, we assume on the contrary that

(4.40)

\displaystyle u>u^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\quad\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\setminus Z^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}(u),

then Lemma
4.5
implies that
u\geq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}
in
\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\nu}
for any
\lambda\in(\lambda_{0}(\nu)-\theta_{1},\lambda_{0}(\nu))
with
\theta_{1}>0
small, which contradicts the definition of
\lambda_{0}(\nu)
. As a consequence, we have proved that

u\equiv u^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\,\,\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\setminus Z^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}(u).

Furthermore, by the definition of
Z^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}(u)
and
u\in C_{loc}^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it follows that

(4.41)

\displaystyle u\equiv u^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\quad\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}.

On the other hand, according to the strong comparison principle Lemma
2.4
, one knows that

u>u^{\nu}_{\lambda}
in
\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda}\setminus Z^{\nu}_{\lambda(\nu)}(u)
for any
\lambda>\lambda_{0}(\nu)
. Consequently,
u
is strictly monotone increasing in
\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\setminus Z^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}(u)
for any direction
\nu
, and hence
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\geq 0
in
\Sigma^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}\setminus Z^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}(u)
. With the same argument, we have the same conclusion about
v
.

Step 3.
We will show
u
and
v
are radial and (strictly) radially decreasing about some point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

It is known that for any direction
\nu
,
u,v
are symmetric about the plane
T^{\nu}_{\lambda_{0}(\nu)}
. Now consider all the
N
orthogonal directions
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and one can obtain in entirely similar way that
u,v
are strictly increasing in each direction
e_{k}
in
\Sigma^{e_{k}}_{\lambda_{0}(e_{k})}
and symmetric about the plane
T^{e_{k}}_{\lambda_{0}(e_{k})}
. This shows that
u,v
are symmetric with respect to a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

(x_{0}=\bigcap^{N}_{k=1}T^{e_{k}}_{\lambda_{0}(e_{k})})
, which is the unique critical point for
u,v
. Furthermore, by exploiting the moving plane procedure in any direction
\nu\in S^{N-1}
, we finally get that
u,v
are radial and (strictly) radially decreasing w.r.t.
x_{0}
. That is, positive solutions
u,v
must assume the form

u(x)=u(|x-x_{0}|)=u(r),v(x)=v(|x-x_{0}|)=v(r)

some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
(u(r),v(r))
with
r=|x-x_{0}|
are the positive radial solutions to (
1
) with
u^{\prime}(0)=v^{\prime}(0)=0
and
u^{\prime}(r)\leq 0,v^{\prime}(r)\leq 0
for any
r>0
.

Next, for
1<p<2
, we will prove that

(4.42)

u^{\prime}(r)<0,\,\,v^{\prime}(r)<0,\quad\,\,\,\forall\,\,r>0.

We will prove (
4.42
) by contradiction arguments. We assume on the contrary that there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
u^{\prime}(r_{0})=0
. Since Lemma
2.7
and Remark
2.8
implies that
|Z_{u}|=0
, one has
u^{\prime}(r)<0
for
r\in(0,r_{0})\cup(r_{0},+\infty)
, so
u(r)>u(r_{0})
in
[0,r_{0})
. Using the Strong Maximum Principle and Höpf’s Lemma in Lemma
2.5
(c.f.
[
70
]
, see also Theorem 2.1 in
[
32
]
, Theorem 2.4 in
[
29
]
or
[
26
,
57
]
) to the positive radial solution
\tilde{u}(x):=u(x)-u(r_{0})=u(|x|)-u(r_{0})
of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}\tilde{u}=u^{\alpha}(r)v^{\beta}(r)>0&\mbox{in}\,\,B_{r_{0}},\\
\tilde{u}>0&\mbox{in}\,\,B_{r_{0}},\\
\tilde{u}=0&\mbox{on}\,\,\partial B_{r_{0}},\end{array}\right.\hskip 28.45274pt

we obtain
\tilde{u}^{\prime}(r_{0})=u^{\prime}(r_{0})<0
, which is absurd. Hence (
4.42
) holds, i.e.,
u^{\prime}(r)<0
for any
r>0
. Similarly, we have
v^{\prime}(r)<0
for any
r>0
as well.

∎

4.3.
Proof of the uniqueness and classification result

Proof.

Let
(u,v)
be a pair of solutions to system (
1
). In subsections 4.1 and 4.2, we have proved that
u
and
v
are radially symmetric and monotone decreasing about some point
x_{0}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{0}=0
.

Since our solution
u
is radially symmetric, we can write the first equation in (
1
) as

\frac{-(r^{N-1}|u^{\prime}(r)|^{p-2}u^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}}{r^{N-1}}=u^{\alpha}(r)v^{\beta}(r),

where
r=|x|
.
Recalling that
u^{\prime}(r)<0
and
v^{\prime}(r)<0
, we get

(4.43)

\displaystyle(r^{N-1}(-u^{\prime}(r))^{p-1})^{\prime}=r^{N-1}u^{\alpha}(r)v^{\beta}(r).

Integrating both sides of the above equation from
0
to
r
yields

(4.44)

\displaystyle-u^{\prime}(r)=\left(\frac{1}{r^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{r}\tau^{N-1}u^{\alpha}(\tau)v^{\beta}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

which implies

(4.45)

\displaystyle u(0)-u(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{s^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{s}\tau^{N-1}u^{\alpha}(\tau)v^{\beta}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\mathrm{d}s.

Similarly, we have

(4.46)

\displaystyle-v^{\prime}(r)=\left(\frac{1}{r^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{r}\tau^{N-1}v^{\alpha}(\tau)u^{\beta}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

which implies

(4.47)

\displaystyle v(0)-v(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{s^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{s}\tau^{N-1}u^{\beta}(\tau)v^{\alpha}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\mathrm{d}s.

Now we show that
u(0)=v(0)
. Otherwise, we may suppose that

(4.48)

\displaystyle u(0)<v(0).

Then by continuity, for small
r>0
,

(4.49)

\displaystyle u(r)<v(r).

In other words, there exists an
R>0
, such that

(4.50)

\displaystyle u(r)<v(r),\qquad\forall\,\,\,r\in\,(0,R).

Let us define

(4.51)

\displaystyle R_{0}:=\sup\{R>0|\,u(r)<v(r),\ \ \forall\,0<r<R\}.

Then
0<R_{0}\leq+\infty
and
u(R_{0})=v(R_{0})
, since
u(+\infty)=v(+\infty)=0
by Theorem
1.3
. By the definition of
R_{0}
and
\alpha\leq\beta
, we have that

u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\geq u^{\beta}v^{\alpha},\qquad\forall\,\,r\,\,\in\,\,(0,R_{0}).

By (
4.45
) and (
4.47
), we have

(4.52)

\displaystyle 0>u(0)-v(0)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{R_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{s^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{s}\tau^{N-1}u^{\alpha}v^{\beta}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{0}^{R_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{s^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{s}\tau^{N-1}u^{\beta}v^{\alpha}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\mathrm{d}s\geq 0.

This is absurd. Similarly, we can show that
u(0)>v(0)
is also impossible. Therefore, we must have
u(0)=v(0)
.

Now we aim to show that
u\equiv v
by using contradiction argument. Assume on the contrary that
v\neq u
. Define

(4.53)

\displaystyle r_{0}:=\sup\{r\geq 0|\,u(s)=v(s),\ \ \forall\,s\leq r\}.

From
u(0)=v(0)
and
u\neq v
, we know
0\leq r_{0}<+\infty
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that, there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
small enough, such that
u(r)>v(r)
and
u^{\prime}(r)>v^{\prime}(r)
for any
r\in(r_{0},r_{0}+\epsilon_{0})
. Let us define

(4.54)

\displaystyle R_{*}:=\sup\{R>0|\,u(r)>v(r),\ \ \forall\,r_{0}<r<R\}.

Then
r_{0}+\epsilon_{0}\leq R_{*}\leq+\infty
and
u(R_{*})=v(R_{*})
, since
u(+\infty)=v(+\infty)=0
by Theorem
1.3
. We will show that
R_{*}=+\infty
.

By subtracting equation (
4.46
) from equation (
4.44
), we can get, for any
r\in(r_{0},R_{*})
,

(4.55)

\displaystyle\quad\quad u^{\prime}(r)-v^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{r^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{r}\tau^{N-1}v^{\alpha}(\tau)u^{\beta}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}-\left(\frac{1}{r^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{r}\tau^{N-1}u^{\alpha}(\tau)v^{\beta}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\geq 0,

and
u^{\prime}(r)-v^{\prime}(r)
is monotone increasing w.r.t.
r
on
(r_{0},R_{*})
, and hence

(4.56)

u^{\prime}(r)-v^{\prime}(r)\geq u^{\prime}\left(r_{0}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\right)-v^{\prime}\left(r_{0}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\right)=:\delta_{0}>0,\qquad\forall\,r\in\Big[r_{0}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2},R_{*}\Big).

If
R_{*}<+\infty
, then
u(R_{*})-v(R_{*})\geq u(r_{0}+\epsilon_{0})-v(r_{0}+\epsilon_{0})>0
, which contradicts with
u(R_{*})=v(R_{*})
. Thus we must have
R_{*}=+\infty
.

By Theorem
1.3
and (
4.56
), we derive that, for any
r\in[r_{0}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2},+\infty)
,

(4.57)

\displaystyle 0<\delta_{0}\leq u^{\prime}(r)-v^{\prime}(r)\leq\frac{C}{r^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}}\rightarrow 0,\qquad\text{as}\,\,r\rightarrow+\infty,

which is absurd. Thus we must have
u\equiv v
, and
u
solves the single
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-critical
p
-Laplacian equation

(4.58)

-\Delta_{p}u=u^{p^{\star}-1}\qquad\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}.

From the classification results for (
4.58
) in
[
29
,
59
,
71
]
, we deduce that positive solutions
(u,v)
must assume the form

u(x)=v(x)=\left[\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(N^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right)}{\lambda^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}\right]^{\frac{N-p}{p}}.

This concludes our proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎
