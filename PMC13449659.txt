Recruitment strategies in the PROMISE Registry: Evaluating institutional outreach, direct-to-patient marketing, and community engagement approaches in a decentralized germline genetic registry

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe PROMISE Registry is a U.S. decentralized, 20-year prospective prostate cancer germline genetic registry. Here, we examine the effectiveness of different recruitment strategies, including institutional partnerships, direct-to-patient marketing outreach, and community engagement grants (CEGs).MethodsRecruitment efforts were categorized into: (1) Institutional outreach through physician referrals, (2) Direct-to-patient marketing outreach, through online vehicles, including a PROMISE website and sponsored content, webinars, media relations, and other efforts to drive traffic to this site. Events and conferences were used on a limited basis, and (3) CEGs supported local organizations in reaching disproportionately affected populations. Effectiveness of each category was assessed through enrollment data, demographic diversity, and overall cost.ResultsFrom May 2021 to December 2024, 5649 individuals enrolled. Marketing outreach ​accounted for 61% of enrollment compared to other outreach methods. Non-White and Hispanic individuals consisted of 9.4% and 2.3%, of all consented participants, respectively. Almost one-fifth (19%) of participants enrolled were estimated to face greater levels of disadvantage, as defined by the Area Deprivation Index (ADI).ConclusionA multifaceted recruitment approach was key to exceeding recruitment goals. Online marketing and strategic partnerships with patient advocacy organizations were effective and impactful. Opportunities for future research may explore refinements in digital outreach and ways to overcome accessibility barriers to improve rates of engagement and proportional representation in clinical registries.

Introduction

Recruiting participants for registries presents a significant challenge in medical research, often requiring substantial time and financial resources. The implementation of effective and cost-efficient recruitment strategies is critical to ensure the timely completion and overall success of a study. Various methods have been employed to enhance participant enrollment, including media advertising, physician referrals, press releases, flyers, direct mail campaigns, and internet-based outreach [1]. Media channels such as television, radio, and newspapers enable broad dissemination of registry information, while physician referrals leverage the trust and relationships between healthcare providers and patients to facilitate recruitment [2]. Distributing informational materials in community centers and clinics through flyers and press releases is also a commonly used strategy [3]. Direct mail campaigns allow for targeted outreach, and the growing use of online platforms and social media has further expanded recruitment opportunities by improving accessibility and engagement [4].

The cost-effectiveness of these recruitment strategies varies considerably, necessitating careful evaluation of their financial implications. Studies suggest that online recruitment strategies can be generally more time-efficient and cost-effective compared with traditional offline approaches [2].

The PROMISE Registry is a 20-year, prospective genetic registry for prostate cancer patients across the United States (including the US territories) [5]. Five thousand individuals will be screened through at-home germline testing and an online platform to identify and enroll 500 individuals with germline mutations in specific genes of interest. Study of rare germline genetic variants contributing to prostate cancer risk and severity is constrained by limited case numbers outside of deeply phenotyped and genetically annotated cohorts, highlighting the need for prospective registries that can systematically collect and follow rare variant carriers [6].

Participants in the PROMISE Registry will enter long-term follow-up (6-month interval patient-reported outcomes surveys and medical record retrieval). The PROMISE Registry aims to collect data to understand disease characteristics and treatment responses across the disease spectrum for individuals with prostate cancer harboring germline mutations. This study describes its outreach program that sought to broaden enrollment of individuals with prostate cancer not only through traditional academic institution-based approaches, but also through prostate cancer clinics in the community setting. By broadening enrollment to individuals treated in a community setting, the PROMISE Registry aims to include individuals who would not otherwise have access to germline genetic testing and genetic counseling.

Methods

Organization and staffing

The PROMISE Registry outreach was led by a team of marketing specialists and supported by members of the study team. The marketing team was selected by the study funder, Advancing Cancer Treatment, based on relevant experience and expertise. Marketing specialists managed strategic planning, target audience research, and contracts discussions with national patient advocacy organizations. The study team facilitated enrollment by providing direct support to enrolling patients on the online study platform, and training and logistical support for CEG awardees and other site-based recruiting investigators. Both the marketing and study teams generated outreach program content, travelled to and staffed in-person events, presented at and hosted online webinars, and analyzed recruitment data from several sources. A Program Manager from the study funder and Project Manager from the study team coordinated efforts between both groups.

The marketing team implemented an informal, collaborative organization approach (Fig. 1). Each area was led by an individual with responsibility for decision making, implementation, and resource allocation, but all team members provided input across functions and hands-on support as needed.Fig. 1Organizational Structure of Direct-to-Patient Marketing OutreachKey components of the marketing outreach program developed and implemented by the PROMISE Registry team.

By design, the number of marketing team members varied over the course of the project to match changes in activity type and intensity. For example, during the initial development and launch of PROMISE (visual identity, website build, assets), the creative resources team included an external marketing agency partner with a team of 6-8 members. After the initial launch and transition to ongoing recruitment activities, the creative resource's function was staffed by one person on an as-needed project basis. Over the course of outreach activities, the average size of the PROMISE marketing team was 3-4 members, each working approximately 15-25 h per month, with periods of higher levels of staffing and hours as referenced above. On the study team, outreach efforts were led by one team member and supported by three others on an ad-hoc basis based on availability for in-person and online events. The team lead formed relationships and coordinated efforts with local patient advocacy organizations, and developed patient-facing content with support from other team members.

Marketing partners

To reach prostate cancer patients efficiently, PROMISE partnered with organizations already serving this community, including national and state-level prostate cancer patient advocacy organizations, community groups, and organizations focused on disproportionately affected populations. These partnerships were crucial to recruitment efforts, helping PROMISE reach prostate cancer patients with multiple communication vehicles, including online newsletters, email, website advertising, and social media posts (primarily Facebook and X, formerly known as Twitter).

To identify the most productive partners, the PROMISE team established criteria to guide the selection of partners (Fig. 2). Marketing outreach

PROMISE outreach efforts from 2021 to 2024 prioritized the use of integrated marketing campaigns which utilized multiple communications vehicles in an integrated approach. Examples of these vehicles include the following (not all campaigns used all vehicles).

Sponsored Content and Online Advertising: PROMISE produced feature articles, newsletter stories, blog posts, interviews, and advertising for use on partners' websites and in their existing communications vehicles. For example, PROMISE sponsored the annual genetics issue of Prostatepedia, published by the National Alliance of State Prostate Cancer Coalitions (NASPCC) and collaborated on creating a special edition entitled, “Black Men and Prostate Cancer”. These issues included PROMISE patient stories, interviews with the registry's Principal Investigators, advertising, and a Q&A section.

Webinars: In collaboration with marketing partners, PROMISE developed webinars to inform and engage both support group leaders and prostate cancer patients. Webinar topics and speakers focused on the PROMISE study and topics most relevant to the specific partner's audience. Overall, PROMISE held 55 webinars to support recruitment. Examples include a collaboration with ZERO Prostate Cancer for the leaders of its patient support groups and a focused session on Black men and prostate cancer with the Prostate Health Education Network (PHEN).

Direct-to-Patient Email: PROMISE had select opportunities to reach prostate cancer patients with direct email messages via third-party partners with opt-in lists. Email content was tailored to address the unique concerns of the recipients which included those disproportionately affected by prostate cancer and veteran groups. Partner organizations managed and sent the emails to adhere to confidentiality guidelines.

Ambassador Program: Many participants in PROMISE were passionate about sharing their stories and supporting prostate cancer research. To provide these participants with a platform to advocate for joining the registry and for participation in research, the PROMISE Ambassador Program was developed and offered on a voluntary basis. The Ambassadors’ personal stories featured prominently in advertising, newsletters, blogs, and media interviews.

Patient Conferences: The PROMISE team secured speaking opportunities at local and national, patient-centered events, including the NASPCC annual meeting and ZERO's Annual Summit. Principal Investigators and Prostate Cancer Clinical Trials Consortium (PCCTC) members presented the PROMISE Registry's mission and benefits to patients and caregivers.

In-person Events: PROMISE participated in in-person prostate cancer awareness events including ZERO's regional fundraising walks. Locations were chosen for high rates of prostate cancer incidence, reach to disproportionately affected populations, and proximity to team members. Events were staffed by PCCTC and marketing team members.

Targeted Campaigns to Increase Diversity: PROMISE marketing also included targeted campaigns developed to increase participation in the study by Black people with prostate cancer. One example focused on the fact that Black men are disproportionately affected by prostate cancer, yet are underrepresented in research and clinical trials. This campaign was developed in partnership with local, grass-roots organizations and was called "From Disparity to Discovery". Efforts were targeted to the Charlotte, North Carolina and Memphis, Tennessee regions and communication vehicles included a survey of Black prostate cancer patients’ attitudes and knowledge of prostate cancer, genetics, and research, advertising, webinars, sponsored content, and media interviews.

Google Ads Grants Program: To reach more potential study participants, PROMISE applied for and received approval to participate in the Google Ads Grants program [7]. The program provided the opportunity to run text ads that appear on Google search results for prostate cancer-related topics. PROMISE worked with an online marketing firm to manage strategy and implementation, including development of optimal keywords, writing effective ad copy, ongoing testing & refinement, and more.

Community Engagement Grants: Marketing for the PROMISE Registry included a Community Engagement Grants (CEG) initiative that offered grants to local organizations and medical centers to support evidence-based programs that improve awareness of and access to the PROMISE Registry among disproportionately affected populations—particularly Black and Hispanic people with prostate cancer. The goal was to increase participation among Black, Hispanic, Asian, and Native American participants and extend their proposed systems to other clinical registry and trial accrual efforts within their institutions. The first CEG recipient began their funding period and recruitment in 2022 and CEG recruitment is currently ongoing.

Medical Institutions: Efforts initiated by the study team included video content recorded on the study website by the co-Principal Investigators, presentations at patient- and provider-facing webinars and conferences, podcast guest features, online articles and newsletter features. The co-Principal Investigators also expanded the study's site network via known contacts as well as forming new relationships with academic and patient advocacy contacts, and supported study enrollment in their and colleagues' clinics. Referring community and academic medical institutions distributed information about the PROMISE Registry to eligible patients through IRB-approved brochures. Some had dedicated staff available to introduce and discuss the study with patients in clinic.

Results

Projected and actual recruitment

As of December 2024, 5,649 people have consented to participate in the PROMISE Registry across all 50 U S. states, and 4,534 have undergone genetic testing. A total of 401 participants with germline mutations of interest were enrolled in the long-term follow-up period of the registry which tracks with projections of 10-15% eligibility rates. Lessons learned

Website: an essential role

The PROMISE website was the nexus for participants to get information and navigate to self-enrollment in the study and thereby played the most critical role (Fig. 5) in the success of recruitment efforts. All marketing activities were designed to drive visitors to the website so it was essential their experience on the site was a satisfying and high quality one. Of the 4,413 total Patient Experience Surveys completed, 1,270 responses were received to the question: “Overall, how helpful did you find the PROMISE website content?” 78.9% of participant responses to this question indicated that the website was “somewhat helpful” or “very helpful.” The site effectively balanced both the research requirement to provide clear, detailed information and the marketing requirement to be compelling and generate a response. The PROMISE team's decision to devote a high level of attention and resources to the development of the destination website was a key success factor in overall recruitment.Fig. 5PROMISE Website Home ScreenThe study website was designed as a functional recruitment hub as well as informational source for participants. Marketing outreach

Integrated campaigns with marketing partners were the largest and most productive driver of registrations. These campaigns were designed to reach potential participants multiple times, using a variety of vehicles including sponsored content, online advertising, and more. The PROMISE marketing team did not find any one communications vehicle to be consistently more effective than others. Rather, a campaign of multiple vehicles, delivered over a focused period of time, in an integrated fashion, was more effective than any one vehicle. The key success factor for these integrated campaigns was the degree of alignment with the partnership criteria cited previously. Strong alignment with all four criteria, consistently delivered strong results.

Direct mail was available to PROMISE on a limited basis and was an efficient source of registrations. For example, a state cancer registry agreed to send a letter on agency letterhead with a PROMISE brochure directly to their list of prostate cancer patients. While the total number of registrations generated did not represent a large percentage of the total study registrations, the response rate for this specific method was high and the resource requirement was low relative to other outreach methods. Key success factors were the quality of the mailing list, credibility of the partner, focused communication, and value of PROMISE to the recipients.

The Google Ads Program was a consistent source of traffic to the PROMISE website and delivered a low rate of registrations. With guidance from a resource with program expertise, Google Ads proved to be a worthwhile vehicle to make a small enhancement to recruitment at little cost.

In-person events such as patient conferences, including those organized by ZERO and NASPCC, served an important role for PROMISE in establishing relationships and building visibility in the prostate cancer community. While these events did not provide significant recruitment results, the PROMISE marketing team recognized their value and importance and invested appropriate time and resources.

Fundraising events, walks, etc. provided limited value for recruitment efforts. The nature of these events and venues are typically not ideal for meaningful consideration of a decision to participate in a research study. PROMISE presence at these events focused on generating visibility, goodwill, and community support.

Diverse patient outreach efforts (Targeted Campaigns) by PROMISE showed some initial increase in participant diversity. The process of recruiting diverse participants is time and labor intensive. Speed and cost efficiency are not the most appropriate measures of success.

Recruitment at medical institutions

These activities, conducted by the study team were also a significant and effective driver of registrations for the PROMISE Registry. The PCCTC site network contributed a significant amount of referring sites, 10 of which were provided funding to support recruitment activities. At the highest point, over 30 dedicated sites across the U.S. referred patients to PROMISE. A key factor contributing to success in this area was the active engagement of the co-Principal Investigators in the outreach program as spokespeople and advocates for the study. The co-Principal Investigators invested significant time in content generation and preparation for a wide range of outreach efforts.

Community engagement grants

Some CEGs were more successful than others. However, while as an overall effort recruitment results were low, recipients were able to provide more opportunities for participation to Black patients and the initiative provided important knowledge. Grant recipients faced challenges similar to those encountered by the overall program, including the need for personalized support due to limited technology use, particularly among older patients, lack of essential resources, including internet access, email, and digital literacy and the inability to sustain recruitment efforts without ongoing financial support. Key success factors were dedicated clinical trial navigators that were able to consistently follow-up with patients to support study registration, sample collection, and survey completion, as well as existing connections to the desired cohorts.

Discussion

Participant barriers to enrollment

As a decentralized trial, the PROMISE Registry was designed to broaden the opportunity to participate in research to individuals with prostate cancer treated in the community setting outside of academic medical centers. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has supported increased implementation of decentralized trials due to several clear benefits of this type of design. Particularly, for study participants, decentralized trials provide increased convenience of participation, removal of transportation and logistics barriers, reduced burden on caregivers, and overall increased access to disproportionately affected populations. However, while decentralized trials can increase access to potential participants, they can also introduce barriers to access for others. One of the barriers the team had to consider was the study's reliance on technology. Some participants found the initial online registration and navigation to be challenging. In addition, some individuals did not have access to essential resources needed to register fully, including their own email address or device connected to the internet, deterring them from engaging with online marketing efforts and registering for the study. Many individuals also required additional assistance from caregivers to register for the study, which also prevented registration if a caregiver was not available to assist them.

Urban vs rural enrollment, socioeconomic status and participant diversity

The PROMISE Registry successfully expanded its reach into community-based settings, enrolling a socioeconomically diverse population, as evidenced by Area Deprivation Index (ADI) data. With a median ADI of 83 (IQR 69–96), the registry skewed toward participants residing in more socioeconomically deprived areas. Notably, 19% of participants fell into the 100–150 ADI range, and less than 8% were from the most affluent areas (ADI 0–50). This suggests that PROMISE's decentralized design and targeted outreach strategies—including webinars, direct-to-patient messaging, and partnerships with community organizations—were effective in reaching populations with higher social and economic disadvantage, often underrepresented in clinical research. These findings underscore the importance of community-based enrollment strategies to reduce disparities in genetic testing and research participation. Furthermore, these results demonstrate the feasibility of community-based accrual to a 100% electronic clinical trial, which was timely given the start of recruitment in mid-2021 amidst the COVD-19 pandemic.

Urban vs. rural considerations are also relevant in interpreting ADI data, as higher ADI scores are frequently associated with urban neighborhoods that face concentrated poverty, underemployment, and limited healthcare access. Conversely, rural populations may be underrepresented in the registry despite facing their own unique access barriers, such as geographic isolation, lack of broadband internet, and fewer healthcare infrastructure resources. While the PROMISE Registry made strides in reaching disproportionately affected urban communities, future efforts should consider dedicated strategies for rural populations. These may include mobile enrollment clinics or partnerships with rural healthcare providers. Broadening this geographic reach is essential to ensure equitable representation and improve the generalizability of findings from genomic prostate cancer research across diverse socioeconomic and geographic populations.

Finally, while it was not the study's primary objective to exceed previously recorded levels of disproportionately affected participants, the study team made several efforts to improve participant diversity, including targeted campaigns with community organizations. However, these efforts were not as successful as hoped, warranting further exploration for future efforts and studies.

Availability of germline testing

The PROMISE Registry began enrollment when genetic testing for prostate cancer had only recently been incorporated into clinical practice guidelines. Although the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) first recommended germline testing for patients in 2019, uptick remained limited initially, as insurance coverage was inconsistent and clinical awareness was low [4]. Over subsequent years, as payers increasingly recognized germline testing as standard of care, educational efforts expanded, and multiple-FDA approved therapies emerged with indications tied to genetic biomarkers, familiarity with and adoption of testing grew, particularly within academic centers. Nevertheless, persistent challenges remain with broad, equitable implementation, and many patients still face barriers to accessing recommended testing.

As expected, enrollment of individuals with known germline mutations has occurred on study. However, some potential recruiting institutions with existing in-house genetic testing programs were hesitant to ask their patients to expend additional effort to re-test. Similarly, some potential participants did not find personal benefits in re-testing. The PROMISE Registry continues to be a genetic testing resource for individuals with prostate cancer who would have otherwise not been able to be tested, however, the study is also open to those who have known germline mutations and wish to contribute to research and knowledge of genetics and prostate cancer.

Recommendations for future outreach

Based on the results and review of the PROMISE Registry outreach program, investment in integrated campaigns with marketing partners to support traditional academic institution-based recruitment was the most effective method in driving study registrations. The engagement of partners, consisting of national- and smaller-level patient-facing organizations, was essential to reach potential participants who would not have otherwise learned of the study through traditional academic medicine settings. However, the outreach program would not have been successful without a high quality study website that served as the central entry point for potential study participants. Finally, dedicated and knowledgeable team members responsible for the strategic execution of the outreach program, and highly engaged Co-Principal Investigators were essential components of this program implementation.

Research teams with limited funding may consider prioritizing a well-designed study website with informative but concise structure, partnering with a small number of trusted patient advocacy organizations who provide broad membership and electronic marketing vehicles and utilizing an engaged Principal Investigator in patient-facing outreach materials. However, as critical groundwork for the implementation of any decentralized study and participant outreach program, research teams should prioritize simplifying the participant workflow as much as possible.
