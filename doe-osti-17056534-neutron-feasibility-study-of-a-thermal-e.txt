Executive Summary
This CED-1 scoping study evaluates whether the standard TEX-Pu plate-stack approach from PU-
MET-MIXED-002 can be extended to nickel and chromium nuclear-data validation using candidate
materials reported to be in the National Criticality Experiments Research Center (NCERC) inventory.
The designs retain the PMM-002 style of stacked PANN fuel trays with polyethylene moderation
and inserted diluent plates, using nickel plates for TEX-Ni and NiCr plates for TEX-NiCr. The objec-
tive was to identify practical fast, intermediate, mixed spectrum, and thermal conﬁgurations that
are close enough to critical for subsequent design reﬁnement and sensitive to the target material
without unnecessary stack height or material use.
Twelve recommended conﬁgurations were retained for further design, with six per diluent fam-
ily ordered from the most moderated stack to the least moderated stack. Each family includes
1 in, 0.375 in, 0.125 in, and 0.0625 in nominal moderator thicknesses plus unmoderated standard
and bunched fast conﬁgurations. The nickel conﬁgurations use nominally 0.25 in-thick diluent
plates; the NiCr conﬁgurations use nominally 0.125 in-thick diluent plates and double loading where
needed. The nickel fast cases have fast ﬁssion fractions from 75.57 % to 88.47 % and fast nickel
sensitivities from 1.30 × 10−1 to 1.68 × 10−1 %/% (% change in keff per 1% change in nuclide con-
centration). The moderated nickel cases provide intermediate ﬁssion fractions up to 49.16 %, and
the thermal nickel case gives a 63.15 % thermal ﬁssion fraction with 1.39 × 10−1 %/% thermal nickel
sensitivity.
The TEX-NiCr cases provide corresponding chromium-bearing conﬁgurations while retaining cal-
culable responses to both chromium and nickel in the mixed alloy. The retained fast NiCr cases
have fast ﬁssion fractions from 73.88 % to 87.94 % and absolute fast chromium sensitivities near
5.3 × 10−2 –5.5 × 10−2 %/%. The intermediate NiCr cases reach a 48.04 % intermediate ﬁssion frac-
tion with 1.14 × 10−2 %/% absolute intermediate chromium sensitivity, while the thermal NiCr case
gives a 63.64 % thermal ﬁssion fraction and 2.46 × 10−2 %/% absolute thermal chromium sensitivity.
We were successful in identifying TEX experimental cases with sensitivity to both the Ni and Cr
nuclear data with current NCSP inventories.

1 Introduction
The Thermal/Epithermal eXperiments (TEX) program was developed as a ﬂexible platform for crit-
ical experiments spanning thermal, intermediate, and fast neutron spectra and for testing materi-
als important to criticality safety. Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) and Los Alamos
National Laboratory (LANL) completed the plutonium baseline experiments under IER 184; the eval-
uated conﬁgurations are published as PU-MET-MIXED-002 [3]. Subsequent TEX work added tan-
talum and thick-moderator benchmark series. A TEX-Fe campaign is currently under benchmark
evaluation for submission to the 2027 ICSBEP with 3 critical cases [4]. This report evaluates the
feasibility of two additional TEX-Pu campaigns using nickel and nickel–chromium (NiCr) with dilu-
ents materials currently available in the Nuclear Criticality Safety Program’s (NCSP) inventory [5].

1.1 Program Justiﬁcation
Iron, manganese, chromium, and nickel are major constituents of steels used in storage racks,
containers, gloveboxes, and other nuclear operations equipment across the NNSA complex. Fig-
ure 1 shows keff sensitivity to Ni and Cr isotopes and reactions for existing International Criticality
Safety Benchmark Evaluation Project (ICSBEP) benchmarks. Additionally the Hanford Tank Farms
criticality-safety basis credits neutron absorption by waste constituents including chromium and
nickel along with iron. The 2022 Working Party on Nuclear Criticality Safety Subgroup on Exper-
imental Needs for Criticality Safety Purposes (SG-5) assigned both Ni and Cr Priority 3 of 5 for
integral-experiment need [5]. The heat map is color coded to indicate the total number of bench-
marks that have at least 1.00 × 10−3 k-effective sensitivity to a 1 % cross-section change at a given
energy. The reproduced maps show fewer benchmarks with fast sensitivity than with thermal sen-
sitivity and sparse coverage in the intermediate-energy range [5].
NCSP has funded the Ni-58 and Ni-60 evaluations, including measurements at ORELA in 2008,
resonance evaluation at ORNL, and fast evaluation at LANL, with a re-evaluation planned in FY28-
FY30. For Cr, there are currently known issues with the capture cross section from 1-100 keV.
LANL is planning new fast elastic and inelastic measurements with the CoGNAC detector assembly
at LANSCE, and ORNL is also planning new measurements to inform a reevaluation for neutron
capture in the resonance region below 10 keV [2, 5]. The NCSP has made signiﬁcant investments
in improving the Ni and Cr nuclear data including funding new measurements and evaluations.
Integral critical experiments complement differential measurements by testing the combined ef-
fect of evaluated nuclear data in application-relevant systems. For chromium, Pérez-Maroto et al.
report discrepancies of as much as 30 % among evaluated 50 Cr and 53 Cr capture cross sections
and effects of approximately 1000 pcm in sensitive criticality benchmarks. Their new 1–100 keV
measurements do not support the increase adopted in the recent INDEN evaluation and ENDF/B-
VIII.1, strengthening the case for independent integral validation [6].

1.2 TEX Background
The plutonium series of TEX experiments uses layered assemblies of Plutonium-Aluminum No-
Nickel (PANN) Zero Power Physics Reactor (ZPPR) fuel, polyethylene moderator, and recommended
diluent materials [7, 3]. The evaluated baseline spans thermal, intermediate, and fast spectra. Later
campaigns retained the plate-stack approach while adding interstitial diluents [3, 4]. The assem-
blies are performed on the Planet vertical-lift machine at National Criticality Experiments Research
Center (NCERC) by LANL personnel [8, 4].

This report documents the feasibility of extending the TEX-Pu design to provide criticality safety

use of the ZPPR diluent inventory at NCERC [5]. 28,000 nickel plates with nominal dimensions of

by 2 in by 6 in and a composition of 69.4 % Ni and 29.5 % Cr by mass [5]. The present models
test whether the NiCr inventory can provide sufﬁcient chromium sensitivity without procuring a
separate chromium diluent.

2 Methodology
The preliminary design study was performed as a parametric scoping calculation based on the
detailed TEX-Pu benchmark model. The starting point was the PU-MET-MIXED-002 geometry with:

 • PANN ZPPR fuel plates loaded in the standard TEX fuel trays;

 • Polyethylene reﬂection on top and bottom and interstitial moderator for moderated cases;

 • Vertical lift-machine aluminum hardware; and

 • Central diaphragm membrane.

The model was then generalized so that a stack could be assembled from a sequence of layers
of fuel trays, polyethylene moderator sheets, and diluent layers. This approach preserved the de-
tailed plate/tray geometry while allowing many candidate stacks to be generated consistently from
a common model deﬁnition. As a scoping simpliﬁcation, the NiCr diluent is represented as a ho-
mogeneous, uniform-density region while preserving the modeled diluent mass. The primary ob-
jectives of the parameter study were to identify models with:

 • keff ≈ 1;

 • Total diluent and fuel plates used are limited to current NCERC inventory; and

 9

 Figure 2: Schematic of Ni and NiCr diluent plates.

 • An absolute energy-integrated sensitivity coefﬁcient of approximately 1.00 × 10−2 or greater
 for the diluent of interest.
This scoping study intentionally used a bounded parameter sweep rather than a formal optimizer.
The number of moderator and diluent options was small enough that direct enumeration provided
clear design trends while keeping the link between the physical stack arrangement and the com-
puted sensitivity straightforward.
Two diluent families were studied. The ﬁrst used the existing nickel ZPPR diluent inventory as the
material of interest for a TEX-Ni experiment. The nickel model uses ZPPR plate ID No. 365 from
reference [1] as a surrogate, with assumed dimensions of 2 in by 4 in by 0.25 in and an average
mass of 283 g per plate. Figure 2 shows a schematic of the ZPPR diluent plates used in this study.
Each diluent layer was modeled as a homogeneous 12 in by 12 in region ﬁlled by plates, requiring 24
No. 365 plates per layer. The modeled bulk density is 8.64 g/cm3 , calculated from those assumed
dimensions and mass. The second family uses the NiCr inventory as a practical chromium-bearing
surrogate. Inventory No. 359 from reference [1] is modeled with dimensions of 2 in by 6 in by
0.125 in, an average mass of 194.25 g, 12 plates per layer, and a bulk density of 8.371 g/cm3 . The
modeled elemental compositions are given in Table 1 [5].
The parameter study varied both spectrum-shaping moderator thickness and diluent placement to
identify near-critical conﬁgurations with useful sensitivity in distinct energy regions. The primary
moderator cases were no added interstitial moderator, 0.0625 in, 0.375 in, and 1 in polyethylene
between fuel regions; 0.125 in cases were also calculated as an intermediate moderation point.
For the 1 in cases, a split-moderator arrangement was also investigated for ﬂux-trap cases, with
the diluent placed between moderator sheets rather than immediately adjacent to only one side of
a fuel tray. For each arrangement, the number of fuel layers was varied until one or more cases
bracketed delayed critical, with the preliminary target taken as keff near 1.0. The conﬁgurations
explored in the scoping calculations are:
 • Total moderator thickness per layer (primarily used to adjust neutron spectrum): 0 in (no
     interstitial moderator), 0.0625 in, 0.125 in, 0.375 in, 1 in;
 • Diluent placement in the stack (primarily used to increase sensitivity and ﬁssion fraction in a
 speciﬁc energy domain):

Figure 3 shows the standard, ﬂux-trap, and bunched arrangements used in the study. Some cases

All scoping cases were run with MCNP6.3.1 [9] using ENDF/B-VIII.1 continuous-energy cross-section
data [10]. The eigenvalue calculations used the same source and cycle controls across the study
with 100 000 histories per cycle, an initial guess of keff = 1.0, 150 inactive cycles, and 1000 active cy-
cles. The MCNP outputs were post-processed to extract keff , plot sensitivities per unit lethargy, and
collapse the sensitivity results into thermal (E < 0.625 eV), intermediate (0.625 eV≤ E <100 keV),
and fast (E ≥ 100 keV) groups.
First-order cross-section sensitivities were computed directly in MCNP using KSEN cards applied
to every diluent cell in each generated deck. The cards requested the SCALE-238 energy-group
structure and listed the isotopes explicitly. For NiCr, three chromium proﬁles used RXN=1, RXN=2, and
RXN=102 for total, elastic-scattering, and radiative-capture sensitivity, respectively; three analogous
proﬁles were used for nickel. MCNP therefore reported a separate energy-dependent coefﬁcient
for each isotope and reaction proﬁle. Contributions from repeated spatial zones were summed bin
by bin, with their Monte Carlo uncertainties combined in quadrature.

4.3 All Cases
Figures 16 and 17 compare the absolute sensitivity distributions of the retained conﬁgurations.
The nickel results show the intended progression from a thermal-dominated response in the 1 in-
moderated conﬁguration to a fast-dominated response in the unmoderated conﬁgurations, while
the partially moderated cases retain substantial intermediate sensitivity. The chromium results
show the same progression and demonstrate that 52 Cr provides most of the fast response, whereas
53 Cr contributes strongly to the thermal response of the most moderated conﬁguration. Together,

the ﬁgures show that the recommended cases provide complementary integral sensitivity across
all three energy regions.

5 Conclusions
The completed scoping calculations support the feasibility of extending the TEX-Pu platform to
nickel and NiCr diluents across a broad range of neutron spectra. The recommended nickel cases
span thermal ﬁssion fractions from 2.19 % to 63.15 % and fast ﬁssion fractions from 13.77 % to
88.47 %. The retained NiCr cases provide a similar span, from 2.28 % to 63.64 % thermal ﬁssions
and from 13.59 % to 87.94 % fast ﬁssions. The intermediate candidates do not exceed a 50 % inter-
mediate ﬁssion fraction, but the 0.125 in ﬂux-trap nickel and double-diluent NiCr selections reach
49.16 % and 48.04 %, respectively.
