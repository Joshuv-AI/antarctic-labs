Dear Adm. Turner:

With the deterioration of U.S. -Soviet relations following the
Soviet invasion of Afghanistan and the withdrawal and near
demise of the SALT II Treaty, an assesment of U.S./U.S.S.R.
policy and the U.S. perceptions of the Soviet Union which
undergird that policy seems necessary at this time. Many
analysts disagree in their perceptions of the Soviet threat,
and many Americans fear the Soviet Union. This fear is sometimes
based on fact, sometimes on fiction, sometimes on appearances
and sometimes on reality. Because this perception strongly
affects both formulation and implementation of our policy
towards the Soviet Union, we hope through a seminar to gain
some insight into it.

In order to assist religious leaders in this assesment, the
Foreign Policy and Military Spending Task Force of the
Washington Interreligious Staff Council (WISC) is preparing
a seminar on "U.S. Perceptions of the Soviet Threat". WISC
is a cooperative organization of over -40 religious groups
which maintain Washington government liason offices. We are
inviting 150 religious leaders to jiart ici pate in this seminar
to be held in Washington on May 29~30, 1980.

Because of your experience in this area, it is our privilege
to invite you to address the seminar concerning the Central
Intelligence Agency's perceptions of the Soviet Union on
Friday, May 30 at 9:45 a.m. for about 45 minutes. The seminar
is by nature exploratory and educational, and we hope to
establish a thorough and free exchange of divergent points
of view on the issue.
