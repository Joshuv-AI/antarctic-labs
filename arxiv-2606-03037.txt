Abstract
We report JWST/NIRSpec integral field unit (IFU) observations of the quasar J1512 + + 4422 at z ∼ 6.2 z\sim 6.2 from the Aether survey. At ∼ \sim 900 Myr after the Big Bang, this object already lies on the M BH M_{\rm BH} – σ ∗ \sigma_{\ast} relation found in the local universe, with an M BH ≃ 8.9 × 10 8 ​ M ⊙ M_{\rm BH}\simeq 8.9\times 10^{8}\,M_{\odot} and a stellar velocity dispersion σ ∗ ≃ 288 \sigma_{\ast}\simeq 288 km s -1 . We detect an outflow with a velocity of ∼ \sim 478 km s -1 in the nuclear region, which likely extends to ∼ \sim 3.2 kpc in projection and has a median velocity of ∼ \sim 352 km s -1 . The outflow dynamical time scale ( ∼ \sim 9 Myr) is consistent with the time scale of the current quenching process based on the star formation history as reported previously. The total mass outflow rate (92.6 − 74.1 + 92.6 {}^{+92.6}_{-74.1} M ☉ yr -1 ) is larger than the current star formation rate (0.9 − 0.8 + 3.8 {}^{+3.8}_{-0.8} or 4.3 − 3.7 + 5.8 {}^{+5.8}_{-3.7} M ☉ yr -1 ), and the total kinetic energy outflow rate (0.6 − 0.5 + 0.6 {}^{+0.6}_{-0.5} % of quasar luminosity) meets the threshold for negative quasar feedback as suggested by simulations. These results suggest that the outflow is capable of suppressing/quenching the star formation activity within the host galaxy. Furthermore, J1512 + + 4422 exhibits σ ∗ \sigma_{\ast} , stellar mass and size similar to those of z ≳ z\gtrsim 3 quiescent/post-starburst galaxies, implying a link between the two. Overall, for objects like J1512 + + 4422, the evolution of their SMBHs and host galaxies appears to be tightly coupled within the first billion years. The quasar feedback likely plays a critical role in both placing them on the M BH M_{\rm BH} – σ ∗ \sigma_{\ast} relation and quenching.

We fit the nuclear spectrum extracted from a circular aperture with

It utilizes the Bayesian affine-invariant Markov-Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampler for robust parameter and uncertainty estimation. Readers are referred to their paper for more details on the design of the code.

Our fitting model consisted of a quasar power-law continuum, quasar
Fe
II

102

74

but found negligible difference in our final best-fits. In the fitting, the models are further convolved with the line spread function of the NIRSpec/IFU G395H grating.

Due to the degeneracy between the quasar continuum and stellar continuum in the fitting, we need to first obtain their relative strength independently. Therefore, we used GALFIT
(
75
)
to decompose the pure continuum images obtained from the IFU data (see Appendix
A

The stellar continuum and absorption lines were modeled using the widely-used pPXF
(

integrated in BADASS, adopting the Indo-U.S. Library of Coudé Feed Stellar Spectra
(

with an intrinsic model spectral broadening of FWHM

For emission lines, the broad

\alpha

\beta

\gamma

-1

\alpha
,

]
\lambda

The kinematics (i.e., velocity and velocity dispersion) of each corresponding Gaussian component in all these lines are tied together, which are free to vary in the ranges of [
-

-1
and [500, 5000] km s
-1

Moreover, broad

\beta

\beta

Finally, as shown in our Fig.

Extended Line Emission

We adopt
q3dfit

(
81
)
to subtract the quasar PSF in the IFU data and uncover the extended line emission within the galaxy, following the same approach adopted by previous studies
(
110
;
97
;

;
56
;
58
;
107

. Here we summarize the key steps of

First, we construct a quasar template spectrum from an aperture (
r=
0
\farcs
05) centered on the brightest spaxel. For each spaxel in the IFU data cube, we then model the corresponding spectrum as a linear combination of a scaled quasar template spectrum representing the quasar PSF contribution in that spaxel (
I^{n}_{\rm quasar}
), a featureless monotonic polynomial representing the host-galaxy continuum emission (
I^{n}_{\rm starlight,exp.\;model}
), and a set of host-galaxy emission lines modeled with up to two components with Gaussian profiles (
I^{n}_{\rm emission}
). For the emission line, a second Gaussian component is kept only if it has peak S/N
>
3. In this analysis, we adopt no stellar population models in the fitting, since we only focus on the extended emission lines and see no evidence of spatially-extended stellar absorption lines.

As shown in Fig.
2
, the extended line emission in each spaxel can be modeled by a single Gaussian profile (c1 components) in general, except for several spaxels where a secondary Gaussian profile (c2 components) is needed (i.e., the c2 component has a peak S/N
>
3). Five example spectra from representative spaxels, along with their best-fit models of extended emission and subtracted quasar PSF contribution, are shown in Fig.
3
. The locations of these spaxels are denoted in Fig.
2
. The extended line emission mainly consists of two parts in terms of their kinematic nature: The
Systemic Gas
includes the southern and northwestern parts of the c1 components with centroid velocities
v_{50}
close to the systemic velocity (
<
50 km s
-1
) and narrow line widths (
\sigma

<
100 km s
-1
) in general. It traces the kinematically quiescent gas within the system. The
Outflowing Gas
includes the remaining regions of the c1 components and the c2 components, which are all blueshifted (
v_{50}

\sim

-
20 –
-
1040 km s
-1
; median:
\sim
-
174 km s
-1
) and have much broader line widths (
\sigma

\sim

-1
; median:
\sim
156 km s
-1
) with respect to the
Systemic Gas
. It traces a galaxy-scale outflow in this system. The approximate locations of
the c1 components belong to the
Outflowing Gas
are indicated by the red ellipses in Fig.

Outflowing Gas
as those traced by Gaussian components (c1 or c2) with either (i)

<
0 km s

and
\sigma

>

-

Nevertheless, it should be noted that we cannot formally rule out other interpretations for the nature of the
Outflowing Gas
. For example, it could still come from the host galaxy itself but with much more turbulent motions as reflected by its larger line width than the
Systemic Gas

Part of the broad and blueshifted line emission could potentially trace a combination of outflowing and rotating gas, although the current data are not be deep enough to disentangle the two.
Moreover, the entire line-emitting nebulae may still trace the rotating gas, although the gas kinematics observed is apparently different from those of normal rotating disks where the line emission would shift from blueshift to redshift more gradually.

A Fast Outflow Quenching the Galaxy

Quasar/AGN feedback via outflows is expected to be an important mechanism for quenching
(

;

;

5
;

+
4422.

V.1
Outflow Time Scale

As described in Sec.
III.3
, we detect a fast outflow in J1512
+
4422 that likely extends on galaxy scale. Here we estimate how long this outflow has traveled, using

t_{\rm out}=R_{\rm out}/v_{\rm out}

(3)

Here
R_{\rm out}

\simeq
3.2 kpc is estimated as the maximum radial distance of the extended outflow in projection. We define outflow velocity as
v_{\rm out}=|v_{50}|+\sigma
. As adopted in previous studies
(
98
;
56
, e.g.,)
, this formalism of outflow velocity is adopted to account for the inclination correction needed to recover the true outflow velocity in the 3D space. The line width is included as the
\sigma
term and encodes both the outflow velocity out of the line of sight and the turbulent motion of the outflowing gas.
In the Eq.
3
above,
v_{\rm out}
is estimated as the median value across all spaxels with outflowing gas (
352\pm{75}
km s
-1
). The corresponding
t_{\rm out}
is then
\sim
9 Myr.

As stated earlier,
71
suggests that the sSFR of J1512
+
4422 drops below 0.2 Gyr
-1
within the last 10 Myr. This time scale is comparable to the time scale of the outflow derived above, which supports the scenario that the outflow plays a major role in the current quenching process.

V.2
Outflow Energetics

V.2.1
Nuclear Outflow

In the nuclear spectrum (Fig.
1
), the [O
III
]
\lambda
5007 emission line has a prominent blueshifted and broad component, tracing a fast outflow in our object.
The luminosity of the broad [O
III
]
\lambda
5007 emission line component from the best-fit in Fig.
1
is adopted to derive the mass of the outflowing gas following
11
;
100
:

\displaystyle M_{\rm ionized}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 5.3\times 10^{8}~\frac{C_{e}L_{44}([\mathrm{O~III}])}{n_{e,2}10^{\mathrm{[O/H]}}}M_{\odot},

(4)

where
L_{44}([\mathrm{O~III}])
is the dust-extinction corrected luminosity of [O
iii
] normalized to 10
44
erg s
-1
. Here we assume case B conditions with an electron temperature
T\sim 10^{4}
K
(
73
)
. We use the overall non-BLR
H
\alpha
/
H
\beta
ratio (
\sim
6.0) to estimate the dust extinction of the outflowing gas, adopting the extinction law from
13
and an intrinsic
H
\alpha
/
H
\beta
ratio of 3.1
(
73
)
. This gives E(B
-
V)
\simeq
0.7 and a correction factor of
\sim
8.4 for [O
iii
] luminosity. Such dust extinction is a bit high but still within the ranges seen in
z\sim 6
quasar host galaxies
(
22
, e.g.,)
. The dust extinction derived from the
H
\alpha
/
H
\beta
ratio of the outflow component alone is even higher, although it suffers from much larger uncertainties and thus not adopted in our calculation. The quantity
n_{e,2}
is the average electron density, normalized to 100 cm
-3
. It is set to 2, a value widely adopted in previous studies of quasar-driven outflows and comparable to the observed values when direct measurements are available
(
53
;
37
, e.g.,)
. A range of
\sim
1–10 for
n_{e,2}
is as reported in the literature
(
38
, e.g.,)
, which is adopted to estimate the uncertainties of outflow dynamics.
The quantity
C_{e}\equiv\langle n_{e}\rangle^{2}/\langle n_{e}^{2}\rangle
is the electron density clumping factor, which can be assumed to be of order unity on a cloud-by-cloud basis (i.e., each gas cloud has uniform density). The oxygen-to-hydrogen abundance ratio relative to the solar value, [O/H] is assumed to be 0 (i.e., solar oxygen abundance) in our calculation.
Finally, we obtain a gas mass of
(1.5\pm{0.1})\times 10^{8}

M_{\odot}
for the outflow.

To estimate the mass, momentum, and kinetic energy outflow rates, we follow the same approach adopted in
56
, namely:

\displaystyle\dot{M}_{\rm nuc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{\rm nuc}(v_{\rm nuc}/R_{\rm nuc})

(5)

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{\rm nuc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{\rm nuc}v_{\rm nuc}

(6)

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{\rm nuc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}~\dot{m}_{\rm nuc}(v_{\rm nuc})^{2}

(7)

Here the size of the outflow
R_{\rm nuc}
is assumed to be half of the PSF, which is
\sim
0.8 kpc. The outflow velocity is again measured as
v_{\rm nuc}=|v_{50}|+\sigma
, which gives
\sim
478
\pm{14}
km s
-1
.
The final results are listed in Table
2
. Our measured outflow velocity is smaller than that (721
\pm{39}
km s
-1
) measured from the FS spectrum with a lower spectral resolution and a much larger extraction aperture
(
71
, 0
\farcs
6
\times
0
\farcs
2,)
.

V.2.2
Extended Outflow

Following
56

III.3
for details) in the PSF-subtracted data cube, following:

\displaystyle\dot{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma~\dot{m_{i}}=\Sigma~m_{i}~(v_{i}/R_{i})

(8)

\displaystyle\dot{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma~\dot{m_{i}}~v_{i}

(9)

\displaystyle\dot{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}~\Sigma~\dot{m_{i}}(v_{i})^{2}

(10)

Here the ionized gas mass of each spaxel, m

4
, except that we apply no dust extinction correction to the [O

H
\beta

and has a maximum of
\sim

-1

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we report new JWST NIRSpec/IFU observations of the quasar J1512
+
4422 at
z\sim 6.18
from the
Aether
survey.
Our main results are summarized below:

•

Based on our new data, J1512
+
4422 has an log(
L_{\rm bol}
/
L_{\sun}
) of
\sim
45.1 from the 5100 Å continuum luminosity, and an
M_{\rm BH}
of
\sim
8.9
\times

8

M_{\odot}
(
\sim
6.2
\times

8

M_{\odot}

\lambda_{\rm Edd}
of
\sim
0.011 (0.016) from the broad
H
\alpha

\sigma_{\ast}

\simeq
288
\pm{60}
km s
-1
based on the Balmer absorption lines. This value is larger than the previously reported value (
<
190 km s
-1

\sim
86
\pm{9}
km s
-1
) is much smaller than
\sigma_{\ast}
, suggesting that the gas velocity dispersion is not a good surrogate for the stellar one in this object. Our new results indicate that J1512
+
4422 is already on the local M–
\sigma_{\ast}
relation just 900 Myr after the Big Bang, similar to the other quasar, J2236
+
0032, with stellar absorption lines detected in

•

In J1512
+
4422, we detect a quasar-driven outflow with a velocity of 478
\pm{14}
km s
-1
in the nuclear region. The outflow likely extends to
\sim
3.2 kpc in projection and has a median velocity of 352
\pm{75}
km s
-1

\lesssim
50 km s
-1
.
The outflow is launched about
\sim

\sim
10 Myr ago) of the recent quenching process (sSFR
\lesssim
0.2 Gyr

The total mass outflow rate (97.7

\tau
SFH (0.9

The total momentum outflow rate is
6.7^{+6.7}_{-5.4}\times
the momentum flux from the quasar radiation.
The total kinetic energy outflow rate is
0.6^{+0.6}_{-0.5}
% of the quasar bolometric luminosity, reaching the lowest threshold for negative quasar feedback as suggested by theoretical models. Overall, this quasar-driven outflow is capable of suppressing or quenching the star formation activity within this system. It could also help place J1512
+
4422 on the local M–
\sigma_{\ast}

•

In the
M_{\rm\ast}

+

Our results point to a tantalizing picture for the evolution of, at least a certain population of, quasar host galaxies within the first billion years after the Big Bang:
The growth of the SMBHs and their host galaxies are tightly connected via effective feedback, with galaxy-scale outflows representing one critical channel. Such feedback both helps place these objects on the local M-
\sigma_{\ast}
relation and suppresses/quenches the star formation within their host galaxies very effectively. These quasar host galaxies are among the most promising progenitors of high-
z
massive quiescent galaxies found by recent studies.
