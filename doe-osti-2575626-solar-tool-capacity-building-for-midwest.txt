 Project Summary
 The “Solar Tool Capacity Building for Midwestern Tribes” project sought to overcome barriers
 hindering Tribal solar deployment by providing hands-on, tailored solar photovoltaic (PV) and
 storage modeling training for Tribal environmental professionals. The project team focused on
 challenges identified through a previous 3-year collaboration between the National Laboratory of
 the Rockies (NLR) and the Midwest Tribal Energy Resources Association (MTERA). That
 project, “Addressing Regulatory Challenges to Tribal Solar Deployment,” resulted in a
 guidebook that summarizes challenges and presents short- and long-term solutions. Through this
 work, Tribal staff capacity emerged as one key barrier. “Tribes are often understaffed and under-
 resourced and may not have relevant prior technical experience” (Beshilas 2023).

 To help build Tribal staff capacity, this project developed and implemented training on NLR’s
 REopt® platform, a techno-economic decision support platform used to optimize distributed
 energy systems. Seven Tribal environmental professionals from the MTERA network
 participated in this tailored, hands-on training where they modeled a potential solar or solar-plus-
 storage project for their Tribe in the free REopt web tool, working through the process step-by-
 step with NLR subject matter experts. Participants left the workshop with a “pitch deck” that
 included all information needed to share details about the potential project with their respective
 Tribal Councils, potential funding organizations, and other potential partners.

 Project Objectives and Outcomes
 Tribal energy professionals expressed interest in learning how to use open-access solar design
 tools during the “Addressing Regulatory Challenges to Tribal Solar Deployment” project.
 Building their own skills would reduce their need to request technical assistance (which often
 takes time, even if there is no monetary cost) or pay outside consultants. In response, NLR and
 MTERA designed this project so Tribal energy practitioners are empowered to use REopt to
 identify viable behind-the-meter solar projects with their Tribes, without needing outside
 assistance. REopt estimates the least-cost mix of solar, wind, backup generators, combined heat
 and power, heat pumps, energy storage technologies, and other technologies to meet a user’s
 articulated goals, including cost savings, resilience, and emissions reductions.

 1

 NLR planned and hosted a tailored, hands-on, in-person REopt workshop for seven Tribal
 energy practitioners from the MTERA network. Workshop participants learned how to use the
 decision support tool while modeling a solar or solar-plus-storage system for a specific Tribal
 building. Each participant modeled their selected site using the REopt web tool and prepared a
 slide deck based on guidance tailored for this workshop. The slide decks documented data inputs,
 assumptions, goals, REopt processes, and key findings of the analysis. Key metrics included
 optimal system sizing, expected energy bill savings, level of resilience to be provided, and
 capital costs. The slide deck is meant to serve two purposes: (1) to be shared with decision-
 makers to convey the costs and benefits of the energy projects, and (2) to serve as a reference
 document so workshop participants can revisit the modeling processes later.

 To enhance the workshop, NLR and MTERA conducted a REopt analysis of a fish hatchery that
 provided a real-world example application of the tool. The project team partnered with the Lac
 du Flambeau Tribe in Wisconsin to analyze how solar PV and battery storage could reduce
 energy costs and improve resilience to power outages at the Lac du Flambeau William J.
 Poupart, Sr. Fish Hatchery. The analysis estimated that a 106-kW solar PV plus 24 kW/383 kWh
 battery energy storage system can support the hatchery’s resilience goals. The team created a
 slide deck with the detailed steps behind the analysis, which Tribal staff can use as a guide for
 modeling a project on the REopt platform and as a template to present REopt results. In addition,
 the Lac du Flambeau Tribe fish hatchery staff can use the slide deck to discuss project concepts
 with the Lac du Flambeau Tribal Council and pursue funding opportunities.

 In addition to REopt training, the workshop included information on financing solar projects,
 including information about tax incentives, tax equity partnerships, credits, green banks, and
 other mechanisms.

 Feedback from participants about the workshop was positive:
 • “This workshop exceeded initial expectations. The workshop was able to present at
 different levels.”
 • “I learned so much at this workshop!”
 • “One-on-one assistance was the most valuable part of this workshop for me.”

 In addition to positive feedback, results from pre- and post-workshop knowledge checks
 indicated that participant capacity increased. Participant understanding of key concepts,
 including electric load profiles, critical load, and net present value increased. Before the
 workshop, most participants were “very unfamiliar” or “not familiar” with these concepts. After
 the workshop, all participants rated their understanding as “very familiar,” “familiar,” or
 “neutral.” In addition, participants indicated that their level of proficiency with the REopt web
 tool increased. Before the workshop, participants were unable to articulate the types of questions
 the tool can help answer or list the key data inputs needed; they were also unable to interpret and
 use the outputs. After the workshop, participants felt capable of these tasks.

 NLR provided additional support to participants after the workshop through the following
 mechanisms:
 • One-on-one meetings
 • Open office hours
 • Resources created for the internal MTERA Info Hub, available to all MTERA members.

 The project milestones, according to the technical work plan, were as follows:
 Milestone Description
 1.1.1 Application period launched for the selection of the workshop participants
 1.2.1 At least four data segments selected to establish the list of data to collect to
 participate in the REopt workshop
 1.1.2 Six to eight Tribal energy professional participants selected and confirmed
 to participate in the workshops
 1.2.2 Data collection assistance provided to workshop participants by the project
 team
 1.2.3 Two-day workshop held with six to eight Tribal energy professional
 participants
 2.3.1 Four financing one-on-one conversations held and a summary of resources
 published on the MTERA Info Hub
 2.3.2 At least four one-on-one sessions for additional REopt support and at least
 four Office Hour calls held
 2.4.1 Summary report of lessons from workshop participants to NLR REopt
 team finalized
 2.4.2 The REopt template slide deck finalized and published online for use by
 Tribal energy professionals

 The project plan changed four times during the period of performance:
 1. The project planned for six to eight participants. However, nine Tribal energy
 professionals applied, and the project plan was adapted to serve all nine applicants. Two
 planned participants were not able to attend due to internal conflicts.
 2. Milestone 2.3.1 (four financing one-on-one calls) was planned to finish on Sept. 30,
 2024. However, participants were too busy during the summer of 2024 to engage with the
 NLR team. The milestone was extended to Dec. 31, 2024 to accommodate participant
 schedules.
 3. The project was planned to end Dec. 31, 2024. However, the participants again faced
 time constraints. Therefore, the project period of performance was extended with a no-
 cost time extension until March 31, 2025.
 4. Milestone 2.3.2 required the NLR team to host four open office hours for any MTERA
 members. Despite consistent engagement and advertising, the office hours were under-
 utilized. For this reason, the NLR team decided to create resources MTERA could use at
 a future date. NLR created three resources, based on participant feedback, that are
 available to all MTERA members on the internal MTERA hub:
 a. Accounting for Grant Funding in the REopt Energy Planning Platform
 b. Guidance on Microgrid Design when using REopt
 c. REopt to Deployment
 In addition, NLR updated the REopt web tool to allow users to put a capital cost
 constraint as an input, a useful feature for modeling grant-funded projects. This change to
 the REopt web tool was proposed and completed based on feedback from workshop
 participants.

 3

 Path Forward
 The in-person workshop successfully built internal capacity for Tribal staff members to screen
 and plan energy projects. Participants are now able to use REopt to identify viable behind-the-
 meter solar projects with their Tribes, without the need for outside assistance. They also
 completed an analysis of a solar or solar-plus-storage system for a specific Tribal building,
 providing a valuable and immediately useful product. This training is replicable for future Tribal
 participants, with several proposed modifications, including:
 • Additional time and support for data collection: Participants used a data collection form
 to gather the information needed to model their proposed project in REopt. This form was
 useful, but the NLR team found that the process required more time and support than
 anticipated. The NLR team provided support for data formatting and clean up, as well as
 the processes to request data from utilities.
 • Extended workshop: The workshop could have been three days. Participants wanted
 additional time for REopt work periods, a longer tour of the NLR campus, and more one-
 on-one time with the NLR team.
 • Learning checks: Regular learning checks throughout the workshop could help keep
 participants on track and also ensure that the NLR team understands the participants’
 capacities.
