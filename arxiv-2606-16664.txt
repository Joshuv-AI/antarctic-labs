Dilating Semigroup Representations to the Boundary Quotient
Ujan Chakraborty

Abstract
. We prove that a representation of a group embeddable or right LCM cancellative semigroup may be dilated to a representation of its reduced boundary quotient C ⋆ C^{\star} -algebra if and only if it extends to a completely contractive representation of the reduced semigroup operator algebra. We show that the latter property is satisfied not only by all constructible representations of amenable semigroups, but also a very large class of other representations, which encompasses several classical dilation theorems and the corresponding matricial von Neumann inequalities. In fact, our criterion of completely contractive extension to the operator algebra turns out to be an appropriate generalisation of the matricial von Neumann inequality to semigroups more general than ℕ k \mathbb{N}^{k} , and dilation to the boundary quotient turns out to be an apt generalisation of unitary dilations. Thus, our theorem is in spirit and in practice a generalisation of Sz.-Nagy and Ando’s dilation theorems for general semigroups. In addition, this also demonstrates that any completely contractive representation of the operator algebra dilates to a representation with additional relations among its generators, the new relations coming from the boundary quotient. In particular, for Ore semigroups, this completely characterises which representations admit unitary dilations.

Introduction

We show in this paper that if
P
is a group-embeddable or right LCM cancellative semigroup, then every representation of
P
that extends to a completely contractive representation of the non-selfadjoint operator algebra generated by the left-regular representation
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
, dilates to a representation of the reduced boundary quotient
C^{\star}
-algebra
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
.
The beauty of our technique is that it is quite a general dilation theoretic idea that we use.
While the first mentioned property (complete contractivity) might be difficult to verify in general, it turns out to be an appropriate generalisation of the matricial von Neumann’s inequality for
\mathbb{N}^{k}
.
And while the boundary quotient is sometimes difficult to compute, for Ore semigroups, it is just the
C^{\star}
-algebra of the enveloping group, and thus dilation to the boundary quotient turns out to be an appropriate generalisation of the notion of unitary dilation.
This allows us to bring existing well-known dilation theorems like those of B. Sz.-Nagy
[
46
]
, T. Ando
[
1
]
, and J. A. Holbrook
[
21
]
under the same umbrella, provides necessary and sufficient conditions for unitary dilations of Ore semigroup representations, and demonstrates that they are all manifestations of the same dilation phenomenon.
This is considerably more general than existing generalisations of Sz.-Nagy’s dilation theorem, like the results by S. Brehmer
[
5
]
.

The general goal of dilation theory is to view maps between spaces as parts of nicer maps between potentially larger spaces.
For example, contractions on Hilbert spaces may be dilated to unitaries on larger Hilbert spaces, upto powers
[
46
]
: for any contraction
T
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}

U
on a bigger Hilbert space

such that
T^{n}=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}U^{n}|_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

.
This can be done for commuting pairs of contractions as well
[
1
]
.
However, such a result fails to hold more generally for arbitrary commuting triples of contractions, as noted in
[
38
]
, unless they are
2\times 2
complex matrices, in which case power dilations work for all
k
-tuples
[
21
]
.
This leads one to the following question, the answer to which is well-known:

Question A:
Under what conditions may commuting
k
-tuples of contractions be dilated to commuting
k
-tuples of unitaries upto powers?

Answer A:
A contractive
k
-tuple of commuting contractions has a commuting unitary dilation upto powers if and only if it satisfies a matricial von Neumann inequality.

Since
k
commuting contractions induce a contractive representation of
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, we realise that perhaps this question is answered in greater generality by viewing the representations of semigroups (in this case
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, but potentially a lot more general) as (non-selfadjoint) operator algebras, and their dilation theory.
For example, one may also formulate the following question:

Question B:
Under what conditions may a contractive representation of a semigroup be dilated to a unitary representation?
Or, more generally, what might be the counterpart of a unitary dilation?

As it turns out, the boundary quotient is the correct choice for generalising unitary dilations.
We briefly recall some of the motivation behind dilation to the “boundary”.
Consider the disk algebra
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{D}})
, holomorphic functions on the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
which are bounded and hence extend to continuous functions on its closure
\bar{{\mathbb{D}}}
.
They form a non-selfadjoint algebra.
Their closure under involution
C(\bar{{\mathbb{D}}})
is a
C^{\star}
-algebra, but it is not the “smallest”
C^{\star}
-algebra into which
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{D}})
embeds isometrically.
The right candidate for the latter would be all continuous functions on the unit circle,
C(\mathbb{T})
, and one may verify this by observing that by the maximum modulus principle, the map that sends a function in
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{D}})
to its restriction on
\mathbb{T}
is in fact an isometry.
One notes that
\mathbb{T}
is the (Šilov) boundary, and this phenomenon generalises classically to all homogeneous algebras.
C^{\star}
-algebras.
This was one of the motivations behinds the development of the theory of
C^{\star}
-envelopes.
For a (possibly noncommutative and non-selfadjoint) operator algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, one may consider
C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}})
to be the “smallest”
C^{\star}
-algebra into which
{\mathcal{A}}
embeds completely isometrically.

C^{\star}
-envelopes were first defined by W. Arveson
[
2
]
, and he conjectured their existence in general.
In case of unital operator algebras, the existence of the
C^{\star}
-envelope was proved by M. Hamana through injective envelopes
[
20
]
and M. Dritschel and S. A. McCullough through dilation-theoretic techniques
[
18
]
.
The non-unital case follows from R. Meyer’s work
[
32
]
.
The Dritschel-McCullough dilation theorem shows that every completely contractive representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
has a maximal dilation, and the
C^{\star}
-algebra generated by a maximal dilation of a completely isometric representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
is
C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}})
, which is in general “smaller” than
C^{\star}({\mathcal{A}})
(in fact, a quotient), in a similar fashion as
C(\mathbb{T})
, the
C^{\star}
-envelope of
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{D}})
, is a quotient of
C(\bar{{\mathbb{D}}})
.
Moreover, ever completely contractive representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
dilates to a representation of
C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}})
.

One makes a connection here, that
{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})
is in fact the operator algebra generated by a representation of the semigroup
\mathbb{N}
on the Hilbert space
L_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{D}})
that maps the generator
1
to
z
.
So, one might venture to ask what a suitable notion of boundary might be for representations of semigroups in general.
We point out that in the context of representations of semigroups, one has a quotient object, the boundary quotient, which is in general smaller than the
C^{\star}
-algebra generated by the left-regular representation of the semigroup.
The representation theory of semigroups is considerably more involved than their counterpart for groups, in the same fashion as non-selfadjoint operator algebras often offer more flexibility (and hence, hide greater complexity) in their representations than their selfadjoint counterparts.
In constructing both universal and reduced
C^{\star}
-algebras of semigroups (
C^{\star}_{s}(P)
and
C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
respectively), one must account for their ideal structure
[
30
]
.
Even then, one looks for “smaller”
C^{\star}
-algebras which encode complete information about
P
, and this leads us to the boundary quotient.
For
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, the (full and reduced) boundary quotient is isomorphic to
C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\cong C^{\star}(\mathbb{Z}^{k})
, which is a quotient of
C^{\star}(\mathbb{N}^{k})
, a much bigger
C^{\star}
-algebra (the Toeplitz algebra in the

Similar phenomena are seen for free semigroups
\mathbb{F}_{k}^{+}
, where the
C^{\star}
-algebra of the semigroup is the Cuntz-Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{TO}_{k}
, while the boundary quotient is the Cuntz algebra
\mathcal{O}_{k}
, a quotient of
\mathcal{TO}_{k}
.
This phenomenon was generalised to right-angled Artin monoids by J. Crisp and M. Laca in
[
13
]
.
Their construction was further extended to more general semigroups in two directions, one through full and reduced partial crossed products (e.g.
[
25
,
22
]
), the other through quotienting these
C^{\star}
-algebras by products of complements of projections of ideals which intersect every ideal non-trivially (called the boundary quotient relations)
[
8
]
. If
P
embeds into a group
G
, one has the co-universal property that reduced boundary quotient
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
is the “smallest”
C^{\star}
-algebra generated by a
G
-equivariant representation of
C^{\star}_{s}(P)

[
22
]
.
C. Sehnem proved in
[
43
]
that
C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P))
is canonically isomorphic to
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
if
P
is group-embeddable, and the same result was proved without the group-embeddability assumption by K. Brix, C. Bruce, and A. Dor-On in
[
6
]
in the right LCM setting, assuming cancellation.

And this finally leads us to our dilation result.
If a representation of a semigroup
P
extends to a completely contractive representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
, then we may use the Dritschel-McCullough dilation machine to dilate it to a representation of
C^{\star}_{env}(P)
.
Then, if the criterion of
[
43
,
6
]
, are met, we may dilate it to a representation of
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
, and thus we have our main theorem:

Theorem

(Theorem
6.10
)
.

Let
P
be a group-embeddable or right LCM cancellative semigroup.
Then, a representation of
P
dilates to a representation of
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
if and only if it extends to a completely contractive representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
.

This immediately allows us to answer question
B
posed above.
By Theorem
5.7
, we have that for a semigroup
P
with enveloping group
G
,
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
is isomorphic to
C^{\star}_{\lambda}(G)
if and only if
P
is right Ore.
Given that group representations are unitary, this allows us to answer Question B:

Answer B:
If
P
is Ore, then every contractive representation that extends to a completely contractive representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
, has a unitary dilation.

Sufficient conditions for representations to dilate to
\mathcal{Q}(P)
in the right LCM setting were obtained by M. Laca and B. Li in
[
24
]
through constructive methods, but their conditions are very strict, and are not satisfied by many representations, including the classical ones in
[
1
,
21
]
.
But the conditions we require for the Dritschel-McCoullough dilation machinery to operate for the dilation to
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
are more general than theirs, and not just sufficient but also necessary.
This allows one to reconcile with some existing operator-theoretic dilation results like the ones mentioned above.
The only drawback is that it might be difficult to check complete contractivity in specific cases, and may still need heavy operator-theoretic machinery.
But we show that this condition is equivalent to the matricial von Neumann inequality in the case of
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, and hence is not expected to require techniques considerably more difficult than existing ones, and is in fact more generally applicable.

This relation between matricial norm properties of a representation of a semigroup and the existence of a dilation to its boundary quotient might seem unexpected, and makes sense only through reflection into the nature of the Dritschel-McCullough dilation machine, and the equivalence of the
C^{\star}
-envelope and the boundary quotient.
Meanwhile, one may note that though the semigroup and its boundary quotient are generated by the same elements, the latter might have many more relations, simply due to the fact that the former does not have inverses in general while the latter has adjoints which must respect the ideal structure of the former.
These are precisely the foundation set relations.
This leads us to the Corollary
6.18
, that every representation that extends to a completely contractive representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
, also admits a dilation with these additional relations among the generators.

The structure of the paper is the following:
in section 2, we provide dilation-theoretic preliminaries, and introduce the notions of envelopes and boundaries.
In section 3, we provide background on semigroups, while 4 and 5 are background on semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebras and boundary quotients respectively.
Finally, in section 6, we demonstrate the dilation phenomenon.

Preliminaries on Dilation Theory, Envelopes, and Boundaries

The general goal of dilation theory is to view an operator (or a map into a space of operators) as a part of something simpler on a larger space.
Its origins can be traced to operator dilation theory stemming from B. Szőkefalvi-Nagy’s celebrated dilation theorem
[
46
]
. In this paper, we shall be dealing principally with dilations of representations. For us, an
operator algebra

{\mathcal{A}}
will be a norm-closed subalgebra of
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, not necessarily self-adjoint. Furthermore, we shall assume our operator algebras to be unital, and our representations to be unital as well.
We refer the reader to K. R. Davidson’s recent textbook
[
15
]
for a detailed account on some of the topics we briefly mention in this section, and O. M. Shalit’s notes
[
44
]
for a detailed introduction to dilation theory.
For a general introduction to completely bounded maps we refer to V. I. Paulsen’s celebrated textbook
[
40
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given an operator algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, we call
(\mathcal{C},{\iota})
a
C^{\star}
-cover
of
{\mathcal{A}}
, if
{\iota}:{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{C}
is a completely isometric representation with
\mathcal{C}=C^{\star}({\iota}({\mathcal{A}}))
.
The
C^{\star}
-envelope
of
{\mathcal{A}}
is a
C^{\star}
-cover
(C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}}),{\iota})
that has the following co-universal property: if
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},{\iota}^{\prime})
is another
C^{\star}
-cover of
{\mathcal{A}}
, then there exists a (necessarily unique)
\star
-epimorphism
\Phi:\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\to C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}})
such that
\Phi({\iota}^{\prime}(a))={\iota}(a)
for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

We should mention that there is a high amount of non-uniformity in terminology regarding the terms introduced in the rest of this section.
What we refer to as “maximal” dilations, are occasionally called “extremal”, or
\partial
-representations. We refer the reader to
[
18
,
3
,
4
,
15
]
for further reading.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an operator algebra.
Given a representation
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, another representation
\phi^{\prime}:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
is called a
dilation
of
\phi
if
{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
, and
\phi(a)=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\phi^{\prime}(a)|_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
A completely contractive representation
\phi
is called
maximal
if every dilation is trivial, that is, for every dilation
\phi^{\prime}:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
,
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\phi^{\prime}(a)=\phi^{\prime}(a)|_{\mathcal{H}}=\phi(a)
for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
(which is equivalent to saying that one may decompose
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime\prime}
such that
\phi^{\prime}=\phi\oplus\phi^{\prime\prime}
for some representation
\phi^{\prime\prime}
).

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem 1.1,
[
18
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an operator algebra.
Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is a maximal representation.

(ii)

For every
C^{\star}
-cover
(\mathcal{C},\iota)
of
{\mathcal{A}}
, there exists a representation
\psi:\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
satisfying
\psi\circ\iota=\phi
, and the only completely positive map agreeing with
\psi
when restricted to
\iota({\mathcal{A}})
is
\psi
itself.

The second condition in Theorem
2.3
is called the unique extension property.

Theorem 2.4

(Parts of theorems 1.2 and 4.1,
[
18
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an operator algebra, and
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
a completely contractive representation.
Then,
\phi
has a maximal dilation.
Moreover, if
\phi
is completely isometric, and
\phi^{\prime}
is its maximal dilation, then
C^{\star}(\phi^{\prime}({\mathcal{A}}))\cong C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}})
.

This theorem also proved that the
C^{\star}
-envelope exists for every operator algebra. We are yet to properly define what a boundary means in this context. The basic examples of
C^{\star}
-envelopes, as well as motivation for boundaries, arise in the context of uniform algebras.

Example 2.5
.

Recall that we observed in the introduction, for the disk algebra
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{D}})
, we have
C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{D}}))\cong C(\mathbb{T})
, the quotient map mapping every bounded holomorphic function to a function on the unit circle obtained by restricting its domain to the latter, and the maximum modulus ensuring that this is an isometry.

The result in Example
2.5
is not an isolated one.
The
C^{\star}
-envelope of any uniform algebra is the
C^{\star}
-algebra of continuous functions on its Šilov boundary.
The unconditional existence of the
C^{\star}
-envelope (Theorem
2.4
) provides a noncommutative analogue of this result.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an operator algebra.
An ideal
\mathcal{I}\lhd C^{\star}({\mathcal{A}})
is called a
boundary ideal
if the quotient map
q_{\mathcal{I}}:C^{\star}({\mathcal{A}})\to C^{\star}({\mathcal{A}})/\mathcal{I}
restricts to a complete isometry on
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq C^{\star}({\mathcal{A}})
.
The
Šilov ideal

\mathcal{I}_{s}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
is defined to to be the boundary ideal that contains all boundary ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}
.

The existence of the Šilov ideal can be seen from the existence of
C^{\star}
-envelope:
C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}})
is canonically isomorphic to
C^{\star}({\mathcal{A}})/\mathcal{I}_{s}
.

Preliminaries on Semigroups

A semigroup is a set with an associative binary operation, traditionally referred to as a multiplication.
In this paper that by semigroup we mean a semigroup with an identity element, that is, a monoid.
For example, we shall in this spirit assume
(\mathbb{N},+)
to mean the additive natural numbers with
0
.
All semigroups under consideration in this paper will be discrete and countable.
We begin with the following essential property:

Definition 3.1
.

A semigroup
P
is said to be
left cancellative
if for any
p,q,x\in P
,
xp=xq\implies p=q
.
Correspondingly, we say
P
is
right cancellative
if for any
p,q,x\in P
,
px=qx\implies p=q
.
We say
P
is
cancellative
if it is left and right cancellative.

We denote the set of invertible elements (also called units) of
P
by
P^{\star}
.
Every group is (trivially) a semigroup, and it is interesting to consider which conditions on a semigroup would ensure that it is (faithfully) embeddable in a group.
A necessary but not sufficient condition for this is the cancellative property.
A. Mal’cev gave an infinite list of conditions which are necessary and sufficient for group-embeddability of a semigroup, and showed that no finite list of those conditions suffices.
We refer the reader to Section 12 of
[
11
]
for further details.
We mention one particular situation in which
P
is group-embeddable, which will be useful later.
We denote
pP:=\{{px\;|\;x\in P}\}
and define
Pp
in a similar fashion.

Definition 3.2
.

We say a semigroup
P
is
right-reversible
if for all
p,q\in P
,
Pp\cap Pq\neq\emptyset
; and
left-reversible
if for all
p,q\in P
,
pP\cap qP\neq\emptyset
.

P
is called
left Ore
if it is cancellative and right-reversible; and
right Ore
if it is cancellative and left-reversible.

Theorem 3.3
.

[Ore, Dubreil]
A semigroup
P
embeds into a group
G
of the form
G=P^{-1}P
if and only if it is left-Ore.
Similarly,
P
embeds into a group
G
of the form
G=PP^{-1}
if and only if it is right-Ore.

We direct the reader to Theorem 1.24 in
[
10
]
or Section 1.1 of
[
26
]
for further details on Theorem
3.3
.
Not all group-embeddable semigroups are Ore:
for example, see
[
37
]
for the case of Artin monoids.
It is also a pertinent question as to which group we choose to embed
P
into, if there are multiple options.
Here, we may carry out a universal construction following
[
11
]
. If a semigroup
P
is group-embeddable, there exists a universal group embedding
\iota_{univ}:P\hookrightarrow G_{univ}(P)
such that for any other group embedding
\iota:P\hookrightarrow G
, we have a unique homomorphism
\Phi:G_{univ}(P)\to G
such that
\iota=\Phi\circ\iota_{univ}
, that is, the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{univ}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{univ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

Hence, it will suffice for us to just state that that
P
embeds into some group
G
without specifying which group it is.

In the group-embeddable setting, A. Nica first defined quasi-lattice ordered semigroups in
[
35
]
, and studied their isometric covariant representations.
The following generalisation, which was developed in
[
8
]
, removed the group-embeddability assumption and the reference to the order.

Definition 3.4
.

A left-cancellative semigroup
P
is said to be right LCM if for all
p,q\in P
, either
pP\cap qP=rP
for some
r\in P
, or
pP\cap qP=\emptyset
.

This
r
may possibly be non-unique, but one observes directly that if
r
and
r^{\prime}
both satisfy
pP\cap qP=rP=r^{\prime}P
, then there exists
u\in P^{\star}
such that
r^{\prime}=ru
.
We denote the set of right LCM elements of
p,q
by
p\vee q=\{{r\in P\textrm{ }\;|\;\textrm{ }pP\cap qP=rP}\}
.
For a finite subset
F\subset P
, we denote
\vee F:=\{{r\in P\textrm{ }\;|\;\textrm{ }\bigcap_{p\in F}pP=rP}\}
.
These semigroups have been referred to and studied under various names in the past: for examples, M. Lawson considers the dual definition in
[
27
]
as CRM monoids (after Clifford, Reilly, and McAlister), and M. D. Norling refers to them as semigroups satisfying Clifford’s condition in
[
36
]
.

We clarify a further bit of notation for the subsequent considerations.
For all
p\in P
and
X\subseteq P
, we denote
p^{-1}X:=\{{x\in P\;|\;px\in X}\}
.
Further, if
P
embeds in
G
,
p^{-1}X=\{{p^{-1}x\;|\;x\in G}\}\cap P
.
For
p_{1},q_{1},\dots,p_{n},q_{n}\in P
, the set
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}p_{2}^{-1}q_{2}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}P
does not depend on the actual product of these elements, but is equal to
p_{1}^{-1}(q_{1}(p_{2}^{-1}(q_{2}(\dots(p_{n}^{-1}(q_{n}P))))))
, which is equal to

p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}P\cap p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}p_{2}^{-1}q_{2}P\cap\dots\cap p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}p_{2}^{-1}q_{2}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}P
.

such that
p=qr
(resp.
q=pr
), then
p\vee q=p
, (resp.
p\vee q=q
) otherwise
p\vee q=\emptyset
.

(iii)

Artin monoids are an important case.
Denote by
\langle s,t\rangle_{m}
the word where
s
and
t
alternate
m
times, that is,
\langle s,t\rangle_{4}=stst
.
Consider a symmetric matrix
M=(m_{i,j})_{i,j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
such that
m_{i,i}=1
for all
i
, and
m_{i,j}\in\{{2,3,\dots,+\infty}\}
for
i\neq j
.
Then, define
A_{M}^{+}:=\left\langle e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\;|\;\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle_{m_{i,j}}=\langle e_{j},e_{i}\rangle_{m_{j,i}}\right\rangle^{+}
to be the Artin monoid associated with
M
, and
A_{M}:=\left\langle e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\;|\;\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle_{m_{i,j}}=\langle e_{j},e_{i}\rangle_{m_{j,i}}\right\rangle
to be the Artin group associated with
M
, with the convention that
m_{i,j}=\infty
implies that there is no relation between
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
.

A_{M}^{+}
embeds into
A_{M}

[
37
]
, but is not always Ore.

A_{M}^{+}
is always right LCM.
If
m_{i,j}\in\{{2,+\infty}\}
for
i\neq j
, we call
A_{M}^{+}
(resp.
A_{M}
) a right-angled Artin monoid (resp. right-angled Artin group).
Right-angled Artin groups are very closely related to Coxeter groups
C_{M}
, generated by the same relations as
A_{M}
along with the additional relation that
e_{i}^{2}=e
.
If
C_{M}
is finite, then we say that
A_{M}^{+}
is of finite type (or spherical type).
Their

-algebras have been discussed in detail in
[
12
,
13
]
.
It was proved in
[
16
]
that
A_{M}^{+}
is Ore if and only if it is of finite type.
Note that if
A_{M}^{+}
has
k
generators with
m_{i,j}=2
for all
i\neq j
then we get
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, while for
m_{i,j}=\infty
we get
\mathbb{F}_{k}^{+}
.

4.
Semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebras and Amenability

In recent years, there has been significant interest in the study of
C^{\star}
-algebras and operator algebras of semigroups
[
14
]
.
The representation theory of semigroups is much more nuanced than their counterpart for groups.
While it is a perfectly good idea to define the reduced semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebra of a semigroup
P
as the norm-closed subalgebra of
{\mathcal{B}}(\ell_{2}(P))
generated by the (isometric) left-regular representation and closed under involution, unfortunately, considering the universal algebra generated by these isometries yields something that is far too large.
In fact, it was proved by G. J. Murphy
[
33
]
that such a
C^{\star}
-algebra for even
\mathbb{N}^{2}
, the universal
C^{\star}
-algebra generated by two commuting isometries, is non-nuclear!
The problem was addressed by X. Li in
[
30
]
by insisting that the representation keep track of the ideal structure of the semigroup as well, and this led to proper notions of universal
C^{\star}
-algebras of semigroups.
X. Li also studied the notion of amenability in the context of semigroups in the same paper.

4.1.
Semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebras

Consider a discrete countable left cancellative semigroup
P
, and its left-regular representation
V:P\to{\mathcal{B}}(\ell_{2}(P))
,
V_{p}(\delta_{q})=\delta_{pq}
.
It is straightforward to verify that
V
is an isometric representation (i.e.,
V_{p}
is an isometry for every
p\in P
).
This allows us to define the following reduced semigroup algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

We define the
reduced semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebra
of
P
to be the
C^{\star}
-algebra generated by its left-regular representation, i.e.
C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P):=C^{\star}(V_{p}\;|\;p\in P)\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(\ell_{2}(P))
.
Similarly, we define the
reduced semigroup operator algebra
of
P
to be the (possibly non-selfadjoint) operator algebra generated by the left-regular representation, i.e.
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P):=\overline{\textrm{alg}}\left\{V_{p}\;|\;p\in P\right\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(\ell_{2}(P))
.

Some authors also refer to
C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
as the Toeplitz algebra of the semigroup,
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(P)
.
A proper candidate for a universal semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebra was proposed by X. Li in
[
30
]
, using the structure of the space of right ideals of the semigroup.
We say a subset
X\subseteq P
is a right (resp. left) ideal of
P
if
Xp\subseteq X
(resp.
pX\subseteq X
) for all
p\in P
.
Groups lack non-trivial ideals, but for semigroups such spaces can be interesting.
We further restrict ourselves to the space of
constructible right ideals
, which is defined in the following fashion:

Definition 4.2
.

We define the set of
constructible right ideals

\mathcal{J}
of
P
to be the smallest family of right ideals of
P
satisfying the following properties:

(I1)

P\in\mathcal{J}
and
\emptyset\in\mathcal{J}
.

(I2)

For all
p\in P
and
X\in\mathcal{J}
,
pX\in\mathcal{J}
and
p^{-1}X\in\mathcal{J}
.

(I3)

For all
X,Y\in\mathcal{J}
,
X\cap Y\in\mathcal{J}
.

One might observe here that the set
\mathcal{J}
is actually a semilattice, i.e. an abelian semigroup of idempotents with the operation being intersection of ideals.
There is a duality between semilattices and
totally disconnected locally compact Hausdorff spaces.
We refer the reader to
[
14
]
for further details on this duality.
It is proved in Lemma 3.3 of
[
30
]
that (I1) and (I2) imply (I3), that is, one may state:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=\left\{q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}\dots q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}P\;|\;n\in\mathbb{N},\textrm{ and }p_{i},q_{i}\in P\textrm{ for all }i\in\{1,\dots n\}\right\}\cup\{\emptyset\}.

We further need to define the following independence condition on
\mathcal{J}
, from
[
30
]
:

Definition 4.3
.

If for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all collections
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of ideals in
\mathcal{J}
,
X=\cup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in\mathcal{J}
implies
X=X_{i}
for some
i
, then we say that
\mathcal{J}
is
independent
.

If
P
is right LCM (as in Definition
3.4
), then all constructible right ideals are
principal
, that is,
\mathcal{J}=\{pP\;|\;p\in P\}
.
The independence condition on
\mathcal{J}
is thus automatically satisfied if
P
is right LCM.
In
[
30
]
, X. Li formulated the following two universal
C^{\star}
-algebras, Definitions
4.4
and
4.5
, which capture the ideal structure of the semigroup:

Definition 4.4
.

The
full semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebra

C^{\star}(P)
of a semigroup
P
is the universal
C^{\star}
-algebra constructed by the set of isometries
\{v_{p}\;|\;p\in P\}
and the set of projections
e_{X}\;|\;X\in\mathcal{J}\}
satisfying the following relations, for all
p,q\in P
and all
X,Y\in\mathcal{J}
:

(F1i)

v_{p}v_{q}=v_{pq}
,

(F1ii)

v_{p}e_{X}v_{p}^{\star}=e_{pX}
,

(F2i)

e_{P}=1
,

(F2ii)

e_{\emptyset}=0
, and

(F2iii)

e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X\cap Y}
.

By Lemma 2.8 of
[
30
]
, these definitions imply that
v_{p}^{\star}e_{X}v_{p}=e_{p^{-1}X}
, and thus

(4.2)

\displaystyle e_{q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}\dots q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}P}=v_{q_{1}}^{\star}v_{p_{1}}\dots v_{q_{n}}^{\star}v_{p_{n}}v_{p_{n}}^{\star}v_{q_{n}}\dots v_{p_{1}}^{\star}v_{q_{1}}

By equation
4.1
, this proves that
C^{\star}(P)
is generated by the isometries
\{v_{p}\;|\;p\in P\}
.
The canonical surjective
\star
-homomorphism, the left-regular representation
\lambda:C^{\star}(P)\to C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
, can thus be affected by mapping
v_{p}
to
V_{p}
for every
p\in P
.
We define the diagonal subalgebra

D(P):=C^{\star}(e_{X}\;|\;X\in\mathcal{J})\subseteq C^{\star}(P),

that is, the universal
C^{\star}
-algebra generated by the projections corresponding to elements in
\mathcal{J}
.
By (F2iii), one may observe that this is a commutative
C^{\star}
-algebra.
For every
X\in\mathcal{J}
, one has the projection
E_{X}\in C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
(on the subspace
\ell_{2}(X)\subseteq\ell_{2}(P)
) which may be obtained from equation
4.2
by applying the map
\lambda
.
This allows one to define

D_{\lambda}(P):=C^{\star}(E_{X}\;|\;X\in\mathcal{J})=\lambda(D(P))\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(\ell_{2}(P)).

X. Li further defines the following universal
C^{\star}
-algebra when
P
is group-embeddable, which is instrumental in most considerations:

Definition 4.5
.

Let
P
be a semigroup which embeds into a group
G
.
The
constructible semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebra

C^{\star}_{s}(P)
of a semigroup
P
is the universal
C^{\star}
-algebra constructed by the set of isometries
\{v_{p}\;|\;p\in P\}
and the set of projections
\{e_{X}\;|\;X\in\mathcal{J}\}
satisfying the following relations, for all
p,q\in P
and all
X,Y\in\mathcal{J}
:

(S1)

v_{p}v_{q}=v_{pq}
,

(S2)

e_{\emptyset}=0
,

(S3)

for all
p_{1},q_{1},\dots p_{n},q_{n}\in P
satisfying
p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}=e
in
G
,

v_{p_{1}}^{\star}v_{q_{1}}\dots v_{p_{n}}^{\star}v_{q_{n}}=e_{q_{n}^{-1}p_{n}\dots q_{1}^{-1}p_{1}P}.

It can be shown that conditions (F1ii), (F2i), (F2iii) can be deduced to hold from (S3), and hence there exists a canonical surjective
\star
-homorphism
\pi_{s}:C^{\star}(P)\to C^{\star}_{s}(P)
that maps
v_{p}\in C^{\star}(P)
to
v_{p}\in C^{\star}_{s}(P)
and
e_{X}\in C^{\star}(P)
to
e_{X}\in C^{\star}_{s}(P)
.

C^{\star}_{s}(P)
is also generated by
\{v_{p}\;|\;p\in P\}
, and one has a a canonical
\star
-homomorphism, the left-regular representation,
\lambda_{s}:C^{\star}_{s}(P)\to C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
, which maps
v_{p}
to
V_{p}
.
Furthermore, one defines

D_{s}(P):=C^{\star}(e_{X}\;|\;X\in\mathcal{J})\subseteq C^{\star}_{s}(P),

and
\pi_{s}|_{D(P)}:D(P)\to D_{s}{(P)}
is a surjective
\star
-homomorphism.

That is, one has the following commutative diagrams:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\star}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\star}_{s}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{s}}

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(P)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{s}|_{D_{s}(p)}}

Corollary 4.6

(Corollary 3.3 of
[

]
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{J}
is independent.

(ii)

\lambda|_{D(P)}
is an isomorphism.

(iii)

\lambda|_{D_{s}(P)}
is an isomorphism.

One should be careful that unlike the reduced case, the term “universal Toeplitz
C^{\star}
-algebra”
\mathcal{T}_{u}(P)
means something different from the universal
C^{\star}
-algebras defined here.
M. Laca and C. Sehnem defined and studied these universal algebras.

\mathcal{T}_{u}(P)
is in general a quotient of
C^{\star}_{s}(P)
, and they are isomorphic if and only if
\mathcal{J}
satisfies the independence condition.
We refer the reader to
[
25
]
for details.

Remark 4.7
.

It is not completely clear when the map
\pi_{s}:C^{\star}(P)\to C^{\star}_{s}(P)
will be an isomorphism.
It can be shown to be true when
P
is quasi-lattice ordered, or when
P
is Ore.
However, as we shall see in the next subsection, in the amenable case, if
\mathcal{J}
is independent,
C^{\star}(P)\cong C^{\star}_{s}(P)\cong C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
.
Moreover, these
C^{\star}
-algebras are nuclear.

4.2.
Amenability of Semigroups

We briefly look at how amenability of the semigroup manifests itself in the relation between its semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebras.
We state the definition used by X. Li in
[
30
]
:

Definition 4.8
.

Let
P
be a discrete semigroup.
We say
P
is left-amenable if there exists a left-invariant mean on
\ell_{\infty}(P)
, that is, a linear functional
\mu\in(\ell_{\infty}(P))^{\star}
such that
\mu(f(p\sqcup))=\mu(f)
for all
f\in\ell_{\infty}(P)
and all
p\in P
, where
f(p\sqcup)
refers to the composition of
f
with the left-multiplication by
p
.

It is also possible to define right-amenability in a similar fashion.
Amenability of semigroups is in general a broad and interesting topic: for example, X. Li showed in
[
30
]
that one may obtain for semigroups conditions analogous to (1)-(7), Theorem 6.8,
[
7
]
, as stated for groups.
However, we restrict ourselves to only two of these conditions for the moment, and refer the interested reader to Section 4 of
[
30
]
, or to A. Paterson’s celebrated monograph
[
39
]
for further details.

Theorem 4.9

(Part of Statements 4.1,
[
30
]
)
.

Let
P
be a group-embeddable semigroup, consider the following two conditions:

(i)

P
is left-amenable.

(ii)

The left-regular representation
\lambda:C^{\star}_{s}(P)\to C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
is an isomorphism and there exists a non-zero character on
C^{\star}_{s}(P)
.

It is always true that
(i)\implies(ii)
.
In addition, if
\mathcal{J}
is independent, then
(ii)\implies(i)
as well.

One further has, for the full semigroup
C^{\star}
-algebra:

Theorem 4.10

(Part of Theorem 5.6.42,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
P
be a cancellative semigroup, and
\mathcal{J}
be independent.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

P
is left-amenable.

(ii)

The left-regular representation
\lambda:C^{\star}(P)\to C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
is an isomorphism and there exists a non-zero character on
C^{\star}(P)
.

A. Nica developed the following notion of amenability for quasi-lattice ordered semigroups (generalisable to the right LCM case) in
[
35
]
:

Definition 4.11
.

A right LCM semigroup
P
is said to be Nica-amenable if the left-regular representation
\lambda:C^{\star}(P)\to C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
is an isomorphism.

Remark 4.12
.

For right LCM semigroups, the independence condition of
\mathcal{J}
always holds, and hence conditions (i) and (ii) of Theorem
4.10
are equivalent.
At this point, it is tempting to conclude that Nica-amenability as defined in Definition
4.11
is equivalent to the general notion of left-amenability as defined in Definition
4.8
.
However, that is not actually true, since even if
P
is Nica-amenable, it might fail to have a non-zero character on the
C^{\star}
-algebra.
And this is indeed what happens for
\mathbb{F}_{k}^{+}
, as we shall see in the examples below.
So, for right LCM semigroups, left-amenability in the sense of X. Li is strictly stronger than Nica-amenability.
In the subsequent considerations, we shall use “amenability” to mean left-amenability in the sense of X. Li, unless explicitly mentioned otherwise.

We conclude this section with a couple of examples (and non-examples):

Example 4.13
.

We return to the examples listed at the end of Section 2:

(i)

{\mathbb{N}^{k}}
is amenable.
In fact, all abelian semigroups are left (and right) amenable, both in the sense of X. Li and A. Nica.

(ii)

\mathbb{F}_{k}^{+}
is Nica amenable, but not amenable in the sense of X. Li.
We refer the reader to
[
35
]
for a proof.

(iii)

Artin monoids are Nica amenable if and only if they are right-angled
[
12
,
23
]
.

5.
Boundary Quotients

Early studies into boundary quotients of semigroups were carried out by J. Crisp and M. Laca
[
13
]
for right-angled Artin monoids (
\mathbb{F}_{k}^{+}
and
\mathbb{N}^{k}
are both special cases of these), and they also investigated questions of pure infiniteness and simplicity.
Their construction was generalised in two separate ways, which we briefly describe in this section.
If
P
embeds into a group
G
, there is a natural partial action of
G
on the (abelian) reduced diagonal subalgebra
D_{\lambda}(P)
, and consequently its spectrum
\Omega_{P}
.

\Omega_{P}
can be shown to have a unique smallest
G
-invariant subspace, which we refer to as
\partial\Omega_{P}
.
Then, the reduced crossed product
C^{\star}
-algebra
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P):=C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes_{r}G
is defined to be the boundary quotient
C^{\star}
-algebra of the semigroup.
It satisfies certain desirable co-universal properties:
it is the co-universal object in the category of
G
-equivariant representations of
C^{\star}_{s}(P)
, and is isomorphic to the
C^{\star}
-envelope of the co-system of
G
acting on
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
.
We refer the reader to
[
42
,
25
,
17
,
22
]
for details.
For right LCM semigroups
P
, one also has a characterisation of the boundary quotient
\mathcal{Q}(P)
via a maximality of relations picture.
One defines foundation sets of
P
as those which generate principal ideals that intersect any other principal ideal non-trivially.
A vanishing condition on the products of complement projections for the ideals corresponding to the foundation set elements, generates an ideal of the universal
C^{\star}
-algebra, and quotienting with respect to this ideal yields the boundary quotient
C^{\star}
-algebra. We refer the reader to
[
45
,
8
,
9
,
24
]
for details.
The connection between them has been briefly discussed in
[
25
]
.
The core of the idea is that closed
G
-invariant subsets of the Nica spectrum (
\Omega_{P}
) correspond to sets of elementary relations on
P
, and the unique smallest
G
-invariant subset
\partial\Omega_{P}
corresponds to the maximal such set of elementary relations.

5.1.
Boundary Quotients via Partial Crossed Products

One way to define boundary quotients in the general setting is through partial actions, which are a generalisation of the notion of an action.
The core of the idea is that instead of an action of a group by bijections of a set (or suitable isomorphisms, e.g. homeomorphisms in the topological case), we only ask for
partial
bijections on a set, that is, maps which are bijections on suitably restricted domains, and compositions / inverses that are defined on suitably restricted domains as well.
It is also possible to define reduced and universal (partial) crossed product
C^{\star}
-algebras from these actions, and one has the canonical surjective
\star
-homomorphism from the latter to the former.
We refer the interested reader to
[
19
,
31
]
for further background.

Now, we consider the inverse semigroup for
P
, constructed in the following fashion using the left-regular representation:

\mathcal{I}_{V}=\left\{V_{p_{1}}^{\star}V_{q_{1}}\dots V_{p_{n}}^{\star}V_{q_{n}}\;|\;\textrm{ for all }p_{i},q_{i}\in P,\;1\leq i\leq n,\;n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

It is straightforward to verify that it is indeed an inverse semigroup (recall that an inverse semigroup is a semigroup satisfying that for every
x
there exists a unique
y
with
xyx=x
) with
VV^{\star}V=V
for all
V\in\mathcal{I}_{V}
.

Assume that
P
embeds into a group
G
.
We denote
\mathcal{I}_{V}^{\times}=\mathcal{I}_{V}\backslash\{0\}
, and define the homomorphism

\sigma:\mathcal{I}_{V}^{\times}\to G,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;V_{p_{1}}^{\star}V_{q_{1}}\dots V_{p_{n}}^{\star}V_{q_{n}}\mapsto p_{1}^{-1}q_{1}\dots p_{n}^{-1}q_{n}.

It can be proved
[
22
]
that
D_{\lambda}(P)=\overline{span}\{\sigma^{-1}(e)\}
.
We further define

D_{g^{-1}}:=\overline{span}\{V^{\star}V\;|\;V\in\mathcal{I}_{V}^{\times},\;\sigma(V)=g\}\subseteq D_{\lambda}(P).

It was shown in Section 3.3
[
31
]
, that
D_{g^{-1}}
is an ideal of
D_{\lambda}(P)
for every
g\in G
.
Noting that
\ell_{2}(P)
is a subspace of
\ell_{2}(G)
, we may identify
C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(\ell_{2}(P))
.
We observe that for all
V\in\sigma^{-1}(g)
, denoting
\lambda_{g}
as the left-regular representation of
G
on
\ell_{2}(G)
,
V=\lambda_{g}V^{\star}V
, and hence it induces a
\star
-homomorphism

\theta_{g}^{\star}:D_{g^{-1}}\to D_{g},\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;V^{\star}V\mapsto\lambda_{g}V^{\star}V\lambda_{g}^{\star}=VV^{\star}.

By construction,
D_{\lambda}(P)
and
D_{g^{-1}}
are abelian
C^{\star}
-algebras, and if we denote their spectra as
\Omega_{P}
and
\Omega_{g^{-1}}
respectively, we may identify
\Omega_{g^{-1}}
as an open subset of
\Omega_{P}
, and
\theta_{g}^{\star}
as described above induces
\theta_{g^{-1}}
to be a partial action of
G
on
\Omega_{P}
by

\theta_{g}^{\star}(\chi)=\chi\circ\theta_{g^{-1}},\;\;\;\;\textrm{ for all }\chi\in D_{g^{-1}}\cong C_{0}(\Omega_{g^{-1}}).

Proposition 5.1

(Proposition 3.12,
[
22
]
; Proposition 3.10,
[
31
]
)
.

Let
P
be a semigroup-embeddable in a group
G
.
There exists a canonical isomorphism

C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\to D_{\lambda}(P)\rtimes_{r}G,

which maps
V_{p}
to the image of
p\in G
in
D_{\lambda}(P)\rtimes_{r}G
.

In
[
31
]
it was shown that
\Omega_{P}
has a unique smallest
G
-invariant subset, which we shall denote by
\partial\Omega_{P}
.
The space
\partial\Omega_{P}
can be identified using the semilattice of idempotents on
P
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
P
be a semigroup that embeds in a group
G
, and the space
\partial\Omega_{P}
defined as above.
The boundary quotient of
C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
is defined to be:

\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P):=C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes_{r}G.

One may define full boundary quotients as well, in an analogous fashion as done above, we refer the interested reader to
[
25
]
for further details. And now we conclude this subsection by stating a co-universality criterion for the boundary quotient.

Definition 5.3
.

We call an isometric representation of
P

constructible
if it induces a representation of
C^{\star}_{s}(P)
.

Theorem 5.4

(Theorem 4.2,
[
22
]
)
.

Let
P
be a semigroup, embeddable in a group
G
.
If
\partial_{G}[C^{\star}_{s}(P)]
is the
C^{\star}
-algebra of a
G
-equivariant constructible representation of
P
such that for every
G
-equivariant constructible representation
T
, there exists a canonical surjective
\star
-homomorphism
C^{\star}(T)\to\partial_{G}[C^{\star}_{s}(P)]
that maps the generators to themselves, then

\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\cong\partial_{G}[C^{\star}_{s}(P)].

In the right LCM cancellative setting situation, even if

is not group-embeddable, it is still possible to construct boundary quotients through normal coactions, as developed in
[
6
]
.

5.2.
Boundary Quotients via Foundation Sets

One has an alternate definition of the boundary quotient in terms of foundation sets.
This definition was developed in the context of right LCM semigroups by
[
45
,
8
,
24
]
, and extended to the general setting in
[
25
]
, and further refined in the right LCM setting in
[
9
]
.
C. Starling proved in
[
45
]
that this boundary quotient is in fact the tight
C^{\star}
-algebra of the inverse semigroup, and addressed the issues of pure infiniteness and simplicity.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
P
be a right LCM semigroup.
A finite subset
F
is called a
foundation set
for
P
is for any
p\in P
, there exists some
f\in F
such that
pP\cap fP\neq\emptyset
.
The boundary quotient
\mathcal{Q}(P)
is the universal
C^{\star}
-algebra generated by the isometries
\{v_{p}\;|\;p\in P\}
and the projections
\{e_{X}\;|\;X\in\mathcal{J}\}
satisfying the relations in Defintion
4.4
and that whenever
F
is a foundation set of
P
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Pi_{f\in F}(1-e_{fP})=0.

That is,
\mathcal{Q}(P)
is a quotient of
C^{\star}(P)
by the ideal generated by relations of the form
\Pi_{f\in F}(1-e_{fP})=0
for every foundation set
F
.
As pointed out by B. Li and M. Laca in
[
24
]
, denoting
I_{\infty}
to be the ideal of
D(P)\cong D_{\lambda}(P)=C(\Omega_{P})
generated by projections of the form
\Pi_{f\in F}(1-e_{fP})=0
for every foundation set
F
, one has
C(\partial\Omega_{P})\cong C(\Omega_{P})/I_{\infty}
, and
\mathcal{Q}(P)\cong C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes G\cong C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes P
, where the last equivalence (partial crossed product with respect to a semigroup action) is defined and studied in
[
24
]
.

This notion may extended beyond the right LCM setting as well, as noted in Remark 5.5 of
[
8
]
by replacing finite subsets of
P
by finite subsets of
\mathcal{J}
which have non-trivial intersection with every ideal (since not every constructible ideal is principle in the general case).
This was further studied in
[
25
]
, where they
used it in the concrete presentation of the covariance algebra, which is the full counterpart of the crossed-product version of the boundary quotient. We conclude this section by recognising that in practice it might be difficult to take the quotient with respect to
every
foundation set, but N. Brownlowe and N. Stammeier developed the notion of
accurate refinement property
in
[
9
]
, where they reduced the computation by restricting to only certain types of foundation sets, and showed that this property holds in practice for a very large class of semigroups, in particular including directed semigroups and semigroups where principal right ideals corresponding to non-comparable elements are disjoint.

5.3.
Computing Boundary Quotients

We begin this section with the following celebrated result by C. Sehnem.

Theorem 5.6

(Corollary 5.4,

]
and Theorem 5.4,
[
6
]

Let

via an isomorphism that identifies the canonical generating isometries.

Furthermore, when

is just a single point, and thus

Theorem 5.7

(Part of Theorem 4.6,
[
22
]
)
.

Let
P

Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

P

(ii)

The map
V_{p}\mapsto\lambda_{p}
extends to a completely isometric map

(iii)

Any of the conditions above further imply that

We state and prove the following proposition about
\mathcal{Q}(P)
below .

Proposition 5.8
.

If

group-embeddable, right LCM, and left-reversible, (i.e. right-Ore) then

\mathcal{Q}(P)\cong C^{\star}(G),

where

Proof.

If

), then for every

pP\cap qP\neq\emptyset
.
This implies that every singleton

Hence, if the condition

in the definition of boundary sets reduces to

1-v_{p}v_{p}^{\star}=0\;\;\textrm{ for all }\;\;p\in P.

That is every isometry

This implies that the

-algebra of the group it generates.
This proves that
\mathcal{Q}(P)\cong C^{\star}(G)
. ∎

And now, we provide some explicit examples of computations below.

Example 5.9
.

We compute the boundary quotients for the semigroups we have considered so far:

(i)

For
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, since it is Ore, and
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
is its enveloping group, by Theorem

\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{N}^{k})\cong C^{\star}(\mathbb{Z}^{k})

Even from the foundation set viewpoint, we see that since

\mathcal{Q}(P)\cong C^{\star}(\mathbb{Z}^{k})

(ii)

For
\mathbb{F}_{k}^{+}

(the reader is referred to

Section 5.1 of

(iii)

.
They used the quasi-lattice order property to characterise the Nica spectrum

If

Remark 5.10
.

We see that
\mathcal{Q}(P)\cong\partial C_{\lambda}^{\star}(P)
in the examples computed above in

and
(ii)
.
One should not expect them to be isomorphic in general: for example, one might quite trivially consider

This happens more generally for right-Ore monoids whose enveloping groups are non-amenable.
Connections between the two notions of boundary quotients have been investigated, but to the best of our knowledge,
how they are related is not very clear in the most general cases.
An interesting problem would be to investigate if there exists a semigroup where the universal counterpart of

6.
Dilating Semigroup Representations to the Boundary

In this section we address the problem of dilation of a representation of a semigroup to its boundary quotient. But first, we briefly discuss dilations of semigroup representations.

6.1.
Dilations of Representations of Semigroups

Dilations of representations of semigroups are defined in a fashion identical to that for representations of operator algebras.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
P
be a semigroup, and
T:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a representation of
P
.
We say a representation
V:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
, with
{\mathscr{K}}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
, is a
dilation
of
T
if
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}V(p)|_{\mathcal{H}}=T(p)
for all
p\in P
.
If each element
V(p)
is an isometry (or unitary), we call
(V,{\mathscr{K}})
an
isometric (corrsp. unitary) dilation
.
If
{\mathscr{K}}=\overline{span}\{V(p)h\;|\;h\in{\mathcal{H}}\textrm{, }p\in P\}
, we call the dilation
minimal
.

It can be easily seen that if
T
has an isometric dilation, then
T
must be a contractive representation (i.e., for each
p\in P
,
T(p)
is a contraction).
To the best of our knowledge, there is no general characterisation of constructible representations (Definition
5.3
).
However, in the right LCM setting, we do have the following notion of “covariant representations”, which was first formulated by A. Nica in
[
35
]
for quasi-lattice ordered semigroups and their
C^{\star}
-algebras, and generalised to right LCM semigroups in
[
14
]
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
P
be a left-cancellative right LCM semigroup.
A representation

V:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is said to be an
isometric Nica-covariant representation
if
V(p)
is an isometry for every
p
in
P
, and for all
p,q\in P
,

\displaystyle V(p)V(p)^{\star}V(q)V(q)^{\star}=\begin{cases}V(r)V(r)^{\star}&\textrm{ if }rP=pP\cap qP\textrm{ ,}\\
0&\textrm{ if }pP\cap qP=\emptyset\textrm{ .}\end{cases}

One may see that this is indeed well-defined: for any
r,r^{\prime}\in p\vee q
, there exists
u\in P^{\star}
such that
r^{\prime}=ru
, and since
u
is invertible, so must be
V(u)
, i.e.
V(u)
must be a unitary.
Hence
V(r^{\prime})V(r^{\prime})^{\star}=V(ru)V(ru)^{\star}=V(r)V(u)V(u)^{\star}V(r)^{\star}=V(r)V(r)^{\star}
.

Example 6.3
.

We provide a couple of examples of isometric Nica-covariant representations:

(i)

For
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be the canonical generating set, and denote
V(e_{i})=:V_{i}
.
Then,
V_{i}V_{j}=V_{j}V_{i}
necessarily.
Every isometric representation is easily seen to be Nica-covariant for
k=1
.
For
k\geq 2
,
i\neq j
implies
e_{i}\vee e_{j}=e_{i}e_{j}
, and hence Nica-covariance reduces to
V_{i}V_{i}^{\star}V_{j}V_{j}^{\star}=V_{i}V_{j}V_{j}^{\star}V_{i}^{\star}
.
This reduces to
V_{i}V_{j}^{\star}=V_{j}^{\star}V_{i}
for all
i\neq j
.
That is, the
V_{i}
’s must form a doubly commuting family of isometries.
Any such doubly commuting family of isometries provides a Nica-covariant representation of
\mathbb{N}^{k}
.

(ii)

For
\mathbb{F}^{+}_{k}
with
k\geq 2
, using similar notation as above,
i\neq j
implies
e_{i}\vee e_{j}=\emptyset
.
Hence Nica-covariance demands that
V_{i}V_{i}^{\star}V_{j}V_{j}^{\star}=0
, which reduces to
V_{i}^{\star}V_{j}=0
for all
i\neq j
.
Thus, an isometric representation is Nica-covariant if and only if the images of the generators yields a family of isometries with pairwise orthogonal ranges.

One might go a bit further and formulate conditions for contractive representations to admit isometric Nica-covariant dilations.
We refer the reader to B. Li’s work on this
[
29
]
.
Motivated by Neumark dilations
[
34
,
41
]
, B. Li introduced the concept of
\star
-regular dilation in
[
28
,
29
]
, which we shall not define here.
We provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of isometric Nica-covariant dilations:

Theorem 6.4

(Part of Theorem 3.9,
[
29
]
)
.

Let
T
be a representation of a right LCM semigroup.
Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
has an isometric Nica-covariant dilation.

(ii)

For any finite set
F\subset P
,

(6.1)

\displaystyle Z(F)=\sum_{U\subseteq F}(-1)^{\lvert U\rvert}T(\vee U)T^{\star}(\vee U)\geq 0.

6.2.
Dilating to
\mathcal{Q}(P)
in the right LCM case

The following lemma is straightforward, but we still include it for the sake of completeness:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
P
be a right LCM semigroup, and
V:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be an isometric Nica-covariant representation of
P
. Furthermore, let
V
satisfy the condition that for every foundation set
F\subset P
,

\Pi_{f\in F}\left(1_{\mathcal{H}}-V(f)V(f)^{\star}\right)=0.

Then, there exists a representation of
\mathcal{Q}(P)
,
\psi:\mathcal{Q}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
such that
(\psi\circ q)(v_{p})=V(p)
for all

, where
q:C^{\star}(P)\to\mathcal{Q}(P)
is the canonical quotient map.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma follows from the universal construction of
\mathcal{Q}(P)
. ∎

M. Laca and B. Li further extended this result to contractive representations using Theorem
6.4
:

Theorem 6.6

(Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.7,
[
24
]
)
.

Let
P
be a right LCM semigroup, and
T:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a contractive representation of
P
. Furthermore, let the following two conditions hold:

(i)

For every finite subset
F\subset P
,
Z(F)=\sum_{U\subseteq F}(-1)^{\lvert U\rvert}T(\vee U)T^{\star}(\vee U)\geq 0
, and

(ii)

For every foundation set
F\subset P
,
Z(F)=\sum_{U\subseteq F}(-1)^{\lvert U\rvert}T(\vee U)T^{\star}(\vee U)=0

Then there exists a dilation
\pi:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
of
T
to some
{\mathscr{K}}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\pi
induces a representation of
\mathcal{Q}(P)
: that is, there exists a representation
\psi:\mathcal{Q}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
satisfying

(\psi\circ q)(v_{p})=\pi(p)\;\;\;\;\textrm{ for all }p\in P,\textrm{ and }

\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}(\psi\circ q)(v_{p})|_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(p)|_{\mathcal{H}}=T(p).

We now formulate a dilation theorem for representations of Ore semigroups, that provides a necessary and sufficient condition for unitary dilations.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
P
be a right LCM right Ore semigroup,
T:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a representation.
Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

T
has a dilation that induces a representation of
\mathcal{Q}(P)
.

(ii)

T
has a unitary dilation.

Proof.

For
(i)\implies(ii)
, recall that for right Ore semigroups, by Proposition
5.8

\mathcal{Q}(P)\cong C^{\star}(G)
by the canonical isomorphism that sends
q(v_{p})
to
u_{p}
, where
q:C^{\star}(P)\to\mathcal{Q}(P)
is the canonical quotient map, and

u_{g}
for
g\in G
are the (unitary) generators of
C^{\star}(G)
.
Hence, if
\pi:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
is a dilation of
T
(i.e.
{\mathscr{K}}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
and
T(p)=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(p)|_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
p\in P
) that induces a representation
\psi:\mathcal{Q}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
satisfying
(\psi\circ q)(v_{p})=\pi(p)
, then, as
q(v_{p})
is unitary (
u_{p}
being unitary), so is
\pi(p)
.

For
(ii)\implies(i)
, consider a unitary dilation
\pi:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
with
{\mathscr{K}}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
and
T(p)=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(p)|_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
p\in P
.
Since
P
is Ore,
\pi
extends to a representation of the enveloping group
G
as well, and consequently a representation of
C^{\star}(G)
.
As
C^{\star}(G)\cong\mathcal{Q}(P)
by Proposition
5.8
, we have a representation of
\mathcal{Q}(P)
. ∎

And now we formulate the counterparts of these results for
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
using the Dritschel-McCullough dilation machine, and show that though our results have a very different flavour, they are stronger in many cases even when the full and reduced boundary quotients agree.

6.3.
Dilating to
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)

The following are the main results of our paper. We begin with our generalisation of the matricial von Neumann’s inequality.

Definition 6.8
.

Let
P
be a semigroup, and
T:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a representation of
P
.
We say that
T
has the
extension property (A)
if it extends to a completely contractive representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
.
That is,
T:P\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H})
has extension property (A) iff there exists a completely contractive representation
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\phi(V_{p})=T(p)
for all
p\in P
.

Note that since the algebra
\{{V_{p}\;|\;p\in P}\}
is dense in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
, there is only one way to extend a representation of
P
to one of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
mapping
V_{p}
to
T(p)
.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
P
be a semigroup that is group-embeddable or right LCM cancellative, and
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a representation.
Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\phi
is completely contractive.

(ii)

\phi
has a dilation
\pi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
of
\phi
to some
{\mathscr{K}}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\pi
canonically induces a representation of
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
.
That is, we have a representation
\psi:\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{K})
with the property that if
q:C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\to\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
is the canonical quotient map, then

(\psi\circ q)(V_{p})=\pi(V_{p})\;\;\;\;\textrm{ for all }p\in P,

where
V_{p}
is the left regular representation of
p\in P
.
Moreover, since
\pi
is a dilation of
\phi
,

\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}(\psi\circ q)(V_{p})|_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(V_{p})|_{\mathcal{H}}=\phi(V_{p})\;\;\;\;\textrm{ for all }p\in P.

Proof.

For the subsequent considerations, we shall abbreviate
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
as simply
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}
when there is no ambiguity regarding
P
.
First we prove
(i)\implies(ii)
.
By Theorem
2.4
,
\phi
must have a maximal dilation
\pi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{K})
for some
\mathcal{K}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
.
Since
\pi
is a maximal representation, by Theorem
2.3
, it must also have the unique extension property (though we do not need uniqueness here):
given the
C^{\star}
-envelope
(C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}),\iota)
, there exists a representation
\Pi:C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda})\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\Pi\circ\iota=\pi
, and the only completely positive map which agrees with
\Pi
when restricted to
\iota({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda})
is
\Pi
itself.
This yields that

\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Pi\circ\iota)(V_{p})|_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(V_{p})|_{\mathcal{H}}=\phi(V_{p}).

Now consider the isomorphism
\Psi:\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\to C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P))
, that maps
q(V_{p})
to
\iota(V_{p})
by Theorem
5.6
. Then,
\psi=\Pi\circ\Psi
is a representation of
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
on
\mathcal{K}
that satisfies

(\psi\circ q)(V_{p})=(\Pi\circ\Psi\circ q)(V_{p})=(\Pi\circ\iota)(V_{p})=\pi(V_{p}).

For
(ii)\implies(i)
, note that
\psi\circ\Psi^{-1}:C^{\star}_{env}({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda})\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{K})
is a representation that maps
\iota(V_{p})
to
(\psi\circ q)(V_{p})=\pi(V_{p})
.
Moreover, since
\psi\circ\Psi^{-1}
is a representation of a
C^{\star}
-algebra, it is completely contractive as well
[
40
]
.
Since
\iota
is a complete isometry,
\psi\circ\Psi^{-1}\circ\iota:{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{K})
turns out to a complete contraction that agrees with
\pi
on the span of
\{V_{p}\;|\;p\in P\}
, which is a dense subalgebra of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}
.
This implies that
\pi
is a complete contraction, and
\phi
satisfying that
\phi(V_{p})=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(V_{p})|_{\mathcal{H}}
must be a complete contraction as well.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
P
be a semigroup that is group-embeddable or right LCM cancellative, and let
T:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a representation.
Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

T
has extension property (A), that is,
T
extends to a completely contractive representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
.

(ii)

T
has a dilation
\pi:P\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{K})
for some
\mathcal{K}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
that induces a representation of
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
.
That is, we have a representation
\psi:\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{K})
with the property that if
q:C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\to\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
is the canonical quotient map, then

(\psi\circ q)(V_{p})=\pi(p)\;\;\;\;\textrm{ for all }p\in P.

Moreover, since
\pi
is a dilation of

,

\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}{\psi}(q(V_{p})|_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(p)|_{\mathcal{H}}=T(p)\;\;\;\;\textrm{ for all }p\in P.

Proof.

Note that we may identify a representation

with a representation of the algebra generated by

, which is a dense subalgebra of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
.
The rest follows from an application of Theorem
6.9
.
∎

These two theorems very directly lead us to the following dilation results:

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
P
be a right LCM right Ore semigroup, and
T:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a representation.
Then, consider the following conditions:

(i)

T
satisfies extension property (A).

(ii)

T
has a unitary dilation.

It is always true that
(i)\implies(ii)
.
If

is amenable, then
(ii)\implies(i)

Proof.

First we prove

Assume

Then, by Theorem

\pi:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
where

and
T(p)=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(p)|_{\mathscr{K}}
such that
\pi(p)=(\psi\circ q)(V_{p})
where
\psi:\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
where
\pi
is a representation of
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
.
Now, since
P
is Ore, recall from Theorem
5.7
that
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)\cong C^{\star}_{\lambda}(G)
by the canonical isomorphism sending
q(V_{p})
to
\lambda_{p}
, where
G
is the group generated by
P
as
G=PP^{-1}
, and
\lambda_{g}
is the left-regular representation of
g\in G
.
Since
\lambda_{g}
is a unitary, so must be
q(V_{p})
, and since
\psi
is a representation of a
C^{\star}
-algebra, we conclude that
\pi(p)
is a unitary as well.

For
(ii)\implies(i)
, consider a unitary dilation
\pi:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{K}})
with
{\mathscr{K}}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
and
T(p)=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\pi(p)|_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
p\in P
.
Since
P
is Ore,
\pi
induces a representation of the enveloping group
G
as well, and consequently a representation of
C^{\star}(G)
.
When
G
is amenable,
C^{\star}(G)\cong C^{\star}_{\lambda}(G)
.
Since
P
is right Ore,
G=PP^{-1}
is amenable if and only if
P
is left-amenable.
Moreover, the Ore condition implies that
C^{\star}_{\lambda}(G)\cong\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
by Theorem
5.7
.
Hence, by Theorem
6.10
, we have that
T
satisfies extension property (A).
∎

One must mention that extension property (A) is not automatically guaranteed, that is, not every representation has this property.
An example of a contractive representation of
\mathbb{N}^{3}
that fails to satisfy extension property (A) was provided in
[
38
]
, through a triple of commuting matrices which fail to satisfy a von Neumann inequality, and hence do not admit a unitary dilation.
An interesting question now is to consider which semigroup representations have extension property (A).

Proposition 6.12
.

Let
P
be a group-embeddable or right LCM cancellative semigroup, such that
C^{\star}_{s}(P)\cong C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
.
Then, every constructible representation of
P
has extension property (A).

Proof.

By Definition
5.3
, every constructible representation of
P
extends to a representation of
C^{\star}_{s}(P)
.
Since
C^{\star}_{s}(P)\cong C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
, this is also a representation of
C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
.
Since representations of
C^{\star}
-algebras are completely contractive, restricting to the operator algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
, we get a completely contractive representation, which is an extension of the representation of
P
that we started with.
∎

Corollary 6.13
.

Let
P
be a group-embeddable or right LCM cancellative semigroup.
If
P
is left-amenable, then every constructible representation has extension property (A).
If
P
is right LCM and Nica-amenable, then every representation satisfying the condition in Inequality
6.1
has extension property (A).

Proof.

The proof in the left-amenable case follows from Theorem
4.10
and Proposition
6.12
.
If
P
is Nica-amenable, it follows from Definition
4.11
that every constructible representation extends to a representation of
C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
.
Now, the constructible representations are precisely the isometric Nica-covariant representations (Definition
6.2
), and it follows from Theorem
6.4
that any representation satisfying the Inequality
6.1
admits an isometric Nica-covariant dilation.
The rest follows from Proposition
6.12
.
∎

Remark 6.14
.

We would like to point out that the extension property (A) of a representation of a semigroup holds in fact in much more general conditions than those outlined in Proposition
6.12
and Corollary
6.13
. However, complete contractivity of a representation of an operator algebra is quite difficult to verify in general.
A slightly easier problem to address might be to check contractivity instead of complete contractivity.
In Theorem 3.9,
[
40
]
shows that if the
\phi
is a representation of an operator algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, and
C^{\star}(\phi({\mathcal{A}}))
is commutative, then
\left\lVert{\phi}\right\rVert=\left\lVert{\phi}\right\rVert_{CB}
.
This in turn implies that if the representation of a semigroup is doubly commuting, then we only need to check contractivity of its extension to
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
for extension property (A). This also includes the one-dimensional representations.

We now provide an alternate characterisation of extension property (A) using von Neumann inequalities, and demonstrate that our extension property (A) may be viewed as a generalisation of matricial von Neumann inequalities.

Definition 6.15
.

Let
T_{1},T_{2},\dots T_{k}
be
k
commuting contractions on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.
We say that the
k
-tuple
(T_{1},T_{2},\dots T_{k})
satisfies a
von Neumann inequality
if for every polynomial
p
in
k
variables, we have the inequality:

\left\lVert{p(T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{k})}\right\rVert\leq\sup_{(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k})\in{\mathbb{T}^{k}}}\left\lvert{p(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k})}\right\rvert=\left\lVert{p}\right\rVert_{C(\mathbb{T}^{k})}.

Furthermore, we say that the
k
-tuple
(T_{1},T_{2},\dots T_{k})
satisfies a
matricial von Neumann inequality
if for every
n
, for every set of polynomials
p_{i,j}
in
k
variables for
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we have the inequality:

\left\lVert{\left(p_{i,j}(T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{k})\right)_{i,j}}\right\rVert\leq\sup_{(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k})\in{\mathbb{T}^{k}}}\left\lVert{\left(p_{i,j}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k})\right)_{i,j}}\right\rVert=\left\lVert{(p_{i,j})_{i,j}}\right\rVert_{M_{n}(C(\mathbb{T}^{k}))}.

We state and sketch a proof of the following theorem, which is now straightforward, and parts of which are somewhat well known.

Theorem 6.16
.

Let
T_{1},T_{2},\dots T_{k}
be
k
commuting contractions on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

There exist
k
commuting unitaries
U_{1},U_{2},\dots U_{k}
on a Hilbert space
{\mathscr{K}}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
satisfying

T_{1}^{n_{1}}T_{2}^{n_{2}}\dots T_{k}^{n_{k}}=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}U_{1}^{n_{1}}U_{2}^{n_{2}}\dots U_{k}^{n_{k}}|_{\mathcal{H}},

for all
n_{1},n_{2},\dots n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)

The
k
-tuple
(T_{1},T_{2},\dots T_{k})
satisfies a matricial von Neumann inequality.

(iii)

Let
{\mathcal{A}}\subset C(\mathbb{T}^{k})
be the (non-selfadjoint) operator algebra that is the linear span of polynomials on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
.
The map
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
,
z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\dots z_{k}^{n_{k}}\mapsto T_{1}^{n_{1}}T_{2}^{n_{2}}\dots T_{k}^{n_{k}}
is a completely contractive representation.

(iv)

The representation
T:\mathbb{N}^{k}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, that maps the canonical generators
e_{i}
to
T_{i}
has the extension property (A).

Proof.

To prove
(i)\implies(ii)
, we just observe that for the commuting
k
-tuple of unitaries, they must be doubly commuting as well, and hence we may identify the
C^{\star}
-algebra they generate with
C(X)
where
X
is some compact subset of
\mathbb{T}^{k}
.
The rest is straightforward.

To prove
(ii)\implies(iii)
is straightforward from the definition.

To prove
(iii)\implies(i)
, observe that since
\phi
is unital completely contractive, so is its extension
\tilde{\phi}:{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{A}}^{\star}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
that maps
p+\bar{q}\mapsto\phi(p)+(\phi(q))^{\star}
for all polynomials
p,q
.
Since every unital complete contraction on an operator system is unital completely positive
[
40
]
, we may now use Arveson’s extension theorem to extend it to a unital completely positive map
\Phi:C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
.
The Stinespring dilation of this map will be a representation of
C(\mathbb{T}^{k})
, and the unitary
U_{i}
corresponding to the image of
z_{i}
will be the unitary dilation of
T_{i}
.

To prove
(i)\iff(iv)
, note that since
\mathbb{N}^{k}
is Ore and amenable,
T
having unitary dilation is equivalent to
T
having extension property (A), by Theorem
6.11
.
∎

Theorem
6.16
brings things full circle in our paper: we demonstrate that the matricial von Neumann inequality is equivalent to extension property (A) for
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, and unitary dilation is equivalent to dilation to the reduced boundary quotient for amenable Ore semigroups (of which
\mathbb{N}^{k}
are an example).
Hence, we present extension property (A) as a generalisation of the matricial von Neumann inequality, and dilation to the reduced boundary quotient as a generalisation of unitary dilations, for semigroups more general than
\mathbb{N}^{k}
.
And just as the matricial von Neumann inequality is equivalent to existence of unitary dilations for
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, our extension property (A) is equivalent to the existence of dilations to the boundary quotient.
In this sense, our work is a true generalisation of Sz.-Nagy’s dilation theorem and its counterparts.

Example 6.17
.

To demonstrate the dilation phenomena we have proved in the general case, we provide a couple of well-known concrete examples of semigroup representations which extend to completely contractive representations of the reduced semigroup operator algebra (i.e., satisfy extension property (A)) and hence the dilation property.
We refer the interested reader to
[
40
]
for an introduction to matricial von Neumann inequalities and unitary dilations, which are often an operator-theoretic tool in some of the dilation results stated below.

(i)

Any contractive representation of
\mathbb{N}
has extension property (A).
This is equivalent to a matricial von Neumann’s inequality which follows from Sz.-Nagy’s dilation theorem
[
46
]
, that says one may dilate every contraction on a Hilbert space to a unitary upto powers.
This unitary dilation induces a representation of
C(\mathbb{T})
, which is the isomorphic to
\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{N})
.

(ii)

Any contractive representation of
\mathbb{N}^{2}
has extension property (A).
Let
T:\mathbb{N}^{2}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a contractive representation, that is, with the canonical generators of
\mathbb{N}^{2}
as
e_{1},e_{2}
, we have that
T_{i}=T(e_{i})
for
i=1,2
are contractions such that
T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}T_{1}
.
By Ando’s dilation theorem
[
1
]
, there exists a commuting pair of unitaries
U_{1},U_{2}
in
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{K})
with
\mathcal{K}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
such that for all
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
T_{1}^{n_{1}}T_{2}^{n_{2}}=\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{H}}U_{1}^{n_{1}}U_{2}^{n_{2}}|_{\mathcal{H}}
.
These unitaries help one obtain a matricial von Neumann inequality as well as induce a representation of
C(\mathbb{T}^{2})\cong\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
.

(iii)

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
T:\mathbb{N}^{k}\to M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
be a contractive representation, where
M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
denotes the space of all
2\times 2
matrices.
Then,
T
has property (A).
A well known result by J. Holbrook
[
21
]
states that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, any
k
-tuple of commuting
2\times 2
matrices dilates to a
k
-tuple of commuting unitaries.
The rest follows, as this induces a unitary representation of
C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\cong\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{N}^{k})
, and also demonstrates extension property (A) through a matricial von Neumann’s inequality.

(iv)

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
V:\mathbb{N}^{k}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be an isometric representation.
Then,
V
dilates to a unitary representation of
C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\cong\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{N}^{k})
, and hence also satisfies a matricial von Neumann’s inequality, and thus extension property (A).
We refer the reader to
[
40
]
for a proof that any
k
-tuple of commuting isometries dilates to a
k
-tuple of commuting unitaries.

Note that none of the examples stated above, except for the
k=1
case of (iv) actually satisfy either Nica-covariance or even Inequality
6.1
needed for a Nica-covariant dilation.
This demonstrates our point that extension property (A) is in fact more general than the conditions required in
[
24
]
.
However, it is not trivially satisfied by just any contractive representation, as is demonstrated by the failure to obtain an Ando-type dilation result with
3
or more contractions in general: S. Parrott
[
38
]
first gave an example of
3
contractions which fail to satisfy a von Neumann inequality, and hence do not have extension property (A).
How one might formulate a necessary condition for extension property (A) in terms of the generators of the representation generally, is a very interesting question for which we do not yet have a concrete answer.

It is interesting to consider what the relations between the images of the generators of the dilation would be in the most general case.
Note that one always has a canonical
\star
-homomorphism from the full crossed product to the reduced one,
\mathcal{Q}(P)\cong C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes G\to C(\partial\Omega_{P})\rtimes_{r}G\cong\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
that maps the image of
v_{p}
in the first to
q(V_{p})
in the second.
Thus, in the right LCM case, by Equation
5.1
,
q(V_{p})\in\partial C^{\star}_{\lambda}(P)
are always going to satisfy the condition
\Pi_{f\in F}\left(1-q(V_{f})q(V_{f})^{\star}\right)=0
for every foundation set
F
.
This leads us to the following result:

Corollary 6.18
.

Let
P
be a right LCM, group-embeddable semigroup, and let
T:P\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a representation with extension property (A) (i.e., it extends to a completely contractive representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(P)
).
Then,
T
has a dilation
\pi:P\to{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{K})
for some
\mathcal{K}\supseteq{\mathcal{H}}
that satisfies

\Pi_{f\in F}\left(1_{\mathcal{K}}-\pi(f)\pi(f)^{\star}\right)=0,

for every foundation set
F\subset P
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Corollary
6.10
, and the discussion above.
∎
