Pigeon employs a conserved IRF7 gene to mediate innate immune response
Authors: Qi Shao, Qiuju Liu, Jie Wang, Shuhan Li, Zhaofei Wang, Jingjiao Ma, Hengan Wang, Yaxian Yan, Yuqiang Cheng, Jianhe Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCompared with chickens and ducks, pigeons exhibit higher resistance to RNA virus infection. Interferon regulatory factor 7 (IRF7) was identified a member of the interferon regulatory factors (IRFs) family, which plays an important role in innate immune response. Subsequent studies have shown that IRF7 performs a diverse function in multiple biological processes. In this study, pigeon IRF7 (piIRF7) was first cloned, and bioinformatics analysis showed that IRF7 varies among species, but the IRF and IRF3 domains are highly conserved. Further study showed that only NDV infection upregulated IRF7 mRNA levels in vivo, and NDV, AIV, FPV infection, and nucleic acids mimic stimulation also significantly upregulated piIRF7 mRNA levels in PEFs. Furthermore, overexpressing piIRF7 promoted IFNs and IFN-stimulated genes (ISGs) activation and inhibited NDV and VSV replication. Importantly, we observed that pigeon and chicken IRF7 suppressed viral replication more efficiently than human and bat IRF7 in IRF7KO DF-1 cells. Structurally, we discovered that the IRF domain of piIRF7 is indispensable for IFN-β activation. This study provides data to identify the function of piIRF7 and to better understand antiviral innate immunity in pigeons.Keywords: Pigeon, Interferon regulatory factor 7, Innate immunity, Viral replication

Introduction

Pigeons are widely distributed and are in close contact with human and poultry in many regions. Avian influenza virus (AIV) and Newcastle disease Virus (NDV) infection cause high mortality in chickens, lower mortality in ducks, and the lowest mortality in pigeons. Compared to chickens and ducks, viruses infect pigeons at lower titers; however, pigeons generally remain asymptomatic after infection, even when exposed to highly pathogenic avian influenza viruses (HPAIV) (Abolnik, 2014). Virus-specific antibodies were evident in only a small proportion of pigeons that received high dose HPAIV (Abolnik et al., 2018; Al-Attar, et al., 2008). Asymptomatic pigeons still shed the virus, which can cause illness of poultry farmers and enable cross-species transmission (Abolnik, 2014; Cui, et al., 2025; Li, et al., 2014). Innate immunity is the first line of defense against invading pathogens and also contributes to the activation of adaptive immunity. The high resistance of pigeons to viruses may be related to it, but this requires further research to explore.

During viral infection, the host specifically recognizes pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) by pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), induces the production of interferons (IFNs) and interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), and restricts viral replication (Carpenter and O'Neill, 2024). Interferon regulatory factor (IRF) family members are transcription factors, which contain an N-terminal DNA binding domain (DBD) and a C-terminal IRF associated domain (IAD) (Chen and Royer, 2010) and are characterized as key factors in the regulation of multiple processes, especially immunity (Wang, et al., 2024). IRF3 and IRF7 are the master regulators of IFN-I responses, through dimerization, nuclear translocation, and binding to Interferon-stimulated response element (ISRE) (Ahmad, et al., 2024; Dalskov, et al., 2020; Ning, et al., 2005). In mammals, IRF3 is constitutively expressed in many tissues, with its expression unaffected by viral infection (Au, et al., 1995); in contrast, IRF7 is predominantly expressed in immune cells, such as B cells and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), and its expression can be significantly induced by viral infection or IFN treatment (Wang, et al., 2024). Upon viral infection, TBK1, activated by RLRs, phosphorylates IRF3 and IRF7, which relieves their autoinhibitory state, promotes nuclear translocation and interaction with CBP, and facilitates homo- or heterodimer formation, ultimately inducing the expression of IFN-I and IFN-III (Liu, et al., 2015). These cytokines are critical for the host to restrict viral replication, clear infected cells, and finally establish an antiviral state (Yang and Shu, 2020).

Compared with mammals, avian innate immunity exhibits distinctive features because of lineage-specific loss and remodeling of key PRRs. Notably, IRF3 is absent in birds except in paleognath birds (Magor, et al., 2013; Ungrová, et al., 2025); chickens lack RIG-I but can still initiate stronger IFN-I responses and remain susceptible to AIV infection. In parallel, pigeons were reported to lack IRF9 but show high resistance to viral infection (Santhakumar, et al., 2017). In chickens, the absence of IRF3 makes IRF7 critical in activation of IFN-I during DNA stimulation or RNA virus infection; chicken STING functions as a scaffold in chicken IRF7 (chIRF7) activation by chicken TBK1 through interacting with them (Cheng, et al., 2019). The expressions of chIRF7, duck IRF7 (duIRF7), and goose IRF7 (goIRF7) were induced in response to DNA or RNA stimulation and inhibit viral replication (Huang, et al., 2022; Kim and Zhou, 2015; Lin, et al., 2022). Also, viruses have evolved multiple mechanisms to reduce the function of IRF7. Duck plague virus (DPV) UL24 and novel goose parvovirus (NGPV) VP1 target IRF7 to antagonize innate immune response (Ruan, et al., 2024; Yang, et al., 2024). However, the characteristics and antiviral role of pigeon IRF7 (piIRF7) remain unclear.

Here, we characterized piIRF7 and clarified its role in pigeon innate immunity. First, we detected the mRNA level of piIRF7 both in vivo and in vitro upon NDV infection. Then, we cloned piIRF7 and analyzed its effect on innate immune response and viral replication. The results suggest that piIRF7 activates IFN-β and ISGs production, thus inhibits NDV and VSV replication, and only the C-terminal IRF3 domain is not essential for activating IFN-β promoter. Besides, piIRF7, chIRF7, huIRF7, and bat IRF7 (bIRF7) show different abilities to activate IFNs/ISGs and resist viral infection. Our results not only deepen insights into poultry innate immune system but also provide data for comparative studies of innate immunity across avian and mammalian lineages.

Materials and methods

Cells and virus

Pigeon embryonic fibroblasts (PEFs) were isolated at embryonic day 9. DF-1 cells, IRF7KO DF-1 cells (Cheng, et al., 2019), AIV (H9N2 SH010), Green fluorescent protein (GFP)-tagged NDV (NDV-GFP), and NDV Herts/33 are stored in our laboratory. The GFP-tagged vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV) VSV-GFP was kindly provided by prof. Tao Sun, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, China (Shanghai, China). PEFs and DF-1 cells were cultured in high-glucose Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco). All cells were maintained at an incubator at 37°C with 5% CO2.

Ethics statement

The animal experiment was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Shanghai Veterinary Research Institute (Shanghai, China). The Animal Experimental Ethics Review Number was ''SHVRI-SZ-20191101-01″. All animal experiments were performed according to the guidelines for Animal Experimentation of the Shanghai Veterinary Research Institute.

Animal infection experiments

Six pigeons were intramuscularly inoculated with 0.1 mL of NDV per pigeon (2 × 104 TCID50/mL) as the infection group, three pigeons were intramuscularly inoculated with 0.1 mL of AIV per pigeon (2 × 104 TCID50/mL), while the other 3 pigeons received 0.1 mL of PBS per pigeon as the control group. Three pigeons in each group were killed at 3 days post-infection (dpi), and the other pigeons in NDV-infection group were killed at 7 days post-infection (dpi). Tissue samples including trachea, spleen, bursa, muscle, kidney, lung, cardiac, small intestine, blood, liver, thymus and large intestine were collected for further analysis.

Cloning and bioinformatic analysis of piIRF7

According to the predicted coding sequence of piIRF7 (XM_065065057.1) from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), the primers were designed to amplify piIRF7 gene from tissue samples via polymerase chain reaction (PCR). The PCR product was ligated into a pTOPO-Blunt vector (CV1701, Aidlab Biotech, Beijing, China) for sequencing, and the positive colonies were sent to the Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China) for sequencing. The sequence alignment of IRF7 amino acids from 8 animals was performed with Clustal W (https://www.genome.jp/tools-bin/clustalw) and edited with ESPript 3.0 (http://espript.ibcp.fr/ESPript/cgi-bin/ESPript.cgi). A phylogenetic tree was conducted with Megalign using the neighbor-joining method based on the amino acids of IRF7. PiIRF7 domains were predicted with CD-Search of NCBI. And AlphaFold was used to predict the three-dimensional structure of piIRF7 (https://colab.research.google.com/github/sokrypton/ColabFold/blob/main/AlphaFold2.ipynb).

Plasmid construction

The Flag-tagged piIRF7 plasmid (pcDNA4.0-Flag- piIRF7) were constructed by inserting the full-length piIRF7 into the EcoR I and EcoR Ⅴ sites of pcDNA4.0-Flag. PiIRF7 fragment and linear vector was ligated by 2X MultiF Seamless Assembly Mix (RK21020, ABclonal, Shanghai, China). The truncated plasmids of piIRF7, including deletion of amino acids 6 to 116 (dIRF), d117-220 aa, d221-327 aa, and d328-999 aa(dIRF3), were constructed with the modified homologous recombination method. The DH5α Chemically Competent Cell (Tsingke Biological Technology, Beijing, China) was used for plasmid transformation. The chicken IFN-β promoter luciferase reporter plasmid (chIFN-β-Luc), pRL-TK, and pcDNA4.0-Flag plasmid were stored in our laboratory. All primers used for cloning are listed in Table 1.

Dual luciferase reporter assays

DF-1 cells or IRF7KO DF-1 cells were plated in 24-well plates (NEST Biotechnology, Wuxi, China) and cultured to 90–95% confluence; they were then transiently transfected with reporter plasmid chIFN-β (100 ng/well), internal control Renilla luciferase (pRL-TK, 50 ng/well) and the indicated plasmids, using Nulen PlusTrans™ Transfection Reagent (Nulen, Shanghai, China). The cells were lysed at 24 hours post transfection (hpt), and the luciferase activity was detected with the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay (DLR) System (Promega, USA) according to the instructions. Renilla luciferase activity was used for normalization. All reporter assays were repeated at least three times.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cell lines or tissues by using AG RNAex Pro Reagent (AG21101, Accurate Biology, Hunan, China) based on the instructions. The RNA was reverse transcribed to cDNA with the reverse transcription kits (Q121-02, Vazyme, Nanjing, China). Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) tests were conducted by using the ABI 7500 RT-PCR system with AceQ qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Without ROX) (Q121-02, Vazyme, Nanjing, China). The conditions and data processing method for the qPCR were the same as in our previous study (Cheng, et al., 2017). All primers used in this study are listed in Table 1.

Viral Replication Analysis

DF-1 cells or IRF7KO DF-1 cells were plated in 12-well plates at 1 × 106 cells/mL and transfected with various plasmids. Cells were infected with VSV-GFP (MOI=0.5) at 24 hpt. The fluorescence from the cells was measured using the fluorescence microscope at 12 hpi and 24 hpi. Cells were washed twice with phosphate buffer saline (PBS) (Gibco) and then lysed with RIPA Lysis Buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) containing a InStab™ protease cocktail (Yeasen, Shanghai, China) and phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF) (Yeasen, Shanghai, China) at 24 hpi. Western blot analysis was performed in accordance with standard protocols (Wang, et al., 2022). Images were obtained using the Tanon 5200 imaging system (Tanon, Shanghai, China), as described in our previous study (Lin, et al., 2021).

Viral infection

PEFs were plated in 12-wells plate, washed twice with PBS (Gibco), and infected with NDV-GFP, AIV, and Fowlpox virus (FPV). Following absorption for 1 h, the unattached viruses were removed, and cells were washed three times with PBS and cultured in 2% FBS medium.

Nucleic acid mimic transfection

PEFs were plated in 12-well plate and cultured to 85–90% confluence; then transiently transfected with poly(I:C) (1000 ng/well) or poly(dA:dT) (1000 ng/well), using Nulen PlusTrans™ Transfection Reagent (Nulen, Shanghai, China). The cells were lysed for further assays at different hours post transfection.

Statistical analysis

The data were expressed as means ± standard deviations. Significance was determined using Two-way ANOVA, One-way ANOVA, a two-tailed independent Student’s t-test, or Welch’s test (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001).

Results

Molecular cloning and conserved sequence characterization of piIRF7

To better understand the pigeon innate immunity, piIRF7, a key molecule of innate immunity, was first cloned and subjected to bioinformatic analysis. The full-length piIRF7 nucleotide sequence (XM_065065057.1) is 1545 bp and encodes 514 amino acids (Fig. 1A). IRF7 protein sequence of pigeon (XP_064921129.1), chicken (AJS11515.1), duck (XP_038035080.1), goose (XP_047914542.1), human (NP_004022.2), monkey (XP_028688388.1), mouse (NP_058546.1), and zebrafish (NP_956971.2) were subjected to multiple sequence alignment. The result shows that N-terminal and C-terminal domains of IRF7 are highly conserved (Fig. 1A). The putative domain of piIRF7 was subsequently predicted with CD-research of NCBI. PiIRF7 has two domains, an N-terminal IRF domain (16-116aa) and a C-terminal IRF3 domain (328-480aa), which are highly conserved (Fig. 1B). IRF domain contains a highly conserved “5W” motif (Fujii, et al., 1999), the hallmark of IRF family members, which is responsible for binding with ISRE (Interferon Stimulatory Response Element). Besides, the C-terminal IRF3 domain is predicted to mediate IRF7 dimerization and transcriptional complex assembly. Collectively, these results demonstrate that piIRF7 retains canonical domains and conserved motifs of IRF7, providing structural evidence for its conserved role in signaling transduction.

Phylogenetic analysis and predicted three-dimensional structure of piIRF7

IRF7, a key transcription factor, plays an important role in antiviral innate immunity, especially in chickens. Sequence analysis revealed that piIRF7 shares 62.8% and 64.4% sequence identity with chIRF7 and duIRF7, respectively, while exhibiting lower homology with mammalian IRF7 (Fig. 2A). Although the identity of IRF7 among species is lower, multiple critical functional motifs, including ISRE-binding domain and nuclear localization signal (NLS), remain highly conserved. Furthermore, IRF7 protein sequences were retrieved from NCBI database and used to construct a phylogenetic tree, in which the sequences clustered into three major lineages corresponding to mammals, poultry, and fishes (Fig. 2C). Consistent with the previous result, avian IRF7 proteins are relatively conserved and cluster within the first subgroup. Moreover, the similarity between pigeons and other poultry is higher than that of pigeons and mammals. And IRF7 of mammals are the second subgroup (Fig. 2C). Zebrafish IRF7 has only 35.3% and 32.5% similarities with piIRF7 and huIRF7, respectively (Fig. 2A). This suggests IRF7 may play a different role in innate immunity and exhibit evolutionary conservation. The predicted three-dimensional(3D) model of piIRF7 with AlphaFold are shown in Fig. 2B.

Tissue distribution of piIRF7 expression

To explore the tissue distribution of piIRF7 in pigeons, we collected 13 tissue samples including trachea, spleen, bursa, muscle, kidney, lung, cardiac, small intestine, blood, liver, thymus and large intestine for Real-time Quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction (qPCR) to analyze the mRNA level of piIRF7. The result showed that piIRF7 expressed in most tissues from 35-day-old pigeons analyzed except muscle tissue. The highest expression was observed in blood, while relatively high levels were detected in liver, small intestine, and large intestine. Moderate expression levels were found in the trachea, bursa, and lung; and lower levels in kidney, cardiac, and thymus; and the lowest level in muscle (Fig. 3). PiIRF7 tissue distribution pattern in this assay is similar to that of other key innate immune genes (Shao, et al., 2023), suggesting that these genes may share regulatory mechanisms that enable a more efficient and rapid activation of innate immune response.

PiIRF7 expression is upregulated upon RNA virus infection

In domestic pigeons, NDV infection causes reduction in egg production and high mortality rate in young pigeons (Thomazelli, et al., 2021). To explore the role of piIRF7 in antiviral innate immunity, pigeons and PEFs were infected with NDV or AIV. Following NDV infection (Fig. S1A), both IRF7 and IFN-γ showed the highest expression at 3 dpi (days post infection), and then declined at 7 dpi (Fig. 4A, 4B). IFN-γ expression was significantly upregulated at 3 dpi in lung, blood, thymus and large intestine, while showing no obvious changes in other tested tissues (Fig. 4A). At 7 dpi, IFN-γ expression showed no significance in any tested tissues (Fig. 4A). The mRNA level of IRF7 was upregulated in 83% of the tested tissues at 3 dpi with the highest level detected in blood (Fig. 4B). At 7 dpi, IRF7 mRNA levels was increased in cardiac tissue and blood, while expression was decreased in the bursa and large intestine (Fig. 4B). However, AIV infection only upregulated the mRNA level of IRF7 in the small intestine and thymus, and elevated IFN-γ expression in the burasa, liver, and cardiac tissue (Fig. 4C and D). The mRNA level of IRF7 was found to be decreased in the spleen, bursa, muscle, kidney, bloos, liver, and large intestine upon AIV infection. A similar pattern was also observed for IFN-γ expression in spleen, thymus, and large intestine (Fig. 4C and D). These relatively restricted expression patterns may be associated with the low-level viral replication of the H9N2 subtype AIV strain (Table S1). In PEFs, IRF7 was markedly upregulated in response to stimulation with nucleic acid mimics or viruses (Fig. 4E-I and Fig. S1B-S1F). The results suggest that viral infection induces the upregulation of IRF7 mRNA level.

PiIRF7 overexpression suppresses RNA viral replication through promoting IFN-β and ISGs expression

Viral infection triggers antiviral innate immunity, which in turn restricts viral replication (Rehwinkel and Gack, 2020).To clarify the role of piIRF7 during viral infection, DF-1 cells were transfected with a piIRF7-Flag expression plasmid or the vector control and then infected with NDV at 12 hpt. Compared with the control group, piIRF7 overexpression significantly reduced the cytopathic effect (CPE) caused by NDV infection (Fig. 5A) and NP mRNA levels, and the mRNA levels of IFN-α, IFN-β, OASL, MX1, and IL-8 increased (Fig. 5B), which function in activating adaptive immunity and repressing viral replication. The result suggests that piIRF7 inhibiting viral replication correlates with its function in regulating IFNs and ISGs expression. To further identify the differences, IRF7KO DF-1 cells were transfected with piIRF7, chIRF7, and huIRF7 expression plasmids and infected with NDV-GFP or VSV-GFP at 12 hpt. Viral replication was analyzed by GFP fluorescence intensity or western blot (WB) and cytokines were detected by qPCR. IRF7 overexpression significantly repressed NDV-GFP and VSV-GFP replication (Fig. 5C, D, E and G), whereas huIRF7 did not show a significant inhibitory effect during VSV-GFP infection (Fig. 5C and G). There are differences in the ability of IRF7 to induce IFNs and ISGs among different species, which may be one of the underlying causes for the differences in their antiviral activity (Fig. 5F). Moreover, IRF7 from different species exhibited different resistances to viral infection, but piIRF7 exhibited the stronger antiviral activity among the IRF7 homologs tested while ensuing the consistent IRF7 protein expression levels (Fig. 5G).

The Critical Domain of piIRF7 responsible for IFN-β induction

In mammals, IRF7 cooperates with IRF3 to regulate IFNs and ISGs production (Qing and Liu, 2023); however, in chickens, which lack IRF3, IRF7 acts as the critical transcriptional regulator in innate immunity (Cheng, et al., 2019). To explore the role of piIRF7 in innate immunity, DF-1 cells were transfected with piIRF7 expression plasmid and chIFN-β-luc plasmid. PiIRF7 overexpression significantly activates IFN-β promoter, and this activation was related to the dosage of piIRF7 expression plasmid (Fig. 6A); a similar result was also observed in IRF7KO DF-1 cells (Fig. 6B). The protein level of piIRF7 in DF-1 cells was also analyzed with WB (Fig. 6C). The N-terminal IRF domain and C-terminal IRF3 domain of IRF7 are highly conserved among species (Fig. 1A). To identify the key structural domain of piIRF7 responsible for IFN-β activation, various truncations were generated and transfected into IRF7KO DF-1 cells to detect the ability of activating IFN-β promoter (Fig. 6D). Deletion of the IRF domain completely abolished piIRF7-mediated IFN-β promoter activity, whereas deletion of amino acids 117-220 or 221-327 markedly reduced this activity. In contrast, deletion of the C-terminal IRF3 domain did not significantly affect the activity under the conditions of this reporter assay (Fig. 6E and F). These results indicate that the IRF domain is indispensable for piIRF7-mediated IFN-β promoter activation in this reporter system.

Discussion

In this study, we cloned piIRF7 and systematically characterized its role in innate immunity. Sequence similarity analysis, multiple sequences alignment, and phylogenetic tree construction demonstrated that piIRF7 clusters with avian IRF7 proteins and is separated from mammalian and fish IRF7 proteins (Fig. 1, Fig. 2). Despite this phylogenetic divergence, key functional motifs, particularly the conserved “5W” tryptophan cluster within the DBD and NLS, remain highly conserved across species (Fujii, et al., 1999; Lin, et al., 2000). Structural prediction suggested that piIRF7 adopts the canonical architecture of IRF7. The N-terminal IRF domain is likely responsible for DNA binding and transcription activation, whereas the C-terminal region may participate in signal transduction and protein-protein interactions (Ma, et al., 2023). The predicted structural conservation of piIRF7 further supports its functional competence in pigeon innate immunity.

RNA virus infection and aberrant dsRNA accumulation trigger innate immunity, and IRF7 is induced to promote IFNs and ISGs expression (Chow, et al., 2018; Wang, et al., 2022). Here, we observed that NDV infection upregulates piIRF7 mRNA levels in vivo and in vitro, and DNA stimulation and FPV infection also increased piIRF7 mRNA levels (Fig. 4). In other poultry, viral infection also induced the upregulation and activation of IRF7(Cheng, et al., 2019; Lin, et al., 2022). Notably, AIV infection significantly induced piIRF7 expression in PEFs, whereas no obvious upregulation was observed in pigeon tissues. This discrepancy may be associated with differences between in vitro and in vivo infection conditions. In vitro, virus infect a large proportion of cells synchronously, thereby more readily inducing detectable antiviral signaling. In contrast, AIV replication in pigeon tissues appeared to be relatively limited in vivo, as HA transcripts were detected only in several tissues and at relatively high Ct values, indicating a low viral load (Table S1). In addition, tissue samples consist of heterogeneous cell populations, and only a subset of cells may be directly infected by the virus, which could dilute antiviral signaling at the whole-tissue level. Therefore, the absence of significant piIRF7 or IFN-γ upregulation in tissues does not necessarily indicate a lack of innate immune response, but may instead reflect a relatively limited overall response under low-level infection conditions.

DF-1 cells are a well-established avian fibroblast cell line with stable growth, high transfection efficiency, and robust compatibility with luciferase reporter assays and viral infection experiments. IRF7KO DF-1 cells reduced interference from endogenous chicken IRF7 and thereby provided a cleaner background for evaluating the activity of exogenous IRF7 homologs from different species. In DF-1 or IRF7KO DF-1 cells followed by infection with RNA viruses, we found that overexpressing piIRF7 inhibited NDV replication through enhancing IFN-β, OASL and MX1 expression in DF-1 cells (Fig. 5), and goIRF7 and bIRF7 were also reported to show the same result (Lin, et al., 2022; Wang, et al., 2025). These findings establish piIRF7 as a positive regulator of antiviral innate immunity in pigeons. Given that pigeons can be infected with low viral titer and exhibit minimal clinical symptoms following infection with HPAIV (Abolnik, 2014; Cui, et al., 2025; Di Genova, et al., 2025), the robust IFN response mediated by piIRF7 may contribute to their relative resistance to viral infection.

IRF7 is a key transcription factor in antiviral innate immunity (Ma, et al., 2023). Comparative analyses revealed that piIRF7, chIRF7, huIRF7, and bIRF7 differ in their ability to induce IFNs and ISGs and to repress viral replication (Fig. 5). Although these IRF7 homologs share conserved domains and key motifs (Fig. 1), their functional outputs are not equivalent. This divergence likely reflects species-specific differences in upstream PRR repertoire and signaling architecture. For example, in chickens, the absence of RIG-I remodels RLR pathway in which MDA5 senses a broad range of RNA (Hayashi, et al., 2014; Liniger, et al., 2012), thereby altering the strength and kinetics of downstream IRF7 activation compared with mammals. In most birds, IRF7 acts as a central transcription factor of both anti-DNA and anti-RNA virus innate immunity, particularly downstream of MAVS and STING, a role that is partially shared with IRF3 in mammals (Kim and Zhou, 2015). Together, differences in PRR composition, signaling cascade, and transcriptional regulation provide a mechanistic basis for the observed species-specificity of IRF7 activity during evolution.

In summary, piIRF7 was characterized for the first time in this study. As a key transcriptional factor, IRF7 is highly evolutionary conserved and is essential for the host to resist viral infection and initiate antiviral innate immunity. This study helps to understand avian innate immune system and to help explain differences in antiviral resistance between mammals and birds.
