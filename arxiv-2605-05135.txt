de la Vallée Poussin Means of Walsh-Fourier Expansions
Ushangi Goginava

Abstract
. We study de la Vallée Poussin means of Walsh–Fourier series associated with a nondecreasing window sequence. We establish a sharp criterion for almost everywhere convergence for integrable functions. We further show that, when this criterion fails, every Orlicz class below the logarithmic square-root scale contains a function whose de la Vallée Poussin means diverge everywhere.

Let
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a nondecreasing sequence of integers
satisfying

(1.1)

1\leq\lambda_{n}\leq n\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

For
f\in L^{1}([0,1))
we define the de la Vallée Poussin means of the
Walsh–Fourier series of
f
by

(1.2)

V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(f;x):=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}+1}\sum_{k=n-\lambda_{n}}^{n}S_{k}(f;x),\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
S_{k}(f)
denotes the
k
th Walsh partial sum of
f
.

For an Orlicz function
\omega
, we denote by
L_{\omega}([0,1))
the Orlicz
class

L_{\omega}([0,1)):=\Bigl\{f\text{ measurable on }[0,1):\int_{0}^{1}\omega(|f(x)|)\,dx<\infty\Bigr\}.

Recall that for every Orlicz function, the quotient
\omega(t)/t
is
nondecreasing on
(0,\infty)
; see, for example,
[
17
, Ch. 1]
.

Our main result identifies the sharp window criterion for almost everywhere
convergence and shows that, on the divergence side, the obstruction to
convergence already appears in substantially smaller Orlicz clases.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfy (
1.1
).

(a)

If

(1.3)

n=O(\lambda_{n})\qquad(n\to\infty),

then for every
f\in L^{1}([0,1))
,

V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(f;x)\longrightarrow f(x)\qquad\text{for almost every }x\in[0,1).

(b)

Let
\omega:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an
Orlicz function
1
1

1

\omega
is a continuous convex function on
[0,\infty)
such that
\omega(0)=0
,
\omega(u)>0
for
u>0
, and
\omega(u)\rightarrow\infty
as
u\rightarrow\infty
.
, and assume that

(1.4)

\omega(t)=o\bigl(t\sqrt{\log t}\bigr)\qquad(t\rightarrow\infty).

If

(1.5)

\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{n}{\lambda_{n}}=\infty,

then there exists a function
f\in L_{\omega}([0,1))
such that

(1.6)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(f;x)|=\infty\qquad\text{for every }x\in[0,1).

2.
Preliminaries and an auxiliary block polynomial

We write
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and identify
[0,1)
with
the dyadic group in the standard way. For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we denote by

(2.1)

n=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}n_{j}2^{j},\qquad n_{j}\in\{0,1\},

its binary expansion, and we write

|n|:=\max\{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:n_{j}=1\}\qquad(n\geq 1).

The dyadic sum of nonnegative integers is defined by

n\oplus m:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|n_{j}-m_{j}|2^{j}.

Let
r_{j}(x):=(-1)^{\lfloor 2^{j+1}x\rfloor}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, be
the Rademacher functions. The Walsh–Paley system is defined by

w_{n}(x):=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}r_{j}(x)^{n_{j}},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

If
f\in L^{1}([0,1))
, then its Walsh coefficients are

\widehat{f}(n):=\int_{0}^{1}f(x)w_{n}(x)\,dx,

and the
n
th Walsh partial sum is

S_{n}(f;x):=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}(k)w_{k}(x),\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

For a Walsh polynomial
P
, we write

\mathrm{Spec}(P):=\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\widehat{P}(k)\neq 0\}.

The next proposition is the basic building block of the divergence
construction.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
m,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
with
2\gamma<m
. Define

(2.2)

\mu(v,j):=2^{m-\gamma}v+2^{m-2\gamma}(v\oplus 2^{j}),\qquad 0\leq v<2^{\gamma},\quad 0\leq j<\gamma,

and

(2.3)

P_{m,\gamma}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\sum_{v=0}^{2^{\gamma}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-1}w_{\mu(v,j)}(x).

Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

\|P_{m,\gamma}\|_{L^{1}([0,1))}\leq 1
.

(ii)

\|P_{m,\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1))}\leq 2^{\gamma}\sqrt{\gamma}
.

(iii)

\mathrm{Spec}(P_{m,\gamma})\subset[2^{m-2\gamma},2^{m})\cap 2^{m-2\gamma}\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(iv)

For every
x\in[0,1)
there exists an integer
\ell(x)
such
that

\ell(x)\in[2^{m-\gamma},2^{m})\cap 2^{m-2\gamma}\mathbb{N}

and

|S_{\ell(x)}(P_{m,\gamma};x)|\geq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\gamma}.

Proof.

Let

v=\sum_{k=0}^{\gamma-1}\nu_{k}2^{k},\qquad\nu_{k}\in\{0,1\}.

Since the binary digit blocks of
2^{m-\gamma}v
and
2^{m-2\gamma}(v\oplus 2^{j})
are disjoint, we have

w_{\mu(v,j)}(x)=w_{2^{m-\gamma}v}(x)\,w_{2^{m-2\gamma}(v\oplus 2^{j})}(x).

A direct computation gives

w_{2^{m-\gamma}v}(x)=\prod_{k=0}^{\gamma-1}r_{m-\gamma+k}(x)^{\nu_{k}}

and

w_{2^{m-2\gamma}(v\oplus 2^{j})}(x)=r_{m-2\gamma+j}(x)\prod_{k=0}^{\gamma-1}r_{m-2\gamma+k}(x)^{\nu_{k}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle w_{\mu(v,j)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{m-2\gamma+j}(x)\prod_{k=0}^{\gamma-1}\bigl(r_{m-\gamma+k}(x)r_{m-2\gamma+k}(x)\bigr)^{\nu_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{m-2\gamma+j}(x)c\left(x\right),

where

c\left(x\right):=\prod_{k=0}^{\gamma-1}\bigl(r_{m-\gamma+k}(x)r_{m-2\gamma+k}(x)\bigr)^{\nu_{k}}\in\left\{-1,1\right\}.

Summing over
v
yields

(2.4)

P_{m,\gamma}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-1}r_{m-2\gamma+j}(x)\prod_{k=0}^{\gamma-1}\bigl(1+r_{m-\gamma+k}(x)r_{m-2\gamma+k}(x)\bigr).

Let

E_{m,\gamma}:=\bigl\{x\in[0,1):r_{m-\gamma+k}(x)=r_{m-2\gamma+k}(x)\text{
for }0\leq k<\gamma\bigr\}.

Then (
2.4
) becomes

(2.5)

P_{m,\gamma}(x)=\frac{2^{\gamma}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{m,\gamma}}(x)\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-1}r_{m-2\gamma+j}(x).

Assertion (ii) follows immediately from (
2.5
).

To prove (i), note that on
E_{m,\gamma}
the vector

\bigl(r_{m-2\gamma}(x),\dots,r_{m-\gamma-1}(x)\bigr)

assumes each value in
\{\pm 1\}^{\gamma}
on a set of measure
2^{-2\gamma}
. Hence, by (
2.5
),

\displaystyle\|P_{m,\gamma}\|_{L^{1}([0,1))}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\gamma}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\int_{E_{m,\gamma}}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-1}r_{m-2\gamma+j}(x)\right|\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\gamma}\sqrt{\gamma}}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{\gamma}}|\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{\gamma}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{\gamma}}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{\gamma}}|\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{\gamma}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq 1.

This proves (i).

Since every
\mu(v,j)
is a multiple of
2^{m-2\gamma}
, the right-hand side
of (
2.3
) shows that

\mathrm{Spec}(P_{m,\gamma})\subset 2^{m-2\gamma}\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Moreover,

2^{m-2\gamma}\leq\mu(v,j)<2^{m},

which proves (iii).

Fix
x\in[0,1)
. Choose
\sigma(x)\in\{\pm 1\}
that occurs at least
\gamma/2
times among

r_{m-2\gamma}(x),\dots,r_{m-\gamma-1}(x),

and put

J(x):=\{0\leq j<\gamma:r_{m-2\gamma+j}(x)=\sigma(x)\},\qquad\#J(x)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2},

v(x):=\sum_{j\in J(x)}2^{j}.

Define

\ell_{1}(x):=2^{m-\gamma}v(x),\qquad\ell_{2}(x):=2^{m-\gamma}v(x)+2^{m-2\gamma}v(x).

Since
v(x)\geq 1
, we have

2^{m-\gamma}\leq\ell_{1}(x)<\ell_{2}(x)<2^{m},\qquad\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x)\in 2^{m-2\gamma}\mathbb{N}.

For fixed
v=v(x)
we have

\mu(v,j)=2^{m-\gamma}v+2^{m-2\gamma}(v\oplus 2^{j}).

Because
v\oplus 2^{j}<v
if and only if the
j
th binary digit of
v
equals
1
, it follows that

\mu(v(x),j)\in[\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x))\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad j\in J(x).

Consequently,

\displaystyle S_{\ell_{2}(x)}(P_{m,\gamma};x)-S_{\ell_{1}(x)}(P_{m,\gamma};x)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\sum_{j\in J(x)}w_{\mu(v(x),j)}(x)=\frac{c(x)}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\sum_{j\in J(x)}r_{m-2\gamma+j}(x)

with a unimodular factor
c(x)\in\{\pm 1\}
independent of
j
. Since
r_{m-2\gamma+j}(x)=\sigma(x)
for every
j\in J(x)
and
|c(x)|=1
, it
follows that

\left|S_{\ell_{2}(x)}(P_{m,\gamma};x)-S_{\ell_{1}(x)}(P_{m,\gamma};x)\right|=\frac{\#J(x)}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\gamma}.

Since for arbitrary real numbers
A
and
B
one has

\max\{|A|,|A+B|\}\geq\frac{1}{2}|B|,

we conclude that at least one of
\ell_{1}(x)
and
\ell_{2}(x)
satisfies

|S_{\ell(x)}(P_{m,\gamma};x)|\geq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\gamma}.

This proves (iv).
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
m,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
with
2\gamma<m
,
let
P_{m,\gamma}
be as in Proposition
2.1
, and let
\ell(x)

be chosen as in Proposition
2.1
(iv). If
\lambda_{\ell(x)}<2^{m-2\gamma}
, then

V_{\ell(x)}^{(\lambda)}(P_{m,\gamma};x)=S_{\ell(x)}(P_{m,\gamma};x).

In particular,

|V_{\ell(x)}^{(\lambda)}(P_{m,\gamma};x)|\geq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\gamma}.

Proof.

Put
B:=2^{m-2\gamma}
. By Proposition
2.1
(iii), every Walsh
frequency of
P_{m,\gamma}
is a multiple of
B
. Hence

(2.6)

S_{k}(P_{m,\gamma};x)=S_{qB}(P_{m,\gamma};x)\qquad((q-1)B<k\leq qB).

Since
\ell(x)\in B\mathbb{N}
, we may write
\ell(x)=qB
. If
\lambda_{\ell(x)}<B
, then every integer

k\in[\ell(x)-\lambda_{\ell(x)},\ell(x)]

belongs to the same block
((q-1)B,qB]
, and therefore (
2.6
) implies

V_{\ell(x)}^{(\lambda)}(P_{m,\gamma};x)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell(x)}+1}\sum_{k=\ell(x)-\lambda_{\ell(x)}}^{\ell(x)}S_{k}(P_{m,\gamma};x)=S_{\ell(x)}(P_{m,\gamma};x).

The lower bound now follows from Proposition
2.1
(iv).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(a).

Assume (
1.3
). Then there exists
c>0
such that
\lambda_{n}\geq cn
for all sufficiently large
n
. Define the maximal operator

M_{\lambda}f(x):=\sup_{n\geq 1}|V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(f;x)|.

For all sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle|V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(f;x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}+1}\sum_{k=n-\lambda_{n}}^{n}|S_{k}(f;x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{\lambda_{n}+1}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|S_{k}(f;x)|\leq C\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|S_{k}(f;x)|

with a constant
C
independent of
n
and
x
. Therefore, fore some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}

(3.1)

M_{\lambda}f(x)\leq C\sigma^{*}f(x)+\max_{1\leq n<n_{0}}|V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(f;x)|,

where

\sigma^{*}f(x):=\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|S_{k}(f;x)|.

Rodin proved that

(3.2)

\|\sigma^{*}f\|_{L^{1,\infty}([0,1))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}([0,1))}\qquad(f\in L^{1}([0,1)))

for the Walsh system
[
23
]
. Moreover, the second term on the
right-hand side of (
3.1
) is the maximum of finitely
many fixed bounded operators and is therefore bounded from
L^{1}([0,1))
to
itself. Hence (
3.1
) implies the weak-type estimate

(3.3)

\|M_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{1,\infty}([0,1))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}([0,1))}.

Let
P
be a Walsh polynomial. Then
S_{k}(P)=P
for all
k
larger than the
degree of
P
, and therefore

V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(P;x)\longrightarrow P(x)\qquad(n\to\infty)

for every
x\in[0,1)
: the number of indices
k
in the averaging interval
for which
S_{k}(P)\neq P
is bounded independently of
n
, while
\lambda_{n}\to\infty
by (
1.3
).

Now let
f\in L^{1}([0,1))
, and choose Walsh polynomials
P_{j}
such that
\|f-P_{j}\|_{L^{1}([0,1))}\to 0
. Since
V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(P_{j};x)\to P_{j}(x)

everywhere, for every
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\bigl\{x:\limsup_{n\to\infty}|V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(f;x)-f(x)|>2\eta\bigr\}

\displaystyle\subset\{x:M_{\lambda}(f-P_{j})(x)>\eta\}

\displaystyle\cup\{x:|f(x)-P_{j}(x)|>\eta\}.

Taking measures and using (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mu\bigl\{x:\limsup_{n\to\infty}|V_{n}^{(\lambda)}(f;x)-f(x)|>2\eta\bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\eta}\|f-P_{j}\|_{L^{1}([0,1))}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\eta}\|f-P_{j}\|_{L^{1}([0,1))}.

Letting
j\to\infty
gives the desired almost everywhere convergence.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(b).

Fix an Orlicz function
\omega
satisfying (
1.4
),
and assume (
1.5
).

For each
a\in\mathbb{N}
and each integer
q\geq 1
, set

\delta(a,q):=\min\left\{2^{-a},\frac{2^{2q}}{4^{a}\,\omega(2^{2q})}\right\}.

Because of (
1.4
),

\frac{2^{2q}\sqrt{q}}{\omega(2^{2q})}\longrightarrow\infty\qquad(q\to\infty),

and therefore, for each fixed
a
,

\delta(a,q)\sqrt{q}\longrightarrow\infty\qquad(q\to\infty).

Choose recursively a sequence
\{\gamma_{a}\}_{a\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{N}
such
that, with

\delta_{a}:=\delta(a,\gamma_{a}),

we have

(3.4)

\delta_{a}\sqrt{\gamma_{a}}>16\left(a+\sum_{k=1}^{a-1}\delta_{k}2^{\gamma_{k}}\sqrt{\gamma_{k}}\right)\qquad(a\geq 1).

Next, using (
1.5
), choose an increasing sequence
\{N_{a}\}_{a\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that, with
m_{a}:=|N_{a}|
,

(3.5)

\frac{N_{a}}{\lambda_{N_{a}}}>2^{2\gamma_{a}+1}

and

(3.6)

m_{a}>m_{a-1}+2\gamma_{a}\qquad(a\geq 2).

Indeed, since the ratios
N/\lambda_{N}
are unbounded, for every prescribed
threshold there are arbitrarily large integers
N
satisfying that
threshold, so the recursive choice is possible.

Now (
3.5
) implies

(3.7)

\lambda_{N_{a}}<\frac{N_{a}}{2^{2\gamma_{a}+1}}<2^{m_{a}-2\gamma_{a}}.

Let

W_{a}:=P_{m_{a},\gamma_{a}},\qquad f(x):=\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\delta_{a}W_{a}(x).

Since
\delta_{a}\leq 2^{-a}
and
\|W_{a}\|_{L^{1}([0,1))}\leq 1
by Proposition
2.1
(i), the series converges absolutely for almost every
x

and in
L^{1}([0,1))
, so
f\in L^{1}([0,1))
.

We now prove that
f\in L_{\omega}([0,1))
. By Proposition
2.1
(ii),

|W_{a}(x)|\leq 2^{\gamma_{a}}\sqrt{\gamma_{a}}\leq 2^{2\gamma_{a}}\qquad(x\in[0,1)).

Since
\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}2^{-a}=1
and
\omega
is convex with
\omega(0)=0
, Jensen’s inequality yields

\omega(|f(x)|)\leq\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}2^{-a}\,\omega\bigl(2^{a}\delta_{a}|W_{a}(x)|\bigr)\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in[0,1).

Now
2^{a}\delta_{a}\leq 1
, and the monotonicity of
\omega(t)/t
gives

\omega\bigl(2^{a}\delta_{a}|W_{a}(x)|\bigr)\leq 2^{a}\delta_{a}\,\omega(|W_{a}(x)|)\leq 2^{a}\delta_{a}\,\frac{\omega(2^{2\gamma_{a}})}{2^{2\gamma_{a}}}\,|W_{a}(x)|.

Integrating and using Proposition
2.1
(i), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\omega(|f(x)|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}2^{-a}\cdot 2^{a}\delta_{a}\,\frac{\omega(2^{2\gamma_{a}})}{2^{2\gamma_{a}}}\,\|W_{a}\|_{L^{1}([0,1))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\delta_{a}\,\frac{\omega(2^{2\gamma_{a}})}{2^{2\gamma_{a}}}\leq\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}4^{-a}<\infty.

Hence
f\in L_{\omega}([0,1))
.

Fix
x\in[0,1)
. By Proposition
2.1
(iv), choose
\ell_{a}(x)
so
that

\ell_{a}(x)\in[2^{m_{a}-\gamma_{a}},2^{m_{a}})\cap 2^{m_{a}-2\gamma_{a}}\mathbb{N}

and

|S_{\ell_{a}(x)}(W_{a};x)|\geq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\gamma_{a}}.

Since
\ell_{a}(x)<2^{m_{a}}\leq N_{a}
, the monotonicity of
\lambda
and (

) imply

\lambda_{\ell_{a}(x)}\leq\lambda_{N_{a}}<2^{m_{a}-2\gamma_{a}}.

Therefore Corollary
2.2

|V_{\ell_{a}(x)}^{(\lambda)}(W_{a};x)|\geq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\gamma_{a}}.

We decompose

V_{\ell_{a}(x)}^{(\lambda)}(f;x)=I_{a}(x)+II_{a}(x)+III_{a}(x),

where

I_{a}(x):=\delta_{a}V_{\ell_{a}(x)}^{(\lambda)}(W_{a};x),

We first show that
III_{a}(x)=0
. Indeed, by (
3.6
), for
every
k>a
,

m_{a}<m_{k}-2\gamma_{k}.

Hence

\ell_{a}(x)<2^{m_{a}}<2^{m_{k}-2\gamma_{k}}\leq\min\mathrm{Spec}(W_{k}),

where the last inequality follows from Proposition

(iii).
Therefore all Walsh partial sums of

Next we estimate

. If

)

\ell_{a}(x)-\lambda_{\ell_{a}(x)}\geq 2^{m_{a}-\gamma_{a}}-\left(2^{m_{a}-2\gamma_{a}}-1\right)>2^{m_{a}-2\gamma_{a}},

since

, and therefore

V_{\ell_{a}(x)}^{(\lambda)}(W_{k};x)=W_{k}(x).

Using Proposition

(ii), we conclude that

|II_{a}(x)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{a-1}\delta_{k}|W_{k}(x)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{a-1}\delta_{k}2^{\gamma_{k}}\sqrt{\gamma_{k}}<\frac{1}{16}\delta_{a}\sqrt{\gamma_{a}},

where the last inequality follows from (

).

Finally, (
3.8
) yields

(3.10)

|I_{a}(x)|\geq\frac{1}{4}\delta_{a}\sqrt{\gamma_{a}}.

Combining (

), (

), and the identity

, we obtain

|V_{\ell_{a}(x)}^{(\lambda)}(f;x)|\geq|I_{a}(x)|-|II_{a}(x)|\geq\frac{1}{4}\delta_{a}\sqrt{\gamma_{a}}-\frac{1}{16}\delta_{a}\sqrt{\gamma_{a}}=\frac{3}{16}\delta_{a}\sqrt{\gamma_{a}}.

By (

), the last quantity is greater than

. Hence

Since

is arbitrary, (
