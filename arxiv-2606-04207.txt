Abstract
This contribution presents an experimental integrated real-time 8 × 8 8\times 8 distributed MIMO (D-MIMO) testbed for wideband backscatter communication (BSC) and wireless power transfer (WPT) . The testbed operates in the 2.45 GHz 2.45\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz} band with coherent sampling at 200 MS / s 200\text{\,}\mathrm{MS}\text{/}\mathrm{s} , employs a backscatter link frequency of 40 kHz 40\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz} , and uses wideband 5G NR reference signals for excitation. We evaluate the testbed by exploiting the estimated channel state information (CSI) in two target applications: wireless power transfer towards the backscatter device (BD) and real-time positioning of a BD in an indoor environment. In conjunction with the baseband processing chain introduced, the testbed requires less than 2 ms 2\text{\,}\mathrm{ms} of total airtime to excite the system and acquire the signals for subsequent synchronization and CSI estimation on uplink BSC signals. With the CSI , we demonstrate effective energy harvesting gains of up to 12 dB 12\text{\,}\mathrm{dB} .



I
Introduction

A wide range of applications demands massive wireless sensor deployments, including inventory and asset tracking,
monitoring of industrial processes, environmental sensing,
supply chain monitoring, and end-to-end logistics.
Realizing such applications requires minimizing the per-device cost and maintenance effort to ensure long-term economic viability.

Energy-neutral devices (ENDs)
achieve this by eliminating the need for batteries and
reducing device complexity,
but their ultra-low power budgets severely constrain their connectivity.
Consequently,
backscatter communication (BSC)
has emerged as a promising physical-layer
technology for
ENDs
,
as evident from its adoption in billions of
ultra-high frequency (UHF)

radio frequency identification (RFID)
devices
[
1
]
.
In order to further reduce deployment costs,
recent work has focused on integrating
ENDs
into
existing wireless infrastructures such as 5G and Wi-Fi
[
9
,
12
]
.
While this integration is challenged by weak
BSC
signals, strong
direct link interference (DLI)
,
and the lack of synchronization between
ENDs
and the infrastructure
[
5
]
,
it enables the acquisition of wideband
channel state information (CSI)
, which can be exploited for device
localization
[
6
]
and enhanced
wireless power transfer (WPT)

[
4
]
.

In this paper, we present a
distributed MIMO (D-MIMO)

BSC
testbed with multiple
TXs and RXs to facilitate efficient
WPT
towards backscattering
ENDs
in the
downlink (DL)
and to exploit antenna diversity in the
uplink (UL)
.
We introduce a wideband
D-MIMO
signal model applicable to generic baseband signals,
such as high data-rate
orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM)
transmissions, while explicitly accounting for synchronization impairments of the
backscatter device (BD)
. Based on the signal model, we derive an algorithm estimating the synchronization parameters and the
CSI
of the system. Having estimated the
CSI
,
we perform
approximate conjugate
beamforming (BF)
towards the
BD
for
WPT
.
Finally, we position the
BD
by using the estimated
CSI
in
[
6
]
.

At the RXs, we observe the sum of the direct transmission from the TXs to the respective RX, including multipath propagation,

Let us consider the system and signal model in

.
At the

]

The antenna mode scattering for binary

is described by the physical modulation signal

\Gamma[n]\in\{\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
,
approximated with a

The samples of

are stacked into the vector

.
The resulting operator
\bm{\mathrm{B}}=\diag(\bm{\mathrm{b}})
enjoys the following properties:

\bm{\mathrm{B}}=\bm{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathsf{H}}
,

\bm{\mathrm{B}}=\bm{\mathrm{B}}\bm{\mathrm{B}}
,
and there exists a

\bm{\mathrm{A}}=\bm{\mathrm{I}}-\bm{\mathrm{B}}

Figure 2:

\vartheta\in\mathbb{N}
.
The
logical modulator
adheres to the same template waveform, but is forced to the domain
\{0,1\}
.
The signals are sampled with a rate of

We use complex-valued discrete-time

and the hardware (e.g., antennas, cables, and

The resulting signal vector at RX
m
is

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}=\sum_{l=1}^{L}\bigg(\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{l,m}\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{l}+\bm{\mathrm{G}}_{m}\bm{\mathrm{B}}(\eta,\vartheta)\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{l}\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{l}\bigg)+\bm{\mathrm{w}}_{m}

(1)

where
\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{l}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{x}\times 1}
are the samples of a wideband TX baseband signal.
The matrix

\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{l,m}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N_{c}+N_{x}-1)\times N_{x}}
is the linear convolution with the direct channel from TX
l
to RX
m

\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{c}\times 1}
,

\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{l}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N_{h}+N_{x}-1)\times N_{x}}
is the convolution with the
l

DL

\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{l}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{h}\times 1}
,
and
\bm{\mathrm{G}}_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N_{g}+N_{h}+N_{x}-2)\times(N_{h}+N_{x}-1)}

m

UL
channel
\bm{\mathrm{g}}_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{g}\times 1}
.
The parameterized
BSC
modulation operator is
\bm{\mathrm{B}}(\eta,\vartheta)=\diag(\bm{\mathrm{b}}(\eta,\vartheta))
with
\bm{\mathrm{b}}(\eta,\vartheta)\in\{0,1\}^{(N_{h}+N_{x}-1)\times 1}
, where
\eta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is the start time of the modulation signal and
\vartheta\in\mathbb{N}
is
the symbol period.
At each RX
m
, we assume i.i.d. noise
\bm{\mathrm{w}}_{m}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\bm{\mathrm{0}},\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{I}})
.

We assume
\bm{\mathrm{b}}
to be “narrowband” w.r.t. the transmit signals
\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{l}
and the coherence bandwidth of the radio channels
that is large
[
10
, 125–143]
.
This allows us to shift the
BSC
modulation to the RXs,
so we can write the
BSC
channel
\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{l,m}=\bm{\mathrm{G}}_{m}\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{l}
as the convolution of
DL
and
UL
channels
having the cascaded
channel impulse response (CIR)

\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l,m}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N_{h}+N_{g}-1)\times 1}
, resulting in

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}\overset{\mathrm{EMA}}{\approx}\sum_{l=1}^{L}\bigg(\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{l}\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m}+\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{l}\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l,m}\bigg)+\bm{\mathrm{w}}_{m},

(2)

where where
\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{l}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N_{x}+N_{c}-1)\times N_{c}}
is the convolution with the signal
\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{l}
.
The
BSC
modulator is zero-padded to compensate for the dimensionality gain of the
UL
channel,
resulting in
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)=\diag\big[\bm{\mathrm{b}}^{\mathsf{T}}(\eta,\vartheta),\bm{\mathrm{0}}_{1\times(N_{g}\!-\!1)}\big]
.
With this approximation, we neglect the transients to the envelope
\bm{\mathrm{b}}(\eta,\vartheta)
caused by the
UL

CIR
.
We refer to this as the
endpoint modulator approximation (EMA)
. For the following considerations, we assume
N_{c}=N_{h}+N_{g}-1
.

III-B

Synchronization and CSI Estimation

By assuming no prior knowledge about the parameters in
Equation

1

and by assuming stationarity of the environment during the
BD
’s transmission,
we can resort to snapshot-based
concentrated maximum likelihood estimation (CMLE)
to obtain a practical estimator.
The strategy is to get an objective function which is concentrated
w.r.t. the linear systems
\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m}
,
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l,m}
,
and thus depends only on the parameters
\eta
and
\vartheta
.
Once solved for
\eta
and
\vartheta
, we obtain closed-form solutions for
all
\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m}
and
\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l,m}
.

We get the joint
log-likelihood function (LLF)
over all TXs and RXs

\displaystyle\ell\big(\big\{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{l,m}\big\}_{\forall l,m};\{\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m},\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l,m}\}_{\forall l,m},\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)\big)

\displaystyle\propto-\sum_{l,m}\big\|\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{l,m}-\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l}\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m}-\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l}\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l,m}\big\|^{2}

(5)

with
(l,m)\in\{1,\ldots,L\}\times\{1,\ldots,M\}
.
With this we get
LM

single-input single-output (SISO)
estimates of the
BSC
channels conditioned on
(\eta,\vartheta)
and
\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m}
,
\forall(l,m)
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\argmax_{\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}\ell\big(\big\{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{l,m}\big\}_{\forall l,m};\{\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m},\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l,m}\}_{\forall l,m},\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l^{\prime}}\big)^{+}\big(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}-\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l^{\prime}}\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\big)

(6)

which can be computed independently.

Reinserting
Equation

6
into
Equation

5
and maximizing w.r.t. the
DLI

CIRs
yields

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\argmax_{\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}\ell\big(\big\{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{l,m}\big\}_{\forall l,m};\{\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m},\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l,m}}\}_{\forall l,m},\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}_{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l^{\prime}}}^{\perp}\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l^{\prime}}\big)^{+}\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}.

(7)

With
\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}_{\bm{\mathrm{P}}}\coloneqq\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}(\bm{\mathrm{P}})=\bm{\mathrm{P}}\bm{\mathrm{P}}^{+}
and
\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}_{\bm{\mathrm{P}}}^{\perp}\coloneqq\bm{\mathrm{I}}-\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}_{\bm{\mathrm{P}}}

we denote the projection onto the column space and onto the space orthogonal to the column space of
\bm{\mathrm{P}}
, respectively.
With
\bm{\mathrm{P}}^{+}\coloneqq(\bm{\mathrm{P}}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mathrm{P}})^{-1}\bm{\mathrm{P}}^{\mathsf{H}}
we denote the pseudoinverse of
\bm{\mathrm{P}}
.
Exploiting the properties of the logical modulator (see
Section

II
), we can simplify
Equation

7
to

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=(\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l^{\prime}}-\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l^{\prime}})^{+}\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}.

(8)

Reinserting
Equation

6
and
Equation

8
into
Equation

5
yields
the concentrated
LLF
, which is maximized w.r.t.
(\eta,\vartheta)
to obtain synchronization

\displaystyle(\widehat{\eta},\widehat{\vartheta})

\displaystyle=\argmax_{\eta,\vartheta}\ell\big(\big\{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{l,m}\big\}_{\forall l,m};\{\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{l,m}},\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{v}}_{l,m}}\}_{\forall l,m},\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)\big)

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\eta,\vartheta}\sum_{l,m}\big\|(\bm{\mathrm{I}}-\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}_{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{A}}}\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l}}-\bm{\mathrm{\Pi}}_{\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{l}})\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{l,m}\big\|^{2},

(9)

where
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{A}}}(\eta,\vartheta)=\bm{\mathrm{I}}-\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)
is the
complementary modulator
of

\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{B}}}(\eta,\vartheta)

Equation

9
into
Equation

8
,
the solution of
Equation

8
into
Equation

6
, and computing

In this work, we present an integrated real-time
D-MIMO

BSC
testbed
for wideband
RFID
, implementing synchronization,
CSI
estimation,
BF
for
WPT
and single-shot positioning of a
BD
in a realistic indoor scenario.
The
WPT
evaluation shows that
CSI
-based
BF
increases the estimated
RF
input power at the
BD
by approximately
5.9\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}
, while the harvested
DC
power increases by approximately
12.0\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}
due to the improved
PCE
of the Test-IC at the higher operating point.
These results demonstrate the validity of the proposed signal model and processing chain.

With the integrated testbed, we aim to practically validate future developments related to
BSC
,
including initial access schemes for
WPT
,
DL
and
UL
communication, enhanced synchronization and
CSI
estimation algorithms,
and novel positioning concepts.
