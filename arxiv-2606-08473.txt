Physically Consistent Null Space Alignment for Detection of Low-Magnitude False Data Injection Attacks
Xin Li 1 , Chenhan Xiao 2 , Jonathan Cohen 1 , Aviad Elyashar 3 4 , Yang Weng 2 , Rami Puzis 1 4

Abstract
\Acfp fdia introducing small measurement perturbations can still cause large deviations in power system state estimation when the injected signals align with the pseudo-null space of the system model. Existing model- and data-driven detectors may fail to identify such low-magnitude but high-impact attacks because residual tests ignore changes hidden in the pseudo-null space, while subspace learning methods capture correlation patterns without enforcing physical consistency. This paper proposes ping ( ping ), a framework that detects stealthy fdia by preserving, through preprocessing, the geometric correspondence between the physical null space and the measurement-derived pseudo-null space. The key point is a pscp ( pscp ) step that re-expresses measurements in the physical coordinate frame before subspace extraction. We prove that pscp preserves the separation between row space and its orthogonal complement, a property that conventional per-feature standardization violates. This keeps the svd ( svd )-derived pseudo-null subspace aligned with the physical residual space without explicit knowledge of 𝐇 \mathbf{H} . Experiments on IEEE 14-, 30-, 57-, and 118-bus systems confirm this principle in practice: stealthy attacks that evade XTM, LSTM, AE and Isolation Forest baselines appear as clear deviations in the aligned subspace, yielding higher F1-score and detection accuracy while remaining robust under partial observability and realistic PMU noise.

(3)

II-A

Pseudo-null space and operational interpretation.

A central concept for this paper is the pseudo-null space. In theory, state perturbations within the exact null space of
\mathbf{H}
(i.e.,
\mathbf{c}\in\mathrm{Null}(\mathbf{H})
) map to zero measurement change (i.e.,
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{0}
).
In practical transmission
se
formulations, the Jacobian is rank-deficient (reference/slack bus, measurement sparsity, etc.), and measurement/model mismatch, heterogeneous scaling, and numerical errors make the exact null space ill-defined for detection purposes. We therefore use the term pseudo-null space to denote the near-null directions of
\mathbf{H}
(or of a reduced Jacobian) that produce only negligible measurement changes, i.e., vectors
\mathbf{c}_{n}
with
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{c}_{n}\approx\mathbf{0}
in the presence of realistic noise and modeling error.
Attacks constructed along these directions can cause significant state bias while leaving residuals (and many statistical detectors) unchanged
[
19
]
(see
Sections

IV-B
and
7
).

II-B

Practical Bridge: Model null space vs. Measurement-derived Pseudo-Null Space.

In many modern operational settings, the true Jacobian
\mathbf{H}
is partially unknown, outdated, or effectively time-varying (outsourced preprocessing, heterogeneous
pmu

ai

Consequently, computing
\mathrm{Null}(\mathbf{H})
may be infeasible or unreliable.
Therefore, in practice, we infer an empirical pseudo-null space from a short history of measurements using low-rank subspace extraction (e.g.,
svd
/
pca

This measurement-derived pseudo-null space encodes the empirical geometric directions that carry little measurement magnitude. The detection principle used in this paper is to (i) make the measurement-derived pseudo-null extraction physically meaningful through appropriate normalization and standardization, and then (ii) monitor the projection into that inferred pseudo-null subspace: anomalous increases indicate geometric misalignment consistent with a low-magnitude, null space aligned
fdia
.
This strategy therefore implements the conceptual comparison between the physical invariance implied by
\mathbf{H}
and the behavior observed in data, without requiring explicit, perfect knowledge of
\mathbf{H}
.

Although the discussion above focuses on the
dc

ac

In the
ac

\mathbf{z}=\boldsymbol{h}(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{e}
, where
\boldsymbol{h}(\cdot)
encodes nonlinear power-flow relationships between voltage magnitudes and phase angles.
The corresponding

\hat{\mathbf{x}}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{z}-\boldsymbol{h}(\mathbf{x}))^{\top}\mathbf{R}^{-1}(\mathbf{z}-\boldsymbol{h}(\mathbf{x})),

(6)

which requires iterative numerical solutions but preserves the same geometric interpretation.
Small perturbations aligned with the local column space of the Jacobian
\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x})
can still induce significant state deviations while evading residual-based tests.

III-A

Pseudo-Null Space
fdia

We assume two capabilities commonly attributed to adversaries:
(1) the ability to modify arbitrary measurement values, e.g., by compromising the data flow; and
(2) the ability to estimate partial or approximate system parameters using historical data.
Prior works show that even without explicit topology access, the system Jacobian
\mathbf{H}
can be inferred from spatiotemporal correlations
[
6
]
.

Classic stealthy
fdia

[
14
]
exploit the inferred Jacobian to inject
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{c}
, where
\mathbf{c}
is any vector in the state space.
Because
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{0}
, the resulting residual
\|\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{H}\mathbf{c})\|_{2}^{2}=\|\mathbf{S}\mathbf{z}\|_{2}^{2}
remains unchanged, enabling the attack to bypass
bdd

However, such model-based
fdia
distort the geometry of the measurement manifold, producing persistent temporal patterns detectable by machine-learning methods
[
39
]
.
Here, the measurement manifold in the
dc
model refers to the set of all physically feasible measurements generated by the DC state-estimation model. Since the DC measurement function is linear, this set is

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{DC}}=\{\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}.

Because the number of measurements is typically larger than the number of states,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{DC}}
is a low-dimensional linear subspace embedded in the measurement space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Normal measurements lie near this subspace, with deviations mainly caused by measurement noise.

In this paper, we consider a new class of pseudo-null space attacks that regain stealth under non-stationary and heterogeneous conditions by manipulating measurements along directions that remain approximately invariant even as the system Jacobian drifts.

Under the
dc
model,
\mathbf{H}
is rank-deficient due to the reference bus.
After removing the reference-bus column, we obtain a reduced Jacobian
\mathbf{H}_{r}
.
We define the
\tau
-pseudo-null space of
\mathbf{H}_{r}
as

\mathcal{N}_{\tau}(\mathbf{H}_{r})=\left\{\mathbf{c}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:\|\mathbf{H}_{r}\mathbf{c}_{n}\|_{2}\leq\tau\|\mathbf{c}_{n}\|_{2}\right\},

(7)

where
\tau>0
is a prescribed tolerance.
Equivalently, if
\mathbf{H}_{r}=U\Sigma V^{\top}
, then
\mathcal{N}_{\tau}(\mathbf{H}_{r})
is spanned by the right singular vectors associated with singular values below
\tau
.

An attacker constructs a stealth perturbation
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{H}_{r}\mathbf{c}
with
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}_{n}+\mathbf{c}_{e}
, where
\mathbf{c}_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\tau}(\mathbf{H}_{r})
and
\mathbf{c}_{e}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{c}^{2}I_{n-1})
adds small Gaussian fluctuations.
Because
\|\mathbf{H}_{r}\mathbf{c}_{n}\|_{2}\leq\tau\|\mathbf{c}_{n}\|_{2}
, the deterministic component induced by
\mathbf{c}_{n}
is small, so the effective perturbation
\mathbf{a}
becomes statistically close to
pmu
noise when
\tau
is properly chosen.
By tuning the variance of
\mathbf{c}_{e}
to half the measurement-noise variance, the injected modification hides within nominal uncertainty while altering the state estimate.
Thus, the attack reshapes state estimation without triggering either
bdd

Section

IV-B
,
Figures

2
and
3

The parameter
\tau
controls the tradeoff between stealth and attack flexibility: smaller
\tau
yields more stealthy but less diverse perturbations, while larger

enlarges the feasible attack subspace at the cost of increased detectability.
In practice,
\tau
can be chosen by singular-value truncation or relative to the measurement noise level.

Unlike the

In this case, a stealthy perturbation should satisfy

(8)

Linearizing

Since

We emphasize that the proposed pseudo-null space FDIA represents a worst-case threat model for evaluating detector robustness. Specifically, the attacker is assumed to have access to an abundant volume of normal historical measurements and can use these data to learn or approximate the measurement manifold and its pseudo-null directions. Similar assumptions are common in blind FDIA studies, where the attacker does not require exact system parameters but can infer attack directions from measurement data. The effectiveness of such attacks depends on data availability and data quality: with fewer normal samples, partial meter access, inaccurate historical data, or distribution drift, the learned measurement manifold becomes less accurate and the attack performance is expected to degrade. Therefore, the attack proposed in Section
III-A
should be interpreted as a stress-test scenario for evaluating whether the detection method introduced in Section
III-B
can identify low-magnitude, physically consistent FDIAs under strong adversarial conditions.

Extension to
ac
model.

The proposed
ping
framework naturally extends to the
ac

ac

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{AC}}=\{\boldsymbol{h}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},
which is generally a nonlinear manifold embedded in the measurement space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Around an operating point
\mathbf{x}_{0}

\boldsymbol{J}(\mathbf{x}_{0})
. Specifically,

\boldsymbol{h}(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\Delta\mathbf{x})=\boldsymbol{h}(\mathbf{x}_{0})+\boldsymbol{J}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\Delta\mathbf{x}+\mathcal{O}(\|\Delta\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}).

Thus, the accuracy of the local pseudo-null space approximation depends on the curvature of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{AC}}
and the size of the operating-point variation within the analysis window. When the trajectory remains in a locally coherent operating region, the second-order term is small and the tangent-space approximation is reliable. When the trajectory spans strongly nonlinear or rapidly changing regions, the tangent space may rotate across samples, so a single
svd
-derived subspace approximates a union of nearby local low-rank subspaces rather than one fixed flat subspace.

This local low-rank structure is further reinforced by physical dependencies in
ac
networks. In particular, the effective rank of
\mathbf{Z}
decreases when linear dependencies arise among currents and flows, especially along degree-2 series chains of injection-free buses.

1

ac

remains effective in standard IEEE

dc

Conclusion

This paper presented the
ping
(
ping
) framework for detecting low-magnitude
fdia
(
fdia
) that remain hidden within measurement noise yet distort state estimation.
By preserving the physical null space structure using a preprocessing step formalized via null space
conservation,
ping
identifies small but physically inconsistent perturbations that are overlooked by
conventional detectors.

Such low-magnitude attacks can induce significant physical consequences such as a 5% bias in state estimation as demonstrated in
Section

IV-B
.
Such bias may yield over 10% voltage-sensitive load mismatch, degrading reactive power balance, frequency regulation
[
37
]
, and economic dispatch
[
31
]
.
These findings highlight the need for a detection mechanism that operates without assuming fixed or fully known measurement-space models.
The proposed
pscp
(
pscp
) ensures normalization in the physical coordinate frame, improving robustness against heterogeneous
pmu
scaling and numerical drift. The numerical results demonstrate that enforcing physical data alignment offers an effective and scalable defense against stealthy
fdia
in modern power grids.
As a result, the proposed approach creates a conceptual shift from detecting statistical deviation to identifying physical misalignment, thus linking data-driven analysis with the underlying system physics in a unified manner.
