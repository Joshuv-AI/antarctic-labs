Final Technical Report
DOE award number: DE-SC0021629
Sponsoring program office: Office of Fusion Energy Sciences
Name of recipient: Massachusetts Institute of Technology
Project title: Role of Neutrals Versus Transport in Determining the Pedestal Density Structure
Name of Principal Investigator: Jerry Hughes, MIT
Teaming Member: Saskia Mordijck, William & Mary

Abstract

In fusion devices the plasma density plays a crucial role in determining the fusion reaction rate
and has a direct impact on the fusion gain of a given device. This density is in general regulated
by the particle sources and transport near the plasma edge, which give rise to an edge density
pedestal. When predicting the performance of future devices, this density pedestal is often
prescribed, rather than predicted, due to a lack of models which allow confident extrapolation.
This project aims to advance these models through the focused validation of theoretical models
related to the transport of fueling neutral particles, and through interpretive transport modeling
in present day fusion plasmas, in which the penetration of neutrals is altered to better simulate
future reactor-like conditions. Achievements in theory and model validation under this project
have advanced our understanding of how much of the edge density profile is set by transport
versus direct ionization, enabling interesting projections to future burning plasma devices.

1. Project description

This project was performed under Award DE-SC0021629 (May 2021—April 2025), in
collaboration with Prof. Saskia Mordijck of William & Mary (through Award DE-SC0007880).
Under this Award MIT and W&M collaborated in order to investigate particle transport and the
role of the neutral density source at the edge of tokamak plasmas.

A long term aim of research at both MIT and W&M is to develop predictive capability for the
density edge pedestal profile in tokamaks in a self-consistent manner, taking into account both
plasma and neutral particle transport processes. As part of this Award, we identified several steps
toward accomplishing our goals. In the first phase of the Award, we focused on the validation of
computational neutral models that not only provide direct information on how much the neutrals
contribute to the formation of the pedestal, but also provides crucial information on the particle
flux. A good estimate of the particle flux is necessary to validate transport models. The modeling
and validation focused on the DIII-D and Alcator C-Mod tokamaks, which had measurements of
edge ionization rates under various neutral fueling conditions.

MIT effort on the project was focused on
 • Assembling large experimental data sets from existing C-Mod parameter scans, with
 requisite data for calculating particle transport empirically
 • Identifying specific C-Mod discharges in the data archive with fuller data sets for targeted
 validation with the SOLPS-ITER code

 • Performing select interpretive modeling of C-Mod data set using the SOLPS-ITER code
 • Determining empirical relations and scalings for empirical edge particle transport from
 selected C-Mod databases
Key achievements under these tasks will be described below, with comprehensive details found
in the cited references.

At MIT this Award supported effort from the PI and a graduate student in the MIT Nuclear Science
and Engineering Department, Andrés Miller. Miller successfully defended his PhD thesis, which
included work under this Award, in 2025. He received one on one mentorship from the MIT PI
(Hughes), and benefited from regular project meetings with our W&M collaborators. Since the
beginning of the project, MIT and W&M have held a monthly gathering of the co-PIs and their
students, inviting other scientists and students from our institutions also working on neutral
physics. This has provided a collegial environment for the sharing of experience and knowledge,
and has fostered collaboration across teams.

During his PhD Miller gained experience in both analysis of experimental data from fusion devices
and in running sophisticated modeling codes, including SOLPS-ITER. He installed SOLPS-ITER on
a shared computing platform at MIT for the first time, a step which was encouraging for other
MIT personnel desiring to use the code, including students and early career researchers.

2. Motivation

In current day fusion experiments the recycling of particles in the divertor and main chamber
along with gas fueling results in a neutral source that penetrates past the separatrix and directly
contributes to the electron density profile through ionization. Multiple theories exist on where
the neutrals mainly originate, what their energies are, and whether they solely determine the
density profile at the plasma edge or whether an inward particle pinch is necessary to explain
realized profiles. Moreover, predictions from models depend on highly variable quantities like
the machine size, divertor geometry, wall materials as well as plasma conditions. Progress was
needed on validation of neutral models as the use of direct measurements of neutrals in
experimental devices. By combining empirical results with dedicated edge particle transport
modeling, we aimed to develop improved models applicable to future reactor-like devices, in
which direct fueling inside the separatrix by recycled or puﬀed neutrals will be nearly zero.

In C-Mod, our modeling focused on discharges that are highly opaque to neutrals. High neutral
opaqueness implies that the penetration of neutrals inside the separatrix is strongly reduced. We
set out to compare simulations of the neutral densities to archival C-Mod discharges, in which
the neutral densities at the mid plane could be inferred experimentally. At the same time, we
wished to compare to simulations of DIII-D discharges. DIII-D is less opaque to neutrals than C-
Mod and both the wall material as well as the divertor configuration are very diﬀerent. Such a
breadth of discharge type was intended to enable our testing the neutral model in SOLPS-ITER in
various conditions, and also to allow us to identify the underlying dominant plasma transport
mechanisms, as well as expand the conditions for which the neutral model in SOLPS-ITER is valid.

Accurately determining the ionization source in current day devices (1) tells us the source’s
influence on the edge plasma density profile structure, and (2) enables development of improved
edge plasma transport models. Since the neutral sources are not poloidally uniform and this
asymmetry is diﬀerent for each device, it is not adequate to rely solely on measurements to
constrain the source. Since models often contain incomplete physics or deliberate simplifications,
it is not adequate to rely on simulations unconstrained by experiment. Thus our emphasis on
validated models of the neutral transport within the plasma boundary, which then can provide
direct reliable input for the particle flux, which acts as a constraint for the transport models.

3. Technical accomplishments

Year 1
(May 2021 – April 2022)

Our team developed a workflow for analyzing data from archival C-Mod experimental campaigns,
in which radial profiles of Lyman alpha brightness were available. The workflow inverts brightness
to generate emissivity profiles, then maps electron density and temperature measurements from
Thomson scattering onto the same coordinates, and finally uses ADAS rates to compute neutral
density profiles and ionization rates, with robust uncertainty estimates. Profiles obtained using
these techniques were directly compared with SOLPS-ITER simulations performed by W&M. As
shown in [Mordijck 2024], good agreement could be obtained between experiment and
simulation for three distinct confinement regimes (i.e. L-mode, H-mode, I-mode), with
reasonable matches to edge electron density and temperature, as well as Lyman-alpha
brightness. This constituted a novel use of Lyman-alpha measurements to validate and constrain
2D simulations of edge ionization sources.

We used the above workflow to process a set of C-Mod discharges from the 2007 and 2008
campaigns, and constructed a database containing profile information and a number of discharge
parameters. We then explored the data set more deeply to identify discharges that had extended
data, for example from reciprocating probes in the edge. Additional analysis was performed on
several of these discharges to build more accurate models of the scrape-off layer density and
temperature, for use in extended SOLPS-ITER validation. A number of validation test cases were
evaluated, including discharges from experiments that examined the effects of strong vs. weak
divertor pumping. Empirically inferred edge ionization rates measured at the midplane of C-Mod
were confirmed to be significantly reduced in the pumped cases, concurrent with reduced
separatrix density [Miller 2025a].

In Year 1 our work contributed to two graduate student presentations at the Fall 2021 APS
Division of Plasma Physics meeting, a contributed oral from a William & Mary student and a
poster from MIT student Andrés Miller.

Year 2
(May 2022 – April 2023)

The database developed from archival C-Mod data was extended to include a broader set of
experimental conditions, and the workflows above were used to fully populate this database.
Profiles of several edge plasma quantities were added to the database, including computed
ionization sources and plasma density gradients. These have enabled calculations of estimated
particle fluxes and, consequently, and graphical estimations of flux-gradient relationships for
particle transport. Considering a power scan in C-Mod, the empirical method clearly
demonstrated a diffusive model with an invariant D profile is not suitable to explain the inferred
particle fluxes. Instead, enhanced D is found at reduced power and is correlated to increased
collisionality at the separatrix. [Miller 2025b] That the separatrix quantities best organized the
transport data, as opposed to conditions further into the confined plasma, was a new and
important finding for C-Mod H-modes.

SOLPS-ITER was run in an interpretive mode on a number of C-Mod discharges selected on the
basis of good plasma profile and neutral data. Part of this process involved adjusting transport
coefficients in simulation in order to match plasma kinetic profiles. This process was accelerated
by the adaptation of an iterative numerical technique developed for DIII-D SOLPS-ITER
simulations. In collaboration with W&M partners, we generalized this algorithm for use on
arbitrary devices. Lyman-alpha profile data were found to provide a powerful constraint on the
SOLPS-ITER solutions, although this requires thoughtful adjustment of input transport coefficient
profiles and boundary conditions, including recycling coefficients. Simulations of specific C-Mod
experiments that compared pumped and unpumped H-modes were performed, demonstrating
conclusively that a purely diffusive pedestal transport model is not sufficient to predict the
response of plasma profiles to changes in pumping [Miller 2025a].

Extending SOLPS-ITER modeling from C-Mod to DIII-D, with our William & Mary partners, we
further showed that the neutral opaqueness can act as a control parameter for the localization
of neutral fueling. As opaqueness increases (for example by increasing density on DIII-D, and then
from increasing density on C-Mod), the importance of divertor fueling decreases and poloidal
asymmetries in neutral sources diminishes [Mordijck 2024].

In Year 2 our work contributed to two poster presentations from Miller: one at the 18th
International Workshop on H-mode Physics and Transport Barriers and another at the 2022 APS
Division of Plasma Physics meeting.

Year 3
(May 2023 – April 2024)

We successfully completed the SOLPS-ITER validation exercise of an experimental C-Mod survey
with high fidelity neutral emissivity data and established improved confidence in the technique
developed earlier for inferring particle transport directly from experimental data. We did this by
directly comparing the diffusivity calculated with the full 2D SOLPS-ITER code with the effective
D calculated in the database for a number of representative cases. Good agreement was
obtained, and moreover the interpretative simulations were used to show a marked increase in
thermal diffusivity as H-mode net power dropped to levels below the usual threshold for H-mode

access. [Miller2025b] The collisionality at the separatrix continued to show distinct correlations
with the derived transport.

Moreover, we found that a turbulence control parameter proportional to collisionality and edge
safety factor was also highly effective: at. The at parameter is fundamental to the determination
of an operational space for the tokamak separatrix put forward by Eich and Manz in [Nucl. Fusion
61 086017 (2021)] and known as SepOS. Working closely with both Eich and Manz, Miller
confirmed the efficacy of this model on C-Mod, partially with the support of this Award [Miller
2025c]. After expressing C-Mod particle transport with an effective diffusion D that increases
substantially with at>1, a contemporary pedestal density model due to Saarelma and Connor
[Nucl. Fusion 63 052002 (2023)] was tested on a C-Mod density scan with some success. Notably,
obtaining agreement at the highest pedestal density values required the assumption of enhanced
diffusivity proportional to at [Miller 2026]. The Saarelma-Connor model, modified thus, was
applied to the SPARC tokamak with promising results.

Overall the diffusivity estimates from C-Mod were a key ingredient in a survey of transport which
estimated the values of a possible particle pinch, existing in the H-mode pedestal region
[Mordijck 2024]. While this exercise cannot conclusively prove the existence of a pinch, it is highly
suggestive of inward convective transport around the pedestal top, as shown independently
through dynamic density perturbation experiments on DIII-D led by MIT PhD student A. Rosenthal
[Nucl. Fusion 63 042002 (2023); Nucl. Fusion 64 036006 (2024)].

In Year 3 our work contributed to three student presentations at the 2023 APS Division of
Plasma Physics meeting, a poster from a W&M undergrad, a poster from a recent W&M PhD,
and an oral from Miller from MIT. The award supported co-authorship of a modeling paper
published as [Parisi 2024].

(May 2024 – April 2025)

In Year 4 the proposed technical work was completed and several supported publications

3. Summary

This Award supported advances in the validation of computational neutral models that inform
density profile formation and fuel particle inventory in fusion plasmas, with particular emphasis
on the impact of increasing opaqueness to neutrals, which will prove increasingly relevant in
burning plasma regimes. Achievements include:

 • Development of techniques to build a self-consistent data set from the Alcator C-Mod
 archive, containing plasma density and temperature profiles as well as edge neutral data
 constraining edge ionization source rates and radial particle fluxes
 • Targeted validation of the SOLPS-ITER code using selected subsets of the broader C-Mod
 data and demonstration of interpretive particle transport constrained by inferred neutral
 profiles
 • Interpretive modeling of C-Mod data set using the SOLPS-ITER code to validate the
 empirical inference of particle transport coefficients using a diffusive model
 • Evidence of radial particle transport near the last closed flux surface being regulated by
 collisionality, or by a related quantity set largely by the edge density, temperature and
 safety factor
 • The application of the above radial transport to a prospective high-density scenario in
 SPARC

Overall, the Award was successful in accomplishing its goals, establishing a foundation for
improved particle transport and density projections for future burning plasmas. The Award also
contributed to workforce development, having supported M.A. Miller’s PhD thesis work. It is
acknowledged in eight peer-reviewed publications, included five with Miller as lead author.
