Algebraic cobordism rings of wonderful varieties and matroids
Raj Gandhi and Ethan Partida

Abstract
. We give two combinatorial presentations for the algebraic cobordism ring Ω ∗ ​ ( M ) \Omega^{*}(M) of the toric variety of the Bergman fan of any loopless matroid M M . As a consequence of our presentations, we obtain an Ω ∗ ​ ( pt ) \Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt}) -algebra isomorphism Ω ∗ ​ ( M ) ≃ CH ∗ ​ ( M ) ⊗ ℤ Ω ∗ ​ ( pt ) \Omega^{*}(M)\simeq\mathrm{CH}^{*}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt}) , where CH ∗ ​ ( M ) \mathrm{CH}^{*}(M) is the Chow ring of M M and Ω ∗ ​ ( pt ) \Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt}) is the algebraic cobordism ring of the point. 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an arrangement of hyperplanes in a complex vector space
L
whose intersection is the origin in
L
. The arrangement
\mathcal{H}
gives rise to a loopless matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
whose flats are indexed by the various intersections of hyperplanes in
\mathcal{H}
. De Concini and Procesi defined a smooth compactification
W_{\mathcal{H}}
of the complement of the hyperplane arrangement in
L
, known as the
wonderful compactification
of
\mathcal{H}

[
10
]
, whose boundary is a simple normal crossings divisor. Much of the geometry of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
is dictated by invariants of the matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
. As
W_{\mathcal{H}}
is a smooth, projective variety, such invariants of
M_{\mathcal{H}}
must satisfy strong structural properties. Sometimes these properties can be given a purely combinatorial explanation. This, in turn, lets one prove new structural results about matroids, even those not realizable over any field.

Given a loopless matroid
M
, there is a (usually non-compact) toric variety
X_{M}
canonically associated to
M
whose Chow ring
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
has an explicit description
[
16
]
. Feichtner and Yuzvinsky proved that when
M_{\mathcal{H}}
is the matroid of a complex hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}
, there is a canonical inclusion
W_{\mathcal{H}}\hookrightarrow X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}
which induces an isomorphism of Chow rings
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}})\simeq\mathrm{CH}^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})

[
16
]
. As the cycle class map is an isomorphism for
W_{\mathcal{H}}
(Lemma
5.4
), this gives the Chow ring of
X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}
the structure of the cohomology ring of a smooth, complex, projective variety. An important consequence of this is that
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}})
has the Kähler package
[
15
]
. Remarkably, Adiprasito–Huh–Katz proved that
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
has the Kähler package for every loopless matroid
M
, regardless of whether or not
M
is realizable
[
1
]
. This result is a key step in their proof of the Heron–Rota–Welsh conjecture
[
1
]
.

In recent work
[
27
]
, Larson–Li–Payne–Proudfoot studied the Grothendieck ring of algebraic vector bundles on
W_{\mathcal{H}}
, which we denote by
K^{0}(W_{\mathcal{H}})
(following the notation of Levine and Morel
[
29
]
), and refer to as the
K
-ring of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
. They proved that the inclusion
W_{\mathcal{H}}\hookrightarrow X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}
induces an isomorphism of
K
-rings
K^{0}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}})\simeq K^{0}(W_{\mathcal{H}})
. The authors also proved that, for any loopless matroid
M
, the
K
-ring of
X_{M}
admits structures such as Adams operations, an Euler characteristic map, and Serre duality. Surprisingly, they also showed that the Chow ring and
K
-ring of
X_{M}
are integrally isomorphic. This isomorphism is known as an
exceptional isomorphism
in the literature and was first proven in the Boolean case by Berget–Eur–Spink–Tseng
[
5
]
. The exceptional isomorphism is not directly related to the Chern character isomorphism between the rational
K
-ring and the rational Chow ring of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
.

The functors
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(-)
and
K^{0}(-)
which send a smooth complex variety to its Chow ring and
K
-ring are examples of
oriented cohomology theories
(
Section

3.2
). There is a universal oriented cohomology theory
\Omega^{*}(-)
called
algebraic cobordism
constructed by Levine and Morel
[
29
]
. Levine and Morel’s result is an analogue of the fundamental observation of Quillen that complex cobordism
MU^{*}(-)
is the universal complex-oriented cohomology theory
[
30
]
. For a smooth complex variety
X
, there is a natural morphism
\Omega^{*}(X)\to MU^{*}(X)
which is an isomorphism when
X
is a point, but is not an isomorphism in general (
3.15
). For any free oriented cohomology theory
h^{*}(-)
, of which
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(-)
and
K^{0}(-)
are examples, the natural morphism
\Omega^{*}(X)\to h^{*}(X)
is surjective and
h^{*}(X)
can be recovered from
\Omega^{*}(X)
(
3.20
). Computations of algebraic cobordism rings are generally very difficult and explicit ring presentations are uncommon. Some of the few families of varieties for which presentations of their algebraic cobordism rings are known are smooth toric varieties
[
25
]
, complete flag varieties
[
21
,
7
]
, Pﬁster quadrics
[
37
]
, and wonderful symmetric varieties of minimal rank
[
23
]
. We refer the reader to Section
3
for an introduction to algebraic cobordism.

In this paper, we study the algebraic cobordism rings of wonderful compactifications
W_{\mathcal{H}}
and toric varieties of Bergman fans of loopless matroids
X_{M}
. Our first result is a “simplicial” presentation of the ring
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})
. In this presentation, the generators of
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})
are lifts of the “simplicial generators” for
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
, introduced by Backman–Eur–Simpson
[
3
]
, to the algebraic cobordism ring of
X_{M}
. We derive the simplicial presentation from careful manipulations of the universal formal group laws that appear in the presentation of
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})
deduced from Krishna and Uma’s description of the algebraic cobordism ring of a smooth toric variety
[
25
]
. In the following, we use
\mathrm{pt}=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
and refer the reader to Sections
2
and
3
for undefined terminology.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
4.12
)
Let
M
be a loopless matroid. The algebraic cobordism ring of
X_{M}
is isomorphic to

\frac{\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})[h_{G}:\text{ $G$ a non-empty flat of $M$}]}{\left\langle(h_{G}-h_{G\vee H})(h_{H}-h_{G\vee H}):G,H\text{ non-empty flats of $M$}\right\rangle+\left\langle h_{G}:G\text{ atom}\right\rangle}

as an
\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
-algebra. This presentation yields an isomorphism of
\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
-algebras:

(1)

\Omega^{*}(X_{M})\simeq\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt}).

The isomorphism (
1
) is unexpected, and it immediately implies that there is an integral ring isomorphism
K^{0}(X_{M})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
(
4.16
). In fact, for any free oriented cohomology theory
\mathrm{h}^{*}(-)
, we have that
\mathrm{h}^{*}(X_{M})
is isomorphic to
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})\otimes_{\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})}\mathrm{h}^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
as an
\mathrm{h}^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
-algebra. Thus, the isomorphism (
1
) can be viewed as a generalization of the exceptional isomorphism
K^{0}(X_{M})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
. The only other varieties
X
that the authors are aware of which have an
\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
-isomorphism
\Omega^{*}(X)\simeq\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
are projective space
[
29
]
, del Pezzo surfaces
[
12
, Proposition 4.1]
, and complete flag varieties
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
. Even if
X
is a smooth, projective toric variety, there can fail to be such an isomorphism. Indeed, Speyer
[
32
]
gives an example of a smooth, projective toric variety
X
such that
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
and
K^{0}(X)
are not isomorphic as rings, and from this it follows that
\Omega^{*}(X)
and
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
are not isomorphic as
\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
-algebras. This example suggests that the isomorphism (
1
) is very special. It would be interesting to have a more systematic study of the varieties
X
for which
\Omega^{*}(X)\simeq\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
. For completeness of our exposition, we have included Speyer’s example as
4.17
in this article.

As our next main result, we prove that the inclusion
W_{\mathcal{H}}\hookrightarrow X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}
induces an isomorphism of algebraic cobordism rings
\Omega^{*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}})\simeq\Omega^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
5.10
)
Let
W_{\mathcal{H}}
be the wonderful compactification of a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}
and
M_{\mathcal{H}}
its associated matroid. The inclusion
\iota:W_{\mathcal{H}}\hookrightarrow X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}
induces an isomorphism of
\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
-algebras:

\iota^{*}:\Omega^{*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}}).

Along the way to proving
1.2
, we prove that
\Omega^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})
is isomorphic, as an
\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
-algebra, to the complex cobordism ring
MU^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})
of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
(
5.10
). Comparisons between the complex and algebraic cobordism rings of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
are a key technical aspect of our proof of
1.2
.

In a very recent preprint, Debnath and Zeng give a presentation of the oriented cohomology ring of the blowup of a smooth scheme along a smooth center
[
12
]
. As
W_{\mathcal{H}}
is an iterated blowup of projective space and
\Omega^{*}(-)
is an oriented cohomology theory, it should be possible to derive a presentation of the algebraic cobordism ring of
W_{H}
from Debnath and Zeng’s result. However, such a ring presentation will likely be a few steps removed from the presentation of
\Omega^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})
given by Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. In fact, the analogous translation between two different presentations of the Chow ring of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
is one of the main results of
[
16
]
.

We close by noting that the class
[W_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathrm{pt}]
of the wonderful compactification in the algebraic (and also complex) cobordism ring of a point only depends on the matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
of
\mathcal{H}
. This follows from results of Cheng on the Chern numbers of the tangent bundle of
W_{\mathcal{H}}

[
9
]
. In future work, the first author plans to give a recursive formula for the class of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
in
\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
using recent techniques of Ellis-Bloor
[
14
]
, who gives a recursive formula for the class of
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{0,n}}
in
\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
. Obtaining such a formula can be seen as the first step towards explicitly describing the proper push-forward map
\Omega^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})\to\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
. See
5.18
for further details.

This paper is organized as follows. In

2.1.
Matroids and hyperplane arrangements

We will recall the definition of a matroid, and describe the matroid corresponding to a hyperplane arrangement.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E
be a finite set. A loopless
matroid

M
on the ground set
E
is a collection
\mathcal{F}
of subsets of
E
called
flats
that satisfy:

•

\emptyset\in\mathcal{F}
.

•

If
G,H\in\mathcal{F}
, then
G\cap H\in\mathcal{F}
.

•

For any
G\in\mathcal{F}
and any
i\in E\setminus G
, there is a unique, inclusion-minimal flat
H\in\mathcal{F}
containing
i
and
G
.

For two flats
G
and
H
, their
meet

G\wedge H
is the intersection
G\cap H
and their
join

G\vee H
is the smallest flat containing
G\cup H
. Two flats
H
and
G
are called
incomparable
if
H\not\subseteq G
and
G\not\subseteq H
. If
H
and
G
are not incomparable, then they are
comparable
.
When ordered by inclusion, the set
\mathcal{F}
forms a geometric lattice. The
rank

\mathrm{rk}(F)
of a flat
F
is equal to the length of the largest chain of comparable flats properly contained in
F
. An
atom
is a flat of rank
1
. It follows from the axioms that
E\in\mathcal{F}
. Let
r
be the rank of
E
. In this case, we say that
M
has rank
r
. The
corank
of a flat
G
of
M
is the number
r-\mathrm{rk}(G)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A complex, central and essential
hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{H}
is a multiset of hyperplanes
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in E}
in a complex vector space
L
indexed by a finite set
E
such that
\bigcap_{i\in E}H_{i}=\{0\}
.

In this paper, all hyperplane arrangements will be complex, central and essential. We henceforth drop the extra adjectives.

Every hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{i}\}_{i\in E}
determines a loopless matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
on the ground set
E
. For a subset
S\subseteq E
, let
L_{S}
be the linear subspace
L_{S}=\bigcap_{i\in S}H_{i}
. The set of flats of
M_{\mathcal{H}}
is equal to

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}=\{F\subseteq E:\text{ if }i\in E\setminus F,\text{ then }L_{F\cup i}\neq L_{F}\}.

In this case, the rank of a flat
F
is equal to the codimension of
L_{F}
in
L
.

For an integer
n\geq 1
, let
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an arrangement of generic hyperplanes indexed by a finite set
E
in an
r
-dimensional vector space
L
. The flats of
M_{\mathcal{H}}
consist of all subsets
S\subseteq E
with size strictly less than
r
along with the set
E
. In this case, we refer to
M_{\mathcal{H}}
as the
uniform matroid
of rank
r
on the ground set
E
. If
r=|E|
, then we say that
M_{\mathcal{H}}
is the
Boolean matroid
on the ground set
E
.
When
E=[n]
, then we denote
M_{\mathcal{H}}
by
U_{r,n}
.

2.2.
Wonderful varieties of hyperplane arrangements

We recall the definition of the wonderful compactification of a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the maximal building set. Wonderful compactifications were introduced in the work of de Concini and Procesi
[
10
]
. There, they define wonderful compactifications with respect to any choice of
building set
. A building set is a subset of the flats of
M_{\mathcal{H}}
which obeys certain axioms. The set
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}\setminus\emptyset
is always a building set and is known as the maximal building set. Wonderful compactifications, as we define them here, will always be with respect to the maximal building set.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hyperplane arrangement in a complex vector space
L
whose hyperplanes are indexed by a finite set
E
. Now projectivise each hyperplane
H_{i}
in
\mathcal{H}
to obtain a collection of hyperplanes
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{H}=\{\mathbb{P}(H_{i})\}_{i\in E}
inside the projective space
\mathbb{P}(L)
. The wonderful variety
W_{\mathcal{H}}
is a smooth compactification of the open subset
\mathbb{P}(L)\setminus\bigcup_{i\in E}\mathbb{P}(H_{i})
of
\mathbb{P}(L)
, whose boundary is a simple normal crossings divisor. We will now discuss the construction of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Definition 2.5
.

The
wonderful variety

W_{\mathcal{H}}
of a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}
can be constructed as an iterated blowup of
\mathbb{P}(L)
.

(1)

First, blow up
\mathbb{P}(L)
at
\mathbb{P}(L_{G})
for all corank
1
flats
G
in
M_{\mathcal{H}}
. Call the resulting variety
W_{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
.

(2)

Next, blow up
W_{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
at the strict transforms of
\mathbb{P}(L_{G})
for all corank
2
flats
G
in
M_{\mathcal{H}}
. Call the resulting variety
W_{\mathcal{H}}^{(2)}
.

(3)

Continue in this way to obtain
W_{\mathcal{H}}^{(k)}
for each
k=1,\ldots,r-1
. This results in a sequence of blow-ups

\pi\colon W_{\mathcal{H}}^{(r-1)}\to W_{\mathcal{H}}^{(r-2)}\to\dotsb W_{\mathcal{H}}^{(2)}\to W_{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}\to\mathbb{P}(L)

and we define
W_{\mathcal{H}}=W_{\mathcal{H}}^{(r-1)}
.

Theorem 2.6
.

(
[
10
]
)
The iterated blow-up
\pi\colon W_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{P}(L)
is a smooth compactification of
\mathbb{P}(L)\setminus\bigcup_{i\in E}\mathbb{P}(H_{i})
whose boundary is a simple normal crossings divisor. The irreducible components of this divisor are indexed by the proper non-empty flats of
M_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Definition 2.7
.

For each flat
G
of
M_{\mathcal{H}}
, let
U_{G}
be the open locus
U_{G}=\mathbb{P}(L_{G})\setminus\bigcup_{G\subsetneq H\neq E}\mathbb{P}(L_{H})
and define the divisor
D_{G}
to be the closure of the preimage
\pi^{-1}(U_{G})
in
W_{\mathcal{H}}
.

2.3.
Toric varieties and Bergman fans

Let
N
be a lattice of rank
n
and
N_{\mathbb{R}}
be the
n
-dimensional vector space
N_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. A
k
-dimensional, rational, simplicial cone
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a subset that can be written as

\sigma=\mathrm{cone}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}\coloneqq\{\lambda_{1}v_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}v_{k}:\lambda_{i}\geq 0\}\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}

where
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
are
k
linearly independent vectors in
N
. A cone
\tau
is a
face
of
\sigma
if it can be written as
\tau=\mathrm{cone}\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{\ell}}\}
for some subset
\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{\ell}}\}\subset\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
. We declare
\mathrm{cone}(\emptyset)=\{0\}
so that
\{0\}
is a face of every cone. A
rational, simplicial fan

\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a non-empty collection of rational, simplicial cones
\sigma\in\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

•

If
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, then
\tau\in\Sigma
, and

•

Every two cones
\sigma,\tau\in\Sigma
intersect along a common face.

We say that
\Sigma
is
complete
if the union of all of its cones is equal to the whole vector space
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\Sigma(1)
denote the set of rays, i.e. one dimensional cones, of
\Sigma
. For a ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, the
primitive ray generator

\nu_{\rho}\in N
of
\rho
is the generator of the semigroup
N\cap\rho
. We say that
\Sigma
is
unimodular
if, for all cones
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the set of primitive ray generators of
\sigma
can be extended to a basis of
N
.

Let
T
be an
n
-dimensional complex torus, and let
N=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{*},T)
be its lattice of one-parameter subgroups. Denote the lattice of characters
\mathrm{Hom}(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})
of
T
by
N^{\vee}
. A rational fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
gives rise to a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
with torus
T
. There is a rich dictionary between the combinatorics of
\Sigma
and the geometry of
X_{\Sigma}
. We refer the reader to the textbook
[
19
]
for details.

Let
M
be a loopless matroid on ground set
E
and
\mathbb{Z}^{E}
be the lattice with basis
\{e_{i}:i\in E\}
. For a subset
G\subset E
, define
e_{G}=\sum_{i\in G}e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{E}
. Let
N_{E}
be the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{E}/\mathbb{Z}e_{E}
and
\overline{e}_{G}
denote the image of
e_{G}
in
N_{E}
.

Definition 2.9

3.1.
Formal group laws

Formal group laws are an important technical tool in the study of oriented cohomology theories. We explain in
Section

3.2
that every oriented cohomology theory comes equipped with a formal group law which governs how the first Chern class of the tensor product of two line bundles on a smooth complex variety can be expressed in terms of the first Chern classes of the individual line bundles. The axioms defining a formal group law reflect properties of the tensor product operation on line bundles, as we explain in
3.2
. In this subsection, we review the basic properties of formal group laws that we need in this article, closely following the exposition in
[
18
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
18
, Ch. III, §1]
)
Let
R
be a commutative ring. A one-dimensional, commutative
formal group law

(R,F)
is a formal power series
F(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}a_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}\in R\llbracket x,y\rrbracket
, such that for all
w,v,z\in R\llbracket x,y\rrbracket
:

(1)

F(0,v)=v
,

(2)

F(w,v)=F(v,w)
, and

(3)

F(w,F(v,z))=F(F(w,v),z)
.

In this article, all formal group laws are one-dimensional and commutative. For brevity, we will drop the adjectives and refer to them simply as “formal group laws.”

Remark 3.2
.

The three axioms defining a formal group law in
3.1
reflect the fact that, for any three line bundles
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
on a smooth, complex variety
X
:

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{L}
,

(2)

\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{L}
, and

(3)

\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{N})\simeq(\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{N}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
F(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}a_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}\in R\llbracket x,y\rrbracket
be a formal group law. Axiom (
1
) in
3.1
implies that
a_{0,0}=0
and
a_{1,0}=a_{0,1}=1
. Axiom (
2
) implies
a_{i,j}=a_{j,i}
for all
i,j
. The relations among the
a_{i,j}
arising from Axiom (
3
) are complicated.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[
18
, Ch. I, Section 3, Proposition 1]
)
Given a formal group law
(R,F)
, there is a unique formal power series

-_{F}x:=-x-c_{2}x^{2}-c_{3}x^{3}-\dotsb\in R\llbracket x\rrbracket

called the
formal inverse
of
(R,F)
that satisfies
F(x,-_{F}x)=0
.

Example 3.5
.

The
additive formal group law
over
\mathbb{Z}
is
F_{A}(x,y)=x+y
, and
-_{F_{A}}x=-x
.

Example 3.6
.

The
multiplicative-periodic formal group law
over
\mathbb{Z}[\beta,\beta^{-1}]
is
F_{M}(x,y)=x+y-\beta xy
, and
-_{F_{M}}x=\frac{-x}{1-\beta x}

Definition
3.7
below is important for us, as the
Lazard ring
is the algebraic cobordism ring of a point.

Definition 3.7
.

The
Lazard ring

\mathbb{L}^{*}
is the quotient of the free
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra with generators

,

\mathbb{L}^{*}
is graded, with the degree of the generator

being
1-i-j
. The
universal formal group law
is the formal group law
(\mathbb{L}^{*},F_{U})
defined by
F_{U}(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}a_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}\in\mathbb{L}^{*}\llbracket x,y\rrbracket
.

The formal group law
(\mathbb{L}^{*},F_{U})
is universal in the following sense. This universal property follows immediately from the construction of
(\mathbb{L}^{*},F_{U})
.

Proposition 3.8
.

For every formal group law
(R,F)
, there exists a unique ring homomorphism

f:\mathbb{L}^{*}\to R

such that

f_{*}F_{U}\coloneqq\sum_{i,j\geq 0}f(a_{i,j})x^{i}y^{j}=F.

A fundamental result of Lazard gives a particularly simple presentation of
\mathbb{L}^{*}
. This presentation is not at all apparent from our definition of
\mathbb{L}^{*}
.

Theorem 3.9

(
[
28
]
)
.

The Lazard ring is isomorphic to a polynomial ring in infinitely many variables

where the generator

has degree
-i
.

For a formal group law
(R,F)
, elements
g_{1},\dotsc,g_{k}\in R\llbracket x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\rrbracket
, and

, we define

g_{1}+_{F}g_{2}:=F(g_{1},g_{2});\quad\quad a\cdot_{F}g_{1}:=\underbrace{g_{1}+_{F}g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{1}}_{\text{$a$ times}};\quad\quad\left.\sum_{i\in[k]}\right.^{F}g_{i}:=g_{1}+_{F}g_{2}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k}.

We now record two technical lemmas,

Lemma 3.10

Let
(R,F)
be a formal group law. For any

and

, we have

(a+b)\cdot_{F}g=(a\cdot_{F}g)+_{F}(b\cdot_{F}g).

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(R,F)
be a formal group law and suppose
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\in R\llbracket x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\rrbracket
, where each
g_{i}
has constant term
0
. If

, then there are
h_{j}\in R\llbracket x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\rrbracket
, with

and each

having constant term

\left(\left.\sum_{i\in[k]}\right.^{F}g_{i}\right)-\left(\left.\sum_{i\in I}\right.^{F}g_{i}\right)=\sum_{j\in[k]\setminus I}g_{j}h_{j}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
I=[\ell]
for some
\ell<k
. By definition, we have

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{i\in[k]}\right.^{F}g_{i}

\displaystyle=(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{\ell})+_{F}(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})

\displaystyle=(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{\ell})+(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{i,j}(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{\ell})^{i}(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})^{j}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left(\left.\sum_{i\in[k]}\right.^{F}g_{i}\right)-\left(\left.\sum_{i\in I}\right.^{F}g_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{i,j}(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{\ell})^{i}(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})^{j}

\displaystyle=(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})\left(1+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{i,j}(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{\ell})^{i}(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})^{j-1}\right).

If
\ell=k-1
, then we can take
h_{k}:=\left(1+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{i,j}(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{\ell})^{i}(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})^{j-1}\right)
. As each
g_{i}
has constant term
0
, the constant term of
h_{k}
is
1
, concluding the proof of the lemma in this case.

Now assume that
\ell<k-1
. We claim that
(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})=g_{\ell+1}h_{\ell+1}^{\prime}+\dotsb+g_{k}h_{k}^{\prime}
, where each
h_{j}^{\prime}\in R\llbracket x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\rrbracket
has constant term
1
. If this claim is true, then setting

h_{j}:=h_{j}^{\prime}\cdot\left(1+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{i,j}(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{\ell})^{i}(g_{\ell+1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{k})^{j-1}\right)

concludes the proof of the lemma.

We will now prove the claim, using induction on the number of summands
s
that appear in
g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{s}
. For the base case, we note that
g_{1}=g_{1}\cdot h_{1}^{\prime}
, where
h_{1}^{\prime}=1
. Now assume that
g_{1}+_{F}g_{2}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{s-1}=g_{1}h_{1}^{\prime}+g_{2}h_{2}^{\prime}+\dotsb+g_{s-1}h_{s-1}^{\prime}
for some
h_{1}^{\prime},h_{2}^{\prime},\dotsc,h_{s-1}^{\prime}\in R\llbracket x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\rrbracket
, where each
h_{i}^{\prime}
has constant term
1
. We have

\displaystyle(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{s})

\displaystyle=(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{s-1})+_{F}g_{s}

\displaystyle=(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{s-1})+g_{s}+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{i,j}(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{s-1})^{i}g_{s}^{j}

\displaystyle=g_{1}h_{1}^{\prime}+\dotsb+g_{s-1}h_{s-1}^{\prime}+g_{s}\left(1+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{i,j}(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{s-1})^{i}g_{s}^{j-1}\right).

Setting
h_{s}^{\prime}:=\left(1+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{i,j}(g_{1}+_{F}\dotsb+_{F}g_{s-1})^{i}g_{s}^{j-1}\right)
concludes the induction step.
∎

3.3.
Construction of algebraic cobordism

In this subsection, we will describe the construction of
algebraic cobordism
. Our exposition will closely follow
[
24
, Section 2.1]
and
[
29
, Chapter 2.1.2]
.

Let
X\in\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\mathcal{Z}^{*}(X)
be the free abelian group generated by
cobordism cycles
, which are tuples
\alpha=[f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}]
, where
f
is a projective morphism,
Y\in\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and the
L_{i}
are line bundles on
Y
. As in
[
29
, Def. 2.1.6]
, we consider two cobordism cycles
[f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}]
and
[f^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\to X,L_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,L_{r}^{\prime}]
to be equal if there is a triple
\Phi=(\phi\colon Y\to Y^{\prime},\sigma,(\psi_{1},\dotsc,\psi_{r}))
consisting of (i) an isomorphism
\phi\colon Y\xrightarrow{\simeq}Y^{\prime}
of
X
-schemes; (ii) a bijection
\sigma\colon\{1,\dotsc,r\}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\{1,\dotsc,r^{\prime}\}
(so that
r
must equal
r^{\prime}
); and (iii) for each
i=1,\dotsc,r
, an isomorphism
\psi_{i}\colon L_{i}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\phi^{*}(L_{\sigma(i)}^{\prime})
of line bundles on
Y
. The
degree

\mathrm{deg}(\alpha)
of the cobordism cycle
\alpha=[f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}]
is
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(Y)-r
. The group
\mathcal{Z}^{*}(X)
is graded by the codimension of a cobordism cycle, where the
codimension
of
\alpha
is defined to be
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(X)-\mathrm{deg}(\alpha)
. That is,
\mathcal{Z}^{j}(X)
is generated as an abelian group by the cycles
[f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}]
with
j=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(X)-\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(Y)+r
. We will now impose several relations on
\mathcal{Z}^{*}(X)
to define the algebraic cobordism
group

\Omega^{*}(X)
. We will discuss the ring structure on
\Omega^{*}(X)
later in this subsection.

(1)

(
Dimension Axiom
) Let
\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\mathrm{dim}}(X)
be the graded subgroup of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}(X)
generated by the cobordism cycles
[f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}]
with
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(Y)<r
. Define

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{dim}}^{*}(X):=\frac{\mathcal{Z}^{*}(X)}{\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\mathrm{dim}}(X)}.

Given any line bundle
L
on
X
and cobordism cycle
[f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}]
, one defines a
Chern class operator

\widetilde{c_{1}}(L)\colon\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{dim}}^{*}(X)\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{dim}}^{*+1}(X)
by

\widetilde{c_{1}}(L)[f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}]:=[f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r},f^{*}(L)].

(2)

(
Section Axiom
) Let
\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\mathrm{sec}}(X)
be the graded subgroup of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{dim}}^{*}(X)
generated by the cobordism cycles of the form
[Y\to X,L]-[Z\to X]
, where
s\colon Y\to L
is a section of the line bundle
L
transverse to the zero-section, and
Z\hookrightarrow Y
is the closed subvariety of
Y
defined by the zeros of
s
. Define

\underline{\Omega}^{*}(X):=\frac{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{\mathrm{dim}}(X)}{\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\mathrm{sec}}(X)}.

(3)

(
Formal Group Law Axiom
) Let
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{FGL}}^{*}(X)
be the graded
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-submodule of
\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\underline{\Omega}^{*}(X)
generated by elements of the form

F_{U}(\widetilde{c_{1}}(L_{1}),\widetilde{c_{1}}(L_{2}))(\alpha)-\widetilde{c_{1}}(L_{1}\otimes L_{2})(\alpha),

over all
\alpha\in\underline{\Omega}^{*}(X)
, and all line bundles
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
on
X
. The Dimension Axiom (
1
) implies that
\widetilde{c_{1}}(L)^{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(Y)-r+1}([f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}])=0
, when
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(Y)>r
. This means that all but finitely many terms of
F_{U}(\widetilde{c_{1}}(L_{1}),\widetilde{c_{1}}(L_{2}))(\alpha)
are zero.

Definition 3.22
.

The
algebraic cobordism group
of
X
is

\Omega^{*}(X):=\frac{\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\underline{\Omega}^{*}(X)}{\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\mathrm{FGL}}(X)}.

3.23
below gives a simple generating set for
\Omega^{*}(X)
.

Lemma 3.23
.

(
[
29
, Lemma 2.5.11]
)
Let
X\in\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The graded, abelian group
\Omega^{*}(X)
is generated as a group by the classes
[Y\to X]
of projective morphisms
Y\to X
with
Y
smooth, quasi-projective, and irreducible.

If
g\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is a projective morphism in
\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
of relative codimension
d
, then the
pushforward along
g
is the group homomorphism defined by post-composition:

g_{*}\colon\Omega^{*}(X)\to\Omega^{*+d}(X^{\prime}),

g_{*}([f\colon Y\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}]):=[g\circ f\colon Y\to X^{\prime},L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}].

If
g\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is an arbitrary morphism in
\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
, then
g
defines a
pullback map

g^{*}\colon\Omega^{*}(X^{\prime})\to\Omega^{*}(X)
which is difficult to describe in general; see
[
29
, Section 6.5, Section 6.6]
. The main difficulty is that if
[Y\to X^{\prime}]
is a class in
\Omega^{*}(X^{\prime})
, then one would expect
g^{*}([Y\to X^{\prime}])
to be represented by the map
Y\times_{X^{\prime}}X\to X
. However,
Y\times_{X^{\prime}}X
often fails to be smooth and thus the map
Y\times_{X^{\prime}}X\to X
does not define a class in
\Omega^{*}(X)
. However, in special situations, this is exactly how pullbacks act.

Lemma 3.24
.

(
[
29
, Corollary 6.5.5]
)
Let
Z,X\in\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
,
i:Z\to X
be a regular embedding, and
[Y\to X]\in\Omega^{*}(X)
. If
Y\times_{X}Z
is in
\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
, then
i^{*}([Y\to X])=[Y\times_{X}Z\to Z]
.

To construct a cup product for
\Omega^{*}(X)
, we first define an
external product

\times:\Omega^{*}(X)\otimes\Omega^{*}(Y)\to\Omega^{*}(X\times Y)
for
X
and
Y
in
\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The external product is a group homomorphism and is defined as

\displaystyle[f

\displaystyle\colon X^{\prime}\to X,L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}]\times[g\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y,M_{1},\dotsc,M_{s}]

\displaystyle=[f\times g\colon X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}\to X\times Y,p_{X^{\prime}}^{*}(L_{1}),\dotsc,p_{X^{\prime}}^{*}(L_{r}),p_{Y^{\prime}}^{*}(M_{1}),\dotsc,p_{Y^{\prime}}^{*}(M_{s})],

where
p_{X^{\prime}}\colon X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
and
p_{Y^{\prime}}\colon X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
are the projections onto
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
, respectively. Now consider the diagonal embedding
\delta\colon X\to X\times X
in
\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
. For any
X\in\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
, the
cup product
on
\Omega^{*}(X)
is defined by

\cup:=\delta^{*}\circ\times\colon\Omega^{*}(X)\times\Omega^{*}(X)\to\Omega^{*}(X\times_{\mathbb{C}}X)\to\Omega^{*}(X).

As the cup product is defined using
\delta^{*}
, it can often be hard to compute the product of two classes in
\Omega^{*}(X)
. One situation in which the product is easy to compute is the following.

Lemma 3.25
.

Let
D,E,X\in\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and suppose
f\colon D\hookrightarrow X
and
g\colon E\hookrightarrow X
are regular embeddings. Consider the respective cobordism classes
[f\colon D\to X]
and
[g\colon E\to X]
in
\Omega^{*}(X)
. Assume that
f(D)\cap g(E)=\emptyset
. Then
[f\colon D\to X]\cup[g\colon E\to X]=0
in
\Omega^{*}(X)
.

Lemma
3.25
follows from Lemma
3.24
. We close this section by recalling the definition of the first Chern class of a line bundle in algebraic cobordism.

Definition 3.26
.

(
[
29
, Section 5.2]
)
The
first Chern class
in
\Omega^{*}
of a line bundle
L\to X
in
\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
is defined to be

c_{1}(L):=\widetilde{c_{1}}(L)(1_{X}).

Let
D
be a smooth divisor in
X\in\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The Section Axiom (
2
) implies that

c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=[D\to X]\in\Omega^{*}(X).

The first Chern class
c_{1}
satisfies the property that for any line bundle
L\to X
in
\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
, we have
c_{1}(L)=s^{*}s_{*}(1_{X})\in\Omega^{1}(X)
, where
s\colon X\to L
denotes the zero section.

4.1.
Algebraic cobordism rings of toric varieties

In
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
, Krishna and Uma give a presentation for the algebraic cobordism ring of a smooth toric variety. We recall their presentation in
4.1
and compare it to the standard presentation for the Chow ring of a smooth toric variety in
4.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a unimodular fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
with
n
-dimensional lattice
N
and smooth toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
. For each ray
\rho
in
\Sigma
, let
v_{\rho}
be the primitive ray generator of
\rho
, and let
D_{\rho}
be the divisor in
X_{\Sigma}
corresponding to
\rho
. The inclusion
D_{\rho}\hookrightarrow X_{\Sigma}
defines a class
[D_{\rho}\hookrightarrow X_{\Sigma}]
in
\Omega^{*}(X_{\Sigma})
. Consider the formal power series ring over
\mathbb{L}^{*}
with variables indexed by the rays in
\Sigma

T_{\Sigma}:=\mathbb{L}^{*}\llbracket x_{\rho}\text{ $\colon$ }\text{$\rho$ is a ray in $\Sigma$}\rrbracket.

Consider the following ideals in
T_{\Sigma}
:

\displaystyle I_{1}^{\Sigma}:=

\displaystyle\langle x_{\rho_{1}}\cdots x_{\rho_{k}}:\text{ the rays }\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\text{ do not generate a cone of }\Sigma\rangle,

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\Sigma}:=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left.\sum_{\text{$\rho$ ray}}\right.^{F_{U}}\langle\chi,v_{\rho}\rangle\cdot_{F_{U}}x_{\rho}:\chi\in N^{\vee}\right\rangle,\text{ and}

\displaystyle I^{\Sigma}_{\text{nil}}:=

\displaystyle\langle x_{\rho}^{n+1}:\text{$\rho$ is a ray in $\Sigma$}\rangle.

In the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 8.2]
, it is shown that
I_{\text{nil}}^{\Sigma}\subseteq I_{1}^{\Sigma}+I_{2}^{\Sigma}
and that the following isomorphisms hold. We choose to include the ideal
I_{\text{nil}}^{\Sigma}
in our presentation because it will make later arguments more transparent.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
)
There is an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

\frac{T_{\Sigma}}{I_{1}^{\Sigma}+I_{2}^{\Sigma}+I^{\Sigma}_{\text{nil}}}\to\Omega^{*}(X_{\Sigma}),\quad x_{\rho}\mapsto[D_{\rho}\hookrightarrow X_{\Sigma}].

Remark 4.2
.

Using
4.1
and
3.17
, one recovers the standard presentation for

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
as
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})\simeq\Omega^{*}(X_{M})\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}\mathbb{Z}
. Heuristically, simply replace the pair
(\mathbb{L}^{*},F_{U})
with the pair
(\mathbb{Z},F_{A})
everywhere in sight:
T_{\Sigma}
becomes
\mathbb{Z}[x_{\rho}:\rho\text{ a ray of }\Sigma]
,
I_{2}^{\Sigma}
is generated by the ordinary sums
\sum_{\text{$\rho$ ray}}\langle\chi,v_{\rho}\rangle x_{\rho}
, where
\chi\in N^{\vee}
, and
I_{1}^{\Sigma}
remains the same.

Let
\chi_{1},\dotsc,\chi_{n}
be generators for the character lattice
N^{\vee}
. Consider the following ideal in
T_{\Sigma}
:

(I_{2}^{\Sigma})^{\prime}:=\left\langle\left.\sum_{\text{$\rho$ ray}}\right.^{F_{U}}\langle\chi_{i},v_{\rho}\rangle\cdot_{F_{U}}x_{\rho}:i\in[n]\right\rangle.

Observe that
(I_{2}^{\Sigma})^{\prime}
is contained in
I_{2}^{\Sigma}
. We will now prove
4.3
, which implies that only finitely many relations are required in the ring presentation for
\Omega^{*}(X_{\Sigma})
of
4.1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

There is an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

\frac{T_{\Sigma}}{I_{1}^{\Sigma}+(I_{2}^{\Sigma})^{\prime}+I_{\text{nil}}^{\Sigma}}\to\Omega^{*}(X_{\Sigma}),\quad x_{\rho}\mapsto[D_{\rho}\hookrightarrow X_{\Sigma}].

Proof.

In light of Theorem
4.1
and the fact that
(I_{2}^{\Sigma})^{\prime}\subseteq I_{2}^{\Sigma}
, it suffices to prove that the image of
I_{2}^{\Sigma}
in
T_{\Sigma}/(I_{1}^{\Sigma}+I_{\text{nil}}^{\Sigma})
is contained in the image of
(I_{2}^{\Sigma})^{\prime}
in
T_{\Sigma}/(I_{1}^{\Sigma}+I_{\text{nil}}^{\Sigma})
. Let
\chi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{n}\chi_{n}
be an arbitrary character in
N^{\vee}
. It follows from
3.10
and the axioms of a formal group law that

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{\text{$\rho$ ray}}\right.^{F_{U}}\left\langle\chi,v_{\rho}\right\rangle\cdot_{F_{U}}x_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left.\sum_{\text{$\rho$ ray}}\right.^{F_{U}}\left\langle\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}\chi_{i}\right),v_{\rho}\right\rangle\cdot_{F_{U}}x_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left.\sum_{\text{$\rho$ ray}}\right.^{F_{U}}\left.\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\right.^{F_{U}}\left(a_{i}\langle\chi_{i},v_{\rho}\rangle\right)\cdot_{F_{U}}x_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left.\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\right.^{F_{U}}\left(a_{i}\cdot_{F_{U}}\left.\sum_{\text{$\rho$ ray}}\right.^{F_{U}}\langle\chi_{i},v_{\rho}\rangle\cdot_{F_{U}}x_{\rho}\right).

As
x_{\rho}
is nilpotent in
T_{\Sigma}/(I_{1}^{\Sigma}+I_{\text{nil}}^{\Sigma})
, it follows that the last expression is contained in the image of
(I_{2}^{\Sigma})^{\prime}
in
T_{\Sigma}/(I_{1}^{\Sigma}+I_{\text{nil}}^{\Sigma})
.
∎

4.2.
The Feichtner-Yuzvinsky presentation

We give a Feichtner–Yuzvinsky style presentation for the algebraic cobordism ring of the toric variety of the Bergman fan of a loopless matroid in
4.6
.

Let
M
be a loopless matroid of rank
r
on ground set
E
with flats
\mathcal{F}
. Recall the notation
X_{M}:=X_{\Sigma_{M}}
for the toric variety of the Bergman fan
\Sigma_{M}
of
M
. The character lattice of
\Sigma_{M}
is the lattice
N_{E}^{\vee}:=(\mathbb{Z}^{E}/\mathbb{Z}e_{E})^{*}
in
(\mathbb{R}^{E}/\mathbb{R}e_{E})^{*}
, and the set
\mathcal{B}_{M}:=\{e_{i}^{*}-e_{j}^{*}:i,j\in E\}
contains a basis for the lattice
N_{E}^{\vee}
. Consider the formal power series ring

T_{M}:=\mathbb{L}^{*}\llbracket x_{G}:\text{ $G$ a proper and non-empty flat of $M$}\rrbracket

and the following two ideals in
T_{M}
:

I_{1}^{M}:=\langle x_{G}x_{G^{\prime}}:\text{ $G$, $G^{\prime}$ incomparable flats}\rangle,\text{ and}

I_{2}^{M}:=\left\langle\left.\sum_{i_{0}\in G}\right.^{F_{U}}x_{G}+_{F_{U}}\left.\sum_{j_{0}\in G^{\prime}}\right.^{F_{U}}\left(-_{F_{U}}x_{G^{\prime}}\right):i_{0}\in E\text{ and }j_{0}\in E\right\rangle.

Definition 4.4
.

The
algebraic cobordism ring of
M
is

\Omega^{*}(M):=\frac{T_{M}}{I_{1}^{M}+I_{2}^{M}}.

A priori, it is unclear whether
\Omega^{*}(M)
is isomorphic to
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})
.
For the remainder of this section, we verify that indeed
\Omega^{*}(M)\simeq\Omega^{*}(X_{M})
. Consider the ideal
I^{M}_{\text{nil}}:=\langle x_{G}^{r}:\text{$G$ is a proper, non-empty flat of $M$}\rangle
in
T_{M}
. Below, we prove
4.5
, which implies that the generators
x_{G}
are nilpotent in
\Omega^{*}(M)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The ideal
\langle x_{G}:G\text{ a proper, non-empty flat of }M\rangle^{r}

is contained in
I_{1}^{M}+I_{2}^{M}
. In particular, the ideal

I^{M}_{\text{nil}}
is contained in
I_{1}^{M}+I_{2}^{M}
.

Proof.

To prove the claim, we show that if
x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}x_{G_{2}}^{d_{2}}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}
is a monomial with
d_{1}+d_{2}+\ldots+d_{k}\geq r
, then
x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}x_{G_{2}}^{d_{2}}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}=0
in
\Omega^{*}(M)
. By the relations of
I_{1}^{M}
, if
G_{1},\ldots,G_{k}
is not a chain of proper, non-empty flats, then
x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}x_{G_{2}}^{d_{2}}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}=0
. Thus, in particular, our claim holds when
k\geq r
. We will now assume that
G_{1}\subsetneq G_{2}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq G_{k}
is a chain of proper, non-empty flats and prove our claim by induction on
r-k
. The base case where
r-k=0
is covered by our previous observation. For the inductive step, let
r-k\geq 1
and assume that every monomial
x_{G_{1}^{\prime}}^{d_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots x_{G_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}^{d_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}
supported on a chain of flats with
d_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+d_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}\geq r
and
k^{\prime}>k
is equal to zero in
\Omega^{*}(M)
. As
r-k\geq 1
, there is an index
\ell
such that
d_{\ell}\geq 2
.
Fix elements
i_{0}\in G_{\ell+1}\setminus G_{\ell}
and
j_{0}\in G_{\ell}\setminus G_{\ell-1}
where we say
G_{0}=\emptyset
and
G_{k+1}=E
. By the relations of
I_{2}^{M}
, we have that

x_{G_{\ell}}=\left(\left.\sum_{i_{0}\in H}\right.^{F_{U}}x_{H}\right)+_{F_{U}}\left(\left.\sum_{j_{0}\in H^{\prime},H^{\prime}\neq G_{\ell}}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H^{\prime}})\right).

Using this identity and the relations of
I_{1}^{M}
, we get

\displaystyle x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{G_{\ell}}^{d_{\ell}}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}

\displaystyle=x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{G_{\ell}}^{d_{\ell}-1}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}\left(\left(\left.\sum_{i_{0}\in H}\right.^{F_{U}}x_{H}\right)+_{F_{U}}\left(\left.\sum_{j_{0}\in H^{\prime},H^{\prime}\neq G_{\ell}}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H^{\prime}})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{G_{\ell}}^{d_{\ell}-1}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}\left(\left(\left.\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G_{\ell}\cup i_{0}\subseteq H\end{subarray}}\right.^{F_{U}}x_{H}\right)+_{F_{U}}\left(\left.\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G_{\ell-1}\cup j_{0}\subseteq H^{\prime},G_{\ell}\neq H^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H^{\prime}})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{G_{\ell}}^{d_{\ell}-1}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}\left(\left.\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G_{\ell-1}\cup j_{0}\subseteq H^{\prime}\\
G_{\ell}\cup i_{0}\not\subseteq H^{\prime},\text{ }G_{\ell}\neq H^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H^{\prime}})\right)

(2)

\displaystyle=x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{G_{\ell}}^{d_{\ell}-1}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}\left(\left.\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G_{\ell-1}\cup j_{0}\subseteq H^{\prime}\subsetneq G_{\ell+1}\\
i_{0}\not\in H^{\prime},H^{\prime}\neq G_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H^{\prime}})\right).

By Lemma
3.4
, the quantity
-_{F}x_{H^{\prime}}
in the sum of expression (
2
) can be written as
x_{H^{\prime}}f_{H^{\prime}}
where
f_{H^{\prime}}
is an element of
T_{M}
. By Lemma
3.11
,
the large formal sum of expression (
2
) can be written, in a highly non-canonical way, as a finite sum of terms of the form
x_{H^{\prime}}f_{H^{\prime}}g_{H^{\prime}}
where each
g_{H^{\prime}}
is an element of
T_{M}
. This lets us write

x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{G_{\ell}}^{d_{\ell}}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G_{\ell-1}\cup j_{0}\subseteq H^{\prime}\subsetneq G_{\ell+1}\\
i_{0}\not\in H^{\prime},H^{\prime}\neq G_{\ell}\end{subarray}}x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{G_{\ell}}^{d_{\ell}-1}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}x_{H^{\prime}}f_{H^{\prime}}g_{H^{\prime}}.

Each monomial
x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{G_{\ell}}^{d_{\ell}-1}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}x_{H^{\prime}}
is zero in

by our inductive hypothesis. As
x_{G_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{G_{\ell}}^{d_{\ell}}\cdots x_{G_{k}}^{d_{k}}
is a finite sum of elements that are zero in
\Omega^{*}(M)
, our claim now follows.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

There is an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

\varphi\colon\Omega^{*}(M)\to\Omega^{*}(X_{M}),\quad x_{G}\mapsto[D_{\rho_{G}}\hookrightarrow X_{M}]\text{ for all $G\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{E,\emptyset\}$}.

Proof.

By

has the property that

If

, then there are two indices

. After choosing

∎

Remark 4.7

4.3.
The simplicial presentation and its consequences

The goal of this subsection is to prove
4.12
, which is a
simplicial presentation
for
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})
analogous to the simplicial presentations for
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
and

. We also present

.

We recall a basic fact about formal power series rings which we will use repeatedly in this section.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
R
be any commutative ring, and set

\mathcal{S}=R\llbracket x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rrbracket.

If
g\in\mathcal{S}
and the constant term of
g
is a unit in
R
, then
g
is a unit in
\mathcal{S}
.

Define

S_{M}:=\mathbb{Z}[h_{G}:\text{ $G$ a non-empty flat of $M$}]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{M}:=\mathbb{L}^{*}\llbracket h_{G}:\text{ $G$ a non-empty flat of $M$}\rrbracket,

and let

\displaystyle J_{1}^{M}:=\langle(h_{G}-h_{G\vee H})(h_{H}-h_{G\vee H}):G,H\text{ non-empty flats of $M$}\rangle,\quad J_{2}^{M}:=\langle h_{G}:\mathrm{rank}_{M}(G)=1\rangle\subseteq S_{M}

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M}:=\langle(h_{G}-h_{G\vee H})(h_{H}-h_{G\vee H}):G,H\text{ non-empty flats of $M$}\rangle,\quad\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M}:=\langle h_{G}:\mathrm{rank}_{M}(G)=1\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{M}

be ideals of
S_{M}
and
\mathcal{S}_{M}
, respectively.

The ring presentation
\frac{S_{M}}{J_{1}^{M}+J_{2}^{M}}
is equal to the
simplicial presentation
of the Chow ring of
X_{M}
, constructed in
[
4
]
and explicitly described in
[
27
]
. The elements in
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
that correspond to the generators
h_{G}
are called
simplicial generators
of
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
. In
[
27
]
, the authors showed that
K^{0}(X_{M})
can be presented using the
same
simplicial presentation. The elements in
K^{0}(X_{M})
corresponding to the
h_{G}
are called the
simplicial generators
of
K^{0}(X_{M})
. The ring isomorphism that sends a simplicial generator
h_{G}
in
K^{0}(X_{M})
to the corresponding simplicial generator
h_{G}
in
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
is referred to as the
exceptional isomorphism
between
K^{0}(X_{M})
and
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
in
[
27
]
.

Lemma 4.11
.

There is an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})\simeq\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\frac{S_{M}}{J_{1}^{M}+J_{2}^{M}}\to\frac{\mathcal{S}_{M}}{\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M}+\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M}},\quad\quad h_{G}\mapsto h_{G}.

Proof.

The claim follows formally as soon we verify that the generators
h_{G}
are nilpotent in
\frac{\mathcal{S}_{M}}{\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M}+\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M}}
. Indeed, the elements

h_{G}^{r}
for non-empty flats
G
are all contained in
\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M}+\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M}
. This can be shown directly from the definition of
\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M}
and
\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M}
. Alternatively, this also follows from the fact that
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})\simeq\frac{S_{M}}{J_{1}^{M}+J_{2}^{M}}
and
\mathrm{CH}^{k}(X_{M})=0
for
k\geq r
; see
[
26
]
.
∎

We now prove a simplicial presentation for
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})
.

Theorem 4.12
.

There are
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra isomorphisms

\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})\simeq\frac{\mathcal{S}_{M}}{\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M}+\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M}}\xrightarrow{\Phi}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{M}}{\mathcal{I}_{1}^{M}+\mathcal{I}_{2}^{M}}\simeq\Omega^{*}(X_{M})

where
\Phi
is defined by declaring
\Phi(h_{G})=\left.\sum_{H\supseteq G}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H})
.

Proof.

By
4.8
and
4.11
, all we need to show is that
\Phi
is an isomorphism. Let
\mu
be the
Möbius function of the lattice of flats
M
. Consider the two
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra maps:

\phi:\mathcal{S}_{M}\to T_{M},h_{G}\mapsto\left.\sum_{G\subseteq H}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H})\quad\text{and}\quad\psi:T_{M}\to\mathcal{S}_{M},x_{H}\mapsto\left.\sum_{H\subseteq G}\right.^{F_{U}}\mu(H,G)\cdot_{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}h_{G}).

One can verify using Möbius inversion that
\phi
and
\psi
are inverse to each other. That is,
for a non-empty flat
H
of
M
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(\psi(x_{H}))

\displaystyle=\phi\left(\left.\sum_{H\subseteq G}\right.^{F_{U}}\mu(H,G)\cdot_{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}h_{G})\right)

\displaystyle=\left.\sum_{H\subseteq G}\right.^{F_{U}}\left(\mu(H,G)\cdot_{F_{U}}\left(\left.\sum_{G\subseteq K}\right.^{F_{U}}x_{K}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left.\sum_{H\subseteq K}\right.^{F_{U}}\left(\sum_{H\subseteq G\subseteq K}\mu(H,G)\right)\cdot_{F_{U}}x_{K}

\displaystyle=x_{H}

and

\displaystyle\psi(\phi(h_{G}))

\displaystyle=\psi\left(\left.\sum_{G\subseteq H}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H})\right)

\displaystyle=\left.\sum_{G\subseteq H}\right.^{F_{U}}\left(-_{F_{U}}\left(\left.\sum_{H\subseteq K}\right.^{F_{U}}\mu(H,K)\cdot_{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}h_{K})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left.\sum_{G\subseteq H}\right.^{F_{U}}\left(\left.\sum_{H\subseteq K}\right.^{F_{U}}\mu(H,K)\cdot_{F_{U}}h_{K}\right)

\displaystyle=\left.\sum_{G\subseteq K}\right.^{F_{U}}\left(\sum_{G\subseteq H\subseteq K}\mu(H,K)\right)\cdot_{F_{U}}h_{K}

\displaystyle=h_{G}.

Therefore,
\phi
is in fact an isomorphism of
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebras, with inverse
\psi
.
To show that
\Phi
is an isomorphism, it is enough to show that
{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M})=\mathcal{I}_{1}^{M}
and
{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M})=\mathcal{I}_{2}^{M}
.

First, we show that
{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M})=\mathcal{I}_{2}^{M}
. Let
G
be any rank
1
flat of
M
, and choose
i_{0}\in G
. We have that

{\phi}(h_{G})=\left.\sum_{H\supseteq G}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H})=\left.\sum_{i_{0}\in H}\right.^{F_{U}}(-_{F_{U}}x_{H})=-_{F_{U}}\left(\left.\sum_{i_{0}\in H}\right.^{F_{U}}x_{H}\right)\in\mathcal{I}_{2}^{M}

and thus
{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{2}^{M}
. To see that the images of the
{\phi}(h_{G})
generate
\mathcal{I}_{2}^{M}
, first take any
i_{0}\in E
and choose the flat
G
of rank
1
containing
i_{0}
.
3.4
implies

{\phi}(h_{G})=-_{F_{U}}\left(\left.\sum_{i_{0}\in H}\right.^{F_{U}}x_{H}\right)=g_{i_{0}}\cdot\left(\left.\sum_{i_{0}\in H}\right.^{F_{U}}x_{H}\right),

where
g_{i_{0}}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
is an element with constant term
-1
. Now,
4.10
says the
g_{i_{0}}
is a unit in
\mathcal{S}_{M}
, so the images of the
{\phi}(h_{G})
, with
\mathrm{rank}_{M}(G)=1
, generate the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{2}^{M}
. This tells us that
{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{2}^{M})=\mathcal{I}_{2}^{M}
.

Next, we show that
{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M})=\mathcal{I}_{1}^{M}
. Observe that
(h_{G}-h_{G\vee H})(h_{H}-h_{G\vee H})
is non-zero only when
H
and
G
are incomparable. Therefore,
{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M})
is generated by the elements

({\phi}(h_{G})-{\phi}(h_{G\vee H}))({\phi}(h_{H})-{\phi}(h_{G\vee H})),

where
G
and
H
are incomparable flats of
M
. For
G
and
H
incomparable, the set of flats containing
G\vee H
is a strict subset of both the set of flats containing
G
and the set of flats containing
H
. Therefore, we can use
3.4
and
3.11
to write

({\phi}(h_{G})-{\phi}(h_{G\vee H}))({\phi}(h_{H})-{\phi}(h_{G\vee H}))

as

(3)

z_{G,H}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{G\subseteq G^{\prime}\subsetneq G\vee H}x_{G^{\prime}}g_{G^{\prime}}\right)\left(\sum_{H\subseteq H^{\prime}\subsetneq G\vee H}x_{H^{\prime}}g_{H^{\prime}}\right)

where each
g_{G^{\prime}}
and
g_{H^{\prime}}
is a formal power series over
\mathbb{L}^{*}
in the
x_{G}
variables whose constant term is
-1
. Any flats
G^{\prime},H^{\prime}
with
G\subseteq G^{\prime}\subsetneq G\vee H
and
H\subseteq H^{\prime}\subsetneq G\vee H
are forced to be incomparable since
G\vee H
is the smallest flat containing both
G
and
H
. Thus each
z_{G,H}
is contained in
\mathcal{I}_{1}^{M}
, giving us
{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{1}^{M}
.

To show
\mathcal{I}_{1}^{M}\subseteq{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M})
, we will show that the
z_{G,H}
generate
\mathcal{I}_{1}^{M}
. As in the proof of
[
27
, Lemma
A.1
]
, we will prove, by induction on the sum of the coranks of incomparable flats
G
and
H
, that
x_{G}x_{H}
is contained in the ideal
{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M})
. For the base cases, assume that
G
and
H
are incomparable flats whose coranks are each equal to one. In this case,
z_{G,H}=x_{G}x_{H}g_{G}g_{H}
. The polynomials
g_{G}
and
g_{H}
in
\mathcal{T}_{M}
have constant term
-1
, so by
4.10
, they are units in
\mathcal{T}_{M}
. Therefore
x_{G}x_{H}=g_{G}^{-1}g_{H}^{-1}z_{G,H}\in{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M})
.

For general
G
and
H
, each term in
z_{G,H}
besides
x_{G}x_{H}g_{G}g_{H}
is divisible by a monomial
x_{G^{\prime}}x_{H^{\prime}}
where
G^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
are incomparable flats whose sum of coranks is larger than the sum of the coranks of
G
and
H
. Thus, our inductive hypothesis implies that
x_{G}x_{H}g_{G}g_{H}\in{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M})
, and as before we conclude that
x_{G}x_{H}\in{\phi}(\mathcal{J}_{1}^{M})
.
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

The algebraic cobordism ring
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})
is a free
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-module of finite rank.

Proof.

As
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})\simeq\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
, this follows from the fact that
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of finite rank (
[
16
, Corollary 1]
).
∎

We now note some formal consequences of the existence of an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra isomorphism
\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)\simeq\Omega^{*}(X)
for a smooth variety
X
.
Recall
3.20
, which says that a free oriented cohomology theory
\mathrm{h}_{(R,F)}^{*}
gives rise to a formal group law
(R,F)
such that
\mathrm{h}_{(R,F)}^{*}(X)=\Omega^{*}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{L^{*}}}R
. By definition,
\mathrm{h}_{(R,F)}^{*}(\mathrm{pt})=R
.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\mathrm{h}_{(R,F)}^{*}
be a free oriented cohomology theory with formal group law
(R,F)
, and let
X
be a smooth variety with an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra isomorphism
\Theta_{(\mathbb{L}^{*},F_{U})}\colon\Omega^{*}(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
. Then there is an induced
R
-algebra isomorphism

\Theta_{(R,F)}\colon\mathrm{h}^{*}_{(R,F)}(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
and, in particular, a ring isomorphism

\Theta\colon K^{0}(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X).

Proof.

The
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra isomorphism
\Theta_{(\mathbb{L}^{*},F_{U})}
induces an
R
-algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle\Theta_{(\mathbb{L}^{*},F_{U})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{R}\colon\Omega^{*}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}R\xrightarrow{\simeq}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}R,

where the ring homomorphism
\mathbb{L}^{*}\to R
is induced by the formal group law
(R,F)
(see
3.8
).
Noting the identifications of
R
-algebras

\Theta_{(R,F)}^{\prime}\colon\left(\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}R\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}R)\xrightarrow{\simeq}R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X),

we define
\Theta_{(R,F)}
to be
\Theta_{(R,F)}:=\Theta_{(R,F)}^{\prime}\circ(\Theta_{(\mathbb{L}^{*},F_{U})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{R})
, which completes the proof of the first claim.

It follows from the argument above that there is a
\mathbb{Z}[\beta,\beta^{-1}]
-algebra isomorphism

\Theta_{(\mathbb{Z}[\beta,\beta^{-1}],F_{M})}\colon\Omega^{*}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}\mathbb{Z}[\beta,\beta^{-1}]\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{Z}[\beta,\beta^{-1}]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X).

The isomorphism
\Theta
can be obtained from the one above by setting
\beta=1
, noting
3.18
and Remark
3.19
.
∎

By
4.14
, the following proposition holds if
X
is a smooth variety such that
\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)\simeq\Omega^{*}(X)
and the Chow ring of
X
has Poincaré duality.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety whose Chow ring vanishes in degree greater than
d
and is equipped with an isomorphism
\mathrm{deg}:\mathrm{CH}^{d}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
such that for all
i
, the pairing

\mathrm{CH}^{i}(X)\times\mathrm{CH}^{d-i}(X)\to\mathbb{Z},\quad(x,y)\mapsto\mathrm{deg}(xy)

is perfect. Suppose there is a ring isomorphism
\zeta:K^{0}(X)\to\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
and let
\chi:K^{0}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
be any group homomorphism. Then there is an element
f\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
such that

\chi(x)=\mathrm{deg}(\zeta(x)f),\quad\text{for all }x\in K^{0}(X).

Proof.

Consider the group homomorphism
\chi\circ\zeta^{-1}\colon\mathrm{CH}^{i}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
. As
\mathrm{deg}
induces a perfect pairing, there is an element
t_{d-i}\in\mathrm{CH}^{d-i}(X)
satisfying the equality
\chi\circ\zeta^{-1}(x)=\mathrm{deg}(xt_{d-i})
. Taking
f
to be the sum of all such
t_{d-i}
s yields the result.
∎

If
X
is a smooth projective variety, the structure map
\pi:X\to\mathrm{pt}
induces a group homomorphism
\chi\coloneqq\pi_{*}:K^{0}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
known as the Euler characteristic map. If, in addition, there is a ring isomorphism
\zeta:K^{0}(X)\to\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
, then
4.15
tells us that there is an element
f\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
such that
\chi(y)=\mathrm{deg}(\zeta(y)f)
. The relationship between
\zeta
and
f
is analogous to the relationship between the Chern character map and the Todd class in the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch formula. Despite this analogy, we note that
\zeta
is not equal to the Chern character map and
f
is not equal to the Todd class of
X
.

Combining
4.12
,
4.14
, and
4.15
, we obtain the following corollary. This corollary appears, in a slightly stronger form, as Theorem 1.2 of
[
27
]
and is known as the exceptional isomorphism for
W_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Corollary 4.16
.

There is an integral ring isomorphism
\zeta\colon K^{0}(X_{M})\to\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
defined by sending the simplicial generator
h_{G}\in K^{0}(X_{M})
to the corresponding simplicial generator
h_{G}\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
. If

is the matroid of a complex hyperplane arrangement

, then there is an element

such that

\chi(x)=\deg(\tilde{\zeta}(x)f),\quad\text{for all }x\in K^{0}(W_{\mathcal{H}})

where
\chi
is the Euler characteristic map and
\tilde{\zeta}
is the composition of ring isomorphisms

\tilde{\zeta}:K^{0}(W_{\mathcal{H}})\xrightarrow{(\iota^{*})^{-1}}K^{0}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}})\xrightarrow{\zeta}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}})\xrightarrow{\iota^{*}}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}}).

We note to the reader that in Theorem 1.2 of

]
it is proven that, in the situation of the above corollary,

is equal to
1+h_{E}+h_{E}^{2}+\ldots\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})
. This explicit expression for

is useful for calculations and does not follow abstractly from our
4.12
.

The isomorphism
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})\simeq\mathbb{L}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M})
is quite special and does not hold for many varieties, even in the case of smooth, projective toric varieties. The following example, due to Speyer
[

]
, illustrates this phenomenon. Note that, for a smooth variety

, an isomorphism of
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebras

would imply a ring isomorphism
K^{0}(X)\simeq\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
.

Example 4.17
.

[A smooth projective toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
where
K^{0}(X_{\Sigma})\not\simeq\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{\Sigma})
]
Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the simplicial fan whose rays have primitive vectors equal to the columns of the matrix

\bordermatrix{&a&b&d&A&B&D\cr&1&0&0&-1&0&0\cr&0&1&0&-1&-1&0\cr&0&0&1&2&0&-1}

and whose maximal cones correspond to the subsets
S\subseteq\{a,A,b,B,d,D\}
such that
|S|=3
and
S
does not contain any of the sets
\{a,A\}
,
\{b,B\}
,
\{d,D\}
. The fan
\Sigma
is projective, unimodular and combinatorially equivalent to the normal fan of the cube. The associated toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is smooth and projective. Let

R_{\Sigma}=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[x_{a},x_{A},x_{b},x_{B},x_{d},x_{D}]}{\left\langle x_{a}x_{A},x_{b}x_{B},x_{d}x_{D}\right\rangle}

be the Stanley–Reisner ring of
\Sigma
. By
[
19
, Section 5.2]
and
[
8
, Theorem 1]
, we can compute ring presentations of
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{\Sigma})
and
K^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
as quotients of
R_{\Sigma}
. Their respective ring presentations are

\displaystyle\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{\Sigma})\simeq R_{\Sigma}/\left\langle x_{a}-x_{A},x_{b}-x_{B}-x_{A},x_{d}+2x_{A}-x_{D}\right\rangle

\displaystyle K^{0}(X_{\Sigma})\simeq R_{\Sigma}/\left\langle x_{a}-x_{A},(1-x_{b})-(1-x_{A})(1-x_{B}),(1-x_{d})(1-x_{A})^{2}-(1-x_{D})\right\rangle.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
be the field with two elements. We show that the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-algebras
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
K^{0}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
are not isomorphic (as they would be had we tensored with
\mathbb{Q}
) and hence that
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{\Sigma})\not\simeq K^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
.
One can check, for instance using the computer program Macaulay2
[
20
]
, that the sets

(4)

\{1,x_{A},x_{B},x_{D},x_{A}x_{D},x_{B}x_{D},x_{B}^{2},x_{B}^{2}x_{D}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{1,x_{A},x_{b},x_{B},x_{d},x_{D},x_{b}x_{D},x_{B}x_{D}\}

are
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-bases of
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
K^{0}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
, respectively. Over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, squaring is a linear map and thus the spaces of squares in
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
K^{0}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
are spanned by the squares of the bases given in (
4
). We calculate that

\{1,x_{B}^{2}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{1,x_{b}+x_{A}+x_{B},x_{d}+x_{A}+x_{D}\}

are the non-zero squares of these bases. The dimensions of these spaces are different and hence
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
is not isomorphic to
K^{0}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
as an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-algebra. Thus,
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{\Sigma})
and
K^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
are not isomorphic as rings.

5.1.
Liftings to cobordism

In our proof of Theorem

-module generating set for the algebraic and complex cobordism rings of

. We collect two general theorems which let us lift generators from the Chow ring and singular cohomology ring of a space to its algebraic and complex cobordism rings. The first such result appears as Lemma 4.1 in unpublished work of Deshpande
[
13
]

Lemma 5.1
.

(cf.
[
13
, Lemma 4.1]
)
Let
X\in\textbf{Sm}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Suppose that
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}
are homogeneous elements in
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
that generate
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
as an abelian group. If
y_{1},\dotsc,y_{n}
are homogeneous elements in
\Omega^{*}(X)
that map to
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}
under the universal morphism
\Omega^{*}(X)\to\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
, then
y_{1},\dotsc,y_{n}
generate
\Omega^{*}(X)
as an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-module.

Proof.

Recall the isomorphism of rings from
3.17
,

(5)

\Omega^{*}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X),

which is induced by the universal morphism of oriented cohomology theories
\Omega^{*}\to\mathrm{CH}^{*}
of
3.16
. Let
y\in\Omega^{*}(X)
be homogeneous of degree
d
. Consider the image
\overline{y}
of
y
in
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
under the isomorphism (
5
). As
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}
generate
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)
as an abelian group, there are integers
\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\overline{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}.

Therefore,

y-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}y_{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{<0}\cdot\Omega^{*}(X).

In particular, there are homogeneous elements
\ell_{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{<0}
and homogeneous elements
q_{i}\in\Omega^{*}(X)
, such that

(6)

y-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}y_{i}=\ell_{1}q_{1}+\dotsb+\ell_{t}q_{t}

As
y
is homogeneous of degree
d
and each
y_{i}
is homogeneous in
\Omega^{*}(X)
, we may assume that
\lambda_{i}=0
whenever
\mathrm{deg}(y_{i})\neq d
and that
\mathrm{deg}(\ell_{i}q_{i})=d
. As each
\ell_{i}
has degree strictly less than
0
in
\Omega^{*}(X)
, it follows that each
q_{i}
must have degree strictly greater than
d
in
\Omega^{*}(X)
.

Our goal is to express
y
as an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-linear combination of the
y_{i}
s. We will proceed by decreasing induction on
d
, beginning with the base cases of
d>\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
and
d=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
. As
\Omega^{*}(X)
vanishes in degrees larger than
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
, if
d>\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
, then
\Omega^{d}(X)=(0)
. Thus,
y=0
in this case. If
d=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
, then, as
\mathrm{deg}(q_{i})>d
, we must have that each
q_{i}=0
. So Equation (
6
) implies that
y=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}y_{i}
in this case, which completes our base cases.

For our inductive hypothesis, we assume that every homogeneous element in
\Omega^{*}(X)
of degree greater than
d
is expressible as an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-linear combination of the
y_{i}
s. As each
q_{i}
in Equation (
6
) is homogeneous of degree
>d
, each
q_{i}
is expressible as an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-linear combination of the
y_{i}
s. Therefore, Equation (
6
) says that
y
is an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-linear combination of the
y_{i}
s. This completes the inductive step.

As every homogeneous element in
\Omega^{*}(X)
is a
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-linear combination of the
y_{i}
s, and as every element in
\Omega^{*}(X)
is an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-linear combination of homogeneous elements, it follows that every element in
\Omega^{*}(X)
is an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-linear combination of the
y_{i}
s. Thus,
y_{1},\dotsc,y_{n}
generate
\Omega^{*}(X)
as a
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-module.
∎

.

(
[

The following lemma is well known to experts. As we were unable to find a reference, we give a proof. The idea for this proof was communicated to us by Matt Larson.

Lemma 5.4
.

The cycle class map

and

and
\iota^{MU^{*}}
are ring isomorphisms. In the analogous diagram

(8)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathbb{C}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2*}(W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\mathrm{CH}^{*}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{H^{*}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c_{W_{\mathcal{H}}}}

we have that
\iota^{\mathrm{CH}^{*}}
and
c_{W_{\mathcal{H}}}
are always ring isomorphisms but
c_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}
and
\iota^{H^{*}}
can fail to be ring isomorphisms. This is because the singular cohomology of
X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}
can have non-zero even cohomology that is not of Hodge–Tate type. For example, in work of Binder
[
6
]
, it is shown that if
\mathcal{H}
is an arrangement of
4
points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then
\mathrm{Gr}_{6}H^{4}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}})=\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. In particular,
H^{4}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathbb{C}))
is non-zero. However,
W_{\mathcal{H}}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
so the sequence of maps

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})\to H^{2*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathbb{C}))\to H^{2*}(W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C}))

cannot consist of ring isomorphisms.

We note to the reader that
Eq.

8
does not immediately tell us information about
Eq.

7
. As the formal group laws of singular cohomology and Chow theory are additive, one can try to tensor
Eq.

7
with
\mathbb{Z}
via the map
\mathbb{L}^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
induced by the additive formal group law. This successfully recovers three of the terms of
Eq.

8
. However, as
X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathbb{C})
is not compact, we do not know how
MU^{*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathbb{C}))\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}\mathbb{Z}
compares to
H^{*}(X_{M_{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathbb{C}))
. The comparison between the functors
MU^{*}(-)\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}\mathbb{Z}
and
H^{*}(-)
is quite subtle; see
[
35
]
.

In
[
6
]
, Binder extensively studies the singular cohomology rings
H^{*}(X_{M})
of matroids
[
6
]
. It would be interesting to see if one can extend their work to the complex cobordism rings
MU^{*}(X_{M})
of matroids.

5.4.
On the cobordism class of the wonderful variety

As
W_{\mathcal{H}}
is a smooth projective complex variety, it determines a class
[W_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathrm{pt}]\in\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
. In this subsection, we show that this class only depends on the matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
of
H
. We first recall the classical classification of classes in
MU^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
by their Chern numbers
[
30
]
.

Definition 5.12
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of complex dimension
n
. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{r})
be a partition of
n
, i.e.,
\lambda
is a sequence of weakly increasing positive integers such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dotsb+\lambda_{r}=n
. The
\lambda
-th Chern number
of
X
is defined to be the integer

c_{\lambda}(X):=\langle c_{\lambda_{1}}(T_{X})\dotsb c_{\lambda_{r}}(T_{X}),[X]\rangle,

where
T_{X}
is the tangent bundle of
X
,
[X]
is the fundamental class of
X
in the homology group
H_{*}(X)
, and
\langle\text{ $,$ }\rangle
is the Poincaré duality pairing between
H^{*}(X)
and
H_{*}(X)
. The
total Chern class

c(E)
of a rank
r
vector bundle
E
on
X
is defined to be the element
c(E):=\sum_{i=0}^{r}c_{i}(E)\in H^{*}(X)
.

Theorem 5.13
.

(
[
30
, Theorem 6.5]
)
Suppose
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are two compact complex manifolds. The classes
[X_{1}\to\mathrm{pt}]
and
[X_{2}\to\mathrm{pt}]
in
MU^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
are equal if and only if
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
have the same Chern numbers.

Remark 5.14
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of complex dimension
n
. The Chern number
c_{(n)}(X)=\langle c_{n}(T_{X}),[X]\rangle
is equal to the Euler characteristic of
X
. If
X=W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C})
, then, as
W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C})
has no odd cohomology,
c_{(n)}(W_{\mathcal{H}})=\chi(W_{\mathcal{H}})=\dim H^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})
which is an invariant of the matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
. The combinatorial study of this invariant is of recent interest
[
17
]
.

The total Chern class of
T_{W_{\mathcal{H}}}
was recently given an explicit formula which depends only on the matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
in the work of Cheng
[
9
]
. The total Chern class of
T_{W_{\mathcal{H}}}
is closely related to the Chern classes of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{H}}
on the permutahedral variety
[
5
]
and the Poincaré dual of the Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class of the complement
W_{\mathcal{H}}\setminus\mathcal{H}

[
2
]
. We now recall Cheng’s formula using the presentation for
H^{2*}(W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C}))
given by
4.9
and the cycle class isomorphism
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})\simeq H^{2*}(W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C}))
. This presentation agrees with the one given by Feichtner and Yuzvinsky in
[
16
]
.

Definition 5.15
.

For an integer
k\geq 0
, let

S_{k,M_{\mathcal{H}}}:=\sum_{G\text{ a rank $r-k$ flat of $M_{\mathcal{H}}$}}x_{G}\in H^{2}(W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C})).

Note that
S_{0,M_{\mathcal{H}}}=x_{E}
, which is the class denoted by
-\alpha
in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 5.16

(Theorem 3.4 of
[
9
]
)
.

The total Chern class of the tangent bundle of
W_{{\mathcal{H}}}(\mathbb{C})
is equal to

c(T_{W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C})})=\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1+S_{i,M_{\mathcal{H}}})\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\sum_{k=0}^{j}S_{k,M_{\mathcal{H}}}\right).

Theorem 5.17
.

The complex cobordism class
[W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{pt}]\in MU^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
of the wonderful compactification
W_{\mathcal{H}}
of a complex hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}
depends only on the matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
of
\mathcal{H}
. Similarly, the algebraic cobordism class
[W_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathrm{pt}]\in\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
depends only on the matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
of
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

As the ring structure of
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})
and the total Chern class
c(T_{W_{\mathcal{H}}})
depend only on
M_{\mathcal{H}}
, the Chern numbers of
W_{\mathcal{H}}
depend only on
M_{\mathcal{H}}
. This observation, combined with
5.13
, yields the claim for
[W_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{pt}]\in MU^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
. That the claim also holds for
[W_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathrm{pt}]\in\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
follows from the fact that the
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-algebra homomorphism
\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})\to MU^{2*}(\mathrm{pt})
which sends a class
[X\to\mathrm{pt}]\in\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
to
[X(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{pt}]\in MU^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
is an isomorphism
[
29
, Corollary 1.2.11]
.
∎

Remark 5.18
.

It would be interesting to give an explicit formula for the class
[W_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathrm{pt}]\in\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
that depends only on the matroid
M_{\mathcal{H}}
. It would also be interesting to give a complete description of the proper push-forward map
\Omega^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})\to\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
. Upon doing so, this may generalize to yield an artificial “cobordism class”
[X_{M}]\in\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
of a matroid
M
which agrees with the class
[W_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathrm{pt}]\in\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
when
M
is realizable by
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathbb{C}
, and also yield an
\mathbb{L}^{*}
-module homomorphism
\Omega^{*}(X_{M})\to\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
which agrees with the proper push-forward map
\Omega^{*}(W_{\mathcal{H}})\to\Omega^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
when
M
is realizable by
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathbb{C}
. A similar program was carried out for
K
-theory in
[
27
]
. An explicit recursive formula for the algebraic cobordism class
[\overline{\mathcal{M}_{0,n}}\to\mathrm{pt}]
of the moduli space of stable genus zero curves with
n
marked points is given in
[
14
]
in terms of the generators of the Lazard ring
\mathbb{L}^{*}
. The space
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{0,n}}
is the wonderful compactification of a hyperplane arrangement with respect to a non-maximal building set, namely the minimal building set
[
11
, Section 4.3]
. Generalizing the techniques developed in
[
14
]
to give a recursive formula for the algebraic cobordism class of a wonderful variety
[W_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathrm{pt}]
with respect to a maximal building set is work in progress of the first author.
