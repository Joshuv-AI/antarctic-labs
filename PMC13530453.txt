Poster 64. Is Pie-Crusting of the Medial Collateral Ligament Safe in Knee Arthroscopy? A Systematic Review of Visualization, Outcomes, and Complications
Authors: Francesca Docters, Gray W. Ricca, Ryan G. Smolarsky, Brian Tao, John J. Corvi, Nikan K. Namiri, Junho Song, Robert L. Parisien
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Knee arthroscopy is one of the most frequently performed orthopedic surgeries including for the treatment of medial meniscal pathology. Achieving sufficient visualization and working space in the medial compartment can be challenging particularly for varus knees or otherwise tight medial compartments. Pie-crusting of the medial collateral ligament is a described technique for increasing medial joint space in knee arthroscopy. The purpose of this study was to review indications, patient reported outcomes, amount of medial joint space widening, residual valgus laxity, and complications of pie crusting for knee arthroscopy.Methods:A systematic review was conducted according to PRISMA guidelines. PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library were queried from inception to April 19, 2024, using search terms related to 'knee,' 'arthroscopy,' 'medial collateral ligament release,' 'pie-crusting,' and 'outside-in percutaneous release.' Search terms were combined using Boolean operators. After automatic removal of duplicates, titles and abstracts were screened, and full texts were assessed by two independent reviewers. A third reviewer resolved conflicts. Inclusion criteria were randomized controlled trials, cohort studies, case-control studies, and case series reporting on pie-crusting of the medial collateral ligament during knee arthroscopy. Exclusion criteria included case reports, technical notes, review articles, cadaveric or biomechanical studies, and abstracts without full text. Three reviewers independently extracted study design, patient and surgical characteristics, details of the pie-crusting technique, measures of laxity, patient-reported outcome measures, and complications. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus. Methodological quality was assessed using the MINORS criteria. Patient and operative characteristics were tabulated. Meta analysis of improvements was performed on joint space width and Lysholm scores and reflect within-group changes from pre- to post-intervention. Due to the lack of homogeneity amongst procedures, pooling across operations was not analyzed. However, pooling of studies was done within the same procedure groups. Procedure groups included Anterior Cruciate Ligament Reconstruction, Meniscal Repair, Meniscal Root Repair, Meniscectomy, and Meniscal Surgery. Studies with outcomes specific to each of the former four procedures were included in such procedure group. The Meniscal Surgery group included studies who report on surgery for meniscal pathology but do not provide breakdown of outcomes according to specific procedure (repair vs meniscectomy, etc.). Only pie-crusting cohorts were analyzed, as inclusion of the substantially larger number of control group studies (no pie-crusting) would have created a severely imbalanced ratio and reduced the statistical power of the comparison beyond reasonable statistical conclusion. All statistical analyses were done in R Studio Version 2023.09.0+473 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).Results:A total of 16 studies met inclusion criteria, consisting of 775 knees in study groups with pie crusting performed. The weighted mean patient age was 49.0 ± 11.8 years (range, 14-77 years), with a mean body mass index of 26.1 ± 4.4 kg/m2. The weighted mean follow-up duration was 23.2 ± 16.2 months (range, 1.5-50.4 months). One study (128 knees) reported a minimum follow-up of 24 months but did not provide a mean and was not included in the mean follow-up duration calculation. Across studies, 403 patients (52%) were male and 354 (45.7%) were female. With respect to tear morphology, there were 180 root, 71 bucket handle, 240 radial, 8 sagittal, 56 longitudinal, 43 horizontal cleavage, 70 complex, 6 parrot beak, and 11 flap tears. In total, there were 302 meniscectomies, 283 meniscal repairs, and 185 meniscal root repairs. There were 86 concomitant ACL reconstructions, 30 unspecified ligament procedures, and 16 cartilage procedures. In six studies, medial compartment visualization was consistently reported to improve significantly, with intraoperative medial joint space width increasing by a range of 3.2-8.2 mm following pie-crusting. This effect diminished over time, with radiographic joint space width narrowing back toward preoperative values. Across six studies, the difference from baseline was no longer statistically significant by 12 months, with some as early as 6 weeks. In six studies reporting on post-operative valgus stress tests, 10 of 264 patients had residual laxity at final follow-up, which ranged from 3 months to 3.5 years. Across eight studies reporting Lysholm scores, patients demonstrated substantial improvement postoperatively, with pooled mean differences of 26 to 42 points across procedure types. Within each procedure type, Lysholm scores improved significantly. With regards to complications, there were 0 reports of iatrogenic cartilage injury, 5 instances of saphenous nerve injury, 1 infection, 1 retear (2 year lead time), 2 cases of stiffness, and 2 conversions to total knee arthroplasty (lead time not specified). There were 112 patients with transient MCL site pain, with most resolving within 2 weeks.Conclusions:Pie-crusting of the MCL is a safe and effective adjunct to improve visualization of the medial compartment during knee arthroscopy. Intraoperative joint space widening facilitates meniscal surgery and resulting postoperative joint space widening and valgus laxity consistently resolves within one year. Functional outcomes of arthroscopic surgery with pie crusting are favorable despite the potential negative sequelae, and complication rates are low, supporting pie-crusting as a reliable technique without long-term morbidity.
