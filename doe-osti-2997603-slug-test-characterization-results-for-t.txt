 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Slug tests were performed on 21 wells screened within the Upper Three Runs aquifer (UTRA) at
the Chemical, Metals, and Pesticide (CMP) Pits Operable Unit (OU) to determine unit-specific
hydraulic properties. The UTRA is the water table aquifer at CMP Pits and is comprised of three
discrete zones that are separated by two semi-continuous confining zones. These zones include
the transmissive zone (TZ, unconfined), the middle aquifer zone (MAZ, semi-confined), and the
lower aquifer zone (LAZ, semi-confined). The LAZ, the most substantial portion of the UTRA in
the area, extends vertically to the green clay confining zone (GCCZ). A subset of wells from each
zone was selected for slug testing. The objective of the project was to determine hydraulic
properties of the three zones of the UTRA for use in future updates to the CMP pits groundwater
flow and contaminant transport modeling.

Each slug test was conducted by rapidly adding or removing a solid PVC slugging rod of known
volume to each well to displace water in the well casing. The resulting water level response and
recovery was monitored as a function of time. For each well tested, both slug-in (falling head) and
slug-out (rising head) tests were conducted. A data logging pressure transducer was used to collect
water level data. The observed changes in water levels were used to analyze aquifer characteristics.
Data were interpreted using the Bouwer and Rice method (Bouwer and Rice, 1976; Bouwer 1989)
for analyzing slug test results in unconfined aquifers as implemented in the software package
AQTESOLV (Duffield, 2007). This method allowed for the calculation of hydraulic conductivity
(K) for each test well.

A total of 6 wells in the transmissive zone (TZ) and 5 wells in the middle aquifer zone (MAZ)
were tested with all wells producing datasets adequate for analysis. For the LAZ, 10 wells were
tested with 9 wells producing data suitable for analysis. Hydraulic conductivity for the TZ was
found to range from 2.6x10-05 to 3.9x10-03 cm/sec (0.1 to 11.0 ft/day) with n median value of
3.0x10-04 cm/sec (0.9 ft/day). For the MAZ, hydraulic conductivity was found to range from
5.1x10-05 to 1.0x10-03 cm/sec (0.1 to 2.9 ft/day) with a median value of 3.1x10-04 cm/sec (0.9 ft/day).
For the LAZ, hydraulic conductivity was found to range from 2.2x10-05 to 1.8x10-03 cm/sec (0.1 to
5.0 ft/day) with a median value of 6.5x10-05 cm/sec (0.2 ft/day).

1.0 Introduction

Area Closure Projects (ACP) requested the Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) to perform
slug testing on several wells at the Chemical, Metals, and Pesticide (CMP) Pits Operable Unit (OU)
to determine unit-specific hydraulic properties. Testing was conducted between January and May
2025. A slug test is a hydrogeological technique used to estimate the hydraulic conductivity of aquifer
sediments near a test well by quickly adding or removing a solid slug. The displacement of water
caused by insertion or removal of the slug and subsequent water level recovery is monitored as a
function of time. The rate at which the water level returns to static conditions is used to estimate the
hydraulic conductivity of the aquifer sediments.

Data collected from these monitoring and testing initiatives will be used to support groundwater flow
modeling efforts for CMP Pits. This report discusses the test methods, data analysis, and results from
slug testing 21 water table wells at the CMP OU (Table 1).

1.1 CMP Pits Background

The CMP Pits OU is comprised of seven former, unlined, waste disposal units that received non-
radioactive waste from August 1971 to February 1979 and were designated as a regulated waste unit
on the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act/Comprehensive Environmental Response,
Compensation and Liability Act (RCRA/CERCLA) unit list for the SRS Federal Facility Agreement
in 1989 (SRNS-RP-2024-00606). The former burial unit is located approximately 1500 ft south of Pen
Branch and situated on a knoll 310 ft above mean sea level. Groundwater occurs approximately 90
feet below land surface at the CMP Pits location. During active use, the CMP Pits accepted waste that
included organic chemicals, metals, and pesticides which included polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs).
The pits were excavated, preceding the installation of a liner, and filled to grade in 1984 (SRNS-TR-
2017-00312). Presently, contamination remains in soils of the vadose zone below the extent of the
historical excavated area. The general layout of the CMP Pits OU is shown in Figure 1.

1.2 CMP Pits Hydrostratigraphy

The local hydrostratigraphy of the CMP Pits OU has been described previously in various regulatory
documents including the CMP Pits Effectiveness Monitoring Report (SRNS 2023a) and is summarized
as follows. For the CMP Pits OU, the aquifers of interest include the Upper Three Runs aquifer

 1

(UTRA) and the underlying Gordon aquifer (GA). Horizontal flow within the UTRA is divided into
three discrete horizons that are separated by two semi-continuous confining zones, which can be
comprised of sandy clays in areas and therefore are potentially discontinuous and leaky. The horizons
are: 1) the transmissive zone (TZ) – a thin aquifer feature that lies above the top portion of the tan
clay, the tan clay confining zone (TCCZ), 2) the middle aquifer zone (MAZ) – a thin aquifer horizon
between the TCCZ and the lower portion of the tan clay, the tan clay lower clay (TCLC), and 3) the
lower aquifer zone (LAZ) – the most substantial portion of the UTRA in the area, which extends
vertically to the green clay confining zone (GCCZ) with a thickness up to 100 ft (30.48 m). The GCCZ
separates the UTRA from the GA and is comprised of single or multiple layers of dark greenish grey
to black clay to sandy clay. Fine- to medium-grained sands to silty/clayey sands exist in-between the
clay layers. The confining zones are hummocky, vary in thickness, and can be almost non-existent or
leaky in areas. In general, the TCCZ is thinner in the UTRA than the TCLC. A conceptual site model
that depicts the CMP Pits OU hydrostratigraphy is shown in Figure 2. Elevations for each stratigraphic
zone at each test well is provided in Table 2.

2.0 Hydrologic Test Methods and Objectives

The objective of this project was to estimate the hydraulic conductivity of each of the three zones of
the UTRA at the CMP Pits OU. This was accomplished by conducting water displacement slug tests
on several wells located within each zone of interest. A total of 6 wells in the TZ, 5 wells in the MAZ,
and 10 wells in the LAZ were tested. Figure 3 through Figure 5 shows the locations of the test wells
for each aquifer zone. The construction details for all wells tested are provided in Table 1.

2.1 Slug Test Methods

Slug tests were conducted using solid Poly Vinyl Chloride (PVC) slugging rods to displace water in
the well casings. A 3.6 cm (1.4 in) diameter by 149 cm (59 in) long slugging rod with a displacement
of 2.0 L (0.52 gal) was used for all 2-inch wells. The expected initial water column displacement for
this slug in a 2-inch diameter well was 97.5 cm (3.2 ft). A 7.6 cm (3.0 in) by 83 cm (33 in) slugging
rod with a displacement of 3.8 L (1.0 gal) was used for all 4-inch wells. The expected initial water
column displacement for this slug in a 4-inch diameter well was 45.7 cm (1.5 ft).

The slug-in tests were performed by quickly submerging the PVC slugging rods to displace a volume
of water. Insertion of the slug caused the water level in the well to rise quickly. Water then drained

 2

from the well through the screen into the aquifer because of the increased hydraulic head causing the
water level to fall back toward the original static water level (falling-head test). The subsequent return
to equilibrium was then monitored to determine when equilibrium was reached. Once equilibrium
was achieved, the slug was quickly removed causing a rapid drop in water level. In response to the
drop in hydraulic head (due to slug removal), water entered the well from the aquifer sediments rising
to static conditions as the well recharged (rising head). The subsequent return to equilibrium was
monitored completing a slug-out test. Water levels were monitored during each test with a data
logging pressure transducer (In-Situ Rugged Troll 200 non-vented pressure transducer). For each
well, three falling head tests (where the water level was raised above the static water level by inserting
the slug) and three rising head tests (where the water level was lowered by removing the slug) were
performed. This approach was used to verify repeatability and to get a statistically reliable value for
hydraulic conductivity for each well tested.

Rice method include:

The Bouwer and Rice method was implemented using the computer code AQTESOLV (Duffield,
2007). Given the high frequency of data collection for each test, all test data was filtered in
AQTESOLV to reduce the total number of observations and to improve the overall fit quality.
AQTESOLV allows for both automatic and visual curve matching solutions and both techniques were
used to analyze the data. Visual curve matching can sometimes provide better estimates of hydraulic
conductivity because it allows the curve match to fit the specific range of data that meets the
assumptions of the model. AQTESOLV recommends head ranges for visual curve matching. This
feature was used for most cases where visual curve matching provided the best fit to the data.

 4

AQTESOLV also implements corrections for effective casing radius based on solutions provided in
Butler (1998). These corrections improve the estimates for hydraulic conductivity, particularly for
wells screened across the water table (e.g. several TZ wells). For wells screened across the water table,
the correction accounts for the temporary storage of water in the filter pack which can distort early
time data. These corrections were applied in AQTESOLV to each slug test data set.

Input requirements for the Bouwer and Rice method included the radius of the well casing, the radius
of the borehole, length of the screened interval, the initial displacement caused by slug
insertion/removal, the saturated thickness of the aquifer, and the thickness of overlying and underlying
confining layers (if any). Figure 7 provides an illustration of the inputs used in the Bouwer and Rice
method. Stratigraphic elevations used in the analysis were provided by ACP and are given in Table 2.

Due to the short duration of each slug test (< 1 day), corrections for barometric pressure fluctuations
were not necessary. Any effects due to barometric pressure fluctuations were minimal compared to
the head change induced by slug insertion and removal.

3.0 Results

Slug testing was performed on 21 UTRA (water table) wells at the CMP Pits (OU). Wells were located
within three zones of the UTRA including the TZ, MAZ, and LAZ. Hydraulic conductivity of each
zone was determined from the test data using the Bouwer and Rice method (Bouwer and Rice, 1976;
Bouwer, 1989) as described in Section 2.2. Table 4 presents the slug test results for each well, Table
5 presents the solution type used for each test, and Table 6 presents summary statistics. Appendix A
provides graphs of each slug test for each well. The following sections discuss the results for each
aquifer zone.

3.1 Transmissive Zone

A total of six wells screened in the TZ were tested as shown in Figure 3. These wells include
CMP-11D, CMP-14D, CMP-35D, CMP-37D, CMP-62D, and CMP-63D. The shallow, saturated
sediments of the TZ comprise the uppermost portion of the UTRA, which is considered an unconfined
aquifer for analysis purposes. Wells CMP-11D, CMP-14D, and CMP35-D are screened across the
water table. Results from all three of these wells indicated filter pack drainage in the early time data
(Figure 8). For these wells, a correction factor for effective casing radius was applied (Butler, 1998).

 5

The early time data where filter pack drainage occurred were excluded from the analysis. Bouwer and
Rice curve fitting was applied to the straight-line portion of the data sets using the AQTESOLV
recommended head range to fit the data (Figure 8).

Hydraulic conductivity for the TZ was found to range from 2.6x10-05 to 3.9x10-03 cm/sec (0.1 to 11.0
ft/day) with n median value of 3.0x10-04 cm/sec (0.9 ft/day). Hydraulic conductivities for wells CMP-
11D, CMP-14D, CMP-35D, and CMP-37D were roughly an order of magnitude higher than those for
wells CMP-62D and CMP63D.

3.2 Middle Aquifer Zone

A total of five wells screened in the MAZ were tested as shown in Figure 4. These wells include
CMP-8, CMP-14CR, CMP-48D, CMP-56D, and CMP-62C. The MAZ is a thin layer of the UTRA
bounded on the top and bottom by discontinuous, semi-confining clay layers (TCCZ and TCLC). For
analysis purposes, the MAZ was considered unconfined, but the clay layers were included in the
AQTESOLV model. The hydraulic conductivity of the MAZ sediments ranged from 5.1x10-05 to
1.0x10-03 cm/sec (0.1 to 2.9 ft/day) with a median value of 3.1x10-04 cm/sec (0.9 ft/day).

3.3 Lower Aquifer Zone

A total of ten wells screened in the LAZ were tested as shown in Figure 5. These wells include
CMP-10C, CMP-14B, CMP-50B, CMP-52BU, CMP-54C, CMP-56B, CMP061B, CMP062B,
CMP064BU, CMP066B. The LAZ is the deeper portion of the UTRA and is bounded on the top by
the TCLC and on the bottom by the GCCZ. The LAZ is the most extensive portion of the UTRA. As
with the MAZ wells, the LAZ was considered an unconfined aquifer, but the clay layers were included
in the AQTESOLV model.

The hydraulic conductivity of the LAZ sediments ranged from 2.2x10-05 to 1.8x10-03 cm/sec (0.1 to
5.0 ft/day) with a median value of 6.5x10-05 cm/sec (0.2 ft/day). Hydraulic conductivity values for the
LAZ portion of the UTRA were generally lower than the TZ and MAZ. Exceptions to this
generalization are noted for CMP-14B and CMP066B. For well CMP066B, it took less than 5 minutes
from insertion/removal of the slug rod for the water levels to return to static conditions. This indicates
that either the sediments around CMP066B are very permeable or that the testing only influenced the
filter pack. Additional slug testing is recommended to verify the high conductivity values.

4.0 Conclusions

Slug tests were performed on 21 wells screened within the Upper Three Runs aquifer (UTRA) at the
CMP Pits Operable Unit (OU) to determine unit-specific hydraulic properties. The UTRA is the water
table aquifer at CMP Pits and is comprised of three discrete zones that are separated by two semi-
continuous confining zones. These zones include the transmissive zone (TZ, unconfined), the middle
aquifer zone (MAZ, semi-confined), and the lower aquifer zone (LAZ, semi-confined). The LAZ, the
most substantial portion of the UTRA in the area, extends to the green clay confining zone (GCCZ).
A subset of wells from each zone was selected for slug testing. The objective of the project was to
determine hydraulic properties of the three zones of the UTRA for use in future updates to the CMP
pits groundwater flow and contaminant transport modeling.

Each slug test was conducted by rapidly adding or removing a solid PVC slugging rod of known
volume to each well to displace water in the well casing. The resulting water level response and
recovery was monitored as a function of time. For each well tested, both slug-in (falling head) and
slug-out (rising head) tests were conducted. A data logging pressure transducer was used to collect
water level data. The observed changes in water levels were used to analyze aquifer characteristics.
Data were interpreted using the Bouwer and Rice method (Bouwer and Rice, 1976; Bouwer 1989) for
analyzing slug test results in unconfined aquifers as implemented in the software package
AQTESOLV (Duffield, 2007). This method allowed for the calculation of hydraulic conductivity (K)
for each test well.

A total of 6 wells in the transmissive zone (TZ) and 5 wells in the middle aquifer zone (MAZ) were
tested with all wells producing datasets adequate for analysis. For the LAZ, 10 wells were tested with
9 wells producing data suitable for analysis. Hydraulic conductivity for the TZ was found to range
from 2.6x10-05 to 3.9x10-03 cm/sec (0.1 to 11.0 ft/day) with n median value of 3.0x10-04 cm/sec
(0.9 ft/day). For the MAZ, hydraulic conductivity was found to range from 5.1x10-05 to 1.0x10-
03
 cm/sec (0.1 to 2.9 ft/day) with a median value of 3.1x10-04 cm/sec (0.9 ft/day). For the LAZ,
hydraulic conductivity was found to range from 2.2x10-05 to 1.8x10-03 cm/sec (0.1 to 5.0 ft/day) with
a median value of 6.5x10-05 cm/sec (0.2 ft/day).
