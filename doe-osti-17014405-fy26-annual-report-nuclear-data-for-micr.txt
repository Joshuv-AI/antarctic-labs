Execu ve Summary
The goal of this work is to re-determine key gamma-ray emission probabili es for U, Pu, and Am in the
50 - 208 keV energy range for microcalorimeter gamma spectroscopy to reach uncertainty limits in analysis
of safeguards samples at the IAEA NML. We will design and conduct experimental campaigns using
complementary detec on methods and advanced sample prepara on to measure this data. In this second
year of the project, work is focused on conduc ng the Pu data measurement campaign and evalua ng
new Pu/Am data that result, as well as finalizing results from the U data campaign.

In FY26, there are three tasks on the PWP. The first task is to conduct the Pu data campaign; this task will
be carried out at LANL, PNNL, and INL. The second task will be evalua on of new Pu/Am data, which will
be a collabora on between LANL and LBNL. The final task for FY26 is to finalize the U measurement results.
All labs will contribute to task 3.

Sensi vity of plutonium isotopic ra o determina ons to gamma-ray branching ra o nuclear
data (LANL)
This study evaluated whether improved gamma-ray branching ra o nuclear data could improve plutonium
isotopic ra o determina ons in microcalorimeter data. The analysis used fourteen plutonium
measurements collected in 2019 and 2026, spanning mul ple standard types, including PIDIE, CRM, and
CBNM materials.

The work focused on two related ques ons:

 Does reducing branching ra o uncertainty improve isotopic ra o precision or reduce bias?

 Does shi ing branching ra o values within their quoted uncertainty aﬀect isotopic ra o bias?

Overall, reducing the uncertainty of any single branching ra o generally has li le eﬀect on the calculated
isotopic ra os. In contrast, reducing uncertain es for all relevant gamma-ray peaks together produces a
clear improvement in precision. This indicates that the final uncertainty is driven by the combined eﬀect
of mul ple nuclear data inputs rather than by one dominant branching ra o.

The most notable single-line sensi vity was associated with the ²⁴¹Am gamma lines, par cularly the
125.3 keV line, and to a lesser extent the 98.97 keV line. These lines produced the largest observed
changes in selected ra o results when either their uncertain es or branching ra o values were modified.

Background
Plutonium isotopic composi on is determined from gamma-ray spectra by rela ng measured peak areas
to isotope ac vi es through the expression:

Peak Area = Isotope Ac vity × Gamma Branching Ra o × Detector Eﬃciency at Peak.

In prac ce, peak areas are divided by the corresponding branching ra os, allowing the analysis to
simultaneously fit isotope ac vi es and the detector eﬃciency curve as a func on of energy.

This approach is powerful, but several complica ons aﬀect the final ra o determina on:

 The detector eﬃciency curve shape can be weakly constrained in some energy regions.

 Some gamma-ray peaks contain contribu ons from more than one radionuclide, such as
overlapping or coupled contribu ons from ²⁴¹Am and ²⁴¹Pu.

 Branching ra o uncertain es propagate directly into both the eﬃciency fit and the isotope ac vity
determina on.

Because of this, improved nuclear data may reduce uncertainty or bias in plutonium isotopic ra os, but
the impact depends on how strongly each gamma line constrains the fit.

Study Approach
Fourteen plutonium measurements were selected from 2019 and 2026 datasets. The set included
mul ple standard types to test whether the observed sensi vi es were consistent across materials and
measurement condi ons. Two sensi vity methods were applied.

Method 1: Varying Branching Ra o Uncertain es
For each gamma peak used in the eﬃciency curve and isotopic ra o calcula on, the branching ra o
uncertainty was modified while the nominal value was held fixed. The cases were the original uncertainty,
σ_BR, and reduced uncertain es, 0.5 σ_BR and 0.1 σ_BR.

This method tested whether reducing branching-ra o uncertainty improved either mass-ra o bias,
evaluated rela ve to the cer fied mass ra o, or mass-ra o precision, evaluated using the SAPPY
minimiza on rou nes with both sta s cal and systema c uncertainty contribu ons included. Individual
peaks were varied one at a me. In addi on, cases were examined where the branching ra o uncertain es
for all peaks were reduced together.

Method 2: Varying Branching Ra o Values
For each gamma peak, the branching ra o value itself was shi ed within its quoted uncertainty by
repea ng analysis using BR, BR + σ_BR, and BR 〖- σ〗_BR for each peak. This method tested whether
plausible changes in the evaluated branching ra o values could introduce meaningful changes in isotopic
ra o bias or precision.
Bias Results Rela ve to Cer fied Values
Bias was calculated as the diﬀerence between each calculated mass ra o and the cer fied value for the
corresponding standard.

Eﬀect of Reducing Branching Ra o Uncertainty
Changing the uncertainty of an individual branching ra o generally did not produce a large change in mass-
ra o bias within a given sample. This behavior is expected: reducing the uncertainty on a single nuclear
data input aﬀects the propagated uncertainty, but it does not directly move the central value of the
branching ra o.

For the ²³⁸Pu/²³⁹Pu ra o, the bias was largely stable when individual branching ra o uncertain es were
reduced. The only small but visible changes occurred when modifying the uncertainty on the 98.97 keV
and 125.3 keV ²⁴¹Am lines. These lines appear to be among the more influen al nuclear data inputs in the
current analysis configura on.

Eﬀect of Shi ing Branching Ra o Values
When branching ra o values were shi ed by ±1σ, the resul ng bias changes were generally small. This
suggests that, for most gamma lines, the isotopic ra o results are not highly sensi ve to branching ra o
value changes within the currently quoted uncertain es.

The largest observed spread in ra o bias occurred when varying the ²⁴¹Am 125.3 keV branching ra o from
BR+σ to BR-σ. This was especially no ceable for ra os involving ²⁴¹Pu or ²⁴¹Am, where the peak contributes
more directly to the analysis.

Precision Results
Precision was determined from the SAPPY minimiza on rou nes, including both sta s cal and systema c
sources of uncertainty.

Eﬀect of Reducing Branching Ra o Uncertainty
For individual gamma peaks, reducing a single branching ra o uncertainty from σ_BR to 0.5σ_BR or
0.1σ_BR generally did not appreciably change the final ra o precision. This was observed across all mass
ra os. The main excep on was a small change in selected measurements when the ²⁴¹Am 125.3 keV
branching ra o uncertainty was modified.

Although individual peak changes had limited impact, reducing the branching ra o uncertain es for all
peaks together improved precision for all measurements. This is an important result: it shows that
improved nuclear data can benefit the analysis, but the improvement is realized through a coordinated
reduc on in uncertainty across the set of relevant gamma lines.

Eﬀect of Shi ing Branching Ra o Values
As expected, changing the branching ra o value itself did not significantly aﬀect the calculated precision.
Precision is driven by uncertainty propaga on, coun ng sta s cs, and systema c uncertainty terms;
therefore, shi ing the central value of a branching ra o changes the calculated ac vity or bias more than
the uncertainty assigned to the ra o. This behavior was consistent for all mass ra os.

Key Observa ons
The principal findings from the sensi vity analysis are:

 Single-line improvements have limited eﬀect, but aggregate nuclear data improvement ma ers.

Reducing individual branching-ra o uncertain es produced limited changes, while reducing all relevant
uncertain es together improved mass-ra o precision across the measurement set, as summarized in Table
1.

 The ²⁴¹Am lines should be considered high-priority candidates for improved nuclear data.

The 125.3 keV line showed the strongest eﬀect on both mass-ra o precision and bias, with the 98.97 keV
line also producing small observable changes in some cases (see Table A1 in Appendix A).

Detailed sensi vity results for the individual branching-ra o perturba ons are provided in Appendix A.
The precision entries report the average absolute improvement in mass-ra o precision obtained when
each individual branching-ra o uncertainty is reduced. These values are percentage-point changes in the
quoted rela ve uncertainty, not percent changes rela ve to the original uncertainty. The bias-change
entries report the average absolute change in mass-ra o bias associated with varying each branching ra o
between BR-σ_BR and BR+σ_BR. Because the direc on of any future branching-ra o revision is not known,
these bias-change values should be interpreted as the magnitude of a possible bias change, not as a signed
correc on.

The aggregate precision improvement from reducing all relevant branching-ra o uncertain es is
summarized in Table 4. Across the evaluated mass ra os, the rela ve uncertainty decreases by
approximately 0.3 to 1.4 percentage points, reported as an absolute change in the quoted percent
precision. These reduc ons indicate that coordinated improvements to the branching-ra o data can
measurably reduce the final mass-ra o uncertainty, even though changing the uncertainty of any single
branching ra o generally has li le eﬀect.

 Conclusion of sensi vity study
 The sensi vity study shows that plutonium isotopic ra o determina ons are not strongly controlled by
 any single branching ra o. Instead, nuclear data eﬀects enter through the combined influence of
 mul ple gamma lines used in the eﬃciency and ac vity fits. Reducing all branching-ra o uncertain es
 together improves precision across the evaluated measurements, while reducing individual uncertain es
 has limited impact. The 241Am 125.3 keV line is the most important individual line iden fied, with the
 241Am 98.97 keV line also showing some sensi vity. Future nuclear data improvement eﬀorts should
 priori ze these lines while also considering coordinated improvements across the full set of gamma rays
 used in the analysis.
