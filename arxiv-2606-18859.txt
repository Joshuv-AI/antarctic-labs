1.
Introduction

Proving unconditionally that a given integer is prime is a central task of computational number
theory. For generic large primes, the most practical route is the Pocklington–Lehmer
criterion
[
5
,
4
]
: exhibit a fully factored divisor
F>\sqrt{p}
of
p-1
and
verify witnesses. The key difficulty is finding such an
F
.

The subfamily

p=3m(m+1)+1,\qquad m=2^{a}3^{b}-1,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{*},

is constructed so that
m+1=2^{a}3^{b}
is
3
-smooth by design, making

F=2^{a}3^{b+1}
fully factored and satisfying
F>\sqrt{p}
for every
(a,b)
without any
condition on
p
. Equivalently,
p=3m^{2}+3m+1=(m+1)^{3}-m^{3}
is a centred hexagonal number, and the
family is the locus where its
N-1
certificate is unconditional and minimal.

Contributions

This paper makes two kinds of contribution.

(A) Deterministic Pocklington witnesses (rigorous, new).
In every certificate produced by
the
N-1
method one must exhibit, for each prime
q\mid F
, a witness
w_{q}
with

w_{q}^{(p-1)/q}\not\equiv 1
. Here
q\in\{2,3\}
. Earlier computations used the fixed witnesses

w_{2}=5,\ w_{3}=7
and observed that they always worked; whether this holds for the whole subfamily
was left open. We settle it:
w_{2}=5
and
w_{3}=7
are
not
universal, but their validity is
governed by exact congruences in
(a,b)
:

•

Theorem
4.1
:
w_{2}=5
is valid
\iff a-b\equiv 1,2\pmod{4}
(quadratic reciprocity);

•

Theorem
4.5
:
w_{3}=7
is valid
\iff m\not\equiv 2\pmod{7}
(cubic reciprocity).

These give a deterministic witness-selection rule (Corollary
4.9
), making the search
provably correct rather than reliant on the luck of fixed witnesses.

(B) Arithmetic filters and a computational demonstration.
Three elementary congruence
theorems (§
3
) remove
\approx 87\%
of candidate pairs
(a,b)
before any
large-number arithmetic. A multi-core implementation (§
5
–§
6
)
produced four unconditional certificates, the largest of
29\,998
digits, on a consumer laptop.

2.
The Pocklington subfamily

Definition 2.1
.

For integers
a,b\geq 1
, set
m(a,b)=2^{a}3^{b}-1
and

p(a,b)=3m(m+1)+1=3m^{2}+3m+1
. Its decimal digit count is

D(a,b)=\lfloor 2a\log_{10}2+(2b+1)\log_{10}3\rfloor+1
.

Lemma 2.2

(Explicit factorisation)
.

For
p
as in Definition
2.1
,

p-1=2^{a}3^{b+1}\,m,\qquad F:=2^{a}3^{b+1}>\sqrt{p}.

Proof.

Since
m+1=2^{a}3^{b}
,
p-1=3m(m+1)=3m\cdot 2^{a}3^{b}=2^{a}3^{b+1}m
. As

p=3m^{2}+3m+1<3(m+1)^{2}
, we get
\sqrt{p}<\sqrt{3}\,(m+1)<3(m+1)=2^{a}3^{b+1}=F
.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(Pocklington–Lehmer certificate
[
5
,
4
]
)
.

Let
p=p(a,b)
. Then
p
is prime iff there exist
w_{2},w_{3}
such that, for
q\in\{2,3\}
,

w_{q}^{\,p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p},\qquad\gcd\!\bigl(w_{q}^{(p-1)/q}-1,\,p\bigr)=1.

Corollary 2.4
.

By Lemma
2.2
,
F>\sqrt{p}
holds unconditionally and the only primes dividing
F
are

2,3
; hence the certificate needs at most the two witnesses
w_{2},w_{3}
.

For
p
prime,
w_{q}^{p-1}\equiv 1
is automatic (Fermat), and the second condition is equivalent
to
w_{q}^{(p-1)/q}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. Since (Theorem
3.1
)
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
, both

(p-1)/2
and
(p-1)/3
are integers, and

(1)

w_{2}\text{ valid}\iff w_{2}\text{ is a quadratic non-residue mod }p,\qquad w_{3}\text{ valid}\iff w_{3}\text{ is a cubic non-residue mod }p.

Three arithmetic filters

Theorem 3.1

(mod
6
)
.

For all
a,b\geq 1
,
p(a,b)\equiv 1\pmod{6}
.

Proof.

m=2^{a}3^{b}-1
is odd, so
m(m+1)
is even and
p=3m(m+1)+1
is odd; and
3\mid 3m(m+1)
gives

p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. By CRT,
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(
(-3)
-sieve; quadratic reciprocity)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
p=3m(m+1)+1
, and
q\geq 5

q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. Then
q\nmid p
.

Proof.

q\mid p
is equivalent to

(6m+3)^{2}\equiv-3\pmod{q}

\left(\frac{-3}{q}\right)=+1

. For
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}

q\nmid p
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

In trial division up to

need be tested, i.e. half of the

by Dirichlet.

4.
Deterministic Pocklington witnesses

This section is the theoretical core. By (
1
), the validity of a fixed witness is a
residuosity question. We resolve both witnesses exactly.

4.1.
The witness
w_{2}=5

Theorem 4.1

(
w_{2}=5
; rigorous)
.

For
p=p(a,b)
prime,
w_{2}=5
is a valid Pocklington witness if and only if

m\equiv 1\text{ or }3\pmod{5}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad a-b\equiv 1\text{ or }2\pmod{4}.

Proof.

By (
1
),
w_{2}=5
is valid iff
\left(\frac{5}{p}\right)=-1
. As
5\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, quadratic reciprocity
gives
\left(\frac{5}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{p}{5}\right)
, and
\left(\frac{p}{5}\right)=-1
iff
p\equiv 2,3\pmod{5}
. Modulo
5
,

p=3m(m+1)+1
takes the values
1,2,4,2,1
for
m\equiv 0,1,2,3,4
; hence
p\equiv 2,3\pmod{5}

(the value
3
never occurs) iff
m\equiv 1,3\pmod{5}
. Finally
m+1=2^{a}3^{b}
and

3\equiv 2^{3}\pmod{5}
give
m+1\equiv 2^{\,a+3b}\pmod{5}
with
\mathrm{ord}_{5}(2)=4
, so

m\equiv 1\iff a+3b\equiv 1
and
m\equiv 3\iff a+3b\equiv 2\pmod{4}
. Since
a+3b\equiv a-b\pmod{4}
,
the claim follows.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

For
a-b\equiv 0,3\pmod{4}
,
5
is a quadratic residue and
w_{2}=5

fails
; the least
quadratic non-residue is then a valid witness.

4.2.
An explicit Eisenstein prime

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
. Then in
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
,

p=(m+1)^{3}-m^{3}=\pi\,\overline{\pi},\qquad\pi:=(1+m)-m\omega,\qquad N(\pi)=p,

so
\pi
is a prime of
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
above
p
. Consequently
7
is a cubic residue mod
p
iff

\left(\frac{7}{\pi}\right)_{\!3}=1
, where
\left(\frac{\cdot}{\pi}\right)_{\!3}
is the cubic residue symbol.

Proof.

3m^{2}+3m+1=(m+1)^{3}-m^{3}
(centred hexagonal identity). With

X^{3}-Y^{3}=(X-Y)(X-\omega Y)(X-\omega^{2}Y)
,
X=m+1
,
Y=m
:

p=\bigl((1+m)-\omega m\bigr)\bigl((1+m)-\omega^{2}m\bigr)
. A direct computation gives

N(\pi)=(1+m)^{2}-(1+m)(-m)+m^{2}=1+3m+3m^{2}=p
; since
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
is prime it splits into two
conjugate primes of norm
p
, and
\pi
is one of them.
∎

4.3.
The witness
w_{3}=7

The proof rests on the following proposition, which holds for the broader family

p=3m^{2}+3m+1
subject only to
m\equiv 2\pmod{3}

Proposition 4.4

(Cubic character of
7
)
.

Let
p=3m^{2}+3m+1
be prime,
p\neq 7
, with

Proof.

Factor

with
\lambda=3+\omega
,
\overline{\lambda}=2-\omega

(

\lambda^{*}=2+3\omega=-\omega^{2}\lambda

\lambda=-\omega\,\lambda^{*}

Since

\pi^{*}=-\omega^{2}\pi=(1+2m)+(1+m)\omega\qquad(1+2m\equiv 2,\ 1+m\equiv 0\bmod 3).

\left(\frac{7}{\pi}\right)_{\!3}=\left(\frac{7}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}

\left(\frac{\lambda^{*}}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}=\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{\lambda^{*}}\right)_{\!3}

\overline{\lambda}^{*}

\left(\frac{7}{\pi}\right)_{\!3}=\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{\lambda^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\cdot\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{\overline{\lambda}^{*}}\right)_{\!3}.

We evaluate in

valid for all primes of the subfamily; its
validity is the conjunction
a-b\equiv 1,2\pmod{4}
and
m\not\equiv 2\pmod{7}
. A correct witness
choice is determined by the residue class of
(a,b)
:

w_{2}=\begin{cases}5,&a-b\equiv 1,2\ (4),\\
\text{least quadratic non-residue},&\text{else;}\end{cases}\qquad w_{3}=\begin{cases}7,&m\not\equiv 2\ (7),\\
\text{least cubic non-residue},&m\equiv 2\ (7).\end{cases}

In the case
m\equiv 2\pmod{7}
a cubic non-residue is found deterministically by testing small
primes: cubic non-residues form two-thirds of
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}
, so one is reached after a bounded
search. Hence the certificate search is provably correct, rather than reliant on fixed witnesses
succeeding by chance.

5.
The PrimeQuest algorithm

For target digit count
D
the search centre is
a_{0}=b_{0}=\lfloor D/(2\log_{10}6)\rfloor\approx 0.643\,D
, and the zigzag expands outward, enumerating for each
a
all
b
with

|D(a,b)-D|\leq\tau
(
\tau=10
). Each pair passes through a four-stage pipeline:

(1)

mod-
7
filter
(Theorem
3.4
): reject if

(a\bmod 3,b\bmod 6)\in\mathcal{F}_{7}
. Cost
O(1)
.

(2)

Sieve
(Corollary
3.3
): for primes
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
,
q\leq L
, reject
if
p\equiv 0
.

(3)

Miller–Rabin
:
k
rounds on
p(a,b)
.

(4)

Pocklington
: select witnesses by Corollary
4.9
and verify
Theorem
2.3
.

Pairs are distributed over CPU cores; checkpoint-resume every
60
s allows exact restart. We
note that stage 4 is now deterministic: by Corollary
4.9
the witnesses are read off

(a,b)
rather than searched.

Remark 5.1

(MR round reduction)
.

Miller–Rabin is deterministic for primes (every prime passes every round); reducing the initial
round count carries zero risk of discarding a true prime, and rare composites are removed by
confirmation rounds and by Pocklington.

7.
Performance and scaling

The dominant cost is Miller–Rabin: one round on a
D
-digit modulus costs

O(D^{2}\log D\log\log D)
bit operations via FFT multiplication
[
3
]
. Empirically, wall time
per MR event scales as
\tau\propto D^{2.5}
over the tested range, consistent with the FFT
complexity; with only two distinct

[2.3,2.7]
. Extrapolating

to

projects
6

10

hardware; this is an extrapolation, not a measurement.

9.
Conclusion

For the
3
-smooth subfamily
p=3m(m+1)+1
,
m=2^{a}3^{b}-1
, the Pocklington–Lehmer certificate
is unconditional and minimal, and — the main new point — the two required witnesses are

deterministically characterised
:
w_{2}=5
is valid iff
a-b\equiv 1,2\pmod{4}

(Theorem
4.1
), and
w_{3}=7
iff
m\not\equiv 2\pmod{7}
(Theorem
4.5
), the latter
via cubic reciprocity through the explicit Eisenstein factorisation

p=(m+1)^{3}-m^{3}
. Together with three elementary filters removing
\approx 87\%
of candidates,
this yields a provably correct, low-overhead certification pipeline, demonstrated by certificates
up to
29\,998
digits on commodity hardware. The contribution is structural and explicit rather
than record-breaking: the value lies in identifying a family where both the certificate and its
witnesses become exact arithmetic statements.
