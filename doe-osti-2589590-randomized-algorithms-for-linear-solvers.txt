LDRD PROJECT TITLE: Randomized Algorithms for Linear Solvers
PROJECT TEAM MEMBERS:

ABSTRACT:
Recently, randomized algorithms in numerical linear algebra, specifically those centered around
random sketching, have gained traction in primarily theoretical research due to their potential to
significantly reduce problem dimensionality at the cost of an O(1) multiplicative distortion
factor. It has been assumed that this sketching can be done efficiently, but thorough investigation
into how precisely to do it has been neglected. Moreover, the theory-based community has
argued for sketching’s ability to reduce computational cost via complexity analysis, but has not
researched how it affects the stability of the algorithms.

At Sandia, efficient linear solvers that scale well on modern HPC architectures while maintaining
stability are imperative for practical applications. In this LDRD, we developed a random
sketching strategy that is substantially faster than existing ones, and demonstrate its superior
performance in practice on a NVIDIA H100 GPU. Moreover, we show how this can be used to
significantly outperform existing linear least squares solvers while improving the solver’s
stability as well. Additionally, we demonstrate how this sketching strategy can be used to make a
fast, stable QR factorization that can subsequently be used in s-step and block Krylov solvers.
Finally, we incorporate a sketching-based block orthogonalization scheme into s-step GMRES,
which is stable and faster than existing approaches on the Perlmutter supercomputer.

INTRODUCTION AND EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF RESULTS:
In this LDRD, we first draw attention to the first paper in the addendum: “A High Performance
GPU CountSketch Implementation and Its Application to Multisketching and Least Squares
Problems”. Section 1 of this paper provides an introduction to the various sketches used in
literature, drawing particular attention to the Gaussian sketch (a dense matrix), the CountSketch
(a sparse matrix consisting of exactly one ±1 per column), and the Subsampled Randomized
Hadamard Transform (SRHT, which uses a FFT-like method). In this section, we discuss the
advantages and disadvantages of each type of sketch, and introduce the concept of multi-
sketching, which combines the CountSketch and Gaussian sketch to get the advantages of each.
We later develop high performance GPU implementations of each sketch in Sections 4-5, which
has not previously been done, and demonstrate in Section 6.2 that using the multi-sketch
approach with our custom CountSketch kernel is substantially faster than the aforementioned
sketches studied in theoretical literature. Further, in Section 2, we demonstrate how this strategy
can be integrated into least squares solvers. In Section 6.3, we find that our multi-sketched least

squares solver can outperform the normal equations—the classically fastest dense least squares
solver—by up to 77% while substantially improving its stability.

In the second paper in the addendum, “Analysis of Randomized Householder-Cholesky QR
Factorization with Multisketching”, we use multi-sketching to develop a fast QR solver called
“rand_cholQR” (described in Section 4), which performs approximately the same as the current
state-of-the-art QR solver CholeskyQR2 (cholQR2), while substantially improving its stability.
We demonstrate these improved stability properties empirically in Section 6.3, and also
mathematically prove that they hold in Section 5.3. Finally, we demonstrate the performance of
each algorithm on a A100 GPU in Section 6.2.

In the third paper in the addendum, “Random-sketching Techniques to Enhance the Numerical
Stability of Block Orthogonalization Algorithms for s-step GMRES”, we incorporate our QR
solver rand_cholQR into s-step GMRES—a parallel GMRES implementation—and demonstrate
that it improves the stability of the algorithm at virtually no cost in Sections 9 and 11.
Furthermore, we develop a more intricate randomized two-stage block orthogonalization scheme
in Sections 6 and 7, and show that it is faster than the currently used block orthogonalization
methods while maintaining stability in Sections 9 and 11. We demonstrate the performance of
each algorithm on the Perlmutter supercomputer. A higher-level summary of the contributions of
each paper can also be found in the slide in the addendum.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT AND
METHODOLOGY:
In the first paper in the addendum: “A High Performance GPU CountSketch Implementation and
Its Application to Multisketching and Least Squares Problems”, we detail how the CountSketch
(a sparse matrix consisting of exactly one ±1 per column) can be applied to a dense matrix either
by using a sparse matrix multiply (SpMM) or by instead reframing the algorithm as
adding/subtracting rows of the matrix into a reduced result in Section 4. We wrote a custom
CountSketch kernel in CUDA doing this row addition/subtraction scheme and compare it to
cuSPARSE’s general purpose SpMM algorithm for the same task. We then investigate how
integrating this custom CountSketch kernel into a multi-sketch strategy compares to existing
sketches. Further, we compare this multi-sketch strategy into a randomized least squares solver
can outperform the normal equations and standard LAPACK least squares routines. All
experiments done in this paper were on the Hops cluster, and all code was written in CUDA
C++, using the cuBLAS, cuSPARSE, cuSOLVER, and cuRAND libraries for optimized linear
algebra and random number generation on the H100 GPUs on Hops.

The second paper in the addendum, “Analysis of Randomized Householder-Cholesky QR
Factorization with Multisketching”, consists mostly of mathematical stability proofs and
roundoff error analysis of our randomized QR solver rand_cholQR in Section 5. We additionally

test the stability of rand_cholQR compared to a variety of other popular QR algorithms
(including Householder QR and cholQR2) empirically in MATLAB, by generating matrices with
a prescribed condition number, factorizing them, and analyzing their factorization and
orthogonality errors in Section 6. Finally, we test the performance of rand_cholQR compared to
cholQR2, along with a variety of other QR algorithms on an A100 GPU on one of the CEE
machines using a mix of Kokkos and CUDA (specifically to call LAPACK routines that
KokkosKernels does not currently support), also in Section 6.

The third paper in the addendum, “Random-sketching Techniques to Enhance the Numerical
Stability of Block Orthogonalization Algorithms for s-step GMRES” investigates the stability
effect of integrating rand_cholQR into s-step GMRES during the block orthogonalization
procedure. Furthermore, in Sections 6 and 7, we develop a more intricate randomized two-stage
block orthogonalization scheme designed to reduce processor communication. We analyze the
stability of the resulting two-stage algorithm both mathematically and empirically on
manufactured test matrices to orthogonalize in MATLAB in Section 9. We demonstrate the
performance of the standard GMRES algorithm compared to a vanilla s-step GMRES
implementation using cholQR in the block orthogonalization, s-step GMRES using
rand_cholQR, and finally s-step GMRES using the randomized two-stage block
orthogonalization scheme on the Perlmutter supercomputer in Section 11.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION:
The first paper provided in the addendum: “A High Performance GPU CountSketch
Implementation and Its Application to Multisketching and Least Squares Problems”, is the first
paper to our knowledge to contain a hardware-tuned implementation of any sketching methods;
particularly focusing on strong GPU implementations of the CountSketch and the SRHT in
Sections 4 and 5. Although much of the literature implements the CountSketch using a simple
SpMM, we demonstrate that the custom kernel we wrote is substantially faster (about 3 times
faster), and thus using a simple SpMM is often inadequate if maximizing performance is the
goal, which it often is when random sketching is used. Generally, our custom kernels for
sketching (CountSketch and SRHT) are quite efficient; both are memory-bound operations and
achieve 60-70% of the peak memory throughput on a H100, as demonstrated in Section 6.
Furthermore, we demonstrate in Section 6 that our custom CountSketch implementation used
within a multi-sketch strategy on a large, overdetermined dense least squares problem
consistently beat the performance of the normal equations in practice, which is a feat we have
also not seen accomplished before by any least squares algorithm for similar problems in existing
literature. Finally, we demonstrate that along with the performance gains the multi-sketched least
squares solver has over the normal equations, we also get significantly better stability properties.

The second paper attached in the addendum, “Analysis of Randomized Householder-Cholesky
QR Factorization with Multisketching”, proves that our randomized QR solver rand_cholQR is

significantly more stable than the currently highest performing QR algorithm cholQR2. The
proofs are based on classical roundoff error analysis, and shown in extreme detail in Section 5.
Additionally, we show that when multi-sketching is employed, there is little to no performance
hit in practice when using rand_cholQR over cholQR2 in Section 6. To our knowledge, this is
the first QR algorithm to provide comparable performance to cholQR2, let alone while also
substantially improving upon its stability. We also demonstrate that rand_cholQR is significantly
faster than other popular alternatives, like Householder QR, implying that it is a practical, stable
QR algorithm.

The third paper included in the addendum, “Random-sketching Techniques to Enhance the
Numerical Stability of Block Orthogonalization Algorithms for s-step GMRES” demonstrates
that integrating rand_cholQR into s-step GMRES boosts the stability of s-step GMRES using
cholQR2 without taking a performance hit, in a similar vein to the second paper. We then show
that our novel randomized two-stage orthogonalization framework maintains a similar level of
stability, but boosts the performance even further over standard GMRES the aforementioned s-
step GMRES implementations.

ANTICIPATED OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS:
The first paper provided in the addendum: “A High Performance GPU CountSketch
Implementation and Its Application to Multisketching and Least Squares Problems”,
demonstrates how powerful multi-sketching can be as a tool for accelerating overdetermined
least squares problems, and potentially other types of randomized solvers. Essentially, it shows a
multi-sketched least squares solver provides significantly better stability and better performance
than the current fastest least squares solver (the normal equations), at the miniscule cost of an
O(1) multiplicative distortion on the least squares residual. Simultaneously improving upon
performance and stability for a relatively broad class of problems like dense tall-skinny
overdetermined least squares problems is exceedingly rare in numerical linear algebra. This work
has been accepted into the ScalAH workshop (Workshop on Latest Advances in Scalable
Algorithms for Large-Scale Heterogeneous Systems) at Supercomputing 25, where I plan to
present it.

The second paper attached in the addendum, “Analysis of Randomized Householder-Cholesky
QR Factorization with Multisketching”, developed a QR algorithm that significantly boosts the
stability of the current fastest QR algorithm (cholQR2) at essentially no computational cost. This
has potential for impact in a variety of other routines reliant on QR factorizations, such as certain
types of Krylov methods used to solve linear systems and eigenvalue problems, along with other
common routines like singular value decompositions (SVDs). This paper was among the first to
investigate the stability of randomized linear algebra routines, and has since been accepted and
published in Numerische Mathematik. It has garnered interest in the numerical linear algebra
community, and I have presented on it at several venues, including invited presentations at the

SIAM Annual conference 2025, ILAS 2025 (unable to attend), SIAM Linear Algebra 2025
(unable to attend), along with a talk at Householder Symposium XXII (arguably the most
prestigious numerical linear algebra conference, which can only be attended by invitation).

The third paper included in the addendum, “Random-sketching Techniques to Enhance the
Numerical Stability of Block Orthogonalization Algorithms for s-step GMRES” developed a
novel randomized two-stage orthogonalization framework that can potentially increase both the
reliability (via stability gains) and performance of s-step GMRES. Since GMRES is the most
generic iterative linear solver that offers monotonic convergence guarantees, it is widely used in
many modelling and simulation applications at Sandia and elsewhere. Because s-step GMRES is
a parallel GMRES implementation, our results potentially offer faster simulations which could
be impactful to many applications. We have submitted this paper and it is currently in the review
stage.

Going forward, these results could motivate the lab to invest in integrating randomized solver
techniques into Sandia-owned software, such as Trilinos. Further, the potential gains realized
through the linear least squares solver could potentially be useful to applications outside
modelling and simulation, such as those driven by large-scale data analysis and machine
learning. Finally, modelling and simulation applications could be further impacted if we applied
randomized techniques into low-rank approximations to develop preconditioning techniques for
existing linear solvers.

CONCLUSION:
In this LDRD, we showed both via empirical study and mathematical proof that linear solver
performance on modern hardware and stability can be simultaneously improved using random
sketching, demonstrating this tangibly on overdetermined least squares problems, and via
orthogonalization schemes used within s-step Krylov methods. Furthermore, we demonstrated
that in order to attain high performance using such randomized linear solvers, the sketching
technique chosen makes a substantial impact. Motivated by this finding, we fine-tuned a
CountSketch implementation that when coupled with our multi-sketch strategy allowed for
higher performance than existing sketches developed in the literature.
