Spectral Asymptotics of Neural Network Loss Landscapes: An Exact Decomposition of the Curvature Exponent
Anherutowa Calvo

Abstract
The curvature exponent α \alpha in h k ∝ σ k α h_{k}\propto\sigma_{k}^{\alpha} —governing how Hessian eigenvalues scale with gradient singular values—varies systematically across layer types ( α ≈ 2 \alpha\approx 2 for convolutions, ≈ 1 \approx 1 for transformer attention, < 1 <1 for MLP up-projections). Why? We prove the Spectral Alignment Decomposition : α = 2 + d ​ log ⁡ Φ k / d ​ log ⁡ σ k \alpha=2+d\log\Phi_{k}/d\log\sigma_{k} , where Φ k \Phi_{k} measures alignment between Kronecker factor eigenbases and gradient singular directions. This reduces “why does α \alpha vary?” to a geometric question we answer for LayerNorm, residual connections, and softmax heads. The decomposition implies a spectral transfer identity s = α ​ γ s=\alpha\gamma linking curvature exponent, effective gradient rank-decay γ \gamma , and Hessian decay exponent s s [ 1 ] . The identity is algebraic; its empirical content is that α \alpha and γ \gamma , fit on independent data (HVPs vs. SVD), recover s s to ≈ \approx 2% median error across 93 layers, five architectures, and three datasets—with no free parameters. A zeta-function bound on participation ratio shows curvature concentrates onto effectively one direction per layer. As a proof of concept, we derive the architecture-adaptive preconditioner T ⁡ ( σ , α ) T(\sigma;\alpha) and show that Spectral Newton —implementing T T in the gradient singular basis—outperforms AdamW on vision benchmarks where α ≈ 2 \alpha\approx 2 .

Introduction

The eigenvalue spectrum of the neural network loss Hessian encodes how optimization navigates parameter space. Recent work has established
what
the spectrum looks like: Hessian eigenvalues decay as power laws with exponent
s

[
1
,
5
]
, weight matrices exhibit heavy-tailed spectral densities correlated with generalization
[
2
]
, and Kronecker-factored approximations capture much of the Hessian structure
[
3
,
4
]
. These results describe
different projections
of the same underlying object, but the mechanistic question—
why

s
takes the values it does, and how it connects to gradient structure—remains unanswered.

We study curvature
along gradient singular directions
. For layer
\ell
with gradient
G_{\ell}=U\Sigma V^{\top}
, let
h_{k}
denote the exact Hessian eigenvalue along direction
u_{k}v_{k}^{\top}
, measured via Hessian-vector products (HVPs). Empirically,
h_{k}
follows a power law in the gradient singular value
\sigma_{k}
:

h_{k}=c\cdot\sigma_{k}^{\alpha}

(1)

The exponent
\alpha
is
not universal
: it depends on layer type, architecture, and task (Section
3
). Understanding
\alpha
is the key to predicting which preconditioner a layer requires.

Our central contribution is the
Spectral Alignment Decomposition
(Theorem
1
): an exact decomposition of
h_{k}
into Kronecker factor eigenvalues and measurable alignment ratios, yielding an identity for
\alpha
in terms of log-log slopes of these quantities. We then:

1.

Derive mechanism-specific predictions for LayerNorm (
\alpha\approx 1
), residual connections, and softmax heads (
\alpha>2
), validated by controlled ablations.

2.

Establish the
spectral transfer identity

s=\alpha\gamma
: an algebraic link between
\alpha
, an effective rank-decay exponent
\gamma
fit on gradient singular values, and Hessian decay
s

[
1
]
, validated on 89 layers across three datasets (Section
7
).

3.

Bound the curvature participation ratio via the Riemann zeta function, showing learning is effectively one-dimensional per layer.

4.

Prescribe architecture-adaptive spectral transfer functions
T(\sigma;\alpha)
and validate
Spectral Newton
as their optimizer instantiation (Section
7.3
).

This is a spectral asymptotics result in the spirit of Weyl’s law: the decay exponent encodes geometric information (alignment structure) of the loss landscape, not merely a fitted phenomenological parameter.

Setup and Measurement

For layer
\ell
with weight
W_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, gradient
G_{\ell}=\nabla_{W_{\ell}}\mathcal{L}
, and mini-batch activations
A\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times n}
, errors
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times m}
, the gradient factors as
G_{\ell}=\frac{1}{B}\delta^{\top}A
. Under the Gauss–Newton (GN) approximation, the per-layer Hessian satisfies
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{GN}}^{(\ell)}\approx C_{\delta}\otimes C_{A}
with
C_{\delta}=\frac{1}{B}\delta^{\top}\delta
and
C_{A}=\frac{1}{B}A^{\top}A
.

Measurement protocol.

We train models on CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet-200 to convergence, and also evaluate pretrained ImageNet-1K weights (IMAGENET1K_V1). For each, we fix a batch of
B=2048
–
4096
samples, compute top-
k
gradient singular directions via SVD, and measure
h_{k}=v_{k}^{\top}\mathcal{H}v_{k}
via double backpropagation (exact HVP). We fit
\alpha
by log-log regression on
(\sigma_{k},h_{k})
. All results use exact HVPs; finite-difference Hessians yield
R^{2}\approx 0.17
with 50% spurious negative curvatures and are excluded.

Exponents
\alpha
and
\gamma
.

We fit
\alpha
from exact HVPs on
(\sigma_{k},h_{k})
. We define
\gamma
as the negated log-log slope of rank-ordered
\sigma_{k}
on top-
k
gradient singular values (typically
k{=}20
;
k{=}100
for extended spectra in Appendix
G
). This is an
effective
decay exponent over a finite rank window, not a claim that
\sigma_{k}
follows a global power law.

. Wu et al.

5
The Spectral Alignment Decomposition

Definition 1

(Spectral alignment ratios)
.

Let
G=U\Sigma V^{\top}
be the gradient SVD, and
C_{\delta}=Q_{\delta}\Lambda_{\delta}Q_{\delta}^{\top}
,
C_{A}=Q_{A}\Lambda_{A}Q_{A}^{\top}
eigendecompositions with eigenvalues in decreasing order. Define:

\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}=\frac{u_{k}^{\top}C_{\delta}u_{k}}{[\Lambda_{\delta}]_{kk}},\qquad\rho_{k}^{(A)}=\frac{v_{k}^{\top}C_{A}v_{k}}{[\Lambda_{A}]_{kk}}

Theorem 1

(Spectral Alignment Decomposition)
.

Under
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{GN}}^{(\ell)}\approx C_{\delta}\otimes C_{A}
:

1.

(Exact decomposition.)

h_{k}=\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}\cdot\rho_{k}^{(A)}\cdot[\Lambda_{\delta}]_{kk}\cdot[\Lambda_{A}]_{kk}
.

2.

(
\alpha
as alignment slope.)
With
\Phi_{k}=\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}\rho_{k}^{(A)}
and power-law fit
h_{k}=c\sigma_{k}^{\alpha}
:

\alpha=\frac{d\log\Lambda_{k}}{d\log\sigma_{k}}+\frac{d\log\Phi_{k}}{d\log\sigma_{k}},\quad\Lambda_{k}=[\Lambda_{\delta}]_{kk}[\Lambda_{A}]_{kk}

(2)

3.

(Canonical form.)
When
Q_{\delta}=U
,
Q_{A}=V
(perfect alignment),
[\Lambda_{\delta}]_{kk}[\Lambda_{A}]_{kk}=\sigma_{k}^{2}/\cos^{2}\theta_{k}
and

\alpha=2+\frac{d\log\Phi_{k}}{d\log\sigma_{k}}-\frac{d\log\cos^{2}\!\theta_{k}}{d\log\sigma_{k}}

(3)

where
\cos^{2}\theta_{k}=h_{k}^{\mathrm{exact}}/h_{k}^{\mathrm{Kron}}
measures the GN-to-exact gap.

Proof.

Part 1.
The curvature along
u_{k}v_{k}^{\top}
is
h_{k}=\vecop(u_{k}v_{k}^{\top})^{\top}(C_{\delta}\otimes C_{A})\vecop(u_{k}v_{k}^{\top})=(u_{k}^{\top}C_{\delta}u_{k})(v_{k}^{\top}C_{A}v_{k})
. By Definition
1
,
u_{k}^{\top}C_{\delta}u_{k}=\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}[\Lambda_{\delta}]_{kk}
and
v_{k}^{\top}C_{A}v_{k}=\rho_{k}^{(A)}[\Lambda_{A}]_{kk}
, giving the decomposition.

Part 2.
Taking logs:
\log h_{k}=\log\Phi_{k}+\log\Lambda_{k}
. If
h_{k}=c\sigma_{k}^{\alpha}
, then
d\log h_{k}/d\log\sigma_{k}=\alpha
. Differentiating the decomposition yields Eq. (
2
).

Part 3.
When
Q_{\delta}=U
and
Q_{A}=V
, we have
\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}=\rho_{k}^{(A)}=1
, so
\Phi_{k}=1
. Under the alignment condition of Theorem
2
,
[\Lambda_{\delta}]_{kk}[\Lambda_{A}]_{kk}=\sigma_{k}^{2}/\cos^{2}\theta_{k}
, hence
d\log\Lambda_{k}/d\log\sigma_{k}=2-d\log\cos^{2}\theta_{k}/d\log\sigma_{k}
. Substituting into Part 2 with
d\log\Phi_{k}=0
gives Eq. (
3
).
∎

Remark 1
.

Equation (
3
) is the central result:
\alpha
deviates from
2
iff
\Phi_{k}
or
\cos^{2}\theta_{k}
correlate with
\sigma_{k}
. When both are
k
-independent,
\alpha=2
regardless of their magnitudes.

Theorem 2

(Baseline:
\alpha=2
under perfect alignment)
.

When
C_{\delta}
,
C_{A}
share the singular/eigen-basis of
G
and per-sample
(u_{k}^{\top}\delta_{i})^{2}
,
(v_{k}^{\top}a_{i})^{2}
are approximately independent across samples,
h_{k}\propto\sigma_{k}^{2}
.

6
Architectural Mechanisms

We now show how each architectural component produces a specific pattern in
\Phi_{k}
vs.
\sigma_{k}
.

6.1
LayerNorm

Theorem 3

(LayerNorm flattens activation alignment)
.

For
y=W\cdot\mathrm{LN}(x)
, the activation covariance
C_{A}^{\mathrm{LN}}=\Gamma\hat{C}\Gamma
with
\hat{C}
the normalized-input covariance. The condition number
\kappa(\hat{C}|_{\mathbf{1}^{\perp}})=1+O(\sqrt{n/B})
, and
\rho_{k}^{(A)}
is confined to a
\sigma_{k}
-independent band. Thus
d\log\rho_{k}^{(A)}/d\log\sigma_{k}\approx 0
, reducing
\alpha
from
2
toward
1
.

Corollary 4
.

With
\cos^{2}\theta_{k}
approximately constant,
\alpha\approx 1+d\log\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}/d\log\sigma_{k}
. If
u_{k}^{\top}C_{\delta}u_{k}\propto\sigma_{k}
, then
\alpha\approx 1
.

6.2
Residual connections

Proposition 5

(Residual decorrelation)
.

In
y=x+f(x)
with skip-dominated regime (
\|J_{f}^{\top}\delta_{\mathrm{out}}\|\ll\|\delta_{\mathrm{out}}\|
),
C_{\delta}\approx C_{\delta_{\mathrm{out}}}
whose eigenvectors bear no relation to current-layer
u_{k}
. Thus
\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}
has no systematic
\sigma_{k}
dependence and
d\log\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}/d\log\sigma_{k}\approx 0
.

Combined with LayerNorm,
\alpha\approx 2-d\log\cos^{2}\theta_{k}/d\log\sigma_{k}
, explaining MLP up-projections with
\alpha\approx 0.8
.

6.3
Softmax concentration

Proposition 6

(Softmax creates
\alpha>2
)
.

For output layer with
c
classes,
C_{\delta}
has rank
\leq c
. Top singular vectors of
G
align with top eigenvectors of
C_{\delta}
, giving
\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}\approx 1
for
k\leq c
and
\rho_{k}^{(\delta)}\ll 1
beyond—a sharp drop creating
d\log\Phi_{k}/d\log\sigma_{k}>0
and
\alpha>2
.

6.4
The conv gap: GN vs. Kronecker

For conv layers,
\cos^{2}\theta_{k}\sim 10^{3}
–
10^{7}
yet
\alpha\approx 2
. The
concentration ratio

\bar{h}^{\mathrm{SoP}}/\bar{h}^{\mathrm{PoS}}\approx 1
(Kronecker factorization is accurate), while
\cos^{2}\theta_{k}\gg 1
(GN-to-exact gap from weight-sharing). What matters for
\alpha
is the
slope

d\log\cos^{2}\theta_{k}/d\log\sigma_{k}\approx 0
, not the magnitude (Appendix
D
).

6.5
Refined prediction: LayerNorm is necessary but not sufficient

ViT-Tiny on CIFAR-10 has LayerNorm yet
\alpha\approx 2
. Controlled ablations (Appendix
C
) show that large output dimension creates sparse, anisotropic
C_{\delta}
, pulling
\alpha
below
2
on CIFAR-100; switching GPT-2 from LM to binary classification raises
\alpha
toward
2
. The corrected condition:
\alpha<2
requires
both
LayerNorm
and
sufficiently large output dimension.

7.1
The Alpha Triangle

Three exponents summarize the spectral structure: curvature
\alpha
(
h_{k}\propto\sigma_{k}^{\alpha}
), effective gradient rank decay
\gamma
(slope of
\log\sigma_{k}
vs.
\log k

\lambda_{k}\sim k^{-s}

Theorem 7

(Spectral transfer identity)
.

Suppose
\sigma_{k}\sim k^{-\gamma}
and
h_{k}=c\sigma_{k}^{\alpha}
on a rank interval. Then
\lambda_{k}\sim k^{-s}
with
s=\alpha\gamma
.

Proof.

\lambda_{k}=h_{k}=c(c^{\prime}k^{-\gamma})^{\alpha}=c^{\prime\prime}k^{-\alpha\gamma}
.
∎

Remark 2

(Why this is not a tautology)
.

The identity
s=\alpha\gamma
is algebraic under the local power-law ansatz—the one-line proof is intentional. The empirical content is threefold:
(i)

\alpha
and
\gamma
are fit on
different
data (
(\sigma_{k},h_{k})
from HVPs vs.
(\log k,\log\sigma_{k})
from SVD), yet their product recovers
s
from rank-ordered curvatures to median error
1.9%
on CIFAR,
1.0%
on Tiny-ImageNet,
1.6%
on ImageNet-1K (Figure
3
);
(ii)

\sigma_{k}
is
not
globally
k^{-\gamma}
—BIC selects log-normal over power-law on most layers (Figure
4
; Appendix
G
)—yet the effective exponent
\gamma
on the top-
k
window still produces a tight identity; and
(iii)
the identity holds across 93 layers spanning five architectures and three datasets with no free parameters. A tautology produces no testable predictions; this identity is violated whenever the local power-law ansatz breaks down, and it
does
break at boundary layers (Appendix
H
).

7.2
Near-rank-one curvature

Theorem 9

(Zeta function bound)
.

With
h_{k}\sim k^{-\alpha\gamma}
, the participation ratio
\mathrm{PR}_{h}=(\sum h_{k})^{2}/\sum h_{k}^{2}\leq\zeta(\alpha\gamma)^{2}/\zeta(2\alpha\gamma)
, bounded for
\alpha\gamma>1
. For
\alpha\gamma=2
:
\mathrm{PR}_{h}\lesssim 2.5
.

Despite hundreds of singular directions, curvature concentrates onto
\mathrm{PR}_{h}\in[1.0,1.8]
while gradients span moderate-dimensional subspaces (
\mathrm{PR}_{\mathrm{grad}}\in[1.8,58]

7.3
From decomposition to Spectral Newton

The decomposition predicts
which layers
admit Kronecker-style curvature approximations: when
|\alpha-2|
is small and the concentration ratio
\bar{h}^{\mathrm{SoP}}/\bar{h}^{\mathrm{PoS}}\approx 1
, the Kronecker product-of-means matches the exact mean-of-products Hessian along gradient directions (Figure
5
). Conversely, layers with
\alpha\ll 2
(LayerNorm + large output dimension) or
\alpha\gg 2
(softmax heads) break alignment; full Hessian or task-specific preconditioning is required, consistent with K-FAC/Shampoo using richer structure than diagonal Adam.

Prescriptive transfer function.

When
h_{k}\propto\sigma_{k}^{\alpha}
, the optimal step along singular direction

scales as

rather than a uniform spectral flattening (Muon) or a single scalar per layer. For convolutional layers with
\alpha\approx 2
, this reduces to Spectral Newton (SN):
\Delta W=U\,\diag(\sigma_{k}/(\sigma_{k}^{2}+d))\,V^{\top}
for
G=U\Sigma V^{\top}
. Per-layer
\alpha
from Table
1
selects the family member—
\alpha\approx 1
for transformers suggests
T(\sigma)=\sigma/(\sigma+d)
instead.

Discussion

Spectral asymptotics interpretation.

The identity
\alpha=2+d\log\Phi_{k}/d\log\sigma_{k}
is a Weyl-law analog: the decay exponent of curvature along gradient directions encodes the
alignment geometry
of the loss Hessian restricted to the gradient subspace. Just as
\lambda_{k}\sim k^{2/d}
reveals manifold dimension
d
,
\alpha
reveals how Kronecker factor eigenbases align with gradient singular directions.

Optimizer implications.

The decomposition yields curvature-adaptive transfer functions
T(\sigma;\alpha)=\sigma/(\sigma^{\alpha}+d)
with architecture-dependent
\alpha
. As directional evidence, Spectral Newton implements this prescription in the gradient singular basis and outperforms AdamW on vision benchmarks where
\alpha\approx 2
(Table
3
; single-seed, proof of concept). The deeper structural insight is that Kronecker-style methods are justified when
|\alpha-2|
is small (Figure
5
), and that scalar per-layer scaling from
s=\alpha\gamma
fails because eigenvector alignment (Theorem
1
) is directional—the theory prescribes
T(\sigma_{k};\alpha_{\ell})
per singular direction, not one learning rate per layer.

Gradient rank profile and phase transitions.

BIC favors log-normal-in-rank over strict power-law on most CIFAR layers (Figure
4
). During training, the winning rank model shifts: Figure
6
shows VGG-16 on Tiny-ImageNet transitioning from log-normal dominance at initialization toward mixed power-law/exponential regimes by epoch 15–25 (slower than ResNet-50, where the transition peaks by epoch 5). Thus
\gamma
is an effective exponent; Appendix
L
shows that the same Kronecker factors predict the rank-profile shape.

Limitations and open questions.

Scale.
Our largest models are GPT-2 (6-layer, 85M) and ResNet-50; the theory is architecture-generic and makes concrete, falsifiable predictions at larger scales—for instance, Theorem
1
predicts that ViT-Large attention layers will exhibit
\alpha\approx 1
(driven by LayerNorm-induced alignment collapse, Section
6
) and that LLaMA-7B MLP layers will follow
\alpha<1.2
with the same Kronecker decomposition—but we have not yet verified these. Validation at the 1–10B parameter regime is the most important next step.

Spectral Newton.
SN results (Table
3
) are single-seed, single-architecture, and serve as directional evidence rather than a comprehensive optimizer comparison. Multi-seed evaluation with wall-clock accounting is needed.

1.

Extend the zeta PR bound to log-normal or exponential rank spectra via Tauberian theorems.

2.

Validate the full Kronecker spectral theory at LLM scale (ViT-Large, LLaMA-7B, GPT-3 class).

3.

Connect spectral phase-transition timing to gradient flow on
(C_{\delta}(t),C_{A}(t))
.

Appendix L
Kronecker Spectral Identity

The Spectral Alignment Decomposition (Theorem
1
) determines
\alpha
; we show here that the same Kronecker factors also predict the
shape
of the gradient rank profile.

Separable variance structure.

In the Kronecker eigenbasis, the rotated gradient
\widetilde{G}=Q_{\delta}^{\top}GQ_{A}
has entry variance

\mathrm{Var}(\widetilde{G}_{ij})\propto\lambda_{i}^{(\delta)}\lambda_{j}^{(A)},

a rank-one separable profile. The singular value distribution of matrices with separable variance is characterized by the Couillet–Hachem Stieltjes transform
[
11
]
: the limiting spectral measure of
\widetilde{G}^{\top}\widetilde{G}
depends only on the empirical distributions of
\{\lambda_{i}^{(\delta)}\}
and
\{\lambda_{j}^{(A)}\}
.
