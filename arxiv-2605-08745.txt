Exclusion reshapes the operational manifestation of preparation contextuality
Pritam Roy, Thansingh Jankawat, Ranendu Adhikary, A. S. Majumdar

Abstract
Replacing the task of retrieval with exclusion changes how preparation contextuality manifests operationally under parity-oblivious constraints, with exclusion showing a quantum advantage where retrieval does not. We introduce the parity-oblivious random exclusion code (POREC) and show that for prime symbol size m m , classical and preparation-noncontextual encodings provide a tight noncontextual bound. For the first nontrivial case (two digits, three symbols) our derived exact qubit optimum violates this bound, in contrast to parity-oblivious retrieval which displays no quantum advantage. This characteristic difference is absent without parity constraints. For general prime m m , qubit strategies achieve a quantum-to-noncontextual gap that grows linearly relative to the random exclusion code (REC) gap, exceeding both parity-oblivious retrieval and standard REC. The exact qubit bound yields a sharp semi-device-independent certification of dimension d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 . Our analysis of noise robustness demonstrates POREC to be amenable for experimental implementation on existing prepare-and-measure platforms, establishing parity-oblivious exclusion as a distinct operational probe of preparation contextuality, as well as a practical information processing protocol with wide applications.

Parity-Oblivious Random Exclusion Code.–
Parity-oblivious prepare-and-measure tasks for binary symbols provide a natural framework to probe preparation contextuality and quantum advantages
Spekkens et al. 2009
;
Chailloux et al. 2016
;
Ghorai and Pan 2018
;
Singh et al. 2025
;
Roy and Pan 2024
. In the retrieval setting, multi-symbol
m
-PORAC was introduced by Ambainis
et al.

Ambainis et al. 2019
for a single parity class and later extended to multi-mask constraints by Hameedi
et al.

(
Hameedi et al. 2017
, Supplementary Sec. I)
.
We restrict to prime
m
, for which
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
forms a field, making all parity classes uniform. Parity-obliviousness imposes equal-weight constraints, and every nonzero mask is surjective, enabling an exact Fourier-analytic derivation of the classical (preparation-noncontextual) bound
POR
(see Supplemental Material Sec. I).

Operationally, Alice receives a uniformly random string
x=x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
, and Bob receives a uniformly random index
y\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Alice encodes
x
into a message (classical or quantum) sent to Bob, subject to
parity-obliviousness
; she may communicate information about
x
except any information about multi-digit parities.
Formally, for masks
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
with Hamming weight
\mathrm{wt}(r)\geq 2
(the number of nonzero components of
r
), defining

\Pi_{r}(x)=r\cdot x\pmod{m},

(1)

ensures that
\Pi_{r}
probes only multi-digit linear correlations; allowing
\mathrm{wt}(r)=1
would forbid all single-digit information and trivialize the task, whereas
\mathrm{wt}(r)\geq 2
removes only joint information while preserving access to individual digits. Parity-obliviousness then requires that the average encoding over each class
C_{k}^{(r)}=\{x:r\cdot x\equiv k\pmod{m}\}
is independent of
k
, so that no information about any such parity can be inferred.

We now define the figure of merit. Under parity-obliviousness, the success probability for retrieval (
b=x_{y}
) is
Hameedi et al. 2017
;
Ambainis et al. 2019

P_{\mathrm{PORAC}}=\frac{1}{nm^{n}}\sum_{x}\sum_{y=1}^{n}P(b=x_{y}\mid x,y).

(2)

To contrast this with
exclusion
, we consider the objective
b\neq x_{y}
, with success probability

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}=\frac{1}{nm^{n}}\sum_{x}\sum_{y=1}^{n}P(b\neq x_{y}\mid x,y).

(3)

We denote this
n
-digit,
m
-symbol setting as the
(n,m)
parity-oblivious random exclusion task.

In the quantum setting, inputs are encoded into states
\rho_{x}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, and we define the parity-class averaged states
\rho^{(r)}_{k}=\tfrac{1}{|C_{k}^{(r)}|}\sum_{x\in C_{k}^{(r)}}\rho_{x}
. Parity-obliviousness then requires

\rho^{(r)}_{k}=\rho^{(r)}_{k^{\prime}},\quad\forall\,k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}.

(4)

ensuring parity-obliviousness at the level of preparations. The corresponding quantum success probability is

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d)=\frac{1}{nm^{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}}\sum_{y=1}^{n}P(b\neq x_{y}\mid x,y),

(5)

while for PORAC,

P_{\mathrm{PORAC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d)=\frac{1}{nm^{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}}\sum_{y=1}^{n}P(b=x_{y}\mid x,y).

(6)

The RAC and REC figures of merit have the same form but without the parity-obliviousness constraint in Eq. (
1
).

Classical and noncontextual bounds.–

We now establish the optimal benchmark attainable by all classical and preparation-noncontextual theories under full parity-obliviousness.

Theorem 1

(Classical and noncontextual bounds)
.

For
n
digits and prime
m
, any parity-oblivious prepare-and-measure protocol can encode information about at most one digit of
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
. Hence, the optimal preparation-noncontextual success probability of POREC is

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{NC}}=1-\frac{n-1}{mn}.

(7)

Proof sketch.
—
The argument proceeds in three steps.

(i) Structural collapse:
—
For a classical encoding
p(M|x)
with
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
, write

p(M|x)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}}\hat{p}(M,r)\,\omega^{r\cdot x}
with

\omega=e^{2\pi i/m}
. For prime
m
, parity-obliviousness enforces
equality of averages over all classes with
\mathrm{wt}(r)\geq 2
, eliminating
all multi-digit Fourier components. The encoding therefore contains only
constant and single-digit terms, reducing to a convex mixture of single-digit
channels (see Supplemental Material Sec. I
POR
).

(ii) Optimal classical strategy:
—
Within this class, sending a single digit is optimal. For fixed
i
, optimality requires the supports of
q_{i}(M|l)
to be disjoint across
l
, so that
M
identifies
x_{i}
deterministically, yielding success
1
when
y=i
and
(m-1)/m
otherwise, with
P_{i}-P_{0}=1/(nm)>0
.

(iii) Noncontextual reduction:
—
Let
\lambda\in\Lambda
be the ontic state, with preparation distributions
\mu(\lambda|P_{x})
and response functions
\xi(b|y,\lambda)
such that
Leifer 2014
,

P(b|x,y)=\int d\lambda\,\mu(\lambda|P_{x})\,\xi(b|y,\lambda),

(8)

where
\int d\lambda\,\mu(\lambda|P_{x})=1
,
0\leq\xi(b|y,\lambda)\leq 1
, and
\sum_{b}\xi(b|y,\lambda)=1
.

For masks
r
with
\mathrm{wt}(r)\geq 2
, define the coarse-grained preparations

P_{k}^{(r)}=\frac{1}{|C_{k}^{(r)}|}\sum_{x\in C_{k}^{(r)}}P_{x},\quad C_{k}^{(r)}=\{x:\,r\cdot x\equiv k\pmod{m}\}.

(9)

Parity-obliviousness implies the operational equivalence
P(\cdot|P_{k}^{(r)},M)=P(\cdot|P_{k^{\prime}}^{(r)},M)
for all
M,k,k^{\prime}
, which, by preparation noncontextuality
Spekkens 2005
, yields
\mu(\lambda|P_{k}^{(r)})=\mu(\lambda|P_{k^{\prime}}^{(r)})
.

Using linearity in Eq. (
9
) leads to

\sum_{x:\,r\cdot x\equiv k}\mu(\lambda|P_{x})=\sum_{x:\,r\cdot x\equiv k^{\prime}}\mu(\lambda|P_{x})\quad\forall\,\lambda,k,k^{\prime}.

(10)

Using Bayes’ rule with a uniform prior over preparations,
p(P_{x}|\lambda)=\mu(\lambda|P_{x})/\sum_{x^{\prime}}\mu(\lambda|P_{x^{\prime}})
. Eq. (
10
) then implies

\sum_{x:\,r\cdot x\equiv k}p(P_{x}|\lambda)=\sum_{x:\,r\cdot x\equiv k^{\prime}}p(P_{x}|\lambda)\quad\forall\,\lambda,k,k^{\prime}.

(11)

Hence, even conditioned on
\lambda
, no multi-digit parity information is available. The posterior
p(P_{x}|\lambda)
therefore defines a classical parity-oblivious encoding of the preparation label, which by (i) depends on at most one digit.

Substituting into Eq. (
3
),

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}=\frac{1}{nm^{n}}\sum_{x,y}\int d\lambda\,\mu(\lambda|P_{x})\,\xi(b\neq x_{y}|y,\lambda),

(12)

the optimal strategy reduces to encoding a single digit (as in step (ii)), yielding success
1
when
y=i
and
(m-1)/m
otherwise. Hence,

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}\leq 1-\frac{n-1}{mn}=P^{\mathrm{NC}}_{\mathrm{POREC}}.

Thus, preparation noncontextuality enforces a single-digit reduction; any violation implies a breakdown of Eq. (
10
), and hence preparation contextuality.

Quantum advantage.–

We now present the central result for POREC, namely that quantum strategies violate the preparation-noncontextual bound under parity-oblivious constraints. We make this explicit in the minimal nontrivial instance
(n,m)=(2,3)
by identifying the independent parity masks. For inputs
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}
, parity-obliviousness is imposed for masks
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}
with
\mathrm{wt}(r)\geq 2
. Up to nonzero scalar multiples in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, there are two independent masks,
r=(1,1)
and
r=(1,2)
, with parity classes:

\displaystyle r=(1,1)\Rightarrow\;

\displaystyle\{(0,0),(1,2),(2,1)\},\ \{(0,1),(1,0),(2,2)\},

\displaystyle\{(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)\},

\displaystyle r=(1,2)\Rightarrow\;

\displaystyle\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,2)\},\ \{(0,2),(1,0),(2,1)\},

\displaystyle\{(0,1),(1,2),(2,0)\}.

Parity-obliviousness requires equal averages over each class (see Supplemental Material Sec. II
POR
), imposing linear constraints on the Bloch vectors and yielding the additive structure below.

Lemma 1

(Additive Bloch structure)
.

For
n=2
and prime
m
, any qubit encoding
\{\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}\}
satisfying full multi-mask parity-obliviousness, written as
\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(I+\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}\cdot\vec{\sigma})
, admits a decomposition
\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}
, where
\vec{a}_{x_{1}}
and
\vec{b}_{x_{2}}
depend only on the respective digits.

Without loss of generality, fix the gauge
\sum_{x_{1}}\vec{a}_{x_{1}}=\sum_{x_{2}}\vec{b}_{x_{2}}=0
(Corollary 1, see Supplemental Material Sec. II
POR
). For
(n,m)=(2,3)
, this gives
\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}
with
\vec{a}_{0}+\vec{a}_{1}+\vec{a}_{2}=0
and
\vec{b}_{0}+\vec{b}_{1}+\vec{b}_{2}=0
, so the nine states arise from two zero-sum triples with no residual joint dependence on
(x_{1},x_{2})
—the only structure needed for the next theorem.

Theorem 2

(Exact qubit optimum)
.

For
(n,m)=(2,3)
, the optimal qubit success probability is

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{Q}(2)=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}},

(13)

exceeding the noncontextual bound
P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{NC}}=5/6
. The optimum is attained by complementary Pauli measurements, certifying preparation contextuality.

Proof sketch.
—
Using Lemma
1
, the success probability reduces to

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{Q}(2)=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{1}{12}F
, where
F
is linear in the Bloch vectors and POVM elements (see Supplemental Material Sec. III
POR
). Thus, maximizing the success probability is equivalent to minimizing
F
, and by convexity the optimum is attained with extremal POVMs.

The constraint
|\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}|\leq 1
, together with the zero-sum relations, ensures that negative overlaps are balanced by positive ones, which sets the tight bound. At the optimum this reduces to
(\lambda t_{*})^{2}+(\mu s_{*})^{2}\leq 1
. Minimizing
F
is then achieved by aligning each Bloch vector anti-parallel to its measurement direction, giving a linear objective under a quadratic constraint. By Cauchy–Schwarz,
|F|\leq 2\sqrt{2}
, which is tight and yields Eq. (
13
).

Physical origin of the POREC–PORAC contrast:
Parity-obliviousness reduces all classical and preparation-noncontextual encodings to a single-digit form, while qubit encodings retain the additive structure
\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}
. Retrieval requires isolating one component while ignoring the other, which for qubits requires incompatible measurements. This is possible for
m=2
, but for
m\geq 3
the additive structure and finer discrimination make such isolation impossible, so no quantum advantage arises in PORAC. In contrast, exclusion requires ruling out only the correct value and uses anti-aligned measurements, so a quantum advantage arises in POREC.

This structural difference persists for all prime
m\geq 3

m

Discussion.—
In this work we have demonstrated how replacing the task of retrieval
with exclusion characteristically modifies or reshapes the operational manifestation of preparation contextuality under parity-oblivious constraints. Our results show that parity-obliviousness collapses all classical and preparation-noncontextual encodings to a single-digit form, while admissible quantum encodings retain an additive structure for qubits. The operational consequences are task dependent. In retrieval (PORAC)
Hameedi et al. 2017
, measurement incompatibility together with the additive coupling prevents accessing one component independently, eliminating any qubit advantage. In contrast, exclusion (POREC) is compatible with this structure, since ruling out outcomes suffices, yielding a qubit advantage already at the two-digit three-symbol
(2,3)
level. The equivalence between retrieval and exclusion observed in RAC/REC
Bae et al. 2026
is broken for all prime symbol size
m
in POREC. Stronger suppression of classical encodings compared to quantum ones leads to an enhanced quantum-to-noncontextual gap in POREC, providing linear advantage with
m
relative to the REC gap.

Nontrivial preparation constraints give rise to a dimension-dependent hierarchy,
P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{Q}(d)<P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{Q}(d+1)
, in contrast to the existing results based on preparation contextuality
Spekkens et al. 2009
;
Ambainis et al. 2019
;
Hameedi et al. 2017
;
Ghorai and Pan 2018
;
Roy and Pan 2024
;
Singh et al. 2025
;
Patra et al. 2025
. The exact qubit value yields a sharp semi-device-independent witness certifying
d\geq 3
with analytic noise robustness (for
m=3
, the see-saw value at
d=6
coincides with the NPA upper bound
Navascués et al. 2008
within numerical precision). In essence, exclusion remains nontrivial even when retrieval becomes effectively classical, identifying POREC as a distinct regime with an enhanced quantum-to-noncontextual gap, favorable for
a variety of applications. The protocol is compatible with current prepare-and-measure platforms
Ahrens et al. 2012
;
Hendrych et al. 2012
;
Sun et al. 2016
;
Mazurek et al. 2016
;
Sun et al. 2026
and is robust against noise, supporting near-term implementations of preparation contextuality for dimension certification and cryptographic applications such as high-rate multi-outcome randomness generation
Herrero-Collantes and Garcia-Escartin 2017
;
Brown et al. 2021
;
Hazra et al. 2026
, and quantum key distribution
Ding et al. 2017
;
Tan et al. 2021
;
Brown et al. 2021
;
Chaturvedi et al. 2021
.

Our results inspire further studies in several directions.
Since the noncontextual bound holds for all
(n,m)
, the behaviour seen for
(2,m)
is not incidental, but the simplest instance of a general phenomenon, motivating a systematic study of
(n>2,m\geq 3)
POREC scenarios where the advantage persists with new scaling laws. On the other hand,
identifying constraints that further tighten the obtained bounds or modify the scaling remains open. A natural extension is to sequential scenarios, where multiple observers act on the same system
Silva et al. 2015
;
Mal et al. 2016
;
Das et al. 2019
;
Sasmal et al. 2018
;
Brown and Colbeck 2020
. As in sequential RACs
Mohan et al. 2019
;
Miklin et al. 2020
;
Anwer et al. 2021
;
S. S. et al. 2023
;
Roy et al. 2026
, controlled disturbance can enhance information extraction and enable distributed contextuality. Since sequential measurements can enhance device-independent randomness generation
Bowles et al. 2020
, sequential POREC offers a route to certifying high-rate multi-outcome randomness under parity-oblivious constraints.

Sec I: Proof of (Theorem 1) optimal classical and preparation-noncontextual bound for prime

In this section, we derive the optimal classical (preparation-noncontextual) in Theorem 1, the success probability for the
(n,m)
POREC task with
m
prime:

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{cl}}=1-\frac{n-1}{mn}.

Problem definitions and parity-obliviousness:

Alice receives an input string
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
uniformly at random and encodes it into a classical message
M
via a channel
p(M\mid x)
. Bob receives an index
y\in\{1,\dots,n\}
uniformly at random and must output a value
b\neq x_{y}
.

Parity-obliviousness requires that for every mask
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
with
\mathrm{wt}(r)\geq 2
(at least two
r_{i}\neq 0
),

\displaystyle P(r\cdot X=k\mid M)=\tfrac{1}{m},\quad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}.

(S1)

Define
C_{k}^{(r)}=\{x:\,r\cdot x\equiv k\!\!\!\pmod{m}\}
. For prime
m
,
|C_{k}^{(r)}|=m^{n-1}
for all nonzero
r
and all
k
. Applying Bayes’ rule (with a uniform prior over
x
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in C_{k}^{(r)}}p(M\mid x)=\sum_{x\in C_{k^{\prime}}^{(r)}}p(M\mid x),\quad\forall\,k,k^{\prime}.

(S2)

Let
\omega=e^{2\pi i/m}
and expand

\displaystyle p(M\mid x)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}}\hat{p}(M,r)\,\omega^{r\cdot x},\quad\hat{p}(M,r)=\frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{x}p(M\mid x)\,\omega^{-r\cdot x}.

(S3)

For any
r
with
\mathrm{wt}(r)\geq 2
, group the defining sum by parity classes:

\displaystyle\hat{p}(M,r)=\frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\left(\sum_{x\in C_{k}^{(r)}}p(M\mid x)\right)\omega^{-k}.

(S4)

By Eq. (
S2
), the inner sum is independent of
k
, so it factors out:

\displaystyle\hat{p}(M,r)=C(M,r)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\omega^{-k}=0.

(S5)

Thus all multi-digit Fourier modes vanish, leaving only constant and single-digit terms. The encoding must therefore have the form

p(M\mid x)=f_{0}(M)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}t_{i}^{(l)}(M)\,\delta_{x_{i},l}.

The coefficients satisfy
\sum_{l}t_{i}^{(l)}(M)=0
for each
i
and
M
, which follows from orthogonality of Fourier characters. To obtain a nonnegative decomposition, define

\alpha_{i}(M):=\min_{l}t_{i}^{(l)}(M)\leq 0,

and set

a_{i,l}(M):=t_{i}^{(l)}(M)-\alpha_{i}(M)\geq 0,\qquad a_{0}(M):=f_{0}(M)+\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}(M).

Then

p(M\mid x)=a_{0}(M)+\sum_{i,l}a_{i,l}(M)\,\delta_{x_{i},l}.

(S6)

For each
i
, there exists
l_{i}^{*}
such that
a_{i,l_{i}^{*}}(M)=0
. Evaluating at
x=(l_{1}^{*},\dots,l_{n}^{*})
gives

p(M\mid x)=a_{0}(M)\geq 0,

so
a_{0}(M)\geq 0
.

Define

K_{i,l}:=\sum_{M}a_{i,l}(M),\qquad p_{0}:=\sum_{M}a_{0}(M)\geq 0.

Summing over
M
yields

p_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{i,x_{i}}=1\quad\forall x.

Comparing inputs differing only at coordinate
i
shows that
K_{i,l}
is independent of
l
, so we write
K_{i,l}=K_{i}
. Hence

p_{0}+\sum_{i}K_{i}=1.

Defining

q_{0}(M):=\frac{a_{0}(M)}{p_{0}},\qquad q_{i}(M\mid l):=\frac{a_{i,l}(M)}{K_{i}},

and using Eq. (
S6
), we obtain

p(M\mid x)=p_{0}\,q_{0}(M)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}\,q_{i}(M\mid x_{i}).

By linearity,

P_{\mathrm{succ}}=p_{0}P_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}P_{i},

where
P_{0}=\frac{m-1}{m}
. Since
p_{0}+\sum_{i}K_{i}=1
, this is a convex combination, and therefore

P_{\mathrm{succ}}\leq\max\{P_{0},\max_{i}P_{i}\}.

For a fixed
i
,

P_{i}=\frac{1}{n}P(\mathrm{succ}\mid y=i)+\frac{n-1}{n}\cdot\frac{m-1}{m}.

The second term is fixed. The first term is maximised when
M
determines
x_{i}
uniquely, which requires disjoint supports of
q_{i}(\cdot\mid l)
for different
l
. Any overlap reduces the success probability. Thus the optimal choice is deterministic,
M=x_{i}
, giving

P_{i}=\frac{1}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}\cdot\frac{m-1}{m}=\frac{m-1}{m}+\frac{1}{nm}.

Since
P_{i}>P_{0}
, the optimal value satisfies

P_{\mathrm{succ}}\leq 1-\frac{n-1}{mn}.

Achievability:

Alice sends
M=x_{1}
. If
y=1
, Bob outputs any
b\neq M
; if
y\neq 1
, he outputs a fixed
b_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. This achieves

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac{1}{n}\cdot 1+\frac{n-1}{n}\cdot\frac{m-1}{m}=1-\frac{n-1}{mn}.

(S7)

As discussed in the main text, this classical bound coincides with the preparation-noncontextual bound
P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{NC}}
.

Sec II: Proof of (Lemma 1) Additive structure under parity-obliviousness and zero sum gauge

In this section, we prove that full parity-obliviousness enforces an additive structure on the preparation Bloch vectors. We consider
(2,m)
scenario
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{2}
, where
m
is prime, and a family of qubit states
\{\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}\}
. Writing

\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(I+\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\bigr),

we analyze the constraints imposed on the Bloch vectors
\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
by parity-obliviousness.

II.1
Proof of Lemma 1 (Additive Bloch structure)

Under full parity-obliviousness, the Bloch vectors admit a decomposition

\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}\qquad\forall\,x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}.

Proof.

Full parity-obliviousness is defined with respect to all masks
r=(r_{1},r_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{2}
with
r_{1},r_{2}\neq 0
, through the parity function
\Pi_{r}(x_{1},x_{2})=r_{1}x_{1}+r_{2}x_{2}\pmod{m}
.

The condition requires that, for every such mask
r
, the averaged states over each parity class

\rho^{(r)}_{k}:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}:\,r_{1}x_{1}+r_{2}x_{2}=k}\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}

are independent of
k
.

Since
m
is prime, every nonzero element of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
is invertible; in particular,
r_{2}\neq 0
implies
r_{2}^{-1}
exists, and multiplying the parity equation by
r_{2}^{-1}
yields the equivalent form
r_{2}^{-1}r_{1}x_{1}+x_{2}=r_{2}^{-1}k
. Defining
\alpha:=r_{1}r_{2}^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times},
this becomes
\alpha x_{1}+x_{2}=k^{\prime},
where
k^{\prime}=r_{2}^{-1}k
is simply a relabelling of the parity class. Since parity-obliviousness requires independence for all
k
, this relabelling does not affect the condition.

Thus, without loss of generality, it suffices to impose parity-obliviousness for all masks of the form
(\alpha,1)
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}
. For each
\alpha
, the parity classes are therefore described by
\alpha x_{1}+x_{2}=k\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ m),
which can be written as
x_{2}=k-\alpha x_{1}.

Parity-obliviousness then requires that

\rho^{(\alpha)}_{k}:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\rho_{x_{1},\,k-\alpha x_{1}}

is independent of
k
.

Passing to Bloch vectors using linearity of the map
\rho\mapsto\vec{n}
, this is equivalent to requiring that

f(k):=\sum_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\vec{n}_{x_{1},\,k-\alpha x_{1}}

is independent of
k
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}
.

A function on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
is constant if and only if all its nonzero discrete Fourier coefficients vanish. Applying this component-wise to
f(k)
, we obtain that for every
p\neq 0
,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(k)\,\omega^{pk}=0,\qquad\omega:=e^{2\pi i/m}.

Substituting the definition of
f(k)
gives

\sum_{k}\sum_{x_{1}}\vec{n}_{x_{1},\,k-\alpha x_{1}}\,\omega^{pk}=0.

Introduce the change of variables
x_{2}=k-\alpha x_{1}
, equivalently
k=x_{2}+\alpha x_{1}
, which defines a bijection on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{2}
. The sum becomes

\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}}\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}\,\omega^{p(x_{2}+\alpha x_{1})}=\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}}\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}\,\omega^{(p\alpha)x_{1}+px_{2}}.

Define the discrete Fourier transform

\widehat{\vec{n}}_{s,t}:=\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}\,\omega^{sx_{1}+tx_{2}}.

Then parity-obliviousness implies

\widehat{\vec{n}}_{p\alpha,\,p}=0\qquad\forall\,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times},\;p\neq 0.

(S8)

Let
s,t\neq 0
. Since
m
is prime,
t
is invertible, so set
p=t
and
\alpha=st^{-1}
, giving
p\alpha=s
, and hence by Eq. (
S8
),
\widehat{\vec{n}}_{s,t}=0
.

Thus, all mixed Fourier modes vanish. The inverse transform,

\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{s,t}\widehat{\vec{n}}_{s,t}\,\omega^{-(sx_{1}+tx_{2})}.

Only modes with
s=0
or
t=0
surviving,

\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{s}\widehat{\vec{n}}_{s,0}\,\omega^{-sx_{1}}+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{t}\widehat{\vec{n}}_{0,t}\,\omega^{-tx_{2}}-\frac{\widehat{\vec{n}}_{0,0}}{m^{2}}.

Define

\vec{a}_{x_{1}}:=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{s}\widehat{\vec{n}}_{s,0}\,\omega^{-sx_{1}}-\frac{\widehat{\vec{n}}_{0,0}}{m^{2}},\qquad\vec{b}_{x_{2}}:=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{t}\widehat{\vec{n}}_{0,t}\,\omega^{-tx_{2}}.

Then

\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}},

(S9)

which completes the proof.
∎

Together, these constraints eliminate all joint dependence on
(x_{1},x_{2})
and enforce the additive structure. This is precisely the decomposition in Eq. (
S9
).

Remark
(Role of multiple masks and primality):

A single mask only constrains averages along one family of parallel hyperplanes, so it removes only part of the mixed Fourier structure; correlations that depend jointly on
(x_{1},x_{2})
can still remain. To eliminate all such mixed terms, one needs the full set of masks. In particular, for any mixed mode
(s,t)
, the mask with slope
\alpha=st^{-1}
removes it. This construction relies on the fact that every nonzero
t
has an inverse, which holds precisely when
m
is prime. If
m
is composite, some elements are not invertible, so certain mixed modes cannot be accessed by any mask, and the additive decomposition may fail.

Corollary 1

(Zero-sum gauge)
.

The decomposition can always be chosen such that

\sum_{x_{1}}\vec{a}_{x_{1}}=0,\qquad\sum_{x_{2}}\vec{b}_{x_{2}}=0.

(S10)

Proof.

The decomposition is not unique, since it is invariant under the transformation

\vec{a}_{x_{1}}\to\vec{a}_{x_{1}}-\vec{c},\qquad\vec{b}_{x_{2}}\to\vec{b}_{x_{2}}+\vec{c},

for any fixed vector
\vec{c}
. Choosing

\vec{c}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{x_{1}}\vec{a}_{x_{1}}
ensures that
\sum_{x_{1}}\vec{a}_{x_{1}}=0
.

Summing the decomposition over all inputs gives

\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}}\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=m\sum_{x_{1}}\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+m\sum_{x_{2}}\vec{b}_{x_{2}}.

Hence,

\sum_{x_{2}}\vec{b}_{x_{2}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}}\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}.

For our figure of merit, only differences between states are relevant (we will see details in the next section). Therefore, we can shift all preparations by a constant Bloch vector without affecting either parity-obliviousness or the success probability. In particular, we may choose the centroid of the ensemble to be the maximally mixed state:

\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}}\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\frac{I}{2},

which is equivalent to

\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}}\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=0.

Under this choice, it follows that

\sum_{x_{2}}\vec{b}_{x_{2}}=0,

as required by Eq. (
S10
), completing the proof.
∎

\bm{(2,3)}
case.

For
m=3
, full parity-obliviousness involves two independent masks,
(1,1)
and
(1,2)
, each defining a partition of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}
into three parity classes.

Mask
(1,1)
:
The parity
k=x_{1}+x_{2}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)
yields

\displaystyle k=0:\{(0,0),(1,2),(2,1)\},\;k=1:\{(0,1),(1,0),(2,2)\},\;k=2:\{(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)\}.

which imply

\vec{n}_{00}+\vec{n}_{12}+\vec{n}_{21}=\vec{n}_{01}+\vec{n}_{10}+\vec{n}_{22}=\vec{n}_{02}+\vec{n}_{11}+\vec{n}_{20}.

Mask
(1,2)
:

The parity
k=x_{1}+2x_{2}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)
yields

\displaystyle k=0:\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,2)\},\;k=1:\{(0,2),(1,0),(2,1)\},\;k=2:\{(0,1),(1,2),(2,0)\},

which imply

\vec{n}_{00}+\vec{n}_{11}+\vec{n}_{22}=\vec{n}_{02}+\vec{n}_{10}+\vec{n}_{21}=\vec{n}_{01}+\vec{n}_{12}+\vec{n}_{20}.

Taken together, these two independent sets of constraints eliminate all joint dependence on
(x_{1},x_{2})
and enforce the additive structure

\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}.

Imposing the zero-sum gauge,

\vec{a}_{0}+\vec{a}_{1}+\vec{a}_{2}=0,\qquad\vec{b}_{0}+\vec{b}_{1}+\vec{b}_{2}=0,

each family
\{\vec{a}_{x_{1}}\}
and
\{\vec{b}_{x_{2}}\}
forms a closed triangle in Bloch space. The nine Bloch vectors are then obtained as pairwise sums

\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}},

so the full encoding is generated by translating one triangle by the vertices of the other. This geometric picture makes explicit that all correlations arise from independent single-digit contributions, with no residual joint structure.

Sec III: Proof of (Theorem 2) qubit upper bound for the
(2,3)
POREC/PORAC task and saturating strategy

In this section, we derive the tight upper bound for qubit (
d=2
) strategies in the
(2,3)
parity-oblivious exclusion task. The same derivation applies, with a simple change of alignment, to the corresponding parity-oblivious retrieval (PORAC) task. As described in the main text, Alice receives an input string
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}
, while Bob receives an index
y\in\{1,2\}
. In the PORAC task, Bob is required to output the value of the selected digit, i.e.,
b=x_{y}
. In the POREC task, his goal is instead to output a symbol
b
that differs from
x_{y}
.

The corresponding average success probabilities for qubit (
d=2
) strategies are

P_{\mathrm{PORAC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)=\frac{1}{2\cdot 3^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}}\sum_{y=1}^{2}P(b=x_{y}\mid x,y),

(S11)

and

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)=\frac{1}{2\cdot 3^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2}}\sum_{y=1}^{2}P(b\neq x_{y}\mid x,y).

Using the Born rule
P(b=x_{y}\mid x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{x}M_{x_{y}|y}),

and noting that exclusion succeeds exactly when
b\neq x_{y}
, we rewrite

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)=1-\frac{1}{18}\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}=0}^{2}\Bigl[\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}M_{x_{1}|1})+\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}M_{x_{2}|2})\Bigr].

(S12)

Hence, introduce the marginal operators,

R_{x_{1}}:=\sum_{x_{2}}\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}},\qquad C_{x_{2}}:=\sum_{x_{1}}\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}.

Rewriting Eq. (
S12
) in terms of these marginals, we obtain

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)=1-\frac{1}{18}\Bigl[\sum_{x_{1}}\operatorname{Tr}(R_{x_{1}}M_{x_{1}|1})+\sum_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Tr}(C_{x_{2}}M_{x_{2}|2})\Bigr].

(S13)

Using the completeness relation
M_{2|y}=I-M_{0|y}-M_{1|y}
together with

\operatorname{Tr}(R_{2})=\operatorname{Tr}(C_{2})=3
, Eq. (
S13
) can be rewritten as

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{1}{18}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=0,1\\
y=1,2\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[M_{x|y}\,S_{x|y}\bigr],

(S14)

where
S_{x|1}=R_{x}-R_{2}
and
S_{x|2}=C_{x}-C_{2}
.

We now use the additive Bloch decomposition derived in Sec. II,

\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(I+(\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}})\cdot\vec{\sigma}\bigr),\qquad\sum_{x_{1}}\vec{a}_{x_{1}}=0,\quad\sum_{x_{2}}\vec{b}_{x_{2}}=0,

which gives

R_{x_{1}}-R_{2}=\frac{3}{2}(\vec{a}_{x_{1}}-\vec{a}_{2})\cdot\vec{\sigma},\qquad C_{x_{2}}-C_{2}=\frac{3}{2}(\vec{b}_{x_{2}}-\vec{b}_{2})\cdot\vec{\sigma}.

We parametrize the measurement operators as

M_{k|y}=\frac{t_{k|y}}{2}(I+\hat{m}_{k|y}\cdot\vec{\sigma}),\qquad\sum_{k}t_{k|y}=2,\quad\sum_{k}t_{k|y}\hat{m}_{k|y}=0,

(S15)

and use the identity
\operatorname{Tr}[M(\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\sigma})]=t\,\hat{m}\cdot\vec{v}.

Substituting into Eq. (
S14
), we obtain

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{1}{12}F,

(S16)

where

F=\sum_{x_{1}=0}^{2}t_{x_{1}|1}(\vec{a}_{x_{1}}\cdot\hat{m}_{x_{1}|1})+\sum_{x_{2}=0}^{2}t_{x_{2}|2}(\vec{b}_{x_{2}}\cdot\hat{m}_{x_{2}|2}).

(S17)

From Eq. (
S16
), maximizing
P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}
is equivalent to minimizing
F
.

Proposition 1
.

At the optimum, up to relabeling of outcomes, the preparation Bloch vectors can be chosen as

\vec{a}_{x_{1}}=-\lambda\,t_{x_{1}|1}\,\hat{m}_{x_{1}|1},\qquad\vec{b}_{x_{2}}=-\mu\,t_{x_{2}|2}\,\hat{m}_{x_{2}|2},

(S18)

for some constants
\lambda,\mu>0
.

Proof.

The objective function
F
is affine in both the preparations and the POVM elements. Hence the optimum is attained at extremal points of the convex set of admissible strategies, so it suffices to consider extremal POVMs.

For fixed measurement directions, each contribution
t_{x|y}(\vec{v}\cdot\hat{m}_{x|y})
is minimized when
\vec{v}
is anti-parallel to
\hat{m}_{x|y}
. Therefore, without loss of optimality, the preparation vectors can be written as

\vec{a}_{x_{1}}=-\lambda_{x_{1}}\hat{m}_{x_{1}|1},\qquad\vec{b}_{x_{2}}=-\mu_{x_{2}}\hat{m}_{x_{2}|2},

(S19)

with
\lambda_{x_{1}},\mu_{x_{2}}\geq 0
.

The constraints
\sum_{x_{1}}\vec{a}_{x_{1}}=0
and
\sum_{x_{2}}\vec{b}_{x_{2}}=0
imply

\sum_{x_{1}}\lambda_{x_{1}}\hat{m}_{x_{1}|1}=0,\qquad\sum_{x_{2}}\mu_{x_{2}}\hat{m}_{x_{2}|2}=0.

(S20)

For an extremal three-outcome qubit POVM, the effects are rank-1 and satisfy
\sum_{k}t_{k|y}\hat{m}_{k|y}=0
. This implies that the Bloch vectors
\{\hat{m}_{k|y}\}
lie in a plane and are not collinear; hence the space of linear dependencies among them is one-dimensional.

Comparing with the POVM completeness relation

\sum_{x_{1}}t_{x_{1}|1}\hat{m}_{x_{1}|1}=0,

(S21)

it follows that the coefficients must be proportional, i.e.,

\lambda_{x_{1}}=\lambda\,t_{x_{1}|1}.

(S22)

An identical argument yields

\mu_{x_{2}}=\mu\,t_{x_{2}|2}.

(S23)

Since
\lambda=\mu=0
gives
F=0
, which is strictly suboptimal, we conclude
\lambda,\mu>0
.

The preparation vectors must satisfy
|\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}|\leq 1
for all
(x_{1},x_{2})
. This couples the two sets of vectors and constrains the admissible measurement directions. The resulting geometric structure will be exploited in the proof of Theorem 2.
∎

IV
Proof of Theorem 2

We prove that
|F|\leq 2\sqrt{2}
and that this bound is tight.

Proof.

Recall

\displaystyle F=-(\lambda A+\mu B),\qquad A=\sum_{x_{1}}t_{x_{1}|1}^{2},\quad B=\sum_{x_{2}}t_{x_{2}|2}^{2},

so that
|F|=\lambda A+\mu B
.

Let
t_{*}=\max_{x_{1}}t_{x_{1}|1}
and
s_{*}=\max_{x_{2}}t_{x_{2}|2}
. Since
\sum_{x_{1}}t_{x_{1}|1}=2
and
t_{x_{1}|1}\leq t_{*}
,

\displaystyle A=\sum_{x_{1}}t_{x_{1}|1}^{2}\leq t_{*}\sum_{x_{1}}t_{x_{1}|1}=2t_{*},

with equality iff
t_{x_{1}|1}\in\{0,t_{*}\}
. Similarly
B\leq 2s_{*}
. Hence

|F|\leq 2(\lambda t_{*}+\mu s_{*}).

(S24)

Since
|F|
is convex in the weights over a compact simplex, its maximum is attained at an extreme point, i.e.,
t_{x|y}\in\{0,t_{*}\}
. It suffices to consider the saturating case.

Squaring the Bloch-sphere constraint
\lvert\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}\rvert\leq 1
and using Proposition
1
, we obtain Eq. (
S25
).

(\lambda t_{x_{1}|1})^{2}+(\mu t_{x_{2}|2})^{2}+2\lambda t_{x_{1}|1}\mu t_{x_{2}|2}\,c_{x_{1}x_{2}}\leq 1,

(S25)

where
c_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\hat{m}_{x_{1}|1}\cdot\hat{m}_{x_{2}|2}
.

Fix
x_{2}^{*}
with
t_{x_{2}^{*}|2}=s_{*}
. From the measurement constraint Eq. (
S15
),

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1}}t_{x_{1}|1}\hat{m}_{x_{1}|1}=0,

so

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1}}t_{x_{1}|1}\,c_{x_{1}x_{2}^{*}}=0.

Under saturation,
t_{x_{1}|1}=t_{*}
on the support

S:=\{x_{1}:t_{x_{1}|1}>0\}
, hence

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1}\in S}c_{x_{1}x_{2}^{*}}=0.

Therefore the coefficients cannot all be negative, and there exists

x_{1}^{\prime}\in S
such that
c_{x_{1}^{\prime}x_{2}^{*}}\geq 0
.

Evaluating Eq. (
S25
) at
(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{*})
, where

t_{x_{1}^{\prime}|1}=t_{*}
and
t_{x_{2}^{*}|2}=s_{*}
, gives

\displaystyle(\lambda t_{*})^{2}+(\mu s_{*})^{2}+2\lambda t_{*}\mu s_{*}\,c_{x_{1}^{\prime}x_{2}^{*}}\leq 1,

which implies

\displaystyle(\lambda t_{*})^{2}+(\mu s_{*})^{2}\leq 1.

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1}\in S}c_{x_{1}x_{2}^{*}}=0,\;\exists\,c_{x_{1}x_{2}^{*}}<0\;\Rightarrow\;\exists\,c_{x_{1}^{\prime}x_{2}^{*}}>0\;\Rightarrow\;(\lambda t_{*})^{2}+(\mu s_{*})^{2}<1.

Hence equality can hold only when
c_{x_{1}x_{2}^{*}}=0
on the support.

By Cauchy-Schwarz to Eq. (
S24
),

\displaystyle\lambda t_{*}+\mu s_{*}\leq\sqrt{2}\sqrt{(\lambda t_{*})^{2}+(\mu s_{*})^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2},

and therefore

\displaystyle|F|\leq 2\sqrt{2}.

∎

Saturation of the bound.

The bound
|F|\leq 2\sqrt{2}
is tight. Saturation of
A\leq 2t_{*}
and
B\leq 2s_{*}
requires

t_{x_{1}|1}\in\{0,t_{*}\}
and
t_{x_{2}|2}\in\{0,s_{*}\}
, so the weights are uniform on their supports; for qubits this corresponds to
t_{0|y}=t_{1|y}=1
and
t_{2|y}=0
, i.e., only two outcomes contribute and the third is null. Together with Eq. (
S15
), this enforces
(\lambda t_{*})^{2}+(\mu s_{*})^{2}=1
and vanishing overlaps on the support,
c_{x_{1}x_{2}}=0
. Hence the bound is achieved by two incompatible measurements with orthogonal Bloch directions, e.g., mutually orthogonal Pauli measurements. Saturation can also be realized with asymmetric choices (such as a suitably aligned trine and a projective POVM), but we focus on this symmetric projective construction.

From the zero-sum constraints, the third Bloch vectors are fixed as

\displaystyle\vec{a}_{2}

\displaystyle=-\vec{a}_{0}-\vec{a}_{1}=0,

\displaystyle\vec{b}_{2}

\displaystyle=-\vec{b}_{0}-\vec{b}_{1}=0.

Consider the additive decomposition
\vec{n}_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}
and choose

\displaystyle\vec{a}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hat{z}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad\vec{a}_{1}=\frac{\hat{z}}{\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle\vec{b}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hat{x}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad\vec{b}_{1}=\frac{\hat{x}}{\sqrt{2}}.

The corresponding three-outcome POVMs are

\displaystyle M_{0|1}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}+\vec{\sigma}\cdot\hat{z}),\quad M_{1|1}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}-\vec{\sigma}\cdot\hat{z}),\quad M_{2|1}=0,

\displaystyle M_{0|2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}+\vec{\sigma}\cdot\hat{x}),\quad M_{1|2}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}-\vec{\sigma}\cdot\hat{x}),\quad M_{2|2}=0,

which satisfy
\sum_{b}M_{b|y}=\mathbb{I}
. The Bloch directions are

\displaystyle\hat{m}_{0|1}

\displaystyle=\hat{z},\quad\hat{m}_{1|1}=-\hat{z},

\displaystyle\hat{m}_{0|2}

\displaystyle=\hat{x},\quad\hat{m}_{1|2}=-\hat{x}.

Substituting into

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{1}}t_{x_{1}|1}\,\vec{a}_{x_{1}}\cdot\hat{m}_{x_{1}|1}+\sum_{x_{2}}t_{x_{2}|2}\,\vec{b}_{x_{2}}\cdot\hat{m}_{x_{2}|2},

with
t_{0|y}=t_{1|y}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}=-2\sqrt{2}.

Substituting into

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{3}-\tfrac{1}{12}F

gives

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}.

For the PORAC task, using Eq. (
S11
), the same construction applies with parallel (rather than anti-parallel) alignment, which flips the sign of
F
. The bound is thus attained at
F=+2\sqrt{2}
, yielding

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{PORAC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}.

V
Sec IV: Proof of (Theorem 3),
(2,m)
POREC qubit bound under two-outcome projective measurements

As discussed in the main text, Alice receives an input

x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{2}
, and Bob receives a choice

y\in\{1,2\}
. In the PORAC task, Bob must output
b=x_{y}
, whereas in the POREC task he must output any symbol different from
x_{y}
.

The corresponding average success probability for qubit strategies is

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)=\frac{1}{2m^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{2}}\sum_{y=1}^{2}P(b\neq x_{y}\mid x,y).

(S26)

Using the Born rule
P(b=x_{y}\mid x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{x}M_{x_{y}|y})
and noting that exclusion succeeds iff
b\neq x_{y}
, Eq. (
S26
) becomes

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2m^{2}}\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}}\Big[\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}M_{x_{1}|1})+\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}M_{x_{2}|2})\Big].

Define the marginals

R_{x_{1}}:=\sum_{x_{2}}\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}},\qquad C_{x_{2}}:=\sum_{x_{1}}\rho_{x_{1}x_{2}}.

(S27)

Reordering the sums yields

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2m^{2}}\left[\sum_{x_{1}}\operatorname{Tr}(R_{x_{1}}M_{x_{1}|1})+\sum_{x_{2}}\operatorname{Tr}(C_{x_{2}}M_{x_{2}|2})\right].

Using POVM completeness,

M_{m-1|y}=I-\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}M_{k|y},

(S28)

we obtain

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)

\displaystyle=\frac{m-1}{m}-\frac{1}{2m^{2}}\Bigg[\sum_{x_{1}=0}^{m-2}\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(M_{x_{1}|1}(R_{x_{1}}-R_{m-1})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{x_{2}=0}^{m-2}\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(M_{x_{2}|2}(C_{x_{2}}-C_{m-1})\right)\Bigg].

For
n=2
and prime
m
, parity-obliviousness enforces an additive Bloch decomposition (see Sec. II), giving

R_{x_{1}}-R_{m-1}=\frac{m}{2}(\vec{a}_{x_{1}}-\vec{a}_{m-1})\cdot\vec{\sigma},\quad C_{x_{2}}-C_{m-1}=\frac{m}{2}(\vec{b}_{x_{2}}-\vec{b}_{m-1})\cdot\vec{\sigma}.

(S29)

Substituting Eq. (
S29
),

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(d=2)

\displaystyle=\frac{m-1}{m}-\frac{1}{4m}\Bigg[\sum_{x_{1}=0}^{m-2}\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(M_{x_{1}|1}(\vec{a}_{x_{1}}-\vec{a}_{m-1})\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{x_{2}=0}^{m-2}\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(M_{x_{2}|2}(\vec{b}_{x_{2}}-\vec{b}_{m-1})\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right)\Bigg].

Specializing to two-outcome projective measurements,

M_{0|y}=\tfrac{1}{2}(I+\hat{m}_{y}\cdot\vec{\sigma}),\quad M_{1|y}=\tfrac{1}{2}(I-\hat{m}_{y}\cdot\vec{\sigma}),\quad M_{i|y}=0\;\;(i\geq 2),

(S30)

the expression simplifies to

P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{Q}(d=2)=\frac{m-1}{m}-\frac{1}{4m}\Big[\hat{m}_{1}\cdot(\vec{a}_{0}-\vec{a}_{1})+\hat{m}_{2}\cdot(\vec{b}_{0}-\vec{b}_{1})\Big].

(S31)

From Eq. (
S31
), maximizing
P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{Q}
is equivalent to minimizing

F:=\hat{m}_{1}\cdot(\vec{a}_{0}-\vec{a}_{1})+\hat{m}_{2}\cdot(\vec{b}_{0}-\vec{b}_{1}).

(S32)

From Eq. (
S32
), define

\displaystyle\tilde{A}:=|\vec{a}_{0}-\vec{a}_{1}|,\qquad\tilde{B}:=|\vec{b}_{0}-\vec{b}_{1}|.

Using
|\hat{m}_{1}|=|\hat{m}_{2}|=1
, Eq. (
S32
) implies
F\geq-\bigl(\tilde{A}+\tilde{B}\bigr),
with equality when
\hat{m}_{1}
and
\hat{m}_{2}
are anti-parallel to the respective differences.

To bound
\tilde{A}^{2}+\tilde{B}^{2}
, we use the Bloch-sphere constraint

|\vec{a}_{x_{1}}+\vec{b}_{x_{2}}|\leq 1
, which holds for all
(x_{1},x_{2})
and in particular for
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\{0,1\}^{2}
:

\displaystyle|\vec{a}_{0}+\vec{b}_{0}|^{2}+|\vec{a}_{0}+\vec{b}_{1}|^{2}+|\vec{a}_{1}+\vec{b}_{0}|^{2}+|\vec{a}_{1}+\vec{b}_{1}|^{2}\leq 4.

Applying the parallelogram identity twice gives

\displaystyle 4\left|\frac{\vec{a}_{0}+\vec{a}_{1}+\vec{b}_{0}+\vec{b}_{1}}{2}\right|^{2}+|\vec{a}_{0}-\vec{a}_{1}|^{2}+|\vec{b}_{0}-\vec{b}_{1}|^{2}\leq 4.

Dropping the non-negative first term yields

\tilde{A}^{2}+\tilde{B}^{2}\leq 4.

(S33)

By Cauchy-Schwarz and Eq. (
S33
),

\displaystyle\tilde{A}+\tilde{B}\leq\sqrt{2(\tilde{A}^{2}+\tilde{B}^{2})}\leq 2\sqrt{2},

and hence
F\geq-2\sqrt{2}.

Substituting this into Eq. (
S31
) gives

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{POREC}}^{Q,\mathrm{Proj}}(d=2)\leq\frac{m-1}{m}+\frac{1}{m\sqrt{2}}.

The bound is saturated by binary projective measurements along orthogonal Bloch directions, e.g.
\hat{m}_{1}=\hat{z}
and
\hat{m}_{2}=\hat{x}
, with
\vec{a}_{0}=-\hat{z}/\sqrt{2}
,
\vec{a}_{1}=\hat{z}/\sqrt{2}
,
\vec{b}_{0}=-\hat{x}/\sqrt{2}
, and
\vec{b}_{1}=\hat{x}/\sqrt{2}
.
