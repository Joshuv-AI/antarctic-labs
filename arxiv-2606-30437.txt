The massless Boltzmann equation in Minkowski spacetime
Ho Lee, Ernesto Nungesser, John Stalker, Paul Tod

Abstract
We study the spatially homogeneous, massless Boltzmann equation in Minkowski spacetime for a certain range of hard and soft interactions. For hard interactions, we derive a Povzner-type inequality for massless particles and show that solutions exist for all time into the future. For soft interactions, we employ singular weights to control singularities at p = 0 p=0 , which arise from the masslessness of particles, to obtain local existence. These results, which are among rather few proofs of existence for the massless Boltzmann equation, are motivated by our earlier work on the massless Einstein–Boltzmann system in certain cosmological settings.

, are those in
[

In this paper, we study the massless Boltzmann equation on a fixed Minkowski background but will allow for a wider range of collision cross-sections:

\displaystyle\sigma=\varrho^{\gamma},\qquad-2<\gamma\leq 1,

where
\varrho
denotes the relative momentum of two colliding particles (see (
2
) for its definition), and which contains both hard (
\gamma\geq 0
) and soft (
\gamma<0
) interactions. The case of a constant cross-section corresponds to
\gamma=0
.

Our interest in massless particles comes from the fact that in cosmology, near the initial singularity, massive particles are expected to behave like massless ones in the sense that the ratio of the pressure to the energy density tends to
1/3
, which corresponds to the massless case. Also, as a general rule, rest mass can be neglected if it is small compared to energy. However, existing literature on the massless Boltzmann equation is surprisingly scarce. There are several reasons for this scarcity. First, most available literature concerns the non-relativistic Boltzmann equation, and it is unnatural to consider massless particles. Second, the energy of massive particles is bounded away from zero, but this boundedness does not hold for massless particles. This introduces the extra complication that the energy of a massless particle can be arbitrarily small, which introduces a singularity in the collision term at
p=0
. Also the distribution function is now supported on the light cone, which is not a smooth submanifold of the spacetime cotangent bundle, rather than on the mass hyperboloid, which is. For these reasons, existence proofs for the massless Boltzmann or Einstein–Boltzmann system call for a new start – one cannot just carry over proofs from the massive case.

In the present paper we seek to fill that gap by proving local existence for the massless Boltzmann equation for both soft and hard interactions. In the latter case we are able to prove global existence. It is important to note that our results should not to be confused with results concerning the stability of Minkowski spacetime, since here our background, Minkowski spacetime, is fixed and not coupled to the Einstein equations. In other words we are considering only the special relativistic case.

1.1
The massless Boltzmann equation

Let us consider the relativistic Boltzmann equation with massless particles. By the mass shell condition the massless particles with momentum
p_{\alpha}
satisfy

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}=-m^{2}=0,

so that we obtain

\displaystyle p^{0}=\sqrt{m^{2}+|p|^{2}}=|p|,

(1)

where
p
denotes the three-dimensional vector
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
, and
|\cdot|
is the usual Euclidean norm. The relative momentum
\varrho\geq 0
of two colliding particles with momenta
p_{\alpha}
and
q_{\alpha}
is defined by

\displaystyle\varrho^{2}:=(p_{\alpha}-q_{\alpha})(p^{\alpha}-q^{\alpha})=2(p^{0}q^{0}-p\cdot q).

(2)

In the case of massive particles we need to introduce a quantity
s
, called the total energy, but in the massless case we have
\varrho=\sqrt{s}
. We assume that the collision cross-section does not depend on the scattering angle, and the spatially homogeneous, massless Boltzmann equation reads

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f(p^{\prime})f(q^{\prime})-f(p)f(q))\,d\omega\,d^{3}q.

(3)

We will consider the case of both hard and soft interactions:

\displaystyle-2<\gamma\leq 1,

but the analysis will be separated into the cases of hard
(\gamma\geq 0)
and soft
(\gamma<0)
interactions. For the post-collision momentum we write

\displaystyle n^{0}=p^{0}+q^{0},\qquad n=p+q,

to have

\displaystyle p^{\prime 0}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{0}}{2}+\frac{n\cdot\omega}{2},

(4)

\displaystyle q^{\prime 0}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{0}}{2}-\frac{n\cdot\omega}{2},

(5)

and

\displaystyle p^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}+\frac{\varrho}{2}\left(\omega+\frac{(n\cdot\omega)n}{\varrho(n^{0}+\varrho)}\right),

(6)

\displaystyle q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}-\frac{\varrho}{2}\left(\omega+\frac{(n\cdot\omega)n}{\varrho(n^{0}+\varrho)}\right),

(7)

for
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
. The above parametrization was used in
[
24
]
, which can be derived from
[
31
]
by taking
\sqrt{s}=\varrho
. By energy conservation we have

\displaystyle n^{\prime 0}=n^{0},

(8)

where
n^{\prime 0}=p^{\prime 0}+q^{\prime 0}
, and the change of variables between
(p,q)
and
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{0}q^{0}}\,d\omega\,d^{3}p\,d^{3}q=\frac{1}{p^{\prime 0}q^{\prime 0}}\,d\omega\,d^{3}p^{\prime}\,d^{3}q^{\prime}.

(9)

The following are well-known facts for the relativistic Boltzmann equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f\,d^{3}p=0,\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}fp^{0}d^{3}p=0,

(10)

which hold also for the massless Boltzmann equation.

1.2
Main results

In this part we state our main results. We define the following weighted
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
norms:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1}_{r}}=\int f(p)(p^{0})^{r}d^{3}p,\qquad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{w}}=\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|w(p)f(p)|,\qquad w(p)=p^{0}e^{p^{0}}.

Then, we obtain the following results. In Theorem
1
, we obtain global existence for hard interactions. In Theorem
2
, we only obtain local existence for soft interactions. The proof will be given in Sections
3.3
and
3.4
, respectively.

Theorem 1
.

Let
0\leq f_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be an initial datum of the Boltzmann equation (
3
) with
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
. Then, there exists a unique non-negative solution
f\in C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
. If, in addition,
f_{0}\in L^{1}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
r\geq 1
, then for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{r}}\leq C,

(11)

where
C>0
depends on
T
and
r
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
0\leq f_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{-2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}_{w}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be an initial datum of the Boltzmann equation (
3
) with
-2<\gamma<0
. Then, there exists a
T>0
such that the Boltzmann equation (
3
) has a unique non-negative solution
f\in C^{1}([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{-2}}+\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{w}})\leq C,

(12)

where
C>0
depends on
T
.

Remark 1
.

In
[
15
]
, massless particles were considered in a fixed homogeneous and isotropic cosmological background with a scale factor
R\sim t^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The main argument was to use the monotonicity and integrability of
R^{-3-\gamma}
, which led to global existence for
-1<\gamma<2
. However, this argument is no longer valid in the Minkowski case, which would correspond to
R=1
. For hard interactions, we instead use a Povzner-type inequality to obtain global existence for
0\leq\gamma<1
. For soft interactions, we only obtain local existence, but for a wider range of collision cross-sections:
-2<\gamma<0
.

2
Basic estimates

We collect basic estimates. Lemma
1
gives a different expression for the relative momentum of massless particles. Lemma
2
is a Povzner-type inequality for massless particles. This will be used to obtain global existence for hard interactions. Such a Povzner-type inequality is unavailable for soft interactions, but the estimates in Lemma
3
will be used to control the singularity of the relative momentum.

Lemma 1
.

The relative momentum
\varrho
is a collisional invariant and can be written as

\displaystyle\varrho=\sqrt{(n^{0})^{2}-|n|^{2}}=2\sqrt{p^{0}q^{0}}\sin\frac{\phi}{2},

(13)

where
\phi
is the angle between the three-dimensional vectors
p
and
q
.

Proof.

We refer to
[
24
]
for the proof.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
p^{\prime 0}
and
q^{\prime 0}
be the post-collision momenta for given
p,q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
. Then, we have for
r\geq 1
,

\displaystyle(p^{\prime 0})^{r}+(q^{\prime 0})^{r}-(p^{0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}\leq C((p^{0})^{r-1}q^{0}+p^{0}(q^{0})^{r-1}),

(14)

for some
C>0
which depends on
r
.
Moreover, there exists
d_{r}>0
such that if
\omega
is restricted to
|n\cdot\omega|\leq d_{r}|n|
, then we have

\displaystyle(p^{\prime 0})^{r}+(q^{\prime 0})^{r}-(p^{0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C((p^{0})^{r-1}q^{0}+p^{0}(q^{0})^{r-1})-\left(1-\frac{2(1+d_{r})^{r}}{2^{r}}\right)((p^{0})^{r}+(q^{0})^{r}),

(15)

where
1-2(1+d_{r})^{r}/2^{r}>0
.

Proof.

The proof is almost the same as the one in
[
25
]
. We suppose that
p
and
q
are given and notice that the left hand side of (
14
) attains its maximum when
\omega
is parallel to
n
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle(p^{\prime 0})^{r}+(q^{\prime 0})^{r}-(p^{0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n^{0}}{2}+\frac{n\cdot\omega}{2}\right)^{r}+\left(\frac{n^{0}}{2}-\frac{n\cdot\omega}{2}\right)^{r}-(p^{0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{n^{0}+|n|}{2}\right)^{r}+\left(\frac{n^{0}-|n|}{2}\right)^{r}-(p^{0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{n^{0}+|n|}{2}\right)^{r}-\max\Big((p^{0})^{r},(q^{0})^{r}\Big),

where we used the fact that
(n^{0}-|n|)/2
is smaller than both
p^{0}
and
q^{0}
. Since
(n^{0}+|n|)/2\leq n^{0}
, we have

\displaystyle(p^{\prime 0})^{r}+(q^{\prime 0})^{r}-(p^{0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}

\displaystyle\leq(p^{0}+q^{0})^{r}-\max\Big((p^{0})^{r},(q^{0})^{r}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(p^{0})^{r}+(q^{0})^{r}+C_{r}((p^{0})^{r-1}q^{0}+p^{0}(q^{0})^{r-1})-\max\Big((p^{0})^{r},(q^{0})^{r}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{r}((p^{0})^{r-1}q^{0}+p^{0}(q^{0})^{r-1}),

for some
C_{r}>0
. For the second result we notice that

\displaystyle p^{\prime 0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{0}}{2}+\frac{|n\cdot\omega|}{2}\leq\frac{n^{0}}{2}+\frac{d_{r}|n|}{2}\leq\frac{(1+d_{r})n^{0}}{2},

and have the same estimate for
q^{\prime 0}
. Now, we obtain

\displaystyle(p^{\prime 0})^{r}+(q^{\prime 0})^{r}-(p^{0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(1+d_{r})^{r}(n^{0})^{r}}{2^{r}}-(p^{0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(1+d_{r})^{r}}{2^{r}}((p^{0})^{r}+(q^{0})^{r}+C_{r}((p^{0})^{r-1}q^{0}+p^{0}(q^{0})^{r-1}))-(p^{0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r}((p^{0})^{r-1}q^{0}+p^{0}(q^{0})^{r-1})-\left(1-\frac{2(1+d_{r})^{r}}{2^{r}}\right)((p^{0})^{r}+(q^{0})^{r}).

Now, we can choose a small
d_{r}
so that the factor
(1-2(1+d_{r})^{r}/2^{r})
is strictly positive.
∎

In the case of
r=2
one can obtain the result of Lemma
2
by direct calculation:

\displaystyle(p^{\prime 0})^{2}+(q^{\prime 0})^{2}-(p^{0})^{2}-(q^{0})^{2}\leq 2p^{0}q^{0},

(16)

as in
[
24
]
.

Lemma 3
.

The post-collision momenta satisfy for any
a>0
the following:

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{1}{p^{\prime 0}}\,d\omega=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{1}{q^{\prime 0}}\,d\omega\leq\frac{C}{\varrho^{a}(n^{0})^{1-a}},

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{1}{(p^{\prime 0})^{2}}\,d\omega=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{1}{(q^{\prime 0})^{2}}\,d\omega=\frac{16\pi}{\varrho^{2}},

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{1}{p^{\prime 0}q^{\prime 0}}\,d\omega\leq\frac{C}{\varrho^{a}(n^{0})^{2-a}},

where
C>0
depends on
a
.

Proof.

We refer to
[
15
,
24
]
for the proof.
∎

3
Proof of the main results

In this paper we consider collision cross-sections of the type:

\displaystyle\sigma=\varrho^{\gamma},\qquad-2<\gamma\leq 1.

They are not bounded for both hard and soft interactions. We follow the standard approach to cut off the collision cross-sections. For hard interactions, we consider

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=Q^{\mathrm{hard}}_{n}(f,f),

(17)

where
Q_{n}^{\mathrm{hard}}
is the collision operator with cut-off defined by

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{\mathrm{hard}}(f,f)=\iint_{\varrho\leq n}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f(p^{\prime})f(q^{\prime})-f(p)f(q))\,d\omega\,d^{3}q,\qquad 0\leq\gamma\leq 1.

(18)

For soft interactions, we consider

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=Q^{\mathrm{soft}}_{n}(f,f),

(19)

with cut-off defined by

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{\mathrm{soft}}(f,f)=\iint_{\varrho\geq n^{-1}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f(p^{\prime})f(q^{\prime})-f(p)f(q))\,d\omega\,d^{3}q,\qquad-2<\gamma<0.

(20)

The collision kernels are now bounded, so that the modified equations (
17
) with (
18
) and (
19
) with (
20
) admit global solutions in
L^{1}
by standard arguments (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
26
]
). Below, in Sections
3.1
and
3.3
, we consider
L^{1}_{r}
with
r>0
to obtain global existence for hard interactions. In Sections
3.2
and
3.4
, we consider
L^{1}_{r}\cap L^{\infty}_{w}
with
r<0
for soft interactions, and will obtain local existence.

3.1
Existence for hard interactions with cut-off

The modified equation (
17
) with (
18
) has global, non-negative solutions in
L^{1}
. Let
f_{n}
be the solution of (
17
) with (
18
). We need to show that the
f_{n}
are uniformly bounded with respect to
n
. It is clear that the
f_{n}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{1}
and
L^{1}_{1}
as in (
10
). We consider the space
L^{1}_{r}
with
r\geq 1
. We first multiply equation (
17
) by
(p^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int f_{n}(p)(p^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\iiint_{\varrho\leq n}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)((p^{\prime 0})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}+(q^{\prime 0})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}-(p^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}-(q^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}})\,d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p.

Applying Lemma
2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int f_{n}(p)(p^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iiint_{\varrho\leq n}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)(p^{0})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}q^{0}\,d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)(p^{0})^{\gamma}(q^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{\gamma}}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}.

Since
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
, the quantity
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{\gamma}}
is bounded. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq C\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}},

which shows that the
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}
are uniformly bounded on any interval
[0,T]
. Now, we multiply (
17
) by
(p^{0})^{r}
for
r\geq 1
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int f_{n}(p)(p^{0})^{r}\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iiint_{\varrho\leq n}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)(p^{0})^{r-1}q^{0}\,d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)(p^{0})^{r-1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(q^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{r-1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}.

Since
0\leq r-1+\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq r
, we conclude that the
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{r}}
are uniformly bounded on any interval
[0,T]
. Hence, we obtain the following lemma:

.

For any initial datum
0\leq f_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, equation (
17
) for hard interactions with cut-off (

) has a unique non-negative solution
f_{n}\in C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
. If, in addition,
f_{0}\in L^{1}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with

, then for any

,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f_{n}(t)\|_{L^{1}_{r}}\leq C,

where
C>0
depends on
T
and

.

, the modified equation (

) has global, non-negative solutions in

. In the case of soft interactions we need to show that the

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int f_{n}(p)\frac{1}{(p^{0})^{2}}\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\iiint_{\varrho\geq n^{-1}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)\left(\frac{1}{(p^{\prime 0})^{2}}+\frac{1}{(q^{\prime 0})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(p^{0})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(q^{0})^{2}}\right)d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p.

3
with the above estimate in

, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int f_{n}(p)\frac{1}{(p^{0})^{2}}\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{\varrho\geq n^{-1}}\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{(p^{0}q^{0})^{2}}e^{-p^{0}}e^{-q^{0}}d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\frac{\sin^{\gamma}(\frac{\phi}{2})}{(p^{0}q^{0})^{2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}e^{-p^{0}}e^{-q^{0}}d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

where we used (

. Hence, we conclude that the

on the time interval
[0,T]
, which we obtained above. Hence, we obtain the following lemma:

.

For any initial datum

, the equation (

) for soft interactions with cut-off (

) has a unique non-negative solution

. If, in addition,

Proof of Theorem

We restate Theorem

Theorem 1
.

Let
0\leq f_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be an initial datum of the Boltzmann equation (
3
) with
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
. Then, there exists a unique non-negative solution
f\in C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
. If, in addition,
f_{0}\in L^{1}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
r\geq 1
, then for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{r}}\leq C,

(21)

where
C>0
depends on
T
and
r
.

Proof.

We need to show that the solutions
f_{n}
of Lemma
4
converge to the solution of the original Boltzmann equation (
3
) as
n\to\infty
. We will show that the sequence
f_{n}
converges in
L^{1}\cap L^{1}_{1}
. Let
k<n
, and consider

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(f_{k}-f_{n})}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\iint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq k\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}(p^{\prime})f_{k}(q^{\prime})-f_{k}(p)f_{k}(q))\,d\omega\,d^{3}q

\displaystyle\quad-\iint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{n}(p^{\prime})f_{n}(q^{\prime})-f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q))\,d\omega\,d^{3}q,

where
\mathds{1}
denotes the indicator function. We write the above as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(f_{k}-f_{n})}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\iint\mathds{1}_{\{k\leq\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}(p^{\prime})f_{k}(q^{\prime})-f_{k}(p)f_{k}(q))\,d\omega\,d^{3}q

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt+\iint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}(p^{\prime})f_{k}(q^{\prime})-f_{k}(p)f_{k}(q)-f_{n}(p^{\prime})f_{n}(q^{\prime})+f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q))\,d\omega\,d^{3}q.

Multiplying the above by
\mbox{sgn}(f_{k}-f_{n})(p)
and
(p^{0})^{r}
with
r=0,1
, and integrating over
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{k\leq\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}(p^{\prime})f_{k}(q^{\prime})+f_{k}(p)f_{k}(q))(p^{0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}\Big(|f_{k}-f_{n}|(p^{\prime})(f_{k}+f_{n})(q^{\prime})+(f_{k}+f_{n})(p^{\prime})|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-|f_{k}-f_{n}|(p)(f_{k}+f_{n})(q)+(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)\Big)(p^{0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle=\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{k\leq\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{k}(p)f_{k}(q)((p^{0})^{r}+(p^{\prime 0})^{r})\,d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)((p^{\prime 0})^{r}+(q^{\prime 0})^{r}-(q^{0})^{r}+(p^{0})^{r})\,d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{k\leq\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{k}(p)f_{k}(q)(p^{0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

(22)

\displaystyle\quad+\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)(p^{0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

(23)

where we used (
8
) for
r=1
. The quantity in (
22
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{k\leq\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{k}(p)f_{k}(q)(p^{0})^{r}\,d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{k}\iint\frac{\varrho^{3+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{k}(p)f_{k}(q)(p^{0})^{r}\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{k}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{1}_{r+\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{1}_{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{k},

since the quantities
\|f_{k}\|_{L^{1}_{r+(1+\gamma)/2}}
and
\|f_{k}\|_{L^{1}_{(1+\gamma)/2}}
are bounded by Lemma
4
for
r=0,1
. The quantity in (
23
) is estimated as

\displaystyle\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq n\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)(p^{0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iint(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)(p^{0})^{r+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(q^{0})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\|f_{k}+f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{r+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}.

The last quantity can be estimated as the sum of
\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{1}}
since
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
. We combine the above results to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}}+\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{1}}\right)\leq C\left(k^{-1}+\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}}+\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{1}}\right).

This shows that the sequence
f_{k}
converges in
L^{1}\cap L^{1}_{1}
as
k\to\infty
on each interval
[0,T]
, so that the solution exists globally in time. The property (
21
) can be obtained as in Lemma
4
, and this completes the proof.
∎

3.4
Proof of Theorem
2

In this part we show that the solutions
f_{n}
of Lemma
5
converge to the solution of the original Boltzmann equation (
3
) for
-2<\gamma<0
. In
[
15
]
, the massless Boltzmann equation was considered in a cosmological setting with a restricted range of the collision cross-section by
\gamma>-1
. This restriction was necessary to obtain the integrability of
R^{-3-\gamma}
, which leads to global existence. In a different cosmological setting, the massless Boltzmann equation was studied in
[
24
]
, restricting the collision cross-section to
\gamma<-1
to avoid the singularity at
t=0
. In this paper, for soft interactions, we will only consider local existence and have no singularity at
t=0
, so the restrictions of
[
15
,
24
]
do not apply to our case. We obtain local existence for
-2<\gamma<0
in Theorem
2
, which we restated below.

Theorem 2
.

Let
0\leq f_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{-2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}_{w}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be an initial datum of the Boltzmann equation (
3
) with
-2<\gamma<0
. Then, there exists a
T>0
such that the Boltzmann equation (
3
) has a unique non-negative solution
f\in C^{1}([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{-2}}+\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{w}})\leq C,

(24)

where
C>0
depends on
T
.

Proof.

In this theorem we will show that the sequence
f_{n}
converges in
L^{1}\cap L^{1}_{-1}
. We follow the computation in the proof of Theorem
1
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{r}}\leq J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}+J_{5},

where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\iiint\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)(p^{\prime 0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\iiint\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)(q^{\prime 0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\iiint\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)(p^{0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle=\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq k^{-1}\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)(p^{\prime 0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

\displaystyle J_{5}

\displaystyle=\iiint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq k^{-1}\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)(p^{0})^{r}d\omega\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

for
r=0,-1
. We first choose
-1<\delta<0
satisfying

\displaystyle-2<\gamma<\delta<0,\qquad-2<\gamma+\delta<0,

and consider Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle(p^{0}+q^{0})^{1+\delta}\geq c(p^{0})^{\frac{-\gamma+\delta}{2}}(q^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}}.

(25)

Then,
J_{1}
is estimated as follows. For
r=0
, we have

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint(p^{0}q^{0})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}}+\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1}}),

since
-2<\gamma<0
. For
r=-1
, we apply Lemma
3
and use Young’s inequality (
25
) to obtain

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma+\delta}}{p^{0}q^{0}(p^{0}+q^{0})^{1+\delta}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma+\delta}}{p^{0}q^{0}(p^{0})^{\frac{-\gamma+\delta}{2}}(q^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\frac{(p^{0}q^{0})^{1+\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}}}{(p^{0})^{1+\frac{-\gamma+\delta}{2}}(q^{0})^{2+\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\frac{(p^{0})^{\gamma}}{q^{0}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p,

\displaystyle\leq C\|f_{k}+f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{\gamma}}\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1}},

since
-2<\gamma<0
. The estimate of
J_{2}
is the same as
J_{1}
:

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq C(\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}}+\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1}}),

for
r=0,-1
. The estimate of
J_{3}
for
r=0
is the same as
J_{1}
:

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq C(\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}}+\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1}}),

but for
r=-1
, we estimate as follows:

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{(p^{0})^{2}q^{0}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\frac{(q^{0})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}{(p^{0})^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}(f_{k}+f_{n})(p)|f_{k}-f_{n}|(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq C\|f_{k}+f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}}+\|f_{k}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1}}).

For
J_{4}
, we obtain for
r=0
,

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq k^{-1}\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{p^{0}q^{0}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{-2-\gamma}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1}}^{2}.

For
r=-1
, we follow the above computation of the estimate of
J_{1}
to obtain

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq k^{-1}\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma+\delta}}{(p^{0})^{1+\frac{-\gamma+\delta}{2}}(q^{0})^{2+\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{-2-\gamma-\delta}\iint\frac{1}{(p^{0})^{1+\frac{-\gamma+\delta}{2}}(q^{0})^{2+\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{-2-\gamma-\delta}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1-\frac{-\gamma+\delta}{2}}}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-2-\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{-2-\gamma-\delta},

where the weighted norms of
f_{n}
are bounded by Lemma
5
, since

\displaystyle-2<-1+\frac{\gamma}{2}<-1+\frac{\gamma-\delta}{2}<-1,

\displaystyle-2<-2-\frac{\gamma+\delta}{2}<-1.

The estimate of
J_{5}
with
r=0
is the same as
J_{4}
:

\displaystyle J_{5}\leq Ck^{-2-\gamma},

and for
r=-1
, we have

\displaystyle J_{5}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\leq k^{-1}\}}\frac{\varrho^{2+\gamma}}{(p^{0})^{2}q^{0}}f_{n}(p)f_{n}(q)\,d^{3}q\,d^{3}p

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{-2-\gamma}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-2}}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{-1}}.

We combine the above estimates and apply Lemma
5
to conclude that the sequence
f_{k}
converges and there exists a solution
f
in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
on a time interval
[0,T]
. The property (
24
) can be obtained by following the calculation in the proof of Lemma
5
, and this completes the proof.
∎
