Robust Multi-step Model Predictive Control with Feasibility Guarantees
Sebastian Steffen sebastian.steffen@eng.ox.ac.uk Mark Cannon mark.cannon@eng.ox.ac.uk

Abstract
We present a method of ensuring recursive feasibility and stability for robust nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) with multi-step predictors. Although feasibility guarantees are well-established for the case of single-step models applied recursively over a finite horizon, such guarantees are missing in naive MPC formulations that use distinct multi-step models to predict the system state at different future points in time. This issue arises because of potential inconsistencies in multi-step predictions generated at different times. Our approach checks for possible infeasibility of the robust nonlinear MPC optimisation problem a priori , and uses information from previous solutions to provide a fall-back based on previously verified reachable sets. We illustrate the proposed predictor-substitution strategy with a simple numerical example.



I
Introduction

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a widely used control strategy based on optimising the predicted future behaviour of a system. MPC is applicable to problems with state and input constraints, external disturbances and model uncertainty
[
12
,
7
]
. At each discrete time-step, a constrained receding horizon optimal control problem (RHOCP) is solved to find the control sequence that minimises a performance criterion over a future horizon. Nonlinear MPC has found applications across a range of industrial processes and engineering systems due to the potential for nonlinear models to accurately predict system evolution
[
13
,
12
]
.
Nonlinear models present additional challenges over linear MPC however, including increased online computational load since the RHOCP is in general a nonconvex nonlinear program (NLP), and the need for more sophisticated tools to ensure robustness by propagating the effects of disturbances and model uncertainty through nonlinear dynamics.

A large body of work has investigated various ways to address these issues. A common approach reformulates the optimal control problem as a sequential convex program (SCP), with the NLP typically approximated using local linearisations around candidate trajectories
[
3
]
. The convexified problem (often a QP) is then iterated by linearising using trajectories generated using previous solutions
[
11
,
9
]
. These approaches require a method of propagating model uncertainty and approximation errors across subsequent time-steps in the prediction horizon. In our recent work
[
17
]
, we propose a nonlinear MPC scheme using learnt multi-step predictors. The use of distinct functions for each of the predicted steps allows for a simpler formulation of the SCP, which
only needs to account for linearisation errors at individual future time-steps rather than bounding their accumulation over a horizon.

An additional advantage of multi-step models emerges when they are derived from data rather than analytically. For the linear case, recent comparisons of single-step and multi-step models show that when models are misspecified due to partial observability, learnt multi-step models show lower compounding error
[
15
]
, and better data-efficiency during learning
[
16
]
. In our previous work, we found that multi-step predictions using deep neural networks provided lower mean average error than recursive application of a single-step model
[
17
]
.

Fig. 1:
Comparison of predictor substitution method for recovering recursive feasibility in multi-step MPC despite inconsistent tube sections across optimisation steps, compared to standard tube MPC.

The use of linear multi-step predictors in MPC is well-established.
In
[
18
]
, the authors propose a linear multi-rate MPC scheme using an autoregressive model with exogenous input (ARX) structure.
The approach partitions the prediction horizon into blocks corresponding to multi-rate models, and in contrast to standard receding-horizon MPC, an entire block of inputs is applied before re-computation. In
[
14
]
, this multi-rate approach is extended to state-space models with parametric uncertainty and combined with homothetic tubes to ensure robust constraint satisfaction. Multi-step models are identified from data in
[
1
]

and used in stochastic MPC with chance constraints. Likewise
[
5
]
considers stochastic MPC, but employs a probabilistic multi-step identification method which implicitly incorporates state estimation. The authors demonstrate applicability of the approach to certain nonlinear systems treating the effects of nonlinearity as disturbances.

A smaller body of work has directly considered the nonlinear case. In
[
10
]
, kernel-based methods are used to learn multi-step nonlinear mappings from data. The authors formulate a finite-horizon MPC problem with deterministic safety guarantees,
and propose a method for using information from previous solutions to tighten predicted uncertainty sets and reduce conservatism.
However, to the best of our knowledge, no nonlinear MPC schemes with multi-step predictors are able to ensure recursive feasibility of the optimal control problem. Moreover, existing multi-step linear MPC schemes with recursive feasibility guarantees rely on multi-rate formulations, and therefore do not use the standard receding-horizon approach of recomputing the control sequence at each sampling step, given the latest state or output measurement.

We present a novel approach for guaranteeing feasibility with multi-step predictors, given the existence of an initial solution to the RHOCP. Each time the RHOCP is solved online, the method first identifies a suboptimal feasible solution by searching over a set of candidate trajectories, replacing predictions that violate constraints with previously verified alternatives via a predictor substitution strategy (Fig.
1
). Robustness to possible infeasibility of a terminal condition is ensured through a variable horizon strategy.
Our method applies to general nonlinear multi-step prediction models with bounded additive disturbances.

The paper is organised as follows. After the problem formulation in Section
II
, Sections
III
and
IV
discuss tube MPC with single-step and multi-step models and explain the proposed predictor substitution algorithm. Section
V
gives the RHOCP for multi-step MPC with feasibility and stability guarantees.
A numerical example
demonstrating the application of the algorithm
is discussed in Section
VI
, and we provide concluding remarks in Section
VII
.

III
Tube MPC with single-step Predictors

To motivate the development for multi-step predictors, we consider a generic robust tube MPC based on a single-step model with a RHOCP employing a worst-case cost of the form

J(v_{0:N-1|k},\mathbb{X}_{0:N|k})=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\max_{x\in\mathbb{X}_{i|k}}\ell(x,v_{i|k})+\max_{x\in\mathbb{X}_{N|k}}\ell_{T}(x)

where the terminal cost is given by
\ell_{T}(x)=\|x\|_{P}^{2}
for
P\succ 0
, and

is a quadratic stage cost given by

\ell(x,v)=\|x\|^{2}_{Q}+\|\kappa(x)+v\|^{2}_{R},

(9)

and

denotes a tube generated by recursive application of the single-step model (

), satisfying

\mathbb{X}_{i+1|k}\supseteq\{g(x,\kappa(x)+v_{i|k})\}\oplus\mathbb{D}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{X}_{i|k},

(10)

for
i=0,\ldots,N-1
, with
x_{k}\in\mathbb{X}_{0|k}

The RHOCP solved at the
k
th discrete time-step is given by

(v^{*}_{0:N-1|k},\mathbb{X}^{\ast}_{0:N|k})=\argmin_{v_{0:N-1|k},\mathbb{X}_{0:N|k}}J(v_{0:N-1|k},\mathbb{X}_{0:N|k})

(11a)

subject to
x_{k}\in\mathbb{X}_{0|k}
, and, for all
i=0,\dots,N-1
, (
10
) and

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{i|k}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathbb{X}

(11b)

\displaystyle\kappa(\mathbb{X}_{i|k})\oplus\{v_{i|k}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathbb{U}

(11c)

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{N|k}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathbb{X}_{T}.

(11d)

The control input at the
k
th time-step is

, and the
RHOCP is solved for all
k\geqslant 0
.
The terminal set
\mathbb{X}_{T}
is robust positively invariant (RPI), and satisfies, for all

\{g(x,\kappa(x))\}\oplus\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{X}_{T}\subseteq\mathbb{X}\ \text{and}\ \kappa(x)\in\mathbb{U}.

The parameterisation of the tube

Recursive feasibility is the property that if a feasible solution for the RHOCP exists at time
k
, then the problem will be feasible at
k+1
. This property applies to (
11

) and the terminal constraint (

In particular, the time-shifted control perturbation trajectory

\tilde{v}_{0:N-1|k+1}=\{v^{\ast}_{1:N-1|k},\,0\}

gives a feasible RHOCP solution at

because
{x_{k+1}\in\mathbb{X}^{\ast}_{1|k}}
implies (
10
)-(
11

\mathbb{X}_{i|k+1}=\mathbb{X}_{i}(x_{k+1},v^{\ast}_{1:i|k})\subseteq\mathbb{X}_{i+1|k}^{\ast}
,
and (
12
) ensures
\mathbb{X}_{N|k+1}\subseteq\mathbb{X}_{T}

\mathbb{X}_{N-1|k+1}=\mathbb{X}_{N-1}(x_{k+1},v^{\ast}_{1:N-1|k})\subseteq\mathbb{X}_{N|k}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathbb{X}_{T}
,
so (
11

Closed-loop stability of this generic tube MPC formulation follows from a Lyapunov-like property of the optimal RHOCP objective
at consecutive time-steps. To ensure robust stability,
P\succ 0
in the terminal cost
\ell_{T}(x)=\|x\|^{2}_{P}
is chosen to satisfy

\ell_{T}\bigl(g(x,\kappa(x))+d\bigr)-\ell_{T}(x)\leqslant{-\ell(x,0)}+\sigma^{2}

, for all

Under mild conditions (e.g.

,
\mathbb{D}
are compact and

yields a bounded optimal cost

(implying a limit on the impact of disturbances on performance), and that the origin,

1
) is input-to-state practically stable (ISpS)
[

(which implies that the closed-loop system state converges to a neighbourhood of the origin that depends on the size of the disturbance set
\mathbb{D}
).

IV-A

Checking constraint satisfaction a priori

A computationally inexpensive sufficient condition for RHOCP feasibility can be checked by evaluating the state and input trajectories generated by a prescribed set of control-perturbation sequences.
Let
\mathbb{V}_{k}
be a set of candidate perturbation sequences at time
k
,
and let
\mathcal{I}(x,v_{0:N-1},N)
denote, for given
v_{0:N-1}
, initial state
x
, and horizon
N
, the indices of prediction time-steps at which state or control constraints are violated by the multi-step prediction models of (
5

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(x,v_{0:N-1},N)=\{i\in\{1,\dots,N\}:\mathbb{X}_{i}(x,v_{0:i-1})\nsubseteq\mathbb{X},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{or }\kappa(\mathbb{X}_{i}(x,v_{0:i-1}))\oplus\{v_{i}\}\nsubseteq\mathbb{U},

(17)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{or }\mathbb{X}_{N}(x,v_{0:N-1})\nsubseteq\mathbb{X}_{T}\}.

A sufficient condition for RHOCP feasibility at time
k
is

\exists\ v_{0:N-1|k}\in\mathbb{V}_{k}\ \text{such that}\ \mathcal{I}(x_{k},v_{0:N-1|k},N)=\emptyset.

(18)

Given a candidate perturbation set
\mathbb{V}_{k}
, this is the basis of a method for ensuring
a priori
that the RHOCP is feasible.

IV-B

Feasibility recovery and predictor substitution

Under Assumption

Assume the RHOCP has solution

Suppose that

Then, for all

VII
Conclusions and Future Directions

This paper presents a novel method for guaranteeing recursive feasibility of robust receding horizon multi-step MPC. The strategy employs predictor substitutions to avoid potential infeasibility of the multi-step RHOCP that arises from inconsistencies in robust predictors at different time-steps. Crucially, the proposed method does not depend on a multi-rate formulation, and therefore allows the optimal control sequence to be recomputed at each time-step, given the latest state information. The algorithm is agnostic to the specific solution technique for the nonlinear RHOCP. We demonstrate the functionality of the algorithm via a simple numerical example.
A limitation of the approach is that predictor substitutions may introduce unnecessary suboptimality if the prediction horizon is reduced to maintain a prediction format consisting of one predictor per time-step. Future work will consider relaxing this requirement by allowing each prediction time-step to be defined by arbitrary compositions of multi-step predictors, and will investigate methods of constructing candidate control sequences for checking feasibility.
