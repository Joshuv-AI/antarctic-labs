Final Scientific/Technical Report (Award DE-SC0021253 / DE-SC0021358)

Project Title: FAIR Data and Interpretable AI Framework for Architectured Metamaterials

Principal Investigator: Prof. Chiara Daraio, California Institute of Technology

Collaborating Institutions: Duke University (Prof. L. Catherine Brinson, Prof. Cynthia
Rudin)

Abstract Our interdisciplinary effort successfully generated FAIR (Findable, Accessible,
Interoperable, and Reusable) benchmark datasets for mechanical metamaterials while
introducing a novel Artificial Intelligence (AI) framework known as Learning Refined
Compositional Rules (LRCR). This framework was specifically designed to bridge the gap
across varying computational length scales and extract the underlying physical
mechanisms that connect a material's structural geometry to its bulk acoustic properties.
Historically, the discovery of such structured materials relied heavily on human intuition or
opaque, black-box optimization algorithms that were difficult to generalize. By combining
interpretable machine learning techniques with rigorous experimental validation, this
project established clear, generalizable design guidelines for tuning wave dispersion and
controlling vibrations. Ultimately, the public availability of these structured datasets and
algorithms will significantly reduce computational costs and accelerate the design of
advanced multi-functional acoustic devices, offering broad societal impacts across fields
like aerospace engineering, telecommunications, and biomedical implant design.

1. Summary of Project Activities (Award DE-SC0021253 / DE-SC0021358)

Original Hypotheses and Objectives The core hypothesis of this research program was
that combining Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable (FAIR) benchmark
datasets with a novel, interpretable Artificial Intelligence (AI) framework could overcome
fundamental limitations in understanding the relationships between metamaterial
structural patterns and their bulk physical properties. Furthermore, we hypothesized that
this data-driven, interpretable approach would successfully bridge the gap between
simulations conducted at different length scales, which has traditionally been a severe
bottleneck in multi-functional material design.

The primary objectives were to:

 • Generate FAIR benchmark datasets and data curation tools for mechanical
 metamaterials.

 • Develop an interpretable AI framework, titled Learning Refined Compositional Rules
 (LRCR), to discover physical rules governing elastic energy transport.

 • Apply these frameworks to solve concrete benchmark challenges, including
 hierarchical design, directional propagation, and experimental validation.

Approaches Used

To achieve these objectives, the team employed a highly interdisciplinary approach
combining computational mechanics, advanced machine learning, and experimental
physical validation:

 • Learning Refined Compositional Rules (LRCR): We developed and deployed the
 LRCR framework, which relies on shape-frequency features, integer programming-
 based unit-cell templates, and multi-resolution sampling across scales.

 • Unit Cell Template Extension: The framework was extended to design materials
 incorporating "void" spaces. We systematically added manufacturing constraints to
 these templates to enforce minimum feature sizes and ensure physical connectivity
 for printability.

 • Interpretable Machine Learning Acceleration: We utilized Generalized Optimal
 Sparse Decision Trees (GOSDT) to find interpretable and sparse predictive models
 for bandgaps. To optimize search speeds, we leveraged black-box machine learning
 models (boosted decision trees) to guess lower bounds, depths, and thresholds,
 drastically accelerating the decision tree generation process.

 • Surrogate Modeling: A customized surrogate model based on Gaussian Process
 Regression (GPR) was developed to approximate complex dispersion relations.

 • Ontology Development: A comprehensive metamaterials ontology was
 constructed for the MetaMine portal to formally define relationships between
 material properties, physics models, and geometrical parameters.

Findings, Facts, and Analyses

Our approaches yielded significant technical findings and validated our initial hypotheses
regarding AI-driven materials discovery:

 • Hierarchical Metamaterials with Multiple Bandgaps: Using the extended Unit Cell
 Template framework, we successfully generated non-self-similar hierarchical
 materials capable of supporting multiple bandgaps. We established a physical
 experimental setup to measure wave transmission at varying frequencies, which
 successfully validated the numerical wave propagation predictions. This proves the

 viability of using these templates for multi-frequency vibration control and
 waveguiding.

 • High-Accuracy, High-Sparsity AI Models: Our optimization of the GOSDT algorithm
 produced the fastest code currently available for optimal sparse decision trees.
 Analytical benchmarking demonstrated that our method could classify complex
 data (such as the challenging spiral dataset) with 95% to 97% accuracy while
 maintaining highly sparse, interpretable models requiring only 13 to 14 leaves.

 • Computational Efficiency Breakthroughs: The GPR surrogate model allowed us to
 successfully compute dispersion relations for out-of-plane waves, as well as
 complex dispersion relations describing both propagating and attenuating waves, at
 a fraction of the traditional computational cost.

 • FAIR Data Ecosystem Establishment: We successfully translated the developed
 ontology into the MetaMine platform to make metamaterial data searchable and
 interoperable between human and machine agents. Furthermore, foundational
 computational datasets for metamaterials band gaps were published and donated
 to the UCI Machine Learning Repository.

2. Brief Technical Description of Most Relevant Projects/Publications

 • Visual Vibration Tomography: We pioneered a novel non-destructive testing
 methodology to estimate interior material properties directly from monocular video
 of surface vibrations. By analyzing sub-pixel motion and surface wave dispersion,
 this approach enables the inference of heterogeneous physical properties (such as
 stiffness and density) without requiring traditional, expensive 3D imaging
 techniques. (Feng et al., 2022)

 • Interpretable Machine Learning for Discovering Design Rules: A core achievement of
 the project was the deployment of interpretable machine learning to bridge the gap
 between complex metamaterial geometries and their bulk acoustic properties.
 Rather than relying on black-box models, we developed algorithms to extract
 logical, rule-based "hidden patterns" (such as shape-frequency features and unit-
 cell templates). This provides clear, generalizable guidelines for engineering 2D
 metamaterials with targeted frequency bandgaps. (Chen et al., 2022)

 • Surrogate Modeling for Rapid Dispersion Computations: To overcome the immense
 computational bottleneck of traditional finite element analysis in phononic crystal
 design, we developed a highly efficient surrogate model based on Gaussian Process

 Regression (GPR). This model rapidly and accurately computes complex dispersion
 relations by leveraging covariance information, significantly accelerating the inverse
 design space exploration. (Ogren et al., 2024)

 • Multiscale and Hierarchical Metamaterials Design: We successfully extended our
 interpretable machine learning frameworks to design complex, multi-scale
 phononic materials. This included generating hierarchical structures with effectively
 scale-separated features for distinct multi-frequency vibration control, as well as
 developing new multiscale methods to handle metamaterials where fine and coarse
 structural scales closely interact. (Bastawrous et al., 2024; Bastawrous et al., 2025)

 • Irregular and Gradient Acoustic Metamaterials: Pushing beyond standard periodic
 lattices, we established a graph-based design methodology to engineer irregular
 metamaterials with highly programmable properties. Building upon this, we
 introduced a robust computational framework for designing 3D acoustic
 metamaterials featuring stiffness gradients, specifically optimized for complex
 tasks such as broadband acoustic impedance matching. (Mahbadi et al., 2025;
 Karimi Mahabadi et al., 2026)

3. Paragraph indicating the number of presentations made and venues

 • Project findings were aggressively disseminated through academic publications and
 invited seminars at major industry and academic conferences, including keynote
 presentations at the Duke Soft Matter Symposium and the Bloch Symposium, as
 well as talks at the American Physical Society March Meeting.

 • To maximize educational impact, team members integrated the project's
 interpretable AI algorithms—specifically the shape-frequency feature algorithms—
 into a graduate-level curriculum at Duke University focusing on AI applications in
 materials science.

 • A publicly available video module was produced to serve as a short introductory
 course on phononic metamaterials and AI-driven design, aimed at broadening
 community understanding.

 • Project researchers actively participated in lifelong learning panels for university
 alumni to discuss partnering artificial intelligence with computation to accelerate
 scientific discovery.

4. Personnel Involvement and Human Resources Impact This project profoundly
integrated researchers across institutions, fostering a unique set of cross-disciplinary skills
at the intersection of data science and materials science.

 • Prof. Chiara Daraio (Principal Investigator, Caltech): Led the Caltech team,
 supervising graduate researchers focused on physical modeling, structural
 optimization, and the integration of machine learning models.

 • Prof. L. Catherine Brinson (Co-Investigator, Duke): Directed overall collaborative
 efforts, co-supervised graduate students working on machine learning and
 simulations, and oversaw the curation and publication of FAIR data.

 • Prof. Cynthia Rudin (Co-Investigator, Duke): Guided the methodological
 development and application of advanced, interpretable machine learning
 algorithms.

 • Graduate Students & Postdoctoral Researchers: Crucial technical contributions
 were made by Alex Ogren and Berthy Feng at Caltech, alongside Mary Bastawrous,
 Zhi Chen, Yingfan Wang, and Chudi Zhong at Duke. Together, they collaborated on
 the metamaterials ontology framework, hierarchical structural concepts, and AI
 method development, gaining highly sought-after expertise that translated directly
 into competitive industry placements.
