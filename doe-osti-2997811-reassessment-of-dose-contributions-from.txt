 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
During a recent revision to the groundwater (GW) and inadvertent human intruder (IHI) screening
analysis report (Aleman and Hamm, 2023), weaknesses within the screening logic were observed
resulting in U-235 being brought back into the list of radionuclides requiring inventory limits
within the M-Area Glass SWF. In addition, while reviewing the earlier vadose zone (VZ) transport
analysis efforts, an input deck error was observed in the U-234G VZ transport simulation.
This report documents the GW and IHI updated analyses for the M-Area Glass special waste form
(SWF). The GW radionuclides analyzed are U-234G and U-235G. The IHI radionuclides analyzed
are U-234G, U-235G, U-236G and U-238G.
The GW analysis in the new 2022 PA was performed assuming a dissolution-controlled release
mechanism in the VZ, along with updated aquifer flow and transport models. For IHI inventory
limits and doses the standard generic assumptions are applied without dynamic compaction of the
waste.

1 Introduction
M-Area waste sludge accumulated over time at SRS because of plating line, metal-finishing, and
aluminum-forming operations that were used to produce nickel-plated, aluminum-clad depleted
uranium targets for 239Pu production. The sludge was classified as an F006-listed mixed waste with
nickel the primary hazardous constituent and depleted uranium the major radioactive component.
SRNL performed an initial evaluation for the disposal of low-level waste consisting of vitrified
depleted uranium sludge from M-Area in slit trenches (STs) (Cook, 2002). The conclusion of that
evaluation was that such disposal exceeds the radionuclide inventory limits derived from the
Performance Assessment (PA), and that a Special Analysis (SA) had to be prepared. The first SA
done for the M-Area Glass SWF was completed by Cook and Yu (2002). In that SA, inventory
limits were generated for U-234, U-235, U-236 and U-238. This study evaluated the effect of the
disposal of low-level waste (LLW) consisting of vitrified sludge from M-Area in STs. The
conclusion of the analysis was that this waste can be disposed of at SRS and meet the performance
objectives (POs) of DOE Order 435.1. Updated inventory analyses were performed during the
development of the 2008 PA and then during the development of the new 2022 PA.
During a recent revision to the Groundwater (GW) and Inadvertent Human Intruder (IHI)
screening analysis report (Aleman and Hamm, 2023), weaknesses within the screening logic were
observed resulting in the addition of U-235 back into the list of radionuclides requiring inventory
limits within the special waste form (SWF) referred to as the M-Area Glass. In addition, while
reviewing the earlier vadose zone (VZ) transport analysis efforts, an input deck error was observed
in the U-234G VZ transport simulation. This SWF resides within the E-Area Low-Level Waste
Facility (ELLWF) in ST02. Note that radionuclides involving a SWF has a letter designation
appended to its name (e.g., U-235 within the SWF M-Area Glass is referred to as U-235G). For
the Air and Radon screening no such findings were observed.
Based on these recent assessments the following list of parent radionuclides have been added back
into the required list of radionuclides requiring inventory limits:
 • for GW analyses – U-234G and U-235G
   •   for IHI analyses - U-234G, U-235G, U-236G, and U-238G
For GW inventory limits within the PA, the M-Area Glass SWF radionuclides of interest are
handled by assuming that the release mechanism is dissolution controlled. Due to the quantity of
material present and the rates of estimated dissolution, this dissolution-controlled assumption
applies for many thousands of years. For IHI inventory limits, the standard generic assumptions
are applied except for no dynamic compaction of the waste.
For each of these parent radionuclides, inventory limits for the GW and IHI pathways are created.
For GW limits, the dissolution-controlled source terms (i.e., in tabular form) are inputted into VZ
PORFLOW transport simulations creating flux-to-the-water table (WT) sources. These fluxes are
then inputted into aquifer PORFLOW simulations creating concentrations of the parent and its
short-chain daughters at the 100-m Point of Assessment (POA). GW and IHI dose calculations are
generated where inventory limits per pathway are produced. Each of these computational pieces is
discussed within this report and this report serves as the new technical baseline for their inventory
limits.

were placed within individual drums and, later in November of 2002, were buried within one of
the trenches (as shown in Figure 2-1) residing within ST02. A photograph showing numerous
drums containing these glass beads within the partially filled trench is provided in Figure 2-3.

2.4 Long-term Normalized Glass Dissolution Rate Constants
Whited et al. (1998) summarized the average long-term normalized glass dissolution rate constants
for 13 glass formulations and 29 samples in a variety of water types, including some prepared at
the Savannah River Technology Center (SRTC). The results for experimental and scaled
dissolution rates for prepared samples of borosilicate glass leached in water from various leachants
are shown in Table 2-2 at 90 and 25°C, respectively.
The long-term normalized leach rates based on boron release (NLRB) measured at 90°C were
scaled to the ambient temperature in the STs, which is assumed to be 25°C. A survey of activation
energy values for nuclear waste glass dissolution in water reveals an activation energy within the
range 60 to 90 kJ/mol for most waste glass compositions. Consequently, an average value of 75
kJ/mol was assumed for the activation energy. The effect of Arrhenius scaling at any given
activation energy is found by calculating the ratio of leach rates over the desired temperature range.
The ratio of leach rates at 90°C to leach rates at 25°C is computed to be 225, indicating that the
leach rate derived from 90°C experimental conditions is more than two orders of magnitude greater
than the scaled leach rate for trench conditions.

3.1 Source Term Conceptual Model
The dissolution model of the M-Area glass beads is independent of the local aquifer conditions, as
well as radionuclide concentrations within the GW. Therefore, the source term model represents
the injection of radionuclides directly into the local GW from the dissolution of the glass beads.
The source term model represents the solid-liquid interfacial mass transfer of radionuclides
contained within the glass material as the glass surface dissolves. It is assumed that the initial
inventory of the parent radionuclide was uniformly distributed throughout each glass bead and that
the initial loadings were identical from bead to bead. A simple schematic representing this source
term model is shown in Figure 3-1. It is also assumed that the number density of glass beads
located within the WZ is approximately constant.

4 Inadvertent Intruder Analyses
POs are assessed for the IHI who may physically disrupt the facility at some time after the end of
IC and when the POA is at the DUs.
The CP is 1,000 years (calendar years 2165 to 3165), commencing after the 100-year IC period,
which runs from operational closure in calendar year 2065 to 2165. The applicable performance
measures are 500 mrem EDE for acute exposure scenarios and 100 mrem yr-1 for chronic exposure
scenarios. The POs for IHI scenarios are radionuclide and DU specific inventory limits or trigger
values that will be monitored during ELLWF operations based on a SOF approach.

4.1 Intruder Scenarios
The following six stylized exposure scenarios are considered for IHIs in the ELLWF (Smith et al.,
2019):
 • Acute Basement Construction: IHI constructs a basement and encounters waste during
 excavation which is inadvertently mixed with clean soil and diluted.
 • Acute Well Drilling: IHI drills a water well through waste and is exposed to drill cuttings
 mixed with clean soil that are brought to the surface.
 • Acute Discovery: IHI begins constructing a basement but stops when encountering the
 riprap in the final closure cap and is exposed to photon radiation from unexcavated material
 residing in the undisturbed WZ.
 • Chronic Agriculture: Resident IHI is exposed to waste that was excavated for basement
 construction and mixed with native soil in the intruder’s vegetable garden.
 • Chronic Post-Drilling: Resident IHI is exposed to waste from drill cuttings mixed with
 native soil and scattered in the garden area.
 • Chronic Residential: Resident IHI is exposed to external radiation while in home located
 above waste with shielding provided by the concrete basement floor and any soil or
 engineered material remaining between the basement and waste.
These scenarios assume that IC and memory are lost in calendar year 2165 (100 years after
operational closure in calendar year 2065) and that the IHI is on site after that time. Waste is
assumed to be indistinguishable from, and mistaken for, soil. However, an IHI is assumed to be
able to recognize concrete covers and erosion barriers when they are encountered. These scenarios
and their associated exposure pathways are summarized in Table 1-2 (Smith et al., 2019)
The total acute dose to an IHI is the sum of individual doses due to the three acute scenarios, as
applicable. Therefore, the individual constructing the basement is the same person who drills the
well, and who discovers the waste. Likewise, the IHI chronic dose is the sum of the three individual
chronic exposure scenarios. Note that these scenarios do not include direct ingestion of
contaminated GW or the use of contaminated GW for irrigation.

4.2 Inadvertent Human Intruder Model Details
The intruder model consists of the M-Area Glass parent radionuclides (U234G, U235G, U-236G,
and U-238G) uniformly distributed throughout containers placed in the second trench of ST02
with no depletion of the parent or progeny inventories over time due to leaching from infiltration.
The containers take up more than 50% of the total trench length as shown in Figure 2-1. Within
the trench segment where the M-Area Glass containers reside no other forms of waste can be
buried, and no dynamic compaction of waste is allowed. The full (un-collapsed) 16-foot waste
height of ST02 was assumed in determining whether WZ contaminants were within the standard
3-meter excavation for the acute basement construction scenario. The WZ represents the areal
footprint (polygon ABCDEF in Figure 2-2) and a waste height of 16 feet. The initial inventories
of the M-Area Glass parent radionuclides are set equal to 1012 pCi. The initial activities of progeny
are set to zero.
Precise decay and ingrowth calculations for the DUs are complicated because inventory additions
occur many times over the life of each DU and decay and ingrowth times are unique for each
disposed waste box. For simplicity and to bound possible outcomes, the IHI analysis is performed
twice for two different end-member cases which span the burial of the first (Case 1) and the last
(Case 2) waste container. The burial of the first and last waste container occurred at 8.092 and
11.925 years after the start of ELLWF operations, respectively. Inventory limits and doses are
computed using the maximum soil concentrations over time from Case 1 and Case 2 designated as
CaseMX.
IHI inventory limits and doses are sensitive to the 3-D geometry of ST02 as well as the overlying
material. Critical dimensions of DUs are shown in Figure 4-1 . Vertical profiles are presented for
the various types of DUs, along with the final depths below backfill and the engineered cover. The
M-Area Glass resides in the uncompacted ST02 nominal WZ. The initial depth below the CAP
(ground surface) to the top of the WZ is 11 feet.

5 Dose and Inventory Limit Analyses
The analyses presented earlier provide the necessary information to compute:
 • GW preliminary inventory limits – these inventory values are incorporated into the
 overall 2022 PA where final inventory limits per GW pathway are created using the most
 recent GW Plume Interaction Factors (GW PIFs).
 • IHI final inventory limits – for IHI analyses the preliminary and final inventory limits are
 equivalent since the POA is at the surface of each DU and no plume inaction takes place.
Note that inventory limits for the Air and Radon pathways are not required for these radionuclide
parents, except for U-234G for the Radon pathway, since they were screened out.

5.1 GW Pathways
This section focuses on the calculational aspects associated with determining the “preliminary”
inventory limits and corresponding doses for the various GW pathways of concern. The following
subsections describe the calculational sequences involved.
The preliminary DU-specific GW inventory limits are provided in this section for each parent
radionuclide of interest. These are not generic limits since specific engineered barriers are

employed within the computational VZ model (dissolution-controlled source terms). These are
typically referred to as Tier-4 analyses representing a complex SWF.
For the ST02, most of its footprint can have up to 2.0% non-crushable containers resulting in
subsidence potential; however, for the portion of the second trench where the M-Area Glass resides
no non-crushable containers are allowed. Thus, for the M-Area Glass only the intact scenario is
viable, and by default, this scenario becomes the maximum case, CaseMX.

inventory limits in Chapter 7 and 8 are updated for IHI and GW, respectively.
