 ABSTRACT

 A procedure has been developed for selection and screening of nickel

 cadmium cell materials to improve cell uniformity and reliability. It will be

 proven that selection and screening by weight has been found to be a successful

 method for improving uniformity of plate capacity. A statist ,*cal analysis of

 the test results provides high weight-to-capacity correlations and a means of

 predicting plate capacity of every }date in a batch from weight alone.

 INTRODUCTION

 Means for the selection and screening of nickel-cadmium cell materials are

 being investigated at GSFC. The primary aim of this work is the improvement
 i
 of cell uniformityiand reliability. The results reported here show that selec-

 tion and screening through the use of single-plate weights within a batch has been
z
 found to be a successful method for improving uniformity of plate capacity. A

 statistical analysis of the test results provided high weight-to-capacity correla-
:f
 tions and thus gave a reliable means of predicting the capacity of a plate from

 its weight alone.

 This effort has been directed mainly toward the 20-ampere-hour (A-h) cells

 used in the Orbiting Astronomical Observatory (OAO), but the technique also

 merits consideration by users involved with other projects.

 Plates from a batch manufactured by the SAFT Corporation of America and

 assembled by GLlton Industries for the OAO Flight III (batteries 32 and 33) were

 i

 t

 considered in the evaluation. Also included in the investigation were 20..A-h

 cell plates from the General Electric Company, considered as a backup source

 for OAO, the 20-A-h cell plates manufactured by Gulton Industries for aircraft

 use, and some 20-A-h aircraft-grade plates from batches manufactured by

 Texas Instruments, although this company no longer produces nickel-cadmium

 cells.

 DISCUSSION

 The selection and screening evaluation is centered on the relationship be-

 tween plate weight and plate capacity. The basis for this relationship is tied to

 the composition of the positive and negative plates. The plates of a nickel-

 cadmium cell are produced in the following manner:
 4

 (1) Nickel powder is sintered onto a screen or perforated sheet substrate

 to produce an 80% porous plaque structure.

 (2) The plaque structure is then impregnated with the active material tG pro-

 duce a plate-nickel hydroxide as the active material for a positive plate- and cad-

 mium hydroxide as that for a negative plate.

 The weight of a plate is therefore dependent on the weight of the substrate,

{ the weight of porous nickel deposited to produce the plaque, and the weight of the

 active material in the plaque. The weight of the substrate for each plate is rel-

 atively constant within a batch because the dimensions are essentially the same

 for each plate. The plaque length and width are also the same for each plate,

 and thus only the thickness, density, and therefore the quantity of nickel can vary.
 2

 6

 r

 If the manufacturing process is well controlled and the nickel quantity is con-

 stant, then the final plate weight will vary only as a function of the quantity of
 i active material added to the plaque. Because the capacity (ampere hours) of a

 plate is a strong function of the quantity of active material, the weight of a plate
 can be related to the plate capacity, Variation in plaque weight would have an

 added, compli:ating effect on the weight-to-capacity relationship.

 These data indicate that the weight of single plates is a reliable measure of

 plate capacity. A recommended practical procedure for screening by weight is
 the following:

 (1) Perform a visual inspection of each plate in the batch. Reject plates

 with obvious defects or abnormalities.

 (2) Weigh a statistically sound sample of more than 100 randomly selected
 plates from the remaining acceptable plates to determine the average weight of
 each plate in the entire batch.
 (3) Select only those plates within ± 3% of the average weight.

 (4) Select ten plates and perform a charge-discharge cycling test as

 described in the procedure listed in this report.

 (5) Perform a statistical analysis of the weight and capacity data. If the
^rf

 X^.
 correlation coefficient is greater than 0. 85, an acceptably close relationship
 5
 exists between capacity and weight.

 (6) Perform a regression analysis that will provide a mathematical re-
 w
 lationship between weight and capacity. This can then be used to predict
 lationship r

 individual plate capacity and negative-to-positive capacity ratios for the entire
 i
 } batch of plates.

 r

 In summary, the use of weight as a selection and screening method for

 nickel-cadmium cell plates has been shown to be reliable. There exists a
 f

 strong relationship between the capacity and weight of plates for 20-A-h nickel-

 cadmium cells of the type used on the OAO and other satellites. This is not

 unexpected, considering the details of a typical plate assembly, if the basic

 structure of a plate, namely the substrate and plaque, is made uniformly and
 r reproducibly. With a uniform and reproducible structure the plate weight is a
 M

 linear function of the weight of active material incorporated in the plate, and this
 t

 is directly related to the capacity.

 CONCLUSIONS

 The following can be concluded from this effort:

. (1) The capacity of plates from nickel-cadmium cells of the OAO 20-A-h
 r

 type is highly correlated with weight.
 y.
 (2) Plate weight is an effective means of screening for abnormal plates,

 and it can also be used to select plates for a narrow capacity distribution,

 thereby improving uniformity.

 (3) A ±3% tolerance on weight is recommended* as a working limit in the

 selection and acceptance of plates for nickel-cadmium cells.

 (4) Mathematical relationships are derived which, for a given manufacturer,

 will predict plate capacity from plate weight with a standard error in capacity
