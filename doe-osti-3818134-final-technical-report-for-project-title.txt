 Photoionization mechanism of branching of
 streamer discharge in air

 Andrey Starikovskiy

Executive Summary

This project supported the work of the Princeton University research group (Dr. Andrey
Starikovskiy) at the Princeton Collaborative Low Temperature Plasma Research Facility
(PCRF), which is a collaborative research facility supported by the U.S. Department of Energy,
Office of Science, Office of Fusion Energy Sciences. In the context of the project, the objective
was to investigate the mechanism responsible for the branching of the streamer discharge
channel through experimental and numerical means. This investigation entailed the development
of analytical and numerical models that enabled the quantitative description of the development
of the 3D structure of the streamer discharge, the production of active particles, and the
distribution of electric fields. An analysis was conducted of the branching of streamers,
discharges of positive and negative polarities, and a wide range of pulse voltages. An analytical
model of streamer branching has been developed. The experimental validation of the model was
conducted using ultra-fast imaging to observe streamer propagation under various conditions.
The data obtained from this study make it possible to validate the leading role of photoionization
mechanism in the process of branching of streamer discharges in air and to elaborate a numerical
model for describing the process. It has been demonstrated that the process of streamer
branching is governed by the concerted action of photoionization, ionization, and the diffusion of
electrons in the pre-existing ionization wave in the streamer head. The discrepancy in the
branching patterns of positive and negative streamers has been elucidated. The numerical criteria
of the branching have been derived and verified.

Accomplishments

Branching of a streamer channel is a key process that governs the development of a streamer
flash, the degree of plasma filling in plasma-chemical reactors, and the uniformity of active-
particle distribution within a streamer flash.

Streamer branching was studied in a plate-to-plate geometry with a 30 mm discharge gap. The
voltage amplitude ranged from 10 to 40 kV, with a pulse rise time of 2 ns and a half-maximum
duration of 20 ns. Experiments were performed in air at atmospheric pressure.
Integral images of the cathode-directed streamer show that the number of branches increases as

the applied voltage decreases. Within the pressure range of 680-1000 Torr, a branched streamer
flash was observed for all voltages from +16 to +40 kV. As the voltage increased, the number of
branches decreased: in the 24-40 kV range we typically observed three or fewer channels, while
below 20 kV the number of branches increased sharply. At voltages above 40 kV, only a single
streamer channel was observed.

The anode-directed streamer behaves entirely differently. Negative streamers form several
filaments near the cathode but exhibit no branching within the discharge gap. No branching in a
free space has been observed for the voltage range from -24 to -40 kV.

We have thus established the dependence of branching intensity on discharge parameters and
studied the dynamics of the streamer-flash structure. Detailed measurements of streamer-flash
development in air were performed in a plane-to-plane geometry over the pressure range 680-
1000 Torr. Streamers were initiated by 20-42 kV high-voltage pulses of both polarities, with an
11 ns rise time and a 400 ns pulse width. The discharge conditions under which the branched
form of the streamer flash appears were identified for both cathode- and anode-directed
streamers, and a model of the process has been developed.

An analytical model of streamer branching was formulated and validated using ultra-fast imaging
of streamer propagation under various conditions. The results confirm the leading role of
photoionization in streamer-branching processes in air and enable the development of a
numerical model describing this phenomenon. It was shown that streamer branching is governed
by the combined action of photoionization, electron-impact ionization, and electron diffusion
ahead of the leading ionization wave at the streamer head. The differences between positive and
negative streamer branching have been explained. Numerical criteria for branching have been
derived and verified.

Results dissemination

The results of the program were reported and discussed at 3 international conferences, including
a plenary lecture at 17th International Conference Gas Discharge Plasmas and Their Applications
