SRSF3 plays a key role in liver cancer sex disparity by facilitating hepatocyte expression of ERα and FOXA

Lulu Yu, Masahiko Ajiro, Vladimir Majerciak, Alexei Lobanov, Yafeng He, Maggie Cam, Baktiar Karim, Deanna Gotte, Chuxia Deng, Keji Zhao, Douglas R. Lowy, Nicholas J. G. Webster, Zhi-Ming Zheng

Abstract

Conditional Srsf3 KO from mouse liver hepatocytes or mammary glands revealed that Srsf3 is tumor-suppressive in development of DEN-induced liver cancer but oncogenic in Erbb2-mammary tumorigenesis by differential regulation of different set of genes in two different organs. Despite ERα and FOXA family are involved in the sex disparity in development of liver cancer, what controls the expression of ERα and FOXA family genes was a mystery. We found that SRSF3, in addition to regulate RNA splicing for expression of a subset genes, is a key regulator for sex disparity in the development of liver cancer and is required, presumably through transcriptional regulation, for the expression of ERα and FOXA family genes and their downstream targets LIFR, EGFR, and Myc.
SRSF3 (SRp20) is an essential RNA splicing factor. By conditional knockout (KO) of Srsf3, we found Srsf3 at its physiological level plays a tumor-suppressive role in mouse liver carcinogenesis. Srsf3 KO in hepatocytes disrupted sex disparity in the development of DEN-induced liver cancer and resulted in high incidence of liver cancer in both male and female mice. In contrast, Srsf3 is oncogenic in the mouse mammary glands and Srsf3 KO significantly delayed the development of Erbb2 breast cancer. Srsf3 KO significantly increased the expression of transcription factors Sox4, E2f1, and Myc, calcium channel factor Trpv4, and zinc-binding Trim6 in liver cancer, but did not in Erbb2 breast cancer. Srsf3 KO enhanced the expression of glucose transmembrane transporter Mfsd4a and translation initiator Eif4a2 in DEN-induced liver cancer but reduced Mfsd4a and Eif4a2 expression in breast cancer. More importantly, Srsf3 KO reduced the expression of hepatocyte ERα and transcription factor Foxa family genes essential for sexual dimorphism in liver cancer and their downstream targets leukemia inhibitory factor receptor Lifr and epidermal growth factor receptor Egfr, but increased the expression of Myc. We further evidenced that SRSF3 binds to FOXA1 promoter and is responsible for FOXA1 promoter activity and FOXA1 expression in liver cancer cells. Together, our data highlight a functional paradigm of SRSF3 at its physiological level in tissue context-dependent gene regulation and in facilitating hepatocyte ERα and FOXA1 expression for sex disparity in liver cancer development.

Serine/arginine-rich splicing factor 3 (SRSF3), also called SRp20, is the smallest member of serine/arginine-rich proteins (SR proteins) (–). SRSF3 promotes viral and host RNA splicing when binding to an exonic cis-element (–), but inhibits RNA splicing when binding to an intronic element (). Besides mRNA splicing, SRSF3 acts as a cytoplasm-nuclear shuttling protein and plays a critical role in many cellular processes, including RNA polyadenylation (, ), RNA export (, ), miRNA biogenesis (, , ), and protein translation (, ). SRSF3 was found indispensable for embryo development (). Moreover, we recently discovered that SRSF3 is an antiapoptotic and antisenescence factor essential for cell proliferation by promoting the G2/M transition and is oncogenic as a proto-oncogene when overexpressed (, ) by regulating genome-wide expression of numerous genes at transcriptional and posttranscriptional levels (, ). SRSF3 also prevents a cryptic intron in SRSF1 exon 4 from RNA splicing to escape nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) of the spliced SRSF1 mRNA () and to express SRSF1 as a proto-oncoprotein (–). Cancer is a chronic and polygenic disease involving a complex, multistep process and multiple risk factors (–). A variety of human cancer tissues exhibit highly increased expression of SRSF3 (, , , ).

Liver cancer is the sixth most diagnosed cancer and ranks third in cancer mortality worldwide, with liver cancer incidence and death in men significantly higher than in women (). Estrogen and estrogen receptor α (ERα) are contributing factors for female resistance to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) (, ). Foxa1 and Foxa2 are also essential for sexual dimorphism in liver cancer and in cooperating ERα, suppress Myc expression (). SRSF3 expression is high in most liver cancer cases () and is essential for the differentiation of hepatocytes and their metabolic function (). SRSF3 reduction contributes to progressive liver disease (), and mice with Srsf3 knock-out (KO) developed liver cancer with aging (), suggesting a possible tumor suppressive effect of SRSF3. However, the molecular mechanisms of how Srsf3 functions at a physiological level and in the development of liver cancer remain to be determined.

Breast cancer is the most frequently diagnosed cancer and a leading cause of cancer death in women worldwide (), with over 70% of breast cancers harboring the activation of the nuclear hormone receptor ERα (). A recent study shows that ERα RNA-binding activity contributes to breast cancer progression (). Breast cancer tissues also show increased SRSF3 expression (), and the increased expression of SRSF3 was found to correlate with breast cancer progression and 5-y overall survival (). SRSF3 was found to be essential for triple-negative breast cancer progression by interacting with TDP43 and regulation of alternative RNA splicing of CD44 variants (, ). Nevertheless, the function of SRSF3 in other types of breast cancer remains to be explored.

Human SRSF3 gene is in size of 8,422 bp on chromosome 6 and mouse Srsf3 gene is in size of 9,423 bp on chromosome 17 (). Both contain seven exons and six introns and encode 164-aa SRSF3 protein with 100% amino acid identity between the mouse and human homologues. Their transcription start sites and polyadenylation cleavage sites were recently remapped and updated (). The major SRSF3 mRNA isoform with exclusion of exon 4 (a poison exon), which encodes the full-length protein, is 1,411-nt long generated by alternative RNA splicing and alternative polyadenylation (). Similarly, major mouse Srsf3 mRNA isoform is only 1,295-nt long with a shorter 3′ UTR over the human SRSF3 3′ UTR. The sizes of both human and mouse SRSF3 mRNAs are much shorter than the NCBI annotated SRSF3 transcripts (). By using Cre-LoxP technology and albumin (Alb)-promoter-driving Cre expression in liver tissues and mouse mammary tumor virus (MMTV) promoter-driving Cre expression in mammary tissues, we conditionally knocked out Srsf3 expression separately from liver and mouse mammary glands. Srsf3 KO on liver carcinogenesis was evaluated by intraperitoneal injection of 15-d-old mice with diethylnitrosamine (DEN), a chemical liver carcinogen (). Srsf3 KO on breast carcinogenesis was evaluated by further crossing the Srsf3 KO mouse with the mouse carrying a mutant c-neu (Erbb2 or HER2) (). We demonstrated that Srsf3 at its physiological level is tumor-suppressive in liver cancer development but oncogenic in breast cancer formation by regulation of expression of a distinguished set of genes. Importantly, we found that SRSF3 is a key player facilitating the expression of ERα and FOXA family genes which contribute to sex disparity in the development of liver cancer.

Results

Srsf3 Conditional KO in Mouse Liver Tissues and Mammary Glands.

SRSF3 is overexpressed in a variety of human cancers (, , , ), including liver cancer and breast cancer (SI Appendix, Fig. S1). To further understand how SRSF3 functions in regulation of expression of host genes which ensure cell proliferation and cell cycle progression (, ), we investigated roles of SRSF3 at its physiological level in tissues in development of liver and breast cancer. Conditional liver-specific Srsf3 KO by Alb-Cre expression and Srsf3 KO in mouse mammary glands by MMTV-Cre expression were based on Srsf3-floxed mice () (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 A and B). We confirmed the specific homozygous Srsf3 KO in liver tissues and mouse mammary gland tissues first by RNA-seq (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 C and D) and subsequently by RT-PCR (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 E and F), showing the KO of Srsf3 exon 2 and exon 3. Because the poison exon 4 is normally skipped in the WT mRNA (, ), the detected Srsf3 mRNA having the Cre-mediated KO of exon 2 and exon 3 in liver and breast tissues appeared as the spliced product from the exon 1 to exon 5 (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 E and F). The residual WT Srsf3 expression in the Srsf3 KO liver or mammary gland tissues (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 C–F) was also detected from infiltrated leukocytes or other types of cells in the tissues lacking Albumin or MMTV promoter activities.

We noticed that mice with conditional Srsf3 KO in livers and mammary glands did not show embryonic lethality. However, mice with liver conditional Srsf3 KO were born in a smaller size, less active, and have less appetite. The most (~90%) died by ~14 d of birth () (SI Appendix, Fig. S3A). After chemical carcinogen diethylnitrosamine (DEN) intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of survived mice at day 15 of age (SI Appendix, Fig. S3B), the death incidence of liver homozygous Srsf3 KO mice reduced to ~24% in a month after birth (SI Appendix, Fig. S3C). However, the survived male and female mice from the death crisis recovered their body weight and size comparable to the Srsf3 WT mice by 2 mo of age, consistent with previous study (). Such growth failure or early death phenotype did not happen to the mice with mammary Srsf3 KO, nor to the mice bearing only heterozygous Srsf3 KO (hetKO) in the liver tissues (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 B and C). Data suggest that conditional Srsf3 KO in liver had transient impacts on liver function responsible for the newborn growth, which subsequently could be overcome at the adulthood age. Liver function tests indicated that mice livers bearing Srsf3 KO displayed more damage to their liver function, with significant increase of ALT, ALP, and amylase activities, whereas urea nitrogen and total bilirubin were normal (SI Appendix, Fig. S4).

Srsf3 Is Tumor-Suppressive in DEN-Induced Liver Carcinogenesis but Oncogenic in the Development of Erbb2 Breast Cancer.

We then investigated the tissue-specific KO of Srsf3 on cancer induction in two well-established mouse cancer models (SI Appendix, Fig. S3B): DEN-induced liver cancer () and Erbb2-induced breast cancer () (Erbb2 breast cancer in this report). The mice with conditional KO of Srsf3 in the liver hepatocytes with intraperitoneal (i.p.) DEN injection (25 mg/kg at 15 d of age) and in the mammary glands bearing the mutant rat c-neu (V664E) replacement of mouse Erbb2 were examined weekly by palpation for liver tumor development from 6 mo of age and for breast tumor development from a month of age, up to 18 mo (a total of 78 wk). Additionally, a subgroup of mice was killed humanely in the sixth month for tissue pathological examination. The Kaplan-Meier curves for animals with palpable tumors were plotted up to 18 mo.

We found that Srsf3 plays opposite roles in the development of liver and breast cancers. In DEN-induced liver cancer model, Srsf3 was tumor suppressive. We found seven out of nine mice (78%) with liver Srsf3 KO in the sixth month of animal age, but only one of 21 mice (4.8%) bearing WT Srsf3 with DEN injection, developed pathological HCC (). In contrast, Srsf3 in the Erbb2 breast tumor model was oncogenic in promoting breast tumor formation because all mammary glands with WT Srsf3 expression in six Erbb2 mice exhibited c-neu (Erbb2)-positive tumor foci, but none of eight Erbb2 mice with the Srsf3 KO had any detectable tumor foci in the mammary glands at the sixth month of mice age ().

Srsf3’s tumor suppressive role in liver became more obvious during 18 mo of observation, of which cancer was further confirmed by histopathological examination of killed humanely mouse livers. Surprisingly, Srsf3 was found to play an important sex-disparity role in the development of DEN-induced liver cancer, which male mice are prone to develop DEN-induce liver cancer more often than female mice (). Because of female sex hormone, female mice in general are more resistant than male mice to liver cancer induction () by DEN (P < 0.01) (SI Appendix, Fig. S5A). We found Srsf3 KO diminished this sex disparity in the liver cancer development induced by DEN (compare with , also SI Appendix, Fig. S5B). Both male () and female () mice with Srsf3 KO show a much higher incidence in DEN-induced liver cancer (P < 0.0001) when compared to WT Srsf3 mice, with no difference in incidence of palpable liver tumor up to 50 wk of DEN injection (SI Appendix, Fig. S5B). The female mice with Srsf3 KO exhibited substantially increased tumor burden than WT mice by pathological measurement (SI Appendix, Fig. S5C). The mice with hetSrsf3 KO showed no difference in DEN-induction of liver cancer from WT Srsf3 mice (), suggesting a single copy of Srsf3 allele expression (hetSrsf3 KO) is sufficient to delay liver cancer formation.

In contrast, Srsf3’s oncogenic role in the development of mouse breast tumors by 18 mo was striking. A significant delay (P = 0.0112) in tumor development in Erbb2 mice with Srsf3 KO was observed when compared to Erbb2 mice with WT Srsf3 (), whereas the Erbb2 mice with hetSrsf3 KO showed intermediate outcome (no statistical significance) in overall tumor formation. Mice without Erbb2 exhibited no breast tumor during 18 mo of observation period ().

Srsf3 Regulates the Expression of a Different Set of the Genes Associated with DEN-Induced Liver Cancer Distinguishable from Erbb2 Breast Cancer.

To understand how Srsf3 KO in liver hepatocytes and mammary glands could have an opposite effect on carcinogenesis, which might associate with altered gene expression and RNA splicing in two different organs, we conducted RNA-seq analyses for DEN-induced Srsf3 KO and Srsf3 WT liver cancer tissues (SI Appendix, Fig. S6 A and B and Dataset S1), and Erbb2-induced Srsf3 KO and Srsf3 WT breast cancer tissues (SI Appendix, Fig. S6 D and E and Dataset S2). We found that the gene expression profile from DEN-induced Srsf3 KO liver cancer was very different from Srsf3 KO Erbb2 breast cancer. In addition to tissue-specific gene expression, this difference could be also interpreted as different carcinogens used in our studies, with DEN as a chemical carcinogen in liver cancer induction and Erbb2 as an oncogene in breast cancer induction.

In DEN-induced liver cancer tissues, we found 927 genes significantly upregulated, and 1,017 genes significantly downregulated in Srsf3 KO vs. Srsf3 WT liver cancers (SI Appendix, Fig. S6B and Dataset S1). The genes upregulated in Srsf3 KO liver cancer tissues were involved in cancer-related oxidative phosphorylation (downregulated in Srsf3 KO breast cancer), epithelial–mesenchymal transition, fatty acid metabolism, and E2F targets based on the GSEA hallmark gene-sets pathway analysis, whereas those downregulated genes in Srsf3 KO liver cancer were the genes responsible for interferon responses (also in Srsf3 KO breast cancer), coagulation, and KRAS signaling (SI Appendix, Fig. S6C). Subsequently, we identified and verified by NanoString analysis of 43 genes with altered expression by RNA-seq in Srsf3 KO liver cancer tissues including the top listed genes with upregulated or down-regulated expression (SI Appendix, Fig. S7A and Dataset S3) and the genes not on the RNA-seq top list but with important functions (SI Appendix, Fig. S7B, in black, and Dataset S3), of which Nol3, Egfr, and Ahnak2 are involved in RNA processing, Cxcl11, Zbp1, and Fgl2 participate the regulated interferon pathway, and Slc12a2 and Errfi1 are Srsf3-regulated genes identified in our previous publication (). Interestingly at sixth month of animal age, 11 out of the 43 validated genes already showed the up- or down-regulated expression in Srsf3 KO liver cancer (SI Appendix, Fig. S8A and Dataset S3), suggesting that these genes could remain the altered expression up to 18 mo (terminal time).

In the Erbb2 breast cancer, a total of 425 differentially expressed genes were identified, of which 159 genes were upregulated, and 266 genes were downregulated in Srsf3 KO breast cancer when compared to WT Srsf3 breast cancer (SI Appendix, Fig. S6E and Dataset S2). The top enriched gene sets in upregulated pathways were involved in cancer-related hypoxia, mitotic spindle, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and p53 pathways, but the top enriched gene sets in the downregulated pathways were involved in interferon responses and oxidative phosphorylation (SI Appendix, Fig. S6F). The heatmap showed the expression of RNA-seq identified top 25 genes either abundantly (RPKM ≥ 2) upregulated or downregulated (SI Appendix, Fig. S7C). By NanoString technology, we verified the differential expression of top 40 genes identified by RNA-seq (SI Appendix, Fig. S7D and Dataset S3). Among them, upregulated Pcbp4, Ahnak2, and Ctif are the genes involved in RNA processing and translation initiation, and downregulated Stat2, IL18bp, and Casp7 are the genes involved in interferon responsive pathway. Interestingly, 20 out of the 40 NanoString-validated genes at the sixth month of animal age (SI Appendix, Fig. S8B and Dataset S3) remained persistently being upregulated or downregulated in Srsf3 KO breast cancer tissues for 18 mo (terminal time) when compared to Srsf3 WT breast cancer tissues.

We also analyzed the differential expressions of Sox4, E2f1, Trpv4, and Trim6, which are the genes important for cancer stem cell behavior and function (–) -(), and found that Srsf3 KO increased the expression of Sox4, E2f1, Trpv4, and Trim6 expression in DEN-induced liver cancer when compared to DEN-induced Srsf3 WT liver cancer -(). We further verified the increased expression of Sox4, E2f1, Trpv4, and Trim6 in DEN-induced Srsf3 KO liver cancer by RT-qPCR (). In contrast, Srsf3 KO had no effect on the expression of Sox4, E2f1, Trpv4, and Trim6 in the Srsf3 KO Erbb2 breast cancer when compared to the Srsf3 WT Erbb2 breast cancer -(). Together, these data clearly indicate that Srsf3 functions differently in a tissue-context manner by altering the expression of different genes or the same genes with opposite expression in two organs, which could partially explain the differential role Srsf3 might play in the DEN-induced liver cancer and Erbb2 breast cancer.

Identification and Validation of Srsf3-Regulated Splicing Events from DEN-Induced Liver Cancer to Erbb2 Breast Cancer.

To understand Srsf3-induced changes in RNA splicing in contribution to the observed opposite effect of Srsf3 KO on liver and breast carcinogenesis, we performed alternative splicing analysis using rMATS () and identified 677 Srsf3-regulated splicing events of 575 genes in DEN-induced Srsf3 KO over Srsf3 WT liver cancer ( and Dataset S4). We selectively validated exon skipping of Arap1 exon 30, Nprl3 exon 3, Clip1 exon 9, and Pabpc4 exon 11, retention of Dnase1 intron 3 and Kin intron 2, selection of an alternative 3′ splice site of Hcfc1r1 exon 2, Tgfb2 exon 5, and Arv1 exon 3, and selection of an alternative 5′ splice site of Zfp512b exon 8 ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S9 A–F). These alternative RNA splicing changes led to altered production of the corresponding protein isoforms in DEN-induced Srsf3 KO liver cancer (SI Appendix, Fig. S10 A–J). The exon skipping of Pabpc4 targeted by Srsf3 was reported in our previous study (). In addition, we found Srsf3 control of Rnf128 promoter usage and found an alternative promoter being activated in Srsf3 KO liver cancer (SI Appendix, Fig. S11 A and B) for expression of a shorter isoform (402 aa residues) than the authentic promoter-derived full-length (FL, 428 aa residues) Rnf128 protein, an E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase abundantly expressed in liver and other digestive tract organs (). Two isoforms of Rnf128 appear differently on p53 degradation (, ). Because two alternative exon 1 are separately spliced to a common exon 2 (SI Appendix, Fig. S11 A and B), the short Rnf128 has the N-terminal 135 aa residues differing from the N-terminal 161 aa residues of FL Rnf128 (SI Appendix, Fig. S11C).

A total of 1,118 Srsf3-responsive splicing events in 899 genes in Srsf3 KO vs. Srsf3 WT Erbb2 breast cancers were also identified by rMATS ( and Dataset S5). We further selectively validated the Srsf3-regulated skipping of Slain2 exon 7-8, Fam221a exon 6, and Ralgapa1 exon 41, retention of Yipf2 intron 9 in the 3′ UTR region, selection of an alternative 5′ splice site in Mynn exon 2, Srsf5 exon 1 in the 5′ UTR region, and Tmem161b exon 8, selection of an alternative 3′ splice site in Depdc5 exon 26, and a cryptic intron inclusion within Srsf1 exon 4 ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S9 G–K). Data indicate that these alternative RNA splicing changes led to altered production of the corresponding protein isoforms (except Yipf2 and Srsf5) (SI Appendix, Fig. S10 K–P). Srsf3 KO in Erbb2 breast cancer was also promoting RNA splicing of a cryptic intron within the 3′ UTR region of Srsf1 exon 4 (SI Appendix, Fig. S9K), leading to NMD of Srsf1 RNA.

Identification of Common Srsf3-Responsive Genes with Opposite Expression Directions from DEN-Induced Liver Cancer to Erbb2 Breast Cancer.

Although the difference in Srsf3 KO-induced gene expression profiles identified above could be most likely resulted from the different organs (liver vs. breast) and different carcinogens (DEN vs. Erbb2) used in the tumor induction, we further investigated the common Srsf3-responsive genes to explore how Srsf3 at its physiological level plays its differential roles in the development of DEN-induced liver cancer as a tumor suppressor and of Erbb2 breast cancer as an oncogene. By Venn diagram analysis (), we identified 41 genes commonly responsive to Srsf3 KO, with 15 genes significantly upregulated in their expression in liver cancer but downregulated in breast cancer, and 9 genes significantly downregulated in their expression in liver cancer upon Srsf3 KO but upregulated in breast cancer (). The remaining 17 genes were common Srsf3 KO responders with the same expression trend, of which 4 genes were upregulated, and 13 genes were downregulated. Major facilitator superfamily domain containing 4A protein (Mfsd4a) is a tumor suppressor (, ) involved in glucose transmembrane transport and was on the top list of dysregulated genes in their expression. Mfsd4a was up-regulated 293-fold in DEN-induced Srsf3 KO liver cancer, but down-regulated 16-fold in Srsf3 KO Erbb2 breast cancer (). This opposite response of Mfsd4a expression to Srsf3 KO from DEN-induced liver cancer to Erbb2 breast cancer was remarkable by IGV visualization of RNA-seq reads-coverage () and easily validated by NanoString analysis (SI Appendix, Fig. S7 B and D). Similarly, Htatip2 (Tip30), a tumor suppressor in various cancers (–), was upregulated in DEN-induced liver cancer, but downregulated in Erbb2 breast cancer following Srsf3 KO (SI Appendix, Fig. S12A). Srsf3 KD in Hepa1-6 cells also promoted the expression of Htatip2 (SI Appendix, Fig. S12 B and C).

Eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4A2 (Eif4a2 or DDX2B), a protumor RNA helicase (, ), was identified as another Srsf3 KO responder, of which expression was upregulated in DEN-induced Srsf3 KO liver cancer but downregulated in Srsf3 KO Erbb2 breast cancer (). Eif4a2 gene has 12 exons and its exon 11 is a “poison exon.” Inclusion of exon 11 in Eif4a2 RNA leads to Eif4a2 RNA degradation by NMD (, ). Only the Eif4a2 mRNAs without exon 11 encode a full-length (FL) Eif4a2 protein. Therefore, inclusion of the exon 11 in Eif4a2 RNA splicing will reduce the expression of FL Eif4a2 protein. We found by RNA-seq that DEN-induced Srsf3 KO liver cancer exhibited more exon 11 exclusion and enhanced the expression of Eif4a2 RNA (). In contrast, Srsf3 KO Erbb2 breast cancer displayed more Eif4a2 exon 11 inclusion and reduced Eif4a2 RNA level due to induction of Eif4a2 RNA degradation by NMD (). RT-PCR analyses of the total RNA from DEN-induced liver cancer and Erbb2 breast cancer tissues and western blot analyses of their tissue lysates, respectively, confirmed Srsf3 KO promoting exon 11 exclusion and increasing the production of eIF4A2 protein in the DEN-induced liver cancer (), but exon 11 inclusion and decreasing eIF4A2 protein production in the Erbb2 breast cancer (). Consistently with Erbb2 cancer tissues, knockdown of Srsf3 expression in mouse c-neu+ NF639 cells and two human breast cancer cell lines ER+ MCF7 and HER2+ SKBR3 cells was also found to promote exon 11 inclusion of Eif4a2 by RT-PCR analysis of total cell RNA and subsequent reduction of eIF4A2 protein by immunoblotting of cell lysates (SI Appendix, Fig. S12D).

Srsf3 KO also affected the opposite expression of other genes involved in RNA export and translation (Datasets S4 and S5). These were Srsf3 KO-mediated reduction of RNA export factor Aly/REF in DEN-induced liver cancer but not in Erbb2 breast cancer, and Srsf3 KO reduced expression of nuclear export factor NXF1 in Erbb2 breast cancer but not in DEN-induced liver cancer. Besides translation initiation factor eIF4a2, Srsf3 KO also decreased the expression of eIF4ebp2 in DEN-induced liver cancer and eIF1ad and eIF3A in Erbb2 breast cancer but increased the expression of eIF4e only in DEN-induced liver cancer.

Srsf3 KO-Mediated Reduction of ERα and Foxa Responsible for Sex Disparity in the Development of DEN-Induced Srsf3 KO Liver Cancer.

Liver cancer incidence and death in men are significantly higher than in women (). Male mice are prone to the development of liver cancer over female mice induced by chemical carcinogen (, ). Estrogen and estrogen receptor ERα appear to be protective for female from suffering liver cancer (, , ). Foxa gene family members also contribute to sex disparity in the development of liver cancer and female Foxa1/2-deficient livers are prone to HCC as male livers. Thus, ERα and Foxa gene family are assumed to coregulate the certain genes responsible for female mouse resistance to liver cancer induction by DEN (). Consistent with the previous report, our result also showed higher DEN-induced liver cancer incidence in Srsf3 WT male mice than in Srsf3 WT female mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S5A). Surprisingly, we found that Srsf3 KO remarkably reduced the gender disparity of DEN-induced liver cancer incidence between male and female mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S5B).

To investigate how Srsf3 KO led to reduction of gender disparity in DEN-induced liver cancer, we examined potential effect of Srsf3 KO on estrogen signaling (, , ) () and expression of Foxa family genes () (). RNA-seq analyses and RT-qPCR validation showed a significantly reduced expression of ERα RNA from Esr1 gene () in DEN-induced Srsf3 KO liver cancer than DEN-induced Srsf3 WT liver cancer in female mice. Western blot analysis () and immunostaining () of DEN-induced liver cancer tissues also showed a decreased level of ERα protein in the Srsf3 KO female hepatocytes. Interestingly, the Srsf3 KO-mediated reduction of ERα protein in hepatocytes led to an increased level of plasma estradiol E2 in Srsf3 KO female mice than the corresponding Srsf3 WT female mice (), suggesting the impaired uptake of estradiol E2 by hepatocytes in the Srsf3 KO female mice. Srsf3 KO mediated reduction of ERα expression was not limited to female, but also to male hepatocytes (SI Appendix, Fig. S13 A–C). When SRSF1, a sensitive SRSF3 responder (SI Appendix, Fig. S9K) (), was used as a positive control, we found that hepatocyte Srsf3 KO in both male and female liver cancer and breast cancer displayed, as expected, a reduced expression of SRSF1 ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S13).

Cancer-related leukemia inhibitory factor receptor Lifr, epidermal growth factor receptor Egfr, and oncogene Myc are three downstream genes coregulated by ERα and Foxa for their expression (). Our RNA-seq and RT-qPCR validation of DEN-induced Srsf3 WT and Srsf3 KO liver cancer demonstrated a significantly decreased expression of Foxa1 (also known as hepatocyte nuclear factor 3), Foxa2, and Foxa3, and Lifr and Egfr, but increased expression of Myc () in DEN-induced Srsf3 KO liver cancer in both female () and male mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S14). Analyses of the TCGA datasets show that high SRSF3 level is associated with decreased liver HCC (LIHC) survival (SI Appendix, Fig. S15A), a positive coexpression with FOXA1 in LIHC and LIHC-adjacent normal tissues (SI Appendix, Fig. S15B), and a positive correlation of FOXA1 and ERα in the adjacent normal livers (SI Appendix, Fig. S15C). Consistently, mouse Srsf3 KO liver cancer with a reduced Foxa1 had further reduction of Esr1 expression (SI Appendix, Fig. S15D). We further confirmed the reduction of Foxa1/2 RNA expression after Srsf3 KD in a mouse liver cancer cell line Hepa1-6 () and of FOXA1 protein expression from Hepa1-6 cells and two human liver cancer cell lines, Huh7 and HepG2 cells (). As these three liver cancer cell lines do not express detectable ERα (SI Appendix, Fig. S16A) by Western blot (), we chose a human breast cancer cell line, ERα+ MCF7 cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S16A), and demonstrated that SRSF3 KD in MCF7 cells reduces ERα expression, in addition to FOXA1 (). Altogether, our data indicate that SRSF3 at its physiological level is essential for expression of FOXA1/2/3 and ERα, subsequently, Lifr, Egfr, and Myc, and thereby is responsible for sex disparity in the development of DEN-induced liver cancer in the presence of estrogen.

SRSF3 Regulates the Expression of FOXA1 and ERα Presumably Via Transcription.

Both FOXA1 and ERα are nuclear transcription factors crucial for expression of hormone-responsible genes, organ development and animal reproduction, of which FOXA1 and ERα mutually affect their recruitment and DNA binding to its cis-regulatory elements (, –). We next examined this mutual regulation in MCF7 cells and found that FOXA1 controls both ERα and SRSF3 expression evidenced by their reduction upon FOXA1 KD but did not see ERα KD-induced reduction of FOXA1 expression and only minimal on SRSF3 (). Data indicate the existence of a feedback loop in this regulation mainly between SRSF3 and FOXA1.

To provide mechanistic evidence supporting SRSF3 regulation of FOXA1 and ERα expression, we first aimed to generate SRSF3-deficient hepatocytes in vitro to examine whether exogenous SRSF3 could rescue FOXA1 expression. Unfortunately, SRSF3 KD inhibits cell proliferation and induces cell apoptosis (SI Appendix, Fig. S16 B–D) as we reported (). However, hepatoblastoma-derived HepG2 (, ) appeared resilient to SRSF3 KD but displayed distinguishable cell spread-out morphology and increased lipid accumulation upon SRSF3 KD when compared with HCC-derived Hepa1-6 and Huh7 cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S17) as reported (). Consequently, HepG2 cells were chosen for the rescue experiments and showed that overexpressed FLAG-SRSF3 could rescue FOXA1 expression in the SRSF3-deficient cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S16E). By using doxycycline-inducible T7-SRp20 (SRSF3) MEF/3T3 cells (), we obtained a 17-fold enhancement of FOXA1 expression by exogenous T7-SRSF3 in the cells ().

Given that RNA-seq reads-coverage showed a uniformed RNA reads-reduction of ERα () and Foxa in Srsf3 KO liver cancer (), we assume Srsf3 KO in hepatocytes most likely prevents transcription of Esr1 and Foxa genes and their downstream targets. Because FOXA1 binding to the Esr1 promoter by ChIP-seq in estrogen-treated MCF7 cells has been reported () (SI Appendix, Fig. S18), we examined SRSF3 interaction with FOXA1 promoter and revealed SRSF3-specific association with the FOXA1 promoter region in Huh7 cells by using ChIC-seq and in HepG2 cells by using ChIP-seq (). The same HepG2 region was also associated with RNA polymerase II subunit G and other RBPs (SI Appendix, Fig. S19A) (). By inserting the 1.2 kb DNA fragment from mouse Foxa1 promoter region (1,016 nts upstream and 100 nts downstream of the Foxa1 transcription start site) into a luciferase reporter ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S19 B and C), we subsequently demonstrated that Foxa1 promoter activity requires cellular Srsf3 in Hepa1-6 cells because Srsf3 KD significantly reduced Foxa1 promoter activity in the assays ().

Discussion

As an important splicing factor, SRSF3 has profound functions in health and its aberrant expression has been associated with many diseases and cancers (). In this report, we further demonstrated that SRSF3 even at its physiological level functions as a tumor-suppressor in DEN-induced liver cancer, but a tumor-promotor in the development of Erbb2 breast cancer through differential regulation of RNA transcription and alternative RNA splicing of a different set of genes in two different organs (). This surprising observation makes SRSF3 as another gene which functions with two different faces in a tissue- or cell type–dependent manner for its differential tumor suppressive or oncogenic potentials, as reported for TGF beta (, ), NF-kB (), PTEN (, ), PML (, ), BRD4 (), etc.

SRSF3 is tumor suppressive in DEN-induced liver cancer. We found that Srsf3 KO in mouse hepatocytes could promote the development of DEN-induced liver cancer, consistent with the report of Srsf3 KO enhancing liver cancer incidence in aged mice (). Srsf3 at a physiological level is critical for hepatocyte maturation and metabolic function () and prevention of DNA damage (). Interestingly, despite its tumor suppressive function, SRSF3 is highly expressed in HCC patients (). We found an increased SRSF3 protein expression in 110 HCC samples (grade 2 and 3) (SI Appendix, Fig. S1A), as seen in many other cancer tissues (, , ). The increased expression of SRSF3 is essential for cancer cell proliferation () and shorter LIHC survival, but loss of SRSF3 expression leads to cell apoptosis () and senescence (), and genome instability (). The increased expression of tumor-suppressors () is common in many cancer types, including CDK inhibitor p16Ink4a in glioma and HPV-induced cancers (–), CDK4/6 inhibitor p18Ink4c in cervical cancer and glioblastoma (, ), and CDK2 and PCNA inhibitor p21 in many types of cancers ().

However, the increased expression of SRSF3 is correlated with breast cancer progression and 5-y overall survival (), making our initial discovery of SRSF3 as a proto-oncogene responsible for cancer cell proliferation and tumor maintenance (). SRSF3 is responsible for 23% of alternative RNA splicing events in 682 invasive breast ductal carcinoma patients and regulates HER2 RNA splicing to produce oncogenic Δ16HER2 (, ). Higher expression of SRSF3 was linked with shorter survival time and poorer prognosis (). Consistently, conditional Srsf3 KO in mouse mammary glands harmed c-neu (Erbb2) expression and the development of Erbb2 breast cancer. RNA-seq profiling showed Srsf3 regulation of mammary gland expression of 425 genes including Mfsd4a, Pcbp4, Ahnak2, and Ctif, and of 1,118 RNA splicing events in 899 genes, including exon exclusion of Slain2, Fam221a, and Ralgapa1, intron splicing of Yipf2, and alternative 5′ or 3′ splice site usage of Mynn, Depdc5, and Tmem161b. Altogether, our data indicate that the altered gene transcription and alternative RNA splicing by conditional Srsf3 KO in mouse mammary glands were the contributing factors for reduction of Erbb2 breast cancer.

Srsf3 in mouse hepatocytes was found to regulate the expression of 1,944 genes and 677 splicing events in 575 genes, including the expression of Hnf1α, Hmgcr, Srebp1, Srebp2, and Scap, and alternative RNA splicing of Fn1, Myo1b, Insr, which were previously reported (–). Srsf3 KO in mouse hepatocytes, but not in mouse mammary glands, promoted the expression of Sox4, E2f1, Trpv4, and Trim6, and alternative RNA splicing of Arap1, Pabpc4, Clip1, Hcfc1r1, Tgfb2, and Kin. We identified that 24 out of 41 genes responsive to Srsf3 KO exhibited differentially opposite expression directions between liver cancer and breast cancer. Srsf3 KO in mouse hepatocytes increased the expression of tumor suppressor Mfsd4a () and protumor RNA translation initiator Eif4a2 (, ), but decreased their expression in mouse mammary glands. Others, including Cox8a, Ndufb8, and Atad1, which are the genes involved in oxidative phosphorylation pathway (), and nuclear export factor NXF1 and Aly/REF (, ), also exhibited striking opposite regulation directions by Srsf3 KO from DEN-induced liver cancer to Erbb2 breast cancer. These gene expression patterns exemplify how Srsf3 could act as a tumor suppressor in DEN-induced liver cancer, but a proto-oncogene in Erbb2 breast cancer. Thus, our comparative studies by using two different cancer models could illustrate such sharp contrast regulation and outcomes by Srsf3, which would be buried or misinterpreted if only one cancer model was applied.

Liver cancer has an incidence and mortality rate twice higher in men than in women (). Male mice are also prone to the development of liver cancer over female mice in DEN-induced liver carcinogenesis (). Surprisingly, we showed that Srsf3 KO decreases gender disparity in the development of liver cancer. It has been known that loss of ERα or Foxa1 and Foxa2 expression in female liver increases DEN-induced liver cancer in female mice (, ). What controls the expression of Foxa family genes remains unknown. Surprisingly, Srsf3 KO in our study decreases the expression of both ERα and Foxa1-3 and Foxa downstream genes Lifr and Egfr but increases oncogenic Myc expression both in female and male livers. Further studies showed that SRSF3 may transcriptionally control the expression of FOXA family and ESR1 genes () with a feedback loop of this regulation between SRSF3-FOXA1. This presumption was based on 1) SRSF3 specifically associating with the FOXA1 promoter region, 2) this SRSF3-association with this region overlapping with POL2RG and other RBPs (), 3) SRSF3 being responsible for FOXA1 promoter activity in luciferase assays. Altogether, we believe that SRSF3, FOXA, ERα, LIFR, EGFR, and Myc are all contributing factors to sex disparity in the development of liver cancer.

Materials and Methods

Tissue-Specific Srsf3 KO, Tumor Induction, Pathology, and Biochemical Analyses.

Liver-specific Srsf3-KO mice were generated by crossing Srsf3-floxed mice with Alb-Cre transgenic mice. Liver cancer was induced by a single i.p. DEN injection. Mammary-specific Srsf3-KO mice were generated by crossing Srsf3-floxed mice with MMTV-Cre mice and further outbred with MMTV-Erbb2 transgenic mice for mammary tumorigenesis. Tumor development was monitored weekly by palpation for 18 mo. Tissues and blood were collected for histopathology and liver function analyses, respectively.

RNA-Sequencing, Bioinformatics, and Validation.

Liver and mammary tissue total RNA-seq were performed by Illumina platform and the sequence reads obtained were mapped to the mouse mm10 genome, followed by differential gene expression and alternative splicing analyses, and then validated by NanoString nCounter assays, RT-qPCR, immunoblotting, and immunohistochemistry.

Gene Functional Assays.

These studies were conducted in mouse Hepa1-6 and NF639 cells and human Huh7, HepG2, SKBR3, and MCF7 cells by siRNA and immunoblotting assays. Cell proliferation, lipid accumulation, gene expression, and RNA splicing were evaluated, respectively, by CCK-8 assays, Oil Red O staining, RT-PCR, 5′RACE, and RT-qPCR. SRSF3 regulation of Foxa1 expression was investigated by ChIC-seq, ChIP-seq, and promoter-reporter assays. Statistics were Student’s t test, Fisher’s exact test, two-way ANOVA, and Kaplan–Meier survival analysis.
