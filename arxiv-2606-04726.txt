Abstract.
A ring
R
with a non-zero identity element is said to be centrally essential if for any non-zero element
a\in R
, there exist non-zero central elements
x,y\in R
such that
ax=y
. We describe centrally essential rings in a large subclass of formal triangular matrix rings and in a subclass of the matrix ring
M_{3}(R)
over the ring
R
.

Keywords:
centrally essential ring, formal triangular matrix ring, essential submodule.

Introduction

We consider only associative rings with a non-zero identity element and unitary modules. A ring
R
is said to be
centrally essential
if for any non-zero element
a\in R
, there exist non-zero central elements
x,y\in R
such that
ax=y
. Centrally essential rings were studied in
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and other papers.

Let
R
,
S
be two rings and let
M
be a non-zero
R
-
S
-bimodule. A
formal triangular matrix ring

T(R,S,M)=\begin{pmatrix}R&M\\
0&S\\
\end{pmatrix}
consists of formal matrices
\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}
, where
a\in R
,
b\in S
and
m\in M
with component-wise addition and the following multiplication:

\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&m^{\prime}\\
0&b^{\prime}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}aa^{\prime}&am^{\prime}+mb^{\prime}\\
0&bb^{\prime}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Formal triangular matrix rings are of great importance in ring theory and have been studied, for example, in
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
. Let
R=S
and denote the resulting ring as
T(R,M)
.
The ring
T(R,M)
is not centrally essential, since it contains non-central idempotents; cf.
[
7
, Proposition 2.4]
. We consider unital subrings of
T(R,M)
with
M\neq 0
.

1.1. Remark.
If
K
is a centrally essential subring of
T(R,M)
and
Z(K)
is the center
of
K
, then

Z(M)=\left\{m\in M\mid A=\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K)\right\}\neq 0
. Otherwise, for the non-zero matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&m\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\in K
, we have
\begin{pmatrix}0&m\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\cdot Z(K)\cap Z(K)=0
; this is a contradiction, since the ring
K
is centrally essential.

For an
R
-
R
-bimodule
M
, we set
\text{Ann}_{M}I=\{m\in M\,|\,mI=0\}
,
\text{Ann}_{I}M=\{i\in I\,|\,iM=0\}
,
C_{R}(M)=\{m\in M\,|\,mr=rm,\forall r\in R\}
. We recall that a submodule
A
of a module
M
is said to be
essential
(notation:
A\leq_{e}M
) if
A\cap B\neq 0
for any non-zero submodule
B
of
M
.

The main result of the paper is Theorem 1.2.

1.2. Theorem.
Let
R
be a ring,
Z(R)=Z
be the center of
R
, and let
M
be a non-zero
R
-
R
-bimodule. A unital subring
K
of
T(R,M)
is centrally essential if and only if

K=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\right|\;a,b\in R;\,m\in M\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,a-b\in I\vartriangleleft R\right\},

where
I=\left\{a-b\in R\mid A=\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\in K\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,Z(M)(a-b)=0\right\}
, and the subbimodule
N=\text{Ann}_{M}I\cap C_{R}(M)
of
M
and the ideal
J=\text{Ann}_{R}N
of
R
satisfy the following conditions:

1)

\text{Ann}_{I}M\cap Z\leq_{e}I_{Z}
and
J\cap Z\leq_{e}J_{Z}
;

2)

N\leq_{e}M_{Z}
.

1.3. Corollary.
Let
R
be a commutative ring. The ring
K
is centrally essential if and only if
\text{Ann}_{I}M\leq_{e}I
and
\text{Ann}_{M}I\leq_{e}M
.

1.4. Corollary.
Let
R
be a commutative domain. The ring
K
is centrally essential if and only if
\text{Ann}_{I}M\neq 0
and
\text{Ann}_{M}I\leq_{e}M
.

1.5. Corollary.
If
R
is a field and
K
is a centrally essential ring, then
K
is commutative.

For a ring
R
and an
(R,R)
-bimodule
M
, the
trivial extension

\Lambda=R\ltimes M
of
R
by
M
(for example, see
[
8
]
) is the Cartesian product
R\times M
with componentwise addition and multiplication given by
(r,m)(r^{\prime},m^{\prime})=(rr^{\prime},mr^{\prime}+rm^{\prime})
. Then
\Lambda\cong\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}r&m\\
0&r\\
\end{pmatrix}\right|\;\;r\in R,m\in M\right\}
. As a formal triangular matrix subring defined above, we have
I=0
and
N=C_{R}(M)
.

1.6. Corollary.
The ring
\Lambda
is centrally essential if and only if
J\cap Z\leq_{e}J_{Z}
and
C_{R}(M)\leq_{e}M_{Z}
.

We also consider a class of subrings in the full matrix ring
M_{3}(R)
over the ring
R
. Let
s,t\in Z(R)
. We define rings

H_{(s,t)}(R)=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{pmatrix}\right.\in M_{3}(R)\;\mid\;a,c,d,e,f\in R;\,a=d+sc,d=f+te\right\}.

The rings
H_{(s,t)}(R)
were previously investigated for being
CU
rings and
C\Delta
rings; for more details see
[
5
]
,
[
2
]
.

1.7. Theorem.
The ring
H_{(s,t)}(R)
is centrally essential if and only if
R
is a centrally essential ring.

Proof of Theorem 1.2

We recall that
Z
denotes the center of the ring
R
,
Z(K)
is the center of the ring
K
,
N=\text{Ann}_{M}I\cap C_{R}(M)
.

2.1. Lemma.

Z(K)=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\right|\;a,b\in Z,\,a-b\in\text{Ann}_{I}M,\,m\in N\right\}.

Proof.
If
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K)
, then

AA^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}aa^{\prime}&am^{\prime}+mb^{\prime}\\
0&bb^{\prime}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}a&a^{\prime}m+m^{\prime}b\\
0&b^{\prime}b\\
\end{pmatrix}=A^{\prime}A

for any matrix
A^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&m^{\prime}\\
0&b^{\prime}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in K
. Then
aa^{\prime}=a^{\prime}a
,
bb^{\prime}=b^{\prime}b
for all
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in R
, i.e.
a,b\in Z
. In addition, we have
am^{\prime}+mb^{\prime}=a^{\prime}m+m^{\prime}b
. Since
a,b\in Z
, we have
(a-b)m^{\prime}=a^{\prime}m-mb^{\prime}
. If
a^{\prime}=b^{\prime}=1
, then
(a-b)m^{\prime}=0
for any
m^{\prime}\in M
, i.e.
a-b\in\text{Ann}_{I}M
. Then

mb^{\prime}=a^{\prime}m
for all
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in R
. If
a^{\prime}=b^{\prime}
, then
m\in C_{R}(M)
. Now, if
a^{\prime}\neq b^{\prime}
, then
m(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})=0
and
m\in\text{Ann}_{M}I
. Therefore
m\in N
.

Conversely, let
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\in K
where
a,b\in Z
,
a-b\in\text{Ann}_{I}M
and
m\in N
. For any matrix
A^{\prime}\in K
, we have
aa^{\prime}=a^{\prime}a
,
bb^{\prime}=b^{\prime}b
for all
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in R
, since
a,b\in Z
. Furthemore, it follows from
m\in N
that
m(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})=0
. Finally, it follows from the conditions
a,b\in Z
and
a-b\in\text{Ann}_{I}M
that
m^{\prime}b=am^{\prime}
Therefore,
AA^{\prime}=A^{\prime}A
and
A\in Z(K)
.
\square

2.2. Completion of the Proof of Theorem 1.2.

Assume that
K
is a centrally essential ring.
It follows from Lemma 2.1 that for arbitrary matrices
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K)
and
A^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&m^{\prime}\\
0&b^{\prime}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in K
, we have
m(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})=0
. With the use of Remark 1.1, we have that the set

I=\left\{a-b\in R\mid A=\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\in K\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,Z(M)(a-b)=0\right\}
forms a proper ideal of
R
. It is easy to see that the set of matrices
K
forms a ring.

Now we verify that conditions 1) and 2) hold.

1)
Let
0\neq i\in I\backslash(\text{Ann}_{I}M\cap Z)
. Then the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}
is not contained in
Z(K)
. Since
K
is a centrally essential ring, we have

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&n\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ia&in\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}

for some central matrices
\begin{pmatrix}a&n\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}ia&in\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}
. It follows from Lemma 2.1 that
in=0
. Then
0\neq ia\in\text{Ann}_{I}M\cap Z
, where
a\in Z
.

Let
0\neq j\in J\backslash(J\cap Z)
. Then
\begin{pmatrix}j&0\\
0&j\\
\end{pmatrix}\notin Z(K)
and

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}j&0\\
0&j\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&n\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ja&jn\\
0&jb\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K),

where
\begin{pmatrix}a&n\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K)
. Since
n\in N
, we have
jn=0
. Therefore,
0\neq ja\in Z
or
0\neq jb\in Z
for
a,b\in Z
.

2)
Let
m\notin N
for some
0\neq m\in M
. Then
\begin{pmatrix}0&m\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\notin Z(K)
and

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}0&m\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&n\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&mb\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K),

where
0\neq mb\in N\cap mZ
.

Conversely, assume that the ring
K
is of the specified form and conditions 1) and 2) hold. We prove that the ring
K
is centrally essential.

Let
\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\notin Z(K)
. If
a=b=0
, then it follows from
2)
that
0\neq mz\in N
for some
z\in Z
. Then

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}0&m\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}z&0\\
0&z\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&mz\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K).

Let
a\neq 0
. If
a\notin J
, then
am^{\prime}\neq 0
for some
m^{\prime}\in N
. Then

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&m^{\prime}\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&am^{\prime}\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

where
\begin{pmatrix}0&m^{\prime}\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K)
. If
am^{\prime}\notin N
, then it follows from
2)
that
0\neq(am^{\prime})z\in N
for some
z\in Z
. Then

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&m^{\prime}z\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&am^{\prime}z\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K).

Let
a\in J
. It follows from
1)
that
0\neq ac\in J\cap Z
for some
c\in Z
. We assume that
ac=bc
. If
mc\in N
, then

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c&0\\
0&c\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ac&mc\\
0&ac\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K).

Otherwise, it follows from
2)
that there exists
z\in Z
such that
0\neq(mc)z\in N
. Then

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}cz&0\\
0&cz\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}acz&mcz\\
0&acz\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K).

Let
ac-bc\neq 0
. It follows from
1)
that there exists
z\in Z
such that
0\neq(ac-bc)z\in\text{Ann}_{I}M\cap Z
. Then
bcz\in Z
. If
mcz\in N
, then

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}cz&0\\
0&cz\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}acz&mcz\\
0&bcz\ \end{pmatrix}\in Z(K).

Otherwise, it follows from
2)
that the relation
0\neq(mcz)z^{\prime}\in N
is true for some
z^{\prime}\in Z
. Then

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}czz^{\prime}&0\\
0&czz^{\prime}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}aczz^{\prime}&(mcz)z^{\prime}\\
0&bczz^{\prime}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in Z(K).

It remains to consider the case
a=0
,
b\neq 0
. By condition
1)
, we have
0\neq bc\in\text{Ann}_{I}M\cap Z
for some
c\in Z
. If
mc\in N
, then

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}0&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c&0\\
0&c\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&mc\\
0&bc\ \end{pmatrix}\in Z(K).

Otherwise, it follows from
2)
that
0\neq(mc)z\in N
for some
z\in Z
. Then

0\neq\begin{pmatrix}0&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}cz&0\\
0&cz\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&mcz\\
0&bcz\ \end{pmatrix}\in Z(K).

Consequently,
K
is a centrally essential ring.
\square

Proof of Theorem 1.7

3.1. Lemma
[
5
, Lemma 3.3]
.
Let
R
be a ring and let
s,t\in Z(R)
. Then

Z(H_{(s,t)}(R))=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{pmatrix}\right.\in H_{(s,t)}(R)\;\mid\;c,e,f\in Z(R)\right\}.

3.2. Lemma.
If
H_{(s,t)}(R)
is a centrally essential ring, then
R
is a centrally essential ring.

Proof.
Let
0\neq r\in R
. Since
H_{(s,t)}(R)
is a centrally essential ring, we have that for a matrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}r&0&0\\
0&r&0\\
0&0&r\end{pmatrix}
(in this case, we have
c=e=0
and
a=d=f=r
), there exists a matrix

B=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&0&0\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}&e^{\prime}\\
0&0&f^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\in Z(H_{(s,t)}(R))
such that

0\neq AB=\begin{pmatrix}r&0&0\\
0&r&0\\
0&0&r\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&0&0\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}&e^{\prime}\\
0&0&f^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ra^{\prime}&0&0\\
rc^{\prime}&rd^{\prime}&re^{\prime}\\
0&0&rf^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\in Z(H_{(s,t)}(R)),

where
rc^{\prime},re^{\prime},rf^{\prime}\in Z(R)
and at least one of these products is not equal to zero. Otherwise,
rd^{\prime}=r(f^{\prime}+te^{\prime})=rf^{\prime}+tre^{\prime}=0
,
ra^{\prime}=r(d^{\prime}+sc^{\prime})=rd^{\prime}+src^{\prime}=0
. Then
AB=0
. This is a contradiction. Consequently,
R
is a centrally essential ring.
\square

3.3. Lemma.
Let
R
be a centrally essential ring and let
0\neq A=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{pmatrix}\in H_{(s,t)}(R)
. In each of the following cases, the ring
H_{(s,t)}(R)
contains a central matrix
B
such that

0\neq AB\in Z(H_{(s,t)}(R))
:

1)

a\neq 0
;

2)

c=0
;

3)

f\neq 0
.

Proof.

1)
Let
a\neq 0
. Then
d\neq-sc
. In the matrix
B=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&0&0\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}&e^{\prime}\\
0&0&f^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}
, we set
e^{\prime}=f^{\prime}=0
. Then
d^{\prime}=0
and
a^{\prime}=sc^{\prime}
. We have

AB=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}sc^{\prime}&0&0\\
c^{\prime}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}sac^{\prime}&0&0\\
(cs+d)c^{\prime}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
0\neq(cs+d)c^{\prime}\in Z(R)
for a suitable
c^{\prime}\in Z(R)
and
0\neq AB\in Z(H_{(s,t)}(R))
,
B\in Z(H_{(s,t)}(R))
.

2)
Let
c=0
. If
d\neq 0
, then
a\neq 0
, and we are in case
1)
. Let
d=-sc=0
. Then
a=0
,
f=-te
and
e\neq 0
(otherwise
A=0
). In this case, we have

AB=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&e\\
0&0&-te\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&0&0\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}&e^{\prime}\\
0&0&f^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&ef^{\prime}\\
0&0&-tef^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},

where
c^{\prime},e^{\prime}\in Z(R)
and
0\neq ef^{\prime}\in Z(R)
for a suitable
f^{\prime}\in Z(R)
.

3)
Assume that
1)
and
2)
do not hold. It follows from
a=0
that
-sc=f+te
and
f=-sc-te\neq 0
. We set
c^{\prime}=d^{\prime}=0
. Then
a^{\prime}=0
and
f^{\prime}=-te^{\prime}
. We have:

AB=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&e^{\prime}\\
0&0&f^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&de^{\prime}+ef^{\prime}\\
0&0&ff^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},

where
ff^{\prime}=-tfe^{\prime}
,
de^{\prime}+ef^{\prime}=-sce^{\prime}-tee^{\prime}=(-sc-te)e^{\prime}=fe^{\prime}
. Then
0\neq fe^{\prime}\in Z(R)
for a suitable
e^{\prime}\in Z(R)
.
\square

3.4. Lemma.
If
R
is a centrally essential ring, then for any
a,b\in R
not simultaneously equal to zero, there exists an element
z\in Z(R)
such that
az,bz\in Z(R)
and
az\neq 0
or
bz\neq 0
. In particular, for any non-zero-divisors
a,b\in R
, there exists
z\in Z(R)
such that

0\neq az,bz\in Z(R)
.

Proof.
It is sufficient to consider the case
a,b\notin Z(R)
,
a\neq 0
,
b\neq 0
. Since
R
is centrally essential, we have that
0\neq az\in Z(R)
and
0\neq(az-b)z^{\prime}\in Z(R)
for some
z,z^{\prime}\in Z(R)
. Then
bz^{\prime}\in Z(R)
. If
azz^{\prime}\neq 0
, then
bzz^{\prime}\in Z(R)
. If
azz^{\prime}=0
, then
bz^{\prime}\neq 0
. Now, if
az^{\prime}\neq 0
, then
0\neq(az^{\prime})z^{\prime\prime}\in Z(R)
for some
z^{\prime\prime}\in Z(R)
and
bz^{\prime}z^{\prime\prime}\in Z(R)
.
\square

3.5. Completion of the proof of Theorem 1.7.

Let
H_{(s,t)}(R)
be a centrally essential ring. Then
R
is a centrally essential ring by Lemma 3.2.

Conversely, let
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{pmatrix}\in H_{(s,t)}(R)
. Let us find such a matrix
B=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&0&0\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}&e^{\prime}\\
0&0&f^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\in Z(H_{(s,t)}(R))
that
0\neq AB\in Z(H_{(s,t)}(R))
. It follows from Lemma 3.3 that such a matrix
B
exists provided
a\neq 0
,
f\neq 0
and

c=0
. Therefore, we assume that
a=f=0
and
c\neq 0
.
We have:

AB=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&0&0\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}&e^{\prime}\\
0&0&f^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
ca^{\prime}+dc^{\prime}&dd^{\prime}&de^{\prime}+ef^{\prime}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

where

ca^{\prime}+dc^{\prime}=c(d^{\prime}+sc^{\prime})-scc^{\prime}=cd^{\prime}=cf^{\prime}+tce^{\prime},

dd^{\prime}=-scd^{\prime}=-sc(f^{\prime}+te^{\prime})=-scf^{\prime}-stce^{\prime},

de^{\prime}+ef^{\prime}=-sce^{\prime}+ef^{\prime}.

We assume that
sc\neq 0
or
tc\neq 0
. We set
f^{\prime}=c^{\prime}=0
. Then
a^{\prime}=d^{\prime}=te^{\prime}
and
cd^{\prime}=tce^{\prime}
,
dd^{\prime}=-stce^{\prime}
,
de^{\prime}=-sce^{\prime}
. By Lemma 3.4, we have
(sc)e^{\prime},(tc)e^{\prime}\in Z(R)
for a suitable
e^{\prime}\in Z(R)
, and at least one of these products is not equal to zero. In both cases,
dd^{\prime}\in Z(R)
. Consequently,

0\neq AB\in Z(H_{(s,t)}(R))
, where
B\in Z(H_{(s,t)}(R))
.

If
sc=0
and
tc=0
, then
ca^{\prime}+dc^{\prime}=cf^{\prime}
,
dd^{\prime}=0
,
de^{\prime}+ef^{\prime}=ef^{\prime}
. It follows from Lemma 3.4 that for some
f^{\prime}\in Z(R)
, we have that
cf^{\prime},ef^{\prime}\in Z(R)
and
cf^{\prime}\neq 0
or
ef^{\prime}\neq 0
. Consequently,
H_{(s,t)}(R)
is a centrally essential ring.
\square

4
Remarks and Examples

For a ring
R
, we denote by
C(R)
the set of non-zero-divisors of
R
.

4.1. Remark.
Let
R
be a ring and let
I
be a central ideal such that
I\cap C(R)\neq 0
. Then
R
is a centrally essential ring.

Indeed, for
0\neq r\in R
, we have
0\neq rz\in I\subseteq Z(R)
for every
z\in I\cap C(R)
.

4.2. Remark.
Let
R
be a semiprime ring. The ring
R
is commutative if and only if
I\cap C(R)\neq 0
for some central ideal
I
. In particular, a domain is commutative if and only if it contains a non-zero central ideal.

Indeed, if
R
is commutative, then we may take the ring
R
as
I
. In the converse implication, it follows from Remark 4.1 that
R
is a centrally essential ring. Then the ring
R
is commutative by
[
7
, Proposition 2.8]
.

4.3. Example.

[
6
, Example 5.1]
We consider a non-commutative ring

K=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}\;\;\right|\;\;a,b\in\mathbb{Z},\,a-b\in 2\mathbb{Z},\,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}\right\}.

It follows from Corollary 1.4 that the ring
K
is centrally essential. Moreover, it is shown in
[
6
]
that
K
is a centrally essential ring such that all quotient rings of
K
are centrally essential (such rings are called
completely centrally essential rings
in
[
6
]
).

4.4. Remark.
Let
R
be a ring and let
I
be a central ideal of
R
such that the quotient ring
R/I
is commutative (such rings are called
CIFC
rings
in
[
4
]
). The ring

K=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\right.\Big|\;a-b\in I;\,m\in R/I\right\}

is centrally essential. Indeed, with the use of the notation of Theorem 1.2, we have
N=R/I
and
J=\text{Ann}_{R}R/I=I
. Therefore, the conditions 1) and 2) of the theorem are fulfilled. Consequently,
K
is a centrally essential ring.

4.5. Example.
We give an example of a centrally essential ring
K
such that the ring
R
is not centrally essential. We consider the
CIFC
ring from
[
4
, Example 1.2]
as the ring
R
. Let
F
be a field and let
A=\langle x,y\rangle
be the free algebra generated by non-commuting variables
x,y
over
F
. Let
B=\{f\in A|\,f(0,0)=0\}
. We consider the ideal
I
of
A
generated by all products
abc
, where
a,b,c\in B
. We set
R=A/I
. We identify elements of
A
with their images in
R
and we remark that
B^{3}=0
. Furthemore, let
J
be the ideal of
R
generated by
xy
and
yx
. It is clear that
J\subseteq Z(R)
. In addition, it is proved in
[
4
, Example 1.2]
that the quotient ring
R/J
is commutative. Consequently,
R
is a
CIFC
ring. In addition,
R
is not a centrally essential ring. Indeed, for example, for the element
x
, there does not exist
z\in Z(R)
such that
0\neq xz\in Z(R)
. However, the ring
K

K=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\right.\Big|\;a,b\in R;\ a-b\in J;\,m\in R/J\right\}

is centrally essential by Remark 4.4.

4.6. Remark.
In
[
7
, Example 1.7]
, it is proved that if a ring
R
has a central ideal
I
such that
R/I
is a field, then
R
is a centrally essential ring. Example 4.5 shows that this assertion is not true provided
R/I
is a commutative ring which is not a field.

4.7. Example.
If a ring
R
is centrally essential, then the ring
K
is not necessarily centrally essential. Indeed, the ring

K=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\right.\Big|\;a,b\in\mathbb{Z};\,a-b\in 2\mathbb{Z};\,m\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

is not centrally essential by Corollary 1.4, since
\text{Ann}_{I}M=\text{Ann}_{2\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}=0
.

4.8. Example.
Let
R
be a commutative uniserial artinian ring with Jacobson radical
J=J(R)
of nilpotence index
n
. Then
R
is a local ring with composition series
0=J^{n}\subset J^{n-1}\subset\ldots\subset J\subset R
. We consider the ring

K=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\right|\;a,b\in R;\,m\in J\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,a-b\in J\right\}.

We have
\text{Ann}_{I}M=\text{Ann}_{M}I=J^{n-1}\leq_{e}J
. By Corollary 1.3, the ring
K
is centrally essential.
In particular, specializing
R
, we obtain a sequence of finite non-commutative centrally essential subrings
K
of
T(R,M)
for for each prime integer
p
:

K=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}a&m\\
0&b\\
\end{pmatrix}\right|\;a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}};\,m\in p\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,a-b\in p\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}\right\}.

The ring
K
is non-commutative for
k>2
. The smallest ring of the specified form has
128
elements for
p=2
and
k=3
.
