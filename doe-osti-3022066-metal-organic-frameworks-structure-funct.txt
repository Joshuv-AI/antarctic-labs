Executive Summary

This project sought to develop and deploy optically driven dynamic nuclear polarization (DNP)
within metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) to enable (i) substantial sensitivity enhancement in
nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, (ii) structural interrogation of MOFs and their
defects with unprecedented sensitivity, and (iii) exploration of MOFs as platforms for quantum
sensing and quantum information science.
 The central concept was the incorporation of organic linkers possessing long-lived
photoexcited triplet states into crystalline MOF architectures. Upon optical excitation, these triplet
states can generate highly polarized electron spin populations. Through controlled spin
manipulation protocols, this polarization can be transferred to surrounding nuclear spins, yielding
large enhancements in NMR signal intensity. Realizing this vision required advances in three
interconnected areas:
 1. Synthesis of MOFs containing optically addressable triplet-state linkers
 2. Development of specialized pulsed EPR/NMR instrumentation
 3. Design of field-cycling and spin-transfer protocols to enable optical DNP
Despite substantial disruptions due to the COVID-19 pandemic that brought upon significant
supply chain disruptions in critical components required for instrumentation construction and
personnel challenges, the project achieved several major milestones:

 •Successful synthesis of aluminum-, magnesium-, and zirconium-based MOFs
 incorporating pyrene and porphyrin linkers known for favorable triplet-state photophysics.
 • Construction of a custom pulsed and transient optical EPR spectrometer operating at 0.3 T
 with cryogenic capability (4–300 K), RF detection coils for 1H and 13C NMR, and fully
 programmable microwave/RF control.
 • Development of a unique cryogenic field-cycling and shuttling platform integrated with a
 9.4 T superconducting NMR magnet, enabling rapid optical hyperpolarization and
 broadband nuclear spin manipulation across 10 mT–9.4 T.
 • Demonstration of system performance for hyperpolarization and nuclear spin readout using
 model systems, including ^13C spins in diamond polarized via optically pumped NV⁻
 centers.
The instrumentation and materials platform established in this project has provided the technical
foundation for optical DNP in MOFs and position the research team to pursue high-sensitivity
structural analysis and quantum-enabled sensing in extended porous solids.

Technical Narrative
MOF Synthesis and Characterization
 A central objective of this project was the incorporation of optically active, long-lived
triplet-state chromophores into crystalline MOF architectures. The photophysics of singlet–triplet
intersystem crossing in polyaromatic systems is well established1, and such states have been
exploited previously to generate spin polarization in molecular crystals. Translating this chemistry
into extended porous frameworks required linker modification to enable metal coordination while
preserving triplet-state lifetimes.
We successfully synthesized:
 • Aluminum and magnesium MOFs incorporating pyrene-based linkers
 • Zirconium MOFs incorporating porphyrin-based linkers
These linkers were functionalized with coordinating groups (e.g., dicarboxylates) compatible with
MOF assembly. Structural integrity was confirmed through standard crystallographic and

Pulsed and Transient Optical EPR
Characterizing photoexcited triplet states and their spin dynamics required development of new
instrumentation. We designed and constructed a novel pulsed and transient optical EPR system
centered around a 0.3 T magnet with the following features:
 • X-band microwave cavity with optical access
 • Programmable magnet power supply and field-stepping controller
 • Integrated arbitrary waveform generator for microwave and RF pulse shaping
 • Internal RF coils for pulsed ^1H and ^13C NMR detection
 • Cryogenic operation from 4–300 K via integrated cryostat
The system enables transient EPR detection immediately following laser excitation, allowing
measurement of triplet-state spin polarization and relaxation dynamics as a function of magnetic
field and temperature.
 The integrated RF capability was incorporated to enable future electron-to-nuclear
polarization transfer experiments, including pulsed spin-locking at the nuclear Larmor frequency.
This design positions the apparatus for direct optical DNP experiments within MOF samples.

Cryogenic Field-Cycling and Hyperpolarization System
 In parallel, a second major instrumentation advance was a cryogenic field-cycling platform
designed to implement optical DNP protocols requiring low-field polarization generation followed
by high-field NMR detection.
This system integrates:
 • A 4 T superconducting NMR magnet
 • External sample shuttling assembly
 • Operating magnetic field range of 10 mT–9.4 T
 • Temperature range ~10 K–300 K
 • Continuous cryogen management for long-duration experiments
The instrument enables rapid (~60 s) nuclear hyperpolarization followed by high-field NMR
interrogation using advanced pulse sequences involving large numbers of RF pulses.

Integration and Outlook
 The combined advances in MOF synthesis and custom instrumentation establish the
necessary platform for implementing optical triplet-driven DNP in extended porous solids. While
full demonstration of MOF-based optical hyperpolarization remains ongoing, all critical enabling

components—materials, pulsed EPR capability, cryogenic field-cycling, and polarization transfer
protocols—are now operational.
 This work significantly expands the technical infrastructure available for quantum-
enhanced NMR spectroscopy and lays the groundwork for high-sensitivity characterization of
framework materials and defect structures relevant to energy science.
