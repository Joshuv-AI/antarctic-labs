Introduction

Two-time-scale stochastic approximation (TTSA) studies coupled recursions in which a fast iterate is updated with stepsize
\alpha_{k}
and a slow iterate is updated with a smaller stepsize
\beta_{k}
. In its fixed-point form, the recursion is often written as

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}\big(f(x_{k},y_{k})-x_{k}+M_{k+1}\big),

\displaystyle y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=y_{k}+\beta_{k}\big(g(x_{k},y_{k})-y_{k}+M^{\prime}_{k+1}\big),

where
\beta_{k}/\alpha_{k}\to 0
in the truly two-time-scale regime. TTSA is a central tool in reinforcement learning, stochastic optimization, stochastic control, saddle-point learning, and game control; see, for example, the classical monographs and early analyses
[
9
,
8
,
1
,
7
]
.

For linear TTSA, the slow iterate may enjoy a decoupled
O(k^{-1})
mean-square rate under
\beta_{k}=\Theta(k^{-1})
even when
\alpha_{k}=\Theta(k^{-a})
with
a<1

[
7
,
6
,
5
]
. For nonlinear contractive TTSA the situation is more delicate. Doan
[
3
]
established finite-time guarantees for nonlinear TTSA, and a recent averaged-noise analysis of Chandak
[
2
]
improved the truly separated nonlinear rate to
O(k^{-a})
under standard contractive assumptions. Meanwhile, Han, Li, and Zhang
[
4
]
proved that the decoupled
O(k^{-1})
rate is recovered under
nested local linearity
assumptions, and provided empirical evidence that local linearity may be necessary. Chandak
[
2
]
explicitly posed it as an open problem whether
O(k^{-1})
is possible for nonlinear TTSA
without additional assumptions
, conjecturing that
O(k^{-a})
is the best achievable rate in general.

This leaves a structural question:

What feature of the slow drift determines whether the slow iterate converges at the decoupled
k^{-1}
rate or at the fast-tracking rate
k^{-a}
?

It also raises an algorithmic open problem: if the slow rate is limited by nonlinear bias, can a change in the update rule remove that bias and recover the decoupled rate without adding simulator access or oracle debiasing?

We answer both questions using a
local normal-form
model that isolates the rate-determining terms from the otherwise involved mechanics of the general TTSA recursion. Let
X_{k}
denote the fast tracking error and
Y_{k}
the slow error. After linearizing the fast dynamics and expanding the slow drift in the fast error, the leading model is

\displaystyle X_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-\alpha_{k}A)X_{k}+\alpha_{k}\xi_{k+1},

(1)

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-\beta_{k}B)Y_{k}+\beta_{k}\big(HX_{k}+R(X_{k})+\zeta_{k+1}\big).

(2)

Here
HX_{k}
is the locally linear leakage of fast error into the slow recursion, and
R(X_{k})
is the genuinely nonlinear remainder. We assume

\left\lVert R(x)\right\rVert\leq L_{R}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{1+\rho},\qquad\rho\in[0,1],

where
\rho
measures local regularity:
\rho=0
is a Lipschitz remainder,
\rho=1
a second
-
order remainder.

Prior work
[
2
,
4
]
suggested that decoupled
O(k^{-1})
slow convergence requires local linearity, and that without local linearity the best possible rate is
O(k^{-a})
. Our results show that this binary picture is incomplete. The determining quantity is the local regularity and predictable bias of the nonlinear remainder. For an order-
(1+\rho)
remainder, the sharp uncorrected rate is
O(k^{-1}+k^{-a(1+\rho)})
, with a matching lower bound. Thus local linearity is sufficient but not necessary: sufficiently regular nonlinear remainders still recover the decoupled rate.

Sharp rate for the uncorrected recursion.

For
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{0}(k+k_{0})^{-a}
and
\beta_{k}=\beta_{0}(k+k_{0})^{-1}
,
a\in(1/2,1)
, our first main theorem proves

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\left((k+k_{0})^{-1}+(k+k_{0})^{-a(1+\rho)}\right).

(3)

The term
(k+k_{0})^{-1}
is the usual stochastic approximation noise floor for the slow recursion. The term
(k+k_{0})^{-a(1+\rho)}
is the squared nonlinear bias induced by the fast tracking fluctuations. Thus the uncorrected slow recursion exhibits the phase transition

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}=\begin{cases}O(k^{-a(1+\rho)}),&a(1+\rho)<1,\\
O(k^{-1}),&a(1+\rho)\geq 1.\end{cases}

Why the linear leakage does not hurt.

The fast variable has
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}=\Theta(\alpha_{k})=\Theta(k^{-a})
. A direct input-to-state bound on the term
HX_{k}
would therefore lose the desired
k^{-1}
rate. The key observation, also underlying averaged-noise analyses of nonlinear TTSA, is that
X_{k}
itself satisfies a stable stochastic approximation recursion. Consequently,

AX_{k}=\alpha_{k}^{-1}(X_{k}-X_{k+1})+\xi_{k+1}.

After substituting this identity into the slow convolution, the contribution of
HX_{k}
splits into a martingale term with variance
O(k^{-1})
and a telescoping Abel-transform term that is also
O(k^{-1})
. This is the precise mechanism by which local linearity restores decoupling.

Why the nonlinear remainder is rate-limiting.

In contrast, if
R(x)
is of order
\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{1+\rho}
, then

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert R(X_{k})\right\rVert\asymp\alpha_{k}^{(1+\rho)/2}\asymp k^{-a(1+\rho)/2}

in Gaussian normal-form examples. This contribution is not a martingale fluctuation and therefore does not average down to
k^{-1}
; it appears as a slowly varying bias in the slow recursion. We prove that this obstruction is not an artifact of the proof by constructing a one-dimensional Gaussian instance for which

\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}^{2}]\geq c_{1}k^{-a(1+\rho)}+c_{2}k^{-1}.

(4)

The lower bound uses
H=0
, so it is caused solely by nonlinear curvature, not by linear leakage. It is a lower bound for the uncorrected update rule (
2
); it does not preclude algorithms that estimate and subtract the predictable nonlinear bias.

Online bias tracking resolves the algorithmic question in the normal form.

The lower bound identifies what must be removed. We introduce an auxiliary bias tracker

M_{k+1}=M_{k}+\gamma_{k}\big(R(X_{k})-M_{k}\big),\qquad\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{0}(k+k_{0})^{-c},

(5)

with an intermediate time scale
\beta_{k}\ll\gamma_{k}\ll\alpha_{k}
. The corrected slow update is

\widetilde{Y}_{k+1}=(I-\beta_{k}B)\widetilde{Y}_{k}+\beta_{k}\big(HX_{k}+R(X_{k})-M_{k}+\zeta_{k+1}\big).

(6)

This is a fully online correction for the normal-form recursion: it uses only the observed nonlinear term
R(X_{k})
, adds no inner fast loops, and assumes no simulator or oracle access. The theorem proves

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\widetilde{Y}_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq Ck^{-1}

for every
\rho\in[0,1]
, including the regimes
a(1+\rho)<1
where the uncorrected recursion has the lower bound (
4
). The proof uses a second Abel transform. Since

R(X_{k})-M_{k}=\gamma_{k}^{-1}(M_{k+1}-M_{k}),

the slow convolution of the corrected nonlinear input telescopes with coefficient
\beta_{k}/\gamma_{k}
; the intermediate time scale is chosen so that the resulting boundary and variation terms are below the
k^{-1}
noise floor.

Technical novelty.

The paper has two related technical messages. First, the same Abel-transform idea that averages locally linear fast-error leakage also gives a sharp diagnostic for nonlinear curvature: only the predictable part of the nonlinear input survives slow averaging. Second, once this predictable part is explicitly tracked on an intermediate time scale, it can be removed by another Abel-transform cancellation. This proves that the lower bound is an obstruction for the raw recursion, not an information-theoretic limitation of the normal-form problem class.

Contributions.

The paper makes five technical contributions.

1.

We formulate a normal-form TTSA model that separates the locally linear fast-error leakage from the genuinely nonlinear slow-drift remainder.

2.

We prove a finite-time upper bound with the regularity-dependent rate
O(k^{-1}+k^{-a(1+\rho)})
. The proof is non-asymptotic and relies on an Abel-transform form of averaged-noise cancellation.

3.

We prove a matching lower bound in a scalar Gaussian subclass. Hence, for the uncorrected normal-form recursion, the exponent
\min\{1,a(1+\rho)\}
cannot be improved uniformly.

4.

We introduce an online three-time-scale bias-tracking recursion that subtracts a running estimate of the nonlinear predictable bias. Under the same normal-form stability, moment, and regularity assumptions, the corrected slow iterate satisfies
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\widetilde{Y}_{k}\right\rVert^{2}=O(k^{-1})
for all
\rho\in[0,1]
.

5.

We transfer the normal-form phase transition to nonlinear TTSA and clarify its mechanism. A centering/debiasing result shows that nonlinear fluctuations of the same instantaneous size recover the decoupled
k^{-1}
rate when they are martingale-centered. Localized transfer and verification theorems in fast-manifold coordinates then show that the same phase transition persists under moving fast equilibria, induced slow-noise perturbations, variable local linearizations, and both separable and mixed higher-order Taylor terms, whenever the required finite-time localized moment estimates hold.

Local normal form

We work on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. All random variables below are assumed to be adapted to this filtration. For a positive integer
d
,
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
denotes the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the induced operator norm on matrices.

Fix
a\in(1/2,1)
and define

\alpha_{k}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{(k+k_{0})^{a}},\qquad\beta_{k}=\frac{\beta_{0}}{k+k_{0}},\qquad k\geq 0,

(7)

where
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}>0
. The offset
k_{0}\geq 2
is chosen once and for all large enough so that all contraction inequalities below hold and so that every small-step comparison used in the proofs is valid. In particular, we require
\lambda\alpha_{k}\leq 1/4
,
b\beta_{k}\leq 1/2
,
2a/\bar{k}\leq(\lambda/2)\alpha_{k}
, and the fourth-moment comparison (
69
). Such a finite choice exists because
a<1
and
\bar{k}^{-1}=o(\alpha_{k})
. If a smaller offset is used, the same rates hold after a finite burn-in. We write

\bar{k}\coloneqq k+k_{0}.

The normal form is the coupled recursion

\displaystyle X_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-\alpha_{k}A)X_{k}+\alpha_{k}\xi_{k+1},

(8)

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-\beta_{k}B)Y_{k}+\beta_{k}\big(HX_{k}+R(X_{k})+\zeta_{k+1}\big),

(9)

where
X_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
is the fast tracking error and
Y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
is the slow error. The matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}\times d_{x}}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}\times d_{y}}
describe the local stable linearizations of the fast and slow dynamics. The matrix
H\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}\times d_{x}}
is the first-order dependence of the slow drift on the fast tracking error. The map
R:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
is the nonlinear remainder.

Assumption 2.1

(Stability and noise)
.

There exist constants
\lambda,b>0
such that, for all
k\geq 0
,

\left\lVert I-\alpha_{k}A\right\rVert\leq 1-\lambda\alpha_{k},\qquad\left\lVert I-\beta_{k}B\right\rVert\leq 1-b\beta_{k}.

(10)

The matrix
A
is invertible. The noise sequences are martingale differences:

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0.

(11)

Moreover, for a finite constant
\sigma_{4}
,

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\xi_{k+1}\right\rVert^{4}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq\sigma_{4}^{4},\qquad\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\zeta_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq\sigma_{4}^{2}.

(12)

The initial conditions
X_{0},Y_{0}
are deterministic.

Assumption 2.2

(Remainder regularity)
.

For some
\rho\in[0,1]
and
L_{R}<\infty
,

\left\lVert R(x)\right\rVert\leq L_{R}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{1+\rho},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}.

(13)

The quantity

r\coloneqq b\beta_{0},\qquad s\coloneqq a(1+\rho)

(14)

will appear throughout the paper. The exponent
r
is the polynomial strength of the slow linear contraction, while
s
is the exponent generated by the nonlinear remainder.

Assumption 2.3

(Slow contraction strength)
.

The slow contraction exponent satisfies

r>\frac{1}{2}.

(15)

This is the standard finite-time requirement that the linear part of the slow recursion be strong enough to wash out the initial condition and to make the slow stochastic convolution square summable at rate
k^{-1}
. No additional condition involving the nonlinear exponent
s=a(1+\rho)
is needed. When
s>1
, the nonlinear convolution need only be controlled at the
k^{-1}
noise floor; the more flexible weighted-convolution estimate in the appendix handles this regime.

Remark 2.4

(Relation to nonlinear TTSA)
.

For a nonlinear TTSA recursion, set
X_{k}=x_{k}-x^{*}(y_{k})
and
Y_{k}=y_{k}-y^{*}
. A local expansion of the slow drift around
(x^{*}(y),y)
has the form

g(x,y)-y^{*}=g(x^{*}(y),y)-y^{*}+H(y)(x-x^{*}(y))+R(x-x^{*}(y),y).

The normal form (
8
)–(
9
) freezes the stable linear parts at the fixed point and isolates the two fast-error mechanisms that matter for rates: the locally linear term and the nonlinear remainder. The normal-form theorem is a sharp analysis of the leading local mechanism. Section 4 gives perturbation conditions under which this mechanism transfers to nonlinear TTSA in fast-manifold coordinates, while global stability or localization is handled separately.

,

Theorem

Under the assumptions of Theorem

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\begin{cases}C\bar{k}^{-a(1+\rho)},&a(1+\rho)<1,\\[5.69054pt]

Remark 3.3

(Uniform interpretation of the threshold)
.

The word “threshold” is used in a uniform worst-case sense over the normal-form class. Special instances below the threshold can converge faster, for example when

or when the nonlinear remainder has a symmetry that cancels its leading mean. The lower bound below shows that no rate better than

2.3
alone.

The next theorem shows that the exponent in Corollary

Remark 3.5

(Interpretation)
.

The lower bound uses
H=0
. Thus the obstruction to
k^{-1}
for
a(1+\rho)<1
is not imperfect averaging of the locally linear term. It is the nonzero mean of the nonlinear remainder generated by the fast fluctuation scale
\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert\approx\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}
. The statement is not that every nonlinear remainder creates this obstruction; odd or otherwise cancelling remainders may behave like centered noise. The result is a minimax sharpness statement.

The next proposition makes the last sentence precise in a form that is useful for bias correction. It separates the size of a nonlinear fluctuation from its predictable component. A nonlinear term of size
\alpha_{k}^{(1+\rho)/2}
causes the slower
k^{-a(1+\rho)}
rate only when it has a persistent predictable part of that size.

Proposition 3.6

(Centering and debiasing principle)
.

Consider the same fast recursion (
8
). Replace the slow recursion (
9
) by

Y_{k+1}=(I-\beta_{k}B)Y_{k}+\beta_{k}\big(HX_{k}+m_{k}+\nu_{k+1}+\zeta_{k+1}\big),

(23)

where
m_{k}
is
\mathcal{F}_{k}
-measurable and
\nu_{k+1}
is a martingale difference satisfying

\mathbb{E}[\nu_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\nu_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq K_{\nu}\alpha_{k}^{1+\rho}.

(24)

Assume also that, for some
q>0
and
K_{m}<\infty
,

\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert m_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}\leq K_{m}\bar{k}^{-q}.

(25)

Let

\Psi_{q}(k)=\begin{cases}\bar{k}^{-q},&q<r,\\
\bar{k}^{-r}\log(\bar{k}),&q=r,\\
\bar{k}^{-r},&q>r.\end{cases}

(26)

Under Assumptions

and

such that, for all

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\left(\bar{k}^{-1}+\Psi_{q}(k)^{2}\right).

(27)

In particular, if

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}

3.1
Online bias tracking in the normal form

Proposition
3.6
shows that a nonlinear input of the same instantaneous size as
R(X_{k})
is harmless when it is centered. We now give an online update that performs this centering in the normal-form model itself. The update does not require an independent simulator, a batch of fast loops, or an oracle for
\mathbb{E}[R(X_{k})]
. It simply tracks the observed nonlinear term on an intermediate time scale.

Let

\gamma_{k}=\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\bar{k}^{c}},\qquad\gamma_{0}>0,

(28)

where the exponent
c
satisfies

a<c<\min\left\{1,\frac{1+s}{2}\right\},\qquad s=a(1+\rho).

(29)

The interval is non-empty for every
a\in(1/2,1)
and
\rho\in[0,1]
. Indeed, if
s\geq 1
, then
a<1
gives
a<\min\{1,(1+s)/2\}=1
; if
s<1
, then

\frac{1+s}{2}-a=\frac{1-a+a\rho}{2}>0.

Increasing
k_{0}
if necessary, assume also that
\gamma_{k}\leq 1
for all
k
.

The bias-tracked normal-form recursion is

\displaystyle X_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-\alpha_{k}A)X_{k}+\alpha_{k}\xi_{k+1},

(30)

\displaystyle M_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma_{k})M_{k}+\gamma_{k}R(X_{k}),

(31)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-\beta_{k}B)\widetilde{Y}_{k}+\beta_{k}\big(HX_{k}+R(X_{k})-M_{k}+\zeta_{k+1}\big).

(32)

The variable
M_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
is a running estimate of the predictable nonlinear bias. The time-scale ordering

\frac{\beta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}\to 0,\qquad\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\to 0

means that

evolves faster than the slow recursion but slower than the fast tracking recursion.

Remark 3.8

(What the theorem does and does not require)
.

The theorem uses no additional statistical assumptions beyond those already imposed for Theorem

31
) tracks this observed quantity. In a general nonlinear TTSA recursion, the analogous coupling error is hidden inside
g(x_{k},y_{k})-g(x^{*}(y_{k}),y_{k})
, and
x^{*}(y_{k})
is typically unknown. Extending the online bias tracker to that setting therefore requires additional structure, such as a local Taylor control variate, an estimator of the reduced drift, or a frozen slow iterate batching construction. The present theorem resolves the algorithmic bias question in the local normal form without such additional access.

4
Localized transfer to nonlinear TTSA

The normal-form analysis isolates the mechanism by which local regularity of the slow drift controls the slow rate. We now show how the same mechanism transfers to nonlinear two-time-scale stochastic approximation near a stable equilibrium. The result is local in nature: it applies on time horizons where the iterates remain in a neighborhood in which fast-manifold coordinates and the displayed local error recursions are valid. This includes projected, truncated, or otherwise localized algorithms, as well as analyses combined with an independent stability argument. Any correction terms introduced by the chosen localization mechanism may be included in the perturbations below, provided they satisfy the bounds in Assumption
4.1
.

Consider the nonlinear two-time-scale recursion

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}\big(h(x_{k},y_{k})+M^{x}_{k+1}\big),

(34)

\displaystyle y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=y_{k}+\beta_{k}\big(g(x_{k},y_{k})+M^{y}_{k+1}\big),

(35)

where
h:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
and

g:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
. Suppose that, in a neighborhood

\mathcal{U}
of the target slow equilibrium
y^{*}
, there is a map

\lambda:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
satisfying

h(\lambda(y),y)=0,\qquad y\in\mathcal{U}.

The map
\lambda
is the
fast equilibrium manifold
. For a fixed value of the slow variable
y
, the fast mean field
x\mapsto h(x,y)
has equilibrium
\lambda(y)
. Thus, if
y
were frozen, the fast recursion would be expected to track
\lambda(y)
. In the two-time-scale recursion, however,
y_{k}
changes slowly over time, so the fast iterate tracks a moving target. This motivates the fast-manifold coordinates

X_{k}=x_{k}-\lambda(y_{k}),\qquad Y_{k}=y_{k}-y^{*}.

Here
X_{k}
is the genuine fast tracking error, while
Y_{k}
is the slow error. The reduced slow drift is the map

G(y)=g(\lambda(y),y),

and the point
y^{*}
is a stable zero of this reduced drift in the local model below.

The movement of the manifold
\lambda(y_{k})
is the main new feature relative to the normal form. Since

X_{k+1}=x_{k+1}-\lambda(y_{k+1}),

increments of
y_{k}
feed back into the fast tracking recursion. In particular, the slow noise
M^{y}_{k+1}
produces a perturbation of size
\beta_{k}
in the
X
-recursion. The transfer theorem below treats the nonlinear recursion in fast-manifold coordinates as a perturbation of the normal form. The term
p_{k+1}
represents fast-recursion residuals at the
\alpha_{k}
scale,
d_{k}
represents predictable perturbations entering through the moving manifold at the
\beta_{k}
scale, and
e_{k}
represents higher-order slow-drift terms beyond the components
HX_{k}+R(X_{k})
. The assumption is designed so that these perturbations remain below the phase-transition rate after the same Abel-transform cancellation used in the normal-form analysis.

where

and

5
Proof ideas

All proofs are given in full detail in the appendix. We summarize the main mechanisms here. Throughout this discussion, write

\bar{k}=k+k_{0},\qquad s=a(1+\rho).

The proof separates three effects in the slow recursion: ordinary slow noise, locally linear leakage from the fast tracking error, and nonlinear predictable bias.

Fast fluctuation scale.

The fast recursion is stable and is driven by noise through the stepsize
\alpha_{k}
. A standard Lyapunov calculation therefore gives

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}=O(\alpha_{k}),\qquad\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}=O(\alpha_{k}^{2}).

By interpolation,

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2+2\rho}=O(\alpha_{k}^{1+\rho}).

Thus the natural size of the fast tracking error is

\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert\approx\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\asymp\bar{k}^{-a/2}.

Consequently, a nonlinear remainder satisfying

\left\lVert R(x)\right\rVert\lesssim\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{1+\rho}

has typical magnitude

\left\lVert R(X_{k})\right\rVert\approx\alpha_{k}^{(1+\rho)/2}\asymp\bar{k}^{-a(1+\rho)/2}.

This is the source of the phase-transition exponent.

Unrolling the slow recursion.

Let

\Pi^{B}_{i,k}=\prod_{\ell=i}^{k-1}(I-\beta_{\ell}B),\qquad\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{i,k}\right\rVert\leq C\left(\frac{i+k_{0}}{k+k_{0}}\right)^{r}.

Unrolling the slow recursion gives

Y_{k}=\Pi^{B}_{0,k}Y_{0}+\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}HX_{j}+\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}R(X_{j})+\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\zeta_{j+1}.

The initial-condition term decays faster than the rates of interest once the slow contraction is chosen sufficiently strong.

The slow-noise term is standard. Since
\zeta_{j+1}
is a martingale difference,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\zeta_{j+1}\right\|^{2}\leq C\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}^{2}\left(\frac{j+k_{0}}{k+k_{0}}\right)^{2r}=O(\bar{k}^{-1}).

This is the intrinsic
k^{-1}
noise floor of the slow stochastic approximation.

The nonlinear remainder.

The nonlinear term is bounded directly. By Minkowski’s inequality and the fast moment estimate,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}R(X_{j})\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\left(\frac{j+k_{0}}{k+k_{0}}\right)^{r}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{j}\right\rVert^{2+2\rho}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j<k}\frac{1}{j+k_{0}}\left(\frac{j+k_{0}}{k+k_{0}}\right)^{r}(j+k_{0})^{-a(1+\rho)/2}.

A weighted-convolution estimate gives an
L^{2}
bound of order

O\!\left(\bar{k}^{-\min\{r,a(1+\rho)/2\}}\right)

up to a logarithm in the borderline case. After squaring, this is always bounded by

O\!\left(\bar{k}^{-1}+\bar{k}^{-a(1+\rho)}\right),

because
r>1/2
. This term is not improved by averaging because it is a predictable drift input rather than a martingale-centered fluctuation.

Locally linear leakage and Abel cancellation.

The locally linear term
HX_{j}
is subtler. A direct estimate using

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{j}\right\rVert^{2}=O(\alpha_{j})
would only give the slower
O(k^{-a})
rate. The key point is that
X_{j}
is not an arbitrary error sequence; it is generated by a stable fast recursion. From

X_{j+1}=(I-\alpha_{j}A)X_{j}+\alpha_{j}\xi_{j+1},

we obtain the identity

AX_{j}=\alpha_{j}^{-1}(X_{j}-X_{j+1})+\xi_{j+1}.

With
C_{H}=HA^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}HX_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}C_{H}\xi_{j+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j<k}\frac{\beta_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}C_{H}(X_{j}-X_{j+1}).

The first term is again a martingale convolution and has mean square
O(\bar{k}^{-1})
.

The second term is handled by summation by parts. The boundary coefficients are of order

\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\asymp\bar{k}^{a-1}.

Since
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}=O(\bar{k}^{-a})
, the terminal boundary term contributes

\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}=O(\bar{k}^{a-2})\leq O(\bar{k}^{-1}).

The initial boundary is negligible by contraction, and the interior coefficient differences are small enough to give the same rate. This summation-by-parts step is the Abel-transform form of averaged-noise cancellation: locally linear leakage from the fast recursion averages down to the slow
k^{-1}
noise floor.

Combining the three contributions yields

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\left(\bar{k}^{-1}+\bar{k}^{-a(1+\rho)}\right).

Why the lower bound matches.

The lower bound uses a scalar Gaussian normal form. In that example, the fast variable is Gaussian with variance

\mathbb{E}X_{k}^{2}=\Theta(\alpha_{k}).

Therefore

\mathbb{E}|X_{k}|^{1+\rho}=\Theta\!\left(\alpha_{k}^{(1+\rho)/2}\right)=\Theta\!\left(\bar{k}^{-a(1+\rho)/2}\right).

The slow recursion contains the positive predictable drift input

\gamma|X_{k}|^{1+\rho}.

Summing the slow recursion over the window
j\in[k/2,k]
, where the slow transition weights remain uniformly comparable to constants and the input varies only polynomially, gives

\mathbb{E}Y_{k}\geq c\bar{k}^{-a(1+\rho)/2}.

Hence

\mathbb{E}Y_{k}^{2}\geq(\mathbb{E}Y_{k})^{2}\geq c\bar{k}^{-a(1+\rho)}.

If the slow Gaussian noise level is nonzero, the usual martingale variance calculation gives the additional lower bound
c\bar{k}^{-1}
. Thus the upper bound is sharp uniformly over the normal-form class.

Centering and debiasing.

The centering result explains why the obstruction is not the size of the nonlinear term alone. Suppose the nonlinear input is replaced by a martingale-centered fluctuation
\widetilde{R}_{k+1}
satisfying

\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{R}_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\widetilde{R}_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\lesssim\alpha_{k}^{1+\rho}.

Then its slow convolution satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\widetilde{R}_{j+1}\right\|^{2}\leq C\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}^{2}\left(\frac{j+k_{0}}{k+k_{0}}\right)^{2r}\alpha_{j}^{1+\rho}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

Thus a nonlinear term of the same instantaneous magnitude does not slow the slow iterate when it is martingale-centered. The rate loss comes from persistent predictable bias.

Online bias tracking.

The bias-tracking recursion removes the predictable component without an oracle. Since

M_{k+1}-M_{k}=\gamma_{k}\big(R(X_{k})-M_{k}\big),

the corrected nonlinear input satisfies

R(X_{k})-M_{k}=\gamma_{k}^{-1}(M_{k+1}-M_{k}).

Therefore its slow convolution is

\sum_{j<k}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\big(R(X_{j})-M_{j}\big)=\sum_{j<k}\frac{\beta_{j}}{\gamma_{j}}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}(M_{j+1}-M_{j}).

This is another Abel-transform term. The tracker itself satisfies

\left\lVert M_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=O(\bar{k}^{-s/2}),

because it is an exponentially weighted average of inputs
R(X_{j})
of
L^{2}
size
O(\bar{j}^{-s/2})
. The coefficient
\beta_{j}/\gamma_{j}\asymp\bar{j}^{c-1}
is small because the tracking time scale is faster than the slow time scale. The condition
c<\min\{1,(1+s)/2\}
ensures that the boundary and coefficient-variation terms in this Abel transform have
L^{2}
size
O(\bar{k}^{-1/2})
. Thus the tracked nonlinear residual contributes only
O(\bar{k}^{-1})
to the slow mean-square error.

Localized nonlinear transfer.

For nonlinear TTSA, the same argument is applied after changing coordinates to the fast-manifold error

X_{k}=x_{k}-\lambda(y_{k}),\qquad Y_{k}=y_{k}-y^{*}.

Here
\lambda(y)
is the equilibrium of the fast mean field with the slow variable frozen. Because this target moves with
y_{k}
, the
X
-recursion contains additional perturbations beyond the normal form. Assumption
4.1
collects these perturbations as
p_{k+1}
,
d_{k}
, and
e_{k}
.

The proof of the transfer theorem repeats the normal-form decomposition. The slow error is unrolled into the same three main terms, plus perturbation convolutions. The direct slow perturbation
e_{k}
is controlled by the same convolution estimate used for
R(X_{k})
. The perturbations in the fast recursion are handled through the Abel transform for the locally linear term
HX_{k}
. Terms entering the fast recursion at the
\beta_{k}
scale acquire an additional factor
\beta_{k}/\alpha_{k}
after the transform; since
\beta_{k}/\alpha_{k}\asymp\bar{k}^{a-1}
and
a<1
, these terms remain below the phase-transition rate under the stated moment bounds. The verification propositions show that the required perturbation bounds follow from local Taylor expansions of
h
and
g
, bounded derivatives of the manifold
\lambda
, and the corresponding localized moment estimates.

6
Conclusion and Discussion

This paper identifies a regularity-dependent boundary for the uncorrected normal-form recursion in nonlinear two-time-scale stochastic approximation. Locally linear fast-error leakage averages down to the intrinsic
O(k^{-1})
slow noise floor, while a nonlinear remainder of order
1+\rho
creates a predictable bias at the fast fluctuation scale. This yields the sharp rate

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\left(k^{-1}+k^{-a(1+\rho)}\right),

with a matching scalar lower bound. Thus, for the uncorrected normal-form recursion, the decoupled
O(k^{-1})
rate is guaranteed uniformly precisely when
a(1+\rho)\geq 1
.

The lower bound also identifies how to beat it. It is not an information-theoretic impossibility result for the normal-form problem class; it is the bias of a particular update rule. The online tracker

M_{k+1}=M_{k}+\gamma_{k}\big(R(X_{k})-M_{k}\big)

estimates the predictable nonlinear component on an intermediate time scale, and subtracting
M_{k}
from the slow update restores

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\widetilde{Y}_{k}\right\rVert^{2}=O(k^{-1})

for every
\rho\in[0,1]
. The proof uses a second Abel transform, now applied to
R(X_{k})-M_{k}=\gamma_{k}^{-1}(M_{k+1}-M_{k})
.

The localized transfer results show that the same phase-transition mechanism persists for nonlinear TTSA in fast-manifold coordinates under the stated localized moment estimates. Extending the online bias tracker beyond the normal form is a natural next step, but it requires a way to observe or estimate the hidden coupling error
g(x_{k},y_{k})-g(x^{*}(y_{k}),y_{k})
. Other directions include simulator-based batching or multilevel debiasing for general TTSA, high-probability analogues, and non-asymptotic central limit theorems with the nonlinear bias explicitly centered.

Appendix A
Deterministic estimates

Throughout the appendix,
C
denotes a finite positive constant whose value may change from line to line but depends only on the fixed problem parameters. It never depends on
k
or on summation indices. Recall that
\bar{k}=k+k_{0}
.

For
0\leq i\leq k
, define the slow transition matrix

\Pi^{B}_{i,k}\coloneqq\begin{cases}(I-\beta_{k-1}B)(I-\beta_{k-2}B)\cdots(I-\beta_{i}B),&i<k,\\
I,&i=k.\end{cases}

(49)

The order of multiplication is irrelevant for the estimates because every factor is a polynomial in the same matrix
B
, but the displayed convention matches the recursion.

Lemma A.1

(Polynomial decay of the slow transition)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, for all
0\leq i\leq k
,

\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{i,k}\right\rVert\leq\left(\frac{\bar{i}}{\bar{k}}\right)^{r},\qquad r=b\beta_{0}.

(50)

Proof.

If
i=k
, then (
50
) is equality. Assume
i<k
. By (
10
),

\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{i,k}\right\rVert\leq\prod_{\ell=i}^{k-1}(1-b\beta_{\ell})\leq\exp\left(-b\sum_{\ell=i}^{k-1}\beta_{\ell}\right),

where we used
1-u\leq e^{-u}
for
u\geq 0
. Since
\beta_{\ell}=\beta_{0}/(\ell+k_{0})
,

\sum_{\ell=i}^{k-1}\beta_{\ell}=\beta_{0}\sum_{\ell=i}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\ell+k_{0}}\geq\beta_{0}\int_{i}^{k}\frac{dt}{t+k_{0}}=\beta_{0}\log\left(\frac{\bar{k}}{\bar{i}}\right).

Combining the two inequalities gives

\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{i,k}\right\rVert\leq\exp\left(-b\beta_{0}\log\left(\frac{\bar{k}}{\bar{i}}\right)\right)=\left(\frac{\bar{i}}{\bar{k}}\right)^{b\beta_{0}}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Weighted convolution estimates)
.

Let
u\geq 0
and
r>0
. Then, for all
k\geq 1
,

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\bar{j}^{-u}\leq C\begin{cases}\bar{k}^{-u},&u<r,\\
\bar{k}^{-r}\log(\bar{k}),&u=r,\\
\bar{k}^{-r},&u>r.\end{cases}

(51)

Consequently, if
r>1/2
, then

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\bar{j}^{-u}\right)^{2}\leq C\left(\bar{k}^{-1}+\bar{k}^{-2u}\right).

(52)

If
r>1/2
, then, for all
k\geq 1
,

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}^{2}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

(53)

Proof.

For (
51
), Lemma
A.1
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\bar{j}^{-u}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{0}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{-1}\left(\frac{\overline{j+1}}{\bar{k}}\right)^{r}\bar{j}^{-u}.

Because
k_{0}\geq 2
,
\overline{j+1}=\bar{j}+1\leq 2\bar{j}
. Therefore

\left(\frac{\overline{j+1}}{\bar{k}}\right)^{r}\leq 2^{r}\bar{k}^{-r}\bar{j}^{r},

and hence

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\bar{j}^{-u}\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{r-u-1}.

(54)

If
r-u>0
, the sum in (
54
) is at most
C\bar{k}^{r-u}
. If
r-u=0
, it is at most
C\log(\bar{k})
. If
r-u<0
, it is bounded by a constant. These three cases prove (
51
).

We next prove (
52
). If
u<r
, then (
51
) gives
C\bar{k}^{-2u}
. If
u>r
, then it gives
C\bar{k}^{-2r}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}
because

. If
u=r
, then
2u=2r>1
, and

\bar{k}^{-2r}(\log\bar{k})^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

Thus (
52
) holds in all cases.

For (
53
), again by Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}^{2}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-2r}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{2r-2}.

Since
2r-2>-1
is equivalent to
r>1/2
, the sum is at most
C\bar{k}^{2r-1}
. Therefore the last display is bounded by
C\bar{k}^{-1}
.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Abel coefficient bounds)
.

Let
C_{H}=HA^{-1}
and define, for
0\leq j\leq k-1
,

Q_{j,k}\coloneqq\frac{\beta_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}C_{H}.

(55)

Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert Q_{j,k}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\bar{j}^{r+a-1},

(56)

\displaystyle\left\lVert Q_{j,k}-Q_{j-1,k}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\bar{j}^{r+a-2},\qquad 1\leq j\leq k-1.

(57)

Proof.

First,

\frac{\beta_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}=\frac{\beta_{0}}{\alpha_{0}}\bar{j}^{a-1}.

Using Lemma
A.1
,

\left\lVert Q_{j,k}\right\rVert\leq\frac{\beta_{0}}{\alpha_{0}}\left\lVert C_{H}\right\rVert\bar{j}^{a-1}\left(\frac{\overline{j+1}}{\bar{k}}\right)^{r}\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\bar{j}^{r+a-1},

which proves (
56
).

For the variation bound, write

q_{j}\coloneqq\frac{\beta_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}=\frac{\beta_{0}}{\alpha_{0}}\bar{j}^{a-1}.

Then

\displaystyle Q_{j,k}-Q_{j-1,k}

\displaystyle=\left(q_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}-q_{j-1}\Pi^{B}_{j,k}\right)C_{H}

\displaystyle=\left((q_{j}-q_{j-1})\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right)C_{H}+q_{j-1}\left(\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}-\Pi^{B}_{j,k}\right)C_{H}.

Because
a-1<0
, the map
t\mapsto(t+k_{0})^{a-1}
is differentiable with derivative of magnitude
(1-a)(t+k_{0})^{a-2}
. The mean-value theorem gives

|q_{j}-q_{j-1}|\leq C\bar{j}^{a-2}.

(58)

Moreover, from the definition of the transition matrix,

\Pi^{B}_{j,k}=\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}(I-\beta_{j}B),

so

\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}-\Pi^{B}_{j,k}=\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}B.

(59)

Combining (
58
), (
59
), Lemma
A.1
, and
q_{j-1}\leq C\bar{j}^{a-1}
yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert Q_{j,k}-Q_{j-1,k}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{j}^{a-2}\left(\frac{\overline{j+1}}{\bar{k}}\right)^{r}+C\bar{j}^{a-1}\beta_{j}\left(\frac{\overline{j+1}}{\bar{k}}\right)^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{j}^{a-2}\bar{k}^{-r}\bar{j}^{r}+C\bar{j}^{a-1}\bar{j}^{-1}\bar{k}^{-r}\bar{j}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\bar{j}^{r+a-2}.

This proves (
57
).
∎

Appendix B
Proof of the upper bound

We now prove Theorem
3.1
. The proof is divided into three lemmas and then assembled.

Lemma B.1

(Fast moments)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, there exists
C<\infty
such that, for every
k\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\alpha_{k},\qquad\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}\leq C\alpha_{k}^{2}.

(60)

Consequently, for every
\rho\in[0,1]
,

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2+2\rho}\leq C\alpha_{k}^{1+\rho}.

(61)

Proof.

Let
D_{k}=I-\alpha_{k}A
. By Assumption
2.1
,
\left\lVert D_{k}\right\rVert\leq 1-\lambda\alpha_{k}
.

Second moment.

Conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{k}
and using
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert X_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert D_{k}X_{k}+\alpha_{k}\xi_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\left\lVert D_{k}X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+2\alpha_{k}\left\langle D_{k}X_{k},\mathbb{E}[\xi_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right\rangle+\alpha_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert\xi_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda\alpha_{k})^{2}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\sigma_{4}^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}.

Since
\lambda\alpha_{k}\leq 1
after increasing
k_{0}
if necessary,
(1-\lambda\alpha_{k})^{2}\leq 1-\lambda\alpha_{k}
. Thus

m_{k+1}\leq(1-\lambda\alpha_{k})m_{k}+\sigma_{4}^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2},\qquad m_{k}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

(62)

We prove
m_{k}\leq C_{2}\alpha_{k}
by induction. Since
X_{0}
is deterministic and
\alpha_{0}
in the display below denotes the fixed numerator in the stepsize formula, choose
C_{2}
so large that
m_{0}\leq C_{2}\alpha_{0}k_{0}^{-a}
. Because
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{0}\bar{k}^{-a}
,

\frac{\alpha_{k+1}}{\alpha_{k}}=\left(\frac{\bar{k}}{\bar{k}+1}\right)^{a}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\bar{k}}\right)^{-a}.

The inequality
(1+t)^{-a}\geq 1-at
for
t\geq 0
gives

\alpha_{k+1}\geq\alpha_{k}\left(1-\frac{a}{\bar{k}}\right).

(63)

Since
a<1
, we have
\bar{k}^{-1}=o(\alpha_{k})
because
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{0}\bar{k}^{-a}
. Hence, after increasing
k_{0}
if necessary,

\frac{a}{\bar{k}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\alpha_{k},\qquad k\geq 0.

(64)

Combining (
63
) and (
64
),

\alpha_{k+1}\geq\alpha_{k}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2}\alpha_{k}\right).

(65)

Assume
m_{k}\leq C_{2}\alpha_{k}
. From (
62
),

m_{k+1}\leq C_{2}\alpha_{k}(1-\lambda\alpha_{k})+\sigma_{4}^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}=C_{2}\alpha_{k}\left(1-\lambda\alpha_{k}+\frac{\sigma_{4}^{2}}{C_{2}}\alpha_{k}\right).

Choose
C_{2}\geq 2\sigma_{4}^{2}/\lambda
. Then

m_{k+1}\leq C_{2}\alpha_{k}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2}\alpha_{k}\right)\leq C_{2}\alpha_{k+1},

where the last inequality uses (
65
). This completes the induction and proves
m_{k}\leq C_{2}\alpha_{k}
.

Fourth moment.

Let
u=D_{k}X_{k}
and
v=\alpha_{k}\xi_{k+1}
. The identity

\left\lVert u+v\right\rVert^{4}=(\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}+2\left\langle u,v\right\rangle+\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2})^{2}

expands as

\displaystyle\left\lVert u+v\right\rVert^{4}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{4}+4\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\left\langle u,v\right\rangle+2\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}+4\left\langle u,v\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+4\left\langle u,v\right\rangle\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{4}.

Taking conditional expectation, the term
4\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\left\langle u,v\right\rangle
vanishes because
\mathbb{E}[v\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
. For the remaining terms, using
\left\lVert u\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert
and (
12
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[2\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma_{4}^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[4\left\langle u,v\right\rangle^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq 4\sigma_{4}^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{4}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{4}^{4}\alpha_{k}^{4}.

For the mixed cubic term, first use Cauchy’s inequality and Jensen’s inequality:

\left|\mathbb{E}[4\left\langle u,v\right\rangle\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right|\leq 4\left\lVert u\right\rVert\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{3}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq 4\sigma_{4}^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert.

We now apply Young’s inequality in the form

c_{1}\alpha_{k}^{3}t\leq\frac{\lambda}{4}\alpha_{k}t^{4}+C\alpha_{k}^{11/3},\qquad t\geq 0,

which follows from
ab\leq\varepsilon a^{4}+C\varepsilon^{-1/3}b^{4/3}
by taking
a=t
,
b=c_{1}\alpha_{k}^{3}
, and
\varepsilon=(\lambda/4)\alpha_{k}
. Since
\alpha_{k}\leq 1
,
\alpha_{k}^{11/3}\leq\alpha_{k}^{3}
. Therefore

\left|\mathbb{E}[4\left\langle u,v\right\rangle\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right|\leq\frac{\lambda}{4}\alpha_{k}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}+C\alpha_{k}^{3}.

(66)

Finally,

\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{4}\leq(1-\lambda\alpha_{k})^{4}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}.

For
\lambda\alpha_{k}\leq 1/4
, the elementary inequality

(1-x)^{4}\leq 1-\frac{15}{8}x,\qquad 0\leq x\leq\frac{1}{4},

with
x=\lambda\alpha_{k}
gives

\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{4}\leq\left(1-\frac{15\lambda}{8}\alpha_{k}\right)\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}.

Combining this estimate with (
66
) and the remaining second- and fourth-order noise terms gives

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert X_{k+1}\right\rVert^{4}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq\left(1-\frac{13\lambda}{8}\alpha_{k}\right)\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}+C\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+C\alpha_{k}^{3}.

Absorbing constants and taking expectation,

n_{k+1}\leq(1-c_{4}\alpha_{k})n_{k}+C\alpha_{k}^{2}m_{k}+C\alpha_{k}^{3},\qquad n_{k}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4},

(67)

for
c_{4}=13\lambda/8
replaced by any smaller positive constant. The already-proved second moment bound gives
m_{k}\leq C\alpha_{k}
, hence

n_{k+1}\leq(1-c_{4}\alpha_{k})n_{k}+C\alpha_{k}^{3}.

(68)

We prove
n_{k}\leq C_{4}\alpha_{k}^{2}
by induction. Choose
C_{4}
so large that
n_{0}\leq C_{4}(\alpha_{0}k_{0}^{-a})^{2}
. Since

\frac{\alpha_{k+1}^{2}}{\alpha_{k}^{2}}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\bar{k}}\right)^{-2a}\geq 1-\frac{2a}{\bar{k}},

and since
\bar{k}^{-1}=o(\alpha_{k})
, after increasing
k_{0}
if necessary,

\alpha_{k+1}^{2}\geq\alpha_{k}^{2}\left(1-\frac{c_{4}}{2}\alpha_{k}\right).

(69)

Assume
n_{k}\leq C_{4}\alpha_{k}^{2}
. From (
68
),

n_{k+1}\leq C_{4}\alpha_{k}^{2}(1-c_{4}\alpha_{k})+C\alpha_{k}^{3}=C_{4}\alpha_{k}^{2}\left(1-c_{4}\alpha_{k}+\frac{C}{C_{4}}\alpha_{k}\right).

Choosing
C_{4}\geq 2C/c_{4}
gives

n_{k+1}\leq C_{4}\alpha_{k}^{2}\left(1-\frac{c_{4}}{2}\alpha_{k}\right)\leq C_{4}\alpha_{k+1}^{2}.

This completes the induction and proves the fourth moment bound.

Interpolation.

Let
p=2+2\rho\in[2,4]
. By Lyapunov’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}\right)^{p/4}\leq C\left(\alpha_{k}^{2}\right)^{p/4}=C\alpha_{k}^{p/2}=C\alpha_{k}^{1+\rho}.

This proves (
61
) and the lemma.
∎

Lemma B.2

(Averaging of the locally linear fast leakage)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}HX_{j}\right\|^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

(70)

Proof.

Let
C_{H}=HA^{-1}
. From the fast recursion,

X_{j+1}=X_{j}-\alpha_{j}AX_{j}+\alpha_{j}\xi_{j+1}.

Rearranging,

AX_{j}=\alpha_{j}^{-1}(X_{j}-X_{j+1})+\xi_{j+1}.

(71)

Multiplying by
HA^{-1}=C_{H}
gives

HX_{j}=C_{H}\alpha_{j}^{-1}(X_{j}-X_{j+1})+C_{H}\xi_{j+1}.

(72)

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}HX_{j}

\displaystyle=M_{k}+T_{k},

(73)

\displaystyle M_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}C_{H}\xi_{j+1},

(74)

\displaystyle T_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Q_{j,k}(X_{j}-X_{j+1}),

(75)

where
Q_{j,k}
is defined in (
55
).

We first bound
M_{k}
. For
i<j
, the random vector
\xi_{i+1}
is
\mathcal{F}_{j}
-measurable and
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{j+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{j}]=0
. Hence cross terms vanish. Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert M_{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}C_{H}\xi_{j+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert C_{H}\right\rVert^{2}\sigma_{4}^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}^{2}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1},

where the last step uses Lemma
A.2
and
r>1/2
.

We now bound
T_{k}
. Abel summation gives

T_{k}=Q_{0,k}X_{0}-Q_{k-1,k}X_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(Q_{j,k}-Q_{j-1,k})X_{j}.

(76)

Indeed, expanding the right-hand side produces

Q_{0,k}X_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}Q_{j,k}X_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}Q_{j-1,k}X_{j}-Q_{k-1,k}X_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Q_{j,k}(X_{j}-X_{j+1}).

We treat the three terms in (
76
) separately.

By Lemma
A.3
,

\left\lVert Q_{0,k}X_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-2r}\left\lVert X_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1},

because
r>1/2
. For the terminal boundary term, Lemma
A.3
and Lemma
B.1
imply

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Q_{k-1,k}X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{2a-2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{2a-2}\alpha_{k}\leq C\bar{k}^{a-2}.

Since
a<1
,
\bar{k}^{a-2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}
.

It remains to bound the interior sum. By the triangle inequality in
L^{2}
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(Q_{j,k}-Q_{j-1,k})X_{j}\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left\lVert Q_{j,k}-Q_{j-1,k}\right\rVert\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{j}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{r+a-2}\alpha_{j}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{r+a-2-a/2}

\displaystyle=C\bar{k}^{-r}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{r+a/2-2}.

If
r+a/2-2<-1
, then the sum is bounded by a constant, and the squared expression is at most
C\bar{k}^{-2r}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}
. If
r+a/2-2=-1
, then the sum is at most
C\log(\bar{k})
, and the squared expression is at most
C\bar{k}^{-2r}(\log\bar{k})^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}
because
r>1/2
. If
r+a/2-2>-1
, then the sum is at most
C\bar{k}^{r+a/2-1}
, and the squared expression is at most

C\bar{k}^{-2r}\bar{k}^{2r+a-2}=C\bar{k}^{a-2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

Thus the interior term also has second moment at most
C\bar{k}^{-1}
.

Using
(u+v)^{2}\leq 2u^{2}+2v^{2}
in (
73
), and using the bounds for
M_{k}
and
T_{k}
, proves (
70
).
∎

Lemma B.3

(Nonlinear and slow-noise convolutions)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
, and
2.3
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}R(X_{j})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\bar{k}^{-1}+\bar{k}^{-s}\right),

(77)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\zeta_{j+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

(78)

Proof.

For the nonlinear term, the triangle inequality in
L^{2}
, Assumption
2.2
, and Lemma
B.1
give

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}R(X_{j})\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert R(X_{j})\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{R}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{j}\right\rVert^{2+2\rho}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\alpha_{j}^{(1+\rho)/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\bar{j}^{-s/2}.

By (
52
) with
u=s/2
,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}R(X_{j})\right\|^{2}\leq C\left(\bar{k}^{-1}+\bar{k}^{-s}\right).

This proves (
77
).

For the slow-noise convolution, martingale cross terms vanish exactly as in the proof of Lemma
B.2
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\zeta_{j+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\zeta_{j+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{4}^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}^{2}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1},

where the last inequality is (
53
). This proves (
78
).
∎

.

Unroll the slow recursion (

Appendix C
Proof of the centering principle

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

Unrolling (
23
) gives

\displaystyle Y_{k}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{B}_{0,k}Y_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}HX_{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}m_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\nu_{j+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\zeta_{j+1}.

The initial term satisfies
\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{0,k}Y_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-2r}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}
because
r>1/2
. Lemma
B.2
gives

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}HX_{j}\right\|^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

The slow martingale noise is bounded as in Lemma
B.3
:

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\zeta_{j+1}\right\|^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

We next treat the predictable component
m_{j}
. By the
L^{2}
triangle inequality, (
25
), and Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}m_{j}\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{r-1}\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert m_{j}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{r-q-1}.

If
q<r
, the last sum is at most
C\bar{k}^{r-q}
; if
q=r
, it is at most
C\log(\bar{k})
; if
q>r
, it is bounded by a constant. Therefore

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}m_{j}\right\|^{2}\leq C\Psi_{q}(k)^{2}.

(79)

It remains to control the martingale-centered nonlinear fluctuation. Since
\nu_{j+1}
is a martingale difference and the matrices
\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}
are
\mathcal{F}_{j}
-measurable deterministic coefficients, cross terms vanish. Hence, using (
24
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\nu_{j+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}^{2}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert^{2}\alpha_{j}^{1+\rho}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-2r}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{2r-2-s},

where
s=a(1+\rho)
. If
2r-2-s<-1
, the sum is bounded, and the display is at most
C\bar{k}^{-2r}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}
. If
2r-2-s=-1
, the display is at most
C\bar{k}^{-2r}\log(\bar{k})
, which is
O(\bar{k}^{-1})
because
2r=s+1>1
. If
2r-2-s>-1
, the sum is at most
C\bar{k}^{2r-1-s}
, and the display is at most
C\bar{k}^{-1-s}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}
. Thus

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\nu_{j+1}\right\|^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

(80)

Combining the five displayed bounds and using
\|z_{1}+\cdots+z_{5}\|^{2}\leq 5\sum_{i}\|z_{i}\|^{2}
proves (
27
). If
m_{k}\equiv 0
, the term (
79
) is absent, and the rate is
C\bar{k}^{-1}
.
∎

Appendix D
Proof of online bias tracking

This appendix proves Theorem
3.7
. The argument has two steps. First, the tracker
M_{k}
inherits the
L^{2}
decay of the nonlinear input
R(X_{k})
. Second, the corrected residual
R(X_{k})-M_{k}
is written as a scaled difference of consecutive tracker values, so its slow convolution is controlled by Abel summation.

For
0\leq i\leq k
, define the scalar tracking transition

\Pi^{\gamma}_{i,k}=\begin{cases}\prod_{\ell=i}^{k-1}(1-\gamma_{\ell}),&i<k,\\
1,&i=k.\end{cases}

Lemma D.1

(Moment bound for the bias tracker)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.7
,

\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert M_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-s/2},\qquad k\geq 1.

Consequently,
\mathbb{E}\left\lVert M_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-s}
.

Proof.

Unrolling (
31
) gives

M_{k}=\Pi^{\gamma}_{0,k}M_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma_{i}\Pi^{\gamma}_{i+1,k}R(X_{i}).

By Assumption
2.2
and Lemma
B.1
,

\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert R(X_{i})\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}\leq L_{R}\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{i}\right\rVert^{2+2\rho}\big)^{1/2}\leq C\alpha_{i}^{(1+\rho)/2}\leq C\bar{i}^{-s/2}.

Thus, by the
L^{2}
triangle inequality,

\left\lVert M_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\Pi^{\gamma}_{0,k}\left\lVert M_{0}\right\rVert+C\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma_{i}\Pi^{\gamma}_{i+1,k}\bar{i}^{-s/2}.

(81)

We bound the two terms on the right.

Because
c<1
and
\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{0}\bar{i}^{-c}
,

\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma_{i}\geq c_{0}\bar{k}^{1-c}

for all
k
and a constant
c_{0}>0
after changing constants for small
k
. Hence

\Pi^{\gamma}_{0,k}\leq\exp\!\left(-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma_{i}\right)\leq\exp(-c_{0}\bar{k}^{1-c})\leq C\bar{k}^{-s/2}.

This controls the initial term.

For the convolution term, split the sum in (
81
) at
\lfloor k/2\rfloor
. If
i\leq k/2
, then

\sum_{\ell=i+1}^{k-1}\gamma_{\ell}\geq c_{1}\bar{k}^{1-c}

for some
c_{1}>0
, and therefore

\gamma_{i}\Pi^{\gamma}_{i+1,k}\bar{i}^{-s/2}\leq C\bar{i}^{-c-s/2}e^{-c_{1}\bar{k}^{1-c}}.

Summing over
i\leq k/2
gives at most
C\bar{k}^{-s/2}
, because the stretched-exponential factor dominates every polynomial. If
i>k/2
, then
\bar{i}^{-s/2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-s/2}
, and

\sum_{i=\lfloor k/2\rfloor+1}^{k-1}\gamma_{i}\Pi^{\gamma}_{i+1,k}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma_{i}\Pi^{\gamma}_{i+1,k}=1-\Pi^{\gamma}_{0,k}\leq 1.

Therefore the recent half of the sum is also at most
C\bar{k}^{-s/2}
. Combining the initial, early, and recent bounds proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma D.2

(Abel bound for the tracked residual)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.7
,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\big(R(X_{j})-M_{j}\big)\right\|^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

(82)

Proof.

From (
31
),

R(X_{j})-M_{j}=\gamma_{j}^{-1}(M_{j+1}-M_{j}).

Define

q_{j}=\frac{\beta_{j}}{\gamma_{j}}=\frac{\beta_{0}}{\gamma_{0}}\bar{j}^{c-1},\qquad Q_{j,k}=q_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k},\qquad 0\leq j\leq k-1.

Then

S_{k}\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\big(R(X_{j})-M_{j}\big)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Q_{j,k}(M_{j+1}-M_{j}).

Abel summation gives

S_{k}=-Q_{0,k}M_{0}+Q_{k-1,k}M_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(Q_{j-1,k}-Q_{j,k})M_{j}.

(83)

We first record coefficient bounds. Lemma
A.1
gives

\left\lVert Q_{j,k}\right\rVert\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\bar{j}^{r+c-1}.

(84)

Moreover, the same calculation as in Lemma
A.3
, with
a
replaced by
c
, yields

\left\lVert Q_{j,k}-Q_{j-1,k}\right\rVert\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\bar{j}^{r+c-2},\qquad 1\leq j\leq k-1.

(85)

Indeed,
|q_{j}-q_{j-1}|\leq C\bar{j}^{c-2}
and
q_{j-1}\beta_{j}\leq C\bar{j}^{c-2}
.

We now bound the three terms in (
83
). The initial boundary satisfies

\left\lVert Q_{0,k}M_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-2r}\left\lVert M_{0}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1},

because
r>1/2
. For the terminal boundary, (
84
) and Lemma
D.1
give

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Q_{k-1,k}M_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{2c-2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert M_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{2c-2-s}.

The condition
c<(1+s)/2
implies
2c-2-s<-1
, and hence this term is at most
C\bar{k}^{-1}
.

For the interior term, the
L^{2}
triangle inequality, (
85
), and Lemma
D.1
imply

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(Q_{j-1,k}-Q_{j,k})M_{j}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{r+c-2}\bar{j}^{-s/2}

\displaystyle=C\bar{k}^{-r}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{r+c-2-s/2}.

If
r+c-2-s/2<-1
, the sum is bounded, so the squared expression is at most
C\bar{k}^{-2r}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}
. If
r+c-2-s/2=-1
, the sum is logarithmic, and
C\bar{k}^{-2r}(\log\bar{k})^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}
because
r>1/2
. If
r+c-2-s/2>-1
, the sum is at most
C\bar{k}^{r+c-1-s/2}
, and the square is at most

C\bar{k}^{-2r}\bar{k}^{2r+2c-2-s}=C\bar{k}^{2c-2-s}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1},

again by
c<(1+s)/2
. Combining the three terms in (
83
) proves (
82
).
∎

Proof of Theorem

The estimate for

Appendix E
Proof of the localized nonlinear transfer theorem

This appendix proves Theorem
4.2
and Propositions
4.3
and
4.4
. We first record a weighted-sum estimate that allows borderline logarithms.

Lemma E.1

(General weighted convolution)
.

Let
u\geq 0
and
r>0
. Then, for all
k\geq 1
,

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\bar{j}^{-u}\leq C\begin{cases}\bar{k}^{-u},&u<r,\\
\bar{k}^{-r}\log(\bar{k}),&u=r,\\
\bar{k}^{-r},&u>r.\end{cases}

(86)

In particular, if
\min\{u,r\}>v
, then the square of the left-hand side is at most
C\bar{k}^{-2v}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.1
and the inequality
\overline{j+1}\leq 2\bar{j}
,

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\left\lVert\Pi^{B}_{j+1,k}\right\rVert\bar{j}^{-u}\leq C\bar{k}^{-r}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bar{j}^{r-u-1}.

If
r-u>0
, the sum is at most
C\bar{k}^{r-u}
. If
r-u=0
, the sum is at most
C\log(\bar{k})
. If
r-u<0
, the sum is bounded by a constant because
k_{0}\geq 2
and the exponent

is strictly less than
-1
. These three cases give (
86
). If
\min\{u,r\}>v
, the first and third cases give
C\bar{k}^{-2v}
after squaring, while the borderline case gives
C\bar{k}^{-2r}(\log\bar{k})^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-2v}
because
r>v
.
∎

100
) gives

\beta_{k}^{-1}\left\lVert r^{\lambda}_{k+1}\right\rVert\leq C\beta_{k}\left\lVert g(x_{k},y_{k})+\zeta_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}.

By the localization hypothesis, the process stays in the neighborhood where (
44
) holds. Hence
g
is locally bounded there, and
\zeta_{k+1}
has a uniformly bounded fourth conditional moment. Therefore

\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\beta_{k}^{-1}r^{\lambda}_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}\leq C\beta_{k}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}\leq C\bar{k}^{-a/2},

because
a<1
. Combining the three estimates proves (
40
). Therefore Assumption
4.1
holds, and Theorem
4.2
gives the claimed rate.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

The proof follows the same coordinate calculation as Proposition
4.3
, but we spell out the estimates because the mixed terms are the point of the result. Define

X_{k}=x_{k}-\lambda(y_{k}),\qquad Y_{k}=y_{k}-y^{*},\qquad\xi_{k+1}=M^{x}_{k+1},\qquad\zeta_{k+1}=M^{y}_{k+1}.

By Jensen’s inequality and (
48
),

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\alpha_{k},\qquad\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-a}.

(102)

By Lyapunov’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2+2\rho}\leq\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}\big)^{(1+\rho)/2}\leq C\alpha_{k}^{1+\rho}.

(103)

Thus the fast moment requirements (
38
) hold.

The slow equation follows directly from (
47
):

Y_{k+1}=(I-\beta_{k}B)Y_{k}+\beta_{k}\big(HX_{k}+R(X_{k})+\zeta_{k+1}+e_{k}\big),\qquad e_{k}=E(X_{k},y_{k}).

We bound
e_{k}
in
L^{2}
. By (
47
), Cauchy’s inequality, (
48
), and (
103
),

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2+2\rho}\big)^{1/2}+C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}+C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{4}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-s/2}+C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}\big)^{1/4}\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{4}\big)^{1/4}+C\bar{k}^{-a}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-s/2}+C\bar{k}^{-a}+C\bar{k}^{-a}.

Let
\delta=\min\{1,s\}
. Since
a>1/2\geq\delta/2
and
s/2\geq\delta/2
, the last line is at most
C\bar{k}^{-\delta/2}
. Hence (
41
) holds.

We next derive the fast equation. Taylor’s theorem for
\lambda
gives

\lambda(y_{k+1})=\lambda(y_{k})+D\lambda(y_{k})(y_{k+1}-y_{k})+r^{\lambda}_{k+1},\qquad\left\lVert r^{\lambda}_{k+1}\right\rVert\leq C\left\lVert y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

(104)

Using (
46
) and
y_{k+1}-y_{k}=\beta_{k}(g(x_{k},y_{k})+\zeta_{k+1})
, we get

\displaystyle X_{k+1}

\displaystyle=x_{k+1}-\lambda(y_{k+1})

\displaystyle=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}\big(h(x_{k},y_{k})+\xi_{k+1}\big)-\lambda(y_{k})-D\lambda(y_{k})\beta_{k}\big(g(x_{k},y_{k})+\zeta_{k+1}\big)-r^{\lambda}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-\alpha_{k}A)X_{k}+\alpha_{k}\xi_{k+1}+\alpha_{k}P(X_{k},y_{k})-\beta_{k}D\lambda(y^{*})\zeta_{k+1}+\beta_{k}d_{k},

where

d_{k}=-D\lambda(y_{k})g(x_{k},y_{k})-\big(D\lambda(y_{k})-D\lambda(y^{*})\big)\zeta_{k+1}-\beta_{k}^{-1}r^{\lambda}_{k+1}.

(105)

Thus (
36
) holds with

p_{k+1}=P(X_{k},y_{k}),\qquad C_{\zeta}=-D\lambda(y^{*}).

For
p_{k+1}
, (
46
), Cauchy’s inequality, and (
48
) imply

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert p_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}\big)^{1/2}+C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha_{k}+C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{4}\big)^{1/4}\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{4}\big)^{1/4}\leq C\bar{k}^{-a}.

Because
a>1/2\geq\delta/2
, (
39
) holds with
\mu_{p}=a
.

It remains to prove (
40
). First, by (
47
), Assumption
2.2
, (
102
), (
103
), and the bound just proved for
e_{k}
,

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert g(x_{k},y_{k})\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}+C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}+C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X_{k}\right\rVert^{2+2\rho}\big)^{1/2}+C\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert e_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{k}^{-a/2}+C\bar{k}^{-\delta/2}.

The derivative
D\lambda
is locally bounded, so the first term in (
105
) has this same
L^{2}
bound.

For the second term in (
105
), boundedness of the Hessian of
\lambda
gives

\left\lVert D\lambda(y_{k})-D\lambda(y^{*})\right\rVert\leq C\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert.

Using the conditional second-moment bound on
\zeta_{k+1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(D\lambda(y_{k})-D\lambda(y^{*}))\zeta_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\big[\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\mathbb{E}(\left\lVert\zeta_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k})\big]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-a}.

Therefore this term has
L^{2}
norm at most
C\bar{k}^{-a/2}
.

For the Taylor remainder term, (
104
) gives

\beta_{k}^{-1}\left\lVert r^{\lambda}_{k+1}\right\rVert\leq C\beta_{k}\left\lVert g(x_{k},y_{k})+\zeta_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}.

The localized process remains in the neighborhood on which
g
is bounded, and
\zeta_{k+1}
has a uniformly bounded fourth conditional moment. Hence

\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\beta_{k}^{-1}r^{\lambda}_{k+1}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}\leq C\beta_{k}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}\leq C\bar{k}^{-a/2},

because
a<1
. Combining the three bounds for the terms in (
105
) yields

\big(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert d_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\big)^{1/2}\leq C\big(\bar{k}^{-a/2}+\bar{k}^{-\delta/2}\big),

which is (
40
). All parts of Assumption
4.1
have now been verified, and Theorem
4.2
gives the claimed rate.
∎

Appendix F
Proof of the lower bound

We prove Theorem
3.4
. The proof uses elementary properties of the scalar Gaussian fast recursion.

Lemma F.1

(Fast variance lower bound)
.

Consider the scalar recursion

X_{k+1}=(1-\lambda\alpha_{k})X_{k}+\alpha_{k}\xi_{k+1},\qquad X_{0}=0,

where
\xi_{k}
are i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables and
0<\lambda\alpha_{k}\leq 1/2
. Then there are constants
c_{v},C_{v}>0
and
k_{v}\geq 1
such that, for all
k\geq k_{v}
,

c_{v}\alpha_{k}\leq\mathbb{E}[X_{k}^{2}]\leq C_{v}\alpha_{k}.

(106)

Consequently, for every
p>0
there exists
c_{p}>0
such that, for all
k\geq k_{v}
,

\mathbb{E}|X_{k}|^{p}\geq c_{p}\alpha_{k}^{p/2}.

(107)

Proof.

Because the recursion is linear and the driving noises are Gaussian,
X_{k}
is a centered Gaussian random variable. Let
v_{k}=\mathbb{E}[X_{k}^{2}]
. Then

v_{k+1}=(1-\lambda\alpha_{k})^{2}v_{k}+\alpha_{k}^{2}.

(108)

The upper bound
v_{k}\leq C_{v}\alpha_{k}
follows from the same second-moment induction used in Lemma
B.1
; we repeat the one-line argument. Since
(1-\lambda\alpha_{k})^{2}\leq 1-\lambda\alpha_{k}
and
\alpha_{k+1}\geq\alpha_{k}(1-(\lambda/2)\alpha_{k})
for all large
k
, choosing
C_{v}\geq 2/\lambda
yields

v_{k}\leq C_{v}\alpha_{k}\quad\Rightarrow\quad v_{k+1}\leq C_{v}\alpha_{k}(1-\lambda\alpha_{k})+\alpha_{k}^{2}\leq C_{v}\alpha_{k+1}.

Increasing
C_{v}
handles finitely many initial indices.

For the lower bound, unroll (
108
):

v_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}^{2}\prod_{\ell=i+1}^{k-1}(1-\lambda\alpha_{\ell})^{2}.

(109)

Fix a small constant
\delta\in(0,1)
to be chosen below and define

m_{k}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{\alpha_{k}}\right\rfloor.

Because
a<1
,
m_{k}=o(k)
, so for all large
k
we have
m_{k}\leq k/2
. For
i\in\{k-m_{k},\ldots,k-1\}
and all large
k
, monotonicity of
\alpha_{j}
and
m_{k}=o(k)
imply

\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k}\leq\alpha_{i}\leq 2\alpha_{k}.

(110)

Also, since
\lambda\alpha_{\ell}\leq 1/2
, the inequality
\log(1-u)\geq-2u
for
u\in[0,1/2]
gives

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=i+1}^{k-1}(1-\lambda\alpha_{\ell})^{2}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(2\sum_{\ell=i+1}^{k-1}\log(1-\lambda\alpha_{\ell})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-4\lambda\sum_{\ell=i+1}^{k-1}\alpha_{\ell}\right).

For
i\geq k-m_{k}
, (
110
) gives

\sum_{\ell=i+1}^{k-1}\alpha_{\ell}\leq 2m_{k}\alpha_{k}\leq 2\delta.

Therefore every product in the last
m_{k}
terms is at least
e^{-8\lambda\delta}
. Restricting the sum (
109
) to these terms,

v_{k}\geq\sum_{i=k-m_{k}}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}^{2}e^{-8\lambda\delta}\geq m_{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k}\right)^{2}e^{-8\lambda\delta}.

For all large
k
,
m_{k}\geq\delta/(2\alpha_{k})
, so

v_{k}\geq\frac{\delta e^{-8\lambda\delta}}{8}\alpha_{k}.

This proves the lower variance bound.

Finally, if
X_{k}\sim N(0,v_{k})
, then for any
p>0
,

\mathbb{E}|X_{k}|^{p}=v_{k}^{p/2}\mathbb{E}|Z|^{p},\qquad Z\sim N(0,1).

Using
v_{k}\geq c_{v}\alpha_{k}
proves (
107
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Let

\phi_{i,k}\coloneqq\prod_{\ell=i}^{k-1}(1-b\beta_{\ell}),\qquad 0\leq i\leq k,

with the empty product
\phi_{k,k}=1
. Unrolling (
21
) gives

Y_{k}=\gamma\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\phi_{j+1,k}|X_{j}|^{1+\rho}+\tau\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\phi_{j+1,k}\eta_{j+1}.

(111)

Denote the two sums by
P_{k}
and
N_{k}
, respectively, so that
Y_{k}=\gamma P_{k}+\tau N_{k}
.

The process
P_{k}
is a measurable function of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}
, whereas
N_{k}
is a linear function of
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k}
. The two noise sequences are independent, so
P_{k}
and
N_{k}
are independent. Moreover,
\mathbb{E}N_{k}=0
. Hence

\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}^{2}]=\gamma^{2}\mathbb{E}[P_{k}^{2}]+\tau^{2}\mathbb{E}[N_{k}^{2}]\geq\gamma^{2}(\mathbb{E}P_{k})^{2}+\tau^{2}\mathbb{E}[N_{k}^{2}].

(112)

We lower bound the two terms separately.

Nonlinear bias term.

For
j\in\{\lfloor k/2\rfloor,\ldots,k-1\}
and all large
k
, the product
\phi_{j+1,k}
is bounded below by a positive constant. To see this, use
\log(1-u)\geq-2u
for
u\in[0,1/2]
:

\displaystyle\phi_{j+1,k}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{k-1}\log(1-b\beta_{\ell})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-2b\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{k-1}\beta_{\ell}\right)\geq\exp\left(-2b\beta_{0}\sum_{\ell=\lfloor k/2\rfloor}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\ell+k_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\exp(-C_{b})\eqqcolon c_{\phi}>0.

Also, for these
j
,
\beta_{j}\geq c/k
and
\alpha_{j}\geq c\alpha_{k}
. Lemma
F.1
with
p=1+\rho
gives

\mathbb{E}|X_{j}|^{1+\rho}\geq c\alpha_{j}^{(1+\rho)/2}\geq c\alpha_{k}^{(1+\rho)/2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}P_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\phi_{j+1,k}\mathbb{E}|X_{j}|^{1+\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=\lfloor k/2\rfloor}^{k-1}\beta_{j}\phi_{j+1,k}\mathbb{E}|X_{j}|^{1+\rho}

\displaystyle\geq c\sum_{j=\lfloor k/2\rfloor}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k}\alpha_{k}^{(1+\rho)/2}\geq c\alpha_{k}^{(1+\rho)/2}.

Since
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{0}\bar{k}^{-a}
, this implies

(\mathbb{E}P_{k})^{2}\geq c\bar{k}^{-a(1+\rho)}.

(113)

Slow-noise term.

Because the
\eta_{j}
are independent standard Gaussians,

\mathbb{E}[N_{k}^{2}]=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta_{j}^{2}\phi_{j+1,k}^{2}.

Restricting again to
j\in\{\lfloor k/2\rfloor,\ldots,k-1\}
and using
\phi_{j+1,k}\geq c_{\phi}
and
\beta_{j}\geq c/k
,

\mathbb{E}[N_{k}^{2}]\geq\sum_{j=\lfloor k/2\rfloor}^{k-1}\frac{c}{k^{2}}\geq ck^{-1}\geq c\bar{k}^{-1}.

(114)

Substituting (
113
) and (
114
) into (
112
) proves

\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}^{2}]\geq c\gamma^{2}\bar{k}^{-a(1+\rho)}+c\tau^{2}\bar{k}^{-1}

for all sufficiently large
k
. This is (
22
).
∎

Appendix G
Additional comments on the threshold

The upper and lower bounds together show that the exponent

\min\{1,a(1+\rho)\}

is sharp for the normal-form class. This section records two simple consequences.

Proposition G.1

(Merely Lipschitz remainders cannot decouple)
.

Set
\rho=0
. In the scalar lower-bound instance with
\gamma>0
, there exists
c>0
such that

\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}^{2}]\geq c\bar{k}^{-a}

for all large
k
. Hence no theorem that assumes only a Lipschitz nonlinear remainder can guarantee the decoupled
k^{-1}
rate uniformly over the normal-form class when
a<1
.

Proof.

This is Theorem
3.4
with
\rho=0
and with the nonnegative
\tau^{2}\bar{k}^{-1}
term discarded.
∎

Proposition G.2

(Second-order remainders decouple for
a>1/2
)
.

Set
\rho=1
. Under Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
, Theorem
3.1
gives

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert Y_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C\bar{k}^{-1}.

Proof.

When
\rho=1
,
s=2a
. Since
a>1/2
,
s>1
. Thus
\bar{k}^{-s}\leq\bar{k}^{-1}
, and Theorem
3.1
gives the result.
∎
