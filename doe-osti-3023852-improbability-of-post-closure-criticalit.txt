 ABSTRACT

 As part of its periodic re-certification of the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP), an operating repository in bedded
salt for the disposal of transuranic (TRU) waste from atomic energy defense activities, the United States
Environmental Protection Agency expects a re-evaluation of features, events, and processes, such as post-closure
nuclear criticality. Although salt creep beneficially encapsulates the TRU waste in the closed WIPP repository, the
spacing between an array of waste packages is disrupted as the salt creep closes disposal rooms and containers lose
structural integrity. For most TRU waste, the possibility of post-closure criticality is exceedingly small either because
the salt neutronically isolates TRU waste canisters or because closure of a disposal room from salt creep does not
sufficiently compact the low mass of fissile material. The criticality evaluation was updated, however, because of the
introduction of criticality control overpack (CCO) containers, which may dispose up to 380 fissile gram equivalent
plutonium-239 in each container. The criticality potential is evaluated through high-fidelity geomechanical modeling
of a disposal room filled with CCO containers during two representative conditions: (1) large salt block fall, and (2)
gradual disposal room closure from salt creep. Geomechanical models of roof fall demonstrate three tiers of CCO
containers are not greatly disrupted. Geomechanical models of gradual room closure from salt creep (without brine
seepage and subsequent gas generation to permit maximum room closure) were used to predict irregular arrays of
closely packed CCOs after 1000 years, when room closure has asymptotically approached maximum compaction.
Models of spheres or cylinders with 380 fissile gram equivalent of plutonium (as oxide) at the predicted irregular
compacted spacing demonstrate that an array of CCO containers is not critical when surrounded by salt and magnesium
oxide, provided the mass of hydrogenous material shipped in CCO containers (usually plastics) is controlled or boron
carbide (a neutron poison) is mixed with the fissile contents.

Keywords: post-closure criticality evaluation; features, events, and processes screening; transuranic radioactive waste
disposal; safety assessment; performance assessment; Waste Isolation Pilot Plant

FOREWARD
 The nuclear criticality calculations referenced within this document were originally published by Oak Ridge
National Laboratory (ORNL) to meet the requirements of the US Environmental Protection Agency’s (EPA)
implementing regulation, 40 CFR §194.32(e)(3). The ORNL calculations are relied on to justify the exclusion of
nuclear criticality impacting the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP) disposal system during the post-closure regulatory
timeframe. This justification is performed in accordance with EPA "reasonable expectation" standard of proof given
in 40 CFR §191.13(b) for the post-closure timeframe. This report is being issued outside of Sandia National
Laboratories’ Nuclear Criticality Safety Program that is governed under 10 CFR 830 and US Department of Energy
(DOE) Order 420.1C, Chg. 3 without receiving that program’s concurrence or dissension of the conclusions within.
PREFACE
 EPA has regulatory authority for certifying the performance of WIPP in isolating radionuclides over the next
10 000 years against its long-term environmental standard specified in 40 CFR 191. DOE is not self-regulating when
evaluating WIPP performance after closure. DOE has assigned Sandia National Laboratories (Sandia) as responsible
for evaluating WIPP post-closure performance for EPA certification. Part of the performance assessment includes a
formal analysis of all features, events, and processes (FEPs) that may impact WIPP isolation.
 EPA does not designate the post-closure criticality FEP for special consideration in 40 CFR 191. Rather, criticality
is considered like all other FEPs. The EPA Standard, 40 CFR 191, is probabilistic and Sandia has developed a
probabilistic rationale that criticality is improbable in the post-closure timeframe when humans are absent, using EPA
exclusion criteria specified in its implementing regulation, 40 CFR 194, at §194.32(e)(3). Also in 40 CFR 194, EPA
requires compliance evaluations for WIPP about every 5 years. Thus, the screening rationale for omitting the post-
closure criticality FEP is periodically documented in certification applications to EPA, along with the compliance
evaluation. The first application to EPA was in 1996. The most recent Compliance Recertification Application was in
2019. This report supports the 2026 Compliance Recertification Application.
 We refer to the analysis underlying the rationale as a post-closure criticality evaluation. Estimates of mean
criticality behavior are used for this post-closure criticality evaluation, not worst-case behavior, in keeping with the
concept of reasonable expectation invoked by EPA as the standard of proof for demonstrating compliance with 40
CFR 191. We use the term nuclear criticality safety analysis when referring to analysis performed to show criticality
is incredible when humans are present during operations or transporting transuranic waste to WIPP. Furthermore, we
avoid referring to the criticality screening as a credibility argument because the term connotes a rule-based approach
that estimates worst-case behavior and uses great care in conservatively defining an upper subcriticality limit.
 The criticality control overpack (CCO) evaluated here was designed by the WIPP management and operating
(M&O) contractor for the US Department of Energy (DOE). The M&O is responsible for the nuclear criticality safety
analysis in the Safety Analysis Report to the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) that has shown criticality is
incredible in CCOs under normal and accident conditions. NRC has certified use of CCOs in TRUPACT-II packages
for transporting transuranic waste to WIPP. Furthermore, the M&O has shown criticality in CCOs is incredible during
normal and accident conditions at the WIPP facility prior to closure in the Design Safety Analysis report to DOE.
 This report supersedes report SAND2022-0952, Improbability of Nuclear Criticality in Compacted Criticality
Control Overpacks after Room Closure by Salt Creep at Waste Isolation Pilot Plant. No additional criticality
calculations or CCO compaction simulations were conducted; rather, the update ensures applicability to the 2026
Compliance Recertification Application and coordinates with companion reports.
