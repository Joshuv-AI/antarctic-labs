Large values of quadratic Dirichlet L L -functions of prime-related moduli
Peng Gao

Abstract
. In this paper, we apply the long resonator method to exhibit large values at the central point of the family of quadratic Dirichlet L L -functions of prime-related moduli under the generalized Riemann hypothesis.



Mathematics Subject Classification (2010)
: 11M06, 11N56

Keywords
: Dirichlet
L
-functions, large values, resonance method, character sums

1.
Introduction

Much progress has been made towards understandings of maximum sizes of
L
-functions ever since the introduction of the resonance method by K. Soundararajan in
[
Sound08
]
, who showed that for the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
and sufficiently large
T
,

\max_{T\leq t\leq 2T}|\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+it)|\geq\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log T}{\log_{2}T}}\right),

where we denote
\log_{j}
the
j
-fold iterated logarithm throughout the paper. The above result was improved by A. Bondarenko and K. Seip in
[
BS18
]
using the long resonators introduced by C. Aistleitner in
[
Aistleitner16
]
to be

\max_{0\leq t\leq T}|\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+it)|\geq\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log T\log_{3}T}{\log_{2}T}}\right).

The constant
1
was further improved to
\sqrt{2}
by R. de la Bretèche and G. Tenenbaum in
[
BT19
]
.

It is also shown in
[
Sound08
, Theorem 2]
that for sufficiently large
X
,

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\text{ fundamental discriminant }\\
X<|d|\leq 2X\end{subarray}}|L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{d})|\geq\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log_{2}X}}\right),

where
\chi_{d}=\left(\frac{d}{\cdot}\right)
is the Kronecker symbol.

In
[
DM25
]
, P. Darbar and G. Maiti employed the long resonator technique of Bondarenko–Seip to study the large value of
L(1/2,\chi_{d})
to show that under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), for sufficiently large
X
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\text{ fundamental discrimint }\\
X<|d|\leq 2X\end{subarray}}|L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{d})|\geq\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log X\log_{3}X}{\log_{2}X}}\right).

Throughout the paper, we reserve the letters
p,q
for primes. In
[
FHX26
]
, M. Fan, S. Hua and S. Xie proved that for sufficiently large
X
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<p\leq 2X\\
p\equiv 1\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{8}\end{subarray}}|L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{p})|\geq\exp\left(\left(\sqrt{\frac{8}{45}}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log_{2}X}}\right).

It is the aim of this paper to carry the approach of Darbar and Maiti to study large values at the central point of the family of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions of prime moduli under GRH. Due to some technical reasons, we consider the family of
L
-functions
\{L(s,\chi_{8q})\}
with
q
running over odd primes. Here we note as shown in
[
MVa1
, Theorem 9.13]
, the character
\chi_{8q}
is primitive modulo
8q
.

Our main result investigates the maximum sizes of
L(s,\chi_{8q})
for
q
running over primes.

Theorem 1.1
.

With the notation as above and assuming the truth of GRH. We have for sufficiently large
X
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\text{ \rm prime }\\
X<q\leq 2X\end{subarray}}\Big|L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{8q})\Big|\geq\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log X\log_{3}X}{\log_{2}X}}\right).\end{split}

As mentioned above, our proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the approaches introduced in
[
DM25
]
. The result of Theorem
1.1
implies that given in (
1.1
). Without much effort, one may show that Theorem
1.1
is also valid with
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{8q})
replaced by
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{q})
for
q
being a fundamental discriminant so that our result also improves that given in (
1.2
) under GRH.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Approximate functional equation

We have the following approximate functional equation concerning
L(1/2,\chi_{8q})
given in
[
sound1
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any odd prime
q
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}L\left(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{8q}\right)=&2\sum^{\infty}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{8q}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}V\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{q}}\right),\end{split}

where for any real number
x>0
,

\displaystyle V(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits\limits_{(2)}\left(\frac{8}{\pi}\right)^{s/2}\frac{\Gamma(s/2+1/4)}{\Gamma(1/4)}x^{-s}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}.

As shown in
[
sound1
, Lemma 2.1]
, the function
V(x)
is real-valued, smooth on
(0,+\infty)
, bounded as
x
approaches
0
and decays exponentially as
x\to+\infty
. More precisely, we have for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(2.1)

V\left(x\right)=1+O(x^{1/2-\varepsilon})\;\mbox{for}\;0<x<1\quad\mbox{and}\quad V^{(j)}\left(x\right)=O(e^{-x})\;\mbox{for}\;x>0,\;j\geq 0.

We also note that

(2.2)

\displaystyle V^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits\limits_{(2)}\left(\frac{8}{\pi}\right)^{s/2}\frac{\Gamma(s/2+1/4)}{\Gamma(1/4)}x^{-s-1}ds.

Recall that for
\Re(s)>0
,

\displaystyle\Gamma(s)=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-x}x^{s-1}dx.

It follows from the inverse Mellin transformation that for
x>0
,

\displaystyle e^{-x}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits\limits_{(2)}\Gamma(s)x^{-s}ds.

We deduce from the above that

\displaystyle 2x^{1/4}e^{-x}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits\limits_{(2)}\Gamma(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4})x^{-s}ds.

It follows from (
2.2
) and the above that we have
V^{\prime}(x)<0
for
x>0
. Note moreover that by (
2.1
) we have
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}V(x)=0
. Thus we conclude that
V(x)
is positive for
x\geq 0
.

2.3.
Smoothed character sums

We define
\delta_{c=\square}=1
if
c
is a perfect square and
\delta_{c=\square}=0
otherwise. In the remainder of the paper, let
\Phi
denote a smooth, non-negative function compactly supported on
[1,2]
satisfying
\Phi(x)=1
for
x\in[5/4,7/4]
. The Mellin transform of
\Phi(x)
is wirtten as
{\widehat{\Phi}}(s)
so that for any complex number
s
,

{\widehat{\Phi}}(s)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(x)x^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}.

We have the following result on the smoothed quadratic character sums.

Lemma 2.4
.

With the notation as above and assuming the truth of GRH. Let
c
be a positive odd integer and
\Phi(X)
be a smooth function fitting the above descriptions. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{(q,2)=1}(\log q)\chi_{8q}(c)\Phi\left(\frac{q}{X}\right)=\delta_{c=\square}\widehat{\Phi}(1)X+O\left(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}\log(c+2)\right).

3.
Proof of the Theorem
1.1

3.1.
Initial Treatments

Let
a\in(1,\infty),\delta\in(0,1)
be fixed such that
1<a<\frac{1}{\delta}
, and let
N
be a large number to be specified later. Let
[x]
denote the largest integer not exceeding
x
for any real
x
. For
k=1,\ldots,[(\log_{2}N)^{\delta}]
, denote
\mathcal{P}_{k}
the set of all primes
p
such that

e^{k}\log N\log_{2}N<p\leq e^{k+1}\log N\log_{2}N.

We further denote
\mathcal{R}_{k}
the set of positive integers that have at least
\frac{a\log N}{k^{2}\log_{3}N}
prime divisors in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
, and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{k}
the set of positive integers from
\mathcal{R}_{k}
that have all their prime divisors in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
.

We let
\mathcal{P}
be the union of
\mathcal{P}_{k}
so that it is the set of all primes
p
such that

e\log N\log_{2}N<p\leq e^{(\log_{2}N)^{\delta}}\log N\log_{2}N.

We define a multiplicative function
\psi
supported on the set of square-free integers such that for any
p\in\mathcal{P}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\psi(p)=\sqrt{\frac{\log N\log_{2}N}{\log_{3}N}}p^{-1/2}\left(\log p-\log(\log N\log_{2}N)\right)^{-1}.

We further define
\psi(p)=0
for
p\notin\mathcal{P}
.

We now set

\mathcal{R}:=\rm{supp}(\psi)\setminus\displaystyle\bigcup_{k=1}^{[(\log_{2}N)^{\delta}]}\mathcal{R}_{k}.

Then, we define the resonator for
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{8q})
for any prime
q
to be the Dirichlet polynomial

R_{q}:=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\chi_{8q}(m).

The following lemma, taken from
[
DM25
, Lemma 3]
, gives estimations on the sizes of
|\mathcal{R}|
, respectively.

Lemma 3.2
.

With the notation as above. Suppose that
1<a<\frac{1}{\delta}
. Then we have
|\mathcal{R}|\leq N
for
N
large enough.

We now define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{N}:=\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\psi(m)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\psi{(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l|n\end{subarray}}\psi(l)\sqrt{l}.

We end this section by including three results from
[
BS18
, Lemma 1–3]
concerning various sums related to
\mathcal{A}_{N}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

With the notation as above. We have as
N\to\infty
,

\mathcal{A}_{N}\geq\exp\left((\delta+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log N\log_{3}N}{\log_{2}N}}\right).

Lemma 3.4
.

With the notation as above. We have as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\psi(m)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\\
n\notin\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi{(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l|n\end{subarray}}\psi(l)\sqrt{l}=o(\mathcal{A}_{N}).

Lemma 3.5
.

With the notation as above. We have as
N\to\infty
, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\psi(m)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi{(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l|n\\
l\leq n/N^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\psi(l)\sqrt{l}=o(\mathcal{A}_{N}),

where the implicit constant only depends on
\varepsilon
.

3.6.
Completion of the proof

Let
\Phi
be the function described in Section
2.3
. We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}:=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{q}(\log q)L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{8q})R_{q}^{2}\Phi(\frac{q}{X}),\quad\mathcal{S}_{2}:=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{q}(\log q)R_{q}^{2}\Phi(\frac{q}{X}).

As
R^{2}_{d}\geq 0
and
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{8q})\in\mathbb{R}
, we then observe that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<q\leq 2X\end{subarray}}\Big|L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{8q})\Big|\geq\frac{\mathcal{S}_{1}}{\mathcal{S}_{2}}.

It remains to establish a lower bound for
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and an upper bound for
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. We first note that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{1}=&\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{q}(\log q)L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{8q})R_{q}^{2}\Phi(\frac{q}{X})\\
=&2\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\psi(n)\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{q}\chi_{8q}(lmn)V\left(\frac{l}{\sqrt{q}}\right).\end{split}

We now apply Lemma
2.4
and partial summation to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{q}\chi_{8q}(lmn)V\left(\frac{l}{\sqrt{q}}\right)\Phi(\frac{q}{X})\\
=&\int^{2X}_{X}V\left(\frac{l}{\sqrt{t}}\right)d\left(t\delta_{lmn=\square}{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)+O\left(t^{1/2+\varepsilon}\log(lmn+2)\right)\right)\\
=&{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\delta_{lmn=\square}\int^{2}_{1}V\left(\frac{l}{\sqrt{Xt}}\right)dt\\
&+V\left(\frac{l}{\sqrt{t}}\right)O\left(t^{1/2+\varepsilon}\log(lmn+2)\right)\Big|^{2X}_{X}-\int^{2X}_{X}O\left(t^{1/2+\varepsilon}\log(lmn+2)\right)V^{\prime}\left(\frac{l}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\frac{l}{2t^{3/2}}dt\\
=:&{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\delta_{lmn=\square}\int^{2}_{1}V\left(\frac{l}{\sqrt{Xt}}\right)dt+R.\end{split}

It follows from this and (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}={\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{R}}

\displaystyle\psi(m)\psi(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}lmn=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}\int_{1}^{2}V\left(\frac{l}{\sqrt{Xt}}\right)dt+O\Bigg(\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\psi(n)\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}R\Bigg).

In view of the rapid decay of
V
and
V^{\prime}
given in (
2.1
), we see that we may restrict the sum over
l
to be
l\leq X^{1/2+\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
in the error term above. We further set
N=X^{\frac{1}{4}-5\varepsilon}
for some
0<\varepsilon<1/20
and observe that
|\mathcal{R}|\leq N
from Lemma
3.2
. It follows that

\displaystyle R\ll X^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

We deduce from the above that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{1}=&{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\psi(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}lmn=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}\int_{1}^{2}V\left(\frac{N(l)}{\sqrt{Xt}}\right)dt+O\Bigg(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\leq X^{1/2+\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\right)^{2}\Bigg)\\
=&{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\psi(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}lmn=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}\int_{1}^{2}V\left(\frac{N(l)}{\sqrt{Xt}}\right)dt+O\Bigg(X^{3/4+2\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\right)^{2}\Bigg)\\
=&{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\psi(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}lmn=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}\int_{1}^{2}V\left(\frac{N(l)}{\sqrt{Xt}}\right)dt+O\Bigg(X^{3/4+2\varepsilon}|\mathcal{R}|\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\psi^{2}(m)\Bigg),\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

As
\psi(n)\geq 0
and
V(x)\geq 0
, we may thus keep only the case
lm=n
in the main term of (
3.4
). Together with the observation that
|\mathcal{R}|\leq N
from Lemma
3.2
, we see that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}\geq

\displaystyle{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\sum_{n\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{\psi(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\end{subarray}}\psi(m)\sqrt{m}\int_{1}^{2}V\left(\frac{n}{m\sqrt{Xt}}\right)dt+O\left(X^{3/4+2\varepsilon}N\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)^{2}\right)\right).

As
N=X^{\frac{1}{4}-5\varepsilon}
, it follows that when
m\geq n/N^{\varepsilon}
, we have

0<\frac{n}{m\sqrt{Xt}}<1
for
1\leq t\leq 2
. We now apply the estimation
V(x)=1+O\left(x^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\right)
given in (
2.1
) to deduce from the above that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{1}\geq&{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\sum_{n\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{\psi(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\\
m\geq n/N^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)\sqrt{m}\\
&+O\left(X^{3/4+2\varepsilon}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\\
m\geq n/N^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)\right)+O\left(X^{1-\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)^{2}\right)\right).\end{split}

We observe from (
3.1
) that we have
0<\psi(p)\leq 1
for
p\in\mathcal{P}
. It follows from this that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\\
m\geq n/N^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)\ll\sum_{n\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\end{subarray}}\psi(m)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathcal{R}\\
m\mid n\end{subarray}}\psi(n)\ll|\mathcal{R}|\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\psi^{2}(m).

As
|\mathcal{R}|\leq N
from Lemma
3.2
and
N=X^{\frac{1}{4}-5\varepsilon}
, we deduce from the above and (
3.6
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{1}\geq&{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\sum_{n\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{\psi(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\\
m\geq n/N^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)\sqrt{m}+O\left(X^{1-\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)^{2}\right)\right).\end{split}

We now apply above together with Lemma
3.3
, Lemma
3.4
, and Lemma
3.5
to see that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{1}\geq&\left(1+o(1)\right){\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\exp\left((\delta+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log N\log_{3}N}{\log_{2}N}}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)^{2}\right)+O\left(X^{1-\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)^{2}\right)\right)\\
\geq&\left(1+o(1)\right){\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\exp\left(\left(\delta\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-5\varepsilon}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log X\log_{3}X}{\log_{2}X}}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)^{2}\right).\end{split}

Next, we proceed to establish an upper bound of
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Again, applying Lemma
2.4
and arguing similar to those that lead to (
3.5
) imply that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}=

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{q}(\log q)R_{q}^{2}\Phi(\frac{q}{X})=\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\psi(n)\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{q}(\log q)\chi_{8q}(mn)\Phi(\frac{q}{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathcal{R}\\
mn=\square\end{subarray}}\psi(m)\psi(n)+O\Bigg(X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)\psi(n)\log(mn+2)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(n)^{2}+O\left(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}N\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)^{2}\right),

where the last equality above follows by noting that
mn=\square
implies that
m=n
since
m
and
n
are square-free.

Again using
|\mathcal{R}|\leq N=X^{\frac{1}{4}-5\varepsilon}
, we deduce from the above that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}\leq\left(1+o(1)\right){\widehat{\Phi}}(1)X\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\psi(m)^{2}\right).

Hence, for sufficiently large
X
and arbitrary small
\varepsilon>0
, we deduce from (
3.2
), (
3.7
) and the above that

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\text{ \rm prime }\\
X<q\leq 2X\end{subarray}}\Big|L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{8q})\Big|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathcal{S}_{1}}{\mathcal{S}_{2}}\geq\exp\left(\left(\delta\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-5\varepsilon}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log X\log_{3}X}{\log_{2}X}}\right).

By letting
\delta\rightarrow 1^{+}
, we obtain the desired estimation given in (
1.3
). This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
