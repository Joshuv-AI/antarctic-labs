Ricci solitons from the perspectives of energy function
Shubham Yadav, Hemangi Madhusudan Shah

Abstract
. In this article, we explore to what extent the geometry of gradient Ricci solitons can be carried over to the geometry of non-gradient Ricci solitons. We use energy function E E of the soliton as a tool to study this. We use Omori-Yau maximum principle to investigate the bounds on the energy function. One of the main results obtained is that a non-steady Ricci soliton with symmetric covariant derivative is gradient. We explore non-gradient Ricci solitons of constant scalar curvature. We prove a weighted L 1 L^{1} -Liouville type theorem with respect to the energy function under mild assumptions on the scalar curvature. Finally, we show that measure e − E ​ d ​ Vol g e^{-E}d\Vol_{g} for complete shrinking Ricci soliton is finite, partially generalizing a result by Aaron Naber. Subsequently, this implies that nongradient shrinking Ricci solitons also have finite fundamental groups similar to their gradient counterparts.

1.
Introduction

A Ricci soliton is a quadruple
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)
, where
(M^{n},g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold,
V\in\chi(M),
a complete vector field, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(1)

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{V}g+\ricci=\frac{\lambda}{2}g,

where
\mathcal{L}_{V}
is the Lie derivative with respect to
V
and
\ricci
is the Ricci tensor of
(M,g)
.
The vector field
V
is called the soliton vector field.
The soliton is said to be shrinking when
\lambda>0
, steady when
\lambda=0
, or expanding when
\lambda<0
.
Immediately, one notices that all Einstein manifolds can be considered as Ricci solitons by taking
V
as zero or a Killing field.
Such solitons are said to be trivial.
If the soliton vector field is gradient, i.e.
V=\nabla f
, then the soliton is said to be gradient and the function
f
is called the soliton function.
Thus, the soliton function of a gradient Ricci soliton satisfies the equation

(2)

\hessian f+\ricci=\frac{\lambda}{2}g.

Following
[
4
]
, we define the energy function of a Ricci soliton as

(3)

E=\frac{1}{2}g(V,V).

This article primarily aims to explore the relationship between the geometry of Ricci solitons and the properties of their associated energy functions.
This was also explored by Sharief Deshmukh and Hana Alsodais in
[
4
]
, primarily relying on the fact that
eq.

1
is equivalent to

\nabla_{X}V=\frac{\lambda}{2}X+\phi X-\Qricci X,

where
Q
is the Ricci tensor, and
\phi
being the (1,1) tensor given by

\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\eta(X,Y)=g(\phi X,Y),

with
\eta
as the dual 1-form of the potential vector field
V
, i.e.,

\eta(X)=g(X,V).

Our approach is quite different (independent of
\eta
) and the geometric results obtained in the article
are also of different flavour.

The term
\mathcal{L}_{V}g
can be thought of as the symmetrization of
\nabla V
.
If the vector field
V
is gradient,
\nabla V
is already symmetric and
\mathcal{L}_{V}g
produces the same term.
However, a vector field
W
may have symmetric covariant derivative
\nabla W
but fail to be gradient.
For all known examples of non-gradient Ricci solitons, the soliton vector field does not have a symmetric covariant derivative.
This leads us to believe that symmetry of a covariant derivative of the soliton vector field is a sufficient condition for a Ricci soliton to be gradient.
We prove this for the non-steady case in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For a non-steady Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)
, if
\nabla V
is symmetric, then the soliton is gradient.

Throughout this article, we will denote the scalar curvature by
\scalar
and the
(1,1)
-tensor field dual to Ricci tensor on
M
by
\Qricci
.
Thus, for any vector fields
X,Y
on
M
,

g(\Qricci X,Y)=\ricci(X,Y)=g(X,\Qricci Y).

The traceless Ricci tensor will be denoted by
\mathring{\Qricci}
,

\mathring{\Qricci}X=\Qricci X-\frac{\scalar}{n}X.

The norm of any vector field or tensor
T
will be denoted by
\left|T\right|
.

In what follows, we will be interested in exploring the geometry of a priori nongradient Ricci solitons.
Towards this we prove the following result.

Theorem 2
.

For a Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)
, the following holds:

(4)

\displaystyle\Delta\scalar-V\scalar-\lambda\scalar=-2\left|\Qricci\right|^{2},

(5)

\displaystyle\Delta\scalar-V\scalar-\frac{2\scalar}{n}\left(\divergence V\right)=-2\left|\mathring{\Qricci}\right|^{2}.

Note that this theorem allows us to generalise the first half of Proposition 3.3 of
[
11
]
to the nongradient case (see
Proposition

6
).

It is known that for a gradient Ricci soliton with soliton function
f
(see Lemma 1.1 of
[
2
]
)

(6)

\nabla(\scalar+\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}-\lambda f)=0.

We establish a weaker form of this for non-gradient Ricci solitons in the following.

Corollary 1
.

For any Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)
,

(7)

\divergence(\nabla S+\nabla E+\nabla_{V}V-\lambda V)=0.

Note that for a gradient Ricci soliton,
V=\nabla f
and
\nabla E=\nabla_{\nabla f}\nabla f
. Thus, the above equation reduces to

\Delta(\scalar+2E-\lambda f)=0.

The boundedness of the energy function is a particularly restrictive condition for an expanding soliton. This is captured in the following theorem.
Note that a zero energy function implies that a Ricci soliton is trivial.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)
be a Ricci soliton with
E
as its energy function.

(1)

If the soliton is nontrivial and
E
attains a maximum,
then the soliton is shrinking or steady.
If further
\ricci(V,V)\leq 0
,
then the soliton is steady.

(2)

For a nontrivial soliton, if
E
is bounded from above by
E^{*}=\sup E
and

\ricci\geq-(n-1)\Lambda^{2}
, then the soliton is steady or shrinking.

(3)

If
\ricci(V,V)\leq 0
and
\left|\nabla E\right|\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(M)
,
then the soliton vector field is parallel and the soliton is trivial.

In particular, this theorem partially recovers the well-known result by Perelman (see (2.4) of
[
10
]
): There are no nontrivial compact expanding or steady Ricci solitons.

The drifted Laplacian of a function
h
is defined as
\Delta_{V}h=\Delta h-Vh
.
The energy function is also linked to the existence of nonconstant
V
-harmonic functions (that is harmonic with respect to
\Delta_{V}
).
For a compact manifold, all Ricci solitons are gradient and subsequently, the strong maximum principle for elliptic operators implies that all
f
-harmonic functions (i.e.
\Delta_{f}u=\Delta u-g(\nabla f,\nabla u)=0
) must be constant.
The following
L^{1}
Liouville Type theorem shows that on a noncompact Ricci soliton with scalar curvature bounded below by some appropriate constant,
the existence of nonconstant
V
-harmonic functions is intimately connected with the energy function.

Theorem 4
.

Any
V
-harmonic function
u
with

\int\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}E\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}<\infty

on a noncompact Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)
with
\scalar>\frac{(n-2)}{2}\lambda

is constant.

Note that for any Ricci soliton, the scalar curvature
\scalar
is always bounded below (see Theorem 2.14,
[
3
]
).
In view of this, the assumption in the above theorem on the scalar curvature is milder one.
Moreover, we also obtain bounds on scalar curvature on Ricci solitons when scalar curvature is subharmonic with respect to the drifted Laplacian
\Delta_{V}
(see
proposition

6
).
We see in
[
11
]
that a constant scalar curvature is a very restrictive condition on gradient Ricci solitons.
We explore some of these results in
Section
4
for the nongradient case.
We also give examples of Cigar soliton and
\mbox{Sol}_{3}
solitons supporting some of our results.

Theorem 1 of
[
9
]
implies that the measure
e^{-f}\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}
of a complete shrinking gradient Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,f,\lambda)
with bounded Ricci curvature is finite.
We extend this result to complete, nongradient shrinking Ricci solitons, by using similar techniques as in this paper. In fact, we show that:

Theorem 5
.

Let
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)
be a complete shrinking Ricci soliton with bounded Ricci curvature.
Then the measure
e^{-E}\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}
is finite. Consequently,
M
has finite fundamental group.

As a particular case of the above theorem, we partially recover the aforementioned result of
[
9
]
for gradient Ricci solitons.
See
Corollary

3
for details.

The paper is divided into 6 sections.
The Section
2
is devoted to Preliminaries, Section
3
explores general computations on Ricci solitons, while the Section
4
investigates interplay between the scalar curvature, energy function and soliton vector field while the Section
5
investigates the
L^{1}
-Liouville Type Theorem on Ricci solitons.
The final section namely,
Section

6
generalizes a particular result of Aaron Naber
[
9
]
to nongradient shrinking Ricci solitons.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we describe some of the basics on any Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
pertaining to Ricci solitons. The concepts and basic formulae can be found in
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
.

In the sequel, the following Bochner’s formula will be used.

(8)

\Delta\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|X\right|^{2}\right)=g(\Delta X,X)+\left|\nabla X\right|^{2},

where
\Delta
on the right hand side indicates the connection Laplacian and is defined as

\Delta X=\trace(\nabla^{2}X).

The gradient version of the Bochner formula is

(9)

\Delta\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right)=\ricci\left(\nabla u,\nabla u\right)+g\left(\nabla u,\nabla\left(\Delta u\right)\right)+\left|\hessian u\right|^{2}.

The Lie derivative of the connection with respect to a vector field
V
on
M
is defined as follows:

(\mathcal{L}_{V}\nabla)(X,Y)=\mathcal{L}_{V}(\nabla_{X}Y)-\nabla_{\mathcal{L}_{V}X}Y-\nabla_{X}(\mathcal{L}_{V}Y).

We define the curvature tensor as

\curvature(X,Y)Z=\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}Z-\nabla^{2}_{Y,X}Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,

and its Lie derivative with respect to
V
as

(\mathcal{L}_{V}\curvature)(X,Y)Z=\mathcal{L}_{V}(\curvature(X,Y)Z)-\curvature(\mathcal{L}_{V}X,Y)Z-\curvature(X,\mathcal{L}_{V}Y)Z-\curvature(X,Y)\mathcal{L}_{V}Z.

The following formulas can be found in
[
13
]
, we present their proofs here for the sake of convenience.

Proposition 1

(Yano’s formulae)
.

For any vector fields
X,Y,Z
on a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
we have,

(10)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\nabla\right)(X,Y)=\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}V+\curvature(V,X)Y,

(11)

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{X}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}g\right)\right)(Y,Z)=g(\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}V,Z)+g(Y,\nabla^{2}_{X,Z}V),

(12)

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{X}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}g\right)\right)(Y,Z)=g\left(\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\nabla\right)(X,Y),Z\right)+g\left(\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\nabla\right)(X,Z),Y\right),

(13)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{V}\curvature)(X,Y)Z=\left(\nabla_{X}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\nabla\right)\right)(Y,Z)-\left(\nabla_{Y}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\nabla\right)\right)(X,Z).

Proof.

The first two equations follow from unwinding the definition.

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\nabla\right)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{V}\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{\nabla_{X}Y}V-\nabla_{\nabla_{V}X-\nabla_{X}V}Y-\nabla_{X}(\nabla_{V}Y-\nabla_{Y}V),

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}_{V,X}Y-\nabla^{2}_{X,V}Y+\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}V=\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}V+\curvature(V,X)Y.

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{X}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}g\right)\right)(Y,Z)

\displaystyle=X\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}g(Y,Z)\right)-(\mathcal{L}_{V}g)(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-(\mathcal{L}_{V}g)(Y,\nabla_{X}Z),

\displaystyle=X\left(g(\nabla_{Y}V,Z)+g(Y,\nabla_{Z}V)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-(\mathcal{L}_{V}g)(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-(\mathcal{L}_{V}g)(Y,\nabla_{X}Z),

\displaystyle=g(\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}V,Z)+g(Y,\nabla^{2}_{X,Z}V).

The third equation follows from the first two and the symmetries of the curvature tensor.

\displaystyle g((\mathcal{L}_{V}\nabla)(X,Y)

\displaystyle,Z)+g\left(\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}\nabla\right)(X,Z),Y\right)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}V,Z)+g(\nabla^{2}_{X,Z}V,Y)+\curvature(V,X,Y,Z)+\curvature(V,X,Z,Y),

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla_{X}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}g\right)\right)(Y,Z).

The last equation follows from
[
13
, eqn 5.16]
or can be computed directly.
∎

It is well known that for any function
f
and vector field
X
,

\divergence\hessian f(X)=\ricci(\nabla f,X)+X(\Delta f).

The following is the nongradient version of the above formula (see also Lemma 2.1 of
[
11
]
).

Proposition 2
.

Any two vector fields
V,X
on a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)

satisfy:

(14)

[\divergence(\mathcal{L}_{V}g)]X=g(\Delta V,X)+\ricci(V,X)+X(\divergence V).

Proof.

For a point
p\in M
, let
\{E_{i}\}
be an orthonormal frame in a neighbourhood of
point
p
which is parallel at
p
. At point
p\in M
we get,

which implies

(15)

X(\divergence V)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(E_{i},\nabla^{2}_{X,E_{i}}V).

Now, using
eq.

11
at

we obtain,

\displaystyle[\divergence(\mathcal{L}_{V}g)]X

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla_{E_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{V}g)\right)(E_{i},X),

\displaystyle=g(\nabla^{2}_{E_{i},E_{i}}V,X)+g(E_{i},\nabla^{2}_{E_{i},X}V),

\displaystyle=g(\Delta V,X)+g(E_{i},\curvature(E_{i},X)V)+g(E_{i},\nabla^{2}_{X,E_{i}}V),

\displaystyle=g(\Delta V,X)+\ricci(X,V)+X(\divergence V),

where the last equality uses
eq.

15
.
∎

Lemma 2

Let
(M^{n},g)

\divergence(TX)=(\divergence T)X+\frac{1}{2}g\left(\mathring{T},\mathcal{L}_{X}g\right)+\frac{\trace T}{n}\divergence X.

Proof.

Let
(E_{i})

\displaystyle g(T,\mathcal{L}_{X}g)

\displaystyle=g(T(E_{i}),E_{j})~(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)(E_{i},E_{j})

\displaystyle=T_{i}^{j}\left(g(\nabla_{E_{i}}X,E_{j})+g(E_{i},\nabla_{E_{j}}X\right))

\displaystyle=2g(TE_{i},\nabla_{E_{i}}X)=2g(E_{i},T(\nabla_{E_{i}}X)).

\nabla_{Y}(TX)=(\nabla_{Y}T)X+T(\nabla_{Y}X).

\divergence(TX)=(\divergence T)X+\frac{1}{2}g(T,\mathcal{L}_{X}g).

Substituting

Lemma 3

(

For a subharmonic function

Proof.

Fix point

The Omori-Yau maximum principle extends the classical maximum principle to non-compact complete Riemannian manifolds with Ricci bounded below.

Theorem 6

(Cheng-Yau,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded below

Ric\geq-(n-1)\Lambda^{2}
.
Then for any
u\in C^{2}(M)
with
u^{*}=\sup_{M}u<\infty
, there exists a sequence of points
x_{k}\in M
, depending on
M
and on
u
, such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}u(x_{k})=u^{*},\quad\left|\nabla u\right|(x_{k})<\frac{1}{k},\quad\Delta u(x_{k})<\frac{1}{k}.

See
3

Proof.

For a nontrivial Ricci soliton, the energy function
E
is not identically zero.

(1)

Equation

18
implies

(28)

\Delta E+\lambda E=\left|\nabla V\right|^{2}+g(V,\nabla E).

If
E
attains a maximum at
p
, then
\nabla E|_{p}=0
and
\Delta E|_{p}\leq 0
. Thus,
\lambda\geq 0
.

If further,
\ricci(V,V)\leq 0
, the other part of
eq.

18
implies

\Delta E=\left|\nabla V\right|^{2}-\ricci(V,V)\geq 0.

Thus
E
is subharmonic and if it attains a maximum, it must be constant.
Then

\left|\nabla V\right|=0
and
\lambda E=0
, which gives the result.

(2)

The Omori-Yau maximum principle (
Theorem

6
) implies the existence of a sequence
(x_{k})
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}E(x_{k})=E^{*},~\left|\nabla E\right|(x_{k})<\frac{1}{k},~\Delta E(x_{k})<\frac{1}{k}.

Noting
g(V,\nabla E)\geq-\left|V\right|\left|\nabla E\right|=-\sqrt{2E}\left|\nabla E\right|
and evaluating
eq.

28
at
x_{k}

\displaystyle\lambda E(x_{k})

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla V\right|^{2}(x_{k})-\Delta E(x_{k})+g(V,\nabla E)(x_{k})

\displaystyle>\left|\nabla V\right|^{2}(x_{k})-\frac{1}{k}-\sqrt{2E}\left|\nabla E\right|(x_{k})

\displaystyle>\left|\nabla V\right|^{2}(x_{k})-\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k}\sqrt{2E(x_{k})}

As

, which implies

\lambda\geq 0
.

(3)

From

18

.
Consequently, by the hypothesis that

we obtain that

\left|\nabla V\right|^{2}=\ricci(V,V)=0

Gaffney’s Stokes theorem states that for a complete orientable Riemannian manifold, if

Proposition 7
.

The following corollary gives an estimate of the unboundedness of

under the hypothesis of the above proposition.

Corollary 2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)
be an orientable Ricci soliton of constant scalar curvature
\scalar\neq\frac{n\lambda}{2}
of finite volume. Then, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\int_{E\geq N}E\;\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g})
is not finite.

Proof.

If
M
has finite volume, say
{\Vol}(M)
, then

\int_{E\leq 1}\sqrt{2E}\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\leq\sqrt{2}~{\Vol}(M),

and, whenever
E\geq 1
,
\sqrt{E}\leq E
, which gives

\int_{E\geq 1}\sqrt{2E}\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\leq\sqrt{2}\int_{E\geq 1}E\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}.

From
Proposition

7
, we have
\sqrt{2E}=\left|V\right|\not\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(M)
, which combined with the above implies

\int_{E\geq 1}E\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\to\infty.

Further for any finite
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\int_{E\leq N}E\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\leq N~{\Vol}(M),

and hence,

\int_{E\geq N}E\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\to\infty.

∎

L^{1}
-Liouville type theorem with respect to energy function

In this section, we prove

-Liouville type theorem for Ricci
soliton
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)

Theorem

See

Proof.

For any point
p
, let
\phi
be the standard cut-off function on
\mathrm{B}_{p}(2r)
.
Let
A_{r}=\mathrm{B}_{p}(2r)\setminus\mathrm{B}_{p}(r)
.
Throughout this proof, we will denote
\mathcal{U}=\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}
.

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left(V\mathcal{U}\right)\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\mathcal{U}\left(\phi^{2}\divergence V+2\phi V\phi\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\mathcal{U}(\phi^{2}\divergence V)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}+\int_{A_{r}}\mathcal{U}(2\phi\left|V\right|\left|\nabla\phi\right|)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\mathcal{U}(\phi^{2}\divergence V)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}+\int_{A_{r}}\mathcal{U}\left(\phi^{2}\left|V\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\mathcal{U}(\phi^{2}\divergence V)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}+\int_{A_{r}}\mathcal{U}\left(2\phi^{2}E+\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}.

Also,

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\Delta\mathcal{U}\right)\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}2\phi g\left(\nabla\mathcal{U},\nabla\phi\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=-\int_{A_{r}}2\phi\hessian u(\nabla u,\nabla\phi)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{r}}2\phi\left|\hessian u\right|\left|\nabla u\right|\left|\nabla\phi\right|\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{r}}\left(\phi^{2}\left|\hessian u\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{r}}\left(\phi^{2}\left|\hessian u\right|^{2}+2\mathcal{U}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}.

Using the above inequalities,

\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{V}\mathcal{U}\right)\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\leq\int_{M}\mathcal{U}\left(\phi^{2}\divergence V\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}+\int_{A_{r}}\left(\phi^{2}\left|\hessian u\right|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\\
+\int_{A_{r}}\left(3\mathcal{U}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}+\int_{A_{r}}(2\phi^{2}\mathcal{U}E)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}.

Comparing this with
eq.

19
for a
V
-harmonic function
u

and noting,

\int_{M}\left|\hessian u\right|^{2}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\geq\int_{A_{r}}\left|\hessian u\right|^{2}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g},

we obtain

\int_{M}\left(\lambda-\divergence V\right)\mathcal{U}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\leq\int_{A_{r}}\left(3\mathcal{U}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}+\int_{A_{r}}(2\phi^{2}\mathcal{U}E)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}.

Lastly, taking
r\to\infty
, and using
eq.

16
,

0\leq\int_{M}\left(\scalar-\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}\leq\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{A_{r}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right)\left(2E\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\Vol}_{g}=0.

By hypothesis on scalar curvature, the above inequality implies that

is constant.

6.
Finiteness of
E
-Volume of Shrinking Ricci Solitons

We now turn our attention to proving
Theorem

5
.
We first begin with a lower estimate on the energy function of a Ricci soliton with bounded Ricci curvature.

Lemma 4
.

Let
(M^{n},g,V,\lambda)
be a noncompact shrinking Ricci soliton with the bounded Ricci curvature.
Then there exists an
L_{0}
such that for every
unit speed geodesic
\gamma:[0,L]\to M
with
L\geq L_{0}
and

\gamma(0)=p\in M
,

E(L)\geq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8}L^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}aL+\frac{a^{2}}{2},

where
a
is a constant that depends only on the geometry of the soliton on the unit ball
B(p,1)
.

Proof.

We first assume that
L\geq 2
.
Let
E^{i}(p)
denote an orthonormal basis along
\gamma
with
E^{n}(t)={\gamma}^{\prime}(t)
and

E^{i}(t)
denote the parallel transport of
E^{i}
over
\gamma(t)
. Write

V=V^{i}E_{i}
. Then for any Lipschitz function
\phi:[0,L]\to[0,1]
with
\phi(0)=\phi(L)=0
, the second variation formula implies

(n-1)\int_{0}^{L}(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{L}\phi^{2}\ricci(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using the Ricci soliton equation and

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{V}g(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=\dot{V^{n}},

in the above equation, we get

(n-1)\int_{0}^{L}(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{L}\phi^{2}\dot{V^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Subsequently,

(n-1)\int_{0}^{L}(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{L}\dot{V^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{L}(1-\phi^{2})\dot{V^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using the Ricci soliton equation again and noting the bound on Ricci curvature,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\left(1-\phi^{2}\right)\dot{V^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\left(1-\phi^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}-\ricci\left(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{L}\left(1-\phi^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}-C\right)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Therefore,

(30)

(n-1)\int_{0}^{L}(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq-V^{n}(L)+V^{n}(0)+\int_{0}^{L}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}-C\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{0}^{L}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Now we choose,

\phi=\begin{cases}t,&0\leq t\leq 1;\\
1,&1\leq t\leq L-1;\\
L-t,&L-1\leq t\leq L.\\
\end{cases}

Substituting in
eq.

30
,

V^{n}(L)\geq V^{n}(0)-2(n-1)+\frac{\lambda}{2}L-\frac{4}{3}C,

or

\sqrt{2E}(L)\geq V^{n}(L)\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}L+a,

where
a
is a constant that depends only on the geometry of the soliton on the unit ball
B(p,1)
. Since
\lambda>0
, we can choose
L_{0}
large enough so that the right hand side of the above equation is positive. Then we have for
L\geq L_{0}
,

E(L)\geq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8}L^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}aL+\frac{a^{2}}{2}.

∎

.

Proof.

Using exponential coordinates at

\sqrt{\mathrm{det}g}\leq(\sinh\sqrt{C}r)^{n-1}\lesssim e^{(n-1)\sqrt{C}r}.

{\Vol}_{E}(M)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-E}\sqrt{\mathrm{det}g}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s^{n-1}\\
