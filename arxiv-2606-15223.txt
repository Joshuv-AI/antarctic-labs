A Staggered Discontinuous Galerkin Method for linear elasticity problem on Polytopal Meshes Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Long Chen, Xuehai Huang, Ruishu Wang, Ran Zhang

Abstract
This paper develops a novel staggered discontinuous Galerkin (SDG) method for linear elasticity based on the Hellinger-Reissner variational principle. We construct symmetric stress spaces with normal continuity across element boundaries on arbitrary polytopal meshes, while approximating the displacement field using piecewise polynomial functions defined on the same meshes. The method is locking-free and satisfies a local balance of linear momentum and angular momentum. We present a comprehensive theoretical analysis, including proofs of stability and error estimates. The formulation admits a hybridizable structure, which significantly simplifies the numerical implementation. Numerical experiments validate the theoretical results and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

Introduction

We develop a novel staggered discontinuous Galerkin (SDG) method for the linear elasticity problem. The Hellinger-Reissner mixed variational principle seeks the stress
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in H(\operatorname{div},\Omega;\mathbb{S})
and the displacement
\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau})+(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{u})

\displaystyle=0\qquad\quad\;\;\,\forall~\boldsymbol{\tau}\in H(\operatorname{div},\Omega;\mathbb{S}),

(1a)

\displaystyle(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=-(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v})\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1b)

where the symmetric stress space is

H(\operatorname{div},\Omega;\mathbb{S})=\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d\times d}:\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\boldsymbol{\tau}=\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\intercal}\}.

The transition from the primal displacement formulation to this mixed system, including the definition of the compliance tensor
\mathcal{A}
, is detailed in Section 2.

Discretizing this mixed formulation is difficult because the scheme must: (i) satisfy the inf-sup condition; (ii) ensure stress symmetry and normal continuity; and (iii) provide optimal order of convergence. Many finite elements have been developed to meet these requirements

[

[

]
. A hybridizable method was also presented in

Managing continuity across mesh boundaries is central to finite element design. Standard mixed methods use strong continuity for one variable and weak continuity for the other. In contrast, the SDG framework
[
18
,
19
]
uses a primal-dual mesh structure where each variable is continuous only on its own mesh boundaries. For mixed elasticity,
[
31
,
41
]
showed that the stress tensor can be discontinuous across primal edges but remains normal-continuous on dual edges, while the displacement follows the opposite pattern. This staggered continuity ensures stability without extra stabilization terms. However, current SDG methods for elasticity only enforce weak symmetry rather than strong symmetry of the stress tensor
[
31
,
41
]
.

In this work, we develop a new SDG scheme for mixed linear elasticity that interchanges the continuity assignments of stress and displacement compared with
[
31
,
41
]
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{h}
be a polyhedral mesh and
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{*}
be its dual mesh by adding a vertex to the center of each element in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
. In the proposed new SDG method, the stress tensor is piecewise
\mathbb{P}_{k}(T;\mathbb{S})
but in
H(\operatorname{div},\mathbb{S};\omega_{F})
for the patch containing primal face
F
. The stress is strongly symmetric, in contrast to the weak symmetry employed in previous SDG formulations
[
31
,
41
]
. The displacement, is approximated by piecewise polynomials over general polytopal elements which is in
\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset H^{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the primal cell
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
. The normal continuity of stress is on the boundary of the primal element, while the displacement is continuous on the boundary of dual elements. This complementary continuity ensures a discrete inf-sup condition.

In classical linear elasticity, the equilibrium equation
\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{f}

expresses the balance of linear momentum.
The symmetry of the stress tensor,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\intercal}
, follows from the balance of angular momentum.
Together, these balance laws provide the physical basis for symmetric,
H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming stress formulations. In our scheme, both important balance law are preserved locally.

Reversing continuity assignments in an SDG framework was recently applied to the Poisson equation
[
16
]
. That work highlights links between this SDG design and nonconforming Crouzeix-Raviart, weak Galerkin, and virtual element methods. Extending this approach to linear elasticity is difficult. It requires symmetric-tensor DoFs that are normal-continuous across polytopal boundaries. The method is locking-free; its stability and convergence are robust in the incompressible limit
\lambda\to\infty
.

Finite Element Spaces

2.1
Linear elastic equations

The linear elasticity problem is given by

-\operatorname{div}(2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})+\lambda(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{I})=\boldsymbol{f}\;\;\textrm{ in }\Omega;\quad\boldsymbol{u}|_{\partial\Omega}=\boldsymbol{0},

(2)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d\geq 2)
is a bounded polytope. Here
\boldsymbol{u}
denotes the displacement field,
\boldsymbol{f}
is the body force,
\mu
and
\lambda
are the Lamé constants, and
\boldsymbol{I}
is the identity matrix.
Introducing the stress tensor

\boldsymbol{\sigma}=2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})+\lambda(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{I}
,
the problem (
2
) can be rewritten as the first-order system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}&=\boldsymbol{f}\;\qquad\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma}&=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})\;\,\,\;\,\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{u}&=\boldsymbol{0}\,\;\;\;\;\,\quad\text{on}\;\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

where

\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2\mu}\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\frac{\lambda}{2\mu(2\mu+d\lambda)}\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\boldsymbol{I}=\frac{1}{2\mu}\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}+\frac{1}{d(2\mu+d\lambda)}\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\boldsymbol{I},

\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
denotes the trace of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
, and

\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}:=\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\frac{1}{d}\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\boldsymbol{I}.

(4)

The mixed variational formulation (
1
) is derived from the Hellinger-Reissner mixed variational principle.

Lemma 3
.

Let

satisfies Assumption

if

Proof.

Take three edges of K and denote them as

Lemma 4
.

Let
K
be a 3D polyhedron. Then
K
satisfies Assumption
2
.

Proof.

For any polyhedron that is homeomorphic to a sphere, equivalently with Euler characteristic
2
, the sum of the angle defects over all vertices equals

, as given by Descartes’ theorem or, more generally, the Gauss–Bonnet theorem. Since this total defect is positive, there must exist at least one vertex with a positive angle defect. By definition of a polyhedron, each vertex is the intersection of at least three faces, and each face contributes an incident edge, so at least three edges also meet at that vertex. Therefore, any spherical polyhedron necessarily has a vertex where at least three faces and three edges meet and where the angle defect is positive.

Let
\texttt{v}_{0}
be such a vertex of
K
where

faces meet. Let

be the set of edges incident to

If

, the six vectors in

\mathbb{S}

[
25
]

For

, let

\hat{\mathbb{S}}=\textrm{span}\{\boldsymbol{e}_{1}\otimes\boldsymbol{e}_{1},\boldsymbol{e}_{2}\otimes\boldsymbol{e}_{2},\boldsymbol{e}_{3}\otimes\boldsymbol{e}_{3},\operatorname{sym}(\boldsymbol{e}_{1}\otimes\boldsymbol{e}_{2}),\operatorname{sym}(\boldsymbol{e}_{2}\otimes\boldsymbol{e}_{3})\}\subset\cup_{F\in\partial K}\mathscr{T}^{F}(\mathbb{S}).

We have one more edge

\boldsymbol{e}_{4}=c_{1}\boldsymbol{e}_{1}+c_{2}\boldsymbol{e}_{2}+c_{3}\boldsymbol{e}_{3}\quad\textrm{with }c_{1}\neq 0,c_{3}\neq 0;

(14)

otherwise, it will reduce to the case

Next we prove that

By (

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}_{\partial\Omega},&F\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}^{i}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}|_{T_{i}}
.

The approximation space for the displacement
\boldsymbol{u}
consists of vector-valued piecewise polynomials over the primal mesh
\mathcal{K}_{h}
:

U_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):\;\boldsymbol{u}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d}),K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\}=\prod_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Functions in
U_{h}
are polynomials within each polytope
K
but may be discontinuous across
\partial K
. Let
Q_{k+1}
be the
L^{2}
projection onto
U_{h}
. To enforce stability, functions in the stress space
\Sigma_{h}
are required to have continuous normal flux across the primal faces. This global stress space is defined as

\Sigma_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}):\;\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}|_{T}\in\Sigma_{k}(T),\;T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\,[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]|_{F}=0,\;F\in\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{K}\}.

2.6
Finite element spaces of the lowest order

In the remainder of this section, we extend the approximation spaces to the lowest-order (
k=0
) case. To ensure the well-posedness of the corresponding numerical scheme, we incorporate the space of rigid motions.

The kernel of the strain operator
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
on an element
K
is the rigid motion space
[
32
]
:

{\rm RM}(K)=\mathbb{P}_{0}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d})\oplus\mathbb{P}_{0}(K;\mathbb{K})\boldsymbol{x},

where
\boldsymbol{x}
represents the position vector. For any face
F\in\partial K
, the polynomial space
\mathbb{P}_{k}(F)
can be constructed using the following basis:

\mathbb{P}_{k}(F)=\textrm{span}\big\{(\boldsymbol{t}_{1}^{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{x})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\boldsymbol{t}_{d-1}^{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{x})^{\alpha_{d-1}}:\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}\leq k,\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\big\}.

Based on this basis, we naturally extend the domain of polynomials in
\mathbb{P}_{k}(F)
from
F
to
K
(or to a sub-element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(K)
sharing
F
) by assuming the function is constant along lines perpendicular to
F
.

The rigid motion space on face
F
is defined as:

{\rm RM}(F):=\mathbb{P}_{0}(F;\mathscr{T}^{F})\oplus\mathbb{P}_{0}(F;\mathscr{T}^{F}(\mathbb{K}))\boldsymbol{x}.

It follows that
{\rm RM}(F)=\Pi_{F}{\rm RM}(K)

[
30
, Lemma 11]
, which allows us to interpret
{\rm RM}(F)
as being defined on either the face
F
or the polytope
K
.

Lemma 6
.

For any face
F\in\partial K
, we have the following decomposition:

{\rm RM}(K)|_{F}=\mathbb{P}_{1}(F)\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\oplus{\rm RM}(F).

(17)

Proof.

Given that
{\rm RM}(F)=\Pi_{F}{\rm RM}(K)
, the inclusion
{\rm RM}(K)|_{F}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{1}(F)\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\oplus{\rm RM}(F)
is immediate. To prove the reverse inclusion, first note that

\mathbb{P}_{0}(F)\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\oplus{\rm RM}(F)\subseteq{\rm RM}(K)|_{F}.

For
i=1,\dots,d-1
, since
\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}
is constant on
F
, we have

(\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{n}_{F}=\big((\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\otimes\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{F}-\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{F}\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_{F})\boldsymbol{x}+(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{F}\big)\big|_{F}\in{\rm RM}(K)|_{F}.

Hence, the inclusion
\mathbb{P}_{1}(F)\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\oplus{\rm RM}(F)\subseteq{\rm RM}(K)|_{F}
holds, which completes the proof.

For
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the lowest-order local stress space is defined as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}(T)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sym}(V_{0}(F)\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_{F})\oplus\mathbb{P}_{0}(T;\mathscr{T}^{F}(\mathbb{S})),

where
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}\cap\partial T
and

V_{0}(F):={\rm RM}(K)|_{F}=\mathbb{P}_{1}(F)\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\oplus{\rm RM}(F).

We have

\dim\Sigma_{0}(T)=d^{2},\quad\dim V_{0}(F)=\frac{1}{2}d(d+1).

The trace of
\Sigma_{0}(T)
onto the face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}\cap\partial T
is then given by

\operatorname{tr}_{F}(\Sigma_{0}(T))=V_{0}(F).

The space
\Sigma_{0}(T)
is an enrichment of
\mathbb{P}_{0}(T;\mathbb{S})
, designed so that the trace operator defines a semi-norm. Together with the strain operator, this results in a norm that satisfies the piecewise Korn’s inequality (
20
).
The enrichment
\Sigma_{0}(T)
is divergence free.
This property is inherited from the
{\rm RM}(K)
space, due to its construction via an extension of the trace
{\rm RM}(K)|_{F}
.

Lemma 7
.

For
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we have

\operatorname{div}\Sigma_{0}(T)=\{0\}.

(18)

Proof.

It suffices to demonstrate that

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\big(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_{F}(\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{x})\big)

\displaystyle=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,d-1,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\operatorname{sym}\big(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\otimes(\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{x})\big)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathscr{T}^{F}(\mathbb{K}).

Using the identity
(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
, we obtain

\operatorname{div}\big(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_{F}(\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{x})\big)=\boldsymbol{n}_{F}(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla)(\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{n}_{F}(\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F})=0.

Furthermore, since
\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{x}\in{\rm RM}(T)\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{1}(T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\ker(\operatorname{div})
, it follows that

\operatorname{div}\operatorname{sym}\big(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\otimes(\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{x})\big)=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\cdot\nabla)(\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}_{F}=0.

This completes the proof.

For
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, we define:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{h}^{-1}(K)

\displaystyle=\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\oplus$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{T_{i}}\Sigma_{0}(T_{i}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{0}(\operatorname{div},K;\mathbb{S})

\displaystyle=\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}(K):\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}|_{\partial K}=\boldsymbol{0}\}.

Following the logic of Lemmas
1
and
5
, we state the following results.

Lemma 8
.

For
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
and
k=0
, the geometric decompositions of
\Sigma_{h}^{-1}(K)
and
\mathbb{B}_{0}(\operatorname{div},K;\mathbb{S})
are

\displaystyle\Sigma_{h}^{-1}(K)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{B}_{0}(\operatorname{div},K;\mathbb{S})\oplus\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\oplus$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{T_{i}}\operatorname{sym}(V_{0}(F_{i})\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_{i}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{0}(\operatorname{div},K;\mathbb{S})

\displaystyle=\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\oplus$}}}_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{T_{i}}\mathbb{P}_{0}(T_{i};\mathscr{T}^{F_{i}}(\mathbb{S})).

The corresponding trace space is

\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{h}^{-1}(K))=\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{0}(F_{i}).

Lemma 9
.

For
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
satisfying Assumption
2
. The space
\Sigma_{h}^{-1}(K)
for
k=0
is uniquely determined by the following DoFs:

\displaystyle\int_{F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}\,{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{q}\in V_{0}(F),F\in\partial K,

(19a)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\boldsymbol{\tau}\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{B}_{0}(\operatorname{div},K;\mathbb{S})\setminus\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\oplus$}}}_{\ell=1}^{d(d+1)/2}\left(\chi_{T_{\boldsymbol{e}_{\ell}}}\mathbb{P}_{0}(T_{\boldsymbol{e}_{\ell}})b_{\ell}\right),

(19b)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\boldsymbol{\tau}\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K;\mathbb{S}),

(19c)

where
T_{\boldsymbol{e}_{\ell}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(K)
is a sub-element containing the edge vector
\boldsymbol{e}_{\ell}
chosen in (
13
).

Based on the decomposition (
17
), the DoFs in (
19a
) are equivalent to

\int_{F}\boldsymbol{n}^{\intercal}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}\,q\,{\rm d}s,\quad q\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(F);\qquad\int_{F}\Pi_{F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}\,{\rm d}s,\quad\boldsymbol{q}\in{\rm RM}(F).

We extend the global approximation spaces
U_{h}
and
\Sigma_{h}
to
k=0
:

\displaystyle U_{h}

\displaystyle=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):\;\boldsymbol{u}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d}),K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\}=\prod_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d}),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{h}

\displaystyle=\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}):\;\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}|_{T}\in\Sigma_{0}(T),\;T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\,[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]|_{F}=0,\;F\in\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{K}\}.

2.7
Norm equivalence

For
k\geq 0
and face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
, let
\Pi_{k}^{F}
be the
L^{2}
-projection operator onto the space
V_{k}(F)
, defined as:

\Pi_{k}^{F}:\begin{cases}L^{2}(F;\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow{\rm RM}(K)|_{F},&\textrm{if }k=0,\\
L^{2}(F;\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}(F;\mathbb{R}^{d}),&\textrm{if }k\geq 1.\end{cases}

We introduce the piecewise
H^{1}
spaces
H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{d}):=H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h};\mathbb{S}):=H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\otimes\mathbb{S}
, where

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in H^{1}(K)\text{ for each }K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\},

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):v|_{T}\in H^{1}(T)\text{ for each }T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

The spaces
H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h};\mathbb{S})
are equipped with the following norms, respectively:

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{1,h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}^{-1}\|\Pi_{k}^{F}[\boldsymbol{v}]\|_{F}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\tau}\|^{2}_{0,h}

\displaystyle=\|\boldsymbol{\tau}\|^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}\|\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}\|^{2}_{F}.

Lemma 10
(Korn’s inequality for piecewise
H^{1}
vector functions).

The following discrete Korn’s inequality holds for any
\boldsymbol{v}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{1,h}\eqsim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}^{-1}\|\Pi_{0}^{F}[\boldsymbol{v}]\|_{F}^{2}\eqsim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}^{-1}\|[\boldsymbol{v}]\|_{F}^{2}.

(20)

Proof.

By adapting the proof of Lemma 3.2 in
[
12
]
and utilizing the decomposition in (
17
), we obtain:

\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{1,h}\eqsim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}^{-1}\|\Pi_{0}^{F}[\boldsymbol{v}]\|_{F}^{2}.

The full equivalence in (
20
) then follows directly from the established Korn’s inequalities for piecewise
H^{1}
vector functions presented in
[
8
, (1.22)]
and
[
3
, (34)]
.

3
The Staggered Discontinuous Galerkin Scheme

This section presents the SDG numerical scheme for the Hellinger-Reissner mixed variational formulation of linear elasticity problems. The well-posedness is established by proving continuity, coercivity, and the inf-sup condition.

3.1
Variational formulation

We propose the primal SDG method based on the first-order system (
3
) as follows: for
k\geq 0
, find
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in U_{h}
such that

\displaystyle a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}(\boldsymbol{f},\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})_{T}\;\;\forall\,\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h},

(21a)

\displaystyle b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle=-(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\,\forall\,\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in U_{h},

(21b)

where the bilinear forms are

\displaystyle a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})_{T}+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{T}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}\langle[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}],[\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\rangle_{F},

\displaystyle b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle:=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}))_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}]\rangle_{F}.

The Dirichlet boundary condition
\boldsymbol{u}|_{\partial\Omega}=\boldsymbol{0}
is imposed weakly in the second term of
b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})
by
\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}
.
For
k=0
, by (
18
), the right hand side of (
21a
) vanishes, and the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
can be simplified to

a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}):=(\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{T}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}\langle[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}],[\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\rangle_{F}.

From the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality and the inverse inequality, we get the following lemma.

Lemma 11
.

The bilinear forms
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
are continuous, i.e.

\displaystyle a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,h}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}+H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}),

\displaystyle b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}+H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}),\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in U_{h}+H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

3.2
The well-posedness

We first verify the inf-sup condition of
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
. The modification of the interior DoFs for
\Sigma_{k}
is to impose this condition.

Lemma 12
.

We have the discrete inf-sup condition

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\lesssim\sup_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}}\frac{b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}{\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in U_{h}.

(22)

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}
satisfy

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{q}\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle=\langle h_{F}^{-1}\Pi_{k}^{F}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}],\boldsymbol{q}\rangle_{F}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{q}\in V_{k}(F),F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K},

(23)

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau})_{K}

\displaystyle=-(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{\tau})_{K}\quad\quad\;\forall~\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K;\mathbb{S}),K\in\mathcal{K}_{h},

(24)

and DoFs (
16b
) vanish. Using the scaling argument, we have

\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}.

Based on (
23
)-(
24
) and the definitions of
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\|\cdot\|_{1,h}
, we get

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}^{-1}\|\Pi_{k}[\boldsymbol{u}_{h}]\|_{F}^{2}=b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}).

Therefore, the discrete inf-sup condition (
22
) holds.

Next we show that the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is uniformly coercive on the kernel space

Z_{h}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}:\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega),\textrm{ and }b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=0\textrm{ for all }\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in U_{h}\right\}.

In Theorem
15
, we will prove that
\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. Therefore, we include the condition
\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
in the definition of
Z_{h}
.

Lemma 13
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in Z_{h}
, we have

\|\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\|^{2}\lesssim\|\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}\|\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{T}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{T}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}\|[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\|_{F}^{2},

(25)

where the constant is independent of the mesh size
h
and Lamé constants.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, we have a
\boldsymbol{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

-\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}=\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1}\lesssim\|\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\|.

(26)

Let
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=Q_{k+1}\boldsymbol{v}\in U_{h}
.
Noting that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in Z_{h}
, we obtain from the integration by parts and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\|\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\|^{2}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{d}(\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}),\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v})=-\frac{1}{d}(\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\boldsymbol{I},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}))=(\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}))

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}))+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v})=(\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}))+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}))+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})_{T}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{T}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}\langle[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}],\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{T}\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{T}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{T}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}\|[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\|_{F}\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{F}.

This combined with the error estimate of
Q_{k+1}
and (
26
) yields (
25
).

This lemma shows that the trace of the stress can be controlled by the deviatoric part together with appropriate stabilization terms. The subsequent corollary will demonstrate that the coercivity of the numerical scheme is independent of the Lamé constants, implying the robustness of the proposed scheme.

Corollary 14
.

The following coercivity condition holds,

\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}^{2}\lesssim a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in Z_{h},

(27)

with a constant independent of the mesh size
h
and the Lamé constants.

Proof.

After a simple rearrangement, we get

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})=\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{d(2\mu+d\lambda)}\|\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\|^{2}.

(28)

From the conclusion given in Lemma
13
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{d}\|\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{dev}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}\|\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{T}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{T}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}\|[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}).

Then (
27
) holds from the trace inequality and inverse inequality.

As shown by (
28
), the coercivity of the bilinear form
(\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})
is lost in the limit
\lambda\to 0
. Hence, to achieve a stable numerical scheme, a stabilization term

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})_{T}+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{T}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}\langle[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}],[\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\rangle_{F}

is added to the bilinear form
a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})
. We stress that this stabilizer is specifically designed to prevent numerical locking, and not to handle any continuity issues arising from nonconforming elements. This differs from virtual element methods
[
7
]
and weak Galerkin finite element methods
[
37
]
, where the purpose of introducing a stabilizer is to obtain a stable bilinear form, and therefore the definition of their stabilizer depends on the choice of the approximation function space.

Theorem 15
.

The SDG method (
21
) is well-posed and stable. There exists a unique solution pair
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\in\Sigma_{h}\times U_{h}
satisfying
\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
and

\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{f}\|.

Proof.

We first show the well-posedness. Assume
\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{0}
.
Testing (
21a
) with
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\boldsymbol{I}
gives
\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.
From (
21b
) we have
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in Z_{h}
.
Taking
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
in (
21a
) yields

a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})=0,

and the discrete coercivity (
27
) then implies
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
.
With this, (
21a
) reduces to

b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})=0\qquad\forall~\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}.

By the discrete inf-sup condition (
22
), we conclude
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
.
Therefore, the SDG method (
21
) is well-posed.

For a general
\boldsymbol{f}
, testing (
21a
) with
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\boldsymbol{I}
again shows
\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.
The stability estimate follows by combining the discrete inf-sup condition (
22
) with the discrete coercivity (
27
).

4
Hybridization

In this section, we will present the hybridization of the SDG method (
21
). If we eliminate the stress variable, we obtain a primal formulation.

4.1
Spaces and weak differential operators

Let

M_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{b}\}:\;\boldsymbol{u}_{0}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\boldsymbol{u}_{b}|_{F}\in V_{k}(F),\;K\in\mathcal{K}_{h},\;F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}\},

and

\Sigma^{-1}_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}):\;\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}|_{T}\in\Sigma_{k}(T),T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

Let
M_{h}^{0}
be a subspace of
M_{h}
with vanishing boundary values on
\partial\Omega
, that is

M_{h}^{0}=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{b}\}\in M_{h}:\;\boldsymbol{u}_{b}|_{F}=\boldsymbol{0},F\in\partial\Omega\}.

Define a weak symmetric gradient operator

\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w}:M_{h}\rightarrow\Sigma^{-1}_{h}

as follows: for
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in M_{h}
,
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})|_{K}=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w,K}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})
for each
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, where

(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w,K}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{\tau})_{K}=(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0}),\boldsymbol{\tau})_{K}+\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{b}-\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}\rangle_{\partial K}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}(K).

Unlike the weak Galerkin method
[
37
]
, the local space to compute
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})
is enriched from a single polynomial space
\mathbb{P}_{k}(K;\mathbb{S})
to the discontinuous polynomial space
\Sigma^{-1}_{h}(K)
. This enrichment eliminates the stabilization.

Define the weak divergence operator

\operatorname{div}_{w}:\Sigma^{-1}_{h}\rightarrow M_{h}

by

\operatorname{div}_{w}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}=\{\operatorname{div}_{w,K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},-h_{F}^{-1}[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\}_{\mathcal{K}_{h},\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}},

where the local weak divergence
\operatorname{div}_{w,K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as

(\operatorname{div}_{w,K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{0})_{K}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(K)}(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{0})_{T}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{T}(K)\setminus\partial K}\langle[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}],\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\rangle_{F},

for any
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For
\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in M_{h}
, introduce a discrete
L^{2}
inner product

(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})_{0,h}=(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{v}_{0})+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{b},\boldsymbol{v}_{b}\rangle_{F},

and the discrete
L^{2}
norm

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,h}^{2}=(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})_{0,h}.

Lemma 16
(Adjoint Property of Weak Differential Operators).

Let
M_{h}
and
\Sigma_{h}^{-1}
be the discrete spaces for the displacement and stress fields, respectively. For any
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{b}\}\in M_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}
, the weak strain operator
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w}
and the weak divergence operator
\operatorname{div}_{w}
satisfy the following adjoint relation:

(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})=-(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\operatorname{div}_{w}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})_{0,h}.

(29)

Proof.

By applying the definition of the weak strain operator
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w,K}
on each element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
and using integration by parts, we have:

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w,K}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})_{K}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\left((\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0}),\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})_{K}+\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{b}-\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}\rangle_{\partial K}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})_{T}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{T}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{h}}\langle[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}],\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\rangle_{F}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{b},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}\rangle_{\partial K}.

By the definitions of
\operatorname{div}_{w,K}
and
\operatorname{div}_{w}
,

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{w}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\operatorname{div}_{w,K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{0})_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{b},[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\rangle_{F}=-(\operatorname{div}_{w}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})_{0,h}.

This completes the proof that the weak gradient (strain) and weak divergence are adjoint operators in the discrete setting.

4.2
The hybridized formulation

The hybridized formulation of the SDG method (
21
): for
k\geq 0
, find
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0}
such that

\displaystyle a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}(\boldsymbol{f},\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})_{T}\;\;\forall~\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1},

(30a)

\displaystyle b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle=-(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{0})\;\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall~\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0},

(30b)

where

\displaystyle a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})_{T}+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{T}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}\langle[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}],[\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\rangle_{F},

\displaystyle b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle:=(\operatorname{div}_{w}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})_{0,h}=\!-\!\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0}))_{K}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{u}_{0}-\boldsymbol{u}_{b}\rangle_{\partial K}.

We respectively equip spaces
M_{h}
and
\Sigma_{h}^{-1}
with semi-norms

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}\|\Pi_{k}^{F}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0}-\boldsymbol{u}_{b})\|_{\partial K}^{2},\qquad\qquad\;\;\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in M_{h},

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}h_{K}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}\|^{2}_{\partial K}+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{T}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{K}}h_{F}\|[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}]\|_{F}^{2},\qquad\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}.

It is easy to prove that

\|\cdot\|_{1,h}
is a norm on space
M_{h}^{0}

and
\|\cdot\|_{0,h}
is a norm on space
\Sigma_{h}^{-1}
with
k\geq 0
.
We have the continuity

\displaystyle a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,h}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1},

\displaystyle b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0}.

Lemma 17
.

We have the discrete inf-sup condition

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\lesssim\sup_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}}\frac{b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}{\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0}.

(31)

Proof.

For any given
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0}
, let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma^{-1}_{h}
satisfy

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{q}\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle=\langle h_{K}^{-1}\Pi_{k}^{F}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0}-\boldsymbol{u}_{b}),\boldsymbol{q}\rangle_{F}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{q}\in V_{k}(F),F\in\partial K,

(32)

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau})_{K}

\displaystyle=-(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0}),\boldsymbol{\tau})_{K}\qquad\qquad\;\;\,\forall~\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K;\mathbb{S}),

(33)

and DoFs (
16b
) vanish on each
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
. Using the scaling argument, we have

\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}.

Based on (
32
)-(
33
) and the definitions of
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\|\cdot\|_{1,h}
, we get

b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}\|\Pi_{k}^{F}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0}-\boldsymbol{u}_{b})\|_{\partial K}^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}.

Therefore, the discrete inf-sup condition (
31
) follows.

Lemma 18
.

We have the discrete coercivity

\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}^{2}\lesssim a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in Z_{h},

(34)

where

Z_{h}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}:\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega),\textrm{ and }b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=0\textrm{ for all }\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0}\right\}.

Proof.

By choosing
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\{0,\boldsymbol{v}_{b}\}\in M_{h}^{0}
in the definition of
Z_{h}
, we find that
Z_{h}\subseteq\Sigma_{h}
.
Thus, we end the proof by applying the discrete coercivity (
27
).

Theorem 19
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{b}\}\in M_{h}^{0}
be the solution of the hybridized formulation (
30
).
The hybridized SDG method (
30
) is well-posed and stable.
The following stability estimate holds:

\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\lesssim\sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0}}\frac{a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}{\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{0,h}+\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,h}}.

(35)

Then
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}
, and the pair
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{0})
is exactly the solution of the SDG method (
21
).

Proof.

By the same argument as in Theorem
15
, the hybridized SDG method (
30
) is well-posed.
The stability estimate (
35
) follows directly from the discrete inf-sup condition (
31
) and the discrete coercivity (
34
).

To show that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}
, take
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{v}_{b}\}
in (
30b
) to obtain

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{v}_{b}\rangle_{\partial K}=0\qquad\forall\,\boldsymbol{v}_{b}\in\prod_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{K}}V_{k}(F).

This is precisely the condition ensuring
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}
.

Finally, taking
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{v}_{0},\boldsymbol{0}\}
in (
30
) eliminates the facet term and reduces the hybridized formulation to the SDG method (
21
).
Thus
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{0})
is exactly the SDG solution.

4.3
Error estimates

In order to conduct error analysis, we introduce nodal interpolations.
Let

\boldsymbol{Q}_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{Q}_{k+1},\Pi_{k}^{F}\}
be the
L^{2}-
projection operator from
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
onto spaces
M_{h}
.
For
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})
, let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\in\Sigma_{h}
be the nodal interpolation based on DoFs (
16
) and (
19
).
We have

\displaystyle b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I},\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I})\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{v}_{0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{b}\rangle_{\partial K}\quad\forall~\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}),\boldsymbol{v}\in M_{h}^{0},

(36)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}\|_{0,h}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{k+1}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{k+1}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\forall~\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in H^{k+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}).

(37)

Theorem 20
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in H^{k+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})
and
\boldsymbol{u}\in H^{k+2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the solution of the linear elasticity (
3
), and let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0}
be the solution of the hybridized method (
30
). We have the following error estimate

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}+\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}\lesssim h^{k+1}(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{k+1}+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{k+2}).\end{array}

(38)

Proof.

From the continuous problem (
1
), for any
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0}
we have

\displaystyle a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{Q}_{h}\boldsymbol{u})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}(\boldsymbol{f},\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})_{T}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{Q}_{k+1}\boldsymbol{u}))_{K}

\displaystyle\quad\;-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{Q}_{k+1}\boldsymbol{u})_{\partial K},

\displaystyle b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=-(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{0}).

Subtracting the discrete equations (
30a
)-(
30b
) from these yields the error system

\displaystyle a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{Q}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{Q}_{k+1}\boldsymbol{u}))_{K}

(39a)

\displaystyle\quad\;-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{Q}_{k+1}\boldsymbol{u})_{\partial K},

\displaystyle b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}-\boldsymbol{\sigma})\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{v}_{0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{b}\rangle_{\partial K}

(39b)

for any
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}^{-1}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in M_{h}^{0}
. We have used (
36
) for (
39b
).

The sum of (
39a
) and (
39b
) gives

\displaystyle\quad a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{Q}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h})+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}-\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{Q}_{k+1}\boldsymbol{u}))_{K}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{Q}_{k+1}\boldsymbol{u})_{\partial K}

\displaystyle\quad\;+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}-\boldsymbol{\sigma})\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{v}_{0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{b}\rangle_{\partial K}.

Applying the stability estimate (
35
), the approximation property (
37
), and the standard estimate for the projection
Q_{k+1}
gives

\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,h}+\|\boldsymbol{Q}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\lesssim h^{k+1}(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{k+1}+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{k+2}).

Finally, the triangle inequality and the approximation properties of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{I}
and
\boldsymbol{Q}_{h}
yield (
38
).

Next, we consider the
L^{2}
-error estimate for
\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\|
. Consider the dual problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\operatorname{div}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}&=\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\quad\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\mathcal{A}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}&=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})\quad\quad\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}&=\boldsymbol{0}\qquad\quad\;\;\text{on}\;\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(40)

Assume the dual problem (
40
) satisfies the regularity estimate

\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\|_{1}+\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{2}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\|.

(41)

The proof is standard and thus skipped here.

Conclusion

In this paper, we develop and analyze a SDG method for linear elasticity problems. The formulation is derived from the Hellinger-Reissner variational principle, which enables the simultaneous approximation of both the stress tensor and the displacement field. One of the central contribution of this work is the construction of strongly symmetric stress tensors that enforce normal continuity across element boundaries on general polytopal meshes. The careful design of the DoFs for the stress approximation plays a crucial role in achieving this structure.

To achieve this structure, the stress tensor is represented as a piecewise discontinuous polynomial within each polygonal element. The DoFs for the stress are defined without any vertex-based unknowns, which significantly simplifies the implementation after using the hybridization techniques. The use of hybridization not only reduces programming complexity but also, when combined with a Schur complement approach, leads to a final stiffness matrix that involves only DoFs associated with element boundaries. This can substantially decrease the computational cost.
