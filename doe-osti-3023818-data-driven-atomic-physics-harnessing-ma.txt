  Data-Driven Atomic Physics: Harnessing Machine Learning and High-Repetition-Rate
 Experiments for Laser-driven HED

 Derek A. Mariscal

 LDRD Project Tracking Number: 23-ERD-035

Project Overview

High-energy-density plasma experiments are central to progress in atomic physics, fusion
energy, and national security science, but they have traditionally been constrained by slow data
collection and manual, time-intensive analysis. This project targeted that bottleneck by enabling
high-repetition-rate experiments to produce and interpret much larger volumes of data quickly
enough to guide experiments while they run.

We developed a suite of machine learning tools and experimental frameworks that connect rapid
diagnostics, multi-physics modeling, and automated decision-making. Key outcomes include
machine learning analysis toolkits for spectroscopy, surrogate models for atomic physics
calculations that deliver approximately 10,000 times faster analysis than conventional
approaches, and autonomous control methods that steer experiments based on real-time
diagnostic outputs. Together, these advances increased data throughput by more than a factor of
10 and demonstrated the feasibility of autonomous, data-driven laser experiments. The result is a
foundation for faster learning cycles, improved model validation, and more efficient exploration
of experimental conditions in laser-driven high-energy-density science.

Mission Impact

This work strengthens Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory capabilities in high-
performance computing, simulation, and data science by integrating machine learning with
established physics models and experimental platforms. Faster, more accurate interpretation of
high-repetition-rate diagnostic data and improved computational efficiency support stockpile
stewardship by enhancing prediction and validation of material behavior under extreme
conditions. The demonstrated autonomous experimentation and machine learning surrogate
modeling accelerate discovery, expand the pace and scale of high-energy-density research
relevant to fusion energy, and provide new pathways for rapid model validation supporting
nuclear deterrence, nonproliferation, and emergency response missions across Department of
Energy and National Nuclear Security Administration priorities.
