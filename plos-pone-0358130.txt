Integrative transcriptomics identifies an 18-gene candidate blood-accessible biomarker panel for longitudinal monitoring of spaceflight-induced muscle atrophy

Jae Ho Shim, Ji Yeon Lee, Jihye Han, Hyeon Soo Kim

ABSTRACT
Skeletal muscle atrophy is a critical physiological risk during long-duration spaceflight, yet candidate blood-accessible transcriptomic biomarkers for longitudinal monitoring remain unavailable. We developed an integrative multi-mission framework combining a primary discovery transcriptome from THP-1 monocytic cells exposed to International Space Station (ISS) microgravity (GEO: Sci. Adv. 2026 (https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adw7832, Data S1-S10); NASA OSDR: Al-Ahmadi et al. 2026; n = 8 spaceflight, 8 ground control) — yielding 4,522 spaceflight-differentially expressed genes (SDEGs) — with a PRISMA-compliant meta-analysis of 46 verified spaceflight studies, producing a 726-gene consensus set. Cross-referencing these datasets identified 103 intersection genes; application of our Integrated Concordance Score (ICS) nominated an 18-gene candidate biomarker panel nominated across six external missions. Orthogonal cross-tissue concordance analysis using six C2C12 myotube simulated-microgravity datasets demonstrated Pearson r = 0.79 (95% CI 0.52–0.92, p < 0.001), with a conserved stress module achieving r = 0.88; complementary concordance in human primary skeletal myotubes confirmed tri-model directional concordance for 13 stress-module genes. Gold-tier biomarkers (TNNT3, TRIM63, ACTC1, IGF2; ICS ≥ 8.0) showed ≥83% multi-mission concordance. Temporal modelling informs a preliminary two-tier fortnightly/monthly monitoring framework aligned with measured saturation kinetics, and the prioritised panel converges on coordinated ubiquitin-proteasome activation, AKT–mTOR–FOXO3a remodelling, E2F suppression, and sarcomeric reprogramming. Integrated drug–gene analysis identified bimagrumab combined with leucine as the top predicted prioritised countermeasure pair (computational Bliss = 2.8). These findings establish a proposed candidate blood-accessible biomarker framework for periodic muscle health surveillance during long-duration human spaceflight, available prospective validation in astronaut blood samples. The unit of analysis was the analytical contrast (spaceflight vs. matched ground control), not the publication. Cross-platform harmonization was performed via gene-level mapping and rank-based normalization.

Introduction Prolonged microgravity induces progressive skeletal muscle atrophy, with reductions of 10–20% in muscle mass documented across ISS missions [ 1 – 3 ]. This loss accompanies slow-to-fast fibre-type remodelling, reduced oxidative capacity, and impaired post-flight recovery [ 4 – 7 ]. Resistive exercise countermeasures partially mitigate but do not fully prevent atrophy in all crew members [ 8 ]. A critical operational gap is the absence of candidate blood-accessible molecular biomarkers for periodic longitudinal muscle health surveillance during flight. Existing approaches rely on ground-based imaging and dynamometry — operationally constrained and infrequent within ISS schedules [ 9 , 10 ]. Transcriptomic biomarkers from peripheral blood samples offer a practical alternative compatible with established RNA stabilisation protocols and ISS sample-return logistics [ 11 ]. Monocytic cells occupy the skeletal muscle microenvironment as muscle-resident macrophages and participate in atrophy-associated paracrine signalling, sharing conserved stress-response architecture — ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS), mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), Forkhead box O (FOXO), and nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) — with myofibres [ 12 – 14 ]. THP-1 human monocytic cells (ATCC TIB-202) are therefore well-suited as an ISS discovery platform, given their suspension-culture compatibility, cryopreservation tolerance (RNA integrity number [RIN] ≥ 8.5 post-ISS return), and full stress-pathway gene expression ( S1 File , S1 Text). We refer to ISS-induced THP-1 differentially expressed genes as spaceflight-differentially expressed genes (SDEGs) throughout the manuscript [ 15 ]. Here we introduce the Integrated Concordance Score (ICS), a composite metric combining discovery-level evidence with cross-study consistency, and validate an 18-gene panel through cross-tissue concordance analysis in C2C12 myotubes [ 16 ], orthogonal confirmation in human primary skeletal myotubes, multi-mission concordance across six independent missions, and temporal modelling to inform a preliminary monitoring framework. Materials and methods Cell culture and ISS experiment THP-1 cells (ATCC TIB-202) were maintained in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated foetal bovine serum, 2 mM L-glutamine, and 0.05 mM 2-mercaptoethanol at 37 °C in 5% CO 2 . ISS culture comprised 14 days of microgravity exposure in NASA Biological Research in Canisters (BRIC) hardware aboard the ISS; RNA was stabilised in RNAlater immediately post-culture, and simultaneous ground-control cultures were maintained at NASA Johnson Space Center (JSC). No human-subject or animal experiments were performed; the study was exempt under JSC-IRB-2019–0847 (commercially available cell line). RNA sequencing and differential expression analysis TruSeq Stranded mRNA libraries (Illumina) were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (150 bp paired-end; ≥ 30 M reads per sample). Reads were aligned with STAR v2.7.10a to GRCh38.p13/GENCODE v38; gene-level quantification used featureCounts v2.0.1; differential expression analysis used DESeq2 v1.34.0 with apeglm log2 fold change (Log2FC) shrinkage, applying thresholds of false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and |Log2FC| > 0.5 [ 17 ]. The sample size (n = 8 biological replicates per group) exceeds the minimum recommended for DESeq2-based differential expression at these thresholds. Raw data were deposited at GEO (Sci. Adv. 2026 ( https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adw7832 , Data S1-S10)) and NASA OSDR (Al-Ahmadi et al. 2026). ICS meta-analysis A PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review was conducted ( S1 File , Checklist 1) and identified 46 PubMed-verified empirical transcriptomics studies (S13 Table ( S1 Table ), verified registry) published between 2000 and 2024. Random-effects models with REML estimation were fitted using metafor v3.8.1. The Integrated Concordance Score was defined as ICS = 0.6 × S4522 + 0.4 × S726, where S4522 = 0.6 × Log2FC_norm + 0.4 × (−log10FDR_norm) and S726 = 0.5 × N_studies,norm + 0.25 × DisGeNET_norm + 0.25 × Category_norm. All sub-scores were min–max normalised to [0,10] across 103 intersection genes. Tier thresholds were Gold (ICS ≥ 8.0), High (7.0–7.9), and Moderate (6.0–6.9). Sensitivity analysis used four alternative weighting configurations (A–D); bootstrap validation used n = 10,000 BCa iterations; permutation testing used n = 1,000 shuffled gene–disease annotation datasets. C2C12 and human primary myotube cross-tissue concordance Cross-platform and cross-cohort validation refined the spaceflight-associated muscle signature from the initial THP-1 discovery set to independent murine, astronaut, and orthogonal muscle-cell validation systems ( Fig 3 ). Differential-expression and recurrence filtering retained a core candidate set carried forward into the ICS framework, with directional concordance preserved for representative upregulated contractile and calcium-handling genes (TNNT3, TNNI3, MYH6, MYH7, CASQ2, RYR1) and downregulated cytoskeletal, growth-signalling, and cell-cycle markers (TPM2, IGF2, PCNA, MCM2) across independent in vivo and astronaut-derived datasets. Temporal trajectories indicated progressive change during spaceflight or unloading rather than transient single-time-point shifts, supporting longitudinal monitoring applications. Gene-set enrichment summarised coordinated activation of muscle remodelling, proteostasis, and stress-response programmes alongside repression of proliferative and DNA-repair modules, and a cross-dataset correlation matrix confirmed directional consistency despite species, platform, and mission heterogeneity. Orthogonal validation in differentiated C2C12 myotubes and human primary skeletal myotubes under simulated microgravity recovered induction of TNNI3, MYH6, MYH7, CASQ2, and RYR1 with concomitant suppression of TPM2 and proliferation-associated transcripts (S-M1–S-M4), establishing that the muscle-remodelling programme is reproducible in bona fide muscle-cell systems beyond the hematopoietic discovery context ( Fig 3 ). The three independent biological donors served as the experimental units (n = 3) for all statistical analyses. Temporal modelling Temporal modelling was performed in R using the nls package (v4.2.1) with four candidate model types: linear, exponential saturation [y = A(1 − e^(−t/τ)) + b], sigmoid [y = L/(1 + e^(−k(t − t₀))) + b], and biphasic. Model selection was guided by Akaike Information Criterion (AIC). Data sources were the NASA Twins Study (dbGaP phs001453; 6 time points; n = 1 spaceflight astronaut, which is a single-subject limitation) and the JAXA cfRNA dataset (GEO GSE185989; n = 8; 4 time points). Drug–gene interaction and pathway integration Drug–gene interactions were assembled from DGIdb v4.2.0, DrugBank v5.1.9, and CMap L1000. Protein–protein interactions were generated with STRING v11.5 (confidence score ≥ 0.7) with Louvain community detection ( Fig 6 ). Pairwise countermeasure combinations were evaluated using the Bliss independence synergy model. The translational implications of the prioritised panel are presented as hypothesis-generating context ( Fig 8 ) rather than as a proven drug-repurposing claim. Combined microgravity and ionising-radiation exposure To approximate the dual-stressor profile of low Earth orbit, C2C12 myotubes were exposed in a four-arm design to 1 g control, 72-hour simulated microgravity (3D-clinostat), 0.5 Gy proton-beam ionising radiation (IR; 200 MeV protons; approximately 1 Gy/min; delivered as an accelerated experimental exposure approximating, but exceeding, the cumulative ambient dose of a 14-day low Earth orbit mission; IAEA TRS-398 dosimetry), or combined µG + IR, followed by RNA-seq and synergy analysis against an additive model ( S1 File , Text S8; S-M6). Species notation and ethics Human–mouse cross-species comparisons used directional concordance; separate S726 strata were maintained for human and rodent studies; Type IIX (human) versus Type IIB (mouse) fibre-type stratification was applied. Rodent datasets OSD-104 and OSD-379 were used as secondary analyses under their original NASA IACUC protocols; no new animal experiments were conducted in the present study. Human primary skeletal myoblasts were obtained de-identified from commercial vendors; no identifiable human-subject data were generated. JAXA refers to the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency. TPM2-centred cytoskeletal remodelling and fibre-type transition Cytoskeletal remodelling emerged as a reproducible axis of spaceflight-associated muscle adaptation ( Fig 4 ). TPM2 was consistently downregulated across the discovery and validation datasets, behaving in the opposite direction to several contractile markers (TNNT3, MYH isoforms) and marking remodelling rather than global transcriptional shutdown. The revised mechanistic framework depicts bilateral atrophy of both slow- and fast-twitch fibres with a relative proportional shift toward fast-twitch characteristics, rather than selective fast-fibre hypertrophy. Functional enrichment highlighted myofibril organisation, actomyosin structure, contractile fibre, and calcium-ion handling. Comparative species analysis indicated preservation of the structural-remodelling module across mouse and human datasets, while acknowledging known fibre-type composition differences. Orthogonal validation in differentiated C2C12 myotubes and human primary myotubes under simulated microgravity confirmed TPM2 downregulation at transcript and protein levels (qRT-PCR, immunoblotting, immunofluorescence), accompanied by increased MuRF1/FBXO32 signalling, reduced myotube diameter, and preservation of the broader remodelling axis (S-M1, S-M2; donor-level qPCR/WB in S-M4), strengthening the argument that TPM2 reflects a genuine muscle-cell response rather than a discovery-platform artefact ( Fig 4 ). 10.1371/journal.pone.0358130.g004 Fig 4 TPM2-centred cytoskeletal remodelling and fibre-type transition emerge as a reproducible axis of spaceflight-associated muscle adaptation. Three-model cross-tissue concordance: THP-1 ISS, C2C12 µG, and human primary myotube µG To establish that the prioritised biomarker panel generalises beyond the monocytic discovery context, ISS THP-1 SDEGs were compared with simulated-microgravity transcriptomes from differentiated C2C12 myotubes and human primary skeletal myotubes processed through identical DESeq2 pipelines (S-M1–S-M4). Across the 18 Gold/High/Moderate-tier ICS biomarker genes, tri-model Spearman concordance separated a conserved stress module (13 genes: TRIM63, FBXO32, PCNA, MCM2, MCM7, ACTA1, TPM2, FOXO3A, MYH7, EXO1, POLD1, RFC3, CDKN1A; shared by all three models) from a tissue-specific module (TNNI3, MYH6, CASQ2, RYR1, MYH13) that is present in skeletal muscle models but absent from the myeloid (THP-1) transcriptome, consistent with established tissue-restricted gene expression programmes [ 27 – 29 ]. Phenotypically, C2C12 and human primary myotubes under 72-hour simulated microgravity showed concordant reductions in fusion index and myotube diameter (S-M1, S-M2), activation of the AKT–mTOR–FOXO3a–UPS axis with nuclear FOXO3a translocation and increased MuRF1/FBXO32 (S-M2), and enhanced autophagic flux (LC3-II/LC3-I ↑ , p62 ↓ ; S-M2). Oxidative stress, mitochondrial depolarisation, and DNA damage responses were concurrently activated (CellROX, MitoSOX, γH2AX foci, BRCA1/RAD51 modulation; S-M5), and a four-arm exposure to combined simulated microgravity and 0.5 Gy proton-beam ionising radiation produced the highest Pearson correlation with ISS THP-1 SDEGs (S-M6), supporting the dual-stressor nature of the spaceflight environment. The tri-model integrated framework (S-M7) consolidates these results into a high-confidence “muscle microgravity signature” defined by concordant induction of TNNT3, TNNI3, MYH6, MYH7, CASQ2, and TRIM63 together with repression of TPM2, IGF2, PCNA, and MCM2 in authentic muscle-cell systems. Temporal dynamics and two-tier monitoring protocol Temporal modelling was performed on the NASA Twins Study (dbGaP phs001453; 6 time points; n = 1 spaceflight astronaut; kinetic parameters corroborated by JAXA cfRNA n = 8 as described in S1 File , S5 Text) and the JAXA cfRNA dataset (GSE185989; n = 8; 4 time points). Model selection by AIC identified two dominant temporal patterns. Acute-plateau (exponential saturation; TRIM63 and IGF2): TRIM63 reaches 95% maximum at Day 14.1 (τ = 3.2 d) and IGF2 at Day 17.5 (τ = 4.1 d), and fortnightly sampling (every 14 days) captures both peaks. Progressive-accumulation (sigmoid; TNNT3 and TPM2): inflection points t₀ = 65 d and 58 d, respectively, with monthly sampling (t = 0, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180 days) resolving the inflection trajectories. Integrated prioritisation and translational countermeasure context Final evidence integration combined statistical significance, effect magnitude, recurrence across datasets, biologic coherence, and external validation to classify genes into confidence tiers, yielding a high-confidence multi-mission muscle microgravity signature suitable for translational monitoring ( Fig 8 ). After final curation, the Gold-tier comprised TNNT3, TRIM63, ACTC1, and IGF2. Enrichment analysis of the spaceflight-response programme demonstrated significant repression of E2F targets, DNA replication, and cell-cycle progression pathways, with coherent downregulation of representative E2F-regulated genes (PCNA, MCM2) across discovery and validation datasets. IGF2 and TRIM63 showed opposite directional behaviour with complementary interpretive value, supporting their joint use within the integrated model. Discussion This study establishes the first systematically nominated, candidate blood-accessible transcriptomic biomarker panel for spaceflight-induced skeletal muscle atrophy, combining ISS primary discovery in THP-1 monocytic cells (spaceflight-differentially expressed genes; SDEGs) with PRISMA-compliant cross-study meta-analysis, C2C12 and human primary myotube cross-tissue concordance, and multi-mission concordance. Cross-tissue concordance of r = 0.88 for the conserved stress module supports that THP-1 captures the core UPS/mTOR/FOXO atrophy programme [ 33 , 34 ]. The two-module framework distinguishes a universally conserved stress signature (putatively blood-accessible; r = 0.88) from a sarcomeric tissue-specific signal (concordant in rodent muscle and human primary myotubes; r = 0.52). This distinction is biologically principled: monocytes serve as muscle-resident macrophage precursors and participate directly in atrophy paracrine signalling [ 12 , 35 ]. The lower concordance (r = 0.52) for sarcomeric genes reflects expected tissue-specificity differences rather than platform failure; these genes were identified through the S726 meta-analysis and are robustly recovered in authentic muscle-cell systems (S-M3, S-M4, S-M7). TNNT3 upregulation marks a slow-to-fast remodelling-plus-atrophy state, correctly depicted in Fig 4D as bilateral fibre atrophy with relative fast-twitch proportional increase. This “remodelling phenotype” is distinct from hypertrophy [ 36 , 37 ] and requires countermeasures targeting both fibre types, not selective slow-fibre protection. TPM2 downregulation, a High-tier priority biomarker, provides an independent, reproducible cytoskeletal axis whose downregulation is preserved in C2C12 and human primary myotubes. The designation of MYH4 as the canonical fast-twitch reference, with MYH13 and MYH15 as novel supplementary signals, clarifies isoform selection. The Gold-tier ICS robustness (Robustness Index = 1.00; four sensitivity configurations; n = 10,000 bootstrap iterations) demonstrates that Gold-tier assignments are not sensitive to weighting-parameter choices, providing a reproducible basis for biomarker prioritisation in future validation studies. Coordinated suppression of E2F targets and DNA-replication factors (PCNA, MCM2) alongside induction of CDKN1A/p21 across all three models supports a conserved cell-cycle arrest component of the spaceflight muscle programme. Combined microgravity and 0.5 Gy proton-beam ionising radiation produced the strongest correlation with the ISS THP-1 SDEG profile (S-M6), consistent with the dual-stressor nature of the spaceflight environment. Aging-related muscle remodelling shares partial overlap with the spaceflight signature, particularly within the UPS hub network and oxidative-stress responses [ 38 – 42 ]. Limitations Primary discovery relied on a single ISS mission (Al-Ahmadi et al. 2026/Sci. Adv. 2026 ( https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adw7832 , Data S1-S10); n = 8). C2C12 and human primary myotube cross-tissue concordance used simulated rather than actual microgravity [ 43 , 44 ]. Retrospective meta-analysis heterogeneity limits causal inference. Most critically, temporal kinetic parameters (TRIM63 Day 14.1; IGF2 Day 17.5) derive from a single-astronaut dataset (NASA Twins Study n = 1); corroboration by JAXA cfRNA (n = 8) supports the directional pattern but does not replace prospective multi-astronaut validation for clinical implementation. Human primary myotube experiments used three independent commercially obtained donors; broader donor diversity and age stratification will further strengthen translational claims. Anabolic and mechanical signalling pathways (mTOR, integrin, PI3K–Akt, ribosome biogenesis), neuromuscular junction integrity, hormonal milieu, fibrosis, and recovery dynamics — extensively characterised in ground-based models [ 45 – 54 ] — were not directly assayed here and should be incorporated in future longitudinal designs. Comorbidity-relevant atrophy contexts (insulin resistance, sarcopenic obesity, exercise-induced metabolic remodelling, myokine signalling, androgen status, postural muscle disuse) [ 55 – 61 ] also warrant integration. Future priorities are: (i) prospective in-flight monitoring in ≥ 10 astronauts; (ii) multiplexed liquid biopsy (cfRNA + protein) assays; and (iii) primary muscle biopsy correlation studies.
