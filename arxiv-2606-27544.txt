Gravitational wave scattering at 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 4 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{4}) : Murua construction and elliptics Preprint: HU-EP-26/19
Yilber Fabian Bautista, Mathias Driesse, Kays Haddad, Gustav Uhre Jakobsen

Abstract
We compute the amplitude for the scattering of a gravitational wave off of a spinless point particle at fourth order in Newton’s constant, using the worldline quantum field theory formalism. A decomposition of our master integrals incorporating Murua coefficients allows us to entirely bypass the cut subtraction needed to convert the scattering amplitude into the Magnusian, the latter being desirable as it maps directly onto the scattering phase shift in partial wave space. This is then matched to the prediction from black hole perturbation theory, proving that point-particle worldline quantum field theory accurately describes Schwarzschild black holes up to 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 4 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{4}) . Elliptic functions appear in momentum space for the first time for this process at this order.



I
Introduction

Due to their demand for highly accurate waveform templates, now-routine and upcoming higher-sensitivity gravitational wave measurements challenge the theoretical modeling of compact binary coalescence in general relativity (GR)
Abbott et al. 2018
;
Colpi et al. 2024
.
Effective field theoretic (EFT) techniques have risen to the challenge, importing a rich catalogue of tools from quantum field theory to this classical setup
Goldberger and Rothstein 2006
;
Porto 2006
;
Levi and Steinhoff 2015
;
Levi 2020
;
Damgaard et al. 2019
;
Kälin et al. 2020
;
Jakobsen et al. 2022
;
Bern et al. 2021a
;
Levi et al. 2021
;
Goldberger 2022
;
Ben-Shahar 2024
;
Haddad et al. 2025
.
Such frameworks model compact bodies as point particles corrected with higher-dimensional operators, organized into powers of the curvature tensor.
This paradigm must be validated by matching EFT predictions to GR solutions, as has been done to establish a set of linear-in-curvature operators faithfully describing both Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes (BHs)
Levi and Steinhoff 2015
;
Vines 2018
.

Wilsonian naturalness anticipates that quadratic-in-curvature operators are first relevant to binary dynamics at the sixth post-Minkowskian (6PM,
\mathcal{O}(G^{6})
) order
Goldberger and Rothstein 2006
;
Porto 2006
;
Levi and Steinhoff 2015
;
Levi 2020
;
Levi et al. 2021
;
Goldberger 2022
;
Haddad et al. 2025
; their associated Wilson coefficients must also be fixed to BH values by matching to GR.
While originally motivated by the modeling of Kerr black holes as elementary spinning particles
Guevara et al. 2019
;
Chung et al. 2019
;
Bautista et al. 2023a
;
Bern et al. 2023
;
Aoude et al. 2022
;
Bautista et al. 2023b
;
Bautista 2023
;
Bautista et al. 2024
, the nearly complete 5PM binary scattering dynamics
Driesse et al. 2024
;
Driesse et al. 2025
;
Bern et al. 2025
;
Driesse et al. 2026
give this matching new urgency in the absence of spin.

Gravitational wave scattering off of a BH background is a natural arena for testing quadratic-in-curvature augmentations to the EFT.
In addition to the tidal effects encoded by these operators, the GR description of this process – elaborated by BH perturbation theory (BHPT)
Matzner and Ryan 1977
;
Matzner and Ryan 1978
– contains information about long-range gravitational exchanges.
One must therefore include this information in an EFT matching as well, involving technically demanding multi-loop calculations.

Fortunately, the extensive machinery developed for the two-body problem can be adapted to this setting
Bern et al. 2019
;
Kälin et al. 2020
;
Bern et al. 2021b
;
Bern et al. 2022
;
Kälin et al. 2023
;
Jakobsen and Mogull 2022
;
Jakobsen et al. 2023
;
Dlapa et al. 2023a
;
Driesse et al. 2024
;
Driesse et al. 2025
;
Bern et al. 2025
;
Driesse et al. 2026
;
Bohnenblust et al. 2026
.
In this vein, we developed in ref.
Bautista et al. 2026
the framework for matching worldline QFT (WQFT)
Mogull et al. 2021
with BHPT through the scattering amplitude for the aforementioned process.
Carrying out the matching at the level of the elastic phase shift, we demonstrated that point particles and Schwarzschild BHs are equivalent up to next-to-next-to-leading order,
\mathcal{O}(G^{3})
; see also
Ivanov et al. 2026
;
Bjerrum-Bohr et al. 2026
.

Here we advance this program to the next order in Newton’s constant, entailing a three-loop calculation.
We explicitly show that, after projecting onto a spherical wave basis, WQFT reproduces the corresponding BHPT phase shift.
As corrections to the spinless point-particle description are not yet relevant at this precision, this establishes the indistinguishability of Schwarzschild black holes from point particles up to
\mathcal{O}(G^{4})
.

Our work here provides an opportunity to probe additional aspects of the calculation.
First, incorporating the so-called Murua coefficients
Kim et al. 2025a
;
Brandhuber et al. 2025
;
Gonzo and Mogull 2026
, we implement the conversion between the scattering amplitude and the Magnusian
Damgaard et al. 2021
;
Kim et al. 2025a
;
Kim et al. 2025b
directly at the level of the master integrals, a drastic improvement over ref.
Bautista et al. 2026
.
Second, we find that the infrared finiteness of the Magnusian persists at three-loop order.
Finally, we observe that the WQFT amplitude at
\mathcal{O}(G^{4})
displays a richer-than-anticipated mathematical structure, with elliptic functions appearing for the first time.

Complementary techniques employing the background field method
Ivanov et al. 2024
, an effective wave equation
Correia and Isabella 2025
;
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
, and the formulation of a shell-EFT
Kosmopoulos et al. 2026
have recently been developed for scalar-wave scattering.
Extensions to waves of other spin weights have likewise appeared
Combaluzier-Szteinsznaider et al. 2026
;
Solon 2026
.
Insights from wave scattering off BHs have also found applications to BH binaries in the identification of universal contributions to the gravitational waveform
Ivanov et al. 2025
;
Cipriani et al. 2026
;
Chang et al. 2026
.

Even in view of this remarkable progress, WQFT sets itself apart in the ease of incorporating spin effects
Jakobsen et al. 2022
;
Haddad et al. 2025
.
Ultimately, we aim to pin down an EFT for Kerr BHs beyond linear order in curvature and fourth order in spin (see e.g. refs.
Bern et al. 2023
;
Bonocore et al. 2025
;
Ben-Shahar et al. 2025
for non-BHPT-based approaches).

We begin the rest of this paper with a recap of the procedure developed in ref.
Bautista et al. 2026
for matching WQFT to BHPT.
Then, in
Section

III
, we discuss how to leverage the Murua coefficients to easily extract the Magnusian from the amplitude.
Feynman integration is addressed in
Section

IV
.
Finally, the result of our computation and matching to BHPT can be found in
Section

V
.

\displaystyle g(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\omega}\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{\pi}}{}_{-2}Y_{\ell 2}(\pi-\theta,\phi)

(5b)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{P=\pm 1}(-1)^{\ell}P\left({}_{-2}\eta_{\ell 2}e^{2i{}^{\vphantom{P}}_{-2\vphantom{\ell}}\delta_{\ell 2}^{P}}-1\right)\,.

All angular dependence of the scattering is described by the spin-weighted spherical harmonics
{}_{-2}Y_{\ell 2}(\theta,\phi)
, with the scattering phase shift
{}_{-2}\delta^{P}_{\ell 2}
and absorption factor
{}_{-2}\eta_{\ell 2}
respectively encoding the remaining conservative and dissipative scattering information.
Similarly to the scattering amplitude, the latter two admit a perturbative expansion in Newton’s constant, with dissipation only becoming relevant from
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\rm PM}^{5})
.

The amplitude derived from WQFT can be matched directly onto that from BHPT:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1,{\rm fin.}}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=2\pi\,f(\theta,\phi)

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-1,{\rm fin.}}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=2\pi\,g(\theta,\phi).

However this matching is subtle, as vanishing pieces of the amplitude in the
d\rightarrow 4
limit interfere with the resummed Weinberg phase to produce the finite amplitude
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,{\rm fin.}}
needed for the matching.
A suitable prescription for matching at the level of the amplitude was put forth in ref.
Bjerrum-Bohr et al. 2026
.
While we do not check
eq.

6
in this work, we discuss in
Section

V
the construction of the finite amplitude which matches BHPT.

An alternative approach, requiring only up to the finite part of the amplitude in the
d\rightarrow 4
limit, passes through the exponential representation of the
S
-matrix
Damgaard et al. 2021
,

\displaystyle\hat{S}=e^{i\hat{N}},

(7)

where
\hat{N}
is referred to as the Magnus operator
Kim et al. 2025a
.
As we demonstrated in ref.
Bautista et al. 2026
(see also refs.
Ivanov et al. 2024
;
Ivanov et al. 2026
), the
N
-matrix element (or Magnusian
Kim et al. 2025a
;
Kim et al. 2025b
)

\displaystyle\delta\!\!\!{}^{-}\!(v\cdot k_{1}-v\cdot k_{2})

\displaystyle N_{h_{1},h_{2}}^{(n)}(\omega;\theta,\phi)

(8)

\displaystyle\equiv\langle k_{2},h_{2}|\hat{N}|k_{1},h_{1}\rangle|_{\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\rm PM}^{n})}

can be directly related to the elastic phase shift.
Specifically, at
O(\epsilon_{\text{PM}}^{4})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{N_{\ell;2,2}^{(4)}}{\sqrt{\pi(2\ell+1)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P=\pm}{}_{-2}\delta_{\ell 2}^{P,(4)},

(9a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{N_{\ell;2,-2}^{(4)}}{\sqrt{\pi(2\ell+1)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P=\pm}P(-1)^{\ell}\,{}_{-2}\delta_{\ell 2}^{P,(4)},

(9b)

where the left-hand side involves the projection of the
N
-matrix element on a basis of spin-weighted spherical harmonics,

\displaystyle N_{\ell;2,2}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\omega\int{\rm d}\Omega_{2}\,{}_{2}Y_{\ell,-2}(\theta,\phi)N^{(4)}_{2,2}(\omega;\theta,\phi)\,,

(10a)

\displaystyle N_{\ell;2,-2}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\omega\int{\rm d}\Omega_{2}\,{}_{2}Y_{\ell,-2}(\pi-\theta,\phi)N^{(4)}_{2,-2}(\omega;\theta,\phi)\,.

(10b)

As we will see, the Magnusian for this process at the present order is infrared safe and finite in the forward limit
x\rightarrow 0
.
This justifies the above projection onto a basis of functions in four-dimensions;
see refs.
Ivanov et al. 2024
;
Ivanov et al. 2026
for a
d
-dimensional treatment.

Just as the traditional Dyson series underlies the computation of

An essential difference is that, instead of the time-ordered products comprising the time-evolution operator in the former, the Magnus series involves commutators of the system’s Hamiltonian.
Functionally, this induces diagrams with retarded and advanced – rather than Feynman – causality prescriptions.
Diagrams differing only by causality prescriptions receive relative weights determined by the so-called Murua coefficients.

At first, it may seem that we have dramatically increased the work to be done, as a diagram may possess many distinct prescriptions; however, we are spared by two considerations.

For

V.1
Analysis of the scattering amplitude

Using integrals with only retarded propagators in our integrand produces the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\rm PM}^{4})
scattering amplitude, as defined by
eq.

2
, rather than the Magnusian.
The structure of this amplitude is more intricate than that of the Magnusian at all but the leading perturbative order.
First,
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
has infrared poles and the associated regularization scheme dependence.
Second, it is generally complex, while the Magnusian is real.
Third, the amplitude involves iterated integrals of weight three, while the maximum weight in the Magnusian is two.

The infrared structure of the amplitude provides important consistency checks on our calculation.
Specifically, Weinberg’s soft theorem dictates that its infrared poles in
\varepsilon
must exponentiate
Weinberg 1965
,

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}=\exp(-i\epsilon_{\rm PM}/\varepsilon)i\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\varepsilon^{0}}\ .

(24)

Here,
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\varepsilon^{0}}
starts at
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{0})
, manifesting that all poles of the amplitude
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
come from the exponential on the right-hand side.
Thus it is immediately clear that the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\rm PM}^{n})
contribution to
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
has infrared poles as deep as
\varepsilon^{-n+1}
.
Importantly, eq. (
24
) should be taken as an analytic formula in
\varepsilon
, rather than a limit, such that otherwise-vanishing
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{n\geq 1})
parts of the amplitude are included.
In particular, the interference of
\varepsilon/\varepsilon
terms is an essential mechanism in the exponentiation of infrared poles.

To derive the perturbative version of
eq.

24
, we expand
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}=\sum_{n=1}^{4}\sum_{m=-n+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{\rm PM}^{n}\varepsilon^{m}\mathcal{M}^{(n,m)}_{\sigma}\ .

(25)

Similarly decomposing
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\varepsilon^{0}}
– noting that
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\varepsilon^{0}}^{(n,m)}=0
for
m<0
– and solving
eq.

24
perturbatively, we determine that the poles of the amplitude up to 4PM must obey

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{(n,-m)}=\frac{(-i)^{m}}{m!}\mathcal{M}^{(n-m,0)}_{\sigma}\ ,

(26)

for all pairs
(n,-m)
except
(4,-1)
.
In this exceptional case,
\varepsilon/\varepsilon
cancellations enter for the first time, such that
eq.

24
demands

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{(4,-1)}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{(2,1)}-i\mathcal{M}^{(3,0)}_{\sigma}\ .

(27)

Checking this formula necessitates the computation of
\mathcal{M}^{(2,1)}_{\sigma}
.
Producing this missing piece, our amplitude passes the non-trivial checks in both
eqs.

26
and
27
.

The finite amplitude
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\rm fin.}
offers an alternative route to matching with BHPT through
eq.

6

Bjerrum-Bohr et al. 2026
.
It is easiest to define by inverting
eq.

24
:

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\rm fin.}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\exp(i\ePM/\varepsilon)i\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}i\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\varepsilon^{0}}\ .

(28)

Like
eq.

27
, then, this finite amplitude depends on
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{n\geq 1})
parts of
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
.
In particular, at 4PM,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\rm fin.}^{(4)}=\mathcal{M}^{(4,0)}_{\sigma}+i\mathcal{M}^{(3,1)}_{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}^{(2,2)}_{\sigma}\ .

(29)

As the pole relations
eqs.

26
and
27
provide checks on the divergent part of the amplitude, this relation can be used to check the finite part once we input data about its divergence in the forward limit.

The finite amplitude diverges quadratically as
x\rightarrow 0
at arbitrary PM orders, a behavior which is closely tied to the amplitude’s infrared divergences.
Similarly to the Weinberg phase, these poles can be resummed into a so-called Newtonian phase
Futterman et al. 1988
;
Dolan 2008
;
Lippstreu 2025
.
Following e.g. ref.
Bjerrum-Bohr et al. 2026
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\rm Newt.}=\epsilon_{\rm PM}\bigg(\frac{2\omega x}{\mu e^{\gamma_{\rm E}}}\bigg)^{2i\epsilon_{\rm PM}}\frac{\Gamma(1-i\epsilon_{\rm PM})}{\Gamma(1+i\epsilon_{\rm PM})}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}^{(1,0)}\ ,

(30)

with
\mu
the infrared scale introduced by continuing the four-dimensional
G
to arbitrary dimensions
G_{d}=G\tilde{\mu}^{2\varepsilon}
.
As this encapsulates the quadratic divergence, the difference

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\rm fin.}-\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\rm Newt.}

(31)

should diverge at worst as
1/x
in the forward limit.
Computing
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma,\rm fin.}^{(4)}
, we confirm that this is the case.

VI
Conclusion

We have taken the next step in the program of matching WQFT to BHPT.
Building on the methods of ref.
Bautista et al. 2026
, we have demonstrated that the former formalism reproduces the phase shift for gravitational wave scattering from the latter now up to
\mathcal{O}(G^{4})
.
This confirms that the point-particle approximation of Schwarzschild black holes is accurate at least up to this perturbative order.

In service of this matching, we have shown that the Murua coefficients introduced in refs.
Kim et al. 2025a
;
Brandhuber et al. 2025
;
Gonzo and Mogull 2026
provide an immensely economic approach for the extraction of the Magnusian.
The entirety of the cut subtraction employed in ref.
Bautista et al. 2026
results in the simple Murua decomposition of master integrals in
eq.

11
, such that directly employing the latter alleviates the computational burden of constructing cuts explicitly.

Two remarks are in order about the function space of the amplitude and Magnusian.
First, while iterated integrals of weight three make their way into the amplitude, only weight-two and below integrals appear in the Magnusian, the same maximum weight as the previous loop order.
Interestingly, a similar phenomenon occurs at one loop, where the function space in the Magnusian is the same as tree level (polynomial), though the amplitude contains logarithms.
We might therefore expect that the maximum weight of iterated integrals appearing in the Magnusian at
\mathcal{O}(G^{n})
is
2\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
.

Second, elliptic integrals have made their first appearance at this order.
At the same PM order in the two-body problem, these functions appear quadratically.
Complete elliptic integrals are periods of elliptic curves, while their squares are periods of the more complicated K3 geometry
Klemm et al. 2024
;
preliminary analysis indicates that the function space for four-loop gravitational wave scattering will be identical to three loops, pointing to the relative simplicity of this amplitude.
Moreover, the function space to three loops can be determined simply by analyzing the ladder topologies, begging the question whether all-loop statements about the function space can be made.

We highlight that the Magnusian continues to present an infrared-safe description of this process.
Moreover, the data up to three-loop order suggests that the worst behavior of the Magnusian in the forward limit is at tree level, with the
\mathcal{O}(G^{n})
contributions behaving as

\displaystyle N\overset{x\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}\frac{G}{x^{2}}+\frac{G^{2}}{x}+G^{3}\log(x)+G^{4}+\mathcal{O}(G^{5}).

(32)

It would be interesting to draw an explicit connection between these two observations, as well as to the Newtonian phase encoding the long-distance behavior of gravity
Dolan 2008
;
Lippstreu 2025
;
Bjerrum-Bohr et al. 2026
.
More broadly, the infrared properties of the Magnusian demand further exploration.

Immediate next steps to this computation are clear.
Non-minimal operators in the spinless WQFT action first become relevant at the next perturbative order, such that their values can be fixed by matching the amplitude or Magnusian at
\mathcal{O}(G^{5})
to BHPT.
Additionally, it is conceptually straightforward to extend this calculation to include spin through the bosonic oscillator formalism of ref.
Haddad et al. 2025
.
As each spin order is commensurate with a PM order, the four-loop spinless amplitude in conjunction with spin-augmented results up to three loops is sufficient to match effective descriptions of both Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes to BHPT up to
\mathcal{O}(G^{5})
.
