Spectral Gap for the Binary Fixed-Margin Swap Chain
Weibo Fu, Qian Qin, Guanyang Wang

Abstract
We prove an inverse-polynomial spectral-gap bound for the lazy swap chain on binary matrices with prescribed row and column sums. This chain is a standard sampler for fixed-margin null models in ecology, statistics, and network analysis, and its rapid mixing for arbitrary feasible margins was conjectured by 16 . We show that for every feasible set of margins on an m × n m\times n binary matrix, the lazy swap chain has spectral gap at least ( m 2 ) − 1 ​ ( n 2 ) − 1 , \binom{m}{2}^{-1}\binom{n}{2}^{-1}, which is tight in the worst case. The proof compares the swap chain with a two-row heat-bath chain, reduces the analysis from arbitrary m × n m\times n matrices to the case of three rows, and proves the resulting three-row inequality by decomposing functions according to the column-count variable and the associated Johnson harmonic sectors. The proof itself was generated by ChatGPT 5.5 Pro. ChatGPT proposed the whole proof strategy, including the comparison with the two-row heat-bath chain, the reduction to the three-row case, and the decomposition of the three-row function space into the count sector and the Johnson harmonic sectors. It also generated all the technical lemmas and initial proofs. The author’s role was to pose the problem, guide the search direction, evaluate the AI-generated arguments, rewrite the proof, and take responsibility for the final form and validity of the result.

Introduction

Binary matrices are a natural way to encode binary relations between two sets of objects. For example, in ecology, one may record which bird species are observed on which islands by an
m\times n
binary matrix
A
, where
A_{ij}=1
if species
i
is present on island
j
, and
A_{ij}=0
otherwise.

A basic task is to generate uniformly random binary matrices with prescribed row and column sums. This problem arises naturally in ecological null models. Given an observed species–island matrix, ecologists ask whether its pattern reflects real ecological structure, such as competition between species, or can be explained by random chance after controlling for species prevalence and island richness. This leads to the fixed-margin null model, in which one samples uniformly from all binary matrices with the same row and column sums as the observed matrix. The same sampling problem also appears in testing the Rasch model for binary response data, where conditioning on the row and column sums makes all feasible matrices equally likely
(
21
;
3
)
. Related fixed-margin sampling problems arise in network reconstruction, economics, and the social sciences; see
19
for a survey.

The swap algorithm is the most basic Markov chain for this sampling problem. Starting from a binary matrix with the prescribed row and column sums, the algorithm repeatedly chooses two rows and two columns uniformly at random. If the resulting
2\times 2
submatrix is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{or}\quad\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

then the algorithm replaces it by the other pattern; otherwise, it leaves the matrix unchanged.

Despite decades of use, a central open problem is to determine how many steps the
swap algorithm needs to approach the uniform distribution, or equivalently, to bound
its mixing time. In 1997,
16
conjectured that the swap chain
mixes in polynomial time for every feasible choice of row and column sums. This
question has led to a long line of work on swap and switch chains for fixed-margin
binary matrices and closely related prescribed-margin models. Early progress
includes the work of
18
, who proved rapid mixing for
semi-regular bipartite margins. Further work developed structural, stability-based,
and comparison methods for larger families of margins and related models

(
9
;
6
)
. A major theme in this literature is
that rapid mixing can often be proved under suitable stability assumptions on the
margins, starting from the P-stability framework of
15
.
This approach has led to rapid-mixing results for several large classes of margins

(
1
;
8
;
11
)
. These contributions
form much of the current theoretical basis for the swap chain, but they still leave
open the original conjecture for arbitrary row and column sums.

This paper proves a worst-case optimal spectral-gap bound for the swap chain on binary matrices with prescribed margins. For every feasible set of row and column sums on an
m\times n
binary matrix, the lazy swap chain has spectral gap at least

\binom{m}{2}^{-1}\binom{n}{2}^{-1}.

This bound is tight in the worst case. In particular, it implies polynomial-time mixing in the matrix dimensions and resolves the binary-matrix case of the conjecture of
16
. In fact, it proves a result stronger than the conjecture itself.

The proof has three main steps. First, we compare the swap chain with the two-row heat-bath chain, equivalently the row-pair Curveball algorithm introduced in the ecology literature by
24
. Second, we apply the reduction method of
5
to reduce the
m\times n
problem to the corresponding chain on
3\times n
matrices. Third, we prove the three-row bound by combining a representation-theoretic decomposition of the function space with Johnson harmonic analysis, and by controlling the scalar count sector through path coupling. As a consequence, our argument also gives a spectral-gap lower bound of
\binom{m}{2}^{-1}
for the row-pair Curveball chain. This proves unconditional rapid mixing of Curveball for arbitrary fixed margins, strengthening earlier results
(
6
)
which showed rapid mixing of Curveball whenever the switch chain is rapidly mixing.

Finally, we note that binary matrices with fixed row and column sums are equivalently bipartite graphs with fixed degrees. The conjecture of
16
is part of a broader set of questions on switch chains for graph realizations with prescribed degrees. We refer to
7
;
13
;
18
;
14
;
12
;
1
;
8
;
11
for important progress on these questions.
While the result here resolves the bipartite case, the analogous rapid-mixing problem for simple undirected graphs with arbitrary degree sequences remains open. For directed graphs, the usual switch chain is not always irreducible
(
2
)
, so the corresponding question must be formulated for an enlarged set of moves.

Main result

Let

\Omega(r,c)=\left\{X\in\{0,1\}^{m\times n}:\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{ij}=r_{i}\ \forall i,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{ij}=c_{j}\ \forall j\right\}.

Assume throughout that
\Omega(r,c)\neq\varnothing
.

The lazy swap chain is the following reversible Markov chain on
\Omega(r,c)
. With probability
1/2
it stays put. Otherwise it chooses an unordered pair of distinct rows
\{a,b\}
and an unordered pair of distinct columns
\{u,v\}
, both uniformly. If the resulting
2\times 2
submatrix is

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{or}\quad\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

it replaces it by the other one. If the chosen submatrix is not switchable, the chain stays put. Let
P_{\rm sw}
denote its transition matrix. We use the following convention for the spectral gap. If

|\Omega(r,c)|\geq 2
, then

\gamma_{\rm sw}=1-\lambda_{2}(P_{\rm sw}),

where
\lambda_{2}(P_{\rm sw})
is the second largest eigenvalue of
P_{\rm sw}
.
If
|\Omega(r,c)|=1
, we set

\gamma_{\rm sw}=1.

The following result shows that the lazy chain is irreducible and aperiodic. Irreducibility is equivalent to the statement that any two binary matrices with the same row and column sums can be connected by a sequence of swap moves, which follows from
22
. Aperiodicity follows from the laziness of the chain.

Proposition 2.1

(Connectivity of the swap chain)
.

For every feasible pair of margins
(r,c)
, the non-lazy swaps connect
\Omega(r,c)
. Consequently the lazy swap chain is irreducible and aperiodic on
\Omega(r,c)
.

Our main result is the following spectral-gap bound for the lazy swap chain.

Theorem 2.2

(Main theorem)
.

For every feasible pair of margins
(r,c)
, the lazy swap chain satisfies

\gamma_{\rm sw}\geq\binom{m}{2}^{-1}\binom{n}{2}^{-1}

whenever
m,n\geq 2
. Consequently,

t_{\rm mix}(\varepsilon)\leq\binom{m}{2}\binom{n}{2}\left(\log|\Omega(r,c)|+\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right)\leq\binom{m}{2}\binom{n}{2}\left(mn\log 2+\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right).

Remark 2.3

(Sharpness of the constant)
.

The bound in Theorem
2.2
is sharp in the worst case. Indeed, fix two rows and two columns, say
rows
1,2
and columns
1,2
, and take margins

r_{1}=r_{2}=1,\qquad r_{3}=\cdots=r_{m}=0,

and

c_{1}=c_{2}=1,\qquad c_{3}=\cdots=c_{n}=0.

Then the state space consists of exactly the two matrices whose only
nonzero entries form one of the two
2\times 2
permutation matrices
inside rows
1,2
and columns
1,2
. Thus the unique nontrivial move is
the swap on this single checkerboard block.

For the lazy swap chain, from either state the probability of moving to
the other state is

p=\frac{1}{2}\binom{m}{2}^{-1}\binom{n}{2}^{-1},

because the chain must first avoid the lazy step and then choose the
unique active row pair and column pair. Hence the transition matrix on this
two-point space is

\begin{pmatrix}1-p&p\\
p&1-p\end{pmatrix},

whose spectral gap is
2p
. Therefore

\gamma_{\mathrm{sw}}=\binom{m}{2}^{-1}\binom{n}{2}^{-1}.

Thus no larger uniform lower bound, even by a constant factor in this
normalization, can hold for all feasible margins.

3
Comparison to a Two-row Heat-bath Chain

3.1
The two-row heat-bath chain

We compare
\gamma_{\rm sw}
to the spectral gap of the following two-row heat-bath chain targeting the uniform distribution on
\Omega(r,c)
.
Given the current binary matrix
X\in\Omega(r,c)
, choose an unordered pair
\{a,b\}\subset[m]
uniformly, where
a\neq b
; freeze all rows except rows
a,b
; then resample rows
a,b
uniformly among all completions satisfying
\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{aj}=r_{a}
,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{bj}=r_{b}
, and
X_{aj}+X_{bj}=c_{j}-\sum_{i\not\in\{a,b\}}X_{ij}
for
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Let
P_{H}
be its transition matrix and let

\gamma_{H}=1-\lambda_{2}(P_{H}).

Then
P_{H}
corresponds to a Gibbs algorithm that updates two coordinates (rows) at a time, while
P_{\rm sw}
corresponds to a hybrid version of this Gibbs algorithm, with the resampling of the two selected rows replaced by a possible column swap.
We use a decomposition technique
(
20
)
to compare their spectral gaps.

3.2
Spectral gap comparison

For a matrix
X\in\Omega(r,c)
and
\{a,b\}\subset[m]
, let
X_{-\{a,b\}}
be
X
excluding rows
a,b
.
The transition matrix
P_{H}
may be written as

P_{H}(X,X^{\prime})={m\choose 2}^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{a,b\}\subset[m]\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\pi^{a,b}(X^{\prime}\mid X_{-\{a,b\}}),

where
\pi^{a,b}(\cdot\mid z)
is the uniform distribution on
\{X\in\Omega(r,c):\,X_{-\{a,b\}}=z\}
.

The transition matrix
P_{\rm sw}
can be written into a similar form.
For
\{a,b\}\subset[m]
and a value of
X_{-\{a,b\}}
denoted by
z
, let
P^{a,b}_{\rm sw}(\cdot,\cdot\mid z)
be the transition matrix of the following Markov chain on the support of
\pi^{a,b}(\cdot\mid z)
:
Given the current state
X\in\Omega(r,c)
satisfying
X_{-\{a,b\}}=z
, with probability
1/2
, stay put.
Otherwise, uniformly select a pair of distinct indices
(u,v)
from
[n]
; if

\begin{pmatrix}X_{au}&X_{av}\\
X_{bu}&X_{bv}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\;\text{or}\;\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

replace the submatrix with the other one.
Then

P_{\rm sw}(X,X^{\prime})={m\choose 2}^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{a,b\}\subset[m]\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}P^{a,b}_{\rm sw}(X,X^{\prime}\mid X_{-\{a,b\}}).

We compare the two Markov chains in terms of Dirichlet forms.
For a reversible Markov chain with transition matrix
M
and stationary
measure
\varpi
, and a function
f
, define the Dirichlet form

\mathcal{E}_{M}(f,f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y}\varpi(x)\,M(x,y)\,[f(x)-f(y)]^{2}.

Lemma 3.1

(Comparison with heat bath)
.

We have

\gamma_{\rm sw}\geq\binom{n}{2}^{-1}\gamma_{H}.

Proof.

Fix a pair of indices
\{a,b\}\subset[m]
where
a\neq b
.
Consider a matrix
X
in
\Omega(r,c)
with
X_{-\{a,b\}}
frozen at some value
z
.
For each
column
j
, the value
X_{aj}+X_{bj}
is then fixed. Columns with value

0
or
2
are forced, while columns with value
1
each have two possible configurations.
Let

T=\{j:X_{aj}+X_{bj}=1\}.

The number of
j
’s in
T
such that
X_{aj}=1
is fixed due to the restriction
\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{aj}=r_{a}
; call it
k
.
Thus we may represent rows
a,b
as a set

S\subseteq T,\qquad|S|=k,

where
j\in S
means
X_{aj}=1
and
X_{bj}=0
. Let
\nu
be the
uniform measure on the collection of all such
S
, and write
g(S)
for the corresponding
value of
f
.

Having frozen all other rows, the two-row heat-bath
P_{H}
update replaces the current value of
S
by an independent uniform element
of
\{S^{\prime}\subseteq T:|S^{\prime}|=k\}
. Hence its conditional Dirichlet form is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{H}^{a,b}(f,f\mid z):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{X,X^{\prime}}\pi^{a,b}(X\mid z)\,\pi^{a,b}(X^{\prime}\mid z)\,[f(X)-f(X^{\prime})]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{S,S^{\prime}}\nu(\{S\})\,\nu(\{S^{\prime}\})\,[g(S)-g(S^{\prime})]^{2}=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(g),

We write
\nu(\{S\})
instead of
\nu(S)
to emphasize
\nu
is a distribution on the
k
-subsets of
T
as opposed to
T
itself.

On the other hand,
P_{\rm sw}^{a,b}(\cdot,\cdot\mid z)
can be reformulated as a conditional swap chain that is reversible with respect to
\nu
.
We will denote by
P_{\rm csw}
the transition matrix of the conditional swap chain described below:
With probability
1/2
, this local chain stays put, and otherwise it updates the current state
S
as follows: two distinct elements
u,v\in[n]
are chosen uniformly at random, and if one belongs to
S
and the other belongs to
T\setminus S
, the former is swapped out of
S
for the latter.
Thus, a particular transition

S\longmapsto(S\setminus\{u\})\cup\{v\},\qquad u\in S,\quad v\in T\setminus S,

occurs with probability

p=\frac{1}{2\binom{n}{2}},

where the factor
1/2
is the laziness.

Consider two adjacent local states

S=C\cup\{u\},\qquad S^{\prime}=C\cup\{u^{\prime}\},

where
u\neq u^{\prime}
and
u,u^{\prime}\not\in C
.
We couple one step of the two chains as follows. With probability
p
, let
the chain from
S
jump to
S^{\prime}
and let the chain from
S^{\prime}
stay put.
With probability
p
, let the chain from
S^{\prime}
jump to
S
and let the
chain from
S
stay put. In either case the two chains coalesce. All
remaining transition mass can be coupled so that the two resulting states remain adjacent or become identical:
when the chain from
S
jumps to
C\cup\{v\}
where
v\not\in C\cup\{u,u^{\prime}\}
, so does the chain from
S^{\prime}
;
when the chain from
S
jumps to
(C\setminus\{v\})\cup\{u,u^{\prime}\}
where
v\in C
, so does the chain from
S^{\prime}
;
when the chain from
S
jumps to
(C\setminus\{v\})\cup\{u,w\}
where
v\in C
and
w\not\in C\cup\{u,u^{\prime}\}
, the chain from
S^{\prime}
jumps to
(C\setminus\{v\})\cup\{u^{\prime},w\}
.

Define the graphical distance

d(S,S^{\prime})=|S\setminus S^{\prime}|,

Then, whenever
S,S^{\prime}
are adjacent, if
(S_{1},S_{1}^{\prime})
is generated by one step of the above coupled chain,

\mathbb{E}[d(S_{1},S^{\prime}_{1})]\leq 1-2p=1-\binom{n}{2}^{-1}.

By path coupling
(
4
)
, the conditional lazy swap chain has spectral gap at least

\binom{n}{2}^{-1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm sw}^{a,b}(f,f\mid z):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{X,X^{\prime}}\pi^{a,b}(X\mid z)\,P^{a,b}_{\rm sw}(X,X^{\prime}\mid z)\,[f(X)-f(X^{\prime})]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{S,S^{\prime}}\nu(\{S\})\,P_{\rm csw}(S,S^{\prime})\,[g(S)-g(S^{\prime})]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(g).

Since
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(g)
is exactly the conditional Dirichlet
form of the two-row heat-bath update, we get

\mathcal{E}_{\rm sw}^{a,b}(f,f\mid z)\geq\binom{n}{2}^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{H}^{a,b}(f,f\mid z).

Averaging over the conditioned
z
and over the uniformly chosen row
pair
\{a,b\}
, we obtain

\mathcal{E}_{\rm sw}(f,f)\geq\binom{n}{2}^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{H}(f,f).

Dividing by
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi}(f)
, where
\pi
is the uniform
measure on matrices with the prescribed row and column sums, and taking the
infimum over all nonconstant
f
, gives

\gamma_{\rm sw}\geq\binom{n}{2}^{-1}\gamma_{H}.

∎

4
Reduction to Three Rows

The following reduction is a special case of the projection argument of

5
. We give a self-contained proof for the row-pair
heat-bath chain on binary matrices with fixed row and column sums.

Recall
\Omega=\Omega(r,c)
is the set of
m\times n
binary matrices with
prescribed row sums
r
and column sums
c
, and let
\pi
be the uniform
measure on
\Omega
. For a pair of row indices
\theta=\{a,b\}\subset[m]
where
a\neq b
, let
E_{\theta}
denote
conditional expectation given all rows outside
\theta
.
In other words,

E_{\theta}f(X)=\sum_{X^{\prime}}f(X^{\prime})\,\pi^{a,b}(X^{\prime}\mid X_{-\{a,b\}}).

Put

R_{\theta}=\Id-E_{\theta}.

Since
E_{\theta}
is a conditional expectation, it is an orthogonal projection on
L^{2}(\pi)
, and
so
R_{\theta}
is also an orthogonal projection.

The row-pair heat-bath transition matrix is

P_{H}=\binom{m}{2}^{-1}\sum_{\theta\in\binom{[m]}{2}}E_{\theta}.

Equivalently,

P_{H}=\Id-\binom{m}{2}^{-1}Q_{H},\qquad Q_{H}=\sum_{\theta\in\binom{[m]}{2}}R_{\theta}.

Moreover, the row-pair heat-bath chain is irreducible because its transition contains the usual
2\times 2
swap chain, which is connected on
\Omega(r,c)
by Proposition
2.1
; it is aperiodic because each heat-bath update has positive probability of returning the current state. Therefore, to prove

\gamma_{H}\geq\binom{m}{2}^{-1},

it is enough to show that every positive eigenvalue of
Q_{H}
is at least

1
.

Lemma 4.1

(Three-row reduction)
.

Assume that for every triple of rows
T\subseteq[m]
, and every
positive-probability fixing of the rows outside
T
, the row-pair heat-bath
chain on the remaining three rows has spectral gap at least
1/3
. Then

\gamma_{H}\geq\binom{m}{2}^{-1}.

Proof.

For a test function
f
, define

a_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}=\left\langle R_{\theta}f,R_{\theta^{\prime}}f\right\rangle,\qquad\mathcal{A}=\sum_{\theta}a_{\theta,\theta}=\sum_{\theta}\left\lVert R_{\theta}f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

where
\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{X}\pi(X)\,f(X)\,g(X)
, and
\|f\|_{2}=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle}
.
Since
R_{\theta}
is an orthogonal projection,

\left\langle f,R_{\theta}f\right\rangle=\left\langle R_{\theta}f,R_{\theta}f\right\rangle=\left\lVert R_{\theta}f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Therefore

\left\langle f,Q_{H}f\right\rangle=\mathcal{A},\qquad\left\lVert Q_{H}f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}a_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}.

We first claim the following commutation fact. If

\theta\cap\theta^{\prime}=\varnothing
, then

E_{\theta}E_{\theta^{\prime}}=E_{\theta^{\prime}}E_{\theta}.

Indeed, condition on all rows outside
\theta\cup\theta^{\prime}
. During the
\theta
-update,
the two rows in
\theta^{\prime}
are held fixed, so the column-wise sum of the two rows
in
\theta
is fixed. Similarly, during the
\theta^{\prime}
-update, the column-wise sum of
the two rows in
\theta^{\prime}
is fixed. Once these two column-wise pair sums are fixed,
the choices for the two row pairs are independent uniform choices, and averaging
over one pair then the other is independent of the order. Hence the two
conditional expectations commute.

Thus
R_{\theta}=\Id-E_{\theta}
and
R_{\theta^{\prime}}=\Id-E_{\theta^{\prime}}
are commuting orthogonal
projections. Hence
R_{\theta}R_{\theta^{\prime}}
is positive semidefinite, and

a_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}=\left\langle R_{\theta}f,R_{\theta^{\prime}}f\right\rangle=\left\langle f,R_{\theta}R_{\theta^{\prime}}f\right\rangle\geq 0

whenever
\theta\cap\theta^{\prime}=\varnothing
.

Now fix a triple of row indices
T\subseteq[m]
, and define

Q_{H}^{(T)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\subseteq T\\
|\theta|=2\end{subarray}}R_{\theta}.

After conditioning on
X_{-T}
, the rows outside
T
, the three-row heat-bath transition
matrix is

P_{T}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\subseteq T\\
|\theta|=2\end{subarray}}E_{\theta}=\Id-\frac{1}{3}Q_{H}^{(T)}.

By assumption, this conditional chain has spectral gap at least
1/3
. Hence
every positive eigenvalue of
Q_{H}^{(T)}
is at least
1
.
Then, if
X
is distributed as
\pi
,

\mathbb{E}\{[Q_{H}^{(T)}f(X)]^{2}\mid X_{-T}\}\geq\mathbb{E}[f(X)\,Q_{H}^{(T)}f(X)\mid X_{-T}].

Taking the average yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta,\theta^{\prime}\subseteq T\\
|\theta|=|\theta^{\prime}|=2\end{subarray}}a_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\subseteq T\\
|\theta|=2\end{subarray}}a_{\theta,\theta}.

Sum this inequality over all triples
T\subseteq[m]
. Each diagonal term

a_{\theta,\theta}
is counted in exactly
m-2
triples. Each ordered off-diagonal
term
a_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}
with
\theta\cap\theta^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
is counted exactly once,
namely in the triple
\theta\cup\theta^{\prime}
. Disjoint pair-pairs are not counted. Thus

(m-2)\mathcal{A}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\neq\theta^{\prime}\\
\theta\cap\theta^{\prime}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}a_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\geq(m-2)\mathcal{A}.

Therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\neq\theta^{\prime}\\
\theta\cap\theta^{\prime}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}a_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\geq 0.

Together with the nonnegativity of the disjoint-pair terms, this gives

\sum_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}a_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\geq\sum_{\theta}a_{\theta,\theta}=\mathcal{A}.

Equivalently,

\left\lVert Q_{H}f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\left\langle f,Q_{H}f\right\rangle.

Since
Q_{H}
is self-adjoint and nonnegative, diagonalizing
Q_{H}
shows that the
last inequality for all
f
is equivalent to saying that every positive
eigenvalue of
Q_{H}
is at least
1
. Finally,

P_{H}=\Id-\binom{m}{2}^{-1}Q_{H},

so the row-pair heat-bath chain has spectral gap at least

\binom{m}{2}^{-1}.

∎

Thus, to prove the row-pair heat-bath bound for all
m\geq 3
, it remains to
show that every row-pair heat-bath chain on
3\times n
binary matrices with
arbitrary feasible margins has spectral gap at least
1/3
.

Analysis of the Three-row Model

5.1
Overview

In this section, we study the three-row heat-bath chain.
Since the analysis is quite involved, it helps to introduce a new set of notations.

Consider a conditional three-row state space obtained after fixing all rows
outside a chosen triple. Columns whose residual column sum is
0
or
3

are forced and may be deleted. Let
P
be the set of remaining columns with
residual column sum
1
, and let
Q
be the set of remaining columns with
residual column sum
2
. Put
p=|P|
and
q=|Q|
.

A state is encoded by two ordered partitions

A_{1}\sqcup A_{2}\sqcup A_{3}=P,\qquad B_{1}\sqcup B_{2}\sqcup B_{3}=Q,

where
A_{i}
is the set of sum-one columns occupied by row
i
, and
B_{i}
is the set of sum-two columns missed by row
i
. The row-sum constraints are equivalent, after absorbing forced columns into constants, to

|A_{i}|-|B_{i}|=d_{i},\qquad i=1,2,3,

where
d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}=p-q
. The stationary measure of the three-row heat-bath is uniform over all labeled states satisfying these constraints.

In view of the stationary measure, let

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\sigma(A_{i},B_{i}),\qquad P_{i}=\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{i}],\qquad K_{3}=\frac{1}{3}(P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3}).

Updating the pair of rows different from
i
is the same as conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, so the pair heat-bath chain on three rows is exactly
K_{3}
.

Theorem 5.1

(Three-row projection inequality)
.

For every feasible conditional three-row state space,

\operatorname{gap}(K_{3})\geq\frac{1}{3}.

Equivalently, for every mean-zero function
f
,

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Var\left(\mathbb{E}[f\mid\mathcal{F}_{i}]\right)\leq 2\Var(f).

The next subsections prove Theorem
5.1
.

5.2
The scalar count sector

First consider test functions depending only on the row-count vector. We use the vector

(|B_{1}|,|B_{2}|,|B_{3}|),

which is equivalent to using the corresponding
A
-counts.

Lemma 5.2

(One-dimensional sandwich coupling)
.

Let
u,v
be positive log-concave weights on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and define

\mu_{s}(t)=\frac{u(t)v(s-t)}{\sum_{a}u(a)v(s-a)}

on its natural support. If
T_{s}\sim\mu_{s}
and
T_{s+1}\sim\mu_{s+1}
, then there is a coupling such that

T_{s+1}\in\{T_{s},T_{s}+1\}

almost surely.

Proof.

The likelihood ratio

\frac{\mu_{s+1}(t)}{\mu_{s}(t)}\propto\frac{v(s+1-t)}{v(s-t)}

is increasing in
t
, because
v(k+1)/v(k)
is decreasing in
k
. Hence
\mu_{s}\preceq_{\rm st}\mu_{s+1}
. Applying the same argument after swapping the two coordinates shows that
s+1-T_{s+1}
stochastically dominates
s-T_{s}
, equivalently

T_{s+1}\preceq_{\rm st}T_{s}+1.

Thus

T_{s}\preceq_{\rm st}T_{s+1}\preceq_{\rm st}T_{s}+1.

For integer laws, the quantile coupling realizes both inequalities at the same time: if
U
is uniform on
[0,1]
and
F_{\nu}^{-1}(U)=\min\{t:F_{\nu}(t)\geq U\}
, set

T_{s}=F_{\mu_{s}}^{-1}(U),\qquad T_{s+1}=F_{\mu_{s+1}}^{-1}(U).

The two stochastic inequalities imply
T_{s}\leq T_{s+1}\leq T_{s}+1
for every
U
. Since the variables are integer-valued, this is the desired coupling.
∎

Lemma 5.3

(Scalar Bessel inequality)
.

Let

\Lambda_{N}=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{3}:x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=N\},

and let

\pi(x)=\frac{1}{Z}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{x_{i}!(x_{i}+\delta_{i})!},\qquad\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Let
Q_{i}f=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[f\mid X_{i}]
, and let
K=(Q_{1}+Q_{2}+Q_{3})/3
. Then

\operatorname{gap}(K)\geq\frac{1}{3}.

Proof.

Put

w_{i}(k)=\frac{1}{k!(k+\delta_{i})!}.

The ratios

\frac{w_{i}(k+1)}{w_{i}(k)}=\frac{1}{(k+1)(k+\delta_{i}+1)}

are decreasing in
k
, so each
w_{i}
is log-concave.

Put on
\Lambda_{N}
the graph metric

d(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}|x_{i}-y_{i}|.

Adjacent states have the form
y=x-e_{a}+e_{b}
. Couple one step of
K
from
x
and from
y
using the same frozen coordinate.

If coordinate
c
, the third coordinate different from
a,b
, is frozen, then
x_{c}=y_{c}
, so the conditional laws of the other two coordinates are identical; couple them identically, and the chains coalesce. If coordinate
a
is frozen, then the two remaining totals differ by one. Lemma
5.2
couples the two conditional resamplings so that the full updated configurations remain adjacent. The same argument applies when coordinate
b
is frozen. Therefore, given adjacent
x,y
, if
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
are generated via one step of the coupled chain,

\mathbb{E}[d(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})]\leq\frac{1}{3}\cdot 0+\frac{1}{3}\cdot 1+\frac{1}{3}\cdot 1=\frac{2}{3}.

By path coupling
(
4
)
,

W_{1}(K(x,\cdot),K(y,\cdot))\leq\frac{2}{3}d(x,y)

for all
x,y
. Kantorovich duality then gives contraction of the Lipschitz seminorm:

\Lip(Kf)\leq\frac{2}{3}\Lip(f).

If
Kf=\lambda f
and
f
is nonconstant, then
\Lip(f)>0
, so

|\lambda|\Lip(f)=\Lip(Kf)\leq\frac{2}{3}\Lip(f).

Thus every nonconstant eigenvalue has absolute value at most
2/3
, and in particular
\operatorname{gap}(K)\geq 1/3
.
∎

Lemma 5.4

(Identification of the count chain)
.

The restriction of
K_{3}
to count-dependent functions is of the form in Lemma
5.3
.

Proof.

Let

b_{i}=|B_{i}|,\qquad a_{i}=|A_{i}|=b_{i}+d_{i}.

The number of labeled states with these counts is

\frac{p!}{a_{1}!a_{2}!a_{3}!}\frac{q!}{b_{1}!b_{2}!b_{3}!},

which is proportional, as a function of the counts, to

\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{a_{i}!b_{i}!}.

If
d_{i}\geq 0
, set
x_{i}=b_{i}
. If
d_{i}<0
, set
x_{i}=a_{i}=b_{i}+d_{i}
. Then

a_{i}!b_{i}!=x_{i}!(x_{i}+|d_{i}|)!,

and
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}
is fixed. Hence the count law is precisely the Bessel law of Lemma
5.3
, with
\delta_{i}=|d_{i}|
. The apparent upper bounds
a_{i}\leq p
and
b_{i}\leq q
are automatic from nonnegativity and the fixed sums
\sum_{i}a_{i}=p
,
\sum_{i}b_{i}=q
.

For a count-dependent function, conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\sigma(A_{i},B_{i})
is the same as conditioning on
|B_{i}|
, since labels are exchangeable within fixed counts. Therefore
K_{3}
on count-dependent functions is exactly the chain in Lemma
5.3
.
∎

5.3
Johnson harmonics and non-scalar sectors

5.3.1
Johnson harmonic preliminaries

Let
E
be a finite set with
|E|=N
. For
\ell\geq 0
, let
H_{E}^{\ell}
be the space of functions
h
on
\ell
-subsets of
E
whose lower shadows vanish:

\sum_{L\supset T,\ |L|=\ell}h(L)=0\quad\text{for every }T\subset E,\ |T|<\ell.

For
a\geq\ell
, define the lift

h^{(a)}(A)=\sum_{L\subset A,\ |L|=\ell}h(L),\qquad|A|=a,

and set
h^{(a)}=0
if
a<\ell
. For
\ell=0
,
H_{E}^{0}
is the space of constants and
h^{(a)}
is constant.

The next lemma is the standard harmonic decomposition for functions on a Boolean slice. This decomposition can be viewed either as the eigenspace decomposition of the Johnson association scheme or as the harmonic multilinear-polynomial representation of functions on a slice; see, for example,
10
. From the representation-theoretic viewpoint, it is the two-row case of Young’s rule for the Young permutation module
M^{(N-a,a)}
; see Theorem 2.11.2 of
23
. We include a self-contained finite-dimensional proof.

Lemma 5.5

(Johnson harmonic decomposition)
.

For each
a
, the functions on
a
-subsets of
E
decompose orthogonally as

L^{2}\!\left(\binom{E}{a}\right)=\bigoplus_{0\leq\ell\leq\min(a,N-a)}\{h^{(a)}:h\in H_{E}^{\ell}\}.

Proof.

Write

V_{r}=L^{2}\!\left(\binom{E}{r}\right).

Define the up and down operators

U_{r}:V_{r}\to V_{r+1},\qquad(U_{r}f)(A)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subset A\\
|B|=r\end{subarray}}f(B),

and

D_{r}:V_{r}\to V_{r-1},\qquad(D_{r}f)(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\supset B\\
|A|=r\end{subarray}}f(A).

Then

D_{r+1}=U_{r}^{*}.

The lower-shadow condition is exactly

H_{E}^{\ell}=\ker D_{\ell}

for
\ell\geq 1
, while
H_{E}^{0}=V_{0}
.

We use the elementary identity

\displaystyle D_{r+1}U_{r}-U_{r-1}D_{r}=(N-2r)I_{V_{r}}.

(1)

Indeed, evaluating both sides at an
r
-set
S
, the off-diagonal terms cancel, while the coefficient of
f(S)
is
(N-r)-r=N-2r
.

Set
W_{\ell}^{\ell}=H_{E}^{\ell}
and
W_{r+1}^{\ell}=U_{r}W_{r}^{\ell}
. A direct induction using (
1
) gives, for every
\ell\leq r<N-\ell
,

\displaystyle U_{r}^{*}U_{r}=D_{r+1}U_{r}=(r+1-\ell)(N-r-\ell)I\quad\text{on }W_{r}^{\ell}.

(2)

Since the scalar in (
2
) is positive,
U_{r}
has full column rank on
W_{r}^{\ell}
. Hence the successive lifts are injective up to level
N-\ell
. In particular, the lift
h\mapsto h^{(a)}
is injective on
H_{E}^{\ell}
whenever
\ell\leq\min(a,N-a)
.

Also, since
D_{\ell}=U_{\ell-1}^{*}
, the injectivity of
U_{\ell-1}
for
\ell\leq N/2
implies that
D_{\ell}:V_{\ell}\to V_{\ell-1}
is surjective. Therefore

\dim H_{E}^{\ell}=\dim\ker D_{\ell}=\binom{N}{\ell}-\binom{N}{\ell-1},\qquad 0\leq\ell\leq N/2,

with
\binom{N}{-1}=0
. Hence

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min(a,N-a)}\dim H_{E}^{\ell}=\binom{N}{\min(a,N-a)}=\binom{N}{a}.

It remains to prove orthogonality. Let
\ell<\ell^{\prime}
,
h\in H_{E}^{\ell}
, and
g\in H_{E}^{\ell^{\prime}}
. Here
U_{\ell\to a}
denotes the iterated up map from level
\ell
to level
a
, so
U_{\ell\to a}h
is a positive scalar multiple of
h^{(a)}
. By adjointness and (
2
),

\left\langle U_{\ell\to a}h,\ U_{\ell^{\prime}\to a}g\right\rangle=\left\langle D_{\ell^{\prime}+1}\cdots D_{a}\,U_{\ell\to a}h,\ g\right\rangle=c\left\langle U_{\ell\to\ell^{\prime}}h,\ g\right\rangle

for some scalar
c
. Applying adjointness once more,

\left\langle U_{\ell\to\ell^{\prime}}h,\ g\right\rangle=\left\langle h,\ D_{\ell+1}\cdots D_{\ell^{\prime}}g\right\rangle=0,

because
g\in\ker D_{\ell^{\prime}}
. Thus the lifted spaces for different
\ell
are orthogonal.

The dimension count above shows that these orthogonal injective images have total dimension
\binom{N}{a}
, which is the dimension of
V_{a}=L^{2}\!\left(\binom{E}{a}\right)
. Hence they fill
V_{a}
, proving the claimed orthogonal decomposition.
∎

Lemma 5.6

(Disjointness averaging)
.

Let
h\in H_{E}^{\ell}
. If
|A|=a
, and
B
, given
A
, is chosen uniformly among the
b
-subsets of
E\setminus A
, then

\mathbb{E}[h^{(b)}(B)\mid A]=(-1)^{\ell}\frac{(b)_{\ell}}{(N-a)_{\ell}}h^{(a)}(A),

with the convention that the right side is zero if the denominator vanishes. Here
(r)_{\ell}=r(r-1)\cdots(r-\ell+1)
.

Proof.

When
(N-a)_{\ell}>0
, averaging over
B\subseteq E\setminus A
gives

\mathbb{E}[h^{(b)}(B)\mid A]={N-a\choose b}^{-1}\sum_{B\subseteq E\setminus A,\,|B|=b}h^{(b)}(B)=\frac{(b)_{\ell}}{(N-a)_{\ell}}\sum_{L\subseteq E\setminus A,\ |L|=\ell}h(L).

(3)

On the set
\{L\subset E:\,|L|=\ell\}
, define the measure
\mu(\{L\})=h(L)
.
For
u\in A
, let
C_{u}=\{L\subset E:\,|L|=\ell,\,u\in L\}
.
By inclusion-exclusion,

\mu\left(\bigcup_{u\in A}C_{u}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{a}(-1)^{j-1}\sum_{J:\,|J|=j}\mu\left(\bigcap_{u\in J}C_{u}\right).

That is,

\sum_{L:\,L\cap A\neq\emptyset}h(L)=\sum_{j=1}^{a}(-1)^{j-1}\sum_{J:\,|J|=j}\sum_{L\supseteq J,\,|L|=\ell}h(L)=\sum_{J\subseteq A,\,|J|\geq 1}(-1)^{|J|-1}\sum_{L\supseteq J,\,|L|=\ell}h(L).

Equivalently,

\sum_{L\subseteq E\setminus A,\ |L|=\ell}h(L)=\sum_{J\subseteq A}(-1)^{|J|}\sum_{L\supseteq J,\ |L|=\ell}h(L).

All terms with
|J|<\ell
vanish by the lower-shadow condition. Only terms with
|J|=\ell
remain, giving

\sum_{L\subseteq E\setminus A,\ |L|=\ell}h(L)=(-1)^{\ell}\sum_{J\subseteq A,\ |J|=\ell}h(J)=(-1)^{\ell}h^{(a)}(A).

Combining this with (
3
) gives the desired relation.
If
(N-a)_{\ell}=0
, then no
b
-subset of
E\setminus A
can carry a nonzero
\ell
-lift in the active cases, and both sides are zero by convention. This proves the claim.
∎

5.3.2
Generated sectors and the coefficient matrices

Let

R=L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{1})+L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{2})+L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{3}).

If
f\perp R
, then
P_{i}f=0
for each
i
, so
S=P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3}
vanishes on
R^{\perp}
. It is therefore enough to analyze
S
on
R
.

For a feasible value
s=|B_{i}|
, write

a_{i}(s)=s+d_{i}.

Fix harmonic degrees
(\ell,m)
. Put

V_{\ell,m}=H_{P}^{\ell}\otimes H_{Q}^{m}.

Let
I_{\ell,m}
be the set of active indices
(i,s)
such that the
(\ell,m)
-layer is present in functions of
(A_{i},B_{i})
, namely

\ell\leq\min(a_{i}(s),p-a_{i}(s)),\qquad m\leq\min(s,q-s).

For
(i,s)\in I_{\ell,m}
,
h\in H_{P}^{\ell}
, and
g\in H_{Q}^{m}
, define

F_{i,s}^{h,g}(A,B)=\mathbf{1}_{\{|B_{i}|=s\}}h^{(a_{i}(s))}(A_{i})g^{(s)}(B_{i}).

Let
R_{\ell,m}
be the span of these functions over all
(i,s)\in I_{\ell,m}
and all
h\otimes g\in V_{\ell,m}
.

Now fix
i\neq j
. Given
A_{i},B_{i}
, row
j
is obtained by choosing

A_{j}\subseteq P\setminus A_{i},\qquad B_{j}\subseteq Q\setminus B_{i},

with sizes
|A_{j}|=a_{j}(t)=t+d_{j}
and
|B_{j}|=t
, where
t
ranges over feasible values. The probability of a given
t
, conditioned on
|B_{i}|=s
, is

q_{i,s}^{j,t}=\frac{\binom{p-a_{i}(s)}{a_{j}(t)}\binom{q-s}{t}}{N_{i}(s)},

where
N_{i}(s)
is the total number of completions after row
i
is frozen. Using Lemma
5.6
for
P
and
Q
gives, for
(j,t)\in I_{\ell,m}
,

P_{i}F_{j,t}^{h,g}=\sum_{s:(i,s)\in I_{\ell,m}}(-1)^{\ell+m}q_{i,s}^{j,t}\frac{(a_{j}(t))_{\ell}}{(p-a_{i}(s))_{\ell}}\frac{(t)_{m}}{(q-s)_{m}}F_{i,s}^{h,g}.

(4)

Also
P_{j}F_{j,t}^{h,g}=F_{j,t}^{h,g}
. Thus, on the generated sector, the operator

S=P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3}

is intertwined with

M=I+(-1)^{\ell+m}C,

where
C
acts on the multiplicity indices
(i,s)\in I_{\ell,m}
, has zero diagonal, and has nonnegative off-diagonal entries

C_{i,s;j,t}=q_{i,s}^{j,t}\frac{(a_{j}(t))_{\ell}}{(p-a_{i}(s))_{\ell}}\frac{(t)_{m}}{(q-s)_{m}},\qquad i\neq j.

(5)

Equivalently, on the full coefficient space
\bigoplus_{(i,s)\in I_{\ell,m}}V_{\ell,m}
, the operator is
M\otimes\Id_{V_{\ell,m}}
.

Lemma 5.7

(Sector decomposition of
R
)
.

The spaces
R_{\ell,m}
form an orthogonal direct sum

R=\bigoplus_{\ell,m}^{\perp}R_{\ell,m}.

Each
R_{\ell,m}
is invariant under every
P_{i}
, and hence under
S=P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3}
. The space
R_{0,0}
is the additive scalar count sector

R_{0,0}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}L^{2}(|B_{i}|),

viewed as a subspace of the full count-dependent functions.

Proof.

For each feasible pair
(i,s)
, define

W_{i,s}=\{\mathbf{1}_{\{|B_{i}|=s\}}\varphi(A_{i},B_{i})\}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i}),

where
\varphi
ranges over functions on pairs
(A_{i},B_{i})
with
|B_{i}|=s
and
|A_{i}|=a_{i}(s)
. Then

L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\bigoplus_{s}W_{i,s},\qquad R=\sum_{i,s}W_{i,s}.

For fixed
(i,s)
, Lemma
5.5
applied to
P
and
Q
gives

W_{i,s}=\bigoplus_{\ell,m}^{\perp}W_{i,s}^{\ell,m},

where
W_{i,s}^{\ell,m}
is the span of the functions
F_{i,s}^{h,g}
. Hence
R=\sum_{\ell,m}R_{\ell,m}
.

It remains to prove orthogonality across different degree pairs. Take generators

F_{i,s}^{h,g}\in W_{i,s}^{\ell,m},\qquad F_{j,t}^{h^{\prime},g^{\prime}}\in W_{j,t}^{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}},

with
(\ell,m)\neq(\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime})
. If
i=j
, then either
s\neq t
, in which case the supports are disjoint, or
s=t
, in which case orthogonality follows from the tensor product of the Johnson decompositions on
P
and
Q
.

Suppose now that
i\neq j
. Since
F_{i,s}^{h,g}
is
\mathcal{F}_{i}
-measurable and
P_{i}
is the orthogonal projection onto
L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
,

\left\langle F_{i,s}^{h,g},F_{j,t}^{h^{\prime},g^{\prime}}\right\rangle=\left\langle F_{i,s}^{h,g},P_{i}F_{j,t}^{h^{\prime},g^{\prime}}\right\rangle.

Formula (
4
), applied to the degree pair
(\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime})
, shows that
P_{i}F_{j,t}^{h^{\prime},g^{\prime}}
is a linear combination of functions
F_{i,s^{\prime}}^{h^{\prime},g^{\prime}}
with the same degree pair
(\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime})
. Only the term
s^{\prime}=s
can have nonzero inner product with
F_{i,s}^{h,g}
, and that inner product is zero by fixed-row Johnson orthogonality because
(\ell,m)\neq(\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime})
. Hence
R_{\ell,m}\perp R_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
.

The same conditional expectation formula proves invariance. If
F_{j,t}^{h,g}\in R_{\ell,m}
, then
P_{j}F_{j,t}^{h,g}=F_{j,t}^{h,g}
, while for
i\neq j
,
P_{i}F_{j,t}^{h,g}
is a linear combination of generators of
R_{\ell,m}
. Thus
P_{i}R_{\ell,m}\subseteq R_{\ell,m}
for every
i
.

Finally, when
(\ell,m)=(0,0)
, the lifted functions are constants on fixed count blocks, so the resulting space is exactly
\sum_{i}L^{2}(|B_{i}|)
. This is contained in the full space of functions of
(|B_{1}|,|B_{2}|,|B_{3}|)
.
∎

Lemma 5.8

(Row-substochastic bound)
.

For every
(\ell,m)\neq(0,0)
, every row sum of
C
is at most
1
. Consequently
\rho(C)\leq 1
.

Proof.

Fix an output index
(i,s)\in I_{\ell,m}
, and let
j,k
be the two rows different from
i
. Conditional on row
i
, the remaining counts satisfy

a_{j}+a_{k}=p-a_{i}(s),\qquad b_{j}+b_{k}=q-s.

The row sum of
C
is at most the same sum extended over all feasible counts of the two remaining rows, namely

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(a_{j})_{\ell}(b_{j})_{m}+(a_{k})_{\ell}(b_{k})_{m}}{(a_{j}+a_{k})_{\ell}(b_{j}+b_{k})_{m}}\ \middle|\ |B_{i}|=s\right],

with the convention that the ratio is zero when the denominator is zero. For active
(i,s)
, this convention is harmless.

For nonnegative integers
x,y,u,v
, and
(\ell,m)\neq(0,0)
,

(x)_{\ell}(u)_{m}+(y)_{\ell}(v)_{m}\leq(x+y)_{\ell}(u+v)_{m}.

The right side counts ordered choices of
\ell
distinct plus labels and
m
distinct minus labels from the union of two parts of sizes
(x,u)
and
(y,v)
. The two terms on the left count only the choices lying entirely in the first part or entirely in the second part. Since at least one of
\ell,m
is positive, these two classes are disjoint subfamilies of the choices counted by the right side. Therefore every row sum is at most
1
, and the standard matrix norm bound gives
\rho(C)\leq 1
.
∎

Lemma 5.9

(Quotient/intertwining lemma)
.

Let
U,W
be finite-dimensional real inner-product spaces. Let
T:U\to W
be a linear map, let
A:W\to W
be a self-adjoint operator preserving
\im T
, and let
M=I+\epsilon C
be an operator on
U
, with
\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}
, such that

AT=TM.

If
\rho(C)\leq 1
, then every eigenvalue of
A
on
\im T
is at most
2
.

Proof.

The relation
AT=TM
implies that
\ker T
is
M
-invariant. Therefore
M
induces an operator
\overline{M}
on the quotient
U/\ker T
, and
T
identifies this quotient with
\im T
. Under this identification,
A|_{\im T}
is exactly
\overline{M}
.

Choose a basis of
U
adapted to
\ker T
. The matrix of
M
is block upper triangular, with one diagonal block giving the action on
\ker T
and the other diagonal block giving
\overline{M}
. Hence every eigenvalue of
\overline{M}
is an eigenvalue of
M
. Since
M=I+\epsilon C
and
\rho(C)\leq 1
, every eigenvalue of
M
has the form
1+\epsilon\mu
with
|\mu|\leq 1
. Because
A
is self-adjoint and
\im T
is invariant, the restriction
A|_{\im T}
is self-adjoint; its eigenvalues are real. Thus, for such an eigenvalue
\lambda
,
\lambda=1+\epsilon\mu
with
|\mu|\leq 1
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, which implies
\lambda\leq 2
.
∎

Proposition 5.10

(Non-scalar harmonic sectors)
.

On every harmonic sector
R_{\ell,m}
with
(\ell,m)\neq(0,0)
, all eigenvalues of

K_{3}=\frac{1}{3}(P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3})

are at most
2/3
.

Proof.

Let

U_{\ell,m}=\bigoplus_{(i,s)\in I_{\ell,m}}V_{\ell,m},

and define
T_{\ell,m}:U_{\ell,m}\to L^{2}
by

T_{\ell,m}(e_{i,s}\otimes(h\otimes g))=F_{i,s}^{h,g}.

By definition,
\im T_{\ell,m}=R_{\ell,m}
. Lemma
5.7
gives that
R_{\ell,m}
is invariant under
S=P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3}
, and
S|_{R_{\ell,m}}
is self-adjoint. Formula (
4
) gives the exact intertwining relation

ST_{\ell,m}=T_{\ell,m}\left((I+(-1)^{\ell+m}C)\otimes\Id_{V_{\ell,m}}\right).

Lemma
5.8
gives
\rho(C)\leq 1
, hence the same spectral-radius bound for
C\otimes\Id_{V_{\ell,m}}
. Applying Lemma
5.9
with
A=S|_{R_{\ell,m}}
shows that every eigenvalue of
S
on
R_{\ell,m}
is at most
2
. Dividing by
3
proves the claim for
K_{3}=S/3
.
∎

5.4
Proof of the three-row inequality

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

The operator
S=P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3}
is self-adjoint. Since

R=L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{1})+L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{2})+L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{3}),

we have
P_{i}f=0
for all
i
whenever
f\perp R
. Thus
K_{3}=S/3
vanishes on
R^{\perp}
, and all nonzero eigenvalues occur inside
R
.

By Lemma
5.7
,

R=\bigoplus_{\ell,m}^{\perp}R_{\ell,m},

and each sector is invariant under
K_{3}
. On the additive scalar count sector
R_{0,0}
, Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
give nonconstant eigenvalues at most
2/3
, because
R_{0,0}
is an invariant subspace of the full count-dependent chain. On every non-scalar sector, Proposition
5.10
gives the same upper bound. The only eigenvalue larger than
2/3
is the constant eigenvalue
1
. Hence
\operatorname{gap}(K_{3})\geq 1/3
.

Finally,

\left\langle f,K_{3}f\right\rangle=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left\langle f,P_{i}f\right\rangle=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left\lVert P_{i}f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

For mean-zero
f
,
\left\lVert P_{i}f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\Var(\mathbb{E}[f\mid\mathcal{F}_{i}])
. The eigenvalue bound
K_{3}\leq(2/3)I
on the mean-zero space is therefore equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Var(\mathbb{E}[f\mid\mathcal{F}_{i}])\leq 2\Var(f).

∎

6
Completion of the Proof of the Main Theorem

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

First assume
m\geq 3
. Fix any triple of rows and condition on all rows outside this triple. The remaining configurations form a feasible
3\times n
fixed-margin state space. Applying Theorem
5.1
with
m=3
, the corresponding row-pair heat-bath chain has spectral gap at least
1/3
. Proposition
2.1
implies that the two-row heat-bath chain is irreducible, because every single swap is contained in the support of the corresponding two-row heat-bath update. Lemma
4.1
, applied with
M=m
, gives

\gamma_{H}\geq\binom{m}{2}^{-1}.

Then Lemma
3.1
gives

\gamma_{\rm sw}\geq\binom{n}{2}^{-1}\gamma_{H}\geq\binom{m}{2}^{-1}\binom{n}{2}^{-1}.

For
m=2
, the row-pair heat-bath update is a one-step perfect sampler and therefore has spectral gap
1
, so Lemma
3.1
yields

\gamma_{\rm sw}\geq\binom{n}{2}^{-1}=\binom{2}{2}^{-1}\binom{n}{2}^{-1}.

The mixing-time estimate follows from the standard reversible chain bound (Theorem 12.4 of
17
)

t_{\rm mix}(\varepsilon)\leq\gamma_{\rm sw}^{-1}\left(\log\frac{1}{\pi_{\min}}+\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right),

with
\pi_{\min}=1/|\Omega(r,c)|
, and from
|\Omega(r,c)|\leq 2^{mn}
.
∎
