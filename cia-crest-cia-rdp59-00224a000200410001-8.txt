Director of Logistics

Comment on H. R. 4933 is as follows:

1. This bill which was introduced in the House would permit State and local
Governments to tax the private Interest in property, work in process, and inven-
tories acquired by the Federal Government but in the possession of contractors
with the Federal Government.

2. The Office of Logistics considers that this proposed legislation will
definitely increase the cost to the Government of obtaining supplies and services
by contract* It is believed that H.R. 4933 will Inject a great deal of uncertainty
in the field of State and local taxation of Government contracts.

3. The typical Government cost-plus-fixed-fee contract using a prescribed
Armed Services Procurement Regulation property clause provides for the early
passage of title to the Government on personal property, supplies and mete rials
acquired by the contractor for use on the contract. Heretofore the question of
Government title in the material or property has been the deciding factor as to
whether or not local taxes could be assessed. H.R. 4933 appears to disregard
the question of title and establishes in lieu thereof, a new criteria measured by
the amount due and unpaid or to become due to the manufacturer under the contract.
Stated in other words, the private property interest of the Government contractor
is to be measured not by passage of title but by the amount due the contractor.

4. It is believed that H.R. 4933 would introduce a new concept of private
property interest in Government property in the possession of Government con-
tractors and would necessarily increase the cost to the Federal Government by
permitting State and local Governments to tax Government property. To this
extent, therefore, the Office of Logistics objects to the proposed legislation.

Sec. 2. The Congress declares that it is the policy of
the Federal Government in carrying out the national pro-
gram of military security and national defense to avoid
insofar as feasible the impairment of the finances of State
and local government through the contracting for, or acquisi-
tion or ownership of, military products, and inventories and
supplies.

The Congress further declares that in the activities
referred to in the preceding paragraph the Federal Govern-
ment does not purpose or intend to render exempt or im-
mune to taxation private interests in the property referred to.

Sec. 4. Whenever the Federal Government shall ac-
quire title to artillery, ordnance, or other products, or the
materials or supplies for manufacture thereof under contract
for the manufacture of products for military security or the
national defense, consent is hereby granted to all State and
local governments to tax such manufacturers their portion
of private property interest in such artillery, ordnance, and
other products and in the said materials and supplies.

Sec. 5. Any taxes of the kind in section 4 above pro-
vided for, heretofore levied or collected are hereby fully au-
thorized and validated.

Sec. 6. Insofar as the provisions of any other law are in-
consistent with the provisions of this Act, the provisions of
this Act shall be controlling.

Sec. 7. If any provisions of this Act or the application
thereof to any person or circumstances is held invalid, the
remainder of this Act and the application of such provisions
to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected
thereby.

Sec. 8. This Act shall become effective on the date of
its enactment.
