Poster 16. Changes in Coronal Plane Alignment After Transphyseal ACL Reconstruction Performed Within 2 Years of Skeletal Maturity: Results From the PLUTO Study Group
Authors: Joshua T. Bram, Meghan Whalen, Farah Subrina, Grace Saville, Dai Sugimoto, Daniel W. Green, Theodore J. Ganley, John Lawrence, Crystal Perkins, Michael Busch, Andrew Pennock, Henry Chambers, Eric Edmonds, Frank A. Cordasco, Christian Anderson, Benton E. Heyworth, Matthew Matava, Lyle Micheli, Matthew D. Milewski, Jeffrey J. Nepple, Shital N. Parikh, Paul Saluan, Kevin Shea, Eric Wall, Samuel Willimon, Mininder S. Kocher, Peter D. Fabricant
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:For patients with open physes approaching skeletal maturity (i.e. <=2 years of remaining growth), transphyseal anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction (ACLR) with soft tissue autograft is often the technique of choice. While generally considered low risk for iatrogenic growth disturbance, angular deformities and limb length discrepancies (LLD) have been reported due to physeal injury in small series. However, there is limited prospective data quantifying post-operative coronal plane angular changes after ACLR in this population. Therefore, this study sought to evaluate post-operative changes in lower extremity coronal plane alignment 1-year after transphyseal ACLR in skeletally immature patients with less than 2 years of growth remaining at the time of surgery. We hypothesized that while some minor changes may be observed, there would be no clinically relevant changes in alignment or limb length necessitating further intervention.Methods:This was a multicenter prospective cohort study of skeletally immature patients with <=2 years of remaining growth (skeletal age 12-14 for females and 14-16 for males) who underwent a primary transphyseal, all soft-tissue autograft ACL reconstruction. All patients had a documented skeletal age assessment within 3 months of surgery and confirmed open physes on pre-operative knee MRI. Additionally, patients had both pre- and 1-year post-operative standing alignment radiographs. Pre-operative physical examination data was used to confirm knee extension to within 5°in order to ensure accurate determination of radiographic coronal plane alignment. Individuals with genetic or neuromuscular syndromes, multi-ligamentous injuries, prior knee pathologies potentially affecting growth (e.g. congenital ACL deficiency, tibial spine fracture, osteochondritis dissecans, patellar instability), or those undergoing hemi-epiphysiodesis were excluded. The change in the difference in alignment parameters between the involved and uninvolved extremities from pre- to 1-year post-operative was calculated (difference of differences = D2). This outcome was chosen, as opposed solely to the difference in radiographic findings between the injured and uninjured limbs at either time point, to account for naturally occurring alignment changes during growth. Radiographic measurements assessed included the mechanical axis deviation (zones), anatomic lateral distal femoral angle (aLDFA), mechanical medial proximal tibial angle (mMPTA), posterior tibial slope (PTS), and leg length discrepancy (LLD). Clinically relevant D2 values included >=5° for aLDFA, mMPTA, and PTS, >=1cm for LLD, and >=1 zone for MAD (zones). Descriptive results were calculated as median and interquartile range (IQR) based on normality assessment. Wilcoxon signed rank sum tests were used to compare the involved and uninvolved limbs both pre- and post-operatively.Results:A total of 99 patients (50 males, 49 females) met inclusion criteria (Table 1) with median chronological age of 14.1 years (IQR 13.5-15.0) and median skeletal age of 14.0 (IQR 13.0-15.0). Reconstructions were performed with either hamstrings (44%) or quadriceps (56%) autograft with median graft diameter of 9.0mm (IQR 8.5-9.5). Fifteen patients (15%) also underwent a concomitant lateral extra-articular augmentation procedure. Post-operative alignment radiographs were obtained at a median 12.3 months (IQR 11.8-13.2). Pre-operatively, the median aLDFA was lower in the injured limb (80° vs 81°, p=0.003, Table 2), which persisted at 1-year post-operative follow-up (80° vs 81°, p<0.001, Table 3). The median D2 values in mechanical axis deviation (0 zones, IQR -1 to 0), aLDFA (-1°, IQR -3 to 2), mMPTA (0°, IQR -1 to 3), and LLD (0.2cm, IQR -0.2 to 0.7) were small for each measure (Table 4). However, 32% of patients demonstrated a >=1 zone D2 in mechanical axis, while the D2 for aLDFA and mMPTA changed by >=5° in 16% and 14% of patients, respectively. Additionally, the posterior tibial slope changed by >=5° in 14% of patients. There were no patients that developed a LLD >=1cm. Furthermore, no patient developed growth-related complications requiring intervention including for LLD, angular deformity, or growth arrest.Conclusions:This prospective multicenter study demonstrated minimal changes in coronal plane alignment 1-year post-operatively for skeletally immature individuals undergoing transphyseal all-soft tissue autograft ACLR with <=2 years of remaining growth. No patients required intervention for angular deformity or LLD 1 year after surgery. Nonetheless, a meaningful subset of 15-30% of patients demonstrated clinically relevant changes in alignment parameters. Routine pre-operative and post-operative radiographic surveillance in this population is therefore warranted until skeletal maturity.
