Final Technical Report
                               Project DE‐FG02‐08ER54954
 “Gyrokinetic Simulation of Tokamak Fusion Plasmas”

 Prepared October 9, 2025

This is the final technical report for project DE‐FG02‐08ER54954 “Gyrokinetic Simulation of
Tokamak Fusion Plasmas”. This report covers the entire period of the sponsored project. This
grant supported in part the salary, travel, and research supplies for the principal investigator Scott
E. Parker, graduate research associate Stefan Tirkas, who is a Ph.D. graduate student in the
Physics Department. Postdoctoral Scholar Dr. Neeraj Kumar is also collaborating on this project.
The main goals of the project are to investigate the nonlinear saturation of electron scale
turbulence using gyrokinetic theory and simulation and to investigate impurity transport in the
edge pedestal region.

Accomplishments
We have successfully run GENE simulations with a single unstable electron temperature gradient
mode and the n=0 component to compare to our theory of intermediate scale zonal flow
generation [H. Chen, S. Tirkas, S, Parker, Nuclear Fusion 61 066017 (2021)]. Good qualitative
agreement is obtained where zonal flow is generated and suppression of the "pump" is observed.
We have also successfully completed full spectrum simulations showing the behavior of the
saturation amplitude and zonal flow generation versus collisionality, again, agreeing with the
theory. We studied particle transport, including Carbon impurity, for inter‐ELM DIII‐D edge
pedestal plasmas. We studied ion scale turbulence with has ion‐temperature‐gradient‐(ITG)
driven, trapped‐electron, and micro‐tearing mode instabilities present. ITG is the dominant
contributor to particle transport. This is in collaboration with DIII‐D scientists. We have
developed a workflow, including nonlinear gyrokinetic simulation, linear analysis with quasi‐
linear estimates of the fluxes, and neoclassical calculations. We use realistic profiles and
magnetic equilibria obtained from experimental discharges.

Professional Development
We educated Ph.D. physics graduate student, Stefan Tirkas, in fusion simulation, theory, and
modeling. We are also mentored a very early career postdoctoral scholar, Dr. Neeraj Kumar, and
undergraduate physics Major, Sophie Redd and Calder Haubrich, who will help support our
modeling efforts.

Impact on Principle Discipline
Research carried out under this project is in direct support of tokamak fusion energy. The focus
is on how good confinement in the core region is impacted by edge plasma and particle transport
of the electrons, main ions, and impurities.

Impact on other Disciplines
There is an impact on computational mathematics and quantum information sciences through
synergies and collaborative work carried out between the funded projects of this research group.

Development of Human Resources
This research group at the University of Colorado involves physics Ph.D. graduate students,
undergraduate students as hourly research assistants and postdoctoral scholars. This research
project mainly involved graduate student Stefan Tirkas and Dr. Neeraj Kumar. We also had an
undergraduates involved in documenting and running our turbulent transport analysis workflow.

Impact on Infrastructure
The software development and documentation that is done within this project builds the software
infrastructure within the fusion energy sciences program. These codes are made available to the
broader national and international magnetic fusion research community.
