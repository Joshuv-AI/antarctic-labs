 E2C Technical Assistance Opportunities

 In-Depth Partnerships ~3 years
 Multiyear partnership made up of teams (local governments, community-based organizations, and electric utilities)
 that work alongside national laboratory staff to apply robust modeling and analysis tools and simulate hardware in
 conditions that match communities' unique contexts to evaluate potential scenarios and strategies before full
 technology deployment.
 Supports nine communities currently.

 Peer-Learning Cohorts ~6 months
 Six months of regular convenings where cohorts of up to 15 participants exchange strategies and best practices, learn
 in a collaborative environment, and workshop plans and strategies to overcome challenges relating to a similar energy
 topic. Eligibility varies by cohort topic.
 Supports about 100 communities per year.

 Expert Match ~3–6 months
 Short-term, no-cost technical assistance for communities seeking to answer near-term energy questions.
 Eligible entities include local governments, utilities, community-based organizations, schools, and nonprofits.
 Supports about 200 communities per year. Applications are accepted all year on a rolling basis.
 www.nlr.gov/e2c

9 Clean Cities and Communities

 Clean Communities of Central
 New York and Ithaca, NY:
 Community Outreach Coordinator for
 Transportation Community Engagement
 and Planning
 Collaboration between the city and the coalition
 strengthened relationships with the local organizations
 and residents and identified action items for the city of
 Ithaca. The coalition identified nine distinct
 transportation goals through 17 interviews with
 leaders from local nonprofit organizations. This
 informed transportation goals in the Ithaca-Thompkins
 County Transportation Council’s 2045 Long-Range
 Transportation Plan.

 The coalition had someone on the ground in a satellite
 location, which enabled in-person engagement with
 previously hard-to-reach leaders and organizations.
10 Clean Cities and Communities

 Columbia Willamette Clean
 Cities and Vancouver, WA:
 Resourcing Local Nonprofit
 Organizations for Community
 Electrification

 Convened local leaders in a capacity-building
 incubator, the Transportation Electrification Cohort, to
 co-design transportation electrification trainings,
 develop training materials, and train community
 experts. Co-hosted 12 community engagement
 events gathering input from 403 people to inform
 Vancouver’s 2025 city plan.

 The coalition developed a train-the-trainer model that
 created cascading impacts in the long term and
 allowed trusted leaders to share information about EVs
 in their own circles.
11 Clean Cities and Communities

 Drive Clean Colorado and
 Fort Collins Utilities:
 Engagement to Assess e-Mobility
 for Fort Collins Residents
 Developed a community advisory group to co-design
 and execute engagement with residents to inform a
 transportation electrification needs assessment.
 Held four events gathering input from 183 people.
 Directly led to the purchase of two EVs by a local
 business and the installation of 16 water heaters in
 the homes of residents.

 Partnership between the city, utility, and the coalition
 leveraged relationships with local nonprofit
 organizations and met residents’ peripheral needs to
 build long-term trust.

12 Clean Cities and Communities

 East Tennessee Clean
 Fuels and SEEED, Inc.:
 Teaching Electrification and Energy
 Efficiency for Community Harmony
 Led a “bootcamp” workshop series for local
 leaders to learn and build capacity around
 transportation electrification. Identified nine
 goals for SEEED to pursue for future program
 development.

 The coalition incorporated bidirectional
 engagement during presentation-style
 workshops to gather insights on local priorities.

13 Clean Cities and Communities

 Illinois Alliance for Clean
 Transportation and Bronzeville
 Community Development
 Partnership:
 Historic Motor Row and Electric Avenue:
 Empowering Bronzeville's Mobility Future
 Through new partnerships with 14 local nonprofit
 organizations and input from 150 people, the coalition
 gathered input to co-design transportation goals and
 solutions for Bronzeville Community Development
 Partnership’s recently acquired EV Institute.

 The coalition leveraged relationships with trusted
 community leaders and organizations to drive greater
 impact and engagement in neighborhoods where they
 historically haven't had as much success with
 outreach and engagement.
14 Clean Cities and Communities

 Maine Clean Communities
 and Greater Portland
 Council of Governments:
 Regional EV Plan—Charging the
 Region
 Developed a regional EV charging plan
 informed by input from 340 people through
 public, stakeholder, and municipal
 engagement. This led to 33 new partnerships
 with local nonprofit organizations and private
 industry.

 Intentional, goal-focused working groups
 paired with larger public engagement efforts
 led to the development of a regional charging
 plan that involved 33 partners and nearly
 every municipality in the region— the
 coalition convened a range of people and
 organizations with shared goals.
15 Clean Cities and Communities

 Pittsburgh Region Clean
 Cities and Centre County, PA:
 Let’s Talk Electric Mobility:
 Rural Engagement and Needs
 Assessment for EV Readiness Plan
 Through three workshops and 34 interviews, the
 coalition gathered input from 41 rural residents to
 inform the EV Infrastructure Readiness Plan for
 Centre County, Pennsylvania. Outcomes from the
 workshops were shared publicly and with the state
 department of transportation. The coalition also
 provided one local business an EV site
 assessment.

 Transparent data sharing can enable project
 continuity — the coalition made it easy for
 someone else to pick up where this project left off.

16 Clean Cities and Communities

 Silicon Valley Clean
 Cities and San Jose, CA:
 Transportation Electrification
 Engagement in San Jose, CA
 Through three public events, 20 stakeholder
 interviews, and a survey, the coalition gathered
 input in English, Spanish, and Vietnamese from
 3,650 people to inform novel incentive structures
 and transportation programs.

 A summary of the coalition's engagement effort
 was submitted as part of the City of San Jose's
 2025–2026 budget proposal and outlined new
 program offerings to address community priorities
 through a $14 million investment. Insights from
 engagement directly informed the development of
 new programs and budget requests to support
 these programs.
17 Clean Cities and Communities

 Triangle Clean Cities
 and Durham, NC:
 Durham EV Ride
 Through one public event and a survey, the
 coalition gathered input from 84 people to inform
 electric vehicle charger deployment in Durham,
 North Carolina. As a result, the city of Durham
 installed its first public EV charger in November
 2025.

 Early partnership with Durham enabled rapid
 implementation after engagement concluded; the
 coalition did not need to seek buy-in for
 implementation after conducting engagement
 activities.

18 Clean Cities and Communities

 Vermont Clean
 Communities and Stowe
 Electric Department:
 EV Readiness and Charging
 Roadmap
 Through a series of three listening sessions, one
 public event, and a survey, the coalition gathered input
 from 63 people and developed an EV Readiness and
 Charging Roadmap for Stowe, prioritized locations for
 EV charger installation, and supported the
 development of the 2026 Stowe Town Plan.

 The coalition’s community engagement included a
 mapping activity which was easily integrated into the
 federal Joint Office of Energy and
 Transportation's Public EV Charging Station Site
 Selection Checklist process. This enabled the
 coalition to create a map of preferred locations that
 considered both local priorities and technical feasibility.
19 Clean Cities and Communities

 Yellowstone-Teton Clean
 Cities and Missoula, MT:
 Incorporating Public Priorities for
 EVs within Missoula and
 throughout Montana
 Through six public events, two listening
 sessions with local leaders, and a survey,
 the coalition gathered input from 350
 people, which led to the development of
 an EV Readiness Plan for the city of
 Missoula.

 The City of Missoula's clear goals for the
 engagement activities meant that the
 insights gathered by the coalition were all
 directly relevant to and informed the EV
 Readiness Plan.
