Finite difference methods for a continuous-time heterogeneous agent model with recursive utility
Yves Achdou, Qing Tang

Abstract
We propose, analyze and test computational methods for solving a continuous-time heterogenous agent model with Epstein-Zin utility. Such recursive utilities allow the model to disentangle between risk aversion and intertemporal substitution. Having discretized the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation arising in the model, we propose a Howard-Newton algorithm for the late resolution preference case, and a Howard-Tarski-Kantorovich algorithm for the early resolution preference case. We prove the convergence of the iterative algorithms. We obtain as a consequence the existence of solutions to the discretized HJB equations. In the late resolution case, we supply a priori estimates between the unique solutions of the continuous and discretized HJB equations. Keywords: Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation, Mean Field Games, recursive utility, finite difference, iterative algorithms

Introduction

In this work, we propose, analyze and test computational methods for solving the continuous-time heterogeneous agent (HA) models with recursive utility, recently analyzed by the authors in
[
4
]
. The continuous-time formulation of the Aiyagari-Bewley-Huggett models
[
17
]
, classical in recursive macroeconomics, and the related system of partial differential equations can be studied in the light of the mathematical theory of Mean Field Games (cf.
[
1
,
2
,
15
]
). Such models involve a large number of
ex ante
identical but
ex post
heterogeneous agents in an incomplete market setting: each agent continuously optimizes consumption subject to a borrowing constraint and idiosyncratic income risk, resulting in a non-degenerate stationary wealth distribution (cf.
[
4
, (1.1)]
). The Mean Field Game system consists of Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equations and Fokker–Planck–Kolmogorov (FPK) equations coupled through equilibrium conditions on prices. HA models have now become indispensable for quantifying the distributional effects of fiscal and monetary policy. A finite difference method for the constant relative risk aversion (CRRA) utility case was developed in
[
3
]
and its numerical appendix. This method from
[
3
]
has then become popular in the HA macro literature, as the first step for computing stationary equilibria
[
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
. The present paper aims at a rigorous analysis of a related finite difference method for models with recursive utility (Epstein-Zin). In particular, we propose convergent iterative algorithms for solving the discretized Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations.

The Epstein-Zin recursive utility is defined as follows (cf.
[
21
]
):

f(c,v)=\frac{\rho}{1-\psi^{-1}}\frac{c^{1-\psi^{-1}}-((1-\gamma)v)^{\theta}}{((1-\gamma)v)^{\theta-1}},\hskip 20.00003pt\theta=\frac{1-\psi^{-1}}{1-\gamma},

(1.1)

where
c
is the consumption,
v
is the value function of an optimal strategy and
\rho
is the subjective discount rate. It is assumed that
\psi
, the elasticity of intertemporal substitution (EIS) and
\gamma
, the risk aversion parameter, are both positive and do not take the value
1
. A key feature of the Epstein-Zin utility (
1.1
) is the separation between risk aversion and EIS. The time-additive separable CRRA utility is a special case of recursive utility in which
\gamma=\psi^{-1}
. The attitude towards the timing of the resolution of uncertainty is pinned down by the constant
\gamma\psi
: early (resp. late) resolution is preferred if
\gamma\psi>(resp.<)1
, cf.
[
10
]
. With
\gamma=\psi^{-1}
, the agent is indifferent to the timing of uncertainty resolution.

The Mean Field Game system of partial differential equations for an Aiyagari model with the recursive utility

1.1

[

\left\{\begin{aligned} \quad&(i)\qquad&&0=\max_{c\geq 0}\left\{f(c,v_{j})+(r^{*}x+y_{j}-c)Dv_{j}(x)\right\}+\lambda_{j}(v_{\bar{\jmath}}(x)-v_{j}(x)),\\
&&&c_{j}^{*}(x)=\mathop{\arg\max}\limits_{c\geq 0}\left\{f(c,v_{j})+(rx+y_{j}-c)Dv_{j}(x)\right\},\\

&(iii_{A})\qquad&&r^{*}=A\alpha\left(\frac{\mathcal{K}[r^{*}]}{N}\right)^{\alpha-1}-\delta=A\alpha\left(\frac{K[m^{(r^{*})}]}{N}\right)^{\alpha-1}-\delta.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

The HJB equations

(a borrowing limit) is imposed to prevent negative savings at

\mu_{j}

is determined by the fixed-point condition

coupling

To solve the state constrained HJB equation (
i

, we use monotone (upwind) finite difference schemes, as in
[
1
,
3
,
8
,
16
,
20
]
. The main difficulties in this model are: firstly, the Hamiltonian takes infinite values for negative momentum; secondly (this is specific to the recursive utility) the dependence of
f
on
v
. The second difficulty implies that one should use iterative algorithms that differs from the standard Howard algorithms used for example in
[
3
,
8
]
.
A main discovery of the present paper is that different strategies are suitable depending on the timing preference in the models. In the late resolution case
\theta\geq 1
, the system satisfies a comparison principle, and we use a Howard-Newton algorithm to solve for the unique solution. In this method, the policy evaluation step consists of an inner loop of Newton iterations. In the early resolution case
0<\theta<1
, we consider a solution to the HJB equation as a fixed point of a suitably defined map
\Gamma
, and take advantage of the monotonicity and invariance properties of
\Gamma
to design a Howard-Tarski-Kantorovich algorithm. We have decided to name the method so because the design of the outer loop is reminiscent of Tarski-Kantorovich fixed point theorem and its proof in
[
13
, Theorem I, p.68]
. We prove the convergence of these iterative algorithms, and this also implies the existence of solutions to the discretized HJB equations. The Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equations will then be discretized with a finite difference scheme, exploiting the duality structure of
Eq.

1.2
(cf.
[
3
]
).

The methodology for studying the case
0<\theta<1
in the present paper is related to the recent literature about dynamic programming on ordered spaces and Koopman operators, e.g.
[
7
,
18
,
19
]
. In these works, the Tarski-Kantorovich fixed point theorem have been used to study discrete time Bellman equations with recursive utility. In the present paper, we show that the barriers and invariance properties, essential for using this method, can be constructed for the upwind schemes that we propose.

We analyze the convergence of the numerical solution to the constrained viscosity solution of the HJB equation, in the case of
\theta\geq 1
. Recently, a Semi-Lagrangian method was proposed in
[
9
]
to study continuous-time HA models with CRRA utility, with convergence analysis based on the Barles-Souganidis half-relaxed limits method. In the present paper, we obtain error estimates with a technique that does not require doubling the variables, thanks to the strong regularity of the solutions to
Eq.

1.2

(i)
.

3.1
The numerical schemes

Given
{\underline{x}}\leq 0<\bar{x}
,
I\in\mathbb{N}
and a step size
\Delta x=(\bar{x}-{\underline{x}})/I
, let us define the grid
\mathcal{G}^{\Delta x}=\{x_{i}:\,i\in\mathbb{N},\,i=0,\cdots,I,\,x_{i}={\underline{x}}+i\Delta x\}
. For two grid functions
U_{j}
and
V_{j}
defined on
\mathcal{G}^{\Delta x}
, we use
U_{j}\preceq V_{j}
(resp.
U_{j}\succeq V_{j}
) to denote the ordering
U_{i,j}\leq V_{i,j}
(resp.
U_{i,j}\geq V_{i,j}
) for all
i
. If
U=(U_{1},U_{2})
and
V=(V_{1},V_{2})
, then
U\preceq V
(resp.
U\succeq V
) means
U_{i,j}\leq V_{i,j}
(resp.
U_{i,j}\geq V_{i,j}
) for all
i,j
.

For a Hamiltonian strictly convex w.r.t. the
p-
variable, we set

\displaystyle{H}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right)=\min_{p}H\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},p\right),

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\text{and}}\hskip 10.00002pt{p}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right)=\mathop{\arg\min}_{p}H\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},p\right).

The discrete Hamiltonian is defined by

\displaystyle\bm{H}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{F},\bm{p}^{B}\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\,&{H}^{\uparrow}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{F}\right)\,&&\text{if}\,\,x_{i}={\underline{x}},\\
&{H}^{\uparrow}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{F}\right)+{H}^{\downarrow}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{B}\right)-{H}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right)\,&&\text{if}\,\,{\underline{x}}<x_{i}<\bar{x},\\
&{H}^{\downarrow}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{B}\right)\,&&\text{if}\,\,x_{i}=\bar{x}.\end{aligned}\right.

where

{H}^{\uparrow}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{F}\right)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \quad&H\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{F}\right)\quad&&\text{if }\quad\bm{p}^{F}\geq{p}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right),\\
&{H}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right)\quad&&\text{if }\quad\bm{p}^{F}<{p}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right),\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

and

{H}^{\downarrow}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{B}\right)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \quad&H\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{B}\right)\quad&&\text{if }\quad 0\leq\bm{p}^{B}\leq{p}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right),\\
&{H}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right)\quad&&\text{if }\quad\bm{p}^{B}>{p}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right),\,\,\\
&+\infty\quad&&\text{if }\quad\bm{p}^{B}<0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

Lemma 3.1
.

The numerical Hamiltonian
\bm{H}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{F},\bm{p}^{B}\right)
is increasing in
\bm{p}^{F}
and decreasing in
\bm{p}^{B}
. Moreover,

{H}^{\downarrow}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{B}\right)\leq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{if }\hskip 10.00002pt\bm{p}^{B}\geq 0.

(3.5)

Proof.

From the strict convexity of
H\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},p\right)
w.r.t.
p
and
Eq.

3.1
, we deduce for each
(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j})
,
H\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\cdot\right)
is increasing in the domain
[{p}_{\min},+\infty)
and decreasing in
[0,{p}_{\min})
.
∎

In the design of Howard type algorithms, it will be crucial to take advantage of the following relationship between the discrete Hamiltonian and the utility function
Eq.

2.4
:

s^{F}_{i,j}=rx_{i}+y_{j}-c^{F}_{i,j},\hskip 10.00002pts^{B}_{i,j}=rx_{i}+y_{j}-c^{B}_{i,j},

(3.6)

\displaystyle{H}^{\uparrow}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{F}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{c^{F}_{i,j}\in[0,rx_{i}+y_{j}]}\left\{s^{F}_{i,j}\bm{p}^{F}+\mathcal{F}\left(c^{F}_{i,j},V_{i,j}\right)\right\},

(3.7)

\displaystyle{H}^{\downarrow}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j},\bm{p}^{B}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{c^{B}_{i,j}\geq rx_{i}+y_{j}}\left\{s^{B}_{i,j}\bm{p}^{B}+\mathcal{F}\left(c^{B}_{i,j},V_{i,j}\right)\right\}.

Next, we denote the consumption associated to null savings by:

\bar{c}_{j}(x_{i})=rx_{i}+y_{j}.

(3.8)

A straightforward computation yields

{H}_{\min}\left(x_{i},y_{j},V_{i,j}\right)=\mathcal{F}\left(\bar{c}_{j}(x_{i}),V_{i,j}\right).

We denote the forward and backward finite difference operators by

\Delta^{+}{V_{i,j}}=\frac{V_{i+1,j}-V_{i,j}}{\Delta x},\,\,\forall\,\,0\leq i<I\,\,{\text{and}}\,\,\Delta^{-}{V_{i,j}}=\frac{V_{i,j}-V_{i-1,j}}{\Delta x},\,\,\forall\,\,0<i\leq I.

(3.9)

For a function
v
defined on
[{\underline{x}},+\infty)
, we denote for
x\in\mathcal{G}^{\Delta x}
,

\displaystyle\Delta^{+}{v(x)}=\frac{v(x+\Delta x)-v(x)}{\Delta x},\,\,{\rm{if}}\,\,{\underline{x}}<\bar{x},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\Delta^{-}{v(x)}=\frac{v(x)-v(x-\Delta x)}{\Delta x},\,\,{\rm{if}}\,\,{\underline{x}}<x\leq\bar{x}.

We can set
\Delta^{+}{V_{I,j}}
,
\Delta^{-}{V_{0,j}}
,
\Delta^{+}{v(\bar{x})}
,
\Delta^{-}{v({\underline{x}})}
to arbitrary constants since their values have no importance in what follows..

2.2
is, for

\left\{\begin{aligned} \quad&\frac{\rho}{\theta}V_{i,1}=\bm{H}\left(x_{i},y_{1},V_{i,1},\Delta^{+}{V_{i,1}},\Delta^{-}{V_{i,1}}\right)+\lambda_{1}\left(V_{i,2}-V_{i,1}\right),\\
&\frac{\rho}{\theta}V_{i,2}=\bm{H}\left(x_{i},y_{2},V_{i,2},\Delta^{+}{V_{i,2}},\Delta^{-}{V_{i,2}}\right)+\lambda_{2}\left(V_{i,1}-V_{i,2}\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.11)

Observe that by design of

in

and

and

&c^{B,*}_{0,j}=r{\underline{x}}+y_{j},\quad c^{F,*}_{I,j}=r\bar{x}+y_{j}.\end{aligned}\right.

\lambda_{j^{*}}({U}_{i^{*},\bar{\jmath}^{*}}-{U}_{i^{*},j^{*}})\leq\lambda_{j^{*}}({V}_{i^{*},\bar{\jmath}^{*}}-{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}).

(5.2)

Since

{U}_{i^{*}+1,j^{*}}-{V}_{i^{*}+1,j^{*}}\leq{U}_{i^{*},j^{*}}-{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}},\hskip 10.00002pt{U}_{i^{*}-1,j^{*}}-{V}_{i^{*}-1,j}\leq{U}_{i^{*},j^{*}}-{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}},

we infer that

\Delta^{+}{{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}}\geq\Delta^{+}{{U}_{i^{*},j^{*}}}
,
\Delta^{-}{{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}}\leq\Delta^{-}{{U}_{i^{*},j^{*}}}
. From
Section

3.1
we deduce

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{\varepsilon}\left(x_{i^{*}},y_{j^{*}},\widetilde{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}},\Delta^{+}{{U}_{i^{*},j^{*}}},\Delta^{-}{{U}_{i^{*},j^{*}}}\right)

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{\varepsilon}\left(x_{i^{*}},y_{j^{*}},\widetilde{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}},\Delta^{+}{{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}},\Delta^{-}{{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}}\right).

and

\frac{\rho}{\theta}{U}_{i^{*},j^{*}}\leq\bm{H}_{\varepsilon}\left(x_{i},y_{j},\widetilde{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}},\Delta^{+}{{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}},\Delta^{-}{{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}}\right)+\lambda_{j^{*}}({U}_{i^{*},\bar{\jmath}^{*}}-{U}_{i^{*},j^{*}}).

(5.4)

Since
{V}
is a supersolution,

\frac{\rho}{\theta}{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}\geq\bm{H}_{\varepsilon}\left(x_{i},y_{j},\widetilde{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}},\Delta^{+}{{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}},\Delta^{-}{{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}}\right)+\lambda_{j^{*}}({V}_{i^{*},\bar{\jmath}^{*}}-{V}_{i^{*},j^{*}}).

(5.5)

Subtracting
Eq.

5.5
from
Eq.

5.4
and using
Eq.

5.2
, we get
\rho\delta/\theta\leq 0
, a contradiction.
∎

We observe that, since the value
\widetilde{V}_{i,j}
is fixed in
Eq.

5.1
,
Section

5
holds for all
\theta>0
. A major difficulty in the case
\theta<1
is that
Section

4.1
may not hold. Therefore, we cannot directly use the comparison principle in
Section

4.1
and obtain
\check{U}\preceq{V}\preceq\check{V}
, for all solution
V
to
Eq.

3.11
, as in the case
\theta\geq 1
. However, the following results will allow us to look for a solution
V
such that
\check{U}\preceq{V}\preceq\check{V}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

We have the barrier properties

(i).

If
({U}_{1},{U}_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\check{U}_{1},\check{U}_{2})
, then

{U}\succeq\check{U}.

(ii).

If
({V}_{1},{V}_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\check{V}_{1},\check{V}_{2})
, then

{V}\preceq\check{V}.

Proof.

(i). If
\widetilde{V}_{i,j}=\check{U}_{i,j}
,
(\check{U}_{1},\check{U}_{2})
itself is a subsolution to the HJB equation
Eq.

5.1
. From
Section

5
,
{U}_{i,j}\geq\check{U}_{i,j}
.

(ii). Similarly, if
\widetilde{V}_{i,j}=\check{V}_{i,j}
then
(\check{V}_{1},\check{V}_{2})
itself is a supersolution to the HJB equation
Eq.

5.1
. From
Section

5
,
{V}_{i,j}\leq\check{V}_{i,j}
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

The map
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
is monotone: if
(U_{1},U_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{U}_{1},\widetilde{U}_{2})
,
(V_{1},V_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{V}_{1},\widetilde{V}_{2})
and
\check{U}\preceq\widetilde{U}\preceq\widetilde{V}\preceq\check{V}
, then
{U}\preceq{V}
.

Proof.

From
\check{U}\preceq\widetilde{U}\preceq\widetilde{V}\preceq\check{V}
and
Section

3.2
, we know for all
(i,j)
,

\bm{H}_{\varepsilon}\left(x_{i},y_{j},\widetilde{U}_{i,j},\Delta^{+}{{U}_{i,j}},\Delta^{-}{{U}_{i,j}}\right)\leq\bm{H}_{\varepsilon}\left(x_{i},y_{j},\widetilde{V}_{i,j},\Delta^{+}{{U}_{i,j}},\Delta^{-}{{U}_{i,j}}\right),

hence
(U_{1},U_{2})
is a subsolution to the discrete HJB system
(V_{1},V_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{V}_{1},\widetilde{V}_{2})
.
Section

5
yields the desired result.
∎

We deduce the following invariance principle for
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, analogous to
Section

2
.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
\check{U}\preceq\widetilde{V}\preceq\check{V}
and
V=(V_{1},V_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{V}_{1},\widetilde{V}_{2})
, then
\check{U}\preceq{V}\preceq\check{V}
.

Proof.

Let us denote
\widehat{V}=(\widehat{V}_{1},\widehat{V}_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\check{V}_{1},\check{V}_{2})
. From
Section

5
and
\widetilde{V}\preceq\check{V}
,
V\preceq\widehat{V}
. From
Section

5
we obtain
V\preceq\widehat{V}\preceq\check{V}
. The proof for
\check{U}\preceq{V}
is similar.
∎

Definition 5.5
.

The grid function
V^{\varepsilon}
is a solution to
Eq.

3.16
if the equations are satisfied and
\check{U}\preceq V^{\varepsilon}\preceq\check{V}
. We say that
\underline{V}^{\varepsilon}
is the minimal solution of
Eq.

3.16
if it is a solution and
\underline{V}^{\varepsilon}\preceq V^{\varepsilon}
for all
V^{\varepsilon}
solution to
Eq.

3.16
.

Corollary 5.6
.

If
\check{U}\preceq\widetilde{V}\preceq\underline{V}^{\varepsilon}
and
(V_{1},V_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{V}_{1},\widetilde{V}_{2})
then
{V}\preceq\underline{V}^{\varepsilon}
.

Algorithm 5.1

Howard-Tarski-Kantorovich algorithm: upward variant

1:

Initialize
V^{(0)}_{i,j}=\check{U}_{i,j}
, and set

\triangleright
Outer loop

2:

repeat

3:

Use Howard algorithm to solve:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,&\frac{\rho}{\theta}V^{(k+1)}_{i,1}=\bm{H}_{\varepsilon}\left(x_{i},y_{1},V^{(k)}_{i,1},\Delta^{+}{V^{(k+1)}_{i,1}},\Delta^{-}{V^{(k+1)}_{i,1}}\right)+\lambda_{1}\left(V^{(k+1)}_{i,2}-V^{(k+1)}_{i,1}\right),\\
&\frac{\rho}{\theta}V^{(k+1)}_{i,2}=\bm{H}_{\varepsilon}\left(x_{i},y_{2},V^{(k)}_{i,2},\Delta^{+}{V^{(k+1)}_{i,2}},\Delta^{-}{V^{(k+1)}_{i,2}}\right)+\lambda_{2}\left(V^{(k+1)}_{i,1}-V^{(k+1)}_{i,2}\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(5.6)

4:

repeat

5:

Initialize
V^{(k,0)}_{i,j}=V^{(k)}_{i,j}
, and set

\triangleright
Inner loop

6:

Update the policy

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\,&c^{F,(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j}=\min\left\{\rho^{\psi}\left(\Delta^{+}{V^{(k,{n})}_{i,j}}\right)_{+}^{-\psi}\left((1-\gamma){V}^{(k)}_{i,j}\right)^{\frac{1-\gamma\psi}{1-\gamma}},\bar{c}_{j}(x_{i})\right\}\\
&\text{for}\,\,x_{i}<\bar{x},\quad c^{F,({n}+1)}_{I,j}=r\bar{x}+y_{j},\quad s^{F,(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j}=rx_{i}+y_{j}-c^{F,(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j},\\

&\text{for}\,\,x_{i}>{\underline{x}},\quad c^{B,({n}+1)}_{0,j}=r{\underline{x}}+y_{j},\quad s^{B,(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j}=rx_{i}+y_{j}-c^{B,(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j}.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.7)

7:

Solve

\displaystyle\frac{\rho V^{(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j}}{\theta}={}

\displaystyle s^{F,(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j}\Delta^{+}{V^{(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j}}+s^{B,(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j}\Delta^{-}{V^{(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j}}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{F}\left(c^{F,(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j},V^{(k)}_{i,j}\right)+\mathcal{F}\left(c^{B,(k,{n}+1)}_{i,j},V^{(k)}_{i,j}\right)-\mathcal{F}\left(\bar{c}_{j}(x_{i}),V^{(k)}_{i,j}\right)

Theorem 5.7
.

The sequence
V^{(k)}
generated by
Algorithm

5.1
converges to the minimal solution
\underline{V}^{\varepsilon}
of
Eq.

3.16
.

Proof.

Step 1.
Solvability of the Howard inner loop. We first claim that for each
\left(V^{(k)}_{i,1},V^{(k)}_{i,2}\right)
such that
\check{U}\preceq V^{(k)}\preceq\check{V}
, the sequence
V^{(k,n)}
converges to
V^{(k+1)}
. The proof is similar to that of
Theorem

4.8
, hence we skip it.

Step 2.
Uniform boundedness of the sequence
V^{(k)}
. From
Section

5
, we deduce that if
\check{U}\preceq V^{(k)}\preceq\check{V}
then
\check{U}\preceq V^{(k+1)}\preceq\check{V}
. By induction,
\check{U}\preceq V^{(k)}\preceq\check{V}
holds for all
k
. This ensures that at each outer iteration the term
\left((1-\gamma){V}^{(k)}_{i,j}\right)^{\frac{1-\gamma\psi}{1-\gamma}}
is always well defined and bounded.

Step 3.
Monotonicity and convergence. We first observe that
Section

5
implies
V^{(1)}\succeq V^{(0)}
. From
Section

5
, if
V^{(k)}\succeq V^{(k-1)}
, then
V^{(k+1)}\succeq V^{(k)}
. Arguing by induction, this implies that
V^{(k)}
is increasing. Since
V^{(k)}\preceq\check{V}
holds for all
k
,
V^{(k)}
converges to a limit
\widehat{V}
such that
\widehat{V}\preceq\check{V}
.

Step 4.
Minimality. From
Section

5
, we know if there exists a solution
V^{\varepsilon}
, then
{V}^{(0)}=\check{U}\preceq V^{\varepsilon}
. From
Section

5
, an easy induction leads to
{V}^{(k)}\preceq V^{\varepsilon}
for all
k
, hence
\widehat{V}\preceq V^{\varepsilon}
. From the design of the algorithm,
\widehat{V}
is solution to
Eq.

3.16
, therefore
\widehat{V}=\underline{V}^{\varepsilon}
.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

In
Algorithm

4.1
, we only need a strictly negative upper bound so that
\left((1-\gamma){V}^{(n)}_{i,j}\right)^{\frac{1-\gamma\psi}{1-\gamma}}
is well defined, since
\left((1-\gamma){V}^{(n)}_{i,j}\right)^{\frac{1-\gamma\psi}{1-\gamma}}\to 0
if
{V}^{(n)}_{i,j}\to-\infty
. In
Algorithm

5.1
, with
1-\gamma\psi<0
, we need both upper and lower bounds to ensure that
\left((1-\gamma){V}^{(k)}_{i,j}\right)^{\frac{1-\gamma\psi}{1-\gamma}}
is well defined and bounded.

Proposition 5.9
.

Assume
\varepsilon>\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
\check{U}\preceq\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}\preceq\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\preceq\check{V}
. Let
{V}^{\varepsilon}
and
{V}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
be respectively the solutions to
Eq.

5.1
with
(V^{\varepsilon}_{1},V^{\varepsilon}_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}_{1},\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}_{2})
and
(V^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{1},V^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{2})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{1},\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{2})
. Then
{V}^{\varepsilon}\preceq{V}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Since
\varepsilon>\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
\check{U}_{i,j}\leq\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}_{i,j}\leq\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{i,j}\leq\check{V}_{i,j}
,

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{\varepsilon}\left(x_{i},y_{j},\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}_{i,j},\Delta^{+}{V^{\varepsilon}_{i,j}},\Delta^{-}{V^{\varepsilon}_{i,j}}\right)\leq{}

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\left(x_{i},y_{j},\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}_{i,j},\Delta^{+}{V^{\varepsilon}_{i,j}},\Delta^{-}{V^{\varepsilon}_{i,j}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\left(x_{i},y_{j},\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{i,j},\Delta^{+}{V^{\varepsilon}_{i,j}},\Delta^{-}{V^{\varepsilon}_{i,j}}\right),

where we have used
Section

3.2
for the last inequality. Therefore,
{V}^{\varepsilon}
is a subsolution of the equation satisfied by
{V}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
, we apply
Section

5
and obtain
{V}^{\varepsilon}\preceq{V}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
.
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

If

Let us denote by

\underline{V}^{\varepsilon}\preceq\check{V}
for all

. We thus conclude that the sequence
\underline{V}^{\varepsilon}
converges uniformly on

to the minimal solution of
Eq.

3.11
.
∎
