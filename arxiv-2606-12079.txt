A semi-definite programming formulation of the device-dependent guessing probability
Raffaele D’Avino, Aurora Mugnai, Miguel Navascués, Antonio Acín, Gabriel Senno

Abstract
In quantum mechanics, a measurement applied to a state in general produces some amount of intrinsic randomness. This is not only a fundamental feature of the theory, but is also at the basis of any quantum process to generate random numbers. The simplest of such processes consists of a single, fully charaterized, measurement acting on a single, fully characterized, state. Unfortunately, no general method to estimate the intrinsic randomness produced in such setups is known. In this work, we address this issue by presenting a semidefinite programming formulation of the maximum probability with which an adversary, Eve, can guess the outcomes of characterized but untrusted prepare-and-measure setups. We then present several applications of this construction. First, we apply our method to a variety of specific setups, allowing us both to benchmark the approach and, more importantly, to determine the exact amount of certifiable randomness in scenarios where only upper bounds were previously available. Then, we show that the presence of entanglement between the device preparing the state and the measurement strictly increases Eve’s predictive power, already in the most elementary setup of a binary measurement acting on a qubit state.



I
Introduction

Randomness is a fundamental resource in both classical and quantum information processing, underpinning applications ranging from cryptography and numerical simulation to foundational tests of physical theories. Quantum mechanics provides a principled source of randomness: when a measurement is performed on a quantum system prepared in a state that is not an eigenstate of the measured observable, the outcome cannot be predicted with certainty, even in principle. This intrinsic unpredictability distinguishes quantum randomness from classical stochasticity and forms the basis of quantum random number generators (QRNG)
9
;
2
;
11
.

An operational way of quantifying the amount of intrinsic randomness produced in a quantum setup is via the maximum probability with which an adversary, Eve, can guess its outcomes given side information about it. In this work, we focus on the simplest of such processes, in which a system is prepared in a known quantum state and a known measurement is applied to it, defining a fully characterized, or device-dependent, prepare-and-measure (PM) setup. In the most general and realistic situation, both the prepared state and the measurement are noisy and, therefore, Eve is allowed to have side information about both of them. In
14
, a general definition for the guessing probability in this scenario was provided. Unfortunately, the resulting definition a priori involves a nonconvex optimization for which no general computational procedure is known.

In this work, leveraging the techniques from
13
, we prove that the device-dependent guessing probability
14
can be expressed as a semidefinite program (SDP), establishing a computationally-friendly method to estimate the randomness produced in device-dependent PM setups. This construction is not only relevant from a fundamental perspective, but also for the certification of the simplest QRNG. To illustrate the general applicability of this new computational tool, we apply it to three representative situations. First, we consider a simple scenario in which a system defined by a pure qubit state mixed with depolarizing noise is measured along a complementary basis also mixed with depolarizing noise. We show that the analytic upper bound derived in
5
on the certifiable randomness is tight. Next, we improve the analysis of the amount of intrinsic randomness generated in a scheme targeting an implementation in quantum computers put forward by Berta and Brandão in
3
, tightening the bounds provided therein. And, third, we consider the source-device-independent randomness generation scheme introduced in
1
. Therein, the authors provide a family of
k
-outcome measurements with which, if trusted, unbounded randomness can be extracted from an uncharacterized and untrusted qubit system. We use our SDP formulation to show that if Eve is allowed to be correlated with the characterized measurements, the amount of intrinsic randomness already vanishes for
k=4
. As the last result of this manuscript, we show that the presence of entanglement between the preparation and the measurement devices leads to a strictly higher guessing probability than if only classical correlations are allowed, already for a scenario consisting of a binary measurement on a qubit. To our knowledge, this is the simplest example of the consequences of entanglement assistance on randomness certification in PM setups
7
;
4
.

The remainder of the manuscript is organized as follows. In Section
II
we introduce the PM scenario with characterized but untrusted devices and the guessing probability from
14
. In Section
III
, we present the SDP formulation used to compute this quantity. Section
IV
illustrates the method through three applications. Finally, in Section
V
we use our formulation to show that entanglement between the preparation and measurement devices can enhance Eve’s predictive power.

IV.1
Depolarized state and measurement

We first consider a setting that was studied in Ref.
5

More formally, the state and the measurement are assumed to be

\displaystyle\rho_{S}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\ket{+}\bra{+}+(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{1}/2,

(11)

\displaystyle\{M_{S}^{a}\}_{a}=

\displaystyle\{\varepsilon\ket{0}\bra{0}+(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{1}/2,\;

\displaystyle\varepsilon\ket{1}\bra{1}+(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{1}/2\},

IV.3
Uncharacterized state and equiangular planar measurement

As a final application, we consider the source-device-independent scenario introduced in Ref.
1
. In this framework, no assumption is made on the prepared qubit state
\rho_{S}
, while the measurement is assumed to be fully characterized and described by an equiangular
k
-outcome POVM
\{M_{S}^{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq k}
defined on the
XZ
plane of the Bloch sphere, with elements

M_{S}^{a}=\frac{1}{k}\bigl(\mathbb{1}+\vec{u}_{a}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\bigr),

(15)

where
\vec{u}_{a}=(\cos\theta_{a},0,\sin\theta_{a})
, with
\theta_{a}=2\pi a/k
, are the vertices of a regular polygon in the
XZ

\vec{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
denotes the vector of Pauli operators. Moreover, the measurement is assumed to be fully trusted, in the sense that the eavesdropper cannot have any side information. This is an important assumption, as any qubit measurement with more than four outcomes is not extremal and, therefore, Eve can be coupled to it in an adversarial model. Ref.
1
assumes that this is not the case. In fact, for measurements restricted to the
XZ

Under the mentioned assumptions, it is shown in Ref.
1

m_{n}\equiv\min_{\rho_{S}}H_{\min}(A|E)=\log_{2}(k)-1,

(16)

which is represented by the continuous line in Fig.

To assess whether this conclusion remains valid in a scenario with untrusted measurements, we consider a modified version of the SDP in Eq. (
4

\rho_{S}
is no longer fixed, and is instead included as an optimization variable. Accordingly, the maximization is extended to
\rho_{S}
, subject to the constraints
\rho_{S}\succeq 0
and
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{S}]=1

The results reported in Fig.
4

V
Entanglement-assistance increases Eve’s predicting power

The global state
\rho_{SM}
of the measured system
S
and ancillary system
M
in Eq. (
1
) is allowed to be correlated. As explained above, the rationale behind this decision follows from the fact that the honest user, Alice, is assumed to perform independent tomographic calibration processes on the prepared system
S
and implemented measurement
M
, and is thus unable to detect correlations between these two devices. This assumption, however, contrasts with the one performed in the independently introduced randomness quantification framework from
6
. While also based on the generalized Naimark dilations from
8
, the framework in
6
deviates from
14
in assuming that: 1) systems
S
and
M
are in a product state
\rho_{S}\otimes\sigma_{M}
; and 2) Eve holds systems
E
and
F
purifying, respectively,
S
and
M
, but she cannot measure them jointly. The results from
(
14
, Thms. 3 and 4)
show that in a setting where 1) holds, assuming 2) incurs in a restriction to Eve’s power. Naturally, this leaves open the question of whether 1) alone has any operational consequences. In this section, we answer this question in the affirmative.

Let
P_{{\rm guess}}^{\rm sep}
be as in Eq. (
1
) but with the added constraint of a separable
\rho_{SM}
. In this section, we show that there exist states
\rho_{S}^{*}
and POVMs
M_{S}^{*}
for which

\displaystyle P_{{\rm guess}}(A|E,\rho_{S}^{*},M_{S}^{*})>P_{{\rm guess}}^{\rm sep}(A|E,\rho_{S}^{*},M_{S}^{*}).

We make use of the following lemma, whose proof we defer to Appendix
B
.

Lemma 2
.

Given a state
\rho_{SM}
that is separable, then

\Gamma^{T_{S}}(\rho_{SM})\geq 0.

Here,
T_{S}
denotes the partial transposition with respect to subsystem
S
. Therefore, by constraining the sum
\Gamma=\sum_{e}\Gamma_{e}
of the matrices defined in Eq. (
6
) to have a positive partial transpose (PPT), we obtain upper bounds to
P_{{\rm guess}}^{\rm sep}
.

As a concrete example, for the qubit state
\rho_{S}
and the binary measurement
M_{S}=\{M_{S}^{0},\mathbb{1}_{S}-M_{S}^{0}\}
given by

\rho_{S}=\begin{pmatrix}0.2&0.3\\
0.3&0.8\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_{S}^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0.6&re^{i\phi}\\
re^{-i\phi}&0.1\end{pmatrix},

(18)

with
r=0.24
,
\phi=0.89
, we find that

\displaystyle P_{{\rm guess}}^{\rm sep}\leq 0.909672<P_{{\rm guess}}=0.910376.

In spite of the gap for this particular example being quite small, it suffices to show that, already in the simplest scenario given by a single qubit and a binary measurement, assuming no entanglement between the system
S
and the measurement register
M
can overestimate the amount of intrinsic randomness generated.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we studied the amount of intrinsic randomness that can be generated in a characterized but untrusted prepare-and-measure scenario. In particular, we introduced a semidefinite programming formulation of the conditional min-entropy of Eve possessing quantum side information about the experimental setup.

We apply our method in three different scenarios. This allows us to benchmark our formulation and, more importantly, to determine the exact amount of certifiable randomness in settings where previously only upper bounds were known, often relying on additional assumptions to make the conditional min-entropy computable. Moreover, we used our framework to show that restricting the correlations between the preparation and measurement devices to be classical strictly reduces Eve’s predictive power.

Our results provide a general tool for quantifying randomness in realistic, noise-affected quantum devices. They not only advance the current understanding of randomness generation in the presence of quantum side information, but also provide a general recipe to certify randomness in the simplest device-dependent QRNG consisting of a known measurement implemented on a known quantum state.

(21)

where
\Lambda=\sum_{s\in\cal{O}}s^{\dagger}\otimes\ket{s}
, with
\mathcal{O}:=\{\mathbb{1}\}\cup\{K^{i,j|a}_{M}:a=1,...,k;\,i,j=1,...,d\}
, and
e\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Using this definition, the moment matrices
\{\Gamma_{e}\}_{\text{e}}
can be written explicitly as

\begin{aligned} \Gamma_{e}&=\sum_{s,t\in\mathcal{O}}\Tr_{M}\left[(\mathbb{1}_{S}\otimes s^{\dagger}\otimes\ket{s})\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}(\mathbb{1}_{S}\otimes t\otimes\bra{t})\right]\\
&=\sum_{s,t\in\mathcal{O}}\Tr_{M}\left[(\mathbb{1}_{S}\otimes s^{\dagger}\otimes\ket{s})\left(\sum_{i,j,k,l}c^{e}_{ijkl}\ket{i}\bra{j}\otimes\ket{k}\bra{l}\right)(\mathbb{1}_{S}\otimes t\otimes\bra{t})\right]\\
&=\sum_{s,t\in\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i,j}\ket{i}\bra{j}\Tr\left[(\ket{j}\bra{i}\otimes s^{\dagger}t)\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}\right]\otimes\ket{s}\bra{t}\end{aligned}\qquad\forall e\in\{1,\dots,k\}.

(22)

Let’s write the objective and the constraints in function of the matrices
\{\Gamma_{e}\}_{e}
.

•

Objective function.

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\sum_{i,j}\bra{j}\!\bra{\mathbb{1}}\,\Gamma_{a}\,\ket{i}\!|K_{M}^{i,j|a}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\sum_{i,j}\Tr\!\left[\ket{i}\!\bra{j}\otimes K_{M}^{i,j|a}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{a}\right]

(23)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\Tr\!\left[\Pi_{SM}^{a}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{a}\right].

•

Constraint
\Tr_{M}[\sum_{e}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}]=\rho_{S}
.

\begin{aligned} \sum_{e}\bra{i}\!\bra{\mathbb{1}}\,\Gamma_{e}\,\ket{j}\!\ket{\mathbb{1}}&=\sum_{e}\Tr\!\left[(\ket{j}\!\bra{i}\otimes\mathbb{1})\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}\right]\\
&=\bra{i}\rho_{S}\ket{j}\end{aligned}\qquad\forall i,j.

(24)

•

Constraint
\Tr_{M}[\Pi_{SM}^{a}(\mathbb{1}_{S}\otimes\Tr_{S}[\sum_{e}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}])]=M_{S}^{a}
.

\begin{aligned} \sum_{e}\sum_{l}\bra{l}\!\bra{\mathbb{1}}\,\Gamma_{e}\,\ket{l}\!|K_{M}^{i,j|a}\rangle&=\sum_{e}\Tr\!\left[(\mathbb{1}\otimes K_{M}^{i,j|a})\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}\right]\\
&=\bra{i}M_{S}^{a}\ket{j}\end{aligned}\qquad\forall a,i,j.

(25)

This follows from the identity

\displaystyle M_{S}^{a}

\displaystyle=\Tr_{M}\!\left[\Pi_{SM}^{a}(\mathbb{1}_{S}\otimes\Tr_{S}[\sum_{e}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}])\right]

(26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\ket{i}\!\bra{j}\Tr\!\left[(\mathbb{1}\otimes K_{M}^{i,j|a})\sum_{e}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}\right].

•

Constraint
\Tr[\Pi_{SM}^{a}\sum_{e}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}]=\Tr[M_{S}^{a}\rho_{S}]
.

\begin{aligned} \sum_{e}\sum_{i,j}\bra{j}\!\bra{\mathbb{1}}\,\Gamma_{e}\,\ket{i}\!|K_{M}^{i,j|a}\rangle&=\sum_{e}\Tr\!\left[\Pi_{SM}^{a}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}\right]\end{aligned}\qquad\forall a.

(27)

In addition to the constraints discussed above, further conditions must be imposed on the moment matrices to enforce the algebraic properties of the operators
\{\Pi_{SM}^{a}\}_{a}
.
First, since
\{\Pi_{SM}^{a}\}_{a}
are Hermitian, i.e.
(\Pi_{SM}^{a})^{\dagger}=\Pi_{SM}^{a}

\forall a
, we require

\bigl(K_{M}^{i,j|a}\bigr)^{\dagger}=K_{M}^{j,i|a}\;\,\forall\,i,j,a
. In our formulation, this is enforced through

\bra{m}\!\bra{\mathbb{1}}\,\Gamma_{e}\,\ket{n}\!|s\rangle=\bra{m}\!\bra{s^\dagger}\,\Gamma_{e}\,\ket{n}\!|\mathbb{1}\rangle\qquad\;\,\forall s\in\mathcal{O}\quad\forall\,e,m,n.

(28)

Second, the completeness condition
\sum_{a}\Pi_{SM}^{a}=\mathbb{1}_{SM}
implies

{}_{S}\bra{i}\sum_{a}\Pi_{SM}^{a}\ket{j}_{S}=\sum_{a}K_{M}^{i,j|a}=\delta_{i,j}\mathbb{1}_{M}.

(29)

At the level of the moments, this translates to

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\bra{m}\!\bra{\mathbb{1}}\,\Gamma_{e}\,\ket{n}\!|K_{M}^{i,j|a}\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\Tr[\ket{m}\bra{n}\otimes K_{M}^{i,j|a}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}]

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}\Tr[\ket{m}\bra{n}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{M}\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}]=\delta_{ij}\bra{m}\!\bra{\mathbb{1}}\,\Gamma_{e}\,\ket{n}\!|\mathbb{1}\rangle\qquad\forall\,e,m,n,i,j.

Finally, the projectivity condition
(\Pi_{SM}^{a})^{2}=\Pi_{SM}^{a}
is equivalent to

{}_{S}\bra{i}(\Pi_{SM}^{a})^{2}\ket{j}_{S}=\sum_{l}K_{M}^{i,l|a}K_{M}^{l,j|a}=K_{M}^{i,j|a}={}_{S}\bra{i}\Pi_{SM}^{a}\ket{j}_{S}.

(31)

In terms of expectation values, we impose

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\bra{m}\langle(K_{M}^{l,i|a})^{\dagger}|\,\Gamma_{e}\,\ket{n}\!|K_{M}^{l,j|b}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Tr\!\left[\left(\sum_{l}\ket{m}\bra{n}\otimes K_{M}^{i,l|a}K_{M}^{l,j|b}\right)\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}\right]

(32)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ab}\Tr\!\left[\left(\ket{m}\bra{n}\otimes K_{M}^{i,j|a}\right)\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}^{e}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta_{ab}\bra{m}\!\bra{\mathbb{1}}\,\Gamma_{e}\,\ket{n}\!|K_{M}^{i,j|a}\rangle\qquad\forall\,a,b,e,m,n,i,j.

Appendix B
Moment matrix separability lemma

Lemma 3
.

Given a state
\rho_{SM}
that is separable, then
\Gamma^{T_{S}}(\rho_{SM})\geq 0.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle\Gamma(\rho_{SM})=\sum_{i,j}\ket{i}\bra{j}\otimes\sum_{s,t\in\mathcal{O}}c^{i,j}_{t^{\dagger}s}(\rho_{SM})\ket{s}\bra{t},

with

\displaystyle c^{i,j}_{s}(\rho_{SM})=\Tr\left[(\ket{j}\bra{i}\otimes s)\rho_{SM}\right].

Next, from the fact that

\displaystyle\Tr[(\ket{i}\bra{j}_{S}\otimes s)\sigma_{SM}^{T_{S}}]=\Tr[(\ket{j}\bra{i}_{S}\otimes s)\sigma_{SM}]

we have that

\displaystyle\Gamma(\rho_{SM})^{T_{S}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\ket{j}\bra{i}\otimes\sum_{s,t\in\mathcal{O{}}}c^{ij}_{t^{\dagger}s}(\rho_{SM})\ket{s}\bra{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\ket{j}\bra{i}\otimes\sum_{s,t\in\mathcal{O}}c^{ji}_{t^{\dagger}s}(\rho_{SM}^{T_{S}})\ket{s}\bra{t}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\rho_{SM}^{T_{S}}).

The lemma then follows from the facts that
\rho_{SM}^{T_{S}}\geq 0
for a separable
\rho_{SM}
and
\rho\geq 0\implies\Gamma(\rho)\geq 0
.
∎

Appendix C
Sufficiency of the SDP relaxation

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
from the main text. In fact, we prove a somewhat stronger result, Proposition
4
below, from which the said theorem follows as a corollary.

In the following, we use the symbol
(K^{i,j|a}_{M})^{\dagger}
as a shorthand for
K^{ji|a}_{M}
. The goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Proposition 4
.

For
e\in\{1,...,k\}
, let
\{c^{ij}_{e}(s):i,j=1,...,d,s\in{\cal O}^{2}\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be such that

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{k}c_{e}^{mn}(K^{i,j|a}_{M})=\delta_{ij}c^{mn}_{e}(\mathbb{1}),

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}c^{mn}_{e}((K^{l,i|a}_{M})^{\dagger}K^{l,j|b}_{M})=\delta_{ab}c^{mn}_{e}(K^{i,j|b}_{M}).

(34)

and the matrix

\Gamma_{e}:=\sum_{s,t\in{\cal O}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}c^{ij}_{e}(ts^{\dagger})\ket{i}\bra{j}\otimes\ket{s}\bra{t}

(35)

be positive semidefinite. Then, there exist a Hilbert space
\tilde{{\cal H}}
, non-normalized quantum states states
\{\tilde{\sigma}^{e}_{SM}\}_{e}\subset B({\mathbb{C}}^{d})\otimes B(\tilde{{\cal H}})
and operators
\{\tilde{K}^{ij|a}_{M}\}\subset B(\tilde{{\cal H}})
such that

\tilde{\Pi}^{a}_{SM}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\ket{i}\bra{j}\otimes\tilde{K}^{i,j|a}_{M},\quad\forall a\in\{1,...,k\}

(36)

defines a projective measurement and

\tr(\ket{j}\bra{i}\otimes\tilde{t}\tilde{s}^\dagger\tilde{\sigma}^e_{SM})=c_{e}^{ij}(ts^{\dagger}),

(37)

for
s,t\in{\cal O}
.

To prove the proposition, we will make use of the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\{c^{ij}(s):i,j=1,...,d,s\in{\cal O}^{2}\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
satisfy the constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{k}c^{mn}(K^{i,j|a}_{M})=\delta_{ij}c^{mn}(\mathbb{1}),

(38)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}c^{mn}((K^{l,i|a}_{M})^{\dagger}K^{l,j|b}_{M})=\delta_{ab}c^{mn}(K^{i,j|b}_{M}).

(39)

Let the matrix

\Gamma=\sum_{s,t\in{\cal O}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}c^{ij}(ts^{\dagger})\ket{i}\bra{j}\otimes\ket{s}\bra{t}

(40)

be positive semidefinite. Then, there exist a Hilbert space
\tilde{{\cal H}}
of dimension
d|{\cal O}|
, a non-normalized quantum state
\tilde{\sigma}\in B({\mathbb{C}}^{d})\otimes B(\tilde{{\cal H}})
and operators
\{\tilde{K}^{i,j|a}_{M}\}_{i,j,a}\subset B(\tilde{{\cal H}})
such that

\tilde{\Pi}^{a}_{SM}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\ket{i}\bra{j}\otimes\tilde{K}^{i,j|a}_{M},\quad\forall a\in\{1,...,k\}

(41)

defines a projective measurement and

\tr(\ket{j}\bra{i}\otimes\tilde{t}\tilde{s}^\dagger\tilde{\sigma}_{SM})=c^{ij}(ts^{\dagger}),

(42)

for
s,t\in{\cal O}
.

The intuition of the proof of this lemma is that
\sigma_{SM}
can be chosen to be of the form
\sigma_{SM}=\ket{\tilde{\psi}}\bra{\tilde{\psi}}
, with

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{m=1}^{d}\ket{m}\ket{\tilde{\psi}_m}.

(43)

If there exist projector operators of the form (
41
) and relation (
42
) is satisfied, then we have that:

c^{mn}(ts^{\dagger})=\bra{\tilde{\psi}_n}\tilde{t}\tilde{s}^{\dagger}\ket{\tilde{\psi}_m}.

(44)

In the proof, we will use a guess on
\{\tilde{s}\ket{\psi_m}:s\in{\cal O}\}
to construct the projector operators
\{\tilde{\Pi}^{a}_{SM}\}_{a}
.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is positive semidefinite, it admits a Gram decomposition, i.e., there exist vectors
\{\ket{m, s}:m=1,...,d,s\in{\cal O}\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{d|{\cal O}|}=:\tilde{{\cal H}}
such that

c^{mn}(ts^{\dagger})=\innerproduct{m,s}{n,t}.

(45)

Comparing this expression with Eq. (
44
) suggests one to define the vectors

\ket{\psi_m, s^\dagger}:=\overline{\ket{m,s}},

(46)

which therefore satisfy

c^{mn}(t^{\dagger}s)=\innerproduct{\psi_n, t}{\psi_m,s}.

(47)

Intuitively,
\ket{\psi_m,s}
would represent the vector
\tilde{s}\ket{\tilde{\psi}_m}
alluded to above; accordingly, the vector

\ket{\Phi^j_{m,a}}:=\sum_{i}\ket{i}\ket{\psi_m,K_M^{i,j|a}}

(48)

would correspond to
\tilde{\Pi}^{a}_{SM}\ket{j}\ket{\tilde{\psi}_m}
. In this regard, note that

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\Phi^i_{m,a}}{\Phi^j_{n,b}}=\sum_{l}\innerproduct{\psi_m, K^{l,i|a}_M}{\psi_n, K^{l,j|b}_M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}c^{nm}((K^{l,i|a}_{M})^{\dagger}K^{l,j|b}_{M})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ab}c^{nm}(K^{i,j|b}_{M});

(49)

in particular,
\ket{\Phi_{m,a}^i}\perp\ket{\Phi^j_{n,b}}
for
a\not=b
. For
a=1,...,k
, the subspaces

H_{a}:=\mbox{span}\{\ket{\Phi^i_{m,a}}:m,i\}

(50)

are therefore pair-wise orthogonal; this suggests defining the projectors

\tilde{\Pi}_{SM}^{a}:=\mbox{proj}\left(H_{a}\right),\quad\forall a\in\{1,...,k\}.

(51)

The orthogonality relations between the subspaces
\{H_{a}\}_{a}
guarantee that
\{\tilde{\Pi}_{SM}^{a}\}_{a}
are either a complete or an undercomplete set of projectors. In the latter case, we redefine
\tilde{\Pi}^{1}_{SM}:=\mathbb{1}-\sum_{a\not=1}\tilde{\Pi}_{SM}^{a}
.

Now, consider the vector

\displaystyle\ket{i}\ket{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}-\sum_{a}\ket{\Phi^i_{m,a}}.

(52)

This vector is null. Indeed, its norm squared is

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}+\sum_{a}\innerproduct{\Phi^i_{m,a}}{\Phi^i_{m,a}}-\sum_{a}\bra{i}\bra{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}\ket{\Phi^i_{m,a}}-\sum_{a}\bra{\Phi^i_{m,a}}\ket{i}\ket{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}

\displaystyle=\innerproduct{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}+\sum_{a}\sum_{j}\innerproduct{\psi_m,K_M^{j,i|a}}{\psi_m,K_M^{j,i|a}}-\sum_{a}\innerproduct{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}{\psi_m,K_M^{i,i|a}}-\sum_{a}\innerproduct{\psi_m,K_M^{i,i|a}}{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}

\displaystyle=c^{mm}(\mathbb{1})+\sum_{a}\sum_{j}c^{mm}((K_{M}^{j,i|a})^{\dagger}K_{M}^{j,i|a})-\sum_{a}c^{mm}(K_{M}^{i,i|a})-\sum_{a}c^{mm}(K_{M}^{i,i|a})

\displaystyle=c^{mm}(\mathbb{1})+\sum_{a}c^{mm}(K_{M}^{i,i|a})-2\sum_{a}c^{mm}(K_{M}^{i,i|a})

\displaystyle=c^{mm}(\mathbb{1})-\sum_{a}c^{mm}(K_{M}^{i,i|a})=0,

(53)

where we made use of relations (
38
), (
39
).
Thus, for all
m,i
, it holds that

\ket{i}\ket{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}=\sum_{a}\ket{\Phi^i_{m,a}}.

(54)

By the orthogonality relations
\{H_{a}\perp H_{b}:a\not=b\}
, this implies that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}^{a}_{SM}\ket{i}\ket{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}=\tilde{\Pi}_{SM}^{a}\sum_{b}\ket{\Phi^i_{m,b}}

\displaystyle=\ket{\Phi^i_{m,a}}.

(55)

Let
\{\tilde{K}^{ij|a}_{SM}\}_{a,i,j}\subset B(\tilde{{\cal H}})
be such that

\tilde{\Pi}_{SM}^{a}=\sum_{i,j}\ket{i}\bra{j}\otimes\tilde{K}^{i,j|a}_{M},

(56)

and define

\ket{\tilde{\psi}}:=\sum_{m}\ket{m}\ket{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}.

(57)

Then we have that

\displaystyle\bra{m}\otimes\tilde{K}^{j,k|a}_{M}\ket{\tilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle=(\bra{j}\otimes\mathbb{1})\tilde{\Pi}^{a}_{SM}(\ket{k}\bra{m}\otimes\mathbb{1})\ket{\tilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle=(\bra{j}\otimes\mathbb{1})\tilde{\Pi}^{a}_{SM}\ket{k}\ket{\psi_m,\mathbb{1}}

\displaystyle=(\bra{j}\otimes\mathbb{1})\ket{\Phi^k_{m,a}}

\displaystyle=\ket{\psi_m,K^{j,k|a}_M},

(58)

where we invoked eq. (
55
) and the definition of
\ket{\Phi^i_{a,m}}
in the last two lines.

Finally, define

\tilde{\sigma}_{SM}:=\ket{\tilde{\psi}}\!\bra{\tilde{\psi}}.

(59)

By eqs. (
47
) and (
58
), for all
s,t\in{\cal O}
it holds that

\displaystyle\tr(\ket{n}\bra{m}\otimes\tilde{t}\tilde{s}^\dagger\tilde{\sigma}_{SM})

\displaystyle=\bra{\tilde{\psi}}(\ket{n}\otimes\tilde{t})(\bra{m}\otimes\tilde{s}^{\dagger})\ket{\tilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle=\innerproduct{\psi_n,\tilde{t}^\dagger}{\psi_m,\tilde{s}^\dagger}=c^{mn}(ts^{\dagger}).

(60)

∎

Now we are ready to prove the proposition.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

By Lemma
5
, there exists a Hilbert space
\hat{{\cal H}}
of dimension
d|{\cal O}|
such that, for
e=1,...,k
, there is a set of operators
\{\hat{s}(e):s\in{\cal O}^{2}\}\subset B(\hat{{\cal H}})

\hat{\sigma}^{e}_{SM^{\prime}}\subset B({\mathbb{C}}^{d}\otimes\hat{{\cal H}})

\tr(\ket{j}\bra{i}\otimes\hat{t}(e)\hat{s}(e)^\dagger\hat{\sigma}_e)=c^{ij}_{e}(ts^{\dagger})\;\,\forall s,t\in{\cal O}
.

2.

The operators

\tilde{\Pi}_{SM}^{a}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\ket{i}\bra{j}\otimes\tilde{K}^{i,j|a}_{M}(e),\quad\forall a\in\{1,...,k\}

We next integrate all the operators above in an extended Hilbert space

\tilde{\sigma}^{e}_{SM}:=\hat{\sigma}^{e}_{SM^{\prime}}\otimes\ket{e}\!\bra{e}.
