Proofs of four generating function conjectures for arbor polytopes
Feihu Liu, Jinlong Tang, [2mm] School of Mathematical Sciences, [-0.8ex] Capital Normal University, Beijing, 100048, P.R. China, Email address: liufeihu7476@163.com, Email address: jinlong_tang@cnu.edu.cn

Abstract
This paper proves four conjectured generating series, due to Chapoton, which concern invariants of posets and polytopes associated with a specific sequence of arbors. Two of these conjectures provide closed-form formulas for the generating series of the Zeta polynomial and the generating series of the M-triangle of the poset, respectively. The remaining two conjectures pertain, respectively, to the Ehrhart polynomial and the Laplace transform of the volume function of the associated arbor polytope.

[

Q_{n,k}

[

An
arbor

t

v
is decorated by a non-empty subset of a finite set
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, and these subsets form a partition of
[n]
. We call
n
the
size
of the arbor. The cardinality of a vertex
v
is denoted by
|v|
.
For example, Figure
1
is an arbor on the set
[8]
.

To every arbor
t

Q_{t}

P_{t}
.

Definition 1.1

(The Polytope
Q_{t}
)
.

The polytope
Q_{t}
is defined in the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with coordinates
(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}
by the inequalities
x_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in[n]
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}(v)}x_{i}\leq|\mathcal{D}(v)|

for every vertex
v
, where
\mathcal{D}(v)

v

For the example in Figure

A convex polytope
\mathcal{P}

Chapoton

The poset

\mathrm{ht}(x)=\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}

The reader is referred to reference
[

A standard reference on the polytope is
[

.

•

•

•

2
Generating Functions for Poset Invariants

2.1
Zeta Polynomials

Let
P
be a finite poset. If
u\geq 2
, then define
Z_{P}(u)
to be the number of chains
e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\cdots\leq e_{u-1}
in
P
. We call
Z_{P}(u)
(regarded as a function of
u
) the
Zeta polynomial
of
P
.
For the relevant properties of the Zeta polynomial, we refer the reader to
[
9
, Section 3.12]

For an arbor
t
, Chapoton extends the Zeta polynomial of the poset
P_{t}
.
Let
Z_{t}(u,X)
be the polynomial in
u
and
X
such that for every integer
m\geq 2
,

\displaystyle Z_{t}(m,X)=\sum_{e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\cdots\leq e_{m-1}}X^{\mathrm{ht}(e_{m-1})},

where
\mathrm{ht}
is the height of an element.
Setting
X=1
yields the classical Zeta polynomial of the poset
P_{t}
.

Let
t
be an arbor on a set of cardinality
n\geq 2
. Let
(t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(w)})
be the sub-trees of the root vertex of
t
. Let
r
be the size of the root vertex of
t
.
Chapoton
[
4
]
derived the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
4
, Proposition 2.7]

Let
Z_{W}=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{w}Z_{t^{(k)}}(u,X)
.
Write
Z_{W}=\sum\limits_{j}W_{j}X^{j}
. Then for
0\leq j\leq n
, the coefficient of
X^{j}
in
Z_{t}(u,X)
is

\sum_{l=\max(0,j-n+r)}^{j}\binom{r(u-1)+l-1}{l}W_{j-l}.

The first equality in the following theorem was conjectured by Chapoton.
In fact, we obtain a simple closed formula during the proof.

Theorem 2.2
.

The generating series for the Zeta polynomials of
P_{t_{n}}
is

1+\sum_{n\geq 1}Z_{t_{n}}(u,1)s^{n}=\exp\left(\int\frac{u}{(1-us)(1+s-us)}\,ds\right)=\left(\frac{1-su+s}{1-su}\right)^{u}.

Proof.

For the arbor
t
on one element, it is clear that the Zeta polynomial is
1+(u-1)X
.
Therefore, for
n-1
leaves, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{W}=(1+(u-1)X)^{n-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}W_{k}X^{k},\quad\text{where}\quad W_{k}=\binom{n-1}{k}(u-1)^{k}.

By Proposition
2.1
, for
0\leq j\leq n
, the coefficient of
X^{j}
in
Z_{t_{n}}(u,X)
is

\sum\limits_{l=\max(0,j-n+1)}^{j}\binom{u-2+l}{l}W_{j-l}=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{min(j,n-1)}\binom{u-2+j-l}{j-l}W_{l}=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{j}\binom{u-2+j-l}{j-l}W_{l},

with the convention that
W_{l}=0
if
l\notin\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
.
Summing over all possible heights
0\leq j\leq n
, we get

Z_{t_{n}}(u,1)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{j}\binom{u-2+j-l}{j-l}W_{l}.

We extract the coefficients of
W_{k}
(
0\leq k\leq n-1
) and obtain

\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{u+2+j-k}{j-k}=\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{u-2+l}{l}=\binom{u+n-k-1}{n-k}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle Z_{t_{n}}(u,1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}W_{k}\binom{u+n-k-1}{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}(u-1)^{k}\binom{u+n-k-1}{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n-1}{k}(u-1)^{k}\binom{u+n-k-1}{n-k}.

Let
G(s)=1+\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}Z_{t_{n}}(u,1)s^{n}
. By making the substitution
m=n-k-1
, and given that
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 0
and
\binom{n-1}{k}=0
if
n-k-1<0
, the summation range for
m
can be restricted to
[0,\infty)
without altering the final sum. We get

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{n-1}{k}(u-1)^{k}\binom{u+n-k-1}{n-k}s^{n}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{m\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{k+m}{k}(u-1)^{k}\binom{u+m}{m+1}s^{k+m+1}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{m\geq 0}s^{m+1}\binom{u+m}{m+1}\left[\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{k+m}{k}(s(u-1))^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{m\geq 0}\binom{u+m}{m+1}\frac{s^{m+1}}{(1-su+s)^{m+1}}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\binom{u+m-1}{m}\frac{s^{m}}{(1-su+s)^{m}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{s}{1-su+s}}\right)^{u}=\left(\frac{1-su+s}{1-su}\right)^{u}.

Taking the natural logarithm and differentiating with respect to
s
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\ln G(s)

\displaystyle=u\frac{d}{ds}\Big(\ln(1-us+s)-\ln(1-us)\Big)=\frac{u}{(1-us)(1+s-us)}.

Integrating both sides and exponentiating (with the constant
C=0
since
G(0)=1
) yields the desired result.
∎

2.2
M-triangles

Given a poset
P
, the
Möbius function
of
P
(denoted
\mu_{P}
) is defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{P}(a,a)

\displaystyle=1,\quad\text{for all}\quad a\in P;

\displaystyle\mu_{P}(a,b)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{a\leq e<b}\mu_{P}(a,e),\quad\text{for all}\quad a<b\quad\text{in}\quad P.

Given a graded poset
P
, one can define the
M
-triangle
of
P
as the following sum of Möbius numbers weighted using the rank function
\mathrm{ht}

\displaystyle M_{P}(X,Y)=\sum_{a\leq b}\mu_{P}(a,b)X^{\mathrm{ht}(a)}Y^{\mathrm{ht}(b)}.

For an arbor
t
, in order to obtain
M_{P_{t}}(X,Y)
, Chapoton introduced the polynomial

K_{t}(X,Y)=\sum_{a\in P_{t}}X^{\mathrm{nz}(a)}Y^{\mathrm{ht}(a)},

where
\mathrm{nz}(a)
is the number of non-zero coordinates in
a
.
For the arbor
t
on one element, it is clear that
K_{t}(X,Y)=1+XY
.
Let
t
be an arbor on
[n]
with
n\geq 2
. Let
(t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(w)})
be the sub-trees of the root vertex of
t
. Let
r
be the size of the root vertex of
t
. Chapoton obtained the following characterization of the coefficients of the polynomial
K_{t}(X,Y)
.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
4
, Proposition 2.8]

Let
W(X,Y)=\prod_{k=1}^{w}K_{t^{(k)}}(X,Y)
and write
W(X,Y)=\sum_{i,j}W_{i,j}X^{i}Y^{j}
. Then for
0\leq j\leq k\leq n
, the coefficient of
X^{j}Y^{k}
in
K_{t}(X,Y)
is

\sum_{\ell=\max(0,j-n+r)}^{\min(j,r)}\sum_{m=\max(\ell,k-n+r)}^{k+\ell-j}\binom{r}{\ell}\binom{m-1}{m-\ell}W_{j-\ell,k-m}.

Furthermore, Chapoton derived a transformation formula form
K_{t}(X,Y)
to
M_{t}(X,Y)
.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
4
, Proposition 2.10]

For every arbor
t
, we have

M_{t}(X,Y)=K_{t}(1-1/X,XY).

In the particular case
t_{n}
under consideration, we obtain a reduced formula.

Proposition 2.5
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the polynomial
K_{t_{n}}(X,Y)
is given by

K_{t_{n}}(X,Y)=(1+XY)^{n}+\frac{XY^{2}}{1-Y}(1+XY)^{n-1}-\frac{XY^{2}}{1-Y}(Y(1+X))^{n-1}.

Proof.

For
0\leq j\leq k\leq n
, we consider the coefficient of
X^{j}Y^{k}
in
K_{t_{n}}(X,Y)
.

If
j=k
which implies that the enumerated
a\in P_{t_{n}}
possesses property
\mathrm{nz}(a)=\mathrm{ht}(a)
, so
a
has exactly
j
non-zero components, all of which are equal to
1
. One can easily see that there exist exactly
\binom{n}{j}
such
a
.

If
j<k
, it follows that the enumerated
a
takes the value
k-j
at the component of the root (which means
j\geq 1
), and among the remaining
n-1
components, it possesses exactly
j-1
non-zero entries, all of which are equal to
1
. There exist
\binom{n-1}{j-1}
such
a
.

This yields the explicit expression for
K_{t_{n}}(X,Y)
,

\displaystyle K_{t_{n}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}X^{j}Y^{j}+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\binom{n-1}{j-1}X^{j}Y^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(XY)^{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j-1}X^{j}\sum_{k=j+1}^{n}Y^{k}

\displaystyle=(1+XY)^{n}+\frac{XY^{2}}{1-Y}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j-1}(XY)^{j-1}-\frac{XY^{n+1}}{1-Y}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j-1}X^{j-1}

\displaystyle=(1+XY)^{n}+\frac{XY^{2}}{1-Y}(1+XY)^{n-1}-\frac{XY^{2}}{1-Y}(Y(1+X))^{n-1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

(Conjectured by Chapoton)
The generating series for the M-triangles of
P_{t_{n}}
is

1+\sum_{n\geq 1}M_{t_{n}}(X,Y)s^{n}=\frac{(XYs-Ys-1)(XYs-1)}{(2XYs-Ys-1)(XYs-Ys+s-1)}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.5
and Proposition
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle M_{t_{n}}(X,Y)=K_{t_{n}}(1-1/X,XY)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+XY-Y)^{n}+\frac{X^{2}Y^{2}-XY^{2}}{1-XY}(1+XY-Y)^{n-1}-\frac{X^{2}Y^{2}-XY^{2}}{1-XY}(2XY-Y)^{n-1}.

Defining the substituted variables

A=1+XY-Y,\quad B=2XY-Y,\quad\text{and}\quad C=\frac{X^{2}Y^{2}-XY^{2}}{1-XY},

we get

M_{t_{n}}(X,Y)=A^{n}+CA^{n-1}-CB^{n-1}.

Now, by computing the generating function
G(s)=1+\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}M_{t_{n}}(X,Y)s^{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}(As)^{n}+Cs\sum_{n\geq 1}A^{n-1}s^{n-1}-Cs\sum_{n\geq 1}B^{n-1}s^{n-1}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{As}{1-As}+\frac{Cs}{1-As}-\frac{Cs}{1-Bs}

\displaystyle=\frac{(XYs-Ys-1)(XYs-1)}{(2XYs-Ys-1)(XYs-Ys+s-1)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3
Generating Functions for Polytope Invariants

3.1
Ehrhart Polynomials

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a
d
-dimensional lattice convex polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The
Ehrhart function

E_{\mathcal{P}}(u)=|u\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|,\quad u=1,2,\ldots,

counts the number of integer points in the
u
-th dilation
u\mathcal{P}=\{u\alpha:\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\}
of
\mathcal{P}
.

Ehrhart’s famous theorem
[
6
]
says that
E_{\mathcal{P}}(u)
is a polynomial in
u
of degree
d
, now known as the
Ehrhart polynomial
of
\mathcal{P}
.
It is well-known
[
3
, Corollary 3.20; Theorem 5.6]
that the leading coefficient of
E_{\mathcal{P}}(u)
equals the (relative) volume of
\mathcal{P}
, the second highest coefficient of
E_{\mathcal{P}}(u)
equals half of the boundary volume of
\mathcal{P}
, and the constant term is
1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

(Conjectured by Chapoton)
The generating series for the Ehrhart polynomials of
Q_{t_{n}}
is

1+\sum_{n\geq 1}E_{t_{n}}(u)s^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{s}{(us+s-1)}-\frac{s-1}{(us+s-1)^{2}}\right).

First proof of Theorem
3.1
.

For every integer
m\geq 1
, Chapoton
[
4
]
introduced the height distribution polynomial
F_{t,m}(X)
that counts lattice points in the dilated polytope
mQ_{t}
with weight
X^{h}
at height
h
, which is multiplicative with respect to direct product of polytopes.

Fix an integer
m\geq 1
and consider the dilated polytope
mQ_{t_{n}}
.

The arbor
t_{n}
consists of a root of size 1 and
n-1
leaves of size 1. For a single leaf, the height distribution polynomial is
1+X+\dots+X^{m}
. For
n-1
independent leaves, their joint distribution is
F_{n-1,m}(X)=(1+X+\dots+X^{m})^{n-1}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m(n-1)}w_{k}X^{k}
.

Obviously, the number of lattice points at height
j
in
mQ_{t_{n}}
is
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\min(j,m(n-1))}w_{k}
. Summing over all possible heights
0\leq j\leq mn
, the Ehrhart polynomial
E_{t_{n}}(u)
evaluated at
m
gives

\displaystyle E_{t_{n}}(m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{mn}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\min(j,m(n-1))}w_{k}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{m(n-1)}(mn-k+1)w_{k}

\displaystyle=(mn+1)\sum_{k}w_{k}-\sum_{k}kw_{k}

\displaystyle=(mn+1)F_{n-1,m}(1)-F^{\prime}_{n-1,m}(1)

\displaystyle=(m+1)^{n-1}\left(\frac{mn}{2}+\frac{m}{2}+1\right).

Replacing
m
with the continuous variable
u
, we construct the generating function
G(s,u)=1+\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}E_{t_{n}}(u)s^{n}
. Then we have

\displaystyle G(s,u)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}(u+1)^{n-1}\left(\frac{un}{2}+\frac{u}{2}+1\right)s^{n}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\frac{u}{2}+1}{u+1}\sum_{n\geq 1}\left((u+1)s\right)^{n}+\frac{u}{2(u+1)}\sum_{n\geq 1}n((u+1)s)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{s}{us+s-1}-\frac{s-1}{(us+s-1)^{2}}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Second proof of Theorem
3.1
.

In
[
2
]
, the Ehrhart polynomial of the arbor polytope
t_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle E_{t_{n}}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-1}{j}\binom{(n-j)(u+1)}{n}.

For the sake of completeness, we briefly outline the proof of this formula due to Athanasiadis et al.
[
2
]
.
Observe that
E_{t_{n}}(u)
counts the lattice points
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
satisfying

x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}\leq un,\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq x_{i}\leq u\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1.

Since the number of points
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
which satisfy the first inequality and violate the second for any
j
given values of
i\in[k]
is equal to
\binom{(n-j)(u+1)}{n}
.
By the principle of inclusion–exclusion, we therefore obtain

\displaystyle E_{t_{n}}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-1}{j}\binom{(n-j)(u+1)}{n},

as desired.

Let
[x^{m}]f(x)
be defined as the coefficient of
x^{m}
in the polynomial
f(x)
.
Set
a=u+1
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{t_{n}}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-1}{j}\binom{(n-j)a}{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-1}{j}[x^{n}](1+x)^{a(n-j)}

\displaystyle=[x^{n}](1+x)^{an}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}(-(1+x)^{-a})^{j}

\displaystyle=[x^{n}](1+x)^{an}(1-(1+x)^{-a})^{n-1}=[x^{n}](1+x)^{a}((1+x)^{a}-1)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=[x^{n}](1+x)^{a}x^{n-1}(g(x))^{n-1}=[x](1+x)^{a}(g(x))^{n-1},

where
g(x)=\frac{(1+x)^{a}-1}{x}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{t_{n}}(u)s^{n}=1+s\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[x](1+x)^{a}(s\cdot g(x))^{n-1}=1+s[x]\frac{(1+x)^{a}}{1-s\cdot g(x)}.

Let
G(x)=\frac{(1+x)^{a}}{1-s\cdot g(x)}
and
h(x)=1-s\cdot g(x)
. We have
[x]G(x)=G^{\prime}(0)
.
According to

g(0)=a,\quad g^{\prime}(0)=\frac{a(a-1)}{2},\quad h(0)=1-as,\quad h^{\prime}(0)=-sg^{\prime}(0)=\frac{-sa(a-1)}{2},

we get

G^{\prime}(0)=\frac{a\cdot h(0)-h^{\prime}(0)}{(h(0))^{2}}=\frac{a-\frac{a(a+1)s}{2}}{(1-as)^{2}}.

Hence we conclude that

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{t_{n}}(u)s^{n}

\displaystyle=1+s\cdot G^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1-as+\frac{a(a-1)s^{2}}{2}}{(1-as)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{s}{as-1}-\frac{s-1}{(as-1)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{s}{(us+s-1)}-\frac{s-1}{(us+s-1)^{2}}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Laplace Transform of Volume Functions

For a given lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}
, we know that the leading coefficient of
E_{\mathcal{P}}(u)
equals the volume of
\mathcal{P}
.
For an arbor
t
, Chapoton refine the volume of
Q_{t}
into a volume function
V_{t}(h)
, that records the volume of every slice of
Q_{t}
according to the height function
\mathrm{ht}
. This function vanishes outside the segment
[0,n]
, where
n
is the size of
t
.
For example, for the arbor
t
on one element,
V_{t}(h)=1
for
0\leq h\leq 1
and
0
otherwise.

The
Laplace transform
of volume function
V_{t}(h)
is defined by

\displaystyle L_{t}(v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{exp}(-vh)V_{t}(h)dh.

Note that
L_{t}(0)
is the volume of
Q_{t}
.

The operator
\mathbf{T}_{n}
acts on a Laplace transform function as follows:

\mathbf{T}_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{exp}(-vh)f(h)dh\right):=\int_{0}^{n}\mathrm{exp}(-vh)f(h)dh.

Chapoton
[
4
, Section 2.3]
introduced a bivariate polynomial
L_{t}(E,V)
for computing the Laplace transform
L_{t}(v)
where
E=e^{-v}
and
V=1/v
.
For a given arbor
t
, one can compute
L_{t}(v)
by first computing polynomial
L_{t}(E,V)
, and then replace
E
by
e^{-v}
and
V
by
1/v
. Chapoton also derived a formula which tranlates the action of
\mathbf{T}_{n}
on polynomials in
E
and
V
.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
4
, Lemma 2.5]

If
l\geq n
, then
\mathbf{T}_{n}(V^{k}E^{l})=0
. Othewise

\mathbf{T}_{n}(V^{k+1}E^{l})=V^{k+1}E^{l}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{(n-l)^{k-j}}{(k-j)!}V^{j+1}E^{n}.

The initial value
L_{t}(E,V)
for the arbor of size 1 is
-VE+V
.
Let
t
be an arbor on the set
[n]
with
n\geq 2
.
Let
(t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(w)})
be the sub-trees of the root vertex of
t
.
Let
r
be the size of the root vertex of
t
.
The polynomial
L_{t}(E,V)
is

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{n}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{w}L_{t^{(k)}}(E,V)\cdot\mathbf{T}_{n}(V^{r})\right).

(1)

Theorem 3.3
.

(Conjectured by Chapoton)
The generating series for the Laplace transforms of volume functions of
Q_{t_{n}}
is

\sum_{n\geq 1}L_{t_{n}}(E,V)s^{n}=V\left(\frac{s}{EVs-Vs+1}+\frac{Es}{Es-1}\right).

Proof.

By Equation (
1
), the Laplace transform of the volume function follows the recursion

L_{t_{n}}(E,V)=\mathbf{T}_{n}\left(L_{t_{1}}^{n-1}\cdot\mathbf{T}_{n}(V)\right),

where
L_{t_{1}}(E,V)=V(1-E)
. According to Proposition
3.2
,
\mathbf{T}_{n}(V)=V-VE^{n}
. Substituting this into the recursion gives

L_{t_{n}}(E,V)=\mathbf{T}_{n}\left(V^{n}(1-E)^{n-1}-V^{n}(1-E)^{n-1}E^{n}\right).

The truncation operator
\mathbf{T}_{n}
maps
V^{k}E^{l}\mapsto 0
for
l\geq n
. Since the expanded terms of
V^{n}(1-E)^{n-1}E^{n}
all possess an
E
degree of at least
n
,
\mathbf{T}_{n}
annihilates this entire section. Thus, we only need to evaluate
\mathbf{T}_{n}\left(V^{n}(1-E)^{n-1}\right)
.
Using the binomial expansion and Proposition
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{t_{n}}(E,V)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{T}_{n}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{l}(-1)^{l}V^{n}E^{l}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{l}(-1)^{l}\left(V^{n}E^{l}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(n-l)^{n-1-j}}{(n-1-j)!}V^{j+1}E^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=V^{n}(1-E)^{n-1}-E^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}V^{j+1}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{l}(-1)^{l}\frac{(n-l)^{n-1-j}}{(n-1-j)!}\right].

The inner bracketed sum corresponds to the
(n-1)
-th order forward difference of the polynomial
P(x)=\frac{x^{n-1-j}}{(n-1-j)!}
evaluated at
x=1
, denoted
\Delta^{n-1}P(1)
. Since the degree of
P(x)
is
n-1-j
, the difference
\Delta^{n-1}P(1)
is strictly
0
for
j>0
. When
j=0
, the degree is exactly
n-1
, and
\Delta^{n-1}P(1)=1
. Consequently, the double sum collapses to a single term

L_{t_{n}}(E,V)=V^{n}(1-E)^{n-1}-VE^{n}.

Generating the formal power series over
n
:

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(V^{n}(1-E)^{n-1}-VE^{n}\right)s^{n}

\displaystyle=Vs\sum_{n\geq 1}(Vs(1-E))^{n-1}-V\sum_{n\geq 1}(Es)^{n}

\displaystyle=V\left(\frac{s}{EVs-Vs+1}+\frac{Es}{Es-1}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎
