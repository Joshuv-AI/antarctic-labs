Improved Lower Bounds for the Hales–Jewett Numbers via Symmetric Colorings
Younes Mouhib, Lorenz Halbeisen

Abstract
. The Hales–Jewett number HJ ⁡ ( t , r ) \mathrm{HJ}(t,r) is the least dimension n n in which every r r -coloring of the cube [ t ] n [t]^{n} contains a monochromatic combinatorial line. We prove HJ ⁡ ( 3 , 3 ) ≥ 22 \mathrm{HJ}(3,3)\geq 22 and HJ ⁡ ( 4 , 2 ) ≥ 14 \mathrm{HJ}(4,2)\geq 14 , improving the previous records HJ ⁡ ( 3 , 3 ) ≥ 14 \mathrm{HJ}(3,3)\geq 14 (Farnsworth) and HJ ⁡ ( 4 , 2 ) ≥ 12 \mathrm{HJ}(4,2)\geq 12 (the van der Waerden bound). Both bounds are obtained from coordinate-symmetric colorings, which compress the cube onto the discrete simplex of letter-count vectors; a symmetric coloring is line-free if and only if no corner tuple on the simplex is monochromatic, an exact equivalence that turns line-freeness into a constraint-satisfaction problem of size polynomial in n n . Each bound is certified by an explicit table of fewer than 600 600 cells together with a finite, mechanical check of the corner tuples; the SAT solver only finds the witness, while correctness rests on the published table, the reduction lemma, and a dependency-free verification that is in principle hand-auditable.

1.
Introduction

The Hales–Jewett theorem
[
4
]
is one of the central results of Ramsey theory; informally, it asserts that high-dimensional tic-tac-toe cannot end in a draw. Write
[t]=\{1,\dots,t\}
for an alphabet of size
t
, and call an element of
[t]^{n}
a
word
. A
root
is an element
\tau
of
(\{*\}\cup[t])^{n}
containing at least one star; substituting a letter
a\in[t]
for every star yields the word
\tau(a)
, and the set
L_{\tau}=\{\tau(1),\dots,\tau(t)\}
is the
combinatorial line
generated by
\tau
. The coordinates carrying a star are the
active
coordinates of the line. The theorem states that for all positive integers
t,r
there is an
n
such that every
r
-coloring of
[t]^{n}
contains a monochromatic combinatorial line; the least such
n
is the
Hales–Jewett number

\mathrm{HJ}(t,r)
, abbreviated
\mathrm{HJ}(t)
when
r=2
.

Very little is known about these numbers. Shelah’s proof
[
9
]
reduced the upper bounds from Ackermannian to primitive-recursive, but they remain enormous. Beyond the family
\mathrm{HJ}(2,r)=r
, the only exactly known value is
\mathrm{HJ}(3,2)=4
, due to Hindman and Tressler
[
5
]
. For four letters and two colors, the best available bounds are
12\leq\mathrm{HJ}(4,2)<10^{11}
, the lower bound coming from the van der Waerden numbers and the upper bound from Lavrov
[
6
]
. The van der Waerden lower bound was tight in every exactly known case until Farnsworth
[
3
]
proved
\mathrm{HJ}(3,3)\geq 14
using SAT solvers, exceeding the van der Waerden value of
13
. That result is the starting point of the present work: the classical lower-bound mechanism is not the final word.

Results

Our main results improve the classical lower bounds through a single device, the
symmetric reduction
. Restricting attention to colorings invariant under all permutations of the coordinates compresses the cube
[t]^{n}
, with its
t^{n}
points and
(t+1)^{n}-t^{n}
combinatorial lines, onto the discrete simplex of letter-count vectors, with only
\binom{n+t-1}{t-1}
cells; on the simplex, line-freeness becomes a system of
\binom{n+t-1}{t}
local conditions (Lemma
). Unexpectedly, this severely restricted class of colorings already contains line-free witnesses beyond all previously known lower bounds. We prove

\mathrm{HJ}(3,3)\geq 22\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{HJ}(4,2)\geq 14

(Theorems
and
), each certified by an explicit table:
253
colored cells deciding all
4{,}387{,}586{,}157{,}901
combinatorial lines of
[3]^{21}
, and
560
cells deciding all
1{,}153{,}594{,}261
lines of
[4]^{13}
. We emphasize a point of methodology: the SAT solver plays no role in the correctness of any bound. Every witness is published as a table of fewer than
600
entries, and the proof consists of that table, the reduction lemma, and a finite mechanical check that a patient reader could replicate by hand (Appendix
).

The article is largely a self-contained redaction of results from the author’s thesis
[
7
]
. For
t=3
, the simplex correspondence is the coloring counterpart of the Fujimura-set device of the Polymath project
[
8
,
2
]
for the density problem (Remark
).

2.
Background

2.1.
The Hales–Jewett numbers

We recall the small known values. It is elementary that
\mathrm{HJ}(2)=2
, and an easy pigeonhole gives the general two-letter value.

Proposition 2.1
.

\mathrm{HJ}(2,r)=r
for every
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

In
[2]^{r}
consider the
r+1
words
w_{0},\dots,w_{r}
, where
w_{k}
has its first
k
coordinates equal to
1
and the rest equal to
2
. Under any
r
-coloring two of them, say
w_{a}
and
w_{b}
with
a<b
, share a color; they agree outside coordinates
a+1,\dots,b
, on which
w_{b}
is all
1
’s and
w_{a}
all
2
’s, so they form a monochromatic line and
\mathrm{HJ}(2,r)\leq r
. Conversely, coloring each point of
[2]^{r-1}
by its number of
1
’s (a value in
\{0,\dots,r-1\}
) leaves no line monochromatic, since the two endpoints of any line differ in that count; hence
\mathrm{HJ}(2,r)\geq r
.
∎

The value
\mathrm{HJ}(3,2)=4
was established by Hindman and Tressler
[
5
]
through exhaustive computation and forced-assignment logic; no value
\mathrm{HJ}(t)
with
t\geq 4
is known, and the best upper bound,
\mathrm{HJ}(4,2)<10^{11}
, is due to Lavrov
[
6
]
.

2.2.
Combinatorial lines and bracket families

For a root
\tau
let
I(\tau)\subseteq[n]
denote its set of
active
coordinates (those carrying a star), and call
|I(\tau)|
the number of active coordinates of the line
L_{\tau}
. For
1\leq K\leq n
we write

L^{[K]}([t]^{n})=\{\,l:|I(\tau(l))|\leq K\,\}

for the family of combinatorial lines with at most
K
active coordinates, so that
L([t]^{n})=L^{[n]}([t]^{n})
is the family of all lines. The truncation parameter
K
plays no role in the main theorems — which use the case
K=n
— but it makes the reduction lemma below slightly more general at no cost.

2.3.
The van der Waerden lower bound

Van der Waerden’s theorem
[
10
]
asserts that for all
k,r
there is an
n
such that every
r
-coloring of
[n]
contains a monochromatic
k
-term arithmetic progression; the least such
n
is the van der Waerden number
W(k,r)
. The proof passes through the Hales–Jewett theorem and yields the following lower bound for
\mathrm{HJ}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For all
t,r
,

(1)

\mathrm{HJ}(t,r)\;\geq\;\left\lceil\frac{W(t,r)-1}{t-1}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Let
N=\mathrm{HJ}(t,r)
. For any
r
-coloring
c
of
[(t-1)N+1]
, color each
w\in[t]^{N}
by
c^{\prime}(w)=c(\sum_{i}w_{i}-N+1)
. A line with active set
I
and inactive weight
S
has
\tau(x)
of weight
S+x|I|
, mapping to the
t
-term progression
\{S-N+1+x|I|\}_{x=1}^{t}
. Since
|I|\geq 1
, this progression has a strictly positive common difference. By definition of
N
,
[t]^{N}
contains a
c^{\prime}
-monochromatic line, which yields a
c
-monochromatic
t
-term progression. Therefore,
W(t,r)\leq(t-1)N+1
, which rearranges to (
1
).
∎

Using the values
W(3,3)=27
and
W(4,2)=35
of Chvátal
[
1
]
, the bound (
1
) gives

\mathrm{HJ}(3,3)\geq 13\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{HJ}(4,2)\geq 12,

both of which we improve in Section
.

\par Write $n_{\mathrm{sym}}(t,r)$ for the \emph{symmetric threshold}, the largest
$n$ for which $[t]^{n}$ admits a line-free symmetric $r$-coloring, and
$\mathrm{HJ}^{[K]}(t,r)$ for the least $n$ forcing a monochromatic line with at most
$K$ active coordinates, so that $\mathrm{HJ}^{[n]}(t,r)=\mathrm{HJ}(t,r)$. A line-free
symmetric coloring is in particular line-free, whence
$$n_{\mathrm{sym}}(t,r)\leq\mathrm{HJ}(t,r)-1,$$with $n_{\mathrm{sym}}(3,3)\geq 21$ and $n_{\mathrm{sym}}(4,2)\geq 13$ by
Theorems~\ref{thm:hj33-22} and~\ref{thm:hj42-14}.
\par\begin{conjecture}$n_{\mathrm{sym}}(t,r)=\mathrm{HJ}(t,r)-1$ for all $t,r\geq 2$.
\end{conjecture}\par\par\begin{conjecture}For every $n\leq\mathrm{HJ}(t,r)$, the cube $[t]^{n}$ admits an $r$-coloring whose
only monochromatic line is the main diagonal.
\end{conjecture}\par\par\begin{conjecture}$\mathrm{HJ}^{[\mathrm{HJ}(t,r)-1]}(t,r)=\infty$ for all $t\geq 2$, $r\geq 2$.

\par\par Beyond the reduction lemma, the correctness of Theorems~\ref{thm:hj33-22} and~\ref{thm:hj42-14} rests only on a finite check of the corner tuples of the two witness tables. We performed this check with a standalone, dependency-free Python script {verify\_hj.py} (about $170$ lines, standard library only), provided as an ancillary file. It re-derives the cells of $T_{n}^{(t)}$ and all corner tuples from scratch, embeds the two witness tables \emph{byte-for-byte} (Tables~\ref{tab:T21} and~\ref{tab:T13}), and confirms no monochromatic tuple --- among $1771$ corner triples for Theorem~\ref{thm:hj33-22} and $1820$ corner quadruples for Theorem~\ref{thm:hj42-14} --- which by Lemma~\ref{lem:sym-reduction} proves each lift line-free. As an independent cross-check on the full cube it also samples $200{,}000$ random combinatorial lines, finding none monochromatic.
