Weak-strong uniqueness and low Mach number limit for a viscous compressible fluid around a rotating body
Thomas Eiter, Šárka Nečasová, Florian Oschmann

Abstract
. We study the flow of an isothermal compressible Newtonian fluid around a body that performs a (time-independent) rigid motion. We derive a weak-strong uniqueness principle, and show that in the low Mach number limit, the governing equation is well approximated by the Navier–Stokes equations for incompressible rotating flow. Both results are based on the derivation of a relative energy inequality for weak solutions to this exterior-domain problem.

1.
Introduction

We consider a rigid body
\mathcal{S}
that rotates with a fixed angular velocity
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
in the three-dimensional space filled
with a compressible viscous fluid.
In a frame attached to the body,
we denote the fluid domain by
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{S}}
,
which is the complement of the rigid body.
The fluid flow in
\Omega
is then governed by the Navier–Stokes equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\rho+\div(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))&=0&&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\boldsymbol{u})+\div(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\otimes\boldsymbol{u})+\rho\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{u}&=\rho\boldsymbol{f}+\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\nabla p(\rho)&&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{u}&=\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x&&\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\rho(\cdot,x)\to\rho_{\infty},\ \boldsymbol{u}(\cdot,x)&\to\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}&&\mbox{as }|x|\to\infty,\\
\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0},\ (\rho\boldsymbol{u})(0,\cdot)&=\rho_{0}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}&&\mbox{in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
T>0
is a given time horizon,
and
\rho\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\to[0,\infty)
and
\boldsymbol{u}\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

denote the unknown fluid density and velocity, respectively.
We further prescribe an external force
\boldsymbol{f}\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the density
\rho_{\infty}>0
and the velocity
\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

at spatial infinity, where we assume that
\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}
are parallel.
The viscous stress tensor
\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})
satisfies Newton’s rheological law

(1.2)

\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})=\mu\Big(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{T}-\frac{2}{3}\div\boldsymbol{u}\operatorname{Id}\Big)+\eta\div\boldsymbol{u}\operatorname{Id},

where
\mu>0
and
\eta\geq 0
denote shear and bulk viscosity coefficients, respectively.
The pressure
p(\rho)
satisfies

(1.3)

p\in C^{1}([0,\infty))\cap C^{2}((0,\infty)),\quad p(0)=0,\quad p^{\prime}(\rho)>0\ (\rho>0),\quad\liminf_{\rho\to\infty}\frac{p^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho^{\gamma-1}}>0

for some
\gamma>1
.
While the existence of weak solutions requires
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
,
see also Theorem
2.3
below,
we merely have to assume
\gamma>\frac{6}{5}
for the results established in this article, hence including a larger part of the physically relevant regime where
\gamma\in(1,\frac{5}{3}]
.

The first two lines of (
1.1
) describe mass conservation and momentum balance, respectively.
We assume that fluid particles are attached to the body,
which is reflected by no-slip boundary conditions.
The system is complemented by conditions at spatial infinity
as well as initial conditions
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{0}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}
for the density and the momentum, respectively.
For more details on the physical motivation, the derivation of the model,
and the transformation into the body frame,
we refer the reader to
[
20
]
.
See also
[
13
]
for the case of an incompressible fluid.
Additional to the rotation of
\mathcal{S}
, one could prescribe a time-independent
translational motion
with velocity
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
parallel to

\boldsymbol{\omega}
.
By a simple change of frames,
this is equivalent to considering (
1.1
) with
\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}
replaced with
\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\tau}
.
Therefore, this case is included in the setting treated here.

Concerning the mathematical theory of
compressible fluids,
the fundamental results on existence of global-in-time weak solutions in three dimensions
were obtained by P.-L. Lions
[
23
]

in the barotropic case with
p(\rho)=a\rho^{\gamma}
,
a>0
,
\gamma\geq\frac{9}{5}
,
and by Feireisl, Novotný, Petzeltová
[
6
]
,
who provided an extension to exponents
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
.
In particular, the latter result includes the physically relevant case

\gamma=\frac{5}{3}
of a monoatomic gas.
While these works consider the flow in a bounded domain,
the results have been generalized to a plethora of other configurations in the recent years.
Several results and further references
can be found in the monographs
[
4
,
21
,
27
]
,
where, among other cases, the flow around a body at rest,
the temperature-dependent case,
and the flow in moving domains are considered, respectively.
Concerning the present case of the flow around a rigid body with prescribed translational and rotational motion,
the existence of weak solutions to (
1.1
) was derived in
[
20
]
.
An existence result for a system describing self-propelled motion in an unbounded three-dimensional domain can be found in
[
24
]
.

The uniqueness of weak solutions to the compressible Navier–Stokes equations
is only known under restrictive assumptions,
see
[
17
]
for instance,
but it is still an open question in the general case, even in a bounded domain.
However, there are multiple results on weak-strong uniqueness,
that is, the property that any weak solution coincides with the (hypothetical) strong solution
with the same initial data
as long as the latter exists.
The first result in this respect is due to Germain
[
16
]
,
where the considered weak solutions satisfied a regularity assumption
that could not be expected to hold in general.
In
[
5
,
7
]
this condition was omitted,
and the weak-strong uniqueness property was instead
established for the class of weak solutions
constructed in
[
6
,
23
]
,
which satisfy an energy inequality,
so-called
finite-energy weak solutions
.
Since then, this result has been generalized in various ways, see e.g.
[
8
,
18
]
for the temperature-dependent case, or
[
22
,
26
]
for the case with moving rigid body in a bounded domain filled by a compressible barotropic fluid with homogeneous or inhomogeneous boundary data. For the case of a prescribed moving domain filled by a compressible fluid, the weak-strong uniqueness was shown in
[
21
]
.
One main result of the present article is to establish
a weak-strong uniqueness principle
for finite-energy weak solutions to (
1.1
),
see Theorem
4.2
below.

The weak-strong uniqueness results in all these works
were derived from a
relative energy inequality (REI)
.
In the present case, the relative energy is given by

(1.4)

E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})=\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}|^{2}+P(\rho|\sigma)

for suitable functions
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})
and
(\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})
,
where
the absolute and relative internal energy are defined as

(1.5)

\displaystyle H(\rho)=\rho\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{p^{\prime}(z)}{z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z,

\displaystyle P(\rho|\sigma)=H(\rho)-H^{\prime}(\sigma)(\rho-\sigma)-H(\sigma),

respectively.
Due to
p^{\prime}>0
, the function
H
is strictly convex,
and we have
E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})=0

if and only if
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})=(\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})
.
In this way, the relative energy provides a natural distance measure.
We derive a REI that describes (an inequality for)
the evolution of
E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})

when
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})
is a solution to (
1.1
).
More precisely, we consider finite-energy weak solutions
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})
to (
1.1
)
and sufficiently smooth comparison functions
(\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})
,
see Theorem
3.1
below.

Besides the weak-strong uniqueness principle,
we also use the REI to study
the limit of low Mach number,
which means that the flow velocity is small compared to
the speed of sound,
which is the case in many real-world applications.
In this setting, the fluid is nearly incompressible,
and one would expect convergence towards
the Navier–Stokes equations for incompressible fluids,
in our framework given by

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \div\boldsymbol{U}&=0&&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\rho_{\infty}\big(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{U}+\div((\boldsymbol{U}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\otimes\boldsymbol{U})+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{U}\big)&=\rho_{\infty}\boldsymbol{f}+\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{U})-\nabla\Pi&&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{U}&=\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x&&\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{U}(\cdot,x)&\to\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}&&\mbox{as }|x|\to\infty,\\
\boldsymbol{U}(0,\cdot)&=\boldsymbol{U}_{0}&&\mbox{in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\boldsymbol{U}\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\Pi\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}

are the velocity and pressure field of the incompressible fluid flow, respectively.
A brief sketch of the proof of existence,
further properties like asymptotic behavior, and an overview of the state-of-the-art theory on system (
1.6
)
can be found in the recent monograph
[
25
]
.

The mathematical study of the low Mach number limit for systems of equations describing a motion of fluids goes back to the seminal work of Klainerman and Majda
[
19
]
. In general, studying various types of singular limits allows to eliminate unimportant or unwanted features of the motion as a consequence of scaling and asymptotic analysis.
The mathematical analysis of singular limits in the frame of both strong and weak solutions was carried out in
[
15
,
28
]
and
[
3
,
4
]
, respectively.
We show that in the low Mach number limit,
solutions
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})
to (
1.1
)
can be approximated by
(\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U})
,
where
(\boldsymbol{U},\Pi)
solves (
1.6
),
see Theorem
5.1
below.
The analogous question in the case of pure rotation
and with a gravitational force, partial slip boundary conditions, and additional low Froude number limit (strong stratification)
was studied in
[
11
]
using compactness arguments.
In contrast to
[
11
]
,
we only consider so-called well-prepared initial data here,
which prevents the occurrence of acoustic waves, but we allow the fluid not to be at rest when
|x|\to\infty
.

General assumptions

Throughout the whole article, we make the following assumptions:
Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain
and
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{S}}
be an exterior domain
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega=\partial\mathcal{S}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
0\in{\rm int}\,\mathcal{S}
and
\overline{\mathcal{S}}\subset B_{1/2}(0)
.
We assume that
\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfy

\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}=0
, and that
\rho_{\infty}>0
.
The viscous stress and the pressure are given by (
1.2
) and (
1.3
)
with viscosity coefficients
\mu>0
and
\eta\geq 0
,
and with
\gamma>\frac{6}{5}
.
We consider an external force that satisfies

\boldsymbol{f}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}](\Omega))
.
For the initital data

(\rho_{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\times L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

we assume that

\rho_{0}\geq 0,\qquad E(\rho_{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{0}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\in L^{1}(\Omega),

that is, finiteness of the initial (relative) energy.

2.
Weak solutions

We introduce the class of weak solutions to (
1.1
)
satisfying an energy inequality.
Since we study the problem in an unbounded domain,
the formulation of the latter requires the consideration of a reference velocity
\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying

(2.1)

\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}(x)=\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x\quad\text{for }x\in\mathcal{S},\qquad\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}(x)=\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{1}.

Such a vector field can be constructed by standard methods. Observe that
\div\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})
.
Moreover, we have

E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\in L^{1}(\Omega)

if and only if

E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.1
.

We call a couple
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})
a
finite-energy weak solution
to system (
1.1
) with initial data
(\rho_{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{0})
if it satisfies:

•

the following regularity assumptions:

(2.2)

\begin{gathered}\rho\geq 0,\quad\rho-\rho_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{\gamma}+L^{2}](\Omega)),\\
\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}|^{2}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega)),\\
\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),\\
\boldsymbol{u}(t)|_{\partial\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x\text{ for a.e. }t\in(0,T).\end{gathered}

•

the weak form of the continuity equation: we have
\rho\in C_{weak}([0,T];L^{\gamma}(K))
for any compact
K\subset\overline{\Omega}
, and

(2.3)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho\partial_{t}\phi+\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\,\phi(0,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}x

for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
.

•

the weak form of the momentum equation: we have
\rho\boldsymbol{u}\in C_{weak}([0,T];L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(K))
for any compact
K\subset\overline{\Omega}
, and

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\psi

\displaystyle+\rho((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\otimes\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla\psi-\rho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}p(\rho)\div\psi-\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla\psi-\rho\boldsymbol{f}\cdot\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\cdot\psi(0,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}x

for any
\psi\in C_{c}^{1}([0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

•

the energy inequality:
there exists a reference velocity
\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
with (
2.1
)
such that for a.a.
\tau\in[0,T]
it holds

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}|^{2}+P(\rho|\rho_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\rho)\div\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{f}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Remark 2.2
.

At first glace,
the energy inequality (
2.5
) only yields a bound on

\nabla\boldsymbol{u}=\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})
in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
,
but not on the function
\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}
itself.
However, using the finiteness of the total energy
and the Poincaré inequality from
[

one can conclude
\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))
.

We have the following existence result:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
.
Then there exists a finite-energy weak solution to system (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

Proof.

Weak solutions to (
1.1
) were constructed in
[
20
]
for the case
\boldsymbol{f}=0
. However, it is evident that the same proof applies in the case
\boldsymbol{f}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}](\Omega))
.
For the validity of the energy inequality (
2.5
), see also
[
20
, Remark 6.1]
.
∎

As mentioned above,
although we cannot ensure the existence of solutions if
\gamma\leq\frac{3}{2}
,
all arguments in the subsequent sections will only make use of
the weaker assumption
\gamma>\frac{6}{5}
.

3.
Relative energy inequality

In this section, we derive a REI for weak solutions in the sense of Defintion
2.1
.
Recall the definition of the relative energy

E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})
from (
1.4
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})
be a finite-energy weak solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
.
Let
0<\underline{\sigma}\leq\rho_{\infty}\leq\overline{\sigma}<\infty
,
and let
(\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})\in C^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
be in the following regularity class:

(3.1)

\begin{gathered}\sigma-\rho_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{2}\cap L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega)),\qquad\underline{\sigma}\leq\sigma\leq\overline{\sigma},\\
\nabla\sigma\in L^{1}(0,T;[L^{2}\cap L^{4}\cap L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega)),\qquad\partial_{t}\sigma\in L^{1}(0,T;[L^{2}\cap L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega)),\\
(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\sigma\in L^{1}(0,T;[L^{1}\cap L^{2}\cap L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega)),\\
\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{2}\cap L^{4}\cap L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega)),\\
\nabla\boldsymbol{V}\in L^{1}(0,T;[L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\cap L^{\infty}](\Omega))\cap L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega),\\
(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{V}\in L^{1}(0,T;[L^{2}\cap L^{4}\cap L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega)),\\
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}\in L^{1}(0,T;[L^{2}\cap L^{4}\cap L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega)).\end{gathered}

Further assume that

\boldsymbol{V}=\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x
on
(0,T)\times\partial\Omega
.
Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{f})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{V}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\rho)-p(\sigma))\div\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-\sigma)\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma)+(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)-\sigma(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
.

Remark 3.2
.

To see where the assumptions (
3.1
) come from, let us rewrite the REI (
3.2
) in the following form, where we also used that

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot((\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot((\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot\boldsymbol{f}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\boldsymbol{f}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{V}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{V}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\rho)-p^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})(\rho-\rho_{\infty})-p(\rho_{\infty}))\div\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})[(\rho-\rho_{\infty})-(\sigma-\rho_{\infty})]\div\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\sigma)-p^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})(\sigma-\rho_{\infty})-p(\rho_{\infty}))\div\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-\rho_{\infty})H^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\partial_{t}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\infty}-\sigma)H^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\partial_{t}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot H^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\nabla\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-\rho_{\infty})(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot H^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\nabla\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot H^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\nabla\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot H^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\nabla\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot H^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\nabla\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using the regularity of
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})
mentioned in (
2.2
), which also gives us
\sqrt{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(L^{2\gamma}+L^{4}+L^{\infty})
and hence

\displaystyle\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})=\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\rho}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\in L^{\infty}(L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}+L^{4/3}+L^{2}),

leads to the regularity from (
3.1
),
where we also use the general facts
(L^{p}\cap L^{q})^{\prime}=L^{p^{\prime}}+L^{q^{\prime}}
and
L^{p}\cap L^{q}\subset L^{p}\subset L^{p}+L^{q}
.

In the case
\boldsymbol{\omega}=0
, formula (
3.2
) corresponds to the usual REI as can be found, for instance, in
[
5
]
. Note also that this inequality is independent of
\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}
and thus of the choice of
\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}
.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we first consider the case that
(\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})
differs from the limit values
(\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})
only on a compact set.

Lemma 3.3
.

In addition to the assumptions from Theorem
3.1
, assume that
that there exists
R>1
such that

\sigma(t,x)=\rho_{\infty}
and
\boldsymbol{V}(t,x)=\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}
for
t\in(0,T)
and
|x|>R
.
Then (
3.2
) holds for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

We first use the momentum equation (
2.4
) tested by
\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}
to see

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{u}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\otimes\boldsymbol{u}:\nabla(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\rho)\div(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{f}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Note that this choice of test function is admissible due to the definition of
\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}
in (
2.1
) and the requirement that
\boldsymbol{V}=\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}
for
|x|>R
.
Similarly, we can use
\frac{1}{2}|\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}|^{2}
as test function in the continuity equation (
2.3
), which yields

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

By the identity

|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}|^{2}=|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}|^{2}-2(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})

and the energy inequality (
2.5
), we infer

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}|^{2}+P(\rho|\rho_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}|^{2}+P(\rho|\rho_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\left[\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}|^{2}-\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\rho)\div\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{f}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\otimes\boldsymbol{u}:\nabla(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\rho)\div(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{f}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\left[\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V})\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{f}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\rho)\div\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\left[\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau},

where we additionally used that

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})-(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})+(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=-(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}).

Lastly, we generate the term
P(\rho|\rho_{\infty})
that shall replace
P(\rho|\sigma)
. To this end,
recall (
1.5
) and find

P(\rho|\sigma)-P(\rho|\rho_{\infty})=-\rho(H^{\prime}(\sigma)-H^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty}))+\sigma H^{\prime}(\sigma)-H(\sigma)-\rho_{\infty}H^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})+H(\rho_{\infty}).

With the identity

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\sigma H^{\prime}(\sigma)-H(\sigma)-\rho_{\infty}H^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})+H(\rho_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\sigma H^{\prime}(\sigma)-H(\sigma)-\rho_{\infty}H^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})+H(\rho_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\sigma\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

we thus obtain

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}P(\rho|\sigma)-P(\rho|\rho_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{\Omega}-\rho(H^{\prime}(\sigma)-H^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty}))\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\sigma\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

We further test the continuity equation (
2.3
) with
H^{\prime}(\sigma)-H^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})
, which is admissible due to
\sigma=\rho_{\infty}
for
|x|>R
,
to get

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\rho(H^{\prime}(\sigma)-H^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty}))\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma)+\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

With these two additional identities, we find

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left[\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}|^{2}+P(\rho|\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\left[\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}|^{2}+P(\rho|\rho_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad+\left[\int_{\Omega}P(\rho|\sigma)-P(\rho|\rho_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V})\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{f}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\rho)\div\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\left[\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma)+\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\sigma\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Finally, let
\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}^{R}
be a function satisfying

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}^{R}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad\div\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}^{R}=0,\qquad\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}^{R}(x)=\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x&\text{for }x\in B_{R},\\
\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{2R}.\end{cases}

Existence of such a function can be shown with the help of the Bogovskiĭ operator,
compare the construction of
\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}
in (
5.8
) below.
We then have by Gauß’ theorem

\displaystyle 0=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\div(p(\sigma)(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}^{R}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\sigma)\div\boldsymbol{V}+(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}^{R})\cdot\nabla p(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

As
\sigma=\rho_{\infty}
outside
B_{R}
, we have
\nabla p(\sigma)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{R}
,
and we find together with
\nabla p(\sigma)=\sigma\nabla H^{\prime}(\sigma)
that

\displaystyle 0=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\sigma)\div\boldsymbol{V}+\sigma(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Moreover, using
\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})
as test function in the continuity equation (
2.3
) gives

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla(\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Employing both identities in (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}|^{2}+P(\rho|\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V})\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho\boldsymbol{f}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{U}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{V}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\rho)-p(\sigma))\div\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-\sigma)\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma)+(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)-\sigma(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

The first and the fourth integral on the right-hand side
add up to

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{u})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

so that we finally find (
3.2
), provided that
(\sigma,\boldsymbol{U})=(\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})
for
|x|>R
.
∎

To omit the additional assumptions from Lemma
3.3
,
we proceed with an approximation argument.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\phi(s)=1
for
s<1
and
\phi(s)=0
for
s>2
, and define
\phi_{R}(x)=\phi(|x|/R)
for
R>1
.
We approximate
(\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})
by pairs
(\sigma_{R},\boldsymbol{V}_{R})
defined as

\sigma_{R}(t,x)=\phi_{R}(x)\sigma(t,x)+(1-\phi_{R}(x))\rho_{\infty},\qquad\boldsymbol{V}_{R}(t,x)=\phi_{R}(x)\boldsymbol{V}(t,x)+(1-\phi_{R}(x))\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}

such that
\supp(\sigma_{R}-\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{V}_{R}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\subset(0,T)\times B_{2R}
. By Lemma
3.3
, we then have the REI

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma_{R},\boldsymbol{V}_{R})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}_{R})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}_{R}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}_{R}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V}_{R}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}_{R}-\boldsymbol{f})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\rho)-p(\sigma_{R}))\div\boldsymbol{V}_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-\sigma_{R})\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma_{R})+(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)-\sigma_{R}(\boldsymbol{V}_{R}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(\sigma_{R})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
.
To shorten the presentation, we have collected the viscous terms on the left-hand side.
For conclusion of (
2.5
), we now pass to the limit
R\to\infty

in each term separately.

First of all, we have

\displaystyle E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})-E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma_{R},\boldsymbol{V}_{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\rho\big[-2(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})+|\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}|^{2}+2(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}_{R}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})-|\boldsymbol{V}_{R}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}P(\rho\mid(1-\theta)\sigma_{R}+\theta\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\rho\big[2(\phi_{R}-1)(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})+(1-\phi_{R}^{2})|\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle-(1-\phi_{R})(\sigma-\rho_{\infty})\int_{0}^{1}H^{\prime\prime}\big(\sigma-(1-\theta)(1-\phi_{R})(\sigma-\rho_{\infty})\big)\big[\rho-\sigma-(1-\theta)(1-\phi_{R})(\sigma-\rho_{\infty})\big]\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

where we used (
1.5
).
Since
\sqrt{\rho}|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}|\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

and
\rho=(\rho-\rho_{\infty})+\rho_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{2}+L^{\gamma}+L^{\infty}](\Omega))
, see Definition
2.1
,
the integrability properties of
\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}
stated in (
3.1
) imply
that the first term on the right-hand converges to
0
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
as
R\to\infty
, which is due to the fact that
\phi_{R},\phi_{R}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}1
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
For the second term, we use the identity

\displaystyle(1-\phi_{R})(\sigma-\rho_{\infty})\big[\rho-\sigma-(1-\theta)(1-\phi_{R})(\sigma-\rho_{\infty})\big]

\displaystyle=(1-\phi_{R})(\sigma-\rho_{\infty})(\rho-\rho_{\infty})-(1-\phi_{R})\big[1-(1-\theta)(1-\phi_{R})\big](\sigma-\rho_{\infty})^{2}

and conclude the convergence to
0
in a similar way,
using that convex combinations of
\sigma
and
\rho_{\infty}
are bounded from above and below.
In summary, we see
E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma_{R},\boldsymbol{V}_{R})\to E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
as
R\to\infty
.

For the diffusive term, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})-\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}_{R})=\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\big[(1-\phi_{R})\nabla\boldsymbol{V}-(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\otimes\nabla\phi_{R})\big].

Due to the convergences

\phi_{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}1
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

\nabla\phi_{R}\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{3}(\Omega)
as
R\to\infty
,
which one readily verifies,
and due to (
3.1
),
which also implies
\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))

by Sobolev inequality,
we conclude the convergence

\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}_{R})\to\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})

in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
as
R\to\infty
.

To derive convergence of the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.4
),
we observe that

\displaystyle\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{f})

\displaystyle-\rho(\boldsymbol{V}_{R}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}_{R}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V}_{R}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}_{R}-\boldsymbol{f})

\displaystyle=(1-\phi_{R})\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{f})

\displaystyle+(1-\phi_{R})\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{V}-a_{\infty})+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V})

\displaystyle+\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi_{R}).

In virtue of the integrability assumptions from (
2.2
) and (
3.1
)
and the aforementioned convergence properties of
\phi_{R}
and
\nabla\phi_{R}
,
one concludes that all terms on the right-hand side converge to
0
in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
as
R\to\infty
.

For the remaining terms in (
3.4
), we can use similar arguments as before.
In particular, for the last term, we use the representation

\displaystyle(\rho-\sigma)\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma)+(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)-\sigma(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=H^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\big[(\rho{-}\rho_{\infty})-(\sigma{-}\rho_{\infty})\big]\partial_{t}\sigma+H^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\big[\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\rho}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})-\sigma(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})+(\rho{-}\sigma)(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\big]\cdot\nabla\sigma.

Since
\rho-\rho_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{2}+L^{\gamma}](\Omega))
,
and
\sqrt{\rho}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
,
this explains the regularity assumptions on
\partial_{t}\sigma
and
\nabla\sigma
in (
3.1
).
Finally, passing to the limit
R\to\infty
in (
3.4
) leads to (
3.2
)
and completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

In the proof of Lemma
3.3

we did not make use of any structural assumptions on the pressure
p
,
so that the statement keeps valid for any
p\in C^{0}([0,\infty))\cap C^{1}((0,\infty))
.
For the approximation argument to
obtain Theorem
3.1
,
we use the growth conditions from (
1.3
),
but the restriction on
\gamma
could be relaxed to
\gamma>1
.

4.
Weak-strong uniqueness

In this section, we show that a weak solution to (
1.1
) coincides with a strong one emanating from the same initial data, at least as long as the latter exists.
To this end, we make use of the following Korn-type inequality.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. For any
\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})
it holds

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\Big\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla^{T}\boldsymbol{u}-\frac{2}{3}\div\boldsymbol{u}\operatorname{Id}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Proof.

First, let
\boldsymbol{u}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Then integration by parts yields

\Big\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla^{T}\boldsymbol{u}-\frac{2}{3}\div\boldsymbol{u}\operatorname{Id}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}2|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}+2\nabla\boldsymbol{u}:\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{T}-\frac{4}{3}(\div\boldsymbol{u})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}2|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}+\frac{2}{3}(\div\boldsymbol{u})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
(\div\boldsymbol{u})^{2}\geq 0
, this gives the asserted inequality for
\boldsymbol{u}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
The general case follows by a standard approximation argument.
∎

Using the REI established in Theorem
3.1
,
we derive the following weak-strong uniqueness result.
Our argument follows the presentation in
[
2
]
and
[
9
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})
be a finite-energy weak solution to system (
1.1
) with initial data
(\rho_{0},\boldsymbol{u}_{0})
.
Let further
(\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})=(r,\boldsymbol{v})
belong to the regularity class specified in (
3.1
) and satisfy

(4.1)

\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{3}\cap L^{\frac{6\gamma}{5\gamma-6}}](\Omega)),\qquad\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega)).

Moreover, assume that
(r,\boldsymbol{v})
satisfies the system (
1.1
) with the same initial data as
(\rho,\boldsymbol{u})
.
Then
\rho=r
and
\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{v}
a.e. in
(0,T)\times\Omega
.

Proof.

We use the momentum equation satisfied by
(r,\boldsymbol{v})
, specifically,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{v}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{f}=\frac{1}{r}\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}-\frac{1}{r}\nabla p(r).

Moreover, the continuity equation for
(r,\boldsymbol{v})
implies

\displaystyle(r-\rho)(\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(r)+\nabla H^{\prime}(r)\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))

\displaystyle=(r-\rho)H^{\prime\prime}(r)(\partial_{t}r+\nabla r\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))

\displaystyle=(r-\rho)H^{\prime\prime}(r)(-r\div(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))

\displaystyle=(\rho-r)p^{\prime}(r)\div\boldsymbol{v}.

Then the REI (
3.2
), together with
r^{-1}\nabla p(r)=\nabla H^{\prime}(r)
, yields

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\Bigg[\frac{1}{r}\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}-\frac{1}{r}\nabla p(r)\Bigg]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\rho)-p(r))\div\boldsymbol{v}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-r)p^{\prime}(r)\div\boldsymbol{v}-\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(r)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\rho}{r}-1\Big)(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\big(p(\rho)-p^{\prime}(r)(\rho-r)-p(r)\big)\div\boldsymbol{v}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

where we integrated by parts in the second term of the first inequality and used that
(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. To treat the viscous terms on the left-hand side, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\Big\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})+\nabla^{T}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})-\frac{2}{3}\div(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\operatorname{Id}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\mu\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where we prolonged
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{v}
by
\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x
outside of
\Omega
, and used the Korn-type inequality from Lemma
4.1
.
The first integral on the right-hand side of (
4.2
) is easily estimated as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Introducing the essential and residual part of a function
f
as

\displaystyle[f]_{\mathrm{ess}}=\chi_{\frac{1}{2}\leq\rho/r\leq 2}f,

\displaystyle[f]_{\mathrm{res}}=f-[f]_{\mathrm{ess}},

we find that the relative internal energy is coercive with respect to
\rho

in the sense that

(4.3)

[\rho]_{\mathrm{res}}^{\gamma}+[1]_{\mathrm{res}}+[\rho-r]_{\mathrm{ess}}^{2}\leq CP(\rho|r),

where
C>0
only depends on
\underline{r}
and
\overline{r}
if
\underline{r}\leq r\leq\overline{r}
,
see
[
10
, Equ. (4.23)]
or
[
18
, Equ. (5.9)]
.
For the second integral on the right-hand side of (
4.2
), we now calculate for the residual part that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\rho}{r}-1\Big]_{\mathrm{res}}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\big(\|[\rho/r]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+\|[1]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\big)\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{\frac{6\gamma}{5\gamma-6}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{\frac{6\gamma}{5\gamma-6}}(\Omega)}^{2}(\underline{r}^{-2}\|[\rho]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|[1]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{2})+\delta\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where
\delta>0
is arbitrary, and where we used the Sobolev inequality.
In view of the coercivity estimate (
4.3
),
we find for
\frac{6}{5}\leq\gamma\leq 2
that

\displaystyle\|[\rho]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|[1]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}\leq C\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x,

since also
E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
.
If
\gamma>2
and
\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))
, we may estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\rho}{r}-1\Big]_{\mathrm{res}}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\big(\|[\rho/r]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|[1]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}(\underline{r}^{-2}\|[\rho]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|[1]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})+\delta\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

by the Sobolev inequality.
By
\gamma>2
and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle[\rho]_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}+[1]_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}\lesssim[\rho]_{\mathrm{res}}^{\gamma}+[1]_{\mathrm{res}}\lesssim E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})

due to (
4.3
).
Consequently, for any
\gamma\geq\frac{6}{5}
, we arrive at

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\rho}{r}-1\Big]_{\mathrm{res}}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\|\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{2}\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

with
p=\max\{\frac{6\gamma}{5\gamma-6},3\}
.
An analogous argument for the essential part leads to

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\rho}{r}-1\Big]_{\mathrm{ess}}(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\underline{r}^{-1}\|[\rho-r]_{\mathrm{ess}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The very last pressure integral in (
4.2
) is handled similarly.
Here we use Taylor’s theorem for the essential part,
exploiting
p\in C^{2}((0,\infty))
,
and Young’s inequality for the residual part, and find (compare
[
18
, Section 5.4, Step 4]
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\div\boldsymbol{v}\big(p(\rho)-p^{\prime}(r)(\rho-r)-p(r)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\div\boldsymbol{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big(\|[\rho-r]_{\mathrm{ess}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|[p(\rho)]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|[1]_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\div\boldsymbol{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we used
p(\rho)\lesssim 1+\rho^{\gamma}
and (
4.3
).

Gathering all estimates, choosing
\delta>0
small enough to absorb the dissipative terms to the left-hand side of (
4.2
),
and employing (
4.1
),
we are left with the inequality

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}E(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|r,\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Applying Grönwall’s inequality completes the proof.
∎

5.
Low Mach number limit

In this section, we consider system (
1.1
), but with a rescaled pressure of the form

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\rho+\div(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))&=0&&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\boldsymbol{u})+\div(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\otimes\boldsymbol{u})+\rho\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{u}&=\rho\boldsymbol{f}+\div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\nabla p(\rho)&&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{u}&=\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x&&\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\rho(\cdot,x)\to\rho_{\infty},\ \boldsymbol{u}(\cdot,x)&\to\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}&&\mbox{as }|x|\to\infty,\\
\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0},\ (\rho\boldsymbol{u})(0,\cdot)&=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}&&\mbox{in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

The additional factor
\varepsilon^{-2}
in front of the pressure plays the role of a low Mach number. In turn, we obtain the rescaled relative energy

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})=\frac{1}{2}\rho|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}P(\rho|\sigma)

and find the relative energy inequality of the form

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\boldsymbol{u}|\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{u})\cdot(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{V}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V}+((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{f})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{V}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{V})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\rho)-p(\sigma))\div\boldsymbol{V}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-\sigma)\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\sigma)+(\rho(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)-\sigma(\boldsymbol{V}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x))\cdot\nabla H^{\prime}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

which follows from Theorem
3.1
.
We shall use (
5.2
) to quantify the convergence
of solutions to (
5.1
) (for
\varepsilon>0
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
This limit passage formally leads to the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations (
1.6
).
We specify this low Mach number limit, where the convergence is stated in terms of the relative energy.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
let
(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})
be a finite energy weak solution to system (
5.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
, emanating from the initial data
(\rho_{\varepsilon,0},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon,0})
. Let
(\boldsymbol{U},\Pi)
be such that the pair
(\sigma,\boldsymbol{V})=(\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U})
lies in the regularity class specified in (
3.1
), with the additional properties

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \div\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{U})&\in L^{2}(0,T;[L^{2}\cap L^{3}\cap L^{\frac{6\gamma}{5\gamma-6}}](\Omega)),&\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{U}&\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})),\\
\nabla\Pi&\in L^{2}(0,T;[L^{2}\cap L^{3}\cap L^{\frac{6\gamma}{5\gamma-6}}](\Omega)),&\Pi&\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega),\end{aligned}

and such that system (
1.6
) is satisfied
for some initial data
\boldsymbol{U}_{0}
with
\boldsymbol{U}_{0}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
If

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon,0},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon,0}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U}_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

then

(5.5)

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U})(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

The existence of local-in-time strong solutions
(\boldsymbol{U},\Pi)
to (
1.6
)
was shown in
[
12
, Therorem 1]
for
\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}=0
, and global-in-time strong solutions were found in
[
12
, Theorem 2]
for small initial data and small rotation. We emphasize that the regularity of the strong solutions in
[
12
]
differs from ours.
For extensions of this existence result,
allowing even for time-dependence of
\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}
,
see the monograph
[
25
]
,
the recent article
[
1
]
,
and references therein.

Under additional assumptions on the pressure
\Pi
, we can show the following quantified version of Theorem
5.1
:

Theorem 5.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, assume additionally that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\Pi,\,(\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\Pi\in L^{1}(0,T;[L^{1}\cap L^{2}\cap L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega)),&&\Pi(0,\cdot)\in[L^{1}\cap L^{2}\cap L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega).\end{aligned}

Then

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U})(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x+\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon,0},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon,0}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U}_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x+C(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}),

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Remark 5.3
.

Thanks to the coercivity properties of the relative energy, we also have

\displaystyle\|[\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}]_{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega))}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon,0},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon,0}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U}_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x+C(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}),

where we define the
\varepsilon
-dependent essential and residual part of a function
f
as

(5.7)

[f]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}=\chi_{\frac{1}{2}\leq\rho_{\varepsilon}/\rho_{\infty}\leq 2}f,\qquad[f]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}=f-[f]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}.

The following two subsections are devoted to the proofs of Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
.

5.1.
Qualitative convergence, proof of Theorem
5.1

We split the proof of Theorem
5.1

into several lemmas.
First, we use the REI (
5.2
) to derive suitable bounds on
(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})
.

Lemma 5.4
.

The weak solutions
(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})
satisfy the
\varepsilon
-uniform bounds

\displaystyle\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega))}^{\gamma}+\|[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2},

\displaystyle\|[\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

where the essential and residual parts are defined as in (
5.7
).

Proof.

Since the limiting system (
1.6
) is incompressible, we wish to take an incompressible test function that is not “too far away” from
\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}
in the REI (
3.2
). To this end, we first recall the notion of the Bogovskiĭ operator.
Let
q\in(1,\infty)
.
By
L_{0}^{q}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})
we denote the set of all functions
f\in L^{q}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})
with
\int_{B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S}}f\,\mathrm{d}x=0
.
Since
B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, there exists a bounded linear operator
\mathcal{B}:(L_{0}^{q}\cap C^{\infty})(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})\to C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S};\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

\displaystyle\div\mathcal{B}(f)

\displaystyle=f\ \text{ in }\ B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(f)\|_{W^{1,q}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})}

for any
f\in(L_{0}^{q}\cap C^{\infty})(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})
,
see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem III.3.1]
.
Recalling the definition of
\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}
from (
2.1
), we immediately see that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\div\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\,\mathrm{d}S=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\,\mathrm{d}S=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}\div\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\div(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

and hence
\div\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\in(L_{0}^{q}\cap C^{\infty})(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})
for any
q\in(1,\infty)
. In turn, we define

(5.8)

\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}=\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\mathcal{B}(\div\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Note especially that we have
\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}|_{\partial\mathcal{S}}=\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}|_{\partial\mathcal{S}}=\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x
and
{\rm supp}(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty})\subset\overline{B}_{1}

due to
\mathcal{B}(f)|_{\partial(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})}=0
for any
f\in L_{0}^{q}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S})
. Moreover,
\div\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and

(5.9)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\Big(\|\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\|_{W^{1,q}(B_{1})}+\|\div\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}\Big)\leq C\|\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\|_{W^{1,q}(B_{1})}.

By (
5.4
), we further have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon,0},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon,0}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U}_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim 1.

In particular, since
\boldsymbol{U}_{0}-\boldsymbol{a}_{\infty}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
this implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon,0},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon,0}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim 1.

Now we use the REI (
5.2
) with test functions
(\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})
.
In view of
\partial_{t}H^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})=0
and
\nabla H^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})=0
, we find

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}+((\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{f})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot((\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Note that the choice

satisfies (

)
in Theorem

.
In particular, we have
\nabla\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}=0
outside the bounded domain

, and

.
Having this in mind, we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot((\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}|\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

and for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{B_{1}}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}+\delta\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\|_{L^{2}((0,\tau)\times\Omega)}^{2}.

For the first integral on the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\boldsymbol{b}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\boldsymbol{b}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\boldsymbol{b}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Similarly to the estimate (

),
we see that the relative energy is coercive in the sense that

(5.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big([\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}^{\gamma}+[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}+[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}^{2}\Big)+[\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq CE_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})

with a constant

only depending on
\rho_{\infty}
, and thus independent of
\varepsilon
. Note that

and
\boldsymbol{b}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}](\Omega))
since

. Hence, we find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\boldsymbol{b}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{6}(\Omega))}\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\delta\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\|_{L^{2}((0,\tau)\times\Omega)}^{2}

for any
\delta>0

For the residual part, we use Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities multiple times to conclude

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\boldsymbol{b}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\rho_{\varepsilon}|\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}|^{2}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\rho_{\varepsilon}|\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}|^{2}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

due to (

and
\varepsilon<1

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim 1.

The coercivity of the relative energy from (

)
now enforces

\displaystyle\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega))}^{\gamma}+\|[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2},

\displaystyle\|[\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1.

Returning to
\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}
, we use (
5.9
) to see that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\|[\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\|[\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|[\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1.

In conclusion, we obtain the asserted bounds.
∎

From the uniform estimates obtained in Lemma

, we can extract convergent subsequences.

Lemma 5.5
.

There exists a
(not relabeled) subsequence of
(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})

such that

\displaystyle{[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]}_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\to 0

\displaystyle\text{ strongly in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\to 0

\displaystyle\text{ strongly in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\to 0

\displaystyle\text{ strongly in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\Omega)),\ p\in[1,\infty),

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup^{\ast}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\text{ weakly-$\ast$ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})

\displaystyle\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

for some
\boldsymbol{u}
with

\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

and
\div\boldsymbol{u}=0
.

Proof.

If we take into account that

\|[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\Omega))}^{p}=\|[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}^{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}=\|[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}

for any
1\leq p<\infty
,
the existence of a convergent subsequence in the asserted topologies follows directly from Lemma
5.4
.
It only remains to show that
\boldsymbol{u}
is divergence free.
To this end, we take the limit in the weak form of the continuity equation.
For any
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty})\partial_{t}\phi+\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

For the first part, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty})\partial_{t}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega))}+\|[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}

and the right-hand side vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
by the convergences obtained before. For the second term, we rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using the convergences of
[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}
,
[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}
, and
[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}
, the last integral converges to

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

For the first integral, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty})(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\rho_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty})(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\rho_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\div(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|[\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{ess},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\to 0,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}[\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\Big(\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega)}+\|[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega)}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\|[\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}+\|[1]_{{\mathrm{res},\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega))}\to 0,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\div(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\div(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Gathering the terms above, we infer

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty})\partial_{t}\phi+\rho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\infty}\div(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty})\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{U}_{\infty}-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\infty}\phi\div\boldsymbol{u}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
,
leading to
\div\boldsymbol{u}=0
.
∎

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\infty}}(\boldsymbol{U}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\infty}}-1\Big)(\boldsymbol{U}-\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty})\cdot\nabla\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\infty}}-1\Big)(\boldsymbol{V}_{\infty}-\omega\times x)\cdot\nabla\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\infty}}-1\Big)\partial_{t}\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\left[\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\infty}}-1\Big)\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}.

Splitting as before in essential and residual part, and using that

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\infty}}-1\Big)\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\leq C\|\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{2}+L^{\gamma}](\Omega))}\|\Pi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{2}\cap L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}](\Omega))}\leq C(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\gamma}})

due to (
5.6
),
we thus infer

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\infty}}(\boldsymbol{U}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla\Pi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}).

Back to (
5.13
), we find

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U})\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U})):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\gamma}})+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

so that Grönwall’s inequality finally yields

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U})(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U})):\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\boldsymbol{U})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega}E_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon,0},\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon,0}|\rho_{\infty},\boldsymbol{U}_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}\Big).

We finish the proof of Theorem
5.2
by applying Korn’s inequality from Lemma
4.1
.
∎
