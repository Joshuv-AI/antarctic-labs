Technical note: clinical observations of a modified BOPT protocol with advanced surface refinement
Authors: Radan Kassab, Amal Hasan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis uncontrolled prospective case series evaluated the 12-month biological and periodontal outcomes of a novel modified Biologically Oriented Preparation Technique (BOPT). This protocol specifically integrated piezoelectric subgingival finish-line refinement, subgingival mechanical polishing, and four-step ultra-polished zirconia restorations to optimize gingival thickness (GT), marginal positional stability, and peri-restorative tissue health. Four patients requiring fixed prostheses (totaling 35 teeth) were treated using this integrated modified BOPT framework. Following bulk tooth reduction, finish lines were refined using specialized piezoelectric ultrasonic tips and smoothed subgingivally with silicone points. The subgingival emergence profiles of definitive zirconia crowns were managed through a strict four-step ultra-polishing protocol. Periodontal variables—including GT changes across different baseline phenotypes, gingival margin stability, plaque index, bleeding on probing, and gingival stippling—were recorded at baseline, 1 week, 3, 6, and 12 months. At 12 months, stable gingival margins were maintained with no observable tissue recession across all monitored sites. A notable descriptive increase in GT occurred, particularly within the baseline thin phenotype group, which shifted from 0.59 ± 0.09 mm to 1.14 ± 0.23 mm. Furthermore, favorable soft-tissue responses were observed, characterized by minimal bleeding scores and a widespread clinical reappearance of gingival stippling at the 1-year follow-up interval. Within the study’s limitations, this modified BOPT protocol demonstrated promising trends regarding soft-tissue thickening and short-term marginal stability. Integrating piezoelectric refinement with rigorous clinical and laboratory zirconia polishing protocols exhibits excellent biological integration and soft-tissue maturation. Incorporating piezoelectric finishing tools, subgingival silicone polishing, and meticulous zirconia ultra-polishing within a BOPT philosophy offers a tissue-friendly, biologically guided approach that effectively supports peri-restorative tissue harmony and phenotype stability around fixed restorations.Keywords: Vertical tooth preparation, Gingival phenotype, Modified BOPT, Piezoelectric finishing, Zirconia ultra-polishing, Soft tissue stability

Introduction

Historically, success in fixed prosthodontics centered on mechanical performance and structural durability. However, substantial evidence indicates that many restoration failures stem from biological complications, notably secondary caries and progressive periodontal disease (Felton et al. 1991; Gavelis et al. 1981; Jacobs and Windeler 1991; SORENSEN et al. 1986). Modern restorative dentistry emphasizes a “bio-integrated” paradigm. This approach mandates that preserving periodontal health must coexist with mechanical longevity (Loi 2013).

The Biologically Oriented Preparation Technique (BOPT) represents a significant departure from traditional tooth preparation geometries (Loi 2013). Conventional horizontal finish lines often require aggressive removal of sound tooth structure. They can also restrict adaptive gingival remodeling. Conversely, the vertical philosophy eliminates a predefined macroscopic finish line (Loi 2013; Rosenstiel et al. 2022). By eradicating this geometric limit, BOPT redefines the dynamic interface between the prepared tooth, prosthetic margin, and surrounding soft tissues (Loi, 2013).

This technique exploits the inherent capacity of marginal gingival tissues to remodel and adapt around a prosthetic emergence profile. However, the supracrestal tissue attachment must be strictly respected (Loi 2013). Landmark periodontal principles demonstrate that vertical preparation geometries support excellent tissue compatibility. The qualitative precision and smooth adaptation of the prosthetic interface govern long-term soft-tissue health, rather than a rigid finish line location (Carnevale et al. 1990).

Preventing gingival recession around crown margins remains a formidable clinical challenge. This issue is driven by baseline gingival phenotype, mechanical trauma, and prosthetic inaccuracies like over-contouring (Loi 2013; Merijohn 2016; Podhorsky et al. 2015). Thin gingival phenotypes may be more susceptible to recession (Claffey and Shanley 1986), Meanwhile, thick phenotypes typically respond via pocket formation and vertical osseous defects (Müller et al. 2003). Within this context, the vertical approach aims to stabilize tissue responses. This facilitates an increase in local tissue volume and creates a stable, aesthetic gingival architecture (Maggio et al. 2020).

Achieving optimal marginal adaptation is crucial to minimize micro-gaps (Goodacre et al. 2001). Historically, subgingival finishing relied on rotary burs, which frequently generate pronounced dentinal roughness (Syu et al. 1993). In contrast, piezoelectric ultrasonic instrumentation enables precise refinement of the subgingival tooth structure. This technology smoothens the vertical interface while safeguarding the adjacent delicate marginal gingiva from mechanical laceration (Stübinger et al. 2005).

Furthermore, applying subgingival silicone rubber polishers eliminates micro-irregularities. This step provides a surface conducive to periodontal health (Jefferies 2007; Quirynen and Bollen 1995) Attaining an ultra-smooth topography below the critical threshold of 0.2 μm deters initial bacterial adherence (Quirynen and Bollen 1995). Consequently, this refined surface promotes a stable biological width and minimizes chronic margin inflammation (Jefferies 2007; Quirynen and Bollen 1995).

Additionally, integrating a multi-stage polishing protocol for definitive zirconia restorations substantially decreases surface roughness and subsequent bacterial colonization (Al-Mulla and Al-Haddad 2023; Vohra et al. 2017).

While the clinical benefits of the standard BOPT approach are documented, prospective clinical data evaluating a combined protocol remain scarce. To date, no clinical studies have investigated a combined protocol that integrates piezoelectric ultrasonic refinement, localized subgingival silicone polishing of the tooth structure, and a four-step ultra-polished monolithic zirconia workflow. Previous research has only evaluated these modalities independently, leaving a gap in understanding their synergistic effects on periodontal health. Therefore, this uncontrolled prospective case series aimed to evaluate the 12-month biological and periodontal responses to this newly modified BOPT protocol, specifically tracking descriptive trends in gingival thickness conversion, marginal stability, and soft-tissue health indicators.

Materials and methods

Study design and participant selection

This uncontrolled prospective case series was conducted fro m May 2023 to May 2025 at the Department of Fixed Prosthodontics, Faculty of Dentistry, University of Aleppo, Syria. The study enrolled four systemically healthy adult patients (N = 4; age range: 25–55 years) with high oral hygiene compliance, providing 35 teeth (n = 35) located between the maxillary and mandibular second premolars. This manuscript was prepared in accordance with the CARE guidelines for reporting clinical cases. Although 35 teeth were evaluated, the patient was considered the primary clinical unit, and the findings should be interpreted cautiously because multiple teeth were clustered within individual patients.

Included teeth required single zirconia crowns fabricated via a modified Biologically Oriented Preparation Technique (BOPT) due to advanced attrition, extensive caries, or failing restorations. Candidates were excluded if they presented with chronic systemic illnesses affecting healing, heavy smoking (> 10 cigarettes/day), severe parafunctional habits (bruxism, clenching, or temporomandibular disorders), or unwillingness to attend follow-up evaluations.

Clinical protocol and procedural sequence

Pre-operative periodontal and diagnostic phase

Prior to preparation, patients underwent phase-I non-surgical periodontal therapy to eliminate plaque-induced inflammation. Preliminary anatomical impressions were taken using alginate (Hydrogum; Zhermack, Badia Polesine, Italy) in modified perforated trays to fabricate diagnostic wax-ups on Type IV dental stone models. Using these models, provisional shell restorations were prefabricated from CAD/CAM milled polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) blanks, intentionally designed with slight under-preparation to ensure subsequent passive intraoral seating.

Clinical tooth preparation within the modified BOPT framework

Before mechanical reduction, baseline sulcus depth and alveolar crest position were determined via periodontal probing and transgingival bone sounding. For intact teeth, initial supragingival reduction was performed using a coarse 120 μm tapered diamond bur under water cooling, whereas existing restorations were disassembled to preserve sound tissue.

Subgingival vertical preparation and circumferential gingitage were executed within the modified BOPT framework using a tapered diamond bur (REF 863.314.014, Edenta AG, AU/SG, Switzerland) to dilate the sulcus, eliminate the cemento-enamel junction (CEJ) or existing finish lines, and create adequate vertical space for the future prosthetic emergence profile.

The vertical subgingival root surface was refined using a sequential three-step protocol:

Rotary Smoothing: Micro-roughness was reduced using fine-grit tapered diamond burs (20–30 μm, REF F863.314.014, Edenta AG).

Rotary Smoothing: Micro-roughness was reduced using fine-grit tapered diamond burs (20–30 μm, REF F863.314.014, Edenta AG).

Piezoelectric Ultrasonic Refinement: Advanced subgingival smoothing was performed using a piezoelectric ultrasonic scaler equipped with a specialized micro-refinement tip (H1 tip, Satelec, Acteon Group, France) under continuous 0.9% sterile saline irrigation to prevent thermal damage and structural micro-cracks.

Provisionalization and soft-tissue guidance

The prefabricated CAD/CAM PMMA shells were relined chairside over the isolated abutments using a dual-cured bis-acrylic temporary material (Care C&B, Vericom, Korea). After polymerization, the deepest apical limit of the internal relined interface was demarcated using a graphite pencil to guide the customized contouring. The space between this marked boundary and the external anatomical margin was optimized using a light-cured flowable composite (EsFlow, Spident, Korea) to support the gingival architecture and establish the new prosthetic emergence profile.

Following finishing and ultra-high gloss polishing, the provisional restorations were cemented during the initial appointment using a non-eugenol temporary resin cement (ChemiTemp Orange, Spident) to avoid chemical interference with future definitive resin bonding. This immediate stabilization aimed to support tissue healing and position the therapeutic prosthetic margin 0.5–1.0 mm subgingivally, preserving the supracrestal tissue attachment.

Final impression and sulcular displacement

Following a 3-to-6-week soft-tissue maturation phase, definitive marginal stability was clinically verified. A dual-cord gingival displacement protocol was implemented to expose the subgingival therapeutic space (Figs. 4: a). Definitive impressions were captured using a high-precision condensation silicone material (Zhermack SpA, Badia Polesine, Italy) within rigid metal impression trays (Medesy, Italy) to ensure dimensional stability.

Visual inspection of the elastomeric impressions consistently revealed a single, continuous, circumferential line of silicone material representing the streamlined vertical interface and sulcular morphology. This topographical presentation directly contrasts with conventional horizontal preparations (shoulders/chamfers), which characteristically manifest as two distinct parallel lines representing the finish line geometry and the deeper sulcular architecture (Fig. 4: c).

Laboratory protocols and margin customization

The modified BOPT framework operates within a dynamic vertical “finishing area” rather than a rigid horizontal finish line, allowing customized tissue modeling. Within this therapeutic zone, the definitive prosthetic margin was established using a standardized laboratory sequence:

Marginal reference marking

A black reference line was marked on the definitive stone model 0.5 mm coronal to the cast’s gingival margin.

Gingival guttering

The simulated gingival tissue on the master cast was carefully removed to completely expose the subgingival preparation plane.

Apical boundary delineation

The most apical extent of the clinical preparation was identified and highlighted with a blue line; the zone between the coronal black line and apical blue line defined the therapeutic “Finishing Area.”

Definitive margin customization

The final prosthetic finish line was established within this area and marked in red, positioned in strict accordance with the pre-recorded clinical sulcus depth and aesthetic parameters.

Following margin delineation, the stone material apical to the red customized line was ditched. Restoration emergence profile management

Laboratory configuration

The subgingival emergence profile was fabricated with a continuous feather-edge geometry tapering to an apical knife-edge configuration to prevent over-contouring (Fig. 5).

Clinical try-in and adjustment

The profile was evaluated intraorally by marking the free gingival margin onto the zirconia crown using a graphite line, guided by two criteria:

Vertical extension

Crowns exceeding the 0.5–1.0 mm therapeutic sulcus depth were shortened to preserve supracrestal tissue attachment.

Horizontal contour

If localized gingival blanching (transient ischemia) occurred, the horizontal profile was thinned and re-polished to eliminate soft-tissue pressure (Fig. 5).

Four-step ultra-polishing of zirconia restoration and cementation

Gingival-contact zirconia surfaces were polished to a mirror-like finish using the HI GLAZE kit (Dedeco, USA) and M.P.F ALLBRIGHT paste (Cyprus) through a sequential four-step protocol (Fig. 6). Under rubber dam isolation, the crowns were sandblasted, ultrasonically cleaned, treated with a 10-MDP primer, and definitively cemented with a dual-cure resin cement.

Clinical follow-up and periodontal evaluation

Patients were clinically evaluated at 1 week, along with 1, 3, 6, and 12 months post-cementation. At each recall interval, Plaque Index (PI), Gingival Index (GI), and the presence of anatomical stippling were recorded. Additionally, gingival thickness was measured transgingivally 2.0 mm apical to the free gingival margin using an endodontic file and a digital caliper. To ensure measurement accuracy and control clinical bias, all evaluations were performed by a single, pre-calibrated examiner; each site was measured three times consecutively, and the repeated measurements were averaged. Based on these values, the peri-prosthetic tissues were categorized into thin (0.5–1.0 mm), moderate (1.0–2.0 mm), or thick (> 2.0 mm) phenotypes.

Longitudinal gingival margin stability was quantified relative to the definitive crown margin using a three-point scoring system: Score (+ 1) indicated coronal overlap (subgingival restoration), Score (0) denoted perfect coincidence, and Score (-1) represented apical recession. Results

Only descriptive statistics (means, standard deviations, and percentages) were calculated because of the exploratory nature of this case series and the limited sample size. Clinical tracking showed a positive trend in oral hygiene. At the 12-month recall, 88.6% of patients reported brushing twice daily, and 11.4% brushed three times daily. Concurrently, marked reductions occurred in Plaque Index (PI) and Gingival Index (GI) scores. Pre-operatively, 82.9% of teeth exhibited moderate plaque. At 12 months, 80.0% of sites recorded a score of zero, indicating no visible plaque. Bleeding on probing (BOP) was documented in 88.6% of cases at baseline, with a progressive and near-complete resolution observed across all sites at the 1-year follow-up interval.

Complete cumulative gingival margin stability was achieved in 100% of the evaluated sites. No localized gingival recession occurred throughout the 12-month period. Baseline transgingival thickness (GT) ranged from 0.5 mm to 2.22 mm. Pre-operatively, 57.1% of sites presented a thin phenotype, 31.4% a moderate phenotype, and 11.4% a thick phenotype. At 1 year, the thin phenotype prevalence decreased to 25.7%. Conversely, moderate and thick phenotypes increased to 51.4% and 22.9%, respectively. The initial thin phenotype cohort demonstrated the most prominent tissue volume expansion (Table 1). Additionally, classical anatomical stippling (“orange-peel” appearance) was observed in 85.7% of cases 1 week post-cementation, reaching 100% at the 1-year follow-up.

Discussion

Piezoelectric micro-surgery offers distinct clinical advantages over conventional rotary instruments in hard-tissue management (Vercellotti 2004). Driven by selective cutting kinetics, piezoelectric tips precisely modify mineralized dental structures while minimizing iatrogenic trauma to adjacent soft tissues (Stübinger et al. 2005). This cutting selectivity limits structural injuries to the sulcular epithelium, which may optimize early post-operative healing and favorably alter the initial response of the gingival phenotype to subgingival margins (Vercellotti et al. 2005). Conversely, conventional high-speed air turbines frequently induce microscopic irregularities, non-parallel finish lines, and torque-related structural defects along the margin, potentially compromising the adaptation of CAD/CAM restorations (Rapani et al. 2023; Renne et al. 2012, 2015). Utilizing piezoelectric refinement may be associated with a smoother dentin topography, contributing to enhanced marginal fidelity.

Building upon this refined preparation, achieving a highly biocompatible subgingival micro-environment was further supported by restricting high-speed air turbines to initial macro-reduction and reserving specialized piezoelectric tips for subgingival finishing (Jadid and Al-Zahawi 2025) followed by secondary silicone polishing. In the Biologically Oriented Preparation Technique (BOPT), eliminating the cementoenamel junction facilitates a customized prosthetic emergence profile (Agustín-Panadero et al. 2020). Our descriptive outcomes align with previous observations demonstrating that specific piezoelectric geometries induce high dentin smoothness during root surface treatment (Da Costa Noble et al. 1993), which may support peri-prosthetic soft-tissue health and marginal adaptation. Because conventional rotary diamond burs often leave microscopic defects that can compromise the long-term marginal seal (Jefferies 2007), maintaining a surface roughness parameter (Ra) below 0.2 μm via silicone polishing is vital for subgingival biomaterials. This mechanical approach may limit initial pathogen attachment and subgingival biofilm maturation (Quirynen and Bollen 1995) more effectively than conventional glazing, as glazed zirconia often retains a higher microscopic roughness that may unfavorably affect the adjacent soft-tissue architecture (Linkevicius et al. 2020; Ribeiro et al. 2025).

Minimizing this microbial challenge promotes a stable biological width, potentially mitigating the risks of localized chronic inflammation or marginal recession (Jefferies 2007; Quirynen and Bollen 1995).

These technical and biological optimizations reflected favorably on our 12-month clinical outcomes. A descriptive increase in gingival thickness (GT) occurred in both the thin gingival phenotype (0.54 ± 0.20 mm) and moderate gingival phenotype (0.46 ± 0.14 mm) groups, comparing favorably with the 2-year BOPT benchmarks of Agustín-Panadero et al. (2018) (0.41 ± 0.28 mm) for single crowns and (0.38 ± 0.38 mm) for bridges (Agustín-Panadero et al. 2018). Conversely, the thick gingival phenotype group exhibited a minimal descriptive increase (0.18 ± 0.08 mm). This subtle variation, alongside the absence of pathological pockets (Müller et al. 2003), suggests that the modified protocol may serve as a stabilizer and enhancer for the gingival phenotype. Histologically, immediate subgingival provisional placement stabilizes the initial blood clot, which differentiates into a mature attachment (Agustín-Panadero et al. 2020). This tissue adaptation is further supported by the coronal connective tissue migration driven by mechanotransduction mechanisms (Chiquet et al. 2009; Rodríguez et al. 2017). Crucially, complete positional stability of the treated gingival margins was maintained throughout the 12-month follow-up. This consistency, even in vulnerable thin gingival phenotypes, mirrors the biological concepts of Perelli et al. (2019), demonstrating that gentle, customized mechanical stimulation may successfully induce soft-tissue thickening and predictable creeping attachment without surgical flaps (Perelli et al. 2019).

In addition to dimensional stability, plaque dynamics and tissue maturation showed highly positive trends. At 12 months, a minimal plaque layer was observed in only 20% of evaluated sites, representing a promising trend compared to BOPT literature reporting plaque accumulation up to 38.9% at two years (Agustín-Panadero et al. 2018). Mild inflammation was restricted to 8.6% of cases with a 0% Bleeding on Probing (BOP) rate, comparing favorably with the 19.5% incidence reported by Agustín-Panadero et al. (2018). Furthermore, a progressive, descriptive reappearance of classic “orange-peel” gingival stippling was clinically observed. While BOPT literature generally focuses on dimensional changes, this surface micro-topography remains largely unreported. Clinically, stippling reflects an adapted relationship between the epithelium and dense connective tissue papillae, typically indicating a lack of overt inflammatory edema (Newman et al. 2018). Establishing this healthy morphology may be associated with functional collagen fiber bundles and optimal epithelial keratinization (Beertsen 2015) supported by the biocompatible subgingival environment.

Strengths and limitations of the study

The primary strength of this evaluation is its prospective documentation of a novel, multi-stage hybrid BOPT workflow. This protocol standardizes selective piezoelectric finishing, definitive mechanical polishing, and a meticulous four-stage zirconia ultra-polishing regimen.

However, several methodological limitations must be acknowledged. First, the 35 treated teeth were clustered within only four patients; this clustering effect lacks strict statistical independence, which may introduce bias and limit wider generalizability. Second, as an uncontrolled prospective case series, the absence of a control group prevents establishing direct causal relationships. Finally, longitudinal changes in probing depth (PD) and clinical attachment level (CAL) were not quantified. Fourth, the 12-month follow-up period represents a short-to-medium-term evaluation, which restricts the ability to confirm the definitive long-term stability of the remodeled tissues. Furthermore, heightened patient oral hygiene compliance acts as a major confounding factor, which may be associated with the resolution of gingival inflammation independently of the prosthetic modifications.

Conclusions

Within the limitations of this uncontrolled prospective case series, this modified BOPT protocol demonstrated favorable soft-tissue responses over 12 months. Descriptive trends indicate thickness gains in thin phenotypes, complete positional stability of the marginal gingiva, and qualitative visual improvements in stippling. These preliminary findings suggest promising trends in tissue maturation around ultra-polished zirconia interfaces under strict plaque control. However, further long-term randomized clinical trials with larger, independent patient samples are required to confirm these outcomes.

Clinical implications and recommendations

Clinically, integrating piezoelectric technology into the BOPT workflow offers a precise, micro-conservative alternative to traditional rotary finishing methods. This approach potentially optimizes the subgingival micro-environment and supports soft-tissue preservation.
