Selflessness for twisted group C ∗ C^{*} -algebras of amenable groups and their inclusions
Tron Omland

Abstract
. For a discrete amenable group G G with a two-cocycle σ \sigma we first record a few results on when the twisted group C ∗ C^{*} -algebra C r ∗ ​ ( G , σ ) C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma) is selfless, in the sense of Robert. In particular, for an infinite finitely generated virtually nilpotent G G , this holds exactly when ( G , σ ) (G,\sigma) satisfies Kleppner’s condition. For the larger class of FC-hypercentral groups the same holds modulo 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -stability, equivalently finite nuclear dimension. Further, using the relative Kleppner condition we obtain corresponding selflessness results for inclusions C r ∗ ​ ( H , σ ′ ) ⊆ C r ∗ ​ ( G , σ ) C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma) , when H H is a normal subgroup of G G . For amenable G G such an inclusion is selfless precisely when C r ∗ ​ ( H , σ ′ ) C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime}) is selfless and ( H ≤ G , σ ) (H\leq G,\sigma) satisfies the relative Kleppner condition. Thus, for an infinite finitely generated virtually nilpotent G G , selflessness of the inclusion C r ∗ ​ ( H , σ ′ ) ⊆ C r ∗ ​ ( G , σ ) C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma) is equivalent to the relative Kleppner condition.

1.
Introduction

A tracial
C^{*}
-probability space
(A,\rho)
is
selfless

[
19
]
if the first-factor embedding
(A,\rho)\hookrightarrow(A,\rho)\star(A,\rho)
into the reduced free product is existential, or equivalently, if there is a trace-preserving embedding of the reduced free product into an ultrapower
(A^{\omega},\rho^{\omega})
that restricts to the diagonal embedding on the first copy of
A
. Selflessness is a free-probabilistic regularity property that forces
A
to be simple and have a unique tracial state, with strict comparison and stable rank one. For non-amenable groups, reduced (twisted) group
C^{*}
-algebras have been shown selfless for large classes of groups
[
1
,
18
,
16
,
12
,
9
]
. This note concerns the amenable side, where the study of selflessness combines the Toms–Winter theory and the classical question of when
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is simple.

The arguments combine four inputs: In the nuclear setting selflessness coincides with the regularity package “simple
+
unique tracial state
+

\mathcal{Z}
-stable”
[
19
,
14
,
16
]
. For amenable groups a unique tracial state implies simplicity
[
4
]
. For FC-hypercentral groups a unique tracial state and simplicity are both equivalent to Kleppner’s condition
[
3
,
4
]
. Finally, twisted group
C^{*}
-algebras of virtually polycyclic groups have finite nuclear dimension
[
7
]
. For simple infinite-dimensional algebras this is equivalent to
\mathcal{Z}
-stability, so it is automatic in the infinite finitely generated virtually nilpotent case.

For inclusions we use four further inputs: The first is the theory of
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusions
[
22
]
. The second is the equivariant
\mathcal{Z}
-stability of twisted actions of countable amenable groups on simple, nuclear,
\mathcal{Z}
-stable
C^{*}
-algebras with a unique tracial state. For genuine actions this is due to
[
23
,
10
,
28
]
, while the twisted actions (or cocycle actions) we need are covered by the equivariant
\mathcal{Z}
-stability Theorem
[
25
]
, which applies to twisted actions (Lemma

). The
\mathcal{Z}
-stability of the inclusion needs no outerness or other condition on the dynamics, it is automatic once
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
has a unique tracial state and is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. The third is the relative Kleppner condition of
[
4
,
5
]
that handles
C^{*}
-irreducibility and the relative Dixmier property. Finally, we apply the selfless inclusion criterion of
[
12
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a discrete group with a two-cocycle
\sigma
. We write
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
for the reduced twisted group
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the
\sigma
-projective left regular representation
\{\lambda_{\sigma}(g)\}_{g\in G}
on
\ell^{2}(G)
, and
\tau
for its canonical faithful tracial state, determined by
\tau(\lambda_{\sigma}(g))=\delta_{g,e}
. Then
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is unital, and it is nuclear if and only if
G
is amenable, see e.g.,
[
15
, Proposition 2.14]
. In that case it is also exact and the full and reduced twisted group
C^{*}
-algebras coincide.

Definition 2.1

(Kleppner
[
13
]
)
.

An element
g\in G
is
\sigma
-regular
if
\sigma(g,h)=\sigma(h,g)
for all
h\in G
with
gh=hg
. One checks that
\sigma
-regularity is a property of conjugacy classes. The pair
(G,\sigma)
satisfies
Kleppner’s condition
if every nontrivial
\sigma
-regular conjugacy class is infinite (equivalently, if the twisted group von Neumann algebra
W^{*}(G,\sigma)
is a factor
[
13
]
). Kleppner’s condition is necessary, but in general not sufficient, for
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
to be simple or to have a unique tracial state.

We collect the external results used below and use the label
Fact
for statements not proved here.

Fact 2.2

(folklore, cf.
[
3
]
)
.

Every FC-hypercentral group is amenable, and every subgroup of an FC-hypercentral group is again FC-hypercentral. Every virtually nilpotent group is FC-hypercentral, and every finitely generated virtually nilpotent group is virtually polycyclic. Moreover, every FC-hypercentral group has polynomial growth, so by Gromov’s theorem, for finitely generated groups, virtually nilpotent, FC-hypercentral, and polynomial growth are all equivalent.

Fact 2.3

(Eckhardt–Wu
[
7
]
)
.

If
G
is virtually polycyclic, then
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
has finite nuclear dimension.

Fact 2.4

(Murphy, see
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

If
G
is amenable and
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
has a unique tracial state, then
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is simple.

Fact 2.5

(
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

If
G
is FC-hypercentral and
(G,\sigma)
satisfies Kleppner’s condition, then
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
has a unique tracial state. Thus, by Fact
2.4
, simplicity of
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
, a unique tracial state of
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
, and Kleppner’s condition for
(G,\sigma)
are equivalent in this case.

3.
Selflessness

Definition 3.1

(Robert
[
19
]
)
.

A
tracial
C^{*}
-probability space
is a pair
(A,\tau)
with
A
unital and
\tau
a tracial state. A morphism
(A,\tau)\to(B,\rho)
is a unital
*
-homomorphism
\theta
with
\rho\circ\theta=\tau
. Such
(A,\tau)
, with
A\neq\mathbb{C}
and
\tau
faithful, is
selfless
if the first-factor embedding

(A,\tau)\longrightarrow(A,\tau)\star(A,\tau)

into the reduced free product is existential, that is, for some free ultrafilter
\omega
there is a trace-preserving embedding

(A,\tau)\star(A,\tau)\hookrightarrow(A^{\omega},\tau^{\omega})
restricting to the diagonal embedding on the first factor.

We always regard
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
as a tracial
C^{*}
-probability space with its canonical trace
\tau
. Thus “
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is selfless” means
(C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma),\tau)
is selfless. Likewise all inclusions of twisted group
C^{*}
-algebras are taken with respect to the canonical traces, which we suppress from the notation.

Fact 3.2

(Robert
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

A selfless tracial
C^{*}
-algebra is simple, has a unique tracial state, has strict comparison, and has stable rank one.

Fact 3.3

(Ozawa
[
16
, Theorem 3]
, cf.
[
12
]
)
.

A simple, separable, unital, exact,
\mathcal{Z}
-stable
C^{*}
-algebra with a unique tracial state is selfless.

Fact 3.4

(Matui–Sato
[
14
]
)
.

A simple, separable, unital, nuclear, infinite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra with a unique tracial state and strict comparison is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable.

Fact 3.5

(Toms–Winter
[
27
,
6
]
)
.

For simple, separable, unital, nuclear, infinite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras, finite nuclear dimension and
\mathcal{Z}
-stability are equivalent.

Let
A
be separable, unital, nuclear and infinite-dimensional. Then
A
is selfless if and only if
A
is simple, has a unique tracial state, and is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. Indeed, selflessness implies simplicity, unique tracial state and strict comparison by Fact
3.2
, hence
\mathcal{Z}
-stability by Fact
3.4
. Conversely, simplicity, exactness,
\mathcal{Z}
-stability and unique tracial state imply selflessness by Fact
3.3
. By Fact
3.5
, in this equivalence one may replace
\mathcal{Z}
-stability by finite nuclear dimension. Moreover, for a unique tracial state
\mathcal{Z}
-stability gives strict comparison
[
20
]
, and strict comparison gives back
\mathcal{Z}
-stability by Fact
3.4
. So under a unique tracial state the three regularity properties
\mathcal{Z}
-stability, finite nuclear dimension and strict comparison coincide.

Proposition 3.6

(Amenable groups)
.

Let
G
be a countably infinite amenable group with a two-cocycle
\sigma
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is selfless;

(ii)

C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
has a unique tracial state and is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable;

(iii)

C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
has a unique tracial state and has finite nuclear dimension.

Proof.

In (ii) and (iii)
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
has a unique tracial state, so it is simple by Fact
2.4
, and the equivalences follow directly from the above.
∎

Corollary 3.7

(FC-hypercentral groups)
.

Let
G
be a countably infinite FC-hypercentral group with a two-cocycle
\sigma
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is selfless;

(ii)

(G,\sigma)
satisfies Kleppner’s condition and
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable;

(iii)

(G,\sigma)
satisfies Kleppner’s condition and
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
has finite nuclear dimension.

Proof.

By Fact
2.5
, Kleppner’s condition for
(G,\sigma)
is equivalent to
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
having a unique tracial state. Since
G
is amenable, the claims follow from Proposition
3.6
.
∎

Theorem 3.8

(Virtually nilpotent groups)
.

Let
G
be an infinite finitely generated virtually nilpotent group with a two-cocycle
\sigma
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is selfless;

(ii)

(G,\sigma)
satisfies Kleppner’s condition.

Proof.

By Fact
2.2
,
G
is both FC-hypercentral and virtually polycyclic. Hence
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
has finite nuclear dimension by Fact
2.3
. Under Kleppner’s condition,
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is moreover simple by Fact
2.5
and infinite-dimensional, hence
\mathcal{Z}
-stable by Fact
3.5
. Thus condition (ii) of Corollary
3.7
reduces to Kleppner’s condition, and the equivalence follows from Corollary
3.7
.
∎

The result for virtually nilpotent groups is via finite nuclear dimension, which is stable under finite extensions, while
\mathcal{Z}
-stability is
not
stable under (twisted) finite-group crossed products. In the non-selfless situations, there is no known characterization of
\mathcal{Z}
-stability of
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
in general. For finitely generated nilpotent groups, an alternative approach is
[
8
]
, where
\mathcal{Z}
-stability is shown equivalent to nowhere scatteredness and to a non-rationality of
\sigma
that is implied by Kleppner’s condition. Whether this criterion, or Kleppner’s condition itself, gives
\mathcal{Z}
-stability or finite nuclear dimension for FC-hypercentral groups in general is not clear.

Therefore, the question of whether Theorem
3.8
generalizes to all FC-hypercentral groups is open. In this regard, the next example may be instructive.

Example 3.9

(Infinite-dimensional noncommutative tori)
.

Consider the free abelian group
G=\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}\mathbb{Z}
on an infinite set of generators
(e_{j})_{j\geq 1}
, which is not finitely generated. Let
\sigma
be the antisymmetric two-cocycle determined by

\lambda_{\sigma}(e_{j})\lambda_{\sigma}(e_{k})=e^{2\pi it_{jk}}\,\lambda_{\sigma}(e_{k})\lambda_{\sigma}(e_{j}),\qquad j<k,\qquad t_{jk}\in[0,1).

If at least one of the
t_{jk}
’s is irrational, then
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable by an inductive limit argument, but not necessarily simple, and may have either finite or infinite nuclear dimension. However, if it is simple, i.e., if
(G,\sigma)
satisfies Kleppner’s condition, then it is selfless by Corollary
3.7
, and has finite nuclear dimension.

On the other hand, consider
\sigma
defined by
t_{jk}=\frac{1}{j+k}
. Even though each value of
\sigma
is a root of unity,
(G,\sigma)
satisfies Kleppner’s condition. Indeed, a nonzero
x=\sum_{j\leq N}a_{j}e_{j}
is
\sigma
-regular only if
\sum_{j\leq N}a_{j}/(j+k)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
k>N
. The left-hand side is a nonzero rational function of
k
which tends to
0
as
k\to\infty
. Hence, for all sufficiently large
k
, it lies in
(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
, and therefore is not an integer. Thus no nonzero
x
is
\sigma
-regular, and by Fact
2.5
the algebra
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is simple with a unique tracial state.

Whether
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable in this case appears to be open. The group
G
is not finitely generated, so the criterion of
[
8
]
does not apply. By Fact
3.5
and
[
6
]
, it is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable if and only if its nuclear dimension is at most
1
. Since it is not AF (indeed
K_{1}(C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma))\neq 0
, by continuity of
K
-theory and the nonvanishing of
K_{1}
for the rational noncommutative tori in this inductive system), the nuclear dimension is therefore either
1
, the
\mathcal{Z}
-stable case, or infinite, and which of these occurs is unknown.

4.
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusions

Throughout the rest of the paper
H\trianglelefteq G
and
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma|_{H\times H}
, and the algebras carry the canonical traces. The canonical inclusion
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is unital and trace-preserving. It admits a faithful trace-preserving conditional expectation
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)\to C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
, and
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)\cong C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\rtimes_{r,(\alpha,u)}K
with
K=G/H
and
(\alpha,u)
the canonical twisted action of
K
on
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})

[
2
,
4
]
.

Definition 4.1

(
[
22
]
)
.

A unital inclusion
B\subseteq A
of separable
C^{*}
-algebras is a
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusion
if there is an isomorphism
A\to A\otimes\mathcal{Z}
that restricts to an isomorphism
B\to B\otimes\mathcal{Z}
.

Fact 4.2

(Sarkowicz
[
22
, Theorem 4.4]
)
.

Let
B\subseteq A
be a unital inclusion of separable
C^{*}
-algebras. Then
B\subseteq A
is a
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusion if and only if there is a unital embedding
\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow B^{\omega}\cap A^{\prime}
into the relative central sequence algebra (where the relative commutant is formed inside
A^{\omega}
, so that
B^{\omega}
and
A
sits in
A^{\omega}
as constant sequences).

The main tool of this section is an equivariant
\mathcal{Z}
-stability statement for twisted actions in the unique-trace case (Lemma
4.3
). For genuine actions the unique-trace case is due to
[
23
,
10
,
28
]
.

Lemma 4.3

(Equivariant
\mathcal{Z}
-stability from unique tracial state)
.

Let
D
be a simple, separable, unital, nuclear, infinite-dimensional,
\mathcal{Z}
-stable
C^{*}
-algebra with a unique tracial state
\tau
. Let
(\beta,w)
be a twisted action of a countable amenable group
K
on
D
. The cocycle takes values in
\mathcal{U}(D)
, so each
\mathrm{Ad}\,w(k,l)
acts trivially on the central sequence algebra
D^{\omega}\cap D^{\prime}
, and
(\beta,w)
induces a genuine action of
K
on
D^{\omega}\cap D^{\prime}
. Then there is a unital embedding
\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow(D^{\omega}\cap D^{\prime})^{K}
.

Proof.

We verify the hypotheses of
[
25
, Theorem 5.20]
for
D
. The group
K
is countable amenable. The algebra
D
is separable, simple, and nuclear. It is non-elementary because it is simple and infinite-dimensional. It is finite because it is unital with a faithful trace. Being
\mathcal{Z}
-stable it has strict comparison, hence the very weak comparison required in
[
25
]
. The trace
\tau
is the only tracial state, so
D
has a single ray of extremal traces. Under these hypotheses
[
25
, Theorem 5.20]
gives equivariant
\mathcal{Z}
-stability for every action of
K
on
D
, and by
[
25
, Remark 5.25]
the same holds for cocycle actions. Applied to
(\beta,w)
it yields the unital embedding
\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow(D^{\omega}\cap D^{\prime})^{K}
.
∎

The next proposition is the main step, which gives the
\mathcal{Z}
-stability of the inclusion from regularity of
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
alone.

Proposition 4.4

(
\mathcal{Z}
-stability of the inclusion)
.

Let
G
be a countable amenable group with a two-cocycle
\sigma
and let
H\trianglelefteq G
be infinite. Suppose that
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
has a unique tracial state and is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. Then
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is a
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusion.

Proof.

Write
K=G/H
,
A=C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
, and
B=C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
, and identify
A\cong B\rtimes_{r,(\alpha,u)}K

[
2
,
4
]
. Here
K
is countable amenable as a quotient of
G
, and
(\alpha,u)
is the canonical twisted action of
K
on
B
. Since
H
is countable amenable,
B
is unital, separable, and nuclear. By hypothesis it has a unique tracial state and is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. So it is infinite-dimensional and, by Fact
2.4
, simple.

By Fact
4.2
it suffices to produce a unital embedding
\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow B^{\omega}\cap A^{\prime}
. The algebra
A
is generated by
B
and the implementing unitaries
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in K}
. So an element
x\in B^{\omega}
commutes with
A
if and only if it commutes with
B
and with each
\lambda_{k}
. Since
\lambda_{k}x\lambda_{k}^{*}=\alpha_{k}^{\omega}(x)
, this means
x\in B^{\omega}\cap B^{\prime}
and
\alpha_{k}^{\omega}(x)=x
for all
k
. The cocycle
u(k,l)\in\mathcal{U}(B)
adds no condition because
x
commutes with
B
. So
k\mapsto\alpha_{k}^{\omega}
is a genuine action of
K
on
B^{\omega}\cap B^{\prime}
, and

B^{\omega}\cap A^{\prime}=(B^{\omega}\cap B^{\prime})^{K}.

Lemma
4.3
applies to
(\alpha,u)
on
B
and gives a unital embedding
\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow(B^{\omega}\cap B^{\prime})^{K}=B^{\omega}\cap A^{\prime}
. By Fact
4.2
,
B\subseteq A
is a
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusion.
∎

No outerness of the quotient action is needed for Proposition
4.4
. The unique tracial state of
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
makes the trace simplex a point and removes the orbit conditions in Lemma
4.3
. The same input can be read through the equivariant uniform property
\Gamma

[
26
]
, which is equivalent to equivariant
\mathcal{Z}
-stability and, for a unique tracial state, holds with no condition on the dynamics. For a general trace simplex it is not known whether equivariant
\mathcal{Z}
-stability still holds for arbitrary actions of amenable groups on simple, nuclear,
\mathcal{Z}
-stable
C^{*}
-algebras
[
24
, Problem XXXIX]
.

5.
Selfless inclusions

We now add the dynamical condition that turns the
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusion of Section
4
into a selfless inclusion. The condition is the relative Kleppner condition. We first recall the notions involved.

Definition 5.1

(Hayes–Kunnawalkam Elayavalli–Patchell–Robert
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(A,\tau)
be a tracial
C^{*}
-probability space and
B\subseteq A
a unital
C^{*}
-subalgebra, regarded as a tracial
C^{*}
-probability space with
\tau|_{B}
. The inclusion
B\subseteq(A,\tau)
is a
selfless inclusion
if, for some nontrivial
C^{*}
-probability space
(C,\kappa)
, the first-factor embedding of inclusions

(B\subseteq A)\longrightarrow(B\star C\subseteq A\star C)

is existential, or equivalently, if for some free ultrafilter
\omega
there is a trace-preserving embedding
(A,\tau)\star(C,\kappa)\hookrightarrow(A^{\omega},\tau^{\omega})
restricting to the diagonal embedding on
A
and carrying
B\star C
into
B^{\omega}
.

Fact 5.2

(Hayes–Kunnawalkam Elayavalli–Patchell–Robert
[
12
]
)
.

If
B\subseteq(A,\tau)
is a selfless inclusion, then every intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebra
B\subseteq D\subseteq A
is selfless (with
\tau|_{D}
). In particular both
B
and
A
are selfless, and
B\subseteq A
is
C^{*}
-irreducible
[
21
]
.

Definition 5.3

(
[
5
, Definition 3.6]
)
.

For
g\in G
, write
g^{H}=\{\,hgh^{-1}:h\in H\,\}
for its
H
-conjugacy class, and call
g

\sigma
-regular relative to
H
if
\sigma(g,h)=\sigma(h,g)
for all
h\in H
with
gh=hg
. This is a property of
H
-conjugacy classes. The triple
(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the
relative Kleppner condition
if every nontrivial
H
-conjugacy class in
G
that is
\sigma
-regular relative to
H
is infinite. This condition incorporates Kleppner’s condition for
(H,\sigma^{\prime})
and implies it for
(G,\sigma)
.

We follow the convention of
[
5
, Definition 3.6]
. In
[
4
, Definition 4.5]
only
H
-conjugacy classes in
G\setminus H
are required to be infinite, so Kleppner’s condition for
(H,\sigma^{\prime})
is not built in. The two conventions agree once
(H,\sigma^{\prime})
satisfies Kleppner’s condition, which holds throughout the results below.

Fact 5.4

(
[
5
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

The inclusion
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is
C^{*}
-irreducible if and only if
(H,\sigma^{\prime})
is
C^{*}
-simple and
(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the relative Kleppner condition. If
(H,\sigma^{\prime})
has a unique tracial state, these are moreover equivalent to the inclusion having the relative Dixmier property (cf.,
[
17
]
). In that case
\Gamma\mapsto C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma,\sigma|_{\Gamma})
is a bijection between the intermediate subgroups
H\leq\Gamma\leq G
and the intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebras of
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
.

Fact 5.5

(Hayes–Kunnawalkam Elayavalli–Patchell–Robert
[
12
, Theorem 3.8]
)
.

Let
B\subseteq(A,\rho)
be an inclusion of
C^{*}
-probability spaces. If
B\subseteq A
is a
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusion with the relative Dixmier property,
A
is exact, and
\rho
is invariant under conjugation by the unitaries of
B
, then
B\subseteq(A,\rho)
is a selfless inclusion.

Proposition 5.6

(Selflessness of inclusions)
.

Let
G
be a countable amenable group with a two-cocycle
\sigma
and let
H\trianglelefteq G
be infinite. Suppose that
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
has a unique tracial state and is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable, and that
(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the relative Kleppner condition. Then
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is a selfless inclusion. In particular, every intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebra is selfless. Moreover,
\Gamma\mapsto C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma,\sigma|_{\Gamma})
is a bijection between the intermediate subgroups
H\leq\Gamma\leq G
and the intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebras of
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
.

Proof.

Put
A=C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
and
B=C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
. We verify the hypotheses of Fact
5.5
. By Proposition
4.4
,
B\subseteq A
is a
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusion. Since
G
is amenable,
A
is nuclear, hence exact, and the canonical trace
\tau
is
\mathcal{U}(B)
-invariant, being a trace. Moreover
B
has a unique tracial state and is therefore
C^{*}
-simple (Fact
2.4
), and
(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the relative Kleppner condition, so by Fact
5.4
the inclusion
B\subseteq A
has the relative Dixmier property. All hypotheses of Fact
5.5
hold, so
B\subseteq A
is a selfless inclusion, and every intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebra is selfless by Fact
5.2
. The asserted bijection is also part of Fact
5.4
.
∎

The relative Kleppner condition plays for the inclusion the role Kleppner’s condition plays for the algebra. It provides the
C^{*}
-irreducibility, the dynamical part. In the von Neumann picture the condition provides strong outerness of the quotient action of
K=G/H
on
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
, that is, the automorphism induced on
\pi_{\tau}(C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime}))^{\prime\prime}
by each nontrivial element of
K
is outer
[
5
]
. When
K
is FC-hypercentral, the simplicity of the crossed product
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)\cong C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\rtimes K
is also treated by dynamical means in
[
11
]
. The next results are the inclusion analogues of Proposition
3.6
, Corollary
3.7
, and Theorem
3.8
.

Theorem 5.7

(Amenable groups)
.

Let
G
be a countable amenable group with a two-cocycle
\sigma
and let
H\trianglelefteq G
be infinite. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is a selfless inclusion;

(ii)

C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
has a unique tracial state and is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable, and
(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the relative Kleppner condition;

(iii)

C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
has a unique tracial state and has finite nuclear dimension, and
(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the relative Kleppner condition.

When these hold, every intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebra is of the form
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma,\sigma|_{\Gamma})
for a unique intermediate subgroup
H\leq\Gamma\leq G
, and is selfless.

Proof.

Put
A=C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
and
B=C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
, and as
H
is countable amenable,
B
is separable, unital and nuclear.

(ii)
\Longleftrightarrow
(iii): Since
B
has a unique tracial state, Fact
2.4
gives simplicity, and Fact
3.5
applies, so
\mathcal{Z}
-stability and finite nuclear dimension are equivalent. The relative Kleppner condition is common to both.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i), together with the final statements, is Proposition
5.6
.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii): If
B\subseteq A
is a selfless inclusion, then
B
is selfless and the inclusion is
C^{*}
-irreducible (Fact
5.2
). A selfless
C^{*}
-algebra is simple, has a unique tracial state, and has strict comparison (Fact
3.2
). As
B
is separable, unital, nuclear and infinite-dimensional, it is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable by Fact
3.4
. In particular
B
is
C^{*}
-simple, so by Fact
5.4
the
C^{*}
-irreducibility of
B\subseteq A
is equivalent to the relative Kleppner condition for
(H\leq G,\sigma)
. This gives (ii).
∎

Corollary 5.8

(FC-hypercentral groups)
.

Let
G
be a countable FC-hypercentral group with a two-cocycle
\sigma
and let
H\trianglelefteq G
be infinite. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is a selfless inclusion;

(ii)

C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable and
(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the relative Kleppner condition;

(iii)

C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
has finite nuclear dimension and
(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the relative Kleppner condition.

When these hold, every intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebra is of the form
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma,\sigma|_{\Gamma})
for a unique intermediate subgroup
H\leq\Gamma\leq G
, and is selfless.

Proof.

The subgroup
H
is FC-hypercentral (Fact
2.2
), so by Fact
2.5
the algebra
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
has a unique tracial state if and only if
(H,\sigma^{\prime})
satisfies Kleppner’s condition. As the relative Kleppner condition includes Kleppner’s condition for
(H,\sigma^{\prime})
(Definition
5.3
), under it
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
automatically has a unique tracial state. Hence conditions (ii) and (iii) here are equivalent to (ii) and (iii) of Theorem
5.7
, and the equivalence with (i), together with the final statement, follows from that theorem.
∎

Theorem 5.9

(Finitely generated virtually nilpotent groups)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually nilpotent group with a two-cocycle
\sigma
and let
H\trianglelefteq G
be infinite. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is a selfless inclusion;

(ii)

(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the relative Kleppner condition.

When these hold, every intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebra is of the form
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma,\sigma|_{\Gamma})
for a unique intermediate subgroup
H\leq\Gamma\leq G
, and is selfless.

Proof.

The subgroup
H
is finitely generated virtually nilpotent, hence FC-hypercentral and virtually polycyclic, so
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
has finite nuclear dimension by Fact
2.3
. Under the relative Kleppner condition,
(H,\sigma^{\prime})
satisfies Kleppner’s condition (Definition
5.3
), so
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
is simple with a unique tracial state by Fact
2.5
. Being also infinite-dimensional, it is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable by Fact
3.5
. Thus the
\mathcal{Z}
-stability in Corollary
5.8
(ii) is automatic, and that condition reduces to (ii). The equivalence with (i) and the final statement then follow from Corollary
5.8
, the intermediate groups
\Gamma
being finitely generated virtually nilpotent by Fact
2.2
.
∎

The inclusion results applies to amenable groups also outside the finitely generated virtually nilpotent class, as the lamplighter group illustrates.

Example 5.10

(The lamplighter group)
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathbb{Z}=H\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
be the lamplighter group, with
H=\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and let
\sigma
be a two-cocycle on
G
of the form considered in
[
4
, Section 5.2.2]
. For a suitable choice giving a simple noncommutative lamplighter, the base algebra
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})
is the UHF algebra of type
2^{\infty}
, and
(H\leq G,\sigma)
satisfies the relative Kleppner condition. Since the UHF algebra has a unique tracial state and is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable, Theorem
5.7
applies. The inclusion
C^{*}_{r}(H,\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is selfless, and hence
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is selfless by Fact
5.2
. In particular
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is simple, has a unique tracial state, and is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable, so it has finite nuclear dimension by Fact
3.5
. Here
G
is amenable and finitely generated but not virtually nilpotent, so
C^{*}_{r}(G,\sigma)
is covered neither by Theorem
3.8
nor by Fact
2.3
. By contrast, for
\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}
the base algebra is an infinite-dimensional noncommutative torus as in Example
3.9
, where
\mathcal{Z}
-stability is open.
