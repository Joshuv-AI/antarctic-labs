Construction of a comprehensive competency evaluation index system for clinical nursing instructors based on a multifaceted feedback model
Authors: Kele Wang, Jin Pang, Xin Jia, Yun Zheng, Heng Yang, Na Chen, Li Yang, Lidan Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study constructed a comprehensive competency evaluation Index system for clinical nursing instructors based on a Multifaceted Feedback Model, which can provide a scientific reference for the recruitment, appointment and competency assessment of clinical nursing teachers.MethodsBased on the multifaceted feedback model, the initial draft of the evaluation index system was formulated through literature review, mixed-method research, and group discussions. The expert consultation method was then used to refine and finalize the evaluation system. The weight of each indicator was determined using the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), and a consistency test was conducted to ensure reliability.ResultsTwo rounds of expert consultations were conducted. The expert response rates were 100% and 93%, the authority coefficients were 0.922 and 0.929, and the Kendall's coefficients of concordance were 0.175 and 0.235, respectively. The final evaluation index system for the competencies of clinical nursing instructors consists of four first-level indicators, twelve second-level indicators, and fifty-six third-level indicators.Conclusion The comprehensive competency evaluation indicator system for clinical nursing instructors constructed in this study establishes a foundation for the recruitment, training, performance appraisal, competency evaluation and standardized management of clinical nursing teachers. Moreover, this system provides theoretical and practical references for cultivating high-quality nursing talents that meet the development requirements of new medical disciplines and the reform objectives of the medical and health system.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12912-026-05084-y.Keywords: Clinical nursing instructors, Multifaceted feedback model, Competency evaluation, Evaluation index system

Introduction

In 2024, the Ministry of Education designated nursing and midwifery as nationally controlled specialties (hereinafter referred to as “nationally controlled specialties”) to improve the quality of nursing education from the point of entry and strengthen standards for clinical education and training [1, 2]. As the critical link between academic education and clinical practice, the competence and quality of clinical nursing instructors directly influence the effectiveness of clinical competency training for nursing students [3]. Under the closed-loop management requirements for nationally controlled specialties—characterized by “strict admission, process monitoring, and outcome evaluation“ [4], it is essential to evaluate the competencies of clinical nursing instructors in a scientific and objective manner. Such evaluation is necessary for instructor selection and development, ensuring the quality of clinical nursing education, and meeting the demands of modern healthcare development and healthcare system reform. Recent studies have explored the core competencies of clinical nursing instructors [5, 6]. However, existing evaluation index systems are generally developed from a single perspective, such as that of nursing students, nursing managers, or the instructors themselves [7–9]. Consequently, these systems often have limited evaluation dimensions, are susceptible to subjective bias, and fail to fully reflect the realities of clinical teaching. Furthermore, with the continuous evolution of clinical teaching philosophies and methods, as well as advances in information technology and intelligent learning environments [10], traditional evaluation models are increasingly unable to meet the diverse needs of contemporary nursing education. The multifaceted feedback model is an evaluation and feedback approach grounded in modern educational theory, information theory, cybernetics, and systems theory [11]. Its core principle is to improve the objectivity of evaluation outcomes by incorporating feedback from multiple evaluators. Although this model has been applied in educational settings, its application to the evaluation of clinical nursing instructors’ competencies remains limited. Therefore, this study adopts the multifaceted feedback model by integrating the perspectives of nursing managers, clinical nursing instructors, and nursing students to develop a scientific and practical evaluation index system for the comprehensive competencies of clinical nursing instructors. This system is intended to provide a reference for improving the selection, evaluation, and training of clinical nursing instructors.

Methods

Theoretical framework

This study adopts the multifaceted feedback model as its core theoretical framework. It follows the principles of comprehensiveness, timeliness, differentiation, and specificity [8], and clearly defines the roles of the three stakeholder groups involved in the feedback process: clinical nursing instructors, nursing students, and nursing managers. Nursing students, as the direct recipients of clinical teaching, provide feedback on teaching effectiveness and learning outcomes. Clinical nursing instructors, as the primary participants in self-evaluation, provide feedback on the competencies required for their roles and their professional development needs. Nursing managers, as the primary supervisors and administrators, provide feedback on the standards and management expectations for the development of clinical nursing instructors. Feedback from these three stakeholder groups is incorporated throughout the development of the evaluation index system, forming a complete closed-loop process consisting of needs assessment, index development, expert validation, and weight assignment. This approach ensures that the evaluation system comprehensively addresses the core needs of all three stakeholder groups.

Literature research

A systematic literature search was conducted in the CNKI, Wanfang, VIP, SinoMed, PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, MEDLINE, ProQuest, and CINAHL databases using a combination of subject headings and free-text terms. The Chinese search terms included “clinical nursing teacher,” “nursing teacher,” “ability,” “quality,” “core competence,” “competence,” “post ability,” “evaluation,” and “assessment.” The English search terms included “faculty nursing,” “nurse teacher,” “nursing teacher,” “nurse educator,” “nurse preceptor,” “preceptor,” “nurse instructor,” “instructor,” “clinical instructor,” “part-time faculty,” “adjunct faculty,” and “assess*,” “estimate*,” and “evaluate*.” The search covered the period from the inception of each database to January 2024. Two researchers independently screened the retrieved studies, excluding those that were unrelated to the research topic or of low methodological quality. The eligible literature was then organized and analyzed to extract and preliminarily integrate competency evaluation indicators for clinical nursing instructors, while categorizing the corresponding competency domains.

Questionnaire method

This study adopted the self-developed"Questionnaire on the Importance of Core Competencies for Clinical Nursing Instructors”.Based on a systematic literature review and the findings of our earlier preliminary research, the questionnaire was developed, and exhibited favorable internal consistency with a Cronbach’sα coefficient of 0.942. The full version of the questionnaire is provided in Supplementary File 1. This study distributed anonymous online questionnaires to clinical nursing instructors and nursing students via the Wenjuanxing platform. A total of 172 clinical nursing instructors and 1542 nursing students were initially included as research participants. After the questionnaires were recovered, two researchers completed independent double entry of the data and cross-checking. Finally,150 valid questionnaires from clinical nursing instructors and 610 valid questionnaires from nursing students were obtained. This study sorted, analyzed and summarized the collected survey data to screen out competency indicators with high demand level.

Semi-structured interviews

This study adopted purposive sampling following the principle of maximum variation. Interviewees were recruited from clinical nursing educators and nursing administrators at a tertiary Class A hospital in Guizhou Province, with demographic characteristics including educational background, professional title, and years of working experience sufficiently taken into account. The inclusion criteria were set as follows: ① Hold a college diploma or above; ② Hold a professional title of nurse practitioner or higher; ③ Have no less than 3 years of clinical nursing experience, and at least 1 year of clinical nursing teaching or management experience; ④ Voluntarily participate in this study; ⑤ Undertake teaching responsibilities for full-time junior college and undergraduate nursing students. The exclusion criteria were set as follows: ① Staff on leave or resigned employees; ② Participants who did not complete the entire interview process. Sample size was determined on the basis of data saturation, and 15 interviewees were ultimately included in this study. The interview outline was revised and optimized via literature review, research group discussion and two preliminary pilot interviews. The semi-structured interview guide was designed around three core dimensions—entry qualification requirements, connotation of core competencies, and current situation and deficiencies of the teaching team—and the full guide is provided in Supplementary File 2. All face-to-face interviews were conducted by researchers who had received standardized qualitative research training, accompanied by one postgraduate student, and carried out in an independent demonstration classroom of the hospital. Each interview lasted 20–30 min. Before each interview, the research purpose and relevant ethical principles were fully clarified to participants. Written informed consent was obtained prior to audio recording of the entire interview process, and researchers recorded non-verbal information from interviewees in real time during the interview. All audio recordings were transcribed verbatim within 24 h after the interview to guarantee data integrity. The Colaizzi seven-step phenomenological analysis method was employed to process the transcribed text [12].Two researchers performed independent coding on the transcripts separately; inconsistent coding results were resolved through collective discussion within the research group, and three core themes were finally summarized and extracted. Finally, the results of qualitative interviews and questionnaire surveys were integrated to preliminarily construct the draft of the comprehensive competency evaluation indicator system for clinical nursing teachers, which includes 5 first level,15. second-level, and 69 third-level indicators.

Ethical considerations

This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Guizhou Provincial People’s Hospital (Approval number: Ethics Review (Scientific Research) 2024-083). All procedures performed in studies involving human participants were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional research committee and with the 1964 Helsinki declaration and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants included in the study. The anonymous survey guaranteed participant confidentiality, all individuals reserved the right to withdraw at any time, and raw data was encrypted for strict data protection.

Delphi method

Identification of advisory experts

In accordance with the Delphi methodology and the objectives of this study, 18 experts in nursing education, nursing management, and clinical nursing instruction were recruited from nursing colleges and tertiary hospitals. The expert selection criteria were as follows: (1) Experts in clinical nursing management and nursing education: ① Deputy senior or higher professional title with ≥ 10 years of experience in clinical nursing management or nursing education, or a master’s degree or above with ≥ 5 years of experience in clinical nursing management or nursing education; ② Willingness to participate voluntarily in the study. (2) Clinical nursing instructors: ① Intermediate or higher professional title; ② Bachelor’s degree or above; ③ ≥5 years of experience in clinical nursing teaching; ④ Willingness to participate voluntarily in the study.

Drafting of expert consultation questionnaire

Based on the findings of the literature review, questionnaire survey, and semi-structured interviews, the preliminary evaluation index system was developed through discussions among the research team. The first-round Delphi expert consultation questionnaire was subsequently developed. The questionnaire consisted of three sections:

(1) Questionnaire description: An overview of the study objectives, significance, and evaluation methods, together with the rationale for the inclusion of each indicator.

(2) Importance scoring: Experts were asked to rate the importance of each item using a five-point Likert scale. Sections for “additional items” and “modification suggestions” were provided.

(3) Expert information: Collection of basic expert information, including their familiarity with the content and the basis for their judgments.

Implementation

Two rounds of Delphi expert consultation were conducted by distributing and collecting questionnaires via WeChat or email. After the first round, the research team reviewed the experts’ feedback, the indicator screening results, and the outcomes of group discussions. Based on these findings, indicators were added, deleted, or revised to develop the second-round questionnaire, which was distributed two weeks later. The results of the first round were provided to the experts for reference. The consultation process continued until a stable consensus was reached among the experts. Following the second round, the research team further organized, analyzed, and refined the evaluation indicators. The indicator screening criteria were as follows: indicators were retained if they simultaneously met the following conditions: importance score ≥ 3.5 points, full score rate ≥ 20% ,coefficient of variation ≤ 0.25 [13]. Indicators that failed to meet all of these criteria were excluded. If an indicator met only one of the criteria, the research team discussed and determined whether it should be retained.

Analytic Hierarchy Process

The Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) was used to calculate the weights of the evaluation indicators based on importance scores collected from the second round of Delphi expert consultation [14]. The specific implementation steps were as follows: First, a three-level hierarchical model was established containing a target layer, criterion layer and index layer. The target layer referred to the comprehensive competency evaluation index system for clinical nursing instructors; the criterion layer contained 4 first-level indicators and 12 s-level indicators; the index layer consisted of 56 third-level indicators. Second, judgment matrices were built to quantify the relative importance of indicators at each layer against their corresponding upper-layer factors, with pairwise comparisons scored using the Saaty1–9 scale. Yaahp12.0 software was adopted to calculate the maximum eigenvalue, eigenvector and indicator weight of each matrix, followed by consistency testing. A matrix was regarded as consistent if the consistency ratio (CR) < 0.1, which meant the weight assignment logic was acceptable. Finally, weighted averaging was performed on weight data from all experts to generate the final integrated weight of each indicator. The full weighting analysis workflow is provided in Supplementary File 4.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages (%). Expert engagement was assessed using the questionnaire response rate (%). Expert authority was evaluated using the authority coefficient (Cr), which was calculated as the arithmetic mean of the judgment basis coefficient (Ca) and the familiarity coefficient (Cs): Cr = (Ca + Cs)/2. A Cr value ≥ 0.7 was considered acceptable [15]. The degree of consensus among experts was assessed using the coefficient of variation (CV) and Kendall’s coefficient of concordance (Kendall’s W). Kendall’s W ranges from 0 to 1, with higher values indicating stronger agreement among experts. Similarly, a lower coefficient of variation indicates greater consistency in expert opinions. Finally, the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) was used to calculate the weight of each indicator.

Expert engagement and authority

In the first round of the Delphi consultation, 18 questionnaires were distributed and all were returned, yielding a response rate of 100%. In the second round, 16 questionnaires were distributed and 15 were returned, resulting in a response rate of 93%. During the first round, 10 experts provided revision suggestions, whereas in the second round, 3 experts (20%) proposed additional modifications. These findings indicate a high level of expert engagement and commitment throughout the consultation process. The expert authority coefficients were 0.922 and 0.929 in the first and second rounds, respectively, demonstrating a high level of expert authority.

Coordination and consensus of expert opinions

The results of both rounds of the Delphi consultation showed that the coefficient of variation for all evaluation indicators was below 0.25. The overall Kendall’s coefficients of concordance (Kendall’s W) were 0.175 and 0.235 for the first and second rounds, respectively. For the first-, second-, and third-level indicators, Kendall’s W ranged from 0.166 to 0.289 in the first round and from 0.231 to 0.409 in the second round. All differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05), indicating a satisfactory level of agreement and consensus among the experts.Detailed information is presented in Table 2.

Discussion

Scientific basis of the evaluation index system and innovative value of the multifaceted feedback model

The evaluation index system developed in this study is founded on the core principles of the multifaceted feedback model. From needs assessment to indicator development and weight assignment, it integrates the perspectives of nursing students, clinical nursing instructors, and nursing managers. Two rounds of Delphi expert consultation were implemented, and all participating experts had professional backgrounds in nursing education, nursing management, and clinical nursing. The expert authority coefficients (Cr) of the two consultation rounds were 0.922 and 0.929 respectively, while The overall Kendall’s coefficients of concordance (Kendall’s W) were 0.175 and 0.235.These results indicate that the experts’ ratings were relatively consistent, which supports the reliability of the consultation results and the scientific basis of the indicator screening. On this basis, the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) was applied to calculate the weight of each indicator, and the obtained weight distribution was confirmed to be reasonable.

Different from most existing evaluation systems that are constructed only based on single-stakeholder feedback (i.e., feedback only from nursing students, nursing managers, or nursing instructors), the multifaceted feedback model proposed in this study not only realizes multi-stakeholder participation in the evaluation process, but also fully incorporates the actual demands of three types of stakeholders at the indicator construction stage. In the preliminary stage of this study, questionnaire surveys were distributed to150 clinical nursing instructors and 610 nursing students, to conduct quantitative analysis on the perceived importance of different competencies from the perspective of each stakeholder group. Meanwhile, semi-structured interviews were conducted with 15 nursing managers and clinical nursing instructors, to explore the practical problems and competency gaps in current clinical teaching practice. The weight distribution of the constructed index system is the direct output of this multifaceted feedback. For example, among professional competencies,“communication and coordination ability"obtained the highest weight of 0.493, which is inconsistent with the conclusions of existing studies that generally prioritize professional knowledge and technical skills [8, 9]. This difference is attributed to the incorporation of core demands of nursing students in this study: as the recipients of clinical teaching, nursing students attach greater importance to the communication ability and empathy of nursing instructors, rather than merely focusing on the instructors’ professional expertise. This result confirms that the evaluation index system developed based on the multifaceted feedback model can truly reflect the actual demands of clinical teaching, and effectively avoid subjective bias and disconnection from practical needs.

Teaching ability as the core competence of clinical nursing instructors

Among the first-level indicators, “Teaching Ability” received the highest weight, which is consistent with the findings of Shao et al. [16]. This result indicates that nursing managers, clinical nursing instructors, and nursing students all regard teaching ability as the core competency of clinical nursing instructors. Improving the quality of talent cultivation is a primary objective in the development of nationally controlled specialties, and the teaching ability of clinical nursing instructors not only determines the quality of clinical education but also serves as the critical link between theoretical instruction and clinical practice in colleges and universities. Among the second-level indicators, “Teaching Methods” received the highest weight (0.392), reflecting the increasing demand for innovative teaching approaches in contemporary nursing education. With the rapid advancement of information technology and intelligent learning environments, traditional teaching methods alone are no longer sufficient to meet the learning needs of nursing students [17–21]. At the third level, indicators such as “Focus on Motivation and Inspiration,” “Rational Use of Teaching Media,” and “Integration of Theory with Clinical Practice” received the highest weights. These findings indicate that clinical nursing instructors, nursing students, and nursing managers all recognize the importance of instructors’ ability to flexibly adopt diverse teaching strategies and effectively integrate theoretical knowledge with clinical practice. Therefore, nursing managers should place greater emphasis on providing specialized training in technology-enhanced teaching and the design of innovative instructional methods, rather than focusing exclusively on instructors’ clinical expertise.

Professional ability and assessment ability as the foundational support of clinical nursing instructors

In this study, the combined weight of “Professional Ability” and “Assessment Ability” was 0.21, ranking second among the first-level indicators. This finding highlights that both competencies are fundamental components of the comprehensive evaluation system for clinical nursing instructors. Unlike previous studies that incorporated assessment ability into the broader domain of teaching ability [22, 23], this study identified “Assessment Ability” as an independent first-level indicator. This distinction reflects the consensus among clinical nursing instructors, nursing students, and nursing managers that clinical nursing education is a continuous process of “teaching → assessment → feedback → improvement.” Strong assessment competencies are essential not only for ensuring teaching quality but also for fulfilling the requirements of “process monitoring and outcome evaluation” for nationally controlled specialties. Among the second-level indicators under “Assessment Ability,” “Self-Teaching Assessment” received the highest weight (0.493), highlighting the importance of reflective teaching practice and continuous quality improvement. Ongoing self-assessment enables clinical nursing instructors to identify deficiencies in their teaching and make timely adjustments to their instructional strategies. The second highest-weighted indicator, “Student Learning Assessment” (0.310), further emphasizes that instructors should be able to comprehensively evaluate students’ learning needs, motivation, and learning outcomes in order to provide targeted and effective instruction.

“Professional Ability” also received a relatively high weight, which is consistent with the findings of Lu et al. [24]. Within this dimension, “Communication and Coordination Ability” received the highest weight (0.493), differing from previous studies that have primarily emphasized professional knowledge and technical skills. This finding further demonstrates the value of incorporating multifaceted feedback throughout the development of the evaluation index system. Clinical nursing instructors serve dual roles as educators and healthcare professionals. Effective communication is essential not only for clinical practice but also for achieving educational objectives. Through clear, effective, and empathetic communication, instructors can monitor students’ learning progress and emotional responses in a timely manner. In addition, by demonstrating effective communication with patients, instructors help foster nursing students’ capacity for humanistic care [25]. Therefore, nursing managers should not focus exclusively on instructors’ professional knowledge and technical skills. Equal emphasis should be placed on developing communication, coordination, and empathy, as these competencies are essential for both high-quality clinical practice and effective clinical teaching.

Positioning research ability to meet the actual needs of clinical nursing instructors

Although “Scientific Research Ability” received a relatively low weight in the evaluation index system, this does not imply that it is unimportant. Rather, it reflects an appropriate positioning based on the actual responsibilities of clinical nursing instructors. Clinical nursing instructors primarily engage in clinical practice and teaching, and many currently have limited knowledge and experience in scientific research [26], making it difficult for them to undertake intensive research activities. Consequently, placing excessive emphasis on scientific research ability could result in an evaluation system that is disconnected from the realities of clinical teaching. Among the second-level indicators within this dimension, “Innovation and Development Ability” received a higher weight than “Application of Scientific Research Methods.” This finding suggests that clinical nursing instructors, nursing students, and nursing managers believe that instructors should embrace lifelong learning, continuously update their professional knowledge, and translate clinical problems into research questions. The National Nursing Development Plan (2021–2025) emphasizes promoting the cultivation of nursing professionals and advancing the high-quality development of nursing through disciplinary development [27]. Scientific research serves not only as a means of professional development but also as an important criterion for evaluating the academic competitiveness of hospitals and a key driving force for disciplinary advancement [28]. Strong professional knowledge and clinical skills provide the foundation for clinical nursing instructors, whereas research awareness and the ability to address practical clinical problems through research support the long-term advancement of the nursing discipline [29]. Therefore, nursing managers should provide basic training in scientific research, including topic selection, literature retrieval, and evidence-based nursing. In doing so, they can guide clinical nursing instructors to integrate research into teaching and cultivate students’ clinical thinking and innovative consciousness.

Conclusion

Based on the multifaceted feedback model, this study employed literature review, questionnaire surveys, semi-structured interviews, expert consultation, and the Analytic Hierarchy Process to develop a comprehensive competency evaluation index system for clinical nursing instructors. The final system includes 4 first-level indicators,12 s-level indicators, and 56 third-level indicators, The construction of this evaluation index system followed a scientifically rigorous, reliable, and rational process. This indicator system provides a solid scientific foundation for the selection, capacity training, performance appraisal and overall management of clinical nursing teachers. Furthermore, it offers valuable reference for the cultivation of high-quality nursing talents that can adapt to the development requirements of the new medical education reform and the continuous deepening of healthcare system reform. Nevertheless, this study still has certain limitations his study has some limitations. Although the comprehensive competency evaluation index system has been constructed, empirical testing has not yet been conducted due to time and resource constraints. Future research should apply this system in practice to validate and refine the indicators further.
