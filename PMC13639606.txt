Global, regional, and national burden of tetanus in 204 countries and territories, 1990–2023: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023

GBD 2023 Tetanus Collaborators

Abstract

SummaryBackgroundTetanus is a life-threatening, vaccine-preventable disease. Its incidence decreased substantially following global initiatives such as the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) and Maternal and Neonatal Tetanus Elimination (MNTE). Despite these efforts, tetanus elimination has not been achieved, and significant disparities in morbidity and mortality persist across various regions. Therefore, we sought to investigate the global, regional, and national burden of tetanus from 1990 to 2023, using data from the Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) 2023.MethodsWe estimated the global burden of tetanus across 204 countries and territories, seven GBD super-regions, and 21 GBD regions from 1990 to 2023. For investigation, tetanus was defined based on International Classification of Diseases (ICD)-10 codes A33–A35.0 and Z23.5 and ICD-9 codes 037–037.9, 771.3, and V03.7. The tetanus case fatality rate (CFR) was defined as the ratio of fatal tetanus cases to the total number of tetanus cases within a given sample. Incidence and prevalence of tetanus were estimated by linking cause-specific mortality estimates with modeled CFRs and disease-duration assumptions. Disease burden was quantified using age-standardized prevalence, incidence, disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), and mortality rates per 100,000 population, along with corresponding 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) and total percent change over the study period. Estimates were stratified by age group, sex, and Socio-demographic Index (SDI) across countries and regions.FindingsGlobal age-standardized incidence rate of tetanus significantly decreased from 11.32 (95% UI, 7.98–16.36) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 0.70 (0.40–1.22) in 2023, representing a 93.8% decrease. The global age-standardized DALY rate also declined by 95.37% between 1990 and 2023. In 2023, the highest age-standardized DALY burden was estimated in sub-Saharan Africa (46.07 [95% UI, 25.71–77.17] per 100,000 population) and South Asia (20.47 [11.99–32.50]), whereas Southeast Asia had the highest age-standardized prevalence and incidence rates. In contrast, high-income regions and Central Europe, Eastern Europe, and Central Asia had the lowest burden of tetanus in 2023, with near-zero incidence and mortality. Tetanus burden showed a strong inverse association with SDI across all epidemiological measures. At the national level, Somalia, the Lao People's Democratic Republic, and Chad had the highest age-standardized DALY rates. Pronounced age- and sex-specific patterns were observed, with the highest rates concentrated among neonates younger than 28 days and generally higher rates in males than in females across most age groups. Although absolute rates declined over time, these demographic inequities persisted.InterpretationOver the past three decades, global initiatives such as the EPI and MNTE have led to a substantial reduction in the burden of tetanus, bringing it to very low levels in high-income and high SDI countries and territories. Nevertheless, tetanus continues to impose a considerable burden in several regions, particularly in South Asia and sub-Saharan Africa. Because tetanus is a vaccine-preventable infection, the remaining burden and persistent inequities are largely avoidable and should be prioritized in immunization policy.FundingGates Foundation.

Research in contextEvidence before this studyOn Jan 20, 2026, we searched PubMed for global trends in the burden of tetanus by searching the following terms ([“tetanus”] AND [“burden” OR “disability-adjusted life years (DALYs)”]) and variants. We then systematically collected and reviewed papers related to global, regional, and national trends in prevalence, incidence, mortality, and DALYs due to tetanus. Previous Global Burden of Disease (GBD) studies primarily addressed tetanus in the context of vaccine-preventable diseases or within broader infectious disease frameworks. Other studies have reported tetanus burden as part of broader pathogen-based analyses or described the global epidemiology of tetanus in the pre-COVID-19 period. Prior GBD-based research, including GBD 2019, showed that tetanus has often been assessed as one of multiple pathogens within aggregated infectious disease frameworks, underscoring the need for more focused evaluation. Previous studies have not fully characterized tetanus-specific patterns in prevalence, incidence, mortality, DALYs, and sociodemographic inequalities using GBD 2023 estimates.Added value of this studyUsing data from GBD 2023, this study provides an updated, tetanus-specific assessment of tetanus burden from 1990 to 2023 at global, regional, subregional, and national levels. We extend previous work by quantifying trends in prevalence, incidence, mortality, and DALYs across all age groups, characterizing age- and sex-specific patterns, and examining associations with socio-demographic development. Our analysis shows that the global burden of tetanus declined substantially over the study period but remained closely associated with socio-demographic disparities. Countries and territories with high Socio-demographic Index (SDI) achieved near-zero tetanus incidence and burden by 2023, whereas countries and territories with low SDI continued to experience a substantial burden of this preventable condition.Implications of all the available evidenceThe available evidence indicates that while global tetanus burden has declined markedly, elimination has not yet been achieved and substantial inequities persist. Our findings underscore the need for sustained and equitable implementation of immunization strategies, including routine childhood vaccination, maternal immunization, and booster coverage across the life course. The persistence of tetanus burden beyond childhood, including among older adults, suggests that waning immunity and inadequate booster delivery remain neglected gaps in current programs. Sustained progress toward tetanus elimination requires targeted policy efforts in low-resource and high-burden settings to strengthen health system capacity, reduce barriers to care, and ensure equitable access to vaccination.

Introduction

The Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI), launched by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 1974, established routine childhood vaccination as a cornerstone of global infectious disease control, with the diphtheria–tetanus–pertussis (DTP) vaccine as a central component., Using a multidose schedule delivered in early infancy and reinforced by maternal tetanus toxoid vaccination, EPI has substantially reduced tetanus-related morbidity and mortality worldwide. These efforts were later strengthened by the Maternal and Neonatal Tetanus Elimination (MNTE) initiative, which integrated maternal immunization with improved antenatal care and hygienic safe delivery practices., As a result, most high-income countries (HICs) and territories have achieved near-zero incidence of tetanus, demonstrating that sustained vaccination coverage and robust health systems can effectively eliminate this preventable disease. Tetanus remains a sensitive tracer of health system fragility, reflecting gaps in routine immunization, maternal and delivery care, wound management, and booster coverage, and continues to be highlighted within the Immunization Agenda 2030 (IA2030) as part of global concern regarding zero-dose and under-immunized populations.

Despite these achievements, tetanus remains a public health concern in many low- and middle-income settings. Previous evidence indicates that the global burden of tetanus is highly uneven, with persistently high incidence and mortality in regions with low-resource settings., These disparities reflect structural vulnerabilities, including incomplete routine immunization, limited access to skilled birth attendance, fragile health systems, and other barriers to care. The persistence of tetanus in settings with a low Socio-demographic Index (SDI) also aligns with global concerns highlighted in the IA2030, particularly regarding zero-dose and under-immunized children who remain outside routine vaccination systems., Moreover, recent stagnation or decline in coverage of essential childhood vaccines, including DTP, in several countries has raised concerns about renewed susceptibility to these vaccine-preventable diseases., Sustaining and expanding tetanus protection beyond primary immunization schedules requires the introduction of tetanus toxoid-containing vaccine booster doses, which provide protection against waning immunity, enable catch-up vaccination among under-immunized populations, and offer a more sustainable framework for protecting mothers and neonates in low-income settings., , The WHO recommends booster doses throughout the life course, and Gavi (Global Alliance for Vaccines and Immunization) began offering programmatic support for their introduction in 2023.,

Previous Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD)-based studies have assessed tetanus within the context of childhood vaccine-preventable diseases or broader infectious disease and pathogen burden frameworks., GBD 2019 estimates have been used to quantify the global burden of tetanus from 1990 to 2019, whereas GBD 2021 estimates have more recently been used to assess temporal trends and project its future burden., However, an updated tetanus-specific assessment using GBD 2023 data remains needed to comprehensively evaluate prevalence, incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) across geographic, age, and sex strata. This study aimed to (i) evaluate global trends in tetanus burden from 1990 to 2023, (ii) describe age- and sex-specific patterns, and (iii) examine the associations between tetanus burden and the SDI, using data from GBD 2023. Our findings provide updated and comprehensive evidence on the global burden of tetanus to inform equitable and evidence-based strategies towards elimination, and to support sustained progress in strengthening routine immunization coverage and primary health systems worldwide.

Methods

Overview

GBD 2023 quantified the burden of 375 diseases and injuries and 88 risk factors across 204 countries and territories., It provides annual estimates of incidence, prevalence, years lived with disability (YLDs), deaths, years of life lost (YLLs), and DALYs from 1990 to 2023, disaggregated by age, sex, and location., , Using data from GBD 2023, the current study assessed the global, regional, and national burden of tetanus from 1990 to 2023. The burden of tetanus was evaluated using prevalence, incidence, mortality, and DALYs, in accordance with GBD case definitions and modeling frameworks., This analysis followed the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting (GATHER). Data sources are accessible through the Global Health Data Exchange (https://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-2023), and the estimates used in this study are available through the GBD Results Tool (https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/). This study was conducted as part of the GBD Collaborator Network under the GBD protocol.

Input data and estimation of tetanus

Tetanus estimates in GBD 2023 were calculated using an integrated estimation framework that combined multiple data sources and modeling approaches to quantify fatal and non-fatal disease burden across locations, age groups, sexes, and years., For cause-of-death estimation, tetanus mortality data were mapped using International Classification of Diseases (ICD)-10 codes A33–A35.0 and ICD-9 codes 037–037.9 and 771.3 within the GBD cause-of-death framework.,, In the GBD 2023 non-fatal estimation framework, the tetanus case-definition documentation lists ICD-10 codes A33–A35.0 and Z23.5 and ICD-9 codes 037–037.9, 771.3, and V03.7.

Non-fatal burden was derived from cause-specific mortality estimates and modeled case fatality rates (CFRs), and acute prevalence was subsequently estimated from incidence using disease-duration assumptions., Tetanus CFRs were estimated using data obtained from systematic reviews of the tetanus CFR literature. CFRs were defined as the proportion of fatal tetanus cases among tetanus cases reported in the source studies., CFR data that were not sex-specific or were reported in age intervals wider than ten years were split into sex- and age-specific groups prior to modeling. Given the limited availability of location- and year-specific CFR data, global sex ratios and age patterns were estimated using all available sex- and age-specific data and used to disaggregate the aggregated observations, while propagating uncertainty throughout this process. Sex-specific adjustment factors were estimated using Meta-Regression Bayesian, Regularized, Trimmed (MR-BRT), a Bayesian meta-regression framework designed to reduce the influence of sparse or outlying data. Global age patterns were generated using Disease Modeling Meta-Regression 2.1 (DisMod-MR 2.1), a Bayesian meta-regression model that synthesizes epidemiological data across age, sex, location, and year. Finally, DisMod-MR 2.1 was fit to all available CFR data following age- and sex-disaggregation, with the Healthcare Access and Quality (HAQ) Index included as a location-level covariate and a directional prior specifying lower CFR at higher HAQ levels. This model produced location-, year-, age-, and sex-specific tetanus CFR estimates, which were subsequently used to derive incidence by dividing GBD 2023 mortality estimates by the predicted CFRs.

Prevalence of acute tetanus was estimated from incidence by applying an average disease duration of 20 days, as specified in the GBD 2023 non-fatal estimation framework and informed by prior clinical and review literature., Because incidence was estimated on an annual scale, duration was expressed as a fraction of a year to derive the expected point prevalence of the acute tetanus health state. For neonatal tetanus, long-term impairments among survivors were modeled separately and contributed to prevalence and YLD estimates across subsequent ages. Impairment distributions followed those used for neonatal encephalopathy, with additional modeling conducted to estimate the prevalence of mild and moderate-to-severe long-term impairments among survivors across ages, sexes, years, and locations.

The cause-specific mortality estimates used in this derivation were generated as follows. Mortality data were excluded before modeling when they were implausibly high or low, inconsistent with established age or temporal patterns, or markedly discrepant from other available data from the same location or from locations with similar sociodemographic characteristics. Age-, sex-, location-, and year-specific tetanus mortality estimates were produced using the Cause of Death Ensemble modeling (CODEm) framework. CODEm is an ensemble approach that combines individual statistical models weighted by their out-of-sample predictive validity. Separate sex-specific models were developed for neonatal tetanus (under 1 year of age) and for tetanus among children and adults aged 1 year and older. Models were additionally stratified by the quality of vital registration data, with both data-rich and global models fitted for each age–sex group and subsequently combined to generate a single set of mortality estimates across all sex-age-locations. Covariates included in the tetanus CODEm framework comprised indicators of immunization coverage and sociodemographic development, including the HAQ Index, lag-distributed income (LDI), SDI, and mean years of education per capita. In the neonatal (under 1 year of age) models, additional covariates related to maternal and perinatal care were included, such as tetanus toxoid coverage, in-facility deliveries, and skilled birth attendance. To account for disruptions in routine immunization during the COVID-19 pandemic, the COVID-inclusive five-year lagged coverage of the third dose of the diphtheria–tetanus–pertussis vaccine (DTP3) was included as a covariate. Further methodological details are described in the .

Uncertainty estimation

Uncertainty was quantified by randomly sampling 250 draws from the parameter distributions and incorporated throughout all stages of the analysis. Final estimates were presented using the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the posterior distribution, defining the 95% uncertainty interval (UI).,

Data presentation

Tetanus burden was calculated using age-standardized rates per 100,000 population for prevalence, incidence, mortality, and DALYs. All age-standardized rates were calculated using the GBD global standard population to ensure comparability across locations and over time., Estimates were presented at the global, regional, and national levels, stratified by age and sex where appropriate. Countries and territories were grouped according to the GBD location hierarchy into 21 regions and seven super-regions, which reflect both geographic proximity and broad sociodemographic characteristics. The full classification is provided in the . We also analyzed the relationship between tetanus burden and the SDI. SDI is a composite indicator defined as the geometric mean of three normalized components: total fertility rate among women younger than 25 years, mean educational attainment among individuals aged 15 years and older, and lag-distributed income per capita. SDI values range from 0 to 1, with higher values serving as a proxy for higher levels of sociodemographic development. The association between SDI and tetanus burden was assessed using linear regression models, with age-standardized tetanus rates as the outcome and SDI as the explanatory variable. Regression coefficients (β) and corresponding two-sided p-values were estimated to quantify the relationship between SDI and tetanus burden.

To further explore country-level deviations from the overall association between SDI and tetanus burden, we conducted an exploratory country-level benchmarking analysis across 204 countries and territories. For each burden metric, age-standardized rates for both sexes from 1990 to 2023 were plotted against annual country-level SDI values. Expected age-standardized burden in 2023 was estimated using a locally weighted scatterplot smoothing-fitted relationship between SDI and each burden metric across all countries. Among countries with SDI < 0.50 in 2023, the five countries with the lowest observed-to-expected ratios were highlighted for each measure., , All statistical analyses were conducted using Python (version 3.11.11).

The funders of the study had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, manuscript preparation, or the decision to submit the manuscript for publication.

Results

In 2023, tetanus was responsible for an estimated 53,478 incident cases (95% UI, 30,198–93,877), 1,243,074 DALYs (95% UI, 748,566–1,921,250), and 19,758 deaths (95% UI, 11,735–31,048) globally (). The global age-standardized incidence rate of tetanus decreased substantially from 11.32 (95% UI, 7.98–16.36) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 0.70 (95% UI, 0.40–1.22) in 2023, representing a 93.8% decrease (95% UI, 89.0–96.3; and ). In 2023, Southeast Asia (2.68 [95% UI, 1.16–5.49]), Eastern sub-Saharan Africa (2.23 [95% UI, 1.10–4.71]), and Central sub-Saharan Africa (1.64 [95% UI, 0.39–4.89]) per 100,000 population had the highest age-standardized incidence rates of tetanus. In contrast, the lowest age-standardized incidence rates were estimated in Central Europe, Eastern Europe, and Central Asia (0.0046 [95% UI, 0.0030–0.0117]) per 100,000 population, followed by the High-income region (0.0130 [95% UI, 0.0082–0.0222]) in 2023. At the national level, the highest age-standardized incidence rates in 2023 were observed in Lao People's Democratic Republic (18.72 [95% UI, 7.72–35.20]) per 100,000 population, followed by Somalia Discussion

This study provides an updated analysis of the burden of tetanus at global, regional, subregional, and national levels from 1990 to 2023. These findings are broadly consistent with those of a recent secondary analysis of GBD 2021, which similarly showed declining global burden of tetanus and concentrated burden among neonates and males. The present study, conducted within the official GBD 2023 Collaborator Network, extends this evidence through 2023 using updated data sources and methodologically refined estimates. There are several key findings. First, the global age-standardized prevalence, incidence, mortality, and DALY rates of tetanus declined substantially over the past three decades, indicating major progress in tetanus prevention worldwide. Despite this achievement, tetanus remains an important and preventable cause of morbidity and mortality, with a global age-standardized incidence rate of 0.70 per 100,000 population and DALY rate of 17.88 per 100,000 population in 2023. Second, marked geographic disparities persisted throughout the study period. South Asia and sub-Saharan Africa experienced the highest DALY burden, whereas high-income regions and Central Europe, Eastern Europe, and Central Asia remained among the lowest-burden settings. These findings highlight ongoing regional inequities despite overall global progress. Third, the relative ordering of regions changed little over time. Southeast Asia had the highest age-standardized prevalence and incidence rates during the study period, whereas Eastern sub-Saharan Africa had the highest age-standardized mortality rate throughout the study period. Australasia consistently had the lowest age-standardized DALY and mortality rates. Fourth, the burden of tetanus showed a strong and consistent inverse association with SDI: across all regions and epidemiological measures, higher SDI was associated with substantially lower tetanus prevalence, incidence, mortality, and DALY rates, illustrating persistent global inequalities in tetanus burden. Fifth, pronounced age- and sex-specific patterns persisted over time. Across the study period, the highest burden was observed among neonates younger than 28 days, with generally higher rates in males than in females across most age groups. Although absolute rates declined markedly between 1990 and 2023, the age gradient and sex differences remained evident, and mortality rates tended to increase gradually with advancing age in adulthood, particularly among males.

Our global trend analysis revealed a substantial decline in tetanus burden over the past three decades across all four epidemiological indicators: prevalence, incidence, DALYs, and mortality. This sustained reduction likely reflects long-term progress in global immunization programs, including EPI and IA2030, in alignment with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)., The observed 90% reduction in mortality rate and 95% reduction in DALY rate of tetanus between 1990 and 2023 indicate major progress toward SDG target 3.2, which aims to end preventable deaths among newborns and children younger than 5 years, as well as SDG target 3.8 on universal health coverage., These findings are consistent with previous GBD estimates of tetanus and underscore the resilience of global immunization programs despite disruptions related to the COVID-19 pandemic and regional conflict., Such disruptions to routine immunization and health services, together with rising vaccine hesitancy, might have slowed catch-up vaccination in some settings, although attribution to specific countries warrants caution. The marked decline in global incidence, which decreased by 93.8% from 1990 to 2023, is consistent with coordinated international efforts led by the WHO and the United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF) through the EPI and MNTE. These programs have emphasized expanded vaccination coverage, including diphtheria and tetanus vaccination, maternal immunization, strengthened antenatal care, and the promotion of hygienic delivery practices since the late 1980s.,

Nevertheless, these estimates suggest that substantial regional heterogeneity in both the pace and magnitude of progress in reducing the burden of tetanus persists. This heterogeneity may reflect ongoing inequities in immunization coverage, health system capacity, and access to skilled birth attendance., Notably, previous estimates indicate that coverage of DTP3 declined between 2010 and 2019 in 94 countries and territories. Globally, only 11 countries were estimated to have achieved the WHO Global Vaccine Action Plan target of at least 90% coverage for all assessed vaccines by 2019. Although the global number of zero-dose children, defined as those who had not received the first dose of the diphtheria–tetanus–pertussis vaccine (DTP1), declined markedly from 1980 to 2019, progress has stagnated in recent years, with a disproportionate concentration of zero-dose children in conflict-affected and resource-constrained settings, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa. As vaccination coverage has stagnated or declined, several countries, territories, and regions have experienced new or resurgent vaccine-preventable disease threats, with implications for tetanus susceptibility.,

These geographic disparities were closely aligned with sociodemographic development. Our estimates suggested that incidence, mortality, and DALY rates of tetanus were inversely associated with SDI. SDI is a composite indicator including per capita income, educational attainment, and total fertility rate, which broadly reflects levels of sociodemographic development. Regions with lower SDI often have reduced access to and utilization of antenatal care, immunization services, and safe delivery practices, which may increase susceptibility to tetanus., In addition, lower-SDI settings frequently have higher fertility rates and lower vaccination coverage, increasing the number of susceptible neonates and potentially contributing to higher tetanus burden. Nevertheless, these SDI-related patterns should be interpreted with caution, because vulnerability to tetanus can also be shaped by factors beyond development level, including natural disasters, fragile infrastructure, and gaps in vaccination coverage or post-exposure prophylaxis. Regional averages may also mask substantial country-level heterogeneity, as countries within the same GBD region can differ markedly in SDI, vaccination coverage, health-system capacity, and exposure to acute disruption.

Regionally, East Asia and South Asia experienced the largest absolute reductions in tetanus incidence and mortality, consistent with expanded childhood and maternal immunization coverage and improvements in antenatal and perinatal care. These reductions are consistent with progress toward the WHO's MNTE targets in many countries in these regions., This broader pattern should be interpreted at the super-region level, as Southeast Asia, East Asia, and Oceania include heterogeneous subregions. Slower and more variable progress in Southeast Asia, East Asia, and Oceania may reflect challenges related to geographic isolation, inconsistent vaccine delivery, and limited access to timely critical care following infection, especially in low-income and middle-income countries. In sub-Saharan Africa, although substantial reductions were observed, progress remains uneven across subregions. Persistently higher burden of tetanus in this region likely reflects lower immunization coverage, limited access to skilled birth attendance, and systemic disruptions caused by poverty, conflict, population displacement, and fragile health infrastructure., These factors can interrupt vaccine supply chains, undermine disease surveillance, and delay treatment, thereby sustaining the preventable burden of tetanus.,

Pronounced age- and sex-specific patterns persisted across the study period. The highest tetanus burden was concentrated among neonates younger than 28 days, followed by infants aged 1–5 months and 6–11 months, underscoring the continued importance of MNTE-aligned strategies, including maternal immunization, hygienic delivery practices, clean cord care, and early postnatal health services. Although the neonatal and early infancy burden declined markedly by 2023, residual risk after infancy and in adulthood may reflect waning immunity in the absence of booster vaccination, cumulative exposure to injury, and a higher prevalence of comorbidities such as diabetes and immunosuppression., Consistently higher tetanus burden among males may be attributable to greater exposure to injury-related activities, differences in healthcare-seeking behavior, and lower uptake of routine booster vaccination in adulthood; rare cases have also been reported following voluntary medical male circumcision.,

From a policy perspective, our findings indicate that global efforts to reduce the burden of tetanus have yielded considerable gains in many locations, consistent with progress towards the MNTE and IA2030 targets.,, Western Europe, high-income North America, Australasia, and high-income Asia Pacific showed near-zero tetanus incidence and mortality in 2023, a pattern that is consistent with strong routine immunization systems, maternal care, and health system capacity. However, tetanus remains an important public health concern in South Asia and sub-Saharan Africa, underscoring the need for context-specific policy action. Country-level estimates are therefore essential for policy prioritization, because immunization delivery, maternal and neonatal care, and injury management are largely planned and implemented at the national level.

In some countries, a higher burden of tetanus may reflect persistent structural and financial barriers to effective prevention. Chronic conflict, political instability, population displacement, and fragile health systems continue to undermine routine immunization programs. For example, particularly in Africa, low DTP1 coverage (≤50%) and high relative dropout from DTP1 to DTP3 (≥30%) together contributed to low DTP3 coverage, leaving many children under-immunized or zero-dose, defined as not having received DTP1. Limited access to healthcare facilities and heavy reliance on out-of-pocket health expenditures further constrain vaccine uptake. In low SDI settings, where annual per capita health spending is among the lowest globally, households often face a trade-off between seeking preventive services and avoiding catastrophic health expenditures, which may constrain vaccine uptake and hinder progress toward universal health coverage. Limited immunization financing and inflationary pressure on national health budgets may further weaken vaccine procurement, delivery, and catch-up activities in low-SDI countries, highlighting the need for sustained domestic and international support.

Reducing the persistent tetanus burden in high-risk locations requires proactive policy. Strengthening routine immunization systems and integrating tetanus prevention into broader maternal, neonatal, and child health services remain essential, through continued support for global initiatives such as the EPI and MNTE., Tetanus-containing vaccine coverage among women of reproductive age and pregnant women attending antenatal care should be prioritized, because maternal vaccination protects both mothers and newborns and reinforces opportunities for safe delivery and skilled birth attendance. In conflict-affected settings, strategies such as mobile vaccination teams, community-based engagement, and periodic catch-up campaigns are needed to reach zero-dose children. Beyond primary immunization, the introduction of tetanus toxoid-containing vaccine booster doses offers an important complementary strategy, addressing waning immunity and enabling catch-up vaccination among under-immunized adolescents and adults. The WHO recommends booster doses across the life course, and Gavi began offering programmatic support for their introduction in low-income countries in 2023, providing a critical financing mechanism to accelerate uptake in vulnerable populations., Reducing financial barriers through the expansion of universal health coverage, supported by international partners such as Gavi and UNICEF, will be critical to achieving equitable and durable tetanus control.,

This study has several limitations related to the GBD 2023 analytical framework. First, tetanus can be underdiagnosed and underreported in settings with incomplete vital registration, surveillance systems, and cause-of-death certification. Heterogeneity in reporting practices may contribute to underestimation in data-sparse locations, leading to greater statistical volatility where case counts are low. Accordingly, estimates should be interpreted in the context of uncertainty intervals generated throughout the modeling process, as observed differences may reflect variation in data density and model-related uncertainty rather than true epidemiological contrasts. Furthermore, apparent deviations from the expected association between SDI and tetanus burden in certain regions or countries, including Zimbabwe, Vanuatu, and Solomon Islands, particularly those with limited vital registration coverage and fragile surveillance systems, may reflect data sparsity and underreporting rather than true epidemiological differences. The wide uncertainty intervals observed in such regions are consistent with reduced data availability and limited confidence in the modeled estimates. In addition, country-level estimates may not fully capture subnational disparities in vaccination coverage, health-service access, and tetanus burden, particularly in rural areas. Second, cause-specific mortality estimates may be affected by misclassification of tetanus on death certificates and by the redistribution of deaths coded as resulting from non-specific causes within the GBD cause-of-death pipeline., Although these procedures improve comparability across locations and over time, they introduce additional uncertainty, particularly in areas with limited or heterogeneous mortality data. Third, key non-fatal quantities were not directly observed but were estimated through model-based approaches. Specifically, incidence was inferred by dividing modeled mortality by modeled CFRs, and prevalence was calculated using an assumed average duration of illness. While this approach enables internally consistent estimation across locations and time, residual uncertainty in mortality and CFR inputs may translate into uncertainty in incidence, prevalence, and YLD estimates. In addition, acute tetanus was modeled as a severe acute health state with fixed disability weights. However, clinical severity and outcomes can vary by setting and by access to timely care, which could influence YLD and DALY estimates. Comorbidities such as diabetes and immunosuppression, which can affect immune response or wound healing, were also not explicitly modeled as determinants of tetanus incidence or outcomes. Fourth, CFR inputs were obtained from published literature and required age-sex splitting when granular strata were unavailable. As a result, CFR estimates may be influenced by publication and selection bias in the available literature which can limit generalizability and introduce bias in subsequent estimates, even after adjustment for healthcare access and quality. Lastly, the interpretation of risk factor attribution for tetanus differs from that for many behavioral or metabolic risk factors in the GBD framework, because tetanus prevention is primarily shaped by immunization, safe delivery, clean cord care, and wound management.,, Although maternal immunization, in-facility delivery, and skilled birth attendance were incorporated as covariates in the neonatal tetanus model, the present descriptive analysis was not designed to estimate the independent contribution of each factor to regional differences in neonatal tetanus burden. Accordingly, these programmatic and health-system factors should be interpreted as important contextual determinants rather than as separately attributable risk factors in this study. While we have analyzed the association between tetanus incidence, prevalence, and DALY rates and SDI, we note that these comparisons are ecological and should be interpreted cautiously without implying causality.

Overall, this global analysis found substantial progress in reducing the burden of tetanus from 1990 to 2023, reflecting the long-term effectiveness of vaccination initiatives such as EPI and MNTE. Nevertheless, the remaining burden is largely preventable and is concentrated in populations and settings marked by persistent gaps in vaccination, maternal and neonatal care, safe delivery practices, wound management, and timely treatment. The strong inverse association between tetanus burden and the SDI underscores that persistent inequities are closely linked to structural barriers in prevention and care rather than unavoidable biological differences. Age- and sex-specific patterns, including the continued burden among neonates and males, further suggest that prevention strategies should span different stages of life, including maternal immunization and booster vaccination in adolescence and adulthood. Sustained progress toward tetanus elimination will require coordinated efforts that extend beyond immunization, including strengthening health systems, improving safe delivery and injury care, and advancing universal health coverage. Ultimately, eliminating this vaccine-preventable disease will depend on prioritizing high-burden and under-immunized populations and addressing the structural barriers that allow it to persist.
