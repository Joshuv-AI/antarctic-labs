Circulating Apelin-13, β-catenin and bone mineral density in postmenopausal women
Authors: Qi Yao, Shuang Ma, Xiaoxue Bao, Yukun Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe aim of this study was to investigate associations of circulating Apelin-13, β-catenin, Exendin-4, and Nesfatin-1 with site-specific bone mineral density (BMD) in postmenopausal women, representing adipose-endocrine, Wnt/β-catenin, incretin-related, and energy homeostasis pathways. A total of 315 postmenopausal women were classified into normal bone mass, osteopenia, or osteoporosis group according to World Health Organization criteria using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry. Serum biomarkers were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. Lumbar spine BMD and hip BMD were analyzed separately using group comparisons, Spearman correlation, fully adjusted multivariable linear regression, and exploratory receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses. Lumbar spine BMD was 0.936 ± 0.084, 0.935 ± 0.121, and 0.741 ± 0.105 g/cm2 in the normal bone mass, osteopenia, and osteoporosis groups, respectively; hip BMD was 0.942 ± 0.045, 0.878 ± 0.073, and 0.702 ± 0.084 g/cm2. Apelin-13 decreased across groups from 702.09 ± 56.42 to 624.98 ± 43.99 and 334.37 ± 91.49 pg/mL, and β-catenin from 100.23 ± 11.34 to 76.21 ± 14.65 and 54.33 ± 8.25 ng/mL. In fully adjusted models, Apelin-13 was independently associated with lumbar spine BMD (β = 0.547, P < 0.001) and hip BMD (β = 0.524, P < 0.001), while β-catenin was associated with hip BMD (β = 0.272, P < 0.001). Exploratory ROC analyses suggested apparent discriminatory performance for Apelin-13, β-catenin, Nesfatin-1, Exendin-4, and the combined model, with AUCs of 1.000, 0.944, 0.680, 0.613, and 1.000, respectively; these findings should be interpreted cautiously and require external validation. Apelin-13 and β-catenin may serve as circulating biomarkers related to site-specific bone mass status, requiring external validation.Endocrine connectionsBone loss is common in women after menopause, yet blood-based markers that reflect bone health remain limited. In this study, we measured four circulating biomarkers related to metabolic and bone regulatory pathways and compared them with BMD. Women with higher BMD generally had higher levels of Apelin-13 and β-catenin, and these two biomarkers showed the strongest associations with bone mass. These findings suggest that circulating biomarkers may provide additional information about bone mass status, but further validation in independent populations is needed before clinical use.

Introduction

Postmenopausal osteoporosis is a common skeletal disorder characterized by reduced bone mineral density (BMD), deterioration of bone microarchitecture, and increased fracture risk (1, 2). Although dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) remains the standard method for assessing BMD (2, 3), circulating biomarkers may provide additional information on bone metabolism and help identify biological pathways associated with bone loss (4, 5, 6).

Bone remodeling is closely linked to metabolic and endocrine regulation (5, 6). Several circulating peptides and signaling molecules involved in energy metabolism, inflammation, and skeletal homeostasis have been suggested to participate in the regulation of bone formation and resorption (5, 6). Apelin-13, an endogenous peptide of the apelinergic system, has been implicated in metabolic regulation and may influence osteoblast and osteoclast activity (7, 8, 9, 10, 11). β-catenin is a key downstream mediator of canonical Wnt signaling, which is essential for osteoblast differentiation and maintenance of bone mass (12, 13). Exendin-4, a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist, may affect bone metabolism through both direct and indirect metabolic mechanisms (14, 15, 16, 17, 18). Nesfatin-1 is involved in appetite regulation and energy homeostasis, and emerging evidence suggests a possible association with skeletal metabolism (19, 20).

However, the clinical associations of these circulating biomarkers with BMD in postmenopausal women remain insufficiently understood. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the relationships between circulating Apelin-13, β-catenin, Exendin-4, and Nesfatin-1 and BMD in postmenopausal women and to explore their potential value as complementary biomarkers of bone mass status.

Materials and methods

Subject selection

A total of 315 postmenopausal women aged 45–55 years were included in this study, comprising 105 patients with postmenopausal osteoporosis evaluated at Hebei Medical University Third Hospital between July and September 2025 and 210 postmenopausal women with normal bone mass or osteopenia who underwent routine health examinations and clinical bone health assessment at the same hospital during this period. Participants were classified into normal bone mass, osteopenia, and osteoporosis groups according to World Health Organization criteria based on DXA-derived T-scores. DXA was performed as part of clinical bone health evaluation rather than solely for research purposes, and it was not a universal population-based screening program for all women in this age range. Rather, women underwent DXA according to clinical need, health examination arrangements, or individual willingness to assess bone health. DXA was performed through routine clinical or health examination pathways under the hospital’s standard charging arrangements and was not provided solely for research purposes. Participants were not specifically recruited from a prior-fracture cohort, and women with known previous fragility fractures were excluded. Therefore, the study population should be interpreted as a hospital-based sample of women undergoing DXA assessment, rather than a representative sample of the general postmenopausal population. Fasting blood samples and DXA measurements were obtained on the same day for all participants.

The age range of 45–55 years was selected to focus on the early postmenopausal period, during which accelerated bone loss commonly occurs around the menopausal transition and the early years after menopause. This age restriction was intended to reduce heterogeneity related to advanced age and long postmenopausal duration. Nevertheless, age was still included as a covariate in all multivariable regression models to further account for age-related effects on BMD. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Hebei Medical University Third Hospital, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

The exclusion criteria were as follows:history of previous fragility fractures;metabolic bone diseases other than postmenopausal osteoporosis, including primary hyperparathyroidism, hyperthyroidism, Cushing’s syndrome, osteomalacia, and Paget’s disease of bone;chronic inflammatory, autoimmune, or malignant diseases, including rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and malignant tumors;severe hepatic dysfunction or renal dysfunction;major endocrine or metabolic disorders, including diabetes mellitus;use of medications known to affect bone metabolism or metabolic parameters within the previous 3 months, including glucocorticoids, thyroid hormones, anticonvulsants, bisphosphonates, denosumab, teriparatide, selective estrogen receptor modulators, menopausal hormone therapy, or GLP-1 receptor agonists;incomplete clinical, DXA, or laboratory data;visibly hemolyzed serum samples or serum samples unsuitable for ELISA measurement.

The present analysis was based on eligible participants with complete clinical, DXA, and laboratory data. Because a prospective screening log was not maintained during participant recruitment, the number of individuals excluded for each specific criterion was not available.

General data collection

Baseline characteristics, including age, height, and weight, were collected for all participants. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters (kg/m2).

Serum sample collection

After an overnight fast of 8–10 h, venous blood samples were obtained and centrifuged at 1,790 g (RCF) for 8 min. Serum was separated, aliquoted, and stored at −80°C until analysis. Visibly hemolyzed samples were excluded because hemolysis may release intracellular components and interfere with colorimetric or immunoassay-based measurements, including ELISA detection. In the present study, no serum samples were excluded because of visible hemolysis.

Biochemical parameter assays

Biochemical parameters were assessed as follows: alkaline phosphatase (ALP), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), total bilirubin (TBIL), serum creatinine (SCr), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), fasting blood glucose (FBG), serum calcium (Ca), serum phosphorus (P), and 24 h urinary calcium (uCa) were determined using automated enzymatic colorimetric methods on a Hitachi 7600 biochemical analyzer (Hitachi, Japan). In addition, serum parathyroid hormone (PTH), 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D], calcitonin (CT), osteocalcin (OCN), β-C-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen (β-CTX), procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide (P1NP), luteinizing hormone (LH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), and prolactin (PRL) were measured using the Roche Cobas e801 automated chemiluminescence immunoassay system (Roche Diagnostics, Switzerland). All assays were performed according to the manufacturers’ protocols, and both intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation were <10%.

Bone mineral density measurement

BMD was assessed by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) using a Hologic Discovery A densitometer (Hologic Inc., USA). The device was calibrated daily with a standard phantom to ensure accuracy and precision. Scans were performed at the lumbar spine (L1–L4) and bilateral hip. All measurements were performed by certified technicians following standard quality control and radiation safety procedures. The precision of repeated scans in a random subset of participants yielded a coefficient of variation below 1.5%. For analysis, site-specific BMD values were used, including lumbar spine BMD and hip BMD (defined as the mean value of bilateral total hip BMD measurements).

Detection of the biomarkers

Serum levels of Apelin-13 and Nesfatin-1 were determined using commercial ELISA kits (Elabscience, China), while β-catenin and Exendin-4 were measured using ELISA kits from Abcam (UK; distributed by Agtech, China). All assays were performed according to the manufacturers’ instructions, and intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation were <10%. Samples with concentrations above the upper limit of the standard curve were re-assayed after appropriate dilution. Samples with concentrations below the lower limit of detection or still outside the reliable detection range after repeat testing were excluded from the corresponding analysis. In the present study, all measured values were within the reliable detection range after initial or repeat testing, and no samples were excluded because of values outside the detection range.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas non-normally distributed data are presented as median (interquartile range). Intergroup comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance or the Kruskal–Wallis test, followed by appropriate post hoc comparisons.

Spearman correlation analysis was used to assess associations between circulating biomarkers and clinical, biochemical, hormonal, bone turnover, and site-specific BMD variables. The strength of correlation was interpreted according to the absolute value of the correlation coefficient: |r| < 0.30 was considered weak, 0.30 ≤ |r| < 0.60 was considered moderate, and |r| ≥ 0.60 was considered strong.

Multiple linear regression analyses were performed to identify factors independently associated with lumbar spine BMD and hip BMD. Covariates were selected based on clinical relevance, biological plausibility, previous evidence related to BMD or bone metabolism, and results from univariate comparisons and correlation analyses. Based on these considerations, age, BMI, TG, CT, OCN, LH, PRL, Apelin-13, β-catenin, Nesfatin-1, and Exendin-4 were entered into the multivariable regression models. These variables were selected to account for demographic, metabolic, bone turnover, hormonal, and candidate biomarker-related factors while avoiding excessive model complexity. Multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), and VIF values <3 were considered indicative of no substantial multicollinearity.

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS, version 22.0 (IBM Corp., USA). Cross-validated logistic regression and out-of-fold ROC analyses were performed using Python, version 3.13.5, and scikit-learn, version 1.8.0.

Exploratory ROC analysis was performed to assess the discriminatory performance of circulating biomarkers for distinguishing osteoporosis from non-osteoporosis, with osteoporosis defined as the positive outcome and normal bone mass or osteopenia as the non-osteoporosis reference group. For the individual biomarkers, AUC values and 95% confidence intervals were estimated using a nonparametric approach (21), and cutoff values were determined using the Youden index. The combined binary logistic regression model incorporated Apelin-13, β-catenin, Nesfatin-1, Exendin-4, age, and BMI. Its discriminatory performance was evaluated using out-of-fold predicted probabilities generated by five-fold stratified cross-validation with shuffled folds and a fixed random seed of 42. ROC curves, AUC values, and cutoff values for the combined model were calculated from these out-of-fold predictions. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Circulating biomarker analysis

Serum levels of Apelin-13, β-catenin, Nesfatin-1, and Exendin-4 among the three groups are illustrated in Fig. 1, and the corresponding numerical data are provided in Supplementary Table S1 (see section on Supplementary materials given at the end of the article). Using the normal bone mass group as the reference, Apelin-13 levels were significantly lower in the osteopenia and osteoporosis groups (702.09 ± 56.42 pg/mL vs 624.98 ± 43.99 pg/mL and 334.37 ± 91.49 pg/mL, respectively; all P < 0.05). Similarly, β-catenin levels were lower in the osteopenia and osteoporosis groups than in the normal bone mass group (100.23 ± 11.34 ng/mL vs 76.21 ± 14.65 ng/mL and 54.33 ± 8.25 ng/mL, respectively; all P < 0.05).

Group comparisons of circulating Apelin-13, β-catenin, Nesfatin-1, and Exendin-4 levels among postmenopausal women with different bone mass statuses. Data are presented as mean ± SD. *P < 0.05 vs the normal bone mass group. For Exendin-4, no significant difference was observed between the osteopenia and osteoporosis groups.

Nesfatin-1 also decreased from the normal bone mass group to the osteopenia and osteoporosis groups (241.02 ± 90.52 pg/mL vs 214.31 ± 79.10 pg/mL and 173.51 ± 61.76 pg/mL, respectively; all P < 0.05). Exendin-4 levels were lower in the osteopenia and osteoporosis groups than in the normal bone mass group (253.28 ± 85.90 pg/mL vs 197.13 ± 83.41 pg/mL and 188.92 ± 77.43 pg/mL, respectively; P < 0.05 for comparisons with the normal bone mass group), whereas no significant difference was observed between the osteopenia and osteoporosis groups.

Multiple linear regression analysis

The results of the fully adjusted multivariable linear regression analyses are presented in Table 4 and Fig. 3. Apelin-13 was independently and positively associated with lumbar spine BMD (β = 0.547, P < 0.001), whereas β-catenin, Nesfatin-1, and Exendin-4 were not independently associated with lumbar spine BMD. For hip BMD, both Apelin-13 (β = 0.524, P < 0.001) and β-catenin (β = 0.272, P < 0.001) remained independently and positively associated with BMD, whereas Nesfatin-1 and Exendin-4 did not retain independent significance after adjustment. No substantial multicollinearity was observed among the variables included in the models, with all VIF values below 3.

Exploratory ROC analysis

As shown in Fig. 4 and Table 5, exploratory ROC analysis was performed to evaluate the discriminatory performance of the four circulating biomarkers for distinguishing postmenopausal osteoporosis from non-osteoporosis. Apelin-13 showed the highest discriminatory performance, with an AUC of 1.000 (95% CI: 1.000–1.000), followed by β-catenin with an AUC of 0.944 (95% CI: 0.921–0.966). Nesfatin-1 showed modest discriminatory performance, with an AUC of 0.680 (95% CI: 0.622–0.739), whereas Exendin-4 showed limited discriminatory performance, with an AUC of 0.613 (95% CI: 0.550–0.676).

Exploratory receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for circulating biomarkers in discriminating postmenopausal osteoporosis from non-osteoporosis. Osteoporosis was treated as the positive outcome, whereas normal bone mass and osteopenia constituted the non-osteoporosis reference group. The combined binary logistic regression model incorporated Apelin-13, β-catenin, Nesfatin-1, Exendin-4, age, and BMI and was evaluated using out-of-fold predicted probabilities generated by five-fold stratified cross-validation. The dotted diagonal line indicates the line of no discrimination. Because no independent external validation cohort was available, these results should be interpreted as exploratory rather than as definitive diagnostic thresholds.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that circulating Apelin-13, β-catenin, Nesfatin-1, and Exendin-4 levels were lower in postmenopausal women with osteopenia and osteoporosis than in those with normal bone mass. All four circulating biomarkers showed positive correlations with lumbar spine BMD and hip BMD, with stronger associations observed for Apelin-13 and β-catenin. In the fully adjusted models, Apelin-13 remained independently associated with both lumbar spine BMD and hip BMD, whereas β-catenin remained independently associated with hip BMD. In contrast, Nesfatin-1 and Exendin-4 did not retain independent significance after adjustment for age, BMI, TG, CT, OCN, LH, PRL, and the other circulating biomarkers.

The exploratory ROC analysis further showed that these biomarkers differed in their ability to discriminate postmenopausal osteoporosis from non-osteoporosis. Apelin-13 showed the highest discriminatory performance, with an AUC of 1.000. β-catenin also demonstrated high discriminatory performance, with an AUC of 0.944, whereas Nesfatin-1 and Exendin-4 showed lower discriminatory ability. The combined model incorporating all four circulating biomarkers, age, and BMI also achieved an AUC of 1.000 under five-fold stratified cross-validation. However, these near-perfect AUC values should be interpreted cautiously because the analysis was based on a single-center cohort without external validation.

The biological plausibility of these findings is supported by previous experimental and clinical studies. Apelin-13 has been shown to promote osteoblast differentiation and to be linked to Wnt/β-catenin signaling, AMPK-mediated mitophagy, and BMD-related bone status (7, 8, 9, 10, 11). β-catenin is a key mediator of canonical Wnt signaling and plays an essential role in osteogenesis and bone homeostasis (12, 13). These findings provide a biological basis for the observed associations of Apelin-13 and β-catenin with site-specific BMD. In contrast, although Nesfatin-1 and Exendin-4 were associated with BMD in univariate analyses, these associations were not maintained after multivariable adjustment. This suggests that their relationships with bone metabolism may be more indirect or may be influenced by broader systemic metabolic regulation. This interpretation is consistent with previous studies on GLP-1 receptor agonist-related pathways and bone metabolism (14, 15, 16, 17, 18), as well as emerging evidence linking Nesfatin-1 to skeletal metabolism and osteoclast-related pathways (19, 20).

Another finding of the present study was that classical bone turnover markers showed limited associations with site-specific BMD. Although P1NP showed a borderline intergroup difference and β-CTX did not differ significantly among the three bone mass groups, neither marker was strongly associated with lumbar spine or hip BMD. P1NP and β-CTX are widely used as reference markers of bone formation and bone resorption, respectively, and are particularly useful for assessing dynamic bone remodeling and monitoring treatment response (4). However, BMD reflects cumulative bone mass at specific skeletal sites, whereas bone turnover markers reflect short-term remodeling activity. Therefore, the weak associations between P1NP, β-CTX, and BMD in this cross-sectional cohort may reflect the biological distinction between dynamic turnover and accumulated bone mass. In addition, bone turnover markers are influenced by circadian variation, fasting status, renal function, recent physical activity, and menopausal duration (4). The relatively narrow age range and single-center design of the present study may also have reduced the variability of these markers. These findings suggest that P1NP and β-CTX should be interpreted as complementary indicators of bone remodeling rather than direct surrogates of site-specific BMD.

Our findings are consistent with previous studies suggesting that metabolic and endocrine-related circulating biomarkers are involved in the regulation of skeletal homeostasis (5, 6, 22). Increasing evidence indicates that postmenopausal osteoporosis is not only a skeletal disorder but is also associated with systemic metabolic and endocrine alterations (1, 2). In this context, circulating biomarkers related to adipose-endocrine signaling, Wnt/β-catenin signaling, and energy metabolism may provide additional information beyond traditional clinical and biochemical markers for evaluating bone health.

From a clinical perspective, the present findings should be interpreted as exploratory rather than directly practice-changing. DXA remains the standard method for assessing BMD and diagnosing osteoporosis (2, 3). Circulating Apelin-13 and β-catenin are not proposed as replacements for DXA, but they may provide complementary biological information related to bone mass status. If confirmed in larger multicenter and longitudinal studies, these biomarkers may help identify postmenopausal women who are more likely to have reduced bone mass and who may benefit from further DXA assessment or closer bone health monitoring. However, external validation and prospective evaluation are required before these biomarkers can be incorporated into clinical risk assessment or decision-making.

Despite these strengths, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the cross-sectional design of this study precludes any inference of causal relationships between circulating biomarkers and BMD. Although covariates in the multivariable models were selected based on clinical relevance, biological plausibility, and statistical associations, a formal DAG-based causal framework was not prespecified; therefore, residual confounding and model specification bias cannot be fully excluded. Second, participants were recruited from a single-center, hospital-based population undergoing DXA according to clinical need, health examination arrangements, or individual willingness. This recruitment strategy may have introduced referral and self-selection bias, and the study population may not fully represent the general postmenopausal population. In addition, no external validation cohort was available, particularly for the ROC analyses; therefore, the generalizability and discriminatory performance of these findings require further confirmation. Third, the study population was restricted to postmenopausal women aged 45–55 years in order to reduce heterogeneity related to advanced age and prolonged postmenopausal duration, but this may limit the applicability of the results to older postmenopausal women. In addition, because a prospective screening log was not maintained, the number of individuals excluded for each specific exclusion criterion could not be reported, which may limit the transparency of participant flow. Future multicenter longitudinal studies with prospective screening records, broader age ranges, prespecified causal frameworks, and external validation cohorts are needed to confirm these findings.

In conclusion, circulating Apelin-13 was independently associated with both lumbar spine and hip BMD, whereas β-catenin was independently associated with hip BMD in postmenopausal women. These findings suggest that Apelin-13 and β-catenin may provide complementary information on bone mass status, but they should not be considered substitutes for DXA. Further multicenter longitudinal studies with external validation are needed to determine whether these biomarkers can contribute to clinical risk stratification or bone health assessment in postmenopausal women.
