Executive Summary
Over the course of this project (DOE Award #DE-SC0021315), we have successfully built a program
of nuclear decay spectroscopy at Louisiana State University (LSU) to investigate nuclear structure
and decay properties of isotopes influential to nuclear structure, nuclear energy, the formation of the
heavy elements in stars, and searches of nuclear shape coexistence. We performed β-decay studies
with decay stations using radioactive ion beams from the Argonne Tandem Linac Accelerator
System (ATLAS) and the CAlifornium Rare Isotope Beam Upgrade (CARIBU) facility at Argonne
National Laboratory (ANL). We developed and fielded new instrumentation, designed and built
at LSU, to enable new programs of internal conversion electron (CE) and beta-delayed neutron
spectroscopy. The LSU-Argonne Conversion Electron Spectrometer (LACES) was commissioned
with the X-Array/SATURN decay station at ANL with studies of A=150 and 156 isotopes. The
MUlti-Segment Electron Spectrometer (MUSES) was completed and made ready for high-resolution
electron-γ measurements of neutron-activated samples from the High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR)
at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL). At ANL, the BEtA Recoil-ion trap (BEARtrap), the
first dedicated instrument for β-recoil-ion time-of-flight spectroscopy, was completed and prepared
for measurements of fission fragment beams from the forthcoming nuCARIBU facility. We produced
4 peer-reviewed publications, two PhD dissertations, and formed strong collaborations with U.S.
National Laboratories (ANL, ORNL, and LLNL) as well as domestic and international academic
institutions.

Summary of Project Activities
β and β − γ − γ Studies
At CARIBU/ANL, we performed a high-resolution β-γ and β-γ-γ decay study of 134,134m Sb using
the X-Array HPGe clover detectors and Scintillator And Tape Using Radioactive Nuclei (SATURN)
decay station [1]. The 134 Sb ground state (J π = 0− ) is reported to decay predominantly to the 0+
ground state of 134 Te (Iβ = 97.6±0.5%) via a first-forbidden Gamow-Teller transition [2]. A previous
study of trapped 134 Sb ions using the Beta-decay Paul Trap (BPT) [3] produced a Recoil-Ion Time-
Of-Flight (RI-TOF) spectrum of 134 Te ions best fit by a β- νe correlation coefficient of 0.47±0.16,
which is discrepant from the theoretical value of unity by over 3σ. In Ref. [3], the observed
BPT RI-TOF spectrum could be reconciled by reallocating ∼17% of the total β-decay strength
to a fictitious 5-MeV excited state in 134 Te, implying a need for improved β-decay spectroscopy
of 134 Sb. We performed a high-resolution β–γ–γ spectroscopy study of an isotopically-separated
CARIBU beam of 134,134m Sb using the X-Array/SATURN decay station [1] led by LSU graduate
student Graeme Morgan. The study was sensitive to β branches down to the 0.01% level and the
number of new 134 Te transitions (levels) observed compared to the ENSDF database is 3 (3) and 39
(28) for 134 Sb and 134m Sb decays, respectively. Mr. Morgan successfully defended his dissertation
“Decay Spectroscopy of 134 Sb Using X-Array and SATURN” in Spring 2024. Dr. Morgan (currently
a postdoctoral researcher at Texas A&M University) is preparing a publication that will include
updated level schemes and β-feeding intensities of the decay of 134 Sb and the high-spin (7− ) 134m Sb
isomer.

Internal Conversion Electron Studies
LACES
The new LACES system was successfully commissioned with the X-Array/SATURN decay station
and the first science run has been completed using CARIBU beams (Fig. 1). The LACES system was
designed, assembled, built, and tested at LSU including a new frame to couple it to the existing X-
Array/SATURN system. LSU graduate student Sergio Lopez and PI Marley transported LACES to
ANL in mid-2022 and ran two campaigns with collaborators in Summer 2022 and Spring 2023. The
commissioning goal for LACES was met by measuring the half-life of the 150.3-keV isomer in 156 Pm
via 156 Nd beta decay for which there was only an experimental upper limit (t1/2 < 5 s) [4]. The
second campaign consisted of calibration beams and an experiment to search for electric monopole
(E0) transitions in 98,100 Zr via 98,100 Y β decay (PI. M. Carpenter, ANL). An instrumentation paper
on the performance and simulation-confirmed absolute efficiency for LACES was published in Nucl.
Instrum. Methods. Phys. Res. A. [5] by Sergio Lopez in early 2024, and he successfully defended his
dissertation “The LSU-Argonne Conversion Electron Spectrometer And Measurement of Isomeric
Half-Life In 156 Pm” in Spring 2024. A second manuscript by Dr. Lopez (now a postdoctoral
researcher at ANL) is nearing completion on the spectroscopy of 156 Nd → 156g,m Pm → 156 Sm
decay chain for submission to Phys. Rev. C.

segments of the detector were tested to work optimally with an in-house 207 Bi source. LSU graduate

BEtA Recoil-ion Trap (BEARtrap)
BEARtrap is the culmination of almost a decade of work to develop a dedicated instrument
for studying βn emission using Recoil-Ion Time-Of-Flight (RI-TOF) spectroscopy. The RI-TOF
method for studying βn precursors was established in previous studies [3, 7–9] using the BPT [10],
a radiofrequency quadrupole (RFQ) ion trap designed with an open electrode geometry that allows
detectors to surround a trapped radioactive sample. Beta decays are detected in plastic scintillators
in ∆E-E telescope configurations, and the recoil ions are detected in microchannel plate (MCP) de-

Figure 3: Example of recoil-ion time-of-flight (RI-TOF) spectroscopy with the BPT. Left: A cross-
sectional diagram of the BPT and detectors configured for RI-TOF spectroscopy measurements.
Right: A time-of-flight spectrum for recoil ions from 137 I β decay in the BPT. Recoil events from
βn emission (136 Xe) are observed at shorter time-of-flight relative to those from β decay (137 Xe)
and can be clearly identified without detecting the emitted neutron. Figure adapted from [8].

tectors providing a measure of the RI-TOF. HPGe detectors were placed behind the MCP detectors
to detect γ transitions from the recoil (Fig. 3, left). Due to the difference between the neutron and
lepton masses, the momentum imparted to a recoil ion from βn emission is much greater than that
from β decay, resulting in a shorter time-of-flight (Fig. 3, right). This characteristic time-of-flight
signature allows for clear identification of βn emission without requiring neutron detection.
 During this project, all of the vacuum, electrical, cryogenic, and mechanical support components
for BEARtrap were designed, fabricated and assembled. The two new RFQ ion traps, one for ion
bunch preparation and the other for confinement during decay measurements, were designed and
fabricated by the LSU group. The Measurement RFQ trap electrodes, insulating electrode mounts,
and housing were all fabricated at the LSU P&A Department Machine Shop. In Spring 2024, all
of BEARtrap including the two ion traps and detector system were assembled in the ATLAS low-
energy experimental area. The BEARtrap collaboration was successful in preparing and trapping
radioactive samples of 142 Cs (t1/2 = 1.684 s) from CARIBU in Summer 2024 and confined them
for multiple seconds. The ion transport, trapping, and detector systems have all been subsequently
improved, and the system is now ready to receive commissioning beams from nuCARIBU.
Other Activities
The collaboration with the ion-trapping group at ANL continued to include activities with the Beta-
decay Paul Trap (BPT) including participation in it’s most recent upgrade to perform increasingly
high-precision β-decay measurements using A=8 nuclear beta decays [11]. We published results on
the structure of 104 Mo and 146 Ce populated by in measurements of the β-decay of high-spin isomers
in 104 Nb [12] and 146 La [13], respectively, using the Gammasphere Decay Station.
 Our group has been successful in planning research at new facilities. We submitted two letters
of intent (LOIs): one to the ATLAS PAC to β-decay measurements with beams of 201−203 Ir at
the N=126 Factory. Additionally, we’ve submitted a LOI to the FRIB PAC to use MUSES and
detectors from the FRIB Decay Station initiator (FDSi) with stopped radioactive ion beams. Both
LOIs were accepted with support from both facilities.

Accomplishments vs. Proposed Goals
The original goals of the project involving the development of instrumentation was successful.
LACES and MUSES were spectrometers that came into existence during the award period. BEARtrap
was assembled, and shown to successfully trap ions from CARIBU, however, the commissioning
experiment science runs with 92 Rb and 137 I could not be performed during this award period due
to the shutdown of the CARIBU facility in Summer 2025.
