1. Executive Summary
Under the May/7th/2019 DOE Advanced Manufacturing Office Multi-Topic Funding Opportunity
Announcement # DE-FOA-0001980 Topic 3.1, the Eaton Corporation was granted the
“Megawatt-Class, High-Voltage SiC Power Electronics Converters for Advanced Manufacturing
with Grid Support Services” under FAIN #DEEE0009135, on October 01, 2020.
Eaton successfully completed the program on April 30, 2025.

1.1. Background:
Immediately after the Next Generation Electric Machine – NGEM – program, the DOE devised
an innovative initiative to enhance the grid power quality in the USA. Eaton had successfully
completed the DOE-NGEM program by designing, building, and testing a 3-phase 5kV 1.2MVA
600Hz Fundamental & 7.5kHz Switching AFE Converter that drove a 1MW 15,000rpm 4-pole
500Hz fundamental PMSM.

1.2. Objectives:
Aimed to push the 10kV SiC MOSFET based power electronics to 3-phase 13.8kV 10MW to be
used as a Megawatt Class Medium-Voltage Power Conditioning Systems – PCS – to enable
“Resilient Distribution through Grid-dispatching and Uninterrupted Quality Power to
Manufacturing Facilities” during transitory grid disturbances. As a stretch to those goals, Eaton
added the load power peak shaving opportunity. Following is a summary of the goals:
 1. Design a 10W Silicon Carbide (SiC) based Power Conditioning System (PCS) with
 13.8kV, 3-phase, bidirectional AC and a 22kV DC bus to interface with Distributed Energy
 Resources (DER) and distributed energy storage.
 2. Demonstrate via analysis, modeling & simulation, the grid dispatch capability of
 combining the inertial load of multi-megawatt industrial applications with large inertia and
 DC link capacitance energy storage.
 3. Demonstrate the capability of 10 kV SiC series connected devices to increase the efficiency
 and reliability of 13.8 kV power converters.
 4. Advance the development of 25kV class, 120kV/us EMI enabled and with adequate
 bandwidth ancillary circuits such as – gate-drivers, power supplies and sensors for current
 and voltage feedback - to enable ultra-compact, high-power, medium-voltage (MV)
 converters based on series connected 10 kV SiC MOSFET 20kV switches.
 5. Proof of concept of the proposed 10MW NPC converter paper design is a 1MW, 13.8kV,
 bidirectional, three-phase converter prototype.

Megawatt class, 13.8 kV converters based on the state-of-the-art power semiconductor devices are
generally based on cascaded H-bridge – CHB – or modular multilevel converter (MMC)
topologies, that require many low-voltage (1,200V) semiconductor modules, ancillary circuits, and
a bulky transformer. These impose a reliability limit as well as dimensional challenges to those
applications where compact size matters.

To achieve the challenging power density metrics of less than 0.3m3/MVA, the Eaton project team
developed a 20kVswitch formed by the series-connection of two 10kV SiC MOSFET modules
that can operate continuously with a blocking voltage of up to 16kVDC. A 3-phase, two-level
inverter topology with this 20kV switch will handle 9.5kVRMS.

A 3-level Neutral Point Clamped NPC inverter topology reaches >13.8kVRMS using this 20kV
switch. The NPC converter is the standard topology for industry applications at Eaton for
4.16kVRMS to 7.2kVRMS due to its reliability, simplicity, low number of devices, and option to
operate without a dedicated transformer by using an Active Front-end – AFE.
 Based on its NPC inverter experience and expertise, the Eaton team designed a transformer-
less back-to-back NPC based bidirectional converter to operate at 10 MW, 13.8 kV achieving a
power density of 0.29 m3/MVA per inverter.

The designed system, demonstrated through analysis and simulation its capability to dispatch up
to 20% of its rated power – or 1MW – by using the accumulated energy in its DC link with no
interruption of the supplied manufacturing process – up to the rated 10MW.

This PCS converter was designed to aid in the resilience and the quality of the power distribution.
The amount of energy that can be dispatched depends on the capacitance of the DC link and can
add any other form of energy storage available such as rotating masses.

 7

The Eaton proposed solution for the FOA using the 20kV SiC MOSFET switches in a transformer-
less 10MW 13.8kVRMS 3-level NPC, achieved outstanding efficiency of 99.45% and service life
above 13 years meeting the targets proposed in this project. The manufacturing cost of the 10 kV
SiC MOSFET based 10MW 13.8kV power electronics assembly was also reduced achieving a
mark of $28/kW. The following tables summarizes the minimum, stretch, and achieved goals in
this project.

2. Introduction
A power electronics converter with grid-dispatch, peak shaving and power factor correction
capabilities was designed which can be installed at a manufacturing facility.

The research work identified multi-megawatt industrial applications with large inertia and a
degree of operational freedom to allow the energy stored in that inertia to be injected back to the
distribution network using the proposed converter. Demonstrated the grid dispatch capability
of combining the inertial load and DC link capacitance energy storage.

Traditional electric power systems are composed of distributed resources and loads that are
interconnected to each other in a way that guarantees the best system performance. Manufacturing
facilities, for example, are fed with traditional 13.8 kV to 36 kV standard power systems to support
megawatt class loads. Although such systems have been successfully utilized in the last century,
their limitations give margin to improve its energy flexibility, efficiency, and resiliency
particularly during abnormal transient events. Abnormal plant-triggered transient events, for
example, can result in interruption of process control, electrical grid stress and power quality
issues.

The advances in high-voltage wide-bandgap devices, enable a compact, efficient, and reliable
power conditioning systems (PCS) to support electric power systems in manufacturing facilities.
The PCS system enables functions specified in IEEE-1547-4 to respond to utility commands to
perform grid-dispatch, peak shaving, and power factor correction capabilities. The grid-
dispatchability of the bidirectional PCS can reduce or interrupt power consumption and even inject
energy stored in the load or in the DC link – large capacitance or battery backup – to overcome
the consequences of abnormal transient events. The PCS can be controlled as an energy
management system to enable coordination and control of several DERs and Energy Storage
Systems (ESS) with MW class loads with common and mission critical profiles. The PCS can also
function as an overall “monitoring controller” for the entire manufacturing facility. It can be
programmed to achieve several objective functions - such as to provide a constant power demand
(MW) from the utility at reduced rates of contracted real power (MW) supply with zero or minimal
reactive power (VAR) supply; thereby enabling increased penetration of DERs and ESS.

Designed a Power Conditioning System (PCS) with 13.8kV, 3-phase, bidirectional AC and a
24kV DC bus to interface with Distributed Energy Resources (DER) and distributed energy
storage. A grid services study will identify state-of-the-art manufacturing facilities that are
utilizing DERs along with medium voltage utility power to establish the baseline for showing the
achievement of grid dispatch power performance targets.

Eaton notionally proposed to employ existing hardware and software cyber security protocols
within the PCS control system architecture.
 8

The PCS converter is capable of bi-directional energy flow operating with an input and output
capable of 13.8kV, 3-phase, 60Hz that met the power electronics technical performance targets for
efficiency, cost, service life and power density.

Eaton designed a compact, high-power, high-efficiency, isolated, bidirectional AC/AC converter
for 13.8V-13.8kV at 10 MW that is upward and downward scalable in power. The innovative
approaches employed demonstrated a converter design with 10kV SiC MOSFET modules tested
at 20kV partial discharge free according to IEC 61287-1 standard. The Eaton 12-die 120A 10kV
MOSFET modules in the 13.8kVRMS 60Hz AC to AC with dual 3-level neutral point clamped
inverters of high-performance 10 MW converters for a broad range of demanding power
electronics applications. Moreover, this project advanced the solutions to overcome the barriers
for the effective employment of ≥10kV WBG semiconductors:

 • Fiber optic-based power supplies, gate drivers and sensors to achieve electromagnetic
 compatibility in 120kV/microsecond applications.
 • 120kV/microsecond hardened controls
 • Voltage and current sharing of series/parallel connected ≥10kV SiC MOSFET
 • Enhanced thermal conductive dielectric coordination.
 • Ultra-low commutation path inductance bus bar system
 • 10kV SiC MOSFET Modules with 20kV Partial Discharge Free Dielectric
 Coordination

Although the developed converter was designed to operate as a PCS to increase efficiency,
flexibility, and energy of manufacturing sites it becomes a critical building block for several
medium-voltage high power applications, such as:

 • Compact topside & subsea converters for petroleum pump motors and gas
 compressors with large step-out distances; topside & subsea gas compressors and gas
 separation systems.
 • Distribution solid-state transformers medium voltage dual active bridge and its
 complementing the active front-end converters.

The goals achieved in this project was vital for a future proliferation of high-voltage (≥10kV)
WBG SiC semiconductor switches available today since it generates a high in demand core
converter applicable in markets where the total system cost is more important than the component
cost. Moreover, the designed converter with minimal number of active components reduces the
volume of passive ancillary devices, thereby increasing the power density above a factor of ten.
Its development has the potential to be a disruptive advancement considering the potential
economic impact and significant target performance metric improvements. In fact, a large
American oil-and-gas company is supporting the further development of this AC/AC converter.

They consider it a key building block for the next generation of more efficient platform and subsea
drives for oil and gas extraction. The power density approximately (25) times higher than the

 9

existing state-of-the-art drives is extremely important for applications with strict space (platform)
and sensitive to volume and weight (subsea deployment).

The 10MW 10kV SiC-based three-level neutral-point clamped inverter design was demonstrated
via a scaled down 1MW 13.8kV prototype which was fully tested at Eaton’s MV Power
Conversion Lab to demonstrate its voltage and power handling capabilities while the grid
dispatchability and resiliency of manufacturing plant loads and other energy assets connected
through the PCS was demonstrated with analysis, modeling and simulations including Control-
Hardware-in-the-Loop – CHIL.

The MV PCS developed in the project is a MV 13.8kV, 3-phase, 10 MVA rated grid connected
bidirectional Back-to-Back (B2B) voltage source converter system with 24kV DC bus. The 24kV
DC bus feeds a MV, 3-phase, 10 MVA rated inverter which provides a 0 - 13.8kV AC bus to feed
a manufacturing facility. The MV AC bus 13.8kV supplies large manufacturing facility rotational
and non-rotational loads such as MV motor drives for various applications such as pumps, fans,
compressors, rolling mills, textiles mills, blowers and other air-handling systems, etc. The various
loads on the MV ac bus have very different load profiles in terms of both real (MW) and reactive
(MVAR).

The proposed MV PCS system must not only integrate the various DER and DESS in the form of
DC link capacitors or in the future DC transformers on the DC bus and manage these resources
together with the grid supply, but also provide the various load profiles required by different loads
on the MV ac bus. The DER and DESS on the DC bus also must be grid- dispatchable based on a
real-time control signal from the utility up to 20% of the rated power. Any largescale
manufacturing facility will have high inertia loads such as large rated MV motor drives which will
go through regeneration periods during different load profiles and cycles. It is an objective of the
MV PCS to capture and manage this regenerative energy into the DC bus DESS and as a controlled
grid-dispatchable energy output.

The control algorithms for MV PCS as a grid-dispatchable manufacturing asset with integrated
load, generation and energy storage systems with different load profiles will be developed and
validated on a real-time OPAL-RT system which will be used to validate the developed control
system and to demonstrate and deliver to the DOE. The following use case scenarios will be
addressed:
 1. Operational data from a large manufacturing facility was obtained to develop
 several MV PCS architectures with both regulated DC and AC outputs with
 interfaced DER, DESS and loads. Requirements for control and control mode
 changes will be developed from these MV PCS architectures. DESS in the form of
 DC link capacitor bank or future DC transformer with battery banks.
 2. The MV PCS to be controlled as an “Intelligent Energy Management” (IEM) system
 to coordinate and control DER and DESS connected to the DC bus with MV AC
 bus loads and the grid power flow. The basic power flow control of MV PCS was
 developed and demonstrated as an “Intelligent Power Management” (IPM) system.
 The primary objective is to provide “grid support services” as required by grid
 interconnection standard IEEE 1547-4. This use case will demonstrate that the MV
 PCS can function as a monitoring controller for the entire manufacturing facility.

 10

 3. Develop and validate the control mode changes for different power flows between
 the grid, DC bus connected DER, DESS and the MV AC loads with regeneration
 power.
 4. Develop and validate different objective functions (which can be both economic and
 control objectives) – such as to provide a constant power demand (MW) from the
 utility, as reduced rates for contracted real power (MW) supply with zero or minimal
 reactive power (VAR) supply. This is to provide power factor compensation via the
 proposed PCS.
 5. Investigated and validated response time (required number of cycles) and maximum
 power ramp-up rates for grid-dispatchability and its impact on the design and rating
 of DER and DESS on DC bus and required DC bus energy storage.
 6. Developed controls and demonstrated the utility of the bidirectional MV PCS to
 utilize the large regenerative energy of MV motor drives to the grid and to the DC
 bus connected DESS, to provide on-demand, grid dispatchable energy to ride-thru
 distribution disturbances defined in the targets below.

3. PCS Development and Results
A key part of this project is the controller which monitors and manages the power exchanges
between the utility grid, the PCS, and the manufacturing facility. The PCS must be capable of
sustaining its load side rated power to the manufacturing facility at the same time that it dispatches
energy to the grid.

3.1. Energy Dispatch
Identify electrical power systems – EPS – with multiple distributed resources and/or energy storage
systems according to IEEE-1547 and establish a griddispatch power base line.

Identified multi-megawatt industrial applications with large inertia and a degree of operational
freedom to allow the energy stored in that inertia to be injected back to the distribution network
using the proposed converter; demonstrate the grid dispatch capability of combining the inertial
load and DC link capacitance energy storage; and design a 10MVA SiC Power Conditioning
System (PCS) with 13.8kV, 3-phase, bidirectional AC and a 22kV DC bus to interface with
Distributed Energy Resources (DER) and distributed energy storage. A grid services study will
identify state-of-the-art manufacturing facilities that are utilizing DERs along with medium
voltage utility power to establish the baseline for showing the achievement of grid dispatch power
performance targets.

3.3.1. Grid Model Survey

CIGRE North America Benchmark Distribution Model (Medium Voltage)
The medium voltage (MV) distribution network benchmark is derived from a physical MV
network in southern Germany, which supplies a small town and the surrounding rural area.
Compared with this original network, the number of nodes for the benchmark network was reduced
to enhance user friendliness and flexibility while fully maintaining the realistic character of the
network. The benchmark network in this section is representative of physical MV networks typical
of North America.

North American Configuration
Structure: North American MV distribution feeders are three-phase and either of meshed or radial
structure, with the latter dominating rural installations. The benchmark allows flexibility to model
both meshed and radial structures. Each feeder includes numerous laterals at which MV/LV
transformers would be connected. In North America, radial structures are prevalent, and single-
phase MV lines are included as subnetworks off the three-phase main lines. The nominal voltage
on the three-phase sections is 12.47 kV, and on the single-phase sections the line-to-neutral voltage
is 7.2 kV. The system frequency is 60 Hz.
Symmetry: Due to the existence of single-phase laterals, the North American MV network
configuration is inherently unbalanced. Although effort to balance the loading is made, a balanced
three-phase network should not be assumed, particularly for voltage drop calculations.
 24

Line types: Overhead lines are used with bare conductors made of aluminum with or without
steel reinforcement, i.e., AAC and ACSR.
Grounding: The grounding of the MV network largely depends on regional preferences. The
majority of North American networks are solidly grounded.

3.3.9. Ride-through of consecutive disturbances
 • This standard allows an unlimited number of multiple consecutive voltage disturbances
 that DER are required to ride through within a single disturbance “set,” as long as the
 cumulative duration of all the disturbances in this set does not exceed the maximum
 required ride-through duration for the respective voltage disturbance severity (and
 performance category).
 • The standard also requires that DER ride through several of these sets occurring
 consecutively within a certain time frame. Each voltage disturbance set may correlate to
 voltage disturbances during a reclosing for a particular sustained fault, intermittent fault
 activity, or oscillatory voltage triggered by response and recovery of directly coupled
 (synchronous or induction) generators to a fault.
 • The several distinct sets may correlate to several unrelated faults occurring within a short
 time frame or subsequent reclosing event where the reclosing delay is greater than the
 time separating sets, as would be typical for transmission system faults.
 • The specification for the consecutive voltage disturbance ride-through requirements were

3.3.12. Cyber Security Requirements
 • The security requirements associated with DER entail both local physical access and
 remote network access. The level of security required is proportional to the risk
 associated with a breach of the system and potential impact on other parts of the system.
 • The networks used to connect DER to remote managing entities, and the cyber security
 requirements of these networks, are out of scope of this standard. DER integration
 networks will likely be of many kinds and technologies and will use a variety of cyber
 security mechanisms that evolve over the life of the DER. For this reason, a modular
 approach has been taken in this standard, excluding the DER network requirements from
 scope and only specifying the local DER communication interface.
 • Cyber security is a system-wide issue requiring a system-wide solution. This standard
 specifies the base functionality of a DER including the capability of exchanging specific
 information over a local DER communication interface. This standard cannot correctly
 address system level issues and should not constrain reasonable system solutions.
 • Related standards for cyber security: IEC 62351-12, IEEE Std 2030, and NISTIR 7628

 LCL resonance damping:
 The LCL filter with the given parameters has a resonance frequency of 2.5 kHz. In order to
 dampen the harmonics associated with this frequency, a passive damping method is chosen. A

 impedance at resonant point. It can be seen in the below figure, the response of LCL filter
 without damping (highlighted in blue) and the response with damping (highlighted in orange).

3.5. Efficiency Analysis
The 13.8 kV power converters, based on 10 kV SiC devices operate with voltage variations (dv/dt)
above 100 kV/us and current variations (di/dt) above 30 kA/us. These transients are conducted
through parasitic capacitance and inductance and generate electromagnetic interference (EMI).
Moreover, it is very challenging to accurately measure millivolt level in voltages above 20000 V.
Consequently, it is very challenging to obtain a precise efficiency analysis based on experimental
results. Therefore, the development team adopted an efficiency estimation workflow based on
losses extracted from a physics-based Saber Model of the 10 kV SiC dies provided by the NIST.
This model has proven to be a fair representation of the original die and the same efficiency
estimation workflow was successfully adopted in a previous DOE project conducted by this team.
As shown in the workflow below, the losses are extracted from this model in the software Saber
and to construct realistic thermal models in another software called PLECS. In the same PLECs
interface, it is developed and simulated the full PCS model with controls at nominal conditions.
The simulated nominal conditions are shown in the following table.

3.7. Lifetime Analysis
To estimate the lifetime of the PCS, the Eaton team focused on the 10 kV SiC power modules
developed in this project. These modules utilize a very similar mechanical dimensions of
Infineon’s 6.5kV Si IGBT power modules. Therefore, the lifetime analysis was conducted
considering lifetime models obtained through stress tests in the commercial 6.5 kV IGBT modules.
The lifetime models allow the calculation of number of cycles to failure for specific thermal
variations in different parts of the module, such as, wire bond, AlSiC solder and copper base solder.

5.2. 5W Optical Power Converter
During this project it was developed a 5 W Optical Power Link that provides 100% galvanically
isolated power, with negligible capacitance, to a gate driver and sensors controlling power
converters based on medium voltage SiC devices. This facilitates the electromagnetic
compatibility of ancillary circuits in medium-voltage high speed switching power converters. The
key component of the 5W Optical Power Link is the Optical Power Converter (OPC), which
converts optical power into electrical power. Under Budget Period (BP) 1, the initial efforts were
mainly focused on the high efficiency Optical Power Converter (OPC), which is essential for the
success of 5W Optical Power Link. In the meantime, the most appropriate laser for 5W operation
with the consideration of built-in redundancy was selected and all lasers needed for the entire effort
were purchased and delivered. Substantial progress has led to the prototypes successfully
demonstrating 5W output power at 70C base temperature at 15V (regulated). The Broadcom team
 95

successfully designed and manufactured a reliable 5W Optical Power Link capable of delivering
well over 5W of electrically isolated power, with an efficiency above 50 %, built-in laser
redundancy and fiber-glass optical fiber jacket for extra fire-retardant protection. In addition, the
team developed a robust fiber coupling procedure facilitating reliable and repeatable fiber-to-fiber
coupling at minimal coupling loss. These advanced achievements enable high-speed switching of
high-voltage electronic devices.

5.3. Power Over Fiber Ancillary Circuits
The power over fiber technology enables the power transmission without electrical connections.
As a result, the PoF-based system has negligible coupling capacitance and is partial discharge free
to any application voltage.

5.3.1. Gate Driver
During fast switching, the voltage variation across the device generates displacement currents in
parasitic capacitance of the power converter. The isolated power supply has considerable coupling
capacitance between the windings of its transformer. As a results, noisy currents circulate through
the power supply which results in conductive electromagnetic interference in other ancillary and
control circuits. Moreover, in applications with dc-link in the range of 20 kVdc, an optimum
isolation is required to ensure reliability and safety. Therefore, a gate-driver based on power and
data over fiber is the ideal solution for wide bandgap semiconductor devices. This solution can
eliminate the conductive EMI problem and ensure the required isolation for the system. The gate-
driver is fed by the OPC and uses optical signals to receive PWM commands and send feedback
status to the controller, as seen in Figure 108.

 96

 Figure 108: Optical power converter-based gate-driver system.

5.3.2. Voltage and Current Sensors
Voltage and current sensors for medium-voltage, medium frequency applications are greatly
benefited by the development of optical power converters. As presented in the gate-driver
structure, the advent of OPC eliminates the parasitic coupling capacitance of power supplies and
the necessity of electrical cables travelling close to the power converter. Therefore, it eliminates
the effects of coupled noise and noisy currents caused by the fast voltage and current transients of
the switching devices. Moreover, the isolation is ensured by the fiber optic systems, and the sensors
can be referenced to the MV stage. As shown in Figure 109 below, the sensors is fed by the OPC
and transmit the measured parameters – V(x), I(x) – digitally through fiber optic fibers. These
benefits allowed the development team to create accurate, high-bandwidth and high-isolation
voltage and current sensors for applications of 20kVdc and beyond, which are not available in the
market.

8.2. Learnings and Recommendations
There is no accurate feeder model for performing the various energy dispatch studies. The
existing CIGRE model of the grid with 10 MVA load is not suitable:
 ▪ Changes had to be made to make the entire system stable and SCR values had to be
 extracted and used.
 ▪ Step by step modification needs to be carried out to make the circuit suitable for the PCS
 interconnection. At each step short circuit ratio studies need to be performed and based
 on that modifications were suggested. At the final, corner cases for the source impedance
 need to be calculated.

1. The PCS system cannot be completely defined as a DER according to IEEE-1547
 ▪ Based on the definition of DER, the PCS system in the existing configuration cannot be
 called as a DER and hence all the ride-thru requirements cannot be met.

2. With the existing DC-link based energy storage, the dispatch times are relatively short (in the
 order of cycles) and hence it is not an ideal way for energy storage. One solution for this is to
 have an energy storage system based on batteries connected to the dc-link via a DC-DC
 Converter which can be charged/discharged accordingly, as shown below:

 which can be charged/discharged accordingly

In this project, several systems were developed to enable MV power conversion based on 10 kV
SiC devices. During the multiple test sessions at full voltage and power, the Eaton team discovered
severe electromagnetic interference problems related to parasitic effects, especially parasitic
capacitance as the designed inverter busbar assembly has ultra-low commutation path inductance.

The gate-drivers and sensors based on optical power conversion demonstrated to be the ultimate
solution to solve these problems with an outstanding performance compared to state-of-the-art
systems. The advancement of these components is of primordial importance for future
commercialization of ultra-compact MV power converters.

The first 20 kV partial discharge free power module was also developed during this program.
During this process, several lessons were learned, issues were solved, and some key points has a
potential for further investigation. Considering the advent of ultra-wide bandgap semiconductors,
improvements on potting materials will be required to achieve higher dielectric strength without
additional mechanical stress on the wire bonds and solder joints.
