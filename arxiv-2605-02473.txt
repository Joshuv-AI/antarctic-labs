Low-Order Conservation Law Multipliers for a Generalized Fifth-Order KP Family
Dr. Nitin Serwa

Abstract
We study local conservation law multipliers for a generalized fifth-order Kadomtsev–Petviashvili family whose one-dimensional reductions include the Lax, Sawada–Kotera, and Kaup–Kupershmidt equations. Using the direct multiplier method, we classify zeroth-order multipliers that are independent of the dependent variable within a natural polynomial subclass and construct representative conserved vectors. We then prove that every multiplier of differential order at most two is necessarily of differential order at most one. An unrestricted first-order classification is obtained when the coefficient of the cubic derivative nonlinearity is nonzero, and the same reduction is established on a generic algebraic sub-branch of the complementary case. In these regimes, all first-order multipliers reduce to the zeroth-order family. A finite list of exceptional branches remains open. The results identify the structural sources responsible for the low-order rigidity of the multiplier problem in the generic regimes treated here.



1
Introduction

The Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP) equation
[
7
]

(u_{t}+\alpha uu_{x}+\beta u_{xxx})_{x}+\sigma u_{yy}=0

is a standard two-dimensional dispersive model for weakly nonlinear waves with weak transverse variation. Its classical forms are integrable and possess infinitely many conservation laws; see, for example,
[
1
,
12
]
.

In one spatial dimension, fifth-order dispersive equations provide a natural higher-order extension of KdV-type dynamics. The classical integrable fifth-order equations—the Lax equation
[
14
]
, the Sawada–Kotera equation
[
19
]
, and the Kaup–Kupershmidt equation
[
9
,
13
]
—can be written in the form

u_{t}+c\,u_{xxx}+d\,u_{xxxxx}+e\,u\,u_{xxx}+f\,u_{x}u_{xx}+g\,u^{2}u_{x}=0,

and are distinguished by specific coefficient triples
(e,f,g)
. After normalization
d=1
, these triples are

(10,20,30),\qquad(5,5,5),\qquad(10,25,20),

respectively. In each case, the equation belongs to an integrable setting with an infinite conservation law hierarchy. A different fifth-order model is the Kawahara equation
[
10
]
,

u_{t}+\alpha uu_{x}+\beta u_{xxx}+\gamma u_{xxxxx}=0,

which retains the convective nonlinearity
uu_{x}
together with fifth-order dispersion. A two-dimensional KP-type extension of the Kawahara equation has been studied in
[
15
]
. Further analytical background for higher-order KP-type equations can be found in
[
17
,
18
,
11
]
.

The present paper considers the two-dimensional family

\left(u_{t}+c\,u_{xxx}+d\,u_{xxxxx}+e\,u\,u_{xxx}+f\,u_{x}u_{xx}+g\,u^{2}u_{x}\right)_{x}+\sigma u_{yy}=0,

(1.1)

where
u=u(x,y,t)
, the coefficients
c,d,e,f,g,\sigma
are real, and
d\sigma\neq 0
. Its
y
-independent reduction contains the Lax, Sawada–Kotera, and Kaup–Kupershmidt equations at the corresponding coefficient triples after normalization. In comparison with the Kawahara–KP equation, the present family replaces the lower-order convective term by the derivative-nonlinear structures

u\,u_{xxx},\qquad u_{x}u_{xx},\qquad u^{2}u_{x}.

This places the multiplier problem in a different coefficient geometry from that of the Kawahara–KP setting. For related conservation-law classifications in generalized KP-type equations, see also
[
5
]
.

The purpose of the paper is to study local conservation law multipliers of (
1.1
) up to second differential order by the direct multiplier method of Anco and Bluman
[
3
,
4
]
; see also
[
6
,
16
]
. After passage to a potential
u=v_{x}
, the resulting potential equation is variational only on the parameter submanifold
f=2e
. Accordingly, the direct multiplier method is the main tool for the classification, while Noether’s theorem is available only on that submanifold as an independent verification method. The paper does not address integrability of the full two-dimensional family. Its scope is the classification of low-order multipliers in the regimes treated below.

The results divide into one constructive part and three rigidity results. First, zeroth-order
u
-independent multipliers are classified within a natural polynomial subclass. In the generic regime, this yields a six-dimensional polynomial basis together with representative conserved vectors. Second, every multiplier of differential order at most two is shown to be of differential order at most one, for all parameter values. Third, the unrestricted first-order multiplier problem is classified completely when
g\neq 0
. Fourth, on the branch
g=0
, the same reduction is obtained on a generic algebraic sub-branch. The exceptional branches

e+f=0,\qquad e=f,\qquad e=2f,\qquad f=3e,\qquad 27e=14f

remain open. Thus, the paper gives a generic low-order rigidity classification up to second order, rather than a complete classification over the full parameter family.

The classification identifies three structural sources that govern the determining-system reductions: the sixth-order
x
-jet structure, the transverse term
\sigma u_{yy}
, and the cubic derivative-nonlinearity
g\,u^{2}u_{x}
. In the multiplier orders treated here, these features prevent enlargement of the surviving multiplier space beyond the zeroth-order
u
-independent basis in the generic regimes. The distinguished one-dimensional triples are not exceptional at these orders, since all three lie in the regime
g\neq 0
covered by the first-order classification.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the equation, performs the equivalence reduction, identifies the variational submanifold, and sets up the multiplier framework. Section 3 treats zeroth-order
u
-independent multipliers within the polynomial subclass. Section 4 proves the second-order reduction theorem. Section 5 carries out the unrestricted first-order classification in the generic regimes treated here. Section 6 discusses the structural interpretation of the reductions and compares the results with standard KP-type settings. Section 7 records the remaining open directions.

2
Equation, Equivalence Reduction, and Multiplier Framework

2.1
The equation

We consider the family of fifth-order KP-type equations

\left(u_{t}+c\,u_{xxx}+d\,u_{xxxxx}+e\,u\,u_{xxx}+f\,u_{x}\,u_{xx}+g\,u^{2}\,u_{x}\right)_{x}+\sigma\,u_{yy}=0,

(2.1)

where
u=u(x,y,t)
, the coefficients
c,d,e,f,g,\sigma

d\sigma\neq 0.

This condition ensures that the equation is genuinely fifth-order in the distinguished

The expanded form of (

u_{tx}+c\,u_{xxxx}+d\,u_{xxxxxx}+(e+f)\,u_{x}u_{xxx}+e\,u\,u_{xxxx}+f\,u_{xx}^{2}+2g\,u\,u_{x}^{2}+g\,u^{2}u_{xx}+\sigma\,u_{yy}=0.

(2.2)

This form is used in the Euler-operator computations throughout the paper.

The

u_{t}+c\,u_{xxx}+d\,u_{xxxxx}+e\,u\,u_{xxx}+f\,u_{x}\,u_{xx}+g\,u^{2}u_{x}=0.

(2.3)

After normalizing
d=1
, the distinguished one-dimensional triples are

(e,f,g)=(10,20,30),\qquad(5,5,5),\qquad(10,25,20),

corresponding respectively to the Lax, Sawada–Kotera, and Kaup–Kupershmidt equations

[

2.2
Equivalence reduction

We first determine the coefficient freedom that can be removed by scaling.

Proposition 2.1
.

Under the scaling transformation

x=\lambda\tilde{x},\qquad y=\mu\tilde{y},\qquad t=\nu\tilde{t},\qquad u=\rho\tilde{u},

(2.4)

with
\lambda,\mu,\nu,\rho\neq 0
, equation (
2.1
) retains its form and the coefficients transform according to

\tilde{c}=\frac{c\nu}{\lambda^{3}},\qquad\tilde{d}=\frac{d\nu}{\lambda^{5}},\qquad\tilde{e}=\frac{e\nu\rho}{\lambda^{3}},\qquad\tilde{f}=\frac{f\nu\rho}{\lambda^{3}},\qquad\tilde{g}=\frac{g\nu\rho^{2}}{\lambda},\qquad\tilde{\sigma}=\frac{\sigma\nu\lambda}{\mu^{2}}.

(2.5)

Hence, under the standing assumption
d\sigma\neq 0
, one may normalize

d=1,\qquad\sigma=\pm 1.

(2.6)

Proof.

Substituting (
2.4
) into (
2.1
), using

\partial_{x}=\lambda^{-1}\partial_{\tilde{x}},\qquad\partial_{y}=\mu^{-1}\partial_{\tilde{y}},\qquad\partial_{t}=\nu^{-1}\partial_{\tilde{t}},

and dividing the transformed equation by
\nu\lambda/\rho
yields the coefficient relations
(
2.5
). The normalization (
2.6
) follows by choosing

\nu=\frac{\lambda^{5}}{d}

to obtain
\tilde{d}=1
, and then choosing
\mu
so that

\mu^{2}=\frac{\sigma\nu\lambda}{\varepsilon},\qquad\varepsilon=\operatorname{sign}(\sigma d),

which gives
\tilde{\sigma}=\varepsilon=\pm 1
.
∎

Further normalizations are available on special parameter branches, for example when

g\neq 0
or
c\neq 0
. They are not uniform across the full family, however, and would
obscure the comparison between generic and special parameter regimes. For the multiplier
classification it is therefore preferable to retain
c,e,f
, and
g
as free parameters and
normalize only
d
and
\sigma
.

From now on we work with the normalized family

\left(u_{t}+c\,u_{xxx}+u_{xxxxx}+e\,u\,u_{xxx}+f\,u_{x}\,u_{xx}+g\,u^{2}u_{x}\right)_{x}+\sigma u_{yy}=0,\qquad\sigma=\pm 1.

(2.7)

2.3
Potential form and variational submanifold

To identify the parameter regime on which Noether’s theorem is available, we introduce a
potential

u=v_{x}.

Integrating (
2.7
) once with respect to
x
, under the usual decay assumptions,
yields the local potential equation

v_{tx}+c\,v_{xxxx}+v_{xxxxxx}+e\,v_{x}v_{xxxx}+f\,v_{xx}v_{xxx}+g\,v_{x}^{2}v_{xx}+\sigma v_{yy}=0.

(2.8)

Proposition 2.2
.

The potential equation (
2.8
) is an Euler–Lagrange equation if and only if

f=2e.

Proof.

The only nontrivial compatibility condition comes from the pair of terms

v_{x}v_{xxxx}
and
v_{xx}v_{xxx}
. A Lagrangian term of the form

A\,v_{x}v_{xx}^{2}

contributes, under the Euler operator, the combination

2A\,v_{x}v_{xxxx}+4A\,v_{xx}v_{xxx}.

Matching this with the nonlinear part of (
2.8
) requires

2A=e,\qquad 4A=f,

and hence
f=2e
.

Conversely, when
f=2e
, the Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}=-\tfrac{1}{2}v_{t}v_{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}c\,v_{xx}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}v_{xxx}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}e\,v_{x}v_{xx}^{2}-\tfrac{g}{12}v_{x}^{4}-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma v_{y}^{2}

(2.9)

satisfies

E_{v}(\mathcal{L})=0

if and only if (
2.8
) holds.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

The condition
f=2e
is satisfied by the Lax triple, but not by the
Sawada–Kotera or Kaup–Kupershmidt triples. More precisely, the Lax triple
satisfies
f=2e
, the Sawada–Kotera triple satisfies
f=e
, and the
Kaup–Kupershmidt triple satisfies
f=5e/2
.

Remark 2.4
.

For generic parameters
f\neq 2e
, the normalized family (
2.7
) is nonvariational.
Accordingly, the direct multiplier method is the primary tool in the present paper. On the
submanifold
f=2e
, Noether’s theorem provides an independent verification method.

2.4
Multiplier framework

A local conservation law of (
2.7
) is a continuity equation

D_{t}T+D_{x}X+D_{y}Y=0

(2.10)

holding for all solutions
u(x,y,t)
, where
T
is the conserved density and
(X,Y)
is the
flux; see
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
16
]
.

Let
\Delta
denote the left-hand side of the expanded normalized equation:

\Delta=u_{tx}+c\,u_{xxxx}+u_{xxxxxx}+(e+f)\,u_{x}u_{xxx}+e\,u\,u_{xxxx}+f\,u_{xx}^{2}+2g\,u\,u_{x}^{2}+g\,u^{2}u_{xx}+\sigma\,u_{yy}.

(2.11)

The equation
\Delta=0
is solved for the mixed derivative
u_{tx}
. Accordingly, the
normal jet variables are all partial derivatives of
u
except
u_{tx}
and its differential
consequences. All multiplier ansatz classes and all splittings of the determining system in
this paper are formulated in terms of these normal jet variables.

A function
Q
of the independent variables and normal jet variables is called a multiplier if

Q\,\Delta=D_{t}T+D_{x}X+D_{y}Y

(2.12)

holds as an identity for all functions
u(x,y,t)
. Multipliers are characterized by the Euler
condition

E_{u}(Q\,\Delta)=0,

(2.13)

where
E_{u}
denotes the Euler operator with respect to
u

[
3
,
4
]
. For every admitted multiplier, a representative
conserved vector is reconstructed from (
2.12
) by integration by parts.

2.5
Scope

Equation (
2.7
), together with the normal-jet multiplier framework above, defines
the analytical setting for the remainder of the paper.

Section 3 studies zeroth-order multipliers of the form
Q=Q(x,y,t)
. In the generic regime,
the resulting determining system reduces to

Q_{xx}=0,\qquad Q_{xt}+\sigma Q_{yy}=0.

Within the polynomial subclass, this yields a six-dimensional basis. On the branch

g=0
,
f=3e
, the zeroth-order determining system enlarges; that branch is recorded but
not analyzed further in the present paper.

Section 4 proves that every multiplier of differential order at most two is in fact of
differential order at most one, for all parameter values. Section 5 then classifies the
unrestricted first-order multiplier problem in the generic regimes treated here. The
classification is complete for
g\neq 0
, and the same reduction is obtained on a generic
algebraic sub-branch of
g=0
. The exceptional
g=0
branches are deferred and listed
explicitly in Section 5.

Section 6 interprets the reductions through three structural sources: the sixth-order

x
-jet structure, the transverse term
\sigma u_{yy}
, and the cubic derivative-nonlinearity

g\,u^{2}u_{x}
. Section 7 records the remaining open directions.

3
Zeroth-Order
u
-Independent Multipliers

In this section we classify local multipliers of the form

Q=Q(x,y,t),

depending only on the independent variables. The classification is unrestricted at this stage with respect to the independent variables, but the final basis result will be stated only within the polynomial subclass.

3.1
Determining equations

Substituting the ansatz
Q=Q(x,y,t)
into the Euler condition

E_{u}(Q\,\Delta)=0,

where
\Delta
is given by (
2.11
), and splitting with respect to the jet variables yields the relations

g\,Q_{xx}=0,\qquad(3e-f)\,Q_{xx}=0,\qquad(3e-f)\,Q_{xxx}=0,\qquad e\,Q_{xxxx}=0,

together with

Q_{xt}+c\,Q_{xxxx}+Q_{xxxxxx}+\sigma Q_{yy}=0.

(3.1)

Hence, whenever

g\neq 0\qquad\text{or}\qquad f\neq 3e,

the first two relations force

Q_{xx}=0,

and (
3.1
) reduces to

Q_{xx}=0,\qquad Q_{xt}+\sigma Q_{yy}=0.

(3.2)

Thus, in the generic regime, the zeroth-order
u
-independent multiplier problem is governed entirely by the linear system (
3.2
).

On the branch

g=0,\qquad f=3e,

the condition
Q_{xx}=0
is no longer forced. In this case the determining system enlarges, with
Q_{xxxx}=0
replacing the generic second-
x
-derivative condition. This branch is recorded for later reference but is not studied further in the present paper.

3.2
General solution and polynomial subclass

The general solution of (
3.2
) is

Q(x,y,t)=x\bigl(A_{0}(t)+A_{1}(t)y\bigr)+B_{0}(t)+B_{1}(t)y-\frac{A_{0}^{\prime}(t)y^{2}}{2\sigma}-\frac{A_{1}^{\prime}(t)y^{3}}{6\sigma},

(3.3)

where
A_{0}
,
A_{1}
,
B_{0}
, and
B_{1}
are arbitrary functions of
t
. Accordingly, the full solution space of (
3.2
) is infinite-dimensional.

For the purposes of the present paper, we restrict attention to the polynomial subclass in which
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
are affine functions of
t
and
B_{0}
,
B_{1}
are constant. This yields a six-dimensional space with basis

Q_{0}=1,\qquad Q_{1}=y,\qquad Q_{2}=x,\qquad Q_{3}=xy,\qquad Q_{4}=xt-\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma},\qquad Q_{5}=xyt-\frac{y^{3}}{6\sigma}.

(3.4)

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
g\neq 0
or
f\neq 3e
. Within the polynomial subclass of solutions of (
3.2
), the space of zeroth-order
u
-independent local multipliers for the normalized family (
2.7
) is six-dimensional and is spanned by

1,\qquad y,\qquad x,\qquad xy,\qquad xt-\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma},\qquad xyt-\frac{y^{3}}{6\sigma}.

Proof.

Under the stated parameter assumptions, the determining system reduces to (
3.2
). Solving that system gives (
3.3
). Restricting to the polynomial subclass described above yields the basis (
3.4
). This basis is linearly independent, and every multiplier in the subclass is a linear combination of its elements.
∎

3.3
Representative conserved vectors

To write the conserved vectors compactly, define

F=c\,u_{xxx}+u_{xxxxx}+e\,u\,u_{xxx}+f\,u_{x}u_{xx}+g\,u^{2}u_{x},

(3.5)

so that the normalized equation (
2.7
) takes the form

D_{t}(u_{x})+D_{x}(F)+D_{y}(\sigma u_{y})=0.

(3.6)

Also define

J=-c\,u_{xx}-u_{xxxx}-e\,u\,u_{xx}+\frac{e-f}{2}\,u_{x}^{2}-\frac{g}{3}\,u^{3}.

(3.7)

Then

D_{x}J=-F.

(3.8)

The next result gives a representative conserved vector for every zeroth-order multiplier satisfying (
3.2
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
Q=Q(x,y,t)
satisfy (
3.2
). Then

D_{t}T+D_{x}X+D_{y}Y=Q\,\Delta

(3.9)

holds with

T=Q\,u_{x},\qquad X=Q\,F+Q_{x}\,J-Q_{t}\,u,\qquad Y=\sigma Q\,u_{y}-\sigma Q_{y}\,u.

(3.10)

Hence
(T,X,Y)
is a representative conserved vector for the multiplier
Q
.

Proof.

Starting from (
3.6
), multiplication by
Q
gives

Q\,\Delta=Q\,D_{t}(u_{x})+Q\,D_{x}(F)+Q\,D_{y}(\sigma u_{y}).

Integrating by parts with respect to
t
,
x
, and
y
yields

Q\,\Delta=D_{t}(Q\,u_{x})+D_{x}(Q\,F)+D_{y}(\sigma Q\,u_{y})-Q_{t}u_{x}-Q_{x}F-\sigma Q_{y}u_{y}.

Using (
3.8
), the identity

-Q_{x}F=D_{x}(Q_{x}J)-Q_{xx}J,

and then integrating the remaining two terms by parts gives

-Q_{t}u_{x}=-D_{x}(Q_{t}u)+Q_{tx}u,\qquad-\sigma Q_{y}u_{y}=-D_{y}(\sigma Q_{y}u)+\sigma Q_{yy}u.

Since
Q
satisfies (
3.2
), we have
Q_{xx}=0
and

Q_{tx}+\sigma Q_{yy}=0.

Substituting these relations shows that all non-divergence terms cancel, and (
3.9
) follows with (
3.10
).
∎

4
Second-Order Reduction Theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

For the normalized family (
2.7
), every local multiplier of differential
order at most two is of differential order at most one. That is, if

Q=Q(x,y,t,u,u_{x},u_{y},u_{t},u_{xx},u_{xy},u_{yy},u_{yt},u_{tt}),

then

Q_{u_{xx}}=Q_{u_{xy}}=Q_{u_{yy}}=Q_{u_{yt}}=Q_{u_{tt}}=0.

This holds for all values of the parameters
c
,
e
,
f
, and
g
.

Proof.

Let
J
be any second-order normal jet index, so that

u_{J}\in\{u_{xx},u_{xy},u_{yy},u_{yt},u_{tt}\}.

Consider the jet monomial

u_{J+(6,0,0)}

in the determining equation

E_{u}(Q\Delta)=0,

where
\Delta
is given by (
2.11
). Since
|J|=2
, this monomial has total order

|J|+6=8.

The first contribution to its coefficient comes from the Euler-operator term

(-1)^{|J|}D_{J}\!\left(\frac{\partial(Q\Delta)}{\partial u_{J}}\right).

Because the leading term
u_{xxxxxx}
appears in
\Delta
with coefficient
1
, the quantity

\frac{\partial(Q\Delta)}{\partial u_{J}}

contains the term

Q_{u_{J}}\,u_{xxxxxx}.

Applying
D_{J}
produces

Q_{u_{J}}\,u_{J+(6,0,0)}

as the leading contribution. Since
|J|=2
, the sign is

(-1)^{|J|}=(-1)^{2}=+1.

The second contribution comes from the Euler-operator term

(-1)^{6}D_{x}^{6}\!\left(\frac{\partial(Q\Delta)}{\partial u_{xxxxxx}}\right).

Because

\frac{\partial(Q\Delta)}{\partial u_{xxxxxx}}=Q,

and
Q
depends on the second-order jet
u_{J}
, the leading part of
D_{x}^{6}(Q)
contains

Q_{u_{J}}\,u_{J+(6,0,0)}.

Since
6
is even, this contribution also carries sign

(-1)^{6}=+1.

No other Euler-operator term can produce the jet
u_{J+(6,0,0)}
. Indeed, apart from

u_{xxxxxx}
, every jet appearing in
\Delta
has
x
-order at most
4
. If
D_{K}
is applied
to a function depending on jets of order at most
2
, then the resulting
x
-order is at most

4+2=6<8.

Hence there is no competing contribution to the coefficient of
u_{J+(6,0,0)}
.

Therefore the coefficient of
u_{J+(6,0,0)}
in
E_{u}(Q\Delta)
is

2\,Q_{u_{J}}.

Since the determining equation holds as an identity, this coefficient must vanish. Thus

Q_{u_{J}}=0.

As
J
was arbitrary among the five second-order normal jets, it follows that

Q_{u_{xx}}=Q_{u_{xy}}=Q_{u_{yy}}=Q_{u_{yt}}=Q_{u_{tt}}=0.

This proves the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The argument above is specific to second-order jets. If
|J|=1
, then the two leading
contributions carry opposite signs, namely
(-1)^{1}=-1
and
(-1)^{6}=+1
, and they cancel
rather than reinforce. For this reason, the first-order problem requires the full
determining-system analysis carried out in Section 5.

5
Unrestricted First-Order Classification

By Theorem
4.1
, every multiplier of the normalized family (
2.7
)
of differential order at most two is of differential order at most one. The classification
problem therefore reduces to determining all multipliers of the form

Q=Q(x,y,t,u,u_{x},u_{y},u_{t}),

where the arguments are the independent variables and the normal first-order jets.
No affine or polynomial restriction is imposed on
Q
. The determining equation is

E_{u}(Q\Delta)=0,

where
\Delta
is given by (
2.11
); see

[
3
,
4
]
. Before splitting, the solved derivative

u_{tx}
and its differential consequences are expressed in terms of normal jets.

5.1
Linearity in the derivative arguments

Lemma 5.1
.

For the normalized family (

6
Structural Interpretation and Comparison

6.1
Structural sources of the low-order rigidity

The reductions established in Sections 4 and 5 are governed by three distinct structural sources.

The first is the sixth-order
x
-jet structure. The leading term
u_{xxxxxx}
in
(
2.11
), with coefficient
1
, is responsible for the second-order reduction of
Theorem
4.1
. In the Euler condition, the two contributions to the target jet

u_{J+(6,0,0)}
carry the same sign when
|J|=2
, and hence force the vanishing of every
second-order derivative dependence. At first order, the same sixth-order structure enters
again in Lemma
5.1
, where the coefficients of the highest
x
-jets force the
multiplier to be linear in the derivative arguments.

The second is the transverse term
\sigma u_{yy}
. On the branch
g=0
, this term is the
first source that forces reduction in the determining system. In Proposition
5.4
,
it yields the vanishing of
a_{2}
, the relation
K_{y}=0
, and the identity
B_{x}=4a_{1}
.
Thus the transverse term remains active even when the cubic derivative-nonlinearity is absent.

The third is the cubic derivative-nonlinearity
g\,u^{2}u_{x}
. In the regime
g\neq 0
,
this term is responsible for eliminating the
u
-dependent part of the multiplier, the

u_{x}
-dependence, and the
u_{y}
- and
u_{t}
-dependence in Theorem
5.3
. It also
forces the condition
a_{0,xx}=0
. This source is absent on the branch
g=0
, and the
determining system then proceeds instead through the algebraic factors

e+f,\qquad 27e-14f,\qquad e-2f,\qquad e-f,\qquad 3e-f,

each of which produces a deferred exceptional sub-branch when it vanishes.

These three sources remain distinct in the generic regime. The sixth-order
x
-jet
structure acts for all parameter values, the transverse term acts whenever
\sigma\neq 0
,
and the cubic derivative-nonlinearity acts whenever
g\neq 0
. Their combined effect is
that, in the multiplier orders treated here, no enlargement of the surviving multiplier
space occurs beyond the zeroth-order system (
5.3
) in the generic regimes.

6.2
Comparison with simpler KP-type settings

The most natural comparators are the standard KP equation
[
7
]
,
the generalized KP-type equations studied in
[
5
]
, and the
Kawahara–KP equation studied in
[
15
]
. Both share
the KP-type transverse structure and an
x
-direction dispersive architecture with the
present family, but neither contains the derivative-nonlinear terms

u\,u_{xxx},\qquad u_{x}u_{xx},\qquad u^{2}u_{x}

that distinguish (
2.7
).

At the level of the zeroth-order problem, the determining system in the generic regime is

Q_{xx}=0,\qquad Q_{xt}+\sigma Q_{yy}=0,

which depends only on the linear KP-type differential structure. For this reason, the same
low-order polynomial pattern appears within the corresponding polynomial subclass in several
KP-type models. In the present family, however, the derivative-nonlinear terms do not
produce additional low-order multipliers. Instead, when
g\neq 0
, they provide an
additional obstruction through the term
g\,u^{2}u_{x}
.

There is also a methodological difference. The potential form of the Kawahara–KP equation
is variational, so conservation laws can be obtained there through Noether’s theorem after
introducing a potential
[
15
]
. By contrast, the present
family is variational only on the submanifold
f=2e
; outside that submanifold, the direct
multiplier method is required. The reductions in Section 5 therefore belong to the generic
nonvariational regime of the family.

6.3
Distinguished one-dimensional triples in the two-dimensional setting

The one-dimensional reduction contains the distinguished coefficient triples

(10,20,30),\qquad(5,5,5),\qquad(10,25,20),

corresponding respectively to the Lax, Sawada–Kotera, and Kaup–Kupershmidt equations

[
14
,
19
,
9
,
13
]
. A natural question is whether
these triples produce exceptional low-order multiplier structure after passage to the
two-dimensional KP-type setting.

At the multiplier orders treated here, they do not. All three triples satisfy
g\neq 0
and
are therefore covered directly by Theorem
5.3
. No separate treatment is needed,
and no exceptional enlargement of the multiplier space occurs.

The reason is visible in the determining-system cascade. In the two-dimensional problem, the
transverse term
\sigma u_{yy}
and the sixth-order
x
-jet structure enter before
coefficient-sensitive relations associated with the one-dimensional integrable triples become
relevant. Thus the distinguished one-dimensional coefficient geometry does not produce
separate behavior at the multiplier orders classified in the present paper.

6.4
Interpretation of the surviving basis

In the generic regimes treated here, every first-order multiplier reduces to a function

a_{0}(x,y,t)
satisfying (
5.3
). Within the polynomial subclass, this recovers the
six-dimensional basis obtained in Section 3:

1,\qquad y,\qquad x,\qquad xy,\qquad xt-\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma},\qquad xyt-\frac{y^{3}}{6\sigma}.

This basis is determined by the zeroth-order system (
5.3
) and is independent of
the nonlinear coefficients
c
,
e
,
f
, and
g
. The role of the nonlinear terms in the
generic low-order problem is therefore not to enlarge the multiplier space, but to restrict
it. The condition
f=2e
does not alter the surviving basis itself; it enters only through
the variational formulation discussed in Section 2.

7
Open Directions

The first open problem concerns the five algebraic relations excluded by the
nondegeneracy conditions in Proposition
5.4
:

e+f=0,\qquad e=f,\qquad e=2f,\qquad f=3e,\qquad 27e=14f.

On each of these sub-branches of
g=0
, one or more steps in the proof of
Proposition
5.4
do not apply, and the first-order multiplier space
may be larger. Among them, the branch

g=0,\qquad f=3e

is the most immediate to isolate. On this branch, the zeroth-order

u
-independent determining system already enlarges, since the condition

Q_{xx}=0
is replaced by
Q_{xxxx}=0
in Section 3. Whether the full
first-order multiplier space also enlarges on this branch remains open.
The remaining four relations arise from the algebraic factors appearing in the
determining-system cascade of Proposition
5.4
, and they should be
checked for completeness.

A second question is whether the rigidity established here persists at higher
multiplier order. The present paper classifies multipliers up to second order,
and Theorem
4.1
depends on the parity structure specific to
second-order jets. In that argument, the two contributions to the target
order-
8
jet carry the same sign and therefore reinforce. For third-order jets,
the corresponding signs are opposite, so the second-order reduction argument
does not extend directly. A classification at third order would therefore
require a separate determining-system analysis.

A related question concerns the difference between the one-dimensional reduction
and the two-dimensional KP-type setting. In one spatial dimension, the distinguished triples associated with the Lax,Sawada–Kotera, and Kaup–Kupershmidt equations produce infinite conservation law hierarchies
[
14
,
19
,
9
,
13
]
,
while fifth-order solitary-wave behavior and transverse effects in related
settings have also been studied for Kawahara-type models
[
8
]
. In the present
two-dimensional problem, that parameter sensitivity does not appear at the
multiplier orders treated here, because the transverse term
\sigma u_{yy}
and
the sixth-order
x
-jet structure enter the determining-system cascade before
the coefficient-sensitive factors become relevant. This leads to a natural open
question: does the parameter sensitivity visible in the one-dimensional
reduction reappear at higher multiplier order in the two-dimensional setting,
or is it suppressed by the transverse structure throughout the KP-type class?

A further direction is to ask whether the same fifth-order nonlinear family has
a different low-order multiplier picture under a different two-dimensional
extension. In the present paper, the transverse term is
\sigma u_{yy}
. A
different extension, for example of Zakharov–Kuznetsov type with a mixed
transverse dispersive term such as
\sigma u_{xyy}
, would alter the jet
structure that enters the multiplier determining equations. It is therefore
reasonable to ask whether such an extension admits a different low-order
multiplier classification. For conservation-law calculations in ZK-type
equations with higher-order dispersion, see
[
2
]
.

The results of the present paper determine the low-order multiplier structure
of the generalized fifth-order KP family up to second order in the generic
regimes treated here. The deferred exceptional sub-branches and the higher-order
multiplier problem remain the next natural steps.
