On 𝝅 {\boldsymbol{\pi}} -systems of symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebras Thanks: KNR and SV acknowledge support from DAE under a XII plan project. KR is partially supported by ISF grant no. 1221/17
K. N. Raghavan, Krishanu Roy, Sankaran Viswanath

Abstract
. Given a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , we study its π \pi -systems, which are subsets of real roots, the pairwise differences of whose elements are not roots. Such systems arise as simple systems of regular subalgebras of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , and were originally studied by Dynkin, Morita and Naito. We show that the binary relation introduced by Morita defines a partial order on the set of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} of finite, untwisted affine or hyperbolic type. We also formulate general principles for constructing π \pi -systems as well as for finding forbidden diagrams that cannot occur as Dynkin diagrams of π \pi -systems of a given 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . Among other applications, we use this to determine the set of maximal hyperbolic Dynkin diagrams in ranks 3 3 - 10 10 relative to the Morita partial order.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(A)
be the Kac-Moody algebra associated with a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix (GCM)
A
. Let
\Delta
be its set of roots and
\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}
be the set of real roots.
A
\pi
-system of
\mathfrak{g}
is a subset
\Sigma\subset\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}
satisfying the property that
\alpha-\beta\not\in\Delta
for all
\alpha\neq\beta\in\Sigma
.

These were first studied by Dynkin for finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
and later by Morita
[
9
]
and Naito
[
10
]
in general (see also
[
4
]
). Our previous work
[
2
]
studied the Weyl group action on
\pi
-systems and showed that the number of orbits is finite in various cases of interest.

In this paper, we study the binary relation
\preceq
on the set
\mathbb{G}
of symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrices (identifying two matrices which differ only by a simultaneous reordering of rows and columns). This was first introduced by Morita: given
A,B\in\mathbb{G}
, we say
B\preceq A
if there is a linearly independent
\pi
-system of type
B
in the Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}(A)
(see §
2
for definitions). We establish that
\preceq
defines a partial order on the set of GCMs of finite, untwisted affine or hyperbolic type (Proposition
4.1
), and conjecture that this holds on all of
\mathbb{G}
.

We prove a very general theorem (Theorem
5.3
), from which we deduce necessary conditions for a pair
(A,B)
to satisfy
B\preceq A
under various hypotheses on
A
. This in turn allows us to find
forbidden diagrams
of
\pi
-systems, i.e., those which cannot occur as subdiagrams of Dynkin diagrams of
\pi
-systems of
A
. We give many examples and applications of this in §
5
.

We also prove some general principles for explicit construction of
\pi
-systems; these may be viewed as generalizations of principles obtained in
[
14
]
for simply-laced diagrams. As an application of these constructions and the forbidden diagram analysis, we show how to find the list of all maximal Dynkin diagrams in the set of all hyperbolic Dynkin diagrams. An approach to the poset of hyperbolic type GCMs under
\preceq
is contained in
[
6
,
13
]
, but proceeds via the associated Weyl groups.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall the relevant notation from §2 of
[
2
]
.

2.1.

An integer matrix
A=(a_{ij})
of size
n\times n
, where

is a positive integer, is called a
generalized Cartan matrix
,
GCM
for short, if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

a_{ii}=2
for all
1\leq i\leq n

(2)

a_{ij}\leq 0
whenever
1\leq i,j\leq n,i\neq j

(3)

a_{ij}=0
if
a_{ji}=0
for
1\leq i,j\leq n

Given a GCM
A
of size
n
, we let
\mathfrak{g}(A)
denote the
Kac-Moody Lie algebra
associated to
A

[
8
, §1.3]
, with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}(A)
and Chevalley generators
e_{i},f_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\alpha_{i}(A),1\leq i\leq n
denote the simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}(A)
and let
Q(A)
be its root lattice. We use terminology and notation as in the early chapters of
[
8
]
without any further comment.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a GCM.
A
\pi
-system
in
A
is a finite collection of distinct real roots
\{\beta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
of
\mathfrak{g}(A)
such that
\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}
is not a root for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq m
.

Let
A
be a GCM, and
\Sigma=\{\beta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a
\pi
-system in
A
.
Then the matrix

M(\Sigma):=\left[\langle\,\beta_{i}^{\vee},\beta_{j}\,\rangle\right]_{i,j=1}^{m}

is a GCM. Here, we let
\alpha^{\vee}
denote the coroot corresponding to
\alpha
when
\alpha
is a real root of
\mathfrak{g}(A)
.

Definition 2.2
.

We call
B:=M(\Sigma)
the
type
of
\Sigma
, and refer to
\Sigma
as a
\pi
-system of type
B
in
A
.

2.2.
Symmetrizable GCMs and
\pi
-systems

An
n\times n
GCM
A
is
symmetrizable
if there exists a diagonal
n\times n
matrix
D
with positive rational diagonal entries such that
DA
is symmetric. Let
\Sigma=\{\beta_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
be a
\pi
-system of type
B
in
A
. We note that if
A
is a symmetrizable GCM, then so is
B
. Fix a choice of diagonal matrix
D
which symmetrizes
A
, and let
\left(\cdot\mid\cdot\right)
denote the corresponding symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}(A)

[
8
, §2.1]
, such that:

(2.1)

\left(\alpha_{i}^{\vee}(A)\mid\alpha_{j}^{\vee}(A)\right)=\,a_{ij}/D_{jj}

Since the bilinear form
\left(\cdot\mid\cdot\right)
is non-degenerate on
\mathfrak{h}(A)
, we have an isomorphism
\nu:\mathfrak{h}(A)\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{*}(A)
defined by

(2.2)

\nu(h)(h_{1})=\left(h\mid h_{1}\right),

for
h,h_{1}\in\mathfrak{h}(A)

and the induced bilinear form
\left(\cdot\mid\cdot\right)
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}(A)
such that

(2.3)

\left(\alpha_{i}(A)\mid\alpha_{j}(A)\right)=D_{ii}\,a_{ij}

Since the
\beta_{i}
are real roots of
\mathfrak{g}(A)
, we know by
[
8
, Chapter 5]
that:

b_{ij}=\langle\beta_{i}^{\vee},\beta_{j}\rangle=\frac{2\left(\beta_{i}\mid\beta_{j}\right)}{\left(\beta_{i}\mid\beta_{i}\right)}

Thus,
D^{\prime}=\mathrm{diag}(\left(\beta_{i}\mid\beta_{i}\right)/2)
is a diagonal matrix with positive rational entries that symmetrizes
B
. This choice of symmetrization defines a symmetric bilinear form on
Q(B)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. As in equation (
2.3
) above, this is given by
\left(\alpha_{i}(B)\mid\alpha_{j}(B)\right)=D^{\prime}_{ii}\,b_{ij}=\left(\beta_{i}\mid\beta_{j}\right)
. In other words, given the compatible choices of symmetrizations
(D,D^{\prime})
as above, the
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

(2.4)

Q(B)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\to Q(A)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C},\;\;\;\;\alpha_{i}(B)\mapsto\beta_{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq m

is form preserving.

3.
The relation
\preceq

Consider the set
\mathbb{G}
of symmetrizable GCMs (of all sizes). We identify two such GCMs if they are equal upto a simultaneous permutation of rows and columns.

For
A,B\in\mathbb{G}
, we define the relation
B\preceq A
if there is a
linearly independent

\pi
-system of type
B
in
A
. Clearly this relation is reflexive. By corollary 2.14 in
[
2
]
this relation is transitive. We however do not know if this relation is anti-symmetric.

3.1.

In this section, we derive some key properties of
\preceq
. Later, in §
4
, we use these to establish its anti-symmetry in many important cases, in particular, when one of the matrices is of finite, untwisted affine or hyperbolic type.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
symmetrizable GCM. Let
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}(A)
and
\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
be the corresponding coroots. Let
\{\beta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset Q(A)
and
\{\gamma^{\vee}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset Q^{\vee}(A)
be any
n
-element subsets of the root and coroot lattices respectively. Consider the integer matrix:

B=\left[\langle\,\gamma^{\vee}_{i},\beta_{j}\,\rangle\right]_{ij}
.
Then:

(1)

\det A
divides
\det B
(in
\mathbb{Z}
).

(2)

Further if
A,B
are invertible with
|\det A|=|\det B|
, then
\{\beta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{\gamma_{i}^{\vee}\}_{i=1}^{n}
form
\mathbb{Z}
-bases of
Q(A)
and
Q^{\vee}(A)
respectively.

Proof:
We write:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\gamma^{\vee}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}u_{ik}\,\alpha^{\vee}_{k}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\beta_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}v_{j\ell}\,\alpha_{\ell}

where
u_{ik},v_{j\ell}
are integers. Using the equations above, we compute:

B=UAV^{T}

where
U=\left[u_{ij}\right]
and
V=\left[v_{ij}\right]
are integer matrices. Taking determinants, we obtain
\det B=\det U\det V\det A
, proving the first assertion. For the second assertion, the given condition implies
|\det U|=|\det V|=1
, i.e.,
U
and
V
are in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. This is clearly equivalent to what needs to be shown.

\Box

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{G}
have sizes
n,m
respectively and let
B\preceq A
. Then:

(1)

m\leq n
.

(2)

If
m=n
, then
\det B=k\det A
for some integer
k\geq 1
.

(3)

If
m=n
, then the symmetrizations of
A
and
B
have the same signature.

Proof.

Let
S=\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
denote the set of simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}(A)
.
The hypothesis implies that there is a linearly independent
\pi
-system
\Sigma
of type
B
in
A
. The linear independence of
\Sigma
clearly implies that
m\leq n
. Now if
m=n
, let
\Sigma=\{\beta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. In Lemma
3.1
, we set
\gamma^{\vee}_{i}:=\beta^{\vee}_{i}=2\nu^{-1}(\beta_{i})/|\beta_{i}|^{2}
(i.e, the corresponding coroots). The linear independence of
\Sigma
ensures that the matrices
U,V
of Lemma
3.1
are invertible over
\mathbb{R}
. Further, from equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we conclude that
v_{ik}=u_{ik}|\beta_{i}|^{2}/|\alpha_{k}|^{2}
[
8
, Eq. (5.1.1)]
. Thus the matrices
U,V
are related by
V=D_{1}UD_{2}
where
D_{1},D_{2}
are the diagonal matrices with entries
|\beta_{i}|^{2}
and
|\alpha_{k}|^{2}
respectively. Since these entries are positive rationals, we conclude that
\det V=p\det U
for some positive rational
p
, and thus
\det B=k\det A
where
k=p(\det U)^{2}>0
. Since
k
is known to be an integer from Lemma
3.1
, this proves the second assertion. For the third assertion, we observe that the signature of the form
\left(\cdot\mid\cdot\right)
on the
\mathbb{R}\Sigma
(resp.
\mathbb{R}S
) is exactly the signature of the symmetrization of
B
(resp.
A
). But since
m=n
, we have
\mathbb{R}\Sigma=\mathbb{R}S
.
∎

Corollary 3.3

Let

4.
Finite, Affine and Hyperbolic

4.1.

When one of the GCMs is of finite, untwisted affine or hyperbolic type, we can strengthen the conclusion of Corollary
3.3
as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A
be a GCM of finite, untwisted affine or hyperbolic type and let
B\in\mathbb{G}
such that
A\preceq B\preceq A
. Then
B
coincides with
A
upto a simultaneous permutation of rows and columns.

In other words,
\preceq
is a partial order on the set of such GCMs (identifying GCMs that differ by a simultaneous permutation of rows and columns). We conjecture below that Proposition
4.1
holds for all symmetrizable GCMs. In particular when
A,B
are of twisted affine type, a possible approach is via an appeal to the Tables in
[
5
]
or
[
12
]
.

Conjecture 4.2
.

The relation
\preceq
is a partial order on all of
\mathbb{G}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
:
Observe that by Corollary
3.3
, the types (Finite/Affine/Indefinite) of
A
and
B
are the same and
\det A=\det B
. If
A
and
B
are both of finite type, then by
[
2
, Proposition 2.6]
, there are injective Lie algebra homomorphisms
\mathfrak{g}(A)\to\mathfrak{g}(B)\to\mathfrak{g}(A)
. The composition is injective, and by finite-dimensionality, also surjective. Thus,
\mathfrak{g}(A)\cong\mathfrak{g}(B)
and it follows that
A
and
B
are related by a simultaneous permutation of rows and columns (for instance by
[
11
]
); an alternate proof is an exhaustive check using the Tables of
[
3
]
.

Next, suppose that
A
and
B
are of affine type. Since
A
is untwisted affine, let
\overline{A}
denote the underlying finite type GCM. Theorem 4.2 of
[
10
]
implies that
B\preceq A
iff

is also untwisted affine with
\overline{B}\preceq\overline{A}
, where
\overline{B}
is the underlying finite type GCM of
B
. Thus
A\preceq B\preceq A
implies
\overline{A}\preceq\overline{B}\preceq\overline{A}
and the conclusion follows from the finite case above.

If
A
and
B
are both of hyperbolic type, then the assertion follows from proposition
4.3
below. ∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

be

and

. Suppose
\Sigma=\{\beta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a

-system of type

(1)

\Sigma
is

-conjugate to
\Pi(A)
or
-\Pi(A)

\Pi(A)

(2)

In particular,

and

Proof:
Consider the map
q_{{}_{\Sigma}}:Q(B)\to Q(A)
of equation (

), defined by
\alpha_{i}(B)\mapsto\beta_{i}
for all

, where
\Pi(B)=\{\alpha_{i}(B):1\leq i\leq n\}
is the set of simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}(B)
. We assume for convenience that the symmetric bilinear forms on

and

are chosen compatibly as in §

, so that

is form preserving (the arguments below will still work for any choices of standard invariant forms, since they only differ by scaling by positive rationals).

Using the given hypothesis and the fact that hyperbolic GCMs are necessarily invertible, we obtain from the second part of lemma

that:
(i)

\Sigma
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of

and
(ii)

\Sigma^{\vee}=\{\beta^{\vee}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

-basis of

.

We observe that

is a form preserving lattice isomorphism of

. We now claim that

Towards this end, we recall the following description of the set of roots of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Delta^{re}(C)

\displaystyle=\{\alpha=\sum_{j}k_{j}\,\alpha_{j}(C)\,\in Q(C):|\alpha|^{2}>0\text{ and }k_{j}\,|\alpha_{j}(C)|^{2}/|\alpha|^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }j\}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Delta^{im}(C)

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in Q(C)\backslash\{0\}:|\alpha|^{2}\leq 0\}

forms
where
\alpha_{j}(C)

\mathfrak{g}(C)
.

Since

Propositions

Lemma 4.4
.

(Determinant criterion)

Let
A,B
be symmetrizable hyperbolic GCMs of the same size. If
B\preceq A
and
B\neq A
(up to simultaneous reordering of rows and columns), then

5.
Necessary conditions for
B\preceq A

5.1.

The following is an immediate corollary of the discussion of §
2.2
, together with the fact that a real root is Weyl conjugate to some simple root, and therefore has the same length.

Lemma 5.1
.

(Root length criterion)
Let
A,B
be indecomposable symmetrizable GCMs such that
B\preceq A
. For each pair of simple roots of
B
, the ratio of their lengths equals that of some pair of simple roots of
A
(with respect to any choices of standard invariant forms on
\mathfrak{g}(A)
and
\mathfrak{g}(B)
).

For instance, this implies that there doesn’t exist a
\pi
-system of type
G_{2}
in any other finite type GCM.

5.2.

Let
X
be the Dynkin diagram of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra and let
W
denote its Weyl group. We define
X_{\mathrm{short}}
to be the subdiagram formed by the simple roots of shortest length, i.e,

X_{\mathrm{short}}=\{p\in X:|\alpha_{p}|=\min_{i\in X}|\alpha_{i}|\}

Similarly
X_{\mathrm{long}}
is the subdiagram formed by the simple roots of longest length. We also let

\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{short}}(X)=\{\alpha\in\Delta^{re}(X):|\alpha|=\min_{i\in X}|\alpha_{i}|\}=W\cdot X_{\mathrm{short}}

and
\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{long}}(X)=W\cdot X_{\mathrm{long}}
.
We say
X
is
doubly-laced
(resp.
triply-laced
) if (i) every edge of
X
is either a single edge or a double (resp. triple) edge with an arrow pointing in one direction, and (ii)
X
contains at least one double (resp. triple) edge. For instance, the finite type diagrams
B_{n},C_{n},F_{4}
are doubly-laced while
G_{2}
is triply-laced. The next lemma is a direct consequence of these definitions.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a doubly- or triply-laced Dynkin diagram of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra (we set
d=2
in the former case,
d=3
in the latter). Then:

(1)

d\mid\langle\,\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\,\rangle
for all
i\in X_{\mathrm{short}},\,j\in X\backslash X_{\mathrm{short}}
.

(2)

d\mid\langle\,\alpha_{j}^{\vee},\alpha_{i}\,\rangle
for all
i\in X_{\mathrm{long}},\,j\in X\backslash X_{\mathrm{long}}
.∎

Now consider
\pi
-systems
\Sigma
in
X
such that
\Sigma\subset\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{short}}
or
\Sigma\subset\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{long}}
. We seek to understand the possible types of such
\Sigma
. The proposition of the next subsection is the important result that will enable us to answer this question. This proposition is vastly more general and can be applied to a wide variety of settings.

5.3.

Let
X
be the Dynkin diagram of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra and let
Y
be a subdiagram of
X
. We let
\Delta(Y)
denote the set of roots of
Y
, and identify it with
Q(Y)\cap\Delta(X)
where
Q(Y)=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in Y}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
. Let
W
denote the Weyl group of
X
. The following proposition concerns multisubsets
\Sigma
of the set
W\cdot\Delta^{re}(Y)=\displaystyle\bigcup_{p\in Y}W\alpha_{p}
. We recall also the notation
M(\Sigma)
from §
2.1
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X
be the Dynkin diagram of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra,
Y
a subdiagram of
X
and
d\geq 2
an integer.
Suppose that either:

(5.1)

\displaystyle d\mid\langle\,\alpha_{j}^{\vee},\alpha_{i}\,\rangle

\displaystyle\text{ for all }i\in Y,\,j\in X\backslash Y,\text{ or }

(5.2)

\displaystyle d\mid\langle\,\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\,\rangle

\displaystyle\text{ for all }i\in Y,\,j\in X\backslash Y.

Let
\Sigma=\{\beta_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
be a multiset with
\beta_{i}\in W\cdot\Delta^{re}(Y)
. Then, there exists a multiset
\overline{\Sigma}=\{\overline{\beta}_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
with
\overline{\beta}_{i}\in\Delta^{re}(Y)
such that

M(\Sigma)\equiv M(\overline{\Sigma})\pmod{d}

Proof:
Let
s_{i}
denote the simple reflection corresponding to the vertex
i\in X
and let
W(Y)
be the (standard parabolic) subgroup of
W
generated by the
\{s_{i}:i\in Y\}
. The given hypothesis implies by
[
8
, Prop 3.13]
that for each
i\in Y,\,j\in X\backslash Y
,
(s_{i}s_{j})^{m_{ij}}=1
where
m_{ij}=2,4,6
or
\infty
. Since these are even (or
\infty
), it follows that the map
W\to W(Y)
defined on the generators by:

s_{i}\mapsto\begin{cases}s_{i}&i\in Y\\
1&i\in X\backslash Y\end{cases}

extends to a group homomorphism. We denote it
w\mapsto\overline{w}
.
Let
Q(X),\;Q^{\vee}(X)
denote the root and coroot lattices of
X
. We define sublattices
R,R^{\vee}
as follows. If (
5.1
) holds, then
R:=d\,Q(X)
, and

R^{\vee}:=d\,Q^{\vee}(Y)\oplus Q^{\vee}(X\backslash Y)=\bigoplus_{i\in Y}\;\mathbb{Z}\,(d\alpha^{\vee}_{i})\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{j\not\in Y}\;\mathbb{Z}\alpha^{\vee}_{j}.

If (
5.2
) holds, then

R:=d\,Q(Y)\oplus Q(X\backslash Y)\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;R^{\vee}=d\,Q^{\vee}(X).

The given hypotheses readily imply that
R
and
R^{\vee}
are
W
-invariant.
We now make the following important observation:

(5.3)

\text{Given }(w,\alpha)\in W\times\Delta^{re}(Y),\text{ we have }w\alpha\in\overline{w}\alpha+R\text{ and }w(\alpha^{\vee})\in\overline{w}(\alpha^{\vee})+R^{\vee}

It is enough to prove this on the generators
w=s_{k}
of
W
. This is obvious when
k\in Y
and follows from equations (
5.1
), (
5.2
) when
k\in X\backslash Y
.
Now, given
\beta\in W\cdot\Delta^{re}(Y)
, say
\beta=\sigma\alpha
for some
(\sigma,\alpha)\in W\times\Delta^{re}(Y)
, we define
\overline{\beta}:=\overline{\sigma}\alpha
. This is a real root of
Y
, and in view of (
5.3
) above, the association
\beta\mapsto\overline{\beta}
is well-defined modulo
R
. Further, if
\gamma=\tau\alpha^{\prime}
is another root in the
W
-orbit of
\Delta^{re}(Y)
, then

(5.4)

\langle\,\,\overline{\beta}^{\vee},\overline{\gamma}\,\,\rangle=\langle\,\,\overline{\sigma}(\alpha^{\vee}),\,\overline{\tau}\alpha^{\prime}\,\,\rangle\equiv\langle\,\,\sigma(\alpha^{\vee}),\,\tau\alpha^{\prime}\,\,\rangle\pmod{d}

The congruence modulo
d
in this equation is an easy consequence of equation (
5.3
), together with the observations that

\displaystyle\langle\,Q^{\vee}(X)\,,\,R\,\rangle\equiv\langle\,R^{\vee}\,,\,Q(Y)\,\rangle\equiv 0\pmod{d}

if equation (
5.1
) holds.

\displaystyle\langle\,R^{\vee}\,,\,Q(X)\,\rangle\equiv\langle\,Q^{\vee}(Y)\,,\,R\,\rangle\equiv 0\pmod{d}

if equation (
5.2
) holds.

Finally, if
\Sigma=\{\beta_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
is a multi-subset of
W\cdot\Delta^{re}(Y)
, define
\overline{\Sigma}=\{\overline{\beta}_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
.
Equation (
5.4
) now implies
M(\Sigma)\equiv M(\overline{\Sigma})\pmod{d}
as required.

\Box

We obtain several useful corollaries.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
X
be a doubly-laced Dynkin diagram of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra. Suppose that
X_{\mathrm{short}}
(respectively
X_{\mathrm{long}}
) is of type
A_{1}
, i.e., is a single vertex, then there is no
\pi
-system of type
A_{2}
in
X
contained wholly in
\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{short}}(X)
(respectively
\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{long}}(X)
).

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
X
be a doubly-laced Dynkin diagram of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra. Suppose that
X_{\mathrm{short}}
(respectively
X_{\mathrm{long}}
) is of type
A_{2}
, then there is no
\pi
-system of type
A_{2}\times A_{1}
in
X
contained wholly in
\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{short}}(X)
(respectively
\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{long}}(X)
).

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
X
be a triply-laced Dynkin diagram of a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra. Suppose that
X_{\mathrm{short}}
(respectively
X_{\mathrm{long}}
) is of type
A_{1}

\pi

(respectively
\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{long}}(X)

We indicate how to prove Corollary

, the others being similar. Lemma

allows us to apply Theorem
5.3
with

(or
X_{\mathrm{long}}
) and

. The set of shortest (or longest) real roots of
X
is nothing but
W\cdot\Delta^{re}(Y)
. Given any
\pi
-system (in fact any multiset of real roots)
\Sigma
of

contained wholly in the Weyl group orbit of
\Delta^{re}(Y)
, we obtain the multisubset
\overline{\Sigma}
of
\Delta^{re}(Y)
such that

coincides with

modulo

. For

of type
A_{2}

to be the GCM of type

-1&2&0\\
0&0&2\end{pmatrix}

So let

be such that

is congruent to

mod 2. We observe that the root system of type
A_{2}
has the property that given two (real) roots
\alpha,\beta
, we have
\langle\,\alpha^{\vee},\beta\,\rangle
is even if and only if
\beta=\pm\alpha
. Since the third row and column of

is zero mod 2, we conclude that
\overline{\beta}_{1}
and
\overline{\beta}_{2}
must both be of the form
\pm\overline{\beta}_{3}
. But this would imply
\langle\,\beta^{\vee}_{1},\beta_{2}\,\rangle
is also even, which is a contradiction. ∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose
X
is a triply-laced Dynkin diagram of finite, affine or hyperbolic type. Suppose
X_{\mathrm{short}}
is of type
A_{1}
, then there is no
\pi
-system of type
A_{2}
in
X
contained wholly in
\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{short}}(X)
.

Proof.

Let
p
denote the vertex of
X
such that
X_{\mathrm{short}}=\{p\}
. We normalize the standard invariant form on
X
such that
|\alpha_{p}|^{2}=\displaystyle\min_{j\in X}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=1
. Since
X
is triply-laced,
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}
is a nonzero power of
3
for all
j\neq p
. Now suppose
\Sigma=\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\}
is a
\pi
-system of type
A_{2}
in
X
such that
\Sigma\subset\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{short}}(X)=W\alpha_{p}
(the Weyl group orbit of
\alpha_{p}
). Applying an element of
W
if necessary, we can assume
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{p}
.
By the arguments used in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, specifically equation (
5.3
), we obtain:

\beta_{2}=\pm\alpha_{p}+\gamma

for some
\gamma\in R
, where
R=\mathbb{Z}\,(3\alpha_{p})\oplus\bigoplus_{j\neq p}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{j}
. Thus

\langle\beta_{2},\beta_{1}^{\vee}\rangle=\pm\langle\alpha_{p},\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\rangle+\langle\gamma,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\rangle\in\pm 2+3\,\mathbb{Z}

since
\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\rangle=0
or
-3
for all
j\neq p
.
Now
\Sigma
has type
A_{2}
, so
\langle\beta_{2},\beta_{1}^{\vee}\rangle=-1
.
We must have
\beta_{2}=\alpha_{p}+\gamma
, with
\langle\gamma,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\rangle=-3
.
We compute:

|\beta_{2}|^{2}=|\alpha_{p}|^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}+2\left(\alpha_{p}\mid\gamma\right)=|\alpha_{p}|^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}+\langle\,\gamma,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\,\rangle

since
|\alpha_{p}|^{2}=1
. Since
\beta_{2}
is
W
-conjugate to
\alpha_{p}
, their norms coincide, and we obtain
|\gamma|^{2}=-\langle\,\gamma,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\,\rangle=3
. We write

\gamma=3k_{p}\,\alpha_{p}+\sum\limits_{j\neq p}k_{j}\,\alpha_{j}

where the
k_{\bullet}
are integers. We observe that
\frac{3k_{p}|\alpha_{p}|^{2}}{|\gamma|^{2}}=k_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
For
j\neq p
,
\frac{k_{j}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{|\gamma|^{2}}=\frac{k_{j}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{3}\in\mathbb{Z}
since

divides
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}
. Since
X
is of finite, affine or hyperbolic type, we use equation (
4.1
) to conclude that
\gamma
is a real root of
X
. But
\gamma=\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}
, which contradicts the fact that
\Sigma
is a
\pi
-system.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Suppose
X
is a doubly-laced Dynkin diagram of finite, affine or hyperbolic type. Suppose
X_{\mathrm{short}}
is of type
A_{1}
or
A_{2}
, then there is no
\pi
-system of type
A_{1}\times A_{1}
in

contained wholly in
\Delta^{re}_{\mathrm{short}}(X)
.

Proof.

We prove it in the case that
X_{\mathrm{short}}
is of type
A_{2}
, the other case being similar. So, let
X_{\mathrm{short}}=\{p,q\}
and let
\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\}
be two elements in the
W
-orbit of
\{\alpha_{p},\alpha_{q}\}
which form a
\pi
-system of type
A_{1}\times A_{1}
. Applying an element of
W
and interchanging
p,q
if necessary, we can assume
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{p}
. By the arguments used in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, we obtain:

\beta_{2}=\alpha+\gamma

for some
\alpha\in\Delta^{re}(X_{\mathrm{short}})
and
\gamma\in R
where
R=2\,Q(X_{\mathrm{short}})\oplus Q(X\backslash X_{\mathrm{short}})
.
We have

0=\langle\,\beta_{2},\beta^{\vee}_{1}\,\rangle=\langle\,\alpha,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\,\rangle+\langle\,\gamma,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\,\rangle\in\langle\,\alpha,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\,\rangle+2\mathbb{Z}

As in the proof of Corollary
5.5
, we note that
\langle\,\alpha,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\,\rangle
is even if and only if
\alpha=\pm\alpha_{p}
. Since
\alpha_{p}\equiv-\alpha_{p}\pmod{R}
, we may assume
\beta_{2}=\alpha_{p}+\gamma
. We conclude
\langle\,\gamma,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\,\rangle=-2
. Normalizing the standard invariant form such that

, we compute:
|\beta_{2}|^{2}=|\alpha_{p}|^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}+\langle\,\gamma,\alpha_{p}^{\vee}\,\rangle
. As before, this implies

. Letting:

\gamma=2k_{p}\,\alpha_{p}+2k_{q}\,\alpha_{q}+\sum\limits_{j\neq p,q}k_{j}\,\alpha_{j}

, (ii)
\frac{2k_{q}|\alpha_{q}|^{2}}{|\gamma|^{2}}=k_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and (iii)
\frac{k_{j}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{|\gamma|^{2}}=\frac{k_{j}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
for each

, since in this case

is a nonzero power of 2. Equation (

∎

6.
Sufficient conditions for
B\preceq A
: explicit constructions

In this section we will develop some principles for explicitly constructing
\pi
-systems in a given Dynkin diagram. These are generalizations of the principles developed in
[
14
]
for simply-laced diagrams. While these principles are widely applicable, we will demonstrate them by constructing many examples of
\pi
-systems of hyperbolic type in hyperbolic Dynkin diagrams.

6.1.

All our principles below are instances of the following simple, but powerful method of constructing
\pi
-systems.

General principle:
Let
X
be the Dynkin diagram of a symmetrizable GCM. Let
\Lambda
denote a proper subdiagram of
X
and let
\Lambda^{\prime}
be the subdiagram formed by the vertices not in
\Lambda
. Let
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
be
\pi
-systems in
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}
respectively,
consisting of positive real roots
. Then
\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\prime}
is a
\pi
-system in
X
.

This principle follows from the observations that (i) the (real) roots of a subdiagram are precisely the (real) roots of the ambient diagram that are supported on the subdiagram, (ii) the difference of two positive roots with disjoint supports will have coefficients of mixed sign, and can therefore not be a root.
In all our applications below, we will always take
\Sigma^{\prime}
to consist of the set of all simple roots of
\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Observe that the GCM of
\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\prime}
is of the form

(6.1)

\begin{bmatrix}B&*\\
*&B^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}

where
B,B^{\prime}
are the GCMs of
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
respectively. The terms denoted
*
are of the form
2{\left(\beta_{1}\mid\beta_{2}\right)}/{\left(\beta_{2}\mid\beta_{2}\right)}
where
\beta_{1}\in\Lambda,\beta_{2}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
or vice versa. We now isolate some special instances of this general principle, which will be used repeatedly in the sequel.

6.2.
Principle A:

Let
Y
be an affine Dynkin diagram, twisted or untwisted, but
Y\neq A_{2l}^{(2)}
. Let
\{\alpha_{0},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\}
denote the simple roots of
Y
. Let
\overline{Y}
denote the underlying finite type diagram, obtained from
Y
by deleting the node corresponding to
\alpha_{0}
.

Let
X
be the diagram obtained by adding an extra vertex to
Y
, which is connected only to
\alpha_{0}
, and by a single edge. Since
Y
is symmetrizable, so is
X
. We denote the simple root corresponding to this vertex
\alpha_{-1}
.
Let
A=(a_{ij})
denote the GCM of
X
; thus
a_{ij}={2\left(\alpha_{i}\mid\alpha_{j}\right)}/{\left(\alpha_{i}\mid\alpha_{i}\right)}
for
-1\leq i,j\leq n

(we note in passing that when
Y
is simply-laced,
X
is of
\mathrm{Ext}
type). Let
\delta_{Y}
denote the null root of
Y
, so
\delta_{Y}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
.
We let
s_{i}
denote the reflection corresponding to the simple root
\alpha_{i}
.

Since
Y
is an affine diagram other than
A_{2l}^{(2)}
, we have
a_{0}=1

[
8
, Chapter 4, Tables Aff 1-3]
. In the general principle, we take the subdiagram
\Lambda=Y
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
to be the singleton set containing the vertex
(-1)
. Define
\Sigma
to be the
\pi
-system in
Y
of type
Y
comprising the roots
\{s_{0}\,\gamma_{i}:0\leq i\leq n\}
where the
\gamma_{i}
are given by:

\gamma_{0}=\alpha_{0}+\delta_{Y},\;\;\gamma_{j}=\alpha_{j}\;(j\geq 1)

When
Y
is twisted,
\alpha_{0}
is a short root and hence
\alpha_{0}+\delta_{Y}
is a root in this case; it is of course a root when
Y
is untwisted.
Define
\Sigma^{\prime}=\{\alpha_{-1}\}
; this is clearly of finite type
A_{1}
.
We let
\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\prime}=\{\beta_{i}:-1\leq i\leq n\}
with
\beta_{-1}=\alpha_{-1}
and
\beta_{i}=s_{0}\,\gamma_{i}
for
i\geq 1
.
All the hypotheses of the general principle are satisfied. As observed in equation (
6.1
), to find the type of
\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\prime}
, it only remains to compute the numbers

b_{ij}={2\left(\beta_{i}\mid\beta_{j}\right)}/{\left(\beta_{i}\mid\beta_{i}\right)}
where

i=-1,j\geq 0
or vice-versa.

Now: (i)
\left(\beta_{-1}\mid\beta_{j}\right)=\left(s_{0}\,\beta_{-1}\mid\gamma_{j}\right)=\left(\alpha_{0}\mid\alpha_{j}\right)
for
j\geq 1
, since
s_{0}\,\alpha_{-1}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{-1}
and
\alpha_{-1}
is orthogonal to all roots of
\overline{Y}
. (ii)
|\beta_{-1}|^{2}=|\alpha_{-1}|^{2}=|\alpha_{0}|^{2}
. This gives us:
b_{j,-1}=a_{j0}
and
b_{-1,j}=a_{0j}
for
j\geq 1
.
Finally, we compute:
\left(\beta_{-1}\mid\beta_{0}\right)=\left(\alpha_{-1}\mid s_{0}(\alpha_{0}+\delta_{Y})\right)
. But
s_{0}(\alpha_{0}+\delta_{Y})=-\alpha_{0}+\delta_{Y}=\theta
, where
\theta
is the highest long (respectively short) root of
\overline{Y}
if

Principle C:
(Shrinking) Suppose
I
is a subset of the vertices of
X
such that
I
forms a (connected) subdiagram of Finite type. It is well known that
\beta_{\bullet}=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}
is a root of

. Since
I
is of finite type, this root is real. In the general principle, we choose the subset
\Lambda=I
and the
\pi
-system
\Sigma=\{\beta_{\bullet}\}
. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}=\{\alpha_{j}:j\not\in I\}
.
Let
B
denote the GCM of
\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\prime}
. We have for
j\not\in I
,

\frac{\left(\beta_{\bullet}\mid\alpha_{j}\right)}{\left(\alpha_{j}\mid\alpha_{j}\right)}=\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\left(\alpha_{i}\mid\alpha_{j}\right)}{\left(\alpha_{j}\mid\alpha_{j}\right)}

Further, letting
k_{i}=|\alpha_{i}|^{2}/|\beta_{\bullet}|^{2}
for
i\in I
, we have

\frac{\left(\beta_{\bullet}\mid\alpha_{j}\right)}{\left(\beta_{\bullet}\mid\beta_{\bullet}\right)}=\sum_{i\in I}k_{i}\frac{\left(\alpha_{i}\mid\alpha_{j}\right)}{\left(\alpha_{i}\mid\alpha_{i}\right)}

Thus,

(6.2)

b_{j\bullet}=\sum_{i\in I}a_{ji},\;\;\;b_{\bullet j}=\sum_{i\in I}k_{i}\,a_{ij}

We note that
k_{i}
is the ratio of root lengths in a finite type diagram, and is therefore one of
\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2},1,2,3
.
If no two vertices of

have a common neighbour

(i) Let the vertices of
