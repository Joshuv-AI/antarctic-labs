 ABSTRACT

Livestock wastewater management is a critical concern in the United States, with an annual
production of approximately 1.37 billion tons of waste, surpassing human waste by three to
twenty times. The mismanagement of manure wastewater poses significant threats to freshwater
sources, ecosystems, and public health. Through this project, we proposed an innovative solution
using electrocoagulation (EC) treatment. The EC technique is an electrochemical process
involving the intentional corrosion of aluminum and iron electrodes to introduce trivalent ions
into the solution, facilitating the co-precipitation and coagulation of contaminants and making the
removal of water from sludge easier. The project's primary objective is to use EC to convert
liquid animal manure into clean water for farm irrigation, drinking, and maintenance. This
solution is vital for various farms including those facing drought, pursuing zero-discharge, and
seeking Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) permits for livestock farm manure discharge
into rivers. Preliminary research shows EC's potential to significantly reduce turbidity and
phosphate levels in livestock wastewater, forming the basis for scalable onsite treatment. The goal
of this proposed project is to develop an innovative farm-wastewater-treatment process to achieve
clean water, fertilizer, and reduced greenhouse gases through electrification of current processes
such as coagulation, dewatering, inactivation of viruses and bacteria, and filtration for recycling
surface water from farm lagoons.

 1. STATEMENT OF OBJECTIVES

The goal of this project is to develop an innovative farm wastewater treatment process to produce
clean, reusable water from livestock manure through electrification of current processes such as
coagulation, dewatering, inactivation of viruses and bacteria, and filtration for recycling surface
water from farm lagoons.

The established norm in manure management involves the transportation of waste offsite,
incurring substantial financial burdens associated with biosolids transportation, which can amount
to thousands or even millions of dollars annually. Current solutions for manure management
 1

(including lagoons, storage pits and anaerobic digestors) can lead to impending pollution and
environmental fines for livestock farmers. Additionally, farm water scarcity can bring about
expensive water bills during droughts. Therefore, we proposed an emerging technology using EC
to transform liquid livestock manure into clean water to recycle and reuse on livestock farms,
where manure is stored in a central location, commonly called a lagoon or manure storage pit.
This technology uses electricity to inactivate microorganisms and generate clean water by
precipitation of biosolid waste. Consequently, this technology emerges as a compelling
alternative to addressing the need for farm wastewater treatment, potentially saving farmers
money.

The team will harness our collective expertise in electrolyzing animal wastewater and our
proficiency in upscaling chemical processes for wastewater treatment. The tasks and objectives
needed to achieve these goals are presented below:
1. Characterize and test EC as a water treatment method. Analyze lagoon water samples before
 and after treatment.
2. Design and construct a lab-scale continuous-flow EC system. Test with simulated and real
 lagoon water samples.
3. Conduct a life cycle assessment to evaluate the impacts of environmental sustainability.
 Quantify contributions to byproduct generation, energy conservation, and GHG reduction.

 2. BENEFITS TO THE FUNDING DOE OFFICE’S MISSION

The goal of this project is to develop an innovative farm-wastewater-treatment process through
electrification and filtration for recycling surface water from farm lagoons. Electrifying animal
manure to produce clean water, aligns closely with EERE's mission to accelerate technology
deployment that addresses water pollution.

 3. TECHNICAL DISCUSSION OF WORK PERFORMED BY ALL PARTIES

This case study involved a 200-head dairy farm. A manure slurry sample was recovered from the
lagoon to conduct lab-scale tests. The experimental setup, shown in Figures 2 and 3, included a
pump, EC reactor, iron or aluminum electrodes, and a continuous flow-through reactor.
Experiments using the lab-scale system were evaluated to determine the feasibility of this
technology in producing reusable, clean water free from biosolids. The system operated
continuously with extra processing on the effluent slurry using activated carbon and filtering.
Water quality parameters, turbidity, pH, and total dissolved solids were analyzed appropriately.

The EC of liquid dairy cow manure utilizing a continuous flow-through reactor demonstrated the
capacity to produce irrigation-quality water. The treatment of manure yielded an over 95%
reduction in the turbidity of the initial raw manure sample A, shown in Figure 4.

In Figure 4, Sample A shows cow lagoon wastewater obtained onsite without processing therefore
displaying a high turbidity compared to the treated Sample B & C, as shown. Sample B shows the results of
continuous flow electrolysis treatment on wastewater found in container A, resulting in turbidity of 18 NTU
with a 97% removal efficiency. Sample C demonstrates the result of EC post-treatment, activated carbon.
This three-step process resulted in significantly less odor, lower turbidity, and biosolids than Samples A and
B. The water quality results for Sample C demonstrate turbidity reduced drastically (98%); however, it did
not meet EPA drinking water standard. The final turbidity for the Sample C was 12 NTU; with additional
processing, Sample C was very close to reaching the drinking water standard for turbidity (<2 NTU). Table
2 displays drinking water parameter results as safe. The pH of the sample in Container C was within 8.40,
within the EPA drinking water pH range according to the standard EPA test kit.

 5. COMMERCIALIZATION POSSIBILITIES

Farmers often face significant challenges in managing animal waste, which poses environmental threats
such as freshwater contamination, harm to aquatic ecosystems, compromised air quality, and risks to human
health. The mismanagement of animal waste on farms impacts these critical factors and raises concerns
about regulatory compliance, potentially leading to hefty environmental fines and associated issues.
Recognizing the urgent need for sustainable practices, the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA)
highlights the value of water recycling and reclamation. USDA support emphasizes the importance of
 5

responsible waste management to mitigate environmental impact and ensure a sustainable agricultural
future. Looking ahead, decentralized wastewater treatment, including technologies like electrocoagulation
(EC), is crucial for livestock farmers to reduce pollution discharge. This approach not only improves
environmental health and protects surface waters but also provides cost savings and helps farmers comply
with regulations by adopting a circular water usage model that incorporates non-traditional wastewater
reuse, such as liquid animal manure.

 6. PLANS FOR FUTURE COLLABORATION

This project marked the inaugural demonstration of the efficacy of EC in treating cow manure sourced from
a local dairy farm in Tennessee. Throughout this project, we successfully demonstrated the efficacy of EC in
treating cow manure from a local Tennessee dairy farm. We developed a continuous flow-through reactor
system for this purpose. This advancement shows promise for onsite water purification through recycled
manure, highlighting the potential for decentralized water treatment and onsite fertilizer production.
