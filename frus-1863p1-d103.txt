Mr. Seward to Mr. Adams.

No 494.]

Department of State, Washington, March 2, 1863.

Sir: You are authorized to inform Jerome Murch, esquire, who presided at a meeting of the citizens of Bath, which was held at the Guildhall on the 2d of February last, that the proceedings of that meeting have been submitted to the President of the United States, and that he is deeply affected by the fervent sympathies with our country, and the cordial wishes for its peace and prosperity, which the citizens of Bath have thus kindly expressed. You will add that, if the peace of the world shall be further disturbed in consequence of the unhappy insurrection now existing in this country, that painful result will happen through no fault of this government, which has no other purpose but to maintain the integrity of the United States and their free institutions—an object, as it believes, only in degree more important to them than to all other nations.

I am, sir, your obedient servant,

WILLIAM H. SEWARD.

Charles Francis Adams, Esq., &c., &c., &c.
