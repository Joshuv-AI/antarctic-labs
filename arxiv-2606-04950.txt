The Tricomi equation in the hyperbolic half plane under additive space-time Gaussian White Noise perturbation
Enrico Bernardi, Alberto Lanconelli

Abstract
We study the Cauchy problem for the Tricomi equation perturbed by space-time Gaussian White Noise. To prove existence and uniqueness of the solution, we employ a Fourier transform approach that allows to obtain its representation in terms of certain integrals of the Airy functions. Then, via a careful analysis of the asymptotic behaviour of those integrals, we obtain all the desired properties of the solution, such as square integrability, continuity of its sample paths and stationarity with respect to the space variable. In relation to that stationarity, we also provide the precise description of how the correlation function behaves for small values of the space-lag. We also remark that, in contrast to the findings of the recent paper [ 11 ] , the properties of the solution to our stochastic Tricomi equation are equivalent to those derived in studying the corresponding problem for the wave operator.



Key words and phrases: Tricomi equation, space-time Gaussian White Noise, Airy functions, correlation function.

Introduction and statement of the main results

The aim of this paper is to study the following Cauchy problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}U(t,x)=t\partial_{xx}U(t,x)+W(t,x),&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R};\\
U(0,x)=0,\quad\partial_{t}U(0,x)=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\{W(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
is a space-time Gaussian White Noise. The first equation in (
1.1
) represents a random perturbation of the classic Tricomi equation and we aim at investigating existence, uniqueness as well as probabilistic and analytic properties of its solution.

The Tricomi equation
[
23
]
is a second-order partial
differential equation of mixed elliptic-hyperbolic type with the form:

\displaystyle\partial_{tt}u(t,x)=t\partial_{xx}u(t,x),\quad t,x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.2)

The equation is hyperbolic in the half plane
t>0
, elliptic in the half plane
t<0
, and degenerates on the line
t=0
. Many important problems in fluid mechanics and differential geometry can be reduced to corresponding problems for the Tricomi equation, particularly
transonic flow
problems
[
18
]
and
isometric embedding
problems
[
17
]
(see also
[
16
]
and the references quoted there).

Most of the literature on hyperbolic partial differential equations perturbed by noise is centred on the wave equation. In the seminal works
[
24
]
and
[
19
]
the authors consider the Cauchy problem for the one dimensional wave equation perturbed by additive space-time Gaussian White Noise proving existence, uniqueness and probabilistic properties of the solution. For higher space dimension,
[
15
]
identifies a necessary and sufficient condition relating additive noise terms to space dimension that allow for well posedness of the corresponding stochastic partial differential equations; see also
[
14
]
. The so-called Hyperbolic Anderson model, i.e. wave equation perturbed by a multiplicative noise term with linear diffusion coefficient, is considered in several recent papers: see for instance
[
5
]
,
[
4
]
and the reference quoted there. For non linear problems some relevant pointers are
[
13
]
,
[
21
]
and
[
20
]
.

Stochastic partial differential equations with non strictly hyperbolic operators are not very much explored in the literature. Some recent papers have attempted to investigate how the degeneracies of the operator have an impact on stochastic perturbations. In
[
2
]
the authors study mild solutions of a class of stochastic partial differential equations, involving operators with polynomially bounded coefficients under suitable hyperbolicity hypotheses;
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
investigate the existence of random-field solutions for weakly hyperbolic stochastic partial differential equations; in
[
10
]
and
[
12
]
the authors study the effect of Gaussian perturbations on a hyperbolic partial differential equation with double characteristics and double symplectic characteristics, respectively, in
low spatial dimensions. Furthermore,
[
11
]
considers a class of Tricomi-type partial differential equations and analyze the robustness of the solution by taking the initial data to be Gaussian White Noise: the authors discover that the existence of a well-defined random field solution is lost upon the introduction of lower-order terms in the operator.

In the present paper we focus on the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) and investigate existence and uniqueness of square integrable solutions. We recall that in the series of papers
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
the authors have found an explicit expression for the fundamental solution of the Tricomi operator. However, due to the complexity of such representation, our approach avoids the use of that result and instead employs a Fourier method. Specifically, we consider the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}u(t,x)=t\partial_{xx}u(t,x)+h(t,x),&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R};\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad\partial_{t}u(0,x)=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.3)

for some smooth function
h:[0,+\infty[\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. Taking the Fourier transform in the spatial variable and setting

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(t,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}u(t,x)dx\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\widehat{h}(t,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}h(t,x)dx,

problem (
1.3
) transforms into

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)=-t\xi^{2}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)+\widehat{h}(t,\xi),&t>0,\xi\in\mathbb{R};\\
\widehat{u}(0,\xi)=0,\quad\partial_{t}\widehat{u}(0,\xi)=0,&\xi\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Now, the homogeneous equation

\displaystyle\partial_{tt}v(t,\xi)=-t\xi^{2}v(t,\xi)

can be reduced by the change of variable
\eta:=|\xi|^{2/3}t
to

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta\eta}v(\eta,\xi)=-\eta v(\eta,\xi).

The linearly independent solutions of this equation are the Airy functions, denoted
\mathrm{Ai}
and
\mathrm{Bi}
, whose Wronskian is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ai}(0)\mathrm{Bi}^{\prime}(0)-\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(0)\mathrm{Bi}(0)=\frac{1}{\pi}.

Therefore, if we set

\displaystyle v_{1}(t,\xi):=\mathrm{Ai}\!\left(-|\xi|^{2/3}t\right)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad v_{2}(t,\xi):=\mathrm{Bi}\!\left(-|\xi|^{2/3}t\right),

whose Wronskian with respect to
t
is

\displaystyle v_{1}(0,\xi)\partial_{t}v_{2}(0,\xi)-\partial_{t}v_{1}(0,\xi)v_{2}(0,\xi)=-\frac{|\xi|^{2/3}}{\pi},

we obtain by variation of parameters the solution to (
1.4
) as

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(t,\xi)=\int_{0}^{t}C(t,s,|\xi|)\,\widehat{h}(s,\xi)\,ds,

(1.5)

where, for
\rho>0
,

\displaystyle C(t,s,\rho):=-\frac{\pi}{\rho^{2/3}}\!\left[\mathrm{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}t)\,\mathrm{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}s)-\mathrm{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}t)\,\mathrm{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}s)\right],

(1.6)

and we set

\displaystyle C(t,s,0):=t-s,

which is the continuous extension of (
1.6
) to
\rho=0
.
Thus, inverting the Fourier transform we can represent the solution to (
1.3
) as

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\xi x}\int_{0}^{t}C(t,s,|\xi|)\widehat{h}(s,\xi)dsd\xi.

(1.7)

Formula (
1.7
) will be the starting point for our study of (
1.1
).

To formulate our main result, we now assume that space-time Gaussian White Noise
\{W(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
from (
1.1
) is represented through the formal series

\displaystyle W(t,x)=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\psi_{m}(t)\varphi_{n}(x)\mathtt{z}_{mn},\quad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.8)

Here:

•

\{\psi_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
is a fixed real, smooth orthonormal basis
of
L^{2}([0,+\infty))
, for instance, one may take a Laguerre basis;

•

\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is the orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
made of Hermite functions;

•

\{\mathtt{z}_{mn}\}_{m,n\geq 1}
denotes a family of independent standard Gaussian random variables defined on a common probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

A solution
\{U(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
to problem (
1.1
) is by definition

\displaystyle U(t,x):=\lim_{N\to+\infty}U_{N}(t,x)\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\{U_{N}(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
is solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}U_{N}(t,x)=t\partial_{xx}U_{N}(t,x)+W_{N}(t,x),&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R};\\
U_{N}(0,x)=0,\quad\partial_{t}U_{N}(0,x)=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.9)

while

\displaystyle W_{N}(t,x)=\sum_{m,n=1}^{N}\psi_{m}(t)\varphi_{n}(x)\mathtt{z}_{mn},\quad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R},

provides a finite-rank smooth approximation of the space-time Gaussian White Noise
\{W(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
. Notice that problem (
1.9
) can be treated
\omega
-wise as a non-homogeneous deterministic Tricomi equation of the form (
1.3
).

We are now ready to state the main result of our paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Problem (
1.1
) admits a unique solution, in the above mild random-field sense, which is a Gaussian random field with representation

\displaystyle U(t,x)=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}u_{mn}(t,x)\mathtt{z}_{mn},\quad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.10)

where

\displaystyle u_{mn}(t,x):=\frac{(-i)^{n}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{ix\xi}C(t,s,|\xi|)\psi_{m}(s)\varphi_{n}(\xi)dsd\xi.

Here, for
\rho>0
,

\displaystyle C(t,s,\rho):=-\frac{\pi}{\rho^{2/3}}\!\left[\mathrm{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}t)\,\mathrm{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}s)-\mathrm{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}t)\,\mathrm{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}s)\right],\quad s,t\geq 0,

(1.11)

and
C(t,s,0):=t-s
. The functions
\mathrm{Ai}
and
\mathrm{Bi}
are the Airy functions. The series in (
1.10
) converges in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and almost surely. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|U(t,x)|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left|C(t,s,\rho)\right|^{2}dsd\rho<+\infty,\quad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.12)

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is postponed to Section
2
below. The next result describes probabilistic and analytic properties of the stochastic process
x\mapsto U(t,x)
for fixed
t>0
; in particular, we show that it is second order stationary with correlation function admitting a specific asymptotic behaviour that guarantees continuity of its paths. Such property is shared with the stochastic wave equation, as proved in
[
19
]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

For any
t>0
the stochastic process
\{U(t,x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
is second order stationary with expectation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U(t,x)]=0\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}

and covariance

\displaystyle\mathtt{cov}(U(t,x),U(t,y))=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\cos(\rho|x-y|)\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}dsd\rho\quad\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.13)

Moreover, the correlation function

\displaystyle r_{t}(h):=\frac{\mathtt{cov}(U(t,x+h),U(t,x))}{\mathbb{V}[U(t,x)]},\quad t>0,x,h\in\mathbb{R},

verifies

\displaystyle r_{t}(h)=1-\alpha_{t}|h|+o(|h|)\mbox{ as $|h|$ tends to zero},

with

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}:=\frac{\pi}{2\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left|C(t,s,\rho)\right|^{2}dsd\rho}.

In particular, the stochastic process
\{U(t,x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
admits a continuous modification.

The proof of Corollary
1.2
can be found in Section
3
below. Our last result concerns the continuity property of the stochastic process
t\mapsto U(t,x)
for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Here, in absence of stationarity, we must resort to the Kolmogorov continuity theorem which, in combination with the Gaussian nature of our solution process, amounts at estimating the second moment only. In fact, for any
p\geq 1

there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{p}]=C_{p}\mathbb{E}[|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{2}]^{\frac{p}{2}},\quad s,t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},

and hence an estimate of the type

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{2}]\leq C|t-s|

will be sufficient for the purpose. For the proof of Corollary
1.3
see Section
4
below.

Corollary 1.3
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
the stochastic process
\{U(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0}
admits a continuous modification with H
\ddot{o}
lder regularity of order less than
1/2
.

The paper is organized as follows: Section
2
presents the proof of Theorem
1.1
where we exploit the explicit representation of the White Noise given in (
1.8
) and some basic facts on Airy functions; in Section
3
we prove Corollary
1.2
: the proof is articulated in several fine estimates involving the Airy functions, for negative values of their arguments, and related integrals: this will be the key for obtaining the desired properties of the solution; lastly, Section
4
contains the proof of Corollary
1.3
which amounts, by virtue of Kolmogorov continuity theorem and the Gaussianity of the solution, at proving an upper bound for the second moment of the time increment of the solution.

\displaystyle r_{t}(h)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\cos(\rho|h|)\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}dsd\rho}{\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}dsd\rho}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-\cos(\rho|h|))\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}dsd\rho}{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}dsd\rho}

\displaystyle=1-\hat{\alpha_{t}}\cdot\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-\cos(\rho|h|))\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}dsd\rho,

with

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha_{t}}:=\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}dsd\rho}.

We thus have to investigate the expression

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-\cos(\rho|h|))\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}dsd\rho.

To establish our claim we need the following crucial auxiliary fact.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
t>0
, and set

K_{t}(\rho):=\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}\,ds,

where

C(t,s,\rho)=-\pi\rho^{-2/3}\Big[\operatorname{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}t)\operatorname{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}s)-\operatorname{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}t)\operatorname{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}s)\Big].

Then

K_{t}(\rho)=\rho^{-2}+o(\rho^{-2})\qquad\text{as }\rho\to+\infty.

Equivalently,

\rho^{2}K_{t}(\rho)\longrightarrow 1.

Proof.

Fix
t>0
. We shall prove that

\rho^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}\,ds\longrightarrow 1.

We use the standard Airy asymptotics on the negative real axis. Namely, if

\theta(x):=\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}+\frac{\pi}{4},\qquad x>0,

then, as
x\to+\infty
,

\operatorname{Ai}(-x)=\pi^{-1/2}x^{-1/4}\bigl(\sin\theta(x)+r_{A}(x)\bigr),

and

\operatorname{Bi}(-x)=\pi^{-1/2}x^{-1/4}\bigl(\cos\theta(x)+r_{B}(x)\bigr),

where

r_{A}(x)\to 0,\qquad r_{B}(x)\to 0\qquad\text{as }x\to+\infty.

Equivalently, if

\varepsilon_{L}:=\sup_{x\geq L}\bigl(|r_{A}(x)|+|r_{B}(x)|\bigr),

then

\varepsilon_{L}\to 0\qquad\text{as }L\to+\infty.

Let
L>1
be fixed. For
\rho
large enough we have

L\rho^{-2/3}<t.

We split

\rho^{2}K_{t}(\rho)=\rho^{2}\int_{0}^{L\rho^{-2/3}}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}\,ds+\rho^{2}\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}\,ds=:I_{\mathrm{small}}(\rho,L)+I_{\mathrm{bulk}}(\rho,L).

We first show that the small interval gives no contribution. On

0\leq s\leq L\rho^{-2/3},

the quantities
\rho^{2/3}s
remain in the compact interval
[0,L]
. Hence

\operatorname{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}s)
and
\operatorname{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}s)
are bounded by a constant depending only on
L
. On the other hand,

\operatorname{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}t)=O((\rho^{2/3}t)^{-1/4})=O(\rho^{-1/6}),

and similarly

\operatorname{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}t)=O(\rho^{-1/6}).

Therefore

\displaystyle\Big|\operatorname{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}t)\operatorname{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}s)-\operatorname{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}t)\operatorname{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}s)\Big|\leq C_{L}\rho^{-1/6}.

Consequently,

|C(t,s,\rho)|^{2}\leq C_{L}\rho^{-4/3}\rho^{-1/3}=C_{L}\rho^{-5/3}.

Thus

I_{\mathrm{small}}(\rho,L)\leq\rho^{2}\int_{0}^{L\rho^{-2/3}}C_{L}\rho^{-5/3}\,ds=C_{L}L\rho^{-1/3}\longrightarrow 0

as
\rho\to+\infty
, for every fixed
L
.

We now study
I_{\mathrm{bulk}}(\rho,L)
. Put

x_{t}:=\rho^{2/3}t,\qquad x_{s}:=\rho^{2/3}s.

On the interval
s\in[L\rho^{-2/3},t]
, both
x_{s}
and
x_{t}
are at least
L
, for
\rho
sufficiently large. Therefore the Airy asymptotics above apply uniformly. Define

\displaystyle D(t,s,\rho)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Bi}(-x_{t})\operatorname{Ai}(-x_{s})-\operatorname{Ai}(-x_{t})\operatorname{Bi}(-x_{s}).

Using the asymptotic formulae, we get

\displaystyle D(t,s,\rho)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-1}(x_{t}x_{s})^{-1/4}\Big[\cos\theta(x_{t})\sin\theta(x_{s})-\sin\theta(x_{t})\cos\theta(x_{s})+R_{L}(t,s,\rho)\Big],

where, uniformly for
s\in[L\rho^{-2/3},t]
,

|R_{L}(t,s,\rho)|\leq C\varepsilon_{L}.

Since

\cos\theta(x_{t})\sin\theta(x_{s})-\sin\theta(x_{t})\cos\theta(x_{s})=\sin(\theta(x_{s})-\theta(x_{t})),

and since

\theta(x_{t})-\theta(x_{s})=\frac{2}{3}\rho(t^{3/2}-s^{3/2}),

we obtain

D(t,s,\rho)=\pi^{-1}\rho^{-1/3}(ts)^{-1/4}\Big[-\sin\Big(\frac{2}{3}\rho(t^{3/2}-s^{3/2})\Big)+R_{L}(t,s,\rho)\Big].

Multiplying by the prefactor in
C
, namely
C=-\pi\rho^{-2/3}D
, gives

C(t,s,\rho)=\rho^{-1}(ts)^{-1/4}\Big[\sin\Big(\frac{2}{3}\rho(t^{3/2}-s^{3/2})\Big)+\widetilde{R}_{L}(t,s,\rho)\Big],

with

|\widetilde{R}_{L}(t,s,\rho)|\leq C\varepsilon_{L}

uniformly on the same interval. Therefore

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{bulk}}(\rho,L)

\displaystyle=\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}(ts)^{-1/2}\Big|\sin\Big(\frac{2}{3}\rho(t^{3/2}-s^{3/2})\Big)+\widetilde{R}_{L}(t,s,\rho)\Big|^{2}\,ds.

It follows that

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{bulk}}(\rho,L)

\displaystyle=J(\rho,L)+E(\rho,L),

where

J(\rho,L):=\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}(ts)^{-1/2}\sin^{2}\Big(\frac{2}{3}\rho(t^{3/2}-s^{3/2})\Big)\,ds,

and

|E(\rho,L)|\leq C\varepsilon_{L}\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}(ts)^{-1/2}\,ds.

But

\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}(ts)^{-1/2}\,ds=t^{-1/2}\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}s^{-1/2}\,ds\leq 2.

Hence

|E(\rho,L)|\leq C\varepsilon_{L}.

It remains to compute the limit of
J(\rho,L)
. Using

\sin^{2}y=\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos(2y)),

we write

\displaystyle J(\rho,L)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}t^{-1/2}\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}s^{-1/2}\,ds

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}t^{-1/2}\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}s^{-1/2}\cos\Big(\frac{4}{3}\rho(t^{3/2}-s^{3/2})\Big)\,ds.

The non-oscillatory part is

\frac{1}{2}t^{-1/2}\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}s^{-1/2}\,ds=t^{-1/2}\left(\sqrt{t}-\sqrt{L}\,\rho^{-1/3}\right)\longrightarrow 1.

We claim that the oscillatory part tends to zero. Set

u=\frac{2}{3}(t^{3/2}-s^{3/2}).

Then

du=-s^{1/2}\,ds,\qquad s=\left(t^{3/2}-\frac{3}{2}u\right)^{2/3},

and therefore

s^{-1/2}\,ds=-s^{-1}\,du=-\left(t^{3/2}-\frac{3}{2}u\right)^{-2/3}\,du.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}s^{-1/2}\cos\Big(\frac{4}{3}\rho(t^{3/2}-s^{3/2})\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}(t^{3/2}-L^{3/2}\rho^{-1})}\left(t^{3/2}-\frac{3}{2}u\right)^{-2/3}\cos(2\rho u)\,du.

The function

g(u):=\left(t^{3/2}-\frac{3}{2}u\right)^{-2/3}

belongs to

L^{1}\left(0,\frac{2}{3}t^{3/2}\right),

because its singularity at the endpoint is of order
2/3<1
. Hence, by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma,

\int_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}t^{3/2}}g(u)\cos(2\rho u)\,du\longrightarrow 0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\frac{2}{3}(t^{3/2}-L^{3/2}\rho^{-1})}^{\frac{2}{3}t^{3/2}}g(u)\cos(2\rho u)\,du\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\frac{2}{3}(t^{3/2}-L^{3/2}\rho^{-1})}^{\frac{2}{3}t^{3/2}}g(u)\,du\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore the oscillatory part tends to zero, and so

J(\rho,L)\longrightarrow 1\qquad\text{as }\rho\to+\infty.

We have shown that, for every fixed
L>1
,

\limsup_{\rho\to+\infty}\bigl|\rho^{2}K_{t}(\rho)-1\bigr|\leq C\varepsilon_{L}.

Finally, since
\varepsilon_{L}\to 0
as
L\to+\infty
, we conclude that

\rho^{2}K_{t}(\rho)\longrightarrow 1.

Equivalently,

K_{t}(\rho)=\rho^{-2}+o(\rho^{-2})\qquad\text{as }\rho\to+\infty.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.2

(Control of the Airy remainder terms)
.

The preceding proof avoids the decomposition into a principal part and a separate remainder term on a fixed interval

[s_{\rho},t]
. The reason for introducing the auxiliary parameter
L
is precisely to make the Airy remainders uniformly small on the whole bulk region

L\rho^{-2/3}\leq s\leq t.

Indeed, on this region we obtained

C(t,s,\rho)=\rho^{-1}(ts)^{-1/4}\left[S(t,s,\rho)+\widetilde{R}_{L}(t,s,\rho)\right],

where

S(t,s,\rho):=\sin\!\left(\frac{2}{3}\rho(t^{3/2}-s^{3/2})\right),\qquad|\widetilde{R}_{L}(t,s,\rho)|\leq C\varepsilon_{L},

and

\varepsilon_{L}:=\sup_{x\geq L}\big(|r_{A}(x)|+|r_{B}(x)|\big)\to 0\qquad\text{as }L\to+\infty.

Therefore the whole contribution of the cross term and of the square of the remainder is estimated at once by

\displaystyle\left|\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}(ts)^{-1/2}\left(|S(t,s,\rho)+\widetilde{R}_{L}(t,s,\rho)|^{2}-|S(t,s,\rho)|^{2}\right)ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{L}\int_{L\rho^{-2/3}}^{t}(ts)^{-1/2}\,ds\leq C\varepsilon_{L}.

Thus the error terms are not treated by proving a separate oscillatory estimate for each cross term. They are instead bounded uniformly, for large
\rho
, by a quantity that can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
L
large. This is the point of the two-step argument: first let
\rho\to+\infty
with
L
fixed, and then let
L\to+\infty
.

We are now ready to prove (
3.1
) and hence to complete the proof of Corollary
1.2
.
By virtue of the classical identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1-\cos(\rho h)}{\rho^{2}}d\rho=\frac{\pi}{2}|h|,\quad h\in\mathbb{R}

we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-\cos(\rho h))K_{t}(\rho)d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-\cos(\rho h))(\rho^{-2}+\tilde{K}_{t}(\rho))d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}|h|+\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-\cos(\rho h))\tilde{K}_{t}(\rho)d\rho

where
\tilde{K}_{t}(\rho):=K_{t}(\rho)-\rho^{-2}
. By Lemma
3.1
,

\tilde{K}_{t}(\rho)=o(\rho^{-2})
as
\rho\to+\infty
. In particular, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\rho_{0}
such that
|\tilde{K}_{t}(\rho)|\leq\varepsilon\rho^{-2}
for all
\rho\geq\rho_{0}
. With such
\rho_{0}
we split the last integral as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1-\cos(\rho h))\tilde{K}_{t}(\rho)d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}(1-\cos(\rho h))\tilde{K}_{t}(\rho)d\rho+\int_{\rho_{0}}^{+\infty}(1-\cos(\rho h))\tilde{K}_{t}(\rho)d\rho

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|h|^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\rho^{2}|\tilde{K}_{t}(\rho)|d\rho+\varepsilon\frac{\pi}{2}|h|.

This shows that

\displaystyle r_{t}(h)=1-\alpha_{t}|h|+o(|h|)\mbox{ as }h\to 0

with

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}:=\frac{\pi}{2\int_{0}^{+\infty}K_{t}(\rho)d\rho}.

Proof of Corollary
1.3

We prove the estimate on an arbitrary compact time interval
[0,T]
. By
Gaussianity, this second-moment estimate implies the asserted Hölder
regularity through Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem.

We shall use the following elementary consequences of the Airy asymptotics on
the negative real axis. There exists a constant
C>0
such that, for every

x\geq 0
,

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Ai}(-x)|+|\operatorname{Bi}(-x)|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+x)^{-1/4},

(4.1)

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime}(-x)|+|\operatorname{Bi}^{\prime}(-x)|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+x)^{1/4}.

(4.2)

The estimates are standard: for bounded
x
they follow from smoothness of

\operatorname{Ai}
and
\operatorname{Bi}
, while for large
x
they are exactly the usual
oscillatory Airy estimates.

Let

0\leq s\leq t\leq T,\qquad\delta:=t-s.

The cases
t=s
and
t=0
are trivial. Moreover, it is enough to prove the
estimate for
0<\delta\leq 1
; the case
\delta>1
is absorbed by increasing

C_{T}
, since
\sup_{0\leq u\leq T}\mathbb{E}|U(u,x)|^{2}<+\infty
, by the variance
formula (
1.12
) and the same kernel bounds used below. Thus we assume

0<\delta\leq 1
.

From the covariance formula, or equivalently from the isometry used in the
proof of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{s}|C(t,r,\rho)-C(s,r,\rho)|^{2}\,dr\,d\rho

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{s}^{t}|C(t,r,\rho)|^{2}\,dr\,d\rho

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}(I_{1}+I_{2}).

(4.3)

We prove that
I_{1}+I_{2}\leq C_{T}\delta
. Set

\rho_{t}:=t^{-3/2}.

For
0<\rho\leq\rho_{t}
, all Airy arguments
\rho^{2/3}\tau
, with

0\leq\tau\leq t
, remain in a fixed bounded interval. Differentiating the
formula for
C
in the first time variable gives

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}C(\tau,r,\rho)=\pi\Big[\operatorname{Bi}^{\prime}(-\rho^{2/3}\tau)\operatorname{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}r)-\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime}(-\rho^{2/3}\tau)\operatorname{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}r)\Big],

which is uniformly bounded in this small-argument region. Since

C(r,r,\rho)=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle|C(t,r,\rho)|\leq C_{T}|t-r|,\qquad|C(t,r,\rho)-C(s,r,\rho)|\leq C_{T}\delta.

(4.4)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{t}}\int_{0}^{s}|C(t,r,\rho)-C(s,r,\rho)|^{2}\,dr\,d\rho

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\rho_{t}s\delta^{2}\leq C_{T}t^{-3/2}t\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\delta,

(4.5)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{t}}\int_{s}^{t}|C(t,r,\rho)|^{2}\,dr\,d\rho

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\rho_{t}\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}t^{-3/2}\delta^{3}\leq C_{T}\delta.

(4.6)

Here we used
s\leq t
,
\delta\leq t
, and
t\leq T
.

It remains to estimate the contribution of
\rho\geq\rho_{t}
. We first treat

I_{1}
. Split the
r
-integration into

0\leq r\leq\rho^{-2/3},\qquad\rho^{-2/3}\leq r\leq s.

The first interval corresponds to the region where the second Airy argument

\rho^{2/3}r
is small. In this region
\operatorname{Ai}(-\rho^{2/3}r)
and

\operatorname{Bi}(-\rho^{2/3}r)
are uniformly bounded. From the formula for
C
we
always have

|C(t,r,\rho)-C(s,r,\rho)|\leq C\rho^{-2/3}.

If
\rho\geq 1
, the derivative estimate (
4.2
) also gives

|C(t,r,\rho)-C(s,r,\rho)|\leq C_{T}\delta\rho^{1/6}.

The part
\rho_{t}\leq\rho\leq 1
contributes at most

C_{T}\delta^{2}\int_{\rho_{t}}^{1}\rho^{-2/3}\,d\rho\leq C_{T}\delta.

For the remaining part
\rho\geq\max\{\rho_{t},1\}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\max\{\rho_{t},1\}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\min\{s,\rho^{-2/3}\}}|C(t,r,\rho)-C(s,r,\rho)|^{2}\,dr\,d\rho

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{-2/3}\min\{\rho^{-4/3},\delta^{2}\rho^{1/3}\}\,d\rho\leq C_{T}\delta.

(4.7)

Indeed, splitting the last integral at
\rho=\delta^{-6/5}
gives a bound

C\delta^{6/5}\leq C\delta
, because
0<\delta\leq 1
.

We now consider the second region,
\rho^{-2/3}\leq r\leq s
. Then

\rho^{2/3}r\geq 1
, and hence also
\rho^{2/3}s\geq 1
. Using
(
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle|C(t,r,\rho)-C(s,r,\rho)|\leq C_{T}r^{-1/4}\min\{\rho^{-1}s^{-1/4},\delta t^{1/4}\}.

(4.8)

The first term in the minimum follows by estimating the difference by the sum
of the two values at
t
and
s
; the second follows by the mean-value
formula in the first time variable. Squaring (
4.8
)
and integrating first with respect to
\rho
, we use the elementary identity

\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{A\rho^{-2},B\}\,d\rho\leq 2\sqrt{AB},\qquad A,B>0.

With
A=s^{-1/2}
and
B=\delta^{2}t^{1/2}
, this gives

\displaystyle\int_{\rho_{t}}^{\infty}\int_{\min\{s,\rho^{-2/3}\}}^{s}|C(t,r,\rho)-C(s,r,\rho)|^{2}\,dr\,d\rho

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{s}r^{-1/2}\,dr\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{\rho^{-2}s^{-1/2},\delta^{2}t^{1/2}\}\,d\rho

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{T}s^{1/2}\delta t^{1/4}s^{-1/4}\leq C_{T}\delta.

(4.9)

Combining (
4.5
), (
4.7
), and
(
4.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq C_{T}\delta.

(4.10)

It remains to estimate
I_{2}
in the large-frequency region. For

\rho\geq\rho_{t}
and
0<r\leq t
, estimate (
4.1
) gives

\displaystyle|C(t,r,\rho)|\leq C_{T}\rho^{-1}(tr)^{-1/4}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\rho_{t}}^{\infty}\int_{s}^{t}|C(t,r,\rho)|^{2}\,dr\,d\rho

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\int_{\rho_{t}}^{\infty}\rho^{-2}\,d\rho\int_{s}^{t}(tr)^{-1/2}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\rho_{t}^{-1}t^{-1/2}(\sqrt{t}-\sqrt{s})

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}t^{3/2}t^{-1/2}\frac{t-s}{\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}t^{1/2}\delta\leq C_{T}\delta.

Together with (
4.6
), this proves

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq C_{T}\delta.

(4.11)

From (
4.3
), (
4.10
), and (
4.11
),
we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{2}\leq C_{T}|t-s|,\qquad 0\leq s,t\leq T.

Since the increment is Gaussian, for every
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|U(t,x)-U(s,x)|^{p}\leq C_{p}|t-s|^{p/2}.

Choosing
p>2
and applying Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem gives a
modification whose time paths are Hölder continuous of every order

\gamma<1/2
. This proves Corollary
1.3
.
