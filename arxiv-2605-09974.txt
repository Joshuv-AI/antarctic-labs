Localization phase diagram of the Hexagonal Lattice with irrational magnetic flux
Qi Gao, Shuo Zhang, Wei Chen

Abstract
We study the Hofstadter model on a hexaganal lattice with irrational magnetic flux in this work. The Hofstadter model of the square lattice with irrational flux has been solved mathematically by Avila in his Fields medal work. However, this theory is usually not applicable to lattices with internal degrees of freedom, such as spin or sublattices. In this work, we show that for the hexagonal lattice with only nearest neighbor hoppings, the system can still be characterized by a 2 ∗ 2 2*2 transfer matrix and solved exactly by Avila’s global theory although this lattice has two sublattices. We obtained the exact localization phase diagram of the hexagonal lattice with irrational flux by this theory, which reveals three pure phases, i.e., the extended, localized and critical states but no mobility edge due to the chiral symmetry. We used the renormalization group (RG) theory to verify these results, which can determine part of the phase diagram. We then computed the fractal dimension of the remaining part numerically. The results from both the RG theory and numerical analysis confirmed the phase diagram we get from Avila’s global theory. Our results can be tested in various hexagonal Moire lattices and artificial superlattices in recent experiments.

Discussions.
Recently, a type of one-dimensional spin-
1/2
QP model with the Hamiltonian of the form of Eq.(
2
) was investigated in Ref.

, for which the elements of hopping matrix
\Pi_{n}
and potential matrix

are either uniform or QP. The condition of the existence of mobility edge, critical states and exact solubility was discussed for the model.
Some of these discussions apply to our model in this work. For example, our model has chiral symmetry and as a consequence there is no mobility edge.
Also in our model
{\rm Det}\ \Pi_{n}=0
which is consistent with one of the conditions of exact solubility proposed in Ref.
Unification
. But the potential matrix in our model does not satisfy their second condition that the off-diagonal matrix elements are either constant or pure QP.
Moreover, the critical states in our model do not correspond to the existence of generalized incommensurate zeroes in the hopping matrix, as opposed to the proposal in Ref.
Unification
. Our model in this work then provides a different type of exactly solvable QP model from that in Ref.
Unification
.

Summary.
In summary, we studied the localization phase diagram of the hexagonal lattice with irrational flux. With only NN hopping, the system can be characterized by a
2*2
transfer matrix and the phase diagram can be obtained exactly by Avila’s global theory. We also applied the RG theory to study the model, which can determine part of the phase diagram. Finally, we studied the model by the fractal dimension analysis numerically. Both the results from the RG and fractal dimension analysis are consistent with what we obtained from Avila’s global theory. Our results can be tested in various recently achieved hexagonal Hofstadter models in experiments.

Appendix A
Relationship between the Lyapunov exponent and the localization length

In this Appendix, we present the relationship between the LE and the localization length.

We first note that the definition of the LE
\gamma(T)
given in Eq.(
10
) in the main text is equivalent to the following definition we use in the Appendix
Jitomirskaya2012
:

\gamma(T)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi n}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln||\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}(k_{1})||dk_{1}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln||\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}(k_{1})||.

(27)

To get the relationship between the LE and the localization length, we introduce the Oseledets Theorem:

Oseledets Theorem
(see Appendix G of Ref.
Jitomirskaya2012
): Let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
2*2
matrices in
GL(2,\mathbb{C}).
Set
d_{n}:=(\det A_{n})^{1/2},\ D_{n}:=\frac{1}{d_{n}}A_{n},\ n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
D_{n}\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, i.e.,
\det D_{n}=1
. Suppose that
m:=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\ln|d_{k}|
exists and is finite, and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln||A_{n}||=0
(e.g.,
A_{n}
is bounded), and
\gamma(A):=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln||A_{n}\cdots A_{1}||
exists and is finite with
\gamma(D)=\gamma(A)-m>0
, then there is a one-dimensional subspace
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
such that for a vector
v\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,

•

(i):
\frac{1}{n}\ln||A_{n}\cdots A_{1}v||\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}-\gamma(A)+2m
if
v\in S
,

•

(ii):
\frac{1}{n}\ln||A_{n}\cdots A_{1}v||\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\gamma(A)
if
v\in\mathbb{C}^{2}/S
.

Case (i) corresponds to an exponentially decaying state generated by the transfer matrix
D\equiv\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}D_{n}\cdots D_{1}
and (ii) an exponentially increasing state. For the reason, case (i) is the physical state we keep.

Suppose
A_{n}
is the transfer matrix of a physical system, i.e.,

\psi_{n}\equiv A_{n}\cdots A_{1}\psi_{0}
, where
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{n}
are the states on site
0
and
n
respectively,
for
A_{n}\in GL(2,\mathbb{C})
, there exists a state
\psi_{n}
such that

\frac{1}{n}\ln||\psi_{n}||\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}-\gamma(A)+2m\Rightarrow||\psi_{n}||\sim e^{-(\gamma(A)-2m)n}.

(28)

The localization length of the state
\psi_{n}
is
\xi=1/(\gamma(A)-2m)
, where
\gamma(A)
is the LE of matrix
A
. In the case
m=0
,
\xi=1/\gamma(A)
.

Appendix B
Proof of the non-negativity of Lyapunov exponent

In this appendix, we prove that the LE index
\gamma^{\epsilon\to 0}(T)
in our model is non-negative.

We first prove that
for any transfer matrix
D_{n}\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, its LE
\gamma(D)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln||D_{n}\cdots D_{1}||

Proof.

Suppose the eigenvalues of
D^{\dagger}D

\lambda_{1}(D),\lambda_{2}(D)

\lambda_{1}(D)\geq\lambda_{2}(D)\geq 0

which indicates that
\sigma_{1}(D)\geq 1\geq\sigma_{2}(D)\geq 0
.
We then adopt the spectral norm of matrix, which yields

∎

We then consider the transfer matrix in the hexagonal Hofstadter model in this work, which is
