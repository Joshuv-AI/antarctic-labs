Sales of cigarettes with WS-3, a synthetic cooling agent, in an area with a menthol cigarette sales prohibition
Authors: Jessica K Pepper, In Heon Lee, Alexander Maki, Taylor Lee, Jonathan Hannings, Apoorva O Rajan-Sharma, Kevin C Frissell, Jennifer K Bernat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe synthetic cooling agent WS-3 (N-ethyl-p-menthane-3-carboxamide) elicits a cooling sensation without a minty taste. In areas with menthol cigarette sales prohibitions, such as California, tobacco companies have marketed cigarettes with WS-3 as alternatives to menthol cigarettes. We examined sales of cigarettes with WS-3 in NielsenIQ’s Retail Measurement Services Pacific division, which includes the state of California.MethodsWith NielsenIQ data, we estimated sales of cigarettes with WS-3 from June 2022 to March 2024. We compared the NielsenIQ Pacific division, which includes California and two less populous states (Washington and Oregon), to all other divisions combined. Within the Pacific division, we compared trends in sales of cigarettes with WS-3 to menthol cigarettes.ResultsIn our study period, cigarettes with WS-3 appeared in the NielsenIQ Pacific division approximately 2 weeks before the implementation of California’s menthol cigarette prohibition in December 2022 and did not appear elsewhere in substantive quantities (>5 cartons) until Spring 2023. Most (87.0%) sales of cigarettes with WS-3 during the study period occurred in the Pacific division, peaking near 19 000 weekly carton sales. Weekly sales of Pacific division menthol cigarettes dropped by more than 100 000 cartons in 2 months after California’s prohibition.ConclusionIn the Pacific division, California’s menthol cigarette prohibition appears to have moderately reduced sales of menthol cigarettes and spurred sales of cigarettes with WS-3. Observationally, these sales data suggest that cigarettes with WS-3 only partially displaced the declining sales of menthol cigarettes. Future research could examine reasons for and experiences using cigarettes with WS-3.ImplicationsTobacco companies explicitly marketed cigarettes with the synthetic cooling agent WS-3 as replacements for menthol cigarettes. Sales data from NielsenIQ’s Pacific division suggest that California’s menthol cigarette prohibition may have reduced sales of menthol cigarettes and spurred the introduction of cigarettes with WS-3 into the marketplace. The findings suggest that sales of cigarettes with WS-3 did not fully offset the decrease in menthol cigarette sales.

Introduction

California Senate Bill 793 took effect on December 21, 2022, prohibiting sales of multiple types of flavored tobacco products, including menthol cigarettes.1 California was the second state, after Massachusetts,2 to prohibit the sale of menthol cigarettes. The tobacco industry acknowledges marketing non-menthol cooling cigarettes as a direct result of anticipated demand among people who use menthol cigarettes and can no longer obtain their preferred cigarettes due to state-level restrictions.3 Marketing for non-menthol cooling cigarettes explicitly targets consumers who use menthol cigarettes with packaging characteristics that signal similarities to menthol cigarettes (eg, green or blue package color) and advertising statements like “Heads Up. Menthol won’t be around for much longer in California, but we crafted two new non-menthol styles with a taste and smoking experience you’ll love.”4

Synthetic cooling agents elicit a cooling sensation like menthol but without the same minty taste. Non-menthol cooling cigarettes may contain various synthetic cooling agents, such as the synthetic cooling agent WS-3 (N-ethyl-p-menthane-3-carboxamide). Similar to menthol, synthetic cooling agents like WS-3 are known to agonize cold-menthol receptor TRPM8, which leads to the cooling sensation experienced when using tobacco products that contain them.5,6

Recent studies have documented the use of synthetic cooling agents in a range of tobacco products, including cigarettes, e-liquids, and oral nicotine pouches,6–9 but research about consumer perceptions of synthetic cooling agents in tobacco products is limited. One recent study examined perceptions of e-liquids with WS-23 (N,2,3-trimethyl-2-propan-2-ylbutanamide), another type of synthetic cooling agent. Users found the e-liquids with WS-23 to be more appealing than e-liquids without a coolant and smoother, cooler, and less harsh than e-liquids with menthol.10 To our knowledge, there is no research about consumer perceptions of any synthetic cooling agents in cigarettes.

Retail sales data can shed light on the popularity of non-menthol cooling cigarettes that may act as substitutes for menthol cigarettes in areas with sales restrictions. In this study, we utilized sales data from NielsenIQ to quantify and examine changes in sales of cigarettes with WS-3 and menthol cigarettes in areas with and without menthol cigarette sales prohibitions.

Methods

To analyze sales of cigarettes with WS-3, we utilized NielsenIQ Retail Measurement Services data. NielsenIQ collects sales data of nicotine and tobacco products through product bar code checkout scanners at registers, coding of advertisements and similar written materials, and field auditing to observe in-store promotions. NielsenIQ’s proprietary statistical methods estimate unit sales by Universal Product Code. NielsenIQ provides the U.S. Food and Drug Administration Center for Tobacco Products with licensed data in weekly increments for nicotine and tobacco product sales.

We included only cigarette products publicly known to have WS-3 as an ingredient: Camel Crisp Green, Newport EXP MAX Non-Menthol, Newport Non-Menthol Green, and Newport EXP MIX Non-Menthol.11 Although Kool Non-Menthol and Camel Crush Oasis Non-Menthol (Blue, Silver, and Green) have been marketed as menthol replacements, we excluded them from our analyses because they did not contain WS-3.9 To determine if an entry in the NielsenIQ data matched our list of selected sub-brands, we inspected NielsenIQ’s item (product description), brand-high (brand name), brand-low (sub-brand name), and flavor variables. We also searched the NielsenIQ data for Universal Product Codes associated with relevant products identified in state tobacco directory websites.12–14 From the NielsenIQ data, we identified a total of 31 product-package combinations that met the criteria to be categorized as one of the cigarette products publicly confirmed to include WS-3.

For this study, we analyzed sales by NielsenIQ Retail Measurement Services divisions, which are based on US census divisions. The US Census Bureau’s Pacific division includes Washington, Oregon, California, Alaska, and Hawaii15; however, the NielsenIQ data licensed by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration do not cover sales from Alaska or Hawaii. Therefore, for this study, the “Pacific division” refers to the NielsenIQ Pacific division (California, Oregon, and Washington). The NielsenIQ data licensed by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration are at the division-level rather than the state-level. We use the Pacific division as a proxy for California, which has a statewide prohibition on menthol cigarette sales, because California has a much larger smoking population than the other two states in the Pacific division combined; California has approximately 3 million adults who smoke compared to 400 000 in Oregon and 600 000 in Washington.16,17

We analyzed sales from approximately 6 months prior to the date that California’s menthol prohibition was enacted (the week ending June 4, 2022) until the most recently available data point at the time of the analyses (the week ending March 16, 2024). We calculated the total volume sales of cigarettes with WS-3 by carton (rounded to the nearest 1000 cartons) for the Pacific division and other divisions, and the percentage of total cigarette sales accounted for by cigarettes with WS-3 in the Pacific division and other divisions (ie, the combined sales of all divisions excluding the Pacific division). We also calculated sales of menthol cigarettes in the Pacific division to examine how sales of menthol cigarettes changed as cigarettes with WS-3 were introduced.

Results

In our study period, NielsenIQ first captured cigarettes with WS-3 on the market in the Pacific division the week ending December 10, 2022, approximately 2 weeks before the California menthol cigarette prohibition went into effect. In contrast, during our study period, cigarettes with WS-3 did not appear on the market in substantive quantities (>5 cartons) in other divisions until Spring 2023.

Between the week ending December 10, 2022 and the week ending March 16, 2024, there were approximately 1 022 000 cartons of cigarettes with WS-3 sold in the Pacific division and 153 000 cartons sold in other divisions (see Figure 1 for sales by week). During this timeframe, sales of cigarettes with WS-3 in the Pacific division constituted 87.0% of all sales of cigarettes with WS-3 in the U.S. Peak weekly sales of cigarettes with WS-3 for the Pacific division reached about 19 000 cartons (occurring the week ending March 2, 2024). Peak weekly sales of cigarettes with WS-3 for all other divisions combined reached only 4000 cartons (occurring the week ending September 2, 2023).

Sales of cigarettes with WS-3 by week for the Pacific division and other divisions.

Across the time period, median weekly sales of cartons of cigarettes with WS-3 comprised 1.84% of the total cigarette sales in the Pacific division, qualitatively higher than median percent of sales in other divisions (0.02%). Peak sales of cigarettes with WS-3 as a percentage of total cigarette sales for the Pacific division were 2.45% (occurring the week ending March 9, 2024), and peak sales of cigarettes with WS-3 for the rest of the country were 0.03% (occurring the week ending September 2, 2023).

Sales of menthol cigarettes decreased slightly in the approximately 6 months before the prohibition, from about 210 000 cartons the week ending June 4, 2022 to 162 000 cartons the week ending December 17, 2022. Immediately after the implementation of the prohibition, weekly sales of cigarettes with WS-3 in the Pacific division rose dramatically, while weekly sales of menthol cigarettes decreased from approximately 162 000 cartons to 74 000 cartons during the first 2 months after the implementation and remained relatively unchanged until the end of the analysis period (Figure 2).

Discussion

During the almost 2 years covered in this analysis, nearly 1.2 million cartons of cigarettes with WS-3, equivalent to nearly 240 million cigarettes, were sold nationwide in locations covered by NielsenIQ data. The timing and location of the sales are consistent with the proposition that tobacco companies changed production and marketing practices in response to restrictions of menthol cigarettes. Specifically, during our analysis period, cigarettes with WS-3 first appeared in the NielsenIQ data for the Pacific division the same month as California’s prohibition on selling menthol cigarettes went into effect, and the Pacific division sold the majority of cigarettes with WS-3 in the United States

Our analyses examined changes in the sales of menthol cigarettes when cigarettes with WS-3 came on the market. Observationally, the increase in sales of cigarettes with WS-3 was less than the decrease in menthol cigarette sales. If individuals who smoked menthol cigarettes were substituting cigarettes with WS-3 (which cannot be determined using sales data alone), it would suggest cigarettes with WS-3 only partially displaced the decrease in sales of menthol cigarettes. The failure of cigarettes with WS-3 to fully replace the drop in menthol cigarette sales may be due to low familiarity with cigarettes with WS-3; negative experiences with cigarettes with WS-3; minimal marketing by the tobacco industry; consumers switching to other products, like non-cooling (tobacco flavor) cigarettes or electronic nicotine delivery systems, when they could no longer obtain menthol cigarettes; or consumers quitting or reducing cigarette smoking. Although the goal of our analyses was not to document the marketplace-wide implications of the menthol prohibition, we also examined sales of traditional (non-menthol, non-WS-3) cigarettes in the Pacific division during this time frame. There was no pattern that would suggest switching from menthol to traditional cigarettes; the sale of traditional cigarettes declined slightly in the 4 months leading up to the implementation of the menthol prohibition but remained roughly stable, typically between 650 and 800 million cartons weekly, thereafter (data not shown). There was also no evidence that consumers in the Pacific division bought more menthol cigarettes in anticipation of the upcoming prohibition in California.

The present findings are limited by the availability and coverage of NielsenIQ sales data. Specifically, NielsenIQ does not include all store types (eg, online stores, bodegas), does not account for illicit sales of menthol cigarettes, and there is a time lag before products appear in their database. NielsenIQ data are based on projections and should be viewed as estimates rather than direct reflections of actual product use. Further, we were unable to obtain state-specific results, only division-level results. The NielsenIQ Pacific division includes three states; of those, only California has a statewide prohibition on menthol cigarettes. The proportion of cigarette sales accounted for by cigarettes with WS-3 would likely be higher if data were limited to California. The lack of state-level data also impacts our interpretation of comparisons because the comparison group (ie, all other divisions combined) contains some locations that fully or partially restrict sales of menthol cigarettes, such as Massachusetts18 and Chicago, Illinois.19 Finally, this analysis was based on publicly available ingredient information, and there may be other cigarettes containing WS-3 or other non-menthol cooling agents such as WS-23. Despite these limitations, this study found evidence suggesting that tobacco companies may be marketing cigarettes with WS-3 in response to state-level restrictions and that cigarettes with WS-3 may have at least partially displaced menthol cigarettes following California’s prohibition.

Future research could shed light upon the use behaviors of those who smoke cigarettes with WS-3, as well as whether perceptions or reasons for use vary by individual factors, like preference for menthol vs non-menthol cigarettes. Future research could also examine use experiences of those using cigarettes with WS-3. Following the close of our study period, California amended its bill restricting the sale of tobacco products with characterizing flavors.20 Per the amendment signed in September 2024, the definition of a characterizing flavor now includes “a cooling sensation distinguishable by an ordinary consumer.” This definition could include synthetic cooling agents like WS-3. Whether and how the tobacco industry responds to this change is not yet known. Future surveillance could examine the impact on sales of cigarettes with WS-3.
