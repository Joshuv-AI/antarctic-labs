 Nuclear Structure Studies for Schiff Moment Enhancement

 Work performed May - July 2026

 E. A. Hinrichs, UNM LANL T-2

 Abstract

 One of the major open questions in modern physics is the origin of the matter-antimatter

  asymmetry observed in the universe. While the Standard Model contains sources of charge-

parity (CP) violation, they appear insufficient to explain the observed dominance of matter over

antimatter. As a result, searches for additional sources of CP violation are an important area of

 contemporary nuclear and particle physics. Atomic electric dipole moments (EDMs) provide

sensitive probes of CP-violating interactions. In heavy nuclei, the observable atomic EDM can

be enhanced through the presence of a nuclear Schiff moment, which arises from CP-violating

nuclear forces and nuclear structure effects. The review paper Nuclear Schiff Moments and CP

Violation by Jonathan Engel [1] provides an overview of the theoretical framework connecting

  CP violation, nuclear structure, and Schiff moments. The long-term goal of this project is to

contribute to the identification and characterization of nuclei that may exhibit enhanced Schiff

  moments. Such nuclei are often associated with strong octupole correlations or low-energy

parity-doublet structures that can amplify CP-violating effects. This report summarizes progress

 made during the initial phase of the project, including a survey of candidate isotopes and

 preparation for nuclear-structure calculations using the HFODD code.

1. Schiff Moments

 1. CP-violating forces create a tiny intrinsic Schiff moment.

 o If CP symmetry is violated in the nucleus, the nuclear charge distribution acquires

 a slight asymmetry.

 o This asymmetry produces a Schiff moment, which can induce an atomic EDM.

 2. Most nuclei have relatively small Schiff moments.

 o In a typical spherical or weakly deformed nucleus, the effect is modest because

 opposite-parity states are not strongly mixed. (CP-violating interactions do not

 conserve parity. Such an interaction can therefore “mix” states of opposing

 parity.)

 3. Certain nuclear structures amplify the effect.

 o Nuclei with strong octupole (pear-shaped) deformation have intrinsic reflection

 asymmetry.

 o These nuclei often possess nearly degenerate positive- and negative-parity states

 ("parity doublets").

 o CP-violating interactions can mix these states much more efficiently because the

 energy denominator is small.

2. Survey of Candidate Isotopes

A major component of the project has been the construction of spreadsheets containing candidate

nuclei that may be relevant for Schiff-moment studies. Electric octupole (E3) transitions are

often associated with octupole collectivity and reflection-asymmetric nuclear shapes.

Consequently, identifying nuclei that exhibit E3 transitions may help identify candidates with

enhanced Schiff moments.

The survey was performed by examining nuclear data tables (primarily from RIPL) and level

schemes for isotopes that exhibit possible octupole collectivity. Particular attention was given to

nuclei with:

 • Low-lying negative-parity states

 • Potential E3 gamma-ray transitions connecting excited states to the ground state

 • Potential E1 gamma-ray transitions connecting excited states to the ground state

For each isotope investigated, the following information was collected when available:

 • Ground-state spin and parity

 • Excited-state energies

 • Reduced transition probabilities

 • Whether the decay is a potential E3 or E1 transition

 • Transitions with potential decay to ground state

3. LANSCE Experimentation

The initial experimental work focused on 129Xe, which has long been recognized as an excellent

system for precision electric dipole moment (EDM) measurements. Unlike nuclei such as 225Ra

that are expected to exhibit large Schiff-moment enhancements due to octupole deformation,
129
 Xe was selected because of its favorable experimental properties and the maturity of xenon-

based spin-manipulation techniques.

Several characteristics make 129Xe particularly well suited for EDM experiments. It is a stable

isotope with a nuclear spin of ½, allowing long nuclear spin coherence times that are essential for

precision measurements. The isotope can be polarized efficiently using spin-exchange optical

pumping (SEOP), producing highly polarized xenon gas that can be stored in sealed

measurement cells for extended periods. Long coherence times increase the statistical sensitivity

of an EDM search by allowing the nuclear spin to precess in applied electric and magnetic fields

for many hundreds or thousands of seconds.

Another important advantage is that xenon is a chemically inert noble gas. Because it does not

readily react with container materials or impurities, systematic effects associated with chemistry

and surface interactions are significantly reduced. The atomic structure of xenon is also well

understood, enabling accurate calculations of the relationship between a measured atomic EDM

and the underlying nuclear Schiff moment.

An EDM experiment at LANSCE was therefore designed as both a competitive search for the

EDM of 129Xe and as a platform for developing experimental techniques. The apparatus

combines polarized 129Xe gas with precision magnetic shielding, stable electric fields, and

sensitive magnetometry to detect the extremely small changes in spin-precession frequency that

would indicate the presence of an EDM. The methods developed with 129Xe provide valuable

experience in controlling systematic uncertainties and improving measurement sensitivity.

Although 129Xe is not predicted to possess the large octupole-enhanced Schiff moments expected

in certain pear-shaped nuclei, it serves as an important benchmark system. Its relatively simple

nuclear structure makes theoretical calculations more reliable, allowing experimental results to

be interpreted with smaller nuclear-structure uncertainties. At the same time, the techniques

established using 129Xe can be adapted to future experiments involving isotopes with potentially

much larger Schiff moments, where enhanced sensitivity to CP-violating interactions is

expected.

4. Nuclear Structure Calculations with HFODD

The next stage of the project involves identifying and using a nuclear structure code capable of

computing magnetic moments. HFODD (Hartree-Fock plus Orbital Dependent Density) was

selected for this purpose. HFODD is a self-consistent mean-field code capable of performing

calculations for deformed nuclei within the Hartree-Fock and Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov

frameworks. The code allows the study of nuclear shapes, multipole moments, and other

observables relevant to Schiff-moment calculations.

During the initial phase of the project, input files for HFODD calculations were prepared to

facilitate future calculations. The current plan is to use these input files to compute magnetic

moments and potentially related nuclear properties for selected isotopes identified during the

survey phase.

In the longer term, these calculations may be extended to investigate octupole deformation and

other nuclear structure effects that influence Schiff-moment enhancement.

5. Future Work

Several tasks remain for expansion of the project:

 • Extend and verify the isotope survey database.

 • Continue studying the theoretical foundations of Schiff moments and CP violation.

 • Learn the operation and input structure of HFODD.

 • Perform test calculations for selected nuclei.

 • Compute magnetic moments and compare them with experimental values.

 • Investigate possible indicators of octupole collectivity within the calculated nuclear

 structures.

 • Identify promising isotopes for future Schiff-moment calculations.

6. Conclusion

This project focuses on nuclear Schiff moments as probes of CP-violating physics beyond the

Standard Model. Initial work has concentrated on building a database of candidate isotopes

through examination of nuclear data tables and potential E3 gamma transitions to the ground

state. In parallel, preparations have begun for nuclear structure calculations using the HFODD

code. The ultimate goal is to identify nuclei with enhanced Schiff moments that could provide

increased sensitivity in future searches for CP violation through atomic electric dipole moment

measurements.
