The Scale of ( P P )-closed Groups Acting On Trees
Marcus Chijoff, Michal Ferov, and Stephan Tornier

Abstract
. Reid–Smith parametrised ( P P )-closed groups acting on trees using graph-based combinatorial structures known as local action diagrams. Properties of the acting (topological) group, such as being locally compact, compactly generated, discrete or simple, are reflected in its local action diagram. In this article, we describe the translations of ( P P )-closed groups and their axes in terms of local action diagrams. As applications, we determine the scale function of ( P P )-closed groups and characterise unimodular as well as uniscalar ( P P )-closed groups. The latter provides one possible answer to a question of Thomas Weigel.



1.
Introduction

In the general theory of totally disconnected, locally compact (t.d.l.c.) groups, groups acting on trees play an important role for theoretical and practical reasons.

On the theoretical side, due to the Cayley–Abels graph construction
[
7
]
, every compactly generated t.d.l.c. group acts vertex-transitively on a connected regular graph
\Gamma
of finite degree. The action of
G
on
\Gamma
lifts to an action on its universal cover, which is a regular tree. Note that when
G
is discrete, compact generation amounts to finite generation and
\Gamma
is a standard Cayley graph of
G
.

On the practical side, groups acting on trees constitute a particularly accessible class of t.d.l.c. groups which makes them a popular testing ground for conjectures about general t.d.l.c. groups and the construction of (counter)examples.

Many useful classes of groups acting on trees have been defined and parametrised. This includes Burger–Mozes’
[
3
]
universal groups
\mathrm{U}(F)
, classifying locally transitive (
P
)-closed groups that contain an edge inversion, Smith’s
[
15
]
universal groups
\mathrm{U}(F_{1},F_{2})
, classifying locally transitive (
P
)-closed groups preserving the bipartition of a biregular tree, Tornier’s
[
17
]
generalised universal groups
\mathrm{U}_{k}(F)
, classifying locally transitive (
P_{k}
)-closed (
k\in\mathbb{N}
) groups that contain an involutive edge inversion, as well as Radu’s
[
11
]
groups, classifying locally alternating or symmetric groups that act boundary-
2
-transitively on sufficiently thick trees.

In striking recent work, Reid–Smith
[
13
]
achieved a parametrisation of general (
P
)-closed groups acting on trees in terms of
local action diagrams
, see Section
2.4
.

Definition
2.5

(
[
13
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

A
local action diagram

\Delta
is a triple
(\Gamma,(X_{a})_{a\in A\Gamma},(G(v))_{v\in V\Gamma})
consisting of

(1)

a connected graph
\Gamma=(V\Gamma,A\Gamma,o,t,r)
,

(2)

(

), and

(3)

(

), where

, such that the sets

(
\smash{a\in o^{-1}(v)}
) are precisely the orbits of
G(v)
.

Each
X_{a}
is a
colour set
, its elements are
colours
, and each

is a

.

Reid–Smith introduce isomorphisms for local action diagrams and establish a one-to-one correspondence of their isomorphism classes with isomorphism classes of actions
(T,G)
, where

is a tree and

is (
P
)-closed. Given any group

with data coming from the group action. Conversely, for every local action diagram

\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{T},(G(v))_{v})\leq\Aut(\mathbf{T})

\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{T}}(\Delta)\backslash\mathbf{T}\cong\Gamma

Subsequently, they describe various properties of the group

in terms of its local action diagram, including local compactness, compact generation and simplicity.

In this article, we describe the translations of a (
P
)-closed group
G
and their translation axes in terms of
translatable circuits
of the local action diagram
\Delta
of

.

Definition

and
3.4
.

Let
\Delta=(\Gamma,(X_{a}),(G(v)))
be a local action diagram and

. A

We show that the scale of a translation (equivalently, of its conjugacy class) depends only on the

Theorem

Let
\Delta

\Delta

With a view towards a GAP package for local action diagrams, we rephrase Bass–Kulkarni’s
[
2

unimodularity criterion for groups acting on trees in terms of local action diagrams. In particular, this explicitly identifies unimodularity as a locally determined global property in the sense of Reid–Smith
[

Finally, we characterise uniscalarity of (

)-closed groups in terms of local action diagrams and obtain the following structural description.

Theorem
6.3
.

Let
\Delta=(\Gamma,(X_{a}),(G(v)))
be a local action diagram with finite orbits,
\mathbf{T}=(T,\pi,\mathcal{L})
be a
\Delta

) or contains a compact open normal subgroup.

This provides one possible answer to a question of Thomas Weigel for classes of t.d.l.c. groups in which uniscalarity implies being compact-by-discrete: the class of non-horocyclic
(P)
-closed groups acting on trees with compact vertex stabilisers.

2.1.
Permutation Groups

Let
\Omega
be a set. In this section we collect definitions concerning
\Sym(\Omega)
, the group of bijections from
\Omega
to itself, called the

of
\Omega
. When
\Omega=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, the group
\Sym(\Omega)
is also denoted by

.

A subgroup
G\leq\Sym(\Omega)
is a
permutation group
. For
g\in G
and
\omega\in\Omega
we write

for the image of
\omega
under
g
. The
orbit
of
\omega
is
G\omega:=\{g\omega\mid g\in G\}
and the set of orbits is
G\backslash\Omega:=\{G\omega\mid\omega\in\Omega\}
. For

we define
gA:=\{ga\mid a\in A\}
and
GA:=\{ga\mid a\in A,\ g\in G\}
. The
stabiliser
of
\omega\in\Omega
is
G_{\omega}:=\{g\in G\mid g\omega=\omega\}
. The
pointwise stabiliser
of
A\subseteq\Omega
is
G_{A}:=\{g\in G\mid\forall a\in A:\ ga=a\}
. The
setwise stabiliser
of
A
is
G_{\{A\}}:=\{g\in G\mid\forall a\in A:ga\in A\}
. The
rigid stabiliser
of
A
is
\mathrm{rist}_{G}(A):=G_{\Omega\backslash A}
. The set
A
is
G
-invariant
if
gA=A
for all
g\in G
. In this case, the
restriction
of
G
to
A
yields a subgroup of
\Sym(A)
, denoted
G^{A}
, isomorphic to
G/G_{A}
. A group
G\leq\Sym(\Omega)
is
semiregular
if
G_{\omega}=1
for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

2.2.
Permutation Topology

Let
X
be a set. The left translates of pointwise stabilisers of finite subsets of
X
form the basis of the
permutation topology
on
\Sym(X)
. This topology turns
\Sym(X)
into a Hausdorff, totally disconnected group, see e.g.
[
7
]
and
[
20
]
. For the reader’s convenience, we rephrase the results of the references above in the context relevant here.

For
n\in\bbN
and
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in X^{n}
, define

U_{x,y}:=\{g\in\Sym(X)\mid\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:\ g(x_{i})=y_{i}\}.

Recall that a topological space is
zero-dimensional
if it admits a basis consisting of closed open sets and that a Hausdorff zero-dimensional space is totally disconnected.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a set. Then
\Sym(X)
is Hausdorff and totally disconnected.

Proof.

To see that
\Sym(X)
is Hausdorff, let
g,h\in\Sym(X)
be distinct. Then there is an element
x\in X
such that
g(x)\neq h(x)
. Therefore,
U_{x,g(x)}
and
U_{x,h(x)}
are disjoint open sets containing
g
and
h
respectively.

For zero-dimensionality, note that the sets
U_{x,y}
for
x,y\in X^{n}
and
n\in\bbN
are open by definition. Now consider
g\in\Sym(X)\backslash U_{x,y}
. Then there is
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
g(x_{i})\neq y_{i}
and
U_{x,g(x)}\subseteq\Sym(X)\backslash U_{x,y}
contains
g
. Hence the assertion.
∎

We now characterise compact sets of the permutation topology on
\Sym(X)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a set equipped with the discrete topology. Then the action map
\Phi:\Sym(X)\times X\to X
given by
(g,x)\mapsto g(x)
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
Y\subseteq X
(be open). Then
\Phi^{-1}(Y)=\{(g,x)\in\Sym(X)\times X\mid g(x)\in Y\}
. Hence, if
(g,x)\in\Phi^{-1}(Y)
then so is the open set
U_{x,g(x)}\times\{x\}
containing
(g,x)
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a set and
H\leq\Sym(X)
. Then
H
is compact if and only if
H\leq\Sym(X)
is closed and all its orbits are finite.

Proof.

If
H
is compact, then
H
is closed in
\Sym(X)
as
\Sym(X)
is Hausdorff. Furthermore,
Hx=\Phi|_{H\times\{x\}}
is compact because
\Phi
is continuous and hence finite.

Conversely, assume that
H\leq\Sym(X)
is closed and has finite orbits
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
. Then
H\leq\prod_{i\in I}\Sym(X_{i})
. Since every
X_{i}
is finite,
\Sym(X_{i})
is compact and hence so is
\prod_{i\in I}\Sym(X_{i})
by Tychonoff’s theorem. Therefore, the conclusion follows if we show that the inclusion map
\prod_{i\in I}\Sym(X_{i})\to\Sym(X)
is continuous. Indeed, an intersection
U_{x,y}\cap\prod_{i\in I}\Sym(X_{i})
restricts only finitely many factors and hence gives rise to an open subset of the product topology.
∎

2.3.
Graphs

There are inequivalent definitions for many graph-theoretic concepts. The following ones, based on
[
13
]

A

\Gamma=(V,A,o,t,r)
consists of a vertex set
V
, an arc set

,

and
terminus
maps

and a

map

and

. For

, the pair

is an

. An arc

. Note that a loop

may be either

, i.e.

, or

, i.e.
a=\overline{a}
. A graph is

if it contains no loops and for every

there is at most one

such that

. In this case, an arc

.

A

such that
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
,

,

, and

. For a subset

the subgraph

by

has vertex set

and arc set

.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
be graphs. A

\theta
from
\Gamma
to
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a pair of maps
\theta_{V}:V\Gamma\to V\Gamma^{\prime}
and
\theta_{A}:A\Gamma\to A\Gamma^{\prime}
such that we have
\theta_{V}(o(a))=o(\theta_{A}(a))
and
r(\theta_{A}(a))=\theta_{A}(r(a))
for all
a\in A
. If
\theta_{V}
and
\theta_{A}
are bijective then

is an
isomorphism
. An isomorphism from
\Gamma
to itself is an

. The set of all automorphisms of
\Gamma
is denoted by
\Aut(\Gamma)
and forms a group under composition, which we equip with the permutation topology for its action on
V\Gamma
.

For a graph
\Gamma=(V,A,o,t,r)
and a group
G\leq\Aut(\Gamma)
there is a well-defined
quotient graph

G\backslash\Gamma\!:=\!(G\backslash V,G\backslash A,o^{\prime},t^{\prime},r^{\prime})
with
o^{\prime}(Ga)\!:=\!Go(a)
,
t^{\prime}(Ga)\!:=\!Gt(a)
and
r^{\prime}(Ga):=Gr(a)
(

), and quotient homomorphism
\pi=\pi_{(\Gamma,G)}:\Gamma\to G\backslash\Gamma
.

For an index set
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, put
I^{\prime}:=\{i\in I\mid i+1\in I\}
. A
path
in
\Gamma
indexed by
I
is a sequence of vertices
(v_{i})_{i\in I}
and edges
(\{a_{i},\overline{a_{i}}\})_{i\in I^{\prime}}
of
\Gamma
such that
\{a_{i},\overline{a_{i}}\}
is an edge between
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
for all
i\in I^{\prime}
. The

of a path indexed by
I
is
|I^{\prime}|
. A

only includes arcs
a_{i}
such that
o(a_{i})=v_{i}
and
t(a_{i})=v_{i+1}
for all
i\in I^{\prime}
. The
reverse
of a directed path
P
, denoted
\overline{P}
, only contains the arcs
\overline{a_{i}}

(i\in I^{\prime})
. A (directed) path is
simple
if all its vertices are distinct. A
ray
(respectively

) in a simple graph is a simple directed path indexed by
\mathbb{N}_{0}
(respectively
\mathbb{Z}
). Two rays are equivalent if there is another ray that contains infinitely many vertices of both of them. An
end
of
\Gamma
is an equivalence class of rays. The set of all ends of
\Gamma
is denoted by
\partial\Gamma
. A
cycle
of length

in
\Gamma
is a vertex together with two distinct, mutually reverse loops. A cycle of length

in
\Gamma
is a pair of distinct vertices together with two pairs of mutually reverse arcs connecting them. A cycle of length

in
\Gamma
is a path indexed by
I=\{0,1,\dots,n\}
such that
v_{0}=v_{n}
but otherwise all vertices are distinct. A

of length

is a directed path indexed by
I=\{0,\ldots,n\}
such that
v_{0}=v_{n}
. We let
C_{\Gamma}
denote the set of all circuits of
\Gamma
. Given

, the

between

is the minimal length of a path between

and
v
, if one exists, and infinity otherwise. For

and

, the

of radius

around

is the subgraph induced by

. Similarly, the

of radius

around

. A graph is

An

of a graph

. A

of

.

2.4.
Local Action Diagrams

Property (
P
) was first introduced as an independence property by Tits
[
16
]
to prove the simplicity of certain groups acting on trees. For closed actions, the following easier-to-state generalisation due to Banks–Elder–Willis
[
1
]
includes Tits’ Property (
P
) as the case

.

Definition 2.4

(
[
1
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Let
T
be a tree and
G\leq\Aut(T)
. The
(P_{k})
-
closure
(

) of

is the group

G^{(P_{k})}=\{h\in\Aut(T)\mid\forall v\in V(T)\ \forall X\underset{\!\!\!\text{finite}\!\!\!}{\subseteq}V(B(v,k))\ \exists g\in G:\ g|_{X}=h|_{X}\}.

If
G=G^{(P_{k})}
then

is
(P_{k})
-
closed
, or satisfies
Property
(
P_{k}
).

Retain the notation of Definition
2.4
. When

is locally finite, the quantification over finite sets

may be replaced by considering just

itself. In either case, one can show that
\smash{G^{(P_{0})}\geq G^{(P_{1})}\geq G^{(P_{2})}\geq\!\cdots\!\geq G^{(P_{k})}\geq\!\cdots\!\geq\overline{G}\geq G}
as well as
\smash{\left(G^{(P_{k})}\right)^{(P_{k})}=G^{(P_{k})}}
and

. This suggests parametrising (closed) subgroups of

by parametrising (

)-closed groups and forming intersections. Finally, we also say

-closed
instead of

-closed.

In

, Reid–Smith introduce a powerful parametrisation of (

)-closed groups based on the following combinatorial structure. See also the survey article

]

Definition 2.5

(
[

)

A

(2)

(

(3)

Each

, its elements are

Reid–Smith introduce a notion of isomorphism for local action diagrams and show in

that there is a one-to-one correspondence between isomorphism classes of (

Definition 2.6

(
[
13
, Definition 3.6]
)

Let

be a tree and

\Delta(T,G)=(\Gamma,(X_{a}),(G(v)))

(1)

Put

(2)

For every

(3)

For every

be the closure of the permutation group induced on

by

.

To pass from a local action diagram to a group acting on a tree we first describe how to obtain a tree projecting onto

(

Let

-

2.5.
Willis theory

Finally, we recall central definitions and results of Willis theory. Let
G
be a t.d.l.c. group. In
[
18
]
, Willis introduced the notions of
scale
of an automorphism
\alpha
of
G
and
tidiness
of a compact open subgroup
U
of
G
for
\alpha
.

In this setting, note that
[\alpha(U):\alpha(U)\cap U]\in\bbN
because
\alpha(U)
is compact and
\alpha(U)\cap U
is open in
\alpha(U)
. Following
[
19
]
, the
scale
of
\alpha
is

{\@index{\@indexphrase{scale}}}{\@index[ii]{\@indexphrase{$s(\alpha)$}}}s(\alpha):=\min\big\{[\alpha(U):\alpha(U)\cap U]\mid U\leq G\text{ compact open}\big\}.

A compact open subgroup
U\leq G
is
minimising
if
[\alpha(U):\alpha(U)\cap U]=s(\alpha)
. It is a cornerstone of Willis theory that a compact open subgroup of
G
is minimising for
\alpha
if and only if it is
tidy
. Tidiness is phrased in terms of the following subgroups of
G
. Put
U_{0}:=U
. For
n\in\bbN_{0}
, we define
U_{\pm n}=\bigcap_{k=0}^{n}\alpha^{\pm k}(U)
. Now set

U_{+}:=\bigcap_{n\in\bbN_{0}}U_{n}=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{k}(U),\qquad U_{-}:=\bigcap_{n\in\bbN_{0}}U_{-n}=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{-k}(U),

\ U_{++}:=\bigcup_{n\in\bbN_{0}}\alpha^{n}(U_{+})\qquad\text{and}\qquad U_{--}:=\bigcup_{n\in\bbN_{0}}\alpha^{-n}(U_{-}).

The following verbal descriptions of the above subgroups may serve as a mnemonic.

U_{-}=\left\{\text{\begin{tabular}[]{c}elements of $U$ whose\\
$\alpha$-trajectory is contained in $U$\end{tabular}}\right\},

U_{+}=\left\{\text{\begin{tabular}[]{c}elements of $U$ whose\\
$\alpha^{-1}$-trajectory is contained in $U$\end{tabular}}\right\},

U_{--}=\left\{\text{\begin{tabular}[]{c}elements of $G$ whose $\alpha$-trajectory\\
is eventually contained in $U$\end{tabular}}\right\},

U_{++}=\left\{\text{\begin{tabular}[]{c}elements of $G$ whose $\alpha^{-1}$-trajectory\\
is eventually contained in $U$\end{tabular}}\right\}.

The subgroup
U
is
tidy above
for
\alpha
if
U=U_{+}U_{-}
, and
tidy below
for
\alpha
if
U_{++}
is closed. It is
tidy
for
\alpha
if it is both tidy above and tidy below for
\alpha
. The cornerstone result of Willis theory now reads as follows.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a t.d.l.c. group,
\alpha\in\Aut(G)
and
U\leq G
compact open. Then
U
is minimising for
\alpha
if and only if it is tidy for
\alpha
.

5.
Unimodular Groups

In
[
2
, Proposition 3.6]
, Bass–Kulkarni provide a formula for the modular function and an associated unimodularity criterion for groups acting on locally finite trees without inversion in terms of the associated Bass–Serre graph of groups. See also
[
8
, Proposition 7.1]
. We rephrase this result and its proof in terms of local action diagrams. In doing so, we extend the framework to non-locally finite trees as long as the group has compact vertex stabilisers, treat groups acting with or without inversions via the same argument, and highlight the local nature of the criterion.

In fact, we derive that being unimodular is a locally determined global property for such groups in the sense of Reid–Smith
[
13
, Section 8]
. Moreover, with a view towards a work-in-progress GAP package for local action diagrams, we make the unimodularity criterion computable in the cocompact case by reducing it to a finite condition. Finally, we remark that in the case of (
P
)-closed groups the unimodularity criterion can also be obtained using the theory developed in Sections
3
and
4
.

Given a locally compact group
G
, let
\Delta_{G}:G\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\ast}
denote its modular function. That is, for any left Haar measure
\mu
on
G
, element
g\in G
and measurable set
E\subseteq G
we have
\mu(Eg)=\Delta_{G}(g)\mu(E)
.

Next, we consider a local action diagram
\Delta=(\Gamma,(X_{a}),(G(v)))
with finite orbits. Let
C_{\Gamma}
denote the set of circuits of
\Gamma
and let
\mathbb{Z}[A\Gamma]
be the free abelian group of finitely supported functions from
A\Gamma
to
\mathbb{Z}
. Define
\nu:C_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}[A\Gamma]
by letting
\nu(C)(a)
be the number of times the arc
a\in A\Gamma
appears in the circuit
C\in C_{\Gamma}
. Finally, define a group homomorphism
X:\mathbb{Z}[A\Gamma]\to\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^{\ast}
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}[A\Gamma]
by setting

X(m):=\prod\nolimits_{a\in A\Gamma}\left\lvert X_{a}\right\rvert^{m(a)}.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

be a tree and

(2)

In this section, we characterise uniscalarity of

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

has compact vertex stabilisers and type

(1)

(

), (

) or (

is uniscalar.

(2)

(

Proof.

If

is of type (

), (

) or (

) then it does not contain any translations. Hence

is uniscalar by Remark

If
G
is of type (

), the stabiliser of an arc belonging to the line between the two fixed ends fixes the entire line. It is thus a compact open subgroup normalised by any translation in

and hence

is uniscalar in this case as well.

Finally, suppose

is of type (

\omega_{+}\in\partial T
denote the unique end fixed by

denote the opposite end of

which fixes a vertex

\omega_{-}
. Letting

and thus
|G_{a}\cdot b|\geq 2
.

Since
h^{\prime}\in G
fixes
\omega_{+}
, it cannot be an inversion. If
h^{\prime}
fixes a vertex then, since
h^{\prime}
fixes
\omega_{+}
, it also fixes a vertex
v\in VL
. Since
h^{\prime}\omega_{-}\neq\omega_{-}
we conclude that
h^{\prime}
moves the arc
b\in o^{-1}(v)
oriented towards
\omega_{-}

Otherwise,
h^{\prime}
is a translation of some length

which eventually coincides with

\smash{h:=(h^{\prime})^{-l}g^{l^{\prime}}\in G}

. Then we have

as well as

and

and moves

4
, Theorem 2.6]
, and the following statement characterises uniscalarity for the general type. It is instructive to compare this to the characterisation of discreteness for
(P)
-closed groups, see
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\Delta=(\Gamma,(X_{a}),(G(v)))
be a local action diagram with finite orbits,
\mathbf{T}
be a
\Delta
-tree and
G:=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{T}}(\Delta)
. Suppose that
G
is of type (
General
) and let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the unique smallest cotree of
\Gamma
. Then
G
is uniscalar if and only if for all
v\in V\Gamma^{\prime}
the restriction of
G(v)\!\leq\!\Sym(X_{v})
to
\bigsqcup_{a\in o^{-1}(v)\cap A\Gamma^{\prime}}X_{a}\!\subseteq\!X_{v}
is semiregular.

Proof.

First, suppose that for every
v\in V\Gamma^{\prime}
the restriction of
G(v)\leq\Sym(X_{v})
to
\smash{\bigsqcup_{a\in o^{-1}(v)\cap A\Gamma^{\prime}}X_{a}\subseteq X_{v}}
is semiregular. Since
\Delta
has finite orbits, it suffices to consider the scale of translations of
G
, which in turn stem from translatable circuits by Section
4
. Let
C=(a_{i},S_{i})_{i=0}^{l-1}
be a translatable circuit of length
l
of
\Delta
. We argue that
a_{i}\in A\Gamma^{\prime}
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,l-1\}
. Otherwise, by virtue of
\Gamma^{\prime}
being a cotree, there would exist a pair of (cyclically) consecutive arcs
a_{i-1},a_{i}
(
i\in\{0,\ldots,l-1\}
such that
a_{i}=\overline{a_{i-1}}
is oriented towards
\Gamma^{\prime}
and hence
S_{i}\subseteq X_{\overline{a_{i-1}}}\times X_{a_{i}}
is necessarily the diagonal orbit of
G(o(a_{i}))
, contradicting that
C
is translatable. Combining Corollary
4.4
with the semiregularity assumption, we conclude that any translation
g\in G
associated to
C
via Proposition
3.5
satisfies
s(g)=1
.

Conversely, assume that there is a vertex
v\in V\Gamma^{\prime}
such that
G(v)
does not act semiregularly when restricted to
\bigsqcup_{a\in o^{-1}(v)\cap A\Gamma^{\prime}}X_{a}
. Then there are colours
c,d\in X_{v}
such that
p(c),p(d)\in A\Gamma^{\prime}
and
|G(v)_{c}\cdot d|\geq 2
. In particular,
|X_{p(d)}|\geq 2
. Using
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
, construct a translatable circuit
(a_{i},S_{i})_{i=0}^{l-1}
such that
o(a_{0})=v
and
(d,c)\in S_{0}
. Then the scale of any translation
g\in G
associated to
C
via Proposition
3.5
satisfies
s(g)\geq 2
by Corollary
4.4
.
∎

Next, we examine the structure of uniscalar
(P)
-closed groups. In all cases but the (
Horocyclic
) one, uniscalarity is exhibited through a compact open normal subgroup.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\Delta=(\Gamma,(X_{a}),(G(v)))
be a local action diagram with finite orbits,
\mathbf{T}=(T,\pi,\mathcal{L})
be a
\Delta
-tree and
G:=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{T}}(\Delta)
. Then
G
is uniscalar if and only if
G
is either of type (
Horocyclic
) or contains a compact open normal subgroup.

Proof.

Groups of type (
Horocyclic
) are uniscalar by Lemma
6.1
. In general, the existence of a compact open normal subgroup guarantees uniscalarity by Section
2.5
.

If
G
is of type (
Fixed Vertex
) or (
Inversion
) then
G
is compact by Lemma
2.3
. If
G
is of type (
Lineal
), let
L
denote the line between the two fixed ends of
G
and consider
K:=G_{L}
. For any arc
a\in AL
we have
K=G_{a}
and hence
K
is a compact open subgroup of
G
. Moreover, since
L
is
G
-invariant,
K
is normal in
G
.

By Lemma
6.1
, groups of type (
Focal
) are never uniscalar.

Finally, suppose
G
is of type (
General
). Then
\Delta
contains a unique minimal cotree
\Gamma^{\prime}
by
[
4
, Theorem 2.6]
. Let
T^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(\Gamma^{\prime})
denote its preimage in
T
, the unique smallest
G
-invariant subtree of
T
. Consider the subgroup
K:=G_{T^{\prime}}
of
G
. Given any arc
a\in AT^{\prime}
, Proposition
6.2
implies that
K=G_{a}
is compact open. Since
T^{\prime}
is
G
-invariant,
K
is also normal in
G
. ∎

Complementing Theorem
6.3
, we note that horocyclic
(P)
-closed groups with compact vertex stabilisers never contain a compact open normal subgroup.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\Delta=(\Gamma,(X_{a}),(G(v)))
be a local action diagram with finite orbits,
\mathbf{T}=(T,\pi,\mathcal{L})
be a
\Delta
-tree and
G:=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{T}}(\Delta)
. If
G
is of type (
Horocyclic
) then
G
does not contain a compact open normal subgroup.

Proof.

Suppose that
K\unlhd G
is compact and normal. Since
K
is compact and acts without inversion, it fixes a vertex
v\in VT
. Arguing as in
[
4
, Section 3.5]
, the
G
-orbit of
v
, which is contained in the horosphere of
v
, must be infinite. Since
K
is normal in
G
, it fixes said orbit. But, repeating the argument from
[
4
, Section 3.5]
, for any finite set
S\subseteq VT
the basic open subgroup
H_{S}
acts non-trivially on said orbit due to how
T
is constructed from
\Delta
. Hence
K
is not open.
∎

\smash{G/K=G/G_{T^{\prime}}\cong G^{T^{\prime}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{T^{\prime}}}(\Delta^{\prime})}

\Gamma^{\prime}

\smash{X_{v}^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{a\in o^{-1}(v)\cap A\Gamma^{\prime}}X_{a}}

\mathbf{T^{\prime}}=(T^{\prime},\pi,\mathcal{L})

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\Delta=(\Gamma,(X_{a}),(G(v)))
be a local action diagram with finite orbits and

be a
\Delta

.

Finally, we remark that being uniscalar is not a locally determined global property in the sense of Reid–Smith
[
13
, Section 8]
. For example, given a permutation group
F\leq S_{d}
, the group
\mathrm{U}_{2}(\Gamma(F))\leq\Aut(T_{d})
introduced in
[
17
, Section 4.4.1]
is discrete, and hence uniscalar, whereas its
(P)
-closure, the Burger–Mozes group
\mathrm{U}_{2}(\Gamma(F))^{(P)}=\mathrm{U}_{1}(F)
is uniscalar if and only if
F
is semiregular, see
[
6
, Proposition 6.20]
or Proposition
6.2
. However, a group whose
(P)
-closure is uniscalar must itself be uniscalar.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
T
be a tree and
G\leq\Aut(T)
have compact vertex stabilisers. If
\smash{G^{(P)}}
is uniscalar then so is
G
, and
G
is either of type (
Horocyclic
) or contains a compact open normal subgroup.

Proof.

Since
G
and
G^{(P)}
have the same type by
[
4
, Proposition 2.4]
, the first statement follows from Lemma
6.1
except for type (
General
). The second statement is immediate for types (
Fixed Vertex
), (
Inversion
) and (
Focal
). In the (
Lineal)
case, the stabiliser of the line connecting the two fixed ends is compact open normal.

Finally, suppose that
G
and
\smash{G^{(P)}}
are of type (
General
) and
G^{(P)}
is uniscalar. Let
T^{\prime}
be the unique smallest subtree of
T
that is
\smash{G^{(P)}}
-invariant. Then
\smash{G^{(P)}_{T^{\prime}}}
is a compact open normal subgroup of
G^{(P)}
by the proof of Theorem
6.3
. Since
G\leq G^{(P)}
and the subspace topology on
G
coincides with the permutation topology, the subgroup
\smash{G_{T^{\prime}}=G_{T^{\prime}}^{(P)}\cap G}
is a compact open normal subgroup of
G
. Hence
G
is uniscalar.
∎
