Convergence Rates of Tseng’s Splitting Method and Its Acceleration Schemes for Monotone Inclusion Problem with a Sum of Hölder Continuous Operators
Yi Zhang*

Abstract
The monotone inclusion problem is fundamental in applied mathematics and is closely related to a wide range of practical applications. However, existing solution methods typically require the underlying operator to be Lipschitz continuous. Recently, Hölder continuity, a weaker condition than Lipschitz continuity, has proven useful in characterizing certain real-world problems. To bridge this gap, we investigate the convergence rates of the Tseng’s splitting method (a fundamental algorithm for monotone inclusion problem) and its two accelerated variants, the composite extra anchored gradient method and the symplectic composite extra gradient method, under the Hölder continuity assumption. Our numerical experiments demonstrate that the numerical performance of these algorithms aligns with their respective theoretical convergence rates.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space equipped with the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and the corresponding norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
. We denote
2^{\mathcal{H}}
as the collection of all subsets of
\mathcal{H}
. Throughout this paper, we consider the monotone inclusion problem

0\in T(x):=F(x)+G(x),

(1.1)

where
T:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is a monotone operator,
F:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a continuous operator, and
G:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is a maximally monotone operator. Moreover,
F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
, where each
F_{i}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is Hölder continuous with Hölder exponent
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1]
and Hölder constant
L_{i}
, i.e.,

\left\|F_{i}(x)-F_{i}(y)\right\|\leq L_{i}\left\|x-y\right\|^{\alpha_{i}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(1.2)

If
\alpha_{i}=1
, then (
1.2
) implies that
F_{i}
is
L_{i}
-Lipschitz continuous. We denote the solution set by
\mathrm{SOL}:=\{x\in\mathcal{H}\mid 0\in T(x)\}
and assume that
\mathrm{SOL}\neq\emptyset
.

The monotone inclusion problem (
1.1
) has a strong connection with several important classes of applied mathematical problems, such as optimization problems, minimax problems, duality theory, and variational inequality (VI) problems. These problem classes play an important role in numerous practical applications; see, e.g.,
[
37
,
3
,
19
,
42
,
36
]
. Since many algorithms for solving (
1.1
) are rooted in algorithms for solving VI problems, we discuss the relationship between the VI problem and the monotone inclusion problem (
1.1
) in detail.

Example 1

(VI Problem)
.

The Stampacchia variational inequality problem consists in finding a point
x^{*}\in\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{H}
such that

\left\langle F(x^{*}),x-x^{*}\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{C}.

(1.3)

If
\mathcal{C}
is a closed convex set, (
1.3
) can be reformulated as

0\in F(x^{*})+N_{\mathcal{C}}(x^{*}),

where
N_{\mathcal{C}}
is the normal cone of
\mathcal{C}
defined by

N_{\mathcal{C}}(x^{*}):=\begin{cases}\{u\mid\left\langle u,x-x^{*}\right\rangle\leq 0,\ \forall x\in\mathcal{C}\},&\text{if }x^{*}\in\mathcal{C};\\
\emptyset,&\text{if }x^{*}\notin\mathcal{C}.\end{cases}

Due to the wide range of applications of (
1.1
) in practical problems, the study of algorithms for solving (
1.1
) has attracted increasing interest. First, we give a concise overview of existing algorithms under the assumption that
F
is Lipschitz continuous. The extra-gradient (EG) method, proposed independently in
[
2
,
20
]
, and Popov’s method (also known as the OGDA method
[
10
,
11
]
) , proposed in
[
30
]
, are two classic algorithms for solving the VI problem (
1.3
) where
F
is monotone on
\mathcal{C}
. The Tseng’s splitting method
[
41
]
was developed to solve (
1.1
) when
G
is a maximally monotone operator. The mirror-prox method introduced in
[
26
]
studies the VI problem under the assumption that
F
is monotone and Lipschitz continuous with respect to a non-Euclidean norm on
\mathcal{C}
. The EG+ method
[
12
]
addresses the equation
0=F(x)
under the weak Minty variational inequality (MVI) assumption. Further extensions include the EG method with line search
[
5
]
, which can solve the VI problem when
F
is continuous and pseudomonotone with a convergence guarantee, and the adaptive EG+ method
[
13
,
29
]
, which incorporates adaptive step-sizes when
F
satisfies the weak MVI assumption. As stochastic optimization continues to gain prominence, research on stochastic EG methods has also grown significantly; see, e.g.,
[
14
,
17
,
18
,
25
]
. For a comprehensive overview of the EG method and its variants, we refer the reader to the recent survey
[
39
]
.

Since Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method
[
27
]
achieves a higher-order convergence rate than the vanilla gradient method while maintaining the same per-iteration computational complexity, the study of accelerated first-order methods for solving (
1.1
) has also gained significant attention. Halpern’s iteration, originally proposed for finding fixed points of nonexpansive operators (1-Lipschitz continuous operators)
[
15
]
, has been widely studied
[
22
,
31
]
and has found applications as an acceleration technique
[
8
,
23
,
35
]
. Consequently, several accelerated methods have been derived from Halpern’s iteration. Yoon and Ryu
[
43
]
combined Halpern’s iteration with the EG method to propose the extra anchored gradient (EAG) method for solving
0=F(x)
where
F
is monotone. In
[
21
]
, Lee and Kim further combined the EAG method with the EG+ method to obtain the fast EG (FEG) method, which solves
0=F(x)
under a non-monotonicity assumption. Yang et al.
[
6
]
later extended the EAG and FEG methods to solve (
1.1
) without assuming
G=0
. Other acceleration schemes have also been developed. For instance, the symplectic EG method
[
44
]
, derived from the symplectic acceleration technique
[
45
]
, and the moving anchored EG method improve upon Halpern’s iteration to achieve faster convergence rates, with their extensions presented in
[
38
]
. Tran et al.
[
40
]
used Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method to derive an accelerated forward-backward-forward splitting method. In
[
4
,
33
]
, Boţ et al. proposed accelerated variants of OGDA, called fOGDA-VI, by discretizing an ODE. Qu et al.
[
32
]
combined the EG method with Anderson(1) acceleration
[
1
]
to obtain the EG-Anderson(1) method.

Several works have studied algorithms for solving (
1.1
) in the case where
F
is Hölder continuous. Nemirovski
[
26
]
originally proposed the mirror-prox method for the VI problem when
F
is monotone on a compact convex set
\mathcal{C}
, and showed that the convergence rate of the Minty gap function is
O\bigl(k^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\bigr)
. Dang and Lan
[
9
]
further extended the mirror-prox method to solve the VI problem when
F
is pseudomonotone and
\mathcal{C}
is closed and compact, establishing that

\min_{0\leq k\leq K}\left\|\frac{1}{s_{k+1}}\bigl(x_{k}-P_{\mathcal{C}}(x_{k}-s_{k+1}F(x_{k}))\bigr)\right\|^{2}\leq O\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha}}K^{\alpha}}\right).

Stonyakin et al.
[
34
]
studied a generalization of the mirror-prox method when the Hölder exponent or Hölder constant is unknown, as well as an inexact oracle variant of the method.
[
7
]
combined the Popov method with the mirror-prox method to obtain the Popov mirror-prox method, and studied its convergence behavior under both deterministic and stochastic settings.

1.1
Our Contributions

To the best of our knowledge, there is no work that studies algorithms for solving (
1.1
) where
G
is a maximally monotone operator or
F
is a sum of Hölder continuous operators. The reason why we study the case that
F
is the sum of Hölder continuous operator is that the sum of Hölder continuous operator is not a Hölder continuous operator, as illustrated by the following example.

Example 2
.

[
8
, Example 1]

Consider
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

F=F_{1}+F_{2},\quad F_{1}(x)=x,\quad F_{2}(x)=\operatorname{sign}(x)\,x^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In
[
8
]
, Chen et al. show that
F
is locally, but not globally, Hölder continuous.

This simple example demonstrates that in problem (
1.1
), the operator
F
, expressed as a sum of operators
F_{n}
each of which is Hölder continuous, may itself fail to be Hölder continuous.

In this paper, we aim to develop algorithms for solving (
1.1
) with convergence rate guarantees. We first study the convergence rate of the Tseng’s splitting method introduced by Tseng
[
41
]
under that
F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
and
F_{i}
satisfies (
1.2
). We prove that if

s_{k+1}=\frac{ds}{(k+1)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}},

where
\alpha=\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\alpha_{i}
,
d\in(0,1)
, and
0<s\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\frac{1}{NL_{i}\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}}
, then the convergence rate of Tseng’s splitting method satisfies

\min_{0\leq k\leq K}\mathrm{dist}\bigl(0,T(y_{k+1})\bigr)^{2}\leq O\!\left(\!\left(\frac{(2+2d^{2})\alpha}{1-d^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\alpha\ln(K)}{N(1-d^{2})\alpha_{i}}\right)K^{-\alpha}\right).

Next, we extend the convergence rates of two accelerated Tseng’s splitting methods: the composite FEG (com-FEG) method
[
6
]
and the symplectic composite extra-gradient (SCEG) method
[
44
]
, under the assumption that
F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
and
F_{i}
satisfies (
1.2
) for all
i=1,\cdots,N
. We prove that if
\alpha_{i}\in(\frac{1}{3},1]
for all
i=1,\dots,N
, and

s_{k+1}=\frac{s}{(k+1)^{\frac{3(1-\alpha)}{2}}},

where
\alpha=\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\alpha_{i}
and
0<s\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\frac{1}{(N^{2}L_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i})^{\frac{1}{2\alpha_{i}}}}
, then the convergence rate of the proposed algorithm is

\mathrm{dist}\bigl(0,T(x_{K})\bigr)^{2}\leq O\!\left(\!\left(\frac{1}{(3\alpha-1)^{2}s^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\ln(K)}{N\alpha_{i}}\right)K^{-(3\alpha-1)}\right).

Remark 1
.

If
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
, the convergence rate of the com-FEG method and the SCEG method is faster than the convergence rate of the Tseng’s splitting method. If
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, the convergence rate of the Tseng’s splitting method is faster than the com-FEG method and the SCEG method. Our numerical experiments also support this theoretical result.

1.2
Preliminaries

A set-valued operator
T:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is said to be monotone if

\left\langle u-v,x-y\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H},u\in T(x),v\in T(y).

An operator
G:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is a maximally monotone operator if
G
is a monotone operator and the graph of
G
, defined as
\mathrm{graph}(G):=\{(x,u)|u\in G(x)\}
, is not a proper subset of the graph of another monotone operator.

The proximal point operator of a maximally monotone operator
G
is defined as
J_{sG}:=(I+sG)^{-1}
, where
I:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is the identity map and
s>0
. By invoking the monotonicity of
G
and the Minty’s surjectivity theorem
[
24
]
,
J_{sG}
is a single-valued mapping defined on
\mathcal{H}
.

Given a subset
\mathcal{S}
and a point
x
, the distance between
x
and
\mathcal{S}
is defined as

\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathcal{S})=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{S}}\left\|x-y\right\|.

2
Tseng’s Splitting Method

First, we study the convergence rate of the Tseng’s splitting method under the assumption that
F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
, where
F_{i}
satisfies (
1.2
). The Tseng’s spliiting method is a first-order method to solve (
1.1
), whose iteration formula is given by: start with
x_{0}
,

\displaystyle y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=J_{s_{k+1}G}(x_{k}-s_{k+1}F(x_{k})),

(2.1)

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=y_{k+1}-s_{k+1}(F(y_{k+1})-F(x_{k})),

(2.2)

where
s_{k+1}>0
.

Remark 2
.

It has been proven that if
F
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous and
s_{k+1}\equiv s\in(0,\frac{1}{L})
, the convergence rate of the Tseng’s splitting method is

\min_{0\leq k\leq K}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(y_{k+1}))^{2}\leq O(K^{-1}).

Theorem 1
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{y_{k+1}\}
be the sequences generated by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). If
F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
,
F_{i}
satisfies (
1.2
) for all
i=1,\cdots,N
,
G
is a maximally monotone operator,
T
is monotone and

s_{k+1}=\frac{ds}{(k+1)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}},

(2.3)

where
\alpha=\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\alpha_{i}
,
d\in(0,1)
,
0<s\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\left(\frac{1}{NL_{i}\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}}\right)
, then we have

\min_{0\leq k\leq K}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(y_{k+1}))^{2}\leq O\left(\left(\frac{(2+2d^{2})\alpha}{1-d^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\alpha d^{2}\ln(K)}{N(1-d^{2})\alpha_{i}}\right)K^{-\alpha}\right).

Proof.

We begin by setting
\tilde{g}_{k+1}=s_{k+1}^{-1}(x_{k}-y_{k+1})-F(x_{k})
.
From the definition of
J_{s_{k+1}G}
, it immediately follows that
\tilde{g}_{k+1}\in G(y_{k+1})
.
For an arbitrary point
x^{*}\in\mathrm{SOL}
, the following chain of equalities holds:

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x^{*}-s_{k+1}(F(y_{k+1})-F(x_{k}))\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}-s_{k+1}\left\langle F(y_{k+1})-F(x_{k}),y_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle+\frac{s_{k+1}^{2}}{2}\left\|F(y_{k+1})-F(x_{k})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}+\left\langle y_{k+1}-x_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ -s_{k+1}\left\langle F(y_{k+1})-F(x_{k}),y_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle+\frac{s_{k+1}^{2}}{2}\left\|F(y_{k+1})-F(x_{k})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|-\frac{1}{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}+\frac{s_{k+1}^{2}}{2}\left\|F(y_{k+1})-F(x_{k})\right\|^{2}-s_{k+1}\left\langle F(y_{k+1})+\tilde{g}_{k+1},y_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle.

Applying the assumption on
F
yields

\begin{split}&\ \frac{s_{k+1}^{2}}{2}\left\|F(y_{k+1})-F(x_{k})\right\|^{2}\\
=&\ \frac{s_{k+1}^{2}}{2}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(F_{i}(y_{k+1})-F_{i}(x_{k})\right)\right\|^{2}\\
\leq&\ \frac{Ns_{k+1}^{2}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|F_{i}(y_{k+1})-F_{i}(x_{k})\right\|^{2}\\
\leq&\ \frac{Ns_{k+1}^{2}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{i}^{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2\alpha_{i}}\\
=&\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{NL_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}}{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2\alpha_{i}}-\frac{d^{2}}{2N}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\right)+\frac{d^{2}}{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\\
\leq&\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\lambda^{\alpha_{i}}-d^{2}\lambda}{2N}+\frac{d^{2}}{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

(2.4)

In the case where
\alpha_{i}=1
, we have

N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}=N^{2}L_{i}^{2}d^{2}s^{2}(k+1)^{-(1-\alpha)}\leq\frac{N^{2}L_{i}^{2}d^{2}}{N^{2}L_{i}^{2}}\leq d^{2},

which implies that the supremum vanishes, i.e.,

\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\lambda-d^{2}\lambda}{2N}=0.

If instead
\alpha_{i}<1
, then a direct maximization gives

\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\lambda^{\alpha_{i}}-d^{2}\lambda}{2N}=\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{2N\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\alpha_{i}}{d^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{i}}}.

Recalling the definition of
s_{k+1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ \frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{2N\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\alpha_{i}}{d^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{i}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}(N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s^{2}\alpha_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{i}}}}{2N\alpha_{i}}\cdot(k+1)^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\alpha_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{2N\alpha_{i}(k+1)},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
1-\alpha_{i}\leq 1-\alpha
.
Observe that when
\alpha_{i}=1
, the right-hand side of the above estimate reduces to
0
. Hence,

\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\lambda^{\alpha_{i}}-d^{2}\lambda}{2N}\leq\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{2N\alpha_{i}(k+1)}

holds uniformly for all
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1]
.

Furthermore, since
\tilde{g}_{k+1}\in G(y_{k+1})
and
T
is monotone, we have the inner product estimate
\left\langle F(y_{k+1})+\tilde{g}_{k+1},y_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle\geq 0
.
Consequently, we derive

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1-d^{2}}{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{2N\alpha_{i}(k+1)}.

Rearranging the above inequality gives the recursive bound

\displaystyle\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-d^{2}}\left(\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}-\left\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{N(1-d^{2})\alpha_{i}(k+1)}.

Next, applying (
2.4
) to estimate the term
s_{k+1}^{2}\left\|F(y_{k+1})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\ s_{k+1}^{2}\left\|F(y_{k+1})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2s_{k+1}^{2}\left\|F(x_{k})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+2s_{k+1}^{2}\left\|F(y_{k+1})-F(x_{k})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (2+2d^{2})\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{2(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{N\alpha_{i}(k+1)}.

Thus, substituting the previous bound yields

\displaystyle\ s_{k+1}^{2}\left\|F(y_{k+1})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{2+2d^{2}}{1-d^{2}}\left(\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}-\left\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}\right)+\left(2+\frac{2+2d^{2}}{1-d^{2}}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{N\alpha_{i}(k+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2+2d^{2}}{1-d^{2}}\left(\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}-\left\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}\right)+\frac{4}{1-d^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{N\alpha_{i}(k+1)}.

Finally, summing the above inequality from
k=0
to
K
and invoking the relation
\mathrm{dist}(0,T(y_{k+1}))^{2}\leq\left\|F(y_{k+1})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ \min_{0\leq k\leq K}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(y_{k+1}))^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K}s_{k+1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \min_{0\leq k\leq K}\left\|F(y_{k+1})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K}s_{k+1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=0}^{K}s_{k+1}^{2}\left\|F(y_{k+1})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{2+2d^{2}}{1-d^{2}}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}+\frac{4}{1-d^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})d^{2}}{N\alpha_{i}(k+1)}.

Since
\sum_{k=0}^{K}s_{k+1}^{2}
is of the same order as
\frac{K^{\alpha}}{\alpha}
, and
\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{1}{k+1}
is of the same order as
\ln(K)
, it follows that

\min_{0\leq k\leq K}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(y_{k+1}))^{2}\leq O\left(\left(\frac{(2+2d^{2})\alpha}{1-d^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{4(1-\alpha_{i})\alpha d^{2}\ln(K)}{N(1-d^{2})\alpha_{i}}\right)K^{-\alpha}\right).

∎

3
Generalized Accelerated Tseng’s Splitting Method

In this section, we extend two accelerated extensions of the Tseng’s splitting method to solve (
1.1
) under the assumption that
F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
and
F_{i}
satisfies (
1.2
) for all
i=1,\cdots,N
, one is the com-FEG method
[
6
]
, another one is the SCEG method
[
44
]
.

3.1
Generalized Fast Composite Extra-gradient Method

This section is devoted to study the convergence rate of Algorithm
1
. The main tool for studying Algorithm
1
is the following Lyapunov function:

\mathcal{E}(k):=\Sigma_{k}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}+\Sigma_{k}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k},x_{k}-x_{0}\right\rangle,

(3.1)

where
\tilde{t}_{k}=\begin{cases}0,&k=0;\\
F(x_{k})+\tilde{g}_{k},&k\geq 1.\end{cases}
Because
\tilde{g}_{k+1}\in G(x_{k+1})
for all
k\geq 0
,
\tilde{t}_{k+1}\in T(x_{k+1})
for all
k\geq 0
.

Algorithm 1

Generalized Composite Fast Extra-gradient Method, GCFEG

1:

Initialization:

x_{0}
,
z_{0}=x_{0}
,
\tilde{g}_{0}=0
,
\Sigma_{0}=0
,
0<s\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\left(\frac{1}{(N^{2}L_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i})^{\frac{1}{2\alpha_{i}}}}\right)
.

Theorem 2

(Convergence Rate)
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
,
\{x_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\}
,
\{s_{k+1}\}
and
\{\Sigma_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
1
. If
F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
,
F_{i}
satisfies (
1.2
) with
\alpha_{i}\in\left(\frac{1}{3},1\right]
for all

G
is maximally monotone,
T
is monotone,
\alpha=\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\alpha_{i}
, and

, then we have the following convergence rate:

\mathrm{dist}(0,T(x_{K}))^{2}\leq O\left(\left(\frac{1}{(3\alpha-1)^{2}s^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\ln(K)}{N\alpha_{i}}\right)K^{-(3\alpha-1)}\right).

Proof.

We begin by computing the difference of the sequence
\{\mathcal{E}(k)\}
.
A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \Sigma_{k+1}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\Sigma_{k}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}+\Sigma_{k+1}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x_{0}\right\rangle+\Sigma_{k}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k},x_{k}-x_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \Sigma_{k+1}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\Sigma_{k}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}+\frac{2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1}}{2s_{k+1}}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})x_{k+1}-2\Sigma_{k}x_{k}-s_{k+1}x_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\Sigma_{k}}{s_{k+1}}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k},(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})x_{k+1}-2\Sigma_{k}x_{k}-s_{k+1}x_{0}\right\rangle-\frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})\Sigma_{k}}{s_{k+1}}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1}-\tilde{t}_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \Sigma_{k+1}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\Sigma_{k}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}-\frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{2}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})\Sigma_{k}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k},F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\rangle.

First, by using the equation

-\left\langle a,b\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|a-b\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|a\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|b\right\|^{2},\quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal{H},

we have

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{2}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\left(\left\|F(x_{k+1})-F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}-\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}\right).

Next, exploiting the decomposition
F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
and the Hölder condition (
1.2
) for each
F_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\ \frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\left\|F(x_{k+1})-F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N}NL_{i}^{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}NL_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2\alpha_{i}}}{4}\left\|F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}-\frac{2\Sigma_{k}}{2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1}}\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\left\|F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}-\frac{2\Sigma_{k}}{2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1}}\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\left\|F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}-\frac{2\Sigma_{k}}{2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1}}\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}(N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2\alpha_{i}}\lambda^{\alpha_{i}}-\lambda)-(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})\Sigma_{k}\left\langle F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1},\tilde{t}_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\left\|F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\Sigma_{k}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}.

If
\alpha_{i}=1
, then we have

\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2\alpha_{i}}\lambda-\lambda=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}(N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s^{2\alpha_{i}}(k+1)^{-3(1-\alpha)}-1)\lambda=0.

If instead
\alpha_{i}\in\left(\frac{1}{3},1\right)

s_{k+1}=\frac{s}{(k+1)^{\frac{3(1-\alpha)}{2}}}
gives

\displaystyle\ \sup_{\lambda\geq 0}N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2\alpha_{i}}\lambda^{\alpha_{i}}-\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}(N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2\alpha_{i}}\alpha_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{i}}}}{4N\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}(N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2\alpha_{i}}\alpha_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{i}}}}{N\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}(N^{2}L_{i}^{2}\alpha_{i}s^{2\alpha_{i}})^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{i}}}(k+1)^{-\frac{3(1-\alpha)\alpha_{i}}{1-\alpha_{i}}}}{N\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}}{N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}},

where the last inequality follows from
-\frac{3(1-\alpha)\alpha_{i}}{1-\alpha_{i}}\leq 3\alpha
.
Note that this bound also holds when
\alpha_{i}=1
.

Combining all the preceding estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{2}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}-(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})\Sigma_{k}\left\langle F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1},\tilde{t}_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\Sigma_{k}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}.

Substituting the above inequality back into the expression for
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
yields the simplified recursive bound

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(2\Sigma_{k}+s_{k+1})^{2}}{4N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}}{N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}.

Moreover, for any
x^{*}\in\mathrm{SOL}
, we have the following lower bound for
\mathcal{E}(k)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(k)=

\displaystyle\ \Sigma_{k}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}+\Sigma_{k}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle+\Sigma_{k}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k},x^{*}-x_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ \frac{\Sigma_{k}^{2}}{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}+\Sigma_{k}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2},

which directly implies

\displaystyle\ \frac{\Sigma_{K}^{2}}{2}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(x_{K}))^{2}\leq\frac{\Sigma_{K}^{2}}{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{K}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{E}(K)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}}{N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}.

Since
\alpha_{i}\in(\frac{1}{3},1]
for all
i=1,\cdots,N
, we have
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},1]
and
-\frac{3(1-\alpha)}{2}\in(-1,0)
, which implies that
\Sigma_{k+1}
is the same order as
\frac{(3\alpha-1)s}{2}k^{1-\frac{3(1-\alpha)}{2}}
. Also, because

2-3(1-\alpha)-3\alpha=-1,

we have

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}}{N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}\sim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(3\alpha-1)^{2}s^{2}\ln(K)}{4N\alpha_{i}}.

Combining these results, we finally deduce that

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{dist}(0,T(x_{K}))^{2}\leq\left\|\tilde{t}_{K}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \Sigma_{K}^{-2}\mathcal{E}(0)+\Sigma_{K}^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}}{4N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ O\left(\left(\frac{1}{(3\alpha-1)^{2}s^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\ln(K)}{N\alpha_{i}}\right)K^{-(3\alpha-1)}\right).

∎

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{2\Sigma_{k}}{2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1}}\tilde{t}_{k}-(F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}N^{2}L_{i}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2\alpha_{i}}\lambda^{\alpha_{i}}-\lambda}{2N}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{2\Sigma_{k}}{2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1}}\tilde{t}_{k}-(F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1-\alpha_{i}}{2N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{2\Sigma_{k}}{2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1}}\tilde{t}_{k}-(F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1})\right\|^{2}.

Inserting these estimates back into the expression for II, we arrive at the upper bound

\displaystyle\text{II}\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{d(r^{2}-r)s_{k+1}}{2}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},z_{k}-x_{k+1}\right\rangle+\frac{d^{2}s_{k+1}\Sigma_{k+1}}{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{d(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\left\|\frac{2\Sigma_{k}}{2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1}}\tilde{t}_{k}-(F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{d(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\left(\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}\right)+d\Sigma_{k}(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k},F(x_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{g}_{k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ -\frac{d\Sigma_{k}(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})}{s_{k+1}}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1}-\tilde{t}_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{d(r^{2}-r)s_{k+1}}{2}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},z_{k}-x_{k+1}\right\rangle+\left(\frac{d^{2}s_{k+1}\Sigma_{k+1}}{2}-\frac{d(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4}\right)\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +d\Sigma_{k}^{2}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k}\right\|^{2}-\frac{d\Sigma_{k}(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})}{s_{k+1}}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1}-\tilde{t}_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}.

Step 4: estimate the upper bound of
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
. We turn to part III. Using the identity

\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}=\left\langle z_{k+1}-z_{k},z_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}

and the recursive rule of
z_{k+1}
, we have

\text{III}=-\frac{d(r^{2}-r)s_{k+1}}{2}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},z_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle+\frac{d^{2}(r-1)s_{k+1}^{2}}{8}\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}.

Given by the previous calculation about II and III, we have

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{d(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4}-\frac{d^{2}s_{k+1}\Sigma_{k+1}}{2}-d\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\frac{d^{2}(r-1)s_{k+1}^{2}}{8}\right)\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{d(r^{2}-r)s_{k+1}}{2}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle-\frac{d\Sigma_{k}(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})}{s_{k+1}}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1}-\tilde{t}_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}.

For
k\geq 1
, the monotonicity of
T
together with
\tilde{t}_{k+1}\in T(x_{k+1})
,
\tilde{t}_{k}\in T(x_{k})
, and
x^{*}\in\mathrm{SOL}
implies

\displaystyle-\frac{d(r^{2}-r)s_{k+1}}{2}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 0,\quad\forall k\geq 0,

\displaystyle-\frac{d\Sigma_{k}(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})}{s_{k+1}}\left\langle\tilde{t}_{k+1}-\tilde{t}_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 0,\quad\forall k\geq 1.

Moreover, since
\Sigma_{0}=0
, the second inequality also holds for
k=0
. Consequently, both non-positive inner-product terms can be dropped, yielding

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ -\left(\frac{d(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4}-\frac{d^{2}s_{k+1}\Sigma_{k+1}}{2}-d\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\frac{d^{2}(r-1)s_{k+1}^{2}}{8}\right)\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}.

Using
\Sigma_{k+1}=\Sigma_{k}+\frac{s_{k+1}}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\ \frac{d(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4}-\frac{d^{2}s_{k+1}\Sigma_{k+1}}{2}-d\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\frac{d^{2}(r-1)s_{k+1}^{2}}{8}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{d(2\Sigma_{k+1}+(r-1)s_{k+1})^{2}}{4}-\frac{d^{2}s_{k+1}\Sigma_{k+1}}{2}-d\Sigma_{k+1}^{2}-\frac{d^{2}(r-1)s_{k+1}^{2}}{8}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ d\left(\Sigma_{k+1}s_{k+1}+\frac{(r-1)s_{k+1}^{2}}{4}\right)\left(r-1-\frac{d}{2}\right).

Since the coefficient is nonnegative (as
d<2(r-1)
and the other factors are positive), we obtain the desired recursive bound

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ -d\left(\Sigma_{k+1}s_{k+1}+\frac{(r-1)s_{k+1}^{2}}{4}\right)\left(r-1-\frac{d}{2}\right)\left\|\tilde{t}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})(2\Sigma_{k}+rs_{k+1})^{2}}{4N\alpha_{i}(k+1)^{3\alpha}}.

∎

Theorem 5

(Convergence Rate)
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
,
\{x_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\}
,
\{z_{k}\}
,
\{s_{k+1}\}
and

) with

is maximally monotone,

\displaystyle=

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider the monotone inclusion problem (
1.1
) and study the convergence rates of the Tseng’s splitting method and its accelerated variants, the com-EAG method and the SCEG method, under the assumption that
F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
, where each
F_{i}
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1]
and constant
L_{i}
,
G
is a maximally monotone operator, and
T
is a monotone operator.
First, we prove that the convergence rate of Tseng’s splitting method satisfies

\min_{0\leq k\leq K}\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,T(y_{k+1})\bigr)^{2}\leq O\!\left(\left(\frac{(2+2d^{2})\alpha}{1-d^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\alpha\ln(K)}{N(1-d^{2})\alpha_{i}}\right)K^{-\alpha}\right).

We further prove that the convergence rates of Algorithm
1
and Algorithm
2
satisfy

\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,T(x_{K})\bigr)^{2}\leq O\!\left(\left(\frac{1}{(3\alpha-1)^{2}s^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i})\ln(K)}{N\alpha_{i}}\right)K^{-(3\alpha-1)}\right).

When
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
, the com‑EAG and SCEG methods converge faster than the Tseng’s splitting method; when
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, Tseng’s method converges faster than the two accelerated methods. Our numerical experiments also support this theoretical result.

Appendix A
Properties of
F
in Section
4.2

We consider the operator
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
as:

F(x)=\begin{cases}\|x\|^{\alpha-1}(I+B)x,&\text{if }x\neq 0;\\
0,&\text{if }x=0,\end{cases}

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a skew-symmetric matrix.

Proposition 6
.

If
\|B\|_{2}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}
,
F
is Hölder continuous with Hölder constant
L=2^{1-\alpha}\sqrt{1+\|B\|_{2}^{2}}
.

Proof.

We seek to show that there exists a constant
L>0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\|F(x)-F(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|^{\alpha}.
Let
A=I+B
. Since
A
is a linear operator, we can factor it out:

\|F(x)-F(y)\|=\left\|A\left(x\|x\|^{\alpha-1}-y\|y\|^{\alpha-1}\right)\right\|\leq\|A\|_{2}\left\|x\|x\|^{\alpha-1}-y\|y\|^{\alpha-1}\right\|

First, we determine the spectral norm of
A
, given by
\|A\|_{2}=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(A^{T}A)}
. Because
B
is skew-symmetric, we have:

A^{T}A=(I+B)^{T}(I+B)=(I-B)(I+B)=I-B^{2}

The eigenvalues of a real skew-symmetric matrix are purely imaginary or zero, meaning the eigenvalues of
B^{2}
are real and non-positive. Consequently, the maximum eigenvalue of
I-B^{2}
is exactly
1+\|B\|_{2}^{2}
, yielding:

\|A\|_{2}=\sqrt{1+\|B\|_{2}^{2}}

Next, we prove that

\left\|x\|x\|^{\alpha-1}-y\|y\|^{\alpha-1}\right\|\leq 2^{1-\alpha}\left\|x-y\right\|^{\alpha}.

Let
r=\|x\|,\;s=\|y\|
and let
\theta\in[0,\pi]
such that
\cos\theta=\frac{x\cdot y}{rs}
.

\|x\|x\|^{\alpha-1}-y\|y\|^{\alpha-1}\|^{2}=r^{2\alpha}+s^{2\alpha}-2r^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\cos\theta=(r^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha})^{2}+2r^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}(1-\cos\theta),

\|x-y\|^{2}=r^{2}+s^{2}-2rs\cos\theta=(r-s)^{2}+2rs(1-\cos\theta).

Let
t=1-\cos\theta\in[0,2]
, the desired inequality can be transformed into

(r^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha})^{2}+2r^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}t\;\leq\;4^{1-\alpha}\bigl[(r-s)^{2}+2rst\bigr]^{\alpha}.

The left-hand side of the above inequality is denoted as
L(t)
, the right-hand side of the above inequality is denoted as
R(t)
. Because
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the function
t\mapsto R(t)
is concave, and
L(t)
is linear, thus
H(t)=L(t)-R(t)
is a convex function. Because the maximum of a convex function attains at the boundary of domain. So we discuss
H(0)
and
H(2)
.

•

t=0
: Then we need to prove

(r^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha})^{2}\leq 4^{1-\alpha}(r-s)^{2\alpha}
, i.e.
|r^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha}|\leq 2^{1-\alpha}|r-s|^{\alpha}
.
Because of the inequality
|r^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha}|\leq|r-s|^{\alpha}
and
2^{1-\alpha}\geq 1
,
|r^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha}|\leq 2^{1-\alpha}|r-s|^{\alpha}
holds.

•

t=2
: Then we need to prove
(r^{\alpha}+s^{\alpha})^{2}\leq 4^{1-\alpha}(r+s)^{2\alpha}
, i.e.
r^{\alpha}+s^{\alpha}\leq 2^{1-\alpha}(r+s)^{\alpha}
. Because
u\mapsto u^{\alpha}
is concave, then using the Jensen’s inequality, we have

\frac{r^{\alpha}+s^{\alpha}}{2}\leq\left(\frac{r+s}{2}\right)^{\alpha}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r^{\alpha}+s^{\alpha}\leq 2^{1-\alpha}(r+s)^{\alpha}.

In conclusion,

\left\|x\|x\|^{\alpha-1}-y\|y\|^{\alpha-1}\right\|\leq 2^{1-\alpha}\left\|x-y\right\|^{\alpha}.

Based on the previous discussion

\|F(x)-F(y)\|\leq 2^{1-\alpha}\sqrt{1+\left\|B\right\|^{2}}\left\|x-y\right\|^{\alpha}.

∎

Proposition 7
.

If
\|B\|_{2}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}
, the operator
F
is monotone.

Proof.

To prove that
F
is monotone, we must show that
\langle F(x)-F(y),x-y\rangle\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Because
F
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and continuously differentiable on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, it is sufficient to prove that the symmetric part of its Jacobian matrix,
J_{sym}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(J(x)+J(x)^{T})
, is positive semi-definite for all
x\neq 0
.

For
x\neq 0
, the Jacobian matrix
J(x)
is computed as:

J(x)=\|x\|^{\alpha-1}(I+B)+(\alpha-1)\|x\|^{\alpha-3}(I+B)xx^{T}

Taking the transpose and using the property
B^{T}=-B
, we find:

J(x)^{T}=\|x\|^{\alpha-1}(I-B)+(\alpha-1)\|x\|^{\alpha-3}xx^{T}(I-B)

Averaging
J(x)
and
J(x)^{T}
yields the symmetric part:

J_{sym}(x)=\|x\|^{\alpha-1}I+(\alpha-1)\|x\|^{\alpha-3}xx^{T}+\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\|x\|^{\alpha-3}\left(Bxx^{T}-xx^{T}B\right)

We must demonstrate that for any non-zero vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the quadratic form
v^{T}J_{sym}(x)v\geq 0
. Because

v^{T}(Bxx^{T}-xx^{T}B)v=(v^{T}Bx)(x^{T}v)-(v^{T}x)(x^{T}Bv),

and
x^{T}Bv=(B^{T}x)^{T}v=-(Bx)^{T}v=-v^{T}Bx
, we have
v^{T}(Bxx^{T}-xx^{T}B)v=2(v^{T}x)(v^{T}Bx)
. Then we have

v^{T}J_{sym}(x)v=\|x\|^{\alpha-1}\left[\|v\|^{2}-(1-\alpha)\frac{(v^{T}x)^{2}}{\|x\|^{2}}-(1-\alpha)\frac{(v^{T}x)(v^{T}Bx)}{\|x\|^{2}}\right]

Let
\hat{x}=\frac{x}{\|x\|}
be the unit vector in the direction of
x
. We can decompose
v
into a component parallel to
\hat{x}
and a component orthogonal to it. Without loss of generality, assume
\|v\|=1
and write
v=\cos\theta\hat{x}+\sin\theta\hat{w}
, where
\hat{w}
is a unit vector orthogonal to
\hat{x}
(
\hat{w}^{T}\hat{x}=0
).

First, we evaluate the two inner product term.
v^{T}\hat{x}=\cos\theta
;
v^{T}B\hat{x}=(\cos\theta\hat{x}^{T}+\sin\theta\hat{w}^{T})B\hat{x}=\sin\theta(\hat{w}^{T}B\hat{x})
, exploiting the fact that
\hat{x}^{T}B\hat{x}=0
. Substituting these into the bracketed term of the quadratic form yields:

\Gamma:=1-(1-\alpha)\cos^{2}\theta-(1-\alpha)\cos\theta\sin\theta(\hat{w}^{T}B\hat{x})

Using the identity
1=\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta
, this becomes:

\Gamma=\sin^{2}\theta+\alpha\cos^{2}\theta-(1-\alpha)(\hat{w}^{T}B\hat{x})\cos\theta\sin\theta

For
J_{sym}(x)
to be positive semi-definite, we require
\Gamma\geq 0
for all
\theta
and all allowable
\hat{w}
. We can view
\Gamma
as a quadratic form in terms of variables
X=\cos\theta
and
Y=\sin\theta
:

Q(X,Y)=\alpha X^{2}+Y^{2}-(1-\alpha)(\hat{w}^{T}B\hat{x})XY

This quadratic form is non-negative everywhere if and only if its discriminant with respect to the polynomial in
X/Y
is less than or equal to zero:

\left((1-\alpha)(\hat{w}^{T}B\hat{x})\right)^{2}-4\alpha\leq 0,

which leads to

|\hat{w}^{T}B\hat{x}|\leq\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.

By the definition of the spectral norm and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
|\hat{w}^{T}B\hat{x}|\leq\|\hat{w}\|\|B\hat{x}\|\leq\|B\|_{2}
.
Therefore, if we enforce the bound
\|B\|_{2}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}
, the discriminant condition is universally satisfied. Consequently,
J_{sym}(x)
is positive semi-definite for all
x\neq 0
, proving that
F
is monotone on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎
