 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 To evaluate the potential of Sonic Wafering™, a new manufacturing method that may lower the
 cost of high-efficiency photovoltaic materials. This work is to be conducted in support of the
 Department of Energy’s American Made Challenges Solar Prize.

 Summary of Research Results:

 This report contains Protected CRADA Information, which was produced on 2/21/2021 under
 CRADA No. CRD-19-00831 and is not to be further disclosed for a period of five (5) years from
 the date it was produced except as expressly provided for in the CRADA.

 The available Solar Prize fund was redirected from analysis to experimental demonstration
 prompting two modifications in agreement and funding levels. The analysis task was therefore
 omitted prior to the project.

 Task: NREL will grow multiple iterations of Structure 1 and Structure 2 described. Structure 1
 enables investigation of the accuracy of Sonic Wafering™. Structure 2 enables investigation of
 different passivation approaches on a double heterostructure. Samples will be measured before and
 after application of new passivation layers. NREL will process spalled samples into devices (e.g.
 solar cells and double heterostructures).

 Summary of Task Results:

 NREL grew specific GaAs solar cells (Structure 1) and test structures (Structure 2) that were
 requested by Crystal Sonic using metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD). These
 structures were used to understand the impact of Crystal Sonic’s “sonic wafering” technology on
 device performance and study whether amorphous silicon can be used to passivate GaAs surfaces
 for photovoltaic applications.

 Wafers and samples of Structure 1 and Structure 2 were shipped to Crystal Sonic for spalling, and
 then returned to NREL for device processing and cell measurements. All deliverables in the JWS
 were delivered to the participant within the planned time frame.

 NREL was able to process multiple fully-functioning solar cells from exfoliated III-V thin films
 with promising performance. Figure 2 shows external quantum efficiency (EQE) and current
 density vs voltage data for a Crystal Sonic spalled device and a baseline device (without any
 antireflective coatings applied). The spalled device performs nominally as well as a control sample,
 showing that the Sonic Wafering process does not damage the material quality of the GaAs.

 The preliminary experimental results from this project were used to enable NREL, Arizona State
 University, and Crystal Sonic to win funding from SETO through their FY19 funding process. For
 greater detail on the process and analysis, the researchers are publishing a forthcoming journal
 article titled “Lift-off of GaAs solar cells: A path to flat surfaces and 0.5 $/Wp” that is currently
 under preparation.

 There were originally plans to have the Strategic Energy Analysis Center do some technoeconomic
 modeling related to Crystal Sonic’s Epitaxial Lift Off process, but the work was not completed
 due to bandwidth issues for the modeling team. In Mod 1 to this agreement, those funds were
 transferred to be used for experimental work.
