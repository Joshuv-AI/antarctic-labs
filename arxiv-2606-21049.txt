Locality of rough path lifts
Ilya Chevyrev, Emilio Ferrucci

Abstract
Every Hölder continuous path X X admits a geometric rough path lift 𝑿 \boldsymbol{X} by the Lyons–Victoir extension theorem. A natural question that emerges when lifting more than one path at once is that of locality , namely whether the lift 𝑿 s , t \boldsymbol{X}_{s,t} only depends on the increments X s , u X_{s,u} , u ∈ [ s , t ] u\in[s,t] . We investigate the locality of rough path lifts in deterministic and stochastic settings. On the deterministic side, we show that no local, homogeneous rough path lift can be defined on γ \gamma -Hölder paths for all γ ≤ 1 / 2 \gamma\leq 1/2 . More strongly, we show that no Lévy area can be defined which is at the same time bounded, with no further regularity assumptions, and either local and homogeneous or time translation-invariant. We moreover show that the boundedness requirement is sharp: an unbounded, local, time translation-invariant, and bilinear Lévy area can be defined on all continuous paths. On the stochastic side, we classify all local, square-integrable rough path lifts of multi-dimensional fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter H ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ] H\in(0,1/2] . For H ≤ 1 / 4 H\leq 1/4 , we show that no such lifts exist, while for H > 1 / 4 H>1/4 , we show that all such lifts are stochastic translations of the canonical rough path. We further refine the classification by requiring invariance in law under time translation, scaling, and coordinate permutation, and show that only the canonical lift satisfies these constraints except at H = 1 / 3 H=1/3 , for which there is a one-parameter family of lifts.

Introduction

A rough path over an
\alpha
-Hölder path
X\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

is a collection of two-parameter functions
\boldsymbol{X}^{(n)}_{s,t}
,
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, which plays the role of iterated integrals

\upintop\nolimits_{s<u_{1}<\ldots<u_{n}<t}\,\mathrm{d}X_{u_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\,\mathrm{d}X_{u_{n}}\;.

(1)

Introduced by Lyons
[
39
]
, rough paths are a powerful tool to solve differential equations driven by
X
when
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

When
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
, there is a canonical and unique choice for
\boldsymbol{X}^{(n)}_{s,t}
given by Young integration
[
46
,
38
]
.
For
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
, rough path lifts are no longer unique, but a fundamental result of Lyons–Victoir
[
36
]
is that a rough path exists above every path.
This result is further refined in
[
44
,
3
]
by making the lift constructive and even continuous
(see
[
28
,
5
]
for related results in regularity structures).
All of these constructions, however, are non-canonical and rely on arbitrary choices.

Given that,
\smash{\boldsymbol{X}^{(n)}_{s,t}}
is an abstraction of the (otherwise undefined) iterated integral (
1
),
a natural requirement of any rough path lift is that it is
local
, meaning that
\smash{\boldsymbol{X}^{(n)}_{s,t}}
depends only on the increments of
X
in the interval
[s,t]
.
In the same spirit, one can ask that the lift is
time translation-invariant
, meaning that
\smash{\boldsymbol{X}^{(n)}_{s,t}=\boldsymbol{X}^{(n)}_{u,v}}
whenever the increments of
X
on
[s,t]
and
[u,v]
are equal.
See
Figure 1
for an illustration of these concepts.
These properties are satisfied when the integral
is
defined canonically, e.g. in the Young regime.
The abstract lifts provided in
[
36
,
44
,
3
]
do
not
satisfy either property since data from
X
on the whole interval is needed to define
\boldsymbol{X}
on any subinterval.
A natural question is therefore whether rough path lifts exist that are local and/or time-translation invariant.
In this article, we investigate this question in a deterministic and stochastic setting.

\mathbb{A}

.

.
(The paths should be thought of as being multi-dimensional.)

Deterministic lifts.

Our first contribution is to show that, under further mild assumptions (e.g. bilinearity), no local lift is possible for
\alpha\leq 1/2
.
Specifically, we show that there cannot be a rough path lift of planar
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder paths that is either (a) local and homogeneous (the latter just means that the map
(X,Y)\mapsto\upintop\nolimits\!X\,\mathrm{d}Y
behaves
\mathbb R
-bilinearly) or (b) time translation-invariant.
In fact, we replace the notion of rough path lift with the weaker notion of an ‘area process’, which we require to be merely bounded but not Hölder-continuous.
See
Section 2
for a precise statement.
Our proof is obtained by considering variants of the Lévy C curve
[
34
]
,
[
15
, Ch. 12]
,
first introduced by Cesaro
[
9
]
and Faber
[
17
]
; see
Figure 2
.

A central tool in the construction of rough path lifts and rough integrals is the concept of sewing maps (see
Subsection 2.1
).
Sewing maps were introduced in
[
27
,
18
]
for Hölder exponents
\gamma>1
,
in which case there is only one sewing map,
and in
[
3
]
for
\gamma\leq 1
.
As a consequence of our non-existence results,
we prove in
Subsection 2.1
and
Subsection 2.1
that there are no homogeneous and local or time-translation invariant sewing maps for
\gamma\leq 1
, answering the analogous question of locality in
[
3
, Remark 2.10]
in the negative.

We conclude
Section 2
by showing that, for non-existence, the boundedness requirement of area processes is sharp.
Specifically, we show that there is a version of sewing maps on continuous germs that respect the usual algebraic (but not analytic) constraints and which is linear, local, and time translation-invariant (but necessarily unbounded);
see
Subsection 2.2
and
Subsection 2.2
.
We also show in
Subsection 2.2
that, for discontinuous germs, such local sewing maps still exist but time translation-invariant ones do not.
While not directly related to rough path theory due to lack of regularity requirements, we find these results somewhat surprising, especially that time translation-invariance is the sole obstruction to unbounded sewing maps on arbitrary germs.

Stochastic lifts.

When the path
X
is random, the probabilistic structure often leads to a (more) canonical choice of rough path lift.
Stochastic processes for which rough paths have been constructed and studied include Gaussian processes
[
12
,
25
,
7
,
20
]
, (càdlàg) semi-martingales
[
24
,
10
]
, and Markov processes
[
1
,
23
,
8
,
11
]
;
see the books
[
35
,
22
,
21
]
for further details.
In all of these examples, a ‘canonical’ lift above
X
is given as the limit in probability of the lift of bounded variation (e.g. piecewise linear) approximations of
X
.
Other lifts also naturally arise, e.g. the Itô lift for semi-martingales, or
lifts that are neither Stratonovich nor Itô that emerge in various physical systems
[
19
,
32
,
26
,
16
]
.
It is easy to verify that the above mentioned stochastic lifts are local, understood in terms of measurability with respect to local increments of
X
.
(This does not contradict the non-existence of local rough path lifts for
\alpha\leq 1/2
in the deterministic setting of
Section 2
because, in the stochastic setting, one is only asking for a lift to exist almost surely.)

The stochastic process we focus on in this article is fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
(
H
-fBm),
which is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous for
\alpha<H
.
It is useful to distinguish three regimes:
H>1/2
,
H\in(1/4,1/2]
, and
H\leq 1/4
. The case
H>1/2
is the Young regime in which case rough path lifts are unique.
For
H\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}]
, it was first shown in
[
12
]
that piecewise linear approximations of
H
-fBm converge in
L^{2}
to a rough path lift; this is called the canonical lift.
For
H\leq 1/4
, the same work shows that these approximations have no subsequential limit in probability.
Other constructions do give rough path lifts for
H\leq 1/4
by exploiting the Gaussian Volterra structure
[
45
,
42
]
, but they are not local in the above sense and, for
H>1/4
, do not reduce to the canonical lift.
It was shown in
[
30
]
that another natural way to treat the regime
H\leq 1/4
is by ‘variance renormalisation’, which leads to a convergence in distribution to a random rough path above the constant zero-path.

Our main contribution in the stochastic setting is
Section 3
, which classifies all local, square-integrable lifts of
H
-fBm for
H\in(0,1/2]
.
We show that, for
H\leq 1/4
, no such lifts exist.
On the other hand, for
H>1/4
all such lifts
\boldsymbol{X}
are suitable translations of the canonical lift
\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}
, given by Wiener integrals against a deterministic rough path above the zero-path.
We prove this by expanding the difference
\boldsymbol{X}-\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}
in Wiener chaos and using the Chen identity to show that all its chaos projections vanish except for the zero-th (at degree
2
) and zero-th and first (at degree
3
).
In
Section 3
we further classify which of these lifts are stationary, scale invariant, or invariant with respect to permutation of the spatial coordinates. An unexpected (for us) result is that, apart from the canonical lift, the only other lifts for which all three properties hold occur at
H=\frac{1}{3}
, and there is a one-dimensional continuum of them.

The methodology of Wiener chaos decomposition, the main technique used throughout
Section 3
seems to fall apart as soon as square integrability of the rough path is dropped. We regard the extension of these results to merely measurable lifts as an interesting open problem.

\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{j/4,(j+1)/4}(Z^{n+2})=\frac{b_{n}}{4}\;,\qquad j=0,1,2,3\;.

Since the signed area formed by the four line segments
(Z_{0},Z^{n+2}_{1/4}),\ldots,(Z^{n+2}_{3/4},Z^{n+2}_{1})
is
-1/2
,
we obtain by Chen’s identity (
4
)

b_{n+2}=\sum_{j=0}^{3}\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{j/4,(j+1)/4}(Z^{n+2})-\frac{1}{2}=b_{n}-\frac{1}{2}\;,

which proves (
7
).
It follows by induction that, for all
m\geq 0
,

b_{2m}=-\frac{m}{2}\;.

Since adding the horizontal segment does not change the area, it follows that, for every
m\geq 0
,

\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{0,1}(\widetilde{Z}^{m})=\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{0,1}(Z^{2m})=-\frac{m}{2}\;.

(8)

Since each restriction
Z|_{[2^{-n-1},2^{-n}]}
is piecewise linear with axis-parallel pieces,
\mathbb{A}_{2^{-n-1},2^{-n}}(Z)
is given by the classical signed area (
6
).
Since spatial scaling by
2^{-n/2}
scales the area by
2^{-n}
,
it follows from (
8
) that

\mathbb{A}_{2^{-n-1},2^{-n}}(Z)=2^{-n}\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{0,1}(\widetilde{Z}^{2^{n}})=-2^{-n}\frac{2^{n}}{2}=-1/2\;.

Since
Z|_{[2^{-n-1},2^{-n}]}
is a closed loop, it follows from Chen’s identity that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{A}_{2^{-n},1}(Z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{A}_{2^{-k-1},2^{-k}}(Z)=-n/2\;.

This contradicts the fact that
\mathbb{A}(Z)\in B
, since elements of
B
are bounded on
\triangle
.
∎

Proof of
Section 2
(b)
.

We construct a path
Z\in C^{1/2}\times C^{1/2}
such that no area process on
Z
can be time translation-invariant.

Let
C\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the Lévy C curve, i.e.
C=\lim_{n\to\infty}Z^{n}
where
Z^{n}
is the
n
-th polygonal approximation to the Lévy C curve defined in the proof of
Section 2
. Note that

C_{0}=(0,0)
and
C_{1}=(1,0)
.
For
n\geq 1
, let
L^{n}\colon[0,2^{-n+1}]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the loop obtained by concatenating two paths:
on
[0,2^{-n}]
we traverse the scaled copy

t\mapsto 2^{-n/2}C(2^{n}t)\;,

and on
[2^{-n},2^{-n+1}]
we traverse its image under the rotation by
\pi
, translated so that the resulting path is a loop.
Thus
L^{n}
has time-length
2^{-n+1}
and spatial size
2^{-n/2}
.
(Contrary to the curves
\widetilde{Z}^{n}
in the proof of
Section 2
, we do not close
L^{n}
with straight line segments.)

For
m\geq 0
, define the sequences

N_{m}=2^{2m+1}\;,\quad K_{m}=2^{N_{m}-m-2}\geq 1\;.

Since

K_{m}\,2^{1-N_{m}}=2^{-m-1}\;,

we may define a path
Z\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
by setting
Z_{0}=0
and, on each dyadic block
[2^{-m-1},2^{-m}]

concatenating
K_{m}
copies of the loop
L^{N_{m}}
.

Since each
L^{N_{m}}
is a loop,
Z
vanishes at the endpoints of all copies and in particular at every dyadic point
2^{-m}
.
Moreover, we claim that
Z\in C^{1/2}\times C^{1/2}
.
Indeed, on each half of
L^{n}
, the path is a translate of

t\mapsto 2^{-n/2}C(2^{n}t)\;,

which has the same
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder seminorm as
C
.
Since
Z
vanishes on the endpoint of every copy of
L^{N_{m}}
, the
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder seminorm of
Z
is bounded by a multiple of that of
C
. Thus
Z\in C^{1/2}\times C^{1/2}
.

Assume now, for contradiction, that there exists a time translation-invariant area process
\mathbb{A}
on
Z
.
For even
n\geq 4
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mathcal{I}_{n}^{r}
denote the collection of intervals

I=[s,s+2^{-n}]
such that the restricted path

u\mapsto Z_{s+u}-Z_{s}\;,\qquad u\in[0,2^{-n}]\;,

coincides with

u\mapsto 2^{-n/2}R_{r\pi/2}C(2^{n}u)\;,

where
R_{\theta}
is the rotation of the plane by
\theta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
.
By construction, since
N_{0}=2
, for every even
n\geq 4
and every
r\in\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
, the set
\mathcal{I}_{n}^{r}
is non-empty.

If
I,J\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{r}
, then the corresponding restricted paths are equal up to a time shift inside the path
Z
.
Therefore
\mathbb{A}_{I}(Z)=\mathbb{A}_{J}(Z)
by translation-invariance of
\mathbb{A}
, and we denote

a_{n}^{r}=\mathbb{A}_{I}(Z)\;.

Fix an even
n\geq 4
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
, and let
I\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{r}
.
By the two-step self-similarity of the Lévy C curve, the restriction of
Z
to
I
is the concatenation of four subintervals of length
2^{-n-2}
belonging respectively to the classes

r+1\;,\quad r\;,\quad r\;,\quad r-1\;.

Applying Chen’s identity on
I
, we obtain

a_{n}^{r}=a_{n+2}^{r+1}+2a_{n+2}^{r}+a_{n+2}^{r-1}+2^{-n}c\;,

(9)

where
c=-1/2
is the signed area of the two-step Lévy pattern (i.e. the signed area of the curve
Z^{2}
in
Figure 2
).
Now define

b_{n}=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}^{r}\;.

The recursion (
9
), with
c=-1/2
, implies that

b_{n}=4b_{n+2}-2^{-n+1}\;,

(10)

or equivalently that
B_{n}=2^{n}b_{n}
satisfies the recursion

B_{n}=B_{n+2}-2
,
which implies
B_{n}=B_{4}+n-4
, and therefore

b_{n}=2^{-n}(2^{4}b_{4}+n-4)\;.

(11)

Observe now that
a copy of the loop
L^{n}
is obtained by concatenating one interval of type
r=0
and one interval of type
r=2
,
therefore by Chen’s identity, the area of
L^{n}
is

a_{n}^{0}+a_{n}^{2}=2\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}}a_{n+2}^{r}+2^{-n+1}c=2b_{n+2}-2^{-n}=b_{n}/2\;,

where again
c=-1/2
and we used (
10
) in the last equality.
For
m\geq 1
, since
N_{m}\geq 4
and

Z|_{[2^{-m-1},2^{-m}]}
is
K_{m}=2^{N_{m}-m-2}
concatenations of the loop
L^{N_{m}}
,
we thus obtain by Chen’s identity, using (
11
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{2^{-m-1},2^{-m}}(Z)=K_{m}b_{N_{m}}/2

\displaystyle=2^{N_{m}-m-3}\bigl(2^{-N_{m}}(2^{4}b_{4}+N_{m}-4)\bigr)

\displaystyle=2^{-m-3}(2^{4}b_{4}+N_{m}-4)\;.

Since we have chosen
N_{m}=2^{2m+1}
, the final term is larger than
1
for all
m\geq m_{0}
(where
m_{0}
depends only on
b_{4}
), which implies
that
\mathbb{A}_{2^{-m-1},2^{-m_{0}}}(Z)\geq m-m_{0}+1
,
which contradicts the fact that
\mathbb{A}(Z)\in B
.
∎

The preceding proofs use modified versions of the Lévy C curve and its polygonal approximations.
If one instead makes stronger assumptions on
\mathbb{A}
, namely homogeneity, and parametrisation-, rotation-, and spatial translation-invariance, then the unmodified Lévy C curve already gives a contradiction.
More precisely, suppose there exists a homogeneous area process
\mathbb{A}\colon C^{1/2}\times C^{1/2}\to B

which is invariant under the addition of constants in the sense that
\mathbb{A}(a+Z)=\mathbb{A}(Z)
for
a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and parametrisation-invariant in the sense that, for every affine increasing bijection
\phi\colon[0,1]\to[s,t]
,

\mathbb{A}_{0,1}(Z_{\phi})=\mathbb{A}_{s,t}(Z)

where
Z_{\phi}=Z\circ\phi
. These properties imply strong locality in the sense of
Section 2
: if
Z^{\prime}_{s+\tau,u+\tau}=Z_{s,u}
for all
u\in[s,t]
then

\mathbb{A}_{s,t}(Z)=\mathbb{A}_{0,1}(Z_{\phi})=\mathbb{A}_{0,1}((Z_{s}-Z^{\prime}_{s+\tau})+Z^{\prime}_{\phi(\cdot)+\tau})=\mathbb{A}_{0,1}(Z^{\prime}_{\phi(\cdot)+\tau})=\mathbb{A}_{s+\tau,t+\tau}(Z^{\prime}).

Assume further that
\mathbb{A}
is rotation-invariant
in the sense that
\mathbb{A}_{s,t}(R_{\theta}Z)=\mathbb{A}_{s,t}(Z)
, where we recall that
R_{\theta}
is the rotation of the plane by
\theta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
C=(X,Y)\in C^{1/2}\times C^{1/2}
be the Lévy C curve.
Then two self-similar halves of
C
have the same area as
C
, scaled by the factor
\frac{1}{2}
,
hence Chen’s identity implies

\begin{split}\mathbb{A}_{0,1}(C)&=\mathbb{A}_{0,\frac{1}{2}}(C)+\mathbb{A}_{\frac{1}{2},1}(C)+\frac{1}{2}\bigl(X_{0,\frac{1}{2}}Y_{\frac{1}{2},1}-Y_{0,\frac{1}{2}}X_{\frac{1}{2},1}\bigr)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{A}_{0,1}(C)+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{A}_{0,1}(C)-\frac{1}{4}\;,\end{split}

which is a contradiction. More generally, for a self-similar curve with
m
pieces and spatial dilation scale
\sqrt{\lambda}
, the same computation gives

(1-m\lambda)\mathbb{A}_{0,1}=a,

where
a
is the polygonal area contribution coming from Chen’s identity; the Lévy C curve is the critical case
m\lambda=1
with
a\neq 0
. Other fractals could be constructed with
\lambda m>1
, and for such fractals the optimal Hölder regularity (which is easily seen to be
\log(1/\lambda)/(2\log m)
) would be less than
1/2
, but parametrisation invariance would not immediately yield a contradiction (but arguments similar to those of
Section 2
still would).

2.1
Non-existence of local sewing

We now discuss consequences of
Section 2
and
Section 2
for sewing maps.
Existence of (linear) sewing maps in different contexts was shown in
[
27
,
18
,
3
]
.
We give below an abstract definition of such maps and deduce non-existence results under minimal regularity assumptions.
The link between sewing maps and area processes (and thus rough path lifts)
is discussed after
Subsection 2.1
below.

Denote

\triangle^{3}[s,t]=\{(a,b,c)\in[s,t]^{3}\,:\,a\leq b\leq c\}\;,\qquad\triangle^{3}=\triangle^{3}[0,1]\;,

(12)

and set

\mathcal{C}_{2}=C(\triangle)\;,\qquad\mathcal{C}_{3}=C(\triangle^{3})\;.

For
k=2,3
and
\gamma>0
, define

\mathcal{C}_{k}^{\gamma}\coloneqq\left\{\Xi\in\mathcal{C}_{k}\,:\,\|\Xi\|_{\gamma}<\infty\right\}\;,\qquad\|\Xi\|_{\gamma}\coloneqq\sup_{0\leq t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}\leq 1}\frac{|\Xi_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}}|}{(t_{k}-t_{1})^{\gamma}}\;.

Define further

\mathcal{C}_{2}^{1,\log}\coloneqq\left\{A\in\mathcal{C}_{2}\,:\,\|A\|_{1,\log}<\infty\right\}\;,\qquad\|A\|_{1,\log}\coloneqq\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq 1}\frac{|A_{s,t}|}{(t-s)(1+|\log(t-s)|)}\;.

Define the linear map
\delta_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}^{\triangle^{2}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\triangle^{3}}
by

(\delta_{2}A)_{s,u,t}=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}\;.

(13)

Note that
\delta_{2}
maps
\mathcal{C}_{2}
into
\mathcal{C}_{3}
.

Definition 2.13
.

A
\gamma
-sewing map
is a right inverse to
\delta_{2}
on
\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\gamma}
,
namely a map

\Lambda\colon\delta_{2}(\mathcal{C}_{2})\cap\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\gamma}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}

such that

\delta_{2}\Lambda=\mathbbm 1

with
\Lambda\Xi\in\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\gamma}
for
\gamma\neq 1
, and

\Lambda\Xi\in\mathcal{C}_{2}^{1,\log}
for
\gamma=1
.

Remark 2.14
.

Denoting
\mathcal{C}_{1}=C[0,1]
and defining
\delta_{1}\colon\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}
by

(\delta_{1}f)_{s,t}=f_{t}-f_{s}
,
the maps
\mathcal{C}_{1}\xrightarrow{\delta_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{2}\xrightarrow{\delta_{2}}\mathcal{C}_{3}\cdots

are the first terms in an exact cochain complex, see
[
27
, §2.2]
.
The sequence
(\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{\gamma},\delta_{\bullet})
is a sub-cochain complex. A linear
\gamma
-sewing map
\Lambda
yields a linear
integration map

\mathcal{I}\colon\delta_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\gamma})\to\mathcal{C}_{1}
by

(\mathcal{I}A)_{0}=0\;,\qquad\delta_{1}\mathcal{I}A=A-\Lambda\delta_{2}A\;,

(14)

which satisfies

\mathcal{I}\delta_{1}f=f-f_{0}

and similar analytic properties as
\Lambda
. Conversely, an integration map defines a
\gamma
-sewing map by the identity (
14
) applied to
\Xi=\delta_{2}A\in\delta_{2}(\mathcal{C}_{2})\cap\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\gamma}
(this definition does not depend on the choice of
A\in\delta_{2}^{-1}(\Xi)
).

This link between sewing and integration maps is central in rough path theory
[
27
,
18
,
3
,
21
]
.
We will exploit this link in the construction of strongly local sewing maps under different analytic conditions in the proof of
Subsection 2.2
.

For
\gamma>1
, there exists a unique
\gamma
-sewing map; it is given by Riemann–Stieltjes–Young integration and is a continuous linear map, see
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
or
[
21
, Lemma 4.2]
.
For
\gamma\leq 1
,
[
3
, Theorem 3.2 and Proposition 3.4]
, building on
[
36
,
44
]
, construct continuous linear sewing maps, but their construction is non-local: the value of
\Lambda\Xi
on an interval
[s,t]
may depend on values of
\Xi
outside
\triangle^{3}[s,t]
.
It was left open, see
[
3
, Remark 2.10]
, whether one can choose such a sewing map locally.

Section 2
and
Section 2
give a negative answer to this question for all
\gamma\leq 1
under the natural condition that
\Lambda(0)=0
, see
Subsection 2.1
.
In fact, this and
Subsection 2.1
prove much stronger results:
any right-inverse
\delta_{2}\Lambda=\mathbbm 1
taking values in the space of bounded functions cannot vanish locally at zero or be time translation-invariant.
The following definition makes these notions precise.

Definition 2.15
.

Recall the set
B\supset\mathcal{C}_{2}
from (
3
).
A
weak
\gamma
-sewing map
is a map
\Lambda\colon\delta_{2}(\mathcal{C}_{2})\cap\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\gamma}\to B
such that
\delta_{2}\Lambda=\mathbbm 1
.
We say that

(i)

\Lambda
is
local
if for all
(s,t)\in\triangle
and
\Xi,\Xi^{\prime}\in\delta_{2}(\mathcal{C}_{2})\cap\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\gamma}
such that
\Xi|_{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}=\Xi^{\prime}|_{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}
, it follows that
\Lambda\Xi|_{\triangle[s,t]}=\Lambda\Xi^{\prime}|_{\triangle[s,t]}
,

(ii)

\Lambda

vanishes locally at zero

if for all
\Xi\in\delta_{2}(\mathcal{C}_{2})\cap\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\gamma}
and
(s,t)\in\triangle
such that
\Xi|_{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}=0
,
one has
\Lambda\Xi|_{\triangle[s,t]}=0
,

(iii)

\Lambda
is
time translation-invariant
if for all
(s,t)\in\triangle
and
\tau>0
such that
t+\tau\leq 1
and
\Xi\in\delta_{2}(\mathcal{C}_{2})\cap\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\gamma}

such that
\Xi_{a,b,c}=\Xi_{a+\tau,b+\tau,c+\tau}
for all
(a,b,c)\in\triangle^{3}[s,t]
,
one has
(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,t}=(\Lambda\Xi)_{s+\tau,t+\tau}
.

Note that a
\gamma
-sewing map is always a weak
\gamma
-sewing map, but the converse is not true.
Given
\gamma\leq 1
and
(X,Y)\in C^{\gamma/2}\times C^{\gamma/2}
, consider the ‘germ’ defined by

A\in\mathcal{C}_{2},\quad A_{s,t}=-\frac{1}{2}(X_{s}Y_{s,t}-Y_{s}X_{s,t})\;.

A direct computation shows that

(\delta_{2}A)_{s,u,t}=\frac{1}{2}(X_{s,u}Y_{u,t}-Y_{s,u}X_{u,t})

and thus
\delta_{2}A\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}_{3}
.
A weak
\gamma
-sewing map then defines an area process in the sense of
Section 2
by

\mathbb{A}_{s,t}(X,Y)=(\Lambda\delta_{2}A)_{s,t}\;.

(15)

Consider now
Z=(X,Y)
which is an axis path on
[u,v]\subset[0,1]
, i.e.
Z_{t}=Z_{u}+(t-u)ae_{i}
for all
t\in[u,v]
for some
i=1,2
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then
\delta_{2}A|_{\triangle^{3}[u,v]}=0
.
Therefore, if
\Lambda
vanishes locally at zero, then
\mathbb{A}_{u,v}(Z)=0

and thus
\mathbb{A}
vanishes on axis paths in the sense of
Section 2
.
We thus obtain a corollary of
Section 2
.

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1]
.
There is no weak
\gamma
-sewing map which vanishes locally at zero.
In particular, there is no local weak
\gamma
-sewing map
\Lambda
such that
\Lambda(0)=0
.

By similar considerations, a time-translation-invariant weak
\gamma
-sewing map would yield a time translation-invariant area process on
C^{\gamma/2}\times C^{\gamma/2}
.
We thus obtain the following corollary of
Section 2

(b)
.

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1]
.
There is no weak
\gamma
-sewing map which is time translation-invariant.

2.2
Local unbounded sewing maps and area processes

In the next
Subsection 2.2
, we study the effects of enlarging the target space of
\Lambda
from the space
B
of bounded functions to all of
\mathbb{R}^{\triangle}

as well as enlarging the domain of
\Lambda
.
With target space
\mathbb{R}^{\triangle}
, we show that there do exist strongly local (in particular local and time translation-invariant) sewing maps on the domain
\delta_{2}\mathcal{C}_{2}
, see
Subsection 2.2
(i)
.
Moreover, somewhat surprisingly,
on the larger domain
\delta_{2}\mathbb{R}^{\triangle}
, there also exist local sewing maps but
not
time translation-invariant ones (
Subsection 2.2
(ii)
-
(iii)
).
We obtain in
Subsection 2.2
the existence of (strongly) local area processes taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{\triangle}
and with domain
C[0,1]\times C[0,1]
or
\mathbb{R}^{[0,1]}\times\mathbb{R}^{[0,1]}
.

Denote

\mathcal{C}_{2}[s,t]=C(\triangle[s,t])\;,\quad\mathcal{C}_{3}[s,t]=C(\triangle^{3}[s,t])\;,

where we recall the notation from (
2
) and (
12
).
Define
\delta_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}^{\triangle[s,t]}\to\mathbb{R}^{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}
by (
13
) and denote

E[s,t]=\delta_{2}\mathcal{C}_{2}[s,t]\subset\mathcal{C}_{3}[s,t]\;,\qquad F[s,t]=\delta_{2}\mathbb{R}^{\triangle[s,t]}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}\;.

Theorem 2.18
.

(i)

There exists a linear map
\Lambda\colon E[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{\triangle}

such that
\delta_{2}\Lambda=\mathbbm 1
and
\Lambda
is strongly local (cf.
Section 2
) in the sense that, for all
\tau\geq 0
and
(s,t)\in\triangle
such that
t+\tau\leq 1
and
\Xi,\Xi^{\prime}\in E[0,1]
such that
\Xi_{a,b,c}=\Xi^{\prime}_{a+\tau,b+\tau,c+\tau}
for all
(a,b,c)\in\triangle^{3}[s,t]
,
one has

(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,t}=(\Lambda\Xi^{\prime})_{s+\tau,t+\tau}
.

(ii)

There exists a linear map
\Lambda\colon F[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{\triangle}

such that
\delta_{2}\Lambda=\mathbbm 1

and which is local in the sense that, for all
(s,t)\in\triangle
and
\Xi,\Xi^{\prime}\in F[0,1]
such that
\Xi|_{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}=\Xi^{\prime}|_{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}
,
one has

(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,t}=(\Lambda\Xi^{\prime})_{s,t}
.

(iii)

There does not exist a map

\Lambda\colon F[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{\triangle}

such that

\delta_{2}\Lambda=\mathbbm 1

and which is time translation-invariant in the sense of
Subsection 2.1
(iii)
.
In fact, there exists
\Xi\in F[0,1]
which is translation-invariant in the sense that
\Xi_{s,u,t}=\Xi_{s+\tau,u+\tau,t+\tau}
for all
(s,u,t),(s+\tau,u+\tau,t+\tau)\in\triangle^{3}

and for which there exists no
A\in\mathbb{R}^{\triangle}
with
\delta_{2}A=\Xi
and which is translation-invariant in the sense that
A_{s,t}=A_{s+\tau,t+\tau}
for all
(s,t),(s+\tau,t+\tau)\in\triangle
.

Remark 2.19
.

The assumption that the domain of
\Lambda
is
\delta_{2}\mathcal{C}_{2}
in
(i)
is stronger than necessary.
The proof below reveals that
\mathcal{C}_{2}
can be replaced by bounded measurable functions that are suitably ‘regulated’ on the diagonal.

Proof.

(i)
We follow the idea of
Subsection 2.1

and construct first a ‘strongly local integration map’.
Let
\mathcal{V}_{c}\subset\mathbb{R}^{(0,1]}
be the subspace of all functions

f\colon(0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
for which

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}f(\varepsilon)

exists.
This limit defines a linear functional on

\mathcal{V}_{c}
.
Extending a basis of

\mathcal{V}_{c}
to a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{(0,1]}
(which is possible by the axiom of choice), we obtain
a linear functional

\operatorname{Lim}_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\colon\mathbb{R}^{(0,1]}\to\mathbb{R}

such that

\operatorname{Lim}_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}f(\varepsilon)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}f(\varepsilon)

whenever the ordinary limit on the right-hand side exists.

For
0<L\leq 1
and
A\in\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,L]
, define for

0<\varepsilon<L
the ‘approximate integral’

J_{L}^{\varepsilon}(A)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\upintop\nolimits_{0}^{L-\varepsilon}(A_{r,r+\varepsilon}-A_{r,r})\,\mathrm{d}r\;.

(16)

For
\varepsilon\geq L
, define
J_{L}^{\varepsilon}(A)=0
. Now define the ‘integration map’
I_{L}\colon\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,L]\to\mathbb{R}
by

I_{L}(A)=\operatorname{Lim}_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}J_{L}^{\varepsilon}(A)\;.

For
L=0
, we also define
I_{0}\colon\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,0]\to\mathbb{R}
by
I_{0}=0
.
Since
J_{L}^{\varepsilon}\colon\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,L]\to\mathbb{R}
is linear,
I_{L}\colon\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,L]\to\mathbb{R}
is also linear.

We now prove two properties of
I_{L}
.
First, for
A\in\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,L]
and
0\leq l\leq L
, define
A^{0,l}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,l]
and
A^{l,L}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,L-l]
by

\displaystyle A^{0,l}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=A_{s,t},\qquad 0\leq s\leq t\leq l\;,

\displaystyle A^{l,L}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=A_{l+s,l+t},\qquad 0\leq s\leq t\leq L-l\;.

We claim that
I_{L}
is additive in the sense that

I_{L}(A)=I_{l}(A^{0,l})+I_{L-l}(A^{l,L})\;.

(17)

Indeed, for
l=0
or
l=L
, the claim follows from
I_{0}=0
, so assume that
0<l<L
.
Taking
0<\varepsilon<\min(l,L-l)
, splitting the integral in (
16
) into the intervals
[0,l-\varepsilon]
,
[l-\varepsilon,l]
and
[l,L-\varepsilon]
,
and remarking that the final integral is precisely
J_{L-l}^{\varepsilon}(A^{l,L})
,
we obtain

J_{L}^{\varepsilon}(A)=J_{l}^{\varepsilon}(A^{0,l})+J_{L-l}^{\varepsilon}(A^{l,L})+R_{\varepsilon}\;,

where

R_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\upintop\nolimits_{l-\varepsilon}^{l}(A_{r,r+\varepsilon}-A_{r,r})\,\mathrm{d}r.

Note that
|R_{\varepsilon}|\leq\sup_{r\in[l-\varepsilon,l]}|A_{r,r+\varepsilon}-A_{r,r}|
and that the right-hand side converges to zero as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0

because
A
is continuous at
(l,l)
.
Applying
\operatorname{Lim}_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}
proves the claim (
17
).

Second, for
L\in[0,1]
, we claim that if
K\in\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,L]
satisfies

\delta_{2}K=0
,
then

I_{L}(K)=K_{0,L}\;.

(18)

To see this, define
g_{t}=K_{0,t}
which by
\delta_{2}K=0
satisfies

K_{s,t}=g_{t}-g_{s}

for
0\leq s\leq t\leq L
.
Note that, since we are working on the closed simplex,
\delta_{2}K_{r,r,r}=-K_{r,r}=0

for every
r\in[0,L]
, and in particular
g_{0}=K_{0,0}=0
.
This also shows (
18
) for
L=0
since
I_{0}(K)=0=K_{0,0}
.
On the other hand, if
L>0
, then

J_{L}^{\varepsilon}(K)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\upintop\nolimits_{0}^{L-\varepsilon}K_{r,r+\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\upintop\nolimits_{0}^{L-\varepsilon}(g_{r+\varepsilon}-g_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\upintop\nolimits_{L-\varepsilon}^{L}g_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r-\upintop\nolimits_{0}^{\varepsilon}g_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)\;.

(19)

Since
K
is continuous, so is
g
and thus

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}J_{L}^{\varepsilon}(K)=g_{L}-g_{0}=K_{0,L}\;.

Since the limit as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
of the left-hand side of (
19
) is also equal to
I_{L}(K)
, we obtain (
18
).

We now proceed to define a sewing map on
[0,L]
for
L\in[0,1]
.
For
\xi\in E[0,L]
,
choose any
A\in\mathcal{C}_{2}[0,L]
such that

\delta_{2}A=\xi

and define

\lambda_{L}(\xi)=A_{0,L}-I_{L}(A)\;.

We first claim that
\lambda_{L}(\xi)
is independent of the choice of
A
. Indeed, if
\delta_{2}A^{\prime}=\xi
, then
K\coloneqq A-A^{\prime}
satisfies
\delta_{2}K=0
, hence, by (
18
),

I_{L}(K)=K_{0,L}\;.

Therefore, by linearity of
I_{L}
and writing
A=A^{\prime}+K
,

A_{0,L}-I_{L}(A)=A^{\prime}_{0,L}+K_{0,L}-I_{L}(A^{\prime})-I_{L}(K)=A^{\prime}_{0,L}-I_{L}(A^{\prime}).

Thus

\lambda_{L}\colon E[0,L]\to\mathbb{R}

is a well-defined linear map.

Next, we claim that
\lambda_{L}
satisfies the sewing identity

\lambda_{L}(\xi)=\lambda_{l}(\xi^{0,l})+\lambda_{L-l}(\xi^{l,L})+\xi_{0,l,L}

(20)

for all
0\leq l\leq L
. Indeed, if
\xi=\delta_{2}A
, then
\xi^{0,l}=\delta_{2}A^{0,l}
and

\xi^{l,L}=\delta_{2}A^{l,L}
, and thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{L}(\xi)

\displaystyle=A_{0,L}-I_{L}(A)

\displaystyle=A_{0,L}-I_{l}(A^{0,l})-I_{L-l}(A^{l,L})

\displaystyle=\big(A_{0,l}-I_{l}(A^{0,l})\big)+\big(A_{l,L}-I_{L-l}(A^{l,L})\big)+A_{0,L}-A_{0,l}-A_{l,L}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{l}(\xi^{0,l})+\lambda_{L-l}(\xi^{l,L})+\xi_{0,l,L}\;,

where we used the additivity property (
17
) of
I_{L}
in the second equality.

We now finally define
\Lambda
. Let
\Xi\in E[0,1]
and
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
.
Set
L=t-s
and define the shift and restriction
\theta_{s,t}\Xi\in E[0,t-s]
by

(\theta_{s,t}\Xi)_{a,b,c}=\Xi_{s+a,s+b,s+c}\;,\qquad 0\leq a\leq b\leq c\leq t-s\;.

We then define

(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,t}=\lambda_{t-s}(\theta_{s,t}\Xi)\;.

The map
\Xi\mapsto(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,t}
is linear for every
s,t
because
\lambda_{L}
is linear.
We now verify that
\Lambda
is a right-inverse of
\delta_{2}
.
Consider
0\leq s\leq u\leq t\leq 1
and set

L=t-s\;,\qquad l=u-s\;,\qquad\xi=\theta_{s,t}\Xi\;.

Then

\xi_{0,l,L}=\Xi_{s,u,t}\;,\qquad\xi^{0,l}=\theta_{s,u}\Xi\;,\qquad\xi^{l,L}=\theta_{u,t}\Xi\;.

By the sewing identity (
20
) for
\lambda_{L}
, one has
\lambda_{L}(\xi)=\lambda_{l}(\xi^{0,l})+\lambda_{L-l}(\xi^{l,L})+\xi_{0,l,L}
,
which in terms of
\Lambda
becomes

(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,t}=(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,u}+(\Lambda\Xi)_{u,t}+\Xi_{s,u,t}\;.

This proves the right-inverse property
\delta_{2}\Lambda\Xi=\Xi
.

Furthermore,
\Lambda
is strongly local by construction. Indeed, for
\Xi,\Xi^{\prime}
,
\tau\geq 0
, and
(s,t)\in\triangle
as in
(i)
, one has

\theta_{s,t}\Xi=\theta_{s+\tau,t+\tau}\Xi^{\prime}

as elements of
E[0,t-s]
, and therefore

(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,t}=\lambda_{t-s}(\theta_{s,t}\Xi)=\lambda_{t-s}(\theta_{s+\tau,t+\tau}\Xi^{\prime})=(\Lambda\Xi^{\prime})_{s+\tau,t+\tau}\;.

(ii)

Define the real vector space

G=\mathbb{R}\mathbf{e}\oplus\bigoplus_{[s,t]\subset[0,1]}F[s,t]\;,

where the direct sum is taken over all intervals
[s,t]\subset[0,1]
with
s\leq t
and
\mathbf{e}
is a formal symbol.
We treat
G
as the free vector space generated by
\mathbf{e}
and the formal symbols
[[s,t],\xi]
for
[s,t]\subset[0,1]
and
\xi\in F[s,t]
, subject to the linearity relations
[[s,t],a\xi+b\eta]=a[[s,t],\xi]+b[[s,t],\eta]
.

For
(s,t)\in\triangle
,
u\in[s,t]
, and
\xi\in F[s,t]
, denote

\xi^{s,u}=\xi|_{\triangle^{3}[s,u]}\;,\qquad\xi^{u,t}=\xi|_{\triangle^{3}[u,t]}\;,

and let
R\subset G
be the subspace generated by all vectors of the form

C_{[s,t]u,\xi}\coloneqq[[s,t],\xi]-[[s,u],\xi^{s,u}]-[[u,t],\xi^{u,t}]-\xi_{s,u,t}\mathbf{e}\;.

Denote the corresponding quotient map by

q\colon G\to G/R\;.

We claim first that
\mathbf{e}\notin R
and thus
q(\mathbf{e})\neq 0
.
Suppose, towards a contradiction, that
\mathbf{e}\in R
. Then

\mathbf{e}=\sum_{[s,t],u,\xi}a_{[s,t],u,\xi}C_{[s,t],u,\xi}\;,

(21)

where the sums are finite and
a_{[s,t],u,\xi}\in\mathbb{R}
.
We define a linear functional
S
on the finite-dimensional span of all vectors
[[a,b],\xi]
and
\mathbf{e}
appearing in the right-hand side of (
21
) after expanding the generators
C_{[s,t]u,\xi}
, and we will show that
S
annihilates the right-hand side of (
21
) but
S(\mathbf{e})=1
, which is a contradiction.

Let
\Pi=(0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1)
be a finite partition of
[0,1]
that contains all the time points
s,t,u
appearing in (
21
).
For
s<t
and
[[s,t],\xi]
appearing in the sum (
21
),
let
s=s_{0}<\cdots<s_{\ell}=t
denote the points in
\Pi
that are contained in
[s,t]
and define

S([[s,t],\xi])=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\xi_{s_{0},s_{i},s_{i+1}}\;,

with the convention that
S([[s,t],\xi])=0

if
s_{0}=s
and
s_{1}=t
.
For
[[s,s],\xi]
appearing in (
21
), define

S([[s,s],\xi])=-\xi_{s,s,s}\;.

(22)

If
u\in[s,t]
also appears in (
21
), then
u=s_{j}
for some
j=0,\ldots,\ell
,
and it readily follows from
\xi\in\delta_{2}\mathbb{R}^{\triangle[s,t]}
that

S([[s,t],\xi])=S([[s,u],\xi^{s,u}])+S([[u,t],\xi^{u,t}])+\xi_{s,u,t}\;.

(For
u\in\{s,t\}
, this follows from (
22
),
and for
u\in(s,t)
, this follows from writing
\xi_{a,b,c}=A_{a,c}-A_{a,b}-A_{b,c}
, from which we obtain

S([[s,t],\xi])=A_{s,t}-\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}A_{s_{i},s_{i+1}}
.)
Extending
S
to
\mathbf{e}
by
S(\mathbf{e})=1
and extending to spans of vectors by linearity, we obtain

S(C_{[s,t],u,\xi})=0
.
Hence
S
annihilates the right-hand side of (
21
), but
S(\mathbf{e})=1
, which is a contradiction.
Therefore
\mathbf{e}\notin R
as claimed.

Now define a linear functional on the one-dimensional subspace

\mathbb{R}q(\mathbf{e})\subset G/R

by

\ell(q(\mathbf{e}))=1
.
Using the axiom of choice, we extend
\ell
to a linear functional

\ell\colon G/R\to\mathbb{R}\;.

that extends
\ell\colon\mathbb{R}q(\mathbf{e})\to\mathbb{R}
.
For
\Xi\in F[0,1]
and
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
, we then define

(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,t}=\ell q([[s,t],\Xi|_{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}])\;.

Then
\Lambda
is linear in
\Xi
and local by construction.

We now check that
\Lambda
is a right inverse to
\delta_{2}
. Let

s\leq u\leq t
. Then since
C_{[s,t],u,\Xi|_{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}}\in R
, we have

q([[s,t],\Xi|_{\triangle^{3}[s,t]}])=q([[s,u],\Xi|_{\triangle^{3}[s,u]}])+q([[u,t],\Xi|_{\triangle^{3}[u,t]}])+\Xi_{s,u,t}q(\mathbf{e})\;.

Applying
\ell
, and using
\ell(q(\mathbf{e}))=1
, gives

(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,t}=(\Lambda\Xi)_{s,u}+(\Lambda\Xi)_{u,t}+\Xi_{s,u,t}
,
which is equivalent to
\delta_{2}\Lambda\Xi=\Xi
.

(iii)

Choose
a,b>0
such that

L\coloneqq a+b\leq 1

(23)

and such that
a
and
b
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
(e.g. one is rational, the other irrational).
Using the axiom of choice, extend
\{a,b\}
to a Hamel basis
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathbb{R}
over

\mathbb{Q}
.
Define an alternating
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear map

\omega\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

as follows.
Set

\omega(a,b)=1\;,\qquad\omega(b,a)=-1\;,

(24)

and, for every other ordered pair
(e,e^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
, set
\omega(e,e^{\prime})=0
.
We then extend
\omega

\mathbb{Q}
-bilinearly to

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}

3
Local square-integrable lifts of fractional Brownian motion

Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion (fBm) with Hurst parameter
H\leq 1/2
, that is the
\mathbb R^{d}
-valued Gaussian process with i.i.d. components, each with covariance function given by

R(s,t)\coloneqq\mathbb EX_{s}^{\alpha}X_{t}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\big(s^{2H}+t^{2H}-(t-s)^{2H}\big),\qquad 0\leq s\leq t\leq 1,\quad\alpha=1,\ldots,d.

We briefly recall the framework of isonormal Gaussian processes
[
43
,
41
]
applied to the specific case of
d
-dimensional fBm, with a focus on locality. We view
X_{t}(\omega)=\omega(t)
as the canonical process defined on the Banach space

\Omega\coloneqq\Omega_{0,1}\;,\quad\text{where }\;\Omega_{s,t}\coloneqq C_{0}([s,t],\mathbb R^{d})\coloneqq\{\omega\in C([s,t],\mathbb R^{d})\,:\,\omega_{s}=0\}

and we equip
\Omega
with its Borel sigma-algebra
\mathcal{F}
and a Gaussian measure
\mathbb{P}
under which
X
is a fBm.
For
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in[d]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,d\}
we let
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
denote the set of distinct indices so that
\mathbb R^{\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}}\cong\mathbb R^{k}
for some
k\leq n
. Let

\Omega_{s,t}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}=C_{0}([s,t],\mathbb R^{\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}}),

endowed with its Borel sigma-algebra
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}}_{s,t}
. We denote, using the notation (
5
),

r\colon\Omega\twoheadrightarrow\Omega_{s,t},\quad\omega\mapsto\omega_{s,[s,t]},

and note that this respects indices, i.e. induces maps
\Omega^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}\twoheadrightarrow\Omega_{s,t}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}
.
We equip each
\Omega_{s,t}
with the pushforward Gaussian measure
\mathbb{P}\circ r^{-1}
.

Since the material in this section will not only establish a non-existence result but also a classification result,
we need a notion of local rough path lift which allows for
d
components and degrees higher than level
2
—when
H\in(1/4,1/3]
we need level
3
—and which requires measurability. In particular, the notion of locality is not only w.r.t. time but also spatial indices.
We give the following definition in the context of fractional Brownian motion, but note that it makes sense in greater generality.

We refer to
[
22
]
for background on rough paths.
All rough paths we consider are weakly geometric. We denote by
\mathcal{G}^{n}(\mathbb R^{d})\subset T^{n}(\mathbb R^{d})=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}

the step-
n
free nilpotent Lie group
(i.e. the group of truncated grouplike elements) equipped with a homogeneous metric (e.g. the Carnot–Carathéodory metric). For an element
A\in T^{n}(\mathbb R^{d})
, we will use brackets in superscripts to denote its projections onto tensor powers of
\mathbb R^{d}
, i.e.
A=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A^{(k)}
with
A^{(k)}\in(\mathbb R^{d})^{\otimes k}
.
For a word
\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}
with
\alpha_{i}\in[d]
, we write
A^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}\coloneqq\langle\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n},A\rangle
for the dual pairing with the word.

Definition 3.1

(Local rough path lift of fBm)
.

Let
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
0<\eta<H
with
1/\eta<\lfloor 1/H\rfloor+1
.
A
local
\eta
-Hölder rough path lift
(
local
\eta
-RPL
for short) of
X
is a random variable
\boldsymbol{X}\colon\Omega\to C^{\eta\text{-}\mathrm{H\ddot{o}l}}([0,1],\mathcal{G}^{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(\mathbb R^{d}))
such that
\boldsymbol{X}^{(1)}=X
and
for any
(s,t)\in\triangle
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in[d]
with
n\leq\lfloor 1/H\rfloor
, denoting
\boldsymbol{X}_{s,t}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{X}^{-1}_{s}\boldsymbol{X}_{t}
, it holds that
\boldsymbol{X}_{s,t}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}
is
r^{-1}(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}_{s,t})
-measurable.
We furthermore say that
\boldsymbol{X}
is square-integrable if
\boldsymbol{X}_{s,t}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}\in L^{2}\Omega
for all
(s,t)\in\triangle
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in[d]
.

A local
\eta
-RPL of
X
can be regarded as a random variable defined on the probability space
\Omega_{s,t}
, endowed with the pushforward measure through
r
, which respects indexing. Square-integrability of
\boldsymbol{X}
enables the use of Wiener chaos decompositions, which we proceed to introduce. For
(s,t)\in\triangle
let
\mathcal{H}^{(d)}_{s,t}
be the Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(d)}_{s,t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{H}^{1}_{s,t}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{H}^{d}_{s,t},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{E}}{}^{\alpha}_{s,t},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{s,t}^{\alpha}=\mathrm{span}\{\mathbbm 1^{\alpha}_{[s,u]}\mid u\in[s,t]\},

\displaystyle\langle\mathbbm 1^{\alpha}_{[s,u]},\mathbbm 1^{\alpha}_{[s,v]}\rangle

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbb EX^{\alpha}_{s,u}X^{\alpha}_{s,v}=R(s,s)+R(u,v)-R(s,v)-R(u,s)\;.

where the closure is taken w.r.t. the inner product and the direct sum is orthogonal (equivalent to the components of the Gaussian process being independent). Since the components are all identically distributed,
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{s,t}\cong\ldots\cong\mathcal{H}^{d}_{s,t}
canonically; when we wish to consider a single direct summand we will simply refer to
\mathcal{H}_{s,t}

\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{0,1}
(and similar).

Recall that multiple Wiener integration

41

yields isometries

\sko^{\bullet}_{s,t}\colon\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}^{(d)}_{s,t})^{\odot m}\xrightarrow{\cong}L^{2}\Omega_{s,t},\qquad w^{m}_{s,t}\colon L^{2}\Omega_{s,t}\twoheadrightarrow W^{m}_{s,t}\coloneqq\sko^{m}_{s,t}((\mathcal{H}^{(d)}_{s,t})^{\odot m}).

(27)

We have used
\odot
to denote the symmetric tensor product, i.e. for a Hilbert space

is the closure of the linear span of the symmetric elementary tensors

v_{1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}.

The subspace

is called the

Wiener chaos over
[s,t]
, and
w^{m}_{s,t}
is the orthogonal projection onto it. Since all these operations commute with the natural inclusions, i.e.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{H}^{(d)}_{s,t})^{\odot m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{m}_{s,t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{H}^{(d)})^{\odot m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sko_{s,t}^{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sko^{m}}

(as can be observed on the elementary tensors

, which map to Hermite polynomials and have dense span in
(\mathcal{H}^{(d)}_{s,t})^{\odot n}
), we will frequently omit the subscripts

. We also combine this with the notation for subsets of coordinates and the above considerations naturally restrict, i.e.

\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}_{s,t}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\beta\in\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}}\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{s,t},\qquad L^{2}\Omega^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}_{s,t}\cong\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}_{s,t})^{\odot m}.

It was shown in

31

that for

can be identified with the space of functions (modulo a.e. identity),

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{s,t}&=\{f\colon[s,t]\to\mathbb R\mid f^{*}\in H^{1/2-H}(\mathbb R)\},\qquad\text{with inner product}\\
\langle f,g\rangle&=\frac{\Gamma(2H+1)\sin(H\pi)}{2\pi}\upintop\nolimits_{\mathbb R}\widehat{f^{*}}(x)\overline{\widehat{g^{*}}(x)}|x|^{1-2H}\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(28)

where

is the zero-extension of a function

,

denotes Fourier transform, and

denotes the fractional Sobolev space.
To derive a time-domain representation of the norm, we introduce the Gagliardo seminorm for

Then one has

\begin{split}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{s,t}}&\asymp_{H}[f^{*}]_{H^{1/2-H}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=[f]_{H^{1/2-H}([s,t])}^{2}+2\upintop\nolimits_{s}^{t}|f(v)|^{2}\upintop\nolimits_{\mathbb R\setminus[s,t]}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}u}{|v-u|^{2-2H}}\,\mathrm{d}v\\
&\asymp_{H}[f]_{H^{1/2-H}([s,t])}^{2}+\upintop\nolimits_{s}^{t}|f(v)|^{2}((v-s)^{2H-1}+(t-v)^{2H-1})\,\mathrm{d}v\;,\end{split}

(29)

where in the first bound we used
[

Lemma 3.2
.

\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{s,t}}\asymp_{H}[f]^{2}_{H^{1/2-H}([s,t])}+|t-s|^{2H-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}([s,t])}^{2}

Proof.

This is a standard fact, but we give a proof for convenience.
The case
H=1/2
is obvious, so take
H<1/2

and denote

The lower bound

For the upper bound, by scaling and (

Lemma 3.5

Example 3.10
.

Let
X
be Brownian motion (
H=1/2
). It may seem like setting, e.g.

\boldsymbol{X}^{\alpha\beta}_{s,t}\coloneqq\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}{}^{\alpha\beta}_{s,t}+\upintop\nolimits_{s}^{t}X^{\alpha}_{u}X^{\beta}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u

defines a local RPL different to the canonical (Stratonovich) one
\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}
. This is not true however, since
\upintop\nolimits_{s}^{t}X^{\alpha}_{u}X^{\beta}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u
is not measurable w.r.t. the sigma algebra
\sigma(X^{\alpha}_{s,u},X^{\beta}_{s,u}:u\in[s,t])
, only w.r.t.
\sigma(X^{\alpha}_{u},X^{\beta}_{u}:u\in[s,t])
(the value of this stochastic integral depends on the initial value
X_{s}
). Replacing it with
\upintop\nolimits_{s}^{t}X^{\alpha}_{s,u}X^{\beta}_{s,u}\,\mathrm{d}u
would solve this problem but makes the Chen property fail instead.
Section 3
shows that there can be no local increments of
X
that have the desired regularity.

Finally, we further constrain the admissible rough path lifts by requiring stationarity or scale-invariance. These are properties of the law of
\boldsymbol{X}
that are respectively the probabilistic analogues of time translation-invariance and parametrisation-invariance introduced in
Section 2
. We also consider equality in law under coordinate permutation, to be understood as a spatial isotropy condition. We do not claim that all interesting examples must satisfy these conditions, in particular the third is violated in the case of
3
-dimensional Brownian motion in a magnetic field
[
19
]
. For
\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}
however, they all hold. We will use
\sim
to denote equality in law. As usual, statements regarding third levels should only be considered to apply if
H\leq\frac{1}{3}
.

\varphi_{s,t}=\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{s}
, we have reduced the problem to classifying all additive continuous functions
\varphi\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal{G}^{\lfloor 1/H\rfloor}(\mathbb R^{d})\subset T^{3}(\mathbb R^{d})
. A familiar argument shows that for
\varphi_{1}=q\varphi_{1/q}
and therefore
\varphi_{p/q}=\frac{p}{q}\varphi_{1}
for all rationals
p/q
, and we obtain that
\varphi
is of the claimed form by continuity. Conversely, such choices of
\varphi
are sufficiently regular and

\bigg(\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}^{(2)}_{s,t},\varphi^{(2)}_{s,t},\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}^{(3)}_{s,t},\upintop\nolimits_{s}^{t}\varphi^{(2)}_{s,u}\,\mathrm{d}X_{u},\varphi^{(3)}_{s,t}\bigg)\sim\bigg(\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}^{(2)}_{0,t-s},\varphi^{(2)}_{0,t-s},\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}^{(3)}_{0,t-s},\upintop\nolimits_{0}^{t-s}\varphi^{(2)}_{0,u}\,\mathrm{d}X_{u},\varphi^{(3)}_{0,t-s}\bigg),

(44)

by stationarity of increments of fBm and the fact that all components of this random vector are obtained by limiting procedures that are time-translation invariant (e.g.
\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}
is the limit in
L^{2}
of Stieltjes integrals of piecewise linear interpolations). This implies the statement.

For
(b)
, if
\boldsymbol{X}
is scale-invariant, then by again taking expectations, we obtain

\varphi^{(2)}_{s,t}=(t-s)^{2H}\varphi^{(2)}_{0,1}
, which together with additivity forces either
2H=1
or
\varphi^{(2)}_{0,1}=0
. The third level is handled similarly.
Conversely, the fact that these choices indeed preserve scaling is argued analogously to (
44
).

It remains to prove
(c)
.
Let
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb R^{d})
denote the free Lie algebra over
d
generators and

\mathrm{Inv}_{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}(\mathbb R^{d})^{\otimes n}=\mathrm{span}\Big\{\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}}e_{\sigma(\alpha_{1})}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{\sigma(\alpha_{n})}\ \Big|\ \alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in[d]\Big\}

the space of tensors that are left invariant by the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
(cf.
[
14
, §5]
).
We first claim that

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(\mathbb R^{d})\cap\mathrm{Inv}_{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}(\mathbb R^{d})^{\otimes 2}&=\{0\}\\
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb R^{d})\cap\mathrm{Inv}_{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}(\mathbb R^{d})^{\otimes 3}&=\mathrm{span}\Big\{\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}[e_{\alpha},[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]]\Big\}.\end{split}

(45)

Indeed, the map
\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\sigma_{*}
averages algebra morphisms and thus preserves
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb R^{d})
. Therefore, for any
n

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbb R^{d})\cap\mathrm{Inv}_{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}(\mathbb R^{d})^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\sigma_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{(n)}(\mathbb R^{d}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}\Big\{\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\sigma_{*}[e_{\alpha_{1}},[e_{\alpha_{2}},\ldots[e_{\alpha_{n-1}},e_{\alpha_{n}}]\ldots]\ \Big|\ \alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in[d]\Big\}.

For level
2
we conclude immediately. At level
3
, we consider the
5
orbits:

(1)\ \alpha=\beta=\gamma,\quad(2)\ \alpha\neq\beta=\gamma,\quad(3)\ \alpha=\beta\neq\gamma,\quad(4)\ \alpha=\gamma\neq\beta,\quad(5)\ \alpha\neq\beta\neq\gamma\neq\alpha.

Lie brackets
[e_{\alpha},[e_{\beta},e_{\gamma}]]
corresponding to
(1)
and
(2)
vanish by skew-symmetry, the ones corresponding to
(5)
vanish by Jacobi, and the cases
(3)
and
(4)
are linearly dependent by skew-symmetry.
This proves (
45
).

Next, remark that, if
g\in\mathcal{G}^{N}(\mathbb R^{d})
for some
N
and is zero at degrees
k=1,\ldots,n-1
, then its projection onto
(\mathbb R^{d})^{\otimes n}
must lie in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb R^{d})
: this follows by writing
g=\exp\log g
and projecting onto the first non-zero degree.

Returning to the proof of
(c)
, suppose
\boldsymbol{X}
is coordinate permutation-invariant. Then
\varphi^{(2)}
must also be coordinate invariant and thus belongs to
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb R^{d})\cap\mathrm{Inv}_{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}(\mathbb R^{d})^{\otimes 2}
by the above remark. Therefore
\varphi^{(2)}=0
by (
45
).
Likewise
\varphi^{(3)}
belongs to
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb R^{d})\cap\mathrm{Inv}_{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}(\mathbb R^{d})^{\otimes 3}
,
from which the claimed form of
\varphi
follows again by (
45
) and the fact that

[e_{\alpha},[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]]=e_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}-2e_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\otimes e_{\alpha}+e_{\beta}\otimes e_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\alpha}\;.

Conversely, any such
\varphi
clearly leads to a coordinate permutation-invariant
\boldsymbol{X}
.
∎

A conjecture: dropping the
L^{2}
assumption.

We conjecture that the material in this section holds without the hypothesis of square integrability of the local RPL. We briefly elaborate on why we do not see a way of pushing our arguments further to cover this case, focusing on the non-existence claim for
H\leq\frac{1}{4}
.

Since non-existence already occurs at level
2
, we revert to the notation of
Section 2
. A slightly stronger but still very natural conjecture predicts that if
\mathbb{A}
is a local RPL defined on a Borel set
B\subset\Omega
, it must be that
B
is
\mathbb P
-null. It is sufficient to assume
\mathbb{A}
to be square integrable since
B
can always be replaced with

B^{M}\coloneqq\{\omega\in B\mid\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq 1}|\mathbb{A}_{s,t}(X(\omega),Y(\omega))|<M\}

with
M
arbitrary. Then for a sequence of partitions
\pi_{n}
of
[0,1]
we can write the Chen identity

\mathbb{A}_{0,1}\mathbbm 1_{B}=\Big(\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[u,v],[w,z]\in\pi_{n}\\
u<w\end{subarray}}X_{u,v}Y_{w,z}+\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi_{n}}\mathbb{A}_{s,t}\mathbbm 1_{B_{s,t}}}_{\displaystyle\eqqcolon\mathbb{A}_{\pi_{n}}}\Big)\mathbbm 1_{B}.

(46)

Both
\mathbb{A}_{0,1}\mathbbm 1_{B}
and
\mathbb{A}_{\pi_{n}}
can be assumed in
L^{2}
and the variance of the latter diverges as seen by projecting onto the second chaos and applying
Section 3
, but this no longer leads to a contradiction since this divergence could conceivably be due to its behaviour on
\Omega\setminus B
.

It would be possible to conclude after showing
\mathbb{A}_{\pi_{n}}
diverges in probability, in the sense that for any
r

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb P[|\mathbb{A}_{\pi_{n}}|\leq r]=0.

This is indeed the case for any finite chaos projection, as can be inferred from the divergence in
L^{2}
and the Carbery-Wright inequality
[
6
]
(see e.g. the more explicit formulation in
[
40
, (2.2)]
), with explicit rate depending on the rate of blowup in
L^{2}
and chaos degree, but which of course degenerates in the infinite chaos limit. Wiener chaos is dense in
L^{2}
and thus in probability, but for this to be effective the finite chaos approximation of
\mathbb{A}_{\pi_{n}}
in probability would have to be performed uniformly in
n
; conversely, first approximating
\mathbb{A}_{0,1}
with a finite chaos element relinquishes access to the Chen identity.

For these and other reasons, we believe new ideas are needed to extend our results to the non-
L^{2}
case.
