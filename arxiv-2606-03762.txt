Abstract
Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) equips large language models (LLMs) with tool-use capabilities that substantially improve reasoning on complex tasks. However, integrating external tools often destabilizes training: over-reliance on tools can induce input distribution shift, while overly conservative tool use limits effective exploration. To address this issue, we propose a unified framework TAO-RL that couples tool-aware trajectory filtering with entropy-guided exploration for efficient policy optimization. Specifically, at the data level, TAO-RL filters rollout trajectories along two criteria: discarding those where all tool invocations fail to execute, and removing those where all rollouts are either correct or incorrect, as both cases yield degenerate advantage estimates that contribute no discriminative learning signal. This joint filtering retains data that are both tool-capable and informative, establishing a high-quality training distribution. At the algorithmic level, we introduce a tool-aware entropy-guided bonus that reshapes the advantage function at post-tool-call tokens, encouraging the policy to explore more diverse reasoning paths at critical decision points. These two components are mutually reinforcing: trajectory filtering establishes a clean and informative training foundation, while entropy-guided exploration drives stronger reasoning behaviors at critical tool-interaction junctures. Extensive experiments on 7 7 challenging reasoning benchmarks across 3 3 model scales demonstrate the superiority of TAO-RL over existing methods.

At the data level

filters rollout trajectories according to two complementary criteria. At the algorithmic level

Rather than applying exploration incentives uniformly across all tokens, we identify tokens that follow tool executions and exhibit high entropy as critical decision points. By applying targeted entropy bonuses at these positions,
TAO-RL
encourages the policy to explore more diverse reasoning paths precisely where exploration is critical, progressively raising the reasoning ceiling without destabilizing the training foundation established by trajectory filtering.
The two components are mutually reinforcing: trajectory filtering establishes a clean and informative training foundation by removing failed tool interactions and uninformative trajectories, while entropy-guided exploration drives stronger agentic reasoning behaviors at the critical tokens. GRPO
[
27
]

TAO-RL
is built upon GRPO as its basic optimization algorithm. GRPO stabilizes policy updates by normalizing advantage estimates over groups of sampled responses, eliminating the need for a separate value network. For each query
q
, the rollout policy
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
generates a group of responses
\{o^{i}\}^{G}_{i=1}
with corresponding rewards
\{R^{i}\}^{G}_{i=1}
, and the advantage is estimated as:

\hat{A}^{i}_{t}=\frac{r^{i}-\mathrm{mean}(\{R^{i}\}^{G}_{i=1})}{\mathrm{std}(\{R^{i}\}^{G}_{i=1})}.

(1)

The learning objective of GRPO is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(q,a)\sim\mathcal{D},\,\{o^{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot|q)}

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{|o^{i}|}\sum_{t=1}^{|o^{i}|}\Big(\min\!\big(r^{i}_{t}(\theta)\hat{A}^{i}_{t},\,\mathrm{clip}(r^{i}_{t}(\theta),

\displaystyle 1-\epsilon,\,1+\epsilon)\hat{A}^{i}_{t}\big)-\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})\Big)\Bigg],

where
\epsilon
is the clipping hyperparameter and
\beta
controls the strength of the KL penalty.

Agentic RL

We formalize the agentic RL setting. Unlike standard LLM policy optimization over pure text generation, agentic RL interleaves language generation with tool interaction. In this work, we focus on empowering the model to invoke the
code interpreter
during the reasoning process to execute programs and obtain runtime feedback. Let
x\sim\mathcal{D}
denote an input query and
\pi_{\theta}
as the policy model. In this setting, the overall response
o
consists of two coupled components: an agentic reasoning trajectory
\mathcal{R}=\{s_{t}\}_{t=1}^{|\mathcal{R}|}
, which includes generated reasoning steps, code generation blocks, and returned execution results, and a final answer
y=\{y_{t}\}_{t=1}^{|y|}
conditioned on the trajectory. The joint generation process of
o=(\mathcal{R},y)
is defined as:

P_{\theta}(\mathcal{R},y\mid x)=\underbrace{\prod_{t=1}^{t_{\mathcal{R}}}P_{\theta}\!\left(\mathcal{R}_{t}\mid\mathcal{R}_{<t},x\right)}_{\text{Agentic Reasoning}}\cdot\underbrace{\prod_{t=1}^{t_{y}}P_{\theta}\!\left(y_{t}\mid y_{<t},\mathcal{R},x\right)}_{\text{Answer Generation}},

where
\mathcal{R}
is the reasoning trajectory of length
t_{\mathcal{R}}
, and
y
is the final answer with length
t_{y}
.
Following GRPO-based optimization, the policy is trained to maximize the expected reward of the overall trajectory and final answer:

\max_{\theta}\;\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\,(\mathcal{R},y)\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)}\left[r(x,\mathcal{R},y)\right].

IV
Approach

As illustrated in Fig.
2
,
TAO-RL
consists of two core components: tool-aware trajectory filtering and an entropy-guided exploration mechanism. First, we generate model rollouts across the training dataset and apply two complementary filtering criteria to curate a high-quality training distribution. Following this, we execute token-level optimization by identifying high-entropy tokens following tool executions and applying an entropy-guided bonus to encourage exploration at these critical decision points.

IV-A

Tool-Aware Trajectory Filtering

Not all rollout trajectories provide valid or informative learning signals for training .
Trajectories where tool interactions consistently fail inject only noise into training, while those where the model either answers correctly or incorrectly on every rollout yield degenerate advantage estimates that contribute no discriminative gradient information.
TAO-RL
filters rollout trajectories along two complementary criteria, retaining only those that are both tool-capable and informative contrastive signals.

For each query
x
, we sample
G
rollout trajectories
\{o^{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot\mid x)
with corresponding binary rewards
\{r^{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}\in\{0,1\}^{G}
. We then apply the following two criteria.

Criterion 1: Tool Execution Validity.
In agentic RL, the value of a trajectory depends not only on whether the final answer is correct, but also on whether the intermediate tool interactions provide meaningful environmental feedback. A trajectory that produces an incorrect answer while all of its tool invocations fail with execution errors is doubly uninformative: it neither guides the policy toward correct reasoning nor provides valid grounding from external tool outputs. The entire reasoning chain in such a trajectory is constructed without reliable environmental signal, making it indistinguishable from pure hallucination and harmful to stable policy learning. Including such trajectories in training risks reinforcing erroneous reasoning patterns that happen to invoke tools in syntactically valid but semantically meaningless ways. We therefore discard trajectory
o^{i}
if:

r^{i}=0\;\wedge\;\mathcal{S}_{\text{tool}}(o^{i})=0,

where
\mathcal{S}_{\text{tool}}(o^{i})
denotes the number of successful tool invocations in trajectory
o^{i}
. This criterion specifically targets the intersection of answer incorrectness and tool failure, preserving incorrect trajectories that nonetheless contain at least one successful tool execution, as these still carry informative signals about how tool feedback shapes reasoning.

Criterion 2: Answer Discriminability.
Beyond individual trajectory quality, the collective behavior of rollouts for a given query determines whether meaningful advantage estimates can be derived. We define the empirical pass rate as:

\rho(q)=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}r^{i},

(2)

and analyze the two degenerate extremes. When
\rho(q)=1
, all rollouts produce correct answers, indicating that the current policy has already mastered the task. In this case, all trajectories receive identical rewards, the group-normalized advantage estimates collapse to zero, and no gradient signal is propagated that training on such queries is entirely wasteful. When
\rho(q)=0
, all rollouts produce incorrect answers, meaning the policy receives uniformly zero reward across the group. Without any positive reward signal to contrast against, the policy has no direction from which to improve, and the resulting advantage estimates again carry no useful information for policy improvement. Both extremes thus represent a failure of the contrastive learning mechanism that underpins GRPO-style optimization. We retain only queries satisfying:

0<\rho(q)<1,

ensuring that each retained query contains at least one correct and one incorrect rollout, from which informative contrastive advantage estimates can be reliably derived.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}
denote the set of trajectories surviving both criteria. Unlike SimpleTIR
[
17
]
, which filters solely on format validity,
TAO-RL
additionally removes tool-execution-failed incorrect trajectories and discriminability-degenerate queries, yielding a training distribution that is both tool-capable and contrastively informative for stable policy optimization.

Empirical Analysis.
To investigate the impact of different data processing strategies on training performance, we compare
TAO-RL
against two baselines. The first is SimpleTIR, which only filters trajectories based on format validity. The second is a curriculum-based variant, where instead of discarding degenerate queries via pass rate filtering, we organize all training data in a progressive easy-to-hard order based on task difficulty, allowing us to directly assess whether difficulty-aware data organization is a viable alternative to our answer discriminability filtering. As shown in Fig.
3
,
TAO-RL
achieves the best average accuracy on AIME25, outperforming both baselines. In terms of training stability,
TAO-RL
exhibits the smallest gradient norm fluctuations during training, while both baselines suffer from larger oscillations. These results demonstrate that neither format-based filtering alone nor progressive difficulty ordering can adequately handle the degenerate training signals introduced by tool interaction, validating the effectiveness of our joint tool execution validity and answer discriminability filtering strategy. We conduct further experimental analysis of data organization in section
V-C
, and Appendix D.

IV-B

Entropy-Guided Exploration

While tool-aware trajectory filtering establishes a clean and informative training foundation, it does not explicitly encourage exploration within the retained trajectories. Existing methods address exploration through uniform entropy regularization across all token positions, which is ill-suited to agentic settings: it introduces unnecessary noise at inconsequential positions while failing to concentrate sufficient incentives at the critical junctures where tool execution results are interpreted. Our key insight is that
exploration should be concentrated at tokens following successful tool executions, where predictive uncertainty is highest and the downstream consequence of the policy’s choice is critical.
To this end, we introduce an entropy-guided bonus that reshapes the advantage function at these critical positions.

Post-Tool-Call Token Identification

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\text{post}}
denote the set of all token positions that follow tool execution within the filtered trajectories
\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}
across current rollout group. For each token
o_{t}\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{post}}
, we compute its predictive entropy:

\mathcal{H}_{t}=-\sum_{v}\pi_{\theta}(v\mid x,o_{<t})\cdot\log\pi_{\theta}(v\mid x,o_{<t}).

We retain the top-
q_{\mathcal{H}}
proportion of post-tool-call tokens ranked by predictive entropy for encouraging exploration, and define a binary gate accordingly:

g_{t}=\mathbf{1}\left[t\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{post}}\;\wedge\;\mathcal{H}_{t}\geq\tau_{\mathcal{H}}\right],

where
\tau_{\mathcal{H}}
is the entropy value at the
(1-q_{\mathcal{H}})
-th percentile of
\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{post}}}
. This selective gating concentrates the entropy bonus exclusively on the most uncertain post-tool-call positions, avoiding unnecessary perturbation at positions where the policy is already sufficiently confident.

Entropy-Aware Advantage Reshaping

For the gated tokens, we introduce a normalized entropy-based advantage term to quantify how much each token’s uncertainty deviates from the trajectory mean:

\tilde{A}_{t}^{\Delta\mathcal{H}}=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{t}-\mathrm{mean}\!\left(\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}\right)}{\mathrm{std}\!\left(\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}\right)},

(3)

where
T
is the total number of tokens across all trajectories in the group. This term is estimated per token based on the average token entropy within the same micro-batch. We treat it as a regularization bonus and incorporate it into the advantage defined in Eq.
1
, yielding the reshaped advantage:

\tilde{A}_{t}=\hat{A}_{t}\cdot\left(1+a\cdot g_{t}\cdot\tilde{A}_{t}^{\Delta\mathcal{H}}\right),

(4)

where
a
is a scaling coefficient controlling the strength of the entropy bonus. The gate
g_{t}
ensures the reshaping is applied exclusively to post-tool-call tokens, preserving the original advantage estimates at all other positions. The reshaped
\tilde{A}_{t}
is computed before the policy update and substituted into the standard clipped surrogate objective of GRPO.
Integrating the two components of
TAO-RL
, the final training objective is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{(q,a)\sim\mathcal{D},\,\{o^{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot|q)}

(5)

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{1}{|\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}}\frac{1}{|o^{i}|}\sum_{t=1}^{|o^{i}|}\Big(\min\!\big(r^{i}_{t}(\theta)\tilde{A}^{i}_{t},\,\mathrm{clip}(r^{i}_{t}(\theta),

\displaystyle 1-\epsilon,\,1+\epsilon)\tilde{A}^{i}_{t}\big)-\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})\Big)\Bigg],

where
\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}\subseteq\{o^{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}
denotes the filtered trajectories passing both criteria. Trajectories outside
\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}
are excluded from the policy update, ensuring that gradient updates are computed exclusively over tool-capable rollouts with discriminative learning signals. For tokens outside
\mathcal{M}_{\text{post}}
,
\tilde{A}^{i}_{t}
reduces to the standard advantage
\hat{A}^{i}_{t}
, so
TAO-RL
introduces no additional computational overhead beyond the entropy computation at post-tool-call positions.

The two components are mutually dependent for achieving stable exploration. Without trajectory filtering, the entropy bonus would amplify gradients indiscriminately over noisy degenerate rollouts, causing unstable policy updates. Without the entropy bonus, the filtered gradients would treat all token positions equally, failing to concentrate exploration at the most consequential decision points.
TAO-RL
achieves targeted exploration without sacrificing training stability.

Theoretical Analysis.

We formally analyze how the two components of
TAO-RL
jointly improve the optimization landscape via gradient signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and targeted step-size modulation. The policy gradient over the filtered trajectory set
\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}
with reshaped advantage:

\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{o^{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{|o^{i}|}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(o^{i}_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\tilde{A}_{t}\right].

SNR Maximization via Trajectory Filtering:
Standard GRPO computes gradients over all sampled trajectories. Degenerate queries with
\rho(q)\in\{0,1\}
yield zero advantage estimates (
\hat{A}_{t}=0
), which waste compute and dilute the expected gradient signal. Furthermore, trajectories composed entirely of failed tool calls lack valid environmental grounding, meaning their token updates are driven by pseudo-random advantage fluctuations, injecting zero-mean, high-variance noise into the batch. By restricting the expectation to
\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}
,
TAO-RL
removes both signal diluters and noise injectors, fundamentally improving the SNR of the policy gradient:

\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}]\|^{2}}{\mathrm{Var}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}]}>\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}]\|^{2}}{\mathrm{Var}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}]}.

Targeted Exploration via Entropy Bonus:
within the stabilized gradient, the reshaped advantage further modulates the logit update
[
36
,
32
]
at post-tool-call positions:

\displaystyle\theta^{k+1}_{s_{t},o_{t}}-\theta^{k}_{s_{t},o_{t}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\eta\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\hat{A}_{t}}_{\text{basic update}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\eta\cdot a\cdot g_{t}\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\hat{A}_{t}\cdot\tilde{A}_{t}^{\Delta\mathcal{H}}}_{\text{exploration bonus}},

where
\eta
is the learning rate. The gate
g_{t}
ensures the exploration bonus is applied exclusively to the top-
q_{\mathcal{H}}
highest-entropy post-tool-call tokens. For these gated tokens, the bonus amplifies the logit update in proportion to their entropy deviation, concentrating targeted exploration precisely at the most uncertain positions where the downstream consequence of each action is greatest. For all other positions,
g_{t}=0
and the update reduces to the standard basic update, preserving stable optimization behavior throughout the remainder of the trajectory. We provide detailed theoretical derivation in Appendix B.

Our experiments are designed to answer the following questions: (i) How does

compare against existing agentic RL approaches across diverse reasoning tasks? (ii) Can tool-aware trajectory filtering and entropy-guided exploration jointly improve LLM reasoning performance? (iii) How do individual components and hyperparameters contribute to the overall effectiveness of
TAO-RL
? (iv) How well does
TAO-RL
generalize to out-of-distribution tasks? (v) What insights can be drawn from case studies of specific reasoning trajectories?

K

3
Comparison of AEPO

We further compare
TAO-RL
against AEPO, an exploration-based agentic RL method, as shown in Fig.
7
. Despite its exploration mechanism, AEPO exhibits progressive performance degradation and pronounced gradient norm fluctuations in later training stages, revealing two fundamental limitations: exploration incentives are amplified over unfiltered noisy rollouts, and entropy bonuses are applied uniformly across all token positions without distinguishing the structurally critical post-tool-call tokens from inconsequential ones. In contrast,
TAO-RL
addresses both gaps simultaneously, achieving stable and targeted exploration throughout training.

Notably, as shown in Table
VII
, AEPO invokes tools substantially more frequently than
TAO-RL
across five benchmarks. Rather than reflecting effective tool use, this excessive tool invocation is symptomatic of uncontrolled exploration: without filtering out degenerate trajectories, the policy is encouraged to invoke tools indiscriminately, resulting in redundant and uninformative tool interactions that ultimately destabilize training and degrade performance. This finding reveals a critical distinction between
quantity
and
quality
of tool use that
more frequent tool invocations do not translate into better reasoning if the underlying trajectories lack valid environmental grounding.
In contrast,
TAO-RL
achieves higher accuracy with fewer but more purposeful tool calls, demonstrating that tool-aware trajectory filtering is indispensable for converting exploration incentives into genuine reasoning gains.

VI
Conclusion and Limitation

In this work, we present
TAO-RL
, a unified framework for efficient agentic reinforcement learning that addresses the fundamental tension between training stability and effective exploration introduced by tool integration.
TAO-RL
operates jointly at two levels: at the data level, tool-aware trajectory filtering removes degenerate rollouts by jointly considering tool execution validity and answer discriminability; at the algorithmic level, entropy-guided advantage reshaping concentrates exploration incentives at high entropy post-tool-call tokens. Extensive experiments across challenging reasoning benchmarks and three model scales consistently demonstrate that
TAO-RL
outperforms existing agentic RL methods in both reasoning performance and training stability, while also cultivating deeper and more structured tool-use behaviors as evidenced by substantial gains in response length, code lines, and tool invocation quality.

Limitation and Future Work:

TAO-RL
has several limitations worth noting. The hyperparameters governing the entropy bonus and the gating proportion require tuning. Also,
TAO-RL
considers only code interpreter as the external tool, which represents a relatively constrained form of tool interaction. Real-world agentic scenarios involve more diverse and compositional tool ecosystems, such as search engines, databases, and multi-step calls. The scalability of
TAO-RL
to broader agentic settings involving diverse tool types, multi-turn interactions, or open-ended tasks remains to be explored in future work.

-A

Broader Impact

TAO-RL
addresses a fundamental challenge in agentic reinforcement learning: achieving stable and effective policy optimization in the presence of tool interaction. By coupling tool-aware trajectory filtering with entropy-guided exploration,
TAO-RL
provides a principled framework for improving reasoning capabilities while maintaining training stability. This has promising implications for developing more capable and reliable agentic systems across diverse domains, from mathematical problem-solving to code generation and complex multi-step decision-making tasks.

The unified framework that jointly addresses data quality and algorithmic exploration offers a generalizable paradigm extending beyond the specific setting of tool-integrated reasoning. The principle of filtering degenerate training signals before applying targeted exploration incentives is broadly applicable to any RL setting where noisy or uninformative rollouts risk destabilizing policy optimization. Similarly, the idea of concentrating exploration at structurally critical positions, rather than applying uniform incentives across all tokens, provides a general design principle for more efficient policy gradient methods in long-horizon sequential decision-making.

From a societal perspective, more stable and efficient agentic RL training reduces the computational resources required to develop capable reasoning models, lowering the barrier to research and deployment of LLM-based agents. More reliable tool-use behavior also improves the trustworthiness of agentic systems in real-world applications, where erratic tool invocations can have unintended downstream consequences. We hope the insights from
TAO-RL
inspire further research into structured, tool-aware training paradigms for the broader agentic AI community.

-B

Theoretical Analysis

We first provide a formal proof that restricting the policy gradient expectation to
\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}
strictly improves the gradient signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) over standard GRPO.

Setup.
Let
\mathcal{O}=\{o^{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}
denote the full set of rollout trajectories, partitioned as
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}\cup\mathcal{O}_{\text{deg}}
, where
|\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}|=n
and
|\mathcal{O}_{\text{deg}}|=G-n
. Define the per-trajectory gradient as:

\mathbf{g}_{i}=\sum_{t=1}^{|o^{i}|}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(o^{i}_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\hat{A}^{i}_{t}.

The GRPO and
TAO-RL
gradient estimators are then:

\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\mathbf{g}_{i},\qquad\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}}\mathbf{g}_{i}.

We establish two key properties of
\mathbf{g}_{i}
for
o^{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{\text{deg}}
:

Property 1 (Zero mean signal).
For queries with
\rho(q)\in\{0,1\}
, all rollouts receive identical rewards, so the group-normalized advantage satisfies
\hat{A}^{i}_{t}=0
for all
t
, giving
\mathbf{g}_{i}=\mathbf{0}
. For trajectories with all tool calls failed, the policy receives no valid environmental grounding, so the expected gradient contribution is approximately zero:
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}_{i}]\approx\mathbf{0}
.

Property 2 (Non-zero variance).
Despite carrying zero expected signal, degenerate trajectories exhibit non-zero gradient variance
\sigma^{2}_{\text{deg}}=\mathrm{Var}[\mathbf{g}_{i}]>0
due to stochastic sampling and pseudo-random advantage fluctuations induced by failed tool execution.

Expected Gradient.
Using Property 1:

\mathbb{E}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}]=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}_{i}]=\frac{n}{G}\cdot\mathbb{E}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}].

Hence,
\|\mathbb{E}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}]\|^{2}=\frac{n^{2}}{G^{2}}\|\mathbb{E}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}]\|^{2}
.

Gradient Variance.
Let
\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}_{\text{valid}}}\mathrm{Var}[\mathbf{g}_{i}]
. Assuming independence across trajectories:

\mathrm{Var}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}]=\frac{n\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}+(G-n)\sigma^{2}_{\text{deg}}}{G^{2}},\qquad\mathrm{Var}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}]=\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}}{n}.

SNR Comparison.
Denoting
\mu^{2}=\|\mathbb{E}[\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}]\|^{2}
:

\text{SNR}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}=\frac{\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}/n}=\frac{n\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}},

\text{SNR}_{\text{GRPO}}=\frac{(n^{2}/G^{2})\mu^{2}}{(n\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}+(G-n)\sigma^{2}_{\text{deg}})/G^{2}}=\frac{n^{2}\mu^{2}}{n\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}+(G-n)\sigma^{2}_{\text{deg}}}.

Taking the ratio:

\frac{\text{SNR}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}}{\text{SNR}_{\text{GRPO}}}=\frac{n\mu^{2}/\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}}{n^{2}\mu^{2}/(n\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}+(G-n)\sigma^{2}_{\text{deg}})}=1+\frac{(G-n)\sigma^{2}_{\text{deg}}}{n\sigma^{2}_{\text{valid}}},

since
G>n
(degenerate trajectories exist) and
\sigma^{2}_{\text{deg}}>0
(Property 2), we conclude:

\frac{\text{SNR}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}}{\text{SNR}_{\text{GRPO}}}>1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{SNR}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}>\text{SNR}_{\text{GRPO}},

which proves that trajectory filtering strictly improves the gradient SNR by simultaneously preserving the expected gradient signal and eliminating the variance contribution of degenerate rollouts.

Proposition 1
(Logit Change in Policy Gradient
[
36
,
32
]
).
When updating the policy via Policy Gradient with learning rate
\eta
, the logit difference satisfies:

\theta^{k+1}_{s_{t},o_{t}}-\theta^{k}_{s_{t},o_{t}}=\eta\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot A_{t}.

Proof adapted from
[
32
]
.

\displaystyle\theta^{k+1}_{s_{t},o_{t}}-\theta^{k}_{s_{t},o_{t}}

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\nabla_{\theta_{s_{t},o_{t}}}\mathcal{J}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\mathbb{E}_{o^{\prime}_{t}\sim\pi^{k}_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t})}\left[\nabla_{\theta_{s_{t},o_{t}}}\log\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o^{\prime}_{t}|s_{t})\cdot A_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\sum_{o^{\prime}_{t}\in\mathcal{O}}\left[\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o^{\prime}_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\left(\mathbbm{1}\{o_{t}=o^{\prime}_{t}\}-\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\right)\cdot A_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\left[A_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{o^{\prime}_{t}\sim\pi^{k}_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t})}[A_{t}]\right]

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot A_{t}.

The last step holds because the GRPO advantage
\hat{A}_{t}
is group-normalized and thus has zero mean by construction, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}_{o^{\prime}_{t}\sim\pi^{k}_{\theta}}[A_{t}]=0
.
∎

Proposition 2
(Logit Change under
TAO-RL
).
When updating the policy via
\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}
with the reshaped advantage
\tilde{A}_{t}=\hat{A}_{t}\cdot(1+a\cdot g_{t}\cdot\tilde{A}_{t}^{\Delta\mathcal{H}})
and learning rate
\eta
, the logit difference satisfies:

\theta^{k+1}_{s_{t},o_{t}}-\theta^{k}_{s_{t},o_{t}}=\underbrace{\eta\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\hat{A}_{t}}_{\text{basic update}}+\underbrace{\eta\cdot a\cdot g_{t}\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\hat{A}_{t}\cdot\tilde{A}_{t}^{\Delta\mathcal{H}}}_{\text{exploration bonus}}.

Proof.

Following the same derivation as Proposition 1, with the reshaped advantage
\tilde{A}_{t}
substituted for
A_{t}
:

\displaystyle\theta^{k+1}_{s_{t},o_{t}}-\theta^{k}_{s_{t},o_{t}}

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\nabla_{\theta_{s_{t},o_{t}}}\mathcal{J}_{\texttt{{TAO-RL}}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\mathbb{E}_{o^{\prime}_{t}\sim\pi^{k}_{\theta}(\cdot|s_{t})}\left[\nabla_{\theta_{s_{t},o_{t}}}\log\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o^{\prime}_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\tilde{A}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\tilde{A}_{t}\quad\text{(by Proposition 1)}

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\hat{A}_{t}\cdot\left(1+a\cdot g_{t}\cdot\tilde{A}_{t}^{\Delta\mathcal{H}}\right)

\displaystyle=\eta\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\hat{A}_{t}+\eta\cdot a\cdot g_{t}\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\hat{A}_{t}\cdot\tilde{A}_{t}^{\Delta\mathcal{H}}.

For tokens outside
\mathcal{M}_{\text{post}}
,
g_{t}=0
and the update reduces to the standard Policy Gradient step
\eta\cdot\pi^{k}_{\theta}(o_{t}|s_{t})\cdot\hat{A}_{t}
. For gated tokens (
g_{t}=1
), the additional exploration bonus term amplifies the logit update proportionally to the token’s normalized entropy deviation
\tilde{A}_{t}^{\Delta\mathcal{H}}
, concentrating stronger optimization pressure at the most uncertain post-tool-call positions.
∎

On all three benchmarks of AIME24, AIME25, and MATH500,

Training Dynamics.
The training curves reveal a stark contrast in optimization behavior. TIR exhibits large and irregular gradient norm fluctuations throughout training, reflecting the destabilizing effect of unfiltered tool interactions on policy updates. In contrast,
TAO-RL

Tool-Use Quality.
The response length and code lines curves further highlight the depth of tool engagement cultivated by
TAO-RL

TAO-RL

TAO-RL
, which maintains a near-ceiling valid code rate throughout training. This confirms that without filtering tool-execution-failed trajectories, TIR consistently produces malformed tool interactions that contaminate the training distribution.

Taken together, these results demonstrate that simply incorporating tools into the training loop without principled data filtering and targeted exploration incentives leads to unstable optimization, shallow tool engagement, and degraded reasoning performance, all of which
TAO-RL
systematically addresses through its two complementary components.

Comparison of Curriculum Learning

To further investigate whether preserving data quantity through structured data organization can serve as a viable alternative to our quality-driven filtering strategy, we compare
TAO-RL
against a curriculum learning variant that organizes the full training data in a progressive easy-to-hard order based on task difficulty. Unlike our filtering approach, this variant retains all trajectories, including degenerate ones, aiming to stabilize training through difficulty-aware data ordering rather than explicit data removal.

As shown in Fig.
11
, the curriculum-based approach does yield a moderate stabilization effect compared to vanilla TIR: both gradient norm fluctuations and response length variability are somewhat reduced, confirming that progressive data organization provides a degree of training stability. However, the improvement is limited precisely because degenerate trajectories, those with failed tool executions or uninformative advantage estimates, remain in the training distribution and continue to inject noisy gradient signals throughout training.

In contrast,
TAO-RL
achieves consistently higher Avg@16 on both AIME24 and AIME25, along with substantially lower and more stable gradient norms and greater response lengths throughout training. These results demonstrate that explicitly removing degenerate rollouts is fundamentally more effective than reorganizing them, as the root cause of instability lies in the quality of individual trajectories rather than their ordering. Structured data organization can partially mitigate the symptoms of tool-induced instability, but only principled trajectory filtering can address the underlying noise at its source.

Analysis on Trajectory Filtering Criteria: Retaining All-Wrong Trajectories

A natural concern about our trajectory filtering strategy is that removing uniformly incorrect rollout groups may discard useful negative signals, since such groups can still contain valid tool calls or partial reasoning traces. To test this possibility, we introduce a
Keep-Wrong
variant under the Qwen2.5-7B base model, which discards only all-correct queries with
\rho(q)=1
while retaining all-incorrect queries with
\rho(q)=0
, provided they satisfy the tool-validity criterion.

Table
XIII
shows that retaining all-wrong rollout groups consistently hurts performance. Across six benchmarks,
Keep-Wrong
reduces the average
Avg@16
from
45.43
to
42.56
and the average
Pass@16
from
71.63
to
67.17
. On AIME24, the degradation is more pronounced, with
Avg@16
dropping by
5.42
and
Pass@16
by
13.33
. This suggests that all-wrong groups are not difficult-but-useful samples, but low-information instances that dilute effective optimization.

This degradation is consistent with the contrastive nature of GRPO-style optimization. When all sampled rollouts for a query are incorrect, their final-answer rewards are uniformly low, providing little reward contrast under group-normalized advantage estimation and thus yielding zero or near-zero informative policy-gradient signals. Unlike incorrect rollouts in mixed groups satisfying
0<\rho(q)<1
, all-wrong groups cannot support meaningful within-query comparisons between successful and failed reasoning or tool-use behaviors. They therefore mainly consume rollout and optimization budget without contributing discriminative learning signals, confirming the necessity of our answer-discriminability criterion.
