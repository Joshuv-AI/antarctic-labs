 ABSTRACT

Residential water heating in the United States is dominated by natural-gas and electric resistance storage
water heaters, while high performance water heaters (HPWHs) with a vapor compression cycle remain a
small but rapidly growing share due to their higher first cost and, in some cases, lower capacity relative to
incumbent technologies. This project developed and evaluated lower-cost, high-performance residential
HPWH concepts by replacing conventional refrigerants with R290 (propane), a low-cost, high-volumetric-
capacity A3 refrigerant that enables substantial refrigerant-charge reduction. Using a combination of
literature review, component-level experimentation in psychrometric chambers, and a 1D wrapped-
condenser model, both integrated (all-in-one) and split-system configurations were designed and assessed
with a focus on minimizing charge while maintaining or improving efficiency metrics (coefficient of
performance [COP] and uniform energy factor [UEF]). For the integrated design, testing and modeling with
an optimized microchannel condenser and updated components demonstrated up to an 80% reduction in
charge compared to an R134a baseline, with modeled UEF up to 3.4 at approximately 114 g of R290; the
prototype also achieved UL field certification (though field performance testing was not completed within
the project timeline). Split-system concepts were modeled to achieve high efficiency (modeled COP ≈ 3.7,
modeled UEF ≈ 3.6) at low charge (on the order of 130 g), though experimental testing was limited due to
flammability-related safety constraints. The work demonstrates technical feasibility for low-charge R290
HPWHs and highlights the importance of safety standards and compliance pathways (e.g., UL 60335-2-
40:2025), with split configurations offering an attractive route for managing A3 refrigerant risk by keeping
refrigerant outside the building envelope.

 1. INTRODUCTION

Water heating for residential buildings is typically accomplished by either natural gas or electric energy
sources, with and without a tank to store the hot water. The most common installations are natural gas (NG-
WH) and electric resistance storage type water heaters (ER-WH). New to the market are high performance
water heaters (HPWHs) that use the vapor compression cycle to heat the water by extracting heat from the
air to create domestic hot water inside a tank. HPWHs typically have higher first costs than ER-WHs or
NG-WHs and a lower capacity than NG-WHs.

Storage type NG-WHs and ER-WHs hold 47% and 47% percent of the roughly 100 million water heater
market in the USA (U.S. Census Bureau, 2023). New shipments of these water heaters in 2024 were
4,177,148 natural gas fueled units and 5,057,208 electricity driven units - which includes HPWHs and ER-
WHs (AHRI, n.d.). It is estimated that 2-4% of the electricity driven water heater sales, or roughly 150,000
units, are HPWHs (PHC Pro News, 2025). Compared to ER-WHs, HPWHs are typically 3-4 times more
efficient and have paybacks of roughly 2.7-5.5 years (ENERGY STAR, n.d.). Thus, for roughly 44% of the
water heater market, a HPWH is a good replacement for an ER-WH. Some customers preferred HWPHs
over NG-WHs, as natural gas fueled water heaters saw a 3% decline and electricity driven water heaters
saw a 3% increase, with the HPWH market growing 35% for the year 2024 (PHC Pro News, 2025). The
recent information indicates a growing demand for HPWHs and the cost reduction of HPWHs is the focus
of this project through a low-cost refrigerant replacement.

This report will outline the progress in developing a HPWH with lower first costs and higher capacity by
using R290, an emerging refrigerant with high volumetric capacity that allows for significant reduction in
refrigerant charge. Modeling and experimental testing were performed to evaluate several designs,
including integrated units (all-in-one package) and split configurations (part of the components are located
outside the house and part are inside) with the goals of reducing the refrigerant charge while maintaining
high performance. A charge size of 114 g of R290 was achieved for the integrated HPWH unit, representing
an 80% charge reduction from the baseline R134a unit, which is a 30% to 80% refrigerant cost reduction.

 1

Modeling of the optimized system predicts a UEF of 3.4 for the 114 gram R290 integrated HPWH unit.
The experimental unit received UL Field Certification but was not field tested due to project timeline
constraints. The split system HPWH unit was designed with high expectations of COP (3.7) at a very
minimal refrigerant charge (130 g). A unit was constructed for testing in the 2500 HPWH chamber. Model
predictions indicate that the split configuration will achieve a UEF of 3.6. Limited experimental testing was
completed in this project due to safety concerns with R290. The pertinent product safety standard for
HPWHs with A3 refrigerants is UL 60335-2-40:2025. This standard specifies 14 routine tests in which all
cannot be completed in the field.

The project demonstrates the feasibility of low-cost refrigerants to reduce the first costs of HPWHs while
maintaining high performance at refrigerant charge levels much below that of traditional HPWHs. Both
integrated HPWHs and split configuration HWPHs were studied. Due to the feasibility of keeping
significant charge outside the house, the split configuration is preferred for A3 refrigerants.

 2. STATEMENT OF OBJECTIVES

The objectives of the project were to develop and evaluate residential HPWH designs that reduce first cost
and increase capacity while maintaining high efficiency by adopting the low-charge, high-volumetric-
capacity refrigerant R290. Specifically, the project aimed to (1) use modeling and laboratory testing to
design and optimize both integrated and split-system HPWH configurations, (2) minimize refrigerant
charge while preserving or improving performance metrics such as 𝐶𝑂𝑃 and 𝑈𝐸𝐹, and (3) assess the
practical and safety implications of using an A3 flammable refrigerant, including alignment with applicable
requirements such as UL 60335-2-40:2025.

 3. BENEFITS TO DOE BUILDING TECHNOLOGIES OFFICE

This study benefits the DOE’s Building Technologies Office (BTO) by advancing an emerging, high-
efficiency water-heating technology toward broader adoption while addressing cost, performance, and
safety barriers that limit market scale-up. By developing and validating low-refrigerant-charge HPWH
designs using R290, the project supports BTO goals to reduce building energy use and operating cost, since
HPWHs can cut water-heating electricity consumption substantially relative to electric resistance systems
while maintaining user-acceptable performance. The work also contributes to building flexibility and grid
reliability by improving a major electric end use that can be paired with load management and operated
more efficiently during grid-constrained periods. In addition, the integrated and split-system design
evaluations provide industry-relevant design guidance and performance projections that reduce technical
risk and speed commercialization, aligning with BTO’s mission to validate and integrate affordable, energy-
saving technologies at scale. Finally, the project’s focus on low-charge A3 refrigerant implementation and
relevant safety considerations helps clarify pathways for compliant deployment, enabling efficient products
that can be manufactured and installed broadly while leveraging private-sector follow-on investment.

 4. TECHNICAL DISCUSSION

To optimize the performance of the HPWH in capacity and efficiency, a multifaceted approach was used.
The methods of investigation were literature review, modeling and experimental evaluation. The two
HPWH designs investigated are integrated and split systems. The goal is to reduce the costs of the HPWH
by using a lower cost refrigerant. R134a typically costs about $11.63 per pound mass (Absolute
Refrigerants, n.d.), whereas R290 when fully penetrating the market is estimated to cost 3-5 times less than
R134a. Per water heater, this represents a savings of about $4, which, if R290 fully replaced the R134a in
the 2025 HPWH market, would be about $250K savings to the industry. If all electricity driven water heater

 2

sales were entirely replaced with HPWHs, the savings to the water heater manufacturers would be roughly
$7M from the refrigerant change out, and the ER-WH consumer would experience a slight increase in
capacity with a 2.7 to 5.5 year return on investment.

4.1 DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS

The major component considerations are the compressor, evaporator, fan, expansion device and condenser.
All equipment used an electronic expansion valve to control the flow of refrigerant to the evaporator. The
compressors, condensers and fans were replaced through integrated testing to determine the best
components for the R290 HPWH. The integrated unit was used to optimize the components, which were
then converted into a split system.

4.1.1 EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATIONS

Experimental performance evaluations were conducted in psychometric chambers to optimize the
refrigerant charge for the integrated and split HPWH configurations using a microchannel condenser. The
charge optimization results varied from 114 g to 445 g of R290 for the integrated HPWH (Figure 1). The
compressors and condensers were switched out between tests with a d-shaped condenser wrap. Nominally
smaller and larger compressors were used while modifying a typical condenser (e.g., halving the coil length,
followed by using a quarter of the coil length).

 Figure 1. Experimental performance vs. refrigerant charge for baseline and modified R290 HPWHs.

above 120°F for the entire hot water draw. Refrigerant charge was typically added until all the draws met

could meet the test requirements regarding temperature using a microchannel condenser, improved fan and
compressor. With prior knowledge of how the UEF correlates to refrigerant charge during charge

The optimized HPWH design utilizes a R290 charge of approximately 150 g and a compressor displacement
of 0.63 in³. The evaporator consists of a fin-and-tube heat exchanger with six refrigerant circuits, two tube
rows, and eighteen tubes per row. Copper tubes with aluminum wave fins are employed to maximize heat
transfer performance while minimizing refrigerant charge.

The condenser is a tube-in-tube heat exchanger with an overall length of 60 in, a heat transfer area of
approximately 0.9 ft², and an overall heat transfer coefficient of 156 Btu/hr-ft²-°F.

The developed model was used to predict system performance over a range of operating conditions.
Simulations were conducted with condenser water inlet temperatures representative of domestic water
heating applications and evaporator inlet air temperatures ranging from 50°F to 80°F. Predicted system
performance, including heating capacity, compressor power consumption, and coefficient of performance

 5. COMMERCIALIZATION POSSIBILITIES

A.O. Smith has expressed interest in commercializing high-efficiency HPWHs utilizing R290 (propane) for
the U.S. market, contingent upon the continued evolution of safety guidelines and regulatory frameworks
that permit the installation and use of such systems. R290 offers significant advantages, including excellent
thermodynamic performance, and the potential for improved system efficiency and reduced refrigerant
charge. Internationally, several manufacturers have already successfully commercialized R290-based
HPWHs, demonstrating the technical viability and market acceptance of the technology. For example, the
PHNIX R290 Heat Pump Water Heater Platform showcases commercially available products designed for
residential and commercial water heating applications. Additional commercialization opportunities include
the development of next-generation commercial HPWHs with higher water delivery temperatures, reduced
refrigerant charge through advanced heat exchanger designs, integrated thermal energy storage for load
flexibility, grid-interactive controls, and optimized operation for commercial building applications. Further
opportunities exist in retrofittable HPWH solutions for existing water heating infrastructure, packaged
systems for multifamily and commercial buildings, and high-capacity systems capable reducing operating
costs. Collaboration between industry and ORNL can help accelerate these pathways by reducing technical
risk and validating performance necessary for broader market adoption.

 6. FUTURE COLLABORATION PLANS

This project has further strengthened the long-standing collaboration between A.O. Smith and Oak Ridge
National Laboratory, establishing a strong foundation for future joint research and technology development
in advanced water heating systems. Building on the successes of this effort, future work will focus on
expanding investigations of commercial HPWHs, including performance optimization, cost reduction
strategies, advanced controls, and integration with emerging refrigerants. Ongoing collaborative activities
initiated through this project include the development and validation of advanced hot water storage tank
models to reduce prototyping costs, accelerate product development cycles, and improve system-level
design optimization. Additional efforts will evaluate next-generation refrigerants and their impacts on
efficiency, capacity, safety, reliability, and manufacturability in commercial HPWH applications. Future
collaboration opportunities include joint evaluation and design of high-performance HPWH systems,
advanced thermal energy storage integration, demand-flexible and grid-interactive operation, refrigerant
charge reduction strategies, novel heat exchanger technologies, and digital engineering tools for accelerated
product development. These activities will support the commercialization of next-generation water heating
technologies that improve energy efficiency, enhance grid resiliency, and strengthen U.S. manufacturing
competitiveness.

 7. CONCLUSIONS

This project demonstrated the technical feasibility of utilizing R290 (propane) to develop next-generation
HPWHs with substantially reduced refrigerant charge, lower refrigerant cost, and high energy performance.
Through a combination of experimental testing and modeling, both integrated and split-system HPWH
architectures were evaluated with the objective of reducing first cost while maintaining high efficiency. For
the integrated HPWH configuration, an optimized microchannel wrapped-condenser design enabled
refrigerant charges as low as approximately 114 g while maintaining strong performance. Modeling
projected a UEF of approximately 3.4 at this charge level, representing up to an 80% reduction in refrigerant
charge compared with conventional R134a systems. The integrated prototype also achieved UL field
certification and was successfully installed at the Yarnell Station Test House, demonstrating the practicality
of deploying low-charge R290 technology in residential applications. Modeling results also highlighted the
potential of a split-system architecture, with projected performance of COP ≈ 3.7 and UEF ≈ 3.6 at

 9

refrigerant charges of approximately 130–150 g. Although experimental validation was limited by A3
refrigerant safety considerations, the results suggest that split configurations can achieve high efficiency
while maintaining very low refrigerant inventory. Overall, the project establishes a credible pathway for
reducing HPWH cost while maintaining high performance through low-charge R290 designs. The work
demonstrates the importance of advanced heat exchanger technologies, refrigerant charge optimization, and
compliance with evolving safety standards such as UL 60335-2-40:2025. While both integrated and split-
system approaches show promise, split configurations may offer the most practical long-term
commercialization pathway by locating a significant portion of the refrigerant charge outside the
conditioned living space, simplifying risk management and supporting broader market adoption.
