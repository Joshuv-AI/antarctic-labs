 3. Executive Summary
 The Tandem Photovoltaics Core Program was a multi-year initiative aimed at
 advancing hybrid tandem solar cell technologies to enhance solar module efficiency
 beyond the limits of single junction devices. This project focused on the development,
 testing, and scaling of prototype photovoltaic devices, with the goal of achieving
 commercial relevance and driving industry adoption. The work was divided into three
 tasks:
 1) Comparative Analysis of Tandem Technologies: This task focused on quantifying
 energy yield under real-world conditions and assessing economic viability of tandems
 relative to silicon-based modules. The project's modeling framework incorporated
 performance data, cost of materials, and manufacturing process impacts to optimize
 tandem designs
 2) Tandem Integration and Prototyping: In this task, we developed innovative tandem
 designs by combining metal halide perovskite (MHP) top cells and silicon (Si) bottom
 cells. The project focuses on both mechanical integration and direct deposition
 techniques to enable compatibility with commercially relevant Si technologies, such
 as passivated contact or PERC cells.
 3) Scale-up and Reliability: This task addressed the challenges of large-area fabrication
 by developing scalable deposition methods and robust interconnection schemes for
 tandems. The project looked at different accelerated testing such as thermal cycling,
 damp heat exposure, and potential induced degradation, to ensure long-term stability
 of devices in field conditions.

 Tandem solar cells can greatly increase module efficiency beyond conventional single
 junction (SJ) devices, which are approaching their theoretical limit. There are many ways
 to fabricate a tandem cell or module in terms of materials used, configuration, and terminal
 connection. This SETO core project focused on critical factors in enabling tandems to
 enter the market, including hardware integration, technoeconomic analysis (TEA), and
 energy yield analysis. We focused on MHP/Si hybrid tandem solar cells and modules as
 a model system for their versatility in module design comparisons, providing valuable
 insights for other tandem options. While champion cells with areas <1cm2 are regularly
 demonstrated by groups around the world, it is significantly more challenging to translate
 these advances into modules, and fewer institutions and companies are working at the
 module level. This project addressed questions about module fabrication, testing, and
 reliability that are hard to answer without fabricating prototypes. We also performed
 analysis and roadmapping activities to understand the potential for a wider variety of
 tandems, including all-perovskite tandems fabricated in collaboration with the Perovskite
 PV core program. Detailed technical results from this project are described for each task
 in Section 7.

 5. Background
 Tandem solar cells can exceed the efficiency of single junction devices, which are
 nearing their theoretical limits. This project examined crucial factors for tandems, such as
 module integration, technoeconomic analysis, and energy yield analysis, focusing on into
 metal halide perovskite (MHP) and silicon (Si) hybrid tandems. During the three-year
 period of this project, there was a substantial amount of international research into MHP
 and Si-based photovoltaic (PV) tandem technologies, as well as multiple efficiency
 records set by universities and companies for MHP/Si tandems.1 Many leading groups
 continued to focus on cell-level demonstrations of MHP-based tandems using very small
 device areas.2,3 At the same time, multiple companies publicly announced plans to pursue
 MHP/Si tandems, including Swift Solar, Cubic PV, Oxford PV, Caelux, Beyond Silicon,
 Longi, QCells, Tandem PV, Trina, and others. Many valuable review articles have been
 written that nicely summarize the overall progress in the field. Shi et al. discuss the many
 different approaches to MHP/Si tandems and review different advances in the field.4
 Aydin et al. discuss the pathway to commercialization, including discussions of aging tests
 that are being considered to evaluate the long-term stability and reliability pf MHP/Si
 tandems.5 While there has continued to be a focus on MHP/Si, there are some industry
 perspectives that advocate for considering other thin film tandem options.6
 The specific areas of focus for this project were demonstrating tandem device
 integration, understanding energy yield, and building a baseline to study scale-up and
 reliability. Below we highlight relevant background in each of these areas to show how
 this project fits within the context of the current state of the art in tandem solar cell R&D.

 The options for how cells are interconnected also can influence energy yield, as predicted
 by this project’s work on energy yield modeling of tandem strings, and an experimental

 This project worked closely with the Photovoltaic Accelerator for Commercializing
 Technologies (PACT) which is working to develop testing and validating characterization
 protocols that can quantify module performance and accurately measure power loss and
 degradation. PACT has identified that there are reversible changes in performance
 present in some MHP-based solar cells that can lead to significant changes in power
 output over the course of a single day.12 Remec et al. showed a possible approach to
 combine metastabilities with energy yield analysis for tandems.13

 6. Project Objectives:
 National lab research plays a critical role in “bridging the gap” between university-led
 low TRL efforts and commercialization by analyzing, fabricating, and testing prototypes
 with realistic configurations. In this project, we created tandem photovoltaic devices (cells,
 coupons, and mini-modules), examined their performance and reliability, measured and
 modeled energy yield, and compared them to single junction PV standards. By developing
 commercially relevant prototypes and understanding costs, performance benefits, and
 trade-offs, this NLR Core project aimed to guide the commercialization of tandem
 photovoltaics, addressing concerns like optical losses, stringing, multi-cell interactions,
 and outdoor reliability.
 Key Deliverables

 4

 • Prototypes: Fabrication of mini-modules with multiple interconnected cells that
 include all necessary packaging and interconnections for functionality and reliability.
 Specific goals had tasks focused around analysis, prototype development, and
 reliability. The overall goal of this project was to develop a mini-module with 100cm2
 area and an efficiency >27%. By employing a mix of indoor characterization and
 outdoor performance monitoring, we sought to estimate the annual degradation rate
 for a 25 cm² hybrid tandem minimodule and benchmark this against the latest
 advancements in single-junction perovskite devices.
 • Roadmap Publication: Development of a strategic review synthesizing findings on
 tandem configurations, emerging material prospects, cost analyses, and market
 integration strategies.
 • Outdoor Module Deployment: There is no substitute for real-world data for
 understanding the performance and possible failure mechanisms of emerging PV
 technologies. Deploying tandem modules outdoors and collecting performance data
 is key to advance the maturity of this technology. The project deployed perovskite/Si
 tandem modules outdoors for 6 months in Golden, CO and monitored their
 performance.
 • Data Dissemination: Peer-reviewed publications, industry workshops, and
 collaboration with stakeholders to ensure knowledge transfer and alignment with
 commercial goals.

 Subtask 1.2: Energy Yield Analysis
 • This subtask focused on energy yield modeling of tandem devices under varying
 environmental conditions. Models were built to compare different tandem
 configurations using realistic tandem device physics, accurate optical models, and
 location-specific insolation data (including spectrum), which is not captured in any
 commercial energy yield modeling software.
 • Deliverable M2.1.1: Predict energy yield for at least two tandem configurations

 Our energy yield work built upon frameworks developed in previous projects, such as
 the open source PVCircuit code (publicly available on GitHub),17 and the energy yield
 analysis for different terminal configurations.18 The work in this subtask was successfully
 achieved as demonstrated by the publication of a paper comparing 2T and 4T MHP/Si
 tandems.19 This paper by Witteck et al. carried out an apples-to-apples comparison of
 how spectral differences impact MHP/Si performance.

 Abstract: In this work, the effect of a varying spectral irradiance and top cell bandgap on
 the energy harvesting efficiency of two-terminal (2T) and four-terminal (4T)
 perovskite//silicon tandem solar cells under outdoor operating conditions is investigated.
 For the comparison, an optoelectronic model employing a 1-year outdoor data set for a
 4T mechanical stacked gallium arsenide (GaAs) on crystalline silicon (Si) tandem device
 is first validated. Then, the verified model is used to simulate perovskite//silicon tandem
 devices with a varying perovskite top cell bandgap for a location in Golden, Colorado,
 USA. A spectral binning method to efficiently reduce and improve the visualization of the
 1 min-resolved environmental data while maintaining the simulation accuracy is
 introduced. The findings reveal that, for a device that is current matched under standard
 testing conditions, the annual spectral deviation reduces the energy harvesting efficiency
 by only 2% relative. When additional realistic losses for the 4T are taken into account, 2T

 Deviations in the top cell bandgap are more than 0.1 eV from current matching result in

 Figure 6 shows the modeling flow for computing energy harvesting efficiency (EHE)
 -- the amount of energy produced by a photovoltaic system divided by the total amount
 of incident illumination over a given period of time. The model accounts for the cell
 architecture, including luminescent coupling, as well as meteorological conditions such
 as temperature, wind speed, global irradiance and spectrum. Simulating the EHE for a
 full year using 1-minute time intervals is computationally intensive and often involves
 many redundant calculations for times when the meteorological conditions are similar.
 We investigated ways to reduce the data volume using a spectral binning method that
 groups spectra into sets of similar spectra. We found that doing this and representing
 each set by a single "proxy" spectrum was very computationally efficient and gave
 accurate integrated results. Figure 7 illustrates our binning method. Figure 7a divides the
 complete spectral set into equal energy bins, using 10 bins as an example.

 Figure 6 Modeling flow for the EHE calculations used. [Image reproduced from Witteck et al.
 2023.19]

 Figure 7b shows the resulting proxy spectrum for the fifth bin, which is the mean of
 all measured spectra in that bin. Figure 7c compares the integrated irradiance for all
 measured spectra (colored symbols) with 100 proxy spectra generated with our binning
 method (superimposed green dots). Our binning method follows an approach presented
 by Garcia et al,20 however, instead of the equivalent photocurrent ratio, we use the
 average photon energy (APE). The advantage of utilizing APE is that it remains
 unaffected by device-specific parameters while maintaining a linear relation to device-

 global normal irradiance, which includes both direct and diffuse spectral components,
 whereas Garcia et al. only used direct spectra.

 • Deliverable (M3.1.1): Comparative publication on tandem PV system costs and

 Abstract: Tandem photovoltaic (PV) modules offer an opportunity to improve the
 efﬁciency and energy yield of available solar resources compared with single-junction
 devices. We present a cost model and sensitivity analysis of perovskite/silicon (Si)
 tandem modules to understand how design choices impact overall module costs. We ﬁnd
 a minimum sustainable price (MSP) of $0.428/WDC for our baseline two-terminal design
 and $0.423/WDC for our baseline four-terminal design, each at a module efﬁciency of 25%
 and module production of 3 GW per year in the United States. We ﬁnd that the choice of
 Si cell architecture, overall module efﬁciency, and factory throughput have the most
 signiﬁcant impacts on cost and competitiveness. The dynamic model provided is
 designed for researchers, companies, and interested individuals to use and edit as
 needed to better understand the trade-offs among PV module technologies and enable
 targeted research to improve module design and fabrication.

 Task 2: Tandem Integration and Prototyping
 Description: Focus on fabrication of high-efficiency tandem prototypes.

 To further enable device stability, it is critical to identify selective contact materials

 Understanding the best ways to package MHP solar cells is critical for scaling this
 technology. In collaboration with a SETO-funded SIPS project 39062, we investigated
 the compatibility of vacuum lamination processes with perovskite solar cell (PSC)
 encapsulation, aiming to align device integration with established industrial practices. We
 initially  fabricated standard p-i-n PSC architectures glass/ITO/2PACz/
 perovskite/C₆₀/BCP/Ag and encapsulated the devices between silane-grafted polyolefin
 elastomer (POE) and glass. These were subjected to lamination conditions representative
 of industrial silicon module processes (125-150 °C for 14-23 min; Figure 19). A clear
 inverse correlation was observed between lamination severity and device performance,

 21

 with power conversion efficiency (PCE) losses reaching up to 27% under 150 °C for
 23 min, predominantly due to reductions in short-circuit current density and fill factor. To
 improve thermal stability, we first investigated various HTL compositions, comparing
 conventional PTAA with self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) including 2PACz and MeO-
 2PACz (Figure 19). Devices employing SAM-based HTLs exhibited significantly
 enhanced resilience under thermal stress, maintaining over 90% of their initial PCE even
 at 150 °C for 14 min, whereas PTAA-based devices showed pronounced degradation.
 We then focused on modifying the ETL by replacing thermally unstable BCP with an
 atomic layer deposited (ALD) SnOₓ buffer layer. This substitution further improved thermal
 robustness, limiting PCE losses to below 3% at 150 °C for 14 min and preserving
 performance under milder conditions (125 °C for 14 min). The enhanced device stability
 is attributed to suppressed silver diffusion, confirmed by compositional depth profiling,
 and retention of internal electric fields, as evidenced by cross-sectional Kelvin probe force
 microscopy (cKPFM). Electroluminescence imaging further revealed that devices
 incorporating SnOₓ and SAM-based HTLs remain free of local defects after lamination,
 unlike BCP-buffered devices which exhibit shunting (Figure 20c and d). These findings
 demonstrate that strategic selection of both HTL and ETL materials enables PSCs to
 endure vacuum lamination under industrial conditions, advancing their viability for
 commercial photovoltaic module integration.

 Subtask 3.2: Tandem-Specific Reliability
 • Investigate chemical, mechanical, and electrical compatibility in the tandem

 • Monitor devices for degradation mechanisms unique to tandem configurations.
 • Deliverable: Fabricate modules and carry out PACT protocol tests to identify

 Although advancements have been made in achieving high efficiencies in laboratory-
 scale tandem devices, their widespread use is limited by durability and reliability
 challenges. These issues include material incompatibility, degradation pathways such as
 delamination and potential-induced degradation, and the necessity for robust packaging
 designs to ensure long-term performance. Based on our experimental observations, we
 wrote a perspective article, outlining the potential reliability challenges of different types
 of tandems and provided guidance for how the research community can tackle these
 challenges.26

 The reliability testing of modules, rather than just individual cells, has become
 essential due to the additional mechanical and chemical challenges introduced by larger
 module sizes. Current standards for PV modules, designed for silicon-based
 technologies, do not adequately address the unique reliability concerns of MHP-based
 tandems, such as sensitivity to light and elevated temperatures. To promote the adoption
 of this emerging technology, we wrote a perspective article suggest utilizing decades of
 PV reliability data and concentrating on module-level testing. Collaborative efforts to
 share field performance data and develop new testing protocols will aid in the deployment
 of tandem PV systems, contributing to more efficient and sustainable energy solutions.

 Abstract: Silicon-based tandem photovoltaic cells and modules are forecasted to enter
 mass production around the year 2026. Previous research efforts on tandem photovoltaic
 technology have focused heavily on increasing cell efficiencies. However, for successful
 large-scale deployment of tandem modules, reliability and long-term durability will be
 equally important. Unlike single-junction devices, multi-junction photovoltaic modules are
 more sensitive to spectral variations, which complicates procedures to extract power
 ratings and demands a more advanced methodological approach for degradation
 analysis. Furthermore, outdoor performance data beyond one year are not yet widely
 available but are essential for understanding degradation mechanisms and building
 confidence in the long-term reliability of emerging tandem technologies. In this work, we
 build upon established performance monitoring standards and degradation analysis
 procedures developed for crystalline silicon devices and modify them towards tandem
 devices. We employ a set of filter criteria to select measurement points that are close to
 standard test conditions (STC). This filtering process is crucial for isolating comparable
 spectral and stable ambient conditions, which enhances the robustness of the
 degradation analysis. The methodology includes the normalization of raw power
 production data, correcting for temperature and spectral variations, and calculating a
 dimensionless performance ratio (PR) to facilitate comparisons and extract degradation
 rates. To exemplify our approach, we use an available dataset of a mechanically stacked,

 8. Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
 This project successfully combined material development, scale-up, analysis, and
 reliability testing to greatly advance the state-of-the-art for tandem technology. The team
 published multiple high-impact papers, including a roadmap for tandem technology and
 engaged with many industrial partners through workshops, conferences, and direct
 conversations. Procurement of the large area tools and external Si subcells needed to
 scale the area and throughput of module fabrication was delayed multiple times during
 this project, which delayed some of the area and efficiency related milestones, but the
 team started the process of upgrading the lab’s ability to carry out industrially relevant
 processes. This included purchasing two laser scribing systems for module fabrication
 work and converting a large-area, multi-source sputtering tool to enable deposition of
 inorganic layers for perovskite devices. Working with SETO, the project successfully
 rescoped its focus to developing a repeatable 4TT MHP/Si process flow and documenting
 it using the universal workflow language that was developed in collaboration with the
 perovskite core program.33 This change in scope was valuable in laying the groundwork
 for the FY25-FY27 Perovskite-Enabled Tandems core program.

 10. Path Forward:
 The research conducted in this program is one building block for the new FY25-27
 Perovskite Enabled Tandem (PET) Core Program (Award DE-00052776). The
 understanding of scalable module fabrication, energy yield, and technoeconomic
 modeling are critical to the development of perovskite/Si tandems and understanding the
 roadmap forward for the commercialization of this technology.
 The path forward is focused on simultaneously addressing efficiency,
 manufacturability, performance modeling, and reliability of perovskite-based tandems.
 The combined PET program at NLR is structured to allow rapid feedback between early-
 stage development and outdoor deployment. The technical approach is primarily focused
 on all-perovskite 2T tandems and perovskite-based 4T tandems (that can be agnostic of
 bottom cell), to enable the fastest learning cycles that are relevant to US manufactured
 devices. Modeling approaches are focusing on incorporating tandems into commercially
 relevant modeling tools, to bridge the gap between academic and commercial
 approaches. Reliability tasks will build upon the work in this program and use a hypothesis
 driven approach to identify and understand key degradation mechanisms for perovskite-
 based tandem modules.
