Affine Filtering Measurements and Their Applications to Quantum Decoding
Avijit Mandal, Noah Shutty, Henry D. Pfister, Stephen P. Jordan

Abstract
Unambiguous state discrimination (USD) measurements are attractive because outcomes are either marked as conclusive (i.e., error free) or inconclusive (i.e., erased). We study affine filtering measurements, a structured variant of USD for decoding classical linear codes over pure-state classical-quantum channels, where a conclusive outcome identifies an affine subspace containing the transmitted codeword and an inconclusive outcome is treated as an erasure. For a group-covariant indexing of pure-state codewords, we show that the optimal design of affine filtering measurements is a semidefinite program that can be reduced to a linear program via character-based diagonalization. We use the resulting measurement to build a quantum decoding framework for local codes, and we demonstrate (via simulations on regular LDPC codes from Gallager ensembles using single parity check local constraints) that affine filtering based decoding can outperform symbol-wise USD and symbol-wise pretty good measurement based decoding methods on i.i.d. pure-state channels. In an independent and concurrent work, Buzet and Chailloux study similar fine-grained USD measurements for symmetric families of states. Their focus is on the code-agnostic setting whereas our focus is on code-aware constructions and decoding.

Introduction

Coding theory for classical–quantum (CQ) channels traces back to the foundational work by Holevo
[
14
]
and by Schumacher and Westmoreland
[
31
]
, who established that one can communicate reliably at any rate up to the channel capacity by performing joint (collective) measurements across long codeword blocks. For many practically important structured CQ channels such as pure-state signal constellations that arise in optical communications
[
18
,
7
]
, the main hurdle in practice is
the implementation of these large
collective measurements. This challenge has spurred the development of structured receiver designs whose complexity grows slowly
with blocklength while still offering provable performance.

Recent progress on quantum algorithms based on Regev’s reduction has renewed interest in designing efficient algorithms for the quantum decoding problem
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
35
]
.
In the same spirit, a recently introduced method called decoded quantum interferometry (DQI)
[
17
]
uses reversible decoding together with quantum Fourier sampling to transform certain structured optimization problems into decoding problems.
In
[
32
]
, the authors develop locally quantum decoders for LDPC codes and use them, through the Regev and DQI framework, to obtain improved approximate solutions for average-case
D
-regular max-
k
-XORSAT problems,
further motivating the study of efficient quantum decoding algorithms.

Several works have investigated the performance of classical codes, such as polar and Reed-Muller codes
[
33
,
34
,
24
]
, on classical quantum channels in the context of achieving the Holevo bound. Although these works studied the capacity-achieving potential of such codes, they did not provide efficient constructions to design practical quantum decoding methods.

In pursuit of designing low complexity quantum decoders, Belief propagation with quantum messages (BPQM) was introduced by Renes
[
28
]
as a quantum analogue of classical BP for decoding binary linear codes on pure-state channels (PSCs).
Subsequent works developed BPQM-based density-evolution (DE) for symmetric binary CQ channels and used them to study LDPC and polar constructions
[
1
,
20
,
21
]
, with further extensions to turbo-style constructions
[
27
]
. Recently, BPQM has been extended to codes with prime alphabet sizes and abelian groups with more general factor graphs in
[
25
,
22
]
. Although efficient constructions of BPQM-based decoders have been provided for polar codes
[
21
]
, it is still not clear how to implement an efficient BPQM-based decoder for LDPC codes due to the sequential nature of message update rules.

For a collection of geometrically uniform states with equal priors, the pretty good measurement (PGM)
[
13
,
15
]
has been proven to minimize the error probability of state discrimination

[
10
,
9
]
.
When decoding codes on PSCs, one approach is to apply the
PGM to discriminate between the quantum states associated with individual code symbols
and then model the measurement outcome
as a
classical q-ary channel.
For classical post-processing, one can
use a belief-propagation(BP)-based decoder to estimate the transmitted codeword.
While implementing PGM to decode codeword
\mathbf{c}
of sufficiently large code
\mathcal{C}
is extremely complex, it is possible to apply PGM
to decode smaller local projections
of a larger code. However, this introduces correlated errors, making the analysis of classical post-processing difficult.

For a collection of quantum states, optimal USD
measurements
minimize the probability of an inconclusive outcome
[
16
,
26
,
8
,
5
]
. In the context of decoding codes on a PSC, when we apply the optimal USD measurement to quantum states associated with individual code symbols, the resultant measurement outcome can be modelled as erasure errors.
For linear codes, we then
use a Gaussian-elimination (GE)-based decoder to estimate the transmitted codeword.

In this work, we study affine filtering measurements, a code-aware filtering
form of USD.
The terminology filtering is motivated by the filtering framework of

[
6
]
, where conclusive quantum measurement outcomes provide
partial information about a hidden variable and inconclusive outcomes are
discarded. Our formulation is specific to linear codes where the conclusive information takes the form of affine subspaces containing the transmitted codeword.
A similar class of measurements was studied recently in

[
2
]
, where the authors describe them as fine-grained USD
measurements (FGUM).
While their work does not leverage code structure, our
affine filtering measurements are designed to do so. The affine-subspace
outcomes are converted into linear constraints, which we then use to build a
quantum decoding framework for linear codes on pure-state channels.
More specifically, given a collection of
symmetric quantum states indexed by codewords transmitted over a PSC, an affine
filtering measurement outputs an affine subspace of the code
\mathcal{C}
that
contains the transmitted codeword, or outputs an inconclusive outcome. The goal
is to maximize a chosen objective, such as the expected number of recovered
linear equations.
Constructing the optimal affine filtering measurement is a
semidefinite program (SDP).
However, even for a code with only a few symbols, the number of affine subspaces can be exponentially large.
This makes finding the optimal measurement extremely challenging.

Our primary contributions are the simplification of the SDP and the analysis of the implied decoder. We
prove that, for a collection of symmetric quantum states, the aforementioned SDP
can be simplified to
a linear program (LP). This makes the optimization
much more tractable
and, using the solution of the LP,
we provide an
explicit form of the optimal measurement.
In
[
32
]
, the authors use FGUM as a locally quantum decoding method for the quantum decoding problem arising from average-case
D
-regular max-
k
-XORSAT. These measurements are applied to a specific family of local quantum states obtained from a special blockwise choice of the state-preparation function. For each degree-
D
single parity check (SPC) code
C_{I}
supported on an index set
I
, this gives a family of states indexed by the local codewords
\mathbf{c}\in C_{I}
. The FGUM either identifies
\mathbf{c}
exactly or returns a failure outcome. The failed local measurements are then treated as erasures and combined with the global linear constraints by GE. In this paper, we show that, for the same SPC-associated choice of quantum states, the FGUM is an optimal
affine filtering measurement for maximizing the expected number of recovered linear equations.

Next, we
design a new class of quantum decoding methods
for a global
code
\mathcal{C}

whose code symbols can be partitioned into a collection of subsets
\{I_{j}\}
such that each subset is associated with a local code
\mathcal{C}_{I_{j}}
. The idea is to apply the optimal
affine filtering measurement
to the states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{I_{j}}}
.
For each index set
I_{j}
, the measurement outcome is either inconclusive or an affine subspace containing the correct local codeword.
The objective function is the expected number of equations generated by the
affine filtering decoding of each local code.
After running the above procedure for each index set, we try to recover the codeword using GE. Based on this procedure, we simulate an
affine filtering measurement decoder
that applies the measurement to the qudit states associated with SPC local codes in
a
(k,D)
-regular LDPC code. We observe that, for most
(k,D)
pairs, when we fix the channel to be an i.i.d. PSC, the
affine-filtering+GE-based decoder outperforms single qudit-wise measurement based decoders or more specifically, qudit-PGM+BP decoder and the qudit-USD+GE decoder.

In Section
3
, we give a complete description of the
affine filtering measurement and derive its SDP to LP reduction. We also describe the limiting LP formulation obtained as the limit of full-rank perturbations when the states are linearly dependent. In Section
4
, we specialize the local code to a SPC code and describe the
affine filtering+GE decoding algorithm for the
(k,D)
Gallager ensemble of regular LDPC codes
[
11
,
30
,
29
]
over i.i.d. PSCs. In Section
5
, we present numerical results for the resulting decoder performance.

We use

The canonical basis is represented using

For a vector

For a code

For example, when

\mathcal{C}_{I}=\{\mathbf{c}_{I}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{|I|}:\mathbf{1}_{I}^{T}\mathbf{c}_{I}=0\}.

Define

to be a primitive
q
th

We denote the generalized Pauli shift and phase operators by

, respectively. They satisfy

\displaystyle X^{a}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=\ket{j+a},

\displaystyle Z^{a}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=\omega^{aj}\ket{j},

for all

. For a vector

\displaystyle X^{\mathbf{a}}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{i=0}^{m-1}X^{a_{i}},\quad Z^{\mathbf{a}}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{i=0}^{m-1}Z^{a_{i}}.

The unitary
\QFT
corresponds to the quantum Fourier transform which satisfies

\displaystyle\QFT\ket{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\omega^{jk}\ket{k},

for all
j\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The unitary

satisfies the following relations

\displaystyle\QFT X^{a}\QFT^{\dagger}=Z^{a},\quad\QFT^{\dagger}X^{a}\QFT=Z^{-a}.

2.2
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

A classical-quantum (CQ) channel
W\colon x\rightarrow\rho_{x}
takes a classical input
x
from a finite size alphabet
\mathcal{X}
and outputs a density matrix
\rho_{x}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A CQ channel
W\colon x\rightarrow\rho_{x}
is called a pure-state channel (PSC), if the output state
\rho_{x}=\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}
is a pure state, i.e. a rank-1 matrix
\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The pretty good measurement (PGM), which is also known as the square root measurement, is defined as follows: consider the CQ channel
W\colon x\rightarrow\rho_{x}
with a uniform input distribution on
\mathcal{X}
. For the output state
\rho_{x}
for
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the measurement operator is
M_{x}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}\bar{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{x}\bar{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
where
\bar{\rho}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}\rho_{x}
and the inverse is taken on the support of
\bar{\rho}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Consider the PSC
W\colon x\rightarrow\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}
, with
x\in\mathcal{X}
. The unambiguous state discrimination (USD) measurement constructs the measurement
\{\Pi_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
such that
the measurement operators satisfy the unambiguity criterion
\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{x}\ket{\psi_{x^{\prime}}}\bra{\psi_{x^{\prime}}})=0

\forall x\neq x^{\prime}
and
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\Pi_{x}+\Pi_{e}=\mathbb{I}
. The optimal USD measurement minimizes the average probability of obtaining the inconclusive outcome of the measurement, i.e.,
\Pi_{e}
. For the uniform input distribution on
\mathcal{X}
, the problem is defined by an SDP below:

Minimize

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{e}\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}})

(1)

Subject to:

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{x}\ket{\psi_{x^{\prime}}}\bra{\psi_{x^{\prime}}})=0,\forall x\neq x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\ \sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\Pi_{x}+\Pi_{e}=\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle\ \Pi_{x}\succeq 0,\forall x\in\mathcal{X},\Pi_{e}\succeq 0.

(2)

We will also need the following specialization of Definition 2.4 to the collection of codeword states associated with a local code.

Definition 2.5
.

Consider the collection of quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{I}}
associated with the local code
\mathcal{C}_{I}
. For code
\mathcal{C}_{I}
, the
codeword filtering measurement is the POVM

\displaystyle\left\{\Pi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathrm{Cw\mbox{-}Filt}}\right\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{I}}\cup\left\{\Pi_{e}^{\mathrm{Cw\mbox{-}Filt}}\right\}

such that the measurement operators satisfy

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\Pi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathrm{Cw\mbox{-}Filt}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}\right)=0,\qquad\forall\mathbf{c}\neq\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\ \mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{I},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{I}}\Pi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathrm{Cw\mbox{-}Filt}}+\Pi_{e}^{\mathrm{Cw\mbox{-}Filt}}=\mathbb{I}.

Obtaining the outcome
\Pi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathrm{Cw\mbox{-}Filt}}
corresponds to identifying or filtering the transmitted codeword
\mathbf{c}
exactly, while
\Pi_{e}^{\mathrm{Cw\mbox{-}Filt}}
corresponds to an inconclusive outcome.

To characterize the CQ channel
W
, we use the Holevo information as an information measure. We use the symmetric Holevo information to parameterize and compare the
PSCs used in the numerical experiments.
Symmetric Holevo information measures the maximum amount of classical information that can be sent through the channel
W
when the input distribution is uniform.

Definition 2.6
.

For a CQ channel
W\colon x\rightarrow\rho_{x}
with
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the symmetric Holevo information is computed as

\displaystyle I(W)=S\Bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\rho_{x}\Bigg)-\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}S\Bigg(\rho_{x}\Bigg)

where
S(\rho)=-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log\rho)
is the von Neumann entropy for the density matrix
\rho

Next, consider the classical-quantum channel

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}=X^{\mathbf{c}}\QFT^{\otimes|I|}\ket{\psi}.

For each subspace

We denote

For the parity check matrix

Therefore,

For a syndrome

V_{\mathbf{s}}\coloneqq\{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}:H_{V}\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{s}\}

Our analysis is heavily based on group characters. For a local code
\mathcal{C}
,
define the dual group
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
to be the set of characters
\chi

\chi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{T}
where
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|=1\}
is the unit circle under multiplication
[
19
]
. It follows that

\displaystyle\chi(\mathbf{c}_{1}+\mathbf{c}_{2})=\chi(\mathbf{c}_{1})\chi(\mathbf{c}_{2}),\forall\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2}\in\mathcal{C},\forall\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is finite, each character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies
|\chi(\mathbf{c})|=1
for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
. The group
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is equipped with trivial character
\chi_{\text{triv}}
such that
\chi_{\text{triv}}(\mathbf{c})=1

\forall\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
.
For two characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
, we define
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
as the character such that

\displaystyle(\chi_{1}\chi_{2})(\mathbf{c}):=\chi_{1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c}).

One can readily check that
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. For character
\chi
, let
\chi^{-1}
denote its inverse which satisfies

\displaystyle\chi\chi^{-1}=\chi_{\text{triv}}.

For a character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, the inverse character

is unique and satisfies the following relations
\forall\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}(\mathbf{c})=\chi(-\mathbf{c})=\frac{1}{\chi(\mathbf{c})}=\overline{\chi(\mathbf{c})}

The character group

satisfies
|\widehat{\mathcal{C}}|=|\mathcal{C}|
since

is a finite abelian group. For a subspace

, define its annihilator as

\displaystyle\widehat{V}^{\perp}\coloneqq\{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}:\chi(\mathbf{v})=1,\quad\forall\mathbf{v}\in V\}.

Then
\widehat{V}^{\perp}
is a subgroup of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, with

|\widehat{V}^{\perp}|=q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)}
. Hence
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}
has

cosets, which we denote by
\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}
for

.
The following standard character identities will be used repeatedly throughout the rest of the paper.

Lemma 2.7

(Character identities for finite abelian groups)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite abelian group with character group
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, and let a subspace
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}

have annihilator
\widehat{V}^{\perp}
. Then the following hold.

1.

For any
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c})=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\overline{\chi_{1}(\mathbf{c})}\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c})=|\mathcal{C}|\delta_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}.

2.

For any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{C}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\chi(\mathbf{v})=\begin{cases}|\widehat{V}^{\perp}|,&\text{if $\mathbf{v}\in V$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

3.

If
\chi_{1}\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m_{1}}
and

\chi_{2}\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m_{2}}
, where

\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m_{1}},\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m_{2}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1}\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}\iff m_{1}=m_{2}.

These identities follow from the standard orthogonality relations for characters of finite abelian groups
[
19
]
.

3
Affine Filtering Measurements

In this section, we study a structured class of unambiguous measurements for codeword-indexed symmetric state families.
Instead of attempting to identify the transmitted codeword exactly, these measurements return an affine subspace of the local code
\mathcal{C}_{I}
that is guaranteed to contain the transmitted codeword, or else return an erasure outcome. This leads naturally to an optimization problem in which different affine subspaces may be assigned different rewards according to how much linear information they reveal.

Our goal is to characterize the optimal affine filtering measurement for a local code
\mathcal{C}
and its associated symmetric state family
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{I}}
.
In the remainder of section, we write

\mathcal{C}
for the local code
\mathcal{C}_{I}
to simplify notation.

Definition 3.1
.

An affine filtering measurement for a local code
\mathcal{C}
is a joint generalized measurement (i.e. POVM) on the quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
,
with one measurement outcome
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}
per affine subspace

V_{\mathbf{s}}
where

V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
and

\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
.
We denote this POVM by
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
.
For the unambiguity or filtering validity criterion, an
affine filtering measurement must satisfy

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\right)=0\quad\text{if $\mathbf{c}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}}$}

for all affine subspaces
V_{\mathbf{s}}\subset\mathcal{C}
with
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
.
Since each
V_{\mathbf{s}}
is a coset of
V
, the dimensions satisfy
\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V_{\mathbf{s}})=\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)
. Therefore, obtaining a measurement outcome
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}
corresponds to learning
\dim\mathcal{C}-\dim(V)
linear equations (i.e. parities). The measurement operator
\Pi_{e}
corresponds to the erasure outcome which provides no information about
\mathbf{c}
.

A conclusive affine filtering outcome need not identify the transmitted codeword uniquely; it only needs to identify an affine subspace of
\mathcal{C}
that contains it. This makes it natural to optimize over reward functions that depend on how much linear information the outcome reveals.
For each subspace
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
, let
\mathcal{R}(V)\geq 0
denote the reward function representing the contribution of
V
to the maximization objective. We assume the reward depends only on the linear subspace
V
itself, such that every affine subspace
V_{\mathbf{s}}
with
\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})

yields the same reward
\mathcal{R}(V)
. When we set
\mathcal{R}(V)=\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)
, the problem becomes maximizing the expected number of linear equations.
Also, observe that
codeword filtering is a special case of affine filtering in which every conclusive outcome corresponds to a singleton affine subspace, i.e.,
V=\{\mathbf{0}\}
, and hence
V_{\mathbf{s}}=\{\mathbf{c}\}
for some
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
.
Suppose we wish to find an
affine filtering measurement maximizing the expected reward based on the reward function
\mathcal{R}(.)
. This can be formulated as an SDP:

Maximize

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}})\mathcal{R}(V)

(4)

Subject to:

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\right)=0\quad\text{if $\mathbf{c}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}}$}

\displaystyle\ \sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}+\Pi_{e}=\mathbb{I}

(5)

\displaystyle\ \Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\succeq 0,\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})

\displaystyle\ \Pi_{e}\succeq 0.

For later use, and to state the symmetry reductions without repeatedly
rewriting the full SDP, we separate the objective from the feasible region.
For a POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
, define

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\})\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\,\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}})\mathcal{R}(V).

(6)

Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})
denote the feasible region of (
4
), i.e.,
the set of
affine filtering POVMs satisfying the unambiguity, completeness, and
positivity constraints displayed above. With this notation, the optimal SDP
value is

\displaystyle\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}))

(7)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\text{Max}_{\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})}f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}).

The SDP above is still high-dimensional, since it involves one POVM element
for each affine subspace of
\mathcal{C}
. The rest of this section reduces
this SDP to an equivalent LP by exploiting the symmetries of the state family.
First, we show that any feasible
affine filtering POVM can be symmetrized over
translations in
\mathcal{C}
, without changing the value of

f_{\mathrm{SDP}}
. This allows us to restrict attention to POVMs for which affine
cosets of the same subspace are related by certain translation operators

X^{\mathbf{w}}
with
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{C}
.

After this symmetrization, we aggregate the conclusive outcomes associated
with a fixed subspace
V
into the operator

\Pi_{V}
. The symmetry implies that
\Pi_{V}
commutes
with all translations by codewords in
\mathcal{C}
, and hence it is diagonal
in the character basis
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
. This converts the
positivity constraint for the erasure operator into scalar inequalities in
the corresponding diagonal coefficients.

It remains to handle the individual affine POVM elements

\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}
, since the unambiguity constraints still distinguish
the different affine cosets. We show that these operators can be restricted to
the codeword-state span
\mathcal{W}
, and then further to the subspace
compatible with the
affine filtering constraint. In the character basis, this forces

\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}
to decompose according to cosets of

\widehat{V}^{\perp}
. A final diagonalization in the corresponding

\ket{\zeta}
-basis leaves only scalar variables

\beta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})
.

Substituting this diagonal form into the objective and the erasure-positivity
constraints gives the LP. The proof therefore proceeds in two directions. First, any
optimal SDP solution can be symmetrized and diagonalized to produce a feasible
LP solution with the same value. Conversely, any feasible LP solution
constructs a valid
affine filtering POVM with the same objective value.
We begin by exploiting coset symmetry of the state family in the following section.

3.1
Reduced SDP using Coset Symmetry

In this section we obtain an SDP reduction using the translation symmetry of the state family
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
. We show that any feasible POVM can be symmetrized, without changing its objective value, so that POVM elements corresponding to affine cosets of the same subspace are related by conjugation under translation operators.

Lemma 3.2

(Coset Symmetry)
.

For any feasible POVM
\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})
there exists a feasible POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})

such that

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\})=f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}),

and for every
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
, every
\mathbf{s}_{1},\mathbf{s}_{2}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
, every
\mathbf{w}_{1}\in V_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}
, and every
\mathbf{w}_{2}\in V_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}
,

\displaystyle\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}_{2}}=X^{\mathbf{w}_{2}-\mathbf{w}_{1}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}_{1}}X^{-(\mathbf{w}_{2}-\mathbf{w}_{1})}.

In particular, if
\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}
is optimal, then
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
is also optimal.

Proof.

Let
\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})
be any feasible POVM, where

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}_{e}=\mathbb{I}-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}.

Consider the following transformation for a codeword
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}=X^{\mathbf{w}}\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}-H_{V}\mathbf{w}}X^{-\mathbf{w}}.

That is,
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\}_{V,\mathbf{s}}
is obtained from
\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}
by translating the codeword labels by
\mathbf{w}
and conjugating by the corresponding shift operator.
For this transformed POVM,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}})=\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}-H_{V}\mathbf{w}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{w}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{w}}}).

If
\mathbf{c}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}}
, then
\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{w}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}-H_{V}\mathbf{w}}
. Therefore, when
\mathbf{c}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}})=0.

Next, consider the operator
\Pi_{e}^{(\mathbf{w})}
associated with the erasure outcome after this transformation defined by

\displaystyle\Pi_{e}^{(\mathbf{w})}=\mathbb{I}-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}.

Since the matrix
\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}
is positive semidefinite (PSD), it follows immediately that
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}
is also
PSD
\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
. It also follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}

\displaystyle\ =X^{\mathbf{w}}\left(\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}-H_{V}\mathbf{w}}\right)X^{-\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle\ \preceq\mathbb{I}.

Hence, the operator
\Pi_{e}^{(\mathbf{w})}
is also PSD and
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}^{(\mathbf{w})}\}
is a valid POVM.
On the other hand,
(V_{\mathbf{s}},\mathbf{c})\rightarrow(V_{\mathbf{s}-H_{V}\mathbf{w}},\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{w})
is a bijection with
\dim(V_{\mathbf{s}-H_{V}\mathbf{w}})=\dim(V_{\mathbf{s}})

\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
. Thus the overall objective for an arbitrary codeword
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{C}
stays the same, i.e.,

\displaystyle\ f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}^{(\mathbf{w})}\})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}})\mathcal{R}(V)

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}-H_{V}\mathbf{w}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{w}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{w}}})\mathcal{R}(V)

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}})\mathcal{R}(V)

\displaystyle\ =f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}).

Consequently
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\cup\{\Pi_{e}^{(\mathbf{w})}\}
is feasible for the SDP in (
4
) and has the same objective value as
\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}
.
Now define the following symmetry transformation, where

\displaystyle\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{c})}.

For affine subspaces
V_{\mathbf{s}_{1}},V_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}\subset\mathcal{C}
and codewords
\mathbf{w}_{1}\in V_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}
,
\mathbf{w}_{2}\in V_{\mathbf{s}_{2}}
this gives

\displaystyle X^{\mathbf{w}_{2}-\mathbf{w}_{1}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}_{1}}X^{-(\mathbf{w}_{2}-\mathbf{w}_{1})}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}X^{\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{w}_{2}-\mathbf{w}_{1}}\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}_{1}-H_{V}\mathbf{c}}X^{-(\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{w}_{2}-\mathbf{w}_{1})}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}X^{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}_{1}-H_{V}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}_{2}+\mathbf{w}_{1})}X^{-\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}X^{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}_{1}+\mathbf{s}_{2}-\mathbf{s}_{1}-H_{V}\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}X^{-\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}X^{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}_{2}-H_{V}\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}X^{-\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\ =\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}_{2}}.

Since each
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}
is PSD
\forall\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{C}
, the operator
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}
is also a PSD
\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})

Defining the operator
\Pi_{e}=\mathbb{I}-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}
, we obtain
\Pi_{e}\succeq\mathbf{0}
, so that
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
is a valid POVM.
The overall objective becomes

\displaystyle\ f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}})\mathcal{R}(V)

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{C}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}})\mathcal{R}(V)\right)

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{C}}\left(f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}^{(\mathbf{w})}\})\right)

\displaystyle\ =f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{(\mathbf{w})}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}^{(\mathbf{w})}\})

\displaystyle\ =f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}).

Thus, starting from any feasible POVM
\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})
, we have constructed a feasible POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})
with

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\})=f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}),

that satisfies the stated coset symmetry relation. In particular, if
\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}
is optimal, then
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
is also optimal.
∎

For a fixed POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
, define the operator
\Pi_{V}
associated with the subspace
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
by

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}=\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}({H_{V}})}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}.

Next, we use the coset symmetry lemma, to prove that the operators
\Pi_{V}
are invariant under all transformations
X^{\mathbf{c}}
for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
and
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Consider a POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})
, which achieves the optimal objective value for SDP problem in (

\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}_{1}}
,

for

(8)

\forall\mathbf{w}_{1}\in V_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}

. Then the following equalities hold

\displaystyle\ =X^{\mathbf{c}}\Pi_{V}X^{-\mathbf{c}},\quad\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}.

(9)

Since

,

3.2
Restricting POVM to Subspace Spanned by Codeword Quantum States

In this section, we show that the informative POVM elements can be restricted
to the subspace spanned by the codeword quantum states without losing optimality.
Let
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\otimes|I|}
be the vector space associated with the codeword states for the local code
\mathcal{C}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=\text{span}\left(\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\right).

Let
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{W})=\{T:\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}\}
denote the space of linear maps in the complex vector space
\mathcal{W}
.

Lemma 3.4

(POVM Space)
.

There exists a POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
which achieves the optimal objective for the SDP problem in (

) such that
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{W})
.

Proof.

Let
\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}
be the POVM that achieves the objective described in (
4
). Let
P_{\mathcal{W}}
be the projection operator onto
\mathcal{W}
. Then

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{W}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}=\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}.

Let us define a POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}=P_{\mathcal{W}}\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}P_{\mathcal{W}},

and

\displaystyle\Pi_{e}\coloneqq\mathbb{I}-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}.

Therefore
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\succeq 0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}=P_{\mathcal{W}}(\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}})P_{\mathcal{W}}\preceq\mathbb{I},

which implies
\Pi_{e}\succeq\mathbf{0}
.
The trace constraints are also preserved:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}})

\displaystyle\ =\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}P_{\mathcal{W}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}P_{\mathcal{W}})

\displaystyle\ =\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}).

Therefore, if
\mathbf{c}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}}
,
\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}})=0
and the overall objective remains unchanged:

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\})=f_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\{\tilde{\Pi}_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\tilde{\Pi}_{e}\}).

Consequently, the POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
achieves the optimal objective while satisfying
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{W})

\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
.
∎

For an arbitrary affine subspace
V_{\mathbf{s}}\subset\mathcal{C}
, let
\sim V_{\mathbf{s}}=\mathcal{C}\setminus V_{\mathbf{s}}
and define the vector space

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\sim V_{\mathbf{s}}}=\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{C}\setminus V_{\mathbf{s}}}=\text{span}\left(\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus V_{\mathbf{s}}}\right).

Then we can write the vector space

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\sim V_{\mathbf{s}}}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{W}=\text{span}\left(\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\sim V_{\mathbf{s}}}\right)^{\perp}\cap\text{span}\left(\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\right).

Corollary 3.5
.

There exists a POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
that achieves the optimal objective value for the SDP problem in (
4
) such that
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{W}_{\sim V_{\mathbf{s}}}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{W})

\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
.

Proof.

The USD constraints imply that

\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}=0,\qquad\forall\mathbf{c}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}}.

Since
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\succeq\mathbf{0}
, this implies

\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}=\mathbf{0}
for all

\mathbf{c}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}}
. It follows that

\text{Im}(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}})\subseteq\mathcal{W}^{\perp}_{\sim V_{\mathbf{s}}}.

By Lemma
3.4
, we may furthermore take

\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{W})
. Combining the two facts gives

\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\in\mathcal{L}\bigl(\mathcal{W}^{\perp}_{\sim V_{\mathbf{s}}}\cap\mathcal{W}\bigr),

while preserving the same objective value.
∎

3.3
Diagonalization of the Operator
\Pi_{V}

In this section, we show that the operators
\{\Pi_{V}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}
are diagonal in a carefully constructed basis due to
invariance under the
X^{\mathbf{c}}
transformations
\forall\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
. For this, we use characters
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.
More specifically, for the collection of quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
and the character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, define the
unnormalized character vector by

\displaystyle\ket{\tilde{\chi}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi(\mathbf{c})\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}.

Lemma 3.6
.

For the character vectors
\{\ket{\tilde{\chi}}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
associated with the collection of quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
, the following holds

\displaystyle\braket{\tilde{\chi}_{1}|\tilde{\chi}_{2}}=\kappa_{\chi_{1}}\delta_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}},

(16)

where

\kappa_{\chi}\coloneqq\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}g(\mathbf{c})\chi(\mathbf{c}),

and
g(\mathbf{c})\coloneqq\braket{\psi_{\mathbf{0}}|\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}
.

Proof.

For the Gram matrix
G
for the collection of quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
, the following holds

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\ =\braket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}|\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\ =\bra{\psi}(\QFT^{\dagger})^{\otimes|I|}X^{-\mathbf{c}}X^{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}\QFT^{\otimes|I|}\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle\ =\bra{\psi}(\QFT^{\dagger})^{\otimes|I|}X^{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c}}\QFT^{\otimes|I|}\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=\braket{\psi_{\mathbf{0}}|\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c}}}

\displaystyle\ =g(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c}).

Therefore the Gram matrix element
G(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})
depends only on
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c}
.
In addition

\displaystyle\braket{\tilde{\chi}_{1}|\tilde{\chi}_{2}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\braket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}|\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})g(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{a})g(\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{C}}g(\mathbf{a})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{a})\left(\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c})\right).

The characters also satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c})=|\mathcal{C}|\delta_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}.

Therefore, this yields

\displaystyle\braket{\tilde{\chi}_{1}|\tilde{\chi}_{2}}=\kappa_{\chi_{1}}\delta_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}},

where we use the fact that
\kappa_{\chi_{1}}=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}g(\mathbf{c})\chi_{1}(\mathbf{c})
.
∎

We define the normalized character vectors

\ket{\chi}
as
\ket{\chi}=\frac{\ket{\tilde{\chi}}}{\sqrt{\kappa_{\chi}}}
whenever
\kappa_{\chi}>0
. When
G
is full rank, i.e., the states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
are linearly independent, this implies
\kappa_{\chi}>0
for all
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
and the normalized character vectors are well defined.

Lemma 3.7
.

Consider the collection of symmetric quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
, whose Gram matrix
G
is circulant, i.e.
G(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})
depends only on the difference
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c}
. Then
\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
are the eigenvalues of
G
.

Proof.

Using the canonical states
\{\ket{\mathbf{c}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
, define the vector
\{\ket{v_{\chi}}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
by

\displaystyle\ket{v_{\chi}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\ket{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}.

Let
G(\mathbf{c},:)
denote the row of the Gram matrix
G
indexed by codeword
\mathbf{c}
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{c},:)\ket{v_{\chi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}g(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c})\chi(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi(\mathbf{c})}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}g(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c})\chi(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi(\mathbf{c})}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|}}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{C}}g(\mathbf{a})\chi(\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\chi}\frac{\chi(\mathbf{c})}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|}}.

Therefore, the vector
\ket{v_{\chi}}
satisfies

\displaystyle G\ket{v_{\chi}}=\frac{\kappa_{\chi}}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi(\mathbf{c})\ket{\mathbf{c}}=\kappa_{\chi}\ket{v_{\chi}},\quad\forall\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}.

For two characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, from the character orthogonality relation it follows that

\displaystyle\braket{v_{\chi_{1}}|v_{\chi_{2}}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c})=\delta_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}.

Hence,
\{v_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
are the eigenvectors of the Gram matrix
G
with eigenvalues
\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
.
∎

Since the Gram matrix is PSD, the eigenvalues satisfy
\kappa_{\chi}\geq 0

\forall\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. For the remainder of this subsection, we assume the codeword quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
are linearly independent, which makes the Gram matrix positive definite and
\kappa_{\chi}>0

\forall\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. Next lemma shows that these eigenvalues are always bounded.

Lemma 3.8
.

For the collection of quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
, consider the Gram matrix
G
with eigenvalues
\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
. Then the following relation holds

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\kappa_{\chi}=|\mathcal{C}|.

Proof.

This follows directly from the trace relation

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\kappa_{\chi}=\mathrm{Tr}(G)=\sum_{\mathbf{c}}G(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c})=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\braket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}|\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}=|\mathcal{C}|.

∎

Next, we prove that the character states
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
span the space spanned by codeword states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For character vectors
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\ket{\chi}\bra{\chi}=P_{\mathcal{W}},

where
P_{\mathcal{W}}
is the projection operator onto the codeword state space
\mathcal{W}=\text{span}\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\}
.

Proof.

For
g(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{c})=\braket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}|\psi_{\mathbf{v}}}
and for the codeword
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{C}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\braket{\chi|\psi_{\mathbf{v}}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|\kappa_{\chi}}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\braket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}|\psi_{\mathbf{v}}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|\kappa_{\chi}}}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{C}}g(\mathbf{a})\chi^{-1}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\chi}}}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|}}\chi^{-1}(\mathbf{v}).

Then, for the state
\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{v}}}
for
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{C}
this yields

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{W}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{v}}}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\ket{\chi}\braket{\chi|\psi_{\mathbf{v}}}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{\chi}}}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|}}\chi^{-1}(\mathbf{v})\ket{\chi}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi^{-1}(\mathbf{v})\chi(\mathbf{c})\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}.

Since
\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\chi^{-1}(\mathbf{v})\chi(\mathbf{c})=|\mathcal{C}|\delta_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{c}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{W}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{v}}}=\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{v}}}.

The character vectors
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
also satisfy

\displaystyle\braket{\chi_{1}|\chi_{2}}=\delta_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}.

Since
\ket{\chi}
is defined with respect to the linear span of the codeword states
\{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
, for any vector
\ket{v}\in\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
this implies
\braket{\chi|v}=0
for all
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. Thus we conclude that
P_{\mathcal{W}}
is a projector onto the subspace
\mathcal{W}
.
∎

Based on the above lemmas, we are ready to state the diagonalization lemma for operators
\{\Pi_{V}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Consider the codeword states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
, the corresponding character vectors
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
, and an optimal POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
satisfying Lemma
3.2
. Then the operators
\{\Pi_{V}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}
are diagonal with respect to

\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
. In other words, we can write every operator
\{\Pi_{V}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}
as

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\gamma_{V}(\chi)\ket{\chi}\bra{\chi}.

Proof.

For the operator
\Pi_{V}=\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}
and for the codewords
\mathbf{c}
and
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
, using Lemma
3.3
, the following holds

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\Pi_{V}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\ =\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{0}}}X^{-\mathbf{c}}\Pi_{V}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}

(17)

\displaystyle\ =\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{0}}}\Pi_{V}X^{-\mathbf{c}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\ =\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{0}}}\Pi_{V}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c}}}.

This means
\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\Pi_{V}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}
only depends on
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c}
which we denote as
\mu_{V}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c})
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\bra{\chi_{1}}\Pi_{V}\ket{\chi_{2}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{\chi_{1}}\kappa_{\chi_{2}}}|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\Pi_{V}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{\chi_{1}}\kappa_{\chi_{2}}}|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\mu_{V}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{\chi_{1}}\kappa_{\chi_{2}}}|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{a})\mu_{V}(\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{\chi_{1}}\kappa_{\chi_{2}}}|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{C}}\mu_{V}(\mathbf{a})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{a})\left(\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{c})\right)

\displaystyle\ =\gamma_{V}(\chi_{1})\delta_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}},

where
\gamma_{V}(\chi_{1})=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\chi_{1}}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\mu_{V}(\mathbf{c})\chi_{1}(\mathbf{c})
.
∎

Since each operator
\Pi_{V}
is diagonal with respect to the basis
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
for all
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
, we obtain a simple characterization of the operator
\Pi_{e}
, i.e., the measurement operator for inconclusive outcome in the space spanned by the states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Consider the codeword states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
and the corresponding character vectors
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
, the POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
that achieves the optimal objective such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{e}=\mathbb{I}-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\Pi_{V}.

Then the operator
P_{\mathcal{W}}\Pi_{e}P_{\mathcal{W}}
is diagonalizable with respect to
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
.

Proof.

Applying
P_{\mathcal{W}}
on
\Pi_{e}
, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{W}}\Pi_{e}P_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\ =P_{\mathcal{W}}-P_{\mathcal{W}}\left(\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\Pi_{V}\right)P_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\ =P_{\mathcal{W}}-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\gamma_{V}(\chi)\ket{\chi}\bra{\chi}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}(1-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\gamma_{V}(\chi))\ket{\chi}\bra{\chi}.

∎

Since we require
\Pi_{e}\succeq 0
, this implies
P_{\mathcal{W}}\Pi_{e}P_{\mathcal{W}}\succeq 0
. Hence, we require the following to hold

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\gamma_{V}(\chi)\leq 1,\quad\forall\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}.

Moreover, since
\Pi_{V}\succeq 0

\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
, we must have

\displaystyle\gamma_{V}(\chi)\geq 0,\quad\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}.

Thus, by Lemma
3.10
and
3.11
, the problem in (
4
) reduces to the following equivalent formulation:

Maximize

\displaystyle\ \sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{V}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{0}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{0}}})\mathcal{R}(V)

(18)

Subject to:

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\right)=0,\quad\text{if $\mathbf{c}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}}$},\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})

\displaystyle\ \sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\gamma_{V}(\chi)\leq 1\qquad\forall\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}

(19)

\displaystyle\ \gamma_{V}(\chi)\geq 0,\quad\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}.

3.4
Diagonalization of the operator
\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}

Although the diagonalization of the operators
\{\Pi_{V}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}
reduces the SDP in (
4
) to a much simpler optimization problem, the problem still remains an SDP due to the unambiguity constraints i.e.
\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\bra{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\right)=0

\forall\mathbf{c}\notin V_{\mathbf{s}}

\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
. In this section, we will show that the operators
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}
are diagonalizable in a carefully constructed basis. In Lemma
3.12
, we establish a key property for these operators with respect to the basis
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
.

Lemma 3.12
.

There exists a POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
, which achieves the optimal objective value for the SDP in (
4
) such that the POVM elements satisfy

\displaystyle\bra{\chi_{1}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\chi_{2}}=0,\forall\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1}\notin\widehat{V}^{\perp}.

Proof.

For the unitary
X^{\mathbf{v}}
and the character state
\ket{\chi_{1}}
, the following holds

\displaystyle X^{\mathbf{v}}\ket{\chi_{1}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|\kappa_{\chi_{1}}}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}(\mathbf{c})X^{\mathbf{v}}\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|\kappa_{\chi_{1}}}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}(\mathbf{c})\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{v}}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|\kappa_{\chi_{1}}}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}-\mathbf{v})\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{\chi_{1}(-\mathbf{v})}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}|\kappa_{\chi_{1}}}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{1}(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\ =\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{v})\ket{\chi_{1}}.

Then, for some
\mathbf{v}\in V
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bra{\chi_{1}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\chi_{2}}

\displaystyle\ =\bra{\chi_{1}}X^{\mathbf{v}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}X^{-\mathbf{v}}\ket{\chi_{2}}

\displaystyle\ =\chi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbf{v})\chi_{2}(\mathbf{v})(\bra{\chi_{1}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\chi_{2}})

\displaystyle\ =(\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1})(\mathbf{v})(\bra{\chi_{1}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\chi_{2}}).

This gives

\displaystyle\left((\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1})(\mathbf{v})-1\right)\bra{\chi_{1}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\chi_{2}}=0.

Since
\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1}\notin\widehat{V}^{\perp}
, there exists a codeword
\mathbf{v}\in V
for which
(\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1})(\mathbf{v})\neq 1
. Then, we must have
\bra{\chi_{1}}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\ket{\chi_{2}}=0
for all
\chi_{2},\chi_{1}
such that
\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1}\notin\widehat{V}^{\perp}
.
∎

We now use Lemma
3.12
to diagonalize the operators
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}
. This lemma will play a key role for our LP reduction proof which we discuss in more detail in Section
3.5
.
For the remainder of this subsection, we assume that the Gram matrix
G
is full rank, so that

\kappa_{\chi}>0
for all
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
and the normalized character basis is well defined.
The rank-deficient case is handled separately in Section
3.8
.

Lemma 3.13
.

There exists a POVM
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}\}
, which achieves the optimal objective value for the SDP in (
4
) such that the POVM elements admit the following diagonal form

\displaystyle\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}=\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\beta_{V,\mathbf{s}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\ket{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}}\bra{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}},

where
\beta_{V,\mathbf{s}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\geq 0

\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V}),\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}

and the orthonormal vector
\ket{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}}
associated with the coset

\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}
is represented
in the character basis by

\displaystyle\ket{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}\frac{\chi(-\mathbf{a})}{\sqrt{\kappa_{\chi}}}\ket{\chi},

(20)

for some codeword
\mathbf{a}\in V_{\mathbf{s}}
and
\eta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}\frac{1}{\kappa_{\chi}}
.

Proof.

For
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V}),\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}
and a codeword
\mathbf{a}\in V_{\mathbf{s}}
, define the vector

\displaystyle\ket{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m},\mathbf{a}}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}\frac{\chi(-\mathbf{a})}{\sqrt{\kappa_{\chi}}}\ket{\chi}.

(21)

Consider
\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\in V_{\mathbf{s}}
and
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}
with
\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
and
\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}
such that
\mathbf{a}_{2}-\mathbf{a}_{1}=\mathbf{v}\in V
and
\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1}=\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}
.
Then the characters satisfy

3.5
Obtaining the LP from SDP

Using the diagonal form of the operators
\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}
, we now reduce the SDP in (
4
) to an LP.
Consider scalars
\beta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})
defined for each

\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}

(31)

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}))

\displaystyle\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}))\leq\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})).

Conversely, given any feasible LP variables
\{\beta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V,\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})
, define

\displaystyle\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}:=\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\beta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\ket{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}}\bra{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}},

for each
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})
, and let

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}:=\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}},\qquad\Pi_{e}:=\mathbb{I}-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\Pi_{V}.

Since
\beta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\geq 0
, each operator

\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}
is PSD. By
Lemma
3.13
, every vector

\ket{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}}
lies in

\mathcal{W}^{\perp}_{\sim V_{\mathbf{s}}}\cap\mathcal{W}
, so the
affine filtering
unambiguity constraints hold. Moreover, the LP feasibility constraint is exactly the
condition from Lemma
3.11
guaranteeing

P_{\mathcal{W}}\Pi_{e}P_{\mathcal{W}}\succeq\mathbf{0}.

Since all informative POVM elements act on
\mathcal{W}
, the operator
\Pi_{e}

acts as the identity on
\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
. Hence
\Pi_{e}\succeq\mathbf{0}
.
These operators therefore form a feasible POVM attaining the LP objective, which proves

\displaystyle\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}))\leq\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}})).

Combining the two inequalities proves the theorem.
Consequently the SDP optimization problem stated in (
4
) can be converted into an LP which is formally stated below.

Final LP Form

For a local code
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{F}^{|I|}_{q}
supported on the index set
I

\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}

for the affine filtering measurement defined in Def

\{\beta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V,\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}

Maximize

\displaystyle\ \sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\beta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\frac{q^{2(\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V))}}{\eta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})|\mathcal{C}|}\mathcal{R}(V)

(49)

Subject to:

Maximize

\displaystyle\ \sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\frac{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)}}{|\mathcal{C}|}\mathcal{R}(V)

\displaystyle\ y_{\chi}\geq 0,\quad\forall\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}

(59)

where

3.7
Optimal Objective for Two Level Gram Matrix Eigenvalue Spectrum

In this section, we show that if the Gram matrix for the collection of quantum states

\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
has a specific two-level eigenvalue spectrum

\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
such that

\kappa_{\chi_{\text{triv}}}=\kappa_{\text{max}}
and

\kappa_{\chi}=\kappa_{\text{min}}
for all
\chi\neq\chi_{\text{triv}}
,
and if we fix

\mathcal{R}(V)=\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)
,
then the LP objective, namely the optimal expected number of equations, becomes directly computable.
Moreover, this eigenvalue spectrum is directly related to the choice of quantum states in the local quantum decoding based optimization framework of
[
32
]
.
In Lemma
3.15
, we rigorously prove the closed-form expression of the affine filtering objective for this specific eigenvalue spectrum of the Gram matrix
G
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Consider the collection of quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
, such that
\kappa_{\chi_{\text{triv}}}=\kappa_{\text{max}}
and
\kappa_{\chi}=\kappa_{\text{min}}

\forall\chi\neq\chi_{\text{triv}}
, with
\kappa_{\text{max}}>\kappa_{\text{min}}
and
\mathcal{R}(V)=\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)
. Then the following holds

\displaystyle\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}))=\kappa_{\text{min}}\dim(\mathcal{C}).

Proof.

Since
\kappa_{\chi}=\kappa_{\text{min}}

\forall\chi\neq\chi_{\text{triv}}
, summing constraints over all
\chi\neq\chi_{\text{triv}}
on the LP in (
51
), this yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{\text{triv}}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}:\chi\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\leq\kappa_{\text{min}}(|\mathcal{C}|-1).

For subspace
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
and
\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}
, let
\xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})
be the number of nontrivial characters (i.e.
\chi\neq\chi_{\text{triv}}
) in the coset
\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\leq\kappa_{\text{min}}(|\mathcal{C}|-1).

Define the quantity
\Xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})
as

\displaystyle\Xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})=\frac{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)}\mathcal{R}(V)}{|\mathcal{C}|\xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})}.

Consider the subspace
V=\{\mathbf{0}\}
, in this case
\widehat{V}^{\perp}=\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}=\{\widehat{V}^{\perp}\}
. It also satisfies

\displaystyle\Xi_{V=\{\mathbf{0}\}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})}\dim(\mathcal{C})}{|\mathcal{C}|(|\mathcal{C}|-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{|\mathcal{C}|-1}.

For any other subspace
V\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}
, if
\chi_{\text{triv}}\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)}(\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V))}{|\mathcal{C}|(|\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}|-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)}(\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V))}{|\mathcal{C}|(q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)}-1)}.

For subspace
V\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}
, if
\chi_{\text{triv}}\notin\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}
, then

\displaystyle\Xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)}(\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V))}{|\mathcal{C}||\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)}{|\mathcal{C}|}.

Therefore,
\Xi_{V=\{\mathbf{0}\}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp})\geq\Xi_{V\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})
. Writing the LP objective in terms of
\Xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})
we obtain

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{LP}}(\{\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)}\mathcal{R}(V)

\displaystyle=\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\Xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})

\displaystyle=\theta_{V=\{\mathbf{0}\}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp})\Xi_{V=\{\mathbf{0}\}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp})\xi_{V=\{\mathbf{0}\}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp})+\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}:V\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\Xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})

\displaystyle\leq\Xi_{V=\{\mathbf{0}\}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp})\left(\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\xi_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{|\mathcal{C}|-1}\kappa_{\text{min}}(|\mathcal{C}|-1)=\kappa_{\text{min}}\dim(\mathcal{C}).

This is achieved by setting
\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})=0

\forall V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
such that
V\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}
and
\theta_{V=\{\mathbf{0}\}}(\widehat{V}^{\perp})=\kappa_{\text{min}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.16
.

For the collection of quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
, such that

\kappa_{\chi_{\text{triv}}}=\kappa_{\text{max}}
and
\kappa_{\chi}=\kappa_{\text{min}}
for all
\chi\neq\chi_{\text{triv}}
,
with
\kappa_{\text{max}}>\kappa_{\text{min}}
and

\mathcal{R}(V)=\dim(\mathcal{C})-\dim(V)
, an optimal
affine filtering measurement is a
codeword filtering measurement from Definition
2.5
. Equivalently, one optimal measurement is

\displaystyle\left\{\Pi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathrm{Cw\mbox{-}Filt}}\right\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\cup\left\{\Pi_{e}^{\mathrm{Cw\mbox{-}Filt}}\right\},

which outputs the transmitted codeword
\mathbf{c}
or an erasure.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.15
, the LP optimum is achieved by setting

\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})=0
for all
V\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}
and concentrating all mass on

V=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. In this case every conclusive affine outcome is a singleton affine subspace

\{\mathbf{c}\}
, so the resulting affine filtering measurement coincides with the
codeword filtering measurement from
Definition
2.5
. Hence the
codeword filtering is optimal among affine filtering measurements for this two level Gram matrix eigenvalue spectrum.
∎

Since the FGUM in
[
32
]
has conclusive outcomes that identify the transmitted local SPC codeword exactly, with the remaining outcome treated as an erasure, it is a
codeword filtering measurement for the SPC-associated state family considered above. Therefore, for the same choice of quantum states, the above corollary shows that FGUM is an optimal
affine filtering measurement for maximizing the expected number of recovered linear equations.

3.8
Limiting LP for Full-Rank Perturbations of Rank-Deficient Gram Matrices

So far, we have shown that if the Gram matrix is full rank, or equivalently if the quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
are linearly independent, then we can reduce the SDP for constructing optimal
affine filtering measurement to an LP.
In this section, we provide a limiting LP formulation associated with rank-deficient Gram matrices.
We perturb the state family so that the corresponding Gram matrix becomes full rank,
apply the LP characterization from the previous section, and then pass to the limit.
This gives a limiting LP characterization of the values of the full-rank perturbed Gram matrices.
For
\epsilon>0
, define a perturbed seed state

\ket{\psi^{\epsilon}}=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{|I|}}\alpha_{\mathbf{x}}^{\epsilon}\ket{\mathbf{x}},\qquad\alpha_{\mathbf{x}}^{\epsilon}:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\alpha_{\mathbf{x}}}{|\alpha_{\mathbf{x}}|}\sqrt{\dfrac{|\alpha_{\mathbf{x}}|^{2}+\epsilon/q^{|I|}}{1+\epsilon}},&\alpha_{\mathbf{x}}\neq 0,\\[8.61108pt]
\sqrt{\dfrac{\epsilon/q^{|I|}}{1+\epsilon}},&\alpha_{\mathbf{x}}=0.\end{cases}

and let

\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}}=X^{\mathbf{c}}\QFT^{\otimes|I|}\ket{\psi^{\epsilon}},\qquad\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}.

Lemma 3.17
.

Consider the collection of quantum states

\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
with rank-deficient Gram matrix

G
characterized by eigenvalues
\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
.
For
\epsilon>0
, let
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}

be the perturbed state family defined above. Its Gram matrix is

G_{\epsilon}=\frac{G+\epsilon\mathbb{I}}{1+\epsilon},

with eigenvalues

\kappa_{\chi}^{\epsilon}=\frac{\kappa_{\chi}+\epsilon}{1+\epsilon},\qquad\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}.

For every
\epsilon>0
, the perturbed state family has full-rank Gram
matrix. Hence, by Theorem
3.14
, the
full-rank
affine filtering measurement optimization reduces to

\text{LP}\Big(\{\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}},\{\kappa_{\chi}^{\epsilon}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\Big),

and

\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}))=\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}\Big(\{\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}},\{\kappa_{\chi}^{\epsilon}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\Big).

As
\epsilon\to 0
, these LPs converge to

\text{LP}\Big(\{\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}},\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\Big),

and their optimal values satisfy

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}\Big(\{\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}},\{\kappa_{\chi}^{\epsilon}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\Big)=\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}\Big(\{\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}},\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\Big).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}\Big(\{\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}},\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\Big).

In particular, if
\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}
contains a character
\chi
such that

\kappa_{\chi}=0
, then every feasible solution of the limiting LP satisfies

\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})=0

for all
V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

Consider the LP in (
51
)
\text{LP}\Big(\{\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}},\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}},\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\Big)
where the right hand side values
\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
corresponds to the eigenvalues of rank-deficient Gram matrix
G
. If

\kappa_{\chi}=0
for some
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, then the corresponding constraint
in (
51
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}:\chi\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\leq 0.

Since
\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\geq 0
for all

V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}}
and

\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\theta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})=0,

\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}

\chi

\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}

\kappa_{\chi}>0
for all
\chi\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}
.
For perturbed states
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle\braket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}|\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}^{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{|I|}}\frac{|\alpha_{\mathbf{x}}|^{2}+\epsilon/q^{|I|}}{1+\epsilon}\omega^{\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\rangle}

\braket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}|\psi_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}^{\epsilon}}=\frac{G_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}+\epsilon\delta_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}}{1+\epsilon}.

\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}

is diagonalized by the character basis,

\{\kappa_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}

In particular,

For subspace
V

\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\eta_{V}^{\epsilon}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}\frac{1}{\kappa_{\chi}^{\epsilon}}.

.

G_{\epsilon}
is full rank, using Theorem
3.14
we reduce the SDP in (
4
) for perturbed quantum sates
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}
to an LP. Therefore, from (

), we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{SDP}}(\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{C},\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}^{\epsilon}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}))

4
Affine Filtering Measurement Based Decoding

The previous section characterizes
affine filtering measurements for a fixed local code and a corresponding collection of symmetric codeword states. We now specialize
this construction to local codes arising from SPC constraints in a regular LDPC
code and describe the resulting decoding procedure. The role of the local
measurement is to convert each conclusive outcome into linear constraints on a
small subset of code symbols. These locally generated constraints are then used
together with the global parity-check equations to determine the transmitted
codeword.

4.1
Decoding on I.I.D. States for Single Parity Check Codes

In Section
3
, we described
affine filtering measurements for an arbitrary local code
\mathcal{C}_{I}
supported on an index set
I
. In this section, we specialize that framework to single-parity-check (SPC) local codes and the corresponding
affine filtering decoder for LDPC codes on i.i.d. pure-state channels. Let
\{\beta_{V}^{*}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}_{I}},\,\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}
denote an optimal solution of the LP in (
49
). Then the corresponding POVM

\displaystyle\{\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{*}\}_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}_{I}},\,\mathbf{s}\in\text{Im}(H_{V})}\cup\{\Pi_{e}^{*}\}

(66)

with

\displaystyle\Pi_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\beta_{V}^{*}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\ket{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}}\bra{\zeta_{V,\mathbf{s}}^{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}}},

(67)

\displaystyle\Pi_{e}^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}-\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}_{I}}}\Pi_{V}^{*},

(68)

gives the optimal affine information for a degree-
D
SPC local code. We now consider an i.i.d. pure-state channel in which each symbol is transmitted through the same pure-state channel. Fix
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
and a non-empty subset
S\subset[q]
, define

\displaystyle\ket{\psi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{\alpha}\sum_{i\in S}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|S|}}\ket{i}+\sqrt{1-\alpha}\sum_{j\in[q]\setminus S}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q-|S|}}\ket{j}.

(69)

For each input symbol
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the channel output is the pure state obtained from the same seed by the action of
X^{a}\QFT
. Since the channel is i.i.d., the output corresponding to a local codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{I}
is the tensor product of the single-symbol outputs, and hence is given by

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}

\displaystyle=X^{\mathbf{c}}\QFT^{\otimes|I|}\ket{\psi^{\prime}}^{\otimes|I|}.

(70)

Therefore the family
\{\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}\}_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{I}}
is exactly of the form considered in Section
3
, and the LP in (
49
) applies directly to this local SPC setting. Our goal in this section is to characterize the affine information revealed by the optimal SPC POVM for this family of states as a function of
\alpha
and
S
. For a fixed
(k,D)
pair and a fixed subset
S\subset[q]
, let

\alpha_{(k,D)}^{\mathrm{Affine\text{-}Filtering}}

denote the parameter value for which the LP objective equals
D-k
, namely

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{C}_{I}}}\sum_{\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}/\widehat{V}^{\perp}}\beta_{V}^{*}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})\frac{q^{2(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{I})-\dim(V))}}{\eta_{V}(\widehat{V}^{\perp}_{m})|\mathcal{C}_{I}|}\mathcal{R}(V)

\displaystyle=D-k.

(71)

Now consider the quantum decoder which, upon receiving the state
\ket{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}}
for a codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
, applies the
affine filtering POVM to disjoint SPC constraints and then runs Gaussian elimination to recover
\mathbf{c}
. For a random
(k,D)
-regular LDPC code, dependencies among parity checks may reduce the decoding threshold below

\alpha_{(k,D)}^{\mathrm{Affine\text{-}Filtering}}
.
Moreover, it is not always possible to find disjoint SPC supports
\{I_{j}\}_{j\in[J]}
such that
\bigsqcup_{j\in[J]}I_{j}=[N]
, where
N
is the code length. Accordingly, the value

\alpha_{(k,D)}^{\mathrm{Affine\text{-}Filtering}}

obtained from the LP gives an upper bound on the performance of the
affine-filtering+GE decoder.

Recent works on BPQM
[
1
,
25
]
derive thresholds for
(k,D)
-regular LDPC codes using density-evolution analysis. A basic limitation of this approach is that it relies on the assumption that the neighborhood of a root node in the factor graph induced by a random
(k,D)
-regular LDPC code is tree-like up to sufficiently large depth. Moreover, it is not clear how to construct a practical BPQM decoder for LDPC codes with comparable complexity guarantees. Nevertheless, the threshold obtained from density evolution provides an upper bound on the performance of a BPQM-based quantum decoder.

We now explain how each step of Algorithm

contributes linear information about the transmitted codeword and how this information is assembled into a global system of equations. The decoder begins with the parity-check equations of the LDPC code itself, and initializes

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{update}}=H,\qquad\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0}.

(72)

Throughout the decoding procedure, every additional conclusive measurement outcome contributes further linear constraints that are appended to this system.

I_{j}

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{update}}\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{b}.

(78)

6
Conclusion

We studied affine filtering measurements, a structured class of unambiguous
measurements whose conclusive outcomes filter affine subspaces containing the transmitted codeword of a linear code. For symmetric codeword indexed states, we showed that the optimal affine filtering design
problem reduces from an SDP to an LP, enabling tractable computation of optimal
measurements for small local codes. Using these measurements, we proposed a
quantum decoding framework for pure state channels and demonstrated that
applying affine filtering to qudit states associated with SPC neighborhoods of
regular LDPC codes can improve decoding thresholds relative to qudit-wise USD
and qudit-wise PGM based decoders.
