 ABSTRACT

In FY 2026, Oak Ridge National Laboratory, the Y-12 National Security Complex, Sandia National
Laboratories, and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory started the development of a dashboard that
will provide to the Advanced Reactors International Safeguards Engagement (ARISE) team in National
Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) Headquarters and laboratories a common information platform
to inform and evaluate the relationships of the program with different advanced reactor companies in the
United States. The focus of FY 2026 was to develop a pilot demonstration and the vision of how open-
source data would be collected and processes to be displayed in the dashboard. As part of the initial pilot,
the data scraping approach was developed, and it is expected to be refined and expanded in FY 2027 when
the dashboard is deployed. Update automation and confidence intervals on the data gathered have been
initially integrated into the scraping approach. The development of the dashboard will continue in FY 2027
with scaling and populating of all aspects of the mockup and with iterations and feedback from NNSA
Headquarters.

 1. INTRODUCTION AND OBJECTIVES OF THE ARISE OUTREACH DASHBOARD

The Advanced Reactors International Safeguards Engagement (ARISE) Outreach Dashboard is being
developed as a centralized, user-friendly database to host current and reliable information on advanced
reactor developers and their partners in the nuclear fuel supply chain, including reactor design information,
deployment strategies, licensing progress, and key relationships. Currently, this information is spread across
various sources, including company websites, news sources, licensing documentation, conference
proceedings, and presentations, making it difficult for those in the safeguards community to stay informed.
By bringing these data streams into a single, updated, and easily accessible interface, the dashboard will
provide a scalable capability that supports more efficient mission execution and longer-term value to the
ARISE team to prioritize and plan outreach.

The project objectives for FY 2026 were the following:

 • Design and develop the initial dashboard framework.
 • Define and validate practical approaches for data acquisition and integration.
 • Conduct a limited proof-of-concept demonstration using an example vendor informed by
 Technology Readiness and Challenges for Safeguards (TRACS) analysis and selected open-source
 repositories.
 • Obtain and incorporate ARISE Headquarters (HQ) feedback to identify priority information needs
 and to determine which data-gathering approaches should be emphasized.

1.1 PROGRESS OVERVIEW

1.1.1 Initial Data Selection and Validation

In April, project subject matter experts (SMEs) gathered at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) to
establish the data requirements for each phase of the ARISE engagement strategy. First, the group addressed
the data that matter for industry engagement and for defining the indicators (“values”) most relevant to the
dashboard. These discussions produced a list of core technical and business variables to be tracked. For
companies that are early on the ARISE engagement continuum, these variables rely primarily on open-
source research; for companies with a more mature engagement, variables involve deeper technical data,
information from emails, and other “nonstandard” sources that would require a particular workflow to be
incorporated into the dashboard.

 1

Overall, 41 values1 were considered relevant to track; they cover vendor information; reactor and fuel
characteristics; material control and accounting (MC&A) plans, if available; siting; deployment; licensing
metrics; partnerships; and ARISE engagement relationships. Given that the dashboard absorbs multiple
sources of information, some open-source and others customized by industry engagement and relationship
with the company, there was also a need to develop a hierarchy of source reliability.

The source acquisition decision making must balance primary sources that provide the highest accuracy
(e.g., company press releases and announcements and investor reports), and other sources that are more
easily automated, such as open sources. At some points, it is envisioned that there will be conflicting data
that will need to be deconflicted by an analyst. Following the successful transition to Power BI as the
dashboard platform, the data collection approach was unified. Additionally, a confidence measurement was
integrated for acquired data, alongside instructions for the model to prioritize specific sources. For specific
data that require processing because they are part of a scale and do not need only to be displayed, we
developed methodologies that will be incorporated into the model. The provided methodologies will guide
the processing and classification of data that will be displayed in the dashboard.

1.2 Dashboard Mockup Development

The dashboard was built to centralize data within a user-friendly interface that provides information to
members across all the ARISE team at labs/plants/sites (e.g., points of contact, project managers, principal
investigators, subject matter experts) and ARISE HQ. The mockup, included in section 5, has a landing
page, industry search filters, company profiles, design and fuel characteristics, licensing, relationships,
engagement history, a calendar, a repository, and user support. As part of the planned workflows, access
will be granted via Power BI invitations to registered individuals. The dashboard is not intended to be a
publicly accessible platform; access control will rely on a manual approval process. Additionally, the
dashboard features a feedback form for recommendations regarding displayed metrics or new
visualizations, as well as a data submission form allowing users to submit relevant information for inclusion
on the dashboard.

 2. DATA SOURCES AND ARCHITECTURE

2.1 Why Power BI?

As the ARISE dashboard will be fusing data from different sources and organizing information for display
to assist ARISE team communication regarding engagement status and history, ARISE HQ requires a
dashboard interface that encourages the complex-wide team to use it through optimized usability. The
objective is to facilitate cross-complex information sharing and the identification of engagement
opportunities
Power BI was chosen as the primary dashboard and reporting platform owing to its strong alignment with
the project objective needs, reduced development overhead, and ability to scale up. Compared with
alternative commercial and open-source solutions,2 Power BI provides a balanced combination of cost
efficiency, advanced capabilities, and integration with existing organizational tools. Moreover, Power BI
plugs directly into Excel, SharePoint, Teams, Azure SQL, Synapse, Fabric, and OneDrive. This tight
integration reduces engineering effort, lowers friction for adoption, and makes automation simpler.
In contrast, open-source tools often require custom connectors, application programming interface (API)
work, or separate authentication stacks. Also, Power BI licensing can be more affordable than enterprise
licenses for Tableau or Qlik and often is less expensive than maintaining custom infrastructure for open-

2.2 Data Types

To facilitate the data acquisition and revision processes, the team classified the data based on their
characteristics and focused on their update periodicity. This initial classification was developed with the
objective of finding the best approach to periodically refresh the different sections of the dashboard. The
classifications are the following:

 • Static data: Information regarding the reactor or fuel characteristics could be considered static
 data. These entrees likely either will not change or will have major adjustments that would need
 periodic revisions that could be made manually.
 • Metric data: Data will need not only to be acquired but also to be analyzed by the artificial
 intelligence (AI) agent to enable meaningful output in the dashboard. Individual metrics were
 created for each field that requires one unique approach in the dashboard. These metrics will
 provide guidance for the AI agent to acquire and process the required data.

2.3 Workflows and Data Automation

Interest in advanced reactors continues to grow, as reflected by the large span of vendors within the United
States. Tracking the developments of these companies is a nontrivial task that could take significant human
effort. We opted to implement AI-driven automation to augment the ARISE Dashboard to crawl the open
web to find news and updates on these companies.

We developed a platform-agnostic workflow underpinned by open-source projects and compatible with a
generic OpenAI-compliant API. This allows us to deploy an AI-driven automation pipeline to keep the
ARISE dashboard updated with open information while utilizing on-premises computing resources at
ORNL. Specifically, we use docker compose to deploy degoog for search, crawl4ai for page fetching, and
leverage Google’s Agent Development Kit (ADK) to perform AI-related tasks.

The automation pipeline relies on a regular execution cadence. When executed, an AI agent is provided
instructions for writing a JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) file containing dashboard information related
to specific companies and facilities. The output is defined by a schema to ensure the agent produces
structured output with confidence levels and comprehensive coverage across data categories. degoog is
used to search the web using a variety of backend APIs (e.g., DuckDuckGo, Google, Brave) to find relevant
webpages and summary snippets. crawl4ai is then used to fetch relevant webpages requested by the agent
and convert the content to a format compatible with the agent. Finally, the agent uses retrieved data to
produce the output file read and displayed by the dashboard.

 3. FY 2027 AND FUTURE OUTLOOK

3.1 FEEDBACK FROM HQ ON INITIAL MOCKUP

The following feedback was collected during the ARISE Dashboard presentation to HQ.

3.1.1 Access Control to the Platform

As part of the process to gain access to the dashboard, a registration form would need to be completed. The
form will require the requestor to provide email, name, laboratory, and fields that will probably make the
entry of nonauthorized personnel highly unlikely. Once the registration form is completed, ORNL will
manually grant access to the registered individuals. After being granted access to the dashboard, a user will
need to use the approved credentials to access to the ORNL platform. It will not be possible for a user who
is granted access to enter the dashboard without the account that was initially admitted for usage. This is
part of the workflow used to grant external users access to the ORNL platform hosting the dashboard Power
BI report.

3.1.2 What Types of Events Will Be Listed on the Calendar?

The types of events to be listed will be the prerogative of the main client, ARISE HQ personnel. However,
we envision the calendar including not only conferences relevant to international safeguards but also
laboratory training on safeguards-relevant topics. This approach will require a level of coordination
supported and encouraged by HQ for those instances where information is not provided to ORNL for
ingestion and display. This calendar could also have the potential to promote events from other 241
programs or other NNSA offices.

 5

3.1.3 Will Information Be Populated through AI Scraping of Information?

While AI can be utilized to assist with data entry, we have controls and structured approaches in place to
manage exactly how that information is populated. At the beginning of the fiscal year, there was a mixed
approach to data acquisition, in which we considered pulling information from open sources and from
ARISE documents and information. Currently, our approach is to collect information from open sources,
but the expectation is also to integrate the information that can be collected from ARISE internal documents.

3.1.4 Classification, Export Control, Copyrighted and Proprietary Data

All information collected for the dashboard is pulled from open sources. There is a chance that the
dashboard may pull from information that contains copyrighted, export-controlled, proprietary, or classified
data. There was an internal discussion with relevant parties at ORNL in which it was explained that a
situation in which someone knowingly provides a document containing information that should not be made
available is different from a situation in which a document is found and used online. The main export-
control concern arises from accessing the LLM prompts; however, users will not access these prompts, but
only the products from such prompts. Additional analysis related to these potential issues will be explored
in future phases of the project.

The dashboard envisions eventual coordination with the ARISE TRACS process. At a certain point in the
TRACS process, documents may contain information gathered from interviews and documents that could
be considered confidential. Coordination will be provided upfront with the PI for TRACS to ensure that
information of that nature is excluded from the ingestion dashboard process.

3.2 DEVELOPMENT IN FY 2027 AND ONWARD

3.2.1 Expanding Vendor Coverage

Which vendors to include will be determined in meetings with the sponsor at the beginning of FY 2027.
ORNL, in coordination with the other laboratories that are part of the effort, can provide an initial
assessment based on maturity; and the ARISE engagement strategy3 will be conducted by ORNL to provide
initial input for this discussion.

3.2.2 Enhanced Data Integration

The project team will continue to incorporate additional structured and unstructured datasets and continue
to synergize with ARISE-gathered data input streams. For example, TRACS documents already present a
standardized questionnaire and template format. Adjustments in how information is captured through
TRACS reporting and how it can be stored and deposited into structured data sources (e.g., tables,
submission forms) could improve the scalability of both manual and automated data integration workflows.
Part of the synergy to be developed with TRACS will be to tag and deposit information identified through
TRACS that can be relevant and ingested for the dashboard.
The dashboard envisions a specific workflow that will allow SMEs to provide feedback on information that
they notice is not up to date or that has recently been collected during an event. The SMEs’ “on the go”
input needs to be considered via a method that is easy to implement, does not create additional burden on
contributors, is relevant to the ARISE community and appropriate for the dashboard, and can be naturally
integrated into ARISE team culture and workflows.

3.2.3 Continuous Development

One of the main tasks for FY 2026 was to explore and execute how to develop a data acquisition scheme
that would refresh automatically. The initial process was developed; and future work will include improving
automation of workflows, dashboard functionality, and visualization, with input gathered from HQ, user
feedback, and usability testing. We expect to have a major development effort in future fiscal years with
significant increases in functionality and automation.

3.2.4 Expansion and Refinement of Data Streams and Automation

The continued development of the dashboard will need to expand both structured and unstructured data
streams and the automated data collection process that was developed in FY 2026. FY 2027 activities will
scale the data collection and process pipeline to more companies while maintaining reliability. To expand
the data collection process, standardized data inputs from structured templates and TRACS documents will
be used in combination with open-source repositories. As new vendors are added, there will be more output
schemas for each vendor to make data extraction straightforward for the dashboard. Enhancements to the
data schema will improve the AI-driven search tool’s ability to find and extract the correct data for each
data field. Together, these refinements will support a more scalable, reliable, and adaptive workflow as the
dashboard expands to new vendors.

3.2.5 Data Completeness and Confidence

In FY 2027, the dashboard will include methods to identify gaps in vendor metrics so updates to the
dashboard can be focused on including information that is missing or outdated. The completeness checks
will ensure that automated and SME inputs contribute to a more complete dataset for the dashboard. The
AI agent will generate a confidence scoring method for input during the data ingestion process which uses
source reliability and consistency within the structured output schema. When the AI agent identifies
conflicting information, the fields will be flagged for SME review, and corrected information will be
provided. This hybrid workflow will help to preserve the integrity of the data.
