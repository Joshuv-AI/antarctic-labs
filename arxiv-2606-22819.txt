Connectivity for slice-projections of connected polymatroids
Xiaxia Guan a , Xian’an Jin b,c a Department of Mathematics, Taiyuan University of Technology, P. R. China b School of Mathematical Sciences, Xiamen University, P. R. China c School of Mathematics and Statistics, Qinghai Minzu University, P. R. China Email addresses : guanxiaxia@tyut.edu.cn; xajin@xmu.edu.cn

Abstract
It is well-known that deleting or contracting any element of a connected matroid always yields at least one connected minor. However, for a connected polymatroid, only two such elements can be guaranteed, proved by Hall in 2013. This note investigates the connectivity properties of slice-projections of connected polymatroids, which includes deletion and contraction. We establish that for any element of a connected polymatroid, at least one of its two consecutive slice-projections is connected. We also obtain that the j j -th slice-projection from the top of a polymatroid and j j -th slice-projection from the bottom of its dual have the same connectedness. These results both extend existing connectedness theorems for graphs and matroids.

Introduction

Polymatroids constitute a profound generalization of matroids. They play an indispensable role in combinatorial optimization, matroid theory, and algebraic combinatorics. Connectivity, as a fundamental structural invariant, quantifies the indecomposability of a combinatorial object, while connectivity-preserving operations lie at the heart of structural decomposition and minor theory.

A classical theorem of Tutte
[
8
]
asserts that for any element
e
of a connected matroid
M
, at least one of
M-e
or
M/e
remains connected. Remarkably, this result fails to extend to the setting of polymatroids. In 2013, Hall
[
3
]
proved that every connected polymatroid having at least two elements has at least two elements
e
such that either
P-e
or
P/e
is connected, and this bound is sharp. For further details on connectivity, refer to the literature
[
1
,
7
]
. The purpose of this note is to investigate the connectivity properties of slice-projections of connected polymatroids, which includes deletion and contraction, and extend existing connectivity theorems for graphs and matroids. We first recall some necessary definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
E
be a finite set (without loss of generality, we usually assume that
E=[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
) and
f:2^{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be a function. If
f
satisfies the following three conditions:

(i)

f(\emptyset)=0
(normalization);

(ii)

f(I)\leq f(J)
for any
I\subseteq J\subseteq E
(monotonicity);

(iii)

f(I)+f(J)\geq f(I\cup J)+f(I\cap J)
for any
I,J\subseteq E
(submodularity),

then
f
is called a
rank function
. A
polymatroid

P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
on the ground set
E
with rank function
f
is given by

P=\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}~\middle|~\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\leq f(I)\ \text{for all}\ I\subseteq E\right\}

and

\mathcal{B}_{P}=\left\{\textbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}~\middle|~\textbf{a}\in P\ \text{and}\ \sum_{i\in E}a_{i}=f(E)\right\}.

is the set of its all
bases
.

All independent sets of any matroid
M
yields a polymatroid.
More precisely, let
M\subseteq 2^{E}
be a matroid. For any
B\subseteq E
, let

I_{B}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}\ \text{where}\ a_{i}=1\ \text{if and only if}\ i\in B.

Then

P=P(M)=\{I_{B}~|~\text{$B$ is an independent set of}\ M\}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}

is the polymatoid corresponding to
M
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
P
be a polymatroid on
E
with rank function
f
. For an element
e\in E
, the
deletion

P-e
and

, which are polymatroids on

, are given by the rank functions
f_{P-e}(T)=f(T)
and
f_{P/e}(T)=f(T\cup e)-f(e)
, for any subset

, respectively.

Remark 1.3
.

Deletion and contraction in Definition
1.2
is the same as in the setting of matroids

A polymatroid

Connectedness in Definition

\displaystyle f(E-I-e)+f(I\cup e)-(f(e)-j)=f(E)-(f(e)-j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle f_{f(e)-j}^{e}(E-e-I)+f_{f(e)-j}^{e}(I)=f(E)-(f(e)-j).

(Claim 1 and Proposition
2.1
)

Case 3.
Assume that
f^{*}(I)>f^{*}(I\cup e)-j
and
f^{*}(E-e-I)>f^{*}(E-I)-j
. Then by the definition of
f^{*}
, Proposition
2.1
and Claim 1, we have

\displaystyle(f^{*})_{j}^{e}(I)+(f^{*})_{j}^{e}(E-e-I)=\sum_{i\in E}f(i)-f(E)-j

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle f^{*}(I\cup e)-j+f^{*}(E-I)-j=\sum_{i\in E}f(i)-f(E)-j~~~~\text{(Proposition \ref{rank function of Pj})}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left(f(E-I-e)+\sum_{i\in I\cup e}f(i)-f(E)\right)-j+\left(f(I)+\sum_{i\in E-I}f(i)-f(E)\right)

\displaystyle-j=\sum_{i\in E}f(i)-f(E)-j~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{(the definition of $f^{*}$)}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle f(E-I-e)+f(e)+f(I)-f(E)-j=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle f(E-I-e)+f(I)=f(E)-(f(e)-j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle f_{f(e)-j}^{e}(E-e-I)+f_{f(e)-j}^{e}(I)=f(E)-(f(e)-j).

(Claim 1 and Proposition
2.1
)

Discussions

We discuss the reductions of Theorem
1.10
to matroids and hypergraphs. Let
M
be a matroid on
E
with rank function
r
. Recall that the rank function
r^{*}

r^{*}(I)=r(E-I)+|I|-r(E)~\text{for any}~I\subseteq E.

(13)

Denote
M^{*}
the matroid given by
r^{*}
, called the
dual matroid
of
M
. Clearly, the connectivity properties of
M
and
M^{*}
are identical. Moreover,

M^{*}-e=(M/e)^{*}\text{~and~}M^{*}/e=(M-e)^{*}.

Hence, we have the following results (see
[
6
]
), as also follows from Corollary
1.11
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a matroid
M
on
E
with dual matroid
M^{*}
. Then for any
e\in E
,

(1)

M/e
and
M^{*}-e
are equally connected;

(2)

M-e
and
M^{*}/e
are equally connected.

Proof.

Note that the connectedness of a matroid and its corresponding polymatroid are identical.
Let
P=P(M)
and
P(M^{*})
be the polymatroids corresponding to
M
and
M^{*}
, respectively. Then
P-e
and
P/e
are the polymatroids corresponding to
M-e
and
M/e
, respectively.

Let
r
and
r^{*}
be the rank functions of matroids
M
and
M^{*}
, respectively. If
r(e^{\prime})\neq 0
for any
e^{\prime}\in E
, then
P^{*}=P(M^{*})
by Equalities (
2
) and (
13
). This implies that
P^{*}-e
and
P^{*}/e
are the polymatroids corresponding to
M^{*}-e
and
M^{*}/e
, respectively. By Corollary
1.11
, the conclusions hold.

If
r(e^{\prime})=0
for some
e^{\prime}\in E-e
, then
r^{*}(E-e^{\prime})=r^{*}(E)-1
. Hence,
M/e
,
M^{*}-e
,
M-e
and
M^{*}/e
are disconnected. Let
f
and
f^{*}
be the rank functions of polymatroids
P
and
P^{*}
, respectively. Clearly,
f(e^{\prime})=f^{*}(e^{\prime})=0
, so,
|T^{*}_{e^{\prime}}|=|T_{e^{\prime}}|=1
. Hence,
P/e
,
P^{*}-e
,
P-e
and
P^{*}/e
are also disconnected. In this case, the conclusions hold.

We now assume that
r(e)=0
and
r(e^{\prime})\neq 0
for any
e^{\prime}\in E-e
. Then

f^{*}(I)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{*}(I),&\text{ if }e\notin I,\\
r^{*}(I)-1,&\text{ if }e\in I.\end{array}\right.

This implies that
P^{*}/e=P(M^{*})/e
and
P^{*}-e=P(M^{*})-e
by the definitions of contraction and deletion. Hence, the conclusions also hold by Corollary
1.11
.
∎

If
P
is a hypergraphical polymatroid, then both
\widehat{P}^{t}_{\alpha_{t}}
and
\widehat{P}^{t}_{\beta_{t}}
are hypergraphical. However, maybe
\widehat{P}^{t}_{j}
is not hypergraphical for
j\in T_{t}\setminus\{\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}\}
. For example, if
P
is a polymatroid induced by a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
, where
V=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}
,
E=\{e=\{1,2,3\},a=\{1,4,5\},b=\{2,4,6\},c=\{3,5,6\}\}
, then
\widehat{P}^{e}_{1}
is not hypergraphical.
So Theorem
1.10
can not be reduced to the setting of hypergraphs.
