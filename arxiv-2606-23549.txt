Abstract
. In this paper, we study the well-posedness and exponential stability of a class of abstract boundary-coupled positive systems. Within a general semigroup framework, we establish well-posedness in terms of existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions. More concretely, we derive some simple and readily verifiable tests on the boundary coupling operators for the verification of the well-posedness of the coupled system and, at the same time, provide spectral criteria that guarantee exponential stability of the solutions. The analysis is based on two complementary approaches: the feedback-theoretic method and perturbation techniques for resolvent positive operators on Banach lattices (i.e., Banach spaces equipped with a compatible order structure). This unified framework allows for a systematic treatment of a broad class of boundary couplings in abstract dynamical systems. Several examples are presented to illustrate the applicability of the theoretical results.



1.
Introduction

We consider in this paper a class of dynamical systems which can be described abstractly by

(1.1)

\dot{z}_{1}(t)={{A}}_{1}z_{1}(t),\quad\dot{z}_{2}(t)={{A}}_{2}z_{2}(t),\quad t>0,

with boundary and initial conditions

(1.2)

\displaystyle G_{1}z_{1}(t)=K_{2}z_{2}(t),\quad G_{2}z_{2}(t)=K_{1}z_{1}(t),\quad t>0,\quad z_{1}(0)=x_{1},\quad z_{2}(0)=x_{2};

here and in what follows dots denote (partial) differentiation with respect to time
t
as usual. For
k=1,2
, we let
X_{k}
and
U_{k}
be infinite-dimensional complete normed vector spaces (or Banach spaces) over the same field – real or complex – and we let the initial points
x_{k}\in X_{k}
, the mappings
A_{k}:\bm{D}({{A}}_{k})\subset X_{k}\to X_{k}
be closed generally unbounded linear operators with domains of definition
\bm{D}({A}_{k})
dense in
X_{k}
, and
G_{1}:\bm{D}({A}_{1})\to U_{1}
,
K_{1}:\bm{D}({A}_{1})\to U_{2}
,
G_{2}:\bm{D}({A}_{2})\to U_{2}
,
K_{2}:\bm{D}({A}_{2})\to U_{1}
be linear boundary operators which express the coupling between the two subsystems in (
1.1
). In this way (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) together constitute an abstract boundary-coupled system for which we use the acronym
\mathbf{aBCS}
hereafter. Of particular interest in this study are the well-posedness and stability – asymptotic or long-time behavior – analysis of the (solutions of the)
\mathbf{aBCS}
, and we are very much interested in how the coupling between the two subsystems affects the exponential stability analysis (to be defined later). The boundary conditions
G_{1}z_{1}(t)=K_{2}z_{2}(t)
and
G_{2}z_{2}(t)=K_{1}z_{1}(t)
above will frequently be referred to in what follows in this paper as the
coupling conditions
.

Many coupled or networked problems that arise in applied mathematics and mathematical physics can be represented by abstract dynamical systems in the form of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
. These include transport equations with delay describing population dynamics of the types considered in
[
9
,
28
]
, abstract boundary delay equations introduced in
[
15
]
, elliptic and parabolic partial differential equations of possibly quasilinear type
[
20
,
23
]
, coupled parabolic and hyperbolic equations describing heat transfer in fluid-solid systems including the semilinear and quasilinear types in
[
26
,
29
,
30
]
, and linear hyperbolic partial differential equations of possibly higher order describing the wave-like dynamics of elastic systems
[
24
,
41
,
42
]
. In most of these papers the boundary conditions and couplings are dynamic in nature, by which is meant they contain time derivatives representing damping or inertia coupling, and a number of different assumptions are made concerning these. For example, in
[
28
]
, the well-posedness and exponential stability of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
when
K_{2}
is the identity on
X_{2}
were studied. Similar results were also derived in
[
9
,
10
]
for the case
K_{2}
unbounded and
K_{1}
bounded. Most recently in
[
29
]
, the authors obtained results on the well-posedness and strong stability of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
including a certain subclass of passive systems in Hilbert spaces. In particular, they derived explicit resolvent bounds and decay constants for the
\mathbf{aBCS}
. It is the results of
[
29
]
that provide the motivation for the investigation in the paper at hand and that we wish to extend to the case of exponential stability of positive systems in Banach lattices.

Many systems arising in physics, biology, chemistry, and economics exhibit the natural property that solutions corresponding to positive initial conditions remain positive for all time. Moreover, the concept of positivity in infinite-dimensional systems which are linear and time-invariant makes it possible to encompass coupled physical systems whose semigroup representations imply well-posedness and satisfy a positivity condition which is strong enough to guarantee not only that solutions are always positive, but also, more importantly, that the generated semigroup satisfies the spectrum-determined growth condition (to be defined later). Note that all positive semigroups on spaces of continuous functions and on
\bm{L}_{p}
-spaces for
p\in[1,\infty)
have this latter property. The most general results in this regard, obtained by Banach space methods, were established in
[
3
,
37
,
38
]
. For such systems, we can therefore derive a range of interesting exponential stability results. A recent overview of developments in this area is given in
[
21
]
(see also the earlier survey
[
36
]
). Other contributions to the theory of linear time-invariant infinite-dimensional positive systems can be found in
[
4
,
7
,
6
,
18
,
16
,
13
,
15
,
32
,
14
,
40
]
.

The first task in the analysis of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
is to establish well-posedness via operator-theoretic methods in terms of strongly continuous semigroups of bounded linear operators –
C_{0}
-semigroups in short – on Banach spaces. We propose two complementary perturbation-based approaches to address this issue in the present paper. The first approach is rooted in the feedback theory of positive linear time-invariant infinite-dimensional systems that are regular in the sense of
[
33
,
39
]
. Within this framework, we show that the
\mathbf{aBCS}
, when formulated as an abstract initial/boundary-value problem on a suitable Banach state space, has the required properties of existence, uniqueness, and regularity of its solutions. These results are established using a modified version of the Weiss–Staffans perturbation theorem recently obtained in
[
15
]
. We derive an explicit convolution formula on the space considered, which enables the definition of solutions to the associated inhomogeneous problem. In addition, we discuss the asymptotic behavior of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
, and we prove that the resulting positive
C_{0}
-semigroup is exponentially stable if and only if each coupled subsystem is exponentially stable and the transfer function of the overall system has a spectral radius strictly less than one. The second approach is more direct and relies solely on generation and stability results for domain perturbations of positive semigroup generators
[
14
]
. In particular, we show that we can recover the same well-posedness and stability conclusions as those obtained via the first method, provided each of the decoupled subsystems satisfies suitable resolvent-inverse estimates on the positive semiaxis. For related results on the generation under domain perturbations of positive semigroup generators, we refer to
[
5
,
6
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
25
,
13
]
.

We note that our results will be proven in the context of positive mild solutions under relatively weak assumptions on the system dynamics when compared with other approaches, e.g. see
[
28
,
15
,
9
]
. Additionally, we obtain spectral criteria for well-posedness and exponential stability. Hence, the theory of this study is complete in the sense that it addresses the basic questions concerning well-posedness and exponential stability which arise in coupled physical systems and which are important in applications. Indeed, the reader will see that a number of application examples can be made of the theory with little extra effort when compared with the efforts usually required to obtain the well-posedness and exponential stability results (see
[
12
,
10
,
15
,
28
,
9
]
).

1.1.
Contributions and organization

The contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows. We present a perturbation-based operator-theoretic framework for establishing well-posedness and exponential stability results for the
\mathbf{aBCS}
. While the paper
[
29
]
focused on operator-theoretic formulations in Hilbert space, leading to results for contraction
C_{0}
-semigroups, we treat the problem in the more general setting of Banach spaces. This generalization is essential for studying diffuse boundary couplings in the
\mathbf{aBCS}
, and thus directly links to the underlying physical situation. Our approach hinges on the inherent positivity of the system dynamics, which serves a dual purpose: first, it relaxes the regularity requirements on the internal dynamics of each subsystem; second, it allows us to exploit the powerful theory of positive semigroups on Banach lattices. The resulting well-posedness and exponential stability results are “sharp”, meaning that they cannot be relaxed under the imposed assumptions on the coupled system, thereby extending prior findings in
[
14
,
9
,
15
,
29
,
28
]
. A key contribution of this paper is a spectral characterization for the well-posedness under verifiable conditions on the boundary coupling operators. In particular, we establish simple and readily verifiable tests for
C_{0}
-semigroup generation, i.e. for the well-posedness of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
(Proposition
3.2
, Theorem
3.3
, and also Remarks
3.4
and
3.5
). This characterization is derived from a complete description of the spectrum of the positive semigroup generator of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
(Proposition
3.1
and Remark
3.3
). As a consequence, considerable emphasis is placed on application examples illustrating the theory.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Some notation, definitions, and auxiliary results which will be used in our considerations are recalled in Section
2
. Also introduced in that section is the abstract initial/boundary-value problem, which is used as a prototype for describing the
\mathbf{aBCS}
, together with a brief review of the regular systems approach to
C_{0}
-semigroup generation. In Section
3
, results are established concerning the well-posedness and exponential stability of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
in the Banach lattice setting using two complementary approaches: the feedback-theoretic method and perturbation techniques for resolvent positive operators on Banach lattices. Section
4
presents several applications and physical system models which can be included within our framework. Finally, in Section
5
, we draw some conclusions.

2.1.
Notation and terminology

In this paper,
\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{R}_{+}
denote the sets of real and nonnegative real numbers, and
\mathbf{C}
is the set of complex numbers. Let

and

denote Banach spaces. We denote by
{\mathcal{B}}(E,F)
the space of bounded linear operators from
E
to
F
equipped with the strong operator topology; if
E=F
, we write
{\mathcal{B}}(E)\coloneqq{\mathcal{B}}(E,E)
. For an operator
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(E,F)
and a subspace
Z
in
E
, we denote by

the restriction of
P
to
Z
, which is again a bounded operator. The notation
\operatorname{ran}P
is used to denote the range of

. For any (real)
p\in[1,\infty)
, we use the notation
\bm{L}_{p}(\mathbf{R}_{+};E)
to denote the space of all strongly measurable,
p
-integrable
E
-valued functions. For each compact interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbf{R}
, we denote by
\bm{C}(a,b;E)
the space of all continuous

-valued functions on
[a,b]
, and we denote by
\bm{W}^{1}_{p}(a,b;E)
the Sobolev space of absolutely continuous
E
-valued functions on
[a,b]
whose first-order derivatives lie in
\bm{L}_{p}(a,b;E)
.

Let
E\coloneqq(E,\leq)
be a Banach lattice, i.e. a partially ordered Banach space for which any given elements
x
,
y
of
E
have a supremum denoted by
x\vee y
with respect to the partial ordering
\leq
and we denote by
|x|
the absolute value defined by
|x|\coloneqq x\vee(-x)
if
x\in E
. The following implications then hold:

(i)

x\leq y
implies
x+z\leq y+z
and
\alpha x\leq\alpha y
for all
\alpha\geq 0
and any
x,y,z\in E
; and

(ii)

\|x\|\leq\|y\|
whenever
|x|\leq|y|
.

An element

is positive if
x\geq 0
. A positive cone in
E
is denoted by
E_{+}
(a notation also used for general ordered Banach spaces). The topological dual
E^{\prime}
of a Banach lattice
E
, under the usual dual norm and order, is also a Banach lattice. An operator
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(E,F)
is called positive if it maps
E_{+}
into
F_{+}
, where
F
is a Banach lattice generated by a cone
F_{+}
. The set of all such positive operators is denoted by
{\mathcal{B}}_{+}(E,F)
. This notation is also used when
E
and
F
are ordered Banach spaces.

We will use the symbol
I
generically to denote the identity operator on the corresponding space which will be clear from the context. Let
X
be a Banach lattice and
A:\bm{D}({{A}})\subset X\to X
be a closed densely defined linear operator generating a
C_{0}
-semigroup
{{T}}\coloneqq(T(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
X
. The spectral bound of
{{A}}
is defined by
s({{A}})\coloneqq\sup\{\operatorname{Re}\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma({{A}})\}
, where
\sigma({{A}})
denotes the spectrum of
{{A}}
, and the growth bound (type) of
{{T}}
is defined by
\omega_{0}({{T}})\coloneqq\inf\{\omega\in\mathbf{R}:\text{$\mathop{{}\exists}M\geq 1$ such that $\|T(t)\|\leq Me^{\omega t}$ for all $t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}$}\}
. It holds that
s({{A}})\leq\omega_{0}({{T}})
and
r(T(t))=e^{\omega_{0}({{T}})t}
,
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
, where

denotes the spectral radius of
T(t)
(see, e.g.,
[
19
, Proposition IV.2.2]
). A semigroup
{{T}}
is said to be exponentially stable if its growth bound
\omega_{0}({{T}})<0
. If
s({{A}})=\omega_{0}({{T}})
, then we say that
T
(or
A
) satisfies the spectrum-determined growth condition, and in this case the exponential stability of
{{T}}
is obviously equivalent to the property
s({{A}})<0
. We let
X_{-1}^{{{A}}}
denote the extrapolation space associated with
X
and
A
, which is the completion of
X
in the norm
\|x\|_{-1}^{{{A}}}\coloneqq\|R(\lambda,{{A}})x\|
, the resolvent operator
R(\lambda,{{A}})\coloneqq(\lambda I-{{A}})^{-1}
for some arbitrary, but fixed,
\lambda\in\mathbf{C}
in the resolvent set
\varrho({{A}})
of

. Then we see that
X\subset X_{-1}^{{{A}}}
and
X
is dense and continuously embedded in
X_{-1}^{{{A}}}
. Furthermore, a
C_{0}
-semigroup

extends uniquely to a

-semigroup

is an extension of

to

, i.e. with domain

; the semigroups

and
{{T}}_{-1}
are isomorphic to each other (see
[
19
, Section II.5]
). A semigroup

on
X
is called positive if
T(t)x\geq 0
for all

and for any

.

In our setting,
X
is a Banach lattice with a natural positive cone

. However, it is not immediately clear how to extend this concept of positivity to the extrapolation space

. Recall that if

-semigroup on

in the norm

is a real Banach lattice, then by

see more details in
[

2.2.
Preliminary results on initial/boundary-value problems

Let
{X}
,
{U}
be Banach spaces, called the
state space
and
boundary space
, respectively, and consider the following abstract initial/boundary-value problem on
{X}
:

(2.1)

\dot{z}(t)={{A}}z(t),\quad{G}z(t)={K}z(t),\quad t>0,\quad z(0)=x.

We will assume that
{{A}}:{Z}\subseteq{X}\to{X}
is closed,
{Z}\subseteq{X}
dense and continuously embedded in
{X}
, and
{G},{K}:{Z}\to U
are bounded linear boundary operators.

In order to discuss the well-posedness of the initial/boundary-value problem (
2.1
), we first define the so-called
system operator

\mathcal{A}
in
X
, which will also be used later in Section
3
in the statements of the main results; namely,

(2.2)

{\mathcal{A}}x\coloneqq{A}x,\quad\bm{D}({\mathcal{A}})=\left\{x\in{Z}:\>Gx=Kx\right\}.

By standard semigroup theory, the system (
2.1
) is well posed if and only if the system operator
\mathcal{A}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\mathcal{T}\coloneqq(\mathcal{T}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
X
. For this reason, we focus our attention in the sequel on the
C_{0}
-semigroup generation properties of
\mathcal{A}
. We impose the following two standard assumptions (which we assume throughout):

Assumption 2.1
.

Consider the pair
({{A}},{G})
. The following assumptions hold:

(i)

\tilde{{{A}}}\coloneqq{{A}}|_{\ker{G}}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
{{T}}\coloneqq({T}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
{X}
; and

(ii)

{G}
is onto.

Under Assumption
2.1
we have that the space
{Z}
can be decomposed and related to the domain of
\tilde{{A}}
as follows:

{Z}=\ker{G}\oplus\ker(\lambda I-{A}),\quad\lambda\in\varrho(\tilde{{A}}).

To proceed, we will use the notion of a Dirichlet operator an define

{D}_{\lambda}\coloneqq({G}|_{\ker(\lambda I-{A})})^{-1}:{U}\to\ker(\lambda I-{A})\subseteq{X},\quad\lambda\in\varrho(\tilde{{A}}).

Hence,
{D}_{\lambda}
exists and
{D}_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{B}}(U,X)
(see
[
22
, Lemmas 1.2 and 1.3]
), and we can relate the pair
(A,G)
to a well-defined Dirichlet form by introducing the so-called
boundary control operator

{B}\coloneqq(\lambda I-\tilde{{A}}_{-1}){D}_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{B}}({U},{X}_{-1}^{\tilde{{A}}}),\quad\lambda\in\varrho(\tilde{{A}}).

Note that
{B}
is independent of the choice of
\lambda
,
\operatorname{ran}{B}\cap{X}=\{0\}
, and that

(2.3)

({A}-\tilde{{A}}_{-1})|_{Z}={B}{G}.

Formally, we have the following definition. Throughout
X
and
U
are Banach lattices, and we put

{C}\coloneqq{K}|_{\ker{G}},

the so-called
observation operator
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
be satisfied. Suppose that the operators
K
,
B
, and
T
are positive. Then, for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, we say that the triple
(\tilde{{A}},{B},{C})
is a positive
\bm{L}_{p}
-well-posed triple on
({U},{X},{U})
if the following statements hold:

(1)

The pair
(\tilde{{A}},{B})
is positive
\bm{L}_{p}
-admissible, i.e., for some (and, hence, for all)
t>0
and
u\in\bm{L}_{p,+}(0,t;{U})
, the input map corresponding to
(\tilde{{A}},{B})
is defined by

\Phi_{t}u\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-r){B}u(r)dr\in{X}_{+}.

(2)

The pair
(C,\tilde{A})
is positive
\bm{L}_{p}
-admissible, i.e., for some (and, hence, for all)
t>0
, there exists
\gamma\coloneqq\gamma(t)>0
such that for any
x\in\bm{D}_{+}(\tilde{{A}})
,

\|{C}T(\cdot)x\|_{\bm{L}_{p}(0,t;{U})}\leq\gamma^{p}\|x\|_{X}^{p}.

(3)

For every
t>0
there exists
\eta\coloneqq\eta(t)>0
such that for any
u\in\mathring{\bm{W}}^{1}_{p,+}(0,t;{U})\coloneqq\{u\in\bm{W}^{1}_{p,+}(0,t;{U}):\,u(0)=0\}
,

\|{K}\Phi_{\cdot}u\|_{\bm{L}_{p}(0,t;{U})}\leq\eta\|u\|_{\bm{L}_{p}(0,t;{U})}.

The implications of the definition call for some comments. Consider the operator

(\mathbb{F}u)(r)\coloneqq{K}\Phi_{r}u

for any
u\in\mathring{\bm{W}}^{1}_{p,+}(0,t;{U})
,
r\in[0,t]
, and
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
. If the triple
(\tilde{{A}},{B},{C})
is a positive
\bm{L}_{p}
-well-posed triple, then
\mathbb{F}
extends uniquely to a positive bounded linear operator on
\bm{L}_{p}(0,t;{U})
, still denoted by
\mathbb{F}
. Moreover, there exists a unique positive analytic

, defined on

, which is given by

\mathbf{H}(\lambda)=KR(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{-1})B

and is called the

of the triple

is defined only up to such an extension. Therefore, as all resulting functions are analytic, we do not distinguish between different representations of

To better capture the role of the transfer function

in our well-posedness analysis, we introduce the following subclasses of

-well-posed linear systems.

Definition 2.2
.

Under the assumptions and conditions of Definition

-well-posed triple with zero feedthrough which is of weakly, strongly, or uniformly regular type if, respectively, we have for any

,

, all referring to

.

In the strongly regular case, the extension of

admits the representation

(\mathbb{F}u)(r)=C_{\Lambda}\Phi_{r}u

for any

, a.e.

, and

denotes the Yoshida extension of

, defined by

C_{\Lambda}x:=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}C\lambda R(\lambda,\tilde{A})x,\quad x\in\bm{D}(C_{\Lambda}):=\{x\in X:\text{$\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}C\lambda R(\lambda,\tilde{A})x$ exists in $U$}\}.

We will also need the following concept of an
admissible positive feedback operator
, related to Definition
2.1
.

Definition 2.3
.

The identity operator
I
on
U
is called an admissible positive feedback operator for a positive
\bm{L}_{p}
-well-posed triple
(\tilde{{A}},{B},{C})
with corresponding input/output map

if the inverse of
I-\mathbb{F}
exists and is positive as an operator on
{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{L}_{p}(0,\tau;{U}))
for some
\tau>0
.

We conclude this section with a generation result under domain perturbations of positive semigroup generators, which simultaneously establishes both the well-posedness and positivity of the initial/boundary-value problem (

) (see
[

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that the triple
(\tilde{{A}},{B},{C})
is a positive
\bm{L}_{p}
-well-posed weakly regular triple with zero feedthrough in the sense of Definition
2.2
and
I
is an admissible positive feedback operator in the sense of Definition
2.3
. Then the system operator
{\mathcal{A}}
generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{T}}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
{X}
which can be represented in the form

{\mathcal{T}}(t)x=T(t)x+\int_{0}^{t}{T}_{-1}(t-r){B}{C}_{\Lambda}{\mathcal{T}}(r)xdr,\quad t\in\mathbf{R}_{+},

for any

. The initial/boundary-value problem (

) admits a unique positive mild solution
z\in\bm{C}(\mathbf{R}_{+};{X})
which can can be defined by
\mathbf{R}_{+}\ni t\mapsto z(t)\coloneqq{\mathcal{T}}(t)x

3.
Main results

In this section we present our main results on the well-posedness and exponential stability of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
. The analysis proceeds along two complementary approaches. While the first approach is based on the feedback-theoretic framework presented in Section
2.2
, the second approach relies on perturbation techniques for the so-called
resolvent positive operators
as developed in
[
14
]
(appropriate definitions will be given later). These approaches will be loosely termed in the sequel the “feedback theory approach” and the “perturbation approach”, respectively.

Throughout we assume the spaces
X_{k}
and
U_{k}
,
k=1,2
, to be Banach lattices. To write the
\mathbf{aBCS}
as an abstract initial/boundary-value problem of the form (
2.1
), the state and boundary spaces are decomposed

X=X_{1}\times X_{2},\quad U=U_{1}\times U_{2}

and are Banach lattices with the norms

\left\|\left(\begin{matrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{matrix}\right)\right\|_{X}\coloneqq\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}+\|x_{2}\|_{X_{1}},\quad\left\|\left(\begin{matrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{matrix}\right)\right\|_{U}\coloneqq\|u_{1}\|_{U_{1}}+\|u_{2}\|_{U_{1}}.

In terms of the state function

t\mapsto z(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}z_{1}(t)\\
z_{2}(t)\end{matrix}\right),

the
\mathbf{aBCS}
can then be written in the form (
2.1
) with initial state
z(0)\coloneqq(x_{1},x_{2})^{\top}
, where the operators
{{A}}
,
G
, and
K
are defined in
X
with the same domain

\bm{D}({{A}}_{1})\times\bm{D}({{A}}_{2})

by the block operator matrices

\displaystyle{{A}}\coloneqq\left(\begin{matrix}{{A}}_{1}&0\\
0&{{A}}_{2}\end{matrix}\right),\quad G\coloneqq\left(\begin{matrix}G_{1}&0\\
0&G_{2}\end{matrix}\right),\quad K\coloneqq\left(\begin{matrix}0&K_{1}\\
K_{2}&0\end{matrix}\right).

Therefore, the system operator
\mathcal{A}
in
X
is as defined in (
2.2
), with
Z=\bm{D}(A)\coloneqq\bm{D}({{A}}_{1})\times\bm{D}({{A}}_{2})
used in the definition of
\bm{D}(\mathcal{A})
.

3.1.
Feedback theory approach

Before we proceed to state the main results of this subsection we recall that Assumption
2.1
mentioned in the context of the pair
(A,G)
continues to hold for
(A_{k},G_{k})
,
k=1,2
. Thus, in line with Assumption
2.1
, we make the following assumptions.

Assumption 3.1
.

Consider the pair
(A_{k},G_{k})
,
k=1,2

k=1,2
, the following assumptions hold:

(i)

\tilde{{A}}_{k}\coloneqq{A}_{k}|_{\ker{G}_{k}}

C_{0}
-semigroup

{X}_{k}

(ii)

{G}_{k}
is onto.

With Assumption
3.1
it can be directly seen from the paragraph following the statement of Assumption

that for

(A_{k},G_{k})

Bringing Assumption

and the definitions above together, we now are in a position to state our first main result, which essentially guarantees the existence of unique positive solutions of the
\mathbf{aBCS}

(\tilde{A}_{1},B_{1},C_{1})

Under Assumption

(3.2)

\tilde{A}\coloneqq A,\quad\bm{D}(\tilde{A})\left(\coloneqq\ker G\right)=\bm{D}(\tilde{A}_{1})\times\bm{D}(\tilde{A}_{2})

, we have for each such

\tilde{A}

and we obtain for the input map corresponding to the pair

(3.3)

\Phi_{t}\left(\begin{matrix}u_{1}\\

for any

. Define now the observation operator

C\coloneqq K|_{\bm{D}(\tilde{A})}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&C_{2}\\

The pairs
(C_{1},\tilde{A}_{1})

\bm{L}_{p}
-admissible, implying that the pair

\bm{L}_{p}

Remark 3.1
.

Note from (

is diagonal. Then, with (

) and (

The next result consists of a characterization of the spectral properties of the system operator
{\mathcal{A}}
along with its resolvent operator computation.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let Assumption
3.1
be satisfied and let it be further assumed that
K_{k}
,
k=1,2
, is a positive operator. For
k=1,2
, suppose that

is a positive operator for all

\mathcal{A}
generates a positive

on

. For
\lambda\in\varrho(\tilde{A}_{1})\cap\varrho(\tilde{A}_{2})

\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}\coloneqq K_{1}D_{\lambda}^{1}K_{2}D_{\lambda}^{2}.

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\max\big\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\big\}<\lambda
and

; and

(2)

s(\mathcal{A})<\lambda
.

Moreover, for all
\lambda>s(\mathcal{A})
,

(3.5)

R(\lambda,{\mathcal{A}})=\left(\begin{matrix}\left(I+D_{\lambda}^{1}K_{2}\Delta_{\lambda}\right)R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{1})&D_{\lambda}^{1}K_{2}\left(I+\Delta_{\lambda}D_{\lambda}^{1}K_{2}\right)R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{2})\\
\Delta_{\lambda}R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{1})&I+\Delta_{\lambda}D_{\lambda}^{1}K_{2}R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{2})\end{matrix}\right),

wherein
\Delta_{\lambda}\coloneqq D_{\lambda}^{2}\left(I-\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}\right)^{-1}K_{1}
.

Let
\lambda>\max\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\}
and

. From the representation formula (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\lambda I-{\mathcal{A}})x

\displaystyle=(\lambda I-A)x

\displaystyle=(\lambda I-\tilde{A}_{-1}-BG)x

\displaystyle=(\lambda I-\tilde{A})(x-D_{\lambda}Gx)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=(\lambda I-\tilde{A})(x-D_{\lambda}Kx),

where the third equality holds because
x-D_{\lambda}Gx\in{\bm{D}}(\tilde{A})
. Thus,

(3.7)

\lambda\in\varrho({\mathcal{A}})\iff 1\in\varrho(D_{\lambda}K)\iff 1\in\varrho(KD_{\lambda}),

where we have used that
KD_{\lambda}
and
D_{\lambda}K
share the same nonzero spectrum (since
(I-D_{\lambda}K)^{-1}=I+D_{\lambda}(I-KD_{\lambda})^{-1}K
). Next, for
\lambda>\max\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\}
, we obtain the factorization

(3.8)

I-KD_{\lambda}=\left(\begin{matrix}I&0\\
-K_{1}D_{\lambda}^{1}&I-{\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}I&-K_{2}D_{\lambda}^{2}\\
0&I\end{matrix}\right).

Thus
I-KD_{\lambda}
is invertible if and only if
I-{\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda}
is invertible. Since
{\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda}
is a positive operator, this is equivalent to
r({\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda})<1
.
Combining this with (
3.7
), we obtain

(3.9)

\lambda\in\varrho(\mathcal{A})\iff r({\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda})<1.

If
1
holds, then
\lambda\in\varrho(\mathcal{A})
, hence
s(\mathcal{A})\leq\lambda
. If
s(\mathcal{A})=-\infty
, the positivity of
\mathcal{T}
implies that
R(\lambda,\mathcal{A})
is positive, and therefore, by
[
3
, Theorem C-III.1.1-(b)]
,
s(\mathcal{A})<\lambda
. If
s(\mathcal{A})>-\infty
, the positivity of
\mathcal{T}
implies that
s(\mathcal{A})\in\sigma(\mathcal{A})
(cf.
[
3

s(\mathcal{A})<\lambda
. This proves
1
\implies
2
. For the proof of

\implies
1
, let
s({\mathcal{A}})<\lambda

2.5

s({\mathcal{A}})>\max\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\},

and hence
\lambda>\max\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\}

Now, using (

\displaystyle R(\lambda,{\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle=(I+{D}_{\lambda}(I-KD_{\lambda})^{-1}{K})R(\lambda,\tilde{{A}}).

Remark 3.2
.

It is important to note that the proof of Proposition

does not rely on the

-well-posedness and regularity of the triples
(\tilde{A}_{1},B_{1},C_{1})

along with the positivity of the operators

and

generates a positive

-semigroup on

. This makes the spectral characterization in Proposition

independent of the method used to prove the

-semigroup generation property of

. Therefore, the results established in Proposition

Remark 3.3
.

It is worth noting that Proposition
3.1
provides a complete characterization of the spectral bound of
\mathcal{A}
. More precisely, we have

(3.11)

s(\mathcal{A})=\inf\left\{\omega<\lambda:r({\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda})<1\right\},

wherein
\omega\coloneqq\max\big\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\big\}
. In fact, let

(since

are positive). Hence, the mapping
\lambda\mapsto\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}
is decreasing. By positivity of operators on Banach lattices,

is monotone with respect to the order
\leq
and, thus, is decreasing. Hence, the set
\{\omega<\lambda:r({\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda})<1\}
is nonempty and bounded below by

,

for all

An immediate consequence of Proposition

is that a necessary condition for the system operator

.

, let

Remark 3.4
.

We note that the above result yields a useful and verifiable test for

We can now state and prove the following stability result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Consider the setting of Theorem

. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

s({\mathcal{A}})<0
; and

(2)

.

Moreover, if any of the above conditions hold, then there exist constants
J_{x_{1},x_{2}},\alpha>0
such that

(3.12)

\|z_{1}(t)\|_{X_{1}}+\|z_{2}(t)\|_{X_{2}}\leq J_{x_{1},x_{2}}e^{-\alpha t}\left\|\left(\begin{matrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{matrix}\right)\right\|_{X}

for all
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
and any
x_{1}\in{\bm{D}}(A_{1})
,
x_{2}\in{\bm{D}}(A_{2})
satisfying the coupling conditions
G_{1}x_{1}=K_{2}x_{2}
and
G_{2}x_{2}=K_{1}x_{1}
.

Proof.

The equivalence between
1
and
2
follows directly from Proposition
3.1
. If we suppose that either either
1
or
2
holds, then, according to
[
3
, Corollary C-IV.1.4]
, there exist constants
J_{x},\alpha>0
such that for all
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
and any
x\in{\bm{D}}({\mathcal{A}})
,

\|{\mathcal{T}}(t)x\|_{X}\leq J_{x}e^{-\alpha t}\|x\|_{X}.

Combining this with (
3.1
) yields the estimate (
3.12
), thereby proving the theorem.
∎

The following corollary answers the question as to what happens to the result of Theorem
3.2
if the spectrum-determined growth condition holds.

Corollary 3.1
.

Consider the setting of Theorem
3.1
. Assume that
{\mathcal{T}}
satisfies the spectrum-determined growth condition, i.e.,
s({\mathcal{A}})=\omega_{0}({\mathcal{T}})
. If

s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})<0,\quad r({\mathbb{A}}_{0})<1,

then there exist constants
J,\alpha>0
such that for all
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
and any
x_{1}\in X_{1}
,
x_{2}\in X_{2}
,

\|z_{1}(t)\|_{X_{1}}+\|z_{2}(t)\|_{X_{2}}\leq Je^{-\alpha t}\left(\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}+\|x_{2}\|_{X_{2}}\right).

3.2.
Perturbation approach

In this subsection we present an alternative characterization of exponential stability, which is based on the perturbation approach for resolvent positive operators in
[
14
]
. For this purpose, we recall that a linear operator
Q
is called
resolvent positive
if there exists
\omega\in\mathbf{R}
such that
(\omega,\infty)\subset\rho(Q)
and
R(\lambda,Q)\geq 0
for all
\lambda>\omega
. Every infinitesimal generator of a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup is resolvent positive; however, the converse is not necessarily true (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Section 3]
).

We begin with a generation result for the system operator
\mathcal{A}
in
X
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For
k=1,2
, let the following assumptions hold:

(i)

\ker G_{k}
is dense in
X_{k}
and
{G}_{k}
is onto;

(ii)

\tilde{A}_{k}\coloneqq{A}_{k}|_{\ker{G}_{k}}
is resolvent positive and, for some
\lambda_{0}^{k}>s(\tilde{A}_{k})
, there exists
c_{k}>0
such that

\|R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{k})x_{k}\|_{X_{k}}\geq c_{k}\|x_{k}\|_{X_{k}}

for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}^{k}
and any
x_{k}\in(X_{k})_{+}
; and

(iii)

K_{k}
is positive, and
D_{\lambda}^{k}
is positive for large enough
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}
.

Then
{\mathcal{A}}
generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{T}}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
X
if and only if

(3.13)

r(K_{1}D_{\lambda_{1}}^{1}K_{2}D_{\lambda_{1}}^{2})<1

or, equivalently,
r(K_{2}D_{\lambda_{1}}^{2}K_{1}D_{\lambda_{1}}^{1})<1
for some
\lambda_{1}>\max\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\}
. In this case the spectrum-determined growth condition
\omega_{0}({\mathcal{T}})=s(\mathcal{A})
holds.

Proof.

The proof of necessity is obvious from Proposition
3.1
. For the sufficiency, define the operator

\tilde{A}\coloneqq A,\quad\bm{D}(\tilde{A})\coloneqq\ker G.

Then,

\tilde{A}=\left(\begin{matrix}\tilde{A}_{1}&0\\
0&\tilde{A}_{2}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\bm{D}(\tilde{A})=\bm{D}(\tilde{A}_{1})\times\bm{D}(\tilde{A}_{2}).

Under our assumptions
(i)
and
(ii)
, we see that
\tilde{A}_{1}
and
\tilde{A}_{2}
are densely defined resolvent positive operators. Hence,
\tilde{A}
is a densely defined resolvent positive operator as well, and we have, for
\lambda\in\varrho(\tilde{A})\coloneqq\varrho(\tilde{A}_{1})\cap\varrho(\tilde{A}_{2})
,

R(\lambda,\tilde{A})=\left(\begin{matrix}R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{1})&0\\
0&R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{2})\end{matrix}\right).

Moreover, for all
\lambda\geq\max\{\lambda_{0}^{1},\lambda_{0}^{2}\}
and any
x\in X_{+}
,

\|R(\lambda,\tilde{A})x\|_{X}\geq c\|x\|_{X},

some constant
c\coloneqq\inf\{c_{1},c_{2}\}
. Thus, by
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
,
\tilde{A}
generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
. By assumption
(i)
, there is a Dirichlet operator
D_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{B}(U,X)
given by

D_{\lambda}\coloneqq\left(\begin{matrix}D_{\lambda}^{1}&0\\
0&D_{\lambda}^{2}\end{matrix}\right)

for each
\lambda\in\varrho(\tilde{A})
. By assumption
(iii)
,
D_{\lambda}
is positive for
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}
large enough, and hence, by
[
4
, Proposition 4.3]
, for all
\lambda>s(\tilde{A})
. Furthermore, using the factorization (
3.8
), we obtain for all
\lambda>\max\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\}

I-KD_{\lambda}=\left(\begin{matrix}I&0\\
-K_{1}D_{\lambda}^{1}&I-{\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}I&-K_{2}D_{\lambda}^{2}\\
0&I\end{matrix}\right),

wherein
\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}\coloneqq K_{1}D_{\lambda}^{1}K_{2}D_{\lambda}^{2}
. Since
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are positive, it follows that
r(KD_{\lambda})<1
if and only if
r(\mathbb{A}_{\lambda})<1
, the latter inequality holding by virtue of (
3.13
). Therefore, by
[
14
, Theorem 1]
, we conclude that the system operator
\mathcal{A}
generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{T}}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
X
satisfying
\omega_{0}({\mathcal{T}})=s(\mathcal{A})
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

It is worth noting that the above theorem extends Proposition
3.2
to the Banach lattice setting. In particular, Theorem
3.3
provides a simple and readily verifiable test on the boundary coupling operators for the
C_{0}
-semigroup generation property of the system operator
{\mathcal{A}}
in this general setting.

We are now in a position to state and prove the following stability result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3.3
be satisfied and suppose that

(3.14)

\max\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\}<0,\quad r({\mathbb{A}}_{0})<1.

Then the
C_{0}
-semigroup
{\mathcal{T}}
is exponentially stable.

Proof.

As a result of the properties in (
3.14
), it follows from Theorem
3.3
that the system operator
\mathcal{A}
generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{T}}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
X
satisfying the spectrum-determined growth condition
\omega_{0}(\mathcal{T})=s(\mathcal{A})
. The proof is completed by combining the result of Proposition
3.1
with (
3.14
) to yield
\omega_{0}({\mathcal{T}})<0
.
∎

Returning to the
\mathbf{aBCS}
we see that in view of the above results it is now readily verified that it admits a unique positive mild solution
z\in\bm{C}(\mathbf{R}_{+};{X})
for any
x\in X
, given by

{z}(t)={\mathcal{T}}(t)x,\quad t\in\mathbf{R}_{+},

and that there exist constants
J,\alpha>0
such that for all
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
and any
x\in X
,

\|z(t)\|_{X}\leq Je^{-\alpha t}\|x\|_{X}.

4.
Applications

We now consider the use of the results of the preceding section in determining the well-posedness and exponential stability of a number of applications and physical system models.

4.1.
Boundary delay equations

The aim of this subsection is to show how the class of abstract boundary delay equations introduced in
[
15
]
fits into the framework of Section
2
so that the results of Section
3
can be applied. Following the notational framework of Section
2.2
, we consider an abstract dynamical system described by

(4.1)

\dot{z}(t)=Az(t),\quad y(t)=Kz(t),\quad t>0,

with boundary and initial conditions

(4.2)

Gz(t)=L(y_{t}),\quad t>0,\quad z(0)=x,\quad y_{0}=\varphi,

wherein

•

\varphi\in\bm{L}_{p}(-r,0;U)\coloneqq Y_{p}
for
0<r<\infty
,
1\leq p<\infty
;

•

L:\bm{W}^{1}_{p}(-r,0;U)\to U
is a bounded linear operator; and

•

y:[-r,+\infty)\to U
and
y_{t}:[-r,0]\to U
as defined by
y_{t}(\theta)=y(t+\theta)
.

For the purposes of proving well-posedness and stability results it will be required to reformulate (
4.1
), (
4.2
) in the form of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
. We observe, first of all, that
y_{t}=y_{t}\left(\theta\right)
satisfies the following initial/boundary-value problem for the transport equation:

(4.3)

\dot{y}_{t}=\partial_{\theta}y_{t},\quad y_{t}(0)=y(t),\quad t>0,\quad y_{0}=\varphi.

The reformulation is completed by combining (
4.3
) with (
4.1
), (
4.2
) to give

\dot{z}(t)=Az(t),\quad Gz(t)=L(y_{t}),\quad\dot{y}_{t}=\partial_{\theta}y_{t},\quad y_{t}(0)=Kz(t),\quad t>0,

with
U_{1}=U_{2}=U
. Define the operator

Q\varphi\coloneqq\partial_{\theta}\varphi,\quad\bm{D}(Q)\coloneqq\ker\delta_{0},

where
\delta_{0}:\bm{W}^{1}_{p}(-r,0;U)\to U
is the Dirac measure concentrated at
0
. It is well known that
Q
generates a left-shift semigroup
S\coloneqq(S(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
Y_{p}
given by

(S(t)\varphi)(\theta)=\left\{\begin{split}0&,&&-t\leq\theta\leq 0,\\
\varphi(t+\theta)&,&&-r\leq\theta\leq-t,\end{split}\right.

for any
\varphi\in Y_{p}
and a.e.
\theta\in[-r,0]
. Clearly,
S
is positive and
\varrho(Q)=\mathbf{C}
. We further define the operators

\Theta\coloneqq(-Q_{-1})\varepsilon_{0},\quad\tilde{L}\coloneqq L|_{\bm{D}(Q)},

where
(\varepsilon_{\lambda}y)(\theta)\coloneqq e^{\lambda\theta}y
for
\lambda\in\mathbf{C}
,
\theta\in[-r,0]
, and any
y\in U
.

We are now ready to apply Theorem
3.1
to the system described by (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and obtain the well-posedness result which we state as a corollary.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
X
,
U
be Banach lattices and let Assumption
2.1
be satisfied. Identify the operators
\tilde{A}
,
B
, and
C
of Definition
2.2
as
A
,
G
, and
K
, respectively. Suppose that the triples
(\tilde{A},B,C)
,
(Q,\Theta,\tilde{L})
with corresponding input/output maps
\mathbb{F}_{1}
and
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, respectively, are positive
\bm{L}_{p}
-well-posed weakly regular triples with zero feedthrough in the sense of Definition
2.2
. If the spectral radius
r(\mathbb{F}_{1}\mathbb{F}_{2})<1
or, equivalently,
r(\mathbb{F}_{2}\mathbb{F}_{1})<1
, then the operator

(4.4)

{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}(A,\partial_{\theta}),\quad{\bm{D}}({\mathcal{A}})\coloneqq\left\{x\in{\bm{D}}(A),~\varphi\in\bm{W}^{1}_{p}(-r,0;U):Gx=L(\varphi),~\varphi(0)=Kx\right\}

generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{T}}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
X\times Y_{p}
. Moreover, there exists for any
x\in X
and
\varphi\in Y_{p}
a unique positive mild solution
z\in\bm{C}(\mathbf{R}_{+},X)
of the system (
4.1
), (
4.2
) which can be represented in the form

\displaystyle z(t,x,\varphi)

\displaystyle=T(t)x+\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)B\tilde{L}_{\Lambda}S(s)\varphi ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)B\tilde{L}_{\Lambda}\int_{0}^{s}S_{-1}(s-\sigma)\Theta C_{\Lambda}z(\sigma,x,\varphi)d\sigma ds,\quad t\in\mathbf{R}_{+},

where
\tilde{L}_{\Lambda}
,
C_{\Lambda}
are the Yoshida extensions of
\tilde{L}
and
C
with respect to
Q
and
\tilde{A}
, respectively.

It is worth noting that Corollary

is a slightly generalized statement of
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
. Moreover, in the following result, we recover the spectral characterization of exponential stability given in
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let the assumptions of Corollary
4.1
be satisfied. If

s(\tilde{A})<0,\quad r\left(KD_{0}L(\varepsilon_{0})\right)<1,

then there exist constants
N_{x,\varphi},\alpha>0
such that

\|z(t)\|_{X}+\|y_{t}\|_{Y_{p}}\leq N_{x,\varphi}e^{-\alpha t}\left(\|x\|_{X}+\|\varphi\|_{Y_{p}}\right),

for all
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
and any
x\in{\bm{D}}(A)
,
\varphi\in\bm{W}^{1}_{p}(-r,0;U)
satisfying the coupling conditions
Gx=L(\varphi)
and
\varphi(0)=Kx
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the result of Theorem
3.2
and the fact that
s(Q)=-\infty
.
∎

In closing this application example, we note that in the setting of Hilbert lattices, application of Proposition

provides the following sharp well-posedness result for the system (

), (
4.2
).

Corollary 4.3
.

. Then the system operator

defined by (

) generates a positive

4.2.
Transport equations on a cyclic network with time-delayed vertex conditions

In this subsection we consider a network of
m\geq 2
nonconservative transport equations

(4.5)

\dot{u}_{j}(t,x)=-v_{j}(x)\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,x),\quad 1\leq j\leq m,

in a cyclic arrangement, by which we mean a network consisting of one vertex and
m
loops attached to the vertex. The conditions that specify the coupling at the vertex are

(4.6)

\gamma_{j}v_{j}(0)u_{j}(t,0)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{jk}\xi_{k}v_{k}(\ell_{k})u_{k}(t-r_{k},\ell_{k}),\quad 1\leq j\leq m,

and the initial conditions are

(4.7)

u_{j}(0,x)=f_{j}(x),\quad u_{j}(\theta,\ell_{j})=\varphi_{j}(\theta),\quad 1\leq j\leq m,

given functions
f_{j}
,
\varphi_{j}
. Above
{u}_{j}(t,x)
represents the flow of particles on the
j
-th cycle for
(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}_{+}\times[0,\ell_{j}]
,
\ell_{j}>0
being the length of each cycle. The functions
v_{j}(\cdot)
are the particle-flow velocities, the parameters
\gamma_{j}
,
\xi_{j}
are the absorption and amplification coefficients at the points
x=0
and
x=\ell_{j}
, respectively, the
\sigma_{jk}\in\mathbf{R}
represent the coupling coefficients, and
\theta\in[-r_{j},0]
, the
r_{j}\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
being inter-cycle transmission time delays in the network.

The problem of exponential stability for the initial/boundary-value problem (
4.5
)–(
4.7
) in the time-varying (i.e. nonautonomous) nonconservative case, with partially internal local time-dependent parameters, was addressed in
[
12
]
in the case when time delays are absent. Here, via (
4.6
), we account for both nonconservativeness as well as time delays in the transmission of flows at the vertex and, additionally, assume the flow velocities to be spatially variable (i.e. nonuniform).

To begin our study, we consider the Banach spaces

X_{1}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\bm{L}}_{p}(0,\ell_{j};\mathbf{R}),\quad X_{2}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\bm{L}}_{p}(-r_{j},0;\mathbf{R})

with respective norms

\|f\|_{X_{1}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{{\bm{L}}_{p}(0,\ell_{j};\mathbf{R})},\quad\|\varphi\|_{X_{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{{\bm{L}}_{p}(-r_{j},0;\mathbf{R})}.

We introduce a history segment

[-r_{j},0]\ni\theta\mapsto\tilde{u}_{j}^{t}(\theta)\coloneqq\tilde{u}_{j}(t+\theta),\quad 1\leq j\leq m,

wherein the tilde indicates that the flows are to be evaluated at the points
x=\ell_{j}
, i.e.,
\tilde{u}_{j}(t)=u_{j}(t,\ell_{j})
for every
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
. In particular, for
1\leq j\leq m
, the function
t\mapsto\tilde{u}_{j}^{t}(t)
satisfies (
4.3
). The initial/boundary-value problem (
4.5
)–(
4.7
) can be formulated as an abstract system on
X=X_{1}\times X_{2}
, the product space formed by the state space,

\dot{y}(t)={\mathcal{A}}y(t),\quad t>0,\quad y(0)=((f_{j})_{j=1}^{m},(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{m})^{\top},

where
y(t)=((u_{j}(t,\cdot))_{j=1}^{m},(\tilde{u}_{j}^{t})_{j=1}^{m})^{\top}
and the system operator
{\mathcal{A}}
on
X
is defined by

(4.8)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{A}}&\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}(-V(\cdot)\partial_{x},\partial_{\theta}),\\
{\bm{D}}({\mathcal{A}})&\coloneqq\left\{\begin{gathered}(f,\varphi)^{\top}\in\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\bm{W}}_{p}^{1}(0,\ell_{j};\mathbf{R})\times\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\bm{W}}_{p}^{1}(-r,0;\mathbf{R}):\\
\Gamma V(0)f(0)=(\varphi_{j}(-r_{j}))_{j=1}^{m},~\varphi(0)=\mathbb{M}\Xi\operatorname{diag}(v_{k}(\ell_{k}))_{k=1}^{m}(f_{k}(\ell_{k}))_{k=1}^{m}\end{gathered}\right\},\end{split}

wherein
V(\cdot)\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}(v_{j}(\cdot))_{j=1}^{m}
,
\Gamma\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
,
\mathbb{M}\coloneqq(\sigma_{jk})_{j,j=1}^{m}
, and
\Xi\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}(\xi_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
.

We are now in a position to prove the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
1\leq j\leq m
, suppose
v_{j}\in{\bm{L}}_{\infty}(0,\ell_{j})
with
v_{j}(x)>v_{0}>0
for a.e.
x\in[0,\ell_{j}]
. Suppose further that
\gamma_{j},\xi_{j},\sigma_{jk}>0
for
1\leq j,k\leq m
. If

(4.9)

r(\mathbb{M}\Xi\operatorname{diag}(v_{k}(\ell_{k}))_{k=1}^{m}V^{-1}(0)\Gamma^{-1})<1,

then there exist constants
J,\alpha>0
such that for all
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
and any
f\in X_{1}
,
\varphi\in X_{2}
,

(4.10)

\|u(t)\|_{X_{1}}+\|\tilde{u}^{t}\|_{X_{2}}\leq Je^{-\alpha t}\left(\|f\|_{X_{1}}+\|\varphi\|_{X_{2}}\right).

Proof.

We apply Corollary
3.1
with
U_{1}=U_{2}=\mathbf{R}^{m}
. To this end, let us define

A_{1}f\coloneqq-V(\cdot)\partial_{x}f,\quad A_{2}\varphi\coloneqq\partial_{\theta}\varphi

for any
f\in{\bm{D}}(A_{1})\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\bm{W}}_{p}^{1}(0,\ell_{j};\mathbf{R})
and
\varphi\in{\bm{D}}(A_{2})\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\bm{W}}_{p}^{1}(-r_{j},0;\mathbf{R})
. Let us further define the boundary operators

\displaystyle G_{1}f\coloneqq\Gamma V(0)f(0),\quad K_{1}f\coloneqq\mathbb{M}\Xi\operatorname{diag}(v_{k}(\ell_{k}))_{k=1}^{m}(f_{k}(\ell_{k}))_{k=1}^{m},

\displaystyle G_{2}\varphi\coloneqq\varphi(0),\quad K_{2}\varphi\coloneqq(\varphi_{j}(-r_{j}))_{j=1}^{m}

for any
f\in{\bm{D}}(A_{1})
and
\varphi\in{\bm{D}}(A_{2})
. With these definitions we next show that the system operator
\mathcal{A}
defined by (
4.8
), now with domain

{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}(A_{1},A_{2}),\quad{\bm{D}}({\mathcal{A}})\coloneqq\left\{(f,\varphi)^{\top}\in{\bm{D}}(A_{1})\times{\bm{D}}(A_{2}):G_{1}f=K_{2}\varphi,~G_{2}\varphi=K_{1}f\right\},

generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
. By assumption,
v_{j}(\cdot)>0
for
1\leq j\leq m
; hence, for
1\leq j\leq m
, the function
[0,\ell_{j}]\ni x\mapsto\tau_{j}(x)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{v_{j}(x)}dx
is invertible for
x\in[0,\ell_{j}]
. Then, by a generation argument similar to the one found in
[
27
]
, it can be shown that the operator restriction
\tilde{A}_{1}\coloneqq A_{1}|_{\ker G_{1}}
generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
T_{1}\coloneqq(T_{1}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
X_{1}
given by

(T_{1}(t)f)_{j}(x)=\left\{\begin{split}f_{j}(\tau_{j}^{-1}(\tau_{j}(x)-t))&,&&t\leq\tau_{j}(x),&&1\leq j\leq m,\\
0&,&&\text{otherwise},&&1\leq j\leq m,\end{split}\right.

for any
f\in X_{1}
and a.e.
x\in[0,\ell_{j}]
. Since
\gamma_{j},v_{j}(\cdot)>0
for
1\leq j\leq m
, it follows that the operator
G_{1}
is onto. Moreover, formal calculation shows that the Dirichlet operator associated with the pair
(A_{1},G_{1})
is given by

D_{\lambda}^{1}\mathsf{a}={\rm diag}(e^{-\lambda\tau_{j}(\cdot)})_{j=1}^{m}V^{-1}(0)\Gamma^{-1}\mathsf{a}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbf{C}
and any
\mathsf{a}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}
. We put
B_{1}\coloneqq-(\tilde{A}_{1})_{-1}D_{0}^{1}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{R}^{m},(X_{1})_{-1}^{\tilde{A}_{1}})
. Using Laplace transformation techniques, it is then easily shown that for all
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
the input map corresponding to the pair
(\tilde{A}_{1},B_{1})
is given by

(\Phi^{1}_{t}g)_{j}(x)=\left\{\begin{split}\frac{g_{j}(t-\tau_{j}(x))}{\gamma_{j}v_{j}(0)}&,&&t\geq\tau_{j}(x),&&1\leq j\leq m,\\
0&,&&\text{otherwise},&&1\leq j\leq m,\end{split}\right.

for any
g\in{\bm{L}}_{p}(\mathbf{R}_{+},\mathbf{R}^{m})

Remark 4.1
.

It is interesting to note that Theorem
4.1
provides a spectral characterization of exponential stability for the initial/boundary-value problem (
4.5
)–(
4.7
) that is independent of both the cycle lengths and time delays. A similar result has been established very recently in
[
18
]
for a variant of (
4.5
)–(
4.7
), using a different method.

As an application of Theorem
4.1
we next impose a Kirchhoff type condition on the coupling coefficients
\sigma_{jk}
by adjoining to (
4.5
)–(
4.7
) the condition

(4.12)

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{jk}=1,\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
be satisfied and suppose that the condition (
4.12
) holds. If

(4.13)

\max_{k}\frac{\xi_{k}v_{k}(\ell_{k})}{\gamma_{k}v_{k}(0)}<1,

then solutions to the initial/boundary-value problem (
4.5
)–(
4.7
) decay to zero exponentially.

Proof.

Let
d_{k}=\frac{\xi_{k}v_{k}(\ell_{k})}{\gamma_{k}v_{k}(0)}
,
1\leq k\leq m
. Then

\mathbb{M}\Xi\,{\rm diag}(v_{k}(\ell_{k}))_{k=1}^{m}V^{-1}(0)\Gamma^{-1}=\mathbb{M}\,{\rm diag}(d_{k})_{k=1}^{m},

where
\mathbb{M}
is by (
4.12
) a row-stochastic matrix whose entries are positive. Since the spectral radii
r\left(\mathbb{M}\,{\rm diag}(d_{k})\right)
have the property that

r\left(\mathbb{M}\,{\rm diag}(d_{k})\right)\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{jk}d_{k},

we have, using (
4.13
),

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{jk}d_{k}\leq\max_{k}d_{k}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{jk}=\max_{k}d_{k}<1.

Hence, for
1\leq k\leq m
,

r\left(\mathbb{M}\,{\rm diag}(d_{k})\right)<1.

The result then follows from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}_{+}\times[0,\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}]

j

(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}_{+}\times[\alpha_{j}\ell_{j},\ell_{j}]

are the particle-flow velocities, and the parameters
\kappa_{j}

j

In the following we investigate the problems of well-posedness and exponential stability of solutions of our initial/boundary-value problem (

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\kappa_{j},\beta_{j}>0
for
1\leq j\leq m
. Then the operator restriction

\tilde{A}_{1}\coloneqq A_{1}|_{\ker G_{1}}

is densely defined and resolvent positive. For any
h\in(X_{1})_{+}
, all
\lambda\geq 0
, and
\bar{\beta}:=\sup_{1\leq j\leq m}\beta_{j}
, the following estimate is valid:

(4.20)

\|R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{1})h\|_{X_{1}}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda+\bar{\beta}}\|h\|_{X_{1}}.

Furthermore,
\tilde{A}_{1}
generates an exponentially stable positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
T_{1}\coloneqq(T_{1}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
X_{1}
.

Proof.

We begin by observing that the domain

{\bm{D}}(\tilde{A}_{1})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{gathered}g\in\prod_{j=1}^{m}\bm{W}_{1}^{2}(0,\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}):g_{i}(0)=g_{j}(0),~-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\kappa_{j}\partial_{x}g_{j}(0)=0,\\
\kappa_{j}\partial_{x}g_{j}(\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})=0,~1\leq i,j\leq m\end{gathered}\right\},

dense in
X_{1}
. For
h\in X_{1}
and
g\in\bm{D}(\tilde{A}_{1})
, let us consider the problem
-\tilde{A}_{1}g=h
. Then,

\begin{gathered}-\kappa_{j}\partial_{xx}g+\beta_{j}g_{j}=h_{j},\\
g_{i}(0)=g_{j}(0),\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\kappa_{j}\partial_{x}g_{j}(0)=0,\quad\kappa_{j}\partial_{x}g_{j}(\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})=0,\quad 1\leq i,j\leq m,\end{gathered}

and application of routine computations yields that the solutions
g_{j}=g_{j}(x)
of the problem are given by

g_{j}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{\alpha_{k}\ell_{k}}\Gamma_{j,k}(x,s)h_{k}(s)ds,\quad 1\leq j\leq m,

where

\Gamma_{j,k}(x,s)=\left\{\begin{split}\frac{\cosh(\gamma_{j}(x-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))\cosh(\gamma_{k}(s-\alpha_{k}\ell_{k}))}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})\cosh(\gamma_{k}\alpha_{k}\ell_{k})N}&,&&j\neq k,\\
\frac{\cosh(\gamma_{j}(x-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))\cosh(\gamma_{j}(s-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})^{2}N}+\frac{\cosh(\gamma_{j}(x-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))\sinh(\gamma_{j}s)}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})\kappa_{j}\gamma_{j}}\\
+\frac{\sinh(\gamma_{j}(x-s))}{\kappa_{j}\gamma_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{s\geq x}&,&&j=k,\end{split}\right.

with
\gamma_{j}\coloneqq\sqrt{{\beta_{j}}/{\kappa_{j}}}
and
N=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\kappa_{k}\gamma_{k}\tanh(\gamma_{k}\alpha_{k}\ell_{k})
.
Clearly,
\Gamma_{j,k}(x,s)\geq 0
for
j\neq k
. We show that
\Gamma_{j,k}(x,s)\geq 0
for
j=k
as well. Let us set

\Gamma_{j,k}(x,s)=\Gamma^{1}_{j,k}(x,s)+\Gamma^{2}_{j,k}(x,s),

where

\displaystyle\Gamma^{1}_{j,k}(x,s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\cosh(\gamma_{j}(x-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))\cosh(\gamma_{j}(s-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})^{2}N},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2}_{j,k}(x,s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\cosh(\gamma_{j}(x-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))\sinh(\gamma_{j}s)}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})\kappa_{j}\gamma_{j}}+\frac{\sinh(\gamma_{j}(x-s))}{\kappa_{j}\gamma_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{s\geq x}.

Then we obviously have
\Gamma^{1}_{j,j}(x,s)\geq 0
. Now, using the elementary identities

\displaystyle\sinh(\gamma_{j}(x-s))

\displaystyle=\sinh(\gamma_{j}x)\cosh(\gamma_{j}s)-\cosh(\gamma_{j}x)\sinh(\gamma_{j}s),

\displaystyle\cosh(\gamma_{j}(x-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))

\displaystyle=\cosh(\gamma_{j}x)\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})-\sinh(\gamma_{j}x)\sinh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2}_{j,j}(x,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa_{j}\gamma_{j}}\sinh(\gamma_{j}x)\left(\cosh(\gamma_{j}s)-\tanh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})\sinh(\gamma_{j}s)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa_{j}\gamma_{j}}\sinh(\gamma_{j}x)\frac{\cosh(\gamma_{j}(s-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\kappa_{j}\gamma_{j}}\frac{\sinh(\gamma_{j}x)}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})}.

Since
\cosh(\gamma_{j}(s-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))\geq 1
for
s\in[0,\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}]
, it follows that
\Gamma^{2}_{j,j}(x,s)\geq 0
and we conclude that
\Gamma_{j,j}(x,s)=\Gamma^{1}_{j,j}(x,s)+\Gamma^{2}_{j,j}(x,s)\geq 0
. Hence,
0\in\varrho(\tilde{A}_{1})
, implying that
\tilde{A}_{1}
is boundedly invertible (and is therefore closed and densely defined), and
-\tilde{A}_{1}^{-1}\geq 0
. Since
\tilde{A}_{1}
is coercive for all
\lambda\geq 0
, it follows that
[0,\infty)\subset\varrho(\tilde{A}_{1})
. To proceed further we let
R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{1})h\eqqcolon g
be the positive solution of
(\lambda I-\tilde{A}_{1})g=h
for
h\in(X_{1})_{+}
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Then the
g_{j}=g_{j}(x)
satisfy

(\lambda+\beta_{j})g_{j}-\kappa_{j}\partial_{xx}g_{j}=h_{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

Integrating with respect to
x\in(0,\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})
and using the additivity of the
{\bm{L}}_{1}
-norm on
(X_{1})_{+}
, we arrive at (recall
g\in\bm{D}(\tilde{A}_{1})
)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\lambda+\beta_{j})\int_{0}^{\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}}g_{j}(x)dx=\|h\|_{X_{1}},

from which we obtain

\|g\|_{X_{1}}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda+\bar{\beta}}\|h\|_{X_{1}}

with
\bar{\beta}:=\sup_{j}\beta_{j}
. Therefore, the operator
\tilde{A}_{1}
is densely defined, resolvent positive, and satisfies the estimate (
4.20
). Moreover, according to
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
,
\tilde{A}_{1}
generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
T_{1}\coloneqq(T_{1}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
X_{1}
. Since
0\in\varrho(\tilde{A}_{1})
and
(-\tilde{A}_{1})^{-1}\geq 0
, it follows from
[
3
, Theorem C-IV.1.1 and Corollary C-IV.1.4]
that
\omega_{0}(T_{1})<0
. With this the proof is complete.

Remark 4.2

We note that the operator restriction

could be associated with the abstract Cauchy problem for a compact star-network setup of heat equations. In particular, Lemma

Lemma 4.2
.

Let the assumption of Lemma
4.1
be satisfied. Then the operator
G_{1}
defined in (
4.19
) is onto from
{\bm{D}}(A_{1})
to
\mathbf{R}
. Moreover, for all
\lambda>-\bar{\beta}
, any
d\in\mathbf{R}
, and a.e.
x\in[0,\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}]
, the Dirichlet operator associated with the pair
(A_{1},G_{1})
is given by

(4.21)

(D_{\lambda}^{1}d)_{j}(x)=\frac{\cosh(\gamma_{j}(x-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{d}{\kappa_{k}\gamma_{k}\tanh(\gamma_{k}\alpha_{k}\ell_{k})},\quad 1\leq j\leq m,

with
\gamma_{j}\coloneqq\sqrt{{(\beta_{j}+\lambda)}/{\kappa_{j}}}

Recall the definitions of
A_{1}
,
G_{1}
in (

)–(
4.19

g\in\bm{D}({A}_{1})
and
d\in\mathbf{R}

(\lambda-A_{1})g=0,\quad G_{1}g=d,

Then, for fixed
\lambda>-\bar{\beta}

satisfy

-\kappa_{j}\partial_{xx}g_{j}+(\beta_{j}+\lambda)g_{j}=0,\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

Put

(4.22)

\gamma_{j}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{j}+\lambda}{\kappa_{j}}}>0,\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

It is easily verified that the general solution is given by

Using the conditions
\kappa_{j}\partial_{x}g_{j}(\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})=0

(4.23)

a_{j}=b_{j}e^{-2\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}},\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

The vertex continuity condition

Thus,

(4.24)

g_{j}(x)=\frac{c}{1+e^{-2\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}}}\left(e^{\gamma_{j}x-2\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}}+e^{-\gamma_{j}x}\right)=c\frac{\cosh(\gamma_{j}(x-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})},\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

In order to apply the remaining condition
G_{1}g=d
to the solution obtained in (

\partial_{x}g_{j}(0)=c\gamma_{j}\frac{\sinh(-\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})}=-c\gamma_{j}\tanh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}),\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

Then,

G_{1}g=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\kappa_{j}\partial_{x}g_{j}(0)=c\sum_{j=1}^{m}\kappa_{j}\gamma_{j}\tanh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}),

which yields that

on account of
G_{1}g=d

g_{j}(x)=\frac{\cosh(\gamma_{j}(x-\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}))}{\cosh(\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{d}{\kappa_{k}\gamma_{k}\tanh(\gamma_{k}\alpha_{k}\ell_{k})},\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

This demonstrates that for any
d\in\mathbf{R}
, there exists a
g\in\bm{D}(A_{1})
such that
(\lambda-A_{1})g=0
and
G_{1}g=d
, proving that
G_{1}
is onto. By definition, the Dirichlet operator associated with the pair
(A_{1},G_{1})
is given by
D_{\lambda}^{1}d=g
, which verifies (
4.21
). The fact that, under the condition
d\geq 0
, for all
\lambda>-\bar{\beta}
the operator
D_{\lambda}^{1}
is positive is then obvious from (
4.21
). This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The main result of this subsection presented below addresses the exponential stability of the initial/boundary-value problem (
4.14
)-(
4.16
).

Theorem 4.2
.

For
1\leq j\leq m
, suppose
c_{j}\in\bm{L}_{\infty}(\alpha_{j}\ell_{j},\ell_{j})
with
c_{j}(x)>c_{0}>0
for a.e.
x\in[\alpha_{j}\ell_{j},\ell_{j}]
. Suppose further that
\kappa_{j},\beta_{j}>0
for
1\leq j\leq m
. Then, for any
g,f\in X=X_{1}\times X_{2}\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{m}\bm{L}_{1}(0,\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})\times\prod_{j=1}^{m}\bm{L}_{1}(\alpha_{j}\ell_{j},\ell_{j})
, there exists a unique positive mild solution
z\in\bm{C}(\mathbf{R}_{+};X)
of the
\mathbf{aBCS}
, where the operators are defined by (
4.17
)–(
4.19
). If

(4.25)

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{k}\beta_{k}}\tanh(\alpha_{k}\ell_{k}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{k}}{\kappa_{k}}})}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\cosh(\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{j}}{\kappa_{j}}})}\frac{c_{j}(\ell_{j})}{c_{j}(\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})}\right)<1

then solutions to the initial/boundary-value problem (
4.14
)-(
4.16
) decay to zero exponentially.

Proof.

We can apply Theorem
3.3
and obtain the first statement on the well-posedness. Proceeding in the usual way, let us first define the system operator
\mathcal{A}
by

(4.26)

{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq{\rm diag}(A_{1},A_{2}),\quad{\bm{D}}({\mathcal{A}})\coloneqq\left\{(f,\varphi)^{\top}\in{\bm{D}}(A_{1})\times{\bm{D}}(A_{2}):G_{1}f=K_{2}\varphi,~G_{2}\varphi=K_{1}f\right\},

where the operators
A_{k}
,
G_{k}
,
K_{k}
,
k=1,2
, are defined by (
4.17
)–(
4.19
). For
k=1,2
, let
\tilde{A}_{k}:{\bm{D}}(\tilde{A}_{k})\subseteq X_{k}\to X_{k}
be the operator restriction defined by
\tilde{A}_{k}\coloneqq A_{k}|_{\ker G_{k}}
. By Lemma
4.1
,
\tilde{A}_{1}
is densely defined, resolvent positive, and satisfies the estimate (
4.20
) for any
x\in X_{+}
and all
\lambda\geq 0
. By Lemma
4.2
,
G_{1}
is onto and the Dirichlet operator associated with the pair
(A_{1},G_{1})
,
D_{\lambda}^{1}
, is positive for all
\lambda>-\bar{\beta}
. For
\tilde{A}_{2}
, an argument analogous to that in
[
14
, Proposition 3]
(see also
[
18
, Lemma A.1]
) shows that for any
f\in(X_{2})_{+}
and all
\lambda>0
there is a constant
N>0
, independent of
f
, such that

\|R(\lambda,\tilde{A}_{2})f\|_{X_{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda\bar{c}}N\|f\|_{X_{1}},

with
\bar{c}\coloneqq\sup_{1\leq j\leq m}\|c_{j}\|_{\infty}
. The operator
G_{2}
is clearly onto, and a direct computation shows that the Dirichlet operator associated with the pair
(A_{2},G_{2})
is given by

(D_{\lambda}^{2}\mathsf{a})(x)=\left(\frac{a_{j}}{c_{j}(\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})}e^{-\lambda\int_{\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}}^{x}\frac{1}{c_{j}(s)}ds}\right)_{j=1}^{m}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbf{C}
, any
\mathsf{a}=(a_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}
, and a.e.
x\in[\alpha_{j}\ell_{j},\ell_{j}]
. It follows immediately that
D_{\lambda}^{2}
is positive for all
\lambda>-\infty
. Furthermore, the operators
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are positive as well.

Now, for
\lambda>\max\{s(\tilde{A}_{1}),s(\tilde{A}_{2})\}
, define

{\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda}\coloneqq K_{1}D_{\lambda}^{1}K_{2}D_{\lambda}^{2}\in\mathbf{R}^{m\times m}.

Since
s(\tilde{A}_{2})=-\infty
, it follows from Theorem
3.3
that the system operator
\mathcal{A}
as defined by (
4.26
) generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{T}}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on
X
if and only if
r({\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda_{0}})<1
for some
\lambda_{0}>s(\tilde{A}_{1})
. A straightforward computation yields, with (
4.22
),

({\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda})_{i,j}=\frac{1}{\cosh(\gamma_{i}\alpha_{i}\ell_{i})}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\kappa_{k}\gamma_{k}\tanh(\gamma_{k}\alpha_{k}\ell_{k})}\frac{c_{j}(\ell_{j})}{c_{j}(\alpha_{j}\ell_{j})}e^{-\lambda\int_{\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}}^{\ell_{j}}\frac{1}{c_{j}(s)}ds},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq m.

Thus,

\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\|{\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbf{R}^{m\times m}}=0

and, hence,
\|{\mathbb{A}}_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{\mathbf{R}^{m\times m}}<1
for some large enough

\mathcal{A}
generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{T}}(t))_{t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}}
on

admits a unique positive mild solution

Finally, for the statement on the exponential stability of solutions to the initial/boundary-value problem (

5.
Conclusions

In this paper, we have developed a new perturbation-based approach to the analysis of well-posedness and exponential stability of abstract boundary-coupled positive systems. We have exploited the positivity of both the internal dynamics of each subsystem and the boundary coupling operators. The methods stress the operator-theoretic aspects and rely on the properties of positive
C_{0}
-semigroups on Banach lattices which make it possible to obtain the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions together with their exponential stability using general semigroup theory. In addition to the theoretical contributions, we have provided a number of applications, which demonstrate the applicability of the theory in obtaining well-posedness and exponential stability results.

In this present work, the well-posedness and exponential stability results are sharp in the sense that they cannot be relaxed under the assumptions on the coupled system. In this respect, our results are more directly applicable to the exponential stability problem for positive
C_{0}
-semigroups on Banach lattices. An obvious question, therefore, would ask about the stability of positive systems in the Banach lattice setting just in case the spectral criteria for exponential stability would not be satisfied. To this end, the analysis of a weaker form of stability, strong – and possibly polynomial – stability, could be a natural extension of the present study.
