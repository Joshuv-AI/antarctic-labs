Anterolateral Miniopen Triple‐Row Repair With Acellular Human Dermal Matrix Patch Augmentation for Larger Rotator Cuff Tears

Chul‐Hyun Cho, Byung‐Chan Choi, Hyuk‐Joon Sohn, Du‐Han Kim

Abstract

Large to massive rotator cuff tears are frequently associated with poor tendon quality and high rates of structural failure after repair. Acellular human dermal matrix patch augmentation combined with rotator cuff repair has been introduced to enhance bone‐to‐tendon healing by providing early postoperative mechanical strength. However, fully arthroscopic patch augmentation techniques are technically demanding and associated with increased operative complexity. The described technique combines arthroscopic evaluation and subacromial decompression with an anterolateral miniopen triple‐row repair and onlay acellular human dermal matrix patch augmentation. This approach simplifies patch handling while providing strong fixation and may represent a practical alternative to fully arthroscopic patch augmentation techniques.

Rotator cuff repair (RCR) is a commonly performed procedure in orthopaedic fields., Despite advances in surgical techniques and instrumentation, high retear rates after RCR have been reported, particularly in patients with large to massive rotator cuff tears (RCTs)., Although structural failure does not always correlate with poor clinical outcomes, several studies have shown that intact tendon healing is generally associated with superior long‐term results.,

Patch augmentation using acellular human dermal matrix (AHDM) has been introduced to reinforce RCR by improving load sharing, increasing repair strength, and providing a biologic scaffold for tendon healing., , , Previous studies have reported improved structural integrity and reduced retear rates in patients with large or massive RCTs., , , However, most full arthroscopic techniques are technically demanding, requiring advanced arthroscopic skills and prolonged operative time with intraoperative complexity.

Miniopen RCR remains a valuable option for large tears, offering direct visualization, reliable tendon mobilization, and secure suture management, while minimizing deltoid morbidity when performed through a limited incision., , This Technical Note describes a practical and reproducible anterolateral miniopen triple‐row RCR augmented with an AHDM patch for the treatment of large to massive RCTs. A detailed step‐by‐step description and accompanying video are provided.

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE

The complete surgical technique is shown in Video .

Diagnostic Arthroscopy and Subacromial Decompression

Under general anesthesia, the patient is placed in the lateral decubitus position with a posterior tilt of 20° to 30°. Standard diagnostic arthroscopy of the glenohumeral joint is performed using anterior and posterior portals. The arthroscope is then advanced into the subacromial space through the posterior portal, and a lateral portal is established.

Using a probe, the size, configuration, and mobility of the RCT are carefully assessed. Careful debridement of the margin of the torn rotator cuff and adhesiolysis are performed to improve tendon mobility (Figure ). Subacromial spurs and acromial wear are evaluated, and arthroscopic acromioplasty is performed using a burr when signs of impingement are present.

Anterolateral Miniopen RCR

Miniopen RCR is performed via an anterolateral approach as previously described., A 3‐ to 4‐cm skin incision is made from the anterolateral edge of the acromion distally. The raphe between the anterior and middle deltoid is carefully dissected, and adequate exposure is obtained using a deltoid retractor. The subacromial bursa is excised to expose the torn rotator cuff.

Traction sutures are placed in the torn tendons, and the tear configuration and tendon involvement are confirmed by rotating the arm. After complete tendon mobilization, reduction to the footprint of the greater tuberosity is attempted. Then, footprint preparation is performed using the crimson duvet procedure.

Two medial‐row suture anchors (4.75 mm HEALICOIL, Smith & Nephew, Andover, MA) are placed at the articular margin of the greater tuberosity. Sutures are passed through the medial portion of the torn tendons at a depth of approximately 10 to 12 mm. One or 2 intermediate‐row suture anchors (2.8 mm Y‐Knot, CONMED, Utica, NY) are then inserted into the lateral aspect of the greater tuberosity. Sutures are passed through the tendon and secured using a sliding knot‐tying technique (Figure ).

AHDM Patch Augmentation

An AHDM patch (3‐mm thickness, 3 × 4 cm; ConPatch, MSBIO, Seongnam, Republic of Korea) is applied in an onlay fashion to reinforce the repair. Medial‐row suture limbs are passed through the patch (Figure ) and tied using a sliding knot‐tying technique. These sutures are then secured laterally using knotless anchors (Kinex Fix2Lock; OSTEONIC, Seoul, Republic of Korea) (Figure ). A schematic illustration and magnetic resonance imaging of the triple‐row repair with AHDM patch augmentation are shown in Figures and .

To prevent deltoid detachment, a No. 1 absorbable suture is passed through a bone tunnel created in the acromion for secure deltoid reattachment. Additional sutures are placed 3 to 4 times to repair the anterior and middle deltoid.

Postoperative Rehabilitation

Patients are immobilized in an abduction brace for 6 weeks postoperatively. Passive range‐of‐motion exercises are initiated at 4 to 6 weeks after surgery depending on tear size and repair quality. Active range‐of‐motion exercises begin at 8 weeks, followed by strengthening exercises at 3 months. Return to sports or occupational activities is permitted approximately 6 months after surgery.

DISCUSSION

The key finding of this Technical Note is that an anterolateral miniopen triple‐row RCR with AHDM patch augmentation provides strong fixation while simplifying patch handling and suture management. This technique may serve as a practical and reproducible alternative to fully arthroscopic patch augmentation.

This approach may be particularly beneficial in patients with large or massive RCTs, which are associated with high failure rates because of excessive repair tension and poor tendon quality. Triple‐row constructs incorporating medial, intermediate, and lateral fixation with onlay patch augmentation distribute load more evenly across the footprint and reduce gap formation compared with nonaugmented single‐ or double‐row repairs. In addition, AHDM patch augmentation may enhance early postoperative repair strength and promote tendon healing by serving as a biologic scaffold for cellular ingrowth.

Compared with fully arthroscopic techniques, the miniopen approach offers several advantages. Direct visualization facilitates precise tendon mobilization, accurate footprint preparation, and secure suture handling, particularly in retracted or delaminated tears (Table ). Patch handling and fixation are simplified, potentially reducing operative time and technical complexity (Table ).

Nevertheless, several limitations should be considered. Visualization may be limited in obese or muscular patients, and there is a potential risk of deltoid injury if the procedure is not performed carefully (Table ). However, deltoid integrity can be preserved through meticulous splitting along the deltoid raphe and secure reattachment.

Anterolateral miniopen triple‐row RCR with AHDM patch augmentation is a practical and reproducible technique for the treatment of large to massive RCTs. This approach may represent a valuable alternative to fully arthroscopic patch‐augmented repairs in appropriately selected patients.
