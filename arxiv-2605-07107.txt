Sub-Gaussian Concentration and Entropic Normality of the Maximum Likelihood Estimator
Leighton P. Barnes 1 , Alex Dytso 2

Abstract
It is well known that, under standard regularity conditions, the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) satisfies a central limit theorem and converges in distribution to a Gaussian random variable as the sample size grows. This paper strengthens this classical result by developing several stronger forms of asymptotic normality for the normalized MLE. With additional assumptions on the score, we first establish sub-Gaussian tail bounds and convergence of all moments for the normalized estimation error. We then prove an entropic central limit theorem for a smoothed version of the estimator, showing convergence in relative entropy to the limiting Gaussian law. When the Fisher information of the normalized estimate is bounded, or its density has bounded first derivative, we further show that the smoothing can be removed, yielding entropic normality of the MLE itself. The proofs develop auxiliary tools that may be of independent interest, including exponential consistency bounds, high-moment estimates, and entropy-control arguments for the estimator.



I
Introduction

Suppose we have samples
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
drawn i.i.d. from a density
f(x|\theta)
with a parameter
\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}
. The density
f(x|\theta)
is with respect to a base measure
\mu
on the sample space
\mathcal{X}
. This paper investigates the asymptotic behavior of the distribution of the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE). In particular, we study the distribution of the properly normalized estimation error
Z_{n}=\sqrt{n}\,(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})
as the sample size
n\to\infty
. Under standard regularity conditions, this quantity is known to satisfy a classical central limit theorem (CLT) and converge in distribution to a Gaussian random variable.

The main goal of this paper is to strengthen this classical result in several directions. These strengthened results come at the expense of additional assumptions on the score, which can be considered natural in many statistical models.
First, we establish sub-Gaussian tail behavior for the normalized estimation error
Z_{n}
,
and use this control to upgrade convergence in distribution to convergence of moments.
Second, we prove an entropic form of asymptotic normality for a smoothed version of the estimator,
\widetilde{Z}_{n}=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}Z_{n}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}Z,

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
is independent of
Z_{n}
. Finally, under an additional Fisher information (or bounded first derivative) regularity condition on the densities of
Z_{n}
, we remove the smoothing and obtain entropic normality of
Z_{n}
itself. Convergence in relative entropy is significantly stronger than convergence in distribution and belongs to a class of strong statistical divergences. In particular, convergence in relative entropy implies convergence in total variation distance via Pinsker’s inequality and convergence in Wasserstein distance via Talagrand’s inequality.

I-A

Prior Work

The asymptotic normality of the MLE is a classical result in statistics and forms one of the cornerstones of parametric inference. Under suitable regularity conditions, it is well known that the properly normalized estimation error
\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})
converges in distribution to a Gaussian random variable whose variance is determined by the Fisher information
[
14
,
24
,
16
]
. This CLT result can be extended to a large class of estimators known as M-estimator and L-estimators; the interested reader is referred to classical text such as
[
22
,
17
]
.

The entropic CLT for the sample mean was first studied by
[
18
]
and later considerably generalized by
[
6
]
. There has also been recent activity around finding the rates of convergence and the interested reader is referred to
[
2
,
15
,
19
,
8
,
13
,
10
]
and references therein.
Besides the sample mean, the entropic CLT is also known to hold for all central order statistics
[
11
]
.

Recent work in learning theory has studied the Kullback–Leibler divergence of MLEs, especially monotonicity properties
[
23
,
21
]
. However, those works consider KL divergence over the sample space between the distributions induced by the estimated and true parameters. This differs from our goal, which is to establish a stronger mode of convergence for the distribution of the MLE itself.

II
Problem Definition and Some Preliminaries

In this section we set up the required technical preliminaries. We will make a number of definitions and assumptions here that are standard in the existing literature/results regarding the asymptotic normality of the MLE. The more nonstandard assumptions that we make regarding the tail behavior of the score and the smoothness of the statistical model we enumerate explicitly in Section
II-A
.

We assume the density
f(\cdot|\theta)
has common support at each
\theta
(or equivalently we just assume
f(x|\theta)>0
, since we can ignore parts of the sample space with zero probability density). We consider a parameter space
\Theta
that is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}
and that has a compact closure
K
with
|\theta|<B
for all
\theta\in K
. Let

\displaystyle L_{n}(\theta)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f(X_{i}|\theta)

(1)

be the normalized log-likelihood. Let
\widehat{\theta}_{n}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be a maximum likelihood estimate of
\theta
from the data
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
in the sense that

\widehat{\theta}_{n}\in\arg\max_{\theta\in K}L_{n}(\theta).

(2)

We assume that
\log f(x|\theta)
is twice-differentiable as a function of
\theta
on the set
\Theta
, and that it is continuous on the closure
K
(for almost all
x\in\mathcal{X}
). By this continuity,
L_{n}(\theta)
achieves its maximum over
K
. Note that if
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
is in
\Theta
itself instead of just its closure, then it satisfies

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n})=0\;.

(3)

The key quantity that we seek to study is the normalized error given by

Z_{n}=\sqrt{I_{X}(\theta_{0})n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})\;,

(4)

where
I_{X}(\theta_{0})
is the Fisher information defined as

\displaystyle I_{X}(\theta_{0})=-\mathbb{E}_{X|\theta_{0}}\left[s_{\theta_{0}}^{\prime}(X)\right].

(5)

and
(\theta,x)\mapsto s_{\theta}(x)
is the
score function
given by

s_{\theta}(x)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log f(x|\theta).

(6)

The classical CLT for the maximum likelihood estimate can be stated as follows:

Z_{n}\xrightarrow{d}Z

(7)

as
n\to\infty
where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

We assume the Fisher information at this true parameter is nonzero.
Finally, for simplicity we will assume that the model
f(x|\theta)
is identifiable in the sense that
f(\cdot|\theta)=f(\cdot|\theta^{\prime})
only when
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
. The above assumptions are rather standard for the MLE setup.

II-A

Additional Regularity Conditions

We now collect the additional assumptions used to upgrade the classical distributional CLT to convergence in relative entropy. Assumption
1
gives the uniform concentration needed for exponential consistency. Assumptions
1
and
2
control the second-order Taylor expansion of the likelihood and yield the high-moment bounds. Assumption
3
is used only in the final step, where we pass from a smoothed estimator
\tilde{Z}_{n}
back to
Z_{n}
.

All probabilistic conditions below are imposed under the true law
P_{\theta_{0}}
.

Assumption 1
(Sub-Gaussian Lipschitz envelope for the log-likelihood)

There exists a nonnegative function
H:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that, for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in K
and all
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\left|\log f(x|\theta)-\log f(x|\theta^{\prime})\right|\leq H(x)|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|.

(8)

Moreover,
H(X)
is sub-Gaussian under
P_{\theta_{0}}
.

Remark 1

By the mean-value theorem, one may take

H(x)\leq\sup_{u\in\Theta}|s_{u}(x)|

(9)

whenever the right-hand side is finite. Additionally, note that Assumption 1 implies that
s_{\theta}(X)
is sub-Gaussian.

Assumption 2
(Sub-exponential control of the score derivative)

There exists a nonnegative function
\bar{H}(X)
such that, for all
\theta\in K
and all
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\left|s^{\prime}_{\theta_{0}}(x)-s^{\prime}_{\theta}(x)\right|\leq\bar{H}(x)|\theta-\theta_{0}|.

(10)

Moreover, both
s^{\prime}_{\theta_{0}}(X)
and
\bar{H}(X)
are sub-exponential under
P_{\theta_{0}}
.

Assumption 3
(Smoothness for the normalized estimator)

Let
p_{n}
denote the density of
Z_{n}
. Either

1.

there exist constants
N_{0}<\infty
and
I_{0}<\infty
such that

\sup_{n\geq N_{0}}\int\frac{(p_{n}^{\prime}(z))^{2}}{p_{n}(z)}\,dz\leq I_{0}\;,

(11)

or,

2.

there exist constants
N_{0}
and
R
such that
|p^{\prime}_{n}(z)|\leq R
uniformly in
z
and
n\geq N_{0}
.

II-B

Remarks on Regularity Conditions

The MLE representation in (
37
) is not, in general, a sum of independent or weakly dependent random variables. Thus, the usual convolution and entropy-monotonicity methods for entropic CLTs do not apply directly. Instead, we use a method-of-moments approach. Assumptions
1
and
2
yield exponential consistency and uniform moment bounds for
Z_{n}
. More specifically, using Assumptions
1
and
2
, we can get convergence of
Z_{n}
to
Z
in terms moments.
These two assumptions are also sufficient to prove the entropic CLT for the smoothed estimator

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{n}=\sqrt{1-\epsilon}\,Z_{n}+\sqrt{\epsilon}\,Z.

(12)

We note that, without the additional Assumption
3
, one cannot in general upgrade the convergence of
Z_{n}
to convergence in relative entropy. The reason is that the MLE is defined over the compact set
K
in (
2
), and may place positive probability on the boundary of
K
. Consequently, the distribution of
Z_{n}
may contain both continuous and discrete components. In this case,
Z_{n}
is not absolutely continuous with respect to the limiting Gaussian random variable
Z
, and hence
D(Z_{n}\|Z)=\infty
.

Therefore, entropic normality of
Z_{n}
itself requires an additional regularity condition. Assumption
3
is introduced precisely to rule out such singular behavior, for example discrete atoms in the distribution of
Z_{n}
. While this assumption may be difficult to verify when the MLE has no closed-form expression, it is not needed if one only seeks entropic normality of the smoothed
\widetilde{Z}_{n}
.

II-C

Examples of Maximum Likelihood

If
f(\cdot|\theta)
belongs to an exponential family, then the MLE is the sample mean of the sufficient statistic
[
4
]
. Hence, the entropic CLTs of
[
18
,
6
]
already cover this case. Relatedly, the Pitman–Koopman–Darmois theorem
[
17
]
roughly says that exponential families are the only distributions for which the MLE is a function of a sample mean.

We now list a few examples beyond exponential family that will satisfy our regularity conditions:

•

Pearson Type IV Location Family:
For this family the parent distribution is given by

f(x|\theta)\propto\left(1+\frac{(x-\theta)^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{-m}{\mathrm{e}}^{\nu\arctan\frac{x-\theta}{\sigma}}

(13)

where
\sigma>0
,
m>\tfrac{1}{2}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
are fixed, and
\theta
is the unknown location parameter
[
1
]
. This family includes a large number of distribution such as Cauchy (
\nu=0,m=1)
and student-
t
distribution (
\nu=0,m=\frac{\kappa+1}{2}
where
\kappa
is degree of freedom). The consistency and asymptotic normality of this family of distributions were considered in
[
20
]
; see also
[
3
]
for the study of the MLE of the Cauchy distribution. The score and its derivative for this family is given by

\displaystyle s_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2m(x-\theta)}{\sigma^{2}+(x-\theta)^{2}}-\frac{\nu\sigma}{\sigma^{2}+(x-\theta)^{2}},

(14)

\displaystyle s^{\prime}_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2m((x-\theta)^{2}-\sigma^{2})-2\nu\sigma(x-\theta)}{(\sigma^{2}+(x-\theta)^{2})^{2}},

(15)

and are both bounded functions. In particular, as shown in
[
5
]
, the sub-Gaussianity of the Lipschtiz constant follows since

H(X)\leq\frac{m+|\nu|}{\sigma}

(16)

Finally, since the score
s_{\theta}(x)
is a bounded rational function of
x-\theta
whose numerator and denominator are polynomials of finite degree, its second derivative with respect to
\theta
is also uniformly bounded in
x
.

•

Logistic:

f(x|\theta)=\frac{e^{-(x-\theta)}}{\bigl(1+e^{-(x-\theta)}\bigr)^{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(17)

The score and its derivatives are

\displaystyle s_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=\tanh\left(\frac{x-\theta}{2}\right),

(18)

\displaystyle s_{\theta}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{sech}^{2}\left(\frac{x-\theta}{2}\right),

(19)

where both the score and its derivative are bounded. In particular, as shown in
[
5
]
, the sub-Gaussianity of the Lipschtiz constant follows since

H(X)\leq 1.

(20)

Similarly, it can be shown that the second derivative of the score function is also bounded.

•

Cauchy Model with Unknown Scale:

f(x|\theta)=\frac{1}{\pi\theta}\frac{1}{1+(x/\theta)^{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(21)

The score and its derivative are given by

\displaystyle s_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}-\theta^{2}}{\theta(\theta^{2}+x^{2})}

(22)

\displaystyle s_{\theta}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}+\frac{2(\theta^{2}-x^{2})}{(\theta^{2}+x^{2})^{2}}

(23)

As shown in
[
5
]
, the sub-Gaussianity of the Lipschitz constant follows since

H(X)\leq\frac{1}{\theta_{\min}},

(24)

where
\theta_{\min}>0
is the minimum value of
\theta
on
K
, which we assume to be compact. A similar bound applies to the second derivative, the score function.

This Cauchy example can be generalized to the Pearson VII scale family of distributions where the score function and its derivative would also be bounded.

IV-A
1
Consistency and a Uniform Law of Large Numbers

Let
L(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}[\log f(X|\theta)]
which is maximized at
\theta=\theta_{0}
by the non-negativity of KL. This maximum is unique assuming (as we do) that the model is identifiable. We now consider a uniform law of large large numbers result to compare
L(\theta)
and
L_{n}(\theta)
, with explicit bounds on the probability of deviation.

By the Lipschitz assumption,

\displaystyle L_{n}(\theta)-L_{n}(\theta_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(X_{i})|\theta-\theta_{0}|

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2B}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(X_{i})

(29)

which is sub-Gaussian with parameter
O(1/n)
.

For a small
\varepsilon>0
, consider the set
K^{\prime}=K\setminus\mathsf{Ball}(\theta_{0},\varepsilon)
. Let
D=\max_{\theta\in K^{\prime}}L(\theta)
and note
\Delta=L(\theta_{0})-D>0
by the continuity of
L
and identifiability. Again, by the continuity of
L_{n}
on compact
K^{\prime}
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that
|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|<\delta\implies|L_{n}(\theta)-L_{n}(\theta^{\prime})|<\Delta/2
for
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
. Importantly, we can pick
\delta
to be independent of
n
and the particular samples
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
with high probability (i.e., at least probability
1-e^{-nc}
for some
c>0
) by using the tail bound on the Lipschitz constant, so we will assume this is the case without loss of generality. Consider the open cover
\{\mathsf{Ball}(\theta,\delta)\;:\;\theta\in K^{\prime}\}
of
K^{\prime}
. By compactness there exists a finite subcover with corresponding ball centers
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}
.

Using a sub-Gaussian tail bound and a union bound over
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Pr}\bigg(\big|(L_{n}(\theta_{i})-L(\theta_{i}))-(L_{n}(\theta_{0})-L(\theta_{0}))\big|>\Delta/2

\displaystyle\;\text{ for some }i=1,\ldots,N\bigg)<2N\exp(-cn\Delta^{2})\;.

(30)

For any
\theta\in K^{\prime}
, there exists a
\theta_{i}
for some
i=1,\ldots,N
with
|\theta-\theta_{i}|<\delta
and therefore
|L_{n}(\theta)-L_{n}(\theta_{i})|<\Delta/2
. Suppose we are in the complement of the event in (
IV-A1
). Then

\displaystyle\big|(L_{n}(\theta_{i})-L(\theta_{i}))-(L_{n}(\theta_{0})-L(\theta_{0}))\big|\leq\Delta/2

(31)

for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, and

\displaystyle(L_{n}(\theta)-L_{n}(\theta_{i}))+(L_{n}(\theta_{i})-L(\theta_{i}))

\displaystyle-(L_{n}(\theta_{0})-L(\theta_{0}))

(32)

\displaystyle<\Delta

(33)

\displaystyle\leq L(\theta_{0})-L(\theta_{i})

(34)

so that
L_{n}(\theta)-L_{n}(\theta_{0})<0
(for arbitrary
\theta\in K^{\prime}
) and
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
cannot be in
K^{\prime}
. Thus

\displaystyle|\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0}|<\varepsilon\text{ with prob. at least }1-2N\exp(-cn\Delta^{2})\;.

(35)

The key thing that differentiates this consistency from the usual results is the exponentially decreasing probability of deviation in (
35
). This will be needed to get convergence in moments (for any arbitrarily large moment) below.

IV-A
2
Convergence in Distribution

By the MVT we have

L_{n}^{\prime}(\widehat{\theta}_{n})=L_{n}^{\prime}(\theta_{0})+L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{c}_{n})(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})

(36)

where the random variable
\theta^{c}_{n}
lies between
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
and
\theta_{0}
. Using consistency
L^{\prime}_{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n})=0
with probability approaching one, so we are able to ignore this term.
Now note that

\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})=-\frac{\sqrt{n}L_{n}^{\prime}(\theta_{0})}{L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{c}_{n})}\;.

(37)

The numerator

\sqrt{n}L_{n}^{\prime}(\theta_{0})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{\theta_{0}}(X_{i})

(38)

converges in distribution to normal with zero mean and variance given by
I_{X}(\theta_{0})=\mathsf{var}(s_{\theta_{0}}(X))
.
The denominator can be controlled as follows:

\displaystyle\big|L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{c}_{n})-L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{0})\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{\theta_{n}^{c}}^{\prime}(X_{i})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{\theta_{0}}^{\prime}(X_{i})\right|

(39)

\displaystyle\leq\big|\theta_{n}^{c}-\theta_{0}\big|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{H}(X_{i})\;,

(40)

where the last bound follows from the assumption that
s_{\theta}^{\prime}(x)
has the Lipschitz constant
\bar{H}(x)
.
From (
40
) and by the law of large numbers and consistency, we have that
L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{c}_{n})
converges in probability to the same value that
L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{0})
does, which is
\mathbb{E}\left[s_{\theta_{0}}^{\prime}(X_{i})\right]=-I_{X}(\theta_{0})
. Note that from the differentiability assumptions we have made the two definitions of the Fisher information coincide

-\mathbb{E}\left[s_{\theta_{0}}^{\prime}(X_{i})\right]=\mathsf{var}(s_{\theta_{0}}(X)).

(41)

The proof of convergence in distribution is concluded by using a standard argument via Slutsky’s theorem
[
7
]
this implies
\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})
converges in distribution to
\mathcal{N}(0,I_{X}(\theta_{0})^{-1})
.

IV-A
3
Convergence in Moments

Consider the
m
th moment of
\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})|^{m}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{1}}|\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})|^{m}\mathrm{d}P_{\theta_{0}}^{n}

\displaystyle+\int_{E_{2}}\left|\frac{\sqrt{n}L_{n}^{\prime}(\theta_{0})}{L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{c}_{n})}\right|^{m}\mathrm{d}P_{\theta_{0}}^{n}

(42)

where we have split the integral into a sum of integrals over disjoint events
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. We take
E_{1}
to be the “bad” set, that is, the set of outcomes
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
such that
|\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0}|\geq\varepsilon
or
|-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\theta^{c}_{n})-I_{X}(\theta_{0})|\geq 3\mathbb{E}[\bar{H}(X)]\varepsilon
. Following from (
35
), the first of these conditions happens with probability that is exponentially decaying in
n
. If this is not the case (i.e.,
|\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0}|<\varepsilon
), then

\displaystyle|-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\theta^{c}_{n})-I_{X}(\theta_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq|L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\theta^{c}_{n})-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\theta_{0})|+|-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\theta_{0})-I_{X}(\theta_{0})|

(43)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{H}(X_{i})+|-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\theta_{0})-I_{X}(\theta_{0})|

(44)

where each term in (
44
) is less than
3\mathbb{E}[\bar{H}(X)]\varepsilon/2
with probability decaying exponentially in
n
(using the sub-exponential tail bounds assumed in the theorem).

Thus
\mathsf{Pr}(E_{1})
also decays with probability exponentially decreasing in
n
and

\int_{E_{1}}|\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})|^{m}\mathrm{d}P_{\theta_{0}}^{n}\leq(2B\sqrt{n})^{m}\mathsf{Pr}(E_{1})\to 0

(45)

as
n\to\infty
.

The sub-Gaussianity of the Lipschitz constant
H(X)
immediately implies a similar moment bound on the score, i.e.,

\int_{E_{2}}\left|\sqrt{n}L_{n}^{\prime}(\theta_{0})\right|^{m}\mathrm{d}P_{\theta_{0}}^{n}\leq(\sigma\sqrt{m})^{m}\;.

(46)

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, the denominator
L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\theta^{c}_{n})
becomes close to
I_{X}(\theta_{0})
in the event
E_{2}
, so for small enough
\varepsilon
the integral over this event satisfies

\int_{E_{2}}\left|\frac{\sqrt{n}L_{n}^{\prime}(\theta_{0})}{L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\theta^{c}_{n})}\right|^{m}\mathrm{d}P_{\theta_{0}}^{n}\leq\left(\frac{2}{I_{X}(\theta_{0})}\sigma\sqrt{m}\right)^{m}\;.

(47)

Noting that

\sup_{n}\;(2B\sqrt{n})^{m}e^{-cn}=O(m^{\frac{m}{2}})\;,

(48)

we see the whole integral satisfies

\mathbb{E}|\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})|^{m}\leq(C\sqrt{m})^{m}

(49)

for sufficiently large
C
, and therefore
Z_{n}
itself is also sub-Gaussian.

Using uniform integrability, the moment bound in (
49
) along with the convergence in distribution implies convergence in moments, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}|Z_{n}|^{m}\to\mathbb{E}|Z|^{m}
for all
m
.

Proof of Theorem
2

We have the following existing bound on the KL divergence from a standard normal density
\phi
; see

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we derived an entropic version of the Central Limit Theorem (CLT) for the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE), establishing a stronger mode of convergence than the classical distributional CLT. A natural direction for future work is to extend this line of reasoning beyond MLEs. One promising avenue—likely to benefit from similar technical tools—is the study of M-estimators, where the score function is replaced by a general estimating function with comparable smoothness and boundedness properties.

Another natural extension is the development of multivariate versions of the results presented here. Since the entropic and Fisher-information–based techniques used in this work are inherently vectorial, we expect that the core arguments should carry over with minimal conceptual modification.

A closely related line of research investigates the monotonicity properties of estimators. For example, it is known that the sample mean satisfies a monotonicity property: the mapping

n\mapsto D\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\,\bigg\|\,Z\right)

is non-increasing
[
12
]
. In contrast, central order statistics, while obeying the entropic CLT, do not obey a similar monotonicity property
[
11
]
. It would therefore be interesting to investigate whether and under what conditions an analogous phenomenon can be established in the context of the MLE.
