Executive Summary of CRADA Work:
 In this project, a genetically modified Saccharomyces cerevisiae was used to produce Histamine
via fermentation of dextrose. Zymergen provided a tech transfer document based on their 1 L studies,
where they observed a titer of 0.9 g/L with this strain. The ABPDU staff performed two bench scale
campaigns, with four 2 L reactors in each campaign, to carry out tech transfer and process optimization
studies. Optimization was pursued by varying initial glucose concentration, feed initiation conditions, feed
strategies, vitamin supplementation, inoculum size, etc. We chose a 2 L process condition, with 4%
inoculum volume, continuous feed initiated after complete ethanol depletion, that yielded the most
promising titers (2.41 g/L) for scale up to 300 L.
 ABPDU staff was able to generate a substantially higher histamine titer of 3.57 g/L at the 300 L
scale. This value represented the highest product titer across all fermentations carried out in this project.
The 300 L campaign also included culture clarification via centrifugation and tangential flow filtration.
Product concentration and purification were also pursued but in a separate project and accordingly not
included in this report. The overall aim of this project was to achieve at least the tech transfer titer of
Histamine, 0.9 g/L, in a 300 L fermentation followed by successful separation of the product from
microbial biomass. We were able to achieve 3.57 g/L titer at the 300 L and obtained 235.8 L of culture
broth after separation from microbial biomass at a mass balance of 98%. As such, we were able to
generate 980.5 g of Histamine that is present in 235.8 L of cell-free culture broth.

Summary of Research Results:
 A number of challenges and issues were identified during the tech transfer and optimization of
the fermentation process. Summarized below are a few highlights:

Seed Media:
 The best performing seed 2 media was obtained by sterilizing via filtration. Autoclaving or Steam-
In-Place (SIP) of media led to mitigated growth of yeast compared to that in filter-sterilized media.
However, scaling this filtration process to produce 10 L of filter-sterilized media seemed challenging at
the 300 L scale. Future efforts should focus on investigating alternative and more scalable media
sterilization methods that are relevant to commercial-scale production. Also, we observed that glucose
was consumed faster than the expected rate in Seed 2, leading to some starvation of the culture. We
recommended reducing Seed 2 fermentation time so the culture can be used for inoculation when they
are healthy and growing in exponential phase.

Bench Scale Studies (2 L):

 Best results at bench scale were achieved in the culture in A6 reactor of Campaign #2, which
introduced the following process modifications to tech transfer process:
 1) Administration of a 2X feed media concentration with a 2X continuous feed profile. We adjusted
 the batch to feed volume ratio from 0.4:0.6 to 0.7:0.3 to accommodate this change.
 2) Reduction of initial glucose concentration from 40 g.L-1 to 20 g.L-1
 3) Feed phase initiation tied to complete depletion of glucose and ethanol
Using the conditions listed above, a maximum product titer of 2.41 g/L was achieved. It outperformed any
other bench scale fermentation in this project and exceeded the maximum titer in the tech transfer
document by 168 % (0.9 g/L). Based on the striking performance of this fermentation process, we selected
it for scale up to 300 L pilot scale in the third campaign of this project.

Pilot Scale Study (300 L):
 We observed that the 300 L fermentation reached the highest reported titer of 3.51 g/L and never
reached a plateau. We believe that the fermentation process was not exploited to its fullest potential. We
recommend keeping future processes running as long as the overall process conditions look promising.
This requires a number of measures:
 A. Instant availability of key process parameter measurements, such as optical density and product
 titer for quick assessment of the state of fermentation at the time
 B. Sufficient quantities of prepared and sterilized feed media
 C. Provision of extended continuous feed profiles with increasing pump rates beyond regular times
 D. Invoke the possibility of draining the reactor in case of reaching maximum capacity of the
 fermenter
 The culture broth from the 300 L reactor was harvested with Alfa-laval centrifuge in line with a
Millipore Tangential Flow Filtration Unit. During this harvest process, the supernatant was divided into
two even fractions of roughly 118 kg, which, interestingly, exhibited different product concentrations of
4.93 g/L (Container 1) and 2.12 g/L (Container 2). This aspect needs to be further investigated.
 The ABPDU introduced a number of modifications to the process elucidated in the tech transfer
document that led to a 4X increase in product titer resulting in almost a kilogram of Histamine. Considering
that the overall objective of downstream purification process is to obtain 10 g Histamine at 90% purity,
we were able to provide substantial amounts of Histamine to achieve this goal.
