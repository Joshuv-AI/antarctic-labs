Classification of skew left braces with additive group isomorphic to the infinite dihedral group
Akihide Hanaki, Yuto Sakata, Hiroki Yoshino

Abstract
. We classify all skew left braces with additive group isomorphic to the infinite dihedral group. There are ten isomorphism classes.



1.
Introduction

Let
A
be a set with two binary operations

(a,b)\mapsto a\cdot b
and
(a,b)\mapsto a\circ b
.
The triple
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is called a
skew left brace
if

(A,\cdot)
and
(A,\circ)
are groups and

a\circ(b\cdot c)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ c)

holds for all
a,b,c\in A
,
where
a^{-1}
is the inverse element of
a

with respect to the operation of
(A,\cdot)
.
The groups
(A,\cdot)
and
(A,\circ)
are called the
additive group

and the
multiplicative group
of the skew left brace, respectively.
We remark that the additive group is not necessarily an abelian group.
(If
(A,\cdot)
is abelian, then
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is called a
left brace
.)
We often abbreviate
a\cdot b
as
ab
.
The identity element of
(A,\cdot)
is also the identity element of
(A,\circ)
.

Let
G
be a group.
It is natural to ask how many skew left braces with additive (multiplicative) group
G
exist.
In this direction, there are many results for finite groups
G

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
For the infinite cyclic group
G
as an additive group,
it is known that there are exactly two isomorphism
classes of skew left braces
[
10
, Proof of Proposition 6]
.
In this paper, we determine all isomorphism classes of skew left braces
with additive group isomorphic to the infinite dihedral group
D_{\infty}
.
There are ten isomorphism classes of such skew left braces
(Theorem
3.1
).
This answers a problem raised by Vendramin

[
11
, after Problem 2.27]
.

It is known that skew left braces with additive group
G
correspond to
regular subgroups of the holomorph of
G
, and they correspond to
maps
\gamma:A\to\operatorname{Aut}(A,\cdot)
with some property

[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
,
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
.
We will consider these maps to classify skew left braces.
Our method is very simple,
but it requires a lot of case analysis and calculations.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Holomorphs and
\gamma
-maps

Let
G
be a group, and consider the automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
.
The semidirect product of
G
and
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is called
the
holomorph
of
G
, and denoted by
\operatorname{Hol}(G)
.
Namely,

\operatorname{Hol}(G)=G\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(G)=\{(a,f)\mid a\in G,\ f\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\},

and the product is defined by

(a,f)(b,g)=(af(b),fg).

The holomorph acts on
G
from the left by

(a,f)x=af(x)
for
(a,f)\in\operatorname{Hol}(G)
and
x\in G
.

Let
G
be a group.
A map
\gamma:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is called a
\gamma
-map
on
G
if

\gamma(a\gamma(a)(b))=\gamma(a)\gamma(b)

holds for all
a,b\in G
.
The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
acts on the set of all
\gamma
-maps by

\gamma^{\varphi}(a)=\varphi^{-1}\gamma(\varphi(a))\varphi\quad(a\in G,\ \varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)).

We define an equivalence relation
\sim
by the above action.

Now we are ready to give a correspondence between skew left braces
with additive group
G
and some other objects

[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
,
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group.
There are bijections between the following sets:

(1)

The set of isomorphism classes of skew left braces
with additive group
G
.

(2)

The set of conjugacy classes of regular subgroups of
\operatorname{Hol}(G)
.

(3)

The
\sim
equivalence classes of
\gamma
-maps on
G
.

For any
\gamma
-map
\gamma:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(G,\cdot)
,
the binary operation

a\circ b=a\cdot\gamma(a)(b)\qquad(a,b\in G)

makes
(G,\cdot,\circ)
into a skew left brace.

2.2.
The infinite dihedral group
D_{\infty}

The infinite dihedral group is

D_{\infty}=\langle x,y\mid y^{2}=1,\ yx=x^{-1}y\rangle.

We denote
x^{a}y^{s}
by
(a,s)
, and so

D_{\infty}=\{(a,s)\mid a\in\mathbb{Z},\ s\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\},

where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, with operation

(a,\ s)(b,\ t)=(a+(-1)^{s}b,\ s+t).

For the automorphism group of
D_{\infty}
,
we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

We have

\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\infty})=\{[b,t]\mid b\in\mathbb{Z},\ t\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\}
,
where

[b,t](a,s)=((-1)^{t}a+bs,s).

The following equations hold.

(1)

[a,s][b,t]=[a+(-1)^{s}b,s+t]
,

(2)

[a,s]^{-1}=[(-1)^{s+1}a,s]
,

(3)

[b,t]^{-1}[a,s][b,t]=[(-1)^{t}a+(-1)^{t+1}b+(-1)^{s+t}b,s]
.

We remark that
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
can appear in
the first entry of
[b,t](a,s)=((-1)^{t}a+bs,s)
.
The elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}
are naturally embedded into
\mathbb{Z}
.
We will use similar notation throughout this paper.We use the similar notation throughout this paper.

3.
Main results – skew left braces with additive group
D_{\infty}

We classify all skew left braces with additive group
D_{\infty}
.
By Proposition
2.1
,
we classify equivalence classes of
\gamma
-maps on
D_{\infty}
.
We determine all
\gamma
-maps, and then we consider their equivalence.
Consequently we will get ten isomorphism classes of such skew left braces.
The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace with
(A,\cdot)=D_{\infty}
.
Then one of the following statements holds.
Conversely, the following ten operations define
non-isomorphic skew left braces.

(1)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=(a,s)(b,t)=(a+(-1)^{s}b,s+t)

(the trivial skew brace)

(2,0)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=(a+(-1)^{s}b-2at,s+t)

(2,1)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=(a+(-1)^{s}b-2at+(-1)^{s}st,s+t)

(3)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=(a+b-2at,s+t)

(4,0)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=(a+b,s+t)

(4,1)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=(a+b+(-1)^{s}st,s+t)

(5,0)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=\begin{cases}(a+(-1)^{s}b,s+t),&\text{if $a$ is even},\\
(a+(-1)^{s+1}b,s+t),&\text{if $a$ is odd}.\end{cases}

(5,1)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=\begin{cases}(a+(-1)^{s}b,s+t),&\text{if $a$ is even},\\
(a+(-1)^{s+1}b+(-1)^{s}2t,s+t),&\text{if $a$ is odd}.\end{cases}

(6,0)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=\begin{cases}(a+b-2at,s+t),&\text{if $a+s$ is even},\\
(a-b-2at+2t,s+t),&\text{if $a+s$ is odd}.\end{cases}

(6,1)

(a,s)\circ(b,t)=\begin{cases}(a-2at+(-1)^{s}b+4st,s+t),&\text{if $a$ is even},\\
(a-2(a-1)t+(-1)^{s+1}b+(-1)^{s}2t,s+t),&\text{if $a$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Theorem
3.1
will be proved by applying
a\circ b=a\gamma(a)(b)
to
the classification of
\gamma
-maps (Theorem
5.2
).
For the structure of the multiplicative groups
(A,\circ)
,
we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

The multiplicative groups
(A,\circ)
of the skew left braces

(A,\cdot,\circ)
in Theorem
3.1
are isomorphic to

•

\mathbb{Z}
for (2,1), (4,1),

•

\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for (2,0), (4,0),

•

D_{\infty}=\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for (1), (3), (5,0), (6,0).

•

D_{\infty}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}=(\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for (5,1), (6,1).

Proof.

Write
\mathbb{Z}=\langle x\rangle
.
For (2.1) or (4,1),
x\mapsto(0,1)
gives an isomorphism.

Write
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle x\rangle\times\langle y\rangle
.
For (2,0) or (4,0),
x\mapsto(1,0)
,
y\mapsto(0,1)

gives an isomorphism.

Write
D_{\infty}=\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle x\rangle\rtimes\langle y\rangle
.
For (1) or (3),
x\mapsto(1,0)
,
y\mapsto(0,1)
gives an isomorphism.
For (5,0),
x\mapsto(1,1)
,
y\mapsto(0,1)
gives an isomorphism.
For (6,0),
x\mapsto(0,1)
,
y\mapsto(1,0)
gives an isomorphism.

Write
D_{\infty}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}=(\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}=(\langle x\rangle\rtimes\langle y\rangle)\times\langle z\rangle
.
For (5,1),
x\mapsto(2,0)
,
y\mapsto(1,0)
,
z\mapsto(1,1)

gives an isomorphism.
For (6,1),
x\mapsto(0,1)
,
y\mapsto(1,0)
,
z\mapsto(2,1)

gives an isomorphism.
∎
