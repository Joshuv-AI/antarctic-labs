1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the set of primes, and let
\mathbb{P}_{k}
denote the set of products of at most
k
primes.
It is well known that, for any
A>0
and
X>2
, one has

\sum_{m+n=N}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)=\mathfrak{S}(N)N+O\left(\frac{X}{(\ln{X})^{A}}\right)

for all but
O(X(\ln{X})^{-A})
integers
N\in(X/2,X]
. Here,
\mathfrak{S}(N)
is the singular series, defined by

\mathfrak{S}(N)=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{c_{p}(N)}{\varphi(p)^{2}}\right)=\sum_{q}\frac{\mu(q)^{2}}{\varphi(q)^{2}}c_{q}(N).

A Bombieri–Vinogradov type theorem for
\mathbb{P}\cap(N-\mathbb{P})
has been studied in the following form:

(1)

\sum_{d_{1}\leq D_{1}}\sum_{d_{2}\leq D_{2}}\lambda^{(1)}_{d_{1}}\lambda^{(2)}_{d_{2}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+n=N\\
m\equiv l_{1}\bmod d_{1}\\
n\equiv l_{2}\bmod d_{2}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)-M_{0}\right)\ll\frac{X}{(\ln{X})^{A}}

for almost all
N
, where
l_{1},l_{2}
are non-zero integers,
(\lambda^{(1)}_{d}),(\lambda^{(2)}_{d})
are divisor bounded sequences and
M_{0}=M_{0}(d_{1},l_{1},d_{2},l_{2},N)
is a suitable approximation of the sum over
m,n
.

The case
D_{1}=X^{1/2}
,
D_{2}=1
follows from the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem and a standard circle method argument. See also
[
8
]
,
[
15
]
.
With a minor adaptation to almost-prime weights, this implies that, for almost all positive integers
N\equiv 4\bmod 6
, the equation
p_{1}+p_{2}=N
,
p_{1}+2\in\mathbb{P}_{2}
is solvable.

Tolev’s argument
[
17
]
yields an estimate of the form (
1
) with
D_{1}=X^{1/2},D_{2}=X^{1/3}
. He applied this to show that, for almost all positive
N\equiv 4\bmod 6
, the equation
p_{1}+p_{2}=N,p_{1}+2\in\mathbb{P}_{5},p_{2}+2\in\mathbb{P}_{7}
is solvable.

Matomäki’s argument
[
13
]
yields an estimate of the form (
1
) with
D_{1}=X^{1/2},D_{2}=X^{1/2}
, under the assumption that one of the sequences
\lambda^{(i)}_{d}
is well-factorable. As an application, she proved the solvability of
p_{1}+p_{2}=N,p_{1}+2\in\mathbb{P}_{2},p_{2}+2\in\mathbb{P}_{7}
for almost all
N\equiv 4\bmod 6
.

A related result for
p_{1}+p_{2}=N,p_{1}+2\in\mathbb{P}_{2},p_{2}+2\in\mathbb{P}_{2}
can be obtained, by different methods, from the work of Matomäki and Shao
[
11
]
.
See also
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
.

One can extend (
1
) to the case where
l_{i}
is allowed to depend on
d_{i}
for each
i=1,2
and
D_{1}=X^{1/2},D_{2}=X^{1/3}
, with little additional effort. See, for example,
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Thus, one may obtain similar results for the equation
p_{1}+p_{2}=N,f_{1}(p_{1})\in\mathbb{P}_{r_{1}},f_{2}(p_{2})\in\mathbb{P}_{r_{2}}
for suitable low-complexity functions
f_{i}
such as polynomials and suitable
r_{1},r_{2}\geq 2
.

The basic strategy in the proofs of the above-mentioned results is to rewrite the contribution of
\Lambda
in (
1
) as

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{d_{1}\leq D_{1}}\lambda_{d_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq X\\
m\equiv l_{1}\bmod d_{1}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)e(m\alpha)\right)\left(\sum_{d_{2}\leq D_{2}}\lambda_{d_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv l_{2}\bmod d_{2}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)e(n\alpha)\right)e(-N\alpha)d\alpha,

where
e(\cdot)=e^{2\pi i\cdot}
and
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, and then to use major- and minor-arc estimates for

\sum_{d_{i}\leq D_{i}}\lambda_{d_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv l_{i}\bmod d_{i}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)e(n\alpha).

Thus, in this approach the residue classes
l_{i}
may depend on
d_{i}
, but not on
N
; this independence is used, in particular, when applying Bessel’s inequality over the minor arc.

In this paper, we consider (
1
) in the case where
l_{i}
may also depend on
N
.
We prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1/6)
, let
A>0
and let
k\geq 0
.
Let
w_{1},w_{2}
be smooth functions supported on
[\eta,1]
for some
\eta>0
.

For all integers
N\in(X/2,X]
with
O(X(\ln{X})^{-A})
exceptions, we have

\sum_{d\leq X^{\theta}}\tau(d)^{k}\sup_{l:(d,l(N-l))=1}|R_{d,l}(N)|\ll\frac{X}{(\ln{X})^{A}}

where

R_{d,l}(N)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+n=N\\
m\equiv l\bmod d\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)w_{1}(m/X)w_{2}(n/X)-\frac{\mathfrak{S}(N)}{\psi_{N}(d)}\sum_{m+n=N}w_{1}(m/X)w_{2}(n/X)

and
\psi_{N}
is defined by

\psi_{N}(d)=d\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|d\\
p|N\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|d\\
p\nmid N\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right).

Here and throughout, we use the convention that the supremum over an empty set is
0
.

Indeed, we prove a more general version in which one of the
\Lambda
factors is replaced by a more general sequence such as the indicator function of rough numbers. See Theorem
3.1
.

By a slight modification of the result of Maier-Pomerance
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
concerning the distribution of generalized twin primes in arithmetic progressions, one can establish such a result with
\theta<c
for some absolute constant
c>0
. Their argument is based on the work of Montgomery and Vaughan
[
16
]
on the bounds for the exceptional set in the Goldbach conjecture.
A computation by the author using standard zero-density estimates for Dirichlet
L
-functions shows that one may take
c=\frac{10}{314}=0.0318\ldots
, although this value is probably not optimal.

We use methods that are different from those of
[
9
]
in both major and minor arcs.
We establish a new exponential sum estimate. See Theorem
2.1
. The exponent
1/6
in Theorem
1.1
comes from this estimate.
On the major arc, we use a mean value theorem of Choi and Kumchev
[
3
]
for Dirichlet polynomials, together with an argument of Maynard
[
14
]
.

As an application of Theorems
1.1
and
3.1
, we consider the solvability of the equations of the form
p_{1}+p_{2}=N,f(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{P}_{r}
for some function
f:\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
and
r\geq 2
.

Matomäki and Zúñiga-Alterman
[
12
]
showed that one can find
\mathbb{P}_{3}
if the original and the switched problem have level of distribution at least
0.267
.
We extend their result and show that one can find
\mathbb{P}_{4}
if the original and the switched problem have level of distribution at least
0.1635
. See also the Appendix for similar results for
\mathbb{P}_{k},2\leq k\leq 7
.
Since
1/6=0.166\ldots>0.1635
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A>0
and
X\geq 2
.
For all but
O(X(\ln{X})^{-A})
even integers
N\in(X/2,X]
, there exist primes
p_{1},p_{2}
such that
p_{1}+p_{2}=N
and
p_{1}-p_{2}+1
has at most
4
prime factors.

The numerical comparison in the Appendix indicates that the weights appearing in previous applications of the weighted sieve do not suffice for this purpose, since the resulting criteria remain above the level
1/6
. See the Appendix.

We also use Theorem
1.1
and Richert’s weighted sieve to obtain

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A>0
and
X\geq 2
.
For all but
O(X(\ln{X})^{-A})
integers
N\in(X/2,X]
with
6|N
, there exist primes
p_{1},p_{2}
such that
p_{1}+p_{2}=N
and
2p_{1}p_{2}+1
has at most
13
prime factors.

Note that the number
13
arises as
13=2\times 6+1
.

2.
Exponential sum estimate

In this section, we prove the following estimate for exponential sums.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
D\leq X
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
w:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function supported on
[0,1]
.
Let
\alpha\in[0,1],q\geq 1,(q,a)=1,|\alpha-a/q|\leq 1/q^{2}
. Then,

(2)

\sum_{d\sim D}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/{d\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)e((\alpha+b/d)n)w(n/X)\right|^{2}\ll X^{2+\varepsilon}\left(\frac{D}{q}+\frac{qD}{X}+D^{2}X^{-1/3}\right)

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
, a weaker form of (
2
) with
\mathbb{Z}/{d\mathbb{Z}}
replaced by
(\mathbb{Z}/{d\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
would suffice. However, the argument below treats both cases in the same way, so we state the estimate in the present form.
In this setting, the large sieve gives the bound
\ll X^{2}(\ln X)^{2}
when
D\leq X^{1/2}
, whereas Theorem
2.1
gives a sharper estimate when
q\in[X^{\varepsilon}D,X^{1-\varepsilon}D^{-1}]
and
D\leq X^{1/6-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

The following two lemmas are fundamental.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\|x\|
denote the distance from
x
to the nearest integer.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
w:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth, compactly supported function.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\varepsilon>0
. Then

(3)

\sum_{n}e(\alpha n)w(n/X)\ll X\#\{n^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}:|\alpha-n^{*}|\leq X^{\varepsilon-1}\}+X^{-100}

and

(4)

\sum_{n}e(\alpha n)w(n/X)\ll\frac{1}{\|\alpha\|}.

Here, the implicit constant depends only on
\varepsilon
and
\{\|w^{(j)}\|_{\infty}:j\leq\lceil 200/\varepsilon\rceil\}
.

Proof.

By the Poisson summation formula, we have

\sum_{n}e(\alpha n)w(n/X)=X\sum_{n^{*}}\hat{w}(X(n^{*}-\alpha)),

where
\hat{w}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(y)e(-xy)dy
. Integrating by parts
j
times, we see that

\hat{w}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi ix)^{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}w^{(j)}(y)e(-xy)dy\ll_{j}\|w^{(j)}\|_{\infty}|x|^{-j}\quad(x\neq 0)

and therefore

X\hat{w}(X(n^{*}-\alpha))\ll\frac{X}{(1+X|\alpha-n^{*}|)^{j}}

for any
j\geq 0
. Thus, the contribution from the terms with
|\alpha-n^{*}|>1/2
is
O(X^{-100})
.
If
|\alpha-n^{*}|\leq 1/2
, we take
j=1,\lceil 200/\varepsilon\rceil
. This yields (
3
) and (
4
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},q\geq 1,(a,q)=1,|\alpha-a/q|\leq 1/q^{2}
and
M,\delta>0
.

(5)

\#\{M<m\leq 2M,n\in\mathbb{Z}:|\alpha-n/m|\leq\delta\}\ll M\left(\frac{1}{q}+q\delta+\delta M\right)

Proof.

If
M\delta\geq 1/4
, then there are
O(M\delta)
integers
n
for each
m
, and thus the claim follows. Thus, we may suppose that
M\delta<1/4
. In this case, the left-hand side of (
5
) is bounded by

\#\{M<m\leq 2M:\|\alpha m\|\ll\delta M\}

since
n
is unique for each
m
. Write
m=qs+r
with
0<r\leq q
. Then
\|\alpha m\|=\|ar/q+(qs+r)\epsilon\|
, where
\epsilon=\alpha-a/q
.

If
M\leq q/4
, then
s=0
and
0<r\leq q/2
. Thus, we have

\|\alpha m\|\geq\|ar/q\|-|\epsilon|q/2\geq\|ar/q\|/2

and therefore, there are
O(q\delta M)
possible values of
r
. This gives (
5
).

If
M\geq q/4
, there are
O(M/q)
possible values of
s
, and for each
s
, there are
q\delta M+1
possible values of
r
. This gives (
5
).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
D,N\leq X
. Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
with
q\geq 1,(a,q)=1
and
|\alpha-a/q|\leq 1/q^{2}
. Then we have

(6)

\#\{n\sim N,m\in\mathbb{Z},d\sim D,1\leq b\leq d:|\alpha+b/d+m/n|\leq\delta\}\ll X^{\varepsilon}ND\left(\frac{1}{q}+q\delta+\delta ND\right)

for all
\delta>0
. Here, the implicit constant depends only on
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Since
\alpha+b/d\ll 1
, we may suppose that
m\ll n
.
For each
d,n
, let
g=(d,n)
and write
d=d^{\prime}g,n=n^{\prime}g
. For each
c\ll d^{\prime}n^{\prime}g
, we consider the equation

\frac{b}{d}+\frac{m}{n}=\frac{c}{d^{\prime}n^{\prime}g}

where
(b,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},b\ll d,m\ll n
. Since
bn^{\prime}\equiv c\bmod d^{\prime}
and
(d^{\prime},n^{\prime})=1
, there are
\ll g
possible values of
b
. For each
b
,
m
is unique. Thus the number of solutions to this equation is
\ll g
. Hence, the left-hand side of (
6
) is

\displaystyle\ll(\ln{X})\max_{G\ll\min(D,N)}G\#\{n^{\prime}\sim N/G,d^{\prime}\sim D/G,g\sim G,c\ll d^{\prime}n^{\prime}g:|\alpha+c/d^{\prime}n^{\prime}g|\leq\delta\},

\displaystyle\ll X^{\varepsilon}\max_{G\ll\min(D,N)}G\#\{m\sim ND/G,n\ll m:|\alpha+n/m|\leq\delta\}.

This and Lemma
2.3
complete the proof.
∎

We now prove Theorem
2.1
.
The proof is based on a straightforward application of the above lemmas.
A variant using large-sieve-type estimates might lead to an improved result.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

For any given
U,V\geq 1
, by Vaughan’s identity,
\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)e((\alpha+b/d)n)w(n/X)
can be written as a linear combination of
O((\ln{X})^{2})
sums of the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\sim M\end{subarray}}a_{m}e((\alpha+b/d)mn)w(mn/X),

where
M\leq UV
and
(a_{m})
is a sequence with
|a_{m}|\leq X^{o(1)}
, and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\sim M\\
n\sim N\end{subarray}}a_{m}b_{n}e((\alpha+b/d)mn)w(mn/X),

where
M\geq U,N\geq V,MN\asymp X
and
(a_{m}),(b_{n})
are sequences with
|a_{m}|,|b_{n}|\leq X^{o(1)}
. Thus, setting
U=V=X^{1/3}
, the theorem follows from the Type I bound

(7)

\sum_{d\sim D}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/{d\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\sim M\end{subarray}}a_{m}e((\alpha+b/d)mn)w(mn/X)\right|^{2}\ll X^{2+2\varepsilon}\left(\frac{D}{q}+\frac{qD}{X}+\frac{MD^{2}}{X}\right),

and the Type II bound

(8)

\sum_{d\sim D}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/{d\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\sim M\\
n\sim N\end{subarray}}a_{m}b_{n}e((\alpha+b/d)mn)w(mn/X)\right|^{2}\ll X^{2+2\varepsilon}\left(\frac{D}{q}+\frac{qD}{X}+\frac{MD^{2}}{X}+\frac{ND^{2}}{X}\right),

for any given
\varepsilon>0
,
M,N
with
MN\asymp X
and sequences
(a_{m}),(b_{n})
with
|a_{m}|,|b_{n}|\leq 1
.

We show (
8
). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and interchanging the order of summation, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\sim M\\
n\sim N\end{subarray}}a_{m}b_{n}e((\alpha+b/d)mn)w(mn/X)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{m}\left|\sum_{n\sim N}b_{n}e((\alpha+b/d)mn)w(mn/X)\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\sim N}\left|\sum_{m}e((\alpha+b/d)(n_{1}-n_{2})m)w(mn_{1}/X)w(mn_{2}/X)\right|.

Since
w(\cdot n_{1}/N)\times w(\cdot n_{2}/N)
is a smooth, compactly supported function and its derivatives are bounded with constants independent of
n_{1},n_{2}
, by Lemma
2.2
, this is

\displaystyle\ll M^{2}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\sim N}\#\{m^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}:|(\alpha+b/d)(n_{1}-n_{2})-m^{*}|\leq N/X^{1-\varepsilon}\}+X^{-10},

\displaystyle\ll XM+XM\#\left\{1\leq n\leq N,m^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}:|\alpha+b/d-m^{*}/n|\leq\frac{N}{nX^{1-\varepsilon}}\right\}+X^{-10}.

We divide
n
into dyadic ranges, and use Lemma
2.4
. This gives

\displaystyle\sum_{d\sim D}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/{d\mathbb{Z}}}\#\left\{1\leq n\leq N,m^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}:|\alpha+b/d-m^{*}/n|\leq\frac{N}{nX^{1-\varepsilon}}\right\},

\displaystyle\ll X^{2\varepsilon}\left(\frac{ND}{q}+\frac{qND}{X}+\frac{(ND)^{2}}{X}\right).

This gives (
8
).

To prove (
7
), we simply apply

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\sim M\end{subarray}}a_{m}e((\alpha+b/d)mn)w(mn/X)\right|\ll X,

and

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
m\sim M\end{subarray}}a_{m}e((\alpha+b/d)mn)w(mn/X)\right|\ll\frac{X}{M}\#\{m\sim M,n^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}:|\alpha+b/d-n^{*}/m|\ll X^{\varepsilon-1}\}+X^{-10}.

Thus the left-hand side of (
7
) is bounded by

\frac{X^{2}}{M}\#\{d\sim D,b\in\mathbb{Z}/{d\mathbb{Z}},m\sim M,n^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}:|\alpha+b/d-n^{*}/m|\ll X^{\varepsilon-1}\}+X^{-1}.

This and Lemma
2.4
give (
7
).
∎

3.
Level of distribution of Goldbach primes

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we need Theorem
1.1
and an analogue of Theorem
1.1
for equations of the form
2m+n=N+1
, in which one of the
\Lambda
factors is replaced by a structured weight.
Since the proofs are essentially the same, we prove a generalized version in both settings.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1/6)
and let
\eta>0
be sufficiently small.
Let
k\geq 1
and
\beta
be a function of the form

\beta(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\cdots n_{k}=n\\
N_{i}<n_{i}\leq N^{\prime}_{i}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n_{1})\cdots\Lambda(n_{k}),

where
N_{1}=1,N^{\prime}_{1}=X
if
k=1
, and
X^{\eta}<N_{i}<N^{\prime}_{i}\leq 2N_{i}
,
N_{1}\cdots N_{k}\leq X
if
k>1
.
Let
w_{1},w_{2}
be smooth functions supported on
[\eta,1]
.

Let
A>0
and
X\geq 2
. For all but
O(X(\ln{X})^{-A})
integers
N\in(X/2,X]
, we have

\sum_{d\leq X^{\theta}}\sup_{l:(d,l(N-l))=1}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+n=N\\
m\equiv l\bmod d\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)\beta(n)w_{1}(m/X)w_{2}(n/X)-M_{0}\right|\ll\frac{X}{(\ln{X})^{A}}

where
M_{0}=M_{0}(N,d)
is given by

M_{0}=\frac{\mathfrak{S}(N)}{\psi_{N}(d)}\sum_{m+n=N}\beta(n)w_{1}(m/X)w_{2}(n/X),\quad\mathfrak{S}(N)=2\bm{1}_{2|N}\prod_{p>2}\left(1+\frac{c_{p}(N)}{\varphi(p)^{2}}\right).

For all but
O(X(\ln{X})^{-A})
integers
N\in(X/2,X]
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq X^{\theta}\\
2\nmid d\end{subarray}}\sup_{l:(d,l(N-2l))=1}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2m+n=N\\
m\equiv l\bmod d\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)\beta(n)w_{1}(m/X)w_{2}(n/X)-M^{\prime}_{0}\right|\ll\frac{X}{(\ln{X})^{A}}

where
M^{\prime}_{0}=M^{\prime}_{0}(N,d)
is given by

M^{\prime}_{0}=\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}(N)}{2\psi_{N}(d)}\sum_{2m+n=N}\beta(n)w_{1}(m/X)w_{2}(n/X),\quad\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}(N)=2\bm{1}_{2\nmid N}\prod_{p>2}\left(1+\frac{c_{p}(N)}{\varphi(p)^{2}}\right).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming Theorem
3.1
.

If
k=0
, the claim follows from Theorem
3.1
with
\beta=\Lambda
and the prime number theorem.

By the triangle inequality, we have

R_{d,l}(N)\ll\frac{X}{d}(\ln{X})^{2}

for all
d,l
and
N\leq X
. Hence, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\sum_{d\leq X^{\theta}}\tau(d)^{k}\sup_{l:(d,l(N-l))=1}|R_{d,l}(N)|

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{d\leq X^{\theta}}\tau(d)^{2k}\frac{X}{d}(\ln{X})^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{d\leq X^{\theta}}\sup_{l:(d,l(N-l))=1}|R_{d,l}(N)|\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\ll(\ln{X})^{O_{k}(1)}\left(X\sum_{d\leq X^{\theta}}\sup_{l:(d,l(N-l))=1}|R_{d,l}(N)|\right)^{1/2}.

This reduces the general case to the case

∎

For a Dirichlet character

, let

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

and

be integers such that
(d,l)=(d,N-l)=1
. Let
m,n
be integers with
(m,[d,q])=1
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/{q\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\bm{1}_{m\equiv l\bmod d}e(a(m+n-N)/q)

(9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\ell^{*}|q}\frac{\mu(\ell^{*})}{\varphi([d,q])\varphi(q/\ell)}\sum_{\chi_{1}\bmod[d,q]}\sum_{\chi_{2}\bmod q/\ell}\chi^{*}_{2}(\ell^{*})\tau(\overline{\chi_{2}})\chi^{*}_{1}(m)\bm{1}_{\ell\ell^{*}|n}\chi^{*}_{2}(n/\ell\ell^{*})R(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},q,d,l,N),

where, for characters
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
with moduli dividing
[d,q]
and
q

by

R=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/{q\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\in(\mathbb{Z}/{[d,q]\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}\\
b_{1}\equiv l\bmod d\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi_{1}}(b_{1})e(ab_{1}/q)\right)\chi_{2}(a)e(-aN/q).

Proof.

Let
(n,q)=\ell
and write

. Since
(m,[d,q])=1
and
(n^{\prime},q/\ell)=1
, the orthogonality of Dirichlet characters shows that the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi([d,q])\varphi(q/\ell)}\sum_{\chi_{1}\bmod[d,q]}\sum_{\chi_{2}\bmod q/\ell}\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/{q\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}

Noting that

\sum_{b_{2}\in(\mathbb{Z}/{(q/\ell)\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\overline{\chi_{2}}(b_{2})e(ab_{2}/(q/\ell))=\tau(\overline{\chi_{2}})\chi_{2}(a)

and

\chi_{2}(n^{\prime})=\chi^{*}_{2}(n^{\prime})\bm{1}_{(n/\ell,q/\ell)=1}=\sum_{\ell^{*}|q/\ell,n/\ell}\chi^{*}_{2}(\ell^{*})\mu(\ell^{*})\bm{1}_{\ell^{*}|n^{\prime}}\chi^{*}_{2}(n^{\prime}/\ell^{*}),

∎

Note that

if

and
(\chi_{2})^{*}=(\chi^{\prime}_{2})^{*}
.
The next lemma is essentially given in
[

]
, with a slightly different definition of

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1\leq d,q\leq N
and
l
be integers such that
(d,l)=(d,N-l)=1
. Let
\chi_{1}\bmod[d,q],\chi_{2}\bmod q
be characters, and let
r_{2}
denote the conductor of
\chi_{2}
. Then

(10)

|R|\ll q\tau(q)(r_{2},N)^{1/2}\ln{\ln{N}},,

and, if
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are principal characters, then

(11)

R=\mu(q)c_{q/(d,q)}(N).

Proof.

See
[
9
, Lemmas 11.1, 11.2, and 11.3]
for the proof of (
10
).

A proof of (
11
) for squarefree
q
is also given in
[
9
, Section 8]
, but here we also need the non-squarefree case.

Assume that
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are principal characters. Then

R=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/{q\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in(\mathbb{Z}/{[d,q]\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}\\
b\equiv l\bmod d\end{subarray}}e(ab/q)\right)e(-aN/q).

Put
d^{+}=([d,q],d^{\infty}),q^{-}=[d,q]/d^{+}=q/(d^{\infty},q)
. Then
d|d^{+},q^{-}|q,[d,q]=d^{+}q^{-}
and
(d^{+},q^{-})=1
. Thus

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in(\mathbb{Z}/{[d,q]\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}\\
b\equiv l\bmod d\end{subarray}}e(ab/q)=\sum_{c=1}^{d^{+}/d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in(\mathbb{Z}/{[d,q]\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}\\
b\equiv l+dc\bmod d^{+}\end{subarray}}e(ab/q).

We write
b=(l+dc)q^{-}\overline{q^{-}}+d^{+}b^{\prime}
where
q^{-}\overline{q^{-}}\equiv 1\bmod d^{+}
. This gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in(\mathbb{Z}/{[d,q]\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}\\
b\equiv l+dc\bmod d^{+}\end{subarray}}e(ab/q)=e(a(l+dc)q^{-}\overline{q^{-}}/q)\sum_{b^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{Z}/{q^{-}\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}e(ad^{+}b^{\prime}/q).

Since
d^{+}/q=\frac{d/(d,q)}{q^{-}}
and
(d/(d,q),q^{-})=1
, we see that

\sum_{b^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{Z}/{q^{-}\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}e(ad^{+}b^{\prime}/q)=\mu(q^{-})

and

R=\mu(q^{-})\sum_{c=1}^{d^{+}/d}\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/{q\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}}e(a((l+dc)q^{-}\overline{q^{-}}-N)/q)=\mu(q^{-})\sum_{c=1}^{d^{+}/d}c_{q}((l+dc)q^{-}\overline{q^{-}}-N).

Noting that
((l+dc)q^{-}\overline{q^{-}}-N,p)=(l-N,p)=1
for all
p|(q,d^{\infty})
, we have

\displaystyle c_{q}((l+dc)q^{-}\overline{q^{-}}-N)

\displaystyle=c_{q^{-}}((l+dc)q^{-}\overline{q^{-}}-N)c_{(q,d^{\infty})}((l+dc)q^{-}\overline{q^{-}}-N),

\displaystyle=c_{q^{-}}(N)\mu((q,d^{\infty}))=c_{q/(d,q)}(N)\mu((q,d^{\infty})).

This gives (
11
).
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Let
r_{1}
be the conductor of
\chi_{1}
, and let
r^{-}_{1}=(r_{1},d^{\infty})
. Following the above argument and bounding the resulting sum over
c
trivially, we obtain

|R|\leq\frac{(q,d^{\infty})}{(q,d)}(N,q^{-})(r^{-}_{1}r_{2}[r^{-}_{1},r_{2}])^{1/2}.

However, this estimate can be worse than (

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
d,N\geq 1
with
\psi_{N}(d)\neq 0
. We have

\sum_{q}\frac{\mu(q)^{2}\varphi((d,q))}{\varphi(d)\varphi(q)^{2}}c_{q/(d,q)}(N)=\frac{\mathfrak{S}(N)}{\psi_{N}(d)}.

Proof.

Since the summand is multiplicative with respect to
q
, the left-hand side equals

=\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\prod_{p|d}\left(1+\frac{1}{\varphi(p)}\right)\prod_{p\nmid d}\left(1+\frac{c_{p}(N)}{\varphi(p)^{2}}\right).

Moreover,

\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\prod_{p|d}\left(1+\frac{1}{\varphi(p)}\right)\left(1+\frac{c_{p}(N)}{\varphi(p)^{2}}\right)^{-1}=\frac{1}{d}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|d\\
p|N\end{subarray}}\frac{p}{p-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|d\\
p\nmid N\end{subarray}}\frac{p}{p-2}=\frac{1}{\psi_{N}(d)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Analytic Lemmas

The following classical result follows from Gallagher’s lemma. See also
[
10
, Proposition 5.1]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\varepsilon>0,x\geq 1
, and
\eta>0,\delta\geq 1/(\eta x)
.
Let
f
be an arithmetic function supported on
[1,x/\eta]
and satisfying
f(n)\ll x
, and let
w
be a smooth function supported on
[\eta,1]
. Then

(12)

\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\left|\sum_{n}f(n)e(n\epsilon)w(n/x)\right|^{2}d\epsilon\ll\delta^{2}x\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[\eta x,x]\\
|I|\leq 2\delta^{-1}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{n\in I}f(n)\right|^{2}

and

(13)

\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\left|\sum_{n}f(n)e(n\epsilon)w(n/x)\right|^{2}d\epsilon\ll x^{-1}\left(\int_{|t|\leq\delta x^{1+\varepsilon}}\left|\sum_{n}\frac{f(n)}{n^{it}}\right|dt\right)^{2}+x^{-10}.

Here, the implicit constant depends only on
\varepsilon,\eta
and
w
.

Proof.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth even function supported on
[-1,1]
and satisfying
\hat{\phi}(u)=\int_{-1}^{1}\phi(y)\cos(2\pi uy)dy\geq 1
for
u\in[-1,1]
. Then

(14)

\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\left|\sum_{n}f(n)e(n\epsilon)w(n/x)\right|^{2}d\epsilon\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{n}f(n)e(n\epsilon)w(n/x)\right|^{2}|\hat{\phi}(\epsilon/\delta)|^{2}d\epsilon.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{n}f(n)e(-n\epsilon)w(n/x)\hat{\phi}(\epsilon/\delta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}f(n)e(-n\epsilon)w(n/x)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(y)e(-\epsilon y/\delta)dy

\displaystyle=\delta\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n}f(n)w(n/x)\phi(\delta(z-n))\right)e(-\epsilon z)dz,

it follows from the Plancherel identity that the right-hand side of (
14
) is

\delta^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{n}f(n)w(n/x)\phi(\delta(y-n))\right|^{2}dy.

We bound this trivially by

\ll\delta^{2}x\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sum_{n}f(n)w(n/x)\phi(\delta(y-n))\right|^{2}.

This, together with partial summation, gives (
12
).

Fix
y\in\mathbb{R}
and let

W(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}w(z/x)\phi(\delta(y-z))z^{s-1}dz.

Trivially,

(15)

W(it)\ll\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi(\delta(y-z))|x^{-1}dz\ll(\delta x)^{-1}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Integrating by parts
j\geq 0
times, we obtain

\displaystyle W(it)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{z^{it+j}}{(it+1)(it+2)\cdots(it+j)}\left(\frac{d^{j}}{dz^{j}}w(z/x)\phi(\delta(y-z))z^{-1}\right)dz,

\displaystyle\ll_{j}\int_{\eta x}^{x}\frac{x^{j}}{(1+|t|)^{j}}(1/x+\delta)^{j}x^{-1}dz\ll\left(\frac{x\delta}{1+|t|}\right)^{j},

for any
j\geq 0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
This gives

(16)

W(it)\ll\frac{x^{-100}}{|t|^{2}}

for
|t|\geq\delta x^{1+\varepsilon}
.

By the Mellin inversion formula, we have

w(n/x)\phi(\delta(y-n))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}W(it)n^{-it}dt

and thus

\sum_{n}f(n)w(n/x)\phi(\delta(y-n))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}W(it)\left(\sum_{n}\frac{f(n)}{n^{it}}\right)dt.

Using (
16
) and (
15
), we obtain (
13
).

∎

5.1.
Setting

Recall that for a function
F:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
s,\theta>0
, we write

\displaystyle M_{k}(F,\theta,s)

\displaystyle=s\omega^{-}(s\theta)-s\int_{1/s\leq u\leq\theta}\frac{F(u)\omega^{+}(s(\theta-u))}{u}du

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\theta}\sum_{r=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r!}\int\cdots\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1/s\leq u_{i}\leq 1\\
u_{1}+\cdots+u_{r}=1\end{subarray}}\frac{G(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})}{u_{1}\cdots u_{r}}du

where

G(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})=\max(0,1-F(u_{1})-\cdots-F(u_{r})).

We want to find small
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
M_{k}(F,\theta,s)>0
for some non-negative piecewise smooth function
F
supported on
[0,\theta]
and
s>1/\theta
.

We write

S_{G}=\sum_{r=k+1}^{\infty}T_{r},\quad T_{r}=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1/s\leq u_{1}\leq\cdots\leq u_{r}\leq 1\\
u_{1}+\cdots+u_{r}=1\end{subarray}}\frac{G(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})}{u_{1}\cdots u_{r}}\,du.

and

A^{\pm}(u):=u\omega^{\pm}(u).

The linear sieve functions are normalized by

A^{+}(u)=2\quad(0<u\leq 3),\qquad A^{-}(u)=0\quad(0<u\leq 2),

and by the delay equations

(A^{+})^{\prime}(u)=\frac{A^{-}(u-1)}{u-1}\quad(u>3),\qquad(A^{-})^{\prime}(u)=\frac{A^{+}(u-1)}{u-1}\quad(u>2).

Following
[
12
]
, define

c_{1}(s)=\bm{1}_{s\geq 1},\qquad c_{r}(s)=\int_{r\leq u\leq s}\frac{c_{r-1}(u-1)}{u-1}\,du\quad(r\geq 2).

We have

c_{r}(s)=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1/s\leq u_{1}\leq\cdots\leq u_{r}\\
u_{1}+\cdots+u_{r}=1\end{subarray}}\frac{du}{u_{1}\cdots u_{r}}.

for
r\geq 2
. This follows by direct calculation if
r=2
. Assuming the case
r
, the case
r+1
follows from

\displaystyle\int_{1/s\leq u_{1}\leq\cdots\leq u_{r}\leq 1-u_{1}-\cdots-u_{r}}\frac{du_{1}\cdots du_{r}}{u_{1}\cdots u_{r}(1-u_{1}-\cdots-u_{r})}

\displaystyle=\int_{1/s\leq u_{1}\leq 1/(r+1)}\frac{c_{r}((1-u_{1})/u_{1})}{u_{1}(1-u_{1})}du_{1}=c_{r+1}(s).

Here the first equality follows by writing

u_{i+1}=(1-u_{1})v_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
, and the last equality follows from the change of variables
x=1/u_{1}
.

Three choices of
F
have been used in this context; see
[
12
]
for the history and applications.

(Trivial weight)

F=0
,

(Kuhn’s weight)

F(u)=\frac{1}{2}\bm{1}_{u\leq 1/t}
for some
t<s
.

(Richert’s weight)

F(u)=\lambda\bm{1}_{u\leq 1/t}(1-tu)
for some
t<s
and
\lambda>0
.

For the trivial weight, one has
G=1
, and hence

T_{r}=c_{r}(s).

For Kuhn’s weight, one has

T_{r}=T_{r,0}+T_{r,1},\qquad T_{r,0}=c_{r}(t),\quad T_{r,1}=\frac{t}{2}\int_{t-1}^{t(1-1/s)}\frac{c_{r-1}(u)}{u(t-u)}du

by (
44

is evaluated using

\frac{1}{2}\int_{1/s}^{1/t}\frac{\omega^{+}(s(\theta-u))}{u}\,du=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\beta(1-1/\alpha)}^{\beta-1}\frac{A^{+}(v)}{v(\beta-v)}\,dv,\qquad\alpha=t\theta,\quad\beta=s\theta.

These two weights only require values of
A^{\pm}
and

, which can be computed rigorously by the recursion formulas above.

For Richert’s weight,

was computed numerically by an FFT convolution method. This computation was not enclosed using Arb interval arithmetic.

5.3.
Asymptotic

The following proposition gives an asymptotic upper bound for
\theta
obtainable from the
F=0
construction.
For comparison, it is known that, without switching, one needs a condition of order at least
1/k
to find
\mathbb{P}_{k}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and assume that
k
is sufficiently large in terms of
\varepsilon
.
Then there exists
s_{k}>0
such that
M_{k}(0,\theta_{k},s_{k})>0
where

\theta_{k}=\exp\!\left(-(1-\varepsilon)\frac{k}{e}\right).

Proof.

We first note

(47)

c_{j}(s)\leq\frac{(\ln{s})^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}.

If
j=1
, this is trivial. Assuming the case
j-1
, the case
j
follows from

c_{j}(s)\leq\int_{j\leq u\leq s}\frac{(\ln{(u-1)})^{j-2}}{(j-2)!(u-1)}du=\frac{(\ln{(u-1)})^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\Big|_{u=j}^{s}\leq\frac{(\ln{s})^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}.

Hence we have

C_{k+1}(s)\leq\sum_{r\geq k}\frac{(\ln{s})^{r}}{r!}.

Since
\ln{r!}\geq r(\ln{r}-1)
, we have
(\ln{s})^{r}/r!\leq\left(e\ln{s}/r\right)^{r}
.

We take

s_{k}=3/\theta_{k}.

We may assume
\ln{s_{k}}\leq(1-\varepsilon/2)k/e
if
k
is large. Hence

C_{k+1}(s_{k})\leq\sum_{r\geq k}(1-\varepsilon/2)^{r}\leq\frac{(1-\varepsilon/2)^{k}}{\varepsilon/2}\to 0\quad(k\to\infty),

and

M_{k}(0,\theta_{k},s_{k})\geq s_{k}\left(\omega^{-}(3)-\frac{2}{3}C_{k+1}(s_{k})\right)>0

for large
k
.
∎
