Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors target pancreatic cancer cells and macrophages in the tumor microenvironment
Authors: Anamaris Torres-Sanchez, Stephanie Dorta-Estremera, Victor P Carlo, Suranganie Dharmawardhane
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains one of the deadliest forms of cancer, with a distinct extracellular matrix and immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. Therefore, there is a critical need for therapies that simultaneously target metastatic cancer cells and immunosuppressive cells such as tumor associated macrophages (TAMS) that promote pancreatic cancer progression. Ras-activated homologous GTPases Rac and Cdc42 are ideal targets for pancreatic cancer therapy because they regulate cell migration/invasion, cell polarity/morphology, and cell viability/survival in cancer cells and immune cells, mainly through activation of their common downstream effector p21-activated kinase (PAK) via autophosphorylation. Rac, Cdc42, and PAK are poor prognostic factors in PDAC. Accordingly, we found phospho-PAK levels to be elevated in invasive PDAC from Puerto Rican patient tissue. The potential of the dual Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 were tested in human and mouse PDAC cells or macrophages by pulldown assays for Rac and Cdc42 activation, MTT assays for cell viability, wound-healing assays for cell migration, phagocytosis, as well as co-culture assays with PDAC cells and macrophages. Results show that Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors significantly reduce active Rac and Cdc42 in pancreatic and macrophage cells. Both MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 reduced pancreatic cancer cell viability with higher efficacy than macrophage viability, and inhibited morphology and migration of both cell types. In Transwell co-culture, MBQ-167 and MBQ 168 decreased pancreatic cancer cell migration and reduced inflammatory mediators such as Interleukin-6 (IL-6), Chinase-3-like protein 1 (CHI3L1) and S100A8/A9 (calprotectin). Therefore, MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 are potential PDAC therapeutics by targeting both pancreatic cancer cells and macrophages in the TME.Keywords: Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), Rac, Cdc42, Macrophages, Tumor microenvironment, Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors, MBQ-167, MBQ-168, p21-activated kinase (PAK)

Introduction

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains one of the deadliest forms of cancer, mainly due to non-specific symptoms and a lack of specific biomarkers to detect the disease at an earlier stage. This disease arises from the pancreatic ductal epithelial cells, accounting for 90% of all diagnosed PDAC cases [1,2]. Consequently, PDAC is expected to be the second cause of cancer-related deaths by 2030 [3]. Even though the five-year survival rate for PDAC has increased to 13%, it drops to a dismal 3% following distant metastasis [3,4]. So far, the reliable curative treatment for this disease remains resection of the tumor. However, only 15–20% of patients qualify for surgery at diagnosis [3]. Among the standard first lines of treatments are FOLFIRINOX, gemcitabine, and nab-paclitaxel, albeit with limited efficacy. Notably, the survival rate only improves by 6 to 11 months for responders to these chemotherapies [5,6]. More combination therapies are needed to overcome chemotherapy resistance, as was demonstrated recently in a study testing the NSAID sulindac to sensitize pancreatic cancers to nab-paclitaxel and gemcitabine [7].

Even though multiple studies are developing targeted therapies to combat PDAC [8], targeting KRAS, which is oncogenic in 90–93% of diagnosed PDAC cases has recently met with success [3,6]. KRAS was considered undruggable due to multiple past failures in targeted therapies; however, this has changed in recent years with the development of a number of oncogenic Ras targeting therapies [9]. The FDA approval of Sotorasib and Adagrasib, which are KRAS G12C inhibitors, was a breakthrough, but the KRAS G12C mutation only encompasses <2% of PDAC [10,11]. Targeting KRAS G12D, which is the most prominent mutation in 42% of PDACs, has been met with limited success. For example, the KRAS G12D inhibitor MRTX1133 showed preclinical promise by demonstrating reduction of tumor burden in mouse models, but failed clinical trials [10,12–14]. Another promising drug targeting all Ras mutations is Daraxonrasib, which just completed Phase 3 trials successfully. However, this drug has adverse effects such as skin toxicities (rashes, and slow wound healing), diarrhea, and nausea, and cannot be recommended for all patients [13]. This need reinforces the continued search for novel therapeutic approaches for PDAC and its pro-tumorigenic tumor microenvironment (TME).

The pancreatic TME is comprised of cancer-associated fibroblasts, dense extracellular matrix, and immunosuppressive cells [3]. In an unhealthy state, the TME could enhance pro-tumorigenic behavior, promoting proliferation and invasion of cancer cells into tissues by releasing inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) [14]. In the TME, macrophages are known to aid in pancreatic cancer cell migration by secreting cytokines and chemokines, such as tumor necrosis factor α (TNFα), transforming growth factor β1(TGFβ1), and IL-6 [15,16]. In turn, cancer cells secrete factors such as chemokine ligand 2(CCL2), that signal to macrophage migration [17,18]. Tumor-associated macrophages (TAM) are among the most abundant of the immune cells in the PDAC TME [2]. TAMs are present from the initiation of pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN) lesions and shift to polarized M2 macrophages as the disease progresses, where M2 macrophages are implicated in promoting epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), angiogenesis, T-cell suppression, and metastasis [2,9,19]. TAMs contribute to desmoplasia and poor response to FOLFIRINOX by cross-talking with cancer associated fibroblasts (CAFs) [20]. Studies have demonstrated that targeting macrophages by either depleting or reprogramming them to an M1 macrophage enhances T-cell response, decreases fibrosis, and reduces metastasis [2,5,19]. Therefore, there is promise in novel therapeutics that simultaneously target PDAC and immune cells, such as macrophages, to combat pancreatic cancer. The failure of immune checkpoint therapy, which has limited success in pancreatic cancer, may also be due to the highly active immune microenvironment in pancreatic cancer. Multiple efforts are underway to develop targeted therapies for PDAC, including immune checkpoint therapy, PARP inhibitors, and inhibitors of oncogenic signaling receptors, such as tyrosine kinases [8,21]. Recent reports also show success in combination therapies to target the pancreatic cancer TME by inhibiting necroptosis, which promotes immune evasion in metastatic pancreatic cancer, or by inducing cuproptosis [22,23].

We selected the RAS-related proteins Rac and Cdc42 as ideal targets to combat PDAC because they relay signals from multiple oncogenic receptors such as G-protein coupled receptors (GPCR), receptor tyrosine kinases (RTK), toll-like receptors (TLR) and cytokine receptors, as well as oncogenic Ras. Rac and Cdc42 are central regulators of actin cytoskeleton dynamics leading to cell migration and invasion, as well as cell cycle progression, T-cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and cell-cell and cell-substrate adhesions [24]. In PDAC, Rac and Cdc42 and their downstream effector p21-activated kinase (PAK) are upregulated and associated with poor prognosis [25,26]. Since Ras activates Rac and Cdc42, this pathway directly contributes to PDAC malignancy.

Rac and Cdc42 are also involved in the immune response. Rac can be activated by TLR4 signaling, which leads to secretion of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6. IL-6 signaling can lead to the recruitment and migration of macrophages to the TME, leading to increased angiogenesis, cancer cell survival, and metastatic progression [26,27]. IL-6 stimulation was shown to activate Cdc42 in human PDAC cells and promote the formation of pre-migratory filopodia leading to invasion [28]. Moreover, Rac and Cdc42 are involved in the actin polymerization required for phagocytosis and the differentiation of M2 macrophages in the TME [29]. Therefore, Rac and Cdc42 are ideal targets for blocking pancreatic cancer progression and reprogramming the TME.

Much effort has been spent on developing small molecule compounds to target Rac and Cdc42; however, they are hampered by high effective concentrations and specificity [30–33]. The Rac and Cdc42 direct downstream effector PAK, which is also a risk factor for PDAC, has also been targeted [32]. For example, the Pfizer compound PF-3758,309 and FRAX 597 reached clinical trials [30–32], but failed due to high effective concentration, poor bioavailability, and tumor response [30–32]. To fill the need for more potent Rac/Cdc42 targeted therapies, we developed the related small molecule compounds MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 and characterized them as anti-metastatic breast cancer compounds [34–36].

Our research group has demonstrated that MBQ-167, a dual Rac and Cdc42 inhibitor, reduces cell viability, migration, polarity, cell-substrate adhesions, wound-healing, tumor growth, and metastasis in multiple breast cancer cell and mouse models [34–38]. MBQ-167, which is currently in phase 1 clinical trials, has acceptable bioavailability and an excellent safety profile [40]. In breast cancer cells, the MBQ-167 derivative MBQ-168 acts via the same mechanism by blocking guanine nucleotide binding at the critical glycine 12 residue of Rac1 and Cdc42. Therefore, MBQ-168 is comparable to MBQ-167 in inhibition of Rac, Rac1B oncogenic splice variant, Cdc42, and the consequent decrease in triple negative and HER2 positive breast cancer cell viability and migration, and induction of apoptosis. Notably, MBQ-168 is a more effective inhibitor of P21-activated kinases (1–3) activation. Similar to MBQ-167, MBQ-168 reduced tumor burden and metastasis in a HER2+ metastatic breast cancer model. In addition, MBQ-168 is approximately 10X less potent than MBQ-167 at inhibiting Cytochrome p450 (CYP3A4), thus demonstrating its utility in relevant combination therapies [36]. These characteristics make the MBQ compounds attractive therapies for multiple cancer types with increased Rac/Cdc42/PAK expression as PDAC, where Rac/PAK activation contributes to therapy resistance and malignancy [41,42].

Rac and Cdc42 are involved in the signaling required for secretion of cytokines and chemokines in the TME [43]. Accordingly, we reported that inhibiting Rac and Cdc42 reduces proinflammatory cytokines, and infiltration of neutrophils and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) into the breast cancer TME [38]. Given the involvement of Rac and Cdc42 in metastatic progression and our work demonstrating how their inhibition reduces diseases progression and an anti-tumorigenic TME, Rac and Cdc42 appear to be viable therapeutic targets for PDAC.

Therefore, we investigated the potential of Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 to reduce cancer associated functions of both PDAC cells and macrophage cells. The co-culture media were also analyzed for inflammatory mediators associated with PDAC that we have previously shown to be decreased by MBQ-167, such as IL-6 [38], which is elevated in the pancreatic cancer TME [44]. Additional inflammatory mediators chitinase 3-like 1 (CHI3L1 gene, YKL40 protein) and S100A8/A9 (calprotectin) were tested, because these genes were downregulated in mouse mammary tumors following MBQ-167 administration [39]. Since IL-6, CHI3L1 and S100A8/A9 have been shown to be secreted by cancer cells and monocytes/macrophages and thus, associated with PDAC progression, immunosuppression, therapy resistance, and poor prognosis [45–47], this study also investigated the efficacy of MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 to decrease these inflammatory mediators.

Materials and methods

Synthesis of MBQ compounds

MBQ-167 (9-ethyl-3-(1H-1,2,3-triazol-1-yl)-9H-carbazole) at >98% purity was provided by MBQ Pharma and synthesized under GMP conditions by Galephar Pharmaceutical Research, Humacao, Puerto Rico. MBQ-167 synthesis protocol is described in detail in [34]. MBQ-168 (9-ethyl-3-(5-(3-methoxyprenyl)-1H-1,2,3-triazol-1-yl)-9H-carbazol) was synthesized to 90% purity as described in Medina et al., 2022 by reacting the 3-azido-9-ethyl-9H-carbazole with methyl group [36].

Cell culture

A clinically relevant mouse pancreatic cancer cell line LSL-KrasG12D/+; Trp53R172H/+; Pdx1Cre/+ (KPC) (kind gift of Dr. Cruz-Monserrate, Ohio State University, Columbus, OH) and human PDAC cells PANC1 and Mia PaCa-2 (from ATCC) were cultured as per standard lab protocols. Macrophage cell lines Raw 264.7 (TIB-71) murine macrophage-like cells, and THP-1 (TIB-202) human monocytes were obtained from ATCC. THP-1 cells were differentiated into macrophage-like cells with 100 nM phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA) for two days, as per manufacturer’s instructions.

The KPC, PANC1, Mia PaCa-2, and Raw 264.7 cells were cultured in DMEM media with 1% Penicillin/Streptomycin and 10% Fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Fisher Scientific), and the THP- 1 cells were cultured in RPMI media. Cell lines were authenticated by ATCC.

Rac/Cdc42 activity assay

The GST-p21-binding domain of PAK1 conjugated to glutathione-Sepharose was used to pull down active GTP bound Rac or Cdc42, as previously described [34]. Briefly, cells were treated with vehicle or MBQ compounds for 24 hrs and lysed. Cell lysates were incubated with Glutathione Sepharose beads conjugated to glutathione-S-transferase (GST)-P21-binding domain of PAK1 for 1hr while rotating gently at 4 °C. The pulldowns (Rac-GTP or Cdc42-GTP) and total cell lysates were separated in a 12% SDS-PAGE gel and identified by western blotting with anti-Rac (1:1000, Cell Signaling) or anti-Cdc42 (1:5000, Abcam) antibodies. Integrated density of positive bands was quantified using Image J software (National Institutes of Health). Rac.GTP/Total Rac or Cdc42.GTP/Total Cdc42 was used to quantify the active Rac or Cdc42 from the Image J analysis.

Immunofluorescence microscopy

PANC1 or THP-1 differentiated macrophages were seeded at 1 × 104 cells/mL, then treated with vehicle, MBQ-167 or MBQ-168 for 24 h. Cells were fixed in 3.7% formaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.2% Triton-X 100. Rhodamine Phalloidin (1: 500, ab235138, Abcam) was used to stain F-actin. Anti p-PAK (1/2/3) Thr 423/402/421 antibody (1:100, ab62155, Abcam) conjugated to Alexa-488 (1:500, ab150077, Abcam) was used to stain p-PAK. Image J software was used to quantify the intensity of the p-PAK fluorescence.

Cell viability assay

Promega (Madison, WI) CellTiter 96® Non-Radioactive Cell Proliferation Assay (MTT) was used to quantify the viability of pancreatic and macrophage cells lines treated with vehicle, MBQ-167, or MBQ-168 for 72 h. After the incubation, the MTT reagent was added and incubated for 4 h at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Results were obtained by reading the absorbance at 570 nm wavelength, with appropriate controls, as per manufacturer’s instructions.

Wound-healing assay

PANC1 or Raw 264.7 cells were seeded in a 24-well plate until 95–100% confluency, and serum starved overnight. A wound was created at the center with a 200 μL pipet tip and treated with vehicle, MBQ-167, or MBQ-168 for 48 h. Images were captured using a Keyence Microscope system and analyzed using a wound- healing size tool plug-in for ImageJ software, to obtain the wound area as pixels2. To calculate the percentage of wound closure of the sample, the area obtained after 48 h was compared to that of the initial wound using: [end-area of wound closure/initial area of wound]x100.

Phagocytosis assay

Phagocytosis assays were conducted using Raw 264.7 cells, as previously described by us [38]. Following treatment with MBQ-167 or MBQ-168 for 6 h, cells were allowed to phagocytize fluorescently tagged Zymosan particles for 2.5 h (ab234053, Abcam). The fluorescence from the Zymosan particles was quantified at 490/520 nM in a Bio-Rad fluorescence microplate reader, as per manufacturer’s directions. Representative images were taken using a Keyence digital microscope (Keyence, Corp).

Transwell assays

KPC PDAC cells and Raw 264.7 macrophages were serum-starved for 6 h, then treated with vehicle, or 500 nM MBQ-167 or MBQ-168. For migration assays, either cell type was placed on the top well in 1% FBS and the treatment, and allowed to migrate for 12 h towards 5% FBS in the bottom well. For co-culture assays, PDAC cells were seeded at the bottom well, and the macrophages were placed on the top of the Transwell (8μ pores) and incubated for 12 h in the presence of treatment. In reverse experiments, the macrophages were seeded in the bottom well, and the PDAC cells placed on the top well. Following treatment, cells that migrated through the top well membrane were fixed and stained with propidium iodide (PI) and quantified as described in [34]. Conditioned media was collected for IL-6, CHI3L1 and S100A8/A9 enzyme linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA). Digital microscopic images taken from an OLYMPUS CKX41 fluorescence microscope and OLYMPUS EP50 system were quantified using Image J software.

Enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Conditioned media from single or co-cultures were subjected to specific ELISAs for IL-6 (dy406, R&D Systems), CHI3L1 (ab238262, abcam), and S100A8/A9 (ab263885, abcam), as per manufacturer’s instructions.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) of PDAC tissue

Unstained formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue (FFPE) sections from deidentified patients, diagnosed as PDAC at the time of biopsy, were collected for the IHC experiments, with approval from the Auxilio Mutuo Hospital of Puerto Rico under IRBNet Pkg. 1487,543–3. The protocol was deemed exempt from human subjects research under the relevant guidelines and regulations of the Declaration of Helsinki and the human subjects research policies of the National Institutes of Health, US.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained slides were used as a basis for comparison with IHC staining, to localize the cancer cells. In addition, histological grade information was obtained from pathological records, while the cancer stage was determined according to the TNM system (tumor extension (T), regional lymph node (N) involvement, presence of metastasis (M). IHC was performed on the FFPE tissue to visualize both p-PAK and total PAK expression, as previously described [38]. Tissues were incubated overnight in specific primary antibodies targeting p-PAK (1:50, ab62155, abcam) or total PAK (1:100, ab196834, abcam). Positive staining was detected using the VectaStain Horse radish peroxidase (HRP) Kit (PK-7800, Vector Laboratories), with hematoxylin serving as a counterstain. H-scores were used for quantification considering the staining intensity (scored from 0 to 3) and the percentage of positively stained cells. Scoring was conducted visually, excluding healthy areas such as the islets of Langerhans. Images were also independently scored by a board-certified pathologist, blinded to clinical stage or treatment, for scientific rigor.

Statistical analysis

For all experiments, three biological replicates were used for statistical analyses. Data is presented as mean ± SD. One-way ANOVA (non-parametric) analysis comparing vehicle to drug treatments was conducted using Graph Pad Prism. Data was considered significant at p ≤ 0.05.

Results

Rac/Cdc42 activation is decreased by MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 in PDAC and macrophages

MBQ-167 was characterized by us as a potent dual Rac and Cdc42 inhibitor, with an IC50 of 100 nM for Rac activation and 78 nM for Cdc42 activation in breast cancer cell lines [34]. MBQ-168 decreased Rac and Cdc42 activation in triple negative breast cancer cells (TNBC) at nM concentrations (~200 nM) [36]. In pancreatic cancer and macrophage-like cells, activated GTP bound Rac and Cdc42 were detected and quantified via a pulldown assay, using the binding domain of their common downstream effector PAK, which selectively binds active GTP bound Rac or Cdc42. PANC1 cells were treated with 0, 250, or 500 nM of MBQ-167 or MBQ-168 for 24 hrs, then lysed and subjected to the pulldown assay. Active and total Rac and Cdc42 were detected using specific antibodies for Rac and Cdc42 in Western blots. MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 significantly decreased both Rac and Cdc42 activation in PANC1 cells. However, compared to TNBC, higher concentrations (500 nM) were required to achieve ~70% inhibition of Rac and ~50% inhibition of Cdc42 in this aggressive PDAC cell line (Fig. 1A).

Pulldown samples and cell lysates were subjected to Western blots using specific antibodies (Rac or Cdc42). A) Relative Rac and Cdc42 activity of PANC1 cells after treatment. B) Relative Rac and Cdc42 activity of differentiated THP1 cells after treatment. Representative westerns (N = 5) are shown. Since macrophages in the tumor microenvironment promote tumor malignancy, we tested the effect of MBQ compounds on Rac and Cdc42 activation in macrophages. THP-1 human monocytes were differentiated into macrophages with PMA and the differentiated macrophages were treated with either MBQ-167 or MBQ-168. We previously reported that MBQ-167 reduced Rac activity by 75% and Cdc42 activation by 40% at 500 nM in macrophage-like cells (Fig. 1B) [38]. Here, we show that MBQ-168 at 500 nM also decreases Rac and Cdc42 activation with similar efficacy in differentiated macrophages (~75–80%) (Fig. 1B).

Rac and Cdc42 inhibitors decrease PDAC cell viability, without affecting macrophage-like cells

To assess the impact of MBQ compounds on cell viability, we conducted an MTT assay on PDAC and macrophage cell lines. For PDAC: human Mia PaCa-2 and PANC1, and the mouse LSL-KrasG12D/+; Trp53R172H/+; Pdx1Cre/+(KPC) cell lines were used. The genetically engineered mouse KPC cell line represents a prevalent genetic background of PDAC in humans, and is used widely to model PDAC in immunocompetent mice. In Mia PaCa-2 cells, MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 demonstrated similar GI50s, of 142 nM and 144 nM respectively (Fig. 2A). In the PANC1 cells, the GI50 for MBQ-167 was 463.2 nM and the GI50 for MBQ-168 was similar at 541.4 nM (Fig. 2B). However, MBQ-168 was more effective than MBQ-167 in the KPC cell line, with a GI50 of 324.1 nM, compared to the GI50 of 467.3 nM for MBQ-167 (Fig. 2C). Next, we tested the effect of Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors on the murine Raw 264.7 macrophages and the human THP-1 differentiated macrophages. Macrophages were seeded and treated with either MBQ-167 or MBQ-168 at high concentrations for 72 hrs. Concentration of 1000 nM was selected because we have shown that lower concentrations did not affect macrophage viability. In Raw 264.7 macrophage cells, MBQ-167 was not effective, while MBQ-168 significantly reduced cell viability by 58.6% at 1000 nM (Fig. 2D). However, this is almost three times the GI50 for the KPC cell line and ten times the therapeutic window for MBQ-167 in breast cancer [40,44]. The decrease in THP-1 cell viability was modest, even at 1000 nM, where MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 significantly decreased viability by ~20%.

Effect of Rac and Cdc42 inhibitors on actin structures and PAK activity

Since Rac and Cdc42 are involved in actin reorganization, we wanted to assess the effect of the MBQ compounds on the actin cytoskeleton. PANC1 and THP-1 differentiated macrophage cell lines were treated with either MBQ-167 or MBQ-168 for 24 hrs. Subsequently, the cells were stained with Rhodamine Phalloidin for F-actin, and for activation of the common Rac and Cdc42 downstream effector PAK, i.e. phosphorylated PAK (p-PAK), with an Alexa-488 conjugated anti p-Thr423-PAK antibody. MBQ-167 treated cells resulted in decreased cell polarity for both pancreatic and macrophages cells (Fig. 3A), as has been previously reported by us for breast cancer [34]. While actin-based protrusions can still be observed in macrophages treated with MBQ-168, the PDAC cells appear more rounded, demonstrating a similar loss of cell polarity in response to MBQ-168 as with MBQ-167 (Fig. 3A). In this experiment, when compared to vehicle, MBQ-167 reduced p-PAK activity by 75% in PDAC and by 86% in macrophage-like cells (Fig. 3A). This reduction of the PAK activity was confirmed in lysates of treated PANC1 and THP-1 cells (Fig. 3B). In PANC1, MBQ-167 reduced p-PAK levels starting at 250 nM, whereas MBQ-168 did not affect p-PAK levels at 250 nM but significantly reduced p-PAK levels at 500 nM (Fig. 3B). As with the immunofluorescence experiments, both Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors decreased p-PAK levels in treated cell lysates (Fig. 3B).

Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors decrease cell migration in PDAC and macrophage-like cells

Given that Rac and Cdc42 are involved in the reorganization of the actin cytoskeleton, regulating the formation of lamellipodia and filopodia respectively [43], we wanted to assess the impact of their inhibition on cell migration. A wound-healing assay was conducted to analyze migration of PANC1 cells and Raw 264.7 macrophages. Since MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 decrease Rac/Cdc42 activation in both PDAC and macrophage-like cells at 500 nM, we used this concentration to assess migration. Following a scratch on a confluent cell monolayer, each inhibitor was added and the cells were left to migrate for 48 h. MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 significantly decreased PANC1 cell migration (Fig. 4A). Similarly, in macrophage-like cells, MBQ-167 decreased cell migration by 61.7% and MBQ-168 decreased by 44.8% at 500 nM (Fig. 4B).

A 200μL tip was used to create the wound in a confluent cell layer, and the images were taken at 48 hrs following treatment. Left, representative images shown at 100X. Right, Images were quantified using ImageJ and analyzed using Graph Pad. N = 5, ***=p < 0.001, ****=p < 0.0001.

Additionally, we have tested MBQ compounds in inflammatory fibroblasts, which contribute to the desmoplastic response in PDAC tumors, where MBQ-168 significantly decreased Rac activation and cell migration via wound healing assay in an inflammatory fibroblast cell line (Suppl Fig. 1). Therefore, Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors may also affect other stromal cells in the TME.

Because phagocytosis is one of the key functions of macrophages and reorganization of the cytoskeleton is necessary for the formation of the phagosome, we tested the efficacy of the Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors to affect uptake of fluorescent green zymosan particles, as a measure of phagocytosis. Results demonstrate that MBQ-167 significantly reduces phagocytosis by 43.6% at 500 nM in Raw 264.7 macrophages (Suppl Fig. 2).

Effect of Rac and Cdc42 inhibition on cell migration and IL-6 secretion in PDAC and macrophage co-culture

Herein, we wanted to assess if Rac and Cdc42 inhibitors affect the signaling cross talk between pancreatic cancer cells and macrophages in a closed system. This was accomplished by using an 8 μm pore Trans-well, and culturing either KPC mouse pancreatic cancer cells or Raw 264.7 mouse macrophages on the insert, and the other cell type on the bottom of the plate. Cells from the top well were left to migrate to the underside of the membrane in the presence of either MBQ-167 or MBQ-168 for 12 h. The drug concentration used in this experiment was 500 nM, since in wound-healing we saw a decrease in migration for both PDAC and macrophage-like cells.

In a Transwell assay, with KPC cells in the top well and 5% FBS as a chemoattractant in the bottom well, KPC cell migration was decreased significantly by MBQ-168 with 62.7% at 500 nM (Fig. 5A). MBQ-168 demonstrated a similar reduction of macrophage migration at 71.8% when the PDAC cells were on the bottom well. MBQ-167 also decreased migration of KPC cells in the Transwell with macrophages in the bottom well by 56.8% at 500 nM (Fig. 5B). However, both inhibitors reduced Raw 264.7 macrophage migration in co-cultures, when the macrophages were in the Transwell insert with KPC cells in the bottom well, by >80% (Fig. 5A, 5C).

IL-6 is a key cytokine that promotes inflammation, especially via macrophage migration into inflamed tissue [48], that we have shown to be upregulated in mouse mammary tumors and plasma following MBQ-167 administration [38,39]. Therefore, the effect of MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 were tested on IL-6 levels in the conditioned media from the Transwells of PDAC and macrophages. Results demonstrate that MBQ-168 significantly reduced levels of IL-6 by ~100% in the presence of macrophages, while the response to MBQ-167 was only a trend at ~65% decrease (Fig. 5D). When macrophage-like cells were plated on the top of the Transwell with pancreatic cancer cells in the bottom well, both MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 induced a significant 90–100% decrease in IL-6 secretion (Fig. 5E).

Proinflammatory mediators play a pivotal role in the progression of pancreatic cancer, where inflammation is part of its etiology [49]. In addition to IL-6, CHI3L1 and S100A8/A9 have been correlated with reduced overall survival and response in pancreatic cancer patients [45, 50]. CHI3L1 is a glycoprotein implicated in cancer progression [45,46], while S100A8/A9 is a calcium binding protein that is also associated with inflammation and cancer progression to metastasis [47]. Recently we reported a transcriptomics analysis of TNBC mouse tumors from immunocompetent mice (4T-1/Balb/C model), treated with MBQ-167 5X a week for 4 weeks, where both CHI3L1 and S100A8/A9 were significantly downregulated [39]. Therefore, we wanted to assess if secretion of these inflammatory mediators were regulated in conditioned media between pancreatic cancer cells and macrophages when treated with Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors. Results demonstrate that MBQ-168 reduced CHI3L1 when KPC pancreatic cancer cells were exposed to macrophages by 70% at 500 nM (Fig. 5D). The same pattern was observed in single cultures of KPC cells and macrophages, where MBQ-168 significantly decreased CHI3L1 protein levels (Suppl Fig. 3). While CHI3L1 was not reduced significantly by Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors in macrophages exposed to cancer cells, we did see a significant reduction in S100A8/A9 levels by both MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 (Fig. 5E). However, this experimental design did not allow us to dissect the individual contribution from PDAC cells or macrophages to the release of these inflammatory mediators.

p-PAK levels in Puerto Rican PDAC tissue

We next determined endogenous activation of Rac and Cdc42 in PDAC patient tissue as an initial step towards identifying the patient population that would benefit from Rac/Cdc42 inhibitor therapy. Since Rac/Cdc42 activity assays were not possible from FFPE tissue, p-PAK levels were used as a marker of Rac/Cdc42 activity. Using pancreatic biopsy tissue obtained from Puerto Rican patients, IHC was conducted with specific antibodies for p-PAK (PAK1/2/3, Thr 423/402/421) or total PAK (PAK 1/2/3). Patient tissue was classified by the TNM system, which encompasses tumor extension, affected regional lymph nodes and metastasis.

The islets of Langerhans were used as an internal positive control, since Rac and Cdc42 are involved in insulin production, these insulin producing cells are positive for p-PAK (Suppl Fig.4) [42]. p-PAK staining was quantified from cancerous lesions, excluding healthy portions of the pancreas. Using a scoring system which quantified both the percentage of stained cells and staining intensity (Suppl Fig. 5), results demonstrate that p-PAK levels increase as the cancer stage progresses in pancreatic cancer patients, with a significant increase by stage 3 without changes in total PAK (Fig. 6). These results highlight the possibility that high grade PDAC patients may benefit from Rac/Cdc42 inhibitor therapy.

Discussion

Pancreatic cancer is known for having a lack of T-cell infiltration, dense extracellular matrix, and immunosuppressive cells [3,51]. However, TAMs make up 11% of the PDAC TME [2]. Therefore, studies have demonstrated that simultaneously targeting PDAC cells and TAMs can enhance T-cell response, decrease fibrosis and reduce metastatic progression [2,5,19]. The Rho GTPases Rac and Cdc42 regulate both oncogenic and immunosuppressive signaling and are involved in the differentiation and activation of M2-like TAMs in the TME [33]. Additionally, they contribute to the release of inflammatory cytokines that promote immunosuppression and malignancy [43].

Our studies show that targeting Rac and Cdc42 activity reduces migration, cancer cell viability, and induces apoptosis in different breast cancer cell types [34–36]. We recently reported that the MBQ-167 derivative MBQ-168 is also effective at reducing the viability and migration of breast cancer cells at nM concentrations without affecting normal mammary epithelial cells [36]. In this study, we provide insight on how Rac and Cdc42 inhibitors impact both PDAC and macrophage-like cells. MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 decreased Rac and Cdc42 activation in human PANC1 and THP-1 differentiated macrophages, albeit at higher concentration than in breast cancer cells. These results parallel the decrease of p-PAK activity in cells treated with MBQ-167 or MBQ-168.

We also report that dual Rac and Cdc42 inhibitors decrease cell viability of PDAC cells, without affecting the viability of macrophages at therapeutic concentration. Not only does it reduce Rac/Cdc42 activation, similar to the lead compound MBQ-167, but MBQ-168 reduces the viability of both human PANC1 and mouse KPC cells, which contain the KRAS G12D mutation. Targeted therapies in pancreatic cancer for KRAS mutations have been challenging. Only a handful of KRAS G12D inhibitors have reached clinical trials, such as RMC-9805 and MRTX1133, both targeting active KRAS. Despite its promising preclinical data, MRTX1133 therapy suffered from poor bioavailability and half-life, resulting in inconsistent drug exposure and subsequent Phase-1 clinical trial termination (NCT05737706) [12]. Daraxonrasib, which is effective against all RAS mutations, recently completed Phase 3 trials with successful efficacy data; however, concerns remain about the safety of this drug due to severe side effects [13]. By not affecting the viability of macrophages, our inhibitors may present with a more favorable safety profile in highly inflammatory diseases, such as PDAC. In an ongoing Phase 1 trial in advanced breast cancer patients, MBQ-167 has completed seven cohorts up to 320 mg BID with no adverse effects, and is therefore considered to be a safe alternative [40]. The success of the MBQ-167 Phase 1 trial also addresses a limitation in this study, where we did not test the effect of MBQ compounds on the viability of non-cancer pancreatic cells. However, we have previously shown that MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 do not affect non-cancer mammary epithelial cells [36]. Future studies will determine the safety profiles of MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 in non-cancer pancreatic epithelial cells.

Since cell migration is a pivotal process in metastasis. The results shown in this study demonstrate how inhibiting Rac and Cdc42 can decrease migration in both pancreatic and macrophage-like cells. Compared to our published results with breast cancer cells, in PDAC cells both MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 are effective at higher concentrations at Rac and Cdc42 activation and migration, which suggests that different cancer cell types have different sensitivities. Considering that the PDAC cell lines used are highly aggressive, this is not uncommon in both preclinical and clinical settings. We have previously shown that breast and macrophage-like cell lines have different expression levels of Rac and Cdc42 [38]. On the other hand, phagocytosis response was affected only by MBQ-167, while MBQ-168 was not as effective, which may be because we tested this response with single concentrations of the compounds. This result may also be attributed to the discrepancy in PAK activation observed in macrophages, where MBQ-167 was more effective at reducing p-PAK levels in macrophages compared to MBQ-168. Since, we have shown that PAK is central to the assembly of phagocytic cups in myeloid cells [53], the insensitivity of macrophage phagocytosis to MBQ-168 treatment may reflect the observed reduced efficacy of PAK activation in macrophages by MBQ-168 compared to MBQ-167. Future studies will determine, if the carbonyl group that is unique to MBQ-168 may affect macrophage PAK activation, and thus phagocytosis. Because phagocytosis response was affected by MBQ-167, this may indicate an impairment of defense mechanisms, since phagocytosis is key for the clearance of pathogens and dying cells. Taken together, the decrease in cellular migration and phagocytosis response by Rac/Cdc42 inhibition could be attributed to the observed reduction in cell surface actin structures that are central to migration and phagocytosis.

A limitation is that the wound healing assays needed to be incubated for 48 h for complete wound closure in the vehicle treated cells, which may obscure the effects on migration due to inhibition of cell viability. However, the Transwell assays, where we quantified the cells that traversed a membrane with 8micron diameter pores, were conducted only for 12 h. Since the viability assays demonstrated complete inhibition at 72 h, we conclude that the MBQ compounds inhibit macrophage and PDAC cell migration.

Studies have demonstrated that crosstalk in the TME enhances the migration of pancreatic cancer cells, and thus promotes metastatic progression [15,16]. Here, we demonstrated how inhibition of Rac and Cdc42 decreases pancreatic cancer cell migration when exposed to macrophages. A limitation is that the Transwell assays were conducted with only PDAC cells and macrophages. This reductive approach was not meant to recapitulate the complexities of the TME, but to elucidate the specific interaction of cancer cells with macrophages. In these experiments, MBQ-168 was more effective than MBQ-167 at significantly inhibiting KPC migration, which is also reflected in parallel decreases in IL-6, CHI3L1, and S100A8/A9 in Transwells with KPC cells and macrophages. This may indicate a cross-species difference with the KPC cells, or a reflection of the genetically controlled environment of the KPC cell line that recapitulates the mutations in a majority of PDAC tumors. Similar to the response in wound-healing assays, both MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 decreased macrophage migration when cultured with pancreatic cancer cells. Therefore, the MBQ compounds inhibit two highly relevant cell types of the PDAC tumor microenvironment: fibroblasts and macrophages, while targeting cancer cells.

We previously reported a reduced number of macrophage infiltration in tumors of mice following treatment with the Vav/Rac inhibitor, EHop-016 [38]. Moreover, we have shown that EHop-016 decreases IL-6 in plasma in breast cancer mouse models [38]. In Transwell experiments with PDAC cells and macrophages, dual Rac and Cdc42 inhibitors MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 significantly decrease IL-6, CHI3L1 and S100A8/A9. IL-6 has been attributed to induce M2 macrophage differentiation, proliferation, angiogenesis and survival [14,28,44]. Future studies will evaluate the impact of Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors on macrophage differentiation from M1 to M2 macrophages, since Rac and Cdc42 are involved in macrophage signaling [29]. However, we found that MBQ-167 treatment to mice with mammary fatpad tumors does not change M1/M2 macrophage ratio (CD206 negative vs positive) in spleen extracts [38].

A positive correlation between CHI3L1 and IL-6 levels have been shown in PDAC progression [45]. Moreover, S100A8/A9 (Calprotectin) has been attributed to surgical complications and damage due to inflammatory signaling [49,50]. Therefore, the observed reduction of proinflammatory cytokines such as CHI3L1, S100A8/A9, and IL-6 in response to the MBQ compounds suggest that Rac/Cdc42 inhibition may decrease the inflammatory cues that lead to disease progression and possibly reduce further damage. Given the need for predictive markers in PDAC, these inflammatory mediators can potentially be used as pharmacodynamic markers to assess disease progression and target engagement of Rac/Cdc42 inhibtiors.

IHC for p-PAK of PDAC tissue from Puerto Rican patients, demonstrate that p-PAK increases with disease stage. Of note, PAK 4 has been correlated with disease progression, and its inhibition enhances response to gemcitabine in PDAC patients [54]. PAK4 is a member of group II PAKs, which can be activated by Cdc42 [54]. In this study, we used an antibody to active and total forms of group I PAKs (PAK 1/2/3) because these PAKs are activated by both Rac and Cdc42. Future experiments will assess the expression and activation status of group II PAKS in PDAC patients. Additionally, we plan to evaluate if Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors can sensitize PDACs to chemotherapies such as paclitaxel and gemcitabine in pancreatic cancer, as was demonstrated with MBQ-167 and paclitaxel in breast cancer models [39], and where Sulindac was recently shown to chemosensitize PDAC patients to gemcitabine [7].

To establish if there is a correlation between p-PAK staining and PDAC stage, we need to increase the patient numbers in future studies. However, this initial study has established a working protocol to measure p-PAK levels in patient tissue and demonstrate the viability of using p-PAK as a therapeutic marker for patient stratification in future trials. Notably, these results underscore the potential utility of p-PAK as a predictive marker, following validation with larger patient numbers. Moreover, we found enhanced p-PAK activity in the islets of Langerhans, where Rac and Cdc42 are involved in insulin production (Suppl Fig. 4) [52]. Since PI3K/Akt/mTOR inhibitors have been shown to cause hyperglycemia due to inhibition of glucose uptake [55], the plasma glucose and c-peptide (indicator of insulin secretion) levels will also be monitored closely in future clinical trials of PDAC patients receiving Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors. However, it should be noted that there were no adverse effects on glucose or insulin levels reported from the current participants of the MBQ-167 Phase 1 trial in advanced breast cancer patients.

Overall, this study provides insight into the viability of using Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors as targeted therapeutics for pancreatic cancer. We have successfully demonstrated that MBQ-167 and MBQ-168 inhibit Rac/Cdc42 activation, PAK activation, cell viability and migration in both pancreatic cancer cells and macrophages. The findings in this study also implicate p-PAK as a potential pharmacodynamic marker that can be used to classify patients who will benefit from Rac/Cdc42 inhibitors and as a measure Rac/Cdc42 target engagement and efficacy.

Significance

Given the lack of response to current therapy and the dismal five-year survival rate for PDAC, there is a need for new therapeutic strategies. This study reports on the potential of small molecule compounds targeting the metastasis regulators Rac, Cdc42, and their downstream effector PAK, providing a new therapeutic opportunity for metastatic pancreatic cancer.
