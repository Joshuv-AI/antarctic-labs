Surface Modification for III-V Selective Area Molecular Beam Epitaxy of Non-Selective Mask Materials
Ashlee M. García, Byron D. Aguilar, William J. Doyle, Pernille Undrum Fathi, Federico Capasso, Daniel Wasserman, Seth R. Bank

Abstract
Selective-area embedded regrowth of III–V semiconductors by molecular beam epitaxy enables the seamless integration of metals and dielectrics into crystalline material for novel design of optoelectronic devices. However, traditional masks like SiO 2 \text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} and Si 3 ​ N 4 \text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} limit the design of high-contrast photonics in the infrared due to their high extinction coefficients at technologically relevant wavelengths. Consequently, there is a need to explore alternative mask materials to expand the selective area molecular beam epitaxy capabilities beyond those traditionally used. This study evaluates the deposition selectivity of the alternative materials Al 2 ​ O 3 \text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} , TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} , and HfO 2 \text{HfO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} , films with preferable spectral responses but higher surface reactivity. It was found that Al 2 ​ O 3 \text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} exhibits promising selective growth characteristics within typical GaAs growth temperatures, HfO 2 \text{HfO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} demonstrated a high non-selectivity dominated by Ga adsorption on the mask at temperatures up to 650 ∘ C, and TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} proved reactive during deposition. To achieve selective growth of highly non-selective and even reactive mask materials, a surface modification technique was employed to improve the selective growth characteristics of any given film. Selective growth of Si 3 ​ N 4 \text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} and TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} films was achieved with the application of a thin SiO 2 \text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} capping layer utilizing growth conditions typical of the GaAs/ SiO 2 \text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} system. The relationship between the thickness of SiO 2 \text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} caps and growth selectivity was examined, revealing that sub-1 nm capping layers can significantly influence the mask surface chemistry, indicating that by depositing a thin layer of SiO 2 \text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} , SiO 2 \text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} -like selectivity for any mask material can be realized without degrading its optical response.

Introduction

Selective-area embedded regrowth of III–V semiconductors by molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) enables the seamless integration of metals, dielectrics, and crystalline semiconductors. By defining crystal seeding regions with a mask and then encapsulating the amorphous structure in single crystalline III-V, it creates a unique design space for the development of optoelectronic and photonics devices. Deterministic growth of low-dimensional quantum emitters

The predominant mask materials in selective-area growth (SAG) are
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}

\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

\mu

Similarly, the development of embedded metasurfaces would benefit from the use of different mask materials with a diverse range of refractive indices, both higher and lower than the host III-V semiconductor, coupled with low extinction coefficients. Therefore, there is a need to expand the capabilities of selective-area MBE and enable access to a greater diversity of compatible mask materials. However, all-MBE selective-area regrowth has been historically difficult to achieve because of its poor III–V deposition selectivity, which leads to an aggregation of adatoms on the mask surface and ultimately the parasitic formation of polycrystalline material.

Experimental Section

2.1
MBE Compatibility

The emerging mask materials in this study were identified due to their broad range of optical constants
19
;
7
and their compatibility with the molecular beam epitaxy system. Explored materials were required to be inorganic to avoid carbon contamination and the critical properties of the materials that we identified were vapor pressure and sublimation temperature. Growth of III-V semiconductor via MBE requires substrate temperatures as high as 700 to 750
∘
C, as measured by bandedge thermometry. Therefore, the mask materials needed to be stable in this temperature range such that the sublimation point was far above this regime and the vapor pressure remained sufficiently low. Due to the success of
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, the properties of this thin film were used a metric for the chosen mask materials; all vapor pressures and sublimation temperatures were comparable to
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
.

\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

2.3
Molecular Beam Epitaxial Growth

After film preparation, the samples were baked at 450
∘
C, measured by thermocouple, in the buffer chamber under an atomic hydrogen flux supplied using a Veeco Atomic Hydrogen source (AHS) equipped with an inline Pd purifier. Samples were then transferred into the growth chamber and baked at 450
∘
C oriented away from the cells for 10 min. After rotating the sample toward the sources, co-deposition of Ga and
\text{As}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
was performed on the various films using a Veeco SUMO effusion cell for Ga and a Veeco Mark IV Arsenic cracker with the cracking zone set to 650
∘
C. A growth rate of 0.25
\mu
m/hr under a 12.8
\text{As}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
/Ga flux ratio was used for all growths in this study. The total deposition time was 23.7 min, which is the equivalent of 100 nm of GaAs growth under unity sticking conditions.

Sample Characterization

Results and Discussion

3.1
Selectivity Survey

An exploratory study of material selectivity was performed by observing polycrystalline GaAs growth on unpatterned films under identical MBE conditions. Co-deposition of Ga and As was performed on the unpatterned films at a rate of 0.25
\mu
m/h under a high tetrameric arsenic overpressure with a flux ratio of 12.8 at substrate temperatures ranging from 570 to 630
{}^{\circ}C
to span the ideal growth regime and typical selective growth temperatures for GaAs. After 100 nm GaAs deposition on the various films, planview scanning electron microscopy (SEM) observed variations in the amount of impinged GaAs and resulting nuclei morphology as shown in Figure
1
b. The observed characteristics were correlated with the magnitude of the selective growth mechanisms (Ga adsorption, desorption, diffusion) providing information regarding how readily Ga impinges on the non-crystalline surface and its mobility. Surface coverage, the fractional coverage of the polycrystalline III-V material on film surface, combines these attributes into a single metric to describe growth selectivity as a lower surface coverage can be correlated with increased desorption, increased diffusion, and decreased adsorption.

3.2
Surface Modification

Despite the furthered understanding of mask selectivity, the high reactivity of films such as
\text{HfO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
remained a significant barrier to their use in selective growth applications. In performing the selectivity survey, it was demonstrated that the addition of
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{HfO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
film on top of
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
degraded growth selectivity, therefore, it was hypothesized that the reverse could be leveraged as a ”surface modification” to enable selective MBE regrowth of highly non-selective or reactive materials using growth conditions suitable for
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
films.

The initial proof-of-concept was performed by including a 15 nm capping layer of
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
on the
\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
and
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
films with PECVD. The same aforementioned survey growth conditions were utilized and samples were regrown at the 600
∘
C substrate temperature. The introduction of the capping layer enabled nuclei morphology and surface coverage values nearly identical to the
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
film control growth (Figure
4
a). By then incorporating a
10\%
periodic supply epitaxy cycle (6s Ga shutter open, 54s closed), the selective growth regime of
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
was able to be leveraged to completely mitigate polycrystalline material formation on the capped
\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
and
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
films under conditions that would otherwise have not been selective. It’s highly beneficial to be able to modify materials such that they can be selectively regrown within the
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
regime as its inert nature currently allows for growth closest to that of the ideal III-V regime, which is necessary for high optical quality and band-engineering.

This is significant for embedded photonics and metasurfaces, however it is necessary to limit the influence of the capping layer on the spectral response when designing embedded structures, therefore efforts were taken to minimize the thickness of the modifying layer to achieve a traditional surface modification approach. The ideal case would be the use of a single treatment step to passivate the weak bonding sites on the film surface with the more preferable Si-O groups to achieve
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
-like growth selectivity. ALD of
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
was performed on
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
films identical to those from the previously discussed selectivity survey study. The thickness of this
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
modification layer was varied from 0.9 nm to 9.4 nm. At a substrate temperature of 600
∘
C, 100 nm of GaAs deposition was performed on the films. There was a significant decrease in polycrystalline GaAs build-up on the film with just 0.9 nm of the
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
modification, after which, the measured surface coverage and nuclei density of the resulting polycrystalline films followed an approximately linear relationship for the SiO
x
-modified films approaching the benchmark PECVD
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
values. The increasing coverage of the Si-O bonds as the thickness of the modification layer increases results in a less reactive surface and improved selectivity (Figure
5
).

4
Conclusion

In conclusion, this study underscores the potential of selective-area embedded regrowth of III–V semiconductors via MBE to enable innovative photonics and optoelectronic devices through the integration of diverse mask materials. While traditional mask materials such as
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and
\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
pose limitations in technologically-relevant wavelength ranges, the exploration of alternative materials such as
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, and
\text{HfO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
reveals promising opportunities for expanding selective regrowth capabilities. Our findings indicate that
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
offers favorable selectivity within the typical GaAs growth temperature regime, although
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
currently remains superior in terms of deposition selectivity.
\text{HfO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
on the other hand were not selective under typical regrowth conditions, therefore, we introduced the use of surface modification techniques for improving selective area MBE growth characteristics of a mask material. By applying a thin
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
capping layer to the mask, highly non-selective or reactive mask materials can be made inert, mitigating the polycrystalline deposition on less selective materials and enabling the use of the well-established III-V/
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
growth regimes. This approach opens new avenues for leveraging different mask materials to enhance growth selectivity without compromising on growth selectivity or optical performance.
