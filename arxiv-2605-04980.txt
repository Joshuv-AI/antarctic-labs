Conceptors for Semantic Steering
Ilias Triantafyllopoulos, Young-Min Cho, Ren Tao, Miranda Muqing Miao, Sunny Rai, Lyle Ungar, Sharath Chandra Guntuku, Neville Ryant, João Sedoc

Abstract
Activation-based steering provides control of LLM behavior at inference time, but the dominant paradigm reduces each concept to a single direction whose geometry is left largely unexamined. Rather than selecting a single steering direction, we use conceptors : soft projection matrices estimated from activations pooled across both poles of a bipolar concept, which preserve the concept’s full multidimensional subspace. A geometric analysis shows the bipolar subspace strictly subsumes the single-vector baseline. We further show that the conceptor quota provides a parameter-free layer-selection diagnostic, predicting concept separability with Pearson correlations up to r = 0.96 r=0.96 across three instruction-tuned models and three semantic dimensions. Beyond selection, conceptors admit a closed-form Boolean algebra (AND, OR, NOT): we evaluate conceptor compositionality on thematically related sub-concepts. Across a systematic five-axis design-space evaluation, conceptors match or outperform additive baselines at layers where concept subspaces are multi-dimensional while producing substantially fewer degenerate outputs. Conceptor steering is a geometrically principled, compositional, and practically safer alternative to single-direction steering from a limited number of contrastive pairs.

Rather than collapsing this structure onto one vector, a conceptor retains the full subspace, continuously attenuating each principal direction according to its signal energy (Eq.

).
Additionally, its Boolean algebra (NOT, AND, OR) enables principled composition of multiple concepts without access to original activation data.

2
Background

This section introduces the conceptor framework originally developed by
Jaeger 2024
for controlling recurrent neural networks and describes how its core mathematical machinery can be repurposed for steering the internal representations of transformer-based language models.

Conceptor matrices.

Conceptors were introduced as a mechanism for managing and selectively recalling dynamical patterns stored in recurrent neural networks. Given a collection of
N
neural activation vectors
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
associated with a particular concept, the conceptor matrix
\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is defined as the solution to the regularized reconstruction problem:

\mathbf{C}=\arg\min_{C}\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|x_{i}-Cx_{i}\|^{2}+\alpha^{-2}\|C\|_{F}^{2},

where
\|\cdot\|_{F}
denotes the Frobenius norm and
\alpha>0
is the
aperture
parameter controlling the trade-off between faithfully preserving the activation patterns and regularization. Defining the sample correlation matrix
R=\frac{1}{N}X^{\top}X
, where
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
has activation vectors as rows, the optimization admits the closed-form solution:

\mathbf{C}=R\left(R+\alpha^{-2}I\right)^{-1}.

This reveals the conceptor as a
soft
projection operator: rather than fully retaining or discarding each direction,
\mathbf{C}
continuously attenuates directions according to the signal energy in
R
.

Aperture and eigenvalue interpretation.

In the eigenbasis of
R=U\,\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{d})\,U^{\top}
, the conceptor is diagonal with entries:

\gamma_{j}=\frac{\sigma_{j}}{\sigma_{j}+\alpha^{-2}},\qquad j=1,\ldots,d,

(1)

where each
\gamma_{j}\in[0,1]
is a soft gating coefficient for the
j
-th principal direction. Directions with high signal energy (
\sigma_{j}\gg\alpha^{-2}
) are preserved (
\gamma_{j}\to 1
), while low-energy ones are suppressed (
\gamma_{j}\to 0
). The aperture
\alpha
acts as a resolution control: large
\alpha
causes the conceptor to approach the identity, while small
\alpha
produces a narrow subspace projection. This continuous interpolation distinguishes conceptors from hard subspace methods such as PCA truncation or binary masks.

Spectral diagnostics for layer selection.

Selecting the intervention layer is a practical challenge for any activation-based steering method. The eigenvalue structure of

R
is a natural starting point: a rapidly saturating spectrum indicates a low-dimensional concept subspace, a slowly decaying one indicates distributed structure. The standard summary, cumulative explained variance ratio
\mathrm{EVR}(k)=\tfrac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{j}}
, sees only the top of the spectrum and discards medium-energy structure that we show is informative for steering.

Jaeger 2024
introduced the
conceptor quota

q(\mathbf{C})=\tfrac{1}{d}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{C})=\tfrac{1}{d}\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}
as a soft effective dimensionality of
\mathbf{C}
: the average of the gating coefficients
\gamma_{j}
in Eq.
1
, lying in
[0,1]
.
Because the gating is sigmoidal, the quota integrates the
entire
spectrum, capturing intermediate-energy directions an EVR head-truncation discards.
We adopt
q(\mathbf{C})
as our default diagnostic; Section
4.1
compares it empirically to EVR.

Boolean operations and compositionality.

A key advantage of the conceptor framework is the existence of principled algebraic operations that mirror Boolean logic
(
Jaeger 2024
)
. The
NOT
operation,
\neg\mathbf{C}=I-\mathbf{C}
, suppresses a concept by passing its complementary subspace. The
AND
operation,
\mathbf{C}_{A}\wedge\mathbf{C}_{B}=(\mathbf{C}_{A}^{-1}+\mathbf{C}_{B}^{-1}-I)^{-1}
, yields a conceptor retaining only directions supported by both operands—effectively their subspace intersection—while the
OR
operation, defined via De Morgan’s law, captures the union of two concept subspaces. Crucially, all three operations require only the conceptor matrices themselves, with no access to the original activation data,
making them well-suited for combining conceptors across different corpora. While this algebra is complete, our empirical evaluation focuses on AND and NOT (via AND-NOT), which most directly expose the role of subspace overlap; OR behavior follows from the same geometry via De Morgan’s law.

Application to transformers.

Conceptors were originally developed for echo state networks
(
Jaeger 2001
)
, where they gated temporal patterns in a fixed recurrent reservoir. We adapt them to transformers
(
Vaswani et al. 2017
)
by using concept-associated hidden states in place of reservoir trajectories and applying the conceptor as a forward-pass hook at a chosen layer during generation. This relies on the shared structure of both systems: high-dimensional distributed activations in which concepts occupy overlapping but distinguishable subspaces, while leaving the mathematical core intact.

Bipolar versus unipolar steering.

To capture the full extent of a concept’s subspace, we train each conceptor on the
union of both poles
, pooling positive
and
negative sentiment activations into a single correlation matrix.
This is a key departure from prior work: additive methods compute a difference vector between poles
(
Turner et al. 2024
;
Rimsky et al. 2024
)
or steer toward a single target pole, and prior conceptor work trains separate operators per direction
(
Postmus and Abreu 2024
)
, all inherently unipolar. Conceptors themselves are agnostic to this choice, but for inherently bipolar concepts such as sentiment or political leaning, pooling both poles captures the full between-pole subspace that single-pole training (e.g., a “positive” rather than a general “sentiment” conceptor) misses.

3
Related Work

3.1
Activation-Based Steering

Existing methods for steering language models via internal representations span a spectrum from simple additive interventions to learned subspace edits, but all require either supervised training or collapse concepts onto a single direction.
Activation Addition
(
Turner et al. 2024
)
and Contrastive Activation Addition
(
Rimsky et al. 2024
)
inject a single mean-difference vector into the residual stream; Representation Surgery
(
Singh et al. 2024
)
generalizes these by optimally transforming activations to match both the mean and covariance of concept distributions. Inference-Time Intervention
(
Li et al. 2023
)
and CORAL
(
Miao et al. 2026
)
train probes on hidden activations to identify and shift informative directions. Recent geometric methods such as Spherical Steering
(
You et al. 2026
)
argue against magnitude distortion and steer via norm-preserving geodesic rotations.
Concurrently,
Sofroniew et al. 2026
demonstrate that emotion concepts in Claude Sonnet 4.5 are represented as linear directions recoverable via mean-difference vectors, and that steering along these directions causally shifts model behavior in forced-choice evaluations.
Their work uses 171 unipolar emotion vectors; by contrast, our bipolar conceptor formulation captures the full concept subspace in a single operator, subsuming individual polar directions.
Conceptors unify these geometric intuitions with second-order statistical constraints; they use regularized correlation structures to quiet noisy dimensions without squashing the manifold. This framework characterizes multi-dimensional concept volumes rather than single polar axes, enabling more expressive and algebraically compositional control.
Unlike the polar or asymmetric mappings found in nearly every steering method, conceptors function as bipolar filters that support training-free, algebraically compositional steering via principled Boolean logic.
More expressive approaches such as LoReFT
(
Wu et al. 2024
)
learn low-rank interventions on frozen representations via gradient-based optimization.
Sparse autoencoders
(
Bricken et al. 2023
;
Templeton et al. 2024
)
decompose activations into interpretable features that can be individually amplified, but require substantial training overhead and lack algebraic compositionality.

3.2
Conceptor Theory and Prior Applications

Conceptors were introduced by
Jaeger 2024
for managing dynamical patterns in echo state networks
(
Jaeger 2001
)
and later formalized by
Jaeger 2017
.
In NLP, conceptor negation has been used to remove uninformative variance from word embeddings
(
Liu et al. 2019a
)
and to debias representations
(
Karve et al. 2019
)
, while the Boolean OR operation has supported continual learning of sentence representations
(
Liu et al. 2019b
)
;
He and Jaeger 2018
extended the framework to deep feedforward networks.
Most directly related to our work,
Postmus and Abreu 2024
applied conceptors as steering matrices for GPT-J and GPT-NeoX, showing improvements over additive baselines and effective multi-concept composition via Boolean AND.
We substantially extend this line of research with a parameter-free spectral diagnostic for layer selection, a systematic five-factor design-space exploration, and evaluation across broader semantic dimensions.

3.3
Layer Selection and Evaluation

Selecting where in a network to intervene is typically done by training probing classifiers at every layer and choosing the best
(
Belinkov 2022
)
, a process whose cost scales with model depth and concept count.
Prior probing studies have established that semantic properties peak in middle layers
(
Tenney et al. 2019
;
Conneau et al. 2018
)
, a pattern our spectral diagnostic recovers without any supervised infrastructure.

Bipolar Subspace Geometry

A core design choice is training each conceptor on the
union of both poles
of a bipolar concept.
Prior conceptor-based steering work
(
Postmus and Abreu 2024
)
trained separate unipolar conceptors—one per target function (e.g., antonym, translation)—from activations of a single class of demonstrations, without any contrastive or bipolar structure.
We validate our method geometrically by measuring the capture fraction of the DiffMean steering vector
\Delta\bar{v}=\bar{v}_{+}-\bar{v}_{-}

\text{Capture}(\Delta\bar{v},\mathbf{V}_{k})=\frac{\|\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\top}\Delta\bar{v}\|^{2}}{\|\Delta\bar{v}\|^{2}},

(2)

where
\mathbf{V}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times k}
is the matrix of top-
k
right singular vectors of the activation matrix
\mathbf{X}
, and
\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\top}
is the corresponding orthogonal projector.

5
Conceptor-Based Semantic Steering

We now use conceptors as inference-time steering operators. We evaluate sentiment (positive/negative valence), political leaning (left/right), and depression (depressive/healthy language) on Gemma-2-2B-IT and Gemma-2-9B-IT. We first show that conceptors outperform additive baselines on a per-layer sweep (Section
5.1
), then demonstrate Boolean compositionality on two concept pairs of contrasting subspace overlap (Section
5.2

5.3

5.4
).
As discussed in Section
4.2
, our bipolar conceptors are trained on the union of both poles, so they represent the full concept subspace rather than a single separating direction; geometrically, this subspace contains the DiffMean direction plus surrounding multi-dimensional structure.

Conceptors Outperform Additive Baselines

Figure

shows a consistent advantage for conceptors. On Gemma-2-2B-IT, they reach win ratios of 0.70–0.82 across most layers, while both additive baselines remain near 0.50–0.62. The gap is largest where the concept subspace is multi-dimensional: for sentiment, quota peaks in early-to-mid layers (3–10), matching the layers where conceptors most outperform additive baselines; for political leaning, the richer subspace appears later (15–24), and the advantage shifts accordingly. Qualitative examples in Appendix
A.1
show that the best sentiment conceptor can reverse the model’s argumentative stance while preserving fluency.

Output stability is the main practical difference. Additive baselines often produce
degenerate outputs
: repetitive and incoherent text that fills the generation budget. We classify a configuration as degenerate when its mean output length is more than twice the corresponding base-model length,

\bar{T}_{\text{steered}}/\bar{T}_{\text{base}}>2.0
.
This captures the dominant failure mode while ignoring minor length changes from legitimate content variation, and we verify it by manual inspection. Addition is degenerate in 58% of configurations, DiffMean in 30%, and conceptor interpolation in only 13%. This suggests that conceptors steer within the model’s natural activation manifold, whereas additive perturbations—especially Addition, which injects an absolute mean vector rather than a contrastive direction—can push hidden states out of distribution and disrupt generation.

Forced-choice MCQ task.

Open-ended generation makes multi-concept effects hard to disentangle, since each dimension requires a separate classifier and pole interactions are confounded by fluency degradation.
We therefore introduce a forced-choice multiple-choice question (MCQ) evaluation that reads behavior directly from the logit distribution over answer-letter tokens. Following recent forced-choice steering analyses
(
Sofroniew et al. 2026
;
Rimsky et al. 2024
)
but adapting the setup to bipolar compositional selectivity, each question presents four randomly shuffled options: one aligned with
both
concepts, one with each single concept, and one neutral distractor. The model’s choice is read from the A/B/C/D logits after a single forward pass.
We construct 300 questions per concept pair with OpinionsQA-inspired topics
(
Santurkar et al. 2023
)
, using generic preference prompts such as “what should be the federal government’s top priority?” paired with the four options.
For each pair we compare the base model, AND of the two conceptors, each single-concept conceptor, and AND-NOT (one concept while suppressing the other).

Overlap-driven pair selection.

The AND of two conceptors retains only directions jointly supported by both operands, so near-orthogonal concepts produce a small active subspace by construction. Conversely, the same low overlap makes NOT and OR well-behaved, since removing one concept leaves the other largely intact. This motivates a two-pair design: political
\times
sentiment tests the geometric limit of AND and the selectivity of NOT under low overlap, while abortion stance
\times

6
Conclusion

We presented conceptors as inference-time steering operators for large language models. Training conceptors bipolarly on both concept poles, rather than reducing concepts to single directions or pole-specific subspaces, lets them retain richer concept geometry: bipolar subspaces capture
{\geq}95\%
of the mean-difference direction, while the conceptor quota predicts layer-wise separability with Pearson
r
up to 0.96. Low cross-concept overlap (
0.18
–
0.38
) explains why NOT and OR preserve unrelated concepts, while AND requires thematically shared structure. Across sentiment, political leaning, and depression, conceptors match or outperform additive baselines, while better preserving fluency and reducing degenerate outputs by attenuating rather than translating activations. Beyond steering, the quota enables fast label-free layer selection, and Boolean operations support compositional steering without retraining, making conceptors a practical and stable alternative for multi-dimensional concept control.

Two directions are immediate. First, multi-layer steering may close the remaining gap on harder concepts such as depression and should transfer to deployment-size models, since the geometry we exploit is architecture-agnostic. Second, the closed-form Boolean algebra opens a path to inference-time alignment: AND-NOT to suppress a harmful concept while preserving a target capability, OR to combine related sub-concepts, moving compositional safety from prompt engineering to a routine algebraic operation.

7
Limitations & Ethical Considerations

We evaluate conceptor steering on three models, not on deployment-scale models (13B+/70B+). Scaling cost is dominated by hidden width
H
(each layer’s conceptor inverts a dense
H{\times}H
matrix;
{\sim}30
s per concept on Gemma-2-9B-IT), still cheaper than fine-tuning and only modestly above additive baselines, and the underlying geometry is architecture-agnostic.
Additional limitations include residual incoherence at high steering strength, single-layer steering from only 100 contrastive pairs, reliance on automated classifiers for win-ratio scoring, compositionality experiments limited to two concept pairs, and English-only evaluation.

Ethically, conceptor steering is a general controllability mechanism: the same tools that increase positivity or sharpen analysis could also amplify partisan bias, suppress dissent, or manipulate framing. Our political conceptors inherit biases from their training pairs and are applied to instruction-tuned models with documented left-leaning priors
(
Feng et al. 2023
;
Santurkar et al. 2023
)
; we therefore frame these experiments as controllability analysis, not persuasion. The depression conceptor is likewise only a research probe, not a diagnostic, screening, counseling, or clinical tool: steered outputs may resemble depressive language without reflecting any user’s mental state. We excluded suicidal-ideation pairs and evaluated only on adult, public-domain text. Any released conceptors or training pairs should be used with rate limits, human review, and clear disclosure that outputs are being steered.

B.6
Bipolar vs. Unipolar DiffMean

The standard DiffMean formulation
(
Rimsky et al. 2024
)
computes a steering vector as
\bar{v}_{+}-\bar{v}_{-}
(positive-pole mean minus negative-pole mean), producing a
unipolar
direction that steers exclusively toward the positive pole.
This is appropriate for unipolar concepts (e.g., “speaks like a pirate”)
(
Sofroniew et al. 2026
)
, but for inherently bipolar concepts—where the goal is to steer toward the
concept
(e.g., political content in general) rather than a specific pole—a unipolar vector is inefficient: it boosts one pole at the expense of the other.

Our bipolar DiffMean variant addresses this by computing
\bar{v}_{\text{all}}-\bar{v}_{\text{null}}
, where
\bar{v}_{\text{all}}
averages over
both
poles and
\bar{v}_{\text{null}}
averages over concept-neutral sentences.
This isolates concept-relevant signal while preserving pole symmetry.

Table
4
illustrates the asymmetry of unipolar DiffMean empirically on sentiment steering (Gemma-2-2B-IT).
The standard DiffMean vector (DiffMean
+
)
\bar{v}_{+}-\bar{v}_{-}
achieves a high win ratio on positive prompts (0.64) but only 0.16 on negative prompts: it actively
suppresses
the negative pole.
The reversed vector
\bar{v}_{-}-\bar{v}_{+}
(DiffMean
-
) shows the mirror pattern: 0.72 on negative prompts but only 0.21 on positive.
Neither can steer toward “sentiment in general” without severe pole-specific bias.
The bipolar conceptor, by contrast, achieves balanced performance across both poles (0.76 positive, 0.48 negative, 0.62 overall), confirming that the full subspace projection amplifies both poles rather than favoring one.

Hyperparameter Feature Importance

The design-space evaluation summarized in Table
2

\Delta R^{2}

placement
- Q1,
injection_mode
- Q5) are the primary drivers of steering success, whereas numeric hyperparameters and model architecture exert a comparatively minor influence (Figure

placement
- Q1 is the strongest global predictor of configuration quality, the

operator yields the largest independent boost to win-ratio performance.
