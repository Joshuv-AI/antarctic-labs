Higher-Order Equilibrium Tracking for EM-Compressible Online Estimation
ZhiMing Li, Yue Song

Abstract
We study online estimation in latent-variable models by recasting the problem as tracking a moving empirical equilibrium. Standard online EM and stochastic approximation analyses primarily study convergence toward the population parameter and typically do not isolate the empirical batch optimum from the online tracking error at finite horizon. Our framework decomposes the online estimate into the frozen batch equilibrium at the current running statistic and a tracking lag that captures the algorithm’s delay behind this moving target. We prove a batch-to-online transfer theorem : provided ∥ e T ∥ L 2 = o ( T − 1 / 2 ) \lVert e_{T}\rVert_{L^{2}}=o(T^{-1/2}) , the online estimator inherits the batch central limit theorem and the sharp first-order risk constant. Our key observation is that the empirical optimum evolves on a smooth equilibrium manifold indexed by the running statistic. An m m -th order equilibrium-jet predictor combined with an order- ν \nu frozen corrector yields localized tracking rates O ⁡ ( T − ν ⁡ ( m + 1 ) ) O(T^{-\nu(m+1)}) . We formalize EM-compressibility and EM-jet R -compressibility as the structural conditions that make the equilibrium response and the Newton corrector evaluable from a retained streaming statistic. The theory is instantiated in latent linear Gaussian covariance estimation, where the first-order scheme operates on a compressed d × d d\times d statistic with explicit finite-sample risk envelopes and a certified restart rule.

Discussion and limitations

The key phenomenon is that the empirical optimum evolves on a smooth equilibrium manifold driven by the running statistic. Higher-order prediction exploits this geometric structure to remove successive orders of the moving-target drift.

The compression condition is also structural. A model may admit a score that closes on a retained statistic while its statistic derivatives or Hessian–vector products are not available at the same cost. The EM-jet
1
condition of Definition
2
is therefore part of the method, not a consequence of EM-compressibility alone.

The results are local around a smooth frozen batch-target branch. This is the regime in which the score equation has a unique local solution, the response operator is well conditioned, and the frozen corrector is contractive. The Gaussian specialization provides a restart certificate for entering this regime, but the theory does not assert global convergence from arbitrary initialization without a restart.

Related Works and Broader Impact Statement

Broader Impact Statement

This paper contributes foundational mathematical theory for online covariance estimation in latent variable models. The results—EM-compressibility, equilibrium tracking rates, and batch-to-online transfer—are structural properties of statistical estimators and do not target any specific application domain.

On the positive side, efficient online covariance estimators can benefit scientific applications that process streaming data under memory constraints, such as real-time sensor fusion, adaptive signal processing, and sequential experimental design. The theoretical guarantees developed here (sharp asymptotic risk, certified contraction) could help practitioners choose provably sound algorithms in safety-critical settings where uncertified heuristics carry risk.

We do not identify direct negative societal consequences of the theory itself. The latent Gaussian model studied here is a standard statistical abstraction; no private data, human subjects, or dual-use capabilities are involved. As with any estimation methodology, downstream applications should be evaluated for fairness, privacy, and robustness concerns on a case-by-case basis; such evaluation is outside the scope of this theoretical contribution.

Proposition II.1 (gradient splitting in the spectral–rotational chart)

Auxiliary Lemma VI.L5 (finite-horizon pathwise drift event)

Claim.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
and
t_{0}\leq T
. Define

\beta_{t_{0},T,\delta}:=\beta_{d}+\log\frac{4(T-t_{0}+1)}{\delta},\qquad A_{D}(t_{0},T,\delta):=4\lVert D_{*}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\beta_{t_{0},T,\delta}/t_{0}}+\beta_{t_{0},T,\delta}/t_{0}\Bigr),

with

\lVert R_{t+1}^{\mathrm{ad}}\rVert\leq C_{R}B_{T,\delta}(t_{0})^{2}/(t+1)^{2}
.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
VI.8.2
at each
t\in\{t_{0},\ldots,T\}
with confidence
\delta/(2(T-t_{0}+1))
and union-bound:
\mathbb{P}(\sup_{t}\lVert D_{t}-D_{*}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}>A_{D})\leq\delta/2
. For
\lVert z_{t}\rVert^{2}
: since
\lVert z_{t}\rVert^{2}/\lVert D_{*}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
is dominated by
\chi_{d}^{2}

and a union over

give

. On the intersection event

III.2

\square

VI.10
Theorem VI.10 (finite-horizon high-probability tracking)

Claim.

Assume the linear contraction of Proposition
VI.7
on the radius-
r
tube and
D\in\mathcal{V}_{D}
. Set

\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}:=\min\Bigl\{\tfrac{r}{4},\tfrac{1-q}{4q}r\Bigr\}.

Suppose
\sqrt{d}\,A_{D}(t_{0},T,\delta)\leq r_{D}
and the restart satisfies

VI.10.1
Proof

STEP 1 (
D_{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{D}
and small-remainder regime on
\mathcal{E}_{T,\delta}
).

By Auxiliary Lemma

on
\mathcal{E}_{T,\delta}
,
\lVert D_{t}-D_{*}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq A_{D}(t_{0},T,\delta)
for all

and the local contraction of Proposition
VI.7
is applicable. Furthermore,
\lVert R_{t+1}^{\mathrm{ad}}\rVert\leq C_{R}B_{T,\delta}(t_{0})^{2}/(t+1)^{2}\leq\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}
for every

, where the second inequality uses the monotonicity of
s\mapsto s^{-2}
and (VI.10.3).

STEP 2 (tube invariance by induction).

Set
e_{t}:=\vartheta_{t}-r(D_{t})
. By Proposition

,
\widetilde{\vartheta}_{t+1}-r(D_{t+1})=e_{t}-R_{t+1}^{\mathrm{ad}}

. Inductively assume

so the linear contraction of Proposition
VI.7
applies (noting

from STEP 1):

\lVert e_{t+1}\rVert\leq q\bigl(\tfrac{r}{2}+\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}\bigr)\leq q\tfrac{r}{2}+q\cdot\tfrac{1-q}{4q}r=\tfrac{1+q}{4}r\leq\tfrac{r}{2}.

This closes the induction.

STEP 3 (one-step recursion).

Combining the contraction with the predictor identity, on
\mathcal{E}_{T,\delta}
,

\lVert e_{t+1}\rVert\leq q\,\lVert e_{t}\rVert+q\,\lVert R_{t+1}^{\mathrm{ad}}\rVert\leq q\,\lVert e_{t}\rVert+q\,\frac{C_{R}\,B_{T,\delta}(t_{0})^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}.

to

gives

STEP 4 (geometric-harmonic sum at
t_{0}=\lceil T/2\rceil
).

For
t_{0}=\lceil T/2\rceil
, every
s
in the sum satisfies
s\geq T/2
, so

\sum_{s=t_{0}+1}^{T}q^{T-s}\,s^{-2}\leq\frac{4}{T^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{k}=\frac{4}{(1-q)\,T^{2}},

which substituted into (VI.10.8) gives (VI.10.4).
\square

and

are precomputed once. Let

and

VII.11
VII.11. Reproducibility and compute

All experiments are seeded via
--seed 123
and use float64. Running the full suite:

python experiments/run_all.py # full configuration
python experiments/run_all.py --quick # smoke test (~90s)

Output CSVs are written to
experiments/results/
.

Hardware and runtime.

All experiments were executed on a single laptop equipped with an AMD Ryzen 7-6800H CPU (8 cores, 16 threads, 4.7 GHz turbo), 16 GB DDR5-4800 RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU (not used; all computation is CPU-only in float64). The total wall-clock time for the full experiment suite, including the extended
T
-grid runs, is approximately one hour.
