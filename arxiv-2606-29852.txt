Bessel Distributions and Kloosterman Sums
Li Cai, Jingsong Chai, Yadi Liu

Abstract
. Let G G be a split reductive group over a p p -adic field. We give germ expansions of Kloosterman integrals for G G . As an application, we prove that Bessel distributions are regular for all generic representations on G G provided that Kloosterman sums for any Levi subgroups of G G have nontrivial bounds.

1.
Introduction

1.0.1.
Bessel distributions

Let
G
be a reductive group over a
p
-adic field
F
.
Denote by
{\mathcal{S}}(G)
the space of Schwartz functions on
G
. Let

\pi
be an irreducible smooth admissible representation on
G
with
\pi^{*}
denoting its linear dual. Let
\pi^{\vee}
be the contragredient
representation of
\pi
, that is, the smooth part of
\pi^{*}
. The group
G
then acts on all these spaces and these actions
induce actions of
{\mathcal{S}}(G)
. Note that for any
\ell\in(\pi^{\vee})^{*}
and
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(G)
,
\pi^{\vee}(f)\ell
is smooth so that

\pi^{\vee}(f)\ell\in(\pi^{\vee})^{\vee}=\pi
. For any
\ell_{1}\in\pi^{*}
and
\ell_{2}\in(\pi^{\vee})^{*}
, the relative character
associated to
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
is defined as

B_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(f)=\ell_{1}(\pi^{\vee}(f)\ell_{2}),\quad f\in{\mathcal{S}}(G).

The relative character is a basic object in the relative Langlands program. Such relative characters arise naturally in the relative trace formula and local harmonic analysis of spherical varieties. Usually, the linear functionals
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}

are given to be invariant for a specific subgroup
H
of
G
. Consider the
group case, that is
H
embeds into
G=H^{2}
diagonally. For suitable
H
-invariant linear functionals
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
on an irreducible admissible representation
\pi=\sigma\boxtimes\sigma^{\vee}
on
G
, the relative character
B_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
is essentially the character of
\sigma
.

It is a fundamental problem to determine whether the relative character
B_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
is regular in the sense that there exists a (unique)
smooth function
j
defined on a dense subset of
G
, which is locally integrable on
G
, such that

B_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(f)=\int_{G}j(g)f(g)dg,\quad f\in{\mathcal{S}}(G).

The regularity for (usual) characters of irreducible admissible representations is the
celebrated work of Harish-Chandra
[
HC70
,
HC99
]
.
When
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are
H
-invariant linear functionals, where
H
is the subgroup of fixed points of an involution of
G
, this kind of relative characters are studied in the work of Rader-Rallis
[
RR
]
, which shows that the restriction of
B_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
to the set of regular elements is given by a smooth function
[
RR
, Corollary 5.2]
. But often this smooth function is not locally integrable, and
B_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
is not regular
[
RR
, Section 7]
. It is an interesting and subtle question to find regular relative characters
[
Guo
,
Ha
]
.

In this paper, we shall focus on the Whittaker case. Let
G
be a split reductive group over
F
. Let
B=AN
be
a Borel subgroup of
G
with
A
a maximal torus of
G
and
N
the unipotent radical of
B
. Denote by
X^{*}(A)
the characters on
A

and
\Delta\subset X^{*}(A)
the set of simple roots. Let
\psi
be a generic character on
N
, in other words,
\psi|_{N_{\alpha}}\not=1

for all
\alpha\in\Delta
with
N_{\alpha}
the corresponding root subgroup of
N
. Consider the space
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{N}(\pi,\psi)
of Whittaker
functionals. The dimension of this space is less than one. Assume
\pi
is generic (with respect to
\psi
), that is, there is a nonzero Whittaker functional

\ell_{1}\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{N}(\pi,\psi)
. The contragredient representation
\pi^{\vee}
is generic (with respect to
\psi^{-1}
) and we take a nonzero Whittaker functional
\ell_{2}\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{N}(\pi^{\vee},\psi^{-1})
. The relative character
B_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
is also called the Bessel distribution for
\pi
.

By the multiplicity one property, the regularity of Bessel distributions is independent of the choice of Whittaker functionals.
By the works of Baruch, the regularity is known for
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{3}

[
Ba97
,
Ba04
]
. Moreover, for the general case (that is,
G
is a quasi-split group over a local field
F
), Baruch
[
Ba01
]
showed that the restriction of Bessel distributions to the open Bruhat cell is given by a smooth function (see Theorem
3.3
). We believe that the regularity of Bessel distributions holds for the general case.

1.0.2.
Kloosterman sums

Let
G
be a split reductive group with a Borel subgroup
B=AN
. We consider the system of Kloosterman sums
\{\mathrm{Kl}_{d}\}_{d\geq 1}
on

G
with respect to a triple
(\psi,\dot{w_{0}},\{K_{d}\}_{d\geq 1})
(See Definition
3.15
). Here

\psi
is a generic character on
N
,
\dot{w_{0}}
is a representative for the longest Weyl element
w_{0}\in W
and

\{K_{d}\}_{d\geq 1}
is a filtration of open compact subgroups of
G
satisfying certain conditions.

For each
d\geq 1
,
the Kloosterman sum
{\mathrm{Kl}}_{d}
is the following function on
A

\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(a)=\sum_{x\in X_{d}(\dot{w_{0}}a)}\psi(u(x))\psi(u^{\prime}(x)),\quad a\in A.

In the above, we consider the (finite) Kloosterman set with the two maps

X_{d}(\dot{w_{0}}a)=N_{d}\backslash\left(N\dot{w_{0}}aN\cap K_{d}\right)/N_{d},\quad u:X_{d}(\dot{w_{0}}a)\rightarrow N_{d}\backslash N,\quad u^{\prime}:X_{d}(\dot{w_{0}}a)\rightarrow N/N_{d}.

Here,
N_{d}=N\cap K_{d}
and if
[n_{1}\dot{w_{0}}an_{2}]\in X_{d}(\dot{w_{0}}a)
with
n_{1},n_{2}\in N
and
a\in A
, then

u([n_{1}\dot{w_{0}}an_{2}])=[n_{1}],\quad u^{\prime}([n_{1}\dot{w_{0}}an_{2}])=[n_{2}].

Example 1.1
.

Consider the case
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
. Take
\psi\left[\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right]=\psi_{F}(x)
for some nontrivial additive
character
\psi_{F}
on
F
,
\dot{w_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
and
\{K_{d}\}_{d}
the principal congruence subgroups of
G
.
Consider those
a=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\in A
with
v(\alpha)=k\geq d
. Then
\mathrm{Kl}_{d}
is the restricted Kloosterman sum

\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(a)=\sum_{x\in 1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{d}/1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{k}}\psi_{F}\left(\frac{x+x^{-1}}{\alpha}\right).

In this case, we have the Weil bound (see Proposition
4.1
), that is, there is a constant
C
such that

|\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(a)\cdot\delta(a)^{1/4}|=|\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(a)|\cdot|\alpha|^{1/2}\leq C.

Here,
\delta
is the modulus character of
B
.

The system of Kloosterman sums
\{\mathrm{Kl}_{d}\}_{d}
is called having a nontrivial bound (See Definition
3.15
)
if for any
d\gg 0
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
such that

\left|\mathrm{Kl}_{d}(a)\cdot\delta^{1/2-\varepsilon}(a)\right|\leq C,\quad a\in A.

In the above, the exponent
1/2-\varepsilon
of
\delta
for any
\varepsilon>0
is viewed as a nontrivial bound while the exponent
1/2
is viewed as the trivial bound. This follows from the counting formula for the cardinality of the Kloosterman set
X(\dot{w_{0}}a)
by
Dąbrowski-Reeder
[
DR98
]
. See Example
3.18
for more details.

Now, we can state the main result. Choose the generic character
\psi=\psi_{0}
given before Definition
2.2
.
For each subset
I\subset\Delta
, we fix a representative
w_{I}^{0}
of the longest Weyl element
w_{I}\in W_{I}
. Let
\{K_{d}\}_{d\geq 0}
be
a filtration of open compact subgroups of
G
as above.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.19
)
.

Assume that for any
I\subset\Delta
, the system of Kloosterman sums on the standard Levi
M_{I}
associated to
I
with respect to the triple

(\psi_{0}|_{N_{M_{I}}}^{-1},w_{I}^{0},\{K_{d}\cap M_{I}\}_{d})

has a nontrivial bound. Then, the regularity for the Bessel distribution on
\pi
holds for any irreducible generic representation
\pi
on
G
.

The strategy for the proof of Theorem
1.2
is the same as the one of Baruch for the case
{\mathrm{GL}}_{3}
. The regularity is closely related to the local integrability of Kloosterman integrals
which is a generalization of the Kloosterman sum. The analytic behaviour of Kloosterman integrals is controlled by that of Kloosterman sums
via the Shalika germ expansion.

There is an inductive structure for the germs of Kloosterman integrals
(See Proposition
2.17
). In particular,
the study of Kloosterman sums for proper Levi subgroups usual reduces to
those for lower rank groups.

To obtain the nontrivial bound for Kloosterman sums on
G
, one may consider
the approach of Stevens
[
Ste87
]
. Precisely, by considering the action
of the split torus
A_{d}=A\cap K_{d}
on the Kloosterman set
X_{d}(\dot{w_{0}}a)
, the Kloosterman sum
{\mathrm{Kl}}_{d}(a)
can be written in
terms of Kloosterman sums on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
(Proposition
4.6
).
Applying the Weil bound for the Kloosterman sums on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
(Proposition

4.1
), the study is reduced to a counting
problem for the Kloosterman set (Corollary
4.7
). In fact, in
[
Ste87
]
, only the
case
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
is considered. But it is not hard to generalize to
arbitrary split groups.

Following the approach of Stevens, especially via the study of the above counting problem, we can prove the regularity for lower rank cases
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{4}
(See Theorem
4.9
and Theorem
4.16
).

We should mention that similar bounds of Kloosterman
sums are obtained by Blomer-Man for
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
and
d=0

[
BM24
]
by considering the stratification of
Kloosterman sets in Dąbrowski-Reeder
[
DR98
]
.

1.0.3.
The local integrability

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on several important previous works. Precisely,

•

As we have mentioned above, Baruch showed that the restriction of the Bessel distribution for a generic representation
\pi
to the open Bruhat cell is given by a
smooth function
j_{\pi}^{0}
(See Theorem
3.3
). In particular, if
j_{\pi}^{0}
is locally integrable, then
the regularity for the Bessel distribution of
\pi
holds.

•

By the works of Lapid-Mao and Chai, the function
j_{\pi}^{0}
equals to another function
j_{\pi}
, which is given by
certain regularized integral of Whittaker functions over
N
(See Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.6
).

•

Lapid-Mao proved that
j_{\pi}
is locally given by a Kloosterman integral (See Theorem
3.7
).

Therefore, the local integrability of Kloosterman integrals implies the regularity of Bessel distributions.

To give the definition of Kloosterman integrals and its germ expansion, we fix a system of representatives

\{w_{I}^{0}\in N_{G}(A)\}_{I\subset\Delta}
of
\{w_{I}\in W\}_{I\subset\Delta}
which is relevant (with respect to
\psi_{0}
)
in the sense that for each
I\subset\Delta

•

(w_{I}^{0})^{2}\in A_{I}=Z(M_{I})
.

•

\psi_{0}\left({\mathrm{Ad}}(w^{0}w_{I}^{0})u\right)=\psi_{0}(u)
for any
u\in N_{M_{I}}
with
w^{0}=w_{\Delta}^{0}
.

In fact, the Tits representatives of Weyl elements give such a system of representatives (See Lemma
2.3
).

Consider the following action of
N\times N
on
G

g\cdot(n_{1},n_{2})=\overline{n_{1}}^{-1}gn_{2},\quad\overline{n_{1}}=(w^{0})^{-1}n_{1}w^{0}\in\overline{N}.

Denote by
(N\times N)_{g}
the stabilizer of
g
. An element
g\in G
is called relevant if
\psi\left(n_{1}^{-1}n_{2}\right)=1

for any
(n_{1},n_{2})\in(N\times N)_{g}
. We have the following Bruhat decomposition for relevant elements (See Lemma
2.4
)

G_{\mathrm{rel}}=\bigsqcup_{I\subset\Delta}\overline{N}w_{I}^{0}A_{I}N.

For a Schwartz function
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(G)
and
g\in G_{\mathrm{rel}}
relevant, the Kloosterman integral is defined as

I(g,f)=\int_{(N\times N)_{g}\backslash N\times N}f(g\cdot(n_{1},n_{2}))\psi^{-1}(n_{1}^{-1}n_{2})dn_{1}dn_{2}.

Remark 1.3
.

For the
GL_{n}
case, Jacquet
[
Jac16
]
considers the following
action

g\cdot(n_{1},n_{2})=n_{1}^{t}gn_{2}

with
n_{1}^{t}
the transport of
n_{1}
.
For general split groups, Lapid-Mao
[
LM13
]
considers

g\cdot(n_{1},n_{2})=n_{1}^{-1}gn_{2}.

Our convention is close to the one in
[
LM13
]
. In fact,
our Kloosterman integral and the one in
[
LM13
]
are equal
up to the translate of
w^{0}
(See the proof of
Theorem
3.7
).

Based on the above counting formula by Dąbrowski-Reeder, the Kloosterman integral
I(\cdot,f)

is local integrable if there exist some
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
, such that

\Big|I(a,f)\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}(a)\Big|\leq C,\quad a\in A,\quad\lambda_{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\Phi^{\vee,+}.

See Proposition
3.9
for details.

1.0.4.
The Shalika germ expansion

We consider the germ expansion for Kloosterman integrals.
For this, we need to introduce more notations.

Denote by
\{\lambda_{i}^{\circ}\in{\mathbb{Q}}\Phi\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
the fundamental weights of
G
with
\Phi
the set of roots. Fix a basis
\{\lambda_{i}^{\circ}\}_{m+1\leq i\leq n}
of
X^{*}(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset X^{*}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Moreover, we choose
d_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},1\leq i\leq n
such that
\lambda_{i}:=d_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\circ},1\leq i\leq n
are dominant weights in
X^{*}(A)
. By the highest weight theory, for each
1\leq i\leq n
, there exists a unique irreducible (algebraic) representation

\pi_{i}
of
G
such that its highest weight is
\lambda_{i}
.

Consider the matrix coefficient

\Delta_{i}(g)=\left<\pi_{i}(g)\varepsilon_{i},\eta_{i}^{*}\right>,\quad g\in G.

Here,
\varepsilon_{i}
is a nonzero highest weight vector in
\pi_{i}
and
\eta_{i}^{*}
is a nonzero lowest weight vector in
\pi_{i}^{\vee}
.
If
g=u_{1}\dot{w}au_{2}
with
u_{1}\in\overline{N}
,
w\in W
,
a\in A
and
u_{2}\in N
, then
\Delta_{i}(g)=\lambda_{i}(a)\Delta_{i}(\dot{w})
(See Proposition

2.1
(1)).

Example 1.4
.

In
[
Jac16
]
, for the case
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
, Jacquet considers
the functions
\Delta_{i}(g)
,
1\leq i\leq n
, be the determinant of the
sub-matrix
g_{i,i}
of
g\in G
formed with the first
i
rows and the first
i
columns of
g
. The functions
\Delta_{i}
considered by Jacquet are the matrix coefficients corresponding to the dominant weight
e_{1}+\cdots+e_{i},1\leq i\leq n
. Note that the fundamental weights
\lambda_{1}^{\circ},\cdots\lambda_{n-1}^{\circ}
are

\lambda_{i}^{\circ}=e_{1}+\cdots+e_{i}-\frac{i}{n}(e_{1}+\cdots+e_{n}),\quad e_{i}({\mathrm{diag}}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}))=a_{i}.

Let
I\subset J
be two subsets of
\Delta
. Consider

A_{I}^{J}=\{a\in A_{I}:\Delta_{i}(w_{I}^{0}a)=\Delta_{i}(w_{J}^{0})\text{ for all }i\text{ with }\Delta_{i}(w_{J}^{0})\neq 0\}.

For each
I
,
A_{I}^{I}
is finite (See Lemma
2.5
).

A system of Shalika germs for Kloosterman integrals (See Definition
2.8
) is a family of functions

\{K_{I}^{J}\in C^{\infty}(A_{I}^{J})\}_{I\subset J\subset\Delta}

such that
K_{I}^{I}=\delta_{e}
for any
I
and for each
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, there
exist
\{\omega_{J}\in{\mathcal{S}}(A_{J})\}_{J\subset\Delta}
such that for any
I\subset\Delta

I(w_{I}^{0}a,f)=\sum_{I\subset J}\left(K_{I}^{J}*\omega_{J}\right)(a),\quad a\in A_{I}

with

\left(K_{I}^{J}*\omega_{J}\right)(a)=\sum_{a=bc,b\in A_{I}^{J},c\in A_{J}}K_{I}^{J}(b)\omega_{J}(c).

We now give the result for the Shalika germ expansion
for Kloosterman integrals. This result may stand on its own.

Theorem 1.5
.

(See Theorem
2.10
and Theorem
2.20
)

There exists a system of Shalika germs for Kloosterman integrals on
G
which is unique in a suitable sense.
Moreover, one can construct a system of Shalika germs such that for each
I\subset\Delta
,
the germ
K_{\emptyset}^{I}
equals to the Kloosterman sum
\mathrm{Kl}_{d}
on
M_{I}
for any
d\gg 0
.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
is an analogue of the one by Jacquet-Ye
[
JY99
]
and Jacquet
[
Jac16
]
for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
.

As an application of Theorem
1.5
, the local integrability of Kloosterman integrals follows from nontrivial bounds of Kloosterman sums. Now, Theorem
1.2
follows.

1.0.5.
Future directions

There are several directions one may pursue further after this work. The first one is the regularity of Bessel distributions on general split reductive group
G
. In Theorem
3.19
, we have reduced the regularity of Bessel distributions attached to irreducible generic representations to nontrivial bounds of certain Kloosterman sums. We discussed two examples
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{4}
based on this reduction, and the general case will be considered in our future work.

One may use degenerate Whittaker functional to replace non-degenerate Whittaker functional and consider the corresponding Bessel distributions. In this case, the result of Baruch (Theorem
3.3
) is also true, that is, when restricted to the open Bruhat cell, the Bessel distribution is given by a smooth function. But this smooth function may fail to be locally integrable, and the regularity does not hold (See
[
Ba01
, Remark 2.4]
). The failure of this local integrability may be revealed by looking at the corresponding Shalika germ expansion.

2.1.
The germ expansion

2.1.1.

We let
F
a
p
-adic field, with ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}
.
|\cdot|
is the
p
-adic absolute value on
F
with
v
the additive valuation. Let
q
be the cardinality of its residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Use
\varpi
to denote a fixed uniformizer of

, and let
{\mathfrak{p}}
be the maximal ideal of

.

Let
G

p
-adic field

. We shall make no distinction between an algebraic group and its

-points. We fix a Borel subgroup
B=AN
, where
A
is a maximal split torus of
G

N

. The center of

is denoted by
Z
. Denote by

Denote by

N_{G}(A)
the normalizer of

. Denote by

G=\bigsqcup_{w\in W}\overline{N}wAN,\quad\overline{N}:=w_{0}^{-1}Nw_{0}.

2.1.2.

Denote by
X^{*}(A):={\mathrm{Hom}}(A,F^{\times})
the group of
F

A

W

X^{*}(A)
by
w\cdot x=x\circ{\mathrm{Ad}}(w)
, for
any
w\in W
and
x\in X^{*}(A)

w(x)=w\cdot x
.

Let
\Phi\subset X^{*}(A)
be the set of roots of
G
with respect to
A
.
Then
\Phi=\Phi^{+}\sqcup\Phi^{-}
, where
\Phi^{+}
is the set of positive roots and
\Phi^{-}
is the set of negative roots
with respect to
B
. For each root

the

\alpha
.

Denote by
\Delta=\{\alpha_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}\subset\Phi^{+}

, the set of simple roots of
G
. For each simple root
\alpha\in\Delta
, let
A_{\alpha}={\mathrm{Ker}}(\alpha)^{0}\subset A

. Then

is a split reductive group with

has rank

so that the Weyl group

of

. Denote by

the generator of

Denote by
X_{*}(A):={\mathrm{Hom}}(F^{\times},A)

Let
\Phi^{\vee}\subset X_{*}(A)
be the set of coroots of

\Delta^{\vee}

\Phi^{\vee}=\Phi^{\vee,+}\sqcup\Phi^{\vee,-}

\Phi^{\vee,+}

\Phi^{\vee,-}

There exists a perfect pairing

\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>\colon X^{*}(A)\times X_{*}(A)\to{\mathbb{Z}}

given as follows: for any
\alpha\in X^{*}(A)
and
\lambda\in X_{*}(A)
,

\alpha\left(\lambda(t)\right)=t^{\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle}
for
any
t\in A
. We have the following decomposition

X^{*}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}=X^{*}(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigoplus{\mathbb{Q}}\Phi,\quad X_{*}(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}=X_{*}(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigoplus{\mathbb{Q}}\Phi^{\vee},

such that under the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

X_{*}(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}}=({\mathbb{Q}}\Phi)^{\bot}
and
X^{*}(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}}=({\mathbb{Q}}\Phi^{\vee})^{\bot}

For each

\alpha\in\Delta
, the simple coroot
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Delta^{\vee}
is the unique cocharacter of
A

s_{\alpha}(x)=x-\left<x,\alpha^{\vee}\right>\alpha,\quad x\in X^{*}(A).

2.1.3.

The standard parabolic subgroups can be described in terms of subsets of
\Delta
.
For each subset
I
of
\Delta

P_{I}
of
G
such that

P_{I}=\bigsqcup_{w\in W_{I}}BwB

where
W_{I}
is the subgroup of
W
generated by
s_{\alpha},\alpha\in I
.
We have the Levi decomposition

P_{I}=M_{I}N_{I},\quad N_{I}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\backslash\Phi_{I}}N_{\alpha},\quad\Phi_{I}={\mathbb{Z}}I\cap\Phi.

The group
W_{I}
is the Weyl group for the Levi subgroup

. Moreover, every standard parabolic subgroup

of
G
is of the form

for a unique subset

of

3.
Bessel distributions

In this section, based on the germ expansion of Kloosterman

the nontrivial bound of Kloosterman sums (See Theorem
3.19

Let
G

p
-adic field

B=AN

\Phi\subset X^{*}(A)

\Delta
to denote the set of simple roots. For each

the corresponding root subgroup. Denote by

G

\psi
be a generic character on

Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth admissible representation on
G

\pi^{\vee}
be the contragredient representation of
\pi

Denote by
\pi^{*}
and

\pi
and
\pi^{\vee}
respectively. The representation
\pi

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{N}(\pi,\psi)\neq 0

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{N}(\pi,\psi)=1
