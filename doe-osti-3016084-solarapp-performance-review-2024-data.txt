Executive Summary
The Solar Automated Permit Processing Plus (SolarAPP+) platform is an online portal designed
to facilitate and expedite residential rooftop solar photovoltaic (PV) and battery storage
permitting processes. SolarAPP+ allows PV contractors to upload system specifications, have
that information automatically reviewed for code compliance, and receive instant approval for
code-compliant systems, reducing the authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) staff time needed for
review. SolarAPP+ also provides inspection checklists to verify installation practices and
adherence to approved designs. SolarAPP+ is available to AHJs at no cost.

Consistent with previous performance reviews, in this report, we summarize SolarAPP+
adoption trends to date and compare various metrics for PV systems permitted through
SolarAPP+ to metrics for systems permitted through traditional AHJ permitting processes (i.e.,
nonautomated processes).

As of the end of 2024, 799 AHJs had expressed interest in the SolarAPP+ platform, with 264
fully adopting (215) or piloting (49) the platform across 13 states. In 2024, 861 installers
submitted 37,393 permits through the SolarAPP+ platform, including 27,375 permits for PV-
plus-storage systems. SolarAPP+ permits accounted for around 43% of all permits issued in all
participating AHJs, and more than 60% of all permits in four participating AHJs.

 Figure ES-1. Summary of performance review results
Consistent with previous SolarAPP+ performance reviews, we find that permitting timelines are
substantially shorter for SolarAPP+ projects than for traditionally permitted projects. We
estimate that in 2024, nearly all SolarAPP+ permits were issued the same day as the application,
compared to a median duration of 7 days for traditional permits. The median duration from
SolarAPP+ application submission to passed inspection was 12 business days shorter than the
median duration for traditionally permitted projects, saving roughly 31,300 hours (about 3,900
workdays) of AHJ staff time through automated permit reviews in 2024 (Figure ES-1).

1 Introduction
The Solar Automated Permit Processing Plus (SolarAPP+) platform is an online portal designed
to facilitate and expedite permitting of residential rooftop solar photovoltaic (PV) and PV-plus-
battery-storage systems. SolarAPP+ was developed by the National Laboratory of the Rockies
(NLR) in collaboration with local governments, code development organizations, and industry
stakeholders. In 2024, the SolarAPP+ platform was transitioned from NLR management to the
SolarAPP Foundation, which will be the long-term host of the software.

This report is part of an ongoing series of reviews of SolarAPP+’s performance. Consistent with
previous performance reviews, we summarize SolarAPP+ adoption trends to date and compare
various metrics for PV systems permitted through SolarAPP+ to metrics for systems permitted
through traditional authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) permitting processes. 1 In this
introduction, we briefly explain the impetus for SolarAPP+ development, the functions of the
platform, and the results of previous performance reviews.

1.1 The Need for Standardized PV Permitting
Most rooftop PV systems are subject to local permitting requirements implemented by local
AHJs. Figure 1 depicts a typical permitting process and how it relates to the interconnection
processes implemented by utilities, which is usually, but not always, done in parallel and
culminates in the system receiving permission to operate (PTO), i.e., connect the solar panel
system to the electrical grid and start generating electricity. Rapid and accelerating rooftop PV
deployment has strained the capacity of AHJs to efficiently navigate these processes (Cook et al.
2021). Additionally, although states typically set minimum requirements, individual AHJs often
implement unique permitting requirements (Stanfield et al. 2012). Local permitting variability
has presented a challenge to the expanding rooftop PV market by increasing compliance costs
(Dong and Wiser 2013; Burkhardt et al. 2015; Cook et al. 2021) and permitting timelines
(O’Shaughnessy et al. 2022). A growing number of AHJs and utilities have responded by
reforming and standardizing permitting processes to reduce delays (Stanfield et al. 2012; Fekete
et al. 2022). However, PV permitting reforms have, to date, occurred in a piecemeal fashion, and
many AHJs lack the expertise, staff capacity, and budget to implement reforms (Parsons and
Josefowitz 2020).

1.2 The SolarAPP+ Platform
NLR developed SolarAPP+ in response to the evolving challenges in rooftop PV and PV-plus-
storage permitting. NLR developed the platform in collaboration with industry and the building
safety community, with funding from the U.S. Department of Energy. SolarAPP+ streamlines
AHJ permitting for residential rooftop PV and PV-plus-storage systems that meet certain
eligibility requirements, 2 automating the review of eligible systems through the steps illustrated
in Figure 2. SolarAPP+ allows PV contractors to upload system specifications, have that
information automatically reviewed for code compliance, and obtain instant approval for code-
compliant systems. Based on the application inputs, SolarAPP+ also generates checklists and
electrical schematics for inspectors to confirm that installed systems match preapproved designs.
The SolarAPP+ project formally began in September 2019 with development and testing of the
software for alignment with national model codes. NLR piloted the software with five
communities in early 2021 (Williams et al. 2022). SolarAPP+ was then officially launched in
July 2021. SolarAPP+ is available to local permitting authorities at no cost.

1.3 Past SolarAPP+ Performance Reviews
NLR has published four reviews of the SolarAPP+ platform’s performance to date (Williams et
al. 2022; Cook et al. 2022; Cook et al. 2023; Cook et al. 2024). The four reviews reach similar
conclusions:

 • SolarAPP+ saves AHJ staff time that would otherwise be spent on solar project permit
 and revision reviews.

 • SolarAPP+ instantly issues permits for code-compliant systems, effectively reducing
 permit review times to zero. By contrast, typical review times through traditional
 permitting processes (i.e., nonautomated processes) are a week or more.

 • SolarAPP+ projects had similar inspection durations and passed inspections at similar
 rates as other solar projects permitted through traditional processes.

 • SolarAPP+ projects completed the full permitting timeline (permit submission to passed
 inspection) faster than projects using traditional permitting processes.

This report builds on and largely corroborates the results from these previous performance
reviews with updated results for projects permitted in 2024.

2 Data and Methodology
We review SolarAPP+ performance by comparing metrics for projects processed through
SolarAPP+ to metrics for projects processed through traditional AHJ permitting processes. In
2024, the total universe of SolarAPP+ users and projects included 861 contractors that submitted
37,393 projects and completed 6,783 revisions on these projects (including revisions during
inspection) within the SolarAPP+ platform across 247 AHJs in 13 states (Figure 3). Of those
projects, 27,375 were PV-plus-storage projects submitted in 219 AHJs. AHJs with the greatest
SolarAPP+ permit volumes were concentrated in Arizona and California (Figure 4).

 Figure 3. Key statistics on SolarAPP+ permits and revisions in 2024

The AHJ data for the performance review was collected from two sources. First, the SolarAPP
Foundation provided data directly from the SolarAPP+ software, including data on projects,
installers, interested AHJs, and detailed project-level characteristics such as system size and
information on home electrical upgrades (summary statistics for these project-level
characteristics are provided in the appendix). Second, we collected data from 41 AHJs that had
adopted SolarAPP+, issued at least 30 SolarAPP+ permits in 2024, and provided data on solar
project permits processed through their traditional AHJ permitting processes. These 41 AHJs –
which are responsible for more than 40% of all SolarAPP+ projects installed in 2024 – provided
data for both SolarAPP+ and traditional system adoption timelines, including, when available,
permit submission dates, permit issuance dates, inspection dates, dates of final inspection
passing, inspection failure causes, and whether systems included battery storage. We used the
AHJ-provided dates to calculate durations from permit submission to approval and from permit
approval to passed inspection. All durations reported in days are in terms of business days. The
data completeness of the performance metrics varied by AHJ. The distinct samples used for each
analysis are identified throughout Section 3 and in the figure captions.

The term “SolarAPP+ project” refers to any PV or PV-plus-storage system that was entered into
SolarAPP+ by a contractor. Once evaluated to confirm code compliance, all SolarAPP+ projects

It is worth noting that this performance review is based on a comparison of outcomes for
SolarAPP+ and traditional permits and does not control for potentially confounding factors.
Potential differences between the SolarAPP+ and traditional project groups could cause
misleading deviations between SolarAPP+ and traditional process durations. For instance,
installers that use SolarAPP+ might navigate permitting processes more or less efficiently than
installers that use SolarAPP+ less often. Further, the estimated differences between the AHJs in
this study are not necessarily representative of the impacts of SolarAPP+ in other AHJs. It is
possible that the AHJs in this study had more or less efficient traditional permitting processes
than an average AHJ prior to implementing SolarAPP+. For these reasons, the reported impacts
should be considered approximate impacts of SolarAPP+ on AHJ permitting process durations.
Further research would be required to estimate the precise causal impacts of SolarAPP+ on
permitting process durations.

3 Performance Review
In this section, we present metrics on the performance of SolarAPP+ permitting relative to
traditional permitting. We have strong theoretical reasons to assume that differences in certain
metrics reflect the impacts of SolarAPP+ automated permitting. For instance, permit review
times (the number of days between permit submission and issuance) are much shorter for
SolarAPP+ permits than for traditional permits, largely reflecting the impact of automated
permitting. However, in other cases, differences between metrics could reflect other confounding
factors. For instance, total permitting times (the number of days between permit submission and
passed inspection) are subject to many factors unrelated to SolarAPP+, such as differences in
installation timelines. Differences in these metrics should be interpreted as rough estimates of the
impacts of SolarAPP+. Future research may implement more rigorous methodologies to more
accurately identify the causal impacts of SolarAPP+ on these metrics.

The metrics in this section are based on different subsamples of the data sample of 41 AHJs
described in Section 2. We define each subsample at the beginning of each section.

3.1 AHJ Permit Review Impacts
 Section 3.1 sample = 33 AHJs with available data on permit submission and issuance dates
 for at least 30 SolarAPP+ permits and at least 30 traditional permits.

We compare permit review durations for SolarAPP+ and traditional projects to evaluate the
platform’s performance during the review stage. Permit review durations refer to the time (in
business days 4) between permit submission and permit issuance. In every AHJ, median permit
review times for PV-only SolarAPP+ projects were lower than or equal to median review times
for traditional permits (Figure 8). The median permit review times for traditional PV-only and
PV-plus-storage projects were 7 days. 5 In contrast, SolarAPP+ facilitates instant permits for
code-compliant applications, meaning that the median permit review duration for SolarAPP+
projects was zero days for both project types. Median durations represent the permitting
experiences of most PV customers. However, some customers experience much longer durations.
Around 12% of traditional permits took more than 30 business days, and around 4% took longer
than 60 days. In contrast, fewer than 0.1% of SolarAPP+ permits took longer than 30 days to be
issued.

3.2 AHJ Inspection Impacts
 Section sample = 27 AHJs with available data on inspection outcomes for SolarAPP+
 projects and 23 AHJs with available data on inspection outcomes for traditional projects. Note
 that all AHJs with data on traditional projects also provided data on SolarAPP+ projects. This
 data represents 42,584 inspections.

We define the inspection failure rate as the percentage of projects that failed at least one
inspection. Inspection failure rates were provided by 23 AHJs for traditional projects and 27
AHJs for SolarAPP+ projects. For PV-only projects across all AHJs, inspection failure rates
were slightly lower for SolarAPP+ projects than for traditional projects in 2024: About 31% of
all SolarAPP+ projects failed an inspection at least once, compared to 35% of traditional
projects. In the case of PV-plus-storage permits, inspection failure rates were comparable, with
about 28% of all SolarAPP+ projects failing at least one inspection compared to 29% of
traditional projects.

 10

These results suggest that automated permitting through SolarAPP+ does not drive any issues
with inspections for PV-only or PV-plus-storage projects. Further research with additional data
from AHJs is required to understand the full impact of SolarAPP+ on inspection failure rates.

We tracked the reasons for inspection failure among 1,083 SolarAPP+ projects with available
data (Figure 10). About 79% of the projects cited code compliance issues as the primary failure
reason. About 10% of the reported SolarAPP+ inspection failures unrelated to code compliance
were due to rescheduling, primarily because the homeowner or contractor was not available at
the scheduled inspection time. Of the remaining SolarAPP+ inspection failures, a combined 6%
were due to missing on-site inspection checklists, and 5% were related to a discrepancy with the
permitted plans (installation did not match the SolarAPP+ plan or the SolarAPP+ inspection
checklist, which is the SolarAPP+ document analogous to a plan set for inspector review, was
not on site).

4 SolarAPP+ Estimated Impacts on Installation Costs
The impact of SolarAPP+ on PV permitting processes may translate to broader impacts on the
rooftop PV market. In this section, we explore the potential broader impacts of SolarAPP+
adoption on residential rooftop PV costs and prices. We estimate ranges of potential impacts
based on data for the 37,393 SolarAPP+ permits issued in 2024. As in Section 3, the estimated
impacts in this section should be interpreted as estimates under reasonable assumptions. Again,
some estimated impacts may be sensitive to confounding factors that correlate with SolarAPP+
use by installers. One possibility is that installers that use SolarAPP+ were already more efficient
(i.e., quicker through permitting and inspection) than installers that use traditional permits, in
which case some of the estimated impacts of SolarAPP+ would be attributable to these
preexisting differences. We have no specific reason to suspect that such confounding factors
correlate with SolarAPP+ use, but the analysis that follows should be interpreted in light of that
potential limitation.
We estimate the total impact on costs based on potential savings in permitting fees, permitting
costs, cancellation costs, and inspection costs.

4.1 Permitting Fees
SolarAPP+ could reduce staff time reviewing PV permits and allow AHJs to reduce PV
permitting fees (Plaisted 2022; Cook et al. 2023). Permit fees are the fees collected by the AHJ
for issuing the permit to the contractor, and these fees often include a specific charge for permit
reviews. Fee savings in AHJs that have already adjusted their fees range from $6–$251 per
permit (Cook et al. 2023). Plaisted (2022) estimated fee savings from SolarAPP+ of $215 per
permit for PV-only systems and $390 per permit for PV-plus-storage systems. Here, we assume
that SolarAPP+ adoption reduces net permitting fees in the range of $50–$250.

4.2 Permitting Costs
SolarAPP+ adoption may reduce permitting costs by facilitating the permitting process.
Permitting costs can be direct, such as installer time spent preparing permitting applications for
specific projects, or indirect, such as installer time spent learning how to navigate different
permitting requirements across different AHJs. Available estimates suggest that direct permitting
costs for PV-only systems are about $0.07/W, whereas full (direct and indirect) permitting costs
are about $0.5/W. 8 Our best proxy for the impacts of SolarAPP+ on these costs is the 24%
reduction in permitting timelines estimated in Section 3.4. Assuming that SolarAPP+ has a
similar impact on costs, that equates to a permitting cost reduction of around $0.02/W–$0.12/W,
which we use to define our lower- and upper-bound assumptions for SolarAPP+ permitting cost
reductions. Note that these estimates are based on assumptions around permitting costs for PV-
only systems. While our data suggest that permitting timelines differ between PV-only and

4.3 Cancellation Costs
Estimates across the literature suggest that around 11%–33% of residential rooftop PV customers
cancel their contracts (Liao 2020; Cook et al. 2021; Cruce et al. 2022). 9 Installers incur various
costs prior to these cancellations, such as customer acquisition costs and (depending on the stage
of the cancellation) permitting and project planning costs. To remain profitable, installers need to
recoup costs on cancelled projects by charging higher prices on successful projects. SolarAPP+
could reduce PV costs by reducing permitting delays, reducing cancellation rates, and therefore
preventing installers from incurring costs on projects that do not result in installed systems.
Cruce et al. (2022) estimate that customer cancellations result in installer costs that increase
prices for installed systems by around $0.1/W for a typical system. Further, Cruce et al. (2022)
estimate that about 2% of cancellations occur during permit review. In our lower-bound case, we
use that estimate to assume that SolarAPP+ reduces customer cancellations by 2% by expediting
permit reviews. That share likely understates the role of permitting in driving customer
cancellations, given that installers cite permitting delays as the key driver of cancellations (Cook
et al. 2021) and permitting-related delays can begin before a permit is submitted (Plaisted 2022).
We therefore assume that SolarAPP+ reduces customer cancellations by 5% in our upper-bound
case. Based on that range and assumed cancellation cost impacts of $0.1/W, we estimate that
SolarAPP+ can reduce cancellation costs by $0.002/W–$0.005/W.

4.4 Total Installation Cost Impacts
To convert $/W savings estimates to total savings, we multiply the $/W estimates by 6 kW, the
median system size of SolarAPP+-permitted systems (see Appendix A.1). The total assumed per-
installation cost savings range from $180–$1,000. For perspective, those estimates reflect a 1%–
6% cost reduction relative to residential PV system cost benchmarks (Ramasamy et al. 2023).
We then multiply these system-level savings by the number of SolarAPP+ permits issued in

5 Conclusion
This report reaffirms previous years’ findings that SolarAPP+ streamlines residential solar
permitting by reducing approval timelines and saving staff time. These benefits positively affect
the 264 AHJs that issued nearly 38,000 permits across the platform in 2024. Specifically,
projects permitted through SolarAPP+ were typically issued the same day as submission and
reached passed inspection about 12 business days faster compared to those processed through
traditional permitting pathways. This streamlining of the permit process translates into time and
cost savings for jurisdictions and installers, helping to expedite the approval and construction of
reliable energy infrastructure. Building on this work, future research regarding automated solar
permitting could further explore the causal effects of SolarAPP+ by controlling for installer and
jurisdiction-level differences and exploring downstream outcomes like impacts on
interconnection timelines, customer cancellation rates, and customer costs. Finally, future work
could consider a longitudinal study of SolarAPP+’s performance metrics to understand the trends
and lifetime impact of this permit streamlining technology.
