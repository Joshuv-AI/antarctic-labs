Abstract
For a graph G G with vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) and a positive integer i i , an i i -packing in G G is a subset X X of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) such that the distance between any two distinct vertices of X X is greater than i i . The packing chromatic number of G G , denoted by χ ρ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\rho}(G) , is the smallest positive integer k k for which there exists a partition X 1 , X 2 , … , X k X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k} of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) such that X i X_{i} is an i i -packing in G G for every i ∈ [ k ] i\in[k] . A graph G G is called χ ρ \chi_{\rho} -critical if χ ρ ​ ( H ) < χ ρ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\rho}(H)<\chi_{\rho}(G) holds for every proper subgraph H H of G G . In this paper, we provide a structural characterization of χ ρ \chi_{\rho} -critical graphs with radius 1 1 , and completely determine the χ ρ \chi_{\rho} -critical cactus graphs with radius 2 2 and diameter 2 2 or 3 3 .

Introduction

The concept of
packing coloring
was originally introduced by Goddard et al.
[
17
]
under the name
broadcast coloring
. Brešar et al.
[
8
]
used the term packing coloring for the same concept.

For a graph
G
with vertex set
V(G)
and a positive integer
i
, an
i
-packing in
G
is a subset
X
of
V(G)
such that
d_{G}(u,v)>i
for every two distinct vertices
u,v\in X
.

The packing chromatic number
of
G
, denoted by
\chi_{\rho}(G)
, is the smallest positive integer
k
for which there exists a partition
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k}
of
V(G)
such that
X_{i}
is an
i
-packing in
G
for every
i\in[k]
.
Equivalently, a partition
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k}
of
V(G)
defines a coloring
c:V(G)\to[k]
by setting
c(u)=i
for every
u\in X_{i}
. This coloring is called a
k
-packing coloring of
G
.

A graph
G
is called
packing chromatic critical
or
\chi_{\rho}
-critical if
\chi_{\rho}(H)<\chi_{\rho}(G)
for every proper subgraph
H
of
G
and
\chi_{\rho}(G)=k
, then
G
is called a
k
-
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graph
[
4
]
.
If
\chi_{\rho}(G-v)<\chi_{\rho}(G)
for every
v\in V(G)
, then
G
is called
\chi_{\rho}
-vertex-critical
[
21
]
. Similarly, if
\chi_{\rho}(G-e)<\chi_{\rho}(G)
for every
e\in E(G)
, then
G
is called
\chi_{\rho}
-edge-critical; for graphs with no isolated vertices, this is equivalent to
\chi_{\rho}
-criticality
[
4
]
.

Goddard et al.
[
17
]
determined the packing chromatic number of paths and cycles, characterized graphs with
\chi_{\rho}(G)\in\{2,3\}
, and obtained several results for trees.
Brešar et al.
[
9
]
, established several bounds related to the independence number
\alpha(G)
and provided necessary conditions for graphs satisfying
\chi_{\rho}(G)=\chi(G)
.
Goddard et al.
[
17
]
showed that deciding whether a planar graph admits a
4
-packing coloring is NP-hard. The packing coloring problem is also NP-complete for trees
[
13
]
and for chordal graphs of diameter at least three
[
20
]
.

Numerous results have also been obtained for specific graph classes, such as, trees, subcubic graphs, Sierpiński-type graphs, Cartesian products of graphs, lexicographic products of graphs, corona of graphs, and infinite graphs
[
17
,
25
,
8
,
1
,
3
,
16
,
7
,
10
,
6
,
22
,
2
,
23
,
14
,
26
,
11
,
24
]
. For a comprehensive overview, we refer to the survey
[
5
]
. Packing coloring continues to be actively studied; see, for example,
[
15
,
18
]
.

We now turn to packing chromatic critical graphs. Klavžar and Rall
[
21
]
introduced
\chi_{\rho}
-vertex-critical graphs and established several structural results. In particular, they characterized
3
-
\chi_{\rho}
-vertex-critical graphs and provided a partial characterization for
4
-
\chi_{\rho}
-vertex-critical graphs.
Subsequently, Ferme characterized all
4
-
\chi_{\rho}
-vertex-critical graphs in
[
12
]
.
In
[
4
]
, Brešar and Ferme introduced
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs, and characterized the
2
-
\chi_{\rho}
-critical and
3
-
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs, proved that for trees
\chi_{\rho}
-criticality is equivalent to
\chi_{\rho}
-vertex-criticality, and gave characterizations of
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs with diameter
2
, as well as of
\chi_{\rho}
-critical block graphs with diameter
3
. They posed the open question of characterizing
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs of radius
2
. Motivated by the latter two results, we address this question for cactus graphs by characterizing all
\chi_{\rho}
-critical cactus graphs of radius
2
and diameter
2
or
3
. We also obtain a structural characterization of
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs of radius
1
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present basic definitions and notations used throughout the paper. In Section
3
, we present a structural characterization of
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs with radius
1
. In Section

\chi_{\rho}
-critical cactus graphs with radius

-critical cactus graphs with radius

and diameter

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, all graphs are finite and simple. Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
, and let
u,v\in V(G)
be arbitrary vertices.
We write
G-u
and
G-e
for the subgraphs obtained by removing a vertex
u
and an edge
e
from
G
, respectively.
The
distance
between
u
and
v
, denoted by
d_{G}(u,v)
, is the length of a shortest
u
–
v
path in
G
.
The
neighborhood
of
u
, denoted by
N_{G}(u)
, is the set of vertices adjacent to
u
. If
v\in N_{G}(u)
, then
u
and
v
are called
neighbors
in
G
.

For
S\subseteq V(G)
, the subgraph induced by
S
is the graph
G[S]
with vertex set
S
and edge set consisting of the edges of
G
with both endpoints in
S
. The
eccentricity
of
u
, denoted by
\varepsilon_{G}(u)
,
is the maximum distance between
u
and any other vertex of
G
, that is,

\varepsilon_{G}(u)=\max_{v\in V(G)}\{\mathrm{d}_{G}(u,v)\}.

The
radius
of
G
is the minimum eccentricity of its vertices, and the
diameter
of
G
is the maximum eccentricity of its vertices, that is,

\operatorname{rad}(G)=\min_{u\in V(G)}\varepsilon_{G}(u)
and
\operatorname{diam}(G)=\max_{u\in V(G)}\varepsilon_{G}(u).

The
center
of
G
is the set of all vertices
v
such that the eccentricity of
v
equals the radius of
G
.

A vertex
u
is
universal
if
d_{G}(u,v)=1
for all
v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}
.
A
leaf
is a vertex adjacent to exactly one vertex in
G
.
A vertex
u
is a
cut vertex
if
G-u
is disconnected, and an edge
e
is a
cut edge
if
G-e
is disconnected. The
order
of a graph
G
is its number of vertices. A path, a cycle, and a complete graph of order
n
are denoted by
P_{n}
,
C_{n}
, and
K_{n}
, respectively, and a star of order
n+1
is denoted by
K_{1,n}
.

A
block
is a connected graph with no cut vertex.
A graph
G
is called a
cactus graph
if it is connected and each block is either a cycle or a
K_{2}
.
If
G
is acyclic, then
G
is a tree; if
G
has a cycle
C
, then
C
is chordless, since each edge belongs to at most one cycle.

A set
S\subseteq V(G)
is called an
independent set
if no two vertices in
S
are adjacent. The
independence number
of
G
, denoted by
\alpha(G)
, is the maximum size of an independent set in
G
.
A graph
G
is called an
\alpha
-critical graph
if
\alpha(G-e)>\alpha(G)
for every edge
e\in E(G)
.
For further definitions and basic concepts in graph theory, we refer the reader to
[
27
]
.

We begin with a simple observation.

Observation 2.1

If
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are the connected components of
G
after removing a cut edge
e
, then
\operatorname{diam}(G)\geq\operatorname{diam}(H_{i})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.

3
Structural characterization of
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs with radius
1

In this section, we provide a complete structural characterization of
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs with
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
. If
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
, then
G
contains a universal vertex. It is well known that for any connected graph
G
,

\operatorname{rad}(G)\leq\operatorname{diam}(G)\leq 2\,\operatorname{rad}(G)
. Therefore,
\operatorname{diam}(G)\in\{1,2\}
.

First we consider the case
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
and
\mathrm{diam}(G)=1
.

Observation 3.1

Let
G
be a graph with
\operatorname{rad}(G)=1
and
\operatorname{diam}(G)=1
.
Then
G\cong K_{n}
with
n\geq 2
and
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical.

Proof.
Let
G
be a graph with
\operatorname{rad}(G)=1
and
\operatorname{diam}(G)=1
. Since
\operatorname{diam}(G)=1
,
G
is a complete graph, that is,
G\cong K_{n}
with
n\geq 2
.
It is well known that complete graphs are
\chi_{\rho}
-critical.

\square

We therefore consider the case
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
and
\mathrm{diam}(G)=2
. Brešar and Ferme
[
4
]
characterized
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs of diameter
2
. For graphs with radius
1
and diameter
2
, we reformulate this criterion in structural terms. In particular, we show that if
u
is a universal vertex of
G
, then the
\chi_{\rho}
-criticality of
G
is determined by the structure of
G-u
.

We begin by recalling the following result of Goddard et al.
[
17
]
, which we use frequently.

Lemma 3.2

[
17
]

For every graph
G
,
\chi_{\rho}(G)\leq|V(G)|-\alpha(G)+1,
with equality if
G
has diameter two.

Using Lemma
3.2
, we establish the following result.

Proposition 3.3

If
G
is a
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graph with at least three vertices and
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
, then
G
has no leaf.

Proof.
Let
G
be a
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graph with at least three vertices and
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
. If
\mathrm{diam}(G)=1
, then it is clear by Observation
3.1
.

Let
\mathrm{diam}(G)=2
. Suppose to the contrary that
G
has a leaf
v
. Then
G
has a unique universal vertex
u
and
v
is adjacent only to
u
. Since the diameter of
G
is
2
, it is not a complete graph and hence,
\alpha(G)\geq 2
. So, there is no maximum independent set in
G
that contains the universal vertex
u
, that is, every maximum independent set in
G
contains
v
. Since
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical, we have
\chi_{\rho}(G-e)<\chi_{\rho}(G)
, where
e=uv
is the edge between
u
and
v
. After removing
e
,
G-e
becomes disconnected with connected components
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, where
V\left(H_{2}\right)=\{v\}
. Since
v
belongs to every maximum independent set of
G
,
\alpha\left(H_{1}\right)=\alpha(G)-1
.
Additionally,

\chi_{\rho}(G-e)=\max\left(\chi_{\rho}\left(H_{1}\right),\chi_{\rho}\left(H_{2}\right)\right)=\chi_{\rho}\left(H_{1}\right).

By Observation
2.1
the diameter of
H_{1}
satisfies
\operatorname{diam}\left(H_{1}\right)\leq 2
since
e
is a cut edge.

If
\operatorname{diam}\left(H_{1}\right)=1
, then
H_{1}
is a complete graph. In this case,

\chi_{\rho}(G-e)=\chi_{\rho}\left(H_{1}\right)=|V(H_{1})|=|V(G)|-1.

However, since
\alpha(G)=2
, Lemma
3.2
yields

\chi_{\rho}(G)=|V(G)|-\alpha(G)+1=|V(G)|-1,
since
G
has diameter
2
.
This leads to
\chi_{\rho}(G-e)=\chi_{\rho}(G)
, contradicting with
G
being
\chi_{\rho}
-critical.

If
\operatorname{diam}\left(H_{1}\right)=2
, then by Lemma
3.2

\chi_{\rho}(G-e)=\chi_{\rho}\left(H_{1}\right)=\left|V\left(H_{1}\right)\right|-\alpha\left(H_{1}\right)+1.

By replacing
\left|V\left(H_{1}\right)\right|=|V(G)|-1
and

\alpha\left(H_{1}\right)=\alpha(G)-1
, we have

\chi_{\rho}(G-e)=|V(G)|-\alpha(G)+1=\chi_{\rho}(G)
, which contradicts the assumption that
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical.

Since both cases result in contradictions, we conclude that
G
does not have a leaf.
\square

The next result describes the structure of
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graphs with radius 1.

Proposition 3.4

Let
G
be a graph with at least three vertices and
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
. If
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical, then
G-u
is a disjoint union of
\alpha
-critical graphs for every universal vertex
u\in V(G)
.

Proof.
Let
G
be a
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graph with at least three vertices and
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
. If
\mathrm{diam}(G)=1
, then it is clear by Observation
3.1
.

Let
\mathrm{diam}(G)=2
and
u
be a universal vertex of
G
.
Since
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical, it follows from Proposition
3.3
that
G
has no leaf. Then every connected component of
G-u
contains at least two vertices, and therefore each component contains at least one edge.
Let
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{r}
be the connected components of
G-u
, where
r\geq 1
.
Suppose to the contrary that there exists a connected component of
G-u
, say
A_{1}
, that is not
\alpha
-critical. This implies that there exists an edge
e\in E\left(A_{1}\right)
such that
\alpha\left(A_{1}-e\right)=\alpha\left(A_{1}\right)
. In this case,
\alpha(G-e)=\alpha(A_{1}-e)+\sum_{i=2}^{r}\alpha(A_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha(A_{i})
and
\alpha(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha(A_{i})
.
Since
e\in E(A_{1})
, the diameter of
G-e
remains
2
. Applying Lemma
3.2
, we obtain the following contradiction.

\displaystyle\chi_{\rho}(G-e)

\displaystyle=|V(G-e)|-\alpha(G-e)+1

\displaystyle=|V(G)|-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha(A_{i})+1

\displaystyle=|V(G)|-\alpha(G)+1

\displaystyle=\chi_{\rho}(G).

Thus, every connected component of
G-u
is
\alpha
-critical.

\square

In Lemma

Our proof will use a corollary of the next result due to Haynes et al.

Lemma 3.5

[
19
]

A graph
G
has
\alpha(G)\neq\alpha(G-e)
for all
e\in E
if and only if for each edge
e=uv
there exists a maximum independent set
I
where
u\in I,v\notin I
and
u
is the only neighbor of

in
I
.

Lemma

implies the following result.

Corollary 3.6

Let
G
be an
\alpha
-critical graph with no isolated vertices. Then, there exists a maximum independent set
I
of
G
such that
v\notin I
for every vertex
v\in V(G)
.

Lemma 3.7

Let
G
be a graph with
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
and
\mathrm{diam}(G)=2
, and let
u
be a universal cut vertex of
G
.
Then
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical if and only if
G-u
is a disjoint union of
\alpha
-critical graphs.

Proof.
Let
G
be a graph with
\mathrm{rad}(G)=1
and
\mathrm{diam}(G)=2
, and let
u
be a universal cut vertex of
G
. Let further
G
be a
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graph. By Proposition
3.4
,
G-u
is a disjoint union of
\alpha
-critical graphs.

Conversely, suppose that
G-u
is a disjoint union of
\alpha
-critical graphs
A_{1},\dots,A_{r}
, where
r\geq 2
. There are two types of edges in
G
: edges with both endpoints in the same connected component of
G-u
, and edges connecting a vertex of some connected component of
G-u
to
u
.

Case 1:
Let
e\in E(A_{i})
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
i=1
, that is,
e\in E(A_{1})
.
Since
u
is a universal vertex in
G-e
, the diameter of
G-e
remains 2. Additionally, since
A_{1}
is
\alpha
-critical, removing
e
increases
\alpha\left(A_{1}\right)
. By Lemma
3.2
, we have:

\displaystyle\chi_{\rho}(G-e)

\displaystyle=|V(G-e)|-\alpha(G-e)+1

\displaystyle=|V(G)|-\alpha(A_{1}-e)-\sum_{j=2}^{r}\alpha(A_{j})+1

\displaystyle<|V(G)|-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha(A_{j})+1

\displaystyle=|V(G)|-\alpha(G)+1

\displaystyle=\chi_{\rho}(G).

Therefore,
\chi_{\rho}(G-e)<\chi_{\rho}(G)
for every edge
e
in
G-u
.

Case 2:
Let
e=ux
, where
x\in A_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
. In this case, the diameter of
G-e
becomes 3, since for any vertex
y\in A_{j}
with
j\neq i
, the distance between
x
and
y
is 3 in
G-e
. Relabel the components so that
x\in A_{1}
and
y\in A_{2}
. We know that
\alpha(G-e)\geq\alpha(G)
.
We first consider the case where
\alpha(G-e)>\alpha(G)
. By Lemma
3.2
, we have:

\displaystyle\chi_{\rho}(G-e)

\displaystyle\leq|V(G)|-\alpha(G-e)+1

\displaystyle<|V(G)|-\alpha(G)+1

\displaystyle=\chi_{\rho}(G).

We then consider
\alpha(G-e)=\alpha(G)
. By Corollary
3.6
, there exist maximum independent sets
I_{1}
in
A_{1}
and
I_{2}
in
A_{2}
such that
x\notin I_{1}
and
y\notin I_{2}
.
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
together with maximum inpendent sets from

(

) form an
\alpha(G-e)
-set
I
that excludes both
x
and
y
.
Then in graph
G-e
, color
1
is assigned to every vertex in
I
, whereas color
2
is assigned to both
x
and
y
, and distinct colors are assigned to the remaining
|V(G)|-\alpha(G)-2
vertices. Since
\alpha(G-e)=\alpha(G)
, we obtain a packing coloring of
G-e
with
|V(G)|-\alpha(G)
colors. Thus, we have:

\displaystyle\chi_{\rho}(G-e)

\displaystyle\leq|V(G)|-\alpha(G)

\displaystyle<|V(G)|-\alpha(G)+1

\displaystyle=\chi_{\rho}(G).

Therefore,
\chi_{\rho}(G-e)<\chi_{\rho}(G)
for every edge
e=ux
with
x\in G-u
. Since this holds for every edge
e\in E(G)
,
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical.

\square

Lemma
3.7
provides the characterization for the case where
G-u
is disconnected. Accordingly, we now complete the characterization by considering the case where
G-u
is connected in Lemma

. Our proof will use the next result.

Lemma 3.8

If
G
is an
\alpha
-critical graph with
\operatorname{rad}(G)\geq 3
, then for every two vertices
x
and
y
that are at distance
3
from each other, there exists a maximum independent set that contains neither
x
nor
y
.

Proof.
Let
x\in V(G)
. Since
\operatorname{rad}(G)\geq 3
, we have
\varepsilon_{G}(x)\geq 3
. Then there exists a vertex
z\in V(G)
such that
d_{G}(x,z)\geq 3
. Let
x-a-b-y
be the first four vertices on a shortest
x
–
z
path. Then
d_{G}(x,y)=3
. Note that
y=z
if
\varepsilon_{G}(x)=3
.
Let
e=ab
. It is well known that for any graph
G
and edge
e\in E(G)
,

\alpha(G)\leq\alpha(G-e)\leq\alpha(G)+1.
Since
G
is
\alpha
-critical,
\alpha(G-e)=\alpha(G)+1
.
Let
I
be a maximum independent set of
G-e
. Then
|I|=\alpha(G)+1
.
We claim that
a,b\in I
. If
a\notin I
, then
I
is also independent in
G
, since it does not contain both endpoints of
e
, contradiction. Therefore,
a\in I
. By symmetry
b\in I
.
Since
x
is adjacent to
a
and
y
is adjacent to
b
, we have
x,y\notin I
. Now let
I^{\prime}=I\setminus\{a\}
.
Then
I^{\prime}
is an independent set of
G
with size
\alpha(G)
. Thus,
I^{\prime}
is a maximum independent set of
G
containing neither
x

or it is a vertex of a
C_{3}
block connected to
u_{1}
. In either case, we have
|X_{1}|\leq\alpha(H)-1
.
Consequently,
\chi_{\rho}(H)=|V(H)|-|X_{1}|\geq|V(H)|-\alpha(H)+1.
On the other hand, in all cases by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\chi_{\rho}(H)\leq|V(H)|-\alpha(H)+1.

Combining these bounds, we obtain

\chi_{\rho}(H)=|V(H)|-\alpha(H)+1.

\square

Proposition 4.20

Let
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(4)}(k_{1},m_{1};k_{2},m_{2})
. If
m_{1}+m_{2}\geq 1
and

, then

\chi_{\rho}
-critical.

Proof.
Suppose for the sake of a contradiction that

, where

, is a
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graph.
For the first case suppose that exactly one of

and

is zero. By symmetry, let

. In this case, by the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(4)}(k_{1},m_{1};k_{2},m_{2})
,
k_{2}\geq 1
and
m_{1}\geq 1
.
Consider the edge

.
By removing

4.19
, we have

.

.
Then

, which contradicts the assumption that

\chi_{\rho}
-critical.

For the remaining case suppose that

. Since

or

, say
e
.

. Then
\chi_{\rho}(G-e)=\chi_{\rho}(\mathcal{G}^{(4)}_{2}(k_{1}-1,m_{1};k_{2},m_{2})).
Clearly, by Proposition
4.17
,
\chi_{\rho}(G)=\chi_{\rho}(G-e)=t+3
, which contradicts the assumption that

is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical.

4.2.3
\chi_{\rho}
-critical cactus graphs with radius 2 and diameter
3
, where
C_{3}
is the main block

In this section, we will consider cactus graphs with radius 2 and diameter
3
with
C_{3}
as their main block. Note that such graphs form a subclass of block graphs. Hence, the characterization of block graphs with diameter
3
(and thus radius
2
) given in Lemma
4.22
applies directly to the graphs considered in this section.

Lemma 4.22

[
4
]

Let
G
be a block graph with
\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
, and let
B
be the block induced by the center of
G
(called the central block, and every other block of
G
is called a side block). The graph
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical if and only if one of the following three possibilities holds for the vertices of
B
.

(
a
)

All vertices in
V(B)
have degree
|V(B)|
.

(
b
)

All vertices in
V(B)
have degree
|V(B)|+1
, and exactly
|V(B)|-1
vertices of
B
have two leaf neighbors.

(
c
)

For each vertex
x\in V(B)
, at least one of the following three properties holds.

(
c_{1}
)

x
belongs to at least one side block of order at least
4
, and does not have any leaf neighbor;

(
c_{2}
)

x
belongs to at least two side blocks of order
3
, and does not have any leaf neighbor;

(
c_{3}
)

x
has degree
|V(B)|+1
and has two neighbors, which are both leaves; in addition, at least one vertex in
V(B)
satisfies one of the properties (
c_{1}
) or (
c_{2}
).

Lemma 4.23

Let
G
be a cactus graph with
\mathrm{rad}(G)=2
and
\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
whose main block is
C_{3}
.
Then
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical if and only if
G
is one of the following graphs:

(i)

\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(1,0;\,1,0;\,1,0)
,

(ii)

\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(2,0;\,2,0;\,0,1)
,

(iii)

\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2};\,0,m_{3})\mid m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\geq 2\}
,

(iv)

\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2};\,2,0)\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 2\}
,

(v)

\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,2,0;\,2,0)\mid m_{1}\geq 2\}
,

(vi)

\mathcal{H}(2,0;\,0,1)
,

(vii)

\{\mathcal{H}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2})\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 2\}
,

(viii)

\{\mathcal{H}(0,m_{1};\,2,0)\mid m_{1}\geq 2\}
.

Proof.
Since the main block of
G
is
C_{3}
, the central block
B
of
G
is either isomorphic to
C_{3}
or to
K_{2}
. Assume first that
B\cong C_{3}
. Then
|V(B)|=3
.
If condition (
a
) of Lemma
4.22
holds, then every vertex of
B
has degree
3
. Therefore,

G\cong\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(1,0;\,1,0;\,1,0)
and (
i
) holds.
If condition (
b
) holds, then every vertex of
B
has degree
4
, and exactly two vertices of
B
have two leaf neighbors. Hence,

G\cong\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(2,0;\,2,0;\,0,1)
and (
ii
) holds.
Now suppose that condition (
c
) holds. Since (
c_{1}
) is not valid, at least one vertex of
B
satisfies (
c_{2}
). If all three vertices satisfy (
c_{2}
), then each vertex belongs to at least two side blocks of order
3
, and so

G\in\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2};\,0,m_{3})\mid m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\geq 2\}
. Thus, (
iii
) holds.
If exactly two vertices satisfy (
c_{2}
), then the remaining vertex satisfies (
c_{3}
), and thus

G\in\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2};\,2,0)\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 2\}
and (
iv
) holds.
If exactly one vertex satisfies (
c_{2}
), then the other two satisfy (
c_{3}
), and hence,

G\in\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,2,0;\,2,0)\mid m_{1}\geq 2\}.
Thus, (
v
) holds.

Next assume that
B\cong K_{2}
. Then
|V(B)|=2
. If condition (
a
) of Lemma
4.22
holds, then every vertex of
B
has degree
2
. Therefore,

G\cong P_{4},
which is not valid for our case since its main block is not
C_{3}
.
If condition (
b
) holds, then each vertex of
B
has degree
3
, and exactly one vertex of
B
has two leaf neighbors. Therefore,

G\cong\mathcal{H}(2,0;\,0,1)
and (
vi
) holds.
Finally, suppose that condition (
c
) holds. Again, (
c_{1}
) is not valid, so at least one vertex of
B
satisfies (
c_{2}
). If both vertices satisfy (
c_{2}
), then each belongs to at least two side blocks of order
3
, and we obtain

G\in\{\mathcal{H}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2})\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 2\}.
Thus, (
vii
) holds.
If exactly one vertex satisfies (
c_{2}
), then the other satisfies (
c_{3}
), and thus

G\in\{\mathcal{H}(0,m_{1};\,2,0)\mid m_{1}\geq 2\},
(
viii
) holds.

\square

5
Summary of results

We now establish our main result concerning
\chi_{\rho}
-critical cactus graphs with radius
2
and diameter
3
.

Theorem 5.1

Let
G
be a cactus graph with
\mathrm{rad}(G)=2
and
\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
. Then
G
is
\chi_{\rho}
-critical if and only if it is isomorphic to one of the following graphs:

(i)

P_{4}
,

(ii)

\{\mathcal{G}_{1}^{(5)}(0,m_{1})\mid m_{1}\geq 2\},

(iii)

\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(4)}(1,0;\,1,0)
,

(iv)

\{\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(4)}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2})\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1\},

(v)

\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(1,0;\,1,0;\,1,0)
,

(vi)

\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(2,0;\,2,0;\,0,1)
,

(vii)

\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2};\,0,m_{3})\mid m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\geq 2\},

(viii)

\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2};\,2,0)\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 2\},

(ix)

\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,2,0;\,2,0)\mid m_{1}\geq 2\},

(x)

\mathcal{H}(2,0;\,0,1)
,

(xi)

\{\mathcal{H}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2})\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 2\},

(xii)

\{\mathcal{H}(0,m_{1};\,2,0)\mid m_{1}\geq 2\}.

Proof.
Let
G
be a cactus graph with
\mathrm{rad}(G)=2
and
\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
and assume that
G
is a
\chi_{\rho}
-critical graph. We first consider the case where
G
is tree. In this case, we have
G\cong P_{4}
by Lemma
4.6
. Next, suppose that
G
contains at least one cycle. Let
C^{*}
be the main block. Recall that
C^{*}
is either
C_{3}
,
C_{4}
, or
C_{5}
by Observation
4.2
.

We begin with a common argument for the cases where
C^{*}\cong C_{r}
with
r\in\{4,5\}
.
Since
\operatorname{diam}(C^{*})=2
and
\operatorname{diam}(G)=3
, we have
G\not\cong C^{*}
. Hence,
G
contains at least one outer block. Moreover, each outer block has diameter
1
, and so every outer block is isomorphic to either
K_{2}
or
C_{3}
. We also claim that all outer blocks must be attached to
C^{*}
either at a single vertex or at two consecutive vertices. Indeed, if two outer blocks are attached at vertices
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
with
d_{C^{*}}(x_{i},x_{j})=2
, then for vertices
u
and
v
in these blocks, we obtain
d_{G}(u,v)\geq 4,

contradicting
\operatorname{diam}(G)=3
.

Case 1:
Let
C^{*}\cong C_{5}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})
.

Then
G\in\mathcal{G}_{1}^{(5)}(k_{1},m_{1})
or
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(5)}(k_{1},m_{1};k_{2},m_{2})
in this case. By Lemmas
4.10
and
4.12
, we have
G\in\{\mathcal{G}_{1}^{(5)}(0,m_{1})\mid m_{1}\geq 2\}
.

Case 2:
Let
C^{*}\cong C_{4}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
.

Then
G\in\mathcal{G}_{1}^{(4)}(k_{1},m_{1})
or
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(4)}(k_{1},m_{1};k_{2},m_{2})
in this case. By Lemmas
4.15
and
4.21
, we have
G\cong\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(4)}(1,0;1,0)
or
G\in\{\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(4)}(0,m_{1};0,m_{2})\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1\}
.

Case 3:
Let
C^{*}\cong C_{3}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
.

In this case,
G
is a block graph. Then by Lemma
4.23
,
G
is one of the following graphs:
\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(1,0;\,1,0;\,1,0)
,
\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(2,0;\,2,0;\,0,1)
,

\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2};\,0,m_{3})\mid m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\geq 2\},

\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2};\,2,0)\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 2\},

\{\mathcal{G}_{3}^{(3)}(0,m_{1};\,2,0;\,2,0)\mid m_{1}\geq 2\},

\mathcal{H}(2,0;\,0,1)
,

\{\mathcal{H}(0,m_{1};\,0,m_{2})\mid m_{1},m_{2}\geq 2\},

\{\mathcal{H}(0,m_{1};\,2,0)\mid m_{1}\geq 2\}.

The converse is clear by Lemmas
4.6
,
4.10
,
4.16
,
4.21
, and
4.23
.

\square
