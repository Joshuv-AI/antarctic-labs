Complexified Virasoro Flow and the Logarithmic Graviton at the Chiral Point
Yannick Mvondo-She

Abstract
At the chiral point of topologically massive gravity, the massive graviton becomes degenerate with the left-moving graviton, leading to the appearance of a logarithmic mode and a corresponding rank-two Jordan structure. This logarithmic graviton plays a central role in the conjectured AdS 3 /LCFT 2 correspondence, where it has been widely interpreted as the bulk counterpart of a logarithmic partner in the putative boundary logarithmic conformal field theory. In this work, we develop a geometric interpretation of this Jordan structure based on complexified Virasoro evolution. Starting from the logarithmic graviton of Grumiller and Johansson, we show that its logarithmic coefficient y ⁡ ( τ , ρ ) = − i ​ τ − ln ⁡ cosh ⁡ ρ y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho admits, near the AdS 3 boundary, the asymptotic form y ⁡ ( τ , ρ ) = − s + ln ⁡ 2 + 𝒪 ⁡ ( e − 2 ​ ρ ) , s = ρ + i ​ τ . y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),\qquad s=\rho+i\tau. The same complex parameter naturally appears in the exponentiation of the Virasoro generator L 0 L_{0} acting on a rank-two Jordan cell, L 0 = h ​ 𝟏 + N , N 2 = 0 , L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,\qquad N^{2}=0, for which e s ​ L 0 = e s ​ h ​ ( 1 + s ​ N ) . e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN). We show that the resulting Jordan evolution reproduces the characteristic logarithmic mixing of the logarithmic sector, while analytic continuation s → s + 2 ​ π ​ i s\rightarrow s+2\pi i generates the corresponding logarithmic monodromy. From this perspective, radial evolution, temporal evolution, Jordan mixing, and logarithmic monodromy may be viewed as different manifestations of a single complexified Virasoro flow. The analysis suggests a geometric interpretation of the indecomposable structures characteristic of logarithmic conformal field theory and offers a new perspective on the logarithmic graviton within the conjectural AdS 3 /LCFT 2 framework.

Introduction

Three-dimensional gravity has long served as a valuable laboratory for exploring fundamental questions of quantum gravity, holography, and conformal field theory. In particular, gravity in asymptotically anti-de Sitter space, AdS
3
, occupies a distinguished position because its asymptotic symmetry algebra consists of two copies of the Virasoro algebra, thereby providing one of the earliest and most explicit realizations of holography
[
1
]
.

The addition of a gravitational Chern–Simons term to the Einstein–Hilbert action leads to Topologically Massive Gravity (TMG), a theory that propagates a single local massive graviton while retaining many of the attractive features of three-dimensional gravity
[
4
,
3
]
. When formulated on an AdS
3
background, TMG exhibits a rich spectrum of linearized excitations, including left-moving, right-moving, and massive graviton branches
[
12
,
2
]
.

A particularly interesting regime arises at the so-called chiral point,

\mu\ell=1,

(1)

where the massive graviton becomes degenerate with the left-moving graviton
[
12
]
. Initially, it was conjectured that this degeneracy might lead to a consistent theory of purely chiral gravity
[
12
,
18
]
. Shortly thereafter, Grumiller and Johansson demonstrated that the degeneracy gives rise to an additional logarithmic solution of the linearized field equations
[
9
]
. This logarithmic graviton exhibits asymptotic behaviour incompatible with ordinary highest-weight representations and instead forms a rank-two Jordan cell together with the left-moving graviton.

The appearance of Jordan blocks is a hallmark of logarithmic conformal field theories (LCFTs), whose representation theory differs fundamentally from that of ordinary conformal field theories
[
11
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
14
]
. In LCFTs, the Virasoro generator
L_{0}
is not necessarily diagonalizable. Instead, one encounters generalized eigenvectors satisfying

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{L},

(2)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{\log}+\psi_{L}.

(3)

Such indecomposable but reducible representations generate logarithmic terms in correlation functions and produce characteristic logarithmic monodromies under analytic continuation
[
11
,
5
,
14
]
.

The discovery of the logarithmic graviton led naturally to the proposal that TMG at the chiral point should be holographically dual to a logarithmic conformal field theory
[
9
,
17
,
10

. A variety of consistency checks for this correspondence have subsequently been explored through the analysis of correlation functions, holographic renormalization, asymptotic symmetries, partition functions, and representation theory
[

10

6

. The resulting conjectured AdS
3

[
9
,
17
]

From the gravitational perspective, the logarithmic mode is usually viewed as a consequence of the degeneracy occurring at the chiral point. From the conformal field theory perspective, the same mode is interpreted as the bulk manifestation of a logarithmic partner belonging to an indecomposable Virasoro representation. While the existence of the logarithmic graviton and its structural alignment with LCFT paradigms are widely recognized, the geometric significance of the associated Jordan structure remains only partially understood.

Recent work by the present author has revisited logarithmic sectors in critical topologically massive gravity from the viewpoint of monodromy, correlation functions, and indecomposable representation theory. In
[
15
]
, logarithmic sectors and their associated monodromies were investigated through the structure of two-point functions in critical topologically massive gravity, highlighting the role played by logarithmic modes in the emergence of non-trivial analytic behaviour. Subsequently,
[
16
]
introduced the concept of Virasoro flow and demonstrated that exponentiation of non-diagonalizable Virasoro generators naturally produces logarithmic mixing, Jordan evolution, and logarithmic monodromy. In particular, it was shown that rank-two Jordan cells admit a natural description in terms of a complexified evolution parameter governing the action of the Virasoro generator
L_{0}
.

The present work may be viewed as a continuation of this programme. Whereas
[
15
]
focused on logarithmic sectors and monodromy, and
[
16
]
developed the corresponding Virasoro-flow framework at the level of indecomposable representations, here we demonstrate that the same complexified flow parameter emerges naturally from the asymptotic structure of the logarithmic graviton itself. More precisely, we show that the logarithmic coefficient appearing in the Grumiller–Johansson solution asymptotically reproduces the parameter governing Jordan evolution generated by
L_{0}
. This establishes a direct bridge between the asymptotic bulk geometry and the Virasoro-flow framework developed previously.

The starting point is the logarithmic solution of Grumiller and Johansson
[
9
]
,

\psi_{\log}=y(\tau,\rho),\psi_{L},

(4)

where

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho.

(5)

At first sight, the coefficient
y(\tau,\rho)
appears merely as a multiplicative factor relating the logarithmic graviton to the left-moving graviton. However, near the AdS

\ln\cosh\rho=\rho-\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),

(6)

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),\qquad s=\rho+i\tau.

(7)

This observation suggests that the logarithmic coefficient asymptotically coincides, up to an additive constant, with a complex flow parameter.
Motivated by this fact, we investigate the exponentiation of the Virasoro generator in a rank-two Jordan representation. Writing

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,\qquad N^{2}=0,

(8)

one obtains

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN).

(9)

The same parameter

L_{0}
. This establishes a concrete geometric realization of the dictionary between the asymptotic bulk geometry and the indecomposable structure of the boundary LCFT.

A second objective of this work is to investigate the analytic continuation of this complexified Virasoro flow. Under

s\longrightarrow s+2\pi i,

(10)

\psi_{\log}\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\left(\psi_{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi_{L}\right),

(11)

which is precisely the logarithmic monodromy characteristic of rank-two Jordan cells. We shall show that this monodromy admits a natural interpretation as the analytic continuation of the asymptotic Virasoro-flow parameter.

The principal result of the present paper is therefore not the discovery of a new logarithmic graviton, nor a derivation of the conjectured AdS
3

2

[
16

Specifically, we show that the asymptotic logarithmic coefficient of the Grumiller–Johansson mode naturally generates the same complexified evolution parameter that governs Jordan evolution in indecomposable Virasoro representations. The resulting exponentiation analytically reproduces the characteristic logarithmic mixing and monodromy of the dual LCFT, thereby linking the geometric structure of the bulk logarithmic graviton to the algebraic framework developed in
[
16
]

Radial evolution, temporal evolution, Jordan mixing, and logarithmic monodromy thereby become different manifestations of a single underlying complex flow.

2
The Logarithmic Graviton at the Chiral Point

In this section we review the appearance of the logarithmic graviton in topologically massive gravity at the chiral point. Our goal is not to reproduce all details of the original derivation, but rather to establish the essential results that will be needed in the subsequent sections. In particular, we shall see how the logarithmic mode arises from a degeneracy between the massive graviton and the left-moving graviton, and how its characteristic logarithmic coefficient naturally emerges.

2.1
Topologically Massive Gravity

Topologically massive gravity (TMG) is a three-dimensional theory of gravity whose action consists of the Einstein–Hilbert term supplemented by a gravitational Chern–Simons term

S_{\mathrm{TMG}}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{3}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left(R+\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{16\pi G\mu}S_{\mathrm{CS}},

(12)

where

G=\text{Newton's constant},

(13)

\ell=\text{AdS radius},

(14)

and

\mu=\text{Chern--Simons coupling}.

(15)

The gravitational Chern–Simons action is given by

S_{\mathrm{CS}}=\int d^{3}x\,\epsilon^{\lambda\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda\sigma}\left(\partial_{\mu}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\rho\nu}+\frac{2}{3}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\tau}\Gamma^{\tau}_{\nu\rho}\right).

(16)

The presence of the Chern–Simons term introduces a propagating massive graviton into three-dimensional gravity.

2.2
Field Equations

Varying the action yields the equations of motion

G_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{\mu}C_{\mu\nu}=0,

(17)

where
G_{\mu\nu}
is the Einstein tensor and

C_{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{\mu}^{\ \alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\left(R_{\beta\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\beta\nu}R\right)

(18)

is the Cotton tensor. The AdS
3
metric satisfies these equations and therefore remains a vacuum solution of the theory.

2.3
Linearized Perturbations Around AdS
3

We now consider a small perturbation around the AdS
3
background

g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},

(19)

where

|h_{\mu\nu}|\ll 1.

(20)

Linearizing the equations of motion leads to a third-order differential equation for the perturbation. A remarkable feature of TMG is that the linearized operator factorizes into three first-order operators

\mathcal{D}^{L}\mathcal{D}^{R}\mathcal{D}^{M}\,h_{\mu\nu}=0.

(21)

The three operators correspond respectively to

1.

a left-moving graviton,

2.

a right-moving graviton,

3.

a massive graviton.

The corresponding solutions satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{L}h^{L}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0,

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{R}h^{R}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0,

(23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{M}h^{M}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0.

(24)

2.4
The Chiral Point

A special value of the coupling occurs when

\mu\ell=1.

(25)

This value is known as the
chiral point
. At this point the massive branch and the left-moving branch become degenerate. Schematically,

\mathcal{D}^{M}=\mathcal{D}^{L}.

(26)

As a consequence, the massive graviton and the left-moving graviton coincide

\psi^{M}_{\mu\nu}=\psi^{L}_{\mu\nu}.

(27)

The appearance of this degeneracy is the key mechanism responsible for the logarithmic mode.

2.5
Emergence of the Logarithmic Solution

Whenever two independent solutions become degenerate, a new linearly independent solution may be generated by differentiation with respect to the parameter controlling the degeneracy. In the present case, one considers

\psi^{\mathrm{new}}_{\mu\nu}=\lim_{\mu\ell\rightarrow 1}\frac{\psi^{M}_{\mu\nu}-\psi^{L}_{\mu\nu}}{\mu\ell-1}.

(28)

Since both numerator and denominator vanish at the chiral point, the limit is finite. The resulting field is the logarithmic graviton. Equation (
28
) is completely analogous to the construction of generalized eigenvectors in linear algebra when two eigenvalues become degenerate.

2.6
Explicit Form of the Logarithmic Mode

The resulting logarithmic graviton can be written in the form

\psi^{\mathrm{new}}_{\mu\nu}=y(\tau,\rho)\,\psi^{L}_{\mu\nu},

(29)

where the logarithmic coefficient is

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho.

(30)

This is the key formula from which all later developments of the present paper will originate. At first sight, the function
y(\tau,\rho)
appears merely as a multiplicative coefficient. However, we shall later see that it possesses a deeper interpretation as the asymptotic parameter of a complexified Virasoro flow.

2.7
Properties of the Logarithmic Coefficient

The coefficient

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho

(31)

contains two distinct contributions. The first term,

-i\tau,

(32)

depends linearly on the time coordinate. The second term,

-\ln\cosh\rho,

(33)

depends on the radial coordinate. The coexistence of these two pieces is already suggestive of a complex structure combining temporal and radial evolution. This observation will become central in later sections.

2.8
Jordan Structure of the Logarithmic Graviton

An important property of the logarithmic graviton is its transformation under the Virasoro generator
L_{0}
. The left-moving graviton satisfies

L_{0}\psi_{L}=h\psi_{L}.

(34)

Thus
\psi_{L}
is an ordinary eigenvector. The logarithmic mode satisfies

L_{0}\psi_{\log}=h\psi_{\log}+\psi_{L}.

(35)

Therefore
\psi_{\log}
is not an ordinary eigenvector. Instead, it is a generalized eigenvector.

Equations (
34
) and (
35
) show that the pair

\{\psi_{L},\psi_{\log}\}

(36)

forms a rank-two Jordan cell. This fact is one of the strongest indications that the dual boundary theory should be a logarithmic conformal field theory.

2.9
Connection with LCFT

In logarithmic conformal field theory, one encounters pairs of states satisfying

\displaystyle L_{0}|C\rangle

\displaystyle=h|C\rangle,

(37)

\displaystyle L_{0}|D\rangle

\displaystyle=h|D\rangle+|C\rangle.

(38)

Comparing these equations with (
34
) and (
35
), one immediately identifies

|C\rangle\leftrightarrow\psi_{L},

(39)

and

|D\rangle\leftrightarrow\psi_{\log}.

(40)

The logarithmic graviton therefore provides the bulk realization of the Jordan structure characteristic of logarithmic conformal field theory.

2.10
Physical Significance

The discovery of the logarithmic graviton had profound implications. Prior to its appearance, one might have expected the chiral point merely to eliminate the massive graviton sector. Instead, the degeneracy generates an entirely new logarithmic solution. The resulting mode

1.

is linearly independent of the left-moving graviton,

2.

carries logarithmic behaviour,

3.

forms a Jordan cell with the left-moving graviton,

4.

points naturally toward an LCFT
2
dual description.

The logarithmic graviton therefore represents one of the most important pieces of evidence for the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence.

2.11
Summary

At the chiral point

\mu\ell=1,

(41)

the massive graviton becomes degenerate with the left-moving graviton. This degeneracy generates a new logarithmic solution,

\psi_{\log}=y(\tau,\rho)\,\psi_{L},

(42)

whose coefficient is

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho.

(43)

The logarithmic graviton is not an ordinary eigenstate of
L_{0}
but instead forms a rank-two Jordan cell together with the left-moving graviton

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{L},

(44)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{\log}+\psi_{L}.

(45)

These properties provide the starting point for the analysis developed in the remainder of this work, where the logarithmic coefficient will be reinterpreted as the parameter of a complexified Virasoro flow.

3
Jordan Structure and Logarithmic Partners

The purpose of this section is to review the algebraic structure underlying logarithmic conformal field theories and to explain how logarithmic partners arise from non-diagonalizable representations of the Virasoro algebra. Since the logarithmic graviton appearing in chiral topologically massive gravity is expected to be dual to an operator in a logarithmic conformal field theory, understanding the Jordan structure of the corresponding representation is essential. In particular, the logarithmic mode discovered at the chiral point will later be interpreted as a generalized eigenvector of the Virasoro generator
L_{0}
.

3.1
Ordinary Highest-Weight Representations

We begin by recalling the familiar situation in an ordinary conformal field theory. Let
|\psi\rangle
be a highest-weight state. By definition,

L_{0}|\psi\rangle=h|\psi\rangle,

(46)

where
h
denotes the conformal weight. Equation (
46
) shows that
|\psi\rangle
is an eigenvector of
L_{0}
. In matrix language, the action of
L_{0}
on the one-dimensional vector space spanned by
|\psi\rangle
is simply

L_{0}=(h).

(47)

Thus the operator is diagonalizable. Exponentiating
L_{0}
gives

e^{sL_{0}}|\psi\rangle=e^{sh}|\psi\rangle,

(48)

so the state simply acquires an exponential factor. No mixing with other states occurs.

3.2
Degeneracy and the Appearance of Jordan Cells

The situation changes when two states become degenerate. Suppose that two states possess the same conformal weight
h
. If
L_{0}
remains diagonalizable, one may choose a basis in which

L_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}.

(49)

The two states remain independent eigenvectors. However, logarithmic conformal field theories exhibit a different possibility. Instead of remaining diagonalizable, the operator develops a Jordan block

L_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}h&1\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}.

(50)

The off-diagonal entry prevents diagonalization. This is the algebraic origin of logarithmic behaviour.

3.3
Eigenvectors and Generalized Eigenvectors

To understand Eq. (
50
), let us introduce two states

|\psi_{L}\rangle,\qquad|\psi_{\log}\rangle.

(51)

The first state satisfies

L_{0}|\psi_{L}\rangle=h|\psi_{L}\rangle.

(52)

Thus
|\psi_{L}\rangle
is an ordinary eigenvector.

The second state satisfies

L_{0}|\psi_{\log}\rangle=h|\psi_{\log}\rangle+|\psi_{L}\rangle.

(53)

This is not an ordinary eigenvalue equation. Indeed,

L_{0}|\psi_{\log}\rangle\neq h|\psi_{\log}\rangle.

(54)

Therefore
|\psi_{\log}\rangle
is not an eigenvector. Instead, it is called a
generalized eigenvector
.

3.4
The Nilpotent Operator

The Jordan structure becomes clearer if we define

N=L_{0}-h\mathbf{1}.

(55)

Acting on the ordinary state gives

\displaystyle N|\psi_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=(L_{0}-h\mathbf{1})|\psi_{L}\rangle

(56)

\displaystyle=L_{0}|\psi_{L}\rangle-h|\psi_{L}\rangle

(57)

\displaystyle=0.

(58)

Hence

N|\psi_{L}\rangle=0.

(59)

Acting on the logarithmic state gives

\displaystyle N|\psi_{\log}\rangle

\displaystyle=(L_{0}-h\mathbf{1})|\psi_{\log}\rangle

(60)

\displaystyle=L_{0}|\psi_{\log}\rangle-h|\psi_{\log}\rangle

(61)

\displaystyle=|\psi_{L}\rangle.

(62)

Therefore

N|\psi_{\log}\rangle=|\psi_{L}\rangle.

(63)

Applying
N
once more,

\displaystyle N^{2}|\psi_{\log}\rangle

\displaystyle=N|\psi_{L}\rangle

(64)

\displaystyle=0.

(65)

Thus

N^{2}=0.

(66)

The operator
N
is therefore nilpotent.

3.5
Jordan Decomposition of
L_{0}

Using Eq. (
55
), we may write

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N.

(67)

In matrix form,

L_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}h&1\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}=h\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(68)

The first matrix is diagonal. The second matrix is nilpotent because

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(69)

This decomposition will play a central role throughout the remainder of the paper.

3.6
Reducibility of the Representation

The representation generated by

\{|\psi_{L}\rangle,|\psi_{\log}\rangle\}

(70)

is reducible. To see this, consider the subspace

\mathcal{V}_{L}=\mathrm{span}\{|\psi_{L}\rangle\}.

(71)

Equation (
52
) shows that

L_{0}\mathcal{V}_{L}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{L}.

(72)

Thus
\mathcal{V}_{L}
is an invariant subspace. The representation is therefore reducible.

3.7
Why the Representation is Indecomposable

Although the representation is reducible, it is not decomposable. To understand this point, suppose we attempt to write the full space as

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{L}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\log}.

(73)

For such a decomposition to exist, both subspaces must be invariant under
L_{0}
. However, Eq. (
53
) gives

L_{0}|\psi_{\log}\rangle=h|\psi_{\log}\rangle+|\psi_{L}\rangle.

(74)

The action of
L_{0}
therefore takes a vector from the logarithmic sector into the ordinary sector. Consequently,

L_{0}\mathcal{V}_{\log}\not\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{\log}.

(75)

The second subspace is not invariant. Therefore the representation cannot be decomposed into a direct sum of invariant subspaces. It is thus
indecomposable
.

3.8
Origin of Logarithmic Behaviour

The appearance of logarithms may now be understood algebraically. Consider the exponential

e^{sL_{0}}.

(76)

Using Eq. (
67
),

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{s(h\mathbf{1}+N)}.

(77)

Since
h\mathbf{1}
commutes with
N
,

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}e^{sN}.

(78)

Expanding the second factor,

e^{sN}=1+sN+\frac{s^{2}}{2!}N^{2}+\cdots.

(79)

Because
N^{2}=0
,

e^{sN}=1+sN.

(80)

Therefore

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN).

(81)

Applying Eq. (
81
) to the logarithmic state yields

e^{sL_{0}}|\psi_{\log}\rangle=e^{sh}\left(|\psi_{\log}\rangle+s|\psi_{L}\rangle\right).

(82)

The linear dependence on the flow parameter
s
is the algebraic origin of logarithmic behaviour.

3.9
Connection with Logarithmic Conformal Field Theory

In logarithmic conformal field theories, pairs of operators

C(z),\qquad D(z)

(83)

are associated with states satisfying relations analogous to

\displaystyle L_{0}|C\rangle

\displaystyle=h|C\rangle,

(84)

\displaystyle L_{0}|D\rangle

\displaystyle=h|D\rangle+|C\rangle.

(85)

The operator
D
is referred to as the logarithmic partner of
C
.

The logarithmic graviton encountered in chiral topologically massive gravity is expected to be dual to precisely such a logarithmic partner in the boundary LCFT
2
. The Jordan structure described above therefore provides the algebraic framework proposed to underlie the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence.

3.10
Summary

We have shown that logarithmic conformal field theories are characterized by non-diagonalizable representations of the Virasoro generator
L_{0}
. The logarithmic sector consists of an ordinary eigenvector and a generalized eigenvector satisfying

\displaystyle L_{0}|\psi_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=h|\psi_{L}\rangle,

(86)

\displaystyle L_{0}|\psi_{\log}\rangle

\displaystyle=h|\psi_{\log}\rangle+|\psi_{L}\rangle.

(87)

Introducing the nilpotent operator

N=L_{0}-h\mathbf{1},

(88)

one obtains

N^{2}=0,\qquad L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N.

(89)

The resulting representation is reducible but indecomposable, and exponentiation of
L_{0}
produces the characteristic Jordan flow

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN).

(90)

These algebraic structures will provide the foundation for our subsequent interpretation of the logarithmic graviton as an asymptotic realization of a complexified Virasoro flow.

4
Asymptotic Structure of the Logarithmic Coefficient

The logarithmic graviton of chiral topologically massive gravity is characterized by the appearance of the function

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho,

(91)

which enters the construction of the logarithmic mode through

\psi^{\mathrm{new}}_{\mu\nu}=y(\tau,\rho)\,\psi^{L}_{\mu\nu},

(92)

where
\psi^{L}_{\mu\nu}
denotes the left-moving graviton.

The purpose of this section is to analyze the asymptotic behaviour of the logarithmic coefficient
y(\tau,\rho)
near the AdS
3
boundary. We shall show that it naturally gives rise to a complex parameter built from the radial and temporal coordinates. This observation will later provide the bridge between the logarithmic graviton and the Virasoro-flow picture.

4.1
Asymptotic Region of AdS
3

The AdS
3
boundary is located at

\rho\rightarrow\infty.

(93)

Consequently, the asymptotic behaviour of the logarithmic graviton is determined by the large-
\rho
expansion of the function

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho.

(94)

The nontrivial part of the calculation is therefore the asymptotic expansion of
\ln\cosh\rho
.

4.2
Expansion of
\ln\cosh\rho

We begin from the definition of the hyperbolic cosine

\cosh\rho=\frac{e^{\rho}+e^{-\rho}}{2}.

(95)

Factoring out the dominant exponential
e^{\rho}
gives

\displaystyle\cosh\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\rho}}{2}\left(1+e^{-2\rho}\right).

(96)

Taking the logarithm of both sides yields

\displaystyle\ln\cosh\rho

\displaystyle=\ln\left[\frac{e^{\rho}}{2}\left(1+e^{-2\rho}\right)\right].

(97)

Using the elementary property

\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b,

(98)

we obtain

\displaystyle\ln\cosh\rho

\displaystyle=\ln(e^\rho)+\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)+\ln\left(1+e^{-2\rho}\right).

(99)

Since

\ln(e^\rho)=\rho

(100)

and

\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=-\ln 2,

(101)

it follows that

\ln\cosh\rho=\rho-\ln 2+\ln\left(1+e^{-2\rho}\right).

(102)

4.3
Large-
\rho
Behaviour

As
\rho\rightarrow\infty
, the quantity

e^{-2\rho}

(103)

becomes exponentially small. We may therefore use the Taylor expansion

\ln(1+x)=x-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\frac{x^{3}}{3}-\cdots,\qquad|x|<1,

(104)

with

x=e^{-2\rho}.

(105)

Substituting into Eq. (
102
) gives

\displaystyle\ln\cosh\rho

\displaystyle=\rho-\ln 2+e^{-2\rho}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-4\rho}+\frac{1}{3}e^{-6\rho}-\cdots.

(106)

Retaining only the leading asymptotic correction yields

\boxed{\ln\cosh\rho=\rho-\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho})}.

(107)

Equation (
107
) will be the fundamental asymptotic relation used throughout the remainder of this work.

4.4
Asymptotic Form of the Logarithmic Coefficient

Substituting Eq. (
107
) into Eq. (
91
), we obtain

\displaystyle y(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle=-i\tau-\left(\rho-\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho})\right).

(108)

Distributing the minus sign gives

\displaystyle y(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle=-\rho-i\tau+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}).

(109)

Therefore the logarithmic coefficient behaves asymptotically as

\boxed{y(\tau,\rho)=-\rho-i\tau+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho})}.

(110)

We observe that the radial coordinate
\rho
and the temporal coordinate
\tau
appear together in the combination

\rho+i\tau.

(111)

This motivates the introduction of a complex parameter.

4.5
Emergence of a Complex Parameter

Define

s=\rho+i\tau.

(112)

Using Eq. (
112
), Eq. (
110
) becomes

\displaystyle y(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}).

(113)

Hence we arrive at the central result of this section

\boxed{y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),\qquad s=\rho+i\tau.}

(114)

Thus, near the AdS
3
boundary, the logarithmic coefficient is asymptotically equivalent to a complex quantity constructed from the radial and temporal coordinates.

4.6
Interpretation

At first sight, the function

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho

(115)

appears merely as a coefficient multiplying the left-moving graviton in the construction of the logarithmic mode. However, the asymptotic analysis reveals a deeper structure. Equation (
114
) shows that, near the boundary,

y(\tau,\rho)\sim-s,\qquad s=\rho+i\tau.

(116)

The logarithmic coefficient therefore behaves as a complex parameter combining radial and temporal evolution. This observation suggests that the logarithmic graviton may admit an interpretation in terms of a complexified flow generated by the Virasoro operator
L_{0}
. In the following section we shall demonstrate that the Jordan structure of the logarithmic sector naturally leads to such a Virasoro flow and that the parameter
s
identified above plays precisely the role of the corresponding flow parameter.

4.7
Summary

We have shown that the logarithmic coefficient appearing in the chiral logarithmic graviton satisfies

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho,

(117)

and that its asymptotic behaviour near the AdS
3
boundary is

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),\qquad s=\rho+i\tau.

(118)

The logarithmic coefficient therefore acquires the structure of an asymptotic complex parameter. This result provides the first indication that the logarithmic graviton may be understood in terms of a complexified Virasoro flow, a viewpoint that will be developed in the next section.

5
Complexified Virasoro Flow

In the previous section, we established that the logarithmic coefficient of the chiral graviton satisfies the asymptotic relation

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),\qquad s=\rho+i\tau.

(119)

This result suggests that the logarithmic graviton naturally carries a complex parameter
s
constructed from the radial and temporal coordinates. The purpose of the present section is to show that this parameter arises naturally in the exponentiation of a non-diagonalizable Virasoro generator and therefore admits an interpretation as a complexified Virasoro-flow parameter. We shall proceed step by step, beginning with the Jordan structure of the logarithmic sector.

5.1
Jordan Structure of the Logarithmic Sector

As discussed in the previous section, the logarithmic mode and the left-moving graviton form a rank-two Jordan cell under the action of
L_{0}

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=h\,\psi_{L},

(120)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=h\,\psi_{\log}+\psi_{L}.

(121)

The state
\psi_{L}
is an ordinary eigenvector of
L_{0}
, whereas
\psi_{\log}
is a generalized eigenvector. To make the Jordan structure explicit, it is convenient to introduce the nilpotent operator

N=L_{0}-h\mathbf{1}.

(122)

Using Eqs. (
120
) and (
121
), we obtain

\displaystyle N\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=0,

(123)

\displaystyle N\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=\psi_{L}.

(124)

Applying
N
once more to Eq. (
124
) gives

N^{2}\psi_{\log}=N\psi_{L}=0.

(125)

Since
N
annihilates both states of the Jordan cell after two applications, we have

N^{2}=0.

(126)

Consequently,

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,\qquad N^{2}=0.

(127)

This is the standard Jordan decomposition of the Virasoro generator.

5.2
Definition of Virasoro Flow

Let
\psi
be a state in the logarithmic sector. We define the Virasoro flow generated by
L_{0}
through

\psi(s)=e^{sL_{0}}\psi,

(128)

where
s
is a complex parameter. Equation (
128
) is the natural analogue of standard time evolution generated by a Hamiltonian. The difference is that here the generator is the Virasoro operator
L_{0}
. Our goal is to understand the structure of this flow when
L_{0}
is non-diagonalizable.

5.3
Exponentiation of the Jordan Generator

Substituting Eq. (
127
) into Eq. (
128
) yields

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{s(h\mathbf{1}+N)}.

(129)

Since
h\mathbf{1}
commutes with
N
, the exponential factorizes

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}\,e^{sN}.

(130)

We now compute the exponential of the nilpotent operator. By definition,

e^{sN}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{n}}{n!}N^{n}.

(131)

Using the nilpotency condition (
126
),

N^{2}=0,

(132)

all terms with
n\geq 2
vanish. Therefore

\displaystyle e^{sN}

\displaystyle=1+sN.

(133)

Substituting into Eq. (
130
) gives

\boxed{e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN).}

(134)

This is the fundamental formula governing Virasoro flow in the logarithmic sector.

5.4
Flow of the Ordinary Eigenvector

Let us first apply Eq. (
134
) to the ordinary eigenvector
\psi_{L}
. Using Eq. (
123
),

N\psi_{L}=0,

(135)

we find

\displaystyle e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=e^{sh}(1+sN)\psi_{L}

(136)

\displaystyle=e^{sh}\psi_{L}.

(137)

Hence

\boxed{e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{L}=e^{sh}\psi_{L}.}

(138)

The ordinary eigenvector undergoes a simple exponential rescaling.

5.5
Flow of the Logarithmic Partner

We now consider the logarithmic state
\psi_{\log}
. Applying Eq. (
134
) gives

\displaystyle e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=e^{sh}(1+sN)\psi_{\log}.

(139)

Using Eq. (
124
),

N\psi_{\log}=\psi_{L},

(140)

we obtain

\displaystyle e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=e^{sh}\left(\psi_{\log}+s\psi_{L}\right).

(141)

Thus

\boxed{e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}=e^{sh}\left(\psi_{\log}+s\psi_{L}\right).}

(142)

This is the characteristic feature of Jordan evolution. Unlike an ordinary eigenvector, the logarithmic state mixes with its Jordan partner under the flow.

5.6
Emergence of the Flow Parameter

Equation (
142
) shows that the generalized eigenvector always appears with coefficient

s.

(143)

More precisely, the Jordan contribution is

s\,\psi_{L}.

(144)

The parameter
s
therefore measures the amount of logarithmic mixing generated by the non-diagonalizable structure of
L_{0}
. This observation becomes particularly significant when compared with the asymptotic form of the logarithmic graviton derived in the previous section. Indeed, Eq. (
119
) implies

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}).

(145)

Therefore the logarithmic coefficient itself is asymptotically proportional to the Jordan-flow parameter.

5.7
Comparison with the Logarithmic Graviton

The logarithmic graviton takes the form

\psi_{\log}=y(\tau,\rho)\,\psi_{L}.

(146)

Near the AdS
3
boundary,

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),

(147)

so that

\psi_{\log}=\left(-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho})\right)\psi_{L}.

(148)

The logarithmic partner therefore carries precisely the same linear dependence on
s
that appears in the Jordan-flow formula (
142
). This is the central observation of the present work. The coefficient multiplying the ordinary graviton is not arbitrary. Rather, it coincides asymptotically with the parameter governing the Virasoro evolution generated by the non-diagonalizable operator
L_{0}
.

5.8
Interpretation as a Complexified Virasoro Flow

The results obtained above suggest the following interpretation. The logarithmic graviton is governed by the Jordan structure

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N.

(149)

Exponentiation of this generator produces the flow

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN),

(150)

whose characteristic feature is the appearance of the linear coefficient
s
. On the other hand, the logarithmic graviton itself contains the asymptotic coefficient

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}).

(151)

Thus the logarithmic mode naturally realizes the same structure that emerges from Jordan evolution. This motivates the interpretation of the logarithmic graviton as an asymptotic realization of a complexified Virasoro flow, with

s=\rho+i\tau

(152)

playing the role of the corresponding flow parameter.

5.9
Summary

The central result of this section is the Jordan-flow formula

\boxed{e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN),\qquad N^{2}=0.}

(153)

For the logarithmic partner, this implies

\boxed{e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}=e^{sh}\left(\psi_{\log}+s\psi_{L}\right).}

(154)

Comparing this result with the asymptotic behaviour

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),

(155)

reveals that the logarithmic coefficient of the chiral graviton coincides asymptotically with the parameter governing the Jordan evolution generated by
L_{0}
. This provides the first indication that the logarithmic graviton may be interpreted as an asymptotic realization of a complexified Virasoro flow.

6
Time Evolution, Radial Evolution and Jordan Flow

In the previous section, we established that the logarithmic graviton admits an asymptotic description in terms of the complex parameter

s=\rho+i\tau,

(156)

and that the logarithmic coefficient satisfies

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}).

(157)

We also showed that the Jordan structure of the logarithmic sector leads naturally to the Virasoro flow

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN),\qquad N^{2}=0.

(158)

The purpose of the present section is to examine the geometric meaning of the parameter
s
. In particular, we shall show that the radial coordinate
\rho
and the temporal coordinate
\tau
enter the Jordan flow on an equal footing and together generate a complexified Virasoro evolution.

6.1
The Complex Flow Parameter

Recall that the asymptotic logarithmic coefficient can be written as

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),

(159)

where

s=\rho+i\tau.

(160)

The parameter
s
possesses both a real and an imaginary part

\mathrm{Re}(s)=\rho,\qquad\mathrm{Im}(s)=\tau.

(161)

Consequently, the Jordan flow generated by
L_{0}
contains information about both radial and temporal evolution. To understand this structure more clearly, it is useful to study the contributions of
\rho
and
\tau
separately.

6.2
Decomposition of the Virasoro Flow

Substituting

s=\rho+i\tau

(162)

into the Virasoro evolution operator gives

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{(\rho+i\tau)L_{0}}.

(163)

Since
L_{0}
commutes with itself, the exponential may be factorized

e^{(\rho+i\tau)L_{0}}=e^{\rho L_{0}}e^{i\tau L_{0}}.

(164)

Equation (
164
) reveals that the Virasoro flow consists of two distinct pieces

1.

a radial contribution generated by
e^{\rho L_{0}}
,

2.

a temporal contribution generated by
e^{i\tau L_{0}}
.

The full Jordan flow combines both effects into a single complex evolution.

6.3
Radial Evolution

Let us first consider the radial component

e^{\rho L_{0}}.

(165)

Acting on the ordinary eigenvector
\psi_{L}
yields

e^{\rho L_{0}}\psi_{L}=e^{\rho h}\psi_{L}.

(166)

Thus the radial coordinate controls the scaling behaviour of the ordinary graviton. For the logarithmic partner, Eq. (
158
) gives

e^{\rho L_{0}}\psi_{\log}=e^{\rho h}\left(\psi_{\log}+\rho\,\psi_{L}\right).

(167)

The logarithmic state therefore acquires an additional contribution proportional to
\rho
. This is precisely the type of linear growth expected from a Jordan block. Indeed, the coefficient multiplying
\psi_{L}
is exactly the radial coordinate itself. Consequently, the radial dependence of the logarithmic graviton is directly encoded in the Jordan structure of
L_{0}
.

6.4
Temporal Evolution

We now turn to the temporal component

e^{i\tau L_{0}}.

(168)

Acting on the ordinary eigenvector gives

e^{i\tau L_{0}}\psi_{L}=e^{i\tau h}\psi_{L}.

(169)

This corresponds to the familiar oscillatory behaviour generated by the conformal weight
h
. For the logarithmic partner, we obtain

e^{i\tau L_{0}}\psi_{\log}=e^{i\tau h}\left(\psi_{\log}+i\tau\,\psi_{L}\right).

(170)

The Jordan structure therefore produces a contribution proportional to
i\tau
. This is particularly significant because the logarithmic graviton coefficient contains precisely such a term

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho.

(171)

Thus the temporal dependence of the logarithmic mode mirrors the temporal contribution generated by Jordan evolution.

6.5
Combining Radial and Temporal Evolution

The full Virasoro flow is obtained by combining Eqs. (
167
) and (
170
). Using

s=\rho+i\tau,

(172)

the logarithmic partner evolves according to

e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}=e^{sh}\left(\psi_{\log}+s\,\psi_{L}\right).

(173)

Substituting

s=\rho+i\tau

(174)

gives

e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}=e^{sh}\left[\psi_{\log}+(\rho+i\tau)\psi_{L}\right].

(175)

The Jordan contribution therefore contains both the radial coordinate and the temporal coordinate simultaneously. The logarithmic mixing generated by
L_{0}
is thus controlled by a single complex quantity.

6.6
Comparison with the Logarithmic Graviton

The logarithmic graviton is characterized by the coefficient

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho.

(176)

Near the boundary,

y(\tau,\rho)=-\rho-i\tau+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),

(177)

or equivalently,

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}).

(178)

The same combination
\rho+i\tau
that governs the Jordan evolution therefore appears directly in the logarithmic coefficient. This correspondence is highly nontrivial. The logarithmic mode is not merely a state belonging to a Jordan cell. Rather, its coefficient naturally reproduces the parameter controlling the Jordan flow itself.

6.7
Geometric Interpretation

The preceding analysis suggests the following geometric picture. The radial coordinate
\rho
generates asymptotic scaling behaviour. The temporal coordinate
\tau
generates oscillatory evolution. Together they form the complex quantity

s=\rho+i\tau.

(179)

The logarithmic graviton organizes these two types of evolution into a single object through the coefficient

y(\tau,\rho).

(180)

Near the AdS
3
boundary, this coefficient becomes asymptotically equivalent to the Jordan-flow parameter. Consequently, the logarithmic graviton may be viewed as realizing a complexified Virasoro evolution in which radial and temporal behaviour are unified by the non-diagonalizable generator
L_{0}
.

6.8
Relation to Holographic Evolution

In the AdS
3
/CFT
2
correspondence, the radial coordinate is usually interpreted as encoding holographic scale evolution. Equation

s=\rho+i\tau

(181)

suggests that the logarithmic sector possesses a richer structure. Instead of a purely radial flow, one encounters a complexified evolution involving both the holographic direction and the temporal direction. The Jordan structure of the logarithmic sector therefore naturally combines

•

radial evolution,

•

temporal evolution,

•

logarithmic mixing.

This observation provides a geometric interpretation of the Virasoro flow discussed in the previous section.

6.9
Summary

The principal result of this section is that the Virasoro-flow parameter

s=\rho+i\tau

(182)

admits a direct geometric interpretation. Its real part generates radial evolution, while its imaginary part generates temporal evolution. The Jordan-flow formula

e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}=e^{sh}\left(\psi_{\log}+s\psi_{L}\right)

(183)

therefore combines radial and temporal dynamics into a single complex evolution. Since the logarithmic graviton coefficient satisfies

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),

(184)

the logarithmic mode itself appears to asymptotically realize the same structure as the complexified Jordan flow asymptotically near the AdS
3
boundary. This interpretation will prove particularly useful in the next section, where we investigate the analytic continuation of the flow and its relation to logarithmic monodromy.

7
Analytic Continuation and Logarithmic Monodromy

In the previous sections, we established that the logarithmic graviton admits an asymptotic description in terms of the complex parameter

s=\rho+i\tau,

(185)

and that the corresponding Virasoro evolution is governed by the Jordan-flow operator

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN),\qquad N^{2}=0.

(186)

The purpose of the present section is to investigate the analytic continuation of this flow. We shall show that the logarithmic monodromy arises naturally from the Jordan structure of
L_{0}
when the flow parameter is continued around a nontrivial cycle in the complex plane. This provides a direct connection between the Virasoro-flow picture developed in the present work and the logarithmic monodromy discussed in our previous investigations.

7.1
Analytic Continuation of the Flow Parameter

The asymptotic relation

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho})

(187)

suggests interpreting
s
as the natural parameter governing the logarithmic sector. Up to this point, we have considered

\rho\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

(188)

Consequently,

s=\rho+i\tau

(189)

is simply a complex combination of two real coordinates. To investigate monodromy, we now analytically continue the radial coordinate into the complex plane

\rho\longrightarrow\rho+i\theta,

(190)

where
\theta
is a real parameter. Substituting Eq. (
190
) into Eq. (
185
) gives

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=(\rho+i\theta)+i\tau

(191)

\displaystyle=\rho+i(\tau+\theta).

(192)

The flow parameter therefore acquires an additional imaginary contribution.

7.2
Closed Loops in the Complex Plane

A monodromy transformation is obtained by transporting the complexified coordinate around a closed loop. Specifically, consider the shift

\theta\longrightarrow\theta+2\pi.

(193)

The flow parameter then transforms according to

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\rho+i(\tau+\theta+2\pi)

(194)

\displaystyle=s+2\pi i.

(195)

Thus the analytic continuation produces the fundamental transformation

\boxed{s\longrightarrow s+2\pi i.}

(196)

The monodromy of the logarithmic sector is therefore encoded in the behaviour of the Virasoro flow under shifts of the complex parameter.

7.3
Monodromy of the Ordinary Eigenvector

Let us first examine the effect of Eq. (
196
) on the ordinary eigenvector
\psi_{L}
. Using

e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{L}=e^{sh}\psi_{L},

(197)

we obtain

\displaystyle e^{(s+2\pi i)L_{0}}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=e^{(s+2\pi i)h}\psi_{L}

(198)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi ih}\,e^{sh}\psi_{L}.

(199)

Hence

\boxed{\psi_{L}\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\psi_{L}.}

(200)

The ordinary eigenvector simply acquires a phase. This is precisely the behaviour expected for an ordinary highest-weight state.

7.4
Monodromy of the Logarithmic Partner

The situation is fundamentally different for the logarithmic state. Starting from the Jordan-flow formula

e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}=e^{sh}\left(\psi_{\log}+s\psi_{L}\right),

(201)

we replace
s
by
s+2\pi i

\displaystyle e^{(s+2\pi i)L_{0}}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=e^{(s+2\pi i)h}\left[\psi_{\log}+(s+2\pi i)\psi_{L}\right].

(202)

Factoring out
e^{2\pi ih}
yields

\displaystyle e^{(s+2\pi i)L_{0}}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi ih}e^{sh}\left[\psi_{\log}+s\psi_{L}+2\pi i\,\psi_{L}\right].

(203)

Using Eq. (
201
), we obtain

\boxed{\psi_{\log}\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\left(\psi_{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi_{L}\right).}

(204)

This is the characteristic logarithmic monodromy. Unlike the ordinary eigenvector, the logarithmic partner mixes with its Jordan companion under analytic continuation.

7.5
Monodromy Matrix

It is convenient to collect the states into the vector

\Psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{L}\\
\psi_{\log}\end{pmatrix}.

(205)

Using Eqs. (
200
) and (
204
), the monodromy transformation may be written as

\Psi\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\begin{pmatrix}1&2\pi i\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\Psi.

(206)

The corresponding monodromy matrix is therefore

\boxed{M=e^{2\pi ih}\begin{pmatrix}1&2\pi i\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.}

(207)

The matrix is not diagonalizable. Instead, it possesses precisely the Jordan structure characteristic of logarithmic conformal field theory.

7.6
Derivation from the Jordan Decomposition

The monodromy matrix may also be derived directly from the Jordan decomposition

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,\qquad N^{2}=0.

(208)

The monodromy operator associated with a full circuit in the complex plane is

M=e^{2\pi iL_{0}}.

(209)

Substituting the Jordan decomposition gives

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i(h\mathbf{1}+N)}

(210)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi ih}\,e^{2\pi iN}.

(211)

Since
N^{2}=0
,

e^{2\pi iN}=1+2\pi iN.

(212)

Consequently,

\boxed{M=e^{2\pi ih}\left(\mathbf{1}+2\pi iN\right).}

(213)

Equation (
213
) is equivalent to the matrix form (
207
). The logarithmic monodromy therefore follows directly from the nilpotent part of the Virasoro generator.

7.7
Connection with the Logarithmic Graviton

We now return to the logarithmic coefficient

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho.

(214)

Near the boundary,

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}).

(215)

Under the analytic continuation

s\longrightarrow s+2\pi i,

(216)

the asymptotic coefficient transforms as

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\longrightarrow-(s+2\pi i)+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho})

(217)

\displaystyle=y-2\pi i.

(218)

Thus the logarithmic coefficient itself records the same monodromy that appears in the Jordan-flow picture. The logarithmic graviton therefore carries the imprint of the underlying Jordan structure both algebraically and geometrically.

7.8
Interpretation

The analysis above reveals a remarkably simple picture. The logarithmic graviton possesses an asymptotic flow parameter

s=\rho+i\tau.

(219)

Analytic continuation of this parameter around a nontrivial cycle produces

s\longrightarrow s+2\pi i.

(220)

The Jordan structure of
L_{0}
then converts this shift into logarithmic mixing

\psi_{\log}\longrightarrow\psi_{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi_{L}.

(221)

Monodromy therefore emerges naturally as the analytic continuation of the complexified Virasoro flow. In this sense, logarithmic monodromy is not an independent phenomenon. Rather, it is the manifestation of the Jordan-flow structure under continuation in the complex parameter space.

7.9
Summary

The central result of this section is that analytic continuation of the Virasoro-flow parameter

s\longrightarrow s+2\pi i

(222)

produces the logarithmic monodromy

\psi_{\log}\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\left(\psi_{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi_{L}\right).

(223)

Equivalently, the monodromy operator is

M=e^{2\pi iL_{0}}=e^{2\pi ih}\left(\mathbf{1}+2\pi iN\right).

(224)

The logarithmic monodromy therefore arises directly from the analytic continuation of the complexified Virasoro flow. This provides a natural link between the geometric interpretation developed in the present work and the Jordan-monodromy structure previously associated with logarithmic modes.

Since the absolute validity of the
\text{AdS}_{3}/\text{LCFT}_{2}

While we do not claim a proof of the duality, the fact that the asymptotic geometry of critical TMG naturally assembles into a complexified Virasoro flow parameter provides compelling evidence for how a logarithmic holographic dictionary is realized on a smooth spacetime manifold.

In the previous sections, we have shown that the logarithmic graviton of chiral topologically massive gravity possesses a natural interpretation in terms of a complexified Virasoro flow. The key observations were:

1.

The logarithmic coefficient satisfies the asymptotic relation

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),\qquad s=\rho+i\tau.

(225)

2.

The logarithmic sector forms a rank-two Jordan cell,

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{L},

(226)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{\log}+\psi_{L}.

(227)

3.

Exponentiation of the Virasoro generator produces the Jordan flow

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN).

(228)

4.

Analytic continuation of the flow parameter generates the logarithmic monodromy

M=e^{2\pi iL_{0}}=e^{2\pi ih}(\mathbf{1}+2\pi iN).

(229)

The purpose of the present section is to discuss the implications of these results for the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence.

8.1
The Standard AdS
3
/CFT
2
Dictionary

The AdS
3
/CFT
2
correspondence relates gravitational dynamics in the bulk of AdS
3
to conformal field theory on the boundary. In the Brown–Henneaux analysis, the asymptotic symmetry algebra of AdS
3
consists of two copies of the Virasoro algebra

\mathrm{Vir}_{L}\oplus\mathrm{Vir}_{R}.

(230)

The corresponding generators satisfy

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c_{L}}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\delta_{m+n,0},

(231)

\displaystyle[\bar{L}_{m},\bar{L}_{n}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)\bar{L}_{m+n}+\frac{c_{R}}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\delta_{m+n,0}.

(232)

Ordinary AdS
3
/CFT
2
duality assumes that the boundary theory is an ordinary conformal field theory whose states form highest-weight representations of these Virasoro algebras. In such representations, the operator
L_{0}
is diagonalizable.

8.2
The Chiral Point and Logarithmic Modes

At the chiral point of topologically massive gravity,

\mu\ell=1,

(233)

the structure of the theory changes dramatically. The left-moving graviton becomes degenerate with a massive graviton mode. The resulting logarithmic graviton is not an ordinary highest-weight state. Instead, it forms a Jordan cell together with the left-moving graviton

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{L},

(234)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{\log}+\psi_{L}.

(235)

The appearance of such Jordan blocks is one of the defining characteristics of logarithmic conformal field theory. Consequently, within the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence, the boundary theory is expected to be logarithmic rather than an ordinary CFT
2
.

8.3
Jordan Structure as a Holographic Signature

One of the central observations of the present work is that the Jordan structure is not merely an algebraic property of the boundary theory.
Instead, it is directly encoded in the asymptotic behaviour of the bulk logarithmic graviton. Indeed, the logarithmic coefficient satisfies

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),\qquad s=\rho+i\tau.

(236)

At the same time, the Jordan evolution generated by
L_{0}
takes the form

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN).

(237)

The same parameter
s
therefore appears both in

1.

the asymptotic bulk logarithmic graviton,

2.

the Jordan evolution of the boundary Virasoro representation.

This suggests that, if the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence holds, the Jordan structure expected in the dual LCFT
2
may already be reflected geometrically in the asymptotic bulk solution.

8.4
Radial Evolution and Holographic Scale Transformations

A central feature of holography is the interpretation of the radial coordinate as a scale parameter. In AdS/CFT, motion toward the boundary corresponds to renormalization-group evolution in the dual theory. The parameter

s=\rho+i\tau

(238)

contains the radial coordinate explicitly. Its real part is

\mathrm{Re}(s)=\rho.

(239)

Consequently, the Jordan flow generated by

e^{sL_{0}}

(240)

contains the holographic direction as an intrinsic component. The logarithmic mixing

\psi_{\log}\rightarrow\psi_{\log}+s\psi_{L}

(241)

therefore evolves along the holographic direction itself. From this perspective, the radial behaviour of the bulk graviton appears compatible with the logarithmic structure expected in a putative LCFT
2
dual.

8.5
Time Evolution and Virasoro Evolution

The imaginary part of the flow parameter is

\mathrm{Im}(s)=\tau.

(242)

Consequently, the same Jordan flow simultaneously contains temporal evolution. Rather than treating time evolution and holographic evolution as completely separate notions, the logarithmic sector combines them into a single complex quantity. The bulk logarithmic mode therefore naturally organizes radial and temporal behaviour into one unified structure. This is precisely what allows the logarithmic coefficient to reproduce the Virasoro-flow parameter.

8.6
Monodromy as a Holographic Phenomenon

A second important consequence concerns logarithmic monodromy. Analytic continuation of the flow parameter yields

s\longrightarrow s+2\pi i.

(243)

The corresponding Jordan transformation is

\psi_{\log}\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\left(\psi_{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi_{L}\right).

(244)

Traditionally, such monodromies are viewed as properties of logarithmic conformal blocks or logarithmic operators in the boundary theory. The analysis of the present work suggests a complementary viewpoint. The monodromy may equally be understood as arising from the analytic continuation of the bulk Virasoro flow. Thus the logarithmic monodromy may be viewed not only as a boundary phenomenon but also as a geometric feature of the asymptotic bulk description.

8.7
A Geometric Realization of LCFT Structure

One of the longstanding questions in the AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence is how the characteristic LCFT structures emerge from bulk gravity. The logarithmic graviton is known to provide evidence for the existence of Jordan cells on the boundary. The present analysis sharpens this statement.
We have shown that

1.

the logarithmic coefficient becomes asymptotically equivalent to a complex flow parameter;

2.

this same parameter generates Jordan evolution through the Virasoro operator
L_{0}
;

3.

analytic continuation of the parameter reproduces the expected logarithmic monodromy.

Consequently, the asymptotic geometry of the bulk logarithmic graviton reproduces structures that are compatible with, and may be interpreted in terms of, characteristic features of LCFT
2
.

8.8
Conceptual Interpretation

The results obtained in this work suggest the following picture. In ordinary AdS
3
/CFT
2
, the operator
L_{0}
acts diagonally and generates standard conformal evolution. At the chiral point, the logarithmic graviton introduces a non-diagonalizable component

N=L_{0}-h\mathbf{1}.

(245)

The resulting Jordan structure generates

1.

logarithmic mixing,

2.

complex Virasoro flow,

3.

logarithmic monodromy.

Remarkably, all three structures are encoded in the asymptotic coefficient

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho.

(246)

The logarithmic graviton therefore suggests a geometric interpretation of the indecomposable structures that are expected to arise in the conjectured LCFT
2
dual.

8.9
Implications of the Present Work

The principal implication of the present work is not the discovery of a new logarithmic mode. The logarithmic graviton was already known. Rather, the new observation is that its logarithmic coefficient admits a natural interpretation as the parameter of a complexified Virasoro flow. This viewpoint provides

1.

a geometric interpretation of Jordan evolution;

2.

a direct connection between radial evolution and logarithmic mixing;

3.

a natural origin for logarithmic monodromy;

4.

a new perspective on how LCFT structures might emerge from the bulk description.

In this sense, the Virasoro-flow picture supplies an additional layer of understanding of the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence.

8.10
Summary

We have shown that the logarithmic graviton encodes the same complex parameter that governs Jordan evolution generated by the Virasoro operator
L_{0}
. The asymptotic relation

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),\qquad s=\rho+i\tau,

(247)

connects the bulk logarithmic coefficient directly to the Virasoro-flow parameter. As a consequence, the characteristic structures of logarithmic conformal field theory—Jordan cells, logarithmic mixing, and logarithmic monodromy—may admit a natural geometric interpretation in the asymptotic bulk description. This provides a new perspective on the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence and suggests that the logarithmic graviton may be viewed as the bulk manifestation of a complexified Virasoro flow.

9
Discussion

In this work, we have investigated the logarithmic graviton of chiral topologically massive gravity from the perspective of Virasoro evolution, Jordan structure, and analytic continuation. Our analysis was motivated by the observation that the logarithmic coefficient appearing in the Grumiller–Johansson solution,

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho,

(248)

contains both the temporal coordinate
\tau
and the radial coordinate
\rho
in a remarkably simple form. The central observation of the present paper is that near the AdS
3
boundary this coefficient becomes

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}),\qquad s=\rho+i\tau,

(249)

so that the logarithmic graviton naturally introduces a complex parameter

s=\rho+i\tau.

(250)

This parameter combines radial evolution and temporal evolution into a single complex quantity. While such a rewriting may initially appear to be a simple asymptotic reformulation, the subsequent analysis reveals that the same parameter governs the exponentiation of the Virasoro generator
L_{0}
in the logarithmic sector.

9.1
Summary of Results

The first step of our analysis was a detailed examination of the Jordan structure associated with the logarithmic graviton. We reviewed the rank-two indecomposable representation

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{L},

(251)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{\log}+\psi_{L},

(252)

and introduced the nilpotent operator

N=L_{0}-h\mathbf{1},\qquad N^{2}=0.

(253)

This allowed the decomposition

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,

(254)

which immediately yields

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN).

(255)

Acting on the logarithmic state gives

e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}=e^{sh}\left(\psi_{\log}+s\psi_{L}\right),

(256)

demonstrating that the flow parameter appears directly in the logarithmic mixing.

The second step was the identification of the asymptotic logarithmic coefficient with the complex parameter
s
. This establishes a direct connection between the asymptotic bulk geometry and the Jordan evolution generated by the Virasoro operator.

The third step was the study of analytic continuation. Under

s\longrightarrow s+2\pi i,

(257)

the Jordan evolution produces

\psi_{\log}\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\left(\psi_{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi_{L}\right),

(258)

which reproduces the characteristic logarithmic monodromy of rank-two Jordan cells.

Taken together, these results show that the logarithmic graviton reproduces structures that closely parallel those appearing algebraically in logarithmic conformal field theory.

9.2
Geometric Interpretation of Jordan Structure

One of the main conceptual conclusions of this work is that the Jordan structure need not be viewed purely as an abstract property of the boundary conformal field theory. Traditionally, the relations

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{L},

(259)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{\log}+\psi_{L}

(260)

are introduced as algebraic properties of an indecomposable Virasoro representation. Our analysis suggests a complementary interpretation. The asymptotic coefficient of the logarithmic graviton itself generates the parameter governing Jordan evolution. Consequently, asymptotic bulk geometry exhibits structures that are naturally compatible with the indecomposable structures expected in an LCFT
2
description. From this perspective, logarithmic mixing is not merely a feature of the boundary representation theory but is encoded geometrically in the bulk logarithmic mode.

9.3
Relation to Previous Work

The logarithmic graviton itself is not new. Its existence was established by Grumiller and Johansson through the analysis of linearized topologically massive gravity at the chiral point
[
9
]
. Likewise, the relation between logarithmic gravitons and logarithmic conformal field theories has been extensively investigated in the literature. The present work does not alter those results. Instead, it provides a different viewpoint on their interpretation. The novel element is the observation that the asymptotic logarithmic coefficient naturally defines a complex flow parameter which simultaneously governs

1.

radial evolution,

2.

temporal evolution,

3.

Jordan mixing,

4.

logarithmic monodromy.

This provides a geometric framework in which several previously distinct structures emerge from a single underlying quantity.

9.4
Connection with Virasoro Flow

A central motivation for the present work originates from our earlier investigations of Virasoro flow and logarithmic monodromy
[
15
,
16
]
. In those studies, complexified evolution generated by the Virasoro operator was analyzed independently of the logarithmic graviton. The present paper establishes a direct bridge between those constructions and the AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence. More precisely, the logarithmic coefficient appearing in the bulk solution reproduces asymptotically the same parameter that generates the Virasoro flow. This observation suggests that the logarithmic graviton may be viewed as a geometric realization of the Jordan evolution associated with
L_{0}
. Consequently, the present work provides a natural continuation of our previous studies and suggests a way of interpreting Virasoro flow within a holographic setting.

9.5
Implications for the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
Correspondence

The conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence is usually formulated in terms of asymptotic symmetries, correlation functions, and Jordan representations of the Virasoro algebra. The analysis presented here suggests an additional perspective. The logarithmic coefficient

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho

(261)

already contains structures that naturally reproduce the ingredients associated with the Jordan structure expected in a putative dual LCFT. In particular, the asymptotic relation

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho})

(262)

shows that the same parameter controlling Virasoro evolution emerges naturally from the bulk solution itself. This suggests that, within the conjectural AdS
3
/LCFT
2
framework, the indecomposable structure expected in the boundary theory may be reflected geometrically in the asymptotic bulk description. Although the present work does not constitute a proof of the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence, it provides a new way of understanding how logarithmic structures arise holographically.

9.6
Future Directions

Several directions for future investigation naturally emerge from the present analysis.

First, it would be desirable to formulate the complexified Virasoro flow directly at the level of the asymptotic symmetry algebra. Such a construction could provide a more systematic connection between Brown–Henneaux asymptotic symmetries and Jordan evolution.

Second, it would be interesting to investigate whether analogous structures arise in higher-rank Jordan cells. In logarithmic conformal field theory, indecomposable representations of rank greater than two are known to occur. Their gravitational realization remains largely unexplored.

Third, the role of monodromy deserves further study. The logarithmic monodromy obtained here arises from analytic continuation of the complex flow parameter. Understanding its relation to other monodromy constructions appearing in conformal field theory, holography, and integrable systems may provide additional insight into the geometric origin of logarithmic behaviour.

Fourth, it would be worthwhile to investigate whether similar complexified Virasoro flows appear in other holographic models involving logarithmic sectors, including higher-spin theories, critical gravities, and generalized massive gravities.

Finally, one may ask whether the complex parameter

s=\rho+i\tau

(263)

admits a deeper interpretation within holographic renormalization group flow. Since the radial coordinate is naturally associated with scale evolution, the emergence of a complexified scale parameter may point toward a broader geometric framework underlying logarithmic holography.

9.7
Concluding Remarks

The logarithmic graviton discovered at the chiral point of topologically massive gravity has played a central role in the development of the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence. In the conventional description, its significance lies in the fact that it forms a Jordan cell with the left-moving graviton and thereby signals the emergence of logarithmic conformal structure on the boundary. The analysis presented in this paper suggests a complementary interpretation.

The logarithmic coefficient of the graviton naturally defines a complex parameter that asymptotically coincides with the parameter governing Jordan evolution generated by the Virasoro operator
L_{0}
. Exponentiation of this operator reproduces the characteristic logarithmic mixing, while analytic continuation generates the expected logarithmic monodromy.

Consequently, radial evolution, temporal evolution, Jordan structure, and logarithmic monodromy may all be viewed as different manifestations of a single complexified Virasoro flow.

In this sense, the logarithmic graviton provides a geometric realization of the indecomposable structure characteristic of logarithmic conformal field theory, thereby offering a new perspective on the conjectured AdS
3
/LCFT
2
correspondence and on the role of Virasoro evolution in logarithmic holography.

Appendix A
Jordan Blocks and Generalized Eigenvectors

The purpose of this appendix is to review the mathematical structure of Jordan blocks and generalized eigenvectors. These concepts play a central role in logarithmic conformal field theory and underlie the appearance of logarithmic gravitons in chiral topologically massive gravity.

A.1
Ordinary Eigenvectors

Let
A
be a linear operator acting on a vector space
\mathcal{V}
. A vector
v\in\mathcal{V}
is called an eigenvector of
A
with eigenvalue
\lambda
if

Av=\lambda v.

(264)

For example, consider the matrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\
0&3\end{pmatrix}.

(265)

The vectors

e_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad e_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}

(266)

satisfy

Ae_{1}=2e_{1},\qquad Ae_{2}=3e_{2}.

(267)

Thus
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are ordinary eigenvectors.

A.2
Degeneracy

Now consider

A=\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}.

(268)

Both basis vectors satisfy

Ae_{i}=he_{i}.

(269)

The eigenvalue
h
is degenerate. Nevertheless, the matrix remains diagonalizable because two independent eigenvectors exist.

A.3
Jordan Blocks

A fundamentally different situation occurs for

A=\begin{pmatrix}h&1\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}.

(270)

To determine the eigenvectors, let

v=\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}.

(271)

Equation (
264
) becomes

\begin{pmatrix}h&1\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}=h\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}.

(272)

Subtracting the right-hand side yields

\begin{pmatrix}y\\
0\end{pmatrix}=0.

(273)

Hence

y=0.

(274)

The only eigenvectors are therefore proportional to

\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

(275)

Although the eigenvalue is doubly degenerate, only one eigenvector exists. Consequently the matrix is not diagonalizable.

A.4
Generalized Eigenvectors

Introduce

N=A-h\mathbf{1}.

(276)

For the Jordan block (
270
),

N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(277)

Its square is

N^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(278)

Thus
N
is nilpotent.

Define

v_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad v_{\log}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

(279)

One immediately finds

\displaystyle Nv_{L}

\displaystyle=0,

(280)

\displaystyle Nv_{\log}

\displaystyle=v_{L}.

(281)

Therefore

\displaystyle Av_{L}

\displaystyle=hv_{L},

(282)

\displaystyle Av_{\log}

\displaystyle=hv_{\log}+v_{L}.

(283)

The vector
v_{\log}
is not an ordinary eigenvector. Instead, it is called a generalized eigenvector.

A.5
Relation to LCFT

In logarithmic conformal field theory, the Virasoro generator
L_{0}
acts precisely in this manner

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{L},

(284)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=h\psi_{\log}+\psi_{L}.

(285)

Thus the logarithmic sector forms a rank-two Jordan block. The logarithmic graviton of chiral topologically massive gravity realizes exactly this structure.

Appendix B
Exponentiation of Non-Diagonalizable Operators

The purpose of this appendix is to derive the exponentiation formula used throughout the paper.

B.1
Exponential of an Operator

For any operator
A
,

e^{A}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{A^{n}}{n!}.

(286)

Suppose

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,\qquad N^{2}=0.

(287)

Since
h\mathbf{1}
commutes with
N
,

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{s(h\mathbf{1}+N)}=e^{sh}e^{sN}.

(288)

B.2
Expansion of the Nilpotent Part

Using (
286
),

\displaystyle e^{sN}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(sN)^{n}}{n!}

(289)

\displaystyle=1+sN+\frac{s^{2}}{2!}N^{2}+\cdots.

(290)

Because

N^{2}=0,

(291)

all higher powers vanish. Therefore

e^{sN}=1+sN.

(292)

Substituting (
292
) into (
288
) yields

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN).

(293)

B.3
Action on the Jordan Cell

Applying (
293
) to
\psi_{L}
gives

\displaystyle e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle=e^{sh}(1+sN)\psi_{L}

(294)

\displaystyle=e^{sh}\psi_{L}.

(295)

Applying (
293
) to
\psi_{\log}
gives

\displaystyle e^{sL_{0}}\psi_{\log}

\displaystyle=e^{sh}(1+sN)\psi_{\log}

(296)

\displaystyle=e^{sh}\left(\psi_{\log}+s\psi_{L}\right).

(297)

This is the Jordan flow used throughout the paper.

Appendix C
Asymptotic Expansion of
\ln\cosh\rho

The purpose of this appendix is to derive the asymptotic expansion used in the main text.

C.1
Definition

Recall

\cosh\rho=\frac{e^{\rho}+e^{-\rho}}{2}.

(298)

Factor out
e^{\rho}

\cosh\rho=\frac{e^{\rho}}{2}\left(1+e^{-2\rho}\right).

(299)

Taking the logarithm,

\ln\cosh\rho=\ln\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{2}\right)+\ln\left(1+e^{-2\rho}\right).

(300)

Using

\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b,

(301)

we obtain

\ln\cosh\rho=\rho-\ln 2+\ln(1+e^{-2\rho}).

(302)

C.2
Large-
\rho
Expansion

For large
\rho
,

e^{-2\rho}\ll 1.

(303)

The Taylor series

\ln(1+x)=x-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\frac{x^{3}}{3}-\cdots

(304)

gives

\ln(1+e^{-2\rho})=e^{-2\rho}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-4\rho}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-6\rho}).

(305)

Substituting into (
302
),

\ln\cosh\rho=\rho-\ln 2+e^{-2\rho}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-4\rho}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-6\rho}).

(306)

Hence

\boxed{\ln\cosh\rho=\rho-\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho})}.

(307)

C.3
Application to the Logarithmic Graviton

The logarithmic coefficient is

y(\tau,\rho)=-i\tau-\ln\cosh\rho.

(308)

Using the asymptotic expansion,

\displaystyle y(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle=-i\tau-\left(\rho-\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho})\right)

(309)

\displaystyle=-(\rho+i\tau)+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}).

(310)

Defining

s=\rho+i\tau,

(311)

we obtain

y(\tau,\rho)=-s+\ln 2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\rho}).

(312)

This is the key asymptotic relation used throughout the paper.

Appendix D
Monodromy of Logarithmic Modes

The purpose of this appendix is to derive the logarithmic monodromy associated with Jordan cells.

D.1
Ordinary Primary Fields

Consider a primary field of weight
h

\Phi_{L}(z)=z^{h}.

(313)

Analytic continuation around the origin corresponds to

z\longrightarrow ze^{2\pi i}.

(314)

Therefore

\displaystyle\Phi_{L}(z)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(ze^{2\pi i})^{h}

(315)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi ih}z^{h}.

(316)

Thus

\Phi_{L}\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\Phi_{L}.

(317)

D.2
Logarithmic Partner

Now define

\Phi_{\log}(z)=(\log z)\,z^{h}.

(318)

Under analytic continuation,

\log z\longrightarrow\log z+2\pi i.

(319)

Hence

\displaystyle\Phi_{\log}(z)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(\log z+2\pi i)z^{h}

(320)

\displaystyle=(\log z)z^{h}+2\pi i\,z^{h}.

(321)

Therefore

\Phi_{\log}\longrightarrow\Phi_{\log}+2\pi i\,\Phi_{L}.

(322)

Including the conformal factor,

\Phi_{\log}\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\left(\Phi_{\log}+2\pi i\,\Phi_{L}\right).

(323)

D.3
Matrix Representation

Introduce the vector

\Psi=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{L}\\
\Phi_{\log}\end{pmatrix}.

(324)

The monodromy acts as

\Psi\longrightarrow e^{2\pi ih}\begin{pmatrix}1&2\pi i\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\Psi.

(325)

The monodromy matrix is therefore

M=e^{2\pi ih}\begin{pmatrix}1&2\pi i\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(326)

Using

N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad N^{2}=0,

(327)

one may write

M=e^{2\pi ih}(\mathbf{1}+2\pi iN).

(328)

D.4
Relation to Virasoro Flow

From Appendix B,

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}(1+sN).

(329)

Choosing

s=2\pi i,

(330)

gives

e^{2\pi iL_{0}}=e^{2\pi ih}(\mathbf{1}+2\pi iN).

(331)

Thus the logarithmic monodromy is precisely the exponentiated Jordan evolution evaluated after one circuit around the origin. This establishes the direct connection between logarithmic monodromy and the complexified Virasoro flow discussed in the main text.
