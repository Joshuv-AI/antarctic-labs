Introduction

Scope

 This project utilizes copulas for damage detection in a Structural Health Monitoring
(SHM) application. A copula-based system was chosen for the benefit of multivariate joint
distribution with a goal to detect damage based on how the system as a whole reacts rather
than one or two sensors by themselves. Copulas are commonly used in the field of finance for
risk modeling based on two or more random inputs. A few applications in the field of SHM and
Non-Destructive Evaluation (NDE) have been researched mostly on risk or reliability of the
structure. The goal of this project is to determine if a copula-based approach can be used for
damage detection. An unsupervised learning method was desired to reduce the dimensionality,
minimal training, and be a faster evaluation method than other unsupervised methods. If a
copula method can be used to detect damage what additional information on the damage can
be interpreted. The remainder of this report will go over the background needed, SHM
methodology, SHM applications, conclusions, and future developments.

Background Copula’s

 Copulas are a statistical and model-based tool used in finance to analyze dependence of
multiple random variables to quantify the joint probability of some action happening. An
example in the finance field is risk management by modeling the joint probability of the market
to determine how a change in one portion of the market will affect the others. Copulas are
essential to model complex dependencies and determine a correlation between two or more
unrelated variables. On the side of SHM and NDE few applications of copulas have been
conducted, most of which are risk probability, reliability, and anomaly detection. A few notable
ones that influenced the methods tested in this paper are as follows. “A vine-copulas based
multi-sensor fusion damage monitoring … in dam engineering” (Yu, Zhenyu, 2024) utilized a
vine copula to develop an early warning system for damage based on static monitoring data [1].
“Copula approach for simulated damages caused by landfall US hurricanes” (Leahy, 2020) to
determine correlation between hurricane damage and maximum landfall wind speed [2].
“Structural reliability analysis using a copula-based evidence theory model” (Jiang, Zhang,

Wang, Han, 2014) utilized copula-function-based evidence theory model to determine the
structural reliability [3]. Lastly, “Fatigue Load Modeling of Floating Wind Turbines Based on Vine
Copula Theory and Machine learning” (Yuan, Huang et al, 2024) utilized a machine learning with
a vine copula joint prediction to predict flapwise fatigue loads at blade root of turbines [4].

 Copulas utilize the Cumulative Distribution Functions (CDF) of the two or more variables
to model the joint distribution function of the variables determining the probability that action
of one variable will do an action on the other variables. The output of a copula is the joint
distribution probably plot. Sklar’s theorem is essential to outputting joint distribution models.
Sklar’s theorem (Equation 1) utilizes the copula function selected and the marginal CDF of each
variable to output the joint CDF. C is the copula function selected that change the joint
distribution output each function focuses on different tail dependence methods. The changes in
the tails can be seen in Figure 1. Gaussian copulas are better for uniform distributions, t copula
focuses on the tail dependence of a uniform distribution, and Gumbel has high tail dependence
on the upper end and lower tail dependence on the low end of the plots. The copula type
chosen for this project was t copula reasoning behind this choice will be in the SHM application

Background Data Analyzed

 The data used in this project came from a three-story structure analyzed by Los Alamos
National Laboratory (LANL) called “Structural Health Monitoring Algorithm Comparison Using
Standard Data Sets” (Eloi, Gyuhae, et al, 2009) [7]. The three-story structure was an application
of LANL’s statistical pattern recognition paradigm for SHM in a damaged configuration. The
application of the statistical pattern recognition paradigm included testing many SHM and NDE
techniques to compare the damage detection methods commonly tested. Some of SHM and
NDE methods tested are the first four statistical moments, Probability Density Function (PDF),
time series analysis, autoregression, autocorrelation, and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE).

 The three-story structure consists of aluminum columns and plates using bolted
connections with adjustable stiffness shown in Figure 2 a. The third floor had a column
extending from the third floor to the second floor but not fixed to the second floor. The column
was near an adjustable bumper fixed to the second floor shown in Figure 2 b. The bumper
impact during shaker testing would cause nonlinear behavior on the structure that could be
measured by the sensors. The adjustability of stiffness of each floor and the bumper system
could simulate loose connections or fatigue cracks that open or close under operational and
environmental loading operations [7]. Lastly, the structure was exciting using an electrodynamic
shaker.

SHM Methodology

 The project is aimed at a model-based damage detection system for in-situ data
collection of SHM structures prior to severe damage or failure of the structure. The goal of this
project is for faster damage detection on structures using NDE without using continuous
detection methods. The copula approach is aimed at a system similar to acoustic emission
where once the threshold is passed damage is detected. This method was chosen to reduce the
time needed to perform NDE tests in-situ. The planned implementation of this method involves
performing the data analysis every few months automatically if the threshold is passed a check
of the structure is examined as normal if the threshold is not passed, a routine checkup of the
structure can be delayed or reduce the time during examination. The SHM and NDE field utilize
other statistical and model-based damage detection systems that more accurate damage
detection methods. A few of these methods are autoregressive model, singular value
decomposition, or root mean squared error. The copula-based method isn’t going to replace
those methods; it’s aimed at increasing the time between the routine checkup of the structure.
This increase in the time between routine checkups is done by not being a continuous system
reducing the computation resources, lack of complexity to apply, ease to analyze, and quick
analysis method.

 Farrar and Worden proposed that all SHM and NDE procedures can be put in the context
of statistical pattern recognition problems (Farrar, Worden, 2007) [8]. The four-step process
described by the LANL statistical pattern recognition paradigm:

 i. Operational Evaluation
 ii. Data Acquisition
 iii. Feature Extraction
 iv. Statistical Modeling for Feature Classification

 The layout of the rest of this section follows the above paradigm. First is the operational
evaluation section going over the environment and application goal of SHM or NDE method.
Next is the data acquisition section covering the acquisition system used for data collection.
Next is the feature extraction section covering the damage and severity of the structure. Lastly
the statistical modeling section covers the methods used to reduce noise and data analysis
methods implemented. The data used was from another report which limits the data
acquisition, feature extraction, and feature classification sections were limited.

Operational Evaluation

 The operational evaluation section will cover the justification, definition of damage,
considerations, and limitations of the system proposed. The proposed copula-based statistical

method is aimed for use in the field as identification of possible damage and location. Once the

 The operational evaluation section commonly goes into the environmental or
operational justification of system proposed as a modeling or damage detection system a
copula-based approach is dependent on the structure or system It is applied too. An example of
this is if the copula method proposed is applied to a bridge. Justification is economic concern
that the bridge might need repairs more often than is currently being done, or life-safety that
the bridge might collapse. The justification of this project is economically limiting the time
needed to analyze the structure for damage, or amount of visits the SHM team assigned to a
structure is required to make yearly. The method proposed is an intermediate of continuous
NDE and other more in-depth statistical analysis software’s. As an intermediate between a
continuous NDE system and in-depth analysis software a copula-based system can be applied to
as a system that doesn’t have undamaged training data and has economic constraints that
doesn’t allow for in-depth damage detection methods.

 A copula-based system has some apparent flaws and benefits compared to other
continuous or time/calculation constrained approaches. A benefit over the two approaches is it
requires less computation time and resources. A flaw similar to continuous approaches is that a
copula-based system is limited in the information on damage the system can identify. Farrar and
Worden (Farrar, Worden, 2007) identify a few questions that should be answered to identify the
damage to a SHM structure:

 i. Is the damage presented in the system (Existence)?
 ii. Where is the damage (Location)?
 iii. What kind of damage is present (Type)?
 iv. What is the extent of damage (Severity)?

Feature Extraction

 The features to be extracted from the proposed copula-based statistical method is to
determine if damage in the structure has occurred and severity. Depending on the severity of
damage, other traditional NDE techniques such as visual inspection, dye-penetrant, or machine
learning techniques should be implemented to know if the structure is compromised. Damage
will be defined as threshold if that threshold has been passed the structure has accumulated
damage. The threshold is based on the error in the PDF results of the nominal to copula

 8

predicted data. The threshold for this project was based on the tested data for the known
undamaged states. Future testing of a threshold value that limits false positives will be needed
before the method can be applied to other structures. The location of damage can be inferred
from the multiple accelerometers placed along the structure.

Statistical Model Development

 The copula-based statistical method is the only one tested in this project. Two versions
were conducted a “Trained” and “Tested”.

 Trained method tested the copula approach against itself. The true data for both the
input and output data were used to generate a new prediction which should be identical to
nominal data other than the difference from CDF to time-based data. The trained copula was
generated based on the accelerometer on base floor and the run of the accelerometer being
tested. Example if the data was generated for accelerometer five run 14 the copula was fit from
accelerometer two run 14 data and accelerometer five run 14.

 The tested data copulas were fit to the input of accelerometer two and the undamaged
data. Example if the data was generated for accelerometer five run 14 the copula was fit to
accelerometer two run 14 and accelerometer five run 1. This was done to train the copula on
the undamaged/nominal configuration of the structure then based on the error of the data to
true results, damage could be identified. This method of tested data was chosen not to limit the
usage of the software since this method could be installed in a structure that is not known to be
damaged or undamaged.

 The data used in the project was not modified from the raw data to determine how the
copula-based approach performed with noise and if that noise affected the threshold produced
for identifying damage. Since the copula is fitted to the data collected the selected copula didn’t
change the result on early tests. The data collected in the three-building structure appeared to
be gaussian and would likely provide a better approximation of the data using a gaussian copula.
T-student was chosen instead of the gaussian to make the approach developed useful on more
structures than the one tested. T-student copulas have a larger tail dependence on all four sides
providing them a better fit to more data sets that might have more noise than the lab tested
configuration.

SHM Application

Generation of Copula Data

 The first process in this method was the fitting of the copula to the two data sets for
both the trained and tested data. To fit the copula to the data the first step was to use MATLAB’s
ksdensity function to transform the acceleration data of the x variable (Accelerometer one run
X) and y variable (Accelerometer Y run X) into probability from 0 to 1. Where X is the run being
tested against and Y is the sensor being tested. Run X is specific to multiple runs of the same
data and is not applicable for testing against another structure where its data over the lifetime
of the structure. Once the probability is generated MATLAB copulafit is used to generate the
required Rho, and nu variables for the copula function. The copula variables are then used in
MATLAB copularnd to generate a random set of variables to a joint distribution of the copula
generated. Once the joint distribution is created MATLAB ksdensity is used to transform the
probability functions of the accelerometer two and accelerometer Y into the scale of data from
the true data. Accelerometer two was chosen as the common input for the copulas since it is
separated from the damaged location.

Implementation Issues

 The following section covers the issues that occurred during the development of this
project. The original intent of using copulas was to utilize the joint distribution benefits of the
copula to detect damage based on all the sensors as one system. Vine copulas are able to utilize
the high dimensionality of the data to create a joint distribution. Vine copulas are much more
difficult to implement than standard two-dimensional copulas. The vine copula approach
planned involved similar methods to the vine copula method in dam engineering (Yu, Zhenyu,
2024) discussed earlier where they developed an early warning system based off the data
collected from multiple locations. Another goal was to utilize the joint distribution data
generated from these higher dimensional constrained vine copulas and perform standard
frequency or time-based data analysis SHM methods. The issue is copulas utilize the CDF of the
data instead of the data prediction with respect to an input like machine learning methods
utilize.

Conclusion

Closing Remarks

 The scope of this project included applying a copula-based analysis method for damage
detection in a SHM or NDE. The approach tested involved using the accelerometer on the base
as an input variable and the undamaged or nominal data from the accelerometer wanting to
evaluate to make a prediction set of data to compare against. Comparing the nominal and the
predicted value in a few model and statistical approaches with varying success. Out of the
tested plot or model-based methods on checking for damage PDF and error in PDF were the
most successful. The PDF values showed a strong link to damage. The error plots were much
easier to examine for damage and even able to determine location of damage based on the

comparison of the plots for each sensor. The CDF, and confidence interval plots were
inconclusive for damage difficult to compare different runs of the same damage state. These

nominal. Lastly, the statistical methods tested included p-value, RMSE, AIC, BIC, and KL
divergence. All of these showed difficulty in determining damage alone between states of the
tested data. Although KL divergence showed an increase in the divergence of the data between
the trained and tested data with some more research KL divergence might be used to determine
damage in a statistical approach. P-value, and RMSE are not desired for a statistical approach of
a copula-based method due to how a copula functions. Copulas in the method used in this
project approximate the output values by using the CDF of the trained data this means that the
data is not predicted based on an input variable how common prediction methods function.

Future Developments

 Since the method tested was useful as a starting point for damage detection, a few more
methods would be useful in further development. Future testing on different copula types and
how well the error can determine the difference in the results. A strong desire of this project
was to use a vine copula with all the sensors to train the data. Testing of this approach on a
simpler structure where damage is increased slowly versus a nonlinear bumper design used in
the three-story structure used would be a good data point to examine for damage. Lastly, future
development on determining a threshold for damage detection that one doesn’t miss damage
and two limits false positives will be needed prior to implementation on a structure with no
undamaged state.
