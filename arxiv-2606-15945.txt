An acylindrically hyperbolic group of non-uniform exponential growth
Roman Sauer, Eduard Schesler

Abstract
. We show the existence of an acylindrically hyperbolic group that has non-uniform exponential growth and Kazhdan’s property ( T ) (T) . The group is constructed as a common quotient of automorphism groups of free groups of increasing finite rank.



1.
Introduction

The growth of a finitely generated group records how fast balls in its Cayley
graph expand. For a finitely generated group
G
with a finite generating set

S
, let
\gamma_{G}^{S}(\ell)
denote the number of elements of
G
that can be
represented by words of length at most
\ell
over
S\cup S^{-1}
.
The limit

\omega(G,S)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sqrt[\ell]{\gamma_{G}^{S}(\ell)}=\inf_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt[\ell]{\gamma_{G}^{S}(\ell)},

which exists due to the submultiplicativity of
\gamma_{G}^{S}
, is called the
exponential growth rate
of
G
with respect to
S
. The group
G
is of
exponential growth
if
\omega(G,S)>1
for
some, and hence for every, finite generating set
S
. Whether
G
has
exponential growth is independent of
S
, but the rate
\omega(G,S)
itself is not.
Indeed, by enlarging
S
one can make
\omega(G,S)
arbitrarily large.
However, it is far more subtle to control how small the rates
\omega(G,S)
can become as the generating set varies.
This motivates the study of the infimum

\omega(G):=\inf_{S}\omega(G,S),

taken over all finite generating sets of
G
.
The group
G
has
uniform exponential growth
if
\omega(G)>1
, and

non-uniform exponential growth
if
G
has exponential growth while

\omega(G)=1
.
The question whether these two notions differ was raised by Gromov
[
Gro81
]
and became highly influential.
For many naturally occurring classes of groups the answer is affirmative.
For non-elementary hyperbolic groups, uniform exponential growth was established by
Koubi
[
Kou98
]
. Fujiwara and Sela
[
FS23
]
recently showed that
the infimum defining
\omega(G)
is in fact attained for hyperbolic groups, and
more precisely that the set of all growth rates of a hyperbolic group is well-ordered.
For the more general class of relatively hyperbolic groups it was shown by Xie
[
Xie07
]
that these are either virtually cyclic or of uniform exponential growth.
For the even more general class of acylindrically hyperbolic groups it remained an open problem whether these can have non-uniform exponential growth, see e.g.
[
ANS24
,
LS26
]
.
A further property that has resisted being combined with non-uniform exponential growth is rigidity in the sense of Kazhdan. In Section 7 of his survey
[
dlH02
]
, de la Harpe asked whether there exists a group of non-uniform exponential growth that has Kazhdan’s property (T).
The main result of this article provides an affirmative answer to these questions.

Theorem A
.

There is an acylindrically hyperbolic Kazhdan group of non-uniform exponential growth.

We will construct our example as a common acylindrically hyperbolic quotient of a sequence of automorphism groups of free groups of increasing finite rank. The deduction of
A
from
B
is quite short and presented in
Section

5
. The following
B
is the core of the paper.

Theorem B
.

There are constants
C>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
and
finite generating sets
T_{n}
of
\Aut(F_{4\cdot 2^{n}})
such that

\gamma^{T_{n}}_{\Aut(F_{4\cdot 2^{n}})}(\ell)\leq\exp\bigl(C\ell^{\beta}\bigr)~~\text{for every $0\leq\ell\leq 2^{n/2}/3$}.

2.
Background on Grigorchuk’s group
\mathcal{G}

In this section
we fix some notation for the first Grigorchuk group, which we denote by
\mathcal{G}
, and collect the results about
\mathcal{G}
that will be used in the remainder of the paper.
We refer to
[
BGv03a
]
for a comprehensive introduction to
\mathcal{G}
and to branch groups in general.
Let
\mathcal{T}
denote the binary rooted tree with vertex set
\{0,1\}^{\ast}
in which two vertices
v,w
are connected by an edge if and only if either
v=wx
or
w=vx
for some
x\in\{0,1\}
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the
n
-th level
of
\mathcal{T}
is the set
X_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{0,1\}^{n}
of vertices at distance
n
from the root.
The group of automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}
will be denoted by
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
.
The root of
\mathcal{T}

is the only vertex of valence
2
in
\mathcal{T}
and is therefore fixed by
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
.
Since moreover every element of
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
preserves the distance between vertices, it follows that
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
preserves each level
X_{n}
.
In particular, for every subgroup
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T})
we obtain a natural homomorphism from
G
to the symmetric group
\Sym(X_{n})
, which we denote by
\pi_{n}
.
We write
\sigma_{g}\in\Sym(\{0,1\})
to denote the image of
g
under
\pi_{1}
.
Conversely, we identify each permutation
\sigma\in\Sym(\{0,1\})
with the automorphism of
\mathcal{T}
given by
xw\mapsto\sigma(x)w
for all
x\in\{0,1\}
and
w\in\{0,1\}^{\ast}
.
The two automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}
that arise in this way are called
rooted
.
Another way to produce automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}
is to act on the two subtrees below the root independently: Each pair
(g_{0},g_{1})\in\Aut(\mathcal{T})\times\Aut(\mathcal{T})
gives rise to the automorphism of
\mathcal{T}
given by
xw\mapsto x\,g_{x}(w)
for all
x\in\{0,1\}
and
w\in\{0,1\}^{\ast}
.
Together with the rooted ones, automorphisms of this form can be used to decompose every automorphism of
\mathcal{T}
as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\Aut(\mathcal{T})
and let
v\in\{0,1\}^{\ast}
be a vertex.
The
state
of
\alpha
at
v
is the unique automorphism
\alpha_{v}\in\Aut(\mathcal{T})
that satisfies

\alpha(vw)=\alpha(v)\,\alpha_{v}(w)

for every
w\in\{0,1\}^{\ast}
.
For a vertex
x\in\{0,1\}
of the first level, this reads
\alpha(xw)=\sigma_{\alpha}(x)\,\alpha_{x}(w)
.
The resulting decomposition
\alpha=\sigma_{\alpha}\circ(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1})
is called the
wreath decomposition
of
\alpha
.

The wreath decomposition endows us with an isomorphism

\Aut(\mathcal{T})\longrightarrow\Sym(\{0,1\})\ltimes\bigl(\Aut(\mathcal{T})\times\Aut(\mathcal{T})\bigr),\ \alpha\mapsto\sigma_{\alpha}\cdot(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}),

which we will use to identify an element
\alpha\in\Aut(\mathcal{T})
with its wreath decomposition
\sigma_{\alpha}\cdot(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1})
.

Definition 2.2
.

A subgroup
G\leq\Aut(\mathcal{T})
is called
self-similar
if for every
g\in G
and every vertex
v\in\{0,1\}^{\ast}
the state
g_{v}
is contained in
G
.

A prominent example of a self-similar group is the first Grigorchuk group
\mathcal{G}
, see e.g.
[
BGv03b
]
.
It is the subgroup of
\Aut(\mathcal{T})
generated by the four automorphisms
a,b,c,d
defined as follows.
The generator
a
is the rooted automorphism corresponding to the non-trivial permutation in
\Sym(\{0,1\})
, while
b,c,d
fix the first level and are given by the wreath decompositions

b=(a,c),\qquad c=(a,d),\qquad d=(1,b).

A direct verification shows that each of the generators in
S\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{a,b,c,d\}
is an involution, so that
\mathcal{G}
is generated by
S
as a monoid.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we write

a_{n},b_{n},c_{n},d_{n}\in\Sym(X_{n})

to denote the images of
a,b,c,d
under
\pi_{n}
and we set

\mathcal{G}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\langle a_{n},b_{n},c_{n},d_{n}\rangle\leq\Sym(X_{n}).

The action of
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathcal{T}
is
spherically transitive
, that is, the induced action of
\mathcal{G}
on
X_{n}
is transitive for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
; see e.g.
[
BGv03a
, Section 1]
.
In other words, the (labeled) Schreier graph of the action of
\mathcal{G}
on each level
X_{n}
is connected.
Recall that, given a group
H
acting on a set
\Omega
and a generating set
S
of
H
, the associated
labeled Schreier graph
is the graph with vertex set
\Omega
in which, for every
\omega\in\Omega
and every
s\in S
, there is an edge labeled
s
from
\omega
to
\omega s
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we write
\Gamma_{n}
for the labeled Schreier graph of the action of
\mathcal{G}
on
X_{n}
with respect to
S
.
For the rest of the paper we fix the two vertices

\rho_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=1^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=1^{n-1}0

of
\Gamma_{n}
.
As the action of
\mathcal{G}
on the tree
\mathcal{T}
extends continuously to its boundary
\partial\mathcal{T}=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}=\mathfrak{C}
, we may also consider the labeled Schreier graph
\Gamma_{\infty}
of the orbit of the boundary point
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=1^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{C}
, with respect to
S
.
We now relate the Schreier graphs
\Gamma_{n}
and
\Gamma_{\infty}
.
The key ingredient in doing so is the
contracting property
of
\mathcal{G}
; see e.g.
[
BGv03a
, Corollary 2.5]
.
To formulate it, we write
|g|_{S}
for the word length of an element
g\in\mathcal{G}
with respect to
S
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
g\in\mathcal{G}
and every integer

n\geq\lceil\log_{2}|g|_{S}\rceil+1,

all states
g_{v}
with
v\in X_{n}
lie in the set
\mathcal{N}=\{1,a,b,c,d\}
.

The set
\mathcal{N}
in
Section

2
is called the
nucleus
of
\mathcal{G}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{G}
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a number that satisfies
n\geq\lceil\log_{2}|g|_{S}\rceil+2
.
Then
g
fixes the vertex
\rho_{n}
if and only if
g
fixes the boundary point
\rho
.

Proof.

If
g
fixes
\rho=1^{\infty}
, then it fixes each of its prefixes, and in particular
\rho_{n}=1^{n}
.
Suppose now that
g(\rho_{n})=\rho_{n}
for some
n\geq\lceil\log_{2}|g|_{S}\rceil+2
.
By
Section

2
, the state
g_{1^{m}}
lies in the nucleus
\mathcal{N}=\{1,a,b,c,d\}
for
m=\lceil\log_{2}|g|_{S}\rceil+1
.
Since
g
fixes
1^{n}
and
m<n
, it follows that
g_{1^{m}}
fixes
1^{n-m}
.
As
n-m\geq 1
, we have
g_{1^{m}}\neq a
.
Hence
g_{1^{m}}
lies in the set
\{1,b,c,d\}
all of whose elements fix the ray
\rho=1^{\infty}
.
This gives us

g(\rho)=g(1^{m}\rho)=1^{m}\,g_{1^{m}}(\rho)=1^{m}\rho=\rho,

which completes the proof.
∎

Using
Section

2
, we will deduce that the balls of a certain radius
r_{n}
around the points
\rho_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
in
\Gamma_{n}
coincide with the ball around
\rho
in
\Gamma_{\infty}
of radius
r_{n}
, where
r_{n}
goes to infinity when
n
goes to infinity.
For a labeled graph
G
, a vertex
p
of
G
, and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we will write
G(p,r)
for the labeled ball of radius
r
around
p
in
G
.
To prove the claim, we first record a general criterion under which two such balls are isomorphic.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
H
be a group with generating set
S
that acts on two sets
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
.
Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be the associated Schreier graphs in which we consider two points
\omega\in\Omega
and
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
.
If

\St_{H}(\omega)\cap B_{S}(2r)=\St_{H}(\omega^{\prime})\cap B_{S}(2r),

then the map

\varphi\colon G(\omega,r)\rightarrow G^{\prime}(\omega^{\prime},r),\ \omega h\mapsto\omega^{\prime}h

is a well-defined isomorphism of labeled graphs.

Proof.

For any
g,g^{\prime}\in H
with
|g|_{S},|g^{\prime}|_{S}\leq r
, the element
g^{\prime}g^{-1}
lies in
B_{S}(2r)
.
Our hypothesis therefore gives us

\omega g=\omega g^{\prime}\iff g^{\prime}g^{-1}\in\St_{H}(\omega)\iff g^{\prime}g^{-1}\in\St_{H}(\omega^{\prime})\iff\omega^{\prime}g=\omega^{\prime}g^{\prime}.

In particular we see that
\varphi
is well-defined and injective.
Since every vertex of
G^{\prime}(\omega^{\prime},r)
is of the form
\omega^{\prime}g
with
|g|_{S}\leq r
it follows that
\varphi
is also surjective.
Moreover
\varphi
sends an edge
(\omega g,\omega gs)
of
G(\omega,r)
to the edge
(\omega^{\prime}g,\omega^{\prime}gs)
of
G^{\prime}(\omega^{\prime},r)
, which shows that
\varphi
preserves the labels of the edges.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For every sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
the map

\Gamma_{n}(\rho_{n},2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor})\rightarrow\Gamma_{\infty}(\rho,2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}),\ \rho_{n}h\mapsto\rho h

is an isomorphism of labeled graphs.

Proof.

Let
r_{n}=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
.
For
n\geq 6
we have

\log_{2}(2r_{n})+2=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+3\leq n.

Let
g\in\mathcal{G}
be an element with
|g|_{S}\leq 2r_{n}
.
By
Section

2
, it suffices to show that
g
fixes
\rho_{n}
if and only if it fixes
\rho
.
Since

n\geq\log_{2}(2r_{n})+2\geq\lceil\log_{2}|g|_{S}\rceil+2,

this is precisely the statement of
Section

2
applied to
\rho_{n}
.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

For every sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
the map

\Gamma_{n}(\eta_{n},2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor})\rightarrow\Gamma_{\infty}(\rho,2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}),\ \eta_{n}h\mapsto\rho h

is an isomorphism of labeled graphs.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{G}
acts on
\mathcal{T}
by automorphisms, it directly follows that
\St_{\mathcal{G}}(\rho_{n})=\St_{\mathcal{G}}(\eta_{n})
.
In particular we have

\St_{\mathcal{G}}(\eta_{n})\cap B_{S}(2r_{n})=\St_{\mathcal{G}}(\rho_{n})\cap B_{S}(2r_{n})

for
r_{n}=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
, so
Section

2
yields the isomorphism

\Gamma_{n}(\eta_{n},r_{n})\to\Gamma_{n}(\rho_{n},r_{n}),\ \eta_{n}h\mapsto\rho_{n}h.

Composing it with the isomorphism
\Gamma_{n}(\rho_{n},r_{n})\to\Gamma_{\infty}(\rho,r_{n})
of
Section

2
gives the claimed isomorphism.
∎

Next we show that the two vertices
\rho_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
drift apart as
n
tends to infinity.
In fact their distance grows exponentially.

Lemma 2.8
.

The distance between
\rho_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
in
\Gamma_{n}
satisfies

d_{\Gamma_{n}}(\rho_{n},\eta_{n})\geq 2^{\,n-3}

for every
n\geq 4
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 4
.
Since
\Gamma_{n}
is connected there exists an element
g\in\mathcal{G}
of word length
|g|_{S}=d_{\Gamma_{n}}(\rho_{n},\eta_{n})
that satisfies
\rho_{n}g=\eta_{n}
.
Since
1^{n}
and
1^{n-1}0
share the prefix
1^{n-1}
, the element
g
fixes the vertex
1^{n-1}
, and its state
g_{1^{n-1}}
switches
0
and
1
.
Let
m\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lceil\log_{2}|g|_{S}\rceil+1
.
By
Section

2
, the state
g_{1^{m}}
lies in the nucleus
\mathcal{N}=\{1,a,b,c,d\}
.
Suppose that
m\leq n-2
.
Since
g
fixes
1^{n-1}
, the state
g_{1^{m}}
fixes
1^{n-1-m}
.
Using
n-1-m\geq 1
and the fact that
a
does not fix the first level, we obtain
g_{1^{m}}\in\{1,b,c,d\}
.
Since each element of
\{1,b,c,d\}
fixes
\rho=1^{\infty}
it follows that
g_{1^{m}}
fixes
1^{n-m}
, which is a contradiction to
g_{1^{n-1}}(1)=0
.
Thus we have
m\geq n-1
, which gives us

\log_{2}|g|_{S}+1\geq\lceil\log_{2}|g|_{S}\rceil\geq n-2.

Hence
\log_{2}|g|_{S}\geq n-3
, from which we deduce that
d_{\Gamma_{n}}(\rho_{n},\eta_{n})=|g|_{S}\geq 2^{n-3}
.
∎

We now turn to growth results concerning
\mathcal{G}
and its Schreier graphs
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{n}
.
Let us start by recalling the following seminal result of Grigorchuk
[
Gri84
]
.

Theorem 2.9

(Grigorchuk)
.

The group
\mathcal{G}
is of subexponential growth.
More precisely, there exist constants
C>0
and
\frac{1}{2}<\gamma<1
such that

\gamma_{\mathcal{G}}^{S}(n)\leq\exp(Cn^{\gamma})

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

While
Theorem

2.9
concerns the growth of balls in the Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathcal{G},S)
, the quantity we are interested in for the Schreier graphs
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{n}
is the inverted orbit growth – a notion systematically studied by Bartholdi and Erschler
[
BE12
]
.
Following Bartholdi and Erschler, we will from now on work with right actions as this is convenient for the study of inverted orbits.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
H
be a group acting from the right on a set
\Omega
, let
A
be a generating set of
H
, and let
\omega\in\Omega
.
The
inverted orbit
of a word
w=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{\ell}\in A^{\ast}
at
\omega
is the set

\mathcal{O}_{\omega}(w)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{\omega,\ \omega s_{\ell},\ \omega s_{\ell-1}s_{\ell},\ \ldots,\ \omega s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{\ell}\}\subseteq\Omega.

The cardinality of
\mathcal{O}_{\omega}(w)
will be denoted by
\delta_{\omega}(w)
.
We further define the
inverted orbit growth function

\Delta_{\omega}^{A}(n)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max\{\,\delta_{\omega}(w)\mid w\in A^{\ast},\ |w|\leq n\,\},

and we write

N_{\omega}^{A}(n)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigl|\{\,\mathcal{O}_{\omega}(w)\mid w\in A^{\ast},\ |w|\leq n\,\}\bigr|

for the number of distinct inverted orbits arising from words of length at most
n
.

Bartholdi and Erschler studied the functions
\Delta_{\rho}^{S}
and
N_{\rho}^{S}
for the action of
\mathcal{G}
on the
\mathcal{G}
-orbit of
\rho
.
In
[
BE12
, Proposition 4.4 and Lemma 4.9]
they obtained the following upper bounds for these functions.

Theorem 2.11

(Bartholdi-Erschler)
.

There exist constants
C>0
and
0<\alpha<1
such that

\Delta_{\rho}^{S}(n)\leq Cn^{\alpha}\qquad\text{and}\qquad N_{\rho}^{S}(n)\leq\exp(Cn^{\alpha})

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\Delta_{\rho}^{S}
and
N_{\rho}^{S}
refer to the action of
\mathcal{G}
on the
\mathcal{G}
-orbit of
\rho
.

Using our preceding lemmas, we transfer the estimates from
Theorem

2.11
to the action of
\mathcal{G}
on
\Gamma_{n}
and the point
\rho_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
C
and
\alpha
be as in
Theorem

2.11
and let
r_{n}=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
.
For every sufficiently large
n
, every
\xi_{n}\in\{\rho_{n},\eta_{n}\}
, and every
k\leq r_{n}
, we have

\Delta_{\xi_{n}}^{S_{n}}(k)\leq Ck^{\alpha}\qquad\text{and}\qquad N_{\xi_{n}}^{S_{n}}(k)\leq\exp(Ck^{\alpha}),

where
\Delta_{\xi_{n}}^{S_{n}}
and
N_{\xi_{n}}^{S_{n}}
refer to the action of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
on
X_{n}
with respect to
S_{n}
.

Proof.

We fix
\xi_{n}\in\{\rho_{n},\eta_{n}\}
and
k\leq r_{n}
.
By
Section

2
and
Section

2
, the map

\varphi\colon\Gamma_{n}(\xi_{n},r_{n})\rightarrow\Gamma_{\infty}(\rho,r_{n}),\ \xi_{n}h\mapsto\rho h

is a well-defined isomorphism of labeled graphs.
Consider a word
w=s_{1}\ldots s_{\ell}\in S_{n}^{\ast}
with
|w|=\ell\leq k\leq r_{n}
.
Every element of the inverted orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{n}}(w)
is of the form
\xi_{n}s_{i}s_{i+1}\ldots s_{\ell}
, where
s_{i}\ldots s_{\ell}
is a word of length at most
\ell\leq r_{n}
, and hence lies in the ball
\Gamma_{n}(\xi_{n},r_{n})
.
The same holds for
\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(w)\subseteq\Gamma_{\infty}(\rho,r_{n})
.
Under the isomorphism
\varphi
, the vertex
\xi_{n}s_{i}\ldots s_{\ell}
corresponds to
\rho s_{i}\ldots s_{\ell}
, so the isomorphism restricts to a bijection
\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{n}}(w)\to\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(w)
.
In particular
\delta_{\xi_{n}}(w)=\delta_{\rho}(w)
, and distinct inverted orbits at
\xi_{n}
correspond to distinct inverted orbits at
\rho
, which completes the proof.
∎

3.
The generating sets in automorphism groups of free groups

Let
Y
be a finite set, and let
F(Y)
be the free group over
Y
. A
signed basis permutation
of
F(Y)
is an automorphism of the form

u_{y}\mapsto u_{\sigma(y)}^{\varepsilon_{y}},\qquad\sigma\in\Sym(Y),\quad\varepsilon_{y}\in\{\pm 1\}.

The subgroup of
F(Y)
generated by all signed basis permutations is called the
monomial subgroup

M(Y)
. We have

M(Y)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{Y}\rtimes\Sym(Y).

For distinct
y,z\in Y
, let
\lambda_{y,z}\in\Aut(F(Y))
be the
Nielsen transvection

u_{y}\mapsto u_{y}u_{z},\qquad u_{y^{\prime}}\mapsto u_{y^{\prime}}\quad\text{for }y^{\prime}\neq y.

The following fact is well known
[
LS01
, Chapter I, Section 4]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a finite set
Y
, the group
\Aut(F(Y))
is generated by all signed basis permutations and all Nielsen transvections.

We set

Y_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=X_{n}\times\{1,2,3,4\},

and let
F(Y_{n})
be the free group with basis
\{u_{y}\mid y\in Y_{n}\}
.
Thus
F(Y_{n})\cong F_{4\cdot 2^{n}}
.
Next we define a finite subset
T_{n}\subset\Aut(F(Y_{n}))

of automorphisms of
F(Y_{n})
, which is partitioned into four types.

•

Type 1: The truncated Grigorchuk generators.

For every generator
s_{n}\in S_{n}
of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
, we denote – by the same symbol – the automorphism of
F(Y_{n})
given by

u_{(x,i)}\mapsto u_{(xs_{n},i)}

for every
(x,i)\in Y_{n}
.

•

Type 2: The monomial group over
\eta_{n}
.

Let
M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})\leq\Aut(F(Y_{n}))
be the group of all signed permutations of the four basis elements

u_{(\eta_{n},1)},\ldots,u_{(\eta_{n},4)}

and fixing all other basis elements. We call
M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})
the

\eta_{n}
, and we have
M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{4}\rtimes\Sym(4)
.

•

Type 3: The linking transposition.

Let
\tau_{n}
be the basis transposition swapping

u_{(\rho_{n},1)}
and
u_{(\theta_{n},1)}

and fixing all other basis elements.

•

Type 4: The Nielsen transvection.

Let
\nu_{n}
be the Nielsen transvection

\nu_{n}=\lambda_{(\rho_{n},2),(\rho_{n},3)}.

Definition 3.2
.

We define
T_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=S_{n}\cup M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})\cup\{\tau_{n},\nu_{n}\}\subseteq\Aut(F(Y_{n}))
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The subgroup
\langle T_{n}\rangle
contains the monomial subgroup
M(Y_{n})
.

Proof.

By spherical transitivity of
\mathcal{G}
, the group
\mathcal{G}_{n}
acts transitively on
X_{n}
. Hence the fibrewise monomial subgroup
M_{x}(Y_{n})
appears as the conjugate of
M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})
by some word in
S_{n}
.
In particular,
\langle T_{n}\rangle
contains every signed basis transposition inside each fibre.
The graph
\Lambda_{n}
on the vertex set
X_{n}
whose edges are

\{\rho_{n}g,\theta_{n}g\},~~g\in\mathcal{G}_{n}

is connected, which is shown in the proof of Theorem 5.4 in
[
SS26
]
.

The conjugates of
\tau_{n}
by elements of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
are precisely the transpositions of the basis elements

u_{(\rho_{n}g,1)}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{(\theta_{n}g,1)}

for the edges of
\Lambda_{n}
. Together with the transpositions inside the fibres, these are the edge transpositions of a connected graph on the vertex set
Y_{n}
. Therefore they generate
\Sym(Y_{n})
. Since inversions of basis elements are contained in the conjugates of
M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})
, the statement follows.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

The subset
T_{n}
generates
\Aut(F(Y_{n}))
.

Proof.

By
Section

3
, the group
\langle T_{n}\rangle
contains all signed permutations of the basis. It also contains the Nielsen transvection
\nu_{n}
. Conjugating
\nu_{n}
by the signed basis permutations on

, we obtain every Nielsen transvection.
Hence
\langle T_{n}\rangle=\Aut(F(Y_{n}))
by
Theorem

.
∎

4.
Proof of
B

Throughout this section we write

r_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 5
, and let
g,h\in S_{n}^{\ast}
have length at most
r_{n}/3
. Then every element of
M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})^{h}
commutes with the
g
-conjugates
\tau_{n}^{g}
and
\nu_{n}^{g}
and
(\nu_{n}^{g})^{-1}
.

Proof.

The conjugate of
M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})
by
h
is supported on the basis elements over the fibre
\eta_{n}h
. The conjugate of
\nu_{n}^{\pm 1}
by
g
is supported on the basis elements
(\rho_{n}g,2)
and
(\rho_{n}g,3)
. The conjugate of
\tau_{n}
by
g
is supported on
(\rho_{n}g,1)
and
(\theta_{n}g,1)
.

Since
n\geq 5
, we have

1+2r_{n}/3<2^{n-3}.

Thus it is enough to prove that
\eta_{n}h
is distinct from both
\rho_{n}g
and
\theta_{n}g
. If
\eta_{n}h=\rho_{n}g
, then

d_{\Gamma_{n}}(\rho_{n},\eta_{n})\leq|g|+|h|\leq 2r_{n}/3,

contradicting
Section

2
. If
\eta_{n}h=\theta_{n}g
, then

d_{\Gamma_{n}}(\rho_{n},\eta_{n})\leq 1+|g|+|h|\leq 1+2r_{n}/3,

again contradicting
Section

2
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
n\geq 5
, and let
w
be a word over
T_{n}\cup T_{n}^{-1}
of length
\ell\leq r_{n}/3
. Then the element represented by
w
can be written as

p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4},

where
p_{1}
is represented by a word of the form
g_{1}\cdots g_{\ell}
over
S_{n}\cup\{1\}
, and the remaining factors have the form

\displaystyle p_{2}

\displaystyle=(a_{1}^{(2)})^{g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{\ell}}(a_{2}^{(2)})^{g_{2}\cdots g_{\ell}}\cdots(a_{\ell}^{(2)})^{g_{\ell}},

\displaystyle p_{3}

\displaystyle=(a_{1}^{(3)})^{g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{\ell}}(a_{2}^{(3)})^{g_{2}\cdots g_{\ell}}\cdots(a_{\ell}^{(3)})^{g_{\ell}},

\displaystyle p_{4}

\displaystyle=(a_{1}^{(4)})^{g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{\ell}}(a_{2}^{(4)})^{g_{2}\cdots g_{\ell}}\cdots(a_{\ell}^{(4)})^{g_{\ell}}.

Here, for each
i
, we have

g_{i}\in S_{n}\cup\{1\},\qquad a_{i}^{(2)}\in M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n}),\qquad a_{i}^{(3)}\in\{\tau_{n},1\},\qquad a_{i}^{(4)}\in\{\nu_{n},\nu_{n}^{-1},1\}.

Proof.

Write the word as a product of letters

t_{1}\cdots t_{\ell}

and write each letter in the form

t_{i}=a_{i}^{(2)}a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i}^{(4)}g_{i},

where
g_{i}\in S_{n}\cup\{1\}
,
a_{i}^{(2)}\in M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})
,
a_{i}^{(3)}\in\{\tau_{n},1\}
, and
a_{i}^{(4)}\in\{\nu_{n},\nu_{n}^{-1},1\}
, with at most one of
a_{i}^{(2)},a_{i}^{(3)},a_{i}^{(4)}
non-trivial. This is possible because
M_{\eta_{n}}(Y_{n})
is a group, the elements of
S_{n}
and
\tau_{n}
are involutions, and
T_{n}\cup T_{n}^{-1}
only adds
\nu_{n}^{-1}
.

Pushing all
g_{i}
’s to the left gives

t_{1}\cdots t_{\ell}=g_{1}\cdots g_{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\bigl(a_{i}^{(2)}a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i}^{(4)}\bigr)^{g_{i}\cdots g_{\ell}},

where
x^{g}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=g^{-1}xg
. For
j\in\{2,3,4\}
, call the elements

(a_{i}^{(j)})^{g_{i}\cdots g_{\ell}}\qquad(1\leq i\leq\ell)

the conjugated factors of type
j
. Thus “type” refers to these conjugates appearing after the rewriting, not to different generators of type
3
or type
4
.

For each
i
, the suffix
g_{i}\cdots g_{\ell}
has
S_{n}
-length at most
\ell\leq r_{n}/3
. Hence
Section

4
implies that every conjugated factor of type
2
commutes with every conjugated factor of type
3
and with every conjugated factor of type
4
. A conjugated factor of type
3
is supported on sheet
1
, whereas a conjugated factor of type
4
is supported on sheets
2
and
3
; hence these two factors commute as well. We may therefore move all conjugated factors of type
2
before all conjugated factors of type
3
, and all conjugated factors of type
3
before all conjugated factors of type
4
, preserving the order inside each type. This gives exactly the three displayed products
p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}
, and hence the claimed normal form.
∎

Proof of
B
.

Let
P_{i}(\ell)
be the set of all possible factors
p_{i}
appearing in the normal form of
Section

4
for words of length at most
\ell\leq r_{n}/3
.

The first factor satisfies

|P_{1}(\ell)|\leq\exp(C_{1}\ell^{\gamma})

by
Theorem

2.9
, after passing to the finite quotient
\mathcal{G}_{n}
.

Let
C_{0}>0
be a constant such that the estimates from
Section

2
hold with
C_{0}
for both
\rho_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
.

Consider the factor
p_{2}
of a word of length at most
\ell
. With the notation of
Section

4
, put
u=g_{1}\cdots g_{\ell}
. Each conjugate

(a_{i}^{(2)})^{g_{i}\cdots g_{\ell}}

is supported on the fibre over the point
\eta_{n}g_{i}\cdots g_{\ell}
. Hence
p_{2}
is supported over the inverted orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{n}}(u)
. The number of possible inverted orbits
\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{n}}(u)
, with
u
of length at most
\ell
, is at most
\exp(C_{0}\ell^{\alpha})
. For a fixed such orbit,
Section

2
gives
|\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{n}}(u)|\leq C_{0}\ell^{\alpha}
, and at each point of the orbit the value of

lies in a copy of the finite group

, of order

. Therefore

|P_{2}(\ell)|\leq\exp(C_{0}\ell^{\alpha})\cdot(2^{4}\cdot 4!)^{C_{0}\ell^{\alpha}}\leq\exp(C_{2}\ell^{\alpha})

for a suitable constant

.

The factor
p_{3}
of a word of length
\ell
is a permutation of the sheet-one basis elements. Each non-trivial conjugate

(a_{i}^{(3)})^{g_{i}\cdots g_{\ell}}

is the transposition of
u_{(\rho_{n}g_{i}\cdots g_{\ell},1)}
and
u_{(\theta_{n}g_{i}\cdots g_{\ell},1)}
. Thus the support of

is contained in

We now dominate this set by one inverted orbit based at
\rho_{n}
. Let

of length at most

. Since

and

starting at

, while the suffixes starting at the

immediately before

give the points

There are at most

5.
Proof of
A

The following statement is a direct consequence of the fact that the automorphism group of a free group of rank at least 2 is acylindrically hyperbolic, due to Genevois–Horbez
[
GH20
, Corollary 1.2]
, and the common quotient theorem of Minasyan–Osin
[
MO18
, Theorem 1.1]
. The latter says that every countable family of countable acylindrically hyperbolic groups has a common finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic quotient.

Theorem 5.1
.

The automorphism groups of free groups
\Aut(F_{n})
,
n\geq 2
, have a common finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic quotient.

Proof of
A
.

By
Theorem

5.1
there is a finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group
Q
and epimorphisms

q_{n}\colon\Aut(F_{4\cdot 2^{n}})\twoheadrightarrow Q

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The group
Q
is a Kazhdan group since it is a quotient of Kazhdan groups by the result of Kaluba-Kielak-Nowak
[
KKN21
, Theorem 1.1]
or the earlier result of Kaluba–Nowak–Ozawa
[
KNO18
]
. See also
Section

5
below.
Let
U_{n}=q_{n}(T_{n})
be the image of the generating set
T_{n}
from
B
. Taking quotients cannot increase growth. Thus, by
B
, there are constants
C>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
such that

\gamma_{Q}^{U_{n}}(\ell)\leq\gamma_{\Aut(F_{4\cdot 2^{n}})}^{U_{n}}(\ell)\leq\exp\bigl(C\ell^{\beta}\bigr)\qquad\text{for every }0\leq\ell\leq 2^{n/2}/3.

Set
\ell_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lfloor 2^{n/2}\rfloor/3
. Then

\displaystyle 1\leq\omega(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\omega(Q,U_{n})

\displaystyle=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{\ell\geq 1}\gamma_{Q}^{U_{n}}(\ell)^{1/\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{1\leq\ell\leq 2^{n/2}}\gamma_{Q}^{U_{n}}(\ell)^{1/\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{1\leq\ell\leq 2^{n/2}}\exp\bigl(C\ell^{\beta-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\exp\bigl(C\ell_{n}^{\beta-1}\bigr)=1.

Thus
\omega(Q)=1
.
On the other hand, it is well known that
Q
has exponential growth since an acylindrically hyperbolic group contains
a non-abelian free group (coming from independent loxodromic elements)
[
Osi16
]
.
So
Q
has non-uniform exponential growth.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

To obtain the Kazhdan property for
Q
one does not have to appeal to results about the Kazhdan property for automorphism groups of free groups. One could simply take the common quotient of
Q
as above and a Kazhdan hyperbolic group.
