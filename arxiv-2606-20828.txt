Gauge invariant generalizations of the Proca equation and the Yang-Mills-Proca equation


Abstract
Аннотация The Proca equations (1936) are used in quantum field theory to describe vector bosons (spin 1) with a nonzero mass. The Proca equations are not gauge invariant. In contrast to Stueckelberg’s approach (1938), this article presents a gauge invariant generalization of the Proca equation by introducing an additional vector field into the Proca equation. The results are extended to the Yang-Mills-Proca equations, leading to equations with non-Abelian gauge symmetry.

In 1938, Stueckelberg

,
4
,
5
]
considered a gauge-invariant modification of the Proca equation obtained by introducing an additional scalar field into the Proca equation.

Later, a number of modifications and generalizations of the Proca equation were proposed (see, for example, reviews

This article considers a gauge-invariant generalization of the Proca equation, obtained by introducing an additional vector field into the Proca equation. The results are extended to the Yang-Mills-Proca equations and lead to equations with non-Abelian gauge symmetry.

All physical constants (speed of light, Planck’s constant, positron charge, etc.), except for particle masses, are taken to be equal to one.

Real tensor fields in Minkowski space are considered. Let

The Proca equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}a_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu},\quad\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}f^{\mu\nu}+m^{2}a^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\quad\nu=0,1,2,3,

A gauge invariant generalized Proca equation

The equations (
13
),(
14
) do not have gauge symmetry (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), but they have the following gauge symmetry:

\displaystyle a_{\mu}\to\acute{a}_{\mu}=a_{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}\lambda,

(17)

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu}\to\acute{f}_{\mu\nu}=f_{\mu\nu},

(18)

\displaystyle j^{\nu}\to\acute{j}^{\nu}=j^{\nu}-m^{2}\partial^{\nu}\lambda.

(19)

If we introduce the new notation

k^{\nu}:=\frac{1}{m^{2}}j^{\nu},

then, by (
19
), the vector
k^{\nu}
transforms according to the rule

k^{\nu}\to\acute{k}^{\nu}=k^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}\lambda.

In geometry, such a transformation is usually associated with a connection, and in field theory, with a vector potential.
Where might the vector potential
k^{\nu}
come from?

Our proposal is to take the vector
k^{\nu}
to satisfy the homogeneous Maxwell equations

\partial_{\mu}k_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}k_{\mu}=g_{\mu\nu},\quad\partial_{\mu}g^{\mu\nu}=0.

So, we arrive at the system of equations

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}a_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu},

(20)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}f^{\mu\nu}+m^{2}a^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}k^{\nu},

(21)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}k_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}k_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu},

(22)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}g^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(23)

which is invariant with respect to the gauge transformation

\displaystyle a_{\mu}\to a_{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}\lambda,\quad k^{\nu}\to k^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}\lambda.

(24)

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu}\to f_{\mu\nu},\quad g_{\mu\nu}\to g_{\mu\nu},

(25)

where
\lambda:{\mathbb{R}}^{1,3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. From the equations (
20
), (
21
) for
m\neq 0
we obtain the following consequence

\partial_{\nu}a^{\nu}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}.

Therefore, the Lorentz gauge
\partial_{\nu}a^{\nu}=0
for the vector potential
a^{\nu}
implies the Lorentz gauge
\partial_{\nu}k^{\nu}=0
for the vector potential
k^{\nu}
. And, in the case of the Lorentz gauge, we have

(\square+m^{2})a^{\nu}=m^{2}k^{\nu},\quad\square k^{\nu}=0.

(26)

For equations (
26
) we distinguish two classes of solutions. First,

(\square+m^{2})a^{\nu}=0,\quad k^{\nu}=0.

(27)

Secondly,

\square a^{\nu}=0,\quad\square k^{\nu}=0,\quad a^{\nu}=k^{\nu}.

(28)

We are interested in the solutions (
27
) since they depend on the mass
m
and are solutions of the Proca equation
(
10
), (
11
).

Conclusion.
The gauge-invariant system of equations (
20
), (
21
), (
22
), (
23
) contains among its solutions
all
solutions of the Proca equation (
10
), (
11
). And, therefore, can be considered as a gauge-invariant
generalization of the Proca equation
.

Generalization of Proca’s equation to the case of several masses

Let us consider
n\geq 2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu}(k),

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}f^{\mu\nu}(k)+m_{k}{}^{2}a^{\nu}(k)

\displaystyle m_{k}{}^{2}a^{\nu}(k+1),\quad k=1,\ldots,n-1

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu}(n),\quad\partial_{\mu}f^{\mu\nu}(n)=j^{\nu}

\displaystyle(\square+m_{k}{}^{2})a^{\nu}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{k}{}^{2}a^{\nu}(k+1),\quad k=1,\ldots,n-1,

\displaystyle\square a^{\nu}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j^{\nu}

To take the next step in generalizing the equations (

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We will write its elements as

\hat{e}_{1},\ldots,\hat{e}_{n}

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\hat{e}_{k}

n

We will use tensors (tensor fields) of the Minkowski space
{\mathbb{R}}^{1,3}

) with values in the Euclidean space

\hat{a}_{\mu}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{\mu}(1)\\

\vdots\\
