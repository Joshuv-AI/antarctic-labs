A Theoretical Interpretation of In-Context Learning via Probabilistic Modeling
Zhenyu Liu, Huaze Tang, Shao-Lun Huang

Abstract
ICL ( ICL ) is an emerging paradigm that employs the semantic information inherent in LLM for generating answers to user queries. While the remarkable performance of ICL has been widely known, a general modeling and a rigorous theoretical analysis of this paradigm are still lacking. This work presents a probabilistic model for ICL and derives the performance of ICL for both general parametric distributions and exponential families. Based on the derived results, the work explains the impact of multiple factors such as the number of demonstrations, the sensitivity of the probabilistic model to the variation of its parameters, as well as the similarity between the demonstrations and the query on the performance of ICL .

The overall procedure of

The following probabilistic model is adopted for

ICL
. Both the input text
X
and the answer
Y
are modeled as random variables. As a result,

and
y_{i}
are realizations of random variables
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
, respectively. Given an input text

, the answer
Y
follows a parametric probabilistic model
P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}})}
, where
\underline{{\alpha}}
is a parameter consisting of
K
entries and belongs to a set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{K}
. Conditioned on the
i
-th input text
X_{i}
, the answer
Y_{i}
is independent of
\{X_{j}\}_{j\neq i}
and follows the conditional distribution
P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}})}
. Under such conditional independence, the distribution of
Y^{n}\vcentcolon=(Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots Y_{n})
conditioned on
X^{n}\vcentcolon=(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots X_{n})
factorizes as

\displaystyle P_{Y^{n}|X^{n}}^{(\underline{{\alpha}})}\bigl(y^{n}\bigr|x^{n}\bigr)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}})}(y_{i}|x_{i})

where
y^{n}\vcentcolon=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})
and
x^{n}\vcentcolon=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
.
Using the demonstrations
(x^{n},y^{n})
, the
LLM
computes an estimator of
\underline{{\alpha}}
. In particular, we consider the
MLE
(
MLE
)
\underline{{\alpha}}^{*}
given by

\displaystyle\underline{{\alpha}}^{*}\vcentcolon=\argmax_{{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}}\,\log P_{Y^{n}|X^{n}}^{({\alpha})}\bigl(y^{n}\bigr|x^{n}\bigr).

Then, the
LLM
generates the answer

by sampling from the inferred conditional distribution
P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}}^{*})}(\cdot|x_{\mathrm{Q}})
. Note that the conditional distribution
P_{Y|X}^{({\alpha})}
for any
{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}
is determined via pre-training and is not affected by the demonstrations. Indeed, the demonstrations only affects the estimate of the parameter.

The quality of the answer to the query is affected by the discrepancy between the inferred conditional distribution
P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}}^{*})}(\cdot|x_{\mathrm{Q}})
and the ground-truth
P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}})}(\cdot|x_{\mathrm{Q}})
. Ideally, these two distributions are equivalent so that the answer to the query is sampled from the ground-truth conditional distribution. In practice, such equivalence may not be achieved and we employ the

\displaystyle D_{\KL}\bigl(P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}})}\big\|P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}}^{*})}\bigr)\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}})}}\biggl[\log\frac{P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}})}(Y|x_{\mathrm{Q}})}{P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}}^{*})}(Y|x_{\mathrm{Q}})}\,\biggr|\,X=x_{\mathrm{Q}}\biggr].

This quantity is a random variable as
\underline{{\alpha}}^{*}
is a function of
Y^{n}
. Define the
EER

\ell(x_{\mathrm{Q}};x^{n})
as the conditional expectation of the

\displaystyle\ell(x_{\mathrm{Q}};x^{n})\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}_{Y^{n}|X^{n}}\bigl[D_{\KL}\bigl(P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}})}\bigr\|P_{Y|X}^{(\underline{{\alpha}}^{*})}\bigr)\bigr|X^{n}=x^{n}\bigr].

(1)

This paper investigates properties of the

V
conclusion

In this work, we propose a probabilistic model for
ICL
and analyze its
EER
. For general parametric distributions, we show that the asymptotic
EER
is the ratio between the
EER
coefficient and the number of demonstrations. For exponential families, the
EER
coefficient is a function of the
FIM
at the quest and of the
FIM
averaged over all the demonstrations. Moreover, a non-asymptotic upper bound on the
EER
is derived for exponential families. Based on these results, we show the effect on
ICL
performance of different factors including the number of demonstrations, the sensitivity of the probabilistic model to the variation of its parameters, as well as the similarity between the demonstrations and the query. This work sheds theoretical insights on
ICL
and provides design guidelines for model training and prompt engineering in
LLM
.
