ABSTRACT
We surveyed professionals in the photovoltaic industry to understand interests in education and
training for performance modeling of solar power systems. We found that most professionals are
self-taught and rely on a variety of public sources of technical materials. Available formal education,
such as courses or certification programs, either lacks detail or is focused on software user training.

Responses indicate several opportunities to create public resources that would benefit professional
learning for solar power system modeling:
 • Create a glossary of terms and common variable names.
 • Develop guidance on uncertainty analysis in the context of solar power systems modeling.
 • Assemble a catalog of available educational materials and data sources.

1. INTRODUCTION

At the Photovoltaic Performance Modeling Collaborative (PVPMC) 2025 workshop (May 2025, in
Albuquerque, NM) we conducted a data gathering session to elicit needs and interest in formal
education or training for photovoltaic (PV) modeling and PV-specific analytics. We structured the
elicitation using the common knowledge, skills and abilities (KSA) framework for describing
learning and competencies and encouraged participants to focus on statements of knowledge and
skills.

Elicitation was performed by requesting free-text responses to each of the following questions:
 1. What knowledge or skill do you wish you had when you started in PV modeling?
 2. What knowledge or skill has proven valuable for you over time?
 3. How did you acquire PV modeling knowledge and skills?
 4. If you are recruiting for a PV modeling position, what knowledge or skills would you
 require?
 5. How would you measure knowledge or skill at PV modeling?
Question 1 seeks insight into gaps in knowledge or skills specific to PV modeling that are evident in
hindsight. Question 2 is phrased to invite a broader perspective on useful skills and knowledge.
Question 3 is intended to capture the current state of education and training among PV modeling
professionals. Question 4 and Question 5 were included to aid in forming a syllabus or learning
objectives if formal education in PV modeling is undertaken.

Approximately 120 attendees participated in the PV Academy session, roughly half of workshop
attendance. Participants could see others’ responses and could upvote responses.

After collecting responses to the above questions, we invited participants to discuss each question
and to suggest additional questions which we did not ask, but which may provide insight into the
state of PV modeler’s knowledge and skill.

2. RESULTS

For questions 1, 2 and 3, we used AI to summarize responses into categories to each question. We
edited these summaries and totaled the number of responses and upvotes for responses in each
category. For questions 4 and 5 duplicate responses and upvoting were not common, so we omit
these totals.

Table 1 through Table 5 summarize responses to the questions. In these responses, we find some
specific, near-term opportunities to create resources that would improve PV modeling skills across
the community:
 • Create a glossary of PV terms and common variables (Q1)
 • Develop guidance on uncertainty analysis in the context of PV systems modeling (Q1)
 • Assemble a catalog of available educational materials and data sources for PV modeling.
 (general observation)
The responses show that most professionals are self-taught, usually while engaged in PV modeling
on the job. Respondents rely on a variety of public media and software for their self-education. We
asked participants if they knew of opportunities for formal education or training. Responses confirm
that available courses, certificate programs or training would not meet their interests, either due to a
lack of sufficient detail to fully explain performance modeling, or a focus on learning to use a
specific software platform at the expense of principles and methods.

One participant offered a question we wished we would have asked: what topic or skill was
particularly difficult to learn? Responses would have pointed directly to subjects for which formal
education would find demand.
