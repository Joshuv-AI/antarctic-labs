1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study quasilinear systems of the form

(1.1)

\left(\phi\left(|\dot{q}(t)|\right)\dot{q}(t)\right)^{\prime}=\nabla V(q(t))\quad\text{on}\quad\mathbb{R},

where
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
is an autonomous potential with
N\geq 1
, and
\phi:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
is a
C^{1}
function satisfying

(
\phi_{1}
)

(\phi(s)s)^{\prime}>0
for all
s>0
;

(
\phi_{2}
)

there exist
l,m\in\mathbb{R}
with
1<l\leq m
such that

l-1\leq\dfrac{(\phi(s)s)^{\prime}}{\phi(s)}\leq m-1,\quad\forall\,s>0.

The conditions
(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
allow us to study system (
1.1
) within a variational framework in Orlicz Sobolev spaces. Central to this approach is the function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
defined by

\Phi(s)=\int_{0}^{|s|}\phi(r)\,r\,dr.

This function is convex, even, nonnegative, and vanishes only at zero. Moreover, under
(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
, it satisfies

\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{\Phi(s)}{s}=0,\qquad\lim_{s\to+\infty}\frac{\Phi(s)}{s}=+\infty,

so that
\Phi
is an
N
-function, a class of functions widely studied in the framework of Orlicz spaces (see
[
1
,
33
]
and Section
2
).

Systems of the form (
1.1
) arise at a structural level in models where the constitutive response depends nonlinearly on the velocity or strain rate. In such contexts, the function
\phi
encodes the rheological law of the medium and may exhibit degeneracy at low velocities (
\phi(0)=0
), singular behavior near rest (
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\phi(s)=+\infty
), or superlinear growth at large strains (
\lim_{s\to+\infty}\phi(s)/s=+\infty
). These features occur, for instance, in models of non–Newtonian fluids and rate–dependent solids; see, e.g.,
[
18
,
21
,
29
,
24
]
. The structural assumptions
(\phi_{1})
–
(\phi_{2})

encompass these degenerate, singular, and superlinear regimes.

Quasilinear equations involving
\Phi
–Laplacian type operators have been extensively studied in the elliptic setting, both in bounded and in unbounded domains. Variational methods in Orlicz–Sobolev spaces provide the natural functional framework for these problems (see, e.g., the classical works

[
8
,
14
,
15
,
20
,
22
,
23
,
27
,
28
]
). More recent contributions in this direction include

[
7
,
16
,
32
,
34
]
. Problems related to transition-type solutions in the
\Phi
–Laplacian setting have been investigated in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

In this paper we adopt a different perspective, viewing (
1.1
) as a Lagrangian system and focusing on searching for global connecting solutions on the whole line. System (
1.1
) is formally the Euler–Lagrange system
associated with the Lagrangian

L(q,\dot{q})=\Phi(|\dot{q}|)+V(q).

Consequently, it can be viewed as a conservative autonomous system,
with conjugate momentum

p=\partial_{\dot{q}}L=\phi(|\dot{q}|)\dot{q}
.
In this perspective, the quantity

E_{q}=p\cdot\dot{q}-L=G(|\dot{q}|)-V(q),

with
G(s)=\phi(s)s^{2}-\Phi(s)
, plays the role of a mechanical energy associated with the system.

Building upon previous variational results for quasilinear equations

[
9
,
10
,
11
]
,
and adopting the prescribed–energy variational framework
introduced for semilinear elliptic problems in

[
2
,
4
,
3
]

and later adapted to Newtonian systems in
[
5
]
,
we investigate the multiplicity of connecting orbits
at prescribed energy for system (
1.1
).

For
c\in\mathbb{R}
we consider the sublevel set

\mathcal{V}_{c}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:V(x)\leq c\}.

We assume that for some
c
the set
\mathcal{V}_{c}
admits a decomposition
into two nonempty closed well separated sets:

(
\mathcal{V}_{c}
)

There exists
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\subset\mathcal{V}_{c}
,
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}\subset\mathcal{V}_{c}
such that

\mathcal{V}_{c}=\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\cup\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-},\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})>0,

(here
\operatorname{dist}(A,B)
denotes the usual Euclidean distance in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
).

Under assumption
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
, we consider the problem of finding a

c
–connection between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
.
More precisely, we look for an interval
I=(\alpha,\omega)\subset\mathbb{R}
,
with
-\infty\leq\alpha<\omega\leq+\infty
, and a function

q\in L^{\infty}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

(i)

q\in C^{1}(\overline{I})\cap C^{2}(I)
and solves (
1.1
) on
I
;

(ii)

V(q(t))>c
and

E_{q}(t)=G(|\dot{q}(t)|)-V(q(t))=-c
for all
t\in I
;

(iii)

\lim_{t\to\alpha^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=\lim_{t\to\omega^{-}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0.

The existence of
c
–connections is obtained through a constrained minimization
of the action functional

J_{c}(q)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\Phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)+V(q(t))-c\big)\,dt

over the admissible class

\Gamma_{c}=\Big\{q\in W^{1,\Phi}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N}):V(q(t))\geq c\ \forall t,\ \liminf_{t\to-\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0\Big\}.

Here
W^{1,\Phi}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the local Orlicz–Sobolev space
associated with the
N
–function
\Phi

(see Section
2
).
The constraint
V\geq c
makes the integrand nonnegative, hence
J_{c}
is bounded from below on
\Gamma_{c}
.

A minimizer
q\in\Gamma_{c}
is defined globally on
\mathbb{R}
and,
by admissibility, satisfies

\liminf_{t\to-\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0.

To extract a
c
–connection, we introduce contact times

-\infty\leq\alpha<\omega\leq+\infty

corresponding to the last contact with
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}

and the subsequent first contact with
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
(see definitions (
3.17
), (
3.18
)),
and consider the associated open interval
I=(\alpha,\omega)
.
On this interval the constraint is inactive, namely

V(q(t))>c\quad\text{for }t\in I,

and
q
is a weak solution of (
1.1
).

A crucial step in the present quasilinear setting consists in proving
that
q
satisfies a weak form of energy conservation, namely

E_{q}(t)=G(|\dot{q}(t)|)-V(q(t))\equiv-c\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in I.

This identity is obtained by exploiting the minimality of
q

with respect to suitable time rescalings (see Lemma
3.10
).
In particular,
\dot{q}(t)\neq 0
for a.e.
t\in I
.
The absence of degeneracy along
I
then allows one to recover
the classical
C^{2}
–regularity of
q
on
I
.

The existence of such a minimizer is obtained under the following
mild coercivity condition on
J_{c}
:

(
\mathcal{J}_{c}
)

There exists
R>0
such that

\inf_{q\in\Gamma_{c}}J_{c}(q)=\inf\Big\{J_{c}(q):\,q\in\Gamma_{c},\ \|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R\Big\}.

This abstract assumption reflects the geometry of the potential
V

and ensures that the minimization of
J_{c}
can be carried out within
a uniformly bounded class of trajectories.
In Section
4

we show that
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
is naturally satisfied
for several classical potentials.

Under assumptions
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
and
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
,
the above scheme yields a minimal
c
–connection,
namely the restriction of a minimizer of
J_{c}
on
\Gamma_{c}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
(\phi_{1})
–
(\phi_{2})
.
Let
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
and let
c\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

(\mathcal{V}_{c})
and
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
hold.
Then system (
1.1
) admits a minimal
c
–connection
between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
.

While the variational strategy is inspired by
[
5
]
,
the extension to the present quasilinear framework is far from straightforward.
In the Newtonian case the kinetic term is quadratic,
the Lagrangian is smooth and uniformly convex in
\dot{q}
,
and minimizers of the constrained functional are automatically classical solutions.
In particular, conservation of energy follows from standard arguments.

In contrast, under
(\phi_{1})
–
(\phi_{2})
the kinetic term may be
nonhomogeneous and possibly degenerate or singular at
\dot{q}=0
.
Consequently, conservation of energy and classical
C^{2}
regularity
for weak minimizers, which are essentially automatic in the semilinear setting,
must here be recovered through a refined variational analysis.

Regularity for weak solutions of systems such as (
1.1
) is delicate.
In the elliptic setting,
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity under
\phi
–structure assumptions
was established by Lieberman
[
26
]
in the scalar case, while in the
vectorial case the seminal work of Uhlenbeck
[
36
]
provides

C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for homogeneous systems.
However, these results do not directly apply in our situation due to the
nonhomogeneous and vectorial nature of (
1.1
).

In the quasilinear setting, the prescribed–energy variational scheme
produces a nontrivial segment which is first obtained as a weak solution.
From minimality we derive a weak conservation law, which implies that
the trajectory lies in the region
\{V>c\}
almost everywhere.
In this region the equation becomes nondegenerate, which allows us to
establish classical
C^{2}
regularity together with the strong energy
identity
E_{q}\equiv-c
.

In this way, conservation of energy and classical regularity emerge
as consequences of the constrained variational structure itself.
The method thus carries over to a general quasilinear framework and
selects the prescribed energy level even in the presence of degeneracy
or singular behavior. As a consequence, the variational construction,
although performed in the Orlicz–Sobolev setting, produces classical

C^{2}
trajectories satisfying the strong energy identity
E_{q}\equiv-c
.
This makes it possible to classify the resulting connecting orbits
according to the nature of the contact times with the level set
\{V=c\}
.

As shown in Section
3.3
,
c
–connections admit natural global
continuations and therefore generate entire weak solutions of (
1.1
)
on
\mathbb{R}
with constant energy
-c
.
More precisely, if a
c
–connection is defined on an interval

I=(\alpha,\omega)
, it can be extended across finite contact times
by time reflection and, when both contact times are finite,
by periodic continuation.

The resulting global solutions can be classified according to the nature
of the contact times with the level set
\{V=c\}
.
If both contact times are infinite, the solution remains strictly in the region

\{V>c\}
and is asymptotic as
t\to\pm\infty
to the distinct sets

\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}
, yielding a heteroclinic-type solution.
If exactly one contact time is finite, the corresponding entire solution is asymptotic
to the same set in both time directions and is of homoclinic type.
If both contact times are finite, the reflection procedure produces
a periodic solution oscillating between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
,
namely a brake-type orbit.

Moreover, if at least one contact time is infinite, then
c
must be
a critical value of
V
. In particular, for regular values of
c

the construction produces only brake–type periodic solutions.

Finally, following the approach of
[
5
]
, Section
4

illustrates the abstract variational framework through several classical
models, including double–well, Duffing–type, and multiple pendulum–type systems.
These examples provide concrete situations in which the assumptions

(\mathcal{V}_{c})
and
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
can be verified explicitly.

In these cases the potential
V
possesses isolated minima at level
c=0
,
and there exists a threshold value
c^{*}>0
, determined by the geometry
of the potential, such that
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
and
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
hold for all

0\leq c<c^{*}
. Theorem
1.1
therefore yields
c
–connections
that extend to entire solutions with prescribed energy
-c
for all
such values of
c
.

When
c=0
, the solutions are heteroclinic or homoclinic orbits
connecting minima of
V
. For
0<c<c^{*}
the resulting trajectories
may be heteroclinic, homoclinic, or of brake type. In particular,
whenever
c
is a regular value of
V
, the construction yields
brake–type periodic solutions.

In the case of multiple pendulum–type systems, the geometry of the
sublevel set
\mathcal{V}_{c}
admits different admissible decompositions
into well–separated subsets. This leads to multiplicity results, yielding geometrically
distinct connecting orbits at the same prescribed energy level,
as described in Proposition
4.11
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some basic consequences of assumptions
(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
on the function
\phi
, and recall the corresponding properties of the associated
N
-function
\Phi
and of the Orlicz and Orlicz–Sobolev spaces.
Although all the results stated below are classical and can be found, for instance,
in
[
22
,
1
,
33
,
25
]
,
we briefly indicate the proofs of the main structural estimates
for the reader’s convenience.

We recall that
\phi:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is a
C^{1}
function satisfying assumptions
(\phi_{1})
–
(\phi_{2})
, namely
(\phi(s)s)^{\prime}>0
for all
s>0
and

l-1\leq\frac{(\phi(s)s)^{\prime}}{\phi(s)}\leq m-1,\quad\forall\,s>0,

for some
1<l\leq m
.

Define
\Phi(s)=\int_{0}^{|s|}\phi(r)r\,dr
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then
\Phi
is even, convex,
\Phi(0)=0
,
\Phi(s)>0
for
s\neq 0
, and

\Phi^{\prime}(s)=\phi(|s|)s
for
s\neq 0
.
From
(\phi_{1})
–
(\phi_{2})
one deduces

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\Phi(s)}{s}=0,\qquad\lim_{s\to+\infty}\frac{\Phi(s)}{s}=+\infty,

hence
\Phi
is an
N
-function. Indeed, set

a(s)=\phi(s)s\text{ for }s>0\text{ and }a(0):=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\phi(s)s.

Note that
a(0)\in\mathbb{R}
since, by
(\phi_{1})
, the function
a
is nondecreasing on
(0,+\infty)
. Hence
a\in C([0,+\infty))\cap C^{1}(0,+\infty)
and, by
(\phi_{2})
, it satisfies
(l-1)a(t)\leq a^{\prime}(t)t
for all
t>0
.
Integrating this on
[0,s]
gives
l\int_{0}^{s}a(t)\,dt\leq a(s)s
for all
s>0
.
Since
l>1
, this forces
a(0)=0
and thus
\Phi(s)/s\to 0
as
s\to 0^{+}
.
Moreover, since
a^{\prime}(t)/a(t)\geq(l-1)/t
for
t>0
,
integrating on
[1,s]
yields
a(s)\geq a(1)s^{\,l-1}
for
s>1
,
which implies
\Phi(s)/s\to+\infty
as
s\to+\infty
.

Under assumptions
(\phi_{1})
–
(\phi_{2})
,
\Phi
satisfies the two-sided growth condition

(2.1)

l\leq\frac{\phi(s)s^{2}}{\Phi(s)}\leq m,\qquad\forall\,s>0.

Indeed
(\phi_{2})
gives
(l-1)a(t)\leq a^{\prime}(t)t\leq(m-1)a(t)
for
t>0
. Then, integrating on
[0,s]
, we obtain
l\Phi(s)\leq a(s)s\leq m\Phi(s)
and (
2.1
) follows.

As shown in
[
22
, Lemma 2.1]
, this implies the homogeneity-type estimate

(2.2)

\min\{t^{l},t^{m}\}\,\Phi(s)\leq\Phi(st)\leq\max\{t^{l},t^{m}\}\,\Phi(s),\qquad\forall\,s,t\geq 0.

Indeed for any
s>0
and
\tau>0
,
\frac{d}{d\tau}\Phi(s\tau)=a(s\tau)s

and from (
2.1
),

\frac{l}{\tau}\leq\frac{d}{d\tau}\log\Phi(s\tau)\leq\frac{m}{\tau}
.
Integrating with respect to
\tau
from
1
to
t
(or
t
to
1
) gives

\log t^{l}\leq\log\frac{\Phi(st)}{\Phi(s)}\leq\log t^{m}
for
t\geq 1
,

\log t^{m}\leq\log\frac{\Phi(st)}{\Phi(s)}\leq\log t^{l}
for
0<t\leq 1
.
Exponentiating yields (
2.2
).

From (
2.2
) with
t=2
we obtain

\Phi(2s)\leq 2^{m}\Phi(s)
for all
s\geq 0
. Hence,
\Phi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with
\lambda=2^{m}
, which is a standard structural assumption in the Orlicz space framework.

Some further elementary estimates will be useful later.

First note that, since
\phi(s)s\to 0
as
s\to 0^{+}
, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that
\phi(s)s\leq C_{0}
for all
s\in[0,1]
.
Combined with (
2.1
) this yields

(2.3)

\phi(s)s\leq m\,\Phi(s)+C_{0},\qquad\forall\,s\geq 0.

Second, we have

(2.4)

\Phi(|a\xi_{1}+b\xi_{2}|)\leq 2^{m-1}(\Phi(|\xi_{1}|)+\Phi(|\xi_{2}|))

for any
0<a,b\leq 1
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Indeed note that the map
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto\Phi(|z|)
is convex. Hence, for any
a,b>0
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\Phi(|a\xi_{1}+b\xi_{2}|)=\Phi(|\tfrac{1}{2}(2a\xi_{1})+\tfrac{1}{2}(2b\xi_{2})|)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\Phi(2a|\xi_{1}|)+\tfrac{1}{2}\Phi(2b\,|\xi_{2}|).

Since
0<a,b\leq 1
, by (
2.2
) we have

\Phi(2a|\xi_{1}|)\leq 2^{m}\Phi(|\xi_{1}|),
and
\Phi(2b\,|\xi_{2}|)\leq 2^{m}\Phi(|\xi_{2}|)
, (
2.4
) follows.

We now briefly recall the main properties of the associated Orlicz and Orlicz–Sobolev spaces that will be used in the sequel.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval.
The Orlicz space associated with
\Phi
is defined by

L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})=\Bigl\{q\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{I}\Phi(|q|/\lambda)\,dt<\infty\text{ for some }\lambda>0\Bigr\},

endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\|q\|_{L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\inf\Bigl\{\lambda>0:\int_{I}\Phi(|q|/\lambda)\,dt\leq 1\Bigr\}.

Since
\Phi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition,

q\in L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\int_{I}\Phi(|q|)\,dt<\infty,

and
\|q\|_{L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\infty
if and only if the integral is finite.

The corresponding Orlicz–Sobolev space is

W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})=\Big\{q\in L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N}):\dot{q}\in L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})\Big\},

with the norm

\|q\|_{W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|q\|_{L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\dot{q}\|_{L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

The
\Delta_{2}
-condition ensures that both the Orlicz space
L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the Orlicz–Sobolev space
W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
are Banach, separable, and reflexive spaces.

From (
2.2
) and the superlinear growth of
\Phi
at
+\infty
, one deduces that there exists
C>0
such that
\Phi(s)+C\geq|s|
. Hence on bounded intervals we have continuous embeddings

L^{\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow W^{1,1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

We conclude this section by highlighting a structural property of the vector field associated with
\phi
, which will play a crucial role in the regularity analysis.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
.
Define
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
by
g(x)=\phi(|x|)x
for
x\neq 0
and
g(0)=0
.
Then
g\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

g(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
, and
g
is locally invertible on

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
. In particular, the inverse map

g^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
is of class
C^{1}

on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Since
\phi(s)s\to 0
as
s\to 0^{+}
, we have

|g(x)|=\phi(|x|)|x|\to 0
as
x\to 0
.
Hence
g
extends continuously at
0
, and therefore

g\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

To show that
g(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
, let
a(r)=\phi(r)r
for
r>0
. By
(\phi_{1})
,
a^{\prime}(r)=(\phi(r)r)^{\prime}>0
,
hence
a
is strictly increasing. Moreover, by
(\phi_{2})
one has

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}a(r)=0
and
\lim_{r\to+\infty}a(r)=+\infty
, so
a
is a continuous
bijection
(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
. Writing
x=r\omega
with
r>0
,
|\omega|=1
, we have

g(x)=a(r)\omega
, which implies
g(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

For
x\neq 0
we have

\frac{\partial g_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)=\phi(|x|)\delta_{ij}+\phi^{\prime}(|x|)\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|},

and hence, in matrix form,

Dg(x)=\phi(|x|)I+\phi^{\prime}(|x|)\frac{x\otimes x}{|x|},\qquad x\otimes x:=(x_{i}x_{j})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.

Then
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Moreover, using the assumptions
(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
and the rank-one perturbation formula

\det(A+uv^{T})=\det(A)(1+v^{T}A^{-1}u)
with

A=\phi(|x|)I
and
uv^{T}=\phi^{\prime}(|x|)\frac{x\otimes x}{|x|}
, we obtain

\det(Dg(x))=\phi(|x|)^{\,N-1}(\phi(|x|)+\phi^{\prime}(|x|)|x|)>0\qquad\text{for all }x\neq 0.

Therefore,
g
is a local diffeomorphism on

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.
Since
g(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
its inverse
g^{-1}
is of class
C^{1}

on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.
∎

3.
Minimal

c
–connections and their global extensions

In this section we develop the variational theory leading to the existence of solutions for the quasilinear system (
1.1
), which connect the two sublevel sets
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
at a prescribed energy level
-c
. Such solutions will be referred to as
c
–connections.

The analysis is carried out within the abstract framework introduced in the previous sections. Throughout this section we assume that the structural conditions
(\phi_{1})
,
(\phi_{2})
,
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
, and
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
are satisfied.

We also study qualitative properties of such trajectories. In particular we describe the structure of the contact times with the sublevel sets, prove that the solutions have constant energy
-c
, and that they are classical
C^{2}
solutions of (
1.1
) away from the contact points.

3.1.
Minimization problem on
\Gamma_{c}

Given
c\in\mathbb{R}
and an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we define the action functional

J_{c,I}(q)=\int_{I}\bigl(\Phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)+V(q(t))-c\bigr)dt

on the class

\mathcal{X}_{c}=\bigl\{q\in W^{1,\Phi}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N}):\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}V(q(t))\geq c\bigr\}.

When
I=\mathbb{R}
we write
J_{c}
instead of
J_{c,\mathbb{R}}
.

Functions in
W^{1,\Phi}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
are absolutely continuous, due to the continuous embedding

W^{1,\Phi}_{\text{loc}}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow W^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})

(see Section
2
).
If
(q_{n})\subset W^{1,\Phi}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
q\in W^{1,\Phi}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we write

q_{n}\rightharpoonup q
in
W^{1,\Phi}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
to mean that
q_{n}
converges weakly to
q
in
W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every compact interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.

The next lemma shows that
J_{c,I}
is nonnegative on
\mathcal{X}_{c}
and has useful semicontinuity properties.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we have
J_{c,I}(q)\geq 0
for all
q\in\mathcal{X}_{c}
.
Moreover, if
(q_{n})\subset\mathcal{X}_{c}
with
q_{n}\rightharpoonup q
in
W^{1,\Phi}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
q\in W^{1,\Phi}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

J_{c,I}(q)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}J_{c,I}(q_{n}).

Proof.

Since
\Phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\geq 0
and
V(q(t))\geq c
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

for every
q\in\mathcal{X}_{c}
, we plainly have

J_{c,I}(q)=\int_{I}\big(\Phi(|\dot{q}|)+V(q)-c\big)\,dt\;\geq\;0,\qquad\forall\,q\in\mathcal{X}_{c}.

Now let
(q_{n})\subset\mathcal{X}_{c}
and
q\in W^{1,\Phi}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
q_{n}\rightharpoonup q
in
W^{1,\Phi}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
If
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is a bounded interval,
q_{n}\rightharpoonup q
in
W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then,
(q_{n})
is bounded in
W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})

and since
W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow W^{1,1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
continuously, the sequence is equicontinuous on
I
. Hence
q_{n}\to q
uniformly on
I
and since
V
is continuous

\int_{I}(V(q)-c)\,dt=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{I}(V(q_{n})-c)\,dt.

Moreover, since the functional

v\mapsto\int_{I}\Phi(|\dot{v}|)\,dt

is convex and continuous on
W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it is weakly lower semicontinuous on
W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence

\int_{I}\Phi(|\dot{q}|)\,dt\;\leq\;\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{I}\Phi(|\dot{q}_{n}|)\,dt.

Then

J_{c,I}(q)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}J_{c,I}(q_{n})
for any bounded interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.
If
I
is not bounded, set
I_{R}:=I\cap(-R,R)
for
R>0
. Since the integrand in
J_{c,I}
is nonnegative, we have

J_{c,I}(q)=\sup_{R>0}J_{c,I_{R}}(q),\qquad J_{c,I}(q_{n})=\sup_{R>0}J_{c,I_{R}}(q_{n}).

Therefore, taking the supremum over
R>0
and using
\sup_{R}\liminf_{n}\leq\liminf_{n}\sup_{R}
, we obtain

J_{c,I}(q)=\sup_{R>0}J_{c,I_{R}}(q)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sup_{R>0}J_{c,I_{R}}(q_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}J_{c,I}(q_{n}),

and the lemma follows.
∎

We look for connecting solutions of (
1.1
) by minimizing
J_{c}
on
\Gamma_{c}
, and we set

m_{c}=\inf_{q\in\Gamma_{c}}J_{c}(q).

To get compactness in the minimization process, it is useful to recall an important coercivity inequalities already used in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q\in\mathcal{X}_{c}
and
(\sigma,\tau)\subset\mathbb{R}
. If there exists
d>0
such that
V(q(t))-c\geq d
for all
t\in(\sigma,\tau)
, then

\begin{split}J_{c,(\sigma,\tau)}(q)&\geq\dfrac{\tau-\sigma}{\xi_{1}(\tau-\sigma)}\Phi(|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|)+d(\tau-\sigma)\\
&\geq\lambda_{d}H\left(|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|\right),\end{split}

where
\lambda_{d}:=\min\{d^{(l-1)/l},d^{(m-1)/m}\}
,
H(s)=\min\left\{\Phi(s)^{\frac{1}{l}},\Phi(s)^{\frac{1}{m}}\right\}
and
\xi_{1}(s)=\max\{s^{l},s^{m}\}
.

Proof.

The argument follows the approach in
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
, and we only briefly outline it. Let
q\in\mathcal{X}_{c}
satisfy
V(q(t))-c\geq d
for all
t\in(\sigma,\tau)
.
By the monotonicity and convexity of
\Phi
and Jensen’s inequality

\Phi\big(|\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}\dot{q}(t)\,dt|\big)\leq\Phi\big(\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}|\dot{q}(t)|\,dt\big)\leq\tfrac{1}{\tau-\sigma}\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}\Phi((\tau-\sigma)|\dot{q}(t)|)\,dt.

Using the estimate (
2.2
), denoting
\xi_{1}(\tau-\sigma)=\max\{(\tau-\sigma)^{l},(\tau-\sigma)^{m}\}
, this yields that

\Phi(|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|)\leq\tfrac{\xi_{1}(\tau-\sigma)}{\tau-\sigma}\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}\Phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\,dt.

Hence

J_{c,(\sigma,\tau)}(q)\geq\frac{\tau-\sigma}{\xi_{1}(\tau-\sigma)}\Phi(|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|)+d(\tau-\sigma).

Since
\xi_{1}(\tau-\sigma)=(\tau-\sigma)^{p}
for a
p\in\{l,m\}
(depending on whether
\tau-\sigma\leq 1
or

\tau-\sigma\geq 1
), applying Young’s inequality with conjugate exponents
p
and
p/(p-1)
, we obtain

J_{c,(\sigma,\tau)}(q)\geq d^{(p-1)/p}\,\Phi(|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|)^{1/p}.

Taking the minimum of the right hand side with respect to
p\in\{l,m\}
,
we obtain

J_{c,(\sigma,\tau)}(q)\geq\min\{d^{(l-1)/l},d^{(m-1)/m}\}\,\min\left\{\Phi(|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|)^{\frac{1}{l}},\Phi(|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|)^{\frac{1}{m}}\right\},

completing the proof.
∎

Lemma
3.2
provides the basic coercivity estimate needed for compactness.
It shows that whenever the potential stays above level
c
on an interval, the action grows both with the length of the interval and with the endpoint displacement.
This dual control prevents trajectories of finite action from spreading indefinitely in time or in space.

Remark 3.3
.

We denote by
\rho_{0}
the distance between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
:

\rho_{0}=\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-},\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}).

By
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
, we have
\rho_{0}>0
. Moreover, for given
r>0
and
C>0
, the set

K_{r,C}=\bigl\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x|\leq C\text{ and }\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq r\bigr\}

is compact and, due to the continuity of
V
, there exists
d=d(r,C)>0
such that

\inf_{x\in K_{r,C}}V(x)\geq c+d.

A first consequence of Lemma
3.2
is that the minimal level
m_{c}
cannot vanish.

Lemma 3.4
.

m_{c}\in(0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

It is immediate to see that
m_{c}<+\infty
.

Indeed, by
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
,

(3.1)

\text{there exist }x_{-}\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\text{ and }x_{+}\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}\text{ such that }V(tx_{+}+(1-t)x_{-})>c\text{ for all }t\in(0,1).

Choose any pair
\xi^{-}\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
,
\xi^{+}\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
and set
\gamma(s)=(1-s)\xi^{-}+s\xi^{+}
.
Let
s_{-}:=\sup\{s\in[0,1]:\gamma(s)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\}
and
s_{+}:=\inf\{s>s_{-}:\gamma(s)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}\}
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}
are closed,
x_{-}:=\gamma(s_{-})\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
x_{+}:=\gamma(s_{+})\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
, and by definition
\gamma(s)\notin\mathcal{V}_{c}=\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\cup\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
for all
s\in(s_{-},s_{+})
. Setting
s=(1-t)s_{-}+ts_{+}
we have
V(tx_{+}+(1-t)x_{-})>c
for all
t\in(0,1)
and (
3.1
) follows.

Then, by (
3.1
), the piecewise linear function

\overline{q}(t)=\begin{cases}x_{-},&t\leq 0,\\
tx_{+}+(1-t)x_{-},&t\in(0,1),\\
x_{+},&t\geq 1,\end{cases}

belongs to
\Gamma_{c}
, and hence

m_{c}\leq J_{c}(\overline{q})\leq\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\Phi(|\dot{\overline{q}}(t)|)+V(\overline{q}(t))-c\bigr)\,dt<+\infty.

To show that
m_{c}>0
, consider a
q\in\Gamma_{c}
with
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R
. By definition of
\Gamma_{c}

we have
\liminf_{t\to\pm\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm})=0

As
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}
are closed and
q
is continuous, the maps
t\in\mathbb{R}\to\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm})
are continuous. As
\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0
and
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-},\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=\rho_{0}
, we recover
\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\geq\rho_{0}
. Since

\liminf_{t\to-\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0
, by continuity there exists an interval
(\sigma,\tau)\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

(3.2)

\operatorname{dist}(q(\sigma),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=\rho_{0}/4,\ \operatorname{dist}(q(\tau),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=\rho_{0}/2,\ \rho_{0}/4\leq\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\leq\rho_{0}/2\ \forall t\in(\sigma,\tau).

By (
3.2
) and Remark
3.3
we deduce that
\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\rho_{0}/4
for any
t\in(\sigma,\tau)
. Hence, since
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R
, we have that
q(t)\in K_{\rho_{0}/4,R}
for any
t\in(\sigma,\tau)
. By Remark
3.3
, there exists
d=d(\rho_{0}/4,R)>0
such that

V(q(t))\geq c+d,\quad\forall t\in(\sigma,\tau).

Since (
3.2
) implies also
|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|\geq\rho_{0}/4
, Lemma
3.2
yields

J_{c,(\sigma,\tau)}(q)\geq\lambda_{d}\,H(\rho_{0}/4),\quad\forall q\in\Gamma_{c}\text{ such that }\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R,

and therefore, from
(
\mathcal{J}_{c}
)
, we conclude
m_{c}\geq\lambda_{d}\,H(\rho_{0}/4)
.
∎

A second application of Lemma
3.2
shows that any finite–action trajectory in
\Gamma_{c}
with bounded
L^{\infty}
norm must approach the sets
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}
continuously as
t\to\pm\infty
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
q\in\Gamma_{c}
with
J_{c}(q)<+\infty
and
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R
. Then

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\mathrm{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathrm{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0.

Proof.

Since
q\in\Gamma_{c}
we have
\liminf_{t\to-\infty}\mathrm{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0
. Assume, by contradiction, that
\limsup_{t\to-\infty}\mathrm{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})>2r
for an
r\in\left(0,\rho_{0}/4\right)
.
The continuity of the map
t\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathrm{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})
implies the existence of a sequence of disjoint intervals
(s_{n},t_{n})
, with
t_{n+1}<s_{n}<t_{n}
and
t_{n}\to-\infty
, such that

|q(t_{n})-q(s_{n})|\geq r\quad\text{and}\quad r\leq\mathrm{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\leq 2r,\quad\forall\,\,t\in(s_{n},t_{n})\,\text{ and }\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

By the definition of
\rho_{0}
, this implies

\mathrm{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq r
, so
q(t)\in K_{r,R}
for any
t\in(s_{n},t_{n})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, by Remark
3.3
, there exists
d=d(r,R)>0
such that
V(q(t))\geq c+d
for any
t\in(s_{n},t_{n})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying Lemma
3.2
, we deduce

J_{c,(s_{n},t_{n})}(q)\geq\lambda_{d}H(r)
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

and so,

J_{c}(q)\geq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}J_{c,(s_{n},t_{n})}(q)\geq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}H(r)=+\infty,

which contradicts the assumption
J_{c}(q)<+\infty
. Therefore,
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\mathrm{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0
. Similarly, one obtains
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathrm{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0
, and the proof is complete.
∎

As a further consequence of Lemma
3.2
, we can describe the concentration behavior of trajectories in
\Gamma_{c}
whose action is close to the minimal level
m_{c}
. In particular, such trajectories are forced to transition from a neighborhood of
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
to a neighborhood of
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
within a controlled time interval.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exist
\nu_{0}>0
and
L_{0}>0
such that,
if
q\in\Gamma_{c}
satisfies

(3.3)

\mathrm{dist}(q(0),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{8},\quad\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R\quad\text{and}\quad J_{c}(q)\leq m_{c}+\nu_{0},

then

\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})<\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{8}\text{ for all }t>L_{0},\text{ and }\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})<\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{8}\text{ for all }t<-L_{0}.

Proof.

We begin by fixing the constants
\nu_{0}
and
L_{0}
.
By Remark
3.3
, there exists
d_{\rho_{0}}>0
such that

(3.4)

V(x)\geq c+d_{\rho_{0}}\quad\text{for all }x\text{ with }|x|\leq R+\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{8}\text{ and }\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16}.

For
r\in(0,\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16})
, define

\mu_{r}=\max\Bigl\{V(x)-c:|x|\leq R+\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16},\ \operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal{V}_{c})\leq r\Bigr\},

and note that
\mu_{r}\to 0
as
r\to 0^{+}
.
Since
\Phi(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0^{+}
, we can fix

r_{0}\in(0,\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16})
such that

(3.5)

\nu_{0}:=\Phi(r_{0})+\mu_{r_{0}}<\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{d_{\rho_{0}}}H(\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16}),

where
\lambda_{d_{\rho_{0}}}
and
H
are defined in Lemma
3.2
. Applying Remark
3.3
again with this
r_{0}
, we obtain
d_{r_{0}}>0
satisfying

(3.6)

V(x)\geq c+d_{r_{0}}\quad\text{for all }x\text{ with }|x|\leq R+\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{8}\text{ and }\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq r_{0}.

Finally, we set

L_{0}:=\tfrac{m_{c}+\nu_{0}}{d_{r_{0}}}.

With
\nu_{0}
and
L_{0}
now fixed, let
q\in\Gamma_{c}
satisfy (
3.3
). Define

\sigma_{0}=\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\text{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\leq r_{0}\}\text{ and }\tau_{0}=\inf\{t>\sigma_{0}:\text{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})\leq r_{0}\}.

By Lemma
3.5
the sets in the definitions of
\sigma_{0}
and
\tau_{0}
are nonempty, hence
\sigma_{0}\leq\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
By continuity,

\operatorname{dist}(q(\sigma_{0}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=r_{0}<\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16},\quad\operatorname{dist}(q(\tau_{0}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=r_{0}<\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16},

and since
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-},\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=\rho_{0}
, we have
\sigma_{0}<\tau_{0}
.
Moreover, by definition,

(3.7)

\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c})>r_{0}\quad\text{for all }t\in(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0}).

We claim that

(3.8)

\text{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})<\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{8},\quad\forall\,\,t\leq\sigma_{0}.

Suppose, by contradiction, that there exists
t^{*}<\sigma_{0}
such that

\text{dist}(q(t^{*}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\geq\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{8}.

Since
\text{dist}(q(\sigma_{0}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\leq\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16}
, continuity yields an interval
(\sigma,\tau)\subset(t^{*},\sigma_{0})
where

|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|\geq\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16}\text{ and }\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16}\leq\text{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\leq\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{8},\text{ for all }t\in(\sigma,\tau).

Hence,

\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\frac{\rho_{0}}{16}
, for all
t\in(\sigma,\tau),

and, as
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R
, by (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.2

(3.9)

J_{c,(\sigma,\tau)}(q)\geq\lambda_{d_{\rho_{0}}}H(|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|)\geq\lambda_{d_{\rho_{0}}}H(\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16}).

On the other hand, because
\text{dist}(q(\sigma_{0}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=r_{0}<\rho_{0}/16<\rho_{0}=\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-},\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})
, arguing as in the proof of (
3.1
), we derive that there is
\xi_{\sigma_{0}}\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
such that

(3.10)

V(\xi_{\sigma_{0}})=c,\quad|q(\sigma_{0})-\xi_{\sigma_{0}}|=r_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad V((1-s)q(\sigma_{0})+s\xi_{\sigma_{0}})>c\quad\forall\,\,s\in(0,1).

Then we consider the function

\tilde{q}(t)=\begin{cases}\xi_{\sigma_{0}}&\text{if }t\leq\sigma_{0}-1,\\
(t-\sigma_{0}+1)q(\sigma_{0})+(\sigma_{0}-t)\xi_{\sigma_{0}}&\text{if }\sigma_{0}-1<t<\sigma_{0},\\
q(t)&\text{if }\sigma_{0}\leq t.\end{cases}

Since
q\in\Gamma_{c}
and
\xi_{\sigma_{0}}\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
, it follows from (
3.10
) that
\tilde{q}\in\Gamma_{c}
. Hence
J_{c}(\tilde{q})\geq m_{c}
. Moreover, since
|q(\sigma_{0})-\xi_{\sigma_{0}}|=r_{0}
,
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R
we derive

|\tilde{q}(t)|\leq R+r_{0}
and
\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{q}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\leq r_{0}
for all
t\in(\sigma_{0}-1,\sigma_{0})
.
Then, by the definitions of
\tilde{q}
,
\mu_{r_{0}}
and the monotonicity of
\Phi
,

J_{c,(-\infty,\sigma_{0})}(\tilde{q})\leq\int_{\sigma_{0}-1}^{\sigma_{0}}(\Phi(|q(\sigma_{0})-\xi_{\sigma_{0}}|)+\mu_{r_{0}})\,dt\leq\Phi(r_{0})+\mu_{r_{0}}=\nu_{0}.

Hence

J_{c,(\sigma_{0},+\infty)}(\tilde{q})=J_{c}(\tilde{q})-J_{c,(-\infty,\sigma_{0})}(\tilde{q})\geq m_{c}-\nu_{0},

from which it follows

J_{c}(q)=J_{c,(-\infty,\sigma_{0})}(q)+J_{c,(\sigma_{0},+\infty)}(\tilde{q})\geq J_{c,(-\infty,\sigma_{0})}(q)+m_{c}-\nu_{0}.

Since
J_{c}(q)\leq m_{c}+\nu_{0}
, the above inequality implies that

J_{c,(-\infty,\sigma_{0})}(q)\leq 2\nu_{0}

which, together with (
3.9
), yields

2\nu_{0}\geq J_{c,(-\infty,\sigma_{0})}(q)\geq J_{c,(\sigma,\tau)}(q)\geq\lambda_{d_{\rho_{0}}}H(\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{16}),

which contradicts (
3.5
). A symmetric argument proves that

(3.11)

\text{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})<\dfrac{\rho_{0}}{8},\quad\forall\,\,t\geq\tau_{0}.

Finally, to conclude the proof, we show that

-L_{0}<\sigma_{0}<0<\tau_{0}<L_{0}.

The assumption
\operatorname{dist}(q(0),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\frac{\rho_{0}}{8}
together with (
3.8
) and (
3.11
) implies
0\in(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})
.
Moreover, by (
3.7
) and (
3.6
),
V(q(t))-c\geq d_{r_{0}}
for all
t\in(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})
. Consequently,

m_{c}+\nu_{0}\geq J_{c}(q)\geq J_{c,(0,\tau_{0})}(q)\geq\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}\bigl(V(q(t))-c\bigr)\,dt\geq\tau_{0}\,d_{r_{0}},

so
\tau_{0}<\frac{m_{c}+\nu_{0}}{d_{r_{0}}}=L_{0}
.
A similar estimate gives
\sigma_{0}>-L_{0}
, completing the proof.
∎

Thanks to the compactness properties established above, we can now apply the direct method of the calculus of variations to obtain the existence of a minimizer for the functional
J_{c}
on
\Gamma_{c}
at level
m_{c}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists
q_{0}\in\Gamma_{c}
such that

J_{c}(q_{0})=m_{c},\quad\operatorname{dist}(q_{0}(0),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\frac{\rho_{0}}{8}\quad\text{and}\quad\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R.

Proof.

By condition
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
, we can find a sequence
(q_{n})\subset\Gamma_{c}
satisfying

(3.12)

J_{c}(q_{n})\to m_{c}\quad\text{and}\quad\|q_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, since
q_{n}\in\Gamma_{c}
, by continuity there exists
t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\operatorname{dist}(q_{n}(t_{n}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=\frac{\rho_{0}}{8}
.
Because
\Gamma_{c}
is invariant under time translations, by shifting
q_{n}
, it is not restrictive to assume
t_{n}=0
, so that

(3.13)

\operatorname{dist}(q_{n}(0),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\frac{\rho_{0}}{8}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Without loss of generality we can also assume that

(3.14)

J_{c}(q_{n})\leq m_{c}+\nu_{0}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

with
\nu_{0}
given by Lemma
3.6
.
By (
3.12
), (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and Lemma
3.6
, there exists a constant
L_{0}>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

(3.15)

\operatorname{dist}(q_{n}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})<\dfrac{\rho_{0}}{8},\quad\forall\,\,t\geq L_{0}\text{ and }\operatorname{dist}(q_{n}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})<\dfrac{\rho_{0}}{8}\quad\forall\,\,t\leq-L_{0}.

Since
\|q_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R
and
m_{c}+\nu_{0}\geq J_{c}(q_{n})\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(|\dot{q}_{n}(t)|)\,dt
, we obtain that
(q_{n})
is bounded in
W^{1,\Phi}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any bounded interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
. Then, by the continuity of the immersion
W^{1,\Phi}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
in
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
(q_{n})
is bounded in
W^{1,1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and so equicontinuous on
I
for any bounded interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
. By using a diagonal procedure, we obtain the existence of

q_{0}\in W^{1,\Phi}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and a subsequence of

(q_{n})
, still denoted
(q_{n})
, such that

q_{n}\rightharpoonup q_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad W_{\text{loc}}^{1,\Phi}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\text{and}\quad q_{n}(t)\to q_{0}(t)\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

By Lemma
3.1
, the lower semicontinuity of
J_{c}
yields
J_{c}(q_{0})\leq m_{c}
. By (
3.12
), (
3.13
), (
3.14
), (
3.15
) and pointwise convergence

q_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{c},\quad\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R,\quad\operatorname{dist}(q_{0}(0),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\frac{\rho_{0}}{8},

and

(3.16)

\operatorname{dist}(q_{0}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})\leq\dfrac{\rho_{0}}{8},\quad\forall\,\,t\geq L_{0}\text{ and }\operatorname{dist}(q_{0}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\leq\dfrac{\rho_{0}}{8}\quad\forall\,\,t\leq-L_{0}.

To conclude that
q_{0}\in\Gamma_{c}
we need to verify that
\liminf_{t\to\pm\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm})=0
. Consider first the case
t\to+\infty
. Since
J_{c}(q_{0})\leq m_{c}
we have
\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\left(V(q_{0}(t))-c\right)=0
, so
there exists a sequence
(\tau_{n})\subset(L_{0},+\infty)
with
\tau_{n}\to\infty
such that
V(q_{0}(\tau_{n}))\to c
. Since
|q_{0}(\tau_{n})|\leq R
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that, up to a subsequence,
q_{0}(\tau_{n})\to w
. Therefore,
V(q_{0}(\tau_{n}))\to V(w)=c
. Then
w\in\mathcal{V}_{c}
and, by (
3.16
),
w\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
showing that

\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q_{0}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0.

The argument for
t\to-\infty
is analogous, giving
\liminf_{t\to-\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q_{0}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0.

Therefore
q_{0}\in\Gamma_{c}
with
J_{c}(q_{0})=m_{c}
.
∎

3.2.
Contact times for minima in
\Gamma_{c}
and related prescribed Energy solutions.

By Lemma
3.7
the normalized minimal set

\mathcal{M}_{c}=\left\{q\in\Gamma_{c}\mid\operatorname{dist}(q(0),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{8},\,J_{c}(q)=m_{c},\,\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R\right\},

is nonempty. According to
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
we have

\mathcal{M}_{c}\subset\{\,q\in\Gamma_{c}\mid J_{c}(q)=m_{c}\,\}.

Moreover, by translation
\mathcal{M}_{c}
generates the set of all the minimizers with
L^{\infty}
-norm bounded by
R
.
Indeed, the argument leading to (
3.2
) shows that for any
q\in\Gamma_{c}
there exists
t_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\operatorname{dist}(q(t_{q}),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\frac{\rho_{0}}{8}
.
Hence, if
J_{c}(q)=m_{c}
and
\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R
, then
\tilde{q}(\cdot)=q(\cdot-t_{q})\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
.

For each
q\in\Gamma_{c}
we define

(3.17)

\alpha_{q}=\begin{cases}-\infty&\text{if }q(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}=\emptyset,\\
\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}:q(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\}&\text{if }q(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\neq\emptyset,\end{cases}

and

(3.18)

\omega_{q}=\begin{cases}\inf\{t>\alpha_{q}:q(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}\}&\text{if }q(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}\neq\emptyset,\\
+\infty&\text{if }q(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}=\emptyset.\end{cases}

Thanks to Lemma
3.6

\alpha_{q}
and
\omega_{q}
are always well defined in
\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
for functions
q\in\Gamma_{c}
with action close to
m_{c}
. Indeed
using Lemma
3.6
and Remark
3.3
, if
q\in\Gamma_{c}
is such that

J_{c}(q)\leq m_{c}+\nu_{0}
, the point
q(t)
does not belong to
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
for
t\leq-L_{0}
nor to
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
for
t\geq L_{0}
. This guarantees that

(3.19)

\alpha_{q}\leq L_{0}<+\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega_{q}\geq-L_{0}>-\infty.

These times are called the
contact times
of the trajectory
q
with the sublevel sets
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
of the potential.
The next lemma describes some of the first basic properties of the contact times for points in
\mathcal{M}_{c}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

For all

q\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
, we have

\lim_{t\to\alpha_{q}^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=\lim_{t\to\omega_{q}^{-}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0

and, if
\alpha_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
or
\omega_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
, then

q(\alpha_{q})\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
or
q(\omega_{q})\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
, respectively.
Moreover

(a)

V(q(t))>c
for all
t\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
;

(b)

If
\alpha_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
, then
q(t)=q(\alpha_{q})
for all
t\in(-\infty,\alpha_{q}]
;

(c)

If
\omega_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
, then
q(t)=q(\omega_{q})
for all
t\in[\omega_{q},+\infty)
;

(d)

-\infty\leq\alpha_{q}<0<\omega_{q}\leq+\infty
;

and

(3.20)

m_{c}=J_{c}(q)=J_{c,(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})}(q).

Proof.

If
\alpha_{q}=-\infty
we have
\lim_{t\to\alpha_{q}^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0
by Lemma
3.5
. When
\alpha_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
, by definition there exists a sequence
(t_{n})\subset[\alpha_{q},+\infty)
such that
t_{n}\to\alpha_{q}^{+}
and
q(t_{n})\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
. By continuity
q(t_{n})\to q(\alpha_{q})
and since
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
is closed,
q(\alpha_{q})\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
. This yields that if
\alpha_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
we have
q(\alpha_{q})\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and, by continuity
\lim_{t\to\alpha_{q}^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0
. A symmetric argument shows that
\lim_{t\to\omega_{q}^{-}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0
and that
q(\omega_{q})\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
whenever
\omega_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
.

To show (a), note first that
\alpha_{q}<\omega_{q}
. Indeed, if
\alpha_{q}=\omega_{q}
, then, by (
3.19
), both would be finite, and by continuity we would have the contradiction

q(\alpha_{q})=q(\omega_{q})\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\cap\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}=\emptyset
. Moreover if
t\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
, then, by the definition of
\alpha_{q}
and
\omega_{q}
,
q(t)\not\in\mathcal{V}_{c}
and so
V(q(t))>c
for all
t\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
and (a) follows.

To show (b), assume
\alpha_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
and define the function

\tilde{q}(t)
by setting
\tilde{q}(t)=q(\alpha_{q})
for
t\leq\alpha_{q}
, and

\tilde{q}(t)=q(t)
for
t>\alpha_{q}
.
Then
\tilde{q}(t)\in\Gamma_{c}
, so by minimality of
q
we have

m_{c}\leq J_{c}(\tilde{q})=J_{c,(\alpha_{q},+\infty)}(q)\leq J_{c}(q)=m_{c}.

This implies that
J_{c,(-\infty,\alpha_{q})}(q)=0
, and since
V(q(t))-c\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}
, we deduce

\int_{-\infty}^{\alpha_{q}}\Phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)dt=0
. Hence,
\dot{q}(t)=0
almost everywhere on
(-\infty,\alpha_{q})
, and so
q
is constantly equal to
q(\alpha_{q})
on
(-\infty,\alpha_{q})
. A symmetric argument shows also the point (c).

From (b) and (c) we derive that
q(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
and so (d) follows since
\operatorname{dist}(q(0),\mathcal{V}_{c})\geq\frac{\rho_{0}}{8}
by definition of
\mathcal{M}_{c}
. Finally, using (b) and (c) in the finite cases, we always have
J_{c}(q)=J_{c,(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})}(q)
and (
3.20
) follows.
∎

A key feature of the contact times is that the interval
(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
identifies where the minimal trajectory
q\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
is a weak solution of the system (
1.1
).

Lemma 3.9
.

If
q\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
then
q
is a weak solution of the system (
1.1
) on
(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a function with compact support contained in
(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
. Let
\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset[a,b]\subset(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
. We first show that

(3.21)

\text{there exists }h_{\psi}\in(0,1)\text{ such that }\min_{t\in[a,b]}\left(V(q(t)+h\psi(t))\right)>c\quad\text{for any }h\in(0,h_{\psi}).

To prove (
3.21
), assume by contradiction that for any
\epsilon>0
there are
\delta\in(0,\epsilon)
and
t_{\epsilon}\in[a,b]
such that

V(q(t_{\epsilon})+\delta\psi(t_{\epsilon}))\leq c
.
Choosing a sequence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0^{+}
gives corresponding sequences
\delta_{n}\to 0^{+}
and
(t_{n})\subset[a,b]
such that
V(q(t_{n})+\delta_{n}\psi(t_{n}))\leq c
. By compactness, along a subsequence,
t_{n}\to t_{0}\in[a,b]
, and the continuity of
V
and
q
yields, along this subsequence,
V(q(t_{0}))\leq c,

contradicting (a) of Lemma
3.8
and so proving (
3.21
).

By (
3.21
), it follows that
q+h\psi\in\Gamma_{c}
for all
h\in(0,h_{\psi})
. Since
q
minimizes
J_{c}
over
\Gamma_{c}
, we have

(3.22)

J_{c}(q+h\psi)-J_{c}(q)\geq 0\quad\text{for any }h\in(0,h_{\psi}).

We prove, by Lebesgue’s Theorem, that

(3.23)

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{J_{c,(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})}(q+h\psi)-J_{c,(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})}(q)}{h}=\int_{\alpha_{q}}^{\omega_{q}}\left(\phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\dot{q}(t)\dot{\psi}(t)+\nabla V(q(t))\psi(t)\right)dt.

To prove (
3.23
), we write

\frac{J_{c,(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})}(q+h\psi)-J_{c,(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})}(q)}{h}=\int_{\alpha_{q}}^{\omega_{q}}\frac{\Phi(|\dot{q}+h\dot{\psi}|)-\Phi(|\dot{q}|)}{h}\,dt+\int_{\alpha_{q}}^{\omega_{q}}\frac{V(q+h\psi)-V(q)}{h}\,dt.

Since
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
and
\psi
has compact support in

(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
, it is standard that

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\int_{\alpha_{q}}^{\omega_{q}}\frac{V(q+h\psi)-V(q)}{h}\,dt=\int_{\alpha_{q}}^{\omega_{q}}\nabla V(q(t))\,\psi(t)\,dt.

It therefore remains to justify that

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\int_{\alpha_{q}}^{\omega_{q}}\frac{\Phi(|\dot{q}+h\dot{\psi}|)-\Phi(|\dot{q}|)}{h}\,dt=\int_{\alpha_{q}}^{\omega_{q}}\phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\,\dot{q}(t)\dot{\psi}(t)\,dt.

For a.e.
t\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
the function

f_{t}(h)=\Phi(|\dot{q}(t)+h\dot{\psi}(t)|)

is of class
C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
, with derivative

\frac{d}{dh}f_{t}(h)=\phi(|\dot{q}(t)+h\dot{\psi}(t)|)\,\big(\dot{q}(t)+h\dot{\psi}(t)\big)\cdot\dot{\psi}(t).

By the Mean Value Theorem, for every
h\in(0,h_{\psi})
and for a.e.
t\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
there exists

\eta_{h}=\eta_{h}(t)
with
0<\eta_{h}(t)<h
such that

(3.24)

\big|\frac{\Phi(|\dot{q}+h\dot{\psi}|)-\Phi(|\dot{q}|)}{h}\big|=\big|\frac{f_{t}(h)-f_{t}(0)}{h}\big|=\phi(|\dot{q}+\eta_{h}\dot{\psi}|)\,|\dot{q}+\eta_{h}\dot{\psi}|\,|\dot{\psi}|\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q}).

By (
2.3
) there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that, for all
s\geq 0
,

(3.25)

\phi(s)s\leq m\Phi(s)+C_{0}.

Since
\eta_{h}<h_{\psi}<1
, by (
2.4
) we have furthermore

(3.26)

\Phi(|\dot{q}+\eta_{h}\dot{\psi}|)\leq 2^{m-1}(\Phi(|\dot{q}|)+\Phi(|\dot{\psi}|)).

Combining (
3.24
), (
3.25
) and (
3.26
)
we obtain that there exists
C>0
such that

\big|\frac{\Phi(|\dot{q}+h\dot{\psi}|)-\Phi(|\dot{q}|)}{h}\big|\leq C(\Phi(|\dot{q}|)+\Phi(|\dot{\psi}|)+1)\,|\dot{\psi}|\text{ for any }h\in(0,h_{\psi}).

Since
\Phi(|\dot{q}|)\in L^{1}((\alpha_{q},\omega_{q});\mathbb{R})
and

\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((\alpha_{q},\omega_{q});\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the right hand side belongs to
L^{1}((\alpha_{q},\omega_{q});\mathbb{R})
, being independent of
h
.
Therefore, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\int_{\alpha_{q}}^{\omega_{q}}\frac{\Phi(|\dot{q}+h\dot{\psi}|)-\Phi(|\dot{q}|)}{h}\,dt=\int_{\alpha_{q}}^{\omega_{q}}\phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\,\dot{q}(t)\dot{\psi}(t)\,dt.

and (
3.23
) follows.

Now we observe that, since
\psi
has compact support in
(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
, by (
3.22
)

J_{c}(q+h\psi)-J_{c}(q)=J_{c,(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})}(q+h\psi)-J_{c,(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})}(q)\geq 0\text{ for all }h\in(0,h_{\psi}),

and (
3.23
) yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\dot{q}(t)\dot{\psi}(t)+\nabla V(q(t))\psi(t)\right)dt\geq 0.

Applying the same reasoning for the test function
-\psi
, we get the reversed inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\dot{q}(t)\dot{\psi}(t)+\nabla V(q(t))\psi(t)\right)dt\leq 0,

which allows us to conclude

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\dot{q}(t)\dot{\psi}(t)+\nabla V(q(t))\psi(t)\right)dt=0.

Since
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((\alpha_{q},\omega_{q});\mathbb{R}^{N})
is arbitrary,
q
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) on
(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
.
∎

Starting from the solutions provided by Lemma
3.9
, our aim is to recover entire connecting-orbit weak solutions of (
1.1
), possibly by employing reflection and/or periodic continuation when the contact times are finite. A key step in this construction is to show that these solutions have constant energy equal to
-c
on the interval
(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
.

To be more detailed, consider the Lagrangian associate with (
1.1
),

L(q,\dot{q})=\Phi(|\dot{q}|)+V(q)
.
The conjugate momentum is
p=\nabla_{\dot{q}}L=\phi(|\dot{q}|)\,\dot{q}
and the Hamiltonian is given by

H(p,q)=\widetilde{\Phi}(|p|)-V(q)
,
where
\widetilde{\Phi}
is the Legendre transform, i.e., the complementary function, of
\Phi
. Since
\Phi
is convex,
\widetilde{\Phi}(y)=ys-\Phi(s)
for
y=\Phi^{\prime}(s)=\phi(s)s
. Hence, along the trajectory
q(t)

\widetilde{\Phi}(|p(t)|)=\widetilde{\Phi}(\phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\,|\dot{q}(t)|)=\phi(|\dot{q}(t)|)\,|\dot{q}(t)|^{2}-\Phi(|\dot{q}(t)|).

This allows us to define the energy function along trajectories as

E_{q}(t)=H(p(t),q(t))=G(|\dot{q}(t)|)-V(q(t))

where

G(s)=\phi(s)\,s^{2}-\Phi(s).

We remark that, by (
2.1
),
G
satisfies

(3.27)

(l-1)\Phi(s)\leq G(s)\leq(m-1)\Phi(s)\quad\text{for all}\quad s\geq 0,

and
G(s)=0
if and only if
s=0
.

Note that the conservation of energy along the trajectories
q
given by Lemma
3.9
cannot be deduced from the classical arguments of the calculus of variations. Indeed, our assumptions do not guarantee that the second derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to
\dot{q}
,
D^{2}_{\dot{q}}L(q,\dot{q})=D^{2}_{\dot{q}}\Phi(|\dot{q}|)
, is uniformly positive definite, nor are the functions
q
classical
C^{2}
solutions of the system. Our variational framework, however, allows us to establish both a weak form of the conservation of energy and its exact value by considering suitable variations of the action along dilations of
q
.

Lemma 3.10
.

For every
q\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
, it turns out that
E_{q}(t)=-c
for a.e.
t\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
.

Proof.

Let
q\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
. We begin by proving that for any
r,h\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
with
r<h
, it holds

(3.28)

\int_{r}^{h}(V(q(t))-c)\,dt=\int_{r}^{h}G(|\dot{q}(t)|)\,dt.

We consider the translated function
\tilde{q}=q(\cdot+r)
observing that
\tilde{q}\in\Gamma_{c}
and
J_{c}(\tilde{q})=m_{c}
.
For any
s>0
we define the function

q_{s}(t)=\begin{cases}\tilde{q}(t),&t\leq 0,\\
\tilde{q}\left(\frac{t}{s}\right),&t>0.\end{cases}

By construction,

q_{s}\in\Gamma_{c}
,
\alpha_{q_{s}}=\alpha_{\tilde{q}}
and
\omega_{q_{s}}=s\omega_{\tilde{q}}
.
Then by (
3.20
)

J_{c,(\alpha_{\tilde{q}},s\omega_{\tilde{q}})}(q_{s})\geq m_{c}=J_{c,(\alpha_{\tilde{q}},\omega_{\tilde{q}})}(\tilde{q}).

This inequality implies

0\leq\int_{0}^{s\omega_{\tilde{q}}}(\Phi(|\dot{q_{s}}(t)|)+(V(q_{s}(t))-c))\,dt-\int_{0}^{\omega_{\tilde{q}}}(\Phi(|\dot{{\tilde{q}}}(t)|)+(V({\tilde{q}}(t))-c))\,dt.

Since
\dot{q_{s}}(t)=\frac{1}{s}\dot{{\tilde{q}}}(\frac{t}{s})
for
t>0
, making the change of variable
x=\frac{t}{s}
, we have

0\leq\int_{0}^{\omega_{\tilde{q}}}\left(s\Phi(\tfrac{1}{s}|\dot{{\tilde{q}}}(t)|)-\Phi(|\dot{{\tilde{q}}}(t)|)+(s-1)(V({\tilde{q}}(t))-c)\right)\,dt.

Denoting by
f(s)
the right hand side of the above inequality
we have
f(s)\geq 0
for all
s>0
and
f(1)=0
so that
1
is a minimum of
f
. We claim that
f
is differentiable at
s=1
with

(3.29)

f^{\prime}(1)=\int_{0}^{\omega_{\tilde{q}}}\left(\Phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}|)-\phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}|)|\dot{\tilde{q}}|^{2}+(V({\tilde{q}})-c)\right)dt.

For
s\neq 1
we write the incremental quotient

\frac{f(s)}{s-1}=\int_{0}^{\omega_{\tilde{q}}}\big(\frac{s\,\Phi(\tfrac{1}{s}|\dot{\tilde{q}}|)-\Phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}|)}{s-1}+(V({\tilde{q}})-c)\big)dt.

We prove that there exists
C>0
such that for every
a\geq 0
and every

s\in[1/2,2]
,

(3.30)

\big|\frac{s\,\Phi(a/s)-\Phi(a)}{s-1}\big|\leq C\,\Phi(a).

If
a=0
the inequality is trivial. Let
a>0
and set

F(s)=s\,\Phi(a/s)
for

F\in C^{1}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R})
and

F^{\prime}(s)=\Phi(a/s)-\tfrac{1}{s^{2}}\phi(a/s)a^{2}
.
By the mean value theorem, for every

there exists

such that

\frac{F(s)-F(1)}{s-1}=F^{\prime}(\theta)=\Phi(a/\theta)-\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\phi(a/\theta)a^{2}.

If
s\in[1/2,2]
, then also
\theta\in[1/2,2]
.
By (
2.1
) we have

\phi(a/\theta)(a/\theta)^{2}\leq m\,\Phi(a/\theta)
, and so
since

\theta\in[1/2,2]
,
(
2.2

\big|\Phi(a/\theta)-\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\phi(a/\theta)a^{2}\big|\leq(1+m)\Phi(a/\theta)\leq(1+m)2^{m}\,\Phi(a),

which proves (
3.30
).

For
a=|\dot{\tilde{q}}(t)|
,
(
3.30
) yields that for any

\big|\frac{s\,\Phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}(t)|/s)-\Phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}(t)|)}{s-1}\big|\leq C\,\Phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}|),\text{ for a.e. }t\in(0,\omega_{\tilde{q}}).

Since
J_{c}(q)=m_{c}<\infty
, we have
\Phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}|)\in L^{1}((0,\omega_{\tilde{q}});\mathbb{R})
and also

V({\tilde{q}})-c\in L^{1}((0,\omega_{\tilde{q}});\mathbb{R})
. Then, by dominated convergence

\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{f(s)}{s-1}=\int_{0}^{\omega_{\tilde{q}}}\Big(\Phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}|)-\phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}|)|\dot{\tilde{q}}|^{2}+(V({\tilde{q}})-c)\Big)dt

proving (
3.29
). Since
1
is a minimum point of
f
we have
f^{\prime}(1)=0
and so

\int_{0}^{\omega_{\tilde{q}}}\big(V({\tilde{q}}(t))-c\big)\,dt=\int_{0}^{\omega_{\tilde{q}}}\big(\phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}(t)|)|\dot{\tilde{q}}(t)|^{2}-\Phi(|\dot{\tilde{q}}(t)|)\big)\,dt=\int_{0}^{\omega_{\tilde{q}}}G(|\dot{\tilde{q}}(t)|)\,dt,

where the last equality follows from the definition of
G
.

Returning to the original function
q={\tilde{q}}(\cdot-r)
, this shows that
\int_{r}^{\omega_{q}}(V(q(t))-c)\,dt=\int_{r}^{\omega_{q}}G(|\dot{q}(t)|)\,dt,
for any
r\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
, and since
\int_{r}^{h}=\int_{r}^{\omega_{q}}-\int_{h}^{\omega_{q}}
,
(
3.28
) follows.

Dividing both sides of the equality in (
3.28
) by
h-r
gives

\frac{1}{h-r}\int_{r}^{h}(V(q(t))-c)\,dt=\frac{1}{h-r}\int_{r}^{h}G(|\dot{q}(t)|)\,dt.

Therefore, since both
V(q(t))-c
and
G(|\dot{q}(t)|)
belong to
L^{1}((\alpha_{q},\omega_{q});\mathbb{R})
, the Lebesgue point Theorem
yields

V(q(t_{0}))-c=G(|\dot{q}(t_{0})|)\text{ for a.e. }t_{0}\in(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q}).

Consequently,
E_{q}(t)=-c
a.e. on
(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q})
, completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

By item (a) of Lemma

then

for all

Thanks to these properties, and since by Lemma

.

Lemma 3.11
.

Assume
(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
.
If

, then

Proof.

Let

.
Recall that by Lemma

(g(\dot{q}(t)))^{\prime}=\nabla V(q(t))\quad\text{a.e. on }(\alpha_{q},\omega_{q}).

(3.31)

Since by Lemma
3.8

Since

(3.33)

From (

for some fixed

By Lemma

.
Since, as observed above,

is a classical solution of (

) on

. By the regularity of

is continuous on

a.e. on

for all

We are now in a position to state and prove the main result of this section.
Let us recall the definition of bounded connecting orbit.

Definition 3.12
.

Assume that condition
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
holds for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
I=(\alpha,\omega)\subset\mathbb{R}
, with
-\infty\leq\alpha<\omega\leq+\infty
, and denote by

.
We say that

–connection between

and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
with contact times

and

and solves (

) on

;

(ii)

and

;

\lim_{t\to\alpha^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=\lim_{t\to\omega^{-}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0;

We say moreover that

is a
minimal

c
–connection between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
if it also satisfies

(iv)

J_{c,I}(q)=m_{c}
.

Remark 3.13
.

Note that if
I=(\alpha,\omega)\subset\mathbb{R}
and

is a

–connection between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
then it satisfies
\lim_{t\to\alpha^{+}}|\dot{q}(t)|=\lim_{t\to\omega^{-}}|\dot{q}(t)|=0.

Indeed, by (iii) we have
\lim_{t\to\alpha^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0
.
Hence, for any sequence
t_{n}\to\alpha^{+}
, we have
\operatorname{dist}(q(t_{n}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})\to 0
.
Since
q\in L^{\infty}(I;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
is closed, up to a subsequence
q(t_{n})\to\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
.
Then
V(q(t_{n}))\to V(\xi)\leq c
while
V(q(t_{n}))\geq c
, so
V(q(t_{n}))\to c
.
By (ii),
G(|\dot{q}(t_{n})|)=V(q(t_{n}))-c\to 0
, and by (
3.27
) this implies
\dot{q}(t_{n})\to 0
.
Since
(t_{n})
was arbitrary, this yields
\dot{q}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\alpha^{+}
.

An analogous reasoning applies to show also that
\dot{q}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\omega^{-}
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Assume
(\phi_{1})
–
(\phi_{2})
. Let

and let

be such that

and

Then (
1.1
) has a minimal c-connection between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}

Proof.

By Lemma

with

. Consider the restriction

. By Lemma

and solves (

for all

3.3.
From
c
–connections to entire connecting orbits

In the previous subsections we proved the existence of minimal
c
–connections
between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
(Theorem
3.14
)
and established their main structural properties.
In particular, any minimal
c
–connection is a classical solution of (
1.1
)
on its interior interval and has constant energy
-c
.

In this subsection we prove that every
c
–connection can be extended to an entire
weak solution of (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}
.
Depending on whether none, one, or both contact times are finite,
the resulting global solution is respectively of heteroclinic, homoclinic,
or brake type.

Remark 3.15
.

In the quasilinear case, the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) may fail to
be uniquely solvable at points where
\dot{q}(t_{0})=0
(in particular when the
map
v\mapsto\phi(|v|)v
is degenerate or singular at
v=0
).
Therefore, global continuations across contact times need not be unique in general.
In what follows we adopt the canonical extension obtained by time–reflection
at each finite contact time (and periodic continuation when both contact times
are finite), which is natural in view of the symmetry induced by the energy
constraint.

Let
q:\bar{I}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a
c
–connection, where
I=(\alpha,\omega)

with
-\infty\leq\alpha<\omega\leq+\infty
.
We shall continue to refer to
\alpha
and
\omega
as the contact times of
q
.

We first consider the case in which the contact times are infinite,
that is
\alpha=-\infty
and
\omega=+\infty
.
In this situation the connection is already defined on the whole line and,
by the structural properties of
c
–connections established above,

q\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})

is a classical entire solution of (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying, by definition and Remark
3.13
,

E_{q}(t)=-c\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R},\qquad V(q(t))>c\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\dot{q}(t)=0.

We refer to such solutions as
heteroclinic–type
,
since they have no finite contact times and connect the two sets

\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
at infinity.

We now consider the case in which at least one contact time is finite.
In this situation an entire connecting solution can be obtained by
reflecting the
c
–connection at the finite contact time(s) and,
if necessary, by periodic continuation on
\mathbb{R}
.

More precisely, let
q
be a
c
–connection with contact times

\alpha
and
\omega
.

If
\alpha>-\infty

(3.34)

q_{-}(t)=\begin{cases}q(2\alpha-t)&\text{if }t\in(2\alpha-\omega,\alpha),\\[4.0pt]

which extends

to the left of
\alpha

If

(3.35)

q_{+}(t)=\begin{cases}q(t)&\text{if }t\in(\alpha,\omega],\\[4.0pt]

q
to the right of
\omega
.

The functions

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
q
be a

\alpha
and
\omega
.

(a)

If

, then

(i)

(b)

We prove (a), the argument for (b) is symmetric.

Suppose that
\alpha>-\infty

coincides with

(\alpha,\omega)
and with its reflection on

).
This proves (i).

Point (ii) follows by symmetry and by the properties of

Indeed, since
q
is continuous at
\alpha

q_{-}
.
Moreover, by Remark
3.13

\dot{q}(t)\to 0
as

\dot{q}_{-}(t)\to 0

Hence

\dot{q}(t)\to 0
and
\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})\to 0

.
Hence, by symmetry of the construction, we obtain

\dot{q}_{-}(t)\to 0

(\phi(|\dot{q}_{-}(t)|)\dot{q}_{-}(t))^{\prime}=\nabla V(q_{-}(t))

\displaystyle 0

Finally, the energy

on
(\alpha,\omega)
by the defining properties of a
c
–connection.
By symmetry, the same holds on
(2\alpha-\omega,\alpha)
.
Hence, since
q_{-}\in C^{1}((2\alpha-\omega,\omega);\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
we conclude that

E_{q_{-}}(t)=-c
for all
t\in(2\alpha-\omega,\omega)
.
∎

In particular, if a
c
–connection has exactly one finite contact time,
the corresponding reflected trajectory provides an entire weak solution
of (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}
.

More precisely, as a direct consequence of Lemma
3.16
, we obtain the
following result.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
q
be a
c
–connection with contact times
\alpha
and
\omega
.

(a)

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega=+\infty
, then

q_{c}\equiv q_{-}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\alpha\};\mathbb{R}^{N})

is an entire weak solution of (

and
(\alpha,+\infty)
.
Moreover,

has constant energy

\dot{q}_{c}(\alpha)=0,\quad q_{c}(\alpha)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-},\quad V(q_{c}(t))>c\text{ if }t\neq\alpha,\quad q_{c}(\alpha-t)=q_{c}(\alpha+t)\text{ on }\mathbb{R},

together with the asymptotic properties

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\dot{q}_{c}(t)=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q_{c}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})=0.

(b)

If
\alpha=-\infty
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, then

q_{c}\equiv q_{+}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\omega\};\mathbb{R}^{N})

is an entire weak solution of (
1.1
), classical on

(-\infty,\omega)
and
(\omega,+\infty)
.
Moreover,
q_{c}
has constant energy

and satisfies

\dot{q}_{c}(\omega)=0,\quad q_{c}(\omega)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+},\quad V(q_{c}(t))>c\text{ if }t\neq\omega,\quad q_{c}(\omega-t)=q_{c}(\omega+t)\text{ on }\mathbb{R},

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\dot{q}_{c}(t)=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q_{c}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0.

We refer to these solutions as
homoclinic–type
solutions.
They are obtained by reflection when only one contact time is finite.
They have constant energy
-c
, are symmetric with respect to the
finite contact time at which they touch one of the sets

\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}
, and are asymptotic to the other set with
vanishing velocity at infinity.

We now consider the case in which both contact times are finite.
In this situation, the reflected solution can be further continued by periodic
extension to an entire periodic weak solution of (
1.1
).
More precisely

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
q
be a
c
–connection with contact times
\alpha,\omega\in\mathbb{R}

and
\alpha<\omega
.
Then there exists a
T=2(\omega-\alpha)
–periodic weak solution

q_{c}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
to (
1.1
) satisfying the energy identity

E_{q_{c}}(t)=G(|\dot{q}_{c}(t)|)-V(q_{c}(t))=-c\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

and the following properties:

(a)

q_{c}=q
on
[\alpha,\omega]
;

(b)

V(q_{c}(\alpha))=V(q_{c}(\omega))=c
and

V(q_{c}(t))>c
for every

;

(c)

q_{c}(\alpha)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
,

q_{c}(\omega)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
, and

\dot{q}_{c}(\alpha)=\dot{q}_{c}(\omega)=0
;

(d)

q_{c}
is symmetric with respect to
\alpha
and
\omega
, namely

q_{c}(\alpha-t)=q_{c}(\alpha+t),\qquad q_{c}(\omega-t)=q_{c}(\omega+t)\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Let
q
be a
c
–connection with
-\infty<\alpha<\omega<+\infty
.
By Lemma
3.16
,
q_{+}
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) on
(\alpha,2\omega-\alpha)
, classical on
(\alpha,\omega)
and
(\omega,2\omega-\alpha)
. Since
q
is continuous on
[\alpha,\omega]
, we can extend
q_{+}
continuously at
\alpha
and
2\omega-\alpha

q_{+}(\alpha)=q_{+}(2\omega-\alpha)=q(\alpha)
.
By symmetry and Remark
3.13
we obtain

(3.36)

0=D_{+}q(\alpha)=D_{+}q_{+}(\alpha)=D_{-}q_{+}(2\omega-\alpha).

Since
q_{+}(\alpha)=q_{+}(2\omega-\alpha)
, the periodic extension with period

T=2(\omega-\alpha)
,

q_{c}(t+kT)=q_{+}(t),\qquad\forall t\in[\alpha,\alpha+T),\ k\in\mathbb{Z},

is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, by (
3.36
) we have

q_{c}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
with

\dot{q}_{c}(\alpha)=\dot{q}_{c}(\omega)=0
.

By construction and Lemma
3.16
,
q_{c}
satisfies properties (a)–(d) and the energy identity. It remains to check that
q_{c}
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}
. To this aim we argue as in Lemma
3.16
.

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
q_{c}
coincides with a translation of
q_{+}
on the interval
[\alpha+kT,2\omega-\alpha+kT]
.
Hence
q_{c}

is a classical solution on the subintervals
A_{k}=(\alpha+kT,\omega+kT)
and
B_{k}=(\omega+kT,2\omega-\alpha+kT)

Multiplying the equation
(\phi(|\dot{q}_{c}|)\dot{q}_{c})^{\prime}=\nabla V(q_{c})
by
\psi
and integrating by parts on
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
, the boundary terms at the endpoints vanish because
\dot{q}_{c}=0
there. Thus, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}

\int_{A_{k}}(\phi(|\dot{q}_{c}(t)|)\dot{q}_{c}(t)\cdot\dot{\psi}(t)+\nabla V(q_{c}(t))\cdot\psi(t))\,dt=\int_{B_{k}}(\phi(|\dot{q}_{c}(t)|)\dot{q}_{c}(t)\cdot\dot{\psi}(t)+\nabla V(q_{c}(t))\cdot\psi(t))\,dt=0.

Hence
we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi(|\dot{q}_{c}(t)|)\dot{q}_{c}(t)\cdot\dot{\psi}(t)+\nabla V(q_{c}(t))\cdot\psi(t))\,dt=0,

so that

is a weak solution of (

) on
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

We refer to these solutions as
brake–type
solutions.
They are obtained from
c
–connections which have both contact times finite,
by reflection and periodic continuation.
These solutions are

–periodic with

and symmetric with respect to the two contact times, where they satisfy
q_{c}(\alpha)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
with
\dot{q}_{c}(\alpha)=0
and
q_{c}(\omega)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
with
\dot{q}_{c}(\omega)=0
.
This results in a periodic, symmetric back-and-forth oscillation between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
, with velocity tending to zero while reaching these sets.
They have constant energy
-c
, solve (
1.1
) classically outside the contact times, and are
C^{1}
weak solutions of (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}
.

We conclude this section by examining additional asymptotic properties of heteroclinic– and homoclinic–type orbits.
We have already established that if a
c
–connection has at most one finite contact time, then the corresponding entire solution
q_{c}
is either heteroclinic or homoclinic.
In both cases, it satisfies

\operatorname{dist}(q_{c}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c})\to 0\text{ and }\dot{q}_{c}(t)\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to\pm\infty.

Note moreover that, since by definition
q
is bounded, we also have by construction that
\|q_{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}<+\infty
.

We consider the
\alpha
- and
\omega
-limit sets of
q_{c}
, the set of accumulation points of
q_{c}(t)
respectively as
t\to-\infty
and
t\to+\infty
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{q_{c}}

\displaystyle:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid\exists(t_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }t_{n}\to-\infty\text{ and }\xi=\lim_{n\to+\infty}q_{c}(t_{n})\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{q_{c}}

\displaystyle:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid\exists(t_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }t_{n}\to+\infty\text{ and }\xi=\lim_{n\to+\infty}q_{c}(t_{n})\}.

They are both closed and connected. Moreover, the boundedness of
q_{c}
ensures that
they are nonempty.

Indeed, assume

(t_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
with
t_{n}\to+\infty
. Since
\|q_{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}<+\infty
, along a subsequence (denoted again
(t_{n})
), we have that
q_{c}(t_{n})\to\xi
for some
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By definition
\xi\in\Omega_{q_{c}}
, hence
\Omega_{q_{c}}\neq\emptyset
.
By the same reasoning, taking
(t_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
with
t_{n}\to-\infty
, we obtain that the sequence
q_{c}(t_{n})
accumulates at some point
\zeta\in\mathcal{A}_{q_{c}}\neq\emptyset
.

A further property of the limit sets of
q_{c}
is that

(3.37)

\mathcal{A}_{q_{c}}\cup\Omega_{q_{c}}\subset\overline{B_{R}(0)}\cap\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid V(\xi)=c\}.

Indeed, let
\xi\in\Omega_{q_{c}}
. By definition there exists
(t_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
with
t_{n}\to+\infty
such that
q_{c}(t_{n})\to\xi
as
n\to+\infty
. Since
|q_{c}(t_{n})|\leq R
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that
\xi\in\overline{B_{R}(0)}
. Moreover, since
q_{c}
is a heteroclinic or a homoclinic type solution, we know that
\operatorname{dist}(q_{c}(t_{n}),\mathcal{V}_{c})\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
. Hence

\operatorname{dist}(\xi,\mathcal{V}_{c})=0
, so that
\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{c}
, since
\mathcal{V}_{c}
is closed. Finally,
V(q_{c}(t_{n}))\geq c
for all
n
, and by continuity of
V
we have
V(q_{c}(t_{n}))\to V(\xi)
. Since
\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{c}
, we have
V(\xi)\leq c
, and therefore
V(\xi)=c
.
Thus
\Omega_{q_{c}}\subset\overline{B_{R}(0)}\cap\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid V(\xi)=c\}
. The same argument applies to
\mathcal{A}_{q_{c}}
, proving (
3.37
).

A final property of the limit sets of
q_{c}
is that they consist of critical points of
V

(3.38)

\mathcal{A}_{q_{c}}\cup\Omega_{q_{c}}\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid\nabla V(\xi)=0\}

Indeed, let
\xi\in\Omega_{q_{c}}
and
(t_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
be such that
t_{n}\to+\infty
and
q_{c}(t_{n})\to\xi
as
n\to+\infty
. Fix
t_{0}>0
. Since
q_{c}
satisfies
\dot{q}_{c}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\pm\infty
, we have that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}q_{c}(t_{n}+s)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}q_{c}(t_{n})+\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+s}\dot{q_{c}}(t)\,dt=\xi\text{ uniformly in }s\in[0,t_{0}].

Hence, by continuity,
\nabla V(q_{c}(t_{n}+s))\to\nabla V(\xi)
uniformly in
s\in[0,t_{0}]
. Since
q_{c}
is a classical solution of (
1.1
) outside the possible contact point (which is unique in the homoclinic–type case and absent in the heteroclinic–type case), we derive

(3.39)

\left(\phi(|\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n}+s)|)\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n}+s)\right)^{\prime}=\nabla V(q_{c}(t_{n}+s))\text{ for any }s\in[0,t_{0}].

As
\phi(|u|)u\to 0
for
u\to 0
and
\dot{q_{c}}(t)\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
, we have moreover

\phi(|\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n}+t_{0})|)\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n}+t_{0})-\phi(|\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n})|)\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n})\to 0\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

Since

\phi(|\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n}+t_{0})|)\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n}+t_{0})-\phi(|\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n})|)\dot{q_{c}}(t_{n})=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+t_{0}}\left(\phi(|\dot{q_{c}}(s)|)\dot{q_{c}}(s)\right)^{\prime}\,ds,

by integrating both sides of (
3.39
) with respect to
s
on the interval
[0,t_{0}]
, we conclude that

t_{0}\nabla V(\xi)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+t_{0}}\left(\phi(|\dot{q_{c}}(s)|)\dot{q_{c}}(s)\right)^{\prime}\,ds=0.

Since
t_{0}>0
, it follows that
\nabla V(\xi)=0
. The same argument applies to show the inclusion also for

\mathcal{A}_{q_{c}}
, proving (
3.38
).

Remark 3.19
.

As we have seen in this section, for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
for which
(
\mathcal{V}_{c}
)
and
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
hold true, there exists an entire connecting solution
q_{c}
with energy
-c
, which may exhibit different behaviours depending on whether it is of brake, homoclinic or heteroclinic type.
From (
3.37
) and (
3.38
) we deduce that
q_{c}
can be of heteroclinic or homoclinic type solution only if

is a critical value of
V
. When
c
is a regular value of
V
, then
q_{c}
is necessarily a brake orbit solution.

Remark 3.20
.

As discussed above, the solutions
q_{c}
may lose

regularity at the contact
times, where
\dot{q}_{c}(t)=0
. This happens because the general assumptions

(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
alone are not sufficient
to guarantee that the function
g(\xi)=\phi(|\xi|)\xi
for
\xi\neq 0
, extended by continuity
with

, is differentiable at
\xi=0
. Consequently, the local invertibility
argument used in the proof of Lemma
3.11
does not apply.

This issue can be resolved if

is a

function
satisfying
(\phi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2})
, together with
the additional assumption
\phi(0)>0
. In that case, the computation in the proof of

shows that

also at

,

, the argument
of Lemma

are in fact global

solutions of (

) on

. Moreover, the continuation across the contact

is the unique global extension of the given

–connection.

4.
Applications to Classical Potentials

Following
[
5
]
, we illustrate how the abstract variational
framework developed in Section
3
applies to classical
models such as double–well, Duffing–type, and multiple pendulum
potentials. Such potentials are commonly considered in
the variational literature on homoclinic and heteroclinic solutions, see,
e.g.,
[
19
,
12
,
30
,
31
,
6
,
4
,
13
,
35
]
.

We show that these models fit naturally into the abstract framework
introduced in Section
3
. In particular, both the separation
condition
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
and the coercivity condition
(\mathcal{J}_{c})

hold for all values of
c
below a natural threshold
c^{*}
determined
by the geometry of the potential
V
. Hence the existence results
established in Section
3
apply to all such values of
c

and provide connecting trajectories with prescribed energy
-c
for the
quasilinear system (

).

As in
[
5
]
, the method also allows one to address multiplicity
issues. This aspect will be illustrated in the case of multiple
pendulum–type systems, where the sublevel set
\mathcal{V}_{c}
admits
different admissible decompositions into well–separated subsets,
leading correspondingly to distinct connecting orbits at the same
prescribed energy level.

4.1.
Double-well potential systems

Throughout this subsection, we assume that the potential function
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
and satisfies the following conditions:

(V_{1})

There exist two distinct points
a^{-},a^{+}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

V(a^{+})=V(a^{-})=0,\quad\text{and}\quad V(x)>0\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{a^{-},a^{+}\}.

(V_{2})

The potential is bounded away from zero at infinity, namely

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}V(x)=:\nu_{0}>0.

These conditions describe a typical
double–well potential
, where
V

attains its minimum at two isolated points, is positive elsewhere, and is
uniformly positive at infinity.

Let
\bar{r}=\tfrac{1}{2}|a^{-}-a^{+}|
and set

c^{*}:=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bigl(B_{\bar{r}}(a^{-})\cup B_{\bar{r}}(a^{+})\bigr)}V(x).

Remark 4.1
.

By
(V_{1})
and
(V_{2})
one has

c^{*}\in(0,\nu_{0}].

Indeed
c^{*}\leq\nu_{0}
by definition of
\nu_{0}
.
To prove that
c^{*}>0
, assume by contradiction that
V(x_{n})\to 0
for some

(x_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bigl(B_{\bar{r}}(a^{-})\cup B_{\bar{r}}(a^{+})\bigr)
.
Then
(x_{n})
is bounded by
(V_{2})
and, up to a subsequence,
x_{n}\to x_{0}
.
Hence
V(x_{0})=0
while
x_{0}\neq a^{\pm}
, contradicting
(V_{1})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
satisfies
(V_{1})
and
(V_{2})
, then

(a)

For every
c\in[0,c^{*})
, condition
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
holds with respect to two disjoint compact sets
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
such that

a^{+}\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}\subset B_{\bar{r}}(a^{+})\quad\text{and}\quad a^{-}\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\subset B_{\bar{r}}(a^{-}).

In particular, in the case
c=0
,
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}=\{a^{-}\}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}=\{a^{+}\}
.

(b)

For every
c\in[0,c^{*})
, the coercivity condition
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
holds.

Proof.

(a)

If
c<c^{*}
, then
V>c
on

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bigl(B_{\bar{r}}(a^{-})\cup B_{\bar{r}}(a^{+})\bigr)
, and therefore

\mathcal{V}_{c}\subset B_{\bar{r}}(a^{-})\cup B_{\bar{r}}(a^{+}).

Set

\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}:=\mathcal{V}_{c}\cap B_{\bar{r}}(a^{-}),\qquad\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}:=\mathcal{V}_{c}\cap B_{\bar{r}}(a^{+}).

Then
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}
are compact and disjoint, and satisfy

\mathcal{V}_{c}=\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\cup\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
. For
c=0
, by
(V_{1})
one has

\mathcal{V}_{0}^{-}=\{a^{-}\}
and
\mathcal{V}_{0}^{+}=\{a^{+}\}
.

(b)
Fix
c\in[0,c^{*})
. Since
c<c^{*}\leq\nu_{0}
we have
\mu=(\nu_{0}-c)/2>0
, and
there exists
R_{0}>0
such that

V(x)\geq c+\mu
for all
|x|\geq R_{0}
.
Suppose by contradiction that
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
fails.
Then there exists
(q_{n})\subset\Gamma_{c}
such that

J_{c}(q_{n})\to m_{c}
and

\|q_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to+\infty
.
Since
\|q_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to+\infty
, for any
R>R_{0}
and
n
large, there exist
s_{n}<t_{n}
such that

|q_{n}(t_{n})-q_{n}(s_{n})|\geq R-R_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad V(q_{n}(t))\geq c+\mu\quad\text{for all }t\in(s_{n},t_{n}).

By Lemma
3.2
, there exists
\lambda_{\mu}>0
such that

J_{c}(q_{n})\geq J_{c,(s_{n},t_{n})}(q_{n})\geq\lambda_{\mu}\,H(R-R_{0}).

Since
H(r)\to+\infty
as
r\to+\infty
, letting
R\to+\infty
yields

m_{c}=+\infty
, a contradiction. Hence
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
holds.
∎

The previous lemma shows that, for every
c<c^{*}
, the potential
V

satisfies the separation condition
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
and the coercivity
condition
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
. Hence Theorem
3.14
applies.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
satisfies
(V_{1})
and
(V_{2})
.
Then for every
c\in[0,c^{*})
there exist

with

between

and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
.
In particular,

and solves (

) on

for all

By the extension results of Subsection

,
this connection generates an entire weak solution

of (

)
with constant energy

.

By Remark

, if

is a regular value of

,
then the extended weak solution

4.2.
Duffing-like systems

In this subsection we assume that the potential
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
and satifies

(
V_{3}
)

There exists
r_{0}>0
such that

V(x)>0\quad\text{for any }\penalty\ x\in B_{4r_{0}}(0)\setminus\{0\},

that is,
V
has a strict local minimum at
x_{0}:=0
with value
V(0)=0
.

(
V_{4}
)

The set

C_{0}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:V(x)>0\right\}\cup\{0\},

is bounded and satisfies
\nabla V(x)\neq 0
for any
x\in\partial C_{0}
.

These assumptions describe a typical
Duffing–type potential
, in which

V
has a strict isolated minimum at the origin,
is positive in a bounded region around it,
and changes sign across the boundary of this region.

Remark 4.4
.

Since
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
and
\nabla V(x)\neq 0
for every

x\in\partial C_{0}
, the set
\partial C_{0}
is a compact
C^{1}
hypersurface.
Moreover, denoting

\nu_{x}=-\frac{\nabla V(x)}{\|\nabla V(x)\|}

the outward unit normal, compactness implies that there exists

v_{0}>0
such that

\nabla V(x)\cdot\nu_{x}=-\|\nabla V(x)\|\leq-v_{0}

for all
x\in\partial C_{0}
.
As a consequence, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

(4.1)

V(x)<0\text{ whenever
}0<\operatorname{dist}(x,\overline{C}_{0})<\delta_{0}.

As in the double–well case, the geometry of the potential
V

naturally determines a threshold value
c^{*}
.
We set

\bar{r}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dist}(0,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus C_{0})
,
noting that by
(V_{3})
one has

B_{4r_{0}}(0)\subset C_{0}
and therefore
\bar{r}\geq 2r_{0}>0
.
For a given
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r>0
we denote

N_{r}(A)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\operatorname{dist}(x,A)<r\}.

We define

c^{*}=\inf\{V(x):x\in C_{0}\setminus\bigl(B_{\bar{r}}(0)\cup N_{\bar{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus C_{0})\bigr)\}.

This is the natural analogue of the definition of
c^{*}
in the double–well case,
with the isolated minimum
0
and the boundary
\partial C_{0}

playing the role of the two wells.

Observe that
c^{*}>0
. Indeed, assume by contradiction
c^{*}=0
. Then there exists a sequence
(x_{n})\subset C_{0}
with
|x_{n}|\geq\bar{r}
,
\operatorname{dist}(x_{n},\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus C_{0})\geq\bar{r}
and
V(x_{n})\to 0
. Since
C_{0}
is bounded, up to a subsequence we have
x_{n}\to x_{0}
. By continuity of
V
we have
V(x_{0})=0
, while
x_{0}\in C_{0}\setminus\{0\}
, a contradiction because by definition of
C_{0}
one has
V(x)>0
on
C_{0}\setminus\{0\}

Lemma 4.5
.

If
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
satisfies
(V_{3})
and
(V_{4})
, then

(a)

For every
c\in[0,c^{*})
, the condition
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
is satisfied with respect to two disjoint closed sets
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
such that

\partial C_{0}\subset\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{\bar{r}}(0)\quad\text{and}\quad 0\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\subset B_{\bar{r}}(0).

(b)

For every
c\in[0,c^{*})
, the coercivity condition
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
holds.

Proof.

(a)
Fix
c\in[0,c^{*})
. By definition of
c^{*}
one has

V(x)\geq c^{*}>c\quad\text{for all }x\in C_{0}\setminus(B_{\bar{r}}(0)\cup N_{\bar{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus C_{0})).

Since moreover
V(x)\leq 0
for
x\notin C_{0}
, it follows that

\mathcal{V}_{c}\subset B_{\bar{r}}(0)\cup N_{\bar{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus C_{0}).

Set

\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}=\mathcal{V}_{c}\cap B_{\bar{r}}(0)\text{ and }\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}=\mathcal{V}_{c}\cap N_{\bar{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus C_{0}).

Then
\mathcal{V}_{c}=\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\cup\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
and

\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\cap\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}=\emptyset
. Moreover
0\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
and
\partial C_{0}\subset\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
since
V=0
on
\partial C_{0}
.
Finally
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}
is compact and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}
is closed. Hence,

\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-},\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+})>0,

and therefore condition
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
holds.

(b)
Fix
c\in[0,c^{*})
and let
q\in\Gamma_{c}
.
Then by definition
V(q(t))\geq c
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
c>0
, this implies
V(q(t))>0
for all
t
, hence

q(t)\in C_{0}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since
C_{0}
is bounded, it follows that
q
is bounded.

When
c=0
we claim that
q(t)\in\overline{C}_{0}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Again
since
C_{0}
is bounded, this implies that
q
is bounded and (b) follows.

To prove the claim, argue by contradiction. Assume that there exists
t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\operatorname{dist}(q(t_{1}),\overline{C}_{0})>0
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}\subset B_{\bar{r}}(0)\subset C_{0}
, and

\liminf_{t\to-\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-})=0
,
there exists
t_{0}
such that
q(t_{0})\in C_{0}
so that
\operatorname{dist}(q(t_{0}),\overline{C}_{0})=0
. By continuity of the map
t\mapsto\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\overline{C}_{0})
, there is a point
t_{2}
, between
t_{0}
and
t_{1}
, such that

\delta_{0}>\operatorname{dist}(q(t_{2}),\overline{C}_{0})>0
. By (
4.1
) this yields
V(q(t_{2}))<0
,
contradicting
V(q(t))\geq c=0
.
∎

By Lemma
4.5
, Theorem
3.14
applies.

.

Assume that
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
satisfies
(V_{3})
and
(V_{4})
.
Then for every

there exist

I=(\alpha,\omega)
with

and a minimal

–connection

between

and

. In particular,

) on

, and

for all

.

4.3.
Multiple pendulum-type systems

We now finally consider the case of
multiple pendulum-type systems
where the potential
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
satisfies:

(V_{5})

V
is
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-periodic, namely

V(x+\xi)=V(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

(V_{6})

V(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
V(x)=0
if and only if
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

Assumptions
(V_{5})
–
(V_{6})
induce a simple
geometric structure on the configuration space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which allows us,
as in the preceding cases, to identify a threshold value
c^{*}
below which the
sublevel set
\mathcal{V}_{c}
enjoys suitable separation properties.
More precisely, we set

c^{*}:=\inf\{V(x):\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb{Z}^{N})\geq 1/2\}.

By
(V_{6})
,
V
is strictly positive on

[0,1]^{N}\setminus N_{1/2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
.
Since this set is compact and
V
is continuous, it follows that

c^{*}>0
.

Fix
c\in[0,c^{*})
and set
\nu_{c}=(c^{*}-c)/2
.
By continuity and
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
–periodicity of
V
, there exists

\delta_{c}\in(0,1/2)
such that

(4.2)

V(x)\geq c+\nu_{c}\text{ whenever }\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb{Z}^{N})>1/2-\delta_{c}.

Consequently,

\mathcal{V}_{c}\subset\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb{Z}^{N})\leq 1/2-\delta_{c}\}=\cup_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\overline{B}_{1/2-\delta_{c}}(\xi).

Hence, defining

\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi}=\mathcal{V}_{c}\cap\overline{B}_{1/2-\delta_{c}}(\xi),

it follows that each
\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi}\neq\emptyset
is closed, and that

\mathcal{V}_{c}=\cup_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi}\text{ and }\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi},\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi^{\prime}})\geq 2\delta_{c}\quad\text{for all }\xi\neq\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

In particular we have

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume that
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
satisfies

(V_{5})
–
(V_{6})
. Let
Z_{\pm}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
be nonempty sets such that

Z_{-}\cap Z_{+}=\emptyset
and
\mathbb{Z}^{N}=Z_{-}\cup Z_{+}
. Then, for every
c\in[0,c^{*})
, setting

\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-})=\cup_{\xi\in Z_{-}}\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi}\text{ and }\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+})=\cup_{\xi\in Z_{+}}\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi},

we have

\mathcal{V}_{c}=\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-})\cup\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+}),\ Z_{\pm}\subset\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}(Z_{\pm})\text{ and
}\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+}))\geq 2\delta_{c}.

In particular the sets
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-})
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+})
are closed.

Proof.

Fix
c\in[0,c^{*})
and let
\mathbb{Z}^{N}=Z_{-}\cup Z_{+}
be a disjoint partition as in the statement. Since
\mathcal{V}_{c}=\cup_{\xi\in Z^{N}}\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi}
and
Z_{-}\cup Z_{+}=\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, it follows that

\mathcal{V}_{c}=\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-})\cup\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+}).

Since
\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi}
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
we obtain also

Z_{\pm}\subset\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}(Z_{\pm})
.
Finally, if
x\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-})
and
y\in\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+})
, then there exist

\xi\in Z_{-}
and
\xi^{\prime}\in Z_{+}
such that
x\in\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi}
and

y\in\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi^{\prime}}
. Hence, since
\xi\neq\xi^{\prime}
,

|x-y|\geq\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi},\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi^{\prime}})\geq 2\delta_{c}

and therefore
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+}))\geq 2\delta_{c}
.
Consequently, since
\mathcal{V}_{c}=\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-})\cup\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+})
is closed and
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+}))>0
, the
sets
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}(Z_{\pm})
are closed too.
∎

Lemma
4.7
provides a natural decomposition of the sublevel set

\mathcal{V}_{c}
for every
c\in[0,c^{*})
, associated with an arbitrary
partition
\mathbb{Z}^{N}=Z_{-}\cup Z_{+}
. In particular, this decomposition
ensures that assumption
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
holds for all

c\in[0,c^{*})
.
In order to apply the abstract variational results developed in the previous
sections and obtain the existence of connecting orbits, it therefore remains
to verify the coercivity assumption
(\mathcal{J}_{c})

for suitable choices of the sets
Z_{\pm}
.

The key ingredient for establishing
(\mathcal{J}_{c})

is the following estimate.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
c\in[0,c^{*})
and
M>0
. Then there exists
R_{0}=R_{0}(c,M)>0

such that the following holds.
If
q\in W^{1,\Phi}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\sigma<\tau
satisfy

V(q(t))\geq c\quad\text{for all }t\in(\sigma,\tau),\qquad J_{c,(\sigma,\tau)}(q)\leq M,

then

|q(\tau)-q(\sigma)|\leq R_{0}.

Proof.

Let
M>0
. Arguing by contradiction, assume that there exist sequences

(q_{n})\subset W^{1,\Phi}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\sigma_{n}<\tau_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

V(q_{n}(t))\geq c
on
(\sigma_{n},\tau_{n})
,
J_{c,(\sigma_{n},\tau_{n})}(q_{n})\leq M

and

|q_{n}(\tau_{n})-q_{n}(\sigma_{n})|\to+\infty.

Since
|q_{n}(\tau_{n})-q_{n}(\sigma_{n})|\to+\infty
, there exists an index

i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
such that

(4.3)

|q_{n}(\tau_{n})_{i}-q_{n}(\sigma_{n})_{i}|\to+\infty.

For such value of
i
, define the slabs

S_{i,j}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid 1/2-\delta_{c}<x_{i}-j<1/2+\delta_{c}\},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

By definition,
S_{i,j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\cup_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\overline{B}_{1/2-\delta_{c}}(\xi)
. Hence, by (
4.2
),
V(x)\geq c+\nu_{c}
for any
x\in S_{i,j}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. By (
4.3
) and the continuity of
q_{n}
, it follows that, as
n\to\infty
, the image

q_{n}((\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}))
crosses an increasing number of distinct slabs. More precisely, there exist integers
j^{-}_{n}<j^{+}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
with

j^{+}_{n}-j^{-}_{n}\to+\infty
and times

\sigma_{n}<\alpha_{j^{-}_{n}}<\beta_{j^{-}_{n}}<\alpha_{j^{-}_{n}+1}<\beta_{j^{-}_{n}+1}<\ldots<\alpha_{j^{+}_{n}}<\beta_{j^{+}_{n}}<\tau_{n}

such that, for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
with
j^{-}_{n}\leq j\leq j^{+}_{n}
, one has

(q_{n}(\alpha_{j}))_{i}-j=\tfrac{1}{2}-\delta_{c},\quad(q_{n}(\beta_{j}))_{i}-j=\tfrac{1}{2}+\delta_{c},\text{
and
}q_{n}(t)\in S_{i,j}\quad\text{for all }t\in(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}).

Then,

V(q_{n}(t))-c\geq\nu_{c}
for every
t\in(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j})
and
j\in[j^{-}_{n},j^{+}_{n}]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
|q_{n}(\beta_{j})-q_{n}(\alpha_{j})|\geq 2\delta_{c}
for any
j\in[j^{-}_{n},j^{+}_{n}]\cap\mathbb{Z}
, by Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

J_{c,(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j})}(q_{n})\geq\lambda_{\nu_{c}}H(2\delta_{c})>0.

Hence

M\geq J_{c,(\sigma_{n},\tau_{n})}(q_{n})\geq\sum_{j=j^{-}_{n}}^{j^{+}_{n}}J_{c,(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j})}(q_{n})\geq(j^{+}_{n}-j^{-}_{n}+1)\lambda_{\nu_{c}}H(2\delta_{c})\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

contradicting
j^{+}_{n}-j^{-}_{n}\to+\infty
and concluding the proof.
∎

Given a partition
\mathbb{Z}^{N}=Z_{-}\cup Z_{+}
and the associated sets
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-})
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+})
as in
Lemma
4.7
, in the following we denote

\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})=\{q\in\mathcal{X}_{c}\mid\liminf_{t\to-\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-}))=\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+}))=0\}.

Thanks to Lemma
4.7
and Lemma
4.8
,
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
and
(\mathcal{J}_{c})
hold for many different choices of the sets
Z_{\pm}
.
This makes it possible to obtain multiple geometrically distinct connecting orbits.
To exploit this fact, we proceed inductively,
adapting the arguments in
[
19
,
17
]
, and repeatedly using the following property.

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
c\in[0,c^{*})
there exists
R=R(c)>0
such that, for every partition
\mathbb{Z}^{N}=Z_{-}\cup Z_{+}
satisfying

(4.4)

0\in Z_{-}\neq\mathbb{Z}^{N}\text{ and }\xi+Z_{-}\subset Z_{-}\text{ for any }\xi\in Z_{-},

letting
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}(Z_{\pm})
be the the sets given by Lemma
4.7
, the functional
J_{c}
satisfies
(
\mathcal{J}_{c}
)
on
\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})

with
R=R(c)
, namely,

\inf_{q\in\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})}J_{c}(q)=\inf\left\{J_{c}(q):q\in\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})\text{ and }\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq R\right\}.

Proof.

Let
m_{c}=\inf_{\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})}J_{c}
. We claim that

(4.5)

m_{c}\leq\Phi(1)+\displaystyle\max_{|x|\leq 1}V(x):=M.

To prove (
4.5
), denoting by
e_{m}
the
m
-th canonical basis vector of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we observe
that, since
Z_{-}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
is invariant under addition and

Z_{-}\neq\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, it cannot contain both
e_{m}
and
-e_{m}
for every

m\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Indeed, if this were the case, then by closure under
addition one would have
Z_{-}=\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, a contradiction.
Hence there exists
m\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
\xi_{+}\in\{e_{m},-e_{m}\}
such that
\xi_{+}\in Z_{+}
.

Consider the interpolating function
\zeta
, defined by

\zeta(t)=t\xi_{+}
if
t\in[0,1]
,
\zeta(t)=0
if
t\leq 0
, and
\zeta(t)=\xi_{+}
if
t\geq 1
.
Set

\sigma=\sup\{t\in[0,1]\mid\zeta(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{c,0}\},\quad\tau=\inf\{t\in(\sigma,1]\mid\zeta(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{+}}\}.

By continuity
\zeta(\sigma)\in\mathcal{V}_{c,0}
,
\zeta(\tau)\in\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{+}}
and
\zeta(t)\notin\mathcal{V}_{c,0}\cup\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{+}}
for any
t\in(\sigma,\tau)
. Hence,
because by construction
\zeta([0,1])\subset\bar{B}_{1/2}(0)\cup\bar{B}_{1/2}(\xi_{+})
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}\setminus(\mathcal{V}_{c,0}\cup\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{+}})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(\bar{B}_{1/2}(0)\cup\bar{B}_{1/2}(\xi_{+}))
, we obtain that
\zeta(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{c}
, and so,

V(\zeta(t))>c
, for all
t\in(\sigma,\tau).

Defining the function

\eta(t)=\begin{cases}\zeta(\sigma),&t\leq\sigma,\\
\zeta(t),&t\in(\sigma,\tau),\\
\zeta(\tau),&t\geq\tau,\end{cases}

we have
\eta\in\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})
, and since
|\dot{\eta}|\leq 1
, it follows
m_{c}\leq J_{c}(\eta)\leq\Phi(1)+\displaystyle\max_{|x|\leq 1}V(x)
, as we claimed.

Let
(q_{n})\subset\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})
be a minimizing sequence. By (
4.5
) we can assume that
J_{c}(q_{n})\leq M+1
for all
n
.
By definition of
\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})
, there exist sequences
t_{n}^{-}\to-\infty
and
\xi_{n}^{-}\in Z_{-}
such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q_{n}(t_{n}^{-}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-}))=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q_{n}(t_{n}^{-}),\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{n}^{-}})=0.

Since
\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{n}^{-}}\subset B_{1/2}(\xi_{n}^{-})
we can choose the sequence
(t_{n}^{-})
such that

|q_{n}(t_{n}^{-})-\xi_{n}^{-}|\leq 1/2\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Consider the translated sequence

v_{n}(t)=q_{n}(t)-\xi_{n}^{-}.

By
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-periodicity of
V
, we have
V(v_{n}(t))\geq c
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
J_{c}(v_{n})=J_{c}(q_{n})\leq M+1
.
Moreover, since
\mathcal{V}_{c,0}=\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{n}^{-}}-\xi_{n}^{-}
, we obtain

(4.6)

\operatorname{dist}\bigl(v_{n}(t_{n}^{-}),\mathcal{V}_{c,0}\bigr)=\operatorname{dist}\bigl(q_{n}(t_{n}^{-}),\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{n}^{-}}\bigr)\to 0\text{ and }|v_{n}(t_{n}^{-})|\leq 1/2\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
J_{c}(v_{n})\leq M+1
and
V(v_{n}(t))\geq c
for all
t
, Lemma
4.8
yields a constant
R_{0}=R_{0}(c,M+1)
such that

|v_{n}(t)-v_{n}(t_{n}^{-})|\leq R_{0}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, setting
R(c)=1/2+R_{0}
we obtain

(4.7)

\|v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|v_{n}(t_{n}^{-})|+R_{0}\leq R(c)\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

To conclude the proof of the Lemma, by (
4.7
) it is sufficient to show that
(v_{n})
is a minimizing sequence of
J_{c}
on
\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})
. To this aim, we first recall that
J_{c}(v_{n})=J_{c}(q_{n})\to m_{c}
. Secondly, by (
4.6
) we have also
\liminf_{t\to-\infty}\operatorname{dist}(v_{n}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-}))=0
. Finally, since
q_{n}\in\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})
, there exist sequences
t_{n}^{+}\to+\infty
and
\xi_{n}^{+}\in Z_{+}
such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q_{n}(t_{n}^{+}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+}))=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(q_{n}(t_{n}^{+}),\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{n}^{+}})=0.

Since
v_{n}=q_{n}-\xi_{n}^{-}
, we obtain

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(v_{n}(t_{n}^{+}),\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{n}^{+}-\xi_{n}^{-}})=0.
Then, noting that
\xi_{n}^{+}-\xi_{n}^{-}\in Z_{+}
(since
\xi_{n}^{-}+Z_{-}\subset Z_{-}
), we conclude

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(v_{n}(t_{n}^{+}),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+}))=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(v_{n}(t_{n}^{+}),\cup_{\xi\in Z_{+}}\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi})\leq\operatorname{dist}(v_{n}(t_{n}^{+}),\mathcal{V}_{c,\xi_{n}^{+}-\xi_{n}^{-}})=0,

showing that
\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\operatorname{dist}(v_{n}(t),\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+}))=0
, and so that
v_{n}\in\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})
. ∎

Lemma
4.7
provides, for every
c\in[0,c^{*})
, a decomposition

\mathcal{V}_{c}=\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-})\cup\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+})
associated with any partition

\mathbb{Z}^{N}=Z_{-}\cup Z_{+}
, and ensures the separation condition
(\mathcal{V}_{c})
.
Moreover, if the partition is admissible in the sense of (
4.4
),
then Lemma
4.8
and Lemma
4.9
yield the coercivity condition

(\mathcal{J}_{c})
on
\Gamma_{c}(Z_{-},Z_{+})
with a constant
R(c)
depending only on
c
.
Hence Theorem
3.14
applies.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
c\in[0,c^{*})
. For any
Z_{-}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
satisfying (
4.4
),
set
Z_{+}:=\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus Z_{-}
and let

\mathcal{V}_{c}^{\pm}(Z_{\pm})
be the sets
given by Lemma
4.7
. Then there exist

\xi^{+}\in Z_{+}
, an interval
I=(\alpha,\omega)\subset\mathbb{R}
with

-\infty\leq\alpha<\omega\leq+\infty
, and a minimal
c
–connection

q:\bar{I}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
between
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{-}(Z_{-})
and
\mathcal{V}_{c}^{+}(Z_{+})

such that

Proof.

Fix
