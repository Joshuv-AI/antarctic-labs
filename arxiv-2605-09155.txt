like so:

L(T,C,\chi)=\sum_{D\in\operatorname{Div}_{\geq 0}(U)}\chi\big([D-\deg(D)x]\big)T^{\deg(D)}.

We return to the situation of paragraph

over
F_{q}

.

In the situation and the notation of paragraph
4
, suppose the homomorphism of abstract groups

\psi:J_{\mathfrak{m}}({\overline{F}_{q}})\to J_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}({\overline{F}_{q}})

induces an isomorphism

J_{\mathfrak{m}}(F_{q^{m}})\simeq J_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(F_{q^{m}})

\psi=\tilde{\alpha}\circ\beta^{J_{\mathfrak{m}}}.

The proof hinges on the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
P_{\mathfrak{m}}\subset K(C)^{*}
denote the subgroup of divisors of functions congruent to
1
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
, and write
\equiv_{\mathfrak{m}}
for equivalence of divisors coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
modulo
P_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Let
E\in\operatorname{Div}(U)
be a divisor of degree
0
coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
. Then
E+c
is congruent modulo
P_{\mathfrak{m}}
to an
F_{q}
-rational point of
U
if and only if

\sharp\Big\{D\in\operatorname{Div}_{\geq 0}(U)\;\big|\;D\equiv_{\mathfrak{m}}E+c\Big\}=1.

Proof.

Evidently, equivalence modulo
P_{\mathfrak{m}}

implies
equivalence modulo
K(C)^{*}
, i.e. linear equivalence. Since
E+c
has degree
1
, any effective divisor
D
linearly equivalent to
E+c
must be an
F_{q}
-point
D=d\in U(F_{q})
. Moreover,
d\equiv_{\mathfrak{m}}E+c
if and only if
d\in j_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-1}(E)
. Thus, (*) is equivalent to

\sharp\big(j_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-1}(E)\big)=1.

Our assumption that
\dim J_{\mathfrak{m}}\geq 2
guarantees that
j_{\mathfrak{m}}
is injective.
∎

4.6.
The proof of Corollary
4.4
also makes use of the classical fact that if
A
is a finite abelian group, then the homomorphisms
\chi:A\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
form a basis for the space of functions
{\mathbb{C}}^{A}
. Hence, if
f:A\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a function such that
\sum_{a\in A}\chi(a)f(a)=0
for all characters
\chi
, then
f=0
.

Proof of Corollary
4.4
.

We claim that under the assumptions of the Corollary,

\psi\big(U(F_{q^{m}})\big)=U^{\prime}(F_{q^{m}})

for all
n\geq 1
. By change of base from
F_{q}
to
F_{q^{m}}
, the claim reduces to the case
m=1
. By
4
(*) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in J_{\mathfrak{m}}(F_{q})}

\displaystyle\chi(E)\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\sharp\Big\{D\in\operatorname{Div}_{\geq 0}(U)\;\big|\;D\equiv_{\mathfrak{m}}E+rc\Big\}T^{r}

\displaystyle=L(T,C,\chi)

\displaystyle=L(T,C^{\prime}_{F_{q}},\chi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{E^{\prime}\in J_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(F_{q})}\chi^{\prime}(E^{\prime})\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\sharp\Big\{D^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Div}_{\geq 0}(U^{\prime})\;\big|\;D^{\prime}\equiv_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}E^{\prime}+rc^{\prime}\Big\}T^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in J_{\mathfrak{m}}(F_{q})}\chi(E)\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\sharp\Big\{D^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Div}_{\geq 0}(U^{\prime})\;\big|\;D^{\prime}\equiv_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}\psi(E)+rc^{\prime}\Big\}T^{r}

for all characters
\chi
of
J_{\mathfrak{m}}(F_{q})
. Since characters form a basis for the space of functions (
4
), it follows that

\sharp\Big\{D\in\operatorname{Div}_{\geq 0}(U)\;\big|\;D\equiv_{\mathfrak{m}}E+rc\Big\}=\sharp\Big\{D^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Div}_{\geq 0}(U^{\prime})\;\big|\;D^{\prime}\equiv_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}\psi(E)+rc^{\prime}\Big\}

for all
r
and all
E\in J_{\mathfrak{m}}(F_{q})
. According to Lemma
4.5
applied to the case
r=1
it follows that
E\in U(F_{q})
if and only
\psi(E)\in U^{\prime}(F_{q})
, which establishes the claim. The Corollary now follows from Theorem
3.13
.
∎
