TPRG-08 IMPACT OF SUPRAMARGINAL RESECTION ON PROGRESSION-FREE AND OVERALL SURVIVAL IN INFILTRATIVE BRAIN METASTASES: A PROSPECTIVE COHORT STUDY
Authors: Tommaso Araceli, Raquel Blazquez, Stoerr Eva-Maria, Tobias Pukrop, Nils Ole Schmidt, Martin Proescholdt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe role of infiltrative histological growth patterns (HGP) in determining prognosis in brain metastases (BM), along with the benefit of supramarginal resection (SR), remains unclear. We investigated whether the infiltrative phenotype is linked to reduced survival and whether SR impacts patient outcomes.MethodsIn accordance with the previously established MetInfilt protocol, biopsies from the brain–tumor interface were prospectively obtained in patients undergoing surgical resection of one or more brain metastases (BM) between September 2022-2025. Samples were processed for both histological and molecular analyses. Histological growth patterns (HGP) were evaluated on H&E- and IHC-stained interface sections, with the presence of any infiltrative component defining classification. Supramarginal resection (SR) was assessed using volumetric comparison of pre- and postoperative FLAIR or non–contrast-enhancing (non-CE) tumor volumes, following criteria established by the RANO resect group for primary brain tumors. Outcomes analyzed were progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).ResultsWe analyzed 50 patients (20 female, 30 male) with a median age of 59.8 years (range: 28.5 – 81.8 years). Most patients had multiple metastases (70.0%) and metachronous presentation (64.0%); the most common primary tumor was lung cancer (44.0%). 76.3% of HGPs were infiltrative. SR was achieved in 38.9% of cases. No significant imbalances were observed for age (p = 0.885), sex (p = 0.542), primary tumor (p = 0.143), metastatic status (p = 0.755), metastatic timing (p = 0.226), or lesion location (p = 0.070) between non-infiltrative and infiltrative cases. OS and PFS differed significantly by infiltration phenotype (p < 0.0001 and p = 0.0010). Supramarginal resection improved OS and PFS in patients with infiltrative HGP (p = 0.0002 and p = 0.0010), but not in those with non-infiltrative HGP (p = 0.861 and p = 0.288).ConclusionsInfiltrative tumor growth is associated with worse PFS and OS. Supramarginal resection (SR) improves survival specifically in infiltrative BM, with no observable benefit in non-infiltrative disease. These findings advocate for adapting surgical extent according to infiltrative phenotype.
