Executive Summary

The City of Brewer Recreational Facility Energy Modernization Project replaced the
thirty-four-year-old metal halide lighting system at the City’s primary sports field and
stadium with new energy efficient LED lighting. The project will reduce operating and
maintenance cost while providing a safer environment and additional recreational
opportunities. New lighting fixtures will also reduce wasted energy by eliminating extra
lighting that currently spills into abutting roadways and neighbors’ yards causing safety
hazards and a nuisance.

Project Objectives

The purpose of this project was to modernize an outdated and inefficient 34-year-old
lighting system. Its goals were to lower energy usage and cost, improve lighting
dispersal, and reduce inefficiencies and negative impacts from light spillover. Through
a competitive bid process, the City selected a contractor that removed the old lighting
system and purchased and installed a new energy efficient LED lighting system at its
Doyle Field natural turf stadium.

Project Description of Activities Performed

The Doyle Field lighting system, originally installed in 1988, had reached the end of its
useful life. Fixtures were failing regularly, replacement parts were difficult to obtain, and
lighting performance had deteriorated below the original design levels of 33 foot-candles
with a 4.2:1 uniformity ratio. The system also created light spill into neighboring
residences along State Street, limiting field use.

Following grant approval, a geotechnical investigation was conducted to evaluate soil
conditions at the proposed pole locations. The study determined that the soils were not
suitable for traditional pier foundations, requiring the lighting system to be redesigned
using spreader foundations to properly support the new poles.

Construction began in June 2025 with removal of the existing lighting infrastructure and
installation of the new LED lighting system. The project was substantially completed on
July 20, 2025. Post-installation testing on September 4, 2025 confirmed the system
exceeded the RFP performance standards, achieving an average illumination level of
57.5 foot-candles with a uniformity ratio of 1.72:1.

The upgraded system significantly improves visibility and safety for participants and
spectators while reducing light spill to surrounding residential areas. The lighting was
used extensively during the fall season by high school and middle school athletic
programs, the recreation soccer program, and the Brewer Youth Football Association,
all of whom reported very positive feedback regarding the improved lighting quality.

Challenges Encountered

Two primary challenges were encountered during the project. First, the geotechnical
investigation determined that soils were unsuitable for traditional pole foundations,
requiring larger spreader foundations. This condition differed from a previous
geotechnical report and resulted in additional time and cost.

Weather conditions also affected construction and field restoration. Heavy rainfall early
in the project slowed foundation installation and caused soil compaction and surface
ruts from construction equipment. This was followed by unusually dry conditions later in
the summer, which slowed turf establishment. Additional hydroseeding and restoration
work were required, and one game was relocated to an alternate field to allow adequate
time for turf recovery.

Outcomes

The project significantly improved field lighting performance and uniformity. The original
system provided approximately 33 foot-candles with a 4.2:1 uniformity ratio. Post-
installation testing confirmed the new LED system exceeded performance expectations,
achieving an average illumination level of 57.5 foot-candles with a uniformity ratio of
1.72:1. These improvements enhance player safety, spectator visibility, and overall field
usability.

The LED system also improves light control, reducing light spill into adjacent residential
areas and allowing for more effective evening use of the facility.

Anticipating the installation could run into the early part of the season, the schedule was
adjusted to host more events in later in the season during October, therefore. the
available sample size for energy comparison is limited. However, preliminary data
suggests overall electrical usage remains lower than historic levels despite increased
field use following installation.

Even with increased use of the field following installation, electricity consumption
remains well below levels recorded prior to the lighting upgrade, indicating improved
