A Free Analog of Bobkov’s Gaussian Isoperimetry Inequality
Dima Shlyakhtenko

Abstract
We prove a one-variable functional inequality which is the free probability analog of Bobkov’s isoperimetry inequality. The inequality involves the L 1 L^{1} norm of the difference quotient of a function f f and can be viewed as a non-local isoperimetric inequality. We also prove related inequalities for subsets of an interval as well as for subsets of roots of Hermite polynomials. This paper is also an experiment in AI-based exploration of free analogs of classical probability statements.

Introduction.

Bobkov’s inequality
[
3
]
is a generalization of the Gaussian isoperimetric inequality. One formulation of the inequality is the following. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
be a sufficiently smooth function (e.g.,
C^{1}
). Let
\phi
denote the Gaussian density
\phi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\exp(-x^{2}/2)
, and let
\psi
be the inverse of the Gaussian quantile map determined by

\int_{-\infty}^{\psi(u)}\phi(t)dt=u,

and consider the “profile function”

I(u)=\phi(\psi(u)).

Let
\gamma_{d}(dx)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\exp(-\|x\|^{2}/2)
be the Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then Bobkov’s inequality states that

I\left(\int fd\gamma\right)\leq\int\sqrt{(I\circ f)^{2}+\|\nabla f\|^{2}}d\gamma.

(1)

or, in an equilvaent form (see
[
2
]
),

I\left(\int fd\gamma\right)-\int I\circ fd\gamma\leq\int\|\nabla f\|d\gamma.

(2)

The key feature of Bobkov’s inequality is the right-hand side of (
2
), which involves the
L^{1}
norm of the gradient of
f
.

Bobkov’s inequality has received much attention. For exmaple, Bakry and Ledoux proved (
1
) using the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup, and generalized the inequality to log-concave measures
[
1
]
.
Other proofs and generalizations, using Brownian motion theory, were given by Capitaine, Hsu, and Ledoux in
[
4
]
and Barthe and Maurey
[
2
]
.

In this paper we prove an analog of Bobkov’s inequality (
2
) in free probability theory
[
10
,
7
]
in the one-variable case
d=1
. More precisely:

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in C^{1}([-1,1],\sigma)
and assume that
0\leq f\leq 1
. Then

J\left(\int fd\sigma\right)-\int(J\circ f)d\sigma(x)\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}\right|d\sigma(x)d\sigma(y).

(3)

Here
\sigma
is the semicircle law
\sigma(dx)=\frac{2}{\pi}1_{[-1,1]}(x)\sqrt{1-x^{2}}dx
, and
J
is the free profile map given by

J(x)=\frac{8}{3\pi}(1-\alpha^{2}(x))^{3/2}

where
\alpha(x)
is the inverse quantile map of the semicircle law determined by

\int_{-1}^{\alpha(x)}d\sigma(t)=x.

Curiously,
J
has a structure similar to
I
, except that it is (up to a constant) the composition of the
cube
of the semicircle density with the inverse quantile map. On the other hand, the right-hand side of (
3
) involves the
L^{1}
norm of the difference quotient derivation, which is the free analog of the gradient (see e.g.
[
11
]
).

By continuity and semicontinuity, Theorem
1
holds true for any bounded measurable function
f:[-1,1]\to[0,1]
. If we consider the special case of
f=1_{X}
where
X\subset[-1,1]
is a measurable subset, (
3
) simplifies. Indeed,
J(0)=J(1)=0
and so
J\circ f=0
in this case. If we introduce the notation

E(X)=\int_{x\in X}\int_{y\in[-1,1]\setminus X}\frac{1}{|x-y|}d\sigma(x)d\sigma(y),

Theorem
1
immediately gives a Lieb-style non-local isoperimetry inequality:

Theorem 2
.

Let
X\subset[-1,1]
be a Lebesgue-measurable set. Then

2E(X)\geq J(\sigma(X))=2E([-1,\alpha(\sigma(X))]).

(4)

In fact, Theorem
1
is equivalent to Theorem
2
, and we prove the latter theorem first.

Note that the equality
J(\sigma(X))=2E([-1,\alpha(\sigma(X))])
in Theorem
2
shows that the inequality (
3
) is tight, with equality achieved when
f
is a characteristic function of a set of the form
[-1,a]
or
[a,1]
. Thus
J
is the optimal profile for our problem.

It is natural to ask whether multi-variable or operator-valued generalizations of the inequality exist, but we do not have any results in this direction.

It is also natural to ask whether the inequality (
4
) arises in some large-
n
limit. We show that this is indeed the case: in fact, in the style of finite free probability
[
6
]
, a similar inequality is true for roots of Hermite polynomials. More precisely, denoting by
R
the set of roots of the
n
-th Hermite polynomial, we can define for any
A\subset R
its energy

E(A)=\sum_{x\in A}\sum_{y\in R\setminus A}\frac{1}{|x-y|}.

The statement is then that among all sets
A
of fixed cardinality,
E(A)
is minimized by an interval consisting of the first
|A|
roots (see Theorem
3
for the precise statement).

The idea of the proof comes from exploring a monotonicity property of the quantile maps associated with rescaled semicircular measures (these are no longer probability measures), given by Lemma
1
, or the analogous statement for the Hermite roots, see Lemma
6
.
f
. This monotonicity allows us to compare the energy of a root configuration of a polynomial
H_{n}
with a similar configuration of roots, but of the polynomial
H_{n-1}
(with no rescaling) and then proceed by induction until encountering a configuration that consists of all of the roots of
H_{k}
for some
k<n
.

This paper arose out of the author’s curiosity as to whether it would be possible to set up an AI tool that would be able to automatically explore the landscape of free probability by formulating and subsequently proving or disproving free analogs of classical probability statements, and checking the proofs through formalization in a proof assistant.

A tool like that remains a (distant?) dream. Nonetheless, this paper represents a full run through such a cycle, although with a lot of human assistance. However, many of the high-level ideas of the proof, including the use of Hermite roots and comparison between roots of an Hermite polynomial and its derivative (which led us to find the proof), were human-supplied, but elaborated by AI.

Thus this paper represents an truly AI-assisted mathematical result — many ideas were supplied and elaborated by the human author — but it would not have been written without AI’s involvement.

[-1,1]
. Then

f(x)=\int_{0}^{1}1_{A_{t}}(x)dt,\qquad|f(x)-f(y)|=\int_{0}^{1}|1_{A_{t}}(x)-1_{A_{t}}(y)|dt.

Thus

\mathscr{E}(f)=\int_{0}^{1}\iint\left|\frac{1_{A_{t}}(x)-1_{A_{t}}(y)}{x-y}\right|d\sigma(x)d\sigma(y)dt=\int_{0}^{1}2E(A_{t})dt.

Applying the layer-cake lemma to
J\left(\int fd\sigma)\right)-\int J\circ fd\sigma
(which is possible since
J
is concave and
1/J^{\prime\prime}
is concave) gives the inequality

J\left(\int fd\sigma\right)-\int J\circ fd\sigma\leq\int_{0}^{1}J(\sigma(A_{t}))dt.

Applying Theorem
2
to
A_{t}
for each
t
gives us

J(\sigma(A_{t}))dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}2E(A_{t})dt=\mathscr{E}(f).

Putting this together gives Theorem
1
.

4
An analog of Theorem
2
for roots of Hermite polynomials.

Let
H_{n}
be the
n
-th Hermite polynomial
[
9
, §5.5]
determined recursively by:

H_{0}(x)=1,\qquad H_{1}(x)=2x,

H_{n+1}(x)=2xH_{n}(x)-2nH_{n-1}(x),\qquad n\geq 1.

Then
H^{\prime}(x)=2nH_{n-1}(x)
and these polynomials satisfy the differential equation

H_{n}^{\prime\prime}(x)-2xH^{\prime}_{n}(x)+2nH_{n}(x)=0.

It is well known
[
9
, §3.3 and Chapter 5]
that if the roots
\xi_{n,1},\dots,\xi_{n,n}
of these polynomials are scaled by the factor
(2n)^{-1/2}
, then they lie in the interval
[-1,1]
. Moreover, the atomic probability measures
\sigma_{n}=(1/n)\sum_{j}\delta_{\xi_{n,j}}
converge weakly as
n\to\infty
to the semicircle law. In this section, we prove an analog of Theorem
2
for subsets of the set of roots of
H_{n}
. This result is presented in Theorem
3
and can in fact be used to derive Theorem
3
by a limit argument.

We first gather some standard facts about the polynomials
H_{n}
needed for our proof, and summarize them as a Lemma. We could not find a precise reference for statement (
f
), so we give a direct argument loosely based on
[
5
]
. We refer the reader to
[
9
, §3.3, §3.4]
for the rest of the listed properties of the roots.

Lemma 6
.

The following properties hold:

a.

Each
H_{n}(x)
has
n
simple real roots
\xi_{n,1}<\xi_{n,2}<\dots<\xi_{n,n}

b.

The set of roots is symmetric about zero.

c.

The roots of
H_{n+1}
and
H_{n}
interlace:

\xi_{n+1,1}<\xi_{n,1}<\xi_{n+1,2}<\xi_{n,2}<\cdots<\xi_{n,n}<\xi_{n+1,n+1}.

d.

For each
1\leq i\leq n
,
\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{\xi_{n,i}-\xi_{n,j}}=\xi_{n,i}.

e.

Let
x_{n,i}={(2n)}^{-1/2}\xi_{n,i}
be rescaled roots of
H_{n}
. Then
x_{n,i}\in[-1,1]
, and each
1\leq i\leq n
,

\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{n,i}-x_{n,j}}=2n\ x_{n,i}.

f.

Let
d_{i}=x_{n-1,i}-x_{n,i}
. Then

d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots<d_{n-1}.

Proof of (
f
).

Fix
n
and
1\leq i\leq n-2
.

Let

R(x)=\frac{H^{\prime}_{n}(x)}{H_{n}(x)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{x-\xi_{n,j}}.

The function
R(x)
satisfies the Riccati equation

R^{\prime}(x)=-R(x)^{2}+2xR(x)-2n.

(7)

For
0<t<\xi_{n,i+1}-\xi_{n,i}
, let

Z_{i}(t)=R(\xi_{n,i}+t).

Then
Z_{i}(t)
is a smooth function of
t
on
(0,\xi_{n,i+1}-\xi_{n,i})
.

Since the roots of
H_{n}
are simple,
Z_{i}(t)\to+\infty
as
t\downarrow 0
and
Z_{i}(t)\to-\infty
as
t\uparrow\xi_{n,i+1}-\xi_{n,i}
. Thus
Z_{i}(t)
has a zero in the interval
(0,\xi_{n,i+1}-\xi_{n,i})
. If
Z_{i}(t)=0
, then

0=R(\xi_{n,i}+t)=\frac{H_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n,i}+t)}{H_{n}(\xi_{n,i}+t)},

so that
H_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n,i}+t)=0
. Since the zeros of
H_{n}
and
H_{n}^{\prime}
interlace, this means that
\xi_{n,i}+t=\xi_{n-1,i}
.

Thus the unique zero of
Z_{i}(t)
occurs at
t_{i}=\xi_{n-1,i}-\xi_{n,i}
; moreover,
Z_{i}(t)>0
on the interval
(0,t_{i})
.

We claim that it’s sufficient to prove that

Z_{i+1}(t)>Z_{i}(t),\qquad 0<t<t_{i}.

(8)

Indeed, suppose for a contradiction that
d_{i+1}<d_{i}
for some
i=1,\dots,n-2
, i.e., that
t_{i+1}<t_{i}
. Let
s=t_{i+1}
. Then
Z_{i+1}(s)=0
. Since by assumption
s=t_{i+1}<t_{i}
, we have that
Z_{i}(s)>0=Z_{i+1}(s)
, contradicting (
8
).

To prove (
8
), let

W_{i}(t)=Z_{i+1}(t)-Z_{i}(t).

Since

Z_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{t}+\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{\xi_{n,i}+t-\xi_{n,j}},

we have that

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\left(Z_{i+1}(s)-Z_{i}(s)\right)=\sum_{j\neq i+1}\frac{1}{\xi_{n,i+1}-\xi_{n,j}}-\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{\xi_{n,i}-\xi_{n,j}}=\xi_{n,i+1}-\xi_{n,i}

by part (
d
) of the Lemma. Since the roots are listed in a monotone order, it follows that

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\left(Z_{i+1}(t)-Z_{i}(t)\right)\geq 0,

and so
W_{i}(t)\geq 0
for all sufficiently small
t>0
.

The Ricatti equation (
7
) and the definition of
Z_{i}
implies

Z^{\prime}_{i}(t)=-Z_{i}(t)^{2}+2(\xi_{n,i}+t)Z_{i}(t)-2n.

Since
W_{i}(t)=Z_{i+1}(t)-Z+i(t)
, we get

\displaystyle W^{\prime}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(Z_{i+1}(t)^{2}-Z_{i}(t)^{2})+2(\xi_{n,i+1}+t)Z_{i+1}(t)-2(\xi_{n,i}+t)Z_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[-Z_{i+1}(t)-Z_{i}(t)+2(\xi_{n,i+1}+t)]W_{i}(t)+2(\xi_{n,i+1}-\xi_{n,i})Z_{i}(t).

If
W_{i}(t)
is not positive on
(0,t_{i})
, then there must exist some
t_{0}
,
0<t_{0}<t_{i}
, so that
W_{i}(t)>0
for all
0<t<t_{0}
and
W_{i}(t_{0})=0
. Thus
W_{i}^{\prime}(t_{0})\leq 0
. Substituting this into the equation for
W^{\prime}_{i}(t)
above gives

W_{i}^{\prime}(t_{0})=2(\xi_{n,i+1}-\xi_{n,i})Z_{i}(t_{0})>0

since
t_{0}<t_{i}
,
Z_{i}(t)>0
on
(0,t_{i})
, and the roots are increasing. But this contradicts
W_{i}^{\prime}(t_{0})\leq 0
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Suppose that
z_{1}<z_{2}\dots<z_{N}
are
N
points that satisfy

\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{z_{i}-z_{j}}=cz_{i}

for all
i=1,\dots,N
.

For
S\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
put

E_{z}(S)=\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S}\frac{1}{|z_{i}-z_{j}|},\qquad B_{z}(S)=\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S,i>j}\frac{1}{z_{i}-z_{j}}.

Then

E_{z}(S)=-c\sum_{i\in S}z_{i}+2B_{z}(S).

Proof.

Notice that by symmetry
\sum_{i,j\in S,i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j})^{-1}=0
. Moreover, if
i>j
, then
z_{i}-z_{j}>0
, and if
i<j
, then
z_{i}-z_{j}<0
. Using this, we get:

\displaystyle E_{z}(S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S,i<j}\frac{1}{|z_{i}-z_{j}|}+\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S,i>j}\frac{1}{|z_{i}-z_{j}|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S}\frac{1}{z_{i}-z_{j}}+2\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S,i>j}\frac{1}{z_{i}-z_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S}\frac{1}{z_{i}-z_{j}}-\underbrace{\sum_{i,j\in S,i\neq j}\frac{1}{z_{i}-z_{j}}}_{0}+2B_{z}(S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{z_{i}-z_{j}}+2B_{z}(S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c\sum_{i\in S}z_{i}+2B_{z}(S),

where in the next to last line we used the hypothesis of the Lemma.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Keeping the notations of Lemma
7
, assume that
S\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
, and let
m=|S|
. Put
I_{m}=\{1,\dots,m\}
. Then

E_{z}(S)-E_{z}(I_{m})=2B_{z}(S)-c\left(\sum_{i\in S}z_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_{i}\right).

Proof.

Since if
i\in I_{m}
,
j\notin I_{m}
,
i<j
,
B_{z}(I_{m})=0
.
By Lemma
7
,

E_{z}(I_{m})=-c\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_{i}+2B_{z}(I_{m})=-c\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_{i}.

Using this and the same Lemma gives

E_{z}(S)-E(I_{m})=2B(S)-c\sum_{i\in S}z_{i}+c\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_{i},

which is the claimed expression.
∎

Let now
x_{1}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{n}
be the rescaled roots of
H_{n}
,
x_{i}=(2n)^{-1/2}\xi_{n,i}
, and let
y_{1}<y_{2}<\dots,y_{n-1}
be the roots of
H_{n-1}
rescaled by the same constant,
y_{i}=(2n)^{-1/2}\xi_{n-1,i}
. Then both sets
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}
and
y_{1}<\cdots<y_{n-1}
satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma
7
with
c=2n
. For
S\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
and
S^{\prime}\subset\{1,\dots,n-1\}
we use the notation of Lemma
7
:
E_{x}(S)=\sum_{i\in S,j\in I_{n}\setminus S}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-}1
,
E_{y}(S)=\sum_{i\in S^{\prime},j\in I_{n-1}\setminus S^{\prime}}|y_{i}-y_{j}|^{-1}
.

For
S\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
let
T_{L}(S)=S\cap\{1,\dots,n-1\}\subset\{1,\dots,n-1\}
.

Lemma 9
.

Keeping the above notation, let
m=|S|
,
m^{\prime}=|T_{L}(S)|
. Then

E_{x}(S)-E_{x}(I_{m})\geq E_{y}(T_{L}(S))-E_{y}(I_{m^{\prime}}).

Proof.

Suppose first that
n\notin S
, i.e.,
T_{L}(S)=S
, so
m=m^{\prime}
. Let
d_{i}=y_{i}-x_{i}
. Write
S=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{m}\}
where
s_{1}<\dots<s_{m}
. By Lemma
6
.
f
,
d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots<d_{n-1}
. Therefore, using Lemma
8
,

\displaystyle E_{x}(S)-E_{x}(I_{m})-E_{y}(S)+E_{y}(I_{m})=2B_{x}(S)-2B_{y}(S)

\displaystyle+2n\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}-\sum_{i\in S}y_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=2B_{x}(S)-2B_{y}(S)+2n\left(\sum_{i\in S}d_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=2B_{x}(S)-2B_{y}(S)+2n\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{s_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2B_{x}(S)-2B_{y}(S).

since
s_{i}\geq i
and thus
d_{s_{i}}\geq d_{i}
. Now, if
i\in S
,
j\notin S
,

, then

. Moreover,

Thus

Suppose now that

Moreover,

Theorem 3
.

Let
S\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
,
m=|S|
. Then

\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S}\frac{1}{|x_{n,i}-x_{n,j}|}\geq\sum_{i\in I_{m},j\notin I_{m}}\frac{1}{|x_{n,i}-x_{n,j}|},

where as before
x_{n,i}=(2n)^{-1/2}\xi_{n,i}
are the scaled roots of the
n
-th Hermite polynomial
H_{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, the statement is clear, since then
S=I_{m}
.

Assume now that the statement of the Theorem is true for
n-1
. Then by Lemma
9
,

\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S}\frac{1}{|x_{n,i}-x_{n,j}|}-\sum_{i\in I_{m},j\notin I_{m}}\frac{1}{|x_{n,i}-x_{n,j}|}=\left(E_{x}(S)-E_{x}(I_{m})\right)\\
\geq\left(E_{y}(T_{L}(S))-E_{y}(I_{m^{\prime}})\right),

where
m^{\prime}=|T_{L}(S)|
. But
y_{1}<\dots<y_{n-1}
are exactly the roots of
H_{n-1}
rescaled by
{2n}^{-1/2}
, thus with
C=(n/(n-1))^{1/2}
,

\sum_{i\in S,j\notin S}\frac{1}{|x_{n,i}-x_{n,j}|}-\sum_{i\in I_{m},j\notin I_{m}}\frac{1}{|x_{n,i}-x_{n,j}|}\\
\geq C\left[\sum_{i\in T_{L}(S),j\notin T_{L}(S)}\frac{1}{|x_{n-1,i}-x_{n-1,j}|}-\sum_{i\in I_{m^{\prime}},j\notin I_{m^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{|x_{n-1,i}-x_{n-1,j}|}\right]\geq 0

by the induction hypothesis. This completes the proof.
∎
