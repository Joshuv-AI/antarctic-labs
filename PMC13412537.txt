Association Between GRACE Score and Right Ventricular Function Assessed by 3D Echocardiography in Patients With Acute Inferior STEMI Undergoing Primary PCI: A Pilot Study
Authors: Hedieh Alimi, Milad Abouzari, Habibollah Esmaily, Ramin Khameneh Bagheri, Hadi Shariatifar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectiveThis pilot study aimed to explore the association between the GRACE score and right ventricular (RV) function, including ejection fraction, volumes, and stroke volume, assessed using three‐dimensional echocardiography in patients with acute inferior STEMI undergoing primary PCI.MethodsThis cross‐sectional pilot study was conducted in the angiography department of Qaem Hospital, Mashhad, from January 2020 to June 2021. Patients with acute inferior STEMI who underwent successful primary PCI and were hemodynamically stable were included. Three‐dimensional echocardiography was performed 24–48 h after PCI. The GRACE in‐hospital mortality risk score was calculated at admission. Associations between GRACE score and RV parameters were analyzed using Pearson correlation and multivariable linear regression.ResultsA total of 40 patients (mean age 56.75 ± 11.31 years; 70% male) were included. Significant negative correlations were observed between GRACE score and RV end‐diastolic volume (r = −0.314, p = 0.048) and stroke volume (r = −0.340, p = 0.032). Heart rate also showed significant associations with left and right ventricular functional parameters.ConclusionThis study demonstrates an exploratory association between GRACE score and right ventricular function in patients with acute inferior STEMI undergoing primary PCI. These findings are hypothesis‐generating and require validation in larger prospective studies with clinical outcomes.Keywords: 3D echocardiography, GRACE score, primary PCI, right ventricular function, STEMI

Introduction

Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) includes unstable angina, NSTEMI, and STEMI and remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [1, 2, 3, 4]. Risk stratification plays a central role in management of ACS, particularly in NSTE‐ACS populations [5, 6, 7].

The GRACE score is a widely validated tool used for early risk assessment in ACS and is based on clinical and laboratory variables such as age, heart rate, blood pressure, serum creatinine, Killip class, and markers of myocardial injury. However, it does not include parameters of right ventricular (RV) function [8, 9, 10].

RV dysfunction is particularly relevant in inferior STEMI due to right coronary artery involvement and may influence hemodynamic status and prognosis [4, 11]. Despite its clinical importance, RV function is not incorporated into current risk models.

This study aims to explore the association between GRACE score and RV function assessed by 3D echocardiography in patients with acute inferior STEMI undergoing primary PCI.

Methods and Materials

Study Design and Population

This cross‐sectional pilot study was conducted in Qaem Hospital, Mashhad University of Medical Sciences, from January 2020 to June 2021. A total of 40 patients with acute inferior STEMI undergoing primary PCI were included (Figure 1).

The patient selection process.

Ethical Approval

The study was approved by the institutional review board (IR.MUMS.MEDICAL.REC.1401.165). Informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Timing of Echocardiography

Three‐dimensional echocardiography was performed 24–48 h after successful PCI, following hemodynamic stabilization. This timing was chosen to ensure optimal image quality; however, RV function at this stage may still be influenced by reperfusion status, loading conditions, and recovery from ischemic stunning.

Echocardiography Protocol

Echocardiographic assessments were performed using a Philips EPIQ system with X5‐1 transducer and automated RV 3D analysis (Philips QLAB software, version 10.8 Philips QLAB software, version 10.8). Measurements included RV end‐diastolic volume, stroke volume, and ejection fraction.

All studies were performed by experienced operators blinded to clinical data. Inter‐ and intra‐observer variability was not formally assessed, which is acknowledged as a limitation due to the pilot nature of the study.

GRACE Score Calculation

The GRACE in‐hospital mortality risk model was used, incorporating age, heart rate, systolic blood pressure, creatinine, Killip class, cardiac arrest at admission, ST‐segment deviation, and elevated cardiac enzymes.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 20. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD. Pearson correlation and multivariable linear regression were used to assess associations.

Regression models were exploratory and adjusted for age, hypertension, diabetes, BMI, and LVEF. Due to small sample size, results should be interpreted cautiously. Collinearity diagnostics using variance inflation factor (VIF) showed no significant multicollinearity.

Missing data were handled using deletion for < 5% missingness and multiple imputation (m = 20 datasets) under the assumption of missing at random (MAR), including all clinical and echocardiographic variables in the imputation model.

A p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The study was reported according to the STROBE guidelines for observational studies [12].

Multivariable linear regression analysis was subsequently performed to identify variables independently associated with GRACE score (Table 4). Increasing age was independently associated with higher GRACE scores (B = 2.66, 95% CI: 1.16–4.17, p = 0.001). Conversely, higher left ventricular ejection fraction was independently associated with lower GRACE scores (B = −3.19, 95% CI: −5.08 to −1.31, p = 0.002). Among echocardiographic parameters, indexed end‐diastolic volume (EDVi) demonstrated a positive association with GRACE score (B = 3.82, 95% CI: 0.93–6.71, p = 0.012), whereas indexed end‐systolic volume (ESVi) (B = −7.85, 95% CI: −14.77 to −0.95, p = 0.028) and stroke volume (SV) (B = −1.66, 95% CI: −3.22 to −0.10, p = 0.038) remained significantly associated with GRACE score. Given the pilot nature of the study and the limited sample size, these regression findings should be interpreted cautiously and considered exploratory.

Discussion

This pilot study demonstrated an exploratory association between the GRACE risk score and RV function assessed by three‐dimensional echocardiography in patients with acute inferior STEMI undergoing primary PCI. Higher GRACE scores were associated with lower RV stroke volume and adverse ventricular functional parameters, suggesting a potential link between global clinical risk burden and RV performance in this clinical setting.

Risk stratification remains a cornerstone in the management of ACS, enabling early identification of high‐risk patients and guiding therapeutic decision‐making [1, 2, 3]. Current guidelines emphasize early risk assessment using validated tools. The GRACE score is a widely validated and guideline‐endorsed tool for risk prediction in ACS, incorporating key clinical and laboratory variables such as age, heart rate, blood pressure, renal function, and markers of myocardial injury. However, it does not include direct assessment of RV function, which may represent an additional determinant of hemodynamic status, particularly in inferior myocardial infarction [8, 9].

RV dysfunction is a well‐recognized complication of inferior STEMI due to right coronary artery involvement and may contribute to reduced cardiac output, systemic hypotension, and worse clinical outcomes [4]. In this context, RV functional impairment may provide incremental pathophysiological and prognostic information beyond traditional risk scores. Previous studies in NSTEMI patients have also demonstrated associations between electrical abnormalities and echocardiographic findings in myocardial infarction, supporting the interaction between electrical and mechanical myocardial dysfunction [13].

Coronary artery disease is characterized by complex pathophysiological processes leading to myocardial ischemia, ventricular remodeling, and functional impairment [14]. These mechanisms may partially explain the observed relationship between higher GRACE scores and impaired RV performance, as both reflect the overall burden of ischemia and systemic stress.

Three‐dimensional echocardiography provides more accurate and reproducible assessment of RV volumes and function compared with conventional two‐dimensional techniques, due to its ability to account for the complex geometry of the right ventricle. This may enhance the detection of subtle functional changes in the acute phase of myocardial infarction [11].

Heart rate, a key component of the GRACE score, was also associated with ventricular functional parameters, which may reflect increased sympathetic activation and its impact on myocardial performance during acute ischemic stress.

Clinical Implications

While RV dysfunction is not included in current GRACE‐based risk stratification, our findings suggest a potential association that warrants further investigation in larger outcome‐based studies.

Conclusion

This pilot study demonstrates an exploratory association between GRACE score and RV function in patients with acute inferior STEMI undergoing primary PCI. These findings are hypothesis‐generating and require validation in larger prospective studies with clinical outcome data.
