Pressure-Induced
Interlayer Bonding in Arsenic Revealed
by Single-Crystal Synchrotron X‐ray Diffraction in a Diamond
Anvil Cell

Matteo Ceppatelli, Marta Morana, Enrico Berretti, Kamil Dziubek, Konstantin Glazyrin, Demetrio Scelta, Alessandro Lavacchi, Roberto Bini, Maurizio Peruzzini, Manuel Serrano-Ruiz

Abstract

Arsenic was studied
by single-crystal synchrotron X-ray diffraction
during room T compression up to 37.8 GPa across the
rhombohedral (As–I, A7) to simple cubic (As–II, sc)
phase transition, using a diamond anvil cell for pressure generation
and He as a pressure-transmitting medium. The pressure dependence
of the rhombohedral lattice parameter a, angle α,
atomic position parameter u, and first (d
1) and second (d
2) neighbor
distances provides experimental evidence for a two-step pressure-induced
interlayer bond formation mechanism, underlying the transition from
the layered A7 structure to the nonlayered simple cubic one. Consistent
with phosphorus, our data demonstrate the existence of an intermediate
pseudo simple-cubic (p-sc) structure between 26.5 and 31.8 GPa. Within
this pressure interval, where α = 60° and u < 0.250, the
progressive reduction of the interlayer distances (d
1) and the corresponding elongation of the intralayer
ones (d
2) reveal a pressure-induced trans-influence effect, underpinning the transition from
trigonal pyramidal to octahedral atomic coordination and relating
the formation of the p-sc structure to the persistence of s-p orbital mixing and stereochemically active lone pair
electron density. While solving a long-standing structural issue in
As, this study provides in-depth chemical insights concerning bond
theory in pnictogens and the synthesis of innovative As-based materials.

Introduction

The high-pressure behavior of arsenic
has been explored in the
literature up to 250 GPa along the room T isotherm. At ambient pressure, the thermodynamically stable
allotrope of As is the layered crystalline As–I structure (A7, R3̅m), commonly known as gray arsenic,
which is isostructural to high-pressure A7 phosphorus and to ambient
pressure Sb–I and Bi–I.
,
 Upon room T compression, As–I converts into simple cubic As–II
(sc, Pm3̅m), with contrasting
transition pressures experimentally reported at 25 GPa and between 31.4 and 36.6 GPa. On further compression, at ∼48 GPa, As–II sluggishly
transforms into host–guest incommensurately modulated As–III,
−

 which has been observed to persist up to 94 GPa and 126 GPa, before ultimately
converting into bcc As–IV.
,

The phase transition from A7 As–I to sc As–II is
particularly important for several aspects. While setting the high-pressure
limit for the existence of layers in crystalline As, this phase transition
represents indeed a long-debated prototypical transformation in structural
chemistry. Particularly, it describes the pressure-induced transition
from a semimetallic layered structure, in which As exhibits trigonal
pyramidal coordination by three nearest neighbors in the same layer
and hosts stereochemically active lone pair (LP) electron density
(A), to a metallic
nonlayered structure, in which As is octahedrally coordinated by six
equivalent nearest neighbors with no LP stereochemical activity (B). From a chemical
point of view, understanding the mechanism of the A7 to sc phase transition
in As is paramount for chemical bond theory to model phenomena, including
electron LP localization and interlayer bond formation, which represent
key features to enable specific properties in systems exhibiting strong
primary bonds and weak secondary interactions.
−

 Moreover, like in P, this phase
transition in As is apparently related to the pressure dependence
of the superconducting T
c.
,

On a practical perspective, potential implications
concern arsenene
and in general pnictogen-based layered systems and thermoelectric,
topological, and phase change materials.
−

Nevertheless, after several decades of investigations, the
transition
pressure, the nature, and the mechanism of the A7 →
sc phase transition in
As remain elusive and still debated, essentially due to the lack of
conclusive experimental data.
,,,
 Surprisingly, most of the articles
attempting to model this phase transition in As are entirely grounded
on the contrasting powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) data by two experimental
studies dating back to a few decades,
,
 which have
set the benchmark for any theoretical investigation so far.

Despite providing precious information, the XRD studies by Kikegawa
and Iwasaki and by Beister et al. are affected by critical limitations essentially
related to the lack of atomic position determination, to the use of
a nonhydrostatic pressure-transmitting medium (PTM), to the insufficient
pressure sampling resolution and investigated pressure range, and
to the identification criterion for the phase transition, thereby
making the fine structural details of this elusive transformation
inaccessible. Particularly, as the nature of the sample is concerned,
the inherent occurrence of heavy texturing in polycrystalline As samples
makes powder-XRD ineffective to full structure determination (Section SI-1 in the Supporting Information).

On the theoretical side, various computational studies have modeled
the A7 → sc phase transition in As.
,,−

 However, the small energy difference between the two structures
and the rapid change in the density of states (DOS) across the Fermi
level, requiring high levels of convergence, have represented a computational
challenge for a long time, leading to calculated transition pressures
scattered from 19 to 36 GPa (SI-1).

Recent studies, exploring the high-pressure
behavior of gray and metastable black
arsenic,
,
 locating the A7 → sc phase transition
at 22 GPa (Raman), 25 GPa (XRD), 25.9
GPa (Raman), and 28 GPa (Raman), are affected by the same experimental limitations,
with additional complications raised by the coexistence of different
crystal structures, by the presence of defects and impurities and
by the use of black-As flakes (SI-2).

To the best of our knowledge, no conclusive experimental structural
data on this prototypical phase transition have been published.

Here, we report a synchrotron XRD study on the room T compression of single-crystal As in a diamond anvil cell (DAC) up
to 37.8 GPa, using He as a PTM. Our experiments, enabling unambiguous
high-pressure single-crystal structure determination, provide conclusive
in-depth characterization of the pressure-induced interlayer bonding
mechanism underlying the A7 to sc transformation, revealing the existence
of an intermediate pseudo simple-cubic (p-sc) structure between A7
As–I and sc As–II, analogous to the p-sc structure observed
in P,
,
 and highlighting the occurrence of trans-influence associated with the increase in As coordination.
While finally addressing a long-standing open issue about As, our
results demonstrate structural consistency in the high-pressure behavior
of As compared with lighter P, highlighting the importance of orbital
effects and LP electron density localization, which has recently emerged
as a key feature in the high-pressure chemistry of group 15 elements.
−

Results and Discussion

The A7 structure of As–I
belongs
to the space group R3̅m (n.
166) in the trigonal crystal
system and can be alternatively described in terms of hexagonal (Z = 6) or rhombohedral (Z = 2) settings
(A). The sc
structure of As–II belongs to the higher symmetry space group Pm3̅m (n. 221) in the cubic crystal
system (B).
However, the hexagonal or rhombohedral unit cell can still be used
to describe sc As–II, while comparing specific structural parameters.
For the purpose of this study, the adoption of the rhombohedral setting
description for both As–I and As–II structures turns
out to be particularly convenient, as it enables monitoring of the
A7 to sc transformation throughout the entire process by the pressure
dependence of the same quantities: the lattice parameter a, the angle α, the atomic position parameter u, and the first (d
1, intralayer) and
second (d
2, interlayer) neighbor distances
(C–E).
In the rhombohedral setting (Z = 2), the As atoms
occupy 2c Wyckoff sites at u,u,u and u̅, u̅, u̅ positions. The dimensionless atomic position parameter u represents the fractional atomic coordinate along each
of the three axes and along the body diagonal of the unit cell. The
sc structure corresponds to the special conditions defined by α
= 60° and u = 0.250.

Our experiments were
specifically designed and performed to address
all the above-mentioned issues affecting previous studies and to catch
the fine details of this elusive phase transition. All the steps of
sample preparation, aimed at obtaining and preserving single-crystal
quality throughout the whole process, from the synthesis of large
high-purity As single crystals to the obtainment of small high-quality
single crystals of suitable size for the DAC sample chamber by focused
ion beam (FIB) processing, represented indeed critical challenges
to the success of the experiments. Experimental details are highlighted
in and extensively
described in the SI.

Single-crystal synchrotron XRD in DAC was performed
at the P02.2
beamline (PETRA-III, DESY), under the
best possible chemically inert and hydrostatic conditions ensured
by the use of He as a PTM, and under high-resolution pressure sampling.
Single-crystal structure determination enabled fine monitoring of
the pressure dependence of a, α, u, d
1, and d
2 along the room T isotherm up to 37.8 GPa, providing
direct insights into the elusive details of the A7 to sc phase transition.

Relevant refinement parameters and related errors are reported
in Table SI-1. Data errors are typically
smaller than the symbol size used in the figures. The Crystallographic

Our results are essentially condensed
in . In the
stability range of the As–I
structure, α and u smoothly increase with pressure.
Correspondingly, due to the increase of density, a, d
1, and d
2 reduce. The faster decrease of d
2 compared
to d
1 is consistent with the chemical
bonding framework of layered A7 As–I, in which d
2 corresponds to longer interlayer atomic distances ruled
by weak interactions involving the electron LPs and d
1 corresponds to shorter intralayer atomic distances,
determined by covalent single bonds. This occurrence is particularly
evident also by the pressure dependence of the a
hex and c
hex lattice parameters
of the As–I structure described in hexagonal setting, which
are indeed aligned parallel and perpendicular to the layers, respectively.
The compressibility along c
hex is accordingly
larger (A and B). No discontinuity due to the solidification of
He can be observed at 11.5 GPa.

Noticeably, a slight slope variation can be appreciated
at 8–10
GPa in the pressure dependence of α, u, a, d
1 and d
2 ().

However, on further compression, our single-crystal data
reveal
new structural and chemical insights compared to previous studies.
As a matter of fact, our data clearly show that α and u, respectively, reach their 60° and 0.250 values,
characterizing the sc structure, at different pressures. While at
26.5 GPa, α suddenly jumps to 60°, without changing anymore
on further compression, at the same pressure, u =
0.241 is still lower than the 0.250 value of sc As–II, which
is only reached on further compression at 31.8 GPa (A, B, and SI-3). Furthermore, while a smoothly decreases with pressure, showing only slight
slope variations at 26.5 and 31.8 GPa, the pressure dependence of d
1 and d
2 exhibits
instead remarkable and highly informative changes at 26.5 GPa, when
α = 60° (). Indeed, while the slope of d
2 becomes more negative, indicating faster shortening of the three
interlayer distances, the slope of d
1 becomes
positive, revealing an elongation of the three intralayer ones, until d
1 and d
2 converge
into six equivalent interatomic distances (d
sc) at 31.8 GPa, as expected in the sc structure. From an atomic
coordination point of view, this experimental evidence marks the transition
from a layered structure with trigonal pyramidal coordination to a
nonlayered structure with octahedral coordination. Noticeably, the
pressure value, at which d
1 and d
2 are observed to merge into d
sc, corresponds to the attainment of the 0.250 value by
the atomic position u. Above 31.8 GPa and up to the
highest investigated pressure, d
sc slightly
and continuously decreases with pressure, consistent with the expected
stability of the sc structure.

Our data demonstrate that the
A7 to sc phase transition is a two-step
process. In the first step, taking place at 26.5 GPa, α suddenly
reaches the 60° value expected in the sc structure, while u = 0.241 still remains significantly lower than the 0.250
sc value (A, B, and SI-3). From a structural point of view, this occurrence indicates a first-order
rhombohedral to simple cubic lattice transition, in which the atoms
have not yet moved to the expected positions of the simple cubic structure.
In the second step, taking place on further compression above 26.5
GPa and culminating at 31.8 GPa, while α remains constantly
equal to 60°, the atomic position parameter u increases faster, progressively reaching the 0.250 value expected
in the sc structure at 31.8 GPa and thereafter remaining constant
up to the highest investigated pressure (A, B, and SI-3). This occurrence, indicating the continuous
displacement of the atoms toward the sc sites, corresponds to the
attainment of the full symmetry of the sc structure (Pm3̅m), through a second-order phase transition (SI-7).

Overall,
the identification of a pressure interval, in which α
= 60° and u < 0.250, reveals the existence
of a pseudo simple-cubic (p-sc) structure (R3̅m) in the phase diagram of As, intermediate between layered
A7 As–I and nonlayered sc As–II.

The transformation
of the rhombohedral lattice into a simple cubic
one during the first step of the transformation is even more evident
from the pressure dependence of the c/a hexagonal
lattice parameter ratio, suddenly reaching the theoretical 2.45 value
for a cubic lattice at 26.5 GPa, when α becomes 60° (C).
,,,
 In this regard, the separation in pressure of the second step, involving
the atomic displacement toward the sc sites, clearly emerges when
comparing the pressure dependence of c/a with that
of d
1 and d
2 (C). Indeed,
at 26.5 GPa, when α = 60° and c/a = 2.45,
the three interlayer atomic distances d
2 begin to shorten faster with pressure, as attested by the slope
of d
2 becoming more negative, indicating
the strengthening of the corresponding secondary interactions. Remarkably,
at the same pressure, the three intralayer atomic distances d
1 begin to elongate upon compression, as attested
by the slope of d
1 turning to positive,
indicating the weakening of the corresponding covalent bonds. The
shortening d
2 and the elongating d
1 distances finally merge into six equivalent
interatomic distances at 31.8 GPa.

This phenomenon is known
in inorganic chemistry as trans-influence of the
weak secondary interactions on the strong primary
bonds and is a typical scenario observed during multicenter bond formation,
,
 thus suggesting a similar mechanism to underlie the interlayer bond
formation process characterizing the phase transition from A7 As–I
to sc As–II and the related increase in the As coordination
number from 3 to 6. Noticeably, the pressure range of the trans-influence corresponds to the pressure range of the
p-sc structure, indicating that the two occurrences are intimately
related.

The existence of the p-sc structure between 26.5 and
31.8 GPa is
further confirmed by the observation of characteristic reflections
in this pressure interval, consistently emerging in the p-sc structure,
but not expected in the sc one. Despite the sample being a single
crystal and Bragg peaks appearing as spots rather than rings, the
integrated diffraction patterns shown in qualitatively highlight this occurrence.
While the merging of the As peaks is completed between 26.5 and 27.5
GPa, weak reflections originating from this merging, which are not
expected in the sc structure, still persist above this pressure. Their
intensity progressively weakens on compression above 26.5 GPa, until
completely vanishing when the full Pm3̅m symmetry of the sc structure is attained at 31.8 GPa.
The pressure interval, where the weak reflections are observed to
persist, corresponds to the pressure range of the p-sc structure,
consistent with the behavior of the p-sc structure in P.
,

Overall, considering a slight difference in the
absolute
pressure
values, at which α and u reach their limit
values, and in the width of the pressure interval separating these
two occurrences, our experimental results are in excellent agreement
with the pressure dependence of α, u, a, d
1 and d
2 calculated by Silas et al. using DFT with GGA-PBE approximation (). Silas et al. were somehow unsure whether their finding
of α and u, respectively, reaching their limit
values at different pressures (α = 60° at 25 GPa and u = 0.25 at 28 GPa) was due to a computational artifact
deriving from the use of smearing of the cutoff energy in their calculations.
However, our experiments provide evidence of this occurrence and indicate
that the corresponding pressure interval (5.6 GPa) is actually almost
twice as large as the one predicted by Silas et al. (3 GPa).

It is worth noticing
that although it is not mentioned in their
articles,
,
 a possible alternative explanation for this
occurrence, related to the existence of a different intermediate As
structure between A7 As–I and sc As–II, was elsewhere
suggested by Silas et al. to account for the mismatch in their calculated
pressure dependence of α and u. Interestingly, a similar hypothesis was proposed
by Chan et al. in the case of P, before
the experimental discovery of the p-sc structure in the phase diagram
of this element.
,

The pressure-induced mechanism
of interlayer bond formation underlying
the A7 to sc transformation, through the intermediate p-sc structure,
is indeed analogous to that observed in lighter P (A7 → p-sc
→ sc).
,
 Compared to P, where the p-sc
structure appears at 10.5 GPa and persists up to at least ∼30
GPa, the p-sc structure in As forms at 26.5 GPa and extends over a
much narrower pressure interval (5.6 GPa) up to 31.8 GPa. Noticeably,
regarding the first step, while a sharp discontinuity of α at
the A7 → p-sc transition is observed both in As (26.5 GPa)
and P (10.5 GPa), the pressure dependence
of u in As does not exhibit the discontinuity observed
in P. Regarding the second step, the experimental results on As are
particularly meaningful, providing the full pressure dependence of u, which in the case of P could not be conclusively ascertained.

Compared to the previous literature,
,−
,,,−
,,
 where the A7 structure was thought
to directly transform into the sc one, the discovery of the intermediate
p-sc structure in As, along with the observation of the trans-influence, not only redefines its phase diagram but also provides
new chemical insights into the mechanism of interlayer bond formation,
while adding focus on the orbital interactions underlying this process
(SI-7). In this regard, the discovery of
the p-sc structure in As can be indeed accounted for in terms of competing
pressure-dependent orbital effects and electrostatic interactions.
,−

Experimentally, the p-sc structure still exhibits three short
intralayer d
1 and three long interlayer d
2 distances, rather than six equivalent ones,
as instead
expected in the sc structure. The p-sc structure is thus still a layered
structure related to A7 As–I, featuring trigonal pyramidal
rather than octahedral atomic coordination. This occurrence underlies
the persistence of LP electron density localization and stereochemical
activity, that are fingerprints of s-p orbital mixing.
Theoretically, the A7 structure has been traditionally interpreted
as a Peierls distortion of the sc structure and the A7 → sc
phase transition has been accordingly pictured as a pressure-induced
suppression of this Peierls distortion.
,,−

 However, the presence of s-p orbital mixing has been shown to play a role in stabilizing
the A7 structure of P and suggested to be relevant also in As.
,,,,,
 Specifically, s-p orbital mixing is found to dominate at low pressure
and to stabilize the A7 structure, whereas s-d orbital
mixing, along with the electrostatic contribution, is found to take
over on further compression and to stabilize the more compact sc structure
at higher pressure, consistent with the increased contribution of
the electron density of states at the Fermi level upon the phase transition.
,−

According to our interpretation, in the intermediate pressure
range,
a delicate interplay, between s-p orbital mixing
on one side and s-d orbital mixing and electrostatic
interactions on the other, is responsible for the existence of the
p-sc structure. In these conditions, the lattice is cubic, but the
displacement of the atoms toward the sc sites is hindered by the persistence
of residual stereochemically active LP electron density, which is
stabilized by s-p orbital mixing. LP electron expression
and stereochemical activity, however, progressively vanish during
the interlayer bond formation process, as s-p orbital
mixing is reduced and s-d orbital mixing and electrostatic
interactions become dominant with increasing pressure. The attainment
of the sc structure is therefore impeded as long as this delicate
interplay persists, suggesting the effective atomic orbital overlap
characterizing the sc structure to be prevented by the persistence
of s-p orbital mixing. Overall, the process reveals
a progressive change from coordination number 3 in trigonal pyramidal
coordination to coordination number 6 in octahedral coordination,
in agreement with the pressure coordination rule.
−

This
interpretation also accounts for the differences in the high-pressure
behavior of the p-sc structure in As compared with that in P, actually
strengthening their consistency. As a matter of fact, the smaller
pressure interval of the p-sc structure in As compared to P is consistent
with the reduced extent of the s-p orbital mixing
with increasing atomic number and with increasing pressure, whereas
the onset of the p-sc structure in P at lower pressure compared to
As can be accounted for by the fact that the s-d orbital
mixing in P increases faster with pressure and is more effective than
in As
,,
 due to the
presence of the empty 3d shell and to the specific
pressure dependence of d orbitals in P
,,,
 (SI-8).

The progressive loss of
LP electron density localization during
the A7 to sc phase transition, through the intermediate p-sc structure
(A7 → p-sc → sc), has been modeled for the analogous
transformation in P. Specifically, Galy
and Vignoles reported the gradual pressure-induced splitting of the
LP electron density characterizing the A7 structure of P into a three-lobe
orbital interaction with the three second neighbors during interlayer
bond formation toward the sc structure.
,,
 Interestingly, a similar orbital behavior has been
recently modeled by Osman et al. for the A7 → sc phase transition
in As. Despite not reporting any intermediate
structure and calculating the phase transition to sharply take place
at 25 GPa, Osman et al. observed a residual trigonal distortion of
the sc structure in their calculations just above this pressure. Moreover,
their calculated interatomic distances are in qualitative agreement
with our experimental values and with the calculated values by Silas
et al., thus further supporting the occurrence
of trans-influence and multicenter bonding.

Additionally, in their first study, Silas et al. correctly predicted
α to reach its high-symmetry value of 60° at lower pressure
compared to u, proposing
the pressure-induced electronic changes associated with the A7 →
sc phase transition to drive u to the 0.250 high-symmetry
value. However, in their successive calculations on the pressure dependence
of the Fermi surface, they suggested these electronic changes to rather
first drive α to its high-symmetry value. This picture is fully consistent with our experimental
data and further support the key role of orbital interactions and
LP electron density localization in driving the mechanism of the A7
to sc phase transition and in determining the existence of the p-sc
structure.

The persistence of s-p orbital mixing
and the
stereochemically active role of the LP electron density in determining
the existence of the p-sc structure between 26.5 and 31.8 GPa are
also highlighted by the pressure dependence of φ1, φ2, and φ3, respectively, representing
the intralayer As–As–As angle of the trigonal pyramidal
As coordination (φ1) and the interlayer equatorial-to-axial
(φ2) and axial-to-axial (φ3) angles
of the incipient octahedral coordination ().

While the three angles smoothly
evolve with pressure in the A7
structure, φ2 suddenly approaches the 90° value
at 26.5 GPa, when α = 60°. Correspondingly, at the same
pressure, the pressure dependence of φ1 and φ3 exhibits an abrupt slope change. Between 26.5 and 31.8 GPa,
in the pressure range of the p-sc structure, the abrupt decrease of
the intralayer angle φ1 and the steep increase of
the interlayer axial-to-axial one φ3, respectively,
reaching their 90° and 180° sc values, mirror the trans-influence of the interatomic distances d
1 and d
2.

Based on the
valence shell electron pair repulsion (VSEPR) theory,
bonding angles resulting from local atomic orbital hybridization are
particularly insightful at revealing LP stereochemical activity. The LP electron density at the apex of the trigonal
pyramidal As coordination polyhedron, pointing at the adjacent layer
and occupying the space between the three second neighbors, is expected
to be directly involved in this process and to be particularly affected
by the displacement of the atoms along the body cell diagonal, taking
place in the second step of the transformation and leading to the
final reduction of the interlayer distance. This interpretation consistently
accounts for the abrupt changes in the pressure dependence of φ1 and φ3, underpinning the persistence of
LP stereochemical activity and s-p orbital mixing
in the pressure range of the p-sc structure. The attainment of the
sc structure at 31.8 GPa, associated with the loss of LP stereochemical
activity, is highlighted by the fulfillment of the φ1 = 90°, φ2 = 90°, and φ3 = 180° conditions.

Finally, relevant insights emerge
from our data by considering
that interlayer interactions involving the electron LPs apparently
begin to strengthen with density in the stability field of the A7
structure, where the interlayer distances reduce more than the intralayer
ones, well before the onset of the p-sc structure (). This is clearly revealed
by the smooth and sluggish change in the slope of the pressure dependence
of α, u, a, d
1, and d
2 at ∼8–10
GPa (). Interestingly,
the occurrence of subtle structural modifications in the interatomic
distances between 10 and 22 GPa was suggested also by Beister et al.,
based on the flattening of the room T Raman phonon
frequency ratio R = ν­(E

g
)/ν­(A
1g
) in this pressure interval and possibly related
to the decrease of the resistivity and the onset of superconductivity
at low temperature. In this regard, despite
not correlating their findings to the Raman data by Beister et al.,
Silas et al. observed the opening of an electron pocket at 8–10
GPa at the T point of the first Brillouin zone (BZ),
while calculating the pressure dependence of the Fermi surface of
As across the A7 to sc phase transition. In agreement with their previous study, they found the phase transition to take place at 27 GPa, when the
hole pockets appearing at the W point of the first
BZ connect on opposite sides with two neighboring electron pockets,
one of which corresponds to the electron pocket opened at the T point at 10 GPa. In this
respect, a recent DFT theoretical study has reported superconductivity
to originate from broken-symmetry atomic configurations, in which
the electron–phonon interaction creates resistance-free straight
one-dimensional tunnels (SODTs) for charge density of electrons and/or
holes, that are related to the Cooper
pairs in the Bardeen–Cooper–Schrieffer theory. In the case of As, these tunnels are not present
at ambient conditions, but their formation is seemingly induced by
pressure, in agreement with the calculations by Silas et al.

Moreover, according to the experiments
by Chen et al. and to the calculations
by Chan et al., the pressure dependence
of the superconducting T
c of As exhibits
an asymmetrically shaped maximum
at ∼32 GPa, which appears to be in excellent agreement with
the observation of the p-sc structure in the 26.5–31.8 GPa
pressure range and with the attainment of the sc structure at 31.8
GPa. While these phenomena have been traditionally interpreted based
on the A7 and sc structures, with most recent experimental studies
providing low sampling resolution in this pressure range and contrasting
results at higher pressure,
,,,,,−

 the discovery of the
intermediate p-sc structure in the phase diagram of As at room T may suggest the existence of a different underlying crystal
structure and help shed light on the controversial pressure dependence
of the superconducting T
c in As.

Conclusions

The results of this study provide in-depth
structural and chemical
insights into the long-debated prototypical phase transition from
layered A7 As–I to nonlayered sc As–II. Taking advantage
of single-crystal structure determination by synchrotron XRD in DAC,

Our data unveil a two-step pressure-induced mechanism
driving the
interlayer bond formation process across the A7 to sc phase transition.
The first step takes place at 26.5 GPa, when the attainment of α
= 60° marks a rhombohedral to simple cubic lattice transition,
whereas the second step takes place at 31.8 GPa, when u finally reaches the 0.250 value, indicating that atoms have finally
moved to the expected sc sites. This experimental evidence demonstrates
that a pseudo simple-cubic structure exists in the phase diagram of

Furthermore, the pressure dependence of the first and second
neighbor
interatomic distances reveals the occurrence of a trans-influence effect in the pressure range of the p-sc structure, underlying
the interlayer bond formation mechanism and leading to the increase
in the coordination number of As from 3 to 6. From a chemical point
of view, the discovery of the p-sc structure in As, bridging the transition
from A7 As–I to sc As–II, highlights the persistence
of s-p orbital mixing, which favors LP electron density
localization and trigonal pyramidal over octahedral atomic coordination,
while preventing the attainment of the full symmetry of the simple
cubic structure by the hindered atomic occupation of the sc sites.

While solving a long-standing structural debate on As, this study
provides experimental evidence for the pressure-driven interlayer
bonding mechanism underlying the increase of the density of states
at the Fermi level across the associated semimetal-to-metal transition.
