Abstract
. We introduce the notion of a uniformly recurrent subalgebra (URA) for a trace-preserving action of a countable discrete group Γ \Gamma on a finite von Neumann algebra M M , providing an operator-algebraic counterpart to the theory of uniformly recurrent subgroups (URS). We also show that the Effros-Maréchal space Sub ⁡ ( M ) \Sub(M) is compact if and only if M M lacks a diffuse direct summand. Leveraging this, we show that URAs can exhibit arbitrary topological complexity and construct exotic URAs homeomorphic to any prescribed minimal Polish space. In the context of crossed products M ⋊ Γ M\rtimes\Gamma with amenable coefficients, we utilize URAs to formulate a new characterization of C*-simplicity, proving that Γ \Gamma is C*-simple if and only if the only amenable URA of the crossed product containing M M is { M } \{M\} . Finally, to bypass the failure of compactness in Sub ⁡ ( M ) \Sub(M) , we develop a generalized state-space machinery using Baire-category methods on the weak-* compact space of trace-extending states. This construction captures compact, discrete, and exotic URAs, while recovering the classical URS framework as a special case.



1.
Introduction

The study of
\text{Sub}(\Gamma)
associated with a group
\Gamma
, has opened up new directions of research at the interface of geometric group theory, ergodic theory, and operator algebras. The Chabauty space
\Sub(\Gamma)
serves as a unifying geometric object for a wide range of rigidity questions. Classical rigidity phenomena—such as Margulis’s normal subgroup theorem
[
17
]
and the Nevo–Stuck–Zimmer theorems
[
24
,
21
,
20
]
on lattices in higher-rank semisimple Lie groups—can all be formulated as statements about the
\Gamma
-dynamics on
\Sub(\Gamma)
via the classification of invariant random subgroups (IRS)
[
1
,
4
]
. On the topological dynamics side, using the notion of Uniform Recurrent subgroups introduced by Glasner-Weiss
[
7
]
, Kennedy’s fundamental theorem
[
13
]
characterizes the
C^{*}
-simplicity of
\Gamma
in terms of the absence of non-trivial amenable URS. The subsequent works of Le Boudec and Matte Bon
[
15
]
have placed URS at the heart of the modern theory of
C^{*}
-simplicity. More recently, this dynamical perspective has been greatly expanded through the study of stationary random subgroups (SRS) by Fraczyk-Gelander
[
6
]
, illustrating the broader scope of stationary measures and random dynamics on
\Sub(\Gamma)
.

The space of von Neumann subalgebras carries a natural Polish topology whose
history is somewhat winding, and which stands in marked contrast to the
Chabauty space
\Sub(\Gamma)
discussed above. Whereas
\Sub(\Gamma)
sits
inside
\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}
and is thereby a compact metric space, its operator-algebraic counterpart is in general only Polish, and this loss of compactness will shape the entire theory. The relevant topology has a long
lineage. Maréchal
[
16
]

introduced a Polish topology on the space of von Neumann algebras acting on a
fixed separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, refining the standard Borel
structure that Effros had defined on this space
[
5
]
.

Although Maréchal already observed that this topology yields streamlined
proofs for results on Borel subsets of the space of von Neumann algebras, the
circle of ideas lay largely dormant until Haagerup and Winsløw revived it in
two influential papers. In the first
[
8
]
, they established the
continuity of many natural operations on this space, including the commutant
map; in the second
[
9
]
, they uncovered a striking link with Connes’
embedding problem, proving it equivalent to the density of the isomorphism
class of the injective type III
1
factor in the space of all von Neumann
algebras acting on a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space — a
formulation resting on the Baire category theorem and on Maréchal’s theorem
that the ambient space is Polish. It is this Effros–Maréchal topology, in the
form carried by the space
\Sub(M)
of von Neumann subalgebras of a finite von
Neumann algebra
(M,\tau)
with separable predual, that all of our minimality
and continuity statements take place in.

More recently, an operator-algebraic counterpart of the study on
\text{Sub}(\Gamma)
picture has begun to emerge in two parallel directions. On the invariant-random side,
[
2
]
develops an operator-algebraic analog of the theory of invariant random subgroups, putting the rigidity story of the Invariant Random Subgroups into a non-commutative setting. On the topological-dynamics side, the recent work
[
3
]
develops an operator-algebraic analogue of uniformly recurrent subgroups by studying the space
\mathrm{PD}_{1}(\Gamma)
of normalized positive definite functions on
\Gamma
, recovering both URS and a corresponding
C^{*}
-simplicity criterion as orbit closures in the pointwise-compact space
\mathrm{PD}_{1}(\Gamma)
.

In this paper, we take a complementary approach. The natural map associating a positive definite function
\phi_{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathrm{PD}_{1}(\Gamma)
to a von Neumann subalgebra
\mathcal{M}\leq L(\Gamma)
is not injective. Therefore, rather than studying
\mathrm{PD}_{1}(\Gamma)
, we develop the operator-algebraic analogue of URS directly inside the space of von Neumann subalgebras. Let
(M,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra with separable predual; the space
\Sub(M)
of von Neumann subalgebras of
M
is a Polish space under the Effros–Maréchal topology. Given a trace-preserving action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau)
, we define a
uniformly recurrent subalgebra
(URA) to be a minimal closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
\Sub(M)
. The basic question driving this paper is to understand the richness of the world of URAs and the tools available for constructing and classifying them.

Before we can pursue this, however, we must address a structural point. In the URS setting,
\Sub(\Gamma)
is a compact metric space, and Zorn’s lemma immediately produces minimal
\Gamma
-invariant subsets. For von Neumann subalgebras, the situation is markedly different.
\Sub(M)
is in general only a Polish space, and whether or not it is compact turns out to depend on the type structure of
M
. Our first main theorem gives a complete characterization, along with an embedding theorem that quantifies just how badly compactness can fail.

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a finite von Neumann algebra with separable predual. The following are equivalent.

(1)

M
has a non-zero diffuse direct summand.

(2)

For every Polish space
X
, there exists a closed topological embedding
X\hookrightarrow\Sub(M)
.

(3)

\Sub(M)
is not compact.

This improves
[
3
, Theorem 2.5]
, where it was shown that
\Sub(M)
is non-compact in the non-trivial case. The failure of compactness is the structural obstruction that shapes the entire theory developed in this paper. Despite this obstruction, URAs are abundant and exhibit remarkable structural diversity. URAs in
\Sub(L(\Gamma))
can be wild and exotic in a way that has no counterpart on the subgroup side since the Chabauty space
\Sub(\Gamma)
is a closed subset of
\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}
and is, in particular, compact. In contrast,
\Sub(L(\Gamma))
supports URAs with any prescribed minimal Polish topology. We make this precise in our second main theorem, which can be regarded as a wildness statement complementing Theorem
A
. We say that a URA of
L(\Gamma)
is called
exotic
if it is not of the form
\{L(\Lambda):\Lambda\in X\}
for some URS
X
.

Theorem B
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countably infinite abelian group. For every minimal Polish space
X
, there exists an exotic URA
\mathcal{U}_{X}\subset\Sub(L(\Lambda\wr\mathbb{F}_{\infty}))
that is homeomorphic to
X
.

This says that the topology of URAs can be arbitrarily complicated. In a different direction,
\Sub(L(\Gamma))
also supports discrete URAs that have no analog inside
\Sub(\Gamma)
. Discreteness in the Chabauty topology forces a URS to be a finite conjugacy class, whereas
\Sub(L(\Gamma))
contains discrete URAs of infinite cardinality coming from fixed-point subalgebras of automorphisms of
\Gamma
(see Section
6.4
for concrete examples and Theorem
6.9
for the theorem). The full structural diversity of URAs — compact, exotic, and discrete — is the object of the subsequent sections (Sections
4
,
6.2
,
6.3
, and
6.4
).

We now explain the construction underlying many of our URA examples, which makes Theorem
D
effective. The natural attempt is to imitate
[
3
]
and study orbit closures of
\phi_{\mathcal{M}}
inside the pointwise-compact space
\mathrm{PD}_{1}(\Gamma)
. This works perfectly at the level of subgroups, but at the level of subalgebras, the program runs into the obstruction quantified by Theorem
A
since the pointwise limits of
\phi_{\mathcal{M}}
need not arise from any von Neumann subalgebra, and the natural ambient space — subalgebras of
L(\Gamma)
— is generically not compact. To bypass this obstruction, we develop a highly general state-space centralizer construction. Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra faithfully represented on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, equipped with a trace-preserving
\Gamma
-action. Let
A\subset\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a chosen
\Gamma
-invariant unital
C^{*}
-algebra. Rather than working directly in
\Sub(\mathcal{M})
, we enlarge the ambient space to the weak-
*
compact convex set of states on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
extending the canonical trace:

K=\{\varphi\in S(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})):\varphi|_{\mathcal{M}}=\tau\}.

Zorn’s lemma produces minimal closed
\Gamma
-invariant subsets
\mathcal{Z}\subset K
. To each
\varphi\in\mathcal{Z}
we associate its centralizer
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\subset\mathcal{M}
relative to
A
, defined as the von Neumann algebra generated by elements
z\in\mathcal{M}
satisfying
\varphi(zy)=\varphi(yz)
for all
y\in A
. The centralizer map
\Phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\Sub(\mathcal{M})
given by
\Phi(\varphi)=\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
is only upper semi-continuous, but a Baire-category argument supplies a dense
G_{\delta}
set of continuity points.
The space
K
is weak-
*
compact, so Zorn’s lemma produces minimal closed
\Gamma
-invariant subsets
\mathcal{Z}\subset K
.

Theorem C
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}\subset K
be a minimal closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset. For any continuity point
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}
of the centralizer map
\Phi
, the Effros–Maréchal orbit closure

\mathcal{H}=\overline{\{\alpha_{g}(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi_{0}}):g\in\Gamma\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}

is a minimal closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
\Sub(\mathcal{M})
, and thus a URA. Moreover, every compact URA arises in this way.

As mentioned above, every
compact
URA arises from the minimal-state-space construction of Theorem
C
(see Theorem
6.7
). Moreover, every
discrete
URA also arises from a broader version of the construction in which the ambient state space is no longer required to be minimal (Theorem
6.9
). This broadening is forced on us because the minimal version provably cannot capture discrete URAs with infinite-index stabilizers, as a concrete example shows (Proposition
6.8
). The dichotomy is itself a manifestation of the compactness obstruction of Theorem
A
. We also show that every URS arises from this state-space machinery (Theorem
6.21
), recovering the Glasner–Weiss classification in our framework.

We now turn to applications. Given a trace-preserving action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau_{M})
on an amenable finite von Neumann algebra, we show that C*-simplicity of
\Gamma
is equivalent to the absence of non-trivial amenable URAs in the crossed product
M\rtimes\Gamma
containing
M
. This extends
[
3
]
from the trivial coefficient algebra
\mathbb{C}
to arbitrary amenable coefficients, and at the same time provides a URA-theoretic characterization of C*-simplicity. Our result also generalizes Kawabe’s work
[
11
]
on the ideal structure of
C(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
for minimal
\Gamma
-spaces
X
.

Theorem D
.

Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau_{M})
be a trace-preserving action of a countable discrete group on an
amenable finite von Neumann algebra with separable predual, and
X
be a minimal
\Gamma
-space. The following are equivalent.

(1)

The reduced crossed product

is simple.

(2)

For all

containing

-confined in the crossed product

.

(3)

There exists

is not
M
-confined in the crossed product
M\rtimes\Gamma
.

(4)

For all
x\in X

of

M
and such that
\mathcal{U}\cap\Sub(M\rtimes\Gamma_{x})\neq\emptyset

.

(5)

There exists

such that the only amenable URA

of

Taking

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present the background on which the rest of the paper rests. We begin with hypertraces and the characterization of amenability for von Neumann subalgebras, then introduce the Effros–Maréchal topology on
\Sub(M)
, and finally record several lemmas on fixed-point subalgebras of finite-order automorphisms and on minimal Polish spaces that will be used repeatedly.

2.1.
Von Neumann algebras and hypertraces

Let
(M,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra. We will always assume that
M\subset\mathbb{B}({\rm L}^{2}(M))

2.2.
The space of von Neumann subalgebras

We now describe the topology in which all of our minimality and continuity statements take place.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra and
{\rm Sub}(M)
the set of its von Neumann subalgebras (with the same unit). For
N\in\Sub(M)
, let
E_{N}:M\rightarrow N
be the unique
\tau
-preserving conditional expectation. The Effros-Maréchal topology on
\Sub(M)
is the smallest topology for which the maps
\Sub(M)\rightarrow{\rm L}^{2}(M)
,
N\mapsto E_{N}(x)\xi_{\tau}
are continuous for the norm topology on
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
,
\forall x\in M
(where
\xi_{\tau}\in{\rm L}^{2}(M)
is the cyclic vector). If
M
has a separable predual, then
\Sub(M)
is a Polish space. The Effros-Maréchal topology is also the smallest topology on
\Sub(M)
for which the maps
N\mapsto\|E_{N}(x)\|_{2}
are continuous, for all
x\in M
. For the remainder of this section, we assume that
M
has a separable predual. We sometimes abbreviate the Effros-Maréchal topology as EM-topology.

2.3.
The Jones and Pimsner–Popa index

Two of our families of exotic URAs are distinguished by an index invariant, which
we now recall. Let
(M,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra and

N\in\Sub(M)
, with
E_{N}:M\to N
the trace-preserving conditional expectation.
Following Pimsner–Popa
[
22
]
, set

\lambda(N):=\sup\{c>0:E_{N}(x)\geq cx\ \forall x\in M^{+}\}\in[0,1],

where
M^{+}
denotes the positive cone of
M
. The
Pimsner–Popa index

of
N
in
M
is

[M:N]:=\lambda(N)^{-1}\in[1,\infty].

When
M
is a
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor and
N\subseteq M
a subfactor, this
coincides with the Jones index
[
10
]
defined via the basic
construction
\langle M,e_{N}\rangle
, where
e_{N}:L^{2}(M,\tau)\to L^{2}(N,\tau)
is the Jones projection introduced above. We will only need the following two elementary facts.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra and
\theta\in{\rm Aut}(M,\tau)
a
trace-preserving automorphism. Then
[M:\theta(N)]=[M:N]
for every

N\in\Sub(M)
. In particular, for a trace-preserving action

\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau)
, the index
[M:gNg^{-1}]
is constant along the
orbit
\{gNg^{-1}:g\in\Gamma\}
.

Proof.

Since
\theta
is a trace-preserving automorphism,
E_{\theta(N)}=\theta\circ E_{N}\circ\theta^{-1}
, and
\theta
preserves positivity and the order. Hence

E_{N}(x)\geq cx
for all
x\in M^{+}
if and only if
E_{\theta(N)}(y)\geq cy
for
all
y\in M^{+}
, so
\lambda(\theta(N))=\lambda(N)
.

x\in L(G)^{+}
, we have
E(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}g^{j}xg^{-j}\geq\frac{1}{n}x
. Thus
[L(G):N]\leq n
.

Since all projections
p_{k}
have trace
\frac{1}{n}
and
L(G)
is a
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor,
they are equivalent. Choose partial isometries
\{e_{ij}\}_{i,j=0}^{n-1}
in
L(G)

such that

e_{ii}=p_{i},e_{ij}^{*}=e_{ji},e_{ij}e_{jk}=e_{ik}
.
Set
x=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}e_{ij}
, which is a projection in
L(G)
and hence positive with norm one. Moreover,
E(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}e_{ij})p_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{n}e_{kk}=\frac{1}{n}
. Thus, if
E(x)\geq cx
for some
c>0
, then
c=\|cx\|\leq\|E(x)\|=\frac{1}{n}
. Thus
[L(G):N]\geq n
.

Hence,
[L(G):N]=n
.
∎

2.4.
\Gamma
-spaces and
\Gamma
-boundaries

We will repeatedly use the notions of minimality and strong proximality. A
\Gamma
-space is called
minimal
if it has no non-trivial
\Gamma
-invariant closed subset, or equivalently if every
\Gamma
-orbit is dense. A
\Gamma
-boundary
(Furstenberg) is a compact Hausdorff
\Gamma
-space
X
that is:

•

minimal
: no non-trivial closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset;

•

strongly proximal
: for every
\mu\in\mathrm{Prob}(X)
, the orbit closure
\overline{\Gamma\cdot\mu}
in
\mathrm{Prob}(X)
meets the set of Dirac measures
\{\delta_{x}:x\in X\}
.

Up to
\Gamma
-equivariant continuous surjection, there is a unique maximal
\Gamma
-boundary, the
Furstenberg boundary

\partial_{F}\Gamma
.

We will also need a relative version of this notion in the presence of a
\Gamma
-action on a compact space
X
. Following
[
11
]
(and
[
18
]
), the
generalized Furstenberg boundary

\widetilde{X}
of
X
is a compact
\Gamma
-space equipped with a
\Gamma
-equivariant continuous surjection
q:\widetilde{X}\to X
which is universal among
\Gamma
-boundaries fibring over
X
that satisfies the following.
\widetilde{X}
is minimal and for every
\nu\in\text{Prob}(\widetilde{X})
, if the push forward of
\nu
on X via
q
is contractible, then
\nu
is contractible. This object plays the role for the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
that the Furstenberg boundary plays for
\Gamma
itself, and underlies our criterion for simplicity of
C(X)\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
.

2.5.
Automorphisms and fixed point subalgebras

Several of the URA constructions later in the paper rest on fixed-point subalgebras of finite-order automorphisms. The next three lemmas collect the technical facts we will need. The first is a rigidity statement which says that if two automorphisms of an infinite i.c.c. group have the same fixed-point subalgebra in
L(G)
, then they generate the same cyclic group of automorphisms.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be an infinite i.c.c. group. Let
\phi,\psi
be two automorphisms on
G
with finite order
n\geq 2
. Let
L(G)^{\phi}=\{x\in L(G):\phi(x)=x\}
be the fixed point von Neumann subalgebra. Define
L(G)^{\psi}
similarly. If
L(G)^{\phi}=L(G)^{\psi}
, then
\langle\phi\rangle=\langle\psi\rangle\subset Aut(G)
.

Proof.

Because
L(G)^{\phi}=L(G)^{\psi}
, the unique trace-preserving conditional expectations from
L(G)
onto these fixed-point subalgebras must be identical. Let
E_{\phi}
and
E_{\psi}
denote these conditional expectations. For any canonical unitary
u_{g}\in L(G)
, their actions are given by averaging over the generated cyclic groups:

E_{\phi}(u_{g})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}u_{\phi^{i}(g)}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{\psi}(u_{g})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}u_{\psi^{i}(g)}

Since
E_{\phi}=E_{\psi}
, we have the operator equality
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}u_{\phi^{i}(g)}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}u_{\psi^{i}(g)}
for every
g\in G
. Because the canonical unitaries
\{u_{h}\}_{h\in G}
form an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(G)
, the terms in these two finite sums must match exactly. Consequently, the multisets of group elements must be identical, i.e.,

\{\phi^{i}(g)\}_{i=0}^{n-1}=\{\psi^{i}(g)\}_{i=0}^{n-1}.

Thus, for every
g\in G
, there exists an integer
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
such that
\psi(g)=\phi^{k}(g)
. For each
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
, define

H_{k}=\{g\in G\mid\psi(g)=\phi^{k}(g)\}.

Because
\psi
and
\phi^{k}
are both group automorphisms, it is straightforward to verify that each
H_{k}
is a subgroup of
G
. Note that
G=\bigcup_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{k}
. It is well-known that a group cannot be the union of finitely many subgroups unless at least one of those subgroups has finite index (see
[
19
]
). Therefore, there exists some
m\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
such that the subgroup
H_{m}
has finite index in
G
.

Let
\alpha=\psi^{-1}\circ\phi^{m}
. Note that
\alpha
is an automorphism of
G
, and by definition of
H_{m}
, we have
\alpha(x)=x
for all
x\in H_{m}
. Let
N
be the normal core of
H_{m}
. Since
H_{m}
has finite index in
G
,
N\triangleleft G
has also finite index in
G
. Moreover,
\alpha(x)=x
for all
x\in N
. Let
g\in G
and
x\in N
be given. Since
gxg^{-1}\in N
, applying the automorphism
\alpha
gives us that

\alpha(gxg^{-1})=gxg^{-1}
which in turn
simplifies to
\alpha(g)x\alpha(g)^{-1}=gxg^{-1}
. Consequently, it follows that

g^{-1}\alpha(g)x=xg^{-1}\alpha(g)
. Since
x\in N
is arbitrary, it follows that
g^{-1}\alpha(g)\in C_{G}(N)
. We claim that
C_{G}(N)=\{e\}
. If an element
c\in C_{G}(N)
, then we have that
N\leq C_{G}(c)\leq G
so that
C_{G}(c)
has finite index in
G
. This shows that
\#\{gcg^{-1}:g\in G\}<\infty
. Since
G
is i.c.c., it must be that
c=e
.

Consequently,
g^{-1}\alpha(g)=e
, which implies
\psi=\phi^{m}
. This implies that
\langle\psi\rangle\subseteq\langle\phi\rangle
. Since both generated groups have order
n
, we conclude that
\langle\phi\rangle=\langle\psi\rangle
.
∎

The second technical fact is an index estimate that says that a non-trivial finite-order automorphism of an i.c.c. group cannot have a finite-index fixed-point subgroup.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be an infinite i.c.c. group. Let
\phi
be an automorphism on
G
with finite order
n\geq 2
. Then the fixed point subgroup
Fix(\phi)
has infinite index in
G
.

Proof.

Assume not, say
[G:Fix(\phi)]=k<\infty
. Write
G=\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}Fix(\phi)g_{i}
for some
g_{i}\in G
.

Define
\Phi:G\rightarrow G
by
\Phi(g)=g^{-1}\phi(g)
. Then note that for each
h\in Fix(\phi)
and
i\geq 1
, we have that

\displaystyle\Phi(hg_{i})=(hg_{i})^{-1}\phi(hg_{i})=g_{i}^{-1}\phi(g_{i}).

Thus
\Phi
is constant on each right coset
Fix(\phi)g_{i}
, therefore
\sharp\Phi(G)\leq k
.

Next, since
\phi\neq Id
, there exists some
s\in G
with
s\neq\phi(s)
; equivalently,
t:=s^{-1}\phi(s)=\Phi(s)\neq e
.

Then for each
h\in Fix(\phi)
, observe that

\displaystyle h^{-1}th=h^{-1}s^{-1}\phi(s)\phi(h)=(sh)^{-1}\phi(sh)=\Phi(sh)\in\Phi(G).

Therefore,
\sharp\{h^{-1}th:h\in Fix(\phi)\}\leq\sharp\Phi(G)\leq k<\infty
; equivalently,
[Fix(\phi):C_{G}(t)\cap Fix(\phi)]<\infty
. Since
[G:Fix(\phi)]<\infty
, the above implies that
[G:C_{G}(t)]\leq[G:Fix(\phi)]\cdot[Fix(\phi):C_{G}(t)\cap Fix(\phi)]<\infty
. This means the non-trivial element
t
has a finite
G
-conjugacy class, a contradiction to
the i.c.c. assumption on
G
.
∎

Finally, we record a multiset comparison. Two finite sums of canonical group unitaries that are within
\ell^{2}
-distance less than
1
must come from identical multisets of group elements. This will let us control
\ell^{2}
-distances between conditional expectations onto fixed-point subalgebras.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
be elements in a group
G
. Assume
\|v_{s_{1}}+\cdots+v_{s_{n}}-v_{t_{1}}-\cdots-v_{t_{m}}\|_{2}<1
, then
n=m
and the multisets
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
and
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\}
are equal, i.e. the two lists have the same elements with the same multiplicities. Here,
v_{g}
denotes the canonical unitary in
L(G)
for any
g\in G
.

Proof.

This is clear since

v_{s_{1}}+\cdots+v_{s_{n}}-v_{t_{1}}-\cdots-v_{t_{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}G\subseteq L(G)
and every element in
\mathbb{Z}G
with
\ell^{2}
-norm less than one must be zero.
∎

2.6.
Minimal Polish spaces

When we construct URAs from minimal dynamical systems later, the universal source of countable groups acting minimally on a given space comes from the following classical observation. In the second-countable setting, the abstract homeomorphism-group action carries a countable subgroup that acts just as minimally.

Definition 2.8
.

A topological space
X
will be called
minimal
if
\Homeo(X)\curvearrowright X
is a minimal action.

Proposition 2.9
.

Suppose that
X
is second countable. The following are equivalent.

(1)

X
is minimal.

(2)

There exists a minimal action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
of a countable discrete group
\Gamma
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Let
(U_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a countable basis of non-empty open subsets of
X
. Fix
n\geq 1
. By minimality, for every

x\in X
, the orbit
\Homeo(X)\cdot x
is dense in
X
, thus there exists
h\in\Homeo(X)
such that
h(x)\in U_{n}
. Equivalently,
x\in h^{-1}(U_{n})
. Therefore
X=\bigcup_{h\in\Homeo(X)}h^{-1}(U_{n})
is an open cover of
X
. Since
X
is second countable, we may extract a countable subcover. Thus, for each
n
, there exists a sequence
(h_{n,m})_{m\geq 1}\subset\Homeo(X)
such that
X=\bigcup_{m\geq 1}h_{n,m}^{-1}(U_{n})
. Define

\Gamma:=\langle h_{n,m}:n,m\geq 1\rangle\leq\Homeo(X).

Since
\Gamma
is generated by countably many elements, it is a countable group. We claim that
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is minimal. Let
x\in X
, and let

U\subset X
be a non-empty open set. Choose
n
such that
U_{n}\subset U
. By construction,
X=\bigcup_{m\geq 1}h_{n,m}^{-1}(U_{n})
. Hence there exists
m\geq 1
such that
x\in h_{n,m}^{-1}(U_{n})
,
or equivalently,
h_{n,m}(x)\in U_{n}\subset U
. This shows that
\Gamma\cdot x\cap U\neq\emptyset
. Thus
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is minimal.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Suppose that a countable group

\Gamma
acts minimally on
X
by homeomorphisms. Then, for every
x\in X
,
\Gamma\cdot x\subseteq\Homeo(X)\cdot x
.
Since
\overline{\Gamma\cdot x}=X
we have
\overline{\Homeo(X)\cdot x}=X
thus
\Homeo(X)\curvearrowright X
is minimal.∎

3.
Compactness of
\Sub(M)

A central feature of the group-theoretic theory of URS is that the ambient space
\Sub(\Gamma)
is compact. This compactness, inherited from the embedding into
\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}
, is what makes Zorn’s lemma immediately produce minimal
\Gamma
-invariant subsets, and underlies the entire dynamical study of URS in
\mathrm{PD}_{1}(\Gamma)
carried out in
[
3
]
.

For von Neumann subalgebras, the situation is markedly different. The aim of this section is to characterize precisely when
\Sub(M)
is compact, and to show that whenever compactness fails, it does so in such a way that
\Sub(M)
captures any Polish space as a closed subspace. The reason compactness can fail is that
M
may have a diffuse part. With a diffuse direct summand at our disposal, we can build a continuum of pairwise far-apart two-dimensional subalgebras, and these embed any Polish space.

The construction proceeds in three steps. We first encode the real line as a continuous family of projections of trace
\tfrac{1}{2}
inside
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}([0,1])
, using an explicit measurable dynamics on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
to enforce closedness (Lemma
3.1
). We then upgrade this single embedding
\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\Proj
to a closed embedding
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
inside a sufficiently large set of projections, by exploiting a diffuse direct summand (Lemma
3.2
). Finally, we pass from marked projections to subalgebras via
p\mapsto\mathbb{C}p\oplus\mathbb{C}(1-p)
(Lemma
3.3
); a Kechris embedding theorem of arbitrary Polish spaces into
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
then completes the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a closed topological embedding
\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\Proj({\rm L}^{\infty}([0,1]))
,
t\mapsto q_{t}

for the
{\rm L}^{2}
-topology. Moreover, the embedding may be chosen so that
\tau(q_{t})=\frac{1}{2}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Consider the compact metric space
Y:=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}\times[0,1]
with Borel probability measure

\nu:=\mu\otimes\lambda
where
\mu:=\bigotimes_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1}\right)
and
\lambda
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
. Since
(Y,\nu)
is a standard non-atomic probability space, there is a
trace-preserving isomorphism
{\rm L}^{\infty}(Y)\cong{\rm L}^{\infty}([0,1])
and it suffices to construct a closed topological embedding
\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\Proj({\rm L}^{\infty}(Y))
.

Write a point of
Y
as
(\omega,u)
, where
\omega=(\omega_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
u\in[0,1]
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, put
R_{n}:=\{\omega:\omega_{n}=1\}
. Then
\mu(R_{n})=\frac{1}{2}
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, write uniquely
t=n+s
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s\in[0,1)
. Define

A_{t}:=R_{n}\times(s,1]\cup R_{n+1}\times[0,s]
and
q_{t}:=1_{A_{t}}\in{\rm Proj}({\rm L}^{\infty}(Y))
. Note that
\nu(A_{t})=(1-s)\nu(R_{n})+s\nu(R_{n+1})=\frac{1}{2}
. Hence
\tau(q_{t})=\frac{1}{2}

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}
and it suffices to show that
t\mapsto q_{t}
is a closed topological embedding for the
{\rm L}^{2}
-topology.

Let us show that
t\mapsto q_{t}
is continuous. Note that, for all Borel sets
A,B\subseteq Y
,

\|1_{A}-1_{B}\|_{2}^{2}=\nu(A\triangle B).

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and let us show continuity on
[n,n+1)
. If
t=n+s
and
t^{\prime}=n+s^{\prime}
lie in the same interval

[n,n+1)
, then
A_{t}
and
A_{t^{\prime}}
differ only on the strip
\min(s,s^{\prime})<u\leq\max(s,s^{\prime})
. Therefore

\|q_{t}-q_{t^{\prime}}\|_{2}^{2}=\nu(A_{t}\triangle A_{t^{\prime}})\leq|s-s^{\prime}|
. It remains to check continuity at the integers. Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. By
definition,
A_{m}=R_{m}\times(0,1]\cup R_{m+1}\times\{0\}
hence, in
{\rm L}^{\infty}(Y)
one has
q_{m}=1_{{R_{m}}\times[0,1]}
. For
t=m+s
with
s\in[0,1)
. Since

A_{t}\setminus\left(R_{m}\times[0,1]\right)\subset R_{m+1}\times[0,s]\text{ and }\left(R_{m}\times[0,1]\right)\setminus A_{t}\subset R_{m}\times[0,s]

we have
\nu(A_{t}\triangle R_{m})\leq s
. Hence, if
t\to m
from the right, say
t=m+s
with
s\to 0^{+}
, then
\|q_{t}-q_{m}\|_{2}^{2}=\nu(A_{t}\triangle R_{m})\leq s\to 0
. If
t\to m
from the left, say
t=(m-1)+s
with
s\to 1^{-}
, then

A_{t}\setminus(R_{m}\times[0,1])\subset R_{m-1}\times(s,1]\text{ and }(R_{m}\times[0,1])\setminus A_{t}\subset R_{m}\times(s,1].

Therefore
\nu(A_{t}\triangle(R_{m}\times[0,1]))\leq 1-s\to 0
. This proves continuity at
m
.

We now show that the map is injective. Let
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
with
t\neq t^{\prime}
and write
t=n+s
and
t^{\prime}=m+r,

with
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s,r\in[0,1)
. Define
c_{t},c_{t^{\prime}}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{Z}
by

c_{t}(u)=\begin{cases}n+1,&u\leq s,\\
n,&u>s,\end{cases}\qquad c_{t^{\prime}}(u)=\begin{cases}m+1,&u\leq r,\\
m,&u>r.\end{cases}

Then
(\omega,u)\in A_{t}\Leftrightarrow\omega_{c_{t}(u)}=1
. Since
t\neq t^{\prime}
, the step functions
c_{t}
and
c_{t^{\prime}}
differ on the Borel set
U:=\{u\in[0,1]:c_{t}(u)\neq c_{t^{\prime}}(u)\}

of positive Lebesgue measure (indeed, by distinguishing the cases
n=m
,
|n-m|\geq 2
, and
|n-m|=1
, a direct computation shows that
\lambda(U)>0
). Note that, for every
u\in U
,
\mu(\{\omega:\omega_{c_{t}(u)}\neq\omega_{c_{t^{\prime}}(u)}\})=\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, by Fubini,

\nu(A_{t}\triangle A_{t^{\prime}})=\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\{\omega:\omega_{c_{t}(u)}\neq\omega_{c_{t^{\prime}}(u)}\})\,d\lambda(u)=\frac{1}{2}\lambda(U)>0.

Thus
q_{t}\neq q_{t^{\prime}}
.

Finally, we show that the image is closed and the inverse is continuous on the image. We will use the following Claim.

Claim.

If
(t_{m})_{m}
be an unbounded sequence in
\mathbb{R}
then the sequence
(q_{t_{m}})_{m}
is not Cauchy in
{\rm L}^{2}(Y)
.

Proof of the Claim.
After passing to a subsequence, we may write
t_{m}=n_{m}+s_{m}
,
n_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
s_{m}\in[0,1)
, with the pairs
\{n_{m},n_{m}+1\}
pairwise disjoint. Therefore, for every
u\in[0,1]
and
m\neq l\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mu(\{\omega:1_{A_{t_{m}}}(\omega,u)\neq 1_{A_{t_{\ell}}}(\omega,u)\})=\frac{1}{2}
. By Fubini,

\nu(A_{t_{m}}\triangle A_{t_{\ell}})=\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\{\omega:1_{A_{t_{m}}}(\omega,u)\neq 1_{A_{t_{\ell}}}(\omega,u)\})\,d\lambda(u)=\frac{1}{2}.

Hence

\|q_{t_{m}}-q_{t_{\ell}}\|_{2}^{2}=\nu(A_{t_{m}}\triangle A_{t_{\ell}})=\frac{1}{2}.

Thus the subsequence
(q_{t_{m}})_{m}
is not Cauchy.
\hfill\qed

Let us show that the image is closed in
{\rm L}^{2}(Y)
. Let
(t_{m})_{m}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{R}
such that
(q_{t_{m}})
converges in
{\rm L}^{2}(Y)
. By the Claim,
(t_{m})_{m}
is bounded. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume
t_{m}\to t\in\mathbb{R}
. By continuity,
q_{t_{m}}\to q_{t}
. This shows that the image is closed. Finally, we show that the inverse is continuous on the image. Assume that
q_{t_{m}}\to q_{t}
then, by the Claim, the sequence
(t_{m})_{m}
is bounded, so it suffices to show that
t
is the only accumulation point of the sequence
(t_{m})_{m}
. Consider a subsequence such that
t_{m_{k}}\to t^{\prime}
. By continuity,
q_{t_{m_{k}}}\to q_{t^{\prime}}
and by uniqueness of limits
q_{t^{\prime}}=q_{t}
. By injectivity, we have
t^{\prime}=t
. It completes the proof.∎

With the line of projections in hand, we now amplify it to a countable product. The key is that a sufficiently large diffuse algebra contains a sequence of mutually orthogonal projections, on each of which we can install an independent copy of the previous lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a diffuse finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal trace.
Let
e_{0}\in M
be a projection such that
\tau(e_{0})<\frac{1}{2}
. Then, for any
r\in(\tau(e_{0}),\frac{1}{2})
, there exists a closed topological embedding

\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
for the
{\rm L}^{2}
-topology, where

\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r):=\{p\in\Proj(M):e_{0}\leq p,\ \tau(p)=r\}.

Proof.

Write
s:=\tau(e_{0})
. Since
(1-e_{0})M(1-e_{0})
is diffuse, we may choose pairwise orthogonal non-zero
projections
f_{1},f_{2},\dots\leq 1-e_{0}
such that
\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau(f_{j})=2(r-s)
. For instance, one may choose
\tau(f_{j})=2^{-j}\,2(r-s)
,
j\geq 1
, inside a diffuse abelian von Neumann subalgebra of
(1-e_{0})M(1-e_{0})
. For every
j\geq 1
, the finite von Neumann algebra
f_{j}Mf_{j}
, equipped with the
normalised trace
\tau_{j}(x):=\frac{\tau(x)}{\tau(f_{j})}
,
x\in f_{j}Mf_{j}
, is diffuse. Hence,
there exists a trace-preserving embedding
{\rm L}^{\infty}([0,1])\hookrightarrow f_{j}Mf_{j}
where
f_{j}Mf_{j}
is viewed with the normalised trace
\tau_{j}
. By Lemma
3.1
, transported through this embedding, we may choose a closed topological embedding
\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\Proj(f_{j}Mf_{j})
,
t\mapsto q_{t}^{(j)}
, such that
\tau_{j}(q_{t}^{(j)})=\frac{1}{2}

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}
. In terms of the original trace
\tau
, this means
\tau(q_{t}^{(j)})=\frac{1}{2}\tau(f_{j})
. For
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
define
p_{y}:=e_{0}+\sum_{j\geq 1}q_{y_{j}}^{(j)}
. The sum is the strong operator, hence the
{\rm L}^{2}
-limit, of the increasing sequence of
finite partial sums, since the projections are supported on pairwise orthogonal
projections
f_{j}
. Thus
p_{y}
is a projection. Moreover,
e_{0}\leq p_{y}
and

\tau(p_{y})=\tau(e_{0})+\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau(q_{y_{j}}^{(j)})=s+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau(f_{j})=s+\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2(r-s)=r.

Therefore
p_{y}\in\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
. Let us show that
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
,
\Phi(y):=p_{y}
is a closed topological embedding.

Let us show that
\Phi
is continuous. Let
(y^{(m)})_{m}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
converging to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, for every fixed
j
,
q_{y^{(m)}_{j}}^{(j)}\to q_{y_{j}}^{(j)}
in
{\rm L}^{2}(f_{j}Mf_{j})
. Since
\|q_{y^{(m)}_{j}}^{(j)}-q_{y_{j}}^{(j)}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\tau(f_{j})
and
\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau(f_{j})<\infty
, the dominated convergence theorem gives

\|p_{y^{(m)}}-p_{y}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\|q_{y^{(m)}_{j}}^{(j)}-q_{y_{j}}^{(j)}\|_{2}^{2}\longrightarrow 0.

Thus
\Phi
is continuous.

Let us show that
\Phi
is injective. If
y\neq z
, choose
j
such that
y_{j}\neq z_{j}
. Since
t\mapsto q_{t}^{(j)}
is injective, we have
q_{y_{j}}^{(j)}\neq q_{z_{j}}^{(j)}
. Compressing by
f_{j}
, we get

f_{j}p_{y}f_{j}=q_{y_{j}}^{(j)}\neq q_{z_{j}}^{(j)}=f_{j}p_{z}f_{j}.

Hence
p_{y}\neq p_{z}
.

Let us show that the image is closed. Suppose that
p_{y^{(m)}}\to p
in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
, where
p\in\Proj(M)
. For each
j\geq 1
, compression by
f_{j}
gives
q_{y^{(m)}_{j}}^{(j)}=f_{j}p_{y^{(m)}}f_{j}\to f_{j}pf_{j}
in
{\rm L}^{2}(f_{j}Mf_{j})
. Since the image of
t\mapsto q_{t}^{(j)}
is closed, there exists
y_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f_{j}pf_{j}=q_{y_{j}}^{(j)}
. Also,
e_{0}p_{y^{(m)}}=e_{0}
for all
m
. Passing to the
{\rm L}^{2}
-limit gives

. Similarly,
p_{y^{(m)}}e_{0}=e_{0}
for all

so

. Thus
e_{0}\leq p
. Define

and note that
f_{\infty}p_{y^{(m)}}=0=p_{y^{(m)}}f_{\infty}
for all

. It follows that
f_{\infty}p=0=pf_{\infty}
. Moreover, for
j\neq k
,
f_{j}p_{y^{(m)}}f_{k}=0
for all

so
f_{j}pf_{k}=0
. It follows that

is block diagonal with respect to the orthogonal decomposition

and its diagonal blocks are
e_{0}
,
q_{y_{j}}^{(j)}\ (j\geq 1)
and

. Therefore

where

. Thus, the image of
\Phi
is closed.

Finally, we show that the inverse is continuous on the image. Suppose
p_{y^{(m)}}\to p_{y}
in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
. Compressing by
f_{j}
, we obtain
q_{y^{(m)}_{j}}^{(j)}=f_{j}p_{y^{(m)}}f_{j}\rightarrow f_{j}p_{y}f_{j}=q_{y_{j}}^{(j)}
in
{\rm L}^{2}(f_{j}Mf_{j})
. Since
t\mapsto q_{t}^{(j)}
is a topological embedding, this implies
y^{(m)}_{j}\to y_{j}
for all

hence

in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
.∎

Marked projections produce closed embeddings into a space of subalgebras almost for free, by sending a projection to the two-dimensional algebra it generates.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra. Let
0<s<r<1/2
, and let

e_{0}\in M
be a projection with
\tau(e_{0})=s
. The map

\Psi:\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)\rightarrow\Sub(M),\quad p\mapsto W^{*}(p):=\mathbb{C}p\oplus\mathbb{C}(1-p),

is a closed topological embedding, where
\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
is equipped with the

{\rm L}^{2}
-topology and
\Sub(M)
with the Effros-Maréchal topology.

Proof.

For
p\in\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
, the algebra
W^{*}(p)
is two-dimensional, and the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto
W^{*}(p)

is given by

E_{W^{*}(p)}(x)=\frac{\tau(xp)}{r}p+\frac{\tau(x(1-p))}{1-r}(1-p)\qquad x\in M.

We first prove continuity. Let
p_{i}\to p
in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
, with

p_{i},p\in\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
. For every
x\in M
, we have
\tau(xp_{i})\to\tau(xp)
and
\tau(x(1-p_{i}))\to\tau(x(1-p))
.
Using the formula above, it follows that
E_{W^{*}(p_{i})}(x)\to E_{W^{*}(p)}(x)
in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
.

Next, we show that
\Psi
is injective. Suppose that
W^{*}(p)=W^{*}(q)
for
p,q\in\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
. The non-zero minimal projections of this two-dimensional
algebra are
p,\ 1-p
but also
q,\ 1-q
. Hence
q=p
or
q=1-p
. Since
\tau(q)=r<1/2
and
\tau(1-p)=1-r>1/2
we deduce that
q=p
hence
\Psi
is injective.

We now prove that the inverse is continuous on the image. Note that for any
p\in\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
we have
e_{0}p=e_{0}
. Therefore
\tau(e_{0}p)=\tau(e_{0})=s
and
\tau(e_{0}(1-p))=0
. Using the conditional expectation formula we get
E_{W^{*}(p)}(e_{0})=\frac{\tau(e_{0}p)}{r}p+\frac{\tau(e_{0}(1-p))}{1-r}(1-p)=\frac{s}{r}p
. Hence
p=\frac{r}{s}E_{W^{*}(p)}(e_{0})
. Consequently, if
W^{*}(p_{i})\to W^{*}(p)
in
\Sub(M)
then

p_{i}=\frac{r}{s}E_{W^{*}(p_{i})}(e_{0})\longrightarrow\frac{r}{s}E_{W^{*}(p)}(e_{0})=p

in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
. Thus
\Psi^{-1}
is continuous on its image.

It remains to prove that the image is closed. Let
(p_{i})_{i}
be a net in

\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
such that

W^{*}(p_{i})\to N\quad\text{in }\Sub(M).

Then
p_{i}=\frac{r}{s}E_{W^{*}(p_{i})}(e_{0})\rightarrow\frac{r}{s}E_{N}(e_{0})
in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
. Since the set
\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
is closed in the
{\rm L}^{2}
-topology, there exists

p\in\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
such that
p_{i}\to p
in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
. By continuity,
W^{*}(p_{i})\to W^{*}(p)
and since
\Sub(M)
is Hausdorff
N=W^{*}(p)
.∎

In order to upgrade an embedding into
\Sub(zM)
for a diffuse summand
zM
to one into the whole
\Sub(M)
, we use a decompression trick, in particular to extend a subalgebra of
zM
to a subalgebra of
M
by adjoining all of
(1-z)M
on the orthogonal complement.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
M
be a finite von Neumann algebra with separable predual. For any non-zero central projection
z\in M
, the map

\Theta:\Sub(zM)\to\Sub(M)\quad N\mapsto N\oplus(1-z)M

is a closed topological embedding.

Proof.

Note that, for
N\in\Sub(zM)
, one has
E_{\Theta(N)}(a)=E_{N}(za)+(1-z)a
for all
a\in M
. It implies that
\Theta
is continuous. Note also that
z\Theta(N)=N
, for all
N\in\Sub(M)
. This implies that
\Theta
is injective. Moreover, on the image of
\Theta
, the inverse map is explicitly
\Theta^{-1}(A)=zA
for all
A\in\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)
. Hence, it suffices to check that the map
\Sub(M)\to\Sub(zM)
,
A\mapsto zA
is continuous. Write
E^{zM}_{zA}:zM\to zA
the trace preserving conditional expectation, we have
E^{zM}_{zA}(zx)=zE_{A}(x)
for all
x\in M
. Since the maps
\Sub(M)\to M
,
A\mapsto E_{A}(x)
and
M\mapsto zM
,
a\mapsto za
are both continuous for the
\|\cdot\|_{2}
norms, we deduce that
A\mapsto zA
is continuous. It remains to show that
\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)
is closed. Let
\Theta(N_{i})\to Q
in
\Sub(M)
. Since, for all
x\in M
,
(1-z)x\in\Theta(N_{i})
for all
i
, we have
E_{\Theta(N_{i})}((1-z)x)=(1-z)x
. Taking the limit we get
E_{Q}((1-z)x)=(1-z)x
hence
(1-z)M\subseteq Q
. Hence,

\Theta(zQ)=zQ\oplus(1-z)M=zQ\oplus(1-z)Q=Q

Therefore, the image of
\Theta
is closed.∎

We can now combine the previous four lemmas into the main result of the section. As soon as

has any diffuse part, the topology of
\Sub(M)
is rich enough to accommodate every Polish space; absent a diffuse summand,
M
is essentially finite-dimensional after central decomposition,
\Sub(M)
is compact, and the URS-style machinery applies directly.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
M
be a finite von Neumann algebra with separable predual. The following
are equivalent.

(1)

M
has a non-zero diffuse direct summand.

(2)

For every Polish space
X
, there exists a closed topological embedding

X\hookrightarrow\Sub(M).

(3)

\Sub(M)
is not compact.

Proof.

Note that
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
is obvious.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Let
z
be a non-zero central projection such that
zM
is diffuse. Let
\tau
be a faithful normal tracial state on
zM
. Since
zM
is diffuse, there exists a projection
e_{0}\in zM
with
0<\tau(e_{0})<\frac{1}{2}
. Let
r\in(\tau(e_{0}),1/2)
. Combining Lemma
3.2
, Lemma
3.3
, and Lemma
3.4
, we get a closed topological embedding
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\hookrightarrow\Sub(M)
. Let
X
be a Polish space. By
[
12
, Theorem 4.17]
, there exists a closed topological embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
, and by compositing with
\theta
, we get the result.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. Assume that
M
has no non-zero diffuse direct summand and let us show that
\Sub(M)
is compact. We start with the following Claim.

Claim.

For every
x\in M
, the set
\{E_{N}(x):N\in\Sub(M)\}
is relatively compact in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
.

Proof of the Claim.

Fix
x\in M
and define
S_{x}:=\{E_{N}(x):N\in\Sub(M)\}
. It suffices to show that
S_{x}
is totally bounded in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
M
is finite with separable predual and no diffuse direct summand, there exists a sequence
(z_{n})_{n}
of central projections in
M
such that
z_{n}\to 1
strongly and
z_{n}M
is finite dimensional for all
n
. One has
\|(1-z_{n})x\|_{2}\to 0
and
\tau(1-z_{n})\to 0
. Choose

large enough so that
\|(1-z_{n})x\|_{2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
and
\|x\|_{\infty}\,\tau(1-z_{n})^{1/2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
. For
N\in\Sub(M)
, one has

\displaystyle\|E_{N}(x)-z_{n}E_{N}(z_{n}x)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|E_{N}(x)-E_{N}(z_{n}x)\|_{2}+\|E_{N}(z_{n}x)-z_{n}E_{N}(z_{n}x)\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|E_{N}((1-z_{n})x)\|_{2}+\|(1-z_{n})E_{N}(z_{n}x)\|_{2}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(1-z_{n})x\|_{2}+\|E_{N}(z_{n}x)\|_{\infty}\,\tau(1-z_{n})^{1/2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\|x\|_{\infty}\,\tau(1-z_{n})^{1/2}<\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}.

Define
T_{n}:=\{z_{n}E_{N}(z_{n}x):N\in\Sub(M)\}\subset z_{n}M
and note that
T_{n}
is bounded for the
{\rm L}^{2}
-norm in the finite dimensional space
z_{n}M
. Therefore
T_{n}
is totally bounded for the
{\rm L}^{2}
-norm. Hence there exist
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k}\in{\rm L}^{2}(M)
such that
T_{n}\subset\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{k}B_{L^{2}}\left(\eta_{\ell},\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)
. Let
N\in\Sub(M)
. Since
z_{n}E_{N}(z_{n}x)\in T_{n}
, there exists
\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that
\|z_{n}E_{N}(z_{n}x)-\eta_{\ell}\|_{2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
. Using the previous estimate, we get

\|E_{N}(x)-\eta_{\ell}\|_{2}\leq\|E_{N}(x)-z_{n}E_{N}(z_{n}x)\|_{2}+\|z_{n}E_{N}(z_{n}x)-\eta_{\ell}\|_{2}<\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}=\varepsilon.

Therefore
S_{x}\subset\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{k}B_{L^{2}}(\eta_{\ell},\varepsilon)

S_{x}
is totally bounded in
L^{2}(M)
.
\hfil\qed

End of the proof of the Theorem.
Now let
(N_{m})_{m}
be a sequence in
\Sub(M)
. Since
M
has separable
predual, choose a countable
{\rm L}^{2}
-dense subset

(N_{m_{j}})_{j}
such that, for every

{\rm L}^{2}(M)

{\rm L}^{2}

{\rm L}^{2}

(E_{N_{m_{j}}}(x))_{j}

{\rm L}^{2}

, for all

. Indeed, for

and for all

\|E_{N_{m_{j}}}(x)\|_{\infty}\leq\|x\|_{\infty}
. Since the operator norm closed ball of

) is
{\rm L}^{2}

. The map linear map

\displaystyle\|E_{N_{m_{j}}}(E(x))-E_{N_{m_{j}}}(x)\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|E_{N_{m_{j}}}(E(x))-E_{N_{m_{j}}}(E_{N_{m_{j}}}(x))\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|E(x)-E_{N_{m_{j}}}(x)\|_{2}\to 0.

Therefore

. To finish the proof, it suffices to show that

is a von Neumann subalgebra of

. Indeed, Tomiyama’s theorem implies that

N_{m_{j}}\to E(M)
in

.

\displaystyle\|E_{N_{m_{j}}}(a)E_{N_{m_{j}}}(b)-xy\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(E_{N_{m_{j}}}(a)-x)E_{N_{m_{j}}}(b)\|_{2}+\|x((E_{N_{m_{j}}}(b)-y)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\|b\|\,\|E_{N_{m_{j}}}(a)-x\|_{2}+\|x\|\,\|E_{N_{m_{j}}}(b)-y\|_{2}\to_{j}0.

4.
Uniformly recurrent subalgebras

In this section, we fix a finite von Neumann algebra
(M,\tau)
with separable predual and consider the Effros-Maréchal topology on
\Sub(M)
. Let
\alpha:\Gamma\curvearrowright M
be a trace preserving action of
\Gamma
on
M
. Then, for all
g\in\Gamma
, the map
N\mapsto\alpha_{g}(N)
is a homeomorphism of
\Sub(M)
since,
\alpha
being
\tau
-preserving, we have
E_{\alpha_{g}(N)}=\alpha_{g}\circ E_{N}\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}}
for all
N\in\Sub(M)
. Hence, we get an action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Sub(M)
by homeomorphisms, still denoted
\alpha
. A subalgebra
N\subseteq M
is called
\Gamma
-invariant
if
\alpha_{g}(N)=N
for all
g\in\Gamma
.

Recall that the Chabauty topology on the set of subgroups of
\Gamma
, denoted by
\Sub(\Gamma)
, is the smallest topology for which the maps
\Sub(\Gamma)\rightarrow\{0,1\}

\Lambda\mapsto 1_{\Lambda}(g)
are continuous, for all
g\in\Gamma
. It is easy to see that
\Sub(\Gamma)
is a compact metric space since it identifies with a closed subset of
\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}
with the product topology. A URS on
\Gamma
is a minimal closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
\Sub(\Gamma)
(for the conjugation action). We now formalize the von Neumann analog.

Definition 4.1
.

A
uniformly recurrent subalgebra of
M
(URA) is a minimal closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
\Sub(M)
. A URA
\mathcal{U}\subset\Sub(M)
is called
amenable
if every
N\in\mathcal{U}
is amenable.

Two basic constructions of URAs are immediate from the definition. First, every URS of
\Gamma
gives a URA of
{\rm L}(\Gamma)
through the continuous map
\Lambda\mapsto L(\Lambda)
; the resulting URA is automatically compact since the Chabauty space
\Sub(\Gamma)
is. Second, the singleton
\{\mathbb{C}1\}
is always an amenable URA, the trivial one.

Example 4.2
.

The following are always URAs of
{\rm L}(\Gamma)
.

(1)

For every URS
X\subset\Sub(\Gamma)
, the image
\psi(X)\subset\Sub({\rm L}(\Gamma))
under the continuous map
\psi:\Sub(\Gamma)\rightarrow\Sub({\rm L}(\Gamma))
,
\Lambda\mapsto L(\Lambda)
(continuity is the special case
M=\mathbb{C}
of Lemma
5.3
below). Such a URA is always compact.

(2)

The trivial URA
\mathcal{U}:=\{\mathbb{C}1\}
, which is amenable.

Neither of these settles the interesting question — whether
{\rm L}(\Gamma)
admits URAs that genuinely belong to the operator-algebraic world rather than reducing to subgroup data — so we set apart the URAs that do. Consequently, we define the following.

Definition 4.3
.

A URA of
{\rm L}(\Gamma)
is called
exotic
if it is not of the form
\psi(X)
for any URS
X
of
\Gamma
. In particular, every non-compact URA is exotic.

The rest of this section is devoted to constructing an explicit family of exotic URAs. Specifically, we prove that every minimal Polish space arises as a URA inside
{\rm L}(\Lambda\wr\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
for
\Lambda
countable abelian. Two observations motivate this construction. First, an obstruction lemma says that if a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant image of a minimal compact space has a discrete orbit closure, then the stabilizer must have finite index. Second, every URA in
\Sub(L(\Gamma))
is automatically a minimal Polish space.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright Z
be a minimal action on a compact space,
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
an action on a topological space
X
and
\Phi:Z\to X
a
\Gamma
-equivariant map. If
z_{0}\in Z
is a point of continuity of

\Phi
and
\mathcal{O}:=\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(z_{0})}
is discrete then
\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(\Phi(z_{0}))
has finite index.

Proof.

Write
x_{0}:=\Phi(z_{0})
. Since
\mathcal{O}
is discrete, there exists an open neighborhood

W\subset X
of
x_{0}
such that
W\cap\mathcal{O}=\{x_{0}\}
. Since
\Phi
is continuous at
z_{0}
, there exists an open neighborhood

V\subset Z
of
z_{0}
such that
\Phi(V)\subset W
. Since
\Gamma\curvearrowright Z
is minimal, the return-time set
N(z_{0},V):=\{g\in\Gamma:gz_{0}\in V\}
is syndetic. Hence, it suffices to show that
N(z_{0},V)\subset\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(x_{0})
. Let
g\in N(z_{0},V)
, then
gz_{0}\in V
, hence
\Phi(gz_{0})\in W
. By equivariance,
\Phi(gz_{0})=g\Phi(z_{0})=gx_{0}
. Moreover
gx_{0}\in\mathcal{O}
. Thus
gx_{0}\in W\cap\mathcal{O}=\{x_{0}\}
so
gx_{0}=x_{0}
, i.e.
g\in\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(x_{0})
.∎

Proposition
2.9
provides the dual fact that any minimal Polish space
X
is acted upon minimally by some countable group
\Gamma\leq\Homeo(X)
. To realize

as a URA in
\Sub(M)
, one therefore needs only a closed topological
\Gamma
-equivariant encoding

; minimality of the
\Gamma
-orbit on

then transports automatically. The next lemma constructs such an encoding inside an infinite tensor product, starting from a closed topological embedding
\beta:X\hookrightarrow\Sub(M)
that need not yet be equivariant.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra with separable predual. Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
be an action by homeomorphisms of a countable group
\Gamma
on the topological space
X

\beta:X\hookrightarrow\Sub(M)
is a closed topological embedding then the map

\widetilde{\beta}:X\to\Sub\left(\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}(M,\tau)\right),\quad x\mapsto\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}(\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x),\tau)

Proof.

Recall that the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}=\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}(M,\tau)
satisfies
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
, where
\mathcal{A}
is the linear span of elementary tensors
\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}
,
a_{g}\in M
,
a_{g}=1
for all but finitely many
g
with product trace
\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}\right)=\prod_{g\in\Gamma}\tau(a_{g})
. Note that
\mathcal{A}
is a unital
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
and it is
\|\cdot\|_{2}
dense in
\mathcal{M}
.
Note that
\widetilde{\beta}(x)=\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x)
is the von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
generated by all elementary tensors
\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}b_{g}
,
b_{g}\in\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x)
, with
b_{g}=1
for all but finitely many
g
. Moreover, the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto
\widetilde{\beta}(x)
satisfies

E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}\left(\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}\right)=\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}E_{\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x)}(a_{g})

for every elementary tensor
\bigotimes_{g}a_{g}
, where
a_{g}=1
for all but
finitely many
g
.

We now prove continuity. Define the vector space

\mathcal{Z}:=\{a\in\mathcal{M}:X\to{\rm L}^{2}(\mathcal{M}),\,\,x\mapsto E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}(a)\xi_{\tau_{\mathcal{M}}}\text{ is continuous}\}

and note that
\mathcal{Z}
is
\|\cdot\|_{2}
-closed in
\mathcal{M}
. Indeed, if
\|a-a_{n}\|_{2}\to 0
with
a_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}
for all
n
and
a\in\mathcal{M}
then, for all
x\in X
,

\|E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}(a)\xi_{\tau_{\mathcal{M}}}-E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}(a_{n})\xi_{\tau_{\mathcal{M}}}\|\leq\|a-a_{n}\|_{2}\rightarrow 0.

hence, the sequence of continuous functions
(x\mapsto E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}(a_{n})\xi_{\tau_{\mathcal{M}}})
converges uniformly on
X
to the function
(x\mapsto E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}(a)\xi_{\tau_{\mathcal{M}}})
which implies that
a\in\mathcal{Z}
. Hence, to show continuity of
\widetilde{\beta}
it suffices to show that
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{Z}
and since
\mathcal{Z}
is a linear space, it suffices to show that it contains elementary tensors. Let
a=\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}
be an elementary tensor and consider the finite set
F:=\{g\in\Gamma:a_{g}\neq 1\}
. Let
(x_{i})_{i}
be a net in
X
converging to
x\in X
. The conditional expectation formula and the trace formula give that
\|E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}(a)\xi_{\tau_{\mathcal{M}}}-E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x_{i})}(a)\xi_{\tau_{\mathcal{M}}}\|
equals:

\displaystyle\|E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}(a)-E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x_{i})}(a)\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}E_{\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x)}(a_{g})-\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}E_{\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x_{i})}(a_{g})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\bigotimes_{g\in F}E_{\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x)}(a_{g})-\bigotimes_{g\in F}E_{\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x_{i})}(a_{g})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{|F|-1}\sum_{g\in F}\left\|E_{\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x)}(a_{g})-E_{\beta(g^{-1}\cdot x_{i})}(a_{g})\right\|_{2},

where
C:=\max\{1,\|a_{g}\|_{2}:g\in F\}
. Since the action is by homeomorphisms and
\beta
is continuous, we deduce that
a\in\mathcal{Z}
.

Next, we prove that
\widetilde{\beta}
is injective and that its inverse on the image
is continuous. For
a\in M
define
a^{(e)}
to be the elementary tensor
a^{(e)}:=\bigotimes_{g\in F}a_{g}\in\mathcal{A}
, where
a_{e}=a
and
a_{g}=1
for
g\neq e
. Note that
M\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
,
a\mapsto a^{(e)}
is a normal isomorphism and
E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}(a^{(e)})=E_{\beta(x)}(a)^{(e)}
. Let
(x_{i})_{i}
be a net in
X
and
x\in X
and suppose that
\widetilde{\beta}(x_{i})\to\widetilde{\beta}(x)
in
\Sub(\mathcal{M})
. Testing on
a^{(e)}
, we get

E_{\beta(x_{i})}(a)^{(e)}=E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x_{i})}(a^{(e)})\to E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x)}(a^{(e)})=E_{\beta(x)}(a)^{(e)}

in
{\rm L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
. Hence
E_{\beta(x_{i})}(a)\to E_{\beta(x)}(a)
in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
for every
a\in M
. Thus
\beta(x_{i})\to\beta(x)
in
\Sub(M)
. Since
\beta
is a topological embedding, this implies that
x_{i}\to x
. In particular,
\widetilde{\beta}
is injective and its inverse on its image is
continuous.

It remains to show that the image is closed. Let
(x_{i})_{i}
be a net in
X
and
Q\in\Sub(\mathcal{M})
be such that
\widetilde{\beta}(x_{i})\to Q
. We again restrict to the identity coordinate
e
. For
a\in M
,

E_{\widetilde{\beta}(x_{i})}(a^{(e)})=E_{\beta(x_{i})}(a)^{(e)}.

The left-hand side converges in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
to
E_{Q}(a^{(e)})
. Let
M_{e}\subset\mathcal{M}
be the von Neumann subalgebra consisting of elementary tensor
\bigotimes_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}
with
a_{g}=1
for all
g\neq 1
. Since
{\rm L}^{2}(M_{e})\subset{\rm L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
is a closed subspace and each
E_{\beta(x_{i})}(a)^{(e)}
belongs to
L^{2}(M_{e})
,
we get
E_{Q}(a^{(e)})\in L^{2}(M_{e})
. But
E_{Q}(a^{(e)})\in Q\subset\mathcal{M}
, and
L^{2}(M_{e})\cap\mathcal{M}=M_{e}

inside
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
. Therefore
E_{Q}(a^{(e)})\in M_{e}
. Hence there is a unique element
T(a)\in M
such that
E_{Q}(a^{(e)})=T(a)^{(e)}
. Define
B:=\{a\in M:a^{(e)}\in Q\}
. Since
B^{(e)}=Q\cap M_{e}
,
B
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
M
. We claim that
T=E_{B}
. Indeed, since
E_{Q}
is linear, unital, positive, normal, and trace-preserving, the same is true for
T
. Being positive and unital,
T
is a contraction and, since it is trace-preserving, it suffices to show, by Tomiyama’s Theorem, that
T(M)\subset B
and
T(b)=b
for all
b\in B
. For every
a\in M
,
T(a)^{(e)}=E_{Q}(a^{(e)})\in Q
.
Since
T(a)^{(e)}\in M_{e}
, we get

T(a)^{(e)}\in Q\cap M_{e}=B^{(e)}.

Hence
T(a)\in B
so
T(M)\subset B
. Let
b\in B
, then
b^{(e)}\in Q
, and since
E_{Q}
fixes
Q

pointwise,
T(b)^{(e)}=E_{Q}(b^{(e)})=b^{(e)}
. Thus
T(b)=b
. This shows that
T=E_{B}
. Consequently, for every
a\in M
,
E_{\beta(x_{i})}(a)\to E_{B}(a)
in
{\rm L}^{2}(M)
. Thus
\beta(x_{i})\to B
.
Since
\beta(X)
is closed in
\Sub(M)
, there exists
x\in X
such that
B=\beta(x)
. Since
\beta
is a topological embedding, the convergence
\beta(x_{i})\to\beta(x)
implies
x_{i}\to x
.
By the continuity of
\widetilde{\beta}
, already proved,
\widetilde{\beta}(x_{i})\to\widetilde{\beta}(x)
. But also
\widetilde{\beta}(x_{i})\to Q
and since
\Sub(\mathcal{M})
is Hausdorff for the Effros-Maréchal topology, limits are
unique. Therefore
Q=\widetilde{\beta}(x)
. So the image of
\widetilde{\beta}
is closed.∎

With all the pieces in place, we now prove the universality theorem for exotic URAs. We use the strategy of Theorem
3.5
to encode an arbitrary minimal Polish space
X
as a closed topological subset of
\Sub(L(\Lambda))
, transport this embedding through Lemma
4.5
to
\Sub(L(\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})}))
, and finally use a minimal action of
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
on
X
(guaranteed by Proposition
2.9
) to make the image a URA.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countably infinite abelian group. For every minimal Polish space
X
, there exists an exotic
URA

\mathcal{U}_{X}\subset\Sub({\rm L}(\Lambda\wr\mathbb{F}_{\infty}))

such that
\mathcal{U}_{X}
is homeomorphic to
X
.

Proof.

Write
\Gamma:=\Lambda\wr\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
and let
X
be a minimal Polish space. By Proposition
2.9
, there exists
a countable group
\Gamma_{X}\leq\Homeo(X)
acting minimally on
X
. Choose a surjective homomorphism
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{\infty}\to\Gamma_{X}
. Thus
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
acts minimally on
X
by
g\cdot x:=\pi(g)x
.

Since
\Lambda
is infinite, the finite von Neumann algebra
{\rm L}(\Lambda)
is
diffuse hence, there exist a projection
e_{0}\in{\rm L}(\Lambda)
with
0<\tau(e_{0})<\frac{1}{2}
. Fix a
r\in(\tau(e_{0}),1/2)
. Combining Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
we get a closed topological embedding
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\hookrightarrow\Sub({\rm L}(\Lambda))
. By Kechris
[
12
, Theorem 4.17]
there exists a closed topological embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and by compositing with
\theta
we get a closed topological embedding
\beta:X\hookrightarrow\Sub({\rm L}(\Lambda))
,
x\mapsto B_{x}:=W^{*}(p_{x})=\mathbb{C}p_{x}\oplus\mathbb{C}(1-p_{x})
where
p_{x}\in\mathcal{P}(e_{0},r)
for all
x\in X
.

We use the standard identification:

\left({\rm L}(\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})}),\tau_{\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})}}\right)\simeq\bigotimes_{g\in\mathbb{F}_{\infty}}\left({\rm L}(\Lambda),\tau_{\Lambda}\right)

Under this identification, the trace-preserving action
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}\curvearrowright{\rm L}(\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})})
becomes for
a=\bigotimes_{g\in\mathbb{F}_{\infty}}a_{g}
an elementary tensor, with
a_{g}\in{\rm L}(\Lambda)
such that
a_{g}=1
for all but finitely many
g\in\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
and for
h\in\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
,
h\cdot a:=\bigotimes_{g\in\mathbb{F}_{\infty}}a_{h^{-1}g}
.
For
x\in X
define
M_{x}:=\bigotimes_{g\in\mathbb{F}_{\infty}}\left(B_{g^{-1}\cdot x},\tau_{\Lambda}\right)\subset{\rm L}(\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})})\subset{\rm L}(\Gamma)
. It follows from Lemma
4.5
that the map
\iota:X\to{\rm Sub}({\rm L}(\Gamma))
,
x\mapsto M_{x}
, is a closed topological embedding and, using the identification above, we see that
\iota
is
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
-equivariant where the
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
-action on
{\rm Sub}({\rm L}(\Gamma))
is given by restriction of the
\Gamma
action to the subgroup
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}\leq\Gamma
. Then, the set
\mathcal{U}_{X}:=\iota(X)=\{M_{x}:x\in X\}
is a minimal closed
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
-invariant subset of
{\rm L}(\Gamma)
(since the action
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}\curvearrowright X
is minimal). Since
\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})}
is abelian and
\mathcal{U}_{X}\subset\Sub({\rm L}(\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})}))
, the set
\mathcal{U}_{X}
is actually
\Gamma
-invariant. Hence,
\mathcal{U}_{X}\subset\Sub({\rm L}(\Gamma)
is a URA, and it is homeomorphic to
X
. Let us show that
\mathcal{U}_{X}
is exotic. Fix
x\in X
and suppose, by
contradiction, that
M_{x}={\rm L}(H)
for some subgroup
H\leq\Gamma
. Since
M_{x}\subset{\rm L}\bigl(\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})}\bigr)
we must have
H\leq\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})}
.
Let
\Lambda_{e}\leq\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})}
be the subgroup of
\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})}
consisting of maps
a:\mathbb{F}_{\infty}\to\Lambda
satisfying
a(g)=e
for all
g\neq e
. One has
E_{{\rm L}(\Lambda_{e})}({\rm L}(H))={\rm L}(H\cap\Lambda_{e})
and, using the notations of the proof of Lemma
4.5
,
E_{{\rm L}(\Lambda_{e})}(M_{x})=B_{x}^{(e)}
. Since
M_{x}={\rm L}(H)
we deduce that
B_{x}^{(e)}={\rm L}(H\cap\Lambda_{e})={\rm L}(H_{e})^{(e)}
, where
H_{e}:=\{\gamma\in\Lambda:a_{\gamma}\in H\}\leq\Lambda
, where
a_{\gamma}(e)=\gamma
and
\gamma(g)=e
for all
g\neq e
. By injectivity of the map
{\rm L}(\Lambda)\to{\rm L}(\Lambda^{(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})})
,
x\mapsto x^{(e)}
, we deduce that
B_{x}={\rm L}(H_{e})
. This is a contradiction since for all
x\in X
and all
K\in\Sub(\Lambda)
one has
B_{x}\neq{\rm L}(K)
. Indeed,
B_{x}=\mathbb{C}p_{x}\oplus\mathbb{C}(1-p_{x})
is two-dimensional, and its two non-zero minimal projections have traces
r
and
1-r
and
r<\frac{1}{2}
. However, if
{\rm L}(K)
is two-dimensional, then
K\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, and the
two non-zero minimal projections of
{\rm L}(K)
both have trace
1/2
.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
B
. The contrast with the URS world is sharp: while compactness of
\Sub(\Gamma)
forces every URS to be a compact metric space, the failure of compactness in
\Sub({\rm L}(\Gamma))
— precisely characterized by Theorem
3.5
— allows URAs to inherit every minimal Polish topology.

5.
Confined subalgebras, simplicity and crossed products

Having developed URAs in
\Sub(M)
in Section
4
for an arbitrary trace-preserving action, we now specialize the setup to crossed products, where the C*-simplicity question naturally lives. Given a trace-preserving action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau_{M})
on a finite von Neumann algebra, the natural ambient algebra is the crossed product
M\rtimes\Gamma
, and the appropriate notion replacing confinement of subgroups is relative
M
-confinement of intermediate subalgebras. We first fix the setup.

Let
(M,\tau_{M})
be a finite von Neumann algebra with separable predual and faithful normal trace, and let
\alpha:\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau_{M})
be a trace-preserving action of a countable discrete group. We view
M\subset\mathbb{B}({\rm L}^{2}(M))
in standard form, with cyclic vector
\xi_{\tau}
, and write
u_{\alpha}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}({\rm L}^{2}(M))
for the Koopman representation determined by
u_{\alpha}(g)(x\xi_{\tau})=\alpha_{g}(x)\xi_{\tau}
. The crossed product
M\rtimes\Gamma
is the von Neumann algebra acting on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma,{\rm L}^{2}(M))
generated by
\pi(M)\cup\{u_{g}:g\in\Gamma\}
, where
\pi
is the diagonal representation
[\pi(x)\xi](h)=x\xi(h)
and
[u_{g}\xi](h):=u_{\alpha}(g)\xi(g^{-1}h)
. We will use the standard properties:

•

u_{g}\pi(x)u_{g}^{*}=\pi(\alpha_{g}(x))
for all
g\in\Gamma
and
x\in M
;

•

there is a faithful normal
M
-bimodular conditional expectation
E_{M}:M\rtimes\Gamma\rightarrow M
with
E_{M}(\pi(x)u_{g}^{*})=\delta_{g,e}x
.

Then
M\rtimes\Gamma
is a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal trace
\tau:=\tau_{M}\circ E_{M}
, and the triple
(\ell^{2}(\Gamma,{\rm L}^{2}(M)),{\rm id},\Omega)
with
\Omega(h):=\delta_{h,e}\xi_{\tau}
is an explicit GNS construction for
\tau
. We will routinely identify
M
with
\pi(M)\subset M\rtimes\Gamma
.

Remark 5.1
.

For any subgroup
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
, the conditional expectation onto
M\rtimes\Lambda\subset M\rtimes\Gamma
satisfies
E_{M\rtimes\Lambda}(u_{s})=\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(s)\,u_{s}
for all
s\in\Gamma
.

To test whether a net of intermediate subalgebras of
M\rtimes\Gamma
containing
M
converges to
M
in the Effros-Maréchal topology, it is enough to test on the canonical unitaries
u_{g}
. This reduction is the content of the next lemma, which will be applied repeatedly throughout the section.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau_{M})
be a trace-preserving action on a finite
von Neumann algebra and let
(N_{i})_{i}
be a net of von Neumann subalgebras of
M\rtimes\Gamma
containing
M
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\|E_{N_{i}}(u_{g})\|_{2}\longrightarrow 0

\forall g\in\Gamma\setminus\{e\}
.

(2)

N_{i}\rightarrow M
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. It suffices to show that
\|E_{N_{i}}(x)-E_{M}(x)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
for all
x\in M\rtimes\Gamma
. Let
X:=\{x\in M\rtimes\Gamma:\|E_{N_{i}}(x)-E_{M}(x)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0\}
. Then
X
is clearly a linear subspace of
M\rtimes\Gamma
and it contains the set
\{au_{g}:g\in\Gamma,a\in M\}
since:

\|E_{N_{i}}(au_{g})-E_{M}(au_{g})\|_{2}=\|a(E_{N_{i}}(u_{g})-E_{M}(u_{g})\|_{2}\leq\|a\|\,\|E_{N_{i}}(u_{g})-\delta_{g,e}1\|_{2}\rightarrow 0.

Hence, it suffices to show that
X
is
\|\cdot\|_{2}
-closed. Let
(x_{n})_{n}
be a sequence in
X
and
x\in M\rtimes\Gamma
be such that
\|x_{n}-x\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\|x_{n_{0}}-x\|_{2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
. Since
x_{n_{0}}\in X
, there exists
i_{0}
such that, for all
i\geq i_{0}

\|E_{N_{i}}(x_{n_{0}})-E_{M}(x_{n_{0}})\|_{2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Then, since
E_{N}
is contractive in
\|\cdot\|_{2}
-norm for any subalgebra
N
one has, for all
i\geq i_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|E_{N_{i}}(x)-E_{M}(x)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|E_{N_{i}}(x-x_{n_{0}})\|_{2}+\|E_{N_{i}}(x_{n_{0}})-E_{M}(x_{n_{0}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\|E_{M}(x_{n_{0}}-x)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|x_{n_{0}}-x\|_{2}+\|E_{N_{i}}(x_{n_{0}})-E_{M}(x_{n_{0}})\|_{2}<\varepsilon.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. This is obvious since
E_{M}(u_{g})=0

\forall g\in\Gamma\setminus\{e\}
.
∎

The next lemma transports continuity from the Chabauty topology on
\Sub(\Gamma)
to the Effros-Maréchal topology on
\Sub(M\rtimes\Gamma)
. It is what makes the comparison between confined subgroups and confined subalgebras work seamlessly.

Lemma 5.3
.

The map
\Sub(\Gamma)\to\Sub(M\rtimes\Gamma)
,
\Lambda\mapsto M\rtimes\Lambda
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda_{n}\rightarrow\Lambda
is
\Sub(\Gamma)
. It suffices to show that
\|E_{M\rtimes\Lambda_{n}}(x)-E_{M\rtimes\Lambda}(x)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
for all
x\in M\rtimes\Gamma
. Let
X:=\{x\in M\rtimes\Gamma:\|E_{M\rtimes\Lambda_{n}}(x)-E_{M\rtimes\Lambda}(x)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0\}
. Then
X
is a linear subspace of
M\rtimes\Gamma
and it contains the set
\{au_{g}:g\in\Gamma,a\in M\}
since:

\displaystyle\|E_{M\rtimes\Lambda_{n}}(au_{g})-E_{M\rtimes\Lambda}(au_{g})\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|a(E_{M\rtimes\Lambda_{n}}(u_{g})-E_{M\rtimes\Lambda}(u_{g}))\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|a\|\,\|E_{M\rtimes\Lambda_{n}}(u_{g})-E_{M\rtimes\Lambda}(u_{g})\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|a\|\,\|1_{\Lambda_{n}}(g)u_{g}-1_{\Lambda}(g)u_{g}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|a\|\,|1_{\Lambda_{n}}(g)-1_{\Lambda}(g)|\to 0.

Hence, it suffices to show that
X
is
\|\cdot\|_{2}
-closed and it can be proved as in Lemma
5.2
.∎

We consider the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright M\rtimes\Gamma
given by
g\cdot x:=u_{g}xu_{g}^{*}
, for
x\in M\rtimes\Gamma
and
g\in\Gamma
and the associated action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Sub(M\rtimes\Gamma)
given by
g\cdot N:=u_{g}Nu_{g}^{*}
for
N\in\Sub(M\rtimes\Gamma)
and
g\in\Gamma
. We are now in a position to introduce the central notion of this section.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau_{M})
be a trace-preserving action on a finite
von Neumann algebra. A subalgebra
N\subset M\rtimes\Gamma
containing
M
is
M
-confined
if

M\notin\overline{\{u_{g}Nu_{g}^{*}:g\in\Gamma\}}

where the closure is taken in the Effros-Maréchal topology on
\Sub(M\rtimes\Gamma)
.

This definition above is motivated by
[
3
, Definition 1.1]
which was itself motivated by the notion of confined subgroup, recalled below.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group. A subgroup
\Lambda
is called
confined
if the trivial subgroup is not in the closure of
\{g\Lambda g^{-1}:g\in\Lambda\}
in the Chabauty topology on the space of subgroups.

These two notions are related as follows.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau_{M})
be a trace-preserving action on a finite
von Neumann algebra and
\Lambda
a subgroup of
\Gamma
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\Lambda
is a confined subgroup of
\Gamma

(2)

The subalgebra
M\rtimes\Lambda\subset M\rtimes\Gamma
is
M
-confined.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Assume
M\rtimes\Lambda\subset M\rtimes\Gamma
is not
M
-confined. Then there exists
a net
(g_{i})_{i}
in
\Gamma
such that
u_{g_{i}}M\rtimes\Lambda u_{g_{i}}^{*}=M\rtimes g_{i}\Lambda g_{i}^{-1}\longrightarrow M

in the Effros-Maréchal topology on
\Sub(M\rtimes\Gamma)
. Hence, for all
s\in\Gamma
,

1_{g_{i}\Lambda g_{i}^{-1}}(s)=\|1_{g_{i}\Lambda g_{i}^{-1}}(s)u_{s}\|_{2}=\|E_{M\rtimes g_{i}\Lambda g_{i}^{-1}}(u_{s}))\|_{2}\longrightarrow\|E_{M}(u_{s})\|_{2}=\delta_{s,e}=1_{\{e\}}(s).

This shows that
g_{i}\Lambda g_{i}^{-1}\longrightarrow\{e\}
hence
\Lambda
is not confined.

(2)\Rightarrow(1).
Assume that
\Lambda
is not confined. Then there exists a net
(g_{i})_{i}
in
\Gamma
such that
g_{i}\Lambda g_{i}^{-1}\longrightarrow\{e\}
. Then, Lemma
5.3
implies that:

u_{g_{i}}M\rtimes\Lambda u_{g_{i}}^{*}=M\rtimes g_{i}\Lambda g_{i}^{-1}\rightarrow M.

Hence,
M\rtimes\Lambda
is not
M
-confined.
∎

To compare confined subalgebras with hypertraces and the Furstenberg boundary, we need a Hilbert-space framework that simultaneously carries the
\Gamma
-action on
C(X)
and the covariant representation defining the crossed product. A topological space
X
is called a
\Gamma
-space
if
X
is equipped with an action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
by homeomorphisms. Fix a
\Gamma
-space
X
and a Hilbert space
H
. For each
x\in X
, define the map:

\varphi_{x}^{H}:C(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma,H))\quad[\varphi_{x}^{H}(f)\xi](g)=f(gx)\xi(g).

Then, for all

6.
The state-space construction of URAs

We now turn to the central construction of the paper. As discussed in the introduction, the URS theory of

carries out a Glasner–Weiss-style dynamics inside the pointwise-compact space

of positive definite functions, and recovers URS as
\Gamma

is the positive definite function

To bypass this obstruction, we enlarge the ambient space to states on

extending the canonical trace, which form a compact convex
\Gamma
-space
K
. Zorn’s lemma then produces minimal closed
\Gamma
-invariant subsets

; the centralizer of any

relative to a chosen
\Gamma
-invariant test algebra

is a von Neumann subalgebra of the algebra of interest. The catch is that the centralizer map
\Phi
is only upper semi-continuous; the construction of URAs from it relies on a Baire-category argument supplying a dense

of continuity points. The general construction is the content of Theorem
6.4
.

\Gamma

(z_{i}-z)(z_{i}-z)^{*}\in\mathcal{M}
, we get that

\varphi_{i}((z_{i}-z)(z_{i}-z)^{*})=\tau((z_{i}-z)(z_{i}-z)^{*})=\|(z_{i}-z)^{*}\|_{2}^{2}.

Because
z_{i}\to z
in the strong-* topology, we have
\|(z_{i}-z)^{*}\|_{2}\to 0
. Thus,
|\varphi_{i}((z_{i}-z)y)|\to 0
. This implies that
\varphi_{i}(z_{i}y)\to\varphi(zy)
. By an identical, symmetric argument,
\varphi_{i}(yz_{i})\to\varphi(yz)
. Since
\varphi_{i}(z_{i}y)=\varphi_{i}(yz_{i})
for all
i
, taking the limit yields
\varphi(zy)=\varphi(yz)
. Therefore,
z
commutes with
A
under
\varphi
, concluding that
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
and proving that
\Phi
is upper semicontinuous.∎

Upper semi-continuity alone is too weak to generate URAs out of the centralizer map: minimal subsets of
\mathcal{Z}
might fail to map onto minimal subsets of
\mathrm{Sub}(\mathcal{M})
. The Baire-category theorem rescues the construction, by guaranteeing a dense
G_{\delta}
of strict continuity points on which
\Phi
does produce a minimal orbit closure.

Theorem 6.4

(Construction of General URAs)
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
and
\Phi
be defined as above. The map
\Phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathrm{Sub}(\mathcal{M})
possesses a dense
G_{\delta}
set of points of continuity in the Effros-Maréchal topology. For any such point of continuity
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the Effros-Maréchal orbit closure

\mathcal{H}=\overline{\left\{\alpha_{g}(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi_{0}})\;\middle|\;g\in\Gamma\right\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}

is a minimal, closed,
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
\mathrm{Sub}(\mathcal{M})
, and thus a Uniformly Recurrent Subalgebra.

Proof.

First, we establish the existence of points of continuity. Since
\mathrm{Sub}(\mathcal{M})
equipped with the Effros-Maréchal topology is a Polish space, it is a metrizable Baire space. The space
\mathcal{Z}\subset K
is a compact Hausdorff space and also a Baire space. Because the map
\Phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathrm{Sub}(\mathcal{M})
is upper semi-continuous (as established in Lemma
6.3
), a classical application of the Baire Category Theorem guarantees that the set
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
of points of continuity of
\Phi
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
\mathcal{Z}
(see, e.g., Kuratowski
[
14
, Section 39.IV, Theorem 1]
). Let
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}
be one such point of continuity. Let

\mathcal{H}=\overline{\left\{\alpha_{g}(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi_{0}})\;\middle|\;g\in\Gamma\right\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}=\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}.

We now claim that
\mathcal{H}
is minimal. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a non-empty closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset. We must show
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{H}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is closed and invariant, it suffices to show
\Phi(\varphi_{0})\in\mathcal{A}
. Pick any
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{A}
. Because
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a net
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\Gamma
such that
g_{i}\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})=\Phi(g_{i}\varphi_{0})\to\mathcal{N}
in the EM-topology. Because
\mathcal{Z}
is a compact space, we may pass to a subnet to assume that
g_{i}\varphi_{0}\to\psi\in\mathcal{Z}
in the weak-* topology.Since
\mathcal{Z}
is a minimal
\Gamma
-space, the orbit
\Gamma\cdot\psi
is dense in
\mathcal{Z}
. Thus, there exists a net
(h_{j})_{j\in J}
in
\Gamma
such that
h_{j}\psi\to\varphi_{0}
. Since the EM-topology on
\mathrm{Sub}(\mathcal{M})
is Polish, it is a regular topological space. Let
W
be an arbitrary open neighborhood of
\Phi(\varphi_{0})
in
\mathrm{Sub}(\mathcal{M})
. By regularity, there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
\Phi(\varphi_{0})
such that its closure satisfies
\overline{U}\subset W
. Since
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}
is a point of continuity for
\Phi
,
V=\Phi^{-1}(U)
is an open neighborhood of
\varphi_{0}
in
\mathcal{Z}
. Since
h_{j}\psi\to\varphi_{0}
, there exists an index
j\in J
such that
h_{j}\psi\in V
. Now, since
g_{i}\varphi_{0}\to\psi
, we have
h_{j}g_{i}\varphi_{0}\to h_{j}\psi
. Since
h_{j}\psi\in V
and
V
is open, there exists an index
i_{0}\in I
such that for all
i\geq i_{0}
, we have
h_{j}g_{i}\varphi_{0}\in V
. Therefore, for all
i\geq i_{0}
,
\Phi(h_{j}g_{i}\varphi_{0})\in U
. This means the net
\left(h_{j}\cdot\Phi(g_{i}\varphi_{0})\right)_{i\geq i_{0}}
lies entirely inside the open set
U
. Since the action of
\Gamma
on the EM-topology is continuous, taking the limit over
i
yields

h_{j}\cdot\mathcal{N}=\lim_{i\to\infty}h_{j}\cdot\Phi(g_{i}\varphi_{0})\in\overline{U}.

Since
\overline{U}\subset W
, we obtain
h_{j}\cdot\mathcal{N}\in W
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is
\Gamma
-invariant,
h_{j}\cdot\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{A}
. Because
W
was an arbitrary open neighborhood of
\Phi(\varphi_{0})
, we have shown that
\Phi(\varphi_{0})
lies in the EM-closure of
\mathcal{A}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is closed,
\Phi(\varphi_{0})\in\mathcal{A}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{H}
, confirming that
\mathcal{H}
is minimal.∎

The crossed-product setting fits naturally into this framework, with the test algebra
A
being the reduced crossed product
B\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
for a
\Gamma
-C*-algebra
B
.

Example 6.5

(Crossed Product)
.

Let
B
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra equipped with an action of a countable discrete group
\Gamma
by automorphisms. We view the reduced crossed product
A=B\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
as faithfully represented on some
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
containing
C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)
canonically. Let
S(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}))
denote the state space of
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
. We define

K=\left\{\varphi\in S(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}))\;\middle|\;\varphi|_{L(\Gamma)}=\tau_{0}\right\}.

For each
\varphi\in\mathcal{Z}
(as defined in Lemma
6.1
), we define its algebraic centralizer relative to
A=B\rtimes_{r}\Gamma
as

N_{\varphi}=\left\{z\in C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)\;\middle|\;\varphi(zy)=\varphi(yz)\text{ for all }y\in A\right\}.

We define the corresponding von Neumann subalgebra
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}=(N_{\varphi})^{\prime\prime}\subseteq L(\Gamma)
. It follows from Lemma
6.3
that the map
\Phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathrm{Sub}(L(\Gamma))
defined by
\Phi(\varphi)=\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant and upper semicontinuous. Moreover, by Theorem
6.4
, the map
\Phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathrm{Sub}(L(\Gamma))
possesses a dense
G_{\delta}
set of points of continuity in the Effros-Maréchal topology. For any such point of continuity
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the Effros-Maréchal orbit closure

\mathcal{H}=\overline{\left\{\lambda_{g}\mathcal{M}_{\varphi_{0}}\lambda_{g}^{*}\;\middle|\;g\in\Gamma\right\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}

is a minimal, closed,
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
\mathrm{Sub}(L(\Gamma))
, and thus a Uniformly Recurrent Subalgebra.

6.2.
Compact URAs

We now show that, in the minimal version of the construction of Theorem
6.4
, the compact URAs are exactly the URAs produced. The argument has two parts: a Jones-basic-construction lemma identifying when a hypertrace-style state is centralized by elements of a subalgebra, and a Stone–Čech compactification argument that turns the discrete coset space
\Gamma/\Sigma
of stabilizers into a continuous parametrization of the URA.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra, represented on

L^{2}(M,\tau)
, and let
N\in\operatorname{Sub}(M)
. Denote by
e_{N}:L^{2}(M,\tau)\to L^{2}(N,\tau)
the Jones projection, so that
e_{N}(a\xi_{\tau})=E_{N}(a)\xi_{\tau}
for all
a\in N
. Let
\omega\in\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(M,\tau))_{*}
,

\omega(T):=\langle T\xi_{\tau},\xi_{\tau}\rangle,\qquad T\in\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(M,\tau)).

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
M
and

e_{N}
inside
\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(M,\tau))
. Then, for every
x\in N
and every

y\in\mathcal{A}
,
\omega(xy)=\omega(yx)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{0}
be the
∗
-algebra generated by
M
and

e_{N}
inside
\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(M,\tau))
. By continuity and density, it is enough to show the relation for
y\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
. We first recall the basic Jones relation
e_{N}ae_{N}=E_{N}(a)e_{N}
for all
a\in M
. It follows that
\mathcal{A}_{0}=M+Me_{N}M
. It is therefore enough to verify the desired identity for
y\in M
and for

y=ae_{N}b
, with
a,b\in M
. First, if
y=a\in M
, then

\omega(xa)=\tau(xa)=\tau(ax)=\omega(ax),

by traciality of
\tau
. Now let
y=ae_{N}b
, with
a,b\in M
. Then, since
x\in N
,

\displaystyle\omega(xae_{N}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle xaE_{N}(b)\xi_{\tau},\xi_{\tau}\rangle=\tau(xaE_{N}(b))=\tau(aE_{N}(b)x)=\tau(aE_{N}(bx))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle aE_{N}(bx)\xi_{\tau},\xi_{\tau}\rangle=\omega(ae_{N}bx)

By linearity, the equality
\omega(xy)=\omega(yx)
holds for every
y\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.∎

The next theorem says precisely that the minimal version of the construction captures every compact URA. The construction is explicit: given a compact URA
\mathcal{U}
, we represent
M
diagonally on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma/\Sigma,L^{2}(M))
where
\Sigma
is the stabilizer of a basepoint
N_{0}\in\mathcal{U}
, and use a coset-indexed family of Jones projections to encode the URA itself.

Theorem 6.7

(State-space realization of compact URAs)
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra with separable predual with a trace preserving
\alpha:\Gamma\curvearrowright(M,\tau)
of a countable discrete group. Let
\mathcal{U}\subset\operatorname{Sub}(M)
be a compact URA. Then there exists a unitary representation
u:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(H)
and a faithful
\Gamma
-equivariant representation
M\subset\mathbb{B}(H)
(where the action on
\mathbb{B}(H)
is by
{\rm Ad}(u)
), a
\Gamma
-invariant unital C*-subalgebra
A
containing
\mathcal{D}:=M
, a compact minimal
\Gamma
-invariant subset
\mathcal{Z}\subset K=\left\{\varphi\in S(\mathbb{B}(H)):\varphi|_{M}=\tau\right\}
such that the map
\Phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\Sub(M)
,

\Phi(\varphi):=\left\{z\in\mathcal{D}:\varphi(zy)=\varphi(yz)\ \forall y\in A\right\}^{\prime\prime}

is continuous on
\mathcal{Z}
and for any
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}
one has
\overline{\Gamma\cdot\mathcal{M}_{\varphi_{0}}}^{\,EM}=\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

Fix
N_{0}\in\mathcal{U}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is minimal,
\mathcal{U}=\overline{\Gamma\cdot N_{0}}^{\,EM}
. Let
\Sigma:=\operatorname{Stab}(N_{0})\leq\Gamma
and

H_{0}:=L^{2}(M,\tau),\qquad H:=\ell^{2}(\Gamma/\Sigma,H_{0}).

View
\mathcal{D}:=M\subset\mathbb{B}(H)
after identification via the map
x\mapsto 1\otimes x
. For
N\in\operatorname{Sub}(M)
, let
e_{N}:L^{2}(M,\tau)\to L^{2}(N,\tau)
be the Jones projection, equivalently

e_{N}(x\xi_{\tau})=E_{N}(x)\xi_{\tau},\qquad x\in M.

Define
T\in\mathbb{B}(H)
by
(T\xi)(h\Sigma):=e_{\alpha_{h}(N_{0})}\xi(h\Sigma)
, for all
\xi\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma/\Sigma,H_{0})
and
h\in\Gamma
. Note that
T
is well defined since
g\Sigma=h\Sigma\Rightarrow\alpha_{g}(N_{0})=\alpha_{h}(N_{0})
and
T=T^{*}
. Let
A:=C^{*}(M,T)\subset B(H)
.
Consider the Koopman representation
u_{\alpha}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(H_{0})
defined by
u_{\alpha}(g)(x\xi_{\tau})=\alpha_{g}(x)\xi_{\tau}
and the unitary representation
u:\Gamma\to\mathcal{U}(H)
,
(u(g)\xi)(h\Sigma)=u_{\alpha}(g)\xi(g^{-1}h\Sigma)
. Then,
u(g)(1\otimes x)u(g)^{*}=1\otimes\alpha_{g}(x)
and,

[u(g)Tu(g)^{*}\xi](h\Sigma)=u_{\alpha}(g)e_{\alpha_{g^{-1}h}(N_{0})}u_{\alpha}(g)^{*}\xi(h\Sigma).

Since
\alpha_{g}\circ E_{\alpha_{g^{-1}h}(N_{0})}\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}}=E_{\alpha_{h}(N_{0})}
we have
u_{\alpha}(g)e_{\alpha_{g^{-1}h}(N_{0})}u_{\alpha}(g)^{*}=e_{\alpha_{h}(N_{0})}
which implies that
u(g)Tu(g)^{*}=T
. Thus
A
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

For each
g\Sigma\in\Gamma/\Sigma
, let
\eta_{g\Sigma}:=\delta_{g\Sigma}\otimes\xi_{\tau}\in H
. Define
\omega_{g\Sigma}\in\mathbb{B}(H)_{*}
by

\omega_{g\Sigma}(S):=\langle S\eta_{g\Sigma},\eta_{g\Sigma}\rangle\quad\text{for all }S\in B(H).

Then
\omega_{g\Sigma}|_{M}=\tau
for all
g\in\Gamma
. Put

Y:=\overline{\{\omega_{g\Sigma}:g\Sigma\in\Gamma/\Sigma\}}^{\,w^{*}}\subset K=\{\varphi\in S(B(H)):\varphi|_{M}=\tau\}

and note that
Y
is compact Hausdorff with the weak* topology and it is
\Gamma
-invariant.

Claim.
There exists a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant map
q:Y\to\mathcal{U}
such that

q(\omega_{g\Sigma})=\alpha_{g}(N_{0})\quad\text{for all }g\in\Gamma.

Moreover, for all
\varphi\in Y
, one has
\varphi(aTb)=\tau(aE_{q(\varphi)}(b))

\forall a,b\in M
.

Proof of the Claim.
Let
\beta\left(\Gamma/\Sigma\right)
be the Stone–Čech compactification of the discrete space
\Gamma/\Sigma
. Since

\mathcal{U}
is compact Hausdorff, the well-defined
\Gamma
-equivariant map

\Gamma/\Sigma\to\mathcal{U},\qquad g\Sigma\mapsto\alpha_{g}(N_{0}),

extends uniquely to a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant map
\widetilde{q}:\beta\left(\Gamma/\Sigma\right)\to\mathcal{U}
.
Similarly, the
\Gamma
-equivariant map
\Gamma/\Sigma\to Y
,
g\Sigma\mapsto\omega_{g\Sigma}
extends to a continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant map
\rho:\beta\left(\Gamma/\Sigma\right)\to Y
. Note that
\rho
is surjective. We claim that
\widetilde{q}
factors through
\rho
. Let
p,p^{\prime}\in\beta\left(\Gamma/\Sigma\right)
and
assume that
\rho(p)=\rho(p^{\prime})
. Put
N:=\widetilde{q}(p)
and
P:=\widetilde{q}(p^{\prime})
. For
a,b\in M
and
g\in\Gamma
, we have
\omega_{g\Sigma}(aTb)=\tau(aE_{\alpha_{g}(N_{0})}(b))
. By definition,
\rho(p)=\lim_{g\Sigma\rightarrow p}\omega_{g\Sigma}
, the limit along the ultrafilter
p
being taken in the weak* topology. Also,
\widetilde{q}(p)=\lim_{g\Sigma\rightarrow p}\alpha_{g}(N_{0})
the limit along the ultrafilter
p
being taken in the Effros-Maréchal topology. By definition of the Effros-Maréchal topology,
E_{\alpha_{g}(N_{0})}(b)\xi_{\tau}\rightarrow_{g\Sigma\to p}E_{N}(b)\xi_{\tau}
in
{\rm L}^{2}(M,\tau)
. Hence,
\rho(p)(aTb)=\tau(aE_{N}(b))
.
Similarly,
\rho(p^{\prime})(aTb)=\tau(aE_{P}(b))
. Since
\rho(p)=\rho(p^{\prime})
, we get
\tau(aE_{N}(b))=\tau(aE_{P}(b))

\forall a,b\in M
.
By faithfulness of
\tau
,
E_{N}=E_{P}
. Therefore
N=P
. Thus
\widetilde{q}
factors through
\rho
, giving a
well-defined continuous
\Gamma
-equivariant map
q:Y\to\mathcal{U}
such that

q(\omega_{g\Sigma})=\alpha_{g}(N_{0})\qquad(g\in\Gamma).

The last part of the Claim has already been proved (using that
\rho
is surjective). ∎

End of the Proof of Theorem
6.7
.
Let
\mathcal{Z}\subset Y
be a minimal closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset. Since
q
is continuous and
equivariant,
q(\mathcal{Z})
is a non-empty closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
\mathcal{U}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is minimal, we have
q(\mathcal{Z})=\mathcal{U}
. For

, recall that

N_{\varphi}:=\left\{x\in M:\varphi(xy)=\varphi(yx)\ \forall y\in A\right\}.

We claim that
N_{\varphi}=q(\varphi)
. First let
x\in N_{\varphi}
. Testing the centralizer identity against elements of

y=aTb\in A
, for

. By the Claim and traciality of

\tau(aE_{q(\varphi)}(b)x)=\tau(xaE_{q(\varphi)}(b))=\tau(aE_{q(\varphi)}(bx)).

Since
\tau
is faithful,
E_{q(\varphi)}(b)x=E_{q(\varphi)}(bx)
for all
b\in M
. Taking

, we get

. Therefore
N_{\varphi}\subset q(\varphi)
.

Conversely, let
x\in q(\varphi)
and let
\mathcal{A}_{0}
be the
∗
-algebra generated by
M
and

T
. It is enough to show that
\varphi(xy)=\varphi(yx)

\forall y\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
, because
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is norm dense in
A
. By construction of
q
, there is an ultrafilter
p\in\beta\left(\Gamma/\Sigma\right)
such that

in Effros-Maréchal topology and
\omega_{g\Sigma}\rightarrow\varphi
in weak* topology. Fix

, then

. For

, write
y_{N}

6.3.
Discrete URAs

The minimal-state-space framework of Theorem
6.4
captures every compact URA, as we just saw. We turn now to discrete URAs, which behave very differently: the basic obstruction of Lemma
4.4
immediately implies that, when stabilizers have infinite index in
\Gamma
, the minimal construction cannot produce such URAs. We open this subsection with a concrete example exhibiting this phenomenon (Proposition
6.8
), then upgrade the state-space machinery to a broader, non-minimal version that does capture discrete URAs (Theorem
6.9
).

Consider the abelian group
A:=\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\oplus\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/{3\mathbb{Z}}
and write
s,s_{n}

(n\in\mathbb{Z})
the different copies of the generator of
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
so that
A:=\langle s\rangle\oplus\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle s_{n}\rangle
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the unique
\theta_{n}\in{\rm Aut}(A)
such that

\theta_{n}(s)=s_{n},\qquad\theta_{n}(s_{n})=s,\qquad\theta_{n}(s_{k})=s_{k}\quad(k\neq n)

and note that
\theta_{n}^{2}={\rm id}
. We still denote by
\theta_{n}
the associated trace preserving automorphism of
{\rm L}(A)
. Define
M_{n}:={\rm L}(A)^{\theta_{n}}=\{x\in{\rm L}(A):\theta_{n}(x)=x\}
. Consider the group
\Gamma:=A\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
, where the generator
u\in\mathbb{Z}
acts on
A
by

usu^{-1}=s,\qquad us_{n}u^{-1}=s_{n+1}

and view
M_{n}
as a von Neumann subalgebra of
{\rm L}(A)\subset{\rm L}(\Gamma)
. Let

\mathcal{U}:=\{M_{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subset\Sub({\rm L}(\Gamma)).

Proposition 6.8
.

\mathcal{U}
is a discrete URA of
{\rm L}(\Gamma)
of infinite cardinality. Moreover, there is no compact minimal
\Gamma
-space
\mathcal{Z}
, equivariant upper semi-continuous map
\Phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\Sub(L(\Gamma))
, and continuity point
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}
of
\Phi
, such that
\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}=\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\mathcal{U}
is
\Gamma
-invariant. Since
A
is
abelian, every
\lambda_{a}
,
a\in A
, commutes with
{\rm L}(A)
. Hence
\lambda_{a}M_{n}\lambda_{a}^{*}=M_{n}

\forall a\in A,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
On the other hand, direct computations on the generators
s,s_{k}
give
u\theta_{n}(u^{-1}\cdot u)u^{-1}=\theta_{n+1}
.
Therefore,
\lambda_{u}M_{n}\lambda_{u}^{*}=M_{n+1}
. It follows that, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\lambda_{u^{k}}M_{n}\lambda_{u^{k}}^{*}=M_{n+k}
. Thus

\Gamma\cdot M_{n}=\{M_{m}:m\in\mathbb{Z}\}=\mathcal{U}.

In particular,
\mathcal{U}
is
\Gamma
-invariant and the action of

\Gamma
on
\mathcal{U}
is transitive. We now prove that
\mathcal{U}
is closed and discrete in the Effros-Maréchal topology.
Since
\theta_{n}
has order
2
, the trace-preserving conditional expectation
onto
M_{n}={\rm L}(A)^{\theta_{n}}
is
E_{M_{n}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x+\theta_{n}(x))

\forall x\in{\rm L}(A)
. In particular,
E_{M_{n}}(\lambda_{s})=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{\theta_{n}(s)})=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{s_{n}})
. Hence, if
n\neq m
, then

E_{M_{n}}(\lambda_{s})-E_{M_{m}}(\lambda_{s})=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{s_{n}}-\lambda_{s_{m}})
. Since the canonical group unitaries form an orthonormal basis of

L^{2}({\rm L}(\Gamma))
, we get

\bigl\|E_{M_{n}}(\lambda_{s})-E_{M_{m}}(\lambda_{s})\bigr\|_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

This easily implies that
\mathcal{U}
is
closed and discrete in
\Sub({\rm L}(\Gamma))
. Moreover,
\mathcal{U}
is clearly minimal since the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathcal{U}
is transitive.

We turn to the second assertion. Suppose, towards a contradiction, that there exist a compact minimal
\Gamma
-space
\mathcal{Z}
, a
\Gamma
-equivariant map
\Phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\Sub({\rm L}(\Gamma))
, and a continuity point
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}
of
\Phi
such that

\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}=\mathcal{U}.

Since
\mathcal{U}
is discrete, Lemma
4.4
forces
\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(\Phi(\varphi_{0}))
to have finite index in
\Gamma
. On the other hand
\Phi(\varphi_{0})\in\mathcal{U}
, hence
\Phi(\varphi_{0})=M_{n}
for some

, and a direct computation gives
\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(M_{n})=A
, which has infinite index in
\Gamma=A\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
. This contradiction proves the claim.
∎

Any discrete URA with infinite-index stabilizers is therefore invisible to the minimal state-space construction of Theorem
6.4
. Nonetheless, such URAs can still be captured by the state-space machinery if we drop the minimality requirement on the ambient state space and instead rely on isolated points of an orbit closure as continuity points of the centralizer map. The next theorem implements this strategy, following the same overall template as Theorem
6.7
with details adapted to the discrete setting.

Remark 6.10
.

The contrast between Theorem
6.7
and Theorem
6.9
is itself a manifestation of the compactness obstruction quantified by Theorem

. For the compact URAs of Theorem

is itself a compact
\Gamma

\Gamma

We first make the following definition.

Definition 6.16
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable i.c.c. group and let

be a

Proposition 6.17
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable infinite i.c.c. group. Assume that
\Gamma
contains some non-trivial element
s
with order
m\geq 3
. Set
M=L(\Gamma)
and
N=pMp\oplus(1-p)M(1-p)

\mathcal{U}:=\overline{\{gNg^{-1}:g\in\Gamma\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}
is a discrete corner URA.

Proof.

Let
H=\langle s\rangle
. For any
r\in\Gamma
, we define the projection
\sigma_{r}(p)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{t\in rHr^{-1}}v_{t}
and the corresponding self-adjoint unitary
u_{r}=2\sigma_{r}(p)-1
.

We first verify that
E_{rNr^{-1}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x+u_{r}xu_{r})
for all

rNr^{-1}
is given by

\displaystyle E_{rNr^{-1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{r}(p)x\sigma_{r}(p)+(1-\sigma_{r}(p))x(1-\sigma_{r}(p))

\displaystyle=\sigma_{r}(p)x\sigma_{r}(p)+x-x\sigma_{r}(p)-\sigma_{r}(p)x+\sigma_{r}(p)x\sigma_{r}(p)

\displaystyle=x-\sigma_{r}(p)x-x\sigma_{r}(p)+2\sigma_{r}(p)x\sigma_{r}(p).

On the other hand, substituting
u_{r}=2\sigma_{r}(p)-1

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(x+u_{r}xu_{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(x+(2\sigma_{r}(p)-1)x(2\sigma_{r}(p)-1)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(x+4\sigma_{r}(p)x\sigma_{r}(p)-2\sigma_{r}(p)x-2x\sigma_{r}(p)+x\Big)

\displaystyle=x-\sigma_{r}(p)x-x\sigma_{r}(p)+2\sigma_{r}(p)x\sigma_{r}(p).

Thus, it follows that

(4)

E_{rNr^{-1}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x+u_{r}xu_{r}).

Now, let
Q\in\overline{\Gamma\cdot N}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}

\Gamma
is countable, the EM-topology on the space of subalgebras of

is metrizable. Thus, there exists a sequence
(r_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\Gamma
such that
E_{r_{i}Nr_{i}^{-1}}(x)\to E_{Q}(x)
in
\|\cdot\|_{2}
for all

. Using equation (
4

(5)

u_{r_{i}}xu_{r_{i}}\xrightarrow{\|\cdot\|_{2}}2E_{Q}(x)-x

To understand the algebraic structure of the elements in this sequence, let
H_{i}=r_{i}\langle s\rangle r_{i}^{-1}
and define the set of non-identity elements

. For any

, we obtain

\displaystyle m^{2}E_{r_{i}Nr_{i}^{-1}}(g)

\displaystyle=(m^{2}-2m+2)g

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{x,y\in B_{i}}xgy

\displaystyle\quad-(m-2)\sum_{x\in B_{i}}xg

\displaystyle\quad-(m-2)\sum_{x\in B_{i}}gx.

This establishes that for any
g\in\Gamma

lies within the integral group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
.

Enumerate the group elements as
\Gamma=\{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\dots\}
. By definition of EM-convergence, the sequence
E_{r_{i}Nr_{i}^{-1}}(g_{1})
is Cauchy in
\|\cdot\|_{2}
. Because

\|\cdot\|_{2}
-distance between any two distinct elements in the discrete space

is at least
1

4
) and (
5
), we see that
u_{r_{i}}g_{1}u_{r_{i}}
eventually stabilizes.

Applying this iteratively to
g_{2},g_{3},\dots
and using a standard Cantor diagonal argument, we extract a single subsequence—still denoted
(r_{i})
—such that for
every

g\in\Gamma
, the sequence of operators
u_{r_{i}}gu_{r_{i}}
is eventually constant. Let
j
and
k
be any two indices far enough along this diagonal subsequence such that the sequences have stabilized. Then, for every
g\in\Gamma
, we have
u_{r_{j}}gu_{r_{j}}=u_{r_{k}}gu_{r_{k}}
which in turn implies that

(u_{r_{k}}u_{r_{j}})g=g(u_{r_{k}}u_{r_{j}})\quad\text{for all }g\in\Gamma.

This implies that the product
u_{r_{k}}u_{r_{j}}
commutes with every element of
\Gamma
. Thus,

. Therefore,
u_{r_{k}}u_{r_{j}}=\lambda 1
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Since the unitaries are self-adjoint, we deduce that

and thus
\lambda
must be real with

. Therefore, either
u_{r_{j}}=u_{r_{k}}
or
u_{r_{j}}=-u_{r_{k}}

\displaystyle\tau(2\sigma_{r_{j}}(p)-1)=\tau(-2\sigma_{r_{k}}(p)+1)

\displaystyle\implies\frac{2}{m}-1=-\frac{2}{m}+1

\displaystyle\implies\frac{4}{m}=2\implies m=2.

Since we assumed

.

This proves that along our diagonal subsequence, the unitaries

[

6.5.
URS revisited

We now show that every URS in
\Sub(\Gamma)

\Gamma

Fix an arbitrary base point
x_{0}\in X
. We define a representation

injective. We view

as diagonal operators on

induces a canonical, strictly faithful representation of the reduced crossed product

onto

\displaystyle\|E_{N}(a)-a\|_{2}

\displaystyle\|E_{N}(f(x_{i}))-E_{A_{x_{i}}^{\prime\prime}}(f(x_{i}))\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|E_{N}(f(x_{i})-f(x))\|_{2}+\|E_{N}(f(x))-E_{A_{x_{i}}^{\prime\prime}}(f(x))\|_{2}+\|E_{A_{x_{i}}^{\prime\prime}}(f(x)-f(x_{i}))\|_{2}

i\to\infty
. For the middle component, note that
\|E_{N}(f(x))-E_{A_{x_{i}}^{\prime\prime}}(f(x))\|_{2}\to 0
by the definition of Effros-Maréchal convergence evaluated at
f(x)\in M
. Thus,
E_{N}(a)=a
so that
a\in N
, yielding
A_{x}\subseteq N
and
A_{x}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq N
. Therefore, the map
\pi
is lower semicontinuous.∎

The fiberwise picture connects to the state-space construction as follows.

Example 6.23

(URA associated with continuous fields)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group acting minimally on a compact Hausdorff space
X
. We consider the minimal tensor product
C(X)\otimes_{\min}B
, which we naturally identify with the
C^{*}
-algebra
C(X,B)
. The diagonal action is defined on elementary tensors by
g\cdot(f\otimes b)=(g\cdot f)\otimes\alpha_{g}(b)
. This ensures that
\pi(gx)=A_{gx}^{\prime\prime}=\alpha_{g}(A_{x}^{\prime\prime})
, making the fiber map
x\mapsto A_{x}^{\prime\prime}

\Gamma
-equivariant. Since the map
\pi:X\to\mathrm{Sub}(M)
given by
x\mapsto A_{x}^{\prime\prime}
is lower semi-continuous (Lemma
6.22
), a classical application of the Baire Category Theorem (see e.g., Kuratowski
[
14
, Section 39.IV]
) guarantees that the set
X_{0}
of points of continuity of
\pi
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
. Since
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is minimal, for any
x_{0}\in X_{0}
, arguing similarly as in Theorem
6.4
, we see that

\mathcal{U}=\overline{\{\alpha_{g}(A_{x_{0}}^{\prime\prime})\mid g\in\Gamma\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}\subset\mathrm{Sub}(M).

is a URA.

6.7.
Amenable URAs via hypertrace dynamics

Up to now, we have looked at the centralizer of a state relative to a chosen
\Gamma
-invariant test algebra
A
. There is an important degenerate case — the maximal one,
A=\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
— which yields a different and equally interesting construction: the fixed-point set of
\varphi
in
L(\Gamma)
. This produces
amenable
URAs, because the relevant centralizer becomes a hypertrace and Connes’s theorem gives amenability.

Taking the pointwise closure of positive definite functions
\phi_{\mathcal{M}}
introduces limit states that may not correspond to actual von Neumann subalgebras. As such, we begin by isolating the space of states that extend the canonical trace. Let
S(\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)))
denote the state space of
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
. We define the subset
K
as before

K=\left\{\varphi\in S(\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)))\;\middle|\;\varphi|_{L(\Gamma)}=\tau_{0}\right\}.

It follows from Lemma
6.1

that the set
K
is a compact, convex
\Gamma
-space. Furthermore, there exists a minimal, closed,
\Gamma
-invariant subset
X\subset K
.

To extract von Neumann subalgebras from this minimal state space, we look to the centralizer of each state as before, but enlarging it.

Definition 6.24
.

For each
\varphi\in X
, we define its fixed point in the group von Neumann algebra as

\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)=\left\{x\in L(\Gamma)\;\middle|\;\varphi(xT)=\varphi(Tx)\text{ for all }T\in\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))\right\}.

Arguing similarly as in Lemma
6.3
, we get that for every
\varphi\in X
, the set
\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)
is an amenable von Neumann subalgebra of
L(\Gamma)
. Moreover, the map
\Phi:X\to\mathrm{Sub}(L(\Gamma))
defined by
\Phi(\varphi)=\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)
is upper semicontinuous. Moreover, we have the following.

Example 6.25

(Construction of Amenable URAs)
.

Let
X
and
\Phi
be defined as above. The map
\Phi:X\to\mathrm{Sub}(L(\Gamma))
possesses a dense
G_{\delta}
set of points of continuity in the Effros-Maréchal topology. For any such point of continuity
\varphi_{0}\in X
, the Effros-Maréchal orbit closure

\mathcal{H}=\overline{\left\{\lambda_{g}\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi_{0})\lambda_{g}^{*}\;\middle|\;g\in\Gamma\right\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}

is a minimal, closed,
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
\mathrm{Sub}(L(\Gamma))
consisting of amenable von Neumann subalgebras. First, arguing similarly as in Theorem
6.4
, we get the existence of points of continuity. We are guaranteed that the set
X_{0}
of points of continuity of
\Phi
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
. Moreover, also arguing similarly as in Theorem
6.4
, we get that

\mathcal{H}=\overline{\left\{\lambda_{g}\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi_{0})\lambda_{g}^{*}\;\middle|\;g\in\Gamma\right\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}=\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}.

is minimal. Since amenability is closed under EM-topology (see
[
2
, Proposition 4.7]
), it follows that every element
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{H}
is amenable.

Definition 6.26

(Amenable Uniformly Recurrent Algebra)
.

Let
X
and
\Phi
be defined as above. For a point of continuity
\varphi_{0}\in X
of
\Phi
, let

\mathcal{H}=\overline{\left\{\lambda_{g}\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi_{0})\lambda_{g}^{*}\;\middle|\;g\in\Gamma\right\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}

We define any such
\mathcal{H}
to be an
Amenable Uniformly Recurrent Algebra
.

Remark 6.27
.

In Example
6.25
, we defined the Amenable URA
\mathcal{H}
as the Effros-Maréchal orbit closure of a single point of continuity,
\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}
. One might naturally ask how this relates to the full closure of the continuity domain,
\overline{\Phi(X_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}
, which directly parallels the original geometric definition of URSs by Glasner and Weiss
[
7
]
. We claim that these two sets are equal, i.e.,

\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}=\overline{\{\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)\mid\varphi\in X_{0}\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}.

To see this, first observe that the conjugation action of
\Gamma
is continuous on both spaces, ensuring that the set of continuity points
X_{0}
is
\Gamma
-invariant. Thus, the orbit
\Gamma\cdot\varphi_{0}
is contained within
X_{0}
, which immediately implies
\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}\subseteq\overline{\Phi(X_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}
.

Conversely, pick any
\psi\in X_{0}
. Because
X
is a minimal
\Gamma
-space, the orbit
\Gamma\cdot\varphi_{0}
is dense in
X
, meaning there exists a net
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\Gamma
such that
g_{i}\varphi_{0}\to\psi
. Since
\psi
is a point of continuity for
\Phi
, we see that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\Phi(g_{i}\varphi_{0})=\Phi(\psi).

Since each
\Phi(g_{i}\varphi_{0})
belongs to
\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})
, we see that
\Phi(\psi)\in\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}
. As this holds for all
\psi\in X_{0}
, we obtain
\overline{\Phi(X_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}\subseteq\overline{\Gamma\cdot\Phi(\varphi_{0})}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}
.

Remark 6.28
.

If
\Gamma
is
C^{*}
-simple, then it follows from
[
3
, Theorem 4.6]
that
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}
.

Recall that
K=\{\varphi\in S(\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)))\mid\varphi|_{L(\Gamma)}=\tau_{0}\}
is a weak-* compact
\Gamma
-space. By Zorn’s lemma,
K
contains a minimal closed
\Gamma

.

Definition 6.29

(The Furstenberg URA)
.

Let
X\subset K

\Gamma

\Phi:X\to\mathrm{Sub}(L(\Gamma))

\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}\in\mathrm{URA}(L(\Gamma))

\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}=\overline{\left\{\lambda_{g}\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi_{0})\lambda_{g}^{*}\;\middle|\;g\in\Gamma\right\}}^{\,\mathrm{EM}}.
