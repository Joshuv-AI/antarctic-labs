Icepaxx Development Plan August 2026

1 Executive summary
Icepaxx will be a next-generation AI-integrated, scale-aware sea-ice package for Earth system prediction founded upon
the physics and biogeochemistry of the CICE-Consortium’s Icepack. Icepaxx will be developed as a C++/Kokkos,
GPU-resident sea-ice column package whose first responsibility is to reproduce a deliberately selected subset of
current Icepack physics in a form that can be tested in two primary host environments: E3SM and neXtSIM. The
package will remain host-neutral at its public interface. The first coupled E3SM application will place Icepaxx
inside the Eulerian MPAS-SeaIce component of E3SMv4. A subsequent E3SMv5 application will couple Icepaxx
inside Omega-swift, a new version of Omega that will integrate ocean, sea ice, waves, icebergs, floating ice shelves,
and tides and will include Material Point Method (MPM) sea-ice dynamics. E3SM provides the global coupled
Earth-system test and the strongest architectural reason for a native C++/Kokkos implementation; neXtSIM
provides a contrasting Lagrangian sea-ice test of host independence, deformation forcing, and column-state exchange
[1]. The scientific reference remains the CICE Consortium Icepack capability [2–4].
The plan makes five changes to the earlier draft.

1. Tier the scope. The first release focuses on the state model, thermodynamics, radiation, snow, ponds,
 ice-thickness distribution (ITD), remapping, flux exchange, and diagnostics. Full biogeochemistry (BGC),
 broader alternate-salt systems, the dedicated iceberg thickness class and near-zero-salinity mushy-physics
 extension, the Icepaxx hybrid-AI layer (Icepaxx-AI), and quantum experiments become optional extensions
 rather than release-blocking work.
2. Use the standard ITD as the operational baseline. The five-category ITD and existing tracer structure
 provide the first coupled state. A proposed joint distribution of sea-ice thickness, macro-porosity, and floe
 size, and the observation-oriented distribution of sea ice and snow thickness and ice density, remain important
 research objects for morphology and observation operators, but they do not initially replace the state used by
 every physics kernel.
3. Design process-level interchangeability. Each process has a stable input-output contract and can be
 implemented by reference physics, a new physical formulation, a learned residual, a learned closure, or an
 emulator. Processes return proposed tendencies; a central update layer owns conservation and admissibility.
4. Migrate incrementally from Icepack. A Fortran reference path, batch-column driver, and differential test
 harness remain available while modules are ported. Configuration coverage is limited to the selected production
 path plus one maintained predecessor where scientifically needed.
5. Gate research extensions. Generative data assimilation, Icepaxx-AI, BGC expansion, and quantum-oriented
 experiments are admitted only after the core package has demonstrated scientific equivalence, coupled stability,
 and a material performance benefit.

The recommended near-term product is therefore not “all of Icepack in C++” and not an end-to-end neural surrogate.
It is a compact, testable system that preserves trusted sea-ice budgets while allowing empirical or expensive
processes to be replaced when evidence supports the change.

Icepaxx Development Plan August 2026

2 Introduction
Sea-ice models must combine increasingly detailed physics with accelerator-oriented computing and rapidly evolving
artificial intelligence (AI). We present Icepaxx, an open-source, scale-aware sea-ice physics and biogeochemistry
package that will be developed in C++ with Kokkos for performance-portable execution on central processing units
(CPUs) and graphics processing units (GPUs). Its two primary testing platforms are the Energy Exascale Earth
System Model (E3SM) and the neXt generation Sea Ice Model (neXtSIM). Coupled E3SM testing will begin in
E3SMv4 with Icepaxx inside the Eulerian Model for Prediction Across Scales–Sea Ice (MPAS-SeaIce) component.
It will then advance in E3SMv5 to Omega-swift, a new version of Omega that will integrate ocean, sea ice, waves,
icebergs, floating ice shelves, and tides and include MPM sea-ice dynamics.
Icepaxx will draw its initial scientific content from the CICE Consortium Icepack package while reorganizing state,
configuration, coupling, and process kernels around explicit interfaces and accelerator-resident data. Thermody-
namics, radiation, snow, melt ponds, thickness redistribution, ridging, floe processes, iceberg thermodynamics,
and biogeochemistry will be migrated incrementally using differential tests, conservation checks, backend compar-
isons, controlled mixed-precision experiments, and coupled validation. The iceberg extension will use a separate
thickness class and near-zero-salinity mushy physics to permit marine-ice growth beneath freshwater icebergs.
Scale-dependent parameters will carry calibration and resolution metadata rather than being treated as universal
constants. Performance assessment will include time, data movement, and joules per trusted solution.
A supported Python interface will expose state and process services for hybrid AI training, analysis, uncertainty
characterization, generative data assimilation, and experiment control. Conventional parameterizations, learned
residuals, closures, and process emulators will share common interfaces, while deterministic update layers enforce
physical bounds and conservation. This combines deductive physical reasoning with inductive learning. Generative
data assimilation development will connect with the U.S. Department of Energy Genesis Mission through Marine
Assimilation with Generative Graphs in E3SM (MAGGIE). An optional experimental layer will support quantum-
emulation research without becoming a production dependency.
Icepaxx provides a verification-driven path from mature sea-ice physics to an AI-integrated package for Earth
system prediction.

3 Scope and design principles
3.1 Scope
Icepaxx will provide a scientifically traceable, performance-portable sea-ice column capability for coupled prediction,
process evaluation, and observation interpretation. The project will be divided into three distinct scope tracks:
Core, Extension, and Research tracks as summarized in Table 1. It will support horizontal resolutions centered
on the 1 km to 10 km regime while keeping its public application programming interface (API) independent of
mesh topology and dynamical-core formulation. Its two primary testing platforms are E3SM and neXtSIM. In
E3SM, coupled testing will begin in E3SMv4 inside the Eulerian MPAS-SeaIce framework and subsequently move
in E3SMv5 to Omega-swift. The latter will combine ocean, sea ice, waves, icebergs, floating ice shelves, and tides
and will use the Material Point Method (MPM) for sea-ice dynamics. neXtSIM exercises a contrasting Lagrangian
sea-ice host [1, 5].
A central scientific thrust is to combine deductive reasoning encoded in conservation laws and process models with
inductive learning from observations, high-resolution and stand-alone process simulations, and coupled ensembles.
Icepaxx will use each where it is strongest: physics to define admissible states and budgets, and AI to infer unresolved
relationships, emulate expensive processes, and expose scale dependence [6].

3.2 Governing principles
Icepaxx development will be governed by the following six design principles:
1. Budgets before novelty. Mass, salt, enthalpy, tracers, and atmosphere/ocean exchange are owned by
 deterministic code.
2. Interfaces before implementations. Public contracts are stable; physical and learned implementations may

3. Evidence before option retention. An option enters the primary path only when an active scientific
 configuration, validation need, or migration requirement justifies it.
4. Reference before replacement. Each ported module is compared against Icepack before scientific changes

5. Energy-aware performance by measurement. Algorithms, software, data layout, and target hardware will

6. Research isolation. Experimental AI, distributional state, BGC, and quantum work use the same APIs but
 do not destabilize the production path.
These governing principles are implemented within the scientific, software, verification, reproducibility, and emerging-
computing standards framework described in Appendix E. The first supported release will not attempt to preserve
every historical Icepack option, reproduce all BGC chemistry, make g(h, ϕ, f ) the universal prognostic state (Table 1),
execute Python in the coupled timestep, provide an end-to-end neural replacement for the whole column, or depend
on quantum hardware. These are deliberate scope controls, not rejections of the longer-term science.

4 Scientific state and process architecture
4.1 A hierarchical state rather than one universal distribution
The state model has four levels.

1. Operational state. A configurable ITD with category area, volume, snow, enthalpy, salinity, ponds, and
 registered tracers. Five categories provide the initial compatibility baseline [7, 8].
2. Morphology extension. Floe-size, ridge/keel descriptors, macro-porosity, and other statistics needed by
 selected processes. These are carried only when an enabled process requires them. Ridge geometry can draw on
 observational constraints such as average keel shape [9, 10].
3. Iceberg extension state. Icebergs will occupy a separate thickness class, distinct from the mobile sea-ice
 categories. This class will support near-zero-salinity mushy thermodynamics and a basal marine-ice accretion
 state, permitting marine ice to grow beneath freshwater icebergs while retaining explicit and separate mass,
 salt, and enthalpy accounting.
4. Observation and assimilation state. The distributions g(h, ϕ, f ) and G(h, hs , ρ) and their probabilistic
 transforms are represented by separate schemas. They are first developed in batch mode for altimetry, freeboard,
 and GenDAexperiments rather than embedded in every production kernel.

This hierarchy prevents an ambitious observation-space representation from forcing every thermodynamic and
mechanical kernel to operate on a high-rank state before its benefit is established.

4.2 Process contracts and central state update
Each process receives validated state and forcing views and returns a compact set of proposed tendencies, fluxes,
diagnostics, and status information (Figure 1). The process does not allocate column state and does not commit
the update. A central update service applies bounds, resolves competing tendencies, closes budgets, and records
corrections. A representative structure for the process context and returned process result is provided in Appendix C.

4.3 Core process set
The core sequence will be:

1. receive and validate atmosphere, ocean, wave, and host-dynamics forcing;
2. diagnose radiative and turbulent surface terms;
3. evolve snow and melt ponds;
4. solve vertical thermodynamics and basal/frazil exchange;
5. remap the ITD and apply mechanically supplied redistribution tendencies;
6. update registered tracers and diagnostics;
7. reconcile budgets and return aggregate fluxes to the host.

The first thermodynamic target remains mushy-layer sea ice with prognostic enthalpy and salinity [11, 12]. An
iceberg extension will apply the mushy formulation in the near-zero-salinity limit for the separate iceberg thickness
class and support marine-ice accretion at the iceberg base, with iceberg, accreted marine-ice, and ocean budgets
kept explicit. Radiation will begin with the current Delta-Eddington pathway and the SNow, ICe, and Aerosol
Radiative model (SNICAR)-derived snow treatment, and expose a band interface that can later align with the
Rapid Radiative Transfer Model for General Circulation Model Applications – Parallel (RRTMGP) [13–15]. Snow
design will preserve density, liquid-water, grain-size, and redistribution extensibility without requiring all advanced
options in the first coupled milestone [16, 17].

5.1 Language division
C++/Kokkos is the production language and execution model. Python remains the preferred environment for

5.2 Data layout and kernel rules
The production implementation will follow these rules.

• State is held in typed bundles of Kokkos::View objects or equivalent lightweight wrappers. Read-only forcing,
  prognostic state, diagnostics, and scratch storage are distinct.
• Physics kernels do not allocate persistent state, perform hidden host-device copies, read global mutable
  configuration, or call host-model I/O.
• Category, layer, tracer, and spectral dimensions are explicit in schemas. A small set of layouts is benchmarked
  before one becomes the default.
• Batch columns are the primary parallel unit for the first port. Team and vector policies may be introduced only
  where layer or spectral loops justify them.
• Every GPU kernel has a CPU reference execution path, but cross-backend acceptance is based on documented
  tolerances and conservation, not universal bit-for-bit identity.

Process-level contracts will use static dispatch, templates, or compact tagged variants inside GPU kernels rather

5.4 Configuration and provenance
The July draft correctly emphasizes that resolved coupled artifacts can override source defaults. The plan retains
this requirement but separates three layers:

1. Scientific configuration: named process choices, parameters, tracer registry, dimensions, units, and admissible
 ranges.
2. Host mapping: field names, staggering, units, exchange timing, restart mapping, and memory-space ownership.
3. Run provenance: resolved configuration, source commit, compiler, Kokkos backend, hardware, input data,
 model artifact hashes, and tolerances.

Schemas will generate validation code and publication summaries. They will not generate a large compile-time
option matrix.

6.3 Design Convergence policy
For each process, the active source tree will contain one primary production implementation and at most one
maintained predecessor or reference implementation. Older paths remain available through tagged releases, test
fixtures, or a reference branch. Exceptions require a documented scientific need, an owner, and a retirement
criterion.

7 Hybrid-AI plan
7.1 Allowed modes of learned participation
The architecture supports progressively more ambitious learned components.

Residual correction Retain the physical model and learn a bounded correction to its tendency or flux. This
 is the preferred first coupled use.
Learned closure Keep governing budgets explicit but replace an empirical coefficient, regime map, or
 unresolved redistribution function.

Process emulator Replace an expensive process solver with a surrogate trained to reproduce or improve its
 input-output behavior.
Adaptive selection Use uncertainty and regime information to select among validated physical and learned
 implementations. This is a later-stage research capability.

7.2 Candidate prioritization
Not every empirical formula is a good AI target. A simple empirical expression may be cheap, interpretable, and
more robust outside its calibration domain than a neural model. Candidate selection will therefore score scientific
need, training evidence, computational cost, coupling risk, and observability (Table 3).

7.3 Conservative wrapper
Every learned component must declare:

• input and output units, dimensions, normalization, and valid ranges;
• training datasets, sampling strategy, source code, and weight hash;
• uncertainty or reliability measure and an out-of-distribution (OOD) test;
• proposed tendencies rather than unrestricted state replacement;
• conservation and admissibility projection;
• deterministic physical fallback and a reproducible CPU inference mode;
• coupled stability and restart tests before supported use.

7.4 Icepaxx-AI and generative data assimilation
The Icepaxx hybrid-AI research layer (Icepaxx-AI) is best treated as a research program built on top of the stable
core, not as the core package itself. It may learn multimodal representations of model state, forcing, altimetry,
snow, morphology, and process regime. Potential uses include GenDA, synthetic ensembles, counterfactual process
studies, and adaptive parameterization selection.
This GenDA work will be developed in collaboration with the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Genesis Mission
through the Marine Assimilation with Generative Graphs in E3SM (MAGGIE) project [22].
The first GenDA milestone will be a standalone batch workflow that maps between an operational ITD state and
likelihoods in G(h, hs , ρ) using mission-specific observation uncertainty. Relevant observation foundations include
the Ice, Cloud, and Land Elevation Satellite (ICESat), CryoSat-2, the Ice, Cloud, and Land Elevation Satellite-2
(ICESat-2), and the Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) mission [23–26]. The workflow will initialize or
constrain coupled simulations only after the transform and posterior samples are validated.

7.5 Quantum-oriented research
Quantum or quantum-inspired experiments will operate on compact latent or optimization problems, never on the
full production state by default. They remain optional, have a classical reference implementation, and advance only
if they demonstrate an advantage over strong classical baselines. No quantum runtime belongs in the core release
plan.

8.1 Primary testing platforms: E3SM and neXtSIM
The two primary testing platforms will be E3SM and neXtSIM. They are complementary rather than interchangeable.
Within E3SM, the coupled path will be staged. The first application will place Icepaxx inside the Eulerian MPAS-
SeaIce component of E3SMv4. The next application will place it inside Omega-swift in E3SMv5. Omega-swift
will be a new version of Omega that integrates ocean, sea ice, waves, icebergs, floating ice shelves, and tides,
with MPM sea-ice dynamics. neXtSIM tests the same public API in a Lagrangian sea-ice host, with emphasis on

deformation forcing, category and tracer evolution, and column-state exchange [1].

8.1.1 MPAS-SeaIce/E3SMv4 adapter
The initial MPAS-SeaIce/E3SMv4 adapter will preserve the host’s Eulerian category, layer, tracer, and coupling
semantics while making ownership, synchronization, and host-device transfer explicit. It will provide the first
coupled demonstration of Icepaxx in an Earth system model without requiring the later Omega-swift architecture
to be complete.
The adapter will initially support:

• atmosphere and ocean flux inputs with units and sign conventions encoded;
• Eulerian category/layer state and restart mapping;
• ocean frazil, heat, salt, freshwater, and stress exchange;
• aggregate concentration, volume, snow, albedo, surface temperature, and diagnostic fields required by E3SMv4;
• optional wave and floe descriptors through versioned extension views;
• device-aware stepping with measured transfer counts.

8.1.2 OMEGA-swift/E3SMv5 adapter
The subsequent Omega-swift/E3SMv5 adapter will embed Icepaxx in an integrated marine component containing
ocean, sea ice, waves, icebergs, floating ice shelves, and tides. It will use the public dynamics views to receive
drift, deformation, and strain-rate information from the Omega-swift MPM sea-ice dynamics and will keep
marine-component synchronization and data movement explicit.
The adapter will additionally support:

• shared exchange contracts among ocean, sea ice, waves, icebergs, floating ice shelves, and tides;
• MPM deformation and mechanics forcing for ridging, floe, and morphology processes;
• the separate iceberg thickness class and its restart and diagnostic mappings;
• near-zero-salinity mushy thermodynamics and basal marine-ice growth beneath icebergs; and
• accelerator-resident execution where the coupled decomposition permits, with synchronization and transfer
  accounting.

8.1.3 neXtSIM adapter
The neXtSIM adapter will initially support:

• column-state and forcing exchange on the host’s Lagrangian representation;
• explicit deformation and strain-rate inputs for mechanics and ridging;
• category, tracer, diagnostic, and restart mapping as the host state evolves;
• versioned wave and floe descriptors where used; and
• platform-specific timing, memory, and reproducibility measurements.

8.2 Batch driver before additional host adapters
A high-throughput batch-column driver is a core deliverable, not an ancillary test. It enables differential testing,
sensitivity studies, calibration, observation operators, AI training data generation, and performance profiling
without coupled-model ambiguity.
The testing architecture will also support an optional sea-ice dynamics emulator that supplies drift, deformation,
strain-rate, and mechanics forcing to the column package. The emulator may use prescribed behavior or be trained
on output from neXtSIM, MPM, or other sea-ice dynamical cores, allowing host responses and coupling edge cases
to be tested without running every full host model.

9 Verification, validation, and performance
9.1 Test hierarchy
1. Unit tests: formulas, units, boundary conditions, and small deterministic cases.
2. Property tests: mass, salt, enthalpy, area, positivity, monotonic limits, and invariants across randomized
 admissible states.
3. Agentic tests: LLM-driven adversarial, fuzz, edge-case, property/invariant, regression, and failure-scenario
 testing to identify extreme, pathological, and unexpected behaviors, and diagnose failures.
4. Differential tests: Icepack and Icepaxx columns under the same forcing, options, and solve order.
5. Reference experiments: seasonal column cases based on the Surface Heat Budget of the Arctic Ocean
 (SHEBA), the Multidisciplinary drifting Observatory for the Study of Arctic Climate (MOSAiC), and available
 Antarctic data [27, 28].
6. Backend tests: CPU and every claimed GPU backend, including memory-space checks, tolerance envelopes,
 controlled mixed-precision experiments, and performance regression.
7. Host-adapter tests: exchange semantics, restart, decomposition, and conservation in MPAS-SeaIce/E3SMv4,
 neXtSIM, and subsequently Omega-swift/E3SMv5.
8. Integrated host tests: short stability tests and seasonal integrations first in MPAS-SeaIce/E3SMv4 and
 neXtSIM, followed by Omega-swift/E3SMv5 and platform-appropriate prediction and process experiments.
9. AI tests: held-out skill, OOD behavior, uncertainty calibration, projection frequency, fallback behavior, and
 coupled stability.

9.2 Scientific equivalence rather than universal bit identity
Bit-for-bit agreement is useful within a controlled compiler/backend path but will not be the sole cross-architecture
standard. Production acceptance will combine:

• exact checks for discrete state transitions where appropriate;
• tight numerical tolerances for deterministic column outputs;
• exact or tolerance-based budget closure;
• distributional checks for stochastic or learned pathways;
• sensitivity and regime-transition comparisons;
• matched-forcing cross-host checks where E3SM and neXtSIM semantics permit; and
• coupled climatology, variability, and drift diagnostics.

9.3 Performance metrics
A GPU port is justified by measured coupled benefit, not language alone. Following Dongarra et al. [6], performance
assessment will emphasize the energy required to produce a scientifically trusted result, not peak throughput alone.
For Icepaxx, joules per trusted solution is the total energy consumed over a defined workflow boundary divided by
the number of accepted, scientifically valid outcomes. The workflow boundary and scientific acceptance criteria
will be declared for each benchmark so that energy measurements remain tied to verification, budget closure, and
validation. Each milestone will report:

Icepaxx Development Plan August 2026

• column-updates per second and cost per simulated year;
• Icepaxx joules per trusted solution over the declared workflow boundary;
• time in physics, inference, synchronization, and host-device transfer;
• memory footprint per column and per category/layer/tracer;
• strong and weak scaling for representative batch sizes;
• speedup relative to the Fortran reference on the same node class;
• effect on total MPAS-SeaIce/E3SMv4, Omega-swift/E3SMv5, and neXtSIM timestep cost, reported separately
  for each host;
• reproducibility and performance variance across supported systems.

A module will not be rewritten solely for GPU use if profiling shows that it is cheap and transfers can be eliminated
by a narrower adapter. Conversely, a modest kernel speedup may still be valuable if it enables a fully resident
coupled execution path.

9.4 Reproducibility and publication
Releases and scientific publications will record version, commit, schema, host, compiler, Kokkos backend, hardware,
option set, forcing data, observation operators, AI artifact hashes, and validation protocol. Machine-readable run
summaries, persistent release identifiers, software citation, and Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable
(FAIR) data practices will follow established principles [29, 30].

10 Governance and relationship to the CICE Consortium
Icepaxx will be governed as a CICE Consortium capability or closely coupled Consortium project, with explicit
agreement on naming, licensing, release ownership, and the relationship to Icepack. This plan does not assume that
Icepaxx automatically replaces Icepack.
Icepaxx will be developed and released as open-source software. Gate 0 will confirm an open-source license compatible
with the Icepack license and distribution policy. The public repository will follow established CICE Consortium
contribution and testing practices, with documented contribution guidelines, public issue and pull-request review,
automated tests, a code of conduct, transparent release notes, citation metadata, and machine-readable license and
dependency records (Software Package Data Exchange (SPDX)-compatible where applicable) [3].
A productive relationship has four parts:

1. Icepack remains the mature portable Fortran reference and development source for hosts that do not need a
 C++/Kokkos path.
2. Icepaxx becomes the GPU-native path and process experimentation environment, initially tested through
 MPAS-SeaIce/E3SMv4 and neXtSIM, then through Omega-swift/E3SMv5.
3. Scientific changes are proposed upstream with evidence, rather than allowing silent divergence of formulas and
 parameters.
4. Shared tests, cases, documentation concepts, and release metadata reduce duplication even where implementa-
 tions differ.

The dynamics-emulator pathway described in Section 8 will provide a controlled mechanism for using additional
dynamical cores in Consortium testing, including emulators trained on their output, without requiring every
dynamical core to become a maintained primary adapter.
Architecture decision records will be required for public API changes, state semantics, new external dependencies,
primary/predecessor process changes, and AI admission to coupled production. Each major module needs a scientific
owner and a software owner; the same person may fill both roles, but both responsibilities must be explicit.

14 Success criteria
The program will be judged by outcomes rather than the fraction of Fortran translated. A successful first production
release will satisfy the following.

1. A documented, stable public API supports a batch driver, MPAS-SeaIce/E3SMv4, and neXtSIM without
 host-specific physics branches, and carries unchanged process contracts into Omega-swift/E3SMv5.
2. The selected core physics reaches scientific-equivalence and production-readiness criteria relative to Icepack
 reference cases.
3. Mass, salt, enthalpy, area, and coupled flux budgets are auditable through a central ledger.
4. CPU and supported GPU backends pass correctness, memory-space, restart, and performance-regression tests.
5. Integrated execution in both primary platforms demonstrates a measurable benefit appropriate to each host:
 lower cost, reduced data movement, enabled resolution or complexity, or a combination of these.
6. New processes can be introduced without modifying the host adapter or state ownership model.
7. At least one learned process demonstrates an evidence-based improvement with uncertainty, fallback, and
 coupled stability, or the project explicitly records that no candidate yet meets the gate.
8. Releases contain machine-readable provenance and are citable and reproducible.

16 Conclusion
Icepaxx will establish a shared, open-source foundation for advancing sea-ice physics and biogeochemistry in Earth
system prediction. Its development will preserve the scientific maturity of Icepack while reorganizing state, process
interfaces, coupling, and verification for performance-portable execution and AI-integrated research. E3SM and
neXtSIM will provide the primary and deliberately contrasting test environments. Coupled E3SM use will begin in
the Eulerian MPAS-SeaIce component of E3SMv4 and advance to Omega-swift in E3SMv5, where MPM sea-ice
dynamics will operate within a marine component that also includes ocean, waves, icebergs, floating ice shelves,
and tides. This sequence will test both Eulerian and Lagrangian applications without embedding host-specific
physics in the core.
The staged implementation will place scientific trust ahead of code volume. Each migrated process will be
evaluated against Icepack through differential testing, conservation budgets, observational and coupled validation,
backend checks, and energy-aware performance measures, including joules per trusted solution. Scale-dependent
assumptions and calibration domains will be explicit, versioned, and reproducible. The iceberg extension will use a
separate thickness class and near-zero-salinity mushy physics to represent marine-ice growth beneath icebergs while
retaining explicit mass, salt, and enthalpy budgets. Learned closures and emulators will enter through the same
process contracts as conventional parameterizations and will remain subject to deterministic bounds, conservation,
uncertainty assessment, and physical fallback.
This architecture will allow Icepaxx to combine deductive physical reasoning with inductive learning while
remaining reviewable and extensible. By developing the software openly with the CICE Consortium and host-model
communities, the project will provide a credible route from established sea-ice science to efficient, scale-aware, and
testable capabilities for future Earth system models.

E.3 Agentic testing as a proposed sea-ice community standard
Agentic testing is a first-class part of the nine-category Icepaxx testing regime. The proposed community objective
is:

 Lead the sea ice community with agentic testing standards: LLM-driven adversarial, fuzz, edge-case, prop-
 erty/invariant, regression, and failure-scenario testing to identify extreme, pathological, and unexpected
 behaviors, and diagnose failures.

This does not delegate scientific acceptance to an agent. Agentic testing is a mechanism for generating, perturbing,
triaging, and explaining tests that are then judged by deterministic scientific requirements. For a generated test to
enter the trusted suite, the case must be reproducible and its expected behavior must be tied to a requirement,
invariant, reference calculation, or reviewed failure criterion. At minimum, the test record will include the
model or agent version, prompt or task template, generated inputs, relevant random seeds, software commit,
configuration/schema version, backend, and the human review decision.

The agentic layer complements rather than replaces the other eight categories: unit tests, property tests, differential
tests, reference experiments, backend tests, host-adapter tests, integrated host tests, and AI tests. A useful CICE
Consortium contribution would be a shared corpus of adversarial sea-ice cases and failure signatures that can be
exercised against both Icepack and Icepaxx, and eventually against additional Consortium host configurations.

E.4 Status, conformance, and review
Each standard or community criterion used by Icepaxx will carry one of four statuses in the release record:

1. Adopted external standard: a mature published standard or reference convention that the relevant Icepaxx
 component follows.
2. CICE Consortium reference practice: a Consortium or Icepack practice used as the community compatibility
 baseline.
3. Icepaxx project requirement: a documented rule used for acceptance now, even if no external standard yet
 exists.
4. Proposed community extension: a practice that Icepaxx can prototype and bring to the CICE Consortium
 for review, but that is not represented as Consortium policy until formally adopted through Consortium
 governance.

This status distinction is especially important for rapidly changing AI and quantum-computing methods. It allows
Icepaxx to move quickly and test new approaches while keeping the scientific contract stable, the evidence visible,
and the relationship to CICE Consortium governance explicit.

E.5 Minimum standards checklist for a release or benchmark
A release, published benchmark, or Consortium-facing comparison will state, as applicable:

• the physical-constant set, thermodynamic convention, and sea-ice nomenclature used;
• the Icepack/CICE reference case, version, and option set used for scientific comparison;
• the applicable results from the nine-part testing regime, including any new agentic tests and the reviewed failure
  criteria they exercise;
• CPU/GPU backend, compiler, floating-point mode, tolerances, and any controlled mixed-precision choices;
• configuration, restart, provenance, and observation-operator schema versions;
• AI artifact identifiers, hashes, training-domain statement, uncertainty, OOD, projection, and fallback evidence
  when a learned component is used;
• end-to-end performance and energy information, including joules per trusted solution when the benchmark is
  used to justify a new compute path;
• the status of each nonstandard practice as an Icepaxx project requirement or a proposed CICE Consortium
  community extension rather than an already adopted external standard.
