Analytic summation of series involving higher-order derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind and their applications to convolved linear recurrent sequences
Khamitov V, Dmytryshyn D, Gray D, Stokolos A

Abstract
This paper considers functional series whose terms are higher-order derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, where the degree of the polynomial is related to the order of the derivative. Analytic summation is used to determine the rational functions to which these series converge. These functions are expressed in terms of Chebyshev polynomials evaluated at a specific argument. Connections are established between derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind and special numerical sequences generated by linear recurrence relations. New closed-form formulas are obtained for the sums of the series at various values of the argument. As consequences, combinatorial identities are derived for the Fibonacci, Lucas, and Pell numbers, for sections of the Fibonacci sequence, and for their convolutions. By means of analytic continuation, sums of formally divergent series are obtained, which in special cases correspond to the classical Euler formulas. Keywords. Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, Gegenbauer polynomials, functional series, analytic continuation, Fibonacci numbers, Lucas numbers, Pell numbers, convolved Fibonacci numbers, convolved Pell numbers, convolved k-sections of the Fibonacci sequence.

Introduction and Problem Statement

In
[
14
,
11
,
2
]
, it was noted that identifying internal connections among Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, their derivatives, and analytic functions is an important and interesting problem and, moreover, that the results in this direction are clearly insufficient. This paper considers specific series associated with derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, in which the degree of the polynomial is related to the order of the derivative, and which converge to rational functions. These functions are expressed through Chebyshev polynomials with a composite argument. Consequently, various identities are obtained for series related to the Fibonacci, Lucas, and Pell numbers, to the convolved Fibonacci and Pell numbers, and to the
\displaystyle k
-section numbers of the Fibonacci sequence. Further consequences concern the summation of formally divergent series by analytic continuation to the boundary of the domain of convergence of these series.

For every integer
\displaystyle N\geq 0
, the family of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind
\displaystyle U_{N}(x)
is defined as follows; see, for example,
[
14
]

U_{N}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}{N-j\choose j}(2z)^{N-2j},

(1)

where

\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]=\begin{cases}\frac{N}{2},&N\mbox{ is even}\\
\\
\frac{N-1}{2},&N\mbox{ is odd}\end{cases},\qquad{N-j\choose j}=\frac{(N-j)!}{j!(N-2j)!}.

For
\displaystyle N<0
, the family of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind can be defined by the formula
\displaystyle U_{-N}(z)=-U_{N-2}(z)
.

Formula (
1
) makes it possible to define the polynomials
\displaystyle U_{N}(z)
for all complex
\displaystyle z
, and also yields explicit formulas for derivatives of order
\displaystyle s
(
\displaystyle 0\leq s\leq N
) of the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind:

U_{N}^{(s)}(z)=s!\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N-s}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}{N-j\choose j}{N-2j\choose s}2^{N-2j}z^{N-2j-s}.

Formulas connecting derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind to the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind themselves can be found in
[

,
11
,
2
,
5
]
.

The main objective of the present work is:

•

to derive formulas for the sums of the series

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\sigma}U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(z)}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!},\quad|z|<1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{-\sigma}U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(z)}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!},\quad|z|>1;

•

to obtain special formulas for certain linear recurrent sequences;

•

and to derive summation formulas for certain divergent series by analytic continuation of the resulting rational functions.

The relevance of the present work is due to the growing interest in the search for analytic representations of various types of sums involving orthogonal polynomials, in particular Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind. These polynomials are widely used in approximation theory, numerical methods, and cryptography
[
12
]
. However, whereas the properties of the polynomials themselves have been studied in detail, series involving their higher-order derivatives remain less investigated
[
3
]
. One of the motivations for writing this article is the possibility of extending properties of derivatives
\displaystyle U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(z)
to number theory. Establishing exact values for the sums of such series not only makes it possible to discover new properties of convolved recurrent sequences, but also provides tools for summing certain divergent series in the sense of analytic continuation.

Lemma 2.1
.

For convolved Pell numbers, the following relation holds

P_{N}^{(s)}=\frac{(-i)^{N-1}}{2^{s}s!}U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(i).

Proof.

The function
\displaystyle p(z)=\frac{z}{1-2z-z^{2}}
is the generating function for the Pell sequence
[

]

\frac{z}{1-2z-z^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}P_{j}z^{j}.

For the convolved Pell numbers
\displaystyle P_{N}^{(s)}

Indeed, using

\frac{z}{1-2z-z^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-2z-z^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}P_{\ell+1}P_{j-\ell}\right)z^{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}P_{j}^{(1)}z^{j},

\displaystyle g(z,t)=\frac{1}{1-2tz+z^{2}}
is the generating function for the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind
\displaystyle U_{N}(t)

[
12
]
, so that

\frac{1}{1-2tz+z^{2}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}U_{j}(t)z^{j}.

Let us determine the higher-order derivatives of
\displaystyle g(z,t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{s}}{\partial t^{s}}g(z,t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2^{s}s!\frac{z^{s}}{(1-2tz+z^{2})^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}U_{j}^{(s)}(t)z^{j}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=s}^{\infty}U_{j}^{(s)}(t)z^{j}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}U_{j+s-1}^{(s)}(t)z^{j+s-1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle(U_{j}^{(s)}(t)=0,\quad j<s).

\frac{z}{(1-2tz+z^{2})^{s+1}}=\frac{1}{2^{s}s!}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}U_{j+s-1}^{(s)}(t)z^{j}.

\displaystyle P_{N}^{(s)}=\frac{(-i)^{N-1}}{2^{s}s!}U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(i)

Main Results

Denote
\displaystyle A_{N,s,j}={N-1+s-j\choose j}{N-1+s-2j\choose s}2^{N-1-2j}
. Then,

\frac{1}{2^{s}s!}U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}A_{N,s,j}z^{N-1-2j}.

(6)

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
\displaystyle j=0,1,\ldots,\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]
and
\displaystyle|z|<1
, the representation

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}A_{N,\sigma,j}z^{\sigma}={N-1-j\choose j}\frac{2^{N-1-2j}}{(1-z)^{N-j}}

(7)

holds.

Proof.

By Newton’s binomial formula,

\frac{1}{(1-z)^{N-j}}=\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}{N-1-j+\sigma\choose\sigma}z^{\sigma}.

Let us compare the coefficients of the series in (
7
). On the left-hand-side the coefficients are equal to

{N-1+\sigma-j\choose j}{N-1+\sigma-2j\choose\sigma}2^{N-1-2j},

and on the right-hand-side are equal to

{N-1-j\choose j}{N-1-j+\sigma\choose\sigma}2^{N-1-2j}.

It is easy to verify that these coefficients coincide.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The following relations hold:

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\sigma}U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(z)}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}{N-1-j\choose j}\frac{(2z)^{N-1-2j}}{(1-z)^{N-j}},\quad|z|<1;

(8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{-\sigma}U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(z)}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}

(9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}{N-1-j\choose j}\frac{2^{N-1-2j}z^{2N-1-3j}}{(z-1)^{N-j}},\quad|z|>1.

Proof.

It follows from (
6
) that
\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(z)}{2^{s}s!}\sim\frac{2^{N-1}}{(N-1)!}s^{N-1}z^{N-1}
for
\displaystyle s\rightarrow\infty
. Consequently, the series
\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\sigma}U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(z)}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}
converges for
\displaystyle|z|<1
. Since

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\sigma}U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(z)}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}z^{\sigma}\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}A_{N,\sigma,j}z^{N-1-2j}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}z^{N-1-2j}\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}A_{N,\sigma,j}z^{\sigma},

taking Lemma
3.1
into account, we obtain formula (
8
). Formula (
9
) is proved analogously by replacing
\displaystyle z
with
\displaystyle\frac{1}{z}
. Convergence of the series is guaranteed when
\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{z}\right|<1\Leftrightarrow|z|>1
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

The following relations hold:

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\sigma}U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(z)}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}=(1-z)^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}U_{N-1}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-z}}\right),\quad|z|<1;

(10)

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{-\sigma}U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(z)}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}=\left(\frac{z}{z-1}\right)^{\frac{N+1}{2}}U_{N-1}\left(z\sqrt{\frac{z}{1-z}}\right),\quad|z|>1.

(11)

Proof.

According to (
1
),
\displaystyle\displaystyle U_{N-1}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}{N-1-j\choose j}(2x)^{N-1-2j}
. Let us represent

(2z)^{N-1-2j}(1-z)^{j}=\left(\frac{2z}{\sqrt{1-z}}\right)(\sqrt{1-z})^{N-1}.

Equating
\displaystyle 2x=\frac{2z}{\sqrt{1-z}}
, i.e.
\displaystyle x=\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-z}}
, we then have

\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}{N-1-j\choose j}\frac{(2z)^{N-1-2j}}{(1-z)^{N-j}}=(1-z)^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}U_{N-1}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-z}}\right).

Formula (
10
) is proved. For formula (
11
), we transform the argument as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle(2z)^{N-1-2j}z^{N}z^{-j}(z-1)^{-N+j}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(2z)^{N-1-2j}\frac{z^{N}}{(z-1)^{N}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{z}{z-1}}\right)^{N-1-2j}\left(\sqrt{\frac{z}{z-1}}\right)^{-N+1}.

Now, let
\displaystyle\displaystyle 2x=2z\sqrt{\frac{z}{z-1}}
or
\displaystyle\displaystyle x=z\sqrt{\frac{z}{z-1}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]}(-1)^{j}{N-1-j\choose j}\frac{(2)^{N-1-2j}(z)^{2N-1-3j}}{(z-1)^{N-j}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{z^{N}}{(z-1)^{N}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{z-1}{z}}\right)^{N-1}U_{N-1}\left(\frac{z\sqrt{z}}{\sqrt{z-1}}\right),

. Consider the analytic continuation of this function at the point
\displaystyle z=e^{it}
.

Corollary 3.7
.

The following formulas hold:

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}e^{it\sigma}\frac{U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(e^{it})}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}=\left(2\sin\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right)^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}e^{-\frac{(t-\pi)(N+1)}{4}i}U_{N-1}\left(\frac{e^{\frac{3t+\pi}{4}i}}{\sqrt{2\sin(t/2)}}\right),

(12)

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sigma}\frac{U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(e^{it})}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}=\left(2\sin\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right)^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}e^{\frac{(t-\pi)(N+1)}{4}i}U_{N-1}\left(\frac{e^{\frac{5t-\pi}{4}i}}{\sqrt{2\sin(t/2)}}\right),

(13)

where the sums are understood in the sense of the analytic continuation of the corresponding functions.

Let us consider the particular cases of formula (
12
).

•

Let
\displaystyle t=\frac{\pi}{3}
. Then,

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}e^{i\pi\sigma/3}\frac{U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(e^{i\pi/3})}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}=e^{\frac{(N+1)\pi}{6}i}U_{N-1}(i),

whence, taking (
5
) into account, we obtain

e^{-\frac{(2N-1)\pi}{3}i}\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}e^{i\pi\sigma/3}\frac{U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(e^{i\pi/3})}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}=P_{N}.

•

Let
\displaystyle t=\frac{\pi}{2}
. Then,

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}e^{i\pi\sigma/2}\frac{U_{N+\sigma-1}^{(\sigma)}(e^{i\pi/2})}{2^{\sigma}\sigma!}=(2)^{-\frac{N+1}{4}}e^{\frac{(N+1)\pi}{8}i}U_{N-1}(2^{-1/4}e^{i5\pi/8}),

whence, taking (
5
) into account, we obtain

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(i)^{\sigma}P_{N}^{(\sigma)}=(2)^{-\frac{N+1}{4}}e^{\frac{(3N-5)\pi}{8}i}U_{N-1}(2^{-1/4}e^{i5\pi/8}).

Examples:

\displaystyle\displaystyle N=1:\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(i)^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2};

\displaystyle\displaystyle N=2:\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(i)^{\sigma}(2+2\sigma)=i,\mbox{whence}

\qquad\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(i)^{\sigma}\sigma=-\frac{1}{2};

\displaystyle\displaystyle N=3:\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(i)^{\sigma}(1+\sigma)(5+2\sigma)=-1+i\frac{3}{2},\mbox{whence}

\hskip 28.45274pt\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(i)^{\sigma}\sigma^{2}=-\frac{i}{2};

\displaystyle\displaystyle N=4:\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(i)^{\sigma}\frac{2}{3}(1+\sigma)(2+\sigma)(9+2\sigma)=-3+i,\mbox{whence}

\hskip 14.22636pt\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(i)^{\sigma}\sigma^{3}=1,

etc. The above formulas were known to Euler.

•

Letting
\displaystyle t=\pi
yields the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

The following equality holds:

\frac{1}{2^{s}s!}U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(-1)=(-1)^{N-1}{N+2s\choose N-1}.

Proof.

Formula (4) in
[
5
]
implies that

\frac{1}{2^{s}s!}U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(z^{1/2}+z^{-1/2}\right)\right)=z^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{N+s-1-j\choose s}{s+j\choose j}z^{j}.

Hence,

\frac{1}{2^{s}s!}U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(1)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{N+s-1-j\choose N-1-j}{s+j\choose j}.

A variation of Vandermonde’s identity
[
13
]
gives the following,

\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{N+s-1-j\choose N-1-j}{s+j\choose j}={N+2s\choose N-1}.

This can be illustrated by appealing to a counting example of placing balls into distinguishable bins. Quite famously,
\displaystyle{n+k-1\choose k-1}={n+k-1\choose n}
counts the number of ways of distributing
\displaystyle n
balls into
\displaystyle k
distinguishable bins. Then, let there be
\displaystyle 2s+2
distinguishable containers and
\displaystyle N-1
balls. We have that
\displaystyle{N-1+s-j\choose N-1-j}
counts the number of ways to distribute
\displaystyle N-1-j
balls into the first
\displaystyle s+1
distinguishable containers, while
\displaystyle{s+j\choose j}
is the number of ways of distributing
\displaystyle j
balls into the next
\displaystyle s+1
containers. Thus, summing over all
\displaystyle j=0
to
\displaystyle N-1
just yields the total number of ways of distributing
\displaystyle N-1
balls into
\displaystyle 2s+2
containers. Whence,

\frac{1}{2^{s}s!}U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(1)={N+2s\choose N-1}.

Since
\displaystyle U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(z)=(-1)^{N-1}U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(-z)
, the lemma is proved.
∎

From letting
\displaystyle t=\pi
in (
13
) and using Lemma
3.8
, it follows that

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}{(-1)^{\sigma}}{N+2\sigma\choose N-1}=(-1)^{N-1}2^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}U_{N-1}\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right),

whence

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sigma}{N+2\sigma\choose N-1}=2^{-\frac{N}{2}}\sin\left(\frac{N\pi}{4}\right).

(14)

Note that

2^{-\frac{N}{2}}\sin\left(\frac{N\pi}{4}\right)=\begin{cases}0,&N\equiv 0(\mbox{mod}\ 4)\\
\frac{(-1)^{\left[\frac{N}{4}\right]}}{2^{\left[\frac{N+1}{2}\right]}},&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Let us consider examples.

•

\displaystyle N=1\rightarrow\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}
;

•

\displaystyle N=2\rightarrow\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sigma}(2+2\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}
, whence

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma=-\frac{1}{4};

•

\displaystyle N=3\rightarrow\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sigma}\frac{1}{2}(2+2\sigma)(3+2\sigma)=\frac{1}{4}
, whence

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma^{2}=0;

•

\displaystyle N=4\rightarrow\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sigma}\frac{1}{6}(2+2\sigma)(3+2\sigma)(4+2\sigma)=0
,
whence

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sigma}\sigma^{3}=\frac{1}{8};

and so on. These formulas coincide with Euler’s formulas.

More generally, formula (
14
) can be considered for any complex number
\displaystyle N
if we replace
\displaystyle{N+2\sigma\choose N-1}
by
\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(N+2\sigma+1)}{\Gamma(N)\Gamma(2+2\sigma)}
. Then, for an arbitrary complex
\displaystyle z
(including the poles of the corresponding Gamma functions), we obtain

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(z+2\sigma+1)}{\Gamma(2+2\sigma)}=\Gamma(z)2^{-z/2}\sin\left(\frac{z\pi}{4}\right).

(15)

For
\displaystyle|z|\geq 1
the series can be understood in the sense of the analytic continuation of the corresponding function. Formula (
15
) generalizes Euler’s formulas for summations of alternating power series.

4
Conclusion

In the present article, we develop the properties of the specific polynomials
\displaystyle U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(z)
, i.e. derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind in which the order of the polynomial is related to the order of the derivative. Such polynomials are related to the Gegenbauer polynomials
\displaystyle C_{N}^{(\alpha)}(z)

[
6
]
by the relation

\frac{1}{2^{s}s!}U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(x)=C_{N-1}^{(s+1)}(x).

Formulas (
10
) and (
11
) can be rewritten as follows:

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}z^{\sigma}C_{N}^{(1+\sigma)}(z)=\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)^{\frac{N+2}{2}}C_{N}^{(1)}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-z}}\right),\quad|z|<1;

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}z^{-\sigma}C_{N}^{(1+\sigma)}(z)=\left(\frac{z}{z-1}\right)^{\frac{N+2}{2}}C_{N}^{(1)}\left(z\sqrt{\frac{z}{z-1}}\right),\quad|z|>1.

Similarly, from Corollaries
3.6
and
3.7
we obtain formulas connecting a classical combinatorial sequence with certain aspects of the theory of special functions and complex sequences:

F_{N}=(-i)^{N-1}(1-z_{0})^{\frac{N+1}{2}}\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}(z_{0})^{\sigma}C_{N-1}^{(1+\sigma)}(z_{0}),

where
\displaystyle z_{0}=\frac{1}{2}e^{i\arctan(\sqrt{15})}=\frac{1}{8}(1+i\sqrt{15})
, and

P_{N}=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{3}(2N-1)}\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}e^{i\pi\sigma/3}C_{N-1}^{(1+\sigma)}(e^{i\pi/3}).

And then there are “magic” values of the argument for which the infinite sum of transcendental functions degenerates into a discrete sequence of integers, i.e.

\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\infty}z^{\sigma}C_{N}^{(1+\sigma)}(1/2)=(2)^{\frac{N+2}{2}}C_{N}^{(1)}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)=(2)^{\frac{N+2}{2}}\frac{\sin\left(\frac{(N+1)\pi}{4}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}.

Note that the series of Gegenbauer polynomials in which the summation is taken over the upper index do not occur in the classical handbooks.

In this work, exact analytic expressions are obtained for the sums of infinite series involving the polynomials
\displaystyle U_{N+s-1}^{(s)}(z)
(or
\displaystyle C_{N-1}^{(s+1)}(z)
). These sums are expressed in terms of Chebyshev polynomials with complex argument. It is shown that derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials are an effective tool for representing convolved Fibonacci numbers, Pell numbers, and the
\displaystyle k
-sections of the Fibonacci sequence. This made it possible to derive new identities for classical numerical sequences. The possibility of evaluating sums of formally divergent series by analytic continuation is demonstrated, which in particular leads to results consistent with the classical Euler formulas. The applied role of derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials is described, for example, in
[
1
]
. The obtained results may be applied in approximation theory and in the development of numerical methods using orthogonal polynomials. Moreover, in view of the formula

U_{M-1}(T_{N}(x))U_{N-1}(x)=U_{NM-1}(x),

they may also prove useful in cryptography
[
10
]
.
