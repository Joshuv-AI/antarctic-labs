Probing pyrazolo-pyrimidines as SIRT2 inhibitors via molecular modelling and biological assays
Authors: Francesca Musumeci, Cecilia Astigiano, Naomi Scarano, Marta Falesiedi, Giorgia Guccione, Jade Calamano, Annalisa Salis, Caterina Miro, Chiara Brullo, Monica Dentice, Elena Cichero, Santina Bruzzone, Silvia Schenone
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Sirtuins (SIRTs) involvement in different biological pathways opens a promising scenario for the development of related ligands to be evaluated as therapeutic agents in different diseases, such as cancer, neurodegenerative disorders, diabetes, cardiovascular and autoimmune diseases. The continuous elucidation of several SIRT2–ligand complexes fostered the discovery of novel and more selective SIRT2 inhibitors (SIRT2Is). Among them, SIRT2 in the presence of SirReal2 analogues was the most investigated. We recently reported pyrazolo-pyrimidine compounds (1–5) as SIRT2Is (1, SIRT2 % inhibition = 81.2%, at 150 μM). Herein, combined ligand- and structure-based approaches enabled us to filter a further series of pyrazolo-pyrimidine-based analogues (6–16) endowed with improved SIRT2 inhibitory activity in comparison to 1–5. The applied molecular modelling studies revealed superimposable molecular interaction fields (MIFs) and protein–ligand contacts compared with the reference SIRT2Is. Subsequent biochemical and biological assays validated the reported strategy and pointed to compounds 11 and 15 (SIRT2 IC50 = 0.3 and 0.1 μM, respectively) as the most promising SIRT2Is, to be further investigated as anticancer agent.

Introduction

Sirtuins (SIRTs) are known as class III histone deacetylases (HDACs), a group of enzymes that counteracts the activity played by histone acetyltransferases (HATs)1 catalyzing the removal of acetyl groups from ε-N-acetyl lysine aminoacids of histones and other substrate proteins.2

SIRTs include seven isoforms (SIRT1–7) characterized by different subcellular localization and substrate specificity. Structurally, SIRT1–7 share a central catalytic domain including approximately 270 amino acids, delimited by a Rossmann fold, and by a smaller cavity with NAD+- and a zinc-binding site. However, the seven sirtuins differ in the N-terminal and C-terminal domains.3 Based on SIRTs involvement in different biological pathways, SIRTs dysregulation has been investigated as a possible therapeutic target in many diseases, such as cancer, neurodegenerative disorders, diabetes, and cardiovascular diseases.4–7 In particular, the search for effective anti-cancer agents was attempted by probing SIRT1/2.8–10 SIRT1 represents the first human sirtuin to be identified. In cancer, SIRT1 has a controversial role, being reported with oncopromoter and oncosuppressor functions.11,12 In addition, SIRT1 activation was deeply explored in neurodegenerative disorders13,14 such as Parkinson's disease (PD)15 and Alzheimer's (AD).16,17

SIRT2 is mainly expressed in the central nervous system (CNS) being overexpressed in neurological diseases, where it seems to promote neurodegenerative events.18,19 Recently, the effect exerted by the SIRT2 inhibitor AGK2 in neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration was explored.20 Moreover, this SIRT2 inhibitor was assessed in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated HAPI microglial cells and inflammation-conditioned SH-SY5Y neuronal cells, showing neuroprotection and inhibiting LPS-stimulated production of TNF-α and PGE2.20

SIRT2 expression is down- or up-regulated in different malignancies, making SIRT2 modulators interesting in the search for anticancer agents. Indeed, SIRT2 was reported to promote tumorigenesis by supporting cancer cell proliferation and survival.9 For example, in glioblastoma, SIRT2 deacetylates p73, a tumor suppressor, thereby inactivating its transcriptional activity and fostering tumor growth.9,21 Increasing evidence suggests that SIRT2 plays a key role in the response of melanoma cells to chemotherapeutics: cells treated with SIRT2Is revealed increased sensitivity to cisplatin and exhibited increased accumulation of γ-H2AX and reduced EGFR-AKT-RAF-ERK1/2 (epidermal growth factor receptor-protein B kinase-RAF kinase-extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2) pathway signaling, compared to control cells.22 Recently, AGK2, via SIRT2 inhibition, proved to increase ATP citrate lyase (ACLY) acetylation level, this in turn reducing proliferation and migration of oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) cells. Conversely, overexpression of ACLY partially reversed the inhibitory effect of AGK2 on ESCC cells.23

Currently, several human SIRT2 crystal structures are available,24 including SIRT2 apo-conformations or SIRT2-inhibitor complexes such as isoform-selective ligands. In particular, most of the reported inhibitors were SirReal2 highly related analogues.25–28 SirReal2 occupies the binding pocket at the interface between the Rossmann fold and the zinc binding domain (PDB code = 4RMG)29 featuring non-polar interactions with Ile118, Leu138, Tyr139, Pro140, Ile93, Ile232, Ile169, and π–π stacking with Phe96, Phe119 Phe143, Phe190 and Phe234 (Fig. 1A).

In addition, thienopyrimidinone-derivatives have been reported as tricyclic-based inhibitors.30

The thienopyrimidinone compound (TP) establishes comparable hydrophobic and π–π stacking contact with the biological target (Fig. 1A and B). Both SirReal2 and TP were able to occupy the so-called selectivity pocket, including Phe131, Tyr139 and Phe143.24 The selective behavior of these two compounds was explained with the unique features exhibited by the so-called selectivity pocket in SIRT2, with respect to SIRT1 and SIRT3.31 The superimposition of the different SIRT2 X-ray crystallographic data is shown in Fig. 1C and D. In particular, Fig. 1C represents the overall superimposition of different experimental data including SirReal2-like inhibitors, while Fig. 1D highlights a markedly different conformation of SIRT2 in the complexes with various non-SirReal2 SIRT2Is (PDB: 5MAR, 5Y5N, 5Y0Z, 5D7P, 5D7Q, and 5MAT), when superposed onto SIRT2 in the presence of SirReal2 (PDB code = 4 RMG) (Fig. 1D, in blue). The conformational landscape of SIRT2 when bound to non-SirReal inhibitors highlighted a certain variability. 5Y5N PDB ID exhibits a flexible ligand differing from the 5D7P and 5D7Q tricyclic ligands. The presence of the bulky ligand of 5Y0Z induces a different SIRT2 conformation, as well as the presence of small ligands, such as in the case of 5MAR.32

This piece of information allowed us to develop computational studies guiding the discovery of thiazole-containing derivatives exhibiting SIRT2 inhibitory ability,33,34 as well as of pyrazole pyrimidine compounds, endowed with selectivity for SIRT2 over SIRT6.25

In particular, we applied structure-based studies to identify pyrazolo-pyrimidine compounds (1–5, Fig. S1) as new promising SIRT2Is.25 For most of them, key contacts with Phe235 and His187 were predicted via π–π stacking and cation–π interactions, being in silico also H-bonded to Phe96.25 As a result, 1–5 mimicked the docking positioning of the aforementioned reference SIRT2Is, with 1 (SIRT2 % inhibition = 81.2%, at 150 μM) being the most effective SIRT2I of the series. All the compounds showed selectivity towards SIRT6, but not towards SIRT1 and SIRT3.25 Compound 1 inhibition on the deacetylase activity catalyzed by SIRT1 and SIRT3 was 76 and 33%.25 Thus, our previous results encouraged deeper studies to guide the identification of further pyrazole pyrimidine-based compounds as ameliorated SIRT1/2 inhibitors.

Herein, ligand-based studies and molecular docking calculations have been performed to in silico screen a small library of further pyrazolo-pyrimidines (6–16), previously reported as anticancer agents,35–41 as putative SIRT2Is. Notably, the development of dual-targeting compounds in cancer has been widely reported to be a promising therapeutic strategy for improving drug efficiency or overcoming resistance mechanism.42 The compounds have been analysed in silico to investigate the role played by flexible moieties in place of the previous hits 1–5 styrene group as well as the introduction of substituents at the pyrazolo-pyrimidine position 6.

Compounds 6–16 had shown inhibition activity towards Src family kinases, and, moreover, selected derivatives, bearing a methylthio group at the C6 position of the pyrazolo[3,4-d]pyrimidine scaffold, displayed a promising anticancer activity in SH-SY5Y (neuroblastoma), A549 (lung adenocarcinoma), 8701-BC (breast cancer), CaCo2 (colon cancer) cells.36,41,43–45 For many of them, parallel artificial membrane permeability assay (PAMPA) analyses have been conducted, suggesting good permeability properties. The applied computational methods allowed to highlight their binding ability towards SIRT2: the effectiveness of the proposed compounds 6–16 as SIRT2Is was ascertained by subsequent in vitro evaluation of the enzymatic activity using the recombinant enzyme and cancer cells.

Results and discussion

In silico screening of pyrazolo-pyrimidines as SIRT2Is

Several structure-based campaigns have been performed in the search for novel SIRT1/2Is, via computational methods.46 Relying on X-ray crystallographic information, we reported the discovery of pyrazolo-pyrimidine-based compounds featuring SIRT1/2 inhibitory ability (1–5; Fig. S1).25

As we previously reported, SIRT2I pharmacophore features included two (hetero)aromatic terminal groups, with at least one exhibiting H-bonding moieties (Fig. S1). Among compounds 1–5, the presence of two terminal aromatic features (1, 2) or of flexible H-bonding groups (3) proved to be beneficial (Fig. 2A). Indeed, the 1–2 phenylalkyl amine group was engaged in π–π stacking and cation–π contacts with Phe235 and His187, being also H-bonded to Val233, via water-bridge (Fig. 2B).

A comparable docking mode was also experienced by analogue 3, taken as representative of those compounds bearing an alkyl-containing terminal group in place of an aromatic ring. This structure maintained the aforementioned key contacts with His187, Val233, and Phe96.

Herein, we screened in silico a further small series of compounds (6–16; Fig. 2C), as analogues of the previously reported 1–6, modified at the main heterocyclic core positions 1, 4, and 6, with respect to the pyrazolo-pyrimidine hit 1. Derivatives 6–16 have been described in the literature as c-Src inhibitors and the protocol for their synthesis was reported in ref. 35–41. The compounds have been selected for preliminary computational evaluation in order to explore the role played by: (i) the introduction of flexible aromatic pendants at the heterocyclic ring position 1, in place of the styrene portion of the hit compound 1, to optimize hydrophobic interactions with Phe96, Phe131, Leu137; (ii) structural and chain length variations at the main core position 4, to achieve contacts with the surrounding residues His187, Val233, Phe235; (iii) the presence of substituents at the pyrazolo-pyrimidine position 6. The last one is expected to guide the compound positioning at the enzyme cavity, in proximity of the residue Ile169. Based on the above, compound 6, featuring only one of the proposed chemical variations in N1, was the most similar compound to 1, compared to other congeners 7–16. Compounds 7 and 8 displayed a halogen atom on the para position of the N1 side chain, analogously to 6, and, in addition, a chlorinated benzylamine substituent in C4; whereas 9–16 experienced all three aforementioned structural variations, including a lipophilic group at the main ring position 6. While compounds 6–14 have been previously designed, synthesized and evaluated as racemic mixture,35–4115–16 were proposed and synthesized as bearing a phenylethyl chain in N1, to avoid chiral centers and maintain the required hydrophobic properties.36 To preliminary evaluate via computational methods compounds 6–16 as putative SIRT2Is, a combined ligand- and structure-based strategy has been applied. Initially, molecular interaction fields (MIFs) analyses have been performed for the proposed candidates 6–16, as we previously described,34,47 in comparison to those of the hit compound 1 (model A), of the 5MAT thienopyrimidinone compound TP (model B) and of SirReal2 (model C), taken as reference SIRT2Is. This approach is intended to compare the electrostatic and hydrophobic properties of the candidate molecules, as well as their shape and volume similarity, with those of a reference compound, taken as representative of the desired bioactive ligand. The analysis relies on the evaluation of favorable/unfavorable contacts with a series of probes simulating moieties prone to hydrophobic or polar interactions.

The following series of MIF probes have been taken into account: (i) H probe to evaluate shape similarity, (ii) DRY probe to assess the role played by hydrophobic features, (iii) N1 MIF is related to favorable interaction with the H-bond donor (HBD) probe, (iv) O MIF is related to favorable interaction with the H-bond acceptor (HBA) probe. For each analysis, the probe score represented the overlap degree of the candidate compound MIFs when compared to that of the reference compound, for each probe individually. The product of the individual probe scores turns in the Glob_Prod global score, ranging from 0 (bad) to 1 (good) (details in Materials and methods).

To compare the results obtained with the three models, the Glob_Prod score, DRY and the H ones have been considered for compound candidate ranking.

While the Glob-Prod score displayed an overall perspective of the candidate-template similarity, the DRY and the H probes allowed to compare the derivatives based on shape and hydrophobicity properties, being the main structural variations featured by 6–16 if compared to 1. Based on the chemical similarity, ligand-based analysis focused on compound 1 as template (model A), gave the higher Glob_Prod values for 6–16 (Glob_Prod = 0.390–0.553; mean value = 0.485), when compared to those derived from models B (Glob_Prod = 0.315–0.384; mean value = 0347) and C (Glob_Prod = 0.278–0.405 mean value = 0.338), which are referred to the SIRT2Is references thienopyrimidinone compound and SirReal2.

Then, the 6–16 DRY scores (model A, DRY = 0.352–0.436; model B, DRY = 0.310–0.374; model C, DRY = 0.260–0.383) and H probe scores (model A, H = 0.547–0.651; model B, H = 0.481–0.576; model C, H = 0.509–0.628) followed quite the same trend comparing model A (DRY mean value = 0.398, H mean value = 0.586) with model B (DRY mean value = 0.341, H mean value = 0.527) and C (DRY mean value = 0.327, H mean value = 0.557), respectively. As shown in Table 1, in model A compounds 10, 11, 15 and 16 (Glob_prod score = 0.474–0.546, DRY score = 0.410–0.436, H score = 0.589–0.651) were often top-ranked for each probe (model A; Glob_prod mean value = 0.485, DRY mean value = 0.398, H mean value = 0.586), being predicted as putative SIRT2Is, whereas compound 8 was reported as inactive/poorly active for all the descriptors. Compound 9 (Glob_prod score = 0.472–0.476, DRY score = 0.380–0.413, H score = 0.576–0.601) stably occupied the middle of the ranking, being predicted as endowed with mild SIRT2 inhibiting activity. Based on the above, a perspective of MIFs comparison between 10 (R), 11 (R), 15, 16 and the template 1 is given in Fig. 3. Favorable interactions with N1 MIF, O MIF, and DRY MIF are reported in blue, red, and yellow, respectively. This comparison highlighted favored H-bond acceptor, H-bond donor, and lipophilic or aromatic groups in the corresponding areas of the compound. Compounds 11, 15 and 16 have been predicted as properly matching most of the reference 1 DRY MIFs, as most of the yellow solid and wireframe areas were superposed (Fig. 3B–D), thanks to the common positioning of the N1 substituent. On the contrary, compound 10 (Fig. 3A) moved the phenyl-ethyl substituted group on the opposite side, impairing hydrophobic similarity with the prototype 1.

Based on the above, molecular docking calculations were performed to assess in silico the putative SIRT2 binding ability of 6–16, prior to biological evaluation. Details are reported as follows.

Molecular docking studies of pyrazolo-pyrimidines 6–16

Molecular docking studies of compounds 6–16 have been performed, relying on the SIRT2 X-ray crystallographic structure in the presence of the thienopyrimidinone inhibitor TP (PDB code = 5MAT).30 The 5MAT co-crystallized ligand, as well as a series of analogues, have been previously re-docked in the crystal binding site33 to assess the reliability of the calculation. The obtained values revealed that most of the thienopyrimidinone analogues best-ranked conformers featured S values (predicted ΔG value of each protein–ligand complex) spanning from approximately −100 000 kcal mol−1, to −130 000 kcal mol−1. Details of the applied molecular docking approach are reported in the experimental section.

As regards compounds 6–16, all the chiral compounds were evaluated in terms of R and S enantiomers. The obtained results revealed comparable positioning for most of them, also supporting the previously derived information obtained by the applied ligand-based analyses: the simultaneous presence of substituents at the position C6 of the pyrazolo-pyrimidine ring and on the aromatic pendant at N1, turned in favoured S enantiomer rather than R, otherwise the R one is preferred. In particular, the final docking S scoring function values are shown in Table S1.

Based on the PDB code 5MAT, the re-docking pose of the co-crystallized thienopyrimidinone derivative TP (SIRT2 IC50 = 0.58 μM)33 was in agreement with the experimental one (Fig. S2). The inhibitor featured π–π stacking between the aromatic naphthyl group and the Tyr139, Phe143, Phe190, and Phe214 residues, also displaying van der Waals contacts with Pro140, Leu206, and Leu213 (Fig. 6). The terminal oxazole ring was oriented in proximity of Phe219 and Phe235, while the main tricyclic scaffold was stabilized at the protein binding site via hydrophobic contacts with Ile93, Leu138, Leu134, Leu169, and Ile232.

According to the developed structure-based studies, when a chiral atom bearing a methyl group or a halogen atom is included in the N1 pendant, two docking positioning are plausible, moving the two terminal aromatic groups on the protein cavity at the opposite sides, alternatively.

On the other hand, the main bicyclic ring is partially able to mimic the thienopyrimidinone core of the reference compound. The presence of the unsubstituted phenyl ring in N1 (compounds 15, 16) allowed to better place the ligand in the protein cavity, despite of any substituent onto the aromatic group at the pyrazole pyrimidine position 4. We subsequently determined the IC50 values for the most promising compounds when added at 150 μM: four compounds were found to inhibit SIRT2 activity in the very low micromolar range, with 11 and 15 having the lowest IC50 (Table 4). These compounds exhibited a certain degree of selectivity for SIRT2 over SIRT1: when added at 100 μM, SIRT1 activity was inhibited by approximately 50% by these five compounds (Table 4). Given that compounds 11, 12, 15 and 16 exhibited the lowest IC50 for SIRT2, their ability to cross the plasma membrane and inhibit endogenous SIRT2 activity was tested on A549 cells (adenocarcinomic human alveolar basal epithelial cells). In addition, compound 8 was tested, as a negative control of compound not inhibiting SIRT2 in our in vitro test using the recombinant protein (Table 4). Compound 8 did not alter α-tubulin acetylation, in line with not inhibiting SIRT2 in vitro. Instead, compounds 11, 12, 15 and 16 effectively increased α-tubulin acetylation levels, confirming inhibition of the endogenous SIRT2.

Next, the cytotoxic effect of compounds 8, 11, 12, 15 and 16 was tested using A549 cells. These compounds exhibited a similar Ki towards Src family kinases (ranging from 0.5 to 1.4 μM (ref. 35, 36, 38 and 44). Treatment of A549 cells with increasing concentrations of the compounds determined a concentration-dependent cell death, as measured after 48 h (Fig. 8): the IC50 was calculated as 17.6, 32.4; 20.3, 25.5 μM, for compound 11, 12, 15 and 16, respectively. Compound 8, a Src family kinase inhibitor not active on SIRT2, showed cell toxicity at concentrations higher than 20 μM (Fig. 8). Thus, the cytotoxic effect exerted by the dual inhibitors (acting on both Src kinases and SIRT2), in this cell line is likely due to their effect on SIRT2, at concentrations up to than 20 μM. Notably, the IC50 values for cell cytotoxicity obtained in this study (approximately 20–30 μM) are of the same order of magnitude as previously reported data for compound 12 in other cell lines, where IC50 values of approximately 38–40 μM were observed.39,44 The SIRT2 inhibiting activity likely contributed to the previously reported overall cytotoxicity exerted by these compounds.

Probing in silico SIRT2/1 selectivity of pyrazolo-pyrimidine-based inhibitors

Based on the above, we deemed interesting probing in silico the putative 10, 11, 12, 15, 16 SIRT2 over SIRT1 selective inhibitor ability. Since the previous hit compound 1 (SIRT2 % inhibition = 81%; SIRT1 % inhibition = 76%, at 150 mM) was reported as a dual SIRT1/2 inhibitor, it has been taken as control compound. Molecular docking studies of the afore mentioned 10, 11, 12, 15, 16 have been performed at the human SIRT1 crystallographic data (PDB code = 4I5I).48 The derived scoring functions have been listed in Table S2.

The exploited SIRT1 structural information includes an indole-based inhibitor as EX527 analogue. EX527 is a nanomolar SIRT1 inhibitor and a micromolar SIRT2 inhibitor. The reference SIRT1 inhibitor was re-docked within the SIRT1 crevice in order to assess the docking protocol reliability. As shown in Fig. 9A, the docking pose of the EX527 analogue (EX527 analogue C atom, light yellow) was in agreement with the crystallographic data of the PDB code 4I5I (EX527 analogue C atom, green). The crystallized ligand was stabilized at the enzyme crevice via water-bridges between the compound amine group and Asn346, while the amide group was H-bonded to Ile347. In addition, the tricyclic core was involved in hydrophobic contacts with Ile411, Ile316, Ile279, Phe273, whereas the inhibitor aromatic group was involved in π–π stacking with His363 and Phe297.

Notably, the previous hit 1 exhibited dual SIRT1/2 inhibitory ability, featuring a benzylamine substituent at the main core position 4, a rigid and planar group in N1, being C6 unsubstituted. According to our molecular docking calculations, the benzylamine substituent of the inhibitor fulfilled the hydrophobic requirements of the co-crystallized ligand, mimicking the indole portion of the EX527 analogue. In particular, 1 featured hydrophobic and π–π stacking contacts with Ile347, Ile411 and Phe297, Phe413 (Fig. 9B). Notably, the amine group was properly superposed on the 4I5I ligand basic core. This kind of positioning allowed 1 to interact via water-bridges with Asn346. The pyrimidine ring was in proximity of the reference compound amide group, while the styrene group occupies a narrow cavity delimited by Pro271, Arg282, Ile316, Phe321. This kind of positioning is thought to be favoured by the choice of a rigid and planar moiety tethered to the pyrazolo-pyrimidine position 1, such as the styrene group. Indeed, this group obliged the ligand in the aforementioned docking mode.

Conversely, the introduction of a flexible phenylethyl amine group in N1 and the choice of a substituent in C6, led to a reversed docking positioning, as displayed by 15, 16. As shown in Fig. 10, the aniline portion was oriented towards Ile316 and not towards Asn346, being the phenylethyl group engaged in π–π stacking with Phe273, His363, Phe413.

As a result, the two SIRT2 inhibitors 15 and 16 were not properly stabilized at the SIRT1 binding cavity, exhibiting a selective SIRT2 inhibitory ability, while the previous hits 1–5 featured a dual SIRT1/2 binding behaviour. Similarly, compound 11, 12 exhibiting a chlorine atom on the phenylethyl chain maintaining a flexible ability, and a bulky substituent in C6, featured the aforementioned reversed docking positioning.

Conclusions

Sirtuin 2 (SIRT2) is a ubiquitously expressed cellular enzyme that deacylates protein lysine residues using NAD+ as a cofactor. Deacetylation by SIRTs induces epigenetic changes and regulates transcription and DNA repair. SIRT2-mediated post-translational modifications on a plethora of protein targets result in a wide-ranging SIRT2 regulatory role, in many physiological and pathological processes. Crucially, SIRTs have emerged as promising therapeutic targets in cancer therapy.

More than 39 SIRT2 crystal structures in complex with substrates, products, mimetics of substrates and products, and modulators have been reported. However, only a few SIRT2Is exhibit sub-micromolar IC50 values,49–51 making the structural optimization of variable chemo-types desirable. Computational techniques were extensively employed for the discovery of novel SIRT2 directed chemotypes24,46 paving the way for the following hit-to-lead optimization step.

Recently, we applied structure-based studies to identify five pyrazolo-pyrimidine compounds (1–5) as SIRT2Is. Among them, compound 1 (SIRT2 % inhibition = 81.2%, at 150 μM) was the most effective. In this work, combined ligand-based and molecular docking studies enabled us to screen in silico a small library of further pyrazolo-pyrimidines (6–16), originally reported as anticancer agents and endowed with different substituents at the main core positions 1, 4, 6, with respect to the previous hits 1–5. Indeed, the previously exploited styrene moiety has been modified in different flexible substituents, while the aromatic group in position 4 included different aliphatic spacers. The introduction of small hydrophobic groups at the pyrazolo-pyrimidine position 6 was considered, with the aim at gaining more contacts with the surrounding enzyme pocket. The previously described MIF analysis, as well as the structure-based studies, prompted biological evaluation of all compounds, with 9, 11, 15, and 16 predicted as the most interesting. Following enzymatic assays confirmed the applied computational studies, revealing the compounds 6–16 SIRT2 inhibitor ability. In particular, three compounds were found to inhibit SIRT2 activity in the very low micromolar range, with 11 (SIRT2 IC50 = 0.305 μM) and 15 (SIRT2 IC50 = 0.094 μM), displaying the lowest IC50 values.

Western blot analyses showed increased α-tubulin acetylation in A549 cells when treated with 20 μM 11, 15, or 16 for 36 h, while treatment of A549 cells with increasing concentrations of the compounds revealed a concentration-dependent toxicity. The final results of our studies pointed out the effectiveness of the pyrazolo-pyrimidine scaffold for the further design of novel and selective SIRT2Is, supporting compounds 11 and 15 for future development. Furthermore, the compound dual ability to inhibit SIRT2 and kinases offers the opportunity to optimize dual-targeting compounds as effective agents for the treatment of cancer.

Materials and methods

Molecular modelling studies

All the studied compounds were built in silico by the MOE Builder tool included in the MOE2019.01 software.52 The prevalent protonation state at pH = 7.4 was fixed by means of the wash tool. Ligands have been parametrized with the AM1 partial charges calculation method. Energy minimization step has been performed by the energy minimize tool using MMFF94x forcefield, included in MOE. RMS (root mean square) gradient was equal to 0.0001, being the root mean square gradient the norm of the gradient times the square root of the number of (unfixed) atoms.

The ligand-based studies were performed with FLAP2.2.1 Ligand-Based module,53,54 referring to the previously derived docking pose of 1 (model A)25 and to the X-crystallographic structure of a thienopyrimidinone derivative (PDB code = 5MAT)30 (model B) and of SirReal2 (PDB code = 4RMG)29 (model C), as SIRT2Is templates.

For each of the candidates, 25 conformers were generated starting from their optimized geometry following the procedure previously described.47 Four probes were chosen for models A–C, namely the H probe (explaining for shape similarity properties), DRY probe (hydrophobic properties), O (HBA probe, corresponding to a carbonyl-group), and N1 (HBD probe, corresponding to amide NH). The probe scores represent the degree of overlap of the MIFs of the evaluated molecule with respect to the template for each probe, individually.

MIF information was exploited to visually compare compounds 6–16 and each template. Indeed, the superposition of the same kind of MIF between the screened compound and the corresponding template turns in ligand similarity, supporting for a similar biological ability.53

All the molecular docking simulations at the 5MAT and 4I5I proteins have been performed by means of the DOCK tool included in MOE software, all those residues placed at 4.5 Å distance from the PDB code co-crystallized inhibitor have been considered. The Alpha Triangle method and Affinity ΔG prediction as final scoring function have been applied, to obtain from 50 to 10 best scored conformers.33,55

Enzymatic assays

The synthesis of acetylated peptide H3K9 (H3K9Ac) and the evaluation of SIRT2 and SIRT1 deacetylase activity were performed as described in ref. 25. The recombinant SIRT1 was produced in house: SIRT1.1 was a gift from Prof. John Denu (Addgene plasmid #13735; http://n2t.net/addgene:13735; RRID: Addgene_13735; ref. 56).

Cell culture, Western blotting and cell viability

A549 cell line was a kind gift from Prof. Alessio Nencioni (University of Genoa), and cells were maintained in Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium supplied with 50 IU mL−1 penicillin, 50 μg mL−1 streptomycin and 10% fetal bovine serum. Cells were treated with the putative SIRT2Is at a final concentration of 20 μM or the respective amount of DMSO vehicle. After 36 h, cells were lysed, and the level of acetylated α-tubulin was quantified by Western blot analysis, as described in ref. 34. Alternatively, A549 cells were treated for 48 h with the putative SIRT2Is, and cell viability was analyzed by a sulforhodamine B assay. Briefly, 2000 cells per well were seeded in 96-well plates in triplicate and allowed to adhere overnight. The day after, the medium was replaced with fresh medium containing increasing concentrations of compounds 11, 15 or 16. After 48 h, TCA was added, and samples were processed as previously described.57
