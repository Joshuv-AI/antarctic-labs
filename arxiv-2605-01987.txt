Misclassification Rate and Privacy-Utility Trade-offs in Graph Convolutional Networks via Subsampling Stability
Yexin Zhang, Zhongtian Ma, Qiaosheng Zhang, Zhen Wang

Abstract
We study differential privacy (DP) in Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) through the framework of subsampling stability . We derive upper bounds on the misclassification rate that depend explicitly on the subsampling probability p s p_{s} . Furthermore, we characterize the privacy–utility trade-off by identifying feasible ranges of p s p_{s} ; if p s p_{s} is too large, the stability-based privacy condition becomes difficult to satisfy, yielding vacuous guarantees, whereas if it is too small, accuracy deteriorates. Our results provide the first rigorous theoretical framework for understanding subsampling stability in GCNs under DP.

Introduction

Graph signal processing has become instrumental in many domains, such as network science, recommendation systems, and cybersecurity
[
16
,
5
]
. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), and in particular Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), have emerged as powerful tools for learning from such data, leveraging iterative aggregation of neighborhood information to construct hierarchical node representations
[
8
]
. By combining graph filters with pointwise nonlinearities, GCNs effectively capture multi-hop dependencies through localized spectral convolutions, achieving strong performance in tasks such as node classification
[
18
]
and community detection.

However, the growing use of GCNs raises significant privacy concerns, as graph data often contains sensitive interactions between entities
[
7
,
6
,
13
]
. Differential privacy (DP) provides a rigorous framework that ensures an algorithm’s output remains nearly unchanged when any single data point is modified
[
4
]
.
DP has become a popular research direction for privacy protection in graph data, particularly in the context of GCNs
[
14
]
. Existing studies span several directions, including perturbing node features or edges to protect sensitive inputs
[
3
]
, applying DP-SGD style training to safeguard gradients during optimization, and developing methods for private graph data publishing and querying
[
1
,
2
,
15
]
.

Despite these advances, theoretical investigations of DP in the context of GCNs remain scarce. A rigorous theoretical foundation, however, is essential for understanding the fundamental limits of privacy preservation and guiding principled algorithm design
[
12
]
. Such theory has been extensively studied in related settings, including spectral clustering
[
9
]
and semi-definite programming (SDP)
[
17
]
. In this work, we aim to provide such a perspective by analyzing a well-trained GCN under the incorporation of DP guarantees through a
subsampling stability mechanism

[
20
,
19
]
. Our focus is to rigorously characterize the resulting
privacy–utility trade-offs
and to establish misclassification bounds that explicitly depend on the subsampling parameter.

Our contributions are three-fold:

1.

Subsampling stability for DP in GCNs.
To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to introduce and analyze a subsampling-stability mechanism to provide differential privacy guarantees for GCNs;

2.

Misclassification rate under subsampling.
We derive an explicit upper bound on the misclassification rate under this mechanism, where the bound depends directly on the subsampling probability;

3.

Characterization of privacy–utility trade-offs.
We establish precise analytical expressions that quantify the resulting privacy–utility trade-off by deriving feasible ranges of the subsampling probability
p_{s}
, showing in particular that
p_{s}
cannot be too small or too large to simultaneously guarantee privacy and utility.

2
Preliminaries and problem setup

2.1
Graph Convolutional Networks

Given an input graph
G=(V,E)
with
n=|V|
nodes, the adjacency matrix of the graph is denoted by
\mathbf{A}\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}
. We denote the degree matrix
\mathbf{D}=\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
with
d_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}
, and the (combinatorial) graph Laplacian as
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{A}
. When only first-order neighborhood information is retained (i.e.,
k=1
), a single-layer GCN can be expressed as,

\mathbf{y}=\sigma\left(h_{0}\mathbf{I}+h_{1}\mathbf{L}\right)\mathbf{x},

(1)

where
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the input node feature vector,
\mathbf{I}
is the identity matrix,
h_{0}
,
h_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
are filter coefficients, and
\sigma(\cdot)
is the nonlinear activation function.

2.2
Differential privacy

To protect structural privacy in graph-valued data, we adopt the framework of DP under edge-level perturbations. The core idea, rooted in the DP principle of indistinguishability between neighboring datasets, is to ensure that the distribution of the estimator’s output remains stable under small structural modifications of the input graph.

In the context of graph learning, we use the notion of
neighboring graphs
[
7
]
, which serves as the adjacency relation for DP in the graphs.

Definition 1

(Neighboring Graphs)
.

Two graphs
G=(V,E)
and
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
are said to be
neighboring
, denoted by
G\sim G^{\prime}
, if they share the same vertex set (
V=V^{\prime}
) and their edge sets differ in exactly one edge. That is,

|E\mathbin{\triangle}E^{\prime}|=1,

where
\triangle
denotes the symmetric difference.

Using this adjacency relation, we define
(\epsilon,\delta)
-edge differential privacy following
[
4
]
(see also
[
12
]
), which bounds the distinguishability between the output distributions on neighboring graphs.

Definition 2

((
\epsilon,\delta
)-Edge Differential Privacy)
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. A randomized estimator
\hat{\mathbf{y}}
satisfies
(\epsilon,\delta)
-edge DP
if for every pair of neighboring graphs
G\sim G^{\prime}
and every measurable set of outputs
\Phi\subseteq\mathrm{Range}(\hat{\mathbf{y}})
,

\displaystyle\Pr\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}(G)\in\Phi\big)\leq e^{\epsilon}\cdot\Pr\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}(G^{\prime})\in\Phi\big)+\delta,

(2)

where the probability is taken over the internal randomness of
\hat{\mathbf{y}}
. When
\delta=0
, the mechanism satisfies
pure
\epsilon
-edge DP
; otherwise, it satisfies
approximate
(\epsilon,\delta)
-edge DP
.

2.3
Node classification

Node classification is among the most common tasks for GCNs, with the goal of inferring unknown node labels from the observed graph topology and associated node features
[
10
]
. Concretely, given a graph represented by an adjacency matrix
\mathbf{A}
, ground-truth labels
\mathbf{y}\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
and a node feature vector
\mathbf{x}
, an algorithm
\hat{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{x})
produces a predicted label vector
\hat{\mathbf{y}}
. We measure the prediction error by using
misclassification rate
with the Hamming distance, defined as

R(\mathbf{y},\hat{\mathbf{y}}):=\frac{\mathrm{Ham}(\mathbf{y},\hat{\mathbf{y}})}{n}
and

\mathrm{Ham}(\mathbf{y},\hat{\mathbf{y}})=\left|\left\{i\in[n]:y_{i}\neq\hat{y}_{i}\right\}\right|
.

3
Subsampling stability Mechanism

Given an input graph
G=(V,E)
with
n=|V|
nodes, we generate
m
independent subgraphs
\{G_{1},\dots,G_{m}\}
via edge-level subsampling, where each edge is retained independently with probability
p_{s}\in(0,1]
. For each subsampled graph
G_{\ell}
, a base GCN classifier produces a binary node labeling
\hat{\mathbf{y}}(G_{\ell})\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
. The stability of this classifier under subsampling is critical to the overall mechanism. We aggregate the
m
label vectors
\{\hat{y}(G_{l})\}_{l=1}^{m}
via majority voting, denoted by
g(\cdot)
, to produce a consensus estimate
\bar{\mathbf{y}}
. To evaluate whether this estimate is sufficiently stable for release under differential privacy, we compute a
stability score
of the results of majority voting:

\hat{d}=\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{4m\cdot p_{s}}-1,

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
represent the frequencies of the first and second most common label vectors among the
m
outputs.

To achieve
(\epsilon,\delta)
-edge DP, we adopt the
Propose-Test-Release
(PTR) framework
[
4
]
. We perturb
\hat{d}
with Laplace noise of scale
1/\epsilon
, yielding
\tilde{d}=\hat{d}+\mathrm{Lap}(0,1/\epsilon)
. If
\tilde{d}>\log(1/\delta)/\epsilon
, we get the final output
\bar{\mathbf{y}}
; otherwise, the algorithm returns
\perp
, indicating insufficient stability. The parameters
p_{s}
and
m
are chosen based on the derived upper bound of the misclassification rate and the requirements of the DP mechanism, aiming to achieve an optimal trade-off between privacy and utility.

Theoretical Results

We first derive upper bounds on the misclassification rate for both a single subsampled graph and the full
A_{\text{samp}}
GCN (Theorem
3

4
). We then provide a quantitative characterization of the privacy–utility trade-off exhibited by

Theorem 3

(Misclassification Rate of GCN on a Single Subsampled Graph)
.

Let
\widehat{G}\sim\mathcal{D}_{s}(G)
be obtained by independently retaining each edge with probability
p_{s}\in(0,1]
.
Consider the one-layer GCN in (
1
) with an elementwise activation function
\sigma(\cdot)
that is
C_{\sigma}
-Lipschitz in
\ell_{2}
norm.
Let
f(G):=\sigma\!\big((h_{0}\mathbf{I}+h_{1}\mathbf{L})\mathbf{x}\big)
, and define

\mathbf{y}:=\mathbf{y}(G)\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
and
\hat{\mathbf{y}}:=\mathbf{y}(\widehat{G})\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}

by thresholding
f(\cdot)
at level
\tau
. And define the minimum margin
\gamma_{\min}:=\min_{i\in[n]}|f_{i}(G)-\tau|
, assuming
\gamma_{\min}>0
.
Assume that
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}=1
(
L_{2}
-normalization). Then, for every
\eta\in(0,1)
, the following inequality holds with probability at least
1-\eta
:

\displaystyle R(\mathbf{y},\hat{\mathbf{y}})\leq f(p_{s},\eta),

(3)

\displaystyle\text{where}\,f(p_{s},\eta):=\frac{C_{\sigma}\,|h_{1}|}{\sqrt{n}\,\,\,\gamma_{\min}}\,\Big\{(1-p_{s})\,\|\mathbf{L}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sqrt{\,4\,p_{s}(1-p_{s})\,\|\mathbf{L}\|_{2}\,\log(2n/\eta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\tfrac{4}{3}\log(2n/\eta)\Big\}.

(4)

Theorem 4

(Misclassification Rate for Subsampling Stability Mechanism)
.

Consider the subsampling stability-based mechanism with
(\epsilon,\delta)
-edge DP. Let
\mathbf{y},\bar{\mathbf{y}}
denote the original results and the estimated labels obtained via mechanism over
m
subgraphs
\{G_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. Assume that
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}=1
, then the misclassification rate satisfies:

R(\mathbf{y},\bar{\mathbf{y}})\leq\frac{f(p_{s},\eta)}{4}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\right),

with probability at least
1-\eta
, where
f(p_{s},\eta)
is the misclassification contribution from the GCN for a single subsampled graph
G_{\ell}
.

Remark 1
.

The proof of Theorem

follows from combining the misclassification bound for a single graph with the majority voting scheme and Hoeffding’s inequality; see Lemma III.6 in

]
for details.

Theorem 5

(Privacy-Utility Trade-off)
.

The following guarantees hold for
A_{\text{samp}}GCN
if the subsampling probability
p_{s}\in(p_{s}^{*},\,\frac{\epsilon}{32\ln(1/\sigma))})
:

•
Privacy:
The
A_{\text{samp}}GCN
method is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-edge differentially private.

•
Utility:

\Pr[\,A_{\text{samp}}GCN(G)=\mathbf{y}(G)\,]\geq 1-3\delta,

where
p_{s}^{*}
is the larger root of the following quadratic equation:

\displaystyle(1-p_{s})\|\mathbf{L}\|_{2}+\sqrt{4\,\|\mathbf{L}\|_{2}\,\log(8n)\,p_{s}(1-p_{s})}

(5)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{4}{3}\log(8n)-\frac{\gamma_{\min}}{\sqrt{n}\,C_{\sigma}\,|h_{1}|}=0

Remark 2
.

The upper bound on the sampling rate
p_{s}
is imposed to ensure compatibility with the DP mechanism and is determined by the prescribed privacy parameters, with Theorem 7.3 of
[
4
]
showing that this bound indeed guarantees DP. The lower bound, derived from Theorem
3
(by setting
\eta=1/4
and requiring the misclassification rate to be less than
1/n
), guarantees that, for any single graph,
\Pr\{\mathrm{Ham}(\mathbf{y},\hat{\mathbf{y}})=0\}>3/4
. As shown in Theorem 7.3 of
[
4
]
, this probabilistic guarantee further implies a formal utility bound.

Intuitively, if
p_{s}
is too large, the stability condition becomes difficult to satisfy, yielding vacuous privacy–utility guarantees; conversely, if
p_{s}
is too small, it suppresses the extraction of informative signals, leading to significant degradation in accuracy. Thus,
p_{s}
inherently governs the fundamental privacy–utility trade-off.

We consider a one-layer GCN
f(G):=\sigma\!\big((h_{0}\mathbf{I}+h_{1}\mathbf{L})\,\mathbf{x}\big)

h_{0},h_{1}\in\mathbb{R}

\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\sigma

f(\widetilde{G}):=\sigma\!\big((h_{0}\mathbf{I}+h_{1}\widetilde{\mathbf{L}})\,\mathbf{x}\big)
.

The feature-level change is measured by

d_{f}(G,\widetilde{G}):=\left\lVert f(G)-f(\widetilde{G})\right\rVert_{2}.

For convenience, we write
H(\mathbf{L}):=(h_{0}\mathbf{I}+h_{1}\mathbf{L})\mathbf{x}
,
f(G)=\phi(\mathbf{L}):=\sigma\!\big(H(\mathbf{L})\big)
and
f(\widetilde{G})=\phi(\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}):=\sigma\!\big(H(\widetilde{\mathbf{L}})\big)
.

Let
\mathbf{y}(G),\mathbf{y}(\widetilde{G})\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
be estimated vectors obtained by thresholding at level
\tau
.
We measure the output distance between original graph and subsampled graph with the Hamming distance of labels

\text{Ham}\big(\mathbf{y}(G),\mathbf{y}(\widetilde{G})\big)=\Big|\big\{\,i\in[n]:y_{i}(G)\neq y_{i}(\widetilde{G})\,\big\}\Big|.

Lemma 6

(Flip-count via margins)
.

If
\sigma
is elementwise and
C_{\sigma}
-Lipschitz in
\ell_{2}
norm, then for any
\mathbf{x}
with
\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}=1
,

\mathrm{Ham}\!\big(\mathbf{y}(G),\mathbf{y}(\widetilde{G})\big)\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}\,C_{\sigma}\,\left|h_{1}\right|}{\gamma_{\min}}\,\left\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}-\mathbf{L}\right\rVert_{2}.

(6)

Proof.

If a flip occurs at node
i
, then

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{i}(\mathbf{L})-\tau\right|\leq\left|\phi_{i}(\mathbf{L})-\phi_{i}(\widetilde{\mathbf{L}})\right|.

(7)

Summing over flipped points and using the standard
\ell_{1}
–
\ell_{2}
inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ham}\!\big(\mathbf{y}(G),\mathbf{y}(\widetilde{G})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{\min}}\left\lVert\phi(\mathbf{L})-\phi(\widetilde{\mathbf{L}})\right\rVert_{1}

(8)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\gamma_{\min}}\left\lVert\phi(\mathbf{L})-\phi(\widetilde{\mathbf{L}})\right\rVert_{2}.

(9)

By Lipschitzness of
\sigma
, linearity of
H(\cdot)
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\phi(\mathbf{L})-\phi(\widetilde{\mathbf{L}})\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\sigma}\left\lVert H(\mathbf{L})-H(\widetilde{\mathbf{L}})\right\rVert_{2}

(10)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\sigma}\left|h_{1}\right|\,\left\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}-\mathbf{L}\right\rVert_{2}.

(11)

Substituting Eq.(

) into Eq.(
9
) completes the proof of Lemma

Then we turn to analyze
\big\|\,\Delta\mathbf{L}\big\|_{2}:=\|\widehat{\mathbf{L}}-\mathbf{L}\|
. Firstly, we decompose the original Laplacian of graphs
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{A}
. The adjacency matrix
\mathbf{A}
and degree matrix
\mathbf{D}
can be decomposed as follows:

\mathbf{A}=\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{A}_{ij}(\mathbf{e}_{i}\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\top}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}),

(12)

\mathbf{D}=\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{A}_{ij}(\mathbf{e}_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\top}),

(13)

then we obtain

\mathbf{L}=\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{A}_{ij}(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})^{\top},

where
\mathbf{e}_{i}
and
\mathbf{e}_{j}
are the standard basis vectors.

For each edge
(i,j)
, we define a Bernoulli random variable
\mathbf{B}_{ij}\sim\text{Bernoulli}(p_{s})
that determines edge retention. This process yields a perturbed adjacency matrix
\widehat{\mathbf{A}}
, where each entry
\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{ij}=\mathbf{A}_{ij}\cdot\mathbf{B}_{ij}
. The perturbed degree matrix
\widehat{\mathbf{D}}
is consequently defined as a diagonal matrix with
\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{ii}=\sum_{j}\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{ij}
. The perturbed adjacency matrix and perturbed degree matrix admits the same decomposition. The subsampled graph Laplacian is given as

\widehat{\mathbf{L}}=\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{A}_{ij}\mathbf{B}_{ij}(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})^{\top}.

Then we have

\Delta\mathbf{L}=\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{A}_{ij}(\mathbf{B}_{ij}-1)\,(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})^{\top}.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{ij}:=\mathbf{A}_{ij}(\mathbf{B}_{ij}-1)\,{\mathbf{u}}_{ij}{\mathbf{u}}_{ij}^{\top}
with
{\mathbf{u}}_{ij}:=\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
, and define centered terms as

\mathbf{Y}_{ij}:=\mathbf{X}_{ij}-\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{ij}]=\mathbf{A}_{ij}(\mathbf{B}_{ij}-p_{s})\,\mathbf{u}_{ij}\mathbf{u}_{ij}^{\top}.

(14)

Then

\Delta\mathbf{L}=\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{ij}]+\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{Y}_{ij}=(p_{s}-1)\mathbf{L}+\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{Y}_{ij}.

(15)

For
\mathbf{A}_{ij}\in\{0,1\}

is self-adjoint, mean-zero and satisfies:

\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}_{ij}\right\rVert_{2}\leq 2,\quad\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Y}_{ij}^{2}]=2p_{s}(1-p_{s})\,\mathbf{L},

(16)

hence
\sigma^{2}:=\big\|\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{Y}_{ij}^{2}]\big\|_{2}=2p_{s}(1-p_{s})\,\left\lVert\mathbf{L}\right\rVert_{2}

\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\left\|\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{Y}_{ij}\right\|_{2}\geq t\right\}\leq 2n\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}+\tfrac{2}{3}Rt}\right).

\left\|\sum\mathbf{Y}_{ij}\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}\log\frac{2n}{\eta}}+\frac{2}{3}R\log\frac{2n}{\eta}.

In addition, the bias term satisfies

\Big\|\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{ij}]\Big\|_{2}=\;|1-p_{s}|\,\|\mathbf{L}\|_{2}.

(19)

Therefore, by the triangle inequality, with probability at least

\displaystyle\|\Delta\mathbf{L}\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle|1-p_{s}|\,\|\mathbf{L}\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\,4\,p_{s}(1-p_{s})\,\|\mathbf{L}\|_{2}\,\log(2n/\eta)}

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{3}\,\log(2n/\eta).

(20)

Finally, under the margin condition

, with probability at least

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ham}\!\big(\mathbf{y}(G),\mathbf{y}(\widehat{G})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}\,C_{\sigma}\,|h_{1}|}{\gamma_{\min}}\,\Big\{|1-p_{s}|\,\|\mathbf{L}\|_{2}

Therefore, Theorem

6
Conclusion

In this work, we study differential privacy in GCNs through the framework of subsampling stability, and formalize a stability-based PTR mechanism (AsampGCN) built on edge-level subsampling and majority-vote aggregation for private node labeling. We derive misclassification-rate bounds that depend explicitly on the subsampling probability
p_{s}
, and characterize the privacy–utility trade-off via feasible ranges of
p_{s}
: if
p_{s}
is too large, the sufficient condition required by the stability-based DP mechanism may fail; whereas overly small
p_{s}
leads to accuracy degradation. Overall, our results provide a rigorous theoretical foundation for understanding subsampling stability in GCNs under DP and for guiding parameter selection in private graph learning.
