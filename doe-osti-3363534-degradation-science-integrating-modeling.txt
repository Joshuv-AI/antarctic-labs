Title of the award: Degradation science: Integrating modeling and experiments to predict
localized corrosion processes.

Investigators: Pranjali Khajanji, PhD candidate, University of California San Diego.
Abstract:

 Additively manufactured (AM) eutectic high-entropy alloys (EHEAs), such as nano-lamellar
AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁, have excellent strength, ductility, and wear resistance even at elevated temperatures, but
their corrosion behavior in aggressive acids at different length scales remain poorly understood. This work
investigates the corrosion behavior of laser powder bed–fused (L-PBF) AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ as a function of
annealing temperatures, probing degradation mechanisms from nanoscopic to macroscopic length scales.
The alloy is dual phase consisting of a ductile FCC L1₂ phase and a high-strength BCC B2 phase. Rapid
solidification during L-PBF produces a far-from-equilibrium nano-lamellar structure with nearly
homogeneous elemental distribution, which tends to evolve upon annealing toward Cr/Co/Fe-enriched FCC
and Al/Ni-enriched B2. Three conditions were studied: as-printed, 600 °C/5 h, and 1000 °C/1 h, over which
B2 lamellae coarsen, lamellar spacing increases, and elemental segregation becomes more prominent.

 Microstructure and chemistry were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and
energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS), while in-situ electrochemical atomic force microscopy (EC-AFM)
was used to link early (<5 h) local dissolution to microstructure after exposure in sulfuric acid. EC-AFM
highlights preferential dissolution of the BCC/B2 phase where the surrounding matrix is Cr-depleted and
directly quantifies the dissolution rates within individual phases, tracks the transition from early nano-scale
attack to partial repassivation, to correlate height differences with current and impedance responses. To
monitor longer-term behavior (up to 96 h), ex-situ AFM, SEM, and confocal imaging were combined with
conventional bulk electrochemical tests, bridging nanoscale observations to micro/meso-scale damage
morphologies. At the meso-scale, the deepest dissolution channels align with the build-direction lamellae
and melt-pool boundaries, indicating that printing directionality guides the propagation of these localized
corrosion sites.

 Annealing modifies corrosion by restructuring BCC/FCC phase fractions, lamellar spacing, and
Cr/Al segregation, thereby changing the cathode/anode ratio and passive film stability. The results clarify
how as-printed and annealed nano-lamellar architectures differ in their susceptibility to selective
dissolution; how elemental segregation competes with residual stresses along the build direction. With these
insights, future work will use CALPHAD-guided alloy modification to stabilize higher Cr contents in the
B2 phase while retaining a dominant FCC+B2/BCC microstructure, with the goal of designing
mechanically robust, corrosion-resistant EHEAs for safety-critical applications to leverage the LLNL’s
broader national and global security mission.

Introduction:

 The overall goal of this study is to establish how annealing-induced changes in
nano-lamellar microstructure govern the corrosion behavior of L-PBF AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ in strong
acid. More specifically, the work aims to quantitatively link phase-selective dissolution and early
nanoscale morphology evolution to long-term electrochemical response across different length
scales, and to use these mechanistic insights to guide microstructure- and composition-informed
strategies for designing next generation mechanically robust, corrosion-resistant EHEAs. Ren et.
al investigated the mechanical properties of these EHEAs to highlight that the annealed materials
show superior properties including the 600 °C/5 hour exhibiting yield strength of 1.6 GPa and 1000
°C/1 hour has highest uniform elongation more than 20%. Although there has been an extensive
effort on understanding the microstructure, mechanical properties like strength, hardness, friction
and wear and thermal properties, corrosion properties of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEAs in acidic
environment at different length scales is less explored.

 Current research work tries to address the following hypotheses-
 - Do Cr-rich regions form a spatially non-uniform passive film that competes with Al
 dissolution yielding a partial protection at one length scale (local repassivation), but
 ineffective at another (deep attacks at printing directions), producing time-dependent
 electrochemical signatures?
 - Does annealing systematically change BCC/FCC characteristic length scale (phase size
 and interfacial area density) so as the selective dissolution appears more lateral in the
 nanoscopic topography but a different depth profile severity at the micro/meso length
 scales?
 - At the micro/meso scale, is it the elemental segregation or the residual stresses
 (dislocation density) more contributing factor for the corrosion of these AM EHEA
 materials?

Results:

 3.1 Starting stage: Microstructure and surface conditions
 At the starting stage, the AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁ samples exhibit a well-defined nano-lamellar
eutectic architecture whose morphology and surface state evolve systematically along the
annealing temperature (). In SEM, the as-printed condition shows relatively fine, closely spaced
lamellae, whereas annealing at 600 °C induces a moderate coarsening of the colony structure, and
1000 °C produces markedly coarser, more widely spaced lamellae with increased colony contrast,
consistent with thermally driven phase coarsening and segregation. Secondary-electron SEM
images showing the eutectic lamellar morphology, where dark valleys correspond to Al–Ni-rich
B2 lamellae and bright peaks to Co–Cr–Fe-rich FCC/L12 lamellae. In parallel, AFM height maps
reveal that the nanoscale topography becomes progressively more textured with annealing, and
roughness increases as a function of annealing temperatures. Collectively, from microstructural
characterization, it is observed that heat treatment progressively coarsens the lamellar
microstructure and, at 1000 °C/1 h, produces a uniformly rougher nanoscale surface, thereby
modifying both the characteristic length scale and geometric distribution of potential corrosion
initiation sites.

3.2 Early-time corrosion (0-5 hours); in situ EC-AFM

 From the EC-AFM time-lapse imaging during the first 5 hours of exposure indicates a
highly localized, phase-selective dissolution in the Cr depleted regions. In the as-printed alloy,
early attack is relatively shallow (depth of 2 nm), whereas the 600 °C/5h (depth of 7 nm) and
especially the 1000 °C/1h samples (depth of 10 nm) develop progressively deeper grooves and
broader dissolution sites. Depth-distribution histograms quantify a clear shift of the surface height
profile toward larger penetration depths with increasing time and annealing temperatures.

3.3 Extended time corrosion (96 hours)
 Across all processing conditions, the open-circuit potential (OCP) stabilizes within the first
few hours and then remains nearly constant over 96 h. Though the differences in OCP values is
liow, the stabilized value tend to shift positively with annealing, showing that heat treatment
changes the balance between anodic dissolution and cathodic reactions at the surface. From the
potentiodynamic polarization data, it is observed that the as-printed alloy has the lowest corrosion
current density and the most stable passive film formation, whereas both annealed states exhibit
higher 𝑗corr and more frequent passive-film breakdown/defects. From SEM images, it can be
observed that the passive films are porous which suggests that the electrochemical trends can be
attributed to the underlying lamellar architecture.
 In the as-printed condition, both the B2/BCC and L1₂/FCC nano-lamellae are tightly
interleaved, giving short diffusion paths and a high density of phase boundaries; thus, a Cr-rich
film can rapidly cover both the phases, producing a relatively noble, quickly stabilizing OCP, and
reducing the attack at the dissolution sites. At 600 °C/5 h, lamellae coarsen, interlamellar spacing
increases, and B2 connectivity along the build direction is enhanced, so larger anodic B2 bands
are galvanically coupled to slightly broader FCC regions. This microstructure supports a somewhat
nobler OCP due to a thicker Cr-rich film on the FCC matrix, but the polarization curves show
higher 𝑗corr and earlier breakdown because the defects at B2/FCC interfaces now access a larger
effective anodic area. After 1000 °C/1 h, lamellae are coarsened at maximum and most widely
spaced, the local BCC fraction in the probed regions decreases, and the FCC matrix becomes
nearly continuous, so each remaining B2 lamella behaves as an intense local anode embedded in
a Cr-rich cathodic network. The OCP remains noble or even shifts slightly positive, reflecting the
globally passivated FCC surface, yet once the film ruptures at a B2/FCC boundary (which is
observed to be prominent at the build directions), the current localizes into these few anodic active
sites, giving deeper attack and pronounced current spikes.
 This effect can be elucidated through mixed-potential/Evans-diagram concepts that
annealing effectively moves the mixed potential to more positive values while also raising the
corrosion current, the annealed alloys look more noble (thermodynamically) but actually corrode
more rapidly (kinetically) as the lamellar size, spacing, and connectivity amplify phase-selective
dissolution from the nano- to the macro-scale.

3.4 Morphological evolution (multi-scale degradation) post 96 hours
 Post-corrosion morphology of AM AlCoCrFeNi2.1 in the as-printed, 600 °C/5 h, and
1000 °C/1 h conditions at low-magnification SEM images shows the corrosion-affected banded
morphology at the build direction which form unit cells of repetitive corrosion domains especially
for the annealed samples. Corresponding 3D height maps from the confocal microscopy highlights

the processing-dependent dissolution topography. The as-printed surface exhibits relatively
shallow banded topography, the 600 °C/5 h condition shows uniformly deepened groove-like
attack localized along melt-pool bands, and the 1000 °C/1 h condition shows highest depth and a
more diffused distribution of pits and grooves.

3.5 SEM/EDS chemistry post corrosion

 After 96 hours of exposure, EDS elemental maps reveal more pronounced
annealing-dependent differences in the distribution of passive films and residual matrix chemistry.
In the as-printed alloy, the maps remain relatively homogeneous, with only subtle oxygen
enrichment and limited evidence of large-scale elemental partitioning, consistent with more
spatially uniform, shallow dissolution and a comparatively thin, mixed oxide film. In contrast, the
annealed conditions show strongly heterogeneous O-rich regions intertwined with Cr- and
Fe-enriched areas, while Al- and Ni-rich domains are noticeably depleted, indicating preferential
dissolution of the Al–Ni-rich B2 lamellae and the formation of a discontinuous Cr-bearing passive
layer.

4. Conclusions:
- Annealing systematically restructures the nano-lamellar B2/BCC–L1₂/FCC architecture in
 L-PBF AlCoCrFeNi₂.₁, leading to coarsening of the lamellae, modifying BCC fraction and
 connectivity along the build direction, and thereby re-distributing the Al- and Cr-rich regions
 across nano- to meso-scales.
- In-situ EC-AFM reveals that corrosion initiates and propagates in a phase-selective manner: Al–
 Ni-rich B2/BCC lamellae and their boundaries dissolve preferentially, while Cr-rich FCC regions
 tend to locally stabilize and spatially form heterogeneous passive films whose effectiveness
 decreases with increasing annealing temperature.
- Long-term OCP and polarization measurements indicate that annealing make the alloy
 thermodynamically more passive-like yet kinetically more susceptible to rapid, localized attack.
- Post-corrosion SEM/confocal imaging and EDS mapping across length scales link early
 nanoscale height changes to macroscopic degraded morphologies, demonstrating that
 microstructure-informed control of lamellar spacing, phase fractions, and segregation is essential
 for designing AM EHEAs with optimized strength–corrosion trade-offs in strong acidic
 environments.

5. Future goals:
 The objective is to make corrosion resistant material while maintaining its superior
mechanical properties. Using CALPHAD, a computational thermodynamics framework tool, we
plan to narrow the compositional space and to connect phase stability to screen candidates for
corrosion-resistant HEA compositions. The objective is to identify ranges of Cr and Al that (i)
maintain a dominant FCC + B2/BCC microstructure, (ii) change composition of BCC phase so
that it has more Cr and therefore can form a Cr oxide rich passive film and (iii) avoid σ or other
brittle intermetallic at service temperatures. Currently, there is limited understanding on whether

such system is possible, since high entropy alloys are complex materials. These predictions will
guide which compositions are practical to manufacture by L-PBF and then will be characterized
by EBSD/EDS and corrosion testing using AFM and electrochemistry, turning CALPHAD tool as
a hypothesis-generation and screening tool rather than a purely descriptive exercise.
