Constraints on the inflationary vacuum and reheating era from NANOGrav
Sudipta Show

Abstract
NANOGrav and various pulsar timing array experiments recently reported evidence for a common red noise signal across millisecond pulsars. This signal exhibits Hellings-Downs inter-pulsar correlation patterns, providing compelling evidence for a stochastic gravitational wave background (SGWB) signal. In general, such a background can come from several astrophysical and cosmological phenomena. Assuming such SGWB has an inflationary origin, we use latest NANOGrav 15-year dataset to constrain the inflationary parameters e.g., tensor spectral index ( n t n_{t} ), tensor-to-scalar ratio ( r r ), and explore the implications for the reheating phase through constraints on the reheating equation of state ( ω re \omega_{\text{re}} ) and reheating temperature ( OPEN T re ) T_{\text{re}}) . We find the preference for an extremely blue-tilted tensor spectrum n t = 2.20 − 1.2 + 0.36 n_{t}=2.20^{+0.36}_{-1.2} and the radiation-like reheating scenario ω re = 0.33 − 0.36 + 0.14 \omega_{\text{re}}=0.33^{+0.14}_{-0.36} . Despite having no concrete evidence for the nature of the primordial vacua, the computation of gravitational wave (GW) sourced by tensor perturbations assumes the inflationary vacuum to be a Bunch-Davies vacuum. In this work, we examine modifications to the GW spectrum originating from the non-Bunch-Davies primordial vacuum. We find that NANOGrav observations favor a specific type of non-Bunch-Davies vacuum, known as the alpha-vacuum. Furthermore, our analysis demonstrates that the observations strikingly narrow down the range of the parameter α \alpha characterizing the vacua. On top of that, we find that a frequency-dependent parametrization of the vacuum parameter α \alpha beyond a threshold frequency can yield a minimal solution to alleviate the blue-titled issue. Finally, we highlight the possibility of testing such frequency dependence of α \alpha by probing the GW spectrum through future GW experiments.

2
Gravitational wave spectrum in a non-Bunch-Davies vacuum

Primordial GW, seeded by tensor perturbations in the inflationary universe, is a plausible interpretation of the observed SGWB. Suppose the universe starts from an initial state such as a non-Bunch-Davies vacuum, which will leave its imprint on the primordial GW spectrum. In this section, we calculate the GW energy spectrum originating from a non-Bunch-Davies primordial vacuum.

In the synchronous gauge, the line element of a spatially-flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric describing the inflationary tensor perturbations
h_{ij}

\displaystyle ds^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)[-d\tau^{2}+(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij})dx^{i}dx^{j}],

(2.1)

We treat the tensor perturbation as a quantum field in an unperturbed FLRW metric.
Now, the second order action for the tensor perturbation

\displaystyle S=\int d\tau d\textbf{x}\,a^{2}\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{8}\bigg[(h_{ij}^{\prime})^{2}-(\partial_{l}h_{ij})^{2}\bigg],

(2.2)

denotes the Newton’s constant. Here, the prime (

In Fourier space, the tensor perturbation

\displaystyle h_{ij}(\tau,\textbf{x})=\sum_{r=+,\times}\int\frac{d\textbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\epsilon_{ij}^{r}(\textbf{{k}})h_{\textbf{k}}^{r}(\tau)e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{x}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle v_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\left(k^{2}-\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}\right)v_{k}=0,

(2.7)

Constraints on inflationary and reheating parameters

The NANOGrav collaboration provides a strong evidence for a stochastic signal with a common amplitude and spectrum by analysing their latest 15 years of data. Blue-tilted spectrum and frequency dependent alpha-vacuum

A blue-tilted GW energy spectrum, when extrapolated to high frequencies, can violate the constraints imposed by big bang nucleosynthesis. In this section, we demonstrate that introducing a frequency-dependent parametrization of the vacuum parameter
\alpha_{t}
provides a viable resolution to this issue.

Before introducing a frequency-dependent parametrization, we first study the behaviour of the GW energy spectrum as a function of frequency. For illustration, we choose a benchmark from the 2
\sigma

n_{t}
and

MeV.

[

. The evolution of the universe can also be understood by looking at the slope of the plot in Figure

f\sim 10^{-17}
Hz during the matter-radiation equality and at nHz frequency during radiation-reheating equality as highlighted in Figure
3
inset. It is obvious that after satisfying the NANOGrav observation, the GW spectrum hits the BBN disallowed region i.e., the required blue-tilted spectrum violates the BBN bound.

An interesting approach to address the blue-tilted issue is to introduce frequency dependence in
\alpha_{t}
. As discussed previously in Eq. (
4.3
), the primordial tensor power spectrum in case of alpha-vacuum is

\displaystyle P_{T}^{NBD}=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\frac{H_{*}^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}}e^{-2\alpha_{t}}\bigg(\frac{k}{k_{*}}\bigg)^{n_{t}}.

(5.1)

The tensor spectral index is defined as
n_{t}=\frac{d\ln P_{T}}{d\ln k}
. If the vacuum parameter
\alpha_{t}
is allowed to have a frequency dependence, then the tensor spectral index in presence of a non-Bunch-Davies vacuum takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{d\ln P_{T}^{NBD}}{d\ln k}=n_{t}-2\frac{d\alpha_{t}}{d\ln k}~.

(5.2)

During the reheating epoch, the frequency-dependency of the GW spectrum in Eq. (
2.20
) is given by
\Omega_{GW}\propto f^{2+n_{t}-\chi}
where
\chi=4/(1+3\omega_{re})

\Omega_{GW}\propto f^{2+n_{t}-\chi-\gamma(k)}
where
\gamma(k)=2~{d\alpha_{t}}/{d\ln k}
. If the frequency-dependent term
\gamma(k)
exceeds the combination
2+n_{t}-\chi
, the GW energy spectrum remains consistent with the BBN bound. For simplicity, we may parametrize
\gamma(k)=2D
with

. However, one must be careful while considering this frequency dependence since if it persists across the entire frequency range, the resulting GW spectrum may fail to remain consistent with NANOGrav observations. So, any frequency dependence, if present, should arise beyond a certain scale. Therefore, we adopt a frequency-dependent parametrization given by

\alpha_{t}(k)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{0}\hskip 81.94406pt\text{for}~~k\leq k_{S},\\
\alpha_{0}+D\ln\left(\frac{k}{k_{S}}\right)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for}~~k\geq k_{S}.\end{cases}

(5.3)

k_{S}

6
Summary and Conclusion

Recently, NANOGrav and other pulsar timing array experiments (EPTA, IPTA, PPTA, InPTA, and CPTA) have provided evidence for a common red-spectrum stochastic signal across pulsars in the low-frequency regime. On top of that, a compelling evidence of the Hellings-Downs inter-pulsar correlation has been observed, which provides the first detection of the stochastic gravitational wave background in the nHz frequency range. There is a potential room for several possible explanations of the quest for the origin of such SGWB, for example, the background of merging supermassive black hole binaries, cosmological origins, etc. It is essential to scrutinize whether the cosmological explanation is consistent with the observation because it helps to decide among the mentioned possibilities. In this work, we test whether the SGWB has an inflationary origin, where cosmological GWs sourced during the inflationary era constrain the inflationary reheating phase.

The primary result of our analysis, shown in Figure
1
, is that the NANOGrav data prefer an extremely blue-tilted spectrum with a large equation-of-state parameter for reheating. In particular, by performing an MCMC analysis we have obtained
n_{t}=2.20^{+0.36}_{-1.2}
,
\omega_{\text{re}}=0.33^{+0.14}_{-0.36}
in addition to placing an lower bound on tensor-to-scalar ratio,
r>3.16\times 10^{-11}
for fixed reheating temperature,
T_{\text{re}}=50
MeV.
Our fit shows that the radiation-like reheating picture (
\omega_{\text{re}}=1/3
) is preferred; however, matter-like reheating (
\omega_{\text{re}}=0
) is viable within the 1
\sigma
range. Also, our analysis shows that realizing a nearly scale-invariant scenario (
n_{t}\approx 0
) is excluded at more than 2
\sigma
confidence level.

Generally, most studies of inflationary GWs sourced by tensor perturbations assume that the primordial vacuum is a Bunch-Davies vacuum. However, there is no solid evidence for such a choice.
In this work, we explore the possibility of a non-Bunch-Davies primordial vacuum and examine the NANOGrav observations, suggesting that the SGWB has an inflationary origin. We find that the NANOGrav 15-year data constrain the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
that characterize the non-Bunch-Davies vacuum. Notably, observations from CMB — particularly the small amplitude of the scalar power spectrum and the tensor-to-scalar ratio — indicate that
\beta_{t}
has to be zero. Therefore, the NANOGrav observations favour a specific type of vacuum, namely the alpha-vacuum parametrized by
\alpha_{t}
. Furthermore, MCMC analysis shows that the NANOGrav data significantly narrow the range of the parameter
\alpha_{t}
by reducing its upper limit to
\alpha_{t}<11.5
. For the first time, we provide a phenomenological constraint on the parameters that define the alpha-vacuum.

We have previously noted that observation requires
n_{t}>0
; as a result, the GW spectrum exceeds the Planck upper limit when extrapolated to higher frequencies. This issue is common across all scenarios attempting to explain the observed SGWB as having an inflationary origin. In our setup, we have found that if the parameter
\alpha_{t}
, which describes the non-Bunch-Davies vacuum, exhibits a specific frequency dependence above a threshold, the blue-tilted issue can be resolved. Therefore, we choose the value of
D
so that the GW spectrum begins to decline with increasing frequency after the threshold frequency. In summary, we have investigated how 15-year NANOGrav data can constrain the inflationary reheating phase and the primordial vacuum of inflation, and have shown that observations prefer a specific non-Bunch-Davies vacuum, known as the alpha-vacuum. On top of that, we find that a frequency-dependent parametrization of
\alpha_{t}
beyond a specific frequency provides the simplest approach to resolve the long-standing blue-tilted issue. In addition, the resulting GW spectrum will be further tested in future GW experiments such as LISA, DECIGO, Cosmic Explorer, and the Einstein Telescope.
