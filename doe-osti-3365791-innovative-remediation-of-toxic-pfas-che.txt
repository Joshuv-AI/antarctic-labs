SUMMARY

Why Innovative PFAS Remediation deserves to be considered for an
R&D 100 Award
The compelling aspect of this innovative technology is successfully applying total
remediation of toxic PFAS with ease of implementation in traditional wastewater
treatment plants. Our designed sorbent materials not only effectively capture PFAS, but
also facilitate their thermal degradation at a temperature range that prevents recycling
back into the environment. Furthermore, the sorbent can also preconcentrate PFAS
from very low concentration, which can be used for trace level detection in wastewater
treatment plants. The starting materials used to create the sorbent are non-toxic. The
heat treatment of sorbent during thermal degradation of PFAS converts it back to bare
mesoporous carbon that can be reused. The developed sorbent is also adoptable
in current wastewater treatment plants and can be used as a replacement for, or in
combination with activated carbon.

Our innovative approach represents a significant advancement in PFAS remediation
strategies. This technology has attracted companies interested in PFAS remediation and
is being adopted by them. We are currently collaborating with a wastewater treatment
company that has acquired a test and evaluation license for our technology. The results
from their independent testing suggested that our sorbents still outperform leading
products in the market with respect to removing a variety of PFAS compounds at lower
concentration (ppt level) and different physiochemical conditions. We are currently in
discussions with another company that is interested in licensing our product to explore
applications beyond PFAS remediation, such as critical mineral extraction and heavy
metal removal.

 R&D 100 Awards Selection Committee

 Dear Committee Members:

 Per- and poly-fluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are well recognised as global contaminants of concern. In addition to their
 health impacts, PFAS containing products and uses are literally too numerous to name in a brief letter but the
 ubiquitous and historical use of aqueous film forming foams (AFFF) at sites for fire suppression has resulted in PFAS
 contamination of environmental matrices such as soils, groundwaters, and surface waters across the globe.
 Internationally, there are increasing calls to restrict and/or ban the production and use of PFAS for the protection of
 human health and the environment. For example, in Australia, the PFAS National Environmental Management Plan
 (most recently updated as version 3.0 in 2025) was developed to provide a nationally agreed approach to the
 investigation, assessment and management of PFAS contaminated sites and wastes.
 In my roles at Curtin University and Eutectiq I have been actively researching the environmental consequences of PFAS
 in terms of the contamination and leachability from concrete and asphalt pavements and the evaluation of commercial
 sealants to mitigate their mobility. This research follows on from that undertaken at the CSIRO in Australia. More
 recently, I have been actively developing fluorescent PFAS proxy molecules to aid in both laboratory studies and
 environmental fate and exploring the bonding of PFAS to surfaces at the atomic scale.

 Several years ago, I began an active correspondence with researchers from Sandia National Laboratories (particularly
 Dr. Patrick Brady and Dr. Mark Rigali) on several common research interests between the Sandia in the US and CSIRO
 in Australia; most notably the remediation challenges of PFAS removal from the environment. I have actively followed
 their pivotal research in this area and am quite familiar with their work in both rapid PFAS detection and remediation.
 I remain an admirer of the excellent outcomes achieved by this Sandia team over an extended period both in terms of
 publication and practical impact.

 Given their outstanding track record in PFAS research, today, I am writing to wholeheartedly support the nomination
 of the technical team from Sandia National Laboratories for an R&D 100 award for their novel technology “Innovative
 remediation of toxic PFAS chemicals in water using improved mesoporous carbon material”.

 The Sandia product is a simple modification to the surface chemistry of commercial carbon based PFAS sorbents. By
 grafting alkyl ammonium compounds to the carbon substrate, a significant improvement in PFAS absorption capacity
 is realized. In fact, in recently published literature, the team compared performance of their sorbents with different
 commercially available carbon-based PFAS sorbents and reported significant capacity increase.

 In closing, as an active researcher in the field of PFAS remediation, I wholeheartedly endorse this technology for your
 consideration for an R&D 100 award based on the technical expertise, commitment to task, and outstanding outcomes
 that have been achieved.

 Dear Committee Members:

 It is becoming increasingly clear that the cleanup of perfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) from drinking
 water supplies represents one of the most challenging environmental problems in the US and the world.
 According to the EPA, because of their widespread production and use, as well as their ability to move
 and persist in the environment, approximately 97% of the people in the United States have been exposed
 to PFAS. Even at low levels, these compounds are being associated with several human health impacts
 including altered immune function, liver and kidney disease, lipid and insulin dysregulation,
 reproductive and developmental disorders and cancer.
 Hence, I am writing on behalf of Sandia National Laboratories Energy Water Program to nominate the
 technical team from Sandia National Laboratories for an R&D 100 award for their novel technology
 “Innovative remediation of toxic PFAS chemicals in water using improved mesoporous carbon
 material”.
 Sandia has been active in water treatment and desalination research and development for more than two
 decades with numerous publications and patents in these and other closely related fields. As the largest
 DOE laboratory in the complex, we are active in numerous fields of cutting-edge R&D. Hence, we
 employ our own rigorous internal selection process before advancing a limited number of technologies
 to the awards committee for consideration. I am proud to inform this committee that the laboratory has
 selected this water treatment technology for R&D 100 Award consideration.
 The Sandia product combines an effective pore size that enhances PFAS diffusion into the carbonaceous
 matrix combined with incorporation of positively charged aliphatic quaternary amines that are
 chemically grafted to the pore surfaces. This leads to a product with an enhanced capacity for and an
 increased electrostatic attraction with PFAS molecules ultimately leading to substantially improved
 adsorption performance when compared to currently available sorbents.
 In closing I strongly endorse this technology for your consideration.

 To: R & D 100 Selection Committee R&D
 I write to offer my strong endorsement of “Innovative remediation of toxic PFAS chemicals in
 water using improved mesoporous carbon material” by Kruichak-Duhigg et al. for an R&D 100
 award, and hope that the application will be considered in the highest light.

 PFAS (polyfluoroalkyl substances) control is a key goal of global environmental research
 because of the ubiquity and toxicity of PFAS, and because there is no single target:
 instead there are multiple PFAS compounds, each with different reactivity. A sorbent that
 removes trace FPAS levels from water is a Holy Grail.

 What distinguishes the Mesoporous-PFAS work of Kruichak-Duhigg et al. are: 1. The
 development of quite effective sorbents for PFOA, perfluorooctanoic acid – a common
 PFAS, combined with; 2. A rigorous mechanistic analysis of why PFOA sorbed, and; 3. A
 stunningly thorough experimental analysis. Collectively this means that, in addition to
 demonstrating an effective and new PFOA sorbent, the groundwork has been laid for
 improving PFOA sorbents, and for designing sorbents for other PFAS. In other words, the
 Mesoporous-PFAS work of Kruichak-Duhigg et al. not only solves an important problem –
 sorption of PFOA, it develops and documents the technique for determining how to sorb
 the other PFAS molecules.

 Having previously led Sandia’s advanced water treatment program for a decade I see the
 Mesoporous-PFAS work of Kruichak-Duhigg et al. as ground-breaking and innovative. And
 because of its immediate applicability to attacking widespread PFAS contamination, I
 believe the Mesoporous-PFAS work of Kruichak-Duhigg et al. to be a perfect example of
     R&D 100 work.

 Sincerely,
