Analysis of Influencing Factors of eHealth Literacy in Patients with Obstructive Sleep Apnea Hypopnea Syndrome: A Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Wenchen Zhao, Xinxia Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aims to investigate the factors influencing eHealth literacy among patients with obstructive sleep apnea-hypopnea syndrome (OSAHS) and to provide a reference for clinical interventions.MethodsFrom April 2025 to December 2025, a cross-sectional survey was conducted among patients diagnosed with OSAHS. Data were collected using a general information questionnaire, the eHealth Literacy Scale, the Health Information Seeking Behavior Scale, the Chronic Disease Self-Efficacy Scale, the Social Support Scale, and the OSAHS Patient Self-Management Ability Questionnaire. Pearson or Spearman rank correlation coefficients were selected to conduct correlation analyses, aiming to explore the relationships among health information seeking behavior, information self-efficacy, social support, and eHealth literacy. Variables demonstrating statistical significance in the correlation analyses were then included in a multiple linear regression model to ultimately identify the factors influencing eHealth literacy in patients with OSAHS.ResultsThe study included 241 patients. The mean eHealth literacy score was 22.28±7.68, and the health information seeking behavior score was 141.00±20.16. The mean self-efficacy score was 9.20±2.96, social support score was 37.80±6.99, and self-management ability score was 62.51±13.76. Correlation analysis indicated that eHealth literacy was positively correlated with health information seeking behavior, self-efficacy, social support, and self-management ability (P<0.05). Multiple linear regression analysis revealed that family education level, duration of online health information seeking, health information behaviors, information demand, information sources, barriers to information access, social support, and life management skills were significant influencing factors of eHealth literacy.ConclusioneHealth literacy among patients with OSAHS is at a low level. Meanwhile, family education level, duration of online health information seeking, health information behavior, information needs, information sources, barriers to information access, social support, and life management skills have all been confirmed as significant predictors of eHealth literacy. Medical staff should assess patients’ eHealth literacy and use identified factors to develop targeted strategies for improving self-management.

Introduction

Obstructive sleep apnea hypopnea syndrome (OSAHS) is a chronic condition characterized by episodes of apnoea, hypnopaedia, hypercritical, and hypoxaemia resulting from the collapse of the upper airway during sleep.1 Clinically, it is frequently associated with loud snoring, disrupted sleep-wake cycles, and excessive daytime sleepiness, all of which adversely impact the patient’s daily life and work.2 1As OSAHS progresses, it can lead to severe complications, including pulmonary hypertension, coronary heart disease, and cerebrovascular accidents, potentially jeopardizing the patient’s life. Currently, clinical practice in the treatment of patients with OSAHS primarily emphasizes therapeutic outcomes, often neglecting the importance of patient self-management. This oversight can hinder patients from achieving their desired therapeutic results. The ongoing integration of the Internet with healthcare has fostered rapid advancements in tools such as electronic medical records, wearable devices, mobile health applications, and medicine, thereby propelling the evolution of smart healthcare. Consequently, the concept of electronic health literacy(eHealth) has garnered significant attention.3 e-HL is defined as an individual’s capacity to access, comprehend, and evaluate health-related information from electronic resources, as well as to utilize this information effectively to address health issues.4

Currently, international research on the eHealth literacy of patients with OSAHS remains relatively scarce. Existing literature focuses on chronic disease patients and older adults, consistently indicating that this population has a low level of eHealth literacy.5 Meanwhile, the factors influencing eHealth literacy across different diseases are highly complex and diverse, with no unified conclusions yet established.6 In clinical practice, the vast majority of OSAHS patients do not require long-term hospitalization; instead, they rely on self-management at home. However, with the rapid development of the Internet, artificial intelligence, and virtual technologies, online health information varies widely in quality. Surveys indicate that up to 90% of patients refer to or blindly trust online information.7 This easily leads to adverse outcomes, such as delayed treatment, due to misinformation guidance. Therefore, for OSAHS patients who primarily manage their condition at home, possessing high eHealth literacy to discern and obtain accurate, reliable health information is particularly urgent and crucial.

Therefore, this study focuses on patients with OSAHS to explore the factors influencing their eHealth literacy. The findings aim to provide a basis for clinicians to develop targeted intervention strategies, thereby effectively improving patients’ eHealth literacy and self-management capabilities.

Serials and Methods

Participants

Patients diagnosed with OSAHS at a tertiary hospital in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region from April 2025 to December 2025 were selected as research subjects. The inclusion criteria were as follows: ①Adherence to the diagnostic criteria outlined in the 2011 guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of OSAHS;8 ②Age of 18 years or older; ③Possession of basic communication and comprehension skills; ④Proficiency in using electronic devices, such as mobile phones; and ⑤Provision of informed consent and voluntary participation in the study. The exclusion criteria included: ①Individuals with severe functional disorders of vital organs, including the heart, brain, and kidneys; ②Individuals unable to complete the questionnaire due to severe visual or hearing impairments or other factors; ③ Individuals with cognitive or mental impairments.

Sample Size Determination and Calculation

Based on the item-based sample size estimation method, the required sample size should be 5 to 10 times the number of predictor variables. In this study, the general information section involved 17 variables, the eHealth Literacy Scale comprised 8 variables, the Social Support Scale included 10 variables, the OSAHS Patient Self-Management Behavior Questionnaire contained 21 variables, the Health Information Seeking Behavior Scale had 43 variables, and the Information Self-Efficacy Scale consisted of 3 variables. Using the maximum number of variables among the scales (43) as the baseline, the calculated sample size was 215 to 430 cases. Considering a 10% rate of invalid responses, the final target sample size was determined to be 239 to 478 cases.

Research Tools

General Information Questionnaire

A self-designed questionnaire was used to collect data on sociodemographic characteristics, including age, gender, place of residence, occupation, per capita monthly income, education level, and marital status. Clinical characteristics, such as disease awareness and Internet usage, were also assessed.

eHealth Literacy Scale

The eHealth literacy was originally developed by Norman and Skinner9 and subsequently validated in Chinese by Guo et al10 The scale consists of three dimensions: skills in seeking online health information (items 1–5), evaluating the quality of information (items 6–7), and applying the information to health decisions (item 8). Items are rated on a 5-point Likert scale, with total scores ranging from 8 to 40. Higher scores indicate higher levels of eHealth literacy, with scores >32 considered indicative of adequate literacy. The Cronbach’s α coefficient in this study was 0.956.

Health Information Seeking Behavior

The scale was originally developed by international researchers, including Zamani et al11 and subsequently validated in Chinese by Sun et al.12 It comprises 43 items across four dimensions: attitudes towards health information, information needs, information sources, and barriers to access. Items are rated on a 5-point Likert scale. Items 1–35 are positively framed, whereas items 36–43 are negatively framed. Total scores range from 43 to 215, with higher scores indicating more frequent health information-seeking behavior. In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.870.

Information Self-Efficacy Scale

Developed by Pavlou et al,13 this scale assesses an individual’s confidence in searching for, evaluating, and comparing online information. It comprises three items, each rated on a 5-point Likert scale. Total scores range from 3 to 15, with higher scores indicating greater information self-efficacy. Scores of ≥13, 10–12, and <10 denote high, medium, and low levels of efficacy, respectively. In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.883.

Social Support Scale

Currently, the social support scale system is well established. This study utilized the scale developed by Shuiyuan Xiao14 in 1986, which has been employed in numerous domestic studies since its inception. Feedback indicates that the scale demonstrates strong reliability and validity. It comprises a total of 10 items categorized into three dimensions: subjective support (4 items), social support (4 items), and objective support (3 items). A score below 33 signifies low social support, a score ranging from 33 to 45 indicates average social support, and a score exceeding 46 reflects high social support. Thus, a higher total score corresponds to greater social support. The test-retest reliability coefficient for the scale was 0.92, while the internal consistency, measured by Cronbach’s α, ranged from 0.89 to 0.94.

The OSAHS Self-Management Assessment Scale

The OSAHS Self-management Assessment Scale was developed independently by a research team led by Chinese scholar Wanli Dong.15 This scale comprises four dimensions: the life management dimension includes 9 items, the treatment management dimension contains 6 items, the knowledge and skills management dimension has 3 items, and the psychological management dimension consists of 3 items. Utilizing a Likert 5-point scoring system, each item can be rated as follows: 5 points (completely achieved), 4 points (mostly achieved), 3 points (basically achieved), 2 points (occasionally achieved), and 1 point (not achieved at all). The total score of the scale is calculated by summing the scores of all items. Based on this scoring, the self-management levels of OSAHS patients can be categorized into three tiers: average (total score ≤63 points), medium (63–84 points), and good (total score ≥84 points).

Data Collection

The researchers first contacted the administration of the selected hospital to provide a detailed introduction to the study’s objectives, significance, and content, thereby securing their support. Subsequently, the investigators thoroughly explained the purpose and significance of the study to the participants to obtain their agreement and secure signed informed consent forms. The investigators then distributed the questionnaires and explained the instructions and precautions for completion. If participants had difficulty completing the questionnaire, the investigators assisted them based on their actual conditions. The completion time was approximately 15–20 minutes. The investigators collected the questionnaires on-site and checked them for completeness. In case of any missing items, participants were asked to complete them immediately to ensure the valid return of the questionnaires.

Quality Control

Participants were screened according to established inclusion and exclusion criteria. The purpose and significance of the study were explained to patients, and questionnaires were administered only after informed consent was obtained. Patients completed the forms following instructions on how to answer. For those with literacy difficulties, a face-to-face interview was conducted where researchers read the questions in a neutral tone and recorded the responses on their behalf. Completed questionnaires were collected immediately and checked for omissions or errors. Data entry was performed independently by two researchers.

Statistical Analyses

For continuous variables that follow a normal distribution, the mean ± standard deviation is employed for description. In contrast, for continuous variables that do not adhere to a normal distribution, the median and interquartile range are utilized. Categorical variables are characterized by frequency and percentage. Correlation analysis, utilizing either the Pearson correlation coefficient or the Spearman rank correlation coefficient depending on the presence of a linear trend between the variables, was conducted to investigate the relationships among health information search behavior, information self-efficacy, social support, and eHealth. Variables from the general information and the scales (Health Information Seeking Behavior, Information Self-Efficacy, Social Support, and OSAHS Self-Management Ability) that demonstrated statistically significant univariate linear correlations with eHealth literacy (P ≤ 0.05) were selected. Subsequently, these variables were incorporated into a multiple linear regression model to explore the influencing factors of eHealth literacy in OSAHS patients and to analyze the effect sizes of each factor.

Ethical Considerations

This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Medical University (Approval No. KY2025165). The research strictly adhered to the principles of informed consent, respect, beneficence, justice, and confidentiality.

Results

A total of 251 questionnaires were administered, and all were retrieved, resulting in a 100% response rate. After excluding 10 incomplete or invalid responses, 241 valid questionnaires remained, yielding an effective response rate of 96.01%.

General Information Questionnaire Analysis

The majority of the research subjects were male (183 cases, 75.9%), possessed a college education or higher (76 cases, 31.5%), were married (196 cases, 81.3%), reported a per capita family income of less than 3000 per month (76 cases, 31.5%), and predominantly resided in urban areas (171 cases, 71.0%). The patients had been diagnosed with OSAHS for a duration of 0–5 years (173 cases, 71.8%). The frequency with which patients searched online for disease-related information was several times a year (132 cases, 54.8%). The time spent by patients searching for health information online was less than 1 hour per day (177 cases, 73.4%). A majority of individuals (148 cases, 61.4%) exhibited an average attitude toward online health information.

Analysis of the Total Score of the eHealth Literacy Scale for Patients

The average electronic health literacy score among the 241 patients surveyed was 22.28 ± 7.68 points. Of these patients, 213 (88.38%) exhibited a low level of eHealth literacy, defined as a score of less than 32 points.

Model Robustness Analysis

This study first conducted rigorous diagnostic tests on the regression model. The multicollinearity diagnosis revealed that the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) values for all independent variables were well below the critical threshold of 5. Furthermore, the p-values from the residual normality tests were all greater than 0.05, indicating that the residuals are normally distributed. These results confirm that the model is free from severe multicollinearity and satisfies the normality assumption of classical linear regression.

Regarding the model fit, the coefficient of determination(R2) was 0.325, meaning that the independent variables collectively explain 32.5% of the variance in the dependent variable. Although this value does not indicate an exceptionally high goodness-of-fit, an explanatory power of approximately 0.3 is generally considered a medium effect size in empirical social science research. Moreover, given that the model has passed these stringent assumption tests, the regression results demonstrate statistical robustness and reliability.

Results of Multiple Linear Regression Analysis

The results indicated that patients with a higher family educational level exhibited elevated electronic health literacy (β=1.098, P=0.030). Additionally, prolonged online searches for health information correlated with increased eHealth literacy (β=1.632, P=0.008). Patients who engaged in more proactive health information behaviors demonstrated higher levels of eHealth literacy (β=0.380, P=0.008). Furthermore, a greater demand for information among patients was associated with enhanced eHealth literacy (β=−0.429, P=0.032). The accuracy of the information source utilized by patients positively influenced their eHealth literacy (β=−0.367, P=0.017). Moreover, patients who faced fewer barriers to accessing information reported higher levels of eHealth literacy (β=−0.324, P=0.046). Those with strong social support also exhibited elevated eHealth literacy (β=0.494, P=0.024). Discussion

The Current Status of eHealth Literacy in Patients with OSAHS

The mean eHealth literacy score among patients with OSAHS was 22.28 ± 7.68, indicating a suboptimal level of eHealth literacy in this population. This finding is consistent with studies by Huang et al16 and Zeng et al17 Potential contributing factors include limited disease awareness, poor treatment compliance, and inadequate self-management skills. Furthermore, our study revealed that patients often struggled to identify reliable sources of online health information. This uncertainty may stem from low proficiency in using electronic devices, a lack of basic digital skills, and ineffective information-seeking strategies.

In recent years, global and national initiatives—such as the WHO’s“‘Global Strategy for Digital Health (2020–2024”’ and China’s“‘Healthy China 203”’ and“‘Digital Chin”’ plans—have prioritized the integration of digital technologies into healthcare. These policies have driven innovation in health service models and elevated the importance of digital health competencies. Consequently, the advancement of digital health has become a cornerstone of global medical policy frameworks.18

OSAHS treatment is well designed for telemedicine with remote telemonitoring. Telemedicine has demonstrated tremendous potential in both the diagnosis and treatment of OSAHS patients. Compared with traditional face-to-face consultations, remote delivery of polysomnography (PSG) testing and continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy is more cost-effective.19 This is mainly attributed to the savings in transportation costs and the reduction in expenses resulting from fewer unnecessary clinic visits. However, the effective implementation of telemedicine requires patients to possess a relatively high level of eHealth literacy. Therefore, considering the specific treatment modalities for OSAHS, healthcare professionals should provide supplementary interventions during the treatment process. Through training programs or educational lectures, patients can be educated on the concept of eHealth literacy and master basic digital skills, enabling them to conduct fundamental online health information searches.

Influencing Factors of eHealth Literacy in Patients with OSAHS

General Demographic Factors

Educational attainment and the duration of online health information searches were identified as significant predictors of eHealth literacy among patients with OSAHS. Consistent with previous findings, patients with lower educational levels demonstrated significantly diminished eHealth literacy.20 This disparity is often linked to limited knowledge acquisition capabilities, particularly among older adults. As aging is frequently accompanied by a decline in physical and cognitive functions, the ability to obtain, evaluate, and apply eHealth information is often compromised.21

A study by Xiao et al22 found that health literacy levels are relatively low among middle-aged and older adults who search for health information either “daily” or “less than once a month”. To address the “U-shaped” phenomenon where eHealth literacy is low at both extremes among OSAHS patients, clinical practice should implement a stratified intervention strategy. On the one hand, for patients with low-frequency searches, healthcare institutions should leverage official platforms to regularly and precisely push professionally reviewed core OSAHS knowledge. This lowers the threshold for accessing information and ensures that patients lacking knowledge can still receive basic health education. On the other hand, for high-frequency searchers, nurses should focus on guiding them to identify and access information from authoritative medical platforms, helping them effectively filter out invalid and harmful information online, thereby mitigating the negative impacts of information overload. Therefore, in future clinical practice, given that the internet and other new media have become the primary channels for health information dissemination, healthcare professionals should incorporate these digital tools into health education to further improve patients’ eHealth literacy.

Health Information Search Behavior

The results showed that information needs, information sources, and barriers to acquiring information were all significantly negatively correlated with eHealth literacy in OSAHS patients. This may be because when patients have fewer information needs, their information-seeking behaviors are often more targeted, allowing them to focus more precisely on the knowledge they require. Meanwhile, a relatively limited number of information sources reduces the probability of patients being exposed to and having to evaluate misinformation to some extent. Therefore, in such scenarios, the eHealth literacy levels of patients may be higher.

Duan Yuehan’s23 study highlights that frequent searches for online health information are a critical factor affecting patients’ eHealth literacy. Furthermore, the demand for information reflects an intrinsic motivation that encourages patients to learn more effectively how to search for information and identify authoritative websites. This motivation directly contributes to enhancing eHealth literacy.

Research indicates an increasing demand for nursing care for OSAHS.24 Patients need not only master basic disease knowledge but also possess the ability to acquire, understand, appraise, and apply health information from the internet. Therefore, in future clinical management plans, healthcare professionals should guide OSAHS patients to obtain reliable online health information and official social media resources through health education. It is also essential to recommend specialized sleep information websites, instruct patients on how to identify credible information, provide fact-checking resources, foster a proactive attitude toward information seeking, and ultimately enhance patients’ capacity to effectively obtain health information.

Social Support

The results indicate a positive correlation between social support and eHealth literacy among patients. According to Social Cognitive Theory,25 an individual’s behavior, cognition, and emotions are inextricably linked to their social environment. As a critical component of this environment, social support enhances patients’ self-efficacy and disease management capacity by providing informational, emotional, and instrumental assistance. In the long-term management of OSAHS, continuous CPAP therapy is the clinically recognized first-line treatment. Research indicates that emotional and informational support, along with positive social interactions, play a crucial role in promoting CPAP treatment adherence.26 Strong instrumental support (such as assisting with the operation of remote monitoring devices) and informational support (such as guiding patients on how to search for CPAP care knowledge online) can transform OSAHS patients from passive recipients into active seekers and evaluators of information. This positive social interaction environment encourages OSAHS patients to more frequently try out digital health resources, thereby continuously accumulating digital skills and health knowledge through practice.

Meanwhile, patients with robust social support networks are more likely to engage in “alternative online health information seeking”—a behavior often facilitated by family members. This term refers to the active or passive pursuit of health information by non-professionals on behalf of others.27 Investigating this phenomenon is of paramount importance, as it offers a vital pathway for patients to access and manage health information, ultimately improving their health outcomes and quality of life.28

The OSAHS Self-Management Assessment Scale

The results demonstrate a significant positive correlation between eHealth literacy and self-management abilities in patients with OSAHS, a finding that corroborates previous studies by Gong et al29 and Sui et al30 Patients with superior self-management skills typically exhibit higher standards for information quality and possess sharper critical judgment. This enables them to more effectively evaluate the authenticity and applicability of online health information relative to their specific needs. Furthermore, individuals with strong self-management abilities tend to be good at exploring and utilizing emerging digital health tools to optimize their health outcomes.

In this study, the mean self-management score among the 241 participants was 62.51 ± 13.76, indicating a moderate level of self-management. Effective health self-management empowers individuals—particularly older adults—to maintain and enhance their functional status through autonomous behaviors and the strategic use of available resources. This is especially vital in the context of constrained medical resources, as it maximizes health potential and aids in the prevention and management of diseases.31

OSAHS patients can utilize clinical video telemedicine to transcend the temporal and spatial limitations of traditional medical visits, establishing an immediate and efficient online interaction model with physicians to obtain professional guidance. Meanwhile, based on a comprehensive understanding of the necessity of CPAP therapy, patients can use smartphone applications to remotely record and upload device signal data daily, enabling physicians to monitor treatment efficacy in real time and provide timely interventions. Furthermore, patients should persist in daily weight tracking to improve their condition at its source through scientific weight management. Ultimately, these measures will collectively build a convenient, smart, and efficient closed-loop home self-management system, thereby impacting patients’ eHealth literacy.

Research32 indicates that telemedicine helps integrate and optimize healthcare resources, provides guidance for patients’ home-based self-management, and enhances the continuity of medical services. This, in turn, improves patients’ treatment adherence and satisfaction. Consequently, these findings underscore the necessity for targeted interventions to bolster self-management abilities in OSAHS patients.

Conclusion

In conclusion, patients with OSAHS demonstrate relatively low levels of eHealth literacy. The key influencing factors include education level, time spent searching for health information online, information needs, information sources, barriers to accessing information, social support, and life management. Given that the Internet has become the primary source of health information due to its accessibility and vast resources, it is imperative for healthcare institutions and professionals to prioritize eHealth literacy. Comprehensive strategies tailored to these influencing factors should be developed to optimize nursing practices and foster eHealth literacy among OSAHS patients. However, this study has certain limitations. First, all variables were collected through self-administered questionnaires at a single time point, which may introduce common method bias and response bias. Second, the sample size was relatively small. Finally, due to the cross-sectional design of this study, no causal inferences can be drawn. Therefore, future research should employ longitudinal cohort designs with multiple time points and expand the sample size to further elucidate the factors influencing eHealth literacy in this population.
