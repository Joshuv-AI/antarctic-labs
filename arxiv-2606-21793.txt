Geometric Structures of Pseudo-Sonic Curves in Self-Similar Solutions of the Euler Equations for Potential Flow
Gui-Qiang G. Chen, Mikhail Feldman, Xin Gao, Wei Xiang

Abstract
. We are concerned with the geometric structures of pseudo-sonic curves in two-dimensional self-similar solutions for the Euler equations for potential flow, allowing for non-uniform supersonic states. Mathematically, the governing second-order potential flow equation is of mixed hyperbolic-elliptic type, with degeneracy occurring along the pseudo-sonic curve. In this paper, we develop rigorous analytical approaches to analyze the geometric structures of pseudo-sonic curves in such self-similar solutions. We first show that the pseudo-sonic curve is necessarily a circle if the pseudo-velocity at each point is a normal to the curve. We then analyze the general case in which the pseudo-velocity on the pseudo-sonic point is not a normal to the curve, and study the geometric properties of streamlines in a neighborhood of the pseudo-sonic curve. Next, we establish two theorems that provide sufficient conditions ensuring that the pseudo-velocity at a pseudo-sonic point is normal to the curve, under natural assumptions on the local behavior of the solution. These results yield a precise characterization of the geometry of pseudo-sonic curves. Finally, we apply the developed theory to the shock reflection-diffraction problem with non-uniform incoming flow. We prove that the pseudo-sonic curve must be an arc if the solution is a C 2 C^{2} -small perturbation, either in the pseudo-supersonic or pseudo-subsonic region, of a solution with uniform incoming flow (as constructed in [ 9 ] ). In particular, the density and velocity must be constant, corresponding to the radius (with a fixed power) and the center of the pseudo-sonic arc, respectively. Moreover, we prove that the solution is C 2 , α C^{2,\alpha} -regular in the pseudo-subsonic region up to the sonic arc (except at point P 1 P_{1} ). The techniques and ideas developed in this paper are expected to be applicable to other nonlinear problems involving similar mixed-type degeneracies.

Introduction

We are concerned with the geometric structures of pseudo-sonic curves
in two-dimensional (2-D) self-similar solutions of the Euler equations for potential flow,
allowing for non-uniform supersonic states.
Such self-similar solutions play a central role in many important applications,
including multidimensional (M-D) Riemann problems and shock reflection-diffraction problem.
They not only arise naturally in a wide range of physical experiments and applications, but also
are fundamental to the mathematical theory of M-D systems of conservation laws,
which remains largely unsolved.

2.
Self-Similar Potential Flow and Properties of the Pseudo-Sonic Curves

In this section, we present the pseudo-steady potential flow equation, introduce the pseudo-sonic curves, and state
the main theorems
concerning the geometric structures of pseudo-sonic curves
and the behavior of streamlines near pseudo-sonic points.

2.1.
Self-similar potential flow equation

Two-dimensional self-similar potential flow is governed by the conservation law of mass and the Bernoulli law
for the density
\rho
and the pseudo-velocity potential
\varphi
in the self-similar variables

(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

(
cf.

[
7
]
):

(2.1)

\displaystyle\di(\rho(|D\varphi|^{2},\varphi)D\varphi)+2\rho(|D\varphi|^{2},\varphi)=0,

with

(2.2)

\displaystyle\rho(|D\varphi|^{2},\varphi)=(K-\varphi-\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}},

where
K
is the Bernoulli constant and
the divergence
\di
and gradient
D
are with respect to
(\xi,\eta)
.
In addition,
(u,v)=D\varphi+(\xi,\eta)
is the velocity field and

c^{2}(\rho)=(\gamma-1)\rho^{\gamma-1}
is the sonic speed with
\gamma>1
.
Then the sonic speed can be expressed as

(2.3)

\displaystyle c^{2}=c^{2}(|D\varphi|^{2},\varphi)=(\gamma-1)\big(K-\varphi-\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2}\big).

Moreover, (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) can be rewritten as in the following form:

(2.4)

\displaystyle(c^{2}-U^{2})u_{\xi}-UV(u_{\eta}+v_{\xi})+(c^{2}-V^{2})v_{\eta}=0,

(2.5)

\displaystyle u_{\eta}=v_{\xi},

which is closed with the Bernoulli law:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}}{\gamma-1}+\frac{U^{2}+V^{2}}{2}+\varphi=K,

where the pseudo-velocity
(U,V)
satisfying

(2.7)

\displaystyle U:=u-\xi=\varphi_{\xi},\quad V:=v-\eta=\varphi_{\eta}.

Equation (
2.1
) is a nonlinear equation of mixed elliptic-hyperbolic type.
It is elliptic (
i.e.
, subsonic) if and only if

(2.8)

\displaystyle|D\varphi|<c(|D\varphi|^{2},\varphi),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle|D\varphi|<c_{*}(\varphi):=\sqrt{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}(K-\varphi)}.

It is hyperbolic (
i.e.
, supersonic) if and only if

(2.9)

\displaystyle|D\varphi|>c(|D\varphi|^{2},\varphi).

The points at which

(2.10)

\displaystyle|D\varphi|=c(|D\varphi|^{2},\varphi)

are called pseudo-sonic points.
Through this paper, we assume that the set of all pseudo-sonic points forms a curve, called the pseudo-sonic curve.
The relative interior of the pseudo-sonic curve is denoted
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
:

(2.11)

\Gamma_{\rm sonic}=\Gamma_{\rm sonic}^{0}.

We sometimes write
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}^{0}
to emphasize that only interior points are under consideration.

Denote by
\Omega^{-}
and
\Omega^{+}
the pseudo-supersonic region and the pseudo-subsonic region
of a solution
\varphi
, respectively.
Assume that there exist solutions
\varphi^{-}
and
\varphi^{+}

to equations (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) in
\Omega^{-}
and
\Omega^{+}

respectively (see Fig.
2.1
) such that

(i).

In
\Omega^{-}
,
\varphi^{-}
is a pseudo-supersonic solution and satisfies
equations (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) with

(2.12)

\displaystyle\varphi^{-}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega^{-}}),\quad\partial_{\xi}\varphi^{-}=U_{-},\quad\partial_{\eta}\varphi^{-}=V_{-};

(ii).

In
\Omega^{+}
,
\varphi^{+}
is a pseudo-subsonic solution and satisfies
equations (
2.1
)–(
2.2
)
with

(2.13)

\displaystyle\varphi^{+}\in C^{2}(\Omega^{+}\cup\Gamma_{\rm sonic}^{0}),\quad\partial_{\xi}\varphi^{+}=U_{+},\quad\partial_{\eta}\varphi^{+}=V_{+}.

(iii).

On the pseudo-sonic curve
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
, the following continuity conditions hold:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\varphi^{+}=\varphi^{-},\quad D\varphi^{+}=D\varphi^{-}\qquad\text{on $\Gamma_{\rm sonic}$}.

(\xi,\eta)
–coordinates.

2.2.
Geometric structures of pseudo-sonic curves

The first property concerns the case in which all pseudo-sonic points are exceptional
(see Definition
2.1
below).
Locally,
i.e.
, in a neighborhood of each fixed pseudo-sonic point
Q\in\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
,
assume that one can choose suitable
(\xi,\eta)
–coordinates such that there exists a constant
r>0

for which the pseudo-sonic curve
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
is represented by

(2.15)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm sonic}\cap B_{r}(Q):=\big\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\eta=f(\xi)\in C^{1,1}((l_{1},l_{2}))\big\}\cap B_{r}(Q),

where
l_{1}<l_{2}
are real numbers and
B_{r}(Q)
denotes the ball centered at

We then introduce the notion of exceptional points for self-similar potential flows.

Definition 2.1
.

For

2.15

(U,V)(\xi,f(\xi))
is the normal vector
to
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
at point
(\xi,f(\xi))

Let

(2.16)

\displaystyle\partial^{\perp}:=V\partial_{\xi}-U\partial_{\eta},\quad\partial_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:=\partial_{\xi}+f^{\prime}(\xi)\partial_{\eta},\quad\partial_{\mathbf{n}}:=f^{\prime}(\xi)\partial_{\xi}-\partial_{\eta}.

Then we have the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that (
2.12
)–(
2.15
) hold and that the sonic curve is connected. Then

(i)

A pseudo-sonic point
Q
is exceptional if and only if
{\partial}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}c(Q)=0
or
c(Q)=0
.

(ii)

If all pseudo-sonic points are exceptional and the pseudo-sonic speed satisfies
c>0
on the pseudo-sonic curve,
then
c=c_{2}
on the pseudo-sonic curve for some constant
c_{2}
, and the pseudo-sonic curve lies on a circle of radius
c_{2}
.

(iii)

There exists a local solution whose pseudo-sonic curve lies on a circle, and all its sonic points are non-exceptional.

.

By

, if all pseudo-sonic points are exceptional and
c>0
on
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
,
then the pseudo-sonic speed satisfies

on
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
for some constant
c_{2}
,
and
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
lies on a circle.
As shown in

8.3
below, it then follows that,
on
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}

(U,V)=(u_{2}-\xi,v_{2}-\eta)\qquad\mbox{for some constants $(u_{2},v_{2})$}.

Consequently,
Theorem 3.1
in
[
1
, page 517]
can be applied.

In the next two theorems, we consider the case in which the pseudo-velocity is tangential to the pseudo-sonic curve.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that (
2.12
)–(
2.15
) hold and that
the pseudo-sonic curve is connected. Then

(i)

If the pseudo-velocity field
(U,V)
is tangential to the pseudo-sonic curve,
then
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
if and only if
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
is contained in a straight line.

(ii)

There exists a local solution whose pseudo-sonic curve contained in
a straight line such that

(U,V)|_{\Gamma_{\rm sonic}}
is not tangential to the pseudo-sonic line and
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c\neq 0
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that (
2.12
)–(
2.15
) hold and that the sonic curve is connected.
If the pseudo-velocity field
(U,V)
is tangential to the pseudo-sonic curve
and
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c\neq 0
, then the pseudo-sonic curve is strictly convex
when viewed from the pseudo-supersonic domain
\Omega^{-}
.

Next, assume that, in a suitable local coordinate system,
the pseudo-streamlines are represented by

\eta=F(\xi)\in C^{2}((b_{1},b_{2})),

where
b_{1}<b_{2}
are real numbers.
Then we have the following result concerning the geometric structure of pseudo-streamlines.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that (
2.12
)–(
2.15
) hold.
Let
Q
be a pseudo-sonic point with
U(Q)\neq 0
. Then

(i)

If
\partial^{\perp}c(Q)=0
, then
F^{\prime\prime}(Q)=0
.

(ii)

If
\partial^{\perp}c(Q)\neq 0
and
F
can be defined (or extended) on the entire real line
so that
\Omega^{-}\subset\{\eta>F(\xi)\}
, then
F^{\prime\prime}(Q)>0
.

The final two theorems show that a pseudo-sonic point must be exceptional,
whenever the solution satisfies certain natural estimates near
\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
.
For any fixed pseudo-sonic point
Q\in\Gamma_{\rm sonic}
, set
Q:=(\xi_{*},\eta_{*})
with
\eta_{*}=f(\xi_{*})
.
Let
\varphi_{2}^{Q}
be the pseudo-velocity potential with a constant velocity
(u_{2}^{Q},v_{2}^{Q})=D\varphi(Q)+(\xi_{*},\eta_{*})

and satisfying
\varphi_{2}^{Q}(Q)=\varphi(Q)

so that

(2.17)

\varphi_{2}^{Q}(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\big((\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})-(\xi_{*}^{2}+\eta_{*}^{2})\big)+u_{2}^{Q}(\xi-\xi_{*})+v_{2}^{Q}(\eta-\eta_{*})+\varphi(Q).

Furthermore, let
(U,V)(Q):=(c\cos\sigma_{*},c\sin\sigma_{*})

and define

(2.18)

\displaystyle\psi^{Q}:=\varphi-\varphi_{2}^{Q}.

We now state the following two theorems:

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that (
2.12
)–(
2.15
) hold.
Let
{\psi}^{Q}
be defined as in (
2.18
).
Let

\mathbf{n}_{Q}=\frac{(f^{\prime}(\xi_{*}),-1)}{\sqrt{1+(f^{\prime}(\xi_{*}))^{2}}},\qquad\,\,\boldsymbol{\tau}_{Q}=\frac{(1,f^{\prime}(\xi_{*}))}{\sqrt{1+(f^{\prime}(\xi_{*}))^{2}}}

denote the unit normal and unit tangential vectors to the pseudo-sonic curve at
Q
, respectively.
Suppose that
{\partial}^{\perp}c(Q)=0
.
If there exist a constant
a_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{\gamma+1})

and a sufficiently small constant
\varepsilon>0
such that
either

(2.19)

\displaystyle\psi^{Q}(Q+t\mathbf{n}_{Q})<a_{1}(\frac{\mathbf{n}_{Q}\cdot(U,V)(Q)}{c(Q)})^{2}t^{2}\qquad\,\mbox{for either $t\in(0,\varepsilon)$ or $t\in(-\varepsilon,0)$},

or

(2.20)

\displaystyle\psi^{Q}(Q+s\boldsymbol{\tau}_{Q})<a_{1}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{Q}\cdot(U,V)(Q)}{c(Q)})^{2}s^{2}\qquad\,\mbox{for either $s\in(0,\varepsilon)$ or $s\in(-\varepsilon,0)$},

then the pseudo-sonic point
Q
is exceptional.

.

For a small perturbation problem of the regular shock reflection,

Therefore, condition (

Remark 2.3

Based on the proof of
