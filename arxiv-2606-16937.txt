Abstract
. We provide examples of spaces satisfying generalized combinatorial covering properties such as the Hurewicz, Menger, and γ \gamma -properties in an uncountable setting. Our approach is motivated by canonical constructions from the classical countable case, including the examples of Bartoszyński and Shelah separating the Hurewicz property from σ \sigma -compactness, the examples of Tsaban and Zdomskyy separating the Hurewicz and Menger properties, and Tsaban’s construction of a nontrivial set of reals with the γ \gamma -property. We focus on the genuinely nontrivial aspects of these higher-cardinal generalizations, uncovering several open problems whose nature appears substantially different from that of their countable counterparts.

The primary objects of investigation are

These properties have also found applications in several areas, including forcing

During numerous conferences devoted to topology and set theory, we were repeatedly asked whether these properties admit meaningful generalizations to an uncountable setting, where the Cantor cube
{2^{\omega}}
is replaced by its natural counterpart
2^{\kappa}
for an uncountable cardinal
\kappa

Formulating the corresponding definitions presents little difficulty, and the first substantial progress in this direction was made by Korch–Weiss
[
18
]
.
However, the construction of genuinely nontrivial examples of spaces with generalized covering properties by combinatorial methods in
2^{\kappa}
seems to have received comparatively little attention.
This is somewhat surprising, as decades of research in the classical countable setting have produced a rich collection of elegant, albeit often highly nontrivial, constructions and examples.
Often, generalizations from the countable to the uncountable setting are essentially devoid of new mathematical content.
This happens when the proofs require no genuinely new ideas and amount merely to replacing
\omega
by an uncountable cardinal
\kappa
in classical arguments.
Generalized combinatorial covering properties are easy to formulate, and their combinatorial characterizations are frequently straightforward adaptations of the countable case.
Our goal was to avoid such trivialities.
Accordingly, whenever an argument is merely a routine modification of a known proof, we omit the details.
Instead, we focus on genuinely nontrivial examples.
Although the underlying intuition often originates from classical constructions, obtaining the desired results requires new ingredients and considerably more delicate arguments.
In several cases, such as the
\kappa
-Hurewicz Conjecture (see Section
3
), this approach leads to highly nontrivial and apparently difficult open questions.

2.
Galvin–Miller Lemma

One of the standard tools used in constructions in classical case is a technical Lemma due to Galvin–Miller
[
13
, Lemma 1.2]
.
The importance of this lemma suggests that it deserves for a separate section.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
f\in\kappa^{\kappa}
is

•

strictly increasing
if for all
\alpha<\beta<\kappa

we have
f(\alpha)<f(\beta)
,

•

continuous
if for any
limit ordinal
\alpha<\kappa
we have

f(\alpha)=\sup_{\beta<\alpha}f(\beta)
.

•

normal
if it is strictly increasing and continuous.

Remark 2.2
.

Observe that if
f\in\kappa^{\kappa}
is normal then

\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}[f(\alpha),f(\alpha+1))=[f(0),\kappa).

Define
\mathsf{Q}_{\kappa}:=[\kappa]^{<\kappa}
.

Lemma 2.3

(Galvin–Miller
[
13
, Lemma 1.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}\in\Omega_{\kappa}(\mathsf{Q}_{\kappa})
be a family of open subsets of
\operatorname{P}(\kappa)
. Then
there are a normal function
a\in\kappa^{\kappa}
and a sequence
{\langle\,U_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\,\rangle}
of pairwise distinct sets from
\mathcal{U}

such that for every
x\in\operatorname{P}(\kappa)
and
\alpha<\kappa
:

(1)

\text{If }x\cap[a(\alpha),a(\alpha+1))=\emptyset,\text{ then }x\in U_{\alpha}.

In our proof we need the following rather easy fact.

Fact 2.4
.

Let
X
be a space with
\left|X\right|\geq\kappa
and
\mathcal{U}
be an
\omega_{\kappa}
-cover of
X
.
Then for each set
A\subseteq X
with
\left|A\right|<\kappa
, there are
\kappa
many sets
U\in\mathcal{U}
such that
A\subseteq\mathcal{U}
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Let
a(0):=0
and
U_{0}\in\mathcal{U}
be a set such that
\operatorname{P}([0,a(0)))=\{\emptyset\}\subseteq U_{0}
.
Fix an ordinal number
\alpha<\kappa
and assume that
a(\alpha)<\kappa
and sets
U_{\beta}\in\mathcal{U}
have been defined for all
\beta<\alpha
such that for any limit ordinal
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
we have
a(\alpha^{\prime})=\sup_{\beta<\alpha^{\prime}}a(\beta)
.

Define
a(\alpha+1)
and
U_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{U}
as follows.
Since
\kappa
is strongly inaccessible, we have
\left|\operatorname{P}([0,a(\alpha)))\right|<\kappa
.
By Lemma
2.4
, each subset of
\mathsf{Q}_{\kappa}
with cardinality less than
\kappa
, is contained in
\kappa
many
elements of
\mathcal{U}
, thus there is a set

U_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{U}\setminus\{\,U_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}\quad\text{with}\quad\operatorname{P}([0,a(\alpha)))\subseteq U_{\alpha}.

Fix
s\in\operatorname{P}([0,a(\alpha)))
.
Since
s\in U_{\alpha}
and the set
U_{\alpha}
is open in
\operatorname{P}(\kappa)
, there is
a(\alpha)<\alpha_{s}<\kappa
such that

B_{s}:=\{\,s\cup t:t\in\operatorname{P}([\alpha_{s},\kappa))\,\}\subseteq U_{\alpha},

the set
B_{s}
is an open basic neighborhood of
s
in
\operatorname{P}(\kappa)
.
Let

a(\alpha+1):=\sup_{s\in\operatorname{P}([0,a(\alpha)))}\alpha_{s}.

Since
\kappa
is regular, we have
a(\alpha+1)<\kappa
.

Fix
x\in\operatorname{P}(\kappa)
such that
x\cap[a(\alpha),a(\alpha+1))=\emptyset
.
Then for
s:=x\cap[0,a(\alpha))
, we have
x\in B_{s}\subseteq U_{\alpha}
.
∎

The Galvin–Miller Lemma has served as a sharp tool in many constructions related to products of spaces in the countable case
[
32
,
31
,
22
]
.
We expect that the above modification will also play an important role when considering products of spaces in the uncountable setting.

3.
\kappa
-Hurewicz Conjecture

Hurewicz’s Conjecture asserts that, in the class of metrizable spaces,
\sigma
-compactness is equivalent to the Hurewicz property.
This Conjecture was refuted by Just–Miller–Scheeprs–Szeptycki
[
29
]
(using a dichotomic proof) and then by Bartoszyński–Shelah
[
4
]
(providing a
\mathsf{ZFC}

The counterexample of Bartoszyński–Shelah is a starting point for our canonical examples.
We need the following notions.
For functions
a,b\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
we write
a\leq^{*}b
, if the set

\{\,n\in\omega:a(n)>b(n)\,\}
is finite.
A set
A\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is
bounded
, if there is a function
b\in{\omega^{\omega}}
such that
a\leq^{*}b
for all
a\in A
.
Let
\mathfrak{b}
be the minimal cardinality of a subset of
{[\omega]^{\omega}}

unbounded
, i.e., it is not bounded,
A set

is a
\mathfrak{b}
-scale
if
X
is unbounded and
x_{\alpha}\leq^{*}x_{\beta}
for all
\alpha<\beta<\mathfrak{b}
.
A
\mathfrak{b}
-scale exists in
\mathsf{ZFC}
.
Let
\mathrm{Fin}:=[\omega]^{<\omega}
.
A set of the form

, where

is a
\mathfrak{b}
-scale is called a
\mathfrak{b}
-scale set

Bartoszyński–Shelah
[
4
]

For functions

A set

b\in[\kappa]^{\kappa}

.
Let
\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}

Lemma 3.3

\operatorname{non}(\mathsf{S}_{1}^{\kappa}(\Gamma_{\kappa},\Gamma_{\kappa})=\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}

4.
\kappa
-Hurewicz Problem

The original Hurewicz Problem asks whether in
\mathsf{ZFC}
, there is a Menger set of reals which is not Hurewicz
[
16
]
.
It was first solved by Chaber–Pol
[
7
]
(using a dychotomic proof) and then by Tsaban–Zdomskyy
[
35
]
(a
\mathsf{ZFC}
uniform construction).
We briefly describe the latter construction here.
A set
A\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is
dominating
, if for each function
x\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
, there is a function
a\in A
with
x\leq^{*}a
.
Let
\mathfrak{d}
be the minimal cardinality of a dominating set in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
.
Let
\{\,d_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{d}\,\}
be a dominating set in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
.
For
a,b\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
, we write
a\leq^{\infty}b
if the set
\{\,n:a(n)\leq b(n)\,\}
is infinite.
For
x\in{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
by
x^{\text{\tt c}}
we denote the set
\omega\setminus x
. By
[\omega]^{\omega,\omega}
we denote the family of all
x\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
such that
x^{\text{\tt c}}\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
.
In
\mathsf{ZFC}
, there is a set
X=\{\,x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{d}\,\}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega,\omega}
such that

\{\,d_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}\leq^{\infty}x_{\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{\alpha}\leq^{\infty}x_{\alpha}^{\text{\tt c}}

for all
\alpha<\mathfrak{d}
.
Then the set
X\cup\mathrm{Fin}
is Menger but its homeomorphic image

\{\,x^{\text{\tt c}}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{d}\,\}\cup\{\,x^{\text{\tt c}}:x\in\mathrm{Fin}\,\}

in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is unbounded, and thus the set
X\cup\mathrm{Fin}
is not Hurewicz.

Adjusting the Hurewicz Problem to our context we formulate the following question.

Problem 4.1

(
\kappa
-Hurewicz Problem)

Assume that
\kappa
is a weakly compact cardinal number.
Is there a
\kappa
-Menger subspace of
\operatorname{P}(\kappa)
which is not
\kappa
-Hurewicz?

A set
A\subseteq[\kappa]^{\kappa}
is
dominating
if for each function
x\in[\kappa]^{\kappa}
, there is a function
a\in A
such that
x\leq_{\kappa}^{*}a
.
Let
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}
be the minimal cardinality of a dominating set in
[\kappa]^{\kappa}
.
A zero-dimensional metrizable space

is Menger if and only if
X
is Lindelöf and each continuous image of
X
into
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is not dominating
[
26
, Proposition 3]
.
Basically, the same approach allows to get the following characterization of

-Menger spaces.
Since a proof has a one-to-one correspondence to the classical one, it is omitted.

Proposition 4.2

Let

be a zero-dimensional

-space.
The space

is

-Menger if and only if

is
\kappa
-Lindelöf and each

\operatorname{non}(\kappa\text{-Menger})=\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}

Our starting point is a combination of Tsaban’s streamlined version of the Tsaban–Zdomskyy construction

5.
\kappa
-Menger Conjecture

The original Menger Conjecture asserts that in the class of metrizable spaces,
\sigma
-compactness is equivalent to the Menger property.
This conjecture was refuted firstly, by Fremlin–Miller
[
11
]
(using a dichotomic proof) and then by Bartoszyński–Tsaban
[
5
]
(a uniform
\mathsf{ZFC}
construction).
A counterexample provided by Bartoszyński–Tsaban has the following form. Let
\{\,d_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{d}\,\}
be a dominating set in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
.
Then there is a set
X=\{\,x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{d}\,\}\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
such that

\{\,d_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}\leq^{\infty}x_{\alpha}

for all
\alpha<\mathfrak{d}
.
Then the set
X\cup\mathrm{Fin}
is Menger but it is
\mathfrak{d}
-concentrated on
\mathrm{Fin}
, and thus it does not contain an uncountable compact sets.
Consequently, it is not
\sigma
-compact.
Of course, we can formulate also an analogue of the Menger Conjecture.

Conjecture 5.1

(
\kappa
-Menger Conjecture)
.

Assume that
\kappa
is a weakly compact cardinal.
A subspace of
\operatorname{P}(\kappa)
is a
K_{\kappa}
-space if and only if it is
\kappa
-Menger.

A counterpart of the construction due to Bartoszyński–Tsaban can be achieved easily by transfinite induction also in the uncountable case.
However, a resulting set
X\cup\mathsf{Q}_{\kappa}
is
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}
-concentrated on
\mathsf{Q}_{\kappa}
(and thus
\kappa
-Menger), but as it was already pointed out in the case of the
\kappa
-Hurewicz Conjecture, it does not provide that it is not
K_{\kappa}
.
The main result from the previous section provides a counterexample for the
\kappa
-Menger Conjecture, and we are not aware of any direct (not mentioning the
\kappa
-Hurewicz property) way to construct a
\kappa
-Menger non
K_{\kappa}
-space in
\mathsf{ZFC}
.

many
\beta
.
Thus for any function
h\in{\kappa^{\uparrow\kappa}}

such that
g\leq_{\kappa}h
we have

\{\,U_{\xi,h(\xi)}:\xi<\kappa\,\}\in\Gamma_{\kappa}(X^{\prime}\setminus X_{\gamma})
.

For each
x\in X_{\gamma}
and each
\delta<\kappa
we define

f_{x}(\delta)\coloneqq\min{\left\{\,\beta<\kappa:x\in\bigcap_{\xi\geq\beta}U_{\delta,\xi}\,\right\}}

if the set is nonempty and
f_{x}(\delta)=0
otherwise.
Note that if
\nu<\gamma
, then
\nu<\gamma_{\delta}
for some
\delta<\kappa

\beta<\kappa
with

Since
\left|X_{\gamma}\right|<\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}
, there
is

such that

is an upper bound of

with respect to
\leq_{\kappa}^{*}
and the sets

\mathsf{S}_{1}^{\kappa}(\Gamma_{\kappa},\Gamma_{\kappa})

First we recall an example of a non-trivial set of reals with the classical
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Gamma)

[
23
]
, where
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Gamma)
is the property
\mathsf{S}_{1}^{\omega}(\Gamma_{\omega},\Gamma_{\omega})
.
If
X\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is an unbounded tower of cardinality
\mathfrak{b}
, then
X\cup\mathrm{Fin}
satisfies

\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Gamma)
.
An unbounded tower of cardinality
\mathfrak{b}
exists for example, if
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{b}
or
\mathfrak{b}<\mathfrak{d}

[
23
]

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
X
be an unbounded
\kappa
-tower of size
\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}
.
Then
X\cup\mathsf{Q}_{\kappa}
satisfies
\mathsf{S}_{1}^{\kappa}(\Gamma_{\kappa},\Gamma_{\kappa})
.

Proof.

Let
X=\{\,x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}\,\}
and

X_{\alpha}:=\{\,x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}\cup\mathsf{Q}_{\kappa}
for all
\alpha<\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}

Let
{\langle\,\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\,\rangle}
be a sequence of
\gamma_{\kappa}
-covers of

which are open in
\operatorname{P}(\kappa)
.
Fix
\alpha<\kappa
.
Applying Lemma
2.3
to the family
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
, we get a function
a_{\alpha}\in[\kappa]^{\kappa}
and a sequence

{\langle\,U^{(\alpha)}_{\xi}:\xi<\kappa\,\rangle}
of sets from
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
such that for every
x\in\operatorname{P}(\kappa)
and
\xi<\kappa

\text{If }x\cap[a_{\alpha}(\xi),a_{\alpha}(\xi+1))=\emptyset,\text{ then }x\in U_{\xi}^{(\alpha)}.

By Lemma
6.12
, for each
\alpha<\kappa
, there is
\delta_{\alpha}<\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}
such that the set

I_{\alpha}^{\prime}:=\{\,\xi<\kappa:x_{\delta_{\alpha}}\cap[a_{\alpha}(\xi),a_{\alpha}(\xi+1))=\emptyset\,\}

has size
\kappa
.
Let

\delta:=\sup_{\alpha<\kappa}\delta_{\alpha}
. Since
\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}
is regular, it follows that
\delta<\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}
. Furthermore for each
\alpha<\kappa
we have that

x_{\delta}\cap[a_{\alpha}(\xi),a_{\alpha}(\xi+1))\subseteq x_{\delta_{\alpha}}\cap[a_{\alpha}(\xi),a_{\alpha}(\xi+1))

for all but less than
\kappa
many

\xi<\kappa
.
Thus for each
\alpha<\kappa
and all but less than
\kappa
many
\xi\in I_{\alpha}^{\prime}
we have

x_{\delta}\cap[a_{\alpha}(\xi),a_{\alpha}(\xi+1))=\emptyset.

It follows that the sets

I_{\alpha}:=\{\,\xi<\kappa:x_{\delta}\cap[a_{\alpha}(\xi),a_{\alpha}(\xi+1))=\emptyset\,\}

have size
\kappa
for all
\alpha<\kappa
.

Since
\left|X_{\delta}\right|<\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}
,
there is a function
f\in[\kappa]^{\kappa}

such that for any function
g\in[\kappa]^{\kappa}
with

, we have

{\bigl\{\,U^{(\alpha)}_{g(\alpha)}:\alpha<\kappa\,\bigr\}}\in\Gamma_{\kappa}(X_{\delta}).

Take a function
g\in[\kappa]^{\kappa}\cap\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}I_{\alpha}
with
f\leq g
.

Fix
x\in X\setminus X_{\delta}
.
Since
x\subseteq^{*}_{\kappa}x_{\delta}
, there is
\tilde{\alpha}<\kappa
such that

x\cap[\tilde{\alpha},\kappa)\subseteq x_{\delta}\cap[\tilde{\alpha},\kappa).

Fix
\alpha
with
\tilde{\alpha}\leq\alpha<\kappa
.
Since
g(\alpha)\in I_{\alpha}
, we have

x\cap[a_{\alpha}(g(\alpha)),a_{\alpha}(g(\alpha)+1))\subseteq x_{\delta}\cap[a_{\alpha}(g(\alpha)),a_{\alpha}(g(\alpha)+1))=\emptyset,

and thus

We conclude that

\{\,U^{(\alpha)}_{g(\alpha)}:\alpha<\kappa\,\}\in\Gamma_{\kappa}(X\cup\mathsf{Q}_{\kappa}).\qed

\kappa
-tower of size

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}<\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}
.
Then there is an unbounded
\kappa
-tower of
size
\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}
.

Proof.

Let

be a

\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}
-scale.

\leq_{\kappa}^{*}
-
