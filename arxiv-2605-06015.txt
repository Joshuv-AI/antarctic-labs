Distribution of spin norm along pencils: the S ​ p ​ ( p , q ) Sp(p,q) case
Chao-Ping Dong, Zhan Ying

Abstract
. As a sequel to [ 2 ] and Theorem C of [ 3 ] , this paper shows that for S ​ p ​ ( p , q ) Sp(p,q) , the spin norm strictly increases along any Vogan pencil once it goes beyond the unitarily small convex hull.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we fix
G
as
Sp(p,q)
, which is
not
Hermitian symmetric and has a maximal compact subgroup
K:=Sp(p)\times Sp(q)
. We assume that
p\leq q
and put
n:=p+q
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.,
\mathfrak{k}
) be the complexified Lie algebra of
G
(resp.,
K
). Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}+\mathfrak{p}
be the Cartan decomposition. By a
K
-type, we mean an irreducible representation of
K
.
We may and we will identify a
K
-type with its highest weight. For example,
\mathfrak{p}
is a
K
-type with highest weight
\beta
. Let
T
be a maximal torus of
K
. Fix

(1)

\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}):=\{e_{i}\pm e_{j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq p\mbox{ or }p+1\leq i<j\leq n\}\cup\{2e_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}.

Let
\pi
be an infinite-dimensional irreducible representation of
G
.
A result of Vogan in
[
7
]
says that the
K
-types of
\pi
is a union of
pencils
:

(2)

P(\mu):=\{\mu+m\beta\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

Here
\mu
is the highest weight of a
K
-type. The
unitarily small convex hull
was introduced by Salamanca-Riba and Vogan in
[
6
]
. It will be recalled in Section
2
. We say
\mu
is
u-small
if it lies within the unitarily small convex hull; otherwise, we say
\mu
is
u-large
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
Sp(p,q)
, the
spin norm increases strictly along any pencil once it goes beyond
the u-small convex hull. Namely, for any u-large weight
\mu
such
that
\mu-\beta
is dominant, we have

(3)

\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{spin}}>\|\mu-\beta\|_{\mathrm{spin}}.

The notion spin norm was introduced in
[
1
]
. It will be recalled in Section
2
. Let us explain motivation of the above theorem. Now assume that
\pi
is
unitary
. An ongoing research topic is the
Dirac cohomology

H_{D}(\pi)
. See
[
8
,
4
]
and references therein for necessary background on Dirac cohomology. To compute
H_{D}(\pi)
, Theorem
1.1
helps one to reduce
K
-types along any pencil
P(\mu)
of
\pi
. Indeed, if
\mu
is u-large, it suffices to consider
\mu
itself; otherwise, it suffices to consider the finitely many u-small
K
-types among
P(\mu)
.

The analogue of Theorem
1.1
has been obtained for complex Lie groups in
[
2
]
and for
SL(n,\mathbb{R})
,
SL(n,\mathbb{H})
and all non-Hermitian symmetric exceptional simple Lie groups in Theorem C of
[
3
]
. Since 2018, the first named author has kept trying to attain Theorem
1.1
but in vain. We realized that it was more difficult in the
Sp(p,q)
case since the unitarily small convex hull became much more complicated. From a conventional point of view, this is a problem of linear programming or integer partition, and it is related to the arrangement of Young diagrams. Therefore, computationally, it is easy to verify the theorem for relatively small values of
p
and
q
. However, as
p
and
q
increase, the constraints increase rapidly to the extent that even computers cannot handle them. The spin norm is defined via a minimum. For our problem, one might generally consider how to attain this minimum. Nevertheless, in the discrete setting we are dealing with, attaining the minimum is extremely difficult. In this paper, we present a completely different approach that allows us to circumvent this difficulty, which may serve as a new technique for solving similar problems. We hope the current work could shed some light on dealing with the unitarily small convex hull.

The paper is organized as follows: Section
2
recalls necessary preliminaries. Section
3
proves Theorem
1.1
.

2.2.
spin norm

Notice that
\mathfrak{k}
has no center, we have

\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})=\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\cup\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}).

Let

is the dominant Weyl chamber corresponding to
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
. Denote by
C_{\mathfrak{g}}
the closed Weyl chamber corresponding to
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
. Then
C_{\mathfrak{g}}
is contained in
C
. Define

W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}=\{w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})\mid w(C_{\mathfrak{g}})\subset C\}.

As in
[
3
]

\{w\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})\mid w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}\}

are exactly all the choices of positive roots systems for
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
containing
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
. Let us enumerate the elements of
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
as
w^{(0)}=e,\dots,w^{(s-1)}
; the enumeration will specified in Section
2.5
. For
0\leq\ell\leq s-1
, put

(\Delta^{+})^{(\ell)}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})=w^{(\ell)}\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}),\quad(\Delta^{+})^{(\ell)}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})=\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\cup w^{(\ell)}\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}).

Denote by
\rho_{n}^{(\ell)}
the half sum of the positive roots in
(\Delta^{+})^{(\ell)}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})
. Then we have

w^{(\ell)}\rho=\rho_{c}+\rho_{n}^{(\ell)},\quad 0\leq\ell\leq s-1.

Now as in
[
1
]
, the spin norm of a
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
-dominat weight
\nu
is defined to be

\|\nu\|_{\mathrm{spin}}:=\min_{0\leq\ell\leq s-1}\|\{\nu-\rho_{n}^{(\ell)}\}+\rho_{c}\|.

The Weyl group
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
acts by permutations and sign changes, and that a weight is
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
-dominat if and only if both its first
p
coordinates and its last
q
coordinates form a weakly decreasing sequence. For
i<j
, let

s_{i,j}=s_{e_{i}-e_{i+1}}s_{e_{i+1}-e_{i+2}}\cdots s_{e_{j-1}-e_{j}}.

We make the convention that
s_{i,i}=e
. Then

W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}=\left\{s_{p,i_{p}}s_{p-1,i_{p-1}}\cdots s_{2,i_{2}}s_{1,i_{1}}\mid 1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n\right\},

In particular,
i_{k-1}+1\leq i_{k}\leq q+k
, where
i_{0}:=0
. Thus
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
has cardinality

s:=\dbinom{n}{p}
.

2.3.
u-small
K
-types

The
unitarily small convex hull
was defined by Salamanca-Riba and Vogan in
[
6
]
as

R(\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})):=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})}b_{\alpha}\alpha\mid 0\leq b_{\alpha}\leq 1\right\}.

The following result essentially comes from Theorem 6.7 of
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Lemma 4.4 of
[
3
]
)

Let
\nu
be any
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
-dominat weight. Then
\nu
is u-small if and only if
\langle\nu+2\rho_{c},w\xi_{i}\rangle\leq 2\langle\rho,\xi_{i}\rangle
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and all
w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
.

From now on, we always use

(4)

\mu=(a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\mid b_{1},\dots,b_{q})

to denote a
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
-dominant weight consisting of non-negative integers.
By the above lemma, we obtain a more explicit criterion for
\mu
being u-large.

Proposition 2.2
.

A weight
\mu
in the form of (
4
) is u-large if and only if there exist
0\leq f\leq p
and
0\leq g\leq q
such that

(5)

\sum_{i=1}^{f}a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{g}b_{j}>2pq-2(p-f)(q-g).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\mu
is u-small if and only if
\langle\mu+2\rho_{c},w\xi_{i}\rangle\leq 2\langle\rho,\xi_{i}\rangle
for all
w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
and all
1\leq i\leq n
. Taking into account the action of
w
on weights, the latter condition is equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{f}\big(a_{i}+(p+1-i)\big)+\sum_{j=1}^{g}\big(b_{j}+(q+1-j)\big)\leq 2\sum_{t=1}^{f+g}(n+1-t),

where
0\leq f\leq p
and
0\leq g\leq q
. The above inequality is further equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{f}a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{g}b_{j}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\sum_{t=1}^{f+g}(n+1-t)-\sum_{i=1}^{f}(p+1-i)-\sum_{j=1}^{g}(q+1-j)\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{f}q+\sum_{j=1}^{g}(p-f)\right)

\displaystyle=2(fq+gp)-2fg

\displaystyle=2pq-2(p-f)(q-g).

The lemma follows.
∎

Let
\mu
be as in (
4
).
For
0\leq f\leq p
and
0\leq g\leq q
, put

\mu_{f,g}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{f},q-g,\dots,q-g\mid b_{1},\dots,b_{g},p-f,\dots,p-f).

Let

This shows that

for all

2.4.
Domination

For real vectors
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
and
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
, we say that
u

dominates

v
if
u_{i}\geq v_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and strict inequality happens for at least one
i
. In this case, we write
u\gg v
. We write
\{u\}
for the vector obtained by rearranging the absolute values of the coordinates of
u
in decreasing order. Let
e_{i}
denote the
n
-dimensional vector whose entries are all zero except that the
i
-th entry equals
1
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be consisting of integer entries. Then, if
x_{s}\geq 0
for some
1\leq s\leq n
, one has
\{x+e_{s}\}\gg\{x\}
. If
x_{s}\leq 0
, then
\{x-e_{s}\}\gg\{x\}
.

Proof.

For the first assertion, consider the multiset

K=\{x_{j}\mid|x_{j}|>|x_{s}|\},\quad T=\{x_{j}\mid|x_{j}|\leq|x_{s}|,j\neq s\}.

Arrange the elements of
K
and
T
in decreasing order and write them as
k_{1},\dots,k_{l}
and
t_{1},\dots,t_{r}
, respectively. Then

\{x+e_{t}\}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{l},a_{s}+1,t_{1},\dots,t_{r}),\quad\{x\}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{l},a_{s},t_{1},\dots,t_{r}).

The first assertion follows. The second assertion can be proven similarly.
∎

Let
\nu
denote an
n

-value
of

as follows

Clearly, for any

\|\nu_{1}\|_{\mathfrak{k}}>\|\nu_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{k}}.

2.5.
The structure of
\rho_{n}^{(\ell)}

Recall for any
0\leq\ell\leq s-1
, we have

\rho_{n}^{(\ell)}:=w^{(\ell)}\rho-\rho_{c}.

Denote by
\Omega_{p,q}=\{\rho_{n}^{(\ell)}\mid 0\leq\ell\leq s-1\}
. This set consists of
(x_{1},\dots,x_{p}\mid y_{1},\dots,y_{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
satisfying the following conditions:

\bullet

q\geq x_{1}\geq\cdots\geq x_{p}\geq 0
,
p\geq y_{1}\geq\cdots\geq y_{q}\geq 0
;

\bullet

\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{q}y_{j}=pq
;

\bullet

y_{j}=\mathop{\hbox{card}}\nolimits\{i\mid q-x_{i}\geq j\}=\max\{i\mid q-x_{i}\geq j\}
for any
1\leq j\leq q
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Take any
(x_{1},\dots,x_{p}\mid y_{1},\dots,y_{q})\in\Omega_{p,q}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{f}x_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{g}y_{i}\leq fq+(p-f)g

for all
1\leq f\leq p
,
1\leq g\leq q
.

Proof.

Recall that
y_{j}=\mathop{\hbox{card}}\nolimits\{i\mid q-x_{i}\geq j\}
for
1\leq j\leq q
. Thus

\sum_{j=1}^{g}y_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\min(q-x_{i},g)

since each
1\leq i\leq p
contributes
1
to
y_{1},\dots,y_{\min(q-x_{i},g)}
. This shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{f}x_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{g}y_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{f}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\min(q-x_{i},g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{f}\big(x_{i}+\min(q-x_{i},g)\big)+\sum_{i=f+1}^{p}\min(q-x_{i},g)

\displaystyle\leq fq+(p-f)g.

∎

We can arrange the members of
\Omega_{p,q}
in the
lexicographic order
. Namely,
\rho_{n}^{(j)}
is ahead of
\rho_{n}^{(k)}
if the first nonzero coordinate of
\rho_{n}^{(j)}-\rho_{n}^{(k)}

\rho_{n}^{(0)}=(\underbrace{q,\dots,q}_{p}\mid 0,\dots,0),\quad\rho_{n}^{(s-1)}=(0,\dots,0\mid\underbrace{p,\dots,p}_{q}).

Correspondingly, we enumerate the elements of

as

, as previously agreed.
To simplify notation, we always denote

. By convention,

and
k_{p+1}=0
. Note that the last
