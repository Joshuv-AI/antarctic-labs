In this paper, the main series is

S_{1}(q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n}\,(-q^{n+1};q)_{\infty}^{2}.

Thus
S_{1}(q)
is the generating function for the number of two-color partitions into distinct
parts whose smallest part occurs in one prescribed color only, while every larger part may occur in either color or in both colors.
The constant term counts the empty partition.
The same series also appears in Andrews’ work on concave and convex compositions
[
4
]
.
Indeed, if
V_{d}(n)
denotes the number of strictly concave compositions of
n
in the notation of
[
4
]
, and

v_{d}(q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}V_{d}(n)q^{n},

then

v_{d}(q)=S_{1}(q).

Here the central part contributes
q^{n}
, and the two strict sides independently select distinct parts greater than
n
.
Thus the congruence results for
s_{1}(n)
below may also be read as congruence results for strictly concave compositions.
We record a simple companion identity.

Remark 1
.

If both colors are required to occur in the smallest part, then the natural generating function is

S_{2}(q):=\sum_{n\geq 1}q^{2n}\,(-q^{n+1};q)_{\infty}^{2}.

A short calculation gives

S_{2}(q)=3(-q;q)_{\infty}^{2}-1-2S_{1}(q),

so this variant produces no essentially new series.

Writing

S_{1}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}s_{1}(n)q^{n},

our first main result is the following complete description modulo
4
for the sequence
\{s_{1}(n)\}
.

Theorem 1
.

For every
n\geq 0
,

s_{1}(n)\equiv\begin{cases}(-1)^{r}\pmod{4},&\text{if }n=\dfrac{r(r+1)}{2}\text{ for some }r\geq 0,\\
0\pmod{4},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In particular,
s_{1}(n)
is odd if and only if
n
is triangular.

Remark 2
.

The parity assertion in Theorem
1
is also an immediate consequence of Andrews’ formula for
v_{d}(q)
in
[
4

In our notation that formula reads

S_{1}(q)=-\Theta(q)+2(-q;q)_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n(3n+1)/2}(1-q^{2n+1}),

where

\Theta(q)=\sum_{r\geq 0}(-1)^{r}q^{r(r+1)/2}.

Reducing modulo
2

, and hence

is odd exactly when

Theorem

Since

if and only if

Corollary 1
.

Let

. Then

s_{1}(An+B)\equiv 0\pmod{4}\qquad(n\geq 0)

if and only if

is not a square modulo

For odd primes

shows that the square condition modulo

Corollary 2

Let

s_{1}(pn+B)\equiv 0\pmod{4}\qquad(n\geq 0)

if and only if

Hence for each odd prime

For example, Corollary

The odd and even companion series are

T_{o}(q):=\sum_{m\geq 0}q^{2m}\frac{(-q;q)_{2m}}{(q;q^{2})_{m+1}},\qquad T_{e}(q):=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{(-1;q)_{2m}q^{2m}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{m}}.

An important series in this paper is the eta-normalized series

(1.1)

Writing

Theorem 2
.

For all

c(25n+8)=c(25n+13)=c(25n+18)=c(25n+23)=0,

In particular,

Iterating the relation

The next result shows that the support of

If

3.
Partition identities and strict odd-length partitions

The coefficients of the even companion admit a direct overpartition interpretation. Recall that an overpartition is a partition in which the first occurrence of a part may be overlined; see Corteel and Lovejoy
[
11
]
. Write

T_{e}(q)=1+\sum_{N\geq 1}t_{e}(N)q^{N}.

For
m\geq 1
, the
m
th summand in the defining series for
T_{e}(q)
can be written as

2\frac{q^{2m}}{1-q^{2m}}\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{1+q^{2j}}{1-q^{2j}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+q^{2j-1}).

Thus, for
N\geq 1
, the coefficient
t_{e}(N)
counts overpartitions of
N
such that the largest part is even, only even parts may be overlined, and the odd parts are distinct; the factor
2q^{2m}/(1-q^{2m})
records the two choices for the overline status of the largest part
2m
. In particular,
t_{e}(N)
is even for every
N\geq 1
.

Definition 1
.

For
N\geq 1
, let
a_{e}(N)
denote the number of ordinary partitions of
N
whose first missing part among the positive integers not congruent to
1\pmod{4}
is congruent to
3\pmod{4}
. Equivalently, among the candidate parts

2,3,4,6,7,8,10,11,12,\dots,

the smallest candidate that does not occur is of the form
4m+3
.

For
N\geq 1
, let
b_{e}(N)
denote the number of overpartitions of
N
such that the largest part is even and not overlined, only even parts may be overlined, and the odd parts are distinct.

The direct interpretation of
t_{e}(N)
above shows immediately that

t_{e}(N)=2b_{e}(N)\qquad(N\geq 1).

(3.1)

Remark 3
.

For
N=6
, the eight overpartitions counted by
t_{e}(6)
are

(6),\qquad(\overline{6}),\qquad(4,2),\qquad(\overline{4},2),\qquad(4,\overline{2}),\qquad(\overline{4},\overline{2}),\qquad(2,2,2),\qquad(\overline{2},2,2).

Thus
t_{e}(6)=8
.

Restricting to the overpartitions with nonoverlined largest part leaves the four objects counted by
b_{e}(6)
:

(6),\qquad(4,2),\qquad(4,\overline{2}),\qquad(2,2,2).

Hence
b_{e}(6)=4
.

The ordinary partitions counted by
a_{e}(6)
are

(4,2),\qquad(2,2,2),\qquad(2,2,1,1),\qquad(2,1,1,1,1).

In each case the relevant candidate parts begin

2,3,4,6,7,8,\dots,

and the first missing candidate is
3
. Thus
a_{e}(6)=b_{e}(6)=t_{e}(6)/2=4
.

Theorem 4
.

For every
N\geq 1
,

a_{e}(N)=b_{e}(N)=\frac{t_{e}(N)}{2}.

We now turn to strict odd-length partitions and OEIS A344650.

OEIS A344650 is the sequence whose

into an odd number of parts; equivalently, it counts partitions of

Define

(3.2)

(3.3)

We now make explicit the comparison between

4.
Analytic formulas for the companion series

In this section we collect the analytic identities for the companion series and for the eta-normalized series

C(q):=(q;q)_{\infty}T_{o}(q).

For convenience, write

\displaystyle\Theta(q)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{r\geq 0}(-1)^{r}q^{r(r+1)/2},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Psi(q)

\displaystyle:=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}.

(4.2)

Note that by Gauss’ sum (
2.4
) we have

\Psi(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n(n+1)/2}.

(4.3)

Proposition 1
.

We have

\displaystyle S_{1}(q)

\displaystyle=\Psi(q)T_{e}(q)+2qT_{o}(q),

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\Theta(q)+4qT_{o}(q),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Psi(q)T_{e}(q)

\displaystyle=\Theta(q)+2qT_{o}(q).

(4.6)

Define

P(q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n(3n+1)/2}(1-q^{2n+1}).

The next proposition will be used in the proof of Theorem
4
.

Proposition 2
.

We have

T_{e}(q)=\frac{P(q)}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

(4.7)

Combining Proposition
1
with Proposition
2
, we obtain

2qC(q)=\Psi(q)P(q)-(q;q)_{\infty}\Theta(q).

(4.8)

Remark 5
.

The first identity (
4.4
) is the direct even–odd decomposition of
S_{1}(q)
.
In conjunction with this decomposition, the remaining identities (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) can also be recovered from Andrews’ identity for
v_{d}(q)
.
Since
S_{1}(q)=v_{d}(q)
,
[
4
, Theorem 1, (1.4)]
gives

S_{1}(q)=-\Theta(q)+2(-q;q)_{\infty}^{2}P(q).

(4.9)

On the other hand, Proposition
2
, Euler’s identity (
2.2
), and

(q;q)_{\infty}=(q;q^{2})_{\infty}(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}

give

(-q;q)_{\infty}^{2}P(q)=\frac{P(q)}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\frac{P(q)}{(q;q)_{\infty}}=\Psi(q)T_{e}(q).

Thus (
4.9
) is equivalent to

S_{1}(q)=2\Psi(q)T_{e}(q)-\Theta(q).

Comparing this with the direct even–odd decomposition (

\Psi(q)T_{e}(q)=\Theta(q)+2qT_{o}(q),

which is (

which is (

This provides an alternative derivation of these two companion identities from Andrews’ formula, while the proof below remains self-contained.

The next proposition gives a parity-split representation of
C(q)
.

Proposition 3
.

The eta-normalized series
C(q)
has the representation

\displaystyle C(q)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{6n^{2}+n}(1-q^{4n+1})\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2n^{2}+3n}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{6n^{2}+7n+1}(1-q^{4n+3})\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2n^{2}+n}\right).

(4.10)

Remark 6
.

The quadratic form

that appears in Theorem

is naturally attached to the theta series

\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{x^{2}+3y^{2}},

a classical modular form of weight

places the support of

Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
5

We begin with the standard staircase lemma.

Lemma 4
.

For each fixed
k\geq 1
,

\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{d}(k,n)q^{n}=\frac{q^{k(k+1)/2}}{(q;q)_{k}}.

Proof.

Write a partition into exactly
k
distinct positive parts as

\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\cdots>\lambda_{k}\geq 1.

Remove the staircase
(k,k-1,\dots,1)
by setting

\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}-(k-i+1)\qquad(1\leq i\leq k).

Then

\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{k}\geq 0,

so
\mu
is an ordinary partition with at most
k
parts. Conversely, adding the staircase back to any partition with at most
k
parts gives a partition into exactly
k
distinct parts. The staircase contributes
k(k+1)/2
, and the generating function for partitions with at most
k
parts is
1/(q;q)_{k}
; see, for example, Andrews
[
1
, Chapter 1]
.
∎
