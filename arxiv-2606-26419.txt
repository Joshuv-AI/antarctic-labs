Arbitrarily Many Non-degenerate Local Maxima of First Nonzero Eigenfunctions on Positively Curved Two-spheres
Heng Zhang

Abstract
. We prove that, for every integer m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , there is a smooth closed Riemannian surface ( M , g ) (M,g) diffeomorphic to 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} , with positive Gaussian curvature, such that λ 1 ​ ( M ) \lambda_{1}(M) is simple and, after choosing the sign, its normalized first nonzero Laplace eigenfunction has at least m m distinct non-degenerate local maxima. This gives a negative answer to the Open Question of Grossi and Provenzano (Math. Ann. 389(4): 3447–3470, 2024).



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and main result

The number and configuration of critical points of Laplace eigenfunctions is a
classical problem in spectral geometry. In dimension two, the local structure of
nodal sets of eigenfunctions goes back to Cheng
[
10
]
, while critical
sets of solutions of elliptic equations have been studied, for example, by Hardt,
Hoffmann-Ostenhof, Hoffmann-Ostenhof and Nadirashvili

[
18
]
. Jakobson and Nadirashvili
constructed eigenfunctions with few critical points

[
20
]
, and Freitas constructed surfaces whose first
nonzero eigenfunctions have closed nodal lines and interior hot spots

[
15
]
.

We use the sign convention that
-\Delta
is the non-negative Laplacian. For a
closed connected Riemannian manifold, or more generally for a compact connected
metric-measure space on which the Laplacian has discrete spectrum, we write its
eigenvalues as

(1.1)

0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots,

counted with multiplicity. Throughout the paper, a
second eigenfunction

means an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1}
, the first positive
eigenvalue in the indexing convention (
1.1
).

Without strong geometric restrictions, the critical or level-set geometry of
eigenfunctions may be very complicated. For instance, Enciso and Peralta-Salas
proved a prescribed nodal-set realization result for eigenfunctions on compact
manifolds
[
13
]
. Buhovsky, Logunov and Sodin constructed a
metric on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that, for infinitely many eigenvalues, a
corresponding eigenfunction has infinitely many isolated critical points

[
5
]
. Bérard, Charron and Helffer constructed smooth
metrics on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, arbitrarily close to the flat and
round metrics respectively, for which suitable eigenfunctions have infinitely
many connected components of a level-set complement

[
3
]
. These results show that large nodal, critical, or
level-set complexity is possible for general metrics. They do not, however,
settle what happens at the first positive eigenvalue under a positive Gaussian
curvature assumption.

This leads naturally to the question of whether curvature or convexity-type
hypotheses can restore a rigid critical-point structure for low-energy
eigenfunctions. In the planar theory, convexity is known to impose strong
restrictions in several related problems. Classical log-concavity and convexity
results for first Dirichlet eigenfunctions and associated variational problems
go back to Brascamp–Lieb
[
4
]
and Caffarelli–Spruck

[
8
]
. For semilinear elliptic equations,
Cabré–Chanillo
[
7
]
and
De Regibus–Grossi–Mukherjee
[
12
]
obtained
representative uniqueness results for critical points in convex planar domains.
For second Dirichlet eigenfunctions in convex planar domains, the number of
critical points was studied by De Regibus and Grossi

[
11
]
. Thus, from the point of view of convexity and
curvature, it is natural to ask whether analogous uniqueness phenomena persist
for second eigenfunctions on closed positively curved surfaces.

Grossi and Provenzano
[
17
]
recently pursued this
theme in two directions. They obtained uniqueness results for critical points of
semi-stable solutions on convex domains in two-dimensional model spaces, and
they also studied second eigenfunctions on closed manifolds of revolution. For a
closed surface of revolution diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, their curvature
condition is equivalent to positive Gaussian curvature. Motivated by this
setting, they asked the following question

[
17
, Open Question 3.19]
.

The main result of this paper gives a negative answer in a strong form. Positive

Theorem 1.1

For every integer

Taking

Riemannian two-sphere whose second eigenfunction, with a suitable sign choice,
has at least two distinct non-degenerate local maxima. Since every local
maximum of a smooth eigenfunction is a critical point, such an eigenfunction
cannot have exactly two critical points consisting of one maximum and one
minimum. Thus Question

has a negative answer.

1.2.
Strategy for the construction

The proof is based on a degeneration to a doubled planar domain. The key point
is to find a planar first eigenfunction whose extrema are already sufficiently
non-rigid, and then to transfer this behavior to a closed positively curved
sphere.

For a bounded connected planar domain
U
, we write

0=\mu_{0}^{N}(U)<\mu_{1}^{N}(U)\leq\mu_{2}^{N}(U)\leq\cdots

for the Neumann spectrum and

0<\lambda_{1}^{D}(U)\leq\lambda_{2}^{D}(U)\leq\cdots

for the Dirichlet spectrum, in both cases counted with multiplicity.

The relevant planar tool comes from the classical hot spots problem for Neumann
eigenfunctions. Rauch’s hot spots conjecture predicts that the extrema of a
first nontrivial Neumann eigenfunction of a bounded insulated domain should occur
on the boundary
[
28
]
. Although the conjecture is false for general
planar domains
[
6
,
2
]
, many positive results
are known under additional geometric assumptions; see, for instance,

[
21
,
1
,
26
,
29
,
22
,
9
]
.

Miyamoto’s construction
[
24
]
exhibits a different phenomenon,
which is precisely the one needed here. It does not provide a failure of the
boundary hot spots conclusion in convex domains. Rather, it shows that even in
the class of convex polygons, the boundary hot spots of a first positive Neumann
eigenfunction need not consist of a single maximum and a single minimum. More
precisely, in
[
24
, Theorem A]
, one obtains a
convex polygon
\Omega
whose first positive Neumann eigenvalue is simple and whose
corresponding Neumann eigenfunction
\phi
has
m
isolated boundary maxima.

We then pass from this planar Neumann problem to a closed surface by forming the
metric double of
\Omega
,

X=D\Omega,

obtained by gluing two copies of
\overline{\Omega}
along
\partial\Omega
. This double
is an Alexandrov sphere of curvature at least zero. The involution exchanging
the two sheets decomposes
L^{2}(X)
, and hence the spectrum of
X
, into even and
odd parts. The even part is unitarily equivalent to the Neumann spectrum of

\Omega
, while the odd part is unitarily equivalent to the Dirichlet spectrum of

\Omega
. By Filonov’s strict Friedlander inequality
[
14
]
, we have

\lambda_{1}(X)=\min\{\mu_{1}^{N}(\Omega),\lambda_{1}^{D}(\Omega)\}=\mu_{1}^{N}(\Omega),

so the first positive eigenvalue of the double is realized in the even part of
the spectral decomposition. Since
\mu_{1}^{N}(\Omega)
is simple, the

\lambda_{1}(X)
-eigenspace is spanned by the even double
\Phi
of Miyamoto’s
Neumann eigenfunction. After choosing the sign of
\phi
so that Miyamoto’s hot
spots are maxima, these boundary hot spots become strict local maxima of
\Phi

at the corresponding seam points of the double.

The remaining task is to replace the singular Alexandrov sphere
X
by smooth
positively curved spheres while preserving both the first eigenfunction and its
local maxima. We do this by a pancake approximation. First we replace the
polygon by smooth strictly convex inner domains

\Omega_{\delta}=\{\psi_{\delta}\leq 1\},\qquad\psi_{\delta}(x)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{L}\exp(\ell_{\alpha}(x)/\delta),

where the affine functions
\ell_{\alpha}
define the sides of
\Omega
, equivalently

\Omega=\bigcap_{\alpha=1}^{L}\{\ell_{\alpha}\leq 0\}.

Over each
\Omega_{\delta}
we take the thin strictly convex body

C_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\left\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}:\psi_{\delta}(x)+\frac{z^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\leq 1\right\},\qquad M_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=\partial C_{\varepsilon,\delta}.

The surfaces
M_{\varepsilon,\delta}
are smooth embedded two-spheres with positive
Gaussian curvature. For fixed
\delta
, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, the upper and
lower graphs

z=\pm\varepsilon\sqrt{1-\psi_{\delta}(x)}

collapse to the metric double
D\Omega_{\delta}
; then
D\Omega_{\delta}
converges to

D\Omega
as
\delta\downarrow 0
. Choosing a diagonal sequence gives smooth
positively curved pancakes
M_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
converging to
X
in measured
Gromov–Hausdorff sense.

Next, the spectral and topological features of the limit survive along this
approximation. The spaces involved lie in the compact
\mathrm{RCD}(0,2)
setting, and

\lambda_{1}(X)
is simple. Honda’s spectral convergence theorem
[
19
]

therefore gives uniform convergence, after choosing signs, of the

L^{2}
-normalized
\lambda_{1}(M_{j})
-eigenfunctions to the even doubled eigenfunction

\Phi
, along suitable measured Gromov–Hausdorff approximation maps. A
topological persistence lemma then ensures that the strict local maxima of

\Phi
on the limit produce distinct local maxima of the corresponding first
eigenfunctions on all sufficiently close smooth pancakes. More precisely, the
argument gives pairwise disjoint regions on a smooth pancake on which the first
eigenfunction has a strict boundary gap.

The final non-degeneracy step is a generic perturbation argument. Since the
first positive eigenvalue on the selected pancake is simple, the corresponding
normalized eigenfunction varies continuously under small smooth perturbations of
the metric. The boundary gaps above therefore persist. By Uhlenbeck’s generic
Morse property for eigenfunctions
[
30
]
, one may choose an
arbitrarily small smooth perturbation of the metric for which the first
eigenfunction is Morse. The positive curvature condition is open in the

C^{2}
topology, so this perturbation can be made within the class of positively
curved metrics. Finally, the smooth solution of the Weyl isometric embedding
problem for positively curved metrics on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
realizes the perturbed
metric as a smooth strictly convex embedded surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
25
,
27
]
.

We use the following form of Miyamoto’s polygonal hot-spot construction; see

Theorem 2.1

(Miyamoto hot-spot polygons)
.

For every integer

there exists a bounded convex polygon

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that the first positive Neumann eigenvalue
\mu_{1}^{N}(\Omega)

is simple and such that a real
\mu_{1}^{N}(\Omega)
-eigenfunction

\overline{\Omega}

\partial\Omega

\overline{\Omega}
.

Fix

, and choose
\Omega
and
\phi
as in
Theorem
2.1
. Since
\phi
satisfies the weak Neumann eigenvalue
equation, it also solves the resolvent problem

(-\Delta+1)\phi=(\mu_{1}^{N}(\Omega)+1)\phi

with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition. As
\Omega
is a bounded convex
polygon, Grisvard’s Neumann regularity theorem on bounded convex domains

[
16
, Theorem 3.2.1.3]
gives
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
. Since
\Omega
is a
bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the limiting Sobolev–Morrey embedding

[
16
, Section 1.4]
implies

H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\qquad\text{for every }0\leq\alpha<1.

Hence
\phi
has a continuous representative on
\overline{\Omega}
, and throughout
the sequel
\phi
denotes this representative. We normalize
\phi
by

(2.1)

\int_{\Omega}\phi\,dx=0,\qquad\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}\,dx=1.

Write

p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in\partial\Omega

for the isolated boundary maximum points of
\phi
. Let

(2.2)

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}:=\{x\in\overline{\Omega}:\phi(x)=\max_{\overline{\Omega}}\phi\}

be the compact global maximum set. By Theorem
2.1
,

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For each
i=1,\ldots,m
there are pairwise disjoint relatively open
neighborhoods
V_{i}\subset\overline{\Omega}
of
p_{i}
and a number
\eta>0
such that

(2.3)

V_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\{p_{i}\}

and

(2.4)

\phi(p_{i})\geq\sup_{\partial_{\overline{\Omega}}V_{i}}\phi+4\eta.

Here
\partial_{\overline{\Omega}}V_{i}
denotes the boundary of
V_{i}
relative to

\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Since
p_{i}
is an isolated point of the compact maximum set (
2.2
),
choose pairwise disjoint relative neighborhoods
V_{i}
whose closures meet

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
only at
p_{i}
and whose relative boundaries do not meet

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
. This gives (
2.3
). The continuous
function
\phi
is strictly smaller than
\phi(p_{i})
on each compact set

\partial_{\overline{\Omega}}V_{i}
. Taking the minimum of the resulting positive gaps
and dividing by
4
gives a common

, which proves
(
2.4
).

Lemma 3.1

\mu_{1}^{N}(\Omega)
is simple by
Theorem
2.1
and no odd Dirichlet eigenfunction occurs at that
level.

The formula (
3.11
) is the even lift of the
normalized Neumann eigenfunction
\phi
. By (
2.1
), this
doubled function has

\int_{X}\Phi\,d\mathfrak{m}_{X}=0,\qquad\int_{X}\Phi^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}_{X}=1.

Since
\phi
is continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
and the two sheet values in
(
3.11
) agree on the seam,
\Phi
is continuous on

X
.

Finally, a neighborhood of a seam point
p_{i}\in\partial\Omega\subset X
is the union
of two relative half-neighborhoods of
p_{i}
in
\overline{\Omega}
, one in each sheet.
Because
\Phi
restricts to
2^{-1/2}\phi
on both sheets,
Lemma
2.2
shows that the doubled neighborhoods

DV_{i}
are pairwise disjoint neighborhoods of the corresponding seam points. By
(
2.3
), one has

\Phi(y)<\Phi(p_{i})\qquad\text{for all }y\in DV_{i}\setminus\{p_{i}\}.

Thus each
p_{i}
is a strict local maximum of
\Phi
on

, and the pairwise
disjoint neighborhoods show that these
m
strict local maxima are distinct and
isolated.

4.
Smooth positively curved pancake approximation

We now approximate the singular doubled polygon
X=D\Omega
by smooth embedded
strictly convex surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

4.1.
Smooth inner approximations of the polygon

In this subsection,
d_{H}^{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
denotes Euclidean Hausdorff distance in the
plane, and
|A|
denotes planar Lebesgue measure for measurable sets

A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Write the convex polygon as

(4.1)

\Omega=\bigcap_{\alpha=1}^{L}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ell_{\alpha}(x)\leq 0\},

where
L
is the number of defining half-spaces, each
\ell_{\alpha}
is affine
and nonconstant, and where the outward normals
d\ell_{\alpha}
span
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For

\delta>0
set

(4.2)

\psi_{\delta}(x)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{L}\exp(\ell_{\alpha}(x)/\delta),\qquad\Omega_{\delta}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\psi_{\delta}(x)\leq 1\}.

For all sufficiently small
\delta
,
\Omega_{\delta}
has non-empty interior.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all sufficiently small
\delta>0
,
\Omega_{\delta}
is a bounded smooth strictly
convex domain,
\Omega_{\delta}\subset\Omega
, and

(4.3)

d_{H}^{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\overline{\Omega_{\delta}},\overline{\Omega})\to 0,\qquad d_{H}^{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\partial\Omega_{\delta},\partial\Omega)\to 0,\qquad|\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}|\to 0\quad\text{as }\delta\downarrow 0.

Proof.

Choose
x_{*}\in\Omega^{\circ}
. Since
\ell_{\alpha}(x_{*})<0
for all
\alpha
, one has

\psi_{\delta}(x_{*})<1
for all sufficiently small
\delta
. Thus
\Omega_{\delta}
has
non-empty interior.

The Hessian is

(4.4)

D^{2}\psi_{\delta}(x)=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{L}\exp(\ell_{\alpha}(x)/\delta)\,d\ell_{\alpha}\otimes d\ell_{\alpha}.

Because the covectors
d\ell_{\alpha}
span
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the coefficients in
(
4.4
) are positive,
D^{2}\psi_{\delta}
is positive definite
at every point. Thus
\psi_{\delta}
is smooth and strictly convex, and every
sublevel set
\{\psi_{\delta}\leq c\}
is strictly convex. On the level set

\psi_{\delta}=1
, the gradient cannot vanish: if
\nabla\psi_{\delta}=0
at a point
of that level set, strict convexity would make that point the unique global
minimizer, whereas the existence of
x_{*}
with
\psi_{\delta}(x_{*})<1
shows that
the minimum is strictly less than
1
. Therefore
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
is smooth.
Since
\Omega_{\delta}\subset\Omega
will be shown below and
\Omega
is bounded,

\Omega_{\delta}
is bounded.

If
x\in\Omega_{\delta}
, then each exponential term in (
4.2
)
is at most
1
, hence each
\ell_{\alpha}(x)\leq 0
, so
x\in\Omega
by
(
4.1
). Thus
\Omega_{\delta}\subset\Omega
.

It remains to prove Hausdorff convergence. Put

C_{0}:=\min_{1\leq\alpha\leq L}|d\ell_{\alpha}|>0.

If
x\in\Omega
and
\dist_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x,\partial\Omega)\geq r
, then, for each
\alpha
, the exterior
half-space
\{\ell_{\alpha}>0\}
is contained in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Omega
. Hence

\frac{-\ell_{\alpha}(x)}{|d\ell_{\alpha}|}=\dist_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x,\{\ell_{\alpha}=0\})\geq\dist_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x,\partial\Omega)\geq r.

Consequently, for the inner parallel set

\Omega^{-r}:=\{x\in\Omega:\dist_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x,\partial\Omega)\geq r\},

one has the linear error bound

(4.5)

\ell_{\alpha}(x)\leq-C_{0}r\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega^{-r},\;\alpha=1,\ldots,L.

Choose

(4.6)

r_{\delta}=\frac{2\delta}{C_{0}}\log L.

Then, for
x\in\Omega^{-r_{\delta}}
, equations (
4.5
) and
(
4.6
) give

\psi_{\delta}(x)\leq L\exp(-C_{0}r_{\delta}/\delta)=L^{-1}<1.

Hence

(4.7)

\Omega^{-r_{\delta}}\subset\Omega_{\delta}\subset\Omega.

Since
r_{\delta}\to 0
, (
4.7
) implies

d_{H}^{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\overline{\Omega_{\delta}},\overline{\Omega})\to 0
.

We now prove boundary convergence. Every point of
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
lies
within distance
r_{\delta}
of
\partial\Omega
, otherwise it would belong to

\Omega^{-r_{\delta}}\subset\{\psi_{\delta}<1\}
by (
4.7
).

Conversely, put

s_{*}:=\min_{1\leq\alpha\leq L}\frac{-\ell_{\alpha}(x_{*})}{|d\ell_{\alpha}|}>0.

For
p\in\partial\Omega
define

t_{\delta}=\frac{2r_{\delta}}{s_{*}},\qquad q_{\delta,p}:=(1-t_{\delta})p+t_{\delta}x_{*}.

For all sufficiently small
\delta
,
0<t_{\delta}<1
. By the half-space
inequalities,

\ell_{\alpha}(q_{\delta,p})=(1-t_{\delta})\ell_{\alpha}(p)+t_{\delta}\ell_{\alpha}(x_{*})\leq t_{\delta}\ell_{\alpha}(x_{*})\leq-2r_{\delta}|d\ell_{\alpha}|

for every
\alpha
. Thus
\dist_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(q_{\delta,p},\partial\Omega)\geq 2r_{\delta}
, and in
particular
q_{\delta,p}\in\Omega^{-r_{\delta}}\subset\{\psi_{\delta}<1\}
. On the
other hand, since
p\in\partial\Omega
, at least one defining inequality satisfies

\ell_{\alpha}(p)=0
, hence
\psi_{\delta}(p)\geq 1
. By continuity, the segment

[p,q_{\delta,p}]
meets
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
at some point
b_{\delta,p}
. The
estimate

|b_{\delta,p}-p|\leq|q_{\delta,p}-p|=t_{\delta}|x_{*}-p|\leq\frac{2\diam(\Omega)}{s_{*}}\,r_{\delta}

is uniform in
p
. This proves
d_{H}^{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\partial\Omega_{\delta},\partial\Omega)\to 0
.
Finally,
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
is contained in the boundary layer

\Omega\setminus\Omega^{-r_{\delta}}
, whose Lebesgue measure tends to
0
as

r_{\delta}\downarrow 0
. This completes the proof of (
4.3
).
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

The metric-measure doubles satisfy

(4.8)

(D\Omega_{\delta},d_{D\Omega_{\delta}},\mathfrak{m}_{D\Omega_{\delta}})\longrightarrow(D\Omega,d_{D\Omega},\mathfrak{m}_{D\Omega})

in measured Gromov–Hausdorff sense as
\delta\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

For any compact convex set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with non-empty interior, distances in
the metric double
DC
are given by the following formulas, where
|\cdot|

denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

(4.9a)

\displaystyle d_{DC}(x^{\sigma},y^{\sigma})

\displaystyle=|x-y|,\qquad\sigma\in\{+,-\},

(4.9b)

\displaystyle d_{DC}(x^{+},y^{-})

\displaystyle=\inf_{q\in\partial C}\bigl(|x-q|+|q-y|\bigr).

Formula (
4.9a
) follows because projection to the
plane is length non-increasing and the straight segment in a convex sheet realizes

|x-y|
. Formula (
4.9b
) follows because any
curve from one sheet to the other must cross the seam, while the broken segment

xq\cup qy
gives the displayed upper bound.

Let

a_{\delta}=d_{H}^{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\overline{\Omega_{\delta}},\overline{\Omega}),\qquad b_{\delta}=d_{H}^{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\partial\Omega_{\delta},\partial\Omega),

so
a_{\delta},b_{\delta}\to 0
by Lemma
4.1
. Choose a Borel map

Q_{\delta}:\partial\Omega_{\delta}\to\partial\Omega
with

(4.10)

|Q_{\delta}(a)-a|\leq b_{\delta}+\delta\qquad(a\in\partial\Omega_{\delta}).

Define a seam-compatible map

\mathcal{F}_{\delta}:D\Omega_{\delta}\to D\Omega

as follows: if
x\in\Omega_{\delta}^{\circ}
, set
\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(x^{\pm})=x^{\pm}
; if

a\in\partial\Omega_{\delta}
is a seam point, set
\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(a)=Q_{\delta}(a)
, viewed
as a seam point of
D\Omega
. This map need not be continuous near the seam, but
continuity is not required for a Gromov–Hausdorff approximation; only distortion
and density are used.

The image
\mathcal{F}_{\delta}(D\Omega_{\delta})
is
(a_{\delta}+b_{\delta}+\delta)
-dense in
D\Omega
. Indeed, points in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
are within

a_{\delta}
of
\overline{\Omega_{\delta}}
on the corresponding sheet, and if the
nearest point is on
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, its image is changed by at most

b_{\delta}+\delta
by (
4.10
).

We next estimate distortion. Same-sheet distances between interior points are
preserved exactly. If one or both points are seam points, replacing

a\in\partial\Omega_{\delta}
by
Q_{\delta}(a)
changes the relevant Euclidean length
by at most
b_{\delta}+\delta
per seam point, again by (
4.10
).
Hence same-sheet distances are changed by at most
2(b_{\delta}+\delta)
.

For opposite-sheet distances, use (
4.9b
). For

x,y\in\overline{\Omega_{\delta}}
,

\left|\inf_{q\in\partial\Omega_{\delta}}(|x-q|+|q-y|)-\inf_{q\in\partial\Omega}(|x-q|+|q-y|)\right|\leq 2b_{\delta}.

Indeed, by the definition of Hausdorff distance, for every

q\in\partial\Omega_{\delta}
there is
q^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega
with

|q-q^{\prime}|\leq b_{\delta}
, and conversely. The triangle inequality gives

|x-q^{\prime}|+|q^{\prime}-y|\leq|x-q|+|q-y|+2b_{\delta}
and the reverse comparison in the same
way. If
x
or
y
itself is a seam point, the additional replacement by
Q_{\delta}

changes the two Euclidean terms by at most
2(b_{\delta}+\delta)
. Therefore

(4.11)

\sup_{p,q\in D\Omega_{\delta}}\bigl|d_{D\Omega}(\mathcal{F}_{\delta}p,\mathcal{F}_{\delta}q)-d_{D\Omega_{\delta}}(p,q)\bigr|\leq 4b_{\delta}+2\delta.

The density estimate and (
4.11
) show that
\mathcal{F}_{\delta}
is a
Gromov–Hausdorff approximation.

For the measures,
\mathfrak{m}_{D\Omega_{\delta}}
is the sum of two copies of planar
Lebesgue measure on
\Omega_{\delta}
, and the seam has zero two-dimensional measure.
The push-forward
(\mathcal{F}_{\delta})_{\#}\mathfrak{m}_{D\Omega_{\delta}}
is therefore the sum of two
copies of Lebesgue measure restricted to
\Omega_{\delta}\subset\Omega
, viewed inside
the corresponding sheets of
D\Omega
. Since
\chi_{\Omega_{\delta}}\to\chi_{\Omega}
in

L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by Lemma
4.1
, these measures converge weakly to

\mathfrak{m}_{D\Omega}
. This proves (
4.8
).
∎

4.2.
Elliptic pancakes over a smooth convex base

Fix
\delta>0
small. For
\varepsilon>0
, define

(4.12)

C_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=\left\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}:\psi_{\delta}(x)+\frac{z^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\leq 1\right\},\qquad M_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=\partial C_{\varepsilon,\delta}.

convex polygon

\Omega=\bigcap_{\alpha}\{\ell_{\alpha}\leq 0\}

smooth inner
approximation
\Omega_{\delta}
\Omega_{\delta}\subset\Omega

smooth strictly

convex domain

\Omega_{\delta}=\{\psi_{\delta}\leq 1\}

elliptic
thickening
O(\varepsilon)
M_{\varepsilon,\delta}

smooth positively

curved pancake

M_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\partial C_{\varepsilon,\delta}

Figure 3.
Schematic of the pancake approximation. A convex polygon
\Omega
is first
replaced by smooth strictly convex inner domains
\Omega_{\delta}
, and then by thin
strictly convex surfaces
M_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\partial C_{\varepsilon,\delta}
with positive

Lemma 4.3
.

For every sufficiently small
\delta>0
and every
\varepsilon>0
,

M_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is a smooth embedded two-sphere with positive Gaussian
curvature.

Proof.

Let

G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,z)=\psi_{\delta}(x)+\frac{z^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

Then

(4.13)

D^{2}G_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\begin{pmatrix}D^{2}\psi_{\delta}&0\\[5.69054pt]
0&2\varepsilon^{-2}\end{pmatrix}>0.

The level set
G_{\varepsilon,\delta}=1
is regular. Indeed, if

\nabla G_{\varepsilon,\delta}=0
on that level set, strict convexity would place a
global minimizer on the boundary of a sublevel set whose interior is non-empty,
impossible. Hence
M_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is smooth. It is compact and bounds the
strictly convex body
C_{\varepsilon,\delta}
from (
4.12
),
so it is an embedded topological sphere. Since it is a smooth embedded closed
surface homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, it is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
; equivalently,
for a smooth strictly convex body the outward Gauss map gives a smooth
diffeomorphism with the unit sphere.

Take the outward unit normal

\nu=\frac{\nabla G_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{|\nabla G_{\varepsilon,\delta}|}.

For tangent vectors
v\in TM_{\varepsilon,\delta}
, the second fundamental form in the
convention
\mathrm{II}(v,v)=\langle D_{v}\nu,v\rangle
is

(4.14)

\mathrm{II}(v,v)=\frac{D^{2}G_{\varepsilon,\delta}(v,v)}{|\nabla G_{\varepsilon,\delta}|}.

Since
D^{2}G_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is positive definite by
(
4.13
),
\mathrm{II}
is positive definite in this
convention. With the opposite sign convention for the second fundamental form it
would be negative definite; in either convention the two principal curvatures
have the same nonzero sign, and their product, the Gaussian curvature, is
positive.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For each fixed sufficiently small
\delta>0
,

(4.15)

(M_{\varepsilon,\delta},d_{M_{\varepsilon,\delta}},\mathfrak{m}_{M_{\varepsilon,\delta}})\longrightarrow(D\Omega_{\delta},d_{D\Omega_{\delta}},\mathfrak{m}_{D\Omega_{\delta}})

in measured Gromov–Hausdorff sense as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

Set

f_{\delta}(x)=\sqrt{1-\psi_{\delta}(x)},\qquad x\in\overline{\Omega_{\delta}}.

Because
\psi_{\delta}
is convex,
1-\psi_{\delta}
is concave and nonnegative on

\Omega_{\delta}
; since
t\mapsto\sqrt{t}
is increasing and concave on

[0,\infty)
,
f_{\delta}
is concave. Also
0\leq f_{\delta}\leq 1
.
Note that
M_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is the union of the two graphs

(4.16)

z=\varepsilon f_{\delta}(x),\qquad z=-\varepsilon f_{\delta}(x),\qquad x\in\overline{\Omega_{\delta}}.

The graph parametrization in (
4.16
) is singular at

\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, but Lemma
4.3
proves that the
level surface itself is smooth there.

Define

\pi_{\varepsilon,\delta}:M_{\varepsilon,\delta}\to D\Omega_{\delta}

by horizontal projection, sending the upper graph to the positive sheet and the
lower graph to the negative sheet. This is well-defined on the seam because

f_{\delta}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, and the two boundary copies are identified
in the double. Horizontal projection does not increase the length of rectifiable
curves. Therefore

(4.17)

d_{D\Omega_{\delta}}(\pi_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p),\pi_{\varepsilon,\delta}(q))\leq d_{M_{\varepsilon,\delta}}(p,q)\qquad\text{for all }p,q\in M_{\varepsilon,\delta}.

Conversely, suppose first that
p,q
lie on the same sheet and that

\pi_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p)=x^{\sigma}
,
\pi_{\varepsilon,\delta}(q)=y^{\sigma}
. The Euclidean
segment
\gamma(t)=(1-t)x+ty
is contained in
\Omega_{\delta}
. Since

f_{\delta}\circ\gamma
is concave, it has bounded variation, and the length of its
lift to the corresponding graph satisfies

(4.18)

\mathcal{L}\bigl(t\mapsto(\gamma(t),\pm\varepsilon f_{\delta}(\gamma(t)))\bigr)\leq|x-y|+\varepsilon\operatorname{Var}(f_{\delta}\circ\gamma).

Because
0\leq f_{\delta}\leq 1
, the total variation of the one-dimensional concave
function
f_{\delta}\circ\gamma
is at most
2
. Thus the lifted segment in
(
4.18
) has length at most
|x-y|+2\varepsilon
.

If
p
and
q
lie on opposite sheets, then, for arbitrary
\rho>0
, choose

a\in\partial\Omega_{\delta}
such that

|x-a|+|a-y|\leq d_{D\Omega_{\delta}}(x^{+},y^{-})+\rho.

Lift
x\to a
on the upper graph and
a\to y
on the lower graph. By the same
estimate, the total lifted length is at most

d_{D\Omega_{\delta}}(x^{+},y^{-})+4\varepsilon+\rho
. Letting
\rho\downarrow 0
gives

(4.19)

d_{M_{\varepsilon,\delta}}(p,q)\leq d_{D\Omega_{\delta}}(\pi_{\varepsilon,\delta}(p),\pi_{\varepsilon,\delta}(q))+4\varepsilon.

Equations (
4.17
) and
(
4.19
) show that
\pi_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is onto
and has distortion at most
4\varepsilon
. This is Gromov–Hausdorff convergence.

It remains to prove measure convergence. On one graph, away from the boundary,
the area density is

(4.20)

dA_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla f_{\delta}|^{2}}\,dx.

Although
f_{\delta}
is not Lipschitz up to
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, the formula
(
4.20
) is justified by applying the graph area formula first on
the compact exhaustion

\Omega_{\delta,s}:=\{x\in\Omega_{\delta}:\dist_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x,\partial\Omega_{\delta})\geq s\},

where
f_{\delta}
is smooth, and then letting
s\downarrow 0
. The exhausted graph
pieces increase to the whole graph up to the seam, the seam itself has zero
two-dimensional area, and monotone convergence of the areas applies once the
finite collar contribution is verified by the estimate below.

We claim that

(4.21)

|\nabla f_{\delta}|\in L^{1}(\Omega_{\delta}).

Away from the boundary this is clear. Near
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, the boundary is
smooth and
|\nabla\psi_{\delta}|
is bounded above and below by positive
constants. In a normal collar, if
r=\dist_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x,\partial\Omega_{\delta})
, Taylor
expansion gives
1-\psi_{\delta}(x)\simeq cr
and

|\nabla\psi_{\delta}(x)|\leq C
. Hence

|\nabla f_{\delta}(x)|=\frac{|\nabla\psi_{\delta}(x)|}{2\sqrt{1-\psi_{\delta}(x)}}\leq Cr^{-1/2},

which is integrable in a two-dimensional collar. This proves
(
4.21
). Therefore, for
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,

0\leq\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla f_{\delta}|^{2}}-1\leq\varepsilon|\nabla f_{\delta}|\leq|\nabla f_{\delta}|,

and dominated convergence, using (
4.21
), gives

\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla f_{\delta}|^{2}}\to 1\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega_{\delta}).

The same holds on the lower graph. Thus

(\pi_{\varepsilon,\delta})_{\#}\mathfrak{m}_{M_{\varepsilon,\delta}}
converges weakly to the sum
of two copies of Lebesgue measure on
\Omega_{\delta}
, namely to

\mathfrak{m}_{D\Omega_{\delta}}
. Together with the Gromov–Hausdorff convergence above,
this proves (
4.15
).
∎

(Pancake approximation)
.

There exists a sequence of smooth embedded surfaces

Choose any sequence
\delta_{j}\downarrow 0
. By
Lemma
4.4
, for each
j

to

is less than

(4.23)

By Lemma

, each

5.1.
The spectral-convergence tool

We use Honda’s compactness theorem for spectral data on compact

, Theorem 3.7]
, with convergence of spectral data understood in the

.

Theorem 5.1

(Honda spectral-data compactness)
.

Let

(Y_{j},d_{j},\mathfrak{m}_{j})\longrightarrow(Y,d,\mathfrak{m})

be a measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergent sequence of compact
finite-dimensional
\mathrm{RCD}(\kappa,n)
spaces. Assume that, after discarding

such that

d_{0}^{-1}\leq\diam(Y_{j},d_{j})\leq d_{0},\qquad d_{0}^{-1}\leq\diam(Y,d)\leq d_{0},

v_{0}^{-1}\leq\mathfrak{m}_{j}(Y_{j})\leq v_{0},\qquad v_{0}^{-1}\leq\mathfrak{m}(Y)\leq v_{0}.

Choose Borel maps

\mathcal{F}_{j}:Y_{j}\to Y

realizing the measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence, in the sense that

\mathcal{F}_{j}
is a
\gamma_{j}
-Gromov–Hausdorff approximation,

\gamma_{j}\downarrow 0
, and

(\mathcal{F}_{j})_{\#}\mathfrak{m}_{j}\rightharpoonup\mathfrak{m}.

). Then

\lambda_{k}(Y_{j})\to\lambda_{k}(Y)\qquad(k=0,1,2,\ldots),

with eigenvalues counted with multiplicity.

In particular, for each fixed

(5.2)

\sup_{y_{j}\in Y_{j}}\left|\varphi^{j}_{\ell}(y_{j})-\varphi_{\ell}(\mathcal{F}_{j}(y_{j}))\right|\longrightarrow 0.

Corollary 5.2

(Simple limiting eigenvalue)
.

In the setting of Theorem

, assume that
\lambda_{k}(Y)
is
simple. Then
\lambda_{k}(Y_{j})

Let

-normalized

\sup_{y_{j}\in Y_{j}}\left|u_{j}(y_{j})-u(\mathcal{F}_{j}(y_{j}))\right|\leq\beta_{j}.

Proof.

Since

For these

We remark that
Honda’s definition of measured Gromov–Hausdorff approximation is written using
normalized reference measures together with convergence of the total masses; see

[
19
, Definition 3.4]
. In the finite-measure convention used here this is
equivalent to weak convergence of the unnormalized push-forward measures. A
constant rescaling of the reference measure leaves Rayleigh quotients, and hence
eigenvalues, unchanged; it only rescales
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions.

Lemma 5.3
.

The sequence
M_{j}
from Proposition
4.5
and the limit

X=D\Omega
satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
5.1
with

\kappa=0
and

, after discarding finitely many terms.

Proof.

Let
g_{j}
be the induced Riemannian metric on
M_{j}
. Each
M_{j}
is a
smooth closed two-dimensional Riemannian manifold with positive Gaussian
curvature. In dimension two,

\operatorname{Ric}_{g_{j}}=\mathsf{K}_{g_{j}}g_{j},

and hence
\operatorname{Ric}_{g_{j}}\geq 0
. Therefore each
M_{j}
, equipped with
its Riemannian distance and area measure, is an
\mathrm{RCD}(0,2)
space.

The convex polygon
\Omega
, with its intrinsic Euclidean metric, is a compact
Alexandrov space of curvature at least
0
with boundary. Equivalently, at a
vertex of interior angle
\theta\leq\pi
, the double has cone angle

2\theta\leq 2\pi
, while along non-vertex seam points the total angle is

2\pi
. Thus the polyhedral curvature of the double is non-negative. By
Petrunin’s gluing theorem, in the form used in
Kapovitch–Ketterer–Sturm
[
23
, Theorem 2.13]
, the metric double

X=D\Omega
is a compact two-dimensional Alexandrov space of curvature at least

0
. Kapovitch–Ketterer–Sturm
[
23
, Corollary 2.10]
then implies that

(X,d_{X},\mathfrak{m}_{X})
is an
\mathrm{RCD}(0,2)
space.

Finally, Proposition
4.5
gives measured
Gromov–Hausdorff convergence
M_{j}\to X
. The limit
X=D\Omega
is not a
single point and satisfies
0<\mathfrak{m}_{X}(X)<\infty
. Hence the diameters and total
measures of
M_{j}
are, for all sufficiently large
j
, bounded above and
below away from zero. Thus the spaces
M_{j}
and
X
lie, after discarding
finitely many terms, in a common compact class of
\mathrm{RCD}(0,2)
spaces of the
type required in Theorem
5.1
.
∎

5.2.
A topological persistence lemma

Lemma 5.5

(Persistence of strict local maxima)
.

Let compact metric spaces
Y_{j}
and
Y
be realized as compact subsets of a common
compact metric space
Z
. Assume that there are numbers
\rho_{j}\downarrow 0
such
that

(5.6)

d_{H}^{Z}(Y_{j},Y)\leq\rho_{j},

where
d_{H}^{Z}
denotes Hausdorff distance in the ambient space
Z
. Assume also
that there are continuous functions
v_{j}:Y_{j}\to\mathbb{R}
,
v:Y\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(5.7)

\alpha_{j}:=\sup\bigl\{|v_{j}(y_{j})-v(y)|:\ y_{j}\in Y_{j},\ y\in Y,\ d_{Z}(y_{j},y)\leq\rho_{j}\bigr\}\longrightarrow 0.

If
v
has
m
distinct isolated strict local maxima, then, for all sufficiently
large
j
, there are pairwise disjoint relatively open sets

W_{1,j},\ldots,W_{m,j}\subset Y_{j}
such that

(5.8)

\max_{\overline{W}_{i,j}}v_{j}>\sup_{\partial_{Y_{j}}W_{i,j}}v_{j}\qquad(i=1,\ldots,m),

where closures and boundaries are taken relative to
Y_{j}
and

\sup\emptyset=-\infty
. In particular, for all sufficiently large
j
,
v_{j}

has at least
m
distinct local maxima.

Proof.

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
be distinct isolated strict local maxima of
v
. For each

i
, choose a relative open neighborhood
U_{i}\subset Y
of
p_{i}
such that

v(y)<v(p_{i})
for every
y\in U_{i}\setminus\{p_{i}\}
. Choose
r>0
so small
that the closed
Z
-balls
\overline{B}_{Z}(p_{i},4r)
are pairwise disjoint and

Y\cap\overline{B}_{Z}(p_{i},4r)\subset U_{i}\qquad(i=1,\ldots,m).

For each
i
, set

(5.9)

A_{i}:=Y\cap\bigl(\overline{B}_{Z}(p_{i},4r)\setminus B_{Z}(p_{i},r)\bigr).

If
A_{i}\neq\emptyset
, then
A_{i}
does not contain
p_{i}
, and strict local
maximality gives

(5.10)

\sup_{A_{i}}v\leq v(p_{i})-4\eta_{i}

for some
\eta_{i}>0
. If
A_{i}=\emptyset
, choose any
\eta_{i}>0
; in that case the
annular upper estimate below is vacuous for all large
j
.

For large
j
, (
5.6
) gives
a_{i,j}\in Y_{j}
with

d_{Z}(a_{i,j},p_{i})\leq\rho_{j}
. Hence (
5.7
) gives

(5.11)

v_{j}(a_{i,j})\geq v(p_{i})-\alpha_{j}>v(p_{i})-\eta_{i}

for all sufficiently large
j
.

Consider a point

y_{j}\in Y_{j}\cap\bigl(\overline{B}_{Z}(p_{i},3r)\setminus B_{Z}(p_{i},2r)\bigr).

For large
j
, there exists
y\in Y
with
d_{Z}(y_{j},y)\leq\rho_{j}<r
. Then

y\in A_{i}
by (
5.9
). Hence, if
A_{i}=\emptyset
, no such

y_{j}
exists for large
j
. If
A_{i}\neq\emptyset
, then using
(
5.10
) and (
5.7
) gives

(5.12)

v_{j}(y_{j})\leq v(y)+\alpha_{j}\leq v(p_{i})-4\eta_{i}+\alpha_{j}<v(p_{i})-2\eta_{i}

for all sufficiently large
j
.

Set

W_{i,j}:=Y_{j}\cap B_{Z}(p_{i},3r).

These sets are relatively open in
Y_{j}
and pairwise disjoint for all large
j
.
The point
a_{i,j}
belongs to
W_{i,j}
for all large
j
, and
(
5.11
) gives

(5.13)

\max_{\overline{W}_{i,j}}v_{j}\geq v_{j}(a_{i,j})>v(p_{i})-\eta_{i}.

The relative boundary
\partial_{Y_{j}}W_{i,j}
is contained in

Y_{j}\cap(\overline{B}_{Z}(p_{i},3r)\setminus B_{Z}(p_{i},2r))
; in particular the
annular estimate above gives

(5.14)

\sup_{\partial_{Y_{j}}W_{i,j}}v_{j}\leq v(p_{i})-2\eta_{i}

whenever the boundary is non-empty. If the boundary is empty, the same statement
holds with the convention
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
. Combining
(
5.13
) and
(
5.14
) proves
(
5.8
). Any point where
v_{j}
attains
its maximum on
\overline{W}_{i,j}
must lie in
W_{i,j}
and is therefore a local
maximum of
v_{j}
on
Y_{j}
. Since the sets
W_{i,j}
are pairwise disjoint, these
local maxima are distinct.
∎

5.3.
A generic perturbation lemma

Proposition 5.6

(Non-degenerate perturbation)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth closed Riemannian surface with positive Gaussian
curvature. Assume that
\lambda_{1}(g)
is simple, and let
u
be an

L^{2}
-normalized
\lambda_{1}(g)
-eigenfunction. Suppose that there are pairwise
disjoint open sets
W_{1},\ldots,W_{m}\subset M
, each with non-empty boundary, such
that

(5.15)

\max_{\overline{W}_{i}}u>\sup_{\partial W_{i}}u\qquad(i=1,\ldots,m),

where closures and boundaries are taken in
M
. Then, in every
C^{\infty}

neighborhood of
g
, there exists a smooth metric
\widetilde{g}
such that

K_{\widetilde{g}}>0
,
\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{g})
is simple, and, after choosing
its sign, an
L^{2}
-normalized
\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{g})
-eigenfunction has at
least
m
distinct non-degenerate local maxima.

If, in addition,
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, then
(M,\widetilde{g})

admits a smooth isometric embedding in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as a strictly convex surface.

Proof.

Let

\sigma:=\min_{1\leq i\leq m}\left(\max_{\overline{W}_{i}}u-\sup_{\partial W_{i}}u\right)>0.

Since
\lambda_{1}(g)
is simple and

0=\lambda_{0}(g)<\lambda_{1}(g)<\lambda_{2}(g),

standard elliptic perturbation theory for a simple eigenvalue implies that, after
choosing signs, the
L^{2}
-normalized first positive eigenfunction depends
continuously on the metric in the
C^{0}
topology under sufficiently small

C^{\infty}
perturbations of
g
. Thus there is a
C^{\infty}
neighborhood

\mathcal{U}_{0}
of
g
such that, for every
h\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
, the normalized

\lambda_{1}(h)
-eigenfunction
u_{h}
can be chosen so that

(5.16)

\|u_{h}-u\|_{C^{0}(M)}<\frac{\sigma}{4}.

Shrinking
\mathcal{U}_{0}
if necessary, we may also assume that

K_{h}>0
and that
\lambda_{1}(h)
remains simple for all
h\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
; the
first assertion follows from the
C^{2}
-openness of positive Gaussian curvature,
and the second from spectral-gap continuity.

For every
h\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
, estimate
(
5.16
) gives

\max_{\overline{W}_{i}}u_{h}\geq\max_{\overline{W}_{i}}u-\frac{\sigma}{4}>\sup_{\partial W_{i}}u+\frac{3\sigma}{4}\geq\sup_{\partial W_{i}}u_{h}+\frac{\sigma}{2}.

Hence
u_{h}
has at least one local maximum in each
W_{i}
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be any prescribed
C^{\infty}
neighborhood of
g
. By
Uhlenbeck’s generic theorem
[
30
]
, the set of smooth metrics for
which all non-zero eigenvalues are simple and all corresponding eigenfunctions
are Morse is residual and dense. Therefore we may choose

\widetilde{g}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{U}_{0}
with this generic property. The
preceding boundary-gap argument applied to
h=\widetilde{g}
gives at least one
local maximum of the normalized
\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{g})
-eigenfunction in each

W_{i}
. Since this eigenfunction is Morse, all its critical points are
non-degenerate; in particular these local maxima are non-degenerate. The sets

W_{i}
are pairwise disjoint, so the local maxima obtained in this way are
distinct.

If
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the smooth solution of Weyl’s isometric
embedding problem for positively curved metrics on the two-sphere, due to
Nirenberg and Pogorelov
[
25
,
27
]
, gives a smooth
isometric embedding of
(M,\widetilde{g})
into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as a strictly convex
surface.
∎

6.
Proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
m\geq 2
. Choose the Miyamoto polygon
\Omega
and eigenfunction
\phi
from
Theorem
2.1
. Let
X=D\Omega
and let
\Phi
be the normalized even
double from (
3.11
). By
Proposition
3.4
,
\Phi
is the unique normalized

\lambda_{1}(X)
-eigenfunction up to sign and has at least
m
isolated strict
local maxima.

Let
M_{j}
be the smooth positively curved pancakes from
Proposition
4.5
. By
Proposition
5.4
, for all sufficiently large
j

the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(M_{j})
is simple; after passing to a subsequence and
choosing signs, normalized
\lambda_{1}(M_{j})
-eigenfunctions
u_{j}
converge
uniformly to
\Phi
along measured Gromov–Hausdorff approximations.

We now verify the hypotheses of Lemma
5.5
. By the concrete
form of Honda convergence stated in Theorem
5.1
, after passing to
the same subsequence we have measured Gromov–Hausdorff approximation maps

\mathcal{F}_{j}:M_{j}\to X
and numbers
\gamma_{j},\beta_{j}\downarrow 0
such that

\mathcal{F}_{j}
is a
\gamma_{j}
-Gromov–Hausdorff approximation and

(6.1)

\sup_{q\in M_{j}}|u_{j}(q)-\Phi(\mathcal{F}_{j}(q))|\leq\beta_{j}.

By the standard realization theorem for Gromov–Hausdorff convergence, realize

M_{j}
and
X
isometrically in a common compact metric space
Z
so that

(6.2)

d_{Z}(q,\mathcal{F}_{j}(q))\leq\gamma_{j}\qquad(q\in M_{j}).

Since
\mathcal{F}_{j}(M_{j})
is
\gamma_{j}
-dense in
X
, this realization also gives

d_{H}^{Z}(M_{j},X)\leq 2\gamma_{j}
. Set

\rho_{j}:=2\gamma_{j},

so that
\rho_{j}\downarrow 0
and

(6.3)

d_{H}^{Z}(M_{j},X)\leq\rho_{j},\qquad\gamma_{j}\leq\rho_{j}.

Let
\omega_{\Phi}
be a modulus of continuity of
\Phi
on the compact space
X
.
If
q\in M_{j}
,
y\in X
, and
d_{Z}(q,y)\leq\rho_{j}
, then, by
(
6.2
),

d_{X}(\mathcal{F}_{j}(q),y)=d_{Z}(\mathcal{F}_{j}(q),y)\leq d_{Z}(\mathcal{F}_{j}(q),q)+d_{Z}(q,y)\leq\gamma_{j}+\rho_{j}\leq 2\rho_{j}.

Here the equality holds because
\mathcal{F}_{j}(q)
and
y
both belong to the
isometrically embedded copy of
X
inside
Z
.
Therefore, by (
6.1
),

(6.4)

|u_{j}(q)-\Phi(y)|\leq|u_{j}(q)-\Phi(\mathcal{F}_{j}(q))|+|\Phi(\mathcal{F}_{j}(q))-\Phi(y)|\leq\beta_{j}+\omega_{\Phi}(2\rho_{j})\to 0.

Equations (
6.3
) and
(
6.4
) are precisely the hypotheses
(
5.6
) and (
5.7
) of
Lemma
5.5
, with
Y_{j}=M_{j}
,
Y=X
,
v_{j}=u_{j}
, and
v=\Phi
.
Thus, for all sufficiently large
j
, there are pairwise disjoint open sets

W_{1,j},\ldots,W_{m,j}\subset M_{j}
such that

(6.5)

\max_{\overline{W}_{i,j}}u_{j}>\sup_{\partial W_{i,j}}u_{j}\qquad(i=1,\ldots,m).

In particular,
u_{j}
has at least
m
distinct local maxima. Since
m\geq 2
and
M_{j}
is connected, each
W_{i,j}
is a proper non-empty
open subset of
M_{j}
. Hence
\partial W_{i,j}\neq\emptyset
for every
i
.

Choose such a sufficiently large
j
. Let
g_{j}
be the induced metric on the
smooth embedded pancake
M_{j}
. Applying
Proposition
5.6
to

(M_{j},g_{j})
, the eigenfunction
u_{j}
, and the sets

W_{1,j},\ldots,W_{m,j}
, we obtain a smooth metric
\widetilde{g}_{j}
on the
underlying two-sphere, arbitrarily close to
g_{j}
, such that

K_{\widetilde{g}_{j}}>0
,
\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{g}_{j})
is simple, and a normalized

\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{g}_{j})
-eigenfunction
\widetilde{u}_{j}
, after choosing its
sign, has at least
m
distinct non-degenerate local maxima.

By the last assertion of Proposition
5.6
,
there is a smooth isometric embedding

F_{j}:(M_{j},\widetilde{g}_{j})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}

as a strictly convex surface. Set

The induced metric on

is isometric to
(M_{j},\widetilde{g}_{j})
, hence has
positive Gaussian curvature and simple first positive eigenvalue. Transporting

\widetilde{u}_{j}
by the isometry gives a normalized first nonzero eigenfunction
on
M_{m}
with at least
m
distinct non-degenerate local maxima. Since the first
eigenspace is one-dimensional, the opposite sign has at least
m
distinct
non-degenerate local minima.
∎
