Maximal universal invariants of knots from finite quotients of Verma modules
Cristina Anghel, Jun Murakami

Abstract
. We construct a sequence of new universal quantum knot invariants that are lifts of both the semi-simple and non semi-simple U q ​ ( s ​ l 2 ) U_{q}(sl_{2}) quantum knot invariants. More specifically, for any level 𝒩 \mathcal{N} we define a “level 𝒩 \mathcal{N} universal invariant” Ω ~ 𝒩 ​ ( L ) \widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathcal{N}}(L) arising from quantum traces on finite quotients of the generic Verma module over certain quotient rings. We show that for 𝒩 \mathcal{N} prime, this is the maximal invariant that can arise from the 𝒩 \mathcal{N} -part of the Verma module, and it is a specific interpolation between the 𝒩 t ​ h \mathcal{N}^{th} coloured Jones and 𝒩 t ​ h \mathcal{N}^{th} ADO polynomials. For 𝒩 \mathcal{N} non prime Ω ~ 𝒩 ​ ( L ) ​ ( q , s ) \widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s) has a richer structure, it recovers the 𝒩 t ​ h \mathcal{N}^{th} coloured Jones and 𝒩 t ​ h \mathcal{N}^{th} ADO polynomials, but it could contain more information which is not seen in the sequence of coloured Jones and ADO invariants.



1.
Introduction

We study quantum invariants arising from the infinite dimensional representation theory of the quantum group
U_{q}(sl_{2})
.
The family of finite dimensional representations of this quantum group at generic
q
leads to the sequence of coloured Jones polynomials whereas the representations at roots of unity give the sequence of coloured Alexander polynomials (or ADO invariants
[
1
]
).
The asymptotic behaviour of these invariants is subject to important questions in low dimensional topology: the Volume Conjecture (Kashaev
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
) and Gukov-Manolescu’s Conjecture (
[
9
]
) which predict geometric information that is contained in their limit with respect to the colour.
Motivated by the question of the conjectured geometry contained in these invariants the second author provided a unified topological framework that encodes all coloured Jones and ADO polynomials at bounded colours by graded intersections in configuration spaces (
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
). They lead to universal geometrical invariants for links (
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
). These unification results opened up questions about encoding non-semisimple invariants and semi-simple invariants directly from a unified perspective. In
[
6
]
the author defined a globalisation of Jones and Alexander invariants by constructing a graded intersection that is taking values in a quotient of a polynomial ring in two variables.

Main results
In this paper we address this problem at higher colours, and we want to unify coloured Jones and coloured Alexander invariants corresponding to
\mathcal{N}
dimensional representations of
U_{q}(sl_{2})
.
Our strategy is to use quantum groups over two variables together with their braid group action directly on the infinite dimensional representation, which is given by the Verma module. We will work over the Laurent polynomial ring
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}]
.

Question:
For a fixed level
\mathcal{N}
, which is the maximal quotient of
\mathbb{L}
in which we can obtain invariants from the
\mathcal{N}
-dimensional part of the
U_{q}(sl_{2})
-Verma module?
We start from the generic braid action on the Verma module and define quantum traces on specialisations of the
\mathcal{N}
-dimensional part over certain quotient rings. We prove the following:

1)

Level
\mathcal{N}
unified invariant at prime levels.
If
\mathcal{N}
is prime, then the maximal quotient ring of
\mathbb{L}
in which we get invariants from Verma modules is given by the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{N}}
(as in (
1.1
), Corollary
1.9
).
The quantum trace in this quotient leads to a
“Level
\mathcal{N}
unified invariant” (for any level
\mathcal{N}
):

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)\in{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}}.

2)

Level
\mathcal{N}
universal invariant.
If
\mathcal{N}
is not prime, then the maximal quotient ring in which we get invariants from Verma modules is given by the ideal
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}
(see (
1.2
), Theorem
1.8
).
The intersection form in this quotient leads to a maximal invariant called “Level
\mathcal{N}
universal invariant”:

\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}.

3)

Interpolation formula in the level
\mathcal{N}
quotient ring.
The level
\mathcal{N}
unified invariant
\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)
is an interpolation between the coloured Jones and ADO invariants at level
\mathcal{N}
, which we compute explicitely in Theorem
1.10
.

1.1.
Quotient rings

Let us fix a natural number

Definition 1.1

(Level

We are going to define two ideals in the ring

1.3.
Unique quantum traces over quotient rings

We want to define knot invariants from quantum traces that are constructed on the endomorphism rings of these modules.
First, we will prove that there exists a unique quantum trace associated the level
\mathcal{N}
maximal module.

Definition 1.5
.

A map
\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}:\mathrm{End}(\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes(n+1)})\to\mathrm{End}(\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n})

defined by

\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}(f)_{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n}}}^{v_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n}}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(q,s)\cdot f_{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n},j}}^{v_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n},j}}

for some scalars

is called a “quantum partial trace” if there are two non-zero scalars
a
,
b
satisfying

(1.7)

\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\sigma_{n}\beta_{n}))=a\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\beta_{n}),\quad\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\sigma_{n}^{-1}\beta_{n}))=a^{-1}\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\beta_{n})

for any braid
\beta_{n}\in B_{n}
(see also Notation
2.3
).

Theorem 1.6

(Construction of a unique quantum trace over quotient rings)
.

There exists a unique quantum trace, up to a scalar, defined on the tensor powers of the maximal module (Theorem
4.5

\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}:\operatorname{End}(\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes(n+1)})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}(\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}),

where
g_{j}(q,s)=g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2j}
for some non-zero
g_{0}(q,s)
.

1.5.
Universal versus unified invariants

In subsection
5
we study relations between these invariants, and we show in Theorem
5.3
that the level
\mathcal{N}
universal invariant recovers the level
\mathcal{N}
unified invariant. Then in Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
5.3
we show that both level
\mathcal{N}
universal and unified invariant specialise to the
\mathcal{N}^{th}
coloured Jones and ADO polynomials by change of coefficients.

Then, we show that for any
\mathcal{N}
(not necessary a prime number) the level
\mathcal{N}
invariant
\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)
is an interpolation between the coloured Jones and ADO invariants at level
\mathcal{N}
, as below.

Theorem 1.10

(
Level
\mathcal{N}
unified invariant interpolates coloured Jones and ADO invariants).

We have that
\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)\in{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
is a well defined oriented knot invariant and globalises the
\mathcal{N}^{th}
coloured Jones and coloured Alexander invariants, as follows:

(1.9)

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)=J_{\mathcal{N}}(L,q)+\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L,s)-\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L,q^{1-\mathcal{N}}).

As we have seen above, the level
\mathcal{N}
invariant
\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)\in{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
has a clear structure, providing a globalisation that interpolates coloured Jones and ADO invariants at level
\mathcal{N}
.

Remark 1.11

(Rich structure of the level
\mathcal{N}
universal invariant)
.

The level
\mathcal{N}
universal invariant
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}
has a richer structure, belonging to maximal ring which surjects onto the interpolation ring. We have well-defined specialisation maps from
\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}
to the rings where the
\mathcal{N}^{th}
coloured Jones and ADO invariants belong, so
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)
recovers
J_{\mathcal{N}}(L)
and
\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L)
by specialisation of coefficients.

On the other hand, the maximal ring
\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}
is given by the ideal

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\left(\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2})\cdot(s^{2}q^{-2\mathcal{N}+2}-1)\right)\cap\prod_{\text{\scriptsize$\begin{matrix}d\mid\mathcal{N}\\
d\neq 1,\mathcal{N}\end{matrix}$}}\left(\varphi_{d}(q^{2}),\frac{s^{2d}-1}{s^{2}-1}\right)\subseteq\mathbb{L}.

If we fix
d\mid\mathcal{N}
, the presence of the ideal

\left(\varphi_{d}(q^{2}),\frac{s^{2d}-1}{s^{2}-1}\right)

suggests that
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)
is related to ADO invariants at level
d
and an invariant arising from Verma modules at the highest weight parameter being the
d^{th}
root of unity. This shows that this
d
-part of the ideal, generated by both terms
\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2})
and
\frac{s^{2d}-1}{s^{2}-1}
has a rich structure which in turn suggests that the invariant itself could encode deeper information than the sequence of coloured Jones and ADO invariants at levels that divide
\mathcal{N}
.

2.1.
Notations

We will use the following notations for quantum numbers:

\{x\}=q^{x}-q^{-x},\ \ \ \ [x]_{q}=\frac{q^{x}-q^{-x}}{q-q^{-1}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ [n]_{q}!=[1]_{q}\cdot[2]_{q}\cdot\ldots\cdot[n]_{q},

{n\brack j}_{q}=\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[n-j]_{q}![j]_{q}!}.

Notation 2.1
.

(Specialisation of coefficients)

Let us consider two rings
R,S
and a free
R
-module
M
. We suppose that we have a fixed basis
\mathscr{B}
of
M
. We suppose that we have a specialisation of coefficients that is given by a ring morphism:

\psi:R\rightarrow S.

Then, we define the specialisation of
M
by the change of the coefficients
\psi
as the following
S
-module:

M|_{\psi}:=M\otimes_{R}S

which has the corresponding basis given by

\mathscr{B}_{M|_{\psi}}:=\mathscr{B}\otimes_{R}1\in M|_{\psi}.

Notation 2.2

(Rings and quotient rings)
.

We will use the Laurent polynomial rings, denoted as below:

{\mathbb{L}}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}],\qquad\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}].

Notation 2.3
.

(Endomorphism coefficients) Let us consider a set of indices

i_{1},...,i_{n}\in\{0,\cdots,\mathcal{N}-1\}.

We define the associated vector:

v_{i_{1},...,i_{n}}:=v_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots v_{i_{n}}\in\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}.

Also, for an endomorphism

we denote by:

(2.1)

f_{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n}}}^{v_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n}}}:=\operatorname{Coeff}(v_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n}},f(v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n}})).

Here, for a fixed basis of a module
V_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}
and two basis vectors
v

w
we denote by

the coefficient of the vector
v
in the decomposition of

with respect to the fixed basis.

2.2.
Verma module of
U_{q}(sl_{2})

Let us fix
q,s
to be parameters and in the next part we are going to work over the ring

{\mathbb{L}}
.
We will use the version of the quantum group
U_{q}(sl_{2})
with divided powers, as presented in
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Quantum group over two variables)
.

Let
U_{q}(sl_{2})
be the algebra over
{\mathbb{L}}
that is generated by the sequence of elements
\{E,\,F^{(n)},\,K^{\pm 1}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
subject to the following relations:

\displaystyle KK^{-1}=K^{-1}K=1;\ \ \ KE=q^{2}EK;\ \ \ KF^{(n)}=q^{-2n}F^{(n)}K;

\displaystyle F^{(n)}F^{(m)}={n+m\brack n}_{q}F^{(n+m)};

\displaystyle[E,F^{(n+1)}]=F^{(n)}(q^{-n}K-q^{n}K^{-1}).

Then
U_{q}(sl_{2})
has a structure of a Hopf algebra (as in
[
10
]
).

Definition 2.5

(Verma module)
.

We define
\hat{V}
to be the
{\mathbb{L}}
-module that is generated by a sequence of vectors that we denote as
\{v_{0},v_{1},...\}
. Then, we consider the following
U_{q}(sl_{2})
action on this module
\hat{V}
(see
[
10
]
):

\begin{cases}Kv_{i}=sq^{-2i}v_{i},\\
Ev_{i}=v_{i-1},\\
\displaystyle F^{(n)}v_{i}={n+i\brack i}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(sq^{-k-i}-s^{-1}q^{k+i})v_{i+n}.\end{cases}

Further, using the structure of the Hopf algebra, we have a braiding that leads to the following
R
-matrix.

Proposition 2.6

(Generic R-matrix action (
[
10
]
))
.

We have the following action on the basis of the tensor power
\hat{V}\otimes\hat{V}
:

(2.2)

R(v_{i}\otimes v_{j})=s^{-(i+j)}\sum_{n=0}^{i}q^{2(i-n)(j+n)}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}{n+j\brack j}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(sq^{-k-j}-s^{-1}q^{k+j})\ v_{j+n}\otimes v_{i-n}

(2.3)

R^{-1}(v_{i}\otimes v_{j})=s^{(i+j)}\sum_{n=0}^{j}(-1)^{n}q^{-2ij}q^{-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}{n+i\brack i}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(sq^{-i-k}-s^{-1}q^{k+i})\ v_{j-n}\otimes v_{i+n}.

Then, this will lead to a braid group representation, which we denote as follows:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{n}:B_{n}\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{U_{q}(sl_{2})}\left(\hat{V}^{\otimes n}\right),

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \sigma_{i}^{\pm 1}\mapsto Id_{V}^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes R^{\pm 1}\otimes Id_{V}^{\otimes(n-i-1)}.

3.
Maximal quotient rings

In this section, we introduce various expressions of the ideal
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}
in
{\mathbb{L}}
to define the maximal quotient ring
\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

3.1.
Defining ideals

Definition 3.1

(Condition ideal)
.

Let us consider the following ideals in
\mathbb{L}
:

\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N},k}:=\left(\varphi_{k}(q^{2}),\ \ \prod_{i=0}^{k-2}(s^{2}q^{-2i}-1)\right)

for
k|\mathcal{N}
,
k\neq 1
,
2
,
\mathcal{N}-1
,
\mathcal{N}
where
\varphi_{k}
is the
k^{th}
cyclotomic polynomial, and

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{N}}\cap\!\!\!\!\bigcap_{\text{\scriptsize$\begin{matrix}d|\mathcal{N}\\[-1.0pt]
d\neq 1,\mathcal{N}\end{matrix}$}}\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N},d},

where
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{N}}=\Big(\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2})\cdot(s^{2}q^{-2\mathcal{N}+2}-1)\Big)
given by (
1.1
).
We call
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{N}}
the “condition ideal”.

Definition 3.2

(Maximal quotient ideal)
.

Let us consider as well the ideal

(3.1)

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\
j+n\geqslant\mathcal{N},0\leqslant n\leqslant i\end{subarray}}^{\mathcal{N}-1}\left({n+j\brack j}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(sq^{-k-j}-s^{-1}q^{k+j})\right)\subseteq\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{N}}

and call it the “maximal quotient ideal”.

3.2.
Coincidence of defining ideals

We show that the three ideals
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{N}}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}
are equal.

Proposition 3.3

(Structural inclusion of condition ideals)
.

The above three mentioned structural ideals coincide and they also coincide with
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}
, i.e.

(3.2)

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}.

The proof of this proposition is given in Appendix
6
.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{N}
is prime, then

(3.3)

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{N}}.

Remark 3.5
.

The equality (
3.2
) holds when working over rational coefficients, this is not true if the ground ring is
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1},s^{\pm 1}]
instead of
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1},s^{\pm 1}]
.
On the other hand, the equality (
3.3
) also holds over the ground ring
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1},s^{\pm 1}]
.

3.3.
Maximal quotient ring at level
\mathcal{N}

Definition 3.6

(Quotient ring at non prime levels)
.

Let us consider the following quotient ring, and call it “maximal quotient ring”:

\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}]\ /{\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}]\ /{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}}.

.

If
\mathcal{N}
is prime, then

\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}={\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}={\mathbb{L}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{N}}.

4.
Level
\mathcal{N}
universal invariant

Our aim is to obtain knot invariants at level
\mathcal{N}
, in this case for any natural number
\mathcal{N}
(not necessary prime as in the previous sections). In the following sections we will see that if
\mathcal{N}
is not prime, we are able to obtain invariants in rings that are higher than the ring that we have defined for the prime case, which is
{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

We do so by looking at the
\mathcal{N}
-dimensional submodule arising from the generic Verma module, when specialised over this higher order quotient ring.

Notation 4.1
.

(
\mathcal{N}
-dimensional submodule over the quotient ring)
Let us consider the specialisation the
\mathcal{N}
-dimensional submodule in the Verma module
V_{\mathcal{N}}
specialised over the level
\mathcal{N}
quotient ring as below:

\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}:=\langle v_{0},...,v_{\mathcal{N}-1}\rangle_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}}\subseteq\hat{V}\mid_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}}.

Our aim is to show that we can obtain knot invariants in
\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}
. In order to do so we are going to use braid group actions on tensor powers of
\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

4.1.
Braid action over the quotient ring

First we will see that the specialised
R
-matrix at these higher rings leads to a well defined action onto
\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}
.

Proposition 4.2

(Action on the higher level
\mathcal{N}
module over the quotient ring)
.

The action
\hat{\rho}_{n}
given by (
2.4
) specialized to the level
\mathcal{N}
quotient ring
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
leads to a well defined induced braid group action:

\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}:B_{n}\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}}\left(\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}\right).

Proof.

The condition that we want to prove is that the action
\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}
on
\hat{V}\mid_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}}^{\otimes n}
preserves the vector subspace
\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}
.
Following the definition of the action
\hat{\rho}_{n}
, it is enough prove that
the action of
R
preserves the vector subspace
{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes 2}}
.
We will show the following property:

R\mid_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}}(v_{i}\otimes v_{j})\in\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes 2},\ \ \ \forall i,j\in\{0,...,\mathcal{N}-1\}.

Let
i,j\in\{0,...,\mathcal{N}-1\}
.
The
R
-matrix action is given by (
2.2
) as follows:

R(v_{i}\otimes v_{j})=s^{-(i+j)}\sum_{n=0}^{i}q^{2(i-n)(j+n)}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}{n+j\brack j}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(sq^{-k-j}-s^{-1}q^{k+j})\ v_{j+n}\otimes v_{i-n}.

We recall that we work over the quotient ring

\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}]\ /{\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}}
,
and this is identified with

\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}]\ /{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}}

using Definition
3.6
.
Let us also recall the definition of the ideal
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
from relation (
3.1
):

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\
j+n\geqslant\mathcal{N},0\leqslant n\leqslant i\end{subarray}}^{\mathcal{N}-1}\left({n+j\brack j}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(sq^{-k-j}-s^{-1}q^{k+j})\right)\subseteq\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{N}}

Let

\widetilde{\pi}:\mathbb{L}\rightarrow\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}]\ /{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}}.

be the canonical projection.
We are going to prove that for any indices
i,j\leqslant\mathcal{N}-1\text{ and }j+n\geqslant\mathcal{N}
, we have that:

\widetilde{\pi}\left({n+j\brack j}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(sq^{-k-j}-s^{-1}q^{k+j})\right)=0\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}.

This is equivalent to:

{n+j\brack j}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(sq^{-k-j}-s^{-1}q^{k+j})\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}

if
i,j\leqslant\mathcal{N}-1\text{ and }j+n\geqslant\mathcal{N}
, which holds following the definition of the ideal
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

In turn, this shows that the coefficient of
v_{i}\otimes v_{j}
in
R(v_{i}\otimes v_{j})
vanishes if
j+n\geqslant\mathcal{N}
. So, the image of
R(v_{i}\otimes v_{j})|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}}
is a linear combination of tensors with two components such that their indices are all bounded by
\mathcal{N}-1
. This shows precisely that the action coming from the 2-variable
R
-matrix preserves the module
\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes 2}
(once specialised to the quotient
\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\mathcal{N}}
) and concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Following the above proof we notice that the subset
\left.\left<v_{0},\cdots\!,v_{\mathcal{N}-1}\right>\right|{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}}
of
\hat{V}\mid_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}}
is also invariant under the action of the quantum group
U_{q}(sl_{2})
.

4.2.
Unique quantum trace at level
\mathcal{N}

We recall that our aim is to obtain knot invariants. In order to construct them our strategy will be to make use of the braid group action on tensor powers of the generic Verma module defined above. Our construction will be split into two main parts. So far, we considered a certain quotient ring such that we have a well defined braid action once we specialise the finite part from the Verma module over this ring. Now, once we have such an action, we are going to construct a unique quantum trace, which in turn will provide our knot invariants.

Definition 4.4
.

A quantum partial trace is a map

\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}:\operatorname{End}(\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes(n+1)})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}(\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n})

a map which has the form

\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}(f)_{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n}}}^{v_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n}}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(q,s)\cdot f_{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n},j}}^{v_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n},j}}

and which satisfies

(4.1)

\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},{n+1}}(\sigma_{n}\beta_{n}))=a\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\beta_{n}),\quad\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},{n+1}}(\sigma_{n}^{-1}\beta_{n}))=a^{-1}\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\beta_{n})

for any
\beta_{n}\in B_{n}
and a non-zero element
a\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

Theorem 4.5

(Unique level
\mathcal{N}
quantum trace)
.

There exists a unique quantum trace
\operatorname{qptr}_{V_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}
, up to a scalar multiple,
for the level
\mathcal{N}
sequence of braid group representations

\{\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
In other words, for any choice of a quantum trace associated to
\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}
there is a non-zero element
g_{0}(q,s)\in{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
such that

\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}(f)_{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n}}}^{v_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n}}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2i}\cdot f_{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n},j}}^{v_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n},j}}.

Proof.

First of all, it is well-known that if
g_{j}(q,s)=g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2i}
, then
g_{j}(q,s)
satisfies the condition (
4.1
).

So we only have to prove that
g_{j}(q,s)
must be
g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2i}
.
Since the quantum partial trace of

\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\sigma_{n}^{\pm 1}\beta_{n})

is given by

\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}}\left(\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\sigma_{n}^{\pm 1}\beta_{n})\right)_{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n}}}^{v_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n}}}=\\
=\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(s,q)\left(\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\sigma_{n}^{\pm 1}\beta_{n})\right)_{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n},j}}^{v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{n},j}}=\\
=\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(s,q)\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}(id^{\otimes n-1}\otimes R^{\pm 1})_{v_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n-1},k,j}}^{v_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n-1},j_{n},j}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\beta_{n})\otimes id)_{v_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},i_{n},j}}^{v_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n-1},k,j}}=\\
=\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(s,q)\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}(R^{\pm 1})_{k,j}^{v_{j_{n},j}}\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\beta_{n})_{v_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},i_{n}}}^{v_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n-1},k}}=\\
=\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(s,q)\cdot(R^{\pm 1})_{v_{j_{n},j}}^{v_{j_{n},j}}\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathcal{N},n}(\beta_{n})_{v_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},i_{n}}}^{v_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n-1},j_{n}}}=\\
=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(s,q)\cdot(R^{\pm 1})_{v_{j_{n},j}}^{v_{j_{n},j}}\right)\rho_{\mathcal{N},n}(\beta_{n})_{v_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1},i_{n}}}^{v_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n-1},j_{n}}},

we only have to show that
\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(s,q)\cdot(R^{\pm 1})_{v_{i,j}}^{v_{i,j}}
is a non-zero scalar independent of
i
.

We see
\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(s,q)\cdot R_{v_{i,j}}^{v_{i,j}}
to show that
g_{j}(s,q)
must be
g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2j}

by an induction on
M\in\{0,\dots,\mathcal{N}\}
.
To show

g_{i}(q,s)=q^{-2i}\cdot g_{0}(q,s),\quad\forall\ 0\leqslant i\leqslant M,

we use the fact

(4.2)

\sum_{j=0}^{M}q^{-2j}\cdot\left(R_{v_{M,j}}^{v_{M,j}}-R_{v_{M+1,j}}^{v_{M+1,j}}\right)=q^{-2(M+1)}R_{v_{M+1,M+1}}^{v_{M+1,M+1}}

given in the following Lemma
4.7
.
Now we prove that, if the sum
\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{j}(s,q)\cdot R_{v_{i,j}}^{v_{i,j}}
is not depend on
i
, then

\text{P(M):}\qquad g_{j}(q,s)=g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2j}\quad\text{for $0\leqslant j\leqslant M$}

holds by induction on
M
.

For the case
M=0
, we have

0=g_{0}(q,s)\cdot R_{v_{0,0}}^{v_{0,0}}-g_{0}(q,s)\cdot R_{v_{0,1}}^{v_{0,1}}-g_{1}(q,s)\cdot R_{v_{1,1}}^{v_{1,1}}=(g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2}-g_{1}(q,s))R_{v_{1,1}}^{v_{1,1}}

by (
4.2
) and we get
g_{1}(q,s)=q^{-2}
.

Assuming that P(M) is true, then

0=\sum_{j=0}^{M}g_{j}(q,s)\cdot R_{v_{M,j}}^{v_{M,j}}-\sum_{j=0}^{M+1}g_{j}(q,s)\cdot R_{v_{M+1,j}}^{v_{M+1,j}}=\\
\sum_{j=0}^{M}g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2j}\cdot R_{v_{M,j}}^{v_{M,j}}-\sum_{j=0}^{M}g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2j}\cdot R_{v_{M+1,j}}^{v_{M+1,j}}-g_{M+1}(q,s)R_{v_{M+1,M+1}}^{v_{M+1,M+1}}=\\
(g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2(M+1)}-g_{M+1}(q,s))R_{v_{M+1,M+1}}^{v_{M+1,M+1}}.

Hence
g_{M+1}(q,s)=q_{0}(q,s)q^{-2(M+2)}
and P(M+1) is true.

We have

\sum_{j=0}^{0}g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2j}\cdot R_{v_{0,j}}^{v_{0,j}}=g_{0}(q,s)R_{v_{0,0}}^{v_{0,0}}=g_{0}(q,s),

and

\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}g_{0}(q,s)q^{-2j}\cdot(R^{-1})_{v_{\mathcal{N}-1,j}}^{v_{\mathcal{N}-1,j}}=g_{0}(q,s){q^{-2(\mathcal{N}-1)}}(R^{-1})_{v_{\mathcal{N}-1,\mathcal{N}-1}}^{v_{\mathcal{N}-1,\mathcal{N}-1}}=s^{2\mathcal{N}-2}q^{{-2\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}-1)}}.

Therefore, by putting
g_{0}(q,s)=s^{-\mathcal{N}+1}q^{{\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}-1)}}
,
we get (
4.1
) with
a=s^{-\mathcal{N}+1}q^{{\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}-1)}}
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The matrix element
R_{v_{M+1,M+1}}^{v_{M+1,M+1}}
is invertible in
{\mathbb{L}}
, so we also get
g_{M+1}(q,s)=q_{0}(q,s)q^{-2(M+2)}
in
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
and
{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

The following holds for any
M\in\{0,\dots,\mathcal{N}-2\}
:

(4.3)

\sum_{j=0}^{M}q^{-2j}\cdot\left(R_{v_{M,j}}^{v_{M,j}}-R_{v_{M+1,j}}^{v_{M+1,j}}\right)=q^{-2(M+1)}R_{v_{M+1,M+1}}^{v_{M+1,M+1}}.

Proof.

Recall that

R_{v_{i,j}}^{v_{i,j}}=s^{-(i+j)}q^{\frac{(i+j)^{2}-i+j}{2}}{i\brack j}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{i-j-1}(sq^{-k-j}-s^{-1}q^{k+j}).

Hence we have

R_{v_{M,0}}^{v_{M,0}}-R_{v_{M+1,0}}^{v_{M+1,0}}=s^{-M}q^{\frac{M^{2}-M}{2}}{M\brack 0}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}(sq^{-k}-s^{-1}q^{k})-\\
s^{-(M+1)}q^{\frac{M^{2}-M+2M}{2}}{M+1\brack 0}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{M}(sq^{-k}-s^{-1}q^{k})\\
=s^{-M}q^{\frac{M^{2}-M}{2}}\left(1-1+s^{-2}q^{2M}\right)\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}(sq^{-k}-s^{-1}q^{k})\\
=s^{-M-2}q^{\frac{M^{2}+3M}{2}}\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}(sq^{-k}-s^{-1}q^{k}),

R_{v_{M,0}}^{v_{M,0}}-R_{v_{M+1,0}}^{v_{M+1,0}}-q^{-2}R_{v_{M+1,1}}^{v_{M+1,1}}=\\
s^{-M-2}q^{\frac{M^{2}+3M}{2}}\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}(sq^{-k}-s^{-1}q^{k})-\\
s^{-M-2}q^{\frac{M^{2}+3M}{2}}{M+1\brack 1}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{M-1}(sq^{-k-1}-s^{-1}q^{k+1})=\\
s^{-M-2}q^{\frac{M^{2}+3M}{2}}\left((s-s^{-1})-\frac{\{M+1\}}{\{1\}}(sq^{-M}-s^{-1}q^{M})\right)\prod_{k=1}^{M-1}(sq^{-k}-s^{-1}q^{k})=\\
s^{-M-2}q^{\frac{M^{2}+3M}{2}}\times\hfill\\
\frac{1}{\{1\}}\left((q-q^{-1})(s-s^{-1})-(q^{M+1}-q^{-M-1})(sq^{-M}-s^{-1}q^{M})\right)\prod_{k=1}^{M-1}(sq^{-k}-s^{-1}q^{k})=\\
-s^{-M-2}q^{\frac{M^{2}+3M}{2}}{M\brack 1}_{q}\left(sq^{-M-1}-s^{-1}q^{M+1}\right)\prod_{k=0}^{M-2}(sq^{-k-1}-s^{-1}q^{k+1}),

R_{v_{M,0}}^{v_{M,0}}-R_{v_{M+1,0}}^{v_{M+1,0}}-q^{-2}(R_{v_{M+1,1}}^{v_{M+1,1}}-R_{v_{M,1}}^{v_{M,1}})=\\
-s^{-M-2}q^{\frac{M^{2}+3M}{2}}{M\brack 1}_{q}\left(sq^{-M-1}-s^{-1}q^{M+1}\right)\prod_{k=0}^{M-2}(sq^{-k-1}-s^{-1}q^{k+1})+\\
s^{-M-1}q^{\frac{M^{2}+M-2}{2}}{M\brack 1}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{M-2}(sq^{-k-1}-s^{-1}q^{k+1})=\\
-s^{-M-2}q^{\frac{M^{2}+M-2}{2}}{M\brack 1}_{q}\left(q^{M+1}(sq^{-M-1}-s^{-1}q^{M+1})-s\right)\prod_{k=0}^{M-2}(sq^{-k-1}-s^{-1}q^{k+1})=\\
s^{-M-3}q^{\frac{M^{2}+5M+2}{2}}{M\brack 1}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{M-2}(sq^{-k-1}-s^{-1}q^{k+1}),

R_{v_{M,0}}^{v_{M,0}}-R_{v_{M+1,0}}^{v_{M+1,0}}-q^{-2}(R_{v_{M+1,1}}^{v_{M+1,1}}-R_{v_{M,1}}^{v_{M,1}})-q^{-4}(R_{v_{M+1,2}}^{v_{M+1,2}}-R_{v_{M,2}}^{v_{M,2}})=\\
s^{-M-4}q^{\frac{M^{2}+7M+6}{2}}{M\brack 2}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{M-3}(sq^{-k-1}-s^{-1}q^{k+1}),

R_{v_{M,0}}^{v_{M,0}}-R_{v_{M+1,0}}^{v_{M+1,0}}-q^{-2}(R_{v_{M+1,1}}^{v_{M+1,1}}-R_{v_{M,1}}^{v_{M,1}})-q^{-4}(R_{v_{M+1,2}}^{v_{M+1,2}}-R_{v_{M,2}}^{v_{M,2}})-q^{-6}(R_{v_{M+1,3}}^{v_{M+1,3}}-R_{v_{M,3}}^{v_{M,3}})=\\
s^{-M-5}q^{\frac{M^{2}+9M+12}{2}}{M\brack 3}_{q}\prod_{k=0}^{M-4}(sq^{-k-1}-s^{-1}q^{k+1}).

Following an inductive argument, we deduce this property for any natural number
M
,
M\leqslant\mathcal{N}-2
. Then we get

\sum_{j=0}^{M}q^{-2j}\left(R_{v_{M,j}}^{v_{M,j}}-R_{v_{M+1,j}}^{v_{M+1,j}}\right)=s^{-2M-2}q^{\frac{4M^{2}+4M}{2}}=q^{-2M-2}R_{v_{M+1,M+1}}^{v_{M+1,M+1}}.

∎

4.3.
Level
\mathcal{N}
unified invariant

For the next part of our construction we want to use the uniquely defined quantum traces on these quotient modules together with the induced braid group actions that come from the generic R-matrix in order to obtain knot invariants.
We recall that we are working over the following quotient ring defined in (
1.4
):

\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}]\ /\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{N}}.

Notation 4.8
.

(Partial trace associated to a vector)
We extend the quantum partial trace
\operatorname{qptr}
on
\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes 2}
to
\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n}
, which is given by:

\operatorname{qt}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}}:=\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}}\circ\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes 2}}\circ\cdots\circ\operatorname{qptr}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes(n-1)}}:\operatorname{End}(\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\otimes n})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}(\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}).

where its evaluation on the vector
v_{i}
as:

\operatorname{qt}_{\widetilde{V}_{\mathcal{N}}}(f)_{v_{i}}^{v_{i}}=s^{-(\mathcal{N}-1)(n-1)}q^{{\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}-1)}(n-1)}\sum_{j_{2},j_{3}\cdots,j_{n}=0}^{\mathcal{N}-1}q^{-2\sum_{k=2}^{n}j_{k}}f_{v_{i,j_{2},\cdots j_{n}}}^{v_{i,j_{2},\cdots j_{n}}}.

Definition 4.9
.

For
\mathcal{N}\in\mathbb{N}
and for
\beta_{n}\in B_{n}
let us consider

\widetilde{\Omega}(\beta_{n})(q,s)\in{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}

obtained from the above braid action via the quantum partial trace on the first component:

5.1.
Recovering coloured Jones and ADO invariants

Theorem 5.1

(The level
\mathcal{N}

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)\mid_{s=q^{1-\mathcal{N}}}=J_{\mathcal{N}}(L,q),\qquad\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)\mid_{q=\xi_{\mathcal{N}}}=\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L,s).

We recall that the invariant

is constructed from the representation theory of

over the ring
{\mathbb{L}}
. It uses the induced braid action on the specialisation of the
\mathcal{N}
-part from the Verma module:

V_{\mathcal{N}}:=\langle v_{0},...,v_{\mathcal{N}-1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}}\subseteq\hat{V}\mid_{{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}}.

Further on, if we specialise again at
s=q^{1-\mathcal{N}}
, we obtain that
V_{\mathcal{N}}\mid_{s=q^{1-\mathcal{N}}}
is the usual
\mathcal{N}
-dimensional representation of
U_{q}(sl_{2})
at generic
q

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)\mid_{s=q^{1-\mathcal{N}}}=J_{\mathcal{N}}(L,q).

Dually, if we consider the specialisation at roots of unity
q=\xi_{\mathcal{N}}
we have that
V_{\mathcal{N}}\mid_{q=\xi_{\mathcal{N}}}
is the is the
\mathcal{N}
-dimensional representation of
U_{q}(sl_{2})
at generic the
2\mathcal{N}^{th}
root of unity. This shows that we also have:

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)\mid_{q-\xi_{\mathcal{N}}}=\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L,s)

which concludes the proof of the statement.
∎

5.2.
Interpolation formula

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.10
, which recall below.

Theorem 5.3

(The level
\mathcal{N}
intersection form interpolates coloured Jones and ADO invariants)
.

We have that
\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)\in{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
is a well defined oriented knot invariant and globalises the
\mathcal{N}^{th}
coloured Jones and coloured Alexander invariants, as follows:

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q,s)=J_{\mathcal{N}}(L,q)+\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}({L},s)-\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L,q^{1-\mathcal{N}}).

Proof.

The formula for our quotient ring is the following, as defined in relation (
1.4
):

{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}]\ /\left(\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2})\cdot(s^{2}q^{-2(\mathcal{N}-1)}-1)\right)\subseteq\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 2},s^{\pm 2}].

We start with the specialization of our intersection form at roots of unity, which we know that leads to the
\mathcal{N}^{th}
coloured Alexander polynomial:

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}|_{q=\xi_{\mathcal{N}}}=\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(s).

So there exists
\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Phi}(L)(s,q)\in{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
such that

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(s,q)-\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(s)=\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Phi}(L)(s,q)\,\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2}).

We recall that
\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q)
is the
\mathcal{N}^{th}
cyclotomic polynomial.
This means that:

(5.1)

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(s,q)=\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(s)+\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Phi}(L)(s,q)\,\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2}).

On the other hand, the specialisation at natural parameters gives the
\mathcal{N}^{th}
coloured Jones polynomial:

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q^{1-\mathcal{N}},\,q)=J_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q).

This shows that the following relation holds:

(5.2)

\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q^{1-\mathcal{N}})+\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Phi}(L)(q^{1-\mathcal{N}},q)\,\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2})=J_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q).

On the other hand, by the definition of quotient rings we see that there exists
\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{J}(L)(s,q)\in{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
such that:

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Phi}(L)(s,q)=\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Phi}(L)(q^{1-\mathcal{N}},q)+\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{J}(L)(s,q)\cdot(s-1^{1-\mathcal{N}}).

Then, in the quotient ring we have:

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Phi}(L)(s,q)\cdot\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2})=\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Phi}(L)(q^{1-\mathcal{N}},q)\cdot\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q)+\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{J}(L)(s,q).

since
\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2})(s-q^{1-\mathcal{N}})
becomes zero in
{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{N}}
.
Combining this property with equation (
5.2
) we obtain:

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}^{\Phi}(L)(s,q)\,\varphi_{\mathcal{N}}(q^{2})=J_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q)-\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q^{1-\mathcal{N}}).

This relation together with equation (
5.1
) show that:

\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(s,q)=J_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q)+\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(s)-\Phi_{\mathcal{N}}(L)(q^{1-\mathcal{N}}).

This concludes the interpolation formula presented in the statement.
∎

We denote:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{N},d_{1}}=\left(\varphi_{d_{1}}(q^{2}),\frac{s^{2d_{1}}-1}{s^{2}-1})\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{N},d_{2}}=\left(\varphi_{d_{2}}(q^{2}),\frac{s^{2d_{2}}-1}{s^{2}-1})\right).

Lemma A.6
.

If
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
, then
\varphi_{d_{1}}(q^{2})
and
\varphi_{d_{2}}(q^{2})
are coprime in
\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1}]
and so the ideals
\left(\varphi_{d_{1}}(q^{2})\right)
and
\left(\varphi_{d_{2}}(q^{2})\right)
are comaximal.

We remark that this property is not true if we are working over
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 2}]
, see
[
7
]
for an explicit example.
In turn, this means that
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{N},d_{1}}=\left(\varphi_{d_{1}}(q^{2}),\frac{s^{2d_{1}}-1}{s^{2}-1})\right)
and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{N},d_{2}}=\left(\varphi_{d_{2}}(q^{2}),\frac{s^{2d_{2}}-1}{s^{2}-1})\right)
are comaximal in
\mathbb{L}
. This together with the previous Lemma ensures that

\bigcap_{\text{\scriptsize$\begin{matrix}d\mid\mathcal{N}\\
d\neq 1,\mathcal{N}\end{matrix}$}}\left(\varphi_{d}(q^{2}),\frac{s^{2d}-1}{s^{2}-1}\right)=\prod_{\text{\scriptsize$\begin{matrix}d\mid\mathcal{N}\\
d\neq 1,\mathcal{N}\end{matrix}$}}\left(\varphi_{d}(q^{2}),\frac{s^{2d}-1}{s^{2}-1}\right)

which concludes the proof of this lemma.
