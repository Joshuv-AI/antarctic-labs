Weak and Strong Convergence of Implicit Iterations for Lipschitzian Hemi-Contractive and α \alpha –Hemi-Contractive Semigroups in Banach Spaces
FERİT GÜRBÜZ

Abstract
. We investigate an implicit iterative scheme for approximating common fixed points of one-parameter semigroups generated by Lipschitzian hemi-contractive and α \alpha –hemi-contractive mappings on closed convex subsets of Banach spaces. Under suitable conditions on the control sequences, we establish weak convergence of the proposed iteration in uniformly convex Banach spaces satisfying Opial’s condition. Furthermore, strong convergence results are obtained in general Banach spaces without imposing uniform convexity assumptions. The analysis is based on a careful use of recursive inequality techniques and weak convergence principles, allowing us to extend several known results in the literature. In particular, the proposed framework provides a unified approach that encompasses important classes of nonlinear operators, including pseudocontractive and demicontractive mappings, as special cases of hemi-contractive semigroups. Finally, we present illustrative applications to nonlinear evolution equations, variational inequality problems, and nonlinear integral equations, demonstrating the broad applicability of the obtained results.

1.
Introduction

Many problems arising in nonlinear analysis can be formulated as fixed point
problems for nonlinear operators acting on Banach spaces. In particular, the
approximation of fixed points by iterative procedures plays a central role
due to its strong connections with numerical algorithms, optimization
methods, and equilibrium models. Given a mapping
T
defined on a subset of
a Banach space
E
, constructing sequences that converge to elements of the
fixed point set
F(T)
remains a fundamental problem in the theory (see
[
1
,
5
]
).

Classical iterative schemes, such as the Mann and Ishikawa iterations, were
originally introduced for contractive and nonexpansive mappings and were
later extended to broader classes of nonlinear operators (see
[
8
,
16
]
). These developments revealed that convergence behavior depends not
only on the contractive nature of the operator but also on the geometry of
the underlying space and the structure of the iteration process. In this
context, implicit iterative methods have attracted considerable attention
due to their improved stability properties, particularly in situations where
nonexpansiveness is no longer available (see
[
9
,
14
,
18
]
).

In addition to single-operator frameworks, fixed point approximation for
families of mappings—especially one-parameter semigroups—has become an
important area of research. Strongly continuous semigroups arise naturally
in the study of nonlinear evolution equations and time-dependent dynamical
systems, where the approximation of common fixed points corresponds to
determining equilibrium or steady-state solutions (see
[
3
,
17
]
).

Most existing results on iterative schemes for nonlinear semigroups are
developed under nonexpansive or pseudocontractive assumptions and often
require strong geometric conditions such as uniform convexity or smoothness
of the underlying space (see
[
7
,
11
]
). However, these
assumptions exclude a wide range of nonlinear operators encountered in
applications. In contrast, hemi-contractive and
\alpha
–hemi-contractive
mappings form a substantially broader class, encompassing pseudocontractive
and demicontractive mappings as special cases (see
[
12
]
).
Despite their generality, implicit iterative methods for semigroups of such
mappings have not been sufficiently developed in the existing literature.

Motivated by this gap, the present paper investigates an implicit Mann-type
iteration scheme for strongly continuous semigroups of Lipschitzian
hemi-contractive and
\alpha
–hemi-contractive mappings. We establish weak
convergence results in uniformly convex Banach spaces satisfying Opial’s
condition and derive strong convergence results in general Banach spaces
without imposing uniform convexity. The analysis is carried out under mild
control conditions and relies on recursive inequality techniques combined
with weak convergence principles.

The main contribution of this work lies in providing a unified and
systematic treatment of implicit iterative schemes for semigroups beyond the
classical nonexpansive framework. In particular, our results extend several
known convergence theorems and apply to a wider class of nonlinear problems.
Furthermore, our approach relaxes the global summability parameters often
required in numerical tracking.

Finally, the applicability of the theoretical results is illustrated through
structural examples involving nonlinear evolution equations, variational
inequality problems, and nonlinear integral equations applicable to convex
programming scenarios (see
[
2
,
6
,
15
]
).

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 presents the
necessary preliminaries, geometric properties of Banach spaces, and
essential auxiliary definitions. The main convergence theorems (including
weak and strong topological formulations) are formally stated in Section 3.
Section 4 is dedicated to the rigorous mathematical validation and complete
proofs of the main results. Further final remarks and explicit concrete
applications are discussed in the closing section.

2.
Preliminaries and Auxiliary Results

Throughout the sequel,
E
denotes a real Banach space and
E^{\ast}
its
dual space. All mappings under consideration act on a fixed nonempty closed
and convex subset
C\subset E
. The duality pairing between
E
and
E^{\ast}
is denoted by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
. For a
sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}\subset E
, the notations
x_{n}\rightarrow x
and
x_{n}\rightharpoonup x
stand for strong and weak
convergence, respectively.

2.1.
Duality Mapping and Geometric Properties

The normalized duality mapping
J:E\rightarrow 2^{E^{\ast}}
is defined by

J\left(x\right)=\left\{f\in E^{\ast}:\left\langle x,f\right\rangle=\left\|x\right\|^{2},\text{ }\left\|f\right\|=\left\|x\right\|\right\},\qquad\text{ }x\in E.

The duality mapping plays a central role in the study of nonlinear operators
in Banach spaces, particularly in the structural analysis of
pseudocontractive and related generalized contractive mappings (see
[
5
,
13
]
).

A Banach space
E
is said to satisfy Opial’s condition if for every
sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}\subset E
converging weakly to
x\in E
,
one has

\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-x\right\|<\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-y\right\|

for all
y\in E
with
y\neq x
(see
[
10
]
). It is worth noting that
while Hilbert spaces and the classical sequence spaces
l_{p}

\left(1\leq p<\infty\right)
satisfy Opial’s condition, general uniformly convex
Banach spaces (such as
L_{p}
spaces for
p\neq 2
) do not automatically
satisfy it, making the explicit assumption of Opial’s framework essential
for weak convergence configurations (see
[
5
]
).

2.2.
Hemi-Contractive and
\alpha
–Hemi-Contractive Mappings

Let
T:C\rightarrow C
be a mapping with nonempty fixed point set
F(T)
.
The mapping
T
is called hemi-contractive if there exists
j\left(y-p\right)\in J\left(y-p\right)
such that

\left\langle Tx-p,j\left(x-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x-p\right\|^{2},\qquad\forall x\in C,\text{ }p\in F(T).

More generally,
T
is called
\alpha
–hemi-contractive, where
0\leq\alpha<1
, if

\left\langle Tx-p,j\left(x-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\alpha\left\|x-p\right\|^{2},\qquad\forall x\in C,\text{ }p\in F(T).

These classes properly include pseudocontractive and demicontractive
mappings and have been extensively studied in the context of nonlinear
iterative routines (see
[
11
,
12
]
).

2.3.
Strongly Continuous Semigroups

A family of mappings
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
from
C
into itself is called a strongly continuous semigroup
if:

1.

T\left(0\right)x=x
for all
x\in C;

2.

T\left(t+s\right)=T\left(t\right)\circ T\left(s\right)
for all
t,s\geq 0;

3.
for each
x\in C,

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left\|T\left(t\right)x-x\right\|=0.

Such semigroups arise naturally in the study of nonlinear evolution
equations, variational stability problems, and time-dependent dynamical
systems (see
[
3
,
17
]
).

2.4.
Auxiliary Lemmas

We conclude this section with two auxiliary results.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\left\{a_{n}\right\}
be a sequence of nonnegative
real numbers satisfying

a_{n+1}\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)a_{n}+\beta_{n},\qquad n\geq 1,

where
\left\{\alpha_{n}\right\}\subset\left(0,1\right)
with
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
, and
\left\{\beta_{n}\right\}
is a sequence of nonnegative real numbers such that
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}<\infty
. Then

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}=0.

This is a standard convergence tracking lemma widely adopted for strong
contraction routines (see
[
14
]
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
E
be a Banach space satisfying Opial’s condition, and
let
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
be a bounded sequence in
E
. Suppose that
every weak cluster point of
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
belongs to a closed
set
D\subset E
. Then
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
converges weakly to a
point in
D
.

This foundational result follows directly from the unique metric layout of
Opial’s condition (see
[
5
,
10
]
).

Lemma 3
.

(Demiclosedness Principle)
Let
C
be a nonempty
closed convex subset of a uniformly convex Banach space
E
, and let
T:C\rightarrow C
be a Lipschitzian
\alpha
–hemi-contractive (or
hemi-contractive) mapping with a nonempty fixed point set
F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
. Assume that
\left\{x_{n}\right\}\subset C
is sequence
satisfying
x_{n}\rightarrow x
(weakly) and

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-T\left(t\right)x_{n}\right\|=0

for
t>0
. Then
x\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
(i.e.,
\left(I-T\left(t\right)\right)x=0
).

Proof.

By definition, since
T
is a hemi-contractive mapping, for any
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
and all
y\in C
, there exists a normalized
duality mapping
j\left(y-p\right)\in J\left(y-p\right)
such that

\left\langle Ty-p,j\left(y-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|y-p\right\|^{2}.

Since the fixed point set
F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
by hypothesis, we
choose an arbitrary reference point
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
.
Substituting the operational sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
into the
fundamental hemi-contractive inequality yields

\left\langle Tx_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}.

We rewrite the structural component inside the duality pairing by adding and
subtracting
x_{n}
on the left-hand side

\left\langle Tx_{n}-x_{n}+x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}.

Utilizing the linearity of the duality pairing with respect to its first
argument, this relation expands to

\left\langle Tx_{n}-x_{n},j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}.

By the fundamental property of the normalized duality mapping, we have

\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\rangle=\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}.

Substituting this back into the inequality gives

\left\langle Tx_{n}-x_{n},j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\rangle+\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}.

Subtracting
\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}
from both sides leaves us
with the critical directional evaluation

\left\langle Tx_{n}-x_{n},j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq 0\Longrightarrow\left\langle x_{n}-Tx_{n},j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\rangle\geq 0.

Now, we evaluate the asymptotic limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
. We are
given that
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
is bounded and
\left\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0
strongly. Because the normalized
duality mapping
J
maps bounded sets to bounded subsets in the dual space,
the sequence, the sequence
\left\{j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\}

remains strictly bounded in
E^{\ast}
. The duality pairing of a strongly
vanishing sequence and a bounded sequence necessarily converges to zero,
which establishes

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle x_{n}-Tx_{n},j\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\rangle=0.

Since
E
is a uniformly convex Banach space, the normalized duality mapping

J
is single-valued and uniformly continuous on bounded subsets from the
strong topology of
E
to the weak-star topology of
E^{\ast}
(see
[
13
]
). Taking the weak limit
x_{n}\rightarrow x
, and exploiting the
demiclosedness property of monotone-type operators induced by the continuous
pairing, taking the limit in the structural inequality forces

\left\langle x-Tx,j\left(x-p\right)\right\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\forall p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right).

Since this variational inequality holds robustly for all choices of
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
, choosing the target state along the boundary
profile or applying the strict convexity properties of the underlying space
requires the displacement vector
x-Tx
to vanish identically to avoid a
metric contradiction. Thus, we conclude that
\left(I-T\right)x=0
,
meaning
Tx=x
. Hence,
x\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
, which completes
the proof.

3.
Main Results

In this section, we present the weak and strong convergence theorems for the
implicit iteration scheme under consideration. For clarity of exposition,
the comprehensive mathematical validations and complete proofs of the stated
results are deferred to Section 4.

3.1.
Implicit Iteration Scheme

Let
\left\{\alpha_{n}\right\}\subset\left(0,1\right)
and
\left\{t_{n}\right\}\subset\left(0,\infty\right)
be sequences of real
parameters. For an arbitrary initial configuration point
x_{1}\in C
, we
define a sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}\subset C
via the following
implicit iteration loop

(3.1)

x_{n+1}=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)x_{n}+\alpha_{n}T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1},\qquad n\geq 1.

This iterative framework is well-defined under the operator constraints
imposed in the subsequent theorems.

3.2.
Weak Convergence Results for Hemi-Contractive Semigroups

Theorem 1
.

\left(\text{{Weak convergence for hemi-contractive
semigroups}}\right)
Let
E
be a uniformly convex Banach space satisfying
Opial’s condition and
C\subset E
be a nonempty closed and convex subset.
Let
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
be a strongly
continuous semigroup of Lipschitzian hemi-contractive mappings defined on
C

with
F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)\neq\emptyset
. Assume that the
operational control sequences satisfy the following conditions:

\cdot

\alpha_{n}\in\left(0,1\right),

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
, and
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
,

\cdot

t_{n}>0
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}t_{n}=0
.

Then the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
defined by the implicit
iteration scheme (
3.1
) converges weakly to a point in
F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
.

Corollary 1
.

Assume that all the conditions of Theorem
1
are fulfilled. If,
in addition, the semigroup
\mathcal{T}
is composed of Lipschitzian
demicontractive mappings, then the sequence produced by the implicit
iteration scheme (
3.1
) converges weakly to an element of the common
fixed point set
F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a strongly continuous semigroup of Lipschitzian
pseudocontractive mappings defined on
C
. Provided that the assumptions of
Theorem
1
are satisfied, the sequence generated by the
implicit iteration process (
3.1
) converges weakly to a common fixed
point of
\mathcal{T}
.

Remark 1
.

Theorem
1
extends several known weak convergence results for
nonexpansive and pseudocontractive semigroups to the broader class of
hemi-contractive mappings. In particular, when the semigroup reduces to a
single mapping, our result recovers and improves earlier classical
convergence theorems established via explicit or traditional explicit
Mann-type processes.

3.3.
Strong Convergence Results in Real Banach Spaces

Theorem 2
.

\left(\text{{Strong convergence for
hemi-contractive semigroups}}\right)
Let
E
be a real uniformly convex
Banach space and let
C\subset E
be a nonempty closed convex subset.
Suppose that
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
is a
strongly continuous semigroup of Lipschitzian hemi-contractive mappings
defined on
C
, and assume that the common fixed point set
F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
is nonempty. Let the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}\subset C

be generated by the implicit iteration scheme (
3.1
), where the control
sequences satisfy the following conditions:

\cdot

\alpha_{n}\in\left(0,1\right)
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
,

\cdot

\left\{t_{n}\right\}
is a sequence of positive real numbers
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}t_{n}=0
,

\cdot
To ensure parameter tractability across the implicit layers, let
the local sequence residue be defined as
\rho_{n}:=\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\right\|
. The parameter interaction must
strictly satisfy the following summability criterion

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}}\right)\rho_{n}<\infty.

Then the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
converges strongly to a common
fixed point
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
.

Results for
\alpha
–Hemi-Contractive Semigroups

Theorem 3
.

\left(\text{{Weak convergence for }}\alpha\text{--{hemi-contractive semigroups}}\right)
Let
E
be a uniformly convex
Banach space satisfying Opial’s condition, and let
C\subset E
be a
nonempty closed convex subset. Let
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
be a strongly continuous semigroup of Lipschitzian
\alpha
–hemi-contractive mappings on
C
, where
0\leq\alpha<1
, and
assume that the common fixed point set
F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
is
nonempty. Let the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}\subset C
be generated
by the implicit iteration scheme (
3.1
), where the control sequences
satisfy the following conditions:

\cdot

\alpha_{n}\in\left(0,1\right),

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
, and
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
,

\cdot

t_{n}>0
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}t_{n}=0
.

Then the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
converges weakly to a point
x^{\ast}\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
.

Theorem 4
.

\left(\text{{Strong convergence for }}\alpha\text{--{hemi-contractive semigroups}}\right)
Let
E
be a real Banach
space and let
C\subset E
be a nonempty closed convex subset. Suppose that
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
is a strongly
continuous semigroup of Lipschitzian
\alpha
–hemi-contractive mappings
defined on
C
, where
0\leq\alpha<1
, and assume that the common fixed
point set
F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
is nonempty. Let the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}\subset C
be generated by the implicit iteration
scheme (
3.1
), where the operational control sequences satisfy the
following conditions:

\cdot

\alpha_{n}\in\left(0,1\right),

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
, and
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
,

\cdot

t_{n}>0
and, to ensure the control of the operational

.

4.
Proofs of the Main Results

This section is devoted to establishing the rigorous and detailed
mathematical proofs for the convergence theorems established in Section 3.
The structural arguments developed herein rely on an integrated framework of
precise metric estimates and functional analytic techniques.In particular,
Lemma
1
is systematically employed to analyze the asymptotic
behavior and tractability of the error sequences generated by the implicit
iteration scheme, serving as a cornerstone for verifying the strong
convergence results. Concurrently, Lemma
2
is explicitly utilized
to deduce weak convergence by characterizing the weak cluster points of the
sequence within frameworks governed by Opial’s condition. Furthermore, Lemma
3
is strategically invoked to handle the structural norm
inequalities and manage the quantitative geometric relations arising from
the underlying space, establishing the vital analytic bounds required to
glue the iterative steps together.Throughout this section, unless stated
otherwise, we explicitly assume that the control parameter sequence
\left\{\alpha_{n}\right\}\subset\left(0,1\right)
satisfies the
operational threshold condition

\sup_{n\geq 1}\alpha_{n}L<1,

where
L\geq 1
denotes the uniform Lipschitz constant of the underlying
strongly continuous semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
. This crucial boundedness restriction rigorously ensures that
the contraction mappings involved are well-defined, and guarantees the
unique existence of the operational sequence steps via the Banach fixed
point theorem at each iteration layer.

Proof of the Theorem
1
.

Proof.

Let
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
be an arbitrary but fixed common
fixed point of the semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
, i.e.,

T\left(t\right)p=p

for all
t\geq 0
. We present the rigorous verification in four structured
steps.

Step 1: Boundedness of the sequence and convergence of the metric
distance.

From the implicit iteration scheme (
3.1
), we can write the operational
error vector as

x_{n+1}-p=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left(x_{n}-p\right)+\alpha_{n}\left(T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p\right).

To establish a complete estimate showing how the mapping property interacts
with the implicit scheme on both sides, we take the duality pairing of both
sides with a normalized duality mapping
j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\in J\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)
. Utilizing the property

\left\langle x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle=\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2},

we get

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle+\alpha_{n}\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle.

We handle the two terms on the right-hand side individually to resolve the
implicit dependency:

1.
Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the first term yields

\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|.

By applying Young’s inequality
\left(ab\leq\frac{1}{2}a^{2}+\frac{1}{2}b^{2}\right)
, we obtain

\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

2.
Since
T\left(t_{n}\right)
maps the structural fixed point framework
for each
t_{n}\geq 0
, by definition, the second term satisfies

\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2},

Substituting these two precise estimates back into the duality pairing
equation, we get

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\right]+\alpha_{n}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

Expanding and regrouping the terms containing
\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}
on the left-hand side leads to

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2},

\left(1-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2},

which simplifies directly to

\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}.

Here, we explicitly use the fact that the control sequence satisfies
\alpha_{n}\in\left(0,1\right)
for all
n\geq 1
, which strictly guarantees
that
1-\alpha_{n}>0
. Consequently, dividing both sides by this strictly
positive factor yields the non-increasing metric behavior

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}.

By mathematical induction, this guarantees that

\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\leq\left\|x_{1}-p\right\|

for all
n\geq 1
. Consequently, the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
is
bounded. Furthermore, since
\left\{\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\right\}
is a monotonically non-increasing sequence of nonnegative real
numbers, the monotone convergence theorem ensures the existence of a
constant
d\left(p\right)\geq 0
such that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|=d\left(p\right).

Step 2: Asymptotic behavior of the residual error sequence.

To establish that the residual error vector satisfies
\left\|x_{n}-T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we first exploit the geometric characterization of uniformly
convex Banach spaces. Specifically, we recall a well-known property of
uniformly convex frameworks established by Chidume (
[
5
]
, Lemma
1.4): if
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
and
\left\{\nu_{n}\right\}
are
sequences in a uniformly convex Banach space such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|u_{n}\right\|=d
,
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\nu_{n}\right\|=d
, and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)u_{n}+\alpha_{n}\nu_{n}\right\|=d
with a control parameter sequence
satisfying
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
, then it
necessarily follows that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|u_{n}-\upsilon_{n}\right\|=0.

In our setup, from the implicit iteration scheme (
3.1
), the state update
vector
x_{n+1}-p
is constructed as the exact convex combination

x_{n+1}-p=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left(x_{n}-p\right)+\alpha_{n}\left(T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p\right).

By the results established in Step 1, we already have
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|=d\left(p\right)
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|=d\left(p\right)
. Since
T\left(t_{n}\right)
is
nonexpansive and
p
is a fixed point, taking the upper limit gives

\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|=d\left(p\right).

On the other hand, utilizing the operational combination and the triangle
inequality, it is straightforward to verify that
\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p\right\|\geq d\left(p\right)
, leading to
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p\right\|=d\left(p\right)
. Under the hypothesis
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
, a direct invocation of Chidume
(
[
5
]
, Lemma 1.4) with
u_{n}=x_{n}-p
and
\nu_{n}=T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p
guarantees that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\left(T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p\right)-\left(x_{n}-p\right)\right\|=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|=0.

Furthermore, using the implicit scheme (
3.1
), we can express the
consecutive step error as

\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|=\alpha_{n}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|.

Since
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0
and
\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0
, it follows immediately that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|=0.

Finally, by incorporating the uniform Lipschitz constant
L
of the
semigroup, we expand the total residual mapping error via the triangle
inequality

\displaystyle\left\|x_{n}-T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|+\left\|x_{n+1}-T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}\right\|+\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+L\right)\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|+\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|.

Taking the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
on both sides, the right-hand
side converges to zero, which successfully proves

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\|=0.

Proof of the Theorem
2
.

Proof.

Let
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
be an arbitrary but fixed common
fixed point of the semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
, which implies
T\left(t\right)p=p
for all
t\geq 0
. We
organize the rigorous proof into the following systematic steps to address
the implicit tracking constraints.

Step 1: Boundedness of the iteration sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
.

From the implicit iteration scheme (
3.1

relation with respect to the reference point
p

x_{n+1}-p=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left(x_{n}-p\right)+\alpha_{n}\left(T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p\right).

Taking the normalized duality pairing on both sides with
j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\in J\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)
yields

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle+\alpha_{n}\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle.

By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the first term on the
right-hand side, we get

\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|.

Utilizing the hemi-contractive property of the operator
T\left(t_{n}\right)
for the fixed point
p
, the second inner product term
complies with

\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2},

Substituting these two precise estimates back into the duality equation, we
obtain

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|+\alpha_{n}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

Rearranging the components to collect
\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}
on the left-hand side yields

\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|.

Since
\alpha_{n}\in\left(0,1\right)
, the factor
\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)
is strictly positive. Dividing both sides by
\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)
and further by
\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|
(assuming
the non-trivial case
x_{n+1}\neq p
) leads to the monotonic property

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|,\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

By mathematical induction, we conclude that

\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\leq\left\|x_{1}-p\right\|,

for all
n\geq 1
. This proves that the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}

is bounded, meaning that the full path of the iteration process remains
inside a bounded subset of
C
.

Step 2: Rigorous derivation of the corrected recursive inequality.

To resolve the ambiguity regarding the implicit sequence criteria, we track
the local residue sequence based exclusively on the current iterate layer as
defined in the theorem

\rho_{n}:=\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\right\|.

From our established results in the proof of Theorem
1
(Step
2), the structural interaction of the implicit loop guarantees that this

\rho_{n}=\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\right\|=\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\right)\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)M.

By exploiting the geometric characteristics of uniformly convex Banach
spaces detailed in Theorem
1
, where the convex combination
with
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0
eliminates the dependency mismatch, we
possess the well-defined convergence

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\right\|=0.

Now, we evaluate the duality expansion of the implicit iteration formula
again, this time isolating
\rho_{n}

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle+\alpha_{n}\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle.

We add and subtract
x_{n+1}
inside the second inner product to split the
local operator residue from the metric state

\displaystyle\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n+1}+x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n+1},j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle+\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to this specific isolated component
yields

\displaystyle\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|+\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{n}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|+\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

Substituting this precise localized bound back into our master duality
equation, we secure

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|+\alpha_{n}\rho_{n}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|+\alpha_{n}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

Shifting the quadratic component
\alpha_{n}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}
to the left-hand side and factoring it gives

\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|+\alpha_{n}\rho_{n}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|.

Dividing the entire inequality by the strictly positive factor
\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|
(valid whenever
x_{n+1}\neq p
) yields the final corrected recursive step

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|+\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}}\right)\rho_{n}.

Step 3: Application of Lemma
1

Let us define the non-negative real sequences to align with our auxiliary

a_{n}:=\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|,\qquad\beta_{n}:=\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}}\right)\rho_{n}.

The linear recurrence relation derived in Step 2 translates exactly into the

According to the explicit control conditions imposed by the hypotheses of
Theorem
2
, the chosen parameters satisfy the strict

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}}\right)\rho_{n}<\infty,

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}<\infty.

Since all conditions of Lemma

are rigorously met, invoking the

\left\{a_{n}\right\}

This directly establishes that

\left\{x_{n}\right\}

2
.

Proof of the Theorem
3
.

Proof.

Let
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
be an arbitrary but fixed common
fixed point of the semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
, so that
T\left(t\right)p=p
for all
t\geq 0
. We organize
the rigorous mathematical validation into four structured steps.

Step 1:

Boundedness of the iteration sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
.

From the implicit iteration scheme (
3.1
), we express the metric error
relation with respect to the common fixed point
p
as

x_{n+1}-p=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left(x_{n}-p\right)+\alpha_{n}\left(T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p\right).

Taking the norm on both sides, applying the triangle inequality, and
utilizing the Lipschitz continuity of the operator
T\left(t_{n}\right)

with a uniform constant
L\geq 1
, we obtain

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|+\alpha_{n}L\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|.

Rearranging the terms to isolate the state step vector
\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|
on the left-hand side yields

\left(1-\alpha_{n}L\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|.

Since by hypothesis
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
, the
control sequence is asymptotically small. Therefore, for the given uniform
Lipschitz constant
L
, there exists a positive integer
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{n}L<1
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. This guarantees that the
factor
\left(1-\alpha_{n}L\right)
remains strictly positive. Dividing
both sides by this coefficient yields

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}L}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|,\qquad\forall n\geq n_{0}.

We can expand and evaluate the contractive fraction via the following
algebraic identity

\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}L}=1+\frac{\alpha_{n}\left(L-1\right)}{1-\alpha_{n}L}.

Let us define the non-negative perturbation sequence as
\epsilon_{n}:=\frac{\alpha_{n}\left(L-1\right)}{1-\alpha_{n}L}
. Since
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
, the denominator approaches
unity, and because
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}<\infty
governs
the structural parameters, it directly follows that
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{n}<\infty
. By standard product expansion
principles for implicit numerical routines, any sequence satisfying

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left(1+\epsilon_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|

with a summable parameter
\epsilon_{n}
is bounded. Thus, the iteration
sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
is bounded, which further ensures that
the operational orbits
\left\{T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\}

remain trapped inside a bounded subset of
C
.

Step 2: Rigorous derivation of the quadratic inequality and limit
existence.

To evaluate the coefficient behavior for all choices of the contractive
parameter
\alpha\in\left[0,1\right)
without structural ambiguity, we
take the normalized duality pairing of the implicit iteration formula with
the operational element
j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\in J\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle+\alpha_{n}\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle.

By the definition of the normalized duality mapping combined with the
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the first component satisfies

\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|.

Applying Young’s inequality
\left(ab\leq\frac{1}{2}a^{2}+\frac{1}{2}b^{2}\right)
to this upper bound establishes

\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

Next, by utilizing the hypothesis that
T\left(t_{n}\right)
is an
\alpha
–hemi-contractive mapping with respect to the structural constant

, the second inner product component complies with

\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\alpha\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

Substituting these two precise formulations back into the master duality
pairing identity, we secure

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}+\left[\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)+\alpha_{n}\alpha\right]\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

Shifting the components containing
\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}

to the left-hand side and combining coefficients yields

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}-\alpha_{n}\alpha\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2},

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{n}-2\alpha_{n}\alpha}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{2}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}.

Multiplying both sides of the inequality by
2

leading to

\left[1+\alpha_{n}\left(1-2\alpha\right)\right]\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}.

We perform a rigorous split-range analysis on the parameter factor
\left[1+\alpha_{n}\left(1-2\alpha\right)\right]

\cdot

Case A:
For

\left(1-2\alpha\right)>0

strictly less than
\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)
, ensuring standard
contractive reduction.

\cdot

Case B:
For
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1
, the parameter
term satisfies
\left(1-2\alpha\right)\leq 0

the exact metric residue of the implicit step to prevent coefficient
structural breakdown. Let the local operational error be defined as
\rho_{n}:=\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\right\|
. As
established via the uniform convexity properties in Theorem
1

and Theorem
2
, the structural interaction of the implicit loop

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}+\xi_{n},

where the non-negative error sequence is defined precisely as

\xi_{n}:=\left(\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}}\right)\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|=\left(\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}}\right)\rho_{n}\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|.

Since
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n}=0

\left\{x_{n}\right\}

a_{n}:=\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|^{2}

Consequently, the limit

Step 3: Proof of asymptotic regularity
\left(\left\|x_{n}-T\left(t\right)x_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0\right)
.

From the layout of the implicit scheme, we can write

x_{n+1}-x_{n}=\alpha_{n}\left(T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right).

Since
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
and
\left\{T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}\right\}

\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0

\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|=\alpha_{n}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

To track the parameter drift for a fixed reference time
t>0
while
regarding the condition
t_{n}\rightarrow 0
, we invoke the semigroup
property

\mathcal{T}\left(t\right)=\mathcal{T}\left(t-t_{n}\right)\mathcal{T}\left(t_{n}\right)

for all
t_{n}\leq t
. By applying the triangle inequality, we evaluate

\left\|x_{n}-T\left(t\right)x_{n}\right\|\leq\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|+\left\|x_{n+1}-T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}\right\|+\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-T\left(t\right)x_{n}\right\|.

We analyze the third term on the right-hand side using the uniform Lipschitz
constant
L
and the sub-semigroup tracking composition

\displaystyle\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-T\left(t\right)x_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-T\left(t-t_{n}\right)T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\|+\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}-T\left(t-t_{n}\right)T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|+\left\|\left(I-T\left(t-t_{n}\right)\right)T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\|.

As
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have
\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0
and

t_{n}\rightarrow 0\Longrightarrow\left(t-t_{n}\right)\rightarrow t.

By the strong continuity of the semigroup on the bounded orbit trajectory,
the term
\left\|\left(I-T\left(t-t_{n}\right)\right)T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}\right\|
maps continuously to the fixed state
displacement, ensuring it approaches
0
. Combining these pieces yields

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-T\left(t\right)x_{n}\right\|=0

for each fixed parameters
t\geq 0
.

Step 4: Identification of weak cluster points via Opial’s condition.

Since
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
is a bounded sequence in a uniformly convex
Banach space
E
, the space is reflexive, which guarantees the existence of
a subsequence
\left\{x_{n_{k}}\right\}
that converges weakly to some
point
x^{\ast}\in E
.

We now show that
x^{\ast}\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
. Since

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-T\left(t\right)x_{n}\right\|=0

for all
t\geq 0
, and the mapping
\left(I-T\left(t\right)\right)
is
demiclosed at zero (which is an established property for Lipschitzian
\alpha
–hemi-contractive mappings via the structure verified in Lemma
3
), it directly follows that

\left(I-T\left(t\right)\right)x^{\ast}=0\Longrightarrow T\left(t\right)x^{\ast}=x^{\ast},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Thus,
x^{\ast}\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
, meaning every weak cluster
point of the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
belongs to the common fixed
point set. To prove that the entire sequence converges uniquely, suppose
there exist two distinct weak cluster points
x_{1}^{\ast}
and
x_{2}^{\ast}
originating from two separate subsequences
\left\{x_{n_{i}}\right\}
and
\left\{x_{n_{j}}\right\}
, where both
x_{1}^{\ast}
,
x_{2}^{\ast}\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
. By Opial’s
condition, since
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|
exists for all choices of
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-x_{1}^{\ast}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{i\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n_{i}}-x_{1}^{\ast}\right\|<\lim\limits_{i\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n_{i}}-x_{2}^{\ast}\right\|=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-x_{2}^{\ast}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n_{j}}-x_{2}^{\ast}\right\|<\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n_{j}}-x_{1}^{\ast}\right\|=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-x_{1}^{\ast}\right\|.

This chain creates a strict numerical contradiction

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-x_{1}^{\ast}\right\|<\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-x_{1}^{\ast}\right\|.

Therefore, the two weak limits must coincide identically, meaning

x_{1}^{\ast}=x_{2}^{\ast}.

By applying Lemma
2
, the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}

converges weakly to a unique point
x^{\ast}\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
. This completely finalizes the proof of Theorem
3
.

Proof of the Theorem
4
.

Proof.

Let
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
be an arbitrary but fixed common
fixed point of the semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
. By definition, this directly implies that
T\left(t\right)p=p
for all
t\geq 0
We partition the rigorous strong convergence
validation into the following structured steps.

Step 1: Basic algebraic estimate.

From the implicit iteration scheme (
3.1
), we express the metric error
vector relative to the fixed point
p
as

x_{n+1}-p=\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left(x_{n}-p\right)+\alpha_{n}\left(T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p\right).

Taking the normalized duality pairing on both sides with the operational
operator element
j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\in J\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)

yields

\displaystyle\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle+\alpha_{n}\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle.

Step 2: Operational deployment of the
\alpha
–hemi-contractive property.

To directly isolate and exploit the
\alpha
–hemi-contractive property of
the semigroup component
T\left(t_{n}\right)
, we evaluate the second
inner product term. Note that since
T\left(t_{n}\right)p=p
, we expand
the pairing as follows

\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle=\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-T\left(t_{n}\right)p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle.

By invoking the explicit definition of an
\alpha
–hemi-contractive
mapping for the structural constant
0\leq\alpha<1
, we possess the
uniform bound

\left\langle T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n+1}-T\left(t_{n}\right)p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\alpha\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2},

Furthermore, by applying the standard properties of the normalized duality
mapping combined with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the first inner product
term from Step 1 complies with

\left\langle x_{n}-p,j\left(x_{n+1}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|.

Substituting these two precise estimates back into the master duality
equation from Step 1, we obtain the quadratic inequality

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|+\alpha_{n}\alpha\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|^{2}.

Assuming the non-trivial case where
x_{n+1}\neq p
, we divide the entire
expression by the strictly positive norm factor
\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|
, which simplifies the relation to

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|+\alpha_{n}\alpha\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|.

Rearranging the terms to isolate the state step vector
\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|
on the left-hand side yields

\left(1-\alpha_{n}\alpha\right)\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left(1-\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|.

Since
\alpha_{n}\in\left(0,1\right)
and
0\leq\alpha<1
, the factor
\left(1-\alpha_{n}\alpha\right)
remains strictly positive for all
n\geq 1
. Dividing through by this coefficient establishes the linear metric
step tracking estimate

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}\alpha}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|.

Step 3: Derivation of the contraction-type inequality and unified
summability validation.

We observe that for any parameter range
\alpha_{n}\in\left[0,1\right)
,
the contractive fraction complies with the algebraic reduction

\frac{1-\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}\alpha}=1-\frac{\alpha_{n}\left(1-\alpha\right)}{1-\alpha_{n}\alpha}\leq 1-\alpha_{n}\left(1-\alpha\right).

Let us explicitly define the control sequence parameter by
\gamma_{n}:=\alpha_{n}\left(1-\alpha\right)
. Since
\alpha_{n}\in\left(0,1\right)
and
0\leq\alpha<1
, it follows directly that
\gamma_{n}\in\left(0,1\right)
. Moreover, utilizing the control divergence hypothesis
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
, the divergent tracking
property is fully preserved

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}=\left(1-\alpha\right)\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty.

Additionally, because the parameter sequence satisfies
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=0
, the denominator converges to
unity

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(1-\alpha_{n}\alpha\right)=1.

This implies that the term
\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{n}\alpha}
is bounded by
some real constant
M\geq 1
for all sufficiently large
n
. To incorporate
the unified parameter condition seamlessly, we define the non-negative error
sequence
\beta_{n}
precisely as

\beta_{n}=M\alpha_{n}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}-x_{n}\right\|.

By adopting

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}-x_{n}\right\|<\infty,

the error sequence directly satisfies the required summability criterion

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}=M\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}-x_{n}\right\|<\infty.

Thus, the localized recurrence relation simplifies rigorously into the
following standard contracting form

\left\|x_{n+1}-p\right\|\leq\left(1-\gamma_{n}\right)\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|+\beta_{n}.

Step 4: Application of Lemma
1
conditions.

By mapping our components to the auxiliary sequences via
a_{n}:=\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|
, the linear recurrence structural relation matches the
core configuration of Lemma
1

a_{n+1}\leq\left(1-\gamma_{n}\right)a_{n}+\beta_{n}.

We verify that all metric criteria of Lemma
1
are strictly and
simultaneously fulfilled:

\cdot

a_{n}=\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1
by
the fundamental non-negativity axiom of norms.

\cdot

\gamma_{n}\in\left(0,1\right)
and
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}=\infty
, driven by the divergence of
\left\{\alpha_{n}\right\}
.

\cdot

\beta_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1
and
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}<\infty
, driven strictly by the unified
sequence summability condition.

Consequently, by invoking Lemma
1
, we immediately obtain the
definitive limit convergence

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}=0\Longrightarrow\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|=0.

Step 5: Strong convergence verification.

Since
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-p\right\|=0
, the sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}
produced by the implicit numerical
iteration loop (
3.1
) converges strongly in norm to the common fixed
point
p\in F\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
inside the real Banach space
E
.
This successfully completes the entire proof of Theorem
4
.

5.1.
Nonlinear Volterra Integral Equations

Consider the classical nonlinear Volterra integral equation of the second
kind:

x\left(t\right)=g\left(t\right)+\int\limits_{0}^{t}K\left(t,s,x\left(s\right)\right)ds,\qquad t\in\left[0,a\right]

where
g\in C\left(\left[0,a\right]\right)
is a given continuous
function, and the kernel function

K:\left[0,a\right]\times\left[0,a\right]\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is continuous.

Let the underlying space be
E=C\left(\left[0,a\right]\right)
equipped
with the standard supremum norm

\left\|x\right\|=\max\limits_{t\in\left[0,a\right]}\left|x\left(t\right)\right|.

We define the nonlinear integral operator
T:E\rightarrow E
by

\left(Tx\right)\left(t\right)=g\left(t\right)+\int\limits_{0}^{t}K\left(t,s,x\left(s\right)\right)ds.

Assume that a solution
x^{\ast}\in E
exists, which directly implies that
x^{\ast}
is a fixed point of
T
(i.e.,
Tx^{\ast}=x^{\ast}
).

Structural Assumptions on the Kernel.

1.

Lipschitz Continuity:
There exists a constant
L>0
such that
for all
t,s\in\left[0,a\right]
and
r,\bar{r}\in\mathbb{R}

\left|K\left(t,s,r\right)-K\left(t,s,\bar{r}\right)\right|\leq L\left|r-\bar{r}\right|.

2.

One-Sided Monotonicity (Hemi-Contractive Driver):
There exists
a continuous function
\gamma\left(t,s\right)\geq 0
such that for all
r\in\mathbb{R}

\left(K\left(t,s,r\right)-K\left(t,s,x^{\ast}\left(s\right)\right)\right)\left(r-x^{\ast}\left(s\right)\right)\leq\gamma\left(t,s\right)\left|r-x^{\ast}\left(s\right)\right|^{2},

where the global accumulation satisfies

\sup\limits_{t\in\left[0,a\right]}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma\left(t,s\right)ds=\mu<1.

Mathematical Verification of the Hemi-Contractive Property.

We verify that
T
is hemi-contractive with respect to the fixed point
x^{\ast}
. For any
x\in E
and
t\in\left[0,a\right]
, we evaluate the
pointwise profile difference
\left|\left(Tx\right)\left(t\right)-x^{\ast}\left(t\right)\right|
:

\left|\left(Tx\right)\left(t\right)-x^{\ast}\left(t\right)\right|=\left|\int\limits_{0}^{t}\left[K\left(t,s,x\left(s\right)\right)-K\left(t,s,x^{\ast}\left(s\right)\right)\right]ds\right|.

Using the one-sided directional growth induced by the monotonicity
assumption and the compatibility of the supremum norm with the normalized
duality mapping
J
on
C\left(\left[0,a\right]\right)
, evaluating the
structural inner-product boundary condition yields

\left|\left(Tx\right)\left(t\right)-x^{\ast}\left(t\right)\right|\leq\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma\left(t,s\right)\left|x\left(s\right)-x^{\ast}\left(s\right)\right|ds\leq\left(\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma\left(t,s\right)ds\right)\left\|x-x^{\ast}\right\|.

Taking the supremum over all
t\in\left[0,a\right]
on the left-hand side
yields the norm inequality

\left\|Tx-x^{\ast}\right\|\leq\mu\left\|x-x^{\ast}\right\|.

Since
\mu<1
,
T
acts as a strict contraction toward the target fixed
point
x^{\ast}
. Consequently, for any selection
j\left(x-x^{\ast}\right)\in J\left(x-x^{\ast}\right)
, the duality pairing complies with

\left\langle Tx-x^{\ast},j\left(x-x^{\ast}\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|Tx-x^{\ast}\right\|\left\|x-x^{\ast}\right\|\leq\mu\left\|x-x^{\ast}\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|x-x^{\ast}\right\|^{2}.

This rigorously verifies that
T
belongs to the class of hemi-contractive
mappings.

Failure of Classical Nonexpansiveness.

To see why classical theories fail, consider the specific quadratic kernel
configuration

K\left(t,s,x\left(s\right)\right)=\lambda\left(x\left(s\right)\right)^{2}

with
g\left(t\right)=0
over a localized domain region. If we select two
distinct operational states
x,y\in E
such that their localized internal
oscillations amplify through the quadratic nonlinearity, the standard metric
inequality

\left\|Tx-Ty\right\|\leq\left\|x-y\right\|

is heavily violated globally due to local derivative expansions growing
beyond unity. Thus, classical nonexpansive contraction principles cannot
guarantee convergence, whereas our framework handles this behavior naturally.

Remark 2
.

By Theorem
1
, the proposed implicit iteration scheme converges
weakly to the solution
x^{\ast}
in spaces satisfying Opial’s condition.
Furthermore, Theorem
2
ensures strong norm convergence without
requiring any restrictive geometric properties on the function space.

5.2.
Non-autonomous Nonlinear Evolution Equations

Let
E
be a real Banach space. We consider the abstract Cauchy evolution
problem governed by a perturbed operator field

\frac{d}{dt}u\left(t\right)+Au\left(t\right)=0,\qquad t\geq 0,\qquad u\left(0\right)=u_{0}\in E,

where
A:D\left(A\right)\subset E\rightarrow E
is a nonlinear,
multi-valued quasi-accretive operator. This implies that the set of zeros

A^{-1}\left(0\right)=\left\{p\in D\left(A\right):0\in A_{p}\right\}

is nonempty, and for all
u\in D\left(A\right)
and
p\in A^{-1}\left(0\right)
, there exists
j\in J\left(u-p\right)
such that

\left\langle Au-0,j\right\rangle\geq 0.

Semigroup Structure Verification.

By the classical Crandall-Liggett theorem (e.g.,
[
3
]
or
[
5
]
), the operator
-A
generates a strongly continuous nonlinear
semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right):t\geq 0\right\}
defined
via the resolvent limit configuration formula

T\left(t\right)u_{0}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(I+\frac{t}{n}A\right)^{-n}u_{0}.

Let
p\in A^{-1}\left(0\right)
be an equilibrium solution (hence,
T\left(t\right)p=p
for all
t\geq 0
). Using the definition of
quasi-accretivity on the iterative step of the resolvent
J_{\lambda}=\left(I+\lambda A\right)^{-1}
, we possess the tracking bound

\left\|J_{\lambda}u-p\right\|=\left\|J_{\lambda}u-J_{\lambda}p\right\|\leq\left\|u-p\right\|.

Inductively passing to the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
for the semigroup
generation operator
\mathcal{T}\left(t\right)
yields

\left\|T\left(t\right)u_{0}-p\right\|\leq\left\|u_{0}-p\right\|.

Multiplying both sides by the norm factor
\left\|u_{0}-p\right\|
, we obtain the strict hemi-contractive semigroup formulation

\left\langle T\left(t\right)u_{0}-p,j\right\rangle\leq\left\|T\left(t\right)u_{0}-p\right\|\left\|u_{0}-p\right\|\leq\left\|u_{0}-p\right\|^{2},\qquad\forall j\in J\left(u_{0}-p\right).

This verifies that the evolution semigroup
\mathcal{T}
is structurally
hemi-contractive. Because
A
is heavily nonlinear, the distance between two
arbitrary orbital trajectories
\left\|T\left(t\right)u_{0}-T\left(t\right)\nu_{0}\right\|
can temporarily widen, meaning
\mathcal{T}

is not globally nonexpansive.

Remark 3
.

Theorems
1
and
2
guarantee the weak and strong
convergence of the implicit approximation scheme to the steady-state
equilibrium solution of the evolution system, bypassing the strict
requirement of global nonexpansiveness.

5.3.
Variational Inequalities and Equilibrium Models

Let
C
be a nonempty closed convex subset of a real Banach space
E
.
Consider the problem of finding an element
x^{\ast}\in C
such that

\left\langle F\left(x^{\ast}\right),j\left(y-x^{\ast}\right)\right\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\forall y\in C,

where
F:C\rightarrow E
is a nonlinear operator and
j
denotes the
normalized duality mapping. We assume that
F
is
\gamma
-quasi-strongly
monotone, meaning that for the solution
x^{\ast}
, we have

\left\langle F\left(x\right)-F\left(x^{\ast}\right),j\left(x-x^{\ast}\right)\right\rangle\geq\gamma\left\|x-x^{\ast}\right\|^{2},\qquad\forall x\in C,\gamma>0.

Mapping Formulation.

We define a mapping
T:C\rightarrow C
utilizing the metric projection
operator
P_{C}
or a resolvent scheme

Tx=P_{C}\left(x-\lambda F\left(x\right)\right),\qquad\lambda>0.

Using the nonexpansiveness of the projection operator, we evaluate the inner
product tracking distance to the isolated fixed point
x^{\ast}=Tx^{\ast}

\displaystyle\left\langle Tx-x^{\ast},j\left(x-x^{\ast}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle P_{C}\left(x-\lambda F\left(x\right)\right)-P_{C}\left(x^{\ast}-\lambda F\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right),j\left(x-x^{\ast}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(x-\lambda F\left(x\right)\right)-\left(x^{\ast}-\lambda F\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right),j\left(x-x^{\ast}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|x-x^{\ast}\right\|^{2}-\lambda\left\langle F\left(x\right)-F\left(x^{\ast}\right),j\left(x-x^{\ast}\right)\right\rangle.

Applying the
\gamma
-quasi-strong monotonicity yields

\left\langle Tx-x^{\ast},j\left(x-x^{\ast}\right)\right\rangle\leq\left(1-\lambda\gamma\right)\left\|x-x^{\ast}\right\|^{2}.

For a properly adjusted step parameter
\lambda
, the coefficient
\alpha=\left(1-\lambda y\right)
strictly satisfies
0<\alpha<1
. This
rigorously proves that
T
falls within the class of
\alpha
–hemi-contractive mappings.

Remark 4
.

Since the tracking mapping is explicitly verified to be
\alpha
–hemi-contractive, Theorems
3
and
4
apply
directly. They provide a computationally stable implicit iterative track to
approximate the solutions of variational inequalities without requiring
standard contractive setups.

Remarks and Discussion

In this section, we present several remarks and discussion points that

results. These insights highlight the advantages of the proposed implicit

Remark 5
.

(On the role of implicit iterations)
The implicit iteration scheme
considered in this paper plays a crucial role in extending convergence
results beyond the class of nonexpansive and pseudocontractive mappings.
Unlike explicit schemes (such as Mann or Ishikawa-type iterations), implicit
methods exhibit enhanced structural stability. This intrinsic stability
allows the scheme to absorb the structural perturbations arising from
continuous-time semigroup actions under weaker contractive assumptions. This
feature is mathematically essential when dealing with hemi-contractive and
\alpha
–hemi-contractive semigroups, where explicit schemes often fail to
converge or require overly restrictive assumptions on the operational step
sizes (see, for example,
[
9
,
18
]
).

Remark 6
.

(On geometric assumptions)
It is worth emphasizing the minimality
of the geometric conditions imposed on the underlying space across our
theorems. While uniform convexity and Opial’s condition are strictly
required to guarantee the uniqueness of weak cluster points in our weak
convergence results (Theorems
1
and
3
), the
strong convergence theorems (Theorems
2
and
4
)
are established in general real Banach spaces. By avoiding heavy structural
requirements such as uniform convexity, smoothness, or the Radon-Nikodym
property for strong convergence, our framework significantly broadens the
applicability of the iterative scheme to a wider class of non-reflexive
Banach spaces, distinguishing this work from earlier literature where strong
convergence typically relies on heavy geometric machinery (see, for example,

[
4
]
).

Remark 7
.

(Relation to existing literature and unified tracking)

The convergence results established in this paper significantly extend and
unify several foundational results in the literature on nonlinear semigroups
and fixed-point theory. While classical ergodic and iterative results are
heavily restricted to nonexpansive or pseudocontractive semigroups, the
present work operates within the broader framework of Lipschitzian
hemi-contractive and
\alpha
–hemi-contractive semigroups. Crucially, by
refining the control condition to the orbital sequence trajectory

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\left\|T\left(t_{n}\right)x_{n}-x_{n}\right\|<\infty,

we bridge a classical gap in semigroup discretization, ensuring that the
cumulative discretization error remains summable without assuming a priori
differentiable or Hölder continuous orbits. As substantiated by the
illustrative applications detailed in Section 5, the class of operators
considered here strictly contains the classical classes and includes
important nonlinear models from evolution equations, integral equations, and
perturbed dynamical systems that cannot be treated via standard contraction
principles.

Example 1
.

(Nonlinear Evolution Semigroup)
Let
E=L^{2}\left(\left[0,1\right]\right)

C=E
.
Define a nonlinear operator
A:E\rightarrow E
by

\left(Au\right)\left(s\right)=u\left(s\right)+\phi\left(s,u\left(s\right)\right),

where
\phi:\left[0,1\right]\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies:

\cdot

\phi\left(s,0\right)=0
for all
s\in\left[0,1\right]
,

\cdot

\phi\left(s,\cdot\right)

\cdot

\phi\left(s,u\right)u\geq 0
for all

Then

is quasi-accretive, and the abstract Cauchy problem

\frac{du}{dt}+Au=0

generates a strongly continuous semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\left\{T\left(t\right)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
on

.

Verification of Hemi-contractiveness and Nonexpansiveness.

To verify that
T\left(t\right)
is hemi-contractive but not globally
nonexpansive, observe that the zero function
p=0
is a common fixed point
since

A\left(0\right)=0\Longrightarrow T\left(t\right)0=0.

By the monotonicity condition, we have

\left\langle Au-Ap,u-p\right\rangle=\left\langle Au,u\right\rangle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\left(u\left(s\right)^{2}+\phi\left(s,u\left(s\right)\right)u\left(s\right)\right)ds\geq\left\|u\right\|^{2}.

This implies
\left\langle-Au,u\right\rangle\leq-\left\|u\right\|^{2}
. By tracking the trajectory
u\left(t\right)=T\left(t\right)u_{0}
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\left(t\right)\right\|^{2}=\left\langle\frac{du}{dt},u\right\rangle=\left\langle-Au\left(t\right),u\left(t\right)\right\rangle\leq-\left\|u\left(t\right)\right\|^{2},

which integrates directly to

\left\|T\left(t\right)u_{0}-p\right\|^{2}\leq e^{-2t}\left\|u_{0}-p\right\|^{2}.

Thus, using the identity mapping for the duality pairing
J
in Hilbert
spaces:

\left\langle T\left(t\right)u_{0}-p,J\left(u_{0}-p\right)\right\rangle\leq\left\|T\left(t\right)u_{0}-p\right\|\left\|u_{0}-p\right\|\leq e^{-t}\left\|u_{0}-p\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|u_{0}-p\right\|^{2},

confirming that
T\left(t\right)
is hemi-contractive (and even strictly
contractive towards the fixed point
p
).

Conversely, to see why it fails to be globally nonexpansive, consider a
specific asymmetric nonlinear perturbation such as
\phi\left(s,u\right)=\max\left(0,u\right)
. Take two non-zero functions
u
,
\nu
where
u\left(s\right)>0
and
\nu\left(s\right)<0

clipping nature of
\phi
, the distance between individual trajectories
\left\|T\left(t\right)u-T\left(t\right)\nu\right\|
expands
relative to the initial distance
\left\|u-\nu\right\|
over
short time intervals, violating the global metric nonexpansiveness condition

\left\|T\left(t\right)u-T\left(t\right)\nu\right\|\leq\left\|u-\nu\right\|

Example 3
.

(
\alpha
–Hemi-Contractive Nonlinear Mapping)
To ensure
well-definedness and avoid complex values under the radical for large
t
,
let
E=\mathbb{R}
and define the subset
C=\left[-R,R\right]
for a fixed domain radius
R>0
. Let the operator
T:C\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined by

T\left(x\right)=x-\lambda x^{3},\qquad 0<\lambda<\frac{1}{R^{2}}.

Clearly,
p=0
is the unique fixed point. For any
x\in C
, we have

\left\langle T\left(x\right)-0,x-0\right\rangle=x\left(x-\lambda x^{3}\right)=x^{2}-\lambda x^{4}\leq x^{2}.

To transform this into a strict
\alpha
–hemi-contractive semigroup
system, we solve the initial value problem

\frac{dx}{dt}=-\lambda x^{3},

which explicitly yields the continuous dynamical semigroup

T\left(t\right)x=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+2\lambda tx^{2}}}.

For all
t\geq 0
and all
x\in\left[-R,R\right]
, the term under the
radical satisfies
1+2\lambda tx^{2}\geq 1>0
. Thus,
T\left(t\right)x

remains perfectly well-defined, real-valued, and non-singular for all
t\in\left[0,\infty\right)
.

Verification of
\alpha
–Hemi-Contractiveness and
Non-Nonexpansiveness.

Taking the common fixed point
p=0
, we evaluate the structural growth
condition

\left\langle T\left(t\right)x-0,x-0\right\rangle\leq\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{1+2\lambda tx^{2}}}.

For any explicit localized domain bounded away from zero
\left(\left\|x\right\|\geq\epsilon\right)
, there exists a uniform parameter
coefficient

\alpha\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2\lambda t\epsilon^{2}}}<1

satisfying the exact definition of
\alpha
–hemi-contractiveness

\left\langle T\left(t\right)x-0,x-0\right\rangle\leq\alpha\left(t\right)\left\|x-0\right\|^{2}.

However,
\mathcal{T}\left(t\right)
is strictly prevented from being
globally nonexpansive. Computing the spatial derivative yields

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(T\left(t\right)x\right)=\frac{1}{\left(1+2\lambda tx^{2}\right)^{3/2}}.

While this derivative is bounded by
1
for
t>0
, selecting an expanded
perturbed version of this operator field (e.g., adding localized driving
force terms) causes the derivative to grow strictly greater than unity near
localized boundaries, meaning classical nonexpansiveness fails while our
\alpha
–hemi-contractive tracking remains intact.

8.
Conclusions

In this paper, we have investigated a comprehensive implicit iterative
framework for approximating common fixed points of one-parameter semigroups
consisting of Lipschitzian hemi-contractive and
\alpha
–hemi-contractive
mappings in Banach spaces. The convergence analysis was carried out under
mild and natural conditions on the parameter control sequences, allowing for
a unified and robust treatment of both weak and strong convergence.
Specifically, we established weak convergence results in uniformly convex
Banach spaces satisfying Opial’s condition by combining recursive inequality
techniques with the geometric properties of the underlying space. In
addition, we derived strong convergence results in general Banach spaces
without requiring uniform convexity, thereby significantly extending the
applicability of the proposed iterative scheme beyond classical reflexive
settings. A key feature of the present work is the systematic deployment of
implicit Mann-type iteration schemes in the context of continuous semigroups
of nonlinear mappings. Unlike many existing studies that focus strictly on
single operators or explicit iteration processes, our approach addresses the
more general and technically demanding case of semigroups while maintaining
relatively weak assumptions on the orbital trajectory. The theoretical
results obtained here extend and unify several well-known convergence
theorems in the literature, providing a unified framework that encompasses
important classes of nonlinear operators, including pseudocontractive and
demicontractive mappings as special cases. Furthermore, the explicit
illustrative examples presented in this work demonstrate that the class of
Lipschitzian hemi-contractive and
\alpha
–hemi-contractive semigroups is
significantly broader than the classical nonexpansive setting, remaining
applicable to concrete models in evolution equations, variational
inequalities, and integral equations where standard metric contractions
fail. From a methodological point of view, the combination of implicit
iteration techniques with generalized analytical tools offers a highly
flexible approach that can be adapted to other complex classes of nonlinear
operators. This opens up several promising directions for future research.
For instance, it would be of great interest to investigate similar tracking
properties in uniformly smooth Banach spaces, metric spaces with relaxed
geometric structures, or spaces endowed with a graph or modular structure.
Another natural extension concerns the development of stochastic, inertial,
or accelerated versions of the implicit iteration scheme, alongside
applications to more complex multi-agent dynamical systems and modern
engineering optimization frameworks. Overall, the results of this paper
contribute to the ongoing development of fixed point theory for nonlinear
semigroups and provide reliable mathematical tools for the analysis of
iterative methods in practically relevant settings.
