Evaluation Toolkit
for Technical
Assistance Programs

 This report presents a toolkit for energy technical assistance providers
 to evaluate active and design future technical assistance programs. It
 was developed at the request of, and in close partnership with, the U.S.
 Department of Energy, aligned with their approach to evidence-building
 in evaluation processes, and informed by interviews with program
 managers and technical assistance leads across programs supported by
 the U.S. Department of Energy.

credibly. It may be helpful to review the Glossary of Terms before you begin—to support the first step in the process, so you start
with a common understanding of the evaluation concepts and methods.

 A Logic Model Is Different Than an Evaluation Framework
 When planning an evaluation, it is important to understand that a ToC or Logic Model provides essential context for your
 program but does not automatically define an evaluation framework. These models describe how a program is expected
 to work, such as its activities, outputs, and intended outcomes, but the evaluation framework should instead focus on what
 you need to learn, demonstrate, or improve across programs.

Types of evaluation
This toolkit focuses on five common types of evaluation used
in technical assistance programs:

 • Process evaluation: Examines how a program is being
 implemented and whether activities are occurring as
 intended
 • Outcome evaluation: Measures intermediate changes or
 results produced by a program
 • Impact evaluation: Assesses effects and determines
 whether observed changes can be attributed to the
 program; usually need a baseline comparison or
 counterfactuals
 • Performance evaluation: Tracks progress toward
 predefined targets, milestones, or deliverables to assess
 whether commitments are being met.
Each evaluation type helps you answer different questions
and requires different levels of time, resources, and data.
