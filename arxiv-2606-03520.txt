Finite palette endpoints and degree-square Turán problems

Abstract

Introduction

Turán-type problems ask for the largest possible density of a large combinatorial structure avoiding a prescribed configuration. For ordinary graphs, the asymptotic theory is governed by the Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem
[
3
,
5
]
. Hypergraph Turán theory is much less rigid: even for
3
-uniform hypergraphs, several of the most basic density problems remain open.

One variant, introduced by Erdős and Sós
[
4
]
, imposes a uniformity condition on the host hypergraph. Let
H
be a
3
-uniform hypergraph. For
d\in[0,1]
and

\eta>0
, an
n
-vertex
3
-graph
G
is said to be

(d,\eta)
-
uniformly dense
if every vertex subset
U\subseteq V(G)

with
|U|\geq\eta n
satisfies

e_{G}(U)\geq d\binom{|U|}{3}
.
The
uniform Turán density
of
H
, denoted by
\pi_{\mathrm{u}}(H)
, is
the supremum of all
d\in[0,1]
such that for every
\eta>0
and
every
n_{0}
there exists an
H
-free
(d,\eta)
-uniformly dense

3
-graph on some
n\geq n_{0}
vertices. Roughly speaking, a
3
-uniform hypergraph is uniformly dense if every linear-size vertex subset spans approximately the expected number of edges. The uniform Turán density
\pi_{\mathrm{u}}(H)
of a
3
-graph
H
is the threshold above which every sufficiently large uniformly dense
3
-graph contains
H
. The first non-trivial exact result in this area was the determination of the uniform Turán density of the broken tetrahedron
K_{4}^{(3)-}
, proved independently by Glebov, Král’ and Volec
[
6
]
and by Reiher, Rödl and Schacht
[
14
]
. Further progress includes the determination of the uniform Turán densities of tight cycles by Bucić, Cooper, Král’, Mohr and Munhá Correia
[
2
]
.

A central tool in the uniform Turán density problem is the palette method.
A
palette
is a pair
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
, where
\mathcal{C}
is a finite set of colors and

\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{3}
is a set of admissible ordered triples.
Given an ordered vertex set and a coloring
\chi:\binom{V}{2}\to\mathcal{C}
,
a triple
xyz
, written in increasing order
x<y<z
, is declared to
be an edge whenever
\bigl(\chi(xy),\chi(xz),\chi(yz)\bigr)\in\mathcal{A}
.
A
3
-graph
H
is
\mathcal{P}
-
colorable
if there exists a linear order on

V(H)
and a coloring of all pairs by colors from
\mathcal{C}
such that every
edge of
H
is admissible in this sense.
The
density
of
\mathcal{P}
is
d(\mathcal{P})=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{|\mathcal{C}|^{3}}
.
If
H
is not
\mathcal{P}
-colorable, then the standard random construction
associated with
\mathcal{P}
gives the lower bound
\pi_{\mathrm{u}}(H)\geq d(\mathcal{P})
.

Recent works have shown that palettes are not merely a source of lower-bound constructions. Lamaison
[
9
]
proved that palette constructions always give tight lower bounds for uniform Turán density: equivalently,
\pi_{\mathrm{u}}(H)
is determined by the densities of palettes that do not color
H
. Král’, Kučerák, Lamaison and Tardos
[
8
]
then developed a palette classification theorem, giving a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a finite
3
-graph satisfying prescribed palette colorability and non-colorability constraints. Related work of King, Piga, Sales and Schülke
[
7
]
studied possible uniform Turán densities through palette Lagrangians.

These developments reduce many questions about uniform Turán density to finite extremal problems for palettes. A typical problem is the following: given a family of forbidden palettes and a fixed number of colors, determine the maximum number of admissible triples in a palette avoiding that family. This finite optimization problem is the main object of the present paper.

1.1
Relation to previous palette–digraph reductions

Several recent papers have connected palette extremal problems with digraph extremal theory.
Lamaison and Wu
[
10
]
used an auxiliary digraph associated with a palette in their work on the uniform Turán density of large stars.
Lin and Zhou
[
13
]
further developed this approach for stars in uniformly dense hypergraphs, using auxiliary digraphs together with degree estimates of Caro–Wei type. Lin, Wang, Zhou and Zhou
[
12
]
subsequently introduced a systematic connection between digraph Turán problems and uniform Turán densities of
3
-graphs, including left and right palettes associated with digraphs. Very recently, Lin, Sun, Wang and Zhou
[
11
]
extended the palette framework to certain
k
-uniform settings.

Our contribution can be viewed as a degree-square refinement of this recent line of work. We prove that for a natural family of left and right tournament palettes the finite palette extremal problem is exactly equal to a degree-square Turán problem for digraphs. This exact identity is then combined with a majorization principle for convex degree moments.

1.2
Main results

Let
D
be a loopless digraph. We write
A(D)
for its arc set and regard each arc
uv\in A(D)
as a separate color
c_{uv}
. The left and right palettes generated by
D
are

\mathcal{P}_{D}^{L}=(V(D)\cup A(D),\{(u,v,c_{uv}):uv\in A(D)\})

and

\mathcal{P}_{D}^{R}=(V(D)\cup A(D),\{(c_{uv},u,v):uv\in A(D)\}).

A palette
homomorphism

\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a map between color sets preserving admissible triples; we write
\mathcal{P}\preceq\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
when such a homomorphism exists. For a tournament
T
, define

\pal_{T}(m)=\max\{|\mathcal{A(P)}|:|\mathcal{C(P)}|=m,\mathcal{P}_{T}^{L}\npreceq\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}_{T}^{R}\npreceq\mathcal{P}\}.

Our first main theorem identifies this finite palette problem with a one-sided degree-square Turán problem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T
be a self-converse tournament with at least two vertices. Then, for every
m\geq 1
,

\pal_{T}(m)=\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T),

where

\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T)=\max\left\{\sum_{v\in V(D)}d_{D}^{+}(v)^{2}:|V(D)|=m,\;D\text{ is }T\text{-free}\right\}.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given in Section
5
. It associates with a palette two auxiliary digraphs
G_{L}(\mathcal{P})
and
G_{R}(\mathcal{P})
and reduce the palette extremal problem to a degree-square Turán problem. The reverse inequality is obtained by building a palette directly from an extremal
T
-free digraph.

We next give the degree-square inputs needed for this theorem. In Section 2, we prove a general nested-majorization transfer principle: whenever an ordinary Turán extremal construction has extremal initial segments, the same construction maximizes every nondecreasing convex function of the out-degree sequence. Applying this principle to the Brown–Harary and Zhou–Li extremal digraphs for directed cycles gives the following exact formula. The proof of Theorem
1.2
will be presented in Section
3
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\ell\geq 3
,
n=(\ell-1)q+r
where
0\leq r<\ell-1
, and let
\varphi:[0,n-1]\to\mathbb{R}
be nondecreasing and convex. Then

\operatorname{ex}_{\varphi}^{+}(n,\overrightarrow{C}_{\ell})=\sum_{i=1}^{q}(\ell-1)\,\varphi\!\left((\ell-1)(q-i+1)-1+r\right)+\begin{cases}r\varphi(r-1),&r>0,\\
0,&r=0.\end{cases}

For
\varphi(x)=x^{2}
and
\ell=3
, this yields
\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,\overrightarrow{C}_{3})=\frac{m(m^{2}-1)}{3}
.
Combining this with Theorem
1.1
gives the sharp finite endpoint for the two cyclic-triangle palettes:

\pal_{\overrightarrow{C}_{3}}(m)=\frac{m(m^{2}-1)}{3},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\max d(\mathcal{P})=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3m^{2}}.

Let
TT_{\ell}
be the transitive tournament on
\ell
vertices.
We also show a second exact tournament input,
\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,TT_{3})=m\left\lfloor\frac{m^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
, and the equality can be attained by the balanced complete bidirected bipartite digraph.

Finally, the cyclic-triangle endpoint gives a uniform Turán density consequence.
We will finish the proof of Theorem
1.3
in Section
6
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
m\geq 2
, there exists a finite
3
-uniform hypergraph
H_{m}
such that

\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3m^{2}}\leq\pi_{\mathrm{u}}(H_{m})\leq\frac{1}{3}.

Consequently, the densities
\pi_{\mathrm{u}}(H_{m})
converge to
1/3
as
m\to\infty
.

2
Degree-majorization for digraphs

For a loopless digraph
F
, let

\ex(n,F)=\max\{|A(D)|:|V(D)|=n,\;D\text{ is }F\text{-free}\}

denote the ordinary Turán number. For a nondecreasing convex function
\varphi:[0,n-1]\to\mathbb{R}
, define

\operatorname{ex}_{\varphi}^{+}(n,F)=\max\left\{\sum_{v\in V(D)}\varphi(d_{D}^{+}(v)):|V(D)|=n,\;D\text{ is }F\text{-free}\right\}.

In particular,

\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(n,F)=\operatorname{ex}_{x^{2}}^{+}(n,F).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}\geq 0
and
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}\geq 0
be two nonincreasing sequences of integers bounded by
M
. Suppose that

\sum_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j}b_{i}\qquad\text{for every }1\leq j\leq n.

If
\varphi:[0,M]\to\mathbb{R}
is nondecreasing and convex, then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi(a_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi(b_{i}).

Proof.

For a nonincreasing sequence
c_{1}\geq c_{2}\geq\cdots\geq c_{n}
and a real number
t
,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(c_{i}-t)_{+}=\max_{0\leq j\leq n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i}-jt\right).

The prefix inequalities therefore imply

\sum_{i}(a_{i}-t)_{+}\leq\sum_{i}(b_{i}-t)_{+}\qquad\text{for every }t.

Since the entries are integers, every convex function on
\{0,1,\ldots,M\}
can be written as

\varphi(x)=\varphi(0)+\alpha x+\sum_{t=1}^{M-1}\beta_{t}(x-t)_{+},

where
\alpha=\varphi(1)-\varphi(0)\geq 0
and
\beta_{t}=\varphi(t+1)-2\varphi(t)+\varphi(t-1)\geq 0
. Taking
j=n
in the prefix inequalities gives
\sum_{i}a_{i}\leq\sum_{i}b_{i}
. Combining this with the inequalities for
(x-t)_{+}
proves the lemma.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F
be a loopless digraph and let
n\geq 1
. Suppose that there is an
F
-free digraph
G_{n}
on
n
vertices with nonincreasing out-degree sequence

b_{1}^{(n)}\geq b_{2}^{(n)}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}^{(n)}

such that, for every
1\leq j\leq n
,

\sum_{i=1}^{j}b_{i}^{(n)}=\ex(j,F)+j(n-j).

Then, for every nondecreasing convex function
\varphi:[0,n-1]\to\mathbb{R}
,
\operatorname{ex}_{\varphi}^{+}(n,F)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi(b_{i}^{(n)}).

Proof.

Let
D
be an
F
-free digraph on
n
vertices, and write its out-degrees in nonincreasing order as
d_{1}^{+}\geq d_{2}^{+}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}^{+}
.
For
1\leq j\leq n
, let
U
be a set of
j
vertices of largest out-degree. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{j}d_{i}^{+}=|A(D[U])|+e_{D}^{+}(U,V(D)\setminus U)\leq\ex(j,F)+j(n-j)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}b_{i}^{(n)}.

Thus the out-degree sequence of
D
is weakly majorized by that of
G_{n}
. Lemma
2.1
gives

\sum_{v\in V(D)}\varphi(d_{D}^{+}(v))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi(b_{i}^{(n)}).

The digraph
G_{n}
attains equality, proving the theorem.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

If
F
is isomorphic to its reverse, then the same bound holds for in-degree moments. Indeed, applying Theorem
2.2
to the reverse of an
F
-free digraph bounds
\sum_{v}\varphi(d_{D}^{-}(v))
by the same expression.

3
Directed cycles

Let
\overrightarrow{C}_{\ell}
be a directed cycle of length
\ell
. For
\ell\geq 3
and
n\geq 1
, write
n=(\ell-1)q+r
where
0\leq r<\ell-1
.
Let
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\ell-1}
be the ordered complete-block chain obtained by taking
q
complete digraph blocks of size
\ell-1
, one final block of size
r
if
r>0
, and orienting every inter-block arc from earlier blocks to later blocks. A complete digraph block means that both arcs are present between every pair of distinct vertices in the block.

Theorem 3.1

(Brown–Harary,
[
1
]
; Zhou–Li,
[
15
]
)
.

For every
\ell\geq 3
and every
j\geq 1
, if
j=(\ell-1)q^{\prime}+s
with
0\leq s<\ell-1
, then

\ex(j,\overrightarrow{C}_{\ell})=|A(\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{j,\ell-1})|=\frac{1}{2}j^{2}+\frac{\ell-3}{2}j-\frac{s(\ell-1-s)}{2}.

The case
\ell=3
is due to Brown and Harary, and the general directed-cycle theorem is due to Zhou and Li.

Lemma 3.2
.

The digraph
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\ell-1}
is
\overrightarrow{C}_{\ell}
-free.

Proof.

Any directed cycle using vertices from more than one block would require an inter-block arc going from a later block to an earlier block. However, no such arc exists. Hence every directed cycle lies in a single block of length strictly less than
\ell
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The nonincreasing out-degree sequence of
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\ell-1}
is given by

b_{(i-1)(\ell-1)+a}=(\ell-1)(q-i+1)-1+r

for
1\leq i\leq q
and
1\leq a\leq\ell-1
, together with
r
final terms equal to
r-1
if
r>0
.

Proof.

A vertex in the
i
-th full block sends arcs to
\ell-2
vertices inside its block, to
(\ell-1)(q-i)
vertices in later full blocks, and to
r
vertices in the final block. This gives
(\ell-1)(q-i+1)-1+r
. A vertex in the final block sends arcs only to the other
r-1
vertices of that block.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}
be the out-degree sequence in Lemma
3.3
. Then, for every
1\leq j\leq n
,

\sum_{i=1}^{j}b_{i}=\ex(j,\overrightarrow{C}_{\ell})+j(n-j)
.

Proof.

Let
U_{j}
be the first
j
vertices of
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\ell-1}
in the block order. Then
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\ell-1}[U_{j}]
is isomorphic to
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{j,\ell-1}
, so Theorem
3.1
gives
|A(\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\ell-1}[U_{j}])|=\ex(j,\overrightarrow{C}_{\ell})
.
Every arc from
U_{j}
to its complement is present: this is clear for later blocks, and also holds if
U_{j}
cuts through a complete block. Hence

\sum_{v\in U_{j}}d_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\ell-1}}^{+}(v)=\ex(j,\overrightarrow{C}_{\ell})+j(n-j).

The vertices of
U_{j}
are precisely the
j
vertices of largest out-degree, giving the claimed identity.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma
3.4
verifies the hypothesis of Theorem
2.2
for
F=\overrightarrow{C}_{\ell}
and
G_{n}=\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\ell-1}
. The displayed formula follows from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\ell\geq 3
, and write
n=(\ell-1)q+r
, where
0\leq r<\ell-1
. Then

\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(n,\overrightarrow{C}_{\ell})=\sum_{i=1}^{q}(\ell-1)\left((\ell-1)(q-i+1)-1+r\right)^{2}+\begin{cases}r(r-1)^{2},&r>0,\\
0,&r=0.\end{cases}

In particular,
\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(n,\overrightarrow{C}_{3})=\frac{n(n^{2}-1)}{3}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
D
be a
\overrightarrow{C}_{3}
-free digraph on
n
vertices. Then
\sum_{v\in V(D)}d_{D}^{+}(v)^{2}=\frac{n(n^{2}-1)}{3}
if and only if
D
is isomorphic to the ordered digon chain
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,2}
.

Proof.

The ordered digon chain attains equality by Corollary
3.5
. Conversely,
let
D
be an extremal
\overrightarrow{C}_{3}
-free digraph, and write its out-degrees in nonincreasing order as
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
.
Let
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}
be the out-degree sequence of
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,2}
, namely

n-1,n-1,n-3,n-3,\ldots,

with a final
0
when
n
is odd. In the proof of Theorem
2.2
, the sequence
(d_{i})
is weakly majorized by
(b_{i})
. Since
x^{2}
is strictly convex on the integers, equality in Lemma
2.1
forces
(d_{i})=(b_{i})
. Indeed, using

x^{2}=x+2\sum_{t=1}^{n-2}(x-t)_{+}\qquad(0\leq x\leq n-1),

all inequalities for the hinge functions
(x-t)_{+}
, as well as the total-sum inequality, must be equal; these equalities determine the sorted sequence.

We now prove by induction on
n
that
D\cong\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,2}
. The cases
n\leq 1
are trivial. For
n\geq 2
, the equality of degree sequences gives two vertices
u,v
of out-degree
n-1
. Thus
uv
and
vu
are both arcs, and both
u
and
v
send arcs to every other vertex. If some vertex
w\notin\{u,v\}
sent an arc to
u
, then the arcs
wu,uv,vw

would form a directed triangle, a contradiction. Similarly no vertex outside
\{u,v\}
sends an arc to
v
. Hence
\{u,v\}
is a complete digraph block, all arcs between this block and the remaining vertices are directed away from the block, and there are no arcs in the reverse direction. Removing
u
and
v
leaves a
\overrightarrow{C}_{3}
-free digraph whose out-degree sequence is

n-3,n-3,n-5,n-5,\ldots,

with a final
0
if
n-2
is odd. From the induction hypothesis we know that the remaining digraph must be
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n-2,2}
. Therefore, we conclude that
D\cong\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n,2}
.
∎

4
Palettes and auxiliary digraphs

We recall the palette language used in the study of uniformly dense
3
-graphs. A palette is a pair
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
, where
\mathcal{C}
is a finite set of colors and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{3}
is a set of admissible ordered triples. Repetitions of colors in admissible triples are allowed. Its density is
d(\mathcal{P})=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{|\mathcal{C}|^{3}}.

A
3
-graph
H
is
\mathcal{P}
-colorable if there is a linear order on
V(H)
and a coloring of all pairs of vertices by elements of
\mathcal{C}
such that, whenever
x<y<z
is an edge of
H
, the triple of pair-colors on
xy,xz,yz
belongs to
\mathcal{A}
.

A homomorphism from a palette
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
to a palette
\mathcal{P^{\prime}}=(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
is a map
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
such that

(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{A}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(f(a),f(b),f(c))\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}.

We write
\mathcal{P}\preceq\mathcal{P^{\prime}}
if such a homomorphism exists, and define the reverse palette of
\mathcal{P}
by

\revop(\mathcal{P})=(\mathcal{C},\{(c,b,a):(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{A}\}).

Lemma 4.1
.

A
3
-graph
H
is
\mathcal{P}
-colorable if and only if it is
\revop(\mathcal{P})
-colorable.

Proof.

Reverse the linear order on
V(H)
and keep the same colors on unordered pairs. If an edge has color triple
(a,b,c)
in the original order, then it has color triple
(c,b,a)
in the reversed order.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If
\mathcal{P}\preceq\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
H
is
\mathcal{P}
-colorable, then
H
is
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-colorable by composing the pair-coloring with the palette homomorphism.

Let
D
be a loopless digraph. Let
\mathcal{C}_{D}=V(D)\cup A(D)
,
where each arc
uv\in A(D)
is regarded as a new color
c_{uv}
. The left and right palettes generated by
D
are

\mathcal{P}_{D}^{L}=(\mathcal{C}_{D},\{(u,v,c_{uv}):uv\in A(D)\})

and

\mathcal{P}_{D}^{R}=(\mathcal{C}_{D},\{(c_{uv},u,v):uv\in A(D)\}).

For a palette
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
, define two auxiliary digraphs
G_{L}(\mathcal{P})
and
G_{R}(\mathcal{P})
on vertex set
\mathcal{C}
by

xy\in A(G_{L}(\mathcal{P}))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists z\in\mathcal{C}\text{ such that }(x,y,z)\in\mathcal{A},

and

yz\in A(G_{R}(\mathcal{P}))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists x\in\mathcal{C}\text{ such that }(x,y,z)\in\mathcal{A}.

The auxiliary digraphs may have loops.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
T
be a tournament and
\mathcal{P}
a palette. Then

\mathcal{P}_{T}^{L}\preceq\mathcal{P}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\to G_{L}(\mathcal{P}),

and

\mathcal{P}_{T}^{R}\preceq\mathcal{P}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\to G_{R}(\mathcal{P}).

Proof.

We prove the first equivalence; the second is identical. Suppose first that
f:\mathcal{P}_{T}^{L}\to\mathcal{P}
is a palette homomorphism. Then the restriction of
f
to
V(T)
maps every arc
uv\in A(T)
to an arc
f(u)f(v)\in A(G_{L}(\mathcal{P}))
, because
(f(u),f(v),f(c_{uv}))\in\mathcal{A}(P)
.
Thus
f|_{V(T)}
is a digraph homomorphism from
T
to
G_{L}(\mathcal{P})
.

Conversely, suppose that
g:V(T)\to\mathcal{C(P)}
is a digraph homomorphism from
T
to
G_{L}(\mathcal{P})
. For every arc
uv\in A(T)
, choose a color
w_{uv}\in\mathcal{C(P)}
such that

(g(u),g(v),w_{uv})\in\mathcal{A}(P)
.
Extend
g
to
V(T)\cup A(T)
by sending
c_{uv}
to
w_{uv}
. This extension is a palette homomorphism from
\mathcal{P}_{T}^{L}
to
\mathcal{P}
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Every homomorphism from a tournament to a loopless digraph is injective. Indeed, if two distinct vertices of the tournament were identified, then the arc between them would map to a loop.

5
Self-converse tournaments and finite palette extremality

Let
T
be a tournament. We recall the following definition

\pal_{T}(m)=\max\{|\mathcal{A(P)}|:|\mathcal{C(P)}|=m,\;\mathcal{P}_{T}^{L}\npreceq\mathcal{P},\;\mathcal{P}_{T}^{R}\npreceq\mathcal{P}\}.

We now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We shall finish the proof by showing
\pal_{T}(m)\leq\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T)
and
\pal_{T}(m)\geq\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T)
.
Let
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
be an
m
-color palette such that
\mathcal{P}_{T}^{L}\npreceq\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{T}^{R}\npreceq\mathcal{P}
. We first show that
G_{L}(\mathcal{P})
and
G_{R}(\mathcal{P})
are loopless. If
G_{L}(\mathcal{P})
has a loop at
x
, then there exists
y\in\mathcal{C}
such that
(x,x,y)\in\mathcal{A}
. Since
T
has at least one arc, mapping every vertex of
T
to
x
and every arc-color
c_{uv}
to
y
gives a palette homomorphism
\mathcal{P}_{T}^{L}\to\mathcal{P}
, a contradiction. The proof for
G_{R}(\mathcal{P})
is analogous.

By Lemma
4.3
, neither
G_{L}(\mathcal{P})
nor
G_{R}(\mathcal{P})
admits a homomorphism from
T
. In particular, both auxiliary digraphs are
T
-free. For each
y\in\mathcal{C}
, let

B_{y}=\{(x,z)\in\mathcal{C}^{2}:(x,y,z)\in\mathcal{A}\}.

If
(x,z)\in B_{y}
, then
xy\in A(G_{L}(\mathcal{P}))
and
yz\in A(G_{R}(\mathcal{P}))
. Hence
|B_{y}|\leq d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)
.
It follows from Cauchy–Schwarz that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}|B_{y}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Since
G_{R}(\mathcal{P})
is
T
-free,
\sum_{y}d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)^{2}\leq\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T)
.
Moreover,
G_{L}(\mathcal{P})^{\mathrm{rev}}
is also
T
-free: otherwise
T\to G_{L}(\mathcal{P})^{\mathrm{rev}}
would imply
T^{\mathrm{rev}}\to G_{L}(\mathcal{P})
, and since
T
is self-converse this would give
T\to G_{L}(\mathcal{P})
. Therefore

\sum_{y}d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)^{2}=\sum_{y}d^{+}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})^{\mathrm{rev}}}(y)^{2}\leq\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T).

Thus
|\mathcal{A}|\leq\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T)
.

For the lower bound, let
D
be a
T
-free digraph on
m
vertices with
\sum_{y\in V(D)}d_{D}^{+}(y)^{2}=\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T)
.
Define a palette
\mathcal{P}_{D}=(V(D),\mathcal{A}_{D})
by

(x,y,z)\in\mathcal{A}_{D}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad yx\in A(D)\text{ and }yz\in A(D).

Then
|\mathcal{A}_{D}|=\sum_{y\in V(D)}d_{D}^{+}(y)^{2}
.
Repetitions of colors in triples are allowed. Hence, if
yz\in A(D)
, then taking
x=z
shows
yz\in A(G_{R}(\mathcal{P}_{D}))
, and similarly for
G_{L}(\mathcal{P}_{D})
. Therefore

G_{R}(\mathcal{P}_{D})=D
and
G_{L}(\mathcal{P}_{D})=D^{\mathrm{rev}}
.

Since
D
is
T
-free and
T
is self-converse, both auxiliary digraphs are
T
-free. Lemma
4.3
gives
\mathcal{P}_{T}^{L}\npreceq\mathcal{P}_{D}
and
\mathcal{P}_{T}^{R}\npreceq\mathcal{P}_{D}
. Thus
\pal_{T}(m)\geq\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T)
, completing the proof.
∎

5.1
The transitive triangle

Since
TT_{3}
is the transitive tournament on three vertices, it is self-converse, yet its behavior differs significantly from that of the cyclic triangle.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every
m\geq 1
,

\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,TT_{3})=m\left\lfloor\frac{m^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

Moreover, equality is attained precisely by the complete bidirected bipartite digraph with part sizes
\lfloor m/2\rfloor
and
\lceil m/2\rceil
.

Proof.

The cases
m\leq 2
are immediate, so it is sufficient to consider
m\geq 3
. Let
D
be a

TT_{3}
-free digraph on
m
vertices. For every vertex
w
of
D
, the
out-neighbourhood
N_{D}^{+}(w)
is independent in the underlying graph of

D
: indeed, if
x,y\in N_{D}^{+}(w)
and at least one of
xy
or
yx

is an arc, then
w,x,y
span a transitive triangle.

Choose a vertex
x_{0}
of maximum out-degree, and write
\Delta:=d_{D}^{+}(x_{0})
,

X:=N_{D}^{+}(x_{0})
and
Y:=V(D)\setminus X
.
Then
|X|=\Delta
and
|Y|=m-\Delta
. By the observation above, there
are no arcs inside
X
. Hence every vertex of
X
satisfies that all of its
out-neighbours belong to
Y
, and therefore has out-degree at most

|Y|=m-\Delta
. Every vertex of
Y
has out-degree at most
\Delta
,
by the choice of
x_{0}
. Consequently,

\sum_{u\in V(D)}d_{D}^{+}(u)^{2}\leq|Y|\Delta^{2}+|X|(m-\Delta)^{2}=(m-\Delta)\Delta^{2}+\Delta(m-\Delta)^{2}=m\Delta(m-\Delta)\leq m\left\lfloor\frac{m^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

For the lower bound, take a partition
V=A\cup B
with
|A|=\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor
and
|B|=\left\lceil\frac{m}{2}\right\rceil
,
put both arcs between every pair
a\in A
,
b\in B
, and put no arcs
inside the parts. This digraph is
TT_{3}
-free, since every
out-neighbourhood is one of the two independent parts. Its out-degree
square sum is

|A||B|^{2}+|B||A|^{2}=m|A||B|=m\left\lfloor\frac{m^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

It remains to prove the equality statement. Suppose that
D
is
extremal. Then equality must hold throughout the upper-bound chain. In
particular,
\Delta(m-\Delta)=\left\lfloor\frac{m^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
.
Moreover, every vertex of
X
must have out-degree
m-\Delta
, and
every vertex of
Y
must have out-degree
\Delta
. Since
\Delta

is the maximum out-degree, the first condition implies
m-\Delta\leq\Delta
.
Together with the product equality above, this forces

\Delta=\left\lceil\frac{m}{2}\right\rceil
and
|Y|=m-\Delta=\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor
.

Since
X
is independent and every vertex of
X
has out-degree

|Y|
, every vertex of
X
sends an arc to every vertex of
Y
.

We now show that
Y
is independent. Suppose, on the contrary, that
there exist
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
with
y\to y^{\prime}
. If
y
sent an arc to some

x\in X
, then, since every vertex of
X
sends an arc to every
vertex of
Y
, we would have

y\to x,\qquad y\to y^{\prime},\qquad x\to y^{\prime},

and hence
y,x,y^{\prime}
would span a transitive triangle, a contradiction.
Thus
y
sends no arcs to
X
. Consequently,
d_{D}^{+}(y)\leq|Y|-1<\Delta
,
contradicting the equality condition that every vertex of
Y
has
out-degree
\Delta
. This proves
Y
is independent.

Since
Y
is independent and every vertex of
Y
has out-degree

\Delta=|X|
, every vertex of
Y
sends an arc to every vertex of

X
. We have proved that both arcs are present between every vertex of

X
and every vertex of
Y
, and that there are no arcs inside either
part. Hence
D
is the complete bidirected bipartite digraph with
balanced part sizes.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

For every
m\geq 1
,
\pal_{TT_{3}}(m)=m\left\lfloor\frac{m^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
.
Equivalently, the sharp
m
-color palette density for avoiding both the left and right transitive-triangle palettes is
\frac{1}{m^{2}}\left\lfloor\frac{m^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

Since
TT_{3}
is self-converse, this corollary follows immediately from Theorems
1.1
and
5.1
.
∎

6
The finite endpoint below one third

Let
\mathcal{P}_{L}=\mathcal{P}_{\overrightarrow{C}_{3}}^{L}
and
\mathcal{P}_{R}=\mathcal{P}_{\overrightarrow{C}_{3}}^{R}
.
Since
\overrightarrow{C}_{3}
is self-converse,
\mathcal{P}_{L}
is isomorphic to
\revop(\mathcal{P}_{R})
, and
\mathcal{P}_{R}
is isomorphic to
\revop(\mathcal{P}_{L})
.

We first introduce two useful results which shall be frequently used in the following proof.
The first is Lamaison’s palette characterization of uniform Turán density.

Theorem 6.1

(Lamaison,
[
9
]
)
.

For every finite
3
-graph
H
,

\pi_{\mathrm{u}}(H)=\sup\{d(\mathcal{P}):H\text{ is not }\mathcal{P}\text{-colorable}\}.

The second is the palette separation theorem of Král’, Kučerák, Lamaison and Tardos.

Theorem 6.2

(Král’–Kučerák–Lamaison–Tardos,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be palettes. There exists a finite
3
-graph that is
\mathcal{P}
-colorable but not
\mathcal{P}_{0}
-colorable if and only if
\mathcal{P}\npreceq\mathcal{P}_{0}
and
\mathcal{P}\npreceq\revop(\mathcal{P}_{0})
.

Corollary 6.3
.

For every
m\geq 1
,
\pal_{\overrightarrow{C}_{3}}(m)=\frac{m(m^{2}-1)}{3}
.
Equivalently, if
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
is an
m
-color palette such that
\mathcal{P}_{L}\npreceq\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{R}\npreceq\mathcal{P}
, then
d(\mathcal{P})\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3m^{2}}
.
The bound is best possible.

Proof.

The identity follows from Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
3.5
. Dividing by
m^{3}
gives the density formulation, and the lower-bound construction in Theorem
1.1
shows sharpness.
∎

The next result records the equality conditions behind Corollary
6.3
. It is often more useful than a purely numerical statement because it describes how the two auxiliary digraphs must align.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
be an
m
-color palette such that
\mathcal{P}_{L}\npreceq\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{R}\npreceq\mathcal{P}
. For each
y\in\mathcal{C}
, write

B_{y}=\{(x,z)\in\mathcal{C}^{2}:(x,y,z)\in\mathcal{A}\}.

Then

|\mathcal{A}|=\frac{m(m^{2}-1)}{3}

if and only if the following three conditions hold:

(E1)

G_{R}(\mathcal{P})\cong\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{m,2}
and
G_{L}(\mathcal{P})^{\mathrm{rev}}\cong\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{m,2}
;

(E2)

for every
y\in\mathcal{C}
,
d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)=d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)
;

(E3)

for every
y\in\mathcal{C}
,
B_{y}=N^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)\times N^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)
.

Proof.

Assume first that
|\mathcal{A}|=m(m^{2}-1)/3
.
We recall the following inequalities from the proof of Theorem
1.1
by choosing
T=\overrightarrow{C}_{3}
.
It holds that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)

(1)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m(m^{2}-1)}{3}.

(3)

As
|\mathcal{A}|=m(m^{2}-1)/3
, the inequalities in
(1),(2)
and
(3)
must be equalities simultaneously.
In particular,
\sum_{y}d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)^{2}=\sum_{y}d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)^{2}=\frac{m(m^{2}-1)}{3}
.
Since the auxiliary digraphs are loopless and
\overrightarrow{C}_{3}
-free, Proposition
3.6
implies

G_{R}(\mathcal{P})\cong\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{m,2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad G_{L}(\mathcal{P})^{\mathrm{rev}}\cong\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{m,2}.

Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz gives proportionality of the two nonnegative vectors

\big(d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)\big)_{y\in\mathcal{C}}\quad\text{and}\quad\big(d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)\big)_{y\in\mathcal{C}}.

Their squared norms are equal, so the proportionality constant is
1
, proving (E2). Finally, for each
y
, we always have

B_{y}\subseteq N^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)\times N^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y).

Since equality also holds in the first inequality, all these inclusions must be equalities, proving (E3).

Conversely, suppose (E1)–(E3) hold. Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}|B_{y}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}d^{-}_{G_{L}(\mathcal{P})}(y)d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{C}}d^{+}_{G_{R}(\mathcal{P})}(y)^{2}=\frac{m(m^{2}-1)}{3},

where the last equality follows from (E1) and Corollary
3.5
. This proves the theorem.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
be a
m
-color palette. If
d(\mathcal{P})>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3m^{2}}
,
then
\mathcal{P}_{L}\preceq\mathcal{P}
or
\mathcal{P}_{R}\preceq\mathcal{P}
.

We now prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\star}
be an
m
-color palette avoiding both
\mathcal{P}_{L}
and
\mathcal{P}_{R}
with
d(\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\star})=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3m^{2}}
,
whose existence follows from Corollary
6.3
.
Note that
\revop(\mathcal{P}_{R})
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{P}_{L}
which together with
\mathcal{P}_{R}\npreceq\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\star}
and
\mathcal{P}_{L}\npreceq\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\star}
imply

\mathcal{P}_{R}\npreceq\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\star}
and
\mathcal{P}_{R}\npreceq\revop(\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\star})
.

By Theorem
6.2
, there exists a finite
3
-graph
H_{m}
that is
\mathcal{P}_{R}
-colorable but not
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\star}
-colorable. Theorem
6.1
then gives
\pi_{\mathrm{u}}(H_{m})\geq d(\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\star})=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3m^{2}}
.

For the upper bound, let
\mathcal{Q}=(\mathcal{C}_{Q},\mathcal{A}_{Q})
be any finite palette with
d(\mathcal{Q})>1/3
, and put
s=|\mathcal{C_{Q}}|
. Then

d(\mathcal{Q})>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3s^{2}}
.
By Corollary
6.5
, either
\mathcal{P}_{L}\preceq\mathcal{Q}
or
\mathcal{P}_{R}\preceq\mathcal{Q}
. Since
H_{m}
is
\mathcal{P}_{R}
-colorable, Lemma
4.1
and the isomorphism
\revop(\mathcal{P}_{R})\cong\mathcal{P}_{L}
imply that
H_{m}
is also
\mathcal{P}_{L}
-colorable. Hence, in either case, Remark
4.2
shows that
H_{m}
is
\mathcal{Q}
-colorable. Thus every palette of density greater than
1/3
colors
H_{m}
. By Theorem
6.1
,
\pi_{\mathrm{u}}(H_{m})\leq 1/3
.
∎

7
Open problems

Theorem
1.1
reduces a family of finite palette extremal problems to degree-square Turán problems for digraphs. We evaluated this degree-square problem for directed cycles and for the transitive triangle, but the next cases already appear to be substantially more delicate.

Problem 7.1
.

For which self-converse tournaments
T
can one determine
\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,T)
exactly?

A particularly natural test case is the strongly connected tournament on four vertices. Let
R_{4}
be the tournament with score sequence
(1,1,2,2)
. Unlike the cyclic-triangle case, the ordered complete-block chain need not be extremal for this problem: for
m=5
, the doubled complete tripartite graph with parts of sizes
\{1,2,2\}
is
R_{4}
-free and has degree-square sum
52
, whereas
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{5,3}
has degree-square sum
50
.

Problem 7.2
.

Determine
\operatorname{ex}_{2}^{+}(m,R_{4})
, where
R_{4}
is the strongly connected self-converse tournament on four vertices.

Problem 7.3
.

Is there a stability version of Corollary
6.3
or Theorem
6.4
? More precisely, if an
m
-color palette avoiding
\mathcal{P}_{L}
and
\mathcal{P}_{R}
has density close to
1/3-1/(3m^{2})
, must its auxiliary digraphs be close to the ordered digon-chain extremal construction?
