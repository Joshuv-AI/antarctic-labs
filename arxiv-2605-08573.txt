On the Existence of Boundary Layer Separation for Incompressible Fluid Flow in the Half-Space Thanks:
TongKeun Chang, KyungKeun Kang

Abstract
. We consider the Stokes system in the half-space with localized boundary data. We prove that a boundary layer separation point exists provided that a certain singular integral determined by the boundary data is negative. On the other hand, if this integral is strictly positive, then boundary layer separation does not occur. When boundary layer separation occurs, we also investigate the dynamics of the separation point and the sign of the pressure gradient. Furthermore, by a perturbation argument, we construct solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations in the half-space that exhibit the same qualitative behavior as in the Stokes case.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Stokes system in the half space.

(1.1)

w_{t}-\Delta w+\nabla p=0\qquad\mbox{div }w=0\quad\mbox{ in }\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times(0,T),

where
w
indicates the velocity of the fluid and
p
is the associated pressure
with zero initial data and non-zero boundary data:

(1.2)

\left.w\right|_{t=0}=0,\qquad\left.w\right|_{x_{n}=0}=g.

Here, the boundary data
g:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

are of separable form and have compact support in the spatial and temporal variables, which will be specified later.

Previous works have established the existence of singular solutions to the Stokes equations whose velocity gradients are unbounded near the boundary, away from the support of the boundary data, while both the velocity and its gradient remain locally square-integrable (see
[
23
,
5
,
26
,
6
,
27
]
; see also
[
1
,
2
,
25
]
for related works).
An analogous result was obtained in
[
7
]
for the case of a nonzero localized external force, rather than nonzero boundary data. More recently, it was shown in
[
8
]
that, due to a nonlocal effect, the velocity itself may become unbounded away from the support of the boundary data even under the no-slip boundary condition.

In this paper, we focus on the sign change of the velocity field near the boundary. Assuming that the boundary data are sufficiently regular, this phenomenon implies that the normal derivative of the velocity vanishes and leads to the formation of a boundary layer separation point (see Definition
1.1
below).

We briefly review previous studies on boundary layer separation. To investigate this phenomenon, Prandtl
[
32
]
derived an asymptotic form of the Navier–Stokes equations near the boundary in a two-dimensional half-plane, namely the Prandtl equations, which are written as follows.

\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u+v\partial_{y}v-\partial_{y}^{2}u=-\partial_{x}p_{E},\qquad\partial_{x}u+\partial_{y}v=0

with initial and boundary conditions

u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\quad u|_{y=0}=0,\quad\displaystyle\lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}u(x,y,t)=u_{E}(x,t).

Here
u_{E}
and
p_{E}
denote the tangential velocity and the pressure
of the outer Euler flow evaluated at the boundary. In particular, they satisfy
the Bernoulli relation

\partial_{t}u_{E}+u_{E}\partial_{x}u_{E}=-\partial_{x}p_{E}
.
Thus the pressure gradient appearing in the Prandtl equation is determined
by the corresponding outer Euler flow.

The steady Prandtl theory exhibits a sharp distinction between favorable and
adverse pressure gradients. For
dp_{E}/dx\leq 0
, Oleinik
[
31
]

proved global-in-
x
well-posedness, whereas for
dp_{E}/dx>0
solutions may
separate at a finite streamwise position
x^{*}>0
. At separation the wall
shear degenerates,

\partial_{y}u(x,0)\to 0

as
x\nearrow x^{*}
.
which corresponds to the classical Goldstein singularity
[
18
]
.
This singular behavior, including the vanishing rate of the wall shear, was
rigorously analyzed in
[
13
]
(see also
[
36
]
). The
post-separation steady regime is still much less understood, with recent
progress mainly concerning flow-reversal profiles such as Falkner–Skan
solutions
[
21
,
14
]
.

In the unsteady case, numerical
studies
[
42
,
43
]
led to the conjecture that finite-time
separation is accompanied by singularity formation. This was proved for a
trivial outer flow under symmetry assumptions in
[
16
]
, and for the
nontrivial Euler trace of
[
42
]
in
[
29
]
, via a convexity
argument involving a Lyapunov functional related to the displacement thickness (see also
[
12
,
11
]
).

Recently, the triple-deck model has been proposed as a refined description of the flow near separation, and substantial progress has been made on its mathematical analysis, including local well-posedness and ill-posedness results (see
[
22
,
20
,
15
,
17
]
and the references therein).

Our main objective of this paper is to prove the existence of a boundary layer
separation point for the Stokes system in the half-space. A related flow-reversal
phenomenon was established in
[
9
]
, where it was shown that each
component of the velocity field changes sign at least once. This result suggests
that even the linear Stokes system may exhibit reversal mechanisms connected to
boundary layer separation. In
[
9
]
, however, the localized nonzero
boundary data are singular in the time variable, and the normal derivative of
the tangential velocity becomes unbounded at the singular time around which the
sign change occurs. In contrast, the present work treats sufficiently regular
localized boundary data, for which the normal derivatives of the velocity field
remain bounded. We then establish the existence of a boundary-layer separation
point under appropriate assumptions on certain time-integral quantities
determined by the boundary data.

The main tool in our analysis is the representation formula for solutions to the Stokes system in the half-space with nonzero boundary data (see (
2.1
) and (
2.2
)). Using this representation formula together with the identity (
2.4
), we decompose the solution into five terms, as in (
2.5
). From this decomposition, we derive a formula for the normal derivative of the tangential components of the velocity on the boundary (see Lemma
3.1
).
Since the tangential components of the boundary data vanish and only the normal component is nonzero, this formula simplifies considerably and reduces to the sum of two terms; see (
3.3
). At this stage, the integral quantity (
1.4
), which is associated with the temporal part of the boundary data, turns out to play a crucial role in determining the sign of the normal derivative. One of the main steps in the analysis is to show that the sign of this integral determines whether boundary layer separation occurs (see Theorem
1.7
).

In the case where a boundary layer separation point exists, it is not stationary; rather, it moves away from the origin and tends to infinity in finite time. In fact, the time at which the boundary layer separation point escapes to infinity can also be characterized in terms of the integral (see Theorem
1.8
). This characterization is also obtained through a careful analysis of the representation formula for the normal derivative on the boundary.
Furthermore, if the temporal part of the boundary data is unimodal, namely, increasing and then decreasing to zero, then the boundary layer separation point is unique at each time for which it exists (see Theorem
1.9
).

As for the pressure, using the representation formula (
2.3
), we prove that the tangential derivatives of the pressure undergo a sign change. In particular, if the boundary data are decreasing in time, then the pressure is strictly increasing in the tangential spatial direction (see Theorem
1.6
).

Finally, we show that similar phenomena can be observed for the Navier-Stokes equations, namely

(1.2)

u_{t}-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla q=0,\qquad\operatorname{div}u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,T),

together with the initial and boundary conditions (
1.2
). Using a perturbative argument, we construct solutions that exhibit behavior analogous to that of the Stokes system (see Theorem
1.10
).

\displaystyle\|w_{i}^{L}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(Q_{+}(T))}\leq c\Big(\|g_{n}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(q(T))}+\|R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(q(T))}\Big).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\|w_{ij}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(Q_{+}(T))}\leq c\|g\|_{C^{1+\alpha,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(q(T))}.

This completes the proof of (1) of Theorem
5.1
.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|D_{x}^{2}w_{i}^{L}\|_{C^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}(Q_{+}(T))}&\leq c\Big(\|D^{2}_{x^{\prime}}g_{n}\|_{C^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}(q(T))}+\|D^{2}_{x^{\prime}}R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{C^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}(q(T))}\Big),\\
\|D_{t}w_{i}^{L}\|_{C^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}(Q_{+}(T))}&\leq c\Big(\|D_{t}g_{n}\|_{C^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}(q(T))}+\|D_{t}R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{C^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}(q(T))}\Big).\end{split}

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|w_{i}^{L}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(Q_{+}(T))}\leq c\Big(\|g_{n}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(q(T))}+\|R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(q(T))}\Big).

This completes the proof of (2)of Lemma
5.1
.
∎

6.4.
Proof of Theorem
5.2

Since
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}y_{k}\Gamma^{\prime}(y^{\prime},t-s)dy^{\prime}=0
for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
and
t-s>0
,
applying the mean value theorem twice, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\big(R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(y^{\prime},s)-R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},s)\big)dy^{\prime}\\
=&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{\prime}R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(\theta y^{\prime}+(1-\theta)x^{\prime},s)d\theta\cdot(y^{\prime}-x^{\prime})dy^{\prime}\\
=&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\nabla^{\prime}R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(\theta y^{\prime}+(1-\theta)x^{\prime},s)-\nabla^{\prime}R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},s)\Big)d\theta\cdot(y^{\prime}-x^{\prime})dy^{\prime}\\
\leq&\|R^{\prime}g\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;\dot{C}^{1+\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}))}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\int_{0}^{1}\theta|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}d\theta dy^{\prime}\\
\leq&c\|R^{\prime}g\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;\dot{C}^{1+\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}))}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}.\end{split}

Direct calculations show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\big(R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},s)-R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},t)\big)dy^{\prime}\\
\leq&\|R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\dot{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}dy^{\prime}\\
\leq&c\|R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\dot{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}.\end{split}

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\big(R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(y^{\prime},s)-R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},s)\big)dy^{\prime}ds\\
\leq&c\|R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;\dot{C}^{1+\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}))}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}ds\\
\leq&c\|R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;\dot{C}^{1+\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}))}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.\end{split}

Since
R^{\prime}_{j}g_{n}(x^{\prime},0)=0
, we note that
|R^{\prime}_{j}g_{n}(x^{\prime},t)-R^{\prime}_{j}g_{n}(x^{\prime},0)|\leq\|R^{\prime}_{j}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\cdot C^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}t^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}
, and therefore we observe that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},t)dy^{\prime}ds\\
=&\|R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\cdot C^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}t^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)dy^{\prime}ds\\
\leq&c\|R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\dot{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.\end{split}

It follows ffrom the Holder continuity of
R^{\prime}g_{n}
with respect to
t
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\big(R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},s)-R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},t)\big)dy^{\prime}ds\\
\leq&c\|R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\dot{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}dy^{\prime}ds\\
\leq&c\|R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\dot{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.\end{split}

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\big(R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},t)-R^{\prime}_{i}g_{n}(x^{\prime},s)\big)dy^{\prime}ds\leq c\|R^{\prime}g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\dot{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.\end{split}

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\big(g_{i}(y^{\prime},s)-g_{i}(x^{\prime},s)\big)dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle\leq c\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;\dot{C}^{1+\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}))}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t-s)\big(g_{i}(x^{\prime},s)-g_{i}(x^{\prime},t)\big)dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle\leq c\|g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\dot{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Since
R^{\prime}_{i}g_{j}(x^{\prime},0)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}|D_{x_{i}}R^{\prime}_{j}g_{j}(x^{\prime},t)|&\leq c\|D_{x^{\prime}}R^{\prime}_{j}g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};C^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,t))}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.\end{split}

Then, from Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}|D_{x_{n}}w_{i}(x^{\prime},0,t)|&\leq ct^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Big(\|g\|_{C^{1+\alpha,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times(0,t))}+\|R^{\prime}g\|_{C^{1+\alpha,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times(0,t))}\Big).\end{split}

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

6.5.
Proof of Lemma
5.3

From Proposition 4.4 in
[
3
]
and its proof, we recall that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|V^{1}|_{x_{n}=0}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}))}&\leq cT^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})))},\\
\|V^{1}|_{x_{n}=0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\dot{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,T))}&\leq c\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})))},\\
\|V^{1}|_{x_{n}=0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times(0,T))}&\leq cT^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})))}.\end{split}

Since
R^{\prime}:\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})\rightarrow\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})
is bounded, it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|R^{\prime}V^{1}|_{x_{n}=0}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}))}&\leq cT^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|R^{\prime}f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})))}\\
&\leq cT^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})))}.\end{split}

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|R^{\prime}V^{1}|_{x_{n}=0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1};\dot{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0,T))}&\leq c\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})))},\\
\|R^{\prime}V^{1}|_{x_{n}=0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times(0,T))}&\leq cT^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\dot{C}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})))}.\end{split}

We complete the proof of Lemma
5.3
.

6.6.
Conditions of
\phi
in Assumption
1.4
and Assumption
1.5

In this subsection, we provide some conditions of
\phi
satisfying Assumption
1.4
or Assumption
1.5
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
g
be given by (
1.3
) and let
\phi
satisfy Assumption
1.2
.

(i)

Suppose that there exists constant
r_{0}\in(\frac{3}{2},2)
such that for all
r_{0}<t<2
,

(6.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}2^{\frac{3}{2}}\phi(t)<2^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(s)ds.\end{split}

Then,
M(t)<0
for all
r_{0}<t<2
.

(ii)

Let
\displaystyle a_{0}=\sup_{0<t<1}\phi^{\prime}(t)
. Suppose that
\phi\in C^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}([0,2))
satisfy
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
. Assume further that
there exist
r_{0}\in(1,2)
and
\delta>0
with
r_{0}+\delta<2
such that for
r_{0}<t<r_{0}+\delta
,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{-1}\phi(r_{0})<\phi^{\prime}(t)<-\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{2}a_{0}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Then,
M(r_{0}+\delta)<0
.

(iii)

Suppose that
\phi\in C^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}([0,2))
satisfy
\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
. Assume further that

(6.20)

\displaystyle\inf_{1<s<2}\phi^{\prime}(s)\geq-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(s)(2-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds.

Then,
M(t)>2^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi(1)
for all
1<t<2
.

Proof.

Let
\frac{3}{2}<t<2
. Since
\phi
is decresing in
(1,2)
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}M(t)&\leq\int_{-\infty}^{1}\frac{\phi(t)-\phi(s)}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}ds=2\phi(t)(t-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\phi(s)}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}ds\leq 2^{\frac{3}{2}}\phi(t)-2^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(s)ds.\end{split}

From (
6.19
), we have
M(t)<0
for all
t\in(r_{0},2)
. This completes the proof of part (i).

By integration by parts,
M(t)
can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}M(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{\phi(t)-\phi(s)}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}ds&=2\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\prime}(s)(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds=2\int_{0}^{1}\cdots ds+2\int_{1}^{t}\cdots ds.\end{split}

Direct calculation for
1<t<2
yields:

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(s)(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds\leq 2a_{0}\int_{0}^{1}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds=4a_{0}(t^{\frac{1}{2}}-(t-1)^{\frac{1}{2}})\leq 4\sqrt{2}a_{0}.

Since
\phi
is decreasing in
(1,2)
, for
r_{0}<t<r_{0}+\delta
, we have

\displaystyle 2\int_{1}^{r_{0}+\delta}\phi^{\prime}(s)(r_{0}+\delta-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds\leq-3\sqrt{2}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}a_{0}\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{0}+\delta}(r_{0}+\delta-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds=-6\sqrt{2}a_{0}.

Additionally, observe that

\displaystyle\phi(r_{0}+\delta)=\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{0}+\delta}\phi^{\prime}(s)ds+\phi(r_{0})\geq-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{-1}\phi(r_{0})\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{0}+\delta}ds+\phi(r_{0})=-\frac{1}{2}\phi(r_{0})+\phi(r_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\phi(r_{0}).

Combining these estimates, we obtain
M(t)\leq 4\sqrt{2}a_{0}-6\sqrt{2}a_{0}=-2\sqrt{2}a_{0}<0
, which completes the proof of part (ii).

Note that for
1<t<2
, (
6.20
) implies:

(6.21)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{t}\phi^{\prime}(s)(t-s)^{-1/2}ds\geq\inf_{1<s<2}\phi^{\prime}(s)\int_{1}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}ds\geq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(s)(t-s)^{-1/2}ds.

Substituting (
6.21
) into the expression for
M(t)
, we find:

\displaystyle M(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(s)(t-s)^{-1/2}ds+2\int_{1}^{t}\phi^{\prime}(s)(t-s)^{-1/2}ds

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(s)(t-s)^{-1/2}ds\quad((t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}>(2-0)^{-\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(s)ds=2^{-1/2}\phi(1).

This completes the proof of part (iii).

∎
