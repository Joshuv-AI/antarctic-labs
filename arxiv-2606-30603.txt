Moments and sign changes of symmetric power L L -function coefficients over sums of squares
Jewel Mahajan, Arnab Mitra

Abstract
. Let f f be a normalised Hecke eigenform of even integral weight for the full modular group SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}) , let L ⁡ ( s , sym j ​ f ) L(s,\mathrm{sym}^{j}f) be the j j th symmetric power L L -function attached to f f , and let λ sym j ​ f ​ ( n ) \lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(n) denote its n n th Dirichlet coefficient. 1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a positive even integer. Let
H_{k}
be the space of all normalised Hecke eigencusp forms of weight
k
for the full modular group
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. Let
f\in H_{k}
and
\lambda_{f}(n)
be the
n
-th normalised Fourier coefficient in the Fourier expansion of
f(z)
at the cusp
\infty
, that is,

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)\,n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz},\qquad\Im(z)>0,

with
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
.
The
L
-function attached to
f
(denoted
L(s,f)
) is defined as

L(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad\Re(s)>1.

In 1974, Deligne
[
2
]
proved that for any prime
p
, there exist two complex numbers
\alpha_{f}(p)
and
\beta_{f}(p)
(known as Satake parameters) such that

(1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{f}(p)\beta_{f}(p)=1,

(2)

\displaystyle|\alpha_{f}(p)|=|\beta_{f}(p)|=1,

and

(3)

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(p)=\alpha_{f}(p)+\beta_{f}(p).

Since
f
is primitive, the coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
are multiplicative and satisfy the Hecke recurrence

(4)

\lambda_{f}(p^{\,r+1})=\lambda_{f}(p)\,\lambda_{f}(p^{\,r})-\lambda_{f}(p^{\,r-1}),\qquad r\geq 1.

Consequently, for
\Re(s)>1
, we have the Euler product

(5)

L(s,f)=\prod_{p}\;\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{\,r})}{p^{\,rs}}.

Using induction and the identities
\lambda_{f}(p)=\alpha_{f}(p)+\beta_{f}(p)
and
\alpha_{f}(p)\beta_{f}(p)=1
, together with (
4
), one obtains

(6)

\lambda_{f}(p^{\,r})=\frac{\alpha_{f}(p)^{\,r+1}-\beta_{f}(p)^{\,r+1}}{\alpha_{f}(p)-\beta_{f}(p)}=\alpha_{f}(p)^{\,r}+\alpha_{f}(p)^{\,r-2}+\cdots+\alpha_{f}(p)^{-r}

for all integers
r\geq 1
. From this follows Deligne’s inequality

(7)

|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n),

where
d(n)
is the divisor function.

Now using the multiplicativity of
\lambda_{f}(n)
and formula (
6
), the Euler product (
5
) can be rewritten as

(8)

L(s,f)=\prod_{p}\,\biggl(1-\frac{\alpha_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}\biggr)^{\!-1}\biggl(1-\frac{\beta_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}\biggr)^{\!-1}

in
\Re(s)>1
, where the product runs over all primes
p
.

The
j
th symmetric power
L
-function associated to each
f\in H_{k}
is defined as

L(s,\mathrm{sym}^{j}f):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\prod_{m=0}^{j}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{f}(p)^{m}\beta_{f}(p)^{j-m}}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}

for
\Re(s)>1
, where
\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(n)
is multiplicative. From the multiplicativity, (
1
) and (
2
), we have

(9)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(n)\ll n^{\epsilon}.

Several authors have studied the average behaviour of these Fourier coefficients. In 2004, Fomenko
[
3
]
was able to prove some results for the symmetric square
L
-functions. He showed that

\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2}f}(n)\ll x^{\frac{1}{2}}\log^{2}x.

In 2013, Zhai
[
25
]
proved an asymptotic formula for the
l
th
power sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{2}+b^{2}\leq x\\
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}^{l}(a^{2}+b^{2})

for
l\in[2,8]\cap\mathbb{Z}
and
x\geq 1
. For results related to mean square and higher moments of the coefficients of symmetric square
L
-functions on a certain sequence of positive integers, see
[
18
,
20
]
.

In
[
19
]
, Sharma and Sankaranarayanan established the formulas for the Dirichlet coefficients of the symmetric square
L
-functions over sums of four squares, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{4}^{2}\leq x\\
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{4})\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}^{2}f}(a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{4}^{2})=cx^{2}+O(x^{\frac{9}{5}+\epsilon})

for any
\epsilon>0
, where
c
is an effective constant (see
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
).

In
[
21
]
, Sharma and Sankaranarayanan proved that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{6}^{2}\leq x\\
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{6})\in\mathbb{Z}^{6}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{6}^{2})=c(j)x^{3}+O(x^{3-\frac{6}{3(j+1)^{2}+1}+\epsilon}),

where

c(j)=\frac{16}{3}L(3,\chi_{4})\prod_{n=1}^{j}L(1,\mathrm{sym}^{2n}f)L(3,\mathrm{sym}^{2n}f\otimes\chi_{4})H_{j}(3),

and
\chi_{4}
is the non-principal Dirichlet character modulo
4
.
Here

L(s,\mathrm{sym}^{2n}f\otimes\chi_{4})=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2n}f}(m)\chi_{4}(m)}{m^{s}}

and
H_{j}(s)
is a Dirichlet series, which is absolutely convergent at
s=3
. The explicit expression for
H_{j}(s)
is mentioned in
[
21
]
.

The best known result for the estimate for the sum of six squares is proved by Liu and Yang
[
12
]

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{6}^{2}\leq x\\
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{6})\in\mathbb{Z}^{6}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{6}^{2})=c(j)x^{3}+O(x^{3-\frac{210}{105(j+1)^{2}-103}+\epsilon}),

where
c(j)
is a nonzero constant.

In
[
24
]
, Wang and Wang proved that

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{8}^{2}\leq x\\
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{8})\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{8}^{2})=\begin{cases}O(x^{4-\frac{2}{2j+3}+\epsilon})&\text{ if }j\geq 3\\
O(x^{\frac{25}{7}+\epsilon})&\text{ if }j=2\end{cases}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{8}^{2}\leq x\\
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{8})\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{8}^{2})=c(j)x^{4}+\begin{cases}O(x^{4-\frac{168}{84(j+1)^{2}-95}+\epsilon})&\text{ if }j\geq 3\\
O(x^{\frac{654}{174}+\epsilon})&\text{ if }j=2\end{cases}

where

c(j)=4\zeta(4)\prod_{n=1}^{j}L(4,\mathrm{sym}^{2n}f)^{16}L(1,\mathrm{sym}^{2n}f)^{16}H_{j}(4),

where
H_{j}(4)
is a Dirichlet series.

On the other hand, the study of sign changes in Fourier coefficients is another interesting topic in analytic number theory.

In 2013, Meher, Shankhadhar and Viswanadham
[
14
]
examined the sign changes of
\{a(n^{j})\}
for
j\in\{2,3,4\}
, where
a(n)
is the
n
th Fourier coefficient of normalised Hecke eigencusp forms for the full modular group. In 2014, Meher and Murty examined the sign changes of Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight cusp forms in
[
13
]
and Meher and Tanabe examined the sign changes of Fourier coefficients of Hilbert modular forms in
[
15
]
. In 2025, Kaur and Saha
[
9
]
examined the number of sign changes of Fourier coefficients of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
Hecke–Maass forms at sum of two squares.

In 2026, Kaur
[
10
]
proved that for any
j\geq 2
,
x
sufficiently large, the number of sign changes in
\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(n)
over the sums of two squares and between
x
and
2x
, is at least
x^{1-\delta_{j}}
, where

\frac{21j^{2}+42j+19}{21j^{2}+42j+40}<\delta_{j}<1.

We note that the number of representations of a natural number as a sum of
k
squares, where
k
varies in

, can be written as a linear combination of divisor functions, twisted with the non-principal Dirichlet character modulo
4
. Using this, we examine an asymptotic formula for the average behaviour of
\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(n)
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)
, over sum of
k
squares, where
k\in[2,12]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, we prove the following results.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in H_{k}
,
j\geq 2
be a fixed integer. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(n)=O\left(x^{1-\frac{2}{j+3}+\epsilon}\right).

.

Let

and

be a fixed integer. Then for any

, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}\leq x\\
(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})=O\left(x^{1-\frac{1}{j+1}+\epsilon}\right).

,

3.
Important Lemmas and Bounds

Note that (
2
) yields
\left|1-\frac{\alpha^{j-1}(p)\beta^{i}(p)}{p^{s}}\right|\geq 1-\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}}>0
for
\Re(s)=\sigma>1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|L(s,\mathrm{sym}^{j}f)|

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{p}\prod_{i=0}^{j}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{i=0}^{j}\zeta(\sigma)=\zeta(\sigma)^{j+1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{j+1}(n)}{n^{\sigma}},

where
d_{j+1}(n)
is the number of ways of expressing
n
as a product of
j+1
factors.
Since
d_{k}(n)\leq d(n)^{k-1}
for positive integers
k
and
n
, and since
d(n)\ll_{\epsilon}n^{\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
, we obtain

d_{k}(n)\ll_{k,\epsilon}n^{\epsilon}\quad\text{for any }\epsilon>0.

Therefore, the Dirichlet series for
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{j}f)
is absolutely convergent for
\Re(s)>1
.

Note that (
1
) and (
2
) imply

|\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(n)|\leq d_{j+1}(n).

Consequently, for any
\epsilon>0
,

(38)

|\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(n)|\ll_{j,\epsilon}n^{\epsilon}.

Since
\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(n)
is multiplicative,
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{j}f)
admits an Euler product

(39)

which is absolutely convergent for
\Re(s)>1
.

Observe that

(40)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(p)=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\alpha^{j-m}(p)\beta^{m}(p).

Moreover, Hecke theory gives the relation
\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(p)=\lambda_{f}(p^{j})
for each prime

Lemma 13
.

Let
f
be a Hecke eigenform with Satake parameters
\alpha_{p},\beta_{p}
satisfying
\alpha_{p}\beta_{p}=1
.
For any integer
j\geq 1
and any prime
p
,

\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(p)\;=\;1\;+\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2\ell}f}(p).

Proof.

Write
\lambda:=\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(p)=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\alpha_{p}^{\,j-m}\beta_{p}^{\,m}
.
Then, using
\beta_{p}=\alpha_{p}^{-1}
, we obtain

\lambda^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\sum_{m^{\prime}=0}^{j}\alpha_{p}^{\,2j-(m+m^{\prime})}\beta_{p}^{\,m+m^{\prime}}=\sum_{t=0}^{2j}N_{t}\,\alpha_{p}^{\,2j-t}\beta_{p}^{\,t}=\sum_{t=0}^{2j}N_{t}\,\alpha_{p}^{\,2j-2t},

where
N_{t}
counts pairs
(m,m^{\prime})
with
m+m^{\prime}=t
and
0\leq m,m^{\prime}\leq j
.
One has
N_{t}=t+1
for
0\leq t\leq j
and
N_{t}=2j-t+1
for
j<t\leq 2j
. In particular,
N_{t}=N_{2j-t}
for
0\leq t\leq 2j
. Therefore,

(41)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{2j}N_{t}\,\alpha_{p}^{\,2j-2t}

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{j}+\sum_{t=0}^{j-1}N_{t}\,\alpha_{p}^{\,2j-2t}+\sum_{t=j+1}^{2j}N_{2j-t}\,\alpha_{p}^{\,2j-2t}\quad(\text{since }N_{t}=N_{2j-t})

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{j}+\sum_{t=0}^{j-1}N_{t}\,\alpha_{p}^{\,2j-2t}+\sum_{t=0}^{j-1}N_{t}\,\alpha_{p}^{\,-2j+2t}

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{j}N_{j-k}\,\left(\alpha_{p}^{\,2k}+\alpha_{p}^{\,-2k}\right)

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(j+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{j}(j-k+1)\,\left(\alpha_{p}^{\,2k}+\alpha_{p}^{\,-2k}\right).

On the other hand, we have

\sum_{l=1}^{j}\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2\ell}f}(p)=\sum_{l=1}^{j}\sum_{u=0}^{2\ell}\alpha_{p}^{\,2\ell-u}\beta_{p}^{\,u}=\sum_{l=1}^{j}\sum_{u=0}^{2\ell}\alpha_{p}^{\,2\ell-2u}=\sum_{l=1}^{j}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\alpha_{p}^{\,2m}.

In the double sum, for
k\geq 1
, the term
\alpha_{p}^{\,2k}
(and similarly
\alpha_{p}^{\,-2k}
) occurs once for each
\ell
satisfying
\ell\geq k
, that is, for
\ell=k,k+1,\dots,j
. Thus, the coefficient of
\alpha_{p}^{\,2k}
or
\alpha_{p}^{\,-2k}
is
j-k+1
.
The constant term
\alpha_{p}^{0}=1
occurs in every
\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{2\ell}f}(p)
for
\ell=1,\dots,j
, giving total multiplicity
j
. Therefore,

\sum_{l=1}^{j}\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2\ell}f}(p)=\sum_{l=1}^{j}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\alpha_{p}^{\,2m}=j+\sum_{k=1}^{j}(j-k+1)\,\left(\alpha_{p}^{\,2k}+\alpha_{p}^{\,-2k}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle F_{j}^{(0)}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(n)\,}{n^{s}}=L(s,\mathrm{sym}^{j}f),\qquad\Re(s)>1.

Lemma 14
.

Let
f
be a normalised primitive holomorphic cusp form of weight
k
for
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
,
and let
\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(n)
denote the
n
th normalised Fourier coefficient
of the
j
th symmetric power
L
-function attached to
f
. Define

F_{j}^{(1)}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(n)\,l_{1}(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad\Re(s)>1,

where
l_{1}(n)
is given by (
19
). Then
F_{j}^{(1)}(s)
admits a factorisation

F_{j}^{(1)}(s)=G_{j}^{(1)}(s)\,H_{j}^{(1)}(s),

in which

G_{j}^{(1)}(s):=L\!\bigl(s,\operatorname{sym}^{j}f\bigr)\;L\!\bigl(s,\operatorname{sym}^{j}f\otimes\chi_{4}\bigr),

where
\chi_{4}
is the unique non-principal Dirichlet character modulo
4
,
and
H_{j}^{(1)}(s)
is a Dirichlet series converging absolutely and uniformly
in the half-plane
\Re(s)>\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Here we follow the steps as in
[
21
]
. We know that

\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(n)\,l_{1}(n)\ll n^{\epsilon}\qquad(\epsilon>0),

which implies that the Dirichlet series
F_{j}^{(1)}(s)
converges absolutely for
\Re(s)>1
. Since
\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(n)
is multiplicative,
F_{j}^{(1)}(s)
therefore admits an Euler product in this half-plane in
\Re(s)>1
:

(47)

\displaystyle F_{j}^{(1)}(s)=\prod_{p}\Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(p)\,l_{1}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(p^{2})\,l_{1}(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}+\cdots+\frac{\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(p^{m})\,l_{(}p^{m})}{p^{ms}}+\cdots\Bigr).

Now define the multiplicative function
b_{1}(n)
via its Euler product

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{1}(n)}{n^{s}}:=L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{j}f)\;L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{j}f\otimes\chi_{4})(=:G_{j}(s)),

Therefore, for a prime
p
, we have
b_{1}(p)=\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(p)\,+\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(p)\,\chi_{4}.
Since
l_{1}(p)=1+\chi_{4}(p)
, we obtain that
b_{1}(p)=\lambda_{\operatorname{sym}^{j}f}(p)\,l_{1}(p)
, establishing the desired equality at each prime.

Note that
b_{1}(p^{k})\neq\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(p^{k})l(p^{k})\text{ for all }k>1
and

\displaystyle|b_{1}(n)|=|(\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}*\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}\chi_{4}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{d|n}|\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(d)||\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\chi_{4}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{d|n}d^{\epsilon}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{\epsilon}\leq n^{\epsilon}d(n)\ll_{\epsilon}n^{\epsilon}\text{ for any }\epsilon>0.

So
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{1}(n)}{n^{s}}
is absolutely convergent by
\Re(s)>1
and the Euler product ensures that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{1}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\left(1+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{b_{1}(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}\right)\quad(\Re(s)>1).

Now,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{1}(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{p^{\epsilon m}}{p^{m\sigma}}\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{p^{\epsilon m}}{p^{(1+2\epsilon)m}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{m(1+\epsilon)}}=\frac{1}{p^{1+\epsilon}-1}<1

for
\Re(s)>1+2\epsilon
.

Let

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(p^{m})l_{1}(p^{m})}{p^{ms}},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{1}(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}\quad(|B|<1).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1+A}{1+B}

\displaystyle=(1+A)(1-B+B^{2}-\cdots)

\displaystyle=1+A-B-AB+\cdots

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(p^{2})l_{1}(p^{2})-b_{1}(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}+\cdots+\frac{c(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}+\cdots

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{c_{p}(n)}{n^{s}}\quad\text{(say)},

where

c_{p}(n)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }n=1,\\
c(n),&\text{if }n=p^{m}\;(m\geq 2),\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that the above equality holds for
\Re(s)>1+2\epsilon
for all
\epsilon>0
, and that the series is absolutely convergent in this region.
Also note that
c_{p}(n)\ll n^{\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon>0
.
We define
c(n)
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
by

\prod_{p}\frac{1+A}{1+B}=\prod_{p}\left(1+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{c(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c(n)}{n^{s}}.

By construction,
c(n)
is multiplicative.

Define

\displaystyle H_{j}^{(1)}(s):=\frac{F_{j}^{(1)}(s)}{G_{j}^{(1)}(s)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\frac{1+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(p^{m})l_{1}(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}}{1+\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{b(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\frac{1+A}{1+B}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c(n)}{n^{s}}.

We now find the region of convergence for
H_{j}^{(1)}(s)
. Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 3}\bigg|\frac{c(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\geq 3}\frac{p^{m\epsilon}}{p^{m\sigma}}=\sum_{m\geq 3}\frac{1}{p^{m(\sigma-\epsilon)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{2(\sigma-\epsilon)}(p^{\sigma-\epsilon}-1)}<\frac{1}{p^{2(\sigma-\epsilon)}}\text{ for any }\epsilon>0.

The above inequality of the series is true for
\Re(s)>1+\epsilon
, and

\displaystyle\frac{c(p^{2})}{p^{2\sigma}}=\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}f}(p^{2})l(p^{2})-b(p^{2})}{p^{2\sigma}}=O\left(\frac{p^{2\epsilon}}{p^{2\sigma}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma-2\epsilon}}\right).

Now
\prod_{p}\left(1+\bigg|\frac{c(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}\bigg|+\sum_{m\geq 3}\bigg|\frac{c(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}\bigg|\right)=\prod_{p}\left(1+u_{p}\right)
is convergent if and only if
\sum_{p}u_{p}
is convergent, where
u_{p}=|\frac{c(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}|+\sum_{m\geq 3}|\frac{c(p^{m})}{p^{ms}}|
. Note that

\sum_{p}u_{p}\ll\sum_{p}\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma-2\epsilon}}

is absolutely convergent for
2\sigma-2\epsilon>1
, that is, in the region
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
. So in this region
H_{j}^{(1)}(s)\ll_{\epsilon}1
and
H_{j}^{(1)}(s)
is absolutely convergent in
\Re(s)>\frac{1}{2}
.
∎
