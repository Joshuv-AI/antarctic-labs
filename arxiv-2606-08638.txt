Parameter Tuning with Generalization Guarantees for GPU-Accelerated Linear Programming
Siddharth Prasad, Dravyansh Sharma

Abstract
Recent research has developed practical, parallelizable first-order methods for large scale linear programming, but performance is highly dependent on hyperparameter selection. We derive generalization guarantees for hyperparameter tuning within (cu)PDLP, a state-of-the-art first-order LP solver designed for modern hardware. First, we pin down the behavior of PDHG, the primal-dual hybrid gradient algorithm that underlies PDLP, as a function of its step size and primal weight, leading to linear sample complexity guarantees for learning those parameters. We then conduct a structural analysis of PDLP, which augments PDHG with several specialized techniques like preconditioning, adaptive step sizes, averaging, adaptive restarts, and smoothed primal weight updates. Our analysis captures the behavior of the solution trajectory as a function of the hyperparameters and leverages recent advances in data-driven algorithm design to obtain polynomial sample complexity guarantees for learning those hyperparameters. Finally, we conduct proof-of-concept experiments that demonstrate the need for data-driven PDLP parameter tuning. Our results showcase the versatility of the data-driven algorithm design toolkit for principled hyperparameter tuning within solver-grade implementations of complex modern optimization algorithms.

Introduction

Linear programming (LP) is one of the most fundamental paradigms of optimization and operations research. LP solvers—traditionally based on the simplex algorithm or interior point methods and run on CPUs—are an extremely mature technology. They serve innumerable applications across science and industry. Generalization

Each instance LP is given by its primal data
x=(\bm{c},\bm{G},\bm{h},\bm{A},\bm{b},\bm{\ell},\bm{u})
and is drawn from an underlying distribution
D
over LP instance space
\mathcal{X}
. A
generalization
(or
sample complexity
) guarantee bounds the error
\varepsilon
(uniformly over all parameter settings) between performance on a training set of size
N
and expected future performance on an unseen test instance:
\mathbb{P}_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\sim D}(\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}u_{\phi}(x_{i})-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim D}[u_{\phi}(x)]|\leq\varepsilon)\geq 1-\delta
. Generalization is controlled by
pseudo-dimension

[
36
]
: if
\mathcal{U}=\{u_{\phi}:\mathcal{X}\to[0,T]\mid\phi\in\Phi\}
is the class of (bounded) performance measures (in our case, iteration count) with hyperparameter space
\Phi
,
\mathsf{Pdim}(\mathcal{U})
is the largest integer
N
for which there exist
N
LP instances
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
and
N
thresholds
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}
such that for every sign pattern
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
, there exists
\phi\in\Phi
such that
\mathrm{sign}(u_{\phi}(x_{i})-\gamma_{i})=\sigma_{i}
for all
i
. For a fixed training set size
N
, the resulting generalization error is
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{U}}(N,\delta)\leq T\sqrt{(\mathsf{Pdim}(\mathcal{U})+\log(1/\delta))/N}
. For a fixed error threshold
\varepsilon
, the
sample complexity
is
N_{\mathcal{U}}(\varepsilon,\delta)=O((T/\varepsilon)^{2}(\mathsf{Pdim}(\mathcal{U})+\log(1/\delta)))
.

h_{j}-\bm{G}_{j,\cdot}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)}
is invariant and hence
(h_{j}-\bm{G}_{j,\cdot}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)})^{+}
is a degree
2t
rational function.
Finally, within each such component, add the curve
\lVert\bm{A}\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{b}\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}((h_{j}-\bm{G}_{j,\cdot}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)})^{+})^{2}\leq\gamma_{p}^{2},
which is of degree
4t
. Now, within each component, primal feasibility of
(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})
is invariant.

To handle dual feasibility and duality gap, we need to get a handle on dual variable
\bm{\lambda}^{(t)}=\operatorname{proj}_{\Lambda_{1}\times\cdots\times\Lambda_{n}}(\bm{c}-\bm{K}^{\top}\bm{y}^{(t)})
first. For that, we put down, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that either
\ell_{i}=-\infty
and
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
or
\ell_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
u_{i}=\infty
, the degree
2t
curve
c_{i}-\bm{K}_{\cdot,i}^{\top}\bm{y}^{(t)}=0
. Within each connected component of
\mathcal{C}
induced by these curves,
\lambda^{(t)}_{i}
is thus a degree
2t
rational function in
(\eta,\omega)
.
Now, with
\gamma_{d}\coloneq\varepsilon_{\mathsf{tol}}(1+\lVert\bm{c}\rVert_{2})
, dual feasibility is the condition
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(c_{i}-\bm{K}_{\cdot,i}^{\top}\bm{y}^{(t)}-\lambda_{i}^{(t)})^{2}\leq\gamma_{d}^{2},

which is a degree
4t
curve (after clearing denominators). Within each region induced by these curves, dual feasibility of
(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})
is invariant.
Finally, we handle the duality gap condition, which reads
|\bm{q}^{\top}\bm{y}+\bm{\ell}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{+}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{-}-\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{x}|\leq\gamma_{g}
, where
\gamma_{g}\coloneq\varepsilon_{\mathsf{tol}}(1+\lvert\bm{q}^{\top}\bm{y}+\bm{\ell}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{+}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{-}\rvert+\lvert\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{x}\rvert)
. Put down curves
\lambda_{i}^{(t)}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. In cells where
\lambda_{i}^{(t)}<0
add the curves
\bm{q}^{\top}\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{(t)}-\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)}=0
and
\bm{q}^{\top}\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{(t)}=0
and in cells where
\lambda_{i}^{(t)}\geq 0
add the curves
\bm{q}^{\top}\bm{y}^{(t)}+\bm{\ell}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{(t)}-\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)}=0
and
\bm{q}^{\top}\bm{y}^{(t)}+\bm{\ell}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{(t)}=0
. Add the additional curve
\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)}=0
. Within the induced components, the signs of all absolute value terms in the duality gap condition are invariant. So, within each component the duality gap condition is an algebraic curve of degree
O(t)
, which induces the final partition over which the truth of the duality gap condition is invariant. Finally, collecting all curves over all rounds
t\leq T
(and carefully counting them), we obtain a partition such that
\mathtt{terminate}(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})
is invariant in each connected component for all
t
.
∎

We can finally apply the general result of
3
to obtain a pseudo-dimension bound.

Theorem 3.3
.

\mathsf{Pdim}(\{u_{\eta,\omega}:\eta,\omega>0\})=O(T\log(m_{1}nT))
.

4
Tuning PDLP Parameters

In this section we analyze the sample complexity of tuning key hyperparameters of (cu)PDLP (Alg.
1
). Our analysis builds on recent advances in data-driven algorithm design for function classes with
Pfaffian structure
, which strictly generalizes polynomial structure. Understanding the Pfaffian structure of the numerical computations in Alg.
1
is what allows us to characterize the behavior of the full execution of PDLP.
We summarize here the key tools from prior work needed for our analysis.

Definition 4.1

(Pfaffian chains and functions
[
23
]
)
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. A sequence of functions

f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{q}:U\to\mathbb{R}

forms a
Pfaffian chain
of length
q
and degree at most
\alpha
if for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)=P_{i,j}(x,f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{i}(x)),

where each
P_{i,j}
is a polynomial of degree at most
\alpha
.
A function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
is
Pfaffian
with respect to this chain if

f(x)=Q(x,f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{q}(x)),

for some polynomial
Q
of degree at most
\beta
.
We say
f
has
Pfaffian complexity

(q,\alpha,\beta)
.

Any polynomial is Pfaffian with
q=0
. Exponentials
e^{ax}
and logarithms
\log(ax)
are Pfaffian over their domains. Periodic trigonometric functions like
\sin(ax)
and
\cos(ax)
are
not
Pfaffian.
Pfaffian functions are closed under addition, multiplication, division (away from zero), and composition with
\exp
and
\log

[
5
]
. These properties will be used repeatedly.

Definition 4.2

(Pfaffian piecewise structure
[
5
]
)
.

Function
f:\Phi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
(k_{F},k_{G},q,M,\Delta,d)
-Pfaffian piecewise structured
if there exist
k_{G}
Pfaffian
boundary
functions
and
k_{F}
Pfaffian
piece
functions—all with Pfaffian complexity
(q,M,\Delta)
—such that on each region of
\Phi
induced by the
k_{G}
-bit sign pattern of the boundary functions,
f
coincides with a piece function.

A key learning-theoretic result we will need is the following bound on the pseudo-dimension of piecewise-Pfaffian functions in terms of their structural complexity.

Theorem 4.3

(Pseudo-dimension of Pfaffian piecewise classes
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{u_{\phi}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{Z}\}_{\phi\in\Phi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.
If for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the dual function
u_{x}^{*}(\phi)\coloneq u_{\phi}(x)
is
(k_{F},k_{G},q,M,\Delta,d)
-Pfaffian piecewise structured, then

\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{U})\leq d^{2}q^{2}+2dq\log(\Delta+M)+2\log(\Delta(k_{F}+k_{G}))+16.

First, we analyze the dependence of PDLP on the smoothing parameter

Theorem 4.4

(Pseudo-dimension bound for tuning
\theta
in PDLP)
.

Fix an LP instance
x=(\bm{c},\bm{K},\bm{q},\bm{\ell},\bm{u})\in\mathcal{X}
. Let
u_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{Z}
be the number of iterations of Algorithm
1
using primal-weight update smoothing parameter
\theta
(with all other hyperparameters—in particular the Pock-Chambolle
\alpha

x\in\mathcal{X}
, the dual function
u^{*}_{x}(\theta)\coloneq u_{\theta}(x)

is
(k_{F},k_{G},q,M,\Delta,1)
-Pfaffian piecewise structured with:

\displaystyle k_{F}\leq 2^{k_{G}},

\displaystyle k_{G}\leq(ST)^{2.3}\log\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{2}(L+U+m_{1}+6)+6S,

\displaystyle q=O((ST)^{2.3}\log\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{2}),

\displaystyle M,\Delta\leq 2^{O((ST)^{2.3}\log\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{2})}.

Consequently,

\operatorname{Pdim}(\{u_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{Z}\mid\theta\in\Theta\})=O((ST)^{4.6}\widetilde{K}^{2}+(ST)^{2.3}\widetilde{K}(L+U+m_{1})),

where

and

are the outer and inner loop iteration limits of Algorithm

Proof sketch.

All branching decisions (projections, decisions in the adaptive step-size procedure, the KKT error comparison in line 7, the termination and restart checks in line 9) can be written as sign conditions of the form
\tau(\theta)\geq 0
.
These define
k_{G}
Pfaffian boundary functions.

Fix a region where all predicates have constant sign. The algorithm follows a fixed execution path within such a region. We then induct over iterations: primal and dual updates (line 5) are algebraic operations, averaging (line 6) involves summation and division, and the primal-weight update (line 12) involves
\log
and
\exp
. All operations preserve Pfaffian structure.

Thus, all iterates and outputs are Pfaffian on each region. Counting sign patterns yields
k_{F}\leq 2^{k_{G}}
. Pfaffian complexity bounds follow from carefully counting all operations. A key intermediate technical result we establish is a bound on the number of iterations in the adaptive step size procedure (Algorithm
3

we show that the number of iterations is at most
O((ST)^{1.3}\widetilde{K})

4.3

∎

Next, we present our main structural result for individual tuning of

Theorem 4.5

(Pseudo-dimension bound for tuning
\alpha
in PDLP)
.

Fix an LP instance

\displaystyle k_{F}\leq 2^{K_{\alpha}},~k_{G}\leq K_{\alpha},~q=O(n+m+(ST)^{2.3}\log\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{2}),~M,\Delta\leq 2^{O((ST)^{2.3}\log\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{2})}

S,T

Proof sketch.

The ten Ruiz iterations are independent of
\alpha

\bm{K}
be the post-Ruiz matrix. Pock-Chambolle scaling, with

\bm{D}_{1}(\alpha)=\operatorname{diag}(\mathsf{r}_{1}^{2-\alpha},\dots,\mathsf{r}_{m}^{2-\alpha})

\bm{D}_{2}(\alpha)=\operatorname{diag}(\mathsf{c}_{1}^{\alpha},\dots,\mathsf{c}_{n}^{\alpha})

These computations represent one-variable Pfaffian functions of

\mathsf{r}_{j}^{2-\alpha}=\mathsf{r}_{j}^{2}e^{-\alpha\log\mathsf{r}_{j}},\qquad\mathsf{c}_{i}^{\alpha}=e^{\alpha\log\mathsf{c}_{i}},\qquad\mathsf{c}_{i}^{-\alpha}=e^{-\alpha\log\mathsf{c}_{i}}.

So, all entries of the preconditioned data
{\bm{K}}(\alpha)\leftarrow\bm{D}_{1}(\alpha)\bm{K}\bm{D}_{2}(\alpha)

\alpha
from the

The remainder of the analysis proceeds as in the

6
Conclusions and Future Research

We established generalization guarantees for tuning the most important hyperparameters within (cu)PDLP, a new state-of-the-art first order method for linear programming that runs on GPUs. First, we showed that the step size and primal weight of the vanilla PDHG algorithm—which underpins PDLP—are learnable with linear sample complexity. Our analysis showed that the primal and dual trajectories were piecewise rational functions with bounded degree. Next, we characterized the behavior of PDLP trajectories and leveraged the recent theory of data-driven algorithm design for algorithms with
Pfaffian
structure to obtain polynomial sample complexity guarantees for PDLP hyperparameter tuning. Finally, we presented experiments demonstrating the importance of hyperparameter tuning, and that distribution-dependent tuning is essential, with significant performance gains over default parameter settings.

There are many fruitful directions for future research. First, we believe our results can be directly extended to yield a more unified generalization theory for first-order PDHG-based LP methods including the gamut of algorithms proceeding PDLP
[
28
,
26
,
27
]
. Results in that direction could shed light on automated design of higher-level algorithmic aspects beyond tuning of prespecified hyperparameters. Two important practical next steps are scaling up to larger-scale LP instances and
instance specific
parameter selection, where a (neural network based, say) model is trained to predict good parameter settings based on instance features. Guarantees along this line have been obtained in the related domain of integer programming
[
19
]
, and we expect that they are possible here as well. This direction has the potential for even greater practical performance improvements.

B.1
Termination

Solution progress is measured via duality gap, primal feasibility, and dual feasibility
with respect to the original problem data
. Recall
\bm{r}_{1}
and
\bm{r}_{2}
collect the impacts of all scaling operations performed in preconditioning (Algorithm
2
).
Consider the primal-dual iterates
\bm{x}^{s,t+1},\bm{y}^{s,t+1}
at the point Algorithm
1
enters the termination check at line
9
.
The
unscaled
iterates are:
\bm{r}_{1}^{-1}*\bm{x}^{s,t+1}
,
\bm{r}_{2}^{-1}*\bm{y}^{s,t+1}
(
*
denotes component-wise product). Let
\bm{c}
,
\bm{K}=(\bm{G},\bm{A})
,
\bm{q}=(\bm{h},\bm{b})
,
\bm{\ell}
,
\bm{u}
denote the
original problem data
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathtt{gap}(\bm{x},\bm{y})

\displaystyle\coloneq\left\lvert\bm{q}^{\top}\bm{y}+\bm{\ell}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{+}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{-}-\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{x}\right\rvert,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathtt{pfeas}(\bm{x},\bm{y})

\displaystyle\coloneq\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}\bm{x}-\bm{b}\\
(\bm{h}-\bm{G}\bm{x})^{+}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2},

\displaystyle\mathtt{dfeas}(\bm{x},\bm{y})

\displaystyle\coloneq\left\lVert\bm{c}-\bm{K}^{\top}\bm{y}-\bm{\lambda}\right\rVert_{2}.

Given primal-dual pair
\bm{x},\bm{y}
in the above, dual variable
\bm{\lambda}
—which corresponds to the lower/upper bound constraints
\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{x}\leq\bm{u}
—is computed as

\bm{\lambda}=\operatorname{proj}_{\Lambda_{1}\times\cdots\times\Lambda_{n}}(\bm{c}-\bm{K}^{\top}\bm{y})

by clamping the coordinates of
\bm{c}-\bm{K}^{\top}\bm{y}
to the appropriate range
\Lambda_{i}
.
Let
\mathtt{terminate}
denote the boolean expression

\displaystyle\mathtt{terminate}(\bm{x},\bm{y})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}\left[\mathtt{gap}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\leq\varepsilon_{\mathsf{tol}}(1+\lvert\bm{q}^{\top}\bm{y}+\bm{\ell}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{+}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{-}\rvert+\lvert\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{x}\rvert)\right]

B.2
Restarts

KKT error is a progress metric that combines dual gap and primal-and-dual feasibility. It is used to determine when the inner loop is restarted from a new point and is defined by

\mathtt{kkt}_{\omega}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\sqrt{\omega^{2}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}\bm{x}-\bm{b}\\
(\bm{h}-\bm{G}\bm{x})^{+}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{\left\lVert\bm{c}-\bm{K}^{\top}\bm{y}-\bm{\lambda}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\left\lvert\bm{q}^{\top}\bm{y}+\bm{\ell}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{+}-\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{\lambda}^{-}-\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{x}\right\rvert^{2}}.

(We have not directly used the definitions of
\mathtt{gap}
,
\mathtt{pfeas}
and
\mathtt{dfeas}
within
\mathtt{kkt}
since the former three metrics are always computed with respect to the original problem data. KKT error is computed with respect to the current iterates and preconditioned data. This includes computation of
\bm{\lambda}
.)

The restart condition
\mathtt{res}(\widehat{\bm{x}}^{s,t+1},\widehat{\bm{y}}^{s,t+1},\widehat{\bm{x}}^{s,t},\widehat{\bm{y}}^{s,t},\bm{x}^{s,0},\bm{y}^{s,0},\omega^{s})
is the OR of the following predicates

\displaystyle\mathtt{kkt}_{\omega^{s}}(\widehat{\bm{x}}^{s,t+1},\widehat{\bm{y}}^{s,t+1})\leq 0.2\cdot\mathtt{kkt}(\bm{x}^{s,0},\bm{y}^{s,0};\omega^{s})

\displaystyle\mathtt{kkt}_{\omega^{s}}(\widehat{\bm{x}}^{s,t+1},\widehat{\bm{y}}^{s,t+1})\leq 0.8\cdot\mathtt{kkt}_{\omega^{s}}(\bm{x}^{s,0},\bm{y}^{s,0})\wedge\mathtt{kkt}_{\omega^{s}}(\widehat{\bm{x}}^{s,t+1},\widehat{\bm{y}}^{s,t+1})>\mathtt{kkt}_{\omega^{s}}(\widehat{\bm{x}}^{s,t},\widehat{\bm{y}}^{s,t})

\displaystyle t\leq 0.36k

For restarts, gap, primal feasibility, and dual feasibility are evaluated with respect to the preconditioned problem data.

B.3
Adaptive PDHG Step Algorithm

Algorithm
3
is the line search routine developed by
2
to determine the step size used in the PDHG step for each iteration of the inner loop of PDLP. It dampens the candidate step size
\eta
until the bound of line
4
is met. That bound comes from the convergence rate proof for PDHG
[
17
]
.

Algorithm 3

\mathtt{adaptivePDHG}(\bm{x},\bm{y},\omega,\widehat{\eta},k)

1:

\eta\leftarrow\widehat{\eta}

2:

while

\mathtt{TRUE}

do

3:

\bm{x}^{\prime}\leftarrow\operatorname{proj}_{X}(\bm{x}-\tfrac{\eta}{\omega}(\bm{c}-\bm{K}^{\top}\bm{y}))
;
\bm{y}^{\prime}\leftarrow\operatorname{proj}_{Y}(\bm{y}+\eta\omega(\bm{q}-\bm{K}(2\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{x})))

4:

\overline{\eta}\leftarrow\tfrac{\omega\lVert\bm{x^{\prime}}-\bm{x}\rVert_{2}^{2}+(1/\omega)\lVert\bm{y}^{\prime}-\bm{y}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2(\bm{y}^{\prime}-\bm{y})^{\top}\bm{K}(\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{x})}

if

2(\bm{y}^{\prime}-\bm{y})^{\top}\bm{K}(\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{x})>0

else

\infty

5:

\eta^{\prime}\leftarrow\min\{(1-(k+1)^{-0.3})\overline{\eta},(1+(k+1)^{-0.6})\eta\}

6:

if

\eta\leq\overline{\eta}

then

7:

return

(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{y}^{\prime}),\eta,\eta^{\prime}

8:

\eta\leftarrow\eta^{\prime}

The main loop in Algorithm
3
does not have an explicit bound on the number of iterations. However, we can establish the following bound that is dependent on the input step size
\widehat{\eta}
and the current value of the total iteration counter
k
.

Lemma B.1
.

Assume
\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{\infty}\geq 1
.
The adaptive PDHG step algorithm (Algorithm
3
) terminates in at most

\frac{\log(\widehat{\eta}\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{2})}{\log(1/(1-(k+1)^{-0.3}))}=O\left(ST\frac{\log\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{2}}{\log(1/(1-(k+1)^{-0.3}))}\right)

iterations.

Proof.

The key fact is that in Algorithm
3
,
\overline{\eta}\geq 1/\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{2}
always holds
[
2
]
. So, an upper bound on the number of iterations of the main loop is the number of iterations until
\eta
drops below
1/\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{2}
. Now, for each round that
\eta
is rejected (that is, the if-statement evaluates to false), we have
\eta>\overline{\eta}
, so
(1-(k+1)^{-0.3})\overline{\eta}\leq(1+(k+1)^{-0.6})\eta
. So,
\eta
is updated to
(1-(k+1)^{-0.3})\overline{\eta}\leq(1-(k+1)^{-0.3})\eta
, that is, it reduces by a factor
(1-(k+1)^{-0.3})
on each rejected round, which shows that there are at most
\log(\widehat{\eta}\lVert K\rVert_{2})/\log(1/(1-(k+1)^{-0.3}))
iterations. The right-hand-side bound follows from the fact that Algorithm
3
is entered at most
ST
times, and on a final loop iteration where
\eta
is accepted, it in the worst case increases by a factor of
(1+(k+1)^{-0.6})
. Naively upper bounding this quantity by
2
yields the global upper bound
\widehat{\eta}\leq\widehat{\eta}^{0,0}2^{ST}=2^{ST}/\lVert\bm{K}\rVert_{\infty}<2^{ST}
. Substitution yields the desired bound.
∎

Lemma
B.1
is critical to establishing bounded Pfaffian complexity of PDLP (Appendix

).

In addition, line 12 contributes two norm-threshold predicates per outer iteration, hence at most

2S
predicates in total.
Therefore the total number of scalar predicate functions is at most

K_{\theta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{full}}=ST\bigl(B_{\max}(L+U+m_{1}+3)+7\bigr)+2S.

Step 3: branch partition.

Each predicate can be written as

\tau(\theta,\alpha)\geq 0\quad\text{or}\quad\tau(\theta,\alpha)>0

for some scalar function
\tau
built from the current intermediate quantities.
Let
\Gamma_{x}
denote the union of the zero sets of all such predicate functions.
Since only finitely many predicates occur,
\Gamma_{x}
is a finite union of Pfaffian hypersurfaces in

\Theta\times A
.
On each connected component of

(\Theta\times A)\setminus\Gamma_{x}

all branch outcomes are fixed, hence the entire truncated execution follows one fixed execution
formula on that component.

Therefore
u_{x}^{*}
is piecewise Pfaffian on
\Theta\times A
.

Step 4: Pfaffian piecewise-structure parameters.

Take all scalar predicate functions as boundary functions. Then

k_{G}\leq K_{\theta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{full}}.

A full sign pattern of these predicates determines at most one execution formula for the truncated
trace and hence at most one Pfaffian piece function, so

k_{F}\leq 2^{K_{\theta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{full}}}.

The preconditioning phase contributes a chain of length
O(n+m)
from the
\alpha
-dependent
scaling functions. Each backtracking round contributes only
O(1)
additional reciprocal or
auxiliary functions, and each outer update contributes only
O(1)
additional logarithmic/exponential
functions from the
\theta
-update. Since there are at most
STB_{\max}
backtracking
rounds in total,

q=O(n+m+STB_{\max}).

Using the same conservative bookkeeping as in the one-parameter analyses,

M,\Delta\leq 2^{O(STB_{\max})}.

Thus the dual class
U_{\Theta,A}^{*}
is

(k_{F},k_{G},q,M,\Delta,2)
-Pfaffian piecewise structured.

Step 5: apply Theorem
4.3
.

Applying Theorem
4.3
with parameter dimension
d=2
yields

\operatorname{Pdim}(U_{\Theta,A})\leq 4q^{2}+4q\log(\Delta+M)+8q\log 2+4\log\!\bigl(\Delta(k_{F}+k_{G})\bigr)+32.

Substituting the bounds above gives

\operatorname{Pdim}(U_{\Theta,A})=O\!\Big((n+m+STB_{\max})^{2}+K_{\theta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{full}}\Big).

Finally, substituting the definition of
K_{\theta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{full}}
yields the claimed bound.
∎
